An isoperimetric inequality for the second Robin eigenvalue of the Weighted Laplacian
Yi Gao, Kui Wang, Anqiang Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate a shape optimization problem for the second Robin eigenvalue of the weighted Laplacian on bounded Lipschitz domains symmetric about the origin. Our main theorem states that the ball centered at the origin maximizes the second Robin eigenvalue among all Lipschitz bounded domains of prescribed weighted measure and symmetric about the origin for a range of negative Robin parameters.

35P15, 58G25

1.
Introduction

The classical Szegö-Weinberger inequality
[
23
,
25
]
states that the ball uniquely maximizes the second (first nonzero) Neumann eigenvalue of Laplacian among all bounded domains of the same volume in Euclidean space. Using the arguments of Szegö and Weinberger, analogous inequalities for the second Neumann eigenvalues have been established in various settings: Ashbaugh-Benguria
[
2
]
extended the the inequality to bounded domains in hemisphere and in hyperbolic space; Chiacchio and di Blasio
[
12
]
obtained a sharp isopermetric inequality for the first nonzero Neumann eigenvalue for bounded domians symmetric about the origin in Gauss space.
Moreover, it is expected (cf.
[
17
, Problem 4.41]
) that for a range of negative Robin parameters, the second Robin eigenvalue should be maximized by a round ball. This expectation has recently been confirmed by Freitas and Laugesen via the Szegö
[
14
]
and the Weinberger
[
15
]
approaches.
For further results on Szegö-Weinberger type inequalities, we refer to
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
9
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
22
,
24
,
26
]
and references therein.

Eigenvalue optimization problems for the weighted Laplacian have also attracted considerable attention. For instance, Chiacchio and di Blasio
[
12
]
proved that, among all origin-symmetric regions with fixed Gaussian volume, the ball maximizes the first nonzero Neumann eigenvalue; Brock, Francesco and di Blasio
[
9
]
obtained optimal Szegö-Weinberger type inequalities under a weighted measure.
Additional results on weighted eigenvalue problems can be found in
[
5
,
6
,
9
,
11
,
13
,
16
]
and the references therein.

Inspired by the work of Brock, Francesco and di Blasio
[
9
]
on the weighted Laplace operator and that of Freitas and Laugesen
[
15
]
on the second Robin eigenvalue, we study the shape optimization problem for the second Robin eigenvalue of the following class of problems

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}(e^{h(|x|)}\nabla u)=\lambda e^{h(|x|)}u,\quad&x\in\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\alpha u=0,\quad&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Here and in the sequel,
\Omega
denotes a bounded connected domain in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with Lipchitz boundary,
\nu
is the outward normal to
\partial\Omega
, and
h(r)
is a
C^{2}
function on
[0,+\infty)
. We define the weighted measure associated with
h
by

d\gamma_{h}=e^{h(|x|)}\,dx,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

and denote by
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})
the weighted Sobolev space equipped with the norm

\displaystyle||u||_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,d\gamma_{h}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\gamma_{h}\right)^{1/2}.

It is well known that the problem (
1.1
) has discrete spectrum. We denote its eigenvalue by
\lambda_{k,\alpha}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})
for
k=1,2,\cdots
, which satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,\alpha}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})<\lambda_{2,\alpha}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})\leq\lambda_{3,\alpha}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})\leq\cdots\to+\infty,

with each eigenvalue repeated according to its multiplicity.
By standard spectral theory for self-adjoint compact operators, the first two eigenvalues of (
1.1
) admit the variational characterizations:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,\alpha}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})=\inf\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}d\gamma_{h}+\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{2}e^{h}\,dA}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}d\gamma_{h}}:u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})\setminus\{0\}\},

where
dA
is the
induced measure on
\partial\Omega
;
and

(1.3)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2,\alpha}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})=\inf\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}d\gamma_{h}+\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{2}e^{h}dA}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}d\gamma_{h}}:u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})\setminus\{0\},\int_{\Omega}uu_{1}d\gamma_{h}=0\},

where
u_{1}(x)
is an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{1,\alpha}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})
.

To state our main results, we introduce the following Steklov eigenvalue problem on
\Omega

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}(e^{h(|x|)}\nabla u)=0\quad&x\in\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\sigma u,\quad&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

The first nonzero Steklov eigenvalue can be characterized variationally by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\gamma_{h}}{\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{2}e^{h}\,dA}:u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})\setminus\{0\},\;\;\int_{\partial\Omega}ue^{h(|x|)}\ dA=0\right\}.

In this paper, we establish the following optimal isoperimetric inequality for the second Robin eigenvalue of (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain symmetric about the origin. Suppose
h(r)\in C^{2}([0,+\infty))
satisfies
h^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
and
h^{\prime\prime}(r)\geq 0
for
r\in(0,+\infty)
. Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be the origin-centered round ball with the same
\gamma_{h}
-volume as
\Omega
, namely
\int_{\Omega}e^{h(|x|)}\,dx=\int_{B}e^{h(|x|)}\,dx
. Denote by
\sigma_{1}(B;\gamma_{h})
the first nonzero Steklov eigenvalue of (
1.4
) on
B
, and
\lambda_{2,\alpha}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})
the second Robin eigenvalue of (
1.1
) on
\Omega
. If
\alpha\in[-\sigma_{1}(B;\gamma_{h}),0]
, then

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2,\alpha}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})\leq\lambda_{2,\alpha}(B;\gamma_{h}).

Equality holds if and only if

\Omega=B
.

By taking
\alpha=0
, Theorem
1.1
implies that the ball centered at the
origin is the unique set maximizer the second Neumann eigenvalue
\mu_{2}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})
among all bounded Lipschitz domains
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with prescribed
\gamma_{h}
-measure and symmetric about the origin. This result was previously proved by Brock, Chiacchio, and di Blasio (Theorem 1.2 of
[
9
]
). Note that Theorem
1.1
applies in particular to
\lambda_{2,\alpha}(\Omega;e^{|x|^{2}/2})
. Hence, our result provides information about eigenvalues of the problem

-\Delta u-x\cdot\nabla u=\lambda u

which has been widely studied in literature (see, e.g.
[
7
,
10
]
).
The symmetry assumption on
\Omega
in Theorem
1.1

ensures the validity of the orthogonality conditions (
3.5
), as the weighted measure
e^{h(|x|)}
is radially
symmetric about the origin.
It remains an interesting question that whether (
1.6
)
holds in the case of the Gauss measure
e^{-|x|^{2}/2}dx
.

By taking
\alpha=-\sigma_{1}(B;\gamma_{h})
, Theorem
1.1
yields the following Brock–Weinstock inequality, which was proved by Brock and Chiacchio
[
8
, Theorems 1.4 and 1.5]
and by Mao and Zhang
[
21
, Corollary 1.2]
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Omega;\gamma_{h})\leq\sigma_{1}(B;\gamma_{h}).

Equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is isometric to the round ball
B
.

The main technique in the proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on Weinberger’s trick, in which test functions are constructed using eigenfunctions on a round ball to estimate eigenvalues. The main difficulties in the proof include analyzing the properties of the second eigenfunction of (
1.1
) on the round ball and establishing the required monotonicity to apply Weinberger’s method. The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we study properties of the Robin and Steklov eigenvalue problems for balls in the weighted space. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
.

2.
Eigenvalue Problem for Balls In the Weighted Space

In this section, we prove several properties of the eigenvalue problems (
1.1
) and (
1.4
) for round balls centered at the origin. Denote by
B(R)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
the origin-centered ball of radius
R
. Then Steklov eigenvalue problem of (
1.4
) on
B(R)
takes the form

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u-\nabla h\cdot\nabla u=0\quad&\text{in $B(R)$,}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\sigma u\quad&\text{on $\partial B(R)$,}\end{cases}

and the Robin eigenvalue problem of (
1.1
) on
B(R)
becomes

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u-\nabla h\cdot\nabla u=\lambda u\quad&\text{in $B(R)$,}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\alpha u=0\quad&\text{on $\partial B(R)$,}\end{cases}

where
\nu
is the unit outer normal on
\partial B(R)
.

It is well known that the first nonzero Steklov eigenvalue of the Laplacian on
B(R)
is give by
1/R
. For the weighted Steklov eigenvalue problem (
1.4
), we obtain the following upper bound for
\sigma_{1}(B(R);\gamma_{h})
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\sigma_{1}(B(R);\gamma_{h})
be the first nonzero Steklov eigenvalue of (
2.1
). If
h^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
, then

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(B(R);\gamma_{h})\leq\frac{1}{R}.

Proof.

Recall that the first nonzero eigenvalue of (
2.1
) is characterized by the Rayleigh quotient

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(B(R);\gamma_{h})=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{B(R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\gamma_{h}}{\int_{\partial B(R)}u^{2}e^{h(|x|)}\,dA}:u\in W^{1,2}(B(R))\setminus\{0\},\;\;\int_{\partial B(R)}ue^{h(|x|)}\ dA=0\right\}.

For each
1\leq i\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(B(R);\gamma_{h})\int_{\partial B(R)}x_{i}^{2}e^{h(|x|)}\,dA\leq\int_{B(R)}|\nabla x_{i}|^{2}\,d\gamma_{h}

Summing over
i
yields

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(B(R);\gamma_{h})\leq\frac{\int_{B(R)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\nabla x_{i}|^{2}\,d\gamma_{h}}{\int_{\partial B(R)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}e^{h(|x|)}\,dA}=\frac{m\int_{0}^{R}e^{h(r)}r^{m-1}\,dr}{e^{h(R)}R^{m+1}}\leq\frac{m\int_{0}^{R}e^{h(R)}r^{m-1}\,dr}{e^{h(R)}R^{m+1}}=\frac{1}{R},

where we used the
h^{\prime}\geq 0
in the second inequality.

\square

We now use the separation of variables to study the Robin eigenvalue problem (
2.2
). Let

u(r,\theta)=w(r)T(\theta),\quad r\in(0,R),\quad\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1},

be a Robin eigenfunction on
B(R)
. Substituting into (
2.2
) gives

\displaystyle-w^{\prime\prime}(r)-(\frac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime}(r))w^{\prime}(r)-\frac{\Delta_{S_{r}}T(\theta)}{T(\theta)}w(r)=\lambda w(r),

which implies that the spherical part
T(\theta)
is an eigenfunction of Laplace–Beltrami operator on the distance sphere
S_{r}
.
Since
\lambda_{1,\alpha}(B(R);\gamma_{h})
is simple and
B(R)
is rotationally symmetric, the first Robin eigenfunction of (
2.2
) must be radial. Hence,
\lambda_{1,\alpha}(B(R);\gamma_{h})
is the first eigenvalue of radial problem

(2.4)

\begin{cases}w^{\prime\prime}+\left(\dfrac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime}(r)\right)w^{\prime}+\lambda w=0&\text{in }(0,R),\\
w^{\prime}(0)=0,\qquad w^{\prime}(R)+\alpha w(R)=0.\end{cases}

The second eigenvalue
\lambda_{2,\alpha}(B(R);\gamma_{h})
of (
2.2
) is either the second eigenvalue of (
2.4
) or the first eigenvalue of the problem

(2.5)

\begin{cases}w^{\prime\prime}+\left(\dfrac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime}(r)\right)w^{\prime}+(\lambda-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}})w=0&\text{in }(0,R),\\
w(0)=0,\qquad w^{\prime}(R)+\alpha w(R)=0.\end{cases}

Let
\mu_{1}
denote the first eigenvalue of (
2.5
) and let

g(r)
be a corresponding eigenfunction. Then
g
satisfies

(2.6)

\displaystyle g^{\prime\prime}+\left(\dfrac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime}\right)g^{\prime}+(\mu_{1}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}})g=0

for
r\in(0,R)
, with
g(0)=g^{\prime}(R)+\alpha g(R)=0
. Moreover,

\mu_{1}
admits the variational characterization

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}=\inf_{g\in W^{1,2}((0,R))}\left\{\frac{\int_{0}^{R}\left(g^{\prime}(r)^{2}+\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g^{2}\right)r^{m-1}e^{h(r)}\,dr+\alpha g^{2}(R)R^{m-1}e^{h(R)}}{\int_{0}^{R}g^{2}r^{m-1}e^{h(r)}\,dr}:g(0)=0\right\},

and the associated eigenfunction
g
can be chosen positive on
(0,R]
. In the following, we assume that the first eigenfunction
g(r)
of (
2.5
) is positive on
(0,R)
and satisfies
g^{\prime}(0)=1
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
g(r)
be the positive eigenfunction of (
2.5
). If
\alpha\leq 0
, then

(1)

g^{\prime}(r)>0
for
r\in(0,R)
.

(2)

Assume further that
\alpha\geq-\frac{2}{R}
, then
g^{\prime}(r)\geq-\alpha g(r)
for
r\in(0,R]
.

Proof.

(1) Set

N(r)=r^{m-1}e^{h(r)}g^{\prime}(r),\quad r\in[0,R].

Using (
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle N^{\prime}(r)=\left(\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}-\mu_{1}\right)r^{m-1}e^{h(r)}g(r).

Hence
N^{\prime}(r)
has at most one zero in
(0,R]
and is positive near
0
. Since
N(0)=0
, we have
N(r)>0
for sufficiently small
r
. If there exists
r\in(0,R)
such that

N(r)<0
, then there are points
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in(0,R)
with
N(\xi_{i})=0,i=1,2
. By Rolle’s theorem, there exist
\eta_{1}\in(0,\xi_{1})
and
\eta_{2}\in(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
such that
N^{\prime}(\eta_{i})=0,i=1,2
, a contradiction. Therefore,
N(r)\geq 0
for all
r\in(0,R)
. If
N(r_{0})=0
for some
r_{0}\in(0,R)
, then
N^{\prime}(r_{0})=0
, implying
\frac{m-1}{r_{0}^{2}}=\mu_{1}
. Consequently,
N^{{}^{\prime}}(r)<0
for
r>r_{0}
, a contradiction. Thus
N(r)>0
for all
r\in(0,R)
, and hence
g^{\prime}(r)>0
for
r\in(0,R)
.

(2) To prove (2), set
v(r)=g^{\prime}(r)/g(r)
. From (
2.5
) we obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle v^{\prime}+v^{2}+(\frac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime}(r))v+\left(\mu_{1}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}\right)=0,

with

(2.9)

\displaystyle v(R)=-\alpha,\quad v(r)>0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in(0,R],\quad\text{and}\quad\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}v(r)=+\infty.

We now prove that
v(r)\geq-\alpha
on
(0,R]
. Assume by contradiction that there exists
r_{0}\in(0,R)
such that

\displaystyle v^{\prime}(r_{0})=0,\text{\quad\quad}v^{\prime\prime}(r_{0})\geq 0,\text{\quad and\quad}0<v(r_{0})<-\alpha.

Differentiating (
2.8
) and using (
2.9
), we obtain at
r=r_{0}
that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=v^{\prime\prime}(r_{0})+(h^{\prime\prime}(r_{0})-\frac{m-1}{r_{0}^{2}})v(r_{0})+\frac{2(m-1)}{r_{0}^{3}}\\
&\geq(h^{\prime\prime}(r_{0})-\frac{m-1}{r_{0}^{2}})v(r_{0})+\frac{2(m-1)}{r_{0}^{3}}\\
&\geq-\frac{m-1}{r_{0}^{2}}v(r_{0})+\frac{2(m-1)}{r_{0}^{3}},\end{split}

where we used
h^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
in the last inequality. Thus it follows from (
2.10
) that

\displaystyle v(r_{0})\geq\frac{2}{r_{0}}>\frac{2}{R}.

Hence

-\alpha>v(r_{0})>\frac{2}{R},

contradicting the assumption
\alpha\geq-\frac{2}{R}
. Therefore, (2) is proved.

\square

We now establish the following properties of the second Robin eigenfunctions on round balls when
\alpha\leq 0
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
\alpha\leq 0
and
h^{\prime}(r)>0
. Then the second Robin eigenfunctions of (
2.2
) are given by

\displaystyle u_{i}(x)=g(r)\frac{x_{i}}{r},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,m,

where
g(r):[0,R]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
solves

(2.11)

g^{\prime\prime}(r)+(\frac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime}(r))g^{\prime}(r)+\left(\lambda_{2,\alpha}(B(R);\gamma_{h})-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}\right)g(r)=0

with boundary condition
g(0)=0
and
g^{\prime}(R)=-\alpha g(R)
. Here
\lambda_{2,\alpha}(B(R);\alpha)
denotes the second Robin eigenvalue of (
2.2
).

Proof.

Since the second eigenvalue
\lambda_{2,\alpha}(B(R);\gamma_{h})
of (
2.2
) is either the first eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
of (
2.5
)
or the second eigenvalue
\tau_{2}
of (
2.4
), it suffices to show

\displaystyle\mu_{1}<\tau_{2}.

Recall that an eigenfuction corresponding to the second eigenvalue of (
2.4
) must change sign in
(0,R)
. Without loss of generality, assume
f(r)
is positive in
(0,a)
for some
a<R
and
f(a)=0
. Then
f^{\prime}(a)<0
, and the same argument as in part (1) of Proposition
2.2
shows
f^{\prime}(r)<0
for
r\in(0,a)
.
Let
f(r)
be an eigenfunction of (
2.4
), i.e.

(2.12)

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}+\left(\dfrac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime}\right)f^{\prime}+\tau_{2}f=0

for
r\in(0,R)
, with
f^{\prime}(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(R)+\alpha f(R)=0
.
Differentiating (
2.12
) gives

(2.13)

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime\prime}+(\frac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime})f^{\prime\prime}+(\tau_{2}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}+h^{\prime\prime})f^{\prime}=0.

Set
\phi(r)=e^{h(r)}f^{\prime}(r)
, then

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(r)=e^{h(r)}f^{\prime\prime}+h^{\prime}\phi,

and

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=e^{h(r)}f^{\prime\prime\prime}+e^{h(r)}h^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}+h^{\prime\prime}\phi+h^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=e^{h}(-(\frac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime}(r))f^{\prime\prime}-(\tau_{2}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}+h^{\prime\prime})f^{\prime})+e^{h(r)}h^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}+h^{\prime\prime}\phi+h^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=e^{h}f^{\prime\prime}(-(\frac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime}(r))+h^{\prime})-(\tau_{2}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}+h^{\prime\prime})\phi+h^{\prime\prime}\phi+h^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(\phi^{\prime}(r)-h^{\prime}\phi(r))(-\frac{m-1}{r})-(\tau_{2}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}})\phi+h^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(-\frac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime})\phi^{\prime}-(\tau_{2}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}-\frac{m-1}{r}h^{\prime})\phi.

Thus, (
2.13
) implies

(2.14)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}+(\frac{m-1}{r}-h^{\prime})\phi^{\prime}+(\tau_{2}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}-\frac{m-1}{r}h^{\prime})\phi=0.

From (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=g^{\prime\prime}+\left(\dfrac{m-1}{r}+h^{\prime}\right)g^{\prime}+(\mu_{1}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}})g

\displaystyle=g^{\prime\prime}+(\frac{m-1}{r}-h^{\prime})g^{\prime}+(\mu_{1}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}-\frac{m-1}{r}h^{\prime})g+2h^{\prime}g^{\prime}+\frac{m-1}{r}h^{\prime}g.

and the facts
g(r)>0
,
h^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
and
g^{\prime}(r)>0
for
r\in(0,a)
, we obtain

(2.15)

\displaystyle 0\geq g^{\prime\prime}+(\frac{m-1}{r}-h^{{}^{\prime}})g^{\prime}+(\mu_{1}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}-\frac{m-1}{r}h^{\prime})g.

Because
f^{\prime}(r)<0
for
r\in(0,a)
, we have
\phi(r)<0
for
r\in(0,a)
. Multiplying (
2.15
) by
\phi
yields

(2.16)

\displaystyle 0\leq\phi g^{\prime\prime}+\phi g^{\prime}(\frac{m-1}{r}-h^{\prime})+(\mu_{1}-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}-\frac{m-1}{r}h^{\prime})\phi g.

Subtracting equation (
2.14
) multiplied by
g

from (
2.16
) gives

(2.17)

\displaystyle 0\leq\phi g^{\prime\prime}-g\phi^{\prime\prime}+(\frac{m-1}{r}-h^{\prime})(g^{\prime}\phi-g\phi^{\prime})+(\mu_{1}-\tau_{2})g\phi.

Multiplying (
2.17
) by
r^{m-1}e^{-h}
and integrating over
(0,a)
, we obtain

(2.18)

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{0}^{a}(\phi g^{\prime\prime}-g\phi^{\prime\prime}+(\frac{m-1}{r}-h^{\prime})(g^{\prime}\phi-g\phi^{\prime})+(\mu_{1}-\tau_{2})g\phi)r^{m-1}e^{-h(r)}dr.

Direct computation yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}(\phi g^{\prime\prime}-g\phi^{\prime\prime})r^{m-1}e^{-h(r)}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}(\phi g^{\prime}r^{m-1}e^{-h(r)})^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}g^{\prime}r^{m-1}e^{-h}-(\frac{m-1}{r}-h^{\prime})\phi g^{\prime}r^{m-1}e^{-h}dr

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{a}(g\phi^{\prime}r^{m-1}e^{-h(r)})^{\prime}dr+\int_{0}^{a}g^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}r^{m-1}e^{-h}dr+\int_{0}^{a}(\frac{m-1}{r}-h^{\prime})g\phi^{{}^{\prime}}dr,

and therefore

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{a}(\phi g^{\prime\prime}-\phi^{\prime\prime}g)r^{m-1}e^{-h(r)}dr\\
=&(\phi g^{\prime}-g\phi^{\prime})r^{m-1}e^{-h}\Big|_{0}^{a}+\int_{0}^{a}(\frac{m-1}{r}-h^{\prime})(g\phi^{\prime}-\phi g^{\prime})r^{m-1}e^{h}dr.\end{split}

Substituting (
2.19
) into (
2.18
) yields

(2.20)

\displaystyle 0\leq(\phi g^{\prime}-g\phi^{\prime})r^{m-1}e^{-h}\Big|_{0}^{a}+\int_{0}^{a}(\mu_{1}-\tau_{2})g\phi r^{m-1}e^{-h(r)}dr.

Since
g(0)=0
and
\phi(0)=e^{h(0)}f^{\prime}(0)=0
, we have

\displaystyle(\phi g^{\prime}-g\phi^{\prime})r^{m-1}e^{-h}\Big|_{0}^{a}=\phi(a)g^{\prime}(a)-g(a)\phi^{\prime}(a).

Recall that
g(a)>0
,
g^{\prime}(a)>0
and
\phi(a)=e^{h(a)}f^{\prime}(a)<0
. From (
2.12
), we obtain

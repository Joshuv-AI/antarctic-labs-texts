A remark on the second order estimates for the quaternionic Calabi-Yau problem on hyperkähler manifolds Thanks: This work was supported by GNSAGA of INdAM and by the PRIN project “Differential-geometric aspects of manifolds via Global Analysis” 20225J97H5
Giovanni Gentili and Luigi Vezzoni

Abstract
. We revisit the second order estimate for solutions to the quaternionic Calabi–Yau problem on hyperkähler manifolds, originally established by Dinew and Sroka in [ 6 ] . In this note, we present a simplified argument to derive the estimate.

1.
Introduction

The present paper focuses on the Calabi-Yau problem in hypercomplex geometry. The problem was proposed by Alesker and Verbitsky
[
3
]
as a natural adaptation of the classical Calabi-Yau problem to hypercomplex geometry and it has been the research topic of several papers (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
and the references therein).

Given a compact hyperhermitian manifold
(M,I,J,K,\Omega)
and a smooth function
F
on
M
, the
hypercomplex Calabi-Yau problem
consists in finding
(\varphi,b)\in C^{\infty}(M)\times\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

(\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi)^{n}={\rm e}^{F+b}\Omega^{n}\,,\quad\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi>0\,,\quad{\rm sup}_{M}\varphi=0\,.

Geometric applications of the problem are mostly relevant in the HKT case, i.e. when
\partial\Omega=0

[
9
,
10
]
, since in this case the problem is strictly related to the seek of canonical hyperhermitian metrics. Indeed, in the HKT case under the assumption that the canonical bundle of
(M,I)
is holomorphically trivial, the solvability of the problem would imply that in the quaternionic Bott-Chern class
[\Omega]_{\partial\partial_{J}}
there is always a unique
balanced HKT
metric
[
21
]
. Such a metric is necessarily
Chern-Ricci flat

[
5
]
.

The problem has at most one solution
(\varphi,b)
and (
1
) is usually called
quaternionic Monge-Ampère equation
. The solvability of the problem in its full generality is still an open question, but some important advances have been established in the literature.

According to the strategy of Yau to prove the Calabi conjecture
[
22
]
, the natural approach for solving the problem is via the continuity method.
The
C^{0}
estimate is proved
[
3
,
2
,
13
,
14
]
, while from
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
it follows that, in order to solve the problem, it is enough to show an upper bound on the Chern Laplacian of the solution.

This last estimate was proved by Alesker on flat hyperkähler manifolds
[
1
]
and more recently by Dinew and Sroka on hyperkähler manifolds
[
6
]
.

The purpose of the present note is to present a simplified approach for proving the second order estimate of Dinew and Sroka.
Our key observation, Lemma
3.2
below, is that if
\hat{g}
is an hyperkähler metric, then

{g}^{r\bar{s}}\hat{R}_{r\bar{s}i\bar{j}}=0

for
every hyperhermitian metric

g
on
M
, where
\hat{R}
is the curvature of
\hat{g}
. This allows us to adopt a simple approach to prove the estimate.
We already used the same observation in the parabolic case in
[
5
]
.

More in detail, with respect to
[
6
]
, we use a different test function
Q
, defined in (
14
) below, in the maximum principle argument. Our choice yields fewer terms in the computations thus allowing us to avoid the usage of the critical equation (
dQ=0
at a maximum point). Finally, in our argument we reinterpret the vanishing of certain terms by inspecting explicitly some properties of the curvature of the hyperkähler metric, whereas in
[
6
]
this was achieved somewhat implicitly. See Remark
4.2
for more details on this.

Notation.
In this paper, we adopt the following notation: all tensor components are expressed with respect to complex coordinates that are holomorphic with respect to the first complex structure
I
. We use a comma in the indices to denote partial derivatives. When differentiating functions, the comma is omitted.

2.
Preliminaries

A hyperhermitian manifold is a smooth manifold of real dimension
4n
equipped with a triple of complex structures
I,J,K
satisfying the quaternionic relations

(2)

IJ=-JI=K\,.

Examples of hyperhermitian manifolds are given by: the
quaternionic vector space

\mathbb{H}^{n}
, tori
\mathbb{H}^{n}/\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is a lattice in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
; Hopf manifolds of the form
((\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})\backslash\{(0,0)\})/\langle r\rangle
, where
r
is a real number with
0<r<1
, and
\langle r\rangle
is the group generated by the action

(z_{\alpha},w_{\alpha})\mapsto(r{\rm e}^{i\vartheta_{\alpha}}z_{\alpha},r{\rm e}^{-i\vartheta_{\alpha}}w_{\alpha})

and
\vartheta_{1},\dots,\vartheta_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
.

A fundamental tool in hypercomplex geometry is the so-called
Obata connection
introduced in
[
11
]
as the unique torsion-free affine connection
\nabla
which preserves all the three complex structures. This connection can be used to detect important properties of the hypercomplex structure. For instance it is flat if and only if the manifold is locally isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. That implies strong restrictions on the geometry of the manifold since it turns out to be affine
[
12
]
. Moreover the condition
{\rm Hol}(\nabla)\subseteq{\rm SL}(n,\mathbb{H})
implies that each complex structure of the hyperhermitian structure has holomorphically trivial canonical bundle.

A hyperhermitian metric on a hypercomplex manifold
(M,I,J,K)
is a Riemannian metric
g
which is compatible with each complex structure, i.e.

g(I\cdot,I\cdot)=g(J\cdot,J\cdot)=g(K\cdot,K\cdot)\,.

The metric
g
induces the three fundamental forms

\omega(\cdot,\cdot):=g(I\cdot,\cdot)\,,\quad\omega_{J}(\cdot,\cdot):=g(J\cdot,\cdot)\,,\quad\omega_{K}(\cdot,\cdot):=g(K\cdot,\cdot)\,.

The quaternionic relations (
2
) imply that the
2
-form

\Omega:=\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{J}+i\omega_{K})

is of type
(2,0)
with respect to
I
and it is easy to see that satisfies the
q-real
condition

\Omega(J\cdot,J\cdot)=\overline{\Omega(\cdot,\cdot)\,.}

Furthermore
\Omega
is
positive
, in the sense that

\Omega(Z,J\bar{Z})>0

for every non-zero vector field
Z
of type
(1,0)
with respect to
I
. Vice versa, every
\Omega\in\Lambda^{2,0}_{I}
which is
q
-real and positive induces a hyperhermitian metric
g
via the formula

g=2\mathrm{Re}(\Omega(\cdot,J\cdot))\,.

On the other hand the form
\omega
is a positive real form of type
(1,1)
with respect to
I
and it satisfies the
J
-anti-invariant relation

\omega(J\cdot,J\cdot)=-\omega(\cdot,\cdot)\,.

Vice versa, if
\omega\in\Lambda^{1,1}_{I}
is real positive and
J
-anti-invariant the induced Hermitian metric is hyperhermitian with respect to
(I,J,K)
. Hence the following three spaces are in bijective correspondence

•

hyperhermitian metrics;

•

q
-real positive forms in
\Lambda^{2,0}_{I}
;

•

real positive
J
-anti-invariant forms in
\Lambda^{1,1}_{I}
.

A hyperhermitian metric is hyperkähler if and only if the induced
\Omega
is closed. In this case the three Bismut connections induced by
(g,I)
,
(g,J)
and
(g,K)
agree with the Levi-Civita connection of
g
. In
[
10
]
Howe and Papadopoulos generalized the notion of hyperkähler manifold to geometry with torsion by introducing
HKT manifolds
. An HKT manifold can be defined as a hyperhermitian manifold
(M,I,J,K,g)

whose quaternionic form
\Omega
satisfies

\partial\Omega=0\,,

where
\partial
is with respect to
I
(this is in fact a characterization given in
[
9
]
).

In hypercomplex geometry the role that the
\partial\bar{\partial}
operator plays in complex geometry is taken by the operator
\partial\partial_{J}
, where
\partial_{J}:=J^{-1}\bar{\partial}J\colon\Lambda^{r,0}_{I}\to\Lambda^{r+1,0}_{I}
.
A smooth function
\varphi
on
(M,I,J,K,\Omega)
is
\Omega
-plurisubharmonic
if
\Omega_{\varphi}:=\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi
is
q
-positive. In such a case
\Omega_{\varphi}
gives a hyperhermitian metric on
(M,I,J,K)
. The
I
-fundamental form
\omega_{\varphi}
of the metric induced by
\Omega_{\varphi}
takes the following expression

(3)

\omega_{\varphi}:=\omega+\frac{1}{2}\left(i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi-iJ\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\right)

and, as observed in
[
6
, Section 2.3]
, problem (
1
) is equivalent to the following equation stated in terms of
\omega_{\varphi}
:

(4)

\omega_{\varphi}^{2n}={\rm e}^{2F+2b}\omega^{2n}\,,\quad\omega_{\varphi}>0\,,\quad{\rm sup}_{M}\varphi=0\,.

Note that if
\Omega
is HKT, then
\Omega_{\varphi}
is HKT for every positive
\varphi
and the class

[\Omega]_{\partial\partial_{J}}:=\{\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}f\,\,:\,\,f\in C^{\infty}(M)\}

plays the role that the Bott-Chern cohomology class of
\omega
plays in Kähler geometry.

3.
Hyperhermitian metrics on hyperkähler manifolds

Let
(M,I,J,K)
be a hyperhermitian manifold and denote by
R
the curvature of the Obata connection
\nabla
. Since
\nabla
is torsion-free and preserves
I
we can find
I
-holomorphic coordinates
\{z^{r}\}
around
x_{0}
that are geodesic with respect to
\nabla
at
x_{0}
. Let

R(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{[X,Y]}

be the curvature endomorphisms, then we denote the components of the curvature as

R\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}^{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}^{l}}=R_{\bar{j}k\bar{l}}{}^{\bar{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}^{i}}\,.

The next useful proposition follows from
[
6
, Remark 2.13]
:

Proposition 3.1
.

With respect to
\{z^{r}\}
we have

(5)

J_{r,k}^{\bar{s}}=J^{\bar{s}}_{k,r}\,.

Furthermore

(6)

\displaystyle J_{r,k}^{\bar{s}}=J_{r,\bar{k}}^{\bar{s}}=0\,,

(7)

\displaystyle J_{\bar{j},k\bar{l}}^{s}J_{s}^{\bar{i}}=-J_{\bar{j}}^{s}J_{s,k\bar{l}}^{\bar{i}}\,,

(8)

\displaystyle R_{\bar{j}k\bar{l}}\,^{\bar{i}}=J_{a}^{\bar{i}}J_{\bar{l},k\bar{j}}^{a}

at
x_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
\nabla
is torsion-free and preserves
I
the mixed covariant derivatives
\nabla_{\bar{r}}\partial_{z^{s}}
vanish and we have

0=(\nabla_{k}J)\partial_{z^{r}}=\left(J_{s}^{\bar{m}}\Gamma_{kr}^{s}-J_{r,k}^{\bar{m}}\right)\partial_{\bar{z}^{m}}

which implies

J_{r,k}^{\bar{m}}=J_{s}^{\bar{m}}\Gamma_{kr}^{s}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\Gamma_{kl}^{i}=-J_{\bar{m}}^{i}J_{l,k}^{\bar{m}}\,.

Since
\Gamma_{kl}^{i}=\Gamma_{lk}^{i}
, then (
5
) follows.

Since the Christoffel symbols of
\nabla
vanish at
x_{0}
, the first derivatives of
J_{r}^{\bar{s}}
vanish at
x_{0}
and (
6
) follows.

To prove (
7
) we just compute

0=(J_{\bar{j}}^{s}J_{s}^{\bar{i}})_{k\bar{l}}=J_{\bar{j},k\bar{l}}^{s}J_{s}^{\bar{i}}+J_{\bar{j}}^{s}J_{s,k\bar{l}}^{\bar{i}}

at
x_{0}
.

For the last formula we observe

R_{\bar{j}k\bar{l}}\,^{\bar{i}}=-\Gamma_{\bar{j}\bar{l},k}^{\bar{i}}=\partial_{k}(J_{a}^{\bar{i}}J_{\bar{l},\bar{j}}^{a})=J_{a}^{\bar{i}}J_{\bar{l},k\bar{j}}^{a}

at
x_{0}
, and the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
g
be a hyperhermitian metric. Then

(9)

g^{i\bar{j}}R_{\bar{j}i\bar{q}}\,^{\bar{a}}=0

at
x_{0}
.
In particular if
\hat{g}
is hyperkähler, then

g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}_{i\bar{k}}R_{\bar{s}r\bar{j}}\,^{\bar{k}}=0\,,

for every hyperhermitian metric
g
on
(M,I,J,K)
.

Proof.

We compute using Proposition
3.1

g^{i\bar{j}}R_{\bar{j}i\bar{q}}\,^{\bar{a}}=g^{i\bar{j}}J_{b}^{\bar{a}}J^{b}_{\bar{q},i\bar{j}}=g^{i\bar{j}}J_{b}^{\bar{a}}J^{b}_{\bar{j},i\bar{q}}=-g^{i\bar{j}}J_{b,i\bar{q}}^{\bar{a}}J^{b}_{\bar{j}}\,,

where we used, in order, (
8
), (
5
), (
7
). Now, since
g
is hyperhermitian we have
g^{i\bar{j}}=g^{r\bar{s}}J_{r}^{\bar{j}}J_{\bar{s}}^{i}
and thus

g^{i\bar{j}}R_{\bar{j}i\bar{q}}\,^{\bar{a}}=-g^{r\bar{s}}J_{r}^{\bar{j}}J_{\bar{s}}^{i}J_{b,i\bar{q}}^{\bar{a}}J^{b}_{\bar{j}}\\
=g^{b\bar{s}}J_{\bar{s}}^{i}J_{b,i\bar{q}}^{\bar{a}}=g^{b\bar{s}}J_{\bar{s}}^{i}J_{i,b\bar{q}}^{\bar{a}}=-g^{b\bar{s}}J_{\bar{s},b\bar{q}}^{i}J_{i}^{\bar{a}}=-g^{b\bar{s}}J_{\bar{q},b\bar{s}}^{i}J_{i}^{\bar{a}}=-g^{b\bar{s}}R_{\bar{s}b\bar{q}}\,^{\bar{a}}

where, starting from the third equality, we used (
5
), (
7
), (
5
), and finally (
8
) again. The identity (
9
) is proved. The last sentence follows since if
\hat{g}
is hyperkähler, then
R
is the curvature of
\hat{g}
and

g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}_{i\bar{k}}R_{\bar{s}r\bar{j}}\,^{\bar{k}}=g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}_{r\bar{k}}R_{\bar{j}i\bar{s}}\,^{k}\,.\qed

Remark 3.3
.

From the proof of Lemma
3.2
it is clear that (
9
) still holds if instead of tracing with respect to a hyperhermitian metric we trace with respect to a hyperhermitian symmetric tensor. Also, an alternative perspective of Lemma
3.2
is as follows. Let
g
be a hyperhermitian metric and
\{Z_{1},\dots,Z_{2n}\}
an orthonormal frame of type
(1,0)
with respect to
I
that is
J
-adapted, namely
Z_{2i}=J\bar{Z}_{2i-1}
. Let

R(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{[X,Y]}

be the curvature endomorphisms. Then the trace in (
9
) is essentially

\sum_{j=1}^{2n}R(Z_{j},\bar{Z}_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\left(R(Z_{j},\bar{Z}_{j})+R(J\bar{Z}_{j},JZ_{j})\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\left(R(Z_{j},\bar{Z}_{j})-R(JZ_{j},J\bar{Z}_{j})\right)=0\,,

where the last equality is due to the identity
R(JX,JY)=R(X,Y)
.

The following lemma will be useful

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\hat{g}
be a hyperkähler metric and let
g
be a hyperhermitian metric. Then the following formulas hold

(10)

\displaystyle g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}J_{r,i\bar{j}}^{\bar{a}}J_{\bar{s}}^{b}=0\,,

(11)

\displaystyle g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}J_{r}^{\bar{a}}J_{\bar{s},i\bar{j}}^{b}=0\,,

(12)

\displaystyle g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}J_{i}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j},r\bar{s}}^{a}=0\,,

(13)

\displaystyle g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}J_{i,r\bar{s}}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j}}^{a}=0

at
x_{0}
.

Proof.

Using, hyperhermitianity of
\hat{g}
and
J^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}
we have

g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}J_{r,i\bar{j}}^{\bar{a}}J_{\bar{s}}^{b}=g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{p\bar{q}}J_{p}^{\bar{s}}J_{\bar{q}}^{r}J_{r,i\bar{j}}^{\bar{a}}J_{\bar{s}}^{b}=-g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{b\bar{q}}J_{\bar{q}}^{r}J_{r,i\bar{j}}^{\bar{a}}=g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{b\bar{q}}R_{i\bar{j}\bar{q}}\,\!^{\bar{a}}=0

at
x_{0}
, where the second-to-last equality is due to (
7
) and (
8
), while the last step follows from Lemma
3.2
. Similarly, but using hyperhermitianity of
g
, we deduce

g^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}J_{i}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j},r\bar{s}}^{a}=g^{p\bar{q}}J_{p}^{\bar{j}}J_{\bar{q}}^{i}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}J_{i}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j},r\bar{s}}^{a}=-g^{p\bar{b}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}J_{p}^{\bar{j}}J_{\bar{j},r\bar{s}}^{a}=g^{p\bar{b}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}R_{\bar{s}rp}\,^{a}=0

at
x_{0}
. Hence (
10
) and (
12
) follow. The other two relations can be obtained by conjugation.
∎

4.
A proof of the second order estimate

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a solution to (
1
), then
\hat{\Delta}\varphi:=\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}\varphi_{r\bar{s}}
has an a priori upper
bound.

Proof.

Let

(14)

Q:=\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}-A\varphi\,,

where
A
is a constant to be chosen later.

Denote
\Delta_{\varphi}f:=g_{\varphi}^{r\bar{s}}f_{r\bar{s}}
the Chern-Laplacian with respect to
g_{\varphi}
. We have

\Delta_{\varphi}Q=\Delta_{\varphi}\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}-A\Delta_{\varphi}\varphi=\Delta_{\varphi}\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}-2nA+A\mathrm{tr}_{g_{\varphi}}g\,.

Assume that
Q
achieves a maximum at
x_{0}\in M
and consider
I
-holomorphic coordinates around
x_{0}
such that

\hat{g}_{r\bar{s},k}=J_{r,k}^{\bar{s}}=J_{r,\bar{k}}^{\bar{s}}=0

at
x_{0}
. To find such coordinates simply take normal coordinates for the hyperkähler metric
\hat{g}
at
x_{0}
. Indeed, since the Levi-Civita connection of
\hat{g}
coincides with the Obata connection it is clear from Proposition
3.1
that (
6
) holds at
x_{0}
. Then we directly compute at
x_{0}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varphi}\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\left(((\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}})_{,i\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,r\bar{s}}+\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}g_{\varphi\,r\bar{s},i\bar{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=-g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{a\bar{s}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{b}}\hat{g}_{a\bar{b},i\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,r\bar{s}}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}\left(2g_{r\bar{s},i\bar{j}}+\varphi_{r\bar{s}i\bar{j}}+J^{\bar{a}}_{r}J^{b}_{\bar{s}}\varphi_{b\bar{a}i\bar{j}}+J_{r,i\bar{j}}^{\bar{a}}J_{\bar{s}}^{b}\varphi_{b\bar{a}}+J_{r}^{\bar{a}}J_{\bar{s},i\bar{j}}^{b}\varphi_{b\bar{a}}\right)

\displaystyle=g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{a\bar{s}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{b}}\hat{R}_{a\bar{b}i\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,r\bar{s}}+g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}\left(g_{r\bar{s},i\bar{j}}+\varphi_{r\bar{s}i\bar{j}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}\left(J_{r,i\bar{j}}^{\bar{a}}J_{\bar{s}}^{b}+J_{r}^{\bar{a}}J_{\bar{s},i\bar{j}}^{b}\right)\varphi_{b\bar{a}}\,,

where
\hat{R}_{a\bar{b}i\bar{j}}=\hat{g}_{k\bar{j}}R_{a\bar{b}i}\,^{k}
and we used the fact that
\hat{g}
is
J
-Hermitian, so that
\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}J_{r}^{\bar{a}}J_{\bar{s}}^{b}=\hat{g}^{b\bar{a}}
. Hence Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
imply

(15)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varphi}\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}=g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}\left(g_{r\bar{s},i\bar{j}}+\varphi_{r\bar{s}i\bar{j}}\right)\,.

Next we use equation (
1
) to cancel the fourth order derivatives of
\varphi
in (
15
). Using (
3
) and (
4
) we have

F+b=\log\frac{(\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi)^{n}}{\Omega^{n}}=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{(\omega+\tfrac{1}{2}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi-\tfrac{1}{2}iJ\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{2n}}{\omega^{2n}}

and

\displaystyle\partial_{r}F

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,i\bar{j},r}-g^{i\bar{j}}g_{i\bar{j},r}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\left(g_{i\bar{j},r}+\tfrac{1}{2}\varphi_{ri\bar{j}}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{i}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j}}^{a}\varphi_{ra\bar{b}}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{i}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j},r}^{a}\varphi_{ra\bar{b}}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{i,r}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j}}^{a}\varphi_{a\bar{b}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}g^{i\bar{j}}g_{i\bar{j},r}

as well as

\partial_{r}\partial_{\bar{s}}F=-\frac{1}{2}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{b}}g_{\varphi}^{a\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,a\bar{b},\bar{s}}g_{\varphi\,i\bar{j},r}\\
+\frac{1}{2}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}(g_{i\bar{j},r\bar{s}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\varphi_{r\bar{s}i\bar{j}}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{i}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j}}^{a}\varphi_{r\bar{s}a\bar{b}}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{i}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j},r\bar{s}}^{a}\varphi_{ra\bar{b}}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{i,r\bar{s}}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j}}^{a}\varphi_{a\bar{b}})-H_{r\bar{s}}\,,

where
H_{r\bar{s}}
only depends on
g
. It follows

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\partial_{\bar{s}}F=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{b}}g_{\varphi}^{a\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,a\bar{b},\bar{s}}g_{\varphi\,i\bar{j},r}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}(g_{i\bar{j},r\bar{s}}+\varphi_{r\bar{s}i\bar{j}}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{i}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j},r\bar{s}}^{a}\varphi_{ra\bar{b}}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{i,r\bar{s}}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j}}^{a}\varphi_{a\bar{b}})-H_{r\bar{s}}

and

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}F=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{b}}g_{\varphi}^{a\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,a\bar{b},\bar{s}}g_{\varphi\,i\bar{j},r}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}(g_{i\bar{j},r\bar{s}}+\varphi_{r\bar{s}i\bar{j}}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{i}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j},r\bar{s}}^{a}\varphi_{ra\bar{b}}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{i,r\bar{s}}^{\bar{b}}J_{\bar{j}}^{a}\varphi_{a\bar{b}})-\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}H_{r\bar{s}}\,.

Hence Lemma
3.4
implies

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}F=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{b}}g_{\varphi}^{a\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,a\bar{b},\bar{s}}g_{\varphi\,i\bar{j},r}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}(g_{i\bar{j},r\bar{s}}+\varphi_{r\bar{s}i\bar{j}})-\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}H_{r\bar{s}}\,.

We can then write

\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\varphi_{r\bar{s}i\bar{j}}=\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{b}}g_{\varphi}^{a\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,a\bar{b},\bar{s}}g_{\varphi\,i\bar{j},r}-\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}g_{i\bar{j},r\bar{s}}+h\,,

where
h
is a smooth function on
M
which does not depend on
\varphi
. Thus (

) becomes

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varphi}\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}=\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{b}}g_{\varphi}^{a\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,a\bar{b},\bar{s}}g_{\varphi\,i\bar{j},r}+g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}\left(g_{r\bar{s},i\bar{j}}-g_{i\bar{j},r\bar{s}}\right)+h\,.

Condition

0\geq\Delta_{\varphi}\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}-2nA+A\mathrm{tr}_{g_{\varphi}}g\quad\mbox{ at }x_{0}

reads as

0\geq\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{b}}g_{\varphi}^{a\bar{j}}g_{\varphi\,a\bar{b},\bar{s}}g_{\varphi\,i\bar{j},r}+g_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\hat{g}^{r\bar{s}}\left(g_{r\bar{s},i\bar{j}}-g_{i\bar{j},r\bar{s}}\right)-2nA+A\mathrm{tr}_{g_{\varphi}}g+h

which implies, after dropping the non-negative quadratic term (the first term in the inequality above),

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle-C{\rm tr}_{g_{\varphi}}\hat{g}+A\mathrm{tr}_{g_{\varphi}}g-C-2nA\quad\mbox{ at }x_{0}\,,

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
g
and
h
only.
Using

\mathrm{tr}_{g_{\varphi}}g\geq A^{\prime}\,\mathrm{tr}_{g_{\varphi}}\hat{g}\,,

which holds for a positive constant

depending on
g
and
\hat{g}
only,
we have

0\geq(AA^{\prime}-C){\rm tr}_{g_{\varphi}}\hat{g}-C-2nA\quad\mbox{ at }x_{0}\,.

Hence by taking

we obtain

{\rm tr}_{g_{\varphi}}\hat{g}\leq C\quad\mbox{ at }x_{0}\,.

Now

{\rm tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}\leq\frac{1}{(2n-1)!}({\rm tr}_{g_{\varphi}}\hat{g})^{2n-1}\frac{\omega^{2n}_{\varphi}}{\hat{\omega}^{2n}}

implies

{\rm tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}\leq C\quad\mbox{ at }x_{0}\,.

It follows

\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}-A\varphi=Q\leq Q(x_{0})=\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}}g_{\varphi}(x_{0})-A\varphi(x_{0})\leq C+A\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}}\,,

and since
\varphi
satisfies an a priori
C^{0}

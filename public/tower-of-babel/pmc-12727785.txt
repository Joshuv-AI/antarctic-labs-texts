Coupling between water resource constraints and agricultural sustainability in Henan Province, China
Authors: Yang Yang, Wei Chang, Anjing Qi, Mingli Yan, Zhaoguang Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aligns with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 2: Zero Hunger; SDG 6: Clean Water and Sanitation). Based on the industrial development and the DPSR (Drivers-Pressures-State-Responses) theory, 43 evaluation indicators were selected. Utilizing models including the Comprehensive Development Index, the Coupling Coordination Degree, and the Grey Relational Analysis, this study investigates the coupled coordination association level of agricultural economic development and water resources exploitation and utilization in Henan Province and its 17 prefecture-level cities over nearly two decades. The findings reveal that: (1) Over the past 20 years, both the comprehensive development index for the two systems have shown an upward trend. The growth of the agricultural economic development is primarily driven by Factor Input, while the level of water resources exploitation and utilization is chiefly influenced by Drivers. (2) Over the past two decades, the coupling coordination degree between the two systems also exhibited an upward trend. However, the asynchronous development of the two systems in eight cities resulted in unstable coordination. (3) Variables related to farmer welfare exhibited relatively high rankings in their influence on water resources exploitation and utilization, whereas variables associated with water resources endowment showed the lowest influence on agricultural economic development.

Introduction

Agricultural economy plays a crucial role in regional development and livelihoods1. However, water scarcity, pollution, and inefficient use have become critical constraints on agricultural sustainability2. To address these challenges, the Chinese government has prioritized water resources management in recent policy directives. For instance, the 2024 Central No. 1 Document mandated stringent water conservation measures and groundwater over-extraction control3, while the 2025 policy further emphasized reforms in agricultural water pricing and rights, alongside promoting water-saving irrigation technologies4. These efforts aim to balance agricultural growth with water sustainability. Therefore, studying the interplay between agricultural economic development and water resources is of both theoretical and practical significance.

In recent years, academic research on the relationship between water resources and the agricultural economy can primarily be divided into three aspects. The first aspect concerns the demand analysis of water resources by the agricultural economy. As agriculture represents the largest sector of water consumption, agricultural production directly impacts the demand patterns for water resources5. Studies have shown that factors such as agricultural expansion, changes in crop structure, and innovations in irrigation technology significantly influence agricultural water demand, while the level of economic development, policy regulation, and water pricing mechanisms play crucial roles in regulating water use behavior and optimizing resource allocation6–9. The second aspect involves the support and constraints of water resources on the agricultural economy. Water resources are not only fundamental to agricultural production but also the foundation for the development of the agricultural economy10. Numerous studies indicate that the availability and quality of water resources directly affect agricultural production. Water scarcity and water quality deterioration not only result in reduced crop yields and increased agricultural costs but may also trigger extensive economic and social problems11,12. The third aspect focuses on the coordination between water resources utilization and the agricultural economy. The coordinated development of water resources utilization and the agricultural economy is the basis for sustainable agricultural economic growth13. Scholars have established composite systems such as “agricultural water resources and the socio-economy,” “agricultural water–food–energy,” and “land-water-food " to explore the coordination between water resources and the agricultural economy. However, due to differences in study areas, methods, and data scales, research conclusions vary or even contradict each other14,15. Based on the above analysis, although diversified research fields and perspectives on the relationship between agricultural economic development and water resources have been explored both domestically and internationally, there is relatively little research on the synergistic development relationship between agricultural economic development and water resources, and this research is insufficiently deep, particularly in terms of spatial and temporal dimensions. However, at this stage, China is facing the arduous task of coordinating economic growth with resources and environmental development, while ensuring sustainable use of water resources16.

Henan Province is located in the Huang-Huai-Hai Plain and falls within a transitional monsoon climate zone17. Boasting abundant light and heat resources as well as fertile land, it is one of the most agriculturally productive regions in China, holding a significant position particularly in grain production17. With the rapid development of the agricultural economy and the continuous growth of irrigation demands, agricultural water consumption has increased significantly17. Meanwhile, the temporal and spatial distribution of precipitation in the region is uneven, with frequent seasonal and regional droughts17. Coupled with the impacts of global climate change, the uncertainty of water resources and the contradiction between water supply and demand have been further exacerbated18. In some areas, the pressure on water resources is becoming increasingly prominent, which has emerged as a crucial factor constraining the sustainable development of agriculture19. Given this context, this paper focuses on Henan Province and its 17 prefecture-level cities as the focus of research, based on economic development and DPSR (Driving forces, Pressures, State, Responses) theory. It utilizes methods such as the comprehensive development index, coupling coordination degree, and grey relational degree to study the coordination and grey relational analysis between agricultural economic development and water resources exploitation and utilization levels over the past two decades in Henan Province. This study focuses on regions that are characterized by both high-pressure hydrological conditions and being major grain-producing areas. It aims to provide scientific theoretical foundations and practical guidance for the sustainable development of agricultural economy as well as the rational and efficient utilization of water resources in these regions.

Materials and methods

Study area

Henan Province, situated in the Huang-Huai-Hai Plain, administers 17 prefecture-level cities and covers a total area of approximately 167,000 square kilometers. In 2023, the province’s total grain output reached 66.243 million tons, of which wheat production accounted for 35.497 million tons, constituting 25.99% of the national total wheat output. The added value of the primary industry amounted to 536.015 billion yuan20. As a major agricultural province of national significance, Henan boasts relatively abundant light and heat resources as well as high-quality arable land conditions, endowing it with immense agricultural production potential17. From a hydrological perspective, Henan Province is characterized by a transitional monsoon climate. Although its total water resources are relatively abundant, their temporal and spatial distribution is extremely uneven. Precipitation is mainly concentrated during the flood season (from April to September), which is prone to triggering flood disasters, with the most intense precipitation occurring in July. Additionally, there are significant regional disparities in water resources distribution, with alternating occurrences of droughts and floods, posing severe challenges to agricultural production and water resources management. Currently, the province is facing varying degrees of water resources shortages across various sectors, including domestic, ecological, industrial, and agricultural uses. Agricultural water use accounts for more than half of the province’s total annual water consumption21. Under the dual influences of global climate change and socio-economic development, the contradiction between the growing demand for agricultural water and the insufficient supply of available water resources has become increasingly prominent22. Evaluation index system for agricultural economic development level.

(1) Economic scale is an aggregate indicator reflecting the level of agricultural economic development. Generally speaking, the larger the scale, the higher the level of agricultural economic development. This paper selects the following indicators to measure it: per capita agricultural output value, total agricultural output value, the proportion of primary industry in GDP, and fixed asset investment in the primary industry.

(2) Industrial structure refers to the composition and proportional relationship of agricultural industries. It is measured by the proportions of planting, forestry, animal husbandry, fishery, and agricultural, forestry, animal husbandry, and fishery services.

(3) Factor input refers to the various basic resources used in agricultural production activities to create economic value. The greater the input, the higher the level of agricultural economic development. This paper selects the following indicators to measure it: actual cultivated land area per year, number of rural employees, agricultural water usage per mu, total agricultural machinery power per mu, pesticide usage per mu, fertilizer usage per mu, plastic film usage per mu, and diesel usage per mu.

(4) Farmer welfare refers to the improvement of farmers’ quality of life. This paper selects the following indicators to measure it: per capita disposable income of rural residents, per capita living consumption expenditure of rural residents, Engel’s coefficient of rural residents, and per capita electricity consumption of rural residents.

Indicators of water resources exploitation and utilization level

Water resources refer to water sources that are usable or have the potential to be utilized. Water resources are vital for agricultural production and exhibit complexity, interconnectedness, and indivisibility. The Drivers-Pressures-State-Responses (DPSR) framework outlines a causal chain between the origins and outcomes of environmental issues, encompassing four elements: drivers, pressures, state, and responses. Based on this theory and referencing relevant studies28–32, this paper selects 22 indicators across the categories of drivers, pressures, state, and responses to measure the level of water resources exploitation and utilization (see Table 2).

(1) Drivers refer to the underlying causes affecting the level of water resources exploitation and utilization. This paper considers population growth and economic development, selecting total population, urbanization rate, total GDP, primary sector value added, secondary sector value added, tertiary sector value added, and per capita GDP as indicators.

(2) Pressures refer to the direct impacts of human activities on the level of water resources exploitation and utilization. Data and methods

Data

The water resources data adopted in this study originate from the Water Resources Bulletin of Henan Province published by the Henan Provincial Hydrology and Water Resources Forecast Center. Other data are sourced from the Henan Statistical Yearbook and statistical bulletins released by the Henan Provincial Bureau of Statistics, as well as the statistical yearbooks and bulletins of various prefecture-level cities within the province.

For single-point missing values in time series data, linear interpolation is used for imputation; for consecutive missing values, trend fitting is applied. All imputed results are cross-validated to ensure reliability. To ensure data quality, variables with a proportion of missing values exceeding 15% were excluded. Additionally, to guarantee comparability among various indicators, economic indicators related to output value were uniformly adjusted for price changes. All data were also subjected to non-dimensionalization processing.

Methods

This study employs the entropy weight method to conduct a comprehensive evaluation of the agricultural economic development level and the water resources exploitation and utilization level. Subsequently, it utilizes the water resources development rate approach to assess the thresholds of water resources exploitation and utilization. On this basis, the coupling coordination degree model and the relative development degree model are utilized to explore the coordination relationship between the two. Finally, the grey relational degree model is applied to identify the key driving factors influencing the degree of coordination.

(1) Entropy Weight Method.

The entropy weight method is an objective weighting approach that determines the weights of indicators based on their relative degree of variation and their impact on the overall system, thereby avoiding subjectivity issues. It is now widely applied in various fields such as economics and statistics. The calculation process of the entropy weight method consists of three steps: data standardization, indicator weights, and comprehensive development index33.

① Data standardization.

The data is first standardized to guarantee comparability between indicators and remove the influence of various measurement units and scales34.

Results

Analysis of water resources exploitation and utilization level and agricultural economic development level

Agricultural economic development level

In terms of its impact, the level of agricultural economic development is influenced by factor input (0.3133), industrial structure (0.2402), economic scale (0.2287), and farmer welfare (0.2178), with the degree of influence decreasing in that order. As shown in Fig. 2, ① Regarding the overall development trend, over the past 20 years, the average level of agricultural economic development in Henan Province has shown a significant linear upward trend. The evaluation index has ranged from 0.1729 to 0.3810, with an increase of 120.23%, averaging an annual increment of 6.01%. The increase was relatively slow from 2000 to 2005, compared to the period from 2005 to 2019. This is attributed to China’s adjustment of grain production targets in 2003. Prior to 2003, the focus was on increasing farmers’ income; since 2003, the focus has shifted to increasing both grain production and farmers’ income, leading to yearly increases in grain yields and improved living standards for farmers40. ② In terms of comprehensive development level, over the 20-year period, the agricultural economic development levels of Xinyang, Nanyang, Zhumadian, Zhengzhou, and Xinxiang have consistently ranked among the top in all prefecture-level cities. ③ Regarding development speed, the increase in Heshan, Zhengzhou, Shangqiu, and Jiaozuo ranges from 70.00% to 100.00%, indicating moderate development speed. The increase in Xinyang, Zhumadian, Kaifeng, Xinxiang, Xuchang, Luohe, Anyang ranges from 100.00% to 130.00%, indicating faster development speed. The increase in Nanyang, Puyang, Pingdingshan, Luoyang, and Sanmenxia exceeds 130.00%, indicating the fastest increase. ④ Spatially, agricultural economic development levels initially exhibited a pattern of higher values in the northeastern region compared to the southwestern region. However, due to heterogeneous growth rates across prefecture-level cities, this spatial disparity gradually diminished over time. With the transformation of China’s grain security strategy, the state implemented a series of major policies, including the market-oriented reform of the agricultural product procurement and marketing system and the comprehensive abolition of the agricultural tax. These measures not only effectively accelerated the development of the agricultural economy but also, to a certain extent, weakened geographical barriers to agricultural development among regions, thereby promoting the restructuring of regional development patterns40.

Coupling coordination degree

Zhengzhou exhibits a significantly higher level of coordination compared to traditional agricultural regions such as Xinyang, primarily due to two key factors. First, in terms of resource management, as the economic and policy hub of the province, Zhengzhou has implemented water-saving irrigation technologies and comprehensive agricultural water pricing reforms with greater intensity and at an earlier stage, substantially improving water use efficiency and alleviating pressure on agricultural water demand19. Second, in terms of economic structure, agriculture accounts for a relatively small share of Zhengzhou’s total economic output, enabling an earlier transition toward a low-water-consumption, high-efficiency development model21. In contrast, regions like Xinyang remain heavily reliant on water-intensive traditional agricultural systems, where the pronounced imbalance between agricultural economic development and water resources exploitation and utilization results in greater strain and slower improvement in coordination20.

In terms of spatial evolution, the coupling coordination level between the two systems in Henan Province initially exhibited a pattern of relatively lagging development in the central region and higher coordination levels in the surrounding areas, with particularly notable improvements observed in the southern and western parts. Over time, the spatial differentiation pattern gradually stabilized, ultimately forming a gradient distribution characterized by higher levels in the southeast and lower levels in the northwest. This spatial pattern is primarily shaped by the combined effects of natural conditions and socioeconomic factors. The southeastern region benefits from flat terrain, concentrated arable land, and well-developed irrigation infrastructure, coupled with a relatively high level of agricultural modernization and intensive promotion of water-saving technologies, all of which have effectively promoted the coordinated development of agricultural economic development and water resources exploitation and utilization in Henan Province. In contrast, the northwestern region faces constraints such as insufficient water resources endowments, complex topography, and relatively underdeveloped economic conditions, which have led to slower progress in improving coordination and, consequently, the persistence of regional disparities40.

Discussion

Over the past two decades, the level of water resources development and utilization in Henan Province has demonstrated a consistent upward trend. This developmental trajectory aligns with the overall trend of water resources development and utilization across the country41, and further provides robust support for the conclusions of previous studies or relevant research findings concerning Henan Province42. Previous studies have suggested that the level of water resources exploitation and utilization in Henan Province was in an unsafe state43. However, our research reveals that, despite the increasingly prominent contradiction between agricultural economic development and water resources exploitation and utilization, the water resources development rates in most prefecture-level cities in Henan Province still remain below the standard threshold. This discrepancy arises because our study adopts economically reasonable water resources utilization as the benchmark, whereas previous studies primarily used ecological security as the criterion35. These earlier studies failed to fully consider the widespread drought and water scarcity conditions prevalent in northern China, along with the corresponding geographical and climatic constraints. As a result, they led to an overly pessimistic assessment of the water resources exploitation and utilization level in Henan Province.

The study also reveals that although the coupling degree between the agricultural economic development system and the water resources exploitation and utilization system has remained at a relatively high level for an extended period, indicating significant interactions between the two systems, the overall coupling coordination degree is still at a comparatively low level. This reflects that the contradiction between agricultural economic growth and sustainable water resources utilization has not been effectively addressed. This finding is consistent with previous research findings regarding the “strong dependence-weak coordination” characteristics of the water-agriculture system in Shanxi Province13, further corroborating the systemic contradictions commonly prevalent in high-intensity agricultural regions. When compared with the coordination levels between other systems, such as the water-social development system16 and the water-economic development system22, the coordinated development level of the water-agriculture system lags behind relatively. This highlights the urgency and pivotal role of promoting the coordinated development of water resources and the agricultural economy. The root causes can be traced back to the fact that, as an important national grain production base, the high dependence of agricultural production in Henan Province on water resources forms the basis for strong coupling between the systems. However, issues such as an imperfect water resource management mechanism, low efficiency of agricultural water use, and insufficient promotion of water-saving technologies severely constrain the improvement of system coordination. More critically, ecological problems such as groundwater over-extraction and water environmental pollution triggered by agricultural scale expansion further weaken the sustainable supply capacity of water resources. These factors collectively result in the current situation where the agricultural development in Henan Province has still not broken through the traditional development model of “high resource input-high economic output-low system coordination”.

Further research has uncovered a significant asymmetric dependency relationship between agricultural economic development and water resources exploitation and utilization. The strong correlation between water resource use efficiency indicators and the agricultural economy stems from the long-established “engineering-induced water shortage” response mode in the Huang-Huai-Hai Plain. Through large-scale water conservancy infrastructure construction and technological innovation, this region has effectively overcome the limitations imposed by natural water resources endowments44. The weak correlation of natural water resource quantity indicators, on the other hand, reflects the strong adaptability of the agricultural system in Henan to climate fluctuations45,46. In particular, supported by inter-basin water transfer projects such as the South-to-North Water Diversion Project, the regional agriculture has developed a production system that is relatively independent of local water resources conditions47. The high correlation of agricultural economic indicators with water resources exploitation and utilization embodies the demand expansion effect brought about by economic development. Among them, the notable impacts of rural electricity consumption and plastic film usage indicate that the increase in energy input and the transformation of production modes during the process of agricultural modernization are reshaping the pattern of regional water resources utilization22. This interaction mode essentially reflects the deep-seated intervention of human activities in the natural water cycle, as well as a typical path for achieving sustainable agricultural development through technological innovation under resource constraints.

Policy recommendations are proposed from three aspects to promote the coordinated and sustainable development of agricultural economy and water resources. First, optimize the water resource allocation mechanism to enhance agricultural water use efficiency. While ensuring food security, priority should be given to allocating resources to high-efficiency and water-saving agricultural sectors. It is recommended to promote reforms in agricultural water pricing and improve the water rights trading system, guiding the transformation of agricultural water use from extensive to intensive and efficient utilization. Additionally, in cities with a relatively high water resources development rate—such as Zhengzhou, Xinxiang, and Anyang—efficient water-saving irrigation technologies should be promoted to improve agricultural water use efficiency and prevent ecological degradation and deterioration of the water environment caused by over-exploitation. Second, promote collaborative governance of systems to enhance the coordination level between the agricultural economy and the water resource system. Differentiated policy measures should be implemented according to the coordinated development stages of different regions. For regions like Xinyang, Luoyang, and Pingdingshan, which have a relatively low coordination level and significant fluctuations, it is necessary to focus on enhancing the supporting capacity of water resources for agricultural development. For regions with a relatively high coordination level, such as Zhengzhou, attention should be paid to improving the capacity for sustainable water resources utilization to avoid the risk of “high coordination masking low sustainability”. Through these differentiated policy measures, the agricultural economy and the water resource system can be driven to transition from “weak coordination” to “strong coordination”. Finally, focus on key driving factors to promote the sustainable development of the agricultural economy and water resources. It is suggested that when formulating water resource management policies in the future, more emphasis should be placed on structural variables in agricultural economic development. Meanwhile, indicators of farmers’ welfare should be incorporated into the water resource policy evaluation system to foster a multi-objective governance approach that balances economic growth, resource conservation, and improvement of people’s livelihoods.

This study has constructed a comprehensive evaluation index system for agricultural economic development and water resources exploitation and utilization, and explored the coupling relationship between them, providing regional-level empirical evidence for the implementation of the United Nations’ Sustainable Development Goals in China. However, the research still has certain limitations. On the one hand, the analysis of the driving mechanisms underlying the coordinated development of the two systems remains insufficient. On the other hand, the study does not fully account for the potential impacts of changing precipitation patterns, extreme hydrological events, and drought risks under climate change on water supply and agricultural water demand. Given these limitations, future research will build upon this work by integrating climate change scenarios and regional development policies into multi-scenario simulations and system dynamics modeling of water resource sustainability and agricultural economic development in Henan Province. This integrated approach aims to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the dynamic feedback mechanisms between the systems and their pathways toward sustainable evolution.

Conclusions

This paper constructs indicators for agricultural economic development and water resources exploitation and utilization, quantitatively evaluates the changes in the agricultural economy and water resources exploitation and utilization in 17 prefecture-level cities in Henan Province over the past 20 years, and analyzes the degree of coordinated development between them. Furthermore, it delves into the key factors influencing the development of both, leading to the following conclusions.

(1) Over the past 20 years, the level of agricultural economic development in Henan Province has shown a linear upward trend, with an evaluation index ranging from 0.1729 to 0.3810, representing an increase of 120.36%. Among these, the increase in Anyang, Puyang, Xinxiang, Sanmenxia, Luoyang, Xuchang, Pingdingshan, Luohe, Kaifeng, Zhoukou, Nanyang, Zhumadian, and Xinyang exceeds 100%, indicating a relatively rapid growth rate. The upward trend in the level of water resources exploitation and utilization in Henan Province is also very significant, with an evaluation index ranging from 0.1860 to 0.3440, representing an increase of 84.92%. Among these, the increase in Zhengzhou, Xuchang, Kaifeng, and Xinyang exceeds 100%, indicating a relatively high increase. The water resources development rates in Zhengzhou, Xinxiang, Jiaozuo, Anyang, Hebi, Kaifeng, and Puyang exceed the economically reasonable development utilization rates.

(2) Over the past 20 years, the coupling coordination degree between agricultural economic development and water resources exploitation and utilization in Henan Province has shown an upward trend, with the coupling coordination degree ranging from 0.4204 to 0.5974, and an average annual increase of 2.11%. The coupling coordination degree in all prefecture-level cities has shown an upward trend, improving by 1 to 3 coordination stages. In 2019, the coordination levels of all prefecture-level cities were mainly at the initial coordinated stage, with Zhengzhou even advancing to the moderately coordinated level. However, the coordination degree between the two systems remains relatively low, indicating that there is not a good synergistic working state between agricultural economic development and water resources exploitation and utilization. From the perspective of relative development degree, over the past 20 years, the level of agricultural economic development in Xinyang has consistently outpaced the level of water resources exploitation and utilization; Zhengzhou has transitioned from synchronization to a lagging phase; Anyang, Sanmenxia, Luohe, and Zhoukou have transitioned from synchronization to an outpaced phase; and Luoyang and Pingdingshan have transitioned from a lagging phase to an outpaced phase. The coordination levels of these eight prefecture-level cities are unstable.

(3) Through grey relational degree analysis, it was found that among the 22 indicators measuring the level of water resources exploitation and utilization, primary sector value added (0.7410), urban and rural living water Use (0.7393), and per capita GDP (0.7379) rank in the top three for their correlation with agricultural economic development. The four indicators related to water resources endowments—groundwater resources (0.5421), total water resources (0.5013), per capita water resources (0.4687), and surface water resources (0.4876)—rank in the bottom four positions. Among the 21 indicators measuring the level of agricultural economic development, total agricultural output value (0.6967), per capita electricity consumption of rural residents (0.6940), plastic film usage per mu (0.6911), per capita agricultural output value (0.6901), per capita disposable income of rural residents (0.6852), and per capita living consumption expenditure of rural residents (0.6843) rank in the top six for their correlation with the level of water resources exploitation and utilization. Among these, the per capita electricity consumption of rural residents, disposable income of rural residents, and per capita living expenses of rural residents are important variables for measuring the farmer welfare.

The Iterates of Nesterov’s Accelerated Algorithm Converge in The Critical Regimes
Radu Ioan Boţ, Jalal Fadili, Dang-Khoa Nguyen

Abstract
In this paper, we prove that the iterates of the accelerated Nesterov’s algorithm in the critical regime do converge in the weak topology to a global minimizer of an L L -smooth function in a real Hilbert space, hence answering positively a conjecture posed by H. Attouch and co-authors a decade ago. This result is the algorithmic case of a very recent result on the continuous-time system posted by E. Ryu on X, with assistance from ChatGPT.



1
Introduction

1.1
Problem statement

Throughout the paper,
\mathcal{H}
is a real Hilbert space which is endowed with the scalar product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and associated norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
. We consider the optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}f(x),

(1)

where
f:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex and
L_{\nabla f}
-smooth function, i.e. continuously differentiable with
L_{\nabla f}
-Lipschitz gradient. We denote
\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathcal{H}}f
the solution set of (
1
), which is assumed to be nonempty, and
f_{\star}:=\inf_{x\in\mathcal{H}}f(x)\in\mathbb{R}
the optimal value.

Since the seminal work of Nesterov
[
20
]
(and even before by Gelfand and Tseslin
[
17
]
), accelerated dynamics based on asymptotically vanishing damping, both in continuous-time and their discrete counterparts, have attracted a tremendous interest in the optimization and machine learning literature. For instance, consider the second-order differential equation

\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla f(x(t))=0,

(2)

where
\alpha
is a positive parameter. As a specific feature, the viscous damping coefficient
\frac{\alpha}{t}
vanishes (tends to zero) as time
t
goes to infinity, hence the terminology.

For
\alpha=3
, this system was introduced in
[
23
]
. There, it was also shown to be a continuous version of the accelerated gradient method of Nesterov
[
20
]
. Its adaptation to the case of structured “smooth + nonsmooth” convex optimization gives the Fast Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm (FISTA) of
[
13
]
. The convergence properties of the dynamic (
2
) have been the subject of a large body of work; see e.g.,
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
19
,
23
]
. In particular, for
\alpha\geq 3
, the generated trajectory
x(t)
of (
2
) satisfies the asymptotic rate of convergence of the values
f(x(t))-f_{*}=O\left(1/t^{2}\right)
as
t\to+\infty
. For
\alpha>3
, it was shown in
[
9
]
and
[
19
]
, that the trajectory converges weakly to a minimizer of
f
, with the improved rate of convergence for the function values along the trajectory convergence of
o(1/t^{2})
as
t\to+\infty
.

Corresponding results for the algorithmic case have been obtained by
[
16
]
and
[
9
]
.
Recently, it was shown in
[
2
]
that there is some connection between (
2
) and the Heavy-ball method of Polyak
[
21
]
.

For the critical case
\alpha=3
, the (weak) convergence of the trajectory for the continuous system (
2
), and the iterates for the discrete Nesterov algorithm has remained elusive and a widely open problem. It was only proved, both for the continuous dynamics and Nesterov algorithm, in
[
10
]
and
[
7
]
either under restrictive assumptions on the function
f
, or in the one-dimensional case without any restriction on the parameters.

In a post on X by E. Ryu
[
22
, Part I]
on October 21, the author intensively relied on interaction with ChatGPT to provide a surprisingly simple proof of convergence of the trajectories of (
2
) in the critical case and finite dimensional spaces, hence settling the convergence of the trajectory of (
2
). The discrete algorithmic case was mentioned in a subsequent post, but had not been solved by the time the first version of this note was submitted. The discrete case was subsequently and independently handled in
[
22
, Part III]
and
[
18
]
in finite dimension.

1.2
Contributions

In this paper, we consider the following general accelerated algorithm for solving (
1
):

Algorithm 1
.

Let
\left(\alpha_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}\subseteq[0,+\infty)
and
0<\lambda\leq\frac{1}{L_{\nabla f}}
, and
x_{0},x_{1}
be given in
\mathcal{H}
.
For every
k\geq 1
, set

\begin{cases}y_{k}&\coloneq x_{k}+\alpha_{k}\left(x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right),\\
x_{k+1}&\coloneq y_{k}-\lambda\nabla f\left(y_{k}\right).\end{cases}

The sequence
\left(\alpha_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
is constructed, for every
k\geq 1
, as follows

\alpha_{k}\coloneq\dfrac{t_{k}-1}{t_{k+1}},

with the sequence
\left(t_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}\subseteq(0,+\infty)
chosen appropriately.

Our setting is general enough so that this algorithm covers the critical cases corresponding to both the Nesterov algorithm
[
20
]
and that of Chambolle-Dossal in
[
16
]
. We show that the iterates of the accelerated Nesterov’s algorithm in the critical regime do converge in the weak topology to a minimizer of
f
, hence solving the conjecture posed by Attouch and co-authors a decade ago. This complements the result of
[
22
]
in the continuous-time case. This result also holds for the smooth+nonsmooth case when using FISTA. The crux of the proof relies on three tenets: (i) properties of
(t_{k})_{k\geq 1}
; (ii)
an elegant representation of our sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
as an iterative average of a bounded sequence, allowing us to transfer this boundedness property to
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
via Jensen’s inequality; and (iii) a proof technique reminiscent of that of Opial’s lemma without directly invoking the latter as usually done.

2
Convergence proof

2.1
Properties of the sequence
\left(t_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}

Let
0\leq\theta<1
. We will assume that the sequence
\left(t_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}\subseteq(0,+\infty)
is chosen such that

t_{1}=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad t_{k+1}^{2}-t_{k}^{2}=\left(1-\theta\right)t_{k+1}+\theta t_{k}\quad\forall k\geq 1.

(3)

Remark 1
.

\left(i\right)

The classical choice of Nesterov in
[
20
]
,

t_{1}=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad t_{k+1}:=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_{k}^{2}}}{2}\quad\forall k\geq 1,

(4)

thus satisfying

t_{k+1}^{2}-t_{k}^{2}=t_{k+1}\quad\forall k\geq 1,

corresponds to
\theta=0
.

\left(ii\right)

The choice of Chambolle-Dossal in
[
16
]
,

t_{k}\coloneq 1+\dfrac{k-1}{\alpha-1}\quad\forall k\geq 1,

(5)

satisfies (
3
) in the case
\alpha=3
for
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
, namely,

t_{k+1}^{2}-t_{k}^{2}=\left(t_{k+1}-t_{k}\right)\left(t_{k+1}+t_{k}\right)=\dfrac{1}{\alpha-1}\left(t_{k+1}+t_{k}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(t_{k+1}+t_{k}\right)\quad\forall k\geq 1.

These correspond to the two critical regimes of the accelerated gradient method. While it has long been known that
f(x_{k})-f_{\star}=O(1/k^{2})
as
k\to+\infty
, in both cases, the convergence of the sequence of iterates
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
has remained an open question – the one that we answer affirmatively in this note.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\left(t_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence defined according to (
3
). For every
k\geq 1
, it holds:

\left(i\right)

t_{k+1}=\frac{1-\theta+\sqrt{\left(1-\theta\right)^{2}+4\left(t_{k}^{2}+\theta t_{k}\right)}}{2}
.

\left(ii\right)

t_{k}\geq\frac{\left(1-\theta\right)}{2}\left(k+1\right)
.

Proof.

The explicit formula of
t_{k+1}
in

(
i
)

comes from the fact that it is the positive solution of the quadratic equation

t^{2}-\left(1-\theta\right)t-\left(t_{k}^{2}+\theta t_{k}\right)=0.

We show the inequality in

(

⁢
i
i

)

by induction. The inequality is satisfied in the case
k=1
. Suppose that it is also satisfied for some
k\geq 1
, namely,
2t_{k}\geq\left(1-\theta\right)\left(k+1\right)
.
Then, using the explicit formula of
t_{k+1}
in

(
i
)

, we see that

\displaystyle t_{k+1}=\frac{1-\theta+\sqrt{\left(1-\theta\right)^{2}+4\left(t_{k}^{2}+\theta t_{k}\right)}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\theta+\sqrt{4t_{k}^{2}}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1-\theta+\left(1-\theta\right)\left(k+1\right)}{2}=\frac{\left(1-\theta\right)\left(k+2\right)}{2}.

∎

2.2
Lyapunov analysis and boundedness of the iterates

We introduce the auxiliary sequence
\left(z_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
, defined, for every
k\geq 1
, as

z_{k}\coloneq t_{k}\left(x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right)+x_{k-1}.

(6)

For every
k\geq 1
, it holds

\displaystyle z_{k}=\left(t_{k}-1\right)\left(x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right)+x_{k}=t_{k}x_{k}-\left(t_{k}-1\right)x_{k-1}.

(7)

This representation will lead to the interpretation of
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
as an ergodic sequence, as in
[
15
]
. To this end, we define the sequences
\left(s_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
and
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
, for every
k\geq 1
as follows

\displaystyle s_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneq t_{k}^{2}+\theta t_{k},

(8)

\displaystyle u_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneq z_{k}+\theta\left(x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right).

(9)

By construction, for every
k\geq 1
, we have

s_{k}=\theta+\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}.

(10)

Indeed, for every
k\geq 1
, according to (
3
) it holds

t_{k+1}^{2}+\theta t_{k+1}=t_{k}^{2}+\theta t_{k}+t_{k+1}.

In the view of (
8
), is nothing else than

s_{k+1}=s_{k}+t_{k+1}.

By using the telescoping sum argument, we obtain, for every
k\geq 1
,

s_{k+1}=s_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i+1}=1+\theta+\sum_{i=2}^{k+1}t_{i}=\theta+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}t_{i}.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\left(t_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence defined according to (
3
), and
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence generated by
Algorithm

1
.
For every
k\geq 1
, it holds

x_{k}=\dfrac{1}{s_{k}}\left(\theta x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}u_{i}\right)=\dfrac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{k}t_{i}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}t_{i}u_{i},

(11)

with the convention

t_{0}\coloneq\theta\text{ and }u_{0}\coloneq x_{0}.

Proof.

Firstly, note that

\displaystyle t_{1}u_{1}

\displaystyle=x_{1}+\theta\left(x_{1}-x_{0}\right)=(1+\theta)x_{1}-\theta x_{0}=s_{1}x_{1}-\theta x_{0},

(12)

which means (
11
) holds for
k=1.

Now, consider (
7
) at the index
k+1
, for
k\geq 1
. By multiplying both sides by
t_{k+1}>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle t_{k+1}z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=t_{k+1}^{2}x_{k+1}-\left(t_{k+1}^{2}-t_{k+1}\right)x_{k}

\displaystyle=t_{k+1}^{2}x_{k+1}-\left(t_{k}^{2}+\theta\left(t_{k}-t_{k+1}\right)\right)x_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(t_{k+1}^{2}+\theta t_{k+1}\right)x_{k+1}-\left(t_{k}^{2}+\theta t_{k}\right)x_{k}-\theta t_{k+1}\left(x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right).

In the view of
s_{k}
, we can rewrite the relation above as

t_{k+1}z_{k+1}+\theta t_{k+1}\left(x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right)=\left(t_{k+1}^{2}+\theta t_{k+1}\right)x_{k+1}-\left(t_{k}^{2}+\theta t_{k}\right)x_{k}=s_{k+1}x_{k+1}-s_{x}x_{k}.

By using the telescoping sum argument, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i+1}u_{i+1}=s_{k+1}x_{k+1}-s_{1}x_{1},

which gives

\displaystyle s_{k+1}x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=s_{1}x_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i+1}u_{i+1}=s_{1}x_{1}-t_{1}u_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}t_{i}u_{i}=x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}t_{i}u_{i},

where the last equation comes from (
12
).
∎

Remark 2
.

\left(i\right)

In the original setting of Nesterov, which corresponds to the case when
\theta=0
, the sequences
\left(z_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
and
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
coincide.

\left(ii\right)

To emphasize the averaging property and to simplify the latter proof, we see that, for every
k\geq 1
, we can write

x_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\theta_{k,i}u_{i},

where, for every
i=0,1,2,\dotsc,k
,

\theta_{k,i}\coloneq\dfrac{t_{i}}{s_{k}}

is such that
\theta_{k,i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=0}^{k}\theta_{k,i}=1
.

Let
z\in\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathcal{H}}f
. For every
k\geq 1
, we denote

\displaystyle W_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneq f\left(x_{k}\right)-f_{\star}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rVert^{2},

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{z,k}

\displaystyle\coloneq t_{k}^{2}\left(f\left(x_{k}\right)-f_{\star}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert z_{k}-z\right\rVert^{2}.

(14)

Using that the sequence
\left(t_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
is nondecreasing, we have, for every
k\geq 1
,

t_{k+1}+\theta\left(t_{k}-t_{k+1}\right)\leq t_{k+1}\quad\forall k\geq 1,

therefore,

t_{1}=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad t_{k+1}^{2}-t_{k}^{2}\leq t_{k+1}\quad\forall k\geq 1.

(15)

Since condition (
15
) is fulfilled, we can use existing results from the literature to state the following proposition, see for instance
[
13
]
,
[
16
, Theorem 2]
or
[
4
, Propositions 3 and 6]
.

This means the condition (
15
) is verified allows us to exploit these results.

Proposition 1
.

Let
z\in\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathcal{H}}f
,
\left(t_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence defined according to (
3
), and
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence generated by
Algorithm

1
. Then, the following statements are true:

\left(i\right)

The sequences
\left(W_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
and
\left(\mathcal{E}_{z,k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
are nonincreasing and thus converge.

\left(ii\right)

For every
k\geq 1
, it holds

0\leq f\left(x_{k}\right)-f_{\star}\leq\dfrac{\mathcal{E}_{z,1}}{t_{k}^{2}}\leq\dfrac{4\mathcal{E}_{z,1}}{\left(1-\theta\right)^{2}\left(k+1\right)^{2}}.

The boundedness of the sequence
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
can easily be deduced from the above considerations. Note that the boundedness claim for the Chambolle-Dossal choice, see (
5
) with
\alpha=3
, was shown in
[
7
, Proposition 4.3]
, although with a completely different proof.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\left(t_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence defined according to (
3
), and
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence generated by
Algorithm

1
.
Then, the following statements are true:

\left(i\right)

The sequence
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
is bounded.

\left(ii\right)

For every
k\geq 1
, it holds

\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rVert\leq\dfrac{\sqrt{2}\left(2\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{z,1}}+\theta\sqrt{W_{1}}\right)}{t_{k}}.

(16)

\left(iii\right)

Every weak sequential cluster point of the sequence
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
belongs to
\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathcal{H}}f
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathcal{H}}f
. It follows immediately from
Proposition

1
that the sequences
\left(z_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
and
\left(x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right)_{k\geq 1}
are bounded. Therefore, the sequence
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
, defined in (
9
), is also bounded. Precisely, for every
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert z_{k}-z\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}_{z,1}}<+\infty\textrm{ and }\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{2W_{1}}<+\infty,

which implies

\left\lVert u_{k}-z\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert z_{k}-z\right\rVert+\theta\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rVert\leq C_{z}\coloneq\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}_{z,1}}+\theta\sqrt{2W_{1}}<+\infty.

Using the convexity of the norm and applying Jensen’s inequality, we will show that the sequence
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
is also bounded. Indeed, for every
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert x_{k}-z\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum_{i=0}^{k}\theta_{k,i}\left(u_{i}-z\right)\right\rVert\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\theta_{k,i}\left\lVert u_{i}-z\right\rVert\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\theta_{k,i}C_{z}=C_{z}<+\infty.

Combining the boundedness of
\left(z_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
and
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
, we can deduce the convergence rate for discrete velocity.
According to the equation (
6
). For every
k\geq 1
, we have

t_{k}\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert z_{k}-z\right\rVert+\left\lVert x_{k-1}-z\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}_{z,1}}+C_{z},

which is nothing else than (
16
).

The existence of weak cluster point of sequence
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
is guaranteed by its boundedness.
The fact that
\lim_{k\to+\infty}f\left(x_{k}\right)=f_{\star}
together with the continuity of
f
ensures that every weak sequential cluster point of
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
belongs to
\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathcal{H}}f
.
∎

2.3
Weak convergence of the iterates

For every
z\in\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathcal{H}}f
and
k\geq 1
, we denote

h_{z,k}\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert x_{k}-z\right\rVert^{2}.

(17)

In the view of this notation, we have, for every
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{z,k}

\displaystyle=t_{k}^{2}\left(f\left(x_{k}\right)-f_{\star}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\left(t_{k}-1\right)\left(x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right)+x_{k}-z\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=t_{k}^{2}\left(f\left(x_{k}\right)-f_{\star}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(t_{k}-1\right)^{2}\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left(t_{k}-1\right)\left\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},x_{k}-z\right\rangle+h_{z,k}.

(18)

Theorem 1
.

Let
\left(t_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence defined according to (
3
), and
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence generated by
Algorithm

1
. Then, the sequence
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
converges weakly to a minimizer of
f
.

Proof.

Let
z
and
v
be two arbitrary weak sequential cluster point of
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
. This means that there exists two subsequences
\left(x_{m_{k}}\right)_{k\geq 0}
and
\left(x_{n_{k}}\right)_{k\geq 0}
of
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
, which converge weakly to
z
and
v
, respectively, as
k\to+\infty
.

For every
k\geq 1
, we set

\displaystyle D_{k}\coloneq\mathcal{E}_{z,k}-\mathcal{E}_{v,k}.

(19)

Proposition

2
tells us that
z
and
v
belong to
\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathcal{H}}f
. From
Proposition

1
, we conclude that
D_{\star}:=\lim_{k\to+\infty}D_{k}
exists and is finite.

For every
k\geq 1
, we set

\displaystyle R_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneq h_{z,k}-h_{v,k}.

(20)

According to (
17
), we have, for every
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle R_{k}=h_{z,k}-h_{v,k}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert x_{k}-z\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert x_{k}-v\right\rVert^{2}\right)=\left\langle x_{k}-v,v-z\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert v-z\right\rVert^{2}.

(21)

From here, we deduce that, for every
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle R_{k+1}-R_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(h_{z,k+1}-h_{v,k+1}\right)-\left(h_{z,k}-h_{v,k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\left\langle x_{k+1}-v,v-z\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert v-z\right\rVert^{2}\right)-\left(\left\langle x_{k}-v,v-z\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert v-z\right\rVert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},v-z\right\rangle.

(22)

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we infer

0\leq\left\lvert R_{k+1}-R_{k}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\left\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},v-z\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rVert\left\lVert v-z\right\rVert,

hence, (
16
) yields

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left(R_{k+1}-R_{k}\right)=0.

Next, by using (
18
), we have, for every
k\geq 1
, that

\displaystyle D_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{z,k}-\mathcal{E}_{v,k}

\displaystyle=\left(t_{k}-1\right)\left\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},v-z\right\rangle+\left(h_{z,k}-h_{v,k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(t_{k}-1\right)\left\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},v-z\right\rangle+R_{k},

(23)

which, in the light of (
22
), yields

\displaystyle t_{k+1}D_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\left(t_{k+1}^{2}-t_{k+1}\right)\left\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},v-z\right\rangle+t_{k+1}R_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\left(t_{k+1}^{2}-t_{k+1}\right)\left(R_{k+1}-R_{k}\right)+t_{k+1}R_{k+1}

\displaystyle=t_{k+1}^{2}R_{k+1}-\left(t_{k+1}^{2}-t_{k+1}\right)R_{k}

\displaystyle=t_{k+1}^{2}R_{k+1}-\left(t_{k}^{2}+\theta\left(t_{k}-t_{k+1}\right)\right)R_{k}.

Adding
\theta t_{k+1}\left(R_{k+1}-R_{k}\right)
on both sides, leads, for every
k\geq 1
, to

t_{k+1}\left[D_{k+1}+\theta\left(R_{k+1}-R_{k}\right)\right]=\left(t_{k+1}^{2}+\theta t_{k+1}\right)R_{k+1}-\left(t_{k}^{2}+\theta t_{k}\right)R_{k}

or, equivalently,

s_{k+1}R_{k+1}=s_{k}R_{k}+t_{k+1}\left[D_{k+1}+\theta\left(R_{k+1}-R_{k}\right)\right].

By the telescoping sum argument, we obtain, for every
k\geq 1
, that

\displaystyle s_{k+1}R_{k+1}

2.4
Accelerated proximal-gradient algorithm

The extension of Algorithm
1
to the non-smooth composite optimization problem

\min\limits_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\left\{f\left(x\right)+g\left(x\right)\right\},

where
f:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and
L_{\nabla f}
-smooth, and
g\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{+\infty\right\}
is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous, with nonempty set of minimizers, is the fast proximal-gradient algorithm (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
). For
\left(\alpha_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
0<\lambda\leq\frac{1}{L_{\nabla f}}
, and
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
, the iterates are defined, for every
k\geq 1
, by

\begin{cases}y_{k}&\coloneq x_{k}+\alpha_{k}\left(x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right),\\
x_{k+1}&\coloneq\mathsf{prox}_{\lambda g}\left(y_{k}-\lambda\nabla f\left(y_{k}\right)\right),\end{cases}

where the proximal point operator associated to
g
with parameter
\lambda>0
is given by

\mathsf{prox}_{\lambda g}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H},\quad\mathsf{prox}_{\lambda g}\left(y\right)\coloneq\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\left\{g\left(x\right)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert^{2}\right\}.

If
\left(t_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
is a sequence defined according to (
15
), and therefore also according to (
3
), and

\alpha_{k}\coloneq\dfrac{t_{k}-1}{t_{k+1}}\quad\forall k\geq 1,

then, by defining the energy functions in (
13
)–(

[
16

, one can analogously show that the generated sequence

converges weakly to a minimizer of

This result provides a positive answer to the long-standing open question regarding the convergence of the iterates generated by FISTA, namely, for sequences

following either the Nesterov rule (

]

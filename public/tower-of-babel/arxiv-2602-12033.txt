M. Eleuteri, P. Marcellini, E. Mascolo & \& A. Passarelli di Napoli

Abstract
. We establish the local Lipschitz regularity of the local minimizers of non autonomous integral funtionals of the form ∫ Ω F ⁡ ( x , D ​ z ) ​ 𝑑 x , \int_{\Omega}F(x,Dz)\,dx, where Ω \Omega is a bounded open set of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . 1.
Introduction

The study of the local Lipschitz continuity of the minimizers to variational integrals of the form

(1.1)

\int_{\Omega}F(x,Dz)\,dx,

where
\Omega
is a bounded open set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
and where the energy density
F(x,\xi)
exhibits
(p,q)-
growth conditions with respect to the gradient variable, has become a well-established topic in the literature. The study of such problems can be traced back to the pioneering contributions by Marcellini
[
21
,
22
]
. Nowadays the existing research on the regularity theory for local minimizers of functionals with non-standard growth conditions is so extensive that providing a complete list of references is unfeasible. Therefore, we limit ourselves to direct the interested reader to several survey articles on the subject
[
23
,
25
,
26
]
and to the latest achievements in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
15
,
24
,
27
,
28
]
.

To avoid the occurrence of the so-called
Lavrentiev phenomenon
— a well-known obstacle to the regularity of the minimizers (see the recent works
[
1
,
11
]
) — the typical approach is to identify suitable conditions on the partial map
x\mapsto F(x,\xi)
that ensure a certain degree of regularity for the minimizers.

A first contribution in this direction was given by Eleuteri-Marcellini-Mascolo in
[
12
]
(see also
[
14
]
), where, under the assumption that the partial map
x\to F(x,\xi)
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,r}
, with
r>n
, and that the map
\xi\to F(x,\xi)
satisfies
(p,q)
-growth conditions, a sufficient criterion for the Lipschitz regularity of the minimizers is provided. This condition involves a closeness requirement on the exponents
(p,q)
, which depends on both
n
and
r
, and it is given by

(1.2)

\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{r}.

Condition (
1.2
) has been taken into consideration in various similar contexts; see for example DeFilippis-Mingione
[
9
]
.
The proof of the Lipschitz continuity result in
[
12
]
(see also
[
14
,
10
]
) crucially relies on the strict inequality (
1.2
), therefore it is natural to ask whether the same result can be achieved by approaching the limit case.
This is precisely the purpose of this contribution. In particular we will provide a unified approach to the original situation and the limit case, also covering the standard growth condition situation
[
2
]
.

To describe our results precisely, we now introduce the assumptions.
We assume that the energy density
F(x,\xi)
is a Carathéodory function of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
with respect to
\xi
and there exists
\tilde{F}:\Omega\times[0,+\infty)\mapsto[0,+\infty)
such that
F(x,\xi)=\tilde{F}(x,|\xi|)
. We assume moreover that
F_{\xi x}
is a Carathéodory function, that
F(x,0)=0
and that there exist a couple of exponents
1<p\leq q
, constants
\lambda,\Lambda>0
, a parameter
\mu\in[0,1]
and a nonnegative measurable function
h(x)
such that

(1.3)

\lambda\,(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\zeta|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}F_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}(x,\xi)\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}\leq\Lambda\,(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}|\zeta|^{2},

(1.4)

|F_{\xi x}(x,\xi)|\leq\,h(x)\,(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{q-1}{2}},

for every
\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a.e.
x
in
\Omega
.
As proved in
[
5
, Lemma 2.3]
, assumption (
1.3
) entails the following
(p,q)
growth
conditions for the energy density
F(x,\xi)

(1.5)

\frac{\lambda}{p(p-1)}\,(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi|^{2}\leq F(x,\xi)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{2}\,(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}|\xi|^{2}.

About the function
h(x)
appearing in (
1.4
), we assume that there exist
exponents
r\geq n
and
\alpha\geq 0
such that

(1.6)

\ \int_{B_{R}}h^{r}\log^{\alpha}(e+h)\,dx<+\infty,\qquad\forall B_{R}\Subset\Omega.

The previous assumption implies that the function
h(x)
in (
1.4
) belongs to the Orlicz-Zygmund space
L^{r}\log^{\alpha}L
. Hence, the class of functions describing the regularity of the partial map
x\mapsto F(x,\xi)
is clearly contained in
W^{1,n}
, consistently with the fact that, even in case of standard growth conditions (that corresponds to the case
p=q
in assumption (
1.5
)), the regularity can be obtained assuming a
W^{1,n}\log^{\alpha}L
regularity of the coefficients, with
\alpha
sufficiently large with respect to the dimension
n
(see
[
2
]
and
[
16
]
for the variable growth case). In this respect, our result also retrieves the corresponding one in
[
2
]
, which in turn is a refinement of
[
13
]
.

Let us recall the definition of local minimizer

Definition 1.1
.

A mapping

with

.

,

The proof of previous Theorems is achieved
through the following steps:

•

The first step consists in an
a priori
estimate for
smooth minimizers
of the functional at (
1.1
) with constants depending only on the assumptions (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and (

•

The second step consists in constructing a suitable approximation of the energy integral in (

.

•

The final step consists in applying the a priori estimate to each

The originality of our result mainly relies in the first step, where the right condition on the interplay between the regularity of the coefficients and the growth assumption is the main issue. Indeed, this is where the extra regularity of the coefficients with respect the
W^{1,n}
integrability needs to slow down the rate of growth at
\infty

Once this first step is achieved, the Uhlenbeck structure of the integrands allows to construct the approximating problems following a quite standard procedure.

A generalization of such an interplay between the nonstandard growth and the regularity of the partial map
x\to F(x,\xi)
will be presented in a forthcoming paper
[

]
.

Preliminary result

In this paper we shall denote by

a
general positive constant that may vary on different occasions, even within the

Relevant dependencies will be suitably emphasized using

We shall omit the dependence on the center and on the radius when no confusion arises.
We recall the following well known iteration lemma, whose

Lemma 2.1
.

For

f(r_{1})\leq\vartheta f(r_{2})+\frac{A}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{\alpha}}+\frac{B}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{\beta}}+C\qquad\mbox{for all }R_{1}<r_{1}<r_{2}<R_{2},

where

denote nonnegative constants
and

. Then we have

f(R_{1})\leq c(\alpha,\beta,\vartheta)\bigg(\frac{A}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{\alpha}}+\frac{B}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{\beta}}+C\bigg).

We shall use the elementary inequality that is contained in the following

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\varphi:{[1,+\infty)}\to[0,+\infty)
be the function defined by

\varphi(t)=\frac{t^{\beta}}{\log^{\alpha}(t)},

with
\alpha,\beta>0
. Then

\varphi(st)\leq 2^{\alpha}\sqrt{\varphi(s^{2})\varphi(t^{2})}.

Proof.

One can explicitly calculate

\displaystyle\varphi(st)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{(st)^{\beta}}{\log^{\alpha}(st)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s^{\beta}}{(\log(s)+\log(t))^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\frac{t^{\beta}}{(\log(s)+\log(t))^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s^{\beta}}{\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(s^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\log(t^{2})\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\frac{t^{\beta}}{\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(s^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\log(t^{2})\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{\alpha}\sqrt{\varphi(s^{2})\varphi(t^{2})}

and this concludes the proof.
∎

For further needs, we recall that for a non-trivial function
\Phi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
,
its polar (or Fenchel conjugate) is defined by

(2.1)

\Phi^{*}(s):=\sup_{t\geq 0}\big\{st-\Phi(t)\big\}\qquad{\forall\,}s\geq 0.

The function
\Phi^{*}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is convex and the Young-type (or Fenchel) inequality directly follows from its definition

(2.2)

st\leq\,\Phi^{*}(s)+{\Phi(t)}

for all
s,t\geq 0
.

We conclude by recalling the following

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\varphi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be increasing and diverging at
\infty
. Then there exists

such that

\frac{s}{\psi(s)}\leq\varphi(s)\leq 2\frac{s}{\psi(s)},\qquad\text{for every }s>s_{0}

where for

.

For the proof we refer to
[

, Lemma 2.4]

[

since, by virtue of assumption i), we have
{\alpha>4n}
. If

, we use the second estimate in (

) (where we notice that the denominator in the right hand side is greater than

), to deduce that

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\log{{L}^{-1}\Big(\Theta p_{j}^{4n}\Big)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\left[\log{\Big(\Theta\,p_{j}^{4n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r-n}}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{1}{r-n}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\log\Theta}{p_{j}}+\frac{4n}{r-n}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\log p_{j}}{p_{j}}\right)\leq C(\Theta,n,p,r)

Moreover

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\prod_{j=0}^{i}}\left({\Big(1+\|V(Du)\|^{2(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\Big)}\left(1+{{L}^{-1}\Big(\|V(Du)\|^{2n(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\Big)}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{j}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\log\left({\Big(1+\|V(Du)\|^{2(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}}\right)\left(1+{{L}^{-1}\Big(\|V(Du)\|^{2n(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\Big)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle\exp\left(\log\left({\Big(1+\|V(Du)\|^{2(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\Big)}\left(1+{{L}^{-1}\|V(Du)\|^{2n(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\Big)}\right)\right)\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle{C(p,q)\big(1+\|V(Du)\|^{\frac{n(q-p)}{p}}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\big)}\left(1+{L}^{-1}\Big(\|V(Du)\|^{2n(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\Big)\right)^{\frac{n}{2p}},

where we used again that

.
Therefore, we can let
i\to\infty
in (
4.51
) thus getting

(4.65)

\displaystyle||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{\rho}_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{\left(1+\|V(Du)\|^{\frac{n(q-p)}{p}}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\right)}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(1+{L}^{-1}\Big(\|V(Du)\|^{2n(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\Big)\right)^{\frac{n}{2p}}

(4.67)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(4.69)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(1+\|V(Du)\|^{\frac{n(q-p)}{p}}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\left({L}^{-1}\Big(\|V(Du)\|^{2n(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\Big)\right)^{\frac{n}{2p}}\right)

(4.71)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

with
C=C(\Theta,n,p,{q},r,\alpha)
and where we used that

(4.72)

(1+x)\big(1+L^{-1}(x^{\frac{2}{p}})\big)^{\frac{n}{2p}}\leq C\left(1+x\big(L^{-1}(x^{\frac{2}{p}})\big)^{\frac{n}{2p}}\right).

Indeed, if

and

that yields

Multiplying the last two inequalities we get

(1+x)\big(1+L^{-1}(x^{\frac{2}{p}})\big)^{\frac{n}{2p}}\leq Cx\big(L^{-1}(x^{\frac{2}{p}})\big)^{\frac{n}{2p}}

and summing the results we end up with (
4.72
).
Note that, in case

, estimate (
4.65
) reads as

(4.73)

\displaystyle||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{\rho}_{0}})}

and this ends the proof. Remark also that only assumption i) is needed to achieve this case.

In case

4.64
) in the right hand side of (
4.65
) thus getting

(4.76)

\displaystyle||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{\rho}_{0}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(1+\frac{\|V(Du)\|^{\frac{q-p}{p}\frac{rn}{r-n}}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}}{\log^{{\frac{\alpha n}{2p(r-n)}}}\left(e+L^{-1}\Big(\|V(Du)\|^{2n(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\Big)\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Now, we distinguish between the case

\frac{q-p}{p}\frac{rn}{r-n}<1
and the case
\frac{q-p}{p}\frac{rn}{r-n}=1
.

Case

\mathbf{\frac{q-p}{p}\frac{rn}{r-n}<1{\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{r}}}

In this case, as long as

) implies

\displaystyle||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{\rho}_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\Big(1+\|V(Du)\|^{{\frac{q-p}{p}\frac{rn}{r-n}}}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)}\Big)}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

By Young’s inequality with exponents
\left(\frac{p(r-n)}{rn(q-p)},\frac{p(r-n)}{p(r-n)-rn(q-p)}\right)
, which is legitimate in this case, we deduce that

(4.79)

\displaystyle||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{\rho}_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|V(Du)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}})}+{\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n(r-n)}{p(r-n)-rn(q-p)}}}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{r-n}{p(r-n)-rn(q-p)}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and the iteration Lemma

concludes the proof in this case.

Case

\mathbf{\frac{q-p}{p}\frac{rn}{r-n}=1{\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\frac{q}{p}=1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{r}}}

In this case, estimate (
4.76
) reads as

(4.80)

\displaystyle||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{\rho}_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(1+\frac{\|V(Du)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}})}}{\log^{{\frac{\alpha n}{2p(r-n)}}}\left(e+L^{-1}\Big(\|V(Du)\|^{2n(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}})}\Big)\right)}\right)

(4.82)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(4.86)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\frac{\|V(Du)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}})}}{\log^{{\frac{\alpha n}{2p(r-n)}}}\left(e+L^{-1}\Big(\|V(Du)\|^{2n(q-p)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}})}\Big)\right)}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Define the function
\widehat{L}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
by setting

(4.87)

\widehat{L}(s)=:\log^{{\frac{\alpha n}{2p(r-n)}}}\left(e+L^{-1}\Big(s^{2n(q-p)}\Big)\right)

and note that
\widehat{L}
is a continuous strictly increasing function by virtue of the definition of

and since

. Hence

\displaystyle||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{\rho}_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\frac{\|V(Du)\|_{{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}})}}}{\widehat{L}\Big(\|V(Du)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}})}\Big)}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}[(\widehat{L}^{-1})^{*}]^{-1}\Big({\|V(Du)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}})}}\Big)\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where, as long as
\widehat{L}
is increasing and diverging at
\infty
, we used Lemma
2.3
with
\widehat{L}
in place of
{\varphi}
and
[(\widehat{L}^{-1})^{*}]^{-1}(t)
in place of
{\psi}
. The function
[(\widehat{L}^{-1})^{*}]^{-1}(t)

is clearly concave as it is the inverse of a polar function which is always convex.
Using Young’s inequality at (

) with
\widehat{L}^{-1}
in place of
\Phi
, that is

st={\frac{1}{2}s\,(2\,t)}\leq(\widehat{L}^{-1})^{*}\left({\frac{1}{2}s}\right)+\widehat{L}^{-1}({2t}),

yields

\displaystyle||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{\rho}_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tilde{R}_{0}})}

\displaystyle+\widehat{L}^{-1}\Bigg(\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{R_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{(\tilde{R}_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{R_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Hence, we may use Lemma
2.1
to get

(4.90)

\displaystyle||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\widehat{L}^{-1}\Bigg(\frac{C}{(R_{0}-\rho_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{R_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C}{(R_{0}-\rho_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{R_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By the definition of
\widehat{L}
at (
4.87
), we can easily get the expression of
\widehat{L}^{-1}(\sigma)
arguing as follows

\widehat{L}^{-1}(\sigma)=s\Longleftrightarrow\widehat{L}(s)=\sigma\Longleftrightarrow\sigma=\log^{{\frac{\alpha n}{2p(r-n)}}}\left(e+L^{-1}\Big(s^{2n(q-p)}\Big)\right)

i.e.

\exp\left(\sigma^{{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}}}\right)=e+L^{-1}\Big(s^{2n(q-p)}\Big)\Longleftrightarrow s^{2n(q-p)}=L\left(\exp\left(\sigma^{{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}}}\right)-e\right)

that yields the desired expression of
\widehat{L}^{-1}(\sigma)

\widehat{L}^{-1}(\sigma)=\left[L\left(\exp\left(\sigma^{{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}}}\right)-e\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2n(q-p)}}.

Further, recalling that
L(t)=t^{r-n}\log^{\alpha}(e+t)
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{L}^{-1}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(\exp\big(\sigma^{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}}\big)-e\Big)^{r-n}\log^{\alpha}\Big(\exp(\sigma^{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}})-e+e\Big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2n(q-p)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(\exp\big(\sigma^{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}}\big)-e\Big)^{r-n}\Big(\sigma^{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2n(q-p)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\exp\big(\sigma^{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}}\big)-e\Big)^{\frac{r-n}{2n(q-p)}}\sigma^{\frac{p(r-n)}{n^{2}(q-p)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\Big(\exp\big(\sigma^{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}}\big)-e\Big)\sigma^{\frac{2p}{n}}\right]^{\frac{r-n}{2n(q-p)}}.

Since
\frac{q-p}{p}\frac{rn}{r-n}=1
we have
\frac{r-n}{n}=r\frac{q-p}{p}
and so

(4.91)

\widehat{L}^{-1}(\sigma)\leq\left(\exp\Big(\sigma^{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}}\Big)\sigma^{\frac{2p}{n}}\right)^{\frac{r}{2p}}=\exp\Big(\frac{r}{2p}\cdot\sigma^{\frac{2p(r-n)}{\alpha n}}\Big)\sigma^{\frac{r}{n}}.

Inserting (
4.91
) in (

) we get

\displaystyle||V(Du)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho_{0}})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{C}{(R_{0}-\rho_{0})^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{R_{0}}}V(Du)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

i.e. the conclusion.

5.
The approximation

In this section we give only a sketch of the approximation procedure that comes along quite standard arguments (in view of the assumption
F(x,\xi)=\tilde{F}(x,|\xi|)
).
Let us start recalling an approximation Lemma whose proof can be achieved arguing exactly as in
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
(see also
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
F:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
be a Carathéodory function satisfying assumptions (
1.3
)–(
1.4
) and (
1.6
) in Section 1. Then there exists a sequence of Carathéodory functions
F_{k}:\,\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
monotonically convergent to
F(x,\xi)
such that

i)
F_{k}(x,\xi)=\tilde{F}_{k}(x,|\xi|)
, for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

ii)
F_{k}(x,\xi)\leq F_{k+1}(x,\xi)\leq F(x,\xi)
, for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}

iii) there exist
\ell
independent of
k
and
\ell_{1}
depending also on
k
such that

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{p/2}\leq F_{k}(x,\xi)\leq\ell(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{q/2}\\[2.84526pt]
&F_{k}(x,\xi)\leq\ell_{1}(k)(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{p/2}\end{array}\right.

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

iv) there exists a constant
\tilde{\lambda}
depending only on
\lambda,p
such that

(5.2)

\tilde{\lambda}\,(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\eta|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}(F_{k})_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}(x,\xi)\eta_{i}\eta_{j},

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

v) there exist
\tilde{\Lambda}
independent of
k
and
\tilde{\Lambda}_{1}
depending also on
k
such that

(5.3)

|(F_{k})_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}(x,\xi)|\leq\,\tilde{\Lambda}\,(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\quad|(F_{k})_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}(x,\xi)|\leq\,\tilde{\Lambda}_{1}(k)\,(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

vi) there exist
C
independent of
k
and
C_{1}
depending also on
k
such that

(5.4)

|(F_{k})_{\xi x}(x,\xi)|\leq Ch(x)(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\quad|(F_{k})_{\xi x}(x,\xi)|\leq C_{1}(k)h(x)(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Next, for a smooth mollifier
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{1}(0))
, let us consider

F_{\varepsilon,k}(x,\xi)=\int_{B_{1}(0)}F_{k}(x+\varepsilon y,\xi)\varphi(y)\,dy.

It is easy to check that
F_{\varepsilon,k}
still satisfy (
5.1
)–(
5.4
) of previous Lemma and moreover

(5.5)

|D_{x}D_{\xi}F_{\varepsilon,k}(x,\xi)|\leq C_{2}(K)h_{\varepsilon}(x)(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

where
h_{\varepsilon}
denotes the usual mollification of
h
.

Now fix a ball
B_{R_{0}}\Subset\Omega
and for
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
a local minimizer of the functional at (
1.1
), let
v_{\varepsilon,k}\in u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R_{0}})
be the unique solution to the problem

\min\left\{\widehat{F}_{\varepsilon,k}(v,B_{R_{0}}):\,\,\,v\in u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R_{0}})\right\},

where

\widehat{F}_{\varepsilon,k}(v,B_{R_{0}})=\int_{B_{R_{0}}}F_{\varepsilon,k}(x,Dv)\,dx.

By virtue of (
5.1
)–(
5.5
), we may use classical regularity results (see for example
[
20
]
) to deduce that
v_{\varepsilon,k}\in W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(B_{R_{0}})
and that
(\mu^{2}+|Dv_{\varepsilon,k}|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}Dv_{\varepsilon,k}\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(B_{R_{0}})
for every
\varepsilon>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
The left inequality in (
5.1
) implies that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R_{0}}}(\mu^{2}+|Dv_{\varepsilon,k}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R_{0}}}F_{\varepsilon,k}(x,Dv_{\varepsilon,k})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R_{0}}}F_{\varepsilon,k}(x,Du)\,dx,

where we used the minimality of
v_{\varepsilon,k}
with
u
as an admissible test function.
Since
F_{k}(x,Du)\in L^{1}(B_{R_{0}})
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, by the properties of the convolutions, we get

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{R_{0}}}(\mu^{2}+|Dv_{\varepsilon,k}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\leq\int_{B_{R_{0}}}F_{k}(x,Du)\,dx\leq\int_{B_{R_{0}}}F(x,Du)\,dx,

where the last inequality is due to ii) of Lemma
5.1
.
Therefore
\big(v_{\varepsilon,k}\big)_{\varepsilon}
is a bounded sequence in
u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R_{0}})
and so there exists
v_{k}\in u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R_{0}})
such that

(5.7)

v_{\varepsilon,k}\rightharpoonup v_{k}\qquad\text{in}\,\,u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R_{0}}).

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The weak lower semicontinuity of the
L^{p}
norm implies

\int_{B_{R_{0}}}(\mu^{2}+|Dv_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{R_{0}}}(\mu^{2}+|Dv_{\varepsilon,k}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\leq\int_{B_{R_{0}}}F(x,Du)\,dx

and hence
there exists
v\in u+W^{1,p}(B_{R_{0}})
such that

(5.8)

v_{k}\rightharpoonup v\qquad\text{in}\,\,u+W^{1,p}(B_{R_{0}}).

as
k\to+\infty
. On the other hand, since
v_{\varepsilon,k}\in W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(B_{R_{0}})
,
(\mu^{2}+|Dv_{\varepsilon,k}|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}Dv_{\varepsilon,k}\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(B_{R_{0}})
and the energy density
F_{\varepsilon,k}
satisfies (
5.1
)–(
5.5
), we can use the a priori estimate at (
1.9
) to deduce that

(5.9)

\displaystyle||\mu^{2}+Dv_{\varepsilon,k}||_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\frac{R_{0}}{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}G\left[{\frac{C}{(R_{0}/2)^{n}}}\left(\int_{B_{R_{0}}}\,(1+|Dv_{\varepsilon,k}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right]}

(5.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}G\left({\frac{C}{(R_{0}/2)^{n}}}\left(\int_{B_{R_{0}}}F(x,Du)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)},

where, in the last inequality, we used (
5.6
) and the monotonicity of the function
G
defined in (
4.1
).

Therefore, passing to the limit first as
\varepsilon\to 0
and then as
k\to+\infty
, we infer that

(5.12)

v_{\varepsilon,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}v\qquad\text{weakly * in}\,\,W^{1,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{R_{0}})

and, by the lower semicontinuity of the norm, also

(5.13)

\displaystyle||(\mu^{2}+Dv^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\frac{R_{0}}{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}G\left(C\left(\int_{B_{R_{0}}}F(x,Du)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)}.

The next step consists in
showing that
v=u
a.e. in
B_{R_{0}}
, and this follows by the strict convexity of the map
\xi\to F(x,\xi)
through standard arguments that can be found, for instance, in
[
12
,
4
,
2
]
.
At this point, the conclusion of the proofs of Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.4
follows in view of the definition of the function
G(t)
at (
4.1
).

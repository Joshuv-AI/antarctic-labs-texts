Immersions with small normal curvature
Otis Chodosh, Chao Li

Abstract
. We study Gromov’s problem concerning minimal normal curvature immersions in the unit ball. In particular, we determine the minimal possible value of the normal curvature of an S n × S 1 S^{n}\times S^{1} . We also prove a differentiable sphere theorem and an existence result for minimizers in this context.



1.
Introduction

Fix a smooth manifold
X
. For an immersion into the closed unit
N
-ball
f:X\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
with second fundamental form
\mathbf{A}
, we define the
maximal normal curvature
of
f
as

(1)

\mathfrak{c}(f):=\sup_{|u|=1}|\mathbf{A}(u,u)|,

where
|u|
is measured with respect to the pullback metric
g=f^{*}g_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. In this paper we study Gromov’s
minimal curvature invariant
(see
[
Gro22a
,
Gro22b
,
Gro25
]
)

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{N}(X):=\inf\mathfrak{c}(f)

where the infimum is taken over immersions
f:X^{n}\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
. It is straightforward to see that
\mathcal{C}_{N}(X)
is a decreasing function of
N
. Its behavior as
N
tends to infinity captures intriguing topological properties of
X
, and is the main concern of this paper.

We list the closed
X
for which the value of
\mathcal{C}_{N}(X)
is known for sufficiently large
N
.

(1)

The
n
-sphere has
\mathcal{C}_{N}(S^{n})=1
for
N\geq n+1
(achieved by the inclusion
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}\subset\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
). The maximum principle implies that
\mathcal{C}_{N}(X)\geq 1
always holds.

(2)

The
n
-torus has
\mathcal{C}_{N}(T^{n})=\sqrt{\frac{3n}{n+2}}
for
N\gg n
. Immersions achieving this bound were found by Gromov using spherical designs
[
Gro22a
,
Gro22b
]
. These immersions were shown to be optimal by Petrunin
[
Pet24a
]
.

(3)

The aforementioned result on
T^{n}
implies that for any closed manifold
X
,
\mathcal{C}_{N}(X)<\sqrt{3}
for
N\gg n

[
Gro22a
,
Gro22b
]
. This may be obtained by composing an inflated Whitney embedding of
X
into
\mathbb{R}^{M}
(with arbitrarily small normal curvature) and an immersion
T^{M}\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
with the optimal normal curvature.

(4)

The projective planes have:
\mathcal{C}_{N}(\mathbb{K}P^{2})=\sqrt{\frac{4}{3}}
for
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O}\}
and
N\geq 5
for
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
,
N\geq 8
for
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
,
N\geq 14
for
\mathbb{H}P^{2}
,
N\geq 26
for
\mathbb{O}P^{2}
. The “Veronese immersions” achieve these bounds (cf. Section
3.1
) and these immersions were shown to be optimal by Petrunin
[
Pet24b
]
.

1.1.
The minimal curvature invariant of
S^{n}\times S^{1}

One of our main results is to compute the minimal curvature invariant of
S^{n}\times S^{1}
(for
N
sufficiently large):

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 2
we have
\mathcal{C}_{N}(S^{n}\times S^{1})=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
for
N\geq 2n+4
.

An immersion of
S^{n}\times S^{1}
into
\mathbb{C}P^{n+1}
with low normal curvature was constructed by Naitoh
[
Nai81
, Theorem 6.5]
. Lifting this to an immersion into
\mathbb{S}^{2n+3}
via the Hopf map and then composing with the inclusion into
\mathbb{B}^{2n+4}
yields an immersion with normal curvature
\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
. In Section
3
we describe a (seemingly) different method for finding a map with the same curvature bound.

The bound in the other direction is obtained as follows. We recall that in
[
Pet24a
]
, Petrunin proved
\mathcal{C}_{N}(X^{n})\geq\sqrt{\frac{3n}{n+2}}
for a closed manifold
X^{n}
not admitting a metric of positive scalar curvature. A key idea in this paper is to generalize this method to other curvature conditions.

Proposition 2
.

For
X
closed, if
f:X\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
has
\mathfrak{c}(f)<\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
then the induced metric
g=f^{*}g_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is conformally equivalent to a metric of positive sectional curvature.

Since
S^{n}\times S^{1}
does not admit a metric of positive Ricci (much less sectional) curvature, this proves the lower bound.

Remark 3
.

We observe that
\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
is also the normal curvature of the Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{R}P^{3}
(see Section
3.1
and note that
\varphi_{3,2}
factors through
\mathbb{R}P^{3}
). In light of Petrunin’s work
[
Pet24b
]
, it is natural to conjecture that if
f:X^{3}\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
has
\mathfrak{c}(f)\leq\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
then
X^{3}
is diffeomorphic to
S^{3},\mathbb{R}P^{3}
, or
S^{2}\times S^{1}
.

1.2.
Existence of minimizers

The conformal factor in Proposition
2
is explicit and uniformly bounded. As such, examining the proof of Proposition
2
, we see that if
f:X^{n}\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
has
\mathfrak{c}(f)\leq\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}-\delta
then
(X^{n},g=f^{*}g_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})
has intrinsic diameter
\leq D(\delta)
. In particular, this implies existence of (weak) minimizers in (
2
). More precisely the following result follows immediately from the intrinsic diameter bound and standard compactness results for immersions:

Corollary 4
.

For
X^{n}
closed, suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{N}(X^{n})<\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
. Then there exists a
C^{1,1}
-immersion
f:X^{n}\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
so that
f
has
|A(u,u)|\leq\mathcal{C}_{N}(X)
for almost every
p\in X
and unit vector
u\in T_{p}X
.

One may define several notions of weak normal curvature of a map
f
and we expect that
f
have weak normal curvature
equal
to
\mathcal{C}_{N}(X)
for most of these notions. We hope to investigate this, along with other properties of critical points elsewhere.

1.3.
A differentiable sphere theorem for immersions with small normal curvatures

The technique used to prove Proposition
2
can be generalized to other curvature conditions. For example, by considering isotropic curvature, we obtain

Theorem 5
.

For
X^{n}
closed, if
f:X\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
has
\mathfrak{c}(f)<\sqrt{\frac{4}{3}}
then
X
is diffeomorphic to a standard
n
-sphere.

This answers a question of Petrunin
[
Pet24b
, §5]
. The proof of Theorem
5
relies on the deep result by Brendle–Schoen
[
BS09
]
that a closed, strictly PIC-2 manifold is diffeomorphic to a standard spherical space form.

1.4.
General Veronese tensor products

The map in (
3
) is part of a more general family of maps formed by taking tensor products of “Veronese maps” from spheres (immersions of spheres formed by normalizing an orthonormal basis of an eigenspace of the Laplacian). By optimizing within this class we find other immersions with small normal curvature including:

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{N}(S^{2}\times S^{2})\leq\sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}
for
N\geq 14
(see Proposition
8
).

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{N}(S^{2}\times T^{2})\leq\sqrt{\frac{9}{5}}
for
N\geq 24
(see Proposition
10
).

Whether these maps achieve the minimal curvature is an intriguing question. We pose possible intrinsic curvature conditions that imply the minimality. See Section
5
for further discussion.

On the use of AI

We began working on this problem after an internal DeepMind AI model (cf.
[

]
) produced the map

f:S^{n}\times S^{1}\ni(x,t)\mapsto(r_{1}\cos t\,x,r_{1}\sin t\,x,r_{2}\cos 2t,r_{2}\sin 2t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n+4},

where
r_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}},r_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}
and proved that
\mathfrak{c}(f)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
(in response to a request for low curvature immersions from

). We found this exciting and it inspired us to consider this problem. This also lead us to consider the general ansatz discussed in Section
3
. While finishing a draft of this article, we discovered that Naitoh had previously found
[
Nai81
]
a similar (but not identical) immersion with the same bound as in (

). As such, we cannot give the model credit for discovering (

), but do note that we would have been extremely unlikely to find the reference
[

We also found AI assistance useful in other ways. The DeepMind model found the curvature condition in Conjecture

in response to a request to “reverse engineer” a curvature condition based on considerations of the model case. We also made use of publicly available LLMs (Gemini 2.5 and 3 Deep Think as well as ChatGPT 5.2 Pro). These models provided rigorous and semi-rigorous reasoning and numerical code generation that helped us find the maps in Section
3.3
(although this took significant iteration). These models were also able to help with routine but tedious proofs; namely, they helped us find proofs of optimizations involving second fundamental form bounds (cf. Lemma

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The Gauss equation

We take the curvature convention so that if
X\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an immersion with second fundamental form
\mathbf{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
, the induced metric on
X
has Riemann curvature tensor satisfying

(4)

\Rm(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})=\left\langle\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{l}),\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{k})\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{k}),\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{l})\right\rangle

for orthonormal tangent vectors
\{e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l}\}
. In particular, in this convention,
\Rm(e_{i},e_{j},e_{j},e_{i})
is a sectional curvature of
X
. Taking trace, we have that the Ricci curvature of the induced metric on
X
satisfies

(5)

\Ric(e_{i},e_{i})=\left\langle\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i}),\mathbf{H}\right\rangle-|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},\cdot)|^{2}.

It is convenient to also recall Petrunin’s version
[
Pet24a
, 2.1]
of the traced Gauss equations for an immersion
X^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}

(6)

R=\frac{3}{2}|\mathbf{H}|^{2}-\frac{n(n+2)}{2}\fint_{u\in S_{p}X}|\mathbf{A}(u,u)|^{2}

where
R
is the scalar curvature,
\mathbf{H}=\tr\mathbf{A}
is the mean curvature, and
S_{p}X
is the unit sphere in
T_{p}X
with respect to the pullback metric.

2.2.
Conformal change of curvature

We recall also some standard formulae for the behavior of curvature under a conformal change. For
(X^{n},g)
a Riemannian manifold and
\tilde{g}=e^{2\psi}g
for
\psi\in C^{\infty}(X)
. Then the Riemann and Ricci curvature tensors with respect to
\tilde{g}
satisfy

(7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Rm}

\displaystyle=e^{2\psi}\left(\Rm-(\nabla^{2}\psi)\mathbin{\owedge}g+(d\psi\otimes d\psi)\mathbin{\owedge}g-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}g\mathbin{\owedge}g\right)

(8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Ric}

\displaystyle=\Ric-(n-2)(\nabla^{2}\psi)+(n-2)(d\psi\otimes d\psi)-(\Delta\psi+(n-2)|\nabla\psi|^{2})g,

where
\mathbin{\owedge}
is the Kulkarni–Nomizu product defined by

(h\mathbin{\owedge}k)(w,x,y,z)=h(w,z)k(x,y)+h(x,y)k(w,z)-h(w,y)k(x,z)-h(x,z)k(w,y).

See, e.g.
[
Lee18
, Theorem 7.30]
.

3.
Immersions with small normal curvature

In this section we use tensor products of higher Veronese immersions to construct low curvature immersions of products of spheres. We note that the construction here is closely related to the tensor product construction of submanifolds of finite type as considered by B.-Y. Chen in
[
Che84
,
Che93
]
, although it seems like this has not been considered in the context of (
2
).

3.1.
Higher Veronese immersions

We begin by recalling the notion of higher Veronese immersions (often called the
standard minimal immersion

S^{n}\to S^{m}
). The calculations here are well-known, cf.
[
Tak66
,
dCW71
,
Tsu83
]
but we include them for completeness.

Recall that the spectrum of the Laplacian on the round sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}})
is
\ell(\ell+n-1)
,
\ell=0,1,2\dots
and the corresponding eigenspaces
E(n,\ell)
have dimension
D(n,\ell):=\binom{n+\ell-1}{n-1}+\binom{n+\ell-2}{n-1}
. Fix
n,\ell
, an
L^{2}
-orthonormal eigenbasis
f_{1},\dots,f_{D}
and then define
\varphi=\varphi_{n,\ell}:S^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{D(n,\ell)}
by
\varphi=(|\mathbb{S}^{n}|/D)^{\frac{1}{2}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{D})
. Since an isometry of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
induces a linear isometry of
E(n,\ell)
it’s clear that
|\varphi|^{2}=C
is constant. The given normalization ensures that
C=1
. Indeed, we may compute

C|\mathbb{S}^{n}|=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\varphi|^{2}=|\mathbb{S}^{n}|D^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|f_{i}|^{2}=|\mathbb{S}^{n}|.

Similar reasoning implies that
\varphi^{*}g_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}
is a round metric. Write

\varphi^{*}g_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}=D^{-1}|\mathbb{S}^{n}|\sum_{i=1}^{D}df_{i}\otimes df_{i}=\rho\,g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}

for
\rho=\rho(n,\ell)>0
. Then

\displaystyle n\rho D=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|df_{i}|^{2}=\ell(\ell+n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|f_{i}|^{2}=\ell(\ell+n-1)D

so we find that

(9)

\rho(n,\ell)=\frac{\ell(\ell+n-1)}{n}.

We now turn to the extrinsic curvature of
\varphi
. Since the coefficients of
\varphi
are formed from an eigenbasis, we have that
\varphi
is a minimal immersion into
\mathbb{S}^{D-1}
. Thus, its Euclidean mean curvature satisfies
|\mathbf{H}|=n
. As above, symmetry implies that the immersion is
isotropic
in the sense that
|\mathbf{A}(u,u)|
is constant for all unit tangent vectors
u
. We define
\lambda=\lambda(n,\ell)
by
|\mathbf{A}(u,u)|^{2}=\lambda|u|_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}}^{4}
(note that the right hand side is
not
the pullback metric!). Note that (
9
) gives that the pullback metric
\rho\,g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
has scalar curvature
\frac{n(n-1)}{\rho}
. Using these facts in (
6
) (taking care to use the geometric scaling for the norm of
u
) we find

(10)

\lambda(n,\ell)=\frac{3n}{n+2}\rho(n,\ell)^{2}-\frac{2(n-1)}{(n+2)}\rho(n,\ell).

3.2.
Tensor products of Veronese immersions

We now fix
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
\vec{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}
. We set
X=\prod_{m=1}^{M}S^{n_{m}}
. For
\vec{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}
we set
L=L(\vec{n},\vec{\ell})=\prod_{m=1}^{M}D(n_{m},\ell_{m})
and then define the tensor product maps

\varphi_{\vec{\ell}}:X\to\mathbb{R}^{L},\qquad(x_{1},\dots,x_{M})\mapsto\otimes_{m=1}^{M}\varphi_{n_{m},\ell_{m}}(x_{m}).

Note that if
\ell_{m}=0
our convention is that
\varphi_{n_{m},0}=1:S^{n_{m}}\to\mathbb{R}
is a constant map. In particular,
\varphi_{\vec{\ell}}
will not be an immersion in this case.

Using

d\varphi_{\vec{\ell}}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\varphi_{n_{1},\ell_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes d\varphi_{n_{m},\ell_{m}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varphi_{n_{M},\ell_{M}}

along with
d\varphi_{n,\ell}\perp\varphi_{n,\ell}=0
and
|\varphi_{n,\ell}|=1
we find

(\varphi_{\vec{\ell}})^{*}g_{\mathbb{R}^{L}}=(\rho(n_{1},\ell_{1})g_{\mathbb{S}^{n_{1}}},\dots,\rho(n_{M},\ell_{M})g_{\mathbb{S}^{n_{M}}}).

We now consider a geodesic
\gamma(t)=(\gamma_{1}(t),\dots,\gamma_{M}(t))
with respect to the product metric
(g_{\mathbb{S}^{n_{1}}},\dots,g_{\mathbb{S}^{n_{M}}})
. Let
(x_{1},\dots,x_{M})=\gamma(0)
and
(u_{1},\dots,u_{M})=\gamma^{\prime}(0)
. Note that we are
not
assuming that
\gamma
has unit speed. We compute

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{t=0}\varphi_{\vec{\ell}}\,(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\varphi_{n_{1},\ell_{1}}(x_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{\varphi_{n_{m},\ell_{m}}}(u_{m},u_{m})\otimes\dots\otimes\varphi_{n_{M},\ell_{M}}(x_{M})

\displaystyle+2\sum_{a<b}\varphi_{n_{1},\ell_{1}}(x_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes d\varphi_{n_{a},\ell_{a}}(u_{a})\otimes\dots\otimes d\varphi_{n_{b},\ell_{b}}(u_{b})\otimes\dots\otimes\varphi_{n_{M},\ell_{M}}(x_{M}).

Observe that the inner product of the two sums vanishes, but distinct terms in the first sum are not pairwise orthogonal since for
a\neq b
we have (writing
\varphi_{a}
for
\varphi_{n_{a},\ell_{a}}
and so on)

\left\langle\cdots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{\varphi_{a}}(u_{a},u_{a})\otimes\cdots,\cdots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{\varphi_{b}}(u_{b},u_{b})\otimes\cdots\right\rangle=\rho(n_{a},\ell_{a})\rho(n_{b},\ell_{b})|u_{a}|^{2}|u_{b}|^{2}

where we used
\left\langle\mathbf{A}_{\varphi_{a}}(u_{a},u_{a}),\varphi_{a}\right\rangle=-|d\varphi_{a}(u_{a})|^{2}=-\rho(n_{a},\ell_{a})|u_{a}|^{2}
. Again, we emphasize that here we have written
|\cdot|
for the norm with respect to
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
, not the pullback metric. We use this convention below (note that we also write the norm of a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{L}
, where we unambiguously use the Euclidean inner product). Putting these together, we find

\left|\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{t=0}\varphi_{\vec{\ell}}\,(\gamma(t))\right|^{2}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\lambda(n_{m},\ell_{m})|u_{m}|^{4}+6\sum_{a<b}\rho(n_{a},\ell_{a})\rho(n_{b},\ell_{b})|u_{a}|^{2}|u_{b}|^{2}.

Now, we fix
\alpha:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}\to[0,\infty)
with compact support and

\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\alpha(\vec{\ell})=1.

It will be convenient to identify
\alpha
with the associated compactly supported probability measure
\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\alpha(\vec{\ell})\delta_{\vec{\ell}}
on
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}
. For any such measure,
\alpha(\vec{\ell})
, we define

(11)

F:X\to\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad F(x)=\bigoplus_{\vec{\ell}\in\supp\alpha}\alpha(\vec{\ell})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{\vec{\ell}}\,(x),

for
N=\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in\supp\alpha}L(\vec{n},\vec{\ell})
. Note that
|F(x)|=1
for any
x\in X
, so the image of
F
lies in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
(actually
\partial\mathbb{B}^{N}
). Moreover,

|u|_{F^{*}g_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}^{2}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\alpha(\vec{\ell})\rho(n_{m},\ell_{m})\right)|u_{m}|^{2}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}[\rho_{m}]|u_{m}|^{2}

where
\rho_{m}:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}\to[0,\infty)
is
\rho_{m}(\vec{\ell})=\rho(n_{m},\ell_{m})
and
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
is the expectation with respect to the probability measure
\mu
. In particular we find that
F
is an immersion if and only if
\mathbb{E}[\rho_{m}]>0
for
m=1,\dots,M
. Similarly we may compute

\displaystyle|\mathbf{A}_{F}(u,u)|^{2}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{m}]|u_{m}|^{4}+6\sum_{a<b}\mathbb{E}[\rho_{a}\rho_{b}]\,|u_{a}|^{2}|u_{b}|^{2}

where
\lambda_{m}(\ell)=\lambda(n_{m},\ell)
, and by definition,

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{m}]=\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\alpha(\vec{\ell})\lambda(n_{m},l_{m}),\quad\mathbb{E}[\rho_{a}\rho_{b}]=\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\alpha(\ell)\rho(n_{a},\ell_{a})\rho(n_{b},\ell_{b}).

Let
A
be the symmetric
M\times M
matrix with diagonal coefficients
A_{mm}=\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{m}]
and off diagonal coefficients
A_{ab}=3\mathbb{E}[\rho_{a}\rho_{b}]
. Define
G\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
by
G_{m}=\mathbb{E}[\rho_{m}]
. Finally, define
U\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
by
U_{m}=|u_{m}|^{2}
. Note that
U
lies in the first quadrant:
U\in\mathcal{U}:=\{U_{m}\geq 0\}\setminus\{0\}
. The previous analysis thus gives that the normal curvature of
F
satisfies

\mathfrak{c}(F)^{2}=\max_{U\in\mathcal{U}}\frac{U^{T}AU}{U^{T}GG^{T}U}.

In particular, we have proven:

Proposition 6
.

Fix a probability measure
\mu=\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\alpha(\vec{\ell})\delta_{\vec{\ell}}
with compact support. Assume that
\mathbb{E}[\rho_{m}]>0
for
m=1,\dots,M
. Then the map
F:X\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
defined in (
11
) is an immersion into
\mathbb{B}^{N}
. Let
s\geq 0
be minimal so that

B(s):=sGG^{T}-A

is copositive. Then
\mathfrak{c}(F)=\sqrt{s}
.

Recall that a (symmetric) matrix
B
is
copositive
if
U^{T}BU\geq 0
for all
U\in\mathcal{U}
.

Remark 7
.

Note that
F
is
isotropic
(in the sense that
|\mathbf{A}(u,u)|
is constant for all unit tangent vectors
u
) if and only if
B(s)=0
. This will be the case in the examples below.

3.3.
Low curvature examples

We consider small
M
and obtain measures
\mu
corresponding to low curvature maps (i.e. the parameter
s
is small). The following is a direct generalization of (
3
).

Proposition 8
.

Consider
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
. We have
\mathcal{C}_{N}(S^{n_{1}}\times S^{n_{2}})\leq\sqrt{\frac{2n_{2}+1}{n_{2}+1}}
for
N\geq(n_{1}+1)(n_{2}+1)+\frac{n_{2}(n_{2}+3)}{2}
.

Proof.

We consider

\mu=\alpha(1,1)\,\delta_{(1,1)}+\alpha(0,2)\,\delta_{(0,2)}.

Recall that
\rho(n,0)=0,\rho(n,1)=1,\rho(n,2)=\frac{2(n+1)}{n}
and
\lambda(n,0)=0,\lambda(n,1)=1,\lambda(n,2)=\frac{8(n+1)}{n}
. Then
G=(\alpha(1,1),\alpha(1,1)+\frac{2(n_{2}+1)}{n_{2}}\alpha(0,2))^{T}
and

A=\left(\begin{matrix}\alpha(1,1)&3\alpha(1,1)\\
3\alpha(1,1)&\alpha(1,1)+\frac{8(n_{2}+1)}{n_{2}}\alpha(0,2)\end{matrix}\right)

We seek
\alpha(1,1),\alpha(0,2),s
so that
B(s)=0
. This gives

\displaystyle s\alpha(1,1)^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha(1,1)

\displaystyle s\alpha(1,1)(\alpha(1,1)+\tfrac{2(n_{2}+1)}{n_{2}}\alpha(0,2))

\displaystyle=3\alpha(1,1)

\displaystyle s(\alpha(1,1)+\tfrac{2(n_{2}+1)}{n_{2}}\alpha(0,2))^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha(1,1)+\tfrac{8(n_{2}+1)}{n_{2}}\alpha(0,2).

We can solve this system to find

4.
Obstructions to low curvature maps

4.1.
A generalization of Petrunin’s angle estimate

The following lemma is a generalization of
[
Pet24a
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 12
.

Suppose that for
X
closed,
f:X\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
has
\mathfrak{c}(f)\leq c<2
. For
p\in X
write
\mathbf{x}=f(p)
and
\mathbf{x}^{\perp}
for the projection of
\mathbf{x}
to the normal bundle to
X
at
p
. Then
|\mathbf{x}|>0
and
|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|\geq 1+\frac{c}{2}(|\mathbf{x}|^{2}-1)
.

Proof.

Fix
p\in X
and set
\mathbf{x}=f(p)
. We first show that
\mathbf{x}\neq 0
. If
\mathbf{x}=0
, then fix any unit
\xi\in T_{p}X
and let
\gamma(t)=f(\exp_{p}(t\xi))
be the image of the geodesic in
X
(with respect to the pullback metric) through
p
with initial velocity
\xi
. Because
\gamma
has curvature
\leq c
, the bow lemma (comparison with a circular arc) implies that
|\gamma(\frac{\pi}{c})|\geq\frac{2}{c}>1
, a contradiction. Thus,
|\mathbf{x}|\neq 0
. This proves the first part of the lemma.

We now decompose
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}^{\perp}+\mathbf{x}^{\top}
. If
\mathbf{x}^{\top}\neq 0
then set
\xi=\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}}{|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|}
. Otherwise, choose any
\xi\in T_{p}X
. If
\mathbf{x}^{\perp}\neq 0
then set
\eta=\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|}
. Otherwise, choose any unit
\eta
perpendicular to
\xi
. Let
\Pi=\Span\{\eta,\xi\}
and note that
\mathbf{x}\in\Pi
.

Let
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}-c^{-1}\eta
and set
C=\{\mathbf{z}\in\Pi:|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{y}|=c^{-1}\}
, a circle of radius
c^{-1}
passing through
\mathbf{x}
, tangent to
\xi
there. Observe that if the assertion failed, i.e.

(12)

|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|<1+\frac{c}{2}(|\mathbf{x}|^{2}-1)

then
C
is not contained in the unit ball at the origin. Indeed, this would give

|\mathbf{y}|^{2}=|\mathbf{x}|^{2}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}-\frac{2}{c}\left\langle\mathbf{x},\eta\right\rangle=|\mathbf{x}|^{2}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}-\frac{2}{c}|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|>\frac{1}{c^{2}}-\frac{2}{c}+1=\left(1-\frac{1}{c}\right)^{2}.

At this point, we may conclude the proof exactly as in
[
Pet24a
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Indeed, if (
12
) held, we may form a comparison curve
\sigma*\gamma
using a unit speed circular arc
\sigma
from the origin that meets
\mathbf{x}
with tangent
\xi
. We can do this, so that
\sigma
may be concatenated with a parametrization of a portion of
C
, denoted by
\gamma
, in a
C^{1}
convex manner, so that
\gamma(t_{0})\not\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
. For
\tilde{\gamma}
the image under
f
of the geodesic in
X
from
p
with initial velocity
\xi
, the bow lemma (for
\sigma*\gamma
versus
\sigma*\tilde{\gamma}
; cf.
[
PB20
, Lemma 3.19]
) implies that
\tilde{\gamma}
exits
\mathbb{B}^{N}
. This is a contradiction.
∎

4.2.
Conformally positive sectional curvature

In this section we prove Proposition
2
. Consider
f:X^{n}\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
with
n\geq 2
. Later we will assume
\mathfrak{c}(f)<\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
.

Lemma 13
.

For
i\neq j
, we have
|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i})+\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})|^{2}+4|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{j})|^{2}\leq 4\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2}
.

Proof.

Take
u_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{i}\pm e_{j})
in (
1
) to get
|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i})+\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})\pm 2\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{j})|^{2}\leq 4\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2}
. The assertion then follows from the parallelogram identity.
∎

We are now ready for the proof of conformal positivity of sectional curvature.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

Let
g
denote the induced metric and consider
\psi=\psi(r)
for
r=|\mathbf{x}|
the ambient distance to the origin. We compute

\displaystyle\nabla\psi

\displaystyle=\psi^{\prime}(r)\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}}{r}

\displaystyle|\nabla\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{r^{2}}|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}r^{2}

\displaystyle=2g+2\left\langle\mathbf{x}^{\perp},\mathbf{A}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\psi

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{r^{2}}-\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{r^{3}}\right)\mathbf{x}^{\top}\otimes\mathbf{x}^{\top}+\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{r}g+\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{r}\left\langle\mathbf{x}^{\perp},\mathbf{A}\right\rangle

Let
\tilde{g}=e^{2\psi}g
. For
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
a
g
-orthonormal basis, fix the corresponding
\tilde{g}
-orthonormal basis
\tilde{e}_{i}=e^{-\psi}e_{i}
.

Then, for
i\neq j
, the conformal change of curvature formula (
7
) and the Gauss equations (
4
) gives

\displaystyle e^{2\psi}\tilde{\sec}_{ij}

\displaystyle=e^{2\psi}\widetilde{\Rm}(\tilde{e}_{i},\tilde{e}_{j},\tilde{e}_{j},\tilde{e}_{i})

\displaystyle=\Rm(e_{i},e_{j},e_{j},e_{i})-\nabla^{2}\psi(e_{i},e_{i})-\nabla^{2}\psi(e_{j},e_{j})+(d\psi(e_{i}))^{2}+(d\psi(e_{j}))^{2}-|\nabla\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{r}+\left\langle\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i}),\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})\right\rangle-|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{j})|^{2}-\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{r}\left\langle\mathbf{x}^{\perp},\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i})+\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{r^{2}}-\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{r^{3}}-\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)(\left\langle\mathbf{x},e_{i}\right\rangle^{2}+\left\langle\mathbf{x},e_{j}\right\rangle^{2})-\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{r^{2}}|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}.

We take
\psi(r)=-\frac{c}{2}r^{2}
for
c>0
to be chosen below. This gives

\displaystyle e^{2\psi}\tilde{\sec}_{ij}

\displaystyle=2c+\left\langle\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i}),\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})\right\rangle-|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{j})|^{2}+c\left\langle\mathbf{x}^{\perp},\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i})+\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+c^{2}(\left\langle\mathbf{x},e_{i}\right\rangle^{2}+\left\langle\mathbf{x},e_{j}\right\rangle^{2})-c^{2}|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2c+\left\langle\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i}),\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})\right\rangle-|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{j})|^{2}-c|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}||\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i})+\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})|

\displaystyle\qquad-c^{2}|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2c+\left\langle\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i}),\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})\right\rangle-|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{j})|^{2}-\frac{c}{4}|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i})+\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-c|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-c^{2}|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2c-\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2}+\left\langle\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i}),\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})\right\rangle+\frac{1-c}{4}|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i})+\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-c|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-c^{2}|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}.

We used Lemma
13
to bound the off-diagonal terms in the second fundamental form in the final inequality. By considering the model case (
3
), we see that in some cases the
\left\langle\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i}),\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})\right\rangle
terms cannot be controlled in a sharp manner. Thus, we must choose
c=3
so they cancel. With this choice we have

\displaystyle e^{2\psi}\widetilde{\sec}_{ij}

\displaystyle\geq 2c-\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2}+\frac{3-c}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i}),\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})\right\rangle+\frac{1-c}{4}|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i})|^{2}+\frac{1-c}{4}|\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-c|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-c^{2}|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle=6-\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{A}(e_{i},e_{i})|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{A}(e_{j},e_{j})|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-3|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-9|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 6-2\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2}-3|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-9|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 6-2\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2}+6|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-9|\mathbf{x}|^{2}.

Now, we assume that
\mathfrak{c}(f)\leq\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
. Lemma
12
gives

6|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-9|\mathbf{x}|^{2}\geq 6\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}(|\mathbf{x}|^{2}-1)\right)^{2}-9|\mathbf{x}|^{2}.

This is a quadratic in
|\mathbf{x}|^{2}
, and simple calculation proves the minimum of this expression on
|\mathbf{x}|\in[0,1]
is attained at
|\mathbf{x}|=1
, so

6|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-9|\mathbf{x}|^{2}\geq-3.

Putting this together, we get

e^{2\psi}\widetilde{\sec}_{ij}\geq 3-2\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2}.

This proves Proposition
2
.
∎

-orthonormal set and

Proof of Theorem

Proof.

15
gives

\frac{1}{2(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})}|\mathbf{S}|^{2}+|\mathbf{Y}|^{2}+|\mathbf{Z}|^{2}\leq 2(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2}

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle e^{2\psi}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda,\mu}(\tilde{e}_{1},\tilde{e}_{2},\tilde{e}_{3},\tilde{e}_{4})

\displaystyle\geq 2(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})(c-\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\langle\mathbf{W},\mathbf{X}\right\rangle+\frac{2-c}{4(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})}|\mathbf{S}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-c(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-c^{2}(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}.

Observing that

\frac{|\mathbf{S}|^{2}}{2(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})}=\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{2(1+\mu^{2})}|\mathbf{W}|^{2}+\frac{1+\mu^{2}}{2(1+\lambda^{2})}|\mathbf{X}|^{2}+\left\langle\mathbf{W},\mathbf{X}\right\rangle,

we thus choose
c=4
. Putting this together, we have

\displaystyle e^{2\psi}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda,\mu}(\tilde{e}_{1},\tilde{e}_{2},\tilde{e}_{3},\tilde{e}_{4})

\displaystyle\geq 2(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})(4-\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{2(1+\mu^{2})}|\mathbf{W}|^{2}-\frac{1+\mu^{2}}{2(1+\lambda^{2})}|\mathbf{X}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-4(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-16(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}.

Now, we observe that (
1
) gives

|\mathbf{W}|\leq(1+\mu^{2})\mathfrak{c}(f),\qquad|\mathbf{X}|\leq(1+\lambda^{2})\mathfrak{c}(f),

so

\displaystyle e^{2\psi}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda,\mu}(\tilde{e}_{1},\tilde{e}_{2},\tilde{e}_{3},\tilde{e}_{4})

\displaystyle\geq(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})(8-3\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad-4(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-16(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}.

We may rearrange this and use
|\mathbf{x}|^{2}=|\mathbf{x}^{\top}|^{2}+|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}
to write

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2\psi}}{(1+\lambda^{2})(1+\mu^{2})}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda,\mu}(\tilde{e}_{1},\tilde{e}_{2},\tilde{e}_{3},\tilde{e}_{4})

\displaystyle\geq 8-3\mathfrak{c}(f)^{2}+12|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-16|\mathbf{x}|^{2}.

Now assume that
\mathfrak{c}(f)<\sqrt{\frac{4}{3}}
. Using Lemma
12
(as in Proposition
2
), we find
12|\mathbf{x}^{\perp}|^{2}-16|\mathbf{x}|^{2}\geq-4
. Using this, we have
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda,\mu}(\tilde{e}_{1},\tilde{e}_{2},\tilde{e}_{3},\tilde{e}_{4})>0
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Remarks and Conjectures

A natural question is to understand the effects of other intrinsic curvature conditions on the minimal normal curvature of a closed manifold immersed in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
. In addition to
\sec>0
and strictly PIC-2, we also studied the sharp lower bound for
\mathfrak{c}(f)
, assuming the non-existence of metrics with positive Ricci curvature or PIC. One may speculate that under these stronger conditions might lead to better bounds as compared to Proposition
2
and Proposition
14
. However, similar computations yield the following.

Proposition 16
.

Let
X^{n}
be closed and admits an immersion
f:X\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
.

(1)

Suppose
X
does not admit any metric with
\Ric>0
. Then
\mathfrak{c}(f)<\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{2}}
.

(2)

Suppose
X
does not admit any metric with strictly PIC. Then
\mathfrak{c}(f)<\sqrt{\tfrac{4}{3}}
.

The constants in both statements are sharp.

In other words, one cannot distinguish between
\sec>0
and
\Ric>0
, nor strictly PIC and strictly PIC-2, with only
\mathcal{C}_{N}(X)
.

This also raises several questions (see also Remark
3
). For example, one may ask for the values of
\mathcal{C}_{N}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
or
\mathcal{C}_{N}(S^{2}\times T^{2})
. We had initially hoped to compute a lower bound matching Proposition
10
for
\mathcal{C}_{N}(S^{2}\times T^{2})
by considering bi-Ricci curvature (cf.
[
SY96
]
)

\BiRic(e_{1},e_{2})=\Ric(e_{1},e_{1})+\Ric(e_{2},e_{2})-\sec(e_{1},e_{2})

in place of sectional/PIC-2 in Propositions
2
and
14
(and then appeal to
[
BHJ24
]
for the fact that
S^{2}\times T^{2}
does not admit positive bi-Ricci curvature). However, it seems that this does not yield the sharp bound:

Conjecture 17
.

If
f:X^{4}\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
has
\mathfrak{c}(f)<\sqrt{\frac{12}{7}}
then
g=f^{*}g_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is conformally equivalent to a metric of positive bi-Ricci curvature.

Note that
\sqrt{\frac{12}{7}}\approx 1.31
while Proposition
10
gives
\mathcal{C}_{N}(S^{2}\times T^{2})\leq\sqrt{\frac{9}{5}}\approx 1.34
. Algebraic manipulations of the Gauss equation (
4
) show that Conjecture
17
holds if we assume that the immersion lies on the unit sphere
S^{N-1}
. However, it seems difficult to extend the approach used for Proposition
2
and
14
to the general case of a map to
\mathbb{B}^{N}
.

We also propose the following conjecture that could theoretically yield a matching lower bound:

Conjecture 18
.

For
X^{n}
closed, if
f:X^{n}\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
has
\mathfrak{c}(f)<\sqrt{\frac{9}{5}}
then
g=f^{*}g_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is conformally equivalent to a metric
\tilde{g}
so that if
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\leq\dots\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{4}
are the eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\Ric}
at any point, then
\tilde{\lambda}_{4}<\tilde{\lambda}_{1}+\tilde{\lambda}_{2}+\tilde{\lambda}_{3}
.

It is an interesting problem to determine whether or not
S^{2}\times T^{2}
admits such a metric. Note that since
\tilde{\lambda}_{3}\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{4}
, this condition (which could be rewritten as
2\,\widetilde{\Ric}(u)<\widetilde{\scal}
for all
\tilde{g}
-unit vectors) is stronger than
2
-convexity of
\widetilde{\Ric}
, i.e.
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}+\tilde{\lambda}_{2}>0
. As a first observation, by considering the totally geodesic
T^{3}\subset S^{2}\times T^{2}
, the method from
[
Str87
]
implies that one cannot find a
C^{1}
-path of metrics
\{g_{t}\}_{t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
, such that
g_{0}
is a product metric and
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}(\scal_{g_{t}}-2\Ric_{g_{t}})>0
everywhere on
S^{2}\times T^{2}
.

Protocol for volumetric quantification of intrahepatic triglyceride content in adults with obesity
Authors: Alba Camacho-Cardenosa, Antonio Clavero-Jimeno, Alejandro López-Vázquez, Mara Concepción, José L Martín-Rodríguez, Idoia Labayen, Jonatan R Ruiz, Rafael Cabeza
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe study describes a protocol for the semi-automatic volumetric quantification of intrahepatic triglyceride (IHTG) levels in individuals with obesity based on the Dixon technique. Here, we describe a step-by-step procedure for image acquisition and exportation, followed by segmentation of liver regions and subsequent quantification of IHTG. This protocol is designed for application in studies with a specific focus on adults with obesity.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Camacho-Cardenosa et al.1Subject areas: Health Sciences, Clinical Protocol, Metabolism, Systems biology

Before you begin

This protocol outlines the steps required to perform magnetic resonance imaging proton density fat fraction (MRI-PDFF) using a multi-echo Dixon technique for the volumetric quantification of IHTG content in adults with obesity. It may be more challenging in individuals with obesity compared to subjects with normal-weight, due to limitations in patient positioning and the need to carefully optimize image acquisition to avoid incomplete coverage or truncation of liver segments.

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatosis liver disease (MASLD), characterized by excessive accumulation of triglycerides in hepatocytes, is the most common chronic liver disease worldwide, affecting an estimated 30–40% of the population2 It is strongly associated with metabolic comorbidities, particularly type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease, placing a significant contributor to the global healthcare burden3

IHTG content is typically assessed using MRI, which is well-established, non-invasive gold standards for quantifying hepatic steatosis4 Among the various MRI techniques, image acquisition in this protocol is performed using the multi-echo Dixon sequence (6 echoes), which captures data at two specific time points: in-phase, when the signal of fat and water protons is aligned, and out-of-phase, when the signals are opposed. Each echo generates two datasets: one representing the water (W) signal and the other the fat (F) signal. These datasets are subsequently processed using a fitting algorithm that estimates and corrects signal effects, models the fat signal, and determines the proton density of both fat and water, allowing calculation of the fat fraction (FF), defined as F/(F + W), representing the percentage of fat within a specific tissue region5

IHTG content quantification is often performed using the mean FF from multiple regions of interest (ROIs) to simplify the process6,7 However, this approach may be limited by the heterogeneous distribution of lipids within the liver. We propose that a complete three-dimensional analysis helps avoid biases in the quantification of intrahepatic fat derived from the use of specific two-dimensional ROIs, or even from the use of a complete single slice. The inhomogeneity of ectopic fat distribution in the hepatic parenchyma is well-documented, especially in patients with metabolic diseases.8

Innovation

This protocol lies in the integration of multi-echo Dixon MRI-PDFF with a standardized whole-liver volumetric analysis workflow. Unlike conventional approaches based on selected ROIs, this method enables comprehensive assessment of IHTG distribution, reducing sampling bias associated with hepatic fat heterogeneity. In addition, the protocol incorporates optimized image acquisition and segmentation procedures specifically adapted for individuals with obesity, addressing common challenges such as positioning constraints and incomplete liver coverage. By combining acquisition, segmentation, and volumetric quantification into a single, reproducible pipeline, this approach improves accuracy, consistency, and applicability across studies. This represents a significant advancement over existing ROI-based methods by providing a more robust and representative estimation of liver fat content.

Timing: 30 min

Expected outcomes

This document provides researchers from any discipline with a comprehensive protocol for the acquisition and quantification of IHTG content in individuals with obesity (as patient positioning and proper field-of-view coverage may be challenging). One of the main strengths of this protocol is its implementation using free and open-source software tools developed for medical image informatics, image processing, and three-dimensional visualization.9 The protocol described herein, which relies exclusively on open-source tools, promotes methodological transparency, facilitates reproducibility, and enhances accessibility for the broader scientific community.

Limitations

The present protocol presents several challenges and limitations. It may not be suitable for all patients—for example, individuals who are claustrophobic, exceed the physical size limits of the MRI scanner, or have contraindications such as certain metallic implants. Consequently, it is important to inform patients that MRI-PDFF is specifically intended to quantify liver fat content (particularly intrahepatic triglycerides), and should not be considered a reliably tool for excluding unrelated hepatic abnormalities.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Participant reports feeling anxious prior to the scan.

Potential solution

Participants should be reassured that modern MRI scanners are designed to maximize comfort, featuring wider, ventilated, and well-lit bores. During the examination, continuous communication with the technician is maintained through the scanner’s intercom system, and participants are provided with a call button to report any discomfort. If concerns about tolerating the procedure persist, consultation with a physician may be recommended to consider mild oral medication to reduce anxiety.

Problem 2

In some cases, participants may have difficulty holding their breath for the 20 seconds indicated by the system. It could lead to the PDFF measurements are obtained at low resolution, which may limit detailed evaluation of hepatic fat distribution.

Potential solution

Participants should be instructed and trained in breath-holding prior to the scan, and their breath-hold capacity may be assessed using a stopwatch or wall clock. If necessary, shorter sequences during inspiration can be performed. In the presence of respiratory artifacts, allow a brief rest period and repeat the acquisition.

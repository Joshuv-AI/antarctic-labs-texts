Antiplatelet Therapy and All-Cause Mortality After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement
Authors: Francesco Pelliccia, Vincenzo Pasceri, George Dangas, Roxana Mehran, Italo Porto, Francesco Radico, Fausto Biancari, Fabrizio D’Ascenzo, Francesco Saia, Giampaolo Luzi, Francesco Bedogni, Ignacio J Amat Santos, Vincenzo De Marzo, Arnaldo Dimagli, Timo Mäkikallio, Eugenio Stabile, Sara Blasco-Turrión, Luca Testa, Marco Barbanti, Giuliano Costa, Franco Fabbiocchi, Ahmed Chilmeran, Giulio Stefanini, Federico Conrotto, Corrado Tamburino, Carmen Spaccarotella, Davide Cao, Andrea Macchione, Michele La Torre, Francesco Bendandi, Wojciech Wańha, Wojtek Wojakowski, Umberto Benedetto, Ciro Indolfi, David Hildick-Smith, Davide Capodanno, Fabio Miraldi, Dobromir Dobrev, Marco Zimarino, TRITAVI Study Group

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCurrent guidelines recommend single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) after transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) in patients without an indication for anticoagulation. This recommendation is mainly based on a better risk profile in terms of bleeding over dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT), although data on mortality are inconclusive.ObjectivesWe analyzed 6-month and 24-month mortality associated with SAPT and DAPT after femoral TAVR from the Transfusion Requirements in Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation registry.MethodsThe Transfusion Requirements in Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation is a multicenter registry including 10,071 patients undergoing TAVR. For the purpose of the present study, 4,557 patients with indication to anticoagulation, nonfemoral access, recent percutaneous coronary intervention, and procedural complications were excluded. The remaining 5,514 patients (age 81 ± 7 years, 51% males) were discharged either on SAPT (n = 3,197) or DAPT (n = 2,317).ResultsThe SAPT group, compared with the DAPT group, experienced lower 6-month all-cause mortality (2.4% vs 5.4%; log-rank P < 0.0001) and major bleeding (0.5% vs 1.3%; log-rank P = 0.001). SAPT was associated with reduced both cardiovascular (1.3%) and noncardiovascular mortality (1.1%), as compared to DAPT (2.2%, P = 0.008 and 3.2%, P = 0.0001, respectively). At 24-month follow-up, overall mortality remained significantly lower with SAPT vs DAPT (11.7% vs 14.2%; log-rank P = 0.007). Cox regression hazard model confirmed the association of DAPT with 6-month (adjusted HR: 1.69; 95% CI: 1.20-2.37; P = 0.002) and 24-month (adjusted HR: 1.21; 95% 1.01-1.46; P = 0.04) all-cause mortality.ConclusionsIn a large real-world population of all comers undergoing TAVR, SAPT was associated with a significantly lower 6-month and 24-month all-cause mortality as compared with DAPT. (Transfusion Requirements in Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation [TRITAVI] Study; NCT03740425)Key words: bleeding, coronary artery disease, dual antiplatelet therapy, mortality, single antiplatelet therapy, transcatheter aortic valve replacement

Optimal antiplatelet therapy following transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) in patients without indication to anticoagulation continues to be a matter of debate.1 The 2023 American guidelines state that dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) with aspirin and clopidogrel may be considered for the first 3 to 6 months in patients at low bleeding risk.2 By contrast, the 2025 European guidelines recommend lifelong single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT).3,4 These recommendations are based on randomized controlled trials conducted in recent years which consistently showed a benefit of SAPT over DAPT, although limited to a reduction in the risk of major or life-threatening bleeding.5, 6, 7, 8 However, all these trials enrolled relatively small and highly selected cohorts of TAVR patients and were therefore underpowered to ascertain any effect of antiplatelet therapy on all-cause mortality.9

We sought to compare the relative impact of SAPT and DAPT on overall mortality in contemporary real-world practice, using a large international multicentre registry of patients undergoing TAVR.

Methods

Patients

TRITAVI (Transfusion Requirements in Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation is an investigator-initiated registry designed to collect data on patients with severe aortic stenosis undergoing TAVR that enrolled consecutive patients who underwent TAVR at 20 European sites (12 in Italy, 1 in Spain, 1 in Poland, 5 in Finland, 1 in England) from December 2011 to December 2023.10, 11, 12, 13 For the purpose of the present study, we included all consecutive patients performing TAVR discharged alive. The following patients were excluded: 1) patients with indication to anticoagulation and/or a history of atrial fibrillation; 2) patients with nonfemoral access; 3) patients who died or experienced in-hospital major vascular complications after TAVR; and 4) patients who had had an acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and/or a percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) in the preceding 12 months. All patients provided written informed consent for the procedure and subsequent data collection. The Institutional Review Board of the University Sapienza of Rome, Italy, approved the study. Patient selection, data collection, and data analysis were performed in accordance with the Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis (TRIPOD) guidelines. The study was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03740425).

Transfemoral TAVR procedure

Local heart teams confirmed treatment eligibility and procedural management was per the standard of care at the time of intervention. TAVR was performed either with balloon-expandable or self-expandable valves at the operators’ discretion. For all centers, unfractionated heparin was given during the procedure (70 U/kg) targeting an activated clotting time of 200 to 300 s. After the procedure, the standard antiplatelet therapy with aspirin 100 mg daily indefinitely was advised. At the operators’ discretion, clopidogrel 75 mg daily was also prescribed for 6 months and discontinued thereafter. All periprocedural complications, including bleeding and vascular complications, were recorded. Patients’ data were entered on a common Excel data sheet, and advancement of data collection and analysis were shared among all study participant centers periodically during the study progress.

Clinical follow-up and endpoints

Hospital discharge was defined as departure from the institution at which TAVR was performed. At all institutions, the choice of antithrombotic regimen was determined at the discretion of the discharging physician. All patients had clinical follow-up at 6 and 24 months. During the follow-up, vital status was assessed by direct consultation or telephone interview, soliciting information from relatives or local doctors, or direct consultation. All clinical events were site-reported. Centers were contacted in case of missing or inconsistent values and asked to provide checks or modify results to avoid missing data.

The primary endpoint of the study was the occurrence of all-cause mortality at 6-month follow-up. Secondary endpoints included bleeding, defined as Bleeding Academic Research Consortium 3 or 5,14 and ischemic events, a composite of nonfatal myocardial infarction (MI) and cerebral vascular accidents (ie ischemic stroke and transient ischemic attack),15 at 6 months, as well as all-cause mortality at 24 months.

Statistical analysis

Clinical and procedural features are summarized using mean (SD) or median (IQR) for continuous variables, and counts with percentages for categorical variables. Continuous variables were compared using Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U tests based on the normality of data. Categorical variables were compared by a 2-sided chi-square test with Yates’ correction for continuity or Fisher exact test as appropriate. The Kaplan-Meier curve was plotted to estimate the survival curves to time-to-event variables (primary and secondary endpoints). Comparison between the groups was performed using the log-rank test. Multivariable Cox regression models were used to adjust the imbalances of baseline characteristics among the 2 different antithrombotic regimens (i.e. SAPT or DAPT). Multivariable models were built using forward stepwise selection, with a P value of <0.05 required for inclusion. Accordingly, the models included the following variables: age, sex, antiplatelet treatment (SAPT or DAPT), diabetes, hypertension, smoking, presence of coronary artery disease (CAD), peripheral artery disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, anemia, Society of Thoracic Surgeons score, year when TAVR was performed (2011-2023), left ventricular ejection fraction <40%, hemoglobin <11 g/dL, and estimated glomerular filtration rate. The results of the model were expressed as adjusted HRs and a relative 95% CI. Cox regression assumptions were checked by plotting Schoenfeld residuals and by determining log-minus-log survival curves. Nonlinearity of continuous variables was checked by Martingale residuals. Potential outliers were checked by assessing and plotting standardized difference of the beta (Dfbeta) values. Results of these analyses are shown in Supplemental material. We explored the correlation between temporal trends in prescription of SAPT vs DAPT and mortality between 2011 and 2022. Also, meta-regression was performed to assess the correlation between mortality and the use of DAPT in each center. Subgroup analyses were carried out to identify sources of heterogeneity in antithrombotic regimens, such as the influence of gender or concomitant CAD in decision-making. As the number of missing values was very limited (Supplemental Table 1), we did not use any imputation model or adjustment, but simple pairwise deletion. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 23.0, IBM Corporation). All P values were 2-tailed and a P value <0.05 was considered indicative of a statistically significant association.

Results

Study patients

Among participants in the multicenter TRITAVI registry, a total of 10,071 TAVR procedures performed between December 2011 and January 2023 were included in the analysis. Patients with indication to anticoagulation either before (n = 3,036) or after the procedure (n = 856) were excluded. In addition, 350 patients with nonfemoral access, 205 patients who died or experienced in-hospital major vascular complications after TAVR, as well as 110 patients who had a history of ACS and/or previous PCI in the preceding 12 months were excluded from the analysis (Supplemental Figure 1). Among the 5,514 patients enrolled in the study, 3,197 received SAPT (either aspirin or clopidogrel) and 2,317 received DAPT (aspirin and clopidogrel) after TAVR.

Outcomes

During the 6-month follow-up, the primary endpoint of all-cause mortality was significantly lower in patients receiving SAPT than in those on DAPT (2.4% vs 5.4%) (Table 3). SAPT was associated with reduced both cardiovascular (1.3%) and noncardiovascular mortality (1.1%), as compared to DAPT (2.2%; P = 0.008 and 3.2%, P = 0.0001, respectively) (Table 3). Cumulative incidence curves demonstrated lower all-cause mortality after TAVR in the SAPT as compared with the DAPT group (log-rank P < 0.0001) (Central Illustration).

Multivariate analysis

In the Cox proportional hazard model, after adjustment for confounders, DAPT was associated with a higher 6-month all-cause mortality compared with SAPT (adjusted HR: 1.69; 95% CI: 1.20-2.37 for DAPT vs SAPT; P = 0.002) (Table 4). Independent predictors of 6-month mortality also included NYHA functional class III-IV (adjusted HR: 1.54; 95% CI: 1.03-2.29; P = 0.01), and CAD (adjusted HR: 1.34; 95% CI: 1.01-1.76; P = 0.04). At 24-month follow-up, DAPT remained independently associated with increased all-cause mortality (adjusted HR: 1.21, 95% CI: 1.01-1.46; P = 0.04).

Sensitivity analyses

Results in the overall population were consistent with those obtained in patients both with and without concomitant CAD. Among the 1,781 patients with CAD (730 on SAPT and 1,051 on DAPT), overall 6-month mortality was 4.8%, with 2.6% in the SAPT vs 6.4% in the DAPT group (P < 0.0001). In the 3,733 patients without CAD (2,467 on SAPT and 1,266 on DAPT), overall 6-month mortality was 3.3%, with 2.5% in the SAPT vs 4.6% in the DAPT group (P < 0.001) (Figure 2). In a Cox proportional hazards model adjusted for covariates, DAPT was significantly associated with higher 6-month mortality in patients both with concomitant CAD (adjusted HR: 1.83; 95% CI: 1.01-3.35; P = 0.04) and without CAD (adjusted HR: 1.52; 95% CI: 1.04-2.20; P = 0.03).

Similarly, outcomes were consistent across sexes. Among the 2,671 men (1,624 on SAPT and 1,047 on DAPT) overall 6-month mortality was 3.6%, with 2.2% in the SAPT vs 5.7% in DAPT group (P < 0.0001). Among the 2,843 women (1,573 on SAPT and 1,270 on DAPT), overall 6-month mortality was 3.9%, with 2.9% in the SAPT vs 5.1% in the DAPT group (P = 0.001) (Figure 3). In the adjusted Cox proportional hazards model, DAPT remained significantly associated with 6-month mortality both in men (adjusted HR: 2.08; 95% CI: 1.32-3.30; P = 0.001) and in women (adjusted HR: 1.53; 95% CI: 1.03-2.29; P = 0.04).

Discussion

The main findings of our study among patients without an indication for anticoagulation after TAVR were as follows: 1) SAPT was associated with lower 6 and 24 months all-cause mortality as compared with DAPT; 2) major bleeding was less frequent with SAPT vs DAPT, whereas the incidence of ischemic events was similar between the 2 groups; 3) a positive correlation was observed between mortality and the use of DAPT across participating centers; and 4) the association between DAPT and 6-month overall mortality was independent of the presence of CAD and was consistent in both sexes.

Evidence on antiplatelet therapy after TAVR

Current recommendations on post-TAVR antiplatelet therapy are largely based on the evidence that SAPT may reduce bleeding as compared with DAPT,2, 3, 4 whereas available data on all-cause mortality remain inconclusive.16 The ARTE (Aspirin vs Aspirin Plus Clopidogrel Following Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation) trial7 and the POPULAR TAVI (Antiplatelet Therapy for Patients Undergoing Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation) trial8 both demonstrated that SAPT was associated with a lower risk of major or life-threatening bleeding without any increase in all-cause mortality, ischemic stroke, or MI, thus confirming findings of earlier pivotal investigations.5,6 Collectively, these studies showed that SAPT after TAVR is associated with similar rates of ischemic events but lower rates of major bleeding compared with DAPT, although without any demonstrable impact on overall mortality. It should be noted, however, that the majority of studies carried out so far included relatively small cohorts (≅1,000 patients in total) and were therefore underpowered to detect significant differences in mortality after TAVR. Even the OCEAN-TAVI (Optimized Transcatheter Valvular Intervention-Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation),16 which tested a thought-provoking strategy whereby patients at extremely high risk of bleeding were discharged with a no-antithrombotic regimen, reported a reduced risk of major bleeding without any significant differences in all-cause and cardiovascular death, stroke, or MI during follow-up.

Antiplatelet therapy and mortality

Our findings confirm and extend previous knowledge showing for the first time in a real-world, all-comers TAVI population that SAPT is associated with a significantly lower incidence of all-cause mortality at 6 and 24 months compared with DAPT. These novel data reinforce current recommendations supporting the use of SAPT as an effective strategy not only to reduce bleeding but also to improve survival.2, 3, 4 Indeed, our results slightly differ from those of POPULAR TAVI, which analyzed the 12-month outcomes in 665 patients undergoing TAVR without a concomitant indication for anticoagulation and found a nonsignificant reduction in mortality in patients treated with 3-month DAPT after the procedure (5.7% vs 6.3%).8 Similar findings were reported by the OCEAN-TAVI investigators, who observed a slightly, not statistically significant, lower all-cause mortality in DAPT patients (17.0% vs 21.1%), was likely driven by lower noncardiovascular mortality, as cardiovascular death was similar between the groups (7.4% with DAPT vs 8.6% with SAPT, P = 0.969).16 In contrast with these observations, the ARTE trial, which randomized 222 patients (111 to SAPT and 111 to DAPT), showed a strong trend toward a reduced 3-month mortality in the SAPT group (3.6% vs 6.3%, P = 0.37).7 Further support comes from the CENTER2 (Cerebrovascular Events in Patients Undergoing Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation With Balloon-Expandable Valves vs Self-Expandable Valves) study, a pooled patient-level database from 10 clinical studies including a total of 23,562 transfemoral TAVR patients. In this analysis, DAPT was associated with higher major bleeding rates compared with SAPT (12.2% vs 9.1%; P = 0.002), and major bleeding was strongly linked to increased mortality both within 30 days (14.1% vs 4.3%; P < 0.001) and 1 year (27.8% vs 14.5%; P < 0.001).17 A network meta-analysis by Turgeon et al18 also failed to identify mortality differences, but suggested that SAPT may provide the optimal balance after TAVR by reducing thrombotic events while minimizing the risk of bleeding. More recently, no differences between SAPT and DAPT were observed for major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events and death in a consecutive series of patients treated with valve-in-valve TAVR.19 Interestingly, our finding of a significantly greater mortality in CAD patients treated with DAPT reinforces current recommendations that lifelong SAPT should be prescribed after TAVR regardless of concomitant CAD,20 unless an ACS and/or a PCI have occurred in the preceding 12 months.21,22

Temporal trends in SAPT and DAPT use

Our findings revealed a progressive decline in the use of DAPT after TAVR, suggesting that antithrombotic treatment strategies have evolved over time according to emerging scientific evidence and updated clinical practice guidelines. These results mirror those recently published by Vistisen et al who conducted a nationwide study including 6,447 Danish patients undergoing TAVR and observed a shift toward simpler antithrombotic treatment from 2008 to 2021, with SAPT progressively emerging as the favorite strategy.23 However, despite the strong recommendation following the results of cohort A in POPULAR TAVI trial, SAPT is still underutilized after TAVR in patients without indication to anticoagulation. In our registry, we observed a huge variability in the use of SAPT and DAPT among centers, with some still favoring DAPT even in most recent years. These observations are in line with Bansal et al24 who recently analyzed the electronic health records from a large US TAVI cohort of patients and reported persistent use of DAPT in 54% of cases from 2021 onwards. Clinical concern for thrombotic risk together with inertia in prescribing habits are likely to account for the limited adherence to current guideline recommendations in real-world practice worldwide.

Study limitations

We recognize that the analysis of observational registry data carries inherent risks of bias. Missing values are a recognized limitation in multicenter registries, and although multivariate imputation offers the best available method for handling sparse missing values in health care databases, it cannot fully eliminate bias. Accordingly, we applied multivariable Cox regression modeling to account for differences in baseline characteristics and potential confounders; nevertheless, residual confounding from unknown or unmeasured variables cannot be excluded. The inclusion of patients treated over a 12-year timeframe encompassing significant advances in TAVR practice could potentially have introduced variability in the results. However, the year of TAVR procedure was not associated with survival, despite an increasing proportion of lower risk patients being treated over time, a finding likely linked to the exclusion of in-hospital mortality from the primary endpoint and the selection of the study population limited to patients discharged alive. Prescription bias is also possible, as DAPT rather than SAPT may have been chosen in patients perceived to be at higher atherothrombotic risk.

Further limitations include reliance on site-reported events, which did not detail the specific cause of cardiac and noncardiac deaths. In addition, we cannot exclude heterogeneity in event adjudication among centers or under-reporting of minor events. Since our analysis was limited to patients undergoing successful transfemoral TAVR without indication to anticoagulation, extrapolation of these findings to patients with different clinical profiles or treated via alternative access routes should be made with caution. Despite these limitations, the present study encompasses a large, consecutive, and unselected all-comers population, providing robust statistical power to assess the impact of antiplatelet treatment on mortality after TAVR. Noteworthy, unlike the majority of previous studies, our results were not affected by procedural factors, as deaths or in-hospital major vascular complications after TAVR were excluded.

Conclusions

In a large, real-world all-comers TAVR population, SAPT was associated with a significantly lower incidence of all-cause mortality both at 6 and 24 months, as compared with DAPT. These findings reinforce current recommendations, as they confirm the safety of SAPT in reducing bleeding risk, while also suggesting a survival benefit, irrespective of sex or CAD status.

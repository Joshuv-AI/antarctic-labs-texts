1.
Introduction

The classical theta-lifting is a correspondence relating automorphic functions for a pair of groups forming a Howe dual pair
(see
[
9
]
). In the case of the pair
(\operatorname{SO}_{2},{\operatorname{SL}}_{2})
for the function field of a curve
C_{0}
over a finite field
k
, this gives
a way to produce cuspidal functions on
{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}
-bundles on
C_{0}
from data on a double covering
\widetilde{C}_{0}\to C_{0}
.

In this paper we study an analog of this picture, with the curve
C_{0}
replaced by a curve
C
over the ring of dual numbers
A=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})
(we denote
by
C_{0}
the reduction of
C
from
A
to
k
).
The study of automorphic functions for such
C
was initiated in
[
3
]
, and we refer to loc. cit. for details on this setup.

We set
F=k(C)
, the local ring of the general point of
C
. This is a square-zero extension of the function field
F_{0}=k(C_{0})
.
As in the case of a finite field, we have a naturally defined ring of adeles

{\mathbb{A}}_{C}
which is a square-zero extension of the usual adeles
{\mathbb{A}}_{C_{0}}
.
We denote by
\hat{{\mathcal{O}}}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{C}
the subring of integer adeles.

Let
G
be a split connected reductive group over
\Z
, and let
{\mathfrak{g}}
denote its Lie algebra.
As in the classical case, we are interested in the representation of

G({\mathbb{A}}_{C})
on the space
{\mathcal{S}}(G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}_{C}))
(locally constant functions with compact support),
and more specifically, in the subspace of
G(\hat{{\mathcal{O}}})
-invariant vectors
and the action of the Hecke operators on it. As in the classical case, this subspace can be interpreted as functions on isomorphism
classes of
G
-bundles over
C
.

The algebra of Hecke operators in this case is not commutative, but one can define a natural commuting family of Hecke operators
associated with dominant coweights and relative Cartier divisors in
C\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)
, extending closed points in
C_{0}
(see
[
2
]
).
Furthermore, if our curve
C
over dual numbers is the reduction of a curve
C_{O}
over the ring of integers
O
in a local field
K
,
then our picture should be related to a conjectural picture with the Hecke operators acting on the Schwartz space of
G
-bundles over
C_{K}
, discussed
in
[
1
]
.

In the present paper we develop an analog of theta-lifting for automorphic functions over
C
. This involves constructing
a representation
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}
of
{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{C})
associated with a double covering
\pi:\widetilde{C}\to C
and a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
on
\widetilde{C}
equipped
with an isomorphism
{\operatorname{Nm}}({\mathcal{L}})\simeq\omega_{C/A}
(also depending on a choice of an additive character
\psi
of the finite field
k
), and then
using a natural
{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(F)
-invariant functional on
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}
to construct and study the
theta-lifting operator

\kappa_{{\mathcal{L}}}:{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}\to{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(F)\backslash{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{C})).

Our main result is the interpretation of this construction in terms of the orbit decomposition of
{\mathcal{S}}(G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}_{C}))
for
G={\operatorname{SL}}_{2}
,
defined in
[
2
]
. Namely, we consider orbits
\Omega
of the adjoint action of
G(F_{0})
on
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\omega_{C_{0}}(F_{0})
. Using
\psi

and residues of differentials, we view
\Omega
as a
G(F_{0})
-orbit of characters of
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathbb{A}}_{C_{0}}/F_{0}
. Furthermore, we view
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathbb{A}}_{C_{0}}

as a normal subgroup in
G({\mathbb{A}}_{C})
, and define the subspace
{\mathcal{S}}(G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}_{C}))_{\Omega}\subset{\mathcal{S}}(G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}_{C}))

associated with the corresponding

G(F_{0})
-orbit of characters of this subgroup (see
[
3
, Sec. 3]
and Sec.
2.1
below).

The
{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{C})
-action on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{L}}
is centralized by a natural action of the group
{\mathbb{A}}^{*}_{\widetilde{C},1}
of ideles with the norm
1
. Restricting the latter action to
the subgroup of ideles of
\widetilde{C}
trivial modulo
\epsilon
, we study the spaces of twisted coinvariants
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{L}},c_{0}}
, where
c_{0}
runs through rational antiinvariant differentials on

\widetilde{C}_{0}
, the reduction of
\widetilde{C}
.
We show that for
c_{0}\neq 0
the theta-lifting operator from
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{L}},c_{0}}
lands in the subspace

{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(F)\backslash{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{C}))_{\Omega_{{\mathcal{L}},c_{0}}}
, where
\Omega_{{\mathcal{L}},c_{0}}
is a regular elliptic orbit associated with
{\mathcal{L}}
and
c_{0}
(see Theorem
4.20
).
Furthermore, we show that for every regular elliptic orbit
\Omega
, all functions in
{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(F)\backslash{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{C}))_{\Omega}

(which are known to be cuspidal) arise from theta-lifting for appropriate data
(\widetilde{C}\to C,{\mathcal{L}},c_{0})
(see Theorem
4.23
).

Note that in this setup the space of cuspidal functions in
{\mathcal{S}}(G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}_{C}))

contains a subspace of strongly cuspidal functions (see
[
3
]
and Sec.
2.1
below).
Our main result implies that all strongly cuspidal functions arise from theta-lifting (see Remark
2.2
).

In the case when the double covering
\widetilde{C}_{0}\to C_{0}
is associated with a quadratic differential on
C_{0}
with simple zeros, we give a more precise information on the
theta-lifting of spherical (i.e.,
{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(\hat{{\mathcal{O}}})
-invariant) vectors in
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}
. Namely, in
[
3
, Sec. 3]
we proved that spherical vectors in
{\mathcal{S}}(G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}_{C}))_{\Omega}
have a natural
interpretation as the space of functions on a certain Hitchin fiber (see also Sec.
2.2
below). We show that a natural spherical vector in
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{L}},c_{0}}
(corresponding to the characteristic function of integer points),
where
c_{0}
is the canonical antiinvariant differential on
\widetilde{C}_{0}
, maps to the delta-function of a certain point in the Hitchin fiber (see Theorem
4.26
).
In addition, we compute the action of some Hecke operators
(a more general local computation is done in Sec.
4.2
) on these spherical vectors, and in this
way produce explicit eigenfunctions for these Hecke operators in
{\mathcal{S}}(G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}_{C}))_{\Omega}
(see Theorem
4.26
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
we give some background on automorphic functions for a nilpotent extension
C
of
C_{0}
and for arbitrary
G
,
recalling relevant results from
[
3
]
. In Sections
2.2
and
2.3
we give an interpretation of the relation between

{\mathcal{S}}(G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}_{C}))_{\Omega}
and functions on Higgs
G
-bundles as a certain Fourier transform.
In Sec.
3
we review the classical picture for a curve over a finite field, including computation of the Hecke action in Sec.
3.3
and
the interpretation in terms of vector bundles in Sec.
3.4
. Then in Sec.
4
we study the theta-lifting for a curve
C
over
A=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})
.
First, we study the corresponding local picture in Sec.
4.1
. Then we compute spherical vectors and some Hecke operators in Sec.
4.2
.
Finally, we study the global picture in Sec.
4.3
proving our main results.

Convention.

When considering quadratic extensions (starting from Section

Lemma 2.1

Assume that

Then any

Proof.

This is proved analogously to the case

[
3

It is enough to check that if

is the Lie algebra of the unipotent radical in a standard parabolic subgroup

then for any

\int_{a\in{\mathfrak{u}}_{P}\otimes{\mathcal{N}}({\mathbb{A}}_{C_{0}})/{\mathfrak{u}}_{P}\otimes{\mathcal{N}}(F_{0})}\Pi_{\eta}f((1+a)g)=0

for any

We can rewrite the integral as

\displaystyle\int_{X\in{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathcal{N}}({\mathbb{A}}_{C_{0}})/{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathcal{N}}(F_{0})}\int_{a\in{\mathfrak{u}}_{P}\otimes{\mathcal{N}}({\mathbb{A}}_{C_{0}})/{\mathfrak{u}}_{P}\otimes{\mathcal{N}}(F_{0})}\psi_{\eta}(-X)f((1+X+a)g)dadX=

Now we observe that

unless the restriction

i.e.,
\eta
is orthogonal to

with respect to the Killing form. But the latter orthogonal is exactly the Lie algebra of

this cannot happen (otherwise

∎

Remark 2.2
.

Conversely, the argument of

2.3.
Fourier transform for torsors

Let
\Gamma

a
k
-vector bundle over
\Gamma

\Gamma

indexed by objects, together with linear isomorphisms
\alpha:V_{\gamma}\to V_{\gamma^{\prime}}
corresponding to each arrow

\alpha:\gamma\to\gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma

The total space
\operatorname{tot}(V)
is a groupoid of pairs
(\gamma,v)

\gamma\in\Gamma
,

(\gamma,v)\to(\gamma^{\prime},v^{\prime})

\alpha:\gamma\to\gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma

denote the dual vector bundle to

over
\Gamma

Let

(Sf)(\gamma,v)=\sum_{v^{*}\in V^{\vee}_{\gamma}}\psi(\langle v^{*},v\rangle)f(\gamma,v^{*})

Now let

-torsor over

-torsors

\alpha:\gamma\to\gamma^{\prime}

one has an associated isomorphism

compatible with the isomorphism

and with the torsor structures (and the compositions are
respected). We can also consider the total space
\operatorname{tot}(T)
, which is a groupoid equipped with a representable map to
\Gamma
, with the fiber

over
\gamma
.
We want to describe an analog of the above Fourier transform where
\operatorname{tot}(V)
is replaced by
\operatorname{tot}(T)

Let us define a
\C^{*}
-torsor
{\mathcal{L}}_{T,\psi}
over
\operatorname{tot}(V^{\vee})
as follows.
Let
p^{*}T
denote the
p^{*}(V)
-torsor over
\operatorname{tot}(V^{\vee})
, obtained by taking the pullback of

.
We have a natural homomorphism of vector bundles

(where

is the trivial rank
1

we
get a
k
-torsor. The
\C^{*}
-torsor

is obtained by further taking the push-out with respect to
\psi
.
In down-to-earth terms, for every object
(\gamma,v^{*})
of
\operatorname{tot}(V^{\vee})
, where

, the
\C^{*}
-torsor

is defined as the push-out of the
V_{\gamma}
-torsor

with respect to the homomorphism
V\to\C^{*}:v\mapsto\psi(\langle v^{*},v\rangle)
.

Let us consider the fibered product
\operatorname{tot}(V^{\vee})\times_{\Gamma}\operatorname{tot}(T)
, with the projections

to the factors.
We define the Fourier transform

by

where we use a canonical trivialization of

over

More explicitly, by construction for every

satisfying

\psi_{\gamma,v^{*}}(v+t)=\psi(\langle v^{*},v\rangle)\cdot\psi_{\gamma,v^{*}}(t),

for any

.
The elements

can be thought of as functions on

of weight

-action.
Now for

For functions on the fibers over fixed

to

{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha){}\kern 4.0pt\hbox{}\leaders{\vrule height=5.80646pt,depth=0.96356pt}{\hskip 18.33333pt}\kern 5.6667pt\hskip-14.00002pt\vbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{\hbox{}\kern 1.00006pt${\scriptstyle\sim}$\kern 1.00006pt}\hss}\hbox{}}\hskip 8.33331pt\hbox{$\mkern-1.5mu{\hbox{\kern-2.00002pt\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\tenln\char 45\relax}\kern 0.33334pt}}\mkern-1.5mu$}\kern 4.0pt{\mathcal{L}}

to some

\alpha

and

The identity (

\theta_{{\mathcal{L}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha)}

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{L}}=\pi^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
, where
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
is a theta-characteristic on
C
, defined over
k
.
If
\chi
is a nontrivial character of
K(\widetilde{C}/C)
then the function

(3.3)

on
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}}(C)
is cuspidal and nonzero.

Proof.

We have for
\Phi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{L}}({\mathbb{A}}_{\widetilde{C}}))

\int_{u\in U(F)\backslash U({\mathbb{A}}_{C})}\theta_{\mathcal{L}}(u\Phi)du=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{L}}(k(\widetilde{C}))}\int_{z\in{\mathbb{A}}_{C}/F}\psi(z{\operatorname{Nm}}(x))dz\Phi(x).

Since

is a nontrivial character of

for any

Hence, we get

\int_{u\in U(F)\backslash U({\mathbb{A}}_{C})}\theta_{\mathcal{L}}(u\Phi)du={\operatorname{vol}}({\mathbb{A}}_{C}/F)\cdot\Phi(0).

Therefore, for

, we have

which does not depend on
\alpha
. Hence, summing over
\alpha
with weights

, we get zero.

Now let us show that
\Phi_{{\mathcal{L}},\chi}
is nonzero. For this, let us first calculate

for

, for some
\beta\in{\mathbb{A}}_{C}^{*}
.
By definition, we have

f_{{\mathcal{L}},\alpha}(t)=c(t)\cdot\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{L}}(k(\widetilde{C}))}\delta_{\hat{{\mathcal{L}}}}(\alpha\beta x)=c(t)\cdot|H^{0}(\widetilde{C},{\mathcal{L}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\beta)|,

where

Now let

and it will be trivial only when

which shows that

∎

3.3.
Spherical vectors and the Hecke action

Let us return to the local picture associated with a degree
2
extension
K/E
.
Let
K^{*}_{1}\subset K^{*}
denote the group of elements
x
such that
{\operatorname{Nm}}_{K/E}(x)=1
.
We have an action of
K^{*}_{1}
on
{\mathcal{S}}(K)
given by
r(x)\Phi(y)=\Phi(xy)
. It is easy to see that this
action commutes with
{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(E)
.

Now let us consider the space of spherical vectors
{\mathcal{S}}(K)^{G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})}
, where
G={\operatorname{SL}}_{2}
.
It has the induced action of
K^{*}_{1}

and also the action of the Hecke algebra
{\mathcal{S}}(G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})\backslash G(E)/G({\mathcal{O}}_{E}))
.
Let us set
{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1}:={\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{*}\cap K^{*}_{1}
.
Then the subspace
{\mathcal{S}}(K)^{G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})\times{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1}}
is preserved by the Hecke operators, and has
the induced action of
K^{*}_{1}/{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1}
.
Note that
K^{*}_{1}/{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1}
is trivial if
K
is a field and is isomorphic to
\Z
if
K=E\oplus E
.

Lemma 3.3
.

One has an isomorphism of
K^{*}_{1}/{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1}
-representations

{\mathcal{S}}(K^{*}_{1}/{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{S}}(K)^{G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})\times{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1}}:\varphi\mapsto\sum_{x\in K^{*}_{1}/{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1}}\varphi(x)\delta_{x\cdot{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}.

Proof.

We know that
\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}
is
G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})\times{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1}
-invariant. Hence, the same is true for
\delta_{x{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}
for any
x\in K^{*}_{1}
.

Assume first that the extension
K/E
is non-trivial, i.e.,
K
is a field. Then invariance with respect to upper-triangular integer matrices implies that any

\Phi\in{\mathcal{S}}(K)^{G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})}
is supported on
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
.
By the Fourier invariance of
\Phi
, this implies that
\Phi
is invariant under translations by elements of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
, hence,

\Phi
is proportional to
\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}
.

In the case of the trivial extension
K=E\oplus E
, the proof is obtained using the Fourier transform in one variable
(see
[
6
, Prop. 1.6]
).
∎

Thus, in the case when
K/E
is nontrivial, the space
{\mathcal{S}}(K)^{G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})\times{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1}}
is spanned
by
\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}
, whereas in the case
K=E\oplus E
, it has as a basis

(t^{n},t^{-n})\cdot\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}=\delta_{t^{-n}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus t^{n}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}

numbered by
n\in\Z
, where
t\in{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
is the generator of the maximal ideal.

Now let us consider the action of the Hecke operators
(T_{m})_{m\geq 1}
corresponding to the double cosets

G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})\cdot\operatorname{diag}(t^{m},t^{-m})\cdot G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})

on the space
{\mathcal{S}}(K)^{G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})\times{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,1}}
.

Let
A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}
denote the
G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})
-averaging operator.
Then the Hecke operator
T_{g_{0}}
corresponding to the double coset
G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})g_{0}G({\mathcal{O}}_{E})
can be computed as

T_{g_{0}}f(g)=|G({\mathcal{O}})/G({\mathcal{O}})\cap g_{0}G({\mathcal{O}})g_{0}^{-1}|\cdot A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}g_{0}f

(3.4)

(see
[
3
, Sec. 2]
).
In case of the operator
T_{m}
, i.e., for
g_{0}=\operatorname{diag}(t^{m},t^{-m})
,
G({\mathcal{O}})\cap g_{0}G({\mathcal{O}})g_{0}^{-1}\subset G({\mathcal{O}})
is the subgroup preserving a line in

({\mathcal{O}}/(t^{2m}))^{2}
, so the constant factor in (
3.4
) becomes
|{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathcal{O}}/(t^{2m}))|=q^{2m}+q^{2m-1}
.

Let
\Pi:{\mathcal{S}}(K)\to{\mathcal{S}}(K)
denote the operator of averaging with respect to the group
U({\mathcal{O}}_{E})
(strictly upper triangular integer matrices).
Due to our assumptions on
\psi
, we have

\Pi(\Phi)=\delta_{{\operatorname{Nm}}^{-1}({\mathcal{O}}_{E})}\cdot\Phi.

We also have
A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}=A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}\Pi
.

Lemma 3.4
.

(i) If
K/E
is nontrivial then

T_{m}\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}=(-1)^{m}(q^{m}+q^{m-1})\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}.

(ii) If
K=E\oplus E
then

T_{1}\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=(q-1)\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}+q\delta_{t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}+q\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}.

Proof.

(i) We have
\operatorname{diag}(t^{m},t^{-m})\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}=(-1)^{m}q^{-m}\delta_{t^{-m}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}
. Now we observe that for
m\geq 1
,
\Pi\delta_{t^{-m}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}=\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}
,
and the assertion follows.

(ii) We have

T_{1}\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=(q^{2}+q)A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=(q+1)A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}.

Applying the operator
\Pi
, we can replace
\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}

by

\phi:=\Pi\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=\delta_{(a,b)\in t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}^{2}:ab\in{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}.

We can write

\phi-\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=\delta_{(a,b)\in t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}^{2}\setminus{\mathcal{O}}_{E}^{2}:ab\in{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}-\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}+\delta_{t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}-\delta_{t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}.

Note that

S\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=q^{-1}\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}.

Hence

\displaystyle A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=q^{-1}A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=q^{-1}A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}\Pi\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=

\displaystyle q^{-1}A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}(\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}^{2}}+\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}-\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}),

which gives

A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=\frac{1}{q+1}(\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}^{2}}+\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}).

Similarly,

A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}\delta_{t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}=\frac{1}{q+1}(\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}^{2}}+\delta_{t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}).

Hence,

A_{G({\mathcal{O}})}\phi=\frac{1}{q+1}((q-1)\delta_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E}^{2}}+q\delta_{t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}+q\delta_{t{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\times t^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}}),

which leads to the claimed formula.
∎

Now, let us consider the global picture with the double covering

.

4.1.1.

Let
\overline{E}

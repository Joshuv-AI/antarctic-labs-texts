1.
Introduction

The Hurwitz space
\operatorname{Adm}^{s}_{0,b,d}
parametrizes simply-branched degree-
d
covers from smooth connected genus-
g
curves to smooth genus-
0
curves, marked at
b=2g+2d-2
branch points.
Harris and Mumford introduced the “admissible covers” compactification of
\operatorname{Adm}^{s}_{0,b,d}
, in which the target and source curves of a cover degenerate to nodal curves as branch points come together and the target curve is stable
[
11
]
.
Although this space is singular, its normalization is the smooth moduli space of twisted stable maps
[
1
]
, which we denote by
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}^{s}_{0,b,d}
.
We refer to the normalization whenever we mention the admissible covers compactification.
The Picard Rank Conjecture
[
7
]
predicts that the rational Picard group vanishes in all degrees
d
for both
\operatorname{Adm}_{0,b,d}^{s}
and its variant
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{d,g}=\operatorname{Adm}_{0,b,d}^{s}/S_{b}
.
Recent work on the geometry of
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{d,g}
confirms this conjecture for
d\leq 5

[
6
]
and for
d>g-1

[
13
]
.
Furthermore, the homology of
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{d,g}
stabilizes, implying that the Picard Rank Conjecture holds asymptotically when
b\gg d

[
12
]
.

The Hurwitz space
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{2,g}^{s}
of degree-2 admissible covers is isomorphic to the quotient
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,b}/S_{b}
.
It follows that
H^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{2,g}^{s})=H^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,b})^{S_{b}}=0
.
For admissible covers of degree 3, we develop tools for an inductive argument inspired by
[
2
]
to show that
H^{1}
vanishes for the Hurwitz space of fully-marked admissible covers
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
, a generalization of
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}^{s}_{0,b,d}
.
This Hurwitz space parametrizes possibly disconnected admissible covers with fully-marked fibers of prescribed (not necessarily simple) ramification (see
2.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

The first cohomology group
H^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{3},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))=0
for every partition of
3
, every partition of
g
, and any set of
m
ramification profiles
\mu_{i}
partitioning
3
.

We give examples of 1-dimensional Hurwitz spaces in degree 4 and degree 5 of fully-marked admissible covers with positive genus (see
Section

6.3
).
It follows that
H^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))
does not vanish for
d\geq 4
.

The spaces
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}^{s}_{0,b,d}
and
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{d,g}^{s}
are finite group quotients of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{d,g}((2,1,\dots,1)^{b})
, where the source curve is connected and all marked fibers have simple ramification (see
Section

2.1
).
We immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

The first cohomology groups
H^{1}(\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}^{s}_{0,b,3})=H^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{3,g})=0
.

Our proof of
Theorem

1.1
is inspired by the inductive argument introduced by Arbarello and Cornalba to study the low degree odd cohomology of moduli spaces of stable curves
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

[
2
]
.
Arbarello and Cornalba used the stratification of the boundary of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
by the images of gluing maps from products of lower-dimensional moduli spaces of curves to show that
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
vanishes for
k=1,3,
and
5
.
They conjectured that inductive arguments could be used to show vanishing for higher odd
k
, once the requisite base cases were proven.
Indeed, Bergström, Faber, and Payne showed that
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
vanishes for
k=7
and
9

[
3
]
, thereby proving the conjecture for all odd
k<11
.
This bound is optimal, since the cohomology group
H^{11}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n})
is nonzero once
n\geq 11
.

Adapting these strategies to Hurwitz spaces requires new tools.
First, we must find a Hurwitz space with the correct boundary structure.
Like
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, the boundaries of the simply-branched Hurwitz spaces
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}^{s}_{0,b,d}
and
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{d,g}^{s}
are stratified by the images of gluing maps from products of lower-dimensional Hurwitz spaces.
The Hurwitz spaces appearing in the domains of these gluing maps, however, parametrize admissible covers with arbitrary (not necessarily simple) ramification, since a simply-branched admissible cover in the boundary may have arbitrary ramification over the nodes.
Thus, even if we are primarily interested in the low degree odd cohomology of
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}^{s}_{0,b,d}
and
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{d,g}^{s}
, in order to argue inductively we are inevitably led to study the low degree odd cohomology of the more general Hurwitz spaces
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
.

In
Proposition

5.2
, we show that when
k
is odd, the vanishing of the
k
-th cohomology group of low-dimensional
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d^{\prime}},\underline{g^{\prime}}}(\underline{\mu^{\prime}})
implies the vanishing of the
k
-th cohomology group for all
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
.
Thus, for
k=1
and
d=3
, we must compute
H^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\mu_{4}))
for every possible choice of ramification profile
\mu_{i}
.
To solve this technical challenge, we take advantage of the natural target map from these 1-dimensional Hurwitz spaces to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}
, which is a covering map.
For each base case, it suffices to compute the monodromy of this target map, thereby computing that each Hurwitz space is genus-0.

Theorem

1.1
follows.

In principle,
Proposition

5.2
could be used to prove vanishing for higher odd
k
, analogously to the argument in
[
2
]
; see
Remark

5.3
.

1.1.
Outline of the paper

In Section 2, we define Hurwitz spaces of fully-marked admissible covers and describe their relationships to other Hurwitz spaces in the literature.
In Section 3, we recast the geometric data of an admissible cover as the combinatorial data of monodromy representations, allowing us to combinatorially describe the target map
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m}
.
In Section 4, we show that the boundary divisors of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
are finite quotients of products of lower-dimensional Hurwitz spaces, setting the stage for the inductive proof of vanishing
H^{1}
in Section 5.
Finally, in Section 6, we record the data of the base cases necessary to prove that
H^{1}
vanishes for admissible covers in degrees 3.
We also provide examples of 1-dimensional Hurwitz spaces of fully-marked admissible covers in degrees 4 and 5 which have positive genus.

2.
Fully-marked admissible covers

Here, we define the Hurwitz spaces in this paper and describe their relationships to other Hurwitz spaces in the literature.

Definition 2.1

(Fully-marked admissible covers)
.

Let
\underline{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{k})
be a partition of an integer
d>0
.
Let
\underline{g}=(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})

Let

A fully-marked admissible cover of type

is a finite morphism

satisfying the following:

•

The target curve
D
is an
m
-marked stable curve of genus 0.

•

The source curve

is a disjoint union of stable curves

of genus

.
The map

•

The fibers over the
m

Precisely, if

•

The nodes of

D
.
At a node, the cover is locally given by

on both branches of the node for some

(known as the kissing condition).

•

The map
f
is étale outside of the marked locus and nodes of
D
.

Remark 2.5
.

The Hurwitz space defined in

A helpful discussion of this appears at the end of Chapter 3, Section 3 in

2.1.
Relation to other Hurwitz spaces

The Hurwitz space of fully-marked admissible covers
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
is the same as the Hurwitz space treated in
[
5
]
, except that here we fix the target curve to be genus-0.

In
[
1
]
, Abramovich, Corti, and Vistoli introduced the space
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}_{0,m,\underline{d}}
parametrizing arbitrarily branched covers from possibly disconnected source curves with
m
marked points on the target genus-0 curve, and showed that it is a smooth stack.
Here, we use
\underline{d}
(slightly differing from the notation of
[
1
]
) to denote the degree of the cover from the different components of the source curve.
The space
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}_{0,m,\underline{d}}
has a covering map from
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
given by quotienting by
\prod_{i=1}^{m}S(\mu_{i})
where
S(\mu_{i})
is the product of symmetric groups corresponding to repeated ramification indices in
\mu_{i}
.
For example, the ramification profile
(2,2,1,1,1)
corresponds to the product
S_{2}\times S_{3}
.
Quotienting by
\prod_{i=1}^{m}S(\mu_{i})
corresponds to forgetting the ordering in each marked fiber on the source curve.
In particular, this covering map is étale, since its fibers have constant cardinality.
Therefore
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
is also smooth (as a stack).

We denote the Hurwitz space of connected, fully-marked, simply-branched covers by

\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{d,g}(b,m):=\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{d,g}((2,1,\dots,1)^{b},(1,1,\dots,1)^{m-b}),

where
b=2g+2d-2
. When
m=b
, only the branched fibers are marked.
The Harris-Mumford Hurwitz space introduced in
[
11
]
is the quotient
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{d,g}(b,b)/\prod_{i=1}^{b}S_{d-2}
, which we denote by
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}_{0,b,d}^{s}
.
Finally, the Hurwitz space specified in the Picard Rank Conjecture (see
[
6
,
13
]
) is the quotient
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}^{s}_{0,b,d}/S_{b}
, which we denote by
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{d,g}
.

The Hurwitz spaces
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{d,g}(\underline{\mu})
are the ones discussed in
[
14
]
and
[
8
]
.
We denote the connected admissible covers of
[
1
]
by
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}_{0,m,d}
.
As
m
varies, the collection of
\overline{\operatorname{Adm}}_{0,m,d}/S_{m}
stratifies the Hurwitz spaces
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{d,g}
discussed in
[
4
]
and in
[
15
]
.
In those papers, they work with unmarked, arbitrarily branched covers, so branch points are allowed to collide.

3.1.
The target map

There is a natural
target map

sending an admissible cover

to the target curve

This is a covering map, ramified over the boundary, whose generic fiber at a point

is the set of isomorphism classes of fully-marked admissible covers which have marked locus

Once we fix generators for

Consequently, the sheets of the Hurwitz space above

can be identified with these fully-marked monodromy representations.
This helps us calculate the monodromy of the target map, which we carry out in

Remark 4.1
.

Examining the analogous gluing maps to the boundary of

illustrates how

is not stratified by lower-dimensional

For example, the Hurwitz spaces in the domain of the gluing maps to the boundary divisors of

parametrize covers with one marked fiber.

5.
Vanishing of

In this section, we prove that the cohomology of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
injects into the cohomology of the normalization of the boundary whenever it injects into the cohomology of the boundary (

We use this result to prove that the odd cohomology of

vanishes for all
\underline{d},\underline{g},
and

if certain base cases have this property (

We apply this argument for

and explain how to compute the base cases.

5.1.
Injectivity of pullback map on cohomology

Recall that the target map
T\colon\mathcal{H}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})\to\mathcal{M}_{0,m}
is a finite cover (see
Section

3.1
).
As
\mathcal{M}_{0,m}
is affine of dimension

, it follows that
\mathcal{H}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
is also affine of dimension
m-3
.
Therefore the homology groups
H_{k}(\mathcal{H}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))
vanish for
k>m-3
, and by Poincaré duality, the compactly supported cohomology
H_{c}^{k}(\mathcal{H}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))
vanishes for
k<m-3
.
The long exact sequence for compactly supported cohomology is as follows:

\cdots\to H_{c}^{k}(\mathcal{H}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))\to H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))\to H^{k}({\partial}\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))\to H_{c}^{k+1}(\mathcal{H}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))\to\cdots

Hence the map
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))\to H^{k}({\partial}\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))
is injective when
k\leq m-4
.
Now we show that
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))
also injects into the cohomology of the normalization of the boundary in this range.

Lemma 5.1

(Injectivity lemma)
.

Let
\operatorname{\xi_{\Gamma}}\colon\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}\to\partial\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
be the gluing map associated to the boundary divisor parametrizing admissible covers with dual graph
\Gamma

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d_{1}},\underline{g_{1}}}(\underline{\mu_{1}})\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d_{2}},\underline{g_{2}}}(\underline{\mu_{2}})
.

is injective, then the map
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))\to\oplus_{\Gamma}H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma})
is injective, where the direct sum is taken over all boundary divisors.

Proof.

Let

be the disjoint union of the gluing maps

Similarly to the discussion for

in

is the image of

under

.
Therefore we can factor

as in the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{k}(\partial\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k}(\amalg_{\Gamma}\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{k}(\partial\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))/W_{k-1}H^{k}(\partial\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))}.}

5.2.
Inductive vanishing of odd cohomology

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
k
be an odd integer.
Suppose that
H^{q}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))=0
for all odd
q\leq k
and parameters
(\underline{d},\underline{g},m,\underline{\mu})
such that
q>m-4
.
Then
H^{q}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))=0
for all odd
q\leq k
and all parameters
(\underline{d},\underline{g},m,\underline{\mu})
.

Proof.

We induct on
k
. Assume the claim is true for all
q<k
and all parameters
(\underline{d},\underline{g},m,\underline{\mu})
.
We need to show that
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))=0
for all
(\underline{d},\underline{g},m,\underline{\mu})
with
k\leq m-4
.

Lemma

5.1
holds in this range, so
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))\rightarrow\oplus_{\Gamma}H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma})
is injective.
Each space
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}
is a product
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d_{1}},\underline{g_{1}}}(\underline{\mu_{1}})\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d_{2}},\underline{g_{2}}}(\underline{\mu_{2}})
where
\underline{d_{1}},\underline{d_{2}}
are partitions of
d
and
(m_{1}-1)+(m_{2}-1)=m
.
Crucially, the number of marked fibers
m_{i}
on each factor of the domain is strictly less than
m
.
By the Künneth formula, the cohomology groups decompose as

H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma})=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}H^{p}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d_{1}},\underline{g_{1}}}(\underline{\mu_{1}}))\otimes H^{q}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d_{2}},\underline{g_{2}}}(\underline{\mu_{2}})).

As
k
is odd, there must be a factor with odd cohomological degree in each summand.
These factors with odd degree strictly less than
k
vanish via the induction hypothesis, and therefore
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma})
is a direct sum of tensor products where
p
and
q
are either
k
or 0.

If
k>m_{1}-4
, then
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d_{1}},\underline{g_{1}}}(\underline{\mu_{1}}))=0
.
Otherwise
k\leq m_{1}-4
and one applies the previous argument again to get an injection into a direct sum of tensor products of
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d_{3}},\underline{g_{3}}}(\underline{\mu_{3}}))
and
H^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d_{4}},\underline{g_{4}}}(\underline{\mu_{4}}))
, with
m_{3},m_{4}<m_{1}
.
The same reasoning holds for
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d_{2}},\underline{g_{2}}}(\underline{\mu_{2}})
.
By iterating this argument, eventually
k
becomes larger than
m_{3}-4,m_{4}-4,
etc., and then
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma})=0
.
Therefore

H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))=0
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

We use
Proposition

5.2
to prove
H^{1}
vanishing for
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{3},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
in this paper.
As the analogous proposition for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
was used to prove vanishing of odd cohomology in higher degree, it may be possible to do the same for
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{3},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})

Since the number of base cases grows with the cohomological degree, a successful proof would likely involve calculating Euler characteristics and a robust understanding of the combinatorics of the boundary strata of

5.3.3.
Python program for computing monodromy of target map.

By calculating the endpoints of lifts of loops to
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\mu_{4})
, we can compute the monodromy of
T
.
We implemented this calculation in Python.
The code (with examples) for this program is provided at
https://github.com/amyqhli/1dim-hurwitz-space-monodromy-calculator
. We briefly describe the algorithm here.

Given a Hurwitz space
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\mu_{4})
, we first set
degree
equal to
d
and produce a list of tuples
candidates

=\{(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3},\sigma_{4})\}\subseteq(S_{d})^{4}
where
\sigma_{i}
has ramification profile
\mu_{i}
.
Second, we multiply
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}
(the order of multiplication matters) to check if each tuple of permutations multiplies to the identity using the function
CheckIdentity(degree, candidates)
.
This function outputs a list called
successes
.
We add all the possible markings to each monodromy representation in
successes
, and then filter out tuples which are simultaneous conjugates of other tuples using the function
marked_sc_eqclass(degree, marked_successes)
.
We are left with a list
equivs
of simultaneous conjugacy classes of fully-marked monodromy representations.
We create a dictionary
HurwSpace
which enumerates each tuple in
equivs
as
"Sheet{i}"
to keep track of the sheets in this Hurwitz space.

Next, for each tuple in this list, we apply the monodromy formulae in
Equations

2
,
3
and
4
and check its simultaneous conjugacy class using the function
sheet_connections_with_markings(degree, HurwSpace)
.
This data corresponds to sheets in the Hurwitz space coming together over
0,1,
and
\infty
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}
(we call such sheets “connected by an edge”).
We keep track of this information in a dictionary called
combined_edges
.
Using this dictionary, we can calculate the connected components of the Hurwitz space via
find_connected_components
which searches for sheets connected to each other by an edge in
combined_edges
.
We make a dictionary
component_groups
of the components and the sheets in each component.
For each component in
component_groups
, the function
ram_of_target_map
calculates the degree and ramification of the target map over
0,1,\infty
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}
.
Finally, we output the genus of the Hurwitz space using the Riemann-Hurwitz formula.
The permutations associated to the ramification of the target map are also provided.

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\mu_{4})

.

Theorem

1.1
.

\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{d,g}

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu})
when the source curve is connected and all
\mu_{i}=(2,1,\dots,1)

H^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{3,g})=0
and recovers the fact that
H^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{2,g})=0
.

Section

6.3
.
This implies that
H^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\underline{d},\underline{g}}(\underline{\mu}))
is nonvanishing when
d\geq 4
.
In
Section

6.4
, we include an example of a postive genus Hurwitz space in degree 5.

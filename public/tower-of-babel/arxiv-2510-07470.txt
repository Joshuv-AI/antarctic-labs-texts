Provably Accelerated Imaging with Restarted Inertia and Score-based Image Priors
Marien Renaud 1, †, Julien Hermant 1, †, Deliang Wei 2, †, and Yu Sun 2, ✉

Abstract
Fast convergence and high-quality image recovery are two essential features of algorithms for solving ill-posed imaging inverse problems. Existing methods, such as regularization by denoising (RED) , often focus on designing sophisticated image priors to improve reconstruction quality, while leaving convergence acceleration to heuristics. To bridge the gap, we propose Restarted Inertia with Score-based Priors (RISP) as a principled extension of RED. RISP incorporates a restarting inertia for fast convergence, while still allowing score-based image priors for high-quality reconstruction. We prove that RISP attains a faster stationary-point convergence rate than RED, without requiring the convexity of the image prior. We further derive and analyze the associated continuous-time dynamical system, offering insight into the connection between RISP and the heavy-ball ordinary differential equation (ODE) . Experiments across a range of imaging inverse problems demonstrate that RISP enables fast convergence while achieving high-quality reconstructions.

Introduction

Imaging inverse problems aim to recover an unknown image from its undersampled, noisy measurements.
These problems arise in many domains, ranging from low-level computer vision to biomedical imaging.
Since the measurements often cannot losslessly specify the underlying image, the problem is inherently
ill-posed
.
A standard remedy is to incorporate image priors to regularize reconstruction and promote high-quality solutions
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

There has been considerable interest in using image denoisers as priors in iterative reconstruction algorithms
[
4
]
.
This interest continues to grow with the recognition of the conceptual link between denoisers and the score function used in generative diffusion models
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

Regularization by denoising (RED)

[
8
]
is such a framework that uses an off-the-shelf denoiser to approximate the
score
(
i.e.
, the gradient of the log-density) of the implicit image prior encoded by that denoiser.
When equipped with deep image denoisers, RED methods have been shown to achieve excellent imaging performance in various imaging problems
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.
Similar results have also been achieved by a closely related class of algorithms known as
plug-and-play priors

[
13
,
14
]
.

However, RED methods are inherently iterative optimization procedures and can need a large number of iterations; this can result in substantial runtime on problems that require real-time processing or involve large-scale data.
While considerable interest has been devoted to design sophisticated denoising priors to improve reconstruction quality
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
, fewer attention has been given to convergence acceleration and is often handled through heuristics
[
18
,
19
]
.
A key challenge is that incorporating learned priors typically renders the problem nonconvex, limiting the applicability of classical acceleration techniques developed for convex settings.

Contributions.
In this work, we address the gap by proposing a novel
Restarted Inertia with Score-based Priors (RISP)
method.
RISP significantly extends RED by integrating a
restarted inertia
technique to provably accelerate convergence, while still allowing the use of advanced score-based priors for high-quality image reconstruction.
We present two algorithmic instantiations of RISP, termed
RISP-GM
and
RISP-Prox
, based on gradient and proximal formulations, respectively.
Under practical assumptions, we establish for both algorithms stationary-point convergence at the rate of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-4/7})
, surpassing the
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2})
rate of RED.
Notably, our analysis does not require score-based priors to be convex, thus accommodating those parameterized by deep neural networks.
We further derive and analyze the continuous dynamical system underlying RISP,
and show that RISP-GM and RISP-Prox correspond to alternative discretizations of the second-order
heavy-ball

ordinary differential equation (ODE)
.
We experimentally validate RISP across a range of linear and nonlinear inverse imaging problems.
In particular, our results show that RISP can enable substantial acceleration (up to
24\times
) for large-scale image reconstruction.

Background

Inverse Problems.

We consider the general inverse problem
{\bm{y}}={\bm{A}}({\bm{x}})+{\bm{n}}
, in which the goal is to recover
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
given the measurements
{\bm{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
The forward operator
{\bm{A}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
models the response of the imaging system, and
{\bm{n}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
represents the measurement noise, which is often assumed to be additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN).
One popular inference framework for solving inverse problems is based on the
maximum a posteriori (MAP)
estimation

\hat{{\bm{x}}}\in\argmax_{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\{p({\bm{y}}|{\bm{x}})p({\bm{x}})\}=\argmin_{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big\{F({\bm{x}}):=f({\bm{x}})+g({\bm{x}})\big\},

(1)

where
f=-\mathsf{log\,}p({\bm{y}}|\cdot)
is often known as the data-fidelity term and
g=-\mathsf{log\,}p
as the regularizer.
Commonly, the data-fidelity is set to the least-square loss
f({\bm{x}})=(\lambda/2)\|{\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}({\bm{x}})\|^{2}
, which corresponds to the Gaussian likelihood
{\bm{y}}|{\bm{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}({\bm{A}}({\bm{x}}),(1/\lambda)\bm{I})
.

Regularization by denoising.

RED is a widely used framework
that employs an image denoiser
{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to model prior, where
\sigma>0
controls the denoising strength.
In RED, the noise residual
{\bm{x}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}({\bm{x}})
is used as an surrogate for the gradient of an implicit regularizer.
Two popular RED algorithms are the
RED gradient method (RED-GM)

{\bm{x}}^{+}={\bm{x}}-\eta\big(\nabla f({\bm{x}})+\tau({\bm{x}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}({\bm{x}}))\big)

(2)

and the
RED proximal method (RED-Prox)

{\bm{x}}^{+}=\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}\big({\bm{x}}-\eta\cdot\tau({\bm{x}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}({\bm{x}}))\big),

(3)

where
\tau>0
is the weighting parameter and
\eta>0
the stepsize.
The two algorithms differ in their treatment to
f
, where the former computes the gradient
\nabla f
, while the latter evaluates the
proximal map

\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}({\bm{z}}):=\argmin_{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}\|+\eta f({\bm{x}})\right\}.

The detailed formulations of both algorithms are summarized in Appendix
G
.
By Tweedie’s formula
[
5
]
, when
{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}
is the
minimum mean squared error (MMSE)
denoiser, the noise residual equals to the scaled version of the prior score

{\mathsf{S}}({\bm{x}})=-\big({\bm{x}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}({\bm{x}})\big)/\sigma^{2},

where
{\mathsf{S}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
denotes the score induced by the denoiser. When we fix
\tau=1/\sigma^{2}
, RED can be interpreted as a score-based method
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.
The empirical success has motivated theoretical work on the convergence properties of RED algorithms.
Recent works have analyzed their fixed-point convergence
[
23
,
24
]
, interpreted RED as a MAP estimator
[
25
,
26
]
, or established convergence guarantees for stochastic RED
[
26
,
15
]
.
Our work complements these efforts by providing a formal analysis of accelerated convergence under restarted inertia.

Plug-and-play priors (PnP).

PnP is an alternative framework that exploits denoisers as priors, inspired by the mathematical connection between proximal operator and MAP denoiser
[
13
]
.
Its key idea is to replace the proximal operator in an iterative algorithm directly with
{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}
to impose the prior information.
The applications and theory of PnP have been widely studied in

[
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
and
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
, respectively; see also a review in
[
36
]
.
Recently,
[
37
]
showed that the denoiser in PnP can also be formulated as a score function, rendering PnP as a score-based method.
While we develop RISP through the extension of RED, our framework and analysis can potentially be applied to PnP as well.

Inertial acceleration for RED/PnP.

Several recent works have investigated the use of inertia (
i.e.
, momentum) in RED/PnP
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
.
These adaptations include an inertia update before applying the gradient and denoiser; detailed discussion on different forms of inertia are provided in Appendix
A
.
While these works demonstrate the practical effectiveness of inertia, convergence analysis with an explicit accelerated rate remains missing.
Our work fills the gap by establishing a provable accelerated convergence rate.

3
Restarted Inertia with Score-based Priors

Figure 1
:
Conceptual illustration of how restarting stabilizes inertial algorithms. Without restarting, accumulated inertia can cause overshooting and escape from stationary points (

RISP addresses slow convergence by leveraging an inertia update paired with a restarting mechanism.
We begin by discussing this central component and then present two algorithmic instantiations of RISP.

The first mechanism in RISP is an inertial step.
Consider RED-GM as an example.
A natural approach to accelerate convergence is given by

\displaystyle{\bm{z}}

\displaystyle={\bm{x}}+(1-\theta)({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{-})

(4)

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}^{+}

\displaystyle={\bm{z}}-\eta\big(\nabla f({\bm{z}})-{\mathsf{S}}({\bm{z}})\big),

where
\bm{z}
accounts the inertia from the previous update
{\bm{x}}^{-}
, and parameter
\theta\in(0,1]
controls the inertial contribution.
When
\theta=1
, (
4
) reduces to normal RED-GM.
Note that the corresponding RED
-
Prox variant follows analogously.

Nevertheless, accumulated inertia can cause instability and undesirable overshooting, especially in nonconvex cases
[
54
]
; this behavior causes the theoretical limitation in establishing faster convergence rates.
The top panel of Figure
1
presents an intuitive illustration.
To address the problem, RISP further employs a
restarting mechanism

[
44
]
that resets
the inertia whenever the accumulated relative error exceeds a threshold

\displaystyle\textbf{if}\quad k\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\|\bm{x}^{t+1}-\bm{x}^{t}\|^{2}>B^{2},\quad\textbf{then}\quad\bm{x}^{-1}=\bm{x}^{0}=\bm{x}^{k},k=0

(5)

where
B>0
is a user-defined constant.
Once triggered, (
5
) clears accumulated inertia and forces RISP to rely on the local gradient, which helps prevent overshooting and deviation from stationary points.
The bottom panel of Figure
1
conceptually illustrates this behavior.

We derive RISP-GM and RISP-Prox as two algorithmic instantiations of RIPS, extending RED-GM and RED-Prox, respectively.
The algorithmic details of both algorithms are summarized in Algorithms
1
and
2
, where the inertia and restarting steps are highlighted in color.
Unlike the original RED algorithms in (
2
) and (
3
), RIPS directly employs a pre-trained score function as the negative gradient of a regularizer, which streamlines both the theoretical analysis and the empirical implementation.
In the next section, our analysis shows that the restarting mechanism enables provable accelerated convergence under the nonconvex score-based priors.

4
Theoretical Analysis

In this section, we analyze the convergence behavior of RISP.
We first derive explicit convergence rates for RISP
-
GM and RISP
-
Prox, showing they reach stationary points faster than their RED counterparts.
Next, we analyze the underlying continuous
-
time dynamics of RISP to provide complementary insights into its behavior.

4.1
Convergence Rates of RISP-GM & RISP-Prox

Assumption 1
.

The score function
\mathsf{S}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a gradient field, i.e., there exists a regularizer
g
such that
\mathsf{S}=-\nabla g
.

The assumption formally connects the score function with a regularizer.
In practice, one can implement such a deep score function using the
gradient step denoiser
technique proposed in
[
25
]
.

Assumption 2
.

(i)
The data-fidelity term
f
has an
L_{f}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient, i.e., for all
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\|\nabla f({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{y}})\|\leq L_{f}\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\|
.

(ii)
The score function
{\mathsf{S}}
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{g}
; namely, regularizer
g
has a
L_{g}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient. Throughout the paper, we set
L:=L_{f}+L_{g}
.

Assumption
2
(i) is a standard condition that can be satisfied in most imaging applications.
Assumption
2
(ii) is also practical because it only assumes the existence of the Lipschitz constant. One can enforce this condition by employing
Lipschitz activation functions
. We refer to Apendix
G.1
for a detailed discussion.

To facilitate discussion, we present the convergence rate of RED as baseline. The following proposition states the result for RED-GM; the result for RED-Prox can be found in Appendix
C
.

Proposition 1
.

Let Assumptions
1
-
2
hold and
\eta=1/L
. Then, with at most
n
iterations, RED-GM outputs a point
\hat{\bm{x}}
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla F(\hat{\bm{x}})\right\rVert\leq\frac{A_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2}),

where
A_{0}=\sqrt{2L\Delta_{F}}
, and
\Delta_{F}:=F(\bm{x}^{0})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F
. We recall that
\bm{x}^{0}
is the initialization.

The detailed proof is provided in the appendix.
The proposition shows that RED-GM approximates a stationary point at the rate of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2})
, which is consistent with classic nonconvex results for gradient descent. The same convergence rate also holds for RED-Prox.

We now establish faster convergence rates for the RISP algorithms. Our analysis requires an additional assumption on the second-order properties of the data-fidelity term and of the score function.

Assumption 3
.

(i)
The data-fidelity term
f
has an
\rho_{f}
-Lipschitz continuous Hessian, i.e., for all
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\|\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})-\nabla^{2}f(\bm{y})\|\leq\rho_{f}\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|
.

(ii)
The score function
{\mathsf{S}}
is Lipschitz Jacobian continuous with constant
\rho_{g}
; namely, regularizer
g
has a
\rho_{g}
-Lipschitz continuous Hessian. Throughout the paper, we set
\rho:=\rho_{f}+\rho_{g}
.

This assumption is standard in analyses of accelerated methods for nonconvex problems
[
55
,
56
,
44
,
57
]
.
We note that Assumption
3
(i) holds for all linear inverse problems with AWGN.
Even when the forward model is nonlinear, the assumption could also be satisfied; one example is the Rician denoising problem presented in Section
5
.
While Assumption
3
(ii) may seem strong, we stress that the condition can be satisfied in practice by combining gradient-step denoisers and Lipschitz activation functions.
In Appendix
G
, we present a proposition that formally establishes the existence of
\rho_{g}
for such score functions.
Importantly, both Assumption
2
(ii) and
3
(ii) do not assume the convexity of score-based priors.

Theorem 1
.

Let Assumptions
1
-
3
hold. Let
\eta=1/(4L)
,
B=\sqrt{\varepsilon/\rho}
,
\theta=4(\varepsilon\rho\eta^{2})^{1/4}\in(0,1)
, and
K=\theta^{-1}
.
Then, with at most
n
iterations, RISP-GM outputs a point
\bm{\hat{z}}
such that

\left\lVert\nabla F(\hat{\bm{z}})\right\rVert\leq 82\varepsilon=\mathcal{O}(n^{-4/7}),

where
\varepsilon=2^{4/7}\Delta_{F}^{4/7}L^{2/7}\rho^{1/7}n^{-4/7}+L^{2}\rho^{-1}n^{-4}
.

The proof of the theorem is provided in Appendix
D
, which extends that of
[
44
, Theorem 1]
to the RISP setting.
Theorem
1
establishes that RISP
-
GM approximates a stationary point at the rate of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-4/7})
, which is clearly faster than the
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2})
rate attained by RED.

The second theorem analyzes RISP
-
Prox.
The proximal step introduces additional analytical challenges.
Specifically, it requires a uniform lower bound on the curvature of the objective to control the proximal mapping.
To address this issue, we introduce the following assumption.

Assumption 4
.

The data-fidelity term
f
is convex, and the regularizer
g
is
\nu
-weakly convex, i.e.,
g+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{2}
is convex.

Note that
f
is convex for all linear inverse problems with AWGN.
As
g
is already assumed to be
L_{g}
-Lipschitz, it follows that
g
is weakly convex with
\nu\leq L_{g}

[
58
]
.
Assumption

.

Let Assumptions

-
4

Let

,

-

Together, the two theorems rigorously show that both RISP
-

4.2
Continuous-Time Analysis

We further study the continuous-time system associated with RISP.
We begin by first establishing the connection between RISP and the heavy ball ODE.
Under Assumption
1
, we can show that
inertial part
of RISP algorithms (lines 2-3 in Algorithms
1
&
2
) is governed by

\ddot{\bm{x}}_{t}+\alpha\dot{\bm{x}}_{t}+\nabla F({\bm{x}}_{t})=0,

(6)

where
\alpha:=\mathop{\mathsf{lim}}_{\eta\to 0}(\theta(\eta)/\sqrt{\eta})
, and we recall that
F=f+g
where
g
is defined by the score
{\mathsf{S}}
.
A detailed derivation can be found in Appendix
F
.
Equation (
6
) defines the
heavy ball
equation
[
59
]
, whose solution
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
can be thought as a rolling object on the landscape of

, subject to some friction parameterized by

.

However, the heavy ball ODE does not account for the restarting mechanism used in RISP.
To address this, we introduce a restarted variant of (
6

3
.
The system follows the heavy
-
ball dynamics until a restart criterion is met, at which point the inertia (
i.e.
, the velocity term) is reset to zero. This continuous restarted ODE generalizes the discrete RISP
-
GM and RISP
-
Prox algorithms and thus bridges the continuous and discrete perspectives.
In particular, RISP-GM can be viewed as the Euler discretization of the continuous RISP dynamics, while RISP-Prox corresponds to the forward-backward discretization where the proximal step computes the backward gradient.
We note that continuous RISP serves as a general formulation and can inspire the development of other discrete acceleration algorithms.

The following theorem establishes the convergence guarantee for continuous RISP.

Theorem 3
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
3
hold. Consider the
(\bm{x}^{c}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
running for a total execution time
T\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Define the output
\hat{\bm{x}}
as the average

\hat{\bm{x}}:=\frac{1}{K_{0}}\int_{0}^{K_{0}}\bm{x}^{c}_{t+\sum_{i=1}^{K}T_{i}}dt,

where
K_{0}=\argmin_{t\in\left[\frac{T_{\mathop{\mathsf{max}}}}{2},T_{\mathop{\mathsf{max}}}\right]}\left\lVert\dot{{\bm{x}}}^{c}_{t+\sum_{i=1}^{K}T_{i}}\right\rVert
. Set the parameters as
\alpha=(\varepsilon\rho)^{1/4}
,
T_{\mathop{\mathsf{max}}}=(\varepsilon\rho)^{-1/4}
, and
B=\sqrt{\varepsilon/\rho}
. Then, under these conditions, the gradient norm satisfies

\left\lVert\nabla F(\hat{\bm{x}})\right\rVert\leq 5\varepsilon=\mathcal{O}(T^{-4/7}),

with
\varepsilon=2^{4/7}\rho^{1/7}\Delta_{F}^{4/7}T^{-4/7}+2^{4}\rho^{-1}T^{-4}
.

The complete proof is provided in Appendix
F.3
.
Theorem
3
states that continuous RISP approximate a stationary point at the rate of
\mathcal{O}(T^{-4/7})
, which is consistent with the
\mathcal{O}(n^{-4/7})
convergence achieved by discrete RISP algorithms.
Note that the Lipschitz continuity of the gradient is not required in the theorem. In fact, this condition is often introduced in discrete analyses for selecting a stepsize that preserves the property of the continuous dynamics.
However, the Lipschitz
-
Hessian assumption is essential here, as it ensures that when the process stops, the final point has a small gradient norm.

5
Experiments

We now present our experimental results, which are organized around three goals.
First, we validate the accelerated convergence of RISP on linear inverse problems that satisfy our assumptions, providing empirical support for the theoretical guarantees.
Second, we apply RISP to a nonlinear inverse problem where some assumptions do not hold, demonstrating the method’s robustness beyond the ideal setting.
Third, we evaluate RISP on a large-scale image reconstruction task to highlight its practical efficiency.
All hyperparameters are tuned to maximize the
peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR)
, and additional experiments, implementation details, and hyperparameter analyses are provided in Appendix
G
.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we propose RISP as an extension of the popular RED framework for imaging inverse problems.
RISP achieves provably faster convergence while remaining compatible with score-based image priors for high-quality reconstruction.
The central component of RISP is the restarted inertia mechanism, which employs inertial updates for acceleration but uses the restarting strategy to prevent instability and overshooting.
We theoretically analyze the convergence of RISP, showing that it achieves an accelerated rate of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-4/7})
over RED.
We also provide a continuous
-
time interpretation that connects RISP with the heavy
-
ball dynamics and offers complementary insights into the speed
-
up.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that RISP algorithms substantially reduce reconstruction time while preserving competitive image quality, highlighting the theoretical and practical benefits of integrating restarted inertia with score priors.

Appendix A
Extended Related works

Accelerating gradient descent with inertia.

In convex optimization, it is well known that adding an inertial mechanism to gradient descent can accelerate the optimization process
[
59
,
66
,
67
,
45
]
. As many applications involve the minimization of a non-convex function, the study of inertial acceleration in non-convex optimization has attracted significant interest
[
68
,
69
,
70
,
71
,
72
,
73
,
74
,
52
,
53
]
. Inertial algorithms are often studied on a popular class of non-convex functions, namely functions with Lipschitz continuous gradients. In this case, gradient descent finds a point
\bm{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\left\lVert\nabla f(\bm{x})\right\rVert\leq\varepsilon
in at most
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
iteration. Importantly, only under the gradient Lipschitz assumption, this bound cannot be improved using inertia
[
41
]
. Going one step further,
i.e.
assuming the Hessian of the function is Lipschitz, acceleration using inertia has been established
[
42
,
43
,
44
,
57
,
75
]
. This opens the door to demonstrating the benefits of momentum in applications involving challenging non-convex landscapes.

Previous works on accelerated RED methods.

Since RED methods are known to be slower than end-to-end networks for image reconstruction, a lot of efforts have been made to make RED methods more efficient.
[
18
]
have focused on reducing the computational cost of regularization by implementing a block-coordinate gradient descent. Alternatively,
[
19
]
apply stochastic gradient descent to the data-fidelity term.
[
76
]
modify the RED algorithm by incorporating vector extrapolation.

Inertia in RED methods.

Previous works have proposed to incorporate inertia into the RED framework, not only for acceleration but also for improving restoration quality.
[
39
]
add inertia to enhance restoration quality in deblurring and super-resolution tasks without analyzing the convergence rate. In a similar line of work,
[
40
]
focus on improving the quality for Rician noise removal and phase retrieval.
[
38
]
study Poisson noise removal and observe an empirical acceleration with inertial methods. However, no theoretical justification is provided for this acceleration.

Iterative Schemes.

Our work proposes to adapt the algorithm in
[
44
]
into the RED framework. An extensive line of research has adapted various optimization schemes to solve problem (
1
).
This includes alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM)
[
13
,
33
]
, proximal gradient descent (PGD)
[
77
,
78
]
, half-quadratic splitting (HQS)
[
60
,
25
]
, stochastic gradient descent (SGD)
[
15
]
or Langevin algorithms
[
21
,
22
,
79
]
.

Restarting methods.

To our knowledge, restarted inertia algorithms are first introduced by
[
54
]
in the convex optimization setting. It considers both a fixed restarting criterion—namely, restarting every
N
iterations—and adaptive ones. Specifically, denoting the iterations by
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
, we restart if

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{k+1})>F(\bm{x}^{k})

(Function criterion)

\displaystyle\left<\nabla F(\bm{z}^{k-1}),\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1}\right>>0.

(Gradient criterion)

The authors observed that these restarting techniques exhibited a clear numerical advantage over vanilla inertial algorithms, but lacked strong theoretical guarantees. Using a continuous version of the Gradient criterion,
[
45
, Section 5]
shows that the restarted ODE associated with the following ODE

\bm{\ddot{x}_{t}}+\frac{\alpha}{t}\bm{\dot{x}_{t}}+\nabla F(\bm{x_{t}})=0,

(7)

converges linearly when
F
is
\mu
-strongly convex. Without restart, the best achievable convergence rate in this setting is polynomial
[
80
, Proposition 7]
. This gives a theoretical guarantee of the benefit of restart in the continuous setting.
This result of
[
45
]
is extended to more general dynamical systems, restart criteria and geometric assumptions
[
81
,
82
,
83
]
.
In the discrete setting, still for
\mu
-strongly convex functions (or slightly weaker assumptions), provable fast convergence of restarted inertia relies on fixed criteria.
This criterion involves the knowledge of
\mu

[
84
,
85
]
, which can be estimated by algorithms
[
86
,
87
,
88
]
.
To our knowledge, it remains to demonstrate that adaptive criteria, such as function or gradient criterion, allows for such convergence speed of the algorithm. Finally, closer to our work, in the context of functions with Lipschitz gradient and Hessian,
[
44
]
introduced a new restart criterion related to the length of the trajectory, see Section
3
. This restart mechanism allowed to recover the convergence result of algorithms that mixed the inertial mechanism with an alternative step that exploits the negative curvature of the function, thus showing that this procedure can be discarded. We generalize their algorithm for composite optimization, where the objective function is the sum of two non-convex function, with RISP-GM (Algorithm
1
) and RISP-Prox (Algorithm
2
). The latter uses the proximal operator, which provides better experimental results. Finally, note that we focus on the Nesterov inertial mechanism and not on the heavy ball because the heavy-ball method requires a non-trivial averaging step during restart.

Appendix B
Preliminaries

The following lemmas are classical results. We provide their proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 1
.

Let
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. If
u
has
L_{u}
-Lipschitz gradient and has
\rho_{u}
-Lipschitz Hessian, then for any
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we have

(i)

u(\bm{y})\leq u(\bm{x})+\left<\nabla u(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>+\frac{L_{u}}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}
,

(ii)

u(\bm{y})\leq u(\bm{x})+\left<\nabla u(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>+\frac{1}{2}\left<\nabla u^{2}(\bm{x})(\bm{y}-\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>+\frac{\rho_{u}}{6}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{3}
.

Proof.

(i)
We define
\varphi(t)=u(\bm{x}+t(\bm{y}-\bm{x}))
for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, one has

\displaystyle u(\bm{y})-u(\bm{x})=\varphi(1)-\varphi(0)=\int_{0}^{1}\varphi^{\prime}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{1}\left<\nabla u(\bm{x}+s(\bm{y}-\bm{x})),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>ds.

We subtract on each side
\left<\nabla u(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>
, such that

\displaystyle u(\bm{y})-u(\bm{x})-\left<\nabla u(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left<\nabla u(\bm{x}+s(\bm{y}-\bm{x}))-\nabla u(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert\nabla u(\bm{x}+s(\bm{y}-\bm{x}))-\nabla u(\bm{x})\right\rVert\left\lVert\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right\rVert ds

\displaystyle\leq L\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert\bm{x}+s(\bm{y}-\bm{x})-\bm{x}\right\rVert\left\lVert\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right\rVert ds

\displaystyle=L_{u}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}\int_{0}^{1}sds=\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2},

where we used Cauchy Schwarz and the
L_{u}
-Lipschitz property of
\nabla u
.

(ii)
We define
\varphi(t)=u(\bm{x}+t(\bm{y}-\bm{x}))
for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The proof is similar to point (i), except that we use the second order Taylor formula.

\displaystyle\varphi(1)=\varphi(0)+\varphi^{\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(s)ds,

which also writes

\displaystyle u(\bm{y})-u(\bm{x})-\left<\nabla u(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left<\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x}+s(\bm{y}-\bm{x}))(\bm{y}-\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>ds.

We then subtract
\frac{1}{2}\left<\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})(\bm{y}-\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>
on each side, use Cauchy-Swcharz, the property of the operator norm and the
\rho_{u}
Hessian Lipschitz property to get

\displaystyle u(\bm{y})-u(\bm{x})-\left<\nabla u(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>-\frac{1}{2}\left<\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})(\bm{y}-\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left<(\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x}+s(\bm{y}-\bm{x}))-\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x}))(\bm{y}-\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert(\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x}+s(\bm{y}-\bm{x}))-\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x}))(\bm{y}-\bm{x})\right\rVert\left\lVert\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right\rVert ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}s\left\lVert\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x}+s(\bm{y}-\bm{x}))-\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})\right\rVert\left\lVert\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right\rVert^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\rho_{u}}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{3}\int_{0}^{1}s^{2}ds=\frac{\rho_{u}}{6}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{3}.

∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be
\nu
-weakly convex and differentiable. Then

\forall\bm{x},\bm{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

u(\bm{y})\geq u(\bm{x})+\left<\nabla u(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>-\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

By definition of the weak convexity,
f=u+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{2}
is convex. Then, for all
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle f(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\geq f(\bm{y})+\langle\bm{x}-\bm{y},\nabla f(\bm{y})\rangle

\displaystyle u(\bm{x})+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq u(\bm{y})+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}+\langle\bm{x}-\bm{y},\nabla u(\bm{y})+\nu\bm{y}\rangle

\displaystyle u(\bm{y})

\displaystyle\geq u(\bm{x})+\left<\nabla u(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right>-\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}.

∎

Appendix C
Convergence Analysis of RED

In this Section, we give a proof of the convergence of (
2
) stated in Proposition
1
. This proof’s technique is rather classical
[
67
, Section 1.2.3]
. Proposition
1
adapts it to the RED-GM framework.

Proposition
.

Under Assumptions
1
-
2
, RED-GM in algorithm (
2
) with
n
iterations, with
\eta=\frac{1}{L}
, outputs a point
\tilde{\bm{x}}
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla F(\tilde{\bm{x}})\right\rVert\leq\frac{A_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2}),

with
A_{0}=\sqrt{2L(F(\bm{x}^{0})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F)}
.

Proof.

Recall the iterates of RED-GM

\bm{x}^{k+1}=\bm{x}^{k}-\eta\big(\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k})+\tau({\bm{x}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}(\bm{x}^{k}))\big).

Because the score function is defined as
{\mathsf{S}}({\bm{x}})=-\tau\big({\bm{x}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}({\bm{x}})\big)
, by Assumption
1
, we can write
\tau\big({\bm{x}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}({\bm{x}})\big)=\nabla g(\bm{x})
, such that RED-GM becomes

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{k+1}=\bm{x}^{k}-\eta\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k})-\eta\nabla g(\bm{x}^{k})=\bm{x}^{k}-\eta\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k}).

Writing the algorithm in this form allows us to apply a classical gradient descent result. For completeness, we detail the remainder of the proof.

By Assumption
2
,
F
is
L:=L_{f}+L_{g}
Lipschitz. Hence, Lemma
1
-
(i)
can be applied to
u=F
at
\bm{x}=\bm{x}^{k}
and
\bm{y}=\bm{x}^{k+1}
, yielding

F(\bm{x}^{k+1})\leq F(\bm{x}^{k})+\left<\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k}),\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right>+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}.

By definition,
\bm{x}^{k+1}=\bm{x}^{k}-\eta\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})
, thus

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{k+1})\leq F(\bm{x}^{k})-\eta\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})\right\rVert^{2}=F(\bm{x}^{k})-\eta\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right)\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})\right\rVert^{2}.

Note that
\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right)\geq 0
as long as
\eta\leq\frac{1}{L}
. Then, rearranging and summing over
k=0,\dots,n-1
, we get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{F(\bm{x}^{0})-F(\bm{x}^{n})}{\eta\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right)}\leq\frac{F(\bm{x}^{0})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F}{\eta\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right)}.

We use the elementary relation
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})\right\rVert^{2}\geq n\mathop{\mathsf{min}}_{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})\right\rVert^{2}
, such that

\mathop{\mathsf{min}}_{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}\frac{F(\bm{x}^{0})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F}{\eta\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right)}.

To conclude, noting
\tilde{\bm{x}}:=\arg\mathop{\mathsf{min}}_{\bm{x}^{k},k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})\right\rVert^{2}
, and fixing
\eta=\frac{1}{L}
, the following holds

\left\lVert\nabla F(\tilde{\bm{x}})\right\rVert\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{2L(F(\bm{x}^{0})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F)}.

∎

Existing works studying gradient descent under Assumptions
2
and
3
does not manage to improve over this convergence speed
[
89
]
. This indicates that assuming the Hessian of the function is Lipschitz does not help to achieve faster convergence when only assuming the gradient is Lipschitz, making the inertial mechanism crucial to do so.

Proposition
.

Under Assumptions

2

{\mathsf{S}}({\bm{x}})=-\tau\big({\bm{x}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\sigma}({\bm{x}})\big)

We use the elementary relation
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right)\right\rVert^{2}\geq n\mathop{\mathsf{min}}_{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})\right\rVert^{2}

\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\bm{x}^{k_{0}+1}-\bm{x}^{k_{0}}\right)=-\nabla g(\bm{x}^{k_{0}})-\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k_{0}+1}).

(9)

Then, by the smoothness of

F
to the residuals

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k_{0}})\right\rVert&=\left\lVert\nabla g(\bm{x}^{k_{0}})+\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k_{0}})-\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k_{0}+1})+\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k_{0}+1})\right\rVert\\
&\leq\left\lVert\nabla g(\bm{x}^{k_{0}})+\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k_{0}+1})\right\rVert+\left\lVert\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k_{0}+1})-\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k_{0}})\right\rVert\\
&\leq\left\lVert\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\bm{x}^{k_{0}+1}-\bm{x}^{k_{0}}\right)\right\rVert+L\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k_{0}+1}-\bm{x}^{k_{0}}\right\rVert\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+L\right)\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k_{0}+1}-\bm{x}^{k_{0}}\right\rVert\end{aligned}

(10)

Combining (
8
) and (
10
), we get

\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k_{0}})\right\rVert\leq\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+L\right)^{-1}\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\frac{F(\bm{x}^{0})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F}{\eta\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right)}}.

Fixing
\eta=\frac{1}{L}
, and noting
\tilde{\bm{x}}:=\bm{x}^{k_{0}}
, it becomes

\left\lVert\nabla F(\tilde{\bm{x}})\right\rVert\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{2L(F(\bm{x}^{0})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F)}.

∎

Assumption
4
is only used for the convexity of the data fidelity
f
.

Theorem

E.2
Notations

As in
[
44
]
, we consider the restart time

\mathcal{K}=\mathop{\mathsf{min}}_{k}\left\{k\geq 1\Bigg|k\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\|\bm{x}^{t+1}-\bm{x}^{t}\|^{2}>B^{2}\right\}.

(14)

We call an
epoch
of Algorithm
2
all successive iterates

. Using Assumption

, such that
{\mathsf{S}}=-\nabla g
, we have that inside an epoch, the iterates of Algorithm

verify the following recursive formula

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bm{z}^{k}=\bm{x}^{k}+(1-\theta)(\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1})\\
\bm{x}^{k+1}=\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}(\bm{z}^{k}-\eta\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k}))\end{array}\right.

(15)

From the definition of the Proximal operator, the following equation is verified

\frac{\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}}{\eta}=\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})+\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k}).

(16)

By definition of
\mathcal{K}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\geq 1,\qquad\mathcal{K}\sum_{t=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\|\bm{x}^{t+1}-\bm{x}^{t}\|^{2}>B^{2}.

(17)

It induces a control on the distance of the
k
-th iterate to the starting point of the epoch
\bm{x}^{0}
,
\forall k\in[0,\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{0}\|^{2}=\left\|\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\bm{x}^{t+1}-\bm{x}^{t}\right\|^{2}\leq k\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\|\bm{x}^{t+1}-\bm{x}^{t}\|^{2}\leq B^{2},

(18)

and we have,
\forall k\in[0,\mathcal{K})

\|\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{0}\|\leq\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{0}\|+\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1}\|\leq 2B,

(19)

where we used that by the definition of
\bm{z}^{k}
, one has

\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1}\right\rVert.

(20)

Gradient mapping.

When considering algorithms of the form (
15
), it is natural to consider the gradient mapping

\bm{z}^{k}-\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}(\bm{z}^{k}-\eta\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k}))=\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\overset{\eqref{eq:prox_caracterization}}{=}\eta\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})+\eta\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k}).

(21)

It can be thought as a generalization of the gradient in the composite case, as if
f=0
it reduced to
\eta\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})
.
Importantly, if the gradient mapping is zero,
i.e.

\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}=0
, then

\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})+\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})=\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})+\nabla g(\bm{x}^{k+1})=\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k+1})=0,

i.e.
we found a stationary point of
F
, which justifies this analogy.

In the two following sections, we consider two case regarding on the value of the gradient mapping at the end of the epoch
\left\lVert\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{z}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\right\rVert
.

E.3
Large gradient mapping in the last iteration of the epoch

The goal of this section is to prove Corollary
1
, which shows that
F
decrease sufficiently when the gradient mapping
\left\lVert\bm{z}^{\mathcal{K}-1}-\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}\right\rVert\geq B
is large enough.

The following Lemma is a prox version of the classic descent Lemma, see
e.g.
(1.2.19) in
[
67
]
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\bm{x}^{k}
,
\bm{z}^{k}
be the iterates defined in Equation (
15
), we have for any
k\geq 1

F(\bm{x}^{k+1})\leq F(\bm{z}^{k})-\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2},

with
L=L_{f}+L_{g}
.

Proof.

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq F(\bm{z}^{k})+\left<\nabla F(\bm{z}^{k}),\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=F(\bm{z}^{k})+\left<\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})+\nabla f(\bm{z}^{k}),\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=F(\bm{z}^{k})+\left<\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})+\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1}),\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>-\left<\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})-\nabla f(\bm{z}^{k}),\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=F(\bm{z}^{k})+\left<-\frac{\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}}{\eta},\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>-\left<\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})-\nabla f(\bm{z}^{k}),\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2},

where we use (
16
) in the last equality. Using Cauchy-Schwarz and the fact that
\nabla f
is
L
-Lipschitz, we have

\displaystyle-\left<\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})-\nabla f(\bm{z}^{k}),\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})-\nabla f(\bm{z}^{k})\right\rVert\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq L\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}.

Thus we obtain the desired inequality

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq F(\bm{z}^{k})-\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}.

∎

The following Lemma indicates that the decrease of
F
at the last iteration of the current epoch can be quantified using the cumulated norms of gradient mapping along these iterates, and the cumulated distance between iterates along this epoch.

Lemma 4
.

We have

F(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-F(\bm{x}^{0})\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+L\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\eta L_{f}^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3
, we have

F(\bm{x}^{k+1})\leq F(\bm{z}^{k})-\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}.

(22)

As
F
is
L
-smooth, we have

F(\bm{x}^{k})\geq F(\bm{z}^{k})+\left<\nabla F(\bm{z}^{k}),\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>-\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}.

(23)

Summing Equations (
22
) and (
23
) we get

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{k+1})-F(\bm{x}^{k})\leq-\left<\nabla F(\bm{z}^{k}),\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}.

(24)

From the characterization of Equation (
16
), making
\nabla F(\bm{z}^{k})
appear we have

\nabla F(\bm{z}^{k})=\frac{\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}}{\eta}+\nabla f(\bm{z}^{k})-\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1}).

(25)

Combining Equations (
24
) and (
25
), we have

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{k+1})-F(\bm{x}^{k})\leq

\displaystyle-\left<\frac{\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}}{\eta}+\nabla f(\bm{z}^{k})-\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1}),\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta}\left<\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k},\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>+\left<\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})-\nabla f(\bm{z}^{k}),\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}.

(26)

We have the algebraic identity

\left<\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k},\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right).

(27)

Moreover for some
\lambda>0
, the
L_{f}
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f
gives

\displaystyle\left<\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})-\nabla f(\bm{z}^{k}),\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right>

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})-\nabla f(\bm{z}^{k})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{\lambda L_{f}^{2}}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}.

(28)

By injecting Equations (
27
) and (
E.3
) into Equation (
26
), we get

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{k+1})-F(\bm{x}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\left(\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)+\frac{\lambda L_{f}^{2}}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\lambda}+L\right)\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\lambda L_{f}^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}.

By using Equation (
20
), we get

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{k+1})-F(\bm{x}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\lambda}+L\right)\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\lambda L_{f}^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+2L\right)\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1}\right\rVert^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{L_{f}^{2}}{2L}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}.

where we fix
\lambda=\frac{1}{L}
in the last equality.
Using
\bm{x}^{0}=\bm{x}^{-1}
, we sum on
k=0,\dots\mathcal{K}-1

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-F(\bm{x}^{0})\leq L\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left(-\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+2L\right)\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\eta L_{f}^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-2}\left(-\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+2L\right)\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+2L\right)\left\lVert\bm{x}^{0}-\bm{x}^{-1}\right\rVert

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\right\rVert-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\eta L_{f}^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq L\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\eta L_{f}^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}

∎

Using Lemma
4
, we use the restart criterion ((

)) to ensure the decrease of

in the case of sufficiently large gradient mapping at the last iterate of the epoch.

Corollary 1
.

If the gradient mapping is large
\left\lVert\bm{z}^{\mathcal{K}-1}-\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}\right\rVert\geq B
, then

F(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-F(\bm{x}^{0})\leq-\frac{B^{2}}{8\eta}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4
, we have

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-F(\bm{x}^{0})

\displaystyle\leq L\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\eta L_{f}^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq L\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\eta L_{f}^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)\left\lVert\bm{z}^{\mathcal{K}-1}-\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}\right\rVert^{2}

If
\mathcal{K}=1
,
\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}
is an empty sum, equal to zero. Otherwise, one has

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-F(\bm{x}^{0})

\displaystyle\leq L\frac{B^{2}}{\mathcal{K}-1}-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\eta L_{f}^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)B^{2}

\displaystyle\leq LB^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\eta L_{f}^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\right)B^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(L+\frac{\eta L_{f}^{2}}{2}+\frac{3}{2}L-\frac{1}{2\eta}\right)B^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(L+\frac{\eta L_{f}^{2}}{2}+\frac{3}{2}L-\frac{1}{2\eta}\right)B^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{B^{2}}{8\eta},

where we used
\eta\leq\frac{1}{8L}
.
∎

Remark 3
.

Note that to obtain Corollary
1
, it is not necessary that the large gradient mapping occur in the last iterate. This choice of iterate will however be important in the next section, in order to control the distance between iterates at the beginning and end of the epoch, see (
29
).

E.4
Small gradient mapping for the last iterate of the epoch

The goal of this section is to prove Corollary
2
, which shows that
F
decrease sufficiently when
\left\lVert\bm{z}^{\mathcal{K}-1}-\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}\right\rVert\leq B
. Authors of
[
44
]
use the Hessian Lipschitz Assumption to approximate the function by a quadratic function, and the fact that its Hessian matrix is diagonal up to a change of basis

In our case  considering a sum of two functions and a proximal operator, the choice of the basis must be made carefully.

Preliminary.

If the gradient mapping
\|\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{z}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\|\leq B
, then from Equation (
19
) we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0}\|\leq\|\bm{z}^{\mathcal{K}-1}-\bm{x}^{0}\|+\|\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{z}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\|\leq 3B.

(29)

For each epoch, we denote
\mathbf{H}_{g}=\nabla^{2}g(\bm{x}^{0})
to be the Hessian matrix at the starting iterate
\bm{x}^{0}
and
\mathbf{H}_{g}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{g}\mathbf{U}^{T}
to be its eigenvalue decomposition with
\mathbf{U},\mathbf{\Lambda}_{g}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
,
\mathbf{U}^{T}\mathbf{U}=\mathsf{I}
and
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{g}
being diagonal. We denote
\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}
the
j
th eigenvalue and
\bm{\widetilde{x}}=\mathbf{U}^{T}\bm{x}
,
\bm{\widetilde{z}}=\mathbf{U}^{T}\bm{y}
, and
\widetilde{\nabla}f(\bm{y})=\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla f(\bm{y})
the different objects in the basis of diagonalization for
\mathbf{H}_{g}
.

By Lemma
1
(ii), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} g(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-g(\bm{x}^{0})\leq&\left<\nabla g(\bm{x}^{0}),\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0}\right>+\frac{1}{2}(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0})^{T}\mathbf{H}_{g}(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0})+\frac{\rho_{g}}{6}\|\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0}\|^{3}\\
=&\ \left<\widetilde{\nabla}g(\bm{x}^{0}),\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0}\right>+\frac{1}{2}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})^{T}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{g}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})+\frac{\rho_{g}}{6}\|\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0}\|^{3}\\
\leq&\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}})-\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})+\frac{9}{2}\rho_{g}B^{3},\end{aligned}

(30)

where we used Equation (
29
), and we denote for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
x_{j}
the
j
-th component of
\bm{x}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\hat{g}(\bm{x})=\left<\widetilde{\nabla}g(\bm{x}^{0}),\bm{x}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0}\right>+\frac{1}{2}(\bm{x}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})^{T}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{g}(\bm{x}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\hat{g}_{j}(x_{j})\\
&\hat{g}_{j}(t)=\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}g(\bm{x}^{0})(t-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{0})+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}(t-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{0})^{2}.\end{aligned}

(31)

By following the same change of basis for the function
f
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &f(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-f(\bm{x}^{0})\leq\left<\nabla f(\bm{x}^{0}),\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0}\right>+\frac{1}{2}(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0})^{T}\mathbf{H}_{f}(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0})+\frac{\rho_{f}}{6}\|\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0}\|^{3}\\
=&\ \left<\widetilde{\nabla}f(\bm{x}^{0}),\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0}\right>+\frac{1}{2}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})^{T}\mathbf{U}^{T}\mathbf{H}_{f}\mathbf{U}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})+\frac{\rho_{f}}{6}\|\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}-\bm{x}^{0}\|^{3}\\
\leq&\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}})-\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})+\frac{9}{2}\rho_{f}B^{3},\end{aligned}

(32)

where we used Equation (
29
), and we denote for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hat{f}(x)=\left<\widetilde{\nabla}f(\bm{x}^{0}),x-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0}\right>+\frac{1}{2}(x-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})^{T}\mathbf{U}^{T}\mathbf{H}_{f}\mathbf{U}(x-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})\end{aligned}

(33)

By adding Equation (
30
) and Equation (
32
), we have

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-F(\bm{x}^{0})\leq\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}})-\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})+\frac{9}{2}\rho B^{3},

(34)

where we used Equation (
29
) and
\hat{F}=\hat{g}+\hat{f}
.

Writting
\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1},\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}
as an inexact forward backward algorithm on
\hat{F}
.
We show now that the iterates
\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1},\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}
in the basis of diagonalization of
\mathbf{H}_{g}
follow a inexact forward backward algorithm on the quadratic function
\hat{F}=\hat{g}+\hat{f}
, up to an error term.

By applying the change of basis in equation (
15
), we get

\displaystyle\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}-\eta\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})-\eta\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})

(35)

\displaystyle=\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}-\eta\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k})-\eta\nabla\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})+\eta\widetilde{\delta}^{k},

(36)

where

\widetilde{\delta}^{k}=\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k})+\nabla\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})-\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})-\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1}).

Then, Algorithm
15
can be written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}=\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}+(1-\theta)(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k-1})\\
\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}=\mathsf{prox}_{\eta\hat{f}}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}-\eta\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k})+\eta\widetilde{\delta}^{k})\end{array}\right.

(37)

To ensure good properties of the iterates of (
37
), it is necessary to controll the norm of the error
\left\lVert\widetilde{\delta}^{k}\right\rVert
, as done in the following.

Upper bound on
\left\lVert\widetilde{\delta}^{k}\right\rVert
.
The norm of the error term
\widetilde{\delta}^{k}
can be controlled using the Hessian Lipschitz assumption.

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widetilde{\delta}^{k}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k})+\nabla\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})-\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})-\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k})-\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})\right\rVert+\left\lVert\nabla\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})-\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})\right\rVert.

For
k\in[0,\mathcal{K})
, using the Hessian of
g
is
\rho_{g}
Lipschitz, the fact that
\|\bm{U}^{T}\bm{x}\|=\|\bm{x}\|
for
\bm{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and equation (
19
), we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k})-\mathbf{U}^{t}\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\widetilde{\nabla}g(\bm{x}^{0})+\mathbf{\Lambda}_{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})-\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nabla g(\bm{x}^{0})+\mathbf{H}_{g}(\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{0})-\nabla g(\bm{z}^{k})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{2}g(\bm{x}^{0}+t(\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{0}))-\mathbf{H}_{f}\right)(\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{0})dt\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\rho_{g}}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{0}-\bm{z}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2\rho_{g}B^{2},

(38)

Similarly on
f
, using equation (
29
), we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})-\mathbf{U}^{t}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\widetilde{\nabla}f(\bm{x}^{0})+\mathbf{U}^{T}\mathbf{H}_{f}\mathbf{U}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})-\mathbf{U}^{T}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nabla f(\bm{x}^{0})+\mathbf{H}_{f}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{0})-\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k+1})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x}^{0}+t(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{0}))-\mathbf{H}_{f}\right)(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{0})dt\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{0}-\bm{x}^{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{9}{2}\rho_{f}B^{2}.

(39)

By combining equation (
39
) and equation (
38
), we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widetilde{\delta}^{k}\right\rVert\leq\left(2\rho_{g}+\frac{9}{2}\rho_{f}\right)B^{2}\leq\frac{9}{2}\rho B^{2},

(40)

where
\rho:=\rho_{f}+\rho_{g}
.

In the following Lemma, we use the control on
\left\lVert\widetilde{\delta}^{k}\right\rVert
to upper bound the decrease of
\hat{F}
between the first and last iterate of the current epoch. We will make use of the fact that
\hat{g}
is separable, thanks to the change of basis.

Lemma 5
.

Assume
H_{g}\succeq-\frac{\theta}{\eta}\mathsf{I}=-8(\varepsilon\rho)^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{L}\mathsf{I}
. We have

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}})\leq\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})-\frac{3\theta}{8\eta}\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{8\eta\rho^{2}B^{4}\mathcal{K}}{\theta}.

Proof.

Since
\hat{g}_{j}(x)
, defined in equation (
31
), is quadratic, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hat{g}_{j}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1})=&\hat{g}_{j}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})+\left<\nabla\hat{g}_{j}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}),\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}\right>+\frac{\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}}{2}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\end{aligned}

Convexity of
f
induces convexity of
\hat{f}
, such that

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})+\left<\nabla\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}),\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}\right>.

Then, summing over
j
, using the separability of
\hat{g}
we have

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})+\sum_{j}\hat{g}_{j}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1})=\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})+\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})=\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left(\hat{g}_{j}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})+\left<\nabla\hat{g}_{j}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}),\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}\right>+\frac{\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}}{2}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})+\left<\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}),\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}\right>+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}.

Combining the three previous equations, we get

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})\leq

\displaystyle\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})+\left<\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}),\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}\right>+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})+\left<\nabla\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}),\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})+\left<\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})-\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}),\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}\right>+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left<\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k})+\nabla\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}),\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}\right>.

However, by (
36
), we have

\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k})+\nabla\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})=-\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}-\eta\widetilde{\delta}^{k}\right),

from which we deduce

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})+\left<\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})-\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}),\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}\right>+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\eta}\left<\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}-\eta\widetilde{\delta}^{k},\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k}\right>.

In the expression above, only
\hat{F}
is not separable. So we have

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})-\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left((\nabla\hat{g_{j}}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})-\nabla\hat{g_{j}}(\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}))(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{j}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}\right)

(41)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j}\left(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}-\frac{1}{\eta}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}-\eta\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{k})(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})\right)

(42)

As
\nabla\hat{g_{j}}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})-\nabla\hat{g_{j}}(\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k})=\lambda_{j}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k})
, we consider

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\eta}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k})(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})+\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{k}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})+\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k})(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})+\frac{\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}}{2}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\eta}\left(|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}-|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}-|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\right)+\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{k}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}}{2}\left(|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}-|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\eta}\left(|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}-|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}-|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\alpha}|\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{k}|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}+\frac{\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}}{2}\left(|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}-|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\bm{\widetilde{z}}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\right)

for some positive constant
\alpha
to be specified later, where we use (
36
) in the first equality.

As we assumed
L\mathsf{I}\succeq H_{g}\succeq-\frac{\theta}{\eta}\mathsf{I}
, for all
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}

L\geq\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}\geq-\frac{\theta}{\eta}
.
If
\eta\leq\frac{1}{8L}
, we get that
\left(-\frac{1}{2\eta}+\frac{\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}}{2}\right)|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\leq\left(-4L+\frac{L}{2}\right)|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\leq 0
. By injecting these properties into equation (
E.4
), we have

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})+\sum_{j}\left((\nabla\hat{g_{j}}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})-\nabla\hat{g_{j}}(\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}))(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{j}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j}\left(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}-\frac{1}{\eta}(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}-\eta\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{k})(\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})+\sum_{j}\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}\left(|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}-|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}|\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{k}|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}+\frac{\theta}{2\eta}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\widetilde{z}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})+\sum_{j}\left(\frac{(1+\theta)(1-\theta)^{2}}{2\eta}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k-1}|^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}|\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k})+\sum_{j}\left(\frac{(1+\theta)(1-\theta)^{2}}{2\eta}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k-1}|^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}|\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\right).

We have, using
\bm{x}^{-1}=\bm{x}^{0}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left(\frac{(1+\theta)(1-\theta)^{2}}{2\eta}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k-1}|^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left(\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{(1+\theta)(1-\theta)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3\theta}{8\eta}\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}

where we let
\alpha=\frac{\theta}{4\eta}
in the last inequality, such that

\frac{1}{2\eta}-\frac{\theta}{8\eta}-\frac{(1+\theta)(1-\theta)^{2}}{2\eta}=\frac{3\theta}{8\eta}+\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\eta}-\frac{\theta^{3}}{2\eta}\geq\frac{3\theta}{8\eta}.

So, summing on
k=0,\dots,\mathcal{K}-1
we have

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}})

\displaystyle\leq\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})+\sum_{j}\left(-\frac{3\theta}{8\eta}\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}|\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}|^{2}+\frac{2\eta}{\theta}\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{d}{\leq}\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})-\frac{3\theta}{8\eta}\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{81\eta\rho^{2}B^{4}\mathcal{K}}{2\theta}

where we use (
40
) in
\overset{d}{\leq}
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Assuming
\left\lVert\bm{z}^{\mathcal{K}-1}-\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}}\right\rVert\leq B
, we have

F(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-F(\bm{x}^{0})\leq-\frac{3\theta B^{2}}{8\eta K}+\frac{81\eta\rho^{2}B^{4}K}{2\theta}+\frac{9}{2}\rho B^{3}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{\mathcal{K}})-\hat{F}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{0})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3\theta}{8\eta}\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{81\eta\rho^{2}B^{4}\mathcal{K}}{2\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3\theta}{8\eta}\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{81\eta\rho^{2}B^{4}\mathcal{K}}{2\theta}

Using (
34
), we get

\displaystyle F(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-F(\bm{x}^{0})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3\theta}{8\eta}\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{K}-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}_{j}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{81\eta\rho^{2}B^{4}\mathcal{K}}{2\theta}+\frac{9}{2}\rho B^{3}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3\theta B^{2}}{8\eta\mathcal{K}}+\frac{81\eta\rho^{2}B^{4}\mathcal{K}}{2\theta}+\frac{9}{2}\rho B^{3}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3\theta B^{2}}{8\eta K}+\frac{81\eta\rho^{2}B^{4}K}{2\theta}+\frac{9}{2}\rho B^{3},

where the last inequality uses
\mathcal{K}\leq K
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

If the restart criterion is reached at
\mathcal{K}<K
, assuming
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{64\rho\eta^{2}}
, we have

F(\bm{x}^{\mathcal{K}})-F(\bm{x}^{0})\leq-\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{\rho}}.

Proof.

Combining Corollary
1
and Lemma
6
, and using parameter choice
\eta=\frac{1}{8L}
,
B=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}}
,
\theta=4(\varepsilon\rho\eta^{2})^{1/4}\in(0,1)
, and
K=\frac{1}{\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{3\theta B^{2}}{8\eta K}=\frac{3}{8\eta}\theta^{2}B^{2}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{\rho}},

\displaystyle\frac{81\eta\rho^{2}B^{4}K}{2\theta}=\frac{81}{2\theta^{2}}\eta\rho^{2}B^{4}=\frac{3^{4}}{2^{9}}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{\rho}},

\displaystyle\frac{9}{2}\rho B^{3}=\frac{3^{2}}{2^{4}}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{\rho}},

\displaystyle\frac{B^{2}}{8\eta}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{5}\eta\rho}.

Such that

Lemma 7

If the restart criterion of Algorithm

Proof.

The proof follows three steps. First, we bound the quantity

(i) Bounding

Denote

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\hat{g}(\widetilde{y})+\nabla\hat{f}(\widetilde{x})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\frac{1}{K_{0}+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K_{0}}\nabla\hat{g}(\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k})+\nabla\hat{f}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta(K_{0}+1)}\left\lVert\sum_{k=0}^{K_{0}}(\bm{\widetilde{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{\widetilde{z}}^{k}+\eta\widetilde{\delta}^{k})\right\rVert

(43)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta(K_{0}+1)}\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{K_{0}}\left(\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{k}-(1-\theta)(\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{k}-\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{k-1})+\eta\widetilde{\delta}^{k}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta(K_{0}+1)}\left\|\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{K_{0}+1}-\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{0}-(1-\theta)(\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{K_{0}}-\widetilde{\bm{x}}^{0})+\eta\sum_{k=0}^{K_{0}}\widetilde{\delta}^{k}\right\|

Appendix F
Convergence Analysis of Continuous RISP

Let
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. The Nesterov accelerated gradient

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\bm{z}^{k}=&\bm{x}^{k}+(1-\theta_{n})(\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1})\\
\bm{x}^{k+1}=&\bm{z}^{k}-\eta\nabla F(\bm{z}^{k})\end{array}\right.

(NAG)

can be seen as a discretization of the following continuous dynamical system,

\bm{\ddot{x}}_{t}+\alpha(t)\bm{\dot{x}}_{t}+\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t})=0,

(DS)

where
\bm{\dot{x}}_{t}
and
\bm{\ddot{x}}_{t}
are respectively the first and second order derivative with respect to
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, see
[
45
]
for a seminal work, or
[
91
, Section B.2.2]
for a more general result. Equation
DS
defines a damping system, with damping coefficient
\alpha(t)
. Intuitively,
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a rolling object on the surface defined by the image of
f
, subject to some friction. The higher
\alpha(t)
, the higher the friction. We will consider the constant friction version, called the heavy ball equation
[
59
]

\bm{\ddot{x}}_{t}+\alpha\bm{\dot{x}}_{t}+\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t})=0.

(HB)

We can think of
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
HB
as a continuous version of
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
defined in
NAG
, with constant sequence
\theta_{n}=\theta
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. There are several reasons to study
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
:

1.

Dealing with continuous system offers tools that do not exists in the discrete settings, such as derivation, which may leads to smoother computations. If one’s goal is to study
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
, one can start to study
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in order to gain intuition.

2.

The dynamical systems can be thought as a generalization of the algorithms, as there exists several way to discretize a dynamical system. By instance, considering the gradient flow equation

\bm{\dot{x}}=-\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t}),

(GF)

one can consider gradient descent
\bm{x}^{k+1}=\bm{x}^{k}-\eta\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})
as an explicit discretization of
GF
, while the proximal algorithm
\bm{x}^{k+1}=\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}(\bm{x}^{k})=\bm{x}^{k}-\eta\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k+1})
can be thought as an implicit discretization of
GF
.

F.1
Continous heavy ball

In this section, we show how the inertial mechanisms of RISP-Prox (Algorithm
2
) can be thought as discretization of the
HB

equation. The same derivation for RISP-GM (Algorithm
2
) is deduced in a similar way, and has already been done, see
[
45
]
or
[
91
, Section B.2.2]
.

From RISP-Prox to Heavy Ball.
Under Assumption

, there exists

Sufficient decrease in each epoch.

Similarily to the discrete case, we show that each time the restart criterion is triggered, then we have a sufficient decrease of the function value over the epoch.

Lemma 8
.

Let
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
verifies

Proof.

Set the following Lyapunov function

We differentiate
(E_{t})_{t\geq 0}

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{t}=

\displaystyle\left<\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t}),\bm{\dot{x}}_{t}\right>+\left<\bm{\dot{x}}_{t},\bm{\ddot{x}}_{t}\right>

Finally, noting that assuming

implies

F(\bm{x}_{T})-F(\bm{x}_{0})\leq-\alpha\frac{B^{2}}{T_{\mathop{\mathsf{max}}}}.

Lemma 9
.

Let
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
verifies

with initial condition

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{1}{K_{0}}\int_{0}^{K_{0}}\nabla F(\bm{x}_{s})ds\right\rVert

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\dot{x}}_{K_{0}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T_{\mathop{\mathsf{max}}}-\frac{T_{\mathop{\mathsf{max}}}}{2}}\int_{\frac{T_{\mathop{\mathsf{max}}}}{2}}^{T_{\mathop{\mathsf{max}}}}\left\lVert\bm{\dot{x}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}dt

F.4
Convergence analysis of continuous RED

As mentioned in the introduction of Appendix
F
, the gradient descent algorithm

\bm{x}^{k+1}=\bm{x}^{k}-\eta\nabla F(\bm{x}^{k})

(69)

can be seen as a discretization of a continous dynamical system, namely the gradient flow equation

\bm{\dot{x}}=-\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t}).

(GF)

Under Assumption
1
, the gradient flow equation can be used to formulate a continuous version of (
2
)

\bm{\dot{x}}=-\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})+S(\bm{x}_{t}),

(Cont-RED)

where (
Cont-RED
) is a gradient flow for
\nabla F=\nabla f+\nabla g
. This enables us to formulate a convergence rate for this continuous version of RED, in order to confirm that the benefit of the inertial mechanism also holds in the continuous setting.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a gradient flow (
GF
) for
\nabla F=\nabla f+\nabla g
. In at most
T
execution time, the process achieves a point
\tilde{x}
such that
\left\lVert\nabla F(\tilde{x})\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{\frac{F(\bm{x}_{0})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F}{T}}=\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})
.

Proof.

Let

E_{t}=F(\bm{x}_{t})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F.

We derivate
E_{t}

\dot{E}_{t}=\left<\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t}),\bm{\dot{x}}\right>=-\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}.

We integrate between
0
and
T

\int_{0}^{T}\dot{E}_{t}ds=-\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}dt=E_{T}-E_{0}\geq\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F-F(\bm{x}_{0}).

Rearranging, and taking the min, we get

T\mathop{\mathsf{min}}_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}dt\leq F(\bm{x}_{0})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F.

Dividing by
T
, and considering
\tilde{T}:=\arg\mathop{\mathsf{min}}_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\nabla F(\bm{x}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}
, we obtain

\left\lVert\nabla f(\bm{x}_{\tilde{T}})\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{\frac{F(\bm{x}_{0})-\mathop{\mathsf{min}}F}{T}}.

∎

The result of Theorem
5
highlights that without the inertial mechanism, we lose the
\mathcal{O}(T^{-4/7})
convergence rate (Theorem
3
) for a
\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})
convergence rate. Note also that Theorem
5
is consistent with Theorem
1
, with respectively a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})
for the continuous time, and
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2})
for the discrete time.

Remark 6
.

As it was the case when considering the restarted heavy ball equation, there is no need to assume the gradient Lipschitz. In fact, this gradient Lipschitzness is commonly useful to ensure that the discretization preserves the continuous behavior.

\displaystyle\texttt{SoftPlus}(x)=\mathsf{log\,}\left(1+e^{x}\right).

We have the following derivatives for the Softplus activation function, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle|\texttt{SoftPlus}^{(1)}(x)|

\displaystyle=|\frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}|\leq 1

\displaystyle|\texttt{SoftPlus}^{(2)}(x)|

\displaystyle=|\frac{1}{4}\cosh^{-2}\left(x\right)|\leq\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle|\texttt{SoftPlus}^{(3)}(x)|

\displaystyle=|\frac{1}{2}\tanh\left(x\right)\cosh^{-2}\left(x\right)|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Because
\texttt{SoftPlus}^{(2)}
and
\texttt{SoftPlus}^{(3)}
are bounded, we deduce that
\texttt{SoftPlus}^{(1)}
and
\texttt{SoftPlus}^{(2)}
are Lipschitz and bounded. Therefore, by composition and sum,
J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}
, the Jacobian of
{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}
, and
d_{J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}^{T}}
, the differential of
J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}^{T}
, are Lipschitz and bounded.

Then the gradient of the regularization
g_{\sigma}
is computed by

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\nabla g_{\sigma}(\bm{x})=\bm{x}-{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}(\bm{x})-[J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}(\bm{x})]^{T}\left(\bm{x}-{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}(\bm{x})\right),

with
J_{N_{\sigma}}(\bm{x})
the jacobian matrix of
{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}
at the point
\bm{x}

and the Hessian of the regularization is

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\nabla^{2}g_{\sigma}(\bm{x})=\mathsf{I}-J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}(\bm{x})-d_{J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}^{T}}[\bm{x}]\left(\bm{x}-{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}(\bm{x})\right)-[J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}(\bm{x})]^{T}\left(\mathsf{I}-J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}(\bm{x})\right),

where
d_{J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}^{T}}[\bm{x}]:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
is the differential of
J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}
at point
\bm{x}
, thus
d_{J_{{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}}^{T}}[\bm{x}]\left(\bm{x}-{\mathsf{N}}_{\sigma}(\bm{x})\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
.

Finally, Assumptions
2
-
3
on the regularization are verified.
∎

G.3
Nonlinear inverse problem

In this section, we consider Rician noise removal task.
Let
\bm{n}_{\text{real}}
and
\bm{n}_{\text{imag}}
denote two
i.i.d.
Gaussian noises with zero mean and standard derivation
\sigma_{y}
. The Rician noise corrupted observation
y
is obtained by:

\displaystyle\bm{y}=\sqrt{(\bm{x}+\bm{n}_{\text{real}})^{2}+\bm{n}_{\text{imag}}^{2}}.

According to
[
96
]
, the conditional probability density function (PDF) of
\bm{y}
is

\displaystyle p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})=\frac{\bm{y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\bm{x}^{2}+\bm{y}^{2}}{2\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right)I_{0}\left(\frac{\bm{x\odot y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right),

which is known as
Rice
or
Rician
distribution. Here
I_{0}
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind with order zero. Consider the modified Bessel’s differential equations:

\displaystyle x^{2}y^{\prime\prime}+xy^{\prime}-(x^{2}+n^{2})y=0,n\geq 0.

The solutions of the previous equation
I_{n}(x)=y(x)
continuous in zero are called the modified Bessel function of the first kind with order
n
.

After omitting some constant terms, the data fidelity term
f
for Rician noise removal is

\displaystyle f(\bm{x})=\left\langle\textbf{1},\frac{\bm{x}^{2}}{2\sigma_{y}^{2}}-\mathsf{log\,}I_{0}\left(\frac{\bm{x\odot y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right)\right\rangle.

Based on the recurrence formulas of the derivative of
I_{n}

[
97
]
, we have that
\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle I_{0}^{\prime}(x)=I_{1}(x),I_{1}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(I_{0}(x)+I_{2}(x)),I_{2}(x)=I_{0}(x)-\frac{2}{x}I_{1}(x).

Thus we have

\displaystyle I_{1}^{\prime}(x)=I_{0}(x)-\frac{1}{x}I_{1}(x).

Let
B(x)=\left(\mathsf{log\,}I_{0}(x)\right)^{\prime}=\frac{I_{0}^{\prime}(x)}{I_{0}(x)}
. Then
B(x)=\frac{I_{1}(x)}{I_{0}(x)}
. According to the proof of Proposition 2.1 in
[
98
]
, we know

\displaystyle B^{\prime}(x)=1-\frac{1}{x}B(x)-B^{2}(x).

According to Proposition 2.1 in
[
98
]
,
B(x)
is a increasing concave function on
[0,\infty)
with
B(0)=0
,
B(\infty)=1
.
B^{\prime}(x)
is a decreasing function with
B^{\prime}(0)=0.5
,
B^{\prime}(\infty)=0
. Based on this, we further have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}B^{\prime\prime}(x)=&\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{x}B(x)-B^{2}(x)\right)^{\prime}=\frac{1}{x^{2}}B(x)-\frac{1}{x}B^{\prime}(x)-2B(x)B^{\prime}(x)\\
=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{2}}B(x)-\frac{1}{x}\left(1-\frac{1}{x}B(x)-B^{2}(x)\right)-2B(x)\left(1-\frac{1}{x}B(x)-B^{2}(x)\right)\\
=&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{x}+\left(\frac{2}{x^{2}}-2\right)B(x)+\frac{3}{x}B^{2}(x)+2B^{3}(x).\end{array}

Figure 14
:
Visualizations of function
I_{0},B,B^{\prime},B^{\prime\prime}
.

In Figure
14
, we plot the functions
I_{0}
,
B
,
B^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime\prime}
. It is clear that
B^{\prime\prime}(x)
is bounded by
[-0.25,0]
. Based on this, we now prove that
f
has a Lipschitz gradient and Hessian.

Lemma 12
.

Let
I_{0}
be the modified Bessel function of the first kind with order zero. Let
\bm{y}
be the observation,
f(\bm{x})
be the data fidelity term for the Rician noise removal task, that is,

\displaystyle f(\bm{x})=\left\langle\textbf{1},\frac{\bm{x}^{2}}{2\sigma_{y}^{2}}-\mathsf{log\,}I_{0}\left(\frac{\bm{x\odot y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right)\right\rangle.

Then,
f
has a Lipschitz gradient and Hessian.

Proof.

To prove that
f
has a Lipschitz gradient and Hessian, we only need to prove it in the one dimensional case, because the function is separable. Thus, we only need to consider
\bm{x}=x\in[0,\infty)
.

Clearly, we have that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}x-\frac{y}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}B\left(\frac{xy}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right).

In order to prove that
f^{\prime}
is Lipschitz, we only need to prove that
f^{\prime\prime}
is bounded.

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}-\frac{y^{2}}{\sigma_{y}^{4}}B^{\prime}\left(\frac{xy}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right).

Since
B^{\prime}
is bounded, we know that
f
has a Lipschitz gradient.

To prove the Lipschitz property of
f^{\prime\prime}
, we only need to prove the boundedness of
f^{(3)}
. Note that

\displaystyle f^{(3)}(x)=-\frac{y^{3}}{\sigma_{y}^{6}}B^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{xy}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right),

and that
B^{\prime\prime}
is bounded by
[-0.25,0]
, we complete the proof.
∎

Based on the above derivations, we have already shown how to compute
\nabla f
:

\displaystyle\nabla f(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\bm{x}-\frac{\bm{y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}B\left(\frac{\bm{x\odot y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right).

Computation of
\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}
.

Based on the technique used by
[
99
]
, we are able to solve
\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}
efficiently by the Iterative Reweighted
L^{1}
method (IRL1) given in
[
100
]
. Consider the objective function of
\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}
:

\displaystyle\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}(\bm{z})=\arg\mathop{\mathsf{min}}\limits_{x}\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x-z}\|^{2}+\eta\left\langle\textbf{1},\frac{\bm{x}^{2}}{2\sigma_{y}^{2}}-\mathsf{log\,}I_{0}\left(\frac{\bm{x\odot y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right)\right\rangle.

According to the Lemma 2 in
[
99
]
, it can be rewritten as the sum of a convex function
f_{1}
and a non-decreasing concave function
f_{2}
:

\displaystyle f_{1}(\bm{x})+f_{2}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x-z}\|^{2}+\eta\left\langle\textbf{1},\frac{\bm{x}^{2}}{2\sigma_{y}^{2}}-\mathsf{log\,}I_{0}\left(\frac{\bm{x\odot y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right)\right\rangle,

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}f_{1}(\bm{x})=&\displaystyle\eta\left\langle\textbf{1},\frac{\bm{x}^{2}}{2\sigma_{y}^{2}}-\frac{\bm{x\odot y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x-z}\|^{2},\\
f_{2}(\bm{x})=&\displaystyle\eta\left\langle\textbf{1},\frac{\bm{xy}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}-\mathsf{log\,}I_{0}\left(\frac{\bm{x\odot y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right)\right\rangle.\end{array}

IRL1 algorithm linearizes the non-convex part
f_{2}
, and solve the resulted weighted convex subproblem. The IRL1 algorithm for solving
\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}(\bm{z})
is given as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\bm{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}&=\displaystyle\frac{\bm{y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}B\left(\frac{\bm{x}^{t}\bm{\odot y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right)\\
\bm{x}^{t+1}&=\displaystyle\arg\mathop{\mathsf{min}}\limits_{x}\eta\left\langle\textbf{1},\frac{\bm{x}^{2}}{2\sigma_{y}^{2}}-\frac{\bm{x\odot y}}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x-z}\|^{2}-\eta\langle\bm{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}},\bm{x}\rangle\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{\bm{z}+\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}\bm{y}+\eta\bm{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}}{1+\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{y}^{2}}}.\end{array}

In experiments, we set the maximum inner iteration number for IRL1 to be
10
because it usually solves the proximal sub-problem within
5
iterations.

The linear inverse scattering task aims to reconstruct the permittivity contrast distribution of an object from measurements of its scattered field captured by an array of receivers.
In our setup, we consider the first Born approximation
[
101
]

\bm{y}=\bm{H}(\bm{u}\odot\bm{x})+\bm{n},

(71)

where
\bm{x}
denotes the permittivity contrast distribution,
\bm{y}
is the scattered field measured by receivers,
\bm{H}

\bm{u}
is the light field,
\odot
denotes for the Hadamard product and
\bm{n}
is Gaussian noise.

The corresponding fidelity term is given by

f(\bm{x})=\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\bm{H}(\bm{u}\odot\bm{x})-\bm{y}\|^{2}.

(72)

For this fidelity term
f
, Assumptions
2
–
4
are satisfied.

In this problem, we set 360 receivers and 240 transmitters. The Gaussian noise level is 0.0001, as in
[
102
]
.
The dataset of ODT images was created using the CytoPacq Web Service
[
65
]

1

1

https://cbia.fi.muni.cz/simulator
.

For the denoiser, we use the standard DRUNet architecture as proposed by
[

]
. The training set is generated by the CytoPacq Web Service, which contains

Calculation of
\mathsf{Prox_{\eta f}}

For the computation of
\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}
, there is no efficient solution. Since the image size is large, the matrix
\mathsf{Diag}(\bm{u})\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{H}\mathsf{Diag}(\bm{u})
has size

. Thus, computing the inverse of the matrix
\lambda\mathsf{Diag}(\bm{u})\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{H}\mathsf{Diag}(\bm{u})+I_{1024^{2}}

In order to approximate
\mathsf{prox}_{\eta f}

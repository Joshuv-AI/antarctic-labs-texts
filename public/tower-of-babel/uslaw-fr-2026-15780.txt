Texas Gas Transmission, LLC; Notice of Schedule for the 
Preparation of an Environmental Assessment for the Dearborn County 
Lateral Project

    On May 29, 2026, Texas Gas Transmission, LLC (Texas Gas) filed an 
application in Docket No. CP26-536-000 requesting a Certificate of 
Public Convenience and Necessity pursuant to Section 7(c) of the 
Natural Gas Act to construct and operate certain natural gas pipeline 
facilities. The proposed project is known as the Dearborn County 
Lateral Project (Project) and would entail Texas Gas constructing and 
operating a new interstate natural gas pipeline and associated 
aboveground facilities in Indiana, Kentucky, and Ohio to deliver up to 
265,000 dekatherms per day of firm natural gas transportation service 
to Vistra Corp.'s Miami Fort Power Plant in Hamilton County, Ohio, 
supporting the plant's planned conversion from coal-fueled to natural-
gas-fueled generation.
    On June 12, 2026, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission 
(Commission or FERC) issued its Notice of Application for the Project. 
Among other things, that notice alerted agencies issuing federal 
authorizations of the requirement to complete all necessary reviews and 
to reach a final decision on a request for a federal authorization 
within 90 days of the date of issuance of the Commission staff's 
environmental document for the Project.
    This notice identifies Commission staff's intention to prepare an 
environmental assessment (EA) for the Project and the planned schedule 
for the completion of the environmental review.\1\ The EA will be 
issued for a 30-day comment period.

Project Description

    The Dearborn County Lateral Project would consist of the following 
new facilities:
     an approximately 11.8-mile-long, 20-inch-diameter natural 
gas pipeline beginning in Dearborn County, Indiana and terminating in 
Hamilton County, Ohio;
     tie-in piping, a pig launcher, and associated 
infrastructure to connect the new lateral to the existing Texas Gas 26-
inch-diameter Mainline System in Dearborn County, Indiana;
     a new measurement and flow control station and associated 
infrastructure in Dearborn County, Indiana; and
     a new pig receiver and associated infrastructure at the 
terminus of the pipeline in Hamilton County, Ohio.

Background

    On June 26, 2026, the Commission issued a Notice of Scoping Period 
Requesting Comments on Environmental Issues for the Proposed Dearborn 
County Lateral Project (Notice of Scoping). The Notice of Scoping was 
sent to affected landowners; federal, state, and local government 
agencies; elected officials; environmental and public interest groups; 
Native American tribes; other interested parties; and local libraries 
and newspapers. In response to the Notice of Scoping and the 
Commission's Notice of Application for the Project, the Commission 
received comments from county officials, state legislators, and some 
potentially affected landowners. The Boone County Fiscal Court 
expressed confusion regarding the rationale for routing the Project 
through Boone County, noting that the route appears circuitous and 
would require two crossings of the Ohio River, and requested that any 
construction within the county be conducted in the least impactful 
manner possible while stating its opposition to the use of eminent 
domain. The Commission also received letters of support for the Project 
from Ohio State Senator Louis W. Blessing III and Ohio State 
Representative Mike Odioso.
    The primary issues raised by landowners relate to the routing of 
the Project across their private properties. Concerns include impacts 
on farming operations, maple syrup and honey production, woodland 
resources, groundwater and drinking-water springs, stocked ponds and 
outflow structures, farmland privacy, and property values. Commenters 
also raised concerns regarding the need for the Project; cumulative 
impacts; effects on soil, water, and vegetation resources; impacts on 
special-status species; the use of eminent domain; and the potential 
for increased trespassing along the right-of-way. All substantive 
environmental comments will be addressed in the EA.

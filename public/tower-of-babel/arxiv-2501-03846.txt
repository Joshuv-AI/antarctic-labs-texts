Isogeny graphs with level structures arising from the Verschiebung map
Antonio Lei, Katharina Müller

Abstract
. We enhance an isogeny graph of elliptic curves by incorporating level structures defined by bases of the kernels of iterates of the Verschiebung map. We extend several previous results on isogeny graphs with level structures defined by geometric points to these graphs. Firstly, we prove that these graphs form ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -towers of graph coverings as the power of the Verschiebung map varies. Secondly, we prove that the connected components of these graphs display a volcanic structure.

Introduction

Throughout this article, we fix a prime number
p
. Let
X
be a finite connected graph. Vallières and McGown–Vallières
[
Val21
,
MV23
,
MV24
]
initiated the study of the Iwasawa theory of graph coverings by establishing an asymptotic formula for the
p

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-covering of

, where
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denotes the ring of
p
-adic integers. This formula is analogous to the classical result of Iwasawa
[
Iwa73
]
on class numbers inside a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of a number field. Several authors have proven various results in the context of graph coverings, which resemble their counterparts in classical Iwasawa theory. These results can be found in the works of Gonet
[
Gon21
,
Gon22
]
, Lei–Vallières
[
LV23
]
, Ray–Vallières
[
RV22
]
, Kleine–Müller
[
KM23
,
KM24
]
, Kataoka
[
Kat24
]
, and Pengo–Vallières
[
PV23
]
.

Isogeny graphs refer to graphs, where the vertices represent isomorphism classes of elliptic curves defined over a finite field of characteristic
p
, and the edges represent
\ell
-isogenies between two elliptic curves, where
\ell
is a prime number different from
p
. When the vertices represent supersingular elliptic curves, such graphs have important applications in cryptography; see
[
CLG09
,
DFJP14
,
EHL+18
]
. In recent years, there has been a growing interest in understanding the structure of isogeny graphs enhanced with level structures (given by subgroups or torsion points of the elliptic curves). For example, Arpin
[
Arp24
]
and Roda
[
Rod19
]
studied properties of isogeny graphs of supersingular elliptic curves enhanced with
\Gamma_{0}(N)
-level and
\Gamma(N)
-level structures, respectively. Goren–Kassaei
[
GK21
]
studied similar questions for
\Gamma_{1}(N)
-level structures in both ordinary and supersingular settings. Codogni-Lido recently developed a general framework for studying isogeny graphs with any level structure arising from a congruence subgroup in
[
CL23
]
. In
[
LM24
,
LM23
]
, the authors of the present article investigated how isogeny graphs with level structures result in interesting towers of graph coverings resembling
p
-adic Lie extensions studied in the Iwasawa theory of number fields as one varies the levels.

In
[
LM24
]
, the following
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers were studied. Let
N\geq 1
be an integer coprime to two rational prime numbers
p
and
\ell
. Let
k
be a finite field of characteristic
p
. We define
G_{N}^{m}
as the graph whose vertices consist of isomorphism classes of pairs
(E,P)
, where
E
is an
ordinary
elliptic curve defined over
k
and
P
is a point of
E(\overline{k})
of order
Np^{m}
; the edges of

are given by
\ell
-isogenies. There is a natural projection map

given by

. The main reason we chose to work with ordinary elliptic curves was the lack of points of order

on supersingular elliptic curves defined over a field of characteristic

. In
[
LM23
]
, we circumvented this issue by considering supersingular elliptic curves defined over finite fields of characteristic

of order

, where

.

In this article, we propose an alternative approach. Instead of defining isogeny graphs with level structures given by geometric points, we consider our elliptic curves as group schemes and furnish our graphs with level structures originating from kernels of iterates of the Verschiebung map. This allows us to consider both
ordinary
and
supersingular
cases simultaneously.

From now on,
k
denotes a positive integer, and we write
S
for the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}})
. We fix a prime number
\ell\neq p
and a non-negative integer

coprime to
p
and
\ell
. For an integer

, we define in Definition
3.1
a directed graph
G_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)
, whose set of vertices is given by isomorphism classes of
(E,Q,P)
, where

is an elliptic curve,

is a geometric point on

of order

and

is a generator of
\ker(V_{E}^{n})
(

is the Verschiebung map of

We prove the following generalization of

Let

Remark 1.1

A vertex

The structure of isogeny graphs consisting of ordinary elliptic curves (without level structures) was studied by Kohel
[
Koh96
]

[

,

In the present article, we generalize these results to isogeny graphs whose level structures are given by kernels of iterates of the Verschiebung map, which we denote by

(Theorems

•

In Appendix

2.
Notation

Throughout this article,
E
always denotes an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
. Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to S=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}})
be the structure map. We write
F_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to E^{(p)}
and
V_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{(p)}\to E
for the Frobenius and Verschiebung maps attached to
E
, respectively. Here,
E^{(p)}
is the pull-back of
\pi
and
F_{{S}}
, where
F_{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow S
denotes the absolute Frobenius (on affine rings, this corresponds to the endomorphism
x\mapsto x^{p}
). Note that
F_{E}
and
V_{E}
are dual isogenies of each other. The reader is referred to
[
KM85
, Chapter 12]
for a more detailed discussion on these maps.

For a positive integer
n
, we write
F_{E}^{n}
for the composition of Frobenii

E\to E^{(p)}\to\cdots\to E^{(p^{n})}

and define
V_{E}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{(p^{n})}\to E
similarly. Note that the kernel of
V_{E}^{n}
is cyclic of order
p^{n}
.
This allows us to identify
E^{(p^{n})}
with
E/\ker(F_{E}^{n})
and
E
with
E^{(p^{n})}/\ker(V_{E}^{n})
.
When confusion may not arise, we remove the subscript
E
from the notation writing
F
instead of
F_{E}
and
V
instead of
V_{E}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Both
F
and
V
commute with isogenies.
In particular, if
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
is an isogeny of elliptic curves,
\phi
induces a well-defined isogeny
\phi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{(p^{n})}\to E^{\prime(p^{n})}
that maps
\ker(V_{E}^{n})
to
\ker(V_{E^{\prime}}^{n})
. When no confusion may arise, we simply write
\phi
for
\phi_{n}
.

Given a graph
X
(that is directed or undirected), we write
\mathbb{V}(X)
and
\mathbb{E}(X)
for the set of vertices and edges of
X
, respectively. In this paper, we allow for multiple edges and loops. This is crucial for our purposes, since there can be more than one isogeny relating two (equivalence classes of) elliptic curves.

Definition 2.2
.

A morphism of graphs
\phi\colon Y\to X
consists of two maps
\mathbb{V}(Y)\to\mathbb{V}(X)
and
\mathbb{E}(Y)\to\mathbb{E}(X)
such that if
e
is an edge connecting
v
and
w
, then
\phi(e)
is an edge connecting
\phi(v)
and
\phi(w)
.
We call a surjective morphism of graphs
\pi\colon Y\to X
a covering of graphs if
\pi
is locally a bijection.

Let
Y/X
be a covering of finite directed graphs with the projection map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to X
. We say that
Y/X
is a
d
-sheeted covering
if each element of
\mathbb{V}(X)
has
d
pre-images in
\mathbb{V}(Y)
under
\pi
.

The group of
deck transformations
of
Y/X
, denoted by
\mathrm{Deck}(Y/X)
, is the group of graph automorphisms
\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to Y
such that
\pi\circ\sigma=\pi
.

We say that a
d
-sheeted covering
Y/X
is
Galois
if
d=|\mathrm{Deck}(Y/X)|
. In this case,
\mathrm{Deck}(Y/X)
is called the Galois group of the covering and is denoted by
\Gal(Y/X)
.

We shall call the map that sends a directed graph to an undirected graph by ignoring the directions of the edges the
forgetful map
. Note that we can regard any undirected graph as a directed one after replacing each edge by two directed edges with opposite directions (see
[
Ser77
, §I.2.1]
).

3.
Defining isogeny graphs with level structures

We introduce our definition of isogeny graphs with level structures arising from the Verschiebung map. Similarly to
[
LM24
, §2]
, we analyze the number of connected components as the level varies, which constitutes the main ingredient of the proof of Theorem
A
. Recall that
k\geq 1
is an integer,
p
and
\ell
are fixed distinct prime numbers, and
N\geq 1
is an integer that is coprime to
p
and
\ell
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer.

i)

Let
E
and
E^{\prime}
be elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
. Let
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
be geometric points of order
N
on
E
and
E^{\prime}
, respectively. Let
P_{n}
and
P_{n}^{\prime}
be generators of
\ker(V_{E}^{n}\colon E^{(p^{n})}\to E)
and
\ker(V_{E^{\prime}}^{n}\colon E^{\prime(p^{n})}\to E^{\prime})
, respectively. We say that tuples
(E,Q,P_{n})
and
(E^{\prime},Q^{\prime},P_{n}^{\prime})
are equivalent if there is an isomorphism
{\psi}\colon E\to E^{\prime}
such that
{\psi}(Q)=Q^{\prime}
and
{\psi}(P_{n})=P_{n}^{\prime}
. By an abuse of notation, we shall denote the equivalence class containing the triple
(E,Q,P_{n})
by the same symbol.

ii)

Let
E
and
E^{\prime}
be elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
. Let
\phi,\phi^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to E^{\prime}
be isogenies. We say that
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
are equivalent if they have the same kernel.

iii)

Let
G_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)
be the directed graph given by:

–

The vertices of
G_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)
are the equivalence classes of tuples
(E,Q,P_{n})
given as in i).

–

The directed edges from
(E,Q,P_{n})
to
(E^{\prime},Q^{\prime},P_{n}^{\prime})
are the equivalence classes of
\ell
-isogenies
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
such that
\phi(Q)=Q^{\prime}
and
\phi_{n}(P_{n})=P_{n}^{\prime}
, where
\phi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{(p^{n})}\rightarrow(E^{\prime})^{(p^{n})}
is the isogeny discussed in Remark
2.1
.

If no confusion may arise, we shall drop the subscript
n
and simply write
P
for
P_{n}
. When
n=0
and
N=1
, we may drop
P_{n}
and
Q
from the notation and simply write
E
for a vertex of the graph
G_{\ell}^{p}(0,1)
. We write
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)
for the image of
G_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)
under the forgetful map.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. The map
(E,Q,P)\mapsto(E,Q,V(P))
induces a degree-
p
graph covering

\pi_{n,n-1}\colon G_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)\to G_{\ell}^{p}(n-1,N).

Proof.

It follows from
[
KM85
, Proposition 12.3.2(3)]
that
P\in E^{(p^{n})}({\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}})
generates
\ker(V^{n})
if and only if
V(P)
generates
\ker(V^{n-1})
. Therefore,
\pi_{n,n-1}(\mathbb{V}(G_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)))=\mathbb{V}(G_{\ell}^{p}(n-1,N))
. To show that
\pi_{n,n-1}
is a morphism of graphs, we need to show that if there is an edge
e\in\mathbb{E}(G_{\ell}^{p}(n,N))
linking
(E,Q,P)
to
(E^{\prime},Q^{\prime},P^{\prime})
, then there is an edge in
G_{\ell}^{p}(n-1,N)
linking
(E,Q,V(P))
to
(E^{\prime},Q^{\prime},V(P^{\prime}))
.

Let
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
be an
\ell
-isogeny corresponding to
e
. Then
\phi(Q)=Q^{\prime}
and
\phi(P)=P^{\prime}
. As

\phi(V(P))=V(\phi(P))=V(P^{\prime}),

the isogeny
\phi
gives rise to an edge linking
(E,Q,V(P))
to
(E^{\prime},Q^{\prime},V(P^{\prime}))
.
Therefore,
\pi_{n,n-1}
is indeed a morphism of graphs.

It remains to show that
\pi_{n,n-1}
is a graph covering. Let
v\in\mathbb{V}(G_{\ell}^{p}(n,N))
. We have to show that the edges starting (resp. ending) at
v
are in one-to-one correspondence with the edges starting (resp. ending) at
\pi_{n,n-1}(v)
. Each edge is generated by a unique equivalence class of
\ell
-isogenies. The number of isomorphism classes of these isogenies depends only on the elliptic curve
E
, not on the level structure. Therefore,
\pi_{n,n-1}
is locally a bijection, as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
n\geq m\geq 0
be integers. The composition

\pi_{n,m}=\pi_{n,n-1}\circ\pi_{n-1,n-2}\circ\dots\circ\pi_{m+1,m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)\to G_{\ell}^{p}(m,N)

is a graph covering.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma
3.2
since the composition of graph coverings is a graph covering.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a connected component of
G_{\ell}^{p}(0,1)
that consists not only of an isolated vertex. Let
\mathcal{X}(n,N)
be the subgraph of
G_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)
whose vertices are of the form
(E,Q,P_{n})
where
E
is a vertex of
\mathcal{X}
.
The number of connected components in
\mathcal{X}(n,N)
is uniformly bounded in
n
.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathcal{X}(n,N)
is a finite graph. Let
E
be a representative of a vertex in
\mathcal{X}
, and let
Q
be a fixed geometric point in
E
of order
N
. We define a relation
\sim
in
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{X}(n,N))
as follows:
(E,Q,P)\sim(E^{\prime},Q^{\prime},P^{\prime})
if these two vertices are in the same connected component in
\mathcal{X}(n,N)
. It follows from the discussion in
[
LM24
, Remark 2.4]
that if
\phi
is an
\ell
-isogeny that induces an edge in
\mathcal{X}(n,N)
from
(E,Q,P)
to
(E^{\prime},Q^{\prime},P^{\prime})
, then the dual isogeny
\hat{\phi}
induces an edge in
\mathcal{X}(n,N)
from
(E^{\prime},Q^{\prime},P^{\prime})
to the triple
(\ell E,[\ell]Q,[\ell]P)
, which is equivalent to the triple
(E,[\ell]Q,[\ell]P)
. This implies that
\sim
is an equivalence relation.

We show that the number of equivalence classes of
\sim
is uniformly bounded in
n
. Let
r
be an integer such that
\ell^{r}\equiv 1\pmod{N}
; such an integer exists as
\ell
is coprime to
N
. We have
[\ell]^{r}Q=Q
. Then all vertices of the form
(E,Q,[\ell]^{rj}P)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, are in the same equivalence class. Let
U_{n}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
be the subgroup generated by
\ell^{r}
. Recall that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\cong\mu_{q}\times(1+2p\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, where
q=4
if
p=2
and
q=p-1
otherwise. Here,
\mu_{q}
denotes the group of
q
-th roots of unity. In particular, the index of
U_{n}
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is uniformly bounded by the index of
\overline{\langle\ell^{r}\rangle}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, which is finite since the image of
\ell^{r}
in
1+2p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is non-trivial.

By
[
KM85
, Lemma 12.2.3]
,
V^{n}
is a cyclic
p^{n}
-isogeny. In particular, the automorphism group of
\ker(V^{n})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, which acts transitively on the set of generators of
\ker(V^{n})
. As the index of
U_{n}
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})^{\times}
is uniformly bounded, we see that the number of equivalence classes under
\sim
is also uniformly bounded, which concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We conclude this section by showing that, in the ordinary case, we recover the isogeny graphs defined in
[
LM24
]
.

Definition 3.5
.

Define
H_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)
as the maximal subgraph of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)
whose vertices are of the form
(E,Q,P)
, where
E
is an ordinary elliptic curve.

Let
G_{N}^{m}
be the undirected graph defined in
[
LM24
, Definition 2.1]
. The set of vertices of
G_{N}^{m}
consists of equivalence classes of tuples
(E,P)
of ordinary elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
and geometric points
P
in
E(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
of order
Np^{m}
; as
N
is coprime to
p^{m}
, we can write a tuple
(E,P)
as
(E,Q+P^{\prime})
, where
Q
is of order
N
and
P^{\prime}
is of order
p^{m}
. The edges of
G_{N}^{m}
are given by
\ell
-isogenies. Clearly, we have the following isomorphism of graphs

H_{\ell}^{p}(0,N)\cong G_{N}^{0}.

Proposition 3.6
.

For each integer
n\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism of graphs
H_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)\cong G_{N}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
E/\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
be an ordinary elliptic curve. By
[
KM85
, Proposition 12.3.6]
,
V^{n}
is étale. Let
(E,Q+P)
be a vertex of
G_{N}^{n}
, where
P\in E[p^{n}]
and
Q\in E[N]
. Proposition 12.2.7 in op. cit. tells us that there exist an elliptic curve
E_{n}/\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
and an isomorphism
\psi\colon E\to E_{n}^{(p^{n})}
such that
\psi(P)
is a generator of
\ker(V_{E_{n}}^{n})
. Furthermore, since
N
is coprime to
p
,
V_{E_{n}}^{n}\circ\psi(Q)
is a point on
E_{n}
of order
N
. Therefore, we have a well-defined map

{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{V}(G_{N}^{n})\to\mathbb{V}(H_{\ell}^{p}(n,N)),\quad(E,Q+P)\mapsto(E_{n},V_{E_{n}}^{n}\circ\psi(Q),\psi(P)).

It is clear that
{\alpha}
is a bijection. Indeed, there is a one-to-one correspondence between pairs
(E,P)
and pairs
(E_{n},\psi(P))
. As
V^{n}_{E_{n}}
induces a group isomorphism
E^{(p^{n})}_{n}[N]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}E_{n}[N]
,
\alpha
admits an inverse given by

(E_{n},Q^{\prime},P^{\prime})\mapsto\left(E,\psi^{-1}\circ\left(V_{E_{n}}^{(p^{n})}\right)^{-1}(Q^{\prime}),\psi^{-1}(P^{\prime})\right).

It remains to show that
{\alpha}
preserves the edges of the two graphs. Let
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
be an
\ell
-isogeny, and let
\psi\colon E\to E_{n}^{(p^{n})}
and
\psi^{\prime}\colon E^{\prime}\to{E^{\prime}_{n}}^{(p^{n})}
be the isomorphisms given by
loc. cit.
. Then
\phi_{n}=\psi^{\prime}\circ\phi\circ\psi^{-1}\colon E^{(p^{n})}_{n}\to{E^{\prime}}^{(p^{n})}_{n}
is an
\ell
-isogeny, which in turn induces an
\ell
-isogeny from
E_{n}\to{E_{n}^{\prime}}
by Remark
2.1
. One can directly check that
{\alpha}
commutes with
\phi\mapsto\phi_{n}
, as required.
∎

4.
Voltage assignments and derived graphs

4.1.
General results on voltage assignments and derived graphs

We recall the notion of voltage assignments and their derived graphs.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a directed graph and
(G,\cdot)
a group. A
G
-valued
voltage assignment
on
X
is a function
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{E}(X)\rightarrow G
.

Given a
G
-valued voltage assignment on
X
, we define the derived graph
X(G,\alpha)
whose vertices and edges are given by
\mathbb{V}(X)\times G
and
\mathbb{E}(X)\times G
, respectively. If
e\in\mathbb{E}(X)
is an edge of
X
going from
s
to
t
, then the edge
(e,\sigma)\in\mathbb{E}(X(G,\alpha))=\mathbb{E}(X)\times G
goes from
(s,\sigma)
to
(t,\sigma\cdot\alpha(e))
.

If
X
is an undirected graph, we define
\tilde{X}
to be the directed graph, where
\mathbb{V}(X)=\mathbb{V}(\tilde{X})
and
\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X})
is obtained from replacing each edge in
\mathbb{E}(X)
by two edges with opposite directions. We say that
\alpha
is a voltage assignment on
X
if it is a voltage assignment on
\tilde{X}
that is compatible with the inversion of edges. We denote by
X(G,\alpha)
the undirected graph obtained from the directed graph
\tilde{X}(G,\alpha)
after identifying two edges with opposite directions with a single undirected edge.

The derived graph
X(G,\alpha)
is naturally a covering of
X
(via
(x,\sigma)\mapsto x
). The following theorem gives a criterion on this covering being Galois:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X
be a finite connected undirected graph,
G
a finite group, and
\alpha
a
G
-valued voltage assignment on
X
. If
X(G,\alpha)
is connected, then
X(G,\alpha)
is a Galois covering of
X
. Furthermore, its Galois group is isomorphic to
G
. Conversely, if
Y/X
is a Galois covering of finite connected graphs, then
Y
is isomorphic to the derived graph of a
\Gal(Y/X)
-valued voltage assigment on
X
.

Proof.

See
[
Gon22
, Theorem 2.10]
and
[
Gon21
, Theorem 8]
(note that the proof of this theorem is based on the work of Gross–Tucker in
[
GT87
, Chapter 2]
).
∎

Later in this paper, we shall employ the following generalization of Theorem
4.2
:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X
be a finite connected undirected graph and let
\alpha
be a voltage assignment on
X
with values in a finite abelian group
G
. Let
Z
be a connected component of
X(\alpha,G)
. Let
H
be the stabilizer of
Z
under the action of
G
on connected components. Then
Z
is isomorphic to
X(H,\alpha)
and
Z/X
is a Galois covering whose Galois group is isomorphic to
H
.

Proof.

The group
G
acts on
\mathbb{V}(X(\alpha,G))
via
h\cdot(v,g)=(v,hg)
. This induces an action of
G
on the set of connected components of
X(\alpha,G)
. Let
H
be the stabilizer of
Z
under this action.

Recall from
[
LM23
, Proposition 2.4i) and the proof of Corollary 2.5]
that
G
acts transitively on the connected components of
X(\alpha,G)
and the connected components are isomorphic to each other. This tells us that
X(\alpha,G)
has
|G|/|H|
connected components, and each vertex of
\mathbb{V}(X)
has
|H|
pre-images in
Z
. Since
H
stabilizes
Z
, for each
w\in\mathbb{V}(X)
, its pre-image in
Z
is given by
\{(w,g_{w}h)\mid h\in H\}
for some
g_{w}\in G
.

We define a voltage assignment

\beta\colon\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X})\to H,\quad e\mapsto\alpha(e)g_{w}g_{w^{\prime}}^{-1},

if
e
is an edge from
w
to
w^{\prime}
. There is a natural bijective map

\mathbb{V}(Z)\to\mathbb{V}(X(\beta_{n},H)),\quad(w,g_{w}h)\mapsto(w,h).

It remains to check that this map respects the edges. There is an edge from
(w,g_{w}h)
to
(w^{\prime},g_{w^{\prime}}h^{\prime})
in
Z
if and only if there is an edge
e
from
w
to
w^{\prime}
in
X
and
\alpha(e)g_{w}h=g_{w^{\prime}}h^{\prime}
. This is equivalent to
\beta(e)h=h^{\prime}
, which shows that there is a graph isomorphism

Z\cong X(\beta,H).

As
Z
is connected, it follows from Theorem
4.2
that
Z/X
is a Galois covering, whose Galois group is isomorphic to
H
.
∎

We recall the following definition of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers of graph coverings.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
X
be a finite connected undirected graph. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower
over
X
is a sequence of graph coverings

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\cdots

such that for each
n\geq 0
, the covering
X_{n}/X
is Galois, whose Galois group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
.

This coincides with the definition of "abelian
p
-towers" introduced in
[
Val21
,
MV23
,
MV24
]
. Let
\alpha
be
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued voltage assignment on a finite connected undirected graph
X
. For an integer
n\geq 1
, define
\alpha_{n}
as the composition

\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\to}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z},

where the second map is the natural projection map. A characterization for the connectedness of the derived graphs
X(\alpha_{n},\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
has been established in
[
DLRV24
, Theorem 2.15]
. When these graphs are connected, they form a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower over
X
by Theorem
4.2
.

The following lemma shows how the voltage assignment can be modified to shift the base graph of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower to an intermediate covering.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
X
be a finite connected undirected graph and let
\alpha\colon\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X})\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be a voltage assignment. For an integer
n\geq 0
, write
\alpha_{n}
for the voltage assignment

\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X})\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}

induced by
\alpha
, and write
X_{n}=X(\alpha_{n},\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
.

For a fixed
n\geq 0
, there is a voltage assignment
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}_{n})\to p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that for all integers
m\geq n
,
X_{m}
is isomorphic to the derived graph
X_{n}(\beta_{m},\mathbb{Z}/p^{m-n}\mathbb{Z})
, where
\beta_{m}
is defined by

{\beta_{m}\colon}\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\to}}p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{m-n}\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}_{n})
consists of tuples
(e,\sigma)
, where
\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
and
e\in\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X})
. Fix a set-theoretic section
\tau\colon\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of the projection
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
and denote
\tau(\sigma)
by
\tau_{\sigma}
. We define the voltage assignment
\beta
as follows

\beta\colon\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}_{n})=\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X})\times\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}\to p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p},\quad(e,\sigma)\mapsto\alpha(e)+\tau_{\sigma}-\tau_{\sigma^{\prime}},

where
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma+\alpha_{n}(e)
. The map
\beta
is well-defined because the image of
\alpha(e)+\tau_{\sigma}-\tau_{\sigma^{\prime}}
in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
is equal to
\alpha_{n}(e)+\sigma-{\sigma^{\prime}}=0
. Thus,
\alpha(e)+\tau_{\sigma}-\tau_{\sigma^{\prime}}
lies in
p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

For
m\geq n
, let
\beta_{m}
be the composition

{\beta_{m}\colon}\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\to}}p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to p^{n}\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}.

Let
H_{m}=p^{n}\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
and
G_{m}=\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
. Then
H_{m}
is a normal subgroup of
G_{m}
and
G_{m}/H_{m}\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\bar{\tau}_{\sigma}
denote the image of
\tau_{\sigma}
in
G_{m}
under the natural projection map. Then
\{\bar{\tau}_{\sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}\}
is a set of coset representations of
G_{m}/H_{m}
. In particular, every element
a\in G_{m}
can be expressed as
a=a-\bar{\tau}_{\sigma_{a}}+\bar{\tau}_{\sigma_{a}}
, where
\sigma_{a}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
is the image of
a
under the natural projection map and
a-\bar{\tau}_{\sigma_{a}}\in H_{m}
.

Recall that
\mathbb{V}(X_{m})=\mathbb{V}(X)\times G_{m}
. Define

\displaystyle{\varepsilon_{m}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{V}(X_{m})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{V}(X)\times\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}\times p^{n}\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{V}(X_{n})\times H_{m},

\displaystyle(v,a)

\displaystyle\mapsto(v,\sigma_{a},a-\bar{\tau}_{\sigma_{a}}),

where
v\in\mathbb{V}(X)
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
. Clearly,
{\varepsilon_{m}}
is a bijection. Furthermore, there is an edge in
\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}_{m})
from
(v,a)
to
(w,b)
if and only if there is an edge
e\in\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X})
from
v
to
w
and
b=a+\alpha_{m}(e)
, which means that

\sigma_{b}=\sigma_{a}+\alpha_{n}(e),\quad\beta_{m}((e,\sigma_{a}))=(b-\bar{\tau}_{\sigma_{b}})-(a-\bar{\tau}_{\sigma_{a}}).

Note that the first equation signifies that the edge
(e,\sigma_{a})\in\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}_{n})=\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X})\times\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
links
(v,\sigma_{a})
to
(w,\sigma_{b})
, whereas the second equation signifies that the edge
((e,\sigma_{a}),a-\overline{\tau}_{\sigma_{a}})
in
\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}_{n}(\beta_{m},H_{m}))
links
((v,\sigma_{a}),a-\bar{\tau}_{\sigma_{a}})
to
((w,\sigma_{b}),b-\bar{\tau}_{\sigma_{b}})
.
Thus,
X(\alpha_{m},\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})\cong X_{n}(\beta_{m},H_{m}).

∎

Remark 4.6
.

The proof of Lemma
4.5
may already be well-known to experts. In fact, it can be adapted for more general settings, replacing
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
by a general group
G
, and the subgroup
p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
by a normal subgroup
H
of
G
.

Inspired by our previous work
[
LM24
]
on isogeny graphs arising from ordinary elliptic curves, we prove how
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued voltage assignments give rise to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers without assuming connectivity. Instead, we assume that the number of connected components is uniformly bounded and show that we obtain a collection of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers. Note that if
Y/X
is a graph covering, the number of connected components of
Y
is larger than or equal to that of
X
. Thus, along a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\cdots,

the number of connected components of
X_{n}
is a non-decreasing function in
n
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
X
be a finite connected undirected graph, and let
\alpha
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued voltage assignment on
X
. Let
\alpha_{n}
be as before. Assume that there exists an integer
m_{0}
such that the number of connected components of
X_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=X(\alpha_{n},\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
is equal to that of
X_{m_{0}}
for all
n\geq m_{0}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{m_{0}}
be a connected component of
X_{m_{0}}
. For each
m\geq m_{0}
, there exists a unique subgraph
{\mathcal{G}}_{m}
of
X_{m}
such that

\mathcal{G}_{m_{0}}\longleftarrow\mathcal{G}_{m_{0}+1}\longleftarrow\mathcal{G}_{m_{0}+2}\longleftarrow\dots\longleftarrow\dots

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower.

Proof.

For each
m
, there exists at least one connected component
\mathcal{G}_{m}
that surjects onto
\mathcal{G}_{m_{0}}
. As the number of connected components in
X_{m}
is constant, there exists exactly one such connected component.
By Lemmas
4.3
and
4.5
, there is a voltage assignment
\beta
on
\mathcal{G}_{m_{0}}
with values in
p^{m_{0}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
\mathcal{G}_{m}=X(\beta_{m},p^{m_{0}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. As
\mathcal{G}_{m}
is connected, it follows that
\mathcal{G}_{m}/\mathcal{G}_{m_{0}}
is indeed Galois with
\Gal(\mathcal{G}_{m}/\mathcal{G}_{m_{0}})\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{m-m_{0}}{\mathbb{Z}}
by Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A

Recall that
\mathcal{X}
denotes a connected component of
G_{\ell}^{p}(0,1)
and that
\mathcal{X}(n,N)

whose vertices are of the form

where

\mathcal{X}
(see Lemma

). In light of Corollary
4.7
, in order to prove Theorem

, it suffices to show that there exists a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued voltage assignment
\alpha
on
\mathcal{X}(0,N)
such that
\mathcal{X}(n,N)
is the derived graph for the voltage assignment

given by
\alpha

. We do so by following the strategy of
[

, Appendix A]
.

For every elliptic curve

For each

where

For each equivalence class of isogenies between elliptic curves, we fix one representative once and for all. Let
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}

5.
Volcanic structures of isogeny graphs of oriented supersingular elliptic curves

Recall that an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
is said to be
supersingular
if
E(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{k}})
does not contain an element of order
p
.
Throughout this section, we assume that
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
and that
S=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
. Our goal is to study the volcanic structure of isogeny graphs arising from supersingular elliptic curves enhanced by orientations (first introduced in
[
CK20
]
) together with level structures. Unlike
[
LM23
]
where we studied oriented supersingular elliptic curves with
\Gamma(M)
-level structure for some positive integer

5.1.
Definitions

Throughout,
N
is a positive integer that is coprime to
p
and
\ell
as before.

Definition 5.1
.

Let

.

[\iota(K)\bigcap\textup{End}(E)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\iota(\mathcal{O})]

.

p
and
\ell
, and non-negative integers
N
such that
G
is isomorphic to a connected component of the crater of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{p}(0,N)
that arises from ordinary elliptic curves.

In this appendix, we extend the aforementioned theorem to craters of
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}^{p}(0,N)
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
G
be an abstract tectonic crater of parameters
(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{c})
. There exist infinitely many distinct prime numbers
\ell
and
p
, and non-negative integers
N
such that the crater of a connected component of
\mathfrak{G}_{\ell}^{p}(0,N)
is isomorphic to
G
.

Proof.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field and let
\mathbf{E}
be an elliptic curve defined over a finite abelian extension
K^{\prime}
with complex multiplication by the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of
K
and such that
K^{\prime}(\mathbf{E}_{\textup{tors}})/K
is an abelian extension. Such an extension exists following the discussion in
[
dS87
, §II.1.4]
. Let
L=K^{\prime}(\mathbf{E}[5])
and let
p
be a rational prime that is inert in
K/\mathbb{Q}
and unramified in
L/\mathbb{Q}
. We fix three distinct prime numbers
N^{\prime},M^{\prime},Q^{\prime}
coprime to
5p\cdot\textup{cond}(\mathbf{E})
(where
\textup{cond}(\mathbf{E})
is the conductor of
\mathbf{E}
) that are split in
K
and satisfy

•

N^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{s}}
,

•

M^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{t}}
,

•

Q^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{r}}
.

Note that these three conditions do not contradict the fact that
N^{\prime}
,
M^{\prime}
and
Q^{\prime}
should be split in
K
, which follows from the reciprocity law. The existence of these three primes follows from Dirichlet’s theorem on the infinitude of primes in arithmetic progression.

Let
\mathfrak{N}
(resp.
\mathfrak{M}
,
\mathfrak{Q}
) be prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
lying above
N^{\prime}
(resp.
M^{\prime}
,
Q^{\prime}
). Let
L_{1}=L(E[N^{\prime}M^{\prime}\mathfrak{Q}])
and
L_{2}=L(E[N^{\prime}M^{\prime}\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}])
. By definition, the ray class field
K((5))
lies in
L
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{O}^{{\times}}_{K}
embeds into
(\mathcal{O}_{{K}}/(5))^{\times}
. Thus,
\Gal(L_{1}/L)
is isomorphic to the subgroup of
(\mathcal{O}_{K}/(5N^{\prime}M^{\prime}\mathfrak{Q}))^{\times}
generated by elements that are congruent to
1
modulo
(5)
. Thus,
[
dS87
, Corollary II.1.7]
implies

\displaystyle\Gal(L_{1}/L)\cong\Gal(K(5N^{\prime}M^{\prime}\mathfrak{Q})/K(5))

(A.1)

\displaystyle\cong(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{N})^{\times}\times(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\overline{\mathfrak{N}})^{\times}\times(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{M})^{\times}\times(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\overline{\mathfrak{M}})^{\times}\times(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{Q})^{\times},

\displaystyle\Gal(L_{2}/L)\cong\Gal(K(5N^{\prime}M^{\prime}\overline{\mathfrak{Q}})/K(5))

(A.2)

\displaystyle\cong(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{N})^{\times}\times(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\overline{\mathfrak{N}})^{\times}\times(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{M})^{\times}\times(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\overline{\mathfrak{M}})^{\times}\times(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\overline{\mathfrak{Q}})^{\times}.

By Tchebotarev’s density theorem, there exist infinitely many prime ideals
\mathfrak{L}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with the following properties:

•

\mathfrak{L}
splits in
L/K
.

•

\mathfrak{L}\neq\overline{\mathfrak{L}}

•

The image of the Frobenius of
\mathfrak{L}
in
\Gal(L_{1}/L)
under the isomorphism (
A.1
) is of the form
(a,1,1,b,d)
with
\ord(a)=\mathfrak{s}
,
\ord(b)=\mathfrak{t}
and
\ord(d^{\mathfrak{s}})=\mathfrak{r}
.

•

The image of the Frobenius of
\mathfrak{L}
in
\Gal(L_{2}/L)
under the isomorphism (
A.2
) is of the form
(a,1,1,b,d^{\prime})
with
\ord({d^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{t}})=\mathfrak{r}
and
{d^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{t}}=d^{\mathfrak{s}}
.

Let
P
be a primitive
5\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{M}\mathfrak{Q}
-torsion point on
\mathbf{E}
. Let
\sigma
be the Frobenius of
\mathfrak{L}
in
\Gal(L_{1}/K)
and let
\tau
be the Frobenius of
\overline{\mathfrak{L}}
. Let
H_{1}
(resp.
H_{2}
) be the subgroup generated by
\sigma
(resp.
\tau
). It follows from
[
LM24
, proof of Theorem 5.2]
that the orbit of
P
under the action of
H_{1}
(resp.
H_{2}
) contains
\mathfrak{r}\mathfrak{s}
(resp.
\mathfrak{r}\mathfrak{t}
) elements. Furthermore, the intersection of the two orbits contains
\mathfrak{r}
elements and
\sigma^{\mathfrak{s}}(P)=\tau^{\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{t}}(P)
.

Note that
\mathbf{E}^{\prime}=\mathbf{E}/\mathbf{E}[\mathfrak{L}]
has complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
as well due to the fact that every endomorphism of
E
sends an
\mathfrak{L}
-torsion point to another
\mathfrak{L}
-torsion point. Indeed, every endomorphism of
\mathbf{E}
fixes the subgroup
\mathbf{E}[\mathfrak{L}]
and therefore defines an endomorphism of
\mathbf{E}^{\prime}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal above
p
in
L
. Then there is a horizontal isogeny

\mathbf{E}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}\to\mathbf{E}^{\prime}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}.

Thus, the ideals
\mathfrak{L}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{L}}
induce horizontal isogenies of
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{E}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Let
Q=P\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
and let
\iota
be the natural map

\textup{End}(\mathbf{E})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\to\textup{End}(E)\otimes\mathbb{Q}.

Let
\ell
be the rational prime below
\mathfrak{L}
. Let
\mathfrak{Y}
be the connected component of
\mathfrak{G}_{\ell}^{p}(0,N^{\prime}M^{\prime}Q^{\prime})
that contains
(E,\iota,Q)
. Then the crater of
\mathfrak{Y}
is an abstract tectonic crater with parameters
(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{c})
.
∎

Theorem A.2
.

Let
s
be a non-negative integer. Then there exist infinitely many distinct prime numbers
p
and
\ell
, and infinitely many non-negative integers
N
such that the crater of a connected component of
\mathfrak{G}_{\ell}^{p}(0,N)
is isomorphic to a directed cycle graph of length
s
.

Proof.

Let
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
be a prime number. Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime number distinct from
p
such that
-\ell
is not a square modulo
p
. Assume, in addition, that
\ell-1
has a prime factor different from
2
and
3
. The existence of such a prime follows from quadratic reciprocity, Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progression and the fact that the set of natural numbers whose only prime divisors are
2
and
3
has density zero. Put
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\ell})
. Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
coprime to
6
such that
\mathfrak{f}|(\sqrt{-\ell}-1)
. Let
\mathbf{E}
be an elliptic curve defined over
L=K(\mathfrak{f})
(the ray class field of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
) that has good reduction outside
\mathfrak{f}
and complex multiplication by
{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
(see for example
[
dS87
, Chapter II]
).

By class field theory, the ideal
(\sqrt{-\ell})
splits completely in
L/K
. Pick a prime ideal
\mathfrak{N}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
\ord(\sqrt{-\ell})
in
(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{N})^{\times}
is
s
. Let
P
be a primitive
\mathfrak{Nf}
-torsion point on
\mathbf{E}
. Let
H
be the subgroup generated by the Frobenius of
(\sqrt{-\ell})
in
\Gal(L(E[\mathfrak{N}])/K)
. Then the orbit of
P
under the action
H
is given by
\left\{[\sqrt{-\ell}]^{n}P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
, so it consists of
s
elements. The remainder of the proof is analogous to that of Theorem
A.1
.
∎

Remark A.3
.

The reason why we only consider the crater is that the edges between vertices of different depths do not necessarily satisfy a simple pattern, as is illustrated by the examples given in §
5.1
. Note that we have also removed the level structure arising from the Verschiebung map from consideration. This is because our method relies on working with elliptic curves defined over fields of characteristic zero, for which the Verschiebung map does not apply.

Furthermore, note that the authors in
[
BCP22
]
were able to solve an inverse problem for an arbitrary volcano graph, and not just for craters. After fixing a volcano graph, the prime
\ell
is uniquely determined (by the degree of the vertices). One might ask whether it would be possible to improve Theorems
A.1
and
A.2
to prove that for any given
\ell
(resp.
p
), there are infinitely many
p
(resp.
\ell
) such that the conclusions of the theorems hold. To do so, we would need to find an imaginary quadratic field
K
and integers
N^{\prime},M^{\prime},Q
that satisfy all the conditions used in the proof of Theorem
A.1
. However, our method does not work in such generality.

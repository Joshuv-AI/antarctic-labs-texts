Conjugacy classes of linear actions in the plane Cremona group
Ivan Cheltsov, Yuri Tschinkel, Zhijia Zhang

Abstract
. We classify regular generically free actions of finite groups on the projective plane, up to conjugation in the Cremona group.



1.
Introduction

One of the most intensely studied objects in algebraic geometry is the plane Cremona group

\mathrm{Cr}_{2}=\mathrm{Bir}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}),

the group of birational automorphisms of the plane.
Recall that, over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero,

\mathrm{Cr}_{2}:=\langle\mathsf{PGL}_{3},\iota\rangle,

where

(1.1)

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\iota:&{\mathbb{P}}^{2}&\dashrightarrow&{\mathbb{P}}^{2},\\
&(x,y,z)&\mapsto&\left(\frac{1}{x},\frac{1}{y},\frac{1}{z}\right)\end{array}

is the standard Cremona involution.
Conjugacy classes of finite subgroups of
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
have been described in
[
Bli18
]
: there are three types, recalled in Section
3
:

•

intransitive,

•

transitive but imprimitive,

•

primitive.

There exist isomorphic non-conjugated
finite subgroups
of
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
which are conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
, see Sections
3
and
6
. However, finite subgroups of different types are not conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
.

A century later, in the seminal paper by Dolgachev and Iskovskikh
[
DI09
]
,
came the classification of
finite subgroups of
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
, i.e., finite groups
that can act regularly and generically freely on rational surfaces. However, the classification
of
subgroups
as well as of
actions
,
up to conjugation in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
, remained an open problem.
For finite subgroups of
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
, this was explicitly asked in
[
DI09
, Section 9]
:

Find the conjugacy classes in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
of actions of finite subgroups of
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
. For example, there are two actions of the subgroup of
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
and four actions of
{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}
which differ by outer automorphisms of the groups. Are they conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
?

Since then, it was shown in
[
Che14
]
that the two
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
-actions are conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
, while the four
{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}
-actions are not. A similar problem for the remaining primitive actions follows from
[
Sak19
]
, see Section
4
for an explicit solution.
Furthermore, conjugacy classes of actions of intransitive
abelian
subgroups of
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
have been described in
[
RY02
]
; in particular, actions of finite cyclic subgroups are always conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
.

In this paper, we completely settle the Dolgachev–Iskovskikh problem for the remaining finite subgroups of
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
. Our main new tool to distinguish the actions up to conjugation in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
is the formalism of Burnside invariants, introduced in
[
KT22a
]
and recalled in Section
2
. These fail to distinguish primitive actions
[
TYZ24
, Proposition 7.1 and Example 7.2]
, and are inconclusive for some imprimitive actions
[
TYZ24
]
, but those are accessible via the equivariant Sarkisov program. However, the new invariants are decisive in the case of intransitive actions. One of the main results of this paper, proved in Section
6
, is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Nonabelian intransitive actions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:G\hookrightarrow\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
are conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
if and only if

[{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\righttoleftarrow\phi_{1}(G)]=[{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\righttoleftarrow\phi_{2}(G)]\in\mathrm{Burn}_{2}^{\mathrm{inc}}(G).

We also obtain an explicit geometric description of this condition, see Theorem
6.1
.

Our second main result, proved in Section
5
, is:

Theorem 1.2
.

Distinct imprimitive actions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:G\hookrightarrow\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
are conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
if and only if they are conjugated by the standard Cremona involution.

Together with existing results concerning primitive actions, this settles the Dolgachev–Iskovskikh problem, quoted above
[
DI09
, Section 9]
.
The proof proceeds via classification of groups and actions, followed by a detailed analysis of equivariant Sarkisov links.
As a byproduct, we compute the normalizer
of
G
in the Cremona group in many cases, and answer a question posed by L. Katzarkov:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G\subset\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
be a finite subgroup.
Then the normalizer of
G
in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}

is finite if and only if
G
is transitive
and not isomorphic to any of the following groups:

\mathfrak{A}_{4},\quad C_{3}^{2},\quad C_{3}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{3},\quad C_{7}\rtimes C_{3},\quad C_{3}^{2}\rtimes C_{4}.

Recall that
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
is generated by involutions, over any perfect field
[
LS24
]
. Thus, it is reasonable to expect that the normalizer in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
of any finite subgroup
G\subset\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
is also generated by involutions. However, this is not always the case: if
G\subset\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
is isomorphic to the
Hessian group
, then
G
is unique in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
, up to conjugation
[
DI09
]
, coincides with its normalizer in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
by
[
Sak19
]
, but is not generated by reflections. One can check that normalizers of other transitive subgroups in
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
are generated by reflection.

Keeping in mind results in
[
CLdC13
,
Lon16
,
Zim18
]
, one could ask: When is the normalizer in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
of a finite subgroup
G\subset\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
simple? Such
G
would have to be simple and coincide with their normalizer. By
[
Che14
]
, there are exactly two such nonabelian finite subgroups in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
(up to conjugation), both contained in
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
, namely,
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
and
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7})
. Using the classification of elements of prime order in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
, completed in
[
BB00
,
dF04
,
BB04
,
Bla07
]
, one can also show that a prime order cyclic subgroup
G\subset\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
coincides with its normalizer if and only if
G
is generated by a Geiser or Bertini involution, and the fixed curve of the involution does not admit nontrivial automorphisms.
Such subgroups are conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
if and only if the fixed curves of their generating involutions are isomorphic.

2.
Basic notions in equivariant geometry

Automorphisms and birational automorphisms

Throughout,
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
Let
X
be a smooth projective irreducible variety over
k
and

\mathrm{Aut}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{Bir}(X),

the group of regular, respectively, birational, automorphisms of
X
.
Let
G
be a finite group admitting an embedding

\phi:G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(X),

we refer to this as a choice of a
G
-action on
X
, and call
X
a
G
-variety.
For
G\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, let

\mathrm{Aut}^{G}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{Bir}^{G}(X),

be the normalizers of
G
in the respective groups. This group-theoretic definition has the following geometric interpretation:
\mathrm{Aut}^{G}(X)
is the group of all
G
-
biregular
self-maps of
X
, i.e., all all
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(X)
such that there exists a

\psi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
and a commutative diagram

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi(g)}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{X}

for all
g\in G
. Similarly,
\mathrm{Bir}^{G}(X)
is the group of all
G
-
birational

self-maps, defined by the same diagram, with
\varphi\in\mathrm{Bir}(X)
. There are natural homomorphisms

(2.1)

\textstyle{\mathrm{Aut}^{G}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Bir}^{G}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Aut}(G)}

and we denote the kernels, i.e., centralizers of the respective groups, by

\mathrm{ZAut}^{G}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{ZBir}^{G}(X).

A birational map
X\dashrightarrow Y
between
G
-varieties is called

•

G
-equivariant, if it commutes with the action of
G
,

•

G
-birational, if it commutes with the action of
G
, up to a fixed automorphism
\psi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
.

Conjugacy of groups

Given a
G
-variety
X
and embeddings

\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(X)

one has the following general

Problem A
:
When are
\phi_{1}(G)
and
\phi_{2}(G)

conjugated in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
?

Problem B
:
When are
\phi_{1}(G)
and
\phi_{2}(G)
conjugated
in
\mathrm{Bir}(X)
?

In the first case, this means that there is a
G
-biregular self-map
\varphi:X\to X
conjugating
\phi_{1}(G)
and
\phi_{2}(G)
.
In the second case,
\varphi
is
G
-birational.

Conjugacy of actions

Consider actions

\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(X)
on a
G
-variety
X
as above. They are conjugated in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
if there exists a
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(X)
such that

\phi_{2}=\varphi\circ\phi_{1}\circ\varphi^{-1}.

In this case, we don’t distinguish these two actions! In the following, when we refer to action, we always mean up to conjugation in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
.

Clearly, if
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
give the same action on
X
, then the groups
\phi_{1}(G)
and
\phi_{2}(G)
are conjugated in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
.
However, the converse does not always hold.

Problem AA
:
Assuming that the groups
\phi_{1}(G)
and
\phi_{2}(G)

are conjugated in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, when are the actions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
the same?

We can consider a similar problem in
\mathrm{Bir}(X)
: we say that the actions
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are conjugated in
\mathrm{Bir}(X)
if there exists a

\varphi\in\mathrm{Bir}(X)
such that

\phi_{2}=\varphi\circ\phi_{1}\circ\varphi^{-1}.

As before, if
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are conjugated in
\mathrm{Bir}(X)
, then the groups
\phi_{1}(G)
and
\phi_{2}(G)
are conjugated in
\mathrm{Bir}(X)
.

Problem BA
:
Assuming that the groups
\phi_{1}(G)
and
\phi_{2}(G)

are conjugated in
\mathrm{Bir}(X)
, when are the actions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
conjugated in
\mathrm{Bir}(X)
?

To solve these problems, we can precompose one of the actions with a suitable
G
-biregular self-map
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, respectively, with a
G
-birational map

\varphi\in\mathrm{Bir}(X)
,
to obtain

\phi_{1}(G)=\phi_{2}(G),

as subgroups of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
.
Then
\phi_{2}=\phi_{1}\circ\psi
, for some
\psi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
, and the actions are conjugated in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
if and only if
\psi
lies in the image of
the homomorphism
\alpha
from diagram (
2.1
).
We have a diagram

\textstyle{\phi_{1}(G)=\phi_{2}(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Aut}^{G}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Bir}^{G}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Inn}(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Aut}(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Out}(G),}

where

\mathrm{Inn}(G)
and
\mathrm{Out}(G)
are inner and outer automorphisms of
G
.
Clearly, if
\psi\in\mathrm{Inn}(G)
, the actions are conjugated in
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
.
Therefore, Problem AA reduces to

Problem IA
:
Determine the image of

\bar{\alpha}:\mathrm{Aut}^{G}(X)\to\mathrm{Out}(G).

Similarly, Problem BA reduces to

Problem IB
:
Determine the image of

\bar{\beta}:\mathrm{Bir}^{G}(X)\to\mathrm{Out}(G).

Using this terminology, we can compute the number of
G
-actions with a fixed image in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
as

\frac{|\mathrm{Out}(G)|}{|\mathrm{image}(\bar{\alpha})|}.

Similarly, the number of actions with fixed image in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, up to conjugation in
\mathrm{Bir}(X)
, is given by

\frac{|\mathrm{Out}(G)|}{|\mathrm{image}(\bar{\beta})|}.

Problem IA is purely group-theoretical, and its solution can be automated with computer algebra packages such as
Magma
. On the other hand, Problem IB is of birational nature. If we knew the generators of
\mathrm{Bir}^{G}(X)
then we could compute the image of
\bar{\beta}
. In practice, this is rarely feasible.
However, in some situations, the solution is easy, for example, when

•

\mathrm{Out}(G)
is trivial, e.g.,
G={\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, with
n\neq 6
,

•

\mathrm{Aut}(X)=\mathrm{Bir}(X)
, so that Problems IA and IB coincide, e.g., if
X
is a curve or if the canonical class
K_{X}
is ample,

•

\mathrm{Aut}^{G}(X)=\mathrm{Bir}^{G}(X)
,

•

one can identify sufficiently many elements in
\mathrm{Bir}^{G}(X)
to prove the surjectivity of
\bar{\beta}
.

Example 2.1
.

There is a unique
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}\subset\mathsf{PGL}_{3}\simeq\mathrm{Aut}({\mathbb{P}}^{2})
,
up to conjugation. We have
\mathrm{Out}({\mathfrak{A}}_{5})\simeq C_{2}
and

\mathrm{Aut}^{{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2})\simeq{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}.

As there are no nontrivial homomorphisms
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}\to C_{2}
, we conclude that
there are two
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
-actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. However,

\mathrm{Bir}^{{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2})\simeq{\mathfrak{S}}_{5},

and the homomorphism

\bar{\beta}
is surjective
[
Che14
]
.
It follows that both
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
-actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
are conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
; a realization of this conjugation is in

[
CS16
, Lemma 6.3.3]
, see also Example
2.6
.

Additional examples with surjective
\bar{\beta}

will be given in Section
3
.

Let
X
be a smooth rational
G
-surface. The equivariant Minimal Model Program implies that
X
is
G
-birational to a
G
-surface

•

S
is a smooth del Pezzo surface with
\mathrm{rk}\mathrm{Pic}(S)^{G}=1
, or

•

Remark 2.3
.

Let
S
be a smooth del Pezzo

-surface of degree
\geqslant 2
such that
\mathrm{rk}\mathrm{Pic}(S)^{G}=1
.
Suppose that there exists a
G
-Sarkisov link

\textstyle{S}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\tilde{S}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\sigma^{\prime}}
\textstyle{S^{\prime}}

such that
\sigma
is a blowup of a
G

\Sigma

is a (smooth) del Pezzo

\mathrm{rk}\mathrm{Pic}(S)^{G}=1
,

\sigma^{\prime}

Then

is a smooth del Pezzo surface of degree

, and

.

\sigma^{\prime}\circ\beta=\gamma

\gamma\in\mathrm{Bir}^{G}(S)
, and its image
\beta(\gamma)\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
is trivial, because
\gamma
centralizes
G
.
In the following, we will say that
\gamma
is a Bertini or Geiser (birational) involution of the surface
S
, respectively.

A more detailed description of two-dimensional
G
-Sarkisov links is in
[

.
For instance, if
S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a

-conic bundle with

,

•

, then

is not

-birational to a del Pezzo surface, see

, Section 8]
,

•

if
(-K_{S})^{2}=3,5,6
, then
S
is a del Pezzo surface,

•

if
(-K_{S})^{2}=8
, then
S
is
G
-birational to a del Pezzo surface.

If
(-K_{S})^{2}=4
, there are examples such that
S
is a del Pezzo surface, and there are examples such that
S
is not
G
-birational to a smooth del Pezzo surface, see
[
DI09
, Section 8]
. It was an open question, posed in
[

, Section 9]
whether or not
S
is
G
-birational to a smooth del Pezzo surface when

.
Even though this is not directly related to the main topic of this paper, we include an example that answers this question:

Example 2.4
.

Let
\bar{S}\subset{\mathbb{P}}(1_{x},1_{y},3_{z},3_{w})

let
\eta\colon S\to\bar{S}
be the minimal resolution of singularities,
and let
G

Burnside formalism

This formalism, introduced in
[
KT22a
]
and applied in, e.g.,
[
HKT21
]
,
[
KT22b
]
,
[
TYZ24
]
, allows to distinguish birational types of actions of finite groups in many new situations. It takes into account information from
the
stabilizer stratification
on a
standard
birational model for the action. This is a model
X
such that

•

there is a Zariski open
U\subset X
on which the
G
-action is free,

•

the complement
X\setminus U
is a normal crossings divisor such that for each irreducible component
D_{\alpha}
in
X\setminus U=\cup_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}}D_{\alpha}
, and all
g\in G
, we have that

g(D_{\alpha})=D_{\alpha}\quad\text{ or }\quad g(D_{\alpha})\cap D_{\alpha}=\emptyset.

On such a model, generic stabilizers of all subvarieties are abelian.

We describe the simplest version of the general theory in the case of surfaces: given a standard model as above, a
G
-orbit of irreducible components of
X\setminus U
, and a choice of a representative
D_{\alpha}
of this orbit, we have a symbol

(H_{\alpha},Z_{\alpha}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k(D_{\alpha}),(b_{\alpha})),

where

•

H_{\alpha}
is the (cyclic) generic stabilizer of
D_{\alpha}
,

•

Z_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathrm{Z}_{G}(H_{\alpha})/H_{\alpha}
, a subgroup of the centralizer modulo stabilizer, acting generically freely on
D_{\alpha}
,
and

•

b_{\alpha}\in H_{\alpha}^{\vee}
is the weight of
H_{\alpha}
in the normal bundle of
D_{\alpha}
.

Such symbols are considered up to
G
-conjugation.

A symbol is called
incompressible
if
D_{\alpha}
is a curve of genus
\geq 1
, or if
Z_{\alpha}
is not cyclic.
The class

[X\righttoleftarrow G]:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}/G}(H_{\alpha},Z_{\alpha}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k(D_{\alpha}),(b_{\alpha}))

in the free abelian group

\mathrm{Burn}_{2}^{\mathrm{inc}}(G),

spanned by incompressible symbols (up to conjugation), is an equivariant birational invariant of the
G
-action.

In our application to Theorem
6.1
, we have
D_{\alpha}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, and
Z_{\alpha}
is one of the

{\mathfrak{D}}_{n},\quad{\mathfrak{A}}_{4},\quad{\mathfrak{S}}_{4},\quad{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}.

The following example from
[
KT22b
, Section 10]
and
[
TYZ24
, Section 7]
illustrates this.

Example 2.7
.

For
t\geq 2
and odd
n\geq 5
, consider

G=C_{t}\times\mathfrak{D}_{n}
. Let
\chi
be a primitive character of
C_{t}
and
\psi
a primitive character of
C_{n}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{n}
. Let
V_{\psi}
be the 2-dimensional faithful representation of
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
induced from
\psi
, and
V_{\chi,\psi}=\chi\otimes V_{\psi}
. Then
G
acts generically freely on

{\mathbb{P}}^{2}={\mathbb{P}}(V)={\mathbb{P}}(\mathbf{1}\oplus V_{\chi,\psi}).

The class

[{\mathbb{P}}(V)\righttoleftarrow G]\in\mathrm{Burn}_{2}^{\mathrm{inc}}(G)

equals

(C_{t},\mathfrak{D}_{n}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k({\mathbb{P}}^{1}),(\chi))+(C_{t},\mathfrak{D}_{n}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k({\mathbb{P}}^{1}),(-\chi)).

In particular, if
\chi\neq\pm\chi^{\prime}
or

, the corresponding actions on
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
and

are not birational.

3.
Classification of finite subgroups in
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}

Finite subgroups
G\subset\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
have been classified by Blichfeldt
[
Bli18
]
. These arise via projectivizations
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
of
faithful 3-dimensional representations
V
of central extensions
\tilde{G}
of
G
.
We recall the relevant terminology: a
G
-action
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}={\mathbb{P}}(V)
is called:

(I)

intransitive
: if
G
fixes a point in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
;

(T)

transitive but imprimitive
: if
G
does not fix a point in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
but there exists a
G
-orbit of length
3
;

(P)

primitive
: neither of the above.

The following groups give rise to primitive actions:

{\mathfrak{A}}_{5},\quad{\mathfrak{A}}_{6},\quad\mathsf{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{7}),\quad\mathsf{ASL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{3}),\quad\mathsf{PSU}_{3}({\mathbb{F}}_{2}),\quad C_{3}^{2}\rtimes C_{4}.

Each of these is unique in
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
, up to conjugation. Explicit generators are given in
[
YY93
]
.
The group
\mathsf{ASL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{3})
is known as the
Hessian group
—
after a suitable coordinate change, it leaves invariant the Hesse pencil:

x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}+\lambda xyz=0,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{P}^{1},

in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{x,y,z}
. The action on the curves of this pencil gives an exact sequence of groups

1\to C_{3}^{2}\rtimes C_{2}\to\mathsf{ASL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{3})\to\mathfrak{A}_{4}\to 1,

where
C_{3}^{2}\rtimes C_{2}\subset\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
is the subgroup that leaves invariant a general curve in the pencil,
and
C_{3}^{2}\subset C_{3}^{2}\rtimes C_{2}
acts on a general elliptic curve in the pencil via translations by
3
-torsions.
This subgroup is transitive but not primitive, and
\mathsf{ASL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{3})
can also be defined as its normalizer in
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
.
Up to conjugation, the three primitive subgroups
C_{3}^{2}\rtimes C_{4}
,
\mathsf{PSU}_{3}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
,
\mathsf{ASL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{3})
are nested as follows:

C_{3}^{2}\rtimes C_{4}\subset\mathsf{PSU}_{3}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})\subset\mathsf{ASL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{3}).

We describe groups giving rise to actions of type
(T)
. In this case,
G
has an orbit of length 3, consisting of points in linear general position. Changing coordinates, we may assume that these points are

[1:0,0],\quad[0:1:0],\quad[0:0:1].

The
G
-action on these points gives rise to the exact sequence

1\to T\to G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathfrak{S}}_{3},

which turns out to be split. The kernel of
\nu
consists of diagonal automorphisms.
The image is either
C_{3}
or
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
DI09
, Theorem 4.7]
)
.

Let

G\subset\mathsf{PGL}_{3}\simeq\mathrm{Aut}({\mathbb{P}}^{2})
be a finite subgroup giving rise to an action of type
(T)
. Then,
up to conjugation in
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
, one of the following holds:

•

G\simeq C_{n}^{2}\rtimes C_{3}
is generated by

\mathrm{diag}(\zeta_{n},1,1),\quad\sigma_{123}.

•

G\simeq C_{n}^{2}\rtimes{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
is generated by

\mathrm{diag}(\zeta_{n},1,1),\quad\sigma_{123},\quad\sigma_{12},

•

G\simeq(C_{n}\times C_{n/r})\rtimes C_{3}
is generated by

\mathrm{diag}(\zeta_{n}^{r},1,1),\quad\mathrm{diag}(\zeta_{n}^{s},\zeta_{n},1),\quad\sigma_{123},

where
r\geq 1
,
r\mid n
, and
s^{2}-s+1\equiv 0\pmod{r}
,

•

G\simeq(C_{n}\times C_{n/3})\rtimes{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
is generated by

\mathrm{diag}(\zeta_{n}^{3},1,1),\quad\mathrm{diag}(\zeta_{n}^{2},\zeta_{n},1),\quad\sigma_{123},\quad\sigma_{12},

with
3\mid n
,

where

\sigma_{123}:(x,y,z)\to(y,z,x),\quad\sigma_{12}:(x,y,z)\to(y,x,z),

on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{x,y,z}
. Each of these subgroups is unique up to conjugation in
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
.

Proof.

The classification is achieved in
[
DI09
]
. To show the uniqueness of each subgroup in
\mathsf{PGL}_{3}
, it suffices to show that the two choices of
s
give rise to the same subgroup in the third case
G\simeq(C_{n}\times C_{n/r})\rtimes C_{3}

Let

be groups corresponding to different choices of roots

s_{1},s_{2}
, for fixed
n,r
, in the third case. Since

, we have

\sigma_{12}\cdot\mathrm{diag}(\zeta_{n}^{s_{1}},\zeta_{n},1)\cdot\sigma_{12}=\mathrm{diag}(\zeta_{n}^{s_{1}},1,\zeta_{n}).

Up to multiplying by powers of
\mathrm{diag}(\zeta_{n}^{r},1,1)
, we have

\mathrm{diag}(\zeta_{n}^{s_{2}},\zeta_{n},1)\in\sigma_{12}G_{1}\sigma_{12}.

It follows that

\sigma_{12}\cdot G_{1}\cdot\sigma_{12}=G_{2}.

∎

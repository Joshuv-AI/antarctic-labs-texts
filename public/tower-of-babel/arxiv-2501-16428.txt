Quantum geometric bounds in spinful systems with trivial band topology
Wojciech J. Jankowski, Robert-Jan Slager, Gunnar F. Lange

Abstract
We derive quantum geometric bounds in spinful systems with spin topology characterized by a single ℤ \mathbb{Z} index protected by a spin gap. Our bounds provide geometric conditions on the spin topology, distinct from the known quantum geometric bounds associated with Wilson loops and nontrivial band topologies. As a result, we obtain broader bounds in time-reversal symmetric systems with a nontrivial ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} index and also bounds in systems with a trivial ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} index, where the quantum metric should be otherwise unbounded. We benchmark these findings with first-principles calculations in elemental bismuth realizing a nontrivial even spin-Chern number. Moreover, we connect these bounds to optical responses and show their robustness in the presence of disorder within a real space marker formulation, demonstrating that spin-resolved quantum geometry is observable in realistic experimental settings of impure materials. Finally, we connect spin bounds to quantum Cramér-Rao bounds that are central to quantum metrology, showing that elemental Bi and other spin-topological phases hold promises for topological free fermion quantum sensors.

Spin topology.
—
We consider 2D spinful time-reversal symmetric (
\mathcal{T}^{2}=-1
), periodic systems described by Bloch Hamiltonians
H(\bm{k})
. In a tight-binding basis with spin orbitals
|\alpha\rangle\otimes|\sigma\rangle
, such Hamiltonians can be written as

H(\bm{k})=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{N_{\mathrm{orb}}}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}=\uparrow\downarrow}H_{\alpha\sigma,\beta\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{k})[|\alpha\rangle\otimes|\sigma\rangle][\langle\beta|\otimes\langle\sigma^{\prime}|].

(1)

The spin operator along
\hat{n}
associated with this basis is then given by
S_{\hat{n}}=1_{\mathrm{orb}}\otimes\hat{n}\cdot{\bf\sigma}
. If
[H(\bm{k}),S_{\hat{n}}]=0
, then the eigenstates of
H(\bm{k})
are spin eigenstates, and can be labeled as
|\tilde{u}_{n\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle
, where
n\in\{1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{orb}}\}
. When this can be done for all
\bm{k}
(i.e., there is no entanglement between spin and orbit degrees of freedom), one can define the multiband spin-Berry curvature:

\Omega_{ij,nm}^{\sigma}(\bm{k})=i[\langle\partial_{k_{i}}\tilde{u}_{n\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{k_{j}}\tilde{u}_{m\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle-\mathrm{c.c}].

(2)

Tracing over the occupied bands with spin
\sigma
,
{N_{\mathrm{occ}}^{\sigma}=N_{\mathrm{occ}}/2}
, this will be quantized when integrated over a 2D Brillouin zone (BZ), giving rise to the spin-resolved Chern numbers
{C^{\sigma}_{s}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\text{BZ}}\differential\textbf{k}~\text{Tr}~\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy}\in\mathbb{Z}}
, with the overall spin-Chern number
C_{s}=\frac{1}{2}(C_{s}^{\uparrow}-C_{s}^{\downarrow})
. Time-reversal symmetry ensures that the Chern numbers of the opposite spin bands satisfy:
{C_{s}^{\uparrow}=-C_{s}^{\downarrow}}

[
45
,
2
]
. When spin is not conserved, one can instead formulate the equivalent quantities in terms of the eigenstates
\ket{u_{n\kv}^{\sigma}}
of the
projected
spin operator
S_{P}^{z}=PS_{z}P
[see Fig.
1
(b)], where
P
is the projector onto the occupied bands. These eigenstates will not generically be energy eigenstates when spin is not a good quantum number, but
P=P_{\uparrow}+P_{\downarrow}
, where
P_{\sigma}=\sum_{n}^{N_{\text{occ}}^{\sigma}}\ket{u_{n\kv}^{\sigma}}\bra{u_{n\kv}^{\sigma}}
projects onto the top and bottom
N^{\sigma}_{\text{occ}}
states respectively; for a review of projected spin operators see Ref.
[
1
]
and Sec. I of the Supplemental Material
[
46
]
. As we show in the following, formulating the spin-Berry curvature
\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy}
in terms of projected spin eigenstates
\ket{u_{n\kv}^{\sigma}}
provides a local bound on the quantum metric, which holds at every point of momentum space, even when spin is not a good quantum number.

Spin-resolved quantum geometry.
—
We now define the spin-resolved quantum geometry, which is fully characterized by a spin-resolved quantum geometric tensor (QGT), termed spin-QGT:

Q^{\sigma}_{ij}\equiv\sum^{N^{\sigma}_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\bra{\partial_{k_i} u^\sigma_{n\kv}}1-P_{\sigma}\ket{\partial_{k_j} u^\sigma_{n\kv}}=g^{\sigma}_{ij}-\frac{i}{2}\Omega^{\sigma}_{ij},

(3)

with
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
, and spin-resolved quantum metric (spin metric)
g^{\sigma}_{ij}
. The spin-QGT definitionally satisfies a positive semidefiniteness condition
\text{det}~Q^{\sigma}_{ij}>0
, and as a consequence, we derive the spin-geometric bound (see Sec. II of the Supplemental Material
[
46
]
):

\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}~g^{\sigma}_{ij}\geq\sqrt{\text{det}~g^{\sigma}_{ij}}\geq\frac{|\text{Tr}~\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy}|}{2}.

(4)

When spin-
z
is a good quantum number, we further show that the quantum metric
g_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}~[\partial_{k_{i}}P\partial_{k_{j}}P]=g^{\uparrow}_{ij}+g^{\downarrow}_{ij}

[
4
]
(see Sec. III of the Supplemental Material
[
46
]
). On combining with the spin-resolved bounds and integrating, one obtains
[
14
]
:

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\text{BZ}}~\differential^{2}{\bf k}~(g_{xx}+g_{yy})\geq|C^{\uparrow}_{s}|+|C^{\downarrow}_{s}|\equiv S_{\lambda=0}\in\mathbb{Z}.

(5)

We next focus on the case where spin-
z
is no longer a good quantum number, i.e.,
[H,S_{z}]\neq 0
, but the spin gap is preserved
[
1
,
48
]
. In this case, utilizing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (see Sec. III of the Supplemental Material
[
46
]
) allows us to derive a fundamental bound with a suppression by the number of occupied bands,
N_{\text{occ}}
, due to the spin-orbit entanglement (see Sec. IV of the Supplemental Material
[
46
]
),

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\text{BZ}}~\differential^{2}{\bf k}~(g_{xx}+g_{yy})\geq\frac{|C^{\uparrow}_{s}|+|C^{\downarrow}_{s}|}{N_{\text{occ}}}\equiv S_{\lambda\neq 0}\in\mathbb{Q},

(6)

where on the right, we have a rational number
S_{\lambda\neq 0}
. Intuitively, the suppression by the factor
N_{\text{occ}}
arises due to the mixing of all occupied bands with spin-orbit coupling (SOC), while preserving the winding of the spin-Wilson loop realized by the
PS_{z}P
eigenstates. For odd spin-Chern numbers, our bound is consistent with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
bound of Ref.
[
25
]
, yet realizes a possible generalization to tighter inequalities for higher odd spin-Chern numbers, e.g.,
|C_{s}|=3
, depending on the ratio of
|C_{s}|
and
N_{\mathrm{occ}}
. Furthermore, our bound provides for a more general fundamental quantum geometric condition, even if the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-invariant is trivial, i.e., when
C^{\sigma}_{s}\equiv 0~\text{mod}~2
. Namely, for even spin-Chern numbers Eq. (
6
) is the first topological bound due to spin topology, yet accompanied by trivial band topology. We further detail direct consequences of the spin-geometric bounds, which we further demonstrate to apply to spin-topological materials such as ultrathin Bi.

Spin-resolved geometry in optics.
— We proceed to show that as a direct physical consequence, the spin-resolved quantum geometry has direct manifestations in optical responses of materials with nontrivial spin-resolved topology, such as bismuth central to this work. The integral of the quantum metric amounts to the optical weight due to the optical conductivities
\sigma_{xx}(\omega)
,
\sigma_{yy}(\omega)
at frequencies
\omega

[
12

\int^{\infty}_{0}~\differential\omega~\frac{\text{Re}[\sigma_{xx}(\omega)+\sigma_{yy}(\omega)]}{\omega}\geq\frac{e^{2}}{2\hbar}S_{\lambda}.

(7)

We further retrieve an optical bound on the gap of spin-topological systems (see Sec. V of the Supplemental Material

46

E_{g}\leq\frac{2\pi n\hbar^{2}}{mS_{\lambda}},

(8)

where
n
is electron charge density,
m

E_{g}
is the energy band gap. Our bounds hold also in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-trivial systems, i.e., where the Kane-Mele invariant is vanishing

Spin-resolved quantum metrology.
— We now discuss how the spin-resolved quantum geometry can be utilized for quantum metrology
[
23
]
, providing for a set of applications within quantum sensing
[
22
]
, and opening a new direction of creating topological quantum sensors with time-reversal symmetric spin-topological materials, given their topologically bounded quantum geometry. As such, an implementation of a spin-topological Hamiltonian in a quantum, e.g., free fermion, sensor
[
24
]
, offers for a topologically
\mathbb{Z}
-bounded enhancement of measurement sensitivity in realistic
\mathcal{T}
-symmetric, spinful systems. Quantum sensing relies on the uncertainty in the Cramér-Rao bounds
[
22
]
, which by relation to the quantum Fisher information
[
23
]
can be improved with enhanced quantum metric. We demonstrate the scaling of the quantum sensing-adaptable metric against the SOC realized in the spin-topological Bi Hamiltonian (Fig.

Discussion.
— Below we discuss our results and their applicability. We first stress that the derivation of the here introduced spin-topological bounds was possible because of the presence of a spin gap, which defines the
P_{\uparrow}
,
P_{\downarrow}
eigenstate manifolds. Closing the spin gap amounts to the trivialization of the here-derived geometric bounds, as well as of the spin-Chern numbers
C^{\sigma}_{s}

[
45
,
2
]
. Moreover, the derivation of quantum geometric bounds is possible without a direct use of energy eigenstates; previously a similar strategy was implemented for deriving geometric bounds in topological Euler bands
[
6
,
22
,
23
]
, where auxiliary states rather than eigenstates were utilized for the implementation of a geometric positive semidefiniteness condition, before ultimately connecting to the energy eigenstates via a unitary transformation. It should be noted that our bounds, rather than being of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
type, i.e., given by topological
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
indices, as previously retrieved in electronic
[
57
,
25
]
and excitonic contexts
[
58
,
59
]
, fundamentally belong to the class of
\mathbb{Z}
bounds
[
19
,
5
,
6
,
22
,
23
]
. Having derived new geometric optical bounds due to spin topology, a question of their experimental validation naturally arises. In that regard, quantum geometry was shown to be measurable with inelastic X-ray scattering
[
60
]
and photoemission
[
61
]
, which also applies to the measurements of the quantum metric induced by spin topology studied in this work. A further experimental study of an interplay of the spin-resolved quantum geometry, spin-density response, and entanglement provides for an interesting future pursuit, in particular, given the robustness of our findings in the presence of ubiquitous disorders.
Finally, we stress that while preserving a
\mathbb{Z}
character, our bounds applicable to quantum metrology and quantum sensing remain nontrivial in the presence of time-reversal symmetry, unlike the previous bounds for topological magnetic systems
[
28
,
24
]
. Hence, our bounds, adaptable even for four-state systems as discussed in the SM
[
46
]
, could enable a class of topological quantum sensors operating without magnetic environments.

Conclusions and Outlook.
— We propose an avenue for probing a range of spin-topological systems from the perspective of quantum geometric relations, which notably, for a vanishing
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
index, were previously not targetable with observables. We derive a set of spin-topological
\mathbb{Z}
bounds, even for eigenstates which are trivial from a Wilson loop perspective, which provide optical constraints on optical weights and bands gaps. We show that the bounds are valid in the presence of disorder and up to moderate spin-orbit entanglements, i.e., as long as the spin gap is preserved. We validated these quantum geometric conditions in bismuth and in an effective Hamiltonian with high spin-Chern number. By deriving quantum geometric relations central to quantum metrology, we show that quantum geometry bounded by the spin topology could enable spin-topological quantum sensors.

Appendix A: Spin-geometric and topological markers in disordered systems.
— We here provide minimal technical details on the spin-geometric real-space markers and their interplay with the spin-Chern markers. For full technical details, see Sec. IX of the SM
[
46
]
.

The local spin metric marker, which we define in real space under open boundary conditions for disordered spin-topological systems studied in this work, reads

g^{\sigma}_{ij}(\textbf{r})=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\sum_{\alpha}\bra{r_\alpha}P_{\sigma}\{P_{\sigma}\hat{x}_{i}P_{\sigma},P_{\sigma}\hat{x}_{j}P_{\sigma}\}\ket{r_\alpha}.

(9)

\ket{r_\alpha}
is a real-space physical orbital
\alpha
at a unit cell centered at the position vector
{\bf r}
,
A_{\text{cell}}
is the area of a unit cell, and
\{\ldots,\ldots\}
denotes an anticommutator of spin-projected operators
P_{\sigma}\hat{x}_{i}P_{\sigma}
, with spin-projector
P_{\sigma}
in real-space basis and position operator components
\hat{x}_{i}
. The spin-Chern marker is defined as
[
31
]

C^{\sigma}(\textbf{r})=\frac{4\pi}{A_{\text{cell}}}\mathfrak{Im}~\sum_{\alpha}\bra{r_\alpha}P_{\sigma}[\hat{x},P_{\sigma}][\hat{y},P_{\sigma}]\ket{r_\alpha}.

(10)

On defining
\text{Tr}[g^{\sigma}(\textbf{r})]\equiv g_{xx}^{\sigma}(\textbf{r})+g_{yy}^{\sigma}(\textbf{r})
, the spin metric marker and spin-Chern marker satisfy a topological bound consistent with the results presented in Fig.
3
,

\sum_{i\in\text{cells}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\text{Tr}[g^{\sigma}(\textbf{r}_{i})]\geq\sum_{i\in\text{cells}}|C^{\sigma}(\textbf{r}_{i})|,

(11)

which we rigorously derive in Sec. IX of the Supplemental Material. Supplementary calculations with spin metric and spin-Chern markers are presented in Sec. X of the Supplemental Material.

]
:

C^{\sigma}_{\text{sp}}\equiv-\frac{|{\bf b}_{1}||{\bf b}_{2}|}{\pi}\sum^{N^{\sigma}_{\text{occ}}}_{n}~\mathfrak{Im}~\langle\partial_{k_{x}}u_{n\bm{k}=\Gamma}^{\sigma}|\partial_{k_{y}}u_{n\bm{k}=\Gamma}^{\sigma}\rangle,

(12)

where
{\bf b}_{1}
,
{\bf b}_{2}
are reciprocal lattice vectors of a two-dimensional supercell. Moreover, we here define the single-point (spin) metric:

[{g}^{(\sigma)}_{ij}]_{\text{sp}}\equiv 2|{\bf b}_{1}||{\bf b}_{2}|~\sum^{N^{(\sigma)}_{\text{occ}}}_{n}~\mathfrak{Re}~\langle\partial_{k_{i}}u_{n\bm{k}=\Gamma}^{(\sigma)}|\partial_{k_{j}}u_{n\bm{k}=\Gamma}^{(\sigma)}\rangle,

(13)

with
\frac{1}{2\pi}\text{Tr}[{g}^{(\sigma)}_{ij}]_{\text{sp}}\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}([{g}^{(\sigma)}_{xx}]_{\text{sp}}+[{g}^{(\sigma)}_{yy}]_{\text{sp}})
. We retrieve a bound inherited from the local momentum-space inequality, Eq. (
4
):

\frac{1}{2\pi}\text{Tr}~[{g}^{\sigma}_{ij}]_{\text{sp}}\geq|C^{\sigma}_{\text{sp}}|.

(14)

Further details on the evaluation of numerical derivatives
|\partial_{k_{i}}u_{n\bm{k}=\Gamma}^{\sigma}\rangle
are provided in Ref.
[
34
]
, and the further simplified expressions are detailed in Sec. IX of the Supplemental Material
[
46
]
.

Appendix C: Quantum Cramér-Rao bound and quantum sensing.
— The quantum metric, through its relations to the quantum Fisher information, sets a lower bound on multiparameter estimation with pure states, as captured by the so-called quantum Cramér-Rao bound (QCRB), that is of central interest for quantum metrology and can be used in quantum sensing
[
22
]
. In the following, we consider pure states
\ket{\phi_\kv}
with density matrix
\rho=\ket{\phi_\kv}\bra{\phi_\kv}
constituted by the coherent superpositions of occupied single-particle eigenstates (see Sec. VII of the Supplemental Material
[
46
]
for more details). We furthermore provide a four-band model for
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed spin topology in Sec. VIII of the Supplemental Material
[
46
]
, which can be adapted for qudits or coupled qubits, and where the role of Bloch momenta can be taken by two angles, i.e.,
(k_{x},k_{y})\rightarrow(\phi,\theta)
. The QCRB, given for a pure state by the quantum metric, reads
[
23
]

\sqrt{\text{det}~\Sigma_{ij}}\geq\frac{1}{4M\sqrt{\text{det}~g_{ij}}},

(15)

where
M
is the number of measurements and we introduce the covariance matrix
\Sigma_{ij}
for an unbiased two-parameter estimator
k
. Here, the estimator that was identified with the angular parameters takes values in the two-parameter family over the two-torus [
{\textbf{k}=(k_{x},k_{y})\in T^{2}}
] under a set of positive operator-valued measurements,
{\Pi_{p}}
yielding the expected measurement values of individual parameters
\bar{k}_{i}
, with
i=x,y
. Explicitly, the covariance matrix reads
[
23
]

\Sigma_{ij}=\langle\delta k_{i}\delta k_{j}\rangle\equiv\sum_{p}(k_{i}-\bar{k}_{i})(k_{j}-\bar{k}_{j})\text{Tr}[\rho\Pi_{p}],

(16)

which quantifies the uncertainty in two measured parameters
k_{i},k_{j}
. As we show numerically in spin-topological Hamiltonians (Fig.
4
),
\Sigma_{ij}
can be topologically suppressed by the metric values provided by our
\mathbb{Z}
bound, for spin-topological quantum sensors with topologically reduced uncertainties.

Quantum geometric bounds in spinful systems with trivial band topology

I
Properties of the projected spin operator

In this Section, we summarize the relevant properties of the projected spin operator,
PS_{\hat{n}}P
, where
P({\bf k})=\sum_{n}^{\text{occ}}\ket{u_{n\kv}}\bra{u_{n\kv}}
projects onto an energetically isolated set of bands, and
S_{\hat{n}}=1\otimes(\hat{n}\cdot{\bf\sigma})
is the spin operator along
\hat{n}
in the tight-binding basis with eigenvalues
\pm 1
. We focus on the projected spin-
z
operator,
PS_{z}P
, in what follows. Additional discussions of projected spin operator can be found in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. We begin by noting that this operator can be written as:

PS_{z}P=\sum_{n,m}^{N_{\text{occ}}}\bra{u_{n\kv}}S_{z}\ket{u_{m\kv}}\ket{u_{n\kv}}\bra{u_{m\kv}},

(1)

from which it follows that the operator will always have
N-N_{\text{occ}}
zero eigenvalues, associated with the unoccupied bands
Q=1-P
. We ignore these in the following, and focus on the remaining
N_{\text{occ}}
eigenvalues
s_{n}^{z}({\bf k})\in[-1,1]
, associated with the occupied states, which form the spin band structure. We note that these can be found by directly diagonalizing the reduced spin operator
[s_{r}]_{nm}=\bra{u_{n\kv}}S_{z}\ket{u_{m\kv}}
. We assume time-reversal symmetry (TRS) throughout. TRS guarantees that
N_{\text{occ}}
is even for any energetically isolated group of bands, implying that we can identify the projectors
P_{\uparrow}=\sum_{n}^{N_{\text{occ}}/2}\ket{u_{n\kv}^{\uparrow}}\bra{u_{n\kv}^{\uparrow}}
and
P_{\downarrow}=\sum_{n}^{N_{\text{occ}}/2}\ket{u_{n\kv}^{\downarrow}}\bra{u_{n\kv}^{\downarrow}}
associated with the largest and smallest
N_{\text{occ}}/2
projected spin eigenvalues
s_{n}^{z}
respectively. Note that
P=P_{\uparrow}+P_{\downarrow}
, but that the states
\ket{u_{n\kv}^{\sigma}}
are not in general energy eigenstates. We can further define the
projected spin-gap
as:

\Delta S_{P}\equiv\text{min}_{{\bf k}}[s_{N_{\text{occ}}/2+1}^{z}({\bf k})-s_{N_{\text{occ}}/2}^{z}({\bf k})]\in[0,2].

(2)

If spin is a good quantum number (i.e.
[S_{z},H]=0)
, then all energy eigenstates can be written as product states between orbital and spin degrees of freedom, so that
s_{n}^{z}=\pm 1
and
\Delta S_{P}=2
. We refer to this as the non spin-orbit entangled case. If
\Delta S_{P}<2
, then we say that the system is
spin-orbit entangled
. As such
\Delta S_{P}
gives a rough estimate of the degree of entanglement between spin and orbital degrees of freedom within the energetically isolated band subspace spanned by
P({\bf k})
. It was shown in Ref.
[
1
]
that time-reversal symmetry
\mathcal{T}
guarantees
s_{n}^{z}(\boldsymbol{k})=-s^{z}_{N_{\text{occ}}-n}(-{\bf k})
. Furthermore, inversion symmetry
\mathcal{P}
guarantees that
s_{n}^{z}({\bf k})=s_{n}^{z}(-{\bf k})
, which taken together implies that systems with
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry satisfy
s_{n}^{z}({\bf k})=-s_{N_{\text{occ}}-n}^{z}({\bf k})
, implying that such systems have an effective chiral symmetry in the spin bands. We note finally that
\{\ket{u_{n\kv}}\}_{n=1}^{N_{\text{occ}}}
furnish a complete basis for the space spanned by
P
, so that any eigenstate of
PS_{z}P
in the image of
P
(rather than the zero eigenstates associated with
Q=1-P
) can be expanded as:

\ket{u_{m\kv}^{\sigma}}=\sum_{n}^{N_{\text{occ}}}c_{nm}^{\sigma}\ket{u_{n\kv}}.

(3)

Manifestly, Eq. (
3
) represents a
PS_{z}P
operator eigenstate as a weighted coherent superposition of single-particle Hamiltonian eigenstates. In particular, in the absence of spin-orbit coupling (SOC), in the presence of spin conservation, we have
c_{nm}^{\sigma}=\delta_{nm}
, see Sec.
III
for further details.

II
Spin-resolved quantum geometric bounds and their properties

Here we detail bounds arising from the spin-resolved quantum geometry introduced in the main text. We begin by writing the quantum geometric tensor (QGT)
Q_{ij}
in terms of a projector onto occupied states
P
at momentum
k

[
4
]
:

Q_{ij}=\text{Tr}[P\partial_{k_{i}}P\partial_{k_{j}}P],

(4)

as well as the spin-resolved quantum geometric tensor (spin-QGT),

Q^{\sigma}_{ij}=\text{Tr}[P_{\sigma}\partial_{k_{i}}P_{\sigma}\partial_{k_{j}}P_{\sigma}],

(5)

in terms of projectors
P_{\sigma}
onto
PS_{z}P
eigenstates, with
P_{\uparrow}+P_{\downarrow}=P
. On substituting the latter relation, we have:

\displaystyle Q_{ij}=Q^{\uparrow}_{ij}+Q^{\downarrow}_{ij}+\mathrm{Tr}[P_{\uparrow}\partial_{i}P_{\uparrow}\partial_{j}P_{\downarrow}]

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Tr}[P_{\uparrow}\partial_{i}P_{\downarrow}\partial_{j}P_{\uparrow}]+\mathrm{Tr}[P_{\uparrow}\partial_{i}P_{\downarrow}\partial_{j}P_{\downarrow}]

(6)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Tr}[P_{\downarrow}\partial_{i}P_{\uparrow}\partial_{j}P_{\uparrow}]+\mathrm{Tr}[P_{\downarrow}\partial_{i}P_{\downarrow}\partial_{j}P_{\uparrow}]

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Tr}[P_{\downarrow}\partial_{i}P_{\uparrow}\partial_{j}P_{\downarrow}],

where, as we show in the next Sections, the cross-terms vanish in the parallel-transport gauge, when the spin-orbit coupling (SOC) is vanishingly small.

Furthermore, beyond the projector-based spin-QGTs, we can define a non-Abelian spin-Berry connection matrix:

A^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{nm,i}=i\bra{u^\sigma_{n\kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u^{\sigma'}_{m\kv}},

(7)

which allows to recast the spin-resolved quantum metric, i.e., real part of the spin-QGT:
g^{\sigma}_{ij}\equiv\text{Re}~Q^{\sigma}_{ij}
:

g^{\sigma}_{ij}=\text{Re}~\sum^{N^{\sigma}_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\left[\bra{\partial_{k_i} u^\sigma_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_j} u^\sigma_{n \kv}}+A^{\sigma\sigma}_{nn,i}A^{\sigma\sigma}_{nn,j}\right].

(8)

The imaginary part of spin-QGT gives diagonal elements of the spin-Berry curvature matrix:

\text{Tr}~\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy}=\sum^{N^{\sigma}_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy,nn}=\sum^{N^{\sigma}_{\text{occ}}}_{n}i\left[\bra{\partial_{k_x} u^\sigma_{n\kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_y} u^\sigma_{n\kv}}-\text{c.c.}\right]=-2\text{Im}~\sum^{N^{\sigma}_{\text{occ}}}_{n}~\bra{\partial_{k_x} u^{\sigma}_{n\kv}}(1-P_{\sigma})\ket{\partial_{k_y} u^{\sigma}_{n\kv}}=-2\text{Im}~Q^{\sigma}_{xy}.

(9)

Centrally to this work, the spin-QGT is positive-semidefinite, which we prove below.

To begin the proof, we first decompose spin-QGT in terms of individual occupied spin-band contributions indexed with
n
,
Q^{\sigma}_{ij}=\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}Q^{n,\sigma}_{ij}
:

Q^{n,\sigma}_{ij}=\bra{\partial_{k_i} u^{\sigma}_{n\kv}}(1-P_{\sigma})\ket{\partial_{k_j} u^{\sigma}_{n\kv}},

(10)

with the projector yielding eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}=0,1
, i.e.,
P^{\sigma}_{n}\ket{u^{\sigma}_{m\kv}}=\delta_{mn}\ket{u^{\sigma}_{m\kv}}
. Hence, on constructing an auxiliary state
\ket{\phi}=\sum_{i}c_{i}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u^{\sigma}_{n\kv}}
with arbitrary coefficients
c_{i}
, one obtains
[
5
]

\sum_{i,j}(c_{i})^{*}Q^{n,\sigma}_{ij}c_{j}=\sum_{i,j}(c_{i})^{*}\bra{\partial_{k_i} u^{\sigma}_{n\kv}}(1-P_{\sigma})\ket{\partial_{k_j} u^{\sigma}_{n\kv}}(c_{j})=\bra{\phi}(1-P_{\sigma})\ket{\phi}\geq 0.

(11)

Further on summing over all occupied spin-bands, we retrieve,

\sum_{i,j}(c_{i})^{*}Q^{\sigma}_{ij}c_{j}\geq 0,

(12)

which shows the positive-semidefineteness of spin-QGT over the entire parameter space, analogously to the proofs of positive-semidefiniteness of QGTs of Refs.
[
5
,
6
]
. As a consequence,

\text{det}~Q^{\sigma}_{ij}=g^{\sigma}_{xx}g^{\sigma}_{yy}-(g^{\sigma}_{xy}-\frac{i}{2}\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy})(g^{\sigma}_{yx}-\frac{i}{2}\Omega^{\sigma}_{yx})=g^{\sigma}_{xx}g^{\sigma}_{yy}-g^{\sigma}_{xy}g^{\sigma}_{yx}-\frac{|\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy}|^{2}}{4}\geq 0,

(13)

where we used definitional symmetries
g^{\sigma}_{yx}=g^{\sigma}_{xy}
,
\Omega^{\sigma}_{yx}=-\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy}
. On rearranging and taking a square-root, we have:

\sqrt{\text{det}~g^{\sigma}_{ij}}\geq\frac{|\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy}|}{2},

(14)

and moreover, given that definitionally:
g_{xx},g_{yy}>0
, and given that all metric components are real:

\text{Tr}~g^{\sigma}_{ij}\equiv g^{\sigma}_{xx}+g^{\sigma}_{yy}\geq 2\sqrt{g^{\sigma}_{xx}g^{\sigma}_{yy}}\geq 2\sqrt{g^{\sigma}_{xx}g^{\sigma}_{yy}-(g^{\sigma}_{xy})^{2}}=\sqrt{\text{det}~g^{\sigma}_{ij}},

(15)

where we used an inequality between an arithmetic and geometric means, as well as that the squares of real numbers are non-negative. On combining both here-derived inequalities, we retrieve the hierarchy of spin-resolved inequalities featured in the main text.

III
Connecting spin-resolved quantum geometry to quantum geometry

We now derive the bound on the total (non spin-resolved) quantum metric due to the spin topology, which we further connect to the eigenstate transition dipole matrix elements in the optical contexts. We begin by
writing the
PS_{z}P
eigenstates as coherent superpositions of the occupied Hamiltonian eigenstates as in Eq. (
3
).
We substitute these coherent expansions to connect the matrix elements of the
PS_{z}P
eigenstates and Hamiltonian eigenstates:

\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \Big|\bra{u^\sigma_{m \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{l \kv}}\Big|^{2}&=\Big|\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}(c^{\sigma}_{nm})^{*}\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{l \kv}}\Big|^{2}\\
&=\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}|c^{\sigma}_{nm}|^{2}|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{l \kv}}|^{2}+2\sum_{m^{\prime}\neq n^{\prime}}\text{Re}\Big[(c^{\sigma}_{n^{\prime}m})^{*}c^{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}m}\bra{u_{n' \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{l \kv}}\bra{u_{l \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{m' \kv}}\Big]\\
&\leq 2\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}|c^{\sigma}_{nm}|^{2}|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{l \kv}}|^{2}\leq\Big(\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}|c^{\sigma}_{nm}|^{2}\Big)\Big(\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{l \kv}}|^{2}\Big)=\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\Big|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{l \kv}}\Big|^{2},\end{aligned}\end{split}

(16)

where we used the normalization condition within the occupied state manifold:

\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}|c^{\sigma}_{nm}|^{2}=1,

(17)

which states that a
PS_{z}P
operator eigenstate is a certain normalized superposition of the occupied Hamiltonian eigenstates with momentum
k
. More compactly, this inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\Big|\sum_{n}v^{*}_{n}w_{n}\Big|^{2}\leq\Big(\sum_{n}|v_{n}|^{2}\Big)\Big(\sum_{n}|w_{n}|^{2}\Big),

(18)

where
v_{n}\equiv c^{\sigma}_{nm}
and
w_{n}\equiv\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{l \kv}}
with a fixed derivative
\partial_{k_{i}}
and fixed eigenstate
\ket{u_{l \kv}}
.
Similarly, for matrix elements of type:

\begin{split}\Big|\bra{u^\sigma_{m \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u^{\sigma'}_{l \kv}}\Big|^{2}=\Big|\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}(c^{\sigma}_{nm})^{*}\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u^{\sigma'}_{l \kv}}\Big|^{2}\leq\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\Big|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u^{\sigma'}_{l \kv}}\Big|^{2}\\
=\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\Big|\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n^{\prime}}c^{\sigma}_{n^{\prime}l}\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{n' \kv}}\Big|^{2}\leq\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n^{\prime}}\Big|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{n' \kv}}\Big|^{2}.\end{split}

(19)

From positive-semidefiniteness of
Q^{\sigma}_{ij}
, as derived in the previous Section:

g^{\sigma}_{xx}+g^{\sigma}_{yy}\geq|\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy}|.

(20)

On combining both spin-up and spin-down inequalities:

g^{\uparrow}_{xx}+g^{\uparrow}_{yy}+g^{\downarrow}_{xx}+g^{\downarrow}_{yy}\geq|\Omega^{\uparrow}_{xy}|+|\Omega^{\downarrow}_{xy}|,

(21)

Given the diagonal elements of the spin-QGT are real by definition, we can recast the above inequality as:

\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}/2}_{m}\sum_{i=x,y}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\bra{\partial_{i} u^{\sigma}_{m \kv}}(1-P_{\sigma})\ket{\partial_{i} u^{\sigma}_{m \kv}}=\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}/2}_{m}\sum_{i=x,y}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\bra{\partial_{i} u^{\sigma}_{m \kv}}(Q+P_{\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma})\ket{\partial_{i} u^{\sigma}_{m \kv}}\geq|\Omega_{xy}^{\uparrow}|+|\Omega_{xy}^{\downarrow}|,

(22)

where we used the resolution of identity
1=P_{\uparrow}+P_{\downarrow}+Q
, where
Q
is a projector onto unoccupied bands. Splitting the resolved elements further, we obtain:

\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}/2}_{m}\sum^{\text{unocc}}_{l}\sum_{i=x,y}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\Big|\bra{u^{\sigma}_{m \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{l \kv}}\Big|^{2}+\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}/2}_{m,m^{\prime}}\sum_{i=x,y}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma}\Big|\bra{u^{\sigma}_{m \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u^{\sigma'}_{m' \kv}}\Big|^{2}\geq|\Omega_{xy}^{\uparrow}|+|\Omega_{xy}^{\downarrow}|,

(23)

and on employing the inequalities for individual matrix elements derived above,

\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}/2}_{m}\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\sum^{\text{unocc}}_{l}\sum_{i=x,y}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{l \kv}}|^{2}+\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}/2}_{m,m^{\prime}}\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n,n^{\prime}}\sum_{i=x,y}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{n' \kv}}|^{2}\geq|\Omega_{xy}^{\uparrow}|+|\Omega_{xy}^{\downarrow}|.

(24)

On performing the redundant summations:

2(N_{\text{occ}}/2)\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\sum^{\text{unocc}}_{l}\sum_{i=x,y}|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{l \kv}}|^{2}+2(N^{2}_{\text{occ}}/4)\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n,n^{\prime}}\sum_{i=x,y}|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{n' \kv}}|^{2}\geq|\Omega_{xy}^{\uparrow}|+|\Omega_{xy}^{\downarrow}|

(25)

N_{\text{occ}}\sum_{i=x,y}g_{ii}+(N^{2}_{\text{occ}}/2)\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n,n^{\prime}}\sum_{i=x,y}g^{nn^{\prime}}_{ii}\geq|\Omega_{xy}^{\uparrow}|+|\Omega_{xy}^{\downarrow}|.

(26)

Notably, in a parallel-transport gauge, the multiband matrix elements
between the occupied bands

g^{nn^{\prime}}_{ii}
vanish, unlike the interband matrix elements between occupied and unoccupied bands
\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\sum^{\text{unocc}}_{l}g^{nl}_{ii}=g_{ii}
. To demonstrate this, we note that the non-Abelian Berry connection in the occupied bands is a Hemitian matrix, i.e.,
{\bf A}_{nn^{\prime}}=({\bf A}^{*}_{n^{\prime}n})
, and hence, it can be diagonalized in the occupied band subspace by a similarity transformation:
{\bf A}_{nn^{\prime}}\rightarrow U{\bf A}_{nn^{\prime}}U^{\dagger}
, with
U
the matrix of eigenvectors of
{\bf A}_{nn^{\prime}}
. The unitary change of basis within the occupied band subspace corresponds to choosing the parallel-transport gauge. Hence, we have,

g^{nn^{\prime}}_{ij}\equiv\bra{\partial_{k_i} u_{n \kv}}\ket{ u_{n' \kv}}\bra{u_{n' \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{n \kv}}=A^{i}_{nn^{\prime}}A^{j}_{n^{\prime}n}=\delta_{nn^{\prime}}g^{nn}_{ij}.

(27)

Finally, we recognize that in the context of this work, the leftover terms
g^{nn}_{ii}=|A^{i}_{nn}|^{2}
can be locally gauged away by the
U(1)
gauge transformation:
\ket{u_{n\kv}}\rightarrow e^{i\alpha({\bf k})}\ket{u_{n\kv}}
, resulting in
A^{i}_{nn}\rightarrow A^{i}_{nn}+\partial_{k_{i}}\alpha({\bf k})=0
, on fixing a smooth gauge
locally
. We note that in both the systems with trivial or nontrivial Wilson loop windings [independently from the nontrivial spin Wilson loop], such local gauge fixing amounts to a local bound at a given
k
-point, which is equivalent to fixing the gauge to a local trivialization with
||\partial_{k_{i}}P_{n{\bf k}}||\equiv\sqrt{\text{Tr}(\partial_{k_{i}}P_{n{\bf k}})^{2}}=0
, where
P_{n{\bf k}}=\ket{u_{n\kv}}\bra{u_{n\kv}}
is a local projector. Hence, on applying a parallel-transport gauge, we arrive at a local bound,

N_{\text{occ}}\sum_{i=x,y}g_{ii}\geq|\Omega_{xy}^{\uparrow}|+|\Omega_{xy}^{\downarrow}|,

(28)

in a general spin-orbit coupled, and spin non-conserving, case. This concludes the derivation of the fundamental quantum geometric bound on connecting the spin-resolved quantum geometry to a standard multiband quantum geometry
[
7
,
8
,
6
]
.

Finally, as a special case, in the spin-conserving case, we retrieve a stronger inequality, consistently with Ref.
[
9
]
. Namely, the absence of spin-mixing SOC, allows to sharpen the previous inequality,

\begin{split}\Big|\bra{u^\sigma_{m \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u^{}_{l \kv}}\Big|^{2}=\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\Big|\bra{u_{n \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{l \kv}}\Big|^{2}\rightarrow\Big|\bra{u_{m \kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{l \kv}}\Big|^{2},\end{split}

(29)

as the sum,
\sum_{n}|c^{\sigma}_{nm}|^{2}=|c^{\sigma}_{mm}|^{2}=1
, reduces to a single term. On repeating the previous steps with reduction, one instead obtains a local bound,

\sum_{i=x,y}g_{ii}\geq|\Omega_{xy}^{\uparrow}|+|\Omega_{xy}^{\downarrow}|,

(30)

which on integrating one obtains the bound due to the spin-Chern numbers
[
9
]
. We note that both the bounds in the spin-conserving and non-conserving cases also hold in systems without inversion symmetries, which were
not
assumed in the derivations above.

IV
Effects of spin-orbit coupling and spin-orbit entanglement

We introduce spin-orbit coupling as a perturbation
\Delta H=\sum_{n,m}\lambda_{nm}(1-\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}})\ket{\psi^{\sigma}_{n\kv}}\bra{\psi^{\sigma'}_{m\kv}}
, where the limit
\lambda=0
corresponds to the vanishingly small SOC.
The time-independent perturbation theory applied to first order, yields:

\ket{\psi_{n\kv}}=\ket{\psi^{\lambda=0}_{n\kv}}+\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\bra{\psi^{\lambda=0}_{m\kv}}\Delta H\ket{\psi^{\lambda=0}_{n\kv}}}{E_{n{\bf k}}-E_{m{\bf k}}}\ket{\psi^{\lambda=0}_{m\kv}},

(31)

where
E_{n{\bf k}}
are the energy eigenvalues of the non-degenerate eigenstates for
\lambda=0
. Here, we use the basis of Bloch states
{\ket{\psi_{m\kv}}=e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf\hat{r}}}\ket{u_{m\kv}}}
. Moreover, when
\lambda=0
, the Hamiltonian eigenstates are also the eigenstates of the
PS_{z}P
operator,
\ket{\psi^{\lambda=0}_{n\kv}}=\ket{\psi^\sigma_{n\kv}}
, thus, we have:

\ket{\psi_{n\kv}}=\ket{\psi^\sigma_{n\kv}}+\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\lambda_{mn}(1-\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}})}{E_{n{\bf k}}-E_{m{\bf k}}}\ket{\psi^{\sigma'}_{m\kv}}.

(32)

In the following, we further demonstrate the scaling of the introduced metrics with the spin-orbit entanglement. For simplicity, we set
\lambda_{mn}=\lambda
and assume a flat-band dispersion
E_{n{\bf k}}=E_{n}
. On substituting the perturbative correction, we begin by writing the spin-resolved quantum metrics:

g^{\sigma}_{ij}(\lambda)=g^{\sigma}_{ij}(\lambda=0)-\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\lambda}{E_{n}-E_{m}}A^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{nm,i}A^{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}_{nm,j}+\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{(E_{n}-E_{m})^{2}}A^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{nm,i}A^{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}_{mn,j}+O(\lambda^{3}),

(33)

which at the leading order allows for a possible reduction of the
g_{ij}
in the eigenstate basis, while satisfying the lower bound with an addition of the spin-entanglement factor
1/N_{\text{occ}}
, as demonstrated in Sec. IV. Numerically, we find that instead the metrics increase in magnitude with the increasing
\lambda
, as shown in the main text, which is possible because the band gap and spin gaps reduce in size, as the SOC is increased. While in the above we treat
\lambda
and
|E_{n}-E_{m}|
as independent parameters, these are indeed not independent for any local Hamiltonian, which explains why the numerically observed scaling of the metric is increasing rather than decreasing with SOC.

V
Spin-resolved quantum geometry and optical responses

As in the previous Sections, we begin by noting that
P_{\uparrow}=P-P_{\downarrow}
, so that
1-P_{\uparrow}=1-P+P_{\downarrow}
. Therefore, intuitively, one can interpret
Q_{ij}^{\uparrow}
as transitions into either the unoccupied manifold
or
the occupied states corresponding to projected spin-down states from projected spin-up states.

To connect to optics, we make the following identification, following Refs.
[
10
,
8
]
,

\mathcal{M}\equiv|\bra{u_{n\kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{m\kv}}|^{2}=|\bra{\psi_{n\kv}}\hat{r}_{i}\ket{\psi_{m\kv}}|^{2}

(34)

namely that the non-Abelian Berry connection elements can be rewritten in terms of transition dipole matrix elements for E1 optical transitions, induced by the electron-photon coupling that reads
\Delta H=e{\bf E}(t)\cdot{\bf\hat{r}}
in the length gauge, with
{\bf\hat{r}}
the position oeprator. As such, we can connect the matrix elements to the optical transition rates using Fermi’s golden rule:

\Gamma_{i}(\omega)=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\sum^{\text{unocc}}_{m}|\bra{\psi_{n\kv}}\hat{r}_{i}\ket{\psi_{m\kv}}|^{2}\delta(E_{m{\bf k}}-E_{n{\bf k}}-\hbar\omega),

(35)

which we connect to quantum metric
[
11
]
, to retrieve a new bound

\Gamma_{x}+\Gamma_{y}=\frac{2\pi e^{2}|{\bf E}|^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{\text{BZ}}\differential^{2}{\bf k}~(g_{xx}+g_{yy})\geq\frac{2\pi e^{2}|{\bf E}|^{2}}{N_{\text{occ}}\hbar^{2}}(|C^{\uparrow}_{s}|+|C^{\downarrow}_{s}|),

(36)

with
\Gamma_{x/y}\equiv\int^{\infty}_{0}\differential\omega~\Gamma_{x/y}(\omega)
denoting integrated optical transition rates on coupling to
x
- and
y
-polarized light. Here, we assumed that spin is not necessarily conserved. In the presence of spin conservation, we analogously have:

\Gamma_{x}+\Gamma_{y}\geq\frac{2\pi e^{2}|{\bf E}|^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}(|C^{\uparrow}_{s}|+|C^{\downarrow}_{s}|).

(37)

Further to the bounds on transition rates, we connect the geometric bound to optical conductivity
\sigma_{ij}(\omega)
. The optical conductivity reads

\sigma_{ii}(\omega)=ie^{2}\int_{\text{BZ}}\frac{\differential^{2}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m,n}(f_{n{\bf k}}-f_{m{\bf k}})\frac{\hbar\omega\bra{\psi_{n\kv}}\hat{v}_{i}\ket{\psi_{m\kv}}\bra{\psi_{m\kv}}\hat{v}_{i}\ket{\psi_{n\kv}}}{(E_{n{\bf k}}-E_{m{\bf k}})^{2}(\omega-E_{m{\bf k}}/\hbar+E_{n{\bf k}}/\hbar+i0^{+})},

(38)

where
\hat{v}_{i}=\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H},\hat{r_{i}}]
is a velocity operator, and
f_{n{\bf k}}=\Big[\text{exp}\big((E_{n{\bf k}}-\mu)/T\big)+1\Big]^{-1}
with chemical potential
\mu
are Fermi-Dirac thermal occupation factors, which in the zero temperature
(T=0)
limit
(f_{nm}=f_{n}-f_{m}=1)
reduces to
[
8
]

\sigma_{ii}(\omega)=\frac{\pi\omega e^{2}}{\hbar}\int_{\text{BZ}}\frac{\differential^{2}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m,n}f_{nm}g^{mn}_{ii}\delta(\omega-E_{m{\bf k}}/\hbar+E_{n{\bf k}}/\hbar).

(39)

On integrating, one obtains a well-known relation between the optical conductivity and geometry, namely, the Souza-Wilkens-Martin (SWM) sum rule
[
12
]

\int^{\infty}_{0}\differential\omega~\frac{\text{Re}~\sigma_{ij}(\omega)}{\omega}=\frac{e^{2}}{2h}\int_{\text{BZ}}\differential^{2}{\bf k}~g_{ij},

(40)

which in the main text, we employ combined with the bound by the spin-Chern numbers. Moreover, to arrive at the band gap bound, we combine this result with the
f
-sum rule
[
13
,
14
]
:

\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{ne^{2}}{m}=\int^{\infty}_{0}\differential\omega~\text{Re}~\sigma_{ii}(\omega),

(41)

which culminates in a hierarchy of inequalities,

\frac{nhe^{2}}{2mE_{g}}=\sum_{i=x,y}\frac{\hbar}{E_{g}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\differential\omega~\text{Re}~\sigma_{ii}(\omega)\geq\sum_{i=x,y}\int^{\infty}_{0}\differential\omega~\frac{\text{Re}~\sigma_{ii}(\omega)}{\omega}=\sum_{i=x,y}\frac{e^{2}}{2h}\int_{\text{BZ}}\differential^{2}{\bf k}~g_{ii}\geq\frac{e^{2}}{2\hbar}S_{\lambda}.

(42)

Here, we also assumed that the are no sub-gap excitations contributing to the optical conductivity
\sigma_{ii}(\omega)
at frequencies
0<\omega<E_{g}/\hbar
, which holds for single-particle states in the absence of many-body effects. Fig.
S1
shows the optical response for ultrathin Bi, as well as the four-band TRS-symmetric QWZ model discussed in Sec. VIII, where we define:

K(\omega_{c})=\frac{2\hbar}{e^{2}}\int_{0}^{\omega_{c}}\differential\omega\frac{\mathrm{Re}[\sigma_{xx}(\omega)+\sigma_{yy}(\omega)]}{\omega}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\omega_{c}}\differential\omega\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\differential^{2}{\bf k}\sum_{mn}(g_{xx}^{mn}+g_{yy}^{mn})f_{nm}\delta(\hbar\omega-E_{m}+E_{n}),

(43)

where as expected, in the zero temperature limit,
K(\omega_{c}\rightarrow\infty)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\mathrm{Tr}[g]
.

To get the SOC scaling in Fig. 3 of the main text, we write the tight-binding Hamiltonian in the basis
\{\ket{6p}\otimes\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}\}

\lambda

We further utilize the computed eigenstates to retrieve the optical conductivity
\sigma_{ij}(\omega)
and demonstrate the spin-topological bound realized by
\text{Re}~\sigma_{ij}(\omega)

P_{n{\bf k}}=\sum_{E_{n}=E_{n_{i}}}\ket{u_{n_i\kv}}\bra{u_{n_i\kv}}
and unoccupied bands
P_{m{\bf k}}=\sum_{E_{m}=E_{m_{j}}}\ket{u_{m_j\kv}}\bra{u_{m_j\kv}}

[
8
]
, where
n=\{n_{i}\}
and
m=\{m_{j}\}
denote the sets of degenerate bands at momentum
{\bf k}
with energies
E_{n}
and
E_{m}

[
8

g_{ij}=\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\text{Re}~\bra{\partial_{k_i} u_{n\kv}}\Big(\sum^{\text{unocc}}_{m}\ket{u_{m\kv}}\bra{u_{m\kv}}\Big)\ket{\partial_{k_j} u_{n\kv}}=\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\sum^{\text{unocc}}_{m}g^{mn}_{ij}=\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n}\sum^{\text{unocc}}_{m}\text{Re}~\bra{u_{m\kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_j} u_{n\kv}}\bra{\partial_{k_i} u_{n\kv}}\ket{u_{m\kv}},

(44)

We further note that:
0=\partial_{k_{i}}(\bra{u_{n\kv}}\ket{u_{m\kv}})=\bra{\partial_{k_i} u_{n\kv}}\ket{u_{m\kv}}+\bra{ u_{n\kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i}u_{m\kv}}
. By using (i) the product rule, (ii) orthogonality of eigenstates, (iii) cyclic property of trace, we further have:

\begin{split}g^{mn}_{ij}=-\text{Re}~\bra{u_{m\kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_j} u_{n\kv}}\bra{u_{n\kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{m\kv}}=-\frac{1}{2}\bra{u_{m\kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_j} u_{n\kv}}\bra{u_{n\kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{m\kv}}-\frac{1}{2}\bra{\partial_{k_j} u_{n\kv}}\ket{u_{m\kv}}\bra{\partial_{k_i} u_{m\kv}}\ket{u_{n\kv}}\\
=-\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[(\ket{\partial_{k_j} u_{n\kv}}\bra{u_{n\kv}})(\ket{\partial_{k_i} u_{m\kv}}\bra{u_{m\kv}})]-\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[(\ket{ u_{n\kv}}\bra{\partial_{k_j} u_{n\kv}})(\ket{u_{m\kv}}\bra{\partial_{k_i} u_{m\kv}})]=-\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[\partial_{k_{j}}P_{n{\bf k}}\partial_{k_{i}}P_{m{\bf k}}],\end{split}

(45)

which on combining with the first-principles band energies and Eq. (
39
) obtains the interband optical conductivity of bismuth in an insulating phase. We show our findings in Fig.

VII
Spin-resolved quantum geometry and quantum metrology

In the following, we provide further details on spin-topological quantum metrology. Furthermore, we provide an analytical argument for the scaling of quantum Fisher information (QFI) with the spin-orbit entanglement, which is central to the main text, and where we retrieved this result numerically.

The quantum Fisher information, in the context of multiparameter quantum sensing
[
22
]
with states given by a density matrix
\rho=\sum^{N}_{n=1}\lambda_{n}\ket{\psi_{n}}\bra{\psi_{n}}
, where
{\bf k}
is a vector of parameters, can be defined as
[
23
]
:

F_{ij}[\rho]\equiv\sum^{N}_{n,m}\frac{2\text{Re}~(\bra{\psi_n}\partial_{k_{i}}\rho\ket{\psi_m}\bra{\psi_m}\partial_{k_{j}}\rho\ket{\psi_n})}{\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{m}}

(46)

which intuitively captures how the probability distribution given by a density matrix is changed subject to changing a parameter associated with, i.e., conjugate to, an observable. Indeed, on recognizing the probability distributions associated with the density matrix and POVMs, one can obtain the classical Fisher information in the classical limit,

F_{ij}=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{P(X_{n},r)}\left(\frac{\partial P(X_{n},r)}{\partial r}\right)^{2},

(47)

which reflects the overlaps and changes in the probability distribution
P(X_{n},r)
for a variable
r
, with outcomes
X_{n}
, subject to the parameter changes.

The quantum metric, here bounded by spin topology, fully determines the quantum Fisher information (QFI) in the case of
pure
states. We consider a pure state
\ket{\phi_\kv}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\text{occ}}}}\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n=1}\ket{\psi_{n \kv}}
with density matrix
\rho=\ket{\phi_\kv}\bra{\phi_\kv}
. The QFI matrix
F_{ij}[\rho]
for a pure state, with
i,j
parameter indices and with position operator
\hat{r}_{i}
as an observable, reads
[
23
]
:

\begin{split}F_{ij}[\rho]=\sum_{m,n:\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n}\geq 0}2\frac{(\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{n})^{2}}{\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n}}\\
\bra{\psi_{m\kv}}\hat{r}_{i}\ket{\psi_{n\kv}}\bra{\psi_{n\kv}}\hat{r}_{j}\ket{\psi_{m\kv}}=4g_{ij}.\end{split}

(48)

where
\lambda_{m}
are the eigenvalues of the density matrix
\rho
, and which we recast in terms of quantum metric
[
23
]
. Correspondingly, the quantum Cramér-Rao bound (QCRB) is determined by QFI
[
23
]
,

\sqrt{\text{det}~\Sigma_{ij}(\hat{{\bf k}})}\geq\frac{1}{M\sqrt{F_{ij}[\rho]}}=\frac{1}{4M\sqrt{\text{det}~g_{ij}(\textbf{k})}},

(49)

where
\Sigma_{ij}(\hat{{\bf k}})
is a covariance matrix for a two-parameter estimator
\hat{{\bf k}}
, as defined in the main text. The QCRB is central to the quantum-metrological applications considered and discussed in the main text.

We further consider a decomposition the density matrix in terms of projected spin contributions,

\rho=\rho_{\uparrow}+\rho_{\downarrow},

(50)

which allows us to define a spin-projected quantum Fisher information (spin-QFI). The spin-QFI, for a spin-eigenstate, reads:

F_{ij}[\rho_{\sigma}]=\sum_{m,n:\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n}\geq 0}2\frac{(\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{n})^{2}}{\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n}}\bra{\psi^{\sigma}_{m\kv}}\hat{r}_{i}\ket{\psi^{\sigma}_{n\kv}}\bra{\psi^{\sigma}_{n\kv}}\hat{r}_{j}\ket{\psi^{\sigma}_{m\kv}}=4g^{\sigma}_{ij},

(51)

where in the last line, we connect the spin-resolved quantum geometry to the introduced estimator analogously to the standard QFI
[
23
]
.

Furthermore, we can consider a pure many-body fermionic state resolvable in single-particle quasiparticle states
\ket{\psi_{n\kv}}
with a density matrix
\rho=\ket{\psi_\lambda}\bra{\psi_\lambda}
, which determines a many-body Fisher information
[
24
]
,

F^{\text{MB}}_{ij}[\rho]=4\text{Re}\bra{\partial_{\lambda_i} \psi_\lambda}\ket{\partial_{\lambda_j} \psi_\lambda}-\bra{\partial_{\lambda_i} \psi_\lambda}\ket{\psi_\lambda}\bra{\psi_\lambda}\ket{\partial_{\lambda_j} \psi_\lambda},

(52)

where
\lambda
can be a SOC strength parameter that can be controlled with external environment. In particular, the Fisher information diverges at a critical SOC corresponding to a topological phase transition point
\lambda=\lambda_{c}
due to the divergence of the metric, as previously retrieved for free fermion topological quantum sensors
[
24
]
.

Alternatively, the parameter space
\{\lambda\}
can be spanned by the centre-of mass momenta
{\bf\lambda}=(K_{x},K_{y})
of a many-body state. Correspondingly, we have

\sqrt{\text{det}~\Sigma_{ij}(\hat{{\bf K}})}\geq\frac{1}{M\sqrt{F^{\text{MB}}_{ij}[\rho]}}=\frac{1}{4M\sqrt{\text{det}~g^{\text{MB}}_{ij}(\textbf{K})}},

(53)

where
\Sigma_{ij}(\hat{{\bf K}})
is a covariance matrix for the two-parameter estimation using a many-body state
\ket{\psi_\vec{K}}
and
g^{\text{MB}}_{ij}(\textbf{K})
is a many-body metric,

g^{\text{MB}}_{ij}(\textbf{K})=\frac{1}{2}\bra{\partial_{K_i} \psi_\textbf{K}}(1-\ket{\psi_\textbf{K}}\bra{\psi_\textbf{K}})\ket{\partial_{K_j} \psi_\textbf{K}}+\text{c.c.}.

(54)

On using
{\bf K}=0
and a Slater determinant many-body fermionic state
\ket{\psi_\textbf{0}}=\mathcal{A}(\ket{\psi_{1\kv}},\ldots,\ket{\psi_{m\kv}})
, with
\mathcal{A}
an antisymmetrization over single-particle states, as a reference point for the two-parameter estimation,
we have,

g^{\text{MB}}_{ij}(0)=\sum_{{\bf k}}g_{ij}({\bf k})=\frac{L_{x}L_{y}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\text{BZ}}\differential^{2}{\bf k}~g_{ij},

(55)

with
L_{x},L_{y}
the linear system dimensions, and
A=L_{x}L_{y}
the area of the material. Hence, on combining with the spin-Chern bounds of Sec. II, we obtain:

\frac{1}{4}F_{ii}[\rho]=\text{Tr}~g^{\text{MB}}_{ii}(0)\geq\frac{A(|C_{\uparrow}|+|C_{\downarrow}|)}{2\pi},

(56)

which demonstrates the scaling of the Fisher information, and hence of the precision of the two-parameter measurements, with the size of spin-topological free fermion system.

We now demonstrate the scaling of quantum metric with the spin gap. The eigenvalue equation for the projected spin-operator is:

PS_{z}P\ket{u^\sigma_{n\kv}}=s^{\sigma}_{z}\ket{u^\sigma_{n\kv}}.

(57)

On differentiating the equation, and defining the local spin gap
\Delta S_{mn}\equiv s^{\uparrow}_{m}-s^{\downarrow}_{n}
, we obtain,

\bra{u^{\sigma'}_{m\kv}}\ket{\partial_{k_i} u^\sigma_{n\kv}}=\frac{\bra{u^{\sigma'}_{m\kv}}\partial_{k_{i}}(PS_{z}P)\ket{u^\sigma_{n\kv}}}{s^{\sigma}_{n}-s^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{m}}=\frac{\bra{u^{\sigma'}_{m\kv}}\partial_{k_{i}}(PS_{z}P)\ket{u^\sigma_{n\kv}}}{\Delta S_{mn}},

(58)

and hence for the spin-resolved quantum metric
g^{\sigma}_{ij}
,

g^{\sigma}_{ii}=\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}}_{n,m}\frac{\Big|\bra{u^{\sigma' \neq\sigma}_{m \kv}}\partial_{k_{i}}(PS_{z}P)\ket{u^\sigma_{n\kv}}\Big|^{2}}{(\Delta S_{mn})^{2}}+(\text{unocc}),

(59)

where the
{}^{\prime}\text{unocc}^{\prime}
terms correspond to the matrix elements of spin bands to unoccupied bands, which are bounded by the energy gap. We observe that as the spin gap
\Delta S_{p}=\text{min}~\Delta S_{mn}\rightarrow 0
becomes vanishingly small, the spin-resolved metric scales up accordingly, as long as the matrix elements in the numerator do not decay faster than a power law. We find this scenario to occur in the context of bismuth and four-band Hamiltonians in Sec. VIII. Correspondingly, we observe that the metric elements
g_{ii}
scale up as the spin-gap
\Delta S_{p}
decreases, which we show in the main text, and in the following section. The higher the metric, the better the sensing capability, as underpinned by the QCRBs highlighted in this Section.

IX
Spin-resolved quantum geometry and spin topology in the presence of disorder

Below, we detail how the spin-resolved quantum geometry and its interplay with the spin topology central to this work can be characterized in the presence of disorder. In the following, we first consider systems under open boundary conditions (OBC). We begin by defining the real-space spin-resolved metric markers and retrieve their correspondences to the localization markers capturing the quantum metric. The spin-metric marker proposed in this work reads:

g^{\sigma}_{ij}(\textbf{r})=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\text{Tr}_{\text{cell}}[P_{\sigma}\{P_{\sigma}\hat{x}_{i}P_{\sigma},P_{\sigma}\hat{x}_{j}P_{\sigma}\}]=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\sum_{\alpha}\bra{r_\alpha}P_{\sigma}\{P_{\sigma}\hat{x}_{i}P_{\sigma},P_{\sigma}\hat{x}_{j}P_{\sigma}\}\ket{r_\alpha}.

(61)

where
\{\ldots,\ldots\}
denotes an anticommutator of spin-projected operators
P_{\sigma}\hat{x}_{i}P_{\sigma}
, with position operator components
\hat{x}_{i}
running over
i=1,2
, corresponding to
\hat{x}
and
\hat{y}
, respectively.
A_{\text{cell}}
is an area of a unit cell. The construction of the spin-geometric marker is analogous to the construction of the localization (metric) marker introduced by Marrazzo and Resta in Ref.
[
28
]
.

\mathcal{F}_{ij}(\textbf{r})=-\text{Tr}_{\text{cell}}[P[\hat{x}_{i},P],[\hat{x}_{j},P]]=-\sum_{\alpha}\bra{r_\alpha}P[\hat{x}_{i},P],[\hat{x}_{j},P]\ket{r_\alpha}.

(62)

We adapt a real-space marker for the trace of metric as:

\text{Tr}~g(\textbf{r})\equiv g_{xx}(\textbf{r})+g_{yy}(\textbf{r})=\frac{1}{4\pi A_{\text{cell}}}[\mathcal{F}_{xx}(\textbf{r})+\mathcal{F}_{yy}(\textbf{r})],

(63)

where analogously to the spin-resolved metric markers, we include an expression equivalent to the localization marker
[
29
,
30
]
:

g_{ij}(\textbf{r})=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\text{Tr}_{\text{cell}}[P\{P\hat{x}_{i}P,P\hat{x}_{j}P\}]=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\sum_{\alpha}\bra{r_\alpha}P\{P\hat{x}_{i}P,P\hat{x}_{j}P\}\ket{r_\alpha}.

(64)

Similarly, for the real-space spin-metric marker, we consistently define:

\text{Tr}~g^{\sigma}(\textbf{r})\equiv g^{\sigma}_{xx}(\textbf{r})+g^{\sigma}_{yy}(\textbf{r}).

(65)

The spin-Chern marker, which we compare to the spin-geometric and localization (metric) markers; and in particular to their traces, reads
[
31
]
:

C^{\sigma}(\textbf{r})=\frac{4\pi}{A_{\text{cell}}}\mathfrak{Im}~\text{Tr}_{\text{cell}}[P_{\sigma}[\hat{x},P_{\sigma}],[\hat{y},P_{\sigma}]]=\frac{4\pi}{A_{\text{cell}}}\mathfrak{Im}~\sum_{\alpha}\bra{r_\alpha}P_{\sigma}[\hat{x}_{i},P_{\sigma}],[\hat{x}_{j},P_{\sigma}]\ket{r_\alpha}.

(66)

We find that both the spin-metric and localization (metric) markers are lower-bounded by the spin-Chern marker, demonstrating that the bounds derived in this work hold locally in individual parts of disordered spin-topological systems. The validity of the local bound can be derived starting from the momentum-space inequality, as follows. We rewrite the momentum space integral as
\text{Tr}_{\bf k}[\ldots]=\int_{\text{BZ}}\frac{\differential^{2}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}(\ldots)
. Combining the integration with the local inequality Eq. (
28
), we have:

N_{\text{occ}}\sum_{i=x,y}\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}_{\bf k}(P\{\partial_{k_{i}}P,\partial_{k_{i}}P\})\equiv\text{Tr}_{\bf k}(g_{xx}+g_{yy})\geq\text{Tr}_{\bf k}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}|\Omega^{\sigma}_{xy}|=\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\text{Tr}_{\bf k}[|P_{\sigma}[\partial_{k_{x}}P_{\sigma},\partial_{k_{y}}P_{\sigma}]|],

(67)

where we additionally inserted definitions of the quantum metric and spin-Berry curvature in terms of projectors. Under a substitution
\partial_{k_{i}}\hat{P}_{(\sigma)}=i[\hat{x}_{i},\hat{P}_{(\sigma)}]
, and using basis independence and completeness relations of the trace
\text{Tr}_{\bf k}[\ldots]=\text{Tr}_{\bf r}[\ldots]=\int\differential{\bf r}~\bra{r}\ldots\ket{r}
, we obtain:

N_{\text{occ}}\sum_{i=x,y}\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}_{\bf r}(P\{[\hat{x}_{i},P],[\hat{x}_{i},P]\})\geq\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\text{Tr}_{\bf r}[|P_{\sigma}[[\hat{x},P_{\sigma}],[\hat{y},P_{\sigma}]]|],

(68)

which in terms of real-space markers reads:

\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{i\in\text{cells}}\text{Tr}~g(\textbf{r}_{i})\geq\sum_{i\in\text{cells}}\frac{|C^{\uparrow}(\textbf{r}_{i})|+|C^{\downarrow}(\textbf{r}_{i})|}{N_{\text{occ}}}.

(69)

By repeating the analogous steps, starting from inequality Eq. (
20
),
we have:

\sum_{i\in\text{cells}}\text{Tr}~g^{\sigma}(\textbf{r}_{i})\geq\sum_{i\in\text{cells}}2\pi|C^{\sigma}(\textbf{r}_{i})|.

(70)

As central to this work, the above real-space results show that the spin-geometric bounds also hold in the presence of disorder, as long as the spin-Chern numbers are preserved. It should be noted that the sum over all the cells in the disordered system needs to be performed, as locally:

\text{Tr}~g^{\sigma}(\textbf{r})=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\sum_{\alpha}\bra{r_\alpha}P\{P\hat{x}_{i}P,P\hat{x}_{j}P\}\ket{r_\alpha}=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{{\bf k},{\bf k^{\prime}}}e^{i({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime})\cdot{\bf r}}\bra{\vec{k}'_\alpha}P\{P\hat{x}_{i}P,P\hat{x}_{j}P\}\ket{\vec{k}_\alpha},

(71)

with Bloch orbitals
\ket{\vec{k}_\alpha}=\sum_{\bf r}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}\ket{r_\alpha}
, which reduces to the momentum-space bound encoding the positive-semidefiniteness of quantum-geometric tensors in the momentum parameter space only when the integration/summation
\sum_{\bf r}e^{i({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime})\cdot{\bf r}}=\delta({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime})
is performed. The real-space summation, as present in Eq. (
70
), pins the matrix elements of geometric tensors to diagonal elements
\bra{\vec{k}_\alpha}P\{P\hat{x}_{i}P,P\hat{x}_{j}P\}\ket{\vec{k}_\alpha}
, which combined with the Bloch-orbital flavours
c_{n\alpha}
directly to the eigenstates
\ket{u_{n\kv}}=\sum_{\alpha}c_{n\alpha}\ket{\vec{k}_\alpha}
. It should be stressed that only the corresponding elements diagonal in the eigenstate basis over the parameter space enjoy the geometric bounds, which are necessarily local in the momentum (parameter) space.

In addition to defining the spin-resolved geometric markers under the OBC, we consider the spin-resolved geometric markers for disordered systems under periodic boundary conditions (PBC). The PBC spin-resolved metric marker reads,

[g^{\sigma}_{ij}(\textbf{r})]_{\text{PBC}}=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\text{Tr}_{\text{cell}}[P^{\sigma}_{\Gamma}\{P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{i}},P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{j}}\}]=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\sum_{\alpha}\bra{r_\alpha}P^{\sigma}_{\Gamma}\{P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{i}},P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{j}}\}\ket{r_\alpha}.

(72)

with the PBC metric marker:

[g_{ij}(\textbf{r})]_{\text{PBC}}=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\text{Tr}_{\text{cell}}[P_{\Gamma}\{P_{{\bf b}_{i}},P_{{\bf b}_{j}}\}]=\frac{1}{2A_{\text{cell}}}\sum_{\alpha}\bra{r_\alpha}P_{\Gamma}\{P_{{\bf b}_{i}},P_{{\bf b}_{j}}\}\ket{r_\alpha},

(73)

and the PBC spin-Chern marker, as originally defined in Ref.
[
31
]
:

[C_{\sigma}(\textbf{r})]_{\text{PBC}}=\frac{4\pi}{A_{\text{cell}}}\mathfrak{Im}~\text{Tr}_{\text{cell}}[P^{\sigma}_{\Gamma}[P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{1}},P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{2}}]]=\frac{4\pi}{A_{\text{cell}}}\mathfrak{Im}~\sum_{\alpha}\bra{r_\alpha}P^{\sigma}_{\Gamma}[P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{1}},P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{2}}]\ket{r_\alpha}.

(74)

Here, the PBC projectors are defined within an approximation of Ref.
[
31
]
:

P^{(\sigma)}_{{\bf b}_{i}}\approx P^{(\sigma)}_{\Gamma}+\frac{2\pi i}{L}[\hat{x}_{i},P^{(\sigma)}_{\Gamma}]-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{L^{2}}[\hat{x}_{i},[\hat{x}_{i},P^{(\sigma)}_{\Gamma}]],

(75)

where
L
is the linear system size, and the
P^{\sigma}_{\Gamma}
projector reads
[
31
]
:

P^{\sigma}_{\Gamma}\equiv\sum^{N_{\text{occ}}/2}_{n}\ket{u^\sigma_{n{\Gamma}}}\bra{u^\sigma_{n{\Gamma}}}.

(76)

In the case of the single-point metric, analogously
[
32
]
:

P_{\Gamma}\equiv\sum^{N}_{n}\ket{u_{n{\Gamma}}}\bra{u_{n{\Gamma}}}.

(77)

In the above,
\ket{u_{n{\Gamma}}}
and
\ket{u^\sigma_{n{\Gamma}}}
denote the
\Gamma
-point eigenstates and spin-eigenstates, respectively, i.e., all the occupied zero-momentum (spin-)eigenstates within a supercell BZ shrunk to a point in the aperiodic limit defined in the presence of disorder, as introduced in Refs.
[
33
,
34
]
. The PBC formulation of the markers with the above projectors is consistent with the single-point formulation of topological invariants, as introduced in Ref.
[
33
]
for the standard Chern numbers.

Furthermore, to characterize spin topology and spin-resolved quantum geometry under disorder, we define the single-point (spin-)metric estimators, consistently with the single-point Chern and spin-Chern invariants
[
33
,
34
]
. We demonstrate that the single-point metric estimators satisfy the geometric bounds even in the presence of disorder, demonstrating the robustness of the spin-geometric bound up to the spin-gap closing transition. The single-point spin-resolved metric reads:

[g^{\sigma}_{ij}]_{\text{sp}}=\text{Tr}_{\bf r}[P^{\sigma}_{\Gamma}\{P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{i}},P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{j}}\}],

(78)

with the single-point quantum metric estimator:

[g_{ij}]_{\text{sp}}=\text{Tr}_{\bf r}[P_{\Gamma}\{P_{{\bf b}_{i}},P_{{\bf b}_{j}}\}],

(79)

and the single-point spin-Chern invariant
[
34
]
satisfying a lower bound on the employed single-point metrics defined above:

[C^{\sigma}_{s}]_{\text{sp}}=4\pi~\mathfrak{Im}~\text{Tr}_{\bf r}[P^{\sigma}_{\Gamma}[P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{1}},P^{\sigma}_{{\bf b}_{2}}]].

(80)

The single-point bounds for the disordered systems can be expressed as,

\text{Tr}~[g^{\sigma}_{ij}]_{\text{sp}}\geq 2\pi[C^{\sigma}_{s}]_{\text{sp}},

(81)

for the single-point spin-resolved quantum metric, and for the single-point quantum metric,

N_{\text{occ}}\text{Tr}~[g_{ij}]_{\text{sp}}\geq 2\pi\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}[C^{\sigma}_{s}]_{\text{sp}}.

(82)

The corresponding proofs follow by the same logical argument, analogously to the proof of inequality Eq. (
70
), on connecting the PBC projectors to the momentum-space projectors within the approximations of Refs.
[
33
,
34
,
31
]
.

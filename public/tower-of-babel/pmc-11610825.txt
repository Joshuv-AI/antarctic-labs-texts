Comparison of the effectiveness of ferrous ascorbate with ferrous sulphate in improving haemoglobin percentage among ante-natal mothers in rural field practice area of a medical college
Authors: S L Manojna Govindaraju, Padmasri Yalamanchili, Kesava Lakshmi Prasad Kandipudi, Sarada Vadlamani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Anaemia in pregnancy is defined by the World Health Organisation (WHO) as a haemoglobin concentration of less than 11 g/dl in venous blood. The prevalence of anaemia among ante-natal mothers in India is 50.3%[3]. The government of India supplies ferrous sulphate for prophylaxis and treatment of anaemia among ante-natal mothers for free through the reproductive maternal neonatal and child health + adolescent (RMNCH+A) programme. However, patient compliance is poor due to gastrointestinal disturbances (of all registered pregnancies, less than 1/3 consumed a full course of ferrous sulphate). Previous studies in different settings have shown increased compliance and higher mean haemoglobin levels with ferrous ascorbate, which is more affordable than other commercially available compounds.Objective:Hence, the current study was done to compare the effectiveness of ferrous ascorbate with ferrous sulphate in improving haemoglobin percentage among ante-natal mothers in a rural field practice area of a medical college.Methods:An interventional community-based study was conducted in the rural field practice area of Andhra Medical College. The study was done among 76 registered (registered at Rural Primary Health Centre Simhachalam) ante-natal mothers in gestational age 13–17 weeks with haemoglobin concentration of 7–10.9 g/dl (mild to moderate anaemia) after taking informed consent. The study was performed for two years. Ante-natal mothers who were registered but unavailable during the study period, with a medical history of haematological disorders, chronic cardiac and kidney diseases, known or suspected to have sensitivity to iron compounds, with known thyroid dysfunction or mental illness were excluded from the study.Results:There was a rise in haemoglobin among the entire study population, which was statistically significant (P < 0.0001). There was a higher rise in haemoglobin among the antenatal women who took ferrous ascorbate than among those who took ferrous sulphate (mean difference = 0.344 gm/dl) after 12 weeks of follow-up, which was statistically significant (P = 0.014).Conclusion:The study compared the effectiveness of ferrous ascorbate with ferrous sulphate in improving haemoglobin percentage among antenatal mothers in a medical college’s rural field practice area. There was a significant rise in haemoglobin percentage among the study group, which was on ferrous ascorbate with better compliance and fewer gastrointestinal side effects.

Introduction

Anaemia is a condition in which the number and size of red blood cells, or haemoglobin (Hb) concentration, falls below an established cut-off value, consequently impairing the blood’s capacity to transport oxygen around the body.[1] In pregnancy, World Health Organisation (WHO) defines anaemia as a Hb concentration of less than 11 g/dl in venous blood.Click or tap here to enter text.[2] The prevalence of anaemia among ante-natal mothers in India is 50.3%.[3] Women with maternal anaemia are more likely to deliver preterm and have low-birth-weight offspring with low foetal iron stores, leading to impaired cognitive development. Severe anaemia can lead to complications during delivery, including increased blood loss, cardiac failure, and maternal mortality. Oral iron supplementation has been recognised as an effective prevention and treatment of iron deficiency anaemia. To combat anaemia, the National Nutritional Anaemia Prophylaxis Programme was launched by the maternal and child health (MCH) division of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MOHFW) in 1970 to prevent nutritional anaemia among mothers and children. Under this programme, iron folic acid (IFA) supplementation was given to pregnant women, lactating mothers, acceptors of family planning, and children aged 1–5 years. MOHFW expanded the programme in 1991 to include universal supplementation of IFA for pregnant and lactating women. Despite these guidelines, the high prevalence of anaemia persists. The National Iron Plus Initiative (NIPI) was introduced in 2013 to reduce the incidence and prevalence of iron deficiency anaemia across all life stages.[4] Healthcare providers at all levels including primary healthcare workers have been identified under the NIPI framework to provide a comprehensive package of the continuum of care and management of anaemia.[5] To reduce the prevalence of anaemia among young children (6–59 months), adolescents, women of reproductive age groups (15–49 years), and pregnant and lactating women, the Anaemia Mukt Bharat program has been designed. Anaemia Mukt Bharat has been built upon the existing framework of NIPI with a particular focus on intensive behaviour change communication, vulnerable geographies, procurement and supply chain management issues and others. The critical interventions proposed to be carried out in this programme are IFA supplementation and de-worming across all age groups. Despite all these efforts, compliance with ferrous sulphate was poor due to gastrointestinal disturbances (of the 89.3% of pregnancies registered only 30.3% consumed a full course of ferrous sulphate).[6] Andhra Pradesh reported that only 41.1% of ante-natal mothers consumed a full course of ferrous sulphate.[7] Major barriers to IFA intake were indicated by many studies as gastrointestinal side effects and a lack of comprehensive counselling by healthcare providers.[68] Among the various oral iron supplements available on the market, ferrous ascorbate showed high iron bioavailability and, therefore, emerged as one of the highly prescribed iron preparations and was also more affordable than other commercially available compounds. The incorporation of ascorbic acid helps to convert ferric ions into easily absorbable ferrous forms in the duodenum. Several studies have reported the efficacy of ferrous ascorbate in treating anaemia across all age groups.[9] Previous studies in different settings[4101112] have shown increased compliance and more Hb increases with ferrous ascorbate. Hence, this study was done among ante-natal mothers attending subcentres in rural field practice areas of a medical college to compare the effectiveness of ferrous ascorbate with ferrous sulphate.

Methods

Study design: An interventional community-based study was conducted.

Study setting: This study was conducted in a rural field practice area of a medical college.

Study population: Ante-natal mothers registered in the sub-centres under the rural health centre were included.

Study period: This study was conducted over a two-year duration.

Inclusion criteria: Ante-natal mothers who were registered during the study period with gestational age 13–17 weeks with Hb percentage of 7–10.9 g/dl [mild and moderate anaemia] were included.

Exclusion criteria: Ante-natal mothers who were registered but unavailable during the study period, with a medical history of haematological disorders, chronic cardiac and kidney diseases, known or suspected to have a sensitivity to iron compounds, known thyroid dysfunction or mental illness were excluded.

Study tool: A pre-designed pretested questionnaire and Hb estimation were done by a haemoglobinometer using Sahli’s method.

Method of data collection: The study was conducted after obtaining institutional ethics committee clearance and approval from the respective authorities (medical officer of Rural Primary Health Centre Simhachalam). A list of ante-natal mothers registered at all 12 sub-centres of the rural health centre was obtained. Ante-natal mothers meeting inclusion criteria were shortlisted. Antenatal mothers from sub-centres where the population largely sought government healthcare were included in the control group to prevent private prescription confounding factors. The remaining sub-centres were included in the study group. After taking informed consent, all eligible ante-natal mothers were given either ferrous sulphate or ferrous ascorbate (depending upon in which subcentre area they reside) until the sample size was achieved. Ferrous ascorbate was given for the study group and ferrous sulphate was continued for the control group. Ferrous ascorbate (100 mg ferrous ascorbate and 500 mcg folic acid) was procured from a local pharmacy. One hundred and eighty tablets were distributed free of cost to every ante-natal mother in the study group and it was advised to consume one tablet per day. Ante-natal mothers from the control group received ferrous sulphate (100 mg ferrous sulphate and 500 mcg folic acid) free of cost, through the reproductive maternal neonatal and child health + adolescent (RMNCH+A) programme and were advised to consume two tablets per day. Both the study and control groups were motivated to consume iron tablets daily. Ante-natal mothers are followed for 12 weeks. Hb was estimated at baseline at four weeks, eight weeks and 12 weeks. A questionnaire to test compliance was adopted from a study done by Molla T et al.[13] It was administered to ante-natal mothers of both groups towards the end of the study.

Statistical analysis: An unpaired t-test and Chi-square test were used to compare the data between the two groups at: ‘P’ is < 0.05 and 95% confidence interval (CI).

Standard operating definitions

Anaemia: Anaemia in pregnancy was defined as Hb concentration less than 11 g/dl.[14] Mild anaemia has a Hb value of 10.9–10 g/dl, and moderate anaemia has a Hb value of 9.9–7 g/dl.

Compliance: Consuming at least 13 doses in the recent two weeks was considered good compliance. Consuming 12 or fewer doses in the recent two weeks was considered poor compliance.[15]

Results

The present study aims to identify the mean difference between improved Hb in two groups of ante-natal mothers who received ferrous ascorbate and ferrous sulphate, respectively. A total of 72 ante-natal mothers were given iron and folic acid after giving consent. A group of 36 ante-natal mothers received ferrous ascorbate (study group), and another group of 36 ante-natal mothers received ferrous sulphate (control group). These two groups were followed up for 12 weeks. Initially, socio-demographic characteristics, food habits and initial Hb were recorded. During follow-up, Hb was recorded thrice at four-week intervals. Side effects and compliance were recorded towards the end of the study.

The mean age of the study population was 22.6 + 2.47 years, ranging from 19–31 years. The median age was 22 years. There was no significant difference between age, parity, socioeconomic status, education, food habits, and income in both the study and control groups [Table 1].

Discussion

The average age of the 72 participants in this study is 22.6 (+2.47) years, which is consistent with previous studies by Radha Kumari Paladugu et al.[16] and Mithra et al.,[15] who found mean ages of 25.8 (+4.1) years.

The majority of the study population consumed a mixed diet (both non-vegetarian and vegetarian) (95.83%), comparable to the study by Mithra P et al.,[15] where most of the study population (84.7%), consumed a mixed diet. Food habits of the study population in the present study showed no significant difference between the study and control groups (Chi-square = 0.348, P = 0.555).

Sixty-one percent of the control group had good compliance in the present study, which was comparable to findings in the study by Mithra P et al.[15] and Getachew et al.,[17] where 64.7% of the ante-natal mothers adhered to ferrous sulphate therapy. In a study by Gereklioglu et al.,[18] only 40.6% had good compliance with ferrous sulphate.

Compliance for ferrous ascorbate

The majority of the study group (83.3%) did not experience any side effects, whereas most of the control group (91.7%) experienced side effects. In a study by Adsul et al.,[12] 71.1% of ante-natal mothers consuming ferrous ascorbate had not experienced any side effects. In a study done by Anil Bilimale et al.,[19] 49.2% of the study population experienced nausea, and 41% experienced heartburn (consuming ferrous sulphate). In the present study, they were 36.1% and 13.9%, respectively. Another study done by Tolkien et al.[20] reported that 11% of those consuming ferrous sulphate experienced nausea (36.1% in the current study) and 12% experienced constipation (19.4% in the present study).

There was a significant difference between the side effects experienced by both the study and control groups (Chi-square = 40.783, P = 0.000) in the present study.

In a study by Panchal PJ et al.,[21] 37.2% of the study group consuming ferrous ascorbate experienced side effects, and 62.7% of the control group consuming ferrous sulphate experienced side effects which were 8.3% and 83.3%, respectively, in the present study.

In terms of adverse effects, 18.1% of the study group suffered constipation, 10% experienced heartburn, and 16% experienced nausea, compared to 2.8%, 2.8%, and 5.6% in the current study, respectively. In the control group, 20.2% experienced constipation, and 16% had heartburn. Thirty-one percent reported nausea, which was similar to 19.4%, 13.9%, and 36.1%. The present study found a significant difference in the mean rise of Hb among the study group compared to the control group (P = 0.014). Similar results were observed with various studies.[101222]

Singhal et al.[22] compared various iron salts along with ferrous ascorbate against ferrous sulphate, where ferrous ascorbate showed a significant rise in Hb against ferrous sulphate (P = 0.024). Yewale et al.[23] compared ferrous ascorbate and colloidal iron. Kriplani et al.[24] inferred that ferrous ascorbate and bis-glycinate were more effective and better tolerated than ferrous sulphate. The above studies, including the present study, concluded that ferrous ascorbate significantly improves Hb percentages compared to other iron salts.

Conclusion

The study compared the effectiveness of ferrous ascorbate with ferrous sulphate in improving Hb percentage among antenatal mothers in the rural field practice area of a medical college. There was a significant rise in Hb percentage among the study group, which was on ferrous ascorbate with better compliance and fewer gastrointestinal side effects.

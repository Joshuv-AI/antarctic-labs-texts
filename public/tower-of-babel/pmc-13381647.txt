A multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data collection method for substations based on cloud-edge collaboration and artificial intelligence
Authors: Jing Wang, Yongbo Zhou, Ning Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Real-time collection of operation and maintenance data enables timely identification and resolution of potential faults, thereby ensuring the safe and stable operation of power grids. However, achieving both real-time and secure data acquisition remains a persistent challenge in this field. This paper proposes a cloud-edge collaboration and artificial intelligence-based method for collecting multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data in substations. At the terminal layer, substation equipment terminals gather multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data and transmit them as messages via diverse communication protocols to the nearest edge server. At the edge layer, the received data are aggregated according to the optimal edge server determined by the cloud layer, thereby completing the collection of massive operation and maintenance data. In the cloud layer, the aggregation task offloading unit employs an artificial intelligence-based Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm. With the objective of minimizing total delay and total energy consumption, the algorithm derives data aggregation task offloading decisions to ensure the efficient execution of operation and maintenance data collection tasks. Experimental results show that the proposed method exhibits relatively small fluctuation amplitudes in both uplink and downlink communication links, indicating stable data transmission speed and quality. The maximum latency is approximately 3.4 s and 2.4 s, respectively, while data integrity remains between 94 and 98%, and resource utilization stays between 5 and 9%, confirming its practical effectiveness.

Introduction

With the rapid development of smart grids, substations, as the core nodes of power systems, are facing a substantial increase in both the complexity and data volume of their operation and maintenance management. Operation and maintenance data encompasses not only the real-time monitoring, fault warning, and performance evaluation of electrical equipment but also spans multiple dimensions such as environmental monitoring, safety management, and energy efficiency analysis1–3. These multi-source heterogeneous data resources provide a rich information base for substation operation and maintenance management, yet they also introduce significant challenges in data processing and integration. Multi-source heterogeneous operation and maintenance data originate from diverse sources, including sensors, monitoring systems, and intelligent devices, each employing different data formats4,5. The data types are varied, covering both structured data (e.g., equipment parameters and operational logs) and unstructured data (e.g., video, audio, and images). This diversity leads to complex interdependencies among data sources, requiring effective fusion and analysis to extract actionable insights. Traditional methods for collecting multi-source heterogeneous operation and maintenance data often suffer from limitations such as inefficiency, low accuracy, and poor scalability.

However, existing studies have not fully addressed the following critical challenges in substation O&M data collection:

Real-time performance: Traditionalmethods often fail to meet the low-latency requirements of real-time O&M data acquisition, particularly under high-concurrency scenarios.

Heterogeneity handling: The absence of unified protocols and data formats results in inefficient data fusion and analysis.

Scalability: Current solutions frequently lack the ability to dynamically adapt to the increasing data volume and device diversity in substations.

Energy efficiency: High computational overhead on edge devices constrains the sustainability of data collection systems.

In recent years, scholars worldwide have conducted extensive research on this topic. For instance, Qian et al. investigated wireless communication technology and explored an innovative solution that leveraged its efficiency and flexibility8. This technology simplified the data acquisition process from devices and enhanced the system’s adaptability and scalability. Their work employed wireless communication to collect data from secondary equipment in substations, covering aspects such as the project overview, deployment of data collection nodes, design of the wireless communication network, and real-time data transmission and processing. While this approach offered flexibility and convenience, as the number of data collection nodes increased, traditional wireless access mechanisms may experience channel congestion. This could lead to reduced network throughput, challenges in meeting the concurrent and real-time transmission demands of massive operation and maintenance data, and ultimately a degradation of overall system performance in practical industrial environments. Li et al. proposed a trusted computing-based method for network security data collection in substation monitoring systems9. First, a trusted computing environment was deployed within the monitoring system to establish a trustworthy computing platform, ensure secure application behavior, and create a reliable network communication environment, thereby guaranteeing the security and credibility of the monitoring system’s applications and probes. Second, digital certificates and national cryptographic algorithms were utilized to authenticate both communication parties, ensuring the legality of communication between monitoring system probes and network security monitoring devices. Finally, by verifying the integrity of the collected data, the method enhanced the reliability and accuracy of data collection. However, establishing and maintaining a trusted computing environment, together with frequent identity authentication and integrity verification, demanded substantial computational resources (e.g., CPU and memory), which could impose a significant additional computational burden on data collection terminals and monitoring devices. Chen et al. introduced an Internet of Things (IoT)-based data acquisition system for substation battery equipment10. By integrating mobile work terminals and QR code tags to connect batteries with the data acquisition system, and applying online speech recognition technology to transcribe various detection data, the system achieved battery data synchronization and efficient input into the lean management system for equipment operation and maintenance. Nevertheless, the speed and accuracy of data entry via mobile work terminals and speech recognition heavily relied on the operational proficiency and current working state of on-site personnel. Factors such as fatigue, accent variations, and background noise could introduce input errors or reduce efficiency, making standardization and scaling challenging. Event-driven wireless sensor networks relied on the triggering of operation and maintenance events to transmit data. In some cases, frequent event triggered or large volumes of transmitted data could lead to communication delays or network congestion, impairing the real-time performance of data collection. Cav et al. aimed to achieve comprehensive and efficient collection of multi-source heterogeneous O&M data in substations by optimizing coverage holes in wireless sensor networks11. Their coverage hole optimization strategy ensured that all critical equipment and operation and maintenance scenarios were monitored without omission or redundancy by deploying sensor nodes reasonably, thereby improving the integrity and accuracy of data collection. Moreover, in combination with the multi-hop communication mechanism of wireless sensor networks, reliable transmission and convergence of massive operation and maintenance data were achieved. However, in wireless sensor networks, communication between nodes could be affected by factors such as signal attenuation and multipath effects. These interferences may degrade the reliability and stability of data transmission, thus compromising data acquisition quality. Bentert et al. achieved continuous and efficient collection of multi-source heterogeneous O&M data in substations by utilizing the long-term endurance and stability of low-power wireless sensor networks12. By optimizing energy consumption management, data transmission mechanisms, and data processing algorithms for sensor nodes, their method significantly reduced system energy consumption and extended the service life of the sensor network while maintaining data collection quality. Additionally, considering the characteristics of multi-source heterogeneous data, the method also enabled intelligent classification, fusion, and compression of data, further improving the efficiency and accuracy of data collection. However, low-power wireless sensor networks often needed to balance data processing and transmission, which could increase transmission latency. Cloud-edge collaboration integrated cloud computing and edge computing by deploying computing resources at the network edge, thereby achieving efficient, real-time, and low-latency data processing13.

Although the aforementioned methods have achieved certain results in specific scenarios, they still exhibit several common limitations: wireless communication technologies are prone to channel congestion as the number of nodes increases; trusted computing solutions demand substantial computational resources; IoT-based data input relies on manual operations and is susceptible to personnel conditions and environmental interference; event-driven sensor networks may suffer from reduced real-time performance under high-frequency triggering; and low-power wireless sensor networks often necessitate a trade-off between data processing and transmission, making it difficult to simultaneously guarantee low latency and high energy efficiency. These limitations hinder existing methods from achieving an effective balance among real-time performance, energy efficiency, scalability, and data integrity when addressing the challenges of multi-source, heterogeneous, and massively concurrent operation and maintenance data collection in substations.

Therefore, this paper proposes a multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data collection method for substations based on cloud-edge collaboration and artificial intelligence (AI). This method dynamically allocates data collection, preprocessing, and aggregation tasks to optimal nodes by constructing a three-tier collaborative architecture consisting of the terminal, edge, and cloud layers. Furthermore, it introduces an artificial intelligence algorithm, such as the particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm, to make task offloading decisions aimed at minimizing total latency and energy consumption, thereby achieving efficient resource scheduling. The cloud-edge collaboration mechanism can significantly reduce data transmission and processing latency and improve system response speed, while the introduction of AI enhances the system’s adaptability and optimization capability in heterogeneous data and dynamic environments. This approach consequently improves the overall performance and scalability of the collection system while ensuring data integrity and security. Acquisition of multi-source heterogeneous massive O&M data in substations

Technical architecture of a substation multi‑source heterogeneous O&M Data acquisition method

With the continuous advancement of substation technology, relying solely on a standalone cloud computing center can no longer meet the requirements of low-latency tasks. Data terminals, edge computing centers, and cloud centers are progressively forming a new paradigm of “terminal-edge-cloud” integration. Cloud-edge collaboration can provide low-latency, low-energy-consumption, and high-reliability computational support for multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data collection services. Multi-source heterogeneous communication protocols refer to the diverse communication standards and formats adopted by various devices at the terminal layer of substations, such as Modbus, IEC 61,850, MQTT, and CoAP. These protocols are representative in industrial and power systems: Modbus is a serial communication protocol widely used in industrial control systems, supporting both RTU and TCP modes; IEC 61850 is an international standard in the field of power system automation, which supports object-oriented modeling and service mapping; MQTT is a lightweight publish/subscribe messaging protocol suitable for IoT scenarios; and CoAP is a web transfer protocol specifically designed for resource-constrained devices. To achieve protocol interoperability on edge servers, the system employs protocol adaptation middleware, which incorporates multiple built-in protocol stacks and supports dynamic loading and protocol recognition. When a device terminal is connected, the middleware identifies the protocol type based on message characteristics and then invokes the corresponding parser to convert the raw data into a unified internal data model. Additionally, the middleware provides protocol mapping functionality to enable cross-protocol data exchange and semantic consistency. Through this mechanism, edge servers can seamlessly integrate and collaboratively process operational data from different protocols, thereby enhancing system compatibility and scalability. These protocols differ at the physical, data link, and application layers, resulting in variations in data formats, transmission rates, and response mechanisms. The proposed method achieves unified parsing and conversion of protocols on the edge server side through protocol adaptation middleware. This middleware supports dynamic protocol stack loading and protocol recognition, can automatically match the protocol type used by device terminals, and converts the data into a unified internal format, thereby achieving seamless access and integration of heterogeneous data. This mechanism not only improves system compatibility and scalability but also provides a structurally consistent data foundation for subsequent data aggregation and task offloading. Accordingly, a cloud-edge collaborative method for collecting multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data in substations is designed, and the technical architecture of this method is illustrated in Fig. 1.Fig. 1Technical architecture of the multi-source heterogeneous massive O&M data acquisition method for substations.

The technical architecture is divided into three layers. The bottom layer is the terminal layer, which is primarily responsible for collecting multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data from sensors connected to substation equipment. It packages these data into message format and transmits them to a designated edge server using multi-source heterogeneous communication protocols. Simultaneously, the equipment terminal can receive control commands for substation terminal equipment sent from the edge server or cloud computing center and forward them to the designated substation terminal equipment. Within the terminal layer, the monitoring terminal monitors the operational status of various electrical equipment in the substation in real time, including operation and maintenance data such as voltage, current, power, and temperature14,15. The protection terminal protects the electrical equipment in the substation16 from damage and accidents caused by electrical faults, including operation and maintenance data such as tripping and isolation. The automation terminal is responsible for achieving automatic control and management of equipment within the substation, covering operation and maintenance data such as automatic voltage regulation, automatic closing, and automatic opening. The cross-acquisition terminal performs real-time acquisition of analog signals in the power grid, such as analog operation and maintenance data including power, harmonics, demand, and phase angle. The remote pulse terminal collects pulse signals, such as the pulse output signals from electric energy meters17.

The edge server belongs to the edge layer, which primarily implements functions including accessing terminal equipment; computing, processing, and transmitting multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data; integrating these data into the cloud computing platform; and sending control information to terminal equipment. After receiving data frames containing operation and maintenance data sent by equipment terminals, the edge server is responsible for performing subsequent operations such as protocol analysis, out-of-limit judgment, and data upload. When an out-of-limit event occurs, the edge server also transmits the event information to the cloud computing center for subsequent processing. Since different edge servers receive operation and maintenance data uploaded by different equipment terminals and possess varying computational capabilities, the optimal edge server or cloud computing center must be selected via task offloading from the cloud computing center to perform data aggregation operations, thereby ensuring low latency in processing multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data. To determine the “optimal” edge server for data aggregation, the cloud layer evaluates multiple real-time criteria in an integrated manner. The primary metrics include: (1) current latency between the candidate edge server and the data sources, as well as the expected processing delay based on its computational load; (2) resource utilization (CPU, memory, bandwidth) to avoid overloading any single node; (3) link quality and stability, measured by packet loss rate and jitter; (4) energy efficiency of the edge node, especially for battery-powered or energy-constrained deployments; and (5) task-specific requirements, such as the computational complexity of the aggregation operation and the volume of data to be processed. These metrics are continuously monitored by the cloud-based cluster-status module and are fed into the AI-based offloading algorithm to generate a weighted, adaptive score for each candidate server. The server with the highest overall score—balancing low latency, high resource availability, and reliable connectivity—is selected as the optimal target for the current aggregation task. When the multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data of the substation have been transmitted to the optimal edge server or cloud computing center, data alignment is performed first, followed by different data aggregation operationsaccording to different task requirements. To handle synchronization issues caused by multi-source heterogeneous data streams, the system employs a timestamp-based synchronization mechanism coupled with a buffer management strategy at the edge layer. Each data packet is tagged with a high-precision timestamp at the source terminal. Upon arrival at the edge server, timestamps are normalized using a global time reference provided by Network Time Protocol (NTP) or Precision Time Protocol (PTP). In case of communication delay fluctuations across different protocols, the edge server utilizes a dynamic buffering window to temporarily store incoming data, aligns them based on their timestamps, and performs temporal interpolation if necessary, thereby maintaining temporal consistency before aggregation.

First, the substation multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data uploaded to the designated optimal edge server or cloud computing center are partitioned, and the classification of substation operation and maintenance equipment and the collection time of operation and maintenance data are aligned temporally and spatially. Second, according to different task requirements, the aligned multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data of the substation are aggregated. Data aggregation primarily includes data statistics and out-of-limit judgment. Data statistics involve calculating the maximum, minimum, average, and accumulated values of a parameter within a certain area or across different equipment terminals of the same level simultaneously. Out-of-limit judgment involves determining whether a parameter from different equipment terminals in a certain area or at the same level is abnormal at the same time. In addition, the edge server receives remote control commands from the superior cloud computing center and sends designated remote control messages to the corresponding equipment terminals via multi-source heterogeneous communication protocols to control the operation and maintenance equipment of the substation.

The cloud computing center belongs to the cloud layer and is primarily responsible for monitoring cluster status, offloading aggregation tasks, and managing container deployment. It persistently stores the multi-source heterogeneous massive O&M data reported by edge servers and performs complex data analysis, while also sharing the collected results with edge servers. Cluster status monitoring comprehensively tracks the operational status of each cluster within the cloud computing center, including resource usage, performance, and security, and presents this information to operation and maintenance personnel through charts, alarms, and other forms. Container deployment management handles the container lifecycle, covering creation, deployment, upgrade, migration, and destruction. Aggregation task offloading employs the Artificial Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm from the family of artificial intelligence algorithms to offload multi-source heterogeneous massive O&M data aggregation tasks to the optimal edge server or cloud computing center, thereby reducing the delay and energy consumption of these data collection services and ensuring their efficient execution. These management functions within the cloud computing center work collaboratively to achieve efficient collection, processing, and storage of multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data for substations. They ensure the stable operation of the technical architecture through cluster status monitoring, enhance deployment efficiency and management flexibility through container deployment management, and optimize resource utilization and task execution efficiency through aggregation task offloading, thereby ensuring the real-time performance, accuracy, and reliability of multi-source heterogeneous massive O&M data collection.

The implementation process of the multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data collection method for substations is as follows:

Step 1: At the equipment terminal, collect multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data from specific substation equipment, and transmit the collected data in message format to the nearest edge server via multi-source heterogeneous communication protocols.

Step 2: The edge server identifies the protocol of the message transmitted by the equipment terminal. If the protocol is successfully recognized, it performs protocol analysis and out-of-limit data judgment directly on the edge server and returns the result to the substation equipment terminal. For data identified as out-of-limit, an event is generated and sent to the cloud computing center. For multi-source heterogeneous massive O&M data that are within normal limits, data alignment is performed on the edge server. After data alignment, the data aggregation task offloading strategy is determined using the artificial particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm, and the optimal edge server is selected to perform further data aggregation, which includes statistical analysis of data results and out-of-limit judgment. The out-of-limit data events and the data aggregation results are then sent together to the cloud computing center.

Step 3: If the current edge server lacks the corresponding protocol stack and cannot parse the message sent by the substation equipment terminal, the edge server requests the cloud computing center to download the required protocol stack and returns the unparsable message to the equipment terminal. After the edge server completes downloading the protocol stack, the device terminal re-accesses, and subsequent protocol analysis and out-of-limit data judgment operations are performed. Subsequently, according to the PSO algorithm, the offloading strategy for data aggregation tasks is determined, selecting the optimal edge server or cloud computing center to perform further data aggregation, including statistical analysis of data results and out-of-limit judgment. The out-of-limit data events and data aggregation results are sent to the cloud computing center, thereby completing the multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data collection for the substation.

Design of the edge server

As the core component of the multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data collection architecture for the entire substation, the edge server is implemented based on a containerized scheme that runs edge computing applications and provides an edge computing base. As shown in Fig. 2, the overall structure of the edge server is divided into four main layers: the data distribution layer, the data processing layer, the data exchange layer, and the connection layer.Fig. 2Overall structure of the edge server.

Overall structure of the edge server.

Thespecific structure is as follows: on one hand, it provides an external management interface to enable the cloud computing center to connect to the edge server and manage and control the edge server nodes; on the other hand, it offers interfaces or software development kits (SDKs) for substation terminal equipment access, supporting the connection of substation terminal equipment to edge servers, uploading the collected multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data, and receiving equipment control commands issued by the cloud computing center. To support the above functions, edge servers need to be deployed on dedicated hardware platforms that comply with industry standards. Typical configurations include: the computing unit is equipped with a multi-core high-performance industrial-grade CPU with a clock frequency of ≥ 2.0 GHz to meet the real-time computing demands of protocol parsing, data aggregation, and lightweight AI inference; it is equipped with ≥ 16 GB of DDR4 ECC memory to ensure stability during multi-tasking parallel processing; industrial-grade solid-state drives (SSDs) with a capacity of ≥ 256 GB are used for system and intermediate data storage and support expansion to terabyte (TB)-level hard drives or NVMe storage cards for caching high-frequency collected data. The network interface has multi-mode network adaptation capability, including: Gigabit/10 Gigabit Ethernet ports (≥ 4), used to connect to the local area network within the station and upload data to the cloud; serial communication interfaces (RS-485/RS-232), supporting traditional industrial protocols such as Modbus; and wireless communication modules (such as LoRa, ZigBee, or 4G/5G), used to access wireless sensor networks. The industrial characteristics incorporate a reinforced chassis design, supporting wide-temperature operation from − 40 °C to + 70 °C, with a protection rating of IP40 or above, and equipped with vibration and electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) certification to adapt to the complex electromagnetic environment of substations. It supports dual redundant power input (AC/DC) and includes a built-in uninterruptible power supply (UPS) module to ensure continuous operation during grid fluctuations or brief power outages. It provides PCIe or M.2 expansion slots, supporting the installation of FPGA acceleration cards or AI coprocessing units, and supports Docker container virtualization to achieve secure isolation and flexible deployment of edge applications. The internal components of the edge server, container applications, and the edge server itself are interconnected to forward commands, data, messages, and events through a message bus18.

The connection layer is responsible for providing drivers for the edge server, including serial port drivers, power line carrier drivers, and low-power wireless drivers. The serial port driver serves as a communication bridge within the edge server, enabling interaction with various serial terminal devices in the substation and facilitating real-time data acquisition, transmission, and control. The power line carrier driver allows the edge server to communicate with terminal devices connected to the substation via power lines, thereby collecting multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data from these devices or sending control commands. The low-power wireless driver enables the edge server to communicate with wireless sensors deployed in or around the substation and to wirelessly collect massive multi-source heterogeneous operation and maintenance data from these sensors. This capability assists the cloud computing center in monitoring the operational status of the substation in real time, promptly identifying anomalies, and taking appropriate measures.

The message middleware in the data exchange layer is designed to achieve reliable transmission and distribution of multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data, ensuring efficient and stable data exchange between edge servers. The message middleware also provides message persistence, enabling the storage of multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data when the cloud computing center is offline and facilitating its subsequent transmission to the cloud computing center upon reconnection.

The edge application layer is responsible for providing the edge server with log microservices, local storage services, a rule engine, and extended computing services. These services work together to enable the collection, storage, processing, and analysis of multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data for the substation.

The data routing service in the data distribution layer is responsible for managing and directing the flow of multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data, while the metadata service manages, stores, and provides metadata that describes other multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data.

Cloud-edge collaboration and AI-based task offloading for multi‑source heterogeneous massive O&M data aggregation in substations

In the multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data collection architecture for substations, task offloading refers to the process of transferring data aggregation tasks—originally executed locally on substation equipment terminals—to other locations with more suitable computational resources based on specific decision-making strategies. A task offloading strategy based on artificial particle swarm optimization (PSO) dynamically evaluates the computing resources, network status, and task characteristics of terminals, edge servers, and cloud centers to achieve optimal task allocation. This strategy significantly improves system efficiency in the following aspects: reducing transmission latency by offloading computationally intensive tasks to the edge or cloud, thereby decreasing terminal processing time and avoiding local resource bottlenecks; optimizing energy consumption distribution by allocating tasks to nodes with sufficient or higher energy efficiency, extending the lifespan of terminal devices, and improving system sustainability; and enhancing system resilience by dynamically adjusting offloading targets during network fluctuations or high load on edge nodes to ensure the reliability and real-time performance of task execution. Specifically, after collecting multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data, substation equipment terminals generate corresponding data aggregation tasks19,20. However, the computational capability of the device terminal itself is limited. When the data volume is large or the computational complexity is high, executing these tasks locally may lead to increased latency and higher energy consumption, which affects the efficiency and real-time performance of data collection. Therefore, the task offloading mechanism becomes particularly important21–23. By comprehensively considering task characteristics and employing corresponding algorithms to formulate offloading decisions, the efficient and reliable execution of multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data collection tasks for substations is ensured.

The Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm is a swarm intelligence-based optimization method that simulates the collaborative behavior of particles in a multidimensional search space. This algorithm dynamically allocates tasks by emulating the social behavior of bird flocks or fish schools during a search. It constructs a cooperative optimization framework based on probability selection and neighborhood search, belonging to the category of computational intelligence metaheuristic algorithms. PSO can handle complex nonlinear and multimodal optimization problems and is applicable to various types of optimization objectives. In the decision-making problem of offloading multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data aggregation tasks in substations, due to the presence of multiple variables (such as different data aggregation tasks and different server resources) and complex constraints (such as energy consumption requirements and execution latency), PSO can adapt well to this complex environment. In the application scenario of this paper, the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm is employed to solve the task offloading decision problem for multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data aggregation in substations. Compared with traditional optimization algorithms, the proposed PSO-based approach incorporates a dynamic weight adjustment mechanism tailored for substation environments. This mechanism adapts the delay weight factor in real time according to the network load to achieve quality-of-service (QoS)-aware offloading decisions. To further enhance adaptability and robustness, the proposed PSO-based offloading model integrates a real-time feedback loop and robustness safeguards. The model continuously ingests live telemetry on network congestion, edge server load, and link quality. This real-time data dynamically adjusts the particle fitness evaluation, allowing the swarm to converge toward solutions optimized for current conditions, not merely historical patterns. To guard against significant deviations from training assumptions—such as sudden link failures or unforeseen data surges—the system employs multiple safeguards: (1) a fallback mechanism that defaults to a conservative, latency-prioritized offloading rule if solution quality degrades beyond a threshold; (2) constraint-aware penalty terms in the fitness function that heavily penalize decisions violating critical resource limits or latency bounds; and (3) periodic model retriggering that re-runs the optimization at fixed intervals or upon detecting substantial environmental shifts, ensuring decisions remain near-optimal. Moreover, to address the computational scalability required for massive, real-time decision-making, the core PSO algorithm can be augmented with quantum-inspired optimization techniques. For instance, a Quantum-enhanced PSO (QPSO) variant, as demonstrated in related large-scale scheduling problems, leverages quantum superposition and entanglement to explore the solution space more efficiently. This integration can dramatically accelerate convergence speed and improve solution quality for high-dimensional offloading decisions involving hundreds of concurrent tasks and nodes, ensuring the optimization framework itself does not become a latency bottleneck in dynamic operational environments. The hybrid search strategy, which combines global exploration and local exploitation, integrated with the penalty function method to handle the memory constraints of Eq. These enhancements enable PSO to provide efficient decision support for the substation operation and maintenance data aggregation system when processing heterogeneous data streams in substations.

To achieve QoS-aware offloading, the proposed PSO-based decision mechanism incorporates the following key strategies.

In the offloading decision process, the PSO algorithm dynamically adjusts the weight coefficients of latency and energy consumption to achieve a balance between the two. Specifically, the algorithm introduces dynamic weight factors that are adaptively tuned based on real-time network load and task characteristics. During the initial stage, these dynamic weight factors are set to default values, indicating that latency and energy consumption carry equal importance in the decision-making process.

As tasks are executed and network conditions change, the PSO algorithm monitors network latency and device energy consumption data in real time. When network congestion intensifies, resulting in increased potential latency, the algorithm automatically raises the value of the dynamic weight factor, giving latency a greater weight in the decision-making process, thereby prioritizing the selection of edge servers or cloud centers with lower latency for task offloading. Conversely, when network conditions are favorable but device energy consumption is high, the algorithm reduces the value of the dynamic weight factor to diminish the influence of energy consumption on decisions and preferentially selects offloading targets with lower energy consumption.

Dynamic weight adaptation for multi-objective optimization: The objective function in PSO not only considers minimizing total latency and total energy consumption but also dynamically adjusts the weighting factors based on real-time network conditions and task priorities.

Task classification and priority queueing: Before offloading, data aggregation tasks are classified based on their QoS requirements. This classification is mapped to different priority levels within the PSO fitness evaluation. Higher-priority tasks receive a fitness “bonus” or are subjected to stricter latency constraints in the optimization model, ensuring they are preferentially scheduled to edge servers or cloud centers with sufficient resources and low-latency paths.

Real-time resource and link state monitoring: The cloud layer continuously monitors the resource utilization (CPU, memory, bandwidth) of edge servers and the quality (latency, packet loss) of communication links. This state information is fed as input parameters into the PSO algorithm. The “nectar source” quality (fitness value) is evaluated not only by the theoretical latency and energy models but also by the current actual load and link conditions of candidate offloading targets. This prevents overloading a single edge node and avoids routing tasks through congested or unstable links, thereby maintaining overall service quality.

Predictive proactive offloading: Leveraging historical data and simple trend analysis, the PSO framework can anticipate periods of high data generation or network congestion. It can proactively adjust offloading strategies—for example, by pre-emptively shifting some computational load from edge servers to the cloud center before the edge becomes saturated—to prevent QoS degradation. The integration of these QoS-aware mechanisms ensures that the proposed task offloading strategy is not merely resource-optimal but also service-intelligent. It enables the substation data collection system to differentiate between critical real-time monitoring data and less time-sensitive historical logs, applying appropriate offloading policies for each, which results in a more stable and reliable data collection service.

While the proposed PSO algorithm provides an effective and bio-inspired approach for task offloading optimization in substation data collection, it is worth noting that the field of evolutionary and derivative-free optimization has seen significant advancements in recent years. Several modern optimizers have demonstrated strong performance in complex, constrained, and high-dimensional search spaces, which could offer alternative or complementary strategies for similar cloud-edge scheduling problems.

For instance, Modified L-SHADE [A] enhances the success-history based adaptive differential evolution (SHADE) with linear population size reduction and archive mechanisms, showing improved convergence and robustness on real-parameter optimization benchmarks. Its adaptive parameter control could be beneficial in dynamically changing substation environments.

Forced Breeding Evolution (FBE) [B] introduces a breeding-based selection and mutation strategy that balances exploration and exploitation, which is particularly effective in multimodal and rugged landscapes. FBE’s structured breeding cycles might help escape local optima in large-scale offloading decision spaces.

CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy) [C] is a state-of-the-art evolutionary algorithm for continuous optimization, renowned for its invariance properties and efficient handling of ill-conditioned and non-separable problems. Its recent Python implementations make it accessible for real-world engineering optimizations, including resource-allocation tasks in edge-cloud systems.

These algorithms represent the ongoing progress in metaheuristic optimization and could serve as valuable benchmarks or extensions in future work. The choice of PSO in this study is motivated by its simplicity, efficiency in swarm-based search, and suitability for the formulated mixed-integer offloading model with latency-energy trade-offs. Nevertheless, exploring hybrid or adaptive variants that incorporate insights from the above methods remains a promising direction for enhancing decision-making in more dynamic and large-scale substation scenarios.

Compared with the classic Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm, whose time complexity is also related to the number of particles, tasks, and iterations, the proposed PSO algorithm retains a similar basic structure but incorporates dynamic weight adjustment and a hybrid search strategy. This makes it more adaptive to complex and dynamic substation environments, though it may introduce a slightly higher constant factor in the time complexity. In contrast, the previously mentioned Modified L-SHADE algorithm [A], which enhances the success-history based adaptive differential evolution, has a time complexity that mainly depends on the population size and the number of generations; its adaptive mechanisms may also lead to different performance characteristics in convergence speed and solution quality when compared with PSO. The Forced Breeding Evolution (FBE) algorithm [B], with its breeding-based selection and mutation strategy, has its own time complexity related to breedingcycles and population size, and its effectiveness in large-scale offloading decision spaces may differ from that of the PSO algorithm. The CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy) algorithm [C], known for its efficiency in continuous optimization, has a time complexity associated with covariance-matrix updates and the number of function evaluations; its performance in the discrete offloading decision problem considered in this study may not be as effective as that of the PSO algorithm, which is specifically designed for the formulated mixed-integer offloading model.

These factors can significantly impact system performance and should be considered in further refinements or deployment-specific adaptations:

Container startup overhead: The model assumes instantaneous task execution upon offloading. In practice, deploying a containerized aggregation task on an edge server or cloud instance involves non-negligible startup latency (typically 100 ms to several seconds), which depends on the image size, runtime configuration, and host resource contention. This overhead can affect the overall delay, especially for short-lived or frequently spawned tasks.

Multi-tenancy interference: Edge servers often host multiple concurrent services or tenants. Resource competition between co-located containers can lead to performance degradation and unpredictable execution times, violating the assumption of stable computational capacity. Incorporating interference-aware scheduling or resource reservation mechanisms would enhance model accuracy.

Retransmission dynamics and heterogeneous link quality: The current transmission rate parameters assume stable, loss-free links. In industrial settings, wireless links exhibit variable quality due to fading, interference, or mobility, leading to packet loss and retransmissions. This increases effective transmission delay and energy consumption, particularly for large data volumes. Integrating a loss-aware throughput model would better capture communication overhead.

Task deadlines and priority constraints: The optimization objective minimizes total delay and energy without explicit per-task deadline or priority constraints. In operational scenarios, certain data streams have stringent latency bounds, while others (e.g., historical logs) are delay-tolerant. Introducing deadline-aware penalty terms or priority-weighted objectives would ensure QoS differentiation and compliance with operational requirements.

Dynamic and heterogeneous edge node capabilities: The model treats edge servers as homogeneous in terms of computational capability and energy profile. Real deployments involve heterogeneous hardware (varying CPU architectures, accelerators like FPGAs, and power supplies). Furthermore, node capabilities may change dynamically due to thermal throttling, battery depletion in mobile units, or scheduled maintenance. A dynamic capability profile would improve offloading decision robustness.

Security and verification overhead: Trusted computing or cryptographic verification processes (e.g., data integrity checks, secure boot) introduce computational and temporal overhead that are not accounted for in the current energy and delay models. In security-sensitive substations, these overheads can be substantial and should be factored into task execution estimates.

To address these limitations in practical implementations, the proposed PSO-based offloading framework can be extended with the following enhancements: incorporate a container startup delay term τstart into the edge/cloud execution latency equations; use measured or predicted interference levels to adjust effective computational capacity; employ link-quality estimators to adapt transmission rates and include retransmission energy in Eijtr and Ejctr; introduce task priority classes and deadline constraints into the objective function via penalty methods or multi-objective optimization; integrate a real-time capability registry for edge nodes, updating computational and energy coefficients dynamically; and add a security-overhead module that quantifies verification costs for sensitive tasks. These refinements would bridge the gap between the idealized optimization model and the complex, dynamic reality of substation operation and maintenance data collection, leading to more resilient and deployable solutions.

Signal margin, which quantifies the difference between the actual signal level and the decision threshold, is another crucial parameter. The measurements show that the proposed method achieves a higher signal margin, which contributes to improved transmission reliability.

Among these, packet damage induced by electromagnetic interference is the dominant factor, accounting for approximately 50%-60% of the total loss. The greedy strategy serves as a non-AI baseline, while DE and CMA-ES represent state-of-the-art evolutionary algorithms. Ablation settings include: (a) replacing PSO with random offloading, where each task is randomly assigned to a terminal, edge, or cloud node; (b) using a fixed rule-based scheduler, e.g., offloading all tasks to the cloud. The comparison is performed on the same dataset (100 aggregation tasks) and the same hardware and network configuration. The experimental results show that the PSO algorithm outperforms the other algorithms in terms of total latency (2.67 s), total energy consumption (1.32 J), and energy-delay product (EDP, 3.52 kJ s). Compared with the greedy strategy, PSO achieves a reduction in EDP of about 22%, demonstrating its excellent performance in balancing latency and energy consumption. In contrast, although DE and CMA-ES also show good optimization effects, their overall performance remains slightly inferior to that of PSO. The random offloading strategy, due to the lack of an optimization mechanism, results in significantly higher total latency and energy consumption than the other algorithms. These experimental results validate the effectiveness and superiority of the PSO optimizer in the task of offloading multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data.

Conclusion

The multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data collection method for substations based on cloud-edge collaboration and artificial intelligence proposed in this study demonstrates significant advantages and application value in the field of substation operation and maintenance data acquisition. Experiments verify that the proposed method achieves real-time collection of multi-source heterogeneous massive operation and maintenance data from substations. With the rapid development of smart-grid and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies, substation operation and maintenance data acquisition faces increasing challenges and opportunities.

Study of cross-diffusion effects on unsteady magnetohydrodynamic hybrid nanofluid flow over an inclined vertical plate
Authors: Sunkanaboina Pradeep, P. Vijay Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study is conducted for the comparative analysis of unsteady magnetohydrodynamic flow and heat-mass transfer behaviour of two different hybrid nanofluids such as TiO2, Cu /Water and TiO2, Cu /Water + Ethylene glycol (50:50) over an inclined vertical porous plate subjected to uniform angled magnetic strength, thermal radiation, heat source/sink and coupled influence of Soret and Dufour effects. The flow is induced by buoyancy force, which is modelled using the nonlinear Boussinesq approximation. The equations that can govern the system are non-dimensionalized by using appropriate similarity transformations and dimensionless parameters. Multi regular Perturbation method is employed to get the solution. The various pertinent parameters involved in the system are considered for the analysis of their implications on the various profiles. The analysis of this study reveals that the velocity of the fluid is enhanced with higher values of permeability, buoyancy, Dufour and Soret parameters, whereas diminished with high values of magnetic field strength, magnetic field inclination, volume fraction of the nanoparticles and plate angle parameters. As the values of radiation, Dufour and volume fraction parameters increased, so does the temperature profile. Concentration profile is developed with high Soret number value. Moreover, the numerical values of engineering quantities, including the Skin friction coefficient and Nusselt number are presented in tabular form. The findings of this study are having applications relevant to microelectronics cooling, heat exchangers and battery thermal management systems.

Introduction

In the fields of heat transfer and fluid dynamics, the invention of nanofluids has received significantly more attention than conventional heat transfer fluids because of its enhanced stability and remarkable increase in thermal conductivity. A few years ago, nanofluid concept was coined by Choi [1]. In general, base fluids such as water, oils, organic liquids, lubricants, polymeric solutions, bio-fluids and other common fluids are low in thermal conductivity. Dispersing nanoparticles with diameter well below 100 nm in the base fluid results in a better enhancement in thermal conductivity of the fluid. In the nanofluid preparation, most often used nanoparticles are metal nanoparticles (Cu, Ag, Fe, Al, Ni), non-metals nanoparticles (carbon, graphite, carbon nanotubes), oxide nanoparticles (Al2O3, TiO2, ZnO, CuO, Fe2O3, SiO2), nitride nanoparticles (SiN, BN, AIN, S4, N4) and so on. Extensive research on nanofluids was focused over the past few decades, encompassing their preparation, characterization, modelling, convective and boiling heat transfer behaviour and diverse engineering applications. The thermal conductivity of fluids is strongly influenced by the size, shape, and intrinsic properties of the nanoparticles suspended in the base fluid. Some of the experimental results revealed that metal nanoparticles (e.g. Cu, Ag, Au) are good in thermal conductivity but weak at stability and chemical inertness. Metal oxide nanoparticles (e.g., Al2O3, CuO) are good in stability but exhibits low thermal conductivity. By this results it is clear that nanofluid with single nanoparticle will give either high thermal conductivity or better stability. Over time, the attention of researchers and scientists has shifted towards the innovation of hybrid nanofluids (HNFs) which is the mixture of more than one distinct kind of small size nanoparticle to the base fluid. Due to synergistic interactions between mixed nanoparticles, HNFs typically offer improved thermal properties compared to base fluids and nanofluids. The combination of metal and metal oxide nanoparticle in the base fluid for HNFs preparation will give synergistic effect of high thermal conductivity and stability. Iskandar et al. [2] explored a study on steady flow of HNF past a vertical surface which is embedded in the porous medium. Muneeshwaran et al. [3] conducted a review on HNF role on the heat transfer enhancement phenomena and presented challenges and future research directions related to the HNF. Rajesh et al. [4] presented a study on vertical plate for the comparison of different HNFs flow through the plate subjected to radiation, heat source and magnetic field effects with ramped wall temperature. Vijay et al. [5] examined the time dependent flow of incompressible HNF flow over oscillating disk with Ohmic heating and cross diffusion effects. Lisha et al. [6] conducted an analytical study on non-Newtonian Casson type HNF flow over upright plate with the help of Caputo Fractional Order Derivative. In their study, volume fraction of Gr and MWCNTs enhanced the temperature profile of the fluid and reverse pattern for velocity profile of the fluid. Keerthiga et al. [7] presented a numerical study on unsteady flow of magnetohydrodynamic Casson non-Newtonian HNF past vertical plate in the porous medium. Pattnaik et al. [8] conducted a study on different shape factor tri- HNF flow past a vertical infinite plate for the effective thermal properties in a permeable medium. Divya et al. [9] explored a theoretical examination on Williamson di- HNF flow via sheet in the presence of radiation, magnetic field and viscous dissipation. Pravin et al. [10] presented a study for the analysis of HNF flow over multi-layers using neural network concept. Salahuddin et al. [11] presented the flow evaluation of HNF through circular eccentric cylinders under expanding and contracting wall motion.

The study of the electrically conductive fluid (e.g., copper sulfate solution, molten lead, plasma, etc.) in the environment of magnetic strength is often referred as magnetohydrodynamics (MHD). Understanding the impact of magnetic forces on the velocity field and electrical conductivity of conducting fluids requires a combined analysis of electromagnetic theory and fluid dynamics. This integration forms the basis of MHD, which explains how the Lorentz force modifies flow behaviour, boundary-layer structure and energy transport in electrically conducting fluids. Noticeable research has been done on MHD with nanofluid and HNF due to its significant application on design of heat exchangers, power accelerators, cooling reactors, power pumps and so on. Raghunath et al. [12] proposed a study on time dependent flow of chemically reactive fluid through an infinite plate in the permeable medium with aligned magnetic strength. Nayan et al. [13] presented a water based HNF flow via a vertical plate embedded in porous medium in the environment of angled magnetic field effect with constant wall temperature boundary conditions. Ramana et al. [14] examined the Kuvshinski fluid flow through the inclined plate saturated in the porous medium for the implications of inclined MHD, radiation and Soret effects. Shankar et al. [15] conducted a numerical and theoretical study on MHD nanofluid flow with the impacts of thermal radiation, chemical reaction and heat source/sink past an exponentially accelerated plate by using implicit Crank Nicolson method. Choudhury et al. [16] presented an analytical study of MHD nanofluid flow past a semi-infinite vertical plate for the characteristics of heat and mass transfer with the presence of radiation, porous medium, heat source/sink and first order chemical reaction. Rao et al. [17] presented a numerical study on water based HNF through stretching sheet in the porous medium by thermal radiation, Ohmic heating, dissipation and applied magnetic strength effects with slip boundary conditions. Ramachandra et al. [18] presented a study on time dependent MHD Casson type fluid flow through an isothermal angled plate in the porous medium by the impacts of inclined magnetic strength, Ohmic heating and dissipation. Recently, Rudrappa et al. [19] examined the multi-layer flow analysis of mixed convective viscous nanofluid in the porous medium with magnetic strength, internal heat generation and radiation effects.

Dufour and Soret effects are cross diffusion effects that are involved in the coupled heat transfer and mass transfer phenomena by the free/natural convection of the fluid flow at the same time, which is called double diffusive convection. The mass flux phenomena developed by the thermal diffusion is known as Soret effect and heat flux influenced by the concentration gradients is referred as Dufour effect. In general, these two effects are neglected because their magnitude is relatively small compared to the contributions defined by Fourier’s and Fick’s laws. When there is a density variation in the flow regime, these impacts are considerable. For instance, when a lighter or heavier species are introduced at the surface, buoyancy forces arise because of the density difference relative to the surrounding fluid, then Soret and Dufour effects are considered in the second order phenomenon. This double diffusive convection having considerable applications in science and engineering fields which includes oceanography, hydrology, reactor safety, conversion of building energy, nuclear waste disposal and geosciences etc. Motivated by these applications, many scientists and researchers have examined the implications of the Dufour and Soret effects on fluid flow over various types of surfaces. For instance, Homotopy analysis for fluid flow over a moving porous plate with the combined influence of Dufour and Soret is analysed by Omowaye et al. [20]. From their study it is clear that Skin friction coefficient value is supressed by increasing both Dufour and Soret parameter values of the fluid. Venkateswarlu et al. [21] examined the exact flow analysis of chemically reactive fluid over a vertical porous plate which is exponentially accelerated with radiation and cross diffusion effects. In the presence of Newtonian heating effect with magnetic and cross diffusion effects in the second grade fluid flow over a parabolic shaped stretching sheet is presented by Punith et al. [22]. Abbas et al. [23] explored a study on the flow of a Jeffrey HNF containing gyrotactic microorganisms and dusty particles over a Riga plate, incorporating cross-diffusion effects. Amudhini et al. [24] presented a numerical study on rotating disk for tetra-HNF flow over it with multi slip boundary condition, Dufour and Soret effects. Debasish et al. [25] presented a study on oscillating porous plate for the flow characteristics of elastico‐viscous fluid to examine the impact of thermo-diffusion and diffusion-thermo with magnetic field. From their study it is pointed out that temperature profile of the fluid diminishes with high value of diffusion-thermo parameter. Gayathri et al. [26] explored a study on time dependent convection flow of the fluid over an accelerated plate in a porous medium with the influence of cross diffusion effects, heat generation and magnetic field effect by using numerical methodology. Rajakumar et al. [27] presented an analytical study on vertical porous plate for the fluid flow analysis through it with multiple effects.

Heat transfer is the fundamental branch in the thermal engineering field. It occurs mostly in three different forms such as conduction, convection and radiation. Unlike the other two heat transfer mechanisms, radiation does not require a material medium to occur. It is noteworthy that radiative heat transfer can take place between two bodies even when they are separated by a medium that is cooler than both. Thermal radiation can influence heat transfer rate for the fluid flow and significant particularly in high temperature industrial and engineering systems. Thermal radiation effects become significant at large temperature differences between the surface and the ambient environment. It is the source term that can often appear in the energy equation. Accurate modelling of radiative heat transfer is essential for reliable prediction of temperature fields and for enhancing the efficiency of thermal systems. Srinivasacharya [28] presented a theoretical study with numerical solution on thermal radiation impact on inclined wavy surface for the convective nanofluid flow in a non-Darcy porous medium. Arulmozhi et al. [29] investigated the effect of thermal radiation on boundary layer flow of a chemically reactive nanofluid over a moving vertical plate which is embedded in a porous medium. Raghunath [30] presented an analytical study on time dependent chemically reactive nanofluid flow over the plate in a porous medium in the environment of applied magnetic strength, radiation and thermo diffusion effect. Raghunath et al. [31] observed impacts of thermal radiation, Dufour effect, and chemical reaction on the flow of a non-Newtonian Jeffrey water-based nanofluid over an inclined porous plate. The impacts of thermal radiation and a first-order homogeneous chemical reaction on Casson-based natural convective HNF flow past an oscillating infinite vertical plate in a porous medium was investigated theoretically and numerically by Krishna [32]. Rao et al. [33] examined the heat and mass characteristics of HNF which contains Motile Microorganisms through a stretching sheet by considering various effects which includes thermal radiation. Revathi et al. [34] investigated a numerical study on water based HNF motion in the porous medium for the implications of thermal radiation and viscous dissipation with entropy generation. Akhil et al. [35] presented a study on tri-HNF flow which chemically reactive past a moving isotropic porous plate with the impact of radiation absorption, aligned magnetic strength and heat source. Pradeep et al. [36] presented an analytical investigation of the momentum and thermal boundary layers for a HNF composed of blood as the base fluid and two different nanoparticles, with the flow considered over a vertical plate.

Novelty of the present study

An examination of the existing literature reveals that the study of cross diffusion effects and inclined magnetohydrodynamic HNF flow past a plate that placed in vertical position in the permeable medium was not yet analysed. Therefore, the present study aims to fulfill this gap in the literature. HNFs are having high thermal conductivity than the normal fluid. So, this work compares the thermal and flow behaviour of nanoparticles TiO2 and Cu when suspended in two various base fluids such as water and water + Ethylene glycol (50:50) mixture. In this model, radiation, heat source/sink, inclined magnetic field, diffusion thermo and thermo diffusion effects are considered in the flow of HNF over an inclined plate embedded in the homogeneous porous medium. The multi-regular Perturbation method is then employed to get the solution for system of non-linear and non dimensional partial differential equations (PDEs) after converting non-linear governing equations with appropriate similarity transformations. The findings of this study has applications in the fields of solar thermal engineering, heat exchangers, oceanography, hydrology, cooling reactors and so on.

Results and discussion

This study presents the time dependent MHD, HNF flow past an inclined plate embedded in a porous medium. In the flow configuration, angled magnetic strength, cross diffusion, first order and homogeneous chemical reaction and radiation effects with variable suction are taken into the consideration. This study examines the comparison of behaviour of TiO2, Cu / Water and TiO2, Cu / Water + EG (50:50) HNF. For the HNF preparation, the nanoparticles volume fractions are considered as TiO2 (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\phi_{1}$$\end{document}) = 2% and Cu (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\phi_{2}$$\end{document}) = 2% in the both base fluids (Water, Water + EG). We have considered the Prandtl number Pr = 6 to 7 range for water and Pr = 29 to 30 range for Water + EG. The implications of distinct pertinent parameters that are involved in the system of equations are presented via graphs. Skin friction coefficient, Nusselt number and Sherwood numbers are presented and explained in this section.

Velocity profile

Figure 2 explains the influence of Dufour number (Du) on the momentum boundary layer. From that it is clear that the velocity of HNF is improved as the value of Du rises. Physically, Du represents the heat flux caused by the concentration gradient. High Du value increases the fluid temperature which leads to reduction in the fluid viscosity and strengthen the buoyancy force. Thereby, HNF velocity near the vertical plate increases as the Du rises. It is clearly noticed that water-based HNF velocity performance is better than the water+EG (50:50) HNF. Fig. 3 depicts that the mobility of the fluid is improved as the parameter value of thermo diffusion (Sr) increased. This is due to enhance in species concentration within the boundary layer with high Sr values. The enhanced solutal buoyancy intensifies the flow, resulting in an increase in fluid velocity. Fig. 4 illustrates the impact of the magnetic field parameter (M) on the momentum boundary layer. From the figure it is clear that velocity of the HNF decreased for both water and water + EG as parameter values increased as M=1, 2 and 3. When an external magnetic field is applied to the flow of water then the motion of the fluid promotes electric currents within flow. The interaction between these electric currents and magnetic field creates a drag force which is named as Lorentz force which acts opposite to fluid flow and reduces the velocity of the fluid as show in the Fig. 4. Permeability parameter (kp) implications on the velocity flow region is demonstrated in Fig. 5. As the kp values increases, so does the velocity profile for both water and water + EG based HNF flow over the vertical plate. Usually, high permeability values will give less resistance to the fluid flow which will increase the velocity of the fluid near the plate. Due to this reason, velocity of the fluid rises with high permeability values. Fig. 6 and 7 represents the velocity profile behaviour by the implications of thermal and solutal Grashof numbers respectively. HNF velocity and thickness of the momentum boundary layer is increased with high values of Grashof number. On the boundary layer, the relationship between the buoyancy force and viscous hydrodynamic force is known as Grashof number. A high Grashof number enhances buoyancy forces and causing the fluid to flow more rapidly as presented in the Fig.6 and 7. Conclusions

The aim of this current study is to analyse the characteristics of unsteady HNF flow past a vertically inclined plate with the influence of cross diffusion effects, inclined magnetic strength, thermal radiation, heat sink and chemical reaction terms. The variable suction is considered in the fluid flow phenomenon. The tiny nanoparticles TiO2, Cu are mixed in two different base fluid such as water and water + EG (50:50) and the results are compared for both HNF. The governing equations are framed based on the assumptions that the flow is laminar, fluid is incompressible and stabled thermo physical quantities. The non-linear governing equations are completely changed in to non-linear and non-dimensional PDEs. The multi regular Perturbation method is utilized to solve the set of PDE’s by converting them into ODEs. The implications of various pertinent parameters on the various boundary layers is studied and presented in the Figures. The non-dimensional engineering quantities such as Cf, Nu and Sh are explained by using surface plots and numerical table values.

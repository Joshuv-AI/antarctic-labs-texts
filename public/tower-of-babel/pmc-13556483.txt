110. Glutamatergic changes in the medial prefrontal cortex associated with psychosis history in bipolar disorder and their relationship to executive function
Authors: C-Y Chang, C Y Wu, S-Y Wei, H H Chang, T-H Lu, W H Chang, C-F Hsu, R-Y Lin, F-Z Shaw, Y K Yang, P S Chen, H-H Tseng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPsychosis affects over half of individuals with bipolar disorder (BD) and has been linked to glutamatergic dysfunction within the default mode network (DMN). Altered glutamate/GABA signaling in the DMN may contribute to cognitive deficits, yet findings remain inconsistent. Differences between BD with (BD-P) and without past psychotic experiences (BD-NP) remain underexplored, underscoring the need for further investigation.Aims & ObjectivesThis study aims to examine excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmitter levels, their ratio, and their relationship with executive function in key default mode network (DMN) hubs among BD-P and BD-NP.MethodWe recruited 78 BD patients, comprising 32 BD-P and 46 BD-NP. Proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy (1H-MRS) was performed in the posterior cingulate cortex (PCC) and medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) to quantify excitatory glutamate/glutamine (Glx) and inhibitory GABA, yielding an E/I ratio. Cognitive flexibility was assessed using the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test (WCST), and sustained attention with the Continuous Performance Test (CPT).ResultsBD-P exhibited higher mPFC Glx (p = 0.009) and Glu (p < 0.001) compared to BD-NP, while E/I ratios did not differ in either region. In BD-NP, cognitive flexibility, indexed by WCST completed categories, correlated negatively with mPFC E/I ratio (r = -0.35, p = 0.018), and higher E/I ratio predicted poorer cognitive flexibility after controlling for covariates (β = -0.21, p = 0.014).Discussion & ConclusionsElevated mPFC Glu and Glx in BD-P suggest that localized glutamatergic hyperactivity may underlie psychotic experience of BD. The negative association between mPFC E/I ratio and cognitive flexibility observed only in BD-NP indicates that glutamatergic regulation of executive function differs by psychosis history, potentially reflecting compensatory mechanisms in BD-NP versus more diffuse network dysregulation in BD-P.

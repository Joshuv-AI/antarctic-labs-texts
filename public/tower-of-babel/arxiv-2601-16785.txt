Multisymplectic ȷ ⧫ \scriptscriptstyle\blacklozenge - AKSZ sigma models
Evgeny Skvortsov

Abstract
The Alexandrov–Kontsevich–Schwarz–Zaboronsky (AKSZ) construction encodes all the data of a topological sigma-model in the finite-dimensional symplectic Q Q -manifold. Relaxing the nondegeneracy condition i.e. considering a presymplectic form instead, extends the construction to non-topological models. The gauge-invariant action functional of (presymplectic) AKSZ sigma model is written in terms of space-time differential forms and can be seen as a covariant multidimensional analogue of the usual 1st order Hamiltonian action. In this work, we show that the AKSZ construction has a natural generalisation where the target space Q Q -manifold is equipped with a form of arbitrary degree Ω \Omega (possibly inhomogeneous) which is ( d + L Q ) (\mathrm{d}+L_{Q}) -closed. This data defines a higher-derivative generalisation of the AKSZ action which is still invariant under the natural gauge transformations determined by Q Q and which is efficiently formulated in terms of a version of Chern–Weil map introduced by Kotov and Strobl. It turns out that a variety of interesting gauge theories, including higher-dimensional Chern–Simons theory, MacDowell–Mansouri–Stelle–West action and self-dual gravity as well as its higher spin extension, can be concisely reformulated as such multisymplectic AKSZ models. We also present a version of the construction in the setup of PDE geometry and demonstrate that the counterpart of the multisymplectic AKSZ action is precisely the standard multisymplectic formulation, where the Chern–Weil map corresponds to the usual pullback map.

More precisely, one asks that this manifold

Q\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{M})\,,\qquad\deg(Q)=1\,,\qquad Q^{2}\equiv\tfrac{1}{2}[Q,Q]=0\,,

\omega=\mathrm{d}\chi\,,\qquad\text{for}\qquad\Theta\in{\bigwedge}^{1}\mathcal{M}\,,\qquad\deg(\Theta)=n-1\,,

\imath_{Q}\omega+\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}=0\,,\qquad\text{for}\qquad\mathcal{L}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\,,\quad\deg(\mathcal{L})=n\,,

S[\varphi]=\int_{T[1]\mathscr{X}}\Big(\imath_{d_{\mathscr{X}}}\varphi^{*}\chi+\varphi^{*}\mathcal{L}\Big)\,,\qquad T[1]\mathscr{X}\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{M}\,,

relaxing the condition that the maps between the source

One should in fact refine slightly

\mathfrak{g}[1]

More concretely, denoting the degree
1

, the symplectic form

\omega
, and homological vector field

, is given by

\omega=\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_{ab}\,\mathrm{d}c^{a}\mathrm{d}c^{b}\,,\qquad Q=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,f_{ab}{}^{c}\,c^{a}c^{b}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial c^{c}}\,,

(1.6)

where
\omega_{ab}
and

are the components
of the invariant bilinear form and the structure constants,

\mathfrak{g}
expressed in the basis dual
to the chosen coordinate system on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
.
That the vector field
Q
squares to zero follows
straightforwardly from the Jacobi identity for the Lie bracket
of
\mathfrak{g}
and the symplectic form
\omega

The compatibility between
Q

\omega

L_{Q}\omega=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\omega_{d(a}f_{b)c}{}^{d}=0

\omega

\omega\big([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z\big)=\omega\big(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}\big)\,,\qquad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}\,.

(1.8)

The
1
-form potential for
\omega
and Hamiltonian function
for
Q

\chi=\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_{ab}\,c^{a}\mathrm{d}c^{b}\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}=\tfrac{1}{6}\,\omega_{ad}f_{bc}{}^{d}\,c^{a}c^{b}c^{c}\,,

(1.9)

so that, put together one ends up recovering the well-known
Chern–Simons action functional,

S_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle CS}[A]=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\int_{\mathscr{X}}\omega\big(A,\mathrm{d}A+\tfrac{1}{3}[A,A]_{\mathfrak{g}}\big)\,.

(1.10)

As it turns out, the above Chern–Simons functional

that can be defined in

of
higher Chern–Simons terms
. Given a symmetric,
non-degenerate and ad-invariant multilinear form of arity

\mathfrak{g}

\Omega\in(S^{p}\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}
,
the latter are defined as the
(2p-1)
-form

\mathsf{CS}(A)\in{\bigwedge}^{2p-1}\mathscr{X}
,
made out of the
\mathfrak{g}
-valued gauge field
A

\Omega(\underbrace{F,\dots,F}_{p\ \text{times}})=\mathrm{d}\mathsf{CS}(A)\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad F[A]=\mathrm{d}A+\tfrac{1}{2}[A,A]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,.

(1.11)

From the point of view of the graded manifold
\mathfrak{g}[1]
,
the invariant polynomial is a
closed

p
-form
preserved by

{\bigwedge}^{p}\mathfrak{g}[1]\ \ni\ \Omega=\tfrac{1}{p!}\Omega_{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}\mathrm{d}c^{a_{1}}\dots\mathrm{d}c^{a_{p}}\,\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\Omega=0=L_{Q}\Omega\,.

(1.12)

If this closed

\imath_{X}\Omega=0\,,\qquad X\in\Gamma(T\mathfrak{g}[1])\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad X=0\,,

(1.13)

then it defines a
Q
-invariant

form
on
\mathfrak{g}[1]

theory in higher dimensions as resulting from
a
multisymplectic
version of the AKSZ construction?
If so, what are the details and possible applications

Multisymplectic AKSZ sigma models

Chern–Weil morphism for
Q
-manifolds.

Let us consider a pair of
Q
-manifolds
(\mathcal{X},d_{\mathcal{X}})

and
(\mathcal{M},Q)
, and a morphism of graded manifolds

\sigma:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}
between them. As shown in
[
9
]
, the pullback of such a morphism can be lifted to a morphism of differential graded
commutative algebras,

\sigma^{*}_{W}:\big({\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{M},\mathrm{d}+L_{Q}\big)\longrightarrow\big(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}),d_{\mathcal{X}}\big)\,.

(2.1)

It is uniquely determined by its action on functions and exact 1-forms via:

\sigma^{*}_{W}(f)=\sigma^{*}(f)\,,\qquad\sigma^{*}_{W}(\mathrm{d}f)=d_{\mathcal{X}}\sigma^{*}(f)-\sigma^{*}\big(Qf\big)\,.

(2.2)

and is extended to generic forms as an algebra morphism. In practice, we define it for
\mathcal{M}
coordinates
z^{I}
and the corresponding

1
-form basis
\mathrm{d}z^{I}
:

\sigma^{*}_{W}(z^{I})=\sigma^{*}(z^{I})\,,\qquad\sigma^{*}_{W}(\mathrm{d}z^{I})=d_{\mathcal{X}}\sigma^{*}(z^{I})-\sigma^{*}\big(Q^{I}(z)\big)\,.

(2.3)

The relation between AKSZ sigma models and structures known in the context of characteristic classes and equivariant cohomology was discussed in e.g.
[
10
]
. Let us also mention that characteristic classes of
Q
-manifolds have been studied in
[
11
,
12
]
.

It is useful to explicitly express the morphism
\sigma^{*}_{W}
in terms of the underlying morphism
\sigma^{*}
:

\sigma^{*}_{W}=p\circ e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\circ\sigma^{*}\circ e^{-\imath_{Q}}\,,\qquad\text{where}\qquad p:{\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{X}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})\,,

(2.4)

i.e. map
p
is nothing but a projection of a form
on
\mathcal{X}
onto its
0
-form component. Indeed, one can easily check that the above expression reproduces (
2.10
) when evaluated on
0
-forms and exact
1
-forms on
\mathcal{M}
, and is a morphism of algebra. The latter property follows from the fact that
\imath_{Q}
and
\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
are (degree
0
) derivations of
{\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}
and
{\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{X}
respectively, and that the exponential of a derivation of a commutative algebra is an algebra morphism.

In order to check that the previous formula defines
a chain map, we should first note that the ‘twist’
of the de Rham differential on
\mathcal{M}
by the Lie derivative along
Q
can be understood as resulting
from the transfer of the former under the isomorphism

e^{-\imath_{Q}}
, that is

e^{-\imath_{Q}}\circ\big(\mathrm{d}+L_{Q}\big)=\mathrm{d}\circ e^{-\imath_{Q}}\,.

(2.5)

Put differently,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\big({\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{M},\mathrm{d}+L_{Q}\big)\quad}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\quad\big({\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{M},\mathrm{d}\big)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e^{-\imath_{Q}}}

(2.6)

is an isomorphism of differential graded commutative algebras,
where on both sides
the grading is taken to be the
total degree

that we shall denote
\mathrm{tdeg}(-)
,
i.e. the sum of form degree and homological degree.
Using this simple identity, one finds

\displaystyle e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\circ\sigma^{*}\circ e^{-\imath_{Q}}\circ\big(\mathrm{d}+L_{Q}\big)

\displaystyle=e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\circ\sigma^{*}\circ\mathrm{d}\circ e^{-\imath_{Q}}

(2.7a)

\displaystyle=e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\circ\mathrm{d}\circ\sigma^{*}\circ e^{-\imath_{Q}}

(2.7b)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathrm{d}+L_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\big)\circ e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\circ\sigma^{*}\circ e^{-\imath_{Q}}\,,

(2.7c)

which, combined with

p\circ\big(\mathrm{d}+L_{d_{\mathcal{X}}})=p\circ L_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}=d_{\mathcal{X}}\circ p\,,

(2.8)

leads to

\sigma_{W}^{*}\circ\big(\mathrm{d}+L_{Q}\big)=d_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\sigma_{W}^{*}\,,

(2.9)

as claimed initially. To summarise, the Chern–Weil morphism
is a composition of maps,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\big({\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{M},\mathrm{d}+L_{Q}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\big({\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{M},\mathrm{d}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\big({\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{X},\mathrm{d}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\big({\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{X},\mathrm{d}+L_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\big(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}),d_{\mathcal{X}}\big)\,,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e^{-\imath_{Q}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

all of which are morphisms of
differential graded commutative
algebras
.

In this paper, we are solely concerned with the case
\mathcal{X}=T[1]\mathscr{X}
equipped with its canonical homological vector field
d_{\mathcal{X}}=d_{\mathscr{X}}

corresponding to the de Rham differential on
\mathscr{X}
.

Note that the generalisation to the case where

(2.10)

The functions

are components of the curvature of
\sigma

Their field-theoretical meaning is that these are the components
of the equations of motion of the underlying non-Lagrangian

and solutions are those maps
that preserve the

F^{I}
can be nonvanishing
if the degree of

is either
0
or
1
. In the latter case
the term with

vanishes while
Q^{I}(A)

generally does not. In other words, degree
-1

encoded constraints (=algebraic equations) as was originally

[

.

Multisymplectic AKSZ actions.

Suppose that the
Q
-manifold
\big(\mathcal{X},d_{\mathcal{X}}\big)

admits a
d_{\mathcal{X}}
-invariant measure of degree
-n
, that is

\int_{\mathcal{X}}(-):\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\,,\qquad\text{such that}\qquad\int_{\mathcal{X}}d_{\mathcal{X}}f=0\,,\qquad\forall f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})\,.

(2.11)

For instance, if
\mathcal{X}=T[1]\mathscr{X}
with
\mathscr{X}
an ordinary smooth manifold of dimension
\dim\mathscr{X}=n
without boundary, the Berezin integral defines such an operation,
with the invariance property being nothing but
the incarnation of Stokes theorem in
\mathscr{X}
. More generally, assuming that functions on
\mathcal{X}
are sections of a vector bundle over
a real manifold
\mathscr{X}
and the product is linear over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
one can require that for any
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
:

\int_{\mathcal{X}}d_{\mathcal{X}}f=\int_{\mathscr{X}}\alpha_{f}\,,\qquad\alpha_{f}=d\beta\,.

(2.12)

Here,
\alpha_{f}
is a density on
\mathscr{X}
which is, roughly speaking, obtained by integrating over the fibre coordinates and then represented as a top-form on
\mathscr{X}
.
5
5

5

Under these conditions one can even replace the algebra of function on
T[1]\mathscr{X}
with a more general differential graded algebra, e.g. not freely-generated, see
[
15
,
16
,

,
18
]
.
In this way implements the usual setup for boundary terms.

Let
\Omega\in{\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{M}

be a form of total degree
n+1
, such that

\big(\mathrm{d}+L_{Q}\big)\Omega=0\,.

(2.13)

In light of the isomorphism (
2.6
),
the cohomology of
\mathrm{d}+L_{Q}
is isomorphic
to the de Rham cohomology of
\mathcal{M}
,
which is concentrated in homological degree
0
,
see e.g.
[
19
]
(as is the case for all
\mathbb{Z}
-graded manifold). In particular, this means that if
\Omega
has non-zero homological degree, then there exists a form
\Theta_{W}\in{\bigwedge}\mathcal{M}
of total degree
n
, such that

\Omega=\big(\mathrm{d}+L_{Q}\big)\Theta_{W}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\mathrm{d}\Theta=e^{-\imath_{Q}}\Omega\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\Theta=e^{-\imath_{Q}}\,\Theta_{W}\,.

(2.14)

The closed form
\Omega_{W}=e^{-\imath_{Q}}\Omega
may be degenerate and, indeed, it is the case in some of the examples we consider below. That being said, we will refer to such models as multisymplectic whereas a more precise term would be pre-multisymplectic.
The inhomogeneous form
\Theta
can then be used
to define an action principle, via

S[\sigma]=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\sigma^{*}_{W}(\Theta_{W})=\int_{\mathcal{X}}p\big(e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\,\sigma^{*}\Theta\big)\equiv\int_{\mathcal{X}}p\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\tfrac{1}{k!}\,\underbrace{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\dots\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}_{k}\sigma^{*}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[k]}\bigg)\,,

(2.15)

where
\sigma:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}

is a (degree-preserving) map of graded manifolds,

\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[k]}\in{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathcal{E}
denotes
the
k
-form component of the inhomogeneous form
\Theta
,
and
p
is the highest form degree of the components of
\Omega
.
In plain word, the action is nothing but the integral
over
\mathcal{X}
of the pullback by the Chern–Weil morphism
of the inhomogeneous form
\Theta_{W}

such that
(\mathrm{d}+L_{Q})\Theta_{W}=\Omega
.

Reformulation in terms of
Q
-bundles.

Even if we limit ourselves to the usual sigma-model setup
where the space of fields is the space of maps from the source space
to the target space it is very convenient to represent fields
as sections of the trivial bundle whose fibre is the target space.
This setup also admits a natural generalisation where the bundle
is not necessarily trivial and the structures therein
are not factorised. In the context of
Q
-manifolds
and AKSZ-like sigma models the appropriate notion
is that of
Q
-bundles
[
9
]
. A bundle

\mathcal{E}
over
\mathcal{X}
is a
Q
-bundle if both the base
and the total space are
Q
-manifolds and the projection
respects the
Q
-structures:

\big(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{Q}\big)\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\big(\mathcal{X},d_{\mathcal{X}}\big)\qquad\text{with}\qquad\pi^{*}\circ d_{\mathcal{X}}=\mathcal{Q}\circ\pi^{*}\,.

(2.16)

A section
\sigma
is a
Q
-section if
\sigma^{*}\circ\mathcal{Q}=d_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\sigma^{*}
. In the case where
\mathcal{X}=T[1]\mathscr{X}
and the bundle is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded and possibly infinite-dimensional, such an object is known as gauge PDE
[
20
]
, see also
[
21
,
22
]
, and is known to proved a very flexible version of the Batalin–Vilkovisky formalism at the level of equations of motion. In particular,
Q
-sections are solutions of the gauge PDE while vector fields of the form
[Q,Y]
generate their gauge transformations.

The sigma-model setup described above corresponds to a trivial
Q
-bundle,

\pi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{X}\,,\qquad\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{X}\,,\qquad\mathcal{Q}=d_{\mathcal{X}}+Q\,,

(2.17)

If
\big(\mathcal{M},Q\big)
is in addition equipped with a closed and
Q
-invariant form
\Omega
, it can be considered as a form on
\mathcal{E}
by taking its pullback by the canonical projection. Although we limit ourselves to the sigma-model setup most of the considerations are valid for a generic
Q
-bundle equipped with a closed and
Q
-invariant form
\Omega
of total degree
n+1
. In particular, the properties of Chern–Weil map remain intact if one replaces the target with the total space and restricts maps to be sections so that (
2.15
) is well-defined for a general
Q
-bundle. Of course, in the trivial case this is just reformulation of the same action. Note that when restricted to sections, the Chern–Weil map vanishes on differential forms pulled back from the base. More precisely, for any
\alpha\in{\bigwedge}^{k},k>0
one has
\sigma^{*}_{W}(\pi^{*}\alpha)=0
. Indeed,

\sigma^{*}_{W}(\pi^{*}\alpha)=p\big(e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\sigma^{*}e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\pi^{*}\alpha\big)=p\big(e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\sigma^{*}\pi^{*}e^{-\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\alpha\big)=p(\alpha)=0\,,

(2.18)

where we used the fact that
\sigma
is a section of
\pi

(and hence
\sigma^{*}\circ\pi^{*}=(\pi\circ\sigma)^{*}=\mathbf{1}_{{\bigwedge}\mathcal{X}}
), and that
\alpha

is of non-zero form degree by assumption, which implies
that its projection under
p
vanishes. To show this, let us first note that since
\mathcal{Q}
projects
onto
d_{\mathcal{X}}
under
\pi
, we have the identity

\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}\pi^{*}=\pi^{*}\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\pi^{*}=\pi^{*}e^{-\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\,.

(2.19)

It follows,
\sigma^{*}_{W}(\mathcal{I})=0
, where
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal in
{\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}
generated by forms from the base and hence is well-defined on vertical forms defined as the following quotient:
{\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{I}
.

For
\Omega=\omega\in{\bigwedge}^{2}\mathcal{M}
,
the action (
2.15
)
reduces to the standard AKSZ action (
1.5
), or its presymplectic version
[
23
,
24
]
,
recalled in the Introduction. In this case,
\Theta
has a
1
-form component
\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[1]}=\chi
, and a
0
-form component
\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[0]}=\mathcal{L}
, which verify

\mathrm{d}\chi=\omega\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}+\imath_{Q}\omega=0\,,\qquad\imath_{Q}\imath_{Q}\omega=0\,,

(2.20)

so that the last relation above is nothing but the condition
that the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{L}
for the homological
vector field
Q
self-commutes with respect to the Poisson bracket
induced by the symplectic form
\omega
.
In the case of not necessarily trivial
Q
-bundle this action
is still well-defined and is associated with a gauge PDEs
equipped with a presymplectic structure,
see
[
25
,
26
]
for more details.

Equations of motion and gauge symmetries.

One can compute the Euler–Lagrange equations resulting
from the action (
2.15
) by considering
variations of the section
\sigma
induced by vertical
vector fields of degree
0
,

\delta\sigma^{*}=\sigma^{*}\circ L_{V}\,,\qquad V\in\Gamma(\mathrm{V}\mathcal{E)}\,,\qquad\deg(V)=0\,.

(2.21)

Using Cartan’s homotopy formula, one finds

L_{V}\Theta=\big(\mathrm{d}\imath_{V}+\imath_{V}\mathrm{d}\big)\Theta=\mathrm{d}(\cdots)+e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\imath_{V}\Omega\,,

(2.22)

where we used (
2.14
) in the last step.
Consequently, the variation of the action

\delta S=\int_{\mathcal{X}}p\big(e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\sigma^{*}e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\imath_{V}\Omega\big)+d_{\mathcal{X}}(\cdots)\equiv\int_{\mathcal{X}}\sigma^{*}_{W}\big(\imath_{V}\Omega\big)\,,

(2.23)

where we have discarded the second,
d_{\mathcal{X}}
-exact, term
as we have assumed the integration over
\mathcal{X}

to be
d_{\mathcal{X}}
-invariant, and re-wrote the first piece
in terms of the Chern–Weil morphism. One therefore finds
that the equations for motions are given by

\delta S\approx 0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\Omega(-,F,\dots,F)\approx 0\,,

(2.24)

where
F
are the field strengths of the gauge fields
defined by
\sigma^{*}
. In plain words, the equations of motion
of the action (
2.15
) take the form
of the (pullback of the) multi-presymplectic form
\Omega

whose ‘form-legs’ are, all but one, contracted with the field
strengths. In components, and assuming for simplicity
that
\Omega
has only a
p
-form component, these equations read

\Omega_{JI_{1}\dots I_{p-1}}(A)\,F^{I_{1}}\dots F^{I_{p-1}}\approx 0\,.

(2.25)

Now let us turn our attention to the symmetries
of the action (
2.15
). Our claim
is that it is invariant under the gauge transformations

\delta_{Y}\sigma^{*}=\sigma^{*}\circ L_{[\mathcal{Q},Y]}\,,\qquad Y\in\Gamma(\mathrm{V}\mathcal{E})\,,\quad\deg(Y)=-1\,,

(2.26)

generated by vertical vector fields of homological degree
-1
.
To compute the variation of the action
under these gauge transformations, we can pick-up
our previous computation (
2.22
)
with
V=[\mathcal{Q},Y]
and use the identity

e^{\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}L_{Y}e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}=L_{Y}+\imath_{[\mathcal{Q},Y]}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad[L_{Y},e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}]=\imath_{[\mathcal{Q},Y]}e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\,,

(2.27)

to re-write the second term in this equation as

\displaystyle\imath_{[\mathcal{Q},Y]}e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\Omega

\displaystyle=L_{Y}e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\Omega-e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}L_{Y}\Omega

(2.28)

\displaystyle=L_{Y}\mathrm{d}\Theta-e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}L_{Y}\Omega=\mathrm{d}(\cdots)-e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}L_{Y}\Omega\,,

(2.29)

so that the variation of the action reads

\delta_{Y}S[\sigma]=\int_{\mathcal{X}}p\big(e^{\imath_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\sigma^{*}e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}L_{Y}\Omega\big)\equiv\int_{\mathcal{X}}\sigma^{*}_{W}(L_{Y}\Omega)\,,

(2.30)

where again we discarded the
d_{\mathcal{X}}
-exact terms.
Now assuming that

L_{Y}\Omega\in\mathcal{I}\qquad\text{where}\qquad\mathcal{I}=\pi^{*}\Big({\bigwedge}^{>0}\mathcal{X}\Big)\otimes{\bigwedge}^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}\,,

(2.31)

is the ideal generated by horizontal forms in the image
of the pullback by the projection map

\pi:\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{X}
,
of strictly positive form degree, the above variation vanishes.

We have seen above that the Chern–Weil map determined by a section
\sigma

vanishes on
\mathcal{I}
.
As a consequence,

\sigma^{*}_{W}(L_{Y}\Omega)=0
and hence the action
(
2.15
) is invariant under the gauge
transformations (
2.26
) as claimed.

Let us also mention that, if the
(\mathrm{d}+L_{Q})
-closed form
\Omega

is degenerate—meaning if it has a non-trivial kernel—then the action is invariant under

\delta_{V}\sigma^{*}=\sigma^{*}\circ L_{V}\,,\qquad V\in\mathrm{Ker}(\Omega)\,,\qquad\deg(V)=0\,,

(2.32)

which correspond to algebraic / shift symmetries.

. Indeed,

(2.36)

where we used (
2.5
) and (
2.8
)
in the last step. Using the defining relation (
2.14
)
of
\Theta
, we therefore end up with

S[\sigma]=\int_{T[1]\mathscr{X}}p\big(e^{\imath_{d_{\mathscr{X}}}}\sigma^{*}\Theta\big)=\int_{T[1]\mathscr{Y}}\bar{\sigma}_{W}^{*}(\Omega)\,,

(2.37)

which is nothing but the multisymplectic counterpart

[
9
]
. The analogous representation is well-known for generic multi-symplectic action, see e.g.
[
27
]
, and multisymplectic actions associated to PDEs equipped with symplectic currents, see Section
4
.

3
Examples

3.1
Higher-dimensional Chern–Simons

A first, simple yet important, class of examples
of multisymplectic AKSZ sigma models is that
of higher-dimensional Chern–Simons theories.
For instance, in
n=5
dimensions the action takes the form
6
6

6

This action was studied in the context of gravity
in, for instance,
[
28
,
29
]
.

S_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle CS_{5d}}[A]=\int_{\mathscr{X}}\tfrac{1}{6}\,\Omega\big(A,\mathrm{d}A,\mathrm{d}A\big)+\tfrac{1}{8}\,\Omega\big(A,\mathrm{d}A,[A,A]_{\mathfrak{g}}\big)+\tfrac{1}{40}\,\Omega\big(A,[A,A]_{\mathfrak{g}},[A,A]_{\mathfrak{g}}\big)\,,

(3.1)

where
\Omega\in(S^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant polynomial
homogeneous of degree
3
. Equivalently,

\Omega
is a trilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
which is both symmetric
and ad-invariant, i.e.

\Omega\big([x,u]_{\mathfrak{g}},v,w\big)+\Omega\big(u,[x,v]_{\mathfrak{g}},w\big)+\Omega\big(u,v,[x,w]_{\mathfrak{g}}\big)=0\,,\qquad\forall\,x,u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}\,,

(3.2)

or in components

f_{d(a}{}^{e}\,\Omega_{bc)e}=0\,.

(3.3)

This invariance property of
\Omega
ensures
that the action (
3.1
) is invariant
under the usual gauge transformations

\delta_{\varepsilon}A=\mathrm{d}\varepsilon+[A,\varepsilon]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,,\qquad\varepsilon\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathscr{X})\otimes\mathfrak{g}\,,

(3.4)

up to
a boundary term—exactly like in the
3d
case.

Let us try to make sense of the various terms from the point
of view of the target space
\mathfrak{g}[1]
. As mentioned
in the introduction, the invariant polynomial
\Omega

defines a
closed

3
-form on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
, preserved by
the Lie derivative along the homological vector field

Q
given in (
1.6
),

{\bigwedge}^{3}\mathfrak{g}[1]\ni\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}=\tfrac{1}{6}\,\Omega_{abc}\mathrm{d}c^{a}\mathrm{d}c^{b}\mathrm{d}c^{c}\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}=0=L_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}\,.

(3.5)

The first, two-derivative, term of the action (
3.1
)
clearly originates from a ‘potential’

\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[2]}\ni{\bigwedge}^{2}\mathfrak{g}[1]
for the
3
-form,
meaning

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}=\mathrm{d}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[2]}\,,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[2]}=\tfrac{1}{6}\,\Omega_{abc}\,c^{a}\mathrm{d}c^{b}\mathrm{d}c^{c}\,.

(3.6)

A simple computation shows that the second term
of the action (
3.1
), comes from a Hamiltonian

\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[1]}\in{\bigwedge}^{1}\mathfrak{g}[1]
for
Q

with respect to the
3
-form
\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}
,

\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[1]}=\tfrac{1}{8}\,\Omega_{abc}\,c^{a}\mathrm{d}c^{b}[c,c]_{\mathfrak{g}}^{c}\,,\qquad[c,c]_{\mathfrak{g}}^{a}:=f_{bc}{}^{a}\,c^{b}c^{c}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\imath_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}+\mathrm{d}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[1]}=0\,,

(3.7)

as a consequence of the
\mathfrak{g}
-invariance of
\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}
.
So far, this is simply the higher-form generalisation
of the data needed to define an AKSZ sigma model, namely
a potential for the symplectic form, and a Hamiltonian
for the homological vector field, the only difference being
that the latter is now itself a
1
-form in the target space.
Finally, let us turn our attention to the third and last piece
of the action (
3.1
). It involves
two
insertions
of the Lie bracket in the trilinear form
\Omega
,
or equivalently
two
contractions of
Q

with
\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}
. Here again, a simple computation
shows that

\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[0]}=\tfrac{1}{40}\,\Omega_{abc}\,c^{a}[c,c]_{\mathfrak{g}}^{b}[c,c]_{\mathfrak{g}}^{c}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\tfrac{1}{2}\imath_{Q}\imath_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}-\mathrm{d}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[0]}=0\,,

(3.8)

as a consequence of the
\mathfrak{g}
-invariance of
\Omega
,
and hence the Chern–Simons action (
3.1
)
takes the form

S_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle CS_{5d}}[\sigma]=\int_{T[1]\mathscr{X}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\imath_{d_{\mathscr{X}}}\imath_{d_{\mathscr{X}}}\sigma^{*}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[2]}+\imath_{d_{\mathscr{X}}}\sigma^{*}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[1]}+\sigma^{*}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[0]}\Big)\,,

(3.9)

where
\sigma:T[1]\mathscr{X}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}[1]

is a degree-preserving morphism of graded manifolds,
completely specified by

\sigma^{*}(c^{a})=\theta^{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a}(x)\,,

(3.10)

with
(x^{\mu},\theta^{\mu})
being coordinates of degree
0
and
1

respectively. In other words, the components
A_{\mu}^{a}(x)

are indeed those of a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1
-form on
\mathscr{X}
as expected.
In summary, the
5d
Chern–Simons action can be obtained
from the multisymplectic
Q
-manifold

\big(\mathfrak{g}[1],Q,\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}\big)
,
as an AKSZ-type action given by the pullback
of the
k
-forms
\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[k]}\in{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathfrak{g}[1]

for
k=0,1
and
2
obeying

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[2]}&=\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}\,,\\
\mathrm{d}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[1]}&=-\imath_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}\,,\\
\mathrm{d}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[0]}&=\tfrac{1}{2}\imath_{Q}\imath_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}\,,\\
0\,\,\ &=-\tfrac{1}{6}\,\imath_{Q}\imath_{Q}\imath_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}\,,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\mathrm{d}\big(\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[2]}+\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[1]}+\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[0]}\big)=e^{-\imath_{Q}}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}\,.

(3.11)

On top of that, the gauge transformations (
3.4
)
are reproduced by considering degree
-1
vertical vector fields
on
T[1]\mathscr{X}\times\mathfrak{g}[1]
. Since the fibre,
\mathfrak{g}[1]
here,
is concentrated in degree
1
, and the base
T[1]\mathscr{X}

in degree
0
and
1
, vertical vector fields are necessarily
along
\partial/\partial c^{a}
with components of degree
0
,
and hence

Y=\varepsilon^{a}(x)\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial c^{a}}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\big[d_{\mathscr{X}}+Q,Y\big]=\Big(\theta^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon^{a}(x)+f_{bc}{}^{a}\,c^{b}\varepsilon^{c}(x)\Big)\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial c^{a}}\,.

(3.12)

Accordingly, the gauge transformation of the field

\sigma^{*}(c^{a})=\theta^{\mu}\,A_{\mu}^{a}(x)
is obtained by
applying the above vector field to the coordinate
c^{a}

and pulling back the result by
\sigma^{*}
, leading to

\delta_{Y}\sigma^{*}(c^{a})=\sigma^{*}\big([d_{\mathscr{X}}+Q,Y]c^{a}\big)=\theta^{\mu}\big(\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon^{a}+f_{bc}{}^{a}\,A_{\mu}^{b}\varepsilon^{c}\big)\,,

(3.13)

which is indeed the expected gauge transformation
(
3.4
) upon using the isomorphism
between functions on
T[1]\mathscr{X}
and forms on
\mathscr{X}
.

The Chern–Simons functional on a manifold
\mathscr{X}

of dimension
\dim\mathscr{X}=2p-1
is obtained similarly,
from an invariant polynomial
\Omega\in(S^{p}\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}
,
i.e. a
p
-linear symmetric form on
\mathfrak{g}
verifying

0=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\Omega(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1},[x_{k},y]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{k+1},\dots,x_{p})\,,\qquad\forall x_{1},\dots,x_{p},y\in\mathfrak{g}\,,

(3.14)

considered as a
\mathcal{Q}
-invariant closed
p
-form
of homological degree
|\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}|_{\mathfrak{g}\scriptscriptstyle[1]}=p
,

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}=\tfrac{1}{p!}\,\Omega_{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}\,\mathrm{d}c^{a_{1}}\dots\mathrm{d}c^{a_{p}}\,,\qquad\text{such that}\qquad\Omega_{c(a_{1}\dots a_{p-1}}f_{a_{p})b}{}^{c}=0\,.

(3.15)

To recover the associated Chern–Simons form,
all we need to do is to find
\Theta\in{\bigwedge}^{<p}\mathfrak{g}[1]

such that
\mathrm{d}\Theta=e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}
.
This can be done relatively easily in this case,
upon using the homotopy operator

h(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,c^{a}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{d}c^{a}}\alpha(t\,c,t\,\mathrm{d}c)\,,\qquad\forall\alpha\in{\bigwedge}\mathfrak{g}[1]\,,

(3.16)

for the de Rham differential on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
,
leading to

\Theta=h(e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]})=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\tfrac{p}{2^{k}(p+k)}\binom{p-1}{k}\Omega\big(c,\underbrace{[c,c]_{\mathfrak{g}},\dots,[c,c]_{\mathfrak{g}}}_{k},\underbrace{\mathrm{d}c,\dots,\mathrm{d}c}_{p-k-1}\big)\,.

(3.17)

One can re-write this expression as follows:
since
\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}
is a (degree
0
) derivation
of
{\bigwedge}\mathfrak{g}[1]
, its exponential
is an algebra morphism and hence

e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}=\tfrac{1}{p!}\,\Omega_{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}f^{a_{1}}(c)\dots f^{a_{p}}(c)\,\qquad\text{with}\qquad f^{a}(c):=\mathrm{d}c^{a}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,[c,c]^{a}\equiv e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\mathrm{d}c^{a}\,.

(3.18)

We can then express
\Theta
as

\Theta=\tfrac{1}{(p-1)!}\,\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}t\,\Omega_{a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{p}}c^{a_{1}}f^{a_{2}}(t\,c)\dots f^{a_{p}}(t\,c)\,,

(3.19)

which is nothing but the ‘target space counterpart’
of the standard expression

\mathsf{CS}(A)=p\,\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}t\,\Omega\big(A,F_{t}[A],\dots,F_{t}[A]\big)\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad F_{t}[A]:=t\,\mathrm{d}A+\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\,[A,A]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,,

(3.20)

for the Chern–Simons form, similarly as the relation

\mathrm{d}\Theta=e^{-\imath_{\mathcal{Q}}}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}

is the target space incarnation of the defining relation

\mathrm{d}\mathsf{CS}(A)=\Omega\big(F[A],\dots,F[A]\big)
.

Note that one can obtain
holomorphic
Chern–Simons
theories by using the source manifold
\mathcal{X}=T^{0,1}[1]\mathscr{X}
,
i.e. the shifted
anti-holomorphic
tangent bundle
of an odd-dimensional complex manifold
\mathscr{X}
,
equipped with a holomorphic volume form

\mu\in\bigwedge^{2p-1,0}\mathscr{X}
, where
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathscr{X}=2p-1
.
In this case, the algebra of functions on
T^{0,1}[1]\mathscr{X}
,
is isomorphic to the Dolbeault complex
\bigwedge^{0,\bullet}\mathscr{X}
,
with the Dolbeault differential inducing a homological vector field
on
T^{0,1}[1]\mathscr{X}
.

Simple variation and Lovelock gravity.

Let us consider a simple variation on the previous situation,
by assuming that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a semi-direct sum

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\inplus\mathfrak{p}\,,

(3.21)

with
\mathfrak{h}
a Lie subalgebra and
\mathfrak{p}

and Abelian ideal. Denoting coordinates on
\mathfrak{h}[1]

by
\rho^{\alpha}
and on
\mathfrak{p}[1]
by
\xi^{a}
,
we can consider the
p
-form of total degree
n+1
,

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}:=\Omega_{a_{1}\dots a_{n+2-2p}b|\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{p-1}}\,\xi^{a_{1}}\dots\xi^{a_{n+2-2p}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi^{b}\,\mathrm{d}\rho^{\alpha_{1}}\dots\mathrm{d}\rho^{\alpha_{p-1}}\,,

(3.22)

which is closed, as we assume its components to be totally
antisymmetric in its Latin indices, and completely symmetric
in the Greek ones. Invariance under the
Q
-structure
defined by the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential of
\mathfrak{g}

amounts to the fact that these components form a
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant
tensor. Now instead of the homotopy operator (
3.16
),
let us consider

h(\varpi)=\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,\xi^{a}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{d}\xi^{a}}\,\varpi(t\,\xi,t\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\rho,\mathrm{d}\rho)\,,

(3.23)

which defines a contracting homotopy for the de Rham differential
on
\mathfrak{h}[1]
that we trivially extend to
\mathfrak{g}[1]
. This allows us
to find a primitive for
e^{-\imath_{Q}}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}
, given by

\Theta:=h(e^{-\imath_{Q}}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]})=\tfrac{1}{n-2p}\,\Omega_{a_{1}\dots a_{n+3-2p}|\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{p-1}}\xi^{a_{1}}\dots\xi^{a_{n+3-2p}}\,f^{\alpha_{1}}(\rho)\dots f^{\alpha_{p-1}}(\rho)\,.

(3.24)

In the case of the Poincaré algebra,

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{iso}(n-1,1)\,,\qquad\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{so}(n-1,1)\,,\qquad\mathfrak{p}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,

(3.25)

such a
Q
-invariant
p
-form is given by the totally
antisymmetric Levi–Civita symbol

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}=\epsilon_{a_{1}\dots a_{n+1-2p}b_{1}c_{1}\dots b_{p}c_{p}}\,\xi^{a_{1}}\dots\xi^{a_{n+2-2p}}\mathrm{d}\xi^{a_{n+3-2p}}\mathrm{d}\rho^{b_{1}c_{1}}\dots\mathrm{d}\rho^{b_{p-1}c_{p-1}}\,.

(3.26)

Now for a map
\sigma:T[1]\mathscr{X}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{iso}(d-1,1)[1]
,
which defines a vielbein and a spin-connection via

\sigma^{*}(\xi^{a})=\theta^{\mu}e_{\mu}^{a}(x)\,,\qquad\sigma^{*}(\rho^{ab})=\theta^{\mu}\omega_{\mu}^{ab}(x)\,,

(3.27)

the functional (
2.15
) associated with
the above
(p-1)
-form
\Theta
reads

\int_{\mathscr{X}}\epsilon_{a_{1}\dots a_{n+1-2p}b_{1}c_{1}\dots b_{p}c_{p}}\,e^{a_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e^{a_{n+1-2p}}\wedge R^{b_{1}c_{1}}[\omega]\wedge\dots\wedge R^{b_{p}c_{p}}[\omega]\,,

(3.28)

where

R^{ab}[\omega]=\mathrm{d}\omega^{ab}+\omega^{a}{}_{c}\wedge\omega^{cb}\,,

(3.29)

is the Riemann
2
-form curvature of the spin-connection.
For
p=2
, this is nothing but the Cartan–Weyl / Palatini action
for gravity discussed as a presymplectic AKSZ model
in
[
24
]
, while considering a linear combination
of such functionals with different values of
p
corresponds
to Lovelock gravity models.

3.2
MacDowell–Mansouri–Stelle–West action

Four-dimensional case.

As a second example of multisymplectic AKSZ sigma model,
let us mention the MacDowell–Mansouri–Stelle–West (MMSW) action
for gravity in
4d

[
30
,
31
,
32
]
. In this case, the relevant target space

Q
-manifold is

\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle MMSW}=\mathbb{R}^{5}\times\mathfrak{o}(3,2)[1]\,.

(3.30)

The coordinates are determined by the standard basis
in the orthogonal algebra and its vectorial module,
and are denoted by
V^{\mathsf{A}}

and
\rho^{\mathsf{AB}}=-\rho^{\mathsf{BA}}
, of degree
0

and
1
respectively. The action of
\mathcal{Q}
is determined by

QV^{\mathsf{A}}=-\rho^{\mathsf{A}}{}_{\mathsf{B}}V^{\mathsf{B}}\,,\qquad Q\rho^{\mathsf{AB}}=-\rho^{\mathsf{A}}{}_{\mathsf{C}}\rho^{\mathsf{CB}}\,,

(3.31)

where the indices
\mathsf{A}=0^{\prime},0,1,2,3
are raised
and lowered with the flat metric
\eta_{\mathsf{AB}}

of signature
(-,-,+,+,+)
.
As a multisymplectic form, we take

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}=\mathrm{d}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[2]}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[2]}=\epsilon_{\mathsf{ABCDE}}V^{\mathsf{A}}\mathrm{d}\rho^{\mathsf{BC}}\mathrm{d}\rho^{\mathsf{DE}}\,,

(3.32)

and a straightforward computation leads to

-\imath_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}=\mathrm{d}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[1]}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[1]}=2\,\epsilon_{\mathsf{ABCDE}}\,V^{\mathsf{A}}\rho^{\mathsf{B}}{}_{\mathsf{F}}\rho^{\mathsf{FC}}\mathrm{d}\rho^{\mathsf{DE}}\,,

(3.33)

and

\tfrac{1}{2}\imath_{Q}\imath_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}=\mathrm{d}\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[0]}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[0]}=\epsilon_{\mathsf{ABCDE}}\,V^{\mathsf{A}}\rho^{\mathsf{B}}{}_{\mathsf{F}}\rho^{\mathsf{FC}}\rho^{\mathsf{D}}{}_{\mathsf{G}}\rho^{\mathsf{GE}}

(3.34)

upon using the invariance of
\epsilon_{\mathsf{ABCDE}}

under
\mathfrak{o}(3,2)
. Since the
3
-form
\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}

has homological degree
2
, it has the correct total degree
to define an action on a four-dimensional spacetime
\mathscr{X}
:
with the above data, the formula (
2.15
) yields

S[\omega,V]=\int_{\mathscr{X}}\epsilon_{\mathsf{ABCDE}}\,V^{\mathsf{A}}\,R^{\mathsf{BC}}\wedge R^{\mathsf{DE}}\,,\qquad R^{\mathsf{AB}}:=\mathrm{d}\omega^{\mathsf{AB}}+\omega^{\mathsf{A}}{}_{\mathsf{C}}\wedge\omega^{\mathsf{CB}}\,,

(3.35)

thereby reproducing the MMSW action
for
4d
gravity—modulo the subtlety of constraining
the norm of the ‘compensator field’
V^{\mathsf{A}}
. Let us also mention the presymplectic AKSZ formulation
[
23
]
of the MMSW gravity, which gives an equivalent first order action.

Higher-dimensional generalisation.

Taking a step back, it seems that the above construction
could be carried out similarly for any Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

and a module
\mathcal{V}
thereof,
with
\Omega\in(\mathcal{V}^{*}\otimes S^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}

an invariant symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}

with values in
\mathcal{V}^{*}
, or equivalently a Chevalley–Eilenberg

0
-cocycle valued in the representation
\mathcal{V}^{*}\otimes S^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
In this situation,

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{V}\times\mathfrak{g}[1]\,,

(3.36)

is a
Q
-manifold with degree-
0
coordinates
V^{I}

for the
\mathcal{V}
factor, and degree-
1
coordinates
c^{a}

for the
\mathfrak{g}[1]
part, and whose homological vector field
is given by

QV^{I}=-c^{a}\,r_{a\,J}{}^{I}\,V^{J}\,,\qquad Qc^{a}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,f_{bc}{}^{a}\,c^{b}c^{c}\,,

(3.37)

where
r_{a\,I}{}^{J}
and
f_{bc}{}^{a}
are, respectively,
the components of the representation

r:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{V})
and the structure constants
of
\mathfrak{g}
, in the basis corresponding to the coordinate system
introduced above. Let us consider the
p
-form

{\bigwedge}^{p}\mathcal{M}\ni\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}=\Omega_{I_{1}\dots I_{p-k}|a_{1}\dots a_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}V^{I_{1}}\dots\mathrm{d}V^{I_{p-k}}\mathrm{d}c^{a_{1}}\dots\mathrm{d}c^{a_{k}}\,,\qquad\deg(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]})=k\,,

(3.38)

of homological degree
k
, which is obviously closed
and exact. Its invariance under
Q

L_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad(p-k)\,r_{b\,[I_{1}|}{}^{J}\Omega_{J|I_{2}\dots I_{p-k}]|a_{1}\dots a_{k}}+k\,f_{b(a_{1}|}{}^{c}\,\Omega_{I_{1}\dots I_{p-k}|a_{2}\dots a_{k})c}=0\,,

(3.39)

is equivalently to the requirement that
\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}

defines a Chevalley–Eilenberg
0
-cocycle valued in

\wedge^{p-k}\mathcal{V}^{*}\otimes S^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, that is

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}\in(\wedge^{p-k}\mathcal{V}^{*}\otimes S^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}
.
As a potential
(p-1)
-form for
\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}
,
let us choose

{\bigwedge}^{p-1}\mathcal{M}\ni\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[p-1]}=V^{J}\Omega_{JI_{1}\dots I_{p-k-1}|a_{1}\dots a_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}V^{I_{1}}\dots\mathrm{d}V^{I_{p-k-1}}\mathrm{d}c^{a_{1}}\dots\mathrm{d}c^{a_{k}}\,,

(3.40)

by analogy with the previous example
of MacDowell–Mansouri–Stelle–West gravity.
In order to find the completion of this potential
to an inhomogeneous form verifying

\mathrm{d}\Theta=e^{-\imath_{Q}}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}
,
we can use the operator

h(\alpha):=\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,V^{I}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{d}V^{I}}\alpha(t\,V,t\,\mathrm{d}V,c,\mathrm{d}c)\,,\qquad\forall\alpha\in{\bigwedge}(\mathcal{V}\times\mathfrak{g}[1])\,,

(3.41)

which defines a contracting homotopy for the de Rham
differential on
\mathcal{V}\times\mathfrak{g}[1]
, namely it satisfies

\mathrm{d}\circ h+h\circ\mathrm{d}=\mathbf{1}
on forms
with non-zero components in the direction of
\mathcal{V}
.
This contracting homotopy obviously reproduces the choice
of potential
\Theta_{\scriptscriptstyle[p-1]}
, and since

e^{-\imath_{Q}}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]}=\Omega_{I_{1}\dots I_{p-k}|a_{1}\dots a_{k}}DV^{I_{1}}\dots DV^{I_{p-k}}f^{a_{1}}(c)\dots f^{a_{k}}(c)\,,

(3.42)

where

DV^{I}:=\mathrm{d}V^{I}+c^{a}\,r_{a\,J}{}^{I}V^{J}\,,

(3.43)

is homogeneous in its dependency in
V^{I}
,
the action of the contracting homotopy is particularly simple,
and yields

\Theta=h(e^{-\imath_{Q}}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[p]})=\Omega_{I_{1}I_{2}\dots I_{p-k}|a_{1}\dots a_{k}}V^{I_{1}}DV^{I_{2}}\dots DV^{I_{p-k}}f^{a_{1}}(c)\dots f^{a_{k}}(c)\,.

(3.44)

Specialising to the case
k=2
and
p=n-1
,
the above data gives rise to an action
for a
0
-form
C^{I}=\sigma^{*}(V^{I})

and a
1
-form gauge field
A^{a}=\sigma^{*}(c^{a})

determined by a map

\sigma:T[1]\mathscr{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}\times\mathfrak{g}[1]

with
\dim\mathscr{X}=n
, which reads

\displaystyle S[C,A]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{X}}\Omega\big(C,E,\dots,E|F[A],F[A]\big)

(3.45a)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\mathscr{X}}\Omega_{I_{1}I_{2}\dots I_{n-3}|ab}\,C^{I_{1}}E^{I_{2}}\wedge\dots E^{I_{n-3}}\wedge F^{a}\wedge F^{b}\,,

(3.45b)

where we used the notation

E^{I}:=\mathrm{d}C^{I}+A^{a}\,r_{a\,J}{}^{I}\,C^{J}\,,\qquad F[A]:=\mathrm{d}A+\tfrac{1}{2}[A,A]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,.

(3.46)

The above action is obviously invariant under

\delta_{\varepsilon}A=\mathrm{d}\varepsilon+[A,\varepsilon]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,,\qquad\delta_{\varepsilon}C=r(\varepsilon)C\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\varepsilon\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathscr{X})\otimes\mathfrak{g}\,,

(3.47)

as a consequence of the
\mathfrak{g}
-invariance of
\Omega
.
For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}(n-1,2)
,
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
the
(n-2)
-form

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[n-2]}=\epsilon_{\mathsf{ABCDE_{1}\dots E_{n-3}}}\mathrm{d}V^{\mathsf{E_{1}}}\dots\mathrm{d}V^{\mathsf{E_{n-3}}}\mathrm{d}\rho^{\mathsf{AB}}\mathrm{d}\rho^{\mathsf{CD}}\,,

(3.48)

the inhomogeneous form of total degree
n
obtained
by (
3.44
) reads

\displaystyle\Theta=\epsilon_{\mathsf{ABCDE_{1}\dots E_{n-3}}}V^{\mathsf{E_{1}}}(\mathrm{d}V^{\mathsf{E_{1}}}+\rho^{\mathsf{E_{1}}}{}_{\mathsf{F_{1}}}V^{\mathsf{F_{1}}})\ldots

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}V^{\mathsf{E_{n-3}}}+\rho^{\mathsf{E_{n-3}}}{}_{\mathsf{F_{n-3}}}V^{\mathsf{F_{n-3}}})

(3.49)

\displaystyle\times(\mathrm{d}\rho^{\mathsf{AB}}+\rho^{\mathsf{A}}{}_{\mathsf{G}}\rho^{\mathsf{GB}})(\mathrm{d}\rho^{\mathsf{CD}}+\rho^{\mathsf{C}}{}_{\mathsf{H}}\rho^{\mathsf{HD}})\,,

and reproduces the higher-dimensional generalisation
of the MacDowell–Mansouri–Stelle–West action
discussed previously once pulled back a map

\sigma:T[1]\mathscr{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}

for a spacetime
\mathscr{X}
of dimension
\dim\mathscr{X}=n
,
i.e.

S[\omega,V]=\int_{\mathscr{X}}\epsilon_{\mathsf{ABCDE_{1}\dots E_{n-4}}}V^{\mathsf{E_{1}}}E^{\mathsf{E_{2}}}\wedge\dots\wedge E^{\mathsf{E_{n-4}}}\wedge R^{\mathsf{AB}}\wedge R^{\mathsf{CD}}\,,\qquad E^{\mathsf{A}}:=\mathrm{d}V^{\mathsf{A}}+\omega^{\mathsf{A}}{}_{\mathsf{B}}V^{\mathsf{B}}\,.

(3.50)

In this situation, we see that the combination
E

of (
3.46
) gives rise in this case to the frame
defined by the covariant derivative of the compensator field.

3.3
Self-dual gravity and its higher spin extension

Self-dual gravity in four dimensions with the cosmological constant

[
33
]
, and without
[
34
]
,
can also be obtained as a multisymplectic AKSZ model.
The relevant
Q
-manifold has a structure similar to that
of MacDowell–Mansouri–Stelle–West formulation of gravity,
namely

\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle SDGR}=\mathcal{V}_{5}\times\mathfrak{g}[1]\,,

(3.51)

where
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{su}(2)
for the Euclidean signature
and
\mathcal{V}_{5}
is its dimension
5
irreducible representation.
Indeed, in Euclidean signature the four-dimensional Lorentz algebra
is replaced with the orthogonal algebra

\mathfrak{so}(4)\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)
, so that
\mathfrak{g}
corresponds
to the subspace in which the self-dual component
of the spin-connection takes values (i.e. one of the
\mathfrak{su}(2)

factors) and
\mathcal{V}_{5}
to the irrep in which the self-dual part
of the Weyl tensor takes values.

The graded manifold (
3.51
) is endowed with
a homological vector field, whose action on coordinates

c^{AB}
and
V^{ABCD}
with
A,B,\dots=1,2
,
of degree
1
and
0
respectively, is given by

\displaystyle Qc^{AB}

\displaystyle=-c{}^{A}{}_{C}\,c^{CB}\,,

\displaystyle QV^{ABCD}=-4\,c{}^{(A}{}_{E}\,V^{BCD)E}\,.

(3.52)

where the two-component spinor indices are raised and lowered
with the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
-invariant tensor
\epsilon^{AB}=-\epsilon^{BA}

according to
v^{A}=\epsilon^{AB}v_{B}
and
v_{A}=v^{B}\epsilon_{BA}
.
7
7

7

Note that, accordingly, we use the convention

\epsilon^{AC}\epsilon_{BC}=\delta^{A}_{B}
for the inverse
of this tensor.

The case of vanishing cosmological constant is even simpler,
as we take the same graded manifold but with trivial
Q
-structure,

\displaystyle Qc^{AB}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle QV^{ABCD}=0\,,

(3.53)

which amounts to considering the contraction of
\mathfrak{so}(4)

obtained by making the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
factor accounting for the self-dual
piece of the spin-connection Abelian
[
34
, Sec. 4]
.
This
Q
-manifold is also equipped with an invariant
3
-form,

\Omega=\mathrm{d}V^{ABCD}\mathrm{d}c_{AB}\mathrm{d}c_{CD}\,,

(3.54)

which verifies

e^{-\imath_{Q}}\Omega=\mathrm{d}\Theta\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\Theta=V^{ABCD}\,(\mathrm{d}c_{AB}+\lambda\,c_{AE}\,c{}^{E}{}_{B}\,)(\mathrm{d}c_{CD}+\lambda\,c_{AF}\,c{}^{F}{}_{D}\,)\,,

(3.55)

where we introduced the parameter
\lambda
which we can set to
0

for vanishing cosmological constant, and
1
otherwise.
Denoting the fields resulting from the pullback
by
\sigma:T[1]\mathscr{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle SDGR}

of the coordinate functions

\sigma^{*}(c^{AB})=\theta^{\mu}\omega_{\mu}^{AB}(x)\,,\qquad\sigma^{*}(V^{ABCD})=\Psi^{ABCD}(x)\,,

(3.56)

one finds that the action (
2.15
)
obtained from the above data reads

S[\omega,\Psi]=\int_{\mathscr{X}}\Psi^{ABCD}\,R_{AB}\wedge R_{CD}\,,\qquad R_{AB}:=\mathrm{d}\omega_{AB}+\lambda\,\omega_{AC}\wedge\omega^{C}{}_{B}\,\,,

(3.57)

that is, we recover the action for the self-dual gravity
with the cosmological constant
[
33
]
.
Similarly, the second
Q
-manifold with the same
\Omega
gives

S[\omega,\Psi]=\int_{\mathscr{X}}\Psi^{ABCD}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{AB}\wedge\mathrm{d}\omega_{AB}\,,

(3.58)

which is the self-dual gravity with the vanishing cosmological
constant
[
34
]
.
These actions are invariant under the gauge transformations

\delta_{\xi}\omega_{AB}=\mathrm{d}\xi_{AB}+2\lambda\,\omega_{C(A}\xi^{C}{}_{B)}\,,\qquad\delta_{\xi}\Psi^{ABCD}=4\,\xi_{E}{}^{(A}\Psi^{BCD)E}\,,

(3.59)

which are accounted for by vertical vector fields
of the form
Y=\xi_{AB}(x)\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial c_{AB}}

when used in the formula (
2.26
).
The action are also invariant under diffeomorphisms,

\delta_{\eta}\omega_{AB}=L_{\eta}\omega_{AB}\,,\qquad\delta_{\eta}\Psi^{ABCD}=L_{\eta}\Psi^{ABCD}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\eta\in\Gamma(T\mathscr{X})\,,

(3.60)

which can be represented in terms of the degree
-1

vector field on
T[1]\mathscr{X}
as,

y=\eta^{\mu}(x)\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{\mu}}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad[y,d_{\mathscr{X}}]=\eta^{\mu}(x)\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}+\theta^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\eta^{\nu}(x)\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{\nu}}\,,

(3.61)

i.e. the Lie bracket of
y
with the homological vector field

d_{\mathscr{X}}
is nothing but the vector field on
T[1]\mathscr{X}

corresponding to the Lie derivative along
\eta

(acting on forms on
\mathscr{X}
). Transforming the section
\sigma

according to

\delta_{y}\sigma^{*}=L_{[y,d_{\mathscr{X}}]}\circ\sigma^{*}

(3.62)

reproduces the Lie derivative of
\omega_{AB}

and
\Psi^{ABCD}
when acting on the coordinates
c_{AB}

and
V^{ABCD}
respectively, and hence yields (
3.60
).

Let us note that the Krasnov formulation of the self-dual gravity with the cosmological constant naturally emerges from the Plebański action
[
35
]
. The latter is straightforwardly another example of a multisymplectic theory.

Higher spin extension.

Self-dual gravity in four dimensions
admits an higher spin extension
[
36
]
,
8
8

8

It was fist discovered in the light-cone gauge
in
[
37
]
as a truncation of chiral higher-spin
gravity
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
.

whose action takes the form

S[\omega,\Psi]=\int_{\mathscr{X}}\boldsymbol{\langle}\Psi,F[\omega]\wedge F[\omega]\boldsymbol{\rangle}\,,\qquad F[\omega]=\mathrm{d}\omega+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\{\omega,\omega\}\,,

(3.63)

where
\Psi
and
\omega
are respectively
a
0
- and
1
- form valued in

\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}:=\mathbb{C}[y^{A}]\,,\qquad A=1,2\,,

(3.64)

which is a Poisson algebra, with Poisson bracket

\{f,g\}:=\epsilon^{AB}\,\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y^{A}}\tfrac{\partial g}{\partial y^{B}}\,,\qquad f,g\in\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}\,.

(3.65)

Note that this algebra admits a subalgebra, namely the subspace
consisting of even polynomials in
y^{A}
, which allows one to focus
on bosonic fields only.
The final ingredient used in the action (
3.63
)
is an invariant form
\boldsymbol{\langle}-,-\boldsymbol{\rangle}
defined
on a pair of elements
f,g\in\mathbb{C}[y^{A}]

of the form
f=\sum_{m\geq 0}\tfrac{1}{m!}\,f^{A(m)}\,y_{A(m)}

and
g=\sum_{k\geq 0}\tfrac{1}{k!}\,g_{A(k)}y^{A(k)}
as

\boldsymbol{\langle}f,g\boldsymbol{\rangle}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{n!}\,f^{A(n)}\,g_{A(n)}\,.

(3.66)

This action is invariant under

\delta_{\xi,\eta}\omega=\mathrm{d}\xi+\{\omega,\xi\}+\imath_{\eta}F[\omega]\,,\qquad\delta_{\xi,\eta}\Psi=\Psi\boldsymbol{\circ}\xi+\imath_{\eta}\big(\mathrm{d}\Psi-\Psi\boldsymbol{\circ}\omega\big)\,,

(3.67)

where

\bullet

The operation

\boldsymbol{\circ}:\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}^{*}\,,

(3.68)

defines a right representation of the Poisson bracket
on the dual module of
\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}
by

\boldsymbol{\langle}\psi\boldsymbol{\circ}f,g\boldsymbol{\rangle}=\boldsymbol{\langle}\psi,\{f,g\}\boldsymbol{\rangle}\,,\qquad\psi,f,g\in\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}\,,

(3.69)

where we use the fact that
\boldsymbol{\langle}-,-\boldsymbol{\rangle}

is non-degenerate to identify an element
of
\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}^{*}
as
\boldsymbol{\langle}\psi,-\boldsymbol{\rangle}

for some
\psi\in\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}
.

\bullet

The shift parameter

\eta\in\Gamma(T\mathscr{X})\otimes\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}

is a vector field valued in the higher spin algebra, i.e.

\delta_{\eta}\omega=\eta^{\mu}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial(\mathrm{d}x^{\mu})}\,F\,,\qquad\eta=\sum_{s\geq 0}\tfrac{1}{n!}\,\eta^{\mu}_{A_{1}\dots A_{n}}(x)\,y^{A_{1}}\dots y^{A_{n}}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\,.

(3.70)

We have essentially the same structures at hand
than in the previous cases of MacDowell–Mansouri–Stelle–West
or self-dual gravity, namely a Lie algebra together with
a representation and an invariant form on the latter
tensored with the symmetric tensor product of the former.
More precisely, we should consider here the graded manifold

\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle HS-SDGR}=\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}\times\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}[1]\,,

(3.71)

which is again concentrated in degree
0
and
1
.
A basis of
\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}
is given by (even) monomials
in
y^{A}
tensored which leads to

\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}={\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\big\{y^{A_{1}}\dots y^{A_{n}}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}\qquad\leadsto\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} V_{A_{1}\dots A_{n}}&&\text{in deg. }0\,,\\
c_{A_{1}\dots A_{n}}&&\text{in deg. }1\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.72)

for coordinates on
\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle HS-SDGR}
.
Their pullback by
\sigma:T[1]\mathscr{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle HS-SDGR}

defines the basic fields participating in the higher spin
self-dual gravity action (
3.63
), namely

\sigma^{*}(c^{A_{1}\dots A_{n}})=\theta^{\mu}\omega_{\mu}^{A_{1}\dots A_{n}}(x)\,,\qquad\sigma^{*}(V^{A_{1}\dots A_{n}})=\Psi^{A_{1}\dots A_{n}}(x)\,,

(3.73)

which are nothing but the components of a
1
-form
and a
0
-form, both valued in
\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}
.
Now defining

\Omega:\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}\otimes S^{2}\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}\qquad\text{by}\qquad\Omega(\psi|f,g):=\boldsymbol{\langle}\psi,f\cdot g\boldsymbol{\rangle}\,,\qquad\psi,f,g\in\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}\,,

(3.74)

and thinking of the first factor as a representation
of the Lie algebra
\big(\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}},\{-,-\}\big)
via

r(f)\psi:=-\psi\boldsymbol{\circ}f\,,\qquad f,\psi\in\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}\,,

(3.75)

one can check that
\Omega
obeys the invariance property

\Omega\big(r(\varepsilon)\psi|f,g\big)+\Omega(\psi|\{\varepsilon,f\},g\big)+\Omega(\psi|f,\{\varepsilon,g\}\big)=0\,,

(3.76)

as a consequence of the fact that
\{-,-\}
is a Poisson bracket
(more specifically it satisfies the Leibniz identity),
and the definition (
3.69
). Equivalently,
we present
\Omega
as a
3
-form on
\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle HS-SDGR}
,
whose explicit expression reads

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}=\sum_{m+n\geq 0}\tfrac{1}{(m+n)!}\,\mathrm{d}V^{A_{1}\dots A_{m+n}}\mathrm{d}c_{A_{1}\dots A_{m}}\mathrm{d}c_{A_{m+1}\dots A_{m+n}}\,.

(3.77)

As expected, the
Q
-structure on
\mathcal{M}
is nothing but
Chevalley–Eilenberg differential associated with
\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}}

in the representation
\big(\mathfrak{hs}_{\mathtt{Gr}},r\big)
,
which schematically takes the form

\displaystyle Qc_{A_{1}\dots A_{n}}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\,c_{B(A_{1}\dots A_{m}}\,c^{B}{}_{A_{m+1}\dots A_{n})}\,,

(3.78a)

\displaystyle QV^{A_{1}\dots A_{n}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m\geq 0}\binom{m+n}{n}V^{B_{1}\dots B_{m}(A_{1}\dots A_{n-1}}c^{A_{n})}{}_{B_{1}\dots B_{m}}\,,

(3.78b)

and one can check that
L_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}=0

is equivalent to the invariance condition (
3.76
).

3.4
Twistor space formulation of self-dual gravity

Self-dual gravity, as well as its supergravity extension,
admit a twistor formulation
[
41
]

(see also
[
42
,
43
]
for an overview).
Focusing on the non-supersymmetric case, the idea
is to work on a
3
-dimensional almost complex manifold

\mathcal{PT}
, and consider a line subbundle

L^{*}\subset T^{*}\mathcal{PT}
in the cotangent bundle
of this complex manifold. Locally, this line subbundle
is specified by a
L
-valued
1
-form

\tau\in{\bigwedge}^{1}\mathcal{PT}\otimes L
,
and introducing another
1
-form

b\in{\bigwedge}^{1}\mathcal{PT}\otimes(L^{*})^{\otimes 3}

valued in the third tensor power of
L^{*}
, to play
the role of Lagrange multiplier in the following action

S[b,\tau]=\int_{\mathcal{PT}}b\wedge\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\tau\,,

(3.79)

thereby enforcing
\tau\wedge(\mathrm{d}\tau)^{\wedge 2}=0

as equations of motion. The latter equation implies
that the almost complex structure on
\mathcal{PT}

is integrable
[
41
, Th. 3]
. The action
is invariant under

b\to\tfrac{1}{f^{3}}\,b\,,\qquad\tau\to f\,\tau\,,\qquad f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{PT},\mathbb{C})\,,

(3.80)

which merely expressed the fact that the integrand
of the action (
3.79
) is of weight
0
,
in the sense of homogeneity in the line bundle
L
.

In order to reproduce the above action as a multisymplectic
AKSZ sigma model, let us consider the manifold

\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle tw.-SDGR}=\big(L\oplus(L^{*})^{\otimes 3}\big)[1]\,,

(3.81)

which is concentrated in degree
0
and
1
,
equipped with the
trivial

Q
-structure.
Denoting by
x^{\mu}
the degree
0
coordinates,
i.e. coordinate on the base manifold
\mathcal{PT}
,
and by
\lambda
and
\beta
the degree
1
coordinates,
i.e. the coordinates along the fibres of
L
and
L^{*}

respectively, the relevant pair of fields originate
from the pullback by a map
\sigma:T[1]\mathcal{PT}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle tw.-SDGR}

of the degree
1
coordinates, namely

\sigma^{*}(\lambda)=\theta^{\mu}\tau_{\mu}(x)\,,\qquad\sigma^{*}(\beta)=\theta^{\mu}b_{\mu}(x)\,.

(3.82)

The closed form on
\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle tw.-SDGR}
of interest
for us reads

\Omega=\big(\mathrm{d}\beta\,\lambda-\beta\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\big)\wedge\mathrm{d}\lambda\wedge\mathrm{d}\lambda\equiv\mathrm{d}\Theta\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\Theta=\beta\wedge\lambda\wedge\mathrm{d}\lambda\wedge\mathrm{d}\lambda\,.

(3.83)

Note that
\mathrm{tdeg}(\Omega)=7
, as is appropriate to construct
an action functional on a
real
six-dimensional manifold,
which is our case here for
\mathcal{PT}
. One can easily see
that the pullback by
\sigma^{*}\Theta
yields back the action
(
3.79
). Another straightforward check is that
the degree
0
vector field

V=\lambda\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}-3\beta\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\imath_{V}\Omega=0\,,

(3.84)

sits in the kernel of
\Omega
, and hence generates a symmetry
of the action. This vector field can be thought of
as an Euler vector field measuring the “
L
-weight” in target space,
and unsurprisingly generates the infinitesimal version
of the rescaling symmetry (
3.80
).

3.5
Sparling gravity

In a couple of papers, Sparling proved that Einstein’s
vacuum field equations can be reformulated as the closure
of a certain
3
-form, either on the bundle of orthonormal frames
[
44
]
, or on the spin bundle

[
45
,
46
]
, of the four-dimensional
spacetime manifold. Here we focus on the latter, and show
that the action functional defined by lifting the closure
of the Sparling
3
-form using a Lagrange multiplier
can be recovered as a multisymplectic AKSZ action.

The Sparling
3
-form is given by

\mathfrak{S}:=\nabla\pi_{A^{\prime}}\wedge\nabla\bar{\pi}_{A}\wedge e^{AA^{\prime}}\,,

(3.85)

where
\pi_{A^{\prime}}
and
\bar{\pi}_{A}
are primed and unprimed spinors,

\nabla
a not necessarily torsion-free connection and
e^{AA^{\prime}}

a vierbein. Introducing a Lagrange multiplier
\Psi
,
one can simply consider the following action functional,

S[\Psi,\pi,\bar{\pi},\omega,e]=\int_{\mathscr{X}}\Psi\wedge\nabla\mathfrak{S}\,,

(3.86)

whose equations of motion with respect to
\Psi

impose that
\mathfrak{S}
be closed, and hence that
Einstein’s equations be satisfied.

In order to reproduce this action as a multisymplectic
AKSZ action, let us introduce the graded manifold

\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle Sparling}=\mathfrak{g}[1]\times\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}\,,

(3.87)

where
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(4)\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)
in Euclidean signature,
or the complexification of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
if one avoids fixing
reality conditions, and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{S}

are the primed and unprimed spinors representations.
The
Q
-structure is given by the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential
of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e. it reads

Q\rho_{AB}=-\rho_{AC}\rho_{B}{}^{C}\,,\qquad Q\rho_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=-\rho_{A^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\rho_{B^{\prime}}{}^{C^{\prime}}\,,\qquad Q\xi^{AA^{\prime}}=-\rho^{A}{}_{B}\xi^{BA^{\prime}}-\rho^{A^{\prime}}{}_{B^{\prime}}\xi^{AB^{\prime}}\,,

(3.88)

on the degree
1
coordinates
\rho_{AB}
,
\rho_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

and
\xi_{AA^{\prime}}
(with
A,A^{\prime},\dots=1,2
), and

QV=0\,,\qquad Q\psi_{A^{\prime}}=-\rho_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\,\psi^{B^{\prime}}\,,\qquad Q\bar{\psi}_{A}=-\rho_{AB}\,\bar{\psi}^{B}\,,

(3.89)

on the degree
0
coordinate
V
for the
\mathbb{R}
-factor,
and
\psi_{A^{\prime}}
and
\bar{\psi}_{A}
for the
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

and
\mathcal{S}
factor.
In this case, the relevant form is inhomogeneous
in form degree: it has a
3
-form and a
4
-form component,

\Omega=\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[4]}+\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}\,,\qquad\mathrm{tdeg}(\Omega)=5\,,

(3.90)

respectively given by

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[4]}=\mathrm{d}V\mathrm{d}\psi_{A^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\bar{\psi}_{A}\mathrm{d}\xi^{AA^{\prime}}\,,\qquad\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}=\mathrm{d}V\big(\mathrm{d}\bar{\psi}_{A}\mathrm{d}\rho_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\psi^{B^{\prime}}-\mathrm{d}\psi_{A^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\rho_{AB}\bar{\psi}^{B}\big)\xi^{AA^{\prime}}\,.

(3.91)

One can verify that this form is
(\mathrm{d}+L_{Q})
-closed,

\big(\mathrm{d}+L_{Q})\Omega=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} 0&=\mathrm{d}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[4]}\,,\\
0&=\mathrm{d}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}+L_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[4]}\,,\\
0&=L_{Q}\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle[3]}\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.92)

and that the form of total degree
4
given by

\Theta=V\,\mathrm{d}\vartheta\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\vartheta=\big(\mathrm{d}\psi_{A^{\prime}}+\rho_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\psi^{B^{\prime}})(\mathrm{d}\bar{\psi}_{A}+\rho_{AB}\bar{\psi}^{B})\xi^{AA^{\prime}}\,,

(3.93)

which is the target space counterpart of the Sparling
3
-form.
Finally, a map
\sigma:T[1]\mathscr{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\sf\scriptscriptstyle Sparling}

from the shifted tangent bundle of the four-dimensional
spacetime manifold
\mathscr{X}
to the target space defined
in (
3.87
) gives to a vierbein
and a spin-connection, via

\sigma^{*}(\xi^{AA^{\prime}})=\theta^{\mu}e_{\mu}^{AA^{\prime}}(x)\,,\qquad\sigma^{*}(\rho^{AB})=\theta^{\mu}\omega_{\mu}^{AB}(x)\,,\qquad\sigma^{*}(\rho^{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})=\theta^{\mu}\omega_{\mu}^{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}(x)\,,

(3.94)

as well as the spinors and Lagrange multiplier appearing
in the action (
3.86
), via

\sigma^{*}(V)=\Psi(x)\,,\qquad\sigma^{*}(\psi_{A^{\prime}})=\pi_{A^{\prime}}(x)\,,\qquad\sigma^{*}(\bar{\psi}_{A})=\bar{\pi}_{A}(x)\,.

(3.95)

One can check that applying the construction of the multisymplectic
AKSZ action (
2.15
) with the above data
yields the Sparling gravity action (
3.86
).

4
Relation to multisymplectic actions

In the preceding Sections we have restricted ourselves
to the case where the underlying
Q
-bundle is globally trivial
as a
Q
-bundle.
In plain terms this means that the target space and the source
are completely detached, i.e. the target space structures
do not depend on the source space coordinates. Although
the generalisation to the case of generally nontrivial
Q
-bundles
is relatively straightforward and gives a multisymplectic extension
of the presymplectic BV–AKSZ formalism
of
[
26
,
25
]
we refrain
from discussing it in full generality here because it would have
required introducing additional techniques. Nevertheless,
it is instructive to sketch the generalisation in the particular
but very important case where the fibre of the underlying bundle
is a usual (not graded) manifold or, in other words, the grading
is horizontal. In this case, the underlying geometry
is that of a bundle equipped with a flat connection,
i.e. PDE defined geometrically. In particular, the construction
can be entirely reformulated in terms of the conventional
differential geometry without resorting to supergeometry.
Moreover, it turns out that in this case the multisymplectic
AKSZ actions discussed in this work are quite similar
to multisymplectic actions known in the context
of multisymplectic formalism and geometry
of PDEs
[
27
,
47
,
48
]
.
In the standard symplectic case, i.e.
p=2
, this relation
was observed already in
[
49
]
,
see also
[
50
,
26
]
.

Let us first recall what is usually meant by multi-symplectic
systems. Let
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{X}
be a fibre bundle
equipped with the
n=\dim(\mathscr{X})
-form
\tilde{\Theta}
such that
it vanishes on any
p
vertical vectors and such that

\tilde{\Omega}\equiv\mathrm{d}\tilde{\Theta}
is nondegenerate
and vanishes on any
p+1
vertical vectors. It follows
that this data defines a natural functional on the space
of sections of
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
:

S[\tilde{\sigma}]=\int_{\mathscr{X}}\tilde{\sigma}^{*}(\tilde{\Theta})\,,\qquad\tilde{\sigma}:\mathscr{X}\longrightarrow{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}

(4.1)

Of course, this action is perfectly defined for any
n
-form

\tilde{\Theta}
but under the above condition the integrand
is of homogeneity
p-1
in
\mathrm{d}z^{I}(x)
,

z^{I}(x)\equiv\tilde{\sigma}^{*}(z^{I})
,
where
z^{I}
are fibre coordinates. The Euler–Lagrange equations
for this action can be formulated as follows:
a section
\tilde{\sigma}
is a solution if

\tilde{\sigma}^{*}(i_{V}\tilde{\Omega})=0\,,

(4.2)

for any vertical vector field
V
on
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
. In particular,
if a vertical vector field
W
belongs to the kernel
of
\tilde{\Omega}
then
\delta\tilde{\sigma}^{*}=\tilde{\sigma}^{*}\circ\epsilon W
is an algebraic gauge symmetry
of the above action for an arbitrary function

\epsilon\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}({\tilde{\mathcal{E}}})
. Moreover,
if
W
is a regular vector field, then the action is effectively
defined on the quotient of
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
by the integral curves
of
W
, at least locally. This explains why it is natural
to require
\tilde{\Omega}
to be non-degenerate in the first place.

In applications to field theory, bundle
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}

usually comes equipped with additional structures.
Let us concentrate on the case when
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}

is equipped with a flat connection. In particular,

{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
can be naturally interpreted as PDE
and, reciprocally, any regular PDE gives rise
to such a bundle
[
51
,
52
]
.
Thanks to the flat connection, the differential forms
on
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
are bigraded by the horizontal,
and vertical form degree and the de Rham differential
decomposes as:

\mathrm{d}=\mathrm{d_{h}}+\mathrm{d_{v}}\,.

(4.3)

Assume for simplicity that
\tilde{\Omega}
is of definite bidegree
and hence is a
(n+1-p,p)
form satisfying

\mathrm{d_{v}}\tilde{\Omega}=0=\mathrm{d_{h}}\tilde{\Omega}
. It follows

\tilde{\Theta}=\tilde{\Theta}_{\scriptscriptstyle[p-1]}+\tilde{\Theta}_{\scriptscriptstyle[p-2]}+\ldots+\tilde{\Theta}_{\scriptscriptstyle[0]}\,,

(4.4)

and
\tilde{\Omega}=\mathrm{d_{v}}\tilde{\Theta}_{\scriptscriptstyle[p-1]}
.
Then the equations of motion can be written explicitly as

\tilde{\sigma}^{*}(\tilde{\Omega}_{IJ_{2}\ldots J_{p}})\,E_{\tilde{\sigma}}^{J_{2}}\ldots E_{\tilde{\sigma}}^{J_{p}}=0\,,\qquad E_{\tilde{\sigma}}^{I}\equiv\tilde{\sigma}^{*}\mathrm{d_{v}}z^{I}=\mathrm{d}\tilde{\sigma}^{*}z^{I}-\tilde{\sigma}^{*}\mathrm{d_{h}}z^{I}\,,

(4.5)

where we introduced “vertical coefficients” of
\tilde{\Omega}

as
\tilde{\Omega}=\tilde{\Omega}_{I_{1}\ldots I_{p}}\mathrm{d_{v}}z^{I_{1}}\ldots\mathrm{d_{v}}z^{I}_{p}
. Note that these coefficients
are themselves horizontal forms, i.e.
(n+1-p,0)
-forms.
It is easy to see that if
\tilde{\sigma}
is such that

E_{\tilde{\sigma}}^{I}=0
then
\tilde{\sigma}
is a covariantly
constant section of
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
. In particular,
if we interpret
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
as a PDE then
such a
\tilde{\sigma}
is a solution. It follows
that the Euler–Lagrange equations for
S[\tilde{\sigma}]

are consequences of the equations of motion of
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}

seen as a PDE.

The above multisymplectic setup can be easily reformulated
in the supergeometrical terms of
Q
-bundles. Namely,
let
(\mathcal{E},Q)\to(T[1]\mathscr{X},d_{\mathscr{X}})
be a
Q
-bundle such that the grading is horizontal, i.e. the only nonvanishing degree coordinates are the fibre coordinates
\theta^{\mu}
of
T[1]\mathscr{X}
, then the
Q
-structure determines a flat connection in the bundle
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{X}
which is a restriction of
\mathcal{E}
to the zero section of
T[1]\mathscr{X}
. Note that in our setup
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{X}
is a bundle of usual (not graded) manifolds. Other way around, given a bundle
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{X}
equipped with a flat connection, there is a canonically associated
Q
-bundle which can be obtained by pulling back
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
by the canonical projection
T[1]\mathscr{X}\longrightarrow\mathscr{X}
and reinterpreting the connection as the
Q
-structure.

Moreover, in this setup there is a one-to-one relation between differential forms on
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
and vertical forms on
\mathcal{E}
. More precisely, let
\mathcal{I}
denote the ideal of differential forms on
\mathcal{E}
generated by the positive degree forms pulled back from the base
T[1]\mathscr{X}
. The quotient space
\bigwedge^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E})/\mathcal{I}
is the space of vertical forms. It is easy to check that it inherits the form degree and the ghost degree. In addition, the de Rham differential is well defined on equivalence classes and makes vertical forms into differential graded commutative algebra. Furthermore, vertical forms on
\mathcal{E}
defined in this way are naturally isomorphic to the differential forms on
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
. Under this isomorphism the de Rham differential goes to vertical differential on
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
, ghost degree goes into horizontal form degree, and
L_{Q}

is mapped to the horizontal differential
\mathrm{d_{h}}
, see
[
26
]
for further details. If
x^{\mu},\theta^{\mu},z^{I}
are local coordinates on
\mathcal{E}

one can take the restriction of
x^{\mu},z^{I}
to
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
seen as a submanifold in
\mathcal{E}
(pullback of
\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow T[1]\mathscr{X}
to the zero section of
T[1]\mathscr{X}
) as local coordinates on
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
. The above isomorphism can be defined on representatives as follows:

\Upsilon(\mathrm{d}x^{\mu})=\Upsilon(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\mu})=0\,,\quad\Upsilon(\theta^{\mu})=\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\,,\quad\Upsilon(\mathrm{d}z^{I})=\mathrm{d_{v}}z^{I}\,,\quad\Upsilon f(z,x)=f(z,x)\,.

(4.6)

The properties of
\Upsilon
are

\Upsilon(\mathrm{d}\alpha)=\mathrm{d_{v}}\Upsilon(\alpha)\,,\qquad\Upsilon(L_{Q}\alpha)=\mathrm{d_{h}}\Upsilon(\alpha)\,,

(4.7)

so that
\Upsilon\big((\mathrm{d}+L_{Q})\alpha\big)=\mathrm{d}\Upsilon(\alpha)
.
Moreover,
\Upsilon
is clearly compatible with the projections
and hence defines the map from
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(T[1]\mathscr{X})

to
\bigwedge^{\bullet}(\mathscr{X})
which by slight abuse of conventions
we keep denoting by
\Upsilon
.

The degree-preserving sections of
\mathcal{E}
are 1:1 with sections of
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
and we define by
\tilde{\sigma}
the section of
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
corresponding to
\sigma:T[1]X\to\mathcal{E}
. Then we have the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigwedge^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E})/\mathcal{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigwedge^{(\bullet,\bullet)}({\tilde{\mathcal{E}}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(T[1]\mathscr{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigwedge^{\bullet}(\mathscr{X})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{*}_{W}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\sigma}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upsilon}

(4.8)

It is commutative if
\sigma^{*}_{W}
is precisely the Chern–Weil map
defined in Section
2
. Indeed, it is enough
to check commutativity for functions and basis
1
-forms.
For functions it is obvious while for
1
-forms we get:

\Upsilon\big(\sigma^{*}_{W}(\mathrm{d}z^{I})\big)=\Upsilon\big(d_{\mathscr{X}}\sigma^{*}(z^{I})-\sigma^{*}(Qz^{I})\big)=\mathrm{d}\tilde{\sigma}^{*}(z^{I})-\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\tilde{\sigma}^{*}(D_{\mu}z^{I})\,,

(4.9)

and

\tilde{\sigma}^{*}\big(\Upsilon(\mathrm{d}z^{I})\big)=\tilde{\sigma}^{*}(\mathrm{d_{v}}z^{I})=\tilde{\sigma}^{*}\big(\mathrm{d}z^{I}-\mathrm{d_{h}}z^{I})=\mathrm{d}\tilde{\sigma}^{*}(z^{I})-\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\tilde{\sigma}^{*}(D_{\mu}z^{I})\,,

(4.10)

where we introduced the notation
Qz^{I}=\theta^{\mu}D_{\mu}z^{I}
.
Note also that
\mathrm{d_{h}}z^{I}=\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}D_{\mu}z^{I}
. This diagram shows
that under the identification of differential forms on
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}

and
\mathcal{E}
via
\Upsilon
the Chern–Weil map is just a pullback
by
\tilde{\sigma}
.
Note that the Chern–Weil map vanishes on differential forms
from
\mathcal{I}
and hence is well-defined on
\bigwedge^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E})/\mathcal{I}
.
For instance,
\sigma^{*}_{W}(\mathrm{d}x^{\mu})=d_{\mathscr{X}}\sigma^{*}(x^{\mu})-\sigma^{*}(Qx^{\mu})=0
.

In particular, let
\Omega
be a
\mathrm{d}
-closed and
L_{Q}
-closed

p
-form on
\mathcal{E}
such that
\mathrm{gh}(\Omega)=n-p+1
.
Then
\tilde{\Omega}\equiv\Upsilon(\Omega)
is an
(n+1-p,p)
-form
on
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
. Applying
\Upsilon
to both sides
of
\Omega=(L_{Q}+\mathrm{d})\Theta_{W}

one gets
\tilde{\Omega}=(\mathrm{d_{h}}+\mathrm{d_{v}})\Upsilon(\Theta_{W})
and the commutativity of the above diagram implies that

\int_{\mathscr{X}}\tilde{\sigma}^{*}\Upsilon(\Theta_{W})=\int_{T[1]\mathscr{X}}\sigma^{*}_{W}\Theta_{W}\,.

(4.11)

It follows the AKSZ action coincides with its multisymplectic
analog (
4.1
) modulo boundary term that vanishes
if
\tilde{\Theta}
defined via
\mathrm{d}\tilde{\Theta}=\tilde{\Omega}

modulo
\mathrm{d}
-exact form, is taken to be
\Upsilon(\Theta_{W})
. Note that if
\mathscr{X}
is a boundary of another manifold
\mathscr{Y}
then the multisymplectic action
\int_{\mathscr{X}}\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{\Theta})
is a boundary term of
\int_{\mathscr{Y}}\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{\Omega})
, where it is assumed that
\tilde{\sigma}
is extended to a section of the initial bundle extended to
\mathscr{Y}
. This is of course in agreement with the general statement from Section
2
.

5
Conclusions and Discussion

In this work we explicitly described the multisymplectic generalisation of AKSZ sigma models and presented a variety of examples. Although the main steps of the construction are valid for generic
Q
-bundles, all our examples are background independent and are described by locally trivial
Q
-bundles. The only exception is a version of the construction discussed in Section
4
, where the underlying bundle is a PDE. A full-scale generalisation to the case of generic
Q
-bundles deserves a separate discussion and nontrivial field-theoretical examples. In the case of
p=2
this generalisation amounts to passing from (presymplectic) AKSZ models to gauge PDEs equipped with compatible presymplectic structures
[
25
]
, see also
[
26
]
.

Although multisymplectic AKSZ models contain the usual AKSZ models as a particular case, they are more naturally viewed as generalisations of presymplectic AKSZ models, because they generally describe theories with local degrees of freedom even if the target space is finite dimensional. This happens because both in the presymplectic and in the multisymplectic setting the Euler–Lagrange equations are consequences of the
Q
-map condition, whereas in the usual AKSZ case these coincide, so that the model is topological unless the target space is infinite dimensional. There is, however, an important difference. A presymplectic BV–AKSZ model, or more generally a presymplectic gauge PDE, naturally encodes not only the action and gauge transformations but also the full local BV formulation of the underlying gauge theory, see
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
. In the multisymplectic case, we generally do not see a natural BV structure encoded in the multisymplectic AKSZ data. Of course, under certain regularity assumptions one can always construct the BV formulation of a given gauge theory, but it is not immediately clear how the BV formulation of a multisymplectic AKSZ model can be directly expressed in terms of its basic structures.

Another open question concerns the multisymplectic analogue of presymplectic gauge PDEs. In the presymplectic setup it is natural and very useful to replace the restrictive condition
Q^{2}=0
with a more flexible presymplectic master equation

\tfrac{1}{2}\imath_{Q}\imath_{Q}\omega+Q\mathcal{L}=0

which nevertheless defines a nilpotent differential on a certain subspace and naturally determines the BV formulation
[
25
,
26
]
.
9
9

9

Note that in the locally trivial setup the presymplectic master equation takes a more conventional form
\imath_{Q}\imath_{Q}\omega=0
, so that it is manifest that it defines a homological vector field on the symplectic quotient of the target space.
Just as in the presymplectic case, such a generalisation is expected to result in a more flexible formalism.

In this work we have limited ourselves to AKSZ-like models whose sources have the form
T[1]\mathscr{X}
, with
\mathscr{X}
being spacetime. Because the AKSZ construction can be formulated in algebraic terms, one can replace functions on the source (i.e. the exterior algebra of
\mathscr{X}
) with a more general differential graded algebra that is not necessarily freely generated, while still requiring it to be a module over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathscr{X})
in order not to lose the interpretation in terms of local fields. In this way one can describe nontopological theories even if the target space is a finite-dimensional symplectic
Q
-manifold. For instance, an elegant formulation of Yang–-Mills theory as an AKSZ sigma model of this type was put forward in
[
16
]
, see also
[
15
,
53
,
17
]
. Similar generalisations have recently turned out to be very useful in the AKSZ approach to superfield formulations
[
18
]
of supersymmetric systems.

An alternative other generalisation that affects the source space is to replace
T[1]\mathscr{X}
with another gauge PDE over
T[1]\mathscr{X}
and to relax the defining relations in such a way that the usual conditions are satisfied upon restriction to solutions of the base gauge PDE. This extension precisely corresponds to considering systems coupled to background fields described by the base gauge PDE, see
[
13
]
. There is little doubt that this generalisation can be carried out in the multisymplectic setting as well, but this is postponed to future work.

As far as the applications are concerned, there are plenty of examples in (mathematical) physics of theories that have higher derivative actions, but this does not always imply that the equations have higher derivatives in time. Examples include higher Chern–Simons theories, Lovelock gravity, Horndeski gravity

One immediate application of the formalism introduced in the paper is to higher-spin gravity. As it was discussed in

However, the higher-spin extension of self-dual gravity, which is its subsector, teaches us that the most natural action is of the multisymplectic type. It would be interesting to extend this result to the full theory.

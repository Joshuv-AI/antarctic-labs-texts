Fluctuation response of a superconductor with temporally correlated noise
V. Plastovets

Abstract
We discuss how a finite noise correlation time, which can arise through coupling to engineered nonthermal environments, affects the fluctuation-driven response in a superconductor above its critical temperature. Using the phenomenological time-dependent Ginzburg–Landau model, we formulate the stochastic dynamics within the path-integral framework. Our analysis reveals that the transport response can be enhanced when the noise correlation time becomes comparable to the intrinsic relaxation time of the superconductor. The magnitude and character of this resonant-like effect depend strongly on the system’s dimensionality.



Introduction.–

The time-dependent Ginzburg–Landau (TDGL) model describes the low-frequency dynamics of the order parameter weakly perturbed from its free energy minimum. A generalized TDGL equation
{\int dt^{\prime}K(t-t^{\prime})\psi(t^{\prime})=F[\psi]+\eta(t)}
includes a noise term
\eta
, a nonlinear function
F
and a kernel
K
, that may account for non-Markovian effects, representing memory in the system. In superconductors, however, standard microscopic derivation incorporating relaxation mechanisms always leads to a time-local kernel of the form
K(t-t^{\prime})\propto\delta(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{t^{\prime}}

Schmid 1966
;
Kawabata 1971
;
Lang et al. 2024
. Even when the system is coupled to an external bath with a spectral structure, generating significant non-Markovian effects remains nearly impossible
Ojeda Collado et al. 2019
;
Vadimov et al. 2019
;
Yang et al. 2021
;
Kapustin and Mrini 2023
. Generally speaking, this restriction originates from the structure of the fermionic density of states and quasiparticle occupation near the Fermi energy
E_{F}
, reflecting the inherently overdamped nature of low-energy superconducting gap dynamics in the vicinity of the critical temperature
T_{c}

Abrahams and Tsuneto 1966
;
Gor’kov and Eliashberg 1968
;
Hu 1980
;
Watts-Tobin et al. 1981
.

An external bath can, however, introduce an additional colored noise with distinct spectral properties. According to the fluctuation-dissipation theorem (FDT), the TDGL kernel
K
and the Langevin term
\eta
must be consistent
Levchenko and Kamenev 2007
;
Bonart et al. 2012
. We instead consider a scenario in which dissipation arises from an internal bath, while noise is controlled by a separate external reservoir or source. The resulting dissipative model with colored noise breaks the FDT, so the classical fluctuations of the order parameter can no longer be regarded as thermal and the system must be described in terms of nonequilibrium and nonthermal states
García-Ojalvo et al. 1992
;
*garcia1994colored
;
Seifert and Speck 2010
. Correlated noise is well known in condensed matter theory
Kim et al. 1998
;
Nalbach et al. 2015
, particularly within the TDGL formalism, where it plays a key role in driving and modifying phase transitions
García-Ojalvo et al. 1992
;
*garcia1994colored
. This effect is quite generic, as the TDGL belongs to the model-A universality class
Hohenberg and Halperin 1977
and thus has been intensively studied as a phenomenological framework across diverse systems
Bonart et al. 2012
.

In this work, to isolate the effect of colored noise, we focus on superconducting fluctuations above
T_{c}
, where their contribution is most pronounced
Larkin and Varlamov 2005
. We examine how a finite noise correlation time modifies observables in the fluctuation regime. Starting from the stochastic TDGL equation, we reformulate it into an effective action using the nonequilibrium Keldysh formalism
Martin et al. 1973
;
Levchenko and Kamenev 2007
;
Chtchelkatchev and Vinokur 2009
;
Bonart et al. 2012
;
Lang et al. 2024
and compute the thermoelectric transport response. We found that the finite correlation time imposes an energy cutoff for the order parameter modes, which can enhance certain components of the response functions in a resonant-like manner.

Theoretical framework.–

We start with phenomenological TDGL equation describing superconducting fluctuations with zero mean field at
T>T_{c}
:

\displaystyle\gamma\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=-\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\psi^{*}}+\eta({\bf r},t),

(1)

where
\mathcal{F}
is the GL free energy and
\gamma
is the relaxation constant. We assume the dissipation to arise from intrinsic microscopic mechanisms, while the Langevin noise to be generated by an external source with zero mean and correlations given by
\langle\eta({\bf r},t)\eta^{*}({\bf r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta({\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime})D(t-t^{\prime})
. We focus on dominant colored noise component, neglecting any internal contribution, and model it as temporally correlated:

\displaystyle D(t-t^{\prime})=\frac{D_{0}}{2\tau}\exp\left(-\frac{|t-t^{\prime}|}{\tau}\right)

(2)

with a Lorentzian-type spectral density
{D(\omega)=D_{0}/(1+\omega^{2}\tau^{2})}
and fixed amplitude
D_{0}=2T_{c}\gamma
, so that in the limit
\tau=0
it reduces to white noise with
D(t-t^{\prime})=2T_{c}\gamma\delta(t-t^{\prime})
. We assume
\gamma
to be real unless otherwise stated, and henceforth work with natural units
\hbar=c=1
.

The coarse-grained dynamics of the system governed by Eq. (
1
) can be described by an effective action
Chtchelkatchev and Vinokur 2009
;
Kamenev 2023
. Using the Martin–Siggia–Rose (MSR) formalism
Martin et al. 1973
, the stochastic evolution is encoded as a real-time path integral, which is given by effective action

\displaystyle S_{\text{MSR}}[\psi,\phi]=-\int d{\bf r}~\Big[\int dt~\phi^{*}\big(\gamma\partial_{t}\psi+\delta_{\psi^{*}}\mathcal{F}\big)+\text{c.c.}

\displaystyle-2i\iint dtdt^{\prime}~\phi^{*}(t)D(t-t^{\prime})\phi(t^{\prime})\Big].

(3)

Here we introduce an auxiliary field
\phi({\bf r},t)
, formally identified with the quantum component of the field
\psi_{\text{q}}
in the Keldysh field decomposition, which complements the classical component
\psi_{\text{cl}}\equiv\psi
. This basis naturally emerges from the real-time contour formulation and facilitates the description of stochastic dynamics.

Given the proximity to
T_{c}
, we expand the free energy up to the first term:
{\mathcal{F}[\psi]\approx a_{0}T_{c}\left(\epsilon|\psi|^{2}+\xi^{2}|(\nabla-2ie{\bf A})\psi|^{2}\right)}
, where
{\bf A}
is the vector potential,
a_{0}=8\gamma/\pi
is related to the relaxation parameter,
\xi
is the zero-temperature coherence length and
\epsilon=T/T_{c}-1
. Note that such Gaussian approximation is valid for
Gi_{\text{(D)}}\ll\epsilon
, where
Gi_{\text{(D)}}
is (dimension-dependent) Ginzburg number
Larkin and Varlamov 2005
. This expansion yields a Gaussian action, which in the translational invariant system reads as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{MSR}}[\psi,\phi]=

(4)

\displaystyle\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}(\psi^{*}\phi^{*})\begin{pmatrix}0&&[G^{A}]^{-1}(k,\omega)\\
[G^{R}]^{-1}(k,\omega)&&2iD(\omega)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}.

Here we defined the response functions
G^{R}(k,\omega)=\langle\psi\phi^{*}\rangle=(i\gamma\omega+\varepsilon_{k}+i0^{+})^{-1}
and
G^{A}=[G^{R}]^{*}
; correlation function
C(k,\omega)=(i/2)G_{K}(k,\omega)=\langle\psi\psi^{*}\rangle=G^{R}(k,\omega)D(\omega)G^{A}(k,\omega)

Kamenev 2023
; and the spectrum of the order parameter modes
{\varepsilon_{k}=a_{0}T_{c}(\epsilon+\xi^{2}{\bf k}^{2})}
.

Nonequilibrium steady-state.–

If the noise is white (
\tau\to 0
) the system is in detailed balance and thus satisfied the FDT:
{G^{K}=(G^{R}-G^{A})\coth(\omega/2T)}
. The TDGL regime always implies
\omega\ll T\sim T_{c}
, thus the FDT simply reads as
\omega G^{K}=4iT\text{Im}G^{R}
. In our model the colored and nonthermal noise is not balanced by local and Markovian dissipation in the RHS of the Eq. (
1
), like it was done in
Bonart et al. 2012
. The violation of DFT means that the system does not evolve to a thermal (or Boltzmann) state with
P[\psi]\sim e^{-\mathcal{F}[\psi]/T}
, which we refer as equilibrium steade state (ESS), but instead reaches a
nonequilibrium
steady state (NESS)
García-Ojalvo et al. 1992
;
*garcia1994colored
. In this regime the notion of a fluctuation temperature is ill-defined, and the analysis must proceed in terms of the correlator
D(\omega)
.

The average density of superconducting fluctuations is determined by the correlation function
C(k,\omega)
and reads as

\displaystyle\langle|\psi(k,\omega)|^{2}\rangle=\frac{D_{0}}{1+(\omega\tau)^{2}}\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}\omega^{2}+\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}.

(5)

We emphasize that the static structure factor
{S(k)=\langle|\psi(k)|^{2}\rangle=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\langle|\psi(k,\omega)|^{2}\rangle}
does not coincide with the statistical Gibbs averaging over the equilibrium fluctuation distribution. This can be understood as different
k
-modes having different relaxation time
\tau_{k}=\gamma/\varepsilon_{k}
, and therefore experiencing different noise strength. Low-
k
modes with
\tau_{k}\gg\tau
are unaffected by noise correlation time, as they are uniformly driven by the noise. In contrast, modes with
\tau_{k}\lesssim\tau
are sensitive to noise correlations, so the high-k sector is effectively not excited. This is the direct consequence of the FDT violation, and for white noise the thermal result
S(k)\equiv\langle|\psi(k)|^{2}\rangle_{th}
is recovered.

The NESS is characterized by a continuous entropy flow between the system and the reservoir generating the colored noise, with irreversibility quantified by entropy production
De Groot and Mazur 2013

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S_{\text{prod}}}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{T}\frac{d}{dt}\int d{\bf r}~\mathcal{F}[\psi({\bf r},t)].

(6)

In a deterministic case (
T<T_{c}
) entropy always grows during the order parameter relaxation, since
{d_{t}\mathcal{F}=-(1/\gamma)\left|\delta_{\psi}\mathcal{F}\right|^{2}<0}
, while for stochastic ESS the averaged rate of entropy production is zero
\langle\partial_{t}S_{\text{prod}}\rangle\propto\iint d{\bf k}d\omega~\varepsilon_{k}(i\omega)\langle|\psi({\bf k},\omega)|^{2}\rangle e^{-i\omega t}=0
. In contrast, in a NESS, the entropy production should remain nonzero
\langle\partial_{t}S_{\text{prod}}\rangle\neq 0

Seifert 2012
. This can be demonstrated using a statistical rather than thermodynamic definition of entropy, which involves the marginal probability distribution
P[\psi]\propto\int D\phi~e^{-\mathcal{S}_{\text{MSR}}[\psi,\phi]}
over the direct/time-reversed order parameter trajectories
Seifert 2005
;
Horowitz and Parrondo 2013
. The detailed analysis of this formalism lies beyond the scope of the present work.

The colored noise in NESS may also affect the standard fluctuational shift of the critical temperature
\delta T_{c}

Larkin and Varlamov 2005
. A straightforward approach to see it is to go beyond Gaussian model introduced earlier, use interaction terms like
\propto\phi^{*}|\psi|^{2}\psi
in the MSR action (
3
) and then apply the machinery of renormalization group (RG) and perturbation theory
Hohenberg and Halperin 1977
. We instead provide a simple qualitative estimation. It is known that at least for 2D case the lowest order deviation from the BCS theory within fluctuations gives
{\delta T_{c}^{2D}\approx-2T_{c0}Gi_{(2D)}\ln(C_{2}/Gi_{(2D)})}
, where
C_{2}\sim 1
is a constant. At the same time, full-RG approach result for
T_{c}
differs from that by parametrically small value
\propto 4Gi_{(2D)}

Larkin and Varlamov 2005
. Thus, we can avoid the RG approach and make a very naïve estimation of how
\tau
can enter the critical temperature shift:

{\delta T_{c}^{2D}\propto-T_{c0}Gi_{(2D)}\ln\frac{C_{2}}{(1+T_{c0}\tau)Gi_{(2D)}}}.

The finite correlation time
\tau\gtrsim T_{c0}^{-1}
does not support the high-energy fluctuations and thus reduces the fluctuation-induced shift
\delta T_{c}
.

Physical implementation.–

Up to this point, we have treated Eq. (
1
) primarily as a toy model for a superconductor subject to colored noise. Its microscopic derivation from the BCS model inherently assumes equilibrium internal interactions of quasiparticles with phonons or impurities, which result in overdamped dissipations near
T_{c}
. In order to capture nonequilibrium fluctuation behavior, one can introduce an additional coupling to an external bath with a nonthermal occupation function
n_{b}(\omega)
.
Within the Keldysh formalism, it is possible to systematically integrate out the bath and electronic degrees of freedom, and then introduce order parameter fluctuations
Kamenev 2023
;
Sieberer et al. 2016
;
Lang et al. 2024
. In the regime
|T-T_{c}|\ll T_{c}
this yields exactly the same coarse-grained Gaussian MSR action (
3
), where the noise correlator
D(\omega)
is determined by the bath distribution
n_{b}(\omega)
. If the characteristic energy scale of the bath
\omega_{c}\sim 1/\tau
is sufficiently low, such that
\tau\sim\tau_{\text{GL}}
, then the system can experience colored noise.
The realization of the NESS with broken detailed balance in this case depends sensitively on the physical bath model. One may suggest that bosonic (and potentially fermionic) bath engineering
Verstraten et al. 2023
;
Chakraborty and Takei 2024
allows to design specific forms of
n_{b}(\omega)
using, for instance, external irradiation with spectral filtering
Seetharam et al. 2015
or out-of-equilibrium current or voltage biasing
Chtchelkatchev and Vinokur 2009
.

Conclusion.–

We have shown that finite correlation time of Langevin noise in the stochastic TDGL model affects the electric and heat transport in fluctuating superconductors by unevenly driving order-parameter modes and suppressing high-energy contributions. This selective driving can enhance certain components of the thermoelectric response despite truncating the noise spectrum. For exponentially correlated noise, the nature and strength of this effect depends solely on the system dimensionality. This suggests a potential pathway for controlling superconducting transport properties via coupling to a nonthermal bath. Future work should explore the role of spatial correlations and the development of suitable microscopic models to connect these finding more directly with experimental platforms.

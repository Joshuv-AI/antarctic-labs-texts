Eigenvalue Bounds for Perturbed Periodic Dirac Operators
Ghada Shuker Jameel, Karl Michael Schmidt

Abstract
We characterise regions in the complex plane that contain all non-embedded eigenvalues of a perturbed periodic Dirac operator on the real line with real-valued periodic potential and a generally non-symmetric matrix-valued perturbation V V . We show that the eigenvalues are located close to the end-points of the spectral bands for small V ∈ L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) 2 × 2 V\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})^{2\times 2} , but only close to the spectral bands as a whole for small V ∈ L p ​ ( ℝ ) 2 × 2 V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})^{2\times 2} , p > 1 p>1 . As auxiliary results, we prove the relative compactness of matrix multiplication operators in L 2 ​ p ​ ( ℝ ) 2 × 2 L^{2p}(\mathbb{R})^{2\times 2} with respect to the periodic operator under minimal hypotheses, and find the asymptotic solution of the Dirac equation on a finite interval for spectral parameters with large imaginary part. Keywords: Non-selfadjoint operator; periodic Dirac system; eigenvalue enclosure 2020 MSC: 47B28; 34L40, 47A55, 81Q15

Introduction

In the present paper, we consider the one-dimensional perturbed periodic Dirac
operator

H=-i\,\sigma_{2}\,\frac{d}{dx}+m\,\sigma_{3}+q(x)+V(x)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R}),

where

\sigma_{2}

\sigma_{3}

is a self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})^{2}

operator

is symmetric.
We assume that

is bounded and that

has the same essential spectrum as

discrete eigenvalues in the complex plane

Our aim is to find a priori enclosures for these eigenvalues, i.e. regions
characterised in terms of the properties of the unperturbed periodic Dirac
equation and the

In the absence of a periodic background potential,

The radii of the circles tend to 0 as the 1-norm of

In the present study, we extend this observation to the case where a periodic
background potential

Our main eigenvalue exclusion result (Theorem

From our results, the following picture emerges.
For

Moreover,

5

6

This means that for small

, the eigenvalues are confined to small neighbourhoods of the end-points of spectral bands and, when a coupling parameter is
applied, will emerge from these end-points only.
This behaviour appears to be a natural analogue to that observed in

]
and
[
6
]
.

However, for

grows beyond all bounds as

Therefore the level sets of

However,

We mention that in the recent study

The periodic equation

Let
\Phi(\cdot,\lambda)
be the canonical fundamental system of the periodic Dirac
equation with spectral parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, i.e. the solution
of the (matrix) initial value problem

-i\sigma_{2}\Phi^{\prime}(x,\lambda)+(m\sigma_{3}+q(x))\,\Phi(x,\lambda)=\lambda\Phi(x,\lambda)\quad(x\in\mathbb{R}),\quad\Phi(0,\lambda)=\mathbb{I},

(1)

where
\mathbb{I}
is the
2\times 2
unit matrix
and
q
is a locally integrable, real-valued, periodic function.
The qualitative behaviour of the
solutions can be studied by means of Floquet theory considering the monodromy
matrix

M(\lambda):=\Phi(a,\lambda)

(\lambda\in\mathbb{C})
,
where
a>0
is the period of
q
, see
[
3
]
. As the (Wronskian)
determinant of the monodromy matrix is equal to 1, its eigenvalues are
inverses of each other. Their positions in the complex plane can be characterised
in terms of the discriminant

\mathfrak{D}(\lambda):=\mathop{\rm Tr}M(\lambda)
.
The characteristic equation for
M(\lambda)
,

\mu^{2}-\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\,\mu+1=0,

shows that
M(\lambda)
has two distinct eigenvalues if and only if

\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\notin\{-2,2\}
.
In this case, either the eigenvalues lie on the unit circle and are complex
conjugates of each other (this happens when
\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\in(-2,2)
), or one
eigenvalue,
\rho(\lambda)
, lies outside, the other eigenvalue,
1/\rho(\lambda)
, lies inside the unit circle (this happens when

\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[-2,2]
).
If
\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\in\{-2,2\}
, then either the geometric multiplicity
of the eigenvalue
\pm 1
is 1 or
M(\lambda)=\pm\mathbb{I}

(see
[
3
, Section 1.4]

If
\mu
is an eigenvalue of

and

is a corresponding eigenvector, then

(x\in\mathbb{R})

is a
Floquet solution

of the Dirac equation

-i\sigma_{2}u^{\prime}(x)+(m\sigma_{3}+q(x))\,u(x)=\lambda\,u(x)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R});

(2)

clearly
u(0)=v
. Then the function

\varphi(x):=\mu^{-x/a}\,u(x)

(x\in\mathbb{R})
is
a
-periodic.
This shows that all solutions of the periodic Dirac equation are bounded

\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\in(-2,2)
and that there is one Floquet solution

u_{+}(\cdot,\lambda)

exponentially small at

u_{-}(\cdot,\lambda)
exponentially small at

\infty

\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[-2,2]

\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\in\{-2,2\}

Hence we can deduce that
\sigma(H_{0})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\in[-2,2]\}\subset\mathbb{R}

[

The (entries of the) monodromy matrix

The discriminant can be written in the form

\displaystyle=2\cosh(a\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda))\cos(a\mathop{\rm Re}k(\lambda))-2i\sinh(a\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda))\sin(a\mathop{\rm Re}k(\lambda))

3
Eigenvalue exclusion

We now consider the Dirac operator with an additional non-periodic perturbation,

H:=H_{0}+V
, where
V
is the operator of multiplication with the
matrix-valued function
V:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}

We assume that
V
is bounded and, for some

,
V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})^{2\times 2}

[

\|V\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|V(x)\|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Here

This is different from the operator norm

Lemma 2

Let

compact.

Proof.

(a)
We first show that the statement is true for

Let
{a<b}

\mathop{\rm supp}A\subset[a,b]

Let

\|u_{n}\|_{2}\leq K

(n\in\mathbb{N})
,
and set
y_{n}:=A\,(H_{0}-\lambda)\,u_{n}

(n\in\mathbb{N})

Then, for all

we have by Theorem

\displaystyle|y_{n}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\|A(x)\|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(x,t,\lambda)\,u_{n}(t)\,dt\right|\leq\|A(x)\|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|G(x,t,\lambda)\|^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{n}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\|A(z)\|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda)\,|t|}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,C(\lambda)\,K<\infty

Also, for

\displaystyle|y_{n}(x)-y_{n}(z)|=\left|A(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(x,t,\lambda)\,u_{n}(t)\,dt-A(z)\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(z,t,\lambda)\,u_{n}(t)\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\ +\int_{x}^{z}\left|\left(A(x)e^{ik(\lambda)(t-x)}\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(t)^{T}-A(z)e^{ik(\lambda)(z-t)}\varphi_{-}(z)\varphi_{+}(t)^{T}\right)u_{n}(t)\right|dt

\displaystyle\ +\int_{z}^{\infty}\left|\left(A(x)\,e^{ik(\lambda)\,(t-x)}\,\varphi_{+}(x)-A(z)\,e^{ik(\lambda)\,(t-z)}\,\varphi_{+}(z)\right)\varphi_{-}(t)^{T}\,u_{n}(t)\right|dt\Bigg),

\left|e^{ik(\lambda)x}A(x)\,\varphi_{-}(x)-e^{ik(\lambda)z}A(z)\,\varphi_{-}(z)\right|\left(\int_{-\infty}^{b}e^{2\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda)t}|\varphi_{+}(t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,K,

and as

[a,b]

Analogous reasoning applies to the third integral.
The second integral can be estimated by

\displaystyle\left(\int_{x}^{z}\|A(x)e^{ik(\lambda)\,|t-x|}\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(t)^{T}-A(z)e^{ik(\lambda)\,|t-z|}\varphi_{-}(z)\varphi_{+}(t)^{T}\|^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\int_{x}^{z}|u_{n}(t)|^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|A(t)\|\sup_{t\in[a,b]}|\varphi_{+}(t)|\sup_{t\in[a,b]}|\varphi_{-}(t)|\,K\,\sqrt{z-x},

uniformly on
[a,b]
and in

.
Consequently, the sequence of functions

is also equicontinuous. By the Arzelà-Ascoli Theorem, it has a subsequence that is uniformly
convergent and hence, in view of the compact support, also converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
.

(b)
Now let
A\in L^{2p}(\mathbb{R})^{2\times 2}
.
Let

,
\|u\|_{2}=\|v\|_{2}=1

\displaystyle|(A\,

\displaystyle(H_{0}-\lambda)^{-1}u,v)|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}A(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(x,t,\lambda)\,u(t)\,dt\,\overline{v(x)}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(t)|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda)\,|t-x|}\,\|A(x)\|\,|v(x)|\,dx\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=C(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{F(|u|)}\,F\left(e^{-\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda)\,|\cdot|}*(\|A(\cdot)\|\,|v|)\right)

\displaystyle=C(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{F(|u|)}\sqrt{2\pi}\,F(e^{-\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda)\,|\cdot|})\,F(\|A(\cdot)\|\,|v|)

\displaystyle=2\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda)\,C(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(\|A(\cdot)\|\,|v|)(\xi)\,\frac{\overline{F(|u|)(\xi)}}{\xi^{2}+(\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda))^{2}}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda)\,C(\lambda)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|F(\|A(\cdot)\|\,|v|)\right|^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{F(|u|)(\xi)}{\xi^{2}+(\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda))^{2}}\right|^{r}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},

where we used the Plancherel identity for the Fourier transform

then Hölder’s inequality with exponent

and conjugate

.
(In the case

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|F(\|A(\cdot)\|\,|v|)|^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}

.)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|F(\|A(\cdot)\|\,|v|)|^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\pi}^{1-\frac{2}{r}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|A(\cdot)\|^{r}\,|v|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\|A(\cdot)\|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|A(\cdot)\|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{rq}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{rq^{\prime}}}\!\!=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|A\|_{2p},

using Hölder’s inequality with exponents

The same Hölder inequality gives

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{F(|u|)(\xi)}{\xi^{2}+(\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda))^{2}}\right|^{r}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi^{2}+(\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda))^{2})^{-2p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\|F(|u|)\|_{2}.

As

, taking the supremum over

gives the bound for the operator norm

As

is dense in

in

in

in operator norm and the statement of the lemma follows from (a) and the fact that the space of compact operators is closed in the operator norm.
∎

In the statement of the eigenvalue exclusion theorem, we use the function

D(\Gamma)=\{A\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}\mid A\ \hbox{\rm has two distinct

\ \Gamma(A)=\frac{|\det(v_{+},v_{-})|}{|v_{+}|\,|v_{-}|},

(12)

where

for the

As the eigenvectors are uniquely determined up to a complex factor,

The domain

is an open subset of

has no continuous extension at the unit matrix.
For the monodromy matrix

Lemma 3
.

For all

if and only if

\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\notin\{-2,2\}
.

We can now state the main eigenvalue exclusion theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let

p\geq 1
and let

Then

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma_{e}(H)

if

\displaystyle\|V\|_{1}

\displaystyle<\Gamma(M(\lambda))\,\gamma_{+}(\lambda)\,\gamma_{-}(\lambda)\qquad(\hbox{\it if $p=1$}),

\displaystyle\|V\|_{p}

\displaystyle<\Gamma(M(\lambda))\,\gamma_{+}(\lambda)\,\gamma_{-}(\lambda)\,(\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda))^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\frac{p}{2(p-1)}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\qquad(\hbox{\it if $p>1$}),

where

\gamma_{\pm}(\lambda)=\frac{|\varphi_{\pm}(0,\lambda)|}{\sup_{x\in[0,a]}|\varphi_{\pm}(x,\lambda)|}

(13)

and
\varphi_{\pm}
are the periodic functions in equation

Remark.
The additional factor that appears on the right-hand side of
the inequality in Theorem

, so the exclusion criterion is formally continuous in

.

Proof.

By the Birman-Schwinger Principle (see e.g.
[
2
, Theorem B.2]
),
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
H_{0}+V

if and only if
-1
is an eigenvalue of
A\,(H_{0}-\lambda)^{-1}\,B
,
where
A
,

are as in equation (
10

Case 1:

p=1
.
For
u,v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
, we obtain from Theorem 1 and the estimate (
8
),
noting that
e^{-\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda)\,|t-x|}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\left|(A(H_{0}-\lambda)^{-1}Bu,v)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(A(x)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(x,y,\lambda)B(y)u(y)\,dy\right)^{T}\overline{v(x)}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|A(x)\|\,\|G(x,y,\lambda)\|\,\|B(y)\|\,|u(y)|\,|v(x)|\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\lambda)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|A(x)\|\,|v(x)|\,dx\right)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|B(y)\|\,|u(y)|\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\lambda)\,\|V\|_{1}\,\|v\|_{2}\,\|u\|_{2},

where we used Hölder’s inequality and the estimate (
11
) in the

Setting

and taking the supremum over

, we hence find
the estimate for the operator norm of the Birman-Schwinger kernel

Case 2:

p>1

Let
r:=\frac{2p}{p+1}\in(1,2)
with conjugate exponent

. We now associate the matrix-valued functions
A
and
B
with
multiplication operators

,

and write the Birman-Schwinger kernel as

where the operator

For

\displaystyle\|A_{r^{\prime},2}u\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|A(x)\,u(x)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|A(x)\|^{2}\left(|u(x)|^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{2}{r^{\prime}}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|V(x)\|^{\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}-2}}dx\right)^{\frac{r^{\prime}-2}{2r^{\prime}}}\|u\|_{r^{\prime}},

using Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}
and
\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}-2}

As

, we obtain the operator norm estimate

Similarly, we find for

\displaystyle\|B_{2,r}u\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|B(x)\,u(x)|^{r}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|B(x)\|^{r}\left(|u(x)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{r}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|V(x)\|^{\frac{r}{2-r}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-r}{2r}}\|u\|_{2},

using Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{2}{r}
and

and hence, as

, the operator norm estimate

.
In conjunction with Theorem
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|A(H_{0}-\lambda)^{-1}B\|

\displaystyle=\|A_{r^{\prime},2}R_{r}(\lambda)B_{2,r}\|\leq\|A_{r^{\prime},2}\|\,\|R_{r}(\lambda)\|\,\|B_{2,r}\|

\displaystyle\leq C(\lambda)\left(\frac{2}{\mathop{\rm Im}k(\lambda)}\,\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|V\|_{p},

since

Now if
\lambda

4
Behaviour near the essential spectrum

In this section we study the behaviour of the right-hand side of the inequalities
in Theorem

We begin by finding a positive lower bound for the factors

Theorem 4
.

(a)
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

Proof.

(a)
The upper bound is immediate from the definition of
\gamma_{\pm}
. For the lower

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\,|\varphi_{\pm}(x,\lambda)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\pm}(x,\lambda)^{T}\,\overline{\varphi_{\pm}^{\prime}(x,\lambda)}+\varphi_{\pm}^{\prime}(x,\lambda)^{T}\,\overline{\varphi_{\pm}(x,\lambda)}

(b)

∎

,

We can characterise the derivative of

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Denoting the columns of the canonical fundamental system

of

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{a}(u_{1}(x,\lambda)v_{1}(x,\lambda)+u_{2}(x,\lambda)v_{2}(x,\lambda))\,dx,

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{a}(v_{1}(x,\lambda)^{2}+v_{2}(x,\lambda)^{2})\,dx,

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{a}(u_{1}(x,\lambda)^{2}+u_{2}(x,\lambda)^{2})\,dx

Note that

Lemma 4

For any

and
v
are linearly dependent functions; however, the latter is impossible
as
u(0,\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
and
v(0,\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}
.
∎

We can now find the limit of
\Gamma(M(\lambda))
at a point
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
where

\mathfrak{D}(\lambda_{0})=\pm 2
, distinguishing the cases of

M(\lambda_{0})=\pm\mathbb{I}
and of
M(\lambda_{0})\neq\pm\mathbb{I}
.
Note that in the following theorems
5
and
6
,
the limits
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}
allow complex
\lambda
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
M(\lambda_{0})=s\mathbb{I}
, where

s\in\{-1,1\}
. Then

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}}\Gamma(M(\lambda))=2\,\frac{\sqrt{I_{2}(\lambda_{0})\,I_{3}(\lambda_{0})-I_{1}(\lambda_{0})^{2}}}{I_{2}(\lambda_{0})+I_{3}(\lambda_{0})}>0.

Proof.

Since
M
is entire, we have by equation (
15
) for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
abbreviating
I_{j}:=I_{j}(\lambda_{0})
,
j\in\{1,2,3\}
,

\displaystyle M(\lambda)

\displaystyle=M(\lambda_{0})+M^{\prime}(\lambda_{0})\,(\lambda-\lambda_{0})+R(\lambda-\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=s\mathbb{I}+s\mathbb{I}\begin{pmatrix}I_{1}&I_{2}\\
-I_{3}&-I_{1}\end{pmatrix}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})+R(\lambda-\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=s\mathbb{I}+s\,(\lambda-\lambda_{0})\,N(\lambda-\lambda_{0}),

where

N(\Lambda):=\begin{pmatrix}I_{1}&I_{2}\\
-I_{3}&-I_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{R(\Lambda)}{\Lambda}\qquad(\Lambda\in\mathbb{C}).

Here
R(\Lambda)/\Lambda
is analytic with
\lim\limits_{\Lambda\rightarrow 0}R(\Lambda)/\Lambda=0
.
Clearly,
w\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is an eigenvector of
N(\lambda-\lambda_{0})
for eigenvalue
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
if and only if it is an eigenvector of
M(\lambda)
for eigenvalue
s\,(1+(\lambda-\lambda_{0})\,\mu)
.
Therefore
\Gamma(M(\lambda))=\Gamma(N(\lambda-\lambda_{0}))
and we only
need to find
\lim\limits_{\Lambda\rightarrow 0}\Gamma(N(\Lambda))
.

Using Lemma
4
, we see that the matrix

N(0)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{1}&I_{2}\\
-I_{3}&-I_{1}\end{pmatrix}
has distinct
purely imaginary eigenvalues
\pm i\sqrt{I_{2}I_{3}-I_{1}^{2}}
with corresponding
eigenvectors

w_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{2}\\
-I_{1}+i\sqrt{I_{2}I_{3}-I_{1}^{2}}\end{pmatrix},\qquad w_{-}=\begin{pmatrix}-I_{1}+i\sqrt{I_{2}I_{3}-I_{1}^{2}}\\
I_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence by equation (
12
)

\Gamma(N(0))^{2}=\frac{|2(I_{2}I_{3}-I_{1}^{2}+iI_{1}\sqrt{I_{2}I_{3}-i_{1}^{2}})|^{2}}{I_{2}\,(I_{2}+I_{3})\,I_{3}\,(I_{2}+I_{3})}=4\,\frac{I_{2}I_{3}-I_{1}^{2}}{(I_{2}+I_{3})^{2}}.

By analytic perturbation theory (see
[
11
, Theorem II.1.8]
), the
eigenspaces of
N(\Lambda)
converge to those spanned by
w_{+}
and
w_{-}
as

\Lambda\rightarrow 0
, and we conclude that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}}\Gamma(M(\lambda))=\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow 0}\Gamma(N(\Lambda))=\Gamma(N(0))=2\,\frac{\sqrt{I_{2}I_{3}-I_{1}^{2}}}{I_{2}+I_{3}},

which is positive by Lemma
4
.
∎

Theorem 6
.

Let
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\mathfrak{D}(\lambda_{0})=\pm 2
, but

M(\lambda_{0})\neq\pm\mathbb{I}
. Then

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}}\Gamma(M(\lambda))=0.

Proof.

We can write the monodromy matrix as

M(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}a(\lambda)&b(\lambda)\\
c(\lambda)&\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)-a(\lambda)\end{pmatrix}\qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb{C})

with entire functions
a,b,c
(and
\mathfrak{D}
).
As the (Wronskian)
\det M(\lambda)=1
for all
\lambda
, we find

cb=a\mathfrak{D}-a^{2}-1.

(16)

Therefore, if
b(\lambda_{0})=c(\lambda_{0})=0
, then

a(\lambda_{0})=\mathfrak{D}(\lambda_{0})/2
and hence
M(\lambda_{0})=\pm\mathbb{I}
,
contradicting the hypotheses.
So
b(\lambda_{0})\neq 0
or
c(\lambda_{0})\neq 0
.

We first consider the case
b(\lambda_{0})\neq 0
. Then
b\neq 0
in a
neighbourhood of
\lambda_{0}
. For
\lambda
in this neighbourhood, we can write,
using equation (
16
),

M(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}a(\lambda)&b(\lambda)\\
\frac{a(\lambda)\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)-a(\lambda)^{2}-1}{b(\lambda)}&\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)-a(\lambda)\end{pmatrix},

with eigenvalues

\frac{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\pm\sqrt{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)^{2}-4}}{2}
and
eigenvectors

w_{+}(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}b(\lambda)\\
\frac{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)+\sqrt{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)^{2}-4}}{2}-a(\lambda)\end{pmatrix},\qquad w_{-}(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}b(\lambda)\\
\frac{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)-\sqrt{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)^{2}-4}}{2}-a(\lambda)\end{pmatrix}.

Then
|w_{\pm}(\lambda)|\geq|b(\lambda)|
and, by equation (
12
),

\Gamma(M(\lambda))\leq\frac{|-b(\lambda)\sqrt{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)^{2}-4}|}{|b(\lambda)|^{2}}=\frac{|\sqrt{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)^{2}-4}|}{|b(\lambda)|}\rightarrow 0\qquad(\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}).

If
b(\lambda_{0})=0
, then
c\neq 0
in a neighbourhood of
\lambda_{0}
, and
for
\lambda
in this neighbourhood we can write

M(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}a(\lambda)&\frac{a(\lambda)\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)-a(\lambda)^{2}-1}{c(\lambda)}\\
c(\lambda)&\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)-a(\lambda)\end{pmatrix};

this matrix has the same eigenvalues as above and eigenvectors

w_{\pm}(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)\pm\sqrt{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)^{2}-4}}{2}-\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)+a(\lambda)\\
c(\lambda)\end{pmatrix},

so

|w_{\pm}(\lambda)|\geq|c(\lambda)|
and

\Gamma(M(\lambda))\leq\frac{|c(\lambda)\sqrt{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)^{2}-4}|}{|c(\lambda)|^{2}}=\frac{|\sqrt{\mathfrak{D}(\lambda)^{2}-4}|}{|c(\lambda)|}\rightarrow 0\qquad(\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}).

∎

5
Asymptotics for large
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda

The results of the preceding section show that the functions
\gamma_{\pm}
do
not tend to zero at any point in the complex plane and that
\Gamma\circ M

tends to zero only (and exactly) at the end-points of the spectral bands.
However, they do not yet preclude the possibility that these functions become
small for
\lambda
far away from the real axis; in fact, the lower bound in
Theorem
4
(a) tends to zero as
|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|\rightarrow\infty

and hence is not very good in this respect.
In the present section, we show that in fact
\Gamma(M(\lambda))
and

\gamma_{\pm}(\lambda)
tend to 1 as
|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|\rightarrow\infty
, which
implies that the level sets of
\Gamma(M(\lambda))\gamma_{+}(\lambda)\gamma_{-}(\lambda)
are located in strip neighbourhoods of the real axis.
The basis for this is provided by the following asymptotic of the canonical
fundamental system of a Dirac system with real-valued potential on a bounded
interval; this result may be of interest in its own right.

We focus on the case
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda>0
, as the asymptotics
for
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\rightarrow-\infty
are the same due to the symmetry of
the Dirac equation (
1
) with real-valued
q
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
q\in L^{1}[0,a]
be real-valued and
m\geq 0
. Let

Q(x)=\int_{0}^{x}q

(x\in[0,a])
and let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha>0
.
Then, for each
x\in[0,a]
, the solution of the initial value problem

(\ref{eq:pD})
with
\lambda=\mu+i\alpha
has the asymptotic
for
\alpha\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle e^{-\alpha x}\,\Phi(x,\mu

\displaystyle+i\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{i(Q(x)-\mu x)}+e^{-2\alpha x}e^{-i(Q(x)-\mu x)}\right)\mathbb{I}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}\left(-e^{i(Q(x)-\mu x)}+e^{-2\alpha x}e^{-i(Q(x)-\mu x)}\right)\sigma_{2}+O_{\rm unif}({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}).

(17)

Here
O_{\rm unif}
means that the bound is uniform in
x\in[0,a]
.

Corollary 1
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
7
,

e^{-\alpha x}\,\Phi(x,\mu+i\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\,e^{i(Q(x)-\mu x)}\,(\mathbb{I}-\sigma_{2})+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}})\qquad(\alpha\rightarrow\infty)

for each
x\in(0,a]
.

Proof of Theorem
7
.

Write
\Phi=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{3}\sigma_{j}\,\phi_{j}
with complex-valued
functions
\phi_{0}
,
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}
,
\phi_{3}
and the Pauli matrices

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=\mathbb{I}.

(18)

Then the initial value problem (
1
) is
equivalent to the system

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}\\
\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(-\alpha-iq+i\mu)\sigma_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}\\
\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}+m\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-i\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{3}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{3}\end{pmatrix}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(-\alpha-iq+i\mu)(-\sigma_{2})\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{3}\end{pmatrix}+m\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&i\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}\\
\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(19)

with initial values
\phi_{0}(0)=1
,
\phi_{1}(0)=\phi_{2}(0)=\phi_{3}(0)=0
.
We now make the ansatz

\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}\\
\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{e^{r}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}u_{1}+\frac{e^{-r}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}u_{2},\quad\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{3}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{e^{r}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-i\end{pmatrix}u_{3}+\frac{e^{-r}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
i\end{pmatrix}u_{4},

(20)

with functions
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}
and given
r(x)=-\alpha x-i(Q(x)-\mu x)

(x\in[0,a])
.
This is motivated by the fact that, with
constants

u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}
,
the above are the general solution of the decoupled equation system when

m=0
. The initial conditions translate to

u_{1}(0)=u_{2}(0)=1
,
u_{3}(0)=u_{4}(0)=0
.
Using this ansatz in the coupled differential equation system (
19
) and then multiplying the first equation (from the left) with the row vectors

(1,1)
and
(1,-1)
, the second equation with the vectors
(1,i)
and

(1,-i)
, respectively yields the two separate differential equation systems

\left\{\begin{matrix}u_{1}^{\prime}=m\,e^{-2r}\,u_{4}\\
u_{4}^{\prime}=m\,e^{2r}\,u_{1}\end{matrix}\right.\qquad\left\{\begin{matrix}u_{3}^{\prime}=m\,e^{-2r}\,u_{2}\\
u_{2}^{\prime}=m\,e^{2r}\,u_{3}\end{matrix}\right.

(21)

which are in fact the same system, but with different initial values.
Focusing on the system on the left-hand side first, we observe that

|u_{1}|^{\prime}(x)\leq|u_{1}^{\prime}(x)|=m\,e^{2\alpha x}\,|u_{4}|(x),\qquad|u_{4}|^{\prime}(x)\leq|u_{4}^{\prime}(x)|=m\,e^{-2\alpha x}\,|u_{1}|(x).

The solutions exist on
[0,a]
and, as continuous functions, are bounded.
By an integration by parts and using the initial values,

\displaystyle|u_{4}|(x)

\displaystyle=|u_{4}(0)|+\int_{0}^{x}|u_{4}|^{\prime}\leq\int_{0}^{x}m\,e^{-2\alpha t}\,|u_{1}|(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{m}{2\alpha}\,(e^{-2\alpha x}\,|u_{1}|(x)-1)+\frac{m}{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-2\alpha t}\,|u_{1}|^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m}{2\alpha}-\frac{m}{2\alpha}\,e^{-2\alpha x}\,|u_{1}|(x)+\frac{m^{2}}{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-2\alpha t}e^{2\alpha t}\,|u_{4}|(t)\,dt\qquad(x\in[0,a]),

so

\sup_{x\in[0,a]}|u_{4}(x)|\leq\frac{m}{2\alpha}+\frac{m^{2}a}{2\alpha}\sup_{x\in[0,a]}|u_{4}(x)|

and hence

\sup_{x\in[0,a]}|u_{4}(x)|\leq\frac{m}{2\alpha}\,\frac{1}{1-\frac{m^{2}a}{2\alpha}}\leq\frac{m}{\alpha}

for
\alpha>m^{2}a
. Consequently,

|u_{1}(x)-1|\leq\int_{0}^{x}|u_{1}^{\prime}|=\int_{0}^{x}m\,e^{2\alpha t}\,|u_{4}(t)|\,dt\leq\frac{m^{2}}{2\alpha^{2}}\,(e^{2\alpha x}-1)\qquad(x\in[0,a]).

Now applying an analogous procedure to the right-hand side system in equation
(
21
), we find

\displaystyle|u_{2}|(x)

\displaystyle=|u_{2}|(0)+\int_{0}^{x}|u_{2}|^{\prime}\leq 1+m\int_{0}^{x}e^{-2\alpha t}\,|u_{3}|(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=1-\frac{m}{2\alpha}\,(e^{-\alpha x}\,|u_{3}|(x)-0)+\frac{m}{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-2\alpha t}\,|u_{3}|^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{m}{2\alpha}\,e^{-2\alpha x}\,|u_{3}(x)|+\frac{m^{2}}{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-2\alpha t}e^{2\alpha t}\,|u_{2}|(t)\,dt,

so

\sup_{x\in[0,a]}|u_{2}(x)|\leq 1+\frac{m^{2}a}{2\alpha}\sup_{x\in[0,a]}|u_{2}(x)|

and hence

\sup_{x\in[0,a]}|u_{2}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{1-\frac{m^{2}a}{2\alpha}}<2

for
\alpha>m^{2}a
. Also,

\displaystyle|u_{3}|(x)

\displaystyle=|u_{3}|(0)+\int_{0}^{x}|u_{3}|^{\prime}\leq m\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha t}\,|u_{2}|(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{2\alpha}\,(e^{2\alpha x}\,|u_{2}|(x)-1)-\frac{m}{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha t}\,|u_{2}|^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m}{2\alpha}\,(e^{2\alpha x}\,|u_{2}|(x)-1)+\frac{m^{2}}{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha t}e^{-2\alpha t}\,|u_{3}|(t)\,dt

and therefore

\sup_{t\in[0,x]}|u_{3}(t)|\leq\frac{m}{2\alpha}\,\frac{e^{2\alpha x}}{1-\frac{m^{2}a}{2\alpha}}-\frac{m}{2\alpha}+\frac{m^{2}a}{2\alpha}\sup_{t\in[0,x]}|u_{3}(t)|,

which gives

\sup_{t\in[0,x]}|u_{3}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{1-\frac{m^{2}a}{2\alpha}}\left(\frac{m\,e^{2\alpha x}}{2\alpha-m^{2}a}-\frac{m}{2\alpha}\right)\leq\frac{2m}{\alpha}\,(e^{2\alpha x}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\leq\frac{2m}{\alpha}\,e^{2\alpha x}

for all
x\in[0,a]
and
\alpha>m^{2}a
. Consequently,

|u_{2}(x)-1|\leq m\int_{0}^{x}e^{-2\alpha t}\,|u_{3}(t)|\,dt\leq\frac{2m^{2}a}{\alpha}\qquad(x\in[0,a]).

By equation (
20
), we have thus obtained the asymptotics

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}\\
\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\alpha x}}{2}\,e^{-i(Q(x)-\mu x)}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}(1+O(\frac{e^{2\alpha x}}{\alpha^{2}}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{e^{\alpha x}}{2}\,e^{i(Q(x)-\mu x)}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}(1+O_{\rm unif}({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}})),

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{3}\end{pmatrix}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\alpha x}}{2}\,e^{-i(Q(x)-\mu x)}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-i\end{pmatrix}O(\frac{e^{2\alpha x}}{\alpha})+\frac{e^{\alpha x}}{2}\,e^{i(Q(x)-\mu x)}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
i\end{pmatrix}O_{\rm unif}({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}),

and equation (
17
) follows.
∎

On the basis of the preceding theorem, we now find the asymptotics of the
quasimomentum and of
\Gamma\circ M
(in Theorem
8
) and of

\varphi_{\pm}
and
\gamma_{\pm}
(in Theorem
9
).

Theorem 8
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha>0
. Then we have the following asymptotics
as
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
for the monodromy matrix of the periodic
Dirac equation
(\ref{eq:pD})

e^{-\alpha a}\,M(\mu+i\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\,e^{i(Q(a)-\mu a)}(\mathbb{I}-\sigma_{2})+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}),

the discriminant

e^{-\alpha a}\,\mathfrak{D}(\mu+i\alpha)=e^{i(Q(x)-\mu a)}+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}),

the quasimomentum

k(\mu+i\alpha)=\mu+i\alpha-\frac{Q(a)}{a}+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}})

and the eigenvectors of
M(\mu+i\alpha)

v_{+}(\mu+i\alpha)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-i\end{pmatrix}+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}),\qquad v_{-}(\mu+i\alpha)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
i\end{pmatrix}+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}).

Consequently,

\Gamma(M(\mu+i\alpha))=1+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}).

Proof.

The asymptotics of the monodromy matrix and hence the discriminant follow
directly from Corollary
1
. As
e^{-\alpha a}M(\mu+i\alpha)

has determinant
e^{-2\alpha a}
,
solving its characteristic equation
shows that the two eigenvalues of this matrix have asymptotics

e^{i(Q(a)-\mu a)}+O(\frac{1}{\alpha})
and

e^{-2\alpha a}\,(e^{-i(Q(a)-\mu a)}+O(\frac{1}{\alpha}))
,
respectively. Hence the larger eigenvalue of
M(\mu+i\alpha)
is

e^{\alpha a}\,(e^{i(Q(a)-\mu a)}+O(\frac{1}{\alpha}))=e^{-i(\mu+i\alpha-\frac{Q(a)}{a}+O(\frac{1}{\alpha}))a},

and we can read off the asymptotics for the quasimomentum. The
asymptotic form of the eigenvectors follows from that of the matrix

e^{-\alpha a}M(\mu+i\alpha)
and its eigenvalues.
∎

Theorem 9
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha>0
.
For the periodic Dirac equation
(\ref{eq:pD})
, the periodic functions

\varphi_{\pm}
of equation
(\ref{eq:Flop})
have asymptotics

|\varphi_{\pm}(x,\mu+i\alpha)|=|\varphi_{\pm}(0,\mu+i\alpha)|\,(1+O_{\rm unif}({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}))\qquad(\alpha\rightarrow\infty)

uniformly in
x\in[0,a]
.
Consequently, the functions
\gamma_{\pm}
of equation
(\ref{eq:gammadef})

satisfy

\gamma_{\pm}(\mu+i\alpha)=1+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}})\qquad(\alpha\rightarrow\infty).

Proof.

By Theorem
8
and equation (
17
),
observing that

{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,(\mathbb{I}-\sigma_{2})\,v_{+}(\mu+i\alpha)=v_{+}(\mu+i\alpha)+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}),\quad{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,(\mathbb{I}+\sigma_{2})\,v_{+}(\mu+i\alpha)=O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}),

we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi_{+}(x,\mu+i\alpha)

\displaystyle=e^{ik(\mu+i\alpha)x}\Phi(x,\mu+i\alpha)\,v_{+}(\mu+i\alpha)

\displaystyle=e^{i(\mu-\frac{Q(a)}{a}+O(\frac{1}{\alpha}))x}\,e^{-\alpha x}\,\Phi(x,\mu+i\alpha)\,v_{+}(\mu+i\alpha)

\displaystyle=e^{i(Q(x)-\frac{Q(a)}{a}\,x+O_{\rm unif}({\frac{1}{\alpha}}))}\,(v_{+}(\mu+i\alpha)+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}))

\displaystyle\qquad+e^{-2\alpha x}e^{-i(Q(x)+\frac{Q(a)}{a}\,x-2\mu x+O_{\rm unif}({\frac{1}{\alpha}}))}\,O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}})+O_{\rm unif}({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}})

and hence

\displaystyle|\varphi_{+}(x,\mu+i\alpha)|

\displaystyle=(1+O_{\rm unif}({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}))\,(v_{+}(\mu+i\alpha)+O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}}))+O_{\rm unif}({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}})

\displaystyle=|v_{+}(\mu+i\alpha)|+O_{\rm unif}({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}})=|\varphi_{+}(0,\mu+i\alpha)|(1+O_{\rm unif}({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}})).

Analogous reasoning for
\varphi_{-}(x,\mu+i\alpha)
does not work since the
exponentially large factor
e^{-ik(\mu+i\alpha)x}
(
=\rho(\lambda)^{x/a}
in equation (
6
)) leads to uncontrolled
amplification of the
O({\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha}})
error term.
However, the Floquet solution
u_{-}(x,\lambda)
is equal, up to a constant factor, to

\sigma_{3}\tilde{u}_{+}(a-x,\lambda)

(x\in\mathbb{R})
, where
\tilde{u}_{+}
is the
Floquet solution corresponding to the eigenvalue of modulus greater than 1
of the periodic Dirac equation with potential
\tilde{q}(x):=q(a-x)

(x\in\mathbb{R})
. Therefore the corresponding periodic functions satisfy
(up to a constant factor)

|\varphi_{-}(x,\lambda)|=|\tilde{\varphi}_{+}(a-x,\lambda)|
, so
we obtain the asymptotics of
|\varphi_{-}|
by applying the above reasoning to

|\tilde{\varphi}_{+}|
.
∎

Shock selection in reaction–diffusion equations with partially negative diffusivity using nonlinear regularisation
Thomas Miller, Alexander K. Y. Tam, Robert Marangell, Martin Wechselberger, Bronwyn H. Bradshaw-Hajek

Abstract
We consider a general reaction–nonlinear-diffusion equation with a region of negative diffusivity, and show how a nonlinear regularisation selects a shock position. Negative diffusivity can model population aggregation, but leads to shock-fronted solutions for population density. In general the shock position is non-unique. Previous studies have defined shock selection criteria such as the equal area rule, and shown how these arise from specific regularisations to the reaction–diffusion equation. In this work, we show that a nonlinear regularisation leads to travelling wave solutions where the shock is selected according to a modified equal area rule. Adjusting the nonlinearity in the regularisation moves the shock location. We focus on attaining shocks that conserve diffusivity across the shock, and demonstrate that this condition yields the longest possible shock length. Using geometric singular perturbation theory, we prove the existence of shock-fronted travelling wave solutions with continuous diffusivity, show how to construct them, and demonstrate that they correspond to a unique wave speed. Numerical solutions align with theoretical predictions for shock position and wave speed, confirming that a single regularisation term can vary the shock position and attain shocks with continuous diffusivity.

Introduction

In this paper, we establish a shock selection rule based on continuous diffusivity for a reaction–diffusion equation with a region of negative diffusivity using a nonlinear regularisation. The diffusivity

Shock selection conditions can correspond to regularising the reaction–diffusion equation
[

]
. Regularisation involves adding higher-order terms of small size (

1

\Phi(u)

[

]
demonstrated this for diffusion equations by analysing the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit of the Cahn-Hilliard equation,

\pd{u}{t}=\pdn{}{x}{2}\left(\Phi(u)-\varepsilon^{2}\pdn{u}{x}{2}\right),

(5)

using perturbation methods.
[

]

\pd{u}{t}=\pdn{}{x}{2}\left(\Phi(u)-\varepsilon^{2}\pdn{u}{x}{2}\right)+R(u),

(6)

and a third order viscous regularised reaction–diffusion equation

\pd{u}{t}=\pdn{}{x}{2}\left(\Phi(u)+\varepsilon\pd{u}{t}\right)+R(u),

(7)

in the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit. The third-order viscous regularisation
equation

leads to shock positions that either jump to or from one of the zeros of the diffusivity
[
Li+21
,
Wit96
]
(as per the red or green dotted lines in
Figure

1(b)
). By combining both the non-local and viscous regularisation terms

\pd{u}{t}=\pdn{}{x}{2}\left(\Phi(u)+\omega\varepsilon\pd{u}{t}-\varepsilon^{2}\pdn{u}{x}{2}\right)+R(u),

(8)

the position of the shock can be controlled by using
\omega
to balance the relative weighting of the two regularisation terms
[
Bra+24
]
. A similar result was found for a convective viscous Cahn-Hilliard equation
[
Wit96
]
.

3.3
Continuous diffusivity maximises shock length

Proposition

Proof of
Proposition

The derivative of the shock length

S_{L}^{\prime}(\Phi)=u_{r}^{\prime}(\Phi)-u_{\ell}^{\prime}(\Phi)=\frac{1}{\Phi^{\prime}(u_{r})}-\frac{1}{\Phi^{\prime}(u_{\ell})}=\frac{1}{D(u_{r})}-\frac{1}{D(u_{\ell})}.

(14)

Critical points of the shock length require that
D(u_{r})=D(u_{\ell})\neq 0
. To verify that continuous diffusivity maximises shock length in the decreasing-increasing case, we consider the second derivative of the shock length,

S_{L}^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)=\frac{D^{\prime}(u_{\ell})}{D(u_{\ell})^{3}}-\frac{D^{\prime}(u_{r})}{D(u_{r})^{3}}<0,\quad{\rm because}\quad D^{\prime}(u_{\ell})<0\quad{\rm and}\quad D^{\prime}(u_{r})>0.

(15)

Since the diffusivity is continuous everywhere, the critical point is a local maximum. In the decreasing-increasing diffusivity case, there exists only one shock location with continuous diffusivity. Consequently the critical point is unique and is the global maximum.

3.4
Nonlinear regularisation selects the shock position

[

]

3.4.1
Nonlinear regularisation corresponds to a modified equal area rule

We use a perturbation argument similar to
[
Wit95
,
Wit96
]
to show that the nonlinear regularisation
Equation

11
leads to modified equal area rule
Equation

10
for any reaction term,

Proof.

We start by writing
Equation

11
in terms of the travelling wave coordinate

,

-c\fd{u}{z}-\fdn{}{z}{2}\Phi(u)+\varepsilon^{2}\fdn{}{z}{2}\left(f(u)\fdn{u}{z}{2}\right)-R(u)=0.

(16)

Next, to examine dynamics close to the shock we introduce a new stretched independent variable
\xi=(z-z_{s})/\varepsilon,
where
z_{s}
is the location of the shock in

-\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\fd{u}{\xi}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\fdn{}{\xi}{2}\Phi(u)+\fdn{}{\xi}{2}\left(\frac{f(u)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\fdn{u}{\xi}{2}\right)-R(u)=0,

(17)

for which we consider the leading order problem as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

\fdn{}{\xi}{2}\Phi(u)-\fdn{}{\xi}{2}\left(f(u)\fdn{u}{\xi}{2}\right)=0.

(18)

Integrating twice with respect to

\Phi(u)-f(u)\fdn{u}{\xi}{2}=c_{1}\xi+c_{2}.

To specify the constants of integration

and

we use the boundary conditions for

Since

as

with higher derivatives of

with respect to

Hence,

and

. Then,

\fdn{u}{\xi}{2}=\frac{\Phi(u)-\Phi_{S}}{f(u)},

(20)

with
\Phi_{S}
still to be determined. After multiplying both sides by

and integrating with respect to

\pm\xi+c_{4}=\int\frac{\df{u}}{\sqrt{2\int_{u^{*}}^{u}\left[\Phi(\bar{u})-\Phi_{S}\right]/f(\bar{u})\df{\bar{u}}+c_{3}}},

]
showed yields an equal area rule in
\Phi(u)
. For

and
u=u_{\ell}
, we want

and

(23)

This equation

\displaystyle\lim_{A\rightarrow-\infty}G(A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{A\rightarrow-\infty}\left(\frac{\e^{Au_{r}}}{A}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(-1\right)^{i}\frac{\Phi(u_{r})^{(i)}}{A^{i}}\right)-\frac{\e^{Au_{\ell}}}{A}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(-1\right)^{i}\frac{\Phi(u_{\ell})^{(i)}}{A^{i}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{A\rightarrow-\infty}\e^{Au_{\ell}}\left(\frac{\e^{A(u_{r}-u_{\ell})}}{A}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(-1\right)^{i}\frac{\Phi(u_{r})^{(i)}}{A^{i}}\right)-\frac{1}{A}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(-1\right)^{i}\frac{\Phi(u_{\ell})^{(i)}}{A^{i}}\right)\right).

To leading order,

\lim_{A\rightarrow-\infty}G(A)=\lim_{A\rightarrow-\infty}\e^{Au_{\ell}}\left(\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(u_{\ell})}{A^{2}}\right)=\lim_{A\rightarrow-\infty}e^{Au_{\ell}}\left(\frac{D(u_{\ell})}{A^{2}}\right)\rightarrow 0^{+},

(48)

because
D(u_{\ell})>0.
Since
G(A)\to-\infty
as
A\to-\infty
,
G(A)\rightarrow 0^{+}
as
A\to\infty
, and
G(A)
is continuous, the intermediate value theorem guarantees that there exists a value of
A
such that
G(A)=0.

∎

4
Conclusion

In this paper we used a nonlinear regularisation in order to control the shock position and obtain a continuous diffusivity shock in a reaction–diffusion equation with partially negative diffusivity. This regularisation gave a modified equal area rule
Equation

10
that by choice of regularisation function
f(u)
could control the shock position. The continuous diffusivity shock position is only distinct from the equal area in
\Phi(u)
shock in the case of non-symmetric diffusivity. We showed that the continuous diffusivity rule for decreasing-increasing diffusivities selects the longest possible shock. We explored these dynamics and used geometric singular perturbation theory to prove the existence of travelling wave solutions with the continuous diffusivity shock for cubic reaction terms. The nonlinear regularisation and modified equal area rule derived in this paper also apply to arbitrary reaction terms, including the nonlinear diffusion equation where
R(u)=0
.

In our numerical solutions we solved reaction–diffusion equations with linear non-local
Equation

6
and nonlinear
equation

11
regularisations. In the absence of a regularisation imposed explicitly, numerical error in the discretisation can also implicitly regularise the reaction–diffusion equation. For our numerical solutions, we used the central difference

\pdn{\Phi(u)}{x}{2}\approx\frac{\Phi_{i-1}-2\Phi_{i}+\Phi_{i+1}}{(\Delta x)^{2}},

(54)

where
\Delta x
is the grid spacing, which to leading order has the error

E=-(\Delta x)^{2}\left[\frac{\Phi_{xxxx}}{12}\right]_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^{4})

(55)

as
\Delta x\to 0,
which yields a regularisation of the form
Equation

51
, for which the corresponding shock condition is
Equation

53
. Using this rule to anslyse our numerical error term we get

\int_{u_{\ell}}^{u_{r}}D(u)\left(\Phi(u)-\Phi_{S}\right)\df{u}=\left[\frac{\Phi(u)^{2}}{2}-\Phi(u)\Phi_{S}\right]_{u=u_{\ell}}^{u=u_{r}},

(56)

which is satisfied by any shock with continuous
\Phi(u)
. Alternative discretisations of the diffusivity are possible, for example the conservation form

\pdn{\Phi(u)}{x}{2}=\pd{}{x}\left(D(u)\pd{u}{x}\right)\approx\frac{D_{i+1/2}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})-D_{i-1/2}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})}{(\Delta x)^{2}},

(57)

where

D_{i+1/2}=\frac{D_{i}+D_{i+1}}{2}.

(58)

This discretisation has the numerical error

E=-\frac{(\Delta x)^{2}}{12}\left[Du_{xxxx}+2D_{x}u_{xxx}+3D_{xx}u_{xx}+2D_{xxx}u_{x}\right]_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^{4})

(59)

as
\Delta x\to 0.
This form of the numerical error corresponds to the regularisation

-\varepsilon^{2}\left[\pdn{\Phi(u)}{x}{4}+\pd{}{x}\left(D^{\prime\prime}(u)\left(\pd{u}{x}\right)^{3}\right)\right],

(60)

for which we do not have a shock selection rule. Preliminary numerical solutions of the regularised reaction–diffusion equations
Equations

6
and
11
using the discretisation
Equation

57
seem to show the shocks being moved away from their predicted shock positions. A possible avenue of future research would be to find the shock condition corresponding to the regularisation term
Equation

60
and to see if using the discretisation
Equation

57
is acting to move the shocks to ones that satisfy that shock condition.

Another potential extension is the consider different forms of the diffusivity
D(u).
In this paper we focus on positive-negative-positive diffusivities where the diffusivity has two zeros, but this is not the only possibility. For instance
[
Mai+06
,
Mai+07
]
considered reaction–diffusion equations in the case where the diffusivity had one zero and one sign change.
[
KR11
]
mainly considered a positive-negative-diffusivity but generalised their results to diffusivities with
2n
sign changes. Given that the continuous diffusivity shock is of the longest possible length, another avenue for future work is to identify the consequences for this solution in terms of the entropy of the solutions. Given that there are many other possible shocks, entropy considerations might also provide another way to select a shock position.

New Boundes for Sombor Index of Graphs
Maryam Mohammadi, Hasan Barzegar

Abstract
In this paper, we find some bounds for the Sombor index of the graph G G by triangle inequality, arithmetic index, geometric index, forgotten index (F(G)), arithmetic-geometric (AG) index, geometric-arithmetic (GA) index, symmetric division deg index (SDD(G)) and some central and dispersion indices. The bounds could state estimated values and error intervals of the Sombor index to show limits of accuracy. The error intervals are written as inequalities. Keywords: Sombor index; topological indices; central and dispersion indices; geometric-arithmetic index; arithmetic-geometric index.

The triangle inequality and Sombor index

Definition 2.1
.

The ordered pair

z=(d(u),d(v))

as a point in degree-coordinate (or d-coordinate) called the degree-point (or d-point) of the edge

Definition 2.2
.

The degree-radius (or d-radius) of the edge

uv\in E(G)

is the distance between

(d(u),d(v))

and the origin of the coordinate system denoted by

|z|=r(d(u),d(v))=\sqrt{d^{2}(u)+d^{2}(v)},

and we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}|z|\geq d(u)+d(v).

(2.1)

The distance between two d-points

and

z_{2}=(d(u_{2}),d(v_{2}))

is

|z_{1}-z_{2}|=\sqrt{(d(u_{1})-d(u_{2}))^{2}+(d(v_{1})-d(v_{2}))^{2}}

and triangle inequality gives us a upper bound for the absolute of the summation of d-points

|z_{1}+z_{2}+...+z_{n}|\leq|z_{1}|+|z_{2}|+...+|z_{n}|

Remark 2.3
.

In cases of the reduced Sombor index and averaged Sombor index, it is enough to change once

|z^{\prime}|=r(d(u)-1,d(v)-1)=\sqrt{(d(u)-1)^{2}+(d(v)-1)^{2}}

and

|z^{\prime\prime}|=r(d(u)-\frac{2m}{n},d(v)-\frac{2m}{n})=\sqrt{(d(u)-\frac{2m}{n})^{2}+(d(v)-\frac{2m}{n})^{2}}.

Then similarly use the following definitions and theorems .

Theorem 2.4
.

Let

G

be a connected graph with

n

vertices and

edges such that d-point

\displaystyle|z_{1}+z_{2}+...+z_{m}|\leq SO(G)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sqrt{2(|z_{i}|^{2}-d(u_{i})d(v_{i}))}.

(2.2)

Base on the triangle inequality and its generalization

∎

|w_{1}|>|w_{2}|>...>|w_{m}|,

then

\displaystyle(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|z_{i}|)(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|w_{i}|)\leq m\sum_{i=1}^{m}|z_{i}||w_{i}|\Rightarrow SO(G_{1}).SO(G_{2})\leq m\sum_{i=1}^{m}|z_{i}||w_{i}|.

∎

8
The arithmetic-geometric and geometric-arithmetic indices and Sombor index

Several years ago, the arithmetic-geometric and geometric-arithmetic indices were introduced in mathematical chemistry. Now in this paper, we determine some boundes for the Sombor index by coefficients of each one of these indices.

Definition 8.1
.

Two sample of the topological indices applied in the chemical graph theorem that are stated in refrences
[
3
]
and
[
14
]
are arithmetic-geometry index and geometric-arithmetic index which are defined as follow

\displaystyle AG(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{\frac{d(u)}{d(v)}}+\sqrt{\frac{d(v)}{d(u)}})=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{d(u)+d(v)}{\sqrt{d(u)d(v)}}\big),

(8.1)

and

\displaystyle GA(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\big(\frac{2\sqrt{d(u)d(v)}}{d(u)+d(v)}\big).

(8.2)

Considering the reference
[
14
]
, it is proved that

\displaystyle GA(G)\leq AG(G).

(8.3)

Also note that

\displaystyle AG(G)\leq SO(G),

(8.4)

because for each the edge

e=uv\in E(G)

\displaystyle\frac{d(u)+d(v)}{\sqrt{2}}\leq\sqrt{d^{2}(u)+d^{2}(v)}\Rightarrow\frac{d(u)+d(v)}{2}\leq\sqrt{d^{2}(u)+d^{2}(v)}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{d(u)+d(v)}{2\sqrt{d(u)d(v)}}\leq\sqrt{d^{2}(u)+d^{2}(v)}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{d(u)+d(v)}{2\sqrt{d(u)d(v)}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sqrt{d^{2}(u)+d^{2}(v)}~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\Rightarrow AG(G)\leq SO(G).~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Now we are going to improve the above bound:

Theorem 8.2
.

Let

G

be a graph with

n

vertices,

m

edges and with maximum degree

{\Delta}

and minimum degree

\delta,

then

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\delta AG(G)\leq SO(G)\leq\sqrt{2}(n-1)AG(G).

(8.5)

Proof.

For obtaining lower bound and upper bound, consider the following function

\displaystyle f(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{\frac{x+y}{2\sqrt{xy}}}=\frac{2\sqrt{x^{3}y+y^{3}x}}{x+y}

where

2\leq\delta\leq x\leq y\leq n-1.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=\frac{(x^{3}y+y^{3}x)(3x^{2}y+y^{3})(x+y)-2\sqrt{x^{3}y+y^{3}x}}{(x+y)^{2}}\geq 0

and this means that

f(x,y)

is a increasing function in x. Thus, the function obtains its minimum at point

(\delta,y_{1})

for some

y_{1}

such that

\delta\leq y_{1}\leq n-1

and its maximum at point

(y_{2},y_{2})

for some

y_{2}

such that

\delta\leq y_{2}.

it is concluded that

\displaystyle f(\delta,\delta)\leq f(x,y)\leq f(n-1,n-1)~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\sqrt{2}\delta\leq f(x,y)\leq\sqrt{2}(n-1)~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\sqrt{2}\delta\frac{x+y}{2\sqrt{xy}}\leq\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2}(n-1)\frac{x+y}{2\sqrt{xy}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\sqrt{2}\delta\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{x+y}{2\sqrt{xy}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2}(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{x+y}{2\sqrt{xy}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\sqrt{2}\delta AG(G)\leq SO(G)\leq\sqrt{2}(n-1)AG(G).

∎

Theorem 8.3
.

Let

G

be a graph with

n

vertices and

m

edges, then

\displaystyle GA(G)<SO(G).

(8.6)

Proof.

Base on the inequality (
3.1
) for each edge

e=uv\in E(G),

\displaystyle\sqrt{d(u).d(v)}\leq\frac{d(u)+d(v)}{2}\Rightarrow 2\sqrt{d(u).d(v)}\leq d(u)+d(v),

then

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{2\sqrt{d(u).d(v)}}{d(u)+d(v)}\leq 1,

(8.7)

and since

\sqrt{d^{2}u+d^{2}v}>1,

\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{2\sqrt{d(u).d(v)}}{d(u)+d(v)}<\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\sqrt{d^{2}(u)+d^{2}(v)}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow GA(G)<SO(G).~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

∎

Now we are going to improve this bound:

Theorem 8.4
.

Let

G

be a graph with

n

vertices and

m

edges, then

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\delta GA(G)\leq SO(G)\leq\sqrt{2}(n-1)GA(G)

(8.8)

Proof.

Consider the following function

\displaystyle f(x,y)=(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{\frac{2\sqrt{xy}}{x+y}})^{2}=\frac{(x^{2}+y^{2})(x+y)^{2}}{4xy},

where

2\leq\delta\leq x\leq y\leq n-1

then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=\frac{12x^{4}y+16x^{3}y^{2}+8x^{2}y^{3}-4y^{5}}{(4xy)^{2}}\geq 0,

this means that

f(x,y)

is a increasing function in

x

and give its minimum at point

(\delta,y_{1})

for some

\delta

such that

2\leq\delta\leq y_{1}\leq n-1,

and its maximum at point

(n-1,n-1)
.
it is concluded that

\displaystyle f(\delta,\delta)\leq f(x,y)\leq f(n-1,n-1)\Rightarrow\sqrt{2}\delta\leq f(x,y)\leq\sqrt{2}(n-1)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\sqrt{2}\delta~\frac{2\sqrt{xy}}{x+y}\leq\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2}(n-1)~\frac{2\sqrt{xy}}{x+y}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\sqrt{2}\delta\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{2\sqrt{xy}}{x+y}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2}(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{2\sqrt{xy}}{x+y}~~~~~~

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\sqrt{2}\delta GA(G)\leq SO(G)\leq\sqrt{2}(n-1)GA(G).~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

∎

The symmetric division deg index and Sombor index

Base on the reference
[
15
]
, the symmetric division deg index (SDD(G)) is as another index for predicting some physicochemical properties of substances and its tests are carried out by International Academy of Mathematical Chemistry, so here we want to pay a little attention to it and compare it with Sombor index:

Definition 9.1
.

The symmetric division deg index (SDD(G)) for graph

G

is defined as

\displaystyle SDD(G)=(\frac{min\{d(u),d(v)\}}{max\{d(u),d(v)\}}+\frac{max\{d(u),d(v)\}}{min\{d(u),d(v)\}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\frac{d(u)}{d(v)}+\frac{d(v)}{d(u)}),

so

\displaystyle SDD(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{d^{2}(u)+d^{2}(v)}{d(u)d(v)}

(9.1)

Theorem 9.2
.

Let

G

be a graph with

n

vertices and

m

edges, then

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\delta SDD(G)\leq SO(G)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(n-1)SDD(G).

(9.2)

Proof.

For the other hand side of the inequality, consider the following function

\displaystyle f(x,y)=(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{xy}})=\frac{xy\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{x^{2}+y^{2}},

where

2\leq\delta\leq x\leq y\leq n-1

then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=\frac{(y(x^{2}+y^{2})+x)\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}-2x^{2}y\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}\geq 0,

this means that

f(x,y)

is a increasing function in

x

and give its minimum at point

(\delta,y_{1})

for some

\delta

such that

2\leq\delta\leq y_{1}\leq n-1,

and its maximum at point

(n-1,n-1)
.
it is concluded that

\displaystyle f(\delta,\delta)\leq f(x,y)\leq f(n-1,n-1)\Rightarrow\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\delta\leq f(x,y)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(n-1)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\delta\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{xy}\leq\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(n-1)\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{xy}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\delta\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{xy}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{xy}~~~

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\delta SDD(G)\leq SO(G)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(n-1)SDD(G).~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

∎

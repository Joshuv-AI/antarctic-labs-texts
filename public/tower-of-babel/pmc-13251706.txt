Efficacy and safety of an intravenous amikacin-containing regimen as initial therapy in Mycobacterium avium complex pulmonary disease
Authors: Tomoki Teramoto, Akihiro Ito, Yosuke Nakanishi, Toru Hashimoto, Tadashi Ishida
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThe global incidence of Mycobacterium avium complex pulmonary disease (MAC-PD) has been increasing, particularly in Japan. Although current guidelines recommend adding intravenous amikacin (AMK-IV) for cavitary or severe disease, evidence supporting its use as initial therapy remains limited, especially among elderly patients.MethodsThis retrospective study included patients with cavitary MAC-PD who received an AMK-IV-containing regimen as initial therapy at Kurashiki Central Hospital between September 2021 and July 2024. AMK-IV dosing was adjusted based on therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM). The primary outcome was sputum culture conversion. Secondary outcomes included radiologic improvement, adverse events, recurrence, and all-cause mortality. Clinical characteristics and outcomes were compared between patients aged ≥75 and < 75 years.ResultsTwenty-one patients were analyzed (aged ≥75 years, n = 11; aged <75 years, n = 10; median age, 75 years; 76.2% female). TDM-guided dose adjustment of AMK-IV was performed in all patients, and outpatient treatment was continued using the adjusted dose. Target peak concentrations were achieved in 57.1% of patients, while target trough concentrations were achieved in all patients. At 6/12 months, sputum culture conversion was achieved in 56.3%/62.5% and radiologic improvement was observed in 83.3%/88.9%. No significant differences in sputum culture conversion or radiologic improvement were found between age groups. Treatment discontinuation occurred in two patients (9.5%), whereas 90.9% of elderly patients completed ≥3 months of AMK-IV.ConclusionAMK-IV added to a standard regimen for cavitary MAC-PD may be effective and safe. In this TDM-guided protocol, favorable outcomes were observed despite lower peak concentrations than those generally recommended.

Introduction

The incidence of pulmonary nontuberculous mycobacterial disease (NTM-PD) has been increasing worldwide in recent years [1], [2], [3], [4]. In Japan, approximately 90% of NTM-PD cases are attributable to Mycobacterium avium complex pulmonary disease (MAC-PD) [5]. MAC-PD demonstrates increasing incidence and prevalence with advancing age in both men and women, and therefore frequently requires treatment in elderly patients [3]. According to the latest international guidelines (ATS/ERS/ESCMID/IDSA 2020), the standard regimen for MAC-PD is multidrug therapy including a macrolide [6]. In particular, for patients with cavitary disease or severe bronchiectasis, the addition of intravenous amikacin (AMK-IV) to a regimen consisting of rifampicin (RFP), ethambutol (EB), and azithromycin (AZM) is recommended.

The latest international guidelines recommend the addition of an aminoglycoside to a regimen of rifampicin (RFP), ethambutol (EB), and a macrolide (clarithromycin [CAM] or azithromycin [AZM]) for patients with cavitary, extensive nodular/bronchiectatic disease, or macrolide-resistant MAC-PD [6]. Consistent with these recommendations, the Japanese consensus statement published in 2023 also supports this approach [7]. However, both AMK-IV and intramuscular streptomycin (SM-IM) are listed as options, and the choice between the two antibiotics is left to the discretion of the attending physician. With regard to the efficacy of adding an aminoglycoside to standard therapy for MAC-PD, Kobashi et al. reported in a randomized controlled trial that SM significantly increased sputum culture conversion rates [8]. However, very few studies have evaluated the efficacy and safety of AMK-IV, including its use in elderly patients.

Therefore, we aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of an AMK-IV-containing regimen as initial therapy in patients with MAC-PD.

Methods

Study design and setting

This retrospective study included patients with MAC-PD who were treated with an AMK-IV-containing regimen including at least a macrolide and ethambutol as initial therapy at Kurashiki Central Hospital between September 2021 and July 2024. Patients who received AMK-IV as second-line or later therapy were excluded. The diagnosis of MAC-PD was based on the latest international guideline [6]. In all patients, clinical characteristics, including age, sex, height, weight, body mass index, creatinine clearance (calculated using the Cockcroft–Gault equation), comorbidities, causative species (M. avium, M. intracellulare, or MAC), sputum smear and culture results, radiologic type (noncavitary NB, cavitary NB, fibrocavitary [FC]), treatment regimen and duration, and outcomes were investigated. Patients with positive sputum cultures for M. avium complex, but negative polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assay results for both M. avium and M. intracellulare, precluding species-level identification, were classified as MAC. This study was approved by the institutional review board of Kurashiki Central Hospital (approval No. 4793, December 2, 2025). Due to the retrospective design of this study, the requirement for written informed consent was waived. Instead, an opt-out consent process was implemented, and information about the study was disclosed on the hospital website.

Administration method of AMK-IV

AMK-IV was initiated during hospitalization in all cases. Initial dosing of AMK-IV was started at 10–15 mg/kg, and adjusted based on therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) in accordance with the 2017 British Thoracic Society (BTS) guidelines [9]. Prior to initiation, all patients were referred to the Otolaryngology Department for baseline audiometric screening, and hearing was monitored during AMK-IV therapy as appropriate. AMK-IV was administered over 30 min once daily while patients were hospitalized for a period of 2 to 4 weeks. Peak concentrations were measured 1 h after the initiation of infusion, and trough concentrations were measured immediately before the next dose. The target peak concentration was approximately 25–35 mg/L during daily inpatient administration, and the target trough concentration was <5 mg/L. TDM was performed approximately once weekly until discharge, and dose adjustment was carried out based on the measured concentrations, with the amikacin dose increased or decreased by 50–100 mg, as appropriate. After discharge, AMK-IV was continued at a frequency of three times per week at a local medical facility at the same dosage as at discharge. The duration of AMK-IV was determined to be between 3 and 6 months. However, adjustments to the treatment duration were permitted at the discretion of the attending physician.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome was sputum culture conversion within 6 and 12 months after treatment initiation. The secondary outcomes were radiologic improvement on chest computed tomography (CT) at 6 and 12 months after treatment initiation, adverse events, microbiologic recurrence, and all-cause mortality during the follow-up period. The follow-up period extended through October 15, 2025. Radiologic classification and assessment of treatment response were independently determined by at least two respiratory physicians. Radiologic improvement was defined as a reduction in cavity size and/or improvement in nodular lesions and consolidation on chest CT. Sputum culture conversion was defined as three consecutive negative mycobacterial cultures from sputum samples collected at least four weeks apart, and microbiologic recurrence was defined as the detection of MAC in two consecutive sputum cultures collected at least four weeks apart, based on a previous report [10].

Patient backgrounds and outcomes were compared between patients aged ≥75 years (≥75 years group) and those aged <75 years (<75 years group) to assess the feasibility of this regimen in the elderly population.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are expressed as counts (percentage) and continuous variables as medians and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were assessed using Fisher's exact test. Continuous variables were analyzed using the non-parametric Mann–Whitney U test. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using R (version 4.5.1, Vienna, Austria).

Discussion

In the present study, an AMK-IV-containing regimen as initial treatment in patients with cavitary MAC-PD resulted in sputum culture conversion rates of 56.3% at 6 months and 62.5% at 12 months. Among the 18 patients (85.7%) who underwent CT follow-up at 6 months, 83.3% showed improvement and 88.9% at 12 months. In the ≥75 years group, 80.0% demonstrated radiologic improvement, whereas in the <75 years group, 87.5% showed improvement. Treatment was discontinued in two patients (9.5%) due to adverse events (diarrhea and hearing impairment). No statistically significant difference in the sputum culture conversion rate was observed between the two age groups.

Kim et al. found that in the treatment of cavitary MAC-PD, the use of SM-IM or AMK-IV in addition to macrolide-containing regimen resulted in sputum culture conversion rates at 12 months of 70.9% in the SM-IM group and 80.5% in the AMK-IV group [11]. In the SM-IM group, 74.0% of patients had the cavitary NB type and 26.0% had the FC type; whereas in the AMK-IV group, 80.5% had the cavitary NB type and 19.5% had the FC type. The treatment completion rates were 74.8% in the SM-IM group and 78.0% in the AMK-IV group. In the present study, the overall culture conversion rate with the addition of AMK-IV to a standard regimen including ethambutol and a macrolide was lower than those reported by Kim et al. [13]. In our study, the number of patients was smaller (21 vs 41) and the proportion of patients with FC types was higher than that in Kim's study (42.9% vs 19.5%). These factors could have affected the sputum culture conversion rate.

Although no previous study has directly compared outcomes with or without an AMK-IV-containing regimen as initial treatment, Kobashi et al. conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate whether the addition of SM-IM to a standard regimen could improve clinical outcomes, including sputum culture conversion within 6 months [8]. In that study, 53.4% of patients had cavitary disease, and the sputum culture conversion rate was 71.2%, which appears comparable to the results of our analysis.

In the present study, imaging findings were reviewed by multiple physicians, and we reported the proportion of patients who demonstrated overall radiologic improvement, not limited to cavitary lesions. Previous studies have shown that cavity closure or reduction is significantly associated with microbiologic cure and lower mortality [14]. However, these studies included patients who did not receive NTM-targeted therapy, and the treatment regimens were not fully described. To the best of our knowledge, few studies have quantitatively evaluated radiologic improvement with an AMK-IV-containing regimen in patients with cavitary MAC-PD. Therefore, the present study may provide clinically relevant information regarding radiologic response in this population.

In the report by Kim et al., treatment discontinuation due to newly developed adverse events occurred in 6 of 41 patients (14.6%) who received AMK-IV [11]. The reasons for discontinuation were hearing impairment in 3 patients, tingling sensation in 1 patient, azotemia in 1 patient, and injection site pain in 1 patient. In our study, treatment was discontinued in two patients due to hearing impairment and diarrhea, one case each. Other adverse events were observed during follow-up after completion of AMK-IV; however, these were not considered specific to AMK-IV and were likely attributable to other antibiotics in the standard regimen (e.g., ethambutol-associated visual impairment). In the ≥75 years group, only 1 patient discontinued therapy due to diarrhea, and 90.9% of elderly patients were able to safely complete at least three months of AMK-IV therapy.

The specific adverse events of AMK-IV are related to eighth cranial nerve toxicity, such as vertigo, tinnitus, and hearing loss [15]. In the present study, TDM was performed in all cases to maintain serum amikacin concentrations within the therapeutic range. Peak concentrations exceeding 25 mg/L were achieved in 95.2% of patients, while target trough concentrations were achieved in all patients. Given the small sample size, it was difficult to determine whether adverse events were concentration-dependent. In this study, AMK peak concentrations were adjusted to a target range of 25–35 mg/L based on the BTS guidelines [9]. In contrast, the ATS/ERS/ESCMID/IDSA guidelines [6] recommend a higher target range of 35–45 mg/L. Therefore, the final peak concentrations in our cohort were set relatively low; nevertheless, sputum culture conversion and radiologic improvement were achieved in 56.3% and 85.3% of patients, respectively.

In the present study, the overall sputum culture conversion rate was lower than that reported previously, particularly in the ≥75 years group. However, our cohort included a higher proportion of FC type disease compared with prior studies, specifically in the ≥75 years group, suggesting that a greater number of patients had treatment-refractory disease at baseline. Although four patients died during follow-up, two (Supplementary Table 1; Cases 1 and 13) occurred long after the completion of AMK therapy, and the remaining two (Cases 3 and 10) were attributable to bacterial pneumonia. Therefore, none of the deaths were considered to be treatment-related mortality due to MAC-PD.

Based on these findings, the addition of AMK-IV to a standard regimen as initial treatment for cavitary MAC-PD, as recommended by international guidelines and the 2023 Japanese Consensus Statement [7], [9], appears to be both effective and safe. Furthermore, our results suggest that AMK-IV can be administered safely even in patients aged ≥75 years when managed with TDM. A previous report indicated that prolonged administration of AMK-IV for more than three months is associated with improved treatment success rates [16]; however, this requires daily or thrice-weekly intravenous infusions over an extended period. To ensure accurate TDM, initial inpatient management is desirable, but hospitalization beyond three months is impractical due to financial, psychological, and healthcare resource considerations. Once serum concentrations stabilize within the therapeutic range, outpatient infusion therapy is a reasonable option.

According to the BTS guidelines [9], peak serum concentrations of 65–85 mg/L are recommended for thrice-weekly dosing. However, achieving this target serum concentration has been reported to be difficult even with higher doses of AMK [17], and because high-dose and long-term use of aminoglycosides carries risks of ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity, our institution has adopted a modified approach: after transitioning to outpatient care, patients continue thrice-weekly administration at doses determined by TDM during daily dosing, targeting lower peak concentrations of 25–35 mg/L. The AMK-IV protocol was generally consistent with that reported by Watanabe et al. [18]. In the treatment of multidrug-resistant tuberculosis (MDR-TB), the risk of hearing impairment increases with higher cumulative doses and longer treatment durations [19]. Aminoglycosides cause irreversible damage by injuring cochlear hair cells, which lack regenerative capacity [20]. Therefore, referral to otolaryngologists and regular audiometric monitoring are essential for all patients.

The present study has several limitations. First, it was a single-center retrospective study, which is inherently susceptible to selection and information biases. Second, the sample size was small, comprising only 21 patients. The limited statistical power raises the possibility that the absence of significant differences may not accurately reflect true differences in treatment efficacy. Third, in the assessment of treatment outcomes, some patients were unable to provide sputum samples, and the radiologic evaluations may have been subject to observer bias, which could have influenced the assessment of treatment efficacy.

Conclusion

In this study, daily amikacin administration was initiated during hospitalization, followed by TDM-guided dose adjustment and continuation of outpatient treatment using the adjusted dose. Favorable treatment outcomes were achieved despite peak concentrations lower than those generally recommended. These findings suggest that the addition of AMK-IV to a standard regimen as an initial treatment for patients with cavitary MAC-PD may be effective and safe.

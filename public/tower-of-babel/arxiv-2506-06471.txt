Energy-stable Port-Hamiltonian Systems
Patrick Buchfink † and Silke Glas † and Hans Zwart †,‡

Abstract




Abstract.

We combine energy-stable and port-Hamiltonian (
pH
) systems to obtain
energy-stable port-Hamiltonian (
es-pH
) systems
. The idea is to extend the known energy-stable systems with an input–output port, which results in a
pH
formulation.
One advantage of the new
es-pH
formulation is that it naturally preserves its
es-pH
structure throughout discretization (in space and time) and model reduction.

Keywords:

energy-stable systems,
port-Hamiltonian systems,
structure-preserving discretization,
model reduction

1.
Introduction

Energy-based concepts for evolution problems are important to formulate interpretable and physically consistent models.
Such models typically come with a power and energy balance equation (
PBE
and
EBE
) and/or dissipation inequality (
DI
) bounding the system energy,
which can be used to guarantee properties like passivity, stability, and boundedness of solutions.
The two energy-based systems relevant in this paper are (a) energy-stable
[
3
]
and (b) port-Hamiltonian systems (
pH
) (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
).
While energy-stable systems offer a straight-forward framework for structure-preserving discretization and model reduction (
MOR
),
pH
systems extend Hamiltonian systems by adding ports; thus allowing to formulate open and dissipative systems.
Structure-preservation in discretization and
MOR
is beneficial since it typically enables to transfer physical guarantees of the system to the discrete (or reduced) system.

In this work, we show that (a) and (b) can be combined to a new formalism,
energy-stable port-Hamiltonian (
es-pH
) systems
.
This new formalism combines the advantages of (a) and (b) resulting in energy-based systems equipped with an input–output port and a general framework for structure-preserving discretization and
MOR
.
We present the
es-pH
system in three formulations; each with a separate goal:
(i) a matrix formulation in
Section

1.1
to illustrate the idea,
(ii) a formulation on possibly infinite-dimensional space in
Section

2
, extending energy-stable systems,
and, (iii) a formulation on Dirac structures in
Section

3
to show how
es-pH
systems can be understood as
pH
systems.

The paper provides a general structural framework for modeling energy-based systems with an
EBE
and/or
DI
. For such a general framework, it is hard to address questions of existence and uniqueness. Thus, we do not specify initial value or boundary conditions here, which must be addressed when looking at a particular problem formulation.

Structure-preserving techniques have also been considered for
Hamiltonian systems via the Dirac–Frenkel formalism, see, e.g.,
[
7
, Sec. 2]
,
Hamiltonian PDEs, see, e.g.,
[
1
]
,
Hamiltonian field theory, see, e.g.,
[
8
]
,
and
energy-stable systems
[
3
]
.
Our new
es-pH
formulation extends these works by adding an input–output port that is crucial to model forced systems in control theory.
Port-Hamiltonian systems are already equipped with an input–output port. Moreover, a multitude of discretization techniques and
MOR
algorithms have been presented over the last years, see, e.g.
[
10
,
2
,
6
,
5
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
4
]
.
To our knowledge, however, none of these techniques are as natural as the techniques presented for energy-stable systems in
[
3
]
in the sense that solely a (Petrov–)Galerkin projection is sufficient for structure-preservation, which is our motivation to formulate
es-pH
systems.

As we understand our work as an extension to the related works listed in the previous paragraph, we foresee
es-pH
systems to be applicable to the applications listed therein including, e.g., wave and elasticity equations
[
8
]
, shallow water equations
[
1
]
, and magneto-quasistatics
[
3
]
. A numerical study is subject to future work.

Notationwise, for a vector space
\mathbb{V}
and an interval
I_{t}=[t_{0},t_{\text{end}}]\subset\mathbb{R}
,
we denote an arbitrary point
x\in\mathbb{V}
or a curve
x(\cdot):I_{t}\to\mathbb{V}
using the same symbol
x
. To differentiate the two objects, the curve is always indicated by brackets with
x(\cdot)

t\in I_{t}
.

\left.\kern-1.2pt{(\cdot)}\right|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{V}}}
.

1.1.
Illustrating the main idea in finite-dimensional vector spaces

We are interested in energy-based systems using an
energy functional
(or
Hamiltonian
)
\bm{H}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
,
that describe the dynamics of
a state
\bm{x}(\cdot):I_{t}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
an output curve
\bm{y}(\cdot):I_{t}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{y}}

on a time interval
I_{t}\vcentcolon=[t_{0},t_{\text{end}}]\subset\mathbb{R}
.
As reference, we consider the
input–state–output port-Hamiltonian (
iso-pH
) system (with feedthrough term)

(1.1)

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{\bm{x}}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{J}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{R}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}\right)\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial\bm{H}}{\partial\bm{x}}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}+\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{G}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{P}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}\right)\bm{u}(t),

\displaystyle\bm{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{G}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}^{\top}+\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{P}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}^{\top}\right)\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial\bm{H}}{\partial\bm{x}}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}+\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{S}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}-{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{N}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}}\right)\bm{u}(t),

from, e.g.,
[
13
,
9
]
, with matrix-valued functions
\bm{J},\bm{R}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
,
\bm{G},\bm{P}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times n_{y}}
and
\bm{S},\bm{N}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{y}\times n_{y}}
.
Moreover, it must hold, that the matrix-valued functions
\bm{Z},\bm{W}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{(N+n_{y})\times(N+n_{y})}
combining
\bm{J}
,
\bm{R}
,
\bm{G}
,
\bm{P}
,
\bm{S}
, and
\bm{N}
are pointwise skew-symmetric and pointwise symmetric positive (semi-)definite, respectively,

\displaystyle\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{Z}}\right|_{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\begin{bmatrix}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{J}}\right|_{\bm{x}}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{G}}\right|_{\bm{x}}\\
-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{G}}\right|_{\bm{x}}^{\top}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{N}}\right|_{\bm{x}}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{W}}\right|_{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\begin{bmatrix}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{R}}\right|_{\bm{x}}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{P}}\right|_{\bm{x}}\\
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{P}}\right|_{\bm{x}}^{\top}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{S}}\right|_{\bm{x}}\end{bmatrix},

allowing to rewrite (
1.1
) as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{x}(t)\\
\bm{y}(t)\end{bmatrix}+\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{Z}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{W}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}\right)\begin{bmatrix}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial\bm{H}}{\partial\bm{x}}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}\\
\bm{u}(t)\end{bmatrix}=\bm{0}.

In this paper, we introduce the
energy-stable port-Hamiltonian (
es-pH
) system

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left(-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\omega}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}+\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\rho}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}\right)\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{x}(t)

\displaystyle=-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial\bm{H}}{\partial\bm{x}}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}+\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\gamma}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\pi}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}\right)\bm{u}(t),

\displaystyle\bm{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\gamma}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}^{\top}+\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\pi}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}^{\top}\right)\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{x}(t)+\left(-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\mu}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}+{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\sigma}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}}\right)\bm{u}(t),

where
\bm{\omega},\bm{\rho}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
,
\bm{\gamma},\bm{\pi}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times n_{y}}
and
\bm{\mu},\bm{\sigma}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{y}\times n_{y}}
are matrix-valued maps.
Again, it must hold that the matrix-valued maps
{\bm{\lambda}},\bm{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{(N+n_{y})\times(N+n_{y})}
combining
\bm{\omega}
,
\bm{\rho}
,
\bm{\gamma}
,
\bm{\pi}
,
\bm{\mu}
, and
\bm{\sigma}
are pointwise skew-symmetric
and symmetric positive (semi-)definite, respectively,

\displaystyle\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\lambda}}\right|_{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\begin{bmatrix}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\omega}}\right|_{\bm{x}}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\gamma}}\right|_{\bm{x}}\\
-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\gamma}}\right|_{\bm{x}}^{\top}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\mu}}\right|_{\bm{x}}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\phi}}\right|_{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\begin{bmatrix}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\rho}}\right|_{\bm{x}}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\pi}}\right|_{\bm{x}}\\
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\pi}}\right|_{\bm{x}}^{\top}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\sigma}}\right|_{\bm{x}}\end{bmatrix},

allowing to rewrite (
1.2
) as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial\bm{H}}{\partial\bm{x}}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}\\
\bm{y}(t)\end{bmatrix}+\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\lambda}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\phi}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}\right)\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{x}(t)\\
\bm{u}(t)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}.

We show that the

system and
that it provides a

.
The latter can be conducted from the previous equation by multiplication with

{\left[\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{x}(t)^{\top}\quad\bm{u}(t)^{\top}\right]}
,
using the chain rule, skew-symmetry of
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\lambda}}\right|_{\bm{x}}
and positive (semi-)definiteness of
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\phi}}\right|_{\bm{x}}
with

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left[\bm{H}(\bm{x}(t))\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{x}(t)\right)^{\top}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial\bm{H}}{\partial\bm{x}}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}

\displaystyle=\bm{y}(t)^{\top}\bm{u}(t)+\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{x}(t)\\
\bm{u}(t)\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\lambda}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{x}(t)\\
\bm{u}(t)\end{bmatrix}}_{=0}\underbrace{-\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{x}(t)\\
\bm{u}(t)\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\phi}}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)}\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{x}(t)\\
\bm{u}(t)\end{bmatrix}}_{\leq 0}

\displaystyle\leq\bm{y}(t)^{\top}\bm{u}(t).

2.
Formulation on Banach Spaces: Structure-preserving discretization and reduction via (Petrov-)Galerkin

In this section, we extend energy-stable systems by an input–output port and derive the corresponding
PBE
and
DI
. Moreover, the ideas of structure-preserving discretization and
MOR
are transferred from energy-stable systems to the
es-pH
formulation.

2.1.
Fundamentals: Banach spaces, bounded linear operators and bilinear forms

Given two real Banach spaces
\left(\mathbb{V},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathbb{V}}\right)
,
\left(\mathbb{W},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathbb{W}}\right)
,
we denote the
bounded linear operators from
\mathbb{V}
to
\mathbb{W}
with
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{W})
.
Further, we denote the
dual space
with
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{R})
and the
duality pairing
with
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}}:\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{W};\mathbb{R})
be the space of bounded bilinear forms
a:\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{W}\to\mathbb{R}
.
A bounded bilinear form
a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{W};\mathbb{R})
naturally defines two bounded linear maps
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{W}^{\prime})
and
A^{\vee}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{W},\mathbb{V}^{\prime})
with

\displaystyle\forall v\in\mathbb{V},w\in\mathbb{W}:\left\langle{Av},{w}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{W}}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=a(v,w),

and

\displaystyle\forall v\in\mathbb{V},w\in\mathbb{W}:\left\langle{A^{\vee}w},{v}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=a(v,w)

and vice versa.
It holds
\left\langle{Av},{w}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{W}}=a(v,w)=\left\langle{A^{\vee}w},{v}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}}
for all
v\in\mathbb{V}
,
w\in\mathbb{W}
.
In the case
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{W}
, the bilinear form
a
and respective operators
A
and
A^{\vee}
are called
symmetric
if
a(v_{2},v_{1})=a(v_{1},v_{2})
for all
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{V}
(or analogously
A=A^{\vee}
) and
skew-symmetric
if
a(v_{2},v_{1})=-a(v_{1},v_{2})
for all
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{V}
(or analogously
A=-A^{\vee}
).
Moreover, we refer to these objects as
positive (semi-)definite
if
a(v,v)\geq 0
or
a(v,v)>0
for all
v\in\mathbb{V}
.

2.2.
Fundamentals: Energy-stable systems

An
energy-stable system

(\mathbb{V},\mathcal{C},H,f)
is characterized by
a Banach space (
\mathbb{V}
,
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathbb{V}}
),
a function
\mathcal{C}:\mathbb{V}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{V}^{\prime})
yielding a pointwise bounded linear operator
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mathcal{C}}\right|_{x}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{V}^{\prime})
,
a continuously differentiable functional
H\in C^{1}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{R})
called the
energy functional
with derivative
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x}\in\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
at
x\in\mathbb{V}
,
and an additional right-hand side term
f:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
.
Then, the goal of an energy-stable system is to find
x(\cdot)\in C^{1}(I_{t},\mathbb{V})
of the evolution problem

\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mathcal{C}}\right|_{x(t)}\;\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)=-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)}+\left.\kern-1.2pt{f}\right|_{x(t)}\in\mathbb{V}^{\prime}

or equivalently in the
variational formulation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mathcal{C}}\right|_{x(t)}\;\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)},{v}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}}=-\left\langle{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)}},{v}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}}+\left\langle{\left.\kern-1.2pt{f}\right|_{x(t)}},{v}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}}\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{V}.

Note, that this formalism can equally model systems of partial differential equations (PDEs), ordinary differential equations (ODEs), or differential algebraic equations (DAEs) mostly depending on the choice of
\mathbb{V}
and
\mathcal{C}
.
By the chain rule and evaluating (
2.1
) for
v=\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)
, all classical solution curves
x\in C^{1}(I_{t},\mathbb{V})
satisfy the
PBE

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left[H(x(t))\right]=\left\langle{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)}},{\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}}=\left\langle{\left.\kern-1.2pt{f}\right|_{x(t)}},{\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}}-\left\langle{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mathcal{C}}\right|_{x(t)}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)},{\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}}.

As shown in
[
3
]
or in
Sections

2.4
and
2.5
for the more general
es-pH
systems,
a (Petrov–)Galerkin projection of the variational formulation can be used to naturally preserve the underlying structure throughout discretization and
MOR
.

2.3.
Banach-space formulation of
es-pH
systems

We characterize a
es-pH
system
(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}},\lambda,\phi,H)
with real Banach spaces
(\mathbb{V},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathbb{V}})
,
(\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}}})
,
a pointwise skew-symmetric map

\lambda:\mathbb{V}\to\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}},\left(\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}}\right)^{\prime}\right)
,
a pointwise symmetric and positive (semi-)definite map

\phi:\mathbb{V}\to\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}},\left(\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}}\right)^{\prime}\right)
,
and a continuously differentiable functional
H\in C^{1}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{R})
called the
energy functional
with derivative
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x}\in\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
.
Given an input signal
u(\cdot):I_{t}\to\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}}
,
the goal of a
es-pH
system is to find a solution curve
x(\cdot)\in C^{1}(I_{t},\mathbb{V})
and output curve
y(\cdot):I_{t}\to\mathbb{V}
of

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left(-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\omega}\right|_{x(t)}+\left.\kern-1.2pt{\rho}\right|_{x(t)}\right)\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)

\displaystyle=-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)}+\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\gamma}\right|_{x(t)}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\pi}\right|_{x(t)}\right)u(t)\in\mathbb{V}^{\prime},

(2.3)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\gamma}\right|_{x(t)}^{\vee}+\left.\kern-1.2pt{\pi}\right|_{x(t)}^{\vee}\right)\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)+\left(-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mu}\right|_{x(t)}+{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\sigma}\right|_{x(t)}}\right)u(t)\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}}^{\prime},

where the maps
\omega,\rho:\mathbb{V}\to\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\right)
,
\gamma,\pi:\mathbb{V}\to\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}},\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\right)
and
\mu,\sigma:\mathbb{V}\to\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}},\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}}^{\prime}\right)
are subblocks of

\displaystyle\left.\kern-1.2pt{\lambda}\right|_{x}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\omega}\right|_{x}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\gamma}\right|_{x}\\
-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\gamma}\right|_{x}^{\vee}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mu}\right|_{x}\end{bmatrix},

and

\displaystyle\left.\kern-1.2pt{\phi}\right|_{x}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\rho}\right|_{x}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\pi}\right|_{x}\\
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\pi}\right|_{x}^{\vee}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\sigma}\right|_{x}\end{bmatrix}.

So (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) can be rewritten equivalently as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)}\\
y(t)\end{bmatrix}+\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\lambda}\right|_{x(t)}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\phi}\right|_{x(t)}\right)\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)\\
u(t)\end{bmatrix}=0\in\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}}^{\prime}.

With the choice
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mathcal{C}}\right|_{x(t)}=-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\omega}\right|_{x(t)}+\left.\kern-1.2pt{\rho}\right|_{x(t)}
and neglecting the input and output, the
es-pH
system is an energy-stable system.

.

2.5.

Structure-preserving methods for time discretization can also be obtained within the energy-stable formalism.
The following techniques are formulated for an

but also hold for systems semi-discretized in space or reduced systems as described in the previous section.
We follow the construction of a time-discretized problem from the energy-stable systems

Consider finite-dimensional subspaces

Then the time-discretized problem is given by the Petrov–Galerkin projection:
Given an input curve

,
find a solution curve

and an output curve

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{t}}\left\langle{\begin{bmatrix}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)}\\
y(t)\end{bmatrix}+\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\lambda}\right|_{x(t)}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{\phi}\right|_{x(t)}\right)\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)\\
u(t)\end{bmatrix}},{\begin{bmatrix}v(t)\\

\displaystyle=0

for all

In
[

, Remark 5 and 6]
, it is shown for energy-stable systems that
this formulation covers discrete gradient methods and the average vector field
collocation method and is closely related to the Lobatto-IIIA method.
The present formulation allows to extend those ideas to systems with an input–output port.

If
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x_{N}(\cdot)\in\mathbb{W}_{N}
and

, it can be shown similarly to the proof of

2.1
that an

like (
2.7
) and an integrated

hold:

\displaystyle H\left(x_{N}(t_{\text{end}})\right)-H\left(x_{N}(t_{0})\right)=

\displaystyle-\int_{I_{t}}\left\langle{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\phi}\right|_{x(t)}\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)\\
u(t)\end{bmatrix}},{\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)\\
u(t)\end{bmatrix}}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}}}\text{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{t}}\left\langle{y_{N}(t)},{u_{N}(t)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}}}\text{d}t

\displaystyle\int_{I_{t}}\left\langle{y_{N}(t)},{u_{N}(t)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{IO}}}\text{d}t.

Dirac structure formulation: Connecting to port-Hamiltonian systems

In this section, we show that the
es-pH

To this end,

formulates general
pH

Sections

3.2

For the sake of brevity, we restrict to finite-dimensional systems.

3.1.
Fundamentals: Dirac structure and finite-dimensional port-Hamiltonian systems

Assume to be given a finite-dimensional real Banach space
\mathcal{F}
.
In contrast to the previous sections, we use
\mathcal{F}
instead of
\mathbb{V}
to match the notation established for Dirac structures.
Moreover, an element

f\in\mathcal{F}
is called a
flow vector
,

e\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is called an
effort vector
,
and the dual product
\left\langle{e},{f}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}}
describes a physical power.

We call a subspace
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
a
Dirac structure
if

(3.1)

\displaystyle\text{(i)\penalty\ }\left\langle{e},{f}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=0\quad\forall(f,e)\in\mathcal{D}

and

\displaystyle\text{(ii)\penalty\ }\dim(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle=\dim(\mathcal{F}).

From the first property, (i), it can be shown that
\dim(\mathcal{D})\leq\dim(\mathcal{F})
.
Thus, the interpretation of a Dirac structure is that it is a subspace of
\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
which is (i) power-conserving and (ii) of maximal dimension.

A
pH
system (formulated on a finite-dimensional manifold)

is characterized by a manifold
M
, on which the dynamics evolve,
a continuously differentiable functional
H\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})

(called the
energy functional
or
Hamiltonian
),
finite-dimensional vector spaces
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}
which define spaces for input--output and resistive variables,
and a state-dependent
2
2

2

An alternative naming is
modulated
Dirac structure.

Dirac structure

\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mathcal{D}}\right|_{x}\subset(T_{x}M\times\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}\times\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}})\times(T_{x}M\times\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}\times\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}})^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mathcal{D}}\right|_{x}
is for each fixed
x\in M
a Dirac structure in the understanding of the previous paragraph with the flow space
\mathcal{F}=T_{x}M\times\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}\times\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}
where
T_{x}M
denotes the tangent space of
M
at the point
x\in M
.
The duality pairing of the flow space
\mathcal{F}=T_{x}M\times\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}\times\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}
is assumed to be inherited from the duality pairings on
T_{x}M
,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}
with

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\langle{e},{f}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}}=\left\langle{e_{T_{x}M}},{f_{T_{x}M}}\right\rangle_{T_{x}M}+\left\langle{e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}},{f_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}+\left\langle{e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}},{f_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}},

and

The

based on trajectories

,
e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}(\cdot):I_{t}\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}^{\prime}
for the input–output variables and

,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left(-\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t),f_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}(t),f_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}(t),\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)},e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}(t),e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}(t)\right)\in\mathcal{D}(x(t)),\qquad\forall t\in I_{t}.

The idea of this choice is that it automatically follows from (

), (
3.3
) that

\displaystyle\left\langle{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)}},{-\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)}\right\rangle_{T_{x}M}+\left\langle{e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}(t)},{f_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}(t)}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}+\left\langle{e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}(t)},{f_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}(t)}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}=0

and, thus, with the additional assumption that

,
and by using the chain rule, a

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left[H(x(t))\right]=

\displaystyle\left\langle{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)}},{\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)}\right\rangle_{T_{x}M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}(t)},{f_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}(t)}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{IO}}}+\left\langle{e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}(t)},{f_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}(t)}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}

3.2.
Fundamentals: Input–state–output port-Hamiltonian system based on Dirac structure formulation

The

The resistive variables are split in two parts

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R},1}

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R},2}

The

\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{iso}}}\right|_{x}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left.\kern-1.2pt{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{iso}}}\right|_{x}

\displaystyle=\left\{(f,e)\in\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\,\big|\,f=\left.\kern-1.2pt{K}\right|_{x}\,e\right\},

\displaystyle=\vcentcolon\begin{bmatrix}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{J}\right|_{x}&-\left.\kern-1.2pt{G}\right|_{x}&-\left.\kern-1.2pt{T_{1}}\right|_{x}&0\\
\left.\kern-1.2pt{G}\right|_{x}^{\vee}&-\left.\kern-1.2pt{N}\right|_{x}&0&-\left.\kern-1.2pt{T_{2}}\right|_{x}\\

\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R}}}

\displaystyle=-\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\hat{R}}\right|_{x}&\left.\kern-1.2pt{\hat{P}}\right|_{x}\\

for all
x\in M
.

the choice of the dynamics in (

\displaystyle-\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)

\displaystyle=\left.\kern-1.2pt{T_{1}}\right|_{x(t)}^{\vee}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)},

\displaystyle=\left.\kern-1.2pt{G}\right|_{x(t)}^{\vee}\left.\kern-1.2pt{\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial x}}\right|_{x(t)}-\left.\kern-1.2pt{N}\right|_{x(t)}\,u(t)-\left.\kern-1.2pt{T_{2}}\right|_{x(t)}e_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{R},2}}(t),

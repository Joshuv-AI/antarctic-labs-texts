Expression for g ⁡ ( k ) g(k) Related to Waring’s Problem
Owen Root

Abstract
Waring’s Problem asks whether, for each positive integer k k , there exists an integer s s such that every positive integer is a sum of at most k k th powers. While Hilbert proved the existence of such s s , Waring’s Problem has lead to areas of related work, namely the function g ⁡ ( k ) g(k) , which denotes the least such s s . There is no known general closed form for g ⁡ ( k ) g(k) , though for g ⁡ ( k ) g(k) has been evaluated for small k k . Prior work has reduced the problem to verifying a particular condition, which if never occurs, implies an expression for g ⁡ ( k ) g(k) . In this paper, I present a proof the condition never occurs, thus fixing the value of g ⁡ ( k ) g(k) .

2.3
Relationship Between the Bounds

With

and

As established,

Consider now,

\left\lceil\frac{\ln(\alpha)}{b}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{\ln(n+1)}{b}\right\rceil,

where

is some non-integer
>0
, and

is some non-integer such that

. Under what properties of

) hold? Since

\ln(\alpha)<\ln(n+1)
, and so
\ln(\alpha)/b<\ln(n+1)/b

If

As

Putting (

16

\left\lfloor\frac{\ln(n+1)}{b}\right\rfloor<\frac{\ln(\alpha)}{b}<\left\lfloor\frac{\ln(n+1)}{b}\right\rfloor+1.

Hence,
\left\lceil\ln(\alpha)/b\right\rceil=\left\lfloor\ln(n+1)/b\right\rfloor+1.
From (

\left\lceil\ln(n+1)/b\right\rceil=\left\lfloor\ln(n+1)/b\right\rfloor+1

\left\lceil\frac{\ln(\alpha)}{b}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{\ln(n+1)}{b}\right\rceil\neq\left\lfloor\frac{\ln(n+1)}{b}\right\rfloor.

Therefore, the conditions

Consider the case that
\alpha=t_{n}
and
b:=\ln(3/2)
, so
\ln(\alpha)/b=l_{n}

Notice that

Since

We can specifically enumerate the

are exactly the integers whose unit interval

contains a power of

the first few of which are

2.4

Lemma 2.1
.

For

\left\lfloor\frac{\ln(\left\lfloor\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\right\rfloor+1)}{b}\right\rfloor=n.

\log_{3/2}\left(\left\lfloor\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\right\rfloor\right)>\log_{3/2}\left(\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\right)=n.

Now consider the upper bound. If

is not an integer,
\left\lfloor(3/2)^{n}\right\rfloor+1<(3/2)^{n}+1

(23)

Combining the two bounds we have

).
∎

i\geq 1
, we have

\frac{\ln\left(\left\lfloor(3/2)^{i}\right\rfloor+1\right)}{b}\;<\;\frac{\ln\left((3/2)^{i}+1\right)}{b}\;\leq\;\frac{\ln\left((3/2)^{x}+1\right)}{b}.

(37)

Consider the functions

F(x)=\frac{\ln\!\big((3/2)^{x}+1\big)}{b}\qquad\text{and}\qquad G(x)=x+1.

Then

F^{\prime}(x)=\frac{(3/2)^{x}}{(3/2)^{x}+1}\in(0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad G^{\prime}(x)=1.

Hence
H(x):=G(x)-F(x)
satisfies

H^{\prime}(x)=1-\frac{(3/2)^{x}}{(3/2)^{x}+1}=\frac{1}{(3/2)^{x}+1}>0,

(38)

so
H
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
.
F(x)
and
G(x)
have a single equality at
x=-\frac{\ln\!\big((3/2)-1\big)}{b}\approx 1.7095

Therefore
H(x)>0
for all
x\geq 2
, that is,

\frac{\ln((3/2)^{x}+1)}{b}<x+1\qquad(x\geq 2).

(39)

Applying (
39
) with
x\rightarrow i=\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor
and combining with (
37
) yields

\frac{\ln\left(\left\lfloor(3/2)^{i}\right\rfloor+1\right)}{b}<\frac{\ln\left((3/2)^{i}+1\right)}{b}<i+1,

(40)

thus (
36
) holds.

The other endpoint inequality,

g\left(\ell(f(x))+1-\ell\left(f(x)\right)^{1-q}\right)>f(x)\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\frac{\ln\left(\left\lfloor(3/2)^{i}\right\rfloor+1-\left\lfloor(3/2)^{i}\right\rfloor^{1-q}\right)}{b}>i

(41)

is considerably trickier and deserves a dedicated lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
b:=\ln(3/2)
and
q:=\ln 2\,/\,b\in(1,2)
. For
x\geq 2
set
i:=\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor
and
\ell_{i}:=\left\lfloor(3/2)^{i}\right\rfloor
. Then

\frac{\ln\!\big(\ell_{i}+1-\ell_{i}^{\,1-q}\big)}{b}\;>\;i,

(42)

equivalently,

\ell_{i}+1-\ell_{i}^{\,1-q}\;>\;\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)^{\!i}.

(43)

Proof.

Let
m:=\ell_{i}=\left\lfloor(3/2)^{i}\right\rfloor
and
r:=\{(3/2)^{i}\}\in(0,1)
so that
(3/2)^{i}=m+r
. Let
R_{i}:=3^{i}-2^{i}m=2^{i}r\in\{1,\dots,2^{i}-1\}
. Since
\ln
is strictly increasing and
b>0
, (
42
) is equivalent to (
43
).

Introduce the function used earlier in Lemma
2.2
,

h_{m}(t):=t^{q}\,(m+1-t),\qquad t\in(m,m+1).

Then

h_{m}^{\prime}(t)=t^{q-1}\big(q(m+1-t)-t\big).

For
t\in[m,m+1]
we have
q(m+1-t)\leq q<m\leq t
(since
q<2
and
m\geq 2
for
i\geq 2
), hence
h_{m}^{\prime}(t)<0
on
[m,m+1]
and
h_{m}
is strictly decreasing there.

Because
h_{m}
is strictly decreasing, (
43
) is
equivalent
to

h_{m}\!\Big(\Big(\tfrac{3}{2}\Big)^{i}\Big)\;>\;h_{m}\!\big(m+1-m^{\,1-q}\big).

(44)

We now bound the two sides of (
44
) in opposite directions.

Upper bound for
h_{m}(m+1-m^{\,1-q})
.

Let
\delta:=m^{\,1-q}\in(0,1)
. For
q\in[1,2]
and
a,b\geq 0
one has

(a+b)^{q}\leq a^{q}+q\,a^{q-1}b+b^{q}.

(45)

Which can be seen via the following. By homogeneity, set
t=b/a
and define

h_{q}(t):=1+qt+t^{q}-(1+t)^{q}
, so
h_{q}(0)=0
and

h_{q}^{\prime}(t)=q\big(1+t^{q-1}-(1+t)^{q-1}\big)\geq 0
for
q\in[1,2]
, hence
h_{q}(t)\geq 0
for
t\geq 0
. Applying (
45
) with
a=m
and
b=1-\delta
, then multiplying by
\delta
and using
\delta=m^{1-q}
,
m^{q-1}\delta^{2}=\delta
, and
(1-\delta)^{q}\leq 1
, yields

h_{m}(m+1-\delta)=(m+1-\delta)^{q}\,\delta\leq m+q+\delta\big((1-\delta)^{q}-q\big)\;<\;m+q.

(46)

Lower bound for
h_{m}\big((3/2)^{i}\big)
.

Using
(3/2)^{iq}=2^{i}
and
m+1-(3/2)^{i}=1-r
, we have

h_{m}\!\Big(\Big(\tfrac{3}{2}\Big)^{i}\Big)=2^{i}(1-r)=2^{i}-R_{i}.

(47)

We claim that for
i\geq 3
,

m+R_{i}\leq 2^{i}-2.

(48)

Indeed,
m+R_{i}\neq 2^{i}
since
R_{i}\in\{1,\dots,2^{i}-1\}
. If
m+R_{i}=2^{i}-1
then from
3^{i}=2^{i}m+R_{i}
we get

3^{i}=(2^{i}-1)(m+1).

If
i
is even, unique factorization forces
2^{i}-1=3^{s}
, which is false for even
i\geq 4
. If
i
is odd, then
2^{i}-1\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, so
3^{i}\mid(m+1)
and hence
m+1\geq 3^{i}
, contradicting
m+1=\lceil(3/2)^{i}\rceil<3^{i}
. Thus (
48
) holds, and with (
47
) we obtain

h_{m}\!\Big(\Big(\tfrac{3}{2}\Big)^{i}\Big)=2^{i}-R_{i}\;\geq\;m+2\qquad(i\geq 3).

(49)

Comparison for
i\geq 3
.

Since
q<2
, we have
m+q<m+2
. Combining (
46
) and (
49
) yields, for all
i\geq 3
,

h_{m}\!\Big(\Big(\tfrac{3}{2}\Big)^{i}\Big)\;\geq\;m+2\;>\;m+q\;>\;h_{m}\!\big(m+1-m^{\,1-q}\big),

which is exactly (
44
).

Base cases.

For
i=3
,
m=\left\lfloor(3/2)^{3}\right\rfloor=3
and
R_{3}=3
, hence by (
47
)

h_{m}\!\Big(\Big(\tfrac{3}{2}\Big)^{3}\Big)=8-3=5.

On the other hand, (
46
) gives
h_{m}(m+1-m^{1-q})<m+q=3+q<5
since
q<2
. Thus (
44
) holds.

For
i=2
, the inequality (
43
) reads

2+1-2^{\,1-q}>\Big(\tfrac{3}{2}\Big)^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{3}{4}>2^{\,1-q}.

Taking logarithms and noting that
\ln(3/4)<0
gives

\ln\!\Big(\tfrac{3}{4}\Big)>(1-q)\ln 2\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q>1+\frac{\ln(4/3)}{\ln 2}\approx 1.415\ldots

which is satisfied by our fixed
q=\ln 2/\ln(3/2)\approx 1.7095
. Hence (
44
) also holds for
i=2
.

In all cases
i\geq 2
, we have established (
44
). By strict decrease of
h_{m}
on
[m,m+1]
, this is equivalent to (
43
), which is equivalent to (
42
).
∎

Thus given our
f(x),\ell(x)
, and
g(x)
, via Lemma
2.4
, (
41
) also holds.

2.6
End Result

Since both the endpoint inequalities, (
36
) and (
41
), hold for
i\geq 2
, the inequality for the jump indexes, (
35
), then holds. As discussed at the end of Section
2.3
, since the inequality holds for the jump indexes, it also holds for all
n\geq 2
, that is

\frac{\ln(t_{n})}{b}>\left\lfloor\frac{\ln(n+1)}{b}\right\rfloor.

(50)

As discussed at the start of Section
2.3
, this is a sufficient condition for the following to be true,

\left\lceil\frac{\ln(t_{n})}{b}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{\ln(n+1)}{b}\right\rceil\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\left\lceil l_{n}\right\rceil=\left\lceil u_{n}\right\rceil.

(51)

Since
u_{n}
is never an integer for
n\geq 2
,
\left\lceil u_{n}\right\rceil\neq\left\lfloor u_{n}\right\rfloor
. And so we have that
\left\lceil l_{n}\right\rceil\neq\left\lfloor u_{n}\right\rfloor
. Thus, for
n\geq 2
,
l_{n}
and
u_{n}
never fall on opposite sides of an integer value of
x
, and there are no such
s_{n}
segments that have a midpoint at an integer
x
. Finally then, via the work of Section
2.1
, no such
k
exists that can make (
2
) true, and so the expression for
g(k)
is given precisely by (
1
).

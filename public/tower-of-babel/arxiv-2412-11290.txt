Left-Invariant Riemannian Distances on Higher-Rank Sol-Type Groups
Daniel N. Levitin

Abstract
In this paper, we generalize the results of Le Donne, Pallier, and Xie ( Groups, Geom. Dyn. , 19 (2025), 227–263) to describe the split left-invariant Riemannian distances on higher-rank Sol-type groups G = 𝐍 ⋊ ℝ k G=\mathbf{N}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k} . We show that the rough isometry type of such a distance is determined by a specific restriction of the metric to ℝ k \mathbb{R}^{k} , and therefore the space of rough similarity types of distances is parameterized by the symmetric space S ​ L k ​ ( ℝ ) / S ​ O k ​ ( ℝ ) SL_{k}(\mathbb{R})/SO_{k}(\mathbb{R}) . In order to prove this result, we describe a family of uniformly roughly geodesic paths, which arise by way of the new technique of Euclidean curve surgery .

Introduction

Describing the geometry of solvable Lie groups and their lattices, the discrete virtually polycyclic groups, is one of the major areas of work in geometric group theory. Solvable Lie groups admit an algebraic classification due to a theorem of Auslander
[
1
]
, which states that every simply-connected solvable Lie group
G
fits into a short exact sequence

1\to N_{1}\to G\to N_{2}\to 1,

where
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are simply-connected nilpotent Lie groups. See
[
5
]
for a detailed description of what is known and supposed about these groups. Much of the work of describing the geometry of solvable Lie groups has focused on cases where the defining short exact sequence splits, and where the group
N_{2}
is abelian. In this paper, we focus on
higher-rank Sol-type groups

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where the groups
\mathbf{N}_{i}
are simply-connected nilpotent,
k
is at least
2
, and the group
\mathbb{R}^{k}
acts on each space
\mathbf{N}_{i}
by contraction. See Definitions
2.1
and
2.2
for a complete description.

One set of coarse geometric questions concerns the structure of various groups of self-mappings. A map
f:(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
between metric spaces is a
quasi-isometry
if there are positive constants
A
and
B
, and non-negative
C
so that, for all
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
in
X
,

Ad_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})-C\leq d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq Bd_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})+C,

and if, in addition, each
y
in
Y
is within
C
of a point in
f(X)
. If
A=B
, then such a map is a
rough similarity
. If
A=B=1
, then such a map is a
rough isometry
. Finally, if
C=0
, then such a map is a
bi-Lipschitz equivalence
.

If
(X,d_{X})=(Y,d_{Y})
, then the collection of maps of these types, modulo the equivalence
f\sim g
if
\sup_{x\in X}d_{Y}(f(x),g(x))<\infty
, are groups under composition, denoted respectively
QI(X,d_{X})
,
RS(X,d_{X})
,
RI(X,d_{X})
, and
BiLip(X,d_{X})
. One asks, for a given solvable Lie group
X
and left-invariant Riemannian distance
d_{X}
, if any structure is preserved by these groups, and if so, whether it is sufficient to determine the group. For instance, the work of Eskin–Fisher–Whyte, and later of Peng, provides tools to describe specific metrics on nondegenerate Sol-type groups
[
6
,
7
,
11
,
12
]
, and to compute the groups of quasi-isometries.

One may also ask finer geometric questions. For instance, we often wish to know what the efficient paths with respect to a specific left-invariant Riemannian metric may look like, and how they may differ from efficient paths with respect to some other metric. In a similar vein, while any pair of left-invariant Riemannian distances will always be bi-Lipschitz, they need not be roughly similar or roughly isometric. One fruitful direction of research has been to investigate, of a given space and class of metrics, how much information is required to compute the rough similarity or rough isometry class of a metric, and what space these metrics parameterize (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
9
,
10
]
).

For
Sol-type groups
, which are defined like their higher-rank counterparts, but with
k=1
, Le Donne–Pallier–Xie have shown that all left-invariant Riemannian distances are roughly similar, and the rough isometry class of a distance is determined by its induced metric on the
\mathbb{R}
-factor
[
4
]
. In this paper, we achieve an equivalent result for the subset of
perpendicularly split
left-invariant Riemannian metrics. These are the metrics
g
for which the
\mathbb{R}^{k}
factor can be taken to be
g
perpendicular to the
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a higher-rank Sol-type group. Let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be perpendicularly split left-invariant Riemannian metrics, and let their induced metrics on the
\mathbb{R}^{k}
factors be equal (resp. similar). Then the related left-invariant Riemannian distances
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are roughly isometric (resp. roughly similar).

In rank-1, the perpendicular splitting assumption, which is used throughout
[
4
]
, is immediate from the group theory. However, in higher rank, only a positive-codimension set of left-invariant Riemannian metrics are perpendicularly split.

Theorem
1.1
arises as a special case of a stronger result, which is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

[Main Theorem]

Let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a higher-rank Sol-type group. Let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be metrics for which
G
splits perpendicularly, and let their induced metrics on the
\mathbb{R}^{k}
factors differ with top and bottom eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{k}
respectively. Let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
give rise to distances
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
. Then there is a constant
C>0
so that

\lambda_{k}d_{1}-C\leq d_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}d_{1}+C.

The collection of metrics up to rough similarity on
G
is therefore parameterized by the space of metrics up to rough similarity on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We will describe in Definition
2.8
how to geometrize this space. With respect to this metric, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

The space of left-invariant Riemannian distances that split
G
perpendicularly, modulo rough similarity,
\mathscr{D}_{Riem,\perp}
is isometric to
SL_{k}(\mathbb{R})/SO_{k}(\mathbb{R})
.

This generalizes Le Donne-Pallier-Xie’s theorem that all left-invariant Riemannian distances on rank-1 Sol-type groups are all roughly similar, as
SL_{1}(\mathbb{R})/SO_{1}(\mathbb{R})
consists of a single point.

Finally, we obtain a corollary concerning the metric properties of self quasi-isometries of nondegenerate higher-rank Sol-type groups for some groups.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a nondegenerate unimodular abelian-by-abelian higher-rank Sol-type group. Let
d
be any left-invariant distance on
G
. There is a finite-index subgroup of
QI(G,d)
consisting of maps that are rough isometries for every perpendicularly split left-invariant Riemannian distance.

See Theorem
8.3
for the precise statement of this theorem. It seems likely that similar results should hold for any nondegenerate higher-rank Sol-type group. This would follow from Conjecture
8.2
, which asserts that the results of Peng in
[
11
,
12
]
go through in this level of generality.

Outline of the paper

In Section
2
we state the definitions we will use throughout the paper. Section
3
is devoted to generalities about the Lie algebraic properties of all splittings of the quotient
G\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

We then set about proving the main theorem by an argument analogous to the proof in rank-1 due to
[
4
]
. In Section
4
we describe geometric features of left-invariant Riemannian distances that admit perpendicular splittings. In Section
5
, we introduce to each path a set of
half spaces
and a distance approximation denoted
\rho
, which records the shortest length of a path that visits each half-space (see Definition
2.6
for details). Half-space visiting paths are particularly easy to understand and compare to one another. In Proposition
5.3
we compute the length of a
g_{1}
-half-space visiting curve with respect to a different metric,
g_{2}
. This provides the upper bound in Theorem
1.2
, with
d_{1}
replaced with its
\rho
-approximation, and a similar lower bound.

Therefore, to prove Theorem
1.2
it suffices to find a bound, depending on
g
, on the difference between the corresponding distance and
\rho
-function, i.e., to show that the shortest half-space visiting path between two points is never more than a constant length longer than the shortest path of any kind. In
[
4
]
, this is proven by showing that geodesics must come within a bounded distance meeting each half-space. However, this proof does not go through in higher rank.

Instead, we will take the
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-coordinates of a geodesic, and modify it in a controlled way to visit each half space. In Section
6
, we introduce the technique of
Euclidean curve surgery
. We describe how to change a curve in Euclidean space simultaneously in several different ways without lengthening it very much. Then in Section
7
we apply curve surgery to the
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-coordinate of our geodesic. The result is Proposition
7.4
which show that, without increasing the length of the geodesic much, we can make it visit each half-space.

Finally, in Section
8
, we prove Theorem
1.4
as a corollary of the description of efficient paths obtained in Proposition
7.4
.

Preliminaries

Higher-rank Sol-type groups

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be a Lie algebra. A
derivation
on
\mathfrak{n}
is a linear map
D:\mathfrak{n}\to\mathfrak{n}
such that
D([X,Y])=[D(x),Y]+[X,D(Y)]
.

We will mostly be addressing higher-rank Sol-type groups.

Definition 2.2
.

A
Sol-type group
is a group
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
, such that each
\mathbf{N}_{i}
is a simply-connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
,
D_{i}
is a derivation on
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
with all eigenvalues having positive real part, and where the factor
\mathbb{R}^{k}
acts on each
\mathbf{N}_{i}
by
(\vec{v})\mathbf{N}_{i}(-\vec{v})=e^{\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})D_{i}}\mathbf{N}_{i}
where
\alpha_{i}
is a linear map from
\mathbb{R}^{k}
to
\mathbb{R}
. We will denote
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}=\mathbf{N}
, and the quotient by
\mathbf{N}
to be
q:G\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We term the
\alpha_{i}
to
roots
.

The value of
k
is the
rank
of
G
.
G
is of
higher rank
if
k\geq 2
. We will also scale the
D_{i}
and
\alpha_{i}
simultaneously, to assume that the smallest real part of an eigenvalue of
D_{i}
is
1
.

We will study the large scale geometries of higher-rank Sol-type groups with respect to certain left-invairant Riemannian metrics. Specifically, we want those metrics for which there is some lift of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
perpendicular to
\mathbf{N}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a higher-rank Sol-type group, and let
g
be a left-invariant Riemannian metric. The metric
g
is
perpendicularly split
if there is a section
s_{g}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to G
of the quotient
q:G\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
g(s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k}),\mathbf{N})=0
. We will also say that
s_{g}

splits

G

perpendicularly
, or that
s_{g}
is a
perpendicular splitting
of
g
.

In general, there is always a subspace of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
perpendicular to
\mathbf{N}
, but this subspace need not be a Lie subalgebra. A perpendicular splitting exists for a metric precisely when the subspace is a subalgebra. Note also that in rank-1, any linear subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
is automatically a subalgebra, so that every metric has this property.

A perpendicular splitting gives rise to a metric
s_{g}^{*}g
on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We can the compare two metrics arising this way as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose
G=\mathbf{N}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a higher-rank Sol-type group, and
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are perpendicular splittings for
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
. Coordinatize
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with any
s_{1}^{*}g_{1}
-orthonormal basis
\vec{e}_{1},...\vec{e}_{k}
, and let
\vec{v}_{1},...\vec{v}_{k}
be an
s_{2}^{*}g_{2}
-orthonormal set. Let the matrix
A
rewrite vectors into
\vec{v}_{1},...\vec{v}_{k}
-coordinates, i.e.
A_{i,j}=g_{2}(\vec{v}_{j},\vec{e}_{i})
, and let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the dot product. Then if we have two vectors
\vec{w}_{1}
,
\vec{w}_{2}
written in
\vec{e}_{1},...\vec{e}_{k}

\langle\vec{w}_{1},\vec{w}_{2}\rangle=w_{1}^{T}w_{2}=g_{1}(\vec{w}_{1},\vec{w}_{2})
, while
\langle A\vec{w_{1}},A\vec{w}_{2}\rangle=\vec{w}_{1}^{T}A^{T}A\vec{w}_{2}=g_{2}(\vec{w}_{1},\vec{w}_{2}
. In this sense, the matrix
A^{T}A
is the
change of metric
between
s_{1}^{*}g_{1}
and
s_{2}^{*}g_{2}
. It is evidently symmetric, so that all its singular values are in fact eigenvalues.

We will often tacitly use this last fact and refer to the top eigenvalue as the most the change of metric can stretch a vector.

A perpendicular splitting also gives rise to a coordinate system on
G=\mathbf{N}s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
by writing

as
(h,\vec{v})
. For each
\vec{h}_{i}
in
\mathfrak{n}_{i}=T_{e}(\mathbf{N}_{i})
, this coordinate system gives us a non-left-invariant vector field everywhere tangent to cosets of
\mathbf{N}_{i}
, defined by right-translations of
\vec{h}_{i}
by vectors
\vec{v}
and left-translations by elements of
\mathbf{N}
. Equivalently, these are the coordinate pieces of

, where

is one half of the coordinatization above.

Relative to these coordinates, a tangent vector
\vec{h}_{i}
to a coset
\mathbf{N}_{i}\vec{v}
has the same length as

. The positivity of the eigenvalues of

shows that this length decays asymptotically exponentially with respect to
\alpha(\vec{v})
. We will compute this more precisely in Section
.

This allows us to define certain subspaces and paths of interest.

.

(G,g)
be a higher-rank Sol-type group where

The majority of the sequel will focus on comparing the lengths of paths that visit each half-space in turn.

Retain the previous notation. A path

At various times we will need to project vectors onto subspaces. If

is a left-invariant Riemannian metric, and

, then

Usually, this will happen when we wish to reduce a vector, such as the derivative of a curve, into its

Coarse Geometry

Here we describe the equivalence relations we will be interested in for various spaces.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces. A map
f:X\to Y
is a
(K,C)
-
quasi-isometry
if for all
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
in
X
,

\frac{1}{K}d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})-C\leq d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq Kd_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})+C,

and if additionally each point in
Y
is at most distance
C
from a point in
f(X)
.

If additionally, for each
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
in
X
, it holds that

Kd_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})-C\leq d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq Kd_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})+C,

then
q
is a
(K,C)
-
rough similarity
. A rough similarity with
K=1
is a
C
-
rough isometry
. In each case, we omit the parameters if we mean to say that the map is of the desired form for some choice of parameter.

A bijective map
f:X\to Y
satisfying

\frac{1}{K}d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq Kd_{X}(x_{1},x_{2}),

is a
K
-bi-Lipschitz equivalence.

Note that by the general theory, any two left-invariant Riemannian metrics on the same group are automatically bi-Lipschitz through the identity map. Theorem
1.1
gives conditions when we may replace this multiplicative error, at arbitrary scales, with an additive error.

Spaces of left-invariant distances

We define a notion of distance between different distances, following
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
G
be a Lie group and by
\mathscr{D}_{Riem}(G)
denote the set of left-invariant Riemannian distances, modulo rough similarity. For left-invariant Riemannian distances
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
, we define
\Lambda(d_{1},d_{2})=\inf\{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}:\text{ there is a }C>0\text{ such that }\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}d_{1}-C\leq d_{2}\leq\lambda_{2}d_{1}+C\}
. If
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
are equivalence classes of left-invariant Riemannian distances on
G
, we define
\Delta(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})=\inf\{\log(\Lambda(d_{1},d_{2})):d_{i}\in\delta_{i}\}
.

It is an exercise that
\Delta
is a metric on
\mathscr{D}_{Riem}(G)
. If
G=\mathbb{R}^{k}
, then
\mathscr{D}_{Riem}(G)
is isometric to the symmetric space
SL_{k}\mathbb{R}/SO_{k}\mathbb{R}
. The distance between two metrics is just the logarithm of the eccentricity of the unit ball for one relative to the other.

We will be interested in those metrics that admit a perpendicular splitting.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
G
be a higher-rank Sol-type Lie group. Then
\mathscr{D}_{Riem,\perp}(G)
denotes the set of left-invariant Riemannian distances, modulo rough similarity, induced by metrics that split
G
orthogonally.

Lie algebraic properties of splittings

Let
G=\mathbf{N}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a higher-rank Sol-type Lie group. In this section we will describe properties that hold for every splitting of the quotient
q:G\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. These will matter in particular for the perpendicular splittings with respect to a pair of metrics.

For this section,
\overline{A}
denotes an arbitrary lift of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
to
G
. Its tangent space will be spanned by the vectors
\overline{a}_{1},...\overline{a}_{k}
. Together with vectors in the
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
, this will provide a basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on which we will do our calculations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a higher-rank Sol-type lie group, and let
s:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to G
be a splitting of the quotient
q:G\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
\vec{a}
be a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and write
s(\vec{a})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\overline{a}_{j}
. If
\alpha_{i}(\vec{a})=0
, then
\vec{h}_{i}=\vec{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\vec{a_{1}}
be some element of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
for which
\alpha_{i}(\vec{a_{1}})\neq 0
, and let
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j,1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,1}\overline{a}_{j}
be the element corresponding to
s(\vec{a_{1}})
in the
T_{e}(s(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
. Then since the Lie algebra is abelian,

\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j,1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,1}\overline{a}_{j},\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\overline{a}_{j}\Big]=0.

The
\mathbf{n}_{i}
-coordinate of the above evaluates to

\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a_{1}}))(\vec{h}_{i})-\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a}))\vec{h}_{j,1}+[\vec{h}_{i,1},\vec{h}_{i}].

Since
\vec{a}
is in the kernel of
\alpha_{i}
, we cancel to obtain

\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a_{1}}))\vec{h}_{i}+[\vec{h}_{i,1},\vec{h}_{i}]=0.

Now, consider the filtration of
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
by its lower central series. Let
\mathfrak{n}_{i}=\mathfrak{n}_{i,0}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{i,j+1}=[\mathfrak{n}_{i},\mathfrak{n}_{i,j}]
. We say an element is of step
m
if it is in
\mathfrak{n}_{m}
but not
\mathfrak{n}_{m+1}
. If
\vec{h}_{i}
is nonzero and of step
m
, then
[\vec{h}_{i,1},\vec{h}_{i}]
is of step at least
m+1
. Since
\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a_{1}}))\neq 0
by assumption, then we have shown two elements of different step to be equal. It follows that
\vec{h}_{i}
is
0
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a higher-rank Sol-type Lie group, and let
s:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to G
be a splitting of the quotient
q:G\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
s(\vec{a_{j}})=(\vec{h_{j}},\overline{\vec{a_{j}}})
. If
\alpha_{i}(\vec{a_{1}})=\alpha_{i}(\vec{a_{2}})
, then the
\mathbf{N}_{i}
components of
\vec{h_{1}}
and
\vec{h_{2}}
agree.

Proof.

If
\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a_{1}}))=\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a_{2}}))=0
, then this proposition is a direct application of the previous lemma. So suppose that
\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a_{1}}))=\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a_{2}}))\neq 0.

Let
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j,l}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,l}\overline{a_{j}}
denote the lie algebra elements corresponding to
s(\vec{a_{l}})
. We will prove inductively that the elements
\vec{h}_{j,1}
and
\vec{h}_{j,2}
in
\mathfrak{n}_{j}
agree at every step.

Since
s
is a splitting, the Lie algebra
T_{e}(s(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
is abelian. As a result, the
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
-coordinate of the bracket
[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j,1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,1}\overline{a_{j}},\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j,2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,2}\overline{a_{j}}]
must vanish (as must every other coordinate). Let
\mathfrak{n}_{i}=\bigoplus\mathfrak{n}_{i,l}
be a grading of
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
, with respect to which
\vec{h}_{i,k}=\sum_{l}\vec{h}_{i,k,l}
. Then the
\mathfrak{n}_{i,1}
-component of
[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j,1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,1}\overline{a_{j}},\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j,2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,2}\overline{a_{j}}]
is
\alpha_{i}(\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,1}\overline{a_{j}})\vec{h}_{i,2,1}-\alpha_{i}(\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,2}\overline{a_{j}})\vec{h}_{i,1,1}=0
. But by assumption,
\alpha_{i}(\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,1}\overline{a_{j}})=\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a_{1}}))
must equal
\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a_{2}}))=\alpha_{i}(\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,1}\overline{a_{j}})
. As a result, the assumption that
\alpha_{i}(s(\vec{a_{1}}))\neq 0
implies that
\vec{h}_{i,1,1}=\vec{h}_{i,2,1}
.

Suppose now that
\vec{h}_{i,1,l}=\vec{h}_{i,2,l}
for
1\leq l\leq m
. Then by the grading of
\mathfrak{n}
, the
\mathfrak{n}_{i,m+1}
coordinate (and below) of
[\vec{h}_{i,1},\vec{h}_{i,2}]
must vanish. We then calculate that the
\mathfrak{n}_{i,m+1}
coordinate of

\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j,1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,1}\overline{a_{j}},\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{j,2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,2}\overline{a_{j}}\Big]

is

\alpha_{i}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,1}\overline{a_{j}}\Big)\vec{h}_{i,2,m+1}-\alpha_{i}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j,2}\overline{a_{j}}\Big)\vec{h}_{i,1,m+1},

which must vanish. Therefore
\vec{h}_{i,1,m+1}=\vec{h}_{i,2,m+1}
.
∎

4
Geometric properties of splittings

We now introduce a left-invariant Riemannian metric
g
for which
s_{g}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to G
is a perpendicular splitting of the quotient
q:G\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
. The goal of this section is to establish that, for any other splitting

, there is an element of

so that the functions

and
s_{g}
are at finite supremum distance. That is, if

is the distance associated to
g
, then
\sup_{\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}d_{g}(xs_{1}(\vec{v}),s_{g}(\vec{v}))
is finite. The same will then hold for any other left-invariant Riemannian distance, since each one is bi-Lipschitz to
d_{g}
.

We start by observing that perpendicular splittings are geodesic.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(G,g)
split perpendicularly. Then
s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is totally geodesic.

Proof.

We must verify that for every vector
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
\nabla_{s_{g}(\vec{v})}s_{g}(\vec{v})=0
where
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection for
g
. By Koszul’s formula,

\nabla_{s_{g}(\vec{v})}s_{g}(\vec{v})=\frac{1}{2}(\text{ad}_{s_{g}(\vec{v})}s_{g}(\vec{v})-2\text{ad}^{*}_{s_{g}(\vec{v})}s_{g}(\vec{v})),

where
\text{ad}^{*}_{X}Y
is the vector field
Z
such that
g(Z,W)=g(Y,\text{ad}_{X}Z)
. Since
ad
is antisymmetric,
\text{ad}_{s_{g}(\vec{v})}s_{g}(\vec{v})
is
0
. Since
ad
is valued in
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}
, which is perpendicular to
s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
, it follows that
\text{ad}^{*}_{s_{g}(\vec{v})}s_{g}(\vec{v})
is the vector that is
0
when paired with any other vector.
∎

Since
s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is totally geodesic, we can immediately describe the direction of fastest increase of the maps
\alpha_{i}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a higher-rank Sol-type lie group, with a left-invariant Riemannian metric
g
splitting

perpendicularly. The field
\nabla(\alpha_{i}\circ q)
is left-invariant and lies in

.

Proof.

We first prove left-invariance. Let

be a small open neighborhood of the identity, and let
(h,a)\in G
. Then
(h,a)U
is a small open neighborhood of
(h,a)
. Since
\alpha_{i}\circ q
depends only on the
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-coordinate of elements, and left multiplying by

acts as a translation by

on the
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-coordinate of
U
,
\alpha_{i}\circ q\circ L_{(h,a)}=\alpha_{i}\circ q+\alpha_{i}(a)
. Hence
D_{e}L_{h,a}\nabla(\alpha_{1}\circ q)(e)=\nabla(\alpha_{1}\circ q)(h,a)
as required.

Suppose
\vec{v}
is a tangent vector at the origin, and write
\vec{v}
as a sum of vectors
\vec{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}s_{g}(\vec{a}_{i})
. Since
s_{g}
, where each
\vec{h}_{i}
is in
\mathbf{n}_{i}
. Since
s_{g}
is an orthogonal splitting for
g
,

\lVert\vec{v}\rVert=\sqrt{\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\vec{h}_{i}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}s_{g}(\vec{a}_{i})\rVert^{2}}.

If any of the
\vec{h}_{i}
are nonzero, then
\lVert\vec{v}\rVert>\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}s_{g}(\vec{a}_{i})\rVert
. But this is then a shorter vector than

so that
D_{e}\nabla\alpha_{i}\circ q(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}s_{g}(\vec{a}_{i}))
is the same as

, so that
\vec{v}
is not the gradient direction.
∎

Henceforth, when we have a metric for which
G
splits perpendicularly, we will simply refer to this gradient field as

, suppressing both the quotient

and the dependence on the metric.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a higher-rank Sol-type group and
g
a left-invariant metric splitting

perpendicularly. For each

, we define group quotient

as follows. Extend

. We always assign

a metric keeping the lengths of and angles between the vectors
\nabla\alpha_{i}
and

Since the spaces

are not perpendicular, we choose

so that the inclusion of

We next show that splittings (of any type, perpendicular or otherwise), are parametrized by
k
-tuples in
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(G,g)
split perpendicularly, and let

be any splitting of the quotient

. Then for each

,
\pi_{i}(s(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
is a 1-parameter subgroup of
\mathbf{H}_{i}
that does not sit inside
\mathbf{N}_{i}
, and is therefore determined by two limit points, one of which is the point at infinity, and the other is in the copy of
\mathbf{N}_{i}
in the boundary.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
,
\pi_{i}(s(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
is defined by 1 parameter. It is a subgroup because it is a quotient of a group. The remainder follows because a 1-parameter subgroup in
\mathbf{H}_{i}
that does not lie in
\mathbf{N}_{i}
is asymptotic to the point at infinity and one other point.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(G,g)
split perpendicularly, and let
s_{1}
be any other splitting of the quotient
q:G\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
besides
s_{g}
. Then there is a constant
C
and an element
x
in
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}
so that
d_{g}(xs_{1}(\vec{v}),s_{g}(\vec{v}))\leq C
for any vector
\vec{v}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Proof.

Denote by
a_{i}
the magnitude
|\nabla\alpha_{i}|
.

By the previous lemma, let
n_{i,1}
be the element of
\mathbf{N}_{i}
that
\pi_{i}s_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is asymptotic to, and similarly
n_{i}
the element that
\pi_{1}(s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
is asymptotic to. We set

.

\pi_{i}(xs_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
and
\pi_{i}(s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
are quasi-geodesics in
\mathbf{H}_{i}
which have the same endpoints at infinity. Since
\mathbf{H}_{i}
is negatively curved, it follows that there is some
C_{i}
so that the two images have Hausdorff distance no more than
C_{i}
. Moreover, since the derivative of
\pi_{i}(s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
at the identity is perpendicular to the horocyclic copy of
\mathbf{N}_{i}
, it follows that
s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is a minimizing geodesic. Since points at
\nabla\alpha_{i}
-coordinate greater than
C_{i}
apart cannot be within
C_{i}
of one another, it follows that for any vector
\vec{v}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
\pi_{1}(xs_{1}(\vec{v}))
is at distance
C_{1}
from a point on
\pi_{1}(s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
at height in
[a_{i}(\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})-C_{i}),a_{i}(\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})+C_{i})]
, and thus no more than distance
2C_{i}
from
\pi_{1}(s_{g}(\vec{v}))
, which is necessarily at height
\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})
.

So the points
xs_{1}(\vec{v})
and
s_{g}(\vec{v})
are at distance at most
2C_{i}
in the
i^{\text{th}}
projection each projection. Now, since the
\mathbf{N}_{i}
all commute, if
S
is a set of indices not including
i
, then
\pi_{i}\prod_{S}n_{j,1}n_{j}^{-1}s_{1}=\pi_{i}s_{1}
. Therefore,
\pi_{j}\big(\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}n_{i,1}n_{i}^{-1}s_{1}(\vec{v})\big)=\pi_{j}(s_{1}(\vec{v}))
while
\pi_{j}\big(\prod_{i=1}^{j}n_{i,1}n_{i}^{-1}s_{1}(\vec{v})\big))=\pi_{j}(s_{g}(\vec{v}))
. Since these two points lie on a left coset of
\mathbf{H}_{j}
, they are at most
2LC_{j}
apart in the metric

. It follows by the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle d(xs_{1}(\vec{v}),s_{g}(\vec{v}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}d\Big(\big(\prod_{l=1}^{j}n_{l,1}n_{l}^{-1}\big)s_{1}(\vec{v}),\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}n_{l,1}n_{l}^{-1}s_{1}(\vec{v})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}2LC_{j}.

∎

We may immediately apply this result to two different splittings
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
, neither of which is necessarily

by simply summing constants. Moreover, this also applies for any two cosets of
s_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
and

.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(G,g)
split perpendicularly, and let
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
be any two splittings of the quotient. There is a constant

depending on
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
and an element

in

so that if
\vec{v}
is any element of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and

and

are any elements of

, then
d((x_{2}xx_{1}^{-1})x_{1}s_{1}(\vec{v}),x_{2}s_{2}(\vec{v}))\leq C
. ∎

One final topic of geometric interest is the distortion of the nilpotent subgroups
\mathbf{N}_{i}
. We will wish to show that, if a path stays far from the defining half-space

, then a large amount of distance must be spent moving along the
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
-directions.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
G
be a higher-rank Sol-type group, with left-invariant Riemannian metric
g
and let
s
be any (not necessarily perpendicular) splitting. Denote the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{n}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
as before. We will say that
\mathbf{N}_{i}
is
eventually exponentially distorted
if there exist positive constants

and
C
, and

, so that if
\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})\geq T
, then for all
\vec{h}
in
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
,

Ca^{\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})}\lVert\vec{h}\rVert_{g}\leq\lVert R_{s(-\vec{v})}(\vec{h})\rVert_{g},

where
R_{x}
denotes right-multiplication by
x
.

Note that by the left-invariance of
g
and the fact that left and right multiplications commute, if
\mathbf{N}_{i}
is eventually exponentially distorted, we can make the same statement for each of its cosets.

As a warm-up for the general case, we will show that when the derivation
D_{i}
is diagonalizable, then
\mathbf{N}_{i}
is eventually exponentially distorted.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a higher-rank Sol-type group, and let
g
be a left invariant Riemannian metric on
G
with
s
any splitting. Let the derivation
D_{i}
be diagonalizable over the real numbers, with each eigenvalue positive as before, and the smallest normalized to
1
. There are constants
C>0
and
a>1
so that for each
\vec{v}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
h
in
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
,

Ca^{\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})}\lVert\vec{h}\rVert_{g}\leq\lVert R_{s(-\vec{v})}(\vec{h})\rVert_{g}.

That is, when
D_{i}
is diagonalizable,
\mathbf{N}_{i}
is immediately exponentially distorted (eventually exponentially distorted with
t=0
).

Proof.

Let
g_{i}
be a metric arising from

by making the
D_{i}
-eigenbasis of
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
orthogonal. Then the desired inequality holds with

and

for

. Let the change of metric from

to

have top and bottom eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{\dim\mathfrak{n}_{i}}
.

\displaystyle\lVert R_{s(-\vec{v})}(\vec{h})\rVert_{g}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\lVert R_{s(-\vec{v})}(\vec{h})\rVert_{g_{i}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}e^{\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})}\lVert\vec{h}\rVert_{g_{i}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{\dim\mathfrak{n}_{i}}}{\lambda_{1}}e^{\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})}\lVert\vec{h}\rVert_{g}.

The Lemma then holds for

and

∎

So far we have not used the parameter
T
in the definition of eventual exponential distortion. This parameter will be necessary when one of the derivations cannot be diagonalizable. Our analysis of the on
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
in this case follows Peng in
[

,

]
.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be a nilpotent Lie algebra, with

a derivation. Choose some basis for which

is in real Jordan form, and decompose

into a sum

of

We first show that, at the level of Lie groups,

and

give rise to bi-Lipschitz equivalent Heintze groups.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let

be a simply-connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra

, and

a derivation. Let

are

Proof.

Since any pair of left-invariant Riemannian metrics induce bi-Lipschitz equivalent distances, we may as well take

to agree with

on

. Further, let

and

be lifts of

to

and
|G|
perpendicular to

with respect to

and

respectively (these always exist, see
[
4

Lemma 3.1). Then if we normalize the copy of

in

by its

g

acts by

on

under conjugation, where
a
depends only on

to
|A|
so that the positive element of
|A|
of
|g|
-length

The image of

We next wish to understand the metric on the group
|G|
in the case that all the eigenvalues of

have positive real part. As described in
[

]
, in restriction to each Jordan block

e^{t|D|}|_{V}=e^{|\delta|_{V}t}\begin{bmatrix}1&|\nu|_{V}t&\frac{(|\nu|_{V}t)^{2}}{2}&\dots&\frac{(|\nu|_{V}t)^{n}}{n!}\\
0&1&|\nu|_{V}t&\dots&\frac{(|\nu|_{V}t)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\\
&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
&&&1&|\nu|_{V}t\\
&&&&1\end{bmatrix},

where
|\delta|_{V}
and

are the rates of change of
\delta
and
\nu
with respect to length on the Jordan block

. Working block-by-block, we will get a lower bound good enough to show that if

is a higher-rank Sol-type group, and one of its defining pairs

is

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
D
be a derivation on the nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
all of whose eigenvalues have positive real parts, and let
\mathbf{N}
the simply connected nilpotent Lie group corresponding to
\mathfrak{n}
. Let
|G|=\mathbf{N}_{|D|}\rtimes\mathbb{R}
with left-invariant Riemannian metric
g
, and let
\vec{h}
be tangent to
\mathfrak{n}
. Denote by
|\delta|
the minimum
|\delta_{V}|
on any Jordan block
V
. There are positive real numbers
C
and

so that if

, then

Ce^{\frac{|\delta|t}{2}}\lVert\vec{h}\rVert\leq\lVert R_{-t}\vec{h}\rVert,

where
-t
here refers to the lift of
t\in\mathbb{R}
to
|G|
.

Proof.

We will prove that
\lVert R_{t}\vec{h}\rVert\leq C^{\prime}e^{\frac{-|\delta|t}{2}}\lVert\vec{h}\rVert
, for
C^{\prime}
some positive constant. This will show the result for the vector
R_{-t}\vec{h}
. Since right-translation is a permutation, the result will then hold for each vector
\vec{h}
.

Choose a basis
(\vec{v}_{1},...\vec{v}_{m})
for
\mathfrak{n}
for which
|D|
is in Jordan form. Let
L_{1}
be the bi-Lipschitz constant comparing the norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{g|_{\mathbf{N}}}
and
L^{1}(\vec{v})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|v_{i}|
.

The length of
R_{t}\vec{h}
is the same as that of
L_{-t}R_{t}\vec{h}
, which is
e^{-t|D|}(\vec{h})
. That is, right-multiplying by
t
acts on
\vec{h}
by the inverse of the matrix above. Since the inverse of a matrix is a rational function in the entries of the matrix, we determine that each entry in the matrix is
e^{-|\delta|_{V}t}P_{i,j}(|\nu|_{V}t)
, where
P_{i,j}
is a rational function and
V
is the Jordan block containing entry
{i,j}
.

Since there are only finitely many of these, we can take

large enough that for

e^{-|\delta|_{V}t}|P_{i,j}(|\nu|_{V}t)|\leq e^{-\frac{|\delta|t}{2}}.

Thus each entry in the

is of size at most
me^{-\frac{|\delta|t}{2}}L^{1}(\vec{h})
. Hence
L^{1}(R_{t}\vec{h})
is at most
m^{2}e^{-\frac{|\delta|t}{2}}L^{1}(\vec{h})
. We therefore calculate

\displaystyle\lVert R_{t}\vec{h}\rVert_{g}

\displaystyle\leq L_{1}L^{1}(R_{t}\vec{h})

\displaystyle\leq m^{2}L_{1}e^{-\frac{|\delta|t}{2}}L^{1}(\vec{h})

\displaystyle\leq m^{2}L_{1}^{2}e^{-\frac{|\delta|t}{2}}\lVert\vec{h}\rVert_{g},

as desired.
∎

Since the Heinze group
|G|
above is bi-Lipschitz to the Heinze group

defined by the derivation

, we obtain the same result for

with a different constant

.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let

be a derivation on a Lie algebra
\mathbf{n}
with all eigenvalues of positive real part. Let

∎

As a consequence, each nilpotent group

Proposition 4.13

Let

Proof.

If

If

is not diagonalizable, then there is a quotient

from

with a metric

is a Lie group homomorphism, it is

As a consequence, if a vector
\vec{v}
has
\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})>T
, where

is the value in Corollary

, then
q_{i}(R_{s(\vec{v})}\vec{h})
satisfies the conditions of Corollary
4.12
. Therefore, for any

in
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
,

\displaystyle\lVert R_{s(-\vec{v})}\vec{h}\rVert_{g}

\displaystyle\geq L\lVert q_{i}\circ R_{s(-\vec{v})}\vec{h}\rVert_{g^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq L\lVert R_{-\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})}(q_{i})_{*}\vec{h}\rVert

\displaystyle\geq LCe^{\frac{\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})}{2}}\lVert\vec{h}\rVert_{g^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq LCe^{\frac{\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})}{2}}\lVert\vec{h}\rVert_{g},

where in the final step, we used the fact that,
\vec{h}

are tangent to the respective copies of
\mathbf{N}_{i}
, and
g

agree in restriction to the respective copies of

.
∎

Since any given higher-rank Sol-type group has only finitely many nilpotent factors
\mathbf{N}_{i}
, each eventually exponentially distorted with different values of the parameters

,

, and

, we can take these values to be uniform. In addition, in the sequel, we will care about how far a path is from visiting the half spaces

, rather than the difference in

-values per se. We summarize these two rephrasings in the following corollary.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a higher-rank Sol-type group with
g
a left-invariant Riemannian metric and
s_{g}
a perpendicular splitting. Give
G
coordinates with
s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
-factor on the right. There are constants
C>0
,
T>0
, and

so that for all

and all

, the following holds. If

is a half-space for
\alpha_{i}
, and
\vec{h}
is a vector tangent to
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
based at a point a distance

from

, and
\vec{h}^{\prime}
is the vector equal in coordinates based at a point in

Ca^{t}\lVert\vec{h}^{\prime}\rVert\leq\lVert\vec{h}\rVert

Proof.

Denote
\lVert\alpha_{i}\rVert=\lVert\nabla\alpha_{i}\rVert
. Also, denote

,
T_{i}
, and

the values of

,

, and

for
\mathbf{N}_{i}
guaranteed by Proposition

.

Then
\vec{h}
differs from a point on the same left
\mathbf{N}_{i}
-coset as
\vec{h}^{\prime}
by right-multiplication by a vector
\vec{v}\in s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
so that
\alpha_{i}(\vec{v})=\lVert\alpha_{i}\rVert t
. Since lengths are left-invariant, we may as well take

to be

. In order to apply Proposition

to

, we need

, i.e.

, then

Again, since

, and

.
∎

5
Comparing lengths of box geodesics

In this section we define, for each left-invariant Riemannian metric
g
splitting
G
perpendicularly, a distance approximation
\rho_{g}
. We then show, for a pair of left-invariant Riemannian metrics
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, that the distance
d_{2}
for
g_{2}
is bounded above in terms of the approximate metric
\rho_{g_{1}}
, which we will abbreviate
\rho_{1}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a higher-rank Sol-type group, and
g
a left-invariant metric splitting
G
perpendicularly. Let
p
and
q
be any two points in
G
, and let
S(p,q)
be defined as the set

\{\gamma|\gamma\text{ is a path between }p\text{ and }q,\gamma\text{ is half-space visiting box path}\}
. We define
\rho_{g}(p,q)=\inf_{S(p,q)}\ell_{g}(\gamma)
. A path
\eta
achieving the value of
\rho_{g}
will be termed a
box geodesic
, and
\rho_{g}
is called the
box-geodesic distance
associated to
g
. We will sometimes simply refer to
\rho
when there is only one left-invariant Riemannian metric at hand.

It turns out that
\rho_{g}
differs from the distance
d
induced by
g
by a constant
C
depending on
g
. That is, box geodesics are uniformly roughly geodesic, where the implied constant depends on the underlying metric.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G
be a higher-rank Sol-type group, with left-invariant Riemannian metric
g
splitting
G
perpendicularly. There is a
C
such that
|d(p,q),\rho_{g}(p,q)|\leq C
for all
p
and
q
.

The proof of this theorem is rather involved, and we will postpone it for now. In this section we will demonstrate that Theorem
5.2
implies the main theorem.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
G
be a higher-rank Sol-type group, and let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be two left-invariant Riemannian metrics with perpendicular splittings
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
. Let
d_{2}
(resp.
\rho_{1}
) be the geodesic (resp. box-geodesic) distance associated to
g_{2}
(resp.
g_{1}
). Let the top eigenvalue of the change of metric from
s_{1}^{*}g_{1}
to
s_{2}^{*}g_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
be
\lambda_{1}
. Then there is a
C
so that for all
p
,
q
in
G
,

d_{2}(p,q)\leq\lambda_{1}\rho_{1}(p,q)+C.

Proof.

Let
d_{1}
be the distance for
g_{1}
, and let
L
be the constant for which
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are
L
bi-Lipschitz. Denote by
C_{1}
be the constant in Corollary
4.6
applied to the metric
d_{2}
and the splittings
s_{1}
,
s_{2}
. Let
x\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}
so that for
n\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}
,
ns_{1}(\vec{v})
is within
d_{1}
-distance
C_{1}
of
nxs_{2}(\vec{v})
.

Suppose
\eta_{1}
is a box geodesic for
g_{1}
. As before
\eta_{1}
consists of at most
n+1
segments
\eta_{i,1}
in cosets
n_{i}s_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
of
s_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
, and at most
n
segments
\xi_{i}
in cosets of the
\mathbf{N}_{i}
of length at most
1
. For notational convenience, we will parameterize each such path by
[0,1]
, and we will add extra constant paths in if necessary so that
\eta_{1}
is always the concatenation
\eta_{1,1}\xi_{1}\eta_{2,1}\xi_{2}...\eta_{n+1,1}
.

For each of the
\eta_{i,1}
, denote by
\eta_{i,2}
the segment
n_{i}xs_{2}(q(\eta_{i,1}(t)))
. Then we see immediately that

\ell_{2}(\eta_{i,2})\leq\lambda_{1}\ell_{1}(\eta_{i,1}),

because the two are lifts of the same path in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and

d_{2}(\xi_{i,1}(t),\xi_{i,2}(t))\leq C_{1},

since the two points are at
d_{1}
-distance no more than
C_{1}
. Additionally, since
\eta_{i,1}(1)
and
\eta_{i+1,1}(0)
are separated by the segment
\xi_{i}
of
d_{1}
-length at most
1
, it follows that

d_{2}(\eta_{i,1}(1),\eta_{i+1,1}(0))\leq L.

Now,
\eta_{1,1}(0)=p
and
\eta_{n+2,1}(1)=q
. Therefore, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle d_{2}(p,q)

\displaystyle\leq d_{2}(\eta_{1,1}(0),\eta_{1,2}(0))+\ell_{2}(\eta_{1,2})+d_{2}(\eta_{1,2}(1),\eta_{2,2}(0))+...+d_{2}(\eta_{n+1,2}(1),\eta_{n+1,1}(1))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\ell_{2}(\eta_{i,2})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(d_{2}(\eta_{i,2}(1),\eta_{i+1,2}(0))\big)+d_{2}(\eta_{1,1}(0),\eta_{1,2}(0))+d_{2}(\eta_{n+1,2}(1),\eta_{n+1,1}(1))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\lambda_{1}\ell_{1}(\eta_{i,1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{2}(\eta_{i,2}(1),\eta_{i+1,2}(0))+2C_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\rho_{1}(p,q)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{2}(\eta_{i,2}(1),\eta_{i+1,2}(0))+2C_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\rho_{1}(p,q)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[d_{2}(\eta_{i,2}(1),\eta_{i,1}(1))+d_{2}(\eta_{i,1}(1),\eta_{i+1,1}(0))+d_{2}(\eta_{i+1,1}(0),\eta_{i+1,2}(0))\big]+2LC_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\rho_{1}(p,q)+2nC_{1}+nL+2C_{1}.

The Proposition is proven with
C=2(n+1)C_{1}+nL
.
∎

If
\lambda_{k}
is the smallest eigenvalue of the change of metric from
s_{1}^{*}g_{1}
to
s_{2}^{*}g_{2}
, then
\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}
is the largest eigenvalue of the reverse change of metric. As a result, reversing the roles of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, and increasing the value of
C
as necessary, we obtain

d_{1}(p,q)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\rho_{2}(p,q)+C,

so that

\lambda_{k}d_{1}(p,q)-C\leq\rho_{2}(p,q).

As a consequence, Theorem
5.2
will imply Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.

6
Surgery in Euclidean Space

In order to bound the difference between
\rho_{g}
and
d_{g}
, the box geodesic distance and geodesic distance for a metric
g
, we will need to modify a geodesic path
\gamma
to make it half-space visiting, and control the extra length this creates. In this section, we lay out terminology to describe the changes we will be making to the curve
q(\gamma)
. The definitions and lemmas in this section are included as separate concepts not for their mathematical depth, but because they provide an outline for the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Note that we have little control over the shape of
q(\gamma)
based on the fact that
\gamma
is geodesic, so all of this section will be phrased in terms of curves in Euclidean space. Throughout this section, we assume all curves are piecewise smooth.

We will describe two different ways to modify a curve in Euclidean space. The first involves changing the curve’s value on a sub-interval.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\gamma:[a,b]
be any curve in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. A curve
\eta:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a
path surgery of

\gamma
in
surgery location

(c,d)\subset[a,b]
if
\gamma
and
\eta
agree outside of
(c,d)
.

The second type of modification involves attaching a loop to a curve.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a curve. A curve
\eta:[a,b+c]\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a
length-
c

loop surgery of

\gamma

at

t
if
\gamma(s)=\begin{cases}\eta(s)&s\leq t\\
\eta(s+c)&s\geq t\end{cases}
. By a slight abuse of notation, we will also say
t
is the
location
of the loop surgery.

Of course, general path and loop surgeries are of little interest. Any two curves with the same endpoints differ by one loop surgery followed by one path surgery, for instance. We will describe the types of path surgeries we wish to use in Lemma
6.5
. However, before we get there, some preliminary work is in order. We will need to perform one path or loop surgery for each half-space that we want the curve
q(\gamma)
to visit, and we need to make sure that we can perform them all simultaneously, i.e., that after performing a surgery, we do not change the resulting curve again in the same location. We therefore need not just single surgeries, but collections of interchangeable surgeries, so that we can deal with surgery locations intersecting.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\gamma:[a,b]
be a curve in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. A
surgery family

F
is a set
m
path surgeries
\eta_{1},...\eta_{m}
of
\gamma
located on a family of
m
disjoint open sub-intervals of
[a,b]
. We call
\eta_{1},...\eta_{m}
the
options
of the family
F
. If
\eta_{i}
is located on the open interval
I_{i}
, then
[a,b]\setminus\bigsqcup I_{i}
consists of a component containing
a
, a component containing
b
, and
m-1
either closed intervals or single points interior to
[a,b]
. The
m-1
interior components will be called the
interstices
of the family
F
.

Again, there is no requirement that the
\eta_{i}
have anything to do with one another as
i
varies. In our case, the curves
\eta_{i}
will all be chosen to visit a certain half-space and not lengthen
\gamma
by too much, so that we think of them as interchangeable ways to modify our curve and attain an equally-good result.

Examining the interstices lets us bound how badly two path surgery families can overlap with one another, and use that bound to show that, given a collection of loop surgeries and path surgery families, we will be able to find a single curve incorporating each loop surgery and an option of each path surgery family as long as each family has sufficiently many options,.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a curve. Suppose we have
m_{1}
loop surgeries
\eta_{1},...\eta_{m_{1}}
, such that
\eta_{i}
is located at
t_{i}
and has length
c_{i}
, and
m_{2}
path surgery families
F_{1},...F_{m_{2}}
, such that
m_{1}+m_{2}\leq n
and each path surgery family has
n^{2}
options. Denote the options of
F_{i}
as
\eta_{i,j}
located on intervals
I_{i,j}
. Then we can find a collection of options
\eta_{1,{j_{1}}},...\eta_{m_{2},j_{m_{2}}}
located on disjoint intervals, none of them containing the location of a path surgery. Moreover, there will be at least
2(n-i)
options for the
i^{th}
path surgery family we choose.

There will then be a single (piecewise smooth) curve
\eta
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
consisting of
\gamma
minus all the chosen path surgery locations, together with the images of
\eta_{i}|_{[t_{i},t_{i}+c_{i}]}
and
\eta_{i,j_{i}}|_{I_{i},j_{i}}
.

The choice of the letter
n
in the statement of this proposition is to match the number of nilpotent factors in the group
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
, since
n
is the number of half-spaces there are to visit.

Proof.

We prove the first paragraph by induction on
n
. Families
F_{2}
, …
F_{m_{2}}
each have
n^{2}-1=(n+1)(n-1)
interstices. If the location
I
of an option of
F_{1}
meets at least
2n-1
options of
F_{j}
(for
j\neq i
), then
I
must cover
2n-2
interstices of
F_{j}
. There are therefore at most
\lfloor\frac{n^{2}-1}{2n-2}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor
options of
F_{1}
that meet
2n-1
or more options of each
F_{j}
. There are at most
n-1
total loop surgeries and path surgery families other than
F_{1}
, and each loop surgery meets at most
1
option for
F_{1}
. Therefore, there are at least
n^{2}-(n-1)\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor
options of
F_{1}
that miss every loop surgery location and touch no more than
2n-1
options of any other family. We calculate that

\displaystyle n^{2}-(n-1)\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor-(2n-2)

\displaystyle\geq n^{2}-\frac{n^{2}-1}{2}-(2n-2)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{2}}{2}-2n+2

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(n-2)^{2}\geq 0.

So there are indeed at least
2n-2
options for the first choice, each of them meeting at most
2n-1
options of the remaining families. Since
n^{2}-(2n-1)=(n-1)^{2}
the result follows by induction. One simply throws out any excess options of the families
F_{2}
, …
F_{m_{2}}
until only
(n-1)^{2}
options remain, and then repeats the argument.

Once we have found this collection, there are no conflicts between path and loop surgeries, or between any pair of path surgeries. Therefore, we need to show that there are no conflicts between loop surgeries.

Suppose that some of the loop surgeries have the same location, and permute them so that WLOG
t_{1}=t_{2}
. Since
\eta_{1}(t+c_{1})=\gamma(s)
, and we can simply perform surgery
\eta_{2}
at the point
t+c_{1}
in the curve
\eta_{1}
instead, obtaining a curve

\eta(s)=\begin{cases}\gamma(s)&s\leq t\\
\eta_{1}(s)&t\leq s\leq t+c_{1}\\
\eta_{2}(s-c_{1})&t+c_{1}\leq s\leq t+c_{1}+c_{2}\\
\gamma(s-c_{1}-c_{2})&s\geq t+c_{1}+c_{2}\end{cases}
. By the same token, we can apply any number of loop surgeries with the same location.
∎

When we perform a collection of path and loop surgeries as described above, we will say that the resulting curve
\eta

agrees (on the complement of the path surgery locations) with

\gamma

up to a parameter shift
. We will often encapsulate the parenthesized text into notation instead of writing it out.

We now come to a description of the path surgeries we will perform on the curve
q(\gamma)
. The idea is that if a curve
\gamma(t)
in Euclidean space traverses a very long distance perpendicular to the direction
\vec{v}
while not changing its
\vec{v}
-coordinate much, then we can replace
\vec{v}(\gamma(t))
with a different function
f(t)
in such a way that the resulting curve is not much longer than
\gamma
, and that
f(t)
attains a larger value than
\vec{v}(\gamma(t))
did.

We use the notation
\mathbb{E}^{k}
here to emphasize that we now care about metric properties of the Euclidean space, not just the topological property of a curve being piecewise smooth. We will also fix the notation that if
V
is a vector subspace of
\mathbb{E}^{k}
, then
\vec{v}|_{V}
will denote the orthogonal projection of a vector
\vec{v}
onto
V
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\gamma
be any path in
\mathbb{E}^{k}
, and
\vec{v}
any
k
-dimensional unit vector. Suppose that there is an interval
[a,b]
along which
\int_{a}^{b}\lVert\vec{\gamma^{\prime}(t)}|_{\vec{v}^{\perp}}\rVert\,dt\geq n^{2}L
, and on which
\vec{v}(\gamma(t))\geq d
. Then there is a path surgery family with
n^{2}
options
\eta_{i}
, such that each
\eta_{i}
is no more than
1
longer than
\gamma
, and such that each
\eta_{i}
visits a point whose
\vec{v}
-coordinate is at least
d+\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}
.

Figure 1:
Schematic of the path surgery. On each of
n^{2}
sub-intervals, we will preserve the
\vec{v}^{\perp}
-component of the path, and replace the
\vec{v}
component of the path with a piecewise-linear function of the
\vec{v}^{\perp}
arc length. If the curve is tangent to the
\vec{v}
-direction on some interval, projection down to
\vec{v}^{\perp}
gets rid of this interval.

Proof.

For
i
between
0
and
n^{2}
, let times
t_{i}
in
[a,b]
be such that
\int_{a}^{t_{i}}\lVert\vec{\gamma^{\prime}(t)}|_{\vec{v}^{\perp}}\rVert\,dt=iL
. If
\gamma^{\prime}(t)
is ever parallel to
\vec{v}
, the
t_{i}
may not be uniquely determined, so choose the smallest such time for each. We will construct one surgery for each location
(t_{i},t_{i+1})
. Notice that if the
\vec{v}
-coordinate of
\gamma(t)
on any such interval is ever at least
d+\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}
, then there is nothing to prove about this interval.

So on the interval
[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
, we may assume
\vec{v}(\gamma(t))
is in
[d,d+\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}]
. Denote
\gamma_{i,\vec{v}^{\perp}}:[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\to\mathbb{E}^{k-1}
to be
\gamma|_{\vec{v}^{\perp}}
restricted to the interval
[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
, and re-parameterize
\gamma_{i,\vec{v}^{\perp}}
so that its arc length is linear on the interval. Let
f_{i}(t)
be the piecewise function that interpolates linearly between
\vec{v}(\gamma(t_{i}))
and
d+\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}
on the first half of the interval, and then interpolates linearly between
d+\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}
and
\vec{v}(\gamma(t_{i+1}))
on the second half of the interval. That is,

f_{i}(t)=\begin{cases}\vec{v}(\gamma(t_{i}))\Big(1-\frac{t-t_{i}}{\frac{t_{i+1}-t_{i}}{2}}\Big)+\Big(d+\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}\Big)\Big(\frac{t-t_{i}}{\frac{t_{i+1}-t_{i}}{2}}\Big)&t\leq\frac{t_{i+1}+t_{i}}{2}\\
\Big(d+\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}\Big)\Big(2-\frac{t-t_{i}}{\frac{t_{i+1}-t_{i}}{2}}\Big)+\vec{v}(\gamma(t_{i+1}))\Big(\frac{t-t_{i}}{\frac{t_{i+1}-t_{i}}{2}}-1\Big)&t\geq\frac{t_{i+1}+t_{i}}{2}\end{cases}.

Take
\eta_{i}(t)
to agree with
\gamma(t)
outside of
(t_{i},t_{i+1})
, and to be
\gamma_{i,\vec{v}^{\perp}}(t)+f_{i}(t)\vec{v}
on
(t_{i},t_{i+1})
, where we again identify the target of
\gamma_{i,\vec{v}^{\perp}}(t)
, a copy of
\mathbb{E}^{k-1}
, with the subspace
\vec{v}^{\perp}
. We must calculate the length of
\eta_{i}(t)
. Since
\eta_{i}(t)
agrees with
\gamma
outside of
(t_{i},t_{i+1})
, we only need to compare the lengths on this interval.

On each half of the interval
[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
,
\eta_{i}(t)
traverses distance in the
\vec{v}^{\perp}
coordinates and in the
\vec{v}
coordinate at linear speed. The total change in the
\vec{v}
coordinate over each half is at most
\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\ell(\eta_{i}(t)|_{[t_{i},\frac{t_{i}+t_{i+1}}{2}]}),\ell(\eta_{i}(t)|_{[\frac{t_{i}+t_{i+1}}{2},t_{i+1}]})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\Big(\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{2}+\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Big(\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{2}+\frac{L}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Big(\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{2}+2\Big(\frac{1}{2}\frac{L}{2}\Big)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\Big(\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{2}+2\Big(\frac{1}{2}\frac{L}{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Big(\frac{L}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{2}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\ell(\gamma_{i,\vec{v}^{\perp}}|_{[t_{i},\frac{t_{i}+t_{i+1}}{2}]})+\frac{1}{2},\ell(\gamma_{i,\vec{v}^{\perp}}|_{[\frac{t_{i}+t_{i+1}}{2},t_{i+1}]})+\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore,
\ell(\eta_{i}|_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]})\leq\ell(\gamma_{i,\vec{v}^{\perp}})+1
. Since
\ell(\gamma|_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]})\leq\ell(\gamma_{i,\vec{v}^{\perp}})

. ∎

7
Existence of half-space visiting coarse geodesics.

Throughout this section, we retain the notation that if

denotes the orthogonal projection onto

will be
\mathfrak{g}
, and the inner product will be a left-invariant Riemannian metric

In this section we will, by a slight abuse of notation, perform curve surgeries on curves
\gamma
in

. Moreover, we will always transform
\gamma
into a half-space visiting curve, and take the box path

in

Of course, we can always perform a set of loop surgeries to render

considerably. We will therefore use the following criterion to find a suitable way to replace a loop surgery with a path surgery family.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic between
p
and
q
in
G
. Denote the heights of the half spaces
H_{i}
, and
|\nabla\alpha_{i}|=a_{i}
. Let

of

\nabla\alpha_{i}
-height

Proof.

Suppose that, above
\nabla\alpha_{i}
-height

of

\int_{\mathscr{C}_{2}}\lVert\frac{d(q(\xi(t)))}{dt}|_{\nabla\alpha_{i}^{\perp}}\rVert\,dt\geq 8n^{2}r^{2}=2n^{2}(2r)^{2}
. Then we can apply Corollary
6.6

to obtain the desired surgery family.

Since

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{C}}\sqrt{\lVert\frac{d\gamma(t)}{dt}|_{s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\frac{d\gamma(t)}{dt}|_{\mathbf{N}}\rVert^{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\Big(\int_{\mathscr{C}}\lVert\frac{d\gamma(t)}{dt}|_{s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\rVert\,dt\Big)^{2}+\Big(\int_{\mathscr{C}}\lVert\frac{d\gamma(t)}{dt}|_{\mathbf{N}}\rVert\,dt\Big)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\ell\circ q(\mathscr{C})^{2}+R^{2}}.

By the assumption that

\mathscr{L}_{i+1,H_{i+1}}((\gamma_{H_{i+1}-(j_{i+1}+1)r_{i}}^{H_{i+1}-(j_{i+1})r_{i}})_{i+1}\cap I_{1})\leq\frac{\mathscr{L}_{i+1,H_{i+1}}((\gamma_{H_{i+1}-(j_{i+1}+1)r_{i}}^{H_{i+1}-(j_{i+1})r_{i}})_{i+1}}{2n-2},

8
Intrinsic rough isometries

In this Section, we will prove Theorem
1.4
.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
G
be a Lie group, and
f:G\to G
be a quasi-isometry for some (and therefore all) left-invariant Riemannian metrics on
G
. Then
f
is an
intrinsic rough isometry
if
f
is a rough isometry with respect to every left-invariant Riemannian metric. Note that the implied constant depends on the choice of metric.

Because our theorem has the requirement that the metrics considered must split
G
perpendicularly, we are not able to conclude that quasi-isometries are intrinsic rough isometires. We must also work modulo a conjecture about the structure of quasi-isometries of our groups.

Conjecture 8.2
.

Let
G\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be nondegenerate with
n\geq 2
. Let
q:G\to G
be a quasi-isometry for some (hence any) left-invariant Riemannian metric, and let
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{k}}
be any lift of the quotient map
G\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Give the
G
semi-direct normal coordinates with the factor
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{k}}
on the right. Then
q
is at finite distance from a map
L_{x}\circ(\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i})\circ\sigma
, where
L_{x}
is a left-translation by a group element, each
f_{i}
is a map from
\mathbf{N}_{i}
to itself, and
\sigma
is an element of a finite group of symmetries which simultaneously permute the
\mathbf{N}_{i}
, act on
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{k}}
by a linear isomorphism which permutes the associated roots, such that if
\sigma^{*}\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{j}
, then
\sigma(\mathbf{N}_{i})=\mathbf{N}_{j}
and
(\mathbf{N}_{i},D_{i})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbf{N}_{j},D_{j})
.

This conjecture is expected to hold. Peng has proved the result under the additional assumptions that the
\mathbf{N}_{i}
are abelian, and that the group
G
is unimodular
[
11
,
12
]
. In rank-1, the result is known due to Eskin–Fisher–Whyte without the unimodularity assumption, and in fact this case is easier than the unimodular case
[
6
,
7
]
. So the conjecture is that one can combine Eskin–Fisher–Whyte’s treatment of the non-unimodular case with Peng’s work to remove the unimodular assumption, and that the work can be extended to nilpotent groups as well. See also Ferragut’s proof of a similar result in the setting of horocyclic products
[
8
]
.

The element
\sigma
is denoted to lie in
Sym(G)
, the group of all possible permutations of roots and root spaces as above. The group
Sym(G)
is typically trivial. For instance, any time the
(\mathbf{N}_{i},D_{i})
are in pairwise distinct isomorphism classes, there is no hope for such an isomorphism.

We cannot show that every quasi-isometry is an intrinsic rough isometry at present. However, we can show that those with
\sigma
trivial (whether or not
Sym(G)
is trivial on the whole) are rough isometries for metrics that split
G
perpendicularly.

Theorem 8.3
.

Assume Conjecture
8.2
holds. Let
G\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{N}_{i}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{k}
be nondegenerate,
q:G\to G
be a quasi-isometry of
(G,d_{g})
, where
g
is a left-invariant Riemannian metric that splits
G
perpendicularly. Decompose
q
, up to finite distance, into
L_{x}\circ(\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i})\circ\sigma
, with respect to the lift
s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
, and suppose
\sigma
is trivial. Then
q
is a rough isometry for
(G,d_{g})
.

Of course, in the case where
G
is abelian-by-abelian and unimodular, the Conjecture need not be assumed because it is already a theorem. So this result subsumes Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.4
, there is a
C_{1}
depending on
g
so that any two points
x
and
y
can be connected by a
C_{1}
-rough box geodesic. Let
C_{2}
be the distance between
q
and the map
L_{x}\circ(\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i})
. Denote
q^{\prime}=(\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i})
, and suppose
q^{\prime}
is a
(K,C_{3})
quasi-isometry.

q^{\prime}
sends box paths to box paths, and does not change the length of any segment in a coset of
s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
. Between any two points
x
and
y
there is a
C_{1}
-rough box geodesic
\gamma
so that
\gamma
is composed of at most
n+1
(maximal) segments
\gamma_{2i}
tangent to cosets of
s_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
, together with at most
n
additional subsegments
\gamma_{2i+1}
, each of length at most
1
, where
i
ranges from
0
to
n
. After applying
q^{\prime}
, the lengths of the
\gamma_{2i}
do not change, and the endpoints of the
\gamma_{2i+1}
may be at most
K+C_{3}
apart. So the path between
q^{\prime}(x)
and
q^{\prime}(y)
that agrees with
q^{\prime}(\gamma_{2i})
and replaces
q^{\prime}(\gamma_{2i+1})
with a geodesic between its endpoints has length at most
\ell(\gamma)+n(K+C_{3})
. Therefore,

d_{g}(q^{\prime}(x),q^{\prime}(y))\leq d(x,y)+C_{1}+n(K+C_{3}).

Since
q
is distance
C_{2}
from a composition of
q^{\prime}
with an isometry, it follows that

d_{g}(q(x),q(y))\leq d(x,y)+C_{1}+2C_{2}+n(K+C_{3}).

∎

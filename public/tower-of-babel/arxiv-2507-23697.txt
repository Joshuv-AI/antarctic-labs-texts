Approximation of time-periodic flow past a translating body by flows in bounded domains
Thomas Eiter ,, Ana Leonor Silvestre

Abstract
We consider a time-periodic incompressible three-dimensional Navier-Stokes flow past a translating rigid body. In the first part of the paper, we establish the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions in the exterior domain Ω ⊂ ℝ 3 \Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3} that satisfy pointwise estimates for both the velocity and pressure. The fundamental solution of the time-periodic Oseen equations plays a central role in obtaining these estimates. The second part focuses on approximating this exterior flow within truncated domains Ω ∩ B R \Omega\cap B_{R} , incorporating appropriate artificial boundary conditions on ∂ B R \partial B_{R} . For these bounded domain problems, we prove the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions. Finally, we estimate the error in the velocity component as a function of the truncation radius R R , showing that, as R → ∞ R\to\infty , the velocities of the truncated problems converge, in an appropriate norm, to the velocity of the exterior flow.

Introduction

Consider an incompressible viscous flow around a rigid body translating with a constant velocity
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus{\{0\}}
. For simplicity and without loss of generality, we take the kinematic viscosity of the fluid to be equal to 1. To describe the motion of the fluid, we use a reference frame attached to the solid. Additionally, we assume the fluid to be subject to an external body force and a
distribution of velocities along the fluid-solid boundary, both time-periodic of period
\mathcal{T}>0
. Under these conditions, the motion of the fluid is governed by the following equations

\left\{\ \begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\Delta u-\zeta\cdot\nabla u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\mathcal{p}&=f&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot u&=0&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\Omega,\\
u&=h&&\text{on }\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma,\\
\lim_{{\lvert x\rvert}\to\infty}u(t,x)&=0&&\text{for }t\in\mathbb{T}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here and throughout the paper,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
denotes the exterior domain occupied by the liquid, while
\Sigma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
represents the common boundary between
\Omega
and the compact set corresponding to the rigid body. We assume that
0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. Since we are interested in time-periodic flows, the torus group
\mathbb{T}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}/\mathcal{T}\mathbb{Z}
serves as the time axis in system (
1.1
), so that all functions therein
are time-periodic with period
\mathcal{T}>0
. The functions

u\colon\mathbb{T}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\mathcal{p}\colon\mathbb{T}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}

represent the unknown velocity field and scalar pressure, respectively.

In the context of applications, a crucial question is how to numerically solve the exterior problem (
1.1
). Truncating the fluid domain in order to discretize the equations using, for instance, finite elements necessarily introduces artificial boundaries, which must be chosen so as to ensure the well-posedness of the mathematical model and the numerical stability of the simulations. Prescribing the so-called “do-nothing” condition
[
13
,
18
]
on the artificial boundaries arises naturally in the variational formulation after multiplication of the term
-\Delta u+\nabla\mathcal{p}
with a test function and integration by parts. However, as shown in
[
1
,
15
]
, this Neumann condition does not guarantee the well-posedness of the resulting boundary value problem for the Navier-Stokes equations. In
[
2
]
, the question of how to numerically solve the Dirichlet problem for the Stokes system in the exterior of a three-dimensional bounded Lipschitz domain is addressed using a modified “do-nothing” condition on the outer boundary of a truncated domain. A similar idea was subsequently exploited for more complex fluid models in
[
4
,
3
]
, and we adopt it in this work, as described below.

Formulation of the problem
. Our aim is to investigate how to approximate solutions
(u,\mathcal{p})
to system (
1.1
), formulated in the unbounded domain
\Omega

by solutions
(u_{R},\mathcal{p}_{R})
to problems posed in bounded domains

More precisely, we consider solutions

to the truncated problems

\left\{\ \begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v-\Delta v-\zeta\cdot\nabla v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla{p}&=f&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\Omega_{R},\\
\nabla\cdot v&=0&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\Omega_{R},\\

\bop(v,{p})&=0&&\text{on }\mathbb{T}\times\partial B_{R},\end{aligned}\right.

where

is a suitable boundary operator. The artificial boundary condition

must be selected to ensure both the well-posedness of the resulting mixed boundary value problem and the convergence of

to

\bop(v,{p})(t,x)=\frac{x}{R}\cdot\bigg(\nabla v(t,x)-{p}(t,x){\mathsf{I}}-\frac{1}{2}v(t,x)\otimes v(t,x)\bigg)+\frac{1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)}{R}v(t,x)

(1.3)

, is inspired by
[
3
]
.
The present work is a generalization to the time-periodic case of the results obtained in
[
3
]
for the steady problem (see also
[
4
]
for a linearized steady flow around a rotating and translating body). Note that the operator
\bop
defined in (

) contains the pseudo-stress tensor
\widetilde{\mathsf{T}}(v,{p})=\nabla v-{p}\mathsf{I}
.
However, all results in this paper remain valid if

is replaced by the classical Cauchy stress tensor
\mathsf{T}(v,{p})=\nabla v+\nabla v^{\top}-{p}\mathsf{I}

(\nabla v)_{ij}=\frac{\partial v_{j}}{\partial x_{i}},

Fundamental solutions

\left\{\ \begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\Delta u-\zeta\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\mathcal{p}&=f&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\Omega,\\

Remark 3.2
.

Condition (
3.3
) means that
the total boundary flux

\Phi(t)\coloneqq\int_{\Sigma}h(t,x)\cdot n\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)

(3.5)

is constant in time,
that is,

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\Phi\equiv 0

If this is satisfied,
then the decay rate of the pressure is
{\lvert x\rvert}^{-2}
,
while for non-constant total flux,
the pressure only decays like
{\lvert x\rvert}^{-1}

as
{\lvert x\rvert}\to\infty

Similarly, the decay of the purely periodic part of the velocity field is faster in this case.
This observation is in accordance with
[
8
]
,
where the decay rates for time-periodic weak solutions to (

A similar existence result was obtained
in
[
7
]
,
but with a different spatial decay rate of the solutions.
Since the decay assumptions on external forces considered in
[
7

the decay rates of the derived solutions are slower as well.
In contrast, for solutions
established in Theorem
3.1

namely the anisotropic decay determined by the steady-state part.
Moreover, the purely periodic velocity field

and the decay rate is improved if (

that is, for constant total boundary flux.
For

,
and can thus be considered the optimal decay rate.

Firstly, we will study a linearized version of problem (

3.1
Linear theory

To prove Theorem
3.1
,
we first study the associated linear problem (
2.1
). For pointwise decay estimates of the velocity field
u=u_{0}+u_{\perp}

split into steady-state and purely periodic parts,
we extend the velocity and pressure to zero outside the domain
\Omega
and employ the representation formulas
(see
[
19

8
]
)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{0}&=\Gamma^{\zeta}_{0}\ast_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[f_{0}\chi_{\Omega}+n\cdot\widetilde{\mathsf{T}}(u_{0},\mathcal{p}_{0})\delta_{\Sigma}+(\zeta\cdot n)h_{0}\delta_{\Sigma}]\\
&\qquad+\Gamma^{\zeta}_{0}\ast_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot[(n\otimes h_{0})\delta_{\Sigma}]-P\ast_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[n\cdot h_{0}\delta_{\Sigma}],\end{aligned}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{\perp}&=\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}\ast_{G}[f_{\perp}\chi_{\Omega}+n\cdot\widetilde{\mathsf{T}}(u_{\perp},\mathcal{p}_{\perp})\delta_{\Sigma}+(\zeta\cdot n)h_{\perp}\delta_{\Sigma}]\\
&\qquad+\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}\ast_{G}\nabla\cdot[(n\otimes h_{\perp})\delta_{\Sigma}]-Q\ast_{G}[n\cdot h_{\perp}\delta_{\Sigma}],\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{p}_{0}&=c_{0}+P\ast_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[f_{0}\chi_{\Omega}+n\cdot\widetilde{\mathsf{T}}(u_{0},\mathcal{p}_{0})\delta_{\Sigma}+(\zeta\cdot n)h_{0}\delta_{\Sigma}]\\
&\qquad+P\ast_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot[(n\otimes h_{0})\delta_{\Sigma}]\\
&\qquad+P\ast_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[\zeta(h_{0}\cdot n)\delta_{\Sigma}],\end{aligned}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{p}_{\perp}&=c_{\perp}+Q\ast_{G}[f_{\perp}\chi_{\Omega}+n\cdot\widetilde{\mathsf{T}}(u_{\perp},\mathcal{p}_{\perp})\delta_{\Sigma}+(\zeta\cdot n)h_{\perp})\delta_{\Sigma}]\\
&\qquad+Q\ast_{G}\nabla\cdot[(n\otimes h_{\perp})\delta_{\Sigma}]\\
&\qquad+Q\ast_{G}[\zeta(h_{\perp}\cdot n)\delta_{\Sigma}]+(\delta_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes E)\ast_{G}[(\partial_{t}h\cdot n)\delta_{\Sigma}]\end{aligned}

(3.9)

where
c(t)=c_{0}+c_{\perp}(t)

We next prepare several estimates of the convolutions appearing in (

)–(
3.9

.

Firstly, we consider the terms with contributions at the boundary.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S>0
such that

\Sigma\subset B_{S}
.
Let
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
0<{\lvert\zeta\rvert}\leq\zeta_{0}

for some
\zeta_{0}>0
.
Then there is
C=C(\Sigma,S,\zeta_{0},\mathcal{T})>0
such that
for all

with

\psi_{1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma)
,

{\lvert x\rvert}\geq S

\displaystyle\nu^{1}_{1}(x;\zeta)\,{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\Gamma^{\zeta}_{0}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{T}}\rvert}\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}+\nu^{3/2}_{3/2}(x;\zeta)\,{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\nabla\Gamma^{\zeta}_{0}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{T}}\rvert}\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}

\displaystyle+\nu^{2}_{2}(x;\zeta)\,{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\nabla^{2}\Gamma^{\zeta}_{0}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{T}}\rvert}\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}

\displaystyle+|x|\,{\bigl\lvert(E\otimes\delta_{\mathbb{T}})\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}+|x|^{2}\,{\bigl\lvert{\lvert Q\rvert}\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}+|x|^{3}\,{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\nabla Q\rvert}\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert\psi_{1}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times\Omega_{R})}.

{\lvert x\rvert}^{2}{\bigl\lvert(E\otimes\delta_{\mathbb{T}})\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}+|x|^{3}\,{\bigl\lvert{\lvert Q\rvert}\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}+|x|^{4}\,{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\nabla Q\rvert}\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}\leq C\lVert\psi_{1}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma)}.

Proof.

Let
R\in(0,S)
such that
\Sigma\subset B_{R}
.
For
{\lvert x\rvert}\geq S>R\geq{\lvert y\rvert}
we have

\displaystyle{\lvert x-y\rvert}

\displaystyle\geq{\lvert x\rvert}-{\lvert y\rvert}\geq(1-R/S){\lvert x\rvert}\geq S-R,

\displaystyle(1+2\zeta_{0}R)(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x-y))

\displaystyle\geq 1+2{\lvert\zeta\rvert}{\lvert y\rvert}+{s}_{\zeta}(x-y)\geq 1+{s}_{\zeta}(x).

This yields

\nu^{\alpha}_{\beta}(x;\zeta)\leq C\,\nu^{\alpha}_{\beta}(x-y;\zeta)

for
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
and a constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta,R,S,\zeta_{0})>0
.
Therefore,
for any function
\Theta
with
{\lvert\Theta(t,z)\rvert}\leq C\nu^{-\alpha}_{-\beta}(z;\zeta)
for
{\lvert z\rvert}\geq S-R
,
we obtain

\displaystyle{\bigl\lvert\Theta\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Sigma}\nu^{-\alpha}_{-\beta}(x-y;\zeta)\,{\lvert\psi_{1}(s,y)\rvert}\,{\mathrm{d}}S(y){\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C\nu^{-\alpha}_{-\beta}(x;\zeta)\lVert\psi_{1}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma)}.

In this proof and the ones that follow,

represents a generic positive constant that may take different values in different steps of the argument. Moreover, if
{\lvert\nabla\Theta(t,z)\rvert}\leq C\nu^{-\alpha}_{-\beta}(z;\zeta)

and
\int_{\Sigma}\psi(t,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(y)=0
,
then we obtain

\displaystyle{\bigl\lvert\Theta\ast\psi(t,x)\big\rvert}

\displaystyle={\Bigl\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Sigma}\big(\Theta(t-s,x-y)-\Theta(t-s,x)\big)\psi_{1}(s,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(y){\mathrm{d}}s\Big\rvert}

\displaystyle={\Bigl\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Sigma}\int_{0}^{1}y\cdot\nabla\Theta(t-s,x-\theta y)\psi_{1}(s,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta{\mathrm{d}}S(y){\mathrm{d}}s\Big\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq CR\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Sigma}\nu^{-\alpha}_{-\beta}(x-y;\zeta)\,{\lvert\psi_{1}(s,y)\rvert}\,{\mathrm{d}}S(y){\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C\nu^{-\alpha}_{-\beta}(x;\zeta)\lVert\psi_{1}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma)}.

Due to the estimates (
2.6
), (
2.7
)
and the decay properties of
E

Q
and
\nabla Q

the claim follows from this general result.
∎

For the purely periodic part, we have the following estimates.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,

and
\mu>3
.
Then there is
C>0
such that for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus{\{0\}}

(1+\nu^{\mu})g\in L^{r}(\mathbb{T};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3})
,
and for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
{\lvert x\rvert}\geq\varepsilon

it holds

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\lvert x\rvert}^{3}\,{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}\rvert}\ast g(t,x)\big\rvert}+{\lvert x\rvert}^{\min{\{\mu,4\}}}\,{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\nabla\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}\rvert}\ast g(t,x)\big\rvert}

\displaystyle+{\lvert x\rvert}^{2}\,{\bigl\lvert{\lvert Q\rvert}\ast g(t,x)\big\rvert}\leq C\lVert(1+\nu^{\mu})g\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathbb{T};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

Proof.

Set
M\coloneqq\lVert(1+\nu^{\mu})g\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathbb{T};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}
.
We start with the estimate of
{\lvert\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}\rvert}\ast g
.
We use Hölder’s inequality on
\mathbb{T}
and
Minkowski’s integral inequality
and
split the spatial integral into three parts
to obtain

\displaystyle{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}\rvert}\ast g(t,x)\big\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\bigl\lvert\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}(t-s,x-y)\big\rvert}^{r^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\bigg)^{1/r^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\bigl\lvert g(t,y)\big\rvert}^{r}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\bigg)^{1/r}{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\leq CM\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{A_{j}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\bigl\lvert\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}(s,x-y)\big\rvert}^{r^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\bigg)^{1/r^{\prime}}\big(1+{\lvert y\rvert}^{\mu}\big)^{-1}\,{\mathrm{d}}y\eqqcolon CM\sum_{j=1}^{3}I_{j}

where
r^{\prime}=r/(r-1)
,
and we set
A_{1}=B_{R}
,
A_{2}=B^{4R}
and
A_{3}=B_{R,4R}
with
R={\lvert x\rvert}/2
.
First, since
{\lvert y\rvert}\leq R
implies
{\lvert x-y\rvert}\geq{\lvert x\rvert}/2
,
we can use (
2.7
) to obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq C\int_{B_{R}}{\lvert x-y\rvert}^{-3}(1+{\lvert y\rvert}^{\mu})^{-1}\,{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\leq C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-3}\int_{B_{R}}(1+{\lvert y\rvert})^{-\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}y\leq C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-3}

since
\mu>3
.
For the second integral,
we again use (
2.7
)
and that
{\lvert y\rvert}\geq 4R
implies
{\lvert x-y\rvert}\geq{\lvert y\rvert}/2

to obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B^{4R}}{\lvert x-y\rvert}^{-3}(1+{\lvert y\rvert})^{-\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}y\leq C\int_{B^{4R}}{\lvert y\rvert}^{-3}{\lvert y\rvert}^{-\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}y=C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-\mu}.

For the third integral, we note that
r^{\prime}>1
and
\mu-3>0
, so that we can choose
\tilde{r}\in(1,5/3)
such that
\tilde{r}<r^{\prime}

and
3/\tilde{r}^{\prime}<\mu-3
.
Then Hölder’s inequality and (
2.10
)
yield

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-\mu}\bigg(\int_{B_{R,4R}}1{\mathrm{d}}y\bigg)^{1/\tilde{r}^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{B_{R,4R}}{\bigl\lvert\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}(s,y)\big\rvert}^{\tilde{r}}\,{\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}s\bigg)^{1/\tilde{r}}

\displaystyle=C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-\mu}R^{3/\tilde{r}^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{B_{R,4R}}{\bigl\lvert\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}(s,y)\big\rvert}^{\tilde{r}}\,{\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}s\bigg)^{1/\tilde{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-\mu+3/\tilde{r}^{\prime}}\leq C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-3}.

Collecting the estimates of
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
,
we arrive at

{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}\rvert}\ast g(t,x)\big\rvert}\leq C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-3}

as asserted.
For the estimate of
{\lvert\nabla\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}\rvert}\ast g

we proceed similarly.
At first, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\nabla\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}\rvert}\ast g(t,x)\big\rvert}\leq CM\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{A_{j}}\!\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\bigl\lvert\nabla\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}(s,x-y)\big\rvert}^{r^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\bigg)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{r^{\prime}}}}\!\!\big(1+{\lvert y\rvert}\big)^{-\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}y\eqqcolon CM\sum_{j=1}^{3}J_{j}

for the sets
A_{j}
,
j=1,2,3
, as before.
Repeating the above arguments, we can estimate
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
as

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq C\int_{B_{R}}{\lvert x-y\rvert}^{-4}(1+{\lvert y\rvert})^{-\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\leq C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-4}\int_{B_{R}}(1+{\lvert y\rvert})^{-\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}y\leq C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-4},

\displaystyle J_{2}\leq C\int_{B^{4R}}{\lvert x-y\rvert}^{-4}(1+{\lvert y\rvert})^{-\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B^{4R}}{\lvert y\rvert}^{-4}{\lvert y\rvert}^{-\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}y=C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-1-\mu},

and for

we use

by (
2.11
)
to deduce

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+{\lvert x\rvert})^{-\mu}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{B_{R,4R}}{\bigl\lvert\nabla\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}(s,y)\big\rvert}\,{\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}s\leq C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-\mu}.

In total, these estimates yield

{\bigl\lvert{\lvert\nabla\Gamma^{\zeta}_{\perp}\rvert}\ast g(t,x)\big\rvert}\leq C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-\min{\{4,\mu\}}}.

For the convolutions with

,
we use

{\lvert P(x)\rvert}=C{\lvert x\rvert}^{-2}

and argue similarly.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

(u,\mathcal{p})
to (

)

(3.12)

and the estimates

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lVert\partial_{t}u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T};L^{q}(\Omega))}&+\lVert\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T};L^{q}(\Omega))}+\lVert\nabla\mathcal{p}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T};L^{q}(\Omega))}\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq C\big(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T};L^{q}(\Omega))}+\lVert h\rVert_{\mathfrak{T}^{p,q}(\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma)}\big),\end{split}

(3.13)

&\qquad\qquad\leq C\big(\lVert\nu^{5/2}_{1}(\cdot;\zeta)\,f_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert h_{0}\rVert_{\mathfrak{T}^{q}(\Sigma)}\big),\end{split}

Proof.

3.2
Solutions to the nonlinear problem

For
k=0,1
and
\delta\in(0,1]
, we introduce the function space

\displaystyle{X}_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\bigl\{v\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T};W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2,q}(\Omega)^{3})\cap W^{1,p}(\mathbb{T};L^{q}(\Omega)^{3}):\Div v=0,\,\lVert v\rVert_{{X}_{k}}<\infty\bigr\}},

\displaystyle\lVert v\rVert_{{X}_{k}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lVert\nabla^{2}v\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T};L^{q}(\Omega))}+\lVert\partial_{t}v\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T};L^{q}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle+\lVert Pv\rVert_{\infty,\nu^{1}_{1}(\cdot;\zeta);\Omega}+\lVert\nabla Pv\rVert_{\infty,\nu^{3/2}_{3/2}(\cdot;\zeta);\Omega}+N_{k}(P_{\perp}v)

where

\displaystyle N_{0}(w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lVert w\rVert_{\infty,\nu^{2};\mathbb{T}\times\Omega}+\lVert\nabla w\rVert_{\infty,\nu^{3};\mathbb{T}\times\Omega},

\displaystyle N_{1}(w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lVert w\rVert_{\infty,\nu^{3};\mathbb{T}\times\Omega}+\lVert\nabla w\rVert_{\infty,\nu^{3+\delta};\mathbb{T}\times\Omega}.

For given
v\in{X}_{k}
,
we consider the problem

\left\{\ \begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\Delta u-\zeta\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\mathcal{p}&=f-{N}(v,v)&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot u&=0&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\Omega,\\
u&=h&&\text{on }\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.17)

where the nonlinear term
{N}
is defined as

{N}(v_{1},v_{2})\coloneqq v_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{2}.

Below we show that the linear theory from
Theorem
3.6

provides a solution
(u,\mathcal{p})

to this problem if
v\in{X}_{k}
.
This defines a solution map

\mathcal{S}\colon v\mapsto u
,
and
(u,\mathcal{p})
solves the nonlinear problem (
1.1
)
if
u
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{S}
.
For obtaining such a fixed point,
we first
prove the following estimates of the convection term,
where we again distinguish steady-state and purely periodic part.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
k\in{\{0,1\}}
and let
v_{1},v_{2}\in{X}_{k}
.
Then

\displaystyle\lVert\nu^{5/2}_{1}(\cdot;\zeta)\,P{N}(v_{1},v_{2})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nu^{7/2+k/2}\,P_{\perp}{N}(v_{1},v_{2})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\Omega)}\leq C\lVert v_{1}\rVert_{{X}_{k}}\lVert v_{2}\rVert_{{X}_{k}}.

Proof.

We set
v_{j}=z_{j}+w_{j}
with
z_{j}\coloneqq Pv_{j}

and
w_{j}=P_{\perp}v_{j}
for
j=1,2
.
Then we have

\displaystyle P{N}(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=z_{1}\cdot\nabla z_{2}+P(w_{1}\cdot\nabla w_{2}),

\displaystyle P_{\perp}{N}(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=z_{1}\cdot\nabla w_{2}+w_{1}\cdot\nabla z_{2}+P_{\perp}(w_{1}\cdot\nabla w_{2}).

Therefore, for
x\in\Omega
we can estimate

\displaystyle\nu^{5/2}_{1}(x;\zeta)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\lvert P{N}(v_{1},v_{2})(x)\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\nu^{1}_{1}(x;\zeta)\,{\lvert z_{1}(x)\rvert}\,{\lvert x\rvert}^{3/2}{\lvert\nabla z_{2}(x)\rvert}+\nu^{0}_{1}(x;\zeta)\,{\lvert w_{1}(t,x)\rvert}{\lvert x\rvert}^{5/2}{\lvert\nabla w_{2}(t,x)\rvert}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert v_{1}\rVert_{{X}_{k}}\lVert v_{2}\rVert_{{X}_{k}},

and

\displaystyle{\lvert x\rvert}^{7/2+k/2}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\lvert P_{\perp}{N}(v_{1},v_{2})(t,x)\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq C\big({\lvert x\rvert}\,{\lvert z_{1}(x)\rvert}\,{\lvert x\rvert}^{5/2+k/2}{\lvert\nabla w_{2}(t,x)\rvert}+{\lvert x\rvert}^{2+k/2}\,{\lvert w_{1}(t,x)\rvert}\,{\lvert x\rvert}^{3/2}\,{\lvert\nabla z_{2}(x)\rvert}

\displaystyle+{\lvert x\rvert}^{2+k/2}\,{\lvert w_{1}(t,x)\rvert}\,{\lvert x\rvert}^{3/2}\,{\lvert\nabla w_{2}(t,x)\rvert}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert v_{1}\rVert_{{X}_{k}}\lVert v_{2}\rVert_{{X}_{k}}.

This shows the asserted estimates.
∎

We can now show existence of a solution to (
1.1
)
by a fixed-point argument.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We set
k=0
in the general case and we set
k=1
when (
3.3
) is satisfied.
For
\varepsilon>0

consider the set

{X}_{k,\varepsilon}\coloneqq{\bigl\{v\in{X}_{k}:\lVert v\rVert_{{X}_{k}}\leq\varepsilon\bigr\}}.

In virtue of Lemma
3.7

and Theorem
3.6
,
for any
v\in{X}_{k}

there exists a solution
(u,\mathcal{p})
to (
3.17
)
with the regularity stated in (
3.12
)
and subject to the estimates

\displaystyle\nu^{1}_{1}(x;\zeta)\,{\bigl\lvert u_{0}(x)\big\rvert}+\nu^{3/2}_{3/2}(x;\zeta)\,{\bigl\lvert\nabla u_{0}(x)\big\rvert}+{\lvert x\rvert}^{2}\,{\bigl\lvert\mathcal{p}_{0}(x)\big\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\lVert\nu^{5/2}_{1}(\cdot;\zeta)\,P{N}(v,v)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nu^{5/2}_{1}(\cdot;\zeta)f_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert h_{0}\rVert_{\mathfrak{T}^{q}(\Sigma)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\lVert v\rVert_{{X}_{k}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\big),

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\lvert x\rvert}^{2+k}{\bigl\lvert u_{\perp}(t,x)\big\rvert}+{\lvert x\rvert}^{3+\min{\{k,\delta\}}}{\bigl\lvert u_{\perp}(t,x)\big\rvert}+{\lvert x\rvert}^{1+k}\,{\bigl\lvert\mathcal{p}_{\perp}(t,x)\big\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\lVert\nu^{3+\delta}P_{\perp}{N}(v,v)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T};L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\lVert\nu^{3+\delta}f_{\perp}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T};L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\lVert h_{\perp}\rVert_{\mathfrak{T}^{p,q}(\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\lVert v\rVert_{{X}_{k}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\big).

For
v\in{X}_{k,\varepsilon}
, we thus have

\lVert u\rVert_{{X}_{k}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\leq\varepsilon

if
\varepsilon>0
is chosen sufficiently small.
Then the solution map
\mathcal{S}\colon v\mapsto u
is a well-defined
self mapping
\mathcal{S}\colon{{X}}_{k,\varepsilon}\to{{X}}_{k,\varepsilon}
.
Moreover, for
v_{1},v_{2}\in{X}_{k,\varepsilon}
,
the differences
\overline{u}=u_{1}-u_{2}
and
\overline{\mathcal{p}}=\mathcal{p}_{1}-\mathcal{p}_{2}
,
where
u_{j}\coloneqq\mathcal{S}(v_{j})
with corresponding pressure
\mathcal{p}_{j}
,
j=1,2
, satisfy

\left\{\ \begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\overline{u}-\Delta\overline{u}-\zeta\cdot\nabla\overline{u}+\nabla\overline{\mathcal{p}}&=-{N}(v_{1},v_{1})+{N}(v_{2},v_{2})&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\overline{u}&=0&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\Omega,\\
\overline{u}&=0&&\text{on }\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma.\end{aligned}\right.

Noting that

{N}(v_{1},v_{1})-{N}(v_{2},v_{2})={N}(v_{1}-v_{2},v_{1})+{N}(v_{2},v_{1}-v_{2}),

we can adapt the same argument as before to conclude the estimate

\lVert\mathcal{S}(v_{1})-\mathcal{S}(v_{2})\rVert_{{X}_{k}}=\lVert\overline{u}\rVert_{{X}_{k}}\leq C(\lVert v_{1}\rVert_{{X}}+\lVert v_{2}\rVert_{{X}_{k}})\lVert v_{1}-v_{2}\rVert_{{X}_{k}}\leq 2C\varepsilon\lVert v_{1}-v_{2}\rVert_{{X}_{k}}.

Hence, choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,
we obtain that
\mathcal{S}
is also a contraction.
Finally, the contraction mapping principle yields
the existence of a unique fixed point
u=\mathcal{S}(u)\in{X}_{k,\varepsilon}
.
If
\mathcal{p}
denotes the associated pressure,
then
(u,\mathcal{p})
is a solution to (
1.1
)
with the asserted properties.
∎

4
Existence in the truncated domains

Our aim is to find a solution
(v,{p})
to the problem (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) defined in the truncated domain
\Omega_{R}
. We seek a velocity field in the form
v=\widetilde{h}+\vartheta
with
\widetilde{h}
an appropriate extension of
h
to
\Omega_{R}
and
\vartheta\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T};H^{1}(\Omega_{R})^{3})
satisfying
\vartheta|_{\Sigma}=0
and
\nabla\cdot\vartheta=0
in
{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega_{R}
.

4.1
Functions spaces over the truncated domains

In what follows, the usual inner products in
L^{2}(\Omega_{R})
and
L^{2}(\partial B_{R})
will be denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega_{R}}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\partial B_{R}}
, respectively. As in
[
3
,
4
]
, we consider
H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
endowed with inner product and norm

(v,w)_{(R)}:=(\nabla v,\nabla w)_{\Omega_{R}}+\frac{1}{R}(v,w)_{\partial B_{R}},\,\,\|w\|_{(R)}=\left(\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{2,\Omega_{R}}+\frac{1}{R}\|w\|^{2}_{2,\partial B_{R}}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}

(4.1)

and we equip the space of time-periodic functions
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}};H^{1}(\Omega_{R}))
with the norm

\|w\|_{({\mathbb{T}},R)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\|w\|_{(R)}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}t\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}.

Within this framework, the following estimate holds for time-periodic functions:

Lemma 4.1
.

Take a fixed
S\in(0,\infty)
with
\partial\Omega\subset B_{S}
and
R>S
. Then there is a constant
C(S)>0
such that

\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times B_{R}\setminus\overline{B_{S}}}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\leq C(S)\|u\|_{({\mathbb{T}},R)}

for all
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}};H^{1}(\Omega_{R}))
.

Proof.

We can directly apply the reasoning from
[
3
, Theorem 3.6]
.
∎

The space

W_{R}:=\left\{w\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})^{3}:\,w|_{\Sigma}=0\right\},

with inner product and norm (
4.1
), will be relevant in the analysis of problem (
1.2
)–(
1.3
).

Consider the following subspaces of divergence-free functions of
W_{R}
,

{V}_{R}:=\left\{\varphi|_{\Omega_{R}}:\,\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)^{3}\text{ and }\nabla\cdot\varphi=0\textrm{ in }\Omega\right\},

V_{R}:=\textrm{ the closure of }{V}_{R}\textrm{ in }H^{1}(\Omega_{R})^{3},

and the space

H_{R}:=\textrm{ the closure of }{V}_{R}\textrm{ in }L^{2}(\Omega_{R})^{3}.

If
\Omega
is a domain with a Lipschitz continuous boundary, then

H_{R}=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega_{R})^{3}:\nabla\cdot v=0\textrm{ in }\Omega_{R}\textrm{ and }v\cdot n=0\textrm{ on }{\Sigma}\right\},

where
n
represents the unit outer normal on
\Sigma
, with
\nabla\cdot v=0
and
v\cdot n
interpreted in the weak
sense, and

V_{R}=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})^{3}:\nabla\cdot v=0\textrm{ in }\Omega_{R}\textrm{ and }v=0\textrm{ on }\Sigma\right\}.

For
\nicefrac{{6}}{{5}}\leq q_{1}<6
and
\nicefrac{{4}}{{3}}\leq q_{2}<4
, we have the embeddings

V_{R}\xhookrightarrow{c}L^{q_{1}}(\Omega)\oplus L^{q_{2}}(\partial B_{R})\xhookrightarrow{}V^{\prime}_{R},

(4.2)

which are compact and continuous, respectively.
For
f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{R})^{3}
, a weak solution to the Stokes problem

\left\{\ \begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla{p}&=f&&\textrm{ in }\Omega_{R}\\
\nabla\cdot u&=0&&\textrm{ in }\Omega_{R},\\
u&=0&&\textrm{ on }\Sigma,\\
\frac{x}{R}\cdot\nabla u-{p}\frac{x}{R}+\frac{1}{R}u&=0&&\textrm{ on }\partial B_{R},\end{aligned}\right.

is a field
u\in V_{R}
such that

(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)_{\Omega_{R}}+\frac{1}{R}(u,\varphi)_{\partial B_{R}}=(f,\varphi)_{\Omega_{R}}\quad\forall\varphi\in V_{R}.

Based on this Stokes problem, it is possible to construct a special basis for the spaces
H_{R}
and
V_{R}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The spectral problem

(\nabla\Psi,\nabla\varphi)_{\Omega_{R}}+\frac{1}{R}(\Psi,\varphi)_{\partial B_{R}}=\lambda(\Psi,\varphi)_{\Omega_{R}},\quad\forall\varphi\in V_{R}

admits a sequence
\{\Psi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset V_{R}
of non-zero solutions corresponding to a sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of eigenvalues

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq...

which satisfies
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

Moreover, we can choose
\{\Psi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in such a way that it forms an
orthonormal basis of
H_{R}
and
\left\{\nicefrac{{\Psi_{k}}}{{\lambda_{k}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
V_{R}
.

Proof.

Given
\Psi\in H_{R}
, by Lax-Milgram Theorem, the problem

(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)_{\Omega_{R}}+\frac{1}{R}(u,\varphi)_{\partial B_{R}}=(\Psi,\varphi)_{\Omega_{R}},\quad\forall\varphi\in V_{R}

has a unique solution
u\in V_{R}
.
The solution operator
\mathcal{S}:H_{R}\rightarrow H_{R}
,
\Psi\mapsto u
, is compact, self-adjoint and positive. Hence,
H_{R}
admits an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions
\Psi_{k}\in V_{R}
of
\mathcal{S}
with corresponding eigenvalues
\mu_{k}
satisfying
\mu_{k}>0,
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}},
and
\mu_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty.
Thus, defining
\lambda_{k}=\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}
, we obtain

(\nabla\Psi_{k},\nabla\varphi)_{\Omega_{R}}+\frac{1}{R}(\Psi_{k},\varphi)_{\partial B_{R}}=\lambda_{k}(\Psi_{k},\varphi)_{\Omega_{R}}\quad\forall\varphi\in V_{R}.

(4.3)

Suppose that
v\in V_{R}
satisfies
(\Psi_{k},v)_{(R)}=(\nabla\Psi_{k},\nabla v)_{\Omega_{R}}+(\Psi_{k},v)_{\partial B_{R}}\big/R=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. From (
4.3
), it follows that
(\Psi_{k},v)_{\Omega_{R}}=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and since
\{\Psi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a basis of
H_{R}
, we conclude that
v\equiv 0
. Hence, the linear span of
\{\Psi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is dense in
V_{R}
. From (
4.3
), we further obtain

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\Psi_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}},\frac{\Psi_{j}}{\lambda_{j}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\bigg)_{(R)}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{\nabla\Psi_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}},\frac{\nabla\Psi_{j}}{\lambda_{j}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\bigg)_{\Omega_{R}}+\frac{1}{R}\bigg(\frac{\Psi_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}},\frac{\Psi_{j}}{\lambda_{j}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\bigg)_{\partial B_{R}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lambda_{j}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}(\Psi_{k},\Psi_{j})_{\Omega_{R}}=\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lambda_{j}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\delta_{kj}=\delta_{kj},\qquad\forall j,k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Therefore,
\left\{\nicefrac{{\Psi_{k}}}{{\lambda_{k}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
V_{R}
.
∎

4.2
Weak solutions in the truncated domain

Assume
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain and recall the total flux of
h
over
\Sigma
, given by
\Phi(t):=\int_{\Sigma}h(t,x)\cdot n(x){\mathrm{d}}S(x)
. To simplify the presentation, for each fixed
R
, we define

c(u,v,w):=\int_{\Omega_{R}}u\cdot\nabla v\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left(\frac{x}{R}\cdot u\right)(v\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x),

(4.4)

which is well defined for
u,v,w\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})^{3}
and satisfies

c(u,v,v)=0,\quad\forall u\in H_{R},\,v\in V_{R}.

(4.5)

In what follows,
\sigma(x):=\nabla E(x)=-\frac{x}{4\pi|x|^{3}}
.
Observe that
\sigma=-P
for the pressure part
P
of fundamental solution, defined in (
2.3
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Given
h\in H^{1}({\mathbb{T}};H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)^{3})

define
\Phi
as in (
3.5
).
Let
R_{0}
be such that
\partial\Omega\subset B_{R_{0}}
.
For any
\gamma>0
, there exists
\widetilde{h}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{T}};H^{1}(\Omega)^{3})
satisfying

\left\{\ \begin{aligned} \nabla\cdot\widetilde{h}&=0&&\text{ in }{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega,\\
\widetilde{h}&=h&&\text{ on }{\mathbb{T}}\times\Sigma,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.6)

and the estimate

|c(\vartheta,\vartheta,\tilde{h})|\leq\gamma\|\vartheta\|_{(R)}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{\infty,{\mathbb{T}}}\left(C_{S}\|\sigma\|_{3,\Omega_{R}}+\frac{1}{8\pi R}\right)\|\vartheta\|_{(R)}^{2}\text{ in }{\mathbb{T}}

(4.7)

for all
R>R_{0}
,
where
C_{S}
is a Sobolev embedding constant.

Proof.

Decompose

h(t,x)=[h(t,x)-\Phi(t)\sigma|_{\Sigma}(x)]+\Phi(t)\sigma|_{\Sigma}(x)=:h^{(1)}(t,x)+h^{(2)}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in{\mathbb{T}}\times\Sigma.

Then
\int_{\Sigma}h^{(1)}(t,x)\cdot n(x){\mathrm{d}}S=0
for all
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
and
\nabla\cdot\sigma=0
in
\Omega
.

For fixed
R_{0}>0
such that
\partial\Omega\subset B_{R_{0}}
,
we can find (see
[
11
, Lemma IX.4.1]
and
[
17
, Lemma 3.3]
)
w:{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega_{R_{0}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \nabla\times w&=h^{(1)}&&\text{ on }{\mathbb{T}}\times\Sigma,\\
\nabla\times w&=0&&\text{ on }{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R_{0}},\\
w&=0&&\text{ on }{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R_{0}},\end{aligned}\right.

and

\|w(t,\cdot)\|_{2,2,\Omega_{R_{0}}}\leq C(\Omega_{R_{0}})\|h^{(1)}(t,\cdot)\|_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},2,\Sigma},\quad t\in{\mathbb{T}},

so that
w\in H^{1}({\mathbb{T}};H^{2}(\Omega_{R_{0}})^{3})
along with the estimate

\|w\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}};H^{2}(\Omega_{R_{0}})^{3})}\leq C(\Omega_{R_{0}})\|h^{(1)}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}};H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Sigma)^{3})}.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\Psi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})

be such that
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(\theta)=1
for
\theta<\frac{\exp(-2/\varepsilon)}{2}
,
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(\theta)=0
for
\theta\geq 2\exp(-1/\varepsilon)
,
|\Psi_{\varepsilon}(\theta)|\leq 1
and
|\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\theta)|\leq\varepsilon/\theta
, for all
\theta>0
. Define
d(x)
as the distance of a point
x\in\Omega_{R_{0}}
to the boundary
\partial\Omega_{R_{0}}
and let
\rho(x)
be the corresponding regularized distance (in the sense of Stein). Using these, define the cut-off function for the domain
\Omega_{R_{0}}

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x):=\Psi_{\varepsilon}(\rho(x)),

and extend it by 1 to the exterior domain
\Omega
. The extension satisfies (see
[
11
, Lemma III.6.2]
and
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
)

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad d(x)<\frac{\exp(-2/\varepsilon)}{2\kappa_{1}},\\
0&\text{if}\quad d(x)\geq 2\exp(-1/\varepsilon),\end{cases}

and

\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\rho(x))\nabla\rho(x),\qquad|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon\kappa_{2}}{d(x)},

where
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
are positive constants independent of the domain.

Define

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h^{(1)}}(t,x)+\widetilde{h^{(2)}}(t,x)=\nabla\times(w(t,x)\psi_{\varepsilon}(x))+\Phi(t)\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\times w(t,x)+\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla\times w(t,x)+\Phi(t)\sigma(x),\quad(t,x)\in{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega,

where
w
is extended to 0 outside
{\mathbb{T}}\times B_{R_{0}}
. Clearly, the function
\widetilde{h}
is divergence free.

Now assume
R>R_{0}
. Then, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, following

[
11
, Lemma X.4.2]
or
[
17
, Lemma 3.3]
, we can estimate

\displaystyle|c(\vartheta,\vartheta,\tilde{h})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega_{R}}\vartheta\cdot\nabla\vartheta\cdot\tilde{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{\Phi(t)}{8\pi R^{2}}\|\vartheta\cdot n\|^{2}_{2,\partial B_{R}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\Omega_{R}}\vartheta\cdot\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\times(w\psi_{\varepsilon})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\right|

\displaystyle+|\Phi(t)|\left|\int_{\Omega_{R}}\vartheta\cdot\nabla\vartheta\cdot\sigma\,{\mathrm{d}}x\right|+\frac{|\Phi(t)|}{8\pi R^{2}}\|\vartheta\cdot n\|^{2}_{2,\partial B_{R}}\,

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\|\nabla\vartheta\|^{2}_{2,\Omega_{R}}+C_{S}\|\Phi\|_{\infty,{\mathbb{T}}}\|\sigma\|_{3,\Omega_{R}}\|\nabla\vartheta\|^{2}_{2,\Omega_{R}}+\frac{\|\Phi\|_{\infty,{\mathbb{T}}}}{8\pi R^{2}}\|\vartheta\|^{2}_{2,\partial B_{R}},

where
C_{S}
is a constant related with the Sobolev embedding in
\Omega
, and
\vartheta\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}};V_{R})
.
∎

Taking into account the regularity of the external force used to solve the exterior problem, we can assume that
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega_{R})^{3}
in in (
1.2
)–(
1.3
). Regarding existence and uniqueness of weak solution for (

)–(
1.3
), we fix

and a solenoidal extension
\tilde{h}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{T}};H^{1}(\Omega)^{3})
given by Lemma
4.3
. Then, we will seek the velocity field for system (

) in the form
v:=u_{R}=\vartheta+\tilde{h}
where
\vartheta\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}};V_{R})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}};H_{R})
.
The velocity
\vartheta
and an associated pressure
{p}
should satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega_{R}}\vartheta\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{R}}\nabla\vartheta:\nabla\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega_{R}}\zeta\cdot\nabla\vartheta\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega_{R}}{p}\nabla\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\qquad\quad+\int_{\Omega_{R}}(\vartheta\cdot\nabla)\tilde{h}\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{h}\cdot\nabla)\vartheta\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{R}}(\vartheta\cdot\nabla)\vartheta\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\qquad\quad+\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}\left(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)\right)\vartheta\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)-\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\vartheta\cdot\frac{x}{R}\right)\tilde{h}\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\\
&\qquad\quad-\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{h}\cdot\frac{x}{R}\right)\vartheta\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)-\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\vartheta\cdot\frac{x}{R}\right)\vartheta\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\\
&\qquad=\int_{\Omega_{R}}f\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega_{R}}\partial_{t}\tilde{h}\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\tilde{h}:\nabla\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\qquad\quad+\int_{\Omega_{R}}\zeta\cdot\nabla\tilde{h}\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}\left(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)\right)\tilde{h}\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\\
&\qquad\quad-\int_{\Omega_{R}}(\tilde{h}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{h}\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{h}\cdot\frac{x}{R}\right)\tilde{h}\cdot\Psi\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x),\quad\forall\Psi\in W_{R},\\
&\int_{\Omega_{R}}(\nabla\cdot\vartheta)\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0,\quad\forall\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega_{R}),\end{aligned}\right.

in the sense of distributions in
{\mathbb{T}}

4.6

\displaystyle+4\|f\|^{2}_{2,\Omega_{R}}+4\|\partial_{t}\tilde{h}\|^{2}_{2,\Omega_{R}},

which we combine with (

) and the estimate (

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega_{R}}w\cdot\nabla w\cdot u\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{2}\left(w\cdot\frac{x}{R}\right)(u\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x){\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{2}\left(u\cdot\frac{x}{R}\right)(u\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}\left(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)\right)(u\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{x}{R}\cdot\nabla u\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\mathcal{p}\left(\frac{x}{R}\cdot w\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

Proof.

In what follows, we again consider the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{(R;|\xi|)}
in
H^{1}(\Omega_{R})^{3}
defined in (
4.10
),
and the multi-linear forms
a
and
c
defined in (
4.8
) and (
4.4
), respectively.
Recall the notation
\vartheta:=u_{R}-\tilde{h}
,
and define
\mu:=u-\tilde{h}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T};V_{R})
, so that
w=u-u_{R}=\mu-\vartheta
. Then, we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\|w\|_{(R,|\zeta|)}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\mu,w)_{(R,|\zeta|)}{\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\vartheta,\mu)_{(R,|\zeta|)}{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\|\vartheta\|_{(R,|\zeta|)}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}t

Integration by parts in
\Omega_{R}
and the fact that
(u,\mathcal{p})

is a strong solution to (
1.1
)
yield

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle(\mu,w)_{(R,|\zeta|)}{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\partial B_{R}}\left(\frac{1}{R}+\frac{|\zeta|}{2}\right)(\mu\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S{\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\partial_{t}u,w\right)_{\Omega_{R}}{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\Omega_{R}}\zeta\cdot\nabla u\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\Omega_{R}}u\cdot\nabla u\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f,w)_{\Omega_{R}}{\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\Omega_{R}}\nabla\tilde{h}:\nabla w\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{x}{R}\cdot\nabla u\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x){\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{x}{R}\cdot w\mathcal{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x){\mathrm{d}}t.

We next take the test function
\Phi=\mu=u-\tilde{h}
in the weak formulation (
4.34
),
which is admissible
since
\mu\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T};V_{R})
,

\tilde{h}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T};H^{1}(\Omega)^{3})

and
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T};L^{p}(\Omega_{R})^{3})
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Decomposing
\vartheta=u_{R}-\tilde{h}
, we get

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\vartheta,\mu)_{(R,|\zeta|)}{\mathrm{d}}t=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}(u_{R},\partial_{t}\mu)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{\mathbb{T}}c(u_{R},u_{R},\mu)\,{\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f,\mu)_{\Omega_{R}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{\mathbb{T}}a(\tilde{h},\mu)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Omega_{R}}\zeta\cdot\nabla\vartheta\cdot\mu\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}\frac{(\zeta\cdot x)}{2}(\vartheta\cdot\mu)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x){\mathrm{d}}t.

Since

\vartheta=u_{R}-\tilde{h}
satisfies the energy inequality (
4.13
), we further have

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\|\vartheta\|_{(R,|\zeta|)}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}t\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(f-\partial_{t}\tilde{h},\vartheta\big)_{\Omega_{R}}{\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{\mathbb{T}}c(u_{R},\tilde{h},\vartheta)\,{\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{\mathbb{T}}a(\tilde{h},\vartheta)\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

To combine the terms in the above expressions,
we take into account the identities

\displaystyle(f,w)_{\Omega_{R}}-(f,\mu)_{\Omega_{R}}+(f,\vartheta)_{\Omega_{R}}

\displaystyle=0,

as well as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big[-\left(\partial_{t}u,w\right)_{\Omega_{R}}-(u_{R},\partial_{t}\mu)_{\Omega_{R}}

\displaystyle-(\partial_{t}\tilde{h},\vartheta)_{\Omega_{R}}\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big[-\left(\partial_{t}u,u\right)_{\Omega_{R}}+(\tilde{h},\partial_{t}\tilde{h})_{\Omega_{R}}\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\int_{\Omega_{R}}\big[-\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\tilde{h}|^{2}\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t=0.

Due to the identity

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R}}\zeta\cdot\nabla u\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega_{R}}\nabla\tilde{h}:\nabla w\,{\mathrm{d}}x+a(\tilde{h},\mu)-a(\tilde{h},\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{R}}\zeta\cdot\nabla\mu\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))\tilde{h}\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x),

we can further collect the terms related with
\zeta
as

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left(\frac{1}{R}+\frac{|\zeta|}{2}\right)(\mu\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S+\int_{\Omega_{R}}\zeta\cdot\nabla u\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega_{R}}\nabla\tilde{h}:\nabla w\,{\mathrm{d}}x+a(\tilde{h},\mu)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{R}}\zeta\cdot\nabla\vartheta\cdot\mu\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}\frac{(\zeta\cdot x)}{2}(\vartheta\cdot\mu)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)-a(\tilde{h},\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))(u\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)-\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}\frac{(\zeta\cdot x)}{2}(w\cdot\mu)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)+\int_{\Omega_{R}}\zeta\cdot\nabla\mu\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{R}}\zeta\cdot\nabla\mu\cdot\vartheta\,{\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}\frac{(\zeta\cdot x)}{2}(\vartheta\cdot\mu)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))(u\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x),

where we used integration by parts and that
\mu=w+\vartheta
.
Recalling the property (
4.5
) of
c
,
we further have

\displaystyle c(u_{R},u_{R},\mu)-c(u_{R},\tilde{h},\vartheta)

\displaystyle=c(u_{R},u_{R},\mu)-c(u_{R},u_{R},\vartheta)+c(u_{R},\vartheta,\vartheta)=c(u-w,u-w,w)

\displaystyle=c(u,u,w)-c(w,u,w)-c(u,w,w)+c(w,w,w)=c(u,u,w)+c(w,w,u).

In this way, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\|w\|_{(R,|\zeta|)}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\Omega_{R}}u\cdot\nabla u\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{x}{R}\cdot\nabla u\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x){\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{x}{R}\cdot w\mathcal{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x){\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}c(u,u,w)\,{\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{\mathbb{T}}c(w,w,u)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))(u\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x){\mathrm{d}}t.

Invoking the definition of
c
, see (
4.4
),
we conclude (
5.2
).
∎

With inequality (
5.2
) at hand,
we now show that the velocity error
w
tends to zero in appropriate norms. It is useful to recall the properties of strong solutions in the exterior domain, as outlined in Remark
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

In order to estimate
w
, we use Lemma
5.2

and write (
5.2
) as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\|w\|^{2}_{(R,|\zeta|)}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\leq\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega_{R}}w\cdot\nabla w\cdot u\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\left(w\cdot\frac{x}{R}\right)\right](u\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\left(u\cdot\frac{x}{R}\right)\right](u\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}\left(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)\right)(u\cdot w)\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{x}{R}\cdot\nabla u\cdot w\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\left[-\mathcal{p}\left(\frac{x}{R}\cdot w\right)\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t=:\sum_{i=1}^{6}I_{j},

and we estimate
I_{1}
, …,
I_{6}
separately.

Take a fixed
S\in(0,\infty)
with
\partial B_{S}\subset\Omega
. Let
R>S
.
From Lemma
4.1
and estimate (
3.2
) in Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times B_{R}\setminus\overline{B_{S}}}w\cdot\nabla w\cdot u\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t\right|\,\leq

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times B_{R}\setminus\overline{B_{S}}}\frac{|w(t,x)|}{|x|}|\nabla w(t,x)||x||u(t,x)|\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(S)\|w\|_{({\mathbb{T}},R)}^{2}\|u\|_{\infty,\nu^{1};\mathbb{T}\times B^{S}}

and therefore, by Poincaré’s and Hölder’s inequalities,

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega_{S}}w\cdot\nabla w\cdot u\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t\right|+\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times B_{R}\setminus\overline{B_{S}}}w\cdot\nabla w\cdot u\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(S,\partial\Omega)\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega_{S})}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};L^{3}(\Omega_{S}))}+C(S)\|u\|_{\infty,\nu^{1};\mathbb{T}\times B^{S}}\|w\|_{({\mathbb{T}},R)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(S,\partial\Omega)\left[\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};L^{3}(\Omega_{S}))}+\|u\|_{\infty,\nu^{1};\mathbb{T}\times B^{S}}\right]\|w\|_{({\mathbb{T}},R)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(S,\partial\Omega)\,\varepsilon\|w\|_{({\mathbb{T}},R)}^{2}.

From (
3.2
), we also get the following estimates for the integrals over
{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}
involving the velocity
u
:

\displaystyle I_{2}\,\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}\frac{1}{R}\left|w(t,x)\right|^{2}|x||u(t,x)|\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x){\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\infty,\nu^{1};\mathbb{T}\times B^{S}}\frac{1}{R}\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R})}\leq\varepsilon\|w\|_{({\mathbb{T}},R)}^{2},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2R^{3/2}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}|x|^{2}|u(t,x)|^{2}\frac{|w(t,x)|}{R^{1/2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x){\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{1/2}}\|u\|^{2}_{\infty,\nu^{1};\mathbb{T}\times B^{S}}\|w\|_{({\mathbb{T}},R)}\leq\frac{C}{R^{1/2}}\varepsilon^{2}\|w\|_{({\mathbb{T}},R)}

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{R^{3/2}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\partial B_{R}}|x|\left(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)\right)|u(t,x)|\frac{|w(t,x)|}{R^{1/2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x){\mathrm{d}}t

Combined with estimates (

\displaystyle I_{5}

Remark 5.3
.

For the convergence statement of Theorem
5.1
,
we had to assume
\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\Phi=0
,
that is, that the total flux through the boundary is constant in time.
As shown in Theorem
3.1

this condition ensures that the decay rate of the pressure is

,
compare Remark
3.2
.
In the previous proof, this lead to a suitable estimate of the term
I_{6}
,
which cannot be obtained form the weaker rate

that holds in the general case.

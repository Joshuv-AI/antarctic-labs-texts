Abstract
We present a switching scheme, which uses both the attitude-error quaternion (AEQ) and the angular-velocity error, for controlling the rotational degrees of freedom of an uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV) during flight. In this approach, the proposed controller continually selects the stable closed-loop (CL) equilibrium AEQ corresponding to the smallest cost between those computed with two energy-based Lyapunov functions. To analyze and enforce the stability of the CL switching dynamics, we use basic nonlinear theory. This research problem is relevant because the selection of the stable CL equilibrium AEQ directly determines the power and energy requirements of the controlled UAV during flight. To test and demonstrate the implementation, suitability, functionality, and performance of the proposed approach, we present experimental results obtained using a 𝟑𝟏 \boldsymbol{31} -gram quadrotor, which was controlled to execute high-speed yaw maneuvers in flight. These flight tests show that the proposed switching controller can respectively reduce the control effort and rotational power by as much as 49.75 \boldsymbol{49.75} % and 28.14 \boldsymbol{28.14} %, on average, compared to those corresponding to an often-used benchmark controller.

[
23

. However, the
so-called

issue induces representational and

closed-loop
(CL) configuration. For instance, we show in
[
11
,
12
,
9

10

that the nonlinear dynamics of a micro

(UAV) can be robustly stabilized using a

(LTI) controller; but, interestingly, the resulting CL system has two different fixed points that represent exactly the same kinematic state, one stable and the other unstable. Furthermore, the stability properties of these two equilibria can be interchanged by simply changing the sign of a controller gain. This apparent paradox is explained by the fact that, after closing the loop, the controller becomes an integral part of the resulting CL dynamic system and, therefore, its interaction with the two mathematical representations of the same physical equilibrium generates two distinct CL dynamical behaviors. As discussed in Section
III
, this phenomenon can greatly affect the performance of a controlled UAV during
high-speed
flight.

The ability to minimize both the tracking error and power consumption during flight are essential requirements for UAVs to efficiently perform
energy-demanding
tasks that necessitate time coordination, last long periods of time (
\sim
hours), and involve
high-speed
rotations, such as search and rescue missions, military operations, and wildlife monitoring
[
25
]
. This need for reliability and endurance motivated the research presented in this paper. One conceivable application of the method discussed here is
insect-flight

++
presented in
[
10
]

To start this research, we hypothesized that, depending on both the controlled UAV’s angular velocity and orientation when executing a rotational maneuver, in some instances it is beneficial to interchange the stability properties of the two equilibria of the CL system in real time, according to a
performance figure of merit
(PFM). This hypothesis was inspired by a phenomenon known as

[
18

Exactly the same approach was used in the definition of the backstepping flight control law presented and discussed in

[
19

[

7

.

Except for the case we presented in
[
7
]
, none of the research efforts listed above validated the efficacy and robustness of the proposed methods through
real-time
experiments, and only numerical simulations were used for testing purposes. We believe that the thorough collection of experimental data is an essential step in the development of new algorithms for
real-time
control. In this paper, we introduce a
Lyapunov-based

As part of the proposed approach, we present a method to enforce the stability of the CL system for a switching law specified in terms of the difference between two Lyapunov functions respectively associated with the two subsystems composing the CL switching dynamics. To test and demonstrate the functionality, suitability, and performance of the proposed approach, we present experimental results collected using a
31
-gram
quadrotor during flight. Overall, the results presented here represent relevant theoretical and experimental contributions to the design, analysis, and implementation of controllers capable of selecting the stable CL equilibrium AEQ during
real-time
operation.

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nu}^{T}_{-1}\boldsymbol{n}_{\text{e}}-\boldsymbol{\nu}_{-1}^{T}\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrlap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-4.65pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\displaystyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-4.65pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-4.65pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-4.65pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}}q}}^{-1}\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{-1}+2\dot{m}_{\text{e}}.

\forall\,\left\{\boldsymbol{\mathrlap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\displaystyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}}q}_{\text{e}},\boldsymbol{\nu}_{-1}\right\}\not\in\{\boldsymbol{\mathrlap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\displaystyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}}q}_{\text{e}}^{\dagger},\boldsymbol{\nu}_{-1}^{\star}\}\cup\left\{\boldsymbol{\mathrlap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\displaystyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}}q}_{\text{e}}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\nu}_{-1}^{\star}\right\}

V-A

Performance Evaluation

To evaluate the flight performance obtained with the switching controller specified by (
13
) and compare it to that obtained using the benchmark controller specified by (
12
), we define a PFM that depends on the torque control signal,

\displaystyle\vskip-4.30554pt\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\left(\frac{1}{t_{\text{f}}-t_{0}}\int^{t_{\text{f}}}_{t_{0}}\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{exp}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},~~[\text{N}\cdot\text{m}]

(37)

and a PFM that depends on the aerodynamic rotational power,

\displaystyle\vskip-4.30554pt\Gamma_{\text{p}}=\left(\frac{1}{t_{\text{f}}-t_{0}}\int^{t_{\text{f}}}_{t_{0}}\left[\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{exp}}^{T}(t)\boldsymbol{\omega}(t)\right]^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},~~[\text{N}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{rad}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}]

(38)

where
t_{0}
and
t_{\text{f}}
are the initial and final times of a flight maneuver, and
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{exp}}\in\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{b}},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\hskip-0.60275pt\sigma}\right\}
is the control law employed in a specific flight test. We selected
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
and
\Gamma_{\text{p}}
as PFMs instead of the CL system’s LF because in this way we can compare different control methods using exactly the same metrics.

The
real-time

7
]

six-V
5
-camera

system running at

3.9

[
10
]
, also running at a sampling rate of

and

Experimental data corresponding to two sets of flight tests are presented in Fig.
3

]
and indicated in Figs.

VI
Conclusions and Future Work

We presented a new method for synthesizing, analyzing, and implementing switching controllers capable of selecting the stable equilibrium AEQ of the CL attitude dynamics of a UAV during flight, according to an
energy-based
switching law. To analyze and enforce the stability of the CL switching dynamics, we used basic nonlinear Lyapunov theory. We tested and demonstrated the suitability, functionality, and performance of the proposed approach through
real-time
flight tests. The experimental results presented and discussed here are compelling as they clearly demonstrate that the proposed control approach can significantly improve the performance achieved by UAVs during
high-speed
rotational flight. The collected data show that in the experiments in which switching occurred, the values of two PFMs, respectively defined in terms of the control effort and rotational power, were reduced by
49.75\,\%
and
28.14\,\%
, on average, when compared to those obtained with a benchmark controller. One limitation of the presented method is that we did not consider actuator saturation, which can greatly affect flight performance. This issue is a matter of current and future work in our laboratory.

Derivatives of padded Schubert polynomials through pipe dreams Thanks: The author was partially supported by NSF awards DMS-2231565 and DMS-1945212.
Hugh Dennin

Abstract
. In recent work, Hamaker, Pechenik, Speyer, and Weigandt showed that a certain differential operator ∇ \nabla expands positively in the basis of Schubert polynomials. For π \pi a dominant permutation, Gaetz and Tung showed an analogous positivity result holds for a dual differential operator Δ \Delta acting on π \pi -padded Schubert polynomials. In this paper, we provide a new proof of the result of Gaetz and Tung using the combinatorics of pipe dreams.



1.
Introduction

Fix a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots)
and write
\pi=\dom(\lambda)
for the dominant permutation with code
\lambda
.
The differential operators

\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}y_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}

act on the subspace
V_{\lambda}
of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots;y_{1},y_{2},\dots]
spanned by the monomials
x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}y_{i}^{\beta_{i}}
satisfying
\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}=\lambda_{i}
for each
i\geq 1
.

When
w
lies below
\pi
in (left) weak order, the monomials appearing in the
Schubert polynomial

\mathfrak{S}_{w}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots]
each divide
x^{\lambda}
.
Hence we may consider the homogenization
\mathfrak{S}_{w}^{\pi}(x;y)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=y^{\lambda}\mathfrak{S}_{w}^{\pi}(\frac{x_{1}}{y_{1}},\frac{x_{2}}{y_{2}},\dots)\in V_{\lambda}
, called the
\pi
-padded Schubert polynomial
of
w
.

The goal of this paper is to provide a combinatorial proof of the following identity of Gaetz and Tung
[
5
]
showing that
\Delta
admits a positive expansion when acting on a
\pi
-padded Schubert polynomial:

Theorem 1.1

(
\Delta
identity
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\pi=\dom(\lambda)
be a dominant permutation.
For
w\leq_{L}\pi
, we have

\Delta\mathfrak{S}_{w}^{\pi}(x;y)=\sum_{w\lessdot_{\pi}t_{ab}w}\left(1+|A(w,t_{ab}w)|+|B(w,t_{ab}w)|\right)\mathfrak{S}_{t_{ab}w}^{\pi}(x;y).

Here
w\lessdot_{\pi}t_{ab}w
is shorthand for
w\lessdot t_{ab}w\leq_{L}\pi
and

\displaystyle A(w,t_{ab}w)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{k>b\mid w^{-1}(a)<w^{-1}(k)<w^{-1}(b)\},

\displaystyle B(w,t_{ab}w)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{k>b\mid\pi w^{-1}(b)<\pi w^{-1}(k)<\pi w^{-1}(a)\}.

We remark that the special case of Theorem
1.1
where
\pi=w_{0}^{n}=n\dots 21
is the longest permutation in
S_{n}
was originally proved by Gaetz and Gao
[
4
]
.

To motivate Theorem
1.1
, it is well-known that
\Delta
and
\nabla
act as the raising and lowering operators of a
\fraksl_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representation on
\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}V_{\lambda}
.
In other words, letting
H
denote the commutator
[\Delta,\nabla]=\Delta\nabla-\nabla\Delta
,
one can verify the relations

[\Delta,\nabla]=H,\qquad[H,\Delta]=2\Delta,\qquad[H,\nabla]=-2\nabla.

An earlier result of Hamaker, Pechenik, Speyer, and Weigandt
[
6
]
shows that
\nabla
also expands positively when applied to a
\pi
-padded Schubert polynomial:

Theorem 1.2

(
\nabla
identity
[
6
]
)
.

For
w\in S_{\infty}
, we have

\nabla\mathfrak{S}^{\pi}_{w}(x;y)=\sum_{s_{k}w\lessdot_{L}w}k\cdot\mathfrak{S}^{\pi}_{s_{k}w}(x;y).

Together, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
show that the subspace with basis given by the
\pi
-padded Schubert polynomials
\mathfrak{S}^{\pi}_{w}(x;y)
for
w\leq_{L}\pi
constitutes an
\fraksl_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-subrepresentation of
\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}V_{\lambda}
.
In particular, this implies that the interval
[e,\pi]_{L}
in weak order satisfies the strong Sperner property
[
5
]
.

2.
Background & Definitions

2.1.
Permutations, Bruhat Order, and Weak Order

Let
S_{n}
denote the collection of permutations of
\mathbb{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{1,2,\dots\}
with
support

\{i\geq 1\mid w(i)\neq i\}
contained in
[n]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{1,\dots,n\}
, and let
S_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}S_{n}
denote the set of permutations of
\mathbb{N}
with finite support.
We will frequently write permutations
w\in S_{n}
using their
one-line notation

w(1)w(2)\dots w(n)
.

For
a,b\geq 1
with
a<b
, write
t_{ab}\in S_{\infty}
for the transposition that swaps
a
and
b
.

S_{\infty}
is generated as a group by the simple transpositions
s_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=t_{k\,k+1}
for
k\geq 1
.
The
length
of a permutation
w\in S_{\infty}
is the smallest number
\ell(w)\geq 0
such that
w
can be written as a product of
\ell(w)
simple transpositions.

The
Bruhat order
(or
strong order
) on
S_{\infty}
is the partial order
\leq
obtained as the transitive closure of the cover relations
w\lessdot t_{ab}w
whenever
\ell(t_{ab}w)=\ell(w)+1
.
The suborder
\leq_{L}
of Bruhat order obtained by only considering covers of the form
w\lessdot s_{k}w
where
\ell(s_{k}w)=\ell(w)+1
is called the
(left) weak order
on
S_{n}
.
For permutations
w\leq_{L}\pi
, we use the shorthand
w\lessdot_{\pi}t_{ab}w
to denote that both
w\lessdot t_{ab}w
and
t_{ab}w\leq_{L}\pi
.

The
inversion set
of a permutation
w
is the collection of pairs

\Inv(w)=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid i<j\text{ and }w(i)>w(j)\}.

It is well-known that
\ell(w)=|\Inv(w)|
.
A useful transformation of the inversion set is the
diagram
of
w
, given by

\displaystyle D(w)

\displaystyle=\{(i,w(j))\mid(i,j)\in\Inv(w)\}

\displaystyle=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid i<w^{-1}(j)\text{ and }j<w(i)\}.

We will also make use of
co-inversion set
defined as

\coInv(w)=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid i<j\text{ and }w(i)<w(j)\}.

The weak order on
S_{\infty}
can be characterized in terms of inversion sets.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
w,u\in S_{\infty}
.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

w\leq_{L}u
,

(ii)

\Inv(w)\subseteq\Inv(u)
(or equivalently
\coInv(u)\subseteq\coInv(w)
),

(iii)

\Inv(w^{-1})\subseteq\coInv(uw^{-1})
(or equivalently
\Inv(uw^{-1})\subseteq\coInv(w^{-1})
).

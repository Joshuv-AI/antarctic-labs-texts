1.
Introduction and main results

The aim of this paper is to obtain compactness results for the critical Hardy-Sobolev on closed manifolds.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed and smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. We fix
x_{0}\in M
,
s\in(0,2)
, and let
\crits:=2(n-s)/(n-2)
denote the critical Hardy-Sobolev exponent. We consider the following
Hardy-Sobolev equation
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{g}u+hu=\dfrac{u^{\crits-1}}{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{s}}&\hbox{ in }M\setminus\{x_{0}\},\\
\quad u>0&\hbox{ in }M\setminus\{x_{0}\}.\end{array}\right.

Here
\Delta_{g}:=-\diver_{g}(\nabla)
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator with (minus sign convention) and
h\in C^{1}(M)
. We will also assume throughout this paper that
\Delta+h
is coercive, that is, there exists
\mathscr{C}>0
such that

\int_{M}\left(|\nabla v|_{g}^{2}+hv^{2}\right)\,dv_{g}\geq\mathscr{C}\|v\|^{2}_{H^{2}_{1}}\,\hbox{ for all }v\in H_{1}^{2}(M),

where the Sobolev space
H^{2}_{1}(M)
can equivalently be defined as the completion of
C^{\infty}(M)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{2}_{1}}:=\sqrt{\|\nabla\cdot\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\cdot\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}~
.

The exponent
\crits
is critical for the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}_{1}(M)\to L^{p}(M,d_{g}(\cdot,x_{0})^{-s})
, where
1\leq p\leq\crits
and our equation (
1
) is variationaly noncompact in general. Note that the singular term
d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{-s}
in equation (
1
) breaks the natural conformal invariance property of the problem.

We further let
\operatorname{Scal}_{g}
denote the scalar curvature of the manifold
(M,g)
and

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}:=\frac{(n-2)(6-s)}{12(2n-2-s)},~0\leq s<2.

The case
s=0
corresponds to Yamabe-type equations, and likewise the potential
\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}
plays a similar defining role here in obtaining existence and compactness of solutions of (
1
). For
s>0
it turns out the behaviour around the singularity
x_{0}
only really matters, as the problem is subcritical and hence compact on domains not containing
x_{0}
. We exploit this phenomenon in this paper, and this further simplifies and shortens many of the arguments needed when
s=0
for the case of Yamabe-type problems. We consider solutions that can a priori develop an arbitrary number of bubbles in the bubble tree decomposition. Moreover, we do not assume any bound on the energy or the Sobolev norm of the solutions.

The existence of solutions of (
1
) is well-understood, and in
[
19
]
Jaber showed the existence of positive extremals under the local sign condition
h(x_{0})<\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
for
n\geq 4
. For dimension
n=3
, as in the non-singular case
s=0
, the mass of the operator
\Delta_{g}+h
plays a crucial role, and Jaber in the same paper gave an existence result when the mass at
x_{0}
is positive. For the definition of mass, see (
1.5
) and one may think of it as the constant term in the expansion of the Green’s function of the operator
\Delta_{g}+h
. Concerning regularity, it follows from Jaber
[
19
]
that any solution
u\in H^{2}_{1}(M)
of (
1
) satisfies
u\in C^{0,\theta}(M)\cap C^{2,\eta}_{\loc}(M\setminus\{x_{0}\})
, for all
0<\theta<\min\{1,2-s\}
and
0<\eta<1
. Also see Jaber
[
20
]
and Cheikh-Ali
[
5
]
for a sharp form of the Hardy-Sobolev inequality on manifolds, and the conditions which give the existence of minimizers.

Motivated by results on Yamabe-type problems, we obtain in this paper a compactness result for the solutions of the Hardy-Sobolev equation (
1
).
Compactness of solutions of (
1
) for the case
s=0
is a very well-studied topic and the literature is abundant. For brevity, we refer the interested reader to Druet
[
11
]
and the book by Hebey
[
17
]
and the references therein. Compactness for the Hardy-Sobolev equation with
d_{g}(x_{0},x)
replaced by the distance function from submanifolds was recently explored in
[
18
]
. For the geometric Yamabe poblem some notable developments are Schoen
[
30
,

]
, Li-Zhu
[
25
]
when
n=3
, Druet
[
11
]
when
n\leq 5
, Marques
[

]
when
n\leq 7
, Li-Zhang
[
23
,
24
]
when
n\leq 11
and Khuri-Marques-Schoen
[
21
]
when
n\leq 24
. Also see Druet-Premoselli
[
10
]
for stability of the Einstein–Lichnerowicz constraint system, and Premoselli-Vetois
[

]
for compactness of sign-changing solutions of the Yamabe type equation. For domains in

with Dirichlet boundary conditions and

we refer to Druet and Laurain
[

[

Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed and smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
, fix
x_{0}\in M
and
s\in(0,2)
. Let
h\in C^{1}(M)
be such that the operator
\Delta_{g}+h
is coercive and assume that one of
the following assumptions is satisfied:

•

n\geq 4
and
h(x_{0})<\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
, where
c_{n,s}
is given by (
1.3
).

•

n=3
and
m_{h}(x_{0})\not=0
, where
m_{h}(x_{0})
is the mass of the operator
\Delta_{g}+h
defined in (
1.5
).

Then, there exists a constant
\Lambda=\Lambda(M,g,s,h)
such that any solution
u\in C^{0,\theta}(M)\cap C^{2}(M\setminus\{x_{0}\})
of (
1
), with
0<\theta<\min\{1,2-s\}
satisfies:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{C^{0}(M)}+\left\|1/u\right\|_{C^{0}(M)}\leq\Lambda.

Note, we do not assume any
H^{2}_{1}(M)
bound on solutions. The condition on the scalar curvature is optimal and has appeared before in Jaber
[
19
]
, and in
[
5
]
, where the first author arrived at the same conclusion for a sequence close to the single bubble profile. Very recently, the authors in
[
4
]
constructed a sequence of solutions of (
1
) blowing up with the profile of a bubble such that
h\to h_{0}
in
C^{p}(M)
,
p\geq 2
, where
h_{0}(x_{0})=\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
for
n\geq 7
. Also see
[
6
]
for the construction of a blowing up sequence with perturbations of the nonlinear power.

Our proof is based on a priori asymptotic analysis of sequences of blowing-up solutions
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
of (
1
). Compared to the case
s=0
, the presence of the singularity creates many simplifications and we show that the blow up is highly localized at
x_{0}
and can be controlled globally by the canonical bubble centred at
x_{0}
. Then, using a Pohozaev identity we obtain a balancing condition contradicting the assumptions of our Theorem. We effectively reduce the analysis of multi-bubble blow-up solutions to the study of a single bubble-type configuration. It seems our Theorem
1.1
is the first general compactness result for the Hardy-Sobolev equation on manifolds.

The so-called
m
ass-function
m_{h}
of
\Delta_{g}+h
is defined as follows: let
G_{x_{0}}:M\backslash\{x_{0}\}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Green’s function of the coercive operator
\Delta_{g}+h
. For
n=3
we have the following expansion around the pole at
x_{0}

(1.5)

G_{h}(x_{0},x)=\frac{1}{4\pi d_{g}(x_{0},x)}+\beta(x_{0},x)\hbox{ for all }x\in M\backslash\{x_{0}\},

for some
\beta(x_{0},\cdot)\in C^{2}(M\backslash\{x_{0}\})\cap C^{0,\vartheta}(M)
where
\vartheta\in(0,1)
, and we then define
m_{h}(x_{0})=\beta(x_{0},x_{0})
. For an expansion of the Green’s function, see, for example, Appendix A of Druet, Hebey, and Robert
[
8
]
, and Robert
[
29
]
. By the positive mass theorem of Schoen and Yau
[
32
]
one has
m_{\operatorname{Scal}_{g}/8}(x_{0})\geq 0
and
m_{\operatorname{Scal}_{g}/8}(x_{0})=0
if and only
(M,g)
is conformally diffeomorphic to the unit
3
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
with the spherical metric
g_{std}
.
Then applying the maximum principle, Theorem
1.1
implies the following as in the case
s=0
.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed and smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n=3
with positive scalar curvature
\operatorname{Scal}_{g}>0
in
M
. Fix
x_{0}\in M
and
s\in(0,2)
. Let
h\in C^{1}(M)
be such that the operator
\Delta_{g}+h
is coercive and
h\leq\operatorname{Scal}_{g}/8
in
M
but
h(x_{0})<\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})/8
Then, there exists a constant
\Lambda=\Lambda(M,g,s,h)
such that any solution
u\in C^{0}(M)\cap C^{2}(M\setminus\{x_{0}\})

of (
1
) satisfies the bound (
1.4
).

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

2.
Preliminaries and notations

(M,g)
will denote a closed and smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. The linear space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be endowed with the canonical Euclidean metric. On the tangent bundle of
M
, the exponential map
\exp:\mathcal{TM}\to M
is smooth and there exists an injectivity radius
r_{0}>0
such that for any
x_{0}\in M
the map

\exp_{x_{0}}|_{B(0,r_{0})}:B(0,r_{0})\mapsto B_{g}(x_{0},r_{0})

is a diffeomorphism. Here
B(0,r_{0})
denotes the ball of radius
r_{0}
centered at
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
B_{g}(x_{0},r_{0})=\{y\in M\hbox{ such that }d_{g}(y,x_{0})<r_{0}\}
denotes the geodesic ball of radius
r_{0}
centered at
x_{0}
. Also,
\iota_{g}:=i_{g}(M)
will denote the injectivity radius of
(M,g)
and
\Delta_{g}:=-\diver_{g}(\nabla)
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator with minus sign convention. Recall

-\left(\Delta_{g}-\Delta_{\xi}\right)=\left(g^{ij}-\delta^{ij}\right)\partial_{ij}-g^{ij}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{k},

where
\Delta_{\xi}
denotes the usual Euclidean laplacian, and
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
are the Christoffel symbols and for all
i,j,k,m\in\{1,...,n\}

\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}g^{km}\left(\partial_{i}g_{jm}+\partial_{j}g_{im}-\partial_{m}g_{ij}\right).

Coming back to our Hardy-Sobolev equation, we define for
\mu>0

U_{\mu}(X):=\mu^{\frac{2-n}{2}}U\left(\mu^{-1}X\right)\hbox{ for }X\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
U
is the standard
bubble
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

(2.1)

U(X):=\left(\frac{1}{1+\mathfrak{b}_{n,s}|X|^{2-s}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2-s}}\hbox{ for }X\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\hbox{ with }\mathfrak{b}_{n,s}^{-1}:=(n-s)(n-2).

It follows from
[
7
]
that any function
V\in C^{0}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{2}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
satisfying:

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{\xi}\,V=\dfrac{V^{\crits-1}}{|X|^{s}}&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},\\
V>0&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},\end{array}\right.

equals
U_{\mu}
for some
\mu>0
. Here,
\Delta_{\xi}
is the usual Euclidean Laplacian. It follows that the set of solutions
U_{\mu}
is non-compact.

We remark here that, any nonnegative
u\in C^{0,\theta}(M)\cap C^{2}(M\setminus\{x_{0}\})
, with
0<\theta<\min\{1,2-s\}
, satisfying
\Delta_{g}u+hu=\dfrac{u^{\crits-1}}{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{s}}
in
M\setminus\{x_{0}\}
indeed satisfies the maximum principle, that is, either
u>0
in
M
or
u\equiv 0
in
M
. See for instance Jaber
[
19
]
(Step 6 in Page 45).

3.
Proof of main results

Once an a priori bound is established, our theorems will follow from standard elliptic estimates.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
: The upper bound follows from
standard elliptic estimates
if there exists
C>0
such that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C
for any solution of (
1
), since
s<2
.

Suppose there exists a sequence of solutions
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
of (
1
) such that
\|u_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\to+\infty
as
\alpha\to+\infty
, that is, there exists a blowing up sequence. Then by Proposition
4.1
we obtain
h_{\infty}(x_{0})=\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
when
n\geq 4
, where
\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}
is the constant defined in (
1.3
), and in dimension
3
we obtain that
m_{h}(x_{0})=0
, where
m_{h}(x_{0})
is the mass of the operator
\Delta_{g}+h
. This contradicts the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, thereby establishing the upper bound.

To obtain a uniform bound on
\left\|1/u\right\|_{C^{0}(M)}
we again proceed by contradiction. Assume that there exists a sequence of solutions
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
of (
1
) and a sequence of points
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\in M
such that
u_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})\to 0
as
\alpha\to+\infty
. Since the sequence of solutions
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
of (
1
) is such that
\|u_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C
for some constant
C>0
. We then obtain the existence of
u_{\infty}\in C^{0,\theta}(M)\cap C^{2}(M\setminus\{x_{0}\})
, where
0<\theta<\min\{1,2-s\}
, such that
u_{\alpha}\to u_{\infty}
in
C^{0,\hat{\theta}}(M)\cap C^{2}_{\loc}(M\setminus\{x_{0}\})
with
0\leq\hat{\theta}<\theta
. Furthermore
u_{\infty}\geq 0
in
M
and satifisfies the equation:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}u_{\infty}+hu_{\infty}=\dfrac{u_{\infty}^{\crits-1}}{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{s}}

\displaystyle\hbox{ in }M\setminus\{x_{0}\}.

Note that since for each
\alpha\geq 1
the quantity
d_{g}(x_{0},x)|\nabla u_{\alpha}(x)|=\smallo(1)
as
x\to x_{0}
, we also obtain in this case

(3.1)

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}\to u_{\infty}\hbox{ in }H_{1}^{2}(M)\hbox{ as }\alpha\to+\infty.

Since the operator
\Delta+h_{\infty}
is coercive, so along the sequence of solutions
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
we obtain by applying the Hardy–Sobolev inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{u_{\alpha}^{\crits}}{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{s}}\,dv_{g}\geq C,

for some
C>0
independent of
\alpha
. The strong convergence
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to+\infty}u_{\alpha}\to u_{\infty}
in
C^{0}
then gives us
\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{u_{\infty}^{\crits}}{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{s}}\,dv_{g}\geq C
and this implies
u_{\infty}>0
in
M
by the maximum priciple as stated at the end of Section
2
. This prohibits the existence of a sequence such that
u_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})\to 0
as
\alpha\to+\infty
and this gives the uniform lower bound.
∎

4.
Controlling the Blow-up

Our aim in this section is to extract a necessary condition for blow-up to occur. This is presented in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed and smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
, fix
x_{0}\in M
and
s\in(0,2)
. Let
h_{\infty}\hbox{ in }C^{1}(M)
be such that the operator
\Delta_{g}+h_{\infty}
is coercive, and
h_{\infty}(x_{0})\leq\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
when
n\geq 4
with
\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}
given by (
1.3
).
Consider a sequence of functions
(h_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}\in C^{1}(M)
such that
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to+\infty}h_{\alpha}=h_{\infty}
in
C^{1}(M)
and suppose
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}\in C^{0,\theta}(M)\cap C^{2}(M\setminus\{x_{0}\})
, with
0<\theta<\min\{1,2-s\}
satisfies:

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{g}u_{\alpha}+h_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}=\dfrac{u_{\alpha}^{\crits-1}}{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{s}}&\hbox{ in }M\setminus\{x_{0}\},\\
\quad u_{\alpha}>0&\hbox{ in }M\setminus\{x_{0}\}.\end{array}\right.

If
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
blows-up, that is
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to+\infty}\|u_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}=+\infty
, then

(i)

h_{\infty}(x_{0})=\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
when
n\geq 4
.

(ii)

m_{h_{\infty}}(x_{0})=0
when
n=3
.

Here
m_{h_{\infty}}(x_{0})
is the mass of the operator
\Delta_{g}+h_{\infty}
defined in (
1.5
), and
\mathfrak{c}_{n,s}
is given by (
1.3
).

Note that coercivity is a necessary condition for the existence of positive solutions of (
4.1
) (see Appendix B of
[
8
]
).

We prove this proposition in multiple steps, starting with the following lemma, which implies that only
x_{0}
can be the point of blow-up.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed and smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
, fix
x_{0}\in M
and
s\in(0,2)
. Let
(h_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
be as in the statement of Proposition
4.1
. There exists
C>0
such that for any sequence
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}\in C^{0,\theta}(M)\cap C^{2}(M\setminus\{x_{0}\})
satisfying equation (
4.1
), with
0<\theta<\min\{1,2-s\}
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}u_{\alpha}(x)\leq C~\hbox{ for all }x\in M.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction: assume that there exists a sequence
(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}\in M
such that

(4.3)

\sup_{M}\left(d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}u_{\alpha}(x)\right)=:d_{g}(x_{0},y_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2}{2}}u_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha})\to+\infty,

as
\alpha\to+\infty
. Since
M
is compact, this implies

(4.4)

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}u_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha})=+\infty.

Now, let
\nu_{\alpha}:=u_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha})^{2/(2-n)}
for all
\alpha\geq 1
. Then from (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) it follows that

\lim\limits_{\alpha\to+\infty}\frac{d_{g}(x_{0},y_{\alpha})}{\nu_{\alpha}}=+\infty\hbox{ and }\lim\limits_{\alpha\to+\infty}\nu_{\alpha}=0.

Again, letting
\ell_{\alpha}:=d_{g}(x_{0},y_{\alpha})^{s/2}\nu_{\alpha}^{\frac{2-s}{2}}
for all
\alpha>0
, since
M
is compact, we have

(4.5)

\lim\limits_{\alpha\to+\infty}\frac{d_{g}(x_{0},y_{\alpha})}{\ell_{\alpha}}=+\infty\hbox{ and }\lim\limits_{\alpha\to+\infty}\ell_{\alpha}=0.

We rescale and define for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}

\bar{v}_{\alpha}(X):=\nu_{\alpha}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}u_{\alpha}\left(\exp_{y_{\alpha}}(\ell_{\alpha}X)\right)\hbox{ for all }X\in B(0,\iota_{g}/2\ell_{\alpha}).

Here
\exp_{y_{\alpha}}
is the exponential chart at
y_{\alpha}
with respect to the metric
\bar{g}_{\alpha}:=\exp_{y_{\alpha}}^{*}g(\ell_{\alpha}\cdot)
for all
\alpha\geq 1
.

From the definition we have that

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and using (
4.3
) we obtain

(4.6)

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{\alpha}(X)\leq\left(\frac{d_{g}(x_{0},y_{\alpha})}{d_{g}(x_{0},\exp_{y_{\alpha}}(\ell_{\alpha}X))}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}~\hbox{~in }B(0,\iota_{g}/2\ell_{\alpha}).

Note for

we have for
\alpha
large

|d_{g}(x_{0},\exp_{y_{\alpha}}(\ell_{\alpha}X))-d_{g}(x_{0},y_{\alpha})|\leq\ell_{\alpha}R~\hbox{ for all }X\in B(0,R),

and with the help of (

(4.7)

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\left(\frac{d_{g}(x_{0},y_{\alpha})}{d_{g}(x_{0},\exp_{y_{\alpha}}(\ell_{\alpha}X))}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}=1.

Then in (

) we obtain that as

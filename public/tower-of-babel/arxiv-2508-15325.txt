Some Optimal and Near Optimal Doppler Resilient Complementary Sequence Sets
Bingsheng Shen, Yang Yang, Zhengchun Zhou, Pingzhi Fan

Abstract
Sequences with excellent ambiguity functions are very useful in radar detection and modern mobile communications. Doppler resilient complementary sequence (DRCS) is a new type of sequence proposed recently, which can achieve lower ambiguity function sidelobes by summing the ambiguity functions of subsequences. In this paper, we introduce some new constructions of DRCS sets (DRCSSs) based on one-coincidence frequency-hopping sequence sets (OC-FHSSs), almost difference sets (ADSs), some specific sequences, etc. Critically, the proposed DRCSSs are optimal or near optimal.



I
Introduction

Sequences with low correlation is widely used in engineering, especially in wireless communication and radar sensing.
Such as synchronization, channel estimation, interference cancellation, sensing, positioning, etc
[
1
,
2
]
.
The ideal sequence set is one in which the auto-correlation function of each sequence is an impulse function, and the cross-correlation function of any different sequences is always equal to zero.
However, according to the Welch bound, there is no such sequence set
[
3
]
.

In order to achieve ideal correlation functions, a concept called “complementary sequence (CS)” has been developed and widely studied.
The idea of “complementarity” was first introduced by Marcel J. E. Golay in the 1960s which aims to achieve an ideal aperiodic auto-correlation function by summing the auto-correlation functions of two binary sequences
[
4
]
. A large number of researchers have studied the properties and construction of binary/quaternary CSs
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
Following Golay’s research, Tseng and Liu proposed the concept of mutually orthogonal CS sets (MOCSSs), where each CS contains multiple subsequences and the sums of correlation functions of all subsequences can achieve ideal auto- and cross-correlation properties
[
14
]
.
In a MOCSS, the set size
K
is limited by the number
M
(is also celled flock size) of subsequences in each CS, that is,
K\leq M
. In particular, a MOCSS is called a complete complementary code (CCC)
[
15
]
if
K=M
.
In order to increase the set size, Liu
et al.
proposed the concept of low-correlation-zone CSS (LCZ-CSS), which only considers the amplitude of the correlation function of CSS in a LCZ
[
16
]
. These sequence sets have received a lot of attention in engineering, and there are many mathematical construction methods.

Modern sequence design is more stringent as one is expected to deal with the notorious Doppler effect in various mobile channels
[
17
,
18
]
.
For example, in connected vehicles and radar sensing, the received signal is often a time-shifted and phase-rotated version of the transmitted signal due to propagation delay and mobility-incurred Doppler.
In signal processing, the ambiguity function (AF) is usually used to characterize the delay-Doppler response of the receiver
[
19
]
.
Precise delay-Doppler estimation requires sequence sets that simultaneously minimize both the peak sidelobe level of the auto-AF and the peak magnitude of the cross-AF.
In
[
20
]
, Ding
et al.
first gave a theoretical bound on the AF amplitude of a sequence set. However, this bound is not tight and there is no sequence set that can reach the bound.
Later, Ye
et al.
improved the bound in
[
20
]
by utilizing the property that the auto-AF at zero delay in a unimodular sequence is equal to zero for any non-zero Doppler
[
21
]
.
Besides, Ye
et al.
also referred to such the sequence set as the Doppler-resilient sequence set (DRSS). If the parameters of a DRSS can make the equality of the theoretical lower bound hold, it is called optimal. Designing optimal DRSS is a challenge.
Some optimal DRSSs or DRSSs with low AF amplitudes can be found in
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
.

To minimize AF’s sidelobes, we drew inspiration from DRSSs and complementary sequences and proposed the concept of a Doppler-resilient complementary sequence set (DRCSS)
[
26
]
.
The AF of each Doppler resilient complementary sequence (DRCS) is characterized by the sum of the AFs of its subsequences.
The idea of DRCS originated from
[
27
]
, Turyn studied the mean square value of the AF of binary DRCS containing two subsequences.
Compared with DRSSs, DRCSSs can achieve lower AF sidelobes, even thumbtack-type AF. The research on DRCSSs is still in its initial stage, and there are relatively few related achievements. In this paper, we propose several constructions that can generate optimal or almost optimal periodic DRCSSs.
Firstly, based on the observation of circular Florentine rectangle (CFR), we found that it is essentially an one-coincidence frequency-hopping sequence set (OC-FHSS). Therefore, we directly use the OC-FHSS to obtain many asymptotically optimal DRCSSs with new parameters.
Subsequently, we adapt the framework established in
[
26
]
to generate asymptotically optimal or near-optimal DRCSSs by using ADSs.
Finally, we find that by repeating the complete complementary codes (CCCs), the optimal DRCSSs with zero ambiguity zone (ZAZ) can be obtained. The advantage of this construction is that it can generate DRCSSs for small alphabets.
As a comparison with the known constructions, the parameters of our proposed periodic DRCSSs are listed in Table

II
Preliminaries

In this section, we will introduce the definition of DRCSSs and review the corresponding theoretical bounds. In addition, we will introduce some mathematical tools used in this paper.

II-A

Ambiguity Functions

A conventional sequence set
\mathbf{S}
contains
K
sequences of length
N
, and each of which has all of its entries taking values from a set, called the alphabet. Namely,
\mathbf{S}=\{\mathbf{s}_{k}:0\leq k\leq K-1\}
,
\mathbf{s}_{k}=(s_{k,0},s_{k,1},\cdots,s_{k,N-1})
, where
0\leq k<K
. In this paper, we consider unimodular sequences, that is, each element in the sequence is defined as a complex root of unity. For two sequence
\mathbf{s}_{u}
and
\mathbf{s}_{v}
in
\mathbf{S}
, their periodic cross ambiguity function (PCAF) is defined as

\displaystyle AF_{\mathbf{s}_{u},\mathbf{s}_{v}}(\tau,f)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N-1}s_{u,i}s_{v,i+\tau}^{*}\xi_{N}^{fi},

(1)

where
-N<\tau,f<N
, the summation
i+\tau
is calculated on
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
. If
u=v
,
AF_{\mathbf{s}_{u},\mathbf{s}_{v}}(\tau,f)
is called the periodic auto ambiguity function (PAAF), and denoted by
AF_{\mathbf{s}_{u}}(\tau,f)
.
Especially, when
f=0
, the two-dimensional AF degenerates into a one-dimensional correlation function, with auto- and cross-correlation function denoted as
R_{\mathbf{s}_{u}}(\tau)
and
R_{\mathbf{s}_{u},\mathbf{s}_{v}}(\tau)
, respectively.

II-B

Doppler Resilient Complementary Sequence Sets

A DRCSS
\mathcal{S}
contains
K
(i.e., set size) DRCSs, each DRCS consists of
M
(i.e., flock size) subsequences of length
N
, i.e.,
\mathcal{S}=\{\mathbf{S}^{(k)}:0\leq k<K\}
,
\mathbf{S}^{(k)}=\{\mathbf{s}_{m}^{(k)}:0\leq m<M\}
,
\mathbf{s}_{m}^{(k)}=(s_{m,0}^{(k)},s_{m,1}^{(k)},\cdots,s_{m,N-1}^{(k)})
. Sometimes, we also have a two-dimensional matrix representing DRCS
\mathbf{S}^{(k)}
, which is denoted by

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{(k)}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{s}_{0}^{(k)}\\
\mathbf{s}_{1}^{(k)}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{s}_{M-1}^{(k)}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}s_{0,0}^{(k)}&s_{0,1}^{(k)}&\cdots&s_{0,N-1}^{(k)}\\
s_{1,0}^{(k)}&s_{1,1}^{(k)}&\cdots&s_{1,N-1}^{(k)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
s_{M-1,0}^{(k)}&s_{M-1,1}^{(k)}&\cdots&s_{M-1,N-1}^{(k)}\end{bmatrix}.

(2)

For two DRCSs
\mathbf{S}^{(u)}
and
\mathbf{S}^{(v)}
, their PCAF is defined as the sums of the PCAFs of all subsequences, i.e.,

\displaystyle AF_{\mathbf{S}^{(u)},\mathbf{S}^{(v)}}(\tau,f)=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}AF_{\mathbf{s}_{m}^{(u)},\mathbf{s}_{m}^{(v)}}(\tau,f),

(3)

it is abbreviated to
AF_{\mathbf{S}^{(u)}}(\tau,f)
when
u=v
. For a DRCSS, given a low ambiguity zone (LAZ)
\Pi\subseteq(-N,N)\times(-N,N)
, the maximum periodic ambiguity magnitude of DRCSS
\mathcal{S}
over this region
\Pi
is defined as
\theta_{\max}=\{\theta_{a},\theta_{c}\}
, where

\displaystyle\theta_{a}=\max\{|AF_{\mathbf{C}}(\tau,f)|:\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{S},(\tau,f)\neq(0,0)\in\Pi\}

(4)

is maximal periodic auto-ambiguity magnitude and

\displaystyle\theta_{c}=\max\{|AF_{\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D}}(\tau,f)|:\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D}\in\mathcal{S},(\tau,f)\in\Pi\}

(5)

is maximal periodic cross-ambiguity magnitude.

Remark 1
.

While the formal concept of DRCSs was recently introduced in
[
26
]
, its foundational principle traces back to Turyn’s seminal 1963 work on AF properties of complementary sequences. In
[
28
]
, Turyn established the mean square value of the AF for binary sequence pairs–a precursor to DRCS frameworks.

A DRCSS with maximum ambiguity magnitude
\theta_{\max}
over LAZ
\Pi
is denoted by
(K,M,N,\theta_{\max},\Pi)
-LAZ-DRCSS. For a LAZ-DRCSS, if
\Pi=(-N,N)\times(-N,N)
, it is recorded as
(K,M,N,\theta_{\max})
-DRCSS; if
\theta_{\max}=0
, the LAZ is called the zero ambiguity zone (ZAZ), and it is denoted by
(K,M,N,\Pi)
-ZAZ-DRCSS. Especially, when
M=1
, the DRCSS degenerates into a DRSS
[
21
]
and denoted by
(K,N,\theta_{\max},\Pi)
-DRSS.

Lemma 1

(
[
26
]
)
.

For a
(K,M,N,\theta_{\max},\Pi)
-LAZ-DRCSS, where
\Pi=(-Z_{x},Z_{x})\times(-Z_{y},Z_{y})
and
1\leq Z_{x},Z_{y}\leq N
, then we have

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}=\frac{MN}{\sqrt{Z_{y}}}\sqrt{\frac{\frac{KZ_{x}Z_{y}}{MN}-1}{KZ_{x}-1}}.

(6)

Remark 2
.

Typically, an optimality factor
\rho
is used to describe the closeness between the maximum AF magnitude of the LAZ-DRCSS and theoretical lower bound, which is defined by

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{\theta_{\max}}{\frac{MN}{\sqrt{Z_{y}}}\sqrt{\frac{\frac{KZ_{x}Z_{y}}{MN}-1}{KZ_{x}-1}}}.

(7)

Obviously,
\rho\geq 1
. If
\rho=1
, the LAZ-DRCSS is said to be optimal. If
1<\rho<2
, the LAZ-DRCSS is said to be near-optimal. Specifically, when the parameter approaches infinity and the optimality factor is equal to 1, we refer to it as asymptotically optimal.

Lemma 2

(
[
26
]
)
.

For a
(K,M,N,\Pi)
-ZAZ-DRCSS, where
\Pi=(-Z_{x},Z_{x})\times(-Z_{y},Z_{y})
and
1\leq Z_{x},Z_{y}\leq N
, then we have

\displaystyle KZ_{x}Z_{y}\leq MN.

(8)

Remark 3
.

For a
(K,M,N,\Pi)
-ZAZ-DRCSS, if
KZ_{x}Z_{y}=MN
, the ZAZ-DRCSS is said to be optimal.

II-C

One-Coincidence Frequency-Hopping Sequence Sets

Let
\mathbb{F}=\{f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{Q-1}\}
, where
f_{i}
is a frequency point, and
Q
is a positive integer. Usually,
\mathbb{F}
is equivalent to
\mathbb{Z}_{Q}
.
Let
\mathbf{X}=\{\mathbf{x}_{k}:0\leq k<K\}
be a frequency hopping sequence set (FHSS) of length
N
over
\mathbb{F}
, where
\mathbf{x}_{k}=(x_{k,0},x_{k,1},\cdots,x_{k,N-1})
. The Hamming cross-correlation function of
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{X}
at shift
\tau
is defined by

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(\tau)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h(x_{i},y_{i+\tau}),

(9)

where
0\leq\tau\leq N-1
, the summation
i+\tau
is calculated on
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, and
h(a,b)=0
if
a\neq b
and
1
otherwise. If
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}
, then
H_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(\tau)
is called the Hamming auto-correlation function. Further, its maximum Hamming correlation is defined by
H_{\max}=\{H_{a},H_{c}\}
, where

\displaystyle H_{a}=

\displaystyle\max\{H_{\mathbf{x}}(\tau):\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{X},0<\tau<N\},

(10)

\displaystyle H_{c}=

\displaystyle\max\{H_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(\tau):\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{X},\forall\tau\},

(11)

then
\mathbf{X}
is referred to as
(K,N,H_{\max},Q)
-FHSS. A FHSS is called one-coincidence FHSS (OC-FHSS) if
H_{a}=0
and
H_{c}=1
, and denoted by
(K,N,Q)
-OC-FHSS.

III
Proposed Constructions

In this section, we will propose several constructions of DRCSSs based on the ADS and some known sequences, which can generate asymptotically optimal or near-optimal DRCSSs.

III-A

DRCSSs From One-Coincidence Frequency-Hopping Sequence Sets

This subsection will propose a method to construct DRCSSs using OC-FHSSs. Based on the known OC-FHSSs, we can obtain many asymptotically optimal DRCSSs with new parameters.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbf{F}=\{\mathbf{f}_{k}:0\leq k<K\}
be a
(K,N,Q)
-OC-FHSS, where
N
,
K
,
Q
are three positive integers. For each
0\leq k<K
, define a DRCS
\mathbf{S}^{(k)}=\{\mathbf{s}_{m}^{(k)}:0\leq m<Q\}
, where
\mathbf{s}_{m}^{(s)}=(s_{m,0}^{(k)},s_{m,1}^{(k)},\cdots,s_{m,N-1}^{(k)})
, and

\displaystyle s_{m,n}^{(k)}=\xi_{Q}^{m\times f_{k,n}},

(14)

where
0\leq m<Q
,
0\leq n<N
,
f_{k,n}
is
n
-th element of
\mathbf{f}_{k}
. Then
\mathcal{S}=\{\mathbf{S}^{(k)}:0\leq k<K\}
is a periodic
(K,N,N,Q)
-DRCSS.

Proof.

For any two DRCSs
\mathbf{S}^{(u)}
and
\mathbf{S}^{(v)}
in
\mathcal{S}
, where
0\leq u,v<K
, their AF is calculated as

\displaystyle AF_{\mathbf{S}^{(u)},\mathbf{S}^{(v)}}(\tau,f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{Q-1}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\xi_{Q}^{m(f_{u,i}-f_{v,i+\tau})}\xi_{N}^{if}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\xi_{N}^{if}\sum_{m=0}^{Q-1}\xi_{Q}^{m(f_{u,i}-f_{v,i+\tau})}.

(15)

For
\tau=0
and
u=v
,
AF_{\mathbf{S}^{(u)},\mathbf{S}^{(v)}}(\tau,f)=0
is trivial when
f\neq 0
. For
\tau\neq 0
or
\tau=0
,

, since
\mathbf{F}
is an OC-FHSS, hence

has at most one solution.
If there is no solution, then
AF_{\mathbf{S}^{(u)},\mathbf{S}^{(v)}}(\tau,f)=0
, which corresponds to the case
u=v
and
(\tau,f)\neq(0,0)
. If there is one solution

, then we have
AF_{\mathbf{S}^{(u)},\mathbf{S}^{(v)}}(\tau,f)=\xi_{N}^{i^{\prime}f}Q
. This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle=1,~p\rightarrow\infty,

(16)

which means that the DRCSS obtained by Case 2 is asymptotically optimal. For Case 3, the optimality factor for the generated DRCSS is

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{C3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta_{\max}}{M\sqrt{N}\sqrt{\frac{KN/M-1}{KN-1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}\sqrt{p(p-1)}\sqrt{\frac{p(p(p-1))/p^{2}-1}{p(p(p-1))-1}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{p^{2}(p-1)-1}{p(p-1)(p-2)}}

\displaystyle=1,~p\rightarrow\infty,

(17)

which means that the DRCSS obtained by Case 3 is asymptotically optimal.
∎

ZAZ-DRCSSs From Complete Complementary Codes

In this subsection, we will use complete complementary codes (CCCs) to construct the optimal ZAZ-DRCSS. Below, we will give some brief basic knowledge about CCCs.

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathbf{C}^{(k)}:0\leq k<M\}
be a sequence set, each
\mathbf{C}^{(k)}
consists of
M
subsequences of length
L
, i.e.,
\mathbf{C}^{(k)}=\{\mathbf{c}_{m}^{(k)}:0\leq m<M\}
,
\mathbf{c}_{m}^{(k)}=(c_{m,0}^{(k)},c_{m,1}^{(k)},\cdots,c_{m,N-1}^{(k)})
. For
0\leq k,t<M
, if

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{C}^{(k)},\mathbf{C}^{(t)}}(\tau)=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}R_{\mathbf{c}_{m}^{(k)},\mathbf{c}_{m}^{(t)}}(\tau)=\begin{cases}MN,&\tau=0,k=t;\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

(25)

then
\mathcal{C}
is called a CCC and denoted by
(M,N)
-CCC.

At present, the research on CCCs is very mature, and some construction methods can be found in
[
15
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
. It is worth noting that there exists a CCC with parameters of the form
(N,N)
, where
N
is an arbitrary positive integer.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathbf{C}^{(k)}:0\leq k<M\}
be an
(M,N)
-CCC and
\mathbf{a}
be an all-1 sequence of length
L
. Define a sequence set
\mathcal{S}=\{\mathbf{S}^{(k)}:0\leq k<M\}
and
\mathbf{S}^{(k)}=\{\mathbf{s}_{m}^{(k)}:0\leq m<M\}
, where

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{m}^{(k)}=\mathbf{a}\otimes\mathbf{c}_{m}^{(k)}.

(26)

Then
\mathcal{S}
be an optimal ZAZ-DRCSS with parameter
(M,M,NL,\Pi)
, where
\Pi=(-Z_{x},Z_{y})\times(-Z_{y},Z_{y})
,
Z_{x}=N
and
Z_{y}=L
.

Proof.

For two DRCS
\mathbf{S}^{(k)}
and
\mathbf{S}^{(t)}
, we have

\displaystyle AF_{\mathbf{S}^{(k)},\mathbf{S}^{(t)}}(\tau,f)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\sum_{i=0}^{NL-1}s_{m,i}^{(k)}(s_{m,i+\tau}^{(t)})^{*}\xi_{NL}^{if}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}c_{m,n}^{(k)}(c_{m,n+\tau}^{(t)})^{*}\xi_{NL}^{nf}\xi_{NL}^{lNf}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}c_{m,n}^{(k)}(c_{m,n+\tau}^{(t)})^{*}\xi_{NL}^{nf}\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\xi_{L}^{lf}.

(27)

Obviously,
AF_{\mathbf{S}^{(k)},\mathbf{S}^{(t)}}(\tau,f)=0
when
f\neq 0\pmod{L}
. Therefore, we only need to consider the case where
f=0
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is a CCC, so we have

\displaystyle AF_{\mathbf{S}^{(k)},\mathbf{S}^{(t)}}(\tau,0)=\begin{cases}MNL,&\tau=0~(\text{mod }N),k=t,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(28)

In summary,
\mathcal{S}
is a ZAZ-DRCSS, where ZAZ is
\Pi=(-N,N)\times(-L,L)
. On the other hand, it is evident that the parameters of
\mathcal{S}
can make the equal sign of (
8
) hold, therefore it is optimal.
∎

IV
Conclusion

This paper is devoted to studying novel unimodular LAZ/ZAZ-DRCSSs with new parameters. First, we proposed a construction of DRCSSs based on OC-FHSSs, and deriving several new DRCSSs using the known OC-FHSS, see Theorem
1
, Theorem
2
and Corollary
1
. Then, we proposed the new LAZ-DRCSS by using the (near)-optimal DRSSs and ADSs, see Theorem
3
and Corollary
2
. Finally, we generated the ZAZ-DRCSS by repeating the CCCs, see Theorem
4
. These proposed LAZ/ZAZ-DRCSSs are optimal or near-optimal relative to the periodic theoretical bound in
[
26
]
. Crucially, the proof of Theorem
1
reveals that the Hamming auto-correlation requirement for OC-FHSSs can be relaxed from
H_{a}=0
to
H_{a}=1
while preserving optimality of proposed DRCSSs. Future work will develop weakened OC-FHSSs with larger set sizes to obtain more optimal DRCSSs.

Boundary estimates for singular elliptic problems involving a gradient term
Phuong Le

Abstract
. We study the behavior of weak solutions to the singular quasilinear elliptic problem − Δ p ​ u + ϑ ​ | ∇ u | q = 1 u γ + f ⁡ ( u ) -\Delta_{p}u+\vartheta|\nabla u|^{q}=\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u) , in a bounded domain with the Dirichlet boundary condition, where p > 1 p>1 , γ > 0 \gamma>0 , 0 < q ≤ p 0<q\leq p , ϑ ≥ 0 \vartheta\geq 0 and f : [ 0 , + ∞ ) → ℝ f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R} is a locally Lipschitz continuous function. We obtain a precise estimate for directional derivatives of positive solutions in a neighborhood of the boundary. We also deduce the symmetry of positive solutions to the problem in a bounded symmetric convex domain. Our results are new even in the case p = 2 p=2 and ϑ = 0 \vartheta=0 .

1.
Introduction

We study the behavior near the boundary of solutions to the quasilinear elliptic problems

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u+\vartheta|\nabla u|^{q}=\dfrac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where we always assume that
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{2}
domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
,
p>1
,
\gamma>0
,
0<q\leq p
,
\vartheta\geq 0
and
f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function.

We call
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
a weak solution to the first equation of (
1
) if

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle+\vartheta\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\varphi=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi}{u^{\gamma}}+\int_{\Omega}f(u)\varphi\quad\text{ for every }\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega).

Moreover, the positivity of
u
is understood as

\essinf_{x\in K}u(x)>0\quad\text{ for every }K\subset\subset\Omega,

and the zero Dirichlet boundary condition also has to be understood in the weak meaning

(u-\varepsilon)^{+}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\text{ for every }\varepsilon>0.

In the same spirit, for
v_{1},v_{2}\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we say that
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
on
\partial\Omega
if

(v_{1}-v_{2}-\varepsilon)^{+}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\text{ for every }\varepsilon>0.

We will show later that actually
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Since the nonlinearity is singular near the boundary, it is notable that
C^{1}
regularity cannot be extended up to the boundary and the gradient generally blows up near the boundary in such a way that
u\not\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
(see
[
8
,
20
]
). Furthermore, the difficulty in understanding problem (
1
) lies in the fact that both sides of the problem may exhibit singularity near
\partial\Omega
. A simpler situation was investigated by Esposito and Sciunzi in
[
12
]
for the singular quasilinear elliptic problem without the gradient term

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\dfrac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2)

This is exactly problem (
1
) with
\vartheta=0
.

Various authors have studied singular elliptic problems without a gradient term in the past. We refer in particular to the pioneering papers by Crandall, Rabinowitz and Tartar
[
8
]
and by Lazer and McKenna
[
20
]
, where they combine the method of subsolutions
and supersolutions with the approximation technique to prove the existence, uniqueness and qualitative properties of solutions to

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\dfrac{a(x)}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(3)

with
p=2
(see
[
17
]
for further insights). We also refer to the work by Boccardo and Orsina
[
3
]
on the same problem in a more general setting, exploiting truncation arguments and Schauder’s fixed-point theorem. Later, the existence, uniqueness and regularity of solutions to problem (
3
) with
p>1
were derived in
[
9
,
6
]
. Another possible approach to problem (
2
) with
p=2
and
f(u)=\lambda u^{p}
is the variational one as in
[
7
,
4
,
18
]
(see also
[
2
,
1
]
).

Due to its possible blowup, the study of the behavior of directional derivatives of solutions near the boundary is an important issue. As we mentioned above, the situation is much more difficult when the singular term and gradient term both appear in the equation with the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition. Currently, there are no results on such estimates for problem (
1
) in the literature. Other kinds of results are also limited. We refer to
[
22
,
13
]
for symmetry results for solutions of (
1
) in the case
p=2
and
q\in\{1,2\}
. The existence of a solution to problem (
1
) in the case
1<q=p<N
,
f\equiv 0
was established recently in
[
11
]
.

Now we recall the main result in
[
12
]
(see also
[
5
]
for
p=2
), which motivates our work. Throughout the paper, we denote the distance function for domain
\Omega
by

d_{\Omega}(x):={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\quad\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

For
\delta>0
, we denote by

I_{\delta}(\Omega):=\{x\in\Omega\mid d_{\Omega}(x)<\delta\}

the
\delta
–neighborhood of the boundary of
\Omega
. Since
\Omega
is a
C^{2}
domain,
I_{\delta}(\Omega)
has the unique nearest point property for small
\delta
(see
[
15
]
). For such
\delta
and
x\in I_{\delta}(\Omega)
, let
\hat{x}\in\partial\Omega
be the point such that
|x-\hat{x}|=d_{\Omega}(x)
. Then we define

\eta(x)=\frac{x-\hat{x}}{|x-\hat{x}|}.

It was proved in
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
that for
\gamma>1
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, there exists a neighborhood
I_{\delta}(\Omega)
of
\partial\Omega
such that for any solution
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
to (
2
), we have
\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\nu}>0

whenever
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with
\langle\nu,\eta(x)\rangle\geq\beta
, where

\mathbb{S}^{N-1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|x|=1\}.

This result is regarded as the Höpf boundary lemma for the problem. The Höpf boundary lemma is already known in the non-singular quasilinear setting (see
[
26
]
) and plays an important role in nonlinear analysis.

Intuitively, since the gradient of
u
may blow up near the boundary, we would expect
\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\nu}
goes to
+\infty
when
x
approaches
\partial\Omega
and
\langle\nu,\eta(x)\rangle>\beta
. In this paper, we not only prove such a claim for problem (
1
) but also provide a precise estimate for
\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\nu}
near the boundary. We also consider the situations in which
0<\gamma\leq 1
. These results are new even in the semilinear case
p=2
.

Our first result is concerned with the strongly singular case
\gamma>1
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a solution to (
1
) with
\gamma>1
. Then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Furthermore, the following assertions hold:

(i)

For any sequences
x_{n}\in\Omega
and
\nu_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
such that

d_{\Omega}(x_{n})\to 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\langle\nu_{n},\eta(x_{n})\rangle\to\beta\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty,

we have

d_{\Omega}(x_{n})^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{\partial u(x_{n})}{\partial\nu_{n}}\to\left(\frac{(\gamma+p-1)^{p}}{p^{p-1}(p-1)(\gamma-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{p\beta}{\gamma+p-1}.

(4)

(ii)

There exists
C>0
such that

|\nabla u(x)|\leq Cd_{\Omega}(x)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ for all }x\in\Omega.

(5)

An interesting point of Theorem
1
is that the limit (
4
) does not depend on
\Omega
,
f
or the appearance of the gradient term
|\nabla u|^{q}
.
As a consequence, we can easily derive the following result by a contradiction argument.

Corollary 2
.

Under assumptions of Theorem
1
, for any
\beta\in(0,1)
, there exist
\delta,c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}d_{\Omega}(x)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\nu}\leq c_{2}d_{\Omega}(x)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ for all }x\in I_{\delta}(\Omega)

whenever
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with
\langle\nu,\eta(x)\rangle\geq\beta
.

Let us present our idea of the proof of Theorem
1
. We first establish a sharp estimate of solutions

c_{1}d_{\Omega}(x)^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq u(x)\leq c_{2}d_{\Omega}(x)^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ for all }x\in\Omega,

(6)

where
c_{2}\geq c_{1}>0
(see Theorem
7
).
This step was done for problem (
2
) in
[
12
]
(see also
[
8
]
for the case
p=2
). For problem (
1
), the main difficulty is that weak comparison principles cannot be derived for the problem with arbitrary
q\in(0,p]
due to the unboundedness of
|\nabla u|^{q}
and
\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}
near the boundary. This situation is very different from other papers on regular quasilinear elliptic problems with gradient terms, such as
[
14
,
23
,
21
]
, where the boundedness of gradient terms is vital in deriving a priori estimates. To overcome this difficulty, we introduce a weak comparison principle involving only
|\nabla u|^{p}
and
\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}
and use it in our comparison arguments. Whenever (
6
) is established, we can use the scaling technique to derive precise estimates for directional derivatives of solutions following some ideas in
[
12
]
but in a new way.

In view of (
6
), we find that
C^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}(\overline{\Omega})
is the optimal boundary Hölder regularity for solutions. Another direct consequence of Theorem
1
and (
6
) is the following.

Corollary 3
.

Under assumptions of Theorem
1
, we have
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
if and only if
\gamma<\frac{2p-1}{p-1}
.

Next, we consider the cases
\gamma=1
and
0<\gamma<1
, which are strikingly different. The Höpf boundary lemma in the spirit of
[
12
]
is not established for these cases, even for problem (
2
) with
p=2
.
We provide a sharp estimate for
\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\nu}
in the case
\gamma=1
as follows.

Theorem 4
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a solution to (
1
) with
\gamma=1
. Then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C^{s}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
0<\alpha<1
and all
s\in(0,1)
. Furthermore, the following assertions hold:

(i)

For any
\beta\in(0,1)
, there exist
0<\delta<1
and
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}\left(1-\ln d_{\Omega}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\nu}\leq c_{2}\left(1-\ln d_{\Omega}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{ for all }x\in I_{\delta}(\Omega)

(7)

whenever
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with
\langle\nu,\eta(x)\rangle\geq\beta
.

(ii)

There exists
L,C>0
such that

|\nabla u(x)|\leq C\left(L-\ln d_{\Omega}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{ for all }x\in\Omega.

(8)

Now we consider the remaining case
0<\gamma<1
, which may be referred to as the “weakly singular case”. We show that in this case, solutions to our two problems behave like the ones of regular problems, namely, the gradient of solutions is bounded near the boundary and the Höpf boundary lemma is valid in the classical sense. More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 5
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a solution to (
1
) with
0<\gamma<1
. Then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Moreover, for every
\beta\in(0,1)
, there exists
c>0
such that

\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\nu}\geq c\quad\text{ for all }x\in\partial\Omega\text{ and }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\text{ with }\langle\nu,\eta(x)\rangle\geq\beta,

(9)

where
\eta(x)
denotes the inward normal of
\Omega
at
x
.

Remark 1
.

Notice that we cannot expect an explicit universal value of
\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\nu}
for
x\in\partial\Omega
as in Theorem
1
because it depends on
\Omega
.
Indeed, let
u
be the unique solution to (
2
) with
f\equiv 0
. Then
w
defined by

w(x)=\delta^{-\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}u(\delta x)\quad\text{ in }\Omega

solves the same equation posed in
\Omega/\delta
, where
\delta>0
, and

\frac{\partial w(x)}{\partial\nu}=\delta^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{\partial u(\delta x)}{\partial\nu}\quad\text{ for all }x\in\partial(\Omega/\delta).

The Höpf boundary lemma is a fundamental tool in the moving plane method as well as in many other applications. This tool has been used in
[
12
]
to prove the symmetry of solutions to problem (
2
) with
\gamma>1
. In the same manner, we have the following symmetry result for problems (
1
) with all
\gamma>0
in bounded symmetric convex domains.

Theorem 6
.

Assume
\max\{\frac{p}{2},1\}\leq q\leq p
and
\gamma>0
.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which is strictly convex in the
x_{1}
-direction and symmetric with respect to the hyperplane
\{x_{1}=0\}
. Let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
be a solution of problem (
1
) or (
2
) with
f(s)>0
for
s>0
. Then it follows that
u
is symmetric with respect to the hyperplane
\{x_{1}=0\}
and increasing in the
x_{1}
-direction in
\Omega\cap\{x_{1}<0\}
.
In particular, if the domain is a ball, then the solution is radially symmetric and monotone decreasing about the center of the ball.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we prove an asymptotic estimate of solutions near the boundary. Through the proof, we also deduce that solutions must lie in the space
C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
. In Section
3
, we prove our main result, namely, Theorems
1
,
4
and
5
on the behaviors of the derivatives of solutions near the boundary. In Section
4
, we apply previous results to show the symmetry of solutions stated in Theorem
6
.

2.
Sharp bounds for solutions

We aim to prove the following result in this section.

Theorem 7
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a solution to (
1
) with
\gamma>0
. Then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Moreover, the following assertions hold:

(i)

When
\gamma>1
, there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}d_{\Omega}(x)^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq u(x)\leq c_{2}d_{\Omega}(x)^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

(ii)

When
\gamma=1
, there exist
L,c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}d_{\Omega}(x)\left(L-\ln d_{\Omega}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq u(x)\leq c_{2}d_{\Omega}(x)\left(L-\ln d_{\Omega}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

(iii)

When
0<\gamma<1
, there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}d_{\Omega}(x)\leq u(x)\leq c_{2}d_{\Omega}(x)\quad\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

Typically, in (ii), we can choose any
L>\max_{x\in\Omega}\ln d_{\Omega}(x)
, then there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, which depend on
L
, such that (ii) holds.

The proof of Theorem (
7
) exploits various types of weak comparison principles.
The first one we require in our later arguments is the following.

Lemma 8

(Lemma 3.1 in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
c>0
.
If
u_{1},u_{2}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u_{1}\leq\frac{c}{u_{1}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p}u_{2}\geq\frac{c}{u_{2}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{1},u_{2}>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{1}\leq u_{2}&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

then
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
in
\Omega
.

Lemma
8
is stated in
[
12
]
for functions with stronger regularity, but its proof there is still valid in our setting. This lemma is useful for problem (
2
). However, to deal with the gradient term
|\nabla u|^{q}
in problem (
1
), the principle stated in Lemma
8
is not enough.
Therefore, we start this subsection with another weak comparison principle, which is of independent interest.

Lemma 9

(Weak comparison principle)
.

Let
c>0
and let
u_{1},u_{2}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u_{1}+\vartheta|\nabla u_{1}|^{p}+\vartheta\leq\frac{c}{u_{1}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p}u_{2}+\vartheta|\nabla u_{2}|^{p}+\vartheta\geq\frac{c}{u_{2}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{1},u_{2}>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{1}\leq u_{2}&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

If
\vartheta>0
, we further assume

u_{1}\leq\left(\frac{c}{\vartheta}\right)^{1/\gamma}\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

Then
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since the case
\vartheta=0
was studied in Lemma
8
, we will assume
\vartheta>0
in what follows.
The main obstacle to this principle is the gradient terms, which may be unbounded near the boundary. We eliminate these terms by employing the following change of variable

v_{i}=e^{-\frac{\vartheta u_{i}}{p-1}},\quad i=1,2.

Then
v_{1},v_{2}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and

e^{-\frac{\vartheta}{p-1}\left(\frac{c}{\vartheta}\right)^{1/\gamma}}\leq v_{1}<1,\quad e^{-\frac{\vartheta\|u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{p-1}}\leq v_{2}<1\quad\text{ in }\Omega

in the weak sense. Also in the weak sense, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}v_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\vartheta v_{1}}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}(-\Delta_{p}u_{1}+\vartheta|\nabla u_{1}|^{p})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\vartheta v_{1}}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left(\frac{c}{u_{1}^{\gamma}}-\vartheta\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\vartheta v_{1}}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left(\frac{c}{(-\frac{p-1}{\vartheta}\ln v_{1})^{\gamma}}-\vartheta\right)\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

Similarly,

\Delta_{p}v_{2}\geq\left(\frac{\vartheta v_{2}}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left(\frac{c}{(-\frac{p-1}{\vartheta}\ln v_{2})^{\gamma}}-\vartheta\right)\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

Therefore,
v_{1},v_{2}
solve

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}v_{1}\leq f(v_{1})&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\Delta_{p}v_{2}\geq f(v_{2})&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
0<v_{1},v_{2}<1&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
v_{2}\leq v_{1}&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(10)

where

f(t)=\left(\frac{\vartheta t}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left(\frac{c}{(-\frac{p-1}{\vartheta}\ln t)^{\gamma}}-\vartheta\right),\quad t\in(0,1).

Due to the boundary condition, we can use
w=(v_{2}-v_{1}-\varepsilon)^{+}
as a test function in the first two inequalities of (
10
). Then subtracting, we derive

\int_{\Omega}\langle|\nabla v_{2}|^{p-2}\nabla v_{2}-|\nabla v_{1}|^{p-2}\nabla v_{1},\nabla w\rangle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(f(v_{1})-f(v_{2})\right)w.

We recall the following well-known elementary inequality: there exists a positive constant
\Lambda
depending only on
N,p>1
such that

\langle|\xi|^{p-2}\xi-|\xi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\xi^{\prime},\,\xi-\xi^{\prime}\rangle\geq\Lambda(|\xi|+|\xi^{\prime}|)^{p-2}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{2}

(11)

for all
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|\xi|+|\xi^{\prime}|>0
.
In view of (
11
), we have

\Lambda\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla v_{1}|+|\nabla v_{2}|)^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(f(v_{1})-f(v_{2})\right)w.

(12)

On the other hand,

f^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\vartheta^{p-1}t^{p-2}}{(p-1)^{p-2}}\left[\left(\frac{c}{\left(-\frac{p-1}{\vartheta}\ln t\right)^{\gamma}}-\vartheta\right)+\frac{c\gamma\vartheta^{\gamma}}{\left(-\frac{p-1}{\vartheta}\ln t\right)^{\gamma+1}}\right]\quad\text{for }t\in(0,1).

Hence
f^{\prime}(t)>0
whenever
f(t)\geq 0
. Moreover,

f(t)\geq 0\iff e^{-\frac{\vartheta}{p-1}\left(\frac{c}{\vartheta}\right)^{1/\gamma}}\leq t<1.

Thus,
f
is increasing on
\left[e^{-\frac{\vartheta}{p-1}\left(\frac{c}{\vartheta}\right)^{1/\gamma}},1\right)
. Notice now that in
\Omega\cap\{w>0\}
, we have

e^{-\frac{\vartheta}{p-1}\left(\frac{c}{\vartheta}\right)^{1/\gamma}}\leq v_{1}<v_{1}+\varepsilon<v_{2}<1.

Hence

f(v_{1})-f(v_{2})<0.

Therefore, (
12
) yields

\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla v_{1}|+|\nabla v_{2}|)^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}=0.

Consequently,
v_{2}\leq v_{1}+\varepsilon
in
\Omega
. Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we get
v_{2}\leq v_{1}
in
\Omega
. This means
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
in
\Omega
.
∎

We prepare the following lemma for later use in dealing with the case
0<\gamma<1
.

Lemma 10
.

Assume
0<\gamma<1
. Then for every
M>0
and
r_{2}>r_{1}>0
, the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\frac{M}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}},\\
u>0&\text{ in }B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial(B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}})\end{cases}

(13)

admits a unique solution
U\in C^{1,\alpha}(B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}})\cap C(\overline{B_{r_{2}}\setminus{B_{r_{1}}}})
. Moreover, there exists
C>0
such that

cd_{B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}}(x)\leq U(x)\leq Cd_{B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}}(x)\quad\text{ for all }x\in B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}.

Proof.

The existence of a unique solution
U
to (
13
) is proved in
[
9
,
6
]
. Hence
U
is radial. By abuse of notation, we also write
U(x)=U(r)
, where
r=|x|
. We claim that, for some
c>0
,

U(r)\geq c\min\{r-r_{1},r_{2}-r\}\quad\text{ for all }r\in(r_{1},r_{2}).

(14)

To prove this, let
\lambda_{1}>0
be the first eigenvalue and
\phi_{1}\in C^{1}(\overline{B_{r_{2}}}\setminus{B_{r_{1}}})
a corresponding positive eigenfunction of the
p
-Laplacian in
B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}
with the Dirichlet boundary condition. Then we set

V_{s}=s\phi_{1},

where
s>0
. We have

-\Delta_{p}V_{s}=\frac{h_{s}(x)}{V_{s}^{\gamma}},

where

h_{s}(x)=\lambda_{1}s^{\gamma+p-1}\phi_{1}^{\gamma+p-1}.

Let
\tilde{s}>0
be small such that

V_{\tilde{s}}(x)\leq M\quad\text{ for }x\in B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}.

We have now

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}U=\frac{M}{U^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}},\\
-\Delta_{p}V_{\tilde{s}}\leq\frac{M}{V_{\tilde{s}}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}},\\
U,V_{\tilde{s}}>0&\text{ in }B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}},\\
U=V_{\tilde{s}}=0&\text{ on }\partial(B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}).\end{cases}

Hence Lemma
8
gives

V_{\tilde{s}}\leq U\quad\text{ in }B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}.

This implies

U(x)\geq cd_{B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}}(x)\quad\text{ in }B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}

for some
c>0
. Hence (
14
) is verified.

Since
U
is radial, exploiting (
13
) and (
14
), we get

-\frac{1}{r^{N-1}}(r^{N-1}|U^{\prime}(r)|^{p-2}U^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}=\frac{M}{U(r)^{\gamma}}\leq\frac{M}{c^{\gamma}(r-r_{1})^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ for }r_{1}<r\leq\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{2}.

Integrating this, we deduce

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq r_{1}^{N-1}|U^{\prime}(r_{1})|^{p-2}U^{\prime}(r_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r_{1}}^{\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{2}}\frac{Mr^{N-1}}{c^{\gamma}(r-r_{1})^{\gamma}}dr+\left(\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{2}\right)^{N-1}\left|U^{\prime}\left(\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{p-2}U^{\prime}\left(\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle<+\infty,

thanks to the fact
\gamma<1
. Thus there exists
C>0
such that

U(r)\leq C(r-r_{1})\quad\text{ for all }r\in(r_{1},r_{2}).

Similarly, by integrating on
[r,r_{2}]\subset\left[\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{2},r_{2}\right]
, we have
for some
C^{\prime}>0
,

U(r)\leq C^{\prime}(r_{2}-r)\quad\text{ for all }r\in(r_{1},r_{2}).

The lemma is proved.
∎

We are ready to provide a proof of Theorem
7
.

Proof of Theorem
7
.

Since
\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)
is bounded in each compact set
K\subset\subset\Omega
, we have that
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
by standard elliptic regularity.

Let
\lambda_{1}>0
and
\phi_{1}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be the first eigenvalue and a corresponding positive eigenfunction of the
p
-Laplacian in
\Omega
with the Dirichlet boundary condition, namely,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}\phi_{1}=\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{p-1}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\phi_{1}>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\phi_{1}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

We consider three cases.

Case 1:
\gamma>1
.
Setting

w_{s}=s\phi_{1}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}},

where
s>0
. A direct calculation yields

-\Delta_{p}w_{s}=\frac{a_{s}(x)}{w_{s}^{\gamma}},

where

a_{s}(x)=s^{\gamma+p-1}\left(\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left[\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}(x)^{p}+\frac{(\gamma-1)(p-1)}{\gamma+p-1}|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)|^{p}\right].

Since
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we can find
M>0
sufficiently large such that

\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)<\frac{M}{u^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

(15)

On the other hand, by the positivity of
\phi_{1}
and the Hopf boundary lemma for
\phi_{1}
, we know that

\inf_{x\in\Omega}\left[\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}(x)^{p}+\frac{(\gamma-1)(p-1)}{\gamma+p-1}|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)|^{p}\right]>0.

Hence, we can choose some large
s_{1}>0
such that

a_{s_{1}}(x)>M\quad\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

We have now

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u\leq\frac{M}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p}w_{s_{1}}\geq\frac{M}{w_{s_{1}}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
w_{s_{1}},u>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
w_{s_{1}}=u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Lemma
8
can be applied to yield

u\leq w_{s_{1}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

This implies
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and

u\leq Cd_{\Omega}(x)^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ for some }C>0.

Next, we prove the lower bound. To this end, we write

-\Delta_{p}w_{s}+\vartheta|\nabla w_{s}|^{p}+\vartheta=\frac{b_{s}(x)}{w_{s}^{\gamma}},

where

\displaystyle b_{s}(x)

\displaystyle=a_{s}(x)+\vartheta(|\nabla w_{s}|^{p}+1)w_{s}

\displaystyle=s^{\gamma+p-1}\left(\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left[\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{p}+\left(\frac{\vartheta sp}{\gamma+p-1}\phi_{1}^{p}+\frac{(\gamma-1)(p-1)}{\gamma+p-1}\right)|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\vartheta s^{\gamma}\phi_{1}^{\frac{\gamma p}{\gamma+p-1}}.

Exploiting
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
proved above and the Dirichlet boundary condition, we can find
\delta>0
sufficiently small such that

\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)>\frac{1}{2u^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ for }x\in I_{\delta}(\Omega).

We also choose some small
s_{2}>0
such that

w_{s_{2}}(x)\leq\left(\frac{1}{2\vartheta}\right)^{1/\gamma},\quad b_{s_{2}}(x)<\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{ for }x\in I_{\delta}(\Omega)

and

w_{s_{2}}(x)\leq u(x)\quad\text{ for }x\in\partial I_{\delta}(\Omega)\setminus\partial\Omega.

Since
0<q\leq p
, it follows
|\nabla u|^{q}<|\nabla u|^{p}+1
. Therefore, we have

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}w_{s_{2}}+\vartheta|\nabla w_{s_{2}}|^{p}+\vartheta\leq\frac{1}{2w_{s_{2}}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega),\\
-\Delta_{p}u+\vartheta|\nabla u|^{p}+\vartheta\geq\frac{1}{2u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega),\\
w_{s_{2}},u>0&\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega),\\
w_{s_{2}}\leq u&\text{ on }\partial I_{\delta}(\Omega),\end{cases}

Lemma
9
can be applied now to yield

w_{s_{2}}\leq u\quad\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega).

By compactness arguments, this implies

u\geq cd_{\Omega}(x)^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ for some }c>0.

Case 2:
0<\gamma<1
.
This case is also treated via comparison principles, but with a different choice of barrier functions. The upper barrier based on the power of the first eigenfunction simply do not work. Instead, we use the one from Lemma
10
.

Let
M>0
be as in (
15
). Now we fix
r_{1}>0
small such that for every
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, there is a ball
B_{r_{1}}(y_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
that touch
\Omega
at
x_{0}
. We also fix
r_{2}=\text{diam}(\Omega)+r_{1}
, where

\text{diam}(\Omega)=\sup_{x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega}|x_{1}-x_{2}|

is the diameter of
\Omega
. Let
U
be defined as in Lemma
10
.
Consider any
x\in\Omega
and let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
be such that
d_{\Omega}(x)=|x-x_{0}|
. Let
B_{r_{1}}(y_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
be the ball of radius
r_{1}
that touch
\Omega
at
x_{0}
, then

\Omega\subset B_{r_{2}}(y_{0})\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}(y_{0})}.

Moreover,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u\leq\frac{M}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p}U_{y_{0}}=\frac{M}{U_{y_{0}}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u,U_{y_{0}}>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
0=u\leq U_{y_{0}}&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
U_{y_{0}}(x):=U(x-y_{0})
. Hence by Lemma
8
, we deduce

u\leq U_{y_{0}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

Now Lemma
10
gives

u(x)\leq Cd_{B_{r_{1}}(y_{0})}(x)=Cd_{\Omega}(x)\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

In particular, this implies that
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
.

To derive the lower bound, we set

v_{s}=s\phi_{1},

where
s>0
and we recall that
\phi_{1}
is a first positive eigenfunction of the
p
-Laplacian in
\Omega
. We have

-\Delta_{p}v_{s}+\vartheta|\nabla v_{s}|^{p}+\vartheta=\frac{d_{s}(x)}{v_{s}^{\gamma}},

where

d_{s}(x)=s^{p+\gamma-1}\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{p+\gamma-1}+\vartheta s^{p+\gamma}\phi_{1}^{\gamma}|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{p}+\vartheta s^{\gamma}\phi_{1}^{\gamma}.

Since
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we can find
\delta>0
sufficiently small such that

\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)>\frac{1}{2u^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ for }x\in I_{\delta}(\Omega).

We also choose some small
s_{0}>0
such that

v_{s_{0}}(x)\leq\left(\frac{1}{2\vartheta}\right)^{1/\gamma},\quad d_{s_{0}}(x)<\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{ for }x\in I_{\delta}(\Omega)

and

v_{s_{0}}(x)\leq u(x)\quad\text{ for }x\in\partial I_{\delta}(\Omega)\setminus\partial\Omega.

We have now

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u+\vartheta|\nabla u|^{p}+\vartheta\geq\frac{1}{2u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega),\\
-\Delta_{p}v_{s_{0}}+\vartheta|\nabla v_{s_{0}}|^{p}+\vartheta\leq\frac{1}{2v_{s_{0}}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega),\\
u,v_{s_{0}}>0&\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega),\\
v_{s_{0}}\leq u&\text{ on }\partial I_{\delta}(\Omega).\end{cases}

Hence Lemma
9
gives

v_{s_{0}}\leq u\quad\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega).

From this and the compactness argument, we obtain

u(x)\geq cd_{\Omega}(x)\quad\text{ in }\Omega

for some
c>0
.

Case 3:
\gamma=1
.

Setting

w_{s}=s\phi_{1}(1-\ln\phi_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
s>0
and
\phi_{1}
is the first positive eigenfunction of the
p
-Laplacian in
\Omega
such that
\|\phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
. After tedious computations, we obtain

-\Delta_{p}w_{s}=\frac{a_{s}(x)}{w_{s}},

where

\displaystyle a_{s}(x)

\displaystyle=s^{p}\left[z\left(z-\frac{1}{p}z^{1-p}\right)^{p-1}\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{p}+\frac{p-1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}z^{-p}\right)^{p-2}\left(1+\frac{p-1}{p}z^{-p}\right)|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{p}\right]

with
z:=(1-\ln\phi_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}
. The range of
z
is
[1,+\infty)
. Moreover, by L’Hôpital’s rule, we have
\phi_{1}z^{k}\to 0
as
d_{\Omega}(x)\to 0
for any
k>0
. Hence, both terms in the square bracket are bounded above in
\Omega
. Their sum is also bounded below by a positive constant.

Let
M>0
as in (
15
) and choose some large
s_{1}>0
such that

a_{s_{1}}(x)>M\quad\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

We have now

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u\leq\frac{M}{u}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p}w_{s_{1}}\geq\frac{M}{w_{s_{1}}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
w_{s_{1}},u>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
w_{s_{1}}=u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Lemma
8
can be applied to yield

u\leq w_{s_{1}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

This implies
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and

u\leq Cd_{\Omega}(x)\left(L-\ln d_{\Omega}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ for some }C,L>0.

Next, we prove the lower bound. To this end, we write

-\Delta_{p}w_{s}+\vartheta|\nabla w_{s}|^{p}+\vartheta=\frac{b_{s}(x)}{w_{s}},

where

\displaystyle b_{s}(x)

\displaystyle=a_{s}(x)+\vartheta(|\nabla w_{s}|^{p}+1)w_{s}

\displaystyle=s^{p}\left[z\left(z-\frac{1}{p}z^{1-p}\right)^{p-1}\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{p}+\frac{p-1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}z^{-p}\right)^{p-2}\left(1+\frac{p-1}{p}z^{-p}\right)|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\vartheta s^{q+1}\phi_{1}z\left(z-\frac{1}{p}z^{1-p}\right)^{q}|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{q}.

Exploiting
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and the Dirichlet boundary condition, we find
\delta>0
sufficiently small such that

\frac{1}{u}+f(u)>\frac{1}{2u}\quad\text{ for }x\in I_{\delta}(\Omega).

We also choose some small
s_{2}>0
such that

w_{s_{2}}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2\vartheta},\quad b_{s_{2}}(x)<\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{ for }x\in I_{\delta}(\Omega)

and

w_{s_{2}}(x)\leq u(x)\quad\text{ for }x\in\partial I_{\delta}(\Omega)\setminus\partial\Omega.

We have now

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}w_{s_{2}}+\vartheta|\nabla w_{s_{2}}|^{p}+\vartheta\leq\frac{1}{2w_{s_{2}}}&\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega),\\
-\Delta_{p}u+\vartheta|\nabla u|^{p}+\vartheta\geq\frac{1}{2u}&\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega),\\
w_{s_{2}},u>0&\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega),\\
w_{s_{2}}\leq u&\text{ on }\partial I_{\delta}(\Omega),\end{cases}

Lemma
9
can be applied now to yield

w_{s_{2}}\leq u\quad\text{ in }I_{\delta}(\Omega).

By compactness arguments, this implies

u\geq cd_{\Omega}(x)\left(L-\ln d_{\Omega}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ for some }c>0.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
7
.
∎

3.
Directional derivative estimates

In this section, we prove the main results of the paper, namely, Theorems
1
,
4
and
5
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

By Theorem
7
,
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
for some
0<\alpha<1
.

(i) First, we prove (
4
). Suppose by contradiction that (
4
) does not hold, then there exist
\varepsilon>0
and subsequences, still denoted by
(x_{n})
and
(\nu_{n})
, such that

\left|\delta_{n}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{\partial u(x_{n})}{\partial\nu_{n}}-\left(\frac{(\gamma+p-1)^{p}}{p^{p-1}(p-1)(\gamma-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{p\beta}{\gamma+p-1}\right|\geq\varepsilon,

(16)

where we denote

\delta_{n}=d_{\Omega}(x_{n}).

We start setting up the problem in convenient coordinates as in
[
12
]
.
Given that the domain is of class
C^{2}
, we can and do restrict our attention to a neighborhood of the boundary where the unique nearest point property is valid. Hence for large
n
, there is a unique point
\hat{x}_{n}
such that
|x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}|=d_{\Omega}(x_{n})
.
For each
n
, we choose a new coordinate such that in this coordinate,
\hat{x}_{n}=0
and
\eta(x_{n})=e_{N}
. More precisely, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can define an isometry
T_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
that possesses the aforementioned properties by simply combining a translation and a rotation of the coordinate axes. This process generates a new sequence of points
y_{n}:=T_{n}(x_{n})=\delta_{n}e_{N}
. Defining
u_{n}(y):=u(T_{n}^{-1}(y))
, it follows that

-\Delta_{p}u_{n}+\vartheta|\nabla u_{n}|^{q}=\frac{1}{u_{n}^{\gamma}}+f(u_{n})\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{n},

where
\Omega_{n}=T_{n}(\Omega)
. By Theorem
7
, there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that for any
n
,

c_{1}d_{\Omega_{n}}(x)^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq u_{n}(x)\leq c_{2}d_{\Omega_{n}}(x)^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{n}.

(17)

Now we set

w_{n}(y):=\delta_{n}^{-\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}u_{n}(\delta_{n}y)\quad\text{ for }y\in\tilde{\Omega}_{n},

where

\tilde{\Omega}_{n}:=\Omega_{n}/\delta_{n}.

Then
w_{n}
weakly solves

-\Delta_{p}w_{n}+\delta_{n}^{\frac{\gamma(p-q)+q}{\gamma+p-1}}\vartheta|\nabla w_{n}|^{q}=\frac{1}{w_{n}^{\gamma}}+\delta_{n}^{\frac{p\gamma}{\gamma+p-1}}f\left(\delta_{n}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}w_{n}\right)\quad\text{ in }\tilde{\Omega}_{n}.

(18)

We claim that up to a subsequence

w_{n}\to w_{\infty}\text{ in }C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty\text{ for some }0<\alpha^{\prime}<1.

(19)

To prove this, let any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. For
n
large we have
K\subset\tilde{\Omega}_{n}
, or equivalently,
\delta_{n}K\subset\Omega_{n}
. Hence for any
y\in K
, we can apply (
17
) to get

c_{1}\delta_{n}^{-\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}d_{\Omega_{n}}(\delta_{n}y)^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq w_{n}(y)\leq c_{2}\delta_{n}^{-\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}d_{\Omega_{n}}(\delta_{n}y)^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}.

On the other hand, we have

d_{\Omega_{n}}(\delta_{n}y)=\delta_{n}d_{\tilde{\Omega}_{n}}(y)

and

d_{\tilde{\Omega}_{n}}(y)\to d_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}(y)=y_{N}.

Here we use the fact that
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}
leads to the limiting domain
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
as
n\to\infty
. Consequently, for large
n
,

\frac{c_{1}}{2}y_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq w_{n}(y)\leq 2c_{2}y_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ for all }y\in K.

(20)

Hence for any any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, we have
C_{1}(K)\leq w_{n}(y)\leq C_{2}(K)
for all
y\in K
, where
C_{1}(K),C_{2}(K)
are two positive constants depending on
K
. Thus, the right-hand side of (
18
) is uniformly bounded in
K
for large
n
. By standard regularity theory
[
10
,
25
]
, it follows that
(w_{n})
is uniformly bounded in
C^{1,\alpha}(K)
. Exploiting Ascoli’s Theorem and a standard diagonal process, by passing to a subsequence we derive
w_{n}\to w_{\infty}\text{ in }C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
as
n\to\infty\text{ for }0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
. Hence (
19
) is proved.

Letting
n\to\infty
in (
20
), we deduce

\frac{c_{1}}{2}y_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq w_{\infty}(y)\leq 2c_{2}y_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ for }y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

In particular, we can extend
w_{\infty}
by zero on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, then
w_{\infty}\in C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
. Letting
n\to\infty
in (
18
), we have that
w_{\infty}
weakly solves

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}w_{\infty}=\frac{1}{w_{\infty}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
w_{\infty}>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
w_{\infty}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.\end{cases}

By
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
, we have

w_{\infty}(x)\equiv\left(\frac{(\gamma+p-1)^{p}}{p^{p-1}(p-1)(\gamma-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma+p-1}}x_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}.

On the other hand, setting
\tilde{\nu}_{n}=T_{n}(\nu_{n})\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, we have

\langle\tilde{\nu}_{n},e_{N}\rangle=\langle\nu_{n},\eta(x_{n})\rangle\to\beta.

Up to a subsequence, we may assume
\tilde{\nu}_{n}\to\tilde{\nu_{0}}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with
\langle\tilde{\nu_{0}},e_{N}\rangle=\beta
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\delta_{n}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{\partial u(x_{n})}{\partial\nu_{n}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{n}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{\partial u_{n}(y_{n})}{\partial\tilde{\nu}_{n}}=\frac{\partial w_{n}(e_{N})}{\partial\tilde{\nu}_{n}}

\displaystyle\to\frac{\partial w_{\infty}(e_{N})}{\partial\tilde{\nu_{0}}}=\left(\frac{(\gamma+p-1)^{p}}{p^{p-1}(p-1)(\gamma-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{p\beta}{\gamma+p-1}.

However, this contradicts (
16
).
Hence (
4
) is proved.

(ii) Now we prove (
5
). Suppose by contradiction that there exists a sequence of points
x_{n}\in\Omega
such that

\delta_{n}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}|\nabla u(x_{n})|\to+\infty\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty,

(21)

where
\delta_{n}:=d_{\Omega}(x_{n})
. Notice that for any
\delta>0
we have

0<\inf_{\Omega\setminus I_{\delta}(\Omega)}u\leq u(x)\leq\sup_{\Omega}u<+\infty\text{ for }x\in\Omega\setminus I_{\delta}(\Omega).

Hence
\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)
is bounded in
\Omega\setminus I_{\delta}(\Omega)
. By standard gradient estimates, we deduce that
|\nabla u|
is bounded in
\Omega\setminus I_{\delta}(\Omega)
for any
\delta>0
. Therefore, (
21
) implies

\delta_{n}\to 0.

(22)

With (
22
) in force, we can repeat the above arguments to construct
(u_{n})
,
(w_{n})
and
w_{\infty}
. Then

|\nabla w_{n}(e_{N})|=\delta_{n}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}|\nabla u_{n}(y_{n})|=\delta_{n}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}|\nabla u(x_{n})|\to+\infty\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

However, this contradicts the fact

|\nabla w_{n}(e_{N})|\to|\nabla w_{\infty}(e_{N})|<+\infty.

Hence (
5
) is proved.

Lastly, from (
5
) and Theorem
7
, we deduce

\left|\nabla\left(u^{\frac{\gamma+p-1}{p}}\right)\right|=\frac{\gamma+p-1}{p}u^{\frac{\gamma-1}{p}}|\nabla u|\leq C\frac{\gamma+p-1}{p}c_{2}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{p}}.

Hence
u^{\frac{\gamma+p-1}{p}}
is Lipschitz continuous.
This indicates
u\in C^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}(\overline{\Omega})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Suppose by contradiction that there exist
\beta>0
, a sequence of points
x_{n}\in\Omega
and a sequence of normal vectors
\nu_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with
\langle\nu_{n},\eta(x_{n})\rangle\geq\beta
such that

\left(1-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\partial u(x_{n})}{\partial\nu_{n}}\to 0\text{ and }\delta_{n}\to 0\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty,

(23)

where
\delta_{n}:=d_{\Omega}(x_{n})
.
We set up the problem in convenient coordinates as in the proof of Theorem
1
. We also reuse the relevant notions in that proof.
By Theorem
7
,
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
for some
0<\alpha<1
and there exist
L,c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that for any
n
,

c_{1}d_{\Omega_{n}}(x)\left(L-\ln d_{\Omega_{n}}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq u_{n}(x)\leq c_{2}d_{\Omega_{n}}(x)\left(L-\ln d_{\Omega_{n}}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{n}.

(24)

Now we set

w_{n}(y):=\delta_{n}^{-1}\left(L-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}u_{n}(\delta_{n}y)\quad\text{ for }y\in\tilde{\Omega}_{n}:=\Omega_{n}/\delta_{n}.

Then

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}w_{n}+\delta_{n}\left(L-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{\frac{q-p+1}{p}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{q}

(25)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(L-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{-1}}{w_{n}}+\delta_{n}\left(L-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}f\left(\delta_{n}\left(L-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}w_{n}\right)\quad\text{ in }\tilde{\Omega}_{n}.

We claim that up to a subsequence

w_{n}\to w_{\infty}\text{ in }C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty\text{ for some }0<\alpha^{\prime}<1.

(26)

To prove this, let any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. For
n
large we have
K\subset\tilde{\Omega}_{n}
, or equivalently,
\delta_{n}K\subset\Omega_{n}
. Hence for any
y\in K
, we can apply (
24
) to get

c_{1}\frac{d_{\Omega_{n}}(\delta_{n}y)}{\delta_{n}}\left(\frac{L-\ln d_{\Omega_{n}}(\delta_{n}y)}{L-\ln\delta_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq w_{n}(y)\leq c_{2}\frac{d_{\Omega_{n}}(\delta_{n}y)}{\delta_{n}}\left(\frac{L-\ln d_{\Omega_{n}}(\delta_{n}y)}{L-\ln\delta_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Now exploiting

d_{\Omega_{n}}(\delta_{n}y)=\delta_{n}d_{\tilde{\Omega}_{n}}(y)

and

d_{\tilde{\Omega}_{n}}(y)\to d_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}(y)=y_{N},\quad\delta_{n}\to 0,

we obtain for large
n
,

\frac{c_{1}}{2}y_{N}\leq w_{n}(y)\leq 2c_{2}y_{N}\quad\text{ for }y\in K.

(27)

Thus, the right-hand side of (
25
) is uniformly bounded in
K
for large
n
. By standard regularity theory
[
10
,
25
]
, it follows that
(w_{n})
is uniformly bounded in
C^{1,\alpha}(K)
. Exploiting Ascoli’s Theorem and a standard diagonal process, by passing to a subsequence we derive
w_{n}\to w_{\infty}\text{ in }C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
as
n\to\infty\text{ for }0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
. Hence (
26
) is proved.

Letting
n\to\infty
in (
27
), we deduce

\frac{c_{1}}{2}y_{N}\leq w_{\infty}(y)\leq 2c_{2}y_{N}\quad\text{ for }y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

In particular, we can extend
w_{\infty}
by zero on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, then
w_{\infty}\in C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
. Letting
n\to\infty
in (
25
), we have that
w_{\infty}
weakly solves

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}w_{\infty}=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
w_{\infty}>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
w_{\infty}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.\end{cases}

By
[

, Theorem 3.1]
, we have

w_{\infty}(x)\equiv ax_{N},

for some

.
On the other hand, setting
\tilde{\nu}_{n}=T_{n}(\nu_{n})\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, we have

\langle\tilde{\nu}_{n},e_{N}\rangle=\langle\nu_{n},\eta(x_{n}).

Up to a subsequence, we may assume
\tilde{\nu}_{n}\to\tilde{\nu_{0}}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with
\langle\tilde{\nu_{0}},e_{N}\rangle\geq\beta
.
Therefore,

\left(L-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\partial u(x_{n})}{\partial\nu_{n}}=\left(L-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\partial u_{n}(y_{n})}{\partial\tilde{\nu}_{n}}=\frac{\partial w_{n}(e_{N})}{\partial\tilde{\nu}_{n}}\to\frac{\partial w_{\infty}(e_{N})}{\partial\tilde{\nu_{0}}}=a\beta.

However, this contradicts (
23
).
Hence, the lower bound in (
7
) is proved.

Now we prove (
8
), which also implies the upper bound in (
7
). Suppose by contradiction that there exists a sequence of points
x_{n}\in\Omega
such that

\left(L-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}|\nabla u(x_{n})|\to+\infty\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty,

where
\delta_{n}:=d_{\Omega}(x_{n})
. This implies
\delta_{n}\to 0
. We can repeat the above arguments to construct
(u_{n})
,
(w_{n})
and
w_{\infty}

|\nabla w_{n}(e_{N})|=\left(L-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}|\nabla u_{n}(y_{n})|=\left(L-\ln\delta_{n}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}|\nabla u(x_{n})|\to+\infty.

However, this contradicts the fact

|\nabla w_{n}(e_{N})|\to|\nabla w_{\infty}(e_{N})|<+\infty.

Hence (

Lastly, from (

\left|\nabla\left(u^{\frac{1}{s}}\right)\right|=su^{\frac{1}{s}-1}|\nabla u|\leq C^{\prime}d_{\Omega}(x)^{\frac{1}{s}-1}\left(L-\ln d_{\Omega}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{ps}}<C^{\prime\prime}

Proof of Theorem

4.
Symmetry of solutions

In this section, we prove the symmetry of solutions to problem (
1
). We provide the details needed for the application of the moving plane method developed in
[
23
]
for quasilinear elliptic problems with a gradient term.

We recall some basic notation that will be used in this section. For any real number
\lambda
we set

\Omega_{\lambda}=\{x\in\Omega\mid x_{1}<\lambda\}.

We denote

x_{\lambda}=R_{\lambda}(x)=(2\lambda-x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),

which is the reflection of
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
through the hyperplane

T_{\lambda}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid x_{1}=\lambda\}.

We also let

a=\inf_{x\in\Omega}x_{1}

and set

u_{\lambda}(x)=u(x_{\lambda}).

Finally, we define

\Lambda_{0}=\{a<\lambda<0\mid u\leq u_{t}\text{ in }\Omega_{t}\text{ for all }t\in(a,\lambda)\}.

In what follows, we focus on the set of critical points of
u
, defined by

Z_{u}:=\{\nabla u=0\}.

This set plays a key role in our analysis. From Corollary
2
, Theorems
4
and
5
, it follows that

Z_{u}\subset\Omega.

This inclusion is significant, as it enables the application of the results in
[
23
]
. Indeed, the solution remains positive throughout the interior of the domain, where the nonlinearity no longer exhibits singular behavior. As a consequence, we deduce that

|Z_{u}|=0\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega\setminus Z_{u}\text{ is connected}.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

The argument is based on the moving plane method. We begin by establishing that

\Lambda_{0}\neq\emptyset.

To do so, let us take a value
\lambda>a
with
\lambda-a
sufficiently small. According to Corollary
2
, Theorems
4
and
5
, we have

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}>0\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\lambda}\cup R_{\lambda}(\Omega_{\lambda}),

which directly implies that
u<u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega_{\lambda}
. Next, we define

\lambda_{0}:=\sup\Lambda_{0}.

Our goal is to prove that
u\leq u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in(a,0]
, that is,

\lambda_{0}=0.

To prove this, we proceed by contradiction. Suppose
\lambda_{0}<0
. We aim to show that this leads to a contradiction by proving

u\leq u_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}

for every
0<\tau<\bar{\tau}
, where
\bar{\tau}>0
is suitably small. By the continuity, we already know that
u\leq u_{\lambda_{0}}
in
\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}
.
Moreover, the strong comparison principle (cf.
[
24
]
) applies in
\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus Z_{u}
, which yields

u<u_{\lambda_{0}}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus Z_{u}.

(29)

Indeed, for each connected component
\mathcal{C}\subset\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus Z_{u}
, the strong comparison principle ensures that
u<u_{\lambda_{0}}
throughout
\mathcal{C}
, unless
u\equiv u_{\lambda_{0}}
in
\mathcal{C}
. However, the latter case can be ruled out as follows. If
\partial\mathcal{C}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
, then
u\equiv u_{\lambda_{0}}
is not possible due to the zero Dirichlet boundary condition and the positivity of
u
in the interior. On the other hand, if
\partial\mathcal{C}\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
, then this would imply the existence of a local symmetry region, contradicting the earlier remark that
\Omega\setminus Z_{u}
is connected.

As a consequence of (
29
), for any compact set
K\subset\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus Z_{u}
, the uniform continuity of
u
indicates that

u<u_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}\quad\text{in }K

(30)

for all
0<\tau<\bar{\tau}
, with
\bar{\tau}>0
sufficiently small. In addition, using Corollary
2
, Theorems
4
and
5
and the fact that
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, it is straightforward to verify that there exists
\delta>0
such that

u<u_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}\quad\text{in }I_{\delta}(\Omega)\cap\Omega_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}

(31)

for every
0<\tau<\bar{\tau}
. This conclusion follows from standard arguments once Corollary
2
, Theorems
4
and
5
are assumed.
The more delicate part of the proof concerns the behavior near the region
\partial\Omega\cap T_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
. In this case, we rely on the monotonicity properties of solutions established in Corollary
2
, Theorems
4
and
5
. These properties become applicable once we observe that, due to the smoothness and strict convexity of the domain, the inward unit normal vector
\eta(x)
satisfies

\langle e_{1},\eta(x)\rangle>0

in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega\cap T_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
. Now we define

w_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}:=(u-u_{\lambda_{0}+\tau})^{+}

for all
0<\tau<\bar{\tau}
, where
\bar{\tau}>0
is chosen small. From (
31
), we already know that

\operatorname{supp}(w_{\lambda_{0}+\tau})\subset\subset\Omega_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
.
Additionally, by (
30
), we have
w_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}=0
on the compact set
K
.

Given any fixed
\tau>0
, we can select
\bar{\tau}
small enough and choose
K
large enough so that

|\Omega_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}\setminus K|<\tau.

This step also relies on the fact that the critical set
Z_{u}
has zero Lebesgue measure (cf.
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
).

Next, we take
\tau
sufficiently small so that the weak comparison principle in small domains (see
[
23
, Proposition 4.1]
) applies. This yields

w_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}

for all
0<\tau<\bar{\tau}
, for some small
\bar{\tau}>0
. This conclusion contradicts the definition of
\lambda_{0}
, and thus we must have

\lambda_{0}=0.

The symmetry and monotonicity result then follows by repeating the same argument, now applied in the opposite direction.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

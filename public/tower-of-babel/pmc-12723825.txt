Relationship between mindful attention awareness and perception of individualized care among nursing students: the mediating role of empathic tendencies
Authors: Aysun Bayram, Aysel Özsaban, Şengül Üzen Cura
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNursing students’ mindful attention awareness and empathic tendencies are essential for the sustainability of individualized care.AimTo investigate the mediating effect of empathic tendencies on the relationship between mindful attention awareness and perception of individualized care among nursing students.MethodsThis cross-sectional study involved 546 nursing students from two nursing faculties in Türkiye. Participants completed a sociodemographic questionnaire, the Individualized Care Scale, the Mindful Attention Awareness Scale, and the Empathic Tendency Scale between September 2023 and June 2024. Correlation and mediation analyses were applied to investigate the meditating role of empathic tendencies in the correlations between mindful attention awareness and perception of individualized care.ResultsMindful attention awareness had a significant effect on empathic tendencies (β = 1.516, p = 0.001) and perception of individualized care (β = 4.601, p = 0.002). Empathic tendencies positively affected the perception of individualized care (β = 0.627, p = 0.001). Empathic tendencies played a mediating role between mindful attention awareness and perception of individualized care (β = 4.245, 95% confidence interval 0.002, 0.014).ConclusionsThe study adds evidence on the mechanisms of action between mindful attention awareness and perception of individualized care among nursing students and empathic tendency. This study’s results showed that empathic tendency mediates the relationship between mindful attention awareness and perception of individualized care.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Empathic tendency, Individualized care, Mindful attention awareness, Nursing students

Introduction

Individualized care (IC) is the planning and implementation of nursing care in line with the individual characteristics, needs, preferences, experiences, feelings, perceptions and opinions, and the inclusion of the individual in this process [1]. At the core of IC is the recognition that each individual is unique and that care should be tailored to this uniqueness. Furthermore, IC requires sensitivity not only to clinical practices but also to the psychosocial, cultural, and ethical dimensions of the individual. In this context, individuals’ experiences, feelings, and thoughts should be considered and taken into account when creating a nursing care plan and providing care [2]. Furthermore, for the sustainability of IC, it is important that the relationship established with the patient is based on mutual cooperation, shared decision-making, and trust. At this point, the literature emphasizes that the concepts of patient-centered care, relationship-based care, and personalized care are closely related to IC [1, 3].

Nursing students are in constant interaction with individuals beginning from pre-graduate clinical practice training [4]. They closely witness patients’ experiences in the care process, during which nursing students need to be more attentive and mindful to accept existing situations and experiences without judgment [5]. For this reason, it is imperative to develop not only practical skills but also emotional intelligence, self-awareness, and communication skills in nursing students. It is also essential for nursing students to be aware of their own feelings and thoughts in IC and to have a high perception of the needs of the individual they care for. Mindful attention awareness (MAA) is defined as focusing one’s attention on the current situation without judging or accepting it [6]. MAA also includes dimensions such as emotional regulation, coping with stress, and professional sensitivity. In this respect, MAA both increases the individual’s internal awareness of themselves and strengthens their empathetic response to the person opposite them [6, 7]. Empathic tendency (ET) is defined as communicating with individuals, understanding their feelings and thoughts, and sharing them with the individual [8]. Empathy is considered in two dimensions: cognitive (understanding the other person’s perspective) and affective (sharing the other person’s feelings). In the nursing context, empathy is not only an emotional process but also a professional skill that guides clinical decision-making [9]. Nursing students with high conscious awareness and empathic tendencies in the IC process can ensure that care is managed with easier adaptation strategies [3]. In addition, students can accept the situation by focusing on their experiences with the individual in IC, can identify the latter’s feelings and thoughts, and can teach individuals how to cope with their problems and move on [5]. This situation enhances the therapeutic effect of IC by enabling nursing students to support the individual’s self-efficacy as role models. In light of this information, nursing students’ MAA and ET are very important for the sustainability of individualized care. However, how these variables interact with each other and whether they directly or indirectly affect individualized care perceptions (ICP) has not been sufficiently explained in the literature. Therefore, there is a need for a more in-depth theoretical examination of the relationships between these concepts.

Background

In order for nursing care to be considered individualized, care must be developed together with and specific to the individual, and the individual must know that they can make decisions of their own free will [1]. Furthermore, IC is closely related to ethical principles such as autonomy, respect, justice, and beneficence. In this respect, its theoretical basis rests not only on nursing models but also on humanistic care theories, therapy-focused care, and person-centered care approaches. Nursing students who adopt an IC approach carry out their care plans by taking into account the individual’s experiences, behaviors, thoughts, and perceptions. Awareness is necessary to take into account these variables related to the individual. MAA allows seeing and evaluating events from a different perspective and orienting the situation to achieve the desired result. MAA is associated with cognitive flexibility, problem-solving, and proactive thinking skills. In this respect, it supports not only momentary awareness but also future planning and clinical reasoning. Students with high MAA can evaluate new information from different perspectives in care and manage negative situations by thinking ahead [10]. Additionally, there is a point of empathic communication with the individual at every moment of IC [11]. Empathic communication allows nursing students to plan care according to the individual’s feelings, thoughts, values ​​and preferences and to understand the individual objectively [9]. Furthermore, nursing students with empathic skills can objectively define and interpret evidence-based nursing interventions [8]. In this way, they can effectively use care strategies while providing IC. Theoretically, MAA enhances empathic response, while empathy supports IC by enabling a better understanding of the individual’s unique needs. Therefore, there is a non-linear, dynamic, and mutually interactive relationship between these three concepts. The concepts of mindfulness, empathy, and perception of IC explored in this study reflect both the psychosocial dimensions of nursing education and the essence of patient-centered care. Theoretically, mindfulness strengthens empathic responses by increasing individuals’ self-awareness and emotional regulation skills, which in turn helps nurses develop personalized approaches to care [2, 3, 9]. Therefore, it is anticipated that empathy may play a mediating role in the relationship between mindfulness and perception of IC.

In the literature, separate studies have explored the relationships between nursing students’ individualized care perceptions (ICP) [3], MAA [7], and ET [12]. with various variables. Additionally, some studies have investigated the relationship between ICP and ET [2, 9] or between MAA and empathy skills [13]. There is also one study that examined the relationship solely between nurses’ ICP and MAA [3]. In addition, existing studies have mostly addressed bilateral relationships and have not examined the interactions of variables within a holistic and structural model. In particular, whether ET plays a mediating role in the relationship between mindfulness and ICP has not been sufficiently researched at both the theoretical and practical levels. These previous studies have generally addressed the relationships between mindfulness, empathy, and ICP independently. The novelty of this study lies in its integration of these variables within a holistic mediation model, testing the mediating role of ET in the relationship between MAA and ICP in nursing students. In this respect, the study offers an original contribution to the field from both theoretical and practical perspectives.

The present study attempted to establish a theoretical basis indicating that nursing students’ ICP is directly or indirectly affected by their MAA and empathy abilities. Furthermore, this model fills an important gap in the nursing education literature by bringing together both psychological processes and clinical care outcomes. It is also anticipated that the results of this study will lead to the provision of training in nursing curricula that will develop students’ MAA and ET for the sustainability of IC. In the context of education, MAA based interventions, empathy skills training, reflective practices, and simulation-based learning strategies may be recommended. These approaches may enhance the quality of IC by integrating both theoretical knowledge and clinical application skills.

Aim and research hypotheses

This study aims to fill the aforementioned gap in the literature and to investigate the mediating effect of ET on the relationship between MAA and ICP among nursing students. The hypotheses following the proposed model of this study (Fig. 1) are H1: MAA is positively associated with ICP; H2: ET is positively associated with ICP; H3: ET plays a mediating role between MAA and ICP.

Methods

Study design and sample

This study was planned as a multi-center, cross-sectional study design. The study population consisted of 986 s-, third- and fourth-year nursing students who were continuing their education in two different institutions in two different geographical regions of Türkiye, namely the Eastern Black Sea region and the Marmara region. No sample selection was used in the study, and it was aimed to reach all students who met the inclusion criteria. The sample included 546 nursing students, reaching 55.38% of the population. While this percentage provides a statistically sufficient sample size, it raises the possibility that some characteristics of the non-responding students (e.g., their levels of awareness or empathy) may have limited the study. This limitation was taken into account for the interpretation and generalizability of the findings. The inclusion criteria for the study were (1) continuing their education at these two institutions and (2) agreeing to participate. A post hoc power analysis was conducted using G*Power. Based on the observed effect size (Cohen’s f² = 0.14;medium), the achieved power was 0.95, indicating that our sample size (n = 546) was more than sufficient to detect medium-sized effects with high confidence. This strengthens the robustness of the mediation findings.

Data collection and instruments

Data were collected using the “Student Information Form”, “Individualized Care Scale,” “Mindful Attention Awareness Scale,” and “Empathic Tendency Scale.” The data were collected online through Google Forms between September 2023 and June 2024. The online survey link created by the researchers was sent electronically to the students. Students with access to the online survey link read the explanations about the study and answered the questions after ticking the checkbox indicating whether they agreed to participate. In this link, answering all questions is mandatory. Therefore, there is no missing data in the study. Data integrity is ensured by ensuring that participants cannot proceed without completing all fields in the forms. The response time for the questions in the data collection tools was 25 min on average.

Student Information Form: The form, developed by researchers in line with the literature [2, 7, 9], contains a total of eight questions questioning the students’ age, gender, grade level, desire to work as a nurse after graduation, feeling competent in providing IC, feeling competent in making an accurate nursing diagnosis, and receiving training to develop MAA and empathic skills.

Individualized Care Scale (ICP Score): It was developed by Suhonen et al. [14] to assess nurses’ views on IC. The scale was adapted to Turkish by Acaroğlu et al. [15]. ICP Score consists of two sections: “Support of patient individuality through specific nursing activities- ICP Score-A-Nurse” and “Nurses’ perceptions of individuality in care provided- ICP Score-B-Nurse. “In this research, the ICP Score-B-Nurse section of the scale with a total of 17 items was used in the data collection process. This section includes items such as, " I took into account the feelings patients had about their illness/health condition.“, “I took into account their (patients) everyday activities (e.g. work, leisure activities) outside the hospital.” and “I made sure that patients understood the instructions they received.” The 5-point Likert-type scale is scored as “(1) Strongly Disagree”, “(2) Partially Disagree”, “(3) Undecided”, “(4) Partially Agree” “(5) Strongly Agree”. To calculate the scores from the scale and its sub-dimensions, the total item score is divided by the number of items they contain. The scores that can be obtained from the scale and its sub-dimensions vary between 1 and 5. As the score obtained from the scale increases, the perception of individualized care also increases. The Cronbach alpha value of the scale was 0.91 [14, 15].

Mindful Attention Awareness Scale (MAAS): Developed by Brown & Ryan in 2003 [6], the scale measures the general tendency of individuals to be aware of and attentive to momentary experiences in daily life. This scale includes items such as, " I find it difficult to stay focused on what’s happening in the present.“, " I rush through activities without being really attentive to them.“, and " I snack without being aware that I’m eating.” The scale was adapted to Turkish by Özyeşil et al. [16]. The 6-point scale has a total of 15 items and is scored as follows: “(1) almost always”, “(2) most of the time”, “(3) sometimes”, “(4) rarely”, “(5) very rarely”, “(6) almost never”. The scale score is calculated based on a single total score. As the score obtained from the scale increases, the level of MAA also increases. The Cronbach alpha value of the scale was 0.80 [6, 16].

Empathic Tendency Scale (ETS): It was developed by Dökmen in 1988 to measure the level of empathy people have in their daily lives. The scale, which consists of a total of 20 items (e.g., “I find it strange when people cry while watching a movie.“, “I am a very well-liked person in my enviroment.“, and “Telling someone close to me about my troubles makes me feel better.“), is evaluated on a 5-point Likert-type scale as “Completely Appropriate = 5”, “Quite Appropriate = 4”, “Undecided = 3”, “Quite Disagree = 2”, “Completely Disagree = 1”. Items 3, 6, 7, 8, 11, 12, 14, and 16 on the scale are reverse coded. The minimum score to be obtained from the scale is 20, and the maximum score is 100. The total score represents the ET scores of the subjects. A high score indicates that the ET is high; a low score indicates that the ET is low [17].

Ethical considerations

Ethics Committee Approval (Date: 31.03.2023/Number: 04/28) from the Scientific Research Ethics Committee of a university and institutional permissions from the two institutions were obtained for the conduct of the study. Permission was obtained by email from the authors who adapted the scales, used as data collection tools, to Turkish. All participants were informed about the study’s purpose, the voluntary nature of their participation, their right to withdraw from the research at any time. Informed consent was then obtained from all the participants in this study, and confidentiality and anonymity were also maintained.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed with IBM SPSS (Statistical Package for Social Sciences) V23 and Jamovi V.2.3.28. Data normality was determined with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, and descriptive characteristics were expressed as median and percentage (%) for continuous variables and numbers and percentage (%) for categorical variables. To compare two groups’ means, t-test and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA; Post Hoc: Bonferonni) were used if the distribution was normal, and correlation analysis was used for relationship analyses. Pearson’s correlation coefficient, regression analysis, and mediation analysis were conducted. The indirect effect was examined using both the Sobel test and bias-corrected bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples, which confirmed the mediation effect. In the data evaluation, statistical significance was accepted at a 95% confidence interval and p < 0.05.

The indirect pathway accounted for approximately 21% of the total effect of MAAS on ICP Score, highlighting the mediating role of ETS. Discussion

This study aimed to investigate the mediating effect of ETs on the relationship between MAA and ICP among nursing students. The results, consistent with our hypotheses, showed that MAA was positively associated with ICP, ET was positively associated with ICP, and ET mediated the MAA-ICP relationship. These findings support theoretical assumptions in the literature and emphasize the impact of students’ clinical experiences on their ICP.

The result indicated that the nursing students in this study had a good level of ICP, with a score of 3.98 ± 0.57. Several studies in the literature have likewise shown good ICP scores, ranging from 3.97 ± 0.59 to 4.46 ± 0.39 scores, for nursing students [2, 9, 18–20], demonstrating that this study results are consistent with previous studies. This result demonstrates that the philosophy of IC and clinical experience in nursing education in Türkiye strengthens students’ perceptions. The basis of nursing philosophy is individuality, uniqueness, and integrity beliefs that represent IC [21]. Since nursing education in Turkey recognizes this philosophy, it is an expected outcome for students to have high ICP scores. This finding may also have been affected by the fact that the students participating in the study wanted to work as nurses after graduation and considered themselves competent in providing IC by making an accurate nursing diagnosis. It is reported that loving the profession increases the likelihood of adopting professional behaviors and responsibilities and showing profession-specific behaviors [22], which is supported by other studies in the literature [9, 18, 23].

The results of the present study demonstrated that the nursing students (65.66 ± 5.54) had above-average MAA (57.88 ± 11.86) and ET. Previous studies showed that the nursing students had a moderate level of MAAS score ranging from 52.2 ± 0.73 to 60.14 ± 11.43 [7, 13, 24, 25] and had a moderate and high level in ET, ranging from 67.31 ± 7.98 to 71.26 ± 8.81 [2, 9, 12, 24]. All students included in this study had clinical education experience. The clinical experience nursing students gain during their education offers them the opportunity to take responsibility and develop nursing care behaviors [26]. In clinical education, students need to be able to see the individual holistically, be aware of events or situations, define the individual and their own feelings and thoughts, be objective, make decisions specific to the individual, and evaluate the impact of the decisions they make on the individual [4, 5]. In order to provide these, it is crucial for students with clinical education experience to develop awareness of care and empathy skills to try to understand the individual. Additionally, the 55.38% student participation rate in the study may limit the generalizability of the findings. It should be noted that students who did not respond may have different levels of awareness, empathy, or perception of individualized care. This is an important limitation that should be considered when interpreting the results and assessing the effects of potential response bias.

The present study found that MAA positively affects ICP. Although there is no study with nursing students in the literature, studies conducted with nurses suggest a positive relationship between nurses’ individualized care perceptions and conscious awareness, which supports the study findings [3, 5]. Mindfulness improves mental health and affects learning in nursing students [7, 27], reduces negative emotions such as stress, anxiety, depression, and burnout [28], improves academic skills, emotional regulation, and empathy [27, 29], provides concentration and clarity of thought [30], strengthens working memory capacity, help achieve positive emotions and the ability to control emotions [10, 25], and improves cognitive functions such as attention control and ethical decision-making [31, 32]. These effects of MAA on students are in fact the necessary building blocks for the provision of IC. In Human Caring Theory, Watson states that nursing care as a dual relationship can only be holistic and specific to the individual when the caregiver approaches the individual with high awareness. According to Watson, awareness and empathic skills strengthen nurses’ self-awareness and professional decision-making abilities in the care process [33]. In order to manage the IC process, students need to focus on the moment of care, be able to define the moment holistically or foresee possible situations, and make independent decisions by being aware of their own feelings and thoughts. These findings suggest that incorporating MAA and empathy-based education programs into the nursing curriculum may strengthen ICP.

In the current mediation model, ET mediated the relationship between MAA and ICP. The specific relationship is that MAA is associated with a higher level of ET, which correlates with higher ICP. Previous studies in the literature provide information about the relationship between nursing students’ MAA and ET [34, 35], emotional regulation [10, 24, 25, 36], and emotional control skills [10, 28]. Mindfulness enables individuals to be aware of what is happening around them, to focus on the moment and see events as a whole, to evaluate events objectively, and to notice affective and cognitive distortions related to events. In this way, individuals may have a flexible thought system, effective coping methods, and a strong ET skill. These skills, acquired through awareness, enable students to understand the feelings and thoughts of individuals by purifying themselves from their own feelings and thoughts during care [34]. The ET that develops through awareness serves IC by allowing students to identify the feelings and thoughts of individuals during the care process, evaluate them impartially, make decisions specific to the individual, and evaluate the impact of the decisions they make on the feelings and thoughts of the individual [35]. In other word, results of this study suggest that students with higher levels of mindfulness adopt more individualized approaches to caregiving by better understanding the emotional experiences of others. This finding supports studies demonstrating that mindfulness and adaptive coping strategies reduce anxiety and increase professional competence [37, 38]. In this study, it was determined that the students’ MAAS score affected their ETS score and that this indirectly affected their ICP score statistically significantly, indicating that the study finding is supported by the literature. In addition, the literature provides evidence on the relationship between students’ ICP and ET [9, 23] and emotional skills [19], which supports the study findings. Based on these results, the results of this study offer important implications for nursing education and clinical practice. Given that levels of mindfulness and empathy strengthen the ICP, it is recommended that these skills be systematically integrated into educational programs. Adding short-term mindfulness sessions and empathy-based communication training to nursing curricula can enhance students’ psychological awareness and interpersonal competence. Furthermore, incorporating reflective practices and supervised mindfulness exercises into clinical education can contribute to the IC. On the other hand, Güven Özdemir & Şendir [2] and Avcı & Alp Yılmaz [39] argued that there is a high level of relationship between nurses’ ET and ICP, supporting our study findings. In contrast to these results, Uzen Cura & Atay [13] reported no relationship between MAA and empathy in their study with nurses. This difference in findings may be due to the different sample characteristics and the high perception of caring behaviors of nursing students who are in clinical education for learning purposes.

From a practical perspective, these results highlight the importance of developing mindfulness and empathy-based educational programs in nursing education. MAA-based interventions can strengthen students’ emotional awareness, empathetic responses, and patient-centered care skills. Empathy-focused communication training, reflective practices, and short mindfulness sessions can enhance students’ IC competencies. Systematically incorporating these skills into educational programs could improve both the quality of nursing care and students’ psychological well-being in the future. The current literature indicates that targeted and adaptable mindfulness interventions reduce anxiety levels among nurses, enhance their stress management skills, and strengthen their mental well-being [37]. These findings support the applicability of similar approaches in the education of nursing students. In terms of the research gap, the literature has largely examined the links between MAA, empathy, and ICP at the level of binary relationships. This study, by examining these three constructs in a holistic model, tested the mediating mechanism, thus making a unique contribution to the nursing education literature. Future research might replicate this model with different cultural samples and experimental designs to test the validity and generalizability of the findings. Additionally, longitudinal or intervention studies that directly measure the effect of MAA and empathy-based interventions could more concretely demonstrate the effectiveness of educational programs.

Limitations

The study had some limitations. First, because it consisted only of nursing students from two nursing faculties in Türkiye, the generalizability of these results may be restricted. Besides, although this study aimed to reach all students, the 55.38% response rate may limit generalization because non-participating students may have different ICP, MAA or ET levels. Also, the findings of this study are based on data obtained from nursing students in Türkiye, cultural and contextual factors may limit the generalizability of the results. It should be noted that concepts such as mindfulness, empathy, and perception of caring may be experienced differently across cultures. Multi-center and multi-geographical research may be helpful for improving generalization in future studies. This study used a cross-sectional methodology. As nursing students’ ICP may alter over time, further longitudinal studies are required to better understand this phenomenon. Furthermore, the self-reported data collection method carries the possibility that participants may be influenced by social desirability biases. For these reasons, longitudinal and multi-method designs are recommended for future research. Lastly, the subjective nature of the scales may not fully reflect multidimensional psychological constructs, particularly mindfulness and empathy. Future research is recommended to explore these variables more thoroughly using qualitative data collection methods (e.g., observation, in-depth interviews). This will allow the unique findings of this study to be supported by different samples and methods.

Conclusion

This study revealed a possible mechanism for the relationship between ET, MAA, and ICP. The results demonstrated that ET is an important mediating variable connecting MAA and ICP. The findings of this study provided the first theoretical basis for the literature indicating that nursing students’ ICP is directly or indirectly affected by MA and ET. In line with these results, it can be suggested that education or course content supporting MAA and empathy skills that positively affect IC behaviors of nursing students should be added to the nursing curriculum. However, more studies examining the relationship between these variables with larger sample groups are needed to develop the limited literature on this subject.

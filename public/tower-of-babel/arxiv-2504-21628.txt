Cycles of lengths 3 3 and n − 1 n-1 in digraphs under a Bang-Jensen-Gutin-Li type conditon
Zan-Bo Zhang, Wenhao Wu, Weihua He

Abstract
Bang-Jensen-Gutin-Li type conditions are the conditions for hamiltonicity of digraphs which impose degree restrictions on nonadjacent vertices which have a common in-neighbor or a common out-neighbor. They can be viewed as an extension of Fan type conditions in undirected graphs, as well as generalization of locally (in-, out-)semicomplete digraphs. Since their first appearance in 1996, various Bang-Jensen-Gutin-Li type conditions for hamitonicity have come forth. In this paper we establish a condition of Bang-Jensen-Gutin-Li type which implies not only a hamiltonian cycle but also a 3 3 -cycle and an ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -cycle, with well-characterized exceptional graphs. We conjecture that this condition implies the existence of cycle of every length. Key words : Hamiltonian cycle, pancyclicity, Bang-Jensen-Gutin-Li type condition, Locally semicomplete digraph

Degree conditions are among the most basic conditions for the existence of hamiltonian cycles in graphs.
For digraphs, the most popular hamiltonian degree conditions are possibly
Ghouila-Houri condition and Meyniel condition (see Chapter 6.4 of

In 1996, Bang-Jensen, Gutin and Li (

For a pair of nonadjacent vertices

we say that they form a nonadjacent dominated pair (resp. a nonadjacent dominating pair).

(

The conditions involving nonadjacent dominated pairs and nonadjacent dominating pairs

Such conditions are also generalization of three kinds of well studied digraphs, namely,

An LiSD (resp. LoSD) is a digraph in which the in-neighborhood (resp. out-neighborhood)

An LSD without

The hamiltonicity of LiSD, LoSD and LSD has all been verified (

It then sounds natural to ask for a pancyclic condition of Bang-Jensen-Gutin-Li type.
Some attempts have been made. For example,

However, a potential difficulty to prove pancyclicity lays in the following fact.
The condition in Theorem

poses degree restriction on nonadjacent
dominated pairs only, which generalizes the class of LiSD.
Hence any result that the condition in Theorem

But a complete characterization of non-pancyclic LiSD’s seems to be difficult and has not been found.

Therefore, it makes sense to firstly consider Bang-Jensen-Gutin-Li conditions that

Proof of Theorem
1.4
: the existence of a
\boldsymbol{3}
-cycle

We need the following lemma to prove the theorem.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
D
be a strong digraph without directed
3
-cycle, and
u,v\in V(D)
.
If
uv,vu\in A(D)
, then
d(u)+d(v)\leq 2n
, and equality holds iff for every

x\in V(D)\backslash\{u,v\}
,
d(\{u,v\},x)=2
.

Proof.

For every
x\in V(D)\backslash\{u,v\}
, we can not have
u\rightarrow x\rightarrow v
or

v\rightarrow x\rightarrow u
.
Therefore
|A(D)\cap\{ux,xv\}|\leq 1
,
|A(D)\cap\{vx,xu\}|\leq 1
and thus

d(\{u,v\},x)\leq 2
, and so
d(u)+d(v)\leq 2+2+2(n-2)=2n
.
If equality holds then
d(\{u,v\},x)=2
for every
x\in V(D)\backslash\{u,v\}
.
∎

Suppose that there is no
3
-cycle in
D
.
If both
u_{0}u_{1}\dots u_{k}
and
u_{k}\dots u_{1}u_{0}
are directed paths in
D
, we call

u_{0}u_{1}\dots u_{k}
a
two-way path
, and a
two-way arc
if
k=1
.
Now let’s divide our discussion into two cases with respect to the longest two-way path in
D
.

Case 1. The longest two-way path in
D
is of length at most
1
.

If there is no two-way path at all, then every vertex is of degree at most
n-1
.
By the condition of the theorem, there can not be any nonadjacent dominating pair or nonadjacent dominated pair,
and hence
D
is an LSD.

If there is at least one two-way path of length
1
, say
uv
in
D
, then we can prove that
u
and
u

have the same in-neighborhood and out-neighborhood in
D\backslash\{u,v\}
.
For otherwise, without lose of generality assume that there exists a vertex

x
such that
u\rightarrow x
but
v\nrightarrow x
, since
D
is
3
-cycle-free,
x\nrightarrow v
.
But then
\{x,v\}
is a nonadjacent dominated pair, and by the condition of the theorem
d(v)\geq n
.
By Lemma
2.1
,
d(u)+d(v)\leq 2n
. Thus
d(v)\geq n\geq d(u)
.
Let
w
be any vertex that is adjacent to both
u
and
v
, we have that
d(u,w)=d(v,w)=1
.
Since
x
is adjacent to
u
but not
v
and
d(v)\geq d(u)
,
there must exist another vertex

which is adjacent to
v
but not
u
.
But then
\{u,y\}
forms a nonadjacent dominating pair or a nonadjacent dominated pair.
Hence,
d(u)\geq n
and thus we have
d(u)=d(v)=n
.
Now equality in Lemma
2.1
holds, and we have
d(\{u,v\},x)=2
.
Then we have
x\rightarrow u
and

is a two-way path of length

Thus,
u
and
v
have the same in-neighborhood and out-neighborhood in

.

Now we can let
N^{+}_{uv}
and
N^{-}_{uv}
be the out-neighborhood and in-neighborhood
of both

and

in

. Since there is no

-cycle, there can not be any arc
from

to

.
By the strongness of

Now it has been shown that

is an LSD in all cases. Since

does not contain any

.
Furthermore, every strong semicomplete digraph

is pancyclic, i.e.,

to

(Theorem 1.5.3 of

]

Hence, for every semicomplete digraph in the round decomposition of

.

Case 2. The longest two-way path in

is of length at least
2
.

Firstly we claim that the longest two-way path is actually of length at least
3
in this case.
Let

be a two-way path. Since

is

and

Thus

is a nonadjacent dominating pair, and

.
But then

Since there is not

Thus, for

Therefore,

have the same out-neighborhood, that is

(1)

Let

N^{+}(u_{0})=N^{+}(u_{2})=\dots=N^{+}(u_{2\lfloor k/2\rfloor})\mbox{ and }N^{+}(u_{1})=N^{+}(u_{3})=\dots=N^{+}(u_{2\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor+1}).

(2)

(3)

Since

and

(

. Since

In the proof of this section, we will need several preliminary results. The first one is the
multi-insertion technique, which is a powerful tool for tackling paths and cycles problems in graphs.
A detail explanation and application of multi-insertion technique
in digraphs can be found in Chapter 6.4.2 of
[
1
]
.
To maintain the integrity of the paper we briefly introduce the related concepts
and ideas here.

Let
P=u_{0}u_{2}\dots u_{s}
be a path in a digraph
D
and
let
Q=v_{0}v_{2}\dots v_{t}
be a path in
D\backslash V(P)
.
The path
P
is said to be able to be
inserted into

Q
if there is an integer

0\leq i\leq t-1
such that
v_{i}\rightarrow u_{1}
and
u_{s}\rightarrow v_{i+1}
,
in the sense that the path
Q
can be extended to a new
(v_{0},v_{t})
-path

Q[v_{1},v_{i}]PQ[v_{i+1},v_{t}]
with vertex set
V(P)\cup V(Q)
. Next, the path
P
can
be
multi-inserted into

Q
if there are integers
i_{1}=0<i_{2}<\dots<i_{m}=s+1

such that, for every
k\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, the subpath
P[u_{i_{k-1}},u_{i_{k}-1}]
can be
inserted into
Q
. For the case that
Q
is a cycle similar definitions can be given.

The definition of multi-insertion means that
P
can be partition into subpaths each
of which can be inserted into
Q
.
The key result here is that under this assumption,
all vertices of
P
can then be inserted into
Q
to form a path
with vertex set
V(P)\cup V(Q)
and the same endvertices with
Q
(or merely a cycle
with vertex set
V(P)\cup V(Q)
, when

Lemma 3.1
.

(The Multi-Insertion Technique)
Let
P
be a path in a digraph
D
and let
Q=v_{0}v_{1}\dots v_{t}
be a path (a
cycle, respectively) in
D\backslash V(P)
. If
P
can be multi-inserted into
Q
, then there
is a
(v_{0},v_{t})
-path
R
(a cycle, respectively) in
D
so that
V(R)=V(P)\cup V(Q)
.

If

(

(b)

The below lemma shows that in a digraph satisfying the condition we raise,
a vertex adjacent to a longest nonhamiltonian cycle satisfies certain nice structure or degree conditions.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
D
be a strong digraph with order
n\geq 4
. Suppose that for every vertex
u
which
belongs to a nonadjacent dominating pair or a nonadjacent dominated pair,
d(u)\geq n
.
Let
C
be a longest nonhamiltonian cycle in
D
with
|C|\leq n-2
, and
w\in V(D)\backslash V(C)

be adjacent to some vertex on
C
. If
d(w)\leq n-1
, then either
w\rightarrow C
or
C\rightarrow w
.

Proof.

(of Lemma
3.4
)
Let
C=u_{0}u_{1}\dots u_{k-1}
. By the condition of the lemma
w
is adjacent to at least one vertex on
C
,
say
u_{0}\rightarrow w
. Since
C
is the longest nonhamiltonian cycle with
|C|\leq n-2
,
w
can not
be inserted into
C
, hence
w\nrightarrow u_{1}
. But
w
and
u_{1}
are dominated by
u_{0}
, and by the
condition of the lemma and
d(w)\leq n-1
, they must be adjacent. So
u_{1}\rightarrow w
. Repeatedly
apply the above arguments we conclude that
u_{i}\rightarrow w
, for
0\leq i\leq k-1
, that is,

C\rightarrow w
. Similarly if we assume that
w\rightarrow u_{0}
at the beginning we will get

w\rightarrow C
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.5
. We firstly prove the theorem for

.

.

.

,

and

, or a

-cycle can be formed.
But then
\{u_{1},u_{3}\}
and
\{u_{2},u_{4}\}
are nonadjacent dominated or dominating pairs.
Thus

for

. Then the reverse arc of every arc on

must exist,
and so as
u_{1}u_{4}
. Now there can be quite a few

-cycles in

, say

,
again a contradiction. (3) If there are at least three chords, we must have two chords with
the same end which has been discussed in (2).
(4) So there are exactly two chords which are without a common endvertex, say
u_{0}u_{2}

and
u_{3}u_{1}
, then
u_{4}
and
u_{1}
forms a nonadjacent dominated pair, and

,
but
u_{4}
is not associate with any chord and hence

, a contradiction,
which completes the proof for

.

Now we assume

and there is no
(n-1)
-cycle in

.
We further assume that

.
Note that the condition of the theorem imply a hamiltonian cycle.
Since

.
Let

, where

.

We claim that there exists a
C
-bypass in

.
Suppose to the contrary that there is no
C
-bypass.
By the strongness of

Q
with
|V(Q)\cap V(C)|=1
.
W.l.o.g. we may further assume the common vertex of

and

to be
u_{0}

and denote the successor of
u_{0}
on

.
Now any arc between

will produce a

-bypass and
leads to a contradiction, hence
d_{C}(v)\leq 2
. Meanwhile,
\{v,u_{1}\}

is a nonadjacent dominated pair and thus

by the condition
of the theorem.
For any

, if

or

,

.

Case 1
.

, then

.
We further divide this case into two, according to whether we can

so that

.

Case 1.1
. There exists a bypass

with minimum gap and

.

In this case we have
\alpha\geq 2
, for if

to

.
And by

.

Since

,

,

,

(4)

(5)

) and (

&=d_{R}(x_{1})+d_{R}(u_{\gamma})+d_{C^{\prime}}(x_{1})+d_{C^{\prime}}(u_{\gamma})+d_{C^{\prime\prime}}(x_{1})+d_{C^{\prime\prime}}(u_{\gamma})\\

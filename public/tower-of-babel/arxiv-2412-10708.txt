Bertrand lightcone framed curves in the Lorentz-Minkowski 3-space
Nozomi Nakatsuyama, Masatomo Takahashi

Abstract
We consider mixed types of not only regular curves but also curves with singular points in the Lorentz-Minkowski 3-space. In order to consider mixed type of curves with singular points, we consider the lightcone frame and lightcone framed curves. By using lightcone frame, we can consider Bertrand types for lightcone framed curves, so-called Bertrand lightcone framed curves. We clarify that the existence conditions of Bertrand lightcone framed curves in all cases. As a consequence, we find pseudo-circular involutes and evolutes of mixed type curves which appear as Bertrand lightcone framed curves.

Introduction

A Bertrand (respectively, Mannheim) curve in the Euclidean
3

In
[
9

Moreover, we investigated the other cases, that is, a space curve whose tangent (or, principal normal, bi-normal) line is the same as the tangent (or, principal normal, bi-normal) line of another curve, respectively.
We say that a Bertrand type curve if there exists such another curve.
We investigated the existence conditions of Bertrand type curves in all cases in
[
14
]
.
Moreover, we also investigated curves with singular points. As smooth curves with singular points, it is useful to use the framed curves in the Euclidean space (cf.
[
8
]
).
We investigated the existence conditions of the Bertrand framed curves (Bertrand types of framed curves) in all cases in
[
14
]
.

For a non-lightlike non-degenerate curve, we have the arc-length parameter and the Frenet-Serret type formula by using a moving frame like a non-degenerate space curve in the Euclidean space.
It follows that we have the curvature of a non-lightlike non-degenerate curve (cf.
[
13

15
]

In
[
19
]
, they investigated the necessary and sufficient conditions for timelike Bertrand curves in the Lorentz-Minkowski 3-space.
In
[

Also see in
[
20

[

In this paper, we consider mixed types of not only regular curves but also curves with singular points in the Lorentz-Minkowski 3-space.
In order to consider mixed types of curves with singular points, we consider the lightcone frame and lightcone framed curves (cf.

By using the lightcone frame, we can consider Bertrand lightcone framed curves.
We investigate a space curve whose lightlike (or, spacelike, timelike) vector is the same as the lightlike (or, spacelike, timelike) vector of another curve, respectively.
We say that Bertrand lightcone framed curves if there exists such another curve.
In §3, we clarify that the existence conditions of Bertrand lightcone framed curves in all cases.

1.1
Lorentz-Minkowski
3
-space

The
Lorentz-Minkowski space

\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
is the space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
endowed with the metric induced by the pseudo-scalar product
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\rangle=-x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}+x_{3}y_{3}
, where
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
and
\mbox{\boldmath$y$}=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
.
We say that a non-zero vector
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
is
spacelike
if
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle>0
,
lightlike
if
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle=0
, and
timelike
if
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle<0
respectively. The
norm
of a vector
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
is defined by
||\mbox{\boldmath$x$}||=\sqrt{|\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle|}
.
For any
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),\mbox{\boldmath$y$}=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
, we define a vector
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$y$}
by

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$y$}={\rm det}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{1}&\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{2}&\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{3}\\
x_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}\\
y_{1}&y_{2}&y_{3}\end{array}\right),

where
\{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{3}\}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.
For any
\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\in\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
, we can easily check that

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$w$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\rangle=\textrm{det}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$y$}),

so that
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$y$}
is pseudo-orthogonal to both
x
and
y
.
Moreover, if
x
is a unit timelike vector,
y
is a unit spacelike vector and
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\rangle=0
,
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$y$}=\mbox{\boldmath$z$}
, then by a straightforward calculation we have

\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\mbox{\boldmath$y$},\ \mbox{\boldmath$y$}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$z$}=-\mbox{\boldmath$x$}.

We define
hyperbolic
2
-space, de Sitter
2
-space
or
lightcone
by

\displaystyle H^{2}(-1)

\displaystyle=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}\ |\ \langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle=-1\},

\displaystyle S^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}\ |\ \langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle=1\},

\displaystyle LC^{*}

\displaystyle=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\ |\ \langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle=0\}.

If
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})
is a non-zero lightlike vector, then
x_{0}\not=0
.
Therefore, we have

\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}=\left(1,\frac{x_{1}}{x_{0}},\frac{x_{2}}{x_{0}}\right)\in S^{1}_{+}=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\in LC^{*}|\ x_{0}=1\}.

We call
S^{1}_{+}
the
lightcone circle
.
In this paper, we consider two double Legendrian fibrations (cf.
[
11
]
):

(1)

\Delta_{1}=\{(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$})\in H^{2}(-1)\times S^{2}_{1}\ |\ \langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rangle=0\},

\pi_{11}:\Delta_{1}\to H^{2}(-1),\pi_{11}(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$})=\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\ \pi_{12}:\Delta_{1}\to S^{2}_{1},\pi_{12}(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$})=\mbox{\boldmath$w$}
,

\theta_{11}=\langle d\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rangle|_{\Delta_{1}},\theta_{12}=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$},d\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rangle|_{\Delta_{1}}
.

(2)

\Delta_{4}=\{(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$})\in LC^{*}\times LC^{*}\ |\ \langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rangle=-2\},

\pi_{41}:\Delta_{1}\to LC^{*},\pi_{41}(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$})=\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\ \pi_{42}:\Delta_{1}\to LC^{*},\pi_{42}(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$})=\mbox{\boldmath$w$}
,

\theta_{41}=\langle d\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rangle|_{\Delta_{4}},\theta_{42}=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$},d\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rangle|_{\Delta_{4}}
.

Note that
\Phi:\Delta_{4}\to\Delta_{1},\Phi(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$})=((\mbox{\boldmath$v$}+\mbox{\boldmath$w$})/2,(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}-\mbox{\boldmath$w$})/2)
is a contact diffeomorphsim.

Let
O(1,2)
be the Lorentz group which consists of square matrices
A
of order
3
such that
{}^{t}AZA=Z
, where

Z:={\rm diag}(-1,1,1)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right).

We set
SO(1,2)
as
SO(1,2):=\{A\in O(1,2)\mid{\rm det}A=1\}.

For vectors
\mbox{\boldmath$a$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
and
A\in SO(1,2)
, we have

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$a$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\rangle=\langle A(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}),A(\mbox{\boldmath$b$})\rangle,\ A(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$b$})=A(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})\wedge A(\mbox{\boldmath$b$}).

1.2
Lightcone framed curves

In order to investigate mixed type of curves in the Lorentz-Minkowski space, we introduce the lightcone frame.

Definition 1.1

Let
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
be a smooth mapping.
We say that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
is a
lightcone framed curve
if there exist smooth functions
\alpha,\beta:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\dot{\gamma}(t)=\alpha(t)\ell^{+}(t)+\beta(t)\ell^{-}(t)
for all
t\in I
.
We also say that
\gamma
is a
lightcone framed base curve
if there exists a smooth mapping
(\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\Delta_{4}
such that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
is a lightcone framed curve.

Since
(\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\Delta_{4}
, we have

\langle\ell^{+}(t),\ell^{+}(t)\rangle=0,\ \langle\ell^{-}(t),\ell^{-}(t)\rangle=0,\ \langle\ell^{+}(t),\ell^{-}(t)\rangle=-2

for all
t\in I
.
By
\dot{\gamma}(t)=\alpha(t)\ell^{+}(t)+\beta(t)\ell^{-}(t)
,
\langle\dot{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\rangle=-4\alpha(t)\beta(t)
.
Therefore,
\gamma
is spacelike, lightlike, or timelike at
t
if
\alpha(t)\beta(t)<0
,
\alpha(t)\beta(t)=0
with
\alpha(t)\not=0
or
\beta(t)\not=0
, or
\alpha(t)\beta(t)>0
, respectively.
Moreover,
t
is a singular point of
\gamma
if and only if
\alpha(t)=\beta(t)=0
.

We denote
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}):I\to\Delta_{1}
,

\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)=\frac{\ell^{+}(t)+\ell^{-}(t)}{2},\ \mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(t)=\frac{\ell^{+}(t)-\ell^{-}(t)}{2}.

We define
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}:I\to S^{2}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(t)=-(1/2)\ell^{+}(t)\wedge\ell^{-}(t)
.
Then
\{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(t),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame of
\gamma(t)
.
We say that

\{\ell^{+}(t),\ell^{-}(t),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)\}
is a
lightcone frame
of
\gamma(t)
.
Note that the lightcone frame is not a pseudo-orthonormal frame.
By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{\ell^{+}}(t)\\
\dot{\ell^{-}}(t)\\
\dot{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(t)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\kappa_{1}(t)&0&2\kappa_{3}(t)\\
0&-\kappa_{1}(t)&2\kappa_{2}(t)\\
\kappa_{2}(t)&\kappa_{3}(t)&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\ell^{+}(t)\\
\ell^{-}(t)\\
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(t)\ell^{+}(t)+\beta(t)\ell^{-}(t).

We call
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\alpha,\beta)
a
(lightcone) curvature
of the lightcone framed curve
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}(t)\\
\dot{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t)\\
\dot{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(t)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\kappa_{1}(t)&\kappa^{T}(t)\\
\kappa_{1}(t)&0&-\kappa^{S}(t)\\
\kappa^{T}(t)&\kappa^{S}(t)&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)\\
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(t)\\
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)+b(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(t),

where
\kappa^{T}=\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{3},\ \kappa^{S}=\kappa_{2}-\kappa_{3},a=\alpha+\beta
and
b=\alpha-\beta
.

In
[
5
]
, we proved existence and uniqueness theorem for a special frame.
We can also prove the general case by the similar arguments, we omit it.

Theorem 1.2

(Existence theorem for lightcone framed curves)

Let
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\\
\kappa_{3},\alpha,\beta):

I\to\mathbb{R}^{5}
be a smooth mapping.
There exists a lightcone framed curve
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
such that the curvature is
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\alpha,\beta)
.

Definition 1.3

We say that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
and
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
are
congruent as lightcone framed curves
if there exist
A\in SO(1,2)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
such that
\overline{\gamma}(t)=A(\gamma(t))+\mbox{\boldmath$a$},\ \overline{\ell}^{+}(t)=A(\ell^{+}(t)),\ \overline{\ell}^{-}(t)=A(\ell^{-}(t))
for all
t\in I
.

Theorem 1.4

(Uniqueness theorem for lightcone framed curves)

Let
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\\
\ell^{-})
and
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
be lightcone framed curves with curvatures
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\alpha,\beta)
and
(\overline{\kappa}_{1},\overline{\kappa}_{2},\overline{\kappa}_{3},\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta})
, respectively.

(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
and
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-})
are congruent as lightcone framed curves if and only if
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\alpha,\beta)=(\overline{\kappa}_{1},\overline{\kappa}_{2},\overline{\kappa}_{3},\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta})
.

Proposition 1.5

Let
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
be a lightcone framed curve with curvature
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\alpha,\beta)
.
Then
(\gamma,\ell^{-},\ell^{+})
is also a lightcone framed curve with the curvature
(-\kappa_{1},-\kappa_{3},-\kappa_{2},\beta,\alpha)

We consider a special lightcone frame.
We denote
\overline{\ell}^{+}(t)=(1/c(t))\ell^{+}(t)

By a direct calculation, we have

Then
\{\overline{\ell}^{+}(t),\overline{\ell}^{-}(t),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)\}
is also a lightcone frame of
\gamma(t)
.
By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{\overline{\ell}^{+}}(t)\\
\dot{\overline{\ell}^{-}}(t)\\
\dot{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(t)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\overline{\kappa}_{1}(t)&0&2\overline{\kappa}_{3}(t)\\
0&-\overline{\kappa}_{1}(t)&2\overline{\kappa}_{2}(t)\\
\overline{\kappa}_{2}(t)&\overline{\kappa}_{3}(t)&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{\ell}^{+}(t)\\
\overline{\ell}^{-}(t)\\
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\alpha}(t)\overline{\ell}^{+}(t)+\overline{\beta}(t)\overline{\ell}^{-}(t),

where

\displaystyle\overline{\kappa}_{1}(t)=\frac{-\dot{c}(t)+c(t)\kappa_{1}(t)}{c^{2}(t)},\ \overline{\kappa}_{2}(t)=c(t)\kappa_{2}(t),\ \overline{\kappa}_{3}(t)=\frac{\kappa_{3}(t)}{c(t)},

\displaystyle\overline{\alpha}(t)=c(t)\alpha(t),\ \overline{\beta}(t)=\frac{\beta(t)}{c(t)}.

If we take
\dot{c}(t)=c(t)\kappa_{1}(t)
, that is,
c(t)=Ae^{\int\kappa_{1}(t)dt}
, where

\overline{\kappa}_{1}(t)=0
.
Hence, we can always take

We say that the lightcone frame

with
\overline{\kappa}_{1}(t)=0
is an
adapted frame
.

Moreover, we consider a special lightcone frame (cf.
[
12

Let
(\gamma,\ell^{+},{\ell}^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}

2
Bertrand types of lightcone framed curves

Let
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
and
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
be lightcone framed curves with curvatures
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\alpha,\beta)
and
(\overline{\kappa}_{1},\overline{\kappa}_{2},\overline{\kappa}_{3},\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta})
.

Definition 2.1

We say that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
and
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-})
are
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}})
-mates
if there exists a smooth function
\lambda:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda\not\equiv 0
such that
\overline{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(t)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(t)=\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(t)
for all
t\in I
.
We also say that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
is a
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}})
-Bertrand lightcone framed curve
(or,
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}})
-Bertrand-Mannheim lightcone framed curve
) if there exists another lightcone framed curve
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-})
such that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
and
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-})
are
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}})
-mates. Here
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(t)
and
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(t)
are non-zero vectors.

Note that
\lambda\not\equiv 0
means that
\{t\in I|\lambda(t)\not=0\}
is a dense subset of
I
.
Then
\lambda
is not identically zero for any non-trivial subintervals of
I
.
It follows that
\gamma
and
\overline{\gamma}
are different space curves for any non-trivial subintervals of
I
.
By definition,
v
and
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}
are the same causal type.
If the both vectors are lightlike, timelike or spacelike, then we say that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
and
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-})
are
lightlike mate, timelike mate or spacelike mate
, respectively.
Since
\ell^{+}
and
\ell^{-}
are lightlike,
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}
is a timelike,
n
and
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}
are spacelike vectors, lightlike mates are four cases, timelike mate is one case, and spacelike mates are four cases.
Moreover, we can consider naturally other lightlike vectors
\widetilde{\ell}^{+}=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}
and
\widetilde{\ell}^{-}=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}-\mbox{\boldmath$n$}
by the moving frame
\{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\}
of
\gamma
.
We also add four cases in lightlike mates.
By the construction, we have
(\widetilde{\ell}^{+},\widetilde{\ell}^{-}):I\to\Delta_{4}
, however,
(\gamma,\widetilde{\ell}^{+},\widetilde{\ell}^{-})
is not always a lightcone framed curve.

We clarify that the existence conditions of Bertrand lightcone framed curves in all cases.

2.1
Lightlike mates

Firstly, we consider lightlike mates.
There are eight cases.
Let
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
be a lightcone framed curve with curvature
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\alpha,\beta)
.

Theorem 2.2

(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
is a
(\ell^{+},\overline{\ell}^{+})
-Bertrand lightcone framed curve if and only if there exist smooth functions
\lambda,k:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda\not\equiv 0
such that
k(t)\beta(t)=\lambda(t)\kappa_{3}(t)
for all
t\in I
.

Proof.
Suppose that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
is a
(\ell^{+},\overline{\ell}^{+})
-Bertrand lightcone framed curve. Then there exists another lightcone framed curve
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-})
such that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
and
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-})
are
(\ell^{+},\overline{\ell}^{+})
-mates, that is,
there exists a smooth function
\lambda:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda\not\equiv 0
such that
\overline{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\ell^{+}(t)
and
\ell^{+}(t)=\overline{\ell}^{+}(t)
for all
t\in I
.
By differentiating
\overline{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\ell^{+}(t)

Theorem 2.23

(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
is a
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T})
-Bertrand lightcone framed curve if and only if there exist smooth functions
\lambda,\theta:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda\not\equiv 0
such that

\lambda(t)(\kappa_{2}(t)+\kappa_{3}(t))\cos\theta(t)-(\alpha(t)-\beta(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t))\sin\theta(t)=0

for all
t\in I
.

Proof.
Suppose that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
is a
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T})
-Bertrand lightcone framed curve. Then there exists another lightcone framed curve
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-})
such that
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
and
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-})
are
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T})
-mates, that is,
there exists a smooth function
\lambda:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda\not\equiv 0
such that
\overline{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)=\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}(t)
for all
t\in I
.
By differentiating
\overline{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)
, we have

\overline{a}(t)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}(t)+\overline{b}(t)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t)=(a(t)+\dot{\lambda}(t))\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)+(b(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t))\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa^{T}(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t).

Since
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)=\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}(t)
, we have
\overline{a}(t)=a(t)+\dot{\lambda}(t)
.
Moreover, there exists a smooth function
\theta:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(t)\\
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta(t)&-\sin\theta(t)\\
\sin\theta(t)&\cos\theta(t)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(t)\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t)\end{pmatrix}.

Then we have
\overline{b}(t)\sin\theta(t)=\lambda(t)\kappa^{T}(t)
and

\overline{b}(t)\cos\theta(t)=b(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t)
.
It follows that
\overline{b}(t)=\lambda(t)\kappa^{T}(t)\sin\theta(t)+(b(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t))\cos\theta(t)
and
\lambda(t)\kappa^{T}(t)\cos\theta(t)

-(b(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t))\sin\theta(t)=0
.
Therefore, we have

\lambda(t)(\kappa_{2}(t)+\kappa_{3}(t))\cos\theta(t)-(\alpha(t)-\beta(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t))\sin\theta(t)=0

for all
t\in I
.

Conversely, suppose that there exist smooth functions
\lambda,\theta:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda\not\equiv 0
such that
\lambda(t)\kappa^{T}(t)\cos\theta(t)-(b(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t))\sin\theta(t)=0
for all
t\in I
.
If we consider
\overline{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t),\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(t)=\cos\theta(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)-\sin\theta(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(t),\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t)=\sin\theta(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)+\cos\theta(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(t)
,
then
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}(t)=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)
and

\displaystyle\dot{\overline{\gamma}}(t)

\displaystyle=(a(t)+\dot{\lambda}(t))\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(t)+(b(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t))\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa^{T}(t)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)

\displaystyle=(a(t)+\dot{\lambda}(t))\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}(t)+(b(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t))(-\sin\theta(t)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(t)+\cos\theta(t)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t))

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda(t)\kappa^{T}(t)(\cos\theta(t)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(t)+\sin\theta(t)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t))

\displaystyle=(a(t)+\dot{\lambda}(t))\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}(t)+(\lambda(t)\kappa^{T}(t)\sin\theta(t)+(b(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t))\cos\theta(t))\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t)

\displaystyle=\overline{a}(t)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}(t)+\overline{b}(t)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\overline{a}(t)+\overline{b}(t))\overline{\ell}^{+}(t)+\frac{1}{2}(\overline{a}(t)-\overline{b}(t))\overline{\ell}^{-}(t),

where

\displaystyle\overline{a}(t)=a(t)+\dot{\lambda}(t),\ \overline{b}(t)=\lambda(t)\kappa^{T}(t)\sin\theta(t)+(b(t)+\lambda(t)\kappa_{1}(t))\cos\theta(t),

\displaystyle\overline{\ell}^{+}(t)=\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}(t)+\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t),\ \overline{\ell}^{-}(t)=\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}(t)-\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{S}(t).

It follows that
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}
is a lightcone framed curve.
Hence,
(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})
and
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\ell}^{+},\overline{\ell}^{-})
are
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T})
-mates.

(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-})

(\gamma,\ell^{+},\ell^{-}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\times\Delta_{4}

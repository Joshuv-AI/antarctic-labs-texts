Even Cone Spherical Metrics: Blow-Up at a Cone Singularity.
Ting-Jung Kuo, Xuanpu Liang, Ping-Hsiang Wu

Abstract
. We study families of spherical metrics on the flat torus E τ E_{\tau} = = ℂ / Λ τ \mathbb{C}/\Lambda_{\tau} with blow-up behavior at prescribed conical singularities at 0 0 and ± p \pm p , where the cone angle at 0 0 is 6 ​ π 6\pi , and at ± p \pm p is 4 ​ π 4\pi . We prove that the existence of such a necessarily unique, even family of spherical metrics is completely determined by the geometry of the torus: such a family exists if and only if the Green function G ⁡ ( z , τ ) G(z;\tau) admits a pair of nontrivial critical points ± a \pm a . In this case, the cone point p p must equal a a , and the corresponding monodromy data is ( 2 ​ r , 2 ​ s ) \left(2r,2s\right) , where a = r + s ​ τ . a=r+s\tau. An explicit transformation relating this family to the one with a single conical singularity of angle 6 ​ π 6\pi at the origin is established in Theorem 1.4 . A rigidity result for rhombic tori is proved in Theorem 1.5 .



1.
Introduction

Let
E_{\tau}:=\mathbb{C}/\Lambda_{\tau}
, where
\Lambda_{\tau}

=

\mathbb{Z}\oplus\tau\mathbb{Z}
and
\tau\in

\mathbb{H}

=

\left\{\tau|\operatorname{Im}\tau>0\right\}
, denote the flat torus. The study of
cone spherical metrics on
E_{\tau}
, i.e. metrics of constant Gaussian
curvature
1
with prescribed conical singularities and angles, has been a
central problem in conformal geometry. Significant progress has been made in
this direction; see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
13
,
10
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
19
]
.

Let
p\in E_{\tau}
. Throughout this paper, we assume

(1.1)

p\in E_{\tau}\setminus E_{\tau}[2],

where
E_{\tau}[2]
denotes the 2-torsion points on
E_{\tau}
. That is

E_{\tau}[2]

=

\{\frac{\omega_{k}}{2},k

=

0,1,2,3.\}
Here
\omega_{0}=0,\omega_{1}=1,\omega_{2}=\tau,\omega_{3}=1+\tau.

We investigate the following curvature equation:

(1.2)

\Delta u+e^{u}=8\pi\delta_{0}+4\pi(\delta_{p}+\delta_{-p})\text{ on }E_{\tau}\text{,}

where
\delta_{q}
denotes the Dirac measure at
q\in E_{\tau}
. Any solution

u
gives rise to the conformal metric
\frac{1}{2}e^{u}dz^{2}
, which has
constant positive curvature
1
on the elliptic curve
E_{\tau}
, with a
conical singularity of cone angle
6\pi
at
0
, and conical singularities of
cone angle
4\pi
at
\pm p
.

Physically, equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
appears in statistical mechanics as
the mean-field equation for the Euler flow in Onsager’s vortex model

[
2
]
, and it is also connected to the study of self-dual condensates in
the Chern–Simons–Higgs model of superconductivity
[
22
,
23
]
.

The curvature equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
is integrable equation in the sense
of the classical Liouville Theorem, which asserts that any solution
u(z)
of
equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
is given as the form

(1.3)

u(z)=\log\frac{8|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}}.

See
[
3
,
24
]
for a proof. Here,
f(z)
is locally
meromorphic and is commonly referred to as the developing map in the literature.

When the total cone angle is an odd multiple of
2\pi
, the developing map

f(z)
associated with a solution
u(z)
must satisfy type II condition:

(1.4)

f(z+1)=e^{-4\pi is}f(z)\text{ and }f(z+\tau)=e^{4\pi ir}f(z)

for some real pair
\left(r,s\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
; see
[
3
]
for
a proof. Under this condition (
1.4
), any solution
u(z)
to the
curvature equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
gives rise to a one-parameter family of
solutions

(1.5)

u_{\beta}(z)=\log\frac{8e^{2\beta}\left|f^{\prime}\right|^{2}}{\left(1+e^{2\beta}\left|f\right|^{2}\right)^{2}},\text{ }\beta\in\mathbb{R}\text{,}

which corresponds to replacing
f
by
f_{\beta}:=e^{\beta}f
. The real pair

\left(r,s\right)
is referred to as the
monodromy data
of the
family
\left\{u_{\beta}(z;\tau,p)|\beta\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
. When
necessary, we denote the family as
\left\{u_{\beta}(z;\tau,p)\right\}
or

\left\{u_{\beta}(z;\tau,p)\right\}_{(r,s)}
to emphasize the underlying monodromy.

We say that the family is an
even

family
if it contains an
even solution, which is necessarily unique. Equivalently, there exists
\beta

such that
f_{\beta}(-z)

=

\frac{1}{f_{\beta}(z)}
. Otherwise, it is said
to be an
non-even family
.

The parameter
\beta

\in

\mathbb{R}
serves as a scaling parameter along a
one-parameter family in the moduli space, under which the geometry of the
solution degenerates into delta-mass configurations characterized by curvature
concentration. Specifically, the family
\left\{u_{\beta}(z;\tau,p)\right\}
exhibits blow-up behavior: as
\beta\rightarrow+\infty
, blow-up occurs at
the zeros of
f
, and as
\beta\rightarrow-\infty
, at its poles.

In our previous work
[
18
, Corollary 3.10]
, we proved that when
E_{\tau}
is the
rhombic torus
, i.e.,
\tau=\rho=e^{\pi i/3}
, the curvature
equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
with

\tau=\rho=e^{\pi i/3}\text{ and }p=\frac{1+\rho}{3}

admits a
unique even family
of solutions

(1.6)

\left\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\rho,\frac{1+\rho}{3})\right\}

which exhibit blow-up behavior at the cone singularities

\pm\frac{1+\rho}{3}\text{ mod }\Lambda_{\rho}

as
\beta\rightarrow\pm\infty
, respectively.

Mathematically, the blow-up of a cone spherical metric at a cone point
corresponds to a concentration of curvature that disrupts the regular
geometric structure, as the metric diverges to infinity at the singularity.

A natural question arises:

Question
:
For which pairs
(\tau,p),
with
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and
p\in E_{\tau},
satisfying
(
1.1
), does the the curvature equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
admit an even family of solutions
\{\textbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,p)\}
that blow up behavior
at the cone singularities
\pm p
as
\beta\rightarrow\pm\infty
, respectively?

Since each
\textbf{u}_{\beta}
exhibits a conical singularity at
p
, specifically,

\textbf{u}_{\beta}(z)=2\log|z-p|+O(1),

where the bounded term
O(1)
depends on
\beta
, the blow-up at this point is to be understood as occurring at
p
fter the conical singularity has been removed through the corresponding Green function. That is,

\textbf{u}_{\beta}+4\pi\,G(z-p;\tau)

blows up at
p
as
\beta\to+\infty
.

To address the above question , we first introduce the Green function

G(z;\tau)
on
E_{\tau}
, defined by

-\Delta G(z;\tau)=\delta_{0}-\frac{1}{\left|E_{\tau}\right|}\text{,
}\int_{E_{\tau}}G(z;\tau)=0,

where
\left|E_{\tau}\right|
denotes the area of the torus. The
function
G(z;\tau)
is even and has a unique singularity at
z=0
mod

\Lambda_{\tau}
. By symmetry, it follows that

\nabla G(\frac{\omega_{k}}{2};\tau)=0,k=1,2,3.

That is, each half-period
\frac{\omega_{k}}{2}
is always a critical point of

G(z;\tau)
, and these are referred to as the
trivial critical
points
. As shown in
[
20
]
, for any torus
E_{\tau}
, the Green
function
G(z;\tau)
either has exactly three trivial critical points, or it
additionally admits a pair of
nontrivial critical points

\pm a\in E_{\tau}
, where

a=r+s\tau,\text{ }\left(r,s\right)\in\mathbb{[}0,1\mathbb{]}^{2}\setminus\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

Since nontrivial critical points always appear as a pair
\pm a
, we may,
without loss of generality, assume

(1.7)

a=r+s\tau\text{, }\left(r,s\right)\in[0,1/2]\times[0,1]\setminus\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\text{.}

The existence of nontrivial critical points
\pm\sigma
in the Green function
corresponds to flat tori that admit a necessarily unique, even family of
solutions
\{v_{\beta}(z;\tau)\}
to the following curvature equation:

(1.8)

\Delta v+e^{v}=8\pi\delta_{0}\text{ on }E_{\tau}.

This family has monodromy data
\left(r,s\right)
and exhibits blow-up
behavior at
\pm a

as
\beta\rightarrow\pm\infty
,
respectively, where
a=r+s\tau
; see
[
20
]
.

We are now ready to state our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
. Then there exists
p\in E_{\tau}\setminus E_{\tau}[2]
such that the curvature equation
(
1.2
)
τ,p
admits an even family of solutions

\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,p)\}
, blowing up at the cone singularities
\pm p
as
\beta\rightarrow\pm\infty
, respectively, if and only
if the Green function
G(z;\tau)
admits a pair of nontrivial critical point
\pm a
. Moreover, in this case, the even family is unique, and
p=a
. Equivalently,

\left\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,p)\right\}=\left\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\right\}.

Remark 1.2
.

Surprisingly, Theorem
1.1
reveals that the existence of such
an even blow-up family is governed entirely by the intrinsic geometry of the
torus. Specifically, the moduli parameter
\tau
and the cone singularity
positions
\pm p
are completely determined by the structure of the Green
function
G(z;\tau)
on the torus.

Assume

G(z;\tau)

admits a pair of nontrivial critical
points.
According to Theorem
1.1
, these two even families

\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\}
and
\left\{v_{\beta}(z;\tau)\right\}

both exhibit the
same
blow-up behavior at
\pm a

as
\beta\rightarrow\pm\infty
, respectively. However, they correspond to two
distinct families of cone spherical metrics on
E_{\tau}
: the former has cone
angles
6\pi
at
0
and
4\pi
at
\pm a
, whereas the latter has a single
conical singularity of angle
6\pi
at
0
only.

This naturally raises the question:
What is the monodromy data of the
even family

\left\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z,\tau,a)\right\}

obtained in Theorem
1.1
? Our second main result
provides the answer.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
G(z;\tau)
admits a pair of nontrivial critical
points
\pm a
satisfying (
1.7
). Then the
monodromy data of the unique even family
\left\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\right\}
obtained in Theorem
1.1
, for the
curvature equation (
1.8
) is precisely
\left(2r,2s\right)
mode
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Since both
\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\}
and
\left\{v_{\beta}(z;\tau)\right\}
are even families, without loss of generality, we may
denote by
\mathbf{u}_{0}(z;\tau,a)
and
v_{0}(z;\tau)
the unique even
solutions within each family. Let
f(z;\tau,a)
and
h(z;\tau)
be their
respective type II developing maps, so that

(1.9)

\mathbf{u}_{0}(z;\tau,a)=\log\frac{8|f^{\prime}(z;\tau,a)|^{2}}{(1+|f(z;\tau,a)|^{2})^{2}}\text{, }v_{0}(z;\tau)=\log\frac{8|h^{\prime}(z;\tau)|^{2}}{(1+|h(z;\tau)|^{2})^{2}}.

Our third main theorem establishes the precise transformation relating these
two even families.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that the Green function
G(z;\tau)
admits a pair of nontrivial critical points
\pm a
satisfying (
1.7
). Let

\mathbf{u}_{0}(z;\tau,a)
and
v_{0}(z;\tau)
denote the unique even
solutions in the families
\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\}
and
\left\{v_{\beta}(z;\tau)\right\}
, respectively, as obtained in Theorem

1.1
and the single-source equation (
1.8
).
Suppose
f(z;\tau,a)
and
h(z;\tau)
are their type II developing maps as in
(
1.9
). Then

f(z;\tau,a)=h(z;\tau)^{2}.

This identity establishes an explicit correspondence between the two even families.

Recently, it was shown in
[
18
, Theorem 3.3]
that the curvature equation

(1.10)

\Delta u+e^{u}=8\pi\delta_{0}+4\pi(\delta_{a}+\delta_{-a})\text{ on }E_{\tau},

admits another family of solutions, denoted by
\left\{\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\right\}_{(r,s)}
, which shares the same monodromy data
\left(r,s\right)
as
\left\{v_{\beta}(z;\tau)\right\}_{(r,s)}
arising from
the single-source equation (
1.8
). Moreover,

\left\{\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\right\}_{(r,s)}\text{ is an even family of \eqref{Curvature 1}${}_{\tau,a}$ }\Longleftrightarrow\,\wp(a;\tau)=0;

see Theorem 3.3-(ii) and (3.17) in
[
18
]
for
p_{*}=a=r+s\tau
.

We now state our final main result, which establishes a rigidity phenomenon.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume
G(z;\tau)
admits a pair of nontrivial critical
points
\pm a
satisfying (
1.7
). Then for the
curvature equation (
1.10
), the two families

\left\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\right\}_{(2r,2s)}=\left\{\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\right\}_{(r,s)}

if and only if

\tau=\rho=e^{\pi i/3},\text{ }\left(r,s\right)=\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3}\right),\text{ }a=\frac{1+\rho}{3}

In such case, the even family
\left\{\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\right\}_{(1/3,1/3)}
coincides with the explicit example presented in
(
1.6
).

Accordingly,
\left\{\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\right\}_{(r,s)}
is a
non-even family if
\left(r,s\right)\not=\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3}\right)
. In such case, the blow-up configuration is also completely
determined by
a
; see
[
18
, Theorem 3.8]
. Thus, the non-even family

\{\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\}_{(r,s)}
and the even family
\left\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\right\}_{(2r,2s)}
represent two distinct types
of solutions to the same curvature equation (
1.10
). These
two families are linked via the even family
\left\{v_{\beta}(z;\tau)\right\}_{(r,s)}
.

Remarkably, while all three families are governed by the same underlying torus
geometry, they reflect it in fundamentally different ways. However, the
precise geometric distinction between
\left\{\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}(z;\tau,a)\right\}_{(r,s)}
and
\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}(z;

\tau,a)\}_{(2r,2s)}
remains to be clarified. In particular, the behavior of these
families as
a\rightarrow\frac{\omega_{k}}{2},
for
k=0,1,2,3
is not yet
fully understood. Clarifying the geometric implications of this distinction
represents a promising direction for further study.

2.
Integrability and Spectral Theory for the Curvature Equation

Let
\wp(z;\tau)
be the Weierstrass elliptic function with periods
\omega_{1}=1
and
\omega_{2}=\tau
, defined by

\wp\left(z;\tau\right)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}+\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash(0,0)}\left[\frac{1}{\left(z-m-n\tau\right)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(m+n\tau)^{2}}\right].

The Weierstrass zeta function is defined by

\zeta\left(z;\tau\right):=-\int^{z}\wp(\xi;\tau)\,d\xi,

which is quasi-periodic, satisfying

\zeta\left(z+\omega_{j};\tau\right)=\zeta\left(z;\tau\right)+\eta_{j}(\tau)\text{, }j=1,2,

where
\eta_{1}(\tau)
and
\eta_{2}(\tau)
are the corresponding quasi-periods.
And let
\sigma(z;\tau)
be the Weierstrass sigma function, given by

\sigma(z;\tau):=\exp\int^{z}\zeta(\xi;\tau)d\xi,

which satisfies the following transformation law:

(2.1)

\sigma(z+\omega_{j};\tau)=-e^{\eta_{j}\left(z+\frac{\omega_{j}}{2}\right)}\sigma(z;\tau),\quad\,j=1,2.

For brevity, we denote
\wp(z;\tau)
,
\zeta(z;\tau)
and
\sigma(z;\tau)
simply by
\wp(z)
,
\zeta(z)
and
\sigma(z)
, respectively. For further properties of these classical elliptic functions, see
[
1
]
.

The existence of solutions to the curvature equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
is closely related to the study of the monodromy of the following generalized Lamé-type equation:

(2.2)

y^{\prime\prime}(z)=q(z;A)\,y(z)\quad\text{on}\quad E_{\tau},

where the potential is given by

(2.3)

q(z;A)=\left(\begin{array}[c]{l}2\wp(z)+\dfrac{3}{4}\left(\wp(z+p)+\wp(z-p)\right)\\
+A\left(\zeta(z+p)-\zeta(z-p)\right)+B\end{array}\right),

with
B
determined by

(2.4)

B=A^{2}-\zeta(2p)\,A-\dfrac{3}{4}\wp(2p)-2\wp(p).

Since the potential
q(z;A)
is an even elliptic function, this corresponds to seeking an even family of solutions to the curvature equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
.

Under (
2.4
), all singularities of (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
are
apparent
; namely, all local solutions are free of logarithmic singularities.

We briefly recall the monodromy representation of (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
under the apparent condition (
2.4
).
Note that the local exponents at
z=0,\pm p
are
-1,\,2
and
-1/2,\,3/2
, respectively. Consequently, the corresponding local monodromy matrices are

N_{0}=I_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{\pm p}=-I_{2}.

Fix a base point
z_{0}\in E_{\tau}\setminus\{0,\pm p\}
such that the fundamental cycles
z_{0}\mapsto z_{0}+\omega_{j}
,
j=1,2
, do not intersect with the points
\{0,\pm p\}+\Lambda_{\tau}
. Let

Y(z;z_{0})=(y_{1}(z;z_{0}),\,y_{2}(z;z_{0}))^{t}

be a fundamental solution of the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
near
z_{0}
. The global monodromy matrices
M_{j}(p,A)
,
j=1,2
, are defined by analytic continuation along two fundamental cycles:

Y(z+\omega_{j};z_{0})=M_{j}(p,A)\,Y(z;z_{0}),\quad j=1,2.

These matrices
M_{j}(p,A)
,
j=1,2
, satisfy the following monodromy relation:

M_{1}(p,A)\,M_{2}(p,A)=M_{2}(p,A)\,M_{1}(p,A).

This leads to the following two cases:

(i) Completely Reducible Case:
The matrices
M_{j}(p,A)
,
j=1,2
, can be simultaneously diagonalized, taking the form

(2.5)

M_{1}(p,A)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{-2\pi is}&0\\
0&e^{2\pi is}\end{array}\right),\quad M_{2}(p,A)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{2\pi ir}&0\\
0&e^{-2\pi ir}\end{array}\right),

for some
(r,s)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

(ii) Non-Completely Reducible Case:
The matrices
M_{j}(p,A)
,
j=1,2
, cannot be simultaneously diagonalized. In this situation, there exists
\mathcal{D}\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
such that, up to a common conjugation,
M_{j}(p,A)
,
j=1,2
, can be represented by

(2.6)

M_{1}(p,A)=\pm\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
1&1\end{array}\right),\quad M_{2}(p,A)=\pm\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
\mathcal{D}&1\end{array}\right).

In the case
\mathcal{D}=\infty
, this should be interpreted as

(2.7)

M_{1}(p,A)=\pm\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right),\quad M_{2}(p,A)=\pm\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
1&1\end{array}\right).

The pair
(r,s)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
in Case (i) and
\mathcal{D}\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
in Case (ii), are respectively called the monodromy data of equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
.

The following theorem establishes the deep connection between the even family solutions of the curvature equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
and the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
p\in E_{\tau}\setminus E_{\tau}[2]
. The curvature equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
on
E_{\tau}
admits an
even
family of solutions if and only if there exists
A\in\mathbb{C}
, with
B
given by (
2.4
), such that the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is completely reducible with unitary monodromy.

Theorem
2.1
was proved in
[
9
]
.
For the convenience of the readers, we briefly summarize the idea of the proof:

Proof.

For the necessity, suppose
u(z)
is an even solution of (
1.2
)
τ,p
. Consider the function
u_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}u_{z}^{2}
. By (
1.2
)
τ,p
, it is easy to verify

\left(u_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}u_{z}^{2}\right)_{\bar{z}}=0,\quad\forall z\notin\{0,\pm p\}.

Since the local behavior of
u(z)
at
0
and
\pm p
are given by

u(z)=4\ln|z|+O(1)\quad\text{near }0,

and

u(z)=2\ln|z\mp p|+O(1)\quad\text{near }\pm p,

we obtain that
u_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}u_{z}^{2}
has double poles at
0
and
\pm p
. Consequently,
u_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}u_{z}^{2}
is an even elliptic function, which can be expressed in terms of classical elliptic functions as below:

\displaystyle u_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}u_{z}^{2}=-2\Biggl(

\displaystyle\,2\wp(z)+\frac{3}{4}\left(\wp(z+p)+\wp(z-p)\right)

(2.8)

\displaystyle+A\left(\zeta(z+p)-\zeta(z-p)\right)+B\Biggr)=-2q(z;A),

where

A=\frac{1}{2}Res_{z=p}\left(u_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}u_{z}^{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}Res_{z=-p}\left(u_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}u_{z}^{2}\right),

and
B\in\mathbb{C}
. We will show that
B
is given by (
2.4
).

By Liouville Theorem, there exists a developing map
f(z)
such that

(2.9)

u(z)=\log\frac{8|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}{\left(1+|f(z)|^{2}\right)^{2}}.

A direct computation shows that the Schwartzian derivative
\{f;z\}
satisfies

(2.10)

\left\{f;z\right\}=u_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}u_{z}^{2}.

By (
2.10
) and (
2.8
), we obtain

\{f;z\}=-2q(z;A).

A classical result then implies the existence of two linearly independent solutions
y_{1}(z)
and
y_{2}(z)
of generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
such that

(2.11)

f(z)=\frac{y_{1}(z)}{y_{2}(z)}.

Define the Wronskian

W=y_{1}^{\prime}(z)y_{2}(z)-y_{1}(z)y_{2}^{\prime}(z),

which is a nonzero constant. Substituting (
2.11
) into (
2.10
) yields

2\sqrt{2}We^{-\frac{1}{2}u(z)}=|y_{1}(z)|^{2}+|y_{2}(z)|^{2}.

Since
u(z)
is single-valued and doubly periodic, it follows that the monodromy matrices with respect to
(y_{1}(z),y_{2}(z))
lie in
SU(2)
, that is, the monodromy is unitary. This implies that the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is completely reducible.

Note that if (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
has logarithmic singularities at
\pm p
, the local monodromy matrix at
\pm p
can not be in
\mathrm{SU}(2)
. Therefore, (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is apparent at
\pm p
, which implies that
B
is given by (
2.4
).

For the sufficiency, it suffices to prove the existence of even solution to (
1.2
)
τ,p
. Assume there exist
A\in\mathbb{C}
and
B
as given by (
2.3
) such that (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is completely reducible and has unitary monodromy. Then by Theorem
3.1
, there exists a fundamental solution
Y(z)=(y_{1}(z),y_{2}(z))^{t}
such that the monodromy matrices
M_{j}(p,A)
satisfy Case (i) with
(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Actually, we may take
y_{2}(z)=y_{1}(-z)
.

Define

(2.12)

f(z):=\frac{y_{1}(z)}{y_{2}(z)}=\frac{y_{1}(z)}{y_{1}(-z)}.

Since the local exponent of
y_{j}(z)
at
\pm p
is either
-\frac{1}{2}
or
\frac{3}{2}
, then the local exponent of
f
at
\pm p
is either
0
or
\pm 2
, which implies
f(z)
is single-valued near
z=\pm p
. Thus
f(z)
can be extended to be a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
, and satsfies type II condition (
1.4
) for
(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Define

(2.13)

u(z):=\log\frac{8|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}{\left(1+|f(z)|^{2}\right)^{2}}.

Then by (
1.4
),
u(z)
is doubly periodic. From (
2.12
), we can derive

(2.14)

\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right)^{2}=-2q(z;A).

Since the local exponent of
y_{j}(z)
at
0
is either
-1
or
2
, then the local exponent
\rho
of
f(z)
at
0
belongs to
\{0,\pm 3\}
. Then by (
2.14
), we obtain

f(z)=\begin{cases}f(0)+a_{3}z^{3}+O(z^{4}),\quad&a_{3}\neq 0,\quad\text{if }\rho=0;\\
a_{-3}z^{-3}+O(z^{-2}),\quad&a_{-3}\neq 0,\quad\text{if }\rho=-3;\\
a_{3}^{\prime}z^{3}+O(z^{4}),\quad&a_{3}^{\prime}\neq 0,\quad\text{if }\rho=3.\end{cases}

Substituting into (
2.13
), we see that

u(z)=4\ln|z|+O(1)\quad\text{near }0.

A similar analysis shows that

u(z)=2\ln|z\mp p|+O(1)\quad\text{near }z=\pm p,

and

\Delta u+e^{u}=0\quad\text{on }E_{\tau}\setminus\{0,\pm p\}.

Therefore,
u(z)
is a solution to (
1.2
)
τ,p
. Moreover, since
f(z)=\frac{1}{f(-z)}
by (
2.12
), it follows that
u(z)=u(-z)
is an even solution.
∎

In view of Theorem
2.1
, two fundamental issues arise:

(1)

When does a generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
admit completely reducible monodromy representation?

(2)

After (1), when is the monodromy representation unitary, up to a conjugation?

To address (1), we need to develop the spectral theory of the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
. We borrow the idea from elliptic Korteweg-De Vries (KdV) theory and these computations had been completed in
[
18
]
. For the completeness of this paper, we briefly introduce them as below.

Consider the following second symmetric product of equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
:

(2.15)

\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)-4q(z;A)\,\Phi^{\prime}(z)-2q^{\prime}(z;A)\,\Phi(z)=0\quad\text{on}\quad E_{\tau}.

The next theorem asserts the existence and uniqueness of the elliptic solution to equation (
2.15
).

Theorem 2.2

(
[
18
, Theorem 4.5]
)
.

Up to a nonzero multiple, there exists a unique non-trivial elliptic solution
\Phi_{e}(z;A)
of (
2.15
) given by

(2.16)

\Phi_{e}(z;A)=\left[\begin{array}[c]{l}\wp(z)+\left(\dfrac{A}{2}-\dfrac{3}{8}\dfrac{\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)}{\wp^{\prime}(p)}\right)\left(\zeta(z+p)-\zeta(z-p)\right)\\[10.0pt]
-A^{2}+\left(\dfrac{\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)}{\,2\wp^{\prime}(p)\,}-\zeta(p)\right)A\\[10.0pt]
-\wp(p)+\dfrac{3}{4}\dfrac{\,\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)\,}{\wp^{\prime}(p)}\zeta(p)+\dfrac{3}{16}\dfrac{\,\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)^{2}\,}{\wp^{\prime}(p)^{2}}\end{array}\right].

Define

(2.17)

Q(z;A):=\dfrac{1}{2}\Phi_{e}(z;A)\,\Phi_{e}^{\prime\prime}(z;A)-\dfrac{1}{4}(\Phi_{e}^{\prime}(z;A))^{2}-q(z;A)\,\Phi_{e}^{2}(z;A).

Since
\Phi_{e}(z;A)
solves (
2.15
), it follows that
\frac{d}{dz}Q(z;A)=0
. Hence,
Q(z;A)
is independent of
z
, we therefore write
Q(A)
. Furthermore, since
\Phi_{e}(z;A)
and
q(z;A)
are both polynomials in
A
,
Q(A)
is then a polynomial in
A
.

We refer to
Q(A)
as the
spectral polynomial
associated with the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
, which is computed explicitly in
[
18
, Theorem 4.5]
:

Theorem 2.3

(
[
18
, Theorem 4.5]
)
.

The spectral polynomial
Q(A)
associated with (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is explicitly given by

(2.18)

Q(A)=-Y_{1}(A)\,Y_{2}(A),

where

\displaystyle Y_{1}(A)

\displaystyle=A^{3}-\frac{5\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)}{4\wp^{\prime}(p)}A^{2}+3\left(\wp(p)+\frac{1}{16}\frac{\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)^{2}}{\wp^{\prime}(p)^{2}}\right)A

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{\wp^{\prime}(p)}{2}-\frac{9\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)}{4\wp^{\prime}(p)}\wp(p)+\frac{9}{64}\frac{\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)^{3}}{\wp^{\prime}(p)^{3}},

\displaystyle Y_{2}(A)

\displaystyle=A^{3}-\frac{\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)}{4\wp^{\prime}(p)}A^{2}-\frac{5}{16}\frac{\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)^{2}}{\wp^{\prime}(p)^{2}}A+2\wp^{\prime}(p)-\frac{3}{64}\frac{\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)^{3}}{\wp^{\prime}(p)^{3}}.

The
spectral curve
associated with
Q(A)
is defined by

(2.19)

\Gamma(\tau,p):=\left\{\,(A,C)\,|\,C^{2}=Q(A)\,\right\}.

For
P=(A,C)\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
, denote its dual point by
P^{*}:=(A,-C)\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
.

We now introduce the Baker-Akhiezer
function at
P\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
. First, we define the meromorphic function

(2.20)

\phi(P;z):=\dfrac{\,iC(P)+\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{e}^{\prime}(z;A)\,}{\Phi_{e}(z;A)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

It is easy to verify that
\phi(P;z)
satisfies the Riccati equation:

(2.21)

\phi^{\prime}(P;z)=q(z;A)-\phi^{2}(P;z).

Proposition 2.4
.

The meromorphic function
\phi(P;z)
has at most simple poles. Moreover, if
q_{0}
is a pole of
\phi(P;z)
, then

(2.22)

\operatorname{Res}_{z=q_{0}}\phi(P;z)=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if \,}q_{0}=0,\\
-1/2&\text{if \,}q_{0}=\pm p,\\
~1&\text{if \,}q_{0}\notin\{0,\pm p\}.\end{cases}

The proof is same as the argument in
[
18
, Proposition 4.3]
, based on the Riccati equation (
2.21
).

Fix a base point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,\pm p\}
. For each
P\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
, define the
Baker-Akhiezer
function by

(2.23)

\psi(P;z,z_{0}):=\exp\left(\int_{z_{0}}^{z}\phi(P;\xi)\,d\xi\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

where the integration path is chosen to avoid the singularities of the meromorphic function
\phi(P;\xi)
.

By Proposition
2.4
, the Baker-Akhiezer function
\psi(P;z,z_{0})
is a multivalued meromorphic function with two local branches near
z=\pm p
. Moreover, by the Riccati equation (
2.21
),
\psi(P;z,z_{0})
solves the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
with parameter
A(P)
, where
A(P)
denotes the
A
-coordinate of
P\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
.

Note that
A(P)=A(P^{*})
. Consequently, both
\psi(P;z,z_{0})
and
\psi(P^{*};z,z_{0})
solve the same equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
with parameter
A=A(P)=A(P^{*})
. Therefore,
P,P^{*}\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
represent the same generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
with parameter
A(P)
.

We recall two basic identities for the Baker-Akhiezer functions
\psi(P;z,z_{0})
and
\psi(P^{*};z,z_{0})
associated with (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
Firstly,

(2.24)

\psi(P;z,z_{0})\,\psi(P^{*};z,z_{0})=\dfrac{\Phi_{e}(z;A)}{\Phi_{e}(z_{0};A)}.

Moreover, their Wronskian (with respect to
z
) is given by

(2.25)

W(\psi(P;z,z_{0}),\,\psi(P^{*};z,z_{0}))=\dfrac{2iC}{\Phi_{e}(z_{0};A)}.

The proofs can be found in
[
17
]
.

Since different choices of the base point
z_{0}\notin\{0,\pm p\}
change
\psi(P;z,z_{0})
only by a nonzero multiple, we henceforth write

\psi(P;z,z_{0})=\psi(P;z),\quad\psi(P^{*};z,z_{0})=\psi(P^{*};z).

As a consequence of (
2.25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
P=(A,C)\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
.
The Baker-Akhiezer functions
\psi(P;z)
and
\psi(P^{*};z)
is linearly independent if and only if
Q(A)\neq 0
.

By the definitions (
2.20
) and (
2.23
), together with the fact that
\Phi_{e}
is elliptic, it follows that
\psi(P;z)
is an elliptic function of the second kind; see, for instance,
[
17
,
18
]
for a proof. Hence there exist
\lambda_{j}(P)
,
j=1,2
such that

(2.26)

\psi(P;z+\omega_{j})=\lambda_{j}(P)\,\psi(P;z),\quad j=1,2.

Moreover, by (
2.24
), we have

(2.27)

\lambda_{j}(P^{*})=\dfrac{1}{\,\lambda_{j}(P)\,}.

Theorem
2.5
implies that if
Q(A)\neq 0
, then the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is completely reducible. In fact, the converse also holds. The following theorem answers the issue (1).

Theorem 2.6
.

For
P=(A,C)\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
, the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is completely reducible if and only if
Q(A)\neq 0
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the necessary part. Assume that (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is completely reducible with
Q(A)=0
. By Theorem
2.5
, both Baker-Akhiezer functions
\psi(P;z)
and
\psi(P^{*};z_{0})
are linearly dependent; namely,

(2.28)

\psi(P;z)=c\,\psi(P^{*};z),\quad\text{for some $c\neq 0$.}

Complete reducibility guarantees the existence of two linearly independent
solutions
y_{1}(z;A)
,
y_{2}(z;A)
such that

\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}y_{1}(z+\omega_{j};A)\\
y_{2}(z+\omega_{j};A)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}\widehat{\lambda_{j}}(P)&0\\
0&\dfrac{1}{\,\widehat{\lambda_{j}}(P)\,}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}y_{1}(z;A)\\
y_{2}(z;A)\end{array}\right).

Hence
y_{1}(z;A)\,y_{2}(z;A)
is an elliptic solution of (
2.15
). By Theorem
2.2
and
(
2.24
), we obtain

(2.29)

y_{1}(z;A)\,y_{2}(z;A)=\Phi_{e}(z;A)=\psi(P;z)\,\psi(P^{*};z)=c\,\psi^{2}(P;z).

It follows from (
2.29
) that
y_{1}(z;A)
and
y_{2}(z;A)
are linearly dependent, a contradiction. Hence
Q(A)\neq 0
.
∎

3.
The Monodromy Theory for the ODE

In this section, we study the monodromy theory of the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
via the Baker-Akhiezer function
\psi(P;z)
at
P=(A,C)\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
.

Recall from (
2.26
) that
\psi(P;z)
is an elliptic function of the second kind.
Define
(r(P),s(P))\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
by

(3.1)

\lambda_{1}(P)=e^{-2\pi is(P)}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{2}(P)=e^{2\pi ir(P)}.

Thus
(r(P),s(P))
is defined modulo
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(i.e., up to integer translations).
By (
2.27
), we have

(3.2)

r(P^{*})=-r(P)\quad\text{and}\quad s(P^{*})=-s(P).

We remark that if
(r(P),s(P))\notin\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, at least one of
\lambda_{1}(P),\lambda_{2}(P)
is not
\pm 1
.
Without loss of generality, assume
\lambda_{1}(P)\neq\pm 1
, then
\lambda_{1}(P)
and
\lambda_{1}(P^{*})
are distinct eigenvalues of the monodromy matrices
M_{j}(p,A)
,
j=1,2
, which implies that
\psi(P;z)
and
\psi(P^{*};z)
are linearly independent. Hence, if
(r(P),s(P))\notin\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is completely reducible. In fact, this condition is sufficient as well.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
P=(A,C)\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
.
Then the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is completely reducible if and only if

\left(r(P),s(P)\right)\notin\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the necessity. Suppose that the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is completely reducible. Then
Q(A)\neq 0
, or equivalently, the two Baker-Akhiezer functions
\psi(P;z)
and
\psi(P^{*};z)
are linearly independent.

Assume, for contradiction, that
(r(P),s(P))\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Then

\bigl(\lambda_{1}(P),\lambda_{2}(P)\bigr)\in\{(1,1),(1,-1),(-1,1),(-1,-1)\},

and hence the function

\psi^{2}(P;z)+\psi^{2}(P^{*};z)

is an elliptic solution of (
2.15
). By Theorem
2.2
and equation (
2.24
),

\psi(P;z)\,\psi(P^{*};z)=\Phi_{e}(z;A)=\psi^{2}(P;z)+\psi^{2}(P^{*};z),

up to a nonzero multiple. Thus
\psi(P;z)
and
\psi(P^{*};z)
have common zeros, contradicts to their linear independence.
∎

If the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
is completely reducible, the Baker-Akhiezer functions
\psi(P;z)
and
\psi(P^{*};z)
form a fundamental system with monodromy matrices:

M_{1}(p,A)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{-2\pi is(P)}&0\\
0&e^{2\pi is(P)}\end{array}\right),~M_{2}(p,A)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{2\pi ir(P)}&0\\
0&e^{-2\pi ir(P)}\end{array}\right).

Thus, up to a
\pm
sign and modulo
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the pair
(r(P),s(P))\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is the monodromy data of (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
.

If, in addition, the monodromy matrices
M_{j}(p,A)
,
j=1,2
, are unitary, then
(r(P),s(P))\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
By Theorem
2.1
, the curvature equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
admits an even solution
u(z)
.
As in the sufficiency part of its proof, the developing map
f(z)
associated with
u(z)
can be chosen as

(3.3)

f(z)=\dfrac{\psi(P;z)}{\,\psi(P^{*};z)\,}.

From (
2.26
) and (
3.1
),
f(z)
is a type II developing map satisfying

(3.4)

f(z+\omega_{1})=e^{-4\pi is(P)},\quad f(z+\omega_{2})=e^{4\pi ir(P)}.

Therefore, by (
1.4
),
(r(P),s(P))
is also the monodromy data of the corresponding even family of solutions of curvature equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
. We summarize these as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose the curvature equation (
1.2
)
τ,p
admits an even solution
u(z)
associated with the generalized Lamé-type equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
. Then the monodromy data of this even solution, in the sense of (
1.4
), coincides with the monodromy data
(r(P),s(P))\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
of equation (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
, where
P=(A,C)\in\Gamma(\tau,p)
. Moreover, the type II developing map
f(z)
of
u(z)
is given by the ratio of the two Baker-Akhiezer functions given in (
3.3
).

To locate the blow-up points of an even family of solutions to (
1.2
)
τ,p
, we next analyze the zeros of the Baker-Akhiezer functions.

Since the Baker-Akhiezer function
\psi(P;z)
is an elliptic function of the second kind, it could be written as:

\psi(P;z)=e^{c(P)z}\,\frac{\,\sigma(z-a_{1}(P))\,\sigma(z-a_{2}(P))\,}{\sigma(z)\sqrt{\sigma(z+p)\,\sigma(z-p)}},

where
c(P)\in\mathbb{C}
and
a_{1}(P),a_{2}(P)\in E_{\tau}\setminus\{0\}
are the two zeros of
\psi(P;z)
. The following proposition establishes algebraic relations between
(r(P),s(P))
and
(a_{1}(P),a_{2}(P),c(P))
.

Proposition 3.3
.

With the above notations,

(3.5)

\begin{cases}~r(P)+s(P)\tau=a_{1}(P)+a_{2}(P),\\[5.0pt]
~r(P)\,\eta_{1}(\tau)+s(P)\,\eta_{2}(\tau)=c(P).\end{cases}

Proof.

From (
2.1
), we obtain

\psi(P;z+\omega_{j})=e^{c(P)\omega_{j}-\eta_{j}\left(a_{1}(P)+a_{2}(P)\right)}\psi(P;z),\quad\text{for }\,j=1,2.

Comparing with (
2.26
) and (
3.1
), we deduce

(3.6)

\begin{cases}~c(P)-\eta_{1}\left(a_{1}(P)+a_{2}(P)\right)=-2\pi i\,s(P),\\
~c(P)\tau-\eta_{2}\left(a_{1}(P)+a_{2}(P)\right)=2\pi i\,r(P).\end{cases}

Therefore, (
3.5
) follows from the Legendre relation

(3.7)

\tau\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}=2\pi i.

∎

In what follows, we derive algebraic equations satisfied by zeros
a_{1}(P),a_{2}(P)
. To this end, define

(3.8)

y_{a,c}(z):=e^{cz}\dfrac{\sigma(z-a_{1})\,\sigma(z-a_{2})}{\,\sigma(z)\sqrt{\sigma(z+p)\,\sigma(z-p)}\,},

where
c\in\mathbb{C}
,
a_{1},a_{2}\in E_{\tau}\setminus\{0\}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
y_{a,c}(z)
be defined in (
3.8
).

(i)

Suppose
a_{1}\neq a_{2}\in E_{\tau}\setminus\{0,\pm p\}
.
Then
y_{a,c}(z)
is a solution to (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
for some

A\in\mathbb{C}
, with
B
given by (
2.4
), if and only if

(3.9)

\dfrac{\wp^{\prime}(a_{1})}{\,\wp(p)-\wp(a_{1})\,}+\dfrac{\wp^{\prime}(a_{2})}{\,\wp(p)-\wp(a_{2})\,}=0.

In this case, the constants
c
and
A
are determined by

(3.10)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\zeta(a_{1})+\zeta(a_{2}),

(3.11)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\wp^{\prime}(p)-\wp^{\prime}(a_{1})}{\,2\left(\wp(p)-\wp(a_{1})\right)\,}+\dfrac{\wp^{\prime}(p)-\wp^{\prime}(a_{2})}{\,2\left(\wp(p)-\wp(a_{2})\right)\,}-\dfrac{\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)}{\,4\wp^{\prime}(p)\,}.

(ii)

Suppose
a_{1}=a_{2}\in\{\pm p\}
. Then
y_{a,c}(z)
is a solution
to (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
for some
A\in\mathbb{C}
, with
B
given by
(
2.4
), if and only if

(3.12)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\pm 2\zeta(p),

(3.13)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\,3\wp^{\prime\prime}(p)\,}{4\wp^{\prime}(p)}.

From the expression of
y_{a,c}(z)
in (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{y_{a,c}^{\prime}(z)}{y_{a,c}(z)}

\displaystyle=c+\zeta(z-a_{1})+\zeta(z-a_{2})-\zeta(z)-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\zeta(z+p)+\zeta(z-p)\right),

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{y_{a,c}^{\prime}(z)}{y_{a,c}(z)}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\wp(z)+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\wp(z+p)+\wp(z-p)\right)-\wp(z-a_{1})-\wp(z-a_{2}).

Define the elliptic function

(3.14)

g(z):=\left(\dfrac{y_{a,c}^{\prime}(z)}{y_{a,c}(z)}\right)^{\prime}+\left(\dfrac{y_{a,c}^{\prime}(z)}{y_{a,c}(z)}\right)^{2}\!-q(z;A).

Then
y_{a,c}(z)
solves (
2.2
)
τ,p,A
if and only if
g(z)\equiv 0
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Case (i).
Suppose
a_{1}\neq a_{2}\in E_{\tau}\setminus\{0,\pm p\}

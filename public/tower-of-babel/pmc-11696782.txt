Ayurveda treatment of nevus lipomatosus cutaneous superficialis through Ksharasutra: A case report
Authors: Ranjan Kumar Kasta, Ajit Kumar Pradhan, Priyanka Giri, Prasanta Kumar Sahoo
Published: 2024

Introduction

Nevus Lipomatosus cutaneous Superficialis (NLCS) is a benign cutaneous hamartoma that occurs by the deposition of mature adipose tissue within the collagen of the dermis. It is characterized by soft, non-tender, sessile or pedunculated, normal skin color papule, nodule, or plaque [1]. Clinically, two types have been described: 1) Solitary and 2) Multiple. The solitary types are commonly developed in adults and are usually found on the buttocks and thighs. The multiple types are generally present from birth to the second decade of life and are usually distributed over the pelvic or gluteal region. NLCS has no gender predilection or family inheritance [2].

In Ayurveda, NLCS can be considered Tanumoola Arbuda. Maharshi Sushruta described the characteristic features of Arbuda as Vritam (circular), Sthiram (stable), Mandaruja (mild painful), Mahantam (larger), Analpamoolam (broad-based), Chiravriddhi (slowly growing), and Apakam (nonsuppurative), similar to NLCS [3]. Dalhana classified Arbuda as Tanumoola Arbuda (pedunculated growth) and Bishala Moola Arbuda (sessile growth).

Simple surgical excision is sufficient for the treatment of NLCS. Some authors have reported that laser treatment is better than simple surgical excision. However, insufficient treatment, including laser or any surgical excision, can cause recurrence and suggested that stagged excision can be another treatment modality. Stagged excision can increase the risk of bleeding, infection, delayed healing, and scar formation [4].

Ksharasutra ligation is described for the treatment of Tanumoola Arbuda in Sushruta Samhita. Ksharasutra is prepared with a surgical linen thread size 20 having tensile strength 5.0 kg, coated with Snuhi (Euphorbia nerifolia Linn.) latex, Haridra (Curcuma longa Linn) powder, and Apamarga (Achyranthes aspera Linn.) Kshara. Ksharasutra ligature performs excision under its mechanical pressure and phytochemical cauterization [5].

We report a case of a 47-year-old male with a solitary Nevus Lipomatosus Cutaneous Superficialis at the posterior aspect of the left upper thigh region treated with Ksharasutra ligation method.

Patient information and clinical findings

A 47-year-old male presented with a history of a solitary lesion over the skin of the posterior aspect of the left upper thigh first noted 8 years ago. The patient had no history of pain, ulceration, or foul-smelling discharge but complained of mild discomfort due to friction of the undergarment and cosmetic appearance. The onset of the lesion was insidious and gradually increased in size over 3 years to the present size and has remained static since then. No similar family history was reported. General physical and systemic examination was unremarkable. Dermatological investigation revealed a solitary pedunculated growth sized approximately 5cm × 5cm situated over the skin of the posterior aspect of the left upper thigh (Fig. 1a). Upon palpation, the lesion was soft, non-tender, and firm in consistency. The overlying skin color of the lesion was normal.Fig. 1aNLCS before treatment.Fig. 1a

Diagnosis

On the basis of patient information and clinical findings, it was diagnosed as solitary pedunculated lipoma. Histopathology reveals the presence of stratified squamous epithelium. The reticular dermis was scattered with adipose tissue and fibro-collagenous tissue. No atypical cells or evidence of malignancy was noted.

These findings supported the clinical diagnosis of nevus lipomatosus cutaneous superficialis at the posterior aspect of the left upper thigh.

Investigations

Hematological investigations showed complete blood counts, ESR, PT(INR), and fasting glucose were within normal limits. Viral serological investigations resulted in negative for hepatitis B and hepatitis C virus and nonreactive for HIV I & II.

Therapeutic intervention

Maharshi Sushruta advised to transfix and ligate Ksharasutra at the base of Tanumoola Arbuda (pedunculated tumor) with the help of Yavabaktra Suchi (curved cutting needle) and to follow the wound care after shedding off the necrosed tumor mass from the skin [6].

Para-surgical intervention

Under aseptic conditions, 2% Lignocaine was injected intradermally at the base of the nevus lipomatosus cutaneous superficialis. The Ksharasutra was transfixed and ligated at the base of the NLCS, and a small portion was excised and sent for histopathological examination (Fig. 1b). The regular dressing was done with Triphala Phanta and Jathyadi Ghrita ointment.Fig. 1bTransfixed and ligated NLCS.Fig. 1b

Follow-up and outcomes

After 5 days, the stump of the necrosed mass was detached, and a clean superficial wound was formed (Fig. 1c). The wound was healthy without any discharge and pain reported by the patient. Gradually, the wound showed signs of healing; at the end of 20 days, it had healed completely. No recurrence has been observed until today.Fig. 1cSuperficial clean wound after detaching the stump of NLCS.Fig. 1c

Discussion

NLCS was first described by Haffman & Zirhell in 1921 [7]. It is classified into classical (multiple) and solitary types. Although degeneration of connective tissues of the dermis consequences the deposition of adipose tissues, the exact pathology is unknown. The histopathology of NLCS reveals the proliferation of ectopic mature adipocytes in the reticular dermis. Clinically, NLCS should be differentiated from connective tissue nevus, nevus sebaceous, lymphangioma, hemangioma, and Goltz syndrome. Hence, a histopathological study should be performed to confirm the diagnosis. NLCS conversion to malignancy is uncommon and rarely associated with other systemic illnesses.

In Ayurveda, the pathology of Arbuda is described as the aggravation of Tridosha (Vata, Pitta, and Kapha) vitiate Mamsa Dhatu consequent formation of Arbuda. Sushruta has described Chhedana Karma (excision) for Arbuda. Kshara has Chhedana property. Our case was a 47-year-old man with a solitary pedunculated nevus lipomatosis cutaneous superficialis considered as Tanumoola Arbuda at the posterior aspect of the left upper thigh region treated with Chhedana Karma through Ksharasutra and Vrana Roopana Chikitsa. The drugs selected for Vrana Roopana have analgesic, antibacterial, and wound-healing properties.

Surgical excision is the treatment for NLCS. Excision of NLCS lump by scalpel is easy, with minimal tissue damage, and wound healing is quick but more bloody and painful. Improper excision may cause recurrence. Excision by electrocautery is less bloody but causes more tissue damage and delayed healing [8]. Also, the use of electrocautery is contraindicated in patients with implantable cardiac defibrillators (ICD). However, traditional methods cannot replace all surgical procedures using scalpel or electrocautery.

We preferred Ksharasutra instead of plain thread because Ksharasutra excised the tissue by mechanical pressure and phytochemical cauterization. Whereas plain thread exhibits only mechanical pressure. Microbial growth over Ksharasutra is slower than plain thread. Hence, the chance of infection is less in Ksharasutra than in plain thread.

The presence of proteases in Snuhi Ksheera exhibits hemostatic activities and has antibacterial and anti-inflammatory effects [9]. Kshara has Chhedan (excision), Bhedan (incision), Lekhan (scraping), and Tridoshaghna (alleviating all Dosha) properties [10]. The cutting and healing properties of Kshara are helpful in cutting the base of the Tanumoola Arbuda and healing the wound, respectively. Apamarga Kshara is Kashaya (astringent) in nature that stops bleeding due to its Raktastambhak Karma. Haridra possesses antibacterial, antifungal, anti-inflammatory, and wound-healing properties. The presence of vitamin A and protein in Haridra results in the early synthesis of collagen, mimicking fibroblastic activity [11]. These activities minimized the microbial growth and bleeding from the base of ligated NLCS.

Surgical site infection (SSI) is defined as ‘superficial incisional’ when it involves only skin and subcutaneous tissues; ‘deep incisional’ when it involves fascia and muscular layer; and ‘organ or space’, when it involves any part of the body exposed or manipulated during the procedure [12]. The Ayurvedic drugs prescribed in this study have anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial activities for reducing SSI and accelerating the healing process.

Guggulutikthaka Kashaya (GK) has terpenoids that inhibit the inflammatory signaling process [13]. Gandhak Rasayan (GR) ameliorates diseases caused by Vata Dosha, improves metabolism, and reduces pain [14]. Gandhak (Sulfur), the main ingredient of GR, has antibacterial and antifungal properties. Kaishore Guggulu (KG) is effective in skin diseases, inflammation, carbuncle, and wound. KG is an antibacterial and blood purifier [15]. The main ingredients of KG are Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia), Triphala, and Guggulu (Commiphora wightii), and all have antibacterial properties. Guggulsterone isolated from Guggulu has anti-inflammatory and antibacterial activity for gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria [16]. Hence, GK, GR, and KG reduced pain and inhibited bacterial growth in the ligated NLCS stump.

Triphala has antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and wound-healing properties [17]. The extract of Triphala arrests the bleeding, inhibits microbial growth, and accelerates wound healing. Jathyadi Ghrita (JG) accelerates wound healing by re-epithelialization, reducing inflammation, collagen fiber deposition, and transforming growth factor beta 1 expression [18]. Local application of an extract of Triphala and JG reduced secondary wound infection and quick healing of wound created after removal stump.

Conclusion

Complete removal of solitary pedunculated NLCS is the treatment of preference. Since no invasion of deeper structures, excision through Ksharasutra was preferred. After removing the mass, the wound was treated with Ayurvedic drugs. No adverse events pertaining to the Ksharasutra procedure and Ayurvedic medicines were reported.

ACTION:  Request for information; response to public comments.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY:  On Tuesday, March 24, 2026, the Department of Defense 
(referred to herein as ``the Department'', ``Department of War'' or 
DoW'') published a request for information in the Federal Register (FR) 
that provided an opportunity for the public to submit issues that have 
an impact on service members and their families, where state 
governments are the primary agents for making positive change. Each 
year, DoW

[[Page 65190]]

selects state policy priorities for states to consider that represent 
barriers resulting from the transience and uncertainty of military 
life. For example, DoW has asked states to consider remedies that trade 
residency-based and time-in-state requirements for portability and 
continuity, including interstate compacts, license reciprocity, credit 
for military training, spouse unemployment compensation; cross-
reporting agreements with child welfare agencies; educational 
opportunities including advance enrollment, homeschool solutions, in-
state tuition, records transfer, and waiver waitlist retention for 
families with special needs; support for severely injured personnel; 
custody and juvenile justice protections for deployed parents and 
military-connected youth; information sharing on military protective 
orders; employment and legal protections for military families; and 
predatory lending safeguards. DoW will consider public submissions in 
setting those priorities.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: On Tuesday, March 24, 2026 (91 FR 14002), 
DoW published a notice titled ``Request for Information for 2028 
Department of War (DoW) State Policy Priorities Impacting Service 
Members and Their Families.'' The Department accepted public comments 
for 30 days until April 23, 2026, and received one hundred twenty 
submissions. The DoW responds to the comments as follows:
    Thank you for your submission to the DoD-2026-OS-0232 docket. 
State-level policies directly impact military families' stability and, 
consequently, combat readiness. To sustain a focus on the mission, the 
Department must develop aligned relationships with states that address 
both existing and emerging needs of our military community.
    The Department received fourteen comments proposing issues that met

DoW state policy priorities; we respond as follows:
     The DoW appreciates the feedback on standardized veterans' 
preference policies for local government employment. The DoW is

    Each FY, the DoW considers numerous vital state policy issues, 
aiming to select those that hold the most promise to positively impact 
the lives of service members and their families. Within this 
competitive framework, we evaluate all proposed best practices.
    We rigorously examine these and other issues. Our process is 
designed to ensure that we advance the most impactful and viable 
initiatives to support our military families effectively.
    When military families are stable and supported through effective 
state policies, warfighters can dedicate themselves fully to the 
lethality and readiness our nation demands.
    For the FY beginning on October 1, 2026, the USW(P&R) recently 
announced the Department's 2027 State Policy Priorities:
     License Utilization Reporting and Issuance Uniformity 
(Approved 2027): Federal law requires states to recognize out-of-state 
licenses for military families under orders, but employers and insurers 
require state-issued licenses. States can bridge this gap by issuing 
courtesy licenses to qualified military families, allowing them to 
maintain an active license eligible for recognition and renewal, while 
tracking data that reveals how effectively states are utilizing 
military-trained professionals in critical occupations.
     Solutions for Military Homeschoolers (Approved 2026): 
Military families homeschool their children at nearly twice the rate of 
civilian families and face unique challenges associated with military 
life. State policies that streamline homeschool requirements and expand 
access to educational resources such as extracurricular activities and 
sports, JROTC, school facilities, and special education services 
provide military families with increased stability and improve 
retention.
     Education Policy Implementation and Information 
Enhancements (Approved 2025): States have an opportunity to facilitate 
improved local implementation of policies designed to support military-
connected children and ensure families are empowered with the knowledge 
they need by clarifying requirements, enhancing communication efforts, 
and streamlining access to information on state and local education 
websites.
     Military Exigency Clause in Family Leave Laws (Approved 
2025): States can ensure family leave laws include provisions for 
military-specific needs like activations, training, and transitions. 
This enables readiness by reducing family stress during critical 
mission periods.
     Military Spouse Employment Preference (Approved 2024): 
Military spouses in the civilian work force have a 20 percent 
unemployment rate, which adversely impacts military family economic 
security. States can assist in bolstering military families by enacting 
state laws that permit state and local governments and private industry 
to offer hiring and procurement preferences for military spouses, 
recognizing the need to combat military spouse unemployment rates and 
supporting the financial well-being of military families.
     State Support of Military Families With Special 
Educational Needs (Approved 2024): Highly mobile children, including 
military children, are more likely to experience recurring educational 
disruptions and challenges accessing special education services, 
particularly those who need access. States can assist military families 
by ensuring timely establishment of services upon relocation and 
reducing procedural burdens.
     State Exemption for DoW Family Child Care Homes (Approved 
2023): In many cases, in-home child-care providers who live off a 
military installation must be licensed by both the state and the DoW, 
even when only caring for eligible DoW-affiliated children. By 
exempting in-home childcare providers certified by the DoW from state 
licensure requirements, states can improve access to family child care 
for military families.
     State Response to Military Interpersonal Violence 
(Approved 2023): Interpersonal violence is a pattern of harmful 
behavior in which one person uses various forms of abuse to assert 
power and control over another. States can further protect victims of 
interpersonal violence by enhancing statutes that increase 
accessibility to civilian protection orders for victims and mandating 
reciprocal information sharing between military and civilian law 
enforcement authorities.
     Concurrent Juvenile Jurisdiction (Approved 2022): Military 
installations subject to exclusive Federal jurisdiction often process 
juvenile offenses through the Federal system, which has no established 
juvenile justice system. Adopting policies that facilitate concurrent 
jurisdiction between the state and military installation opens the door 
to the state juvenile justice system and resources, offering improved 
opportunities for rehabilitation tailored to address juveniles.
     Open Enrollment Flexibility (Approved 2022): Military 
families can be disadvantaged in school enrollment options for their 
children due to military-directed moves. States can assist by 
increasing military-connected students' access to schooling options, 
allowing them to remain in their current school placement despite a 
relocation from temporary to permanent housing, and including them 
within existing enrollment prioritization systems.
     Occupational Licensure Interstate Compacts (Approved 
2017): Thirty-five percent of military spouses require an

[[Page 65197]]

occupational license to work in their chosen profession. Licensure 
compacts create seamless licensure portability for all members of a 
profession, including military spouses and Service members. By adopting 
licensure compacts, states can improve military family financial 
readiness.

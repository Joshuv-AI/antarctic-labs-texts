1.
Introduction

1.1.

Let
B
be a complex affine algebraic variety or a Stein space (with arbitrary singularities), of
dim_{\mathbb{C}}B=n.
The classical theorem reads:

(1)

B
admits a deformation-retraction to a closed subanalytic subspace of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n.

Moreover, if
B
is affine algebraic, then the resulting subspace can be chosen compact.

Apparently the first result of this type was in
[
Andreotti-Frankel.59
]
: a Stein manifold

B
has the homotopy type of a CW-complex of real dimension
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n.

The statement was strengthened, in Theorem 7.2 of
[
Milnor
]
, to the deformation-retractions of Stein manifolds.
The statement on the homotopy type was extended to simply connected affine algebraic sets (with arbitrary singularities) in
[
Kato
, pg.49]
, then to arbitrary affine algebraic sets in Theorem 2.12 of
[
Karchyauskas
]
, and finally to Stein spaces
(with arbitrary singularities) in
[
Hamm.83
]
. The version (
1
) of deformation retraction is Theorem 1.1 in
[
Hamm-Mihalache.97
]
.

This theorem is an everyday tool for Geometry and Topology of complex varieties and Stein spaces. In Singularity Theory it implies: the Milnor fibre of any singular germ has homotopy type of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n.
This fundamental property is the starting point in the study of Milnor fibres, a flourishing field in the last 70 years, see e.g. Chapters 6,8,9 in
[
Handbook.I
]
and Chapters 6,7 in
[
Handbook.II
]
.

1.2.

Take a dominant morphism of complex algebraic varieties
X\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ B,
think of it as a fibration over the base
B.
This brings two questions of A.F.K.H.-type:

•

(vertical version) Suppose all the fibres of
X\to B
are affine algebraic varieties of
dim_{\mathbb{C}}\leq n.
Does the family admit a fibrewise deformation-retraction to a subfamily
Z\to B,
with all fibres of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n?

•

(horizontal version) Suppose
B
is affine,
dim_{\mathbb{C}}B\leq n.
Does there exist a deformation retraction
B\rightsquigarrow B^{\prime},
with
dim_{\mathbb{R}}B^{\prime}\leq n,
that lifts to a deformation-retraction of the whole family, sending fibres to fibres?

(Of course, in both cases the deformation-retraction is non-analytic.)

The vertical version is trivial. Take any stratification making
f
a
C^{0}
-stratified-trivial morphism, and invoke A.F.K.H. successively over the strata. For a significantly stronger statement see
[
Hamm.01
]
.

The horizontal version is less trivial. Not many deformation-retractions of
B
can be lifted to deformations of
X,
because
f
is not a
C^{0}
-locally trivial fibration.

We construct a special deformation-retraction of
B,
establishing the horizontal version.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a deformation-retraction
\{\Phi^{B}_{t}\}
that
lifts to
X,
making the commutative diagram. The lifted version satisfies:
\Phi_{0}=Id_{X},\hskip 22.76228ptdim_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{B}_{1}(B)\leq n,\hskip 22.76228pt\Phi_{t}|_{\Phi_{1}(X)}=Id.

\begin{matrix}X\times[0,1]\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\{\Phi_{t}\}}}$}\ X\\
(f,Id)\downarrow\quad\quad\quad\downarrow f\\
B\times[0,1]\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\{\Phi^{B}_{t}\}}}$}\ B\end{matrix}

This homotopy is (naturally) called: a deformation-retraction of the morphism
f.

While the statement of Theorem
1.1
is quite natural, it appears to be not known.

Our proof is an iterative argument on bifurcation loci, and uses the A.F.K.H. theorem (for algebraic varieties) as a “black-box”.
E.g. in the trivial case,
X=B
and
f=Id_{B},
the proof just refers to the original statement(s).
We do not address the case of Stein morphisms, as their bifurcation loci can be quite pathological.

This relative version of the classical A.F.K.H. theorem is of immediate use e.g. in Algebraic Geometry, in the study of families and fibrations.
In Singularity Theory it is needed e.g. to study the Milnor fibre via its projections (or via other maps).

Our particular motivation was the study of fast vanishing cycles on links of singular germs,
[
Kerner-Mendes.a
]
,

[
Kerner-Mendes.b
]
.

2.
Several general (topological) facts

All the spaces below are finite CW complexes. By
dim_{\mathbb{R}}X
we mean the maximum of the dimensions of cells. A deformation-retraction to a subset,
X\rightsquigarrow X^{\prime},
is a homotopy of
X
to
X^{\prime}
that restricts to identity on
X^{\prime}.

A space homotopically equivalent to a space of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n
is called “of homotopy type
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n
”.

i.

Take a pair
X\supset Y
of contractible spaces with the homotopy extension property. Then
X
admits a deformation-retraction to
Y.

See e.g. Corollary 0.20 of
[
Hatcher
]
.

All our pairs will be CW pairs, thus having the homotopy extension property, by Proposition 0.16 of
[
Hatcher
]
.

ii.

Suppose
X
is of homotopy type of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n.
Then
X\cup Cells_{n+1}
is contractible, where
Cells_{n+1}
denotes a union of cells of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n+1,
and they are glued to
X
along their boundaries. This follows, e.g. by the Whitehead theorem,
[
Hatcher
, pg.346]
.

iii.

Suppose a space
Y\cup Z
is contractible, and its subspaces
Z,W
are contractible. Suppose
dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Y\cap Z\cap W)<dim_{\mathbb{R}}Z-1.
Then there exists a deformation retraction
Y\cup Z\rightsquigarrow Z\cup W\cup\Delta,
where
\Delta\subset\overline{Y\setminus(Z\cup W)}
and
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta<dim_{\mathbb{R}}Z.

Proof.
If
Z\cup W
is already contractible, then we invoke §
2
.i. for the pair

Z\cup W\subset Z\cup Y.
In the general case
Z\cup W
has a non-trivial homology and fundamental group.
We eliminate these cycles by gluing cells. Once all the cycles are cancelled, we define
\Delta
as the union of these cells, and invoke §
2
.ii. Here are the details.

Recall the Mayer-Vietoris sequence,
H_{i}(Z)\oplus H_{i}(W)\to H_{i}(Z\cup W)\to H_{i-1}(Z\cap W).
In our case it implies: the non-trivial homology of
Z\cup W
comes only from that of
Z\cap W.
If a cycle of
Z\cap W
has a representative inside
Z\setminus Y,
then it is eliminated by gluing a cell inside
Z.
(Because
Z
is contractible.) Such a cell will not contribute to
\Delta.
Therefore it is enough to consider those cycles of
Z\cap W
that lie inside
Z\cap Y.
But
dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Z\cap Y\cap W)<dim_{\mathbb{R}}Z-1,
thus the cells to be added are all of dimensions
dim_{\mathbb{R}}<dim_{\mathbb{R}}Z.

Therefore
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta<dim_{\mathbb{R}}Z.

\blacksquare

iv.

(Thom’s first isotopy lemma, e.g. Theorem B.2 in
[
Mond. Nuñ.-Bal.
]
) Take a stratified topological space,
X=\amalg X_{\bullet},
with
C^{1}
-smooth strata. Suppose a
C^{1}
-map
X\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ B
is proper, and its restriction to each stratum,
f|_{X_{j}},
is a submersion (onto the image of the stratum). Then each restriction
f|_{X_{j}}
is a
C^{0}
-locally-trivial fibration.

In our case
X\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ B
is a morphism of algebraic varieties, and we make it proper. It admits the trivializing stratification (as above) whose strata
X_{\bullet}
are also algebraic varieties.

v.

Let
X\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ B
be a
C^{0}
-locally-trivial fibration. Then any deformation retraction of
B
lifts to a deformation-retraction of
f,
as in the diagram of Theorem
1.1
.

3.
The proof of the theorem

i.

[A warmup/the simplest case:
B
is contractible and
dim_{\mathbb{C}}B=1.
]

Rename
B
to
B_{1}.
Take the bifurcation locus,
B_{0}\subset B_{1},
so that
f
is a
C^{0}
-locally trivial fibration over
B_{1}\setminus B_{0}.

Here
B_{0}
is a finite set of points.
Denote by
Cells_{1}
a simply connected path in
B_{1},
connecting all the points of
B_{0}.
Thus
B_{0}\cup Cells_{1}
is contractible inside
B_{1}.

Thus
B_{1}
admits a deformation-retraction onto
B_{0}\cup Cells_{1},
by §
2
.i.

This deformation-retraction lifts to a deformation-retraction of the morphism
f:X\to B,
by the local triviality of
f
outside of
B_{0}\cup Cells_{1},
see §
2
.v. Hence the statement.

To extend this argument to higher dimensions we should cope with the locus of local non-triviality of the fibration
X\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ B.

As the map
f
is non-proper, this locus contains (besides the discriminant of
f
) the set of bifurcations at infinity. This latter can be pathological. Therefore below we compactify the map
f,
and then work with the discriminant of the proper map.

A deformation retraction of
B
onto the discriminant (which is identity on the discriminant) lifts to a deformation retraction of
f.
Then we iterate the argument.

ii.

[Compactification of the map
X\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ B
to a proper map
{\bar{X}}\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{{\bar{f}}}}$}\ B
]

Embed the source into a projective space,
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}.
Then re-embed it as the graph
X\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\hookrightarrow}\limits^{j}}$}\ \mathbb{P}^{N}\times B,
by
x\to(x,f(x)).
Thus
j(X)
is an algebraic subvariety, and we take the closure
{\bar{X}}:=\overline{j(X)}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}\times B.

The initial map
f
acts now by the projection
j(X)\to B.
Thus it extends to the projection
{\bar{X}}\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{{\bar{f}}}}$}\ B.
This is a proper algebraic morphism.

iii.

[The trivializing stratification of
{\bar{f}}
] Stratify the map,
{\bar{X}}=\amalg({\bar{X}})_{\bullet}\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{{\bar{f}}}}$}\ B.
Here each stratum
({\bar{X}})_{\bullet}
is a smooth algebraic variety, and each restriction
({\bar{X}})_{\bullet}\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f|}}$}\ B
is a
C^{0}
-locally trivial fibration over its image. Moreover, we can take this stratification compatibly with the “hyperplane at infinity”, i.e. with the splitting
{\bar{X}}=j(X)\amalg({\bar{X}}\setminus j(X)).
Namely, both
j(X)
and
{\bar{X}}\setminus j(X)
are unions of the strata.

Below we will deformation-retract
B
to its subsets. This deformation-retraction will lift to the deformation-retraction of the strata
\amalg({\bar{X}})_{\bullet}.
In particular, this deformation-retraction will be compatible with the splitting
{\bar{X}}=j(X)\amalg({\bar{X}}\setminus j(X)).

Therefore below we replace
X
by
{\bar{X}}.
Thus we assume that the map
X\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ B
is proper.

iv.

Take a proper morphism
X\to B,
where
B
is a complex affine variety of
dim_{\mathbb{C}}B=n
. We construct iterative deformation-retractions of
f.

Rename
B
to
B_{n}.
Take the discriminant of
f,
it is a Zariski-closed subset
B_{n-1}\subset B_{n},
of
dim_{\mathbb{C}}B_{n-1}\leq n-1.

(This
B_{n-1}
is an affine subvariety.)
The restricted map
X\setminus f^{-1}(B_{n-1})\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f_{|}}}$}\ B_{n}\setminus B_{n-1}
is a topologically locally trivial fibration, by §
2
.iv.

By the Andreotti-Frankel-Hamm theorem
B_{n}
is of homotopy type of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n.
Therefore, by §
2
.ii, the space
B_{n}\cup Cells_{n+1}
is contractible, where
Cells_{n+1}
is a union of open cells of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n+1.
The gluing of
Cells_{n+1}
to
B_{n}
goes along the boundaries of these cells. The cells are not algebraic. We can assume that the space
Cells_{n+1}
is connected and therefore contractible.

Similarly
B_{n-1}\cup Cells_{n}
is contractible, where
Cells_{n}\subset B_{n}\cup Cells_{n+1}
is a union of cells of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n.

As
B_{n-1}\subset B_{n},
we have
B_{n-1}\cap Cells_{n+1}\subseteq\partial Cells_{n+1}.
Thus
dim_{\mathbb{R}}(B_{n-1}\cap Cells_{n+1})\leq n.

Moreover, we can assume
dim_{\mathbb{R}}(B_{n-1}\cap Cells_{n+1})<n,
as
B_{n-1}
has no cycles of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}=n.

Now we apply §
2
.iii to the contractible spaces

(2)

Y:=B_{n}\cup Cells_{n+1},\hskip 28.45274ptZ:=Cells_{n+1},\hskip 28.45274ptW:=B_{n-1}\cup Cells_{n}.

Thus
B_{n}\cup Cells_{n+1}
deformation-retracts onto
B_{n-1}\cup Cells_{n}\cup Cells_{n+1}\cup\Delta_{n},
where
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta_{n}<n+1.

This deformation-retraction is identity on
B_{n-1}\cup Cells_{n},
therefore it lifts to a deformation-retraction of the morphism
f.

Finally we check the dimensions:

•

If
dim_{\mathbb{R}}B_{n-1}\leq n,
then
dim_{\mathbb{R}}(B_{n-1}\cup\Delta_{n}\cup Cells_{n})\leq n.
Hence the theorem is proved.

•

Suppose
dim_{\mathbb{R}}B_{n-1}>n,
then
dim_{\mathbb{R}}(B_{n-1}\cup\Delta_{n}\cup Cells_{n})=dim_{\mathbb{R}}B_{n-1}.
Note that
B_{n-1}\cup Cells_{n}\cup Cells_{n+1}\cup\Delta_{n}
is still contractible. And now iterate the proof.

As the sets
\Delta_{n},

Cells_{n}
are of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\leq n,
we ignore them.
We consider the map
f^{-1}(B_{n-1})\to B_{n-1}.

It is proper and algebraic. Take its discriminant,
B_{n-2}\subset B_{n-1}.
Thus
f
is a
C^{0}
-locally trivial fibration over
B_{n-1}\setminus B_{n-2}.
And so on.

\blacksquare

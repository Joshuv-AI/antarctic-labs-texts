Nature is stingy: Universality of Scrooge ensembles in quantum many-body systems
Wai-Keong Mok, Tobias Haug, Wen Wei Ho, John Preskill

Abstract
Recent advances in quantum simulators allow direct experimental access to ensembles of pure states generated by measuring part of an isolated quantum many-body system. These projected ensembles encode fine-grained information beyond thermal expectation values and provide a new window into quantum thermalization. In chaotic dynamics, projected ensembles exhibit universal statistics governed by maximum-entropy principles, known as deep thermalization. At infinite temperature this universality is characterized by Haar-random ensembles. More generally, physical constraints such as finite temperature or conservation laws lead to Scrooge ensembles, which are maximally entropic distributions of pure states consistent with these constraints. Here we introduce Scrooge k k -designs, which approximate Scrooge ensembles, and use this framework to sharpen the conditions under which Scrooge-like behavior emerges. We first show that global Scrooge designs arise from long-time chaotic unitary dynamics alone, without measurements. Second, we show that measuring a complementary subsystem of a scrambled global state drawn from a global Scrooge 2 ​ k 2k -design induces a local Scrooge k k -design. Third, we show that a local Scrooge k k -design arises from an arbitrary entangled state when the complementary system is measured in a scrambled basis induced by a unitary drawn from a Haar 2 ​ k 2k -design. These results show that the resources required to generate approximate Scrooge ensembles scale only with the desired degree of approximation, enabling efficient implementations. Complementing our analytical results, numerical simulations identify coherence, entanglement, non-stabilizerness, and information scrambling as essential ingredients for the emergence of Scrooge-like behavior. Together, our findings advance theoretical explanations for maximally entropic, information-stingy randomness in quantum many-body systems.

Our three theorems delineate, in a unified and systematic way, several physically distinct many-body settings in which hierarchies of Scrooge behavior arise.
In particular, Theorems

This physically natural conclusion was anticipated by Mark et al.

Using the techniques developed in this work, we rigorously confirm this expectation, though strictly speaking not directly from the theorems.

Finally, complementing our analytical theorems, we further clarify the physical resources needed for Scrooge behavior to emerge in projected ensembles. We argue that coherence, entanglement, non-stabilizerness (magic), and information scrambling are all necessary ingredients: removing any one of them can obstruct Scrooge-like universality. Conversely, when these resources are present, local Scrooge behavior can arise even in systems that do not thermalize dynamically.
Extensive numerical simulations in several quantum many-body settings, including commuting circuits, doped Clifford circuits, and Hamiltonian ground states are performed to illustrate these points. In particular, we demonstrate that ground states of one-dimensional integrable Hamiltonians — whose area laws and symmetries preclude thermalization — can nevertheless generate emergent Scrooge designs when measured in a random stabilizer basis. These results indicate that Scrooge universality extends beyond conventional dynamical settings such as deep thermalization or Hilbert-space ergodicity.

Together, our results provide a comprehensive and rigorous theoretical framework to analyze the emergence of maximally entropic, information-stingy randomness in quantum many-body systems, formulated in terms of Scrooge

II
Preliminaries of quantum state ensembles: projected and temporal ensembles, Scrooge ensembles and Scrooge designs

We begin with a general but brief introduction to ensembles of pure quantum states and explain how to characterize them statistically and through their information-theoretic properties. We then review two physical settings where such ensembles naturally appear. The first setting is deep thermalization, which concerns projected ensembles of local post-measurement states. The second setting is Hilbert-space ergodicity, which concerns temporal ensembles of global states generated by quantum chaotic dynamics.
We then elaborate upon the Scrooge ensemble, the ensemble of interest in our paper. The informed reader may skip to our main results in Sec.
III
.

II.1
Quantum state ensembles and their information content

In this work, we are interested in ensembles of pure quantum states

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\{p_{i},|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i}.

II.2
Projected ensembles and temporal ensembles

Thus far, we have introduced quantum state ensembles as purely formal mathematical objects. Recently, two physical settings where quantum state ensembles arise have received increasing attention in the study of quantum many-body systems. One is the projected ensemble composed of local post-measurement states, and the other is the temporal ensemble of global states generated by time-evolution. Both ensembles have been found to exhibit universal features in limiting cases, embodied by the physical phenomena of “deep thermalization” and “Hilbert-space ergodicity,” respectively. Here, we quickly review these topics.

\sum_{i=1}^{k}E_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}E_{i}^{\prime}
if and only if the subsets are equivalent, up to a reordering of energy levels.
This assumption describes the scenario where energy levels are generic,
a physical property which is
intuitively expected to hold true for quantum chaotic systems, whose energy spectra are known to be well governed by random matrix theory
67
. In particular, for
k=1
, this condition is equivalent to level repulsion between the energy levels.

II.3
Scrooge ensemble and Scrooge designs

We have seen how quantum state ensembles arise in many physical settings, and in certain appropriate limits (large system sizes or late times), appear to take various simple universal limiting forms, like the Haar ensemble, the Scrooge ensemble, and the random phase ensemble.
As mentioned in the introduction, our key interest in this paper will be firming up general but precise conditions when the information-stingy Scrooge ensemble can be
rigorously
proven to appear, including but going even beyond the setting of deep thermalization, and in the context of Scrooge designs.
To that end, we now formally define the Scrooge ensemble and Scrooge designs.

Definition 1

(Scrooge ensemble
54
)
.

The Scrooge ensemble with density matrix
\sigma
supported on a
D
-dimensional Hilbert space, denoted Scrooge(
\sigma
), is the unique ensemble
\mathcal{E}
of pure states satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\argmin_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\text{acc}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}),

(10)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\underset{\psi\sim\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}{\mathbb{E}}{|{\psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi}=\sigma.

An explicit construction of
Scrooge(
\sigma
) is given by

\text{Scrooge}(\sigma)=\left\{D\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}\text{d}\phi,\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}}{\left\lVert\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\right\rVert}\right\},

(11)

with
\text{d}\phi
the Haar measure on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{D}

54
. From the expression, one sees that the Scrooge ensemble can be thought of as a deformed version of the Haar ensemble, to account for the constraint that its mean is
\sigma
.

We will also be interested in ensembles that
approximate
the Scrooge ensemble.
Analogously to state

-designs routinely used in quantum information theory

42

th moment, we likewise introduce the notion of

-designs for the Scrooge ensemble:

Definition 2

(Approximate Scrooge

-designs)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an ensemble of pure states, with

\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=\underset{\psi\sim\mathcal{E}}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi}\right)^{\otimes k}.

(12)

We say

is a Scrooge
(\sigma)

k
-design with additive error

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\right\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon\,,

(13)

and a Scrooge
(\sigma)

k
-design with relative error
\varepsilon

2
2

2

The relative error is a strictly stronger notion of approximation: A relative error

\varepsilon
, but the converse is not true.

(1-\varepsilon)\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\preceq\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\,.

(14)

The
k
th moment of Scrooge
(\sigma)
is given by

\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)=D\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{\left(\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma}\right)^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}}\right].

We note that evaluating the

;

;

In our work, the unnormalized Scrooge also plays a useful albeit intermediary role, as captured in the following technical lemma
we derive and whose proof we present in Appendix

Lemma 1

(Scrooge approximation)
.

Consider the ensemble of unnormalized states
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}=\{\sqrt{D\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\}

{|{\phi}\rangle}\sim\text{Haar}(D)

For

Lemma

(see Ref.

3
3

3

In quantum many-body systems,

III
Emergent Scrooge designs in quantum many-body systems

We are now ready to state our main results, which provide sufficient conditions for the rigorous emergence of Scrooge designs in quantum many-body systems.

III.1
Scrooge designs emerge naturally in dynamics

Our first result pertains to the temporal ensemble for energy-conserving Hamiltonian dynamics, which as explained in Sec.

4

4

Conceptually, Theorem

This means that similar maximum entropy principles apply to both deep thermalization and Hilbert-space ergodicity, conceptually unifying these two phenomena.

III.2
Scrooge designs emerge in the projected ensemble of a sufficiently complex initial state

Our next theorem returns to the projected ensemble. Motivated by the insight from the previous result, Theorem

As mentioned in the introduction, Goldstein et al.

Theorem 2

(Emergent Scrooge

-design from Scrooge

III.2.2
(Almost) example: Quantum chaotic Hamiltonian dynamics

Returning to the motivation of Theorem

IV
Physical ingredients behind emergent Scrooge behavior

Figure 2:
Essential physical ingredients for emergent Scrooge designs.
The emergence of Scrooge designs in projected ensembles requires the generator state
{|{\Phi}\rangle}_{AB}
to exhibit magic (or nonstabilizerness), quantum information scrambling (via nonlocal entanglement), and coherence. When any of these ingredients is absent or insufficient, obstructions to Scrooge behavior can arise. Representative examples include stabilizer states, which lack magic; diagonal-scrambled stabilizer states (where a unitary diagonal in the computational basis is applied to subsystem
B
of a stabilizer state), which only weakly scrambles quantum information between subsystems
A
and
B

Our theorems have identified several general conditions under which quantum state ensembles with Scrooge-like behavior, as captured within the framework of Scrooge designs, can be provably shown to emerge: namely, dynamically in the temporal ensemble [Theorem
1
]; and via measurements in the projected ensemble, if the generator state is itself drawn from a Scrooge design [Theorem
2
] or if the measurement basis is rotated by a Haar design [Theorem
3
].
Importantly, these theorems demonstrate that the Scrooge ensemble appears in a myriad of quantum many-body settings. On the other hand, we do not expect our theorems to exhaustively cover all possible scenarios where Scrooge behavior can arise; moreover, they do not directly inform us as to the necessary
physical ingredients
that underlie its emergence.

Here, we discuss the necessary such ingredients that a generator state
|\Psi\rangle_{AB}
on a bipartite system
AB
has to possess, in order for it to exhibit emergent Haar or Scrooge-like behavior in its projected ensemble.

Interestingly, recent works have begun to shed light on this question by quantitatively studying the effect of the amount of
quantum information resources

Intriguingly, they predicted that a generator state

Explicit examples of such states are furnished by so-called random subset phase states

Concretely, for low-coherence states, it was argued that the resulting distribution of the projected ensemble is just a collection of (classical) computational states on the local subsystem — clearly non-ergodic and having maximal accessible information instead of minimal. Further, this breaking of ergodicity happens in a robust fashion, with a phase transition separating the non-ergodic distribution from a deeply thermalized Haar distribution, upon tuning the coherence density past a critical finite value.
Intuitively, the reason for this is because measurements, which we take to be in the computational basis, select only those global bit-strings

The upshot is that coherence (and importantly not only a non-zero value of it, but a sufficiently high density of it!) is a necessary ingredient for the appearance of Scrooge-like behavior in the projected ensemble.

We next focus on magic: a quantifier of the computational resources needed to describe quantum states beyond stabilizer states

13

More generally, our “

However, simply adding non-Clifford elements to the system is not sufficient. To illustrate this point, consider the stabilizer state

What this example shows us is that the unitary (or dynamics) used to construct the generator state

To summarize, the emergence of Scrooge behavior requires the presence of coherence, magic, and information scrambling (nonlocal entanglement within the system). When any of these ingredients is absent or insufficient, obstructions to the formation of Scrooge projected ensembles can arise, see Fig.
2
. This parallels a well-known fact in quantum complexity theory: quantum advantage requires the coexistence of the resources of coherence, magic, and entanglement.
When any of these ingredients is absent, quantum advantage is lost, i.e., efficient classical simulation becomes possible

;
3
;
107

In the next section, we perform numerical simulations supporting the aforementioned theoretical discussion, and explore the interplay between coherence, magic, and quantum information scrambling in producing Scrooge behavior within the projected ensemble
across a variety of models.
These encompass regimes that lie beyond the settings required of our theorems, and thus complement the analytical results of Sec.
III
.
Our simulations suggest that Scrooge designs can emerge even more broadly than in the settings for which we currently have rigorous guarantees.

V
Numerical investigations

In this section, we numerically study the projected ensembles generated in several quantum many-body settings in which the key physical ingredients of coherence, magic and degree of information scrambling, can be systematically controlled:

(i)
Commuting quantum circuit evolution.
We consider generator states produced from so-called “commuting quantum circuits” (i.e., circuits composed of mutually commuting gates, studied in the quantum computing community)
101
;
14
.
Concretely, starting from a product state
{|{+}\rangle}^{\otimes N}
, we apply a unitary circuit that is diagonal in the measurement (computational) basis.
Although such circuits can generate states with extensive entanglement and magic, by changing the regions where interactions are applied, the circuits’ commuting structure allows for controlled tuning of how quantum information is delocalized across the system, such that the resulting projected ensembles can either succeed or fail to form a good state design. In addition, by rotating the measurement basis, we can inject controlled amounts of coherence into the system. We will show that this results in a coherence-induced deep thermalization transition, as predicted recently by Ref.
63
.

(ii)
Doped Clifford circuit evolution.
Next, we study generator states produced from Clifford circuits doped with single-qubit non-Clifford gates. This construction allows information scrambling and magic to be tuned largely independently: Clifford dynamics efficiently scrambles quantum information, while the non-Clifford gates introduce controlled amounts of magic. We study how tuning these two control knobs affects the quality of the Scrooge designs formed in the projected ensemble, and find that Scrooge behavior emerges only when both the circuit depth and density of non-Clifford gates are sufficiently large.

(iii)
Ground states of 1D many-body Hamiltonians.
Finally, we consider ground states of local 1D integrable Hamiltonians, which are characterized by low entanglement and low complexity. To probe the role of the measurement basis, we apply various unitary rotations
U_{B}
to the qubits on subsystem
B
prior to measurement, which injects various amounts of magic and information scrambling. We find that if the rotations are insufficiently complex, no Scrooge behavior forms; while if the rotations are sufficiently complex Scrooge behavior can emerge.

Together, our numerical investigations allow us to showcase the roles of
coherence, magic and quantum information scrambling in driving emergent local Scrooge behavior. They complement and go beyond existing numerical results which have demonstrated Scrooge behavior in late-time quenches or finite-temperature eigenstates of chaotic Hamiltonians
25
;
72
: while the latter examples are physically relevant for experiments, it is difficult to control and isolate the individual ingredients driving Scrooge behavior in them as we do here.

V.3
Ground states of many-body Hamiltonians

Finally, we study emergent Scrooge designs in naturally realizable many-body systems.
In contrast to the usual paradigm of quantum chaotic systems where deep thermalization has been probed

We focus on the ground states

Altogether, our numerical results show that magic and quantum information scrambling are key physical ingredients for emergent Scrooge designs. These properties can manifest intrinsically in naturally occurring chaotic many-body states, or be injected externally by applying a scrambling unitary on subsystem
B

We give additional numerics supporting our observations in Appendix

VI
Discussion and Outlook

This work addresses a fundamental question: What universal principle governs the higher-order statistical fluctuations of quantum many-body systems? Building on prior insights into the information-stinginess of Nature, we provide a unified and rigorous framework for characterizing the emergence of maximally entropic Scrooge-like behavior. Our results, by clarifying when and why Scrooge universality arises, place earlier observations on a precise quantitative footing.

Our approach also highlights the robustness of Scrooge universality across diverse physical settings. In particular, Theorem
2
shows that local Scrooge behavior emerges whenever the global generator state is drawn from a Scrooge design, regardless of the microscopic mechanism by which that state is prepared. This mechanism-agnostic perspective opens the door to identifying new physical scenarios in which Scrooge behavior may arise.

Our numerical investigations further elucidate the physical resources underlying this universality. We find that quantum coherence, entanglement, non-stabilizerness (magic), and information scrambling play essential roles: removing any one of these ingredients can obstruct the emergence of Scrooge behavior. Beyond these core ingredients, other resources may also play a role, including non-Gaussianity
113
, whose absence leads to restricted forms of deep thermalization
68
;
62
, and imaginarity
116
, which can impose effective realness on projected ensembles and alter their universal structure from unitary to orthogonal symmetry classes
9
. Developing a systematic classification of such resources, and incorporating them into refined maximum-entropy principles, is an important direction for future work.

Several concrete open questions arise from our work. First, while our
``2k\to k"
theorems are optimal for
k=2
, it remains open whether the required design order can be reduced for high moments, for example to
``k+\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)\to k"
.
Second, our findings suggest a potential unifying perspective on two complementary notions of information hiding.
Applying a scrambling unitary to the measurement basis on
B
(as in Theorem
3
) hides
quantum
information about
A
in highly nonlocal observables on
B
. On the other hand, the resulting projected ensemble on
A
hides
classical
information about the measurement outcome on
B
. Whether these two mechanisms fit into a common theoretical framework remains an intriguing question.
Third, the universality of Scrooge ensembles may enable new applications in quantum information science. Emergent state designs in infinite-temperature deep thermalization have already led to advances in learning and benchmarking analog quantum simulators
20
;
108
;
75
;
78
. Extending these ideas to Scrooge designs could yield analogous protocols operating in more general, physically constrained settings.

Finally, our results raise the broader question of whether an information-theoretic analogue of the maximum entropy principle extends to even more general settings, such as incomplete measurements or noisy dynamics, where each state in a projected ensemble becomes mixed rather than pure
77
;
118
. An affirmative answer would point toward a unified framework for the emergence of universal randomness in both pure and mixed quantum ensembles, marking a significant conceptual advance in quantum statistical mechanics.

The Weingarten calculus provides a very useful tool for evaluating polynomial functions of Haar random states and unitaries. Here, we will briefly introduce the results relevant for this work, and establish the notation used throughout the manuscript. A detailed treatment can be found in Refs.

4
Projected ensemble

Here, we give a concise review of the projected ensemble, and establish the notation used in this work. Starting from a bipartite quantum state
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
, which we refer to as the
generator state
, we measure subsystem
B
in a complete orthonormal basis
\{{|{z}\rangle}\}_{z=1}^{D_{B}}
. By default, we will choose the measurement basis to be the computational basis, unless stated otherwise. Note that there is no loss of generality here, since any rotation to the measurement basis can be absorbed into the definition of
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
. This yields the projected state

{|{\psi_{z}}\rangle}=\frac{(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z}_{B}){|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}}{\sqrt{p_{z}}}\equiv\frac{{|{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\rangle}}{\sqrt{p_{z}}}

(13)

on subsystem
A
, where

p_{z}=\braket{\tilde{\psi}_{z}|\tilde{\psi}_{z}}=(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z}_{B})\left({|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}\right)(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}_{B})

(14)

is the Born probability of measuring the outcome
z
on
B
. Collectively, this defines the projected ensemble on
A
generated by
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
,

\mathcal{E}(\Psi)=\left\{p_{z},{|{\psi_{z}}\rangle}\right\}_{z=1}^{D_{B}}.

(15)

The
k
th moment of the projected ensemble reads

\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)=\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}p_{z}\left({|{\psi_{z}}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi_{z}}\right)^{\otimes k}=\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\frac{\left({|{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\rangle}\!\bra{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\right)^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\tilde{\psi}_{z}|\tilde{\psi}_{z}}^{k-1}}.

(16)

For
k=1
, this is exactly the reduced density matrix of
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
on
A
. To quantify the statistical closeness between the projected ensemble and a reference ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ref}}
(which, in the context of this work, is the Scrooge ensemble or a variant of it), we compute the trace distance of their
k
th moments,

\Delta^{(k)}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)-\rho_{\text{ref}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1},

(17)

where
\rho_{\text{ref}}^{(k)}
is the
k
th moment of
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ref}}
. In this work, we will often consider the scenario where
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
is drawn from some distribution. Thus, a useful metric is the average trace distance

\E_{\Psi}\left[\Delta^{(k)}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\E_{\Psi}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)-\rho_{\text{ref}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}.

(18)

It is important that the trace distance is computed before the average. This implies that if
\E_{\Psi}\left[\Delta^{(k)}\right]
is small, the projected ensemble
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)
is close to
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ref}}
up to the
k
th moment, for
any
generator state
{|{\Psi}\rangle}
drawn from the distribution, with high probability. This fact can be easily derived from Markov’s inequality, which gives

\mathbb{P}_{\Psi}\left(\Delta^{(k)}>\delta\right)\leq\frac{\E_{\Psi}\left[\Delta^{(k)}\right]}{\delta}

(19)

for any
\delta>0
. Computing the trace distance analytically is often difficult. It is more analytically tractable to compute the Schatten
2
-norm distance (also known as the Hilbert-Schmidt distance), which gives an upper bound for
\E_{\Psi}\left[\Delta^{(k)}\right]
:

\E_{\Psi}\left[\Delta^{(k)}\right]\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{D_{A,k}}\left(\E_{\Psi}\left[\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)-\rho_{\text{ref}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}.

(20)

This can be derived using the monotonicity of Schatten norms, given in Eq. (
2
).

5
Scrooge ensemble

The Scrooge ensemble is defined in Ref.
54

\mathcal{E}
that attains the minimum accessible information
\mathcal{I}_{\text{acc}}(\mathcal{E})

\sigma

94

\mathcal{E}=\{p_{z},{|{\psi_{z}}\rangle}\}_{z}
and sends the state to Alice. The ensemble
\mathcal{E}
is known to both parties a priori. Alice’s goal is to determine which state Bob has sent, i.e., the classical label
z
, by performing a measurement (mathematically represented by the POVM
M
) on the state. In the context of the projected ensemble,
p_{z}
is the probability of measuring the outcome
z
on subsystem
B
, and
{|{\psi_{z}}\rangle}
is the normalized state on subsystem
A
conditioned on the outcome
z
. Note that, as explained in Sec.

to be a continuous label, upon which the probability

should be replaced by a probability measure
\mu(z)

The accessible information quantifies how much classical information about

can be gained by Alice via applying an optimal singly-copy measurement on the quantum state. Thus, we define

\mathcal{I}_{\text{acc}}(\mathcal{E})=\sup_{M}\,\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}:M),

(21)

where
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}:M)
is the mutual information between the measurement

\mathcal{E}
. This leads to the Scrooge ensemble (Definition

\displaystyle\text{Scrooge}(\sigma)=\argmin_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\text{acc}}(\mathcal{E}),

(22)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\underset{\psi\sim\mathcal{E}}{\mathbb{E}}{|{\psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi}=\sigma.

The Scrooge ensemble can be explicitly constructed as

\text{Scrooge}(\sigma)=\left\{D\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}\text{d}\phi,\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}}{\left\lVert\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\right\rVert}\right\},

(23)

with
\text{d}\phi
the Haar measure on
\mathbb{C}^{D}
. The
k
th moment of the Scrooge ensemble is computed as follows

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\underset{\psi\sim\text{Scrooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({|{\psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi}\right)^{\otimes k}\right]

(24)

\displaystyle=\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[D\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}\frac{\left(\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma}\right)^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}^{k}}\right]

\displaystyle=D\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{\left(\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma}\right)^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}}\right].

In the limit
\sigma\to I/D

\underset{\psi\sim\text{Scrooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}[f(\psi)]=D\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}f\left(\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}}{\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}^{1/2}}\right)\right].

(25)

Unlike the Haar ensemble, the

written above, which is a rational function of the Haar random state

\sigma

Scrooge

State

k

(\sigma)

(\sigma)

Definition 3

(Approximate Scrooge

.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an ensemble of pure states, with

\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=\underset{\psi\sim\mathcal{E}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({|{\psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi}\right)^{\otimes k}\right].

(26)

\mathcal{E}
is a Scrooge
(\sigma)

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\right\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon\,,

(\sigma)

\varepsilon

(1-\varepsilon)\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\preceq\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\,.

(28)

Here,
\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)
is the

th moment of Scrooge
(\sigma)
, given in Eq. (

The notation

for positive semidefinite operators

and

is equivalent to the statement that

is positive semidefinite.

A relative error of
\varepsilon
implies an additive error of
\varepsilon
. This can be easily derived: Suppose
\mathcal{E}
has relative error
\varepsilon
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\preceq P\preceq I}\Tr\left[P\left(\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\right)\right]

(29)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\sup_{0\preceq P\preceq I}\Tr\left(P\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left\lVert\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon.

However, the converse is not true. The relative error can be arbitrarily large, even if the additive error is small. For example, if
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
has support on the null space of
\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)
, the relative error is infinitely large.

For ensembles of quantum states, the additive error is a meaningful measure of statistical closeness between the ensembles. In the context above, if
\mathcal{E}
is a Scrooge(
\sigma
)

\mathcal{E}
and Scrooge
(\sigma)
with a success probability of at most

p_{\text{succ}}=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}.

(30)

In other words, if the additive error
\varepsilon
is small, the optimal measurement strategy is only marginally better than the naive strategy of random guessing, which has a success probability of

.

To aid the analysis in the paper, we introduce the following technical lemma, which provides an approximation to the

th moment of an ensemble.

Lemma 2
.

[
k
th moment approximation]
Consider the ensemble of pure states

\mathcal{E}=\left\{p_{j}=\braket{\psi_{j}^{\prime}|\psi_{j}^{\prime}},{|{\psi_{j}}\rangle}=\frac{{|{\psi_{j}^{\prime}}\rangle}}{\sqrt{p_{j}}}\right\}_{j=1,\ldots,|\mathcal{E}|}

(31)

with
k
th moment

\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{E}|}\frac{({|{\psi_{j}^{\prime}}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi_{j}^{\prime}})^{\otimes k}}{p_{j}^{k-1}},

(32)

where each unnormalized state
{|{\psi_{j}^{\prime}}\rangle}
in the ensemble is randomly drawn from a distribution
\mathcal{D}_{j}
.
Construct the proxy

\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{E}|}\frac{({|{\psi_{j}^{\prime}}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi_{j}^{\prime}})^{\otimes k}}{q_{j}^{k-1}},

(33)

where
q_{j}>0
are independent of
{|{\psi_{j}^{\prime}}\rangle}
. Then,

\underset{\psi_{j}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}\leq|\mathcal{E}|\left(\underset{\psi_{j}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\frac{2}{q_{j}^{k-1}}\underset{\psi_{j}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{j}^{k-1}+\frac{1}{q_{j}^{2k-2}}\underset{\psi_{j}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{j}^{2k-2}\right)^{1/2}

(34)

Proof.

\displaystyle\underset{\psi_{j}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\psi_{j}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}{\mathbb{E}}\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{E}|}p_{j}^{k}\left\lvert\frac{1}{p_{j}^{k-1}}-\frac{1}{q_{j}^{k-1}}\right\rvert\quad

\displaystyle\text{(Triangle inequality)}

(35)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{E}|}\underset{\psi_{j}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{j}\left\lvert 1-\frac{p_{j}^{k-1}}{q_{j}^{k-1}}\right\rvert\quad

\displaystyle\text{(Linearity of expectation)}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{E}|\left(\underset{\psi_{j}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\frac{2}{q_{j}^{k-1}}\underset{\psi_{j}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{j}^{k-1}+\frac{1}{q_{j}^{2k-2}}\underset{\psi_{j}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{j}^{2k-2}\right)^{1/2}\quad

\displaystyle\text{(Cauchy-Schwarz)}.

∎

Lemma
2
formalizes the intuition that
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
and the proxy
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
are close, if the random variables
p_{j}
are close to
q_{j}
. This allows us to conveniently replace
p_{j}
(which depends on the distribution
\mathcal{D}_{j}
), by the weight
q_{j}
(independent of the distribution
\mathcal{D}_{j}
), with an error that can be controlled.

The following lemma gives us control over the unnormalized projected ensemble, generated by states drawn from distributions that are
\varepsilon
-close in relative error.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be pure state ensembles which are
\varepsilon
-close in relative error up to the first
2k
moments, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,

(1-\varepsilon)\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}\right)^{\otimes 2k}\preceq\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}\right)^{\otimes 2k}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}\right)^{\otimes 2k},

(36)

with
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Let
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)
be the
k
th moment of the unnormalized projected ensemble generated by the bipartite pure state
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
,

\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)=\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}q_{z}^{1-k}[(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z}){|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})]^{\otimes k},

(37)

for an arbitrary orthonormal basis
\{{|{z}\rangle}\}
on subsystem
B
, and
q_{z}\geq 0
are arbitrary non-negative weights independent of
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
. Define

\Delta=\left\lvert\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)-M\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)-M\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right\rvert

(38)

for some arbitrary fixed positive operator
M
. Then,
\Delta
satisfies the inequality

\Delta\leq\varepsilon\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[2\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right)+\left(\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}q_{z}^{1-k}\braket{\Psi|(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}\!\bra{z})|\Psi}^{k}\right)^{2}\right].

(39)

Proof.

Expanding the definition of
\Delta
gives

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left({\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)}^{2}\right)-\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left({\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)}^{2}\right)-2\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right)+2\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right)\right\rvert

(40)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lvert\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right)-\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right)\right\rvert+\left\lvert\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left({\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)}^{2}\right)-\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left({\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)}^{2}\right)\right\rvert.

Since
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)
can be regarded as a positive linear map acting on
\left({|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}\right)^{\otimes k}
, the positive semidefinite ordering (
36
) is preserved, i.e.,

(1-\varepsilon)\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)\preceq\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)\preceq(1+\varepsilon)\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi).

(41)

For a positive operator
M
, this implies

(1-\varepsilon)\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right)\preceq\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right)\preceq(1+\varepsilon)\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right).

(42)

Thus,

\left\lvert\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right)-\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right)\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)M\right).

(43)

Next, we write

\Tr\left({\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)}^{2}\right)=\Tr\left[\mathcal{N}({|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}^{\otimes 2k})\hat{\tau}_{A}\right],

(44)

where

\mathcal{N}(X)=\sum_{z,z^{\prime}=1}^{D_{B}}(q_{z}q_{z^{\prime}})^{1-k}(I_{A}^{\otimes 2k}\otimes\bra{z^{\otimes k}z^{\prime\otimes k}})X(I_{A}\otimes{|{z^{\otimes k}z^{\prime\otimes k}}\rangle})

(45)

is a positive linear map, and
\hat{\tau}_{A}
is a permutation which swaps between the first and second
k
tensor factors of subsystem
A
, and acts identically on subsystem
B
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left({\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)}^{2}\right)-\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left({\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)}^{2}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\Tr\mathcal{N}\left(\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}\right)^{\otimes k}\right)

(46)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\underset{\Psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\sum_{z,z^{\prime}}(q_{z}q_{z^{\prime}})^{1-k}\braket{\Psi|(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}\!\bra{z})|\Psi}^{k}\braket{\Psi|(I_{A}\otimes{|{z^{\prime}}\rangle}\!\bra{z^{\prime}})|\Psi}^{k}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\left(\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}q_{z}^{1-k}\braket{\Psi|(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}\!\bra{z})|\Psi}^{k}\right)^{2}\,.

Combining (
43
) and (
46
) gives the desired bound for
\Delta
.
∎

Appendix B
Scrooge approximation lemma

Let us define the ensemble of unnormalized states

\tilde{\text{S}}\text{crooge}(\sigma)=\left\{d\phi,\sqrt{D\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\right\},

(47)

where
d\phi
is the Haar measure on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{D}
and
\sigma
is an arbitrary density matrix with dimension
D
. Note that
\tilde{\text{S}}
crooge(
\sigma
) is referred to as
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
in the main text. The
k
th moment of
\tilde{\text{S}}\text{crooge}(\sigma)
is given by

\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)=\sqrt{D\sigma}^{\otimes k}\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}\sqrt{D\sigma}^{\otimes k}=(D\sigma)^{\otimes k}\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)},

(48)

where in the final equality we used the fact that
\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}
commutes with
\sigma^{\otimes k}
. While the ensemble
\tilde{\text{S}}\text{crooge}(\sigma)
describes unnormalized states and is unphysical, it is a useful mathematical object in proving the main results of this paper. First, we note that in the low-purity regime, the states in
\tilde{\text{S}}\text{crooge}(\sigma)
are close to normalized.

Lemma 4

(Moment bounds)
.

Let
\sigma
be a positive operator with unit trace. Denote the overlap between
\sigma
and
{|{\phi}\rangle}
by
p_{\sigma}(\phi)\equiv\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}
, where
{|{\phi}\rangle}
is a Haar random state. Assuming
\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}<1
, the
k
th moment of
p_{\sigma}(\phi)
satisfies the inequality

1+\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq k!D_{k}\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[p_{\sigma}(\phi)^{k}\right]\leq 1+\frac{\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{1-\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}}.

(49)

Proof.

Using Eq. (
8
), we can write the
k
th moment of
p_{\sigma}(\phi)
as

\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[p_{\sigma}(\phi)^{k}\right]=\Tr\left(\sigma^{\otimes k}\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}\right)=\frac{1}{k!D_{k}}\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left(\sigma^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}\right).

(50)

Thus, the quantity we would like to bound is

k!D_{k}\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[p_{\sigma}(\phi)^{k}\right]=\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left(\sigma^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}\right).

(51)

First, note that if
\pi
is the identity permutation,
\Tr\left(\sigma^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}\right)=[\Tr\left(\sigma\right)]^{k}=1
, since
\sigma
has unit trace. Next, consider the case where
\pi
contains a single transposition, i.e.,
\pi
has a Cayley distance of
1
. There are
\binom{k}{2}
such permutations, each contributing
\Tr\left(\sigma^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}\right)=\Tr\left(\sigma^{2}\right)=\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. All the remaining terms omitted are positive. Thus, we have the lower bound

\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left(\sigma^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}\right)\geq 1+\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

(52)

To prove the upper bound, let us examine the contribution from all permutations with a fixed Cayley distance
l\geq 1
. There are at most
\binom{k}{2}^{l}
such permutations, and the contribution of each permutation can be upper bounded by
\Tr\left(\sigma^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}\right)\leq\Tr\left(\sigma^{l+1}\right)
. This bound is attained if
\pi
contains a cycle of length
l+1
. Thus, we have

\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left(\sigma^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}\right)\leq 1+\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{l=2}^{k-1}\binom{k}{2}^{l}\Tr\left(\sigma^{l+1}\right).

(53)

Using the inequality

\Tr\left(\sigma^{l+1}\right)\leq\left[\Tr\left(\sigma^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{l+1}{2}}=\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{l+1},

(54)

we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left(\sigma^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\left[\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right]^{l}

(55)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left[\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right]^{l}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{1-\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}},

where the infinite series converges because we assumed
\binom{k}{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}<1
.
∎

The above lemma tells us that, up to corrections proportional to the purity of
\sigma
,

\left\lVert\sqrt{D\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\right\rVert^{2}=D\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}\approx 1,

(56)

where
{|{\phi}\rangle}\sim\text{Haar}(D)
.

The negative moments of
\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}
can also be upper bounded in terms of
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{\infty}
, as shown in Ref.
76
.

Lemma 5

(Lemma 2,
76
)
.

Let
\sigma
be an arbitrary density operator with
m=\lfloor\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{\infty}^{-1}\rfloor
. For
0\leq q<m
,

\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{-q}\leq\exp\left(\frac{q^{2}}{2(m-q)}\right).

(57)

For
q\ll m
, this simplifies to

\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{-q}\leq 1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{q^{2}}{m}\right).

(58)

We now prove Lemma
1
from the main text, which we reproduce here, stated more formally.

Lemma 6

(Scrooge approximation)
.

Consider the ensemble of unnormalized states
\tilde{\text{S}}\text{crooge}(\sigma)=\{\sqrt{D\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\}
, where
{|{\phi}\rangle}\sim\text{Haar}(D)
, and
\sigma
is an arbitrary density matrix with dimension
D
. The
k
th moment of
\tilde{\text{S}}\text{crooge}(\sigma)
is
\tilde{\rho}^{(k)}_{\text{Scrooge}}(\sigma)=(D\sigma)^{\otimes k}\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}
. For any
k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\ll 1
,
\tilde{\text{S}}\text{crooge}(\sigma)
forms a Scrooge
(\sigma)

k
-design with additive error
\mathcal{O}\left(k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right)
, i.e.,

\left\lVert\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)-\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\right\rVert_{1}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right),

(59)

and a Scrooge
(\sigma)

k
-design with relative error
\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right)
, i.e.,

(1-\varepsilon)\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\preceq\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\preceq(1+\varepsilon)\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma),

(60)

where
\varepsilon=\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right)
.

Proof of additive error
\mathcal{O}\left(k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right)
.

\displaystyle\left\lVert\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)-\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle=D\left\lVert\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma}\right)^{\otimes k}\left(\frac{1}{\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}}-D^{k-1}\right)\right\rVert_{1}

(61)

\displaystyle\leq D\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}^{k}\left\lvert\frac{1}{\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}}-D^{k-1}\right\rvert,

where we used

\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)=D^{k}\sqrt{\sigma}^{\otimes k}\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}\sqrt{\sigma}^{\otimes k}=D^{k}\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma}\right)^{\otimes k}

(62)

in the first line. Defining the random variable

X=D\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi},

(63)

we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)-\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}|X-X^{k}|

(64)

\displaystyle=\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lvert(1-X)(X+X^{2}+\ldots+X^{k-1})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left[\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left(1-X\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\left[\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}(X+X^{2}+\ldots+X^{k-1})^{2}\right]^{1/2}.

Explicitly evaluating the Haar averages gives

\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}X=1

(65)

and

\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}=\frac{D}{D+1}\left(1+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right),

(66)

which yields

\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left(1-X\right)^{2}=\frac{D\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-1}{D+1}.

(67)

For
k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\ll 1
, Lemma
49
implies that

\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}X^{k}=1+\mathcal{O}\left(k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right),

(68)

which leads to

\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}(X+X^{2}+\ldots+X^{k-1})^{2}\leq(k-1)^{2}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\right),

(69)

since the left hand side can be expanded as a sum of
(k-1)^{2}
terms, each contributing
1+\mathcal{O}\left(k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)
. Therefore,

\left\lVert\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)-\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\right\rVert_{1}\leq(k-1)\sqrt{\frac{D\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-1}{D+1}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\right).

(70)

This vanishes exactly when
k=1
, or when
\sigma
is the maximally mixed state (i.e., Haar limit). In general, the error bound is
\mathcal{O}\left(k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right)
.
∎

Proof of relative error
\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right)
.

It suffices to show that
\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}
and

A_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)=\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\frac{\left({|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\right)^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}}

(71)

are
\varepsilon
-close in relative error, since multiplying by
\sqrt{D\sigma}^{\otimes k}
on both sides yields
\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)
and
{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)
respectively, which does not increase the relative error. Both
\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}
and
A_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)
are supported on
\mathcal{H}_{\text{sym}}^{(k)}
, the symmetric subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
. For any
{|{x}\rangle}\in\mathcal{H}_{\text{sym}}^{(k)}
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\braket{x|\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}-A_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)|x}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{x|\left({|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\right)^{\otimes k}|x}\left(\frac{1}{\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}}-1\right)\right\rvert

(72)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{x|\left({|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\right)^{\otimes k}|x}\frac{1}{\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}}\left(1-\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{x|\left({|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\right)^{\otimes k}|x}\frac{1}{\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}}\left\lvert 1-\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}\right\rvert.

Applying Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\braket{x|\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}-A_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)|x}\right\rvert\leq\left(\E_{\phi}\braket{x|\left({|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\right)^{\otimes k}|x}^{4}\right)^{1/4}\left(\E_{\phi}\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{4(1-k)}\right)^{1/4}\left[\E_{\phi}\left(1-\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}.

(73)

Now,

\displaystyle\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{x|\left({|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\right)^{\otimes k}|x}^{4}=\braket{x^{\otimes 4}|\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(4k)}|x^{\otimes 4}}\leq\left\lVert\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(4k)}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\frac{1}{D_{4k}}.

(74)

Next, using Lemma
58

76
, for
k\ll m
,

\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{4(1-k)}\leq 1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{m}\right),

(75)

where
m=\lfloor\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{\infty}^{-1}\rfloor
.
Finally, for
k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\ll 1
, we have

\E_{\phi}\left(1-\braket{\phi|D\sigma|\phi}^{k-1}\right)^{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right).

(76)

Note that
k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\ll 1
implies
k\ll m
, since
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\leq\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}
. Combining these bounds, and using the identity
1=D_{k}\braket{x|\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}|x}
, we find

\left\lvert\braket{x|\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}-A_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}|x}\right\rvert\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}}{D_{4k}^{1/4}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}D_{k}}{D_{4k}^{1/4}}\right)\braket{x|\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}|x}=\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right)\braket{x|\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}|x}.

(77)

Thus, the relative error is
\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right)
.
∎

We speculate that the prefactor of
4^{k}
in the relative error can be improved. An analogous relative-error bound
\mathcal{O}\left(k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{\infty}^{1/2}\right)
, which is controlled by
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{\infty}
instead of
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}
, was proven in Ref.
76
.
Our relative error in Lemma
1
has a better scaling with the norm of
\sigma
, since
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{\infty}^{1/2}
, but has a worse scaling in
k
. In essence, the Scrooge approximation lemma (Lemma
1
) tells us that

\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)\approx(D\sigma)^{\otimes k}\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)},

(78)

for arbitrary low-purity density matrix
\sigma
. Crucially, the right hand side involves only the
k
th moment of the Haar ensemble, while
\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma)
is a rational function of Haar random states. This enables us to analyze the scenarios where the Haar ensemble is replaced by a
k
-design, with the rigorous guarantee given by Lemma
1
. We will make extensive use of this in the following Appendices to prove the main theorems in the paper.

2
Canonical thermal pure quantum (cTPQ) states

Next, we show that the cTPQ ensemble forms a Scrooge

Theorem 5

(cTPQ states form Scrooge
k
-designs)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\text{cTPQ}}
be the ensemble of canonical pure thermal quantum states defined in Eq. (
80
). For
k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}\ll 1
,
\mathcal{E}_{\text{cTPQ}}
forms a Scrooge
(\sigma_{\beta})

k
-design with relative error
\varepsilon
satisfying

1+\varepsilon=\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}\right)\right]^{2},

(86)

where
\sigma_{\beta}=\exp(-\beta H)/\Tr\left[\exp(-\beta H)\right]
. For a fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varepsilon=\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}\right)

Proof.

Define
{|{g}\rangle}=\sum_{j=1}^{D}\xi_{j}{|{j}\rangle}
, which is a random (unnormalized) vector of
D
independent zero-mean complex Gaussian variables with unit variance. Writing

{|{g}\rangle}=\left\lVert{|{g}\rangle}\right\rVert\times{|{\phi}\rangle},

(87)

each Gaussian vector
{|{g}\rangle}
is associated with a Haar random state
{|{\phi}\rangle}
. Note that
\left\lVert{|{g}\rangle}\right\rVert
and
{|{\phi}\rangle}
are statistically independent. Thus, we can write the

\mathcal{E}_{\text{cTPQ}}

\underset{\psi\sim\text{cTPQ}}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi}\right)^{\otimes k}=\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\frac{\left(\sqrt{\sigma_{\beta}}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma_{\beta}}\right)^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|\sigma_{\beta}|\phi}^{k}}.

(88)

For any
{|{x}\rangle}\in\mathcal{H}_{\text{sym}}^{(k)}
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\braket{x|\left(\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\frac{{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|D\sigma_{\beta}|\phi}^{k}}-\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}^{\otimes k}\right)|x}\right\rvert

(89)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lvert\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{x|\left({|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\right)^{\otimes k}|x}\braket{\phi|D\sigma_{\beta}|\phi}^{-k}\left(1-\braket{\phi|D\sigma_{\beta}|\phi}^{k}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\left(\E_{\phi}\braket{x|\left({|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\right)^{\otimes k}|x}^{4}\right)^{1/4}\left(\E_{\phi}\braket{\phi|D\sigma_{\beta}|\phi}^{-4k}\right)^{1/4}\left[\E_{\phi}\left(1-\braket{\phi|D\sigma_{\beta}|\phi}^{k}\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2},

where we used Hölder’s inequality in the final line. Now,

\displaystyle\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{x|\left({|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\right)^{\otimes k}|x}^{4}=\braket{x^{\otimes 4}|\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(4k)}|x^{\otimes 4}}\leq\left\lVert\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(4k)}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\frac{1}{D_{4k}}.

(90)

Next, using Lemma
58
, for
k\ll m

\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{\phi|D\sigma_{\beta}|\phi}^{-4k}\leq 1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{m}\right),

(91)

where
m=\lfloor\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{-1}\rfloor

Note that our assumption

already implies

, since
\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\leq\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}
.
Finally,

\E_{\phi}\left(1-\braket{\phi|D\sigma_{\beta}|\phi}\right)^{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(k^{2}\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right).

(92)

Combining these bounds, and using the identity

, we find

\displaystyle\left\lvert\braket{x|\left(\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\frac{{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|D\sigma_{\beta}|\phi}^{k}}-\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}\right)|x}\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{D_{4k}^{1/4}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{m}\right)\right)\mathcal{O}\left(k\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}\right)\braket{x|\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}|x}.

(93)

Thus,

(1+\varepsilon^{\prime})\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}\preceq\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\frac{{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|D\sigma_{\beta}|\phi}^{k}}\preceq(1+\varepsilon^{\prime})\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)},

(94)

where
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}\right)

does not change the relative error, and we get

(1+\varepsilon^{\prime})\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma_{\beta})\preceq\underset{\psi\sim\text{cTPQ}}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi}\right)^{\otimes k}\preceq(1+\varepsilon^{\prime})\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma_{\beta}).

(95)

Recall from Lemma
1

\tilde{\text{S}}\text{crooge}(\sigma_{\beta})
forms a Scrooge
(\sigma_{\beta})

k

\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}\right)
. Combining the relative errors, we obtain the desired result

(1+\varepsilon){\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma_{\beta})\preceq\underset{\psi\sim\text{cTPQ}}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\psi}\right)^{\otimes k}\preceq(1+\varepsilon){\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\sigma_{\beta}),

(96)

where
1+\varepsilon=(1+\varepsilon^{\prime})(1+\varepsilon^{\prime\prime})
.
∎

If we further assume the low-purity regime

\varepsilon=\mathcal{O}\left(4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}\right).

(97)

Lemma 7
.

[Projected ensemble generated by an unnormalized Scrooge state]

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$\tilde{S}$crooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z})\right\rVert_{1}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{k^{k+2}D_{A}^{k-1}}{D_{B}}\left(1+4^{k}D_{A}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)}\right)+\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\mathcal{O}\left(k\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}\right).

(98)

where
\sigma_{A|z}\equiv(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z})\sigma(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})

For a given generator state
{|{\Psi}\rangle}

k

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$\tilde{S}$crooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}p_{z}^{k}=\frac{D^{k}}{k!D_{k}}\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left(\sigma_{A|z}^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}_{A}\right)=\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D}\right)\right]\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}^{k}\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}_{A}\right),

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$\tilde{S}$crooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$\tilde{S}$crooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\underbrace{\E_{\Psi}\Tr\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)2}\right)}_{(*)}-\underbrace{\Tr\left[\left(E_{\Psi}\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right)^{2}\right]}_{(**)}.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)
be the
k
th moment of the projected ensemble
\mathcal{E}(\Psi)
on subsystem
A
generated by
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
, measured in an arbitrary orthonormal basis
\{{|{z}\rangle}\}_{z=1}^{D_{B}}
on subsystem
B
. For any fixed operator
M
, and any density matrix
\sigma

D

\left\lvert\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Scrooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Psi)-M\right\rVert_{1}-\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$\tilde{\text{S}}$crooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Psi)-M\right\rVert_{1}\right\rvert\leq\frac{D\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-1}{D+1}\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{1}.

(123)

Proof.

Let us write
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
explicitly as

\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\frac{[(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z}){|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})]^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\Psi|(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}\!\bra{z})|\Psi}^{k-1}}.

By the definitions of the ensembles
\text{Scrooge}(\sigma)
and
\tilde{\text{S}}
crooge(
\sigma
), we have

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Scrooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-M\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle=D\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}\left\lVert\frac{1}{\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}}\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\frac{[(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z})\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})]^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|\sqrt{\sigma}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}\!\bra{z})\sqrt{\sigma}|\phi}^{k-1}}-M\right\rVert_{1}

(125)

\displaystyle=\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert D\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\frac{[(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z})\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})]^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|\sqrt{\sigma}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}\!\bra{z})\sqrt{\sigma}|\phi}^{k-1}}-D\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}M\right\rVert_{1},

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$\tilde{\text{S}}$crooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-M\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle=\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert D\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\frac{[(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z})\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})]^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|\sqrt{\sigma}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}\!\bra{z})\sqrt{\sigma}|\phi}^{k-1}}-M\right\rVert_{1}.

(126)

For convenience, let us denote

K(\phi)\equiv D\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\frac{[(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z})\sqrt{\sigma}{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\bra{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})]^{\otimes k}}{\braket{\phi|\sqrt{\sigma}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}\!\bra{z})\sqrt{\sigma}|\phi}^{k-1}}.

(127)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Scrooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Psi)-M\right\rVert_{1}-\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$\tilde{\text{S}}$crooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Psi)-M\right\rVert_{1}\right\rvert

(128)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lvert\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left\lVert K(\phi)-D\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}M\right\rVert_{1}-\left\lVert K(\phi)-M\right\rVert_{1}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lvert\left\lVert K(\phi)-D\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}M\right\rVert_{1}-\left\lVert K(\phi)-M\right\rVert_{1}\right\rvert\quad

\displaystyle\text{(Jensen's inequality)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert D\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}M-M\right\rVert_{1}\quad

\displaystyle\text{(Reverse triangle inequality)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lvert 1-D\braket{\sigma|\phi|\sigma}\right\rvert\right)\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\underset{\phi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left(1-D\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{1}\quad

\displaystyle\text{(Cauchy-Schwarz)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-1}{D+1}\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{1}.

z
. The notation
o(1)
is a shorthand for terms that vanish asymptotically in the limit of large Hilbert space dimensions. Thus,

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)-M\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2k+2}}{D}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{4^{k}k^{2k+2}}{D_{B}}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}k^{2k}\right)

(145)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2k+2}}{D}\left(1+4^{k}D_{A}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{D\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}{k^{2}}\right)\right).

This implies the trace distance bound

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)-M\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{D_{A,k}}\left(\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}(\Psi)-M\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{k^{k+2}{D_{A}}^{k-1}}{D_{B}}\left(1+4^{k}D_{A}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{D\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}{k^{2}}\right)}\right).

(146)

The next step is to show that
{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
is, with high probability, close to
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
, when
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
is sampled from the Scrooge
(\sigma)
2k-design. To this end, we use Lemma
2
to write

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\left(\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{z}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left[1-2\frac{\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{z}^{k-1}}{\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}^{k-1}}+\frac{\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{z}^{2k-2}}{\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}^{2k-2}}\right]^{1/2}.

(147)

Using the relative error property

(1-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime})\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$\tilde{\text{S}}$crooge$(\sigma)$}}{\mathbb{E}}{p_{z}}^{\ell}\leq\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}{p_{z}}^{\ell}\leq(1+\varepsilon^{\prime\prime})\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$\tilde{\text{S}}$crooge$(\sigma)$}}{\mathbb{E}}{p_{z}}^{\ell}

(148)

for

. From Eq. (

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$\tilde{\text{S}}$crooge}(\sigma)}{\mathbb{E}}p_{z}^{\ell}=\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}^{\ell}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\ell^{2}\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\right].

(149)

Therefore,

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}+k^{2}\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right).

(150)

By the triangle inequality, and substituting the definition of
M
,

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z})\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{k^{k+2}{D_{A}}^{k-1}}{D_{B}}\left(1+4^{k}D_{A}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{D\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}{k^{2}}\right)}\right)

(151)

\displaystyle+\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}+k^{2}\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right).

It can be verified that this recovers Theorem

when
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=0
. As stated above,
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon+4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right)
, thus

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\rho_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z})\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{k^{k+2}D_{A}^{k-1}}{D_{B}}\left(1+4^{k}D_{A}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{D\varepsilon}{k^{2}}+\frac{4^{k}}{k}D\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}\right)}\right)

(152)

\displaystyle+\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon+4^{k}k\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}+k^{2}\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right).

∎

to leading order, giving Theorem

Theorem 8

(Projected ensemble generated by an approximate
2k
-design state)
.

Let
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
be sampled from a
2k
-design, with relative error
\varepsilon
. Consider the projected ensemble
\mathcal{E}(\Psi)
obtained by applying projective measurements on
B
in an arbitrary orthonormal basis
\{{|{z}\rangle}\}_{z=1}^{D_{B}}
. Then, assuming
k^{2}\ll D_{A}
, with
D_{A},D_{B}\gg 1
,

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar},A}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{\frac{D_{A,k}}{D_{B}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}+D_{A,k}\varepsilon\right)}.

(154)

Proof.

The
k
th moment of the projected ensemble
\mathcal{E}(\Psi)
can be written as

\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}p_{z}^{1-k}[(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z}){|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})]^{\otimes k},

(155)

where

p_{z}=\braket{\Psi|(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}\!\bra{z})|\Psi}

(156)

is the probability of measuring the outcome
z
. Let us construct the proxy

\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}D_{B}^{k-1}[(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z}){|{\Psi}\rangle}\!\bra{\Psi}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})]^{\otimes k}.

(157)

The average trace distance between
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
can be bounded using Lemma
2
,

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\left(\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{z}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-2D_{B}^{k-1}\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{z}^{k-1}+D_{B}^{2k-2}\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}p_{z}^{2k-2}\right)^{1/2}.

(158)

For any
1\leq\ell\leq 2k
,

(1-\varepsilon)\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}{p_{z}}^{\ell}\leq\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}{p_{z}}^{\ell}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}{p_{z}}^{\ell},

(159)

from the definition of the relative error
\varepsilon
. The Haar average of
{p_{z}}^{\ell}
can be explicitly evaluated as

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}{p_{z}}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{\ell!D_{\ell}}\sum_{\pi\in S_{\ell}}{D_{A}}^{\#\text{cycles}(\pi)}=\frac{D_{A,\ell}}{D_{\ell}}=\frac{1}{D_{B}^{\ell}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell^{2}}{D_{A}}\right)\right).

(160)

Thus,

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon+\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}}\right).

(161)

Next, using Lemma
3
with the choice of
q_{z}=1/D_{B}
,
M=\rho_{\text{Haar},A}^{(k)}
,

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle\equiv\left\lvert\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right\rvert

(162)

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}+\varepsilon D_{B}^{2k-2}\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}p_{z}^{k}\right)^{2}.

The first term can be evaluated using

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}=\frac{1}{D_{A,k}}\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=\frac{D_{B}^{k-1}}{D_{A,k}}\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}p_{z}^{k}=\frac{1}{D_{A,k}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}\right)\right).

(163)

The second term can be evaluated using

\displaystyle D_{B}^{2k-2}\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}p_{z}^{k}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=D_{B}^{2k-2}\sum_{z,z^{\prime}=1}^{D_{B}}(I_{A}^{\otimes 2k}\otimes\bra{z^{k}z^{\prime k}})\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(2k)}(I_{A}^{\otimes 2k}\otimes{|{z^{k}z^{\prime k}}\rangle})

(164)

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{B}^{2k-2}}{D^{2k}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D}\right)\right)\sum_{z,z^{\prime}=1}^{D_{B}}\sum_{\pi\in S_{2k}}{D_{A}}^{\#\text{cycles}(\pi)}\bra{z^{k}z^{\prime k}}\hat{\pi}_{B}{|{z^{k}z^{\prime k}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D_{B}^{2}D_{A}^{2k}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D}\right)\right)\left[\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\sum_{\pi\in S_{2k}}{D_{A}}^{\#\text{cycles}(\pi)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}=1\\
z\neq z^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{D_{B}}\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}{D_{A}}^{\#\text{cycles}(\pi)}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D_{B}^{2}D_{A}^{2k}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D}\right)\right)\left(D_{B}D_{A}^{2k}+D_{B}(D_{B}-1)D_{A}^{2k}\right)\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}\right).

Therefore,
\Delta=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)
, and

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right).

(165)

Now,

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)2}-2\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\rho_{\text{Haar},A}^{(k)}+\frac{1}{D_{A,k}}

(166)

\displaystyle=\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)2}-\frac{2}{D_{A,k}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{D_{A,k}},

where

\displaystyle\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)2}

\displaystyle=D_{B}^{2k-2}\sum_{z,z^{\prime}=1}^{D_{B}}(I_{A}^{\otimes 2k}\otimes\bra{z^{k}z^{\prime k}})\rho_{\text{Haar}}^{(2k)}(I_{A}^{\otimes 2k}\otimes{|{z^{k}z^{\prime k}}\rangle})

(167)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D_{B}^{2}D_{A}^{2k}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D}\right)\right)\sum_{z,z^{\prime}=1}^{D_{B}}\sum_{\pi\in S_{2k}}D_{A}^{\#\text{cycles}(\pi)}\bra{z^{k}z^{\prime k}}\hat{\pi}_{B}{|{z^{\prime k}z^{k}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D_{B}^{2}D_{A}^{2k}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D}\right)\right)\left[D_{B}D_{A}^{2k}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}\right)\right)+D_{B}(D_{B}-1)k!D_{A}^{k}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D_{A,k}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{D_{B}}.

Thus,

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{Haar}(D)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{D_{B}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}D_{A,k}}\right)

(168)

and

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{D_{B}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}D_{A,k}}+\varepsilon\right).

(169)

This implies that

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\left(D_{A,k}\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{D_{A,k}}{D_{B}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}+D_{A,k}\varepsilon\right)}.

(170)

Finally, by the triangle inequality,

\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\text{Haar,A}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}+\underset{\Psi\sim\text{$2k$-design}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{\frac{D_{A,k}}{D_{B}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{A}}+D_{A,k}\varepsilon\right)},

(171)

which yields the desired result.
∎

Late-time chaotic Hamiltonian dynamics

Consider a generator state
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
obtained by evolving the initial state
{|{\Psi_{0}}\rangle}_{AB}
under an ergodic Hamiltonian
H
, for a late-time
t
. Generically, it is reasonable to expect
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
to be modeled by the random phase ensemble

\mathcal{E}_{\text{Random Phase}}=\left\{\frac{d^{D}\varphi}{(2\pi)^{D}},\sum_{j=1}^{D}|\braket{E_{j}|\Psi_{0}}|e^{i\varphi_{j}}{|{E_{j}}\rangle}\right\},

(172)

with the diagonal density matrix (in the energy basis
\{{|{E_{j}}\rangle}\}_{j})

\sigma_{\text{diag}}=\sum_{j=1}^{D}|\braket{E_{j}|\Psi_{0}}|^{2}{|{E_{j}}\rangle}\!\bra{E_{j}},

(173)

as described in the main text. If
H
satisfies the
k
th no-resonance condition, Mark et al.
72
showed that the temporal ensemble

\mathcal{E}_{\text{temp}}=\left\{e^{-iHt}{|{\Psi_{0}}\rangle}\,\,|\,t\in(-\infty,\infty)\right\}

(174)

is close to the random phase ensemble, up to the
k
th moment. While we have shown that the random phase ensemble forms an approximate Scrooge
k
-design (Theorem
1
in the main text), Theorem
2
does not strictly apply in this scenario, since the random phase ensemble is not close to Scrooge
(\sigma)
in relative error. Nonetheless, it is interesting to ask whether the projected ensemble generated by a state drawn from the random phase ensemble forms a generalized Scrooge ensemble, as in Theorem
2
.
This problem is partially resolved with Theorem 4 of Ref.
72
, where they showed that the
k
th moment of the
unnormalized
projected ensemble
\mathcal{E}(\Psi)
is approximately that of the
unnormalized
generalized Scrooge ensemble, i.e.,

\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\approx\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}),

(175)

with an error that is expected to be exponential small in the system size. In the above,
\sigma_{A}
and
\sigma_{B}
are the reduced density matrices of
\sigma_{\text{diag}}
on
A
and
B
, respectively.
\sigma_{A|z}\equiv(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z})\sigma(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})

, we close this conceptual gap, by showing that the Scrooge behavior does hold true for the actual

{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\approx\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}).

(176)

This is stated informally in Proposition
1
of the main text, which we reproduce here, stated more formally.

Theorem 9

(Emergent Scrooge designs from late-time generator states)
.

Let
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
be the generator state drawn from the random phase ensemble (
172
). Denote the reduced state of
\sigma_{\text{diag}}
on
A
and
B
by
\sigma_{A}
and
\sigma_{B}
respectively, where
\sigma_{\text{diag}}
is the diagonal ensemble given in Eq. (
173
). Consider the projected ensemble
\mathcal{E}(\Psi)
obtained by applying projective measurements on
B
in an arbitrary orthonormal basis
\{{|{z}\rangle}\}_{z=1}^{D_{B}}
. Then, assuming
k^{2}\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}\ll 1
,

\E_{\Psi}\left\lVert{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z})\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\mathcal{O}\left(k\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta_{\beta}^{1/2}\right),

(177)

where
\sigma_{A|z}\equiv(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z})\sigma(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle})
, and
\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}=\sigma_{A|z}/\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}
is the normalized conditional mixed state on
A
. Above,

\Delta_{\beta}=\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\frac{\braket{z^{\otimes 2}|\Tr_{A}\left(\sigma_{\text{diag}}^{(2)}\right)|z^{\otimes 2}}}{\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}},

(178)

where

\sigma_{\text{diag}}^{(2)}=\sum_{j}|\braket{E_{j}|\Psi_{0}}|^{4}{|{E_{j}^{\otimes 2}}\rangle}\!\bra{E_{j}^{\otimes 2}}.

(179)

Proof.

We first bound the average trace distance between
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
and
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
. Define
X=p_{z}/\E_{\varphi}p_{z}
, where
\E_{\varphi}
indicates the averaging over the uniformly random phases in Eq. (
79
), equivalent to averaging over the generator states
\E_{\Psi}
. Averaging over the random phase ensemble,

\displaystyle\E_{\varphi}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\E_{\varphi}\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}p_{z}\left\lvert 1-X^{k-1}\right\rvert

(180)

\displaystyle=\E_{\varphi}\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\left\lvert(1-X)(1+X+\ldots+X^{k-2}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\left(\E_{\varphi}p_{z}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\E_{\varphi}(1-X)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\E_{\varphi}\left(1+X+\ldots+X^{k-2}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2},

Using

\displaystyle\E_{\varphi}X^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}^{k}\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left[\sigma^{\otimes k}(I_{A}\otimes{|{z}\rangle}\!\bra{z})^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}\right]

(181)

\displaystyle=\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}^{k}\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left(\sigma_{A|z}^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}_{A}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}^{\otimes k}\hat{\pi}_{A}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\mathcal{O}\left(k^{2}\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\text{(Lemma~\ref{lemma:mom_bound})},

we have

\displaystyle\E_{\varphi}\left\lVert\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\mathcal{O}\left(k\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}\right).

(182)

This implies that
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\approx\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
in the low-purity regime. Similarly, from Lemma
1
, we have

\left\lVert\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z})-{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z})\right)\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\mathcal{O}\left(k\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}\right).

(183)

Thus, in the low-purity regime, we have

\E_{\varphi}\left\lVert{\rho}_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}{\rho}_{\text{Scrooge}}^{(k)}(\hat{\sigma}_{A|z})\right\rVert_{1}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta_{\beta}^{1/2}\right)+\sum_{z=1}^{D_{B}}\braket{z|\sigma_{B}|z}\mathcal{O}\left(k\left\lVert\hat{\sigma}_{A|z}\right\rVert_{2}\right),

(184)

where
\Delta_{\beta}
is the error term defined in Ref.
72
, which is argued to be exponentially small in system size in typical many-body systems. The subscript
\beta
indicates that the
{|{\Psi}\rangle}
has an effective temperature
\beta^{-1}
.

Lemma 9
.

[

{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}

\sigma_{A}

{|{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\rangle}=(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z}U){|{\Psi}\rangle}
is given by

\underset{U\sim\text{Haar}(D_{B})}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\rangle}\!\bra{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\right)^{\otimes k}=\frac{D_{A,k}}{D_{B,k}}\sigma_{A}^{\otimes k}\rho_{\text{Haar},A}^{(k)}.

(185)

Proof.

Let us write the Schmidt decomposition of
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
as

{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}=\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}{|{i}\rangle}_{A}\otimes{|{i}\rangle}_{B},

(186)

where
\lambda_{i}
are the Schmidt coefficients, and
|i\rangle
are the corresponding Schmidt vectors. Then, we have

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\text{Haar}(D_{B})}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\rangle}\!\bra{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\right)^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\\
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{\alpha}}\lambda_{j_{\alpha}}\right)^{1/2}\left({|{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\rangle}\!\bra{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}}\right)_{A}\braket{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}|\rho_{\text{Haar},B}^{(k)}|i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}_{B}

(187)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!D_{B,k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\\
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\left(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{\alpha}}\lambda_{j_{\alpha}}\right)^{1/2}\left({|{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\rangle}\!\bra{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}}\right)_{A}\braket{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}|\hat{\pi}_{B}|i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}_{B}.

By orthonormality of the Schmidt vectors, the term
\braket{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}|\hat{\pi}_{B}|i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}_{B}=\delta_{j_{1},\pi(i_{1})}\ldots\delta_{j_{k},\pi(i_{k})}
enforces the constraints on
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}
. This simplifies the sum to

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\text{Haar}(D_{B})}{\mathbb{E}}\left({|{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\rangle}\!\bra{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\right)^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!D_{B,k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\left(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{\alpha}}\right)\left({|{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\rangle}\!\bra{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\right)_{A}\hat{\pi}_{A}^{\dagger}

(188)

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{A,k}}{D_{B,k}}\sigma_{A}^{\otimes k}\rho_{\text{Haar},A}^{(k)},

where we used
\sigma_{A}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\left({|{\lambda_{i}}\rangle}\!\bra{\lambda_{i}}\right)_{A}
.
∎

Lemma 10

(Average
k
-copy overlap between projected states)
.

Let
{|{\Psi}\rangle}_{AB}
be an arbitrary bipartite state, with subsystem density operators denoted
\sigma_{A}
and
\sigma_{B}
respectively. Denote the unnormalized projected state as
{|{\tilde{\psi}_{z}}\rangle}=(I_{A}\otimes\bra{z}U){|{\Psi}\rangle}
. Assume
k^{2}\ll D_{B}
and
k\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{4}\ll\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}
. Then, for any pair of measurement outcomes
z\neq z^{\prime}
,

\underset{U\sim\text{Haar}(D_{B})}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lvert\braket{\tilde{\psi}_{z}|\tilde{\psi}_{z^{\prime}}}\right\rvert^{2k}\leq\frac{k!\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2k}}{D_{B}^{2k}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(k^{2}\frac{\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{4}^{4}}{\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{4}}\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2k+2}}{D_{B}^{2k+1}}\right).

(189)

Proof.

Using Weingarten calculus, we obtain

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\text{Haar}(D_{B})}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lvert\braket{\tilde{\psi}_{z}|\tilde{\psi}_{z^{\prime}}}\right\rvert^{2k}

\displaystyle=\underset{U\sim\text{Haar}(D_{B})}{\mathbb{E}}\Tr\left(\sigma_{B}^{\otimes 2k}U_{B}^{{\dagger}\otimes 2k}{|{z^{\otimes k}z^{\prime\otimes k}}\rangle}\!\bra{z^{\prime\otimes k}z^{\otimes k}}U_{B}^{\otimes 2k}\right)

(190)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi,\chi\in S_{2k}}\text{Wg}(\chi^{-1}\pi,D_{B})\Tr\left(\sigma_{B}^{\otimes 2k}\hat{\pi}_{B}^{\dagger}\right)\braket{z^{\prime\otimes k}z^{\otimes k}|\hat{\chi}_{B}|z^{\otimes k}z^{\prime\otimes k}}

in terms of permutation operators
\hat{\pi}_{B}
and
\hat{\chi}_{B}
, and the Weingarten function
\text{Wg}(\chi^{-1}\sigma,D_{B})
. To proceed, observe that by orthogonality of
{|{z}\rangle}
and
{|{z^{\prime}}\rangle}
, the non-vanishing contributions to the Weingarten sum are those with
\chi
of the form
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\tau
, where
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in S_{k}
are arbitrary permutations acting on the first and second set of
k
-copy replicas, and
\tau\in S_{2k}
is a fixed permutation which swaps between the first and second set of
k
-copy replicas. This will contract every
\bra{z}
with
{|{z}\rangle}
, and every
\bra{z^{\prime}}
with
{|{z^{\prime}}\rangle}
, giving
\braket{z^{\prime\otimes k}z^{\otimes k}|\hat{\chi}_{B}|z^{\otimes k}z^{\prime\otimes k}}=1
. Thus, we rewrite the sum as

\underset{U\sim\text{Haar}(D_{B})}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lvert\braket{\tilde{\psi}_{z}|\tilde{\psi}_{z^{\prime}}}\right\rvert^{2k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{2k}\\
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in S_{k}\end{subarray}}\text{Wg}((\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\tau)^{-1}\pi,D_{B})\Tr\left(\sigma_{B}^{\otimes 2k}\hat{\pi}_{B}^{\dagger}\right).

(191)

Next, we split the sum over
\pi\in S_{2k}
into terms where
\pi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\tau
and
\pi\neq\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\tau
, respectively. This gives

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\text{Haar}(D_{B})}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lvert\braket{\tilde{\psi}_{z}|\tilde{\psi}_{z^{\prime}}}\right\rvert^{2k}

\displaystyle=\text{Wg}(1_{2k},D_{B})\sum_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in S_{k}}\Tr\left({\sigma_{B}^{2}}^{\otimes 2k}\left(\hat{\chi}_{1,B}\otimes\hat{\chi}_{2,B}\right)\hat{\tau}_{B}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{2k}\\
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in S_{k}\\
\pi\neq\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\tau\end{subarray}}\text{Wg}((\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\tau)^{-1}\pi,D_{B})\Tr\left(\sigma_{B}^{\otimes 2k}\hat{\pi}_{B}^{\dagger}\right)

(192)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{D_{B}^{2k}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{7/4}}{D_{B}^{2}}\right)\right]k!\sum_{\chi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left({\sigma_{B}^{2}}^{\otimes 2k}\hat{\chi}_{B}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{2k}\\
\chi_{1},\chi_{1}\in S_{k}\\
\pi\neq\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\tau\end{subarray}}\left\lvert\text{Wg}((\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\tau)^{-1}\pi,D_{B})\right\rvert

where we used
23

\text{Wg}(1_{2k},D_{B})=\frac{1}{D_{B}^{2k}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{7/4}}{D_{B}^{2}}\right)\right]

(193)

valid for
4k^{2}<D_{B}
.
Applying Lemma
49
,

\sum_{\chi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left({\sigma_{B}^{2}}^{\otimes 2k}\hat{\chi}_{B}\right)=\Tr^{2k}\left(\sigma_{B}^{2}\right)\sum_{\chi\in S_{k}}\Tr\left(\frac{{\sigma_{B}^{2}}^{\otimes 2k}}{\Tr^{2k}\left(\sigma_{B}^{2}\right)}\hat{\chi}_{B}\right)=\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2k}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(k^{2}\frac{\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{4}^{4}}{\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{4}}\right)\right],

(194)

using the fact that
\sigma_{A}
and
\sigma_{B}
share the same non-zero eigenvalues. To bound the last term in Eq. (
192
), we use

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{2k}\\
\chi_{1},\chi_{1}\in S_{k}\\
\pi\neq\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\tau\end{subarray}}\left\lvert\text{Wg}((\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\tau)^{-1}\pi,D_{B})\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{2k}\\
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in S_{k}\\
\pi\neq 1_{2k}\end{subarray}}|\text{Wg}(\pi,D_{B})|

(195)

\displaystyle=(k!)^{2}\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{2k}}\left\lvert\text{Wg}(\pi,D_{B})\right\rvert-\left\lvert\text{Wg}(1_{2k},D_{B})\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle=(k!)^{2}\left\{\frac{(D_{B}-2k)!}{D_{B}!}-\frac{1}{D_{B}^{2k}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{7/4}}{D_{B}^{2}}\right)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=(k!)^{2}\left\{\frac{1}{D_{B}^{2k}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{D_{B}}\right)\right]-\frac{1}{D_{B}^{2k}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{7/4}}{D_{B}^{2}}\right)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2k+2}}{D_{B}^{2k+1}}\right)\,.

Substituting these into Eq. (
192
) yields the desired result.
∎

in the main text, which we reproduce here for convenience.

Theorem 10

1
Application: Local Hamiltonian at finite temperatures

Theorem
3
requires the condition
k\ll\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}/\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{4}
, for the projected ensemble to converge to Scrooge(
\sigma_{A}
). As stated in the main text, we can define the effective dimension of
\sigma_{A}
via

D_{A,\text{eff}}=\left(\frac{\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{4}}\right)^{4},

(217)

If
\sigma_{A}=I_{A}/D_{A}
is the maximally mixed state, then
D_{A,\text{eff}}=D_{A}
. Then, the above condition reads
k^{4}\ll D_{A,\text{eff}}
.

This condition is usually satisfied in many-body quantum systems. To analyze a concrete example, let us consider the case where
\sigma_{A}
is the thermal Gibbs state

\sigma_{A}=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{A}}}{\Tr\left(e^{-\beta H_{A}}\right)},

(218)

where
\beta
is the inverse temperature, and
H_{A}
is the Hamiltonian restricted to subsystem
A
.

Let us denote the spectral density of the Hamiltonian
H_{A}
by
f(E)
, which satisfies the normalization

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dE\,f(E)=1.

(219)

We assume that
H_{A}
has a Gaussian spectral density, i.e.,

f(E)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\Delta^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(E-\mu)^{2}}{2\Delta^{2}}\right),

(220)

where

\mu=\frac{1}{D_{A}}\sum_{i=1}^{D_{A}}E_{i}=\frac{\Tr H_{A}}{D_{A}},

(221)

is the mean energy, and

\Delta^{2}=\frac{1}{D_{A}}\sum_{i=1}^{D_{A}}E_{i}^{2}-\mu^{2}=\frac{\Tr H_{A}^{2}}{D_{A}}-\frac{\Tr^{2}H_{A}}{D_{A}^{2}}

(222)

is the variance. To convert the discrete sum over energies to an integral over the spectral density, we use
D_{A}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{D_{A}}\to\int dE\,f(E)
. Thus, the partition function at inverse temperature
\beta
is given by

Z_{\beta}=\Tr e^{-\beta H_{A}}=\sum_{i=1}^{D_{A}}e^{-\beta E_{i}}=D_{A}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dE\,f(E)e^{-\beta E}=D_{A}\exp\left(-\beta\mu+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}\Delta^{2}\right).

(223)

Note that this gives the partition function for any
H
(regardless of spectral density), to quadratic order in
\beta
, i.e., in the high-temperature regime.

The generalized purities read

\Tr\left({\sigma_{A}}^{q}\right)=\frac{Z_{q\beta}}{{Z_{\beta}}^{q}}=\frac{D_{A}\exp\left(-q\beta\mu+\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\beta^{2}\Delta^{2}\right)}{D_{A}^{q}\exp\left(-q\beta\mu+\frac{1}{2}q\beta^{2}\Delta^{2}\right)}=D_{A}^{1-q}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}q(q-1)\beta^{2}\Delta^{2}\right).

(224)

For the above equation to be self-consistent, we need to impose the constraint
\Tr({\sigma_{A}}^{q})\leq 1
which implies the constraint
\beta^{2}\Delta^{2}\leq(2/q)N_{A}\ln 2
. Outside of this range (e.g., at low temperatures), the expression becomes unphysical. This reflects the fact that the finiteness of the spectrum is relevant in the low-temperature regime, leading to the breakdown of the Gaussian approximation. In what follows, we will work in the self-consistent regime.

Let us expand
H_{A}
in the Pauli basis, i.e.,

H_{A}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}c_{m}P_{m},

(225)

where
P_{m}\neq I
is a Pauli string on
N
qubits, and
|c_{m}|\leq 1
are real coefficients, as explained in Eq. (
21
) of the main text. Then,

\mu=\frac{\Tr H_{A}}{D}=0,

(226)

and

\Delta^{2}=\frac{\Tr H_{A}^{2}}{D_{A}}=\frac{1}{D_{A}}\sum_{m,n=1}^{M}c_{m}c_{n}\Tr\left(P_{m}P_{n}\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}c_{m}^{2}\leq M.

(227)

For a geometrically local
H_{A}
, we expect
M\propto N_{A}
, thus
\Delta^{2}=O(N_{A})
. For simplicity, we set
M=\alpha N_{A}
, where
\alpha
is some constant. Note that we are using dimensionless units here, with the characteristic energy scale given by the typical magnitude of
c_{m}
. Using this, we have

\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}=\frac{1}{D_{A}^{1/2}}e^{\beta^{2}\Delta^{2}/2},

(228)

and

\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{4}=\frac{1}{D_{A}^{3/4}}e^{3\beta^{2}\Delta^{2}/2},

(229)

which yields

\frac{\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{4}}{\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}}=\frac{1}{D_{A}^{1/4}}e^{\beta^{2}\Delta^{2}}=\exp\left(\beta^{2}\Delta^{2}-\frac{N_{A}}{4}\ln 2\right).

(230)

For
\beta<\beta_{c}
, where

\beta_{c}=\sqrt{\frac{N_{A}}{4\Delta^{2}}\ln 2}\geq\sqrt{\frac{N_{A}}{4M}\ln 2}=\sqrt{\frac{\ln 2}{4\alpha}},

(231)

the ratio
\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{4}/\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}
is exponentially suppressed in
N_{A}
, and therefore
k\ll\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{2}/\left\lVert\sigma_{A}\right\rVert_{4}
is satisfied for large
N_{A}
and any sub-exponential
k
. Finally, we remark that the value of
\beta_{c}
lies within the range
\beta^{2}\Delta^{2}\leq N_{A}\ln 2/2
, imposed by self-consistency up to
q=4
, as explained above.

Appendix K
Stabilizer states with different basis measurements

In this section, we study the projected ensemble generated from stabilizer states.
We prepare a random
N
-qubit stabilizer state, apply unitaries
U_{\text{B}}
on the bipartition
B
, then proceed to measure
B
in the computational basis and study the emergent Scrooge ensemble on
A
.
In Fig.
S10

2

U_{\text{B}}

\Delta^{(2)}

In contrast, for random single-qubit Haar unitaries, rotation into the T-basis

This contrasts the case of ground states of local Hamiltonians (such as Ising or Heisenberg model), which are weakly scrambled and thus local (magical) measurements are not sufficient to yield Scrooge designs.

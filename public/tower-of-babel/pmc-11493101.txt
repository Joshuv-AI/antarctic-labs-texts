Pulmonary Pasteurella multocida infection with tree‐in‐bud appearance
Authors: Hirokazu Tokuyasu, Katsunori Arai, Yuriko Sueda, Hiromitsu Sakai, Akira Yamasaki
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key messageHere, we report a case of zoonosis pulmonary Pasteurella multocida infection with a tree‐in‐bud appearance. In cases showing a tree‐in‐bud appearance on chest CT images, pulmonary P. multocida infection should be considered in the differential diagnosis, especially in patients with pets.Keywords: cat, CT imaging, Pasteurella multocida, tree‐in‐bud, zoonosis

CLINICAL IMAGE

A 52‐year‐old male who had a cat for the past 6 years visited our hospital upon detection of an abnormal shadow during a medical checkup. He had no respiratory symptoms. Chest computed tomography (CT) revealed bronchiectasis, bronchial wall thickening, and a cluster of small nodules in the right lower lobe, exhibiting a tree‐in‐bud appearance, suggesting pulmonary mycobacterial infection (Figure 1). Abnormal shadows persisted on chest CT images 2 months later. Bronchial lavage from the right side B10 tested positive for Pasteurella multocida. However, polymerase chain reaction results and specimen cultures were negative for Mycobacterium tuberuculosis and M. avium‐intracellulare complex. A diagnosis of pulmonary P. multocida infection was established. As the patient was asymptomatic, he was observed without treatment. Six months later, chest CT showed some improvement in the small nodular shadows (Figure 2). P. multocida, a small gram‐negative coccobacillus, is a zoonotic pathogen in humans. 1 Pets including dogs and cats, frequently carry Pasteurella species. 2 Penicillin is the preferred drug for P. multocida infections. Lower respiratory tract infections with respiratory symptoms, tracheobronchitis, pneumonia, and empyema require antibiotic therapy. A differential diagnosis of pulmonary P. multocida infection should be considered if patients exhibited a tree‐in‐bud appearance on chest CT and have pets.

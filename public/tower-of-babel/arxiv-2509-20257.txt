On the Conjectured Capillary Blaschke-Santaló Inequality
Carlos Cabezas-Moreno, Yingxiang Hu, Mohammad N. Ivaki

Abstract
. We prove that the conjectured capillary Blaschke–Santaló inequality holds for any unconditional, strictly convex capillary hypersurface when θ ∈ ( 0 , π 2 ) \theta\in\left(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) . Moreover, for θ ∈ ( π 2 , π ) \theta\in\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2},\pi\right) , we show that the capillary volume product has no finite upper bound.



1.
Introduction

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\delta=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,D)
be the Euclidean space equipped with its standard inner product and flat connection. Denote by
(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\bar{g},\bar{\nabla})
the unit sphere with its induced round metric and Levi-Civita connection. Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and define the upper half-space as

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,E_{n}\rangle>0\}.

Let
\Sigma
be a properly embedded, smooth, compact, connected, and orientable hypersurface contained in the closure
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
, such that its interior lies entirely in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and its boundary satisfies
\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. We say that
\Sigma
is a capillary hypersurface with constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
if the following condition holds along
\partial\Sigma
:

(1.1)

\langle\nu,E_{n}\rangle\equiv\cos\theta,

where
\nu
denotes the outer unit normal of
\Sigma
. The region enclosed by the hypersurface
\Sigma
and the hyperplane
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is denoted by
\widehat{\Sigma}
. We say that a capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
is strictly convex if
\widehat{\Sigma}
is a convex body and the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
is positive definite.

The capillary spherical cap of radius
r
intersecting
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
at a constant angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
is defined as

(1.2)

\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}\coloneqq\left\{x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\mid|x+r\cos\theta E_{n}|=r\right\}.

For simplicity, we set
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}=\mathcal{C}_{\theta,1}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{\theta}=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\mid x_{n}\geq\cos\theta\}
.

Let
\tilde{\nu}=\nu-\cos\theta\,E_{n}:\Sigma\to\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
denote the capillary Gauss map of
\Sigma
. When
\Sigma
is strictly convex,
\tilde{\nu}
is a diffeomorphism (cf.
[
MWWX25
, Lem. 2.2]
). In this case, the capillary support function of
\Sigma
,
s_{\Sigma}:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
, is defined as

(1.3)

s_{\Sigma}(\zeta)=\langle\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\zeta),\zeta+\cos\theta E_{n}\rangle,\quad\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

For example, the capillary support function of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
is given by

(1.4)

\ell(\zeta)=\sin^{2}\theta-\cos\theta\,\langle\zeta,E_{n}\rangle=1-\cos\theta\,\langle x,E_{n}\rangle

where
x:=\zeta+\cos\theta\,E_{n}
.

Moreover, the hypersurface
\Sigma
can be parametrized via the inverse capillary Gauss map:

(1.5)

X:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\Sigma,\quad X(\zeta)=\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\zeta)=\nu^{-1}(\zeta+\cos\theta E_{n}).

We say that a function
\varphi\in C(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
is even if

(1.6)

\varphi(-\zeta_{1},\ldots,-\zeta_{n-1},\zeta_{n})=\varphi(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n-1},\zeta_{n})\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

We also say that a function
\varphi\in C(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
is unconditional if

(1.7)

\varphi(\pm\zeta_{1},\ldots,\pm\zeta_{n-1},\zeta_{n})=\varphi(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n-1},\zeta_{n})\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Similarly, a capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
is said to be even (resp. unconditional) if its capillary support function
s_{\Sigma}
is even (resp. unconditional).

Suppose
\Sigma
is an even, strictly convex capillary hypersurface. Define

(1.8)

\displaystyle Y:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}},\quad Y(\zeta)=\frac{\ell(\zeta)}{s_{\Sigma}(\zeta)}\zeta.

We denote the image of
Y
by
\Sigma^{*}=Y(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
, and refer to
\widehat{\Sigma^{*}}
as the
capillary polar body
of
\widehat{\Sigma}
. Define

(1.9)

\sigma_{\Sigma}(\zeta)=\frac{s_{\Sigma}(\zeta)}{\ell(\zeta)}\quad\text{for }\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

By
[
MWW25
, Prop. 2.9]
, we have

(1.10)

\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma^{*}})=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{\Sigma}^{n}}\,dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}},

where
dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}=\frac{1}{n}\ell\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
is the cone volume measure of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}
. We also have

(1.11)

\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\sigma_{\Sigma}}{G_{\Sigma}}\,dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}},

where
G_{\Sigma}
denotes the Gauss curvature of
\Sigma
(as a function on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
).

Remark
.

It is clear that
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}^{*}=\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. In general,
\Sigma^{*}
is not a capillary hypersurface.

We now recall the conjectured Blaschke–Santaló inequality in the class of even capillary hypersurfaces:

Conjecture
.

[
MWW25
]

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
\Sigma
be an even, smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface. Then

(1.12)

\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma})\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma^{*}})\leq V(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})^{2}.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\Sigma=\lambda\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
for some
\lambda>0
.

Note that for
\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}
, the inequality (
1.12
) follows from the classical
Blaschke–Santaló inequality, and equality holds for any half-ellipsoid of the form

(1.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}x_{i}^{2}=1,\quad x_{n}\geq 0,\quad\text{where $a_{i}>0$ for $i=1,\ldots n$. }

The classical Blaschke–Santaló inequality in the class of origin-symmetric convex bodies (i.e., compact convex sets with non-empty interior such that
x\in K\Rightarrow-x\in K
) states that

(1.14)

\operatorname{vol}(K)\operatorname{vol}(K^{\circ})\leq V(B^{n})^{2},

where
K^{\circ}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 1,\,\forall x\in K\}
is the (standard) polar body of
K
and
B^{n}
denotes the unit ball of
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\delta)
. Moreover, equality holds only for origin-centered ellipsoids. The inequality was proved by Blaschke
[
Bla45
]
for
n=2,3
and by Santaló
[
San49
]
for general
n
. The full characterization of the equality cases was later obtained by Saint-Raymond
[
SR80
]
. See also
[
MP90
]
and
[
AAKM04
]
.

We say a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is unconditional if

(1.15)

\displaystyle f(\pm x_{1},\ldots,\pm x_{n})=f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Similarly, a convex body
K
is said to be unconditional if its standard support function
h_{K}
is unconditional.

In this paper, we prove (
1.12
) for unconditional, strictly convex capillary hypersurfaces.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\theta\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
. For any unconditional convex body
K
(not necessarily smooth or capillary) with the standard support function
h_{K}
we have

(1.16)

\frac{\operatorname{vol}(K)}{2n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{\theta}}\frac{(1-\cos\theta\,x_{n})^{n+1}}{h_{K}^{n}(x)}\,dx\leq\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})^{2}.

In particular, when
\Sigma
is an unconditional, smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface, we have

(1.17)

\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma})\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma^{*}})\leq\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})^{2}.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if

\Sigma=\lambda\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
for some
\lambda>0
.

See also
2.5
. Let
\theta\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}
denote the centro-affine Laplacian of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}-\cos\theta\,E_{n}
restricted to
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. By the previous theorem,
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
is a local maximizer of the volume product in the class of unconditional capillary bodies. By linearizing the inequality, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\theta\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
and
f\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
be an unconditional function with
\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f=0
. Then

(1.18)

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f+2nf\right)\,dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\leq 2n\frac{\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f\,dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\right)^{2}}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}}.

2.
Case
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
: Unconditional bodies

2.1.
The volume product

Throughout this section we assume
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Let
C
denote the strictly convex body obtained via
\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}
and its reflection across
x_{n}=0
. The (standard) support function of
C
at
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle h_{C}(x)=\begin{cases}|x^{\prime}|\sin\theta&\text{if }|x_{n}|\leq|x|\cos\theta,\\
|x|-|x_{n}|\cos\theta&\text{if }|x_{n}|\geq|x|\cos\theta.\end{cases}

The Minkowski functional/gauge function of
C
is given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle p_{C}(x)=\frac{\cos\theta\,|x_{n}|+\sqrt{|x_{n}|^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,|x^{\prime}|^{2}}}{\sin^{2}\theta}.

This is the support function of a convex body whose boundary consists of a cylindrical part capped by two spheroids.

Let
V=\frac{1}{2}p_{C}^{2}
and
V^{\ast}=\frac{1}{2}h_{C}^{2}
. Note that
V^{\ast}
is the Legendre transform of
V
; see
[
Sch13
, eq. (1.49)]
and
[
Sch13
, Lem. 1.7.13]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The function

x\mapsto V(\sqrt{x})
is concave in
(0,\infty)^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
s=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n-1}
and
x_{n}=t
. Define

(2.3)

\displaystyle v(s,t)=V(\sqrt{x})=\frac{1}{2\sin^{4}\theta}\left((1+\cos^{2}\theta)t+(\sin^{2}\theta)s+2\cos\theta\sqrt{t^{2}+st\sin^{2}\theta}\right).

Then the Hessian of
v
(a function of two variables
s,t
) is

(2.4)

\displaystyle D^{2}v=\begin{bmatrix}v_{ss}&v_{st}\\
v_{ts}&v_{tt}\end{bmatrix}=-\frac{\cos\theta}{4(t^{2}+st\sin^{2}\theta)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\begin{bmatrix}t^{2}&-st\\
-st&s^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

From this we obtain

(2.5)

D^{2}V(\sqrt{x})=-\frac{\cos\theta}{4(t^{2}+st\sin^{2}\theta)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}t^{2}&\cdots&t^{2}&-st\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
t^{2}&\cdots&t^{2}&-st\\
-st&\cdots&-st&s^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Recall
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Then for any
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there holds

(2.6)

\displaystyle D^{2}V(\sqrt{x})(a,a)=-\frac{\cos\theta}{4(t^{2}+st\sin^{2}\theta)^{\frac{3}{2}{}}}\big(t\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}-sa_{n}\big)^{2}\leq 0.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The function
V^{\ast}
is of class
C^{1}
, and for
y
with
|y_{n}|\leq|y|\cos\theta
, we have

(2.7)

DV^{\ast}(y)=\sin^{2}\theta\,(y^{\prime},0).

Moreover, we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\det D^{2}V^{\ast}(y)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }|y_{n}|<|y|\cos\theta,\\
(1-\frac{|y_{n}|}{|y|}\cos\theta)^{n+1}&\text{if }|y_{n}|>|y|\cos\theta.\end{cases}

In particular, the Monge-Ampère measure of
V^{\ast}
is

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mu_{V^{\ast}}(A)=\int_{A\cap\{x:\,|x_{n}|>|x|\cos\theta\}}\left(1-\frac{|x_{n}|}{|x|}\cos\theta\right)^{n+1}\,dx,

for any Borel set
A
.

Proof.

Since
C
is strictly convex, by
[
Sch13
, Cor. 1.7.3]
,
h_{C}
is differentiable at
y\neq 0
. Since
V^{\ast}
is two-homogeneous, we have
DV^{\ast}(0)=0
. Moreover, a convex differentiable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
C^{1}
; see
[
Sch13
, Thm. 1.5.4]
.

The second claim follows from a well-known identity: a smooth, strictly convex hypersurface with support function
h
and Gauss curvature
\mathcal{K}
satisfies

(2.10)

\displaystyle\det D^{2}\frac{1}{2}h^{2}(x)=\frac{h^{n+1}}{\mathcal{K}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right).

See, for example,
[
MP14
, Thm. 3.1]
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A=(0,\infty)^{n}
and
B=\{y\in(0,\infty)^{n}:\,y_{n}>|y|\cos\theta\}
. Then
DV(A)=B
,
DV:A\to B
is a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism and

(2.11)

\displaystyle\det D^{2}V(x)\det D^{2}V^{\ast}(DV(x))=1\quad\forall x\in A.

Proof.

Let
r=\sqrt{x_{n}^{2}+|x^{\prime}|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}
and define
\phi\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
through
\cos\phi=\frac{x_{n}}{r}
and
\sin\phi=\frac{\sin\theta|x^{\prime}|}{r}
. Let
y=(y^{\prime},y_{n})=DV(x)
. Then

(2.12)

\displaystyle y_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{\sin^{4}\theta}(1+\cos\theta\cos\phi)(\cos\theta+\cos\phi),

\displaystyle|y^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{\sin^{3}\theta}(1+\cos\theta\cos\phi)\sin\phi.

Define
f(\phi)=\frac{\sin\theta\sin\phi}{\cos\theta+\cos\phi}=\frac{|y^{\prime}|}{y_{n}}
for
\phi\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Since
f
is strictly increasing and
f(\phi)\to\tan\theta
as
\phi\to{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{-}
as well as
f(\phi)\to 0
as
\phi\to 0^{+}
, the first claim follows.

Note that
V=\frac{1}{2}p_{C}^{2}
is
C^{\infty}
-smooth and strictly convex on the open set
A
, and thus the gradient map
DV:A\to B
is bijective. By the inverse function theorem, since
D^{2}V(x)
is positive definite for
x\in A
, the map
DV
is a diffeomorphism. Moreover, from the definition of the Legendre transform, for
y\in B
, we have
DV^{*}(y)=x
, where
x\in A
satisfies
y=DV(x)
. Thus,
DV^{\ast}(DV(x))=x
and the relation between the determinants follows.
∎

We next recall the Prékopa–Leindler inequality, along with the characterization of its equality cases; see also
[
BB10
,
BD21
,
BFR23
]
.

Theorem

(Prékopa–Leindler inequality, equality by Dubuc)
.

[
Pre71
,
Lei72
,
Bor75
,
Dub77
]

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
and let
f
,
g
and
h
be non-negative integrable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that

h((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\geq f(x)^{1-\lambda}g(y)^{\lambda}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(z)\,dz\geq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1-\lambda}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(y)\,dy\right)^{\lambda}.

If
f
and
g
have positive integrals and equality holds, then there exist
a>0
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and a log-concave function
\tilde{h}
, such that
h=\tilde{h}
,
f(x)=a^{\lambda}\tilde{h}(x+\lambda w)
, and
g(x)=a^{-(1-\lambda)}\tilde{h}(x-(1-\lambda)w)
for almost every
x
.

For
x,y\in[0,\infty)^{n}
, let us adopt the notation

(2.13)

\displaystyle x^{s}y^{t}=(x_{1}^{s}y_{1}^{t},\ldots,x_{n}^{s}y_{n}^{t}).

Note that
V
is unconditional, two-homogeneous, and
C^{\infty}
-smooth on
(0,\infty)^{n}
.
By
2.1
and
[
CKLR24
, Lem. 5.17]
, we have

(2.14)

\langle a,DV(b)\rangle\;\geq\;\langle\sqrt{ab},DV(\sqrt{ab})\rangle\;=\;2V(\sqrt{ab})\quad\forall\,a,b\in(0,\infty)^{n}.

As in the proof of
[
CKLR24
, Thm. 5.19]
(see also
[
FM07
, Prop. 1]
),
for any unconditional convex function
\Phi
we have

(2.15)

\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}e^{-\Phi(x)}\,dx\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}e^{-\Phi^{\ast}(DV(x))}\,dx\;\leq\;\left(\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}e^{-V(x)}\,dx\right)^{2}.

To see this, define
f,g,h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,\infty)
by

f(t)=e^{-\Phi(e^{t})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}},\,g(s)=e^{-\Phi^{\ast}(DV(e^{s}))+\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}},\,h(r)=e^{-V(e^{r})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}},

where
e^{t}=(e^{t_{1}},\ldots,e^{t_{n}})
for any
n
-tuple
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. From the definition of the Legendre transform together with (
2.14
),
it follows that

(2.16)

\displaystyle\Phi(e^{t})+\Phi^{\ast}(DV(e^{s}))\geq\langle e^{t},DV(e^{s})\rangle\geq 2V(e^{\frac{t+s}{2}}).

Hence,
\sqrt{f(t)g(s)}\;\leq\;h\!\left(\tfrac{s+t}{2}\right)
, and by the Prékopa–Leindler inequality,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h\right)^{2}.

Finally, note the following identities:

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}e^{-\Phi(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\Phi(e^{t})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}}\,dt,

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}e^{-\Phi^{\ast}(DV(x))}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\Phi^{\ast}(DV(e^{s}))+\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}}\,ds,

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}e^{-V(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-V(e^{r})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}}\,dr,

which together yield (
2.15
).

Now, let
K
be an unconditional convex body. We take
\Phi=\frac{1}{2}p_{K}^{2}
, where
p_{K}
denotes the Minkowski functional of
K
. Note that
\Phi
is an unconditional convex function. By
2.2
and
2.3
, we have

(2.17)

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}e^{-\Phi^{\ast}(DV(x))}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{DV((0,\infty)^{n})}e^{-\frac{1}{2}p_{K^{\circ}}^{2}(y)}\det D^{2}V^{\ast}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}\cap\{y:\,y_{n}>|y|\cos\theta\}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}p_{K^{\circ}}^{2}(y)}\left(1-\frac{y_{n}}{|y|}\cos\theta\right)^{n+1}\,dy.

Using spherical coordinates and that
K
and
C
are unconditional, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}\cap\{y:\,y_{n}>|y|\cos\theta\}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}p_{K^{\circ}}^{2}(y)}\left(1-\frac{y_{n}}{|y|}\cos\theta\right)^{n+1}\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n-1}}\frac{h_{C}^{n+1}}{h_{K}^{n}}\,d\sigma,

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}p_{K}^{2}(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{nc}{2^{n}}V(K),

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}p_{C}^{2}(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{nc}{2^{n-1}}V(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}),

where
c:=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{2}s^{2}}s^{n-1}ds
; see also
[
Sch13
, eq. (1.55)]
. Therefore,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{vol}(K)}{2n}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n-1}}\left(\frac{h_{C}}{h_{K}}\right)^{n}h_{C}\,d\sigma\leq\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})^{2}.

This completes the proof of the first inequality in
1.1
.

To prove the second inequality in
1.1
, take
K=\widehat{\Sigma}\cup R(\widehat{\Sigma})
, where
\Sigma
is an unconditional, strictly convex capillary hypersurface and
R
denotes the reflection map across
x_{n}=0
. Since

(2.20)

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma^{\ast}})=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\ell^{n+1}}{s_{\Sigma}^{n}}\,d\sigma=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n-1}}\left(\frac{h_{C}}{h_{K}}\right)^{n}h_{C}\,d\sigma,

we obtain

(2.21)

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma})\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma^{\ast}})\leq\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})^{2}.

Next, we proceed with the characterization of the equality cases. By the characterization of the equality cases of the Prékopa–Leindler inequality, for some
a>0
and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle\Phi(e^{t})=-\frac{1}{2}\log a+V(e^{t+\frac{1}{2}w})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Sending all
t_{i}\to-\infty
, we obtain

(2.23)

\displaystyle 0=\log a+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}.

That is,

(2.24)

\displaystyle p_{K}(e^{t})=p_{C}(e^{t+\frac{1}{2}w})\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Therefore, for the matrix
A:=\operatorname{diag}(e^{\frac{1}{2}w_{1}},\ldots,e^{\frac{1}{2}w_{n}})
, we have

(2.25)

\displaystyle p_{AK}(x)=p_{C}(x)\quad\forall x\in(0,\infty)^{n},

where we used
p_{K}(A^{-1}x)=p_{AK}(x)
. Since
AK,C
are both unconditional, we must have
A\Sigma=\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Due to the following lemma,
w_{1}=\cdots=w_{n}
and so

\Sigma=e^{-\frac{1}{2}w_{n}}\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A=\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
with
a_{i}>0
. If
A\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
is
\theta
-capillary, then for all
i
we have
a_{i}=a_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{\theta}
. The outer unit normal to
A\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{\theta}
at
Ax
is given by

\nu(x)=\frac{A^{-1}x}{|A^{-1}x|},

and we have

\langle\nu(x),E_{n}\rangle=\frac{x_{n}}{a_{n}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tfrac{x_{i}^{2}}{a_{i}^{2}}}}.

In particular, for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, the point
e_{i}=\sin\theta\,E_{i}+\cos\theta\,E_{n}\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{\theta}
satisfies

\langle\nu(e_{i}),E_{n}\rangle=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}\theta+\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{a_{i}^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta}}.

If
A\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
is
\theta
-capillary, then there holds

\cos^{2}\theta+\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{a_{i}^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta=1,\quad i=1,\ldots,n-1.

Hence,
a_{i}=a_{n}
for all
i
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
p\in(-n,0)
. For an unconditional convex body
K
define

(2.26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p}(K)=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n-1}}\left(\frac{h_{K}}{h_{C}}\right)^{p}h_{C}\,d\sigma.

If
\operatorname{vol}(K)=\operatorname{vol}(C)
, then

(2.27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p}(K)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(C)=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n-1}}h_{C}\,d\sigma,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n-1}}h_{C}\log h_{K}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n-1}}h_{C}\log h_{C}\,d\sigma.

Moreover, equality holds only when
K=C
.

Proof.

The inequalities follow from the Hölder inequality and (
2.19
).
∎

2.2.
Linearization

We linearize (
1.12
) at
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Let
\psi\colon\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
be an even,
C^{2}
-smooth function such that
\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\psi=\cot\theta\,\psi
. Then, for small
|t|
, the function
\ell+t\psi
is the capillary support function of an even, strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}
. Define

\mathcal{P}(t)=\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}^{\ast}}).

Then we have

(2.28)

\displaystyle n\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{P}=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\psi}{G_{\Sigma_{t}}}\,d\sigma\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\ell^{n+1}}{s_{\Sigma_{t}}^{n}}\,d\sigma-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{s_{\Sigma_{t}}}{G_{\Sigma_{t}}}\,d\sigma\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\ell^{n+1}}{s_{\Sigma_{t}}^{n+1}}\psi\,d\sigma

and

(2.29)

\displaystyle n\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{t=0}\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\psi(\bar{\Delta}\psi+(n-1)\psi)\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell-2n\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\psi\right)^{2}+(n+1)\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\psi^{2}}{\ell},

where all integrals are taken with respect to
d\sigma=d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
.

Set
f=\psi/\ell
. Rewriting the second derivative of
\mathcal{P}
at
t=0
in terms of the centro-affine geometry of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
(more precisely, of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}-\cos\theta\,E_{n}
), for any even function
f\colon\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f=0
, we obtain

(2.30)

\displaystyle n^{3}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{t=0}\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f+(n-1)f)\,dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle-2n\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f\,dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\right)^{2}+(n+1)\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f^{2}\,dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}.

For detailed background on centro-affine geometry, we refer the reader to
[
Mil23
]
. Here, we only used

(2.31)

\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f+(n-1)f=\bar{\Delta}\psi+(n-1)\psi.

By
1.1
, for
\theta\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2})
,
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
is a local maximizer of
\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma})\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma^{\ast}})
in the class of unconditional capillary hypersurfaces. Hence, if
f\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
is an unconditional function with
\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f=0
, then

(2.32)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f+2nf)\,dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\leq 2n\frac{\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f\,dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\right)^{2}}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}dV_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}}.

This completes the proof of
1.2
.

3.
Case
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
: Unboundedness

From the proof of
2.1
, it follows that
V(\sqrt{x})
is convex for
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
, which makes the method used in the proof of
1.1
ineffective. In this section, we show that the volume product is in fact unbounded when
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
.

Example 1:
We construct a family of
C^{1,1}
-smooth convex bodies that are smooth near
x_{n}=0
and meet this hyperplane at the constant angle
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
. First, we define a family of convex bodies in the plane, which we may then rotate about the
x_{n}
-axis to obtain the desired sequence. Let
B^{2}
denote the unit disk. For
\lambda>0
, define

\displaystyle K_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{(x,y)\in\frac{1}{\lambda}\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}:y\leq-\frac{\cos\theta}{\lambda}\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda},-\frac{\cos\theta}{\lambda}\leq y\leq\lambda\right\}\cup\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}B^{2}+\lambda E_{2}\right).

We have
\operatorname{vol}(\lambda K_{\lambda})\geq\pi(\lambda^{2}+\cos\theta)
and thus
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\operatorname{vol}(K_{\lambda})\geq\pi
. The capillary support function
s_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}
of the boundary hypersurface
\Sigma_{\lambda}=\overline{\partial K_{\lambda}\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
, restricted to the set
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\cap\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y\leq-\frac{1}{2}\cos\theta\right\}
, is:

s_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(\zeta)=\left\langle\frac{1}{\lambda}\zeta,\zeta+\cos\theta E_{2}\right\rangle\leq\frac{1-\cos\theta}{\lambda}.

Moreover, we have

2\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{*}})=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\ell^{3}}{s_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}^{2}}\,d\sigma\geq\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\theta}}\frac{\ell^{3}}{s_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}^{2}}\,d\sigma=\infty,

meaning that
\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{*}})\to\infty
, and thus
\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma_{\lambda}})\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{*}})\to\infty
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Example 2:
We consider the intersection of an ellipse and the half-space
y\geq 0
given by:

(3.1)

\displaystyle K=\left\{(x,y):\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{(y-c)^{2}}{b^{2}}\leq 1,\quad y\geq 0\right\},

where
a>0
,
b>c>0
are constants to be determined later. Let us put
\Sigma=\overline{\partial K\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
. Suppose the contact angle of
\Sigma
with
y=0
is
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
.

Using the polar coordinates,
\Sigma
can be expressed by

(3.2)

\displaystyle X(t)=(a\cos t,b\sin t+c),\quad-\arcsin(\frac{c}{b})\leq t\leq\pi+\arcsin(\frac{c}{b}).

The unit outward normal at the point
X(t)
is given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle N(t)=\frac{(b\cos t,a\sin t)}{\sqrt{b^{2}\cos^{2}t+a^{2}\sin^{2}t}}.

Note that the range of
N(t)
as
t
varies is the set

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{1}:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}^{1}:y\geq\cos\theta=-\frac{ac}{\sqrt{b^{4}+(a^{2}-b^{2})c^{2}}}\right\}.

We take
a=1/b
and set
c=\eta b
, where
\eta\in(0,1)
. Then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq\frac{\pi}{2}.

Moreover, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\cos\theta

\displaystyle=-\frac{ac}{\sqrt{b^{4}+(a^{2}-b^{2})c^{2}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{(1-\eta^{2})b^{4}+\eta^{2}}}

and hence

(3.7)

\displaystyle\eta=-\frac{b^{2}\cos\theta}{\sqrt{b^{4}\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta}}=\frac{b^{2}}{\sqrt{b^{4}+\tan^{2}\theta}}.

The support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}
,
h_{\widehat{\Sigma}}
, over
\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{1}
is given by

(3.8)

\displaystyle h_{\widehat{\Sigma}}(\psi)=h_{\widehat{\Sigma}}(\cos\psi,\sin\psi)

\displaystyle=c\sin\psi+\sqrt{a^{2}\cos^{2}\psi+b^{2}\sin^{2}\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{3}\sin\psi}{\sqrt{b^{4}+\tan^{2}\theta}}+\sqrt{b^{-2}\cos^{2}\psi+b^{2}\sin^{2}\psi},

where
\psi\in[\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta,\frac{\pi}{2}+\theta]
.
Then, for some constant
c_{\theta}>0
we get

(3.9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma^{\ast}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{\theta}}{2}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta}^{\frac{\pi}{2}+\theta}\frac{1}{h_{\widehat{\Sigma}}^{2}(\psi)}\,d\psi

\displaystyle=c_{\theta}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{1}{(\frac{b^{3}\sin\psi}{\sqrt{b^{4}+\tan^{2}\theta}}+\sqrt{b^{-2}\cos^{2}\psi+b^{2}\sin^{2}\psi})^{2}}\,d\psi.

Assume that
b\gg 1
and
\psi\in(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta,\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta+\delta)\subset(-\frac{\pi}{2},0)
, where
\delta:=b^{-3}
. Then we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sqrt{b^{-2}\cos^{2}\psi+b^{2}\sin^{2}\psi}\leq\sqrt{b^{-2}\sin^{2}\theta+b^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}.

To see this, let us define the function

f(\alpha)=\sqrt{b^{-2}\cos^{2}\alpha+b^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha},\quad\alpha\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},0).

Let
\phi=\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta
. Since
\theta\in\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi\right)
, we have
\phi\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},0\right)
, and
\psi\in(\phi,\phi+\delta)\subset\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},0\right)
. The right-hand side of the inequality (
3.10
) is

\sqrt{b^{-2}\sin^{2}\theta+b^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}=\sqrt{b^{-2}\cos^{2}\phi+b^{2}\sin^{2}\phi}=f(\phi).

Thus, the inequality reduces to
f(\psi)\leq f(\phi)
for
\psi\in(\phi,\phi+\delta)
. Define

g(\alpha)=f(\alpha)^{2}=b^{-2}\cos^{2}\alpha+b^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha.

Then

g^{\prime}(\alpha)=-2b^{-2}\cos\alpha\sin\alpha+2b^{2}\sin\alpha\cos\alpha=(b^{2}-b^{-2})\sin 2\alpha<0.

Thus
f(\alpha)
is decreasing.

Now using inequality (
3.10
) we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle h_{\widehat{\Sigma}}(\psi)=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\frac{b^{3}\sin\psi}{\sqrt{b^{4}+\tan^{2}\theta}}+\sqrt{b^{-2}\cos^{2}\psi+b^{2}\sin^{2}\psi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\frac{b^{3}\sin(\phi+\delta)}{\sqrt{b^{4}+\tan^{2}\theta}}+\sqrt{b^{-2}\sin^{2}\theta+b^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\frac{b^{3}\cos(\theta-\delta)}{\sqrt{b^{4}+\tan^{2}\theta}}+b^{-1}(-\cos\theta)\sqrt{b^{4}+\tan^{2}\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\frac{b^{3}(\cos\theta\cos\delta+\sin\theta\sin\delta)-b^{3}\cos\theta-b^{-1}\sin\theta\tan\theta}{\sqrt{b^{4}+\tan^{2}\theta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\frac{b^{3}(\cos\theta\,(\cos\delta-1)+\sin\theta\sin\delta)-b^{-1}\sin\theta\tan\theta}{\sqrt{b^{4}+\tan^{2}\theta}}.

Note that the leading term is

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{b^{3}(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\cos\theta+\delta\sin\theta)-b^{-1}\sin\theta\tan\theta}{b^{2}}\sim(\sin\theta)b^{-2}.

Therefore, we obtain

(3.13)

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma^{\ast}})\geq

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle c_{\theta}^{\prime}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta+\delta}\frac{b^{4}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\,d\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\frac{c_{\theta}^{\prime}b}{\sin^{2}\theta}\rightarrow\infty,\quad\text{as $b\rightarrow\infty$}.

That is,
\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma})\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\Sigma^{\ast}})\rightarrow\infty
as
b\rightarrow\infty
.

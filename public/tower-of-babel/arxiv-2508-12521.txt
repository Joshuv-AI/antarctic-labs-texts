A basis of the alternating diagonal coinvariants
Yuhan Jiang

Abstract
. We give an explicit basis for the alternating part of the diagonal coinvariant ring in terms of bivariate Vandermonde determinants, answering a question of Stump.



1.
Introduction

The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts diagonally on the polynomial ring

\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]:=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n}].

The
diagonal coinvariant ring

DR_{n}
is defined to be
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]/\mathcal{I}
, where
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal generated by all invariant polynomials without constant term
[
Hai94
]
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be the ideal generated by all
alternating polynomials
.
The
q,t
-Catalan numbers were first defined by Haiman as the
bigraded Hilbert series
of
\mathcal{A}
, for which Haglund
[
Hag03
]
later gave combinatorial formulas in terms of Dyck path statistics.

For
X=((\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}),\dots,(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}))\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
, we define the
bivariate Vandermonde determinant
by

\Delta_{X}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):=\det(x_{i}^{\alpha_{j}}y_{i}^{\beta_{j}})_{i,j=1}^{n}.

In
[
Stu10
]
, Stump asked for a vector space basis of
\mathcal{A}
given by a maximal linearly independent subset of the bivariate Vandermonde determinants with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{+}^{S_{n}}
.
We exhibit such a basis of
\mathcal{A}
coming from antisymmetrizing the monomial basis of
DR_{n}
given by
[
CO25
]
.
To state our main theorem, we need to define some Dyck path statistics.

A lattice path is a sequence of north
(0,1)
and east
(1,0)
steps in the first quadrant of the
xy
-plane, starting at the origin
(0,0)
.
We let
L_{n,m}
be the set of lattice paths that end at
(n,m)
, and
L_{n,m}^{+}
denote the subset of
L_{n,m}
that stays above
y=\frac{m}{n}x
.
A Dyck path is an element of
L_{n,n}^{+}
for some
n
.
For a Dyck path
\pi\in L_{n,n}^{+}
, the
area sequence
of
\pi
is the tuple
\mathbf{area}(\pi)=(a_{1}(\pi),\dots,a_{n}(\pi))
such that
a_{i}(\pi)
is the number of complete squares in the
i
th row from the bottom of
\pi
, which are to the right of
\pi
and to the left of the line
x=y

[
Hag08
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

For a Dyck path
\pi\in L_{n,n}^{+}
,, the
dinv sequence
of
\pi
is the tuple
\mathbf{dinv}(\pi)=(d_{1}(\pi),\dots,d_{n}(\pi))
such that
d_{i}(\pi)=|\{j>i\mid a_{j}\in\{a_{i},a_{i}-1\}\}|
.

We recover the dinv statistic of
[

]
by adding up the dinv sequence.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any Dyck path
\pi\in L_{n,n}^{+}

3.
Proof of the main theorem

Proposition 3.1
.

The bivariate Vandermonde determinant
\Delta_{X(\pi)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is nonzero.

Proof.

The bivariate Vandermonde determinant vanishes if and only if
X(\pi)
has repeated entries.
However, if
a_{i}=a_{j}
with
i<j
, then
d_{i}\geq d_{j}+1
, because any
k>j
that forms a diagonal inversion with
j
also forms a diagonal inversion with
i
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

For any two Dyck paths
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in L_{n,n}^{+}
, the underlying set of
X(\pi)
is not equal to the underlying set of
X(\pi^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists two Dyck paths with the same dinv-area set, then their dinv-area sequence differ by a permutation.
Let
i
be the minimal index for which
a_{i}(\pi)\neq a_{i}(\pi^{\prime})
and
d_{i}(\pi)\neq d_{i}(\pi^{\prime})
.
Let
j
be the minimal index for which
a_{j}(\pi^{\prime})=a_{i}(\pi)
and
d_{j}(\pi^{\prime})=d_{i}(\pi)
. In particular,
j>i
.
For
(a_{i})
to be an area sequence, the entries satisfy inequalities

1\leq i-a_{i}\leq j-a_{j}\leq n

for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
.
Since
d_{i}(\pi)=d_{j}(\pi^{\prime})
, we have
|a_{j}(\pi^{\prime})-a_{k}(\pi^{\prime})|\geq 2
for all
k\in[i+1,j-1]
.
Since
a_{k+1}-a_{k}\leq 1
for all
k\in[n-1]
, we have
a_{k}(\pi^{\prime})\geq a_{j}(\pi^{\prime})+2=a_{i}(\pi)+2
for all
k\in[i+1,j-1]
.
In particular,
a_{i+1}(\pi^{\prime})\geq a_{i}(\pi)+2
, but also
a_{i+1}(\pi^{\prime})\leq a_{i}(\pi^{\prime})+1
, so we have
a_{i}(\pi^{\prime})\geq a_{i}(\pi)+1
.
Similarly, by switching the role of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
, we obtain
a_{i}(\pi)\geq a_{i}(\pi^{\prime})+1
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proof of
1.2
.

We want to show that the matrix formed by writing each
\Delta_{X(\pi)}
as a linear combination of the basis in
2.6
or
2.8
has full rank equal to the Catalan number.

By
2.3
, we have

\Delta_{X(\pi)}=\pm\Delta_{X(\phi(\pi))}

which contains the monomial
\sigma(\mathbf{y}^{\mathbf{area}(\phi(\pi))}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{dinv}(\phi(\pi))})
where
\sigma
is the labelling permutation of the parking function
\phi(\pi)
.
By
3.1
and
3.2
,
\{\Delta_{X(\pi)}\}_{\pi\in L_{n,n}^{+}}
does not contain the zero element, and since there is no repetition of monomials, we have

\left|\{\Delta_{X(\pi)}\}_{\pi\in L_{n,n}^{+}}\right|=|L_{n,n}^{+}|=\Cat_{n}=\dim\mathcal{A},

so they form a vector space basis of
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

4.
Discussion and Conjectures

We conclude with two conjectures that point to potential extensions of our framework. These conjectures arise from the duality between diagonal coinvariants and diagonal harmonics, and interests in rational Catalan combinatorics.

4.1.
Diagonal Harmonics

We discuss the relation between the basis of the diagonal harmonics given by Haiman
[
Hai02
]

[
GZ22
]

[
All98

The space of
diagonal harmonics
is defined to be

DH_{n}=\{f\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{h}}{x_{i}^{h}}\frac{\partial^{k}}{y_{i}^{k}}f=0,\forall h+k>0\}.

The space of diagonal harmonics is orthogonal to the space diagonal coinvariants under the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=f(\partial\mathbf{x},\partial\mathbf{y})g(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}=0},

and they are isomorphic as doubly graded
S_{n}
-modules
[
Hai94
]
.
The dual space of
\mathcal{A}
in

is called the space of

.

Let
\Delta=\Delta_{n}(\mathbf{x})

Garsia and Zabrocki

4.2.
q,t-Fuß-Catalan

We conjecture that there exists a way to combine m-tuples of Dyck paths to an
m
-Dyck path such that the area sequence and bounce statistics are additive. However, known bijections that are additive on the area sequence such as
[
Ath05
]
does not preserve additivity on the bounce or dinv statistics.

Let
DR_{n}=\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]/\mathcal{I}
be the diagonal coinvariant ring, where
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal generated by all
S_{n}
-invariant polynomials without constant term, and
\mathcal{A}
be the ideal generated by all alternatign polynomials, Garsia and Haiman
[
GH96
]
defined the
space of generalized diagonal coinvariants
as

DR_{n}^{(m)}:=(\mathcal{A}^{m-1}/\mathcal{A}^{m-1}\mathcal{I})\otimes\epsilon^{\otimes(m-1)},

where
\epsilon
is the 1-dimensional sign representation.
The alternating component of
DR_{n}^{(m)}
is

\displaystyle M^{(m)}

\displaystyle:=e_{\epsilon}(DR_{n}^{(m)})

\displaystyle\cong\left(\mathcal{A}^{m}/\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle\mathcal{A}^{m}\right)\otimes\epsilon^{\otimes(m-1)}.

For a bigraded module
M
, let
M_{i,j}
denote the bihomogeneous component of
M
in bidegree
(i,j)
.
The
bigraded Hilbert series
of
M
is
\mathcal{H}(M;q,t)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\dim(M_{i,j})q^{i}t^{j}
.
The
q,t
-Fuß-Catalan numbers
were defined by Haiman
[
Hai94
]
as the bigraded Hilbert series of the alternating component of the space of generalized diagonal coinvariants,

\Cat_{n}^{(m)}:=\mathcal{H}(M^{(m)};q,t).

Garsia and Haiman
[
GH96
]
showed that

\Cat_{n}^{(m)}(q,1)=\sum_{D\in L_{mn,n}^{+}}q^{\area(D)},

and

q^{m\binom{n}{2}}\Cat_{n}^{(m)}(q,q^{-1})=\frac{1}{[mn+1]_{q}}\left[\begin{matrix}(m+1)n\\
n\end{matrix}\right]_{q}.

The elements of
L_{mn,n}^{+}
are also called
m
-Dyck paths
.

Loehr
[
Loe05
]
defined a bounce statistic for
m
-Dyck paths. We conjecture that the relation between
m
-Dyck paths and tuples of
m
Dyck paths given by Anthanasiadis
[
Ath05
]
is additive on the bounce statistics.

(
[

Given a Dyck path

of

Start at
(0,0)
and travel north along
\pi
until you encounter the beginning of an east step. Then turn east and travel until you hit the diagonal. Then turn north again, etc. Continue this way until you arrive at
(n,n)
.
The bounce path will hit the diagonal at places
(0,0),(j_{1},j_{1}),(j_{2},j_{2}),\dots,(j_{b-1},j_{b-1}),(n,n)
.
We define the
bounce statistic
to be the sum

\bounce(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{b-1}n-j_{i}

(
[
Loe05
]
)
.

Given an
m
-Dyck path
\pi\in L_{mn,n}^{+}
, define the

of
\pi

v_{0},v_{1},\dots
and horizontal moves of lengths

. These lengths are calculated as follows.

Start at
(0,0)
and travel north along
\pi
until you encounter the beginning of an east step; the distance traveled is
v_{0}

Then travel
v_{0}
units east, so
h_{0}=v_{0}
.
Next, travel
v_{1}
units north until you encounter an east step, and travel

units east.
For

For

units until we are blocked by the path, but then we move

Let
\Phi
be a crystallographic root system. The
root poset
of
\Phi
is given by the partial order on the set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
defined by covering relations

\alpha\prec\beta\iff\beta-\alpha\text{ is a simple root. }

An
order ideal

I\trianglelefteq\Phi^{+}
is a subset
I\subseteq\Phi^{+}
such that
\alpha\leq\beta\in I
implies
\alpha\in I
.
Let
\mathcal{I}
be an increasing chain of order ideals
I_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I_{m}\in\Phi^{+}
. We call
\mathcal{I}
a
filtered chain
of length
m
if for
i,j\geq 1

\displaystyle(I_{i}+J_{i})

\displaystyle\cap\Phi^{+}\subseteq I_{i+j},i+j\leq m

\displaystyle(J_{i}+J_{j})

\displaystyle\cap\Phi^{+}\subseteq J_{i+j},

where
J_{i}=\Phi^{+}\setminus I_{i}
and
J_{i}=J_{m}
for
i>m
.

There is a well-known bijection
\phi
between the order ideals in the
\Phi^{+}
and 1-Dyck paths. Therefore, a filtered chain of length
m
is also an
m
-tuple of 1-Dyck paths.

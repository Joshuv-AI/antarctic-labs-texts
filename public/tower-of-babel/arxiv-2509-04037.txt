Reputation, Risk, and Visibility
Georgy Lukyanov

Abstract
When does reputation make experts play it safe, and what policy reverses that? I isolate a single lever—visibility of outcomes. In a two-page model with binary signals and outcomes, I show: (i) with an uninformative safe option and symmetric visibility, career concerns alone do not generate conservatism; (ii) increasing failure visibility (as in Registered Reports) weakens high-reputation caution and relaxes selection. I provide a necessary-and-sufficient local sign test, agnostic about utility curvature and allowing informative safe actions, and an identification map suitable for staggered RR adoption.



Keywords:
reputation, risk-taking, visibility, survival, Bayesian learning, Registered Reports.

JEL:
D82, D83, C73.

Two labs run the same study. One gets a null and quietly shelves it; the other, pre-accepted as a Registered Report, publishes the same null. Which PI takes the next risky project? The answer turns on a simple design choice: whether failures survive and are counted. This paper treats that choice—
visibility of realized outcomes
—as the policy knob and shows how it flips reputational incentives to play it safe.

This paper studies when reputation pushes experts toward safe choices, and how visibility changes that. By visibility I mean whether successes and failures are observed and counted. The object is a survival rule
\sigma(y,\pi)
giving the probability that outcome
y\in\{0,1\}
remains on the record when current reputation is
\pi
. I keep the model minimal and use it to generate clean tests.

The first result is a
no-free-conservatism
statement. If the safe action does not inform about type and visibility treats successes and failures symmetrically (and does not depend on
\pi
at the evaluation point), then career concerns alone do not generically create caution. Locally, the sign of the risky cutoff’s slope is pinned by the curvature of the career value
V(\pi)
; with linear or convex
V
there is no reputational conservatism.

The second result is a
visibility reform
. Increasing failure visibility—holding success visibility fixed or rising less—raises the local slope of the risk premium and weakens high-reputation caution. Under linear
V
the effect is strictly positive in closed form.

These two results deliver testable implications without estimating preferences. After a move like Registered Reports: (i) the share of risky designs rises more for higher-reputation authors; (ii) success conditional on choosing risk falls because selection relaxes; and (iii) null results survive at higher rates, especially where “safe” work was uninformative before the reform. I provide a short identification map for these signs; details are in the Appendix.

The contribution is to center visibility as the policy lever and separate it from transfers, bonuses, and committee rules. I also allow the safe action to be informative and keep the curvature of
V
explicit. A general local sign test summarizes the comparative statics and nests these cases.

Design Tests: No-Free-Conservatism and Visibility Reform

2.1
No-free-conservatism

Proposition 5
.

Suppose the safe action is uninformative and visibility is symmetric and constant:

r_{H}=r_{L}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma(1,\pi)=\sigma(0,\pi)=\sigma_{S}(1,\pi)=\sigma_{S}(0,\pi)=\bar{\sigma}\in(0,1].

Under Assumption
1
, the following hold for every
\pi\in(0,1)
:

(i)

If
V
is locally linear at
\pi
(i.e.,
V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)=0
), then
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)=0
(no conservatism).

(ii)

If
V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)>0
, then
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)>0
(reputation weakly
encourages
risk locally).

(iii)

If
V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)<0
, then
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)<0
(reputation weakly
discourages
risk locally).

Hence, without a visibility asymmetry or informative safe action, reputational conservatism is
not
a generic consequence of career concerns; its local sign is governed only by the curvature of
V
at
\pi
.

Proof.

With
r_{H}=r_{L}
and constant, symmetric visibility
\sigma(1,\pi)=\sigma(0,\pi)=\sigma_{S}(1,\pi)=\sigma_{S}(0,\pi)=\bar{\sigma}
, we have

U(S\mid\pi)=\bar{\sigma}V(\pi)
and
U(R\mid\pi)=\bar{\sigma}\,\mathbb{E}[V(\pi^{\prime})\mid\pi]
, so

\Delta(\pi)=\bar{\sigma}\{\mathbb{E}[V(\pi^{\prime})\mid\pi]-V(\pi)\}
.
Since
\mathbb{E}[\pi^{\prime}|\pi]=\pi
(posteriors are a martingale), a Taylor expansion gives

\mathbb{E}[V(\pi^{\prime})\mid\pi]=V(\pi)+\tfrac{1}{2}V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)\mathrm{Var}(\pi^{\prime}\mid\pi)+o(\mathrm{Var})
.
Hence
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)=\bar{\sigma}\{\tfrac{1}{2}V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)\mathrm{Var}(\pi^{\prime}\mid\pi)+o(\mathrm{Var})\}
,
so locally
\mathrm{sign}\,\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)=\mathrm{sign}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)
. In particular, if
V
is linear,
\mathbb{E}[V(\pi^{\prime})]-V(\pi)=0
exactly, so
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)\equiv 0
.
∎

If
V(x)=ax+b
and visibility is constant and symmetric across
R
and
S
, then
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)\equiv 0

exactly
(not just locally). This follows from the identity

(p_{H}-p_{L})\big(\pi^{+}-\pi^{-}\big)\;+\;p_{\pi}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{+}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}\;+\;(1-p_{\pi})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{-}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}\;=\;1,

which is verified by substituting
\pi^{\pm}
from the odds mapping (
\lambda=p_{H}/p_{L}
,
\phi=(1-p_{H})/(1-p_{L})
).

2.2
Visibility reform reduces conservatism at high reputation

Consider a reform that (weakly) raises failure visibility:
\sigma^{\prime}(0,\pi)>\sigma(0,\pi)
and
\sigma^{\prime}(1,\pi)\geq\sigma(1,\pi)
, with the safe branch still uninformative and visibility there unchanged (constant).

Throughout Proposition
6
and Corollary
8
, we assume the safe-branch visibility is constant in outcome and belief,
\sigma_{S}(1,\pi)=\sigma_{S}(0,\pi)=\bar{\sigma}_{S}
, and that the safe option is uninformative (
r_{H}=r_{L}
).

Proposition 6
.

Fix
\pi\in(0,1)
. Under an uninformative safe action and constant safe visibility, the derivative
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)
is
strictly increasing
in
\sigma(0,\pi)
when
V
is locally linear. In particular,

\frac{\partial\,\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)}{\partial\sigma(0,\pi)}\;=\;1-p_{H}\;>\;0,

so raising failure visibility (e.g., via Registered Reports)
weakens
conservatism (moves
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)
toward
0
). More generally, if
V
is smooth, the same conclusion holds whenever the local curvature term is not too negative (a precise bound is given in Appendix
C
).

Proof.

Assume safe is uninformative and
\sigma_{S}(y,\pi)=\bar{\sigma}_{S}
is constant. Differentiating
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)
w.r.t.
\sigma(0,\pi)
yields

\frac{\partial\,\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)}{\partial\sigma(0,\pi)}=-(p_{H}-p_{L})\,V(\pi^{-})\;+\;(1-p_{\pi})\,V^{\prime}(\pi^{-})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{-}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}.

With
V(x)=x
(so
V^{\prime}(x)=1
) and using the odds-map identities for
\pi^{-}
and
\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{-}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}
, the right-hand side simplifies to

\;1-p_{H}\;>\;0.

Thus increasing failure visibility strictly raises
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)
(weakening conservatism). For smooth
V
, a first-order expansion around
\pi
delivers the same sign up to a bounded curvature term (see OA for the bound).
∎

If the reform also increases
\sigma(1,\pi)
, then under linear
V
we likewise obtain
\partial\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)/\partial\sigma(1,\pi)=p_{H}>0
, so the net effect on
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)
remains positive.

When
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)
rises at high
\pi
, the risky cutoff flattens or declines there; hence the risky share rises and the success rate
conditional on risky
falls (selection relaxes). This is exactly the pair of signs predicted for Registered Reports.

Boundary cases
\pi\to 0,1

Lemma 7
.

With fixed
(\lambda,\phi)
, as
\pi\to 1
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{+}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}\to\frac{1}{\lambda},\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{-}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}\to\frac{1}{\phi},\qquad\pi^{+}-\pi=O(1-\pi),\quad\pi-\pi^{-}=O(1-\pi).

As
\pi\to 0
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{+}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}\to\lambda,\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{-}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}\to\phi,\qquad\pi^{+}-\pi=O(\pi),\quad\pi-\pi^{-}=O(\pi).

Corollary 8
.

Under linear
V(x)=x
and uninformative safe, the effect of failure visibility on the local slope is

\frac{\partial\,\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)}{\partial\sigma(0,\pi)}=-(p_{H}-p_{L})\,\pi^{-}+(1-p_{\pi})\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{-}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}.

Hence,

\lim_{\pi\to 1}\frac{\partial\,\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)}{\partial\sigma(0,\pi)}=1-p_{H}>0,\qquad\lim_{\pi\to 0}\frac{\partial\,\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)}{\partial\sigma(0,\pi)}=1-p_{L}>0.

Similarly, for success visibility,

\frac{\partial\,\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)}{\partial\sigma(1,\pi)}=(p_{H}-p_{L})\,\pi^{+}+p_{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{+}}{\mathrm{d}\pi},

so

\lim_{\pi\to 1}\frac{\partial\,\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)}{\partial\sigma(1,\pi)}=p_{H}>0,\qquad\lim_{\pi\to 0}\frac{\partial\,\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)}{\partial\sigma(1,\pi)}=p_{L}>0.

These limits make precise why visibility changes bite uniformly (and strictly) under linear
V
: they weaken conservatism near
\pi\approx 1
and strengthen experimentation near
\pi\approx 0
in the directions predicted by Proposition
6
.

Identification map

Minimal ingredients.

We need (i) an exogenous or staggered shift that increases failure visibility
\sigma(0,\pi)
(RR adoption); (ii) a pre-period reputation measure
\text{Rep}_{i}

Two elasticities that sign the mechanism.

Let

\mathcal{E}_{R}(\pi):=\frac{\mathrm{d}\,\text{RiskyShare}(\pi)}{\mathrm{d}\,\sigma(0,\pi)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{S}(\pi):=\frac{\mathrm{d}\,\text{SuccRisky}(\pi)}{\mathrm{d}\,\sigma(0,\pi)}.

Under the model with uninformative safe (baseline), Proposition

\mathcal{E}_{R}(\pi)\;>\;0,\qquad\mathcal{E}_{S}(\pi)\;<\;0.

In practice, treat

4
General local sign condition

We now give an exact
local
characterization of the cutoff slope. To formalize “local,” embed the risky and safe outcome technologies in one-parameter families of experiments with strength
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
(Blackwell-dilations):

\begin{split}\lambda(\varepsilon)&=\exp(\varepsilon\ell_{1}),\quad\phi(\varepsilon)=\exp(-\varepsilon\ell_{0}),\\
\lambda_{S}(\varepsilon_{S})&=\exp(\varepsilon_{S}\ell^{S}_{1}),\quad\phi_{S}(\varepsilon_{S})=\exp(-\varepsilon_{S}\ell^{S}_{0}),\end{split}

with
\ell_{1},\ell_{0},\ell^{S}_{1},\ell^{S}_{0}>0
fixed. Let
\pi^{\pm}(\varepsilon)
and
\pi_{S}^{\pm}(\varepsilon_{S})
denote the posteriors generated by these experiments and define the posterior
jumps

\Delta\pi_{+}(\pi;\varepsilon):=\pi^{+}(\varepsilon)-\pi,\qquad\Delta\pi_{-}(\pi;\varepsilon):=\pi-\pi^{-}(\varepsilon),

and analogously
\Delta\pi^{S}_{\pm}(\pi;\varepsilon_{S})
for the safe action.

Theorem 9
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
4
, the local slope of the risky cutoff satisfies

\mathrm{sign}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}s^{\star}(\pi)}{\mathrm{d}\pi}\;=\;\mathrm{sign}\big\{-\Psi(\pi)+\Phi(\pi)\big\}.

By the implicit function theorem,
\Delta(s^{\star}(\pi),\pi)=0
implies

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}s^{\star}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}=-\,\tfrac{\partial\Delta/\partial\pi}{\partial\Delta/\partial s}
; under MLRP
\partial\Delta/\partial s>0
, hence
\mathrm{sign}\,(\mathrm{d}s^{\star}/\mathrm{d}\pi)=-\,\mathrm{sign}\,\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)
.

Here

\begin{split}\Psi(\pi)&:=\sigma(1,\pi)\!\left[V^{\prime}(\pi)\big(\pi^{+}-\pi\big)+\tfrac{1}{2}V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)\big(\pi^{+}-\pi\big)^{2}\right]\\
&-\sigma(0,\pi)\!\left[V^{\prime}(\pi)\big(\pi-\pi^{-}\big)+\tfrac{1}{2}V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)\big(\pi-\pi^{-}\big)^{2}\right],\\
\Phi(\pi)&:=\sigma_{S}(1,\pi)\!\left[V^{\prime}(\pi)\big(\pi^{S}_{+}-\pi\big)+\tfrac{1}{2}V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)\big(\pi^{S}_{+}-\pi\big)^{2}\right]\\
&-\sigma_{S}(0,\pi)\!\left[V^{\prime}(\pi)\big(\pi-\pi^{S}_{-}\big)+\tfrac{1}{2}V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)\big(\pi-\pi^{S}_{-}\big)^{2}\right],\end{split}

(7)

with posteriors
\pi^{\pm}
and
\pi^{S}_{\pm}
defined by the risky and safe likelihood ratios. Consequently,
reputational conservatism (nondecreasing cutoff) holds locally iff

\Phi(\pi)\;\geq\;\Psi(\pi).

Robustness and limitations

The results are deliberately minimal. Here we record what (i) strengthens them, (ii) merely re-weights terms in our sign test, and (iii) would overturn conclusions.

•

Agnostic
V
.

We impose only that
V
is increasing; where curvature matters, Theorem
9
keeps
V^{\prime}(\pi)
and
V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)
explicit.

–

With linear
V
we have the exact neutrality in Proposition
5
and the closed-form visibility effects in Proposition
6
.

–

With convex (concave)
V
, the local term
\tfrac{1}{2}V^{\prime\prime}(\pi)\cdot(\text{jump})^{2}
pushes against (toward) conservatism; the sign test absorbs this via the
V^{\prime\prime}
terms inside
\Psi
and
\Phi
.

–

If
V
has kinks (e.g., promotions), replace
V^{\prime}
by subgradients and
V^{\prime\prime}
by second differences; the sign conclusions hold almost everywhere, with the cutoff slope interpreted as a Dini derivative.

•

Informative safe.

Informative safe (i.e.,
\lambda_{S}>1
and/or
\phi_{S}<1
) enters only through
\Phi(\pi)
in Theorem
9
.

–

Uninformative safe:

\Phi(\pi)\equiv 0
, so conservatism hinges purely on risk visibility
\sigma(1,\pi),\sigma(0,\pi)
and
V
.

–

Strongly informative safe:
large
(\lambda_{S},\phi_{S})
can make
\Phi(\pi)
dominate and
overturn
conservatism even if failures are relatively visible under risk. This delivers the cross-field heterogeneity prediction: fields where “safe” is already revealing should exhibit weaker baseline conservatism and smaller effects of visibility reforms.

–

If the
safe
branch enjoys higher visibility than the risky branch (e.g.,
\sigma_{S}(1,\pi)\gg\sigma(1,\pi)
), the safe terms in
\Phi
expand; the sign test remains valid and makes this force explicit.

•

\sigma
depending on reputation
\pi
.

Our main propositions require only the
level
of
\sigma(y,\pi)
at the evaluation point
\pi
. If
\sigma
varies with
\pi
, then
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)
also includes direct terms

\begin{split}\Gamma(\pi)\;:&=\;\sigma_{\pi}(1,\pi)\,p_{\pi}\,V(\pi^{+})+\sigma_{\pi}(0,\pi)\,(1-p_{\pi})\,V(\pi^{-})\\
\;&-\;\sigma_{S,\pi}(1,\pi)\,r_{\pi}\,V(\pi^{S}_{+})-\sigma_{S,\pi}(0,\pi)\,(1-r_{\pi})\,V(\pi^{S}_{-}).\end{split}

(12)

These terms shift the baseline slope but
do not
change the comparative statics in Proposition
6
(which holds pointwise in
\pi
) or the local test in Theorem
9
(which compares weighted jumps). Economically: if failure visibility naturally rises with reputation, that itself weakens conservatism; if it falls, it strengthens it. The reform result is unaffected as long as the reform raises
\sigma(0,\pi)
at the
\pi
of interest.

•

Multiple outcomes / continuous signals.

The binary-success risky technology is for exposition. With multiple outcomes
y\in\mathcal{Y}
(or continuous
y
), replace the two jumps by the outcome-wise changes in posteriors and sum them with weights
\sigma(y,\pi)\Pr(y|\pi,a)
. Under MLRP, Lemma
13
ensures the cutoff remains monotone; Theorem
9
then applies with
\Psi,\Phi
aggregating over
y
.

•

No bonuses/committees.

We intentionally exclude transfers, success bonuses, and voting/pivotality. Adding any of these introduces extra terms that act as
direct
incentives and can either mask or amplify visibility effects. Our contribution is to isolate the visibility channel; results here should be read as the
policy
effect holding other instruments fixed.

•

Finite horizon and dynamics.

The formulation is a one-shot decision with continuation via
V(\pi)
. In a discounted dynamic model,
V
becomes the value function. Our local test is unchanged because it evaluates weighted posterior movements and
V^{\prime},V^{\prime\prime}
at the current belief; what changes are the magnitudes through the curvature of the dynamic
V
. Near terminal periods,
V
flattens and the
relative
weight of visibility rises (failures that would otherwise be dropped cannot be “washed out” by future successes), strengthening Proposition
6
pointwise.

•

Strategic disclosure by the expert.

If the expert can selectively publicize outcomes, that creates an endogenous
\sigma
constrained by the institution (e.g., RR imposes a lower bound on failure survival). Our results are comparative statics in the
institutional

\sigma
; allowing endogenous disclosure inside those constraints leaves Proposition
6
intact (the reform still raises the minimum
\sigma(0,\pi)
).

•

Multiple audiences / markets.

If reputation aggregates across audiences
m
with weights
w_{m}
, the relevant visibility is a convex combination
\bar{\sigma}(y,\pi)=\sum_{m}w_{m}\sigma_{m}(y,\pi)
. The sign test applies to
\bar{\sigma}
; heterogeneity in
\sigma_{m}
simply re-weights
\Psi
and
\Phi
.

•

Heterogeneous technologies.

Types or projects with different
(\lambda,\phi)
induce different jump sizes. Theorem
9
already scales by these; in cross-section, higher informativeness (larger jumps) increases the curvature terms and magnifies whichever visibility branch dominates at that
\pi
.

•

Misspecification / non-Bayesian evaluation.

If evaluators distort evidence (e.g., overweight successes), this is equivalent to replacing
(\lambda,\phi)
or the visibility weights by “perceived” values. The sign test continues to read off the direction from the distorted
\Psi,\Phi
; empirically, this predicts different responses across fields with different evaluation norms.

•

What would overturn the main results.

(i) If the safe option is highly informative
and
markedly more visible than risky outcomes,
\Phi\gg\Psi
can invert several comparative statics (by design, our test will show this).
(ii) If
\sigma(0,\pi)

falls
while
\sigma(1,\pi)
rises in a reform, the net effect on
\Delta^{\prime}(\pi)
can be negative—our Proposition
6
assumes the opposite direction.
(iii) If MLRP fails (e.g., nonmonotone likelihood ratios), the cutoff need not exist; then Section
4
does not apply without modification.

7
Conclusion

This paper isolates a single, portable design lever—
visibility
of realized outcomes—and shows how it governs reputational risk-taking. Two applied results lead: a
no-free-conservatism
result, which rules out generic caution from career concerns when the safe option is uninformative and visibility is symmetric; and a
visibility-reform
result, which proves that increasing the survival of failures weakens high-reputation caution and relaxes selection among risky projects. A general, necessary-and-sufficient local sign test then characterizes when reputational conservatism arises, allowing informative safe actions and remaining agnostic about the curvature of career value.

The design guidance is immediate. Policies that raise the survival/recognition of failures—such as Registered Reports—flatten or reverse high-
\pi
conservatism; shifting visibility only toward successes does the opposite. Heterogeneity across fields follows from how informative the safe option is: where “safe” is already revealing, conservatism should be weaker and the effect of visibility reforms smaller.

Although the paper is purely theoretical, the predictions are directly testable. A simple event-study DiD around staggered RR adoption can read off two signs: high-reputation authors take more risky projects, and success conditional on risky falls. The same blueprint applies to other settings in which visibility is policy-driven (e.g., clinical-trials registries, security-incident disclosure).

Two limitations suggest productive follow-ups. First, we abstract from fully dynamic careers; extending the visibility mechanism to richer dynamics and multi-agent teams is natural and our local sign test provides the right comparative statics. Second, we treat visibility as exogenous; studying its endogenous adoption and welfare trade-offs (including when transparency may backfire) would complete the design picture. Taken together, these results argue that modest, well-aimed changes to
which outcomes persist
can correct reputational conservatism without transfers, committees, or heavy institutional machinery.

Appendix B
Odds-map calculus and derivative identities

Let
O(\pi)=\pi/(1-\pi)
denote odds and
\ell(\pi)=\log O(\pi)
log-odds. For the
risky
technology with success probabilities
p_{H},p_{L}
and likelihood ratios

\lambda:=\frac{p_{H}}{p_{L}}>1,\qquad\phi:=\frac{1-p_{H}}{\,1-p_{L}\,}<1,

posterior odds update
multiplicatively
:

O(\pi^{+})=\lambda\,O(\pi),\qquad O(\pi^{-})=\phi\,O(\pi),

(A.18)

so that
\ell(\pi^{+})=\ell(\pi)+\log\lambda
and
\ell(\pi^{-})=\ell(\pi)+\log\phi
. In levels,

\pi^{+}=\frac{\lambda\,\pi}{1-\pi+\lambda\,\pi},\qquad\pi^{-}=\frac{\phi\,\pi}{1-\pi+\phi\,\pi}.

(A.19)

Analogous formulas hold for the
safe
arm with
(\lambda_{S},\phi_{S})
replacing
(\lambda,\phi)
. In the standard
p_{H},p_{L}
parameterization of the safe arm,
\lambda_{S}=r_{H}/r_{L}
and
\phi_{S}=(1-r_{H})/(1-r_{L})
.

Let the risky success probability be
p_{\pi}:=p_{L}+(p_{H}-p_{L})\pi
and failure probability
1-p_{\pi}
. Then the posterior is two-point:

\pi^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\pi^{+},&\text{w.p. }p_{\pi},\\
\pi^{-},&\text{w.p. }1-p_{\pi}.\end{cases}

Bayes’ law implies the
martingale property

\mathbb{E}[\pi^{\prime}\mid\pi]=\pi
, equivalently

p_{\pi}\,(\pi^{+}-\pi)=(1-p_{\pi})\,(\pi-\pi^{-}).

(A.20)

Define the
jumps

\Delta^{+}(\pi):=\pi^{+}-\pi
and
\Delta^{-}(\pi):=\pi-\pi^{-}
; from (
A.19
),

\Delta^{+}(\pi)=\frac{(\lambda-1)\,\pi(1-\pi)}{1-\pi+\lambda\pi},\qquad\Delta^{-}(\pi)=\frac{(1-\phi)\,\pi(1-\pi)}{1-\pi+\phi\pi}.

(A.21)

For
D_{\lambda}(\pi):=1-\pi+\lambda\pi
and
D_{\phi}(\pi):=1-\pi+\phi\pi
,

\displaystyle\frac{d\pi^{+}}{d\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{D_{\lambda}(\pi)^{2}}\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\pi^{+}}{d\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\,\frac{2\lambda(\lambda-1)}{D_{\lambda}(\pi)^{3}}\;<\;0\quad\text{(concave)},

(A.22)

\displaystyle\frac{d\pi^{-}}{d\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi}{D_{\phi}(\pi)^{2}}\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\pi^{-}}{d\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\,\frac{2\phi(\phi-1)}{D_{\phi}(\pi)^{3}}\;>\;0\quad\text{(convex)}.

(A.23)

Thus the success posterior is concave in the prior; the failure posterior is convex. Differentiating (
A.21
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{d\Delta^{+}}{d\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda-1)}{D_{\lambda}(\pi)^{2}}\Big[(1-2\pi)D_{\lambda}(\pi)-(\lambda-1)\pi(1-\pi)\Big],

(A.24)

\displaystyle\frac{d\Delta^{-}}{d\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\phi)}{D_{\phi}(\pi)^{2}}\Big[(1-2\pi)D_{\phi}(\pi)-(\phi-1)\pi(1-\pi)\Big].

(A.25)

Both jumps satisfy
\Delta^{\pm}(0)=\Delta^{\pm}(1)=0
and are strictly positive on
(0,1)
.

Using (
A.20
), the posterior variance has several equivalent forms:

\mathrm{Var}(\pi^{\prime}\mid\pi)=p_{\pi}(1-p_{\pi})\,(\pi^{+}-\pi^{-})^{2}=p_{\pi}\,(\Delta^{+})^{2}+(1-p_{\pi})\,(\Delta^{-})^{2}=\Delta^{+}(\pi)\,\Delta^{-}(\pi).

(A.26)

The last equality is particularly convenient in Taylor expansions of
\mathbb{E}[V(\pi^{\prime})]-V(\pi)
.

At fixed
\pi
, posteriors and jumps are increasing in informativeness:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\pi^{+}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi(1-\pi)}{D_{\lambda}(\pi)^{2}}\;>\;0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Delta^{+}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi(1-\pi)}{D_{\lambda}(\pi)^{2}}\;>\;0,

(A.27)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\pi^{-}}{\partial\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi(1-\pi)}{D_{\phi}(\pi)^{2}}\;>\;0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Delta^{-}}{\partial\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi(1-\pi)}{D_{\phi}(\pi)^{2}}\;>\;0.

(A.28)

Small-signal linearizations at
\lambda=1
and
\phi=1
are

\pi^{+}\approx\pi+(\lambda-1)\,\pi(1-\pi),\qquad\pi^{-}\approx\pi-(1-\phi)\,\pi(1-\pi),

(A.29)

consistent with (
A.27
)–(
A.28
) since
D_{\lambda}=D_{\phi}=1
at the expansion point.

As
\pi\to 1
,

\frac{d\pi^{+}}{d\pi}\to\frac{1}{\lambda},\quad\frac{d\pi^{-}}{d\pi}\to\frac{1}{\phi},\quad\Delta^{+}\to 0,\quad\Delta^{-}\to 0.

As
\pi\to 0
,

\frac{d\pi^{+}}{d\pi}\to\lambda,\quad\frac{d\pi^{-}}{d\pi}\to\phi,\quad\Delta^{+}\to 0,\quad\Delta^{-}\to 0.

If realized outcomes are
surviving
with probabilities
\sigma(1,\pi)
and
\sigma(0,\pi)
(risky arm) and
\sigma_{S}(1,\pi)
,
\sigma_{S}(0,\pi)
(safe arm), then the value-relevant posterior movements are scaled by visibility:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}^{+}(\pi)

\displaystyle:=\sigma(1,\pi)\,\Delta^{+}(\pi),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}^{-}(\pi)

\displaystyle:=\sigma(0,\pi)\,\Delta^{-}(\pi),

(A.30)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}^{+}_{S}(\pi)

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{S}(1,\pi)\,\Delta^{+}_{S}(\pi),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}^{-}_{S}(\pi)

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{S}(0,\pi)\,\Delta^{-}_{S}(\pi).

(A.31)

These are precisely the objects that enter the local sign test (Theorem
9
) via
\Psi(\pi)
and
\Phi(\pi)
. When
\sigma
is constant in
\pi
at the evaluation point, derivatives w.r.t.
\pi
act only on
\Delta^{\pm}
; if
\sigma_{\pi}\neq 0
, the direct slope term
\Gamma(\pi)
(main text, Eq. (
12
)) adds.

For later use (e.g., in Proposition
6
and boundary corollaries), note:

\displaystyle\pi^{+}-\pi^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda-\phi)\,\pi(1-\pi)}{(1-\pi+\lambda\pi)(1-\pi+\phi\pi)},

(A.32)

\displaystyle p_{\pi}

\displaystyle=p_{L}+(p_{H}-p_{L})\pi,\qquad\frac{dp_{\pi}}{d\pi}=p_{H}-p_{L}>0,

(A.33)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\pi^{\prime}\mid\pi)

\displaystyle=\Delta^{+}\,\Delta^{-}\;=\;\frac{(\lambda-1)(1-\phi)\,[\pi(1-\pi)]^{2}}{D_{\lambda}(\pi)\,D_{\phi}(\pi)}.

(A.34)

Equation (
A.32
) is useful for explicit two-point variance
p_{\pi}(1-p_{\pi})(\pi^{+}-\pi^{-})^{2}
, and (
A.34
) follows from (
A.21
) and (
A.26
).

Lemma 11

(Posterior maps: monotonicity and curvature)
.

For
\lambda>1
and
\phi<1
, the maps
\pi\mapsto\pi^{+}(\pi)
and
\pi\mapsto\pi^{-}(\pi)
defined in (
A.19
) are strictly increasing on
(0,1)
; moreover
\pi^{+}
is concave and
\pi^{-}
is convex on
(0,1)
. Their derivatives satisfy

\frac{d\pi^{+}}{d\pi}=\frac{\lambda}{(1-\pi+\lambda\pi)^{2}}\searrow\text{ from }\lambda\text{ at }\pi\to 0\text{ to }1/\lambda\text{ at }\pi\to 1,

\frac{d\pi^{-}}{d\pi}=\frac{\phi}{(1-\pi+\phi\pi)^{2}}\nearrow\text{ from }\phi\text{ at }\pi\to 0\text{ to }1/\phi\text{ at }\pi\to 1.

Lemma 12

(Two-point posterior variance identities)
.

With
\pi^{\prime}=\pi^{+}
w.p.
p_{\pi}
and
\pi^{-}
w.p.
1-p_{\pi}
, we have the martingale
\mathbb{E}[\pi^{\prime}\mid\pi]=\pi
(equivalently
p_{\pi}(\pi^{+}-\pi)=(1-p_{\pi})(\pi-\pi^{-})
), and

\mathrm{Var}(\pi^{\prime}\mid\pi)=p_{\pi}(1-p_{\pi})(\pi^{+}-\pi^{-})^{2}=p_{\pi}(\pi^{+}-\pi)^{2}+(1-p_{\pi})(\pi-\pi^{-})^{2}=\Delta^{+}(\pi)\,\Delta^{-}(\pi).

Moreover,
\mathrm{Var}(\pi^{\prime}\mid\pi)
is strictly increasing in informativeness:
\partial\mathrm{Var}/\partial\lambda>0
and
\partial\mathrm{Var}/\partial\phi>0
for fixed
\pi\in(0,1)
.

Safe arm.

, yielding
\pi^{S}_{\pm}
,

Abstract
Large-scale problems in data science are often modeled with optimization, and the optimization model is usually solved with first-order methods that may converge at a sublinear rate. Therefore, it is of interest to terminate the optimization algorithm as soon as the underlying data science task is accomplished. We consider FISTA for solving two binary classification problems: the ellipsoid separation problem (ESP), and the soft-margin support-vector machine (SVM). For the ESP, we cast the dual second-order cone program into a form amenable to FISTA and show that the FISTA residual converges to the infimal displacement vector of the primal-dual hybrid gradient (PDHG) algorithm, that directly encodes a separating hyperplane. We further derive a data-dependent iteration upper bound scaling as 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / δ 𝒜 2 ) \mathcal{O}(1/\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}) , where δ 𝒜 \delta_{\mathcal{A}} is the minimal perturbation that destroys separability. For the SVM, we propose a strongly-concave perturbed dual that admits efficient FISTA updates under a linear time projection scheme, and with our parameter choices, the objective has small condition number, enabling rapid convergence. We prove that, under a reasonable data model, early-stopped iterates identify well-classified points and yield a hyperplane that exactly separates them, where the accuracy required of the dual iterate is governed by geometric properties of the data. In particular, the proposed early-stopping criteria diminish the need for hard-to-select tolerance-based stopping conditions. Our numerical experiments on ESP instances derived from MNIST data and on soft-margin SVM benchmarks indicate competitive runtimes and substantial speedups from stopping early.



1
Introduction

Binary classification is a central task in machine learning with numerous applications. Given data separated into two classes, the task
is to construct a decision boundary called a
classifier
that separates the classes completely, or with minimal
misclassifications. Since many binary classification models, including the support vector machine (SVM), can be modeled as convex optimization problems,
first-order methods are a popular choice for training these models, especially in large-scale settings.
Typically, algorithms for binary classification are guaranteed to yield a classifier under the assumption that numerical convergence
of the underlying optimization problem is achieved. In practice, however, high-accuracy optimization is often unnecessary to correctly classify most points:
a hyperplane that correctly classifies “well-separated” or “easy to classify” points may emerge after only a small number of iterations.
One would expect that the ability to obtain such an approximate separator in few iterations depends on the

classifiability
of the data, i.e. the extent to which the data contain points that are robustly separable from the opposite class.
In such cases, the computational effort required to find a useful classifier is not solely determined by worst-case complexity
bounds of the optimization problem, but can instead be characterized in terms of
data-dependent complexity
, where
the number of iterations required to obtain a classifier scales with geometric properties of the dataset rather than purely algorithmic constants.

The work herein investigates data-dependent complexity in two related binary classification tasks. The first is the
ellipsoid separation problem (ESP), in which each class is represented by a finite
family of ellipsoids, and the task is to determine a separating hyperplane that completely separates one class of ellipsoids from the other.
This problem can be seen as an extension of classic binary classification, where the locations of the data
are known only up to an ellipsoid, and a separating hyperplane must be found which separates all possible locations of the data.
Modeling data uncertainty in this way is a form of robust classification, and was considered by Shivaswamy et al. in
[
26
]
.
In the ellipsoid separation setting, we show that the FISTA algorithm
[
3
]
applied to a second-order cone program (SOCP), which
models the dual of the ESP, yields iterates whose residual vectors encode a separating hyperplane. Furthermore,
we prove that the ESP can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(1/\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{2})
iterations, where
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}
represents the smallest
perturbation to the ellipsoid centers, such that the ellipsoids are no longer separable.
The second problem we consider is the soft-margin support vector machine (SVM) for non-separable data introduced in
[
10
]
.
Here, a perfect separator does not exist, but many points may still be classifiable. We propose a perturbed dual formulation
of the soft-margin SVM that is strongly concave and converges quickly in FISTA due to the objective having a condition number

\kappa=L/\mu\leq 513
. We prove that, under a suitable data model, an approximate solution to the perturbed dual can be used
to identify all well-classified points, and furthermore we show how one can construct a separating hyperplane from this
approximate dual solution that can separate the well-classified points exactly. The degree of approximation required of the dual solution
is also itself dependent on the geometry of the data.

For both the ESP and soft-margin SVM, we perform numerical experiments that show considerable speedup can be achieved
using our methods. In particular, our method for approximately solving soft-margin support vector machines performs similarly to LIBSVM
[
9
]
and LIBLINEAR
[
13
]
, and on average outperforms Pegasos
[
25
]
in terms of runtime in seconds and accuracy on select problems from the UCI Machine Learning Dataset
[
18
]
.

Binary classification has been extensively studied in the
contexts of convex optimization and machine learning, most notably through the development of the soft-margin support vector machine by Cortes and Vapnik
[
10
]
.
Various specialized algorithms to solve the SVM have been proposed over time. Osuna, Freund, and Girosi
[
22
]
developed
a decomposition method for the dual SVM, which partitions the quadratic program (QP) into smaller subproblems and makes
training feasible even when the number of support vectors is large. Platt then refined this work in his development of Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO)
[
23
]
, which instead decomposes the QP into
the smallest possible subproblems, allowing them to be solved analytically. Around the same time, Joachims independently developed SVMlight
[
17
]
, a more general decomposition framework than Platt’s. Further refinements to
the decomposition ideas of Osuna et al., Platt, and Joachims were proposed in
[
14
]
, which forms the basis
of the widely used LIBSVM library for solving SVMs
[
9
]
. In parallel, primal-based algorithms were also developed,
most notably Pegasos
[
25
]
, which employs stochastic subgradient descent to solve the primal SVM efficiently on very large-scale datasets. Shortly after, the LIBLINEAR solver was developed specifically for large-scale linear classification
[
13
]
.
Beyond algorithmic advances, there is also a line of research analyzing the
data-dependent complexity of algorithms. These ideas have been investigated recently in
[
12
,
1
,
20
,
21
,
27
]
, but
are related to earlier work done by Freund and Vera
[
15
]
, and Cucker and Peña
[
11
]

on convergence rates for optimization algorithms that depend on the conditioning of the problem instance. However, all these works focus on the optimization
problem itself, without an underlying data science problem such as binary classification.

The rest of this paper is separated into two main sections. The first, Section
2
, covers the ESP, where
in Section
2.1
we model the ESP, and in Section
2.2
we
discuss using FISTA to solve the ESP. Section
2.3
builds on the theory of the Primal-Dual Hybrid Gradient algorithm to show how to obtain a separating hyperplane from the iterates of the FISTA formulation, and Section
2.4
introduces a data-dependent bound on the number of FISTA iterates required to find an approximate separator. The second main section is Section
3
, which
covers using FISTA to approximately solve the soft-margin SVM problem. Section
3.1
discusses how
FISTA can be used to solve the standard SVM problem by considering its dual. In this section it is observed that the problem of projecting onto the constraint
set of the dual SVM is a continuous quadratic knapsack problem, which can be solved in linear time. The section ends by noting that the
dual SVM may have multiple minimizers, motivating Section
3.2
, which introduces a perturbed dual SVM formulation
and subsequently finds its dual to be a perturbed version of the standard primal SVM problem. Relevant theory is also introduced in this section
that will be relied upon later. Sections
3.3
and
3.4
introduce the assumptions and algorithmic parameters we use
to obtain the results of this main section. Section
3.5
details how well-classified points can be identified from an approximate dual solution,
while Section
3.6
contains the main result of Section
3
: how FISTA stopped early can obtain a hyperplane that separates the well-classified
points.
Implementation details and numerical experiments for both the ESP and SVM are provided in Section
4
, before the conclusion of the paper. Details not essential
to the readability of the paper are left to the appendix.

The contributions of this paper are both theoretical and practical. We show that two binary classification problems, namely the ellipsoid separation problem and the
soft-margin support vector machine, can be solved using FISTA. Moreover, we develop data-dependent complexity results for both the ESP and SVM
that show that a good approximate separator can be obtained from the FISTA iterates when stopping early, giving rise to data-dependent stopping conditions that diminish the need for tolerance-based stopping conditions that may be difficult to select. In numerical experiments, our FISTA implementation for the SVM problem is competitive with widely used algorithms for solving SVMs, especially on large-scale problems, making it a candidate for routine use on large-scale instances of SVM.

2
Ellipsoid separation

2.1
Modeling the ellipsoid separation problem

In the ellipsoid separation problem, we are given
j+l
ellipsoids
C_{1},\ldots,C_{j},D_{1},\ldots,D_{l}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
we want to know if there exists a hyperplane
\{\bm{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\bm{w}^{T}\bm{z}=s\}
such that
\bm{w}^{T}\bm{z}<s
for all
\bm{z}\in C_{1}\cup\ldots\cup C_{j}
and

\bm{w}^{T}\bm{z}>s
for all
\bm{z}\in D_{1}\cup\ldots\cup D_{l}
.

Denote the ellipsoids as

\displaystyle C_{i}=\{\bm{z}:\|A_{i}^{-1}(\bm{z}-\bm{c}_{i})\|\leq 1\},i=1,\ldots,j,

\displaystyle D_{i}=\{\bm{z}:\|B_{i}^{-1}(\bm{z}-\bm{d}_{i})\|\leq 1\},i=1,\ldots,l,

where
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
are
d\times d
nonsingular matrices, and
\bm{c}_{i}
and
\bm{d}_{i}
are
d
-vectors that represent the
centers of the ellipsoids.
We make the assumption throughout this section that

\max\left\{\max_{i}\left\{\lVert\bm{c}_{i}\rVert,\lVert A_{i}\rVert\right\},\max_{j}\left\{\lVert\bm{d}_{j}\rVert,\lVert B_{j}\rVert\right\}\right\}=1.

(1)

Let us begin with a lemma that characterizes the containment of an ellipsoid in a halfspace.

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider the ellipsoid
E:=\{\bm{z}:\|A^{-1}(\bm{z}-\bm{c})\|\leq 1\}
, where
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d},\bm{c}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
\bm{w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
. Then
E
lies in a halfspace
H:=\{\bm{z}:\bm{w}^{T}\bm{z}\leq-s\}
if and only if
-s\geq\|A^{T}\bm{w}\|+\bm{c}^{T}\bm{w}
.
Further,
E\subseteq\mathop{\rm int}(H)
if and only if this inequality is strict.

Proof.

See Section
A
of the appendix.
∎

For each
i=1,\ldots,j
, we want the ellipsoids
C_{i}
to be contained in the halfspace defined by
(\bm{w},-s)
, i.e.
\{\bm{z}:\lVert A_{i}^{-1}(\bm{z}-\bm{c}_{i})\rVert\leq 1\}\subseteq\{\bm{z}:\bm{w}^{T}\bm{z}\leq-s\}
. We have shown this is equivalent to
requiring
\lVert A_{i}^{T}\bm{w}\rVert\leq-s-\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}
and the reader may observe that this is equivalent to writing

\begin{pmatrix}s+\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}\\
A_{i}^{T}\bm{w}\end{pmatrix}\in-C_{2}^{d+1},

where
C_{2}^{d+1}
is the second-order cone. Similarly, we wish for all
i=1,\ldots,l
that
\{\bm{z}:\lVert B_{i}^{-1}(\bm{z}-\bm{d}_{i})\rVert\leq 1\}\subseteq\{\bm{z}:\bm{w}^{T}\bm{z}\geq t\}
, or equivalently

\begin{pmatrix}t-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}\\
B_{i}^{T}\bm{w}\end{pmatrix}\in-C_{2}^{d+1},

The problem of finding two disjoint halfspaces parameterized by
(\bm{w},-s)
and
(\bm{w},t)
such that the ellipsoids
C_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,j
are contained in the former halfspace and the ellipsoids
D_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,l
are contained in the latter, can be written as a homogeneous second-order cone program (SOCP):

\begin{array}[]{rl}\max_{s,t,\bm{w}}&s+t\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\mathcal{A}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}s\\
t\\
\bm{w}\end{pmatrix}\preceq_{\mathcal{C}}\bm{0},\end{array}

(2)

where the matrix
\mathcal{A}^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(j+l)(d+1)\times(d+2)}
is

\mathcal{A}^{T}:=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{A}_{1}^{T}\\
\vdots\\[4.0pt]
\bar{A}_{j}^{T}\\[4.0pt]
\bar{B}_{1}^{T}\\
\vdots\\[4.0pt]
\bar{B}_{l}^{T}\end{pmatrix},

where

\bar{A}_{i}^{T}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&A_{i}^{T}\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{B_{i}}^{T}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&B_{i}^{T}\end{pmatrix},

and the notation
\bm{a}\preceq_{\mathcal{C}}\bm{b}
means that
\bm{a}-\bm{b}\in-\mathcal{C}
, where

\mathcal{C}:=\underbrace{C_{2}^{d+1}\times\cdots\times C_{2}^{d+1}}_{(j+l)\>\mathrm{times}}.

Due to homogeneity, the objective does not yield a finite maximum; rather, feasibility with
s+t>0
certifies that the halfspaces are disjoint and a separator exists. Otherwise, the optimum is
0
, certifying that no separating hyperplane exists.

2.2
FISTA for ellipsoid separation

It is not apparent how to apply FISTA
[
3
]
directly to (
2
), since there is no closed-form expression to project
onto the constraint set
\{(s,t,\bm{w}):\mathcal{A}^{T}(s,t,\bm{w})\preceq_{\mathcal{C}}\bm{0}\}
. It turns out that the dual SOCP admits a constraint set that can be efficiently
projected onto. Introduce the dual variables
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{j},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{l}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\bm{p}_{1},\ldots,\bm{p}_{j},\bm{q}_{1},\ldots,\bm{q}_{l}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and define

\bm{x}:=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\bm{p}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{j}\\
\bm{p}_{j}\\
\mu_{1}\\
\bm{q}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{l}\\
\bm{q}_{l}\end{pmatrix}.

The dual SOCP is then

\begin{array}[]{rl}\min_{\bm{x}}&0\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\mathcal{A}\bm{x}=\bm{b}\\
&\bm{x}\succeq_{\mathcal{C}}\bm{0},\end{array}

(3)

where
\bm{b}=(1,1,\bm{0})
. Written explicitly, the constraint
\mathcal{A}\bm{x}=\bm{b}
becomes

\begin{split}&\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{j}=1\\
&\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{l}=1,\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(\lambda_{i}\bm{c}_{i}+A_{i}\bm{p}_{i}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(\mu_{i}\bm{d}_{i}-B_{i}\bm{q}_{i}\right)=\bm{0},\end{split}

(4)

while the constraint
\bm{x}\succeq_{\mathcal{C}}\bm{0}
becomes

\displaystyle\|\bm{p}_{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle\forall i=1,\ldots,j

\displaystyle\|\bm{q}_{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{i}

\displaystyle\forall i=1,\ldots,l.

The above constraints have a nice geometric interpretation: they characterize the points in

\mathop{\rm conv}\left(C_{1}\cup\ldots\cup C_{j}\right)\cap\mathop{\rm conv}\left(D_{1}\cup\ldots\cup D_{l}\right)
, so solving
(
3
) is equivalent to finding a point in the above intersection of convex hulls. Strong
duality holds for (
3
) due to the geometric fact that either a point exists in the above
intersection or there exists a strict separating hyperplane for the two convex hulls. In this context,
strong duality means that either the primal optimizer is
0
and the dual is feasible and attains the value 0
(there is no separating hyperplane), or the primal maximum is
+\infty
and the dual is infeasible
(there is a separating hyperplane).

In order to formulate (
3
) for FISTA, we use the fact that the objective is identically zero to
move the constraint
\mathcal{A}\bm{x}=\bm{b}
to the objective, obtaining the new problem:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\min_{\bm{x}}&f(\bm{x})\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\bm{x}\succeq_{\mathcal{C}}\bm{0},\end{array}

(5)

where
f(\bm{x}):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mathcal{A}\bm{x}-\bm{b}\rVert^{2}
. Our SOCP (
5
) is now in a form FISTA can solve:
\min f(\bm{x})+i_{\mathcal{C}}(\bm{x})
,
where we note that projection onto the second-order cone can be done in linear time (cf.
[
4
, Example 6.37]
).

2.3
From PDHG to FISTA: obtaining a separating hyperplane from the iterates

Primal-dual hybrid gradient (PDHG)
[
8
]
can also be applied to the primal-dual formulation of the ESP formed by (
2
) and (
3
), however its convergence rate in terms of function values is
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
, compared to FISTA which converges at a rate of
\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})
. Despite this, PDHG has rich theory that we make use of throughout Section
2
. For the theoretical results from PDHG related to this section, we refer the reader to Section
A.1
of the appendix. With the results of Section
A.1
, we may prove a theorem connecting the infimal displacement vector of PDHG to the residuals of FISTA when applied to (
5
):

Theorem 2.1
.

Consider the FISTA formulation of the standard conic form problem where
\bm{c}=\bm{0}
, namely
\min\{f(\bm{x}):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mathcal{A}\bm{x}-\bm{b}\rVert^{2}:\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}\}
, and assume that

\mathrm{Null}(\mathcal{A})\cap\mathcal{C}=\{\bm{0}\}
and
\bm{v}=(\bm{v}_{R},\bm{v}_{D})
is the infimal displacement vector of PDHG applied to the ESP. Then the following hold:

(i)

There exists
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\bm{b}-\mathcal{A}\bar{\bm{x}}=\bm{v}_{D}
.

(ii)

\bm{v}_{D}
is the unique minimal residual vector for FISTA, i.e.
\mathcal{S}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}:\bm{b}-\mathcal{A}\bm{x}=\bm{v}_{D}\}

is the set of optimizers for (
5
).

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{Null}(\mathcal{A})\cap\mathcal{C}=\{\bm{0}\}
, we have from Theorem
A.1
that
\bm{v}_{R}=\bm{0}
and
\bm{v}\in\mathrm{Range}(\mathrm{Id}-T)
, which
means that there exists some
(\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{\bm{y}})
such that
\bm{v}=(\mathrm{Id}-T)(\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{\bm{y}})
.
Referring to the PDHG update (
29
), where
\sigma>0
satisfies
\sigma<1/\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert^{2}
and
\tau=1
, we get

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{x}}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{C}}(\bar{\bm{x}}-\sigma\mathcal{A}^{T}\bar{\bm{y}})

(6)

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{D}=\bar{\bm{y}}-\bar{\bm{y}}-\mathcal{A}(2\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{C}}(\bar{\bm{x}}-\sigma\mathcal{A}^{T}\bar{\bm{y}})-\bar{\bm{x}})+\bm{b}.

(7)

From (
6
),
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{C}
, thus (
7
)
reduces to
\bm{b}-\mathcal{A}\bar{\bm{x}}=\bm{v}_{D}
, which proves (i).

To prove (ii), use (i) to find some
\bar{\bm{x}}
such that
\bm{b}-\mathcal{A}\bar{\bm{x}}=\bm{v}_{D}
. Then for any
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{C}
we may write

\displaystyle(\bm{u}-\bar{\bm{x}})^{T}\mathcal{A}^{T}\bm{v}_{D}

\displaystyle=\bm{u}^{T}\mathcal{A}^{T}\bm{v}_{D}-\bar{\bm{x}}^{T}\mathcal{A}^{T}\bm{v}_{D}

\displaystyle\leq-\bm{v}_{D}^{T}\mathcal{A}\bar{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=0,

where the inequality comes from Theorem
A.1
(i) and the fact that
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{C}
, while the final equality comes from
Theorem
A.1
(iii). The inequality
(\bm{u}-\bar{\bm{x}})^{T}\mathcal{A}^{T}\bm{v}_{D}\leq 0
is equivalent to the condition that

\mathcal{A}^{T}\bm{v}_{D}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(\bar{\bm{x}})
, which is itself equivalent to
-\nabla f(\bar{\bm{x}})\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(\bar{\bm{x}})
,
where
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(\bar{\bm{x}})
is the normal cone at the point
\bar{\bm{x}}
. It follows that
\bar{\bm{x}}
is an optimal solution
to
\min\{f(\bm{x}):\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}\}
, since
f
has full domain, is proper and convex, and
\mathcal{C}
has nonempty interior (cf.
[
4
, Theorem 3.67]
).

To complete the proof of (ii), we note that
\frac{1}{2}\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}
is strongly convex, so any optimal solution must have the
same value as
\bm{b}-\mathcal{A}\bar{\bm{x}}
, which we know is precisely
\bm{v}_{D}
. Hence,
\bm{v}_{D}
is the unique minimal residual vector for the FISTA formulation.
∎

Now, let

\bm{v}_{D}=\begin{pmatrix}s^{*}\\
t^{*}\\
\bm{w}^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

From Theorem
A.1
, we have
\mathcal{A}^{T}\bm{v}_{D}\in\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
, so
\mathcal{A}^{T}(s^{*},t^{*},\bm{w}^{*})\preceq_{\mathcal{C}}\bm{0}
and
\bm{v}_{D}
is feasible for (
2
).
Since
\bm{v}_{D}
is the limiting residual vector of FISTA, the following lemma tells us that in the separable case of the ESP,

\bm{v}_{D}
encodes a separating hyperplane.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
16
, Theorem 6.19]
)
.

\bm{v}_{D}\neq\bm{0}\iff s^{*}+t^{*}>0
.

To obtain a separating hyperplane from the FISTA residual
\bm{v}_{D}=(s^{*},t^{*},\bm{w}^{*})
, we simply choose any
s^{\prime}\in(-s^{*},t^{*})
. The corresponding
separating hyperplane is
\{\bm{z}:(\bm{w}^{*})^{T}\bm{z}=s^{\prime}\}
.
It remains to deduce a separator from the residual of the FISTA iterates
\bm{v}_{k}:=\bm{b}-\mathcal{A}\bm{x}_{k}
, and determine after how many iterations

k
is this separator valid.

Recall for
\bm{v}_{D}=(s^{*},t^{*},\bm{w}^{*})
, we have

\displaystyle(\bm{w}^{*})^{T}\bm{z}\leq-s^{*},\quad\forall\bm{z}\in C_{1}\cup\ldots\cup C_{j},

\displaystyle(\bm{w}^{*})^{T}\bm{z}\geq t^{*},\quad\forall\bm{z}\in D_{1}\cup\ldots\cup D_{l}.

Define
\bm{v}_{k}:=(s_{k},t_{k},\bm{w}_{k})
and let
m_{k}:=(-s_{k}+t_{k})/2
so that we have a candidate separator
\{\bm{z}:\bm{w}_{k}^{T}\bm{z}=m_{k}\}
. We wish
to determine when this candidate separates
C_{1}\cup\ldots\cup C_{j}
from
D_{1}\cup\ldots\cup D_{l}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

The residual
\bm{v}_{k}=(s_{k},t_{k},\bm{w}_{k})
defines an approximate separator
\{\bm{z}:\bm{w}_{k}^{T}\bm{z}=m_{k}\}
provided

\lVert\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{D}\rVert<\frac{\lVert\bm{v}_{D}\rVert^{2}}{\sqrt{18}}

Proof.

For any
\bm{z}\in C_{1}\cup\ldots\cup C_{j}
, we have that
(\bm{w}^{*})^{T}\bm{z}\leq-s^{*}
. This inequality holds true if and only if

\bm{w}_{k}^{T}\bm{z}\leq m_{k}+(\bm{w}_{k}-\bm{w}^{*})^{T}\bm{z}-s^{*}-m_{k}.

Let

r:=\lVert\bm{w}_{k}-\bm{w}^{*}\rVert\cdot\lVert\bm{z}\rVert-\frac{s^{*}-s_{k}}{2}+\frac{t^{*}-t_{k}}{2}-\frac{s^{*}+t^{*}}{2}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz, any
\bm{z}\in C_{1}\cup\ldots\cup C_{j}
satisfies

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{k}^{T}\bm{z}

\displaystyle=m_{k}+(\bm{w}_{k}-\bm{w}^{*})^{T}\bm{z}+{\bm{w}^{*}}^{T}\bm{z}-m_{k}

\displaystyle\leq m_{k}+\lVert\bm{w}_{k}-\bm{w}^{*}\rVert\cdot\lVert\bm{z}\rVert-s^{*}-m_{k}

\displaystyle=m_{k}+r.

It follows that if we have
r<0
, then
\bm{w}_{k}^{T}\bm{z}\leq m_{k}
. Under the normalization assumption (
1
),
we have that
\lVert\bm{z}\rVert\leq 2
, and it is easy to verify that
r<0
is implied by the condition

s^{*}+t^{*}>\lvert s^{*}-s_{k}\rvert+\lvert t^{*}-t_{k}\rvert+4\lVert\bm{w}_{k}-\bm{w}^{*}\rVert.

(8)

The implication
\bm{z}\in D_{1}\cup\ldots\cup D_{l}\implies\bm{w}_{k}^{T}\bm{z}>m_{k}
holds under the same assumption.
Therefore, the satisfaction of the condition (
8
) implies that the residual
\bm{v}_{k}
defines
an approximate separator.

Now observe the bound

\lvert s^{*}-s_{k}\rvert+\lvert t^{*}-t_{k}\rvert+4\lVert\bm{w}_{k}-\bm{w}^{*}\rVert\leq\sqrt{18}\lVert\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{D}\rVert

holds, so a new condition for
\bm{v}_{k}
defining an approximate separator is

\lVert\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{D}\rVert\leq\frac{s^{*}+t^{*}}{\sqrt{18}}.

Applying Lemma
A.1
completes the proof.
∎

2.4
Obtaining a data-dependent bound on the number of FISTA iterations

In order to derive a data-dependent bound on the number of FISTA iterations needed to obtain a separating hyperplane, we must relate
\lVert\bm{v}_{D}\rVert
to the geometry of the underlying data. This is done in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

Define
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}
to be the smallest perturbation to the points
\bm{c}_{i}
and
\bm{d}_{i}
such that their
ellipsoids are no longer separable. Then

\delta_{\mathcal{A}}\leq 3\lVert\bm{v}_{D}\rVert.

Proof.

See Section
A.2
of the appendix.
∎

Corollary 2.1
.

The residual
\bm{v}_{k}=\bm{b}-\mathcal{A}\bm{x}_{k}=(s_{k},t_{k},\bm{w}_{k})
defines an approximate separator
\{\bm{z}:\bm{w}_{k}^{T}\bm{z}=m_{k}\}
, where
m_{k}=(-s_{k}+t_{k})/2
, provided

\lVert\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{D}\rVert<\frac{\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}{27\sqrt{2}}.

Proof.

We have shown in Theorem
2.2
that
\bm{v}_{k}
is an approximate separator provided
\lVert\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{D}\rVert<\lVert\bm{v}_{D}\rVert^{2}/\sqrt{18}
.
We have also shown in Theorem
2.3
that we can lower bound
\lVert\bm{v}_{D}\rVert
by the minimum perturbation
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}
that makes the ESP no longer separable:
\lVert\bm{v}_{D}\rVert\geq\delta_{\mathcal{A}}/3
.
Hence, to ensure
\lVert\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{D}\rVert<\lVert\bm{v}_{D}\rVert^{2}/\sqrt{18}
, we require that

\lVert\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{D}\rVert<\frac{\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}{27\sqrt{2}}.

∎

Theorem 2.4
.

FISTA applied to the reformulation of the ESP (
5
) finds an approximate separating hyperplane in at most

\frac{54\sqrt{2}\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert\cdot\lVert\bm{x}_{0}-\bar{\bm{x}}\rVert}{\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}-1

iterations.

Proof.

From Corollary
2.1
, we know that
\bm{v}_{k}=(s_{k},t_{k},\bm{w}_{k})
encodes a separating hyperplane when
\lVert\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{D}\rVert<\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}/27\sqrt{2}
. We want to know
when
\bm{v}_{k}
lies in a ball of radius
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}/27\sqrt{2}
around
\bm{v}_{D}
. Recall (cf.
[
4
, Theorem 10.34]
) that for iterates

\bm{x}_{k}
generated by FISTA and any
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{S}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}:\bm{b}-\mathcal{A}\bm{x}=\bm{v}_{D}\}
,

f(\bm{x}_{k})-f(\bar{\bm{x}})\leq\frac{2L\lVert\bm{x}_{0}-\bar{\bm{x}}\rVert^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}},

where
\bm{x}_{0}
is the starting point of the algorithm and
L
is the Lipschitz parameter of the gradient of
f
. One can verify that

L=\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert^{2}
, so we obtain

f(\bm{x}_{k})-f(\bar{\bm{x}})\leq\frac{2\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert^{2}\lVert\bm{x}_{0}-\bar{\bm{x}}\rVert^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}}.

From Theorem
A.2
, we have
\lVert\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{D}\rVert\leq\sqrt{2}\sqrt{f(\bm{x}_{k})-f(\bar{\bm{x}})}
, so applying this to the above we obtain the bound

\lVert\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{D}\rVert\leq\frac{2\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert\lVert\bm{x}_{0}-\bar{\bm{x}}\rVert}{k+1}.

It follows that we can guarantee that
\bm{v}_{k}
lies in a ball of radius
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}/27\sqrt{2}
around
\bm{v}_{D}
when

2\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert\lVert\bm{x}_{0}-\bar{\bm{x}}\rVert/(k+1)\leq\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}/27\sqrt{2}
. That is, when

k\geq\frac{54\sqrt{2}\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert\lVert\bm{x}_{0}-\bar{\bm{x}}\rVert}{\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}-1.

(9)

∎

The termination test (
28
) used in the numerical experiments for the ESP efficiently checks whether the algorithm has found a separator, ensuring termination when (
9
) is satisfied.

3
Support Vector Machines

The ellipsoid separation problem assumes the data are separable. We now suppose the data are not separable, but we still
look for a hyperplane that separates the points well. This problem can be modeled as a soft-margin support vector machine (SVM).

In this section we assume the data are points rather than ellipsoids. Let the points be
\bm{c}_{1},\ldots,\bm{c}_{j},\bm{d}_{1},\ldots,\bm{d}_{l}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let the (a priori unknown) separating hyperplane be
(\bm{w},t)
, i.e. we want
\bm{w}^{T}\bm{c}_{i}<-t
for
i=1\ldots j
and

\bm{w}^{T}\bm{d}_{i}>-t
for
i=1,\ldots,l
. The soft-margin SVM is modeled as a convex quadratic programming problem:

\begin{array}[]{rll}\min_{\bm{s},t,\bm{w}}&\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}\|^{2}+\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{j+l}s_{i}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t\geq 1-s_{i},&i=1,\ldots,j,\\
&\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t\leq-1+s_{i+j},&i=1,\ldots,l,\\
&\bm{s}\geq\bm{0},\end{array}

(10)

where we say a point
\bm{c}_{i}
or
\bm{d}_{i}
is misclassified if its corresponding entry of
s
is positive, and
\gamma>0
is a hyperparameter that penalizes misclassification.
Observe that (
10
) is bounded below by zero and always feasible, since one may always choose
\bm{s}
to ensure the constraints
are satisfied. In fact, we can rewrite (
10
) as unconstrained nonsmooth convex optimization by letting

\phi(\theta):=\gamma\max(1-\theta,0)
and writing

\min_{\bm{w},t}\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\phi(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t)+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\phi(-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}-t)=:f(\bm{w},t).

(11)

Unlike in the ESP, there is no question of whether a separating hyperplane exists, but finding the optimal
separating hyperplane requires convergence to
(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*})
. To develop the theory for only an approximate solution,
we need to impose a data model. We return to this point in Section
3.3
.

3.1
FISTA for support vector machines

The soft-margin SVM problem (
10
) is a sum of a convex smooth term and several convex nonsmooth terms. Unfortunately, there is no closed-form prox of the nonsmooth terms.
We get around this problem by again passing to the dual, which for (
10
) is the following:

\begin{array}[]{rll}\max_{\bm{u},\bm{v}}&-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{j}u_{i}\bm{c}_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{l}v_{i}\bm{d}_{i}\right\|^{2}+\bm{e}^{T}\bm{u}+\bm{e}^{T}\bm{v}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\bm{e}^{T}\bm{u}=\bm{e}^{T}\bm{v},\\
&\bm{0}\leq\bm{u}\leq\gamma\bm{e},\\
&\bm{0}\leq\bm{v}\leq\gamma\bm{e}.\end{array}

(12)

Let
\Omega:=\{(\bm{u},\bm{v}):\bm{e}^{T}\bm{u}=\bm{e}^{T}\bm{v},\>\bm{0}\leq\bm{u}\leq\gamma\bm{e},\>\bm{0}\leq\bm{v}\leq\gamma\bm{e}\}
.
The projection
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
is the solution to the following optimization problem:

\begin{array}[]{rll}\min_{\bm{u},\bm{v}}&\frac{1}{2}\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{x}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\bm{v}-\bm{y}\rVert^{2}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\bm{e}^{T}\bm{u}=\bm{e}^{T}\bm{v},\\
&\bm{0}\leq\bm{u}\leq\gamma\bm{e},\\
&\bm{0}\leq\bm{v}\leq\gamma\bm{e}.\end{array}

(13)

After dropping constants, (
13
) can be rewritten in the form of a continuous quadratic knapsack problem:

\begin{array}[]{rll}\min_{\bm{r}}&\frac{1}{2}\bm{r}^{T}I\bm{r}-\bm{q}^{T}\bm{r}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\bm{e}^{T}\bm{r}=\bm{0},\\
&\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{r}\leq\bm{\nu},\end{array}

(14)

where
\bm{r}:=(\bm{u},-\bm{v})
,
\bm{q}:=(\bm{x},-\bm{y})
,
\bm{\ell}:=(\bm{0},-\gamma\bm{e})
, and
\bm{\nu}:=(\gamma\bm{e},\bm{0})
. An algorithm for exactly solving
(
14
) in
\mathcal{O}(j+l)
operations was introduced in
[
7
]
, yielding a linear time method for
projecting onto
\Omega
.

3.1.1
Difficulties with identifying well-classified points

Since we have a linear time algorithm to compute
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
, FISTA can be applied efficiently to (
12
).
In fact, the complementarity relation of the KKT (Karush-Kuhn-Tucker) conditions says that an optimal primal-dual pair
for (
10
) and (
12
) must satisfy
s_{i}(\gamma-u_{i})=0
for all
i\in[j]
and

s_{j+i}(\gamma-v_{i})=0
for all
i\in[l]
. Thus, if we have an optimal dual solution with
u_{i}<\gamma
, then
s_{i}=0
and
\bm{c}_{i}
is
correctly classified. The same applies for
\bm{v}
. A preliminary idea for identifying well-classified points is then to run FISTA on
the dual of the soft-margin SVM problem (
12
) until the indices
i
such that
u_{i}<\gamma
or
v_{i}<\gamma
at the optimum
are identified.

The difficulty with this approach is that the dual problem may have multiple minimizers. Furthermore, in some cases there exist minimizers where a well-classified point
\bm{c}_{i}
or
\bm{d}_{i}
satisfies
u_{i}=\gamma
or
v_{i}=\gamma
. For an example of such a case, see Section
B.1
in the Appendix. The convergence of the iterates of FISTA was recently proved in
[
5
]
; however, for convex problems with multiple minimizers, these results do not identify which minimizer FISTA converges to.

3.2
Solving the perturbed dual

To get around the problem of (
12
) having multiple minimizers, our approach is to make the dual (
12
)

\mu
-strongly concave via a perturbation
to the objective:

\begin{array}[]{rrl}&\max&g(\bm{u},\bm{v}):=-\frac{1}{2}\|C^{T}\bm{u}-D^{T}\bm{v}\|^{2}+\bm{e}^{T}\bm{u}+\bm{e}^{T}\bm{v}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|\bm{u}\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|\bm{v}\|^{2}\\
&\mbox{s.t.}&(\bm{u},\bm{v})\in\Omega,\end{array}

(15)

where
C=(\bm{c}_{1}^{T},\ldots,\bm{c}_{j}^{T})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{j\times d}
and
D=(\bm{d}_{1}^{T},\ldots,\bm{d}_{l}^{T})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l\times d}
.
For the remainder of the section, we will focus on solving the formulation (
15
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose all points lie within a fixed distance
R
from the origin. The objective function
g(\bm{u},\bm{v})
defined in (
15
) is
L
-smooth, where
L=\mu+nR^{2}
.

Proof.

The gradient of
g
with respect to
u
is
-C\left(C^{T}\bm{u}-D^{T}\bm{v}\right)+\bm{e}-\mu\bm{u}
, while the gradient of
g
with respect to
\bm{v}
is

D\left(C^{T}\bm{u}-D^{T}\bm{v}\right)+\bm{e}-\mu\bm{v}
. By letting

\bm{z}:=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}&\bm{v}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad H^{T}:=\begin{pmatrix}C^{T}&-D^{T}\end{pmatrix},

we may write

\nabla g(\bm{z})=-HH^{T}\bm{z}+\bm{e}-\mu\bm{z},

and easily obtain the Hessian:

\nabla^{2}g(\bm{z})=-HH^{T}-\mu I.

It follows that

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla^{2}g(\bm{z})\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert HH^{T}+\mu I\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert H\rVert^{2}_{2}+\mu

\displaystyle\leq nR^{2}+\mu,

where in the final inequality we used Cauchy-Schwarz and that the norm of each row of
H
is bounded by
R
, under the assumption
that all data lie within a fixed distance from the origin.
∎

The next theorem gives a bound that allows us to monitor proximity to the optimizer
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})
of the perturbed dual (
15
)
in terms of the projected gradient. Since the projected gradient is computed on each FISTA iteration, this theorem implies the projected gradient step
is a low cost measure of convergence.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\bm{r}:=(\bm{u},\bm{v})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{j}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
and let

(\bm{u}^{+},\bm{v}^{+})=\bm{r}^{+}:=\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}\left(\bm{r}+\tfrac{1}{L}\nabla g(\bm{r})\right).

Then,

\lVert\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*}\rVert\leq\frac{2L}{\mu}\lVert\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+}\rVert,

where
\bm{r}^{*}=(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})
is the optimal solution of (
15
).

Proof.

Since
\bm{r}^{*}
is optimal, there must exist some
\bm{z}^{*}\in\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}(\bm{r}^{*})
with
\bm{z}^{*}=\nabla g(\bm{r}^{*})
.
Also, from Lemma
B.1
, there exists some
\bm{z}^{+}\in\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}(\bm{r}^{+})
such that
\lVert\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})-\bm{z}^{+}\rVert\leq 2L\lVert\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+}\rVert
.
By
\mu
-strong convexity of
-g
,

-\left(\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})-\nabla g(\bm{r}^{*})\right)^{T}(\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*})\geq\mu\lVert\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*}\rVert^{2},

while by the definition of the normal cone,
(\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*})^{T}\bm{z}^{+}\geq 0
and
(\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*})^{T}\bm{z}^{*}\leq 0
.
We may write
\nabla g(\bm{r}^{*})=\bm{z}^{+}+(\bm{z}^{*}-\bm{z}^{+})
, which gives

\displaystyle-\left(\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})-\nabla g(\bm{r}^{*})\right)^{T}(\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*})

\displaystyle=-\left(\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})-\bm{z}^{+}\right)^{T}(\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*})+(\bm{z}^{*}-\bm{z}^{+})^{T}(\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})-\bm{z}^{+}\right)^{T}(\bm{r}^{*}-\bm{r}^{+})

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})-\bm{z}^{+}\rVert\cdot\lVert\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 2L\lVert\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+}\rVert\cdot\lVert\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*}\rVert.

Combining the inequality obtained by strong concavity, and the above, we get

\mu\lVert\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*}\rVert^{2}\leq 2L\lVert\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+}\rVert\cdot\lVert\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*}\rVert,

which under the assumption that
\bm{r}^{+}\neq\bm{r}^{*}
, gives

\lVert\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r}^{*}\rVert\leq\frac{2L}{\mu}\lVert\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+}\rVert.

∎

In order to connect the perturbed dual back to the problem of finding a separating hyperplane, we take the dual of (
15
):

\begin{array}[]{rrl}&\min_{s,t,\bm{w},\bm{z}}&\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}\|^{2}+\gamma\bm{e}^{T}\bm{s}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\bm{z}\|^{2}\\
&\mbox{s.t.}&C\bm{w}+t\bm{e}+\bm{z}_{1}\geq\bm{e}-\bm{s}_{1},\\
&&D\bm{w}+t\bm{e}-\bm{z}_{2}\leq-\bm{e}+\bm{s}_{2},\\
&&\bm{s}\geq\bm{0},\end{array}

(16)

where
\bm{s}_{1}=\bm{s}(1:j),\bm{s}_{2}=\bm{s}(j+1:j+l)
and
\bm{z}_{1}=\bm{z}(1:j),\bm{z}_{2}=\bm{z}(j+1:j+l)
. The KKT conditions relating (
15
) and (
16
) are

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bm{u}=\bm{z}_{1}/\mu\\
&\bm{v}=\bm{z}_{2}/\mu\\
&\bm{w}=C^{T}\bm{u}-D^{T}\bm{v}\\
&\bm{e}^{T}\bm{u}=\bm{e}^{T}\bm{v}\\
&\bm{z}_{1}=(1-t)\bm{e}-\bm{s}_{1}-C\bm{w}+\bm{\zeta}_{1}\\
&\bm{z}_{2}=(1+t)\bm{e}-\bm{s}_{2}+D\bm{w}+\bm{\zeta}_{2}\\
&\bm{s}_{1}^{T}(\bm{u}-\gamma\bm{e})=0\\
&\bm{s}_{2}^{T}(\bm{v}-\gamma\bm{e})=0\\
&\bm{\zeta}_{1}^{T}\bm{u}=0\\
&\bm{\zeta}_{2}^{T}\bm{v}=0\\
&\bm{0}\leq\bm{u}\leq\gamma\bm{e}\\
&\bm{0}\leq\bm{v}\leq\gamma\bm{e}\\
&\bm{s}\geq\bm{0}\\
&\bm{\zeta}\geq\bm{0},\end{split}

(17)

where
\bm{\zeta}_{1}=\bm{\zeta}(1:j)
and
\bm{\zeta}_{2}=\bm{\zeta}(j+1:j+l)
.
If we suppose
\bm{w},t
are known in (
16
), then the entries
of
\bm{s}
and
\bm{z}
separate. That is, if for some
i=1,\ldots,j
we let
\theta=\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t
or for some
i=j+1,\ldots,l
,
\theta=-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}-t
, then the optimal
s_{i},z_{i}
solve the problem

\psi(\theta):=\min\left\{\gamma s+\frac{1}{2\mu}z^{2}:\theta\geq 1-s-z,\ s\geq 0\right\},

(18)

which can be written in closed form as the following differentiable approximation to hinge loss:

\psi(\theta)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}\mu\gamma^{2}+\gamma(1-\theta),&\theta\in(-\infty,1-\mu\gamma],\\
\frac{1}{2\mu}(1-\theta)^{2},&\theta\in[1-\mu\gamma,1],\\
0,&\theta\in[1,+\infty).\end{cases}

(19)

In the context of (
16
),
\theta
can be viewed as the signed margin of a point
\bm{p}_{i}
with corresponding class label
y_{i}
,
where
y_{i}=1
and
\bm{p}_{i}=\bm{c}_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,j
, and
y_{i}=-1
and
\bm{p}_{i}=\bm{d}_{i}
for
i=j+1,\ldots,l
:

\theta=y_{i}(\bm{w}^{T}\bm{p}_{i}+t).

With a closed-form solution to
\psi(\theta)
, we may now write (
16
) as an unconstrained convex optimization problem
which will be helpful for our analysis.

\min_{\bm{w},t}f(\bm{w},t):=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\psi(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t)+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\psi(-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}-t).

(20)

3.3
Assumptions on the data

We will assume throughout the rest of this section that the data form two clusters, each with a center, plus noisy points.
Let
n:=j+l
, and suppose
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in[1/2,1]
and
0<\rho<\min(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
. We assume that there are

j_{0}
“well-classified” points in
B(\sigma_{1}\bm{e}_{1},\rho)
, there are
l_{0}
“well-classified” points in
B(-\sigma_{2}\bm{e}_{1},\rho)
,
and the
n_{\text{noise}}
noisy points are contained in the ball that encloses the two balls of
well-classified
points:

B\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}{2}\bm{e}_{1},\frac{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}}{2}+\rho\right).

Figure 1:
An illustration of the assumption on the data, where we have two balls of well-classified points centered at
-1
and
+1
, and a larger ball containing the two, which also includes the noisy points.

In our model we have
j_{0}+l_{0}+n_{\text{noise}}=n
, where

j_{0}\geq n/3
,
l_{0}\geq n/3
, and
n_{\text{noise}}\leq\nu n
with
\nu>0
. This assumption also implies that all points must lie within
a fixed distance
R:=\max(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})+\rho
from the origin.

Now we have a data model, we can consider two useful questions:

1.

Can well-classified points be identified with an approximate solution?

2.

Can a (suboptimal) separator be identified from an approximate solution that separates the well-classified points?

3.4
Algorithmic parameters

In order to solve (
15
), we must select values for the misclassification penalty
\gamma
and the strong-concavity parameter
\mu
.
We fix
\gamma:=64/n
and
\mu:=n/128
, and observe that this makes
1-\mu\gamma=1/2
, ensuring that the breakpoints of the quadratic portion of

\psi(\theta)
are
\theta\in[1/2,1]
. Hence, if the signed margin
\theta\geq 1
, the corresponding point makes no contribution to the objective, but as the margin decreases to
\theta\in[1/2,1]
, the contribution begins to increase quadratically, before becoming linear when
\theta\leq 1/2
.
This behavior is depicted in Figure
2
.
Our choice of
\gamma
follows the common convention of normalizing the hinge-loss term by the sample size, which ensures that the objective function remains well-scaled as
n
varies. Such normalization appears throughout the literature, for example in
[
24
, Equation (15.4)]
and
[
25
, Equation (1)]
. Another benefit of this choice of parameters is very fast convergence when using the strongly convex variant of FISTA (cf.
[
4
, Chapter 7]
). Indeed
the strongly convex variant of FISTA converges at the rate
\mathcal{O}((1-1/\sqrt{\kappa})^{k})
, where
\kappa=L/\mu
, and
Theorem
3.1
gives
L=\mu+nR^{2}
, so

\kappa=1+128R^{2}\leq 513,

which is sufficiently small to ensure fast convergence of FISTA.

Figure 2:
A plot of
\psi(\theta)
, where
n=100
,
\gamma=64/n,\mu=n/128
.

3.5
Identifying well-classified points

We say that a point is
properly classified
if its optimal value for (
15
) satisfies
u_{i}^{*}<\gamma
or
v_{i}^{*}<\gamma
. The goal of this section is to prove that all well classified points are properly classified by FISTA provided that the distance
\lVert(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert\leq\Delta
, where
\Delta
is a data-dependent quantity defined in (
26
). This result is stated precisely in Theorem
3.5
. The termination test in this theorem depends on parameters that may be unknown a priori when the algorithm is run. In Section
4.2
, we explain how to terminate FISTA in practice even when the parameters are unknown.

As in the unperturbed case, the complementarity relation of the KKT conditions (
17
) says that an optimal primal-dual pair
for (
15
) and (
16
) must satisfy
s_{i}^{*}(\gamma-u_{i}^{*})=0
for all
i\in[j]
and

s_{j+i}^{*}(\gamma-v^{*}_{i})=0
for all
i\in[l]
. This implies that for a properly classified point,
s^{*}_{i}=0
.

The derivative of
\psi(\theta)
for our choices of
\mu,\gamma
is

\psi^{\prime}(\theta)=\begin{cases}-\frac{64}{n},&\theta\in(-\infty,1/2),\\
\frac{128}{n}(\theta-1),&\theta\in[1/2,1],\\
0,&\theta\in(1,+\infty),\end{cases}

(21)

which we use in the following theorem that connects the separator obtained from the primal optimizers
(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*})
to the optimal dual variables.
Here, we define
\bm{\psi}_{c}^{\prime}(\bm{w},t):=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t)\bm{e}_{i}
and
\bm{\psi}_{d}^{\prime}(\bm{w},t):=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\psi^{\prime}(-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}-t)\bm{e}_{i}
, i.e.
\bm{\psi}_{c}^{\prime}(\bm{w},t)

is the vector with entries
\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})
are optimal solutions to the perturbed dual SVM problem (
15
). Then

\bm{u}^{*}=-\bm{\psi}^{\prime}_{c}(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*}),\ \text{and}\ \bm{v}^{*}=-\bm{\psi}^{\prime}_{d}(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*}).

Proof.

We will prove this result for
\bm{u}^{*}
since the
\bm{v}^{*}
case follows the same reasoning.

From the KKT conditions (
17
), we have
u_{i}^{*}=z_{i,1}^{*}/\mu=(1-\theta_{i}^{*}-s_{i,1}^{*}+\zeta^{*}_{i,1})/\mu
, where
\theta_{i}^{*}:=\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}
.

First, consider the case where
\theta_{i}^{*}\geq 1
. In this case,
\psi^{\prime}(\theta_{i}^{*})=0
, and in the dual of the dual (
16
),

z^{*}_{i,1}=0=s_{i,1}^{*}
must hold. It follows that
u_{i}^{*}=0=-\psi^{\prime}(\theta_{i}^{*})
.

The next case is
\theta_{i}^{*}\in(1-\mu\gamma,1)
. Suppose for sake of contradiction that
u_{i}^{*}=0
, then by the complementary slackness
condition
s^{*}_{i,1}(u_{i}-\gamma)=0
,
s^{*}_{i,1}=0
, and
u_{i}^{*}=(1-\theta_{i}^{*}+\zeta_{i,1}^{*})/\mu
. But then

0=u_{i}^{*}=(1-\theta_{i}^{*}+\zeta^{*}_{i,1})/\mu\iff\zeta^{*}_{i,1}=\theta_{i}^{*}-1,

where
\theta_{i}^{*}-1<0
.
This is a contradiction because
\zeta_{i,1}^{*}\geq 0
. So
u_{i}^{*}>0
, which from the other complementary slackness condition
u_{i}^{*}\zeta_{i,1}^{*}=0
, means
\zeta_{i,1}^{*}=0
.
Now suppose that
u_{i}^{*}=\gamma
. This leads to a contradiction also, since

\gamma=u_{i}^{*}=(1-\theta_{i}^{*}-s_{i,1}^{*})/\mu\iff s_{i,1}^{*}=1-\mu\gamma-\theta_{i}^{*},

but
1-\mu\gamma-\theta_{i}^{*}<0
whereas
s_{i,1}^{*}\geq 0
. It follows that
u_{i}^{*}\in(0,\gamma)
, which implies by complementary slackness that
s_{i,1}^{*}=0=\zeta_{i,1}^{*}
, and

u_{i}^{*}=\frac{1}{\mu}(1-\theta_{i}^{*})=-\psi^{\prime}(\theta_{i}^{*}).

Finally, consider the case where
\theta_{i}^{*}\leq 1-\mu\gamma
. Suppose that
u_{i}^{*}<\gamma
. Then
s_{i,1}^{*}=0
, and

\gamma>u_{i}^{*}=(1-\theta_{i}^{*}+\zeta^{*}_{i,1})/\mu\iff\mu\gamma>1-\theta_{i}^{*}+\zeta^{*}_{i,1},

but
1-\theta_{i}^{*}+\xi_{i,1}^{*}\geq\mu\gamma
, which is a contradiction. It follows that

u_{i}^{*}=\gamma=-\psi^{\prime}(\theta^{*}_{i}).

∎

The following lemma says that, for iteration
k
, under the assumption that
\bm{c}_{i}
is properly classified, once
u_{k,i}^{+}:=(\bm{u}_{k}^{+})_{i}
lies in an open ball of radius

\Delta/2
around
u_{i}^{*}
, we can be sure that
u_{k,i}:=(\bm{u}_{k})_{i}
is more than
\Delta/2
away from its upper bound of
\gamma
.
The exact same reasoning can be applied to
v^{+}_{k,i}:=(\bm{v}_{k}^{+})_{i}
for
i\in[l]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
\gamma-u_{i}^{*}=\Delta
for some
\Delta>0
and
i\in[j]
. If
\lvert u_{k,i}^{+}-u_{i}^{*}\rvert<\Delta/2
,
then
\gamma-u_{k,i}^{+}>\Delta/2
. Likewise, if
\gamma-v_{i}^{*}=\Delta
for some
\Delta>0
and
i\in[l]
, then

\gamma-v_{k,i}^{+}>\Delta/2
.

Proof.

Suppose
\gamma-u_{i}^{*}=\Delta
for some
\Delta>0
and
i\in[j]
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{2}>\lvert u_{k,i}^{+}-u_{i}^{*}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert u_{k,i}^{+}-\gamma+\Delta\rvert

\displaystyle\geq-\lvert u_{k,i}^{+}-\gamma\rvert+\lvert\Delta\rvert

\displaystyle=-(\gamma-u_{k,i}^{+})+\Delta.

The same argument applies for any
v_{i}^{*}
.
∎

In fact, the two inequalities above in terms of
u_{k,i}^{+}
can tell us that
u_{i}^{*}
is properly classified.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Delta>0
and
i\in[j]
. If
\lvert u_{k,i}^{+}-u_{i}^{*}\rvert<\Delta/2
and
\gamma-u_{k,i}^{+}>\Delta/2
, then
u_{i}^{*}<\gamma
and
u_{i}^{*}
is properly classified.

Likewise, if
\lvert v_{k,i}^{+}-v_{i}^{*}\rvert<\Delta/2
and
\gamma-v_{k,i}^{+}>\Delta/2
, then
v_{i}^{*}
is properly classified for any
i\in[l]
.

Proof.

Suppose
\lvert u_{k,i}^{+}-u_{i}^{*}\rvert<\Delta/2
and
\gamma-u_{k,i}^{+}>\Delta/2
. Then we may write

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{2}

\displaystyle>\lvert u_{k,i}^{+}-\gamma+\gamma-u_{i}^{*}\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\lvert u_{k,i}^{+}-\gamma\rvert-\lvert\gamma-u_{i}^{*}\rvert

\displaystyle>\frac{\Delta}{2}-\lvert\gamma-u_{i}^{*}\rvert,

which implies
\lvert\gamma-u_{i}^{*}\rvert>0
, so
u_{i}^{*}<\gamma
. The proof for the case of
v_{i}
is identical.
∎

The conditions
\lvert u_{k,i}^{+}-u_{i}^{*}\rvert<\Delta/2
and
\gamma-u_{k,i}^{+}>\Delta/2
can be easily verified in FISTA.
Recall Theorem
3.2
says that the size of the projected gradient step is an upper bound on

\lVert(\bm{u}_{k}^{+},\bm{v}_{k}^{+})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert
, so we may check upon each iteration if the size of the projected gradient
step is less than
\Delta/2
and then verify for which
i
we satisfy
\gamma-u_{k,i}^{+}>\Delta/2
and

\gamma-v_{k,i}^{+}>\Delta/2
to determine the properly classified points.

The auxiliary primal variables
(\bm{s},\bm{z})
are determined by
(\bm{w},t)
as the solutions to (
18
).
In the remainder of this section we characterize
(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*})
with the goal of finding bounds
for
\bm{z}^{*}
and subsequently expressing the optimal dual variables
\bm{u}^{*}
and
\bm{v}^{*}
via the identities

\bm{u}^{*}=\bm{z}_{1}^{*}/\mu
and
\bm{v}^{*}=\bm{z}_{2}^{*}/\mu
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}\geq 1-K
for some
K\in[0,1/2)
, then
u_{i}^{*}\leq 2K\gamma<\gamma
. Likewise,
if
-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}-t^{*}\geq 1-K
for some
K\in[0,1/2)
, then
v_{i}^{*}\leq 2K\gamma<\gamma
.

Proof.

Suppose
K\in[0,1/2)
. From the KKT conditions of (
18
) it can be shown that
z_{i}^{*}=\max(1-\theta,0)
for all
\theta\geq 1/2
,
and in either case of
\theta=\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}\geq 1-K
or
\theta=-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}-t^{*}\geq 1-K
,

\theta\in(1/2,1]
. We may now apply the KKT conditions (
17
) for the perturbed dual formulation (
15
),
in particular the stationarity conditions
u_{i}^{*}=z_{i}^{*}/\mu=128(1-\theta)/n=2K\gamma
or
v_{i}^{*}=z_{i}^{*}/\mu=128(1-\theta)/n=2K\gamma
.
Since
K<1/2
,
2K\gamma<\gamma
.
∎

The above lemma is useful because it implies that if
\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}\geq 1/2+\Omega(1)
, then
u_{i}^{*}\leq\gamma-\Omega(1)
, certifying that

\bm{c}_{i}
is properly classified. The case for
\bm{d}_{i}
is symmetric. We now prove a bound on the norm of
\bm{w}^{*}
in terms of the positive constant
\xi
.
Notably,
\lim_{\rho,\nu\to 0}\xi=(1-\sigma)/\sigma\in[0,1]
, where
\sigma:=\min(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

\lVert\bm{w}^{*}\rVert\leq 1+\xi
, where
\xi:=\sqrt{(1+\delta)^{2}+32\nu\left(11+8\delta\right)}-1
and
\delta:=\frac{1}{\sigma-\rho}-1
.

Proof.

Recall the objective in (
20
):

f(\bm{w},t)=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\psi(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t)+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\psi(-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}-t)

and note that
\bm{w}=(1+\delta)\bm{e}_{1},t=0
is a valid solution, where the definitions
\sigma=\min(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
and
\rho<\sigma
ensure
\delta>0
.
Under this choice of
\delta
, we have for all
\bm{c}_{i}
well-classified:

\displaystyle\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t

\displaystyle=\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}

\displaystyle=(1+\delta)\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{e}_{1}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\delta)(\sigma_{1}-\rho)\bm{e}_{1}^{T}\bm{e}_{1}

\displaystyle=(1+\delta)(\sigma_{1}-\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{1}-\rho}{\sigma-\rho}

\displaystyle\geq 1,

where we used that all well-classified points
\bm{c}_{i}
lie in
B(\sigma_{1}\bm{e}_{1},\rho)
and that
\rho<\min(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
.
The same argument can be made to see that
\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}\leq-1
for all
\bm{d}_{i}
well-classified.
Therefore,
f(\bm{w},t)
for this choice of
\bm{w}
and
t
has contributions only from the term

\frac{1}{2}\lVert\bm{w}\rVert^{2}
and the misclassified points, since
\psi(\theta)=0
for all well-classified points.
For
\bm{c}_{i}
not well-classified, we have that

\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t=\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}\geq-2(1+\delta),

where
-2(1+\delta)<-2
. The quadratic portion of
\psi
is bounded above by

64/n
, while the linear portion corresponding to the interval
(-\infty,1/2)
is always strictly larger than
64/n

for
\theta<-2
. Hence, we may use the linear portion of
\psi
to obtain the upper bound

\psi(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t)\leq\psi(-2(1+\delta))\leq\frac{16}{n}\left(11+8\delta\right).

The argument for
\psi(-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}-t)\leq\frac{16}{n}(11+8\delta)
for
\bm{d}_{i}
not well-classified is similar. Therefore, the sum terms
in (
20
) must be bounded as follows, since
n_{\text{noise}}\leq\nu n
.

\sum_{i=1}^{j}\psi(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}+t)+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\psi(-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}-t)\leq 16\nu\left(11+8\delta\right).

Moreover,
\frac{1}{2}\lVert\bm{w}\rVert^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\delta)^{2}
, so we have

f(\bm{w},t)\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\delta)^{2}+16\nu\left(11+8\delta\right),

and thus

f(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*})\leq f(\bm{w},t)\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\delta)^{2}+16\nu\left(11+8\delta\right).

We may now obtain a bound on
\lVert\bm{w}^{*}\rVert
by noting that
\lVert\bm{w}^{*}\rVert^{2}\leq 2f(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*})
:

\lVert\bm{w}^{*}\rVert\leq\sqrt{f(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*})}\leq\sqrt{(1+\delta)^{2}+32\nu\left(11+8\delta\right)}.

Letting
\xi:=\sqrt{(1+\delta)^{2}+32\nu\left(11+8\delta\right)}-1
, we may rewrite this bound as
\lVert\bm{w}^{*}\rVert\leq 1+\xi
.
∎

For the rest of this section we make the assumption that
\rho,\nu
in our data model are chosen sufficiently small to ensure that

\frac{1+\xi+64\nu}{\sigma-\rho}\leq\frac{64}{3}.

(22)

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,j\}
be the well-classified points in the
\bm{c}
-class, and
L\subseteq\{1,\ldots,l\}
be the well-classified
points in the
\bm{d}
-class. There exists an
i\in J
such that

\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}\geq 1-\frac{3(1+\xi+64\nu)}{128(\sigma_{1}-\rho)}.

Likewise, there exists an
i\in L
such that

-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}-t^{*}\geq 1-\frac{3(1+\xi+64\nu)}{128(\sigma_{2}-\rho)}.

Proof.

We will prove the inequality for the
\bm{c}
-points only, the proof for the
\bm{d}
-points proceeds analogously.

By optimality of
(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*})
, the following derivative must be zero:

\frac{d}{dw_{1}}f(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*})=w_{1}^{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*})c_{1,i}\\
-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\psi^{\prime}(-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}-t)d_{i,1}.

Define

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle:=\max_{i\in J}\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*})c_{i,1},

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle:=\max_{i\in L}(-\psi^{\prime}(-d_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}-t^{*})d_{i,1}).

Note that in the definition of
\psi^{\prime}(\theta)
,
-1/2\leq\theta-1\leq 0
on the interval
\theta\in[1/2,1]
, so
\lvert\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\rvert\leq 64/n
. Moreover,

\lvert c_{i,1}\rvert\leq 2
and
\lvert d_{i,1}\rvert\leq 2
for
\bm{c}_{i}
and
\bm{d}_{i}
not well-classified, allowing us to write
the bound:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dw_{1}}f(\bm{w}^{*},t^{*})

\displaystyle=w_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*})c_{1,i}-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\psi^{\prime}(-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}-t)d_{i,1}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\xi)+\lvert J\rvert q+\lvert L\rvert r+\frac{64n_{\text{noise}}}{n}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\xi)+\lvert J\rvert q+\lvert L\rvert r+64\nu.

Here, the first term comes from Lemma
3.4
, i.e.
w_{1}\leq\lVert\bm{w}^{*}\rVert\leq 1+\xi
, the second and third terms come from the well-classified points, while the last
term comes from the ill-classified points. Rearranging the above inequality we obtain

\lvert J\rvert q+\lvert L\rvert r\geq-1-\xi-64\nu.

Since both terms on the left hand side are nonpositive (because
\psi^{\prime}(\cdot)\leq 0
and
\bm{c}_{i,1}\geq 0
for well-classified
\bm{c}
-points, and

d_{i,1}\leq 0
for well-classified
\bm{d}
-points), we can deduce two separate inequalities:

\displaystyle\lvert J\rvert q\geq-1-\xi-64\nu,

\displaystyle\lvert L\rvert r\geq-1-\xi-64\nu.

The first inequality is rearranged to:

q\geq-\frac{1+\xi+64\nu}{\lvert J\rvert}.

Thus, for
i\in J
giving the maximum in the definition of
q
,

\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*})c_{i,1}\geq-\frac{1+\xi+64\nu}{\lvert J\rvert},

and since
c_{i,1}\geq\sigma_{1}-\rho
, we must have that
\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*})c_{i,1}\leq\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*})(\sigma_{1}-\rho)
, so

\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*})\geq-\frac{1+\xi+64\nu}{\lvert J\rvert(\sigma_{1}-\rho)}.

(23)

By the assumption (
22
) on
\rho,\nu
we can ensure that

\frac{1+\xi+64\nu}{\sigma_{1}-\rho}\leq\frac{64}{3},

in which case the assumption that
\lvert J\rvert\geq n/3
allows us allows us to write

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*})

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{3}{n}\left(\frac{1+\xi+64\nu}{\sigma_{1}-\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{3}{n}\cdot\frac{64}{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{64}{n}.

At the same time, we know that
\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*})\leq 0
,
thus
\psi^{\prime}(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*})\in(-64/n,0]
and we can be sure from the definition of
\psi^{\prime}
in (
21
)
that
\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}\in(1/2,1]
. This allows us to rewrite (
23
) as

\frac{128(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}-1)}{n}\geq-\frac{1+\xi+64\nu}{\lvert J\rvert(\sigma_{1}-\rho)}.

Multiply both sides by
n
, use the assumption on the data that
n/\lvert J\rvert\leq 3
, and divide by
128
to obtain

\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}-1\geq-\frac{3(1+\xi+64\nu)}{128(\sigma_{1}-\rho)}.

(24)

∎

Corollary 3.1
.

For any
i\in J
,

\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}\geq 1-\frac{3(1+\xi+64\nu)}{128(\sigma-\rho)}-3\rho(1+\xi).

Likewise, for any
i\in L

-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}-t^{*}\geq 1-\frac{3(1+\xi+64\nu)}{128(\sigma-\rho)}-3\rho(1+\xi).

Proof.

Again we prove this for
\bm{c}
-points only because the proof for the
\bm{d}
-points is analogous.

Let
i
be some other index in
J
. We may use Lemma
3.4
and (
24
) in Lemma
3.5
to write

\displaystyle\bm{c}_{i^{\prime}}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}-1

\displaystyle=c_{i^{\prime},1}w_{1}^{*}+\bm{c}(2:d)^{T}\bm{w}^{*}(2:d)+t^{*}-1

\displaystyle\geq(\sigma_{1}-\rho)\bm{w}_{1}^{*}-\lVert\bm{c}(2:d)\rVert\cdot\lVert\bm{w}^{*}(2:d)\rVert+t^{*}-1

\displaystyle\geq(\sigma_{1}-\rho)\bm{w}_{1}^{*}-\rho(1+\xi)+t^{*}-1

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{1}+\rho)w_{1}^{*}+t^{*}-1-\rho(1+\xi)-2\rho w_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle\geq c_{i,1}w_{1}^{*}+t^{*}-1-\rho(1+\xi)-2\rho w_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{3(1+\xi+64\nu)}{128(\sigma_{1}-\rho)}-\rho(1+\xi)-2\rho w_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{3(1+\xi+64\nu)}{128(\sigma-\rho)}-3\rho(1+\xi).

∎

Define
K(\rho,\nu):=3(1+\xi+64\nu)/(128(\sigma-\rho))+3\rho(1+\xi)
. Observe that as
\rho,\nu
tend to zero, the right hand side in the inequality

K(\rho,\nu)\to 3/128\sigma^{2}\leq 3/32\leq 1/2-\Omega(1)
. Hence, for each
i\in J
, a sufficiently small choice of
\rho,\nu
will give
\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}\geq 1-K
for

K\leq 1/2-\Omega(1)
, and by Lemma
3.3
,
u_{i}^{*}\leq\gamma-\Omega(1)
. Likewise for
v_{i}^{*}
where
i\in L
.

3.5.1
Identifying the well-classified points in FISTA

By Corollary
3.1
and Lemma
3.3
, for
\rho,\nu
sufficiently small per (
22
), the well-classified points at the optimum
satisfy
u_{i}^{*},v_{i}^{*}\leq 2K(\rho,\nu)\gamma
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose
\rho,\nu
satisfy (
22
), and
K(\rho,\nu)<1/2
. For all
i\in J
,
u_{i}^{*}\leq 2K(\rho,\nu)\gamma

and for all
i\in L
,
v_{i}^{*}\leq 2K(\rho,\nu)\gamma
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Corollary
3.1
and Lemma
3.3
.
∎

If the data are such that
K(\rho,\nu)<1/2
, then
\gamma-u_{i}^{*}\geq(1-2K(\rho,\nu))\gamma:=\Delta_{0}
for all
i\in J
and
\gamma-v_{i}^{*}\geq\Delta_{0}
for all
i\in L
. By Lemma
3.1
,
when
\lVert(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert<\Delta_{0}/2
, it must be the case that
\gamma-u_{k+1,i}>\Delta_{0}/2
for all
i\in J
and
\gamma-v_{k+1,i}>\Delta_{0}/2

for all
i\in L
. But then we are in the case of Lemma
3.2
, i.e.

\lVert(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert<\Delta_{0}/2
, and
\gamma-u_{k+1,i}>\Delta_{0}/2
for all
i\in J
and
\gamma-v_{k+1,i}>\Delta_{0}/2
for all
i\in L
.

In the iterations of FISTA, we don’t have access to
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})
, however we do have the bound
\lVert(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert\leq\frac{2L}{\mu}\lVert(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k})-(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})\rVert
from Theorem
3.2
.
If
\lVert(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k})-(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})\rVert<\Delta_{0}\mu/4L
, we can be sure that
\lVert(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert<\Delta_{0}/2
as desired.
It follows that we can find all the well-classified points in FISTA by checking the condition
\lVert(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k})-(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})\rVert<\Delta_{0}\mu/4L
, and once this condition is
satisfied, finding all points such that
\gamma-u_{k+1,i}>\Delta_{0}/2
and
\gamma-v_{k+1,i}>\Delta_{0}/2
. These points are properly classified
and must include the well-classified points given by the sets
J
and
L
. Since the projected gradient is computed on each FISTA
iteration, it is efficient to compute
\lVert(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k})-(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})\rVert
on each iteration.

3.6
Obtaining a hyperplane that separates the well-classified points

So far we have shown how to determine which points are well-classified from the dual problem (
15
), but it remains
to show how to obtain a hyperplane from an approximate dual solution that separates the well-classified
\bm{c}
-points from the well-classified
\bm{d}
-points.
In this section we make the assumption that
c_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}+t^{*}\geq 1-K
for all
i\in J
and
-d_{i}^{T}\bm{w}^{*}-t^{*}\geq 1-K
for all
i\in L
, where

K:=K(\rho,\nu)\in[0,1/2)
and
\bar{K}\in[1/2,1)
is chosen such that

\bar{K}-3K^{2}>3\rho\left(1+\xi\right)\left(\frac{4}{3}+2\nu\right)+3\nu\left(\frac{9}{4}+2\xi\right)+\frac{1}{2}.

(25)

The above requires that
\rho,\nu
are sufficiently small. Note that the right hand side above tends to
1/2
as
\rho,\nu\to 0
.

Suppose we stop early with
\lVert(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert\leq\Delta
, where

\Delta:=\frac{\frac{1}{3}(\bar{K}-3K^{2})-\rho(1+\xi)\left(\frac{4}{3}+2\nu\right)-\nu\left(\frac{9}{4}+2\xi\right)-\frac{1}{6}}{4\sqrt{2n}\left(1+\frac{2}{3}\rho\right)},

(26)

and we compute
\bm{w}_{k}:=C^{T}\bm{u}_{k}-D^{T}\bm{v}_{k}
. We take
t_{k}
to be the optimal solution to the unconstrained optimization problem

\min_{t}f(\bm{w}_{k},t),

(27)

Since
f(\bm{w}_{k},t)
is the sum of convex functions, it is convex. Moreover,
\psi(\theta)
is Lipschitz continuous.
This allows us to show our main result, that upon stopping early, we obtain a hyperplane from
(\bm{w}_{k},t_{k})
that separates the well-classified points with a margin of
1-\bar{K}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\bar{K}
and
K
be given according to (
25
). Suppose
\lVert(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert\leq\Delta
,
\bm{w}_{k}:=C^{T}\bm{u}_{k}-D^{T}\bm{v}_{k}
, and

4.1
Ellipsoid Separation Problem

The FISTA update for the ESP formulation (
5
) is

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\bm{y}_{k}+\tfrac{1}{L}\mathcal{A}^{T}(\bm{b}-\mathcal{A}\bm{y}_{k})\right)

\displaystyle t_{k+1}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_{k}^{2}}}{2}

\displaystyle\bm{y}_{k+1}=\bm{x}_{k+1}+\left(\tfrac{t_{k}-1}{t_{k+1}}\right)\left(\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{k+1}=\bm{b}-\mathcal{A}\bm{x}_{k+1},

where
\bm{x}_{0}
is the chosen starting point,
\bm{y}_{0}=\bm{x}_{0}
,
t_{0}=1
, and
L=\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert^{2}_{2}
. Since at every iteration we compute

\bm{v}_{k+1}=(s_{k+1},t_{k+1},\bm{w}_{k+1})
, it is only a small amount of extra work at the end of each iteration to check if the hyperplane

\{\bm{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\bm{w}_{k+1}^{T}\bm{z}=m_{k+1}=(-s_{k+1}+t_{k+1})/2\}
separates
C_{1}\cup\ldots\cup C_{j}
from
D_{1}\cup\ldots\cup D_{l}
by verifying
that

\begin{split}&\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k+1}+\lVert A_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k+1}\rVert<m_{k+1},\quad\forall i\in[j]\\
&\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k+1}-\lVert B_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k+1}\rVert>m_{k+1},\quad\forall i\in[l],\\[10.00002pt]
\text{or that}\ &\\[10.00002pt]
&\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k+1}-\lVert A_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k+1}\rVert>m_{k+1},\quad\forall i\in[j]\\
&\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k+1}+\lVert B_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k+1}\rVert<m_{k+1},\quad\forall i\in[l].\end{split}

(28)

By Lemma
2.1
, if either of these conditions succeed,
\{\bm{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\bm{w}_{k+1}^{T}\bm{z}=m_{k+1}\}
is a separating hyperplane. Moreover, one of these conditions is guaranteed to be satisfied once (
9
) is satisfied.

We tested our Julia implementation of FISTA for the ESP by separating ellipsoids derived from the digits
0
and
1
of the MNIST handwritten digits dataset
[
19
]
.
A single digit from the MNIST dataset is a
28\times 28
matrix with entries in
[0,255]
representing pixel intensity, where

255
corresponds to a completely black pixel and
0
corresponds to a completely white pixel. In order to model an MNIST digit
\bm{x}

as an ellipsoid, we first flatten
\bm{x}
into a vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{784}
and normalize the entries to lie in
[0,1]
. An ellipsoid is then constructed using
\bm{x}
as the center:

E_{\bm{x}}=\left\{\bm{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{784}:\Sigma_{\bm{x}}^{-1}(\bm{z}-\bm{x})\right\},

where

\Sigma_{\bm{x}}=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\epsilon\bm{e}+\alpha(\bm{e}-\bm{\theta})),

and
\theta_{i}
represents the “confidence” in a pixel being black or white:

\theta_{i}=\lvert 2x_{i}-1\rvert.

This model of an MNIST digit as an ellipsoid ensures that the ellipsoid stretches more in directions where the pixel
color (black or white) becomes more uncertain, and contracts where confidence in the pixel color increases.

N
digits from each class (

or
1
),

N
from
1
to

Support-Vector Machines

In order to compute an approximate separator for the SVM problem, we develop a stopping condition based on the results of Sections
3.2
and
3.5
. In particular, from Theorem
3.2

\lVert(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert\leq\frac{2L}{\mu}\lVert(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k})-(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})\rVert
, so set

\Delta:=\frac{2L}{\mu}\lVert(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k})-(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})\rVert.

Clearly,
\lVert(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert<\Delta
always holds, so by Lemma
3.2
, if for any
i\in[j]
,
\gamma-u_{k+1,i}>\Delta
, then
u_{i}^{*}
is properly classified. Similarly, if for any
i\in[l]
,
\gamma-v_{k+1,i}>\Delta
, then
v_{i}^{*}
is properly classified. This means that throughout the FISTA iterations, we can keep a list
T
of the properly classified points
i\in n
, and on each iteration check if any more points should be added to
T
. To determine when to stop, we let
p_{k+1}
denote the percentage of points that have been properly classified at iteration
k+1
:

p_{k+1}=\frac{\lvert T\rvert}{n},

and let
\delta_{\min}>0
be some parameter that represents the minimum acceptable percentage change in properly classified points. Initially, we set two variables
p_{\text{best}}=0
and
\texttt{stale}=0
, then at each iteration we verify
p_{k+1}>p_{\text{best}}+\delta_{\min}
. If the inequality is false, we increment
stale
by one, and if
\texttt{stale}\geq 2
, we stop the algorithm because it has stalled. If the inequality is true, we update
p_{\text{best}}
to
p_{k+1}
and reset
stale
to zero.

In all, we perform the following steps to solve the SVM:

1.

Run FISTA on (
15
) with some choice of
\delta_{\min}>0
until it stalls as described above.

2.

Compute

\bm{w}_{k+1}:=C^{T}\bm{u}_{k+1}-D^{T}\bm{v}_{k+1}.

3.

Solve the unconstrained optimization problem

t_{k+1}:=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{t}f(\bm{w}_{k+1},t)

by bisection.

By our choice of stopping condition, our theory guarantees that this method will produce a separator
(\bm{w}_{k+1},t_{k+1})
that separates all points in
T
.

We tested our implementation of this method on binary classification problems of various sizes chosen from the UCI Machine Learning repository
[

Conclusion

We studied when FISTA applied to binary classification problems could be stopped early while still producing a useful classifier. For both the ellipsoid separation problem (ESP)
and soft-margin support-vector machine problem (SVM), we showed how from the iterates of FISTA applied to the underlying optimization problem, we could
extract a separator. Furthermore, we obtained data-dependent complexity results which showed that the effort to obtain a good separator scales with geometric properties of the data,
rather than parameters of the underlying optimization problem.

For the ESP, we reformulated the dual second-order cone program so that we could apply FISTA, and showed that the FISTA
residuals converge to the primal-dual hybrid gradient (PDHG) infimal displacement vector, which itself encodes a separating hyperplane.
We showed that the number of iterations sufficient to produce a separating hyperplane scales as
\mathcal{O}(\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{-2})
, where

\delta_{\mathcal{A}}
is the smallest perturbation that destroys separability of the data. Our numerical experiments showed that as the classes of ellipsoids grew farther apart, the algorithm
needed fewer iterations to find a separator, corroborating our data-dependent complexity theory.

For SVMs, we proposed a strongly concave perturbation of the standard soft-margin SVM dual, which ensures unique minimizers, and with our parameter choices,
converges rapidly. Under a reasonable data model, we show that early-stopped iterates of FISTA applied to the perturbed dual identify
well-classified points and can be used to efficiently compute a hyperplane. We obtain a data-dependent upper bound on the error

\lVert(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k})-(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*})\rVert
, after which the hyperplane computed from the FISTA iterates becomes a separating hyperplane
for the well-classified points. In our experiments, our method for obtaining an approximate separating hyperplane
performs well, and is competitive with LIBSVM and LIBLINEAR while outperforming Pegasos in runtime and accuracy.

Finally, we highlight two directions for future research. Specifically in the realm of binary classification, we selected parameter
\gamma=64/n
because this parameter worked well in both our theoretical developments and computational experiments. However, there could be data sets with either an exceptionally large or exceptionally small number of misclassified points in which another value for
\gamma
might be appropriate. Future research could extend our theory so that
\gamma
is selected in a data dependent manner. Looking beyond binary classification, there are countless problems in data science that are solved by optimization, both convex and nonconvex, and, to the best of our knowledge, few if any have data dependent termination tests. Therefore, much work remains to be done in this arena.

B.2
Solving the perturbed dual

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\bm{r}:=(\bm{u},\bm{v})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{j}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
and let

\bm{r}^{+}:=\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}\left(\bm{r}+\tfrac{1}{L}\nabla g(\bm{r})\right).

Then,

\mathop{\rm dist}\left(\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+}),\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}(\bm{r}^{+})\right)\leq 2L\lVert\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+}\rVert.

Proof.

The problem (
15
) can be rewritten as the unconstrained maximum of the function
G
, where
G:=g(\bm{u},\bm{v})-i_{\Omega}(\bm{u},\bm{v})
,
which is equivalent to the convex problem
\min\{-G(\bm{r}):\bm{r}\in\Omega\}
.
The projection of
\bm{r}+\frac{1}{L}\nabla g(\bm{r})
is the minimizer of

h(\bm{z})=-g(\bm{r})-\nabla g(\bm{r})^{T}(\bm{z}-\bm{r})+\tfrac{L}{2}\lVert\bm{z}-\bm{r}\rVert^{2}+i_{\Omega}(\bm{z}),

hence
\bm{z}=\bm{r}^{+}
must satisfy the first-order optimality condition
\bm{0}\in\partial h(\bm{r}^{+})
, or equivalently

\bm{0}\in-\nabla g(\bm{r})+L(\bm{r}^{+}-\bm{r})+\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}(\bm{r}^{+}).

Hence, the optimality conditions are
L(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+})+\nabla g(\bm{r})\in\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}(\bm{r}^{+})
. If we add
-\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})
to both
sides we obtain

L(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+})+\nabla g(\bm{r})-\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})\in-\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})+\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}(\bm{r}^{+})=\partial\left(-G(\bm{r}^{+})\right),

where the final equality uses that the subdifferential sum rule holds since
g
is proper and convex with full domain,
and
\Omega
has nonempty interior.
Since
L(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+})+\nabla g(\bm{r})-\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})
is contained in
\partial\left(-G(\bm{r}^{+})\right)
, it gives an upper bound on
\mathop{\rm dist}(\bm{0},\partial\left(-G(\bm{r}^{+})\right))
:

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm dist}(\bm{0},\partial\left(-G(\bm{r}^{+})\right))

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert L(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+})+\nabla g(\bm{r})-\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq L\left\lVert\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\nabla g(\bm{r})-\nabla g(\bm{r}^{+})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 2L\left\lVert\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{+}\right\rVert,

where we used the triangle inequality and
L
-smoothness of
g
.
∎

\displaystyle j_{0}\psi(\bm{c}_{i_{c}}^{T}\bm{w}_{k}+t_{k})

\displaystyle\leq n_{0}\gamma K^{2}+\nu n\left(\frac{7}{4}+2\rho+K+\xi(2+2\rho)\right)\gamma+j_{0}\gamma 2\rho(1+\xi)+\frac{n}{3}\left(\bar{K}-3K^{2}\right)\gamma

\displaystyle\ \ \ -n\rho(1+\xi)\left(\frac{4}{3}+2\nu\right)\gamma-\nu n\left(\frac{9}{4}+2\xi\right)\gamma-\frac{n}{6}\gamma.

We can bound
\frac{n}{3}(\bar{K}-3K^{2})
:

\frac{n}{3}(\bar{K}-3K^{2})\leq\frac{n}{3}(\bar{K}-K^{2})-\frac{2n}{3}K^{2}\leq j_{0}(\bar{K}-K^{2})-l_{0}K^{2}=j_{0}\bar{K}-n_{0}K^{2},

and also

-\frac{n}{6}=-\frac{2n/3}{4n}n\leq-\frac{j_{0}}{4n}n=-\frac{j_{0}}{4},\quad\text{and}\quad 2j_{0}\rho(1+\xi)\leq\frac{4n\rho}{3}(1+\xi).

So

\displaystyle j_{0}\psi(\bm{c}_{i_{c}}^{T}\bm{w}_{k}+t_{k})

\displaystyle\leq j_{0}\gamma\bar{K}+\nu n\left(\frac{7}{4}+2\rho+K+\xi(2+2\rho)\right)\gamma+\gamma\frac{4n\rho}{3}(1+\xi)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -n\rho(1+\xi)\left(\frac{4}{3}+2\nu\right)\gamma-\nu n\left(\frac{9}{4}+2\xi\right)\gamma-\frac{j_{0}}{4}\gamma

\displaystyle\leq j_{0}\gamma\bar{K}+\nu n\left(\frac{9}{4}+2\rho(1+\xi)+2\xi\right)\gamma+\gamma\frac{4n\rho}{3}(1+\xi)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -n\rho(1+\xi)\left(\frac{4}{3}+2\nu\right)\gamma-\nu n\left(\frac{9}{4}+2\xi\right)\gamma-\frac{j_{0}}{4}\gamma

\displaystyle=j_{0}\left(\bar{K}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\gamma,

where in the second inequality we used the assumption that
K<1/2
.
We have shown that
\psi(\bm{c}_{i_{c}}^{T}\bm{w}_{k}+t_{k})\leq\left(\bar{K}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\gamma
. Since,
i_{c}

was chosen arbitrarily, it follows that for any well-classified
\bm{c}_{i}

\psi(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k}+t_{k})\leq\gamma\left(\bar{K}-\frac{1}{4}\right)=\gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}-\left(1-\bar{K}\right)\right).

By our choice of
\gamma
and
\mu
, for
\theta\leq\frac{1}{2}

\psi(\theta)=\gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}-\theta\right).

Moreover, since
\bar{K}\in[1/2,1)
, we have
1-\bar{K}\in(0,1/2]
, and

\psi(1-\bar{K})=\gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}-(1-\bar{K})\right)\geq\psi(\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k}+t_{k}).

We know
\psi
is decreasing, so the only way this can happen is if
\bm{c}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k}+t_{k}\geq 1-\bar{K}
. Identical steps show that for any well-classified
\bm{d}_{i}
, we have that
-\bm{d}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{k}-t_{k}\geq 1-\bar{K}
.
∎

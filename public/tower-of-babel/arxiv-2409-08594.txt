Well-posedness and linearization for a semilinear wave equation with spatially growing nonlinearity
Dhouha Draouil and Mohamed Majdoub

Abstract
. We study the initial value problem for a defocusing semi-linear wave equation with spatially growing nonlinearity. By employing Moser-Trudinger-type inequalities and Strichartz estimates, we establish global well-posedness in the energy space for radially symmetric initial data. Furthermore, we derive the linearization of energy-bounded solutions using the methodology introduced in [ 13 ] . The main challenge in our analysis arises from the spatial growth of the nonlinearity at infinity, which prevents the direct application of Sobolev embeddings or Hardy inequalities to control the potential energy. The main novelty of this work lies in overcoming this challenge within the radial framework through the combined application of the Strauss inequality and Strichartz estimates.

1.
Introduction and main results

This paper is concerned with the Cauchy problem associated to the following semi-linear
wave equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta_{x}u+\mathbf{m}\,u=|x|^{b}f(u),

where

u:=u(t,x)
is a real-valued function of

(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
\mathbf{m}\geq 0
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
, and
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function.
We assume that the initial data

(1.2)

(u_{0},u_{1}):=(u(0),\partial_{t}u(0))

are radially symmetric and belong to the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Equation (
1.1
) has the conserved energy

(1.3)

\begin{split}E(u,t)&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\,u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathbf{m}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\\
&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|x|^{b}\,F(u(t,x))\,dx,\end{split}

where
F(u)=\int_{0}^{u}\,f(v)\,dv.

The nonlinear wave equation is a fundamental equation in mathematical physics that describes the behavior of waves in certain physical systems. It has been the subject of extensive study and research in the past several decades.

For this equation, the term
defocusing
refers to the behavior of the waves, indicating that they tend to spread out and disperse rather than concentrating or focusing at specific points. This is in contrast to the
focusing
case, where waves tend to concentrate and exhibit a more localized behavior.

The equation is classified as a semi-linear wave equation when it involves a linear wave operator combined with a non-linear term. The linear wave operator captures the wave propagation behavior, while the non-linear term introduces non-linear interactions that can arise in various physical phenomena.

Before proceeding further, let us recall some historical context regarding this problem. We start by considering the defocusing semi-linear wave equation in
N\geq 3
spatial dimensions, expressed as

(1.4)

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta_{x}u+|u|^{p-1}u=0,\quad p>1.

The well-posedness of (
1.4
) within the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}
has been extensively explored, as documented in various works such as
[
16
,
17
,
20
,
28
,
40
,
41
]
. It is established that the Cauchy problem associated with (
1.4
) is locally well-posed in the standard Sobolev space
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
under the condition
s>\frac{N}{2}
, or when
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<\frac{N}{2}
and
p\leq 1+\frac{4}{N-2s}
. Furthermore, if
p=1+\frac{4}{N-2s}
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<\frac{N}{2}
, there are global
H^{s}
solutions for small Cauchy data
[
35
]
.

The global solvability within the energy space has a rich historical background. A pivotal value of the power
p
emerges, denoted as
2_{N}^{*}:=\frac{N+2}{N-2}
, leading to three main scenarios. In the subcritical case
(p<2_{N}^{*})
, Ginibre and Velo established in
[

For the critical case

[

[
19

in the general case, and subsequently by Shatah-Struwe
[
41
]
in various dimensions.

Regarding the supercritical case
(p>2_{N}^{*})
, the question remains unresolved except for some partial results (refer to
[
29
,
30
]

[
8

that (

\delta_{0}>0

In two spatial dimensions, any polynomial nonlinearity is regarded as subcritical with respect to the
H^{1}
norm. Therefore, it is reasonable to explore an exponential nonlinearity. Notably, Nakamura and Ozawa
[
35

established global well-posedness and scattering for small Cauchy data in any spatial dimension
N\geq 2
. Subsequently, Atallah Baraket
[
2

demonstrated a local existence result for the equation

(

)

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta_{x}u+u{\rm e}^{\alpha u^{2}}=0,

for

(0,u_{1})
with compact support.
In the subsequent works
[

(E_{4\pi})
-(

It is critical if

It is noteworthy to emphasize that the supercritical case was thoroughly investigated in

,

]

For nonlinearities with weaker growth than

We now return to the equation (
1.1
). For the case where

,

, and

We note that equation (
1.1
) can be interpreted as the relativistic counterpart of the inhomogeneous Schrödinger equation:

(1.5)

{\rm i}\partial_{t}u+\Delta_{x}u=|x|^{b}f(u).

In the framework of quantum mechanics, the Klein-Gordon equation is regarded as the relativistic version of the Schrödinger equation, as it extends the principles of quantum mechanics to incorporate relativistic effects. While the Schrödinger equation is confined to non-relativistic systems, the Klein-Gordon equation offers a consistent description of relativistic quantum systems, particularly for spin
-0
particles. For a more comprehensive insights of this topic, see
[
6
,
18
]

Equation (
1.5
) models the propagation of laser beams in specific plasma media and nonlinear optical systems. For further details, we refer the reader to
[
31
,
42
]
and the references therein.

The primary challenge in analyzing (
1.1
) arises from the spatial growth of
|x|^{b}
at infinity for
b>0
, which hinders the straightforward application of Sobolev embedding or Hardy inequality to control the potential energy for energy-solutions. The key innovation of this work lies in addressing this issue within the radial framework by making use of the Strauss inequality in combination with Strichartz estimates.

We are interested in strong solutions of (
1.1
) for
b>0
in dimensions

N=2
and
N=3
. Specifically, for the three-dimensional we assume
\mathbf{m}=0
, with the nonlinearity
f
given by

(1.6)

f(u)=-|u|^{p-1}u\;\;(p>1).

In the two-dimensional case, we assume
\mathbf{m}=1
, and

(1.7)

f(u)=-\left({\rm e}^{u}-1-u\right).

Note that the energy defined in (
1.3
) takes the form

E(u,t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{p+1}\int|x|^{b}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\,dx,

when the nonlinearity is given as in (
1.6
). In contrast, for the nonlinearity defined in (
1.7
), the energy becomes

\begin{split}E(u,t)&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\\
&+\int|x|^{b}\left(e^{u(t,x)}-1-u(t,x)-\frac{u^{2}(t,x)}{2}\right)dx.\end{split}

Equation (
1.1
) with
f
as specified in (
1.6
) exhibits a scaling invariance. Specifically, if
u
solves (
1.1
), then
u_{\lambda}
also does, where

u_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda^{\frac{2+b}{p-1}}u(\lambda t,\lambda x),\quad\lambda>0.

A straightforward calculation yields

\|u_{\lambda}(0)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\lambda^{s+\frac{2+b}{p-1}-\frac{N}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

indicating that the critical regularity space is
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
, where
s_{c}:=\frac{N}{2}-\frac{2+b}{p-1}.

The case
s_{c}=1
corresponds to the energy-critical nonlinearity
p=\frac{N+2+2b}{N-2}
.

Our primary objective is to establish global well-posedness in the energy space, without imposing any restrictions on the size of the Cauchy data, in the defocusing case. In two dimensions, a key advantage arises from the fact that the nonlinearity is comparatively weaker than
{\rm e}^{\alpha|u|^{2}}
for any
\alpha>0
. As a result, the energy estimate and a set of technical tools are adequate to complete the proof.

However, in dimensions three (or higher), we will need to assume that the nonlinearity is sub-critical in a specific manner, which will be precisely defined later. This assumption is somehow technical to address the heightened complexity arising from the nonlinearity.

In the second part of this work, our aim is to explore the concept of
linearizability
for (
1.1
) by showing, under appropriate assumptions, that the nonlinear term
f(u)
does not induce extra oscillations or energy concentrations. For a rigorous formulation of this concept, refer to Definition
1.1
below.

The study of linearization primarily originated from the investigation of equations featuring a polynomial-type nonlinearity in three spatial dimensions, as exemplified by equation (
1.4
). Key references in this field include
[
4
,
5
,
13
,
32
,
34
]
. These studies have made substantial contributions to our comprehension of linearization techniques and have set the groundwork for subsequent advancements in the field.

To provide a concrete example of the concept of
linearizability
, we examine the linear wave equation associated with (
1.4
):

(1.8)

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta_{x}u=0.

Let
(\varphi_{n},\psi_{n})
be a bounded sequence in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that

(1.9)

(\varphi_{n},\psi_{n})\rightharpoonup(0,0)\quad\hbox{in}\quad H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),

(1.10)

\exists\;{R_{0}}>0,\quad\forall\;n,\quad\hbox{supp}(\varphi_{n})\cup\hbox{supp}(\psi_{n})\subset\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\quad|x|\leq{R_{0}}\}.

We denote by
(u_{n})
, (respectively
(v_{n})
) the sequence of
finite energy
solutions to (
1.4
)), (respectively (
1.8
)) satisfying

(u_{n}(0),\partial_{t}{u_{n}}(0))=(v_{n}(0),\partial_{t}{v_{n}}(0))=(\varphi_{n},\psi_{n}).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
T
a positive real number. We say that the sequence
(u_{n})

is linearizable on
[0,T]
if

(1.11)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\|\partial_{t}(u_{n}-v_{n})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla(u_{n}-v_{n})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

In the work by Gérard
[
13
]
, it was demonstrated that in the subcritical case (
p<5
), the sequence
(u_{n})
exhibits linearizability within every time bounded interval. However, in the critical case (
p=5
), the behavior of the solution can manifest nonlinearity, primarily attributable to the lack of compactness in the Sobolev embedding
{\dot{H}}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
In the aforementioned case, Gérard established the linearization under the following necessary and sufficient condition:

\|v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

A similar result was proved by Ibrahim and Majdoub in

[
22
]
for the equation

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\mbox{div}\left(A(x)\nabla_{x}u\right)+|u|^{p-1}u=0\quad\quad\mbox{in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{N},

where
N\geq 3,1<p\leq 2_{N}^{*}
and
A
is a regular function with values in the space of positive definite matrices of size
N\times N
, which is the identity outside a compact set of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We also refer to
[
34
]
for more general nonlinearities.

Theorem 1.1

2.
Useful tools & Auxiliary results

In this section, we will introduce some tools and auxiliary results crucial for establishing our main results. To estimate the nonlinear term in 2D, we will utilize the following Moser-Trudinger inequalities.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
10
]

Let
0\leq\beta<2
and
0<\alpha<2\pi(2-\beta)
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta)
such that

(2.17)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{{\rm e}^{\alpha|u(x)|^{2}}-1}{|x|^{\beta}}dx\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx,

for all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leqslant 1
.

We point out that
\alpha=2\pi(2-\beta)
becomes admissible in
(
2.17
) if we require
\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq 1
instead of

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq 1
, where

\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}=\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

More precisely, we have

Theorem 2.2
.

[
44
]

Let
0\leq\beta<2
. We have

(2.18)

\mathcal{A}_{\beta}:=\sup_{\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leqslant 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{{\rm e}^{2\pi(2-\beta)|u(x)|^{2}}-1}{|x|^{\beta}}dx<\infty.

Moreover, the inequality (
2.18
) is sharp in the sense that for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\sup_{\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leqslant 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{{\rm e}^{(2\pi(2-\beta)+\epsilon)|u(x)|^{2}}-1}{|x|^{\beta}}dx=\infty.

Remarks 2.1
.

(i)

The case
\beta=0
has been previously examined in
[
1
]
. Therefore, inequality (
2.17
) can be regarded as an extension of
[
1
, Theorem 0.1]
, particularly in the context of incorporating a singular weight, that is
\beta>0
.

(ii)

Inequality (
2.18
) was previously established in
[
36
]
for the case
\beta=0
.

For a function
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with subcritical growth at infinity in 2D and appropriate behavior near zero, it can be shown that

|x|^{b}F(u)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
whenever

u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is radially symmetric. More specifically:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function that satisfies the following conditions:

(2.19)

|F(u)|\underset{u\to 0}{\sim}|u|^{\kappa},\quad\kappa>2,

(2.20)

\lim_{|u|\to\infty}|F(u)|\,{\rm e}^{-\alpha u^{2}}=0,\quad\forall\,\,\alpha>0.

Then, for any radially symmetric
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have
|x|^{b}F(u)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
provided that
0\leq b\leq\kappa/2-1
.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Consider a radially symmetric function
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. By expressing
|x|^{b}|F(u)|=\left(|x|^{1/2}|u|\right)^{2b}\,|u|^{-2b}|F(u)|
and applying the radial Sobolev inequality (
2.26
), it is sufficient to prove that
|u|^{-2b}|F(u)|\in L^{1}
. Using conditions (
2.19
)-(
2.20
), we observe that for any
\alpha>0
, the following holds:

|u|^{-2b}|F(u)|\lesssim_{\alpha}\,|u|^{\kappa-2b}+\left({\rm e}^{\alpha u^{2}}-1\right).

Given that
\kappa-2b\geq 2
and
{\rm e}^{\alpha u^{2}}-1\in L^{1}
(see
[
26
, Lemma 3.5]
), the proof is thus complete.
∎

By following the argument outlined in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we can easily derive the result stated below.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<b\leq 1
and
\alpha>0
. There exists a positive constant
C
such that, for any radially symmetric function
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have

(2.21)

\left||x|^{b}({\rm e}^{u}-1-u)\right|\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2b}\left(|u|^{2(1-b)}+\left({\rm e}^{\alpha u^{2}}-1\right)\right).

Proof.

Observe that

\left||x|^{b}({\rm e}^{u}-1-u)\right|=\left(|x|^{1/2}|u|\right)^{2b}|u|^{-2b}\left|{\rm e}^{u}-1-u\right|.

Since
|u|^{-2b}\left|{\rm e}^{u}-1-u\right|\,\underset{u\to 0}{\sim}\,\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2(1-b)}
and, for
|u|\geq 1
,

|u|^{-2b}\left|{\rm e}^{u}-1-u\right|\leq\left|{\rm e}^{u}-1-u\right|\lesssim_{\alpha}{\rm e}^{\alpha u^{2}}-1\quad\text{for any }\alpha>0,

the desired estimate (
2.21
) follows by applying (
2.26
) below.
∎

The above inequalities will be often combined with the following 2D energy estimate.

Proposition 2.5
.

(
Energy Estimate
)

There exists a constant
C_{0}
such that, for any
T>0
and any
u\in C([0,T];H^{1})\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2})
, the following is true:

(2.22)

\|u\|_{T}\leq C_{0}\,\Big[\|u(0)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\partial_{t}u(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Box u+u\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}\Big],

where

(2.23)

\|u\|_{T}:=\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\Big).

We will also review the following Strichartz estimates. To do this, we introduce the concept of admissible pairs in three dimensions (
N=3
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N=3
. A pair
(q,r)
is said to be
H^{1}
wave-admissible if

(2.24)

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{3}{r}=\frac{1}{2},\;\;2<q\leq\infty,\;\;6\leq r<\infty.

For example,
(\infty,6)
,
(5,10)
,
(4,12)
, and
(8,8)
are admissible pairs.

Proposition 2.6

(Strichartz Estimates)
.

Consider
N=3
, and let
(q,r)
be
H^{1}
wave-admissible as defined in (
2.24
). For
T>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(q)>0
such that, for any
v\in C([0,T];H^{1})\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2})
, the following holds:

(2.25)

\|v\|_{L^{q}([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\leq C\left(\|\nabla v(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}v(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Box v\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}([0,T];L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\right).

Estimating the nonlinear term in

presents a significant challenge due to the weight
|x|^{b}
. This difficulty can be addressed by using the following radial Sobolev inequality.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
45
]
)
.

For
N\geq 2
and any radially symmetric function

, the following holds:

(2.26)

\displaystyle|x|^{\frac{N-1}{2}}|\phi(x)|\leq C\|\nabla\phi\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}\|\phi\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}.

We also recall the following sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.

Lemma 2.8
.

Given
N\geq 2
,
\theta>0
,
\lambda\geq 2+\frac{2\theta}{N-1}
, and
\lambda\leq\frac{2N+2\theta}{N-2}
for

, the sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality stated below holds:

(2.27)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|x|^{\theta}\,|\varphi(x)|^{\lambda}\,dx\leq K_{opt}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{A}\,\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{B},

where
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is radially symmetric and

A=2+\theta-\frac{N-2}{2}\lambda,\quad B=\frac{N}{2}(\lambda-2)-\theta.

Remark 2.1
.

The proof of Lemma
2.8
can be found in several references, such as
[
11
,
12
]
. In particular, it has been established that the upper bound

is optimal for

is also optimal for the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (

).

By utilizing Lemma

, we derive a crucial estimation that will aid in our analysis of well-posedness in three spatial dimensions.

Lemma 2.9
.

Given
N=3
,
b>0
,
T>0
,
3+b<p<4+b
,
u\in L^{\infty}_{T}(H^{1})
, and
v\in L^{q}_{T}(L^{r})
, the following inequality holds

(2.28)

\left\||x|^{b}|u|^{p-1}v\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}\leq CT^{\frac{5+b-p}{2}}\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(H^{1})}^{p-1}\,\|v\|_{L^{q}_{T}(L^{r})},

where
(q,r)
is given by (
3.35
) below.

Proof of Lemma
2.9
.

By employing Hölder’s inequality with
\gamma=\frac{6}{p-1-b}
and
r=\frac{6}{4+b-p}
, followed by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.27
) with
\theta=b\gamma
and

, we derive

\begin{split}\left\||x|^{b}|u|^{p-1}v\right\|_{L^{2}}&\leq\left\||x|^{b}|u|^{p-1}\right\|_{L^{\gamma}}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{r}}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{p-1}\,\|v\|_{L^{r}}.\end{split}

Next, by applying Hölder’s inequality in the temporal domain
[0,T]
with
q=\frac{2}{p-b-3}
, we deduce that

\begin{split}\left\||x|^{b}|u|^{p-1}v\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}&\lesssim\left\|\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{p-1}\right\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{T}}\,\|v\|_{L^{q}_{T}(L^{r})}\\
&\lesssim T^{\frac{5+b-p}{2}}\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(H^{1})}^{p-1}\,\|v\|_{L^{q}_{T}(L^{r})}.\end{split}

This concludes the proof of Lemma
2.9
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

As we will see shortly, the pair
(q,r)
is admissible as it satisfies the condition (

).

To address the linearization, we need to consider some important facts regarding convergence in measure.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
u_{n}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a sequence of measurable functions. We say that a sequence
(u_{n})
converges in measure to
u
on
\Omega
if

\forall\varepsilon>0,\quad\Big|\left\{x\in\Omega,|u_{n}(x)-u(x)|>\varepsilon\right\}\Big|\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\longrightarrow\infty,

where
|\cdot|
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
1\leq q<\infty
, and let
u_{n}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a sequence in
L^{q}(\Omega)
.

1)

If
u_{n}\longrightarrow u\mbox{ in }L^{q}(\Omega)\,(1\leq q<\infty),\;\;\mbox{ then }\;\;u_{n}\longrightarrow u\;\;\mbox{ in measure on }\Omega
.

2)

For any continuous function
G:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

u_{n}\to u\mbox{ in measure on }\Omega\quad\Longrightarrow\quad G(u_{n})\longrightarrow G(u)\;\mbox{ in measure on }\;\Omega.

The proof of Lemma
2.10
is straightforward; hence, we omit the details here.

3.
Global well posedness

This section is dedicated to proving Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, which concern global well-posedness in the energy space. The strategy of proofs can be summarized as follows.
To demonstrate local existence of solutions, we employ a classical fixed-point argument. We then establish uniqueness in an unconditional sense. Finally, we prove that the maximal solution is global.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

3.1.1.
Local Existence

Let us start by proving the existence. For
T>0
, we denote by

\mathbf{X}_{T}:=\mathcal{C}([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))

endowed by the norm
\|.\|_{T}
as defined in (
2.23
).
For
R>1+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}
, let
B_{T}(R)
denotes the ball in
\mathbf{X}_{T}
of radius
R
and centered at the origin. We define the map
\Phi
on the ball
B_{T}(R)
by
v\longmapsto\Phi(v)=\tilde{v}

where
\tilde{v}
solves

\Box\tilde{v}+\tilde{v}=-|x|^{b}f(v+v^{0}),\quad\textbf{}(\tilde{v}(0),\tilde{v}_{t}(0))=(0,0),

and
v^{0}
stands for the solution of the homogeneous equation

\Box{v^{0}}+v^{0}=0,\quad(v^{0}(0),\partial_{t}v^{0}(0)=(u_{0},u_{1}).

Here we suppose that the nonlinearity
f
is given by (
1.7
). Observe that, by combining Lemma
2.4
with the Moser-Trudinger inequality (
2.17
), it is clear that
|x|^{b}f(v+v^{0})
belongs to
L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})
for any
v\in\mathbf{X}_{T}
. Consequently, the existence of the solution
\tilde{v}
follows directly.
We will prove that the map
\Phi
is a contraction from
B_{T}(R)
into itself for
T,R>0
suitably chosen.
We start by showing that
\Phi
maps
B_{T}(R)
to itself. Let
v\in B_{T}(R)
. Applying the energy estimate (
2.22
) one gets

(3.29)

\|\Phi(v)\|_{T}=\|\tilde{v}\|_{T}\leq C_{0}\left\||x|^{b}f(v+v^{0})\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}.

For
\alpha>0
, we obtain by applying Lemma
2.4
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{2b}|f(v+v^{0})|^{2}dx\leq C\|v+v^{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{4b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(|v+v^{0}|^{4(1-b)}+(e^{2\alpha(v+v^{0})^{2}}-1)\right)dx.

Note that by the energy estimate (
2.22
) and the fact that
v\in B_{T}(R)
, we get

\|v+v^{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\Lambda:=R+C_{0}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}).

Therefore, considering (
2.18
) (with
\alpha\leq\frac{2\pi}{\Lambda^{2}}
) and noting that
4(1-b)\geq 2
and
\Lambda>1
, it follows that

(3.30)

\begin{split}\||x|^{b}f(v+v^{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leq C\Lambda^{4b}\left(\|v+v^{0}\|_{L^{4(1-b)}}^{4(1-b)}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}({\rm e}^{2\alpha\Lambda^{2}(\frac{v+v_{0}}{\Lambda})^{2}}-1)dx\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\Lambda^{4}+\Lambda^{4b}\right)\\
&\leq C\Lambda^{4}.\end{split}

From (
3.30
) and (
3.29
), it follows straightforwardly that

\|\Phi(v)\|_{T}\leq C_{0}C\Lambda^{2}T\leq R,

provided
T>0
is chosen to be sufficiently small. This implies that
\Phi(B_{T}(R)\subset B_{T}(R)
.

It remains to show that
\Phi
is a contraction mapping on

B_{T}(R)
. To this end, let
v_{1},v_{2}\in B_{T}(R)
. Define
\tilde{v}:=\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})=\tilde{v}_{1}-\tilde{v}_{2}
, which satisfies

\Box\tilde{v}+\tilde{v}=-|x|^{b}\left(f(v_{1}+v^{0})-f(v_{2}+v^{0})\right),\quad(\tilde{v}(0),\tilde{v}_{t}(0))=(0,0).

By expressing
f(v_{1}+v^{0})-f(v_{2}+v^{0})
as
(v_{1}-v_{2})({\rm e}^{\bar{v}}-1)
, where
\bar{v}=(1-\theta)(v_{1}+v^{0})+\theta(v_{2}+v^{0})
, and applying the energy estimate (
2.22
), we obtain

(3.31)

\|\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})\|_{T}\leq C_{0}\left\||x|^{b}(v_{1}-v_{2})({\rm e}^{\bar{v}}-1)\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}.

First, we estimate the
L^{2}
norm with respect to the spatial variable. Applying the elementary inequality
({\rm e}^{s}-1)^{2}\leq{\rm e}^{2s}-1-2s
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and utilizing a convexity argument, we obtain

(3.32)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{2b}(v_{1}-v_{2})^{2}\left({\rm e}^{\bar{v}}-1\right)^{2}dx\leq\mathbf{I_{1}}+\mathbf{I_{2}},

where

\mathbf{I_{j}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{2b}(v_{1}-v_{2})^{2}\left({\rm e}^{2(v_{j}+v^{0})}-1-2(v_{j}+v^{0})\right)dx,\quad j=1,2.

We estimate
\mathbf{I_{j}}
as follows

\begin{split}\mathbf{I_{j}}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(|x|^{1/2}|v_{1}-v_{2}|\right)^{2}|x|^{2b-1}\left({\rm e}^{2(v_{j}+v^{0})}-1-2(v_{j}+v^{0})\right)dx\\
&\leq C\|v_{1}(t)-v_{2}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{{\rm e}^{4\alpha(v_{j}+v^{0})^{2}}-1}{|x|^{1-2b}}dx\\
&=C\|v_{1}(t)-v_{2}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{{\rm e}^{4\alpha\Lambda^{2}(\frac{v_{j}+v^{0}}{\Lambda})^{2}}-1}{|x|^{1-2b}}dx,\end{split}

where we have applied the radial Sobolev inequality (
2.26
) and the observation that

\mathbf{K}_{\alpha}:=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{{\rm e}^{s}-1-s}{{\rm e}^{\alpha s^{2}}-1}\right)<\infty,\quad\forall\,\alpha>0.

Choosing
\alpha\leq\frac{\pi(1+2b)}{2\Lambda^{2}}
and applying the singular Moser-Trudinger inequality (
2.18
), we infer that

(3.33)

\mathbf{I_{j}}\leq C\|v_{1}(t)-v_{2}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Plugging estimates (
3.31
), (
3.32
), and (
3.33
) together, we find that

\|\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})\|_{T}\leq CT\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{T}.

By choosing
T>0
sufficiently small, we establish that
\Phi
is a contraction mapping. Consequently, the Banach fixed point theorem guarantees the existence of a unique fixed point
v\in B_{T}(R)
. Therefore,
u:=v+v^{0}
constitutes the desired local solution within the energy space. Notably, this result also ensures uniqueness within the class
v_{0}+B_{T}(R)
.

Remark 3.1
.

By noting that
\mathbf{K}_{\alpha}\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha}
for
\alpha\geq 1
, we can conclude that the time of local existence depends solely on the size of the initial data. This insight enables us to establish global existence, as described below.

3.1.2.
Global existence

Let
u
represent the unique maximal solution defined over the time interval
[0,T^{*})
, where
0<T^{*}\leq\infty
denotes the maximum lifespan. We argue by contradiction, assuming that
T^{*}
is finite. Consider, for
0<t_{0}<T^{*}
, the following initial value problem:

(3.34)

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cllll}\Box v+v&=&-|x|^{b}f(v),\\
(v(t_{0}),v_{t}(t_{0}))&=&(u(t_{0}),u_{t}(t_{0})).\\
\end{array}\right.\end{split}

According to the local theory, there exists a non-negative
\tau
and a unique solution

v
to (
3.34
) on the time interval
[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]
.
Since the time of local existence depends only on the size of the initial data and by the conservation of energy,
\tau
is independent of
t_{0}
. By choosing
t_{0}
sufficiently close to
T^{*}
such that
T^{*}-t_{0}<\tau
, we can extend
u
beyond the maximal time
T^{*}
. This contradicts the definition of
T^{*}
, leading us to conclude that
T^{*}=\infty
as desired.

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Here, we assume that
N=3
,
b>0
, and
3+b<p<4+b
. Let
\mathbf{D}:=\left(\partial_{t},\nabla\right)
,
\mathcal{E}_{0}:=\|\mathbf{D}u(0)\|_{L^{2}}
denote the initial kinetic energy, and the pair
(q,r)
be given by

(3.35)

\left(q,r\right)=\left(\frac{2}{p-b-3},\frac{6}{4+b-p}\right).

One can easily verify that
(q,r)
is an admissible pair in the sense that it satisfies (
2.24
). We will employ a standard fixed-point argument. For this purpose, we introduce the following Banach space

{\mathbf{E}}_{T}={\mathcal{C}}([0,T];\dot{H}^{1})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{1}([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{q}([0,T];L^{r})

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{T}:=\|\mathbf{D}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{2})}+\|u\|_{L^{q}_{T}(L^{r})},

where
T>0
and
(q,r)
is given by (
3.35
).
For a positive real number
R
, we
denote by
B_{T}(R)
the ball in
{\mathbf{E}}_{T}

of radius
R
and centered at the origin. On the ball

B_{T}(R)
, we define the map
\Phi
by

v\longmapsto\Phi(v):=\tilde{v},

where

(3.36)

\Box\tilde{v}=-|x|^{b}|v|^{p-1}v,\quad(\tilde{v}(0),\tilde{v}_{t}(0))=(u_{0},u_{1}).

Our aim now is to show that, if
R
and
T
are suitably chosen, the map
\Phi
is well defined from
B_{T}(R)
into itself and it is a contraction. From (
2.28
) we see that
|x|^{b}|v|^{p-1}v
belongs to
L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})
as long as
v\in{\mathbf{E}}_{T}
. It follows that for every
v\in{\mathbf{E}}_{T}
, the Cauchy problem (
3.36
) has a unique solution
\tilde{v}
in
{\mathbf{E}}_{T}
. Moreover, if
v\in B_{T}(R)
, then by (
2.25
) and (
2.28
), we have

(3.37)

\begin{split}\|\Phi(v)\|_{T}&\leq C\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}+\||x|^{b}|v|^{p-1}v\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}+T^{\frac{5+b-p}{2}}\,\|v\|_{T}^{p}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}+T^{\frac{5+b-p}{2}}\,R^{p}\right).\end{split}

Choosing
R=2C\mathcal{E}_{0}
and owing to (
3.37
), we see that
\Phi(B_{T}(R))\subset B_{T}(R)
as long as
T^{\frac{5+b-p}{2}}\,R^{p}\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}
. The latter condition reads in terms of
T
as

(3.38)

T\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0}^{-\kappa},

where
\kappa:=\frac{2(p-1)}{5+b-p}>0
since
1<p<5+b
.

It remains to show that
\Phi
is a contraction mapping on

B_{T}(R)
. Observe that given
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
in
B_{T}(R)
,
w:=\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})=\tilde{v}_{1}-\tilde{v}_{2}
satisfies

\Box w=-|x|^{b}(|v_{1}|^{p-1}v_{1}-|v_{2}|^{p-1}v_{2}),\quad(w(0),w_{t}(0))=(0,0).

Thanks to Strichartz estimate (
2.25
), we get

\|\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})\|_{T}\leq C\left\||x|^{b}(|v_{1}|^{p-1}v_{1}-|v_{2}|^{p-1}v_{2})\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}.

Arguing as above and using (
2.28
) coupled with the elementary inequality

\left||v_{1}|^{p-1}v_{1}-|v_{2}|^{p-1}v_{2}\right|\leq\,p\,|v_{1}-v_{2}|\left(|v_{1}|^{p-1}+|v_{2}|^{p-1}\right),

we end up with

(3.39)

\begin{split}\|\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})\|_{T}&\leq\,C\,\left\||x|^{b}|v_{1}-v_{2}|\left(|v_{1}|^{p-1}+|v_{2}|^{p-1}\right)\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}\\
&\leq\,C\,T^{\frac{5+b-p}{2}}\,\left(\|v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(H^{1})}^{p-1}+\|v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(H^{1})}^{p-1}\right)\,\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{q}_{T}(L^{r})}\\
&\leq\,C\,T^{\frac{5+b-p}{2}}\,\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}+R\right)^{p-1}\left\|v_{1}-v_{2}\right\|_{T}.\end{split}

If
T
satisfies (
3.38
), then (
3.39
) tell us that
\Phi
is a contraction mapping on
B_{T}(R)
. This finishes the local existence part. The unconditional uniqueness can be done easily by using similar argument. Finally, the maximal solution is global in the defocusing regime due to the energy conservation and the blow-up alternative.

4.
Linearization

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Hereafter,
T
stands for a fixed positive time, and
(\varphi_{n},\psi_{n})
a sequence in
H^{1}\times L^{2}
satisfying (
1.14
) and (
1.15
).
We denote by
(u_{n})
, (respectively
(v_{n})
) the sequence of finite energy solutions to (
1.1
), (respectively (
1.13
)) such that

(u_{n}(0),\partial_{t}u_{n}(0))=(v_{n}(0),\partial_{t}v_{n}(0))=(\varphi_{n},\psi_{n}).

4.1.
The 2D case

Recall that in this context, we have
f(u)=-\left({\rm e}^{u}-1-u\right)
, leading to
F(u)=-\left({\rm e}^{u}-1-u-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)
. An essential component of the proof hinges on the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Up to extraction, we have

u_{n}\rightharpoonup 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad H^{1}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{2}),

and

u_{n}\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad L^{2}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Let

M:=\sup_{n}\left(\|\varphi_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0<\alpha<\frac{4\pi}{M}.

By the energy conservation and Lemma
2.3
with
\kappa=3
, we have

\displaystyle E(u_{n},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\varphi_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{b}F(\varphi_{n})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{M}{2}+CM^{b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(|\varphi_{n}|^{3-2b}+\left({\rm e}^{\alpha\varphi_{n}^{2}}-1\right)\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{M}{2}+CM^{3-b}+CM^{b}.

Therefore, once more through energy conservation, we obtain for
0\leq t\leq T
,

(4.40)

\|\nabla u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2E(u_{n},t)\leq C_{M}:=M+2CM^{3-b}+2CM^{b}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(]0,T[\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\|u_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}u_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\Big)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2TE(u_{n},t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2T\left(\frac{M}{2}+CM^{3-b}+CM^{b}\right).

Hence, after extraction, the sequence
(u_{n})
possesses a weak limit
u
in
H^{1}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
. To finalize the proof, we will show that
u
satisfies the following initial value problem

\Box u+|x|^{b}f(u)=0,\quad(u(0,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(0,\cdot))=(0,0),

which ultimately leads to
u=0
through the application of a standard uniqueness argument.

Although we deal here with a spatial growing nonlinearity, the remainder of the proof uses Lemma
2.3
and mimics the same steps performed in
[
32
, Lemma 4.3]
, which specifically addresses the case
b=0
. For brevity, we omit the detailed steps. Consequently, the proof of Lemma
4.1
is now complete.
∎

We return to the proof of the 2D part in Theorem
1.3
. Setting
w_{n}=u_{n}-v_{n}
, we have

\Box w_{n}+w_{n}=-|x|^{b}f(u_{n}),\quad\left(w_{n}(0,.),\partial_{t}w_{n}(0,.)\right)=(0,0).

By applying the energy estimate to
w_{n}
over the interval
[0,T]
, and then utilizing Lemma
2.4
with
0<\alpha<\frac{2\pi}{C_{M}}
(where
C_{M}
is defined in (
4.40
)), along with (
4.40
) and (
2.17
) (with
\beta=0
), we derive for
t\in[0,T]

(4.41)

\begin{split}E_{0}(w_{n},t)&\lesssim\||x|^{b}f(u_{n})\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim T\,\||x|^{b}f(u_{n})\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{L^{4(1-b)}}^{4(1-b)}+\int_{0}^{T}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,\left({\rm e}^{2\alpha\,u_{n}^{2}}-1\right)dx\,dt\\
&\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{L^{4(1-b)}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{4(1-b)}+\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}.\end{split}

To conclude the proof, we observe that the sequence
(u_{n})
converges to
0
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
and is bounded in
H^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
. By interpolation, this convergence extends to
0
in any Lebesgue space
L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
2\leq q<6
. Since
2\leq 4(1-b)<6
due to
b\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we infer that the right-hand side in (
4.41
) tends to zero as
n\to\infty
. This concludes the proof of the 2D component of Theorem
1.3
.

4.2.
The 3D case

We move now to the 3D part of Theorem
1.3
. In the following, we have
N=3
,
b>0
,
1+b<p<4+b
, and
T>0
.

As will become clear later on, the proof borrows some arguments from
[
13
, Theorem 2.3]
. By defining
w_{n}=u_{n}-v_{n}
, we obtain

\Box w_{n}=-|x|^{b}|u_{n}|^{p-1}u_{n},\quad\left(w_{n}(0,.),\partial_{t}w_{n}(0,.)\right)=(0,0).

Applying energy estimate yields

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E_{0}(w_{n},t)\lesssim\||x|^{b}|u_{n}|^{p-1}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}^{2},

where
E_{0}(w,t)
is defined by (
1.16
) with
\mathbf{m}=0
. By (
1.10
) and
the finite propagation speed, the solution
u_{n}
is supported in
\displaystyle\Big\{(t,x);\;0\leq t\leq T,\;|x|\leq R_{0}+t\Big\}
.

By the conservation of energy, (
2.27
) and (
1.14
), we have

\begin{split}E(u_{n},t)&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\nabla\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\,|x|^{b}|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{p+1}\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{M}{2}+\frac{K_{opt}}{p+1}M^{\frac{p+1}{2}},\end{split}

where
M:=\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\|\nabla\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)<\infty
. It follows that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in[0,T]
,

(4.42)

\|u_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq M_{T},

where
M_{T}
is a positive constant. Now, by utilizing the Strichartz estimate (
2.25
) for the admissible pair
(q,r)
as defined in (
3.35
), along with Lemma
2.9
, we obtain

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{T}(L^{r})}\lesssim\sqrt{M}+T^{\frac{5+b-p}{2}}\,\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(H^{1})}^{p-1}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{T}(L^{r})}.

Therefore, utilizing (
4.42
), we deduce that
(u_{n})
is bounded in
L^{q}_{T}(L^{r})
for small
T>0
. By repeating this procedure iteratively, we establish the boundedness of
(u_{n})
in
L^{q}_{T}(L^{r})
for any
T>0
.
Now, the remaining part of the proof can be conducted analogously to the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 2.3]
, incorporating Lemma
2.10
. Consequently, for the sake of brevity, the details are omitted. Thus, we thereby conclude the proof of Theorem
1.3
concerning linearization.

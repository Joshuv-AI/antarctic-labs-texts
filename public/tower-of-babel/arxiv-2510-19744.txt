1.
Introduction

For a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
let
\ba(\mathcal{A})
denote the Banach space of all bounded finitely additive real-valued measures on
\mathcal{A}
endowed with the variation norm (see Section
2
for all further relevant notation and notions). We start with the following standard and well-known definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

A Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the
Nikodym property
if every sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\ba(\mathcal{A})
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
is norm bounded.

Definition 1.2
.

A Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the
Grothendieck property
if every norm bounded sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\ba(\mathcal{A})
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
is weakly convergent to
0
.

Both of the above properties belong to the most important and intensively investigated measure-theoretic properties of Boolean algebras, in particular of fields of sets, having numerous applications in Banach space theory, vector measure theory, theory of topological vector spaces, matrix analysis, etc., see e.g.
[
6
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
43
]
,
[
71
]
.

The class of Boolean algebras having both of the properties include all
\sigma
-complete Boolean algebras (Nikodym
[
68
]
, Andô
[
5
]
; Grothendieck
[
45
]
) or, more generally, all Boolean algebras whose Stone spaces are F-spaces (Seever
[
72
]
); see also
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
40
]
,
[
46
]
,
[
67
]
,
[
76
]
for more examples. On the other hand, if the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
of a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
contains a non-trivial convergent sequence, then
\mathcal{A}
has neither of the properties (cf. Example
3.8
; see also
[
63
]
and
[
88
]
for more general results). It is however more difficult to find examples of algebras which have only one of the properties. The Boolean algebra
\mathscr{J}
of Jordan measurable subsets of the unit interval
[0,1]
was the first known example of a Boolean algebra with the Nikodym property but without the Grothendieck property (Schachermayer
[
71
]
; see also Graves and Wheeler
[
44
]
and Valdivia
[
86
]
for generalizations). Boolean algebras with the Grothendieck property but without the Nikodym property have been so far constructed only consistently—by Talagrand
[
83
]
under the Continuum Hypothesis, by the first author and Zdomskyy
[
77
]
under Martin’s Axiom, and by Głodkowski and Widz
[
42
]
in a specific forcing extension; no
ZFC
example is known.

In the present work we provide further examples of Boolean algebras with the Nikodym property and without the Grothendieck property. Constructed examples are subalgebras of the Boolean algebra
{\wp(\omega)}
, the power set of
\omega
, and have the form
\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}^{*}
where
\mathcal{F}
is a filter on
\omega
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}
is its dual ideal. The used filters are dual to so-called Erdős–Ulam ideals, which yields that the constructed Boolean algebras are of low Borel complexity when thought of as subsets of the Cantor space
2^{\omega}
.

Theorem A
.

There is a family
\mathcal{H}_{1}
of
\mathfrak{c}
many pairwise non-isomorphic
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta}
Boolean subalgebras of
{\wp(\omega)}
, each of them having the Nikodym property but not the Grothendieck property.

The cardinality of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
, i.e. the continuum
\mathfrak{c}
, is optimal, as there are only
\mathfrak{c}
many Borel subsets of
2^{\omega}
. By using appropriate non-analytic filters we may however obtain larger families of Boolean subalgebras of
{\wp(\omega)}
with the Nikodym property but without the Grothendieck property.

Theorem B
.

There is a family
\mathcal{H}_{2}
of
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
many pairwise non-isomorphic non-analytic Boolean algebras of
{\wp(\omega)}
, each of them having the Nikodym property but not the Grothendieck property.

The proofs of Theorems
A
and
B
are presented in Section
5.2
. Here we only briefly discuss their main ingredients, each of them being in fact of its own separate interest.

Our first step towards Theorems
A
and
B
relies on the study of so-called Nikodym concentration points of unbounded sequences of measures on Boolean algebras without the Nikodym property, initiated in
[
3
]
and
[
74
]
. Briefly, given a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
, let us say that a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{A}
is
anti-Nikodym
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|=\infty
, and that in this case a point
t
in the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
(i.e. an ultrafilter
t
in
\mathcal{A}
) is its
Nikodym concentration point
if for each
A\in t
we have
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|=\infty
(cf. Definitions
3.1
and
3.4
). Note that if a Boolean algebra does not have the Nikodym property, then it carries an anti-Nikodym sequence, and that every anti-Nikodym sequence has a Nikodym concentration point (Lemma
3.3
). In Section
3
we will investigate anti-Nikodym sequences of measures having
strong
Nikodym concentration points, that is, Nikodym concentration points satisfying a slightly technical yet natural condition described in Definition
3.7
. In particular, we prove in Theorem
3.18
that if an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has a strong Nikodym concentration point, then
\mathcal{A}
carries an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures with pairwise disjoint supports (or even contained in pairwise disjoint clopen subsets of
St(\mathcal{A})
), which, as one will see below, will appear to be an extremely useful feature.

In the second step towards the proofs of Theorems
A
and
B
we prove in Proposition
4.11
that if a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the Nikodym property but its subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
generated by
\mathcal{I}
(i.e.
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle=\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{*}
) does not, then every anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
has a unique strong Nikodym concentration point, namely, the point
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\in St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
corresponding to the filter
\mathcal{I}^{*}
(which is an ultrafilter in
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
). This, together with aforementioned Theorem
3.18
, enables us to characterize the Nikodym property for Boolean algebras of the form
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
in terms of non-negative measures with pairwise disjoint supports—see Theorem
4.14
for a general statement, below we only provide its variant for a free filter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
and the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}={\wp(\omega)}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle=\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}^{*}
(cf. also Corollary
5.2
).

Theorem C
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a free filter on
\omega
. Then, the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}^{*}
has the Nikodym property if and only if there is no disjointly supported sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of non-negative measures on
S_{\mathcal{F}}=St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}\big)
such that:

(1)

p_{\mathcal{F}}\not\in\supp\big(\mu_{n}\big)
for every
n\in\omega
,

(2)

\sup_{n\in\omega}\mu_{n}\big(S_{\mathcal{F}}\big)=\infty
,

(3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(S_{\mathcal{F}}\setminus U\big)=0
for every clopen neighborhood
U
of
p_{\mathcal{F}}
.

For every free filter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
, the set of isolated points in the Stone space
St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}\big)
of the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
can be naturally associated with the (discrete) space
\omega
, and so we can put
N_{\mathcal{F}}=\omega\cup\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}
, where again the point
p_{\mathcal{F}}
corresponds to filter
\mathcal{F}
in
St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}\big)
. It is easy to see that we have
St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}\big)=St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin\big)\cup N_{\mathcal{F}}
and
St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin\big)\cap N_{\mathcal{F}}=\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
denotes the quotient Boolean algebra of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
modulo finite subsets of
\omega
(see
[
63
, Section 3]
for details). The third step in proving Theorems
A
and
B
thus relies on decomposing the Nikodym property of Boolean algebras of the form
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
into two parts: the Nikodym property of the quotient Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
and the finitely supported Nikodym property of the spaces
N_{\mathcal{F}}
, defined as follows.

Definition 1.3
.

A zero-dimensional topological space
X
has the
finitely supported Nikodym property
if every sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of Borel real-valued measures on
X
with finite supports and such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=0
for every clopen subset
A\subseteq X
is norm bounded.

The announced decomposition of the Nikodym property for Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
reads then precisely as follows (for its proof, using Theorem
C
, see Theorem
5.3
).

Theorem D
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a free filter on
\omega
. Then, the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
has the Nikodym property if and only if the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
has the Nikodym property and the space
N_{\mathcal{F}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property.

The finitely supported Nikodym property of spaces of the form
N_{\mathcal{F}}
was thorougly studied by the second author in
[
88
]
, especially in the context of the Katětov ordering of ideals and so-called non-pathological ideals on
\omega
(see Section
5.1
for definitions of various classes of ideals mentioned below). In particular, it was proved there that, for a density ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
, the space
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property if and only if
\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to an Erdős–Ulam ideal (cf. Theorem
5.17
.(3)). Consequently, using Theorem
D
and Theorem
5.10
, asserting that for a P-filter
\mathcal{F}
the Nikodym property of the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
is equivalent to the finitely supported Nikodym property of the space
N_{\mathcal{F}}
, in Theorem
5.19
we show that for every density ideal
\mathcal{I}
the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the Nikodym property if and only if
\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to an Erdős–Ulam ideal. We then use this result, together with the facts that each Erdős–Ulam ideal is an
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta}
subset of
2^{\omega}
and that there are
\mathfrak{c}
many pairwise non-isomorphic Erdős–Ulam ideals on
\omega
, to finally obtain the family
\mathcal{H}_{1}
of Boolean algebras in Theorem
A
; see the proof of Theorem
5.27
.

The argument for Theorem
B
is a bit different—instead of using Erdős–Ulam ideals, we consider a family of
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
many pairwise non-isomorphic non-analytic ideals of the form
\mathcal{U}^{*}\oplus\mathcal{Z}
, where each
\mathcal{U}
is a free ultrafilter on
\omega
and
\mathcal{Z}
is the standard asymptotic density zero ideal, see the proof of Theorem
5.31
.

The above discussion has so far concerned only the Nikodym property of Boolean algebras of the form
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
. To show that the algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
used to establish Theorems
A
and
B
do not have the Grothendieck property, we exploit the fact that their spaces
N_{\mathcal{F}}
have the following property.

Definition 1.4
.

A topological space
X
has the
bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig property
(in short,
(BJNP)
), if there is a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of Borel real-valued measures on
X
with finite supports and norm
1
and such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}fd\mu_{n}=0
for every bounded continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
.

The bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig property was introduced by Kąkol
et al.

[
55
]
in order to investigate the celebrated Separable Quotient Problem in the class of topological vector spaces
C_{p}(X)
of continuous functions on Tychonoff spaces
X
endowed with the pointwise topology and is, of course, closely related to the classical Josefson–Nissenzweig theorem from Banach space theory (see
[
17
, Chapter XII]
). Based on the work
[
56
]
, Marciszewski and the first author showed in
[
63
]
that if the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
of a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
contains a homeomorphic copy of a space
N_{\mathcal{F}}
which has (BJNP), then
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Grothendieck property, as well as that for an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
the space
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has (BJNP) if and only if
\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\mathcal{Z}
, i.e.
\mathcal{I}
is Katětov below the aforementioned asymptotic density ideal
\mathcal{Z}
. Consequently, since both Erdős–Ulam ideals as well as ideals of the form
\mathcal{U}^{*}\oplus\mathcal{Z}
are all Katětov below
\mathcal{Z}
, we obtain that no algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
from the families
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
can have the Grothendieck property.

Motivated by the above results, in the context of the open problem of the existence in
ZFC
of a Boolean algebra with the Grothendieck property but without the Nikodym property, one might naturally look for such an example among the algebras of the form
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
. This would however appear pointless, as there is no Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
with the Grothendieck property and without the Nikodym property.

Theorem E
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a free filter on
\omega
. If the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
does not have the Nikodym property, then it does not have the Grothendieck property either.

To establish the above result, we again appeal to the notion of strong Nikodym concentration points. Namely, in Section
3.1
we show that the existence of an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
with a strong Nikodym concentration point causes that
\mathcal{A}
in fact has neither the Nikodym property nor the Grothendieck property.

Theorem F
.

If a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Nikodym property and this is witnessed by an anti-Nikodym sequence with a strong Nikodym concentration point in the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
, then
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Grothendieck property.

An immediate important corollary of Theorem
F
is that if a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
carries an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures having a unique Nikodym concentration point, then
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Grothendieck property (Corollary
3.21
). This result may be seen as a variant of the above-mentioned standard fact asserting that if the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
of a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
contains a non-trivial convergent sequence, then
\mathcal{A}
has neither the Nikodym property, nor the Grothendieck property, cf. Example
3.8
. Consequently, as shown in Proposition
4.11
, if a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the Nikodym property and
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal in
\mathcal{A}
such that the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
\mathcal{I}
does not have the Nikodym property, then
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
does not have the Grothendieck property either (cf. also Corollary
4.12
). Theorem
E
is then a special case of the latter statement (for
\mathcal{A}={\wp(\omega)}
and
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{F}^{*}
).

The above-presented main theorems have mostly regarded the Nikodym and Grothendieck properties of Boolean algebras
generated by
ideals. We will now focus on the Nikodym property of ideals
themselves
; the property is defined in a similar way to that of Boolean algebras, see Definitions
4.1
and
4.8
. The research on this variant of the Nikodym property was initiated by Drewnowski, Florencio, and Paúl
[
20
]
in the context of studying properties of barrelled topological vector spaces, with the remarkable result asserting that the ideal
\mathcal{Z}
has the Nikodym property, and later continued, e.g., in
[
4
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
81
]
,
[
82
]
.

The Nikodym properties of Boolean algebras and ideals may initially appear somehow unrelated, but, as first observed by Drewnowski and Paúl
[
25
, Remark 2.2.(a)]
, they are in fact quite the same: an ideal
\mathcal{I}
in a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the Nikodym property if and only if the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
of
\mathcal{A}
has the Nikodym property (cf. Theorem
4.10
). In Section
4.1
we analyse this relation in more detail, primarily by decomposing measures on the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
of a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
into two parts, defined on the open subset of
St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
corresponding to the ideal
\mathcal{I}
and on the singleton
\big\{p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big\}
corresponding to the ultrafilter
\mathcal{I}^{*}
in
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
, respectively. Based on this decomposition, e.g., in Theorem
4.7
we show that the Banach space
\ba(\mathcal{I})
of all finitely additive signed measures on the ideal
\mathcal{I}
is in a natural way isomorphic to a closed linear subspace of the Banach space
\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
of codimension
1
.

Let us point that the above-described topological approach to the study of relations between the two Nikodym properties has several important advantages. First of all, it provides better insight into the general relation between ideals and the generated Boolean algebras as well as into the behavior of sequences of measures on the Stone spaces. Second, it brings natural tools for merging and unifying two, so far essentially separated, lines of research on the Nikodym property: one concerning rings and one regarding Boolean algebras. Third, it creates a natural source of examples for both of the lines, see for instance Theorems
A
and
B
as well as numerous examples and constructions presented in Sections
5
and
6
.

In Section
6
we study relations between various notions of boundedness and non-atomicity of ideals and submeasures on
\omega
, which are closely connected to the Nikodym property. These are, in particular, the total boundedness, the Local-to-Global Boundedness Property, the Bolzano–Weierstrass Property (BWP), the (Strong) Nested Partition Property ((S)NPP), strong non-atomicity of submeasures, and the existence of countable splitting families in quotients
{\wp(\omega)}/\mathcal{I}
. Due to the numerosity of the properties, we simply refer the reader to Sections
5
and
6
for respective definitions, here we only provide two of them: an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
has the
Local-to-Global Boundedness Property for submeasures
(in short,
(LGBPs)
), if whenever
\varphi
is an lsc submeasure on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\fin(\varphi)
, then
\varphi(\omega)<\infty
; and an analytic P-ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
is
totally bounded
, if whenever
\varphi
is an lsc submeasure on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\exh(\varphi)
, then
\varphi(\omega)<\infty
(cf. Proposition
6.6
). The first property was defined by Drewnowski, Florencio, and Paúl
[
21
]
, who showed that it implies the Nikodym property of ideals on
\omega
(and, in fact, in a sense it may be considered as an analogon of the Nikodym property for a single submeasure). The total boundedness was introduced by Hernández-Hernández and Hrušák
[
48
]
as an auxiliary notion in the study of destructibility of ideals on
\omega
by forcing. It was shown in the latter work that, for an analytic P-ideal
\mathcal{I}
, the existence of a countable splitting family in the quotient Boolean algebra
{\wp(\omega)}/\mathcal{I}
implies that
\mathcal{I}
is totally bounded. In Corollaries
6.12
and
6.19
we show that the reverse implication also holds as well as that the total boundedness is equivalent to several further properties. More precisely, supplementing and combining results of Filipów
et al.

[
9
]
and
[
32
]
, Drewnowski and Łuczak
[
23
]
, and Stuart
[
82
]
, we get that for every lsc submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
and the ideal
\mathcal{I}=\exh(\varphi)
the following statements are equivalent:

(1)
\mathcal{I}
is totally bounded,

(2)
\mathcal{I}
has (LGBPs),

(3)
\mathcal{I}
cannot be extended to an
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}
-ideal,

(4)
\mathcal{I}
does not have (BWP),

(5)
{\wp(\omega)}/\mathcal{I}
has a countable splitting family,

(6)
\conv\leq_{K}\mathcal{I}
,

(7)
\varphi^{\bullet}
is strongly non-atomic,

(8)
\mathcal{I}
is non-atomic,

(9)
\mathcal{I}
has (SNPP).

(Here,
\conv
denotes the ideal on
\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
generated by convergent sequences and
\varphi^{\bullet}
is the core of the submeasure
\varphi
, see Section
5.1
.)

As mentioned above, it was proved in paper
[
21
]
that (LGBPs) implies the Nikodym property, but its authors conjectured that the converse does not hold (
[
21
, Conjecture on page 147]
). Confirming their guesswork, we prove in Theorem
6.3
that several ideals on
\omega
already described in the literature indeed have the Nikodym property but not (LGBPs). Moreover, expanding the construction of analytic P-ideals on
\omega
due to Alon, Drewnowski, and Łuczak
[
4
]
, based on sequences of finite Kneser hypergraphs, in Section
6.3
we introduce a new class of ideals called
hypergraph
(see Definition
6.25
) and obtain the following quantitative result.

Theorem G
.

There is a family of
\mathfrak{c}
many pairwise non-isomorphic hypergraph ideals which have the Nikodym property but not the Local-to-Global Boundedness Property for submeasures.

The above theorem is a reformulation of Theorem
6.30
. Let us note that the hypergraph ideals constructed in its proof are induced by sequences of hypergraphs with increasing chromatic numbers, therefore by Proposition
6.28
they cannot have the Nested Partition Property. This fact yields a negative answer to an (implicit) question of Stuart
[
82
, page 153]
, asking whether the Nikodym property of a ring of sets implies the Nested Partition Property, see Corollary
6.35
.

Our results also have some applications to the study of two stronger variants of the Nikodym property, named the
strong Nikodym property
and the
web Nikodym property
, see Definitions
5.7
and
5.8
, introduced by Valdivia
[
85
]
and López-Pellicer
[
62
]
, respectively, who proved that
\sigma
-fields of sets have both the properties (see Section
5
for more information). The web Nikodym property for ideals was first studied by Ferrando
[
29
]
, who proved that the ideal
\mathcal{Z}
has the web Nikodym property, and later by Ferrando, López-Alfonso, and López-Pellicer
[
30
]
. Exploiting the latter work we show in Theorem
5.10
that for a P-ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the Nikodym property,

(2)
\mathcal{I}
has the Nikodym property,

(3)
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the strong Nikodym property,

(4)
\mathcal{I}
has the strong Nikodym property,

(5)
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the web Nikodym property,

(6)
\mathcal{I}
has the web Nikodym property,

(7)
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property.

Moreover, by using the results of
[
88
]
, we observe in Corollaries
5.20
and
6.7
that for a density ideal
\mathcal{I}
the following conditions are additionally equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{I}
has the (strong/web) Nikodym property,

(2)

\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to an Erdős–Ulam ideal,

(3)

\mathcal{I}
is totally bounded.

Some new examples of ideals with the strong and web Nikodym properties are then presented in Examples
5.12
and
5.22
, and in Remarks
5.21
and
6.34
.

To discuss the results presented in Section
7
let us introduce the following notation, which will be used throughout the paper.

Definition 1.5
.

The class of all ideals
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
for which the space
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
does not have the finitely supported Nikodym property is denoted by
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
.

In
[
88
]
the second author studied the cofinal structure of the ordered set
(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N},\leq_{K})
, that is, the set
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
endowed with the Katětov ordering
\leq_{K}
. In particular, he proved that
(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N},\leq_{K})
does not have any maximal elements as well as that it has cofinal families of size
\mathfrak{d}
, i.e. the cardinal characteristic of the continuum called the
dominating number
, consisting only of density ideals. Moreover, it was showed there that the ordered set
(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N},\leq_{K})
is Tukey reducible to the standard poset
(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
and asked whether a converse Tukey reduction also exists (see Section
7
for relevant definitions). In Section
7
we observe, based on a simple measure-theoretic argument, that this is indeed the case.

Theorem H
.

The ordered sets
(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N},\leq_{K})
and
(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
are Tukey equivalent.

Theorem
H
supplements the results of Minami and Sakai
[
66
]
and He, Li, and Zhang
[
47
]
, who proved that the classes of all
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}
-ideals on
\omega
and of all summable ideals on
\omega
, both endowed with
\leq_{K}
, are Tukey equivalent to the poset
(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
. In fact, the most important ingredient of the proof of Theorem
H
is showing that every ideal in
\mathcal{AN}
is contained in a summable ideal, which, as explained in Section
7
, greatly generalizes the theorem of Drewnowski and Paúl
[
25
]
asserting that in the class of P-ideals the Nikodym property coincides with the so-called Positive Summability Property.

Let us note that Theorem
H
corresponds to the intuitive “unbounded” character of the Nikodym property and clearly contrasts the “bounded” character of the Grothendieck property, here expressed by the above-mentioned result of Marciszewski and the first author
[
63
]
asserting that, for an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
, the space
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig property (so, in a sense, does not have the
finitely supported
Grothendieck property; cf.
[
56
]
) if and only if
\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\mathcal{Z}
.

The plan of the paper is as follows. In the next section we recall basic notions and notation concerning measures on rings, Boolean algebras, ideals, and compact spaces. In Section
3
we study sequences of measures on Boolean algebras which witness simultaneously the lack of the Nikodym property and of the Grothendieck property. Section
4
is devoted to relations between the Nikodym properties of ideals and Boolean algebras generated by ideals. In Section
5
we investigate the Nikodym property of Boolean subalgebras of
{\wp(\omega)}
generated by ideals on
\omega
. In Section
6
relations between various notions of boundedness and non-atomicity of ideals on
\omega
and the Nikodym property are studied. Section
7
is devoted to the description of the Tukey class of the ordered set
(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N},\leq_{K})
. In the final section of the paper, Section
8
, we pose several open questions.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we briefly describe the notation and nomenclature used in the paper.

By
\omega
we denote the first infinite cardinal number, i.e. the cardinality of the set
\mathbb{N}
of all non-negative integers, and we write
\mathfrak{c}
for the
continuum
, i.e. the cardinality of the real line
\mathbb{R}
. As usual, we will simply identify
\omega
with the set
\mathbb{N}
.

We will identify each
n\in\omega
with the set
\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
. For each
k\leq n\in\omega
we put
[k,n]=\{k,k+1,\ldots,n\}
.

The set of all functions
\omega\to\omega
is denoted by
\omega^{\omega}
. For every
f,g\in\omega^{\omega}
we write
f\leq^{*}g
if and only if there is
N\in\omega
such that
f(n)\leq g(n)
for every
n\geq N
.

Let
X
be a set. By
\wp(X)
we denote the family of all subsets of
X
.
|X|
stands for the cardinality of
X
. If
\kappa
is a cardinal number, then by
[X]^{<\kappa}
and
[X]^{\kappa}
we denote the families of all subsets of
X
of cardinality strictly less than
\kappa
and of all subsets of
X
of cardinality equal to
\kappa
, respectively. By
X^{<\omega}
we denote the set consisting of all finite sequences of elements of
X
, i.e.
X^{<\omega}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}X^{n}
. We also shortly write
Fin=\left[\omega\right]^{<\omega}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Boolean algebra (with operations
\wedge
,
\vee
,
\setminus
,
c
, zero element
0_{\mathcal{A}}
, and unit element
1_{\mathcal{A}}
). For a subset
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
by
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{X}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
we denote the Boolean subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
\mathcal{X}
; we also set
\mathcal{X}^{*}=\big\{1_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus A\colon A\in\mathcal{X}\}
and call
\mathcal{X}^{*}

dual
to
\mathcal{X}
. For an element
A\in\mathcal{A}
we set
\mathcal{A}_{A}=\{A\wedge B\colon B\in\mathcal{A}\}
; of course,
\mathcal{A}_{A}
is also a Boolean algebra (with obvious operations and constants). A collection
\big\langle A_{i}\colon\ i\in I\big\rangle
of elements of
\mathcal{A}
is an
antichain
if
A_{i}\wedge A_{j}=0_{\mathcal{A}}
for every
i\neq j\in I
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
. Note that, as
\mathcal{I}
is downward closed, for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
we have
\mathcal{A}_{A}=(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)_{A}
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\in\{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{I}\}
. A function
\mu\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
measure
on
\mathcal{X}
if
\mu
is finitely additive and
bounded
, i.e. for the variation norm

\|\mu\|=\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|\mu(B)|\colon\ A,B\in\mathcal{X},\ A\wedge B=0_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}

we have
\|\mu\|<\infty
. By
\ba(\mathcal{X})
we denote the vector space of all measures on
\mathcal{X}
endowed with the variation norm, which makes
\ba(\mathcal{X})
a Banach space.

More generally, for
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{X})
and
X\in\mathcal{X}
we set

|\mu|(X)=\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|\mu(B)|\colon\ A,B\in\mathcal{X},\ A\wedge B=0_{\mathcal{A}},\ A\vee B\leq X\big\},

so, in the case
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{A}
, we have
\|\mu\|=|\mu|\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)
.

For a measure
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{X})
, an element
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and a subset
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, we define the
restricted
measures
\mu\restriction A\in\ba(\mathcal{X})
and
\mu\restriction\mathcal{Y}\in\ba(\mathcal{Y})
as follows:
(\mu\restriction A)(B)=\mu(A\wedge B)
for every
B\in\mathcal{A}
, and
(\mu\restriction\mathcal{Y})(B)=\mu(B)
for every
B\in\mathcal{Y}
. Note that
\|\mu\restriction A\|=\|\mu\restriction\mathcal{A}_{A}\|
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
. For measures
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{A})
and
\nu\in\ba(\mathcal{I})
we will say that
\mu

extends

\nu
if
\mu\restriction\mathcal{I}=\nu
. Of course, since
\mathcal{I}
is downward closed, we have
|\nu|(A)=|\mu|(A)
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
and so
\|\nu\|\leq\|\mu\|
.

All topological spaces considered in this paper are assumed to be Tychonoff. Let
X
be a topological space. By
Bor(X)
we denote the
\sigma
-field of all Borel subsets of
X
and by
Clopen(X)
the field of all clopen subsets of
X
.
C(X)
as usual denotes the set of all continuous real-valued functions on
X
and by
C_{b}(X)
we denote its subset consisting of all bounded functions. If
X
is a compact space, then we endow
C(X)
with the supremum norm, which makes it a Banach space. Recall that a subspace
Y
of
X
is
C*-embedded
if every
f\in C_{b}(Y)
extends to some
f^{\prime}\in C_{b}(X)
.

A point
x
in
X
is a
P-point
if for every sequence
\big\langle U_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of open neighborhoods of
x
in
X
the intersection
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}U_{n}
has non-empty interior in
X
.

By
\beta X
we denote the Čech–Stone compactification of
X
, i.e. a unique (up to equivalence) compactification
K
of
X
satisfying the following property: for every
A,B\subseteq K
such that
A\subseteq f^{-1}(0)
and
B\subseteq f^{-1}(1)
for some
f\in C(X)
we have
\overline{A}^{K}\cap\overline{B}^{K}=\emptyset
(see
[
27
, page 167 and Corollary 3.6.2]
). For
X=\omega
we additionally set
\omega^{*}=(\beta\omega)\setminus\omega
.

A function
\mu\colon Bor(X)\to\mathbb{R}
is a
measure
on
X
if
\mu
is
\sigma
-additive, regular, and
bounded
, i.e. for the variation

|\mu|(Y)=\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|\mu(B)|\colon\ A,B\in Bor(X),\ A\cap B=\emptyset,\ A\cup B\subseteq Y\big\},

where
Y\in Bor(K)
, we have
\|\mu\|=|\mu|(X)<\infty
. The vector space of all measures on
X
endowed with the variation norm will be denoted by
M(X)
.

For a point
x\in X
by
\delta_{x}
we denote the one-point measure concentrated at
x
. A measure
\mu\in M(X)
is said to be
finitely supported
if there are a finite subset
F\subseteq X
and non-zero real numbers
\alpha_{x}
,
x\in F
, such that
\mu=\sum_{x\in F}\alpha_{x}\delta_{x}
; note that in this case we have
\|\mu\|=\sum_{x\in F}\big|\alpha_{x}\big|
.

Let
K
be a compact space. Notice that
M(K)
is a Banach space. Recall that by the classical Riesz–Markov–Kakutani Representation Theorem
M(K)
is isometrically isomorphic to the dual Banach space
C(K)^{*}
.

For each
\mu\in M(K)
by
\supp(\mu)
we denote the
support
of
\mu
, i.e. the smallest closed subset
F
of
K
such that
|\mu|(F)=\|\mu\|
. Note that if
\mu
is finitely supported, then
\supp(\mu)=F
, where
F
is as in the definition of a finitely supported measure. A sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
K
is
disjointly supported
if
\supp\big(\mu_{n}\big)\cap\supp\big(\mu_{n^{\prime}}\big)=\emptyset
for every
n\neq n^{\prime}\in\omega
. Similarly, a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
on a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
is
disjointly supported
if the sequence
\big\langle\widehat{\mu}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of the Radon extensions on
St(\mathcal{A})
is disjointly supported.

For a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
, by
St(\mathcal{A})
we denote the Stone space of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e. the compact (Hausdorff) space of all ultrafilters on
\mathcal{A}
. For every
A\in\mathcal{A}
the corresponding clopen subset of
St(\mathcal{A})
is denoted by
\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
. Recall that the mapping
\mathcal{A}\ni A\mapsto\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\in Clopen(St(\mathcal{A}))
is an isomorphism between the Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
Clopen(St(\mathcal{A}))
. Note that for every
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{A})
there is a unique measure
\widehat{\mu}\in M(St(\mathcal{A}))
such that
\mu(A)=\widehat{\mu}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
\|\mu\|=\|\widehat{\mu}\|
. Similarly, every
\mu\in M(St(\mathcal{A}))
induces a measure
\check{\mu}\in\ba(\mathcal{A})
by setting
\check{\mu}(A)=\mu\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
; we also have
\|\mu\|=\|\check{\mu}\|
. Consequently, the Banach spaces
\ba(\mathcal{A})
and
M(St(\mathcal{A}))
are isometrically isomorphic.

The Boolean algebra
{\wp(\omega)}
and its subalgebras of the form
{\wp(\omega)}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
for ideals
\mathcal{I}
will be of our special interest. For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
(resp. filter
\mathcal{F}
) in
{\wp(\omega)}
, let us briefly say that
\mathcal{I}
is an
ideal on
\omega
(resp.
filter on
\omega
). Similarly, an element
\mu
of
\ba({\wp(\omega)})
is also called a
measure on
\omega
.

The
Fréchet
filter on
\omega
is
Fr=\{A\subseteq\omega\colon|\omega\setminus A|<\infty\}
; we have
Fr^{*}=Fin
. A filter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
is
free
if
\bigcap\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
, or, equivalenty, if
Fr\subseteq\mathcal{F}
. A filter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
is a
P-filter
if for every sequence
\big\langle A_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of sets from
\mathcal{F}
there is
A\in\mathcal{F}
such that
A\setminus A_{n}
is finite for each
n\in\omega
; the ideal on
\omega
dual to a P-filter is a
P-ideal
. A
P-point
(on
\omega
) is a P-filter which is maximal in the sense of inclusion; of course, the notion of P-points on
\omega
coincides with the notion of P-points in the topological space
\omega^{*}
.

The Katětov order
\leq_{K}
is an important tool for comparing the structural complexity of ideals on
\omega
, see e.g.
[
50
]
. For two ideals
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
on
\omega
, we say that
\mathcal{I}
is
Katětov below

\mathcal{J}
, denoted
\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}
, if there is a function
f\colon\omega\to\omega
such that
f^{-1}[A]\in\mathcal{J}
for all
A\in\mathcal{I}
; if there is no such function
f
, then we say that
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
are
Katětov incomparable
. Also, two ideals
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
on
\omega
are
isomorphic
if there is a bijection
f\colon\omega\to\omega
such that for every
A\in{\wp(\omega)}
we have
A\in\mathcal{I}
if and only if
f[A]\in\mathcal{J}
, and they are
Katětov equivalent
, denoted
\mathcal{I}\equiv_{K}\mathcal{J}
, if
\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{J}\leq_{K}\mathcal{I}
. Of course, those definitions translate in the natural way via complements to filters on
\omega
.

Recall that every subset of
\omega
can be naturally identified with a point in the Cantor space
2^{\omega}
and hence subsets of
{\wp(\omega)}
correspond in a natural way to subsets of
2^{\omega}
. Consequently, when considering subsets of
{\wp(\omega)}
we may investigate their Borel complexity, analyticity, measurability (in the sense of the product measure), etc.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a ring of subsets of a set
S
. A function
\mu\colon\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
measure
on
\mathcal{R}
if
\mu
is additive and
bounded
, i.e. for the variation norm

\|\mu\|=\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|\mu(B)|\colon\ A,B\in\mathcal{R},\ A\cap B=\emptyset\big\}

we have
\|\mu\|<\infty
. By
\ba(\mathcal{R})
we denote the Banach space of all measures on
\mathcal{R}
endowed with the variation norm.

For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
in a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
set
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})=\big\{\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\colon A\in\mathcal{I}\big\}
and note that
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
is a ring of clopen subsets of
St(\mathcal{A})
. Similarly as above, every
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{I})
induces a measure
\mathring{\mu}\in\ba(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}))
with
\|\mu\|=\|\mathring{\mu}\|
by setting
\mathring{\mu}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\mu(A)
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
, and every
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}))
induces a measure
\tilde{\mu}\in\ba(\mathcal{I})
with
\|\mu\|=\|\tilde{\mu}\|
by setting
\tilde{\mu}(A)=\mu\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
. Consequently, the Banach spaces
\ba(\mathcal{I})
and
\ba(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}))
are isometrically isomorphic.

Recall that a subset
A
of a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
norm bounded
if
\sup_{x\in A}\|x\|<\infty
. Given two Banach spaces
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
and
(F,\|\cdot\|_{F})
, we will use the following standard norms on the direct sum
E\oplus F
:
\|(x,y)\|_{1}=\|x\|_{E}+\|y\|_{F}
and
\|(x,y)\|_{\infty}=\max(\|x\|_{E},\|y\|_{F})
(for
x\in E
,
y\in F
). We assume by default that
E\oplus F
is endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
. The norm
\|T\|
of a bounded linear operator
T\colon E\to F
is defined as
\|T\|=\sup\{\|T(x)\|\colon\|x\|\leq 1\}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a ring of subsets of a set
S
,
\mathcal{A}
a Boolean algebra, and
\mathcal{I}
an ideal in
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\mathcal{X}\in\{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{I}\}
. A sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures in
\ba(\mathcal{X})
is
pointwise convergent
to some measure
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{X})
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=\mu(A)
for every
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is
weakly convergent
to
\mu
if it is convergent in the weak topology
\sigma(\ba(\mathcal{X}),\ba(\mathcal{X})^{*})
on
\ba(\mathcal{X})
. Moreover, a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures in
\ba(\mathcal{A})
is
weak* convergent
to
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{A})
if the sequence
\big\langle\widehat{\mu}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is weak* convergent to
\widehat{\mu}
in the weak* topology
\sigma\big(M(St(\mathcal{A})),C(St(\mathcal{A}))\big)
on
M(St(\mathcal{A}))
. Note that, by the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\ba(\mathcal{A})
is weak* convergent to
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{A})
if and only if
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is norm bounded and pointwise convergent to
\mu
, and, by
[
17
, Theorem 11, page 90]
,
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is weakly convergent to
\mu
if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{\mu}_{n}(B)=\widehat{\mu}(B)
for every
B\in Bor(St(\mathcal{A}))
.

3.
Concentration points and modifications of anti-Nikodym sequences

It is well known that if the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
of a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
contains a non-eventually constant convergent sequence, then
\mathcal{A}
has neither the Nikodym property, nor the Grothendieck property (cf. Example
3.8
below). The goal of this section is to generalize this observation by studying properties of special sequences of measures on Boolean algebras, which witness the lack of the Nikodym property, and by showing that their existence implies the lack of the Grothendieck property, too.

For the rest of this section
\mathcal{A}
will always stand for an infinite Boolean algebra. We start with the following alike definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
St(\mathcal{A})
is
anti-Nikodym
if we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=0
for every
A\in Clopen(St(\mathcal{A}))
and
\sup_{n\in\omega}\|\mu_{n}\|=\infty
.

A sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{A}
is
anti-Nikodym
if the sequence
\big\langle\widehat{\mu}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of the corresponding measures on
St(\mathcal{A})
is anti-Nikodym.

Definition 3.2
.

A sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{A}
is
anti-Grothendieck
if it is weak* convergent to
0
but not weakly convergent.

Trivially by definition,
\mathcal{A}
has the Nikodym property if and only if there are no anti-Nikodym sequences on
\mathcal{A}
, and, similarly,
\mathcal{A}
has the Grothendieck property if and only if there are no anti-Grothendieck sequences on
\mathcal{A}
.

Anti-Nikodym sequences of measures have a very important property, namely, they “concentrate” around points in the Stone spaces in a sense explained by the following lemma and definition.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
. If
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is a sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
such that
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|=\infty
, then there is
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
such that for every
B\in\mathcal{A}
with
t\in\left[B\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
we have
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction B\big\|=\infty
.

Proof.

Assume that for every
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
there is
B_{t}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
t\in\left[B_{t}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction B_{t}\big\|<\infty
. By the compactness of
\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
there are
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
such that
\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\left[B_{t_{i}}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
. We have

\infty=\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|\leq\sup_{n\in\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction B_{t_{i}}\big\|<\infty,

which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma
3.3
justifies introducing the following notion, already studied in
[
74
]
and
[
75
]
(see also
[
3
]
).

Definition 3.4
.

If
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
, then a point
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
is its
Nikodym concentration point
if for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
with
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
we have
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|=\infty
.

In short, Lemma
3.3
asserts that every anti-Nikodym sequence of measures has a Nikodym concentration point.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
and
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
is its Nikodym concentration point, then for every
\alpha>0
and
A\in\mathcal{A}
with
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
there are
n\in\omega
and
B\leq A
such that
t\not\in\left[B\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\big|\mu_{n}(B)\big|>\alpha
.

Proof.

Since
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|=\infty
, there are
C\leq A
and
n\in\omega
such that

\big|\mu_{n}(C)\big|>\alpha+\sup_{m\in\omega}\big|\mu_{m}(A)\big|.

If
t\not\in\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
, then let
B=C
. Otherwise,
t\not\in\left[A\setminus C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
and we have

\big|\mu_{n}(A\setminus C)\big|=\big|\mu_{n}(A)-\mu_{n}(C)\big|\geq\big|\mu_{n}(C)\big|-\big|\mu_{n}(A)\big|>

>\alpha+\sup_{m\in\omega}\big|\mu_{m}(A)\big|-\big|\mu_{n}(A)\big|\geq\alpha,

so set
B=A\setminus C
.
∎

An inductive application of Lemma
3.5
yields the following standard corollary.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
, then there exist an antichain
\big\langle A_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\mathcal{A}
and a strictly increasing sequence
\big\langle n_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
such that
\big|\mu_{n_{k}}\big(A_{k}\big)\big|>k
for every
k\in\omega
.

To establish a new criterion for a Boolean algebra without the Nikodym property to not have the Grothendieck property either, we will need however the following stronger notion of Nikodym concentration points.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
. If
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
has the property that for every
N\in\omega
and
A\in\mathcal{A}
with
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
there are a clopen set
U\subseteq\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}
and
n\in\omega
such that

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n}(U)\big|>N\cdot\Big(\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A^{c}\big\|+1\Big),

then
t
is a
strong Nikodym concentration point
of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
.

It is easy to see that every strong Nikodym concentration point is a Nikodym concentration point. The converse is however not true, see Example
3.10
below.

Example 3.8
.

Assume that
\big\langle x_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is a non-eventually constant sequence in the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
which converges to some
x\in St(\mathcal{A})
. Then, the point
x
is a strong Nikodym concentration point of the anti-Nikodym sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{A}
, defined for every
n\in\omega
via their Radon extensions as follows:

\widehat{\mu}_{n}=n\big(\delta_{x_{n}}-\delta_{x}\big).

(Note that the sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}/n\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is anti-Grothendieck on
\mathcal{A}
). See also Proposition
5.24
and Corollary
5.25
for similar constructions.

Example 3.9
.

Expanding Example
3.8
, we can easily get an example of an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures with infinitely many strong Nikodym concentration points. Indeed, assume that
\mathcal{A}
contains an antichain
\big\langle A_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
such that for each
k\in\omega
there is a point
t_{k}\in\left[A_{k}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
being the limit of a non-eventually constant sequence
\big\langle x_{k,n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
contained in
\left[A_{k}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
a\colon\omega\to\omega\times\omega
be a bijection; write
a=(b,c)
, where
b,c\colon\omega\to\omega
. For each
n\in\omega
define the measure
\mu_{n}
on
\mathcal{A}
via its Radon extension as follows:

\widehat{\mu}_{n}=n\Big(\delta_{x_{b(n),c(n)}}-\delta_{t_{b(n)}}\Big).

It follows that each point
t_{k}
is a strong Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
.

Example 3.10
.

Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be the sequence of measures on
Clopen(2^{\omega})
defined in the proof of
[
74
, Proposition 4.6]
. Then, every
t\in 2^{\omega}
is a Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
, but
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
has no strong Nikodym concentration points. To see this, let
t\in 2^{\omega}
be arbitrary and set

V=\{x\in 2^{\omega}\colon x(0)=t(0)\}.

For every
n\in\omega
we have

\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction V\big\|=\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction V^{c}\big\|=2^{n}.

Therefore, for every clopen set
U\subseteq V\setminus\{t\}
and
n\in\omega
it holds

\big|\mu_{n}(U)\big|\leq\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction V^{c}\big\|+1,

and so
t
is not a strong Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
.

Example 3.11
.

Lemma
3.13
below asserts that if an anti-Nikodym sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
on a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has a unique Nikodym concentration point
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
, then
t
must be a strong Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
. In Example
5.23
we describe a large class
\mathscr{C}
of Boolean subalgebras of
{\wp(\omega)}
carrying only anti-Nikodym sequences with single Nikodym concentration points and therefore carrying only anti-Nikodym sequences with strong Nikodym concentration points. The simplest element of
\mathscr{C}
is the Boolean algebra

\big\{A\in{\wp(\omega)}\colon\ |A|<\infty\text{ or }|A^{c}|<\infty\big\},

generated by the ideal
Fin
of finite subsets of
\omega
, whose Stone space is homeomorphic to the space
\{1/(n+1)\colon n\in\omega\}\cup\{0\}
endowed with the standard topology.

Example 3.12
.

Examples
3.8
and
3.10
show that the Boolean algebra
Clopen(2^{\omega})
carries anti-Nikodym sequences with strong Nikodym concentration points as well as anti-Nikodym sequences without such points.
Theorem
F
, on the other hand, implies that if a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the Grothendieck property but not the Nikodym property, then no anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
can have a strong Nikodym concentration point. Such Boolean algebras have been however so far constructed only consistently, e.g. under the Continuum Hypothesis (Talagrand
[
83
]
) or Martin’s axiom (Sobota and Zdomskyy
[
78
]
); cf. Question
8.2
.

The following lemma (aforementioned in Example
3.11
) provides a handy criterion for Nikodym concentration points to be strong.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
. Let
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
be the only Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
. Then,
t
is a strong Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
.

Proof.

Fix
N\in\omega
and
A\in\mathcal{A}
such that
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
. Since
t\not\in\left[A^{c}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
has only
t
as its Nikodym concentration point, Lemma
3.3
implies that
\sup_{m\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{m}\restriction A^{c}\big\|<\infty
. As
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|=\infty
, by Lemma
3.5
we get a clopen
U\subseteq\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}
and
n\in\omega
such that

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n}(U)\big|>N\cdot\Big(\sup_{m\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{m}\restriction A^{c}\big\|+1\Big)\geq N\cdot\Big(\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A^{c}\big\|+1\Big),

which proves that
t
is a strong Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
.
∎

Corollary 3.14
.

Assume that
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
and
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
is its Nikodym concentration point such that for some
A\in t
there is no Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}
. Then,
\big\langle\mu_{n}\restriction A\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence on
\mathcal{A}
with a unique strong Nikodym concentration point.

In the following series of lemmas we will show that if a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
carries an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures with a strong Nikodym concentration points, then
\mathcal{A}
carries an anti-Nikodym sequences of measures with pairwise disjoint supports (see Theorem
3.18
). We start with the following standard result.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
\mu
be a measure on
\mathcal{A}
. For every
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
there is
A\in\mathcal{A}
such that
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
and
|\widehat{\mu}|\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}\big)<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Assume not, that is, there are
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
such that for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
with
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
we have
|\widehat{\mu}|\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}\big)\geq\varepsilon
. Let
N\in\omega
be such that
N\cdot\varepsilon/2>\|\widehat{\mu}\|
. By the regularity of
|\widehat{\mu}|
, there is an antichain
A_{1},\ldots,A_{N}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
t\not\in\left[A_{i}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
and
|\widehat{\mu}|\big(\left[A_{i}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)>\varepsilon/2
for
i=1,\ldots,N
. Then,

\|\widehat{\mu}\|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\widehat{\mu}|\big(\left[A_{i}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)>N\cdot\varepsilon/2>\|\widehat{\mu}\|,

a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
and
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
its strong Nikodym concentration point. Let
\big\langle N_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a sequence of positive real numbers. Then, there are an anti-Nikodym sequence
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{A}
and an antichain
\big\langle B_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\mathcal{A}
such that for every
n\in\omega
we have:

•

t\not\in\left[B_{n}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
,

•

\supp(\widehat{\nu}_{n})\subseteq\left[B_{n}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cup\{t\}
,

•

\big\|\widehat{\nu}_{n}\restriction St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big\|>N_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\big\langle M_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be an increasing sequence of positive real numbers such that
M_{0}>1
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}N_{n}\cdot M_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}M_{n}=\infty.

Put
A_{0}=1_{\mathcal{A}}
. Since
t
is a strong Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
, there are
n_{0}\in\omega
and a clopen set
U_{0}\subseteq\left[A_{0}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}
such that

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{0}}\big(U_{0}\big)\big|>N_{0}\cdot M_{0}^{2}.

By Lemma
3.15
, there is
A_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
with
t\in\left[A_{1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\left[A_{0}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus U_{0}
and such that

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{0}}\big|\big(\left[A_{1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}\big)<1.

Similarly, there are
n_{1}>n_{0}
and a clopen set
U_{1}\subseteq\left[A_{1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}
such that

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{1}}\big(U_{1}\big)\big|>N_{1}\cdot M_{1}^{2}\cdot\Big(\big\|\mu_{n_{1}}\restriction A_{1}^{c}\big\|+1\Big).

By Lemma
3.15
, there is
A_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
with
t\in\left[A_{2}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\left[A_{1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus U_{1}
and such that

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{1}}\big|\big(\left[A_{2}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}\big)<1/2.

Again, there are
n_{2}>n_{1}
and a clopen set
U_{2}\subseteq\left[A_{2}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}
such that

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{2}}\big(U_{2}\big)\big|>N_{2}\cdot M_{2}^{2}\cdot\Big(\big\|\mu_{n_{2}}\restriction A_{2}^{c}\big\|+1\Big).

By Lemma
3.15
, there is
A_{3}\in\mathcal{A}
with
t\in\left[A_{3}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\left[A_{2}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus U_{2}
and such that

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{2}}\big|\big(\left[A_{3}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}\big)<1/3.

We continue in this manner until we get a descending sequence
\big\langle A_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\mathcal{A}
, a sequence
\big\langle U_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
of pairwise disjoint clopen subsets of
St(\mathcal{A})
, and a strictly increasing sequence
\big\langle n_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
of natural numbers such that for every
k\in\omega
we have:

•

t\in\left[A_{k}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
,

•

U_{k}\subseteq\left[A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
,

•

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{k}}\big(U_{k}\big)\big|>N_{k}\cdot M_{k}^{2}\cdot\Big(\big\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction A_{k}^{c}\big\|+1\Big)
,

•

\big\|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{k}}\restriction\big(\left[A_{k+1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}\big)\big\|<1/(k+1)
.

We will define the desired sequence
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
in two steps. First, for every
k\in\omega
put

\theta_{k}=\mu_{n_{k}}\big/\big(M_{k}\cdot\big\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction A_{k}^{c}\big\|+M_{k}\big).

We claim that
\big\langle\theta_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is anti-Nikodym. Indeed, for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
k\in\omega
we have

\big|\theta_{k}(A)\big|=\big|\mu_{n_{k}}(A)\big|\big/\big(M_{k}\cdot\big\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction A_{k}^{c}\big\|+M_{k}\big)\leq\big|\mu_{n_{k}}(A)\big|,

as
M_{k}>1
, and hence
\lim_{k\to\infty}\big|\theta_{k}(A)\big|=0
,
which proves that
\big\langle\theta_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is pointwise convergent to
0
. For every
k\in\omega
it also holds

\big\|\theta_{k}\big\|\geq\big\|\theta_{k}\restriction\big(A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}\big)\big\|\geq\big|\widehat{\theta}_{k}\big(U_{k}\big)\big|=

=\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{k}}\big(U_{k}\big)\big|\big/\big(M_{k}\cdot\big\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction A_{k}^{c}\big\|+M_{k}\big)>N_{k}\cdot M_{k},

so
\big\langle\theta_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is not norm bounded by (
*
). Similarly, for every
k\in\omega
we also have

\big|\theta_{k}\big(A\wedge A_{k}^{c}\big)\big|\leq\big|\mu_{n_{k}}\big(A\wedge A_{k}^{c}\big)\big|\big/\big(M_{k}\cdot\big\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction A_{k}^{c}\big\|+M_{k}\big)\leq

\leq\big\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction A_{k}^{c}\big\|\big/\big(M_{k}\cdot\big\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction A_{k}^{c}\big\|+M_{k}\big)\leq 1/M_{k}.

Second, for every
k\in\omega
define
\nu_{k}
via its Radon extension
\widehat{\nu}_{k}
on
St(\mathcal{A})
as follows:

\widehat{\nu}_{k}=\widehat{\theta}_{k}\restriction\big(\left[A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cup\{t\}\big).

It follows immediately that for every
k\neq l\in\omega
we have

\supp\big(\nu_{k}\big)\cap\supp\big(\nu_{l}\big)\subseteq\{t\},

so for every
k\in\omega
put
B_{k}=A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}
. We need to show that
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is anti-Nikodym. Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
k\in\omega
. Having in mind that
A_{k+1}\leq A_{k}
and
t\in\left[A_{k+1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
, we get

\big|\nu_{k}(A)\big|=\big|\theta_{k}\big(A\wedge\big(A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}\big)\big)+\widehat{\theta}_{k}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\{t\}\big)\big|=

=\big|\theta_{k}(A)-\theta_{k}\big(A\setminus\big(A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}\big)\big)+\widehat{\theta}_{k}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\{t\}\big)\big|=

=\big|\theta_{k}(A)-\theta_{k}\big(A\setminus A_{k}\big)-\theta_{k}\big(A\wedge A_{k+1}\big)+\widehat{\theta}_{k}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\{t\}\big)\big|=

=\big|\theta_{k}(A)-\theta_{k}\big(A\wedge A_{k}^{c}\big)-\widehat{\theta}_{k}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\left[A_{k+1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\widehat{\theta}_{k}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\{t\}\big)\big|=

=\big|\theta_{k}(A)-\theta_{k}\big(A\wedge A_{k}^{c}\big)-\widehat{\theta}_{k}\Big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\big(\left[A_{k+1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}\big)\Big)\big|\leq

\leq\big|\theta_{k}(A)\big|+\big|\theta_{k}\big(A\wedge A_{k}^{c}\big)\big|+\big|\widehat{\theta}_{k}\Big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\big(\left[A_{k+1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}\big)\Big)\big|<

<\big|\theta_{k}(A)\big|+1/{M_{k}}+1/(k+1),

where the last inequality follows from (
*\!*\!*
). Consequently,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\big|\nu_{k}(A)\big|=0
,
so
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is pointwise convergent to
0
. To prove that
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is not norm bounded, take
k\in\omega
and note that by (
**
) we have

\big\|\nu_{k}\big\|=\big\|\widehat{\theta}_{k}\restriction\big(\left[A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cup\{t\}\big)\big\|\geq\big\|\theta_{k}\restriction\big(A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}\big)\big\|>N_{k}\cdot M_{k},

which implies that
\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\nu_{k}\big\|=\infty
. Besides, for every
k\in\omega
we have

\big\|\widehat{\nu}_{k}\restriction St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big\|=\big\|\nu_{k}\restriction\big(A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}\big)\big\|>N_{k},

which finishes the proof.
∎

The proof of the following lemma follows closely Step
2
of the proof of
[
88
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
\big\langle N_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}N_{n}=\infty
. Let
\big\langle\rho_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
,
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
, and
\big\langle B_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
an antichain in
\mathcal{A}
such that for every
n\in\omega
we have:

(1)

t\not\in\left[B_{n}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
,

(2)

\supp(\widehat{\rho}_{n})\subseteq\left[B_{n}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cup\{t\}
,

(3)

\big\|\widehat{\rho}_{n}\restriction St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big\|>N_{n}
.

Then, there are an anti-Nikodym sequence
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{A}
and a strictly increasing sequence
\big\langle n_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
such that for every
k\in\omega
we have:

(1)

\supp(\widehat{\nu}_{k})\subseteq\left[B_{n_{2k}}\vee B_{n_{2k+1}}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
,

(2)

\big\|\nu_{k}\big\|>N_{n_{2k}}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two cases.

Case 1.

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\big|\widehat{\rho}_{n}(\{t\})\big|<\infty
.

We find a subsequence
\big\langle\rho_{n_{k}}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\widehat{\rho}_{n_{k}}(\{t\})\to\alpha
, so in particular
\big\langle\widehat{\rho}_{n_{k}}(\{t\})\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is a Cauchy sequence. By (c) the sequence
\big\langle\rho_{n_{k}}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is anti-Nikodym on
\mathcal{A}
. For every
k\in\omega
we define
\nu_{k}
via its Radon extension
\widehat{\nu}_{k}
on
St(\mathcal{A})
as follows:

\widehat{\nu}_{k}=\big(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}-\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k+1}}\big)\restriction\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big).

For every
k\in\omega
by (b) we have

\supp(\widehat{\nu}_{k})\subseteq\big(\supp(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}})\cup\supp(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k+1}})\big)\setminus\{t\}\subseteq\left[B_{n_{2k}}\vee B_{n_{2k+1}}\right]_{\mathcal{A}},

so (A) holds. Since
\big\|\widehat{\rho}_{n}\restriction St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big\|>N_{n}
for every
n\in\omega
and
\big\langle B_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an antichain in
\mathcal{A}
, we get that

\big\|\nu_{k}\big\|=\big\|\widehat{\nu}_{k}\big\|>N_{n_{2k}}+N_{n_{2k+1}}>N_{n_{2k}}

for all
k\in\omega
, so (B) holds as well.

We need to prove that
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is anti-Nikodym on
\mathcal{A}
. Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
. For any
l\in\omega
we have

\nu_{l}(A)=\widehat{\nu}_{l}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\big(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l}}-\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l+1}}\big)\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}\big)=

=\big(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l}}-\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l+1}}\big)\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)-\big(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l}}-\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l+1}}\big)\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\{t\}\big)=

=\big({\rho}_{n_{2l}}(A)-{\rho}_{n_{2l+1}}(A)\big)-\Big(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l}}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\{t\}\big)-\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l+1}}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\{t\}\big)\Big).

As
\big\langle\rho_{n_{k}}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is pointwise convergent to
0
and also

\lim_{k\to\infty}\Big(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}(\{t\})-\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k+1}}(\{t\})\Big)=0

(recall that
\big\langle\widehat{\rho}_{n_{k}}(\{t\})\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is Cauchy), we get that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\nu_{k}(A)=0
. It follows that
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is pointwise convergent to
0
, too.

Next, we have

\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{k}}\restriction\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big)\big\|=\infty,

since

\sup_{k\in\omega}\big|\rho_{n_{k}}\big|=\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{k}}\big\|=\infty

and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\big\|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{k}}\restriction\{t\}\big\|\to|\alpha|\in\mathbb{R}.

Therefore, as
B_{n_{2k}}\wedge B_{n_{2k+1}}=0_{\mathcal{A}}
for every
k\in\omega
, we get

\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\nu_{k}\|=\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\widehat{\nu}_{k}\big\|=\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}\restriction\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big)\big\|+\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k+1}}\restriction\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big)\big\|=\infty,

so
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is not norm bounded.

Case 2.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\widehat{\rho}_{n}(\{t\})\big|=\infty
.

We find a subsequence
\big\langle\widehat{\rho}_{n_{k}}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
such that
\big\langle\big|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{k}}(\{t\})\big|\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is strictly increasing and
\big|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{0}}(\{t\})\big|>0
. By (c) the sequence
\big\langle\rho_{n_{2k}}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is still anti-Nikodym. For each
k\in\omega
we denote

\alpha_{k}=\frac{\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}(\{t\})}{\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k+1}}(\{t\})},

so
|\alpha_{k}|<1
. For every
k\in\omega
we define
\nu_{k}
via its Radon extension
\widehat{\nu}_{k}
on
St(\mathcal{A})
as follows:

\widehat{\nu}_{k}=\big(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}-\alpha_{k}\cdot\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k+1}}\big)\restriction\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big).

Again, as previously, for every
k\in\omega
we have

\supp(\widehat{\nu}_{k})\subseteq\big(\supp(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}})\cup\supp(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k+1}})\big)\setminus\{t\}\subseteq\left[B_{n_{2k}}\vee B_{n_{2k+1}}\right]_{\mathcal{A}},

so (A) holds.

Since
\big\|\widehat{\rho}_{n}\restriction St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big\|>N_{n}
for every
n\in\omega
and
\big\langle B_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an antichain in
\mathcal{A}
, we get that

\big\|\nu_{k}\big\|=\big\|\widehat{\nu}_{k}\big\|>N_{n_{2k}}+|\alpha_{k}|\cdot N_{n_{2k+1}}\geq N_{n_{2k}}

for all
k\in\omega
.

We again need to prove that
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence on
\mathcal{A}
. Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
. For every
l\in\omega
we have

\nu_{l}(A)=\widehat{\nu}_{l}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\big(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l}}-\alpha_{l}\cdot\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l+1}}\big)\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}\big)=

=\big(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l}}-\alpha_{l}\cdot\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l+1}}\big)\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)-\big(\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l}}-\alpha_{l}\cdot\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l+1}}\big)\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap\{t\}\big).

As

\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l}}(\{t\})-\alpha_{l}\cdot\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2l+1}}(\{t\})=0

for every
l\in\omega
,
\big\langle\rho_{n_{k}}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence, and
\big\langle\alpha_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is bounded by
1
, we get that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\nu_{k}(A)=0
. Consequently,
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is pointwise convergent to
0
.

Next, we have

\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}\restriction\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big)\big\|\geq\sup_{k\in\omega}\big|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big)\big|=\sup_{k\in\omega}\big|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}(St(\mathcal{A}))-\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}(\{t\})\big|=\infty,

which follows from the equalities

\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}(St(\mathcal{A}))=\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho_{n_{2k}}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=0

(recall that
\big\langle\rho_{n_{2k}}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is pointwise convergent to
0
) and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\widehat{\rho}_{n}(\{t\})\big|=\infty
.
Therefore, as
B_{n_{2k}}\wedge B_{n_{2k+1}}=0_{\mathcal{A}}
for every
k\in\omega
, we get

\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\nu_{k}\big\|=\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\widehat{\nu}_{k}\big\|\geq\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\widehat{\rho}_{n_{2k}}\restriction\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{t\}\big)\big\|=\infty,

hence
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is not norm bounded.
∎

of measures on

(with

3.1.
Strong Nikodym concentration points and the Grothendieck property

In this short subsection we will quickly show how the existence of anti-Nikodym sequences of measures on a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
with strong Nikodym concentration points implies that
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Grothendieck property. We start with the following folklore proposition (cf.
[
53
, Theorem 19.3.5]
).

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be an anti-Nikodym sequence of non-zero measures on
\mathcal{A}
and
\big\langle B_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
such an antichain in
\mathcal{A}
that
\supp\big(\widehat{\mu}_{n}\big)\subseteq\left[B_{n}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
for every
n\in\omega
. Then, the sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}/\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of normalized measures is an anti-Grothendieck sequence on
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

For every
n\in\omega
let
\nu_{n}=\mu_{n}/\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|
, so
\big\|\nu_{n}\big\|=1
. For every
A\in\mathcal{A}
and sufficiently large
n\in\omega
we have

\big|\nu_{n}(A)\big|=\big|\mu_{n}(A)\big|/\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|\leq\big|\mu_{n}(A)\big|,

so
\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{n}(A)=0
. Consequently,
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is also pointwise convergent to
0
and hence weak* convergent to
0
.

We claim that
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is not weakly convergent to
0
. For every
n\in\omega
, since
\big\|\nu_{n}\big\|=1
and
\supp\big(\widehat{\nu}_{n}\big)\subseteq\left[B_{n}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
, there is a clopen set
U_{n}\subseteq\left[B_{n}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
such that

\big|\widehat{\nu}_{n}\big(U_{n}\big)\big|>1/3.

Set

B=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}U_{n}.

Then,
B
is an open set, so Borel. For each
n\in\omega
, since

B\cap\supp\big(\widehat{\nu}_{n}\big)=U_{n}\cap\supp\big(\widehat{\nu}_{n}\big)\subseteq\left[B_{n}\right]_{\mathcal{A}},

we have
\big|\widehat{\nu}_{n}(B)\big|>1/3
, and hence
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is not weakly convergent to
0
. It follows that
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Grothendieck sequence on
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

The following proposition generalizes the aforementioned well-known fact asserting that if a totally disconnected compact space
K
contains a non-eventually constant convergent sequence, then
Clopen(K)
does not have the Nikodym property nor the Grothendieck property (cf. Example
3.8
).

Proposition 3.20
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
carries a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures which is pointwise convergent to
0
and there is a point
t\in St(\mathcal{A})
such that for every
N\in\omega
and
A\in\mathcal{A}
with
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
there are a clopen
U\subseteq\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{t\}
and
n\in\omega
for which we have

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n}(U)\big|>N\cdot\Big(\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A^{c}\big\|+1\Big).

Then,
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Nikodym property nor the Grothendieck property.

Proof.

\mathcal{A}
does not have the Nikodym property, since
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is by definition anti-Nikodym. Moreover, by Definition
3.7
,
t
is its strong Nikodym concentration point.

From
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
, by Theorem
3.18
, we obtain a new anti-Nikodym sequence
\big\langle\nu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
on
\mathcal{A}
and an antichain
\big\langle B_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\mathcal{A}
such that for every
k\in\omega
we have
\supp\big(\widehat{\nu}_{k}\big)\subseteq\left[B_{k}\right]_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\big\|\nu_{k}\big\|>k
. Proposition
3.19
implies that the sequence
\big\langle\nu_{k}/\big\|\nu_{k}\big\|\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is anti-Grothendieck and so that
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Grothendieck property.
∎

Proposition
3.20
now easily yields
Theorem
F
from Introduction, thus providing a criterion for a Boolean algebra without the Nikodym property to not have the Grothendieck property.

We finish this section with the following important consequence of Proposition
3.20
(or Theorem
F
); cf. Corollary
4.12
below.

Corollary 3.21
.

If
\mathcal{A}
admits an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures having a unique Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
, then
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Grothendieck property.

4.
Boolean algebras generated by ideals

We start with the following definitions, analogous to Definitions
1.1
and
3.1
for Boolean algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

A ring
\mathcal{R}
of subsets of a set
X
has the
Nikodym property
if every sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\ba(\mathcal{R})
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{R}
is norm bounded.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a ring of subsets of a set
X
. A sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{R}
is
anti-Nikodym
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{R}
and
\sup_{n\in\omega}\|\mu_{n}\|=\infty
.

It immediately follows that a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the Nikodym property if and only if the ring
Clopen(St(\mathcal{A}))
of clopen subsets of
St(\mathcal{A})
has the Nikodym property. Also, a ring
\mathcal{R}
has the Nikodym property if and only if there are no anti-Nikodym sequences on
\mathcal{R}
.

Drewnowski and Paúl
[
25
, Remark 2.2.(a)]
observed that a ring
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\wp(X)
of subsets of a set
X
has the Nikodym property if and only if the Boolean subalgebra
\wp(X)\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{R}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
of
\wp(X)
generated by
\mathcal{R}
has the Nikodym property. For the “if” part they briefly noted that every measure
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{R})
can be easily extended to a measure
\nu\in\ba\big(\wp(X)\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{R}\mathclose{}\right\rangle\!\big)
, simply by setting
\nu(X\setminus A)=-\mu(A)
for every
A\in\mathcal{R}
.

We will expand the above observations in the next subsection by describing, for a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
and its ideal
\mathcal{I}
, an isomorphism between the Banach spaces
\ba(\mathcal{I})\oplus\mathbb{R}
and
\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
(Proposition
4.6
and Theorem
4.7
). Our approach is topological, that is, we study the values of the Radon extensions of measures from the space
\ba(\mathcal{I})
on the open subset of the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
corresponding to the ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

In Subsection
4.2
we will use results obtained in the previous sections to show that if a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the Nikodym property, but its subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
generated by an ideal
\mathcal{I}
does not, then
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
does not have the Grothendieck property either (Proposition
4.11
and Corollary
4.12
). We will also obtain a characterization of the Nikodym property for Boolean algebras of the form
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
in terms of sequences of non-negative measures (Theorem
4.14
).

4.1.
Extending measures from ideals to Boolean algebras

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Boolean algebra. Note that if
\mathcal{F}
is a filter in
\mathcal{A}
, then
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle=\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}^{*}
, where
\mathcal{F}^{*}=\{1_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus A\colon A\in\mathcal{F}\}
is the ideal in
\mathcal{A}
dual to
\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{F}
is an ultrafilter in
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
. It follows that
\mathcal{F}\in St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
, but in order to distinguish
\mathcal{F}
as a
filter
in
\mathcal{A}
from the
point
in
St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
we will denote the latter by
p_{\mathcal{F}}
. Of course, formally
\mathcal{F}=p_{\mathcal{F}}
. Note that
p_{\mathcal{F}}\not\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}^{*}
and that

St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)=\bigcup\big\{\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\colon\ A\in\mathcal{F}^{*}\big\}\cup\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}.

Consequently, since
\big\{\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\colon\ \mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}\big\}
is an increasing net of open sets (indexed by the directed set
(\mathcal{F}^{*},\leq)
, where
\leq
is the standard order on
\mathcal{A}
) and by the
\tau
-additivity of Radon measures (see
[
11
, Section 7.2]
), for every measure
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
we have

(
+
)

\displaystyle\|\mu\|

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{\mu}\|=\Big\|\widehat{\mu}\restriction\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{F}^{*}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big\|+\big|\widehat{\mu}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}\big)\big|=|\widehat{\mu}|\Big(\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{F}^{*}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big)+\big|\widehat{\mu}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}\big)\big|=

\displaystyle=\lim_{A\in\mathcal{F}^{*}}|\widehat{\mu}|\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\big)+\big|\widehat{\mu}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}\big)\big|=\lim_{A\in\mathcal{F}^{*}}|\mu|(A)+\big|\widehat{\mu}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}\big)\big|

and so, in the case of non-negative
\mu
,

\mu\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\lim_{A\in\mathcal{F}^{*}}\mu(A)+\widehat{\mu}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}\big).

The following lemma is a standard application of the regularity of Radon measures.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Boolean algebra and
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{A})
. For every Borel set
B
and open set
U
such that
B\subseteq U\subseteq St(\mathcal{A})
and every
\varepsilon>0
there is
C\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq U
and
|\widehat{\mu}(B)|\leq|\mu(C)|+\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
St(\mathcal{A})=P\cup N
be a Hahn decomposition of the space
St(\mathcal{A})
for the measure
\widehat{\mu}
such that
\widehat{\mu}(P\cap B)\geq 0
and
\widehat{\mu}(N\cap B)\leq 0
for every Borel set
B\subseteq St(\mathcal{A})
. Let
\widehat{\mu}=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}
be the Jordan decomposition of
\widehat{\mu}
into two non-negative Radon measures.

Fix a Borel set
B
and an open set
U
such that
B\subseteq U\subseteq St(\mathcal{A})
. Fix also
\varepsilon>0
. We may assume that
\mu^{+}(B)\geq\mu^{-}(B)
as the other case is symmetric. By the regularity of
\mu^{+}
there is a compact set
K\subseteq B\cap P
such that

\mu^{+}(B)\leq\mu^{+}(K)+\varepsilon/2.

By the compactness of
K
and the regularity of the variation
|\widehat{\mu}|
, there is
C\in\mathcal{A}
such that
K\subseteq\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq U
and

|\widehat{\mu}|\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\leq|\widehat{\mu}|(K)+\varepsilon/2=\mu^{+}(K)+\varepsilon/2.

Applying the above inequality, we get

\mu^{-}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=|\widehat{\mu}|\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)-\mu^{+}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\leq|\widehat{\mu}|\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)-\mu^{+}(K)\leq

\leq\mu^{+}(K)+\varepsilon/2-\mu^{+}(K)=\varepsilon/2,

and hence

-\mu^{-}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus B\big)\geq-\mu^{-}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\geq-\varepsilon/2.

Using (
*
) and (
**
), we have

|\widehat{\mu}(B)|=\big|\mu^{+}(B)-\mu^{-}(B)\big|=\mu^{+}(B)-\mu^{-}(B)\leq\mu^{+}(K)+\varepsilon/2-\mu^{-}(B)\leq

\leq\mu^{+}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\varepsilon/2-\mu^{-}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B\big)\leq\mu^{+}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\varepsilon-\mu^{-}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B\big)-\varepsilon/2\leq

\leq\mu^{+}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\varepsilon-\mu^{-}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B\big)-\mu^{-}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus B\big)=

=\mu^{+}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\varepsilon-\mu^{-}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\widehat{\mu}\big(\left[C\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\varepsilon=\mu(C)+\varepsilon\leq|\mu(C)|+\varepsilon,

and the proof of the lemma is finished.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
and
\nu\in\ba(\mathcal{I})
be such measures that
\mu
extends
\nu
. Then,

\Big\|\widehat{\mu}\restriction\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big\|=\|\nu\|.

Proof.

We have

\Big\|\widehat{\mu}\restriction\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big\|=|\widehat{\mu}|\Big(\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big)\geq

\geq\sup\big\{\big|\widehat{\mu}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\big)\big|+\big|\widehat{\mu}\big(\left[A^{\prime}\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\big)\big|\colon\ A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I},\ A\wedge A^{\prime}=0_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}=

=\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|\mu(A^{\prime})|\colon\ A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I},\ A\wedge A^{\prime}=0_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}=

=\sup\big\{|\nu(A)|+|\nu(A^{\prime})|\colon\ A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I},\ A\wedge A^{\prime}=0_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}=\|\nu\|.

For the reverse inequality, fix
\varepsilon>0
and note that by Lemma
4.3
we have

\Big\|\widehat{\mu}\restriction\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big\|=|\widehat{\mu}|\Big(\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big)=

=\sup\bigg\{|\widehat{\mu}(B)|+|\widehat{\mu}(B^{\prime})|\colon B,B^{\prime}\in Bor\Big(\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big),\ B\cap B^{\prime}=\emptyset\bigg\}\leq

\leq\sup\big\{|\mu(C)|+|\mu(C^{\prime})|\colon C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I},\ C\wedge C^{\prime}=0_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}+2\varepsilon=

=\sup\big\{|\nu(C)|+|\nu(C^{\prime})|\colon C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I},\ C\wedge C^{\prime}=0_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}+2\varepsilon=\|\nu\|+2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, we get

\Big\|\widehat{\mu}\restriction\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big\|\leq\|\nu\|,

which finishes the proof of the equality.
∎

Using the above results, we get the following decomposition of the norms of extensions.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
and
\nu\in\ba(\mathcal{I})
be such measures that
\mu
extends
\nu
. Then,

\|\mu\|=\|\nu\|+\big|\widehat{\mu}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big\}\big)\big|=\lim_{A\in\mathcal{I}}|\nu|(A)+\big|\widehat{\mu}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big\}\big)\big|.

Proof.

Combining equalities (
+
) with Lemma
4.4
, we get that
\|\mu\|=\|\nu\|+\big|\widehat{\mu}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big\}\big)\big|
. For every
A\in\mathcal{I}
we have
|\nu|(A)=|\mu|(A)
and so, again by (
+
),
\|\nu\|=\lim_{A\in\mathcal{I}}|\nu|(A)
.
∎

The next proposition essentially extends Drewnowski and Paúl’s observation, mentioned at the beginning of this section.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an infinite Boolean algebra and let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. For every
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{I})
, the function
T_{\alpha}(\mu)\colon\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle\to\mathbb{R}
, given for every
A\in\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
by

T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A)=\begin{cases}\mu(A),&\text{ if }A\in\mathcal{I},\\
-\mu(A^{c})+\alpha,&\text{ if }A\not\in\mathcal{I},\\
\end{cases}

is a measure on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
, i.e.
T_{\alpha}(\mu)\in\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
, extending
\mu
and satisfying:

(1)

\|(\mu,\alpha)\|_{\infty}\leq\big\|T_{\alpha}(\mu)\big\|\leq 2\|(\mu,\alpha)\|_{1}
,

(2)

\widehat{T_{\alpha}(\mu)}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big\}\big)=\alpha-\widehat{T_{\alpha}(\mu)}\Big(\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big)
.

Consequently, if
\mu
is non-negative, then we have

(2)

\widehat{T_{\alpha}(\mu)}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big\}\big)=\alpha-\lim_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\mu(A)
.

Proof.

Fix
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{I})
. Obviously,
T_{\alpha}(\mu)
extends
\mu
onto
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
.

We prove that
T_{\alpha}(\mu)
is finitely additive. It is enough to check that for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
and
B\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
such that
A\wedge B=0_{\mathcal{A}}
we have
T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A\vee B)=T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A)+T_{\alpha}(\mu)(B)
. So, let
A
and
B
be as required. We have
A\vee B\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
, so by definition

T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A\vee B)=-\mu((A\vee B)^{c})+\alpha.

Since
A\leq B^{c}\in\mathcal{I}
, it holds

T_{\alpha}(\mu)(B)=-\mu(B^{c})+\alpha=-\mu(A\wedge B^{c})-\mu(A^{c}\wedge B^{c})+\alpha=-\mu(A\wedge B^{c})-\mu((A\vee B)^{c})+\alpha=

=-\mu(A)+T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A\vee B)=-T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A)+T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A\vee B),

hence
T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A\vee B)=T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A)+T_{\alpha}(\mu)(B)
.

We have

\big\|T_{\alpha}(\mu)\big\|=\sup\big\{\big|T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A)\big|+\big|T_{\alpha}(\mu)(B)\big|\colon\ A,B\in\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle,\ A\wedge B=0_{\mathcal{A}},\ A\vee B=1_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}=

=\sup\big\{\big|T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A)\big|+\big|T_{\alpha}(\mu)(A^{c})\big|\colon\ A\in\mathcal{I}\big\}=\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|-\mu(A)+\alpha|\colon\ A\in\mathcal{I}\big\}\leq

\leq 2\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|\colon\ A\in\mathcal{I}\big\}+|\alpha|\leq 2\|\mu\|+|\alpha|=\|(2\mu,\alpha)\|_{1}\leq 2\|(\mu,\alpha)\|_{1}

and similarly

\big\|T_{\alpha}(\mu)\big\|=\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|-\mu(A)+\alpha|\colon\ A\in\mathcal{I}\big\}\geq\big|\mu\big(0_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\big|+\big|-\mu\big(0_{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\alpha\big|=|\alpha|.

Moreover, by a similar computation we get

\big\|T_{\alpha}(\mu)\big\|=\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|\mu(B)|+|-\mu(A\vee B)+\alpha|\colon\ A,B\in\mathcal{I},\ A\wedge B=0_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}\geq

\geq\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|\mu(B)|\colon\ A,B\in\mathcal{I},\ A\wedge B=0_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}=\|\mu\|,

hence
\big\|T_{\alpha}(\mu)\big\|\geq\|(\mu,\alpha)\|_{\infty}
. Consequently, (i) holds and so also
T_{\alpha}(\mu)\in\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
.

We have

\widehat{T(\mu)}\big(\big\{p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big\}\big)=\widehat{T_{\alpha}(\mu)}\big(St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)\big)-\widehat{T_{\alpha}(\mu)}\Big(\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big)=

=T_{\alpha}(\mu)\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)-\widehat{T_{\alpha}(\mu)}\Big(\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big)=-\mu\big(0_{\mathcal{A}})+\alpha-\widehat{T_{\alpha}(\mu)}\Big(\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big)=

=\alpha-\widehat{T_{\alpha}(\mu)}\Big(\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{I}}\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\Big),

which proves (ii).

If
\mu
is non-negative, then (ii’) follows from equalities (
+
).
∎

Note that by the above proof in condition (i) we even have the stronger inequality:

\big\|T_{\alpha}(\mu)\big\|\leq\|(2\mu,\alpha)\|_{1}.

Proposition
4.6
yields the aforementioned isomorphism between the Banach spaces
\ba(\mathcal{I})\oplus\mathbb{R}
and
\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an infinite Boolean algebra and let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in
\mathcal{A}
. Then, the mapping

\ba(\mathcal{I})\oplus\mathbb{R}\ni(\mu,\alpha)\longmapsto T_{\alpha}(\mu)\in\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle),

defined in Proposition
4.6
, is an isomorphism between the Banach spaces
\ba(\mathcal{I})\oplus\mathbb{R}
and
\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
with
\|T_{\cdot}\|\leq 2
.

Proof.

It is immediate that
T_{\cdot}
is an injective linear mapping.

Let
\mu\in\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
. Set
\nu=\mu\restriction\mathcal{I}
and
\alpha=\mu\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)
. As
T_{\alpha}(\nu)
extends
\nu
, we have
T_{\alpha}(\nu)(A)=\mu(A)
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
. For
A\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
we have

T_{\alpha}(\nu)(A)=-\nu(A^{c})+\alpha=-\mu(A^{c})+\mu\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\mu(A).

It follows that
T_{\alpha}(\nu)=\mu
and so
T_{\cdot}
is a bijection. By Proposition
4.6
.(i),
T_{\cdot}
is bounded with
\|T_{\cdot}\|\leq 2
and hence, by the Open Mapping Theorem,
T_{\cdot}
is an isomorphism.
∎

4.2.
Properties of ideals and Boolean algebras generated by them

We now study the relation between the Nikodym property and the Grothendieck property of Boolean algebras of the form
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
as well as provide a characterization of the former property in terms of non-negative measures. We first introduce the following auxiliary definitions, supplementing Definitions
1.1
,
4.1
,
4.2
, and
3.1
.

Definition 4.8
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
in a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the
Nikodym property
if the ring
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
has the Nikodym property.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
. A sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{I}
is
anti-Nikodym
if the sequence
\big\langle\mathring{\mu}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of the corresponding measures on
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
is anti-Nikodym.

As we will need this result later, for the sake of completeness, we now reprove the aforementioned observation of Drewnowski and Paúl
[
25
, Remark 2.2.(a)]
, in the case of ideals and Boolean algebras. We use the topological language developed in the previous subsection.

Theorem 4.10

(Drewnowski–Paúl
[
25
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an infinite Boolean algebra and let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in
\mathcal{A}
. Then,
\mathcal{I}
has the Nikodym property if and only if the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
has the Nikodym property.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{I}
has the Nikodym property but there is an anti-Nikodym sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
. Of course,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{\mu}_{n}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\big)=0

for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
, and so

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\widehat{\mu}_{n}\restriction\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})\big)(A)=0

for every
A\in\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
. On the other hand, Corollary
3.6
implies that there exist an antichain
\big\langle A_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
and a strictly increasing sequence
\big\langle n_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
satisfying

\big|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{k}}\big(\left[A_{k}\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\big)\big|=\big|\mu_{n_{k}}\big(A_{k}\big)\big|>k

for every
k\in\omega
. At most one of
A_{k}
’s is an element of the dual filter
\mathcal{I}^{*}
, and so for almost all
k\in\omega
we have
A_{k}\in\mathcal{I}
. It follows that

\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\widehat{\mu}_{n_{k}}\restriction\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})\big\|=\infty,

and so
\big\langle\widehat{\mu}_{n_{k}}\restriction\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence on
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
, which is a contradiction as
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
has the Nikodym property.

Assume now that
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
has the Nikodym property but there exists an anti-Nikodym sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
on
\mathcal{I}
. For each
n\in\omega
, let
\mu_{n}^{\prime}=T_{0}\big(\mu_{n}\big)
be the extension of
\mu_{n}
onto
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
given by Proposition
4.6
. It is immediate that for each
A\in\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}^{\prime}(A)=0.

By Proposition
4.6
.(i), it also holds

\infty=\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|\leq\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}^{\prime}\big\|.

Consequently,
\big\langle\mu_{n}^{\prime}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
, which is again a contradiction.
∎

The following proposition, describing the relation between the Nikodym property and the Grothendieck property of Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
, will be crucial for results in the next section.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Boolean algebra and let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in
\mathcal{A}
. Assume that

•

for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
the restricted algebra
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property,

•

the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
does not have the Nikodym property.

Then, for any anti-Nikodym sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
, the point
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\in St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
is the only Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
.

Consequently,
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
does not have the Grothendieck property either.

Proof.

Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
. By Lemma
3.3
,
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
has a Nikodym concentration point
t\in St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
.

Suppose that there is
A\in\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
such that
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}
. By the definition of Nikodym concentration points and the fact that
\mathcal{A}_{A}=(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)_{A}
, we have

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)_{A}\big\|=\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction\mathcal{A}_{A}\big\|=\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|=\infty.

Since we obviously have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(B)=0
for every
B\in(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)_{A}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
as well, we get that the sequence

\big\langle\mu_{n}\restriction(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)_{A}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle

is an anti-Nikodym sequence on
(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)_{A}
. But this is a contradiction, as
(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)_{A}=\mathcal{A}_{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property. Consequently, there is no
A\in\mathcal{I}
such that
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}
. By (
\star
) (from the beginning of this section), it follows that
t=p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
. In particular,
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
is the only Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
.

The second conclusion follows from Corollary
3.21
.
∎

Recall that, by the Nikodym–Andô theorem, every
\sigma
-complete Boolean algebra has the Nikodym property.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Boolean algebra with the Nikodym property (e.g. let
\mathcal{A}
be
\sigma
-complete) and
\mathcal{I}
an ideal in
\mathcal{A}
. If
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
does not have the Nikodym property, then it does not have the Grothendieck property either.

We will now characterize the Nikodym property of Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
in terms of non-negative measures. We start with the following preparatory proposition, rephrasing
[
76
, Proposition 2.4]
(or
[
79
, Lemma 4.1]
) in the context of algebras
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

the algebra
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
has the Nikodym property;

(2)

the restricted algebra
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
and there is no sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
satisfying the following three conditions:

(a)

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|=\infty
,

(b)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=0
,

(c)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
;

(3)

the restricted algebra
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
and there is no sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
satisfying the following three conditions:

(a)

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|=\infty
,

(b)

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big|\mu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\big|<\infty
,

(c)

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|<\infty
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

(A)
\Rightarrow
(B): Suppose that
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
has the Nikodym property. Trivially, for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property, too. Assume however that there is a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of measures on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
satisfying conditions (a)–(c). By conditions (b) and (c), for every
A\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\mu_{n}(A)\big|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\mu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)-\mu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus A\big)\big|\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\mu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\big|+\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\mu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus A\big)\big|\leq

\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\mu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\big|+\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus A\big)\big\|=0,

which implies that
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
and so it does not have the Nikodym property, a contradiction.

(B)
\Rightarrow
(C): Assume that there is a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\ba(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
satisfying conditions (a’)–(c’). By going to a subsequence if necessary, by condition (a’), we may assume that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|=\infty,

and that each
\mu_{n}
is non-zero. For each
n\in\omega
set:

\nu_{n}=\mu_{n}\big/\sqrt{\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|}.

We then have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|\nu_{n}\big\|=\infty
. Moreover, by (
*
) and condition (b’), we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\nu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\big|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\big|\mu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\big|}{\sqrt{\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{m\in\omega}\big|\mu_{m}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\big|}{\sqrt{\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|}}=0.

Similarly, by (
*
) and condition (c’), for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|\nu_{n}\restriction A\big\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|}{\sqrt{\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{m\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{m}\restriction A\big\|}{\sqrt{\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|}}=0.

It follows that the sequence
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
satisfies conditions (a)–(c), which is impossible.

(C)
\Rightarrow
(B): Obvious.

(C)
\Rightarrow
(A): Assume that each restricted algebra
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property and there is no sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
satisfying conditions (a’)–(c’), but yet
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
does not have the Nikodym property. Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be an anti-Nikodym sequence on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
. Immediately by definition
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
satisfies conditions (a’) and (b’). For every
A\in\mathcal{I}
, since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(B)=0
for each
B\leq A
and
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property, we have

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction A\big\|=\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction\mathcal{A}_{A}\big\|<\infty,

that is,
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
satisfies also condition (c’), which is again a contradiction.
∎

Note that, by the Nikodym property of the restricted algebras, condition (c’) in Proposition
4.13
can be exchanged for the following one:

(3)

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big|\mu_{n}(A)\big|<\infty
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
. Then, the algebra
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
has the Nikodym property if and only if the restricted algebra
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
and there is no (disjointly supported) sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of non-negative measures on
St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
such that:

(1)

p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\not\in\supp\big(\mu_{n}\big)
for every
n\in\omega
,

(2)

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|=\infty
,

(3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\big)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Assume first that each restricted algebra
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property and there is no sequence of measures on
St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
satisfying conditions (1)–(3), but yet
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
does not have the Nikodym property. By Proposition
4.11
, there is an anti-Nikodym sequence
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
with
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
being its only Nikodym concentration point. By Lemma
3.13
,
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
is a strong Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
, and hence, by Theorem
3.18
, there is a disjointly supported anti-Nikodym sequence
\big\langle\theta_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of non-zero measures on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
such that
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\not\in\supp\big(\widehat{\theta}_{n}\big)
for every
n\in\omega
. By going to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|\theta_{n}\big\|=\infty.

For each
n\in\omega
let us define the non-negative measure
\mu_{n}
on
St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
by the formula:

\mu_{n}=\big|\widehat{\theta}_{n}\big|\big/\sqrt{\big\|\widehat{\theta}_{n}\big\|}.

Note that
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\not\in\supp\big(\mu_{n}\big)
for every
n\in\omega
and that it follows from (
*
) that
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|=\infty
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{I}
. For every
n\in\omega
we have

\mu_{n}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\big)=\big|\theta_{n}\big|(A)\big/{\sqrt{\big\|\widehat{\theta}_{n}\big\|}}\leq\Big(\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\theta_{k}\restriction A\big\|\Big)\big/\sqrt{\big\|\widehat{\theta}_{n}\big\|},

hence, by (
*
) and the fact that the restricted algebra
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property, it also holds

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\big)=0.

Consequently, the sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
satisfies conditions (1)–(3), which is a contradiction.

Assume now that
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
has the Nikodym property. Trivially, for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property, too. Assume however that there is a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of non-negative measures on
St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
satisfying conditions (1)–(3). For each
n\in\omega
let us define the measure
\nu_{n}
on
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
via its Radon extension
\widehat{\nu}_{n}
on
St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)
as follows:

\widehat{\nu}_{n}=\mu_{n}-\mu_{n}\big(St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)\big)\cdot\delta_{p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}}.

The sequence
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
satisfies conditions (a’)–(c’) of Proposition
4.13
. Indeed, by condition (1) for every
n\in\omega
we have

\big\|\nu_{n}\big\|=\big\|\widehat{\nu}_{n}\big\|=\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|+\big|\mu_{n}\big(St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)\big)\big|\geq\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|,

so by condition (2) we get

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\nu_{n}\big\|\geq\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|=\infty,

hence condition (a’) is satisfied. Condition (b’) holds, as

\nu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\widehat{\nu}_{n}\big(St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)\big)=\mu_{n}\big(St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)\big)-\mu_{n}\big(St(\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle)\big)\cdot 1=0,

so
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big|\nu_{n}\big(1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\big|<\infty
. Finally, by condition (3), for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
and
B\leq A
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{n}(B)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(\left[B\right]_{\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle}\big)=0,

hence, by the fact that
\mathcal{A}_{A}
has the Nikodym property, we get that

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\nu_{n}\restriction A\big\|=\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\nu_{n}\restriction\mathcal{A}_{A}\big\|<\infty,

that is, condition (c’) is satisfied as well.

Consequently, by Proposition
4.13
,
\mathcal{A}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{I}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
does not have the Nikodym property, which is again a contradiction.
∎

5.
Definable Boolean algebras with the Nikodym property and without the Grothendieck property

In this section, exploiting the results from the previous one, we will study the Nikodym property for Boolean algebras of the form
{\wp(\omega)}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a free filter on
\omega
. As a result, we will obtain a simple class of Boolean subalgebras of
{\wp(\omega)}
having the Nikodym property but not the Grothendieck property. Those subalgebras, when considered as subsets of the Cantor space
2^{\omega}
, will also have an interesting additional feature: they will belong to a low level of the Borel hierarchy of subsets of
2^{\omega}
.

From now on, all filters on
\omega
are assumed to be
free
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a filter on
\omega
. Following
[
63
, Section 3]
, by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
we shortly denote the Boolean algebra
{\wp(\omega)}\mathopen{}\left\langle\mathcal{F}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
. Therefore,

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}=\big\{A\in{\wp(\omega)}\colon\ A\in\mathcal{F}\text{ or }A^{c}\in\mathcal{F}\big\}.

Trivially,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
contains the ideal
Fin
and
\mathcal{F}
is an ultrafilter on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Also, we have
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}={\wp(\omega)}
if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is an ultrafilter on
{\wp(\omega)}
.

Again following
[
63
, Section 3]
, we put
S_{\mathcal{F}}=St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}\big)
. For every
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
we will shortly write
\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{F}}=\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}}
. Note that
S_{\mathcal{F}}
contains a countable discrete dense subspace consisting of isolated points which we can naturally associate with
\omega
, and therefore we can put
S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}=S_{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\omega
. For
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
we also write
\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}=\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\omega
. One can easily show that
S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
is homeomorphic to the Stone space
St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin\big)
of the quotient Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
, or, equivalently, that the Boolean algebra of clopen subsets of
S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
. For every
A\in{\wp(\omega)}
we will also simply write
A
for the corresponding subset of
\omega\subseteq S_{\mathcal{F}}
. If
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
, then
\overline{A}^{S_{\mathcal{F}}}=\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
, and conversely, if
A\in{\wp(\omega)}
is such that
\overline{A}^{S_{\mathcal{F}}}
is clopen, then
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
(and hence again
\overline{A}^{S_{\mathcal{F}}}=\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
).
Note that for every infinite set
A\in\mathcal{F}^{*}
we have
\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}\big)_{A}=\wp(A)
(resp.
\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}\big)_{A}/[A]^{<\omega}=\wp(A)/[A]^{<\omega}
) and so the clopen
\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
(resp.
\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
) is homeomorphic to
{\beta\omega}
(resp. to
\omega^{*}
); consequently the Boolean algebras
\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}\big)_{A}
and
\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}\big)_{A}/[A]^{<\omega}
have the Nikodym property (by Seever’s theorem
[
72
]
).

Recall that
p_{\mathcal{F}}
denotes the point in
S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
corresponding to the ultrafilter
\mathcal{F}
in the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Let us define the following set:

N_{\mathcal{F}}=\omega\cup\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}.

The topology of
N_{\mathcal{F}}
inherited from
S_{\mathcal{F}}
can be described as follows: every point of
\omega
is isolated in
N_{\mathcal{F}}
(as it is isolated in
S_{\mathcal{F}}
) and a local open base of
p_{\mathcal{F}}
consists of all (clopen) sets of the form
A\cup\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}
, where
A\in\mathcal{F}
. Note that every open neighborhood of
p_{\mathcal{F}}
in
N_{\mathcal{F}}
is a clopen subset of
N_{\mathcal{F}}
.

For basic properties of spaces
S_{\mathcal{F}}
and
N_{\mathcal{F}}
, see
[
63
, Section 3]
. The following proposition lists the most important ones.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a filter on
\omega
.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{F}
denotes the subset of
{\beta\omega}
consisting of all ultrafilters
x\in{\beta\omega}
such that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq x
, then
\mathfrak{F}
is closed in
{\beta\omega}
and the mapping
\varphi\colon{\beta\omega}/\mathfrak{F}\to S_{\mathcal{F}}
given for every
x\in{\beta\omega}
by the formula
\varphi\big([x]_{\mathfrak{F}}\big)=x\cap\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
, where
[x]_{\mathfrak{F}}
denotes the equivalence class of
x
in
{\beta\omega}/\mathfrak{F}
, is a homeomorphism.

(2)

The following are equivalent:

•

\mathcal{F}=Fr
,

•

S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}=\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}
,

•

N_{\mathcal{F}}=S_{\mathcal{F}}
.

(3)

S_{\mathcal{F}}\cong{\beta\omega}
if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is an ultrafilter on
{\wp(\omega)}
.

(4)

The following are equivalent:

•

\mathcal{F}
is a P-filter on
\omega
,

•

\mathcal{F}
is a maximal P-filter on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
,

•

p_{\mathcal{F}}
is a P-point in
S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
.

(5)

the space
N_{\mathcal{F}}
is
C^{*}
-embedded in
S_{\mathcal{F}}
, and
S_{\mathcal{F}}=\beta\big(N_{\mathcal{F}}\big)
, i.e.
S_{\mathcal{F}}
is the Čech–Stone compactification of
N_{\mathcal{F}}
.

The following corollary is an immediate rephrasing of Theorem
4.14
for Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
, we will use it frequently throughout this section.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a filter on
\omega
and let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
or
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
. Then,
\mathcal{A}
has the Nikodym property if and only if there is no (disjointly supported) sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of non-negative measures on
St(\mathcal{A})
such that:

(1)

p_{\mathcal{F}}\not\in\supp\big(\mu_{n}\big)
for every
n\in\omega
,

(2)

\sup_{n\in\omega}\mu_{n}(St(\mathcal{A}))=\infty
,

(3)

if
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(\left[\omega\setminus A\right]_{\mathcal{F}}\big)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}
, or,

if
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(\left[\omega\setminus A\right]_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\big)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}
.

The corollary and its variant for spaces
N_{\mathcal{F}}
, presented in
[
88
, Theorem 4.1]
, are crucial for proving the following characterization of the Nikodym property for Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
as a conjunction of the Nikodym property of the algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
and of the spaces
N_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a filter on
\omega
. Then, the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
has the Nikodym property if and only if the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
has the Nikodym property and the space
N_{\mathcal{F}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
has the Nikodym property. Since the property is preserved by taking quotients (see
[
71
, Proposition 2.11.(b)]
), the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
has it as well. If the space
N_{\mathcal{F}}
did not have the finitely supported Nikodym property, then
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
would not have the Nikodym property by
[
88
, Theorem 8.2]
, as
S_{\mathcal{F}}
contains a homeomorphic copy of
N_{\mathcal{F}}
. Hence,
N_{\mathcal{F}}
has the required property, too.

Suppose now that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
does not have the Nikodym property. Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a sequence of non-negative measures on
S_{\mathcal{F}}
like in Corollary
5.2
. If
\sup_{n\in\omega}\mu_{n}\big(S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\big)=\infty
, then for each
n\in\omega
we define the measure
\nu_{n}=\mu_{n}\restriction S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
, and it is not difficult to see that
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is a sequence of non-negative measures on
S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
like in Corollary
5.2
, which means that the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
does not have the Nikodym property either.

Thus, let us assume that
\sup_{n\in\omega}\mu_{n}\big(S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\big)<\infty
, which simply implies that
\sup_{n\in\omega}\mu_{n}(\omega)=\infty
.
If for each
n\in\omega
we set
\theta_{n}=\mu_{n}\restriction\omega
, then
\theta_{n}
is a non-negative measure on
S_{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\sup_{n\in\omega}\theta_{n}(\omega)=\infty
and for every
A\in\mathcal{F}
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\theta_{n}(\omega\setminus A)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(\left[\omega\setminus A\right]_{\mathcal{F}}\big)=0.

By the continuity of measures, for every
n\in\omega
there exists a finite set
F_{n}\subseteq\omega
such that

\theta_{n}\big(\omega\setminus F_{n}\big)<1/n.

For each
n\in\omega
let us set
\nu_{n}=\theta_{n}\restriction F_{n}
. We claim that the finitely supported sequence
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of non-negative measures on
N_{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies conditions (1)–(3) from
[
88
, Theorem 4.1]
. First, for every
n\in\omega
we have
\supp\big(\nu_{n}\big)\subseteq\omega
and, by (
*
),

\nu_{n}(\omega)=\theta\big(F_{n}\big)=\theta_{n}(\omega)-\theta_{n}\big(\omega\setminus F_{n}\big)>\theta_{n}(\omega)-1/n,

so
\sup_{n\in\omega}\nu_{n}(\omega)=\infty
.

Next, for every
A\in\mathcal{F}
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{n}(\omega\setminus A)=0
, as
\lim_{n\to\infty}\theta_{n}(\omega\setminus A)=0
and, again by (
*
),

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\nu_{n}(\omega\setminus A)-\theta_{n}(\omega\setminus A)\big|\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|\nu_{n}-\theta_{n}\big\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|\theta_{n}\restriction\big(\omega\setminus F_{n}\big)\big\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\theta_{n}\big(\omega\setminus F_{n}\big)=0.

Therefore, by
[
88
, Theorem 4.1]
, the space
N_{\mathcal{F}}
does not have the finitely supported Nikodym property.
∎

Note that an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
has the Nikodym property as an ideal in the Boolean algebra
{\wp(\omega)}
(i.e. in the sense of Definition
4.8
) if and only if
\mathcal{I}
has the Nikodym property as a ring of subsets of
\omega
(i.e. in the sense of Definition
4.1
)—this follows from the fact that the rings
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
and
\mathcal{I}
are isomorphic. Also, recall that by Theorem
4.10
an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
has the Nikodym property if and only if the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the Nikodym property. Since the Boolean algebra
{\wp(\omega)}
has the Nikodym property, it follows that if an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
is maximal (that is,
\mathcal{I}^{*}
is an ultrafilter and so
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}={\wp(\omega)}
), then
\mathcal{I}
has the Nikodym property.

Let us now turn our attention to P-ideals. We start with the following remark.

Example 5.4
.

In
[
25
, Example 5.6]
an ideal on
\omega
which is not a P-ideal but has the Nikodym property was constructed. It immediately follows from the discussion above that every maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
whose dual filter
\mathcal{I}^{*}
is not a P-point in
\omega^{*}
is also such an ideal. Since there are
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
many non-isomorphic ultrafilters on
\omega
which are not P-points in
\omega^{*}
(see, e.g.,
[
51
, page 76]
), we actually get
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
many non-isomorphic such examples.

In the case of a P-ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
, Theorem
5.3
yields that the Nikodym property of the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
is equivalent to the finitely supported Nikodym property of the space
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
as well as to the following two properties of
\mathcal{I}
, see Theorem
5.10
below.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a ring of subsets of a set
X
. A subset
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
is a
Nikodym set for
\ba(\mathcal{R})
if, for every sequence
\big\langle\mu_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
in
\ba(\mathcal{R})
, if
\lim_{k\in\omega}\mu_{k}(A)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{Q}
, then
\sup_{k\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{k}\big\|<\infty
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a ring of subsets of a set
X
. A collection
\big\langle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}\colon\ \sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}\big\rangle
of subsets of
\mathcal{R}
with
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{\langle\rangle}
and such that for every
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
the sequence
\big\langle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma{{}^{\smallfrown}}n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is increasing and satisfies
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{R}_{\sigma{{}^{\smallfrown}}n}
is called a
web in

\mathcal{R}
.

Definition 5.7
.

A ring
\mathcal{R}
of subsets of a set
X
has the
strong Nikodym property
if for every increasing sequence
\big\langle\mathcal{R}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of subsets of
\mathcal{R}
with
\mathcal{R}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{R}_{n}
there is
n_{0}\in\omega
such that
\mathcal{R}_{n_{0}}
is a Nikodym set for
\ba(\mathcal{R})
.

A ring
\mathcal{R}
of subsets of a set
X
has the
web Nikodym property
if for every web
\big\langle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}\colon\ \sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}\big\rangle
in
\mathcal{R}
there is a sequence
\big\langle\sigma_{m}\in\omega^{m}\colon\ m\in\omega\big\rangle
such that, for every
m\in\omega
,
\sigma_{m+1}
extends
\sigma_{m}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{m}}
is a Nikodym set for
\ba(\mathcal{R})
.

Both of the properties of course immediately imply the standard Nikodym property for rings. The strong Nikodym property and the web Nikodym property were first essentially studied by Valdivia
[
85
]
and López-Pellicer
[
62
]
, respectively, who proved that
\sigma
-fields of sets have both the properties (see also
[
54
]
and
[
61
]
; cf.
[
53
, Chapter 21]
). Later, Valdivia
[
86
]
showed that the field
\mathscr{J}_{n}
of Jordan measurable subsets of the
n
-dimensional cube
[0,1]^{n}
has the strong Nikodym property, which was further extended by López-Alfonso
[
60
]
to the web Nikodym property (consequently, both results strengthen Schachermayer’s theorem
[
71
]
, mentioned in Introduction). Ferrando
[
29
]
proved that the ideal
\mathcal{Z}
has the latter property, too. For further results on the web Nikodym property for ideals, see also
[
30
]
.

We translate Definition
5.7
in a natural way to the class of Boolean algebras and ideals.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Boolean algebra.
\mathcal{A}
has the
strong Nikodym property
(resp. the
web Nikodym property
) if the ring
Clopen(St(\mathcal{A}))
of all clopen subsets of
St(\mathcal{A})
has the strong Nikodym property (resp. the web Nikodym property).

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
in
\mathcal{A}
has the
strong Nikodym property
(resp. the
web Nikodym property
) if the ring
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
of clopen subsets of
St(\mathcal{A})
has the strong Nikodym property (resp. the web Nikodym property).

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the Nikodym property. Let
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
be non-empty and set
\mathcal{Q}^{*}=\big\{S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\setminus A\colon A\in\mathcal{Q}\big\}
. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{Q}
is Nikodym for
\ba(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}))
,

(2)

\mathcal{Q}\cup\big\{S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\!\big\}
is Nikodym for
\ba\!\big(Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big)
,

(3)

\mathcal{Q}\cup\mathcal{Q}^{*}
is Nikodym for
\ba\!\big(Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big)
.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b): Assume that
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
is a Nikodym set for
\ba(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
. Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a sequence in
\ba\!\big(Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{Q}\cup\big\{S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\!\big\}
. It follows that

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})\big\|<\infty,

hence for every
A\in\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
we have
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big|\mu_{n}(A)\big|<\infty
, and so

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big|\mu_{n}(A^{c})\big|\leq\sup_{n\in\omega}\big|\mu_{n}\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big|+\sup_{n\in\omega}\big|\mu_{n}(A)\big|<\infty.

As
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the Nikodym property, we get that
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|<\infty
. This proves that the set
\mathcal{Q}\cup\big\{S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\!\big\}
is Nikodym for
\ba\!\big(Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big)
.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c): Let
\big\langle\mu\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle\in\ba\!\big(Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big)
be such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=0
for every
A\in\mathcal{Q}\cup\mathcal{Q}^{*}
. Let
A\in\mathcal{Q}
. Then,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\mu_{n}(A)+\mu_{n}\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\setminus A\big)\big)=0.

As
\mathcal{Q}\cup\big\{S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big\}
is Nikodym for
\ba\!\big(Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big)
, we get that
\sup_{n\to\infty}\big\|\mu_{n}\big\|<\infty
. Consequently,
\mathcal{Q}\cup\mathcal{Q}^{*}
is Nikodym for
\ba\!\big(Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big)
, too.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(a) The implication follows by a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
4.10
, using the extension operator
T_{0}\colon\ba(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}))\to\ba\!\big(Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big)
.
∎

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a P-ideal on
\omega
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the Nikodym property,

(2)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the strong Nikodym property,

(3)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the web Nikodym property,

(4)

\mathcal{I}
has the Nikodym property,

(5)

\mathcal{I}
has the strong Nikodym property,

(6)

\mathcal{I}
has the web Nikodym property,

(7)

N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property.

Proof.

Implications (3)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow
(1) and (6)
\Rightarrow
(5)
\Rightarrow
(4) are immediate.

Equivalence (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(4) follows from Theorem
4.10
.

Using the nomenclature of
[
30
]
, an ideal
\mathcal{J}
on
\omega
is a P-ideal if and only if the family
\left[\omega\right]^{<\omega}
is
\mathcal{J}
-singular
. Consequently, by
[
30
, Theorem 3.3]
, the ideal
\mathcal{I}
has the web Nikodym property, i.e. implication (4)
\Rightarrow
(6) holds.

We show implication (6)
\Rightarrow
(3). Let
\big\langle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}\colon\ \sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}\big\rangle
be a web in
Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)
(with
\mathcal{R}_{\langle\rangle}=Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)
). Without loss of generality, we may assume that for each
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
we have
S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\in\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}
. For each
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
set
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}\cap\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
. It follows that the collection
\big\langle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}^{\prime}\colon\ \sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}\big\rangle
is a web in
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
(with
\mathcal{R}_{\langle\rangle}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
). As
\mathcal{I}
has the web Nikodym property, and so the (isomorphic) ring
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
has the web Nikodym property, too, there is a sequence
\big\langle\sigma_{m}\colon\ m\in\omega\big\rangle
such that, for every
m\in\omega
,
\sigma_{m+1}
extends
\sigma_{m}
and the set
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{m}}^{\prime}
is Nikodym for
\ba(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}))
. By Lemma
5.9
, for each
m\in\omega
the set
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{m}}
is Nikodym for
\ba\!\big(Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big)
. Consequently, the ring
Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)
has the web Nikodym property and so
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
does, too.

We now prove that implication (3)
\Rightarrow
(6) holds. Let
\big\langle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}\colon\ \sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}\big\rangle
be a web in
\ba(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}))
(with
\mathcal{R}_{\langle\rangle}=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})
); without loss of generality, we may assume that
\emptyset\in\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}
for every
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
. For every
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
set
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}^{*}=\big\{S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\setminus A\colon A\in\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}\big\}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}=\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}\cup\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}^{*}
. Then,
\big\langle\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}\colon\ \sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}\big\rangle
is a web in
Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)
(with
\mathcal{S}_{\langle\rangle}=Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)
). As
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the web Nikodym property, there is a sequence
\big\langle\sigma_{m}\colon\ m\in\omega\big\rangle
such that, for every
m\in\omega
,
\sigma_{m+1}
extends
\sigma_{m}
and the set
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma_{m}}
is Nikodym for
\ba\big(Clopen\big(S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)\big)
. By Lemma
5.9
, for each
m\in\omega
the set
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{m}}
is Nikodym for
\ba(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}))
, which proves that
\mathcal{I}
has the web Nikodym property as well.

Implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(7) follows from Theorem
5.3
.

For implication (7)
\Rightarrow
(1), assume that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
does not have the Nikodym property. It follows by (5) and Theorem
5.3
that the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}/Fin
does not have the Nikodym property and so let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be an anti-Nikodym sequence on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}/Fin
. As
\mathcal{I}
is a P-ideal, Proposition
\ref{prop:sf_properties}.(4)
implies that
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
is a P-point in the space
S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
. Thus, by
[
74
, Proposition 4.5]
,
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
cannot be a Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
. Consequently,
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
has a concentration point
t\in S_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}^{*}
different than
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
. Let
A\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
be such that
t\in\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}^{*}
. Note that for the equivalence class
A^{\bullet}
of
A
in the quotient algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}/Fin
we have
\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}/Fin\big)_{A^{\bullet}}=\wp(A)/[A]^{<\omega}
. Consequently, the sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\restriction\wp(A)/[A]^{<\omega}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is an anti-Nikodym sequence on
\wp(A)/[A]^{<\omega}
, which is impossible as the latter algebra has the Nikodym property by the Seever theorem
[
72
]
.
∎

Theorem
5.10
will be frequently used in the sequel; for its particular applications to the class of so-called density ideals on
\omega
, see Corollaries
5.18
and
5.20
.

Example 5.11
.

Theorem
5.10
does not hold anymore if one drops the assumption that
\mathcal{I}
is a P-ideal. Namely, as described in
[
63
, Sections 3 and 6.3]
, there are free filters
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
such that the spaces
S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
contain non-eventually constant convergent sequences, but the spaces
N_{\mathcal{F}}
do not have the bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig property (recall Definition
1.4
). Consequently, for those filters
\mathcal{F}
, the algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}/Fin
,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
, and the ideals
\mathcal{F}^{*}
do not have the Nikodym property, but, by
[
88
, Proposition 6.1]
, the spaces
N_{\mathcal{F}}
do have the finitely supported Nikodym property.

Example 5.12
.

A careful reader has though surely noticed that the argument for implication (3)
\Rightarrow
(6) is superfluous in the proof of Theorem
5.10
, as this implication follows from combinations of other ones. We provided it however deliberately in order to show that the assumption that the considered ideal
\mathcal{I}
is a P-ideal is actually not required for the validity of equivalences (3)
\Leftrightarrow
(6), (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(4), and (2)
\Leftrightarrow
(5) (which can be proved in a similar but easier way as (3)
\Leftrightarrow
(6)). This way we obtain that every maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
has the web Nikodym property, since for such
\mathcal{I}
we have
{\wp(\omega)}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
and the
\sigma
-field
{\wp(\omega)}
has the latter property (by López-Pellicer
[
62
]
). In particular, by an argument as in Example
5.4
, we get that there are
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
many non-isomorphic maximal ideals with the web Nikodym property (and which are not P-ideals).

By
[
88
, Proposition 5.2]
, given two filters
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{F}\leq_{K}\mathcal{G}
, if
N_{\mathcal{G}}
does not have the finitely supported Nikodym property, then
N_{\mathcal{F}}
does not either. Our last example shows that this does not hold for the Nikodym property of ideals.

Example 5.13
.

Using again the constructions presented in
[

for each ultrafilter
\mathcal{F}
on

one can obtain a filter
\mathcal{G}
such that
\mathcal{F}\leq_{K}\mathcal{G}
and

contains a non-trivial convergent sequence (simply set
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\mathcal{F}

in the construction). As for each ultrafilter

, we consequently get that there are ideals

and

on

and

5.1.
Submeasures and standard classes of ideals on
\omega

We will now introduce several standard definitions concerning submeasures on
\omega
and ideals associated with them. For basic information concerning those objects, we refer the reader to
[
13
]
,
[
28
]
, and
[
50
]
.

A function
\varphi\colon{\wp(\omega)}\to[0,\infty]
is a
submeasure
on
\omega
if
\varphi(\emptyset)=0
,
\varphi(\{n\})<\infty
for every
n\in\omega
,
\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(Y)
whenever
X\subseteq Y\subseteq\omega
, and
\varphi(X\cup Y)\leq\varphi(X)+\varphi(Y)
for every
X,Y\subseteq\omega
. A submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is
lower semicontinuous
(in short,
lsc
) if
\varphi(A)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(A\cap[0,n])
for every
A\subseteq\omega
. A submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is
bounded
if
\varphi(\omega)<\infty
, otherwise
\varphi
is
unbounded
. Note that every non-negative measure
\mu
on
\omega
is a bounded finitely additive lsc submeasure.

We consider the following three standard ideals associated with an (lsc) submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
:

\mathcal{Z}(\varphi)=\big\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\ \varphi(A)=0\big\},

\fin(\varphi)=\big\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\ \varphi(A)<\infty\big\},

\exh(\varphi)=\big\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\ \lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi\big(A\setminus[0,n]\big)=0\big\}.

Trivially,

Fin\cup\mathcal{Z}(\varphi)\subseteq\exh(\varphi)\subseteq\fin(\varphi).

It is also not difficult to show that
\fin(\varphi)
is an
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}
ideal and
\exh(\varphi)
is an
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta}
P-ideal (when, as usual, thought of as subsets of the Cantor space
2^{\omega}
). The following results of Mazur and Solecki characterize
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}
ideals and analytic P-ideals on
\omega
in terms of submeasures.

Theorem 5.14

(Mazur
[
64
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\omega
such that
Fin\subseteq\mathcal{I}
. Then,
\mathcal{I}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}
ideal if and only if there is an lsc submeasure
\varphi
such that
\mathcal{I}=\fin(\varphi)
.

Theorem 5.15

(Solecki
[
80
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\omega
such that
Fin\subseteq\mathcal{I}
. Then,
\mathcal{I}
is an analytic P-ideal if and only if there is an lsc submeasure
\varphi
such that
\mathcal{I}=\exh(\varphi)
.

For an lsc submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
, by the
core
of
\varphi
, denoted by
\varphi^{\bullet}
(see
[
23
, Section 1]
), we mean the submeasure defined by

\varphi^{\bullet}(A)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi\big(A\setminus[0,n]\big)

for every
A\subseteq\omega
(note that
\varphi^{\bullet}(A)
is well-defined by the monotonicity of the submeasure
\varphi
). We trivially have

\exh(\varphi)=\big\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\ \varphi^{\bullet}(A)=0\big\}=\mathcal{Z}(\varphi^{\bullet}).

Let us note that
\varphi^{\bullet}(\cdot)
was denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}
in
[
32
]
.

For submeasures
\varphi
and
\psi
on
\omega
we write
\psi\leq\varphi
if
\psi(A)\leq\varphi(A)
for every
A\subseteq\omega
. Following Farah
[
28
, page 21]
, we call a submeasure
\varphi

non-pathological
if for every
A\subseteq\omega
we have:

\varphi(A)=\sup\big\{\mu(A)\colon\ \mu\textrm{ is a non-negative measure on }\omega\textrm{ such that }\mu\leq\varphi\big\}.

Trivially, every non-negative measure on
\omega
is non-pathological. An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
is
non-pathological
if there is a non-pathological submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{I}=\exh(\varphi)
.

The family of density submeasures is an important subclass of non-pathological lsc submeasures. We say that a submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is a
density submeasure
if there exists a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of finitely supported non-negative measures on
\omega
with pairwise disjoint supports contained in
\omega
and
such that

\varphi=\sup_{n\in\omega}\mu_{n}.

Note that
\varphi^{\bullet}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}
. An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
is a
density ideal
if there is a density submeasure
\varphi
such that
\mathcal{I}=\exh(\varphi)
; in this case we have
X\in\exh(\varphi)
if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(X)=0
.

A prototypical ideal for the class of density ideals is the
(asymptotic) density zero
ideal

\mathcal{Z}=\exh(\varphi_{d})
, where the
asymptotic density submeasur
e
\varphi_{d}
is defined for every
A\subseteq\omega
by

\varphi_{d}(A)=\sup_{n\in\omega}\frac{\big|A\cap[2^{n},2^{n+1})\big|}{2^{n}}.

It is folklore that

\mathcal{Z}=\bigg\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap[0,n)|}{n}=0\bigg\}.

Erdős–Ulam ideals are a generalization of the asymptotic density zero ideal, introduced by Just and Krawczyk
[
52
]
. We say that
f\colon\omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is an
Erdős–Ulam function
if we have

\sum_{n\in\omega}f(n)=\infty\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(n)}{\sum_{i\leq n}f(i)}=0.

The
Erdős–Ulam ideal
associated with an Erdős–Ulam function
f
is defined as

\mathcal{EU}_{f}=\bigg\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i\in A\cap[0,n)}f(i)}{\sum_{i\in[0,n)}f(i)}=0\bigg\}.

By definition,
\mathcal{Z}
is an Erdős–Ulam ideal. For the function
f\in\omega^{\omega}
given by
f(n)=1/(n+1)
for every
n\in\omega
, we also define the
logarithmic density zero ideal

\mathcal{Z}_{\log}=\mathcal{EU}_{f}
. Note that every Erdős–Ulam ideal is a density ideal (see
[
28
, Section 1.13]
).

A submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is a
generalized density submeasure
if there exist a partition
\big\langle I_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of
\omega
into finite sets and a sequence
\big\langle\varphi_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of submeasures on
\omega
such that

\varphi=\sup_{n\in\omega}\varphi_{n}

and for any
n\in\omega
and
A\subseteq\omega
we have
\varphi_{n}(A)=\varphi_{n}\big(A\cap I_{n}\big)
. In this case we again have
\varphi^{\bullet}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{n}
and it is easy to see that the submeasure
\sup_{n\in\omega}\varphi_{n}
is non-pathological if and only if each submeasure
\varphi_{n}
is non-pathological. An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
is a
generalized density ideal
if

\mathcal{I}=\exh(\varphi)
for some generalized density submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
. Of course, every density submeasure (ideal) is a generalized density submeasure (ideal).

Summable ideals constitute yet another important class of non-pathological ideals. Given
f\colon\omega\to[0,\infty)
such that
\sum_{n\in\omega}f(n)=\infty
, the
summable ideal
corresponding to
f
is the ideal

\mathcal{I}_{f}=\bigg\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\ \sum_{n\in A}f(n)<\infty\bigg\}.

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{I}_{f}=\exh\big(\mu_{f}\big)=\fin\big(\mu_{f}\big)
, where
\mu_{f}\colon{\wp(\omega)}\to[0,\infty]
is an unbounded finitely additive submeasure defined for every
A\subseteq\omega
by

\mu_{f}(A)=\sum_{n\in A}f(n).

We will use two other standard ideals. The first one is
\tr(\mathcal{N})
, the
trace of the null ideal
—an ideal on
2^{<\omega}
defined as

\tr(\mathcal{N})=\Big\{A\subseteq 2^{<\omega}\colon\lambda\big(\big\{x\in 2^{\omega}\colon\exists^{\infty}n\in\omega\>(x\restriction n\in A)\big\}\big)=0\Big\},

where
\lambda
denotes the standard product measure on
2^{\omega}
. The ideal
\tr(\mathcal{N})
, when treated as an ideal on
\omega
, is also of the form
\exh(\varphi)
for some non-pathological submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
(see e.g.
[
13
, page 5]
). For more information on
\tr(\mathcal{N})
, see
[
48
]
.

Finally, we will need the ideal
\conv
, defined as the ideal on
\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
generated by sequences in
\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
convergent in
[0,1]
. This ideal is of different type than the ideals associated with submeasures, as it is an
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta\sigma}
ideal. For more information on
\conv
, see
[
50
]
.

One of the first results connecting the Nikodym property with any of the above defined classes of ideals on
\omega
was the result of Drewnowski, Florencio, and Paúl
[
20
, Proposition 6]
asserting that the asymptotic density zero ideal
\mathcal{Z}
has the Nikodym property. Later, the same authors showed in
[
21
, Corollary 2.3]
that the Nikodym property implies either of the following equivalent properties of ideals.

Definition 5.16
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
has the
Positive Summability Property
(in short,
(PSP)
), if, for every sequence
\big\langle x_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of non-negative real numbers, we have
\sum_{n\in\omega}x_{n}<\infty
provided that
\sum_{n\in A}x_{n}<\infty
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
has the
Absolute Summability Property
(in short,
(ASP)
), if, for every sequence
\big\langle x_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of real numbers, we have
\sum_{n\in\omega}\big|x_{n}\big|<\infty
provided that
\sum_{n\in A}x_{n}<\infty
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
.

The equivalence of (PSP) and (ASP) was observed by Sember and Freedman
[
73
, Proposition 1]
. Note that, trivially, an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
has (PSP) if and only if
\mathcal{I}
cannot be extended to a summable ideal. In particular, by aforementioned
[
21
, Corollary 2.3]
, no summable ideal has the Nikodym property (cf. Theorem
5.17
and Section
7
).

By Drewnowski and Paúl
[
25
, Theorem 3.5]
, the Nikodym property coincides with (PSP) and (ASP) in the class of P-ideals (see Section
7
for a generalization of this fact). If an ideal
\mathcal{I}
with (ASP) is not a P-ideal, then
\mathcal{I}
need not have the Nikodym property—for suitable examples, see
[
25
, Section 4]
(or Remark
6.20
).

Note that every ideal
\mathcal{I}
with (PSP) (so, in particular, with the Nikodym property) is
tall
, that is, for any infinite set
A\in{\wp(\omega)}
there is an infinite set
B\in\mathcal{I}
such that
B\subseteq A
.

The relations between the above defined standard classes of non-pathological ideals and the finitely supported Nikodym property of the spaces
N_{\mathcal{F}}
were studied by the first author in
[
88
]
. The following theorem summarizes those of his results, which are the most significant for the context of the present work.

Theorem 5.17

(Żuchowski)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\omega
.

(1)

N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
does not have the finitely supported Nikodym property if and only if there exists an unbounded non-pathological submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\exh(\varphi)
if and only if there exists an unbounded density submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\exh(\varphi)
(
[
88
, Theorem 4.3]
).

(2)

If
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
does not have the finitely supported Nikodym property, then
\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\mathcal{Z}
(
[
88
, Corollary 5.4]
).

(3)

If
\mathcal{I}
is a density ideal, then
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property if and only if
\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to an Erdős–Ulam ideal (
[
88
, Theorem 5.5]
).

Let us note that
[
88
, Theorem 5.5]
lists several additional interesting conditions equivalent for a density ideal to be isomorphic to an Erdős–Ulam ideal, e.g. being Katětov equivalent to
\mathcal{Z}
. The latter in particular implies that if
\mathcal{I}
is a density ideal and
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
does not have the finitely supported Nikodym property, then
\mathcal{I}<_{K}\mathcal{Z}
.

Combining Theorems
5.10
and
5.17
.(2), we get the following corollary.

Corollary 5.18
.

If
\mathcal{I}
is a P-ideal on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{K}\mathcal{Z}
, then
\mathcal{I}
has the web Nikodym property.

As a consequence of Theorem
5.3
we obtain the following important result, being a variant of Drewnowski, Florencio, and Paúl’s
[
21
, Example 4.3]
, implying that Erdős–Ulam ideals belonging to a special class (which includes
\mathcal{Z}
) have the Nikodym property (see also
[
25
, Theorem 6.8]
).

Theorem 5.19
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a density ideal on
\omega
. Then,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the Nikodym property if and only if
\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to an Erdős–Ulam ideal.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the Nikodym property, then
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property, but, as
\mathcal{I}
is a density ideal, this may only happen if
\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to an Erdős–Ulam ideal, by Theorem
5.17
.(3).

Assume then that
\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to an Erdős–Ulam ideal. Again by Theorem
5.17
.(3) the space
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property. As
\mathcal{I}
is a P-ideal, Theorem
5.3
yields that the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has the Nikodym property.
∎

Theorems
5.10
and
5.19
immediately yield together the following result (see also Corollary
6.7
).

Corollary 5.20
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a density ideal on
\omega
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{I}
has the Nikodym property,

(2)

\mathcal{I}
has the strong Nikodym property,

(3)

\mathcal{I}
has the web Nikodym property,

(4)

\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to an Erdős–Ulam ideal.

Remark 5.21
.

Note that, e.g. by Kwela
et al.

[
59
, Theorem 3]
, there are
\mathfrak{c}
many Katětov incomparable density ideals, hence there are
\mathfrak{c}
many Katětov incomparable density ideals which are not isomorphic to Erdős–Ulam ideals (see
[
58
, Section 4]
). Consequently, by Corollary
5.20
, there are
\mathfrak{c}
many non-isomorphic density ideals without the Nikodym property.

On the other hand, by Kwela
[
58
, Propositions 5 and 6]
there are
\mathfrak{c}
many pairwise non-isomorphic Erdős–Ulam ideals, and hence Corollary
5.20
yields
\mathfrak{c}
many pairwise non-isomorphic density ideals with the web Nikodym property.

Example 5.22
.

The preceding results give us several new examples of ideals on
\omega
with the web Nikodym property. The first one is
\tr(\mathcal{N})
, which is a non-pathological P-ideal with the Nikodym property (see
[
88
, page 728]
) and so it has the web Nikodym property by Theorem
5.10
. By Corollary
5.20
, the logarithmic density zero ideal
\mathcal{Z}_{\log}
has the web Nikodym property, too. Finally, a large class of ideals with the web Nikodym property is given in Section
6.3
, see Remark
6.34
.

The following example, appealing to the class
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
of ideals (see Definition
1.5
), supplements Example
3.11
and expands Remark
5.21
.

Example 5.23
.

(Supplement to Example
3.11
). Let us consider the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
for some
\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
. By
[
88
, Corollary 8.3]
, it does not have the Nikodym property (since
N_{F}
homeomorphically embeds into
St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)
). Next, for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
the restricted algebra
{\wp(\omega)}_{A}=\wp(A)
is
\sigma
-complete, so it has the Nikodym property. Therefore, by Proposition
4.11
, every anti-Nikodym sequence on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
has a unique Nikodym concentration point, namely
p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
, which necessarily is strong.

Recall that in
[
88
]
it was shown that
\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
provided that
\mathcal{I}=\exh(\varphi)
where
\varphi
is a non-pathological lsc submeasure on
\omega
satisfying
\varphi(\omega)=\infty
; thus e.g. all summable ideals are in
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
. Consequently, the class
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
is large, that is, it contains
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
many non-isomorphic ideals (cf.
[
88
, Corollary 7.4]
). By Lemma
5.26
, it follows that there are
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
many non-isomorphic Boolean subalgebras of
{\wp(\omega)}
without the Nikodym property and carrying only anti-Nikodym sequences with unique Nikodym concentration points.

As we stated in Example
3.8
, if the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
of a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
contains a non-eventually constant convergent sequence, then
\mathcal{A}
admits an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures with a strong Nikodym concentration point. Since the space
N_{Fr}
is clearly homeomorphic to any space consisting of a non-eventually constant convergent sequence together with its limit, the example may be rephrased as follows: if
N_{Fr}
homeomorphically embeds into
St(\mathcal{A})
, then
\mathcal{A}
admits an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures with a strong Nikodym concentration point. The next proposition asserts that actually the latter statement holds true for any ideal
\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{A}
and the space
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
.

Proposition 5.24
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Boolean algebra such that
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
homeomorphically embeds into
St(\mathcal{A})
. Then,
\mathcal{A}
admits an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures with a unique strong Nikodym concentration point.

Proof.

Let
h\colon N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\to St(\mathcal{A})
be a homeomorphism onto the image
X=h\big[N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big]
. Set
x=h\big(p_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\big)
and
Y=X\setminus\{x\}
. By
[
88
, Theorem 4.1]
, there exists a disjointly supported sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of finitely supported non-negative measures on
St(\mathcal{A})
such that

(1)

\supp\big(\mu_{n}\big)\subseteq Y
for every
n\in\omega
,

(2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(St(\mathcal{A}))=\infty
, and

(3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus U)=0
for every clopen neighborhood
U
of
x
.

For each
n\in\omega
define the measure
\nu_{n}
via its Radon extension as

\widehat{\nu}_{n}=\mu_{n}(St(\mathcal{A}))\cdot\delta_{x}-\mu_{n}.

We claim that the sequence
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is anti-Nikodym on
\mathcal{A}
and
x
is its strong Nikodym concentration point. First, note that by (a) we have
\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\nu_{n}\|=\infty
. Second, by (c), for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
, if
A\not\in x
, then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{\nu}_{n}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=0-\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=0,

and if
A\in x
, then it holds

\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{\nu}_{n}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\mu_{n}(St(\mathcal{A}))-\mu_{n}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\Big)=

=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\mu_{n}(St(\mathcal{A}))-\mu_{n}\big(\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)-\mu_{n}\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\mu_{n}\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)\Big)=

=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\big(St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=0.

Consequently,
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is indeed anti-Nikodym.

We now show that
x
is a unique strong Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
. To achieve this, we prove that
x
is the only Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
and then appeal to Lemma
3.13
. By (b), for any
A\in y
we have

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\nu_{n}\restriction A\big\|\geq\sup_{n\in\omega}\big|\mu_{n}(St(\mathcal{A}))\big|=\infty,

so
x
is a Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
. If, on the other hand,
y\in St(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{x\}
, then for any
A\in y
there is
B\in y
such that
B\not\in x
and
B\leq A
. By (c), we then have

\sup_{n\in\omega}\big\|\nu_{n}\restriction B\big\|=\sup_{n\in\omega}\mu_{n}\big(\left[B\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)<\infty,

so
y
is not a Nikodym concentration point of
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
.
∎

By
[
88
, Corollary 7.4]
we immediately obtain the following result extending Example
3.8
. Note that the cardinal number
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
is here maximal possible.

Corollary 5.25
.

There exists a family
\mathcal{X}
of
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
many pairwise non-homeomorphic countable infinite spaces with exactly one non-isolated point such that, for any
X\in\mathcal{X}
, if
X
homeomorphically embeds into the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
of a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
, then
\mathcal{A}
admits an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures with a unique strong Nikodym concentration point.

5.2.
Large families of algebras with the Nikodym property and without the Grothendieck property

In this section we construct two large families
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
of Boolean algebras with the Nikodym property and without the Grothendieck property. The family
\mathcal{H}_{1}
consists of
\mathfrak{c}
algebras of the form
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
for a Borel (in fact,
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta}
) filter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
, and the family
\mathcal{H}_{2}
consists of
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
algebras of the form
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
for some non-analytic filter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
. These constructions supplement the results of Schachermayer
[
71
]
, Graves and Wheeler
[
44
]
, Valdivia
[
86
]
, and Drewnowski, Florencio, and Paúl
[
20
, Remark 4]
.

We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 5.26
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
be filters on
\omega
which are not ultrafilters. Assume that
\varphi\colon S_{\mathcal{F}}\to S_{\mathcal{G}}
is a homeomorphism. Then,
\varphi\restriction N_{\mathcal{F}}
is a homeomorphism of the spaces
N_{\mathcal{F}}
and
N_{\mathcal{G}}
and the filters
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\colon S_{\mathcal{F}}\to S_{\mathcal{G}}
be a homeomorphism. It satisfies
\varphi[\omega]=\omega
, as the points in
\omega
are the only isolated points in both
S_{\mathcal{F}}
and
S_{\mathcal{G}}
. Note that for every
x\in S_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\setminus\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}
and
y\in S_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\setminus\big\{p_{\mathcal{G}}\big\}
the sets
x
and
y
are ultrafilters in
{\wp(\omega)}
. On the other hand, both
p_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{F}
and
p_{\mathcal{G}}=\mathcal{G}
are not ultrafilters in
{\wp(\omega)}
. It follows that
\varphi[\mathcal{F}]
is also not an ultrafilter in
{\wp(\omega)}
. Consequently,
\varphi\big(p_{\mathcal{F}}\big)=p_{\mathcal{G}}
, and so
\varphi\restriction N_{\mathcal{F}}
is a homeomorphism between
N_{\mathcal{F}}
and
N_{\mathcal{G}}
. Thus, by
[
63
, Proposition 3.10.(2)]
, the filters
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are isomorphic.
∎

Note that, for a filter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
, the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}^{*}
belongs to a Borel class
\Gamma
(resp. is analytic) if and only if
\mathcal{F}
belongs to the Borel class
\Gamma
(resp. is analytic).

Theorem 5.27
.

There is a family
\big\{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\big\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta}
filters on
\omega
such that the family
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\big\{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}}\colon\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\big\}
consists of pairwise non-isomorphic Boolean algebras, each of them having the Nikodym property but not the Grothendieck property.

Proof.

By the results from Kwela
[
58
, Section 4]
, there is a family
\big\{\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\big\}
of pairwise non-isomorphic Erdős–Ulam ideals. Every Erdős–Ulam ideal, as a density ideal, is
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta}
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{*}
for every
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
, and set
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\big\{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}}\colon\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\big\}
. By Lemma
5.26
, the spaces
S_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}}
,
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
, are pairwise non-homeomorphic, and so the Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}}
,
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
, are pairwise non-isomorphic. Next, by
[
63
, Theorem C]
, for all
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
the space
N_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}}
has the so-called bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig property, and so, by
[
63
, Corollary D]
, the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}}
does not have the Grothendieck property. Finally, by Theorem
5.19
, the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}}
has the Nikodym property for every
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
.
∎

Remark 5.28
.

Let us note that in the latter proof we could use as well the family of ideals given by Theorem
6.30
instead of the one from
[
58
, Section 4]
, since it also consists of analytic P-ideals (hence,
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta}
ideals) with the Nikodym property and for which
[
63
, Theorem C and Corollary D]
are applicable.

For a pair of topological spaces
X
,
Y
and points
x\in X
,
y\in Y
, by
(X\sqcup Y)/\{x,y\}
we mean the disjoint topological union
X\sqcup Y
of
X
and
Y
with the points
x
and
y
identified as one point. Both of the spaces
X
and
Y
are naturally identified inside
(X\sqcup Y)/\{x,y\}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
be filters on
\omega
. Fix a partition
(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
of
\omega
into two infinite sets and bijections
b_{i}\colon\omega\to\Omega_{i}
,
i=1,2
. Then, we define the filter
\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}
on
\omega
as follows: for every
A\in{\wp(\omega)}
,
A\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}
if and only if
b_{i}^{-1}\big[A\cap\Omega_{i}\big]\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
for
i=1,2
. For each
i=1,2
and the subspace
N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\prime}=\Omega_{i}\cup\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big\}
of
N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
, the bijection
b_{i}
gives rise to the natural homeomorphism
h_{i}\colon N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}\to N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\prime}
with
h_{i}\big(p_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}\big)=p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
, identifying the spaces
N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
and
N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\prime}
. Consequently, the space
N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
can be naturally identified with
\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\sqcup N_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big)/\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}},p_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big\}
, with the points
p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}
and
p_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}
glued together as the point
p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
. The following lemma extends this identification onto the spaces
S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
and
\big(S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\sqcup S_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big)/\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}},p_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big\}
.

Lemma 5.29
.

For every filters
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
on
\omega
, the space
S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
can be naturally identified with the quotient space
\big(S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\sqcup S_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big)/\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}},p_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big\}
.

Proof.

We first show that

\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}\cap\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}=\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big\}.

Of course, we have

\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big\}=N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\prime}\cap N_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}\cap\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}.

So, assume there is

x\in\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}\cap\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}

such that
x\neq p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
. As
x\neq p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
, for each
i\in\{1,2\}
there is
A_{i}\subseteq\Omega_{i}
such that
b_{i}^{-1}\big[A_{i}\big]\in\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}
and
x\in\left[A_{i}\right]_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
. Note that the sets
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are closed in
N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
. Moreover,
A_{1}\cap A_{2}\subseteq\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}=\emptyset
, so they are also disjoint. Consequently, since the space
N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
is
C^{*}
-embedded in
S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
, the Urysohn Extension Theorem (
[
41
, Theorem 1.17]
) implies that

\overline{A_{1}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}\cap\overline{A_{2}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}=\emptyset,

which is a contradiction as the latter intersection contains
x
. Consequently, (
*
) holds true.

Second, we trivially have

S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}=\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}=\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\prime}\cup N_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}=\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}\cup\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}.

Finally, for every
i\in\{1,2\}
we have

\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}=\beta\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\prime}\big).

Indeed, fix
i=1,2
and note that every
f\in C_{b}\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\prime}\big)
extends to the function
f^{\prime}\in C_{b}\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big)
defined for every
n\in\Omega_{1-i}
by
f^{\prime}(n)=f\big(p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big)
. Since by Proposition
5.1
.(5) the space
N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
is
C^{*}
-embedded in
S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
,
f^{\prime}

is

and therefore, by

and again Proposition

\overline{N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\prime}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}=\beta\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\prime}\big)
.

Now, combining (
*
), (
**
), and (
*\!*\!*
), we get the natural identification of the quotient space

\Big(\beta\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\prime}\big)\sqcup\beta\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\prime}\big)\Big)/\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{1},p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{2}\big\},

where for
i=1,2
the point
p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{i}
is the copy of the point
p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
in the subspace
N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\prime}
of the disjoint union
\beta\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\prime}\big)\sqcup\beta\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\prime}\big)
, with the space
S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
. As for each
i=1,2
, using the homeomorphism

, we have the identification
\beta\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\prime}\big)=\beta\big(N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}\big)=S_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
, we get the required natural identification of the spaces
\big(S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\sqcup S_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big)/\big\{p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}},p_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big\}
and
S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
.

Theorem 5.30
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
be two filters on
\omega
. Then, the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
has the Nikodym property if and only if both algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}
have the Nikodym property.

Proof.

We will use the identification described in Lemma
5.29
.

Assume first that both
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}
have the Nikodym property, but
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
does not. Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a sequence of non-negative measures on
St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big)
as in Corollary
5.2
. By condition (2), there exists
i\in\{1,2\}
for which we have
\sup_{n\in\omega}\mu_{n}\big(S_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}\big)=\infty
. For every
n\in\omega
, define the non-negative measure
\nu_{n}
on
S_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
simply by
\nu_{n}=\mu_{n}\restriction S_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
.

We claim that the sequence
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
too satisfies conditions (1)–(3) of Corollary
5.2
. Indeed, (2) holds trivially. By condition (1) for
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
, for every
n\in\omega
we have
p_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}\not\in\supp\big(\mu_{n}\big)
, so
p_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}\not\in\supp\big(\nu_{n}\big)
, hence condition (1) is satisfied by
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
as well. By condition (3) for
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
, for every
A\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{n}\big(\left[\omega\setminus A\right]_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}\big)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{n}\Big(\overline{\omega\setminus A}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}}\Big)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\Big(\overline{\Omega_{i}\setminus b_{i}[A]}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}\Big)=

=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\Big(\overline{\Omega_{i}\setminus b_{i}[A]}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}\cup\overline{\Omega_{1-i}\setminus\Omega_{1-i}}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}\Big)=

=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\Big(\overline{\omega\setminus\big(b_{i}[A]\cup\Omega_{1-i}\big)}^{S_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}}\Big)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}\Big(\left[\omega\setminus\big(b_{i}[A]\cup\Omega_{i-1}\big)\right]_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}\Big)=0;

it follows that condition (3) also holds for
\big\langle\nu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
. Consequently,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
does not have the Nikodym property, a contradiction.

Assume now that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
has the Nikodym property, but
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
does not, so there exists a sequence
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of non-negative measures on
St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}\big)
as in Corollary
5.2
. It is however immediate that
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is also a sequence on
St\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big)
as in Corollary
5.2
and so
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}}
does not have the Nikodym property either, which is a contradiction.
∎

We are ready to construct the second aforementioned family
\mathcal{H}_{2}
of Boolean algebras.

Theorem 5.31
.

There is a family
\big\{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<2^{\mathfrak{c}}\big\}
of non-analytic filters on
\omega
such that the family
\mathcal{H}_{2}=\big\{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}}\colon\alpha<2^{\mathfrak{c}}\big\}
consists of pairwise non-isomorphic Boolean algebras, each of them having the Nikodym property but not the Grothendieck property.

Proof.

Recall that every ultrafilter on
\omega
is non-analytic. For each ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\omega
let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{U}\oplus\mathcal{Z}^{*}
. Then, for every
\mathcal{U}
the space
N_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{U}}}
has the bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig property by
[
63
, Theorem C and Proposition 5.17]
, and so the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{U}}}
does not have the Grothendieck property by
[
63
, Corollary D]
. On the other hand, by Theorem
5.30
each
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{U}}}
has the Nikodym property, since both
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}}={\wp(\omega)}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}}
have the Nikodym property (by the Nikodym–Andô theorem and by Theorem
5.19
).

Finally, since there are
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
ultrafilters on
\omega
and
each family of pairwise isomorphic filters has the cardinality at most
\mathfrak{c}
, there is a family
\big\{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<2^{\mathfrak{c}}\big\}
of pairwise non-isomorphic filters of the form
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{U}}
. Set
\mathcal{H}_{2}=\big\{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}}\colon\alpha<2^{\mathfrak{c}}\big\}
. By Lemma
5.26
, the spaces
S_{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}}
,
\alpha<2^{\mathfrak{c}}
, are pairwise non-homeomorphic, and so the algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}}
,
\alpha<2^{\mathfrak{c}}
, are pairwise non-isomorphic.
∎

Corollary 6.7

Let

(2)

Lemma 6.8

For every lsc submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega

\psi

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be an lsc submeasure on
\omega
such that
\varphi(\omega)=\infty

, we can define a sequence
\big\langle k_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle\subseteq\omega

for each

For each

and let

be a submeasure on

We will show that

6.2.
Non-atomic ideals and the Strong Nested Partition Property

Let us recall the following standard definition of strongly non-atomic submeasures and its counterpart for ideals.

Definition 6.15
.

A submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is said to be
strongly non-atomic
if for every

\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite partition
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
of
\omega
such that
\varphi(A_{i})<\varepsilon
for each
i=1,\ldots,k
.

Given two partitions
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
\omega
, we write
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\preceq\mathcal{P}
if for any
A\in\mathcal{P}
and
B\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
we either have
A\cap B=\emptyset
or
B\subseteq A
.

Definition 6.16
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
is
non-atomic
if there exists a
\preceq
-decreasing sequence
\big\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of finite partitions of
\omega
such that for every
\subseteq
-decreasing sequence
\big\langle A_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
and every set
E\subseteq\omega
such that
E\setminus A_{n}
is finite for every
n\in\omega
we have
E\in\mathcal{I}
.

For basic information concerning non-atomic submeasures and ideals, see
[
8
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
36
]
. In particular, note that for a strongly non-atomic submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
the ideal
\exh(\varphi)
is also non-atomic, but the converse may not hold—it holds provided that
\varphi=\psi^{\bullet}
for some lsc submeasure
\psi
on
\omega
(see
[
23
, Section 3]
for details).

The relations between the Nikodym property and non-atomic submeasures and ideals were first studied by Drewnowski, Florencio and Paúl
[
21
]
, where it was proved, among others, that for a strongly non-atomic submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
the ideal
\mathcal{Z}(\varphi)=\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\varphi(A)=0\}
has the Nikodym property (so in particular the asymptotic density zero ideal
\mathcal{Z}
has the Nikodym property, cf.
[
20
, Proposition 6]
). Let us note here that a bit earlier Paštéka
[
69
]
proved that such ideal
\mathcal{Z}(\varphi)
has (PSP).

The following non-atomicity property of ideals, shortly called (NPP), was introduced by Stuart
[
82
]
(for rings of sets). We also introduce its strong variant, because it is somewhat easier to handle than (NPP) and, as shown in Proposition
6.18
, it unifies both (NPP) and non-atomicity of ideals.

Definition 6.17
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
has the
Nested Partition Property
(in short,
(NPP)
), if there exists a
\preceq
-decreasing sequence
\big\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of finite partitions of
\omega
such that for every sequence
\big\langle E_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of pairwise disjoint elements of
\mathcal{I}
there is an infinite set
M\in\omega
such that for every
\subseteq
-decreasing sequence
\big\langle A_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
we have

\bigcup_{n\in M}\big(E_{n}\cap A_{n}\big)\in\mathcal{I}.

If the set
M
can be always chosen to be
\omega
, then we say that
\mathcal{I}
has the
Strong Nested Partition Property
(in short,
(SNPP)
).

Of course, if an ideal has (SNPP), then it has (NPP).

Proposition 6.18
.

If an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
has (SNPP), then
\mathcal{I}
is non-atomic.

Proof.

Let
\big\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be the
\preceq
-decreasing sequence of finite partitions of
\omega
witnessing (SNPP) of
\mathcal{I}
. Assume that
\big\langle A_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
is a
\subseteq
-decreasing sequence and that
E\subseteq\omega
is such that
E\setminus A_{n}
is finite for every
n\in\omega
. Moreover, without loss of generality we may assume that
\mathcal{P}_{0}=\{\omega\}
and
A_{0}=\omega
. Let
\kappa=\Big|\bigcap_{n\in\omega}A_{n}\Big|
and enumerate
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}A_{n}=\big\{x_{m}\colon m<\kappa\big\}
. For each
m\in\omega
we define

E_{m}=\Big(\big\{x_{m}\big\}\cup\big(A_{m}\setminus A_{m+1}\big)\Big)\cap E

if
m<\kappa
, and

E_{m}=\big(A_{m}\setminus A_{m+1}\big)\cap E

if
\kappa\leq m<\omega
. Then, the sets
E_{m}
’s are pairwise disjoint, and we have
E=\bigcup_{m\in\omega}E_{m}
, since

\bigcup_{m<\kappa}\Big(\big\{x_{m}\big\}\cup\big(A_{m}\setminus A_{m+1}\big)\Big)\cup\bigcup_{\kappa\leq m<\omega}\big(A_{m}\setminus A_{m+1}\big)=A_{0}=\omega.

Furthermore, for each
m\in\omega
the set
E_{m}
is finite, since
E_{m}\subseteq\big(E\setminus A_{m+1}\big)\cup\big\{x_{m}\big\}
, thus in particular
E_{m}\in\mathcal{I}
. For every
m\in\omega
we also have
E_{m}\subseteq A_{m}
, so
E_{m}\cap A_{m}=E_{m}
. Consequently,
E=\bigcup_{m\in\omega}E_{m}\in\mathcal{I}
, as required.
∎

The next corollary, being a supplement of Corollary
6.12
, shows that the converse to Proposition
6.18
also holds, provided that a considered ideal is of the form
\exh(\varphi)
for some lsc submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
.

.

For every lsc submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
and the ideal
\mathcal{I}=\exh(\varphi)
, conditions (1)–(6) of Corollary

are equivalent to the following ones:

(7)

\varphi^{\bullet}
is strongly non-atomic,

(8)

\mathcal{I}
is non-atomic,

(9)

\mathcal{I}
has (SNPP).

Proof.

Equivalence (4)
\Leftrightarrow

, Theorem 3.6]
. Implication (9)

.

Remark 6.20
.

Recall that an infinite set
\{a_{0}<a_{1}<a_{2}<...\}\subseteq\omega
is
lacunary
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n+1}-a_{n}=\infty
. Let
\mathcal{L}
denote the ideal generated by all lacunary subsets of
\omega
. By
[

, Proposition 2]
(see also
[
1
]
,
[
7
]
, or
[

]
)
\mathcal{L}
is not contained in any summable ideal, so it has (ASP), but by
[

, Example 4.2]
it does not have the Nikodym property.

Drewnowski and Łuczak
[

, Theorem 4.1]
proved that if an lsc submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is such that
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\exh(\varphi)
, then the core
\varphi^{\bullet}
is strongly non-atomic. Since
\conv\leq_{K}\mathcal{L}
(by
[
57
, Lemma 3.2]
and

6.12
and

provides a generalization of the latter fact: if an lsc submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is such that

is strongly non-atomic.

It was proved in
[

]
that every ideal on
\omega
with (NPP) has the Nikodym property, the converse however does not hold—in the next section we provide an example of an ideal which has the Nikodym property but not (NPP), see Corollary
6.35
. Below we show that an ideal with (SNPP) has (LGBPs), which, by
[
21
, Proposition 2.2]
, is also a property stronger than the Nikodym property).

Proposition 6.21
.

If
\varphi
is an lsc submeasure on
\omega
such that
\varphi(\omega)=\infty
, then for every
\preceq
-decreasing sequence
\big\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of finite partitions of
\omega
there exist a
\subseteq
-decreasing sequence
\big\langle A_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
and a sequence
\big\langle F_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of
\omega
such that
F_{n}\subseteq A_{n}
and
\varphi\big(F_{n}\big)\geq n+1
for every
n\in\omega
.

Proof.

Fix a lsc submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
such that
\varphi(\omega)=\infty
. Let
\big\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a
\preceq
-decreasing sequence of finite partitions of
\omega
. By the subadditivity of
\varphi
, there exists
A_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
such that
\varphi\big(A_{0}\big)=\infty
. As
\varphi
is lsc, we can choose a finite set
F_{0}\subseteq A_{0}
with
\varphi\big(F_{0}\big)\geq 1
. Now, since
\varphi\big(A_{0}\setminus F_{0}\big)=\infty
, there exists
A_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
such that
A_{1}\subseteq A_{0}
and
\varphi\big(A_{1}\setminus F_{0}\big)=\infty
. Again, choose a finite set
F_{1}\subseteq\big(A_{1}\setminus F_{0}\big)
with
\varphi\big(F_{1}\big)\geq 2
. Since
\varphi\big(A_{1}\setminus\big(F_{0}\cup F_{1}\big)\big)=\infty
, there exists
A_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
such that
A_{2}\subseteq A_{1}
and
\varphi\big(A_{2}\setminus\big(F_{0}\cup F_{1}\big)\big)=\infty
. And so on.

By repeating the above argument, we get a
\subseteq
-decreasing sequence
\big\langle A_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
and a sequence
\big\langle F_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of
\omega
such that
F_{n}\subseteq A_{n}
and
\varphi\big(F_{n}\big)\geq n+1
for every
n\in\omega
.
∎

Proposition 6.22
.

Every non-atomic ideal on
\omega
has (LGBPs).

Proof.

Assume that for an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
we have
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\fin(\varphi)
for some lsc submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
such that
\varphi(\omega)=\infty
. To show that
\mathcal{I}
is non-atomic, let
\big\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a
\preceq
-decreasing sequence of finite partitions of
\omega
. Let
\big\langle A_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
and
\big\langle F_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be sequences as in Proposition
6.21
.
For
E=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}
, we have
\varphi(E)=\infty
, so
E\notin\mathcal{I}
, even though
E\setminus A_{n}
is finite for every

, which is a contradiction.
∎

Combining Propositions
6.18
and

, we get the following aforementioned result.

Corollary 6.23
.

Every ideal on

with (SNPP) has (LGBPs).

6.3.
Hypergraph ideals

In this subsection we construct a family of
\mathfrak{c}
many pairwise non-isomorphic non-pathological ideals on
\omega
which have the Nikodym property but are not totally bounded or, even stronger, do not have (NPP). In order to do this, we exploit and expand the construction due to Alon, Drewnowski, and Łuczak
[
4
]
of an ideal which has the Nikodym property but is not non-atomic. This construction relies on the notion of a hypergraph ideal, presented below.

Recall that a
hypergraph
is a pair
H=(V(H),E(H))
, where
V(H)
is a finite set and
E(H)\subseteq\wp(V(H))
.

Definition 6.25
.

A submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is
hypergraph
if there exist a sequence
\big\langle H_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of hypergraphs, a sequence
\big\langle G_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of finite non-empty disjoint subsets of
\omega
, and for each
n\in\omega
a bijection
b_{n}\colon G_{n}\to V\big(H_{n}\big)
such that

\varphi(A)=\sup_{e\in E(H_{n})}\frac{\big|b_{n}\big[A\cap G_{n}\big]\cap e\big|}{|e|}.

In this case, we will say that
\varphi
is
induced
by the sequence
\big\langle H_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of hypergraphs.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
is
hypergraph
if there exists a hypergraph submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{I}=\exh(\varphi)
.

Remark 6.26
.

Note that every hypergraph ideal is an analytic P-ideal. Moreover, every hypergraph ideal is a generalized density ideal. On the other hand, every density ideal induced by a sequence of uniform measures is hypergraph.

Let
H
be a hypergraph. Recall that the
chromatic number

\chi(H)
of
H
is the minimal number
n\in\omega
for which there exists a partition
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
of
V(H)
such that no
e\in E(H)
is contained in some
C_{i}
. For a subset
W\subseteq V(H)
we set
H\restriction W=(W,E(H)\cap\wp(W))
. We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 6.27
.

Let
\big\langle H_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a sequence of hypergraphs such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\chi\big(H_{n}\big)=\infty
. Let
\big\langle G_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a sequence of finite non-empty disjoint subsets of
\omega
and for each
n\in\omega
let
b_{n}\colon G_{n}\to V\big(H_{n}\big)
be a bijection.
Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
be a finite partition of
\omega
into infinite sets. Then, there are
1\leq j_{0}\leq k
and
I\in\left[\omega\right]^{\omega}
such that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n\in I\end{subarray}}\chi\Big(H_{n}\restriction b_{n}\big[G_{n}\cap A_{j_{0}}\big]\Big)=\infty.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that for each
1\leq j\leq k
there is
K_{j}\in\omega
such that

\chi\Big(H_{n}\restriction b_{n}\big[G_{n}\cap A_{j}\big]\Big)\leq K_{j}

for every
n\in\omega
. Then, for every
n\in\omega
we have

\chi\big(H_{n}\big)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\chi\Big(H_{n}\restriction b_{n}\big[G_{n}\cap A_{j}\big]\Big)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}K_{j},

so

\sup_{n\in\omega}\chi\big(H_{n}\big)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}K_{j}<\infty,

which is impossible.
∎

The idea behind the proof of the next proposition closely follows the first part of the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
, showing that for no submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
induced by a sequence
\big\langle H_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of hypergraphs such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\chi\big(H_{n}\big)=\infty
the ideal
\exh(\varphi)
is non-atomic.

Proposition 6.28
.

If a hypergraph submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is induced by a sequence
\big\langle H_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of hypergraphs such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\chi\big(H_{n}\big)=\infty
, then the ideal
\mathcal{I}=\exh(\varphi)
does not have the Nested Partition Property.

Proof.

Let us fix sequences
\big\langle G_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
and
\big\langle b_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
as in Definition
6.25
. Let also
\big\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a
\preceq
-decreasing sequence of finite partitions of
\omega
. We will show that
\big\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
does not satisfy the requirements of Definition
6.17
, which will prove that
\mathcal{I}
does not have (NPP).

We start as follows. There is
N_{0}\in\omega
such that
\chi\big(H_{n}\big)>\big|\mathcal{P}_{0}\big|
for every
n\geq N_{0}
. By Lemma
6.27
there are
A_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
and
I_{0}\in\left[\omega\setminus N_{0}\right]^{\omega}
such that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n\in I_{0}\end{subarray}}\chi\Big(H_{n}\restriction b_{n}\big[G_{n}\cap A_{0}\big]\Big)=\infty.

Let
n_{0}\in I_{0}
be such that there exists
e_{0}\in E\Big(H_{n_{0}}\restriction b_{n_{0}}\big[G_{n_{0}}\cap A_{0}\big]\Big)
(such
n_{0}
exists, since otherwise the above limit would be
1
).

Similarly, there is
N_{1}>n_{0}
such that
\chi\big(H_{n}\big)>\big|\mathcal{P}_{1}\big|
for every
n\geq N_{1}
and, again by Lemma
6.27
, there are
A_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
,
A_{1}\subseteq A_{0}
, and
I_{1}\in\left[I_{0}\setminus N_{1}\right]^{\omega}
such that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n\in I_{1}\end{subarray}}\chi\Big(H_{n}\restriction b_{n}\big[G_{n}\cap A_{1}\big]\Big)=\infty.

Let
n_{1}\in I_{1}
be such that there exists
e_{1}\in E\Big(H_{n_{1}}\restriction b_{n_{1}}\big[G_{n_{1}}\cap A_{1}\big]\Big)
.

Repeat the above argument until you get an
\subseteq
-decreasing sequence
\big\langle A_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
, a strictly increasing sequence
\big\langle n_{k}\in\omega\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
, and a sequence
\big\langle e_{k}\in E\big(H_{n_{k}}\big)\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
such that
b_{n_{k}}^{-1}\big[e_{k}\big]\subseteq A_{k}
and
e_{k}\in E\Big(H_{n_{k}}\restriction b_{n_{k}}\big[G_{n_{k}}\cap A_{k}\big]\Big)
for each
k\in\omega
.

For each
k\in\omega
put
B_{k}=b_{n_{k}}^{-1}\big[e_{k}\big]
; as
B_{k}
is a finite set, it belongs to
\mathcal{I}
. For each
M\in\left[\omega\right]^{\omega}
let

B_{M}=\bigcup_{k\in M}\big(B_{k}\cap A_{k}\big)=\bigcup_{k\in M}B_{k}.

We have

\varphi^{\bullet}\big(B_{M}\big)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{e\in E(H_{n})}\frac{\Big|b_{n}\big[B_{M}\cap G_{n}\big]\cap e\Big|}{|e|}\geq\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\to\infty\\
k\in M\end{subarray}}\frac{\Big|b_{n_{k}}\big[B_{k}\cap G_{n_{k}}\big]\cap e_{k}\Big|}{|e_{k}|}=

=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\to\infty\\
k\in M\end{subarray}}\frac{\big|e_{k}\cap e_{k}\big|}{\big|e_{k}\big|}=1,

so
B_{M}\not\in\mathcal{I}
, even though
B_{M}\setminus A_{n}
is finite for every
n\in\omega
.
∎

Corollary 6.29
.

If a hypergraph submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is induced by a sequence
\big\langle H_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
of hypergraphs such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\chi\big(H_{n}\big)=\infty
, then the ideal
\mathcal{I}=\exh(\varphi)
is not totally bounded.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a hypergraph submeasure satisfying the premises. By Proposition
6.28
it follows that the ideal
\mathcal{I}
does not have (NPP) and so, by Corollary
6.19
, it is not totally bounded.
∎

Theorem 6.30
.

There is a family of
\mathfrak{c}
many pairwise non-isomorphic hypergraph ideals which have the Nikodym property but are not totally bounded.

Proof.

Let
\big\langle H_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be the sequence of Kneser hypergraphs defined by Alon, Drewnowski, and Łuczak in
[
4
, Section 4]
, and let
\varphi_{ADL}
be a hypergraph submeasure on
\omega
induced by the sequence
\big\langle H_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
, with sequences
\big\langle G_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
and
\big\langle b_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
as in Definition
6.25
. Set
\mathcal{I}_{ADL}=\exh\big(\varphi_{ADL}\big)
. As for every
k\in\omega
the edges of
H_{k}
are of size
2^{k}
, we can recursively define a strictly increasing sequence
\big\langle n_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
such that

|e|>k\cdot\sum_{i<k}\big|G_{n_{i}}\big|

holds for every
e\in E\big(H_{n_{k}}\big)
and
k\in\omega
. For any
M\in\left[\omega\right]^{\omega}
let us set
\mathcal{I}_{M}=\exh(\varphi_{M})
, where
\varphi_{M}
is the hypegraph submeasure on
\omega
defined for every
A\in{\wp(\omega)}
as

\varphi_{M}(A)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in M\end{subarray}}\sup_{e\in E(H_{n_{k}})}\frac{\Big|b_{n_{k}}\big[A\cap G_{n_{k}}\big]\cap e\Big|}{|e|}.

By
[
4
, Corollary 3.4]
we have
\chi\big(H_{n_{k}}\big)\geq 2^{n_{k}}
for every
k\in\omega
, and so, by Corollary
6.29
, the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{M}
is not totally bounded for any
M\in\left[\omega\right]^{\omega}
. Next,
\mathcal{I}_{ADL}
has the Nikodym property by
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
, and so the space
N_{\mathcal{I}_{ADL}^{*}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property by Theorem
5.10
. Thus, the space
N_{\mathcal{I}_{M}^{*}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property for every
M\in\left[\omega\right]^{\omega}
, by
[
88
, Proposition 5.2]
and the fact that
\mathcal{I}_{ADL}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{M}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{I}_{M}
has the Nikodym property for every
M\in\left[\omega\right]^{\omega}
, too.

Let
\big\{M_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\big\}
be a family of infinite pairwise almost disjoint subsets of
\omega
, that is, the intersection
M_{\alpha}\cap M_{\beta}
is finite for every
\alpha\neq\beta<\mathfrak{c}
. We will show that the ideals
\big\{\mathcal{I}_{M_{\alpha}}\colon\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\big\}
are pairwise non-isomorphic. Fix
\alpha\neq\beta<\mathfrak{c}
and a bijection
\phi\colon\omega\to\omega
. Let
\big\langle m_{i}\colon\ i\in\omega\big\rangle
be an increasing enumeration of
M_{\alpha}\setminus M_{\beta}
. Let us fix an arbitrary edge
e_{k}\in E\big(H_{n_{k}}\big)
for any
k\in\omega
. By (
*
), for every
k\geq 2
there exists
A_{k}\subseteq G_{n_{k}}
such that
b_{n_{k}}\big[A_{k}\big]\subseteq e_{k}
,
\big|A_{k}\big|\geq\cdot\big|e_{k}\big|/2
, and

\phi\big[A_{k}\big]\cap\bigcup_{i<k}G_{n_{i}}=\emptyset.

Let
A=\bigcup_{i\in\omega}A_{m_{i}}
. We have
A\notin\mathcal{I}_{M_{\alpha}}
, as for every
i\geq 2
we have

\varphi_{M_{\alpha}}^{\bullet}(A)\geq\frac{\Big|b_{n_{m_{i}}}\big[A\cap G_{n_{m_{i}}}\big]\cap e_{m_{i}}\Big|}{\big|e_{m_{i}}\big|}=\frac{\big|b_{n_{m_{i}}}\big[A_{m_{i}}\big]\cap e_{m_{i}}\big|}{\big|e_{m_{i}}\big|}\geq 1/2.

We will show that
\phi[A]\in\mathcal{I}_{M_{\beta}}
, which will conclude the proof. By (
**
), for every
k\geq 2
we have

\phi[A]\cap G_{n_{k}}=\bigcup_{i\in\omega}\big(\phi\big[A_{m_{i}}\big]\cap G_{n_{k}}\big)\subseteq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\omega\\
m_{i}\leq k\end{subarray}}\phi\big[A_{m_{i}}\big].

Thus, for any
k\in M_{\beta}\setminus M_{\alpha}
such that
k\geq 2
we get

\phi[A]\cap G_{n_{k}}\subseteq\bigcup_{i<k}\phi\big[A_{i}\big].

By (
*
), for every
k\in\omega
and
e\in E\big(H_{n_{k}}\big)
, we have

\frac{\sum_{i<k}\big|A_{i}\big|}{|e|}\leq\frac{\sum_{i<k}\big|G_{n_{i}}\big|}{|e|}<1/k,

and so, for almost all
k\in M_{\beta}
, it holds

\sup_{e\in E(H_{n_{k}})}\frac{\Big|b_{n_{k}}\big[\phi[A]\cap G_{n_{k}}\big]\cap e\Big|}{|e|}<1/k.

Consequently, we get that
\phi[A]\in\mathcal{I}_{M_{\beta}}
, as required.
∎

As a corollary, we get a positive answer to the second author’s
[
88
, Question 9.1]
.

Corollary 6.31
.

There exists a non-pathological lsc submeasure
\varphi
such that the ideal
\exh(\varphi)
is not totally bounded and the space
N_{\exh(\varphi)^{*}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property.

Proof.

Let
M\in\left[\omega\right]^{\omega}
be arbitrary and let
\varphi=\varphi_{M}
be the corresponding submeasure defined in the proof of Theorem
6.30
(we can here of course consider also the submeasure
\varphi_{ADL}
). By Remark
6.26
,
\varphi
is a non-pathological lsc submeasure. Moreover, the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{M}=\exh(\varphi)
is not totally bounded but has the Nikodym property. Consequently, the space
N_{\exh(\varphi)^{*}}
has the finitely supported Nikodym property by Theorem
5.10
.
∎

Remark 6.32
.

For every
M\in\left[\omega\right]^{\omega}
the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{M}
from the proof of Theorem
6.30
has the Nikodym property and does not have the Local-to-Global Boundedness Property for submeasures as it is not totally bounded. This provides yet another class of examples confirming the conjecture of Drewnowski, Florencio, and Paúl
[
21
, Conjecture, page 147]
, cf. Theorem
6.3
.

Remark 6.33
.

Let us also note that, for each
M\in\left[\omega\right]^{\omega}
, even though the core
\varphi^{\bullet}_{M}
of the submeasure
\varphi_{M}
from the proof of Theorem

and number

such that
\varphi_{M}^{\bullet}(B)=\alpha
, see
[

, Proposition 5.2 and Theorem 6.2]
and
[

for details (note here that every strongly non-atomic submeasure is automatically convexly non-atomic, see
[
25
, page 494]
).

Remark 6.34
.

Recall that each hypergraph ideal is a P-ideal, therefore if a hypergraph ideal
\mathcal{I}
has the Nikodym property, then by Theorem
5.10

\mathcal{I}
has the web Nikodym property. In particular, all the ideals considered in (the proof of) Theorem

have the web Nikodym property.

Stuart
[

, page 153]
asked if every hereditary ring of sets with the Nikodym property has the Nested Partition Property. Theorem

(and its proof) yields a negative answer to this question, since for every

; the same concerns the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{ADL}

.

There exists an ideal on
\omega
with the Nikodym property but without (NPP).

7.
The Tukey class of
(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N},\leq_{K})

As we mentioned earlier, Drewnowski and Paúl
[
25
, Theorem 3.5]
proved that the Nikodym property coincides with the Positive Summability Property in the class of P-ideals on
\omega
. On the other hand, Theorem
5.10
asserts that in this class the Nikodym property of an ideal
\mathcal{I}
is equivalent for the space
N_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
to have the finitely supported Nikodym property, i.e.
\mathcal{I}\not\in\mathcal{AN}
. Combining these two results, we get that, given a P-ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
,
\mathcal{I}
has (PSP) if and only if
\mathcal{I}\not\in\mathcal{AN}
. In this section we generalize this observation to the class of
all
ideals on
\omega
. This result actually already follows from Filipów–Szuca’s
[
35
, Theorem 3.3]
and the proof of their
[
35
, Proposition 3.7]
(cf. also
[
39
, Theorem 11.2]
), however, for the sake of completeness, we present here a very short proof of this fact, based on the arguments used to prove
[
25
, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 3.5]
. (Our method is thus more measure-theoretic in spirit and hence fits the more traditional research on the Nikodym property.) As a corollary, we get that the order
(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N},\leq_{K})
, i.e. the set
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
together with the Katětov ordering
\leq_{K}
, is Tukey equivalent to the standard order
(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
.

Theorem 7.1
.

For every density submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
such that
\varphi(\omega)=\infty
, the ideal
\exh(\varphi)
is contained in some summable ideal.

Consequently, for every ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
, we have
\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
if and only if there exists a summable ideal
\mathcal{J}
such that
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
.

The latter statement in Theorem
7.1
can be of course shortly expressed as follows: for every ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
,
\mathcal{I}\not\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
if and only if
\mathcal{I}
has (PSP).

For the proof we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 7.2
.

For every density submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
such that
\varphi(\omega)=\infty
there exist a finitely additive set function
\rho\colon\exh(\varphi)\to[0,\infty)
and a sequence
\big\langle A_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of
\omega
such that
\rho(A_{k})>k
for every
k\in\omega
.

Proof.

Let
\big\langle\mu_{n}\colon\ n\in\omega\big\rangle
be a sequence of disjointly supported finitely supported non-negative measures on
\omega
, with supports contained in
\omega
, and such that
\varphi=\sup_{n\in\omega}\mu_{n}
. Since
\varphi(\omega)=\infty
, there exists an increasing sequence
\big\langle n_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
such that

\big\|\mu_{n_{k}}\big\|=\mu_{n_{k}}(\omega)>k\cdot 2^{k}

for every
k\in\omega
. We define a finitely additive set function
\rho\colon\exh(\varphi)\to[0,\infty)
by setting

\rho(A)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\cdot\mu_{n_{k}}(A)

for each
A\in\exh(\varphi)
; note that the series in this definition converges because every
A\in\exh(\varphi)
satisfies
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{n_{k}}(A)=0
. For every
k\in\omega
, setting
A_{k}=\supp\big(\mu_{n_{k}}\big)
, we have
\rho\big(A_{k}\big)>k
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a density submeasure on
\omega
such that
\varphi(\omega)=\infty
, and let
\rho
and
\big\langle A_{k}\colon\ k\in\omega\big\rangle
be as in Lemma
7.2
. We define a function
f\colon\omega\to\mathbb{R}
by setting
f(n)=\rho(\{n\})
for each
n\in\omega
. Since
\sum_{n\in A_{k}}f(n)>k
for every
k\in\omega
, we have
\sum_{n\in\omega}f(n)=\infty
. Moreover, since
\rho
is finitely additive and non-negative, we have

\sum_{n\in A}f(n)\leq\rho(A)<\infty

for every
A\in\exh(\varphi)
. Therefore, the ideal

\mathcal{I}_{f}=\Big\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\sum_{n\in A}f(n)<\infty\Big\}

is a summable ideal and we have
\exh(\varphi)\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{f}
.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

Theorem
7.1
together with Corollary
6.12
give yet another reason that no totally bounded analytic P-ideal on
\omega
can be in the class
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
(see
[
88
, Corollary 4.6]
), as every summable ideal is
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}
and no totally bounded ideal is contained in an
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}
ideal.

Theorem
7.1
yields two important corollaries. The first one strengthens Theorem
5.17
.(2), which asserts that for each ideal
\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
we have
\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\mathcal{Z}
, and extends the remark after Theorem
5.17
, stating that for a density ideal
\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
we have
\mathcal{I}<_{K}\mathcal{Z}
. Recall that
\tr(\mathcal{N})
is a totally bounded non-pathological ideal on
\omega
(by
[
48
, Proposition 3.18]
) as well as we have
\tr(\mathcal{N})<_{K}\mathcal{Z}
(by
[
88
, page 728]
).

Corollary 7.4
.

For every
\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
we have
\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\tr(\mathcal{N})
and so
\mathcal{I}<_{K}\mathcal{Z}
.

Proof.

By
[
48
, Proposition 3.5]
, for every summable ideal
\mathcal{J}
we have
\mathcal{J}\leq_{K}\tr(\mathcal{N})
. The conclusion now follows from Theorem
7.1
and the above discussion.
∎

The second corollary is a positive answer to the second author’s
[
88
, Question 9.4]
. For this, we first need to recall definitions regarding the Tukey order.

Let
(P,\leq_{P})
and
(Q,\leq_{Q})
be partial orders. A function
f\colon P\to Q
is said to be a
Tukey reduction
if for every
q_{0}\in Q
there exists
p_{0}\in P
such that, for every
p\in P
, the following implication holds:

f(p)\leq_{Q}q_{0}\>\Longrightarrow\>p\leq_{P}p_{0},

i.e. if the preimages by
f
of bounded subsets of
Q
are bounded subsets of
P
. We write
P\preceq_{T}Q
if there exists a Tukey reduction from
P
to
Q
. We say that
P
and
Q
are
Tukey equivalent
, which we denote by
P\equiv_{T}Q
, if
P\preceq_{T}Q
and
Q\preceq_{T}P
. Recently, several authors considered the Galois–Tukey order
\preceq_{GT}
(see e.g.
[
47
, page 726]
), which is stronger than
\preceq_{T}
in the sense that
P\preceq_{GT}Q
implies
P\preceq_{T}Q
. Again, we denote by
P\equiv_{GT}Q
, if
P\preceq_{GT}Q
and
Q\preceq_{GT}P
. We refer the reader to
[
49
, Section 6]
for basic information and further references concerning the Tukey ordering of ideals.

Corollary 7.5
.

(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N},\leq_{K})\equiv_{T}(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma
denotes the class of all summable ideals on
\omega
. By Theorem
7.1
, the order
(\Sigma,\leq_{K})
is a cofinal subset of the order
(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N},\leq_{K})
, therefore we have
(\Sigma,\leq_{K})\equiv_{T}(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N},\leq_{K})
. The result now follows from He–Li–Zhang’s
[
47
, Theorem 5.6]
, asserting that
(\Sigma,\leq_{K})\equiv_{GT}(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
.
∎

8.
Open questions

In the final section of the paper we state several problems. The first two concern Nikodym concentration points, studied in Section
3
.

In Example
3.8
, Proposition
5.24
, and Corollary
5.25
, using the class
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
of ideals on
\omega
, we described a large class of countable spaces
X
such that if
x
is a (unique) non-isolated point of
X
and
X
homeomorphically embeds into the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
of a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
, then the image of
x
in
St(\mathcal{A})
is a strong Nikodym concentration point of some anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
. This description can be naturally translated into the language of filters of neighborhoods of points in Stone spaces in the following way: given a point
y
in the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
of a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
, if there is a countable discrete set
Y\subseteq St(\mathcal{A})
such that
y\in\overline{Y}\setminus Y
and the filter
\big\{Y\cap\left[A\right]_{\mathcal{A}}\colon\ A\in y\big\}
on
Y
is isomorphic to some filter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{F}^{*}\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
, then
y
is a strong Nikodym concentration point of some anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
. We are interested in other properties of points in Stone spaces implying that they are strong Nikodym concentration points and thus we ask the following general question.

Question 8.1
.

What properties of points in the Stone spaces
St(\mathcal{A})
of Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}
imply that they are strong Nikodym concentration points of some anti-Nikodym sequences on
\mathcal{A}
?

Note that the above issue seems to be closely connected to yet another general problem asking what topological properties of points in compact spaces imply that they (do not) belong to the supports of non-atomic measures, see
[
14
]
.

In Example
3.12
we mentioned several consistent constructions of Boolean algebras without the Nikodym property which is witnessed only by anti-Nikodym sequence with no strong Nikodym concentration points. Since we are not aware of any
ZFC
example of such an algebra, we pose the following question.

Question 8.2
.

Does there exist in
ZFC
a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
without the Nikodym property and such that every anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
has no strong Nikodym concentration points in
St(\mathcal{A})
?

There exist
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}
P-filters
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
for which the Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
have the Nikodym property, for example one can take filters constructed in
[
38
, Theorem 4.12]
or in
[
63
, Example 6.15]
, and appeal to Theorem
5.10
. Similarly, the filter
\mathcal{Z}^{*}
, dual to the asymptotic density ideal
\mathcal{Z}
, is an
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta}
P-filter also such that the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}}
has the Nikodym property. We do not know however of any constructions of Borel filters
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
which do not belong to the Borel class
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta}
and for which the Boolean algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
have the Nikodym property.

Question 8.3
.

Does there exist a non-
\mathbb{F}_{\sigma\delta}
Borel filter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
for which the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
has the Nikodym property?

In Section
6.3
we studied the class of hypergraph ideals. In particular, in Theorem
6.30
we showed that there exists
\mathfrak{c}
many pairwise non-isomorphic hypergraph ideals which have the Nikodym property but are not totally bounded. We then ask if the non-isomorphicity may be strengthened to the Katětov incomparability.

Question 8.4
.

Does there exist a family of
\mathfrak{c}
many pairwise Katětov incomparable hypergraph ideals which have the Nikodym property and are not totally bounded?

A weaker variant of Question
8.4
may read as follows.

Question 8.5
.

Does there exist a family of
\mathfrak{c}
many pairwise Katětov incomparable non-pathological ideals which have the Nikodym property and are not totally bounded?

We finish the paper with the following question connected to Corollary
7.5
. Let
\mathbb{AN}
denote the class of all ideals
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
such that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
does not have the Nikodym property (so, e.g.,
\mathcal{AN}\subseteq\mathbb{AN}
).

Question 8.6
.

What are the Tukey classes of the ordered sets
(\mathbb{AN},\leq_{K})
and
(\mathbb{AN}\setminus\mathcal{AN},\leq_{K})
?

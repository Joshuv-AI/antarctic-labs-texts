Abstract
. In this article, we introduce a new methodology to prove global parabolic Harnack inequalities on Riemannian manifolds. We focus on presenting a new proof of the global pointwise Harnack inequality satisfied by positive solutions of the linear Schrödinger equation on a Riemannian manifold M M with nonnegative Ricci curvature, where the potential term V V is bounded from below. Our approach is based on a multi-point maximum principle argument. Standard proofs of this result (see, for instance, Li-Yau [Acta Math, 1986]) rely on first establishing a gradient estimate. This requires the solution to be at least C 4 C^{4} on M M . We instead prove the Harnack inequality directly, which has the advantage of avoiding higher-order derivatives of the solution in the proof, enabling us to assume it is only C 2 C^{2} on M M . In the particular case that V V is the quadratic potential V ⁡ ( x ) = | x | 2 V(x)=|x|^{2} and M M is the Euclidean space ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , we prove a new Harnack inequality with sharper constants. Finally, we treat positive solutions of the Schrödinger equation with a gradient drift term, including applications to the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator Δ − x ⋅ ∇ \Delta-x\cdot\nabla with quadratic potential in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} .



1.
Introduction and main results

An important milestone in the study of the heat equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-Vu\qquad\text{in $M\times(0,\infty)$}

governed by the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V
on a Riemannian manifold
M
was the result due to Li and Yau
[
32
]
, who demonstrated that every positive solution
u
of (
1.1
) on a complete Riemannian manifold
M
with nonnegative Ricci curvature satisfies the global Harnack inequality

(1.2)

u(x,t)\geq u(y,s)\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{d/2}e^{-C_{2}(\theta^{2/3}+C_{1}^{1/2})(t-s)-\omega(x,y;t,s)}

for all
x,y\in M
and
0<s<t
. Here,
V=V(x,t)\in C^{2,1}(M\times(0,\infty))
, the constant
C_{1}
is such that
\Delta V\leq C_{1}
on
M\times(0,\infty)
,
C_{2}
is a constant depending on the dimension
d
of the manifold
M
,
\theta
describes the growth of
\nabla V
, and

\omega(x,y;t,s):=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y}}\frac{1}{4(t-s)}\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\tau+(t-s)\int_{0}^{1}V(\gamma(\tau),(1-\tau)s+\tau t)\ \mathrm{d}\tau,

where
\Gamma_{x,y}
denotes the set of curves
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
with
\gamma(0)=y
and
\gamma(1)=x
. The Harnack inequality (
1.2
) has several important consequences, for example, it implies sharp bounds on the heat kernel (see
[
32
]
), Hölder continuity of solutions of (
1.1
), and eigenvalue estimates (see
[
9
]
).

In this paper, we are mainly interested in providing a new methodology of the proof of this result. The main idea of Li and Yau is to take
v=\log u
and apply the Laplace operator to the differential equation

\partial_{t}v=\Delta v+|\nabla v|^{2}

solved by
v
. By applying the maximum principle, a local gradient estimate is first obtained on a ball of radius
R
. Taking
R\to\infty
then produces the gradient estimate

(1.3)

-\Delta(\log u)=\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u}-V\leq\frac{d}{2t}+C_{2}\theta^{2/3}+\left(\frac{d}{2}C_{1}\right)^{1/2}

holding globally on
M\times(0,\infty)
. The inequality (
1.3
), often referred to as the
Li-Yau estimate
, describes as the
differential form of the Harnack inequality
, meaning that performing space-time integration on (
1.3
) yields the Harnack inequality (
1.2
). We note that this procedure involves the fourth-order derivatives of the solution
u
.

The results obtained in
[
32
]
have been improved by various authors using similar techniques, especially for the heat equation (
V\equiv 0
) (see, for instance,
[
3
,
4
,
9
,
19
,
31
]
), but also in the general potential case
[
30
,
40
,
46
]
. The approach by Li and Yau has since been used extensively to prove Harnack inequalities in several other contexts, including but not limited to operators of porous medium
[
1
,
8
,
21
,
33
,
34
,
46
]
,
p
-Laplace
[
2
,
13
,
27
]
or doubly nonlinear type
[
12
,
45
]
and involving lower-order terms
[
35
,
44
,
47
]
, as well as geometric flows
[
6
,
7
,
18
,
20
]
.

We acknowledge that besides the argument by Li and Yau
[
32
]
, other methods have been established in the literature to prove parabolic Harnack inequalities. One main standard approach is Moser iteration, which was originally applied to derive Harnack inequalities for positive solutions of parabolic problems involving divergence-form operators on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see
[
36
]
). This technique has since been adapted under suitable conditions to the settings of manifolds
[
16
,
41
]
and metric measure spaces
[
26
]
, and more recently to nonlocal equations (see, for instance,
[
11
,
15
,
23
,
24
,
43
]
). While Moser iteration remains an important tool to prove Harnack inequalities in various contexts, one limitation is that it typically does not produce sharp estimates. Other techniques available to prove parabolic Harnack inequalities include, but are not limited to, the original heat-potential based approach of Hadamard
[
17
]
and Pini
[
38
]
, the probabilistic approach employed by Krylov and Safonov
[
28
]
for non-divergence form operators, and the method of intrinsic scaling developed by DiBenedetto
[

]
to treat degenerate and singular equations. For a more thorough introduction to the topic, one may refer to the survey paper of Kassmann
[

]
.

In this article, we aim to introduce a novel method to prove parabolic Harnack inequalities, which sharpens the constants in (
1.2
). Our approach is based on a multi-point maximum principle argument and allows us to directly prove Harnack inequalities. The differential form can be recovered afterwards using space-time differentiation (see Section
5
). As a consequence, we only require the solution
u
to be
C^{2}
on
M
. This is advantageous, because in order to proceed via the approach by Li and Yau
[
32
]
, one requires
u
to be at least
C^{4}
on
M
. While this is not an obstacle in the linear case, since solutions of (
1.1
) are generally smooth, this can lead to complications when studying solutions of nonlinear equations.

We first consider positive solutions of the Neumann problem associated to the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) on compact manifolds, possibly with boundary, and later obtain a result on complete manifolds
M
by approximating
M
by an increasing sequence of compact domains. While we restrict the scope of this article to linear parabolic equations, we note that our methods can also be applied to derive Harnack inequalities associated to doubly nonlinear equations, such as the porous medium equation and heat equation corresponding to the
p
-Laplace operator. The details will appear in the doctoral thesis of the third author. In addition, the methodology can also be used to derive local Harnack inequalities for the aforementioned equations.

Our main theorems concern sufficient conditions for a global Harnack inequality to be satisfied by positive solutions
u
of

(
1.1
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\Delta u-Vu

\displaystyle\text{in $M\times(0,\infty)$},

where the manifold
M
is either assumed to be complete or compact with or without boundary. In particular, if
V
is bounded from below, the problem (
1.1
) admits a positive solution
u
for every positive initial datum
u_{0}
, for instance,
u_{0}\in L^{2}(M)
(see, for example,
[
25
]
).

A key role is played in the proof by the energy functional
E
defined by

(1.4)

E[\gamma;t,s]:=\frac{1}{4(t-s)}\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\tau+(t-s)\int_{0}^{1}V(\gamma(\tau))\ \mathrm{d}\tau

for every
C^{1}
curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
. We are particularly interested in the minimum value of
E[\gamma;t,s]
over the set

\Gamma_{x,y}:=\{\gamma\in C^{1}([0,1],M)\mid\gamma(0)=y,\gamma(1)=x\}

for every
x,y\in M
and
(t,s)
in the set

S:=\{(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid 0<s<t\}.

We later denote this minimum value by
\omega(x,y;t,s)
. We note that the energy defined in (
1.4
) has already been studied extensively, especially by physicists (see, for example,
[
5
]
), who refer to the function
\omega
as the
Agmon metric
. In particular, it is well-known that energies of the type (
1.4
) do indeed attain a minimum over
\Gamma_{x,y}
(see
[
29
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

A curve
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma_{x,y}
is called a
V
-geodesic
if there exist
0<s<t
such that

E[\gamma_{0};t,s]\leq E[\gamma;t,s].

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y}
. In addition, we say that a compact manifold
M
is
V
-convex
or has a
V
-convex boundary
if

(1.5)

h(\xi,\xi)+g(\nabla V(x),\nu(x))>0

holds for all
x\in\partial M
and
\xi\in T_{x}\partial M\setminus\{0\}
, where
h
denotes the second fundamental form on
\partial M
and
\nu(x)
is the outward pointing unit normal vector to the boundary at
x\in\partial M
.

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

The condition (
1.5
) implies the nonnegativity of the second fundamental form and therefore every
V
-convex domain is necessarily convex. In addition, (
1.5
) implies that
\nabla V\cdot\nu\geq 0
on
\partial M
, which is a condition previously assumed by Yau
[
47
]
.

(2)

By applying a maximum principle argument to analyse the distance of a
V
-geodesic from the boundary
\partial M
, one can show that (
1.5
) is sufficient to ensure that all
V
-geodesics connecting two points
x,y\in M
are contained in
M
, and that the endpoints of a
V
-geodesic cannot intersect the boundary tangentially.

Definition 1.2
.

A manifold
M
is called
V
-approximable
if there exists an increasing sequence
(M_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of
V
-convex regions
M_{n}\subset M
such that
\bigcup_{n\geq 1}M_{n}=M
.

For example, if
V
is geodesically convex and has a minimiser at
x\in M
, then
M
is
V
-approximable via geodesic balls centred at
x
and of increasing radius.

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold without boundary and nonnegative Ricci curvature. Let
V\in C^{2}(M)
be bounded from below and such that
M
is
V
-approximable. Suppose there exist strictly increasing, differentiable functions
\beta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfying
\beta(0)=0
and
A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that

(1.6)

\begin{split}(t-s)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{2}A^{\prime}(\tau t+(1-\tau)s)^{2}&+A(\tau t+(1-\tau)s)^{2}\Delta V(\gamma_{0})\ \mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\leq A(t)^{2}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(t)}{\beta(t)}-A(s)^{2}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s)}{\beta(s)}\end{split}

holds for all
V
-geodesics
\gamma_{0}
and
0<s<t
.
Then every positive solution
u
of (
1.1
) satisfies

(1.7)

u(x,t)\geq u(y,s)\frac{\beta(s)}{\beta(t)}e^{-\omega(x,y;t,s)}.

for all
x,y\in M
and
0<s<t
, where
\omega:M\times M\times S
is given by

(1.8)

\omega(x,y;t,s):=\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y}}\frac{1}{4(t-s)}\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\tau+(t-s)\int_{0}^{1}V(\gamma(\tau))\ \mathrm{d}\tau.

We note that the function
\omega
defined in (
1.8
) coincides with the function studied by Li and Yau in
[
32
]
and we revisit it further below.

In order to prove this theorem, we approximate the positive solution
u
of (
1.1
) by a sequence of positive solutions
u_{n}
of the Neumann problem (
1.9
) defined below on an increasing sequence of compact
V
-convex submanifolds. In particular, we require the following result regarding positive solutions
u
of the Neumann problem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature and (possibly empty) boundary
\partial M
. Let
V\in C^{2}(M)
be bounded from below and such that
\partial M
is
V
-convex. In addition, suppose that (
1.6
) holds for all
V
-geodesics
\gamma_{0}
. Then every positive solution
u
of

(1.9)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u&=\Delta u-Vu&&\qquad\text{in $M\times(0,\infty)$,}\\
\nabla u\cdot\nu&=0&&\qquad\text{on $\partial M\times(0,\infty)$}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

satisfies the Harnack inequality (
1.7
) for all
x,y\in M
and
0<s<t
.

Remark 1.2
.

Without loss of generality, we may always assume that the potential
V
in (
1.1
) is nonnegative. To see this, note that if
V
is merely bounded from below by
-\alpha
for some
\alpha>0
, applying the transformation
u\mapsto ue^{-\alpha t}
to the equation (
1.1
) yields

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-(V_{\alpha})u

with
V_{\alpha}:=V+\alpha\geq 0
. After obtaining a result for this new, nonnegative potential
V+\alpha
, we can reverse the transformation to give a Harnack inequality for solutions of the original equation.

After making a change of variables, Theorem
1.1
immediately implies the following result for positive solutions of the equation (
1.1
) perturbed by a gradient drift term.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold without boundary and nonnegative Ricci curvature. Let
f\in C^{4}(M)
,
V\in C^{2}(M)
, and let
u
be a positive solution of

(1.10)

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-2\nabla f\cdot\nabla u-Vu\qquad\text{in $M\times(0,\infty)$}.

If the conditions of Theorem
1.1
hold for the potential
\tilde{V}:=|\nabla f|^{2}-\Delta f+V
, then
u
satisfies

(1.11)

u(x,t)\geq u(y,s)\frac{\beta(s)}{\beta(t)}e^{f(x)-f(y)-\tilde{\omega}(x,y;t,s)}

for all
x,y\in M
and
0<s<t
, where

\tilde{\omega}(x,y;t,s):=\min_{\Gamma_{x,y}}\frac{1}{4(t-s)}\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\tau+(t-s)\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{V}(\gamma(\tau))\ \mathrm{d}\tau.

By applying Theorem
1.2
instead of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain a similar result on compact manifolds.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature and (possibly empty) boundary
\partial M
. Let
f\in C^{4}(M)
,
V\in C^{2}(M)
, and let
u
be a positive solution of

(1.12)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u&=\Delta u-2\nabla f\cdot\nabla u-Vu&&\qquad\text{in $M\times(0,\infty)$,}\\
\nabla u\cdot\nu&=(\nabla f\cdot\nu)u&&\qquad\text{on $\partial M\times(0,\infty)$.}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

If the conditions of Theorem
1.2
hold for the potential
\tilde{V}:=|\nabla f|^{2}-\Delta f+V
, then
u
satisfies (
1.11
)
for all
x,y\in M
and
0<s<t
.

Let

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega+|\nabla_{x}\omega|^{2}

(1.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\omega-|\nabla_{y}\omega|^{2}

2.
The Euclidean case

2.1.
Proofs of Theorems
1.5
and
1.6

In order to introduce the idea of our method in the simplest possible setting, we begin by considering the case where
M=\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
u
be a positive solution of (
1.20
) and set
v=\log u
. Then
v
is a solution to

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v=\Delta v+|\nabla v|^{2}-V\quad&\text{in $\Omega\times(0,\infty)$,}\\
\nabla v\cdot\nu=0\quad&\text{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty)$}.\end{cases}

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and define a function
Z
on
\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\times S
by

Z(x,y;t,s)=v(x,t)-v(y,s)+\log\left(\frac{\beta(t)}{\beta(s)}\right)+\omega(x,y;t,s)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(t-s)^{2}+\varepsilon.

We make some initial observations about
Z
for
(t,s)
near the boundary of
S
. Firstly, as
s\to 0^{+}
, the term
\log\left(\frac{\beta(t)}{\beta(s)}\right)\to\infty
due to the assumption that
\beta(0)=0
. Therefore,
Z>0
for
s
near 0. Next, consider the behaviour of
Z
as
t\to s^{+}
. If
x\neq y
, then the assumption that
\omega\to\infty
as
t\to s^{+}
guarantees that
Z>0
for
t
near
s
. Otherwise, if
x=y
, then the difference
v(x,t)-v(x,s)\to 0
, so we can assume
v(x,t)-v(x,s)+\varepsilon>0
. By assumption, all other terms in the limit of
Z
are nonnegative, so once again,
Z>0
for
t
near
s
.

Now, let
\rho=t^{2}+s^{2}
. As
\rho\rightarrow 0^{+}
, that is, when
(t,s)
is in a small neighbourhood of
(0,0)
in
S
,
Z>0
by the same argument as for when
t\to s^{+}
. Then, for
\rho>0
,
Z
either remains strictly positive, or there exists
\rho_{0}:=t_{0}^{2}+s_{0}^{2}>0
and a point
P_{0}:=(x_{0},y_{0};t_{0},s_{0})\in\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\times S
such that
Z(P_{0})=0
. We show that the latter cannot occur.

Suppose, by contradiction, that such a point
P_{0}
exists. We assume that
t_{0}
and
s_{0}
are taken so that
\rho_{0}
is minimal, that is,
Z(x,y;t,s)>0
for all
x,y\in\overline{\Omega}
and for all
0<s<t
such that
t^{2}+s^{2}<\rho_{0}
. First, assume that
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega
. Then, it follows that

\partial_{t}Z(P_{0})\leq 0,\quad\partial_{s}Z(P_{0})\leq 0,\quad\nabla_{(x,y)}Z(P_{0})=0,\quad D^{2}_{(x,y)}Z(P_{0})\geq 0.

By direct calculation,

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}Z(x,y;t,s)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{x}v(x,t)+\nabla_{x}\omega(x,y;t,s),

\displaystyle\nabla_{y}Z(x,y;t,s)

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{y}v(y,s)+\nabla_{y}\omega(x,y;t,s),

and thus
\nabla_{(x,y)}Z(P_{0})=0
implies that

(2.1)

\nabla v(x_{0},t_{0})=-\nabla_{x}\omega(P_{0}),\quad\nabla v(y_{0},s_{0})=\nabla_{y}\omega(P_{0}).

Next, we calculate

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z(x,y;t,s)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}v(x,t)+\frac{\beta^{\prime}(t)}{\beta(t)}+\partial_{t}\omega(x,y;t,s)+\varepsilon(t-s),

\displaystyle\partial_{s}Z(x,y;t,s)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{s}v(y,s)-\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s)}{\beta(s)}+\partial_{s}\omega(x,y;t,s)-\varepsilon(t-s).

Using that
\partial_{t}v=\Delta v+|\nabla v|^{2}-V
in
\Omega
,
\partial_{t}Z(P_{0})\leq 0
, and
\partial_{s}Z(P_{0})\leq 0
we have that

\displaystyle\Delta v(x_{0},t_{0})+|\nabla v(x_{0},t_{0})|^{2}-V(x_{0})+\frac{\beta^{\prime}(t_{0})}{\beta(t_{0})}+\partial_{t}\omega(P_{0})+\varepsilon(t_{0}-s_{0})

\displaystyle\leq 0,

\displaystyle-\Delta v(y_{0},s_{0})-|\nabla v(y_{0},s_{0})|^{2}+V(y_{0})-\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s_{0})}{\beta(s_{0})}+\partial_{s}\omega(P_{0})-\varepsilon(t_{0}-s_{0})

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Then, using (
2.1
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta v(x_{0},t_{0})&\leq-|\nabla_{x}\omega(P_{0})|^{2}-\partial_{t}\omega(P_{0})+V(x_{0})-\frac{\beta^{\prime}(t_{0})}{\beta(t_{0})}-\varepsilon(t_{0}-s_{0}),\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\Delta v(y_{0},s_{0})&\leq|\nabla_{y}\omega(P_{0})|^{2}-\partial_{s}\omega(P_{0})-V(y_{0})+\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s_{0})}{\beta(s_{0})}+\varepsilon(t_{0}-s_{0}).\end{split}

Due to the assumed inequalities (
1.13
) and (
1.14
), it follows that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Delta v(x_{0},t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\beta^{\prime}(t_{0})}{\beta(t_{0})}-\varepsilon(t_{0}-s_{0}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle-\Delta v(y_{0},s_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s_{0})}{\beta(s_{0})}+\varepsilon(t_{0}-s_{0}).

Next, we make use of the inequality
D^{2}_{(x,y)}Z(P_{0})\geq 0
. Let
x(\tau)
and
y(\tau)
be two paths in
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle x^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle=A(t_{0})e_{i},\quad x(\tau_{0})=x_{0},

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle=A(s_{0})e_{i},\quad y(\tau_{0})=y_{0}

for some
\tau_{0}
, where
e_{i}
is the
i^{\text{th}}
standard basis vector. We have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{d\tau}Z(x(\tau),y(\tau);t,s)&=A(t_{0})D_{i}v(x,t)-A(s_{0})D_{i}v(y,s)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+A(t_{0})\omega_{x_{i}}(x,y;t,s)+A(s_{0})\omega_{y_{i}}(x,y;t,s)\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}Z(x(\tau),y(\tau);t,s)&=A(t_{0})^{2}D_{ii}v(x,t)-A(s_{0})^{2}D_{ii}v(y,s)+A(t_{0})^{2}\omega_{x_{i}x_{i}}(x,y;t,s)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+A(s_{0})^{2}\omega_{y_{i}y_{i}}(x,y;t,s)+2A(t_{0})A(s_{0})\omega_{x_{i}y_{i}}(x,y;t,s)\end{split}

Therefore, since
\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}Z(P_{0})\geq 0
, we have that

\begin{split}&A(t_{0})^{2}D_{ii}v(x_{0},t_{0})-A(s_{0})^{2}D_{ii}v(y_{0},s_{0})+A(t_{0})^{2}\omega_{x_{i}x_{i}}(P_{0})\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+A(s_{0})^{2}\omega_{y_{i}y_{i}}(P_{0})+2A(t_{0})A(s_{0})\omega_{x_{i}y_{i}}(P_{0})\geq 0,\end{split}

so that summing over
i
yields

\begin{split}A(t_{0})^{2}&\Delta v(x_{0},t_{0})-A(s_{0})^{2}\Delta v(y_{0},s_{0})\\
&+A(t_{0})^{2}\Delta_{x}\omega(P_{0})+A(s_{0})^{2}\Delta_{y}\omega(P_{0})+2A(t_{0})A(s_{0})\sum_{i=1}^{d}\omega_{x_{i}y_{i}}(P_{0})\geq 0.\end{split}

By using the inequality (
1.15
), as well as (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we obtain

0>-\varepsilon(A(t_{0})^{2}-A(s_{0})^{2})(t_{0}-s_{0})\geq 0,

which is a contradiction. Therefore, such a point
P_{0}
with
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega
cannot exist. Thus, either one or both of
x_{0}
,
y_{0}
must be on the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

Without loss of generality, assume
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. Then

(2.4)

\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}Z(x_{0}-\tau\nu(x_{0}),y_{0};t_{0},s_{0})\right|_{\tau=0}=-\nabla v(x_{0},t_{0})\cdot\nu(x_{0})-\nabla_{x}\omega(P_{0})\cdot\nu(x_{0})\leq 0,

where we have used that
\nabla v(x_{0},t_{0})\cdot\nu(x_{0})=0
by the Neumann condition on
v
as well as the assumption (
1.19
) on
\omega
. One of two cases must occur: either the inequality in (
2.4
) is strict, or we have equality.

Firstly, if

\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}Z(x_{0}-\tau\nu(x_{0}),y_{0};t_{0},s_{0})\right|_{\tau=0}<0,

since
Z
is at least continuously differentiable in the first argument, there is some
\tau^{*}>0
such that
\frac{d}{d\tau}Z(x_{0}-\tau\nu(x_{0}),y_{0};t_{0},s_{0})<0
for all
\tau\in[0,\tau^{*})
. Integrating over this interval, we have

Z(x_{0}-\tau^{*}\nu(x_{0}),y_{0};t_{0},s_{0})<Z(x_{0},y_{0};t_{0},s_{0}),

which contradicts
x_{0}
minimising
Z(x,y_{0};t_{0},s_{0})
.

The other possibility is that

(2.5)

\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}Z(x_{0}-\tau\nu(x_{0}),y_{0};t_{0},s_{0})\right|_{\tau=0}=0.

Considering
Z(x)=Z(x,y_{0};t_{0};s_{0})
as a function only of
x
, then (
2.5
) is equivalent to
\nabla_{x}Z(x_{0})\cdot\nu(x_{0})=0
. Since
Z(x)
has a minimum at
x=x_{0}
, then all derivatives of
Z(x)
at
x_{0}
in directions tangential to
\partial\Omega
at
x_{0}
must also be 0. Therefore, we conclude that
\nabla_{x}Z(x_{0})=\nabla_{x}Z(P_{0})=0
.

Now consider whether
y\in\partial\Omega
or
y\in\Omega
. If
y_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, we could repeat our previous arguments to obtain
\nabla_{y}Z(P_{0})=0
. If
y_{0}\in\Omega
, then the function
Z(y)=Z(x_{0},y;t_{0},s_{0})
attains a minimum at
y=y_{0}
and therefore
\nabla_{y}Z(P_{0})=0
. In either case, we have that
\nabla_{x}Z(P_{0})=0
and
\nabla_{y}Z(P_{0})=0
, so
\nabla_{(x,y)}Z(P_{0})=0
. Since
Z
has a minimum at
P_{0}
, it follows that
D^{2}_{(x,y)}Z(P_{0})\geq 0
. Combining these facts with
\partial_{t}Z(P_{0})\leq 0
and
\partial_{s}Z(P_{0})\leq 0
, we may repeat the argument from when
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega
to arrive at a contradiction.

Therefore,
Z>0
for all
x,y\in\overline{\Omega}
and all
0<s<t
. Finally, taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
shows that the Harnack inequality (
1.18
) holds.
∎

We now explain our result for the full-space problem.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Suppose that
(\Omega_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is an increasing sequence of smooth bounded domains
\Omega_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Omega_{n}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that (
1.16
) holds. Let
u
be a positive solution of (
1.1
) with initial value
u_{0}:=u(x,0)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and define
u_{0n}:=u_{0}|_{\Omega_{n}}
for all
n\geq 1
. Let
A_{n}
be the operator
-\Delta+V
with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions and let
u_{n}
be the positive solution to the problem

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u_{n}+A_{n}u&=0&&\qquad\text{in $\Omega\times(0,\infty)$,}\\
u_{n}(\cdot,0)&=u_{0n}&&\qquad\text{in $\Omega_{n}$.}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

By Theorem
1.6
,
u_{n}
satisfies the Harnack inequality

(2.6)

u_{n}(x,t)\geq u_{n}(y,s)\frac{\beta(s)}{\beta(t)}e^{-\omega(x,y;t,s)}

for all
x,y\in\overline{\Omega_{n}}
and
0<s<t
.
We note that
A_{n}
can be realised in
L^{2}(\Omega_{n})
by a continuous, elliptic form, and is therefore a quasi-
m
-accretive operator on
L^{2}(\Omega_{n})
. The extension
\tilde{A}_{n}
of
A_{n}
by zero remains a quasi-
m
-accretive operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Similarly, the operator
A=-\Delta+V
is quasi-
m
-accretive on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, it is not difficult to see that with
\tilde{A_{n}}
converges to
A
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the resolvent sense. Therefore, using standard arguments from linear semigroup theory (see, for example, Kato
[
25
, Theorem 2.16 of Chapter 9]
), it follows that
\tilde{u}_{n}(t)\to u(t)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
uniformly for
t\in[0,T]
and for every
T\geq 0
. In addition, using interior Schauder estimates (see, for example, Friedman
[
14
]
), one obtains that
u_{n}(x,t)\to u(x,t)
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
. Therefore, taking

in the Harnack inequality (
2.6
) yields the claimed result.
∎

2.2.
An optimal
\omega
and comparison results

The function
\omega
in Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.6
could potentially be any function satisfying the conditions of these theorems, especially the inequalities (
1.13
)–(
1.16
). However, an optimal candidate is the function
\omega
defined by

(2.7)

\omega(x,y;t,s):=\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y}}E[\gamma;t,s]

for the energy

(
1.4
)

E[\gamma;t,s]:=\frac{1}{4(t-s)}\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\tau+(t-s)\int_{0}^{1}V(\gamma(\tau))\ \mathrm{d}\tau.

This function
\omega
is already well-known from the work of Li-Yau
[
32
]
. It occurs naturally while performing space-time integration to derive the Harnack inequality (
1.2
) from its differentiated form (
1.3
). The function
\omega
defined in (
2.7
) is also closely related to the fundamental solution
\Gamma
of the equation (
1.1
). In particular, it was proven first in the Euclidean case
M=\mathbb{R}^{d}
by Simon
[
42
]
and later on a complete manifold
M
by Li and Yau
[
32
]
, that
\omega
can be expressed via the limit

\omega(x,y;t,s)=-\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{\log\Gamma_{\lambda}(x-y,\frac{t-s}{\lambda})}{\lambda},

where
\Gamma_{\lambda}
denotes the fundamental solution of the equation

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-\lambda^{2}Vu\qquad\text{in $M\times(0,\infty)$}.

Importantly, it was also demonstrated in
[
32
]
that the function
\omega
defined in (
2.7
) satisfies both (
1.13
) and (
1.14
) with equality. In addition, as explained in the introduction, the inequalities (
1.16
) are satisfied on any
V
-convex domain. However, in general, it is not clear that an inequality of the form

(
1.15
)

A(t)^{2}\Delta_{x}\omega+A(s)^{2}\Delta_{y}\omega+2A(t)A(s)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\omega_{x_{i}y_{i}}\leq A(t)^{2}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(t)}{\beta(t)}-A(s)^{2}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s)}{\beta(s)}

should be satisfied by this particular function
\omega
. We now give some important examples, where
\omega
can be computed explicitly and appropriate functions
A
and
\beta
can be identified, which make inequality (
1.15
) true.

Example 2.1

(Heat equation)
.

In the case of the heat equation (
V\equiv 0
), the optimal choice of
\omega
is the function

\omega(x,y;t,s):=\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-s)},

which is the value attained by
E
along the straight line segment, i.e. geodesic, connecting
x
and
y
. In addition,
\omega
satisfies (
1.15
) with equality if
A(\tau)=\tau
and
\beta(\tau)=\tau^{d/2}
.
With this choice, we recover the classical parabolic Harnack inequality

(2.8)

u(x,t)\geq u(y,s)\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{d/2}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-s)}}.

We remark that (
2.8
) is considered sharp, in the sense that it is satisfied by the fundamental solution

\Gamma(x,t)=(4\pi t)^{-d/2}e^{\frac{-|x|^{2}}{4t}}

with equality on the set of points

\{(x,y;t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times S\mid sx=ty\}.

Example 2.2

(Quadratic potential)
.

Another noteworthy case to consider is that of the quadratic potential

V(x)=C_{1}^{2}|x-a|^{2}+C_{2}

for
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and constants
C_{1},C_{2}\neq 0
.
In this case, one can compute explicitly that the energy
E[\gamma;t,s]
given by (
1.4
) is minimised by the curve defined by

\begin{split}\gamma_{0}(\tau)=a+\frac{1}{\sinh(2C_{1}(t-s))}\Big(\sinh&(2C_{1}\tau(t-s))(x-a)\\
&+\sinh(2C_{1}(1-\tau)(t-s))(y-a)\Big)\end{split}

for all
\tau\in[0,1]
. It then follows that

\begin{split}\omega(x,y;t,s)&=\frac{C_{1}}{2}\left(\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{\sinh(2C_{1}(t-s))}+(|x-a|^{2}+|y-a|^{2})\tanh(C_{1}(t-s))\right)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+C_{2}(t-s).\end{split}

One can verify that this choice of
\omega
satisfies (
1.15
) with equality if we set

A(t)=\sinh(2C_{1}t),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta(t)=\sinh^{d/2}(2C_{1}t).

Then, Theorem
1.5
applied with
\Omega_{n}:=B_{n}(a)
yields that every positive solution
u

of

(2.9)

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-(C_{1}^{2}|x-a|^{2}+C_{2})u\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)$}

satisfies the Harnack inequality

(2.10)

u(x,t)\geq u(y,s)\left(\frac{\sinh(2C_{1}s)}{\sinh(2C_{1}t)}\right)^{d/2}e^{-\omega(x,y;t,s)}

for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<s<t
.

We note that inequality (
2.10
) is sharp in a similar sense to how the classical result for the heat equation without potential is sharp. For
a=0
,
C_{2}=0
, the fundamental solution of (
2.9
) is known from
[
39
]
to have the explicit formula

\Gamma(x,t)=\left(\frac{2\pi}{C_{1}}\sinh(2C_{1}t)\right)^{-d/2}e^{\frac{-C_{1}|x|^{2}}{2\tanh(2C_{1}t)}}.

One can verify that this solution satisfies (
2.10
) with equality on the set of points

\{(x,y;t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times S\mid\sinh(2Cs)x=\sinh(2Ct)y\}.

This is an improvement of the inequality (
1.2
) obtained by Li and Yau
[
32
]
.

While it is difficult to determine whether for a given potential, the function
\omega
defined in (
2.7
) satisfies (
1.15
), the following comparison theorem can aid in finding suitable functions
A
and
\beta
, which make (
1.15
) hold. We mention that throughout the remainder of this section, it is more convenient to work with the reparametrised energy

(2.11)

E[\gamma;t,s]:=\frac{1}{4}\int_{s}^{t}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{s}^{t}V(\gamma(\tau))\ \mathrm{d}\tau

defined for curves
\gamma:[s,t]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
0<s<t
. Importantly, the minimum value denoted by
\omega
does not depend on which definition we use for
E
.

Theorem 2.1

(Comparison theorem)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and suppose that
V_{1},V_{2}\in C^{2}(\Omega)
are two potential functions bounded from below, which satisfy
\Delta V_{1}\leq\Delta V_{2}
in
\Omega
. Assume that there exist strictly increasing, differentiable functions
\beta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfying
\beta(0)=0
and

such that

\int_{s}^{t}\frac{d}{2}A^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}+A(\tau)^{2}\Delta V_{2}(\gamma_{0}(\tau))\ \mathrm{d}\tau\leq A(t)^{2}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(t)}{\beta(t)}-A(s)^{2}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s)}{\beta(s)}

for all
V_{2}
-geodesics
\gamma_{0}
and
0<s<t
. Then the function
\omega_{1}
defined by (
2.7
) for the potential
V=V_{1}
satisfies

A(t)^{2}\Delta_{x}\omega_{1}+A(s)^{2}\Delta_{y}\omega_{1}+2A(t)A(s)\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\omega_{1}}_{x_{i}y_{i}}\leq A(t)^{2}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(t)}{\beta(t)}-A(s)^{2}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s)}{\beta(s)}

in
\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\times S
. Moreover, every positive solution
u
to (
1.20
) with potential

on a domain

satisfies the Harnack inequality

u(x,t)\geq u(y,s)\frac{\beta(s)}{\beta(t)}e^{-\omega_{1}(x,y;t,s)}

for all

and
0<s<t
, where
\Omega_{1}
is assumed to be
V_{1}
-convex if
\Omega\neq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Omega_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
V_{1}
-approximable.

This theorem follows immediately from a short lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
V\in C^{2}(\Omega)
be bounded from below and
A:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
differentiable. Then
\omega
as defined by minimising the energy (
2.11
) satisfies

(2.13)

\begin{split}A(t)^{2}\Delta_{x}\omega+A&(s)^{2}\Delta_{y}\omega+2A(t)A(s)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}\omega}{\partial x_{i}\partial y_{i}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\int_{s}^{t}\frac{d}{2}A^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}+A(\tau)^{2}\Delta V(\gamma_{0}(\tau))\ \mathrm{d}\tau\end{split}

for all
V
-geodesics
\gamma_{0}
in
\Omega
and
0<s<t
.

Proof.

Fix
x,y\in\Omega
and denote by
\gamma_{0}
a path which minimises
E
. Consider a family of curves defined by

\gamma(\tau,r)=\gamma_{0}(\tau)+rA(\tau)e_{i}.

In particular,
\gamma(\tau,0)=\gamma_{0}(\tau)
and the variation of the endpoints of the curves is described by

\displaystyle x(r)

\displaystyle:=\gamma(t,r)=x+rA(t)e_{i},

\displaystyle y(r)

\displaystyle:=\gamma(s,r)=y+rA(s)e_{i}.

Now, for any
r
,

\omega(x(r),y(r);t,s)=\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x(r),y(r)}}E[\gamma;t,s]\leq E[\gamma(\tau,r);t,s],

where equality is attained for
r=0
. Therefore, it follows that as a function of
r
,
\omega(x(r),y(r);t,s)-E[\gamma(\tau,r);t,s]
is negative and attains a maximum value of
0
at
r=0
. Therefore,

\left.\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}\omega(x(r),y(r);t,s)\right\rvert_{r=0}\leq\left.\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}E[\gamma;t,s]\right\rvert_{r=0}.

Computing both sides of this inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}A(t)^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\omega}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+A&(s)^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\omega}{\partial y_{i}^{2}}+2A(t)A(s)\frac{\partial^{2}\omega}{\partial x_{i}\partial y_{i}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{2}A^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}+A(\tau)^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}V(\gamma_{0}(\tau))\ \mathrm{d}\tau.\end{split}

The claimed inequality (
2.13
) follows by summing over
i
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Applying Lemma
2.2
to
\omega_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle A(t)^{2}\Delta_{x}\omega_{1}+A

\displaystyle(s)^{2}\Delta_{y}\omega_{1}+2A(t)A(s)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}\omega_{1}}{\partial x_{i}\partial y_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}\frac{d}{2}A^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}+A(\tau)^{2}\Delta V_{1}(\gamma_{0}(\tau))\ \mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}\frac{d}{2}A^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}+A(\tau)^{2}\Delta V_{2}(\gamma_{0}(\tau))\ \mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq A(t)^{2}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(t)}{\beta(t)}-A(s)^{2}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s)}{\beta(s)}

Hence the function
\omega_{1}
corresponding to
V_{1}
satisfies (
1.15
) with the functions

and
\beta
borrowed from

. All other necessary assumptions from Theorem

(if

) and Theorem

) hold and the Harnack inequality (

∎

Applying Theorem

Corollary 2.3

Let

Then, every positive solution

4.

\frac{1}{4}|\vec{e}|^{2}+\nabla v(y,s)\cdot\vec{e}=|\nabla v(y,s)+\frac{1}{2}\vec{e}|^{2}-|\nabla v(y,s)|^{2}
and then choosing
\vec{e}=-2\nabla v(y,s)
, we have that

\partial_{s}v(y,s)+\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s)}{\beta(s)}-|\nabla v(y,s)|^{2}+V(y)\geq 0,

or equivalently if
u
solves (
1.1
),

\Delta(\log u)\geq-\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s)}{\beta(s)}

in
M\times(0,\infty)
. Alternatively, if
u=e^{-f}\hat{u}
, where
\hat{u}
solves (
1.10
), then

\Delta(\log\hat{u})\geq\Delta f-\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s)}{\beta(s)}

in
M\times(0,\infty)
.

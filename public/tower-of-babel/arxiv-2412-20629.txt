On the pointwise supremum of the set of copulas with a given curvilinear section
Yao Ouyang a, Yonghui Sun a, Hua-peng Zhang b

Abstract
Making use of the total variation of particular functions, we give an explicit formula for the pointwise supremum of the set of all copulas with a given curvilinear section. When the pointwise supremum is a copula is characterized. We also characterize the coincidence of the pointwise supremum and the greatest quasi-copula with the same curvilinear section. Keywords: Copula, Quasi-copula, Curvilinear section, Total variation



1
Introduction

In the realm of statistical modeling and data analysis, understanding and modeling the dependencies among variables is crucial for accurate predictions and informed decision-making. Traditional methods often rely on assumptions about the joint distribution of variables, which can be limiting and restrictive, especially when dealing with complex real-world data. Copula-based method
[
5
,
6
,
15
]
allows for the separate modeling of marginal distributions and the dependency structure among variables, providing a more nuanced and realistic representation of the underlying data-generating process. Thus it offers a flexible and versatile alternative to those traditional methods.

Copulas, which were first introduced by Sklar in
[
14
]
, find uses in various domains such as finance and insurance. In finance, copulas have been instrumental in modeling the joint behavior of asset returns, risk management, and portfolio optimization
[
1
]
. In insurance, they have been used to assess the risk of simultaneous occurrences of extreme events
[
4
]
.

Over the years, the investigation of (quasi-)copulas with a given diagonal section has attracted much attention
[
3
,
10
,
11
]
, since the diagonal section
\delta_{C}
provides some information about the tail dependence of a bivariate random vector and
\delta_{C}
itself is the distribution function of the random variable
\max\{X,Y\}
provided that the copula of
(X,Y)
is
C
. For a given diagonal section, the smallest copula always exists, while the greatest one may not. Recently, Kokol Bukovšek et al.
[
8
]
presented an explicit representation of the pointwise supremum of the set of all copulas with a given diagonal section.

The study of (quasi-)copulas with a given diagonal section has been extended to that with a given curvilinear section
[
2
]
.
For a given curvilinear section
\Gamma_{\phi}
, the set of all copulas (resp. quasi-copulas), denoted as
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
(resp.
\mathbb{Q}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
), is nonempty. The smallest element of
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
and that of
\mathbb{Q}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
exist and coincide. Although the greatest element of
\mathbb{Q}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
exists, denoted as
A_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
, the greatest element of
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
may not.
The present authors
[
12
]
characterized when
A_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
is a copula. However, the pointwise supremum of
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
is unclear compared with the diagonal case.

The central task of this paper is to explore an explicit formula for the pointwise supremum of
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
.
After recalling some necessary knowledge that will be used throughout the paper in Section
2
, we give an explicit formula for the pointwise supremum of
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
in Section
3
. When the pointwise supremum of
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
is a copula is characterized in Section
4
, while Section
5
is devoted to characterizing the coincidence of the pointwise supremum of
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
and
A_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic notions and results concerning (quasi-)copulas with a given curvilinear section.

Definition 2.1
.

[
9
]

A (bivariate)
copula
is a function
C:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
satisfying:

(C1) the boundary condition,
i.e.
,

C(x,0)=C(0,x)=0\ {\rm and}\ C(x,1)=C(1,x)=x\ (\forall\,x\in[0,1]);

(C2) the
2
-increasing property,
i.e.
,

V_{C}(R)\geq 0
for any rectangle
R=[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[y_{1},y_{2}]
,
where
V_{C}(R)=C(x_{2},y_{2})-C(x_{2},y_{1})-C(x_{1},y_{2})+C(x_{1},y_{1})
. We will write
V(\cdot)
instead of
V_{C}(\cdot)
if there is no confusion.

Definition 2.2
.

[
9
]

A (bivariate)
quasi-copula
is a function
Q:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
satisfying (C1) and the following conditions:

(Q1)
Q
is increasing in each variable;

(Q2)
Q
is
1
-Lipschitz,
i.e.
, for all
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in[0,1]
, it holds that

|Q(x_{1},y_{1})-Q(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|y_{1}-y_{2}|.

Note that (C1) and (C2) together imply both (Q1) and (Q2),
i.e.
, every copula is a quasi-copula, but not vice versa.
Three important copulas are
W,M
and
\Pi
given by

W(x,y)=\max\{x+y-1,0\},M(x,y)=\min\{x,y\}\ {\rm and}\ \Pi(x,y)=xy.

W
and
M
are known as the
Fréchet-Hoeffding bounds
for copulas and quasi-copulas since
W\leq Q\leq M

for any quasi-copula
Q
, while
\Pi
is known as the independence copula.

Definition 2.3
.

[
10
]

A
diagonal function
is a function
\delta:[0,1]\to[0,1]
with the properties:

(D1)
\delta(1)=1
and
\delta(t)\leq t
for all
t\in[0,1]
;

(D2)
0\leq\delta(t_{2})-\delta(t_{1})\leq 2(t_{2}-t_{1})
for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,1]
with
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
.

The diagonal section
\delta_{Q}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
of any quasi-copula
Q
defined by
\delta_{Q}(t)=Q(t,t)
is a diagonal function. Conversely, for any diagonal function
\delta
, there exist quasi-copulas with diagonal section
\delta
,
and the smallest quasi-copula
B_{\delta}
with diagonal section
\delta
is given by

B_{\delta}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y-\min\limits_{t\in[y,x]}\hat{\delta}(t)&\quad\text{if\ \ $y\leq x$}\\[5.69054pt]
x-\min\limits_{t\in[x,y]}\hat{\delta}(t)&\quad\text{if\ \ $y>x$,}\end{array}\right.

while the greatest quasi-copula
A_{\delta}
with diagonal section
\delta
is given by

A_{\delta}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\left\{y,x-\max\limits_{t\in[y,x]}\hat{\delta}(t)\right\}&\quad\text{if\ \ $y\leq x$}\\[5.69054pt]
\min\left\{x,y-\max\limits_{t\in[x,y]}\hat{\delta}(t)\right\}&\quad\text{if\ \ $y>x$,}\end{array}\right.

where
\hat{\delta}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is defined by
\hat{\delta}(t)=t-\delta(t)
.
Interestingly,
B_{\delta}
is also a copula (called the
Bertino copula
), but
A_{\delta}
may not be.
There have been characterization theorems for
A_{\delta}
to be a copula (see
[
10
,
11
]
).
Denote by
\mathbb{C}_{\delta}
the set of all copulas with given diagonal section
\delta
. The explicit formula of the pointwise supremum
\bar{C}_{\delta}
of
\mathbb{C}_{\delta}
has recently been given by Kokol Bukovšek et al.
[
8
]
:

\bar{C}_{\delta}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\left\{y,x-\dfrac{1}{2}\Big(\hat{\delta}(x)+\hat{\delta}(y)+\mathbb{V}_{y}^{x}(\hat{\delta})\Big)\right\}&\quad\text{if\ \ $y\leq x$}\\[5.69054pt]
\min\left\{x,y-\dfrac{1}{2}\Big(\hat{\delta}(x)+\hat{\delta}(y)+\mathbb{V}_{x}^{y}(\hat{\delta})\Big)\right\}&\quad\text{if\ \ $y>x$,}\end{array}\right.

where
\mathbb{V}_{x}^{y}(\hat{\delta})
is the total variation of
\hat{\delta}
on
[x,y]
.

A finite sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}\subseteq[a,b]
is called a
partition
of an interval
[a,b]
if it is increasing with
x_{0}=a
and
x_{n}=b
. Denote by
\mathcal{P}[a,b]
the set of all partitions of
[a,b]
. The
total variation

\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(f)
of a real-valued function
f
on
[a,b]
is defined by

\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(f)=\sup\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}|f(x_{k})-f(x_{k-1})|\mid\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}[a,b]\right\}.

If
\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(f)<+\infty
, then
f
is differentiable almost everywhere on
[a,b]
and
\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(f)\geq\int_{a}^{b}|f^{\prime}(t)|{\rm d}t
, where the equality holds if and only if
f
is absolutely continuous
[
13
]
.
We use the convention that
\mathbb{V}_{b}^{a}(f)=-\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(f)
and
\mathbb{V}_{a}^{a}(f)=0
. Clearly,
\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(f)=\mathbb{V}_{a}^{c}(f)+\mathbb{V}_{c}^{b}(f)
for any
c\in[a,b]
,
\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(f)=|f(b)-f(a)|
if
f
is monotone,
and
\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(f+g)\leq\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(f)+\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(g)
for any two real-valued functions
f,g
on
[a,b]
.

Remark 2.4
.

For any
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}[a,b]
, it is easy to prove by induction that there exists
\{y_{i}\}_{i=0}^{2m}\in\mathcal{P}[a,b]
such that the sets
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
and
\{y_{i}\}_{i=0}^{2m}
are equal, and
f(y_{2k-1})\geq\max\{f(y_{2k-2}),f(y_{2k})\}
for all
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}|f(x_{k})-f(x_{k-1})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2m}|f(y_{k})-f(y_{k-1})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}(f(y_{2k-1})-f(y_{2k-2}))+\sum_{k=1}^{m}(f(y_{2k-1})-f(y_{2k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{m}f(y_{2k-1})-2\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}f(y_{2k})-f(a)-f(b).

Therefore, it holds that

\mathbb{V}_{a}^{b}(f)=\sup\left\{2\sum_{k=1}^{m}f(x_{2k-1})-2\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}f(x_{2k})-f(a)-f(b)\mid\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{2m}\in\mathcal{P}[a,b]\right\}.

The study of copulas with a given diagonal section has been extended to that with a given curvilinear section
[
2
]
.
Let
\Phi
be the set of all functions
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
that are continuous and strictly increasing with
\phi(0)=0
and
\phi(1)=1
.
Obviously,
{\rm id}\in\Phi
, where
{\rm id}
is the identity map on
[0,1]
,
i.e.
,
{\rm id}(x)=x
for all
x\in[0,1]
.
For any
\phi\in\Phi
, the curvilinear section
g_{Q,\phi}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
of a quasi-copula
Q
defined by
g_{Q,\phi}(t)=Q(t,\phi(t))
has properties:

(i)

\max\{0,t+\phi(t)-1\}\leq g_{Q,\phi}(t)\leq\min\{t,\phi(t)\}
for all
t\in[0,1]
;

(ii)

0\leq g_{Q,\phi}(t_{2})-g_{Q,\phi}(t_{1})\leq t_{2}-t_{1}+\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1})
for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,1]
with
t_{1}<t_{2}
.

Note that property (ii) can be rewritten as:
g_{Q,\phi}(t)
is increasing and
g_{Q,\phi}(t)-t-\phi(t)
is decreasing.
For any
\phi\in\Phi
, denote by
\Delta_{\phi}
the set of all functions
\Gamma_{\phi}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
with properties (i) and (ii).
Particularly, if
\phi={\rm id}
, then properties (i) and (ii) are equivalent to (D1) and (D2), and thus
\Delta_{\rm id}
is nothing else but the set of all diagonal functions.

For any
\phi\in\Phi
and
\Gamma_{\phi}\in\Delta_{\phi}
, similarly to the diagonal case, there exist quasi-copulas with curvilinear section
\Gamma_{\phi}
,
and the smallest quasi-copula
B_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
with curvilinear section
\Gamma_{\phi}
is given by

B_{\Gamma_{\phi}}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y-\min\limits_{t\in[\phi^{-1}(y),x]}\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t)&\quad\text{if\ \ $y\leq\phi(x)$}\\[5.69054pt]
x-\min\limits_{t\in[x,\phi^{-1}(y)]}\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t)&\quad\text{if\ \ $y>\phi(x)$,}\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi},\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
are defined by
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t)=\phi(t)-\Gamma_{\phi}(t)
and
\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t)=t-\Gamma_{\phi}(t)
.
B_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
is also a copula, called the
Bertino copula with curvilinear section

\Gamma_{\phi}

[
2
]
.
The greatest quasi-copula
A_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
with curvilinear section
\Gamma_{\phi}
is given by

A_{\Gamma_{\phi}}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\left\{y,x-\max\limits_{t\in[\phi^{-1}(y),x]}\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t)\right\}&\text{if\ \ $y\leq\phi(x)$}\\[5.69054pt]
\min\left\{x,y-\max\limits_{t\in[x,\phi^{-1}(y)]}\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t)\right\}&\text{if\ \ $y>\phi(x)$.}\end{array}\right.

However,
A_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
may not be a copula. Quite recently, we
[
12
]
completely characterized when
A_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
is a copula.

3
Representation of the pointwise supremum of
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}

Denote by
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
the set of all copulas with given curvilinear section
\Gamma_{\phi}
.
In this section, inspired by the work
[
8
]
on the diagonal case, we first construct two copulas with curvilinear section
\Gamma_{\phi}
by total variation.
Then we represent the pointwise supremum of
\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
in terms of the two constructed copulas.

To begin with, for
\phi\in\Phi
and
\Gamma_{\phi}\in\Delta_{\phi}
, we define functions
f_{1},f_{2}\colon[0,1]^{2}\to(-\infty,+\infty)
as follows:

f_{1}(x,y)=x-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathbb{V}^{x}_{\phi^{-1}(y)}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})+\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x)+\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(\phi^{-1}(y))\Big)

and

f_{2}(x,y)=y-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathbb{V}^{\phi^{-1}(y)}_{x}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi})+\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x)+\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(\phi^{-1}(y))\Big).

Clearly,
f_{1}(0,0)=f_{2}(0,0)=0
and
f_{1}(1,1)=f_{2}(1,1)=1
. Moreover,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are increasing, implying that they are
binary operations on
[0,1]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are increasing.

Proof.

We only prove that
f_{1}
is increasing since the proof concerning
f_{2}
is similar.
For any
x_{1},x_{2},y\in[0,1]
with
x_{1}<x_{2}
, we have

f_{1}(x_{2},y)-f_{1}(x_{1},y)=x_{2}-x_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathbb{V}_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})+\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{1})\Big).

It follows from

\mathbb{V}_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})\leq\mathbb{V}_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}({\rm id})+\mathbb{V}_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}(\Gamma_{\phi})=(x_{2}-x_{1})+(\Gamma_{\phi}(x_{2})-\Gamma_{\phi}(x_{1}))

that
f_{1}(x_{2},y)-f_{1}(x_{1},y)\geq 0
,
i.e.
,
f_{1}
is increasing in the first variable.

For any
y_{1},y_{2},x\in[0,1]
with
y_{1}<y_{2}
, we have

f_{1}(x,y_{2})-f_{1}(x,y_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathbb{V}_{\phi^{-1}(y_{1})}^{\phi^{-1}(y_{2})}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(\phi^{-1}(y_{2}))+\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(\phi^{-1}(y_{1}))\Big)\geq 0,

i.e.
,
f_{1}
is also increasing in the second variable.

\Box

Before proving that both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are
1
-Lipschitz, we need two lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\phi\in\Phi
and
\Gamma_{\phi}\in\Delta_{\phi}
. Then the following inequalities hold for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,1]
with
t_{1}<t_{2}
:

(i)

|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|\leq 2(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))+(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1}))
;

(ii)

|\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|\leq 2(t_{2}-t_{1})+(\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1}))
;

(iii)

|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|+|\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|\leq(t_{2}-t_{1})+(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))
.

Proof.

(i) We distinguish two possible cases.

(a)

\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})\geq 0
.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))+(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})).

(b)

\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})<0
.

In this case, since
\Gamma_{\phi}(t_{2})-\Gamma_{\phi}(t_{1})\leq(t_{2}-t_{1})+(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))
, we have

(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))+(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1}))\geq 0
. Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))+(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})).

(ii) The proof is similar to that of (i).

(iii) If both
\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})\geq 0
and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|+|\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t_{2}-t_{1})+(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))-2(\Gamma_{\phi}(t_{2})-\Gamma_{\phi}(t_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(t_{2}-t_{1})+(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1})).

If both
\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})<0
and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})<0
, then it follows from
|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|\leq\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1})
and
|\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|\leq t_{2}-t_{1}
that the inequality holds.

If
\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})\geq 0
and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})<0
, then

\displaystyle|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|+|\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t_{2}-t_{1})-(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(t_{2}-t_{1})+(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1})).

Similarly, the inequality holds if
\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})<0
and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})\geq 0
.

\Box

The following estimations for the total variations of
\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}
and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}
are crucial to upcoming results.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\phi\in\Phi
and
\Gamma_{\phi}\in\Delta_{\phi}
. Then the following inequalities hold for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,1]
with
t_{1}<t_{2}
:

(i)

\mathbb{V}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})\leq 2(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))+(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1}))
;

(ii)

\mathbb{V}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi})\leq 2(t_{2}-t_{1})+(\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1}))
;

(iii)

\mathbb{V}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})+\mathbb{V}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi})\leq(t_{2}-t_{1})+(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))
.

Proof.

(i) For any
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}[t_{1},t_{2}]
, it follows from Lemma
3.2
(i) that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i-1})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(2(\phi(x_{i})-\phi(x_{i-1}))+(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i-1}))\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))+(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1})).

Hence,
\mathbb{V}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})\leq 2(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))+(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(t_{1}))
.

(ii) The proof is similar to that of (i).

(iii) For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}[t_{1},t_{2}]
such that

\mathbb{V}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})<\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i-1})|+\epsilon

and

\mathbb{V}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi})<\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i-1})|+\epsilon~.

It follows from Lemma
3.2
(iii) that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i-1})|+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i-1})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(|\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i-1})|+|\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i})-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{i-1})|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big((x_{i}-x_{i-1})+(\phi(x_{i})-\phi(x_{i-1}))\Big)=(t_{2}-t_{1})+(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1})).

Hence,
\mathbb{V}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})+\mathbb{V}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{\phi})<(t_{2}-t_{1})+(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))+2\epsilon
.
Therefore, the conclusion holds due to the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
.

\Box

Proposition 3.4
.

Both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are
1
-Lipschitz.

Proof.

For any
x_{1},x_{2},y\in[0,1]
with
x_{1}<x_{2}
, we have

f_{1}(x_{2},y)-f_{1}(x_{1},y)=(x_{2}-x_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathbb{V}_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})+\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{2})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x_{1})\Big)\leq x_{2}-x_{1}.

Since
f_{1}
is increasing, it follows that
f_{1}
is
1
-Lipschitz w.r.t. the first variable.

For any
y_{1},y_{2},x\in[0,1]
with
y_{1}<y_{2}
, it follows from Lemma
3.3
(i) that

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,y_{2})-f_{1}(x,y_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathbb{V}_{\phi^{-1}(y_{1})}^{\phi^{-1}(y_{2})}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})-\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(\phi^{-1}(y_{2}))+\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(\phi^{-1}(y_{1}))\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle y_{2}-y_{1}.

Since
f_{1}
is increasing,
f_{1}
is also
1
-Lipschitz w.r.t. the second variable.

Similarly, we can show that
f_{2}
is
1
-Lipschitz.

\Box

Based on
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, we define functions
C_{1},C_{2}\colon[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
as follows:

C_{1}(x,y)=\min\{x,y,f_{1}(x,y)\}

and

C_{2}(x,y)=\min\{x,y,f_{2}(x,y)\}.

Interestingly,

and

are copulas with curvilinear section
\Gamma_{\phi}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\phi\in\Phi
and
\Gamma_{\phi}\in\Delta_{\phi}
. Then
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
.

Proof.

We only prove that
C_{1}\in\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
since the proof of
C_{2}\in\mathbb{C}_{\Gamma_{\phi}}
is similar.
It is clear that
C_{1}(x,\phi(x))=\Gamma_{\phi}(x)
for all
x\in[0,1]
. It then remains to prove that
C_{1}
is a copula.

Boundary condition:
For any
x\in[0,1]
, we have

\mathbb{V}^{x}_{\phi^{-1}(1)}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})+\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x)+\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(\phi^{-1}(1))=\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(x)-\mathbb{V}^{1}_{x}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})\leq 0.

Hence,
f_{1}(x,1)\geq x
, and thus
C_{1}(x,1)=x
. It follows from Lemma
3.3
(i) that

\mathbb{V}^{1}_{\phi^{-1}(x)}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi})+\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(1)+\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(\phi^{-1}(x))\leq 2(\phi(1)-x)+2\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi}(1)=2(1-x).

Hence,
f_{1}(1,x)\geq x
, and thus
C_{1}(1,x)=x
. Since
f_{1}
is increasing,
C_{1}
is also increasing, which implies that

C_{1}(0,x)=C_{1}(x,0)=0
.

2
-increasingness:
Note that any rectangle

can be partitioned into at most three non-overlapping rectangles

and
V(R)
is the sum of all

,

belongs to one of the three types of rectangles:

Type 2:
R\subset\{(x,y)\mid y\geq\phi(x)\}
,
i.e.
,
R
is above the curve
\{(x,\phi(x))\mid x\in[0,1]\}

Type 3:

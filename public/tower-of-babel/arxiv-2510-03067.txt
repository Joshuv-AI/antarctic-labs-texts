Spin actions and Polygon spaces
Eunjeong Lee, Jae-Hyouk Lee

Abstract
In this article, we construct correspondences between polygon spaces in Euclidean spaces of dimension 2 , 3 , 5 , 9 2,3,5,9\ and the quotient spaces of 2 2 -Steifel manifolds along the normed division algebra 𝔽 \ \mathbb{F} real ℝ \mathbb{R} , complex ℂ \mathbb{C} , quaternions ℍ \mathbb{H} , octonions 𝕆 \mathbb{O} . For the purpose, we introduce Hopf map on 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}^{2}\ and consider the spin action of S ​ U ​ ( 2 , 𝔽 ) SU\left(2,\mathbb{F}\right) to spinor 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}^{2}\ and the induced S ​ O SO\ action to the Euclidean space ℝ ⊕ 𝔽 \mathbb{R\oplus F} . The correspondences are extension of the work of Hausmann and Knutson for polygon spaces of dimension 2 , 3 2,3\ and 2 2 -Grassmannians over real and complex.

2.1
Polygon spaces

Polygons in this article are closed figures in a finite-dimensional Euclidean
space
(V,\langle,\rangle)
, formed by connecting more than equal to three
vertices with directed straight line segments which are edges of the polygon.
In this article we consider polygons in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space

(V,\langle,\rangle)\
as a map whose values are vectors in
V\
presenting
directed line segments of the polygons. A
k
-gon
p
is a nonzero map

\begin{array}[c]{cccc}p:&\{1,\cdots,k\}&\rightarrow&V\\
&i&\mapsto&p(i)\end{array}

satisfying closed condition

\sum_{i=1}^{k}p(i)=0.

The definition allows polygons with self-intersection, knotted polygons. The
value of the map
p\
can be the zero vector in
V
but this may not occur
simultaneously, and the polygons are possibly degenerate as in
[
6
]
.

On the set
M_{k}\left(V\right)\
of all
k
-gons in
V
, there are
natural actions of the orthogonal group
O(V)\
(also
SO(V)
) and the
multiplicative group
(\mathbb{R}^{+},\times)\
defined respectively by

\displaystyle\left(g\cdot p\right)(i)

\displaystyle:=g\left(p(i)\right)\text{,\ for }g\in O(V),i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}

\displaystyle\left(c\cdot p\right)(i)

\displaystyle:=cp(i)\text{,\ for }\mathbb{R}^{+},i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}.

With a choose of an ordered basis of a
n
-dimensional vector space
V\
over

\mathbb{R}
, polygons
p\
can be presented as
n\times k\
matrices

\left[p\right]:=\begin{pmatrix}|&&|\\
p(1)&\cdots&p\left(k\right)\\
|&&|\end{pmatrix}

where
p\left(i\right)
’s are considered as column vectors presented by
the basis of
V
. Moreover the set of
k
-gons
M_{k}\left(V\right)\
is
regarded as the subset of
n\times k\
matrices
\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times k}(\mathbb{R})\
over real numbers.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{k}(V)
is the quotient space of the set of all
k
-gons in

M_{k}\left(V\right)
by the
(\mathbb{R}^{+},\times)\
action, and we
identify
\mathcal{M}_{k}(V)
as the space of closed polygons in

with

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|p(i)\right|=1

where
\left|\ \right|\
is the Euclidean norm of
V
.

The
polygon spaces
of
k
-gons in the Euclidean space

are defined as the quotient space of

by

\mathcal{P}_{k}(V):=O(V)\backslash\mathcal{M}_{k}(V),\;\mathcal{\tilde{P}}_{k}(V):=SO(V)\backslash\mathcal{M}_{k}(V)

2.2
Stiefel manifold

For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}
, the set

V_{\mathbb{F}}(k,n):=\{X\in\operatorname{Mat}_{k\times n}(\mathbb{F})\mid XX^{\ast}=I_{k}\}

of ordered orthonormal
k
-frames in
\mathbb{F}^{n}
is called the

Stiefel manifold
. Here,
X^{\ast}
denotes the conjugate transpose of

X
.We define conjugation on
\mathbb{F}
by reversing the sign of the
imaginary part while preserving the real part. For example, for

q=a+bi+cj+dk\in\mathbb{H}
,
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
, its conjugation

\overline{q}
is

\overline{q}:=a-bi-cj-dk.

Moreover, we use the standard inner product on
\mathbb{F}^{n}
given by

\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\overline{y}_{i}

for
x=\left(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}\right),y=\left(y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{n}\right)\
in
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. This inner product is
positive definite with signature
(n,0)\
and the condition
XX^{\ast}=I_{k}

ensures that the rows of matrices
X
in
V_{\mathbb{F}}(k,n)\
form an
orthonormal set of
k\
vectors of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
.

Remark 1

Since
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{H\ }
is noncommutative, it is important to fix

\mathbb{H}^{n}\
as left or right
\mathbb{H}
-module. From above definition
of norm and
V_{\mathbb{H}}(k,n)
, the natural choice is right
\mathbb{H}
-module. It is also matched with matrix multiplication from the left to
column vectors of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

The Stiefel manifold
V_{\mathbb{F}}(k,n)
is also a homogenous space. Since

V_{\mathbb{F}}(k,n)
is the set of all
k
-frames of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
, the
Lie groups of orthogonal matrices for each
\mathbb{F}

O(n),U(n),Sp(n)

act transitively on
V_{\mathbb{F}}(k,n)
for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}
, respectively. Then the isotropy subgroup of a given

k
-frame is

O(n-k),U(n-k),Sp(n-k).

Thus, the Stiefel manifolds can be expressed as the homogeneous spaces

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{R}}(k,n)

\displaystyle\simeq O(n)/O(n-k)

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{C}}(k,n)

\displaystyle\simeq U(n)/U(n-k)

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{H}}(k,n)

\displaystyle\simeq Sp(n)/Sp(n-k).

Now, the Stiefel manifolds
V_{\mathbb{F}}(k,n)\
are naturally corresponded
to the
Grassmannian manifold

Gr_{\mathbb{F}}(k,n)
, the set of all

k
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. This space also admits a
transitive action of

O(n),U(n),Sp(n),

but its isotropy subgroup is

O(n-k)\times O(k),\quad U(n-k)\times U(k),\quad Sp(n-k)\times Sp(k).

Hence, the Grassmannian can be described as the homogeneous space and can be
obtained as a quotient of the Stiefel manifold.

\displaystyle Gr_{\mathbb{R}}(k,n)

\displaystyle\simeq O(n)/\left(O(n-k)\times O(k)\right)\simeq V_{\mathbb{R}}(k,n)/O(k)

\displaystyle Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(k,n)

\displaystyle\simeq U(n)/\left(U(n-k)\times U(k)\right)\simeq V_{\mathbb{C}}(k,n)/U(k)

\displaystyle Gr_{\mathbb{H}}(k,n)

\displaystyle\simeq Sp(n)/\left(Sp(n-k)\times Sp(k)\right)\simeq V_{\mathbb{H}}(k,n)/Sp(k).

In addition, for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, a point in the
k
-Stiefel manifold

V_{\mathbb{R}}(k,n)\
represents an ordered orthonormal
k
-frame. By fixing
an orientation of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
V_{\mathbb{R}}(k,n)\ (k<n)\
can also be
described as

V_{\mathbb{R}}(k,n)\simeq SO(n)/SO(n-k)

which is the
SO(k)\
principal bundle over the
oriented Grassmannian

\widetilde{Gr}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,n)

\widetilde{Gr}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,n)\simeq SO(n)/\left(SO(n-k)\times SO(k)\right)\simeq V_{\mathbb{R}}(k,n)/SO(k)

Lemma 4

Let

Proof.
(1) By the condition of normed algebras and its linearization,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(a+b\right)x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|a\right\|^{2}\left\|x\right\|^{2}+2\left\langle ax,bx\right\rangle+\left\|b\right\|^{2}\left\|x\right\|^{2},

\displaystyle\left\|\left(a+b\right)x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|a+b\right\|^{2}\left\|x\right\|^{2}=\left(\left\|a\right\|^{2}+2\left\langle a,b\right\rangle+\left\|b\right\|^{2}\right)\left\|x\right\|^{2},

hence

\left\langle ax,bx\right\rangle=\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\left\|x\right\|^{2}.

In a similar way

\left\langle ya,yb\right\rangle=\left\|y\right\|^{2}\left\langle a,b\right\rangle.

(2) Replace
x

in (1).

(3) By applying (2)

\displaystyle\left\langle xy,ae\right\rangle+\left\langle yx,ae\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\left\langle x,a\right\rangle\left\langle y,e\right\rangle+2\left\langle y,a\right\rangle\left\langle x,e\right\rangle-\left\langle x,ay\right\rangle-\left\langle y,ax\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle 2\left\langle y,e\right\rangle x+2\left\langle x,e\right\rangle y,a\right\rangle-\left\{\left\langle ex,ay\right\rangle+\left\langle ey,ax\right\rangle\right\}

(4) Set

.
\blacksquare

The following identities play important roles for studying normed algebra.

Proposition 9

(Moufang identities) For any

of a normed algebra

the following identities hold

\left(ax\right)\left(ya\right)=a\left(xy\right)a,\ a\left(x\left(ay\right)\right)=\left(axa\right)y,\ x\left(aya\right)=\left(\left(xa\right)y\right)a

\left(ax\right)\left(ya\right)=a\left(\left(xy\right)a\right),\ a\left(x\left(ay\right)\right)=\left(a\left(xa\right)\right)y

For arbitrary

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(ax\right)\left(ya\right),z\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle ya,\overline{\left(ax\right)}z\right\rangle=2\left\langle y,\overline{ax}\right\rangle\left\langle a,z\right\rangle-\left\langle yz,\overline{\left(ax\right)}a\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\left\langle xy,\bar{a}\right\rangle\left\langle a,z\right\rangle-\left\|a\right\|^{2}\left\langle yz,\bar{x}\right\rangle

and

\displaystyle\left\langle a\left(\left(xy\right)a\right),z\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(xy\right)a,\bar{a}z\right\rangle=2\left\langle xy,\bar{a}\right\rangle\left\langle a,z\right\rangle-\left\langle\left(xy\right)z,\bar{a}a\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\left\langle xy,\bar{a}\right\rangle\left\langle a,z\right\rangle-\left\|a\right\|^{2}\left\langle xy,\bar{z}\right\rangle.

Lemma 13

|\Phi(v)|=\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}

Lemma 14

For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}

(1) The Hopf map

(2) For each

0\end{pmatrix}\right\},\;\text{for}\;(\lambda,\alpha)=(0,0)\end{cases}

\left|y\right|^{2}=-\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\left|\alpha\right|^{2}}\end{cases}

\alpha\neq 0

Therefore, when

(x,y)=\left(\sqrt{\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\left|\alpha\right|^{2}}}\frac{\alpha}{\left|\alpha\right|}\theta,\sqrt{-\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\left|\alpha\right|^{2}}}\theta\right),\theta\in\mathbb{F}(1)

Case 2:
\alpha=0

\begin{cases}x\overline{y}=0\\
\frac{1}{2}(\left|x\right|^{2}-\left|y\right|^{2})=\lambda\end{cases}

\left|x\right|^{2}+\left|y\right|^{2}=2\sqrt{\lambda^{2}}=2|\lambda|,

we obtain

\begin{cases}\left|x\right|=\lambda+|\lambda|\\
\left|y\right|=-\lambda+|\lambda|.\end{cases}

The solutions are given as for

(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{c}\left(2\lambda\theta,0\right)\ \text{if }\lambda>0\text{ }\\
\left(0,0\right)\ \ \text{if }\lambda=0\\

\blacksquare

\mathbb{F}

\mathbb{C}

\mathbb{H}

It is well known that these Lie groups are isomorphic to the corresponding

group action of

Ad_{A}(X)=AXA^{\ast}

where

and

still holds for

(Ad_{A}(X))^{\ast}=(AXA^{\ast})^{\ast}=AX^{\ast}A^{\ast}=AXA^{\ast}=Ad_{A}(X)

Lemma 18

There is a group homomorphism
h:M_{n}(\mathbb{H})\rightarrow M_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying

h(A^{\ast})=h(A)^{\ast},\quad\text{and}\quad tr\left(h(A)\right)=2\operatorname{Re}tr(A).

Proof.
We consider quaternionic matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{H})\
as

A=A_{1}+A_{2}j

for
A_{1}
,
\ A_{2}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with respect to the identification

q=z_{1}+z_{2}j\in\mathbb{H\ }
for
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. And we define
the group homomorphism

\begin{array}[c]{cccc}h:&M_{n}(\mathbb{H})&\rightarrow&M_{2n}(\mathbb{C})\\
&A&\longmapsto&\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}A_{1}&A_{2}\\
-\bar{A}_{2}&\bar{A}_{1}\end{array}\right).\end{array}

Then

h(A^{\ast})=h(A_{1}^{\ast}-A_{2}^{t}j)=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}A_{1}^{\ast}&-A_{2}^{t}\\
\bar{A}_{2}^{t}&\overline{\left(A_{1}^{\ast}\right)}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}A_{1}&A_{2}\\
-\bar{A}_{2}&\overline{A_{1}}\end{array}\right)^{\ast}=h(A)^{\ast}\text{,}

and

tr\left(h(A)\right)=tr\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}A_{1}&A_{2}\\
-\bar{A}_{2}&\bar{A}_{1}\end{array}\right)=\left(trA_{1}+tr\bar{A}_{1}\right)=2\operatorname{Re}tr(A).

\blacksquare

Although the cyclic property of the trace does not hold in general for
quaternionic matrices due to non-commutativity, we can verify the invariance
of the trace under the adjoint action via the homomorphism
h

cyclicity of the trace in the complex setting.

Lemma 19

tr(AXA^{\ast})=tr(X),\quad\text{for all }A\in SU(2,\mathbb{H}),X\in\mathcal{H}_{2}^{0}(\mathbb{H}).

Proof.
Using Lemma
18
and the fact that
AXA^{\ast}
is Hermitian for

and

\displaystyle tr(AXA^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}(tr(AXA^{\ast}))=\tfrac{1}{2}tr(h(AXA^{\ast}))=\tfrac{1}{2}tr(h(A)h(X)h(A^{\ast}))

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}tr(h(X))=\operatorname{Re}(tr(X))=tr(X)

as required.
\blacksquare

Remark 34

Our study has primarily relied on the spin representation theory in low
dimensions. A natural direction for extending this work is to consider
replacing the field
\mathbb{F\ }
with the octonions
\mathbb{O}
, the final
member in the family of normed division algebras. However, unlike the real
numbers, complex numbers, and quaternions, the octonions are not associative.
As a result, standard constructions that work for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}
often fail or require significant modification in the
octonionic setting. Despite this, we have attempted to extend our framework to
the case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{O}
, and found that, through adjustments of the
earlier constructions, a similar theory can indeed be formulated considering

SU(2,\mathbb{O})\
as
Spin\left(9\right)\
and related spinors. We
remark the extension to
\mathbb{O\ }
can also be considered for
Spin\left(8\right)
to
\mathbb{O}^{2}
and the triality of
Spin\left(8\right)\
induces various interesting structures on polygon spaces. We discuss this
in another article.

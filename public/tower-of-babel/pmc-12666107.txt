Contribution of laparoscopic surgery to the diagnosis of ovarian metastasis from thymoma: A rare case and review of the literature
Authors: Yui Sejimo, Kenichi Makino, Masato Waga, Enami Kaneko, Takeo Hirakawa, Tae Sugawara, Taichi Yoshida, Hiroyuki Shibata, Hiroshi Nanjo, Yukihiro Terada
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Ovarian metastasis of thymoma is exceptionally rare, and this case represents only the fourth report worldwide.•Preoperative distinction from primary ovarian tumors is difficult, requiring histopathological confirmation.•Diagnostic laparoscopy enabled accurate diagnosis and complete resection of metastatic ovarian lesions.•Minimally invasive surgery offers both diagnostic and therapeutic value in rare metastatic presentations.

Introduction

Thymoma is a rare epithelial tumor arising from the thymus in the anterior mediastinum, accounting for approximately 20–30 % of mediastinal tumors and 0.2–1.5 % of all malignancies.

Although it usually remains confined to the thoracic cavity, extrathoracic metastasis is exceedingly rare (Chiappetta et al., 2021). Ovarian involvement is extremely rare, with only a few cases reported in the literature. Extrathoracic spread of thymoma is associated with poor prognosis. However, complete surgical resection may improve outcomes when possible (Hamaji et al., 2012). Laparoscopic surgery is a minimally option that helps achieve accurate diagnosis and supports treatment planning. Herein we present a rare case of bilateral ovarian metastases and peritoneal dissemination from thymoma diagnosed via laparoscopic surgery. Complete resection was achieved, which contributed to the patient’s management. We also include a brief review of previous cases to help explain and support this unusual clinical finding.

Case report

A 53-year-old woman was referred to our department for evaluation of a right ovarian mass and a nodule in the pouch of Douglas, identified on computed tomography. At the age of 48, an abnormal shadow was noted in the lung field on chest X-ray during a routine medical checkup. Thoracoscopic pleural biopsy was performed at a previous hospital, leading to the diagnosis of thymoma. The tumor was judged to be unresectable by thoracic surgeons. She was therefore referred to our oncology department for systemic chemotherapy. Because thymoma is generally sensitive to chemotherapy, treatment was initiated with the expectation that surgical resection could be reconsidered if tumor shrinkage was achieved After five cycles of preoperative chemotherapy with the ADOC regimen (doxorubicin, cisplatin, vincristine, cyclophosphamide), a marked reduction in tumor size was observed, and she subsequently underwent mediastinal tumor resection with partial resection of the left diaphragm and phrenic nerve, and partial resection of the left upper and lower lobes. Pathology confirmed thymoma, World Health Organization type B3, Masaoka stage IVA (pT4NxM1). She was followed without adjuvant therapy.

At age 50, recurrence in the left pleura was treated with five additional cycles of ADOC therapy. At ages 51 and 52, pleural dissemination in the left upper and lower lungs was treated with 40 Gy of radiotherapy each. At age 53 computed tomography (CT) showed mild enlargement of the right ovary, and a solid nodule in the pouch of Douglas (Fig. 1). On initial evaluation at our department, transvaginal ultrasound revealed mild enlargement of the right ovary (34 × 26 mm) with a solid internal structure. A round and solid tumor (13 × 10 mm) was also observed in the pouch of Douglas. The endometrium was not thickened, and the left ovary was showed no evidence of enlargement. There was no ascites. Tumor markers were within normal ranges; carcinoembryonic antigen 3.6 ng/mL, carbohydrate antigen 19–9 at 9.3 U/mL, cancer antigen 125 at < 1.0 U/mL, alpha-fetoprotein 5.4 ng/mL, and soluble interleukin-2 receptor 217 U/mL. These tumor markers were measured as part of the routine preoperative evaluation to differentiate possible histologic subtypes of ovarian malignancy. Positron emission tomography (PET-CT) showed mild uptake in the enlarged right ovary and the nodule in the pouch of Douglas (Fig. 1), raising suspicion of malignancy.

Diagnostic laparoscopy was performed. Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy and peritoneal nodule resection were planned. Intraoperatively, the right ovary was enlarged with a 5- mm dark red nodule. The left ovary exhibited a similar surface lesion. Multiple small nodules ranging from 2 to 20 mm in diameter were evident in the pouch of Douglas and the Retzius’ space. Single red nodules measuring approximately 10 mm were found on the peritoneum near the liver and left paracolic gutter (Fig. 2). Histopathological examination revealed nests and sheets of atypical thymic epithelial cells proliferating against a background of lymphocytic infiltration. The findings were consistent with the patient’s primary B3 thymoma diagnosed at the initial surgery, leading to the diagnosis of bilateral ovarian metastases and peritoneal dissemination. Postoperative recovery was uneventful, and the patient was discharged on postoperative day three after short-term observation to ensure adequate recovery and exclude complications. Postoperative chemotherapy with the ADOC regimen was resumed by the oncology department for two additional cycles, and the patient has remained disease-free for nine months after surgery.

Discussion

Thymoma generally follows an indolent clinical course. However, recurrence or dissemination may occur even several years after initial treatment. Recurrent thymoma typically spreads locally through direct invasion rather than hematogenous dissemination, and extrathoracic metastasis remains uncommon. Recurrent thymoma usually spreads locally through direct invasion rather than hematogenous dissemination. Although the reported incidence varies among studies, extrathoracic metastasis has been observed in 6.6 % to 23.4 % of cases. The most frequently observed site of distant metastasis was the lung, although liver and bone metastases have also been reported in some cases (Chiappetta et al., 2021). Surgical resection is often performed for recurrent thymoma when possible, usually as part of an interdisciplinary treatment approach combining chemotherapy and radiation therapy. There are several reports that complete resection is associated with significantly better survival than incomplete resection (Chiappetta et al., 2021, Hamaji et al., 2012).

Ovarian metastasis is exceptionally rare, with only three well-documented cases available in the literature to date (Martín-Hernández et al., 2015, Demirkiran et al., 2009, Kothari et al., 2000). In the present case, diagnostic laparoscopic surgery allowed histopathological confirmation and complete resection of the metastatic lesion, leading to a definitive diagnosis and therapeutic intervention. This highlights the utility of minimally invasive surgery in both diagnosis and treatment of rare ovarian metastasis from thymoma.

Metastatic ovarian tumors account for approximately 10–15 % of all malignant ovarian neoplasms. In clinical practice it is often difficult to distinguish them from primary ovarian tumors before surgery. Although tumor markers and imaging findings can provide supportive information, histopathological evaluation is the most reliable method for diagnosis. (Kubeček et al., 2017) Juretzka et al. (2006) reported that among patients with a history of non-gynecologic malignancy who underwent laparoscopic evaluation of ovarian masses detected on imaging, 18.7 % were diagnosed with malignancy, and 61.2 % of these were metastatic ovarian tumors (Juretzka et al., 2006). In patients with a prior history of cancer, distinguishing between metastatic and primary ovarian tumors is particularly challenging, making tissue diagnosis essential. However, the indication for surgery should be carefully determined based on an individualized assessment, taking into account the patient’s general condition and the resectability of other distant metastatic lesions.

It is widely recognized that diagnostic laparoscopy for suspected primary ovarian tumors offers several advantages over open surgical biopsy, including fewer perioperative complications, faster recovery, and earlier initiation of chemotherapy (Fagotti et al., 2016). In the present case laparoscopic surgery also proved valuable for diagnosing metastatic ovarian tumors. It enabled safe and timely confirmation of the tumor origin, and complete resection with minimal invasiveness.

In recent years, laparoscopy has become an internationally accepted approach for both diagnosis and treatment. This case illustrates the rarity of ovarian metastasis from thymoma and highlights the diagnostic and therapeutic value of laparoscopic surgery in such uncommon clinical settings. It also supports the role of minimally invasive surgery in managing metastatic disease when the diagnosis is uncertain.

Conclusion

Ovarian metastasis from thymoma is extremely rare, and often difficult to diagnose preoperatively. The present case highlights the importance of considering metastatic disease in patients with a history of thymoma presenting with ovarian masses. Diagnostic laparoscopy proved to be a valuable tool, enabling both accurate diagnosis and complete tumor resection. Minimally invasive surgery can play a central role in the management of atypical metastatic presentations, contributing to both patient outcomes and diagnostic clarity in rare clinical scenarios.

Introduction

In the literature one can find several examples of processes that approximate the Brownian motion (when dealing with one parameter processes) or the Brownian sheet (when dealing with several parameters). Among these examples, there are very well-known processes like the random walk or the Kac-Stroock process (see, for instance, Corollary 1 in
[
8
]
or
[
2
]
), which have the particularity that are processes of bounded variation and thus, it makes sense to talk about integrals with respect to such processes in a pathwise sense by using the usual Lebesgue-Stieltjes theory of integration. It is then natural to ask if these integrals approximate the stochastic integral with respect to the Brownian motion/sheet defined in the Itô sense (for the one parameter case) or as in (
1
) (for the multidimensional parameter set case).

More precisely, for fixed
0<T
, let
\{\zeta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\zeta_{n}=\{\zeta_{n}(t)\colon t\in[0,T]\}
, be a sequence of continuous processes weakly converging to a Brownian motion
W=\{W(t)\colon t\in[0,T]\}
in the space of continuous functions over
[0,T]
,
\mathcal{C}([0,T])
, and whose sample paths are of bounded variation and let
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
Y_{n}=\{Y_{n}(t)\colon t\in[0,T]\}
, be a sequence of processes converging in some sense to another process
Y=\{Y(t)\colon t\in[0,T]\}
. Is it then true that the processes

X_{n}\coloneqq\left\{X_{n}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}Y_{n}(u)d\zeta_{n}(u)\colon t\in[0,T]\right\},

converge (in some sense) to

X\coloneqq\left\{X(t)=\int_{0}^{t}Y(u)dW(u)\colon t\in[0,T]\right\}

as
n
approaches infinity?

This problem has already been addressed (when a single parameter is taken into account) in, for instance,
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
by considering càdlàg processes
Y_{n}
,
Y
and with
\{\zeta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of càdlàg semimartingales such that the joint law of
(Y_{n},\zeta_{n})
weakly converges, in the space of càdlàg functions, to
(Y,\zeta)
, where
\zeta=\{\zeta(t)\colon t\in[0,T]\}
is some càdlàg process (ultimately, a semimartingale) for which the stochastic integral of
Y
with respect to
\zeta
is well defined.

One can find some results in this direction as well, when the parameter set is of dimension
2
, in
[
1
]
, where the problem of convergence of stochastic integrals is used to give approximations to solutions of the stochastic heat equation when the driving noise is approximated in distribution.

Mostly motivated by the results seen in the latter, the purpose of this paper will be to provide similar results in the multidimensional parameter set case by considering the random walk and the Kac-Stroock processes as approximating sequences, for which we have explicit expressions of
\zeta_{n}
, and by replacing the processes
Y_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by functions satisfying some integrability condition, which we shall denote by
f_{n}
, converging to some other function
f
.

The article is organized in the following way, Section
2
is devoted to introduce the involved processes and some preliminary results. In Section
3
we state and prove the main result. Finally, subsections
3.1
and
3.2
are devoted to check that a couple of families of processes verify the hypotheses of the main result.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we shall provide the main definitions and tools we will be working with.

Let
d\geq 1
and consider
[0,T]=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[0,T_{i}]\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
,
T=(T_{1},...,T_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
, with the usual partial order (total in the case
d=1
). For
s=(s_{1},...,s_{d}),t=(t_{1},...,t_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
s<t
, we write
(s,t]=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(s_{i},t_{i}]
(and analogously for open and closed rectangles) and denote by
\Delta_{s}X(t)
the increment of the process over the rectangle
(s,t]
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},Q)
be a complete probability space and let
\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\colon t\in[0,T]\}
be a family of sub-
\sigma
-fields of
\mathscr{F}
such that
\mathscr{F}_{s}\subset\mathscr{F}_{t}
if
s\leq t
. For fixed
t\in[0,T]
, we also define
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{T}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{d}\mathscr{F}_{T_{1},...,T_{i-1},t_{i},T_{i+1},...,T_{d}}
.

To define the Brownian sheet and the stochastic integral with respect to such process, we will make use of the isonormal Gaussian process over a real separable Hilbert space
H
with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a stochastic process
W=\{W(h)\colon h\in H\}
defined in a complete probability space is an isonormal Gaussian process if it is a centered Gaussian process with covariance function
\text{Cov}(f,g)=\langle f,g\rangle_{H}
for all
f,g\in H
.

From now on, we shall take
H=L^{2}([0,T])
with the usual inner product. A Brownian sheet (or
d
-parameter Wiener process) is then defined as the process
\tilde{W}=\{\tilde{W}(t)\colon t\in[0,T]\}
with

\tilde{W}(t)=W\left(\mathbb{I}_{(0,t]}\right),

where
\mathbb{I}_{A}
is the indicator function of the set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For a given function
f\in L^{2}([0,T])
and
t\in[0,T]
, we then define the Wiener integral of
f
with respect to the Brownian sheet over
[0,t]
as
W(f\mathbb{I}_{[0,t]})
and denote it by

W(f\mathbb{I}_{[0,t]})=\int_{[0,t]}f(u)\tilde{W}(du).

(1)

To simplify the notation, we will write
\int_{[0,t]}f(u){W}(du)
instead of
\int_{[0,t]}f(u)\tilde{W}(du)
.

One can easily check (via Kolmogorov’s continuity Theorem), that the Brownian sheet and the integral of an
L^{2}([0,T])
function with respect to it have a continuous version, so, when talking about these objects, we will be talking about the continuous versions.

We now introduce the two approximating sequences for the Brownian sheet, the random walk and the Kac-Stroock process, which will be defined via the Donsker kernels and the Kac-Stroock kernels, respectively.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\{Z_{k}\colon k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\}
be a sequence of independent and identically distributed centered random variables with unitary variance, the Donsker kernels are the processes
\{\theta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

\theta_{n}(t)=n^{\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{k=(k_{1},...,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}Z_{k}\mathbb{I}_{[k-1,k)}(nt),\hskip 9.24994ptt\in[0,T],

(2)

where
k-1=(k_{1}-1,...,k_{d}-1)
.

The approximating sequence of random walks is then defined to be as the sequence of processes

\zeta_{n}(t)=\int_{[0,t]}\theta_{n}(u)du=n^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{k\leq[nt]}Z_{k}+n^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}Z_{k}\left(\int_{[0,nt]\backslash[0,[nt]]}\mathbb{I}_{[k-1,k)}(u)du\right),

(3)

where
[s]=([s_{1}],...,[s_{d}])
for
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
and
[x]
is the integer part of
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The reader might note that this is not exactly a random walk, but the multiparameter analogous to the corresponding linear interpolation in the one parameter case.

As stated in the introduction, in
[
8
]
, Corollary 1, it is shown that the sums
n^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{k\leq[nt]}Z_{k}
weakly converge towards a Brownian sheet in the space of càdlàg functions as
n
approaches infinity. For the sake of completeness, we will show in the Appendix that the processes
\zeta_{n}
, as defined in (
3
), converge towards the same process in the space of continuous functions over
[0,T]
,
\mathcal{C}([0,T])
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
d
-parameter càdlàg process
N_{\mu}=\{N_{\mu}(t)\colon t\in[0,T]\}
is a Poisson process with intensity
\mu>0
if it is null on the axes and, for all
0\leq s<t
,
\Delta_{s}N_{\mu}(t)
is independent of
\mathscr{F}_{s}^{T}
with a Poisson law of parameter
\mu\prod_{i=1}^{d}(t_{i}-s_{i})
.

If we do not specify the filtration, it will be the one generated by the process itself, completed with the necessary null sets.

Definition 2.4
.

The Kac-Stroock kernels are the processes
\{\theta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

\theta_{n}(t)=n^{\frac{d}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}t_{i}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}(-1)^{N_{n}(t)}.

Similarly to the case of the random walks, the Kac-Stroock processes will be given by
\zeta_{n}(t)=\int_{[0,t]}\theta_{n}(u)du
. It is shown, in
[
2
]
, that these processes weakly converge, in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T])
, towards a Brownian sheet as
n
approaches infinity.

At this point, we observe that both approximating sequences treated in this paper (the random walk and the Kac-Stroock process), have a very specific form which allows us to easily formulate the multiparameter analogue of the weak convergence already studied in
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
and mentioned in the introduction.

More precisely, the aim of this paper will be to stablish the weak convergence of the processes

X_{n}\coloneqq\left\{X_{n}(t)=\int_{[0,t]}f_{n}(u)\theta_{n}(u)du\colon t\in[0,T]\right\}

(4)

towards the process

X\coloneqq\left\{X(t)=\int_{[0,t]}f(u)W(du)\colon t\in[0,T]\right\}

(5)

in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T])
as
n
approaches infinity, where
\zeta_{n}
weakly converges towards a Brownian sheet in
\mathcal{C}([0,T])
and
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{2}([0,T])
.

Finally, we end this section by introducing the main tools used in order to prove the desired convergence in law. As it is customary, to prove such convergence, one needs to prove that the sequence of laws associated to the sequence of processes
X_{n}
is tight in
\mathcal{C}([0,T])
and that their finite dimensional distributions converge towards the ones of
X
. The first result (Theorem
2.1
) is a tightness criteria which is an immediate consequence of the results shown in
[
4
]
. The second one (Lemma
2.1
) is a general result that will be used to prove the convergence of the finite dimensional distributions.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of real valued continuous processes over
[0,T]
vanishing along the axes. Suppose that there exist
\beta>1
,
\gamma>0
and finite nonnegative measures
\mu
and
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
[0,T]
with continuous marginals such that
\mu_{n}
weakly converges to
\mu
and, for each
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Delta_{s}Y_{n}(t)\right|^{\gamma}\right]\leq\left(\mu_{n}((s,t])\right)^{\beta}.

Then the sequence of laws associated to the processes
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(F,||\cdot||)
be a normed vector space and
\{J^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
J
be linear maps from
F
to
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Assume there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for any
f\in F

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|J^{n}(f)\right|\right]\leq C||f||,\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbb{E}\left[\left|J(f)\right|\right]\leq C||f||,

and that, for some dense subspace
D
of
F
, it holds that
J^{n}(f)
converges in law to
J(f)
, as
n
tends to infinity, for all
f\in D
. Then, the sequence of random variables
\{J^{n}(f)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in law to
J(f)
for any
f\in F
.

If, in addition, we have
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset F
and
f\in F
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
(F,||\cdot||)
and

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|J^{n}(f_{n}-f)\right|\right]\leq C||f_{n}-f||,

where
C>0
is independent of
n
,
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
f
, then the sequence
\{J^{n}(f_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in law to
J(f)
.

Proof.

For the first part, recall that a sequence of random variables
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in law to a random variable
X
if, and only if, for any bounded Lipschitz function
g\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[g(X_{n})\right]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[g(X)\right].

Thus, we shall see that, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J^{n}(f)\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J(f)\right)\right]\right|<\varepsilon,

(6)

where
g
is any Lipschitz function as before. Consider any
h\in D
such that
||f-h||<\frac{\varepsilon}{3L_{g}C}
, where
L_{g}>0
is the Lipschitz constant of
g
, and apply the triangle inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J^{n}(f)\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J(f)\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J^{n}(f)\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J^{n}(h)\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J^{n}(h)\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J(h)\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J(h)\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J(f)\right)\right]\right|.

Now observe that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J^{n}(f)\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J^{n}(h)\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|g\left(J^{n}(f)\right)-g\left(J^{n}(h)\right)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq L_{g}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|J^{n}(f-h)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq L_{g}C||f-h||<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

Similarly,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J(f)\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J(h)\right)\right]\right|\leq L_{g}C||f-h||<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Finally, given that
J^{n}(h)
converges in law to
J(h)
for
h\in D
, we have that, for
n
large enough,

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J^{n}(h)\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(J(h)\right)\right]\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Thus, for
n
large enough, we obtain (
6
) as desired.

For the second part, it suffices to show that the sequences
\{J^{n}(f_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{J^{n}(f)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
have the same limit in
L^{1}(\Omega)
(and thus, the same limit in law) since, by the previous part,
J^{n}(f)
converges in law to
J(f)
. But this is immediate from the additional hypothesis, since

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|J^{n}(f_{n})-J^{n}(f)\right|\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|J^{n}(f_{n}-f)\right|\right]\leq C||f_{n}-f||\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

∎

3
Statement and proof of the main result

This section is devoted to prove the convergence in distribution of the processes
X_{n}
defined in (
4
) towards the process
X
defined by (
5
). As shown by the main result of this paper (Theorem
3.1
), it turns out that this problem can be reduced to computing the moments of the increments over rectangles of the processes
X_{n}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
and suppose there is
m>2q
and a positive constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{[0,T]}g_{n}(u)\theta_{n}(u)du\right|^{m}\right]\leq C\left(\int_{[0,T]}|g_{n}(u)|^{2q}du\right)^{\frac{m}{2q}},

(7)

for any sequence of functions
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2q}([0,T])
. Then, the processes
X_{n}
defined by (
4
) converge in law, as
n
approaches infinity, towards the process
X
defined by (
5
) in
\mathcal{C}([0,T])
whenever the sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2q}([0,T])
converges in
L^{2q}([0,T])
to
f\in L^{2q}([0,T])
.

Before we start with the proof, we shall make some remarks.

The introduction of the parameter
q
is due to the fact that, in the case of the Kac-Stroock kernels, when
d\geq 2
, we have only been able to check condition (
7
) for sequences in
L^{2q}([0,T])
with
q>1
.

As the proof shows, it turns out that, as long as condition
(
7
)
holds, it is only required that the finite dimensional distributions of the processes
\zeta_{n}
converge to those of the Brownian sheet. There is no need for the convergence in law of the processes
\zeta_{n}
to the Brownian sheet in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T])
.

One might note as well that, by taking
f_{n}=f\in L^{2q}([0,T])
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the integrals
\int f(u)\theta_{n}(u)du
converge to the integral
\int f(u)W(du)
.

Given that
q\geq 1
and
m>2q
, condition (
7
) implies that, for each
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, the moments
\mathbb{E}\left[|\Delta_{s}X_{n}(t)|^{2}\right]
are uniformly bounded in
n\in\mathbb{N}
(because
f_{n}
converges in
L^{2q}([0,T])
, implying that the sequence of
L^{2q}([0,T])
-norms is bounded). Thus, for each
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, the sequence
\{|\Delta_{s}X_{n}(t)|^{2}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly integrable, meaning that we have, as well, convergence of the first and second moments. Moreover, if condition (
7
) holds for any even integer
m>2q
, then this will imply that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[|\Delta_{s}X_{n}(t)|^{m}\right]=\frac{m!}{2^{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)!}\left(\int_{[s,t]}f^{2}(u)du\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}

for any even integer
m\geq 2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

As mentioned in the previous section, it suffices to show that the sequence is tight and that the finite dimensional distributions of
X_{n}
converge towards the ones of
X
.

Tightness

This is an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.1
by taking, up to some positive factor,

\mu_{n}(A)\coloneqq\int_{A}|f_{n}(u)|^{2q}du,\hskip 9.24994pt\mu(A)\coloneqq\int_{A}|f(u)|^{2q}du,

for all measurable sets
A\subset[0,T]
and noticing that, for any
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,

\Delta_{s}X_{n}(t)=\int_{(s,t]}f_{n}(u)\theta_{n}(u)du=\int_{[0,T]}f_{n}(u)\mathbb{I}_{(s,t]}(u)\theta_{n}(u)du.

Indeed,
\mu_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mu
defined as above are nonnegative finite measures with continuous marginals and, since
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{2q}([0,T])
, we have that
\mu_{n}
converges weakly towards
\mu
. To see this, take any bounded continuous function
g\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
,

\left|\int_{[0,T]}g(u)\mu_{n}(du)-\int_{[0,T]}g(u)\mu(du)\right|\leq\sup_{u\in[0,T]}|g(u)|\int_{[0,T]}\left||f_{n}(u)|^{2q}-|f(u)|^{2q}\right|du.

If we show that this last integral converges to
0
as
n
approaches infinity then we are done. Equivalently, we need to show that

\int_{[0,T]}\left||f_{n}(u)|^{2q}-|f(u)|^{2q}\right|d\lambda\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0,

where
\lambda
is a probability measure on
[0,T]
defined by
\frac{du}{d\lambda}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}T_{i}
. To show this, we will show that the functions
g_{n}(u)=\left||f_{n}(u)|^{2q}-|f(u)|^{2q}\right|
converge to
0
in probability and that they are uniformly integrable (all with respect to the probability measure
\lambda
).

To see the convergence in probability, we first note that, since
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{2q}([0,T])
, we have
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{2q}([0,T],\lambda)
. In particular, we will have convergence in probability with respect to
\lambda
. Since convergence in probability is preserved by continuous transformations, we will have that
g_{n}
converges to
0
in probability. To see that the
g_{n}
are uniformly integrable, we first note that

|g_{n}(u)|\leq|f_{n}(u)|^{2q}+|f(u)|^{2q},

so it suffices to show that the
|f_{n}(u)|^{2q}
are uniformly integrable, but this follows from the fact that the sequence converges in
L^{2q}([0,T],\lambda)
.

Convergence of the finite dimensional distributions

To show that the finite dimensional distributions converge as desired, it suffices to show that, for any,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{1},...,a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and any
t_{1},...,t_{k}\in[0,T]
, the sums
\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}X_{n}(t_{j})
converge in law towards
\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}X(t_{j})
. In order to prove this, we first note that,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}X_{n}(t_{j})=J^{n}\left(f_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{j}]}\right),\hskip 9.24994pt\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}X(t_{j})=J\left(f\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{j}]}\right).

where
J^{n},J
are the linear maps defined by

\displaystyle J^{n}(g)\coloneqq\int_{[0,T]}g(u)\theta_{n}(u)du,\hskip 9.24994ptJ(g)\coloneqq\int_{[0,T]}g(u)W(du).

Since
f_{n},f\in L^{2q}([0,T])
, the elements
f_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{j}]}
and
f\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{j}]}
are in the same space as well, so the maps can be defined in
(L^{2q}([0,T]),||\cdot||_{2q})
, where
||\cdot||_{2q}
is the standard norm in this space. By hypothesis and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|J^{n}(g)\right|\right]\leq C||g||_{2q}.

Similarly, by the isometry property of the Wiener integral and Hölder’s inequality again,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|J(g)\right|\right]\leq C||g||_{2q},

for some positive constant
C
which might be different from the one seen in the hypotheses. This in particular means that the maps
J^{n}
and
J
take values in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Thus, it only remains to show that
J^{n}(g)
converges in law towards
J(g)
for simple functions
g
of the form

g(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{l}g_{j}\mathbb{I}_{(s_{j-1},s_{j}]}(u),

with
l\geq 1
,
g_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
0=s_{0}<s_{1}<...<s_{l}=T
, which are dense in
L^{2q}([0,T])
. Indeed, if we manage to show this, then, by the first part of Lemma
2.1
, we will have that
J^{n}\left(f\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{j}]}\right)
converges in law towards
J\left(f\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{j}]}\right)
and, by the second part and the fact that
f_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{j}]}\to f\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{j}]}
in
L^{2q}([0,T])
, we will have that
J^{n}\left(f_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{j}]}\right)
will converge in law towards
J\left(f\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{j}]}\right)
as well.

To show the convergence in law for simple functions, we only need to notice that

\displaystyle J^{n}(g)=\int_{[0,T]}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{l}g_{j}\mathbb{I}_{(s_{j-1},s_{j}]}(u)\right)\theta_{n}(u)du=\sum_{j=1}^{l}g_{j}\int_{(s_{j-1},s_{j}]}\theta_{n}(u)du

and that this last sum converges in law towards

\sum_{j=1}^{l}g_{j}\int_{(s_{j-1},s_{j}]}W(du)=\int_{[0,T]}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{l}g_{j}\mathbb{I}_{(s_{j-1},s_{j}]}(u)\right)W(du)=J(g)

because the finite dimensional distributions of
\zeta_{n}
converge to those of the Brownian sheet.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Proof.

For any

\displaystyle\Bigg|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{[0,T]}g_{n,p}(u)\theta_{n}(u)du\right)^{m}\right]

Appendix

In this appendix, we give a proof of the convergence in law of the processes

\mathcal{C}([0,T])
when the random variables
\{Z_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}

Theorem A.1
.

If the centered random variables
\{Z_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
are independent and identically distributed with unitary variances and finite moments of order
4
, then the processes
\zeta_{n}
defined as in (
3
) converge towards the Brownian sheet in
\mathcal{C}([0,T])
as
n
approaches infinity.

Proof.

Inspection of the proof of Theorem (
3.1
) shows that the sequence is tight by taking
f_{n}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
q=1
and
m=4
. So it only remains to show that the finite dimensional distributions of the processes
\zeta_{n}
converge to the ones of the Brownian sheet. To this purpose, for each
t\in[0,T]
, let

R_{n}(t)=n^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}Z_{k}\left(\int_{[0,nt]\backslash[0,[nt]]}\mathbb{I}_{[k-1,k)}(u)du\right)

and note that
\mathbb{E}\left[R_{n}^{2}(t)\right]
converges to
0
as
n
approaches infinity. Indeed, if
t_{i}=0
for some
i\in\{1,...,d\}
, then the result follows immediately. If
t>0
, then we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[R_{n}^{2}(t)\right]

\displaystyle=n^{-d}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\left(\int_{[0,nt]\backslash[0,[nt]]}\mathbb{I}_{[k-1,k)}(u)du\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq n^{-d}\sum_{k\in\Delta_{n}}\left(\int_{[0,nt]\backslash[0,[nt]]}\mathbb{I}_{[k-1,k)}(u)du\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq n^{-d}\sum_{k\in\Delta_{n}}1

\displaystyle=n^{-d}|\Delta_{n}|,

where
\Delta_{n}\subset\mathbb{N}^{d}
is the set of points
k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
such that
[k-1,k)\subset[0,[nt]+1]\backslash[0,[nt]]
,
|\Delta_{n}|
is the number of elements in
\Delta_{n}
and have used that
[k-1,k)
has unitary Lebesgue measure. By definition of
\Delta_{n}
, we have

|\Delta_{n}|=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left([nt_{j}]+1\right)-\prod_{j=1}^{d}[nt_{j}],

so, for
n
large enough so that
nt_{j}\geq 1
for each
j\in\{1,...,d\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[R_{n}^{2}(t)\right]

\displaystyle\leq n^{-d}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left([nt_{j}]+1\right)-\prod_{j=1}^{d}[nt_{j}]\right)

\displaystyle=n^{-d}\prod_{j=1}^{d}[nt_{j}]\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{[nt_{j}]+1}{[nt_{j}]}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}T_{j}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{[nt_{j}]}{nT_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{[nt_{j}]+1}{[nt_{j}]}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}T_{j}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{[nt_{j}]+1}{[nt_{j}]}-1\right),

where we have used that
[nt_{j}]\leq nt_{j}\leq nT_{j}
for each
j\in\{1,...,d\}
. Since
x\mapsto\frac{x+1}{x}
decreases
(0,\infty)
, it follows that

\frac{[nt_{j}]+1}{[nt_{j}]}\leq\frac{[n\min_{i}t_{i}]+1}{[n\min_{i}t_{i}]}\leq\frac{n\min_{i}t_{i}}{n\min_{i}t_{i}-1}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}1

where we have used that
x-1<[x]\leq x
per a tot
x\geq 0
. Thus,

\mathbb{E}\left[R_{n}^{2}(t)\right]\leq\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}T_{j}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{[nt_{j}]+1}{[nt_{j}]}-1\right)\leq\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}T_{j}\right)\left[\left(\frac{n\min_{i}t_{i}}{n\min_{i}t_{i}-1}\right)^{d}-1\right]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

Hence, if we set
S_{n}(t)=n^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{k\leq[nt]}Z_{k}
, Chebyshev’s inequality will imply that the finite dimensional distributions of
\zeta_{n}
and
S_{n}
have the same limit. So it only remains to show that the finite dimensional distributions of
S_{n}
converge to the ones of the Brownian sheet.

By the Cramér-Wold device, we only need to show that, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}

t^{1},...,t^{m}\in[0,T]
and each
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{R}

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}W(t^{j})

C(s,t)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(t_{i}\wedge s_{i})
, we have that the latter is a centered Gaussian random variable with variance

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}W(t^{j})\right)^{2}\right]=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\mathbb{E}\left[W(t^{i})W(t^{j})\right]=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\prod_{l=1}^{d}(t_{l}^{i}\wedge t_{l}^{j}).

(15)

Now let
a_{n}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,[nt^{j}]]}(s)
,
s\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, and observe that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}|a_{n}(s)|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\alpha_{j}|<\infty,

and that

\displaystyle n^{-d}\sum_{s\leq[nT]}a_{n}^{2}(s)

\displaystyle=n^{-d}\sum_{s\leq[nT]}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\mathbb{I}_{[0,[nt^{i}]]}(s)\mathbb{I}_{[0,[nt^{j}]]}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\left(n^{-d}\sum_{s\leq[nT]}\mathbb{I}_{[0,[nt^{i}]\wedge[nt^{j}]]}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\prod_{l=1}^{d}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s_{l}\leq[nt_{l}^{i}]\wedge[nt_{l}^{j}]}\mathbb{I}_{[0,1]}\left(\frac{s_{l}}{[nt_{l}^{i}]\wedge[nt_{l}^{j}]}\right)\right).

Since, for each
i,j\in\{1,...,m\}
and

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s_{l}\leq[nt_{l}^{i}]\wedge[nt_{l}^{j}]}\mathbb{I}_{[0,1]}\left(\frac{s_{l}}{[nt_{l}^{i}]\wedge[nt_{l}^{j}]}\right)\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t_{l}^{i}\wedge t_{l}^{j}}du=t_{l}^{i}\wedge t_{l}^{j},

we can conclude, by Lemma 4.2.2 in
[
6

, that the finite dimensional distributions of

converge to the ones of the Brownian motion, finishing the proof.
∎

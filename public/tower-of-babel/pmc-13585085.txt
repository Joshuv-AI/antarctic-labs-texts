Assessment of Matrix Band Systems in Restoring Proximal Caries of Primary Molars

Introduction

Dental caries is the most prevalent oral health problem across the world that can negatively impact the quality of life in children. The risk of caries increases when there is an established posterior proximal contact in children. Restoration of primary teeth with the establishment of appropriate contact between two adjacent teeth is important in maintaining arch integrity, protecting the pulp, maintaining the health of the supporting structures by preventing food impaction, and ensuring the normal functions of the tooth.1 The establishment of interproximal contacts and contours to their natural anatomic form is one of the primary objectives of restorative treatment.2 It is always a challenging task for pedodontists to restore these teeth due to various reasons, such as the inability of children to cooperate, moisture contamination, inadequate access to carious lesions, lack of suitable material for the pediatric age-group, morphological variations of primary teeth such as a convex outline in both the cervical-occlusal and buccolingual directions of the proximal surface, and the need for perfect adaptation of the matrix to compensate for the missing surfaces.3–6 The problems encountered with proximal restorations are overhangs, inadequate adaptation of the restoration to the tooth, and failure to reestablish its natural anatomic form and proximal contact. The success of the restorative material and achievement of a superior restoration depend on the selection of material and techniques that are more advantageous for both clinicians and pediatric patients. Appropriate use of the matrix system from the commercially available matrix systems can help in achieving the contact and contour required to meet the ideal requirements of the restoration.

In this study, three different matrix systems that were used to restore proximal caries of primary molars were Fendermate, T-band, and Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring.

T-band was primarily used for primary teeth in earlier decades and was custom-made according to each patient's needs.7 Fendermate, being a one-piece sectional matrix system, was easy to place and remove, thus making it comfortable for pediatric patients as well as clinicians.8 Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring, a sectional matrix system, had an added advantage with the presence of the sectional ring, the precontoured matrix strip with a coating, and an adaptive wedge that could get accommodated to its embrasure size.

Glass ionomer cement gained popularity among general dental practitioners as well as specialty pediatric dentists due to its various advantages, such as high biocompatibility, ability to form a chemical bond with dentin and enamel with insignificant heat formation or shrinkage, fluoride-releasing properties, anticariogenic properties, a coefficient of thermal expansion similar to dentin, less technique sensitivity, and the ability to be placed in a single increment.9–11 It was also preferred by many dentists and specialty dentists for restoring caries in children.12–19

However, previous studies in this field mainly focused on permanent dentition, with limited literature available on matrix systems for primary teeth. Hence, the present study aimed to evaluate and compare three different matrix band systems (Fendermate, T-band, and Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring) in restoring proximal caries of primary molar teeth.

A null hypothesis was formulated that there would be no difference among the matrix systems used.

General examination and clinical intraoral examination were carried out with the patient seated in the dental chair under illumination, followed by a preoperative radiographic examination to diagnose the caries status of the tooth. Thirty teeth were randomly assigned to each of the three experimental groups. Rubber dam isolation was performed, and caries removal was carried out using a spoon excavator and cavity preparation burs (round bur BR 49, inverted cone bur SI 46) with a high-speed handpiece. Matrix systems in all groups were placed in accordance with the manufacturers' instructions.

Group I—Thirty teeth were restored using Fendermate (Directa Dental) (single-piece stainless steel matrix with a wedge), inserted either buccally or lingually.

Group II—Thirty teeth were restored using a T-band matrix (Pulpdent). Here, the short arm was bent to accommodate the long arm according to the circumference of the tooth, and the matrix was burnished to adapt to the contours of the tooth and was secured with a wedge.

Group III—Thirty teeth were restored using Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring (Garrison Dental Solutions). Here, the sectional matrix was selected according to the size of the tooth and was placed interproximally and secured with a sectional ring and a plastic wedge.

All cavities were restored using Type IX glass ionomer cement (GC HS Posterior Extra) following the manufacturer's instructions. Occlusion was checked, and restorations were finished and polished using finishing and polishing burs (EX-21EF). Clinical and radiographic assessment of proximal restorations (Table 1) was carried out to compare the performance of the three matrix systems. The assessment criteria used were adopted from Souqiyyeh20 and Durr-E-Sadaf21 and were modified for the present study.

Statistical Analysis

All observations were recorded and tabulated using Microsoft Excel. Statistical analysis was conducted using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 22.0 with the Chi-squared test. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

This study evaluated three matrix band systems: Group I—Fendermate (Directa Dental); Group II—T-band matrix (Pulpdent); Group III—Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring (Garrison Dental Solutions); across various clinical and radiographic parameters of the restorations.

Clinically and radiographically, there was no significant difference in the performance of the three matrix systems for the parameters assessed, except for the establishment of proximal contact in the restoration of proximal caries. For this parameter, Fendermate and Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring were significantly superior to the T-band system (p = 0.028, p = 0.001), with no significant difference observed between Fendermate and Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring (p = 0.114) (Table 2).

Discussion

Nearly 60–95% of children in both developed and developing countries are affected by dental caries, particularly proximal caries, which is significant because of its propensity for rapid progression and difficulty in diagnosis, specifically in primary teeth because of morphological variations, broader contact areas, limited access, and salivary contamination.22 Restoration of teeth with proximal caries in primary dentition by recreating the decayed tooth structure to its natural anatomic form is very important, as it is not only needed to replace the lost tooth structure but also to establish a well-contoured, properly positioned, firm proximal contact to maintain arch integrity, the health of the supporting structures, and equal distribution of forces along the long axis of the teeth.23,24

The literature suggests morphological changes in the shape and size of the proximal contacts in the developing occlusion. This can be attributed to the continuously applied intraoral centric and eccentric functional movements during the process of mastication, deglutition, and phonetics, causing increased attritional forces leading to the constant transposition of the dentition and alteration of proximal contact surface positions. It is challenging for dentists to provide an ideal proximal restoration with dynamic changes of occlusion, which can be achieved by the application of acquired skills, technique, knowledge, and appropriate use of available armamentarium.

As Rutsky25 stated, the success or failure of a proximo-occlusal restoration depends upon the matrix band, which is considered the most crucial factor. Traditionally used matrix systems such as the Ivory and Tofflemire systems were difficult to use, time-consuming, and uncomfortable for children.26 To overcome these disadvantages, different manufacturers have modified and improved the precision and design of matrices that are easy to use, provide better adaptation, are comfortable for the patient, and help establish the anatomy of the restoration close to the natural tooth form. Although all recently developed matrices are superior and have better qualities, none of the systems meet all the requisites of an ideal matrix. Earlier research mainly focused on permanent dentition, and very limited studies regarding the use of matrix systems are available in primary dentition. The need for reproducing optimum contacts and contours in pediatric populations often goes unnoticed. Hence, the present study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of three matrix band systems that were available for the pediatric age-group for restoring proximal caries in primary molars.

T-band is one of the most commonly used matrix systems in pediatric dentistry for class II restorations, as it is self-contained and does not require matrix retainers. Commercially available bands made of soft, adaptable, straight stainless steel matrix material [0.002 in (0.05 mm)] (Pulpdent) were used for the study and were stabilized using wooden wedges.

Fendermate, a recently evolved matrix system (Directa Dental), is a precurved one-piece sectional matrix attached to a wedge that can be inserted either buccally or lingually,26 which provides quick application and predictable proximal restorations with tight contacts and proper cervical sealing.27 Four different sizes and colors are available in the market (neon yellow, neon green, green, and blue), of which neon yellow (short) and neon green (long), specially designed for pediatric groups, were used in this study.

A more recently evolved system available in the market is the Composi-Tight 3D Fusion (Garrison Dental Solutions). It is a sectional matrix system kit consisting of three color-coded rings of different sizes (blue, green, and orange) with a silicone stop to hold precontoured bands, a sectional precontoured nonsticky matrix of different sizes, a universal forceps for applying the ring, and a soft flexible plastic wedge. Of these, the blue ring, which was specially designed for primary molars, was used in the study.

With the eruption of teeth and development of occlusion over a period of time, point contact slowly evolves into a broader, larger contact area, and the physiologic spaces of the primary dentition become closed. Even the morphology of contact surfaces can change. Hence, children aged four to seven years were included in the study.

Age and behavior are the most important factors to be considered while treating pediatric patients.6 With growing demands, the current concepts regarding esthetics in restorative dentistry have gained importance among the population. Traditional drilling and filling have become outdated, and modern operative dentistry has adopted a variety of advancements in esthetic restorative materials such as composites and glass ionomer cement. In the present study, glass ionomer cement was used to restore proximal caries of primary molar teeth, as it was less technique-sensitive, had fluoride-releasing activity, anticariogenic activity, and could be placed in a short time, providing comfort to patients without making them uncooperative.2,3 Additionally, many dentists preferred using glass ionomer cement to restore carious lesions in children because of its superior properties.4–8,28–30

The present study evaluated contour, contact, and overhangs by following the modified version of Durr-E-Sadaf and Souqiyyeh,20,21 and the findings were reconfirmed with radiographs. The results showed their efficiency in the reconstruction of proximal carious lesions, with varied performance in terms of the establishment of proximal contacts and contours.

The clinical and radiographic assessment of all matrix systems demonstrated efficiency in the restoration of proximal caries by establishing the contour of the lost tooth structure with a minimal number of overhangs. No significant differences were observed among the three matrix systems. Similar findings were observed by Murali et al.,31 who aimed to compare the efficacy of three matrix systems (Fendermate, Pro-Matrix, and T-band) for restoring proximal caries and concluded that all matrices showed similar results, with no statistical difference among the three groups. A study by Hindi et al.,32 comparing circumferential and sectional matrix systems, suggested that the circumferential matrix system was better than the sectional matrix system. However, in our study, Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring was significantly superior in establishing proximal contact with the adjacent tooth compared with T-band and Fendermate. A literature search showed no similar studies with which to compare our results.

The clinical and radiographic study supported the acceptance of the null hypothesis that was proposed, as there was no significant difference in the performance of the three matrix systems in restoring proximal caries of primary molars using glass ionomer cement.

Further studies are recommended with a larger sample size and a variety of matrix systems to confirm the results of the present study and to recommend the ideal matrix system suitable for primary dentition.

Conclusion

No significant difference was observed in the performance of all three matrix systems in the restoration of proximal caries for all parameters assessed, except for proximal contact, where Fendermate and Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring were significantly superior to the T-band system (p = 0.028, p = 0.001), and no significant difference was seen between Fendermate and Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring (p = 0.114).

Overall, Composi-Tight 3D Fusion Ring was better in terms of matrix adaptation, patient cooperation, separation of the tooth during restorative procedures, and restoration of the natural anatomic tooth form.

Hence, the appropriate adaptation of matrix systems helps in the establishment of successful proximal restorations.

In pediatric restorative practice, preoperative evaluation of the status of caries and tooth morphology, along with appropriate selection of a matrix system specific to the condition, can help restore tooth morphology to near normal.

Clinical Significance

This study was conducted to identify a better matrix system for restoring proximal cavities of primary molars.

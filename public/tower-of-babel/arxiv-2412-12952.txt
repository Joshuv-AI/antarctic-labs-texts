A partial proof of the Brouwer’s conjecture
Slobodan Filipovski

Abstract
Let G G be a simple graph with n n vertices and m m edges and let k k be a natural number such that k ≤ n . k\leq n. Brouwer conjectured that the sum of the k k largest Laplacian eigenvalues of G G is at most m + ( k + 1 2 ) . m+{k+1\choose 2}. In this paper we prove that this conjecture is true for simple ( m , n ) (m,n) -graphs where n ≤ m ≤ 3 − 1 4 ​ ( n − 1 ) ​ n n\leq m\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{4}(n-1)n and k ∈ [ 8 ​ m 2 n − 1 + 4 ​ m ​ n + n 2 3 , n ] . k\in\left[\sqrt[3]{\frac{8m^{2}}{n-1}+4mn+n^{2}},n\right]. Moreover, we prove that the conjecture is true for all simple ( m , n ) (m,n) -graphs where k ( ≤ n ) k(\leq n) is a natural number from the interval [ 2 ​ n − 2 ​ m + 2 ​ 2 ​ m 2 + m ​ n ​ ( n − 1 ) , 1 + 8 ​ m 2 n 2 ​ ( n − 1 ) + 4 ​ m n ] . \left[\sqrt{2n-2m+2\sqrt{2m^{2}+mn(n-1)}},1+\frac{8m^{2}}{n^{2}(n-1)}+\frac{4m}{n}\right].

Introduction

Let
G
be a simple graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
and let
|V(G)|=n
and
|E(G)|=m.

The degree of a vertex
v\in V(G)
denoted by
d(v)
, is the number of neighbours of
v
.
The Laplacian matrix of
G
is the
n\times n
matrix
L(G)=[l_{ij}]
defined as
L(G)=D(G)-A(G)
, where
D(G)
is the diagonal matrix of vertex degrees of the graph
G
, and
A(G)

is the adjacency matrix of
G
.
It is well known that
L(G)
is a positive semidefinite matrix, consequently its eigenvalues are nonnegative real numbers. The eigenvalues of
L(G)
are called the Laplacian
eigenvalues of
G
and are denoted by
\mu_{1}(G)\geq\mu_{2}(G)\geq\ldots\geq\mu_{n}(G)
. Since each row sum of
L(G)
is
0
, it holds
\mu_{n}(G)=0.

In this paper, we study the sum
S_{k}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}(G)
for
1\leq k\leq n
. Brouwer
[
1
]
has conjectured the following upper bound for the sum
S_{k}(G).

Conjecture 1
.

[
1
]
Let
G
be a graph with vertices
n
and edges
m
. Then

S_{k}(G)\leq m+{k+1\choose 2}

for
k=1,2,\ldots,n.

Based on a computer check, Brouwer
[
1
]
showed that the Conjecture 1 is true for all graphs with at most
10
vertices. There have been many partial proofs of this conjecture using specific methods from spectral graph theory. For
k=1
, the conjecture follows from the well-known inequality

\mu_{1}(G)\leq|V(G)|=n,
see in
[
7
]
. The conjecture has been proved to be true for any graphs with
k=2,n-2,n-3
, see
[
5
,
8
]
, trees
[
8
]
, threshold graphs
[
9
]
, unicyclic graphs
[
6
]
, bicyclic graphs
[
6
,
12
]
, tricyclic graphs having no pendant vertices
[
10
,
12
]
, regular graphs
[
2
,
11
]
, split graphs
[
11
]
, cographs
[
2
,
11
]
, planar graphs when
k\geq 11
and bipartite graphs when
k\geq\sqrt{32n}

[
4
]
. Also the cases
k=n
and
k=n-1
are straightforward.
Another related upper bound was given by Zhou in
[
14
]
:

S_{k}(G)\leq\frac{2mk+\sqrt{mk(n-k-1)(n^{2}-n-2m)}}{n-1}

where
1\leq k\leq n-2
and
m=|E(G)|.

In this paper we prove that the Brouwer’s conjecture is true for all simple
(m,n)
-graphs where
n\leq m\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{4}(n-1)n
and
k\in\left[\sqrt[3]{\frac{8m^{2}}{n-1}+4mn+n^{2}},n\right].
Moreover, we prove that this conjecture is true for all simple
(m,n)
-graphs where
k\leq n
is a natural number from the interval
\left[\sqrt{2n-2m+2\sqrt{2m^{2}+mn(n-1)}},1+\frac{8m^{2}}{n^{2}(n-1)}+\frac{4m}{n}\right].

2
A partial proof of the conjecture

Let
J
be all-ones matrix of size
n
and let
L^{{}^{\prime}}=J-L.
Since
(L^{{}^{\prime}})^{\texttt{T}}=J^{\texttt{T}}-L^{\texttt{T}}=J-L=L^{{}^{\prime}},
we obtain that
L^{{}^{\prime}}
is a symmetric matrix.
Let
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\mu_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
L
, and let
\mu_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\geq\mu_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}\geq\ldots\geq\mu_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}
be the eigenvalues of
L^{{}^{\prime}},
in the decreasing order. Moreover, let
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
J
. It is well-known that
\lambda_{1}(J)=n
and
\lambda_{i}(J)=0
, for
i=2,3,\ldots,n.

Applying the Weyl’s inequality
[
13
]
we get the following result.

Lemma 2
.

a)

\mu_{i}+\mu^{{}^{\prime}}_{n-i+2}\leq 0\;\;\text{for}\;\;i=2,3,\ldots,n.

b)

\mu_{1}+\mu_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\geq n.

Proof.

a)

From Weyl’s inequality we get:

\mu_{i}(L)+\mu^{{}^{\prime}}_{j}(L^{{}^{\prime}})\leq\lambda_{i+j-n}(L+L^{{}^{\prime}})=\lambda_{i+j-n}(J).

(1)

Setting
j=n-i+2
in (
1
) we get the inequality:

\mu_{i}+\mu^{{}^{\prime}}_{n-i+2}\leq\lambda_{2}(J)=0,\;\;\text{for}\;\;i=2,3,\ldots,n.

(2)

b)

Since
\text{trace}(L)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i},

\text{trace}(L^{{}^{\prime}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}
and

\text{trace}(L)+\text{trace}(L^{{}^{\prime}})=\text{trace}(J)=n
we have

\mu_{1}+\mu_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\mu_{i}+\mu_{n-i+2}^{{}^{\prime}})=n.

From a) we get
\mu_{1}+\mu_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\geq n.

∎

The
first Zagreb index
for a graph with a vertex set
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and vertex degrees
d_{i}=\text{deg}(v_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
is defined by

M_{1}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{2}.

The following upper bound for
M_{1}
, obtained by de Caen in
[
3
]
, will be used in our proof.

Lemma 3
.

[
3
]
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges. Then

M_{1}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn-2m.

Moreover, if
G
is connected, then equality holds if and only if
G
is either a star
K_{1,n-1}
or a complete graph
K_{n}.

In the next theorem we give an upper bound for
S_{k}(G)
in terms of
m,n
and
k.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges. Then

S_{k}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{k}\leq n+\sqrt{(k-1)\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right)}.

Proof.

From
\text{trace}(L\cdot L^{\texttt{T}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}l_{ij}^{2}
and
L=L^{\texttt{T}}
we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}^{2}=\text{trace}(L^{2})=\text{trace}(L\cdot L^{\texttt{T}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}l_{ij}^{2}.

(3)

Analogously we get

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mu_{i}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}=\text{trace}((L^{{}^{\prime}})^{2})=\text{trace}(L^{{}^{\prime}}\cdot(L^{{}^{\prime}})^{\texttt{T}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(l_{ij}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}.

(4)

Combining (
3
) and (
4
) we get:

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mu_{i}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}l_{ij}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(l_{ij}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(l_{ij}^{2}+(1-l_{ij})^{2})

=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(2l_{ij}^{2}-2l_{ij}+1)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}2d_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}2d_{i}+n\right)+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}(2l_{ij}^{2}-2l_{ij}+1)\right)

=(2M_{1}-4m+n)+((4+4+\ldots+4)+n^{2}-n)

=2M_{1}-4m+n+2m\cdot 4+n^{2}-n=2M_{1}+4m+n^{2}.

Let
t=\frac{\mu_{1}}{n}.
Since
\mu_{1}\leq n
we get
0<t\leq 1.
The inequality
\mu_{1}+\mu_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\geq n
implies

\mu_{1}^{2}+(\mu_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}\geq n^{2}t^{2}+n^{2}(1-t)^{2}=n^{2}(2t^{2}-2t+1).

(5)

Moreover, from
\mu_{i}+\mu^{{}^{\prime}}_{n-i+2}\leq 0,\;\;\text{for}\;\;i=2,3,\ldots,n,
and from
\mu_{i}\geq 0
we easily conclude that
\mu_{i}^{2}\leq(\mu_{n-i+2}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}.

Therefore we have

2M_{1}+4m+n^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mu_{i}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}=(\mu_{1}^{2}+(\mu_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2})+\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\mu_{i}^{2}+(\mu_{n-i+2}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2})

\geq(1-2t(1-t))n^{2}+2\sum_{i=2}^{n}\mu_{i}^{2}.

From the last inequality we get

\sum_{i=2}^{k}\mu_{i}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=2}^{n}\mu_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{2M_{1}+4m+n^{2}}{2}-\frac{(1-2t(1-t))n^{2}}{2}.

(6)

Now, based on the inequality (
6
) and applying the inequality between the arithmetic and quadratic means for the nonnegative eigenvalues
\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\ldots,\mu_{n}
we get

\sum_{i=2}^{k}\mu_{i}\leq\sqrt{(k-1)(\mu_{2}^{2}+\ldots+\mu_{k}^{2})}\leq\sqrt{(k-1)\left(\frac{2M_{1}+4m+n^{2}}{2}-\frac{(1-2t(1-t))n^{2}}{2}\right)}.

(7)

Hence we get

S_{k}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}=\mu_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\mu_{i}\leq nt+\sqrt{(k-1)\left(M_{1}+2m+n^{2}t(1-t)\right)}.

(8)

Setting
M_{1}\leq\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}-2m+mn
in (
8
) we obtain the following inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}\leq nt+\sqrt{(k-1)\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn+n^{2}t(1-t)\right)}.

(9)

In the end, from
t\leq 1
and
t(1-t)\leq\frac{1}{4}
we have

S_{k}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}\leq n+\sqrt{(k-1)\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right)}.

∎

Theorem 5
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges such that

n\leq m\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{4}(n-1)n.

The Brouwer’s conjecture holds for all natural numbers
k
from the interval
\left[\sqrt[3]{\frac{8m^{2}}{n-1}+4mn+n^{2}},n\right].

Proof.

The bound in Theorem 4 and the assumption
n\leq m
yield

S_{k}(G)\leq n+\sqrt{(k-1)\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right)}<m+\sqrt{k\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right)}.

(10)

Now, we are going to show that for
k\in\left[\sqrt[3]{\frac{8m^{2}}{n-1}+4mn+n^{2}},n\right]
, it holds

m+\sqrt{k\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right)}\leq m+{k+1\choose 2}.

Since
\frac{k^{2}}{2}<{k+1\choose 2},
it suffices to prove that

k\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right)\leq\frac{k^{4}}{4},

which obviously holds for
k\geq\sqrt[3]{\frac{8m^{2}}{n-1}+4mn+n^{2}}.

In the end, we verify that for
m\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{4}(n-1)n
it occurs
\sqrt[3]{\frac{8m^{2}}{n-1}+4mn+n^{2}}<n.

The inequality
\sqrt[3]{\frac{8m^{2}}{n-1}+4mn+n^{2}}<n
is equivalent to the quadratic inequality (in
m
)

8m^{2}+4mn(n-1)-n^{2}(n-1)^{2}<0.

(11)

Solving the inequality (
11
) we get
m\in\left(\frac{(-\sqrt{3}-1)}{4}n(n-1),\frac{(\sqrt{3}-1)}{4}n(n-1)\right).

∎

Theorem 7
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges such that

\sqrt{2n-2m+2\sqrt{2m^{2}+mn(n-1)}}<1+\frac{8m^{2}}{n^{2}(n-1)}+\frac{4m}{n}.

The Brouwer’s conjecture is true for any natural number
k\leq n
from the interval

\left[\sqrt{2n-2m+2\sqrt{2m^{2}+mn(n-1)}},1+\frac{8m^{2}}{n^{2}(n-1)}+\frac{4m}{n}\right].

Proof.

Let
g(t)=nt+\sqrt{(k-1)\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn+n^{2}t(1-t)\right)}
where
0\leq t\leq 1.
Then
g^{{}^{\prime}}(t)=n+\frac{n^{2}(k-1)(1-2t)}{2\sqrt{(k-1)\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn+n^{2}t(1-t)\right)}}.

If
0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then it is clear that
g^{{}^{\prime}}(t)>0.

Now, let
t>\frac{1}{2}.

We obtain

g^{{}^{\prime}}(t)>0\Leftrightarrow n>\frac{n^{2}(k-1)(2t-1)}{2\sqrt{(k-1)\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn+n^{2}t(1-t)\right)}}.

(12)

The inequality (
12
) is equivalent to

\frac{8m^{2}}{n-1}+4mn+4n^{2}t(1-t)\geq n^{2}(k-1)(2t-1)^{2}.

(13)

We easily observe that the inequality in (
13
) holds since
2t-1\leq 1
,
n^{2}t(1-t)\geq 0
and
k\leq 1+\frac{8m^{2}}{n^{2}(n-1)}+\frac{4m}{n}.

Since
g^{{}^{\prime}}(t)>0
, we get that
g(t)
is an increasing function on the interval
[0,1]
. Recalling the inequality (
9
) we have

S_{k}(G)\leq g(t)\leq g(1)=n+\sqrt{(k-1)\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn\right)}.

Let us suppose that
k\geq\sqrt{2n-2m+2\sqrt{2m^{2}+mn(n-1)}}
. We get
2m+k^{2}\geq 2n+2\sqrt{2m^{2}+mn(n-1)}.
Hence

m+{k+1\choose 2}>m+\frac{k^{2}}{2}\geq n+\sqrt{2m^{2}+mn(n-1)}=n+\sqrt{(n-1)\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn\right)}

\geq n+\sqrt{(k-1)\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{n-1}+mn\right)}=g(1)\geq S_{k}(G).

∎

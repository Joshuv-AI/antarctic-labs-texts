Weighted BMO-BLO estimates for Littlewood–Paley square operators
Runzhe Zhang and Hua Wang

Abstract

The weighted space BLO ⁡ ( ω ) \mathrm{BLO}(\omega) (the space of functions with bounded lower oscillation) is introduced and studied in this paper. Introduction

In the present paper, we study the existence and boundedness of Littlewood–Paley square operators, which are a generalization of the classical Littlewood–Paley operators defined by Poisson integrals.The weighted version of BLO spaces is introduced and studied, and weighted BMO-BLO estimates for Littlewood–Paley square operators are established. We believe that the results obtained in this paper are original in the weighted case.

The symbols
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{N}
stand for the sets of all real numbers and natural numbers, respectively. Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
n

|x|:=\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

It is well known that the Littlewood–Paley theory is a very important tool in harmonic analysis, complex analysis and PDEs. Littlewood–Paley theory can be viewed as a profound generalization of the Pythagorean theorem. It originated in the 1930’s and developed in the 1960’s. The Littlewood–Paley function in one dimension was first introduced by Littlewood and Paley in studying the dyadic decomposition of Fourier series (see
[

[
27

28

29

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which include the
g
-function,Lusin’s area integral and Stein’s
g^{\ast}_{\lambda}
-function. Let
f
be a locally integrable function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u(x,t):=P_{t}*f(x)
be the Poisson integral of

on the upper half space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}=\big\{(y,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t>0\big\}

P_{t}(x):=c_{n}\cdot\frac{t}{(t^{2}+|x|^{2})^{{(n+1)}/2}}\quad\&\quad c_{n}=\frac{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}{\pi^{(n+1)/2}}

denotes the Poisson kernel in

g(f)(x):=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\nabla u(x,t)\big|^{2}t\,dt\bigg)^{1/2},

\nabla=\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\Big)\quad\&\quad\big|\nabla u(x,t)\big|^{2}=\Big|\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\Big|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\Big|^{2}.

The classical Lusin’s area integral (also referred to as the square function) and Stein’s

-function are defined, respectively, by

S(f)(x):=\bigg(\iint_{\Gamma(x)}\big|\nabla u(y,t)\big|^{2}t^{1-n}\,dydt\bigg)^{1/2}

Let us now recall the definition of the space

Definitions of weighted BMO and BLO spaces

A weight
\omega
is a nonnegative locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that takes values in
(0,+\infty)
almost everywhere.
For
1<p<\infty
, a weight
\omega
is said to belong to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
, if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\bigg(\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\omega(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\bigg)^{p-1}\leq C<+\infty

holds for every ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
p^{\prime}=p/{(p-1)}
denotes the Hölder conjugate exponent of
p
. For
p=1
, a weight
\omega
is said to belong to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{1}
, if there is a positive constant
C
such that

\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\omega(x)\,dx\leq C\cdot\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mbox{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)

holds for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The smallest constant
C>0
such that the above inequality holds is called the
A_{1}
-constant of
\omega
, and is denoted by
[\omega]_{A_{1}}
. It is well known that
A_{1}\subset A_{p}
, for any
1<p<\infty
. Given a Lebesgue measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use the notation

\omega(E):=\int_{E}\omega(x)\,dx

to denote the
\omega
-measure of the set
E
. Then we have

\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\omega(x)\,dx=\frac{\omega(\mathcal{B})}{m(\mathcal{B})}\leq[\omega]_{A_{1}}\cdot\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mbox{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x).

(2.1)

Given a weight
\omega
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as usual, the weighted Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\omega)
for
1\leq p<\infty
is defined as the set of all integrable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\big\|f\big\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}:=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}<+\infty.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.We define the weighted
\mathrm{BMO}

\mathrm{BMO}(\omega):=\Big\{f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}<+\infty\Big\},

where

\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}:=\sup_{\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{B}}|\,dx,

and
f_{\mathcal{B}}
denotes the mean value of the function

\mathcal{B}
.

The weighted space
\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
was introduced and studied by Garcia-Cuerva in

\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{p}(\omega)}:=\sup_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|^{p}\omega(x)^{1-p}dx\right)^{1/p}<+\infty.

. Garcia-Cuerva in

This tells us that the spaces

are equivalent with respect to different values of

, provided that
\omega\in A_{1}
. See Theorem

in the last section. For more results about weighted BMO spaces, we refer the readers to

,

,
36
,

]

.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that a locally integrable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is in the space
\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any ball
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\leq C.

The minimal constant
C
as above is defined to be the
\mathrm{BLO}
-constant of
f
with respect to
\omega
, and is denoted by
\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}
.

It is easy to verify that

\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}.

(2.2)

In fact, for any ball
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big|f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)+\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\Big|\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx+\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\Big|\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\Big|\\
&\leq\frac{2}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)},\end{split}

as desired.

Remark 2.9
.

1.

It should be pointed out that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}
is not a norm and
\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
is not a linear space (it is a proper subspace of
\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
).

2.

For the weighted case, we can also define the above notions with cubes in place of balls, and these two different definitions are actually equivalent.

Similarly, we can also define the general version of weighted BLO spaces. For
1\leq p<\infty
, we say that a locally integrable function
f
is in
\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)
, if

\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)}:=\sup_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]^{p}\omega(x)^{1-p}dx\right)^{1/p}<+\infty.

In this paper, we will establish a weighted version of John–Nirenberg’s inequality suitable for the space
\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
with
\omega\in A_{1}
. There exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, depending only on the dimension
n
, such that for every cube
\mathcal{Q}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
\lambda>0
,

\begin{split}&\nu\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]>\lambda\Big\}\Big)\\
&\leq{C}_{1}\cdot\nu(\mathcal{Q})\exp\bigg\{-\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]^{-1}\frac{{C}_{2}\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}}\bigg\},\end{split}

where
\nu(x):=\omega(x)^{1-p}
for
1<p<\infty
. The proof is based on the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition and stopping time arguments. We shall use this inequality to prove that the spaces
\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)
coincide, and the
\mathrm{BLO}
-constants of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)}
are equivalent with respect to different values of
p
, provided that
\omega\in A_{1}
. For further details, see Lemma
7.24
and Theorem
7.25
in the last section.

The main purpose of this paper is to establish weighted BMO-BLO estimates for classical and generalized Littlewood–Paley square operators. We also give the corresponding
L^{\infty}
-BLO results in the weighted case.

3
Main results

By the weighted theory of vector-valued singular integral operators, we can deduce the following weighted
L^{p}
-boundedness of Littlewood–Paley square operators. The proof of such kind of results can be found in
[
23
, Chapter 5]
and
[
38
, Chapter 7]
(see also
[
32
]
and
[
17
]
).

Theorem 3.10
.

For all
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in A_{p}
, then there exists a positive constant
C>0
, independent of
f
, such that

\big\|\mathcal{G}({f})\big\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}

for every
f\in L^{p}(\omega)
.

Theorem 3.11
.

For all
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in A_{p}
, then there exists a positive constant
C>0
, independent of
f
, such that

\big\|\mathcal{S}({f})\big\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}

for every
f\in L^{p}(\omega)
.

These weighted
L^{p}
estimates were extended by many authors to the multilinear case, one can see
[
26
,
33
,
34
]
for more details.

Inspired by Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.6
, we consider the behavior on weighted
L^{\infty}
and weighted BMO spaces for Littlewood–Paley square operators. Based on Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
3.11
, as well as some properties of
A_{p}
weights and weighted BMO spaces, we establish the existence and boundedness of generalized Littlewood–Paley square operators on weighted BMO spaces, including the
g
-function, Lusin’s area integral and Stein’s
g^{\ast}_{\lambda}
-function for
\lambda>3+{(2\delta+2\gamma)}/n
. It is proved that if the above operators are finite for one point, then they are finite almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, these operators are bounded from
\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
into
\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
when
\omega\in A_{1}
. We will also give the
L^{\infty}
-BLO results for Littlewood–Paley square operators, such kind of results can be viewed as a generalization of Theorem
1.5
in the weighted case.

The main results of this paper are stated as follows.

Theorem 3.12
.

For any
{f}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
and
\omega\in A_{1}
, then
\mathcal{G}({f})
is either infinite everywhere or finite almost everywhere, and in the latter case, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
{f}
, such that

\big\|\mathcal{G}({f})\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.

Theorem 3.13
.

For any
{f}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
and
\omega\in A_{1}
, then
\mathcal{S}({f})
is either infinite everywhere or finite almost everywhere, and in the latter case, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
{f}
, such that

\big\|\mathcal{S}({f})\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.

Theorem 3.14
.

Suppose that
(\lambda-3)n>2\delta+2\gamma
. For any
{f}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
and
\omega\in A_{1}
, then
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})
is either infinite everywhere or finite almost everywhere, and in the latter case, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
{f}
, such that

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.

By remark
1.1
, we may take
\psi(x)
to be

\psi(x)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{t}(x)\Big|_{t=1},

and then the functions
\mathcal{G}({f})(x)
,
\mathcal{S}({f})(x)
and
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})(x)
defined above become the classical Littlewood–Paley functions. Hence, the conclusions of the above theorems also hold for the classical Littlewood–Paley operators.

Throughout this paper, the letter
C
will stand for a positive constant, not necessarily the same at each occurrence but independent of the essential variables. By
\mathbf{X}\lesssim\mathbf{Y}
, we mean that there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that
\mathbf{X}\leq C\mathbf{Y}
. If
\mathbf{X}\lesssim\mathbf{Y}
and
\mathbf{Y}\lesssim\mathbf{X}
, then we write
\mathbf{X}\approx\mathbf{Y}
and say that
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}
are equivalent.

Some key lemmas

Let us begin by recalling some well-known results about
A_{1}
weights (see, for example,
[
5
]
and
[
9
]
).

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have:

(1)

for
\omega\in A_{1}
and for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\omega(2\mathcal{B})\leq 2^{n}[\omega]_{A_{1}}\cdot\omega(\mathcal{B}),

that is,
\omega(x)\,dx
is a doubling measure(
\omega
satisfies the doubling property).

(2)

Let
\omega\in A_{2}
. In general, for any
t>0
and for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\omega(t\mathcal{B})\leq t^{2n}[\omega]_{A_{2}}\cdot\omega(\mathcal{B}).

We also need the following two auxiliary lemmas about
\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
functions.

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
and
\omega\in A_{1}
. Then for any
1\leq p<\infty
and for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we get

\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|^{p}\omega(x)^{1-p}dx\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.

As mentioned before, this estimate was obtained by García-Cuerva in
[
7
]
. We will give a proof of García-Cuerva’s result in the last section. When
\omega(x)\equiv 1
, see
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
.

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
and
\omega\in A_{1}
. Then the following properties hold.

(1)

for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dx\leq\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.

(2)

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\frac{1}{m(2^{k}\mathcal{B})}\int_{2^{k}\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dx\leq Ck\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.

Here the constant
C
is independent of
k
and
f
.

Proof.

(1). Since
\omega\in A_{1}
, by (
2.1
) and the definition of
\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dx&=\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dx\\
&\leq\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)},\end{split}

as desired.

(2). Observe that for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}\big|f_{2\mathcal{B}}-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{2\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{2^{n}}{m(2\mathcal{B})}\int_{2\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{2\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dx\leq 2^{n}\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in 2\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)},\end{split}

by part (1). Similarly, for each
1\leq i\leq k
,

\big|f_{2^{i}\mathcal{B}}-f_{2^{i-1}\mathcal{B}}\big|\leq 2^{n}\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in 2^{i}\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.

Hence,

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{m(2^{k}\mathcal{B})}\int_{2^{k}\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dx\leq\frac{1}{m(2^{k}\mathcal{B})}\int_{2^{k}\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{2^{k}\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dx+\big|f_{2^{k}\mathcal{B}}-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|\\
&\leq\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in 2^{k}\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big|f_{2^{i}\mathcal{B}}-f_{2^{i-1}\mathcal{B}}\big|\\
&\leq\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in 2^{k}\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}+2^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in 2^{i}\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\\
&\leq Ck\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.\end{split}

We are done.
∎

5
Proofs of Theorems
3.12
and
3.13

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a real-valued nonnegative function and measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each fixed ball
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we need the following estimate about the relationship between essential supremum and essential infimum.

\mathcal{F}(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\mathcal{F}(y)\leq\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\big|\mathcal{F}(x)-\mathcal{F}(y)\big|.

(5.1)

We also need the following result. Let
\omega
be a weight function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since for each ball
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\omega(\mathcal{B})=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\omega(x)\,dx\geq\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\cdot m(B),

then we have

\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\leq 1.

(5.2)

Moreover, we will use the following simple fact.

\omega\in A_{2}\Longleftrightarrow\omega^{-1}\in A_{2}.

(5.3)

We are now in a position to prove our main theorems. We first remark that the a.e. existence of the classical and generalized Littlewood–Paley operators was already proved in
[
30
,
41
,
42
]
(see also
[
39
, Chapter 4]
), under the assumption of one point finiteness. For the multilinear case, one can see
[
10
]
. By using similar arguments, we can also obtain the corresponding (a.e. existence) results for the weighted case.

Proof of Theorem
3.12
.

Let
{f}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
and
\omega\in A_{1}
. By the definition of
\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
, it suffices to show that for any given ball
\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with center
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and radius
r\in(0,+\infty)
, the following inequality holds:

\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{G}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{G}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.

(5.4)

First of all, we decompose the integral defining
\mathcal{G}({f})
into two parts.

\begin{split}&\big[\mathcal{G}({f})(x)\big]=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\psi_{t}*f(x)\big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&=\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}\big|\psi_{t}*f(x)\big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}+\int_{r}^{\infty}\big|\psi_{t}*f(x)\big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}\big|\psi_{t}*f(x)\big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\big|\psi_{t}*f(x)\big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{1/2}:=\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big].\end{split}

Obviously,

\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f})(x)\big],\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]\leq\big[\mathcal{G}({f})(x)\big]\leq\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big].

Consequently, in view of (
5.1
), we can deduce that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{G}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{G}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{G}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f})(x)\big]dx+\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Big|\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\,dx\\
&:=I_{0}+I_{\infty}.\end{split}

Let us first estimate the term
I_{0}
. To do this, we decompose the function
f(x)
as follows.

\begin{split}f(x)&=f_{2\mathcal{B}}+[f(x)-f_{2\mathcal{B}}]\cdot\chi_{2\mathcal{B}}(x)+[f(x)-f_{2\mathcal{B}}]\cdot\chi_{(2\mathcal{B})^{\complement}}(x)\\
&:=f^{1}(x)+f^{2}(x)+f^{3}(x),\end{split}

where
2\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},2r)
,
(2\mathcal{B})^{\complement}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(2\mathcal{B})
and
\chi_{E}
denotes the
characteristic function of the set
E
. By the vanishing condition of the kernel
\mathcal{\psi}
, we can see that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\psi_{t}*f_{1}(x)\equiv 0\Longrightarrow\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f_{1}})(x)\big]\equiv 0.

Then we can write

\begin{split}I_{0}&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f})(x)\big]dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f}^{2})(x)\big]dx+\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f}^{3})(x)\big]dx\\
&:=I^{2}_{0}+I^{3}_{0}.\end{split}

Note that
\omega\in A_{1}\subset A_{2}
. Applying Theorem
3.10
with
p=2
and Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality together with (
5.3
), we obtain

\begin{split}I^{2}_{0}&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f}^{2})(x)\big]\omega(x)^{-1/2}\cdot\omega(x)^{1/2}dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f}^{2})(x)\big]^{2}\omega(x)^{-1}dx\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}\big\|\mathcal{G}({f}^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{-1})}\\
&\leq C\cdot\frac{\omega(2\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}{\omega(\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(2\mathcal{B})}\int_{2\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{2\mathcal{B}}\big|^{2}\omega(x)^{-1}dx\bigg)^{1/2}.\end{split}

Moreover, by Lemma
4.16
with
p=2
and part (1) of Lemma
4.15
, we have

\begin{split}I^{2}_{0}&\leq C\cdot\frac{\omega(2\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}{\omega(\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.\end{split}

Note that for any
x\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(2\mathcal{B})
, one has

|x-z|\leq|x-x_{0}|+|x_{0}-z|<r+|x_{0}-z|\leq\frac{3}{\,2\,}|x_{0}-z|,

and

|x_{0}-z|\leq|x_{0}-x|+|x-z|<r+|x-z|\leq\frac{1}{\,2\,}|x_{0}-z|+|x-z|.

That is,
|x-z|\approx|x_{0}-z|
. Thus, by the size condition of the kernel
\psi
, we can see that for any
x\in\mathcal{B}
,

\begin{split}&\big|\psi_{t}*f^{3}(x)\big|=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 2\mathcal{B}}\psi_{t}(x-z)\big[f(z)-f_{2\mathcal{B}}\big]\,dz\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 2\mathcal{B}}\frac{t^{\delta}}{(t+|x-z|)^{n+\delta}}\big|f(z)-f_{2\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz\\
&\lesssim t^{\delta}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}\setminus 2^{j}\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{|x_{0}-z|^{n+\delta}}\big|f(z)-f_{2\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz\\
&\lesssim t^{\delta}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{m(2^{j+1}\mathcal{B})^{1+\delta/n}}\big|f(z)-f_{2\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz.\end{split}

It then follows from part (2) of Lemma
4.17
that

\begin{split}\big|\psi_{t}*f^{3}(x)\big|&\lesssim t^{\delta}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{1}{m(2^{j}\mathcal{B})^{\frac{\delta}{n}}}\Big[j\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{z\in 2\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(z)\Big]\bigg\}\\
&\lesssim t^{\delta}\cdot\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{j}{m(2^{j}\mathcal{B})^{\frac{\delta}{n}}}\bigg\}\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{z\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(z).\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}I^{3}_{0}&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}_{0}({f}^{3})(x)\big]dx\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}t^{2\delta-1}dt\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{j}{m(2^{j}\mathcal{B})^{\frac{\delta}{n}}}\bigg\}\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\\
&\lesssim r^{\delta}\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{j}{(2^{j})^{\delta}r^{\delta}}\bigg\}\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\bigg\{\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\bigg\}\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used (
5.2
). On the other hand, by the smoothness condition of the kernel
\psi
, we can see that when
x,y\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(4\mathcal{B})
,

\begin{split}\Big|\psi_{t}(x-z)-\psi_{t}(y-z)\Big|&=\frac{1}{t^{n}}\cdot\bigg|\psi\Big(\frac{x-z}{t}\Big)-\psi\Big(\frac{y-z}{t}\Big)\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\frac{t^{\delta}\cdot|x-y|^{\gamma}}{(t+|x-z|)^{n+\delta+\gamma}}.\end{split}

(5.5)

Consequently, by (
5.5
), the triangle inequality and the vanishing condition of the kernel
\psi
, we obtain that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
,

\begin{split}&\Big|\big|\psi_{t}*f(x)\big|-\big|\psi_{t}*f(y)\big|\Big|\leq\Big|\psi_{t}*f(x)-\psi_{t}*f(y)\Big|\\
&=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big[\psi_{t}(x-z)-\psi_{t}(y-z)\big]\cdot\big[f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big]\,dz\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\int_{4\mathcal{B}}\big[\big|\psi_{t}(x-z)\big|+\big|\psi_{t}(y-z)\big|\big]\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 4\mathcal{B}}\frac{t^{\delta}\cdot|x-y|^{\gamma}}{(t+|x-z|)^{n+\delta+\gamma}}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz.\end{split}

(5.6)

By the size condition of the kernel
\psi
, we know that for any
t>0
,
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\big|\psi_{t}(x-z)\big|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}.

(5.7)

In view of (
5.7
), the first term in (
5.6
) is naturally controlled by

\begin{split}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\cdot\int_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz.\end{split}

We now proceed to estimate the second term in (
5.6
). Observe that when
t>r
,
x\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(4\mathcal{B})
,

t+|x-z|\leq t+|x-x_{0}|+|x_{0}-z|<2t+|x_{0}-z|\leq 2(t+|x_{0}-z|),

and

t+|x_{0}-z|\leq t+|x_{0}-x|+|x-z|<2t+|x-z|\leq 2(t+|x-z|).

That is,
t+|x-z|\approx t+|x_{0}-z|
. Thus, the second term in (
5.6
) is bounded by

t^{\delta}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 4\mathcal{B}}\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(t+|x_{0}-z|)^{n+\delta+\gamma}}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz.

Hence, by Minkowski’s inequality, we can deduce that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
,

\begin{split}&\Big|\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\Big|\big|\psi_{t}*f(x)\big|-\big|\psi_{t}*f(y)\big|\Big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{1}{t^{n}}\cdot\int_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz\bigg\}^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&+\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\bigg\{t^{\delta}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 4\mathcal{B}}\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(t+|x_{0}-z|)^{n+\delta+\gamma}}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz\bigg\}^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\int_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2n+1}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 4\mathcal{B}}(2r)^{\gamma}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2\delta-1}}{(t+|x_{0}-z|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}.\end{split}

For
\delta,\gamma>0
, a direct computation shows that

\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2n+1}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{n}},

and

\begin{split}&\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2\delta-1}}{(t+|x_{0}-z|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&=\bigg\{\int_{r}^{|x_{0}-z|}\frac{t^{2\delta-1}}{(t+|x_{0}-z|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dt+\int_{|x_{0}-z|}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2\delta-1}}{(t+|x_{0}-z|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&\leq\bigg\{\int_{r}^{|x_{0}-z|}\frac{t^{2\delta-1}}{(|x_{0}-z|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}+\bigg\{\int_{|x_{0}-z|}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2\delta-1}}{t^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&\leq\bigg\{\int_{0}^{|x_{0}-z|}\frac{t^{2\delta-1}}{(|x_{0}-z|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}+\bigg\{\int_{|x_{0}-z|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2(n+\gamma)+1}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{(|x_{0}-z|)^{n+\gamma}}.\end{split}

From this, it follows that

\begin{split}&\Big|\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{m(4\mathcal{B})}\int_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz\\
&+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}\setminus 2^{j}\mathcal{B}}\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(|x_{0}-z|)^{n+\gamma}}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{m(4\mathcal{B})}\int_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(2^{j}r)^{\gamma}}\cdot\frac{1}{m(2^{j+1}\mathcal{B})}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}}\big|f(z)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dz\bigg\}.\end{split}

Furthermore, from part (1) and part (2) of Lemma
4.17
, it then follows that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
,

\begin{split}&\Big|\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{z\in 4\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(z)+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{j}{2^{(j-1)\gamma}}\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{z\in 4\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(z)\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{z\in 4\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(z).\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}I_{\infty}&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Big|\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{G}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\,dx\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\bigg\{\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\underset{z\in 4\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(z)\bigg\}\\
&\leq\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\bigg\{\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\underset{z\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(z)\bigg\}\leq\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)},\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from (
5.2
). Combining the above estimates for both terms
I_{0}
and
I_{\infty}
yields the desired result (
5.4
). This completes the proof of Theorem
3.12
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.13
.

Let
{f}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
and
\omega\in A_{1}
. By the definition of
\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
, it suffices to prove that for any given ball
\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with radius
r\in(0,+\infty)
, the following inequality holds:

\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{S}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{S}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.

(5.8)

To prove (
5.8
), we first decompose the integral defining
\mathcal{S}({f})
into two parts.

\begin{split}\big[\mathcal{S}({f})(x)\big]&=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{|z-x|<t}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&=\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{|z-x|<t}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}+\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z-x|<t}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{|z-x|<t}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z-x|<t}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&:=\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big].\end{split}

It is clear that

\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f})(x)\big],\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]\leq\big[\mathcal{S}({f})(x)\big]\leq\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big].

Consequently, in view of (
5.1
), we can deduce that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{S}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{S}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{S}(\vec{f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f})(x)\big]dx+\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Big|\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\,dx\\
&:=J_{0}+J_{\infty}.\end{split}

As usual, we decompose the function
f(x)
in the following way

\begin{split}f(x)&=f_{4\mathcal{B}}+[f(x)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}]\cdot\chi_{4\mathcal{B}}(x)+[f(x)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}]\cdot\chi_{(4\mathcal{B})^{\complement}}(x)\\
&:=f^{1}(x)+f^{2}(x)+f^{3}(x),\end{split}

where
4\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},4r)
and
(4\mathcal{B})^{\complement}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(4\mathcal{B})
. By the vanishing condition of the kernel
{\psi}
, we can see that

\psi_{t}*f(x)=\psi_{t}*f^{2}(x)+\psi_{t}*f^{3}(x)\quad\&\quad\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f}^{1})(x)\big]\equiv 0.

Then we can write

\begin{split}J_{0}&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f})(x)\big]dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f}^{2})(x)\big]dx+\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f}^{3})(x)\big]dx\\
&:=J^{2}_{0}+J^{3}_{0}.\end{split}

Notice that
\omega\in A_{1}\subset A_{2}
. Applying Theorem
3.11
with
p=2
, Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality and (
5.3
), we obtain

\begin{split}J^{2}_{0}&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f}^{2})(x)\big]\omega(x)^{-1/2}\cdot\omega(x)^{1/2}dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f}^{2})(x)\big]^{2}\omega(x)^{-1}dx\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}\big\|\mathcal{S}({f}^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{-1})}\\
&\leq C\cdot\frac{\omega(4\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}{\omega(\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(4\mathcal{B})}\int_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|^{2}\omega(x)^{-1}dx\bigg)^{1/2}.\end{split}

Moreover, by using Lemma
4.16
with
p=2
and part (1) of Lemma
4.15
, we have

\begin{split}J^{2}_{0}&\leq C\cdot\frac{\omega(4\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}{\omega(\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.\end{split}

Notice that for any
x\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
,
|z-x|<t
with
t<r
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(4\mathcal{B})
, one has

\begin{split}|z-\xi|&\leq|z-x|+|x-x_{0}|+|x_{0}-\xi|\\
&<t+r+|x_{0}-\xi|<2r+|x_{0}-\xi|\leq\frac{3}{\,2\,}|x_{0}-\xi|,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}|x_{0}-\xi|&\leq|x_{0}-x|+|x-z|+|z-\xi|\\
&<r+t+|z-\xi|\leq\frac{1}{\,2\,}|x_{0}-\xi|+|z-\xi|.\end{split}

That is,
|z-\xi|\approx|x_{0}-\xi|
. Hence, by the size condition of the kernel
\psi
, we can see that for any
x\in\mathcal{B}
,

\begin{split}&\big|\psi_{t}*f^{3}(z)\big|=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 4\mathcal{B}}\psi_{t}(z-\xi)\big[f(\xi)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big]\,d\xi\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 4\mathcal{B}}\frac{t^{\delta}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{n+\delta}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&\lesssim t^{\delta}\cdot\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}\setminus 2^{j}\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{|x_{0}-\xi|^{n+\delta}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&\lesssim t^{\delta}\cdot\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{m(2^{j+1}\mathcal{B})^{1+\delta/n}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi.\end{split}

Moreover, by part (2) of Lemma
4.17
, we get

\begin{split}\big|\psi_{t}*f^{3}(z)\big|&\lesssim t^{\delta}\cdot\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{1}{m(2^{j}\mathcal{B})^{\frac{\delta}{n}}}\Big[j\cdot\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{\xi\in 4\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(\xi)\Big]\bigg\}\\
&\lesssim t^{\delta}\cdot\bigg\{\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{j}{m(2^{j}\mathcal{B})^{\frac{\delta}{n}}}\bigg\}\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{\xi\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(\xi).\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}J^{3}_{0}&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{S}_{0}({f}^{3})(x)\big]dx\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{|z-x|<t}t^{2\delta}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\{\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{j}{m(2^{j}\mathcal{B})^{\frac{\delta}{n}}}\bigg\}\\
&\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&\lesssim\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}t^{2\delta-1}dt\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\{\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{j}{m(2^{j}\mathcal{B})^{\frac{\delta}{n}}}\bigg\}\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\\
&\lesssim r^{\delta}\bigg\{\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{j}{(2^{j})^{\delta}r^{\delta}}\bigg\}\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\bigg\{\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\bigg\}\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)},\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from (
5.2
). On the other hand, by the smoothness condition of the kernel
\psi
, we can see that when
x,y\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
,
|z|<t
with
t>r
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(8\mathcal{B})
,

\begin{split}\Big|\psi_{t}(x+z-\xi)-\psi_{t}(y+z-\xi)\Big|&=\frac{1}{t^{n}}\cdot\bigg|\psi\Big(\frac{x+z-\xi}{t}\Big)-\psi\Big(\frac{y+z-\xi}{t}\Big)\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\frac{t^{\delta}\cdot|x-y|^{\gamma}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{n+\delta+\gamma}}.\end{split}

(5.9)

Consequently, by (
5.9
), the triangle inequality and the vanishing condition of the kernel
\psi
, we obtain that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
,

\begin{split}&\Big|\big|\psi_{t}*f(x+z)\big|-\big|\psi_{t}*f(y+z)\big|\Big|\leq\Big|\psi_{t}*f(x+z)-\psi_{t}*f(y+z)\Big|\\
&=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big[\psi_{t}(x+z-\xi)-\psi_{t}(y+z-\xi)\big]\cdot\big[f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big]\,d\xi\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big[\big|\psi_{t}(x+z-\xi)\big|+\big|\psi_{t}(y+z-\xi)\big|\big]\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\mathcal{B}}\frac{t^{\delta}\cdot|x-y|^{\gamma}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{n+\delta+\gamma}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi.\end{split}

(5.10)

From the previous estimate (
5.7
), the first term in (
5.10
) is naturally controlled by

\begin{split}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\cdot\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi.\end{split}

Finally, we consider the second term in (
5.10
). Observe that for any
|z|<t
with
t>r
,
x\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(8\mathcal{B})
,

\begin{split}t+|x+z-\xi|&\leq t+|z|+|x-x_{0}|+|x_{0}-\xi|\\
&<2t+r+|x_{0}-\xi|\\
&<3t+|x_{0}-\xi|\leq 3(t+|x_{0}-\xi|),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}t+|x_{0}-\xi|&\leq t+|x_{0}-x|+|x+z-\xi|+|z|\\
&<r+2t+|x+z-\xi|\\
&<3t+|x+z-\xi|\leq 3(t+|x+z-\xi|).\end{split}

This gives

t+|x+z-\xi|\approx t+|x_{0}-\xi|.

Then the second term in (
5.10
) is bounded by

t^{\delta}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\mathcal{B}}\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{n+\delta+\gamma}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi.

Thus, by using Minkowski’s inequality, we can deduce that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
,

\begin{split}&\Big|\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\\
&=\bigg|\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\big|\psi_{t}*f(x+z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}-\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\big|\psi_{t}*f(y+z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg|\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\Big|\big|\psi_{t}*f(x+z)\big|-\big|\psi_{t}*f(y+z)\big|\Big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\bigg\{\frac{1}{t^{n}}\cdot\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\}^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&+\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\bigg\{t^{\delta}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\mathcal{B}}\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{n+\delta+\gamma}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\}^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\mathcal{B}}(2r)^{\gamma}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\frac{t^{2\delta-n-1}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dzdt\bigg\}^{1/2}.\end{split}

We now proceed exactly as in Theorem
3.12
, and obtain

\begin{split}&\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2n+1}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{n}},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\frac{t^{2\delta-n-1}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dzdt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2\delta-1}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}\lesssim\frac{1}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{n+\gamma}}.\end{split}

Furthermore, from part (1) and part (2) of Lemma
4.17
, it follows that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
,

\begin{split}&\Big|\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{m(8\mathcal{B})}\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&+\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}\setminus 2^{j}\mathcal{B}}\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{n+\gamma}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{m(8\mathcal{B})}\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi+\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(2^{j}r)^{\gamma}}\cdot\frac{1}{m(2^{j+1}\mathcal{B})}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\}\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{\xi\in 8\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(\xi)+\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\frac{j}{2^{(j-1)\gamma}}\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{\xi\in 8\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(\xi)\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{x\in 8\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x).\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}J_{\infty}&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Big|\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{S}_{\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\,dx\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\bigg\{\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\underset{x\in 8\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\bigg\}\\
&\leq\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\bigg\{\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\bigg\}\leq\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)},\end{split}

where in the last step we have used inequality (
5.2
). Combining the above estimates for both terms
J_{0}
and
J_{\infty}
yields the desired result (
5.8
). This concludes the proof of Theorem
3.13
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.14

In this section, we will give the proof of Theorem
3.14
. We first establish the following weighted
L^{2}
-boundedness of
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}
, when
\lambda>2
and
\omega\in A_{2}
.

Theorem 6.18
.

Let
\omega\in A_{2}
. If
\lambda>2
, then the operator
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\omega)
.

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}
, we readily see that

\begin{split}\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})(x)\big]^{2}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|x-z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{|x-z|<t}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|x-z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\\
&+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|x-z|<2^{\ell}t}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|x-z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\\
&\lesssim\big[\mathcal{S}({f})(x)\big]^{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}2^{-\ell\lambda n}\big[\mathcal{S}_{2^{\ell}}({f})(x)\big]^{2},\end{split}

where

\big[\mathcal{S}_{2^{\ell}}({f})(x)\big]:=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{|x-z|<2^{\ell}t}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Since
\omega\in A_{2}
, by part (2) of Lemma
4.15
, we get

\omega\big(B(z,2^{\ell}t)\big)=\omega\big(2^{\ell}{B}(z,t)\big)\leq 2^{2\ell n}[\omega]_{A_{2}}\cdot\omega\big({B}(z,t)\big),

which means that

\int_{|z-x|<2^{\ell}t}\omega(x)\,dx\leq 2^{2\ell n}[\omega]_{A_{2}}\cdot\int_{|z-x|<t}\omega(x)\,dx.

This estimate, together with Fubini’s theorem, implies that for all
f\in L^{2}(\omega)
,

\begin{split}\big\|\mathcal{S}_{2^{\ell}}(f)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{|z-x|<2^{\ell}t}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)\omega(x)\,dx\\
&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{|z-x|<2^{\ell}t}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\\
&\leq 2^{2\ell n}[\omega]_{A_{2}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\bigg(\int_{|z-x|<t}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\\
&=2^{2\ell n}[\omega]_{A_{2}}\big\|\mathcal{S}(f)\big\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}.\end{split}

Here
\chi_{|z-x|<2^{\ell}t}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
\{x:|z-x|<2^{\ell}t\}
. Consequently, by Theorem
3.11
with
p=2
and the fact that
\omega\in A_{2}
, we get

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C\bigg[\big\|f\big\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}2^{-\ell\lambda n}2^{2\ell n}\big\|f\big\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\bigg],

and hence, when
\lambda>2
, there exists a positive constant
C>0
, independent of
f
, such that

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})\big\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

We are done.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.14
.

Let
{f}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
,
\omega\in A_{1}
and
\lambda>3+{(2\delta+2\gamma)}/n
. Let
B(x_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the open ball of radius
r\in(0,\infty)
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Similar to the proofs of Theorems
3.12
and
3.13
, by the definition of
\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
, we are going to prove that for any given ball
\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
, the following inequality holds:

\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.

(6.1)

In order to prove (
6.1
), we decompose the integral defining
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})
into two parts.

\begin{split}\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})(x)\big]&=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|x-z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|x-z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&+\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|x-z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\big|\psi_{t}*f(z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&:=\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(x)\big].\end{split}

It is easy to see that

\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f})(x)\big],\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(x)\big]\leq\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})(x)\big]\leq\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(x)\big].

Consequently, in view of (
5.1
), we can deduce that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\Big[\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f})(x)\big]+\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f})(x)\big]dx+\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Big|\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\,dx\\
&:=\mathcal{K}_{0}+\mathcal{K}_{\infty}.\end{split}

As before, to control the first term
\mathcal{K}_{0}
, we use the decomposition

\begin{split}f(x)&=f_{4\mathcal{B}}+[f(x)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}]\cdot\chi_{4\mathcal{B}}(x)+[f(x)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}]\cdot\chi_{(4\mathcal{B})^{\complement}}(x)\\
&:=f^{1}(x)+f^{2}(x)+f^{3}(x).\end{split}

By the vanishing condition of the kernel
\mathcal{\psi}
, we have that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
,

\psi_{t}*f(x)=\psi_{t}*f^{2}(x)+\psi_{t}*f^{3}(x)\quad\&\quad\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f}^{1})(x)\big]\equiv 0.

Then we can write

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{0}&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f})(x)\big]dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f}^{2})(x)\big]dx+\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f}^{3})(x)\big]dx\\
&:=\mathcal{K}^{2}_{0}+\mathcal{K}^{3}_{0}.\end{split}

Applying Theorem
6.18
,Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality and Lemma
4.16
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}^{2}_{0}&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f}^{2})(x)\big]^{2}\omega(x)^{-1}dx\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}\big\|\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f}^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{-1})}\\
&\leq C\cdot\frac{\omega(4\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}{\omega(\mathcal{B})^{1/2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(4\mathcal{B})}\int_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|^{2}\omega(x)^{-1}dx\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.\end{split}

Let us now estimate the other term
\mathcal{K}^{3}_{0}
. First, by the size condition of the kernel
\psi
, we can see that for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<t<r
,

\begin{split}&\big|\psi_{t}*f^{3}(z)\big|=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 4\mathcal{B}}\psi_{t}(z-\xi)\big[f(\xi)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big]\,d\xi\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 4\mathcal{B}}\frac{t^{\delta}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{n+\delta}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi.\\
\end{split}

Next, by Minkowski’s inequality for integrals,

\begin{split}\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,0}({f}^{3})(x)\big]&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 4\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&\times\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|x-z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}.\end{split}

We now claim that

\mathrm{I}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{t}{t+|x-z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz\lesssim\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}.

(6.2)

In fact, we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}&=\int_{|x-z|<t}\left(\frac{t}{t+|x-z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz\\
&+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|x-z|<2^{\ell}t}\left(\frac{t}{t+|x-z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz\\
&\leq\int_{|x-z|<t}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz\\
&+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|x-z|<2^{\ell}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz.\end{split}

We do our estimates according to different cases.

1.

When
|x-z|<t
with
0<t<r
,
x\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(4\mathcal{B})
, one has

|z-x_{0}|\leq|z-x|+|x-x_{0}|<2r\leq\frac{|x_{0}-\xi|}{2},

and

|z-\xi|\geq|x_{0}-\xi|-|z-x_{0}|>\frac{|x_{0}-\xi|}{2}.

Hence,

\begin{split}\int_{|x-z|<t}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz&\leq\int_{|x-z|<t}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz\\
&\lesssim\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}},\end{split}

as desired.

2.

When
|x-z|<2^{\ell}t
with
0<t<r
and
\ell\geq 1
,
x\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(2^{\ell+2}\mathcal{B})
, in this case, one has

|z-x_{0}|\leq|z-x|+|x-x_{0}|<2^{\ell}r+r\leq 2^{\ell+1}r\leq\frac{|x_{0}-\xi|}{2},

and

|z-\xi|\geq|x_{0}-\xi|-|z-x_{0}|>\frac{|x_{0}-\xi|}{2}.

Hence, for any fixed
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}&\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|x-z|<2^{\ell}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz\\
&\leq\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|x-z|<2^{\ell}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz\\
&\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{(\lambda-1)n}\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}.\end{split}

3.

When
|x-z|<2^{\ell}t
with
0<t<r
and
\ell\geq 1
,
x\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{(}2^{\ell+2}\mathcal{B})\setminus(4\mathcal{B})
, in this case, one has

|\xi-x_{0}|\geq 4r\Longrightarrow r\leq\frac{|\xi-x_{0}|}{4},

and

\begin{split}|x_{0}-\xi|&\leq|x_{0}-x|+|x-z|+|z-\xi|\\
&<r+2^{\ell}t+|z-\xi|<\frac{|x_{0}-\xi|}{4}+2^{\ell}\big(t+|z-\xi|\big),\end{split}

which in turn implies that

|x_{0}-\xi|<\frac{4}{\,3\,}\cdot 2^{\ell}\big(t+|z-\xi|\big).

Thus, for any fixed
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that

\begin{split}&\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|x-z|<2^{\ell}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz\\
&\lesssim\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|x-z|<2^{\ell}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n}\big(2^{\ell}\big)^{2(n+\delta)}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}dz\\
&\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n-3n-2\delta}\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}.\end{split}

Summing up the estimates derived above, we conclude that

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}&\lesssim\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{(\lambda-1)n}+\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n-3n-2\delta}\Big]\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}}\\
&\lesssim\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta)}},\end{split}

where in the last step we have used the fact that
\lambda>3+{(2\delta)}/n>1
. This proves (
6.2
). From this estimate and part (2) of Lemma
4.17
, it follows that

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}^{3}_{0}&\lesssim\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 4\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{|x_{0}-\xi|^{n+\delta}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}t^{2\delta-1}dt\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}\setminus 2^{j}\mathcal{B}}\frac{r^{\delta}}{|x_{0}-\xi|^{n+\delta}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&\lesssim\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}}\frac{r^{\delta}}{m(2^{j+1}\mathcal{B})^{1+\delta/n}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{4\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&\lesssim r^{\delta}\bigg\{\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{j}{(2^{j})^{\delta}r^{\delta}}\bigg\}\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\bigg\{\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\bigg\}\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)},\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from (
5.2
). On the other hand, by (
5.9
), (
5.10
) and the vanishing condition of the kernel
\psi
, we can see that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
and
t>r
,

\begin{split}&\Big|\big|\psi_{t}*f(x+z)\big|-\big|\psi_{t}*f(y+z)\big|\Big|\leq\Big|\psi_{t}*f(x+z)-\psi_{t}*f(y+z)\Big|\\
&=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big[\psi_{t}(x+z-\xi)-\psi_{t}(y+z-\xi)\big]\cdot\big[f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big]\,d\xi\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big[\big|\psi_{t}(x+z-\xi)\big|+\big|\psi_{t}(y+z-\xi)\big|\big]\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\mathcal{B}}\frac{t^{\delta}\cdot|x-y|^{\gamma}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{n+\delta+\gamma}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi.\end{split}

(6.3)

Consequently, by (
6.3
) and the Minkowski inequality for integrals, we obtain that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
,

\begin{split}&\Big|\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\\
&=\bigg|\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\big|\psi_{t}*f(x+z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&-\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\big|\psi_{t}*f(y+z)\big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg|\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\Big|\big|\psi_{t}*f(x+z)\big|-\big|\psi_{t}*f(y+z)\big|\Big|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\bigg\{\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big[\big|\psi_{t}(x+z-\xi)\big|+\big|\psi_{t}(y+z-\xi)\big|\big]\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\}^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&+\bigg(\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\Big)^{\lambda n}\bigg\{t^{\delta}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\mathcal{B}}\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{n+\delta+\gamma}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\}^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2}.\end{split}

(6.4)

In view of (
5.7
), we have that for any
t>0
,

\big|\psi_{t}(x+z-\xi)\big|+\big|\psi_{t}(y+z-\xi)\big|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}.

Moreover,

\begin{split}&\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&=\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\left(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt+\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|\geq t}\left(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&\leq\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt+\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|\geq t}\left(\frac{t}{|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt\bigg\}^{1/2}.\end{split}

Using polar coordinates and the assumption
\lambda>1
, we get

\begin{split}\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|\geq t}\left(\frac{t}{|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt&\lesssim\int_{r}^{\infty}\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\varrho^{n-1}}{\varrho^{\lambda n}}\,d\varrho\bigg)\frac{t^{\lambda n}}{t^{3n+1}}\,dt\\
&\lesssim\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2n+1}}\,dt\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2n}}.\end{split}

In addition,

\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{|z|<t}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt\lesssim\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2n+1}}\,dt\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2n}}.

Thus it follows that

\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt\bigg\}^{1/2}\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{n}}.

Hence, by the Minkowski inequality for integrals, we can see that the first term in (
6.4
) is bounded by

\begin{split}&\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{1}{t^{3n+1}}\,dzdt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{m(8\mathcal{B})}\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi.\end{split}

We now claim that

\mathrm{II}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}dz\lesssim\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}.

(6.5)

In fact, we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{II}&=\int_{|z|<t}\left(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}dz\\
&+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|z|<2^{\ell}t}\left(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}dz\\
&\leq\int_{|z|<t}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}dz\\
&+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|z|<2^{\ell}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}dz.\end{split}

1.

When
|z|<t
with
t\geq r
,
x\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(8\mathcal{B})
, one has

|x_{0}-x-z|\leq|x_{0}-x|+|z|<r+t\leq 2t,

and

t+|x_{0}-\xi|\leq t+|x_{0}-x-z|+|x+z-\xi|\leq 3(t+|x+z-\xi|).

Hence,

\begin{split}\int_{|z|<t}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}dz&\lesssim\int_{|z|<t}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}dz\\
&\lesssim\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}},\end{split}

as desired.

2.

When
|z|<2^{\ell}t
with
t\geq r
and
\ell\geq 1
,
x\in\mathcal{B}=B(x_{0},r)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(8\mathcal{B})
, in this case, one has

|x_{0}-x-z|\leq|x_{0}-x|+|z|<r+2^{\ell}t\leq t+2^{\ell}t,

and

\begin{split}t+|x_{0}-\xi|&\leq t+|x_{0}-x-z|+|x+z-\xi|\\
&<2t+2^{\ell}t+|x+z-\xi|\leq 2^{\ell+1}\big(t+|x+z-\xi|\big).\end{split}

Thus, for any fixed
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that

\begin{split}&\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|z|<2^{\ell}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}dz\\
&\lesssim\int_{2^{\ell-1}t\leq|x-z|<2^{\ell}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n}\big(2^{\ell}\big)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}\frac{t^{2\delta-n}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}dz\\
&\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n-3n-2\delta-2\gamma}\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}.\end{split}

Summing up the estimates derived above, we conclude that

\begin{split}\mathrm{II}&\lesssim\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\Big)^{\lambda n-3n-2\delta-2\gamma}\Big]\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\\
&\lesssim\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used the assumption
\lambda>3+{(2\delta+2\gamma)}/n
. This proves (
6.5
). Furthermore, by the Minkowski inequality for integrals, we can see that the second term in (
6.4
) is dominated by

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\mathcal{B}}(2r)^{\gamma}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{t}{t+|z|}\right)^{\lambda n}\frac{t^{2\delta}}{(t+|x+z-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\mathcal{B}}(2r)^{\gamma}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\{\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2\delta-1}}{(t+|x_{0}-\xi|)^{2(n+\delta+\gamma)}}\,dt\bigg\}^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\mathcal{B}}\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{n+\gamma}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi.\end{split}

From part (1) and part (2) of Lemma
4.17
, it then follows that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{B}
,

\begin{split}&\Big|\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{m(8\mathcal{B})}\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&+\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}\setminus 2^{j}\mathcal{B}}\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(|x_{0}-\xi|)^{n+\gamma}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{m(8\mathcal{B})}\int_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi+\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{(2r)^{\gamma}}{(2^{j}r)^{\gamma}}\cdot\frac{1}{m(2^{j+1}\mathcal{B})}\int_{2^{j+1}\mathcal{B}}\big|f(\xi)-f_{8\mathcal{B}}\big|\,d\xi\bigg\}\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{\xi\in 8\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(\xi)+\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\frac{j}{2^{(j-1)\gamma}}\times\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{\xi\in 8\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(\xi)\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\underset{x\in 8\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x).\end{split}

Therefore, by inequality (
5.2
) again, we conclude that

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{\infty}&=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\underset{y\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Big|\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(x)\big]-\big[\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda,\infty}({f})(y)\big]\Big|\,dx\\
&\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\bigg\{\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\underset{x\in 8\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\bigg\}\\
&\leq\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\bigg\{\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\cdot\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\bigg\}\leq\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}.\end{split}

Combining the above estimates for both terms
\mathcal{K}_{0}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
leads to the desired inequality (
6.1
). This concludes the proof of Theorem
3.14
.
∎

We now introduce the function space
L^{\infty}(\omega)
(the weighted version of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
), which denotes the space of all essentially bounded measurable functions with respect to
\omega
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. More precisely, we say that a measurable function
f
belongs to the weighted space
L^{\infty}(\omega)
, if there is a constant
C>0
such that for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\bigg(\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\bigg)^{-1}\Big(\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})}\Big)\leq C<\infty,

where

\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})}:=\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mbox{ess\,sup}}\,|f(x)|=\inf\Big\{M>0:m\big(\big\{x\in\mathcal{B}:|f(x)|>M\big\}\big)=0\Big\}.

We define

\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}:=\sup_{\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\underset{x\in\mathcal{B}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\bigg)^{-1}\cdot\Big(\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})}\Big).

In particular, when
\omega(x)=1
, it is easy to see that

\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}=\sup_{\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})}=\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mbox{ess\,sup}}\,|f(x)|=\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}}.

For any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{B}}\big|\,dx&\leq\frac{2}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\int_{\mathcal{B}}|f(x)|\,dx\\
&\leq 2\frac{m(\mathcal{B})}{\omega(\mathcal{B})}\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})}\\
&\leq 2\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)},\end{split}

and hence

L^{\infty}(\omega)\subset\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)\quad\&\quad\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\leq 2\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}.

By using similar arguments, the following results can be also derived from Theorems
3.10
,
3.11
and
6.18
, which are the weighted version of Leckband’s result in
[
18
]
.

Theorem 6.19
.

For any
{f}\in L^{\infty}(\omega)
and
\omega\in A_{1}
, then
\mathcal{G}({f})
(or
\mathcal{S}({f})
) is finite everywhere, and there exists a positive constant
C>0
, independent of
f
, such that

\big\|\mathcal{G}({f})\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\big\|\mathcal{S}({f})\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}.

Theorem 6.20
.

Suppose that
(\lambda-3)n>2\delta+2\gamma
. For any
{f}\in L^{\infty}(\omega)
and
\omega\in A_{1}
, then
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})
is finite everywhere, and there exists a positive constant
C>0
, independent of
f
, such that

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\lambda}({f})\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}.

7
John–Nirenberg-type inequalities for weighted BLO and BMO spaces

In this section, we are concerned with the John–Nirenberg-type inequality with precise constants suitable for the
\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
spaces and relevant properties. Before we proceed to prove our next theorems, we need the following two auxiliary lemmas, which can be found in
[
5
,
8
]
and
[
19
]
. Here we give the proofs for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 7.21
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
\omega
be a weight function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for any measurable subset
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(with finite or infinite Lebesgue measure), we have the following integration formula for
f\in L^{p}(\omega)
(the layer cake representation).

\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p}\omega(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\omega\big(\big\{x\in E:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)\,d\lambda.

(7.1)

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a weight function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, we can deduce that

\begin{split}\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p}\omega(x)\,dx&=\int_{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{|f(x)|}p\lambda^{p-1}\,d\lambda\bigg)\omega(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\chi_{[0,|f(x)|)}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\bigg)\omega(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Changing the order of integration (the Fubini theorem) gives us that

\begin{split}\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p}\omega(x)\,dx&=\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\bigg(\int_{E}\chi_{[0,|f(x)|)}(\lambda)\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)d\lambda\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\bigg(\int_{\{x\in E:|f(x)|>\lambda\}}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)d\lambda\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\omega\big(\big\{x\in E:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)\,d\lambda.\end{split}

This proves (
7.1
). Recall that for a measurable function
f
defined on
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the function
d_{f}(\cdot;\omega)
defined on
[0,\infty)
is called the
\omega
-distribution function of
f
, where

d_{f}(\lambda;\omega):=\omega\big(\big\{x\in E:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big).

By the formula (
7.1
), we can evaluate the
L^{p}
-norm (with respect to
\omega
) of a function
f
in terms of the
\omega
-distribution function.
∎

Lemma 7.22
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
\nu\in A_{p}
. Then for any given cube
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a positive number
\delta>0
such that

\frac{\nu(E)}{\nu(\mathcal{Q})}\leq C^{*}\left(\frac{m(E)}{m(\mathcal{Q})}\right)^{\delta}

(7.2)

holds for any measurable subset
E
of
\mathcal{Q}
, where
C^{*}>0
is a (universal) constant which does not depend on
E
and
\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

Since
\nu\in A_{p}
with
1\leq p<\infty
, we know that there exist constants
C
and
\varepsilon>0
, depending only on
p
and the
A_{p}
constant of
\nu
, such that for any cube
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following reverse Hölder inequality holds (see
[
5
]
and
[
9
]
).

\bigg(\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\nu(x)^{1+\varepsilon}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}}\leq C^{*}\bigg(\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\nu(x)\,dx\bigg).

(7.3)

Actually, there is a sharp version of (
7.3
). More precisely, by using the sharp reverse Hölder inequality for
A_{p}
weights obtained recently in
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
, we have that

C^{*}:=2\quad\&\quad\varepsilon:=\frac{1}{2^{2p+1+n}[\nu]_{A_{p}}}.

This result is due to Pérez (see also
[
1
, Lemma 3.26]
). Suppose that
E\subseteq Q
. By Hölder’s inequality with exponent
1+\varepsilon
and (
7.3
), we can deduce that

\begin{split}\nu(E)&=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\chi_{E}(x)\cdot\nu(x)\,dx\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\chi_{E}(x)^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\nu(x)^{1+\varepsilon}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}}\\
&\leq 2m(E)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}m(\mathcal{Q})^{\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}}\bigg(\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\nu(x)\,dx\bigg)\\
&=2\left(\frac{m(E)}{m(\mathcal{Q})}\right)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}\nu(\mathcal{Q}).\end{split}

This gives (
7.2
) with
\delta=\varepsilon/{(1+\varepsilon)}
and
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2^{2p+1+n}[\nu]_{A_{p}}}
.
∎

Remark 7.23
.

It was shown in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
that one can get a sharper reverse Hölder exponent by using the Fujii–Wilson
A_{\infty}
-constant
[\omega]_{A_{\infty}}
, where
[\omega]_{A_{\infty}}
is defined as (
M
stands for the standard Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator)

[\omega]_{A_{\infty}}:=\sup_{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{\omega(Q)}\int_{Q}M(\omega\chi_{Q})(x)\,dx.

By using the results from
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
, the estimate (
7.2
) of Lemma
7.22
can be further improved. Here we omit the details.

Lemma 7.24
.

If
f\in\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
with
\omega\in A_{1}
, then there exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that for every cube
\mathcal{Q}=Q(x_{0},r)
and every
\lambda>0
,

\begin{split}&m\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]>\lambda\Big\}\Big)\\
&\leq{C}_{1}\cdot m(\mathcal{Q})\exp\bigg\{-\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]^{-1}\frac{{C}_{2}\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}}\bigg\}.\end{split}

(7.4)

More specifically, we may choose

{C}_{1}=e\quad and\quad{C}_{2}=\frac{1}{2^{n}e}.

Proof of Lemma
7.24
.

Some ideas of the proof of this lemma come from
[
5
]
and
[
9
]
. The proof has five main steps.

Step 1
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}=1
with
\omega\in A_{1}
. Note that if
\lambda\leq[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)
, then the inequality (
7.4
) holds true by choosing
{C}_{1}=e
and
{C}_{2}=1
. Now we suppose that
\lambda>[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)
. Then for each fixed cube
\mathcal{Q}=Q(x_{0},r)
, we can apply the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition to the function
f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f(y)
inside the cube
\mathcal{Q}
. Let
\sigma>1
be a positive constant to be fixed below.
Since

\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\bigg)\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}=1<\sigma,

from the condition
\omega\in A_{1}
, it then follows that

\begin{split}&\bigg(\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\bigg)\\
&=\frac{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}{m(\mathcal{Q})}\cdot\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\bigg)<[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\cdot\sigma.\end{split}

We set
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}=[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)
and then follow the same argument (the so-called stopping time argument) as in the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 7.1.6]
to obtain a collection of (pairwise disjoint) cubes
\big\{Q^{(1)}_{j}\big\}_{j}
satisfying the following properties:

\begin{split}&(A)\mbox{-1}.~~\mbox{The interior of every cube}~Q^{(1)}_{j}~\mbox{is contained in}~\mathcal{Q};\\
&(B)\mbox{-1}.~~\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot\sigma<\frac{1}{m(Q^{(1)}_{j})}\int_{Q^{(1)}_{j}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot 2^{n}\sigma;\\
&(C)\mbox{-1}.~~0\leq\underset{y\in{Q^{(1)}_{j}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot 2^{n}\sigma;\\
&(D)\mbox{-1}.~~\sum_{j}m\big(Q^{(1)}_{j}\big)\leq\frac{m(\mathcal{Q})}{\sigma};\\
&(E)\mbox{-1}.~~f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot\sigma,~~a.e.~x\in\mathcal{Q}\setminus\bigcup_{j}Q^{(1)}_{j}.\end{split}

We prove these properties
(A)
-1 through
(E)
-1. Obviously, properties
(A)
-1 and
(B)
-1 hold by the selection criterion of the cubes
Q^{(1)}_{j}
(can be viewed as the first generation of
\mathcal{Q}
). Since
Q^{(1)}_{j}\subset\mathcal{Q}
and

\begin{split}\underset{y\in{Q^{(1)}_{j}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)&=\frac{1}{m(Q^{(1)}_{j})}\int_{Q^{(1)}_{j}}\underset{y\in{Q^{(1)}_{j}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(Q^{(1)}_{j})}\int_{Q^{(1)}_{j}}f(x)\,dx=f_{Q^{(1)}_{j}},\end{split}

we get

\begin{split}0&\leq\underset{y\in{Q^{(1)}_{j}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(Q^{(1)}_{j})}\int_{Q^{(1)}_{j}}f(x)\,dx-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\\
&=\frac{1}{m(Q^{(1)}_{j})}\int_{Q^{(1)}_{j}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot 2^{n}\sigma,\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used
(B)
-1. Because the cubes
Q^{(1)}_{j}
are pairwise disjoint, then it follows from
(B)
-1 that

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot\sum_{j}m\big(Q^{(1)}_{j}\big)&<\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{j}\int_{Q^{(1)}_{j}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\bigcup_{j}Q^{(1)}_{j}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{m(\mathcal{Q})}{\sigma}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\omega}.\end{split}

This is equivalent to
(D)
-1.
(E)
-1 is a consequence of the Lebesgue differentiation theorem.

Step 2
. We now fix a selected cube
Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}
(first generation) and apply the same Calderón–Zygmund decomposition to the function
f(x)-\underset{y\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f(y)
inside the cube
Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}
. Also repeat this process for any other cube of the first generation. Let
Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}=Q(x_{1},r_{1})
be the cube centered at
x_{1}
and with side length
r_{1}
. Observe that

\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}})}\int_{Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\bigg)\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}=1<\sigma.

Arguing as in Step 1, we obtain a collection of (pairwise disjoint) cubes
\big\{Q^{(2)}_{j}\big\}_{j}
satisfying the following properties:

\begin{split}&(A)\mbox{-2}.~~\mbox{The interior of every cube}~Q^{(2)}_{j}~\mbox{is contained in a unique cube}~{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}};\\
&(B)\mbox{-2}.~~\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot\sigma<\frac{1}{m(Q^{(2)}_{j})}\int_{Q^{(2)}_{j}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot 2^{n}\sigma;\\
&(C)\mbox{-2}.~~0\leq\underset{y\in{Q}^{(2)}_{j}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)-\underset{y\in{Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot 2^{n}\sigma;\\
&(D)\mbox{-2}.~~\sum_{j}m\big(Q^{(2)}_{j}\big)\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{j^{\prime}}m\big(Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}\big);\\
&(E)\mbox{-2}.~~f(x)-\underset{y\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot\sigma,~~a.e.~x\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}\setminus\bigcup_{j}Q^{(2)}_{j}.\end{split}

In fact, it is clear that properties
(A)
-2 and
(B)
-2 hold by the selection criterion of the cubes
Q^{(2)}_{j}
(can be viewed as the second generation of
\mathcal{Q}
). Since
Q^{(2)}_{j}\subset Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}
and

\begin{split}\underset{y\in{Q^{(2)}_{j}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)&=\frac{1}{m(Q^{(2)}_{j})}\int_{Q^{(2)}_{j}}\underset{y\in{Q^{(2)}_{j}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\,dx\leq\frac{1}{m(Q^{(2)}_{j})}\int_{Q^{(2)}_{j}}f(x)\,dx,\end{split}

so we have

\begin{split}0\leq\underset{y\in{Q}^{(2)}_{j}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)-\underset{y\in{Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)&\leq\frac{1}{m(Q^{(2)}_{j})}\int_{Q^{(2)}_{j}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot 2^{n}\sigma,\end{split}

due to property
(B)
-2. By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem,
(E)
-2 holds. It remains only to study the last property
(D)
-2. Notice that the cubes
Q^{(2)}_{j}
are also pairwise disjoint and each selected cube
Q^{(2)}_{j}
is contained in a unique cube
Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}
, we can deduce that

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot\sum_{j}\big|Q^{(2)}_{j}\big|&<\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{j}\int_{Q^{(2)}_{j}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{j^{\prime}}\int_{Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{j^{\prime}}m\big(Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}\big)\cdot\frac{\omega(Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}})}{m(Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}})}\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{j^{\prime}}m\big(Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}\big)\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\omega}.\end{split}

This is just the desired estimate. Summarizing the estimates derived above(
(E)
-2 and
(C)
-1), we can deduce that for almost every
x\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}\setminus\bigcup_{j}Q^{(2)}_{j}
,

\begin{split}f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)&=f(x)-\underset{y\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)+\underset{y\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\\
&\leq\sigma\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\omega}+2^{n}\sigma\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\omega},~~a.e.~x\in{Q}^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}\setminus\bigcup_{j}Q^{(2)}_{j}.\end{split}

This estimate, together with
(E)
-1, implies that for almost every
x\in{\mathcal{Q}}\setminus\bigcup_{j}Q^{(2)}_{j}
,

\begin{split}f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)&\leq\sigma\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\omega}+2^{n}\sigma\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\\
&\leq 2\sigma\cdot 2^{n}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}.\end{split}

Moreover, from
(D)
-1 and
(D)
-2, we conclude that

\sum_{j}m\big(Q^{(2)}_{j}\big)\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{j^{\prime}}m\big(Q^{(1)}_{j^{\prime}}\big)\leq\frac{m(\mathcal{Q})}{\sigma^{2}}.

Step 3
. We repeat this process indefinitely to obtain a collection of cubes
\big\{Q^{(k)}_{j}\big\}_{j}
satisfying the following properties:

\begin{split}&(A)\mbox{-k}.~~\mbox{The interior of every cube}~Q^{(k)}_{j}~\mbox{is contained in a unique cube}~{Q}^{(k-1)}_{j^{\prime}};\\
&(B)\mbox{-k}.~~\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot\sigma<\frac{1}{m(Q^{(k)}_{j})}\int_{Q^{(k)}_{j}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in{Q}^{(k-1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot 2^{n}\sigma;\\
&(C)\mbox{-k}.~~0\leq\underset{y\in{Q}^{(k)}_{j}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)-\underset{y\in{Q^{(k-1)}_{j^{\prime}}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot 2^{n}\sigma;\\
&(D)\mbox{-k}.~~\sum_{j}m\big(Q^{(k)}_{j}\big)\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{j^{\prime}}m\big(Q^{(k-1)}_{j^{\prime}}\big);\\
&(E)\mbox{-k}.~~f(x)-\underset{y\in{Q}^{(k-1)}_{j^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\leq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot\sigma,~~a.e.~x\in{Q}^{(k-1)}_{j^{\prime}}\setminus\bigcup_{j}Q^{(k)}_{j}.\end{split}

Here
{Q}^{(k-1)}_{j^{\prime}}
denotes the cube centered at
x_{k-1}
with side length
r_{k-1}
. By induction, from the previous proof, it actually follows that

f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\leq k\sigma\cdot 2^{n}\mathcal{A}_{\omega},~~a.e.~x\in\mathcal{Q}\setminus\bigcup_{\ell}Q^{(k)}_{\ell},

and

\sum_{\ell}m\big(Q^{(k)}_{\ell}\big)\leq\frac{m(\mathcal{Q})}{\sigma^{k}},\quad k=1,2,3,\dots.

(7.5)

Therefore

\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]>k\sigma\cdot 2^{n}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\Big\}\subseteq\bigcup_{\ell}Q^{(k)}_{\ell},k=1,2,3,\dots.

(7.6)

Step 4
. Since

(0,+\infty)=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(k\sigma 2^{n}\mathcal{A}_{\omega},(k+1)\sigma 2^{n}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\bigg],

then for each fixed
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
, we can write

k\sigma 2^{n}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}<\lambda\leq(k+1)\sigma 2^{n}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}

for some
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and hence

\begin{split}&m\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]>\lambda\Big\}\Big)\\
&\leq m\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]>k\sigma 2^{n}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\Big\}\Big)\\
&\leq\sum_{\ell}m\big(Q_{\ell}^{(k)}\big)\leq\frac{m(\mathcal{Q})}{\sigma^{k}}\\
&=\sigma\cdot m(\mathcal{Q})\cdot\frac{\exp\{-k\log\sigma\}}{\sigma},\end{split}

where in the last two inequalities we have used (
7.5
) and (
7.6
), respectively. Now choose
\sigma=e>1
, we then have

\begin{split}&m\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]>\lambda\Big\}\Big)\\
&\leq e\cdot m(\mathcal{Q})\exp\big\{-(k+1)\big\}\\
&\leq e\cdot m(\mathcal{Q})\exp\Big\{-\frac{\lambda}{\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\cdot 2^{n}e}\Big\}.\end{split}

This concludes the proof of Lemma
7.24
for the special case that
f\in\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
with
\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}=1
.

Step 5
. We now proceed to the general case. In order to do so, we set

\widetilde{f}(x):=\frac{f(x)}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}}.

By the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}
, we have

\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}=1\quad\&\quad\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)=\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}\cdot\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\widetilde{f}(y).

Hence,

\begin{split}&m\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]>\lambda\Big\}\Big)\\
&=m\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[\widetilde{f}(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\widetilde{f}(y)\Big]>\frac{\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}}\Big\}\Big)\\
&\leq{C}_{1}m(\mathcal{Q})\exp\bigg\{-\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]^{-1}\frac{{C}_{2}\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}}\bigg\},\end{split}

with precise constants

{C}_{1}=e\quad\&\quad{C}_{2}=\frac{1}{2^{n}e}.

We are done.
∎

Based on Lemma
7.24
, we can prove the following result.

Theorem 7.25
.

For any
1\leq p<\infty
and
\omega\in A_{1}
, define

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)}:=\sup_{\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]^{p}\omega(x)^{1-p}dx\bigg)^{1/p}.

When
p=1
, we simply write
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}
. Then we have

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)}\approx\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)},

for each
1<p<\infty
. To be more precise,

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}\leq\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)}\leq[\omega]_{A_{1}}\Big(C^{*}\cdot p\Gamma(p)\Big)^{1/p}\cdot\frac{{C}_{1}^{\delta/p}}{{C}_{2}\delta}\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)},

where
C^{*}
and
\delta
are the constants appearing in Lemma
7.22
, and
\Gamma(\cdot)
denotes the usual gamma function, which is given by

\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu^{x-1}e^{-\mu}\,d\mu.

Now we define

\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega):=\Big\{f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)}<+\infty\Big\},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

This result tells us that for all
1\leq p<\infty
, the spaces
\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)
coincide, and the “norms"
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)}
are mutually equivalent with respect to different values of
p
, provided that
\omega\in A_{1}
.

Proof of Theorem
7.25
.

(1) Suppose that
f\in\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)
with
1<p<\infty
. By using Hölder’s inequality and the fact that
1-p=-{p}/{p^{\prime}}
, we obtain that for any cube
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]\omega(x)^{-1/{p^{\prime}}}\cdot\omega(x)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]^{p}\omega(x)^{-{p}/{p^{\prime}}}dx\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]^{p}\omega(x)^{1-p}\,dx\bigg)^{1/{p}}\leq\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}^{p}(\omega)}.

(2) Let
f\in\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)
with
\omega\in A_{1}
. According to Lemma
7.24
, there are two constants
{C}_{1},{C}_{2}>0
such that for any
\lambda>0
and for any cube
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}&m\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]>\lambda\Big\}\Big)\\
&\leq{C}_{1}m(\mathcal{Q})\exp\bigg\{-\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]^{-1}\frac{{C}_{2}\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}}\bigg\}.\end{split}

By the property of
A_{p}
weights, we know that for
1<p<\infty
,

\omega\in A_{p^{\prime}}\Longleftrightarrow\omega^{1-p}\in A_{p}.

If we set
\nu(x):=\omega(x)^{1-p}
with
1<p<\infty
, then
\nu\in A_{p}
since
\omega\in A_{1}\subset A_{p^{\prime}}
for
1<p<\infty
. By using Lemma
7.22
, we get

\begin{split}&\nu\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]>\lambda\Big\}\Big)\\
&\leq C^{*}\cdot{C}_{1}^{\delta}\nu(\mathcal{Q})\exp\bigg\{-\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]^{-1}\frac{{C}_{2}\delta\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}}\bigg\}.\end{split}

This estimate, together with Lemma
7.21
, gives us that for any
1<p<\infty

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]^{p}\nu(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\nu\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]>\lambda\Big\}\Big)d\lambda\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(C^{*}\cdot{C}_{1}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\exp\bigg\{-\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]^{-1}\frac{{C}_{2}\delta\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}}\bigg\}d\lambda\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{\nu(\mathcal{Q})}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\right)^{1/p}.

Observe that

\nu(\mathcal{Q})^{1/p}=\bigg\{\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{\omega(x)}{\omega(x)^{p}}dx\bigg\}^{1/p}\leq\omega(\mathcal{Q})^{1/p}\cdot\Big[\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]^{-1}.

This implies that

\left(\frac{\nu(\mathcal{Q})}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\right)^{1/p}\leq\Big[\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]^{-1}.

Moreover, by making the substitution

\mu:=\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]^{-1}\frac{{C}_{2}\delta\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}},

we can deduce that for any cube
\mathcal{Q}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Big[f(x)-\underset{y\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,f(y)\Big]^{p}\nu(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(C^{*}\cdot{C}_{1}^{\delta}p\Big)^{1/p}\Big[[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\Big]\frac{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}}{{C}_{2}\delta}\times\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu^{p-1}e^{-\mu}\,d\mu\bigg)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\nu(\mathcal{Q})}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq[\omega]_{A_{1}}\Big(C^{*}\cdot p\Gamma(p)\Big)^{1/p}\cdot\frac{{C}_{1}^{\delta/p}}{{C}_{2}\delta}\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\omega)}.

This gives the desired inequality. Collecting the above estimates, we conclude the proof of Theorem
7.25
.
∎

Let
f\in\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)
and
\omega\in A_{1}
. Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
7.24
, we can also prove that for every cube
\mathcal{Q}=Q(x_{0},r)
and every
\lambda>0
,

\begin{split}&m\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathcal{Q}:\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{Q}}\big|>\lambda\Big\}\Big)\\
&\leq e\cdot m(\mathcal{Q})\exp\bigg\{-\mathcal{A}_{\omega}^{-1}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{2^{n}e\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}}\bigg\},\end{split}

(7.7)

where

\mathcal{A}_{\omega}:=[\omega]_{A_{1}}\underset{x\in\mathcal{Q}}{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x).

Based on this, then we get

Theorem 7.26
.

For any
1\leq p<\infty
and
\omega\in A_{1}
, define

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{p}(\omega)}:=\sup_{\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{Q}}\big|^{p}\omega(x)^{1-p}\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}.

When
p=1
, we simply write
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{p}(\omega)}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}
. Then we have

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{p}(\omega)}\approx\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)},

for each
1<p<\infty
.

As in the proof of Theorem
7.25
, we can also show that

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}\leq\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{p}(\omega)}\leq[\omega]_{A_{1}}\Big(C^{*}\cdot p\Gamma(p)\Big)^{1/p}\cdot\frac{{C}_{1}^{\delta/p}}{{C}_{2}\delta}\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)},

where
C^{*}
and

\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{Q}}\big|\,dx\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{Q}}\big|^{p}\omega(x)^{1-p}dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{Q})}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\big|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{Q}}\big|^{p}\omega(x)^{1-p}dx\bigg)^{1/p}\leq[\omega]_{A_{1}}\Big(C^{*}\cdot p\Gamma(p)\Big)^{1/p}\cdot\frac{{C}_{1}^{\delta/p}}{{C}_{2}\delta}\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\omega)}

by the inequality (
7.7
), Lemmas
7.21
and
7.22
. Hence, the desired result follows.

Similarly, we define

\mathrm{BMO}^{p}(\omega):=\Big\{f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\big\|f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{p}(\omega)}<+\infty\Big\},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

Theorem 7.6 tells us that for all

.

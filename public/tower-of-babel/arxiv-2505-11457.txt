Noise sensitivity of crossings for high temperature Ising model
Vincent Tassion, Hugo Vanneuville

Abstract
Consider the event that there is a + + crossing from left to right in a box for the Ising model on the triangular lattice. We show that this event is noise sensitive under Glauber dynamics t ↦ σ t t\mapsto\sigma_{t} in the subcritical regime β < β c \beta<\beta_{c} . We rely on the non-spectral approach from our previous work [ TV23 ] . An important aspect in this more general setup is the study of the pair ( σ 0 , σ t ) (\sigma_{0},\sigma_{t}) and in particular the establishment of properties such as finite-energy and spatial mixing.

Introduction

1.1
Noise sensitivity

The concept of noise sensitivity was introduced by Benjamini, Kalai and Schramm in
[
BKS99
]
for critical planar Bernoulli percolation. In this model, each site of the triangular lattice is first colored black or white independently with probability
1/2
. Then, the resulting configuration evolves in time by resampling each site at rate
1
.
They considered the event that a large rectangle is crossed from left to right and proved that this event is noise sensitive: for every fixed
t>0
(even very small), its occurrence at time
0
and time
t
are asymptotically independent. A refinement of the result was later obtained by Schramm and Steif
[
SS10
]
using randomized algorithms. Then, Garban, Pete and Schramm
[
GPS10
]
obtained a precise description of the phenomenon by identifying the amount of noise implying asymptotic independence.

Noise sensitivity has also been proven for other planar critical percolation models: Bernoulli percolation under exclusion dynamics
[
BGS13
,
GV19
]
, dynamical Bernoulli percolation in a Liouville environment
[
GHSS21
]
, Poisson Boolean percolation
[
ABGM14
]
, Voronoi percolation
[
AGMT16
,
AB18
,
Van21
]
and percolation of nodal lines
[
GV20
]
. We refer to the book
[
GS14
]
for more about noise sensitivity and its motivations.
All of these results rely on a study of the dynamics via Fourier methods. In
[
TV23
]
, we gave a new proof of the sharp noise sensitivity theorem of
[
GPS10
]
by relying on differential inequalities, inspired by the work of Kesten
[
Kes87
]
on near-critical percolation. One motivation was to provide a more robust approach than the Fourier method. In the present paper, we apply it to high temperature Ising model evolving under Glauber dynamics.

1.2
Noise sensitivity for high temperature Ising model

Consider the regular planar triangular lattice and let
\mathbb{T}
denote the set of sites of this lattice, that is,
\mathbb{T}=\{n+me^{i\pi/3}:n,m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. If
x,y\in\mathbb{T}
, we write
x\sim y
to say that
\{x,y\}
is an edge of the triangular lattice, i.e.
|x-y|=1
, where
|\cdot|
is the Euclidean norm.
Let
n\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{T}
. We consider the rhombus

\Lambda_{n}=\big\{k+me^{i\pi/3}\in\mathbb{T}:k,m\in\{-n,\dots,n\}\big\}.

If
x\in\mathbb{T}
, we let
\Lambda_{n}(x)=x+\Lambda_{n}
be the rhombus centered at
x
. When we say rhombus in this paper, we always refer to a set of this type.
Given a rhombus
\Lambda
and some
\delta>0
, we let
\Lambda^{(\delta)}
denote the ‘thickened rhombus’ whose center is the same as
\Lambda
and whose sides have been scaled by factor
1+\delta
(with the convention that here and in all the paper we consider the ceil of the new size of sides to work with integer-valued sides).

Let

\Omega_{n}=\{-1,+1\}^{\Lambda_{n}}

denote the set of all spin configurations in
\Lambda_{n}
.

Definition 1.1

(Ising measure with free boundary conditions)
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
E(\Lambda_{n})
denote the set of edges whose endpoints are both in
\Lambda_{n}
. The energy of a spin configuration
\eta\in\Omega_{n}
is

H(\eta)=-\sum_{\{x,y\}\in E(\Lambda_{n})}\eta(x)\eta(y).

Given some
\beta\in(0,+\infty)
, the Ising measure at inverse temperature
\beta
(with free boundary conditions and no external field) on
\Lambda_{n}
is the probability measure
\mu_{n}=\mu_{n,\beta}
on
\Omega_{n}
defined by

\forall\eta\in\Omega_{n}\hskip 10.00002pt\mu_{n}(\eta)=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\exp\big(-\beta H(\eta)\big),

where
Z_{n}=Z_{n,\beta}
is the renormalizing constant
\sum_{\eta\in\Omega_{n}}\exp(-\beta H(\eta))
.

In this paper, we prove noise sensitivity properties of crossing events, that we define now.

Definition 1.2
.

For every
n\geq 1
, we let
\mathsf{Cross}_{n}\subset\Omega_{2n}
denote the event that there is a path included in
\Lambda_{n}
from the left side of this rhombus to its right side, that is only made of
+1
spins. We study the crossing of
\Lambda_{n}
for spin configurations in the larger rhombus
\Lambda_{2n}
to avoid boundary effects.

Let
\beta\in(0,+\infty)
,
n\geq 1
and let
\sigma\sim\mu_{2n}=\mu_{2n,\beta}
. By symmetry of the rhombus and of the measure
\mu_{2n}
and by self-duality, we have

\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma\in\mathsf{Cross}_{n}\big)=\mu_{2n}\big(\mathsf{Cross}_{n}\big)=\frac{1}{2}.

We consider a dynamics
(\sigma_{s})_{s\geq 0}
defined by sampling
\sigma_{0}=\sigma\sim\mu_{n}
and letting it evolve according to Glauber resampling dynamics: we associate Poisson clocks to every site and, every time a clock rings, the value of the corresponding spin is resampled (under the law of this spin conditionally on the value of the neighbouring spins). This dynamics is defined more precisely in Section
1.5
.
The main result of our paper is that the crossing events in the Ising model at inverse temperature
\beta<\beta_{c}
are sensitive under Glauber dynamics (see the appendix for the definition of
\beta_{c}
).

Theorem 1.3

(Noise sensitivity)
.

Let
\beta<\beta_{c}
. For any
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in\mathsf{Cross}_{n}\big)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}1/4,

where
(\sigma_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is the Glauber dynamics in
\Lambda_{2n}
(with free boundary conditions), at inverse temperature
\beta
and starting from
\sigma\sim\mu_{2n}
(as defined in Section
1.5
).

1

•

Readers are probably wondering about the cases

Concerning other questions related to noise sensitivity in the Ising model, see in particular the work of Galicza and Pete

1.3
Sharp noise sensitivity

Our analysis is actually quantitative and we obtain the sharp noise sensitivity theorem, i.e. the analogue of the theorem obtained by Garban, Pete and Schramm
[
GPS10
]
for Bernoulli percolation evolving under i.i.d. dynamics. To state this theorem, we need to define the
4
-arm event.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\beta\in(0,+\infty)
and
n\geq 1
. We let
A_{4}(n)\subset\Omega_{2n}
denote the event that there are four paths from a neighbour of the origin
o:=(0,0)
to
\partial\Lambda_{n}:=\{x\in\Lambda_{n}:\exists y\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\Lambda_{n}:y\sim x\}
, such that each path is of constant sign and the signs of the four paths alternate around
o
.
A_{4}(n)
is called the ‘
4
-arm event’. We let

\alpha_{n}=\mu_{2n}(A_{4}(n))\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\varepsilon_{n}=1/(n^{2}\alpha_{n}).

Remark 1.5
.

It can help to keep in mind that, if
\beta<\beta_{c}
, then there exist
c,C>0
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\leq Cn^{-c}
for every
n\geq 1
(see Section
2.1.2
).

The noise sensitivity property Theorem
1.3
is a consequence of the following theorem (and of Remark
1.5
).

Theorem 1.6

(Sharp noise sensitivity)
.

Let
\beta<\beta_{c}
and let
(t_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of non-negative numbers.

•

If
t_{n}/\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
, then
\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma,\sigma_{t_{n}}\in\mathsf{Cross}_{n}\big)\rightarrow 1/4
;

•

If
t_{n}/\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
, then
\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma,\sigma_{t_{n}}\in\mathsf{Cross}_{n}\big)\rightarrow 1/2
;

where
(\sigma_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is the Glauber dynamics in
\Lambda_{2n}
(with free boundary conditions), at inverse temperature
\beta
and starting from
\sigma\sim\mu_{2n}
(as defined in Section
1.5
).

The main novelty in the theorem above is the first item (noise region). Indeed, the second item (stability region) could be derived from the arguments in
[
GPS18
, Section 8]
.

1.4
Strategy of the proof

To prove the noise sensitivity theorems, we follow the approach from our work
[
TV23
]
, which in the present context consists in studying differential inequalities satisfied by
t\mapsto\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A)
for percolation-type events
A
. We prove that, when
t
is small enough, the pair
(\sigma,\sigma_{t})
essentially satisfies all the desired general properties to run this proof. In particular:

•

(Finite-energy). Changing the spin value at times
0
and
t
at some fixed vertex has a cost bounded independently of the rest of the configuration.

•

(Spatial mixing, when
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
). The configuration in a box is quasi-independent of the configuration at macroscopic distance from it. A typical application is about arm events: let
A_{1}(m,n)
be the one-arm event, that is, the event that there is a path made of spins
+1
from
\partial\Lambda_{m}
to
\partial\Lambda_{n}
. If
n\geq 2m
then

\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A_{1}(1,n))\leq C\cdot\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A_{1}(1,m))\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A_{1}(2m,n)).

•

(Russo-type differential formula). The derivative of
t\mapsto\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A)
can be expressed in terms of pivotal events for the event
A
. This essentially follows from standard derivative formulas for Markovian dynamics, together with the above-mentioned finite-energy property.

The first two properties are known for the static Ising model (i.e. for
\sigma
instead of
(\sigma,\sigma_{t})
) and in this case essentially follow from the spatial Markov property of this model. Such a direct approach seems delicate since the pair
(\sigma,\sigma_{t})

does not satisfy the spatial Markov property
.
3
3

3

For more about this lack of spatial Markov property for the pair, see the sketch of proof from Section
3.2
.
Instead, we decompose static and dynamical influences. More precisely the spin at some site
x
at time
0
can influence the spin at some other site
y
at time
t
through two mechanisms: Either the spin at
x
has already influenced the spin at
y
at time
0
, which can be estimated by using the properties of the static Ising model. Or the information of the value of the spin at
x
has traveled through time, which we estimate using that the set of sites that have rung before time
t
is a subcritical percolation (provided
t
is small enough) and that the dynamics is local (the probability that a spin changes when a clock rings only depends on the neighbours of the site).

Finite-energy, spatial mixing and a Russo-type formula are proven in Sections
3
,
4
and
5
. Then, the percolation arguments inspired from
[
TV23
]
are done in Sections
6
and
7
. Most of the work in these last sections consists of proving the so-called quasi-multiplicativity property for the quantities
\mathbb{P}[\text{a $4$-arm event holds at times $0$ and $t$}]
. The general strategy is standard, but new difficulties emerge due to possible interactions between
-1
paths at time
0
and
+1
paths at time
t
. See Section
6
(and in particular the paragraph below Proposition
6.5
).

1.5
Setup

In this section, we introduce the general setup we will be working with in the rest of the paper. More precisely, in Definition
1.8
below, we define a Glauber dynamics in
\Lambda_{n}
. Each time we refer to the ‘Glauber dynamics in
\Lambda_{n}
’ in this paper, we refer to the dynamical process
(\sigma_{s})_{s\geq 0}
that is introduced in this definition. Let us fix some
\beta\in(0,+\infty)
once and for all in this section and let us first introduce some notations:

•

If
n\geq 1
,
\eta\in\Omega_{n}
and
x\in\Lambda_{n}
, we let
\eta^{x}\in\Omega_{n}
be the spin configuration obtained from
\eta
by just flipping the spin at
x
;

•

We define the ‘rates’
c_{x}:\Omega_{n}\rightarrow[0,1]
by

c_{x}(\eta)=\frac{\mu_{n}(\eta^{x})}{\mu_{n}(\eta)+\mu_{n}(\eta^{x})}=\frac{1}{1+\exp\big(2\beta\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{n}:y\sim x}\eta_{x}\eta_{y}\big)}.

N.B:
c_{x}(\eta)
only depends on the value of
\eta
at
x
and its neighbours and is equal to the probability that the spin at
x
equals
-\eta(x)
conditionally on the event that the spin configuration coincides with
\eta
at the neighbours of
x
.

Remark 1.7
.

The rates
c_{x}
defined above correspond to the so-called Glauber heat-bath dynamics. All of our analysis would also work in the case of the so-called Glauber Metropolis dynamics, where
c_{x}(\eta)
is replaced by
\min\{1,\mu_{n}(\eta^{x})/\mu_{n}(\eta)\}
.

Definition 1.8

(Glauber dynamics)
.

Given some
n\geq 1
, we define a continuous time dynamical process
(\sigma_{s})_{s\geq 0}
on
\Omega_{n}
called ‘Glauber dynamics in
\Lambda_{n}
’ (with free boundary conditions and no external field). This process is defined on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and is constructed as follows:

•

We sample
\sigma\sim\mu_{n}
and we let
\sigma_{0}=\sigma
;

•

We associate a Poisson Point Process
Y_{x}
of parameter
1
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(a ‘Poisson clock’) to every
x\in\Lambda_{n}
, independently for every
x
and independently of
\sigma
;

•

We let
(U_{x,i})_{x\in\Lambda_{n},i\geq 1}
be a family of independent variables that are uniform in
[0,1]
and are independent of
\sigma
and the Poisson clocks;

•

If the clock on a vertex
x
has rung for the
i^{th}
time at some time
t
, then we flip the corresponding spin if
U_{x,i}\geq 1-c_{x}(\sigma_{t-})
.

We let
Y=(Y_{x})_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}
and
U=(U_{x,i})_{x\in\Lambda_{n},i\geq 1}
.

We have thus defined a continuous time càdlàg Markov process with values in
\Omega_{n}
. See for instance
[
Lig85
, Chapter 4]
more about this process. It is important to bear in mind that this process starts from its invariant measure
\mu_{n}
, and that this measure is reversible.

2.2.1
Semi-group and infinitesimal generator

In this section, we recall some general properties about the semi-group and the generator of the Glauber dynamics. (See for instance
[
Lig85
, Chapters 1 and 2]
for more details.) The semi-group
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of this dynamics is defined by the following formula. Let
f:\Omega_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Then for every
t\geq 0
,
P_{t}f:\Omega_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the function

\mathbb{E}\big[f(\sigma_{t})\;\big|\;\sigma=\cdot\big].

The infinitesimal generator
L:\mathbb{R}^{\Omega_{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\Omega_{n}}
is defined by

Lf=\Big(\frac{d}{dt}P_{t}f\Big)_{|t=0}.

Let us state a few standard key properties of
P_{t}
and
L
.

Remark 2.1
.

•

For every
s,t\geq 0
,
P_{t}\circ P_{s}=P_{t+s}
;

•

For every
t\geq 0
,
P_{t}(Lf)=L(P_{t}f)=\frac{d}{dt}P_{t}f
;

•

If

is increasing, then for any
t\geq 0
,
P_{t}f
is increasing.
4
4

4

This last property comes from the fact that, if one starts the Glauber dynamics with two different initial values
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega_{n}
such that
\eta^{\prime}(x)\geq\eta(x)
for every
x\in\Lambda_{n}
and uses the same Poisson clocks
Y_{x}
and uniforms

in both cases, then the monotonicity between the two configuration is conserved in time. To show this last fact, one can use that
\eta\in\Omega_{n}\mapsto c_{x}(\eta^{x\leftarrow-})
is increasing and
\eta\in\Omega_{n}\mapsto c_{x}(\eta^{x\leftarrow+})
is decreasing where, for every
x\in\Lambda_{n}
and
\eta\in\Omega_{n}
,

is the spin configuration that is obtained from
\eta
by assigning the value

to the spin at

A formula for the infinitesimal generator.

Recall that, given a spin configuration
\eta\in\Omega_{n}

\eta^{x}
denote the spin configuration obtained from

by flipping the spin at

and every

Lf(\eta)=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}c_{x}(\eta)\big(f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)\big).

We will also rely on the following ‘standard’ results about reversible Markov processes. Let
f:\Omega_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

•

For every

\mathbb{E}[f(\sigma)f(\sigma_{t})]\geq(\int fd\mu_{n})^{2}

2.2.2
A general differential formula

The strategy proposed in
[

]
to prove noise sensitivity properties is based on the study of (integro-)differential inequalities satisfied by

. As a first step, let us state a general differential formula for

(that is not new; this can be seen as a particular case of general differential formulas for Markov semi-groups, see e.g.
[
BGL13
]
). For completeness, we prove this formula in the appendix (Section

).
To state the differential formula in question, we introduce a dynamical process
(\sigma_{s}^{(x)})_{s\geq 0}
for every

. This dynamical process is defined almost exactly like

and the same rates

instead of

A general differential formula.

For any

and

-\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}\big[f(\sigma)g(\sigma_{t})\big]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\mathbb{E}\Big[c_{x}(\sigma)\big(f(\sigma^{x})-f(\sigma)\big)\big(g(\sigma^{(x)}_{t})-g(\sigma_{t})\big)\Big].

2.2.3
Dynamical FKG inequality

We will need the following dynamical FKG inequality.

Dynamical FKG.

Let
F,G:(\Omega_{n})^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be two increasing functions.
5
5

5

Saying that
F
is increasing means that both

and
F(\cdot,\eta)
are increasing for every
\eta\in\Omega_{n}
.
Then, for any

\mathbb{E}\big[F(\sigma,\sigma_{t})G(\sigma,\sigma_{t})\big]\geq\mathbb{E}\big[F(\sigma,\sigma_{t})\big]\mathbb{E}\big[G(\sigma,\sigma_{t})\big].

Proof.

This property is for instance a consequence of
[
Lig85
, Corollary 2.21 of Chapter 2]
. Indeed, by this corollary (together with (
FKG
) and the third item of Remark
2.1
), it is sufficient to show that for every increasing functions

, we have

L(fg)\geq fLg+gLf.

Let
f
and
g
be two such functions and let

. Recall that
\eta^{x}
is obtained from
\eta
by flipping the spin at
x

stated in Section

, the function
L(fg)-fLg-gLf
evaluated at

equals

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}c_{x}(\eta)\big\{f(\eta^{x})g(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)g(\eta)-f(\eta)g(\eta^{x})+f(\eta)g(\eta)-f(\eta^{x})g(\eta)+f(\eta)g(\eta)\big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}c_{x}(\eta)\big(f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)\big)\big(g(\eta^{x})-g(\eta)\big),

which is non-negative since

and
g(\eta^{x})-g(\eta)
have the same sign for every

.
∎

.

.

are chosen in such a way that the spin value can be updated without looking at the values of the neighbours. In order to replace the event
\mathcal{B}
by
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, we use FKG inequality. Let
\mathcal{G}
the sigma-field generated by all the clocks

for
z\in\Lambda_{n}
and the uniforms
U_{z,i}
,
z\notin\partial_{\mathrm{ext}}S\cap D
,

. Conditionally on
\mathcal{G}
, the event

is increasing in the uniforms

,
z\in\partial_{\mathrm{ext}}S\cap D
. Hence, by the FKG inequality and using that the uniforms in
\partial_{\mathrm{ext}}S\cap D
are independent of

, we get

\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G})\geq a^{|\partial_{\mathrm{ext}}S\cap D|}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}|\mathcal{G}).

(16)

An observation that compensates for the lack of spatial Markov property for the pair

is that the events
\mathcal{A}
and

are independent because

is measurable with respect to the clocks and uniforms in

. Hence taking the expectation in the equation above and using

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle\leq a^{-6|S|}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{A})\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=a^{-6|S|}\cdot(1-e^{-t})^{|R\setminus D|+1_{x\in D\setminus R}}\cdot(1-e^{-t}-te^{-t})^{|D\cap R|}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{|D\cap S|+|R|}}{a^{6|S|}}\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{B}^{\prime}).

(17)

Recall that
D^{\prime}=D\Delta\{x\}
and let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
be the event that

•

all the clocks in

ring exactly once,

•

no clock in

rings, and

•

all the first uniforms for

.

A clock at a fixed site rings exactly once with probability

, and it does not ring with probability

occurs with probability at least

Since

t^{|D^{\prime}\cap S|}(a/2)^{|S|}\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\leq\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\leq\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\sigma_{t}=\psi^{x}),

(18)

where the last inequality comes from the fact that on

, no spin in

has changed and all sites of

have changed.

Together with (

4.1
A Cauchy–Schwarz trick

We start with a rather elementary ‘trick’ that we will use both in the proof of ‘general’ results about the events
\{\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A\}
(in Sections
4
and
5
) and in the percolation sections of the paper.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A,B\subseteq\Omega_{n}
. For every
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}(\sigma\in A,\sigma_{t}\in B)\leq\max\big\{\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A),\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in B)\big\}.

Proof.

By the Markov property, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and then reversibility, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\sigma\in A,\sigma_{t}\in B)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{P}\big[\sigma\in A\;\big|\;\sigma_{t/2}\big]\mathbb{P}\big[\sigma_{t}\in B\;\big|\;\sigma_{t/2}\big]\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{P}\big[\sigma\in A\;\big|\;\sigma_{t/2}\big]^{2}\Big]\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{P}\big[\sigma_{t}\in B\;\big|\;\sigma_{t/2}\big]^{2}\Big]}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A)\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in B)}.\qed

Let us now state and prove a lemma that is a consequence of the finite-energy property and the Cauchy–Schwarz trick.

Definition of the green set.

Recall that the Glauber dynamics
(\sigma_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is defined by sampling an initial configuration
\sigma
and then updating it by using the Poisson clocks
Y_{x}
and the uniform variables

(see Definition

). We call green sites all sites in
\Lambda_{n}
whose clock has rung before time
\tau
, we let
\mathcal{G}
be the set of green sites and, for every

and we let
\mathcal{G}_{x}
denote the union of the green component of
x
and of the green components of the neighbours of
x
(Remark: By convention, if a site is not green then its green component is the empty set. In particular,
\mathcal{G}_{x}=\emptyset
means that
x
is not green and has no green neighbour). If
H\subseteq\Lambda_{n}
,
\eta\in\Omega_{n}
and
\alpha\in\{-1,+1\}^{H}
, we let
\alpha|\eta
be the spin configuration that coincides with
\alpha
on
H
and with
\eta
in
\Lambda_{n}\setminus H
. Moreover, we let

A_{\alpha}=\{\eta\in\Omega_{n}\>:\>\alpha|\eta\in A\}.

(24)

(If the reader wants motivation before reading the proof of the following lemma, they can first read Section
4.2
.)

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

,

and

. Let

. Then, for every

\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\{-1,+1\}^{H}}\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma\in A_{\alpha},\sigma_{t}\in B_{\beta},\mathcal{G}_{x}=G\big)\\
\leq\bigg(\frac{16}{c_{\mathrm{FE}}}\bigg)^{|G|+1}\cdot\tau^{|G|}\cdot\max\big\{\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A\big),\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in B\big)\big\}.

Proof.

Let
x,G,A,B,H,t
as in the statement and let
\alpha,\beta\in\{-1,+1\}^{H}
. Recall that
\partial_{\mathrm{ext}}H=\{y\in\mathbb{T}\setminus H:\exists x\in H,x\sim y\}
. In this proof, we will intersect
\partial_{\mathrm{ext}}H
with
\mathcal{G}
, which is a subset of
\Lambda_{n}
, so when the reader thinks of
\partial_{\mathrm{ext}}H
, they may have

in mind.

We note that the event
\{\mathcal{G}_{x}=G\}
is included in the event
\{G\subseteq\mathcal{G},\partial_{\mathrm{ext}}H\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\}
.
An observation concerning the green set that compensates for the lack of spatial Markov property for the pair

is that the event

is measurable with respect to the initial configuration

and all the Poisson clocks and uniforms outside

(because in this event the spins at the exterior boundary of

have not been updated). In particular, it is independent of the Poisson clocks in

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma\in A_{\alpha},\sigma_{t}\in B_{\beta},\mathcal{G}_{x}=G\big)

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{|G|}\mathbb{P}\big(\partial_{\mathrm{ext}}G\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset,\sigma\in A_{\alpha},\sigma_{t}\in B_{\beta}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{|G|}\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma\in A_{\alpha},\sigma_{t}\in B_{\beta}\big)

(26)

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{|G|}\max\big\{\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A_{\alpha}),\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in B_{\beta})\big\}\text{ by Lem.\ \ref{lem:CS}}.

(27)

.

Claim 4.3
.

We have

\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A_{\alpha})\leq\bigg(\frac{4}{c_{\mathrm{FE}}}\bigg)^{|H|}\cdot\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A).

Proof of the claim.

By induction, it is sufficient to prove the result when
H
is a singleton, so let

, let

, let

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A_{s})=\sum_{\eta,\psi\in A_{s}}\mathbb{P}(\sigma=\eta,\sigma_{t}=\psi)

4.2
The decoupling event

In this section, we prove a decoupling result that will imply spatial mixing when we restrict ourself to
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
in the next section. To state this result, we need a definition. Recall the notation
\mathcal{G}_{x}
from Section

Definition 4.4
.

Given a non-empty set
S\subseteq\Lambda_{n}
, we define the decoupling event
\mathsf{dec}(S)
as follows:

\mathsf{dec}(S)=\bigcap_{x\in S}\big\{|\mathcal{G}_{x}|\leq\log|S|\big\}.

The following lemma enables one to include the decoupling event
\mathsf{dec}(S)
in the probability
\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A)
without changing much this quantity.

Lemma 4.5
.

For every static event
A\subseteq\Omega_{n}
, every non-empty set
S\subseteq\Lambda_{n}
and every
t\in[0,\tau]
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\{\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A\}\cap\mathsf{dec}(S)\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A).

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of a union bound on the points of
S
and of the following lemma (applied to
\lambda=\log|S|+1
), that implies that

\mathbb{P}\big(\{\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A\}\cap\mathsf{dec}(S)^{c}\big)\leq|S|e^{-(\log|S|+1)}\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A)\leq\frac{1}{e}\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A).\qed

Lemma 4.6
.

For every
A\subseteq\Omega_{n}
,
x\in\Lambda_{n}
,
\lambda\geq 1
and
t\in[0,\tau]
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A,|\mathcal{G}_{x}|\geq\lambda\big)\leq e^{-\lambda}\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A\big).

(28)

Proof.

Let
A\subseteq\Omega_{n}
,
x\in\Lambda_{n}
,
\lambda\geq 1
and
t\in[0,\tau]
. By decomposing over all possible values for

, we have

\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A,\,|\mathcal{G}_{x}|\geq\lambda\big)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|G|\geq\lambda\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A,\mathcal{G}_{x}=G\big)}_{=:p_{G}}.

(29)

We now fix an admissible set
G
(i.e. a set that is a possible outcome for
\mathcal{G}_{x}
), we let
H=G\cup\{x\}
and we upper bound the summand

by summing over the possible configurations in

at time

and at time

. We obtain that

p_{G}\leq\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\{-1,+1\}^{H}}\mathbb{P}(\sigma\in A_{\alpha},\sigma_{t}\in A_{\beta},\mathcal{G}_{x}=G)\leq\bigg(\frac{16}{c_{\mathrm{FE}}}\bigg)^{|G|+1}\tau^{|G|}\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A\big),

(30)

where the last inequality follows from Lemma

(with

Let us now return to (

and then the definition of

and

(see Section

\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A,\,|\mathcal{G}_{x}|\geq\lambda)\leq\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A)\sum_{k\geq\lambda}\bigg(\frac{16}{c_{\mathrm{FE}}}\bigg)^{k+1}M^{k}\tau^{k}\leq e^{-\lambda}\mathbb{P}(\sigma,\sigma_{t}\in A).\qed

5.1
Spatial mixing

In all this section, we fix some

and we let
(\sigma_{s})_{s\geq 0}
be the Glauber dynamics in

(from Definition
1.8
) that starts from

.
In this section, we prove a spatial mixing result for events of the form

.
As in Section

for the

Proposition 5.1
.

For every
\delta>0
, there exist two constants

that do not depend on

such that the following holds. Let
\Lambda
be a rhombus such that
\Lambda^{(\delta)}\subseteq\Lambda_{n}
and let

and

Our proof of Proposition
5.1
is based on Lemma
4.5
and on another lemma that we state and prove now. Recall that the Glauber dynamics is defined by using the Poisson clocks

and the uniforms

Lemma 5.2
.

For every
\delta>0
, there exist

that do not depend on

such that the following holds. Let

be a rhombus such that
\Lambda^{(\delta)}\subseteq\Lambda_{n}
, let
\mathcal{A}
be a dynamical event measurable with respect to
(\sigma,Y,U)_{|\Lambda}
and let
\mathcal{B}
be a dynamical event measurable with respect to
(\sigma,Y,U)_{|\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda^{(\delta)}}
. Then,

c_{\delta}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})\leq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B})\leq C_{\delta}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}).

Proof.

In this proof, we use the symbol
\asymp_{\delta}
to denote the same thing as
\asymp
but with constants that may depend on
\delta
. To prove the claim, we are going to use the analogous result for the static Ising model (stated in Section
2.1.3
). By writing

as a function of
(\sigma,Y,U)_{|\Lambda}
and
1_{\mathcal{B}}
as a function of
(\sigma,Y,U)_{|\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda^{(\delta)}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}\;\big|\;Y,U\big)\big]

\displaystyle\asymp_{\delta}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{A}\>\big|\;Y,U\big)\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{B}\>\big|\;Y,U\big)\Big]\hskip 10.00002pt\text{by \eqref{eq:stat_mix_used} and since $\sigma$ indep.\ of $(Y,U)$}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{A}\>\big|\;(Y,U)_{|\Lambda}\big)\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{B}\>\big|\;(Y,U)_{|\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda^{(\delta)}}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}),

where is the two last equalities we have used that
\sigma
,
(Y,U)_{|\Lambda}
and
(Y,U)_{|\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda^{(\delta)}}
are independent.
∎

5.2
Combining spatial mixing and FKG inequality

As in the previous section, we fix some
n\geq 1
and we let
(\sigma_{s})_{s\geq 0}
be the Glauber dynamics in
\Lambda_{n}
(from Definition
1.8
) that starts from
\sigma\sim\mu_{n}=\mu_{n,\beta}
. (Recall that for all of Section

we have fixed some
\beta\leq\beta_{c}

By the dynamical FKG inequality (see Section
2.2.3
), two increasing events measurable with respect to the pair

are positively correlated. However, we will sometimes need to use that two events correlate well even if they are not both increasing everywhere. In practice, we will use the following proposition, whose proof is close to that of the spatial mixing property Proposition
5.4
(and also relies on the dynamical FKG inequality).
We say that a static event
A\subseteq\Omega_{n}
is increasing in some set

if
1_{A}(\psi)\geq 1_{A}(\eta)
for every spin configurations
\eta,\psi\in\Omega_{n}
such that
\psi
coincides with
\eta
in
\Lambda_{n}\setminus V
and
\psi(x)\geq\eta(x)
for every

.

Proposition 5.4
.

For every

[

Definition A.1

A.3
Formulas related to the infinitesimal generator

A.3.1
The infinitesimal generator

In this section, we prove the formula for the infinitesimal generator from Section
2.2.1
.

Proof.

For every
t>0
and every
x\in\Lambda_{n}
, we let
\mathcal{C}_{t}(x)
denote the event that the clock at
x
has rung exactly one time in the time interval
[0,t]
(i.e.
\#\{Y_{x}\cap([0,t]\times[0,1])\}=1
), that no other clock has rung in this time interval, and that when the clock has rung, the spin at
x
has changed. For every
t>0
and
\eta\in\Omega_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[f(\sigma_{t})\;\big|\;\sigma=\eta\big]-f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\mathbb{E}\big[1_{\mathcal{C}_{t}(x)}\big(f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)\big)\;\big|\;\sigma=\eta\big]+O_{t\rightarrow 0}(t^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{C}_{t}(x)\;\big|\;\sigma=\eta\big)\big(f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)\big)+O_{t\rightarrow 0}(t^{2}).

We conclude by observing that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}_{t}(x)\mid\sigma=\eta)=tc_{x}(\eta)+O_{t\rightarrow 0}(t^{2})
.
∎

A.3.2
A general differential formula

In this section, we prove the general differential formula stated in Section
2.2.2
, i.e.

-\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}\big[f(\sigma)g(\sigma_{t})\big]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\mathbb{E}\Big[c_{x}(\sigma)\big(f(\sigma^{x})-f(\sigma)\big)\big(g(\sigma^{(x)}_{t})-g(\sigma_{t})\big)\Big],

where
(\sigma_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is the Glauber dynamics starting from
\sigma\sim\mu_{n}
.

Proof.

We let
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
denote the semi group of the Glauber dynamics in
\Lambda_{n}
,
L
denote its infinitesimal generator (see Section
2.2.1
) and let
\langle\cdot\rangle_{n}
denote the expectation with respect to
\mu_{n}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}\big[f(\sigma)g(\sigma_{t})\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\big\langle fP_{t}g\big\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\frac{d}{ds}\big\langle P_{s}fP_{t}g\big\rangle_{n}\Big\}_{|s=0},

where in the second equality we used that, by reversibility of the dynamics, we have
\langle fP_{t+s}g\rangle_{n}=\langle P_{s}fP_{t}g\rangle_{n}
. As a result,

\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}\big[f(\sigma)g(\sigma_{t})\big]=\big\langle LfP_{t}g\big\rangle_{n}=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\mathbb{E}\big[c_{x}(\sigma)(f(\sigma^{x})-f(\sigma))g(\sigma_{t})\big].

(49)

We are now going to do an integration by parts. Fix some
x\in\Lambda_{n}
and, for every
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Omega_{n}\times\Omega_{n}
, let

F(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=c_{x}(\eta)(f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta))g(\eta^{\prime}).

We first observe that (by applying the involution
\eta\mapsto\eta^{x}
in the third equality):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[c_{x}(\sigma)(f(\sigma^{x})-f(\sigma))g(\sigma_{t})\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[F(\sigma,\sigma_{t})\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\Omega_{n}}\mu_{n}(\eta)\mathbb{E}\big[F(\sigma,\sigma_{t})\;\big|\;\sigma=\eta\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\Omega_{n}}\mu_{n}(\eta^{x})\mathbb{E}\big[F(\sigma,\sigma_{t})\;\big|\;\sigma=\eta^{x}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\Omega_{n}}\mu_{n}(\eta^{x})\mathbb{E}\big[F(\sigma^{x},\sigma_{t}^{(x)})\;\big|\;\sigma=\eta\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{\mu_{n}(\sigma^{x})}{\mu_{n}(\sigma)}F\big(\sigma^{x},\sigma_{t}^{(x)}\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\bigg[c_{x}(\sigma)\big(f(\sigma^{x})-f(\sigma)\big)g(\sigma_{t}^{(x)})\bigg]

(50)

where in the last equality we used that
\mu_{n}(\sigma^{x})c_{x}(\sigma^{x})=\mu_{n}(\sigma)
. As a result,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[c_{x}(\sigma)(f(\sigma^{x})-f(\sigma))g(\sigma_{t})\big]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:diff3}}{=}\frac{1}{2}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\big[c_{x}(\sigma)(f(\sigma^{x})-f(\sigma))g(\sigma_{t})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[c_{x}(\sigma)(f(\sigma^{x})-f(\sigma))g(\sigma_{t})\big]\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[c_{x}(\sigma)\big(f(\sigma^{x})-f(\sigma)\big)\big(g(\sigma^{(x)}_{t})-g(\sigma_{t})\big)\Big].

We conclude by combining the above with (
49
).
∎

A novel Pythagorean fuzzy FUCA driven MCGDM algorithm for evaluating green economy oriented corporate sustainability
Authors: Chuan Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe evaluation of corporate sustainability in the context of the green economy is challenging to perform because of uncertainty, the lack of information, and different criteria. To solve this, a new model, Pythagorean fuzzy FUCA (PyF-FUCA) driven multi-criteria group decision-making (MCGDM) model to gauge sustainability performance is proposed in this paper. It employs Pythagorean fuzzy sets (PyFS), which make the FUCA algorithm flexible with the possibility of reflecting the views of an expert and eliminating ambiguities in decision-making. The proposed framework is applicable, as evidenced by an in-depth case study where it was applied in terms of fifteen alternatives, nine criteria, and four decision-makers. Indeed, findings indicate that the global market in the green era is the most sustainable, and sensitivity analysis has ensured that the rankings remain stable under different criteria and weights of decision-makers. In addition, the comparison with the original methods of PyF-MCDM and classical methods shows the increased strength and flexibility of the developed model. In addition to the development of fuzzy methodologies of MCDM, the research can be helpful to managers and policy makers who need to integrate corporate strategy with the concept of a green economy.Keywords: Corporate sustainability, Green economy, Pythagorean fuzzy sets, FUCA method, Multi-criteria group decision-makingSubject terms: Engineering, Environmental sciences, Environmental social sciences, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

A green economy-based approach to corporate sustainability is a holistic concept where corporations are directed by the business strategies on environmental responsibility, growth, and social responsibility1. In contrast to the conventional approach of corporate operation, where the main objective is to achieve a high profit level, the green economy-driven sustainability is aimed at minimizing the ecological footprint, preserving the resources, and facilitating long-term social-economic welfare. It pushes organizations to use cleaner technologies, use more renewable energy, produce responsibly, and create more value for stakeholders in a manner that does not compromise on environmental integrity. This way, corporate sustainability in the green economy has demonstrated a holistic approach that incorporates the environmental, social, and economic aspects in order to come up with resilient and future-ready organizations.

Corporate sustainability based on the green economy has become a necessity rather than a choice in the operations of the present business operations due to its importance. The global challenges of climate change, resource deficits, and growing needs of the stakeholders have led to the need to rethink the operations of the organizations and customize them to the goals of sustainability. The sustainable view of strategy helps companies to be more competitive, robust, and socially responsible, and has a role in regulatory conformity and enhancement of reputation. Therefore, sustainability is emerging as an essential issue for organizations that ought to reflect and prioritize to succeed in a more sustainable business environment. It is, however, linked with many contradictory and complex dimensions that make the very process of decision-making extremely hard and require systematic approaches.

Multi-criteria decision making (MCDM) has been popular in a bid to address this complexity by assessing and prioritising a corporate sustainability project. MCDM is a decision-making tool that enables the decision-maker to compromise on various issues such as economic performance, environmental protection, and social responsibility. Under MCDM, the most suitable option based on green economy-driven corporate sustainability strategies may be located on the basis of the trade-offs of various criteria and decisions. However, the contexts of sustainability of decisions are sure to be uncertain, and the expert judgment that relies on subjectivity is necessary, which implies that the high-level mathematical tools have to be used to operate on the imprecise, vague, and hesitant information.

This vagueness and uncertainty are, however, something traditional crisp models can hardly do, and fuzzy set theory is required. Fuzzy sets introduced by Zadeh2 permit each element to be of a set with membership degree (MD), assumed to be between [0,1]. Although this method is flexible, it cannot model the non-membership degree (NMD) values. To overcome this, the intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFS) were introduced by Atanassov3, which is a combination of both MD and NMD; their sum is limited to [0,1]. The duality provides a more detailed way of showing uncertainty, which makes IFSs more general in problems of decision-making. According to these concepts, the square sum of MD and NMD is not higher than [0,1]. Yager4 proposed the Pythagorean fuzzy sets (PyFSs), which have more flexibility and benefits. In contrast to IFSs, PyFSs enable MD and NMD to have a squared sum, which is less than , and thereby enable the decision space to be broader and the levels of hesitation to be more accommodating. This renders them especially useful to model fuzzy expert opinions in sustainability settings, where there is incomplete and contradictory assessment usually available to decision-makers. PyFSs provide a good compromise between the uncertainty representation and computational simplicity, compared to more advanced structures that include q-rung orthopair fuzzy, spherical fuzzy, and picture fuzzy sets, and are therefore interpretable and practical in group decision-making. The proposed PyFSs are really suitable concerning the issues of sustainability, where the decision-makers must address multiple assessments.

More recently, PyFSs have been effectively applied to different sustainability-oriented MCDM models, such as supplier selection5, inventory policy optimization6, and the implementation of a circular economy7, which highlight their flexibility in complicated decisions. Further, the comparative analysis between TODIM and TOPSIS on PyF settings8 also indicates that PyFSs are more robust to deal with conflicting criteria and human judgmental uncertainty. These results support the rationale of the current research to expand PyFSs to the FUCA model of corporate sustainability assessment.

Parallel to these fuzzy models, many MCDM solutions have been proposed to address complicated decision-making processes. One of the newest and most effective methods, which is based on consistency-checking and an information aggregation method, is the Faire Un Choix Adequat (FUCA) approach that offers effective decision-making. The FUCA has been established in an attempt to eliminate discrepancies in group decision-making and to facilitate the prioritization of the alternatives to be made. Its advantages are that it allows group assessment, consistency, and minimizes biases, hence it is a good option when carrying out the sustainability assessment by taking into account a large number of decision-makers. Corporate sustainability is a compound issue that has divergent economic, social, and environmental objectives, and FUCA will offer credible information to organizations on the way to a green economic system.

Comparison and sensitivity analysis are the necessary features of such studies to affirm the soundness of the decision outcomes. It has also been compared to other PyF-MCDM techniques, which have shown that the proposed framework is correct and competitive in its functionality. Also, sensitivity analysis demonstrates that the findings are insensitive to the changes in preferences and weights of the criteria used by decision-makers. It is a systematic method that guarantees both methodology and managerial applicability, which favours the practical importance of the results.

This paper proposes a new PyF-FUCA framework to quantify the sustainability of corporations in the green economy. The novelty of the work is the combination of the flexibility of PyFSs with the capabilities of evaluating the consistency of a set of FUCA characteristics, which is effective in dealing with the unpredictability of the decision-making process related to sustainability. Unlike the classical FUCA framework, the PyF-FUCA approach mathematically reformulates the process of correlation aggregation by operations of Pythagorean fuzzy (instead of direct multiplication) with operational laws of t-norm and t-conorm, which makes it theoretically sound in dealing with uncertainty. The inclusion of MD and NMD adds to the range of FUCA to a more extensive field of decision modeling and allows modeling the interaction between criteria nonlinearly. This theoretical extension shows that the PyFS framework enables FUCA to represent complex, uncertain and conflicting factors of sustainability better than other fuzzy models, justifying the reason PyFS is used in this work. As compared to the current models, the proposed approach provides more flexibility in making group judgments, is more resistant to sensitivity conditions, and is more discriminative among options. The framework is applicable as shown by a detailed case study of fifteen alternatives and nine criteria, provided with the comparative and sensitivity analysis to confirm the usefulness. The contribution of the study is both a methodological and practical contribution in the sense that it offers organizations and policymakers a credible decision-support model in an attempt to introduce the concept of sustainability in the green economy paradigm.

Literature review

Application of MCDM in green economy-oriented corporate sustainability

Recent research has highlighted the need to develop a corporate sustainability model focused on a green economic transition as one of the key drivers of future resilience and competitiveness. For example1, examined female-led green and inclusive MSMEs in Indonesia, demonstrating how sustainability-oriented activism fosters resource efficiency and corporate growth alongside circular economy practices. Similarly9, stated that the bioeconomy, as part of the green economy paradigm, must be integrated with CSR while employing biophysical principles to achieve sustainability goals. Likewise10, emphasized the crucial role of circular economy practices such as supply-chain optimization, waste reduction, and sustainability-driven innovation, and highlighted CSR and Industry 4.0 as central pillars of sustainable corporate development. Collectively, these studies indicate that corporate sustainability within a green economy is multi-dimensional, requiring organizations to address economic, environmental, and social trade-offs simultaneously. With such complications, MCDM methods have emerged as applicable in the systematic evaluation of sustainability strategies and emphasizing them. As an example11, examined how MCDM can be incorporated into a model of resource optimization of sustainable airline service innovation, which would promote policies of the circular economy and the CSR goals. The review of MCDM strategies of corporate sustainability showed that the latter is one of the most commonly applied strategies, because it provides a systematic manner of considering a variety of, and often irreconcilable, sustainability indicators12. Moreover13, showed that fuzzy-based models are effective in interrelating sustainable development and financial growth in the European green economy and how fuzzy extensions of MCDM provide explanations of uncertainty and vagueness in sustainability estimates. All these studies demonstrate that the use of MCDM methodology to corporate sustainability, that is, green economy-based, allows organizing decision-making and incorporating various sustainability indicators. However, even with this significant improvement, there are still issues in dealing with uncertainty, consistency, and group decision-making that relate to sustainability. The identified gaps highlight the necessity of more sophisticated fuzzy-MCDM approaches, including the PyF-FUCA approach that is offered in this paper and is more attentive to the role of expert judgement, enhanced consistency of decisions, and enabled reasonable ranking of sustainability projects of the company.

Studies applied the FUCA method

The FUCA approach has rendered it a robust MCDM methodology since it produces undeterred rankings, and it addresses decision issues that relate to uncertainty and subjectiveness. It has resulted in a broad spectrum of uses in engineering, manufacturing, and product evaluation due to its flexibility. Indicatively14, used the field of mechanical machining processes to experiment with the FUCA method and contrasted it with the Weighted Sum Approach (WSA), and found that FUCA was better in assessing financial and operational results. Likewise15, used FUCA to optimize the turning process with multi-objectives and combined it with other weighting strategies to demonstrate the effectiveness of such a tool in controlling the process parameters. Based on its applications16, used FUCA along with the PIPRECIA method to address the drawbacks of some of them and extend its adaptability in choosing lathe machines, an example of how hybrid methods can be used. Also17, investigated the integration of FUCA and CURLI and a variety of alternative weighting procedures to choose technical products, including air conditioners, washing machines, and UAVs, indicating the efficiency of FUCA in industrial decision-making tasks. Recently18, compared FUCA to the PSI technique in selecting mini water pumps, and its results showed that FUCA generated more stable and reliable rankings, which validates its benefits over its rivals. Besides, the latest research studies like Qiu et al.19 utilized SWARA-WASPAS in a spherical fuzzy setup to rank low-carbon policies in mitigating climate change and Chang et al.20 employed an ESG assessment framework with Fuzzy Delphi coupled with cluster analysis to the science parks in Taiwan, which showed that hybrid fuzzy MCDM applications remain relevant and are adapted to sustainability contexts. Taken together, these works make it clear that the FUCA method is not only an excellent and stable decision-making tool but also can be successfully utilized in collaboration with other MCDM techniques. This flexibility and adaptability make FUCA a potential solution when addressing the complex decision-making issues, especially in the green-based contextual, sustainability-oriented context, i.e., the Green Economy-Focused Corporate Sustainability focus of this research.

Research gap analysis

Earlier studies have indicated that MCDM techniques are influential in assessing the sustainability-related issues in the green economy. However, most studies rely on classical fuzzy sets, which do not effectively capture higher-order uncertainty and hesitation. Additionally, although the FUCA method has proven useful in engineering and product evaluation, its application in corporate sustainability assessment remains largely unexplored. Furthermore, PyFS has not been combined with FUCA, despite their combined potential to model uncertainty and hesitation in group decision-making. This study addresses this gap by proposing a new PyF-FUCA-based framework for corporate sustainability assessment in the context of the green economy, offering more robust and reliable decision support than existing methods.

Preliminaries and theoretical background

Due to the presence of ambiguity and subjectivity in expert qualitative judgments, as well as the uncertainties associated with incorrect and sporadic information, which are typical in real-life applications, achieving accurate measurements of specific criteria can be challenging. The FS of Zadeh2, a powerful approach to managing complex problems of ambiguity and uncertainty in fields such as management, economics, and industry, has been adopted by many researchers. However, the value of MD in FS theory is limited to the range . To address the limitations of FSs in practice, Atanassov3 proposed IFS, which includes MD, NMD, and the degree of hesitation (HD). MD is the degree of membership of an element in a set, while NMD is the degree of non-membership. When Yager4 applied the idea of PyFS, it was done more as an IFS. PyFS generalizes the model by allowing for the representation of expert uncertainty and hesitation through constraining the sum of the squares of MD and NMD to be equal to 1. Development of the Pythagorean fuzzy FUCA (PyF-FUCA) method

This paper introduces a new design for the PyF-FUCA framework by integrating the theory of PyFS with the FUCA method to address uncertainty in MCDM. The most notable change in methods is that the classical FUCA computation has been transformed into a PyF domain where measures of aggregation, normalization, and correlation are redefined using PyF t-norm and t-conorm operators. The method begins with employing the Pythagorean Fuzzy Weighted Averaging (PyFWA) operator to compute aggregated criteria weights, maintaining the HD present in expert judgments. To enable systematic evaluation, linguistic terms (LTs) provided by human experts are first converted into PyFNs, allowing qualitative assessments to be expressed in a mathematically rigorous manner. The FUCA method is a compromise ranking technique in MCDM that involves ranking alternatives for each criterion, assigning a weight to each criterion, and then calculating the overall scores for ranking the alternatives. FUCA is especially recognized for not requiring explicit data normalization in some scenarios, which simplifies the analysis in many decision-making contexts. Nonetheless, in its typical application, FUCA relies on crisp (precise) values for criteria and alternatives, limiting its capacity to directly represent uncertainty, vagueness, or hesitation in expert judgments. The given PyF-FUCA will address the given limitation by introducing the concept of the PyF score functions into the FUCA ranking mechanism so that every correlation coefficient and utility measure can be considered as representative of MD and NMD at the same time. With the extension of FUCA into a fuzzy system such as PyFS, uncertainties can be more accurately addressed, which leads to more reliable ranking results in sophisticated sustainability decision-making situations. Case study: evaluating green economy-oriented corporate sustainability

Green economy-oriented corporate sustainability has emerged as a revolutionary paradigm in response to rising global awareness about climate change, resource scarcity, and environmental degradation. It stresses the integration of eco-friendly practices into corporate strategies to ensure long-term competitiveness, social responsibility, and resilience against environmental risks. In aligning sustainability with the principles of the green economy, the organizations not only contribute to the mitigation of carbon emissions and preservation of natural resources, but also place themselves in the position of responsible players in achieving the global sustainable goals of development. According to this strategy, companies should embrace innovation, more sustainable technologies, and more open governance, which are all capable of supporting sustainable development.

The significance of the green economy-oriented way of corporate sustainability cannot be overestimated because it has a direct influence on the ecological balance, economic stability, and social well-being. The companies that fail to adapt may find themselves a negatively perceived company, there are regulatory problems, and the investor may lose trust in them. Instead, the companies that incorporate sustainability in their business models operate the companies more efficiently, enjoy greater trust on the part of the stakeholders, and benefit in the long term financially. It is also demanding more transparency and sustainability reporting from investors due to the soaring awareness of consumers. There is a need to ensure that sustainability is central to the strategies of the companies in ways that have never been experienced before. Therefore, adoption of effective corporate sustainability assessment models in the green economy is not an option anymore, but rather a survival and competitive tool in the global business world.

In order to overcome such a challenge, MCDM methods are critical in evaluating the corporate sustainability performance by providing a systematic manner of considering the conflicting goals and interests of different parties. Sustainability is multi-dimensional, which means that it considers the economic, environmental, and social pillars, and therefore, old-fashioned methods that rely on one criterion are not representative of the phenomenon of sustainability. Numerous MCDM techniques, such as TOPSIS, VIKOR, and MARCOS, have been utilized in sustainability assessments. However, this study introduces the PyF-FUCA method, which enhances group decision-making by incorporating higher degrees of uncertainty and hesitation, allowing for a more realistic evaluation of corporate sustainability that aligns with the objectives of the green economy.

As an example of how the proposed methodology could be practically useful, one can refer to a hypothetical dataset that deals with four decision-makers who jointly evaluate the sustainability performance of fifteen corporations. The hypothetical data were generated logically based on the structure of the evaluation criteria and realistic assumptions to reflect practical decision-making scenarios. The decision criteria and expert role were well selected to capture the major areas of sustainability, such as environmental, economic and social dimensions of decision making, in order to provide realistic situations in decision making. Such a method will offer a controlled and open environment for illustrating ranking, aggregation, and sensitivity analysis capabilities of the PyF-FUCA approach, where results are interpretable and applicable in practice.

Result discussion

The findings reached when using the proposed PyF-FUCA approach can offer valuable information about the assessment of the green economy-based corporate sustainability. According to the overall rating, the most sustainable option was (GreenEra Global), which is in first place. This shows that showed better results in the benefit criteria, shown as the use of renewable energy, environmental innovation, and transparency, and also was lower in the cost-related criteria, including operational costs, carbon emissions, and waste. The fact that it has continued to score high in various dimensions indicates that the organization is in line with the goals of the green economy. The second and the third are achieved by (Sustainable Horizons) and (BlueEarth Systems), respectively, both of which demonstrated a moderate performance in the economic and environmental aspects. The fact that these corporations are rated high implies that they have effectively incorporated social responsibility programs and employee welfare in their strategies, as well as ensuring efficiency in the use of their resources. (EcoGrowth Ltd.) and (GreenWave International) also performed well, as they got the fourth and the fifth position, which means their capacity to adhere to the requirements of sustainability and be able to respond to the cost-related issues successfully. (EcoSmart Ltd.) took the sixth place, just after the leading layer of corporations, and demonstrates a prospect of getting the balance between benefits and costs positive.

(TerraNova group), (Naturecore Corp), and (Freshplanet Systems) were ranked as number seven, eight, and nine in the middle ranges. Such companies received average scores in specific benefit criteria, but their results were not so good in addressing carbon emission and waste production, which should have influenced their total scores. The same applies to (EnviroTech Solutions) and (CleanPath Industries), as they occupied the positions of ten and eleven, respectively. This implies that it must improve its sustainability transparency, innovation, and overall sustainability strategies to remain competitive in the green economy model. (GreenFuture Corp.), BIOVision Enterprises, (EcoSphere Ltd.), and (Pureplanet Enterprises) ranked twelfth, thirteenth, fourteenth, and fifteenth, respectively, at the bottom of the list. This situation was particularly problematic for these options. It required them to struggle with the cost demands, which the high rates of renewable energy consumption and innovation activity could not address. The latter is also evidenced by the fact that they are less performing, contributing to the fact that corporations that do not invest enough into the components of sustainability result in lower compliance with the norms of the green economy. Overall, the results of the ranking indicate the effectiveness of the proposed PyF-FUCA tool to identify the best corporations committed to the concept of a green economy-oriented sustainability and differentiate between those that require significant changes. The results not only provide a viable benchmark to be embraced by organizations, but also enhance the argument that the balance of the benefit and the cost requirements is to be achieved to achieve the long-term corporate sustainability objectives.

Sensitivity analysis

The section evaluates the robustness and the permanence of the suggested PyF-FUCA framework, evaluating the influence of the weights of decision-makers and the weights of criteria on the ultimate results. It also investigates the consistency of the criteria, significance, and ranking of options in different contexts so that the decision-making procedure is the same and reliable in other contexts.

Influence of change of DMs’ weight values on criteria weight values

The sensitivity analysis was conducted to investigate how the weight values of decision-makers (DMs) change to inversely affect the derived values of the criteria weight, as shown in (Fig. 4). The ten scenarios were experimented with, changing the relative weight attributed to each of the four DMs, and this implied the weights of the criteria. The purpose of this analysis was to find out whether changes in the weight of DMs would result in any significant alteration in the priorities of the criteria. The findings indicate that the weightings of the requirements are pretty universal in various situations, albeit the weight of DM varies. (Environmental Innovation) and (Carbon Emission Levels) are always prioritized higher as an example; their average of - and - respectively, are correct considering their contribution to the determination of the corporate sustainability, which is green economy-oriented. Likewise, the importance of (Social Responsibility Programs) as well as (Employee Well-being and Safety) are also slightly or equally important, which proves that both of the social aspects are not deprived of weight despite the differences in DM concentration. (Resource Efficiency), In its turn, it displays the lowest values of weight in any scenario (-), which is somewhat irrelevant in terms of the group assessment. The analysis also shows that even though there are some minor variations (e.g., (Transparency and Reporting) indicating slight changes between and ), those changes do not contribute significantly to the overall position of the criteria. This consistency implies that the suggested PyF-FUCA approach is not too dependent on the changes in preferences of the decision-makers, which will guarantee the reliability and robustness of the evaluation framework. Practically, it is to be understood that although the composition or influence of decision-makers can vary, the priorities of sustainability criteria will stay stable. Impact of change of criteria weight values on ranks of the alternatives

The second section of the sensitivity analysis will examine how changes in the value of the criteria weights will affect the ranking of the alternatives, as demonstrated in (Fig. 5). The relative significance of the nine criteria was changed, and ten scenarios were simulated with the alternatives being reconsidered as a result. This experiment aimed to challenge the strength of the suggested PyF-FUCA approach by analyzing whether the changes in the priorities of the criteria would dramatically impact the sustainability ratings of the corporations. According to the results, the general ranking of the best performing alternatives does not change significantly despite significant changes in criteria weights. (GreenEra Global) can be placed regularly among the top-performing options in any situation, which proves that the company performs excellently in both benefit and cost measures. Likewise, (Sustainable Horizons) and (BlueEarth Systems) take leading positions, which indicates their stability to changes in the priorities of evaluation. Even though there are certain deviations in their aggregate scores in some numerical aspects, these variations are not high enough to put them out of the first-tier ranking. In their turn, the compromising options such as (CleanPath Industries), (TerraNova Group), and (EcoSmart Ltd.) have a reasonable degree of variability because they occasionally acquire or lose, depending on the topicality of the accent on environment or cost-related considerations. It implies that the sustainability performance is sensitive to the rotation of the weights of the criteria, and these corporations may require a more balanced view of the improvement to improve their position. Everywhere on the bottom, (PurePlanet Enterprises), and (GreenFuture Corp) are constantly in the company of the worst performers, regardless of different criteria weights. They are unable to increase their rankings in varying weighting conditions, and this fact illustrates the consistent deficiency in terms of benefits and costs. At the same time, (EcoSphere Ltd.) and (BioVision Enterprises) demonstrate slight score changes in some cases and remain under the pressure of stronger competitors. All in all, the sensitivity analysis proves that not all alternatives demonstrate the same variability in terms of ranking; the most successful and the weakest corporations are very similar. Such stability proves the soundness and viability of the PyF-FUCA approach to assess green economy-related corporate sustainability. The results imply that the results of decisions are not excessively sensitive to changes in the weights of criteria, and the procedure can be used to offer a stable and reliable decision support to companies in sustainability-related matters.

Benchmark comparison with existing PyF-MCDM approaches

The benchmark comparison was conducted in order to ensure the efficiency of the proposed PyF-FUCA method against three existing PyF MCDM methods, i.e., PyF-CoCoSo21, PyF-MARCOS22, and PyF-CRADIS23, as the results of which were displayed in (Fig. 6). It was a comparative experiment, which was planned to empirically confirm the methodological novelty of PyF-FUCA and whether the redefined Pythagorean correlation aggregation provides different and more interpretable rankings. As shown in this discussion, the proposed model performs well compared to the familiar methods of ranking green economy-driven corporate sustainability options. The results indicate that the suggested PyF-FUCA method ranks drastically differently from the rest of the approaches, and it is more faithful and accessible. Indicatively, (GreenEra Global), the top ranking by PyF-FUCA was ranked very low in the other ranking approaches by PyF-CoCoSo, PyF-MARCOS, and PyF-CRADIS, respectively. This confirms the reality that the offered approach is more sensitive to the valuable sustainability characteristics and indicative of the balance of benefit and cost needs. Equally, PyF-FUCA ranked (Sustainable Horizons) Second, and fifteenth in both PyF-MARCOS and PyF-CRADIS, in which a more consistent fit between PyF-FUCA and sustainability priorities is stressed. The strength of the proposed way is also confirmed by further consideration of the mediocre-performing alternatives. (BlueEarth Systems), which is the third most ranked corporation, with PyF-FUCA only gaining seventh and eighth positions in the other approaches, it is evident that PyF-FUCA is effective in separating the high-performing corporations that otherwise are not adequately rated. Quite to the contrary, others like (GreenFuture Corp.) and (BioVision Enterprises) had been ranked so much higher in PyF-CoCoSo and PyF-MARCOS, and at the twelfth and thirteenth positions, respectively, on the PyF-FUCA evaluation. This is an indication that other models might over-emphasize some of the attributes, but PyF-FUCA will provide a more balanced and realistic assessment because of the preferences of decision-makers when faced with uncertainty. Overall, the benchmark comparison confirms the fact that the proposed PyF-FUCA approach has a more advanced discriminatory power and provides quality and correlation-based rankings that are oriented towards the sustainability goals. The novelty and contribution of PyF-FUCA to the intricate trade-offs that are involved in MCGDM are brought into focus by this variation in the rankings in comparison to the other techniques. In this way, the approach proves to be superior and an efficient tool to consider corporate sustainability against the green economy.

Practical and managerial implications

The research has extensive practical and managerial implications for companies that wish to make sure that their activities are in accordance with the aspirations of green economy sustainability. The PyF-FUCA method allows the manager to possess a strong tool of decision support that can be used to review the performance of an organization in multiple aspects of sustainability, making the process both cost-efficient and a strategic benefit. The model considers the aspect of uncertainty and group decision-making dispositions whereby the opinions of various parties are considered in the assessment, hence, coming up with more balanced, inclusive and actionable outcomes.

This enables the decision-makers to determine areas of weakness and strength in the corporate sustainability strategies and concentrate on activities that have the highest influence on the long-term competitiveness. As an example, the recognition of GreenEra Global as the most sustainable corporation gives practical advice to other companies on how to implement the best practices, focus on the allocation of resources, and improve green strategies. The findings also provide good information on policy formulation, strategic investment and sustainability reporting frameworks, empowering managers to compare their performance with that of the best and replicate the high-impact practices.

A focus on such criteria as renewable energy, environmental innovation, and social responsibility offers practical information on how firms can be more productive in the green economy. Besides this, the strength of PyF-FUCA in various contexts gives managers the confidence in their strategic choices. All in all, the practical and managerial relevance of the work proves the necessity of adopting the most sophisticated MCDM practices to facilitate corporate sustainability, enhance market competitiveness, and evidence-based policymaking.

Advantages of the study

The current research possesses several strengths that contribute to the sustainability analysis in the model of the green economy. To start with, the PyF environment is quite good at addressing uncertainty and reluctance, as well as uncertainty in and vagueness of expert judgments, but the more traditional crisp or less flexible fuzzy methods provide less realistic and credible results as a rule. Second, the application of the FUCA algorithm in a hybrid MCDM model would render the study innovative, and it may be capable of incorporating the views of many decision-makers and be robust in ranking criteria and priorities of the alternatives. This enhances the precision and the discriminatory power of the decision-making process. Thirdly, the study serves as a bridge between theory development and practical application because it uses the method developed and evaluates the corporate sustainability based on the green economy. Not only does this prove the efficiency of the method, but it also provides theoretical knowledge to the organizations and stakeholders. Fourth, the comparative and sensitivity analysis contribute to the quality of the results, demonstrating that the rankings remain the same in various situations and the PyF-FUCA is better than current MCDM processes. Finally, the study also yields a decision support system that can be incorporated into the existing knowledge base of corporate managers, policymakers, and sustainability practitioners to understand what best practices, weaknesses, and approaches to attain sustainable development goals.

Limitations of the study

The PyF-FUCA framework holds great potential in finding complex solutions to decision-making problems concerning corporate sustainability and the green economy; it has several limitations. To begin with, the study is based on hypothetical information and judgments of experts, which, in a way, prove the competence of the methods, yet might not reflect the actual organizational practices comprehensively. These findings could be enhanced in future research through empirical data by using case studies of industries. Second, the model is computationally expensive since it has a hybrid MCDM framework to combine PyFS and FUCA. This can pose an issue to the practitioners who are not well-versed in advanced fuzzy logic or specialized software, but can be compensated for by straightforward frameworks or decision-support tools. Thirdly, the strategy takes into account the issue of uncertainty, but it is cautious about the issues of the expertise evaluation; however, it does not take into consideration the dynamic or time-based changes in the factors affecting corporate sustainability, which is especially relevant in the context of the fast-evolving economic and environmental conditions. A dynamic decision-making process should be added to the model to make it more flexible. Finally, the given research is limited to the strategy that is in line with the green economy, and this approach, although being highly topical, does not evaluate other important areas of corporate sustainability, including social equity or ethical governance. A multi-dimensional approach to sustainability would be a better assessment model to provide.

Conclusion with future research direction

The current paper suggests a PyF-FUCA-based MCDM model to assess corporate sustainability, which is concerned with the green economy. The analysis of fifteen alternatives and nine criteria conducted on the case showed that it can produce valid and consistent rankings in the face of uncertainty. The sensitivity analysis ensured the strength of the findings, and the comparison with current models of PyF-MCDM and the traditional ones showed that the proposed model is the most suitable one to be used in dealing with the complex decision-making problems. Remarkably, was in the first position on the list, which shows that it aligns with the goals of sustainability and the green economy. Altogether, it can be concluded that the PyF-FUCA approach is efficient, flexible, and viable to help organizations embrace green and sustainable practices.

It is possible to expand on PyF-FUCA with the medical diagnosis spherical fuzzy sets29 or sophisticated operators such as the complex picture fuzzy Dombi operator to PF-FUCA as an assessment of teaching skills30. The other method of dealing with increased uncertainty is the T-spherical fuzzy information that is interval-valued31. In addition, the incorporation of the latest deep learning systems32,33 would also refine the ability of PyF-FUCA to operate with non-linear patterns, dynamic parameters, and adaptable real-time decision-making. Fixed-point theorems in neutrosophic metric spaces may establish more robust theoretical foundations. At the same time, generalized distance and similarity measures of picture hesitant fuzzy sets could enhance discrimination in material recognition and engineering decision-making.

Is Repeater-Assisted Massive MIMO Compatible with Dynamic TDD?
Martin Andersson, Anubhab Chowdhury, Erik G. Larsson

Abstract
We present a framework for joint amplification and phase shift optimization of the repeater gain in dynamic time-division duplex (TDD) repeater-assisted massive MIMO networks. Repeaters, being active scatterers with amplification and phase shift, enhance the received signal strengths for users. However, they inevitably also amplify undesired noise and interference signals, which become particularly prominent in dynamic TDD systems due to the concurrent downlink (DL) and uplink (UL) transmissions, introducing cross-link interference among access points and users operating in opposite transmit directions. This causes a non-trivial trade-off between amplification of desired and undesired signals. To underpin the conditions under which such a trade-off can improve performance, we first derive DL and UL spectral efficiencies (SEs), and then develop a repeater gain optimization algorithm for SE maximization. Numerically, we show that our proposed algorithm successfully calibrates the repeater gain to amplify the desired signal while limiting the interference.

We note that the above-mentioned papers consider static time-division

. On the contrary,

However, due to the concurrent ULand DLcommunication in dynamic TDD
systems, a repeater inevitably amplifies and forwards the cross-link
interference (CLI), i.e., the DLAP to ULAP (inter-AP)
interference and ULuser to DLuser (inter-user)

signals. Thus, it is critical to analyze the conditions
under which repeaters are compatible with dynamic TDD, i.e., when
substantial benefits can be procured by deploying repeaters in dynamic
TDD systems, while ensuring that the CLI amplification does not
overwhelm the desired signal gains.

Contributions:
In this paper, we provide a
definitive framework within which repeater gain optimization is
investigated for dynamic TDD systems. Further, in contrast to existing works on repeater gain optimization (in static TDD) that only consider amplification
[
7
,
8
]
, we account for a complex-valued repeater gain which also includes a phase shift. We provide the following specific results: 1) We derive closed-form
achievable DL and UL SEs for a repeater-assisted massive MIMO
network operating with

interference into consideration; that is, multi-user, inter-AP,
inter-user, and the corresponding amplified interference terms caused
by the repeater. 2) We propose a method to jointly optimize the

optimizing the repeater gain and updating the precoding and combining

optimization algorithm effectively improves the SE in the direction (DL/UL) for which it is optimized, but can slightly reduce the SE in the opposite direction in certain cases; however, the gains are much more significant than the losses. Further, we show that substantial DL and UL SE improvements can be obtained by including the phase shift in the repeater gain optimization.

2.1
DLand ULData Transmission

We now define the data transmission models in DL and UL, latter used to calculate achievable DL and UL SEs. Let
\{s_{\text{d},k}\}
denote the data symbols intended for the
K
DL users and
\{s_{\text{u},j}\}
be the data symbols transmitted by the
J
UL users. All data symbols have zero mean, unit variance, and are mutually uncorrelated. Further, let
\{\eta_{\text{d},k}\}
and
\{\eta_{\text{u},j}\}
be the power control coefficients for the DL and UL users, respectively, where
0\leq\eta_{\text{d},k},\ \forall k
and
0\leq\eta_{\text{u},j}\leq 1,\ \forall j
. Then, the signal transmitted by the DL AP can be expressed as
\x_{\text{d}}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{\eta_{\text{d},k}}\w_{k}s_{\text{d},k}
, where
\{\w_{k}\}
are the precoding vectors applied by the DL AP for each DL user. In addition to being non-negative, the DL power control coefficients must be chosen such that the DL power constraint
\EEE\{\x_{\text{d}}^{H}\x_{\text{d}}\}\leq 1
is fulfilled, which can be equivalently formulated as
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\eta_{\text{d},k}\w_{k}^{H}\w_{k}\leq 1.

Further, each UL user
j
transmits the signal
x_{\text{u},j}=\sqrt{\eta_{\text{u},j}}s_{\text{u},j}

Recall that, in dynamic TDD, both the DL AP and the UL users transmit their data simultaneously. Thus, the signal impinging on the repeater is given by

\displaystyle y_{\text{r}}^{\text{in}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho_{\text{d}}}\h_{\text{d}}^{T}\x_{\text{d}}+\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{J}\sqrt{\rho_{\text{u}}}h_{\text{u},j}x_{\text{u},j}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{\rho_{\text{d}}\eta_{\text{d},k}}\h_{\text{d}}^{T}\w_{k}s_{\text{d},k}+\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{J}\sqrt{\rho_{\text{u}}\eta_{\text{u},j}}h_{\text{u},j}s_{\text{u},j}.

(1)

Here,
\rho_{\text{d}}
and
\rho_{\text{u}}
, respectively, denote the DL and UL signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs).
The input is scaled by the complex-valued repeater gain
\alpha_{\text{r}}=r_{\text{r}}e^{i\phi_{\text{r}}}
, with
r_{\text{r}}=|\alpha_{\text{r}}|
being the amplification gain and
\phi_{\text{r}}=\angle\alpha_{\text{r}}
is the phase shift. Then, the repeater radiates the signal

\displaystyle y_{\text{r}}^{\text{out}}=\alpha_{\text{r}}(y_{\text{r}}^{\text{in}}+w_{\text{r}}),

(2)

where
w_{\text{r}}\sim\CN(0,\sigma_{\text{r}}^{2})
is the additive noise at the repeater. The amplification gain must be chosen such that (
2
) does not exceed the output power constraint
\EEE\{|y_{\text{r}}^{\text{out}}|^{2}\}=r_{\text{r}}^{2}(P_{\text{in}}+\sigma_{\text{r}}^{2})\leq P_{\text{max}}

[
7
]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle 0\leq r_{\text{r}}\leq\sqrt{P_{\text{max}}/(P_{\text{in}}+\sigma_{\text{r}}^{2})},

(3)

where we defined the power of the repeater input as
P_{\text{in}}=\EEE\{|y_{\text{r}}^{\text{in}}|^{2}\}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{\text{d}}\eta_{\text{d},k}|\h_{\text{d}}^{T}\w_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\rho_{\text{u}}\eta_{\text{u},j}|h_{\text{u},j}|^{2}
. Although not of concern here, we note that in scenarios with multiple interacting repeaters, the amplification gain is also limited by stability constraints
[
8
]
.

3.1
Maximizing the SINR in DL and UL

With the SINRs expressed as in (
9
), we can find a globally optimal amplification gain

which maximizes the SINR for a given phase shift
\phi_{\text{r}}
. To this end, first note that by using the quotient rule, the partial derivative of (

can be obtained as

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial r_{\text{r}}}\mathtt{SINR}\!=\!\dfrac{(a_{1}b_{0}-a_{0}b_{1})\!+\!2(a_{2}b_{0}-a_{0}b_{2})r_{\text{r}}\!+\!(a_{2}b_{1}-a_{1}b_{2})r_{\text{r}}^{2}}{(b_{0}+b_{1}r_{\text{r}}+b_{2}r_{\text{r}}^{2})^{2}}.

(22)

The numerator of (
22
) is a second-order polynomial in
r_{\text{r}}
. Thus, with
c_{0}=a_{1}b_{0}-a_{0}b_{1},\ c_{1}=a_{2}b_{0}-a_{0}b_{2},\ c_{2}=a_{2}b_{1}-a_{1}b_{2}
,
the stationary points of (
9

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial r_{\text{r}}}\mathtt{SINR}=0

\displaystyle\iff c_{0}+2c_{1}r_{\text{r}}+c_{2}r_{\text{r}}^{2}=0

\displaystyle\iff r_{\text{r}}=\frac{-c_{1}\pm\sqrt{c_{1}^{2}-c_{0}c_{2}}}{c_{2}}.

(23)

It follows that the optimal amplification gain is either one of the two stationary points in (
3.1
), or one of the boundary points decided by the power constraint (
3
); that is,
0
or
\sqrt{P_{\text{max}}/(P_{\text{in}}+\sigma_{\text{r}}^{2})}
. If either of the two candidates in (
3.1
) falls outside of the feasible set (
3
), it is discarded as a potential solution. To find the optimal amplification gain, we insert the remaining candidates into (
9
) and choose the one that maximizes the SINR. In this way, we find a globally optimal solution
r_{\text{r}}
for a given phase shift
\phi_{\text{r}}
. To find an optimal phase shift, we repeat the above-mentioned procedure for a discrete range of
\phi_{\text{r}}
. Specifically, we use
S\geq 2
equally spaced samples in the range
[0,2\pi]
; namely
\phi_{\text{r}}\in\{2\pi s/S\}_{s=0}^{S-1}
. After iterating through all of these phase shifts and finding the optimal amplification gain for each given
\phi_{\text{r}}
, we let
\phi_{\text{r}}^{*}
and
r_{\text{r}}^{*}
be the pair maximizing the SINR. Then, the optimal repeater gain is
\alpha_{\text{r}}^{*}=r_{\text{r}}^{*}e^{i\phi_{\text{r}}^{*}}
.

3.2
Proposed Method for Repeater Gain Optimization

The procedure in Section
3.1
is simple, efficient, and has a low computational complexity. However, it has one major drawback; the precoding and combining vectors
\{\w_{k}\}
and
\{\vv_{j}\}
must be chosen independently of the amplification gain. Otherwise, higher order terms of
r_{\text{r}}
are introduced in (
9
). We overcome this drawback by iteratively optimizing the repeater gain for fixed precoding/combining vectors, and then updating those vectors using the newly obtained optimal repeater gain. The steps of our proposed method for repeater gain optimization are as follows:

1)

Initialize the precoding and combining vectors
\{\w_{k}^{(0)}\}
and
\{\vv_{j}^{(0)}\}
for some feasible initial value
\alpha_{\text{r}}^{(0)}
. Let
n=1
.

2)

Follow the procedure in Section
3.1
to find an optimal
\alpha_{\text{r}}^{*}
for fixed
\{\w_{k}^{(n-1)}\}
and
\{\vv_{j}^{(n-1)}\}
. Let
\alpha_{\text{r}}^{(n)}=\alpha_{\text{r}}^{*}
.

3)

Update the precoding and combining vectors with
\alpha_{\text{r}}^{(n)}
to obtain
\{\w_{k}^{(n)}\}
and
\{\vv_{j}^{(n)}\}
.

4)

Make
n=n+1
and repeat from step 2) until convergence.

Our algorithm does not provide any theoretical convergence guarantees. However, in our simulations, we run until the incremental SE improvement is smaller than
10^{-5}
, or until the SE decreases between two iterations. This typically requires
5-10
iterations in DL and
2-5
iterations in UL before converging to a stationary point.

5
CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS

This paper has investigated
how much dynamic TDD MIMO systems can benefit from repeater-assistance, as repeaters amplify the CLI in addition to the desired signal.
Our proposed repeater gain optimization framework based on DL/ULSE maximization has been validated to provide improved DLand ULSEs compared to a case when the repeater is deactivated, in most cases. Specifically, we have underscored conditions on the repeater location with respect to the users under which such improvements in the DLand ULSEs can be maintained. Further investigations with multiple repeaters and a joint optimization of repeater gains along with the DL/ULpower control and precoding/combining vectors can be carried out in the future to overcome the cases where the repeater reduces performance.

Continuity of primary care and its impact on prognosis in patients with heart failure referred for cardiology consultation
Authors: Sergio Cinza-Sanjurjo, Pilar Mazón-Ramos, Daniel Rey-Aldana, Inés Gómez-Otero, Oscar Otero-García, Manuel Portela-Romero, Alfonso Varela-Román, José R González-Juanatey
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo assess the prognostic impact of continuity of care (COC), defined as care provided by the same primary care physician (PCP), in patients with heart failure (HF) referred for cardiology consultation.MethodsWe analyzed all patients with previous HF referred by PCPs between January 2010 and December 2023, in our healthcare area (Santiago de Compostela, Galician-Spain). COC was analyzed by considering whether the referring PCP was the patient’s assigned PCP (PCP stability) or COC interrupted. To estimate the association between COC and hospitalization and mortality outcomes (all-cause, cardiovascular, and HF), we used Cox regression to obtain hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (95% CI), adjusting for potential confounders.Results5,899 patients’ referrals were included. Patients with COC interrupted had a higher annual referral rate (2.4 [3.0] vs. 1.5 [0.5], p < 0.001). Patients with PCP stability had a lower risk of all-cause hospitalizations (HR [95% CI]: 0.85 [0.76–0.95], p = 0.006) and all-cause mortality (HR [95% CI]: 0.89 [0.80-1.00], p = 0.044). The COC interrupted was associated with higher risk of mortality for CV causes (HR [95%CI]: 2,65 [2,02–3,48]) and HF causes (HR [95%CI]: 1,66 [1,94 − 1,42]), and also hospitalizations for CV causes (HR [95%CI]: 1,64 [1,27 − 2,12]) and HF causes (HR [95%CI]: 1,78 [1,19 − 2,65]).ConclusionsThe COC, defined as care provided by the same PCP, for a group of patients with HF, reduced the number of referrals and was associated with better outcomes, including lower rates of all-cause hospitalizations and mortality. The COC interruption was associated with worst prognosis at 1-year.Graphical Abstract Keywords: Heart failure, Continuity of care, Primary care, Hospitalization, Mortality

Introduction

Major cardiology societies’ guidelines support integrated heart failure (HF) care, with primary care (PC) professionals (nurses and primary care physicians – PCPs) playing a central role 1. There is a compelling need to prevent or reduce the occurrence of worsening heart failure (WHF) episodes to improve outcomes for patients with HF and reduce the pressure on healthcare resources 2. Transition of care programs are important not only for early follow-up in HF patients discharged after a WHF episode but also for the entire HF patient’s care pathway3.

Early clinical identification of these decompensations is crucial to prevent further advancement of HF. Typically, such cases are initially evaluated and managed by PCPs, who may refer patients for specialist care, such as to cardiologists or internal medicine specialists. A healthcare system oriented towards facilitating early management of worsening episodes, including prompt specialist care following a PCP referral, is imperative to prevent more advanced clinical deterioration and improve clinical outcomes4. A recent publication provides evidence on the association between time to cardiology consultation after PCP referral and outcomes in patients with HF and significantly contributes to our understanding of the impact of delayed cardiology consultation on HF outcomes4. Furthermore, PCPs managing this prevalent group of patients take an integrated approach to frequent comorbidities, improve patients’ and caregivers’ disease knowledge, and enhance patient compliance with lifestyle changes and medications.

Continuity of care (COC) by PCPs involves an ongoing relationship between a patient and a PCP, who takes responsibility for the patient’s medical needs5. Longitudinal PCP-COC is defined as the persistence of contact between physicians and patients over time, has been associated with lower mortality rates6, fewer hospital admissions7, less use of emergency department visits8, and fewer referrals for specialist consultation9. To date, no studies have been published on the prognostic impact of PCP-COC on hospitalization and mortality in patients with HF.

Our hypothesis is that an improvement in PCP-COC in HF patients is associated with lower rates of hospitalization and mortality. The primary objective of our study was to assess the prognostic impact associated with the COC, defined as care provided by the same PCP, in patients with HF referred for cardiology consultation.

Methods

The study was approved by the local Ethics Committee on March 23, 2022 (Reference: 2021/496).

Patients

All patients with a prior diagnosis of HF referred from PC to the cardiology service in our healthcare area (Santiago de Compostela, Galician-Spain) between January 2010 and December 2023 (n = 7,243) were included for analysis. The HF diagnosis was made with codification in PC (ICPC-2: K77) or previous hospitalization (ICD-9 −428.0, 428.1, 428.20, 428.21, 428.22, 428.23, 428.30, 428.32, 428.33, 428.40, 428.42, 428.43, and 428.9- or ICD-10 - I50, I50.43, I50.02, and I50.813-). Patients with origins outside the healthcare area and those without information from their PCP or referring physician were excluded. A final valid sample for analysis of 5,899 patients was obtained.

Our healthcare area provides medical assistance in 46 municipalities (446,603 inhabitants), where 301 doctors work in 56 health centers and 21 clinics. Hospital services, particularly the cardiology department, serve the entire healthcare area, offering scheduled outpatient consultations at hospitals in the capital and a regional hospital. Like other countries in our region (United Kingdom, Netherlands, Norway, or Denmark), each citizen has an assigned PCP, freely accessible for all health concerns. The group of citizens assigned to a PCP is called a “quota.”

Ideally, this model’s main advantage is COC, as it establishes a longitudinal relationship between the PCP and patients, facilitating a better understanding of individual health problems and their social and family context. Occasionally, for various reasons (work permits, illness absences, or voluntary transfers), the PCP assigned to the quota must be replaced to maintain patient care. This guarantees assistance but interrupts COC, especially in quotas where several PCP have rotated over time.

Variables

We recorded epidemiological variables (sex and age), clinical comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, recent myocardial infarction, heart failure, and the rate of hospital admissions for heart failure, stroke, peripheral arterial disease, chronic kidney disease, valvular disease, and cancer), primary care center characteristics (municipality of origin, health center or clinic, and PCP assigned to the patient), and referral characteristics to the cardiology service (e-consultation, in-person consultation, and consultation outcome).

The healthcare system in our country is based on the principle that every citizen entitled to healthcare is assigned a PCP, who is responsible for all their healthcare needs at this level and must refer them to other specialists when deemed necessary for more complex patient care. Ideally, the main advantage of this model is that the same patients are always attended by the same PCP, who in turn knows the patients better because they always attend to the same group of citizens (this group of people is called a quota). The main role of the PCP is to follow up on the majority of the patient’s consultations throughout their life, which is known as longitudinal continuity5; this is what we will refer to as COC.

Traditionally, COC has been analyzed using the patient’s contacts at the health center, such as the Bice-Boxerman Continuity of Care Index (BBI)10 or Usual Provider of Care (UPC) index5, without considering whether it is the PCP assigned to the patient or a different one. Therefore, these indexes are limited because they do not ensure that care is provided by the same PCP and do not accurately measure continuity from a longitudinal and interpersonal perspective.

In previous publications by our group11, we used two calculations: 1) PCP rate in a quota, which reaches values between 1 (we consider high turnover of PCP in the quota when it was higher than 0.7) and 0 (we consider PCP stability in the quota when it was lower than 0.3) and allows us to know the turnover of different PCP in a quota:

2) Quota rate for PCP, which reaches values between 1 (we consider high rotation of a PCP in several quotes when it was higher than 0.7) and 0 (we consider PCP stability in the quota or low rotation in quote when it was lower than 0.3) that reflects the rotation of a specific PCP in different positions, therefore it does not maintain continuity of care for the same patients either:

With these calculations, we can define 3 situations: (a) PCP stability: the same doctor regularly treats the patient, close to 0 in both equations; and other two situations related with the interrupted COC (b) rotation of different PCPs in a quota, PCP rate in a quota close of 1; and (c) rotation of a PCP in different quotas, defined by a quota rate close of 111.

The outcomes analyzed were hospitalizations and mortality at 1-year after consultation, for all-cause, and caused by cardiovascular (CV) diseases or HF disease.

Statistical analysis

Qualitative variables were expressed as percentages (%) and continuous variables, after confirming normally distributed with Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, as mean [standard deviation -SD-]. For hypothesis testing between groups (PCP stability vs. interrupted COC) chi-square, for qualitative variables, or t-student tests, for continuous variables, were used.

To estimate the association between each of continuity parameters and hospitalization and mortality outcomes (all-cause, cardiovascular, and HF) at 1-year after consultation, we used Cox regression to obtain hazard ratio (HR) with confidence interval at 95% (CI95%) and survival curves. The model was adjusted for variables that could influence the prognosis: age, sex, personal history (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, recent myocardial infarction, and the rate of hospital admissions for HF, stroke, peripheral arterial disease, chronic kidney disease, valvular disease, and cancer), geographical parameters (distance to the hospital), and consultation characteristics (e-consultation, consultation outcome, etc.).

These data were analyzed using SPSS, version 27.0 (SPSS Inc., United States).

Results

During the analysis period, 7,243 patients with a prior diagnosis of HF were referred. Of these, 2,033 patients were excluded from the analysis because they were referred to by hospital services or the PCP information was unavailable. Finally, 5,899 patients were analyzed, of whom 4,762 (80.7%) corresponded to PCP stability. No differences were observed in sex (p = 0.133), age (p = 0.476), or cardiovascular risk factors or pathologies (Table 1).

Discussion

The results presented here, from a cohort of 5,899 patients comprising all patients in our health area with a diagnosis of HF referred by PCPs for cardiology consultation from 2010, showed a significant association between PCP stability and a reduction in PCP referrals and hospitalizations and mortality rates. COC interruption, defined by PCP rotation in the same quota or PCP rotation in different quotae, was associated with higher rates of HF, CV, and all-cause hospitalizations and mortality.

To our knowledge, these findings describe for the first time an association between COC by the same PCP and prognosis (hospitalization and mortality) in patients with HF. We believe that our results may have important implications for the management of patients with HF, as COC by the same healthcare professional is associated with improved outcomes.

A previous publication has shown a significant reduction in mortality in patients with CV diseases associated with the number of PCPs in a health area. This association was of the same magnitude as that reported for the number of cardiologists in the area 12. Our results extend this observation, showing that not only the number of PCPs but also PCP stability was associated with a significant benefit in patients with HF referred for cardiology consultation12.

A comprehensive patient care management program in HF needs to include all stakeholders involved in HF process13. Beyond guideline recommendations, implementation efforts at the local level are needed, tailored to the health area’s characteristics and available resources. Main objectives include implementing guideline recommendations for treatment and clinical management to prolong survival, maintain clinical stability, and reduce the risk of hospitalization and functional class deterioration.

For transition of care after hospital discharge following a worsening HF episode, a structured contact with a healthcare professional is recommended within 2 weeks, along with the implementation of treatment strategies associated with improved prognosis1. In the STRONG (Safety, Tolerability, and Efficacy of Rapid Optimization, Helped by NT-proBNP Testing, of Heart Failure Therapies) trial, serial measurements of NT-proBNP, along with physical examination and assessment of symptoms and signs of congestion during frequent follow-up visits to optimize guideline recommended medical therapy (GRMT) for HF, were used to rapidly implement GRMT in patients recently hospitalized for HF. This strategy reduced symptoms, improved quality of life, and reduced the risk of 180-day all-cause death or heart failure readmission compared with usual care14.

Several recent publications have shown that the time to cardiology consultation after a PCP referral in patients with HF was associated with 1-year outcomes (hospitalization and mortality)4. A delay of more than 7 days for a cardiologist visit was linearly associated with increased hospitalization and mortality rates4. These results reinforce the need for local programs to optimize the management of HF patients, with the aim of early identification of worsening episodes or other intercurrent events that lead to poor outcomes. On the other hand, the implementation of HF management programs based on digital health technologies that facilitate communication between healthcare professionals involved in the patient’s care has demonstrated a favorable impact on outcomes15. Electronic consultation, integrated into a shared electronic medical record, among healthcare professionals, particularly PCPs and hospital specialists, may be useful for organizing patient follow-up, improving access to specialized care and outcomes.

Several studies have described the influence of COC by the same healthcare professional, usually defined as care provided by the same PCP, on the efficiency of the health system and patient outcomes. A recent publication by Sandvik et al. assessed the influence of COC disruption, defined as PCP change, on mortality following an organizational reform in the Norwegian Public Health System. COC by the same PCP was associated with an increase in mortality, hospitalizations, and the need for emergency visits compared with the group of patients managed by a different healthcare professional16. Other studies describe a reduction in mortality and hospitalizations associated with COC in primary care11. One publication described the association between patient follow-up at the same primary health center, regardless of the PCP, and mortality in patients after hospital discharge, compared with the group with frequent changes in primary care centers17; and Baker et al. described a reduction in hospitalizations associated with continuity of primary care in more than 230,000 patients8.

Nevertheless, the results presented here describe for the first time a favorable prognostic effect of COC by the same PCP in patients with HF, expressed as a reduction in mortality and hospitalizations (all-cause, CV, and HF-related), compared with changes in the primary healthcare professional in charge of the patient’s care. On the other hand, COC by the same PCP was associated with a reduced rate of cardiology referrals compared with that observed in the group of patients with rotation of primary care healthcare professionals. As mentioned previously, our findings may have implications for HF care programs and underscore the relevance of the primary care system for optimizing the follow-up of patients with HF.

Although we acknowledge certain limitations in the analysis of our results, the data from this large cohort of patients from 2010, with demographic, clinical, and prognostic information integrated into an electronic medical record system, strengthen the relevance of the healthcare management aspects of our findings in patients with HF. Furthermore, the influence of COC on patient outcomes was based on the analysis of six prognostic determinants (all-cause, CV, and HF-related hospitalizations and mortality), which improves the robustness of our work. The lack of knowledge of the specific reasons for PCP referrals or the impossibility of identifying whether patients had contact with professionals outside the National Healthcare System may influence our results. Nevertheless, private healthcare is very limited in our region. We had some limitations in determining the cause of death in some cases, particularly for the year 2023. This information was obtained from administrative databases, and the record may be incomplete in some cases for this year.

Our study on COC in primary care may provide a more complete analysis, as other publications have only analyzed COC based on the number of patients’ visits to the same PCP. Therefore, COC analysis based on PCP referrals to a cardiology department likely reflects changes in patients’ clinical conditions in a HF population and allows a more precise assessment of the associations between COC and patient outcomes.

We can conclude that a healthcare system organization that facilitates COC, defined as the continuity of the same PCP responsible for the care of a group of patients with HF, was associated with better outcomes. This is evidenced by a reduction in referrals for cardiology care and lower rates of hospitalizations and mortality in a highly vulnerable group of patients. Our results may have implications for the characteristics of HF management programs, where COC by PCPs appears to play a significant role in patient outcomes.

The Sample Complexity of Distributed Simple Binary Hypothesis Testing under Information Constraints
Hadi Kazemi, Ankit Pensia, Varun Jog

Abstract
This paper resolves two open problems from a recent paper [ PJL24 ] concerning the sample complexity of distributed simple binary hypothesis testing under information constraints. The first open problem asks whether interaction reduces the sample complexity of distributed simple binary hypothesis testing. In this paper, we show that sequential interaction does not help. The second problem suggests tightening existing sample complexity bounds for communication-constrained simple binary hypothesis testing. We derive optimally tight bounds for this setting and resolve this problem. Our main technical contributions are: (i) a one-shot lower bound on the Bayes error in simple binary hypothesis testing that satisfies a crucial tensorisation property; (ii) a streamlined proof of the formula for the sample complexity of simple binary hypothesis testing without constraints, first established in [ PJL24 ] ; and (iii) a reverse data-processing inequality for Hellinger- λ \lambda divergences, generalising the results from [ BNOP21 ] and [ PJL23 ] . 1 1 1 A 1-page extended abstract of this paper, titled “The Sample Complexity of Simple Binary Hypothesis Testing: Tight Bounds with Sequential Interactivity and Information Constraints,” appears in the Proceedings of the Conference on Learning Theory, 2025.



Keywords
Sample complexity, hypothesis testing, data-processing inequality

Introduction

Hypothesis testing is one of the central problems in statistics that concerns testing different hypotheses based on observed data. The most basic version of this problem is simple binary hypothesis testing, where we wish to decide between two hypotheses
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
. Under hypothesis
H_{0}
(resp.
H_{1}
) we observe i.i.d. samples from distribution
Q
(resp.
P

and

are known. The Bayes formulation of simple binary hypothesis testing, which is the focus of this paper, assumes that the true hypothesis is chosen randomly according to a prior Bernoulli distribution
\mathrm{Ber}(\pi)
. This is a classical problem that has been studied for over a century, and we highlight some important results.

•

Optimal algorithm:
The maximum a posteriori-rule (MAP-rule) minimises the error probability. In the prior-free setting, threshold tests are optimal
[
NP33
]

•

Asymptotic error probability:
The error probability decays exponentially, with the exponents characterised by information-theoretic measures such as the Kullback–Leibler divergence or Chernoff information
[
PW25
, Chapter 16]
.

•

Sample complexity:
The sample-size needed to achieve an error of

[

]
derived a formula for all

In addition to identifying the sample complexity,

]

[

]

Hypothesis testing under information constraints:

Suppose each of

\delta

Preliminaries

We provide some basic definitions and facts about hypothesis testing and
f
-divergences that are needed to discuss our results.

Definition 2

(
f
-divergence
[
PW25
]
)
.

Let
f:[0,\infty]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be a convex function with
f(1)=0
. For two distributions

D_{f}(P\parallel Q)=\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[f\left(\frac{dP}{dQ}(Z)\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\frac{dP}{dQ}(Z)<\infty\}}\right]+f^{\prime}(\infty)P\left(\frac{dP}{dQ}(Z)=\infty\right).

(4)

Fact 1

(Convexity of
f
-divergences)
.

All f-divergences are jointly convex in both inputs.

Definition 3

(Hellinger-
\lambda
divergence)
.

For any
\lambda\in(0,1)
we define the Hellinger-
\lambda
divergence between two distributions

and

H_{\lambda}(P,Q)=1-\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[\left(\frac{dP}{dQ}\right)^{\lambda}\right]
. We also define
\beta_{\lambda}(P,Q)=\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[\left(\frac{dP}{dQ}\right)^{\lambda}\right]=1-H_{\lambda}(P,Q)
to be the Hellinger-
\lambda
affinity. Hellinger-
\lambda
is an
f
-divergence with
f(x)=1-x^{\lambda}
.

Fact 2

(Tensorisation of Hellinger affinity)
.

Hellinger affinity tensorises; i.e., for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
and any
P
,
Q
any
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\beta_{\lambda}(P^{\otimes n},Q^{\otimes n})=\left(\beta_{\lambda}(P,Q)\right)^{n}.

Fact 3

(Inequality between Hellinger-
\lambda
divergences
[
Bar02
]
)
.

For any pair of distributions
\mu
and
\nu
and

\forall\ 0<\alpha<\beta<1
, we have

\frac{\alpha}{\beta}H_{\beta}(\mu,\nu)\leq H_{\alpha}(\mu,\nu)\leq\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\beta}H_{\beta}(\mu,\nu)
.

Fact 4

(Optimal Bayes error)
.

Consider
\mathcal{B}_{B}(P,Q,\pi,\delta)
as in Definition
1
. Let
e_{\pi}(P,Q)
denote the minimum error probability over all estimators
\hat{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
based on a single sample
X
. The following facts are folklore
[
HR70
]
:

1.

The error of the optimal estimation is

e_{\pi}(P,Q)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\min\{\pi P(x),\bar{\pi}Q(x)\}.

2.

For any
\lambda\in[0,1]
we have

e_{\pi}(P,Q)\leq\pi^{\lambda}{\bar{\pi}}^{\bar{\lambda}}\beta_{\lambda}(P,Q).

Notation:

For two real-valued non-negative functions on an arbitrary space

to indicate that there exists universal constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0

\ C_{1}g(x)\leq f(x)\leq C_{2}g(x)
for all

. We use

to indicate that there is a universal constant
C>0
such that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
; The relation
f(x)\gtrsim g(x)
is defined analogously.
For

Main Results

Our first result is a lower bound on

from

Theorem 1

(One-shot lower bound on the Bayes error (informal))
.

Let

be a pair of distributions and
\pi\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
. Also, let

\delta\geq\frac{1}{4}\pi^{\lambda_{*}}\bar{\pi}^{\bar{\lambda_{*}}}\left(\beta_{\lambda_{*}}(P,Q)\right)^{2}.

(5)

The inequality in

in the literature, such as Fano’s bound, is that the right hand side tensorises. This makes it an ideal tool for deriving lower bounds on the sample complexity. A particular application to

and

leads to our next result,

[

]

Theorem 2

(Sample complexity of simple binary hypothesis testing)
.

Consider
\mathcal{B}_{B}(P,Q,\pi,\delta)
as in

where
\pi\in(0,0.5]

\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{16}\pi]

\lambda_{*}=\frac{\log(\frac{\bar{\pi}}{\delta})}{\log(\frac{\bar{\pi}}{\delta})+\log(\frac{\pi}{\delta})}\in[0.5,1)

The above theorem reproduces all the results about the sample complexity of simple binary hypothesis testing in

Motivated by the study of the sample complexity of simple binary hypothesis testing with sequential interaction and information constraints, in this section, we prove a one-shot lower bound on the error probability of simple binary hypothesis testing problem. 4

In light of
3

\inf_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\beta_{\lambda_{*}}(TP,TQ)

\mathcal{T}_{D}
as the set of all channels from
\mathcal{X}
to

Definition 4

(Threshold channels)
.

We say

\frac{dP}{dQ}(x)\in I_{i}

Observe that minimising the Hellinger affinity is equivalent to maximising the Hellinger divergence. Standard data-processing results imply that

H_{\lambda_{*}}(TP,TQ)\leq H_{\lambda_{*}}(P,Q).

We require lower bounds on

]

1/2

Definition 5

(TV-like

We say

1.

There exist

(b,\infty)

C_{1}x\leq f(x)\leq C_{2}x

Some notable f-divergences that satisfy the definition above are

Conclusion

In this paper, we resolved two open problems related to the sample complexity of simple binary hypothesis testing. We showed that sequential interaction is of limited help, and we fully characterised the sample complexity under communication constraints. Our main technical tools, namely, the one-shot lower bound on Bayes error and the reverse data-processing inequality for
f
-divergences, are likely to have other applications as well, such as tightening Le Cam-style lower bounds in statistical estimation. There are several future research directions that can arise from this work. The question of whether the fully-interactive setting, also called the blackboard protocol (see
[
KN96
]
) significantly reduces the sample complexity is still open. Finally, it would be interesting to see if the Bayes error lower bound from
1
can be modified so that a lower bound can be computed without knowing the Bayes error in the first place. Such a bound could be tighter than Fano’s information-theoretic lower bound and have numerous applications in statistical estimation and testing.

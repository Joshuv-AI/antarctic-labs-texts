Bipartite quantum states admitting a causal explanation
Minjeong Song, Arthur J. Parzygnat

Abstract
The statistics of local measurements of joint quantum systems can sometimes be used to distinguish the temporal order in which they were measured. We first prove that every bipartite separable density matrix is temporally compatible with direct causal influence for arbitrary finite-dimensional quantum systems and measurements of a tomographically-complete class of observables, which includes all Pauli observables in the case of multi-qubit systems. Equivalently, if a bipartite density matrix is not temporally compatible with direct causal influence, then it must be entangled. We also provide an operational meaning for the temporal evolution consistent with such correlations in terms of a generalized dephasing channel and a pretty good measurement. The two temporal evolutions turn out to not be Petz recovery maps of each other, but are Bayesian inverses of each other. Finally, we prove necessary and sufficient conditions for an arbitrary bipartite quantum state to be temporally compatible, thereby providing a temporal analogue of the positive partial transpose criterion valid for quantum systems of any dimension.

It is crucial to note that a pseudo-density matrix could be compatible with multiple causal structures. In such a case, we cannot distinguish one causal structure from another in principle using these passive methods, thus giving a fundamental limitation on inferring quantum causal structures. That said, there still remains a quantum advantage for inferring quantum causal structure as compared to the analogous situation in classical probability and statistics

These two points illustrate that it is important to identify when we can and cannot distinguish these causal structures.

A simple example of expectation values that are both spatially and temporally compatible can be obtained from product states (i.e., statistically independent quantum systems) by virtue of there being no correlation. However, it was left as a conjecture in Ref.

In other words, do the set of pseudo-density matrices that are both spatially and temporally compatible contain the set of separable states? Moreover, one could ask which mixed quantum states (possibly entangled) are both spatially and temporally compatible

Remark II.5
.

One could justify calling temporal channels
conditionals
due to the fact that they provide analogues of conditional probabilities
Parzygnat 2021
;
Parzygnat 2020
;
Parzygnat 2017
. In fact, such conditionals indeed satisfy a quantum generalization of Bayes’ rule, as shown in Ref.
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2023
;
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2024
.

We next relate the causal structures above to the representability of calculating such expectation values.
Intuitively, the representability means that the expectation values can be calculated directly from the observables and a single matrix valid for those observables, without having to know the causal structure.
In the following definition, a collection of observables in an algebra is said to be
tomographically complete
iff it forms a spanning set of the algebra, where one takes complex linear combinations to define the span.

Definition II.6
.

Let
\mathpzc{A}
and
\mathpzc{B}
be two algebras. Let
\{M_{a}\}
and
\{N_{b}\}
be tomographically-complete sets of observables on
\mathpzc{A}
and
\mathpzc{B}
, respectively. An associated collection of expectation values
\langle M_{a},N_{b}\rangle\in{{\mathbb{R}}}
is
representable
for the observables
\{M_{a}\}
and
\{N_{b}\}
iff there exists a matrix
R\in\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
such that

{\rm Tr}\big[R(M_{a}\otimes N_{b})\big]=\langle M_{a},N_{b}\rangle

(16)

for all
a,b
. In such a case, we say that the expectation values
\langle M_{a},N_{b}\rangle
are
represented
by
R
.

As an immediate example, we know that if the expectation values are spatially compatible, then they are representable by a joint density matrix
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}\mathpzc{B}}
for
all
observables in
\mathpzc{A}
and
\mathpzc{B}
.
However, expectation values coming from direct causal influence are not representable for
all
observables in general. This is why the above definition of representability is made for general tomographically-complete
sets
of observables, but not necessarily for their real linear combinations, and hence not necessarily
all
observables.
A simple example illustrating this point is given in Example 2.11 (and generalized in Theorem 3.2) in Ref.
Fullwood and Parzygnat 2024
. Namely, let
\mathpzc{A}=\mathpzc{B}=\mathbb{M}_{2}
, and let
(\mathcal{E},\rho_{\mathpzc{A}})
be the process given by
\mathcal{E}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{A}}
and
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}=|-\rangle\langle-|=\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. Then the two-time expectation values
\langle M_{a},N_{b}\rangle
given by (
14
) are
not
linear in the left coordinate
M_{a}
, while the expression on the left of (
16
)
is
linear in
M_{a}
, thus leading to a contradiction (an explicit pair of observables illustrating lack of linearity is presented in Ref.
Fullwood and Parzygnat 2024
).

Note, however, that when the sets of observables in Definition
II.6
are taken to be Pauli observables for systems of qubits (and
not
their linear combinations), then the pseudo-density matrix represents their expectation values
Fitzsimons et al. 2015
. A similar statement can be made for arbitrary quantum systems with light-touch observables
Fullwood and Parzygnat 2024
.
Namely, Proposition
II.4
says that if the observables are light-touch observables and if the expectation values are temporally compatible, then they are represented by the hermitian matrix

\mathcal{E}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}:=\frac{1}{2}\big\{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}},\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{E}]\big\}

(17)

in the case that
\mathpzc{A}
has a direct influence on
\mathpzc{B}
and

\gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\big\{1_{\mathpzc{A}}\otimes\rho_{\mathpzc{B}},\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{F}^{*}]\big\}

(18)

in the case that
\mathpzc{B}
has a direct influence on
\mathpzc{A}
, where
\gamma
is the swap map defined by unique linear extension of

\gamma(B\otimes A):=A\otimes B.

(19)

Equation (
18
) follows from Lemma 2 in Appendix A of Ref.
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2023
.
The representability of these three causal structures for light-touch observables will allow us to prove interesting relations between separability, entanglement, and temporal compatibility, as we show in the next section.

III
Spatial versus temporal correlations

III.1
Separability implies temporal compatibility

Theorem III.1
.

Let
\tau
be a separable density matrix in
\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
and set
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}[\tau]
. Assume
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
is a faithful state, i.e.,
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}>0
. Then there exists a unique CPTP map
\mathpzc{A}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}\mathpzc{B}
such that
\tau=\mathcal{E}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
. Similarly, if
\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}:={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{A}}[\tau]>0
, then there exists a unique CPTP map
\mathpzc{B}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\mathpzc{A}
such that
\tau=\gamma(\mathcal{F}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{B}})
.

Theorem
III.1
says that the expectation values associated with separable density matrices in
\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
are always temporally compatible, thereby resolving an open question in Ref.
Song et al. 2024
. The assumption
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}>0
is not needed in this theorem and
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}\geq 0
is sufficient to guarantee existence, but not necessarily uniqueness (see Appendix
D
for details).
To prove Theorem
III.1
, we will make use of the following lemmas and proposition.

Lemma III.2
.

Let
\{p_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,m}
be a collection of real strictly positive numbers. Then the
m\times m
matrix

\sum_{i,j}\frac{|i\rangle\langle j|}{p_{i}+p_{j}}

(20)

is a positive matrix.

Proof of Lemma
III.2
.

Such a matrix is called a Cauchy matrix, which is known to be positive (see Exercise 1.1.2 in Ref.
Bhatia 2007
for example).
∎

For the next lemma, we recall that the
Hadamard–Schur product

\odot
on square matrices of the same dimension is given by

\langle i|A\odot B|j\rangle=\langle i|A|j\rangle\langle i|B|j\rangle.

(21)

Lemma III.3
.

The Hadamard–Schur product of two positive matrices is positive.

Proof of Lemma
III.3
.

This is called the the Schur Product Theorem (see Exercise 1.2.5 in Ref.
Bhatia 2007
). ∎

Proposition III.4
.

Let
\mathpzc{A}=\mathbb{M}_{m}
and
\mathpzc{B}=\mathbb{M}_{n}
.
Let
\tau
be any hermitian trace-1 matrix in
\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
and set
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}[\tau]
. Assume
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
is a faithful state, i.e.,
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}>0
. Then there exists a unique HPTP map
\mathpzc{A}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}\mathpzc{B}
such that
\tau=\mathcal{E}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
. Moreover, an explicit formula for
\mathcal{E}
is given by the unique linear extension of the map that satisfies

\mathcal{E}\big(|i\rangle\langle j|\big)=\frac{2}{p_{i}+p_{j}}{\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{A}}\Big[\tau\big(|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}\big)\Big]

(22)

for all

, where

is an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors for
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
, i.e.,
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}|i\rangle\langle i|
, with
\{p_{i}\}
a probability distribution.

We note that the channel
\mathcal{E}
obtained from linearly extending (

for
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
. This will be explained in the proof of Proposition

The more general case where

Proof of Proposition
III.4
.

Let us check that the formula (
22
) for
\mathcal{E}
provides the unique solution to
\tau=\mathcal{E}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
in the case that
\tau
is an arbitrary hermitian matrix with trace equal to
1
. If such a map
\mathcal{E}
were to exist, it must necessarily satisfy

\tau=\mathcal{E}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}=\frac{1}{2}\big\{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}},\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{E}]\big\}.

(23)

This equation is of the form
B=AX+XA
, which is known as the Sylvester (sometimes Lyapunov) equation
Bhatia and Rosenthal 1997
. When
A
is positive definite, it has a unique solution
X
given by
X=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tA}Be^{-tA}\,dt
. Hence,
\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{E}]
can be expressed as

\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{E}]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\frac{t}{2}\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}\right)\tau\left(e^{-\frac{t}{2}\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}\right)dt.

(24)

Combining this with the fact that
\mathscr{J}
is an isomorphism, there exists a unique (well-defined) linear map
\mathcal{E}
satisfying (
23
).
By choosing an orthonormal eigenbasis
\{|i\rangle\}
of
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
, we can obtain an alternative expression for
\mathcal{E}
. Namely, rewriting (
23
) in this basis gives

\tau=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}(p_{i}+p_{j})|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\mathcal{E}\big(|j\rangle\langle i|\big).

(25)

Multiplying by
|k\rangle\langle l|\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}
(on either the right or the left) and taking the partial trace over
\mathpzc{A}
gives

{\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{A}}\Big[\tau\big(|k\rangle\langle l|\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}\big)\Big]=\frac{p_{k}+p_{l}}{2}\mathcal{E}\big(|k\rangle\langle l|\big),

(26)

from which the claimed formula (
22

follows.
∎

A manifestly basis-independent formula for
\mathcal{E}
can be obtained from (
24

\mathcal{E}(A)={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{A}}\big[\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{E}](A\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}})\big]

Proof of Theorem
III.1
.

We will prove this theorem in the special case where
\mathpzc{A}
and

are matrix algebras, say
\mathpzc{A}=\mathbb{M}_{m}

\mathcal{E}\big(|i\rangle\langle j|\big)=\frac{2}{p_{i}+p_{j}}\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}\langle j|\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}|i\rangle\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}.

The Choi matrix of

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}[\mathcal{E}]

∎

An immediate consequence of Theorem
III.1
is temporal compatibility in both temporal directions for separable states.

Corollary III.5
.

Let
\tau
be a separable density matrix in
\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
and set
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}[\tau]
and
\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{A}}[\tau]
. Assume
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}>0
and
\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}>0
. Then
\tau
is temporally compatible in both directions, i.e., there exist unique CPTP maps
\mathpzc{A}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}\mathpzc{B}
and
\mathpzc{B}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\mathpzc{A}
such that
\tau=\mathcal{E}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
and
\gamma(\tau)=\mathcal{F}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}
.

We can rephrase this corollary in terms of its contrapositive providing a sufficient condition for entanglement.

Corollary III.6
.

Let
\tau
be a density matrix in
\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
, and suppose
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}:={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}[\tau]>0
and
\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}:={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}[\tau]>0
. If
\tau
is
not
temporally compatible in at least one direction, then
\tau
must be entangled.

We note, however, that it is known that there exist entangled states that are temporally compatible
Song et al. 2024
. Therefore, Corollary
III.5
only gives a sufficient condition for temporal compatibility.
We will come back to necessary and sufficient conditions later in this work.

A

1

Equivalently, an

is a correlation matrix whenever there exist
n

|v_{i}\rangle\in{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}
such that
\langle i|C|j\rangle=\langle v_{i}|v_{j}\rangle
for all
i,j=1,\dots,n
, i.e.,

C=\sum_{i,j}\langle v_{i}|v_{j}\rangle|i\rangle\langle j|=\begin{bmatrix}\text{\raisebox{5.0pt}{\uline{\hskip 11.38109pt}}}\!&\!v_{1}^{{\dagger}}\!&\!\text{\raisebox{5.0pt}{\uline{\hskip 11.38109pt}}}\\
&\vdots&\\
\text{\raisebox{5.0pt}{\uline{\hskip 11.38109pt}}}\!&\!v_{n}^{{\dagger}\!}&\!\text{\raisebox{5.0pt}{\uline{\hskip 11.38109pt}}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}|&&|\\
v_{1}&\cdots&v_{n}\\
|&&|\end{bmatrix}.

(35)

This equivalence follows directly from the Cholesky test for positive semidefinite matrices
Strang 2022
.
In other words, an
n\times n
correlation matrix is the
Gram matrix
associated with a set of
n
normalized vectors.
In what follows, we will say that a correlation matrix is
strict
when it is strictly positive, which implies its off-diagonal entries have magnitude strictly less than
1
(otherwise the subdeterminant would vanish). Note that a correlation matrix
C
is strict whenever there exists a normalized
basis
(though not necessarily orthonormal)
\{|v_{i}\rangle\}
such that
\langle i|C|j\rangle=\langle v_{i}|v_{j}\rangle
for all
i,j
.
Using a correlation matrix with the Hadamard–Schur product yields a
Hadamard–Schur channel
, which is a quantum channel that is given by the Hadamard–Schur product with some matrix with respect to some orthonormal basis
Watrous 2018
. The following is the special case when the matrix is a correlation matrix.

Definition III.8
.

Let
\mathpzc{A}=\mathbb{M}_{m}
. A
generalized dephasing channel
on
\mathpzc{A}
is a Hadamard–Schur channel
\mathpzc{A}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}\mathpzc{A}
of the form

\mathcal{D}(A)=C\odot A=\sum_{i,j}\langle i|C|j\rangle\langle i|A|j\rangle|i\rangle\langle j|,

(36)

where
C
is some correlation matrix with respect to some basis, called the
dephasing basis
,
\{|i\rangle\}
of
{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}
.

We note that a dephasing channel
\mathcal{D}
as defined in (
36
) is in fact completely positive by Proposition 4.17 in Ref.
Watrous 2018
. Moreover,
\mathcal{D}
is also trace-preserving since

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\big[\mathcal{D}(A)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}\langle i|C|j\rangle\langle i|A|j\rangle\langle k|i\rangle\langle j|k\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle i|C|i\rangle\langle i|A|i\rangle={\rm Tr}[A]

(37)

for all
A\in\mathpzc{A}
because
\langle i|C|i\rangle=1
for all
i
. Hence,
\mathcal{D}
is a quantum channel.

Our definition of a generalized dephasing channel is equivalent to that given in Refs.
Tomamichel et al. 2017
;
Devetak and Shor 2005
and Section 5.2 of Ref.
Wilde 2016
due to the equivalence between a correlation matrix and its expression as a Gram matrix as in (
35
). The motivation for this definition of a generalized dephasing channel combines the following observations. First, for any density matrix
\rho_{d}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|i\rangle\langle i|
that is diagonal in the dephasing basis, we have
\mathcal{D}(\rho_{d})=\rho_{d}
.
Moreover, if
C
is a strict correlation matrix, then for an arbitrary density matrix
\rho
(possibly with off-diagonal terms),

\mathcal{D}(\rho)=\sum_{i,j}\langle i|C|j\rangle\langle i|\rho|j\rangle|i\rangle\langle j|.

This implies that the off-diagonal terms of
\rho
decrease in magnitude after the application of
\mathcal{D}
since
|\langle i|C|j\rangle|<1
for all
i\neq j
. Hence, by the strictness condition on the correlation matrix,

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\underbrace{\mathcal{D}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{D}}_{\text{$n$ times}})(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle i|\rho|i\rangle|i\rangle\langle i|

(38)

for all density matrices
\rho
in
\mathpzc{A}
. Thus, iterating a generalized dephasing channel causes any input density matrix to converge towards the density matrix obtained from just its diagonal entries, thereby removing coherence terms in
\rho
with respect to the dephasing basis.
If
C
is not strict, then certain subspaces formed by the dephasing basis cause density matrices to retain coherences within those subspaces.

Lemma III.9
.

Given any probability distribution
\{p_{i}\}
, the matrix

H=\sum_{i,j}\frac{2\sqrt{p_{i}p_{j}}}{p_{i}+p_{j}}|i\rangle\langle j|

(39)

is a correlation matrix.

The
ij
entry
\langle i|H|j\rangle
of the matrix
H
in Lemma
III.9
represents the
harmonic mean
of
p_{i}
and
p_{j}

Bhatia 2007
.

Proof of Lemma
III.9
.

First note that the diagonal entries of
H
are all equal to
1
. The rest follows from the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality

\frac{x+y}{2}\geq\sqrt{xy},

(40)

which holds for all nonnegative real numbers
x,y

Bhatia 2007
.
∎

Having established these definitions and lemmas, notice that
\mathcal{D}
from Equation (
32
) is a generalized dephasing channel of the form

\mathcal{D}(A)=H\odot A,

(41)

where
H
is given by (
39
) and
A\in\mathpzc{A}
is arbitrary.

The next ingredient in Theorem
III.7
is the map
\mathcal{G}
, which represents a pretty good measure-and-prepare channel and appears in the context of quantum state discrimination protocols
Bae and Kwek 2015
, which we discuss later in Section
III.4
. We first recall measure-and-prepare channels.

Given algebras
\mathpzc{A}
and
\mathpzc{B}
, a
measure-and-prepare channel
from
\mathpzc{A}
to
\mathpzc{B}
is a channel
\mathpzc{A}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}\mathpzc{B}
of the form

\mathcal{E}(A)=\sum_{\theta}{\rm Tr}[E_{\theta}A]\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta},

(42)

where
\theta
is an element of a finite index set,
\{E_{\theta}\}
is a positive operator-valued measure (POVM)
Kraus 1983
;
Nielsen and Chuang 2011
, and
\{\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}\}
is a collection of density matrices in
\mathpzc{B}
.
Such a channel can be interpreted as taking an input state
\rho
, measuring outcome
\theta
with probability
{\rm Tr}[E_{\theta}\rho]
, and then preparing the state
\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}
conditioned on measuring the outcome
\theta
. Note that the Choi matrix of measure-and-prepare channel is of the form

\mathscr{C}[\mathcal{E}]=\sum_{\theta}E_{\theta}^{T}\otimes\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta},

(43)

where the transpose is computed with respect to the same basis that the Choi matrix is defined in terms of.

By comparing (
43
) to the Choi matrix of the temporal channel
\mathcal{E}
in (
31
), we deduce that

\big\{E_{\theta}:=t_{\theta}(\omega\odot\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta})\big\}

(44)

defines a POVM, thereby giving an interpretation to
\mathcal{E}
as a measure-and-prepare channel of the form (
42
).
However, as we will see soon, we can provide a clearer interpretation of
\mathcal{E}
by splitting it up into a dephasing channel, as above, followed by a well-known measure-and-prepare channel given by the
pretty good measurement

Hausladen and Wootters 1994
.
We recall this definition next.

Given a finite index set
\Theta
and an algebra
\mathpzc{A}
, a
probabilistic ensemble of states
in
\mathpzc{A}
is a collection
\{\lambda_{\theta},\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\}
, where
\{\lambda_{\theta}\}
defines a probability distribution and each
\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}
is a density matrix in
\mathpzc{A}
. The
average state
associated with this ensemble is
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}:=\sum_{\theta}\lambda_{\theta}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}
. The
pretty good measurement
associated with this ensemble is the POVM
\{G_{\theta}\}
, where

G_{\theta}:=t_{\theta}\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(45)

We still assume
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}>0
here for simplicity (see Appendix
D
for the more general case
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}\geq 0
).
Because
\{G_{\theta}\}
defines a POVM, given a collection
\{\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}\}
of density matrices in
\mathpzc{B}
, the associated measure-and-prepare map

\mathcal{G}(A)=\sum_{\theta}{\rm Tr}[G_{\theta}A]\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}

is indeed a quantum channel.

We now have all the ingredients needed to prove Theorem
III.7
.

Proof of Theorem
III.7
.

Since we have already proved that
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{G}
from the statement of Theorem
III.7
are CPTP maps in the preceeding paragraphs, here we prove that
\mathcal{E}
is the composite of
\mathcal{D}
followed by
\mathcal{G}
. Using the measure-and-prepare formula (
42
) for
\mathcal{E}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(A)=\sum_{\theta}{\rm Tr}\left[\sum_{i,j}\frac{2\langle j|t_{\theta}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}|i\rangle}{p_{i}+p_{j}}|j\rangle\langle i|A\right]\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta}{\rm Tr}\left[t_{\theta}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\sum_{i,j}\frac{2}{p_{i}+p_{j}}|i\rangle\langle i|A|j\rangle\langle j|\right]\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta}{\rm Tr}\left[G_{\theta}\sum_{i,j}\frac{2}{p_{i}+p_{j}}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}{A}|i\rangle\langle i|A|j\rangle\langle j|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}{A}\right]\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta}{\rm Tr}\left[G_{\theta}\sum_{i,j}\frac{2\sqrt{p_{i}p_{j}}}{p_{i}+p_{j}}|i\rangle\langle i|A|j\rangle\langle j|\right]\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{D})(A),

(46)

where the third equality holds by cyclicity of trace, and
G_{\theta}=t_{\theta}\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is as in (
45
).
∎

Remark III.10
.

In the context of Theorem
III.7
, if
\tau\in\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
is a general state (not necessarily separable), it is still the case that
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is given by (
32
) and
\mathcal{G}
is given by (
33
). However,
\mathcal{G}
need not be a quantum channel when
\tau
is an arbitrary state. Nevertheless,
\mathcal{G}
is still a positive trace-preserving map (so it need not be
completely
positive). Positivity of
\mathcal{G}
can be seen by rewriting it as

\mathcal{G}(A)={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{A}}\Big[\Big(\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}\Big)^{{\dagger}}\tau\Big(\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}\Big)\Big],

(47)

which is positive because
{\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{A}}
is a positive map and because the term inside the square brackets is positive for all positive
A\in\mathpzc{A}
.
This extends the results of Ref.
Parzygnat 2021
, since a natural followup question to that work is if positive conditionals defined using (
17
) (cf. Remark
II.5
) always exist for bipartite states. The answer is yes, because
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{D}
is a composite of two positive trace-preserving maps.

Before concluding this subsection, we briefly discuss temporal compatibility in the other direction.
For the other temporal order with
\mathpzc{B}
having a direct influence on
\mathpzc{A}
, a similar interpretation holds for the temporal channel
\mathpzc{B}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\mathpzc{A}
constructed in a way analogous to
\mathcal{E}
. Namely,
\mathcal{F}
decomposes as

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\circ\mathcal{D}^{\prime},

where
\mathpzc{B}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\mathpzc{B}
is a dephasing channel with respect to an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors for
\rho{B}
and
\mathpzc{B}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}\mathpzc{A}
is the pretty good measure-and-prepare channel defined on
B\in\mathpzc{B}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(B)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}\Big[\tau\Big(1_{\mathpzc{A}}\otimes\big(\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}{\rm Tr}\Big[\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}{B}\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}{B}B\Big]\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}.

(48)

III.3
Temporal maps and retrodiction

Interestingly, the two channels
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
from (
34
) and (
48
), respectively, are Petz recovery maps of each other with the reduced states of
\tau
being respective prior states
Connes 1974
;
Accardi and Cecchini 1982
;
Petz 1984
;
Ohya and Petz 1993
;
Parzygnat and Buscemi 2023
. More precisely, the process
(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\rho_{\mathpzc{B}})\in\mathscr{P}(\mathpzc{B},\mathpzc{A})
is the Petz recovery process of
(\mathcal{G},\rho_{\mathpzc{A}})\in\mathscr{P}(\mathpzc{A},\mathpzc{B})
. This can be shown as follows.

First note that the Hilbert–Schmidt adjoint
\mathcal{G}^{*}
of the channel
\mathcal{G}(A)=\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}{\rm Tr}\big[\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}{A}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}{A}A\big]\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}
is given on
B\in\mathpzc{B}
by

\mathcal{G}^{*}(B)=\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}B]\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}{A}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}{A}.

(49)

Now let
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\rho{A}}
be the Petz recovery map of
\mathcal{G}
with respect to the prior state
{\rho{A}}
, i.e.,

\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}(B):=\rho{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}^{*}\Big(\mathcal{G}(\rho{A})^{-\frac{1}{2}}B\mathcal{G}(\rho{A})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\rho{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}

(50)

for all
B\in\mathpzc{B}
.
Since
\mathcal{G}(\rho{A})=\rho{B}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\rho{A}}(B)=\rho{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}^{*}\Big(\rho{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B\rho{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\rho{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\rho{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}{\rm Tr}\Big[\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}\rho{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B\rho{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big]\rho{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\rho{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\rho{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}{\rm Tr}\Big[\rho{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}\rho{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B\Big]\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}=\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(B),

(51)

where we have used (
49
) in the second equality and (
48
) in the third equality. This proves that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is the Petz recovery map of
\mathcal{G}
with respect to the prior state
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{G}
is the Petz recovery map of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
with respect to the prior state
\rho{B}
.

Despite this relationship between
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, we note that
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
are
not
in general Petz recovery maps of each other. Rather, they are
Bayesian inverses
of each other based on the notion of Bayesian inverse associated with the canonical state over time
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2023
, which is reviewed in Appendix
C
. Moreover, a relationship between the two is given by

\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\circ\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}\circ\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}\circ\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}\circ\mathcal{D}^{\prime},

(52)

where the first equality follows from Theorem
III.7
for
\mathcal{F}
, the second equality follows from the fact that the Petz recovery map of the generalized dephasing channel is itself, and the third equality follows from Theorem
III.7
for
\mathcal{E}
and functoriality of the Petz recovery map
Parzygnat and Buscemi 2023
;
Parzygnat 2024
;
Tsang 2023
;
Wilde 2015
. Equation (
52
) illustrates how the Petz recovery map
\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}
and Bayesian inverse
\mathcal{F}
of
(\mathcal{E},\rho_{\mathpzc{A}})
are related through the action of generalized dephasing channels. Therefore, although the Petz recovery map is often considered as the canonical expression extending Bayesian inversion and retrodiction to the quantum setting
Aw et al. 2021
;
Buscemi and Scarani 2021
;
Kwon and Kim 2019
;
Parzygnat and Buscemi 2023
;
Li and Winter 2018
;
Cotler et al. 2019
;
Leifer and Spekkens 2013
;
Aw et al. 2024
;
Coecke and Spekkens 2012
, more general Bayesian inverses as defined in Ref.
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2023
(see also Refs.
Tsang 2022
;
Tsang 2023
;
Scandi et al. 2025
;
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2024
) still admit several key characteristics of retrodiction
Barnett et al. 2000
;
Aharonov et al. 1964
;
Watanabe 1955
;
Jeffers et al. 2024
.

III.4
Relation to state discrimination

We next contrast the implications of Theorems
III.1
and
III.7
with quantum state discrimination due to the somewhat surprising appearance of the pretty-good measurement
Bae and Kwek 2015
;
Hausladen and Wootters 1994
. In brief, our results say that for a separable state, the observed measurement statistics of light-touch observables are compatible with a temporal structure. Our results do not say that quantum states can be prepared in a temporal way, which would otherwise conflict with the results of quantum state discrimination. This section will make these statements more precise.

A typical way to prepare a separable state in a spatially compatible way is by using a classical random number generator and announcing the result to both Alice and Bob, who may be spatially separated from each other. III.5
Entanglement does not imply temporal incompatibility

In this section, we generalize Theorem
III.1
by working with
arbitrary
bipartite states
\tau\in\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
, not necessarily separable states. More precisely, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for a general state
\tau
to be temporally compatible.
In doing so, we will still utilize the map
\mathcal{D}
from Theorem
III.7
. Interestingly,
\mathcal{D}
only depends on
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}[\tau]
and is therefore a quantum channel regardless of whether or not
\tau
is separable or entangled.
To state and prove our necessary and sufficient conditions for temporal compatibility, we recall the notion of distortion from Ref.
Hughston et al. 1993
.

Given any density matrix
\sigma
and a positive definite matrix
\rho
of the same dimension, the
\rho
-distorted matrix associated with
\sigma
is given by

\sigma_{\rho}:=\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(53)

Note that
\sigma_{\rho}
is positive, but it is not necessarily a density matrix. To make it a density matrix, one could normalize it by the formula

\widetilde{\sigma_{\rho}}=\frac{\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{{\rm Tr}[\sigma\rho^{-1}]},

(54)

but this would provide an additional complication to our subsequent formulas that would nevertheless yield an equivalent conclusion. As such, we will use the unnormalized form
\sigma_{\rho}
as in (
53
).
Applying this concept of a distorted matrix to our setup, given a bipartite density matrix
\tau\in\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
with marginal
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}[\tau]
on
\mathpzc{A}
satisfying
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}>0
, let

\tau_{\rho{A}}:=\big(\rho{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}\big)\tau\big(\rho{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}\big).

(55)

be the
(\rho{A}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}})
-distorted matrix associated with
\tau
.
Similarly, if
\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{A}}[\tau]>0
is the marginal on
\mathpzc{B}
, set

\tau_{\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}}:=\big(1_{\mathpzc{A}}\otimes\rho{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)\tau\big(1_{\mathpzc{A}}\otimes\rho{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big).

(56)

Theorem III.11
.

Let
\tau
be a density matrix in
\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
and set
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}[\tau]
. Assume
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
is a faithful state, i.e.,
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}>0
. Then there exists a unique CPTP map
\mathpzc{A}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}\mathpzc{B}
such that
\tau=\mathcal{E}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
if and only if

\big((\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{D})\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})\big)(\tau_{\rho{A}})\geq 0,

(57)

where
\mathcal{T}
denotes the transpose map with respect to an eigenbasis of
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
,
\tau_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}
denotes the
(\rho{A}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}})
-distorted matrix associated with
\tau
, and
\mathcal{D}
denotes the generalized dephasing channel from Theorem
III.7
, specifically (
32
) or (
41
).

A similar statement holds for temporal compatibility in the other direction. Namely, if
\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}:={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{A}}[\tau]>0
, then there exists a unique CPTP map
\mathpzc{B}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\mathpzc{A}
such that
\tau=\gamma(\mathcal{F}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{B}})
if and only if

{\big(\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{A}}\otimes(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\circ\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\big)(\tau_{\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}})\geq 0},

where
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
denotes the transpose map with respect to an eigenbasis of
\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}
,
\tau_{\rho_{B}}
denotes the
(1_{\mathpzc{A}}\otimes\rho_{\mathpzc{B}})
-distorted matrix associated with
\tau
, and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
denotes the generalized dephasing channel analogous to
\mathcal{D}
but with respect to a dephasing basis of eigenvectors for
\rho_{\mathpzc{B}}
.

Proof of Theorem
III.11
.

First note that
any
quantum state
\tau\in\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
can be decomposed as a mixture of product states (also called factorizable states), that is,

\tau=\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\otimes\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}

(58)

with the numbers
\{t_{\theta}\}
defining a
quasiprobability
, i.e.,
\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}=1
(see Ref.
Sanpera et al. 1998
;
Sperling and Vogel 2009
and Theorem 1 of Ref.
Vidal and Tarrach 1999
). Using this decomposition together with the proof of Theorem
III.1
leads us to conclude that the expression in Equation (
31
) is still the Choi matrix of the HPTP map
\mathcal{E}
satisfying
\tau=\mathcal{E}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
. Therefore, we can relate the Choi matrix of
\mathcal{E}
to
\tau_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}
by a similar calculation to the one appearing in the proof of Theorem
III.1
. Namely,

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}[\mathcal{E}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}\left(\omega\odot\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}^{T}\right)\otimes\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}\left(\omega\odot\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\right)\otimes\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})\sum_{i,j,\theta}\frac{2t_{\theta}}{p_{i}+p_{j}}|i\rangle\langle i|\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}\Big(H\odot\big(\rho{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\rho{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Big)\otimes\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}

\displaystyle=\big((\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{D})\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}}\big)\left(\sum_{\theta}t_{\theta}\big(\rho{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\rho{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)\otimes\rho_{\mathpzc{B};\theta}\right)

\displaystyle=\big((\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{D})\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}}\big)(\tau_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}).

(59)

The second equality follows from the fact that
(A\odot B)^{T}=A^{T}\odot B^{T}
for all matrices
A
and
B
of the same size, together with the fact that
\omega
is a real symmetric matrix. The third equality follows from the definition of
\omega
from Equation (
30
). The fourth equality follows from the definition of
H
from Equation (
39
) and the fact that

\langle i|\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}|j\rangle=\sqrt{p_{i}p_{j}}\Big\langle i\Big|\rho{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{\mathpzc{A};\theta}\rho{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big|j\Big\rangle.

(60)

The fifth equality follows from the fact that the generalized dephasing channel
\mathcal{D}
can be expressed as a Hadamard–Schur channel as in (
41
).
The final equality follows from the definition of
\tau_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}
and the properties of the tensor product. The claim in the statement of the theorem then follows from Choi’s theorem
Choi 1975
.
∎

It is worth noting that condition (
57
) is closely related to the positive partial transpose (PPT) criterion
Choi 1982
;
Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
. Recall, the
PPT criterion
states that if a bipartite density matrix
\tau\in\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
is separable, then
(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})(\tau)\geq 0
. More generally, any bipartite density matrix
\tau\in\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
satisfying
(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})(\tau)\geq 0
is called a
PPT state
. Note that this condition is independent of the orthonormal basis used to define the transpose
1

To see that
(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})(\tau)\geq 0
is independent of the orthonormal basis chosen to define
\mathcal{T}
, let
U
be any unitary operator and let
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
be the transpose operation with respect to the basis obtained by applying the unitary operator to the original basis. Then, a calculation shows that

(\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\tau)=\big((U^{\tilde{T}}U)\otimes 1\big)\big((\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\tau)\big)\big((U^{\tilde{T}}U)^{{\dagger}}\otimes 1\big)
,
where it is understood that the identities are on
\mathpzc{B}
. Since the transpose of a unitary is still unitary (in any basis), and since a matrix
W
is positive if and only if
VWV^{{\dagger}}
is positive for a unitary
V
, this shows that
(\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\tau)\geq 0
.

,
and one could instead use a partial transpose on system
\mathpzc{B}
in the sense that
(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})(\tau)\geq 0
is equivalent to
(\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{A}}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{\prime})(\tau)\geq 0
for the transpose
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
on
\mathpzc{B}
with respect to some orthonormal basis. The contrapositive of the PPT criterion therefore reads that if
\tau
is a bipartite state and

\tau

We have the following general fact relating PPT states and temporal compatibility as a consequence of Theorem
III.11
.

Corollary III.12
.

Let
\tau\in\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
be a PPT state, i.e.,
\tau
is a density matrix and
(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})(\tau)\geq 0
, where the transpose map
\mathcal{T}
is defined with respect to any orthonormal basis on
\mathpzc{H}_{\mathpzc{A}}
. Then,
\tau
is temporally compatible in the sense that there exists a CPTP map
\mathpzc{A}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}\mathpzc{B}
such that
\tau=\mathcal{E}\star\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
, where
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}[\tau]
.

Proof.

As stated before Corollary
III.12
, a state is PPT with the transpose defined with respect to one orthonormal basis if and only if it is PPT with respect to any other orthonormal basis. Therefore, we will use an orthonormal eigenbasis of
\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
for the transpose operation on
\mathpzc{A}
. This implies
\mathcal{T}(\rho_{\mathpzc{A}})=\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}
and
(\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{D})(A)=(\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{T})(A)
for all
A\in\mathpzc{A}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is the dephasing channel in (
32
). Secondly, notice that applying the partial transpose to the distorted matrix
\tau_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}}
yields

(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})(\tau_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}})=\Big(\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}\Big)\tau^{T_{\mathpzc{A}}}\Big(\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1_{\mathpzc{B}}\Big),

(61)

where
\tau^{T_{\mathpzc{A}}}:=(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})(\tau)
is shorthand for the partial transpose of
\tau
. Putting these two facts together shows that

\big((\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{D})\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}})\big)(\tau_{\rho{A}})=\Big(\big(\mathcal{D}\circ\mathrm{Ad}_{\rho_{\mathpzc{A}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\big)\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathpzc{B}}\Big)\big(\tau^{T_{\mathpzc{A}}}\big),

(62)

where
\mathrm{Ad}_{V}(W):=VWV^{{\dagger}}
for all square matrices
V
and
W
. Since
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathrm{Ad}_{V}
are completely positive, and since
\tau
is a PPT state, the resulting matrix in (
62
) is always positive. Hence, Theorem
III.11
proves the claim.
∎

Since every separable state is PPT, Corollary
III.12
gives an alternative proof of Theorem

.

IV
Discussion

In this work, we addressed the problem of determining whether a given bipartite density matrix is compatible with a direct causal influence by a temporal channel when measuring light-touch observables, which include all Pauli observables. Specifically, in Theorem
III.11
, we gave necessary and sufficient conditions for such a temporal channel to exist. As a special case, in Theorem
III.1
we showed that separable density matrices always admit such a direct causal influence. The case of more general bipartite hermitian matrices giving rise to physically realizable expectation values is given in Appendix
C
.

Another interesting aspect of the necessary and sufficient conditions in Theorem
III.11
guaranteeing when a bipartite state also admits a plausible direct causal influence perspective is the appearance of a condition closely related to the PPT criterion combined with a dephasing channel. Moreover, in the case of separable bipartite states, the dephasing channel becomes a crucial component of the temporal channel compatible with the correlations. Namely, the temporal channel is the composite of a dephasing channel combined with a pretty good measure-and-prepare protocol, thus providing the temporal channel with a concrete interpretation. It is worth mentioning that the two temporal channels going between the marginals are
not
Petz recovery maps of each other, but rather, they are
Bayesian inverses
of each other, where the notion of Bayesian inverse is that which appears in Ref.
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2023
(additional distinctions between the Petz recovery map and the Bayesian inverse appear in Ref.
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2024
). This explicitly reveals that the Petz recovery map and the Bayesian inverse differ by a dephasing channel. This illustrates that the Petz recovery map, although often thought to be a natural candidate of Bayesian inversion and retrodiction
Parzygnat and Buscemi 2023
;
Li and Winter 2018
, is not necessarily the only retrodiction map to use when describing quantum spatiotemporal correlations.

Let us recapitulate one point regarding our notion of temporal compatibility. Because this notion was only analyzed here in the case of light-touch observables, our conclusions regarding temporal compatibility hold for such observables (which include all Pauli observables). If another set of tomographically-complete observables is chosen, one may need to use an alternative state over time
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2023
;
Fullwood and Parzygnat 2024
. This seems reasonable from the perspective that temporal compatibility depends on the observations made. In other words, temporal compatibility with respect to one set of observables need not imply temporal compatibility with respect to another set of observables. Therefore, our results do not definitively determine what causal structures explain measurement outcomes, but they can be used to rule out certain causal structures. It is therefore important to investigate this direction further to see how it can contribute to the general program of quantum causal inference.

Our work leads to several natural questions within the general program of determining plausible causal structures compatible with a given set of expectation values. We mention four such questions. (1) What is the geometry of the space of bipartite hermitian matrices
\tau\in\mathpzc{A}\otimes\mathpzc{B}
, with
{\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{B}}[\tau]
and
{\rm Tr}_{\mathpzc{A}}[\tau]
density matrices, that are temporally compatible? By our work here, we know this space contains the convex space of separable density matrices (in fact, all PPT states), but we also know by earlier work that this space is neither convex nor does it contain all bipartite density matrices
Song et al. 2024
. (2) To better understand compatible causal structures, it is important to investigate the case of multipartite distributions as well as more general causal structures
Liu et al. 2025
;
Wolfe et al. 2021
;
Allen et al. 2017
;
Fritz and Klingler 2023
;
Fullwood 2025
;
Barrett et al. 2019
. (3) Since our results were concerned with the spatiotemporal correlations obtained from observables that have at most two eigenvalues (termed light-touch observables) with their corresponding projective measurements, it is important to investigate extensions of our results to arbitrary positive operator-valued measures and quantum instruments
Kraus 1983
;
Davies and Lewis 1970
.
(4) Since our work has been formulated in the context of finite-dimensional quantum systems, a natural next step would be to analyze how this work extends to infinite-dimensional quantum systems, where measurement must take account of both quantum field theory and general relativity
Fewster and Verch 2020
.

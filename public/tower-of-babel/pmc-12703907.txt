Identification of diagnostic and prognostic phospholipid biomarkers in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis via machine learning and in vivo validation
Authors: Liqing Yang, Lian Liu, Yi Liao, Jun Zou, Caiyu Jiang, Xiaoshu Liu, Junmei Song
Published: 2025

Background

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a long-lasting and progressively worsening interstitial lung disease characterized by fibrosis and an unclear cause. Once considered rare in respiratory medicine, the detection rate of IPF has increased significantly due to advancements in diagnostic methods, particularly the widespread use of computed tomography (CT) [1]. Currently, the clinical diagnosis of IPF primarily depends on high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT), pulmonary function tests, and, in certain cases, surgical lung biopsy [2]. However, these approaches have notable limitations, including invasiveness, inter-observer variability, and low sensitivity for detecting early-stage disease. Moreover, differentiating IPF from other interstitial lung diseases (ILDs) remains challenging, and there is still a lack of reliable, noninvasive biomarkers for early and accurate diagnosis or for predicting prognosis. The disease predominantly affects individuals over 50, with a greater occurrence in males. The global incidence and prevalence of IPF are increasing, with varying rates between regions, from 0.09 to 1.30 cases per 10,000 people, and prevalence estimates between 0.33 and 4.51 cases per 10,000 people [3]. IPF progression is typically unpredictable, manifesting either as a steady decline in lung function or as abrupt exacerbations [1]. Prognosis remains poor, and most patients have a prognosis of approximately 4 years after diagnosis [4]. Although antifibrotic treatments like pirfenidone and nintedanib can slow disease progression by mitigating the decline in lung function, they are not curative [5, 6]. The rising incidence, coupled with poor outcomes, highlights IPF's growing impact as a significant global public health challenge.

While the exact causes of IPF are still unknown, it is thought to stem from a combination of genetic susceptibility and environmental factors [7]. Lipids play a crucial role in the development and progression of many diseases, including those affecting metabolic processes and respiratory function [8]. Emerging research suggests that lipids may also be involved in IPF pathogenesis [9, 10]. For instance, in rat models of IPF, elevated phospholipid levels in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) were positively correlated with lung inflammation scores [11]. Yan et al. identified certain lipid types in the plasma of patients with IPF that exhibited high diagnostic sensitivity and specificity. Reduced levels of glycerophospholipids and sphingolipids were observed in the plasma of patients with IPF, while other glycerolipids, sterol lipids, and fatty acids were moderately elevated [12]. These findings point to phospholipid alterations in IPF, though further research is needed to identify specific phospholipids as potential biomarkers for diagnosis, disease progression, and prognosis.

Phospholipids are key structural components of cell membranes, and their dysregulated turnover contributes to alveolar injury and fibroblast activation in IPF [9]. Recent studies have shown that oxidative stress and lipid peroxidation damage membrane phospholipids and cholesterol, altering bilayer fluidity and generating reactive aldehydes such as 4-hydroxy-2-nonenal that amplify profibrotic signaling via TGF-β [13]. GABARAPL1, an autophagy-related protein essential for autophagosome maturation, is reduced in IPF and forms part of prognostic signatures [13, 14]. UNC13B encodes a vesicle-priming factor containing diacylglycerol- and calcium-binding domains that facilitate granule tethering to the plasma membrane [15, 16]. Loss of UNC13B reduces exocytic secretion of angiogenic mediators and ameliorates vascular defects [16], underscoring its role in lipid-mediated signaling and fibrovascular remodeling. Collectively, these findings support the investigation of phospholipid-associated genes as biomarkers in IPF.

With the continuous advancements in sequencing technologies, molecular marker-based diagnostic and predictive models are increasingly being utilized across various diseases [17–19]. In the field of IPF, bioinformatics approaches have also gained growing attention, enabling the identification of disease-specific biomarkers and functional pathways [20–23]. Recent studies suggest that lipid metabolism, particularly phospholipid dysregulation, plays a critical role in the pathogenesis of IPF by contributing to epithelial injury, immune imbalance, and fibroblast activation [24–26]. These processes are often triggered by environmental exposures and modulated by genetic susceptibility—two well-established risk factors for IPF. Thus, phospholipid-related molecular markers may reflect underlying pathogenic mechanisms and serve as valuable biomarkers. Leveraging potential phospholipid-related biomarkers for IPF can enable earlier and more precise predictions of disease progression. However, the application of phospholipid-related molecular markers in IPF remains underexplored. This study integrates phospholipid metabolism-related gene expression data from multiple datasets and applies machine learning algorithms alongside bioinformatics analyses to develop diagnostic and prognostic models for IPF. Furthermore, these potential biomarkers were validated in an IPF mouse model, opening up new possibilities for early detection and personalized treatment of IPF.

This research integrates bioinformatics analysis of phospholipid-related genes to identify potential biomarkers for IPF. By applying advanced machine learning techniques to multidimensional sequencing datasets, we identified key genes potentially involved in IPF progression. The resulting models demonstrated strong predictive performance; however, limitations remain in their clinical applicability. The diagnostic model relies on lung biopsies, which restricts its feasibility for routine clinical use. In contrast, the prognostic model is based on liquid biopsy, including alveolar lavage fluid and blood, providing a more practical and non-invasive approach for patient monitoring. Given these differences, further validation is needed to improve the clinical utility of the diagnostic model, while lipidomics approaches, such as direct detection of phospholipids in serum, may offer a more effective strategy for identifying IPF biomarkers and understanding disease mechanisms.

Methods

Data collection and processing

Microarray data were sourced from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, with each dataset containing a minimum of 20 samples. Only datasets containing lung tissue samples from adult human subjects were included. IPF cases were selected based on a clear diagnosis stated in the original study, typically confirmed by clinical, radiological (HRCT), or pathological criteria. Datasets lacking proper annotation, unclear diagnosis, or derived from animal models or in vitro experiments were excluded. The IPF samples were selected from human subjects with confirmed diagnoses. Raw microarray data were downloaded from the GEO database and converted to log2 intensity values. Expression intensities were then quantile-normalized across samples using the normalizeBetweenArrays() function in the limma package with the default method, which ensures that A-values have essentially the same distribution across all arrays [27]. Probe identifiers were mapped to gene symbols according to each platform, and multiple probes for the same gene were averaged. To correct batch effects before differential expression analysis, we used the ComBat() function from the sva package [28], specifying the dataset source as the batch factor. ComBat models known batches with an empirical Bayesian framework and, in our implementation, used a simple intercept-only model matrix and parametric prior estimation, as recommended [29, 30].

Characterization of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

After normalization, the limma package was utilized to perform differential expression analysis to identify DEGs [27]. The Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) procedure was applied to adjust for multiple testing, and genes with an absolute log2 fold change (|log2FC|) > 0.585 (corresponding to a 1.5-fold change) and an adjusted P-value (i.e., false discovery rate, FDR) < 0.05 were considered DEGs. The FDR approach effectively controls the expected proportion of false positives among statistically significant results [31].

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

WGCNA [32] was applied to identify genes and modules linked with IPF progression. The best soft-thresholding power (β) was selected using the pickSoftThreshold() function to ensure the network conformed to a scale-free topology. Gene similarity was assessed using a topological overlap matrix (TOM), and a weighted adjacency matrix was constructed. Genes were organized into distinct modules using hierarchical clustering combined with the dynamic tree-cut method, setting the minimum module size at 100 genes. For each module, the module eigengene (ME)—a summary of the module’s gene expression—was calculated, and its correlation with clinical traits, such as IPF status, was assessed via Pearson correlation. Heatmaps were generated to visualize module-trait associations. Hub genes in IPF-associated modules were identified based on module membership (MM) and gene significance (GS), highlighting genes with high connectivity and potential involvement in IPF progression.

Functional enrichment analysis

To further characterize the function of the identified gene set, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses were conducted. These analyses provided a functional understanding of the genes and their roles in IPF-related mechanisms.

Constructing diagnostic models using machine learning algorithms

Twelve distinct machine learning algorithms were employed to develop diagnostic models, including Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (Lasso), Ridge, Elastic Net (Enet), Stepwise Generalized Linear Model (Stepglm), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), Random Forest (RF), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Generalized Linear Model Boosting (glmBoost), Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA), Partial Least Squares Regression Generalized Linear Model (plsRglm), and Naive Bayes. Feature selection methods, such as Lasso, Enet, and Stepglm, were utilized to pinpoint the most relevant variables for classification, such as disease status, from a broad array of candidate genes. Subsequently, classification models including RF, SVM, and XGBoost were constructed to classify samples as either diseased or healthy controls. The effectiveness of the model was assessed by calculating the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), with values closer to 1 indicating higher classification accuracy. Additionally, confusion matrices were utilized to assess the predictive performance of the models. To avoid overfitting, the data were separated into a training cohort and independent validation cohorts, and repeated tenfold cross-validation was applied within the training cohort using functions such as cv.glmnet() to tune hyperparameters for each algorithm glmnet. The optimized models were then assessed on the validation cohort.

Constructing prognostic models using machine learning algorithms

In the prognostic analysis, machine learning algorithms such as Lasso, Elastic Net, CoxBoost, Random Survival Forest (RSF), and GBM, along with various combinations, were applied through a two-step process involving feature selection and model construction. While some algorithms did not perform feature selection, specific feature selection techniques were used to isolate prognostic variables from candidate genes. These selected variables were then used in the prognostic models. To evaluate the models’ performance, the C-index was calculated on the test set, with higher values closer to 1 reflecting better prognostic accuracy. For prognostic modeling, repeated tenfold cross‑validation in the training cohort was likewise employed to select optimal penalization parameters and prevent overfitting, and C‑index values were calculated on an external validation cohort.

Immune infiltration analysis

Immune cell infiltration was quantified by deconvoluting bulk RNA transcript data with the CIBERSORT algorithm [33]. A total of 1,000 permutations were performed to obtain empirical P values for each cell type. To account for multiple comparisons, the P values were adjusted using the Benjamini and Hochberg false discovery rate method [34].Only cell types with adjusted P < 0.05 were retained for further analysis.

Single-cell sequencing analysis

The GSE128033 dataset [35, 36] was used for single-cell sequencing. Raw data were processed using the Read10X() function, and a Seurat object was generated with a minimum threshold of 3 cells per feature and at least 200 features detected per cell. Quality control was based on mitochondrial gene content and ribosomal gene expression. Filtered cells met criteria including total RNA counts ≥ 1000, detected genes between 200 and 10,000, mitochondrial gene content ≤ 20%, and ribosomal gene content ≤ 20%. Only cells meeting these criteria were retained, and the processed data were saved as an RDS file for subsequent analyses. Normalization was performed using the “LogNormalize” method [37], and highly variable genes were selected for further investigation. Dimensionality reduction was performed using PCA and t-SNE, and the Harmony method was applied to mitigate batch effects [38]. Cell clustering was performed at various resolutions, followed by annotation using the SingleR package and manual curation based on established marker genes. DEGs for each cluster were identified and visualized with heatmaps to demonstrate expression patterns.

Establishment of an IPF mouse model

This study was conducted in strict accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines to ensure the highest standards of animal welfare and reporting. All in vivo experiments were carried out using C57BL/6 J mice, adhering to NIH standards. Male C57BL/6 J mice (SPF grade, 5–6 weeks old, 35–41 days, body weight 18–22 g) were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. Mice were randomly assigned into two groups: a control group and a bleomycin-induced IPF group, with three mice per group. The group size (n = 3) was determined based on prior published study [39] in pulmonary fibrosis models, where comparable biological replicates were used for histological and molecular analyses. The IPF model was established via intratracheal administration of bleomycin (BLM), which was obtained from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA). BLM was dissolved in physiological saline at a concentration of 0.5 mg/ml and kept at 4 °C until administered. Mice were randomly divided into a control group and an IPF group, with each group containing three mice. To induce IPF, 100 µl of the BLM solution was administered intratracheally. After 28 days, the mice were sacrificed, and lung tissues were collected. Some samples were stored by freezing, while others were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for histopathological evaluation.

Histological analyses

Lung tissue specimens were preserved in paraformaldehyde for 48 h, embedded in paraffin, and then sliced into 5 μm sections. These sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE) as well as Masson’s trichrome for pathological examination.

Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Total mRNA was isolated from lung tissues using the Trizol RNA extraction kit, in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. Reverse transcription and PCR were carried out, with GAPDH serving as the reference gene. The detailed PCR primer sequences can be found in Supplementary Table 1.

Western blotting (WB)

For protein analysis, total protein was isolated, and concentrations were determined via the BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein were resolved using gel electrophoresis and transferred to membranes. These membranes were blocked and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies diluted in Tris-buffered saline with Tween 20 (TBST) and 5% non-fat milk. The following day, secondary antibodies were applied at 25 °C for 1.5 h. After multiple TBST washes to remove excess antibodies, protein bands were detected, and their intensities were measured using imaging software. In this study, the following antibodies were used for WB analysis of mouse lung tissue samples: anti-GABARAPL1 antibody (Catalog No. P012678), anti-UNC13B antibody (Catalog No. 21839-1-AP), anti-α-SMA antibody (Catalog No. M012236), and anti-β-Tubulin antibody (Catalog No. LF203).

Statistical analysis

This study was performed using R software (version 4.3.1). Correlations were assessed via Spearman’s rank correlation. Survival differences between groups were analyzed using Kaplan–Meier survival analysis in conjunction with the log-rank test. The optimal cutoff for survival analysis was determined using the surv_cutpoint() function, which identifies the threshold that optimizes survival differences. For statistical comparisons, an independent t-test was applied for two-group comparisons, and one-way ANOVA was used for comparisons across multiple groups. A two-sided P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Data collection and processing

The R code used in this study is provided in Supplementary Data Sheet 1. The dataset overview comprises eight gene expression studies, predominantly conducted in the USA, supplemented by data from Germany and Canada. These studies utilized various platforms, including Affymetrix Human Genome and Gene Arrays, Agilent Whole Human Genome Microarrays, and Illumina sequencing systems. Focused on lung tissue samples from patients with IPF, the datasets range from 10 to 160 IPF cases, paired with 6 to 108 control cases. Spanning data collection from 2010 to 2020, they provide a robust temporal cross-section for IPF research. Additionally, three gene expression datasets from Germany, the USA, and the United Kingdom, centered on bronchoalveolar lavage and blood samples, are included. These datasets, collected in 2011, 2015, and 2017, contain large sample sizes of 176, 120, and 57, respectively, exclusively from patients with IPF, offering critical data for developing prognostic models. Further details on these datasets can be found in Supplementary Table 2.

Identifying potential phospholipid-related hub genes for IPF

Phospholipid-related genes were selected from the GeneCards website [40], selecting those with a relevance score above 1.5 [41] to ensure a strong association with phospholipid metabolism. These genes were intersected with those identified from both WGCNA and DEGs, as shown in Fig. 2E, resulting in 50 potential phospholipid-related hub genes for IPF (Supplementary Table 4). These genes are distributed across all chromosomes except 13 and 14, and the sex chromosomes, as depicted in Fig. 2F and G.

Functional enrichment analysis

Figure 2H and I provide insights into the biological processes and pathways involving these core genes, identified through GO and KEGG analyses. Figure 2H highlights GO terms, focusing on significant biological processes such as lipid transport, glycerolipid and phospholipid metabolic processes, and other lipid-related functions, including high-density lipoprotein particle involvement and lipid binding activities. In contrast, Fig. 2I illustrates the KEGG pathway analysis, emphasizing essential metabolic pathways like ether lipid metabolism, cholesterol metabolism, and glycerophospholipid metabolism, while also linking these lipid pathways to broader biological functions such as vascular smooth muscle contraction and the Ras signaling pathway.

Creation and verification of a phospholipid-related diagnostic model

To develop a phospholipid-related diagnostic model, 108 machine learning algorithms were applied, targeting datasets with sample sizes greater than 40 to ensure stability and minimize the influence of random variation. Four datasets—GSE47460, GSE32537, GSE53845, and GSE150910—were selected for analysis based on their sample sizes. The largest dataset, GSE47460, was utilized as the training cohort, whereas the remaining three datasets were employed as validation sets. The performance metrics of the models are summarized in Fig. 3A, with detailed features provided in Supplementary Table 5. Notably, the model constructed using the StepGLM[both] + RF algorithm, which integrates bidirectional stepwise logistic regression for feature selection with a RF classifier, demonstrated exceptional performance with a classification accuracy of 1.000. To reduce the risk of overfitting, feature selection was first performed to eliminate redundant or irrelevant variables, and the RF classifier was trained using tenfold cross-validation. Although a perfect performance index is uncommon, similar results have been reported in previous studies involving gene expression–based disease classification, supporting the reliability of our approach [42–44]. This model, comprising eight genes, achieved an average AUC of 0.986 across the datasets. As shown in Fig. 3, the ROC curves and confusion matrices demonstrate the robust diagnostic performance of our model across four independent cohorts. The AUC values were 0.9977 in GSE47460 (Fig. 3B), 0.9680 in GSE150910 (Fig. 3D), 0.9795 in GSE32537 (Fig. 3F), and 1.0000 in GSE53845 (Fig. 3H), indicating excellent discriminatory ability. Creation and verification of a phospholipid-related prognostic model

Details of the model features are provided in Supplementary Table 6. GSE70866 served as the training dataset, with two additional datasets used for validation. The prognostic performance of each model is presented in Fig. 4A. The RSF model, based on 11 genes, achieved the highest accuracy with a C-index of 0.914 (95% CI 0.897–0.932) in GSE70866. In contrast, lower predictive performance was observed in the blood-derived cohorts, with a C-index of 0.609 (95% CI: 0.532–0.683) in GSE28221 and 0.614 (95% CI 0.514–0.711) in GSE93606, which may be attributed to differences in sample type, transcriptomic signal strength, and clinical outcome definitions, as also demonstrated in our previous studies [45, 46]. Figure 4B–G presents the Phospholipid-Related Risk Scores (PRS) for samples within each dataset, demonstrating that higher risk-score groups consistently exhibit poorer prognoses across the three datasets. Immune infiltration analysis

Results revealed that naive B cells, plasma cells, follicular helper T cells, gamma delta T cells, macrophages, and resting mast cells were more prevalent in tissues collected from patients with IPF. In contrast, CD8 T cells, CD4 memory resting T cells, resting NK cells, monocytes, eosinophils, and neutrophils were more common in control tissues (Fig. 5A). An intersection analysis of the diagnostic and prognostic models identified GABARAPL1 and UNC13B as key genes (Fig. 5B). GABARAPL1 showed positive correlations with monocytes, M2 macrophages, and resting NK cells, suggesting its involvement in modulating innate immune responses, particularly in anti-inflammatory and tissue maintenance processes. On the other hand, UNC13B was strongly associated with activated NK cells and CD8 + T cells, indicating its potential role in enhancing cytotoxic and adaptive immune responses. Rigorous quality control was then performed on the remaining single-cell RNA sequencing data to eliminate potential doublets, dead cells, and empty droplets, ensuring high-quality data for analysis (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Discussion

Our research integrated multiple datasets and applied the combat algorithm to eliminate batch effects, thereby ensuring data comparability. WGCNA analysis, conducted on the entire merged gene set, successfully identified pathogenic modules closely linked to IPF. An intersection of the identified core genes with phospholipid-related gene sets further revealed several phospholipid-associated core genes implicated in IPF. These models were validated across multiple external datasets, and comparative analysis indicated that the newly constructed models provided significant improvements in predicting clinical outcomes for patients with IPF, particularly in terms of robustness and accuracy. Notably, the diagnostic and prognostic models identified two intersecting phospholipid-related core genes, GABARAPL1 and UNC13B, which were significantly downregulated in IPF mouse lung tissues. This suggests that these genes may have dual roles in IPF pathogenesis, contributing to both disease onset and progression.

The application of lipidomics, particularly the direct detection of phospholipids in serum, has emerged as a powerful tool for elucidating lipid-mediated pathobiological mechanisms and identifying potential biomarkers in IPF. Recent studies employing lipidomic profiling have demonstrated significant dysregulation of bioactive lipids, including lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) species and triglycerides, in IPF patients compared to healthy controls [61–63]. For instance, elevated plasma LPA levels were strongly associated with rapid disease progression, as evidenced by declines in pulmonary function and increased fibrosis on imaging [62]. Similarly, serum lipidomic analyses revealed distinct triglyceride and phosphatidylcholine signatures correlated with disease severity and survival outcomes [64]. These findings highlight the utility of lipidomics in capturing dynamic lipid alterations linked to epithelial injury, macrophage activation, and fibrotic remodeling [61, 63]. Notably, the non-invasive nature of serum/plasma lipid profiling enables longitudinal monitoring of therapeutic responses, as exemplified by nintedanib-induced upregulation of triglycerides and phosphatidylcholines predictive of improved outcomes [61]. While challenges remain in standardizing lipid detection methods and interpreting complex lipid interactions, these studies collectively validate lipidomics as a critical platform for uncovering pathogenic pathways and advancing precision medicine in IPF. Future research integrating multi-omics approaches could further clarify the causal roles of specific phospholipid species in fibrogenesis and their potential as therapeutic targets.

The diagnostic and prognostic models developed in this study demonstrated marked improvements in predictive accuracy and robustness compared to existing models, as evidenced by higher AUC values across external validation datasets. Previous models for diagnosing IPF, such as a chemokine-related eleven-gene signature model [65], an artificial neural network model [66], a random forest model based on circadian rhythm-related genes [67], and a neutrophil- and immune infiltration-related gene model [68], employed fewer external validation datasets than those utilized in this research. The diagnostic model in this study leverages the RF algorithm, which excels at managing high-dimensional data, such as gene expression profiles. RF offers high accuracy and robustness, mitigates overfitting by integrating multiple decision trees, and efficiently handles large feature sets. Additionally, it automatically selects important features and captures complex nonlinear relationships, enhancing its predictive power. However, a significant drawback of RF is its limited transparency, which complicates understanding the individual impact of specific features on the overall prediction outcome.

Recent advances in metabolomics and proteomics have highlighted serum-based biomarkers as promising non-invasive tools, with diagnostic performance approaching or even surpassing that of tissue-derived gene signatures. For instance, Cai et al. used liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry to profile sera from IPF patients and identified two lipids, phenethylamine and 2-amino-1,3,4-octadecanetriol, which were markedly elevated compared with controls and showed near-perfect discrimination [69]. These results underscore the potential of lipid metabolic alterations as powerful indicators of disease without the need for tissue sampling. Likewise, a multi-analyte panel combining advanced glycation end products and matrix metalloproteinase-7 effectively distinguished IPF from both healthy subjects and connective-tissue disease-associated ILD, achieving excellent accuracy [70]. It should be noted, however, that both studies were based on relatively small cohorts, with fewer than 100 participants. In contrast, more conventional serum proteins such as KL-6, SP-D, CCL18, and VEGF-A generally show only moderate predictive value, with reported AUCs around 0.5–0.75 for prognosis or progression [71]. Taken together, these findings indicate that blood-derived lipid and protein markers can offer robust diagnostic information while avoiding invasive lung biopsies, and their integration with tissue-based signatures may further improve risk stratification.

The prognostic model developed in this study demonstrates clear advantages over previously published models, particularly in the GSE70866 dataset, where it achieved the highest accuracy, outperforming all other models. However, several issues warrant attention. The inclusion of bronchoalveolar lavage fluid and blood samples from two additional datasets in constructing the prognostic model presents both benefits and challenges. On the one hand, using distinct biological sources allows for the identification of disease-specific markers, with bronchoalveolar lavage fluid providing insights into local lung-specific processes in IPF. On the other hand, the variation in tissue origin introduces limitations, as blood samples capture systemic changes that may lead to discrepancies in gene expression profiles between datasets. This heterogeneity could affect the model's consistency, reduce its generalizability across different sample types, and complicate direct comparisons of predictive performance between datasets. Moreover, the datasets employed differ in survival outcomes: GSE70866 focuses on overall survival [72], GSE28221 on transplant-free survival [73, 74], and GSE93606 on progression-free survival [75]. These variations in outcome measures may contribute to differences in the model's predictive performance across datasets. While leveraging multiple cohorts improves statistical power and external validation, the heterogeneity in outcome definitions introduces inherent limitations. OS captures all-cause mortality regardless of clinical events, whereas TFS is influenced by lung transplantation decisions, which may vary across institutions and timeframes. In contrast, PFS integrates functional decline and clinical worsening, capturing disease progression rather than mortality. These differences complicate direct comparisons of prognostic accuracy across datasets, as the underlying biological and clinical signals driving each endpoint may vary. Consequently, model performance in one cohort may not fully translate to another due to these endpoint discrepancies. We have acknowledged this issue as a potential confounding factor and recommend that future studies utilize harmonized definitions of clinical outcomes to ensure more robust cross-cohort evaluation.

In the context of IPF, EMT is increasingly recognized as a key contributor to disease progression [76]. GABARAPL1, through its role in selective autophagy, actively regulates EMT by targeting pivotal signaling molecules for degradation. Notably, GABARAPL1 can bind and promote the autophagic degradation of SMAD transcription factors, which are downstream effectors of TGF-β signaling [77]. This process creates a negative feedback loop: TGF-β/SMAD activation initially upregulates GABARAPL1, and in turn GABARAPL1 curtails the EMT program by degrading SMAD proteins and suppressing the pro-EMT signal [77].In IPF, where GABARAPL1 is downregulated, this brake on EMT may be lost, leading to sustained TGF-β/SMAD activity and enhanced transition of alveolar epithelial cells into a mesenchymal phenotype. Single-cell RNA sequencing studies of fibrotic lungs have indeed identified transitional cell populations that exemplify this phenomenon – for instance, aberrant epithelial cells coexpressing mesenchymal markers like collagens and N-cadherin (CDH2) while still retaining some epithelial characteristics [78]. Such cells suggest that differentiated alveolar epithelial cells can acquire mesenchymal traits (partial EMT), contributing to fibrogenesis. By failing to restrain EMT, a GABARAPL1 deficiency may thus facilitate the expansion of fibrogenic mesenchymal cells or EMT-derived signals, exacerbating matrix deposition and lung scarring in IPF. In summary, GABARAPL1 acts as an EMT gatekeeper via autophagy; its reduced expression in IPF likely permits unchecked epithelial plasticity and myofibroblast accumulation, thereby driving disease development.

UNC13B (Munc13-2) is a synaptic vesicle priming factor that promotes vesicle maturation and exocytosis [79]. Beyond its canonical role in neurotransmitter release, UNC13B has been linked to mitochondrial homeostasis and apoptosis regulation [79].Notably, elevated UNC13B expression can enhance cancer cell survival by modulating mitochondrial quality control pathways [79].Given UNC13B’s influence on apoptotic signaling, its deficiency in IPF fibroblasts may confer resistance to apoptosis and prolong cellular survival, thereby contributing to the persistent fibroblast accumulation and excessive extracellular matrix deposition characteristic of fibrosis. Furthermore, UNC13B’s involvement in mitochondrial signaling suggests that its loss could dysregulate oxidative stress responses [79], compounding profibrotic activation. In summary, reduced UNC13B expression may foster an environment of fibroblast longevity and unabated matrix production in IPF lungs, implicating UNC13B as a potential modulator of fibrotic progression.

Although GABARAPL1 and UNC13B are not classical enzymes involved in phospholipid metabolism, accumulating evidence suggests they play essential roles in membrane-associated lipid dynamics. GABARAPL1, a member of the ATG8 family, undergoes covalent conjugation with phosphatidylethanolamine or phosphatidylserine to anchor on the isolation membrane, thereby facilitating autophagosome elongation and autophagic lipid degradation. This lipidation process is crucial for membrane curvature, cargo recognition, and lipid droplet clearance during autophagy [80, 81]. In parallel, the protein product of UNC13B possesses conserved C1 and C2B domains capable of sensing diacylglycerol and phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate, two key lipid signaling molecules involved in vesicle priming and membrane fusion events [82, 83]. These mechanistic insights provide a molecular rationale for the central positioning of GABARAPL1 and UNC13B in our hub gene network. Moreover, they open avenues for future investigations into lipid metabolism and membrane signaling pathways in pulmonary fibrosis. For instance, functional assays could be designed to explore how GABARAPL1-mediated lipophagy or UNC13B-regulated DAG/PIP2 signaling modulates epithelial lipid turnover and fibrogenic cytokine secretion, thereby linking molecular signatures to disease phenotypes across multiple biological layers.

This study has several notable strengths, including the development of diagnostic and prognostic models with robust predictive performance supported by relatively large transcriptomic datasets, as well as the identification of two novel potential core biomarkers, GABARAPL1 and UNC13B, which provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying IPF. However, several limitations should also be acknowledged. The diagnostic model relies on lung biopsy data, limiting its feasibility for routine clinical application, while the prognostic model, although based on liquid biopsy, requires further validation in independent, real-world cohorts to confirm its clinical utility. Much of the analysis is based on publicly available datasets, and although GABARAPL1 and UNC13B expression was validated in an IPF mouse model, no functional experiments were conducted to elucidate their precise roles in pulmonary fibrosis. Moreover, although the study focused on phospholipid-related genes, their direct involvement in phospholipid metabolism remains unclear, and their mechanistic link to lipid dysregulation in IPF is yet to be fully established. Importantly, the number of experimental samples used in this study was relatively small, which may limit the statistical power and robustness of the validation results. In addition, both models were validated exclusively using public data, without confirmation from prospective patient cohorts, and differences in sample type, transcriptomic signal strength, and clinical outcome definitions may affect their consistency and generalizability. Finally, because most bioinformatics analyses were conducted using samples from Western populations, the limited representation of Asian and other ethnic groups may further restrict the global applicability of the findings. Future studies should address these limitations by expanding validation efforts to larger and more diverse cohorts, incorporating functional experiments, and integrating lipidomics and multi-omics approaches to enhance the robustness, clinical utility, and translational relevance of the proposed models.

Conclusion

This study identified key phospholipid-related hub genes through bioinformatics analysis and developed diagnostic and prognostic models for IPF. The identified biomarkers, GABARAPL1 and UNC13B, may play a role in IPF pathology, though their direct link to phospholipid metabolism requires further validation. Future studies incorporating lipidomics approaches may enhance biomarker discovery and clinical translation.

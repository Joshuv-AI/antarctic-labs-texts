Ping-pong for basis-conjugating HNN-extension of free group
Vasily Ionin

Abstract
. We construct a normal form for a multiple HNN-extension of a free group by basis-conjugating embeddings. We provide sufficient conditions on a collection of subgroups to fulfill the requirements of ping-pong lemma. Recall that the pure braid group splits as a semidirect product of free groups. Using our result, we show that certain braids from the first two summands F n ⋊ F n − 1 ⊂ P n + 1 F_{n}\rtimes F_{n-1}\subset P_{n+1} generate a free subgroup.

Theorem 1.1

(Normal form for

Every element of

Theorem 1.2

(Ping-pong lemma for

.

Let

Recall that the pure braid group

There is a decomposition

We use our results to show that one can ‘spoil’ the set

Theorem 1.3
.

Consider

(1)

\exp_{x_{j}}(w_{i})\neq 0

(2)

The condition (1) is equivalent to

The condition (2) is crucial. For example, for

\langle w_{i},t\rangle
is free of rank two by the main result of
[
LM10
]
. The other direction of Lemma
3.3
implies the part (1).

Note that
\varphi\colon G_{n}\to G_{n}
lifts to an automorphism of
F(X\cup Y)
using the same formula. This automorphism clearly induces an identity on
F(X)\times F(Y)
. Hence,
A_{i}\cap\langle Y\rangle=1
in
G_{n}
by Proposition
2.3
. Then, the part (2) follows from Theorem
1.2
.
∎

3.1.
Possible generalization

One may wonder, if the last generator
t=x_{n}
can be replaced with the general word
w_{n}
such that
w_{n}\in\langle Y,x_{n}\rangle
,
\exp_{x_{n}}(w_{n})\neq 0
, and
[w_{i},w_{n}]\neq 1
for every
i<n
. It seems that in this case, the subgroup
\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\rangle
would also be free, but an argument has to be modified: we used the fact that the conjugation by the last generator induces an identity automorphism on a product
F(X)\times F(Y)
to demonstrate that

\langle w_{n}^{-k}w_{i}w_{n}^{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle=1
.
Of course, our argument easily generalizes for words
w_{n}
from the normal closure of
x_{n}
in the group
\langle Y,x_{n}\rangle
. However, for the case of three strands we can ensure that no restrictions are needed.
Note that
G_{2}=\langle y_{1},x_{1}\rangle
and
P^{(2)}_{3}=P_{3}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in P_{3}
be some braids such that
w_{1}\in\langle y_{1},x_{1}\rangle
and
\exp_{x_{1}}(w_{1})\neq 0
. Then the subgroup
\langle w_{1}^{w_{2}^{k}}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\rangle
does not intersect the subgroup
\langle y_{1}\rangle
non-trivially.

Proof.

The center of
P_{3}
is generated by the braid
z=y_{1}x_{1}t
. Hence, we can assume that
w_{2}
is from
\langle y_{1},x_{1}\rangle
. It is sufficient to demonstrate that the normal closure of
w_{1}
in
\langle y_{1},x_{1}\rangle
does not intersect
\langle y_{1}\rangle
.
If
w_{1}=(u^{k})^{v}
, then the normal closure of
w_{1}
is contained in the normal closure of
u
. Hence, we can assume that the cyclic reduction of
w_{1}
is not a proper power.

Note that
\langle y_{1},x_{1}\rangle
is free of rank two. The one-relator group
\langle x_{1},y_{1}\mid w_{1}\rangle
is torsion-free by
[
MKS04
, Theorem 4.12]
. Hence, any non-trivial intersection should give
y_{1}=1
in this group. Hence, this group is generated by
x_{1}
. But since
\exp_{x_{1}}(w_{1})\neq 0
, this would imply that this group is finite cyclic, which is impossible.
∎

3.2.
Lie-theoretic approach

With a group
G
filtered by a lower central series one can associate a graded Lie ring

\mathrm{gr}_{*}(G)\vcentcolon=\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\frac{\gamma_{k}(G)}{\gamma_{k+1}(G)}

with a Lie bracket given by a commutator.
This construction is clearly functorial. Moreover, if
G
is residually nipotent, then an injectivity of a homomorphism
f\colon G\to H
may be deduced from an injectivity of the homomorphism
\mathrm{gr}_{*}(f)\colon\mathrm{gr}_{*}(G)\to\mathrm{gr}_{*}(H)
. This is the approach that was took in
[
CW04
,
CW10
]
to show an injectivity of the natural simplicial map
F[S^{1}]\to\mathrm{AP}
. In degree
n
, it corresponds to some homomorphism
F_{n}\to P_{n+1}
.

Note that if two homomorphism
f,g\colon G\to H
induce the same homomorphism on the abelianizations
G_{\mathrm{ab}}\to H_{\mathrm{ab}}
, then they induce the same homomorphism of graded Lie rings
\mathrm{gr}_{*}(G)\to\mathrm{gr}_{*}(H)
.
Since the subgroup
\langle y_{1}x_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1}x_{n-1},t\rangle
of
P_{n+1}
is isomorphic to the product
F_{n-1}\times\mathbb{Z}
, the corresponding homomorphism
F_{n}\to P_{n+1}
is not injective. Therefore, the corresponding homomorphism
\mathrm{gr}_{*}(F_{n})\to\mathrm{gr}_{*}(P_{n+1})
is not injective. On the other hand, our main theorem implies that the subgroup
\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}y_{n-1},t\rangle
of
P_{n+1}
is free of rank
n
. But this clearly could not be deduced using the Lie rings, since
y_{i}x_{i}=[y_{i},x_{i}]x_{i}y_{i}
and the two homomorphisms
F_{n}\to P_{n+1}
induce the same map on the level of abelianizations.

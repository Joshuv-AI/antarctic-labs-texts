Submonoid Membership in n-dimensional lamplighter groups and S-unit equations
Ruiwen Dong

Abstract
We show that Submonoid Membership is decidable in n n -dimensional lamplighter groups ( ℤ / p ​ ℤ ) ≀ ℤ n (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}^{n} for any prime p p and integer n n . More generally, we show decidability of Submonoid Membership in semidirect products of the form 𝒴 ⋊ ℤ n \mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n} , where 𝒴 \mathcal{Y} is any finitely presented module over the Laurent polynomial ring 𝔽 p ​ [ X 1 ± , … , X n ± ] \mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}] . Combined with a result of Shafrir (2024), this gives the first example of a group G G and a finite index subgroup G ~ ≤ G \widetilde{G}\leq G , such that Submonoid Membership is decidable in G ~ \widetilde{G} but undecidable in G G . To obtain our decidability result, we reduce Submonoid Membership in 𝒴 ⋊ ℤ n \mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n} to solving S-unit equations over 𝔽 p ​ [ X 1 ± , … , X n ± ] \mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}] -modules. We show that the solution set of such equations is effectively p p -automatic, extending a result of Adamczewski and Bell (2012). As an intermediate result, we also obtain that the solution set of the Knapsack Problem in 𝒴 ⋊ ℤ n \mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n} is effectively p p -automatic.

Introduction

Algorithmic problems in infinite groups

Algorithmic problems concerning groups are a classical topic in algebra and theoretical computer science.
Dating back to the work of Max Dehn from the 1910s, this area has traditionally served as a bridge between geometry, algebra and logic, and has now found numerous applications in automata theory, program analysis and complexity theory
[
7
,
9
,
14
,
23
]
.
One of the central decision problems on groups is the
Submonoid Membership
problem: given a finite number of elements
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}
in a group
G
and an element
g\in G
, does
g
belong to the submonoid generated by
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}
?
In the seminal work of Markov from the 1950s
[
31
]
, it was shown that Submonoid Membership is undecidable for a matrix group of dimension six.
The undecidability result was later strengthened to the group
\mathsf{SL}(4,\mathbb{Z})
of
4\times 4
integer matrices of determinant one
[
32
]
.
Subsequently, a number of positive decidability results were obtained, such as for commutative matrix groups
[
2
]
, low-dimensional matrix groups
[
12
,
34
]
, and certain graph groups
[
27
]
.
See
[
16
,
26
]
for recent surveys.

A generalization of Submonoid Membership is the
Rational Subset Membership
problem: given a rational subset
S
of a group
G
and an element
g\in G
, does
g
belong to
S
?
Here, a subset
S
of a group
G
is called a
rational subset
if there is a finite alphabet
\Sigma
, a monoid homomorphism
\varphi\colon\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow G
, and a regular language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
, such that
\varphi(L)=S
.
In particular, if
L=\Sigma^{*}
, then we recover the definition of Submonoid Membership.
In fact, many decidability results for Submonoid Membership are also proven for the more general Rational Subset Membership, such as in free groups
[
8
]
, abelian groups
[
21
]
, and certain wreath products
[
29
]
.
Nevertheless, Bodart
[
10
]
and Shafrir
1

An example appeared in the Bachelor’s thesis of Potthast
[

]
, based on an unpublished draft of Shafrir.
recently provided examples of groups with undecidable Rational Subset Membership but decidable Submonoid Membership.

A longstanding open problem in computational group theory is whether decidability of Submonoid Membership is preserved under finite extension of groups.
Grunschlag
[
21
]
showed that for any group
G
and a finite index subgroup
\widetilde{G}\leq G
,
Rational Subset Membership
is decidable in
\widetilde{G}
if and only if it is decidable in
G
.
The same statement is also true for the
Subgroup Membership
problem
[
21
]
(given
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k},g
, does
g
belong to the subgroup generated by
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}
?).
Whether the same holds for
Submonoid Membership
remained open.
One of the goals of this paper is to construct a counterexample to this open problem: we construct a group
G
and a finite index subgroup
\widetilde{G}\leq G
, such that Submonoid Membership is decidable in
\widetilde{G}
but undecidable in
G
.

n-dimensional lamplighter groups

In this paper, we study Submonoid Membership in
n
-dimensional lamplighter groups
as well as their generalizations.
The lamplighter groups are well-studied objects in the context of geometric group theory
[
20
,
39
]
.
Given an integer
p\geq 2
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
n
-dimensional lamplighter group, denoted
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, can be most easily defined as the (multiplicative)
2\times 2
upper-triangular matrix group

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}^{a_{1}}X_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}&f\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}\;\middle|\;a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z},\;f\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})[X_{1},X_{1}^{-1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{n}^{-1}]\right\},

(1)

where
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})[X_{1},X_{1}^{-1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{n}^{-1}]
is the Laurent polynomial ring over
n
variables, with coefficients in the finite ring
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}=\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
.
When
p
is prime,
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is actually the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Intuitively, an element in
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}^{n}
can be understood as the following configuration: at each position in the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
there is a
lamp
, each lamp has a
state
among
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}=\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
, and there is a
pointer
at some position in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
In particular, for the element

\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}&f\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}

, the pointer is at position
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the lamp at position
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
has state
s
if the monomial
X_{1}^{z_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{z_{n}}
appearing in
f
has coefficient
s
. In particular, all but finitely many lamps have state 0.
Multiplication in the group
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, like matrix multiplication, corresponds to first moving the pointer by a vector
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, then additively changing the states of the lamps around the new location.

Owing to this geometric interpretation, the study of lamplighter groups has close connections to automata theory, tiling problems, random walks, and graph theory
[
3
,
4
,
24
,
28
,
30
]
.
Lamplighter groups are also important examples of
metabelian groups
, which are some of the most tractable generalization of abelian groups.
Recall that a group
G
is called
metabelian
if it admits a normal subgroup
A
such that both
A
and
G/A
are abelian.
An important amount of research in computational group theory concentrates on metabelian groups, since decision problems in abelian groups are already well-understood.
For example, Cadilhac, Chistikov and Zetzsche
[
11
]
showed decidability of Rational Subset Membership in the
Baumslag-Solitar groups

\mathsf{BS}(1,p)
; Lohrey, Steinberg and Zetzsche
[
28
]
showed decidability of Rational Subset Membership in
1
-dimensional lamplighter groups.
On the other hand, the
2
-dimensional lamplighter groups occupy an interesting position as they have undecidable Rational Subset Membership
[
28
]
but decidable Submonoid Membership
[
35
]
.
The decision algorithm in
[
35
]
cannot be generalized to dimensions larger than 2 as it relies on a reduction to Rational Subset Membership in dimension 1.
It is thus left as an open problem whether Submonoid Membership is decidable in lamplighter groups of dimension
n\geq 3
.

Contributions of this paper

In this paper, we show that Submonoid Membership is decidable in lamplighter groups
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of every dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
prime

p
.
In fact, we prove a much more general result (Theorem
3.1
): Submonoid Membership is decidable for semidirect products
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, where
\mathcal{Y}
is a finitely presented module over the Laurent polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
.

An important consequence of the more general Theorem
3.1
is the resolution of a longstanding open problem (Corollary
3.2
).
Combined with a recent result of Shafrir
[
38
]
, we show that there exist a group
G
and a finite index subgroup
\widetilde{G}\leq G
, such that Submonoid Membership decidable in
\widetilde{G}
but undecidable in
G
.

Our main strategy to decide Submonoid Membership in
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is to reduce it to solving
S-unit equations
over modules: these are equations of the form
x_{1}c_{1}+x_{2}c_{2}+\cdots+x_{m}c_{m}=c_{0}
, where the variables
x_{i}
take value in a multiplicative subgroup of a commutative ring.
We show that the solutions of such S-unit equations over an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
-module forms an effectively
p
-automatic set (Theorem
3.3
), generalizing a result of Adamczewski and Bell
[
1
]
(see also the independent work of Derksen and Masser
[
15
]
for a similar result).
As an intermediate result, we also obtain effective
p
-automaticity for the solution set of the
Knapsack Problem
in
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
: given
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k},g\in\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, find
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
such that
g_{1}^{z_{1}}g_{2}^{z_{2}}\cdots g_{k}^{z_{k}}=g
.
We give an example (Example
4.9
) where the solution set is
p
-automatic but not semilinear.
This shows that the situation in semidirect products is much more complicated than in the wreath product of two abelian groups, as seen in the work of Ganardi, König, Lohrey, and Zetzsche
[
19
]
.

Note that our decidability results heavily rely on working over the finite ring
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
instead of the infinite ring
\mathbb{Z}
.
Indeed, Submonoid Membership is known to be undecidable
[
29
]
in
\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for
n\geq 1
, and solving S-unit equations over
\mathbb{Z}[X_{1}^{\pm}]
-modules is also known to be undecidable
[
17
]
.
Nevertheless, it is not clear whether the decidability for
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}^{n}
can be generalized to non-prime numbers
p
, for example when
p=p_{1}p_{2}
for two primes
p_{1}\neq p_{2}
. This is closely related to deciding emptiness of the intersection of a
p_{1}
-automatic set and a
p_{2}
-automatic set, which to the best of our knowledge remains an open problem (however, see
[
22
,
25
]
for recent progress).
Progress towards a solution for non-prime
p
will also be the first step towards a classification of metabelian groups with decidable Submonoid Membership, namely by tackling those with torsion commutators.

Preliminaries

Laurent polynomial ring and modules

Let
p
be a prime number, denote by
\mathbb{F}_{p}
the finite field of size
p
.
Denote by
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
the Laurent polynomial ring over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
n
variables: this is the set of polynomials over the variables
X_{1},X_{1}^{-1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{n}^{-1}
, with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, such that
X_{i}X_{i}^{-1}=1
for all
i
.
When
n
is fixed, we denote by
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
the tuple of variables
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
, and
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
is written in short as
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
.

For a vector
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, denote by
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}}
the monomial
X_{1}^{a_{1}}X_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}
.
When
\Lambda
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we denote by
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\Lambda}]\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
the subring of polynomials of the form
\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}\in\Lambda}c_{\boldsymbol{a}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}}
.

Since we work in polynomial rings over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, it is worth pointing out a useful equation, folklorishly dubbed “the freshman’s dream”. For any
f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
, we have
(f+1)^{p}=f^{p}+1
, and more generally
(f+1)^{p^{k}}=f^{p^{k}}+1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

When
R
is a commutative ring (such as
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
), an
R
-module
is defined as an abelian group
(M,+)
along with an operation
\cdot\;\colon R\times M\rightarrow M
satisfying
f\cdot(m+m^{\prime})=f\cdot m+f\cdot m^{\prime}
,
(f+g)\cdot m=f\cdot m+g\cdot m
,
fg\cdot m=f\cdot(g\cdot m)
and
1\cdot m=m
.
For example, for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module by
f\cdot(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})=(fh_{1},\ldots,fh_{d})
.
We often use a bold symbol
\boldsymbol{h}
to denote a vector
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}
.

Given elements
\boldsymbol{h}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{h}_{k}
in an
R
-module
M
, we say that they
generate
the
R
-module

\sum_{i=1}^{k}R\cdot\boldsymbol{h}_{i}\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{h}_{i}\;\middle|\;r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}\in R\right\}\subseteq M.

Given submodules
M,M^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}
such that
M\supseteq M^{\prime}
, we define the quotient
M/M^{\prime}\coloneqq\{\overline{m}\mid m\in M\}
where
\overline{m}_{1}=\overline{m}_{2}
if and only if
m_{1}-m_{2}\in M^{\prime}
.
This quotient is also an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module.
We say that an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module
\mathcal{Y}
is
finitely presented
if it can be written as a quotient
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}/M
for a finitely generated submodule
M
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
We call a
finite presentation
of
\mathcal{Y}
the generators of such
M
.
Every finitely generated
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module admits a finite presentation.

Given a finitely presented
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
-module
\mathcal{Y}
, we can define the following group using
semidirect product
:

\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}\coloneqq\{(y,\boldsymbol{a})\mid y\in\mathcal{Y},\boldsymbol{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\};

(2)

multiplication and inversion in this group are defined by

(y,\boldsymbol{a})\cdot(y^{\prime},\boldsymbol{a}^{\prime})=\big(y+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}}\cdot y^{\prime},\boldsymbol{a}+\boldsymbol{a}^{\prime}\big),\quad(y,\boldsymbol{a})^{-1}=\big(\!-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{-\boldsymbol{a}}\cdot y,-\boldsymbol{a}\big).

(3)

The neutral element of
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is
(0,\boldsymbol{0})
, where
\boldsymbol{0}
denotes the zero vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Intuitively, the element
(y,\boldsymbol{a})
can be seen as a
2\times 2
matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}}&y\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}
, where group multiplication is represented by matrix multiplication.
We have the formula
(f,\boldsymbol{a})(y,\boldsymbol{0})(f,\boldsymbol{a})^{-1}=(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}}\cdot y,\boldsymbol{0})
.
Note that

\mathcal{Y}\cong\{(y,\boldsymbol{0})\mid y\in\mathcal{Y}\}
, and there is a projection map
\pi\colon\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n},\,(y,\boldsymbol{a})\mapsto\boldsymbol{a}

.

In particular, if we take

-dimensional lamplighter group

p-automatic sets

We recall the standard notion of

-automatic subsets

\mathbb{Z}
, and more generally,

-automatic subsets of

Let
\Sigma

\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_{I},F)

where

\delta\colon Q\times\Sigma\rightarrow Q

For a state

3
Main results and consequences

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p
be a prime number,
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be a tuple of variables and
\mathcal{Y}
be a finitely presented
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module.
Then, Submonoid Membership is decidable in the group
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

In particular, taking
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
, we immediately obtain decidability of Submonoid Membership in the
n
-dimensional lamplighter groups
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for prime
p
.

We say a group is
virtually abelian
if it has a finite index subgroup that is abelian.
A recent result of Shafrir
[
38
, Theorem 8.1]
shows that membership problem in a
fixed
rational subset
S
of a group
L
can be reduced to the membership problem in a fixed submonoid
M
of the group
L\times H
, where
H
is an explicitly constructed finitely generated virtually abelian group.
Combined with Theorem
3.1
, this allows us to construct an example where decidability of Submonoid Membership is not preserved under finite extension of groups:

Corollary 3.2
.

There exist a group
G
and a finite index subgroup
\widetilde{G}\leq G
, such that Submonoid Membership is decidable in
\widetilde{G}
but undecidable in
G
.

Proof.

By
[
28
, Theorem 10]
, there is a rational subset
S
of the two-dimensional lamplighter group
L\coloneqq(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, whose membership problem (given
g\in L
, whether
g\in S
?) is undecidable.
By Shafrir’s result
[
38
, Theorem 8.1]
, the membership problem in
S
reduces to the membership problem in a finitely generated submonoid of the group
G\coloneqq L\times H
, where
H
is finitely generated virtually abelian.
Therefore, Submonoid Membership is undecidable in
G=L\times H
.

Since
H
is finitely generated virtually abelian, it has a finite index subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\leq H
for some
d

Let
\widetilde{G}\coloneqq L\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, so the index
[G\colon\widetilde{G}]=[H\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
is finite.
We now show that Submonoid Membership is decidable in
\widetilde{G}
.

Note that
\widetilde{G}=L\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\left((\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}^{2}\right)\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can be written as a semidirect product
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{d+2}
, where
\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1}^{\pm},X_{2}^{\pm}]
is considered as an
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1}^{\pm},X_{2}^{\pm},X_{3}^{\pm}\ldots,X_{d+2}^{\pm}]
-module where the elements

act trivially. That is,
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{d+2}^{\pm}]/\sum_{i=3}^{d+2}\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{d+2}^{\pm}]\cdot(X_{i}-1)

, Submonoid Membership is decidable in

.
∎

gg_{j_{b}}^{-1}\cdots g_{j_{2}}^{-1}g_{j_{1}}^{-1}\in H\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 12.29114pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 12.29114pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.43451pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 15.46342pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}}}}}H\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 24.08228pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}g_{j_{2}}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 24.08228pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}g_{j_{2}}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 25.75551pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}g_{j_{2}}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 26.78442pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}g_{j_{2}}}}}}H\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 40.88725pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}g_{j_{2}}\cdots g_{j_{b}}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 40.88725pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}g_{j_{2}}\cdots g_{j_{b}}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 40.59035pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}g_{j_{2}}\cdots g_{j_{b}}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 41.61926pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{j_{1}}g_{j_{2}}\cdots g_{j_{b}}}}}}H,

which is a product of conjugate groups.
This finishes the reduction from Submonoid Membership to membership in products of conjugate groups.
∎

In particular, for the case of
G=\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, Proposition
4.1
is effective, meaning one can explicitly compute the elements
q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}\in G
and the generators of
H
.

Example 4.2

(label=exa:cont)
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1}^{\pm},X_{2}^{\pm}]
considered as an
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1}^{\pm},X_{2}^{\pm}]
-module, so
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is the 2-dimensional lamplighter group
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Let
g_{1}=\big(1+X_{2},\,(2,0)\big),g_{2}=\big(1,\,(-2,0)\big),g_{3}=\big(1+X_{1},\,(0,1)\big)
be the generators of a submonoid, and let
g=\big(X_{2}^{2},\,(4,2)\big)
.

We have
\pi(g_{1})=(2,0),\;\pi(g_{2})=(-2,0),\;\pi(g_{3})=(0,1)
, so the
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
-cone they generate is
\mathcal{C}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid y\geq 0\}
.
Its maximal subspace is
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\{(x,0)\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}
.
We have
\pi(g_{1}),\pi(g_{2})\in\mathcal{C}_{0}
and
\pi(g_{3})\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{0}
.

Suppose
g=g_{i_{1}}g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{s}}
with
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{s}\in\{1,2,3\}
.
Let
v=(0,1)
.
Since
v^{\top}\cdot\pi(g)=v^{\top}\cdot(4,2)=2
, and
v^{\top}\cdot\pi(g_{1})=v^{\top}\cdot\pi(g_{2})=0,\;v^{\top}\cdot\pi(g_{3})=1
, the element
g_{3}
must appear exactly twice in the product
g_{i_{1}}g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{s}}
.
Therefore,
g
must be of the form
w_{1}g_{3}w_{2}g_{3}w_{3}
, where
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}
are words over
\{g_{1},g_{2}\}
.
Let
H
be the monoid generated by
g_{1},g_{2}
: this is actually a group because
(g_{1}g_{2})^{2}=(0,\boldsymbol{0})
.
The above discussion shows
g\in\langle{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}}\rangle
if and only if
g\in Hg_{3}Hg_{3}H
: this is equivalent to
gg_{3}^{-2}\in H\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.55528pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{3}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.55528pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{3}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.69865pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{3}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.72757pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{3}}}}}H\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.55528pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{3}^{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.55528pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{3}^{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.69865pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{3}^{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.72757pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{g_{3}^{2}}}}}H
.

\blacksquare

Remark 4.3
.

If

and
\pi(H)
is of rank one (i.e.
\pi(H)\cong\mathbb{Z}
) as in Example
4.2
, then membership problem in the product
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.49031pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.51923pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}H\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.49031pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.51923pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}H\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.7879pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.7879pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.95905pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.98796pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}H
can be reduced to Rational Subset Membership in the 1-dimensional lamplighter group

[
29
]
. In fact, this is the key idea in Shafrir’s solution
[
35
]
for Submonoid Membership in 2-dimensional lamplighter groups.
However, when the dimension

is greater or equal to three, the rank of
\pi(H)
may be greater or equal to two: in this case, the membership problem in
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.49031pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.51923pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}H\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.49031pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.51923pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}H\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.7879pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.7879pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.95905pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.98796pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}H
has been open and will be solved in the rest of this paper.
Notably, we do
not
want to reduce it to Rational Subset Membership in lamplighter groups of dimension
\geq 2
, as it is undecidable
[
28
]
.

Step 2: from Group Product Membership to Knapsack Problem

The goal of this step is to reduce membership problem in the product
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.49031pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.51923pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}H\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.49031pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.51923pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}H\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.7879pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.7879pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.95905pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.98796pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}H
to a version of the
Knapsack Problem
in groups.
Namely, we will prove:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
G=\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
where
\mathcal{Y}
is a finitely presented
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
-module.
Let
g,q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}\in G
and let
H\leq G
be a finitely generated subgroup.
Then, one can effectively find a group
G^{\prime}=\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n^{\prime}}
, where
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
is a finitely presented
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n^{\prime}}^{\pm}]
-module, as well as elements
g^{\prime},h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in G^{\prime}
, such that

g\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.49031pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.51923pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}H\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.49031pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.51923pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{2}}}}}H\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.7879pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.7879pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.95905pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.98796pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}H

if and only if there exist
z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

h_{1}^{z_{1}}h_{2}^{z_{2}}\cdots h_{m}^{z_{m}}=g^{\prime}.

(7)

We now proceed to prove Proposition
4.4
.
Recall that
\mathcal{Y}
can be identified with the subgroup
\mathcal{Y}\times\{\boldsymbol{0}\}=\ker(\pi)
of
G=\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
First, we give the following classic result on the structure of subgroups
H\leq G
.
Note that
\pi(H)\leq\pi(G)=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
d\leq n
be the rank of
\pi(H)
.

Lemma 4.5

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
or
[
36
, Lemma 2]
)
.

Let
G=\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
H\leq G
be a finitely generated subgroup. Let
\boldsymbol{a}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{a}_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\pi(H)
, and choose elements
h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}\in H
so that
\pi(h_{i})=\boldsymbol{a}_{i},i=1,\ldots,d
.
Then, the subgroup
\mathcal{Y}\cap H
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pi(H)}]
-module, whose finite presentation can be effectively computed. Furthermore,
H
is generated by
h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}
and the set
\mathcal{Y}\cap H
.

Example 4.6

(continues=exa:cont)
.

As in Example
4.2
, let
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1}^{\pm},X_{2}^{\pm}]
.
Consider the subgroup
H\leq\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{2}
generated by
g_{1}=\big(1+X_{2},\,(2,0)\big)
and
g_{2}=\big(1,\,(-2,0)\big)
.
Then
\pi(H)
is the subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
generated by
\boldsymbol{a}_{1}\coloneqq(2,0)
, it has rank
d=1
.
We can choose
h_{1}\coloneqq g_{1}
in Lemma
4.5
.

The subgroup
\mathcal{Y}\cap H
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pi(H)}]=\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{2},X_{1}^{-2}]
-module. We have
g_{1}g_{2}=\big(1+X_{2}+X_{1}^{2},\boldsymbol{0}\big)
.
We claim that
1+X_{2}+X_{1}^{2}
is the generator of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{2},X_{1}^{-2}]
-module
\mathcal{Y}\cap H
:
that is,
\mathcal{Y}\cap H=\{F\cdot(1+X_{2}+X_{1}^{2})\mid F\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{2},X_{1}^{-2}]\}
.
Indeed, recall the formula
(f,\boldsymbol{a})(y,\boldsymbol{0})(f,\boldsymbol{a})^{-1}=(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}}\cdot y,\boldsymbol{0})
.
For each
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
g_{1}^{m}(g_{1}g_{2})g_{1}^{-m}=g_{1}^{m}\big(1+X_{2}+X_{1}^{2},\boldsymbol{0}\big)g_{1}^{-m}=\big(X_{1}^{2m}\cdot(1+X_{2}+X_{1}^{2}),\boldsymbol{0}\big)
.
So
X_{1}^{2m}\cdot(1+X_{2}+X_{1}^{2})\in\mathcal{Y}\cap H
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Consequently,
F\cdot(1+X_{2}+X_{1}^{2})\in\mathcal{Y}\cap H
for all
F\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{2},X_{1}^{-2}]
.
It is not difficult to show that
\{F\cdot(1+X_{2}+X_{1}^{2})\mid F\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{2},X_{1}^{-2}]\}
is indeed all the elements in
\mathcal{Y}\cap H
, but we will omit the details.
Note that
\mathcal{Y}\cap H
is finitely generated as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}^{2},X_{1}^{-2}]
-module but not finitely generated
as an abelian group
.

Finally, for any element
h=(f,2z)\in H
, we have
\pi(h_{1}^{-z}h)=\boldsymbol{0}
, so
(y,\boldsymbol{0})\coloneqq h_{1}^{-z}h\in\mathcal{Y}\cap H
.
Consequently,
h
can actually be written in a “canonical form”
h_{1}^{z}\,(y,\boldsymbol{0})
, where
z\in\mathbb{Z}
and
y\in\mathcal{Y}\cap H
.
Such canonical forms will be extensively used below.

\blacksquare

Define

\mathcal{Y}_{i}\coloneqq\mathcal{Y}\cap\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.75533pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.78424pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}H\right),\quad i=1,\ldots,k.

Note that for any
q\in G
, we have
\pi(\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 4.41634pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 4.41634pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.58748pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.6164pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q}}}}H)=\pi(H)
because
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is abelian. Therefore
\pi(\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.31917pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.49031pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.51923pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{1}}}}}H)=\cdots=\pi(\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.7879pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.7879pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.95905pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.98796pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{k}}}}}H)=\pi(H)
.
So by Lemma
4.5
, all the
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
’s are finitely generated
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pi(H)}]
-modules.
Let
\boldsymbol{a}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{a}_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\pi(H)
.
For each
1\leq i\leq k
, choose elements
h_{i,1},\ldots,h_{i,d}\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.75533pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.78424pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}H
so that
\pi(h_{i,1})=\boldsymbol{a}_{1},\ldots,\pi(h_{i,d})=\boldsymbol{a}_{d}
.
Then,
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.75533pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.78424pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}H
is generated by
h_{i,1},\ldots,h_{i,d}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
.

Fix any

. Since
\boldsymbol{a}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{a}_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\pi(\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.75533pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.78424pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}H)

h\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.75533pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.78424pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}H

This shows
\pi(h_{i,d}^{-z_{i,d}}\cdots h_{i,2}^{-z_{i,2}}h_{i,1}^{-z_{i,1}}h)=\boldsymbol{0}
, so
h_{i,d}^{-z_{i,d}}\cdots h_{i,2}^{-z_{i,2}}h_{i,1}^{-z_{i,1}}h\in\ker(\pi)\cap\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.58418pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.75533pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.78424pt\mathopen{\vphantom{H}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{q_{i}}}}}H=\mathcal{Y}_{i}

Consequently,

is equal to
h_{i,1}^{z_{i,1}}h_{i,2}^{z_{i,2}}\cdots h_{i,d}^{z_{i,d}}(y_{i},\boldsymbol{0})
for some

.

The form of this product of powers gives us the motivation to reduce membership in group products to the Knapsack Problem.
Denote

\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}\coloneqq\mathcal{Y}_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{Y}_{k}.

Again we identify
\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}
with the subgroup
\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}\times\{\boldsymbol{0}\}
of
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We show the following:

Lemma 4.7

h_{1}^{n_{1}}h_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots h_{m}^{n_{m}}=g
if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{i-1}}\cdot\frac{1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{i}-\boldsymbol{z}_{i-1}}}{1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}_{i}}}\cdot f_{i}

\displaystyle=f,

(12)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{z}_{m}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{b}.

(13)

Write
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}/N
for some
N\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}
.
Considering
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},f,
as elements in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}
, Equation (
12
) is equivalent to

\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{i-1}}-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{i}})\prod_{j\neq i}(1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}_{j}})\cdot f_{i}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}_{i}})\cdot f\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}_{i}})\cdot N.

Letting
c_{0}\coloneqq-\prod_{j\neq 1}(1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}_{j}})\cdot f_{1}+\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}_{i}})\cdot f
,
\;c_{i}\coloneqq\prod_{j\neq(i+1)}(1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}_{j}})\cdot f_{i+1}-\prod_{j\neq i}(1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}_{j}})\cdot f_{i},\;i=1,\ldots,m-1
and
c_{m}\coloneqq-\prod_{j\neq m}(1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}_{j}})\cdot f_{m}
, the equation above is equivalent to the following equation in
M\coloneqq\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}/\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{a}_{i}})N
:

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{1}}\cdot c_{1}+\cdots+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{m}}\cdot c_{m}=c_{0}.

(14)

Therefore, we have
h_{1}^{n_{1}}h_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots h_{m}^{n_{m}}=g
if and only if
\boldsymbol{z}_{i}\coloneqq n_{1}\boldsymbol{a}_{1}+\cdots+n_{i}\boldsymbol{a}_{i},i=1,\ldots,m
satisfy Equations (
13
) and (
14
). By Theorem
3.3
, the set
\mathcal{Z}
of
(\boldsymbol{z}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{z}_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}
satisfying (
13
) and (
14
) is
p
-automatic.
Define the map
\varphi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{mn},(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\mapsto(n_{1}\boldsymbol{a}_{1},n_{1}\boldsymbol{a}_{1}+n_{2}\boldsymbol{a}_{2},\ldots,n_{1}\boldsymbol{a}_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\boldsymbol{a}_{m})
. Then the solution set of
h_{1}^{n_{1}}h_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots h_{m}^{n_{m}}=g
is
\varphi^{-1}(\mathcal{Z})
. Since
\mathcal{Z}
is
p
-automatic and
\varphi
is linear,
\varphi^{-1}(\mathcal{Z})
is also
p
-automatic (see Lemma
2.3
).

Case 2: some of the
\boldsymbol{a}_{i}
’s are
\boldsymbol{0}
.

We reduce this case to the previous case. Let
i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}
be the indices where
\boldsymbol{a}_{i}=\boldsymbol{0}
.
We rewrite the equation
h_{1}^{n_{1}}h_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots h_{m}^{n_{m}}=g
as

\left(h_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots h_{i_{1}-1}^{n_{i_{1}-1}}\right)h_{i_{1}}^{n_{i_{1}}}\left(h_{i_{1}+1}^{n_{i_{1}+1}}\cdots h_{i_{2}-1}^{n_{i_{2}-1}}\right)h_{i_{2}}^{n_{i_{2}}}\cdots\left(h_{i_{s}+1}^{n_{i_{s}+1}}\cdots h_{m}^{n_{m}}\right)=g.

Since
\boldsymbol{a}_{i_{1}}=\cdots=\boldsymbol{a}_{i_{s}}=\boldsymbol{0}
and
p\cdot f_{i_{1}}=\cdots=p\cdot f_{i_{s}}=0
, we have
h_{i_{1}}^{p}=\cdots=h_{i_{s}}^{p}=(0,\boldsymbol{0})
. Therefore, the powers
h_{i_{1}}^{n_{i_{1}}},h_{i_{2}}^{n_{i_{2}}},\ldots,
and
h_{i_{s}}^{n_{i_{s}}}
can only take on finitely many different values (at most
p
each). It suffices to show that for each value
h_{i_{1}}^{n_{i_{1}}}=g_{1},\ldots,h_{i_{s}}^{n_{i_{s}}}=g_{s}
, the solution set of

\left(h_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots h_{i_{1}-1}^{n_{i_{1}-1}}\right)g_{1}\left(h_{i_{1}+1}^{n_{i_{1}+1}}\cdots h_{i_{2}-1}^{n_{i_{2}-1}}\right)g_{2}\cdots\left(h_{i_{s}+1}^{n_{i_{s}+1}}\cdots h_{m}^{n_{m}}\right)=g

is effectively
p
-automatic (recall that a union of effectively
p
-automatic sets is effectively
p
-automatic, see Lemma
2.3
).
But the above equation is equivalent to the Knapsack equation

h_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots h_{i_{1}-1}^{n_{i_{1}-1}}\left(g_{1}h_{i_{1}+1}g_{1}^{-1}\right)^{n_{i_{1}+1}}\cdots\left(g_{1}h_{i_{2}-1}g_{1}^{-1}\right)^{n_{i_{2}-1}}\left(g_{1}g_{2}h_{i_{2}+1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}\right)^{n_{i_{2}+1}}\cdots\\
\cdots\left(g_{1}g_{2}\dots g_{s}h_{m}g_{s}^{-1}\cdots g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}\right)^{n_{m}}=gg_{s}^{-1}\cdots g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}.

By Case 1, the solution set of the above equation is indeed effectively
p
-automatic.
∎

Combining the three steps in this section, we have proven Theorem
3.1
:

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Proposition
4.1
, Proposition
4.4
and Theorem
3.4
, Submonoid Membership in
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
reduces to checking whether the solution set of the Knapsack Equation (
5
) is empty. Since the solution set is effectively
p
-automatic, its emptiness is decidable (Lemma
2.3
).
∎

5
S-unit equation over modules

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
3.3
on S-unit equations over modules.

See
3.3

As a comparison, S-unit equations over
fields
are an intensively studied area in number theory. In the positive characteristic case, Adamczewski and Bell gave the following result. (Independently, Derksen and Masser
[
15
]
also proved a similar result.)

Theorem 5.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
, let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\in K^{*}
, and let
\overline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\left(K^{*}\right)^{n}
. Then the set of solutions
(\boldsymbol{z}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{z}_{m})\in\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)^{m}
for the equation

\overline{x}^{\boldsymbol{z}_{1}}c_{1}+\cdots+\overline{x}^{\boldsymbol{z}_{m}}c_{m}=1

is an effectively
p
-automatic subset of
\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)^{m}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{nm}
.

Our objective is to generalize Theorem
5.1
from
fields
to
modules
. Notably, we need to replace the field
K
by the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
, and replace the elements
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
in
K^{*}
by elements in the finitely presented
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module
M
.
To give a general idea of our approach, consider the following example.

Example 5.2

(label=exa:Sunit)
.

For simplicity, we present this example using regular polynomial rings instead of Laurent polynomial rings. Consider the equation

X^{a}+Y^{b}=1

(15)

in the
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]
-module
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}(X+Y+1)
. Here, the variables are
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
.
(Technically, in order to match the form of the equation in Theorem
3.3
, one needs to consider the equation
X^{a}Y^{c}+X^{d}Y^{b}=1
instead of (
15
). However, to keep this example simple, we only consider the solutions with
c=d=0
.)

Equation (
15
) in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}(X+Y+1)
is equivalent to
Y^{2}(X+Y+1)\mid X^{a}+Y^{b}+1
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]
, which in turn is equivalent to “
Y^{2}\mid X^{a}+Y^{b}+1
and
X+Y+1\mid X^{a}+Y^{b}+1
”.
In other words, Equation (
15
) holds in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}(X+Y+1)
if and only if it holds in both
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}
and in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/(X+Y+1)
.

The
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]
-module
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}
can be considered as a finitely generated
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]
-module
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]+Y\cdot\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]^{2}
by
1\mapsto(1,0),\;Y\mapsto(0,1)
.
Considered as an equation in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]^{2}
, the equation
X^{a}+Y^{b}=1
becomes

(X^{a},0)=(1,0)\;\text{ if }\;b\geq 2,\quad(X^{a},1)=(1,0)\;\text{ if }\;b=1,\quad(X^{a}+1,0)=(1,0)\;\text{ if }\;b=0.

So the solution set of
X^{a}+Y^{b}=1
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}
is
\mathcal{Z}_{1}\coloneqq\{(a,b)\mid a=0,\;b\geq 2\}
.

The
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]
-module
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/(X+Y+1)
can be considered as the
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]
-module
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]
through the variable substitution
Y=X+1
.
Under this variable substitution, the equation
X^{a}+Y^{b}=1
becomes
X^{a}+(X+1)^{b}=1
, so its solution set is
\mathcal{Z}_{2}\coloneqq\{(a,b)\mid a=b\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}\}
. This can be shown similar to Example
4.9
, or by directly applying Theorem
5.1
on the quotient field
K=\mathbb{F}_{2}(X)
.

Thus, the solution set of Equation (
15
) in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}(X+Y+1)
is
\mathcal{Z}_{1}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{2}=\emptyset
.

\blacksquare

Example
5.2
illustrates the idea that, to solve an equation in a module
M
, we first “decompose” it into several equations in
coprimary modules

M_{1},\ldots,M_{l}
(such as
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}
and
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/(X+Y+1)
). This will be formalized in Lemma
5.6
.
Then, for the equation in each coprimary module, we “eliminate” variables to simplify the quotients (for example
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]^{2}
and
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/(X+Y+1)\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]
both eliminate the variable
Y
). In general, variable elimination might not be directly possible, and we need to perform a “change of variables” using
Noether Normalization
. This will be formalized in Lemma
5.8
and Lemma
5.9
.
Finally, we show that each such equation “without quotient” (more precisely, in
torsion-free
modules) has a
p
-automatic solution set, by reducing to the case of fields (for example, considering equations over
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]
as equations over the quotient field
\mathbb{F}_{2}(X)
). This will be formalized in Lemma
5.11
.
Thus, the solution set of the original equation over
M
is an intersection of effectively
p
-automatic sets, and is therefore also effectively
p
-automatic.
This approach is inspired by Derkson’s proof of the Skolem-Mahler-Lech theorem in modules over positive characteristic
[
13
, Chapter 9]
.

The starting point of our proof is the following deep theorem by Adamczewski and Bell, generalizing Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and let
D
be a positive integer. Let
V
be a Zariski closed subset of
\mathsf{GL}_{D}(K)
and let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{N}
be commuting matrices in
\mathsf{GL}_{D}(K)
. Then the set
\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\mid A_{1}^{z_{1}}A_{2}^{z_{2}}\cdots A_{n}^{z_{N}}\in V\}
is effectively
p
-automatic.

For effectiveness reasons, we need to suppose
K
to be an effectively computable field (field where all arithmetic operations are effective): this will be the case in all our applications.
We will not give the formal definition of a
Zariski closed subset
because in our application of Theorem
5.3
, the set
V
will be of the form

V=\{A\in\mathsf{GL}_{D}(K)\mid BAv=0^{d}\}

(16)

for some
d\in\mathbb{N},B\in K^{d\times D},v\in K^{D\times 1}
. This is a linear subset of
\mathsf{GL}_{D}(K)
and hence Zariski closed.
For a tuple of commutative matrices
\overline{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\left(\mathsf{GL}_{d}(K)\right)^{n}
and a vector
\boldsymbol{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, denote

\overline{A}^{\boldsymbol{z}}\coloneqq A_{1}^{z_{1}}A_{2}^{z_{2}}\cdots A_{n}^{z_{n}},

similar to our notation for monomials.
In the case where
V
has the form (
16
), Theorem
5.3
gives the following corollary, which can be considered as a version of Theorem
3.3
where the module
M
is replaced by a vector space over a field
K
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and let
\overline{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\left(\mathsf{GL}_{d}(K)\right)^{n}
be a tuple of commuting matrices.
Let
c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
be elements in the
d
-dimensional vector space
K^{d}
.
Then, the set of solutions
(\boldsymbol{z}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{z}_{m})\in\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)^{m}
for the equation

\overline{A}^{\boldsymbol{z}_{1}}c_{1}+\cdots+\overline{A}^{\boldsymbol{z}_{m}}c_{m}=c_{0}

(17)

is an effectively
p
-automatic subset of
\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)^{m}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{nm}
.

Proof.

For any
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m+1}\in\mathsf{GL}_{d}(K)
, we denote by
\operatorname{diag}(B_{1},\ldots,B_{m+1})
the block diagonal matrix in
\mathsf{GL}_{(m+1)d}(K)
whose block diagonal entries are
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m+1}
.
Let
I
denote the identity matrix of dimension
d
and denote
D=(m+1)d
.
Consider the following elements in
\mathsf{GL}_{D}(K)
.
For
j=1,\ldots,n
, let
A_{1j}\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}(A_{j},I,\ldots,I),A_{2j}=\operatorname{diag}(I,A_{j},\ldots,I),\ldots,A_{mj}=\operatorname{diag}(I,\ldots,A_{j},I)
.

Furthermore, let
v\coloneqq(c_{1}^{\top},c_{2}^{\top},\ldots,c_{m}^{\top},c_{0}^{\top})^{\top}\in K^{D\times 1}
, and
B\coloneqq(I,I,\ldots,I,-I)\in K^{d\times D}
.
Then, Equation (
17
) is equivalent to

B\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}^{z_{ij}}\right)v=0^{d}.

(18)

We then apply Theorem
5.3
to the set

V=\{A\in\mathsf{GL}_{D}(K)\mid BAv=0^{d}\}

and the commuting matrices
A_{ij},i=1,\ldots,m;j=1,\ldots,n
.
This shows that the set of solutions
(z_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n}
for Equation (
18
) is an effectively
p
-automatic subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{nm}
.
∎

To pass from vector spaces to modules, we now introduce the
primary decomposition
of a module.
First, we recall some standard definitions and tools from commutative algebra
[
18
]
.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring (for example,
R=\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
for some field
\mathbb{K}
).

[(1)]

1.

An
ideal
of
R
is an
R
-submodule of
R
. An ideal
I\subseteq R
is called
prime
if
I\neq R
, and for every
a,b\in R
,
ab\in I
implies
a\in I
or
b\in I
. Prime ideals are usually denoted by the Gothic letter
\mathfrak{p}
.

2.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. The
annihilator
of an element
m\in M
, denoted by
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(m)
, is the set
\{r\in R\mid r\cdot m=0\}
.

3.

An
R
-module
M
is called
torsion-free
if for every
r\in R,m\in M
,
r\cdot m=0
implies
r=0
or
m=0
. That is,
M
is torsion-free if and only if
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(m)=\{0\}
for all
m\in M\setminus\{0\}
.

4.

A prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset R
is called
associated
to
M
if there exists a non-zero
m\in M
such that
\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(m)
.

5.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module. A submodule
N^{\prime}
of
N
is called
primary
if
N/N^{\prime}
has only one associated prime ideal. If we denote this prime ideal by
\mathfrak{p}
, then
N^{\prime}\subseteq N
is called
\mathfrak{p}
-primary
.

6.

Let
N^{\prime}
be a submodule of a finitely generated
R
-module
N
. The
primary decomposition
of
N^{\prime}
is the writing of
N^{\prime}
as a finite intersection
\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}N_{i}
, where
N_{i}
is a
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-primary submodule of
N
for some prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subset R
.
A primary decomposition always exists
[
18
, Theorem 3.10]
.
If
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
and
N,N^{\prime}
are finitely generated submodules of
R^{d}
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
, then a primary decomposition of
N^{\prime}\subseteq N
can be effectively computed
[
37
]
.

7.

A finitely generated
R
-module
M
is called
coprimary
if the submodule
\{0\}
is primary, that is, if
M
has only one associated prime ideal. If we denote this prime ideal by
\mathfrak{p}
, then
M
is called
\mathfrak{p}
-coprimary
.
If
M
is
\mathfrak{p}
-coprimary, and
m
is a non-zero element in
M
, then
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(m)\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
.

Let
M=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}/N,\;N\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}
be the finite presentation of
M
in Theorem
3.3
.
Let
N=\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}N_{i}
be the primary decomposition of
N
as a submodule of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}
, where
N_{i}
is
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-primary for a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
,
i=1,\ldots,l
.
Then
M_{i}\coloneqq\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}/N_{i}
is
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-coprimary.
The finite presentation of
N_{i}
, and hence
M_{i}
, can be effectively computed (Definition
5.5
(6)).
Since
N\subseteq N_{i}
, there is a canonical map
\rho_{i}\colon M=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}/N\rightarrow M_{i}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}/N_{i}
.
Since
N=\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}N_{i}
, the intersection of kernels
\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\ker(\rho_{i})
is
\{0\}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

For
i=1,\ldots,l
, let
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
denote the set of solutions
(\boldsymbol{z}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{z}_{m})\in\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)^{m}
of the following equation in
M_{i}
:

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{1}}\cdot\rho_{i}(c_{1})+\cdots+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{m}}\cdot\rho_{i}(c_{m})=\rho_{i}(c_{0}).

(19)

Then the solution set of Equation (
4
) is exactly the intersection
\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{Z}_{i}
.

Proof.

Since
\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\ker(\rho_{i})=\{0\}
, an element
y\in M
is zero if and only if
\rho_{i}(y)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,l
.
Equation (
4
) is equivalent to

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{1}}\cdot c_{1}+\cdots+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{m}}\cdot c_{m}-c_{0}=0
.
This holds if and only if

\rho_{i}\left(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{1}}\cdot c_{1}+\cdots+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\boldsymbol{z}_{m}}\cdot c_{m}-c_{0}\right)=0

for all
i=1,\ldots,l
.
Therefore, Equation (
4
) holds if and only if Equation (
19
) holds for all
i
.
Hence, the solution set of Equation (
4
) is exactly
\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{Z}_{i}
.
∎

Example 5.7

(continues=exa:Sunit)
.

In Example
5.2
, we took
R
to be the ring
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]
, and
M
to be the finitely presented
R
-module
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}(X+Y+1)
. In this case, we have
M=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/N
with
N=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]\cdot Y^{2}(X+Y+1)
, whose primary decomposition is
N=N_{1}\cap N_{2},\;N_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]\cdot Y^{2},\,N_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]\cdot(X+Y+1)
. The modules
M_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/N_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/Y^{2}
and
M_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/N_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y]/(X+Y+1)
are respectively
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
-coprimary, where
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is the ideal of
R
generated by
Y
, and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
is the ideal of
R
generated by
X+Y+1
.
The sets
\mathcal{Z}_{1},\mathcal{Z}_{2}
are respectively solutions of the equation
X^{a}+Y^{b}=1
in
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
.

\blacksquare

Our next step is to show that each
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
is effectively
p
-automatic.
If so, then their intersection
\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{Z}_{i}
will also be
p
-automatic (see Lemma
2.3
).
Fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
. Note that Equation (
19
) is now an equation over the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-coprimary
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module
M_{i}
.
This motivates us to consider the quotient ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.
We start with the following staple result in commutative algebra, which can be intuitively understood as performing a “change of variable” to simplify
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.

Lemma 5.8

(Noether Normalization Lemma
[
33
, p.2]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an infinite field and
A
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. Then there exist algebraically independent elements
\widetilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{s}\in A
such that
A
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{K}[\widetilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{s}]
-module.

Ideally we would want to apply the Noether Normalization Lemma to
A=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Even though
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is not an infinite field, we can without loss of generality replace it with its algebraic closure
\mathbb{K}
(which is infinite).
Formally, we replace
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
with
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{K}
and
M_{i}
with
M_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{K}
.
Now
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
and
M_{i}
are respectively an ideal and a module over the ring
\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{K}
. Note that this does not change the solution set
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
.

The classical proof of Noether Normalization Lemma is constructive
[
33
, p.2]
.
In our context, this means that given
A=\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, the lemma explicitly gives the expressions for
\widetilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{s}\in A
as elements in
\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.

Choose
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}\in\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
such that
Y_{i}+\mathfrak{p}_{i}=\widetilde{X}_{i}
for all
i
.
Since
\widetilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{s}
are algebraically independent in
A=\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, we have
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\cap\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]=\{0\}
.
Any finitely generated
\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module
M
is also a
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-module. However,
M
might not be finitely generated as a
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-module.
However, in case
M
is also finitely generated as a
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-module, then a finite presentation can be effectively computed.
This is summarized in the following rather standard result in effective commutative algebra.

Lemma 5.9

(
[
5
, Section 2]
)
.

Let
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}\in\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
.
Suppose we are given the finite presentation of an
\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module
M
, as well as a finite number of generators
m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}
of
M
as a
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-module.
Then one can effectively compute the finite presentation of
M
as a
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-module.

Proof.

Let
M=\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{d}/N
be its finite presentation as a
\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module.
Let
f_{1},\ldots f_{s}
be polynomials in
\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
that correspond to the elements
Y_{1},\ldots Y_{s}
.
Then
M
is also an effectively finitely presented module over the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
by

M\cong\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]^{d}/\big(\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]\cdot N+\sum_{i=1}^{s}(Y_{i}-f_{i})\cdot\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]^{d}\big)
.
Then, we can compute the finite presentation of the
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-submodule of
M
generated by
m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}
(see
[
5
, Corollary 2.5]
).
This submodule is exactly
M
.
∎

Example 5.10

(continues=exa:Sunit)
.

Consider the ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
of
\mathbb{K}[X,Y]
generated by
Y
.
Let
A
be the ring
\mathbb{K}[X,Y]/\mathfrak{p}_{1}=\mathbb{K}[X,Y]/Y
.
Then in the Noether Normalization Lemma we can let
s=1
, and
\widetilde{X}_{1}=X+\mathfrak{p}_{1}
. So
\mathbb{K}[X,Y]/Y\cong\mathbb{K}[\widetilde{X}_{1}]
is a
\mathbb{K}[\widetilde{X}_{1}]
-module.
Let
Y_{1}=X_{1}
, then the
\mathbb{K}[X,Y]
-module
M_{1}=\mathbb{K}[X,Y]/Y^{2}

Lemma 5.11
.

The set
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
is effectively
p
-automatic.

Proof.

The main line of our proof follows
[
13
, Lemma 9.7-9.8]
, with additional remarks given on effectiveness.
As above, we can replace the base field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
by its algebraic closure
\mathbb{K}
.

Since
M_{i}
is
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-coprimary, we can compute
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{t}M_{i}=0
(such
t
exists by the ascending chain condition
[
18
, Chapter 1.4]
).
We have

M_{i}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}M_{i}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{2}M_{i}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{t}M_{i}=0
.
Because
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\cdot(\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{j}M_{i}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{j+1}M_{i})=0
, each quotient
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{j}M_{i}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{j+1}M_{i}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-module with effectively computable generators, and therefore a finitely generated
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-module with effectively computable generators.
It follows that the
\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module
M_{i}
is also finitely generated as a
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-module, with effectively computable generators.
Since the finite presentation of
M_{i}
as a
\mathbb{K}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module is given, we can compute the finite presentation of
M_{i}
as a
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-module by Lemma
5.9
.

Since
M_{i}
is
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-coprimary, the annihilator of any non-zero element in
M_{i}
as a
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-module is contained in
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\cap\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]=\{0\}
(see Definition
5.5
(7)). Therefore,
M_{i}
is a finitely generated torsion-free
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
-module (see Definition
5.5
(3)).

Consider the quotient field
K\coloneqq\mathbb{K}(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s})
of
\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]
. The localization
\widetilde{M_{i}}\coloneqq M_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]}K
is a finite dimensional
K
-linear space, so
\widetilde{M_{i}}\cong K^{d}
for some integer
d
.
Because
M_{i}
is torsion-free, the canonical map
\varphi\colon M_{i}\rightarrow M_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}]}K
is injective.
The linear transformations
A_{j}\colon\widetilde{M_{i}}\rightarrow\widetilde{M_{i}},y\mapsto X_{j}\cdot y,
commute for
j=1,\ldots,n
, and they are respectively inverses of the transformations
A_{j}^{-1}\colon\widetilde{M_{i}}\rightarrow\widetilde{M_{i}},y\mapsto X_{j}^{-1}\cdot y
. Hence,
A_{j}
is in
\mathsf{GL}_{d}(K)
for
j=1,\ldots,n
, for a fixed basis of
\widetilde{M_{i}}\cong K^{d}
, and these matrices commute.

Since
\varphi
is injective, Equation (
19
) holds if and only if

\overline{A}^{\boldsymbol{z}_{1}}\cdot\varphi(\rho_{i}(c_{1}))+\cdots+\overline{A}^{\boldsymbol{z}_{m}}\cdot\varphi(\rho_{i}(c_{m}))=\varphi(\rho_{i}(c_{0})),

(20)

where
\overline{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
.
So
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
is the solution set for Equation (
20
).
Note that
K=\mathbb{K}(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s})
is an effective field. We now apply Corollary
5.4
, and conclude that the solution set for Equation (
20
) is an effectively
p
-automatic subset of
\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)^{m}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{nm}
.
∎

Example 5.12

(continues=exa:Sunit)
.

As above, the
\mathbb{K}[X,Y]
-module
M_{1}=\mathbb{K}[X,Y]/Y^{2}
is isomorphic to the
\mathbb{K}[X]
-module
\mathbb{K}[X]^{2}
by
\mathbb{K}[X,Y]/Y^{2}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{K}[X]^{2},\,f_{0}+Yf_{1}+Y^{2}f_{2}+\cdots\mapsto(f_{0},f_{1})
, for
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\ldots\in\mathbb{K}[X]
.

We would like to solve the equation
X^{a}+Y^{b}=1
in
M_{1}
.
Let
\mathbb{K}(X)
denote the quotient field of
\mathbb{K}[X]
: this is the set of univariate rational functions over the field
\mathbb{K}
.
Note that
\varphi:M_{1}\cong\mathbb{K}[X]^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{K}(X)^{2}
is injective.
Consider
\mathbb{K}(X)^{2}
as a 2-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{K}(X)
, then the map
\varphi(m)\mapsto\varphi(X\cdot m),m\in M_{1},
extends to a linear transformation associated to the matrix

A_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}X&0\\
0&X\\
\end{pmatrix}
,
and the map
\varphi(m)\mapsto\varphi(Y\cdot m),m\in M_{1}
extends to a linear transformation associated to the matrix

A_{Y}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}
.
(Here,
A_{Y}
is not invertible because the variable
Y
is not invertible in
\mathbb{K}[X,Y]
. However in the proof of Lemma
5.11
, we are considering Laurent polynomial rings, so all the associated matrices would be invertible.)

As a result, the equation
X^{a}+Y^{b}=1
in
M_{1}
is equivalent to the equation

A_{X}^{a}\cdot(1,0)^{\top}+A_{Y}^{b}(1,0)^{\top}=(1,0)^{\top}

in the 2-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{K}(X)^{2}
.
Subject to the invertibility of
A_{Y}
, we can then use Corollary
5.4
to conclude that the equation admits an effectively
2
-automatic solution set.

\blacksquare

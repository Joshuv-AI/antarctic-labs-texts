SkinAttn-Net: a multi-level attention-based network for skin lesion segmentation
Authors: Ömer Faruk Söylemez
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Accurate segmentation of skin lesions is essential for automated melanoma detection and dermoscopic image analysis. Traditional convolutional networks often fail to capture both fine-grained lesion boundaries and global contextual information. We porpose SkinAttn-Net, a stage-aware segmentation framework that integrates complementary attention mechanisms according to the functional role of each network component. A vision transformer module is employed at the encoder bottleneck to model long-range dependencies and global context, Convolutional Block Attention Modules to refine intermediate encoder–decoder feature interactions, and Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks to recalibrate skip connections and preserve high-resolution lesion characteristics. The model was extensively evaluated on three widely used dermoscopic datasets, including intra- and cross-dataset experiments, ablation analyses, module-wise dropout sensitivity assessments, and qualitative visualization studies. Quantitative results show Dice scores of 0.926, 0.910, and 0.951 on ISIC 2017, ISIC 2018, and HAM10000, respectively, with consistently high precision, recall, and specificity. Cross-dataset evaluation demonstrates robust generalization across imaging conditions and lesion types. Ablation studies confirm the complementary contributions of different modules, while dropout analyses reveal the effectiveness of moderate regularization in enhancing stability and generalization. Comparisons with state-of-the-art methods highlight superior segmentation performance. Grad-CAM visualizations indicate progressive refinement of lesion boundaries across decoder layers. SkinAttn-Net effectively combines hierarchical attention mechanisms to balance local detail preservation with global contextual modeling, offering a robust, generalizable solution for dermoscopic image analysis and clinical decision support in dermatology.

Introduction

Skin cancer is more prevalent than any other type of cancer in the United States, with an estimated 9500 people being diagnosed each day1. One in five Americans is projected to develop skin cancer by the age of 70, a total occurrence more than all other malignancies combined2,3. Ultraviolet radiation from the sun is a major contributing factor, responsible for around 90% of nonmelanoma skin cancers and 90% of melanoma cases by inducing DNA damage in skin cells4. Individuals with a history of five or more sunburns have double the risk of developing melanoma5. Furthermore, the 5-year survival rate for melanoma has been as high as 99% when detected early1. Therefore, it is critical to address this screening gap by developing and implementing new and effective detection systems.

The term skin lesion encompasses pathological changes in the skin that may involve alterations in coloration, abnormal proliferative activity, or modifications in texture. These lesions encompass a spectrum ranging from benign anomalies to malignant tumors, necessitating thorough visual assessment for accurate diagnosis6. In clinical practice, dermatologists predominantly depend on dermoscopic or macroscopic visual examination, guided by diagnostic cues such as asymmetry, border irregularity, color variegation, and lesion diameter7. While expert assessment remains the gold standard, it is inherently subjective and susceptible to inter-observer variability, particularly in the early stages of malignancy where visual cues may be subtle or ambiguous8,9.

The use of computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) in dermatology has grown to address challenges in human-based skin lesion evaluation10–14. By automating critical steps such as lesion segmentation, CAD systems aim to reduce diagnostic errors arising from variability in clinician experience and interpretation. Accurate and reproducible segmentation is essential, as subtle lesion boundaries, skin artifacts, and diverse morphologies complicate manual evaluation15. Automated segmentation provides quantitative insights that support subsequent diagnostic and prognostic models, minimize observer variability, and enhance dermatologist confidence. By integrating these outputs into decision pipelines, CAD tools enable evidence-based reasoning and consistent triage, improving the transparency, efficiency, and quality of clinical decision-making in dermatology.

CAD systems for skin lesion segmentation have traditionally employed classical image processing techniques, which can be broadly classified into region-based methods (e.g., thresholding, region growing, clustering, superpixel segmentation), boundary-based methods (e.g., edge detection, active contours, level set), and hybrid approaches (e.g., watershed segmentation, Chan-Vese model) that combine region and boundary information. While traditional methods are generally faster and require less data, they often struggle with variations in lesion appearance and artifacts16. On the other hand, deep learning models achieve higher accuracy and robustness by learning complex, hierarchical features directly from raw images. Architectures such as U-Net and its variants have become standard for skin lesion segmentation, effectively capturing both local textures and global context.

Although convolution-based architectures have become standard in skin lesion segmentation, they often treat all features equally without differentiating between important lesion regions and irrelevant background. This limitation can reduce segmentation accuracy, especially in challenging cases with low contrast, irregular boundaries, or artifacts such as hair, bubbles, and illumination variations. Moreover, the local receptive fields of convolutional operations restrict the network’s ability to model long-range dependencies, leading to incomplete feature representations and inaccurate segmentation of complex lesion shapes. In addition, multi-scale information is not always effectively integrated across network stages, causing the loss of fine-grained details in deeper layers.

Recent approaches incorporating multi-level attention mechanisms or hybrid CNN–Transformer designs partially address these issues, yet methodological gaps remain17–19. Hybrid CNN–Transformer models typically restrict self-attention to the bottleneck, leaving intermediate encoder–decoder transitions dependent on purely convolutional processing, which weakens boundary representation17,19. Multi-level attention networks often apply channel or spatial attention uniformly, disregarding the semantic differences between shallow, mid-level, and deep features20,21. Moreover, channel recalibration along skip connections is rarely implemented, despite its importance in preserving high-resolution lesion characteristics.Existing methods lack a coordinated, stage-aware strategy that simultaneously models long-range dependencies, intermediate structural cues, and fine-grained feature importance within a unified hierarchical architecture.

To bridge this gap, we propose a stage-aware attention architecture in which complementary attention mechanisms are assigned according to the functional role of each network component. The novelty of this work lies not in a new attention formulation, but in the explicit functional decomposition and hierarchical placement of existing mechanisms. Transformer-based self-attention is applied at the encoder bottleneck to capture long-range dependencies and global context. CBAM modules refine spatial–channel interactions in intermediate encoder–decoder transitions, which is critical for boundary segmentation. SE blocks recalibrate high-resolution feature responses along skip connections. This coordinated, stage-specific design enables the network to capture global, structural, and fine-grained lesion characteristics in a unified and complementary manner.

This study presents SkinAttn-Net, a segmentation framework that combines convolutional and transformer-based representations within a unified architecture. To improve feature representation and contextual understanding, we introduce three types of attention mechanisms at different stages of the network. At the deepest layer of the encoder, a Vision Transformer (ViT) is employed to model long-range dependencies and global context. To refine intermediate representations, Convolutional Block Attention Modules (CBAM) are integrated into the connections between encoder and decoder blocks. Additionally, Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) attention is applied to the skip connections to recalibrate feature responses and emphasize relevant lesion characteristics. This multi-level attention design aims to improve segmentation accuracy by guiding the network to focus on informative features across different spatial and semantic scales.

The proposed model is evaluated on widely used, publicly available dermoscopic image datasets, including ISIC 2017, ISIC 2018, and HAM10000, commonly adopted in skin lesion segmentation studies. To comprehensively assess segmentation performance, we employ standard evaluation metrics, including Dice coefficient, Jaccard index, accuracy, precision, sensitivity, and specificity. We carefully design the experimental pipeline, including data preprocessing steps, augmentation techniques, and training protocols, to ensure both the reproducibility and robustness of the results. Related work

Prior to the dominance of deep learning–based segmentation, a variety of classical and hybrid algorithms were widely explored for skin lesion segmentation in dermoscopic images. Early classical approaches include clustering and region-based techniques such as k-means clustering and GrabCut, which segment lesions by grouping pixels with similar color or intensity characteristics and refining boundaries through iterative optimizations22,23. Active contour and snake models, including gradient vector flow snakes, were also applied to precisely approximate lesion borders by evolving parametric curves under image gradient forces24. Fuzzy c-means and histogram-based thresholding have been investigated to handle uncertainty and variability in lesion appearance25. More recent hybrid strategies have combined classical preprocessing with machine learning or deep network components, such as pipeline methods that integrate conditional adversarial segmentation with downstream classification to improve both segmentation and subtype discrimination26.

While classical and hybrid segmentation approaches provided valuable insights, they often struggled with complex lesion variability, prompting the adoption of deep learning–based methods. Deep learning has become the dominant approach for skin lesion segmentation in recent years, as it enables models to learn features directly from data and thus handle complex variations in lesion appearance. The encoder-decoder framework, popularized by U-Net27, remains the backbone of many state-of-the-art systems due to its ability to preserve spatial details while capturing context. U-Net and its variants have been widely adopted due to their simplicity and effectiveness28. The original U-Net introduced skip connections that help recover spatial information lost during downsampling. Subsequent works extended this design by integrating residual blocks29, dense connections30, and multi-scale feature fusion31, which have also been employed in the context of skin lesion segmentation.

Lei et al.32 proposed proposed a GAN-based framework integrating a dense convolution U-Net segmentation module with a dual discriminator. The segmentation module uses dense dilated convolutions to capture deep features, while the discriminators focus separately on lesion boundaries and contextual information. Liu et al.33 introduced FCP-Net, which incorporates dilated spatial mapping and channel attention modules in the encoder to extract multi-receptive field features, and establishes skip connections with multi-scale feature fusion in the decoder. Both models demonstrate the utility of specialized modules for capturing spatial and contextual cues in lesion segmentation.

Despite their success, encoder-decoder architectures have inherent limitations, including equal weighting of features and difficulty capturing long-range dependencies. To address these issues, attention mechanisms have been introduced at various network stages. Yu et al.34 proposed EIU-Net, which employs inverted residual blocks and a pyramid squeeze attention module to capture both local and global contextual information. Atrous spatial pyramid pooling and multi-layer fusion integrate multi-scale features and refine boundary representations. Zhang et al.35 introduced ACCPG-Net, a U-shaped network that integrates adaptive channel-context-aware pyramid attention and global feature fusion modules. These modules enhance feature representation and semantic interaction between encoder and decoder stages. Li et al.36 proposed PMJAF-Net, incorporating adaptive spatial attention, pyramidal multi-scale channel attention, and channel attention modules to capture long-range, edge, and multi-scale features. A multi-scale adaptive fusion module further integrates these features across decoding stages to improve lesion segmentation.

Transformer-based approaches have been proposed to model global context and long-range interactions, improving segmentation quality. Xin et al.37 proposed SapFormer, a self-adaptive position-aware network for skin lesion analysis that employs multiple hybrid transformers for multi-scale feature encoding and a transformer decoder to capture fine-grained lesion features. A cross-attention network further refines regional features by leveraging information from other regions, enhancing spatial relationship modeling and feature representation. Xu et al.38 proposed SkinFormer, a transformer-based network for skin lesion analysis that integrates statistical texture representation with structural features. The model employs a kurtosis-guided statistical counting operator and a statistical texture fusion transformer to combine structural and statistical information, while a statistical texture enhancement transformer refines multi-scale features using global attention.

More recent segmentation frameworks have demonstrated competitive performance on ISIC-derived datasets by leveraging alternative modeling paradigms. Chen et al.39 introduced a skin lesion segmentation network combining state-space modeling with convolutional perception, enabling efficient long-range dependency modeling with reduced computational overhead. The method achieved strong boundary segmentation on ISIC 2017 and ISIC 2018 by integrating state-space operators into the encoder pathway. Huang et al.40 proposed FastTrans-Net, a fusion network employing shift-window hierarchical attention and multi-level enhanced feature spaces. By coupling local window attention with hierarchical context aggregation, the model reported notable improvements in lesion representation and global consistency across multiple dermoscopic benchmarks. Yang et al.41 proposed SegDINO, a lightweight framework that utilizes a frozen DINOv3 backbone and a minimal decoder to align multi-level features and produce segmentation masks through a compact MLP head, achieving strong performance across medical and natural-image benchmarks. These studies reflect the growing trend of designing hybrid architectures that unify convolutional inductive biases with transformer-based context modeling within the skin lesion segmentation domain.

Although the aforementioned studies incorporate attention mechanisms or transformer-based modeling to enhance skin lesion segmentation, their designs largely treat attention as a generic enhancement rather than a stage-dependent component. CNN-based attention networks commonly apply channel or spatial attention uniformly across encoder or decoder layers, without differentiating the semantic characteristics of features at different depths. Hybrid CNN–Transformer architectures predominantly introduce self-attention at the bottleneck, focusing on global context aggregation while leaving intermediate feature transitions and skip connections unrefined by adaptive attention. Moreover, existing models rarely consider channel recalibration along skip pathways, despite the critical role of skip connections in preserving fine-grained lesion boundaries and high-resolution spatial details. Consequently, prior work does not explicitly address how distinct attention mechanisms can be functionally coordinated across network stages to jointly model global context, intermediate structural information, and high-resolution feature importance.

In contrast, the proposed SkinAttn-Net adopts a stage-specific attention strategy in which complementary attention mechanisms are assigned according to the representational role of each network component. This design enables a structured integration of global self-attention, local spatial–channel refinement, and skip-level feature recalibration within a single segmentation framework.

Materials and methods

Datasets

The proposed model is evaluated on publicly available dermoscopic datasets released as part of the International Skin Imaging Collaboration challenges, which have become standard benchmarks for skin lesion segmentation research. ISIC 201742, ISIC 201843, and HAM1000044 datasets consist of dermoscopic images collected from multiple clinical centers, encompassing a wide range of lesion types, illumination conditions, resolutions, and acquisition settings. The images exhibit considerable diversity, including both rectangular and circular frames originating from polarized and non-polarized dermoscopy techniques, as well as the presence of artifacts such as ruler marks, hair, and color calibration charts. Each image is accompanied by expert-annotated lesion masks, and the corresponding diagnostic classes were verified through histopathological confirmation. Encoder–decoder architecture

The encoder–decoder architecture is a widely adopted framework in image segmentation, designed to extract hierarchical feature representations while preserving spatial information, and it operates by progressively downsampling the input image through the encoder and reconstructing the segmentation mask via the decoder.

Evaluation metrics

We evaluated the model performance using several widely adopted metrics for segmentation tasks, including Dice similarity coefficient, Jaccard index, accuracy, precision, recall, and specificity, which collectively provide a comprehensive assessment of both overlap quality and pixel-wise classification. The Dice similarity coefficient measures the overlap between predicted and ground truth masks and is particularly effective for imbalanced data, combining aspects of precision and recall. The Jaccard index evaluates the ratio of the intersection to the union of predicted and ground truth masks. Accuracy quantifies the proportion of correctly classified pixels, including both lesion and background, whereas precision evaluates the fraction of predicted positive pixels that are correct. Recall measures the proportion of true positive pixels correctly identified, ensuring that lesion regions are fully captured. Specificity assesses the model’s ability to correctly classify background pixels and avoid false positives. Proposed method

Overall architecture

We propose SkinAttn-Net, a segmentation framework designed to enhance skin lesion segmentation through a stage-aware integration of complementary attention mechanisms within a standard encoder–decoder backbone. Unlike prior hybrid CNN–Transformer or multi-attention architectures that introduce attention in a uniform or bottleneck-only manner, the proposed design explicitly assigns different attention mechanisms to distinct network stages based on their representational roles. The overall architecture, illustrated in Fig. 2, follows a hierarchical organization in which global context modeling, intermediate structural refinement, and high-resolution feature recalibration are handled by separate yet coordinated attention modules. The encoder progressively extracts hierarchical feature representations from input dermoscopic images, while the decoder reconstructs segmentation masks by combining high-level semantic information with low-level spatial details through skip connections. The deepest layer of the encoder serves as a bottleneck, modeling global contextual information. Each encoder and decoder stage consists of convolutional blocks with batch normalization and non-linear activations.

At intermediate stages of the network we employ CBAM to recalibrate feature responses in both encoder and decoder paths. Each CBAM follows the standard two-stage structure: the channel attention branch uses global average- and max-pooled descriptors that are processed by a shared MLP implemented with two 1 × 1 convolutions, while the spatial attention branch uses the concatenation of channel-wise average and max projections followed by a 7 × 7 convolution. In our implementation we introduce light regularization inside both branches by inserting dropout between the two 1 × 1 convolutions of the channel pathway and applying dropout after the spatial convolution. CBAM modules are placed after major encoder transitions (following the initial convolutional block and after ResNet layers 1–4) and at the end of each decoder block, following the two convolutional layers and nonlinearities. This configuration preserves CBAM’s recalibration behavior while providing modest regularization against overfitting. The placement and structure of these modules are depicted in Fig. 3.

A learnable class token is prepended and learned positional embeddings are added; at runtime the implementation adapts the positional embedding length to the current number of patches by slicing or repeating the final position token when necessary. Dropout regularization is applied to multiple components including attention weights, projection layers, MLP blocks, and position embeddings. The transformer returns the class token and the sequence of patch tokens; patch tokens are reshaped back into a tensor and passed to the decoder. During inference, input images pass sequentially through the encoder, where features are progressively downsampled and enriched with attention via CBAM modules. At the bottleneck, the ViT encodes global dependencies, after which the decoder upsamples the feature maps. Skip connections augmented with SE blocks provide high-resolution details, and CBAM modules in decoder layers refine intermediate representations. The output is a segmentation mask with improved accuracy and well-defined lesion boundaries.

Design rationale

The architectural placement of the ViT at the bottleneck is motivated by both representational and computational considerations. At the deepest encoder layer, spatial resolution is minimal while semantic abstraction is maximal, making this stage particularly suitable for global self-attention. Applying ViT at this level enables effective modeling of long-range dependencies and global contextual relationships without incurring the substantial computational overhead that would arise from multi-scale transformer deployment17,18. The ViT configuration consists of 12 transformer blocks with 12 attention heads, a patch size of 1, and an embedding dimension of 768, representing a moderate-capacity transformer that captures global context while remaining computationally efficient.

Restricting the transformer to a single bottleneck stage preserves the inductive biases of convolutional layers in earlier encoder stages, where local texture and boundary cues are critical for lesion characterization45. The decoder, in turn, prioritizes spatial reconstruction and fine-grained detail recovery, for which convolutional operations remain more effective than global self-attention. This design allows the transformer to complement, rather than replace, convolutional feature processing, achieving a balance between computational efficiency and representational power.

The use of CBAM in both the encoder and decoder paths is motivated by the complementary roles these stages play in feature abstraction and spatial reconstruction. In the encoder, CBAM is applied after major convolutional transitions to recalibrate intermediate representations by emphasizing diagnostically relevant lesion patterns while suppressing background artifacts. At early and mid-level stages, this channel–spatial recalibration supports more discriminative feature extraction by guiding the network toward salient color, texture, and structural cues.

In the decoder, CBAM serves a different but equally important role. As feature maps are progressively upsampled and fused with skip connections, decoder representations become increasingly sensitive to spatial noise and feature ambiguity. Applying CBAM after decoder convolutional blocks allows the network to selectively refine reconstructed features, reinforcing lesion boundaries and suppressing irrelevant activations introduced during upsampling and feature fusion. This symmetric placement ensures that attention is not confined solely to feature extraction or reconstruction, but is instead distributed across the full segmentation pipeline. By maintaining CBAM in both paths, the model consistently performs channel-wise and spatial recalibration at multiple semantic levels, enabling coherent attention-guided feature propagation from encoder to decoder. Light dropout within CBAM modules further improves robustness by preventing attention overfitting, particularly under limited training data conditions.

Due to the limited size of our datasets, strong regularization was necessary to prevent overfitting45. The dropout configuration was carefully selected to balance regularization strength across different architectural components while preserving the model’s representational capacity. The attention dropout was set to 0.1, intentionally kept low because attention mechanisms are the core component of transformers. Attention weights determine the relational structure between lesion regions and surrounding tissue, and excessive dropout in this component would severely impair the model’s ability to learn meaningful feature relationships and capture fine-grained spatial dependencies essential for dermoscopic segmentation tasks. This approach is consistent with prior work on transformer-based models, which typically adopt attention dropout values in the range of 0.05 to 0.145,46. To compensate for the conservative attention dropout and prevent overfitting, aggressive dropout of 0.4 was applied to the position embeddings, projection layers, and transformer feed-forward (MLP) blocks. The MLP blocks have substantially higher parameter counts compared to attention sublayers and are more prone to overfitting, making stronger regularization appropriate. Similarly, position embeddings and projection layers benefit from higher dropout as they transform input features before attention computation. This configuration controls the complexity of the non-linear transformations and feature projections while leaving the attention patterns intact, consistent with observations that higher dropout in these components contributes more effectively to generalization in transformer-based architectures47,48. The spatial attention modules (CBAM and SE) were regularized with low dropout of 0.1. While the original formulations of these modules did not incorporate dropout, we introduced minimal dropout regularization to address the limited dataset size while preserving their refinement capacity. These modules operate as refinement mechanisms on top of convolutional or transformer features with relatively low-dimensional internal activations. Heavy regularization would weaken their ability to model channel and spatial saliency, so we kept the dropout minimal to maintain their representational power while still providing some protection against overfitting.

Experimental setup

We evaluated SkinAttn-Net on three publicly available dermoscopic datasets: ISIC 2017, ISIC 2018, and HAM10000. Initially, all images and corresponding lesion masks were resized to 256 × 256 pixels. During training, data augmentations were applied, including horizontal flip (p = 0.5), vertical flip (p = 0.5), random brightness and contrast adjustment (p = 0.2), and and geometric transformations involving random shifts (up to 5% of image dimensions), scaling (± 5% variation), and rotations (within ± 15°), applied with a probability of 0.5. All images were normalized with mean (0.5, 0.5, 0.5) and standard deviation (0.5, 0.5, 0.5). For validation, only resizing and normalization were applied. We used a ResNet-50 backbone as the encoder. Multi-level attention mechanisms were integrated, CBAM blocks after each encoder and decoder block, SE blocks before skip-connections and ViT block at the bottleneck stages.

We adopted a unified data split protocol across all datasets. The ISIC 2017 dataset contains 2,000 dermoscopic images and was divided into 80% for training, 10% for validation, and 10% for testing. The ISIC 2018 dataset includes 2,594 images and was partitioned using the same 80–10–10 ratio. The HAM10000 dataset consists of 10,015 images and was split according to the same proportions to ensure consistency across all experimental settings.

No post-processing was applied to the predicted segmentation masks, as the network outputs were already clean and contiguous, and preliminary inspections confirmed that morphological or smoothing operations did not yield any noticeable improvement in segmentation quality.

Ablation studies were conducted with a total of 30 independent runs to assess the contribution of individual architectural components. Additional experiments evaluating auxiliary configurations were repeated with a total of 10 runs. Each main experiment with the full model was conducted once due to computational constraints. Observed performance variations across repeated runs were minimal, indicating that the results are robust to random initialization and data splits.

Training was conducted for 100 epochs on a CUDA-enabled RTX 4090 GPU. Batch size of 8 is used with the Adam optimizer (lr = 0.0001, betas=(0.9, 0.999)). The segmentation network was optimized using the binary cross-entropy with logits loss, which combines a sigmoid activation and binary cross-entropy computation in a numerically stable manner. These results suggest that the proposed approach captures lesion-relevant structures and boundary cues that are shared across ISIC 2017 and ISIC 2018, despite differences in acquisition conditions and annotation protocols. The absence of a significant performance drop under cross-dataset evaluation indicates that the model learns stable and generalizable feature representations. In contrast, training on HAM10000 leads to a more noticeable performance degradation when evaluated on the ISIC datasets, reflecting the larger domain gap introduced by increased visual diversity and heterogeneous annotation characteristics.

Ablation studies

This section presents two complementary ablation analyses designed to evaluate how architectural design choices and regularization strategies influence the performance of the proposed model. The first analysis examines the structural contribution of the core components (CBAM, SE, and the ViT bottleneck) by evaluating how segmentation quality changes when each module is added or removed under identical training settings. The second analysis evaluates the sensitivity of the architecture to module-specific dropout configurations and investigates how dropout applied within the ViT, CBAM, and SE pathways affects stability and generalization. Through these experiments, we aimed to reveal how both structural elements and regularization choices shape the robustness and overall effectiveness of the architecture.

The lack of statistical significance between the SE-based model and the proposed architecture can be attributed to several factors. First, the SE module alone already provides a strong performance baseline by effectively enhancing channel-wise feature representations, which may cause the additional contributions of other modules to manifest as marginal gains that are not statistically separable; This behavior is consistent with a saturation effect commonly observed in deep learning ablation and architectural scaling studies, where strong baselines limit the observable impact of additional model components49,50. Previous work has shown that combining local and global attention mechanisms can improve model robustness and generalization even when point-wise metric improvements are small51,52. In such cases, the combined effect may yield limited improvements in point-wise performance metrics while offering benefits in robustness and consistency across samples.

Module-wise dropout sensitivity analysis

To analyze the impact of dropout on individual architectural components and on overall segmentation performance, we conducted a module-wise dropout sensitivity analysis under a controlled experimental protocol. A baseline configuration was first established using the default dropout settings that yielded the most stable validation performance during development (CBAM dropout of 0.1, SE dropout of 0.1, ViT MLP/projection/positional dropout of 0.4, and ViT attention dropout of 0.1). Starting from this baseline, each dropout component was evaluated independently using a one-factor-at-a-time strategy, while all remaining dropout parameters were kept fixed at their baseline values to prevent interaction effects. For CBAM and SE modules, three regimes were considered: a low setting with dropout disabled, the baseline configuration, and an aggressive setting with substantially increased regularization. An analogous protocol was applied to the ViT module, where MLP/projection/positional dropout was evaluated under low, medium, and aggressive regimes, followed by a separate analysis of attention dropout using the same progression. All ablation experiments were conducted on the ISIC 2018 dataset, and training was limited to 50 epochs to enable rapid comparative evaluation, in contrast to the 100 epochs used in the final model training. This experimental design enabled a direct and isolated assessment of the sensitivity of each module to dropout, thereby clarifying which regularization mechanisms contribute positively to generalization and which lead to performance degradation.

Table 6 summarizes the results of the module-wise dropout sensitivity analysis across CBAM, SE, and ViT components using multiple segmentation metrics. Overall, moderate dropout consistently yields equal or improved performance compared to both the no-dropout and aggressive-dropout settings, while excessively high regularization provides marginal gains at best and may slightly degrade recall-oriented measures.

For the CBAM block, introducing dropout improves performance across all reported metrics relative to the no-dropout configuration. The Dice and Jaccard scores increase steadily from the zero-dropout setting to the aggressive setting, indicating that light-to-moderate regularization within the attention refinement stages enhances robustness without suppressing discriminative features. The marginal difference between dropout rates of 0.1 and 0.4 suggests that CBAM is relatively insensitive to dropout magnitude once a minimum level of regularization is introduced.

A similar trend is observed for the SE block, where the baseline dropout configuration yields the strongest overall balance between precision and recall. Disabling dropout leads to reduced Dice and Jaccard scores, indicating mild overfitting in the channel recalibration mechanism. Increasing dropout beyond the baseline does not result in further improvement, suggesting that excessive regularization begins to attenuate useful channel dependencies.

For ViT attention dropout, moderate regularization again provides the most favorable trade-off across metrics. The baseline attention dropout improves Dice, Jaccard, and specificity compared to the zero-dropout case, while aggressive dropout slightly reduces recall and Dice, reflecting diminished attention selectivity under excessive stochastic suppression.

The transformer MLP, projection, and positional embedding dropout exhibits the clearest sensitivity pattern. Introducing a moderate dropout rate leads to the highest Dice and Jaccard scores and a notable increase in recall, indicating improved generalization of the feed-forward and embedding components. Although the aggressive setting maintains competitive Dice values, it does not provide consistent gains across all metrics, reinforcing the importance of balanced regularization within the transformer’s non-attention pathways.

In summary, the results demonstrate that dropout is most effective when applied at moderate levels across both convolutional attention modules and transformer components. While the architecture remains relatively stable under varying dropout rates, the baseline configurations provide the most consistent improvements, supporting their selection for the final model.

Conclusions

In this study, we proposed SkinAttn-Net, a segmentation framework designed to enhance skin lesion segmentation by integrating multi-level attention mechanisms, including CBAM, SE blocks, and a ViT-based bottleneck, within a standard encoder–decoder backbone. Comprehensive experiments on ISIC 2017, ISIC 2018, and HAM10000 datasets demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms baseline U-Net and its attention-augmented variants, achieving notable improvements in Dice, Jaccard, precision, recall, and specificity metrics. Ablation studies further reveal the complementary contributions of each attention component to the overall performance, highlighting the importance of combining local feature refinement with global contextual modeling.

Despite these promising results, several limitations exist. First, the model was trained and evaluated solely on publicly available dermoscopic datasets, which may limit its generalizability to images acquired under different clinical conditions or imaging devices. Second, while SkinAttn-Net achieves high segmentation accuracy, the inclusion of multiple attention modules increases computational complexity, which may hinder real-time deployment on resource-constrained systems. Third, the current study focuses exclusively on binary lesion segmentation; multi-class lesion analysis or incorporation of lesion attribute classification was not addressed.

Future work may explore lightweight attention designs to reduce computational overhead, and integration with multi-task frameworks that combine segmentation with classification or attribute prediction. Overall, SkinAttn-Net offers a robust approach for precise skin lesion segmentation, providing a foundation for further development of clinically applicable dermoscopic analysis tools.

Comment on “Knowledge mapping of B cell and atherosclerosis over the past 20 years: A bibliometric analysis”
Authors: Ziqi Zhao, Kun Xu, Yizhuo Jiang, Xisheng Xu, Yuliang Liu
Published: 2024

To the Editor

Recently, we had the privilege of reading the outstanding paper titled “A Knowledge Map of B Cells and Atherosclerosis: A Bibliometric Analysis Over the Past 20 Years1” authored by Tao, Cheng, and colleagues. This study delves deeply into the role of B cells in atherosclerosis through a comprehensive and systematic bibliometric approach, analyzing the research trends, hot topics, and the evolution of research themes over the past two decades. It not only offers readers a window to quickly grasp the current state and research hotspots in this field but also aids in predicting future research directions.

We sincerely appreciate the contributions of Tao and their team in this area, which undoubtedly hold significant academic value. However, during our reading, we regrettably noticed a minor error in the manuscript. We believe that this small flaw does not detract from the overall excellence of the paper, but to ensure the rigor of the research, we suggest that the authors make appropriate corrections.

In the study by Tao et al., we noticed an error in the expression concerning the citation pathways in the section “Journals and co-cited journals,” particularly in the explanation of the journal double overlay (original Figure 7). The original text states: “The orange and green paths were the main paths, showing studies published in molecular/biological/genetic journals and health/nursing/medicine journals were mainly cited by literature in molecular/biological/immunological journals, and studies published in health/nursing/medicine journals were mainly cited by literature in medicine/medical/clinical journals.” However, upon careful analysis, we found that the actual situation does not match the description above. In fact, research published in molecular/biological/genetic journals is primarily cited by literature in medicine/medical/clinical journals. This point was incorrectly described in the explanation of the green citation path in the double overlay.

We believe that this error may stem from a misunderstanding of the data or an oversight during the writing process. To maintain the accuracy and reliability of the research, we suggest that the authors verify this section and make corrections if necessary. We believe that such revisions can further enhance the quality and academic value of the paper.

We pay tribute to the contributions of Tao and colleagues in this field and look forward to their continued provision of high-quality academic achievements in future research. At the same time, we also encourage all researchers to maintain a rigorous attitude in writing and reviewing to ensure the accuracy and effectiveness of research findings.

We express our deep respect for the outstanding contributions of Tao and their colleagues in this field, and we are filled with anticipation for the high-quality academic achievements they will continue to provide in their future research. Their research is not only thorough and meticulous but also comprehensively covers the relevant areas, offering us valuable knowledge and insights. We firmly believe that the research outcomes of Tao’s team will help us gain a deeper understanding of the hot issues and development trends in specific research fields. We look forward to Tao and their team making further breakthroughs in future research and providing us with more high-quality academic results. We believe that through the joint efforts and relentless pursuit of all researchers, we can continuously advance the development of scientific knowledge and make a greater contribution to the progress of human society.

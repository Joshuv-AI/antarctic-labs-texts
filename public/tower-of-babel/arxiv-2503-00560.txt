Abstract
. We derive estimates comparing asymptotic Riemannian or sub-Riemannian metrics in step-2 nilpotent Lie groups. Given a sub-Riemannian metric, we construct a Carnot metric whose square remains at a bounded distance from the square of the original metric. As a consequence, we obtain a refined estimate of the error term in the asymptotic expansion of the volume of (sub-)Riemannian metric balls. To achieve this, we develop a novel technique to perturb sub-Riemannian geodesics, allowing us to modify their endpoints in a prescribed vertical direction.



1.
Introduction

The large-scale geometry of metric groups of polynomial growth is closely related to their asymptotic cones; see
[
9
]
and also
[
2
]
. In this work, we focus on a broad class of metrics on Lie groups known as sub-Riemannian metrics, which generalize Riemannian metrics. For sub-Riemannian nilpotent Lie groups, the asymptotic cone is a sub-Riemannian Carnot group; see
[
6
,
15
]
. When the Lie group is simply connected and with nilpotency step
2
, the asymptotic cone can be realized by a left-invariant metric on the same group. Roughly speaking, this metric is defined as the infimum of the sub-Riemannian length over all absolutely continuous curves within a subclass that we call
\Delta_{\infty}
-horizontal curves; see (
9
). We refer to this metric as the
canonical asymptotic metric
and discuss its details in
Section

2.2
.

Our first result provides an estimate comparing the sub-Riemannian metric and its canonical asymptotic metric in each
2
-step nilpotent Lie group.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a simply connected,
2
-step nilpotent Lie group equipped with a left-invariant sub-Riemannian metric
d
. Denote by
d_{\infty}
the canonical asymptotic metric on
G
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

(1)

\lvert d_{\infty}(p,q)^{2}-d(p,q)^{2}\rvert\leq C,\quad\text{for every }p,q\in G.

In particular

(2)

\lvert d_{\infty}(p,q)-d(p,q)\rvert=O\left(\frac{1}{d(p,q)}\right)\to 0,\quad\text{as $d(p,q)\to\infty$.}

Theorem

1.1
has implications for the rate of convergence of a metric group to its asymptotic cone in terms of the Gromov-Hausdorff distance; see
Proposition

4.1
. In fact, it implies that on every
2
-step nilpotent Riemannian Lie group
(G,d)
there exists a Carnot metric
d^{\prime}
on
G
and
C>0
such that

(3)

\lvert d^{\prime}(p,q)-d(p,q)\rvert\leq C\quad\text{for every $p,q\in G$.}

We refer to
Corollary

3.12
for details. Analogous results are known to fail in the more general sub-Finsler setting. Indeed, in
[
3
]
, one finds an example of a
2
-step nilpotent Finsler Lie group that is not at bounded distance from its asymptotic cone. However, in
[
16
]
, property (
3
) is established for nilpotent groups whose asymptotic cones are strongly bracket-generating nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie groups. It is worth noting that the latter condition restricts the nilpotency step to be at most
2
; see
[
12
, Appendix A]
for details. Nevertheless, we point out that
Theorem

1.1
does not extend in this setting. In fact, in
Example

4.2
, we exhibit a Finsler metric
d
on the Heisenberg group such that no Carnot metric
d^{\prime}
satisfies

(4)

\lvert d^{\prime}(p,q)-d(p,q)\rvert\to 0,\quad\text{as $d(p,q)\to\infty$.}

As a consequence, our results highlight a fundamental difference between sub-Riemannian and sub-Finsler metrics in
2
-step nilpotent Lie groups in terms of their asymptotic geometries.

Theorem

1.1
also refines the asymptotic expansion of the volume growth of metric balls; see
Theorem

4.4
. Specifically, in every
2
-step nilpotent Riemannian group
(G,d)
, the asymptotic expansion of the Haar measure
\operatorname{vol}
of the metric ball
B(r)
of radius
r
is given by

(5)

\operatorname{vol}(B(r))=Cr^{Q}+O(r^{Q-2}),\quad\text{as $r\to\infty$,}

where
Q
is the Hausdorff dimension of the asymptotic cone of
(G,d)
. This estimate improves previously known results for
2
-step nilpotent Lie groups established in
[
3
,
16
]
.

Our proof of
Theorem

1.1
builds upon the complete description of sub-Riemannian geodesics in
2
-step nilpotent Lie groups. Given a sub-Riemannian energy-minimizing curve, we develop a technique to perturb it into a
\Delta_{\infty}
-horizontal curve while preserving its endpoint. A key feature of this technique is that it provides a bound, independent of the initial curve, on the energy difference between the original and the perturbed curves. We refer to
Lemma

3.8
and
Lemma

3.10
for details.

We also apply this technique to obtain a rigidity result for asymptotic sub-Riemannian metrics. Two left-invariant metrics
d
and
d^{\prime}
defined on a Lie group
G
are said to be
asymptotic
if

\frac{d^{\prime}(p,q)}{d(p,q)}\to 1,\quad\text{as $d(p,q)\to\infty$.}

On the one hand, every sub-Riemannian metric in a nilpotent Lie group is asymptotic to its canonical asymptotic metric by Pansu’s work
[
15
]
; we point out that Pansu’s metric and the canonical asymptotic metric may differ in nilpotent groups of higher step as explained in
Remark

2.1
.
On the other hand, two asymptotic sub-Riemannian metrics may still have distinct canonical asymptotic metrics, as for example two different Carnot metrics with the same abelianization norm. Nevertheless, we establish the following result, which is meaningful and new also in the Riemannian case.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a simply connected,
2
-step nilpotent Lie group equipped with sub-Riemannian metrics
d
and
d^{\prime}
. Then, the metrics
d
and
d^{\prime}
are asymptotic if and only if there exists
C>0
such that

\lvert d^{\prime}(p,q)-d(p,q)\rvert\leq C,\quad\text{for every }p,q\in G.

Theorem

1.2
, with its more general form
Theorem

3.15
, extends results previously known for the Heisenberg group (see
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
) to the broader class of
2
-step nilpotent Lie groups. The asymptotic condition is well understood in terms of the abelianization norms induced by the metrics; see
[
3
]
. As a consequence, we establish that a Lie group automorphism

\varphi:G\to G

is a rough isometry for a sub-Riemannian 2-step nilpotent Lie group
G
, i.e.,
there exists
C>0
such that

\lvert d(\varphi(p),\varphi(q))-d(p,q))\rvert\leq C,\quad\text{for every $p,q\in G$},

if and only if the abelianization of
\varphi
is a Euclidean isometry.
Using this, we construct an example of a Lie group automorphism that is a rough isometry but is not at bounded distance from any isometry. We discuss these results in
Section

4.7
.

We point out that
Theorem

1.1
is informative of the horoboundary. Thanks to (
2
), the metrics
d
and
d_{\infty}
have the same horoboundary, see
[
13
, Section 5]
for a detailed description of this fact. We obtain the following result.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
G
be a simply connected, 2-step nilpotent Lie group equipped with a sub-Riemannian metric
d
.
Denote by
d_{\infty}
the canonical asymptotic metric on
G
.
Then the horoboundary of
(G,d)
coincides with the horoboundary of
(G,d_{\infty})
.

The usefulness of
Corollary

1.3
stems from the fact that horoboundaries of Carnot groups are better understood than those of general sub-Riemannian Lie groups. Indeed, since the canonical asymptotic metric of
2
-step nilpotent Lie groups coincides with the metric of a Carnot group, its horoboundary can be effectively described using the techniques developed in
[
8
]
. In particular, see
[
8
, Sections 2.6 and 3.3]
, as well as
[
7
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we introduce fundamental concepts on sub-Riemannian metrics in Lie groups and present the construction of the canonical asymptotic metric. In
Section

3
, we develop our perturbation technique for sub-Riemannian geodesics and use it to establish
Lemma

3.10
, which plays a central role in proving
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
. In
Section

4
, we examine the implications of these results for the rate of convergence to the asymptotic cone and the volume growth of metric balls. Additionally, we compute the volume of Riemannian balls in the Heisenberg group and demonstrate that the exponent
2
in (
1
) is optimal. Specifically, for every
\beta>2
, the quantity
\lvert d_{\infty}(p,q)^{\beta}-d(p,q)^{\beta}\rvert
is unbounded in the Riemannian Heisenberg group. Finally, we present an example of a Riemannian metric on the Engel group that is not at bounded distance from its canonical asymptotic metric. The latter example suggests that different techniques may be necessary to address similar questions in higher-step nilpotent groups.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Sub-Riemannian metrics on Lie groups

Let
G
be a connected Lie group, with identity element
e
and associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\coloneq T_{e}G
. For
p\in G
, we denote by
L_{p}
the left-translation by
p
. A subbundle
\Delta
of the tangent bundle
TG
is
left-invariant
whenever

\Delta_{p\cdot q}=\mathrm{d}L_{p}(\Delta_{q})\quad\text{for every $p,q\in G$.}

A left-invariant subbundle
\Delta\subseteq TG
is
bracket-generating
if
\mathrm{Lie}(\Delta_{e})=\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathrm{Lie}(\Delta_{e})
is the Lie algebra generated by
\Delta_{e}
. Hereafter, we refer to a left-invariant, bracket-generating subbundle
\Delta
as
horizontal distribution
, and we say that an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to G
is
horizontal
(or
\Delta
-horizontal
) if
\dot{\gamma}(t)\in\Delta_{\gamma(t)}
for a.e.
t\in[0,1]
.

Consider a horizontal distribution
\Delta\subseteq TG
and fix an inner product
\rho_{e}
on
\Delta_{e}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. The inner product
\rho_{e}
extends by left-translation to a metric tensor
\rho
on
\Delta
, i.e.,

\rho_{p}(X,Y)\coloneq\rho_{e}(\mathrm{d}L_{p}^{-1}(X),\mathrm{d}L_{p}^{-1}(Y))\quad\text{for every $p\in G$ and $X,Y\in\Delta_{p}$.}

We say that
(\Delta,\rho)
is a
sub-Riemannian structure
on
G
.

The choice of a sub-Riemannian structure endows
G
with the structure of metric space, with
sub-Riemannian metric
defined by setting

(6)

d(p,q)=\inf\Set{\ell(\gamma)}{\text{$\gamma\colon[0,1]\to G$ horizontal curve such that $p,q\in\gamma([0,1])$}},

where
\ell(\gamma)\coloneq\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\rho(\dot{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))}\,\mathrm{d}t
denotes the
length
of the horizontal curve
\gamma
.
The bracket-generating condition ensures that (
6
) indeed defines a distance that induces the manifold topology, in particular it is finite; see
[
1
]
. Since
\ell(L_{h}\circ\gamma)=\ell(\gamma)
, the distance
d
is
left-invariant
:

d(h\cdot p,h\cdot q)=d(p,q)\quad\text{for every $h,p,q\in G$.}

Moreover,
G
is complete and the distance
d
is geodesic, meaning that the infimum in (
6
) is always attained; see
[
1
]
. We refer to this class of metric spaces as
sub-Riemannian Lie groups
. The special case
\Delta=TG
corresponds to the well known class of
Riemannian Lie groups
.

2.2.
Nilpotent Lie groups and asymptotic metrics

Simply connected, nilpotent Lie groups, equipped with a sub-Riemannian structure, admit a unique asymptotic cone in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense; see
[
15
]
. The resulting asymptotic cone is again a sub-Riemannian Lie group. If
G
is a
2
-step nilpotent Lie group, the asymptotic cone can be realized by a sub-Riemannian structure on
G
itself, following a canonical construction which we now present.

Let
G
be a simply connected,
2
-step nilpotent Lie group with associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Equip
G
with a sub-Riemannian structure
(\Delta,\rho)
and denote by
d
the corresponding sub-Riemannian metric. Since
\Delta
is bracket-generating and
\mathfrak{g}
is
2
-step nilpotent, then

\mathfrak{g}=\Delta_{e}+[\Delta_{e},\Delta_{e}]\subseteq\Delta_{e}+[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}.

We conclude that
\Delta_{e}+[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{g}
. We define

(7)

V\coloneq\Set{v\in\Delta_{e}}{\text{$\rho(v,w)=0$ for every $w\in\Delta_{e}\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$}}.

It is an easy exercise to show that

(8)

\mathfrak{g}=V\oplus[V,V],

in particular the left-invariant subbundle defined by setting

(9)

\Delta_{\infty}\coloneq\bigcup_{p\in G}\mathrm{d}L_{p}(V)\subseteq\Delta,

is bracket generating. We define the metric tensor
\rho_{\infty}
on
\Delta_{\infty}
to be the restriction of
\rho
. We set

(10)

d_{\infty}(p,q)\coloneq\inf\Set{\ell(\gamma)}{\text{$\gamma\colon[0,1]\to G$ is a $\Delta_{\infty}$-horizontal curve such that $p,q\in\gamma([0,1])$}},

to be the sub-Riemannian metric on
G
associated to the sub-Riemannian structure
(\Delta_{\infty},\rho_{\infty})
. We refer to the metric
d_{\infty}
as the
canonical asymptotic metric
of the sub-Riemannian Lie group
(G,d)
.

We observe that, since
\Delta_{\infty}\subseteq\Delta
, then every
\Delta_{\infty}
-horizontal curve is also
\Delta
-horizontal. Therefore

(11)

d(p,q)\leq d_{\infty}(p,q),\quad\text{for every $p,q\in G$.}

The latter construction differ by the one in
[
15
]
as we identified the quotient space
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
with a subspace
V\subseteq\Delta_{e}
in direct sum with
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, and we replaced the quotient map
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
by the projection
\pi\colon\mathfrak{g}\to V
parallel to
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. We stress, however, that
\rho
is an inner product on
\Delta_{e}
and
V
is orthogonal to
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Therefore, the metric on
V
with unit ball

\pi(B_{g}(1))\coloneq\pi\left(\Set{v\in\Delta_{e}}{\rho(v,v)\leq 1}\right)

is precisely the restriction of
\rho
to
V
. We conclude that our construction is equivalent to Pansu’s description of the asymptotic cone.

Remark 2.1
.

Our construction of the canonical asymptotic metric applies to sub-Riemannian nilpotent Lie groups
(G,d)
of higher step. In contrast with Pansu’s metric
[
15
]
, the canonical asymptotic metric is always left-invariant. The resulting metric space
(G,d_{\infty})
, however, is isometric to the asymptotic cone of
(G,d)
if the subspace
V
, defined as in (
7
), is the first layer of a stratification of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
associated to
G
. For the construction of the asymptotic cone starting with an adapted linear grading we refer to
[
6
]
. In
[
5
]
, one finds an example of nilpotent group whose Pansu’s metric is not left-invariant.

3.
Main results

3.1.
Models for sub-Riemannian
2
-step nilpotent Lie groups

We consider a sub-Riemannian structure
(\Delta,\rho)
on a simply connected,
2
-step nilpotent Lie group
G
, with associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. We define
V
as in (
7
), so that (
8
) is satisfied. By a general result on simply connected nilpotent Lie groups (see
[
10
, Theorem 11.2.6]
), the Lie group
G
is isomorphic to

(12)

(V\oplus[V,V],*),\quad\text{where}\quad(x_{1},y_{1})*(x_{2},y_{2})\coloneq\left(x_{1}+x_{2},y_{1}+y_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}[x_{1},x_{2}]\right),

for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in V
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in[V,V]
. Moreover, the Lie algebra associated to
(V\oplus[V,V],*)
is
\mathfrak{g}
. Hereafter, we consider the group
G
to be already of the form (
12
). We define the map

\pi\colon G\to V,\qquad(x,y)\mapsto x,

to be the projection to the first component. Hereafter we say that
g\in G
is
vertical
if
\pi(g)=0
.

We recall from
[
6
, Proposition 9.1.15]
that, if
\eta\colon[0,1]\to V
is an absolutely continuous curve such that
\eta(0)=0
, then there exists a unique
\Delta_{\infty}
-horizontal curve
\widetilde{\eta}\colon[0,1]\to G
, such that
\widetilde{\eta}(0)=0
and
\pi\circ\widetilde{\eta}=\eta
. We refer to the curve
\widetilde{\eta}
as the
multiplicative integral
of
\eta
. As a simple consequence of the group law (
12
) of
G
, we get the following formula for the multiplicative integral.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\eta\colon[0,1]\to V
be an absolutely continuous curve such that
\eta(0)=0
. Its multiplicative integral
\widetilde{\eta}=(x,y)\colon[0,1]\to G
is given by

(13)

\begin{cases}x(t)=\eta(t)\\
y(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}[\eta(s),\dot{\eta}(s)]\,\mathrm{d}s\end{cases}\quad\text{for every $t\in[0,1]$.}

We include here two results we will need later in this section.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
V\oplus[V,V]
be the Lie group as in (
12
). Let
\eta,\sigma\colon[0,1]\to V
be absolutely continuous curves such that
\eta(0)=\sigma(0)=0
and
\sigma(1)=0
. Assume that

\int^{1}_{0}[\sigma(t),\dot{\eta}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

Then the curve
\gamma\coloneq\eta+\sigma
satisfies
\widetilde{\gamma}(1)=\widetilde{\eta}(1)+\widetilde{\sigma}(1)
, where
\widetilde{\gamma}
,
\widetilde{\eta}
, and
\widetilde{\sigma}
denote the multiplicative integrals of
\gamma
,
\eta
, and
\sigma
, respectively.

Proof.

Integrating by parts, we get

(14)

\int^{1}_{0}[\eta(t),\dot{\sigma}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int^{1}_{0}[\dot{\eta}(t),\sigma(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t=\int^{1}_{0}[\sigma(t),\dot{\eta}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t,

where we used the fact that
\sigma(0)=\sigma(1)=0
. We then compute

\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}(1)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{formula integral}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left(\eta(1)+\sigma(1),\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}[\eta(t)+\sigma(t),\dot{\eta}(t)+\dot{\sigma}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{parts}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left(\eta(1)+\sigma(1),\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}[\eta(t),\dot{\eta}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}[\sigma(t),\dot{\sigma}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{1}_{0}[\sigma(t),\dot{\eta}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}(1)+\widetilde{\sigma}(1),

where in the last equality we used the hypothesis
\int^{1}_{0}[\sigma(t),\dot{\eta}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
2
-step nilpotent Lie algebra of the form
\mathfrak{g}=V\oplus[V,V]
for some
V\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\rho
be an inner product on
V
and
\lvert\cdot\rvert
be a norm on
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Set
m_{2}\coloneq\dim([V,V])
. There exists
K>0
such that, for every
\zeta\in[V,V]
, there exist
v_{1},\dots,v_{m_{2}}\in V
,
w_{1},\dots,w_{m_{2}}\in V
, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m_{2}}\geq 0
such that

(i)

\rho(v_{k},v_{k})=\rho(w_{k},w_{k})=1
for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
,

(ii)

\rho(v_{k},w_{k})=0
for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
,

(iii)

\zeta=\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\alpha_{k}[v_{k},w_{k}]
,

(iv)

\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\alpha_{k}\leq K\lvert\zeta\rvert
.

Proof.

Let
\left([v_{k},w_{k}]\right)_{k=1}^{m_{2}}
, with
v_{k},w_{k}\in V
for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
, be a basis of
[V,V]
made of simple Lie brackets, which exists by definition of
[V,V]
. Up to applying a Gram–Schmidt process and a renormalization, we can assume that
\rho(v_{k},w_{k})=0
and
\rho(v_{k},v_{k})=\rho(w_{k},w_{k})=1
, for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
. The
\ell_{1}
norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
with respect to
\left([v_{k},w_{k}]\right)_{k=1}^{m_{2}}
and
\lvert\cdot\rvert
are equivalent norms on the finite-dimensional vector space
[V,V]
. Let
K>0
be such that
\lVert\cdot\rVert\leq K\lvert\cdot\rvert
. Since
\left([v_{k},w_{k}]\right)_{k=1}^{m_{2}}
is a basis of
[V,V]
, every
\zeta\in[V,V]
is uniquely written as
\zeta=\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\alpha_{k}[v_{k},w_{k}]
. Up to switching
v_{k}
with
w_{k}
, we can assume that
\alpha_{k}\geq 0
for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
. With this assumption
\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\alpha_{k}=\lVert\zeta\rVert
. Therefore, also
(iv)
is verified.
∎

3.2.
Energy of curves

In our discussion, it is convenient to consider energy-minimizing curves in sub-Riemannian Lie groups. We summarize some standard terminology and basic facts, and we refer to
[
6
, Section 6.3]
for details.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(\Delta,\rho)
be a sub-Riemannian structure on a Lie group
G
. Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to G
be an absolutely continuous curve.
The
(doubled) energy

J(\gamma)
of
\gamma
is defined as

(15)

J(\gamma)\coloneq\int^{1}_{0}\rho(\dot{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t.

An absolutely continuous curve is energy-minimizing if and only if it is length-minimizing and it is parameterized with constant speed; see
[
6
, Proposition 2.1.25]
).
Moreover, we get the following formula for the square of the metric in every sub-Riemannian Lie group
(G,d)
:

(16)

d(p,q)^{2}\coloneq\inf\Set{J(\gamma)}{\text{$\gamma\colon[0,1]\to G$ horizontal curve such that $p,q\in\gamma([0,1])$}}.

We recall the following result about energy-minimizing curves in sub-Riemannian
2
-step nilpotent Lie groups.

Proposition 3.5

(
[
6
, Proposition 6.3.28]
)
.

Let
G
be a
2
-step nilpotent group as in (
12
) and let
(\Delta,\rho)
be a sub-Riemannian structure on
G
with
V
as in (
7
). For every energy-minimizing curve
\gamma=(x,y)\colon[0,1]\to G
with
\gamma(0)=(0,0)
, there are
M\in\mathfrak{so}(V)
,
b\in\ker(M)
,
c\in M(V)
, and
\zeta\in[V,V]\cap\Delta
such that

\begin{cases}x(t)=c-e^{tM}c+tb\\
y(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}[x(s),\dot{x}(s)]\,\mathrm{d}s+t\zeta,\end{cases}\quad\text{for every $t\in[0,1]$.}

Remark 3.6
.

Let
G
be a
2
-step nilpotent group as in (
12
) and let
(\Delta,\rho)
be a sub-Riemannian structure on
G
with
V
as in (
7
). Consider
\xi\colon[0,1]\to V
to be an absolutely continuous curve. We claim that

(17)

J(\widetilde{\xi})=\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}(\dot{\xi}(t),\dot{\xi}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\widetilde{\xi}
denotes the multiplicative integral of
\xi
. Indeed, as a simple consequence of
Remark

3.1
, we get

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\widetilde{\xi}(t)=\mathrm{d}L_{\widetilde{\xi}(t)}(\dot{\xi}(t)),\quad\text{for every $t\in[0,1]$.}

Therefore, we obtain

J(\widetilde{\xi})=\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{\widetilde{\xi}(t)}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\widetilde{\xi}(t),\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\widetilde{\xi}(t)\right)\mathrm{d}t=\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{\widetilde{\xi}(t)}\!\left(\mathrm{d}L_{\widetilde{\xi}(t)}(\dot{\xi}(t)),\mathrm{d}L_{\widetilde{\xi}(t)}(\dot{\xi}(t))\right)\mathrm{d}t=\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}(\dot{\xi}(t),\dot{\xi}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t,

where we used that the metric tensor
\rho
is left-invariant.

Remark 3.7
.

Let
G
be a
2
-step nilpotent group as in (
12
) and let
(\Delta,\rho)
be a sub-Riemannian structure on
G
with
V
as in (
7
). Consider
\eta,\sigma\colon[0,1]\to V
to be absolutely continuous curves. Assume that

\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}(\dot{\eta}(t),\dot{\sigma}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

We claim that
J(\widetilde{\gamma})=J(\widetilde{\eta})+J(\widetilde{\sigma})
, where we define
\gamma\coloneq\eta+\sigma
, and
\widetilde{\gamma}
,
\widetilde{\eta}
, and
\widetilde{\sigma}
denote the multiplicative integrals of
\gamma
,
\eta
, and
\sigma
, respectively. Indeed, we compute

J(\widetilde{\gamma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{energy rho}}}{{=}}\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}(\dot{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))=\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}(\dot{\eta}(t),\dot{\eta}(t))+\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}(\dot{\sigma}(t),\dot{\sigma}(t))+2\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}(\dot{\eta}(t),\dot{\sigma}(t))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{energy rho}}}{{=}}J(\widetilde{\eta})+J(\widetilde{\sigma}),

where in the last equality we also used the hypothesis

.

3.3.
A constrained isoperimetric problem

The key step in the proof of
Lemma

3.10
is to perturb a
\Delta_{\infty}
-horizontal curve in order to vary its end-point by a prescribed vertical direction. We make use of the following technical result.

Lemma 3.8
.

We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the inner product on
L^{2}([0,1],\mathbb{C})
, and we write
e^{it\omega}
for the function
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto e^{it\omega}
. For every
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist smooth functions
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{s}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
such that

(i)

\xi_{k}(0)=\xi_{k}(1)=0
for every
1\leq k\leq s
,

(ii)

\langle\xi_{k},e^{it\omega_{h}}\rangle=\langle\xi_{k},e^{-it\omega_{h}}\rangle=0
for every
1\leq h\leq r
and
1\leq k\leq s
,

(iii)

\langle\dot{\xi_{k}},\dot{\xi_{k}}\rangle=1
for every
1\leq k\leq s
,

(iv)

\langle\dot{\xi}_{k_{1}},\xi_{k_{2}}\rangle=\langle\dot{\xi}_{k_{1}},\overline{\xi}_{k_{2}}\rangle=\langle\ddot{\xi}_{k_{1}},\xi_{k_{2}}\rangle=\langle\ddot{\xi}_{k_{1}},\overline{\xi}_{k_{2}}\rangle=0
for every
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
,

(v)

\mathrm{Im}\langle\dot{\xi_{k}},\xi_{k}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{4\pi(r+1)s}
for every
1\leq k\leq s
.

We informally interpret
Lemma

3.8
as a constrained isoperimetric problem in the following sense. Given some linear constraints (conditions
(ii)
and
(iv)
), we are asked to find a closed curve of length one (conditions
(i)
and
(iii)
) that encloses at least some area (condition
(v)
) uniformly within the class of the considered constraints.

Proof of
Lemma

3.8
.

Firstly, we set

E_{k}\coloneq[(2r+2)(k-1)+1,(2r+2)k]\cap\mathbb{N},\quad\text{for every $1\leq k\leq s$.}

We look for functions
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{s}
of the form

(18)

\xi_{k}(t)=\sum_{n\in E_{k}}c_{n,k}e^{it2\pi n},

for a suitable choice of
c_{n,k}
, for every
1\leq k\leq s
. Fix
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
and set

P_{n,h}\coloneq\langle e^{it2\pi n},e^{it\omega_{h}}\rangle,\quad Q_{n,h}\coloneq\langle e^{it2\pi n},e^{-it\omega_{h}}\rangle,\quad\text{for every $1\leq h\leq r$.}

For every
1\leq k\leq s
, we consider the tuple
(c_{n,k})_{n\in E_{k}}
to be a solution of the following system of equations:

(19)

\begin{cases}\sum_{n\in E_{k}}c_{n,k}=0,\\
\sum_{n\in E_{k}}c_{n,k}P_{n,h}=0,\quad\text{for every $1\leq h\leq r$,}\\
\sum_{n\in E_{k}}c_{n,k}Q_{n,h}=0,\quad\text{for every $1\leq h\leq r$,}\\
\sum_{n\in E_{k}}4\pi^{2}n^{2}\lvert c_{n,k}\rvert^{2}=1.\end{cases}

We claim that such a solution exists. Indeed, a non-trivial solution to the linear part of the system exists since there are
2r+1
equations and
|E_{k}|=2r+2
variables. Such a solution can be then normalized so that the last equation is satisfied as well.

We next verify that the functions defined as in (
18
), with
(c_{n,k})_{n\in E_{k}}
being a solution to (
19
), satisfy properties
(i)–(v)
in the statement. Firstly, we get

\displaystyle\xi_{k}(0)=\xi_{k}(1)=\sum_{n\in E_{k}}c_{n,k}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{system_c}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0,\quad\text{for every $1\leq k\leq s$,}

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{k},e^{it\omega_{h}}\rangle=\sum_{n\in E_{k}}c_{n,k}P_{n,h}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{system_c}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0,\quad\text{for every $1\leq h\leq r$ and $1\leq k\leq s$,}

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{k},e^{-it\omega_{h}}\rangle=\sum_{n\in E_{k}}c_{n,k}Q_{n,h}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{system_c}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0,\quad\text{for every $1\leq h\leq r$ and $1\leq k\leq s$,}

\displaystyle\langle\dot{\xi_{k}},\dot{\xi_{k}}\rangle=\sum_{n\in E_{k}}4\pi^{2}n^{2}\lvert c_{n,k}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{system_c}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 1,\quad\text{for every $1\leq k\leq s$.}

Therefore properties
(i)–(iii)
are satisfied. We next observe that the support of the Fourier transforms of
\xi_{k}
,
\dot{\xi_{k}}
, and
\ddot{\xi_{k}}
is contained in
E_{k}
, whilst the Fourier transform of
\overline{\xi}_{k}
is contained in
-E_{k}
, for every
1\leq k\leq s
. Since the sets
E_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
are pairwise disjoint we get that property
(iv)
is satisfied. Lastly, for every
1\leq k\leq s
, we get the estimate

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\left(\langle\dot{\xi_{k}},\xi_{k}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\left(\sum_{n\in E_{k}}2\pi in\lvert c_{n,k}\rvert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in E_{k}}2\pi n\lvert c_{n,k}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi(2r+2)s}\sum_{n\in E_{k}}4\pi^{2}n^{2}\lvert c_{n,k}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{system_c}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi(r+1)s},

where we used the fact if
n\in E_{k}
, then
n\leq(2r+2)s
. We conclude that property
(v)
is satisfied as well. ∎

Remark 3.9
.

We stress that, if
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{s}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
satisfy property
(ii)
in
Lemma

3.8
, then the following equations hold for every
1\leq h\leq r
and
1\leq k\leq s
:

\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k}),\sin(t\omega_{h})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k}),\cos(t\omega_{h})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k}),\sin(t\omega_{h})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k}),\cos(t\omega_{h})\rangle=0.

Moreover, if
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{s}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
satisfy property
(iv)
in
Lemma

3.8
, then for every
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
:

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=0.

3.4.
Vertical variations

Our proofs of
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
both rely on the following fundamental result.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
(G,d)
be a sub-Riemannian
2
-step nilpotent Lie group and denote by
\mathfrak{g}
its associated Lie algebra. Fix a norm
\lvert\cdot\rvert
on
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

(20)

d(e,q\cdot\exp(\zeta))^{2}\leq d(e,q)^{2}+C\lvert\zeta\rvert,\quad\text{for every $q\in G$ and $\zeta\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$.}

We stress that (
20
) is an improvement of the triangular inequality combined with the Ball-Box Theorem (see
[
6
, Theorem 3.3.5]
), which imply the following weaker estimate:

d(e,q\cdot\exp(\zeta))\leq d(e,q)+C\sqrt{\lvert\zeta\rvert},\quad\text{for every $q\in G$ and $\zeta\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$.}

Proof of
Lemma

3.10
.

Thanks to
Section

3.1
, we can assume
G
to be as in (
12
) and the sub-Riemannian metric
d
to be associated to a sub-Riemannian structure
(\Delta,\rho)
on
G
such that (
7
) is satisfied. We set
m_{1}\coloneq\dim(V)
and
m_{2}\coloneq\dim([V,V])
. We fix a norm
\lvert\cdot\rvert
on
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and we set
K>0
to be the constant given in
Lemma

3.3
.

Fix
q\in G
and consider an energy-minimizing curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to G
such that
\gamma(0)=e
and
\gamma(1)=q
. From (
16
), we get that

(21)

d(e,q)^{2}=J(\gamma).

From
Proposition

3.5
, there are
M\in\mathfrak{so}(V)
,
b\in\ker(M)
,
c\in M(V)
, and
\mu\in[V,V]\cap\Delta
such that
\gamma=(x,y)
is given by

(22)

\begin{cases}x(t)=c-e^{tM}c+tb\\
y(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}[x(s),\dot{x}(s)]\,\mathrm{d}s+t\mu,\end{cases}\quad\text{for every $t\in[0,1]$.}

We define
\eta\coloneq\pi\circ\gamma\colon[0,1]\to V
and we observe that, for every
t\in[0,1]
, we have
\gamma(t)=\widetilde{\eta}(t)+t\mu
, where
\widetilde{\eta}
denotes the multiplicative integral of
\eta
. In particular

(23)

q=\widetilde{\eta}(1)+\mu.

Moreover, since
V
and
[V,V]\cap\Delta
are
\rho_{e}
-orthogonal, we also have

(24)

J(\gamma)=J(\widetilde{\eta})+\rho(\mu,\mu).

Fix
\zeta\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Thanks to
Lemma

3.3
, there exist
v_{1},\dots,v_{m_{2}}\in V
,
w_{1},\dots,w_{m_{2}}\in V
, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m_{2}}\geq 0
, satisfying
\rho(v_{k},v_{k})=\rho(w_{k},w_{k})=1
and
\rho(v_{k},w_{k})=0
for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
, such that

(25)

\zeta=\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\alpha_{k}[v_{k},w_{k}]

and

(26)

\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\alpha_{k}\leq K\lvert\zeta\rvert.

Fix a basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{m_{1}}
of
V
. Let
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
be the absolute values of the eigenvalues of
M
, for some
r\leq m_{1}
. Since
M\in\mathfrak{so}(V)
, we recall that
M
is diagonalisable with purely imaginary spectrum. Therefore, there exist

such that

(27)

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{system_proof}}}{{=}}-Me^{tM}c+b

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{h=1}^{r}A_{jh}\sin(t\omega_{h})+B_{jh}\cos(t\omega_{h})\right)e_{j},\quad\text{for every $t\in[0,1]$,}

\displaystyle\ddot{\eta}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{system_proof}}}{{=}}-M^{2}e^{tM}c

Thanks to
Lemma

3.8
, there exist smooth functions
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m_{2}}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
such that

(i)

\xi_{k}(0)=\xi_{k}(1)=0

1\leq k\leq m_{2}

(ii)

1\leq h\leq r
and
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
,

(iii)

\langle\dot{\xi_{k}},\dot{\xi_{k}}\rangle=1
for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
,

(iv)

\langle\dot{\xi}_{k_{1}},\xi_{k_{2}}\rangle=\langle\dot{\xi}_{k_{1}},\overline{\xi}_{k_{2}}\rangle=0

k_{1}\neq k_{2}
,

(v)

\mathrm{Im}\langle\dot{\xi_{k}},\xi_{k}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{4\pi(r+1)m_{2}}
for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
.

We recall that
r\leq m_{1}
. Therefore, from property (v), we get

(29)

\mathrm{Im}\left(\langle\dot{\xi_{k}},\xi_{k}\rangle\right)\geq\frac{1}{4\pi(m_{1}+1)m_{2}}.

Remark

3.9
implies that, for every
1\leq h\leq r
and
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
:

(30)

\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k}),\sin(t\omega_{h})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k}),\cos(t\omega_{h})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k}),\sin(t\omega_{h})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k}),\cos(t\omega_{h})\rangle=0.

Moreover,
Remark

3.9
also implies, for every
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
:

(31)

\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=0,

and

(32)

\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle=0.

We set

(33)

\lambda_{k}\coloneq\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{k}}{\mathrm{Im}\left(\langle\dot{\xi_{k}},\xi_{k}\rangle\right)}},\quad\text{for every $1\leq k\leq m_{2}$.}

We then define

\sigma_{k}\coloneq\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{k}\xi_{k})v_{k}+\mathrm{Im}(\lambda_{k}\xi_{k})w_{k}\colon[0,1]\to V,\quad\text{for every $1\leq k\leq m_{2}$.}

Moreover, we also define

\tau\coloneq\eta+\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\sigma_{k}\colon[0,1]\to V.

We claim that the multiplicative integral
\widetilde{\tau}\colon[0,1]\to G

\widetilde{\tau}(1)=\widetilde{\eta}(1)+\zeta
.
For every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
, we compute

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}[\sigma_{k}(t),\dot{\eta}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_eta_dot}}}{{=}}\int^{1}_{0}\left[\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{k}\xi_{k}(t))v_{k},\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\left(\sum_{h=1}^{r}A_{jh}\sin(t\omega_{h})+B_{jh}\cos(t\omega_{h})\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{1}_{0}\left[\mathrm{Im}(\lambda_{k}\xi_{k}(t))w_{k},\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\left(\sum_{h=1}^{r}A_{jh}\sin(t\omega_{h})+B_{jh}\cos(t\omega_{h})\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{r}\left(A_{jh}\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k}),\sin(t\omega_{h})\rangle+B_{jh}\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k}),\cos(t\omega_{h})\rangle\right)[w_{k},e_{j}]

Analogously, for every
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
, we compute

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}[\sigma_{k_{1}}(t),\dot{\sigma}_{k_{2}}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\lambda_{k_{1}}\lambda_{k_{2}}\bigg(\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle[v_{k_{1}},v_{k_{2}}]+\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle[v_{k_{1}},w_{k_{2}}]

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle[w_{k_{1}},v_{k_{2}}]+\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\dot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle[w_{k_{1}},w_{k_{2}}]\bigg)

Also, for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}_{k}(1)

\displaystyle=\int^{1}_{0}[\sigma_{k}(t),\dot{\sigma}_{k}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=[v_{k},w_{k}]\lambda_{k}^{2}\int^{1}_{0}\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k}(t))\mathrm{Im}(\dot{\xi}_{k}(t))-\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k}(t))\mathrm{Re}(\dot{\xi}_{k}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}[v_{k},w_{k}].

By recursively applying
Lemma

3.2
, we get

(34)

\widetilde{\tau}(1)=\widetilde{\eta}(1)+\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\widetilde{\sigma}_{k}(1)=\widetilde{\eta}(1)+\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\alpha_{k}[v_{k},w_{k}]=\widetilde{\eta}(1)+\zeta.

We have proved that
\widetilde{\tau}
reaches the end-point
\widetilde{\eta}(1)+\zeta
. We next compute

. Firstly, for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
, we integrate by parts and compute

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}(\dot{\sigma}_{k}(t),\dot{\eta}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{r}\left(A^{\prime}_{jh}\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k}),\sin(t\omega_{h})\rangle+B^{\prime}_{jh}\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k}),\cos(t\omega_{h})\rangle\right)\rho_{e}(w_{k},e_{j})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{re_im_xi}}}{{=}}0.

Analogously, for every
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
, we compute

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}

\displaystyle\rho_{e}(\dot{\sigma}_{k_{1}}(t),\dot{\sigma}_{k_{2}}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}(\sigma_{k_{1}}(t),\ddot{\sigma}_{k_{2}}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{k_{1}}\lambda_{k_{2}}\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}\left(\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}(t))v_{k_{1}}+\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}(t))w_{k_{1}},\mathrm{Re}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}}(t))v_{k_{2}}+\mathrm{Im}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}}(t))w_{k_{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{k_{1}}\lambda_{k_{2}}\big(\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle\rho_{e}(v_{k_{1}},v_{k_{2}})+\langle\mathrm{Re}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle\rho_{e}(v_{k_{1}},w_{k_{2}})+

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Re}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle\rho_{e}(w_{k_{1}},v_{k_{2}})+\langle\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{k_{1}}),\mathrm{Im}(\ddot{\xi}_{k_{2}})\rangle\rho_{e}(w_{k_{1}},w_{k_{2}})\big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{re_im_xi_3}}}{{=}}0.

Also, for every
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
, we get

(35)

J(\widetilde{\sigma}_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{energy rho}}}{{=}}\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{e}(\dot{\sigma}_{k}(t),\dot{\sigma}_{k}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t=\lambda_{k}^{2}\int^{1}_{0}\mathrm{Re}(\dot{\xi}_{k})^{2}+\mathrm{Im}(\dot{\xi}_{k})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\lambda_{k}^{2}\langle\dot{\xi}_{k},\dot{\xi}_{k}\rangle=\lambda_{k}^{2},

where we used property (iii) and the fact that
v_{k}
and
w_{k}
are orthonormal. Moreover, we get the following estimate

(36)

\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\lambda_{k}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def_lambda_k}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{\mathrm{Im}\langle\dot{\xi_{k}},\xi_{k}\rangle}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{estimate image}}}{{\leq}}4\pi(m_{1}+1)m_{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\alpha_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{key_bound}}}{{\leq}}4\pi K(m_{1}+1)m_{2}\lvert\zeta\rvert,

By recursively applying
Remark

3.7
, we get

(37)

J(\widetilde{\tau})=J(\widetilde{\eta})+\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}J(\widetilde{\sigma}_{k})=J(\widetilde{\eta})+\sum_{k=1}^{m_{2}}\lambda_{k}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{estimate_lambda}}}{{\leq}}J(\widetilde{\eta})+4\pi K(m_{1}+1)m_{2}\lvert\zeta\rvert.

We define the curve
\psi(t)\coloneq\widetilde{\tau}(t)+t\mu
and we observe that it is
\Delta
-horizontal, since
\widetilde{\tau}
is
\Delta_{\infty}
-horizontal and
\mu\in\Delta_{e}\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Moreover

\psi(1)=\widetilde{\tau}(1)+\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{end_tau}}}{{=}}\widetilde{\eta}(1)+\zeta+\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{q_end_point}}}{{=}}q\cdot\exp(\zeta).

We finally get the estimate

\displaystyle d(e,q\cdot\exp(\zeta))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle J(\psi)=J(\widetilde{\tau})+\rho(\mu,\mu)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{energy_tau}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle J(\widetilde{\eta})+4\pi K(m_{1}+1)m_{2}\lvert\zeta\rvert+\rho(\mu,\mu)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{energy_gamma}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle J(\gamma)+4\pi K(m_{1}+1)m_{2}\lvert\zeta\rvert

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{gamma_geodesic}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle d(e,q)^{2}+4\pi K(m_{1}+1)m_{2}\lvert\zeta\rvert.

The statement is then proved by setting
C=4\pi K(m_{1}+1)m_{2}
.
∎

Thanks to
Lemma

3.10
, we get the following improvement of
[
6
, Proposition 6.3.28]
, obtaining in addition a uniform bound on
\zeta
:

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
G
be a
2
-step nilpotent group as in (
12
) and let
(\Delta,\rho)
be a sub-Riemannian structure on
G
with
V
as in (
7
). Then there exists
C>0
such that, for every energy-minimizing curve
\gamma=(x,y)\colon[0,1]\to G
with
\gamma(0)=(0,0)
, there are
M\in\mathfrak{so}(V)
,
b\in\ker(M)
,
c\in M(V)
, and
\zeta\in[V,V]\cap\Delta
such that
\sqrt{\rho(\zeta,\zeta)}\leq C
and

\begin{cases}x(t)=c-e^{tM}c+tb\\
y(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}[x(s),\dot{x}(s)]\,\mathrm{d}s+t\zeta,\end{cases}\quad\text{for every $t\in[0,1]$.}

Proof.

Fix on
[V,V]
a norm
\lvert\cdot\rvert
that extends the restriction of
\sqrt{\rho(\cdot,\cdot)}
on
\Delta\cap[V,V]
. Let
C_{1}>0
be the constant from

3.10
so that

(38)

d(e,q\cdot\exp(\zeta))^{2}\leq d(e,q)^{2}+C_{1}\lvert\zeta\rvert,\quad\text{for every $q\in G$ and $\zeta\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$.}

Fix
q\in G
and consider an energy-minimizing curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to G
such that
\gamma(0)=e
and
\gamma(1)=q
. From (
16
), we get that

(39)

d(e,q)^{2}=J(\gamma).

From
Proposition

3.5
, there are
M\in\mathfrak{so}(V)
,
b\in\ker(M)
,
c\in M(V)
, and
\zeta\in[V,V]\cap\Delta
such that
\gamma=(x,y)

\begin{cases}x(t)=c-e^{tM}c+tb\\
y(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}[x(s),\dot{x}(s)]\,\mathrm{d}s+t\zeta,\end{cases}\quad\text{for every $t\in[0,1]$.}

We define
\eta\coloneq\pi\circ\gamma\colon[0,1]\to V
and we observe that, for every

\gamma(t)=\widetilde{\eta}(t)+t\zeta
, where
\widetilde{\eta}
denotes the multiplicative integral of
\eta
. In particular

(40)

q=\widetilde{\eta}(1)+\zeta.

Moreover, since

and
[V,V]\cap\Delta
are
\rho_{e}
-orthogonal, we also have

(41)

J(\gamma)=J(\widetilde{\eta})+\rho(\zeta,\zeta).

We then estimate

\displaystyle\rho(\zeta,\zeta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{energy_gamma_2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle J(\gamma)-J(\widetilde{\eta})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{distance_q_2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle d(e,q)^{2}-J(\widetilde{\eta})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{point_q_2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle d(e,\widetilde{\eta}(1)\cdot\exp(\zeta))^{2}-J(\widetilde{\eta})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pitagora_ballbox2}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle d(e,\widetilde{\eta}(1))^{2}+C_{1}\lvert\zeta\rvert-J(\widetilde{\eta})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle J(\widetilde{\eta})+C_{1}\lvert\zeta\rvert-J(\widetilde{\eta})

\displaystyle C_{1}\lvert\zeta\rvert.

Since

coincides with

. We conclude that

. The statement in then proved by setting

.
∎

Proof of

.

Thanks to

in (

. Fix

Corollary 3.12
.

In the assumption of

1.1
,
the identity map
\mathrm{id}\colon(G,d)\to(G,d_{\infty})
is a rough isometry.

Proof.

Clearly, we have

\left\lvert d_{\infty}(p,q)-d(p,q)\right\rvert\leq d_{\infty}(p,q)+d(p,q)\quad\text{for every $p,q\in G$.}

By combining the previous estimate with (

\left\lvert d_{\infty}(p,q)-d(p,q)\right\rvert\leq\min\Set{\frac{C}{d_{\infty}(p,q)+d(p,q)},d_{\infty}(p,q)+d(p,q)}\leq\sqrt{C}\quad\text{for every $p,q\in G,\,p\neq q$,}

where in the last inequality we estimated the minimum with the geometric mean. The estimate for

is clearly satisfied as well. We conclude that the identity map
(G,d)\to(G,d_{\infty})
is a rough isometry.
∎

Before proving
Theorem

1.2
, we recall some terminology and basic facts about the abelianization of a metric Lie group. We refer to
[
6
, Section 9.1]
for details. Hereafter,
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
denotes the group of Lie group automorphism of a Lie group
G
.

Let
G
be a nilpotent Lie group with associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The
abelianization

G_{\mathrm{ab}}
of
G
is the quotient Lie group

G_{\mathrm{ab}}\coloneq G/[G,G].

We denote by
\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}
the quotient map
\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}\colon G\to G_{\mathrm{ab}}
. If
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
, then there exists a unique
\varphi_{\mathrm{ab}}\in\mathrm{Aut}(G_{\mathrm{ab}})
such that
\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}\circ\varphi=\varphi_{\mathrm{ab}}\circ\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}
. We refer to
\varphi_{\mathrm{ab}}
as the
abelianization map of
\varphi
.

If
(G,d)
is a nilpotent sub-Riemannian Lie group, then there exists a unique Euclidean norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}
on
G_{\mathrm{ab}}
such that the quotient map

\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}\colon(G,d)\to(G_{\mathrm{ab}},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}})

is a submetry. We refer to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}
as the
abelianization norm of
(G,d)
.

Remark 3.13
.

If
G
is a simply connected nilpotent Lie group with associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=V\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
are such that
\varphi_{\mathrm{ab}}=\varphi^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ab}}
and
\mathrm{d}_{e}\varphi(V)=\mathrm{d}_{e}\varphi^{\prime}(V)
, then
\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}
. This is a consequence of the fact that, in each nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, every subspace
V\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
complementary to
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is bracket-generating.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
G
be a simply connected,
2
-step nilpotent Lie group, with associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, equipped with sub-Riemannian structures
(\Delta,\rho)
and
(\Delta^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
such that

(49)

\Delta_{e}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{g}\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta^{\prime}_{e}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{g}.

Denote by
d
and
d^{\prime}
the sub-Riemannian metrics associated to
(\Delta,\rho)
and
(\Delta^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
, respectively. Also, denote by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ab}}
the abelianization norms of
(G,d)
and
(G,d^{\prime})
, respectively.
If
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}=\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ab}}
, there exists a Lie group morphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
such that the following hold

(i)

d(p,q)=d^{\prime}(\varphi(p),\varphi(q))
for every
p,q\in G
, i.e.,
\varphi:(G,d)\to(G,d^{\prime})
is an isometry;

(ii)

\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}\circ\varphi=\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}
, in particular there exists a linear map
L\colon G_{\mathrm{ab}}\to[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
such that

\varphi(p)=p\cdot\exp\left(L(\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(p))\right),\quad\text{for every $p\in G$.}

Proof.

By (
49
), there is a linear map
L:\Delta_{e}\to[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
such that
\Delta^{\prime}_{e}
is the graph of
L
, that is,

\Delta^{\prime}_{e}=\{(x,Lx)\in\Delta_{e}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]:x\in\Delta_{e}\}.

Define
f:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by
f(x,y)=(x,y+Lx)
for all
x\in\Delta_{e}
and
y\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.
Since
f
is a linear isomorphism with
f([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])\subseteq[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, and since
\mathfrak{g}
has step 2,
then
f
is a Lie algebra automorphism.
Let
\varphi\coloneq\exp\circ f\circ\exp^{-1}
be the corresponding Lie group automorphism.
Clearly
\varphi
satisfies (ii).

Notice that the abelianization map
\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is a linear isomorphism when restricted to
\Delta_{e}
and
\Delta^{\prime}_{e}
.
It is an easy fact that these restrictions

(\Delta_{e},\sqrt{\rho_{e}})\to(\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}})

and
(\Delta^{\prime}_{e},\sqrt{\rho^{\prime}_{e}})\to(\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ab}})

of
\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}

are submetries (see
[
6
, Definition 9.1.1]
) and thus isometries.
Therefore,

\sqrt{\rho_{e}((x,0),(x,0))}=\lVert\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(x,0)\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}=\lVert\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(x,Lx)\rVert^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ab}}=\sqrt{\rho^{\prime}_{e}((x,Lx),(x,Lx))}.

We conclude that the restriction
f:(\Delta_{e},\rho_{e})\to(\Delta^{\prime}_{e},\rho^{\prime}_{e})
is an isometry and therefore
\varphi:(G,d)\to(G,d^{\prime})
is an isometry.
∎

Instead of
Theorem

1.2
, we actually prove the following sharper statement.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
G
be a simply connected,
2
-step nilpotent Lie group equipped with two sub-Riemannian structures
(\Delta,\rho)
and
(\Delta^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
and denote by
d
and
d^{\prime}
the sub-Riemannian metrics corresponding to
(\Delta,\rho)
and
(\Delta^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
, respectively. The following are equivalent:

(i)

the two metrics are asymptotic, i.e.,
\frac{d^{\prime}(p,q)}{d(p,q)}\to 1
as
d(p,q)\to\infty
;

(ii)

the abelianization norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ab}}
of
(G,d)
and
(G,d^{\prime})
, respectively, coincides;

(iii)

the identity map
\mathrm{id}\colon(G,d)\to(G,d^{\prime})
is a rough isometry, i.e., there exists
C>0
such that

\lvert d(p,q)-d^{\prime}(p,q)\rvert\leq C,\quad\text{for every $p,q\in G$.}

Proof of
Theorem

3.15
.

The equivalence
(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
is known to hold for simply connected sub-Finsler nilpotent Lie groups; see
[
3
, Proposition 3]
). The implication
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
is immediate. We now prove that
(i,ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra associated to
G
. Thanks to
Corollary

3.12
, it is not restrictive to assume that
d
and
d^{\prime}
are canonical asymptotic metrics, for which (
49
) is satisfied. From
Lemma

3.14
, there exists a Lie group morphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
and a linear map
L\colon G_{\mathrm{ab}}\to[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
such that

(50)

\displaystyle d^{\prime}(p,q)

\displaystyle=d(\varphi(p),\varphi(q)),\quad\text{for every $p,q\in G$},

(51)

\displaystyle\varphi(p)

\displaystyle=p\cdot\exp\left(L(\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(p))\right),\quad\text{for every $p\in G$.}

Thanks to
Lemma

3.10
, there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

(52)

d(e,q\cdot\exp(\zeta))^{2}\leq d(e,q)^{2}+C_{1}\lvert\zeta\rvert,\quad\text{for every $q\in G$ and $\zeta\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$.}

We want to bound
\lvert d(p,q)-d^{\prime}(p,q)\rvert
, since
d
and
d^{\prime}
are both left-invariant, it is not restrictive to consider
p=e
. Fix
q\neq e
and a norm
\lvert\cdot\rvert
on
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. We estimate

\displaystyle d^{\prime}(e,q)^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{phi d}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle d(\varphi(e),\varphi(q))^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{phi L}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle d(e,q\cdot\exp\left(L(\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(q))\right))^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pitagora_ballbox4}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle d(e,q)^{2}+C_{1}\lvert L(\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(q))\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(e,q)^{2}+C_{1}\lVert L\rVert\lvert\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(q)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(e,q)^{2}+C_{1}\lVert L\rVert d(e,q),

where
\lVert L\rVert
denotes the operator norm of
L\colon(G_{\mathrm{ab}},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}})\to([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],\lvert\cdot\rvert)
and in the last inequality we used the fact that
\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}
is a submetry. We obtain

(53)

d^{\prime}(e,q)^{2}\leq d(e,q)^{2}+C_{1}\lVert L\rVert d(e,q)\leq(d(e,q)^{2}+C_{1}\lVert L\rVert)^{2},

and therefore
d^{\prime}(e,q)-d(e,q)\leq C_{1}\lVert L\rVert
.
Similarly, thanks to
Lemma

3.10
, there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

d^{\prime}(e,q\cdot\exp(\zeta))^{2}\leq d^{\prime}(e,q)^{2}+C_{2}\lvert\zeta\rvert,\quad\text{for every $q\in G$ and $\zeta\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$.}

An analogous argument shows that
d(e,q)-d^{\prime}(e,q)\leq C_{2}\lVert L\rVert
. The statement is then proved by setting
C\coloneq\max\set{C_{1},C_{2}}\lVert L\rVert
.
∎

4.
Consequences, examples, and sharpness of results

4.1.
Rate of convergence to the asymptotic cone

Theorem

1.1
provides a finer estimate on the rate convergence of a simply connected
2
-step nilpotent sub-Riemannian Lie group
(G,d)
to its asymptotic cone
(\widehat{G},\widehat{d})
. We refer to
[
4
, Chapters 7,8]
for the notion of Gromov-Hausdorff distance between metric spaces, Gromov-Hausdorff limit, and asymptotic cone of a metric space.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(G,d)
be a simply connected
2
-step nilpotent sub-Riemannian Lie group and denote by
(\widehat{G},\widehat{d})
its asymptotic cone. Then

(54)

d_{GH}\left((G,\tfrac{1}{n}d),(\widehat{G},\widehat{d})\right)=O(n^{-1}),\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$,}

where
d_{GH}
denotes the Gromov-Hausdorff distance.

Proof.

Let
(G,d)
be as in the statement and denote by
(G,d_{\infty})
its canonical asymptotic metric, defined in (
10
). As discussed in
Section

2.2
,
(G,d_{\infty})
is isometric to the asymptotic cone
(\widehat{G},\widehat{d})
of
(G,d)
. From
Corollary

3.12
, there exists
C>0
such that

\left\lvert\frac{1}{n}d_{\infty}(p,q)-\frac{1}{n}d(p,q)\right\rvert\leq\frac{C}{n}\quad\text{for every $p,q\in G$, and $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Thus, we infer (see
[
4
, Corollary 7.3.28]
) that

(55)

d_{GH}\left((G,\tfrac{1}{n}d),(G,\tfrac{1}{n}d_{\infty})\right)\leq\frac{2C}{n}.

We recall that
(G,d_{\infty})
is a Carnot metric and therefore it is self-similar, i.e.,
(G,d_{\infty})
is isometric to
(G,\lambda d_{\infty})
for every
\lambda>0
. We conclude that
(G,\tfrac{1}{n}d_{\infty})
is isometric to
(\widehat{G},\widehat{d})
. We have proved (
54
).
∎

The latter result improves similar known estimates for simply connected,
2
-step nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie groups. In
[
3
]
, it is proved that

d_{GH}\left((B(n),\tfrac{1}{n}d),(\widehat{B}(1),\widehat{d})\right)=O(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}),\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$,}

where
B(n)\coloneq\set{p\in G\colon d(0,p)\leq n}
and
\widehat{B}(1)\coloneq\set{p\in\widehat{G}\colon\widehat{d}(0,p)\leq 1}
. The authors provided an example of a
2
-step nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie group
(G,d)
for which the exponent
-\tfrac{1}{2}
is sharp, and in particular it is not
(1,C)
-quasi isometric to its asymptotic cone.

4.2.
The non validity for Finlser metrics

We provide an example of a Finsler metric
d
on the Heisenberg group such that no Carnot metric
d^{\prime}
satisfies

(56)

\lvert d^{\prime}(p,q)-d(p,q)\rvert\to 0,\quad\text{as \quad$d(p,q)\to\infty$.}

For a thorough introduction to Finsler and sub-Finsler metrics in nilpotent Lie groups we refer to
[
6
]
.

Example 4.2
.

The
Heisenberg group

\mathbb{H}
is the simply connected Lie group whose associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a basis
\set{X,Y,Z}
such that the only non-trivial bracket relations between elements of the basis are
[X,Y]=Z
.

We consider the Finsler metric
d
on
\mathbb{H}
such that its associated Finsler norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
on
\mathfrak{g}
has unit ball that is the convex-hull
S
of points

\pm X,\quad\pm Y,\quad X+Z,\quad-X-Z.

We identify
\mathbb{H}
with
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the global diffeomorphism

\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{H},\quad(x,y,z)\mapsto\exp(xX+yY+zZ).

Assume that there exists a Carnot metric
d^{\prime}
such that (
56
) holds, in particular
d
and
d^{\prime}
are asymptotic. Denote by
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}
the sub-Finsler norm associated to
d^{\prime}
. Since
d
and
d^{\prime}
are asymptotic, the abelianization norms of
(\mathbb{H},d)
and
(\mathbb{H},d^{\prime})
coincides; see
[
3
, Proposition 3]
. We recall that, since
d^{\prime}
is a Carnot metric, there exists a subspace
V\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, complementary to
Z
, such that unit ball
S^{\prime}
of
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}
is contained in
V
. Moreover, the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}
is an
\ell^{1}
norm on
V
. We get that there exists a unique
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that
X+\alpha Z\in S^{\prime}
. We fix
\beta\in[0,1]

and define the sequence of points

p_{n}:=(n,0,\beta n),\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

It is immediate to check that
X+\beta Z\in S
and hence

(57)

d(e,p_{n})\leq n.

Moreover, the complete classification of geodesics for the Heisenberg group equipped with sub-Finsler
\ell^{1}
norms (see
[
3
]
) allows us to compute explicitly

(58)

d^{\prime}(e,p_{n})=n+2\lvert\beta-\alpha\rvert.

We observe that, if in addition
\beta\neq\alpha
, then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\lvert d^{\prime}(e,p_{n})-d(e,p_{n})\rvert\geq 2\lvert\beta-\alpha\rvert>0.

Therefore (
56
) is not satisfied.

4.3.
The non validity for 3-step Riemannian metrics

We show that the consequence of
Theorem

1.1
cannot be extended to sub-Riemannian nilpotent Lie groups of higher step. We provide an example of a Riemannian metric
d
on the Engel group that is not at finite distance from its canonical asymptotic metric.

Example 4.3
.

The
Engel group

\mathbb{E}
is the simply connected Lie group whose associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a basis
\set{X_{1},X_{2},Y,Z}
such that the only non-trivial bracket relations between elements of the basis are

[X_{1},X_{2}]=Y,\qquad[X_{1},Y]=Z.

We consider the left-invariant Riemannian metric
d
on
\mathbb{E}
such that
\set{X_{1},X_{2},Y,Z}
is an orthonormal frame for the metric tensor
g
. Since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathrm{span}\set{Y,Z}
, we get that the canonical asymptotic metric
d_{\infty}
associated to
d
is the metric
d_{\infty}
induced by the sub-Riemannian structure
(\Delta,\rho_{|\Delta})
, where

\Delta=\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{E}}\mathrm{span}\set{\mathrm{d}L_{p}(X_{1}),\mathrm{d}L_{p}(X_{2})}.

We identify
\mathbb{E}
and
\mathbb{R}^{4}
with the global diffeomorphism

\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{E},\quad(x_{1},x_{2},y,z)\mapsto\exp(x_{1}X_{1}+x_{2}X_{2}+yY+zZ).

With this identification, we define
p_{n}\coloneq(0,n,0,\sqrt{n})\in\mathbb{E}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By considering the straight line
\gamma_{n}(t)\coloneq(0,tn,0,t\sqrt{n})
, we get the estimate

(59)

d(e,p_{n})\leq\sqrt{n^{2}+n}\leq n+\tfrac{1}{2}.

We stress that
(\mathbb{E},d_{\infty})
is self-similar, in particular

(60)

d_{\infty}(e,(x_{1},x_{2},y,z))=\lambda d_{\infty}(e,(\lambda^{-1}x_{1},\lambda^{-1}x_{2},\lambda^{-2}y,\lambda^{-3}z)),\quad\text{for every $(x_{1},x_{2},y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}$ and $\lambda\geq 0$.}

Moreover, from
[
14
, Lemma 4.1]
, there exists
C>0
such that

(61)

d_{\infty}(e,(0,1,0,\varepsilon))\geq 1+C\varepsilon^{1/3},\quad\text{for every $\varepsilon\in[0,1)$.}

Therefore, we get the estimate

d_{\infty}(e,p_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{dilation_engel}}}{{=}}nd_{\infty}(e,(0,1,0,n^{-\frac{5}{2}}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{cusp_engel}}}{{\geq}}n(1+Cn^{-\frac{5}{6}})=n+Cn^{\frac{1}{6}}.

By comparing it with (
59
), we get

\lvert d_{\infty}(e,p_{n})-d(e,p_{n})\rvert\geq Cn^{\frac{1}{6}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\to\infty,\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

4.4.
Volume growth of metric balls

In the study of groups of polynomial growth, a key aspect is to determine the volume asymptotics of metric balls
B_{r}
. The leading term of this expansion for nilpotent groups has been first characterized in
[
15
]
. The error term, however, is far from being understood, even in the class of
2
-step nilpotent metric groups, see
[
3
]
. Our result
Theorem

1.1
provides a fine estimate for this error term in the class of sub-Riemannian
2
-step nilpotent Lie groups.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(G,d)
be a simply connected
2
-step nilpotent sub-Riemannian Lie group and fix a Haar measure
\mathrm{vol}
on
G
. For every
r\geq 0
, denote by
B(r)\subseteq G
the metric ball of radius
r
, centered at the identity element of
G
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\mathrm{vol}(B(r))=Cr^{Q}+O(r^{Q-2}),\quad\text{as $r\to\infty$,}

where
Q
is the Hausdorff dimension of the asymptotic cone of
(G,d)
.

Proof.

Let
d_{\infty}
be the canonical asymptotic metric of
(G,d)
. Let
C_{1}
be the constant from
Theorem

1.1
. We denote by
B_{\infty}(r)
the metric ball of radius
r
with respect to the distance function
d_{\infty}
, centered at the identity element of
G
. Since the metric
d_{\infty}
is self-similar, we have

(62)

\mathrm{vol}\left(B_{\infty}(r)\right)=Cr^{Q},\quad\text{for every $r\geq 0$,}

where
C\coloneq\mathrm{vol}\left(B_{\infty}(1)\right)
and
Q
is the Hausdorff dimension of
(G,d_{\infty})
, which is isometric to the asymptotic cone of
(G,d)
.

Fix
r\geq 0
. If
p\in G
is such that
d(e,p)\leq r
, then
d_{\infty}(e,p)\leq\sqrt{r^{2}+C_{1}}
by (
1
),. Therefore
B(r)\subseteq B_{\infty}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}+C_{1}}\right)
and

\mathrm{vol}\left(B(r)\right)\leq\mathrm{vol}\left(B_{\infty}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}+C_{1}}\right)\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{volume_spheres}}}{{=}}C(r^{2}+C_{1})^{\frac{Q}{2}}=Cr^{Q}+O(r^{Q-2}),\quad\text{as $r\to\infty$.}

The opposite estimate follows from the fact that
d\leq d_{\infty}
, which implies
\mathrm{vol}(B(r))\geq\mathrm{vol}(B_{\infty}(r))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{volume_spheres}}}{{=}}Cr^{Q}
for every
r\geq 0
.
∎

4.5.
Precise volume growth of the Riemannian Heisenberg group

We show that the consequence of
Theorem

4.4
is sharp for the Riemannian Heisenberg group by explicitly computing that in this case one has

(63)

\mathrm{vol}(B(r))=C_{4}r^{4}+C_{2}r^{2}+C_{0},\quad\text{for every $r>2\pi$,}

for some constants
C_{0},C_{2},C_{4}\neq 0
.

Example 4.5
.

The
Heisenberg group

\mathbb{H}
is the simply connected Lie group whose associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a basis
\set{X,Y,Z}
such that the only non-trivial bracket relations between elements of the basis are
[X,Y]=Z
.

We consider the left-invariant Riemannian metric
d
on
\mathbb{H}
such that
\set{X,Y,Z}
is an orthonormal frame for the metric tensor
\rho
. We fix
r>0
and we study the volume of the metric ball

B(r)\coloneq\set{p\in\mathbb{H}\,\colon\,d(e,p)\leq r}.

We identify
\mathbb{H}
with
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the global diffeomorphism

\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{H},\quad(x,y,z)\mapsto\exp(xX+yY+zZ).

We remark that the pushforward by
\Phi
of the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}
.

With this identification, the function
f\coloneq d(e,\cdot)
has a rotational symmetry around the
Z
axis and a reflectional symmetry along the
XY
plane. Therefore
B(r)
is completely determined by

E(r)\coloneq\set{(x,0,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,\colon x,z>0,\,d(e,(x,0,z))=r}.

As a consequence of
[
13
, Proposition 5.4]
, every point
p\in E(r)
is reached by a unique length-minimizing curve from the identity element
e=(0,0,0)
. Such curve is of the form
\gamma_{p}=(x_{p},y_{p},z_{p})\colon[0,t_{p}]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where

\begin{cases}x_{p}(t)=\frac{1}{k_{p}}\left((\cos\theta_{p}(\cos(k_{p}t)-1)-\sin\theta_{p}\sin(k_{p}t)\right)\\
y_{p}(t)=\frac{1}{k_{p}}\left((\sin\theta_{p}(\cos(k_{p}t)-1)+\cos\theta_{p}\sin(k_{p}t)\right)\\
z_{p}(t)=\frac{t}{2k_{p}}-\frac{1}{2k_{p}^{2}}\sin(k_{p}t)+k_{p}t\end{cases}

for some
k_{p}>0
,
\theta_{p}\in[0,2\pi)
and
0<t_{p}<\frac{2\pi}{k_{p}}
. Therefore, we get

p=\left(\frac{\sqrt{2-2\cos(k_{p}t_{p})}}{k_{p}},0,\frac{k_{p}t_{p}-\sin(k_{p}t_{p})}{2k_{p}^{2}}+k_{p}t_{p}\right)

We observe that
\gamma_{p}
is parametrized with constant speed
\sqrt{1+k_{p}^{2}}
, thus we require
t_{p}\sqrt{1+k_{p}^{2}}=r
.

We set
\alpha_{p}\coloneq k_{p}t_{p}
. The condition
0<t_{p}<\frac{2\pi}{k_{p}}
implies
\alpha_{p}<2\pi
, whilst the condition
t_{p}\sqrt{1+k_{p}^{2}}=r
implies
\alpha_{p}<r
. Vice-versa, we claim that for every
0<\alpha<\min\set{2\pi,r}
, there exists a unique
k>0
and
0<t<\frac{2\pi}{k}
such that
kt=\alpha
and
t\sqrt{1+k^{2}}=r
. Indeed, after some easy computations, these two conditions are equivalent to

\frac{k}{\sqrt{1+k^{2}}}=\frac{\alpha}{r},\quad t=\sqrt{r^{2}-\alpha^{2}},

which admit unique solutions
k,t>0
, since
\alpha<r
. Moreover, since
\alpha<2\pi
, we get
t<\frac{2\pi}{k}
.

We conclude that the curve
p\coloneq(0,\min\set{2\pi,r})\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, defined by setting

p(\alpha)\coloneq\left(\frac{\sqrt{r^{2}-\alpha^{2}}\sqrt{2-2\cos(\alpha)}}{\alpha},0,\frac{(r^{2}-\alpha^{2})(\alpha-\sin(\alpha))}{2\alpha^{2}}+\alpha\right),

parametrizes the set
E(r)
.

The volume
V(r)
of the ball
B(r)
is therefore twice the volume of the solid of revolution with profile
p(\alpha)
around the
Z
axis. Thus

V(r)=2\pi\int_{0}^{\min\set{2\pi,r}}x(\alpha)^{2}\frac{\partial z(\alpha)}{\partial\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha.

After some straightforward computations, we finally get

\displaystyle V(r)

\displaystyle=2\pi r^{4}\int_{0}^{\min\set{2\pi,r}}\frac{1-\cos(\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}\cdot\frac{2\sin(\alpha)-\alpha\cos(\alpha)-\alpha}{\alpha^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle+2\pi r^{2}\int_{0}^{\min\set{2\pi,r}}\frac{\sin^{2}(\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{(1-\cos(\alpha))(2\sin(\alpha)-\alpha\cos(\alpha)-\alpha)}{\alpha^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle-2\pi\int_{0}^{\min\set{2\pi,r}}\sin^{2}(\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\alpha.

We stress that, as expected
V(r)=O(r^{3})
, as
r\to 0
. Whilst for
r\geq 2\pi
, we get (
63
)
for some
C_{0},C_{2},C_{4}\neq 0
.

4.6.
Sharpness of the exponent in
Theorem

1.1

The precise computation (
63
) of the volume growth in the Riemannian Heisenberg group shows the sharpness of
Theorem

1.1
in the following sense.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(\mathbb{H},d)
be the Riemannian Heisenberg group, denote by
d_{\infty}
its canonical asymptotic metric. If
\beta>2
then

(64)

\sup_{p,q\in\mathbb{H}}\lvert d_{\infty}^{\beta}(p,q)-d^{\beta}(p,q)\rvert=+\infty.

Proof.

Suppose that, for some
\beta>2
, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that

\lvert d_{\infty}^{\beta}(p,q)-d^{\beta}(p,q)\rvert\leq C^{\prime},\quad\text{for every $p,q\in\mathbb{H}$.}

Then, by following the same argument in the proof of
Theorem

4.4
, we would get

\mathrm{vol}(B(r))=Cr^{4}+O(r^{4-\beta}),\quad\text{as $r\to\infty$,}

which is in contradiction with (
63
).
∎

4.7.
A vast class of rough isometries

Theorem

3.15
allows to determine a class of rough isometries made of automorphisms. Given a simply connected,
2
-step nilpotent sub-Riemannian Lie group
(G,d)
and a map
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
, we define a new metric
d^{\prime}
as

d^{\prime}(p,q)=d(\varphi(p),\varphi(q)),\quad\text{for every $p,q\in G$.}

The metric space
(G,d^{\prime})
is again a sub-Riemannian Lie group, with sub-Riemannian structure given by the pull-back of the sub-Riemannian structure of
(G,d)
. Let us denote by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ab}}
the abelianization norms of
(G,d)
and
(G,d^{\prime})
, respectively.

If we further assume that
\varphi_{\mathrm{ab}}
is an isometry for
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}
, then
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}=\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ab}}
and
Theorem

3.15
implies that
\varphi
is a rough isometry. We summarize our discussion in the following result.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(G,d)
be simply connected,
2
-step nilpotent sub-Riemannian Lie group. If
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
is such that
\varphi_{\mathrm{ab}}
is an isometry for the abelianization norm of
(G,d)
, then
\varphi
is a rough isometry.

We point out that, in every simply connected,
2
-step nilpotent Lie group
G
, the map

(65)

\varphi\colon G\to G,\quad g\mapsto g\cdot L(\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(g))

is a Lie group automorphism of
G
, for every linear map
L\colon G_{\mathrm{ab}}\to[G,G]
. In particular, an automorphism
\varphi
as in (
65
) satisfies
\varphi_{\mathrm{ab}}=\mathrm{id}
. We conclude that the class of Lie group automorphisms
\varphi
satisfying the hypothesis of
Proposition

4.7
contains an abelian subgroup isomorphic to the vector space of linear homomorphisms from
G_{\mathrm{ab}}
to
[G,G]
.

Thanks to
Proposition

4.7
, we exhibit an example of a rough isometry
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
of a sub-Riemannian Lie group
(G,d)
that is not at bounded distance from an isometry.

Example 4.8
.

We consider
G=\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
to be the simply connected
2
-step nilpotent Lie group whose associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a basis
\set{X,Y,Z,T}
such that the only non-trivial bracket relations between elements of the basis are
[X,Y]=Z
.
We consider on
G
the sub-Riemannian structure
(\Delta,\rho)
such that
\set{X,Y,T}
is an orthonormal frame and we denote by
d
the corresponding sub-Riemannian metric. We identify
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
with
\mathbb{R}^{4}
with the global diffeomorphism

\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R},\quad(x,y,z,t)\mapsto\exp(xX+yY+zZ+tT).

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
be the Lie group such that, in coordinates

\varphi(x,y,z,t)=(x,y,z+t,t).

We observe that
\varphi_{\mathrm{ab}}
is the identity map and therefore, from
Proposition

4.7
, we get that
\varphi
is a
(1,C)
-quasi isometry for some
C>0
.

Assume by contradiction that
\varphi
is at bounded distance from an isometry
f\colon(G,d)\to(G,d)
, i.e., there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that

(66)

d(f(p),\varphi(p))\leq C^{\prime},\quad\text{for every $p\in G$.}

Since
f
is an isometry of a metric nilpotent Lie group, then
f
is affine, i.e., it is of the form
f=L_{g}\circ\widetilde{f}
for some
g\in G
and some isometry
\widetilde{f}\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
; see
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
). We claim that the abelianization map
\widetilde{f}_{\mathrm{ab}}
of
\widetilde{f}
is the identity map. Indeed, since the map
\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}
is a submetry for the abelianization norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}
, we get

C^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{bounded 1}}}{{\geq}}d(f(p),\varphi(p))\geq\lVert\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}\circ f(p)-\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}\circ\varphi(p)\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}=\lVert L_{\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(g)}\circ\widetilde{f}_{\mathrm{ab}}(\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(p))-\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(p)\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}},\quad\text{for every $p\in G$.}

Therefore,

\lVert\widetilde{f}_{\mathrm{ab}}\circ\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(p)-\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(p)\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}}\leq C^{\prime}+\lVert\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}(g)\rVert_{\mathrm{ab}},\quad\text{for every $p\in G$.}

The latter estimate implies that
\widetilde{f}_{\mathrm{ab}}
, which is a linear map on the abelian group
G_{\mathrm{ab}}
, is at bounded distance from the identity map. We conclude that
\widetilde{f}_{\mathrm{ab}}
is the identity map.

We also observe that, since
\widetilde{f}
is itself an isometry of
(G,d)
, it preserves
\Delta
-horizontal curves and therefore
\mathrm{d}\widetilde{f}(\Delta)=\Delta
.
Thanks to
Remark

3.13
, we infer that
\widetilde{f}
is the identity map as well, thus
f=L_{g}
for some
g\in G
.

We stress that
p_{n}=(0,0,0,n)
is in the center of
G
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and that every sub-Riemannian Lie group is a proper metric space. We then estimate

\displaystyle d(\varphi(p_{n}),f(p_{n}))

\displaystyle=d(p_{n}\cdot(0,0,n,0),g\cdot p_{n})

\displaystyle=d((0,0,n,0),g)\to\infty\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$,}

where we used that
(G,d)
is a proper metric space and that
(0,0,n,0)=nZ
is a central element of
G
.
We thus get a contradiction with the fact that
f
was at bounded distance from
\varphi
; we conclude that
\varphi
is not at bounded distance from any isometry of
(G,d)
.

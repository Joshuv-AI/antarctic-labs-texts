Bohr-Sommerfeld Quantization Rules for 1-D Semiclassical Pseudo-Differential Operator: the Method of Microlocal Wronskian and Gram Matrix
Abdelwaheb Ifa

Abstract
In this paper, we revisit the well known Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization rule (BS) of order 2 for a self-adjoint 1-D semiclassical pseudo-differential operator, within the algebraic and microlocal framework of B. Helffer and J. Sjöstrand. BS holds precisely when the Gram matrix consisting of scalar products of certain WKB solutions with respect to the ”flux norm” is not invertible. This condition is obtained using the microlocal Wronskian and does not rely on traditional matching techniques. It is simplified by using action-angle variables. The interest of this procedure lies in its possible generalization to matrixvalued Hamiltonians, like BdG Hamiltonian.

Introduction

Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization rule, in its first formulation, allows to compute the energy levels
E
of a particle in a one-dimensional potential well, its dynamics being described by the semi-classical Schrödinger operator

P(x,hD_{x})=(hD_{x})^{2}+V(x),\quad\text{with }D_{x}=-i\frac{d}{dx}.

It is given at first order in
h
by the well known formula

\frac{1}{2\pi h}\,\oint_{\gamma_{E}}\xi(x)\,dx=n+\frac{1}{2}

Here
\xi(x)=\sqrt{E-V(x)}
denotes the momentum of the particle on its orbit
\gamma_{E}\subset T^{*}\mathbb{R}
above the potential well
\{V(x)\leq E\}
,
n
is an integer and the integral is computed over
\gamma_{E}
in the phase space
T^{*}\mathbb{R}
.

By the implicit function theorem, we then find
E=E_{n}(h)
. In other words, the number of wavelengths (associated with the particle along
\gamma_{E}
by de Broglie correspondence) must be an integer plus 1/2, called
Maslov correction
.

Let
p(x,\xi;h)
be a smooth real classical Hamiltonian on
T^{*}\mathbb{R}
, admitting a semiclassical expansion

p(x,\xi;h)\sim p_{0}(x,\xi)+h\,p_{1}(x,\xi)+h^{2}\,p_{2}(x,\xi)+\cdots,\quad h\to 0,

where
p_{0}
is the principal symbol, and
p_{1}
the sub-principal symbol. We assume that
p\in S^{0}(m)
for some order function
m
, and that
p+i
is elliptic. Here,
S^{0}(m)
denotes the class of symbols satisfying

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}p(x,\xi;h)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}m(x,\xi),\quad\forall\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N},\,(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R},

for some order function
m(x,\xi)\geq 1
, uniformly in
h\in(0,h_{0}]
. This allows to take the Weyl quantization of
p
, namely

P(x,hD_{x};h)u(x;h)=\big(Op^{W}(p)(u)\big)(x)=(2\,\pi\,h)^{-1}\,\int\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{i}{h}\,(x-y)\,\eta}\,p\big(\frac{x+y}{2},\eta\big)\,u(y)\,dy\,d\eta.

(1.1)

We make the geometric assumption (H) of
[
7
]
: fix a compact interval
I=[E_{-},E_{+}]
, and assume that there exists a topological ring
\mathcal{A}\subset p_{0}^{-1}(I)
, such that
\partial\mathcal{A}=A_{-}\cup A_{+}
, with
A_{\pm}
connected components of
p_{0}^{-1}(E_{\pm})
, and that
p_{0}
has no critical point in
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover,
A_{-}
is included in the disk bounded by
A_{+}
. (if it is not the
case, we can always change
p
to
-p
). These conditions guarantee that the spectrum of
P
in
I
is discrete and can be described semiclassically.

We define the microlocal well
W
as the disk bounded by
A_{+}
. For each
E\in I
, let
\gamma_{E}\subset W
be a periodic orbit on the energy surface
\{p_{0}(x,\xi)=E\}
, so that
\gamma_{E}
is an embedded Lagrangian manifold.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{N}(E)
be the microlocal kernel of
P-E
of order
N
, i.e. the space of local solutions of
(P-E)u=\mathcal{O}(h^{N+1})
in the distributional sense, microlocalized on
\gamma_{E}
. This is a smooth complex vector bundle over
\pi_{x}(\gamma_{E})
. Here we address the problem of finding the set of
E=E(h)
such that
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{N}(E)
contains a global section, i.e. of constructing a sequence of quasi-modes
\big(u_{n}(h),E_{n}(h)\big)
of a given order
N
. As usual we denote by
\mathcal{K}_{h}(E)
the microlocal kernel of
P-E
mod
\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})
; since the distinction between
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{N}(E)
and
\mathcal{K}_{h}(E)
plays no important role here, we shall content to write
\mathcal{K}_{h}(E)
.

Then if
E_{+}<E_{0}=\displaystyle\liminf_{|x,\xi|\rightarrow+\infty}p_{0}(x,\xi)
, all eigenvalues of
P
in
I
are indeed by
Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition
(BS)
\mathcal{S}_{h}\big(E_{n}(h)\big)=2\pi nh
, where the semiclassical action
\mathcal{S}_{h}(E)
has the asymptotics

\mathcal{S}_{h}(E)\sim S_{0}(E)+h\,S_{1}(E)+h^{2}\,S_{2}(E)+\cdots

We determine BS at any accuracy by computing quasi-modes. There are a lot of ways to derive BS: the method of matching of WKB solutions
[
4
]
, known also as Liouville-Green method
[

]
, which has received many improvements
(
see
[

]
)
, the method of the monodromy operator
(

, the method of quantization deformation based on Functional Calculus and Trace Formulas

,

,

,

]
,

.

Exemple 1.1
.

(BS quantization rule for the Harmonic Oscillator on
\mathbb{R}
)

We consider the one-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator given by the semiclassical operator:

P_{0}(x,hD_{x})=\frac{1}{2}\,\left(x^{2}+(hD_{x})^{2}\right)

where
h
is the semiclassical parameter. The associated classical Hamiltonian (principal symbol) is:

p_{0}(x,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\,(x^{2}+\xi^{2})

In the phase space
(x,\xi)
, the energy surface
\{p_{0}(x,\xi)=E\}
is a circle of radius
\sqrt{2E}
. The classical motion along this orbit is described by:

x(t)=\sqrt{2E}\,\cos(t),\quad\xi(t)=-\sqrt{2E}\,\sin(t),

which is a closed trajectory with period
T=2\pi
.
To apply the Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization rule, we compute the classical action
S_{0}(E)
, which is the integral of the momentum along the closed orbit
\gamma_{E}
at energy
E
:

S_{0}(E)=\oint_{\gamma_{E}}\xi\,dx.

Using the parametrization of the orbit, we compute:

\displaystyle S_{0}(E)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\xi(t)\,\dot{x}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(-\sqrt{2E}\sin t\right)\cdot\left(-\sqrt{2E}\sin t\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=2E\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin^{2}t\,dt

\displaystyle=2E\cdot\pi=2\pi E.

The Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition, including the Maslov correction, reads:

S_{0}(E)+h\,S_{1}(E)=2\pi hn,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

For the harmonic oscillator, the Maslov index is
\mu=2
, which gives:

S_{1}(E)=-\frac{\pi}{2}.

This leads to the equation:

2\pi E-\pi h=2\pi hn\quad\Rightarrow\quad E=h\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right).

Therefore, the quantized energy levels of the harmonic oscillator are:

E_{n}=h\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right),\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots

These are exactly the energy levels of the quantum harmonic oscillator.

2
The microlocal Wronskian

The best algebraic and microlocal framework for computing 1-D quantization rules in the self-adjoint case, developed in the fundamental works of
[
32
]
,
[
15
]
, is based on Fredholm theory and the classical
positive commutator method
, which involves conservation of a quantity called
quantum flux
.

Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization rules are derived by constructing quasi-modes using the WKB approximation along a closed Lagrangian manifold
\Lambda_{E}\subset\{p_{0}=E\}
, i.e. a periodic orbit of the Hamiltonian vector field
H_{p}
with energy
E
. This construction is local and depends on the rank of the projection
\Lambda_{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{x}
.

Thus, the set
K_{h}(E)
of microlocal solutions to
(P-E)u=0
along
\Lambda_{E}
can be seen as a bundle over
\mathbb{R}
with a compact base, corresponding to the classically allowed region at energy
E
. The eigenvalues
E_{n}(h)
are then determined by the condition that the global quasi-mode obtained by gluing local WKB solutions along
\Lambda_{E}
is singlevalued, i.e. that
K_{h}(E)
has trivial holonomy.

Assuming
\Lambda_{E}
is smoothly embedded in
T^{*}\mathbb{R}
, it can always be parametrized by a non-degenerate phase function. Of particular interest are the
focal points
, i.e. critical points of the phase functions, which are responsible for the change in Maslov index. A point
a_{E}=(x_{E},\xi_{E})\in\Lambda_{E}
is a focal point if
\Lambda_{E}
”turns vertical” at
a_{E}
, meaning that the tangent space
T_{a_{E}}\Lambda_{E}
is no longer transverse to the fiber
x=\text{const}
. in
T^{*}\mathbb{R}
.

In any case however,
\Lambda_{E}
can locally be parametrized either by a phase function
S(x)
(spatial representation) or
\widetilde{S}(\xi)
(Fourier representation). We fix an orientation on
\Lambda_{E}
and for any point
a\in\Lambda_{E}
(not necessarily a focal point), we denote by
\rho=\pm 1
the oriented segments near
a
. Let
\chi^{a}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a smooth cut-off function equal to 1 near
a
, and
\omega^{a}_{\rho}
a small neighborhood of
\operatorname{supp}[P,\chi^{a}]\cap\Lambda_{E}
near
\rho
. Here,
\chi^{a}
holds for
\chi^{a}(x,hD_{x})
as in (
1.1
), and we shall equally write
P(x,hD_{x})
in spatial representation, or
P(-hD_{\xi},\xi)
in Fourier representation.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
P
be self-adjoint and
u_{a},v_{a}\in K_{h}(E)
be microlocal solutions supported on
\Lambda_{E}
. We define the
microlocal Wronskian
of
(u^{a},\overline{v^{a}})
near
a
in
\omega^{a}_{\rho}
as

\mathcal{W}^{a}_{\rho}(u^{a},\overline{v^{a}})=\big(\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]_{\rho}u^{a}|v^{a}\big)

(2.1)

where
\frac{i}{h}[P,\chi^{a}]_{\rho}
denotes the part of the commutator supported microlocally on
\omega^{a}_{\rho}
.

To clarify the meaning of this definition, consider the Schrödinger operator
P(x,hD_{x})=(hD_{x})^{2}+V(x)
, with
x_{E}=0
, and take
\chi
to be the Heaviside step function
\chi(x)
. Then, in the distributional sense,

\frac{i}{h}[P,\chi]=-i\,h\,\chi^{\prime\prime}+2\,\chi^{\prime}\,hD_{x}=-i\,h\,\delta^{\prime}+2\,\delta\,hD_{x},

so that
\big(\displaystyle{i\over h}[P,\chi]u|u\big)=-ih\big(u^{\prime}(0)\overline{u(0)}-u(0)\overline{u^{\prime}(0)}\big)

which is the usual Wronskian of
(u,\overline{u})
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u^{a},v^{a}\in K_{h}(E)
, and denote by
\widehat{u}
the
h
-Fourier (unitary) transform of
u
. Then:

\mathcal{W}=\big(\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]u^{a}|v^{a}\big)=\big(\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]\widehat{u}^{a}|\widehat{v}^{a}\big)=0

(2.2)

and

\mathcal{W}^{a}_{+}(u^{a},\overline{v^{a}})=-\mathcal{W}^{a}_{-}(u^{a},\overline{v^{a}})

(2.3)

(
all equalities being understood mod
\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})
, resp
\mathcal{O}(h^{N+1})
when considering
u^{a},v^{a}\in K_{h}(E)\big)
. Moreover,
\mathcal{W}^{a}_{\rho}(u^{a},\overline{v^{a}})
does not depend modulo
\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})

(
resp.
\mathcal{O}(h^{N+1})
)
on the choice of
\chi^{a}
above.

Proof.

Since
u^{a},v^{a}\in K_{h}(E)
are distributions in
L^{2}
, the first equality (
2.2
) follows from the Plancherel formula and the regularity of microlocal solutions in
L^{2}
,
p+i
being elliptic. If
a
is not a focal point,
u^{a},v^{a}
are smooth WKB solutions near
a
, so we can expand the commutator in
\mathcal{W}=\big(\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]u^{a}|v^{a}\big)
and use that
P
is self-adjoint to show that
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})
. If
a
is a focal point,
u^{a},v^{a}
are smooth WKB solutions in Fourier representation, so again
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})
. Then (
2.3
) follows from Definition
2.1
.
∎

3
Second-order BS quantization for a self-adjoint 1-D
h
-PDO

We apply the method of the microlocal Wronskian and Gram matrix,to derive BS quantization conditions at order 2 for a
h
-PDO of the type (
1.1
). To simplify, we assume that the principal symbol
p_{0}
contains only two focal points along the classical orbit
\gamma_{E}
, but it is clear that by matching together microlocal solutions, the result does not depend on this simplification.

In fact, BS depend on the geometry of
\gamma_{E}
only through its Maslov index, which equals 2 when
\gamma_{E}
is a smooth embedded Lagrangian submanifold. The case where
\gamma_{E}
is not a submanifold (for example, homeomorphic to the figure-eight) and has Maslov index 0, is not considered here (see
[
32
]
or
[
8
]
). (Recall that in dimension 1, the Maslov index, defined modulo 4, is an even number, hence either 0 or 2.)

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
P(x,hD_{x};h)
be a self-adjoint
h
-PDO, given as the Weyl quantization of a real classical symbol

p(x,\xi;h)\sim p_{0}(x,\xi)+h\,p_{1}(x,\xi)+h^{2}\,p_{2}(x,\xi)+\cdots

Assume that the geometry of
p_{0}
satisfies the hypothesis (H) of Section
1
, and that
E_{+}<E_{0}=\displaystyle\liminf_{|x,\xi|\rightarrow+\infty}p_{0}(x,\xi)
. Then the spectrum of
P
in a fixed energy interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is discrete, and given by the BS quantization condition:

S_{h}(E):=S_{0}(E)+h\,S_{1}(E)+h^{2}\,S_{2}(E)+\cdots=2\pi nh,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}

where:

•

S_{0}(E)=\displaystyle\oint_{\gamma_{E}}\xi(x)\,dx=\displaystyle\int\int_{\{p_{0}\leq E\}\cap W}\,d\xi\wedge\,dx
is the
classical action
along the closed orbit
\gamma_{E}\subset\{p_{0}=E\}
;

•

S_{1}(E)=-\pi-\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{E}}p_{1}\big(x(t),\xi(t)\big)\,dt
is the
first-order correction
, including the Maslov index and the integral of the subprincipal 1-form
p_{1}\,dt
;

•

S_{2}(E)
is the
second-order correction
, given by

S_{2}(E)={1\over 24}\,{d\over dE}\int_{\gamma_{E}}\Delta\,dt-\int_{\gamma_{E}}p_{2}\,dt-{1\over 2}\,{d\over dE}\int_{\gamma_{E}}p_{1}^{2}\,dt

with

\Delta(x,\xi)=\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial x^{2}}\,\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{2}}-\big(\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial x\,\partial\xi}\big)^{2},

and
(x(t),\xi(t))
is a parametrization of
\gamma_{E}
by the Hamiltonian flow.

Let us note that the deformation quantization method (see
[
7
]
), easily recovers this result, as well as higher-order terms, in particular
S_{4}(E)
(see
[
30
]
and
[
14
]
for a diagrammatic approach). Recall that all odd-order terms
S_{j}(E)
with
j\geq 3
vanish.

3.1
Quasi-modes mod
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
in Fourier representation

We first recall Hörmander’s asymptotic stationary phase theorem (see e.g.
[
17
]
, Theorem 7.7.5):

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a function such that
\mathrm{Im}\big(\varphi(x)\big)\geq 0
, and suppose that
\varphi
has a non-degenerate critical point at
x_{0}
. Then, we have the asymptotic expansion:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\varphi(x)}\,u(x)\,dx\sim e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\varphi(x_{0})}\,\bigg(\det\big(\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})}{2\,i\,\pi\,h}\big)\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\sum_{j}h^{j}\,L_{j}u(x_{0})

(3.1)

where
L_{j}
are linear differential operators, with
L_{0}u(x_{0})=u(x_{0})
and in particular:

L_{1}u(x_{0})=\sum_{n=0}^{2}\frac{2^{-(n+1)}}{i\,n!\,(n+1)!}\,\big<\big(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})\big)^{-1}\,D_{x},D_{x}\big>^{n+1}\,(\Phi_{x_{0}}^{n}\,u)(x_{0})

(3.2)

with:

\Phi_{x_{0}}(x)=\varphi(x)-\varphi(x_{0})-\frac{1}{2}\,\big<\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x_{0}).\,(x-x_{0}),x-x_{0}\big>

(3.3)

We note that
\Phi_{x_{0}}
vanishes to order 3 at
x_{0}

(
i.e.,
\Phi_{x_{0}}(x_{0})=0,\;\Phi^{\prime}_{x_{0}}(x_{0})=0,\;\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{x_{0}}(x_{0})=0
)
.

In the sequel, we present formulas with accuracy up to the second order in
h
. It is helpful to start building the quasi-modes from a focal point, because this gives both outgoing and incoming approximate solutions at the same time.

For
E\in I
, let
a_{E}=(x_{E},\xi_{E})\in\gamma_{E}
be such that
\left(\displaystyle\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial\xi}\right)(a_{E})=0
(i.e.,
a_{E}
is a focal point). Since
\left(\displaystyle\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial x}\right)(a_{E})\neq 0
, the orbit
\gamma_{E}
can be locally parametrized near
a_{E}
using a phase function
\psi(\xi)=\psi(\xi;E)
, which satisfies the Hamilton-Jacobi equation:

p_{0}\big(-\psi^{\prime}(\xi),\xi\big)=E

(3.4)

and is normalized by
\psi(\xi_{E})=0
. We then look for an asymptotic solution of
\big(P(x,hD_{x};h)-E\big)u(x;h)=0
of the form

u(x;h)=(2\pi h)^{-1/2}\,\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\,x\xi}\,\hat{u}(\xi;h)\,d\xi=(2\pi h)^{-1/2}\,\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\big(x\xi+\psi(\xi)\big)}\,b(\xi;h)\,d\xi

(3.5)

where
\psi,\,b
depend also on
E
. We aim to compute:

Pu(x;h)=(2\pi h)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,\int\int\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\big(\,(x-y)\,\eta+y\,\xi+\psi(\xi)\,\big)}\,p\big(\frac{x+y}{2},\eta;h\big)\,b(\xi;h)\,d\xi\,dy\,d\eta

(3.6)

In (
3.6
), we integrate with respect to the variables
(y,\eta)
. For fixed
\xi
, the phase is:

(y,\eta)\mapsto(x-y)\,\eta+y\,\xi

with critical point
(y_{c},\eta_{c})=(x,\xi)
. Let us set the change of variables:

y-x=2\,y^{\prime},\quad\eta-\xi=\eta^{\prime}

The Jacobian of this transformation is 2, so:

Pu(x;h)=2\,(2\,\pi\,h)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,\int\int\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\big(-2\,y^{\prime}\,\eta^{\prime}+x\,\xi+\psi(\xi)\big)}\,p\big(x+y^{\prime},\xi+\eta^{\prime};h\big)\,b(\xi;h)\,d\xi\,dy^{\prime}\,d\eta^{\prime}

(3.7)

Now, for fixed
\xi
, applying the stationary phase formula to the variables
(y^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
gives:

\displaystyle\int\int e^{-\frac{i}{h}2y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}p(x+y^{\prime},\xi+\eta^{\prime};h)\,dy^{\prime}\,d\eta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\sim\pi h\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{j!(2/h)^{j}i^{j}}\big(\partial^{j}_{y^{\prime}}\partial^{j}_{\eta^{\prime}}p(x+y^{\prime},\xi+\eta^{\prime};h)\big)_{(y^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})=(0,0)}\

\displaystyle\sim\pi h\big(p(x,\xi;h)+\frac{h}{2i}\frac{\partial^{2}p}{\partial x\partial\xi}(x,\xi;h)-\frac{h^{2}}{8}\frac{\partial^{4}p}{\partial x^{2}\partial\xi^{2}}(x,\xi;h)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3})\big)

Hence:

\displaystyle Pu(x;h)

\displaystyle=(2\pi h)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\big(x\,\xi+\psi(\xi)\big)}b(\xi;h)\big(p(x,\xi;h)+\frac{h}{2i}\frac{\partial^{2}p}{\partial x\,\partial\xi}(x,\xi;h)-\frac{h^{2}}{8}\frac{\partial^{4}p}{\partial x^{2}\partial\xi^{2}}(x,\xi;h)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3})\big)\,d\xi\

\displaystyle=(2\pi h)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\big(x\,\xi+\psi(\xi)\big)}b(\xi;h)\,\big(p_{0}(x,\xi)+h\tilde{p}_{1}(x,\xi)+h^{2}\tilde{p}_{2}(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3})\big)\,d\xi

where

\tilde{p}_{1}(x,\xi)=p_{1}(x,\xi)+\frac{1}{2i}\,\frac{\partial^{2}\,p_{0}}{\partial x\,\partial\xi}(x,\xi)

(3.8)

and

\tilde{p}_{2}(x,\xi)=p_{2}(x,\xi)+\frac{1}{2i}\,\frac{\partial^{2}\,p_{1}}{\partial x\,\partial\xi}(x,\xi)-\frac{1}{8}\,\frac{\partial^{4}\,p_{0}}{\partial x^{2}\,\partial\xi^{2}}(x,\xi)

(3.9)

Following
(
[
6
]
, the Maslov Ansatz and Theorem 43
)
, we look for
b(\xi;h)\sim b_{0}(\xi)+h\,b_{1}(\xi)+\cdots
, a classical elliptic symbol, with
b_{0}(0)\neq 0
, such that there exists a symbol
a(x,\xi;h)\sim a_{0}(x,\xi)+h\,a_{1}(x,\xi)+\cdots
satisfying:

h\,D_{\xi}\big(e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\big(x\,\xi+\psi(\xi)\big)}\,a(x,\xi;h)\big)=e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\big(x\,\xi+\psi(\xi)\big)}\,b(\xi;h)\,\big(p_{0}(x,\xi)-E+h\,\tilde{p}_{1}(x,\xi)+h^{2}\,\tilde{p}_{2}(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3})\big)

(3.10)

or more explicitly

\big(x+\psi^{\prime}(\xi)\big)\,a(x,\xi;h)+hD_{\xi}a(x,\xi;h)=b(\xi;h)\,\big(p_{0}(x,\xi)-E+h\,\tilde{p}_{1}(x,\xi)+h^{2}\,\tilde{p}_{2}(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3})\big)

(3.11)

In order to solve (
3.11
) for
\xi
near
\xi_{E}
,
x
near
x_{E}
,
E
near
E_{0}
, it is sufficient to solve the sequence of equations,

3.2
Normalisation

We compute the microlocal Wronskian of
(u^{a},\overline{u^{a}})=(u,\overline{u})
in
\omega^{a}_{\rho}
. Our goal is to normalize the microlocal solution

\hat{u}(\xi;h)=e^{\frac{i}{h}\psi(\xi)}\,b(\xi;h)

using the microlocal Wronskian. That is, we seek constants
C_{0}
and
C_{1}=C_{1}(a_{E})
such that

\mathcal{W}^{a}(\hat{u},\overline{\hat{u}})=1+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}).

In the Fourier representation, we write:

\mathcal{W}^{a}_{\rho}(\hat{u},\overline{\hat{u}})=\displaystyle\big(\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]_{\rho}\,\hat{u}|\hat{u}\big)

where
\chi^{a}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a smooth cut-off equal to 1 near the focal point
a=a_{E}
. Without loss of generality, we can take
\chi^{a}(x,\xi)=\chi_{1}(x)\,\chi_{2}(\xi)
, with
\chi_{2}\equiv 1
on small neighborhoods
\omega^{a}_{\pm}
, of
\mathrm{supp}\big(\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]\big)\cap\{p_{0}(x,\xi)=E\}
in the region
\pm(\xi-\xi_{E})>0
. Therefore, it is sufficient to consider variations of the function
\chi_{1}(x)
only.

In general, if
P
and
Q
are two
h
-pseudodifferential operators, whose Weyl symbols admit the expansions

\sigma^{W}(P)(x,\xi;h)=p_{0}(x,\xi)+h\,p_{1}(x,\xi)+h^{2}\,p_{2}(x,\xi)+\cdots

\sigma^{W}(Q)(x,\xi;h)=q_{0}(x,\xi)+h\,q_{1}(x,\xi)+h^{2}\,q_{2}(x,\xi)+\cdots

then we have

\sigma^{W}\big(\frac{i}{h}[P,Q]\big)=\{\sigma^{W}(P),\sigma^{W}(Q)\}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})

where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
denotes the Poisson bracket. In particular,

\sigma^{W}\big(\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]\big)(x,\xi;h):=c(x,\xi;h)=\big(\partial_{\xi}\,p_{0}(x,\xi)+h\,\partial_{\xi}\,p_{1}(x,\xi)\big)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})

Using the Weyl calculus, the operator acts in Fourier representation as

c^{w}(-h\,D_{\xi},\xi;h)\,v(\xi;h)=(2\,\pi\,h)^{-1}\,\int\int e^{-\frac{i}{h}\,(\xi-\eta)\,y}\,c\big(y,\frac{\xi+\eta}{2};h\big)\,v(\eta;h)\,dy\,d\eta

Hence, applying to
\hat{u}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]\,\hat{u}(\xi;h)=(2\,\pi\,h)^{-1}\,\int\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\big(\psi(\eta)-(\xi-\eta)\,y\big)}\,c\big(y,\frac{\xi+\eta}{2};h\big)\,b(\eta;h)\,dy\,d\eta

For fixed
\xi
, the phase function corresponding to the oscillatory integral defining
\displaystyle\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]\,\hat{u}
is given by

\varphi_{\xi}(y,\eta)=\psi(\eta)-(\xi-\eta)\,y

The critical points of
\varphi_{\xi}
are

\big(y_{c}(\xi),\eta_{c}(\xi)\big)=\big(-\psi^{\prime}(\xi),\xi\big),

and therefore, the corresponding critical values of
\varphi_{\xi}
are

\varphi_{\xi}\big(y_{c}(\xi),\eta_{c}(\xi)\big)=\varphi_{\xi}\big(-\psi^{\prime}(\xi),\xi\big)=\psi(\xi)

A direct computation shows that

\big(\mathrm{Hess}\,\varphi_{\xi}\big)\big(y_{c}(\xi),\eta_{c}(\xi)\big)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\\
\end{array}\right)

Let
c_{j}(y,\eta):=\partial_{\eta}p_{j}(y,\eta)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(y)
, for all

. By the stationary phase theorem (
3.1
), we obtain:

\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]\,\hat{u}(\xi;h)=e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\psi(\xi)}\,\big(d_{0}(\xi)+h\,d_{1}(\xi)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\big)

with

and

d_{1}(\xi)=c_{0}\big(-\psi^{\prime}(\xi),\xi\big)\,b_{1}(\xi)+c_{1}\big(-\psi^{\prime}(\xi),\xi\big)\,b_{0}(\xi)+\frac{i}{2}\,J(\xi)

where

and where we have set

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{a}_{+}(\hat{u},\overline{\hat{u}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\xi_{E}}^{+\infty}d_{0}(\xi)\,\overline{b_{0}(\xi)}\,d\xi+h\,\int_{\xi_{E}}^{+\infty}\big(d_{0}(\xi)\,\overline{b_{1}(\xi)}+d_{1}(\xi)\,\overline{b_{0}(\xi)}\big)\,d\xi+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\

First, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}^{+}

\displaystyle=\int_{\xi_{E}}^{+\infty}\partial_{\xi}p_{0}\big(-\psi^{\prime}(\xi),\xi\big)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}\big(-\psi^{\prime}(\xi)\big)\,|b_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\

The next step is to compute

A few lines of calculations show that

\displaystyle d_{0}\,\overline{b_{1}}+d_{1}\,\overline{b_{0}}

By Hamilton equations

is the parametrization of
\gamma_{E}
by the time evolution. The form

is called sub-principal 1-form. Next we consider
D_{1}(\xi)
as the integral over
\gamma_{E}
of the 1-form
\Omega_{1}(\xi)
, defined near
\xi_{E}
in Fourier representation by the expression (
3.33
). Using WKB construction,
\Omega_{1}(\xi)
can also be extended to the spatial representation. Since
\gamma_{E}
is Lagrangian,
\Omega_{1}(\xi)
is clearly closed on
\gamma_{E}
; our goal is to compute it modulo exact forms. Using integration by parts in (
3.33
), together with the condition
D_{1}(\xi_{E})=0
, we derive the following relations

c(x,\xi;h)=\big(\partial_{\xi}\,p_{0}(x,\xi)+h\,\partial_{\xi}\,p_{1}(x,\xi)\big)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})=c_{0}(x,\xi)+h\,c_{1}(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})

Let:

B^{a}_{\pm}:=\frac{i}{h}[P,\chi^{a}]_{\pm}u^{a}_{\pm}

(3.74)

so:

B^{a}_{\pm}(x;h)=\frac{1}{2\pi h}\,\int\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\,(x-y)\,\eta}\,c\big(\frac{x+y}{2},\eta;h\big)\,u^{a}_{\pm}(y;h)\,dy\,d\eta

For fixed
x
, the phase of the oscillatory integral defining
F^{a}_{\pm}(x;h)
is:

\phi_{x}^{\pm}(y,\eta)=(x-y)\,\eta+\varphi_{\pm}(y).

Its critical points are:

(y_{c}(x),\eta_{c}^{\pm}(x))=\big(x,\xi_{\pm}(x)\big),

and the corresponding critical values are:

\phi_{x}^{\pm}\big(y_{c}(x),\eta_{c}^{\pm}(x)\big)=\varphi_{\pm}(x).

A direct calculation shows that the Hessian matrix is:

\big(\mathrm{Hess}\,\phi^{\pm}_{x}\big)(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\xi^{\prime}_{\pm}(x)&-1\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}\right)

We define:

u_{x}^{\pm}(y,\eta;h)=c(\frac{x+y}{2},\eta;h)\,\big(\pm\,\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(y,\xi_{\pm}(y))\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\exp\big[-i\,\int_{x_{E}}^{y}\frac{p_{1}(z,\xi_{\pm}(z))}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(z,\xi_{\pm}(z))}\,dz\big]\,\big(1+h\,Z(\xi_{\pm}(y))+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\big)

where:

Z(\xi_{\pm}(y))=-\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{x}\big(\frac{p_{1}}{\partial_{x}p_{0}}\big)(y,\xi_{\pm}(y))+i\,\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{Im}\big(D_{1}(\xi_{\pm}(y))\big)+D_{2}(\xi_{\pm}(y))

Thus, the leading term of
u_{x}^{\pm}(y,\eta;h)
is:

u_{x}^{(0,\pm)}(y,\eta)=c_{0}(\frac{x+y}{2},\eta)\,\big(\pm\,\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(y,\xi_{\pm}(y))\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\exp\big[-i\,\int_{x_{E}}^{y}\frac{p_{1}(z,\xi_{\pm}(z))}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(z,\xi_{\pm}(z))}\,dz\big]=c_{0}(\frac{x+y}{2},\eta)\,v_{\pm}(y).

By the stationary phase theorem (
3.1
), we obtain:

B^{a}_{\pm}(x;h)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,e^{\pm i\,\frac{\pi}{4}}\,e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\varphi_{\pm}(x)}\,\bigg(u_{x}^{\pm}\big(x,\xi_{\pm}(x);h\big)+h\,L_{1}u_{x}^{(0,\pm)}\big(x,\xi_{\pm}(x)\big)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\bigg)

where:

L_{1}u_{x}^{(0,\pm)}\big(x,\xi_{\pm}(x)\big)=\sum_{n=0}^{2}\frac{2^{-(n+1)}}{i\,n!\,(n+1)!}\,\big(2\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y\,\partial\eta}+\xi^{\prime}_{\pm}(x)\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta^{2}}\big)^{n+1}(\psi^{n}_{x}\,u^{(0,\pm)}_{x})(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))

(3.75)

and

\psi_{x}(y,\eta)=(x-y)\,\xi_{\pm}(x)+\varphi_{\pm}(y)-\varphi_{\pm}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\,\xi^{\prime}_{\pm}(x)\,(y-x)^{2}=\mathcal{O}\big((y-x)^{3}\big)

(3.76)

A few calculations show that:

\big(2\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y\,\partial\eta}+\xi^{\prime}_{\pm}(x)\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta^{2}}\big)u^{(0,\pm)}_{x}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))=2\,v^{\prime}_{\pm}(x)\,s_{\pm}(x)+v_{\pm}(x)\,s^{\prime}_{\pm}(x),

where:

s_{\pm}(x)=(\frac{\partial c_{0}}{\partial\xi})(x,\xi_{\pm}(x)).

Moreover:

\big(2\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y\,\partial\eta}+\xi^{\prime}_{\pm}(x)\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta^{2}}\big)^{n+1}(\psi^{n}_{x}\,u^{(0,\pm)}_{x})(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))=0,\quad\forall n\in\{1,2\}.

It is easy to see that:

v^{\prime}_{\pm}(x)=\theta_{\pm}(x)\,v_{\pm}(x),

where:

\theta_{\pm}(x)=-\frac{1}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{\pm}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{\pm}(x))}\,\bigg(i\,p_{1}\big(x,\xi_{\pm}(x)\big)-\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi_{\pm}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{\pm}(x))+\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{\pm}(x))\,\alpha^{\prime}(\xi_{\pm}(x))}{2\,\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{\pm}(x))}\bigg)

and:

c_{0}\big(x,\xi_{\pm}(x)\big)\,\big(\pm\,\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\pm\,\big(\pm\,\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)

Consequently:

\displaystyle B^{a}_{\pm}(x;h)

\displaystyle=\pm\,2^{-1/2}\,e^{\pm i\,\frac{\pi}{4}}\,\exp\bigg[\frac{i}{h}\,\bigg(\varphi_{\pm}(x)-h\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{p_{1}\big(y,\xi_{\pm}(y)\big)}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}\big(y,\xi_{\pm}(y)\big)}\,dy\bigg)\,\bigg]\,\big(\pm\,\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\

\displaystyle\times\bigg(1+h\,Z(\xi_{\pm}(x))+h\,\frac{c_{1}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))}{c_{0}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))}+\big(\frac{2\,s_{\pm}(x)\,\theta_{\pm}(x)+s^{\prime}_{\pm}(x)}{2\,i\,c_{0}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))}\big)\,h+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\bigg).

Next, observing that:

s_{\pm}(x)=(\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{2\,}})(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)=\omega_{\pm}(x)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x),

and that:

\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))=\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{\pm}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{\pm}(x)),

we obtain:

\displaystyle B^{a}_{\pm}(x;h)

\displaystyle=\pm\,2^{-1/2}\,e^{\pm i\,\frac{\pi}{4}}\,\exp\bigg[\frac{i}{h}\,\bigg(\varphi_{\pm}(x)-h\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{p_{1}\big(y,\xi_{\pm}(y)\big)}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}\big(y,\xi_{\pm}(y)\big)}\,dy\bigg)\,\bigg]\,\big(\pm\,\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\

(3.77)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(1+h\,Z(\xi_{\pm}(x))+h\,\frac{\partial_{\xi}p_{1}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))}-\frac{i\,h\,\omega_{\pm}(x)\,\theta_{\pm}(x)}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))}-\frac{i\,h}{2}\,\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\big(\omega_{\pm}(x)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\big)}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}(x,\xi_{\pm}(x))\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\bigg).

This gives:

\displaystyle(u^{a}_{+}|B^{a}_{+})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{x_{E}}^{+\infty}\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\,dx+\frac{h}{2}\,\int_{x_{E}}^{+\infty}\bigg(2\,\mathrm{Re}\big(Z(\xi_{+}(x))\big)+\frac{\partial_{\xi}p_{1}(x,\xi_{+}(x))}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))}+\frac{i\,\omega_{+}(x)\,\overline{\theta_{+}(x)}}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))}\bigg)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\,dx\

\displaystyle+\frac{i\,h}{4}\,\int_{x_{E}}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))}\,\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\big(\omega_{+}(x)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\big)\,dx+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{h}{2}\,K_{1}+\frac{i\,h}{4}\,K_{2}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}).

A simple calculation shows that:

2\,\mathrm{Re}\big(Z(\xi_{+}(x))\big)+\frac{\partial_{\xi}p_{1}(x,\xi_{+}(x))}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))}+\frac{i\,\omega_{+}(x)\,\overline{\theta_{+}(x)}}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))}=\frac{\omega_{+}(x)}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))}\,\bigg(i\,\overline{\theta_{+}(x)}+\frac{p_{1}(x,\xi_{+}(x))}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))}\bigg)

=\frac{i\,\omega_{+}(x)}{2\,\big(\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\big)^{3}\,\big(\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))\big)^{2}}\,\bigg(\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))+\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha^{\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\bigg).

Hence:

K_{1}=\frac{i}{2}\,\int_{x_{E}}^{+\infty}\frac{\omega_{+}(x)}{\big(\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\big)^{3}\,\big(\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))\big)^{2}}\,\bigg(\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))+\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha^{\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\bigg)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\,dx.

Here, we used the fact that:

\omega_{+}(x):=(\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{2}})(x,\xi_{+}(x))=\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))+2\,\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha^{\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))+\big(\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\big)^{2}\,(\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial x^{2}})(x,\xi_{+}(x)).

Integrating by parts gives:

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg[\frac{\omega_{+}(x)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))}\bigg]_{x_{E}}^{+\infty}-\int_{x_{E}}^{+\infty}\frac{d}{dx}\big(\frac{1}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))}\big)\,\omega_{+}(x)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\,dx\

\displaystyle=-\int_{x_{E}}^{+\infty}\frac{\omega_{+}(x)}{\big(\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\big)^{3}\,\big(\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))\big)^{2}}\,\bigg(\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha(\xi_{+}(x))+\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\,\alpha^{\prime}(\xi_{+}(x))\bigg)\,\chi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\,dx\

\displaystyle=2\,i\,K_{1}

Thus, we have:

(u^{a}_{+}|B^{a}_{+})=\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}),

and similarly:

(u^{a}_{-}|B^{a}_{-})=-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}),

Consequently:

(u^{a}|B^{a}_{+}-B^{a}_{-})=1+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}).

Note that the mixed terms
(u^{a}_{\pm}|B^{a}_{\mp})
are
\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})
because the phase is non-stationary.
∎

3.5
WKB solutions mod
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
in the spatial representation

We begin by constructing the WKB solutions
u^{a}_{\rho}(x;h)=u^{a}_{\pm}(x;h)
starting from the focal point
a=a_{E}
. These solutions are uniformly valid with respect to
h
for
x
in any interval
I\subset\subset]x^{\prime}_{E},x_{E}[
. The solutions take the form:

u^{a}_{\rho}(x;h)=a_{\rho}(x;h)\,e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\varphi_{\rho}(x)},

(3.78)

where
a_{\rho}(x;h)
is a formal series in
h
, which we shall compute with
h^{2}
accuracy

a_{\rho}(x;h)=a_{\rho,0}(x)+h\,a_{\rho,1}(x)+h^{2}\,a_{\rho,2}(x)+\cdots.

The phase
\varphi_{\rho}(x)
is a real smooth function that satisfies the eikonal equation

p_{0}\big(x,\varphi^{\prime}_{\rho}(x)\big)=E.

(3.79)

For simplicity we shall omit indices
\rho=\pm
whenever no confusion may occur. Let
Q(x,hD_{x};h)=e^{-\frac{i}{h}\,\varphi(x)}P(x,hD_{x})e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\varphi(x)}
, which is an
h
-pseudo-differential operator. Its action on
a(x;h)
is given by:

(Q-E)a(x;h)=(2\pi h)^{-1}\,\int\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\,(x-y)\,\theta}\,p\big(\frac{x+y}{2},\theta+F(x,y);h\big)\,a(y;h)\,dy\,d\theta,

where
F(x,y)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\varphi^{\prime}\big(x+t(y-x)\big)\,dt
. Applying stationary phase theorem (
5.10
) at order 2 (see Appendix), we find modulo
\mathcal{O}(h^{3})
:

\displaystyle\big(Q(x,hD_{x};h)-E\big)a(x;h)=\Big(p\big(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x);h\big)-E\Big)\,a(x;h)+\frac{h}{i}\,\Big(\beta(x;h)\,\partial_{x}a(x;h)+\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{x}\beta(x;h)\,a(x;h)\Big)

(3.80)

\displaystyle-h^{2}\,\Big(\frac{1}{8}\,\partial_{x}r(x;h)\,a(x;h)+\frac{1}{8}\,\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x)\,\partial_{x}\theta(x;h)\,a(x;h)+\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{x}\gamma(x;h)\,\partial_{x}a(x;h)+\frac{1}{2}\,\gamma(x;h)\,\frac{\partial^{2}a(x;h)}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{1}{6}\,\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)\,\theta(x;h)\,a(x;h)\Big).

Suppose now that
p(x,\xi;h)
is real,
p_{0}(x_{E},\xi_{E})=E
,
(\displaystyle\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial\xi})(x_{E},\xi_{E})\neq 0
. We look for formal solutions
(
i.e in the sense of formal classical symbols
)
of

\big(P(x,h\,D_{x};h)-E\big)\big(a(x;h)\,e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\varphi(x)}\big)=0\Leftrightarrow\big(Q(x,h\,D_{x};h)-E\big)a(x;h)=0.

(3.81)

Once the eikonal equation (
3.79
) is satisfied, the first transport equation is obtained by setting the
\mathcal{O}(h)
term in (
3.80
) to zero:

\beta_{0}(x)\,a^{\prime}_{0}(x)+\Big(i\,p_{1}\big(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x)\big)+\frac{1}{2}\,\beta_{0}^{\prime}(x)\Big)\,a_{0}(x)=0.

(3.82)

Its solutions are of the form:

a_{0}(x)=\widetilde{C_{0}}\,|\beta_{0}(x)|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\exp\Big(-i\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{p_{1}\big(y,\varphi^{\prime}(y)\big)}{\beta_{0}(y)}\,dy\Big),

(3.83)

\widetilde{C_{0}}
being so far an arbitrary constant.

Next, setting the
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
term in (
3.80
) to zero yields a differential equation for
a_{1}(x)
:

\displaystyle\beta_{0}(x)\,a^{\prime}_{1}(x)+\Big(i\,p_{1}\big(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x)\big)+\frac{1}{2}\,\beta_{0}^{\prime}(x)\Big)\,a_{1}(x)=-\beta_{1}(x)\,a^{\prime}_{0}(x)-\Big(i\,p_{2}\big(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x)\big)+\frac{1}{2}\,\beta^{\prime}_{1}(x)\Big)\,a_{0}(x)

(3.84)

\displaystyle+i\,\Big(\frac{1}{8}\,r^{\prime}_{0}(x)\,a_{0}(x)+\frac{1}{8}\,\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x)\,\theta^{\prime}_{0}(x)\,a_{0}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\,\gamma^{\prime}_{0}(x)\,a^{\prime}_{0}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\,\gamma_{0}(x)\,a^{\prime\prime}_{0}(x)+\frac{1}{6}\,\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)\,\theta_{0}(x)\,a_{0}(x)\Big).

Here, we have introduced the notations:

\beta_{0}(x)=(\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial\xi})\big(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x)\big),\quad r_{0}(x)=(\frac{\partial^{3}p_{0}}{\partial x\partial\xi^{2}})\big(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x)\big),\quad\gamma_{0}(x)=(\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{2}})\big(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x)\big),\quad\theta_{0}(x)=(\frac{\partial^{3}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{3}})\big(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x)\big).

The homogeneous equation associated with (
3.84
) is the same as (
3.82
); so we are looking for a particular solution of (
3.84
), integrating from
x_{E}
, of the form

a_{1}(x)=\widetilde{D_{1}}(x)\,|\beta_{0}(x)|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\exp\Big(-i\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{p_{1}\big(y,\varphi^{\prime}(y)\big)}{\beta_{0}(y)}\,dy\Big).

(3.85)

Alternatively, we could integrate (
3.84
) from
x^{\prime}_{E}
instead of
x_{E}
. So our main task will consist in computing
\widetilde{D_{1}}(x)
as a multivalued function, due to the presence of the turning points, in the same way we have determined
D_{1}(\xi)
in
[
23
]
(Formula (3.5)), using Fourier representation. We solve (
3.84
) by the method of variation of constants, and find

(\widetilde{C_{0}})^{-1}\,\mathrm{Re}\big(\widetilde{D_{1}}(x)\big)=-\frac{1}{2}\,\Big[\partial_{\xi}(\frac{p_{1}}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}})\big(y,\varphi^{\prime}(y)\big)\Big]_{x_{E}}^{x},

(3.86)

\displaystyle(\widetilde{C_{0}})^{-1}\,\mathrm{Im}\big(\widetilde{D_{1}}(x)\big)=\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}\,\bigg(-p_{2}+\frac{1}{8}\,\frac{\partial^{4}p_{0}}{\partial y^{2}\partial\xi^{2}}+\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{12}\,\frac{\partial^{4}p_{0}}{\partial y\partial\xi^{3}}-\frac{(\varphi^{\prime\prime})^{2}}{24}\,\frac{\partial^{4}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{4}}\bigg)\,dy-\frac{1}{8}\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{(\beta^{\prime}_{0})^{2}}{\beta_{0}^{3}}\,\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\,dy

(3.87)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\,\varphi^{\prime\prime}\,\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{0}}{\beta_{0}^{2}}\,\frac{\partial^{3}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{3}}\,dy+\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{p_{1}}{\beta_{0}^{2}}\,\bigg(\partial_{\xi}p_{1}-\frac{p_{1}}{2\,\beta_{0}}\,\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\bigg)\,dy+\Big[\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{6\,\beta_{0}}\,\frac{\partial^{3}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{3}}-\frac{\beta_{0}^{\prime}}{4\,\beta_{0}^{2}}\,\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\Big]_{x_{E}}^{x}.

Function
\widetilde{D_{1}}(x)
can be normalized by

\widetilde{D_{1}}(x_{E})=0

The general solution of (
3.84
) is then:

a_{1}(x)=\big(\widetilde{C_{1}}+\widetilde{D_{1}}(x)\big)\,|\beta_{0}(x)|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\exp\Big(-i\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{p_{1}\big(y,\varphi^{\prime}(y)\big)}{\beta_{0}(y)}\,dy\Big).

(3.88)

Consequently,

a(x;h)=\Big(\widetilde{C_{0}}+h\,\big(\widetilde{C_{1}}+\widetilde{D_{1}}(x)\big)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\Big)\,|\beta_{0}(x)|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\exp\Big(-i\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{p_{1}\big(y,\varphi^{\prime}(y)\big)}{\beta_{0}(y)}\,dy\Big).

Repeating this construction for the other branch
(\rho=-1)
yields the two branches of WKB solutions:

u^{a}_{\pm}(x;h)=|\beta^{\pm}_{0}(x)|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{\frac{i}{h}\,S_{\pm}(x_{E},x;h)}\,\Big(\widetilde{C_{0}}+h\,\big(\widetilde{C_{1}}+\widetilde{D_{1}}^{\pm}(x)\big)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\Big),

(3.89)

where

S_{\pm}(x_{E},x;h)=\varphi_{\pm}(x_{E})+\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\xi_{\pm}(y)\,dy-h\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{p_{1}\big(y,\varphi^{\prime}_{\pm}(y)\big)}{\beta_{0}^{\pm}(y)}dy,

(3.90)

\beta^{\pm}_{0}(x)=(\partial_{\xi}p_{0})\big(x,\varphi^{\prime}_{\pm}(x)\big).

Here we have used that
\varphi_{\pm}(x)=\varphi_{\pm}(x_{E})+\displaystyle\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\xi_{\pm}(y)\,dy
, with
p_{0}\big(x,\xi_{\pm}(x)\big)=E
.

Normalization with respect to the ”flux norm” consists as above in computing
B^{a}_{\pm}=\displaystyle\frac{i}{h}\,[P,\chi^{a}]_{\pm}u^{a}
by stationary phase theorem (
3.1
) modulo
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
. Assuming already
\widetilde{C_{0}}
,
\widetilde{C_{1}}
to be real, a simple calculation using integration by parts
yields
\widetilde{C_{0}}=C_{0}=2^{-1/2}
, and

\widetilde{C_{1}}=\widetilde{C_{1}}(a_{E})=-2^{-3/2}\,\partial_{\xi}\big(\frac{p_{1}}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}}\big)(a_{E}).

As a result, outside any neighborhood of
x_{E}
, we have

u_{\pm}(x;h)=|\beta_{0}^{\pm}(x)|^{-1/2}\,e^{\frac{i}{h}\,S_{\pm}(x_{E},x;h)}\,\big(\widetilde{C_{0}}+h\,\widetilde{C_{1}}+h\,\widetilde{D_{1}}^{\pm}(x)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\big),

(3.91)

with

S_{\pm}(x_{E},x;h)=\varphi_{\pm}(x_{E})+\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\xi_{\pm}(y)\,dy-h\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x}\frac{p_{1}\big(y,\varphi^{\prime}_{\pm}(y)\big)}{\beta_{0}^{\pm}(y)}\,dy

From (
3.91
) we can recover the homology class of generalized action, considering the superposition
u(x;h)=e^{i\pi/4}\,u_{+}(x;h)+e^{-i\pi/4}\,u_{-}(x;h)
near
a_{E}
. The argument is then similar to that of
[
20
]
, formula (1).

3.6
Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization rule

Recall from (
3.72
) the modified phase function of the microlocal solutions
u^{a}_{\pm}
near the focal point
a_{E}
, accurate to
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
. Similarly, the phase for the asymptotic solution near the other focal point
a^{\prime}_{E}
is given by:

S_{\pm}(x^{\prime}_{E},x;h)=x^{\prime}_{E}\,\xi^{\prime}_{E}+\int_{x^{\prime}_{E}}^{x}\xi_{\pm}(y)\,dy-h\,\int_{x^{\prime}_{E}}^{x}\frac{p_{1}\big(y,\xi_{\pm}(y)\big)}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}\big(y,\xi_{\pm}(y)\big)}\,dy+h^{2}\,\int_{x^{\prime}_{E}}^{x}T_{1}\big(\xi_{\pm}(y)\big)\,\xi^{\prime}_{\pm}(y)\,dy

(3.92)

Now, consider the function
B^{a}_{\pm}(x;h)
with asymptotics (
3.77
), and similarly
B^{a^{\prime}}_{\pm}(x;h)
. The normalized microlocal solutions

u^{a}
and
u^{a^{\prime}}
, extended uniquely along
\gamma_{E}
are denoted
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
. It is then easy to show that

\displaystyle(u_{1}|B^{a^{\prime}}_{+}-B^{a^{\prime}}_{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\,\big(e^{\frac{i}{h}\,A_{-}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)}-e^{\frac{i}{h}\,A_{+}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)}\big)\

(3.93)

\displaystyle(u_{2}|B^{a}_{+}-B^{a}_{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\,\big(e^{-\frac{i}{h}\,A_{-}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)}-e^{-\frac{i}{h}\,A_{+}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)}\big)

modulo
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
. Here, the generalized actions are:

\displaystyle A_{\pm}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)

\displaystyle:=S_{\pm}(x_{E},x;h)-S_{\pm}(x^{\prime}_{E},x;h)\

\displaystyle=x_{E}\,\xi_{E}-x^{\prime}_{E}\,\xi^{\prime}_{E}+\int_{x_{E}}^{x^{\prime}_{E}}\xi_{\pm}(y)\,dy-h\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x^{\prime}_{E}}\frac{p_{1}\big(y,\xi_{\pm}(y)\big)}{\partial_{\xi}p_{0}\big(y,\xi_{\pm}(y)\big)}\,dy+h^{2}\,\int_{x_{E}}^{x^{\prime}_{E}}T_{1}\big(\xi_{\pm}(y)\big)\,\xi^{\prime}_{\pm}(y)\,dy

The Gram matrix
G^{(a,a^{\prime})}(E)
of the solutions
u_{1},u_{2}
in the basis
\big(B^{a}_{+}-B^{a}_{-},B^{a^{\prime}}_{+}-B^{a^{\prime}}_{-}\big)
is given by:

G^{(a,a^{\prime})}(E)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{i}{2}\,\big(e^{-\frac{i}{h}\,A_{-}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)}-e^{-\frac{i}{h}\,A_{+}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)}\big)\\
\frac{i}{2}\,\big(e^{\frac{i}{h}\,A_{-}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)}-e^{\frac{i}{h}\,A_{+}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)}\big)&-1\\
\end{array}\right)

(3.94)

whose determinant is:

-\cos^{2}\big(\frac{A_{-}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)-A_{+}(x_{E},x^{\prime}_{E};h)}{2h}\big)

This determinant vanishes precisely at the eigenvalues of
P
in
I
, leading to the condition modulo
\mathcal{O}(h^{3})

\displaystyle 2\,\pi\,n\,h

(5.6)

with

Appendix. Weyl quantization and conjugation by Fourier integral operators

To fix the ideas we choose the real case (analytic or
C^{\infty}
). To start with, we consider the simple case of conjugation by an elleptic factor. Let
p(x,\xi;h)\sim p_{0}(x,\xi)+h\,p_{1}(x,\xi)+\cdots
be a classical symbol of order 0, defined near
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
\varphi(x)
be a real valued smooth function, defined near
x_{0}
, such that
\varphi^{\prime}(x_{0})=\zeta_{0}
. Let
P(x,hDx;h)=p^{w}(x,hDx;h)
be the Weyl
h
-quantization of
p(x,\xi;h)
. We are then interested in the Weyl symbol of the pseudodifferential operator
Q=e^{-\frac{i}{h}\,\varphi(x)}\circ P\circ e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\varphi(x)}
, which is defined near
(x_{0},\xi_{0}-\zeta_{0})
. We proceed formally, by first writing the integral kernel of
Q
as,

K_{Q}(x,y)=\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\,\big((x-y)\,\theta+\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)\big)}\,p\big(\frac{x+y}{2},\theta;h\big)\,\widetilde{d\theta},\quad\widetilde{d\theta}=(2\,\pi\,h)^{-1}\,d\theta

(5.7)

It is well known (Kuranishi’s Trick) that

\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)=F(x,y)\,(x-y)

with

F(x,y)=\varphi^{\prime}_{x}(\frac{x+y}{2})+\mathcal{O}(x-y)

The change of variables
\theta\mapsto\theta-F(x,y)
then allows us to write

K_{Q}(x,y)=\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\,(x-y)\,\theta}\,p\big(\frac{x+y}{2},\theta+F(x,y);h\big)\,\widetilde{d\theta}

(5.8)

We have

p\big(\frac{x+y}{2},\theta+F(x,y);h\big)=p\big(\frac{x+y}{2},\theta+\varphi^{\prime}_{x}(\frac{x+y}{2});h\big)+r\big(x,y,\theta\big)\,(x-y)^{2}

where

r(x,y,.)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})

By integrating by parts, we obtain:

\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\,(x-y)\,\theta}\,r(x,y,\theta)\,(x-y)^{2}\,\widetilde{d\theta}=\mathcal{O}(h^{2})

Here the
\mathcal{O}
term in (
5.8
) contributes to the Weyl symbol of
Q
by
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
(i.e., by a classical symbol of order -2). Therefore, if we denote by
q
the Weyl symbol of
Q
, we have:

q(x,\xi;h)=p\big(x,\xi+\varphi^{\prime}(x);h\big)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})

x_{0}

\displaystyle Qa(x;h)

Separable functors and firm modules
Patrik Lundström

Abstract
. We develop a theory of separable ring extensions and separable functors for nonunital rings in the setting of firm modules. We prove nonunital analogues of classical results on functorial separability and semisimplicity, and apply these results to obtain a locally unital version of Maschke’s theorem for group rings.

1.
Introduction

Let
A
be a ring. By this we mean that

A
is associative but not necessarily
unital.
Suppose that
B
is another ring and

f:B\to A
is a ring homomorphism.
In that case, we say that
A
is a

ring extension
of
B

and we indicate this by writing
A/B
.

Recall that
A/B

is said to be
separable

if the multiplication map

\mu:A\otimes_{B}A\to A
, defined by
the additive extension of

\mu(a\otimes a^{\prime})=aa^{\prime}
, for
a,a^{\prime}\in A
,
has a
section
in the category of

A
-bimodules, that is if
there is an
A
-bimodule map

\sigma:A\to A\otimes_{B}A
with

\mu\circ\sigma={\rm id}_{A}
.
Here, the
B
-bimodule structure on
A

is defined via
f
.
Separable ring extensions generalize the classical notion of
separable algebras over fields, which in turn extends
separability of field extensions
(see, e.g.,
[
26
]
and the references therein).

Separable ring extensions have been studied
by numerous authors (see, e.g.,

[
4
,
5
,
8
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
24
]
).
One reason for the sustained interest in these extensions
is that important properties of the ground ring,
such as semisimplicity,
are often inherited by the larger ring.
Perhaps the most classical example of this
is the following result.

Maschke’s theorem
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite group of
order
|G|
. Let
B
be a semisimple
unital ring such that
|G|
is
invertible in
B
.
Then the group ring

A=B[G]
is semisimple.

Classically, this result is proved by a direct
semisimplicity argument
(see, e.g.,
[
14
, Thm. (6.1)]
or
Maschke’s original proof
[
17
]
).
However, separable ring extensions provide a more conceptual explanation of Maschke’s theorem.
In
[
19
]
(see Definition
7
),
Nǎstǎsescu, Van den Bergh and Van Oystaeyen introduce
the notion of a
separable functor
.
Such a functor reflects splittings:
if a morphism or exact sequence splits after applying the functor,
then it already splits beforehand.
In the same article
(
[
19
, Prop. 1.3]
),
they prove the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
f:B\to A
is a ring
homomorphism of unital rings.
Then
A/B
is separable if and only if
the restriction functor

{\rm Res}:{}_{A}{\rm Mod}\to{}_{B}{\rm Mod}
is separable.

Specializing to group rings, by

[
8
, p. 41]
, we have:

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite group
of order
|G|
.
Let
B
be a unital ring such that

|G|
is invertible in
B
.
Let
A
denote the group ring
B[G]
.
Then
A/B
is separable.

By Theorem
1
, separability of

A/B
is equivalent to separability
of the restriction functor

{\rm Res}:{}_{A}{\rm Mod}\to{}_{B}{\rm Mod}
.
Consequently, every exact sequence of
A
-modules
that splits as a sequence of
B
-modules
already splits
A
-linearly.
If
B
is semisimple,
separability of
A/B
then implies that
A
is semisimple,
thereby recovering Maschke’s theorem as a consequence of
separability and functorial splitting.

The rings and modules considered in the classical situation
above are unital. However, many important algebraic objects
are defined as infinite direct sums or arise from local constructions,
and therefore do not in general possess a global unit.
This naturally raises
the following question:

Is it possible to extend the classical
separable functorial machinery from the
unital setting to appropriate categories
of nonunital rings and modules?

To our knowledge, there appear to be very few results on
separability of nonunital ring extensions.
The only references we have found are

[
4
]
, where Brzeziński, Kadison, and Wisbauer
study connections between separability of

A
-rings and
A
-corings,
and
[
3
]
, where
Böhm and Gómez-Torrecillas
study splittings of the comultiplication map
in the context of coalgebras.

Apart from these works, in

[
5
, Proposition 26]

we have obtained a nonunital version of
Theorem
1

under the assumption that

A
and
B
are rings with

enough idempotents
.
Recall from Fuller
[
10
]
that
a ring
A

has enough idempotents if there exists a
set
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
pairwise orthogonal idempotents in
A

such that

A=\oplus_{i\in I}Ae_{i}=\oplus_{i\in I}e_{i}A
.
The module categories considered in

[
5
]

are the categories
{}_{A}{\rm UMod}
of

unitary

left
A
-modules, that is, modules

M
satisfying
AM=M
,
where
A
is assumed to be a ring
with enough idempotents.

In the present article, we extend the
separability result of
[
5
]

to the more general setting of

firm
modules introduced by Quillen
(see Theorem
3
).
Recall that if
A
is a ring and
M
is a
left
A
-module, then
M
is said to
be firm if the multiplication map

A\otimes_{A}M\to M
, induced by the
additive extension of

A\otimes_{A}M\ni(a,m)\mapsto am\in M
,
for
a\in A
and
m\in M
,
is an isomorphism.
We denote the category of such modules by

{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.
We say that a ring homomorphism

f:B\to A
is left firm if
A
,
considered as a left
B
-module via
f
,
is firm.
We prove the following firm version of
Theorem
1
:

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
f:B\to A
is a left firm
ring homomorphism. Then
A/B
is
separable if and only if the
restriction functor

{\rm Res}_{f}:{}_{A}{\rm FMod}\to{}_{B}{\rm FMod}

is separable.

We then apply this result to the study of
semisimple modules in the category

{}_{A}{\rm FMod}

when
A
is a
left s-unital
ring
(see Theorem
4
).
Recall from Tominaga
[
25
]

that a left
A
-module
M
is
said to be s-unital if
for every
m\in M
one has
m\in Am
.
A ring is said to be left s-unital if it is s-unital
as a left module over itself.
We say that a ring homomorphism
f:B\to A
is
left s-unital if
A
, regarded as a left
B
-module via
f
,
is s-unital.
We prove the following s-unital
semisimplicity lifting result:

Theorem 4
.

Let
A
and
B
be left s-unital rings
with
B
left semisimple.
Let
f:B\to A
be a left s-unital
ring homomorphism such that
A/B

is separable.
Then
A
is left semisimple.

This allows us to prove a
locally unital
version of Maschke’s
theorem (see Theorem
6
).
Recall from Ánh and
Márki
[
1
]
that a ring
A

is said to be locally unital if for each
finite subset
X\subseteq A
there exists
an idempotent
e\in A
such that

ex=x=xe
for all
x\in X
.
We first establish the following locally unital
analogue of Theorem
2
:

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite group
of order
|G|
.
Let
B
be a locally
unital ring satisfying
|G|B=B
.
Let
A
denote the group ring
B[G]
.
Then
A/B
is separable.

Combining this with Theorem
4
,
we obtain:

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite group
of order
|G|
.
Let
B
be a semisimple locally
unital ring satisfying
|G|B=B
.
Then the group ring
A=B[G]
is semisimple.

Here is an outline of the article.

In Section
2
,
we fix our categorical conventions and
recall the definition of a separable
functor. We then develop a systematic account of the categorical properties reflected by separable functors. In particular, we show that separability reflects key classes of morphisms, such as monomorphisms, epimorphisms, sections, retractions, and isomorphisms, as well as structural properties of objects, including subobject and quotient simplicity and semisimplicity, and the existence of initial, terminal, and zero objects.
Many of the results in this section are well known or implicit in the literature.
For completeness, and to fix notation and conventions, we include detailed proofs.

In Section
3
, we introduce our conventions on nonunital rings and module categories, with particular emphasis on unitary, s-unital, and firm modules. We clarify the relationships between these notions and show that they coincide over left s-unital rings. We then study separability of functors between module categories associated to a ring homomorphism
f:B\to A
, focusing on the restriction functor
{\rm Res}_{f}
and the induction functor
{\rm Ind}_{f}=A\otimes_{B}-
on categories of firm modules.

In Section
4
, we apply the preceding functorial results to the study of simplicity and semisimplicity in the category
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
of firm modules over a left s-unital ring
A
. We show that the usual module-theoretic notions of simple and semisimple modules coincide with the categorical notions of subobject and quotient simplicity and semisimplicity. These results are then used to prove the nonunital version of Maschke’s theorem for group rings over locally unital rings mentioned above, as well as a corresponding hereditary result, thereby extending classical semisimplicity results to a broad nonunital setting.

2.
Separable functors

In this section, we develop the basic categorical theory of separable functors, showing that they reflect splitting properties and categorical notions such as simplicity, semisimplicity, projectivity, and injectivity under mild exactness assumptions. Although many of the arguments are standard, explicit formulations adapted to the present setting are not readily available in the literature, and we therefore include the necessary details for completeness.

Throughout, we use the following notation. For a set
X
, we let
|X|
denote its cardinality, and we let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of positive integers.
We let
\C
denote a category. By this we mean that
\C
consists of a class of objects
\C_{0}
, a class of morphisms
\C_{1}
, domain and codomain maps

d,c:\C_{1}\to\C_{0}
, and a partially defined associative composition

\C_{1}\times\C_{1}\ni(g,f)\mapsto gf\in\C_{1}
,
defined whenever
d(g)=c(f)
. For each
M\in\C_{0}
, there is an identity morphism
{\rm id}_{M}\in\C_{1}
satisfying
d({\rm id}_{M})=c({\rm id}_{M})=M
,

f{\rm id}_{M}=f
whenever
d(f)=M
, and

{\rm id}_{N}f=f
whenever
c(f)=N
.
If
f
is a morphism in
\C
with
d(f)=M
and
c(f)=N
, we write
f:M\to N
, and we denote the class of all such morphisms by
{\rm Hom}_{\C}(M,N)
.

Definition 7
.

Suppose that
\F:\C\to\D
is a functor
(always assumed to be covariant).
Following
[
19
]
,
we say that

\F
is
separable

if for all
M,N\in\C_{0}
, there is a
function

(SF0)

\R_{M,N}:{\rm Hom}_{\D}(\F(M),\F(N))\to{\rm Hom}_{\C}(M,N)
such that

(SF1)

\R_{M,N}(\F(f))=f
, for

f:M\to N
in
\C
, and

(SF2)

\F(g)f^{\prime}=g^{\prime}\F(f)\Rightarrow g\R_{M,N}(f^{\prime})=\R_{M^{\prime},N^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})f
,
for
f:M\to M^{\prime}
,
g:N\to N^{\prime}
in
\C
, and

f^{\prime}:\F(M)\to\F(N)
,
g^{\prime}:\F(M^{\prime})\to\F(N^{\prime})
in
\D
.

At times, we will indicate the functor
\F

for the maps
\R_{M,N}
by writing
\R_{M,N}^{\F}
.

Proposition 8
.

Suppose that
\F:\C\to\D
is a separable
functor. Let
M,N\in\C_{0}
.

(a)

\R_{M,M}({\rm id}_{\F(M)})={\rm id}_{M}
;

(b)

{\rm Hom}_{\C}(M,N)\neq\emptyset

\Longleftrightarrow

{\rm Hom}_{\D}(\F(M),\F(N))\neq\emptyset
;

(c)

\F
is faithful.

Proof.

(a) From (SF1) we get

\R_{M,M}({\rm id}_{\F(M)})=\R_{M,M}(\F({\rm id}_{M}))={\rm id}_{M}
.

(b)
(\Rightarrow)
follows from
the definition of functor, and

(\Leftarrow)
follows from (SF0).

(c) This follows from (SF1).
∎

By Proposition
8
and the next result
(essentially
[
23
, Lemma 1.1]
), the assignment
\R

behaves
almost
like a functor
in the sense that the naturality condition
(SF2) is replaced by the partial
multiplicativity property (SF3) below.

Proposition 9
.

Let
\F:\C\to\D
be a functor.
Then
\F
is
separable if and only if for all

M,N\in\C_{0}
there is a map
\R_{M,N}

satisfying (SF0), (SF1), and

(SF3)

\R_{M,P}(gf)=\R_{N,P}(g)\R_{M,N}(f)
for
M,N,P\in\C_{0}
,

f:\F(M)\!\to\!\F(N)
,

g:\!\F(N)\!\to\!\F(P)
,
whenever

f\in\F({\rm Hom}_{\C}(M,N))
or

g\in\F({\rm Hom}_{\C}(N,P))
.

Proof.

First we show the “only if” statement.
Suppose that
\F
is separable.
We show that (SF3) holds. Take

U,V,W\in\C_{0}
,

u:\F(U)\to\F(V)
and

v:\F(V)\to\F(W)
such that

u\in\F({\rm Hom}_{\C}(U,V))
or

v\in\F({\rm Hom}_{\C}(V,W))
.

Case 1:
u=\F(p)
for some
p:U\to V
in
\C
.
Put
w:=vu
. Then
\F({\rm id}_{W})w=v\F(p)
.
By (SF2),
{\rm id}_{W}\R_{U,W}(w)=\R_{V,W}(v)p
. Hence,

\R_{U,W}(vu)=\R_{U,W}(w)={\rm id}_{W}\R_{U,W}(w)=\R_{V,W}(v)p=\R_{V,W}(v)\R_{U,V}(\F(p))=\R_{V,W}(v)\R_{U,V}(u).

Case 2:
v=\F(q)
for some
q:V\to W
in
\C
.
Put
w:=vu
. Then
w\F({\rm id}_{U})=\F(q)u
.
By (SF2),
\R_{U,W}(w){\rm id}_{U}=q\R(u)
. Thus,

\R_{U,W}(vu)=\R_{U,W}(w)=\R_{U,W}(w){\rm id}_{U}=q\R_{U,V}(u)=\R_{V,W}(\F(q))\R_{U,V}(u)=\R_{V,W}(v)\R_{U,V}(u).

Now we show the “if” statement.
Suppose (SF0), (SF1) and (SF3) hold.
We show (SF2).
Let
\F(g)f^{\prime}=g^{\prime}\F(f)
for some

f:M\to M^{\prime}
,
g:N\to N^{\prime}
in
\C
,

f^{\prime}:\F(M)\to\F(N)
,
g^{\prime}:\F(M^{\prime})\to\F(N^{\prime})
in
\D
.
Put
w:=\F(g)f^{\prime}=g^{\prime}\F(f)
.
By (SF3), with
u:=f^{\prime}
and
v:=\F(g)
,
we get
\R_{M,N^{\prime}}(w)=g\R_{M,N}(f^{\prime})
.
By (SF3), with

u:=\F(f)
and
v:=g^{\prime}
,

\R_{M,N^{\prime}}(w)=\R_{M^{\prime},N^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})f
. Hence

g\R_{M,N}(f^{\prime})=\R_{M,N^{\prime}}(w)=\R_{M^{\prime},N^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})f
.
This establishes (SF2).
∎

(a)

\F

Proof.

(a) Suppose that
\F

\R_{M,N}^{\F}
, for

Clearly,
\R_{M,N}^{\HH}

Using (SF1) for

Now we show that
\R_{M,N}^{\HH}

\HH

Lemma 13

.

(i)
\Rightarrow

Suppose that

. But since also

{\rm id}_{Z}:Z\to Z

{\rm id}_{M}:M\to M
,
and

(ii)
\Rightarrow

Since

(iii)
\Rightarrow

is a

and

0_{M,Z}h=0_{P,Z}
.

is a

(iv)

is a

.

be an epimorphism.

(vi)

In particular,

then equals this isomorphism.

3.
Module categories

In this section, we introduce our
conventions for modules over possibly
nonunital rings and review the notions of
unital, s-unital, unitary, and firm
modules. We show that over left s-unital
rings these notions coincide, and we study
how restriction and induction functors
behave with respect to them.
Finally, we relate separability of these
functors to separability of ring extensions
and establish corresponding closure
properties.

Let
A
be a ring.
By this we mean that
A
is
associative but not necessarily unital.
Let
M
be a left
A
-module.
By this we mean
that
M
is an additive group
equipped with a biadditive
map
A\times M\ni(a,m)\mapsto am\in M

satisfying

(aa^{\prime})m=a(a^{\prime}m)
for
a,a^{\prime}\in A

and
m\in M
. We let
{}_{A}{\rm Mod}
denote
the category having left
A
-modules
as objects and
A
-linear maps as
morphisms. Similarly, the category

{\rm Mod}_{A}
of right
A
-modules is defined.
Let
B
be another ring. If
M
is a
left
A
-module and a right
B
-module, then

M
is said to be an
A
-
B
-bimodule if

(am)b=a(mb)
for
a\in A
,
m\in M

and
b\in B
.
We let
{}_{A}{\rm Mod}_{B}
denote the category
having
A
-
B
-bimodules as objects and
as morphisms maps that are simultaneously
left
A
-linear and right
B
-linear.
If
M
is an
A
-
B
-bimodule where

A=B
, then
M
is said
to be an
A
-bimodule.

Suppose that
M
is a left
A
-module.
Then
M
is said to be
unital

if there is
a\in A
such that for every

m\in M
, the equality
am=m
holds.
We let
AM

denote the set of all finite sums
of elements of the form
am
for
a\in A

and
m\in M
.
Following Ánh and
Márki
[
1
]
,
we say that
M
is

unitary
if
AM=M
.
We let
\mu_{A,M}
denote the

multiplication map

A\otimes_{A}M\to M
defined by
the additive extension of

\mu_{A,M}(a\otimes m)=am
,
for
a\in A
and
m\in M
.
Following Quillen
[
22
]
,
we say that
M
is

if
\mu_{A,M}
is an
isomorphism in
{}_{A}{\rm Mod}
.
Finally, following
Tominaga
[
25
]
,
we say that

is

if
for every
m\in M
, the relation

m\in Am
holds.
The ring
A
is said to be left unital
(s-unital, unitary, firm) if it is
unital (s-unital, unitary, firm) as a
left
A
-module.
In the sequel,
we will make use of the following result
by Tominaga
[
25
, Thm. 1]

(see also
[
21
]
):

Proposition 28
.

Suppose that
A
is a ring and

M
is a left
A
-module.
Then
M
is s-unital if and only
if for each finite subset
X\subseteq M

there is
a\in A
such that

am=m
for all
m\in X
.

We introduce the following full subcategories of
{}_{A}{\rm Mod}
:

•

{}_{A}{\rm UMod}
, the category of unitary left
A
-modules;

•

{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
, the category of firm left

-modules;

•

{}_{A}{\rm SMod}
, the category of s-unital left

-modules;

•

Clearly, the following implications hold
for these categories:

(1)

unital
\Longrightarrow

\Longrightarrow
unitary, and unital
\Longrightarrow

\Longrightarrow

By the following examples, none of these implications is reversible.

Example 29
.

(a) s-unital
\nRightarrow

Let

be a field and
consider the ring

having

, where

,
with pointwise addition and multiplication.
Then

is left s-unital but

(b) unitary
\nRightarrow

Consider the ring

having as elements all real sequences

, such that

From

(d) firm

To show this, we need:

Lemma 30

Suppose that

is a ring and

-module.

is firm if and only if

Proof.

The “only if” part is trivial.
Now we show the “if” part.
Suppose that

has a section

Then
\mu_{A,M}
is surjective.
It remains to

Take

For each

Then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}z&=&\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\otimes x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\otimes\mu_{A,M}(\sigma(x_{i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}a_{i}\otimes b_{ij}y_{ij}\\

∎

4.
Semisimple modules

In this section, we study simplicity and semisimplicity in the category

{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
of firm modules over a
left s-unital ring
A
. We show that the
categorical notions of monomorphism, epimorphism, subobject, and quotient object
agree with their module-theoretic
counterparts, and we establish equivalences
between categorical and classical notions
of simplicity and semisimplicity.
These results allow us to develop a
theory of simplicity and semisimplicity
in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}

parallel to the classical unital case.
We apply separability results from the
previous sections to obtain lifting
theorems, including locally unital versions
of Maschke’s theorem and hereditary
properties.

From now on,
A
denotes
a fixed left s-unital ring and we
consider the category
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}

of firm left
A
-modules. Recall
from
Proposition
31

that
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}={}_{A}{\rm UMod}={}_{A}{\rm SMod}
.

Suppose that
M
and

are modules
in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}

with
P\subseteq M
.
Then
P
is said to be
a submodule of
M
in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.
Then the
quotient module
M/P
in
{}_{A}{\rm Mod}

belongs to
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.
Let
f:M\to N
be a morphism in

{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
. As customary, we put

\Ker(f):=\{m\in M\mid f(m)=0\}

\Ima(f):=\{f(m)\mid m\in M\}
.
It is easy to see that

\Ker(f)
is a submodule of
M

in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}

and
\Ima(f)
is a submodule of

in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.

The next result shows that
the categorical and the set-theoretic

Proposition 36
.

{}_{A}{\rm FMod}

\Longleftrightarrow

(b)

\Longleftrightarrow

(c)

\Longleftrightarrow

(a) The implication
(\Leftarrow)
is trivial.
Now we show
(\Rightarrow)

Let

be a monomorphism.
Define

g(m)=m

h(m)=0
for

.
Then
fg=0=fh
.
Since
f
is a monomorphism,
g=h
. Hence,

\Ker(f)=g(\Ker(f))=h(\Ker(f))=\{0\}

(b) The implication
(\Leftarrow)
is trivial.
Now we show
(\Rightarrow)

Let
f
be
an epimorphism. Define

is
an epimorphism,

, so that

\Ima(f)=N
.

(c) This follows from (a) and (b).
∎

The next result shows that subobjects and
quotient objects
in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
correspond

and quotient modules in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
,
respectively.

Proposition 37
.

Let

. The following maps
are bijections:

\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma:\Sub_{{}_{A}{\rm FMod}}(M)\longrightarrow\{\text{submodules of }M\},\quad\gamma([f:P\to M])=f(P),\\[5.0pt]
\delta:\Quot_{{}_{A}{\rm FMod}}(M)\longrightarrow\{\text{quotient modules of }M\},\quad\delta([q:M\to Q])=Q.\end{array}

Proof.

If
[f]=[g]
in
\Sub(M)
, then
f=gh
for some
isomorphism

. Thus
f(P)=g(h(P))=g(Q)
,

\gamma

If
\gamma([f])=\gamma([g])

[f]=[g]

, the inclusion

is a monomorphism and

\gamma([i])=N

\delta

.

Let

Proposition 38
.

Let
M\in{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

M
is simple
in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
;

(ii)

M
is subobject simple
in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
;

(iii)

M
is quotient simple
in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
37
, categorical subobjects of
M
are in bijection with submodules of
M
. Likewise, categorical quotient objects of
M
are in bijection with quotient modules of
M
. Therefore
\Sub_{A\text{-}{\rm FMod}(M)}
has exactly two elements if and only if
M
has exactly two submodules, and
\Quot_{A\text{-}{\rm FMod}(M)}
has exactly two elements if and only if
M
has exactly two quotient modules, and these conditions are equivalent since the lattice of submodules and the lattice of quotient modules of

are anti-isomorphic via

Lemma 39
.

Suppose that
M
is a subobject semisimple
module in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
and
N
is a nonzero
submodule of
M
. Then
N
contains a
simple submodule.

Proof.

Take a nonzero
n\in N
. By Zorn’s lemma
there is a maximal submodule
P
of
N

with
n\notin P
.
By the assumptions,
M=P\oplus P^{\prime}

for some submodule
P^{\prime}
of
M
. Then

N=P\oplus Q
where
Q:=N\cap P^{\prime}
.
We claim that
Q
is simple. Seeking a
contradiction, suppose that

Q=Q^{\prime}\oplus Q^{\prime\prime}

for some nonzero submodules
Q^{\prime}
and
Q^{\prime\prime}
.
Then
N=P\oplus Q^{\prime}\oplus Q^{\prime\prime}
and
either
P\oplus Q^{\prime}
or
P\oplus Q^{\prime\prime}
does
not contain
n
(since if
n\in(P\oplus Q^{\prime})\cap(P\oplus Q^{\prime\prime})
, then

n\in P
which is impossible).
But then either
n\notin(P\oplus Q^{\prime})
or

n\notin(P\oplus Q^{\prime\prime})
which
contradicts the maximality of
P
.
Thus,
Q
is a simple submodule of
N
.
∎

Let
M
be a module in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.
We say that
M
is
semisimple

in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}

if
M
is the direct sum of simple
submodules in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.

Proposition 40
.

Let
M\in{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

M
is semisimple
in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
;

(ii)

M
is quotient semisimple
in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
;

(iii)

M
is subobject
semisimple in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
Let
q:M\to N
be an epimorphism. We consider
two cases.

Case 1:
\Ker(q)=M
. Then
N=\{0\}
.
Let
s:N\to M
be the zero map.
Then
q\circ s={\rm id}_{N}
.

Case 2:
\Ker(q)\subsetneq M
.
Since
M
is semisimple,
M=\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i}

for some simple submodules
M_{i}
of
M
.
Let
S
be the subset of nonzero submodules
N

of
M
such that
\Ker(q)\cap N=\{0\}
.
Then
S
is nonempty. Indeed,
by the assumptions, there is
i\in I
with

M_{i}\not\subseteq\Ker(q)
, so that

M_{i}\cap\Ker(q)=\{0\}
, by simplicity of
M_{i}
.
The set
S
, ordered by inclusion, is inductive.
Thus, by Zorn’s lemma, it has a maximal element
P
.
Seeking a contradiction, suppose that

\Ker(q)+P\subsetneq M
. Then there is
j\in I

with
M_{j}\not\subseteq\Ker(q)+P
so that

M_{j}\cap(\Ker(q)+P)=\{0\}
by simplicity
of
M_{j}
. But then
P\subsetneq P+M_{j}
and

\Ker(q)\cap(P+M_{j})=\{0\}
which violates
the maximality of
P
, which is a contradiction.
Therefore,
M=\Ker(q)\oplus P
.
The restriction
q|_{P}:P\to N
is injective since

P\cap\ker(q)=\{0\}
, and surjective since

q(P)=q(\Ker(q))+q(P)=q(\Ker(q)+P)=q(M)=N
, hence an isomorphism.
Letting
s:N\to M
be the composite of
(q|_{P})^{-1}
with the inclusion
P\hookrightarrow M
yields

q\circ s={\rm id}_{N}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii):
Suppose that
M
is quotient
semisimple. Let
i:K\to M
be a monomorphism.
Consider the cokernel
p:M\to M/K
, which is an
epimorphism. By hypothesis there is

s:M/K\to M
with

p\circ s=\mathrm{id}_{M/K}
.
For any
m\in M
we then have

m-s(p(m))\in K
, so we
may define a map
r:M\to K
by

r(m)=m-s(p(m))
, and
this is well defined and
A
-linear since
p(m)=0
if
and only if
m\in K
. Moreover, for
k\in K
we have

p(k)=0
, hence
r(k)=k
, so that

r\circ i=\mathrm{id}_{K}
.
Hence,
M
is subobject semisimple.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Suppose that
M
is
subobject semisimple. If
M=\{0\}
,
then
M
is the direct sum of an empty set
of simple submodules. Suppose from now on
that
M
is nonzero. By
Lemma
39
and Zorn’s lemma
there is a nonempty family
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in I}

of simple submodules, maximal such that the
submodule
N
they generate is a
direct sum
N=\oplus_{i\in I}N_{i}
.
By the assumptions,
M=N\oplus P
for some
submodule
P
. Seeking a contradiction,
suppose that
P
is nonzero. By
Lemma
39
, there is a
simple submodule
P^{\prime}
and a module
P^{\prime\prime}

with
P=P^{\prime}\oplus P^{\prime\prime}
.
Then
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in I}\cup\{P^{\prime}\}

contradicts the maximality of
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
Hence,
P=\{0\}
, so that
M
is a
direct sum of simple submodules.
∎

Proposition 41
.

Let
M\in{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
. The following
assertions hold:

(a)

The module
M
is
semisimple in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}

if and only if every submodule and
every quotient
module of

is semisimple in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.

(b)

Suppose that

M=\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i}

for some submodules
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
.
Then
M
is
semisimple in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}

if and only if each
M_{i}

is semisimple in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.

Proof.

(a) The “if” part of the statement
is trivial.
Now we show the “only if” part.
Suppose that

M
is semisimple in
{}_{A}{\rm FMod}
.
Let
N
be a submodule of
M
.

Let
P
be a submodule of
N
.
By Proposition
40
,
there is a submodule
Q
of
M
with

M=P\oplus Q
.
But then
N=N\cap M=P\oplus(N\cap Q)
.
Hence,
N
is subobject semisimple.

again,

is semisimple.

Now we show that

By Proposition
40
,
there is a submodule
R
of
M
with

M=N\oplus R
.
Since
M
is subobject semisimple,
it follows that

R
, and hence
M/N
, is semisimple.

(b) This follows from (a).
∎

Proof of Theorem
5

Put
n:=|G|
and
A:=B[G]
.
We wish to define an
A
-bimodule
splitting
\sigma
of the multiplication
map
\mu_{A/B}:A\otimes_{B}A\to A
.
To this end, take
a\in A
and an
idempotent
e\in B
with
ae=a=ea
.
Since
nB=B
, there is
b\in B

with
nb=e
, Then

(ne)(ebe)=e^{2}(nb)e=e^{2}ee=e
,
so
ne
is invertible in the
unital ring
eBe
. Define

\sigma(a):=\sum_{g\in G}ag\otimes(ne)^{-1}g^{-1}.

Then

(\mu_{A/B}\circ\sigma)(a)=\sum_{g\in G}a(ne)^{-1}gg^{-1}=a(ne)(ne)^{-1}=ae=a.

Therefore,

\sigma
is a splitting of
\mu_{A/B}
.
Also, clearly,
\sigma
is left
A
-linear.

Next we show that
\sigma
is well
defined. Suppose that
e^{\prime}\in B
is
another idempotent with
ae^{\prime}=a=e^{\prime}a
.
Take an idempotent
f\in B
such that

ef=fe=e
and
e^{\prime}f=e^{\prime}=e^{\prime}f
.
Take
c\in fBf
with
(nf)c=f
.
Then
(ne)c=(nef)c=e(nf)c=ef=e
.
Thus
ec=(ne)^{-1}e=(ne)^{-1}
.
Hence
e(nf)^{-1}=(ne)^{-1}
.
Similarly,
e^{\prime}(nf)^{-1}=(ne^{\prime})^{-1}
.
Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{cccccc}&\sum_{g\in G}ag\otimes(ne)^{-1}g^{-1}&=&\sum_{g\in G}ag\otimes e(nf)^{-1}g^{-1}&=&\sum_{g\in G}aeg\otimes(nf)^{-1}g^{-1}\\
=&\sum_{g\in G}ae^{\prime}g\otimes(nf)^{-1}g^{-1}&=&\sum_{g\in G}ag\otimes e^{\prime}(nf)^{-1}g^{-1}&=&\sum_{g\in G}ag\otimes(ne^{\prime})^{-1}g^{-1}.\end{array}

Finally, we show that
\sigma
is
right
A
-linear. Take
b\in B
and

h\in G
. Take an idempotent
e\in B

with
ae=a=ea
and
be=b=eb
.
Since
b(ne)=nb=(ne)b
it follows
that
(ne)^{-1}b=b(ne)^{-1}
.
Therefore,

\sigma(a)bh=\sum_{g\in G}ag\otimes b(ne)^{-1}g^{-1}h=[p:=h^{-1}g]=\sum_{p\in G}abhp\otimes(ne)^{-1}p^{-1}=\sigma(abh).

This establishes that
A/B
is separable.
∎

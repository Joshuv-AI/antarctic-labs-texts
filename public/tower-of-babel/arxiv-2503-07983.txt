Threshold for the existence of scattering states for nonlinear Schrödinger equations without gauge invariance
Hayato Miyazaki, Motohiro Sobajima

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with a threshold phenomenon for the existence of scattering states for nonlinear Schrödinger equations. The nonlinearity includes a non-oscillatory term of the order lower than the Strauss exponent. We show that no scattering states exist for the equation in a weighted Sobolev space. It is emphasized that our method admits initial data with good properties, such as compactly supported smooth functions. The result indicates that the Strauss exponent acts as a threshold for the power of the nonlinearity that determines whether solutions scatter or not in the weighted space.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the nonlinear Schrödinger equation

(NLS)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}\Delta u=F(u),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
u=u(t,x)
is a complex-valued unknown function.
The nonlinearity
F
satisfies the following condition:

(A1)

F(0)=0

and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\absolutevalue{F(z) - F(w)}\leqslant C(|z|+|w|)^{p-1}|z-w|

for any
z
,
w\in\mathbb{C}
and some
p>1
.

(A2)

There exist
c_{0}>0
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\real\left[e^{i\theta}F(z)\right]\geqslant c_{0}|z|^{p}
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

As for the power of the nonlinearity, we further assume

\displaystyle 1<p<p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)\coloneqq\frac{d+2+\sqrt{d^{2}+12d+4}}{2d}.

The exponent
p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
is called the Strauss exponent, which is defined by the positive root of the quadratic equation
dx^{2}-(d+2)x-2=0
.

A typical example of the nonlinearity in our mind is the power-type nonlinearity
F(u)=\eta|u|^{p}
with
\eta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.
In this case, the condition (
A2
) is satisfied with
c_{0}=|\eta|
and
\theta=-\arg\eta
.
For more general nonlinearity, we can treat the homogenous nonlinearity
F
of degree
p
; that is,
F
satisfies

(1.1)

\displaystyle F(\lambda z)=\lambda^{p}F(z)

for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
\lambda>0
.
Remark that, if
F
satisfies (
1.1
), then the condition (
A1
) is equivalent to the fact that the
2\pi
-periodic function

\theta\mapsto F(e^{i\theta})
is Lipschitz continuous (see
[
22
, Appendix A]
).
Using the method of
[
22
,
21
]
via the Fourier series expansion, we can decompose the nonlinearity into

(1.2)

\displaystyle F(u)=g_{0}|u|^{p}+g_{1}|u|^{p-1}u+\sum_{n\neq 0,1}g_{n}|u|^{p-n}u^{n},\quad g_{n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(e^{i\theta})e^{-in\theta}d\theta.

The nonlinearity
(
1.2
) satisfies (
A2
) if
\{g_{n}\}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
and
|g_{0}|>\sum_{n\neq 0}|g_{n}|
.

In the context of nonlinear Schrödinger equations,
the Strauss exponent
p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
was firstly identified by Strauss
[
33
]
, as the lower bound on the power of nonlinearity for which
a scattering operator exists for small data in certain norms
(see also
[
5
,
4
]
).
In the case of (
NLS
) with the gauge invariant nonlinearity

(1.3)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}\Delta u=\eta|u|^{p-1}u,\quad\eta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\quad p>1,

the asymptotic completeness of the scattering operator was proven by Tsutsumi
[
35
]
under the condition
p>p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
(see also
[
11
]
).
The critical case
p=p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
in (
1.3
) was treated by
[
3
,
26
]
.
Under the sub-Strauss case
1+2/d<p<p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
, in
[
3
,
8
,
26
]
, it was proven that (
1.3
) admits a scattering solution.
Remark that Strauss
[
32
]
discussed the non-existence of scattering states for the equation (
1.3
)
under the case
p\leqslant 1+2/d
(cf.
[
1
,
4
]
).
A generalization of scattering to solitary waves or more general wave profiles was presented in
[
25
]
.
The exponent
1+2/d
is known as the critical exponent in view of the long-time behavior of solutions.
In fact, Tsutsumi and Yajima
[
34
]
established the existence of scattering solutions to (
1.3
) for
p>1+2/d
.
Further improvements of their result were investigated in
[
2
]
.
Regarding (
NLS
) with (
A1
),
p>p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
,
Kato
[
19
]
established the existence of the scattering operator for data in its domain, where a certain space-time norm of the corresponding asymptotically free solution is sufficiently small.
It should be mentioned that the method in
[
19
]

does not depend on such a structure of the nonlinearity.
Hence this implies that the structure does not affect the long-time behavior of small solutions under the super-Strauss case
p>p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
.
However, we have observed that
the Strauss exponent in the analysis of (
NLS
)
has been regarded merely as the lower bound of the validity of concrete techniques, but it has not been recognized as the upper bound marking a qualitative change in the behavior of solutions.
This paper aims to reveal such a phenomenon in (
NLS
).

As is well-known, the Strauss exponent also appears in the analysis of
the initial-value problem of the semilinear wave equation

(NW)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u=|u|^{p},\quad(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Roughly speaking, the number
p_{\rm st}(d-1)
(together with
p_{\rm st}(0)=\infty
)
is the threshold for dividing the situation
of existence or non-existence of global-in-time solutions to (
NW
)
with (non-trivial) nonnegative initial data.
More precisely, non-trivial global-in-time solutions (with small amplitude) exist when
p>p_{\rm st}(d-1)

and solutions of (
NW
) with nonnegative small initial data blow up in finite time
when
p\leqslant p_{\rm st}(d-1)
.
For this direction, we refer to, for instance,
John
[
17
]
,
Kato
[
18
]
,
Glassey
[
10
,
9
]
,
Sideris
[
31
]
,
Schaeffer
[
28
]
,
Rammaha
[
27
]
,
Georgiev, Lindblad and Sogge
[
7
]
,
Yordanov and Zhang
[
37
]
, and Zhou
[
38
]
.

Here we focus our attention to
the existence or non-existence of

scattering solutions
to (
NLS
).
It is not difficult to construct small data scattering solutions
to (
NLS
)
in the super-Strauss case

by the contraction mapping principle
with the application of (non-admissible) Strichartz’ estimates, established in the aforementioned paper
[
19
]
.
Therefore the main consideration in
the present paper
is the non-existence of scattering solutions
in the sub-Strauss case
p<p_{\rm st}(d)
.
In this paper, in view of the strategy for the analysis of (
NW
),
we aim to show the non-existence of scattering states for (
NLS
) below the Strauss exponent.
In contrast to the gauge-invariant case (
1.3
), it is not expected that (
NLS
) admits scattering solutions when
p<p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
.
In fact, Shimomura
[
30
]
proved that the equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}\Delta u=\rho|u|^{p},\quad\rho\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},\quad p>1,

with if
d=2
and
p=1+2/d=2
,
has no non-trivial solution that behaves like a free solution (cf.
[
29
]
).
Later on, Masaki and the first author
[
23
]
extended their result. Namely,
regarding (
NLS
) with the nonlinearity of the form (
1.2
) with
g_{0}\neq 0
,
g_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\{g_{n}\}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
,
they showed the non-existence of the solution that behaves like a free solution with or without a phase modification in any dimensions.

Also, with regard to (
NLS
) with (
1.2
),
global-in-time existence is not always true if
p<1+4/d
and
g_{0}\neq 0
.
In fact, Ikeda and Wakasugi
[
16
]
found blow-up solutions to (
1.4
)
when
p\leqslant 1+2/d
.
In
[
12
]
, Ikeda and Inui proved the existence of blow-up solutions to (
1.4
) for
p<1+4/d
under slowly decaying initial data.
The results in
[
16
,
12
]
were partially generalized by Fujiwara and Ozawa
[
6
]
from the viewpoint of an ordinary differential equation for a spatial integral involving both
u
and nonnegative rapidly decreasing functions.
In this paper, instead of considering the slowly decaying initial data in
[
12
]
, we adopt a
L^{2}
-based weighted space as the class of initial data that naturally arises in the study of the scattering problem for nonlinear Schrödinger equations (see
[
8
,
26
]
).
Within this framework, we establish the non-existence of scattering states for (
NLS
) under the condition (
A1
) and (
A2
) with
p<p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
.
This is in contrast to the case of gauge invariance (
1.3
), which admits scattering
solutions in the weighted space.

1.1.
Main results

To state the main result, let us introduce the notations used throughout this paper.
For any
p\geqslant 1
,
L^{p}(\Omega)
denotes the usual Lebesgue space on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
If
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
, we abbreviate
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
L^{p}
.
Set
\expectationvalue{a}=(1+|a|^{2})^{1/2}
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

\mathcal{F}[u]=\widehat{u}
is the usual Fourier transform of a function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
the standard Sobolev space on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined by
H^{s}=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid\norm{u}_{H^{s}}\coloneqq\norm{\ev{\nabla}^{s} u}_{L^{2}}<\infty\}
.
We denote the weighted Sobolev space by
\mathcal{F}H^{s}=\mathcal{F}H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid\norm{u}_{\mathcal{F}H^{s}}\coloneqq\norm{\ev{x}^{s}u}_{L^{2}}<\infty\}
.
Let
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of smooth functions with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

B(0,r)
stands for the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius
r
centered at the origin.
For
A\subset\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
is the characteristic function of
A
.
Let
U(t)
be the Schrödinger evolution operator
e^{it\Delta/2}
.

A\lesssim B
denotes
A\leqslant CB
for some constants
C>0
.

We also give the definition of solutions to (
NLS
).

Definition 1.1

(Solution)
.

Let
I\ni 0
be an interval.
Given
u_{0}\in L^{2}
,
we say a function
u\colon I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is a solution to (
NLS
) on
I
if

C(I;L^{2})\cap L^{q_{0}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I;L^{r_{0}})

satisfies

u(t)=U(t)u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}U(t-s)F(u(s))\,ds

in
L^{2}
for any
t\in I
, where
(q_{0},r_{0})=(4(p+1)/d(p-1),1+p)
.

If the nonlinearity
F
satisfies (
A1
),
then (
NLS
) is locally well-posed in
L^{2}
(cf.
[
36
]
).
Also, the solution
u
belongs to
L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I;L^{r})
for any admissible pair
(q,r)
, where a pair
(q,r)
is said to be admissible if

2\leqslant q,r\leqslant\infty,\quad\frac{2}{q}=d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\right),\quad(d,q,r)\neq(2,2,\infty)

(For details, see
[
36
]
).
In particular, if
d=1
,
2
and
p>2
, then a pair
\left(\frac{4p}{d(p-2)},p\right)
is admissible.
Remark that
p\leqslant 2
always holds when
d\geqslant 3
, because of
p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)\leqslant 2
.

We are in position to state the main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geqslant 1
.
Assume (
A1
) and (
A2
) with
1<p<p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
.
Take
\alpha>0
satisfying

\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{d}{2}\leqslant\alpha.

Given
u_{0}\in\mathcal{F}H^{\alpha}
,
let
u\in C([0,\infty);L^{2})
be a solution to (
NLS
) with
u(0)=u_{0}
.
The following statements hold:

(i)

If
1<p\leqslant 2
and

(1.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\norm{u - U(\cdot)u_{+}}_{L^{\infty}(t,\infty;L^{2})}=0

for some
u_{+}\in L^{2}
,
then
u_{+}\equiv 0
.

(ii)

If
2<p<p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
(that is
d\leqslant 2
) and

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(t^{\frac{d(p-2)}{4p}}\norm{u - U(\cdot)u_{+}}_{L^{\frac{4p}{d(p-2)}}(t,\infty;L^{p})}\right)=0

for some
u_{+}\in\mathcal{F}H^{\beta}
with
\beta>d/2-d/p
,
then
u_{+}\equiv 0
.

Remark 1.3
.

When
p>2
, the decay rate in (
1.6
) coincides with that of
U(t)u_{+}
in the same topology. Indeed, under the assumption
u_{+}\in\mathcal{F}H^{\beta}\setminus\{0\}
, the estimate

0\neq\norm{\widehat{u_{+}}}_{L^{p}}\lesssim t^{\frac{4p}{d(p-2)}}\norm{U(\cdot)u_{+}}_{L^{\frac{4p}{d(p-2)}}(t,\infty;L^{p})}\lesssim\norm{\widehat{u_{+}}}_{L^{p}}

holds for large
t
.
This implies that the condition (
1.6
) is
the minimal assumption to ensure the uniqueness of the scattering state

u_{+}
.
In fact, if
u_{+}\in\mathcal{F}H^{\beta}
and we assume the slower decay condition

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(t^{\gamma}\norm{u - U(\cdot)u_{+}}_{L^{\frac{4p}{d(p-2)}}(t,\infty;L^{p})}\right)=0

for some
\gamma<d(p-2)/(4p)
,
then the same condition holds when
u_{+}
is replaced by any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Hence, the condition (
1.6
) can be regarded as a natural assumption in the study of the scattering problem.

Remark 1.4
.

When
1<p\leqslant\min(2,1+2/d)
, it was shown in
[
4
, Theorem 7.5.2]
that
the gauge invariant case (
1.3
) with
\eta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
admits no scattering states in
\Sigma\coloneqq H^{1}\cap\mathcal{F}H^{1}
via the solution after the pseudo-conformal transformation.
The argument in
[
4
]
actually works for
u_{0}
,
u_{+}\in L^{2}
, though for
d=1
and
2<p\leqslant 3
, the assumption
u_{0}
,
u_{+}\in\mathcal{F}H^{1}
appears to be necessary.
This range was already treated in the earlier work by Barab
[
1
]
, who proved non-scattering in the defocusing case

using explicit time decay derived from the pseudo-conformal energy estimate under the weighted data.
Theorem
1.2
provides a complementary result for the non-gauge-invariant case.

Remark 1.5
.

The weighted condition

0<s_{c}\coloneqq\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{d}{2}\leqslant\alpha

is necessary for our argument. This range corresponds to the scale (super-)critical range in view of the weighted Sobolev space
\mathcal{F}H^{\alpha}
.
We emphasize that the initial data can be taken in the inhomogeneous scale-critical space
\mathcal{F}H^{s_{c}}
, particularly in

,
which was always excluded in previous blow-up results for (

).
For instance, regarding (

),

[

]
proved the following:
If

satisfies the condition

(1.7)

\displaystyle-\imaginary f(x)\geqslant\begin{cases}c|x|^{-k}&|x|>1,\\
0&|x|\leqslant 1\end{cases}

for some

and

, then
there exists a solution to (

) with

Hence, when taking

,
by choosing

with some

,

belongs to
\mathcal{F}H^{\alpha}
and satisfies (

). Thus, a blow-up solution with

u(0)=f

exists, but the

does not belong to
\mathcal{F}H^{s_{c}}
.
Theorem
1.2
holds for any
u_{0}\in\mathcal{F}H^{\alpha}
with

,
and thus no restriction is imposed on the structure of

, unlike in
[

]
.

Remark 1.6
.

Our argument requires only

-solutions, although the initial data belongs to the weighted space
\mathcal{F}H^{\alpha}
.
In particular, we do not know whether local well-posedness for (
NLS
) holds in
\mathcal{F}H^{\alpha}
, because the standard persistence of regularity argument does not apply to non-gauge-invariant nonlinearities.

Remark 1.7
.

Without loss of generality, we may let
\theta=0
in the assumption (

) by change of an unknown function

.
When we consider the nonlinearity of (
1.2
), it also suffices to deal with the case

via scaling.

Remark 1.8
.

In
[
20
]
, Kawamoto, Masaki and the first author showed that
if

and the nonlinearity is of the form (

) with

(1.8)

\displaystyle p\in\left(\frac{7}{2},5\right)\quad(d=1),\qquad p\in(2,3)\quad(d=2),\qquad p=2\quad(d=3),

then for any

,

) has a global-in-time small solution in

for

and a certain summability condition on

. Nevertheless, the existence of local-in-time solutions is shown by the standard well-posedness theory (cf.

,

).

indicates that under

, if

, then any global solutions do not scatter in

, even if global solutions exist.
We impose the time decay condition (
1.6
) in the sense of scattering if
d=1
,
2
, but
Theorem
1.2
suggests that the result of
[

]
may not hold under

.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

Before starting the discussion, we briefly give a crucial idea for treating the non-scattering phenomenon, along with an explanation of our perspective.
It is meaningful to clarify our viewpoint on the analysis of (
NW
), as it helps to explain the main ideas of this paper.
In the proof of small data blow-up solutions to (
NW
) by Ikeda, the second author and Wakasa
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
, one may observe that the Strauss exponent
p_{\rm st}(d-1)
naturally appears when comparing the effect of nonlinearities with the behavior of solutions to the corresponding linear wave equation.
Specifically, the following estimates play an important role in the proof of
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{T}^{2p^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant CT^{d-1-\frac{2}{p-1}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{T}^{2p^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\geqslant\delta T^{d-\frac{d-1}{2}p},

where
\{\eta_{T}\}_{T>0}
is a family of suitable cut-off functions in
t
.
The first estimate (
2.1
) can be interpreted as a condition imposed by the nonlinearity, and a similar observation for (
NLS
) has been addressed by Ikeda and Inui
[
12
,
13
]
, and Ikeda and Wakasugi
[
16
]
.
The second estimate (
2.2
) reflects the large-time behavior of solutions to the corresponding linear wave equation.
By incorporating this perspective into the analysis of (
NLS
), we have realized that (
2.2
) is closely related to solutions of (
NLS
) that are asymptotically free. Thanks to this insight, we are able to prove the non-existence of scattering states for (
NLS
) when
p<p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
.
This perspective on (
NLS
) is the novelty of the present paper.

2.1.
Preliminary

Let us define a cut-off function
\eta

satisfying

\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,1/2]}\leqslant\eta(s)\leqslant\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,1]}.

Then we denote

\displaystyle\psi_{R}(t,x)=\left\{\eta\left(\frac{|x|^{2}+t}{R^{2}}\right)\right\}^{2p^{\prime}}

for any
R>0
, where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of

.
Such a cut-off function is firstly introduced in
[
24
]
(cf.
[
15
]
).
The function
\psi_{R}
has the following property:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R>0
and
p>1
. Then

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\partial_{t} \psi_{R}(t,x)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left[\psi_{R}(t,x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{2p^{\prime}}},\qquad\absolutevalue{ \Delta\psi_{R}}\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left[\psi_{R}(t,x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

hold for any
t\geqslant 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

The desired assertion immediately follows from a direct calculation.
∎

To prove Theorem
1.2
,
we shall introduce the definition of weak solutions to (
NLS
):

Definition 2.2

(Weak solution)
.

We say a function
u\in C([0,T);L^{2})

is a weak solution to (
NLS
)
on
[0,T)
,
T>0
,
if
u\in L^{q_{0}}_{\mathrm{loc}}((0,T);L^{r_{0}})
and the identity

\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)(-i\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+\Delta\psi(t,x))\,dxdt\\
=i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(0,x)\psi(0,x)\,dx+\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(u(t,x))\psi(t,x)\,dxdt

holds for any test function
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((-\infty,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
(q_{0},r_{0})=(4(p+1)/d(p-1),1+p)
.

Remark that solutions in Definition
1.1

always satisfy the property of
weak solutions in Definition
2.2

from the density argument involving Strichartz’ estimates (see
[
16
]
).
Hence, the solution
u
satisfies

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\iint_{(0,R^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\left(-i\partial_{t}\psi_{R}(t,x)+\Delta\psi_{R}(t,x)\right)\,dxdt\\
={}&i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}(x)\psi_{R}(0,x)\,dx+\iint_{(0,R^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(u(t,x))\psi_{R}(t,x)\,dxdt\end{aligned}

for any
R>1
.
Set

(2.4)

\displaystyle I(R)=\iint_{(0,R^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\psi_{R}(t,x)\,dxdt.

The following lemma is essentially used in

Here we give a sketch of the proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.3
.

The estimate

\displaystyle\real\iint_{(0,R^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\left(-i\partial_{t}\psi_{R}(t,x)+\Delta\psi_{R}(t,x)\right)\,dxdt\lesssim R^{d-(d+2)\frac{1}{p}}I(R)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for any
R>1
.

Proof.

Take
R>1
.
By Lemma
2.1
, we see from Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\real\iint_{(0,R^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\left(-i\partial_{t}\psi_{R}(t,x)+\Delta\psi_{R}(t,x)\right)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R^{2}}\iint_{(0,R^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|\left[\psi_{R}(t,x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{2p^{\prime}}}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\iint_{(0,R^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|\left[\psi_{R}(t,x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left(\iint_{\left\{(t,x)\mid|x|^{2}+t\leqslant R^{2}\right\}}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\iint_{\left\{(t,x)\mid|x|^{2}+t\leqslant R^{2}\right\}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\psi_{R}(t,x)\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{d-(d+2)\frac{1}{p}}I(R)^{\frac{1}{p}},

as desired.
∎

The slowly decaying condition given by (
1.7
) is assumed in
[
12
]
, to treat the term involving initial data, which is the first term on the right-hand side of (
2.3
).
This condition disturbs the establishment of global existence.
In contrast, we handle this term using a spatially weighted condition, which naturally arises in the context of scattering theory for nonlinear Schrödinger equations.
Under the weighted condition, the term turns out to be the most harmless in our argument.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha>0
.
The estimate

\displaystyle\left|\imaginary\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}(x)\psi_{R}(0,x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\norm{u_{0}}_{\mathcal{F}H^{\alpha}}\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ \alpha>d/2,\\
\log R&\text{if}\ \alpha=d/2,\\
R^{\frac{d-2\alpha}{2}}&\text{if}\ \alpha<d/2\end{cases}

holds for any
R>2
.

Proof.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\imaginary\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}(x)\psi_{R}(0,x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{B(0,R)}|u_{0}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{B(0,R)}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\expectationvalue{x}^{2\alpha}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{B(0,R)}\expectationvalue{x}^{-2\alpha}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

from which the desired estimate follows.
∎

The following lemma plays a central role in the discussion of this paper, which describes a requirement for the non-zero scattering states.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
u_{+}\in\mathcal{F}H^{\beta}
with
\beta>d/2-d/p
if
p>2
, otherwise
u_{+}\in L^{2}
.
Assume (
1.6
) and (
1.5
).
If
u_{+}\not\equiv 0
, then the estimate

\displaystyle I(R)\gtrsim R^{d+1-\frac{d}{2}p}

holds for
R>0
large enough.

Proof.

Take
r_{0}>1
such that
\norm{\widehat{u_{+}}}_{L^{p}(B(0,r_{0}))}\neq 0
.
Note that
\widehat{u_{+}}\in L^{p}(B(0,r_{0}))
holds by the assumption.
Set

\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}=\left\{(t,x)\in(0,R^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}|x|\leqslant r_{0}t,\;\frac{R}{4r_{0}}\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{R}{2r_{0}}\right\}

for
R>2
.
Remark that
\psi_{R}\equiv 1
on
\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}
.
We define

\phi_{+}(t)=M(t)D(t)\mathcal{F}u_{+},

which is the asymptotics of
U(t)u_{+}
,
where
M(t)
and
D(t)
are unitary operators on
L^{2}
defined by

[M(t)f](x)=e^{i\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t}}f(x),\qquad[D(t)f](x)=(it)^{-\frac{d}{2}}f\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)

for any
t\neq 0
, respectively.
Let us decompose
u
as

u=\phi_{+}(t)+(U(t)u_{+}-\phi_{+}(t))+(u-U(t)u_{+}).

By the triangle inequality in
L^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R})
, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I(R)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geqslant{}&\left(\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|\phi_{+}(t)|^{p}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&-\left(\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|U(t)u_{+}-\phi_{+}(t)|^{p}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-\left(\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|u-U(t)u_{+}|^{p}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{aligned}

Let us first estimate the last term in the right-hand side of (
2.5
).
We shall treat the case
p\leqslant 2
.
Take
\varepsilon>0
small enough.
By the assumption (
1.5
), there exists
t_{\ast}(\varepsilon)>0
such that if
t\geqslant t_{\ast}(\varepsilon)
, then

(2.6)

\displaystyle\norm{u(t) - U(t)u_{+}}_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon.

Hence, Hölder’s inequality leads to

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|u(t)-U(t)u_{+}|^{p}\,dxdt&=\int_{\frac{R}{4r_{0}}}^{\frac{R}{2r_{0}}}\left(\int_{B(0,r_{0}t)}|u(t)-U(t)u_{+}|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dt\\
&\lesssim\int_{\frac{R}{4r_{0}}}^{\frac{R}{2r_{0}}}\left(\int_{B(0,r_{0}t)}|u(t)-U(t)u_{+}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}(r_{0}t)^{d\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}\,dt\\
&\lesssim\sup_{t\geqslant\frac{R}{4r_{0}}}\norm{u(t)-U(t) u_+}_{L^{2}}^{p}R^{d+1-\frac{d}{2}p}\\
&<\varepsilon R^{d+1-\frac{d}{2}p}\end{aligned}

for any
R\geqslant 4r_{0}t_{\ast}(\varepsilon)
.
A direct calculation shows that
U(t)=M(t)D(t)\mathcal{F}M(t)
, and by Plancharel’s theorem,
the second term in (
2.5
) can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|U(t)u_{+}-\phi_{+}(t)|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|D(t)\mathcal{F}(M(t)u_{+}-u_{+})|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{R}{4r_{0}}}^{\frac{R}{2r_{0}}}t^{d-\frac{d}{2}p}\int_{B(0,r_{0})}|\mathcal{F}(M(t)u_{+}-u_{+})|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\frac{R}{4r_{0}}}^{\frac{R}{2r_{0}}}(r_{0}t)^{d-\frac{d}{2}p}\norm{M(t)u_+-u_+}_{L^{2}}^{p}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{d+1-\frac{d}{2}p}\left(\sup_{t\geqslant R/4r_{0}}\|M(t)u_{+}-u_{+}\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{p}.

Since
u_{+}\in L^{2}
,
M(t)\rightarrow 1
as
t\rightarrow\infty
and
|M(t)|=1
, Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R_{\ast}(\varepsilon)>1
such that if
R\geqslant R_{\ast}(\varepsilon)
, then

\sup_{t\geqslant R/4r_{0}}\|M(t)u_{+}-u_{+}\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon.

Then we conclude that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|U(t)u_{+}-\phi_{+}|^{p}\,dxdt\lesssim\varepsilon R^{d+1-\frac{d}{2}p}

for
R>0
large enough.

Let us deal with the case
p>2
, which occurs only in
d=1
,
2
. Set
q=\frac{4p}{d(p-2)}
.
Note that

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{p}=\frac{d}{2}\iff-\frac{2p}{q}=d-\frac{d}{2}p.

By (
1.6
), we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|u(t)-U(t)u_{+}|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\frac{R}{4r_{0}}}^{\frac{R}{2r_{0}}}\norm{u(t)-U(t)u_+}_{L^{p}}^{p}\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\frac{R}{4r_{0}}}^{\frac{R}{2r_{0}}}\norm{u(t)-U(t)u_+}_{L^{p}}^{q}\,dt\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\left(\int_{\frac{R}{4r_{0}}}^{\frac{R}{2r_{0}}}\,dt\right)^{1-\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(R^{\frac{1}{q}}\|u(t)-U(t)u_{+}\|_{L^{q}(R/4r_{0},\infty;L^{p})}\right)^{p}R^{1-\frac{2p}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon R^{d+1-\frac{d}{2}p}

for any
R\geqslant 4r_{0}t_{\ast}(\varepsilon)
.
Also, one sees from Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|U(t)u_{+}-\phi_{+}(t)|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|D(t)\mathcal{F}(M(t)u_{+}-u_{+})|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{R}{4r_{0}}}^{\frac{R}{2r_{0}}}t^{d-\frac{d}{2}p}\int_{B(0,r_{0})}|\mathcal{F}(M(t)u_{+}-u_{+})|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{d+1-\frac{d}{2}p}\left(\sup_{t\geqslant R/4r_{0}}\|M(t)u_{+}-u_{+}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\right)^{p}.

Since
u_{+}\in\mathcal{F}H^{\beta}\hookrightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}
, similarly to the above,
for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R_{\ast}(\varepsilon)>1
such that if
R\geqslant R_{\ast}(\varepsilon)
, then

\sup_{t\geqslant R/4r_{0}}\|M(t)u_{+}-u_{+}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}<\varepsilon,

which implies (
2.8
).

Finally, the first term of (
2.5
) can be explicitly calculated as

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|\phi_{+}(t)|^{p}\,dxdt&=\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|D(t)\mathcal{F}u_{+}|^{p}\,dxdt\\
&=\int_{R/4r_{0}}^{R/2r_{0}}t^{d-\frac{d}{2}p}\int_{\{|x|<r_{0}\}}|\mathcal{F}u_{+}|^{p}\,dxdt\\
&=CR^{d+1-\frac{d}{2}p}\norm{\widehat{u_{+}}}_{L^{p}(B(0,r_{0}))}^{p}.\end{aligned}

Substituting (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) into (
2.5
),
we establish the desired estimate.
This completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Before starting the proof, we highlight the contrast between the gauge-invariant case (
1.3
) and the non-oscillatory case (
NLS
).
When the nonlinearity lacks oscillatory components, it becomes difficult to capture the interaction between solutions through the nonlinearity.
However, this feature allows us to analyze the size of the nonlinearity directly, and as shown in (
2.9
),
we can extract a lower bound for the space-time integral of the nonlinearity from the long-time behavior of the solution.
This stands in sharp contrast to the gauge-invariant case and is precisely what enables the following argument to work.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Assume
u_{+}\not\equiv 0
and let us argue by contradiction.
Taking the real part of the both side of (
2.3
), we see from Lemma
2.3
and (
A2
) that

\displaystyle I(R)

\displaystyle\lesssim\real\iint_{(0,R^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(u(t,x))\psi_{R}(t,x)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\imaginary\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}(x)\psi_{R}(0,x)\,dx\right|+R^{d-(d+2)\frac{1}{p}}I(R)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
R>1
.
By Young’s inequality, one has

\displaystyle R^{d-(d+2)\frac{1}{p}}I(R)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\varepsilon I(R)+C_{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{p}{p-1}\left(d-(d+2)\frac{1}{p}\right)}=\varepsilon I(R)+C_{\varepsilon}R^{d-\frac{2}{p-1}}

for small
\varepsilon>0
.
These imply

\displaystyle I(R)\lesssim\left|\imaginary\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}(x)\psi_{R}(0,x)\,dx\right|+R^{d-\frac{2}{p-1}}.

Let us only consider the case
p>1+2/d
and
\alpha<d/2
, because the other cases are easier.
By Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{r_{0},R}}|u|^{p}\,dxdt\lesssim R^{d-\frac{2}{p-1}}+R^{\frac{d-2\alpha}{2}}

for any
R>2
.
Note that

d-\frac{2}{p-1}\geqslant\frac{d-2\alpha}{2}

if
p-1\geqslant 4/(d+2\alpha)\iff\alpha\geqslant 2/(p-1)-d/2
.
Then Lemma
2.5
yields

R^{d+1-\frac{d}{2}p}\lesssim R^{d-\frac{2}{p-1}}+R^{\frac{d-2\alpha}{2}}\lesssim R^{d-\frac{2}{p-1}}

for large
R
.
This leads to

d+1-\frac{d}{2}p\leqslant d-\frac{2}{p-1}\iff p\geqslant p_{\mathrm{st}}(d),

which contradicts the assumption
p<p_{\mathrm{st}}(d)
.
Thus
u_{+}\equiv 0
.
The proof is completed.
∎

On multiplicative recurrence along linear patterns
DIMITRIOS CHARAMARAS, ANDREAS MOUNTAKIS AND KONSTANTINOS TSINAS

Abstract
. In [ 9 ] , Donoso, Le, Moreira and Sun study sets of recurrence for actions of the multiplicative semigroup ( ℕ , × ) (\mathbb{N},\times) and provide some sufficient conditions for sets of the form S = { ( a ​ n + b ) / ( c ​ n + d ) : n ∈ ℕ } S=\{(an+b)/(cn+d){:}\;n\in\mathbb{N}\} to be sets of recurrence for such actions. A necessary condition for S S to be a set of multiplicative recurrence is that for every completely multiplicative function f f taking values on the unit circle, we have that lim inf n → ∞ | f ⁡ ( a ​ n + b ) − f ⁡ ( c ​ n + d ) | = 0 . \liminf_{n\to\infty}|f(an+b)-f(cn+d)|=0. In this article, we fully characterize the integer quadruples ( a , b , c , d ) (a,b,c,d) which satisfy the latter property. Our result generalizes a result of Klurman and Mangerel from [ 22 ] concerning the pair ( n , n + 1 ) (n,n+1) , as well as some results from [ 9 ] . In addition, we prove that, under the same conditions on ( a , b , c , d ) (a,b,c,d) , the set S S is a set of recurrence for finitely generated actions of ( ℕ , × ) (\mathbb{N},\times) .

In

Recall that a function

1.2.
Multiplicative recurrence for finitely generated systems

Motivated by
[
9

investigate under what conditions on

the set
S=\{(an+b)/(cn+d){:}\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a set of topological multiplicative recurrence, we are interested in
finding sufficient conditions so that

is a set of measurable multiplicative
recurrence (see Definition
1.4

Theorem

1.1
, it is reasonable to ask whether the
condition
a=c
, and
b=d
or
a\mid bd
is sufficient to show that
S
is a set of
measurable (or at least topological) multiplicative recurrence. Note that this
condition is more general than the one in
[
9
, Theorem 1.6]
. We are
able to prove that, under this condition,
S
is indeed a set of measurable
multiplicative recurrence for an important class of multiplicative systems.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
be a Borel probability space. We say that a sequence of invertible transformations

(1)

Each map

\mu(T_{n}^{-1}A)=\mu(A)

(2)

The

We call

It is evident that a multiplicative system is uniquely determined by the transformations

A multiplicative system is called

Finitely generated multiplicative systems are important due to their connections with finitely generated multiplicative functions (see Definition

[
7

1.4.
Proof strategy

Our proof strategy for
Theorem

1.1
is substantially different from the one used in

[
22
]
.
In order to establish
Theorem

1.1
, we employ the
Q
-trick, which appeared in
[
13
]
.
The main idea is that when one wants to handle the additive
correlations of two multiplicative functions
f,g
, it is often useful to
average over the progression
Qn
, where
Q
belongs to a multiplicative
Følner sequence. Then in a lot of cases, one can factor out from the
averages an
f(Q)
term, and then performing a multiplicative averaging
over
Q
, it follows that
the correlations vanish unless
f
is the trivial multiplicative function.

However, in our case, averaging over the progression
Qn
is not
sufficient. In order to make the
Q
-trick work, we need to average over
arithmetic progressions
Q^{2}n+r_{Q}
for some appropriately chosen

r_{Q}\in\{0,1,\ldots,Q^{2}-1\}
.
We choose the
r_{Q}
’s using the Chinese Remainder Theorem so that some helpful (but somewhat complicated)
congruence relations are satisfied. The condition that
a\mid bd
is
crucial for those congruences to have solution.

In order to establish the result, we then have to handle
correlations of the form

(4)

\frac{1}{|\Phi_{K}|}\sum_{Q\in\Phi_{K}}\frac{1}{\log X_{m}}\sum_{n\leq X_{m}}\frac{f^{\ell}(a(Q^{2}n+rQ)+b)\overline{f^{\ell}(a(Q^{2}n+r_{Q})+d)}}{n}

for a finite collection of powers of
f^{\ell}

In the previous,

f^{\ell}
is aperiodic, then along
(X_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
,

behaves like a strongly

2.
Background material on multiplicative functions

2.1.
Averages and density

Given a non-empty finite set

A\subset\mathbb{N}
and a function
f{:}\;A\to\mathbb{C}
, we denote the Cesàro average of
f
by

{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in A}f(a):=\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{n\in A}f(n),

and the logarithmic average by

{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\in A}f(n):=\frac{1}{\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n}}\sum_{n\in A}\frac{f(n)}{n}.

In particular, since
\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{1}{n}=(1+o_{X\to\infty}(1))\log X
, we define the logarithmic average along the interval
[1,X]
by the simpler expression

{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f(n):=\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{f(n)}{n}.

Given a subset
E\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, we define its
upper
and

lower logarithmic densities
respectively by

\displaystyle\overline{d}_{\log}(E)

\displaystyle:=\limsup_{X\to\infty}\ {\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(n),

\displaystyle\underline{d}_{\log}(E)

\displaystyle:=\liminf_{X\to\infty}\ {\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(n),

and when they coincide, the limit exists, and we
say that
E
has logarithmic density equal to this limit.

In our proofs, we will need to average over a suitably chosen progression
with a highly divisible difference. For each
K\in\mathbb{N}
we define

(6)

\Phi_{K}=\left\{p_{1}^{\theta_{1}}\cdots p_{K}^{\theta_{K}}{:}\;2^{K}<\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{K}\leq 2^{K+1}\right\},

where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{K}
are the first
K
primes. A similar
sequence is used in
[
13
]
, with
K<\theta_{i}\leq 2K
in place of
2^{K}<\theta_{i}\leq 2^{K+1}
. We pick our inequalities so that
no number belongs to two distinct sets
\Phi_{K_{1}},\Phi_{K_{2}}
, but
otherwise the choice of inequalities is unimportant.

The sequence
\Phi_{K}
satisfies

(7)

\frac{|\Phi_{K}\cap\ \Phi_{K}/p|}{|\Phi_{K}|}\to 1

as
K\to\infty
,
where for
A\subset\mathbb{N}
we define

A/p:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}{:}\;pn\in A\}.

Sequences that satisfy (
7
) are called
multiplicative Følner
sequences.

2.2.
Distances of multiplicative functions and aperiodicity.

Let
f,g
be multiplicative functions whose modulus is bounded by
1
. Following the terminology of Granville and Soundararajan
[
18
]
, we define the
pretentious distance
of two multiplicative functions between two real numbers
A,B
by the formula

(8)

\mathbb{D}(f,g;A,B):=\left(\sum_{A<p\leq B}\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(f(p)\overline{g(p)})}{p}\right)^{1/2}.

We define
\mathbb{D}(f,g):=\lim\limits_{X\to\infty}\mathbb{D}(f,g;1,X)
and say that
f

pretends
to be
g
, which we denote by
f\sim g
,
if
\mathbb{D}(f,g)<+\infty
. It can be shown that the distance function satisfies a triangle inequality. More specifically, for any multiplicative functions
f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}
, we have

\mathbb{D}(f_{1},g_{1};1,X)+\mathbb{D}(f_{2},g_{2};1,X)\geq\mathbb{D}(f_{1}f_{2},g_{1}g_{2};1,X),

which readily implies that

\mathbb{D}(f,g;1,X)+\mathbb{D}(g,h;1,X)\geq\mathbb{D}(f,h;1,X)

for any
1
-bounded multiplicative functions
f,g,h
. We will use the triangle inequality freely throughout the proofs without further references.

For
z,w\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we have
|z-w|^{2}=2(1-\mathrm{Re}(z\overline{w}))
, so when
f
and

g
take values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we have

\mathbb{D}(f,g;A,B)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{A<p\leq B}\frac{|f(p)-g(p)|^{2}}{p}\right)^{1/2}.

Definition 2.1
.

A
1
-bounded multiplicative function
f
is called
pretentious
if there exists a Dirichlet character
\chi
and a real number
t
, such that
\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it})<+\infty
. Otherwise,
f
is called
non-pretentious
or
aperiodic
.

A multiplicative function
f
is called strongly aperiodic if for any
B>0
,

(9)

\lim_{X\to\infty}\inf_{|t|\leq BX,q\leq B}\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X)=+\infty,

where the infimum is taken over all Dirichlet characters
\chi
of conductor

q
with
q\leq B
.

Throughout, whenever we have an expression as in (
9
),
it is implicit that infimum is taken over all Dirichlet characters
\chi

of conductor
q
with
q\leq B
.

The term aperiodic is related to the following consequence of Halász’s
theorem
[
19
]
: for a 1- bounded multiplicative function
f{:}\;\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{U}
, if
\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it})=+\infty
for all Dirichlet
characters
\chi
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{X}\sum_{n\leq X}f(an+b)=0

for all
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
.
If we consider logarithmic averages instead, then one only
needs to consider the distances in the case where
t=0
, since otherwise
the associated average vanishes.
More specifically, we recall an elementary bound for logarithmic means of bounded multiplicative functions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f{:}\;\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{U}
be a multiplicative function.
Then, we have

(10)

\left|{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f(n)\right|\ll\exp\Bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}f(p)}{p}\Bigg).

This follows from
[
18
, Proposition 1.2.6]
.
Roughly, it asserts that if a function does not pretend to be 1, then its
logarithmic means vanish.
We have the following corollary, which is an extension of the previous
lemma along arithmetic progressions.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
Q
be a positive integer and
f{:}\;\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{U}
be a
multiplicative function such that
\mathbb{D}(f,\chi)=+\infty
for all
Dirichlet characters of modulus
Q
. Then, we have

(11)

\max_{1\leq a\leq Q,(a,Q)=1}\left|{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f(Qn+a)\right|={\rm o}_{f,Q;X\to\infty}(1),

where the error term may depend on
f,Q
.

Proof.

We use the expansion

{\bf 1}_{a\pmod{Q}}(n)=\frac{1}{\phi(Q)}\sum_{\chi\pmod{Q}}\chi(n)\overline{\chi(a)},

where
\phi
denotes Euler’s totient function,
and apply the previous lemma to the functions
f\overline{\chi}
, which are not
1
-pretentious.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

A multiplicative function
f{:}\;\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
is called finitely generated if the set

\{f(p){:}\;p\in\mathbb{P}\}
is finite.

The upside of working with finitely generated functions hinges on the next proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}
be a non-pretentious multiplicative function. If
f
is
finitely generated, then it is strongly aperiodic.

We postpone the proof of this result to the
Appendix

A
. In fact, we shall establish strong
aperiodicity for a larger class of multiplicative functions in
\mathcal{M}
,
including, for instance, all aperiodic multiplicative functions
for which the set

\{f(p){:}\;p\in\mathbb{P}\}
is not dense in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

A particular case of finitely generated functions are functions that take finitely many values on the unit circle. We call these multiplicative functions
finite valued
. It is straightforward to check that a multiplicative function is finite valued if and only if there exists a positive integer
k
such that
f^{k}\equiv 1
. The following is immediate from Proposition
2.5
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
f
be a non-pretentious multiplicative function that is finite valued. Then,
f
is strongly aperiodic.

From this, we further deduce the following result.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
f
be a non-pretentious multiplicative function such that
f^{\ell}
is pretentious for some positive integer
\ell
. Then
f
is strongly aperiodic.

Proof.

Our assumption implies that there exist
t\in\mathbb{R}
and a Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\mathbb{D}(f^{\ell}(n),\chi(n)n^{it})<+\infty
. By raising to an appropriate power (to make
\chi^{m}
principal), we may assume that
\mathbb{D}(f^{\ell}(n),n^{it})<+\infty
.

Claim
: Given
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, there is
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,\ell-1\}
so that
|a-e(j/\ell)|\leq\frac{2\pi}{4\ell}|a^{\ell}-1|.

Proof of the Claim.

Write
a=e(\theta)
for some
\theta\in[0,1)
. Take
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,\ell-1\}
so that
\left\|\theta-j/\ell\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}=\min_{0\leq k\leq\ell-1}\left\|\theta-k/\ell\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}
.
Then it is not difficult to see that
\theta\in((j-1)/\ell,j/\ell]

or
\theta\in[j/\ell,(j+1)/\ell)
, where the intervals are taken
\pmod{1}
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
\theta\in((j-1)/\ell,j/\ell].

Then we have that
|a-e(j/\ell)|\leq 2\pi\left\|\theta-j/\ell\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}
.
The map
\psi{:}\;\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T},\psi(x)=\ell x
, when restricted to

((j-1)/\ell,j/\ell]
is invertible, and its inverse is Lipschitz
continuous with Lipschitz
constant
1/\ell
, and therefore

\left\|\theta-j/\ell\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\frac{1}{\ell}\left\|\ell\theta\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}
.

We deduce that
|a-e(j/\ell)|\leq\frac{2\pi}{\ell}\left\|\ell\theta\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\frac{\pi}{2\ell}|e(\ell\theta)-1|=\frac{\pi}{2\ell}|a^{\ell}-1|
, where for the second inequality we use that
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\left\|x\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\frac{1}{4}|e(x)-1|.
This concludes the
proof of the Claim.

∎

Using the Claim, we deduce that for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
, there exists
\theta_{p}\in\{0,1,\ldots,\ell-1\}
so that
|f(p)p^{-it/\ell}-e(\theta_{p}/\ell)|\leq\frac{\pi}{2\ell}|f^{\ell}(p)p^{-it}-1|
. Consider
the completely multiplicative function
g
defined on the primes by
g(p)=e(\theta_{p}/\ell)
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(f(n)n^{-it/\ell},g(n))^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\frac{|f(p)p^{-it/\ell}-e(\theta_{p}/\ell)|^{2}}{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\pi^{2}}{4\ell^{2}}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\frac{|f^{\ell}(p)p^{-it}-1|^{2}}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4\ell^{2}}\mathbb{D}(f^{\ell}(n),n^{it})^{2}<\infty.

Therefore,
g
pretends to be
f(n)n^{-it/\ell}
and, thus it is
non-pretentious. However,
g
is also finite valued, so Corollary

2.6
implies that it is
strongly aperiodic. Therefore,
f(n)n^{-it/\ell}
, and thus
f
, is
strongly aperiodic. Indeed, suppose there exist
B>0
and an increasing
sequence of integers
(N_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\inf_{|t^{\prime}|\leq BN_{k},q\leq B}\mathbb{D}(f(n)n^{-it/\ell},\chi(n)n^{it^{\prime}};1,N_{k})
is bounded. Then, the
pretentious triangle inequality implies that

\inf_{|t^{\prime}|\leq BN_{k},q\leq B}\mathbb{D}(g(n),\chi(n)n^{it^{\prime}};1,N_{k})
will be bounded, which is a
contradiction.
∎

Finally, we will use the following lemma, an easy consequence of the triangle inequality.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
p,q
be integers and let
f
be a multiplicative function such that
f^{p}
and
f^{q}
are both pretentious. Then
f^{d}
is pretentious, where
d=(p,q)
.

Proof.

Let
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
with
kp-\ell q=d
. If
\mathbb{D}(f^{p}(n),\chi_{1}(n)n^{it_{1}})<+\infty
and
\mathbb{D}(f^{q}(n),\chi_{2}(n)n^{it_{2}})<+\infty
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(f^{d}(n),\chi_{1}^{k}(n)\overline{\chi_{2}^{\ell}(n)}n^{i(kt_{1}-\ell t_{2})})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{D}(f^{kp}(n),\chi_{1}^{k}(n)n^{ikt_{1}})+\mathbb{D}(f^{\ell q}(n),\chi_{2}^{\ell}(n)n^{i\ell t_{2}})

\displaystyle\leq k\mathbb{D}(f^{p}(n),\chi_{1}(n)n^{it_{1}})+\ell\mathbb{D}(f^{q}(n),\chi_{2}(n)n^{it_{2}})<+\infty.

∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Assume that
f
is a multiplicative function such that
f^{k}
is pretentious for some positive integer
k
. Then, there exists a positive integer
\ell_{0}
such that
f^{\ell}
is pretentious if and only if
\ell_{0}\mid\ell
.

Proof.

Take
\ell_{0}
to be the minimal positive integer such that
f^{\ell_{0}}
is pretentious and apply the Lemma
2.8
for
\ell
and
\ell_{0}
to conclude that
\ell_{0}\mid\ell
.
∎

Finally, we will need a way to control the distances of aperiodic functions that are not necessarily strongly aperiodic. This can be done by passing to an appropriate subsequence. The central lemma we will need is the following lemma of Elliott
[
10
, Lemma 17]
which asserts that non-pretentious functions cannot pretend to be different twisted characters on all scales. The proof is a simple exercise in applying the pretentious triangle inequality, coupled with some estimates for the distances between twisted characters. For a generalization, see
[
24
, Lemma 5.3]
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
f
be a
1
-bounded multiplicative function such that for all
X
, we have that

\inf_{|t|\leq BX,q\leq B}\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X)

is bounded. Then, there exists
t\in\mathbb{R}
and a Dirichlet character
\chi
of modulus at most
B
, such that

\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it})<+\infty
.

The following corollary is straightforward.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
B>0
and let
f
be a
1
-bounded non-pretentious multiplicative
function. Then, there exists a
sequence of integers
N_{k}\to\infty
, such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\inf_{|t|\leq BN_{k},q\leq B}\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,N_{k})=+\infty.

2.3.
Some asymptotic estimates for multiplicative functions

The following lemma is a fairly standard consequence of (
7
).

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
f
be a bounded completely multiplicative function. Then,

(1-f(p)){\mathbb{E}}_{n\in\Phi_{K}}f(n)\ll 2^{-K}

holds for any prime
p
dividing all the numbers in
\Phi_{K}
, where the implied constant is absolute.

We deduce that if a multiplicative function is not identically
1
,
then its averages along
(\Phi_{K})_{K\in\mathbb{N}}
vanish.

A key idea in our proof strategy is to consider averages along a suitable arithmetic progression, where the steps will be smooth numbers from
\Phi_{K}
.
This is because we can approximate pretentious multiplicative functions by simpler expressions using the following concentration estimate.

Lemma 2.13

(Cf.
[
23
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

Let
f{:}\;\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{U}
be multiplicative and let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character of conductor
q
. Let
Q,K
be positive integers, such that
\prod_{j\leq K}p_{j}|Q
and
q|Q
. Then, for any
a\in\mathbb{N}
coprime to
Q
and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(12)

\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{1}{n}\left|f(Qn+a)-\chi(a)(Qn)^{it}\exp(F(Q,X))\right|\\
\ll\log X\left(\mathbb{D}(f,\chi(n)n^{it};p_{K},X)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{K}}}+{\rm o}_{a,t,Q;X\to\infty}(1)\right)

where

(13)

F(Q,X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid Q\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)\overline{\chi(p)}p^{-it}-1}{p}.

We note here that the implicit constant in (
12
)
is absolute.

The result in
[
23
]
uses Cesáro averages, but the same proof works with logarithmic averages with a logarithmic version of the Turán-Kubilius inequality.

The previous lemma is useful whenever the term
\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it},K,X)
is small and, in particular, for pretentious
functions
f
(with the appropriate choice of
\chi
and
t
).

Finally, we will need the following result of Tao on the two-point correlations of multiplicative functions.

Theorem A
.

[
30
, Theorem 1.3]

Let
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}
be natural numbers such that
a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}\neq 0
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and suppose that
B
is sufficiently large depending on
\varepsilon,a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}
.
Let
f,g{:}\;\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be
1
-bounded multiplicative functions. Then, for any
X\geq B
, if

\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X)\geq B

for all Dirichlet characters of modulus at most
B

and all real numbers
|t|\leq BX
, then

\left|{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f(a_{1}n+b_{1})g(a_{2}n+b_{2})\right|\leq\varepsilon.

This result illustrates the necessity to consider strongly aperiodic functions as separate from general aperiodic functions. In fact, it is known that there are examples of non-pretentious functions
[
26
]
(see also
[
24
]
),
which locally pretend to be
n^{it_{k}}
on a sequence of scales
X_{k}\to\infty
and a fast-growing sequence of positive real numbers
(t_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. These functions are non-pretentious and satisfy

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X_{k}}f(a_{1}n+b_{1})g(a_{2}n+b_{2})=1.

Since these functions are non-pretentious, one can show using Corollary
2.11
and Theorem
A
that there is a subsequence along which the averages are very small. We use this crucially in the proof of
1.1
. However, when one is dealing with several non-pretentious functions at the same time, it is not clear that one can find a subsequence that works for all functions simultaneously. This is the main reason that we have to impose the finitely generated assumption in Theorem
1.5
.

3.
Optimal conditions for multiplicative recurrence

In this section, we prove the implication
(2)\implies(3)
of Theorem
1.1
, i.e., we prove that for

a,c\in\mathbb{N}
and
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
with
(a,b,c,d)=1
, if for every
f\in\mathcal{M}
,

(14)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}|f(an+b)-f(cn+d)|=0,

then we must have
a=c
, and
b=d
or
a\mid bd
.

First, we prove that given two sequences
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

of natural numbers, if the set
R=\left\{{p_{n}}/{q_{n}}{:}\;n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
is a set of multiplicative recurrence, then for all
f\in\mathcal{M}
,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}|f(p_{n})-f(q_{n})|=0.

This implies that for the set

S=\left\{(an+b)/(cn+d){:}\;n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
to be a set
of multiplicative recurrence, we must have
a=c
, and

b=d
or
a\mid bd
.
We conjecture that these assumptions on
a,b,c
are also
sufficient for multiplicative recurrence for general systems.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two sequences of natural numbers.
If
S=\left\{{p_{n}}/{q_{n}}{:}\;n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
is a set of multiplicative recurrence, then for all
f\in\mathcal{M}

(15)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}|f(p_{n})-f(q_{n})|=0.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
f{:}\;\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a completely multiplicative
function. Consider the unit circle with the Borel

\sigma
-algebra and the Lebesgue measure, and the multiplicative
system on this space generated by the transformations

T_{n}{:}\;\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
T_{n}z=f(n)z
for every

n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Let
A
be any arc of length
\varepsilon/2
in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Observe that

\mu(A)>0
and since
R
is a set of multiplicative recurrence, there exists
a sequence
n_{k}\to\infty
so that for
all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mu(T^{-1}_{p_{n_{k}}}A\cap T^{-1}_{q_{n_{k}}}A)>0.

From the previous we get that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
z_{k}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that
T_{p_{n_{k}}}z_{k}\in A
and
T_{q_{n_{k}}}z_{k}\in A
.
By the choice of
A
, this implies that
|T_{p_{n_{k}}}z_{k}-T_{q_{n_{k}}}z_{k}|<\varepsilon
, or equivalently

\left|f(p_{n_{k}})-f(q_{n_{k}})\right|<\varepsilon

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Given
a,c\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
we want to find necessary conditions on these parameters so that the set

S=\big\{(an+b)/(cn+d){:}\;n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
is a set of multiplicative recurrence.
In view of Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to examine under what conditions (
14
) holds for the quadruple
(a,b,c,d)
.
These will arise by considering (
14
) for modified characters. Here, a
(twisted) modified character
is a multiplicative function
f\in\mathcal{M}
, such that there exist
t\in\mathbb{R}
and a Dirichlet character
\chi
such that

f(p)=\chi(p)p^{it}
for all primes
p
with
\chi(p)\neq 0
.
We know that
\chi(p)=0
for at most finitely many primes, so by
modifying the values of
\chi
at those primes we get a function

\widetilde{\chi}\in\mathcal{M}
with
f(p)=\widetilde{\chi}(p)p^{it}
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
.

The first condition is that we must have
a=c
. This condition was already observed in
[
9
, Proposition 3.2]
. We provide a different proof for convenience.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
a,c\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
and assume that (
14
) holds
for all
f\in\mathcal{M}
. Then
a=c
.

Proof.

We pick
f(n)=n^{it}
and use the fact that
(u_{1}n+u_{2})^{it}=(u_{1}n)^{it}+{\rm O}_{t,u_{1},u_{2}}(1/n)
for all integers
u_{1},u_{2}
. We conclude that

\displaystyle|f(an+b)-f(cn+d)|

\displaystyle=|(an+b)^{it}-(cn+d)^{it}|

\displaystyle=|(an)^{it}-(cn)^{it}|+{\rm o}_{n\to\infty}(1)=|(ac^{-1})^{it}-1|+{\rm o}_{n\to\infty}(1).

If
a\neq c
, then there is
t
so that
(ac^{-1})^{it}=-1
, and using
the previous we see that (
14
) cannot hold for

f(n)=n^{it}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Hence, from now on, we assume that
a=c
, and we study the set

S=\left\{\frac{an+b}{an+d}{:}\;n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

with
(a,b,d)=1
. If
b=d
, (
14
) is satisfied trivially, and
also
S=\{1\}
, which is trivially a set of multiplicative recurrence.
Therefore, we also assume that
b\neq d
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
with
(a,b,d)=1
,
b\neq d

and assume that
a
does not divide
bd
. Then, there is a function
f\in\mathcal{M}
for which (
14
) fails.

Proof.

Since
a\nmid bd
, there is

p\in\mathbb{P}
such that
p^{k}\parallel a
,
p^{\ell}\parallel bd
and
k>\ell
.

First of all, we consider the case
\ell=0
.
Then,
p|a
,
p\nmid b
and
p\nmid d
.

If
p>2
, we
take
u\in\mathbb{N}
such
that
b\not\equiv d\pmod{p^{u}}
. As
p
is an odd prime, the multiplicative
group
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{u}}^{\times}
is cyclic. Let
g
be a generator. Then
for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{u}}^{\times}
, there is a unique
r\in\{0,1,\dots,\phi(p^{u})-1\}
so that
m=g^{r}
, where
\phi
denotes
Euler’s totient function. Consider the Dirichlet character

\chi\mod p^{u}
defined by
\chi(g^{r})=e(r/\phi(p^{u}))
.
For any distinct
r_{1},r_{2}\in\{0,1,\ldots,\phi(p^{u})-1\}
, we have
|e(r_{1}/\phi(p^{u}))-e(r_{2}/\phi(p^{u}))|\geq 2\pi/\phi(p^{u})
.
Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
an+b\not\equiv an+d\pmod{p^{u}}
and

(an+b,p^{u})=(an+d,p^{u})=1
. We infer that
\chi(an+b),\chi(an+d)\in\{e(r/\phi(p^{u})){:}\;r\in\{0,1,\dots,\phi(p^{u})-1\}\}
and

\chi(an+b)\neq\chi(an+d)
. Thus,
|\chi(an+b)-\chi(an+d)|\geq 2\pi/\phi(p^{u})
.
Now, consider the modified Dirichlet character
\widetilde{\chi}
defined on
the primes by

\widetilde{\chi}(q)=\begin{cases}\chi(q),&q\neq p\\
1,&q=p\end{cases}.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\nmid an+b,an+d
. Thus,
\widetilde{\chi}(an+b)=\chi(an+b)
and
\widetilde{\chi}(an+d)=\chi(an+d)

(1),
that is, for
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
with

(a,b,d)=1
, if
b=d
or
a\mid bd
, then for all
f\in\mathcal{M}
and
\varepsilon>0

the set
\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)
defined in (
3
) has positive
upper logarithmic
density. Furthermore, if
f
is pretentious or finitely generated, then

\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)
has positive lower logarithmic density.

In case
b=d
, then for all

and
\varepsilon>0

\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)=\mathbb{N}
, so the
desired conclusions follow trivially.
Therefore, from now on we assume without loss of generality that
b\neq d

and
a\mid bd
. Then our results follow from the following proposition:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(a,b,d)=1
,
b\neq d
and
a\mid bd
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0

\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}{:}\;\left|f(an+b)-f(an+d)\right|<\varepsilon\right\}

In order to prove Proposition

,

not some power of

for some

.
Then Proposition
5.1
follows from
Propositions
5.2
and
5.3
below:

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(a,b,d)=1
,

and

a\mid bd
. Let
f\in\mathcal{M}
, and assume that there are no

and

is identically equal to

. Then for any

\varepsilon>0

the set

\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}{:}\;\left|f(an+b)-f(an+d)\right|<\varepsilon\right\}

has positive upper logarithmic density. Furthermore, if
f
is pretentious or finitely generated, it has positive lower logarithmic

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(a,b,d)=1
,
b\neq d
and

a\mid bd
. Let
f\in\mathcal{M}
, and assume that there are
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

t\in\mathbb{R}
so that
f^{k}
is identically equal to
n^{it}
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0

\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}{:}\;\left|f(an+b)-f(an+d)\right|<\varepsilon\right\}

5.1.
An approximation of the indicator function
\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\varepsilon]\cup(1-\varepsilon,1)}
on
\mathbb{T}

We will need an approximation of
the indicator function of the interval
[0,\varepsilon)\cup(1-\varepsilon,1)
by
trigonometric polynomials. Consider a continuous function
h_{\varepsilon}{:}\;\mathbb{T}\to[0,1]
so that
h_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\varepsilon]\cup(1-\varepsilon,1)}(x)
and

\int_{0}^{1}h_{\varepsilon}(x)dx=\varepsilon
(for example we can take
h_{\varepsilon}
to be
an appropriate trapezoid function).

Since
h_{\varepsilon}
is continuous, we know that the Cesàro averages of its
Fourier partial sums converge uniformly to
h_{\varepsilon}
. Namely, there exists a
sequence
(c_{\ell,R})_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z},R\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{C}
, such that

(37)

\left\|h_{\varepsilon}(x)-\sum_{|\ell|<R}c_{\ell,R}e(\ell x)\right\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\left|h_{\varepsilon}(x)-\sum_{|\ell|<R}c_{\ell,R}e(\ell x)\right|\to 0

as
R\to\infty
, where
c_{0,R}=\int_{0}^{1}h_{\varepsilon}(x)dx=\varepsilon
.
Then, we may pick
R
sufficiently large so that the quantity in
(
37
) is smaller than
\varepsilon^{2}
, and if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small,
for this
R
we have

(38)

h_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq\varepsilon+\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}c_{\ell,R}e(\ell x)\right)-\varepsilon^{2}\geq\varepsilon^{2}+\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}c_{\ell,R}e(\ell x)\right)

for all
x\in[0,1]
. From now on, we denote
c_{\ell,R}
simply by
c_{\ell}
for convenience.

5.2.
The proof of Proposition
5.2

We are now ready to prove Proposition
5.2
.
Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(a,b,d)=1
,
b\neq d
and
a\mid bd
.
Fix a function
f\in\mathcal{M}
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
n
be a natural number not in
\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)
, so that

(39)

\left|f(an+b)-f(an+d)\right|\geq\varepsilon.

This implies that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\arg\left(f(an+b)\overline{f(an+d)}\right)\notin\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi},\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi}\right),

where we use the principal branch of the argument function. Redefining
\varepsilon
to absorb the constant
1/(2\pi)
, we have that

n\in\mathcal{A}\iff\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\arg\left(f(an+b)\overline{f(an+d)}\right)\right\}\in[0,\varepsilon)\cup(1-\varepsilon,1),

where
\big\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\arg\big(f(an+b)\overline{f(an+d)}\big)\big\}
denotes the fractional part of
\frac{1}{2\pi}\arg\big(f(an+b)\overline{f(an+d)}\big)
.
Our task is to show that

(40)

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)}(n)>0.

5.2.1.
A reduction to binary correlations of multiplicative functions

We will now use the approximation carried out in Section

5.1
to reduce the problem to studying
binary correlations of multiplicative functions. Since

(41)

\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)}(n)=\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\varepsilon)\cup(1-\varepsilon,1)}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\arg\left(f(an+b)\overline{f(an+d)}\right)\right\}\right)\\
\geq h_{\varepsilon}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\arg\left(f(an+b)\overline{f(an+d)}\right)\right\}\right),

it suffices to show that

(42)

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}h_{\varepsilon}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\arg\left(f(an+b)\overline{f(an+d)}\right)\right\}\right)>0.

In order to show that (
42
) holds, it suffices to show that there exists
L\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq r_{L}\leq L-1
such that

(43)

\limsup_{X\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}h_{\varepsilon}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\arg\left(f(a(Ln+r_{L})+b)\overline{f(a(Ln+r_{L})+d)}\right)\right\}\right)>0.

This follows from the inequality

(44)

{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}a(Ln+r_{L})\leq L\left(1+{\rm O}\left(\frac{\log L}{\log X}\right)\right){\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq LX+r_{L}}a(n)

for any non-negative sequence
a(n)
, which is proved by dropping all the terms on the right-hand side that are not equal to
r_{L}
modulo
L
.

Applying (
38
), we see that (
43
) would follow if we could prove that

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}e\left(\ell\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\arg(f(a(Ln+r_{L})+b)\overline{f(a(Ln+r_{L})+d)})\right)\right)\right)>-\varepsilon^{2}.

Thus, we will establish our claim if we show that there exist

L\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq r_{L}\leq L-1
such that

(45)

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f^{\ell}\left(a(Ln+r_{L})+b\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(a(Ln+r_{L})+d\right)}\right)>-\varepsilon^{2}.

5.2.2.
Correlations of multiplicative functions along progressions

We will now use Proposition
4.1
to find

Q\in\mathbb{N}
so that (
45
) holds with

L=Q^{2}
and
r_{L}=r_{Q}
.

Let
v=(a,b)
and write
a=va_{0}
and
b=vb_{0}
. Since
a\mid bd
and

a,b,d
are coprime, we conclude that
(v,d)=1
. Furthermore, we have

a_{0}\mid b_{0}d\implies a_{0}\mid d
. Writing
d=d_{0}a_{0}
, we conclude that

(d_{0},v)=1
. By factoring out the greatest common divisors and
redefining
c_{\ell}
to absorb the terms

f^{\ell}(v)\overline{f^{\ell}(a_{0})}
, we can rewrite (
45
)
as

(46)

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Ln+a_{0}r_{L}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vLn+vr_{L}+d_{0}\right)}\right)>-\varepsilon^{2}.

We let
K
be a fixed large parameter and define
the set
\Phi_{K}
as in (
6
) and for each
Q\in\Phi_{K}
, we let
0\leq r_{Q}\leq Q^{2}-1
be the numbers provided by Proposition
4.1
. These numbers exist because the coprimality conditions
(a_{0},b_{0})=(v,d_{0})=(a_{0},v)=1
are satisfied.

We will prove that if
K
is sufficiently large, then for some

Q\in\Phi_{K}
we have that

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right)>-\varepsilon^{2}

In order to prove this, we will show that the double average

(47)

{\mathbb{E}}_{Q\in\Phi_{K}}\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X_{m}}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}

is very close to zero along some subsequence
X_{m}\to\infty
, for
K
large
and
m\to\infty
.
This would imply that there exists at least one
Q\in\Phi_{K}
for which

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X_{m}}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right)>-\varepsilon^{2}

holds.

5.2.3.
Separating pretentious and non-pretentious functions

We split
\{-R+1,\ldots,R-1\}\setminus\{0\}
into two sets
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{B}_{2}
consists of those integers
\ell\in[-R+1,R-1]\setminus\{0\}
such that
f^{\ell}
is pretentious. The sets
\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}
are symmetric around 0. We let
\mathcal{B}_{1,+}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k_{1}}\}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2,+}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k_{2}}\}
denote the sets of positive elements of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
respectively,
where all elements are written in increasing order.

We consider two cases:

i) Assume that
f^{a_{1}\ldots a_{k_{1}}}
is pretentious. Then, Corollary
2.7
implies that
f^{a_{i}}
is strongly aperiodic for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,k_{1}\}
. Thus, for any
B>0
we have

\lim\limits_{X\to\infty}\inf_{|t|\leq BX,q\leq B}\mathbb{D}(f^{a_{i}}(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X)=+\infty

for all
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{+}
.

Furthermore, if
f
is pretentious, then
f^{a_{1}\ldots a_{k_{1}}}
is pretentious and, thus, we always land in this case.

ii) Assume that
f^{a_{1}\ldots a_{k_{1}}}
is non-pretentious and let
B>0
be arbitrary. Then,
Corollary
2.11

implies that there exists an increasing sequence of integers

(X_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\inf_{|t|\leq(a_{1}\ldots a_{k})BX_{m},q\leq B}\mathbb{D}(f^{a_{1}\ldots a_{k_{1}}}(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X_{m})\to+\infty.

Using the triangle inequality, we deduce that for any

t\in[-BX_{m},BX_{m}]
and any character
\chi
of conductor at most
B
, we have

\mathbb{D}(f^{a_{i}}(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X_{m})\geq\frac{a_{1}\ldots a_{k_{1}}}{a_{i}}\mathbb{D}(f^{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}(n),\chi^{\frac{a_{1}\ldots a_{k_{1}}}{a_{i}}}(n)n^{it\frac{a_{1}\ldots a_{k_{1}}}{a_{i}}};1,X_{m})

which implies that for any
a_{i}
we have

\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\inf_{|t|\leq BX_{m},q\leq B}\mathbb{D}(f^{a_{i}}(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X_{m})=+\infty.

Furthermore, if
f
is finitely generated, then
f^{a_{1}\ldots a_{k_{1}}}
is finitely
generated and, thus, strongly aperiodic. In this case, we can take
X_{m}=m
for all

m\in\mathbb{N}
.

To summarize, we deduce (in both cases) that for any
B>0
, there exists a
sequence of integers
X_{m}\to\infty
such that

(48)

\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\inf_{|t|\leq BX_{m},q\leq B}\mathbb{D}(f^{a_{i}}(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X_{m})=+\infty

for all
a_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{1,+}
. Furthermore, when
f
is pretentious or finitely generated we can take
X_{m}=m
. From now on, we will work with averages
along
(X_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
and the constant
B
will be specified later.

We write

\displaystyle E_{1}(K,X_{m})=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}}_{Q\in\Phi_{K}}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X_{m}}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}

\displaystyle E_{2}(K,X_{m})=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{2}}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}}_{Q\in\Phi_{K}}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X_{m}}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}

and we will show that both expressions have real parts very close to zero.

5.2.4.
Concluding the proof

For
\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{2}
,
f^{\ell}
is pretentious, so by Proposition

4.1
we get that

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\left|{\mathbb{E}}_{Q\in\Phi_{K}}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right|=o_{K\to\infty}(1),

unless there exists
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f^{\ell}(n)=n^{it}
.
However, our assumptions on
f
in the hypothesis of Proposition

5.2

exclude this possibility and thus the last asymptotic holds
for
all
\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{2}
. Therefore, by picking
K
sufficiently large, we deduce that

(49)

\left|E_{2}(K,X_{m})\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}

for all
m
sufficiently large.

For this
K
, we have that for all
m
sufficiently large,

(50)

\left|{\mathbb{E}}_{Q\in\Phi_{K}}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{2}}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X_{m}}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right)\right|\\
=\left|\mathrm{Re}\left(E_{2}(K,X_{m})\right)\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4},

from which we obtain that there is some
Q\in\Phi_{K}
so that

(51)

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{2}}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X_{m}}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right)\geq-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}.

Now, from Theorem
A
we know that there exists
B^{\prime}>0
such
that if the 1-bounded multiplicative function
g
satisfies

\left|{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}g\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{g\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right|\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}{|c_{\ell}|}},

and
X
is sufficiently large, then

\inf_{|t|\leq B^{\prime}X,q\leq B^{\prime}}\mathbb{D}(g(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X)\leq B^{\prime}.

Applying this for all instances of
Q\in\Phi_{K}
, we find a
B^{\prime}
that works for all
Q
.

We pick our
B
above to be equal to
B^{\prime}
. Our construction of the sequence
(X_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
implies that for all

\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
, the function
f^{\ell}
satisfies
(
48
). Therefore, we have

\left|{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X_{m}}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right|<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}{|c_{\ell}|}}

for all
\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
and all
m
sufficiently large,
and thus

(52)

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left|\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X_{m}}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right)\right|<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}.

Combining (
51
) and (
52
) we
obtain that

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X_{m}}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right)>-\varepsilon^{2},

i.e., (
46
) holds with
L=Q^{2}
and
r_{L}=r_{Q}
. This
concludes the proof that
\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)
has positive upper logarithmic density.

5.2.5.
Positive lower logarithmic density

If
f
is pretentious or finitely generated, we have
seen that we can pick
X_{m}=m
. Working as in the previous case, we can find
some
K\in\mathbb{N}
and

so that

(53)

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{2}}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right)\geq-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}.

For each
\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{2}
the limit

\lim_{X\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}

exists (see
[

, Theorem 1.5]
or
[

, Theorem 2.14]
),
which implies that in (
53
) we can
replace the
\limsup_{X\to\infty}
by
\lim_{X\to\infty}
, so

(54)

\lim_{X\to\infty}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{2}}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}\frac{f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}}{n}\right)\geq-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}.

Then as in
5.2.4
, and since
X_{m}=m
,
we have that

(55)

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\left|\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}\frac{f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}}{n}\right)\right|<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}

so from (
54
) and
(
55
) we obtain that

\liminf_{X\to\infty}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{1\leq|\ell|<R}c_{\ell}\>{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq X}f^{\ell}\left(a_{0}Q^{2}n+a_{0}r_{Q}+b_{0}\right)\overline{f^{\ell}\left(vQ^{2}n+vr_{Q}+d_{0}\right)}\right)>-\varepsilon^{2}.

Combining the previous with (
41
) we infer that

(56)

\liminf_{X\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}^{\log}_{n\leq Q^{2}X+r_{Q}}}{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)}(n)}>0.

Then writing
Y\in\mathbb{N}
as
Y=Q^{2}\lfloor Y/Q^{2}\rfloor+r_{Y}
, where

, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq Y}{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)}(n)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\log\left({Q^{2}\left(\lfloor Y/Q^{2}\rfloor-1\right)+r_{Q}}\right)}{\log\left({Q^{2}\left(\lfloor Y/Q^{2}\rfloor-1\right)+r_{Q}}\right)+\log\left(\dfrac{Q^{2}\lfloor Y/Q^{2}\rfloor+r_{Y}}{Q^{2}\left(\lfloor Y/Q^{2}\rfloor-1\right)+r_{Q}}\right)}

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt{\mathbb{E}^{\log}}_{n\leq Q^{2}\left(\lfloor Y/Q^{2}\rfloor-1\right)+r_{Q}}{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(n)}

so then taking
\liminf_{Y\to\infty}
, and using that

\lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac{Q^{2}\left\lfloor Y/Q^{2}\right\rfloor+r_{Y}}{Q^{2}\left(\left\lfloor Y/Q^{2}\right\rfloor-1\right)+r_{Q}}=1,

as well as (
56
),
we get that
\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)
has positive lower logarithmic density.

5.3.
The proof of Proposition
5.3

Let us now give the proof of Proposition
5.3
. Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(a,b,d)=1
,
b\neq d
and
a\mid bd
. Fix a function
f\in\mathcal{M}
such that

f^{k}(n)=n^{it}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and some
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Also fix some

\varepsilon>0
. We will prove that the set
\mathcal{A}(f,\varepsilon)
appearing in
Proposition
5.3
has positive lower logarithmic density.

Similarly to the proof of Proposition
5.2
,
let
v=(a,b)
. Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
an+b=v(a_{0}n+b_{0}),an+d=a_{0}(vn+d_{0})

) by (
89
), we obtain that
if
\delta>0
and
\sigma
is a finite Borel measure on
\mathcal{M}^{\textup{fg}}
such that

\sigma(\{1\})\geq\delta^{2}
and

\int_{\mathcal{M}^{\textup{fg}}}f(r)\overline{f(s)}\penalty\ d\sigma(f)\geq 0
for every
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
,
then

(90)

\int_{\mathcal{M}^{\textup{fg}}}f(\ell n)\overline{f(n+1)}\penalty\ d\sigma(f)\geq\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}

for a set of
n\in\mathbb{N}
with positive lower density. As explained in the

, this
implies that the set

is a set of recurrence for finitely generated multiplicative systems.

Appendix A
Strong aperiodicity for a class of multiplicative functions

In this section, we establish that any finitely generated multiplicative function is either pretentious or strongly aperiodic. This will be a simple corollary of the following proposition.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
B>0
,
f\in\mathcal{M}
and
\chi
be a fixed Dirichlet character of conductor
q
.
Assume that there exists an open subset
U
of the unit circle

\mathbb{S}^{1}
, such that

(91)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
f(p)\in U\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=o_{X\to\infty}(\log\log X).

Then, we have

\inf_{t\leq BX}\mathbb{D}(f,\chi(n)n^{it};1,X)\gg_{q,B,U}1+\mathbb{D}(f,\chi;1,X).

This implies that if the multiplicative function
f
satisfies (
91
), then for the infimum over
t
to be small, we must have that the distance of
f
from
\chi
is small.

We recall several standard facts about Dirichlet
L
-functions. We adopt the usual conventions and write
s=\sigma+it
for a complex number.
Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character and throughout this section we assume all characters are primitive. The Dirichlet
L
-function
L(s,\chi):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi(n)n^{-s}
,
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1

has Euler product factorization

L(s,\chi)=\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}

valid on the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
. It possesses a meromorphic continuation to the entire complex plane and if
\chi
is principal, then it has only one pole of order
1
at
s=1
, otherwise, it is entire.

By the Vinogradov-Korobov zero free region for Dirichlet
L
-functions (see
[
27
, p. 176]
without proof, or
[
25
, Lemma 1]
), letting
q
be the modulus of
\chi
, we have that
L(\sigma+it,\chi)\neq 0
in the region

(92)

\left\{\sigma+it{:}\;\sigma>1-\frac{C}{\log{q}+(\log|t|)^{2/3}(\log\log|t|)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right\}

for all
|t|\geq 3
and for some absolute constant
C>0
.
Using standard methods (see for instance
[
32
, pp. 56-60]
for Landau’s method) this zero-free region yields the bound

(93)

\frac{L^{\prime}(\sigma+it,\chi)}{L(\sigma+it,\chi)}\ll\log{q}+(\log|t|)^{2/3}(\log\log|t|)^{1/3}.

in the same region.
Since
L(s,\chi)
does not vanish on this set, we can define a complex logarithm that is holomorphic on the zero-free region. When
\sigma>1
, we have that the identity

\log L(s,\chi)=-\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\log\left(1-\chi(p)p^{-s}\right),

holds, since this is valid for all real
s
with
s>1
.

Now, if
\chi
is principal, then we can use Taylor expansion to get that for
|t|\leq 1/100
(say) and
\sigma
close to
1
,

(94)

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)=\frac{C_{1,\chi}}{s-1}+C_{2,\chi}\gamma+{\rm O}(|s-1|)

where
C_{1,\chi},C_{2,\chi}
are some constants depending on
\chi
.

On the other hand, if
\chi
is non-principal, then the logarithmic derivative
L^{\prime}/L(s,\chi)
does not have poles in this region, which follows from the fact that this is a zero-free region for
L(s,\chi)
and
L(s,\chi)
is pole-free. Then, by continuity, for
|t|\ll 1
and
\sigma
close to
1
, we have

(95)

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)\ll 1.

Finally, for any non-principal Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
q
, we have the classic bound (see
[
31
, Theorem 8.18]
) asserting that for
\sigma\geq 1
,

(96)

L(s,\chi)\ll\log(q|t|).

The main inputs in our method are the following bounds for the sums
\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\chi(p)p^{-1-ia}
where
|a|
can be as large as
X
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
X\geq 10
and set
Y=\exp\left((\log{X})^{C}\right)
for some constant
2/3<C<1
. Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character of conductor
q\geq 1
.

a)

Uniformly for all
(\log X)^{-1}\leq|a|\leq X
, we have

\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}\ll_{q}1.

b)

Uniformly for all
|a|\leq(\log X)^{-1}
, we have

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(\chi(p)p^{-ia})}{p}\ll_{q}1,&\text{if}\penalty\ \chi\penalty\ \text{is principal},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\mathrm{Re}(\chi(p)p^{-ia})}{p}\ll_{q}1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

All asymptotic constants are allowed to depend on
\chi
, or equivalently to
q
(since there are finitely many Dirichlet characters of conductor
q
). First of all, we observe that

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}=\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia}}+{\rm O}(1)

Thus, for the given
X
and
Y
, we have

\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}=\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia}}-\sum_{p\leq Y}\left(\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia}}-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+\frac{1}{\log Y}+ia}}\right)+{\rm O}(1).

Using the mean value theorem, we easily deduce that each term is the second sum is
{\rm O}\left(\log p(\log Y)^{-3}\right)
and thus the whole sum is
{\rm O}\left((\log Y)^{-2}\right)
by Mertens’ theorem. Taking logarithms in the Euler product of
L(s,\chi)
(using the branch defined above) and then using Taylor expansion, we deduce that

\displaystyle\log\left(L\left(1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia,\chi\right)\right)=-\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\log\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia}}+{\rm O}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia}}+{\rm O}(1).

Finally, we have that

\sum_{p\geq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia}}\ll 1

which can be obtained by splitting into dyadic intervals and summing the inequalities

\left|\sum_{2^{k}\leq p<2^{k+1}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{2^{k}\leq p<2^{k+1}}\frac{1}{X^{\frac{1}{\log X}}}\ll\frac{1}{2^{k}}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{k}\right),

by Mertens’ theorem.
In conclusion, we arrive at the formulas

(97)

\log\left(L\left(1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia,\chi\right)\right)=\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}+{\rm O}(1)

and

\log\left(L\left(1+\frac{1}{\log X}+ia,\chi\right)\right)-\log\left(L\left(1+\frac{1}{\log Y}+ia,\chi\right)\right)=\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}+{\rm O}(1).

Therefore, we can rewrite our sum as

(98)

\left|\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}\right|=\left|\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log u}+ia,\chi\right)\frac{du}{u(\log u)^{2}}\right|+{\rm O}(1).

a) We prove the first assertion of the lemma. Firstly, assume that
3\leq|a|\leq X
. Then, we use (
98
) and (
93
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}\right|

\displaystyle\ll\int_{Y}^{X}\left(\log{q}+(\log|a|)^{2/3}(\log\log|a|)^{1/3}\right)\frac{du}{u(\log u)^{2}}+1

\displaystyle\ll(\log X)^{2/3}(\log\log X)^{1/3}\left(\frac{1}{\log Y}-\frac{1}{\log X}\right).

By our assumptions, we have
\log Y=(\log X)^{C}
, which implies the desired bound.

Now, suppose that
(\log X)^{-1}\leq|a|\leq 3
.
If
\chi
is non-principal, we use (
98
) and (
95
) to infer that

\left|\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}\right|\ll\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{u(\log u)^{2}}+1=\frac{1}{\log{Y}}-\frac{1}{\log{X}}+1\ll 1.

If
\chi
is principal,
observe that if
1/100\leq|a|\leq 3
, then we have
\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log u}+ia,\chi\right)\right|\asymp 1
. Thus, the result follows as before.
We now work in the range
(\log Y)^{-1}\leq|a|\leq\frac{1}{100}
.
We use (
94
) to write

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)=\frac{C_{\chi}}{s-1}+{\rm O}(1).

for some constant
C_{\chi}
depending only on
\chi
. Thus,

\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}=C_{\chi}\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{(\frac{1}{\log u}+ia)u(\log u)^{2}}+{\rm O}\left(\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{u\log^{2}u}\right)+{\rm O}(1)

The integral in the second term is
{\rm O}((\log Y)^{-1})
and therefore we can rewrite the last equality as

\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}=\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{({1}+ia\log u)u\log u}+{\rm O}(1)

We rewrite the previous integral as

\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{1-ia\log u}{\left(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2}\right)u\log u}du=\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{\left(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2})\right)u\log u}-ia\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{\left(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2}\right)u}.

It suffices to show that each integral is
{\rm O}(1)
.
Using the inequality
|a|\log Y\geq 1
, we get that

\left|\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{\left(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2})\right)u\log u}\right|\leq\int\frac{du}{2u\log u}=\frac{1}{2\log Y}-\frac{1}{2\log X}\ll 1.

For the second integral, we use the inequality
1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2}\geq 2a\log u
to deduce that

\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{a}{\left(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2}\right)u}\,du\leq\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{2au(\log u)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2a}\left(\frac{1}{\log X}-\frac{1}{\log Y}\right)\ll 1,

since
|a|\log X\geq|a|\log Y\geq 1
.

Finally, assume that
(\log X)^{-1}\leq|a|\leq(\log Y)^{-1}
. We repeat the same argument as above and arrive at the equality

\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{1}{p^{1+ia}}=\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{\left(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2})\right)u\log u}-ia\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{\left(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2}\right)u}+{\rm O}(1).

Here, we calculate each integral.
For the first integral, we have

\displaystyle\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{\left(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2})\right)u\log u}=\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{(|a|\log u)^{\prime}}{(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2})|a|\log u}du=\int_{|a|\log Y}^{|a|\log X}\frac{dt}{t(1+t^{2})}

\displaystyle=\int_{|a|\log Y}^{|a|\log X}\left(\log t-\frac{1}{2}\log(t^{2}+1)\right)^{\prime}dt

\displaystyle=\log(|a|\log X)-\log(|a|\log Y)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\log(1+a^{2}\log^{2}X)-\log(1+a^{2}\log^{2}Y)\right)

\displaystyle=\log(|a|\log X)-\log\sqrt{1+a^{2}(\log X)^{2}}+{\rm O}(1),

where we used that
|a|\log Y
is bounded in the last line. We conclude that

\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{\left(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2})\right)u\log u}=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{|a|\log X}\right)+{\rm O}(1).

Since
|a|\log X\geq 1
, we deduce that the last quantity is
{\rm O}(1)
.
For the second integral, we have

a\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{\left(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2}\right)u}=\int\frac{(a\log u)^{\prime}}{(1+a^{2}(\log u)^{2})}du=\int_{a\log Y}^{a\log X}\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}\leq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}\ll 1.

We conclude that the second integral is bounded.

Combining everything together, we conclude that

\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}\ll 1

for principal
\chi
. This concludes the proof of a).

b) Now we prove the second part, where we always assume that
|a|\leq(\log X)^{-1}
.

Suppose first that
\chi
is principal. Note that

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+ia}}=\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1}{p^{1+ia}}+{\rm O}\left(\sum_{p\mid q}\frac{1}{p}\right)=\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1}{p^{1+ia}}+{\rm O}(\log\log(\omega(q))).

Hence it suffices to show that

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1-\cos(a\log p)}{p}\ll 1.

By Taylor expansion, we have that

\cos(a\log p)=1+{\rm O}(|a\log p|^{2})

Thus, the sum in question is bounded by
{\rm O}(1)
times

|a^{2}|\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p}

By Mertens’ theorem and partial summation, we have that

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p}\ll(\log X)^{2}.

Thus, the original sum is
{\rm O}(|a|^{2}(\log X)^{2})={\rm O}(1)
, as desired.

Finally, suppose that
\chi
is non-principal. Then by (
97
) and (
95
) we have

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\mathrm{Re}(\chi(p)p^{-ia})}{p}=\log\left|L\left(1+\frac{1}{\log{X}}+ia,\chi\right)\right|+{\rm O}(1)\ll\log\log(q|t|)+1\ll 1.

This concludes the proof of b), and hence the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Proposition
A.1
.

We prove the proposition in the case
B=1
. The general case follows by noting that the contribution of primes from
X
to
BX
in the distance function is
O(\log B)
, which is acceptable.

Let
f
be a function satisfying the assumptions of our main theorem. Since intervals generate the topology on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we easily see that the statement follows if we prove it in the case where
U
is a closed interval.

We want to bound

\inf_{|t|\leq X}\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(f(p)\overline{\chi(p)p^{it}})}{p}

from below.
We will consider two cases depending on the size of
t
and we will invoke Lemma
A.2
.

First of all, assume that
|t|\leq(\log X)^{-1}
.
Using the triangle inequality, we have that

\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X)\geq\mathbb{D}(f,\chi;1,X)-\mathbb{D}(1,n^{it};1,X)

Using part b) of Lemma
A.2
for the trivial character, we conclude that

\sup_{|t|\leq(\log X)^{-1}}\mathbb{D}(n^{it},1;1,X)\ll 1.

We deduce that

\inf_{|t|\leq(\log X)^{-1}}\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X)\geq\mathbb{D}(f,\chi;1,X)+{\rm O}(1),

so our claim follows.

Now, we assume that
(\log X)^{-1}\leq|t|\leq X
. We let
Y=\exp((\log X)^{C})
for some
2/3<C<1
.
Our assumption implies that there exists a closed interval
U
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\leq p\leq X\\
f(p)\in U\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}={\rm o}_{X\to\infty}(\log\log{X}).

We let
V_{\varepsilon}
be a sub-interval of
U^{c}
obtained by removing two intervals of length
\varepsilon
from its endpoints (where
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small depending on
\mu(U^{c})
).

We consider a smooth function
F
on the unit circle, such that
F(x)=0
for all
x\notin V_{\varepsilon}
,
F(x)\leq\mathbbm{1}_{V_{\varepsilon}}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and such that
\int F(t)\,dt>\mu(V_{\varepsilon})/2
. Then, we observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(f(p)\overline{\chi(p)}p^{-it})}{p}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{|f(p)-\chi(p)p^{it}|^{2}}{p}+{\rm O}(1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{|f(p)-\chi(p)p^{it}|^{2}}{p}+{\rm O}(1)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\leq p\leq X\\
\chi(p)p^{it}\in V_{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(p)-\chi(p)p^{it}|^{2}}{p}+{\rm O}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{Y\leq p\leq X}}\frac{|f(p)-\chi(p)p^{it}|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{V_{\varepsilon}}(\chi(p)p^{it})}{p}+{\rm O}(1)\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sum_{{Y\leq p\leq X}}\frac{F\left(\chi(p)p^{it}\right)}{p}+{\rm o}_{X\to\infty}(\log\log X).

Here, we used the fact that
f(p)\in U
for all primes outside a set of at most
o_{X\to\infty}(\log\log X)
primes, and thus
|f(p)-\chi(p)p^{it}|>\varepsilon
for all those primes.

Now, it suffices to show that

(99)

\sum_{{Y\leq p\leq X}}\frac{F(\chi(p)p^{it})}{p}\gg\log\log X

holds for all
|t|\geq(\log X)^{-1}
.
Then, the result would follow by combining the bounds from the two cases and noting that
\mathbb{D}(f,\chi;1,X)\ll\sqrt{\log\log X}
.

Since
F
is smooth, we know that

(100)

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\Big|F(z)-\sum_{-R\leq\ell\leq R}\widehat{F}(\ell)z^{\ell}\Big|=o_{R\to\infty}(1).

We pick
R
to be some large, but finite integer and we let
X,Y
be very large in terms of
R
.
Using this approximation and Mertens’ theorem, we rewrite the sum in (
99
) as

(101)

(1-C)\log\log X\int F(t)\,dt+\sum_{0<|\ell|\leq R}\widehat{F}(\ell)\left(\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi^{\ell}(p)p^{i\ell t}}{p}\right)+o_{R\to\infty}(1)\log\log X+{\rm O}(1).

Recall that
|t|\geq(\log X)^{-1}
and
\chi^{\ell}
has conductor bounded by
q
. Then, Lemma
A.2
implies that

\left|\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{\chi(p)p^{i\ell t}}{p}\right|\ll 1

for all
0<|\ell|\leq R
. Since
F
is bounded, we conclude that the sum in (
101
) is

(1-C+o_{R}(1))\log\log X\int F(t)\,dt+{\rm O}(1).

In particular, we conclude that

\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(f(p)p^{it})}{p}\gg\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}(1-C+o_{R\to\infty}(1))\log\log X\int F(t)dt+o_{X\to\infty}(\log\log X)\\
+{\rm O}(1),

which gives the desired conclusion.
∎

We can now prove Proposition
2.5
.

Proof.

Assume that
f
is a non-pretentious finitely generated function. We want to show that

\lim_{X\to\infty}\inf_{|t|\leq BX,q\leq B}\mathbb{D}(f(n),\chi(n)n^{it};1,X)=+\infty

for every
B>0
. Fixing a Dirichlet character
\chi
of modulus at most
B
, we want to show that

(102)

\lim_{X\to\infty}\inf_{{|t|\leq BX}}\mathbb{D}(f(n)\overline{\chi(n)},n^{it};1,X)=+\infty

By modifying our functions on the finitely many primes for which
\chi(p)=0
, we may assume that our function is in
\mathcal{M}
, while only perturbing the associated distances by an
{\rm O}(1)
term.
However, the function
f\cdot\overline{\chi}
is finitely generated, since
f
and
\chi
are. Therefore, it satisfies the assumptions of Proposition

for an interval that does not contain any of the finitely many values of

) holds and the result follows.

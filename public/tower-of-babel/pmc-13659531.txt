Roy Calne, an iconic liver transplanter and compassionate artist
Authors: Thomas M van Gulik
Published: 2026

The cover features a painting by the British surgeon Sir Roy Yorke Calne [1930–2024], entitled Liver Transplant (Figure 1). Roy Calne is renowned worldwide as the pioneer of liver transplantation who was the first in Europe to successfully transplant a donor liver into a patient in 1968 in Cambridge. On the other side of the Atlantic in the United States, the transplant pioneer Thomas Starzl carried out the first clinical liver transplant in 1963 in Denver, Colorado. Although they were competitors in the emerging field of organ transplantation, Calne and Starzl were friends learning from each other’s experiences, particularly in the development of immunosuppressive therapies.

Painting by Roy Y. Calne, entitled Liver Transplant [1991]. ©The artist’s estate, courtesy of the Royal College of Surgeons of England.

Roy Calne received his medical education at Guy’s Hospital Medical School in London and trained in surgery at the Royal Free Hospital (1,2). Calne showed an early interest in organ grafting and rejection, which was further strengthened during a research fellowship in 1960 at Peter Brent Brigham Hospital and Joseph Murray’s laboratory in Harvard Medical School, in Boston. In 1965, he was appointed Professor and Chair of Surgery at the University of Cambridge, where he started a clinical kidney transplantation program at Addenbrooke’s Hospital.

In Cambridge, Calne continued his research on organ grafting and rejection, focusing on immunological tolerance and immunosuppression (3). His close collaboration with immunologists led to the discovery of cyclosporin A (CsA) as a highly effective immunosuppressive agent, dramatically improving graft survival. The advent of CsA revolutionized organ transplantation and boosted the number of centers worldwide engaged in the field of transplantation. His further experimental and clinical work contributed to the development and application of the immunosuppressive agents FK506 (tacrolimus) and rapamycin (sirolimus).

Calne also set out to conduct extensive experimental work on orthotopic liver grafting in pigs to study mechanisms of immune tolerance of liver allografts. These laborious experiments enabled him and his team to refine the surgical techniques of explanting and implanting liver grafts. This foundational work culminated in the first liver transplantation performed in Cambridge at Addenbrooke’s Hospital in 1968.

In addition to his many landmark achievements in organ transplantation and science, Calne was an accomplished artist, working as both a painter and a sculptor. A highlight of his artistic endeavours was an exhibition of his paintings at the Barbican Center in London in 1991, titled The Gift of Life (4). This exhibition featured portraits of his patients as well as scenes from the operating theatre, depicted with striking empathy and humanity.

Roy Calne is remembered not only for his pioneering surgical and scientific achievements, but also for his remarkable artistic talent, through which he translated compassion into art.

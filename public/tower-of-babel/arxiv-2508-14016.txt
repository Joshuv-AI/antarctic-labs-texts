Abstract
. We study the variation of weighted Szegő and Garabedian kernels on planar domains as a function of the weight. A Ramadanov type theorem is shown to hold as the weights vary. As a consequence, we derive properties of the zeros of the weighted Szegő and Garabedian kernel for weights close to the constant function 1 1 on the boundary. We further study the weighted Ahlfors map and strengthen results concerning its boundary behaviour. Explicit examples of the weighted kernels are presented for certain classes of weights. We highlight an interesting property of the weighted Szegő and Garabedian kernels, implicit in Nehari’s work, and explore several of its consequences. Finally, we discuss the weighted Carathéodory metric, and describe relations of the weighted Szegő and Garabedian kernel with certain classical kernel functions.

1.
Introduction

Some aspects of weighted Szegő kernels on a finitely connected planar domain were considered in
[
7
]
. We continue this study by strengthening some of the results obtained therein on the variation of the weighted Szegő kernels as functions of the weight and noting some consequences about the zeros of the weighted Szegő kernel. This has direct consequences about the zeros of the weighted Garabedian kernel. Continuing along this path leads to the consideration of a weighted Ahlfors map (cf. Nehari
[
9
]
) associated with the given planar domain, and we are able to strengthen an observation made by Nehari in
[
9
]
about its boundary behaviour. We then provide explicit formulae for the weighted Szegő kernel, the weighted Garabedian kernel, and the weighted Ahlfors map for certain classes of weights. We conclude by highlighting a specific property about weighted Szegő and Garabedian kernels that is of independent interest but is implicit in
[
9
]
, and exploring a few interesting consequences. Finally, we discuss the weighted Carathéodory metric, as well as expressions for the weighted Szegő kernel and the weighted Garabedian kernel in terms of certain classical kernel functions.

To make all this precise and fix the notation, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a smoothly bounded
n
-connected domain without isolated boundary points and
\varphi
a positive real-valued
C^{\infty}
-smooth function on
\partial\Omega
. Following
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
, let
L^{2}_{\varphi}(\Omega)
be the Hilbert space of complex-valued functions on
\partial\Omega
that are square integrable with respect to the arc length measure
\varphi\,ds
. Here,
ds
is the differential of arc length and is given by
ds=|z^{\prime}(t)|\;dt
where
z=z(t)
is a smooth parametrization of
\partial\Omega
. In terms of the complex unit tangent vector function
T(z(t))=z^{\prime}(t)/|z^{\prime}(t)|
, it is seen that
dz=z^{\prime}(t)\;dt=Tds
. The inner product on
L^{2}_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega)
is

(1.1)

\langle u,v\rangle_{\varphi}=\int_{\partial\Omega}u\bar{v}\,\varphi\,ds\quad\text{for }u,v\in L^{2}_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega).

When
\varphi\equiv 1
, this reduces to the standard inner product
\langle u,v\rangle
. Note that
L^{2}_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega)=L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
as sets. Also,
C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
is dense in
L^{2}_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega)
with respect to its norm. For
u\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, the Cauchy transform of
u
is

(1.2)

(\mathcal{C}u)(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{u(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta,\quad z\in\Omega

which is holomorphic on
\Omega
. For
a\in\Omega
and
z\in\partial\Omega
, let
C_{a}(z)
be the conjugate of

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{T(z)}{z-a}.

Then,
C_{a}
is the Cauchy kernel which defines the Cauchy transform
\mathcal{C}
in the sense that
\mathcal{C}u(z)=\langle u,C_{z}\rangle
. The weighted Cauchy kernel is defined to be
\varphi^{-1}C_{a}
and the corresponding weighted Cauchy transform
\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}u
satisfies

(\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}u)(z)=\langle u,\varphi^{-1}C_{z}\rangle_{\varphi}=\langle u,C_{z}\rangle=(\mathcal{C}u)(z)

and this shows that
\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}=\mathcal{C}
.

Let
A^{\infty}(\Omega)
denote the space of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
that are in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
. The Cauchy transform
\mathcal{C}
maps
C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
into
A^{\infty}(\Omega)
and this allows the Cauchy transform to be viewed as an operator from
C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
into itself. Let
A^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
denote the set of functions on
\partial\Omega
that are the boundary values of functions in
A^{\infty}(\Omega)
. The Hardy space
H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is defined to be the closure in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
of
A^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
and members of the Hardy space are in one-to-one correspondence with the space of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
with
L^{2}
boundary values. This Hardy space can be identified in a natural way with the subspace of
L^{2}_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega)
, equal to the closure of
A^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
in that space. Thus, there is no need to define
H^{2}_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega)
separately. But we shall use the notation
H^{2}_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega)
whenever there is a need to emphasize that the Hardy space is endowed with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\varphi}
.

The orthogonal projection
P_{\varphi}
from
L^{2}_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega)
onto
H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is the weighted Szegő projection. For
a\in\Omega
, the evaluation functional
h\mapsto h(a)
on
H^{2}_{\varphi}(\Omega)
is continuous since

|h(a)|=|\langle h,C_{a}\rangle|=|\langle h,C_{a}\varphi^{-1}\rangle_{\varphi}|\leq\|C_{a}\varphi^{-1}\|_{\varphi}\|h\|_{\varphi}

and hence, there exists a unique function
S_{\varphi}(\cdot,a)\in H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
such that

(1.3)

h(a)=\langle h,S_{\varphi}(\cdot,a)\rangle_{\varphi}

for all
h\in H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. The function
S_{\varphi}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the weighted Szegő kernel of
\Omega
with respect to the weight
\varphi
.

It can be seen that the weighted Szegő kernel is hermitian symmetric, that is, for
z,w\in\Omega
, we have

S_{\varphi}(z,w)=\overline{S_{\varphi}(w,z)}.

Therefore,
S_{\varphi}(\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\times\Omega)
.

The weighted Szegő kernel is the kernel of the weighted Szegő projection because for all
u\in L^{2}_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega)
and
a\in\Omega

(1.4)

(P_{\varphi}u)(a)=\langle P_{\varphi}u,S_{\varphi}(\cdot,a)\rangle_{\varphi}=\langle u,S_{\varphi}(\cdot,a)\rangle_{\varphi}=\int_{\zeta\in\partial\Omega}S_{\varphi}(a,\zeta)u(\zeta)\varphi(\zeta)ds.

Note that for all
h\in H^{2}(\partial\Omega)

h(a)=(\mathcal{C}h)(a)=\langle h,C_{a}\rangle=\langle h,\varphi^{-1}C_{a}\rangle_{\varphi}=\langle h,P_{\varphi}(\varphi^{-1}C_{a})\rangle_{\varphi}.

Therefore, the weighted Szegő kernel
S_{\varphi}(\cdot,a)
is also given by the weighted Szegő projection of
\varphi^{-1}C_{a}
.
That is,

(1.5)

S_{\varphi}(z,a)=(P_{\varphi}(\varphi^{-1}C_{a}))(z)=\int_{\zeta\in\partial\Omega}S_{\varphi}(z,\zeta)C_{a}(\zeta)ds

for every
z\in\Omega
. In
[
7
]
, the following weighted Kerzman–Stein formula

(1.6)

P_{\varphi}(I+\mathcal{A}_{\varphi})=\mathcal{C}

was shown to hold on
C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, where
I
is the identity and
\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}=\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\varphi}
is the weighted Kerzman-Stein operator. Since the Cauchy transform maps
C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
into itself, it follows from the weighted Kerzman–Stein formula that the weighted Szegő projection
P_{\varphi}
also maps
C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
into itself. Thus,
S_{\varphi}(\cdot,a)\in A^{\infty}(\Omega)
. In fact,
S_{\varphi}(z,w)\in\left(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\right)\setminus\Delta
where
\Delta=\{(z,z):z\in\partial\Omega\}
is the diagonal in
\partial\Omega\times\partial\Omega
as Theorem
3.2
in
[
7
]
shows.

A function
u
in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is orthogonal to
H^{2}_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega)
if and only if
u\varphi
is orthogonal to
H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. All the functions
v\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
orthogonal to
H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
are of the form
\overline{HT}
where
H\in H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. If
v\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
then
H\in A^{\infty}(\Omega)
. So, the orthogonal decomposition of
\varphi^{-1}C_{a}
is given by

(1.7)

\varphi^{-1}C_{a}=S_{\varphi}(\cdot,a)+\varphi^{-1}\overline{H_{a}T}

where
H_{a}\in A^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Also, the above decomposition shows that
H_{a}
is holomorphic in
a\in\Omega
for fixed
z\in\partial\Omega
. The weighted Garabedian kernel of
\Omega
with respect to
\varphi
is defined by

(1.8)

L_{\varphi}(z,a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{z-a}-iH_{a}(z).

For a fixed
a\in\Omega
,
L_{\varphi}(z,a)
is a holomorphic function of
z
on
\Omega\setminus\{a\}
with a simple pole at
z=a
with residue
1/2\pi
, and extends
C^{\infty}
-smoothly to
\partial\Omega
. In addition,
L_{\varphi}(z,a)
is holomorphic in
a
on
\Omega
for fixed
z\in\partial\Omega
. Moreover, it is known that (see
[
9
]
)

(1.9)

L_{\varphi}(z,a)=-L_{1/\varphi}(a,z)

for
z,a\in\Omega
. Therefore, for a fixed
z\in\Omega
,
L_{\varphi}(z,a)
is a holomorphic function of
a
on
\Omega\setminus\{z\}
with a simple pole at
a=z
and residue
{1}/2\pi
, and extends
C^{\infty}
-smoothly to
\partial\Omega
. Finally, for
z\in\partial\Omega
,
a\in\Omega

S_{\varphi}(a,z)=\overline{S_{\varphi}(a,z)}=\frac{1}{\varphi(z)}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{T(z)}{z-a}-H_{a}(z)T(z)\right)=\frac{1}{i\varphi(z)}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{z-a}-iH_{a}(z)\right)T(z)

shows that the weighted Szegő kernel and the weighted Garabedian kernel satisfy the identity

(1.10)

S_{\varphi}(a,z)=\frac{1}{i\varphi(z)}L_{\varphi}(z,a)T(z).

It follows from Theorem
3.3
in
[
7
]
that the function
l_{\varphi}(z,w)
defined by

L_{\varphi}(z,w)=\frac{1}{2\pi(z-w)}+l_{\varphi}(z,w)

is holomorphic on
\Omega\times\Omega
and extends to be in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})
.

2.
Convergence of weighted Szegő kernels

The weights for which the weighted Szegő kernel is well-defined are studied in
[
10
]
. We will focus only on a special class of weights that are smooth and positive functions on the boundary and study the continuity of the map
\varphi\mapsto S_{\varphi}
. In
[
7
]
, it was shown that for a sequence of positive real-valued
C^{\infty}
functions
\varphi_{k}
on
\partial\Omega
such that
\varphi_{k}\rightarrow\varphi
in the
C^{\infty}
-topology on
\partial\Omega
,

(2.1)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=S_{\varphi}(z,w)\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}l_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=l_{\varphi}(z,w)

locally uniformly on
(\Omega\times\overline{\Omega})\cup(\overline{\Omega}\times\Omega)
. We extend this to the maximal possible set that is expected, in view of the fact that each Szegő kernel is in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})\setminus\Delta
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a
C^{\infty}
-smoothly bounded
n
-connected domain and
\varphi
a positive real-valued
C^{\infty}
smooth function on
\partial\Omega
.
Let
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive real-valued
C^{\infty}
functions on
\partial\Omega
such that
\varphi_{k}\rightarrow\varphi
in the
C^{\infty}
topology on
\partial\Omega
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Then

(2.2)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=S_{\varphi}(z,w)

locally uniformly on
(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})\setminus\Delta
.

Proof.

Let
z_{0},w_{0}\in\partial\Omega
be distinct arbitrary points. Choose relatively compact neighborhoods
U,V\subset\mathbb{C}
of
z_{0},w_{0}
respectively such that they both intersect only one of the boundary components of
\Omega
namely, the ones containing
z_{0},w_{0}
, and satisfy
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}=\emptyset
. Let
\tilde{U}=U\cap\overline{\Omega}
and
\tilde{V}=V\cap\overline{\Omega}
. It is sufficient to show that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=S_{\varphi}(z,w)

uniformly on
\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}
. Let
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a proper holomorphic map which is an
n
-to-one branched covering and which maps each boundary curve bijectively to the unit circle. Suppose that
f
has zeros at
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\in\Omega
with multiplicities
M(1),\ldots,M(N)
respectively. Note that
M(1)+\cdots+M(N)=n
. For an arbitrary weight
\psi
, let
S_{\psi}^{(0)}(z,w)
denote
S_{\psi}(z,w)
, and let
S_{\psi}^{\overline{n}}(z,w)
denote
(\partial^{n}/\partial{\overline{w}}^{n})S_{\psi}(z,w)
.
By Bell
[
3
]
,

(2.3)

S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-f(z)\overline{f(w)}}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{M(i)}\sum_{m=0}^{M(j)}c_{ijnmk}\,S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})\overline{S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(\bar{m})}(w,a_{j})}\right)

where the constants
c_{ijnmk}
are uniquely determined by the system of equations

(2.4)

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{m=0}^{M(j)}c_{ijnmk}\overline{S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(q,\bar{m})}(a_{l},a_{j})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}1&\text{if }i=l\text{ and }n=q\\
0&\text{if }i\neq l\text{ or }n\neq q\end{array}\right.

where
1\leq l\leq N
,
1\leq i\leq N
,
0\leq q\leq M(l)
and
0\leq n\leq M(i)
. Similarly,

(2.5)

S_{\varphi}(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-f(z)\overline{f(w)}}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{M(i)}\sum_{m=0}^{M(j)}c_{ijnm}\,S_{\varphi}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})\overline{S_{\varphi}^{(\bar{m})}(w,a_{j})}\right)

where the constants
c_{ijnm}
are uniquely determined by the system of equations

(2.6)

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{m=0}^{M(j)}c_{ijnm}\overline{S_{\varphi}^{(q,\bar{m})}(a_{l},a_{j})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}1&\text{if }i=l\text{ and }n=q\\
0&\text{if }i\neq l\text{ or }n\neq q\end{array}\right.

where
1\leq l\leq N
,
1\leq i\leq N
,
0\leq q\leq M(l)
and
0\leq n\leq M(i)
.
We can write the systems (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) of linear equations in matrix form as:

\mathcal{A}_{k}\mathcal{C}_{k}=\mathcal{B}\quad{and}\quad\mathcal{A}\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{B}

where
\mathcal{B}
is an
n\times 1
vector with entries
0
or
1
,
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is an
n\times n
matrix with entries
0
or
S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(q,\bar{m})}(a_{l},a_{j})
,

\mathcal{A}
is same as the matrix
\mathcal{A}_{k}
if we replace the entries
S_{\varphi_{k}}
by
S_{\varphi}
, and

\mathcal{C}_{k}
and
\mathcal{C}
are
n\times 1
vectors with entries
c_{ijnmk}
and
c_{ijnm}
respectively. The fact that
M(1)+\cdots+M(N)=n
is being used here.

Since
S_{\varphi_{k}}\rightarrow S_{\varphi}
locally uniformly (and so its derivatives) on
\Omega\times\Omega
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have that
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{-1}
uniformly as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore,
c_{ijnmk}\rightarrow c_{ijnm}
uniformly as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

If
z_{0}
and
w_{0}
lie on the same boundary curve then
1-f(z)\overline{f(w)}
does not vanish on
\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}
because
f
is bijective on each boundary component of
\partial\Omega
.

When
z_{0},w_{0}
lie on different boundary curves say,
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
respectively, choose
z_{1}\in\gamma_{1}
with
z_{1}\neq z_{0}
. By Bell–Kaleem
[
2
]
, there exists a proper holomorphic map
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
which maps both
z_{1}
and
w_{0}
to
1\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and is bijective on each boundary component of
\partial\Omega
. We can then shrink
U
, if necessary, to make sure that
1-f(z)\overline{f(w)}
does not vanish on
\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}
.

Since the zeros
a_{i}
lie in
\Omega
,
[
7
]
implies that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})=S_{\varphi}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(\bar{m})}(w,a_{j})=S_{\varphi}^{(\bar{m})}(w,a_{j})

uniformly for
z\in\tilde{U}
and
w\in\tilde{V}
respectively. These observations along with (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) show that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=S_{\varphi}(z,w)

uniformly on
\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}
. The convergence of the derivatives also follows similarly.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a
C^{\infty}
-smoothly bounded
n
-connected domain and
\varphi
a positive real-valued
C^{\infty}
smooth function on
\partial\Omega
.
Let
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive real-valued
C^{\infty}
functions on
\partial\Omega
such that
\varphi_{k}\rightarrow\varphi
in
C^{\infty}
topology on
\partial\Omega
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Then

(2.7)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}l_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=l_{\varphi}(z,w)

locally uniformly on
(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})\setminus\Delta
.

Proof.

As before, let
z_{0},w_{0}\in\partial\Omega
be distinct arbitrary points. Choose relatively compact neighborhoods
U,V\subset\mathbb{C}
of
z_{0},w_{0}
respectively such that they both intersect only one of the boundary components of
\Omega
namely, the ones containing
z_{0},w_{0}
, and satisfy
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}=\emptyset
. Let
\tilde{U}=U\cap\overline{\Omega}
and
\tilde{V}=V\cap\overline{\Omega}
. It is sufficient to show that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}l_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=l_{\varphi}(z,w)

uniformly on
\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}
. Let
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a proper holomorphic map which is an
n
-to-one branched covering and which maps each boundary curve bijectively to the unit circle. Suppose that
f
has zeros
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
with multiplicities
M(1),\ldots,M(N)
respectively.

If
z_{0}
and
w_{0}
lie on the same boundary component of
\partial\Omega
, then
f(z)-f(w)
does not vanish on
\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}
because
f
is bijective on each boundary component of
\partial\Omega
.

When
z_{0},w_{0}
lie on different boundary curves, say,
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
respectively, choose
z_{1}\in\gamma_{1}
with
z_{1}\neq z_{0}
. By Bell–Kaleem
[
2
]
, there exists a proper holomorphic map
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
which maps both
z_{1}
and
w_{0}
to
1\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. We can then shrink
U
, if necessary, to make sure that
f(z)-f(w)
does not vanish on
\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}
. We also make sure that none of the points
a_{i}
lie in
\tilde{V}
. Then, for
(z,w)\in\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}
,

(2.8)

S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-f(z)\overline{f(w)}}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{M(i)}\sum_{m=0}^{M(j)}c_{ijnmk}\,S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})\overline{S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(\bar{m})}(w,a_{j})}\right)

where the constants
c_{ijnmk}
are determined by (
2.4
). Similarly, for
(z,w)\in\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}
,

(2.9)

S_{\varphi}(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-f(z)\overline{f(w)}}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{M(i)}\sum_{m=0}^{M(j)}c_{ijnm}\,S_{\varphi}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})\overline{S_{\varphi}^{(\bar{m})}(w,a_{j})}\right)

where the constants
c_{ijnm}
are determined by (
2.6
).
We have seen in the proof of Theorem
2.1
that
c_{ijnmk}\rightarrow c_{ijnm}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. We also know that

\overline{S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)}=\frac{1}{i\varphi_{k}(z)}L_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)T(z),\quad\,\,w\in\Omega,\,z\in\partial\Omega.

For a positive integer
r
, upon differentiation with respect to
w
, we obtain

\overline{S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(\bar{r})}(z,w)}=\frac{1}{i\varphi_{k}(z)}L_{\varphi_{k}}^{(r)}(z,w)T(z)

for
w\in\Omega
and
z\in\partial\Omega
. Now (
2.8
) can therefore be rewritten for
w\in\partial\Omega
, as

\frac{1}{i\varphi_{k}(w)}L_{\varphi_{k}}(w,z)T(w)=\frac{1}{1-f(z)\overline{f(w)}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{M(i)}\sum_{m=0}^{M(j)}c_{ijnmk}\,S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})\frac{1}{i\varphi_{k}(w)}L_{\varphi_{k}}^{(m)}(w,a_{j})T(w).

Since
f(w)\overline{f(w)}=1
, this can be written as,

(2.10)

L_{\varphi_{k}}(w,z)=\frac{f(w)}{f(w)-f(z)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{M(i)}\sum_{m=0}^{M(j)}c_{ijnmk}\,S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})L_{\varphi_{k}}^{(m)}(w,a_{j}),

and this holds by the identity principle for all
(z,w)\in\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}
. Similarly,

(2.11)

L_{\varphi}(w,z)=\frac{f(w)}{f(w)-f(z)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{M(i)}\sum_{m=0}^{M(j)}c_{ijnm}\,S_{\varphi}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})L_{\varphi}^{(m)}(w,a_{j}).

This gives

l_{\varphi_{k}}(w,z)-l_{\varphi}(w,z)=L_{\varphi_{k}}(w,z)-L_{\varphi}(w,z)\\
=\frac{f(w)}{f(w)-f(z)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{M(i)}\sum_{m=0}^{M(j)}c_{ijnmk}\,S_{\varphi_{k}}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})L_{\varphi_{k}}^{(0,m)}(w,a_{j})-c_{ijnm}\,S_{\varphi}^{(\bar{n})}(z,a_{i})L_{\varphi}^{(0,m)}(w,a_{j}).

for
(z,w)\in\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}

Note that
L_{\varphi_{k}}(w,z)-L_{\varphi}(w,z)
is a holomorphic function in
z
and
w
and extends to be in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})

(Nehari

\partial\Omega

Theorem 4.2
.

converge to the corresponding classical Ahlfors map, uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
. The convergence is also uniform with respect to
a\in W
. In other words,

(4.4)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}F_{k}(z,a):=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,a)}{L_{\varphi_{k}}(z,a)}=\frac{S(z,a)}{L(z,a)}=:F(z,a)

uniformly for
a\in W
and
z\in\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Choose
k_{1}\geq 1
such that
L_{\varphi_{k}}(z,a)
and
S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,a)
do not vanish for
k\geq k_{1}
,
z\in\partial\Omega
and
a\in W
. For
k\geq k_{1}
,

\frac{1}{i\varphi_{k}(z)}S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,a)L_{\varphi_{k}}(z,a)T(z)=|S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,a)|^{2},\quad z\in\partial\Omega,a\in\Omega

and this gives

\Delta\arg S_{\varphi_{k}}(\cdot,a)L_{\varphi_{k}}(\cdot,a)=-\Delta\arg T=2\pi(n-2)

Since
L_{\varphi_{k}}(z,a)
has a simple pole at
z=a
, the argument principle implies that the combined number of zeros of
S_{\varphi_{k}}(\cdot,a)
and
L_{\varphi_{k}}(\cdot,a)
in
\Omega
equals
n-1
, counting multiplicity, for all
a\in W
. The function

N_{k}(a)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{{S_{\varphi_{k}}}^{\prime}(z,a)}{S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,a)}dz

gives total number of zeros of
S_{\varphi_{k}}(\cdot,a)
in
\Omega
. The function
N_{k}
converges uniformly for
a\in W
to the function

N(a)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{S^{\prime}(z,a)}{S(z,a)}dz

that gives the number of zeros of
S(\cdot,a)
in
\Omega
, counting multiplicities, which is
n-1
. Therefore, eventually
N_{k}
are constant functions. Choose
k_{0}\geq k_{1}
such that
N_{k}(a)=n-1
for all
k\geq k_{0}
and
a\in W
. Thus,
S_{\varphi_{k}}(\cdot,a)
has
n-1
zeros in
\Omega
and does not vanish on
\partial\Omega
for all
k\geq k_{0}
and
a\in W
. Since the combined number of zeros of
S_{\varphi_{k}}(\cdot,a)
and
L_{\varphi_{k}}(\cdot,a)
equals
n-1
, the function
L_{\varphi_{k}}(\cdot,a)
does not vanish on
\overline{\Omega}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
and
a\in W
.

We know that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=S(z,w)\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}L_{\varphi_{k}}(z,w)=L(z,w)

locally uniformly on
(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})\setminus\{(z,z):z\in\partial\Omega\}
and
(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})\setminus\{(z,z):z\in\overline{\Omega}\}\}
respectively. Therefore,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}F_{k}(z,a)=F(z,a)

uniformly for
a\in W
and
z\in\partial\Omega
. Since
F_{k}(\cdot,a),F(\cdot,a)\in A^{\infty}(\Omega)
for all
k\geq k_{0}
and
a\in W
, it follows by the maximum modulus principle that

\displaystyle\sup\{|F_{k}(z,a)-F(z,a)|:z\in\overline{\Omega},a\in W\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{|F_{k}(z,a)-F(z,a)|:z\in\partial\Omega,a\in W\}.

Thus proves that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}F_{k}(z,a)=F(z,a)
uniformly for
a\in W
and
z\in\overline{\Omega}
.
∎

5.
Explicit Formulas for a class of weights

We start with two examples due to Nehari and Bell, giving more details.

Example 5.1

(Nehari
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded
n
-connected domain with
C^{\infty}
smooth boundary such that no boundary curve is an isolated point. For
a\in\Omega
, let
a_{1},\ldots a_{r}
be the zeros of
S(z,a)
with multiplicity
n_{1},\ldots n_{r}
, respectively. For
1\leq k\leq r
and
1\leq m_{i}\leq n_{i}
, let

\varphi(z)=|(z-a_{1})^{m_{1}}\ldots(z-a_{k})^{m_{k}}|^{2}.

Given
g\in H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
,

\displaystyle g(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(a-a_{i})^{m_{i}}}\,\left(g(a)\prod_{i=1}^{k}(a-a_{i})^{m_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(a-a_{i})^{m_{i}}}\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\zeta)\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\zeta-a_{i})^{m_{i}}\,\overline{S(\zeta,a)}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(a-a_{i})^{m_{i}}}\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\zeta)\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\zeta-a_{i})^{m_{i}}\,\overline{S(\zeta,a)}\frac{\varphi(\zeta)}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}|\zeta-a_{i}|^{2m_{i}}}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\zeta)\,\frac{\overline{S(\zeta,a)}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\overline{\zeta}-\overline{a_{i}})^{m_{i}}(a-a_{i})^{m_{i}}}\,\varphi(\zeta)\,ds.

Therefore, by the uniqueness of the weighted Szegő kernel function
S_{\varphi}(\cdot,a)
, we have

S_{\varphi}(z,a)=\frac{S(z,a)}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(z-a_{i})^{m_{i}}(\overline{a}-\overline{a_{i}})^{m_{i}}}.

Now for
z\in\partial\Omega
,

\displaystyle L_{\varphi}(z,a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\varphi(z)\overline{T(z)}\overline{S_{\varphi}(z,a)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\prod_{i=1}^{k}|z-a_{i}|^{2m_{i}}\,\overline{T(z)}\frac{\overline{S(z,a)}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\overline{z}-\overline{a_{i}})^{m_{i}}(a-a_{i})^{m_{i}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(z-a_{i})^{m_{i}}}{(a-a_{i})^{m_{i}}}\,L(z,a).

The identity principle implies that

L_{\varphi}(z,a)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(z-a_{i})^{m_{i}}}{(a-a_{i})^{m_{i}}}\,L(z,a),\quad z\in\overline{\Omega}.

So, the weighted Garabedian kernel function
L_{\varphi}(\cdot,a)
does not vanish on
\partial\Omega
and has zeros
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
in
\Omega
with multiplicity
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
respectively.

The weighted Ahlfors map
F_{\varphi}
with respect to
a\in\Omega
, is given by

F_{\varphi}(z)=\frac{S_{\varphi}(z,a)}{L_{\varphi}(z,a)}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(a-a_{i})^{2m_{i}}}{|a-a_{i}|^{2m_{i}}}\frac{1}{(z-a_{i})^{2m_{i}}}\frac{S(z,a)}{L(z,a)}.

Thus, the weighted Ahlfors map
F_{\varphi}
and the classical Ahlfors map
F
with respect to
a\in\Omega
are related by

F_{\varphi}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(a-a_{i})^{2m_{i}}}{|a-a_{i}|^{2m_{i}}}\frac{1}{(z-a_{i})^{2m_{i}}}F(z).

If
2m_{i}>n_{i}
, then the function
F_{\varphi}
has a pole at
a_{i}
of order
2m_{i}-n_{i}
. If
2m_{i}\leq n_{i}
for each
i
, then
F_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varphi}
.

The function
F_{\varphi}
maps the
n
boundary curves
\gamma_{i}
of
\Omega
onto
n
Jordan closed curves
\Gamma_{i}
. Some of the images
\Gamma_{i}
may coincide, for example all of them coincide if
\varphi\equiv 1
. The image of
\Omega
under
F_{\varphi}
is open and connected and the boundary is the union of
\Gamma_{i}
. Since
F_{\varphi}
has a pole,
F_{\varphi}(\Omega)
is the connected component of

(\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\})\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}

that contains
\infty
.

Example 5.2

(Bell
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded
n
-connected domain with
C^{\infty}
smooth boundary such that no boundary curve is an isolated point. Let
p(a,z)
denote the Poisson kernel of
\Omega
. For
A_{0}\in\Omega
, let

\varphi(z)=p(A_{0},z).

Note that the weight
\varphi
is well-defined for simply connected domains as well. For
g\in H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
,

g(A_{0})=\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\zeta)\varphi(\zeta)ds,

by the reproducing property of the Poisson kernel. Therefore,

S_{\varphi}(z,A_{0})\equiv 1.

Assume that
\Omega
is simply connected. Then,

p(A_{0},z)=\frac{|S(z,A_{0})|^{2}}{S(A_{0},A_{0})}.

Therefore, for
z\in\partial\Omega
,

L_{\varphi}(z,A_{0})=i\,\varphi(z)\,\overline{S_{\varphi}(z,A_{0})}\,\overline{T(z)}=i\,\frac{|S(z,A_{0})|^{2}}{S(A_{0},A_{0})}\,\overline{T(z)}=\frac{L(z,A_{0})S(z,A_{0})}{S(A_{0},A_{0})}.

By the Identity principle,

L_{\varphi}(z,A_{0})=\frac{L(z,A_{0})\,S(z,A_{0})}{S(A_{0},A_{0})},\quad z\in\overline{\Omega}.

The weighted Ahlfors map
F_{\varphi}
with respect to
A_{0}\in\Omega
, is given by

F_{\varphi}(z)=\frac{S(A_{0},A_{0})}{L(z,A_{0})\,S(z,A_{0})}.

Note that the weighted Ahlfors map
F_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varphi}
since
\Omega
is simply connected.

Example 5.3

(see
[
9
,
14
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded
n
-connected domain with
C^{\infty}
smooth boundary such that no boundary curve is an isolated point. Let

\varphi(z)=|f(z)|^{2},

where
f\in A^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a non-vanishing function on
\overline{\Omega}
. Then

S_{\varphi}(z,w)=\frac{1}{f(z)\overline{f(w)}}S(z,w)\quad\text{and}\quad L_{\varphi}(z,w)=\frac{f(z)}{f(w)}L(z,w).

Given
g\in H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
and
a\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle g(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f(a)}(gf)(a)=\frac{1}{f(a)}\int_{\partial\Omega}(gf)(\zeta)\,\overline{S(\zeta,a)}\,ds=\frac{1}{f(a)}\int_{\partial\Omega}(gf)(\zeta)\,\frac{\overline{S(\zeta,a)}}{|f(\zeta)|^{2}}\,|f(\zeta)|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\zeta)\,\frac{\overline{S(\zeta,a)}}{\overline{f(\zeta)}f(a)}\,\varphi(\zeta)\,ds.

Therefore, by the uniqueness of the weighted Szegő kernel with respect to weight
\varphi
, we have

S_{\varphi}(z,w)=\frac{1}{f(z)\overline{f(w)}}S(z,w),\quad z,w\in\Omega.

Now, for
z\in\partial\Omega
and
w\in\Omega
,

L_{\varphi}(z,w)=i\,|f(z)|^{2}\,\overline{T(z)}\,\overline{S_{\varphi}(z,w)}=i\,|f(z)|^{2}\,\overline{T(z)}\,\frac{1}{f(w)\overline{f(z)}}\,\overline{S(z,w)}=\frac{f(z)}{f(w)}L(z,w).

The identity principle implies that

L_{\varphi}(z,w)=\frac{f(z)}{f(w)}L(z,w),\quad z,w\in\Omega.

Thus, the weighted Szegő kernel and the weighted Garabedian kernel inherit many nice properties of the classical Szegő kernel and the Garabedian kernel, respectively.

The weighted Ahlfors map
F_{\varphi}
with respect to a point
a\in\Omega
, is given by

F_{\varphi}(z)=\frac{S_{\varphi}(z,a)}{L_{\varphi}(z,a)}=\frac{f(a)^{2}}{|f(a)|^{2}}\,\frac{1}{f(z)^{2}}\frac{S(z,a)}{L(z,a)}.

Thus, the weighted Ahlfors map
F_{\varphi}
and the classical Ahlfors map
F
with respect to a point
a\in\Omega
, are related by

F_{\varphi}(z)=\frac{f(a)^{2}}{|f(a)|^{2}}\,\frac{1}{f(z)^{2}}F(z).

Here,
F_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varphi}
for every
a\in\Omega
, just as in the classical case.

The function
F_{\varphi}
maps the
n
boundary curves
\gamma_{i}
of
\Omega
onto
n
Jordan closed curves
\Gamma_{i}
. Some of the images
\Gamma_{i}
may coincide, for example all of them coincide if
\varphi\equiv 1
. The image of
\Omega
under
F_{\varphi}
is open and connected and the boundary is the union of
\Gamma_{i}
. To see the image,
F_{\varphi}(\Omega)
is the connected component of

(\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\})\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}

that contains
0
. The image is bounded as
F_{\varphi}
has no poles.

Remark 5.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded
n
-connected domain with
C^{\infty}
smooth boundary such that no boundary curve is an isolated point. Let
\varphi
be a positive real-valued
C^{\infty}
smooth function on
\partial\Omega
. It is known
(
see
[
12
,
13
]
)
that the weighted Garabedian kernel
L_{\varphi}(z,w)
does not vanish on
\overline{\Omega}
for all
w\in\Omega
if and only if there exists a non-vanishing function
f\in A^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

L_{\varphi}(z,w)=L_{|f|^{2}}(z,w).

Example 5.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded
n
-connected domain with
C^{\infty}
smooth boundary such that no boundary curve is an isolated point. For
A_{0}\in\Omega
, let

\varphi(z)=|S(z,A_{0})|^{2}.

Given
g\in H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
,

\displaystyle g(A_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{S(A_{0},A_{0})}\,g(A_{0})\,S(A_{0},A_{0})=\frac{1}{S(A_{0},A_{0})}\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\zeta)\,S(\zeta,A_{0})\,\overline{S(\zeta,A_{0})}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{S(A_{0},A_{0})}\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\zeta)\,\varphi(\zeta)\,ds.

Therefore, by the uniqueness of the weighted Szegő kernel with respect to weight
\varphi
, we have

S_{\varphi}(z,A_{0})\equiv\frac{1}{S(A_{0},A_{0})}.

Now, for
z\in\partial\Omega
,

L_{\varphi}(z,w)=i\,|S(z,A_{0})|^{2}\,\overline{T(z)}\,\overline{S_{\varphi}(z,w)}=\frac{L(z,A_{0})\,S(z,A_{0})}{S(A_{0},A_{0})}.

The identity principle implies that

L_{\varphi}(z,A_{0})=\frac{L(z,A_{0})\,S(z,A_{0})}{S(A_{0},A_{0})},\quad z\in\overline{\Omega}.

The weighted Ahlfors map
F_{\varphi}
and the classical Ahlfors map
F
with respect to
A_{0}\in\Omega
, are related by

F_{\varphi}(z)=\frac{1}{L(z,A_{0})\,S(z,A_{0})}=\frac{1}{S(z,A_{0})^{2}}F(z).

Here,
F_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varphi}
if and only if
\Omega
is simply connected.

Example 5.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded
n
-connected domain with
C^{\infty}
smooth boundary such that no boundary curve is an isolated point. For
A_{0}\in\Omega
, let
B_{0}
be a zero of
S(\cdot,A_{0})
. Let

\varphi(z)=\frac{1}{|L(z,B_{0})|^{2}}.

Given
g\in H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
,

\displaystyle g(A_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g(A_{0})}{L(A_{0},B_{0})}\,L(A_{0},B_{0})=L(A_{0},B_{0})\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{g(\zeta)}{L(\zeta,B_{0})}\,\overline{S(\zeta,A_{0})}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L(A_{0},B_{0})\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{g(\zeta)}{L(\zeta,B_{0})}\,\overline{S(\zeta,A_{0})}\,|L(\zeta,B_{0})|^{2}\,\varphi(\zeta)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L(A_{0},B_{0})\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\zeta)\,\overline{S(\zeta,A_{0})\,L(\zeta,B_{0})}\,ds.

Since
S(B_{0},A_{0})=0
, the function
S(\cdot,A_{0})\,L(\cdot,B_{0})\in H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
.
Therefore, by the uniqueness of the weighted Szegő kernel with respect to weight
\varphi
, we have

S_{\varphi}(z,A_{0})=S(z,A_{0})\,L(z,B_{0})\,\overline{L(A_{0},B_{0})}.

Now, for
z\in\partial\Omega
,

\displaystyle L_{\varphi}(z,A_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\,\frac{1}{|L(z,B_{0})|^{2}}\,\overline{T(z)}\,\overline{S_{\varphi}(z,A_{0})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\,\frac{1}{|L(z,B_{0})|^{2}}\,\overline{T(z)}\,\overline{S(z,A_{0})}\,\overline{L(z,B_{0})}\,L(A_{0},B_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L(z,A_{0})}{L(z,B_{0})}\,L(A_{0},B_{0}).

The identity principle implies that

L_{\varphi}(z,A_{0})=\frac{L(z,A_{0})}{L(z,B_{0})}\,L(A_{0},B_{0}),\quad z\in\overline{\Omega}.

The weighted Ahlfors map
F_{\varphi}
and the classical Ahlfors map
F
with respect to
A_{0}\in\Omega
, are related by

F_{\varphi}(z)=\frac{\overline{L(A_{0},B_{0})}}{L(A_{0},B_{0})}\,L(z,B_{0})^{2}\,F(z).

The weighted Ahlfors map
F_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varphi}
if
S(z,A_{0})
vanishes at
z=B_{0}
with multiplicity at least
2
. Otherwise,
F_{\varphi}
has a simple pole at
z=B_{0}
.

Remark 5.7
.

(1)

6.
Nehari’s Construction of the weighted Szegő and weighted Garabedian kernel

As before, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded
n
-connected domain with
C^{\infty}
smooth boundary curves
\gamma_{i}
and no isolated boundary point. Let
\varphi
be a positive
C^{\infty}
smooth function on
\partial\Omega
. Theorem
1
in
[
9
]
shows that there exists a meromorphic function
q
on
\Omega
that extends
C^{\infty}
-smoothly to
\partial\Omega
such that the total number of zeros and poles counting multiplicities does not exceed
n-1
, and
|q(z)|^{2}=\varphi(z)
on
\partial\Omega
. Some auxiliary functions arising in the proof of this theorem are of independent interest since they can be identified as the Szegő kernel of certain closed subspaces of
H^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. To make all this clear, we give an exposition of Theorem
1
in
[
9
]
for the sake of completeness and more importantly, to bring out the relevance and definition of the aforementioned auxiliary functions.

For
a\in\Omega
, recall that the Green’s function
G(z,a)
with pole at
z=a
is defined as

G(z,a)=-\log|z-a|+u_{a}(z)

where
u_{a}(z)
is the harmonic function of
z
on
\Omega
that solves the Dirichlet problem with boundary data equal to
\log|z-a|
. Consider the harmonic function

u(z)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\partial\Omega}\log\varphi(t)\frac{\partial G(z,t)}{\partial n}\,ds

which has boundary values
2^{-1}\log\varphi(t)
. Let
2\pi p_{\nu}
,
\nu=1,\ldots,n
, denote the periods of the harmonic conjugate
v
of
u
. The function

(6.1)

U(z)=u(z)+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\epsilon_{\mu}G(z,z_{\mu}),\quad\quad z_{\mu}\in D,\mu=1,\ldots,n-1,

where
\epsilon_{\mu}
is either
+1
or
-1
, has the same boundary values as
u(z)
. The period of
G(z,\zeta_{\mu})
about
\gamma_{\nu}
is

\int_{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{\partial G(z,z_{\mu})}{\partial n}ds=(-2\pi)\frac{-1}{2\pi}\int_{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{\partial G(z,z_{\mu})}{\partial n}ds=(-2\pi)\,\omega_{\nu}(z_{\mu})

where
\omega_{\nu}
denotes the harmonic function on
\Omega
that takes the boundary values
\delta_{\nu\mu}
on
\gamma_{\mu}
. The period
2\pi P_{\nu}
of the harmonic conjugate
V
of
U
about
\gamma_{\nu}
is given by

P_{\nu}=p_{\nu}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\epsilon_{\mu}\omega_{\nu}(z_{\mu}),\quad\quad\nu=1,\ldots,n.

We want to choose
z_{\mu}
and
\epsilon_{\mu}
in such a way that
P_{\nu}
are integers. Since
u
is harmonic,
p_{1}+\ldots+p_{n}=0
. Also,
\omega_{1}+\ldots+\omega_{n}\equiv 1
. So,

P_{n}=-\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}\left(p_{\nu}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\epsilon_{\mu}\omega_{\nu}(z_{\mu})\right)-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\epsilon_{\mu}

and therefore, it is enough to make sure that
P_{\nu}
are integers for
\nu=1,\ldots,n-1
. That is, we have to find
n-1
points
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1}
in
\Omega
for which

(6.2)

\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\epsilon_{\mu}\omega_{\nu}(z_{\mu})=p_{\nu}+m_{\nu},\quad\quad\nu=1,\ldots,n-1

holds for appropriately chosen integers
m_{\nu}
and for a suitable choice of
\epsilon_{\mu}
. A schlicht (univalent) conformal mapping of
\Omega
transforms the harmonic measures of
\Omega
into the harmonic measures of its conformal image. Therefore, in order to solve (
6.2
), we can assume without loss of generality, that
\gamma_{n}
is the real axis and
\Omega
is contained in the upper half plane. Since the harmonic functions
\omega_{\nu}
vanish on the real axis, they can be analytically continued beyond the real axis by the identity
\omega_{\nu}(\bar{z})=-\omega_{\nu}(z)
. The functions
\omega_{\nu}
are thus harmonic in the entire domain
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega\cup\mathbb{R}\cup\tilde{\Omega}
, where
\tilde{\Omega}
is the mirror image of
\Omega
with respect to the real axis. Since
\omega_{\nu}(\bar{z})=-\omega_{\nu}(z)
, the system of equations (
6.2
) is equivalent to the system

(6.3)

\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\omega_{\nu}(z_{\mu})=p_{\nu}+m_{\nu},\quad\quad\nu=1,\ldots,n-1,

if
z_{\mu}
are not restricted to
\Omega
and can take any value in
\Omega^{\prime}
. Now, consider the expression

(6.4)

R=R(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1})=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}\left[\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\omega_{\nu}(z_{\mu})-p_{\nu}-m_{\nu}\right]^{2}.

The function
R(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1})
is continuous with variables coming from
\Omega^{\prime}+\Gamma^{\prime}
where
\Gamma^{\prime}
denotes the boundary of
\Omega^{\prime}
. Thus, for a definite choice of integers
m_{\nu}
,
R
has a non-negative minimum, which is attained for a set of
n-1
points
z_{\mu}
in
\Omega^{\prime}+\Gamma^{\prime}
. We now choose
m_{\nu}
in such a way as to give this minimum its smallest possible value, which is possible because
-1\leq\omega_{\nu}(z)\leq 1
. If
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1}
are a choice of integers which give the smallest minimum of
R
, we write
p_{\nu}+m_{\nu}=\alpha_{\nu}
, and consider the solution of

R=R(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1})=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}\left[\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\omega_{\nu}(z_{\mu})-\alpha_{\nu}\right]^{2}=min,

where
z_{\mu}\in\Omega^{\prime}+\Gamma^{\prime}
. Suppose
z_{1}\in\gamma_{k}
(
k\neq n
). Since
\omega_{k}(z_{1})=1
,
\omega_{\nu}(z_{1})=0

(\nu\neq k)
, and
\omega_{\nu}(z)
(
\nu=1,\ldots,n-1
) vanishes on the real axis, (
6.4
) will remain same if
z_{1}
is transferred to the real axis and
m_{k}
is replaced by
m_{k}-1
. Similarly, a point
z_{\mu}
may be transferred from the mirror image of
\gamma_{k}
to the real axis keeping (
6.4
) unchanged by replacing
m_{k}
with
m_{k}+1
. Therefore, there exists a minimizing set
S=\{z_{\mu}\}
lying entirely in
\Omega^{\prime}
. Similarly, we can assume that
S
does not contain both a point
z_{\mu}
and its conjugate
\bar{z}_{\mu}
, because they can be replaced by points on the real axis without altering
(\ref{Ne2})
. Since
z_{\mu}
minimize (
6.4
), we have

0=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}\left[\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\omega_{\nu}(z_{\mu})-\alpha_{\nu}\right]{w^{\prime}_{\nu}}(z_{\mu})=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}a_{\nu}{w^{\prime}_{\nu}}(z_{\mu}),\quad\quad a_{\nu}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\omega_{\nu}(z_{\mu})-\alpha_{\nu}

where
w_{\nu}(z)
is (locally defined) holomorphic function whose real part is
\omega_{\nu}(z)
. Note that the derivative of
w_{\nu}
is globally defined and equals
(\partial/\partial z)\omega_{\nu}
. Now, either all
a_{\nu}
vanish or the derivative of the (locally defined) holomorphic function

w(z)=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}a_{\nu}w_{\nu}(z)

vanishes at
n-1
points
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1}
. In the first case, we have solved (
6.3
). So, assume that at least one of the constants
a_{\nu}
is non-zero. The points of
S
are critical points of
w(z)
. Since
a_{\nu}
are real, we have
{w^{\prime}_{\nu}}(\bar{z})=-\overline{{w^{\prime}_{\nu}}(z)}
. So, the set
S^{*}
of points conjugate to those of
S
is also a set of critical points of
w(z)
. If no point of
S
is on the real axis, the sets
S
and
S^{*}
are disjoint and we thus have
2(n-1)
critical points of
w(z)
, and
n-1
of these points are in
\Omega
. It is known that

\overline{i\,\frac{\partial\omega_{\nu}}{\partial z}\,T(z)}=i\,\frac{\partial\omega_{\nu}}{\partial z}\,T(z)

on
\partial\Omega
. Therefore,
iw^{\prime}(z)T(z)
is real on
\partial\Omega
. By the argument principle, we have

\text{no. of zeros of }w^{\prime}(z)\text{ in }\Omega=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta\arg w^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\Delta\arg(iw^{\prime}(z)T(z))-\Delta\arg T(z)\right)=n-2,

which is a contradiction. So, assume that one or more points of
S
lie on the real axis. Note that
R
is constant on the real axis. If
z_{\mu}
is a point of
S
lying on the real axis, then it can be replaced with any other point on the real axis without altering the value of the minimum. So, all the points of the real axis are critical points of
w(z)
. This means that

\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}a_{\nu}{w^{\prime}_{\nu}}(z)\equiv 0

on the real axis, and by analytic continuation, throughout
\Omega^{\prime}
. But this is possible only if all
a_{\nu}
vanish because
{w^{\prime}_{\nu}}(z)
are linearly independent. This leads to a contradiction and shows the solvability of (
6.3
).

Therefore, the periods of the harmonic conjugate
V
of
U
are integral multiples of
2\pi
. Thus,

q(z)=\exp\{U(z)+iV(z)\}

is a single-valued meromorphic function on
\Omega
with simple zeros or simple poles at
z_{\mu}
, depending on whether
\epsilon_{\mu}
is equal to
+1
or
-1
. The number of zeros and poles of

counting multiplicity, does not exceed
n-1
, and

|q(z)|^{2}=\exp(2\,U(z))=\exp\left(\log\varphi(z)\right)=\varphi(z),\quad\quad z\in\partial\Omega.

Note that if two of the points
z_{\mu}
coincide but the respective

are opposite in sign then the corresponding terms involving Green’s function in (
6.1
) get cancelled and the total number of zeros and poles of

counting multiplicity will be less than

.

Now, for
\zeta\in\Omega
, consider the functions

(6.5)

S^{(1)}(z,\zeta)=S(z,\zeta)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{\sigma}c_{\nu}S(z,z_{\nu})+\sum_{\nu=\sigma+1}^{n-1}c_{\nu}L(z,z_{\nu}),\quad\quad\sigma\leq n-1,

(6.6)

L^{(1)}(z,\zeta)=L(z,\zeta)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{\sigma}\bar{c}_{\nu}L(z,z_{\nu})+\sum_{\nu=\sigma+1}^{n-1}\bar{c}_{\nu}S(z,z_{\nu})

where
z_{1},\ldots,z_{\sigma}
are the zeros of

and
z_{\sigma+1},\ldots,z_{n-1}
are the poles of
q(z)
. The

constants

shall be determined by

S^{(1)}(z_{\mu},\zeta)=0,\quad\mu=1,\ldots,\sigma;\quad\quad L^{(1)}(z_{\mu},\zeta)=0,\quad\mu=\sigma+1,\ldots,n-1.

This will be possible if the system of
n-1
linear equations

(6.8)

\sum_{\nu=1}^{\sigma}c_{\nu}S(z_{\mu},z_{\nu})+\sum_{\nu=\sigma+1}^{n-1}c_{\nu}L(z_{\mu},z_{\nu})=-S(z_{\mu},\zeta),\quad\quad\mu=1,\ldots,\sigma,\\
\sum_{\nu=1}^{\sigma}c_{\nu}\overline{L(z_{\mu},z_{\nu})}+\sum_{\nu=\sigma+1}^{n-1}c_{\nu}\overline{S(z_{\mu},z_{\nu})}=-\overline{L(z_{\mu},\zeta)},\quad\quad\mu=\sigma+1,\ldots,n-1,

for the
n-1
unknowns
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}
has a solution. We may assume that not all the numbers
S(z_{\mu},\zeta)

(\mu=1,\ldots,\sigma)
,
L(z_{\mu},\zeta)

(\mu=\sigma+1,\ldots,n-1)
are zero. It is enough to show that the associated homogeneous system in
(\ref{Ne3})
does not possess a non-trivial solution. On the contrary, if it had a non-trivial solution, there would exist constants
d_{\nu}

(\nu=1,\ldots,n)
, not all of which are zero, such that

S^{(2)}(z_{\mu})=0,\quad\mu=1,\ldots,\sigma;\quad\quad L^{(2)}(z_{\mu})=0,\quad\mu=\sigma+1,\ldots,n-1,

where

S^{(2)}(z)=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\sigma}d_{\nu}S(z,z_{\nu})+\sum_{\nu=\sigma+1}^{n-1}d_{\nu}L(z,z_{\nu}),\quad L^{(2)}(z)=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\sigma}\bar{d}_{\nu}L(z,z_{\nu})+\sum_{\nu=\sigma+1}^{n-1}\bar{d}_{\nu}S(z,z_{\nu}).

If two of the points
z_{\nu}
coincide, at the point
z_{\tau}
,
\tau\leq\sigma
, then (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) will each have an extra term involving
\partial S(z,z_{\tau})/\partial\bar{z}_{\tau}
and
\partial L(z,z_{\tau})/\partial z_{\tau}
, respectively. The condition in (
6.7
) which is lost by the two points coinciding is replaced by the condition
\partial S^{(1)}(z_{\tau},\zeta)/\partial z_{\tau}=0
. If
\tau>\sigma
, then the roles of the functions
S
and
L
are interchanged in modifying (
6.5
), (
6.6
) and the condition (
6.7
). Coincidence of more than two points will result in the appearance of higher derivatives in the appropriate places. These changes do not affect the validity of the considerations which follow. We have

\overline{S^{(2)}(z)}=\frac{1}{i}L^{(2)}(z)T(z),\quad\quad z\in\partial\Omega.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{i}S^{(2)}(z)L^{(2)}(z)T(z)\geq 0,\quad\quad z\in\partial\Omega,

which implies that

\frac{1}{i}\int_{\partial\Omega}S^{(2)}(z)L^{(2)}(z)dz>0.

By Cauchy’s theorem, the function
S^{(2)}(z)L^{(2)}(z)
cannot be analytic in
\Omega
, which is a contradiction. Therefore, we have proved that (

) has a unique solution. Furthermore,

(6.9)

\overline{S^{(1)}(z,\zeta)}=\frac{1}{i}L^{(1)}(z,\zeta)T(z),\quad\quad z\in\partial\Omega.

The weighted Szegő and Garabedian kernels are given by

(6.10)

S_{\varphi}(z,\zeta)=\frac{S^{(1)}(z,\zeta)}{q(z)\,\overline{q(\zeta)}},\quad\quad L_{\varphi}(z,\zeta)=\frac{L^{(1)}(z,\zeta)\,q(z)}{q(\zeta)}.

If

happens to have a zero or a pole at

S_{\varphi}(z,\zeta)=\lim_{\eta\rightarrow\zeta}S_{\varphi}(z,\eta),\quad\quad L_{\varphi}(z,\zeta)=\lim_{\eta\rightarrow\zeta}L_{\varphi}(z,\eta).

This completes the exposition of Nehari’s theorem and yields expressions for the weighted Szegő and Garabedian kernels in terms of the auxiliary functions
S^{(1)}(z,\zeta),L^{(1)}(z,\zeta)
.

Observation 2

Let
\Omega

n
-connected domain with

Assume that

with multiplicity

The space

The function

If

In the case

is assumed to have no poles.

Now, let

By the definition, it follows that for

7.
Weighted Carathéodory metric and properties

The Carathéodory metric
c_{\Omega}
on a smoothly bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is defined by

(7.1)

c_{\Omega}(z)=\sup\{|f^{\prime}(z)|:\,f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\,\text{ is holomorphic and }\,f(z)=0\},\quad z\in\Omega.

For
z\in\Omega
, the solutions of the above extremal problem are of the form
e^{i\theta}f_{z}
, where
f_{z}
denotes the Ahlfors map of
\Omega
based at point
z
. It is known that

f_{z}(w)=\frac{S(w,z)}{L(w,z)},\quad w\in\Omega.

Since
L(\cdot,z)
has a pole at
w=z
with residue
1/2\pi
, we obtain

{f_{z}}^{\prime}(z)=\lim_{w\rightarrow z}\frac{f_{z}(w)-f_{z}(z)}{w-z}=\lim_{w\rightarrow z}\frac{f_{z}(w)}{w-z}=\lim_{w\rightarrow z}\frac{S(w,z)}{L(w,z)(w-z)}=\frac{\lim_{w\rightarrow z}S(w,z)}{1/2\pi}=2\pi S(z,z).

Therefore,

(7.2)

c_{\Omega}(z)=2\pi\,S(z,z).

Definition 7.1

(Weighted Carathéodory metric)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded
n
-connected domain with
C^{\infty}
smooth boundary such that no boundary curve is an isolated point. Let
\varphi
be a positive real-valued
C^{\infty}
smooth function on
\partial\Omega
. We define the weighted Carathéodory metric
c_{\varphi}
with respect to weight
\varphi
by

(7.3)

c_{\varphi}(z)=2\pi\,S_{\varphi}(z,z)

for
z\in\Omega
.

Let there exist a function
q\in A^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
|q(z)|^{2}=\varphi(z)
for
z\in\partial\Omega
. Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{\sigma}
be the zeros of
q
with multiplicity
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\sigma}
. Let
A=\{(z_{1},m_{1}),\ldots,(z_{\sigma},m_{\sigma})\}
. Then,

(7.4)

c_{\varphi}(z)=2\pi S_{\varphi}(z,z)=2\pi\frac{S_{A}(z,z)}{|q(z)|^{2}},\quad\quad z\neq z_{\mu}.

Let

]

S_{\varphi}(z,w)S_{1/\varphi}(z,w)=S(z,w)^{2}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}a_{\nu}(w)F_{\nu}^{\prime}(z)

(8.2)

L_{\varphi}(z,w)L_{1/\varphi}(z,w)=L(z,w)^{2}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}\overline{a_{\nu}(w)}F_{\nu}^{\prime}(z)

where
a_{\nu}
are constants depending upon
w\in\Omega
.

It is known (see
[
1
,
6
]
) that if
K
denotes the classical Bergman kernel of
\Omega
then

(8.3)

K(z,w)=4\pi S(z,w)^{2}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n-1}c_{jk}F_{j}^{\prime}(z)\overline{F_{k}^{\prime}(w)}

for some constants
c_{jk}
. Infact, if
a\in\Omega
is such that
S(\cdot,a)
has
n-1
distinct zeros
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}
then (see
[
1
,
5
]
)

(8.4)

K(z,a)=4\pi S(z,a)^{2}+2\pi\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{K(a_{j},a)}{S^{\prime}(a_{j},a)}L(z,a_{j})S(z,a).

This is because the
n-1
functions
L(z,a_{j})S(z,a)
form a basis for
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=span_{\mathbb{C}}\{F_{j}^{\prime}(z):j=1,\ldots,n-1\}
(a theorem due to Schiffer, see
[
1
,
5
,
11
]
). This result due to Schiffer also implies that

(8.5)

K(z,w)=4\pi S(z,w)^{2}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{jk}L(z,a_{j})S(z,a)\overline{L(w,a_{k})S(w,a)}

for some constants
\lambda_{jk}
(see
[
4
]
as well).

Coming back to (
8.1
), we will see that

(8.6)

a_{\nu}(w)=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}A_{\mu\nu}\overline{F_{\mu}^{\prime}(w)}

for some constants
A_{\mu\nu}
. This can be seen following an argument in
[
1
, see page 137]
. For
k=1,\ldots,n-1

\int_{\gamma_{k}}(S_{\varphi}(z,w)S_{1/\varphi}(z,w)-S(z,w)^{2})\,dz=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}a_{\nu}(w)\int_{\gamma_{k}}F_{\nu}^{\prime}(z)\,dz=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}P_{k\nu}\,a_{\nu}(w)

where
P=[P_{\mu\nu}]
for
\mu,\nu=1,\ldots,n-1
, denotes the nonsingular matrix of periods

\int_{\gamma_{\mu}}F_{\nu}^{\prime}(z)\,dz.

Let
\tilde{\gamma}_{k}
be a curve that is homotopic to
\gamma_{k}
, but that is inside
\Omega
. Then

(8.7)

\int_{\tilde{\gamma}_{k}}(S_{\varphi}(z,w)S_{1/\varphi}(z,w)-S(z,w)^{2})\,dz=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}P_{k\nu}\,a_{\nu}(w).

Note that
a_{\nu}(w)
are antiholomorphic functions in
w
. Now,

\displaystyle S_{\varphi}(z,w)S_{1/\varphi}(z,w)-S(z,w)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{S_{\varphi}(w,z)S_{1/\varphi}(w,z)-S(w,z)^{2}}=\overline{\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}a_{\nu}(z)F_{\nu}^{\prime}(w)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}\overline{a_{\nu}(z)}\overline{F_{\nu}^{\prime}(w)}.

The integral in (
8.7
) can be approximated by a finite Riemann sum which will therefore lie in the linear span of functions
\{\overline{F_{\nu}^{\prime}(w)}\}_{\nu=1}^{n-1}
. A limiting argument will show that the integral also lies in the linear span of
\overline{F_{\nu}^{\prime}(w)}
. Thus, we have

\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}Q_{k\nu}\overline{F_{\nu}^{\prime}(w)}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n-1}P_{k\nu}\,a_{\nu}(w),\quad\quad k=1,\ldots,n-1

for some constants
Q_{k\nu}
. Let
Q
denote the matrix
[Q_{\mu\nu}]
. Therefore,

P\,\cdot\,[a_{\nu}(w)]_{\nu=1}^{n-1}=Q\,\cdot\,[\overline{F_{\nu}^{\prime}(w)}]_{\nu=1}^{n-1}.

Since
P
is nonsingular, we obtain (
8.6
).
Following the same line of reasoning for (
8.2
), we have

(8.8)

S_{\varphi}(z,w)S_{1/\varphi}(z,w)=S(z,w)^{2}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n-1}a_{jk}\,F_{j}^{\prime}(z)\overline{F_{k}^{\prime}(w)}

(8.9)

L_{\varphi}(z,w)L_{1/\varphi}(z,w)=L(z,w)^{2}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n-1}\overline{a_{jk}}\,F_{j}^{\prime}(z)F_{k}^{\prime}(w)

for some constants
a_{jk}
. This also implies that

(8.10)

S_{\varphi}(z,w)S_{1/\varphi}(z,w)=S(z,w)^{2}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{jk}\,L(z,a_{j})S(z,a)\overline{L(w,a_{k})S(w,a)}

(8.11)

L_{\varphi}(z,w)L_{1/\varphi}(z,w)=L(z,w)^{2}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n-1}\overline{\alpha_{jk}}\,L(z,a_{j})S(z,a)L(w,a_{k})S(w,a)

for some constants
\alpha_{jk}
.
Relations (
6.5
), (
6.6
), Theorem
6.1
, relations (
8.8
) – (
8.11
) together may help to shed more light on the properties of the weighted Szegő and weighted Garabedian kernel.

Finally, we also observe from the relations (
8.3
), (
8.5
), (
8.8
) and (
8.10
) that

(8.12)

4\pi\,S_{\varphi}(z,w)S_{1/\varphi}(z,w)=K(z,w)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n-1}b_{jk}\,F_{j}^{\prime}(z)\overline{F_{k}^{\prime}(w)}

(8.13)

4\pi\,S_{\varphi}(z,w)S_{1/\varphi}(z,w)=K(z,w)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n-1}\beta_{jk}\,L(z,a_{j})S(z,a)\overline{L(w,a_{k})S(w,a)}

for some constants
b_{jk}
and
\beta_{jk}
.

Thus, the weighted Szegő kernels corresponding to weights
\varphi
and
1/\varphi
can be expressed in terms of the classical kernel functions.

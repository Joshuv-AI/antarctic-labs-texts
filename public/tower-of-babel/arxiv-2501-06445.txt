Self-dual pp-wave solutions in chiral higher-spin gravity
Tung Tran

Abstract
We show that chiral higher-spin gravity with a vanishing cosmological constant admits a class of exact self-dual pp-wave solutions derived from harmonic scalar functions and two principal spinors. These solutions satisfy both the linear and non-linear equations of motion, as they annihilate all higher-order vertices, leading to the equations of motion for free fields on a self-dual background sourced by a positive-helicity spin-2 field. Our method employs a simple Kerr-Schild ansatz for positive-helicity chiral higher-spin fields adapted to the self-dual gravity framework.

2.1
Free differential algebra

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
4d
spacetime manifold and
C^{\infty}(T[1]\mathcal{M})\equiv\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})
be its associated graded commutative algebra of differential forms endowed with the exterior differential
d
, which squares to zero. Then, the pair
(T[1]\mathcal{M},Q_{\mathcal{M}}\equiv d)
present the simplest example of a
Q
-manifold.

Now, consider differential-preserving maps
\Phi^{A}(x,dx):T[1]\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}
from the source
T[1]\mathcal{M}
to another target
Q
-manifold
(\mathcal{N},Q_{\mathcal{N}})
, which play the role of fields on
\mathcal{N}
. Then, around a stationary point
\mathtt{p}\in\mathcal{N}
, where
Q_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathtt{p})=0
, the associated set of pde. for
\Phi^{A}
takes the form

\displaystyle d\Phi^{A}

\displaystyle=Q_{\mathcal{N}}^{A}(\Phi)\,.

(2.1)

This is also known as free differential algebra (FDA)
[
43
,
52
,
53
,
54
]
. The nilpotency of
Q_{\mathcal{N}}\equiv Q
is equivalent to the statement:

\displaystyle Q^{B}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Phi^{B}}Q^{A}=0\hskip 20.00003pt\Leftrightarrow\hskip 20.00003pt\sum\pm Q^{B}{}_{M_{1}\ldots M_{n}}Q^{A}{}_{N_{1}\ldots B\ldots N_{k}}=0\,.

(2.2)

This gives us an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
[
44
,
55
]
whose
L_{\infty}
-structure maps are the Taylor’s coefficients
Q^{B}_{M_{1}\ldots N_{n}}
.

In the simplest setting, which works for generic gauge theories and gravity, we can choose
\Phi(x)=(\omega(x),\mathsf{C}(x))
to be the graded coordinates of
\mathcal{N}
. Here,
\omega
is a gauge field of degree one taking values in some Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\mathsf{C}
is a ‘matter’ field of degree zero taking values in some representation
\varrho(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then, the equations of motion resulting from (
2.1
) with the above field content, in general, are

\displaystyle d\omega

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}(\omega,\omega)+\mathcal{V}(\omega,\omega,\mathsf{C})+\mathcal{V}(\omega,\omega,\mathsf{C},\mathsf{C})+\ldots\,,

(2.3a)

\displaystyle d\mathsf{C}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}(\omega,\mathsf{C})+\mathcal{U}(\omega,\mathsf{C},\mathsf{C})+\mathcal{U}(\omega,\mathsf{C},\mathsf{C},\mathsf{C})+\ldots\,.

(2.3b)

where the notations
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{U})
are obvious. To sum things up, we can define a theory whenever we can specify
\{(\omega,\mathsf{C})\}
and
\{\mathcal{V}_{n}(\omega,\omega,\mathsf{C},\ldots)\,,\mathcal{U}_{n}(\omega,\mathsf{C},\ldots)\}
.
3
3

3

The subscripts of
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{U}
denote the number of arguments that enter the corresponding vertices.

Note that (
2.3
) can be referred to as a deformation of the following differential graded Lie algebra (dgLa)

\displaystyle d\omega=\frac{1}{2}[\omega,\omega]\,,\hskip 20.00003ptd\mathsf{C}=\rho(\omega)\mathsf{C}\,,

(2.4)

where now
\rho(\omega)
denotes some appropriate representation of
\omega
.
The first equation is simply the definition of a flat connection associated to the one-form
\omega
, while the second expresses the covariant constancy of the 0-form
\mathsf{C}
wrt. the flat connection.

Chiral HSGRA

2.2.1
Chiral FDA data

The local isomorphism
SO(4,\mathbb{C})\simeq SL(2,\mathbb{C})\times SL(2,\mathbb{C})
in
4d
complexified spacetime allows us to write any tensorial fields in terms of spin-tensor fields

In the case of chiral HSGRA,
(\omega,\mathsf{C})
can be written in terms of generating functions as

1-form

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\frac{1}{(2s)!}\omega_{A(2s)}Y^{A(2s)}=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{1}{m!n!}\omega_{\alpha(m)\,\dot{\alpha}(n)}\,y^{\alpha(m)}\tilde{y}^{\dot{\alpha}(n)}\,,\hskip 18.49988ptm+n\in 2\mathbb{N}\,,

(2.5a)

0-form

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\frac{1}{(2s)!}\mathsf{C}_{A(2s)}Y^{A(2s)}=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{1}{m!n!}\mathsf{C}_{\alpha(m)\,\dot{\alpha}(n)}\,y^{\alpha(m)}\tilde{y}^{\dot{\alpha}(n)}\,,\hskip 18.49988ptm+n\in 2\mathbb{N}\,.

(2.5b)

where
Y^{A}=(y^{\alpha},\tilde{y}^{\dot{\alpha}})
. Here,
y^{\alpha},\tilde{y}^{\dot{\alpha}}
are commutative spinorial variables, and

etc. is our standard abbreviation. The dynamical bosonic fields, which give us free action, are singled out as

helicity
+s

\displaystyle:\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{(2s)!}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha(2s)}y^{\alpha(2s)}=\omega(y,\tilde{y}=0)\,,

(2.6a)

helicity
-s

\displaystyle:\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{(2s)!}B_{\alpha(2s)}y^{\alpha(2s)}=\mathsf{C}(y,\tilde{y}=0)\,,

(2.6b)

helicity
0

\displaystyle:\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\varphi:

\displaystyle=\mathsf{C}(y=0,\tilde{y}=0)\,.

(2.6c)

Using
B^{\alpha(2s)}
and

one can write down the free action for chiral spinning fields as

39
]

\displaystyle S=\int B^{\alpha(2s)}\wedge H_{\alpha\alpha}\nabla\mathcal{A}_{\alpha(2s-2)}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptH_{\alpha\alpha}:=h_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}\wedge h_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\gamma}}\,.

(2.7)

In the above,
h_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
is the background vierbein of any SD spacetime where the ASD component
C_{\alpha(4)}
of the Weyl tensor vanish, and
\nabla
is the associated self-dual connection. This action is invariant on any self-dual background (with or without the cosmological constant) under
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\alpha(2s-2)}=\nabla\xi_{\alpha(2s-2)}+h_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha(2s-3)\,\dot{\beta}}

\nabla^{2}\xi_{\alpha(2s-2)}=H_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\xi_{\alpha(2s-3)\beta}
and

As explained in
[
39
]
, the physical one-form

can be written as

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha(2s-2)}=h^{\beta\dot{\beta}}A_{\alpha(2s-2)\beta\,\dot{\beta}}+h_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}\vartheta_{\alpha(2s-3)\,\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(2.8)

Since we can use the gauge transformation
\delta_{\eta}\omega_{\alpha(2s-2)}=h_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}\eta_{\alpha(2s-3)\,\dot{\alpha}}
to gauge away the second component
\vartheta
in
\omega^{\alpha(2s-2)}
, we will simply consider for most of the time

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha(2s-2)}=h^{\beta\dot{\beta}}A_{\alpha(2s-2)\beta\,\dot{\beta}}.

(2.9)

Now, at the free level the FDA for chiral HSGRA for
\Lambda=0
is simple

\displaystyle\nabla\omega_{\alpha(2s-2-k)\,\dot{\alpha}(k)}

\displaystyle=h_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\beta}}\omega_{\alpha(2s-3-k)\,\dot{\alpha}(k)\dot{\beta}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt0\leq k\leq 2s-2\,,

(2.10a)

\displaystyle\nabla\omega_{\dot{\alpha}(2s-2)}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{H}^{\dot{\beta}\dot{\beta}}C_{\dot{\alpha}(2s-2)\dot{\beta}\dot{\beta}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{H}^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\alpha}}:=h_{\gamma}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}\wedge h^{\gamma\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(2.10b)

\displaystyle\nabla C_{\alpha(k)\,\dot{\alpha}(2s+k)}

\displaystyle=h^{\beta\dot{\beta}}C_{\alpha(k)\beta\,\dot{\alpha}(2s+k)\dot{\beta}}\,,

(2.10c)

\displaystyle\nabla B^{\alpha(2s+k)\,\dot{\alpha}(k)}

\displaystyle=h_{\beta\dot{\beta}}B^{\alpha(2s+k)\beta\,\dot{\alpha}(k)\dot{\beta}}\,,

(2.10d)

where

are the

-generated auxiliary fields of

\nabla

2.6

Since the next (auxiliary) fields can be generated by the previous (auxiliary) fields by acting
\nabla
on the physical fields again and again, the whole spectrum of fields can be obtained starting from the
chiral fields

2.2.2
Vertices

The next ingredients we need are the structure maps/vertices
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{U})

\displaystyle\mathsf{F}(\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{n}):=\mathsf{F}(y,\tilde{y},\partial_{i\,\alpha},\tilde{\partial}_{i\,\dot{\alpha}})\Phi_{1}(y_{1},\tilde{y}_{1})\ldots\Phi_{n}(y_{n},\tilde{y}_{n})\Big|_{y_{i},\tilde{y}_{i}=0}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\Phi=(\omega,\mathsf{C})\,.

(2.11)

at the end of each expression. Here,
\mathsf{C}=(C,B)
is the master 0-form field, which contains both negative-helicity
B
’s and positive-helicity
C
’s fields and the scalar field

\star
-product

The first structure map in the minimal model of interest is the binary structure map
\mathsf{F}_{2}
. It is given by a graded associative star-product:

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}\star\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle=\exp\Big(Y^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{1}^{A}}+Y^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{2}^{A}}-I^{AB}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{1}^{A}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{2}^{B}}\Big)\Phi_{1}(Y_{1})\Phi_{2}(Y_{2})

\displaystyle=\exp\Big(\langle y\,\partial_{1}\rangle+\langle y\,\partial_{2}\rangle+\Lambda\langle\partial_{1}\,\partial_{2}\rangle+[y\,\partial_{1}]+[y\,\partial_{2}]+[\partial_{1}\,\partial_{2}]\Big)\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2}\,,

(2.12)

where
\Lambda
is the cosmological constant,
I^{AB}
is the infinity twistor
[
40
]
obeying

\displaystyle I^{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}&0\\
0&\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\end{pmatrix}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptI_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}&0\\
0&\Lambda\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\end{pmatrix}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptI_{AC}I^{BC}=\Lambda\delta_{A}{}^{B}\,.

(2.13)

Note that

\displaystyle\langle y\,\partial_{i}\rangle=y^{\alpha}\partial_{i\alpha}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\langle\partial_{i}\,\partial_{j}\rangle=\partial_{i}^{\alpha}\partial_{j\alpha}\,;\hskip 20.00003pt[y\,\partial_{i}]=\tilde{y}^{\dot{\alpha}}\tilde{\partial}_{i\dot{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt[\partial_{i}\,\partial_{j}]=\tilde{\partial}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}\tilde{\partial}_{j\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(2.14)

where
\partial_{i\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}^{\alpha}}
,
\tilde{\partial}_{i\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{y}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}}
. Here,
e^{\langle y\,\partial_{i}\rangle}
may be referred to as a shift operator with the property
e^{\langle y\,\partial_{i}\rangle}\Phi(y_{i})=\Phi(y_{i}+y)
. We will use the following convention to raise and lower spinorial indices

\displaystyle v^{\alpha}=v_{\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptv_{\alpha}=v^{\beta}\epsilon_{\beta\alpha}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptv^{\dot{\alpha}}=v_{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptv_{\dot{\alpha}}=v^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(2.15)

The Weyl algebra
\mathsf{A}_{2}
of polynomial functions in
Y^{A}=(y^{\alpha},\tilde{y}^{\dot{\alpha}})
defined in terms of the
\star
-product cf. (
2.12
) admits the decomposition
\mathsf{A}_{2}=\mathsf{A}_{1}(y)\otimes\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}_{1}(\tilde{y})
where the subscript denotes the number of canonical pairs. We then define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{hs}_{\Lambda}:=\mathsf{A}_{1}\otimes\big(\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}_{1}\otimes\mathtt{Mat}(n,\mathbb{K})\big)\,,

(2.16)

to be the underlying higher-spin algebra of the system. Note that when

, the Poisson structure defined by the infinity twistor is degenerate, and
\mathsf{A}_{1}
becomes a commutative algebra while

remains to be an non-commutative (NC) algebra. This is the setting that we consider in this work.

3.5

\displaystyle S_{\chi HSGRA}^{SD\,pp-wave}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle\mathsf{C}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}\wedge D\mathcal{A}\rangle_{y}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}:=\boldsymbol{e}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{e}_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}y^{\alpha}y^{\alpha}\,.

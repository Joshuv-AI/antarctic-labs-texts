Comprehensive psychosocial support in CBRNE incidents: a systematic review of strategies for citizens and frontline personnel
Authors: Sajjad Narimani, Hesam Seyedin, Shandiz Moslehi, Asghar Tavan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCBRNE (Chemical, Biological, Radiological, Nuclear, and Explosive) incidents can significantly disrupt the natural order of human life and pose serious psychosocial challenges. This study aims to identify the components of psychosocial support available to citizens and frontline personnel during such events.MethodsA systematic review was conducted by searching PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Embase databases up to July 2024. All articles that met the inclusion criteria and passed the screening process were included in the review. The screening process followed PRISMA guidelines. Thematic content analysis was conducted using Braun and Clarke’s six-step framework. Extracted data were coded, categorized, and synthesized into key psychosocial support themes.ResultsA total of 14,365 articles were initially identified. After the screening process, 29 studies were included, and 11 additional relevant studies from Google Scholar were added, resulting in a final sample of 40 studies. Thematic analysis revealed four primary categories of psychosocial support: behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and spiritual, further divided into 12 subcategories.ConclusionsPsychosocial support measures in CBRNE incidents encompass a broad range of individual, community-based, and organizational strategies implemented during and after events. Effective risk reduction planning in all identified dimensions is essential to prevent psychosocial disorders and enhance resilience in the face of such complex emergencies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12873-025-01409-3.Keywords: Psychosocial support, CBRNE incidents, Citizens, Frontline personnel

Introduction

Human-made disasters profoundly disrupt individual and societal well-being. Among the most complex and hazardous are incidents involving Chemical, Biological, Radiological, Nuclear, and Explosive (CBRNE) agents. These threats, whether accidental, deliberate, or environmental in nature, have become increasingly relevant in today’s world due to scientific, industrial, and geopolitical developments [1, 2]. The intersection of CBRNE threats with global challenges such as climate change and transnational mobility has further intensified their potential impact on public health and safety.

CBRNE agents are deeply embedded in modern society. Their applications in medicine, industry, military operations, and scientific research are essential and, in many cases, irreplaceable. However, their dual-use nature makes them inherently risky. The consequences of accidental release, malicious use, or large-scale dissemination—such as in bioterrorism or industrial accidents—can be devastating [3–5]. Moreover, climate change adds a new layer of complexity by contributing to the release of long-dormant pathogens from thawing permafrost, increasing the activity of biological agents, and exacerbating environmental instability [6].

The COVID-19 pandemic served as a stark reminder of how rapidly biological threats can disrupt societies. Global connectivity enabled the rapid transmission of the virus, while gaps in emergency preparedness and social cohesion were laid bare [7]. Similarly, past events such as the 2001 anthrax attacks in the U.S. and the 2005 London bombings illustrate that despite technological advancements, the psychological and social consequences of CBRNE events remain significant and often under-addressed [8, 9].

Psychological responses to CBRNE incidents commonly include fear, anxiety, panic, and acute stress reactions. When not managed effectively, these can progress to long-term conditions such as depression, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), and personality disturbances [10]. While various guidelines have been developed to prevent or mitigate these effects [2, 3], the unpredictable and unfamiliar nature of CBRNE threats often leads to fragmented management approaches, particularly in the psychosocial domain. In many cases, informal and inconsistent support mechanisms replace structured psychosocial care, limiting the effectiveness of crisis response [11, 12].

Equally important is the recognition that psychosocial impacts are not confined to the general population. Frontline personnel, such as emergency medical workers, nurses, firefighters, toxicologists, and law enforcement officers, are particularly vulnerable to psychological stress due to prolonged exposure, high-stakes decision-making, and lack of adequate support [13]. Despite receiving technical and operational training, these individuals are not immune to emotional exhaustion, especially in CBRNE contexts, where the fear of contamination or mass casualties can be overwhelming [3, 11].

Social support plays a crucial role in mitigating these effects. Studies show that consistent psychological and social support can enhance individual resilience, reduce the duration of the denial and avoidance phases, and facilitate more adaptive engagement in recovery efforts [14, 15]. However, continuity of social support is highly context-dependent and influenced by cultural factors. In an increasingly mobile and globalized world, individuals affected by a disaster may not identify with the cultural or community norms of the location where it occurs. Thus, a culturally informed and context-sensitive approach is essential for delivering adequate psychosocial support [8, 16].

A growing body of literature has explored various aspects of psychological and social strategies in disaster settings, including trauma-informed care, peer-support programs, and community resilience-building. However, previous systematic reviews have often focused narrowly on medical or operational aspects of CBRNE incidents, with limited attention to psychosocial strategies tailored to both citizens and frontline personnel [9, 17, 18]. Furthermore, few studies provide an integrated analysis of behavioral, cultural, and social components that influence intervention effectiveness.

What distinguishes this systematic review is its comprehensive and dual-focused approach. Unlike previous reviews that have predominantly concentrated on the medical, logistical, or operational aspects of CBRNE response [9, 17, 18] or have treated the needs of citizens and frontline personnel in isolation, this review explicitly synthesizes psychosocial strategies for both groups within a single, integrated framework. This study not only maps the available strategies but also critically examines the cultural, behavioral, and structural dimensions that underpin their success or failure. By identifying and categorizing a wide array of support mechanisms into behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and spiritual domains, this review provides a novel, multidimensional taxonomy of psychosocial support. This holistic framework is essential for developing culturally sensitive and sustainable interventions, thereby addressing a significant gap in the existing CBRNE literature.

Methods

Study design

This study was conducted as a systematic review aimed at identifying and synthesizing psychosocial support components available to citizens and frontline personnel during CBRNE incidents. Given the diversity and heterogeneity of study designs and outcomes in this field, a qualitative approach using thematic content analysis was chosen over quantitative meta-analysis. This method enables an in-depth exploration of complex psychosocial themes across diverse contexts and populations.

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search was conducted in July 2024 across four major databases: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Scopus, supplemented by searches on Google Scholar. Keywords were selected through team discussions and by reviewing Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms and relevant article keywords. The search strategy was customized for each database to maximize the retrieval of relevant studies concerning psychosocial support in CBRNE contexts (Table 1).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Included studies comprised original research articles, communications, opinions, and editorials published in English that addressed psychosocial support during CBRNE incidents. Reviews, articles without accessible full texts, or published in languages other than English were excluded.

The study selection followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines to ensure transparency and reproducibility [19]. Quality assessment

To assess the quality of the articles, the validated and well-documented Critical Appraisal Skills Programme (CASP) checklists and the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal tools were used. Two reviewers independently assessed each study, and discrepancies were resolved by consensus. The limitations identified in the quality appraisal informed the cautious interpretation of findings and were considered when formulating conclusions and recommendations.

Data extraction and management

Key information from included studies, such as authorship, publication year, country, study design, objectives, and main findings, was extracted into a structured Excel spreadsheet to facilitate descriptive analysis and thematic synthesis.

Thematic content analysis

Thematic analysis was conducted following Braun and Clarke’s six-step framework to identify patterns related to psychosocial support components:

1. Familiarization: Researchers immersed themselves in the data by repeatedly reading study results and extracting relevant texts to develop an initial understanding.

2. Generating Initial Codes: The data were systematically coded to capture relevant features of psychosocial strategies. Codes were generated independently by two researchers to enhance consistency.

3. Searching for Themes: Related codes were grouped into preliminary themes representing broader psychosocial concepts.

4. Reviewing Themes: Themes were refined through iterative comparison against coded data and full-text articles to ensure coherence and representativeness.

5. Defining and Naming Themes: Clear definitions and names were assigned to each theme to reflect their scope and significance.

6. Writing Up: The final narrative synthesis integrated thematic findings, supported by representative excerpts from primary studies, to illustrate key psychosocial support components.

Inter-Coder reliability

To ensure the consistency and reliability of the coding process during thematic content analysis, inter-coder agreement was systematically evaluated. Two independent researchers conducted initial coding of the extracted qualitative data from the included studies. The coding process followed an inductive approach, whereby themes were identified based on recurring patterns and concepts within the data.

To measure inter-coder reliability, a random sample comprising 20% of the coded material was selected for comparison between coders. Cohen’s Kappa coefficient was calculated to assess the level of agreement beyond chance. The resulting Cohen’s Kappa value was 0.82, indicating a strong level of agreement.

Discrepancies between coders were minimal and primarily related to the hierarchical placement of sub-sub-themes within broader thematic categories. All discrepancies were resolved through consensus discussions, and, when necessary, consultation with a third senior reviewer was sought to maintain analytic rigor. This systematic approach to inter-coder reliability enhances the trustworthiness and validity of the thematic synthesis presented in this review.

Risk of bias and strength of evidence

Potential biases within individual studies and across the body of evidence were assessed qualitatively during the appraisal and synthesis phases. While no formal heterogeneity or meta-analytic statistics were applicable due to study diversity, the overall strength of evidence was considered in light of quality ratings and consistency of findings. The GRADE framework was reviewed for applicability, although it was not formally applied due to the qualitative nature of the data.

Results

The initial electronic search across four major databases (PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Scopus) yielded a total of 14,365 scientific articles. After removing duplicates, 11,553 unique records remained. Screening titles and abstracts led to the selection of 150 articles for full-text review. Of these, five full texts were not accessible, and 69 articles were excluded due to a lack of relevant psychosocial support components related to the research question, and 12 cases were review articles. Following quality assessment based on standardized checklists, 35 studies received scores of less than 50% and were excluded from the study.

Ultimately, 29 articles were included in the systematic review. Furthermore, 11 additional studies identified through manual searches also met the inclusion criteria, resulting in a total of 40 articles for final analysis (see Fig. 1 for the detailed PRISMA flow diagram).

Qualitative findings

Behavioral strategies

Behavioral support strategies were foundational in translating coping mechanisms into tangible actions, promoting daily functioning and community resilience during CBRNE incidents. The qualitative data from the included studies provide rich insights into the lived experiences and practical applications of these strategies. The following quotes illustrate these strategies in action:

1. Individual Strategies - Behaviors change to stay healthy & Home Visits.

• Quote: “As recommended by the team, I have started exercises and using mindfulness-based stress reduction. I feel better now.“ [38]

2. Social Strategies - Information Dissemination Management.

• Quote: “The most important principle of the support program was the dissemination of clear, simple, consistent, and easily understandable information to the community by a group of trained volunteers.“ [39]

3. Support - Support for the family’s livelihood.

• Quote: “People have lost their jobs … suddenly they have no money to run their house, repay the loans, chitty, etc. They have lost their hopes … leading to anxiety and depression. A few of them have attempted suicide, unable to bear the stress due to financial issues.“ [38]

Emotional strategies

Emotional support was identified as a critical component for mitigating psychological distress and fostering resilience among both citizens and frontline personnel during CBRNE incidents. The qualitative data underscore the importance of strategies that facilitate emotional regulation, self-compassion, and interpersonal connectedness in sustaining mental well-being amid crisis conditions. The following quotes illustrate the application and lived experience of these emotional support strategies:

1. Self-actualization - Emotional management.

• Quote: “Managing our own fears and anxieties was the first step before we could effectively care for others. We had to process the emotional toll to prevent burnout consciously.“ [35]

2. Connectedness - Bonding or talking with colleagues and friends.

• Quote: “Informal debriefings with my colleagues after a stressful shift were invaluable. Simply sharing our experiences and feelings created a sense of shared burden and mutual understanding that was incredibly supportive.” [20]

• Quote: “Talking to friends who were going through the same situation made me feel less alone. It normalized my fears and helped me cope with the immense pressure.” [31]

3. Connectedness - A strong sense of solidarity.

• Quote: “A powerful sense of solidarity emerged within the community. We were all in this together, and that collective spirit was a profound source of emotional strength for everyone.” [22]

Cognitive strategies

Cognitive support plays a vital role in helping individuals process and make sense of CBRNE incidents, manage stress, and rebuild a sense of control. The qualitative data highlights the importance of strategies that foster understanding, provide professional consultation, and enhance critical thinking to navigate the complex and often ambiguous nature of such crises. The following quotes illustrate the application and perceived value of these cognitive support strategies:

1. Consultation - Support for the expression of mental disorders.

• Quote: “Having a structured space to voice our anxieties and receive professional guidance was crucial. It prevented our stress from spiraling into more severe psychological issues.” [35]

2. Consciousness raising - Providing health literacy.

• Quote: “Clear, consistent public health messaging was our first line of defense against panic. When people understood the ‘why’ behind the protocols, their compliance and cooperation improved significantly.“ [25]

Spiritual strategies

Spiritual support, often an overlooked dimension, emerged as a fundamental source of strength, providing comfort, fostering hope, and creating a sense of meaning amidst the chaos of CBRNE events. It encompassed both religious practices and broader existential reflections on purpose and solidarity. The following quotes exemplify the role of spiritual strategies:

1. Hope - Strengthening hope through the promotion of spirituality.

• Quote: “In our darkest hours, it was our shared faith and the hope it gave us that kept us moving forward. It was a light that no disaster could extinguish.” [1]

2. Belief - Faith.

• Quote: “My faith was my anchor. Praying gave me a sense of peace and the strength to face another day of overwhelming fear and uncertainty.“ [51]

3. Belief - Establishing a clear objective and valuing the work.

• Quote: “Remembering the higher purpose of our mission – to save lives and serve our community – gave a profound meaning to our exhausting work and helped us persevere.” [8]

Discussion

CBRNE incidents (Chemical, Biological, Radiological, Nuclear, and Explosive) pose unique and multifaceted psychosocial challenges that differ significantly from those arising in natural disasters [2]. This systematic review synthesized psychosocial support components for citizens and frontline workers involved in high-impact events, offering a multidimensional framework that encompasses behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and spiritual domains.

This systematic review makes a distinct contribution to the literature by providing a comprehensive, integrated, and multidimensional synthesis of psychosocial support strategies for both citizens and frontline personnel in CBRNE contexts. Prior systematic reviews in this field have often had a narrower focus. For instance, some have concentrated primarily on the operational and medical management of CBRNE incidents, such as evacuation, triage, and decontamination [3, 57], while paying limited attention to the psychosocial domain. Others have explored psychosocial care but often from a singular perspective, such as general disaster mental health, without delving into the unique, fear-laden, and often invisible nature of CBRNE threats [9, 58]. A key novelty of our review lies in its dual population focus and its systematic identification and thematic organization of support into four core domains: behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and spiritual. This integrated framework reveals the interdependence of these domains and moves beyond the fragmented, often isolated treatment of psychosocial components found in earlier works. By doing so, it addresses a critical gap by offering a holistic model that can inform the development of more robust, context-sensitive, and inclusive preparedness and response plans.

Behavioral support emerged as foundational in promoting adaptive responses and community resilience. These findings align with prior research that emphasizes the role of behavioral strategies, such as public education and community-based preparedness, in enhancing disaster readiness [15, 22, 25]. However, while earlier reviews [57, 58] mainly focused on logistical aspects of emergency response, our review broadens the scope by integrating psychosocial factors such as daily routine regulation, protective behaviors, and family-oriented strategies. Notably, the inclusion of strategies that prioritize family livelihood support and frontline worker equipment highlights practical dimensions often neglected in psychosocial frameworks, thereby offering a more grounded and actionable perspective.

Emotional support was identified as essential for fostering resilience and reducing psychological strain among frontline personnel. While previous studies have discussed the general benefits of emotional regulation techniques [59, 60], this review contributes further by explicitly detailing and providing qualitative evidence for the role of informal strategies such as dark humor, music, and recreational activities in sustaining morale and group cohesion within the high-stress CBRNE environment, a nuance rarely captured in prior syntheses.

The emphasis on emotional bonding, especially peer support among colleagues, resonates with findings from trauma-informed care literature and is particularly pertinent in high-risk contexts like CBRNE incidents. Moreover, gender-specific concerns, particularly the security and well-being of female responders’ families, emerged as underrepresented but critical elements that require further investigation.

Cognitive support, including consciousness-raising, critical thinking, and trust-building strategies, is deeply interwoven with public health communication. Our results are consistent with the WHO and Sphere guidelines [61–63], which emphasize the importance of transparent and culturally sensitive communication to prevent panic and misinformation. However, this review uniquely highlights the impact of cognitive overload and mistrust in authorities during CBRNE scenarios, issues that are less frequently discussed in prior systematic reviews. This explicit focus on trust dynamics and information processing under conditions of extreme uncertainty represents a significant addition to the cognitive dimension of CBRNE response literature. Given the prevalence of misinformation in such crises, especially biological ones, this theme warrants priority in the design of interventions.

Spiritual support, often overlooked, emerged as a pivotal component. Themes of hope, belief, and meaning-making were found to enhance emotional endurance and behavioral compliance. Although existing literature has sporadically acknowledged spiritual factors [64, 65], our review systematically elevates its status from a peripheral coping mechanism to a central, cross-cutting component of long-term resilience. Notably, this domain extends beyond faith-based coping mechanisms to encompass existential meaning and collective solidarity, suggesting that spiritual care strategies adopt an inclusive and pluralistic approach. This conceptual expansion of spiritual support is a novel contribution of our thematic analysis.

In conclusion, the primary novelty and contribution of this review lie in its integrative and multidimensional framework. Simultaneously addressing the needs of citizens and frontline personnel, and synthesizing evidence across behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and spiritual domains, provides a more comprehensive and nuanced understanding of psychosocial support in CBRNE incidents than previously available. This holistic model serves as a foundational step towards developing evidence-based, comprehensive, and culturally adaptable interventions that are crucial for enhancing resilience in the face of these complex threats.

Limitations

This systematic review has several key limitations that must be acknowledged:

• Publication Bias: Our search strategy focused on peer-reviewed articles, thereby excluding unpublished studies, null results, and grey literature (e.g., NGO reports, field manuals). This exclusion could result in an overrepresentation of positive findings or popular themes.

• Language Bias: Only studies published in English were included, potentially omitting relevant research from non-English-speaking countries. Given the global distribution of CBRNE events, this limits cultural generalizability.

• Methodological Heterogeneity: Included studies varied widely in design and rigor, encompassing expert opinion, qualitative interviews, observational data, and case studies. This heterogeneity hindered direct comparisons and precluded meta-analysis.

• Quality difference: Quality variability among studies also impacts the strength of the conclusions. Some included articles lacked robust methodological frameworks, standardized outcome measures, or precise definitions of psychosocial support components, which could influence the consistency and validity of extracted data.

• Missing some topics: We acknowledge that some relevant indexed terms were not included in the initial search strategy due to the broad scope of the topic. It is suggested that future reviews consider additional terms for broader coverage.

• Contextual Limitations: The cultural, geopolitical, and institutional contexts varied across studies but were not always explicitly discussed, potentially obscuring factors that affected the efficacy of strategies.

• Temporal Gaps and Longitudinal Limitations: Few studies assessed long-term psychosocial outcomes. Most focused on immediate or short-term effects, limiting understanding of the impact of sustained strategies.

These limitations underscore the need for cautious interpretation of the results and emphasize the importance of conducting future high-quality, culturally sensitive research to establish a more comprehensive and generalizable evidence base.

Practical implications and policy relevance

Translating these themes into practice requires integrated psychosocial support programs that are culturally adaptable and embedded in national preparedness frameworks. For example, establishing community education campaigns that incorporate behavioral and cognitive elements can enhance disaster literacy and preparedness. Frontline workforce policies should incorporate emotional and spiritual support structures, such as peer-support groups and access to chaplaincy services, to maintain morale and reduce burnout. The need for trust-building and combating misinformation highlights the importance of coordinated risk communication strategies, which may leverage digital platforms and community leaders.

These recommendations align with global humanitarian standards, including those of WHO and OCHA, which emphasize multi-sectoral collaboration in disaster mental health [66, 67].

Policymakers should also consider gender-sensitive approaches, ensuring family safety plans are integral to frontline worker support, thereby enabling optimal performance during crises.

Future research directions

Future studies should aim to develop standardized frameworks that integrate behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and spiritual domains, possibly adapting and testing these models across diverse cultural contexts. Quantitative research is necessary to assess the effectiveness of specific psychosocial strategies during CBRNE incidents, thereby enhancing evidence-based policy recommendations. Additionally, exploring digital and community-led approaches to psychosocial support could enhance scalability and sustainability.

Conclusion

This review explored the psychosocial support components available to citizens and frontline personnel during CBRNE incidents. It identified four primary domains, including behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and spiritual strategies, each with distinct yet interdependent roles in enhancing psychological resilience and crisis adaptation.

While the review offers a comprehensive thematic synthesis, the conclusions drawn are inherently exploratory and reflective of the qualitative, heterogeneous nature of the evidence base. Therefore, claims regarding causality or universal applicability must be interpreted cautiously.

Nevertheless, the integration of emotional bonding, cognitive awareness, spiritual motivation, and behavioral adaptation offers a valuable framework for strengthening public health resilience in future CBRNE scenarios. Applying these insights to policy, training, and global preparedness frameworks could meaningfully improve psychosocial outcomes across diverse populations.

Universality Classes of delocalization-localization transitions in Chiral Symplectic Class
Shiyin Kuang, Tong Wang, Zhenyu Xiao, Pengwei Zhao, Ryuichi Shindou

Abstract
By a simulation study of three-dimensional (3D) and two-dimensional (2D) disordered lattice models in the chiral symplectic class, we show that one-dimensional (1D) weak topology universally induces an intermediate quasi-localized (QL) phase between metal and Anderson-localized phases, in which the localization length of wave functions is divergent only along the spatial direction associated with the weak topological index. Our numerical evaluation of the critical exponents of the metal-to-QL transition and the Anderson transition (in the absence of the weak topology) demonstrates that they belong to different universality classes. We also confirm that the critical exponents of these two transitions in the chiral symplectic class significantly differ from those in the chiral unitary and chiral orthogonal classes, highlighting the impact of Kramers time-reversal symmetry on quantum critical behavior.

III.5
Kernel polynomial expansion

The density of states (DOS) of the 3D non-topological model
H_{\rm NT}
is calculated using the kernel polynomial method (KPM)
Weiße et al. 2006
, providing complementary information about the model. The lower and upper bounds
-E_{\mathrm{b}}
and
E_{\mathrm{b}}
of the eigenvalues of
H_{\rm NT}
are estimated by the Lanczos algorithm
Lanczos 1950
. We rescale the Hamiltonian by dividing a factor
E_{\rm b}/(1-\epsilon)
with
\epsilon=0.01
, so that the eigenvalues of the rescaled Hamiltonian
\tilde{E}\in[-1,1]
. The DOS as a function of
\tilde{E}
is expanded by the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind
T_{n}(x)=\cos(n\arccos x)
,

\tilde{\rho}(\tilde{E})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tilde{E}^{2}}}\{g_{0}\mu_{0}T_{0}+2\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}g_{n}\mu_{n}T_{n}(\tilde{E})\}.

(13)

Here
\mu_{n}
is the expansion moment;
g_{n}
is the Jackson kernel
Jackson 1912
, which suppresses the Gibbs oscillations due to a finite truncation of the order of expansion. The moments are evaluated stochastically by
Skilling 1989
;
Drabold and Sankey 1993
;
SILVER and RÖDER 1994

\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{D}\mathrm{Tr}(T_{n}(\tilde{H}))\approx\frac{1}{DR}\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}\langle r|T_{n}(\tilde{H})|r\rangle,

(14)

where
D
is the dimension of
H
,
|r\rangle
is a random vector, and
R
is the number of random vectors. We use
R=30
in all calculations.

V
Conclusion

In this work, we investigate disorder-driven quantum phase transitions in the chiral symplectic class based on 3D models with and without 1D weak topology. We determine the universal critical exponent for the 3D Anderson transition in this symmetry class [Fig.
4
(a)] and find that the estimated exponent aligns with previous results for a 3D non-Hermitian class-AII model
Luo et al. 2022
.

Furthermore, we elucidate how 1D weak topology modifies the Anderson transition in the chiral symplectic class. Specifically, the presence of the weak topology splits the delocalization-localization transition into a two-step process, inducing an intermediate quasi-localized (QL) phase between the diffusive metal and Anderson insulator phases [Fig.
4
(b)]. This finding, together with earlier reports of QL phases in chiral orthogonal and unitary classes
Xiao et al. 2023
, is consistent with a Berry-phase mechanism of the emergence of the intermediate QL phase
Zhao et al. 2024
;
Zhao and Shindou 2025
. Notably, the critical exponent of the metal-to-QL transition in the chiral symplectic class differs significantly from those in other chiral symmetry classes, underscoring the nontrivial role of Kramers time-reversal symmetry in the critical nature of the metal-to-QL transition.

By extending the 3D analyses to 2D systems, we also demonstrate that these phenomena persist in the reduced dimension. The critical behavior and emergence of the QL phase in 2D models closely mirror those in 3D, reinforcing the robustness of the weak-topology-induced phases. Our results establish the QL phase as a universal feature of chiral symplectic systems with 1D weak topology.

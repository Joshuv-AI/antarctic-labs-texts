On some functionals involving torsional rigidity, principal eigenvalue and perimeter
Vincenzo Amato, Carlo Nitsch, Cristina Trombetti, Federico Villone

Abstract
In this paper we study some relationships between the first Dirichlet eigenvalue Λ ⁡ ( Ω ) \Lambda(\Omega) and the torsional rigidity T ⁡ ( Ω ) T(\Omega) of a domain Ω \Omega . We consider the problem of optimizing the product Λ ⁡ ( Ω ) ​ T ​ ( Ω ) \Lambda(\Omega)T(\Omega) among sets with prescribed perimeter, both in the class of open sets with finite perimeter and within the class of convex domains. We also present local results for the quantity Λ ⁡ ( Ω ) ​ T ​ ( Ω ) q \Lambda(\Omega)T(\Omega)^{q} , with q > 0 q>0 , under either a volume or a perimeter constraint. Keywords:Pólya functional, Shape optimization, Stability in shape optimization MSC 2020: 49Q10, 35J25, 49K20, 35J05.

Introduction

We study how the shape of a domain influences two fundamental quantities associated with the Laplacian: the
torsional rigidity
, which measures the resistance of a domain to twisting forces, and the
first Dirichlet eigenvalue
, corresponding to its principal vibration frequency. These quantities capture complementary aspects of a domain’s geometry: torsional rigidity relates to elasticity and mechanical stability, while the first eigenvalue is linked to wave propagation and heat diffusion. Understanding their interplay has been a central question in shape optimization since the early 20th century, notably in the seminal work of Pólya
[
38
]
.

In our paper, we focus on the problem of optimizing their product among domains with a prescribed perimeter.
For a measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we define its De Giorgi perimeter as

P(\Omega):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\,\varphi\,dx:\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),\ \|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\},

and we denote by
V(\Omega)
its Lebesgue measure. By the isoperimetric inequality, any set with finite perimeter also has finite measure.

For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
with finite measure, the torsional rigidity of
\Omega
is defined as

T(\Omega):=\max_{\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,dx\right)^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx},

(1.1)

or, equivalently,

T(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}w\,dx,

where
w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is the unique solution to the Poisson problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=1&\text{in }\Omega,\\
w=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.2)

The torsional rigidity of a set
\Omega
satisfies the scaling property

T(t\Omega)=t^{m+2}T(\Omega)\qquad\forall t>0.

By the Saint–Venant inequality, conjectured in
[
42
]
and proved in
[
39
]
, the torsional rigidity is maximized by balls among domains of fixed volume. As a consequence, balls also maximize the torsional rigidity among domains with prescribed perimeter. In a scaling-invariant form, this reads as

P(\Omega)^{-\frac{m+2}{m-1}}T(\Omega)\leq P(B)^{-\frac{m+2}{m-1}}T(B),

(1.3)

where
B
denotes a ball.

Similarly, the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplacian on
\Omega
is defined as

\Lambda(\Omega):=\min_{\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,dx},

or equivalently, it can be characterized as the smallest
\lambda>0
such that the boundary value problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

admits a non-trivial solution
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

The first Dirichlet eigenvalue of a set
\Omega
satisfies the scaling property

\Lambda(t\Omega)=t^{-2}\Lambda(\Omega),\qquad\forall t>0,

By the Faber–Krahn inequality, conjectured in
[
41
]
and proved in
[
25
]
and
[
31
]
,the first Dirichlet eigenvalue is minimized by balls among domains of fixed volume. As a consequence, balls also minimize the first eigenvalue among domains with prescribed perimeter. In a scaling-invariant form, this reads as

P(\Omega)^{\frac{2}{m-1}}\Lambda(\Omega)\geq P(B)^{\frac{2}{m-1}}\Lambda(B).

(1.4)

As mentioned above, the main goal of this paper is to optimize the product

T(\Omega)\,\Lambda(\Omega)

under perimeter constraint.
Equivalently, for each
\Omega\in\mathcal{O}
, where

\mathcal{O}:=\{\text{open subsets of }\mathbb{R}^{m}\text{ with finite perimeter}\},

(1.5)

we consider the scale-invariant functional

G(\Omega):=\frac{T(\Omega)\,\Lambda(\Omega)}{P(\Omega)^{\frac{m}{m-1}}}.

(1.6)

By inequalities (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), the optimization of
G(\Omega)
is non-trivial, since we can rewrite it as a product:

G(\Omega)=\left[P(\Omega)^{-\frac{m+2}{m-1}}\,T(\Omega)\right]\left[P(\Omega)^{\frac{2}{m-1}}\,\Lambda(\Omega)\right].

Hence, we can observe a competition between the two functionals, which exhibit opposite behaviours on the ball.

The same competition can be observed in many other functionals, see for instance
[
2
,
4
,
9
,
8
,
32
]
, and in particular in the scaling invariant functional

F(\Omega):=\dfrac{\Lambda(\Omega)T(\Omega)}{\Vol(\Omega)},\qquad\forall\Omega\in\mathcal{O},

(1.7)

introduced by Pólya in
[
38
]
. In the following, we will refer to the functional
F
also as the Pólya functional. The Blaschke-Santaló diagram associated with this functional is explored in
[
35
]
.

The functional
G
introduced in (
1.6
) is closely related to the Pólya functional
F
in (
1.7
), since

G(\Omega)=F(\Omega)\,V(\Omega)\,P(\Omega)^{-\frac{m}{m-1}}.

(1.8)

This relation provides useful insight into the behaviour of
G
, as several of its properties can be established analogously to those of
F
.

In particular, in
[
10
]
and
[
6
]
the authors proved that

\inf_{\Omega\in\mathcal{O}}F(\Omega)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{\Omega\in\mathcal{O}}F(\Omega)=1,

(1.9)

and that neither of these extremal values is attained. The latter result, combined with (
1.8
), leads us to the following proposition.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
G
be the functional defined in (
1.6
) and
\mathcal{O}
be the set defined in (
1.5
). Then

\inf_{\Omega\in\mathcal{O}}G(\Omega)=0,\qquad\sup_{\Omega\in\mathcal{O}}G(\Omega)=\displaystyle m^{\frac{m}{m-1}}\omega_{m}^{-\frac{1}{m-1}},

where
\omega_{m}
denotes the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Furthermore, neither of these extremal values is attained within the class
\mathcal{O}
.

A minimizing sequence can be obtained, for example, by considering sets that become increasingly thin, for which
G(\Omega_{n})
tends to zero.
Conversely, a maximizing sequence can be constructed by means of a suitable homogenization of a ball. In this way, the functional
F(\Omega_{n})
approaches
1
, and by carefully adjusting the radii of the holes, the corresponding isoperimetric ratio in (
1.8
) can be controlled.

After studying the optimization problem in the class
\mathcal{O}
, it is natural to investigate how the situation may change under additional geometric constraints. In particular, we focus on the subclass of convex domains within
\mathcal{O}
, denoted by

\mathcal{C}:=\{\text{open, bounded, and convex subsets of }\mathbb{R}^{m}\}.

(1.10)

Moving to the convex class
\mathcal{C}
, several known results on the Pólya functional already provide significant information about the behavior of
F
. In particular, as proved in
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
14
, Remark 4.1]
, one has

\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{4}\dfrac{1}{m(m+2)}<F(\Omega)<1-\varepsilon,

(1.11)

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Sharper lower bounds within restricted classes of convex planar sets can be found in
[
7
, Theorems 1.4 & 1.5]
.

In
[
5
]
, it is conjectured that the supremum of
F
over
\mathcal{C}
is approached by sequences of collapsing cones, while the infimum is approached by sequences of collapsing cylindroids.

On the other hand, the behavior of
G
among
\mathcal{C}
is different; indeed, one can prove that if
\Omega_{n}
is a thinning sequence (defined in
subsection 2.1
),
G(\Omega_{n})
converges to
0
. Hence, a maximising sequence cannot collapse and we deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
G
be the functional defined in (
1.6
) and
\mathcal{C}
be the set defined in (
1.10
). Then

\inf_{\Omega\in\mathcal{C}}G(\Omega)=0,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{\Omega\in\mathcal{C}}G(\Omega)=\max_{\Omega\in\mathcal{C}}G(\Omega).

In the planar case (
m=2
), we are able to show that any maximizer of
G
over
\mathcal{C}
enjoys additional regularity compared to other admissible sets.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
G
be the functional defined in (
1.6
), and let
\mathcal{C}
be the set defined in (
1.10
). Let
m=2
and let
\tilde{\Omega}\in\mathcal{C}
satisfy

G(\tilde{\Omega})=\max_{\Omega\in\mathcal{C}}G(\Omega),

then
\tilde{\Omega}
is of class
C^{1}
.

Whether the maximum in
section 1
is attained by a ball remains an open problem; numerical simulations seem to support this conjecture.
In this direction, we prove that the ball is a
local maximizer
of
G
. More precisely, we optimize the functional
G
in the classes of
nearly spherical domains
, defined as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
B_{1}

denote the unit ball in

\R^{m}
. For all
h\in C^{0}(\partial B_{1})
such that
\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})}<\frac{1}{2}
, we denote as
B_{h}
the set defined through its boundary as

\partial B_{h}:=\set{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:x=(1+h(y))y,\,y\in\partial B_{1}}.

For any
\delta,\gamma>0
, we define the class
S_{\delta,\gamma}
of nearly spherical sets as

S_{\delta,\gamma}:=\set{B_{h}:h\in C^{2,\gamma}(\partial B_{1}),\quad\int_{\partial B_{1}}h\,d\mathcal{H}^{m-1}=0,\quad\int_{\partial B_{1}}hx\,d\mathcal{H}^{m-1}=0\,\,\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|h\right\|_{C^{2,\gamma}}\leq\delta}.

(1.12)

We now establish a local optimality and stability result for the ball within the class of nearly spherical sets.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be the functional defined in (
1.6
), and let
\gamma\in(0,1)
. There exists
\delta>0
and a positive constant
C=C(m,\delta)
such that for every

(defined in (
1.12
))

G(B_{1})-G(B_{h})>C\left\|h\right\|_{H^{1}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}.

The constant appearing in
Theorem 1.5
is not given explicitly.

Moreover, we use similar techniques to establish local optimality results for other Pólya-type functionals.
In particular, for any

Notation and Preliminaries

This section is divided into two parts. In the first part, we recall the main definitions and properties of convex sets, while in the second part, we summarize the key results on shape derivatives that will be used throughout the paper.

2.1
Some Properties of Convex Sets

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, open, and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The
support function
of
\Omega
is defined as

h_{\Omega}(y)=\sup_{x\in\Omega}(x\cdot y),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Moreover, the
width
of
\Omega
in the direction
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is

\omega_{\Omega}(y)=h_{\Omega}(y)+h_{\Omega}(-y),

and, since the width is continuous with respect to the direction, there exists the
minimal width
of
\Omega
is

w(\Omega)=\min\{\omega_{\Omega}(y)\mid y\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}\}.

Moreover, we define the
diameter
of
\Omega
as

\diam(\Omega)\coloneqq\sup\{\lVert x-y\rVert_{\R^{m}}\mid x,y\in\Omega\}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\{\Omega_{n}\}
be a sequence of non-empty, bounded, open and convex sets of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We say that
\Omega_{n}
is a sequence of thinning domains if

\lim_{n}\dfrac{w({\Omega_{n}})}{\diam(\Omega_{n})}=0.

If we denote by
R_{\Omega}
the inradius of
\Omega
, i.e.

R_{\Omega}=\sup\{r\in\mathbb{R}:B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega\text{ for some }x\in\Omega\},

we have the following estimate, proved in
[
11
]
for the planar case and extended in
[
13
]
to arbitrary dimensions.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
13
, Lemma B.1 ]

For any
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}
, with
\mathcal{C}
defined in (
1.10
), we have

\dfrac{1}{m}\leq\dfrac{\Vol(\Omega)}{P(\Omega)R_{\Omega}}<1.

(2.1)

The upper bound is sharp for a sequence of thinning cylinders, while the lower bound is sharp, for example, on balls. Moreover, for
m=2
, any circumscribed polygon (i.e., a polygon whose incircle touches all sides) attains the lower bound with equality.

Finally, we recall the following relations between the diameter, perimeter, volume, and minimal width of a convex set.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
24
, Lemma 4.1]

For any
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}
, with
\mathcal{C}
defined in (
1.10
), then there exists a dimensional constant
C=C(m)
such that

\diam(\Omega)\leq C\dfrac{P(\Omega)^{m-1}}{V(\Omega)^{m-2}}.

(2.2)

Proposition 2.5
.

[
11
, Equation (9)]

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, open and convex set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{w(\Omega)}{2}\geq R_{\Omega}\geq\begin{cases}w({\Omega})\displaystyle{\frac{\sqrt{n+2}}{2n+2}}&n\,\,\text{even}\\

2.2
Shape Derivatives and Local Optimality

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
be a class of sets, and let
A:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
shape functional
, that is, a functional that assigns a real value to each set in
\mathcal{A}
.
In the following, we recall some classical properties of shape derivatives, which describe the sensitivity of
A
with respect to infinitesimal deformations of the set (see, for instance,
[
27
,
20
]
).

Theorem 2.6
.

(Structure Theorem of first and second shape derivatives,
[
27
, Theorem 5.9.2]
).
Let
\Omega\subset\R^{m}
be an open, bounded and
C^{3}
set. Let

\mathcal{V}(\Omega)=\set{(I+\theta)\Omega:\theta\in W^{1,\infty}(\R^{m},\R^{m}),\,{\left\|\theta\right\|}_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq 1},

and let
A
be a shape functional defined on
\mathcal{V}(\Omega)
.
Consider the function

A_{\Omega}:\theta\in\{\vartheta\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\R^{m}):\left\|\vartheta\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq 1\}\to A((I+\theta)(\Omega))\in\R,

and assume that
A_{\Omega}(\theta)
is twice Fréchet-differentiable in
0
.
Then

i.

there exists a continuous linear form
\ell_{1}[A](\Omega)
on
{C}^{1}(\partial\Omega)
such that

{A}_{\Omega}^{\prime}(0)\xi=\ell_{1}[A](\Omega)(\xi|_{\partial\Omega}\cdot n)

for all

\xi\in{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m})
, where
{n}
denotes the unit exterior normal vector on
\partial\Omega
.

ii.

there exists a continuous symmetric bilinear form
\ell_{2}[A](\Omega)
on
{C}^{2}(\partial\Omega)^{2}

such that for all
(\xi,\zeta)\in{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m})^{2}

{A}_{\Omega}^{\prime\prime}(0)(\xi,\zeta)=\ell_{2}[A](\Omega)(\xi\cdot n,\zeta\cdot n)+\ell_{1}[A](\Omega)\big(\mathbf{B}(\zeta_{\tau},\xi_{\tau}))-\nabla_{\tau}(\zeta\cdot n)\cdot\xi_{\tau}-\nabla_{\tau}(\xi\cdot n)\cdot\zeta_{\tau}\big),

where
\nabla_{\tau}
is the tangential gradient,
\xi_{\tau}
and
\zeta_{\tau}
are the tangential components of
\xi
and
\zeta
, and

\mathbf{B}=D_{\tau}n
is the second fundamental form of
\partial\Omega
.

When
\Omega
is the unit ball we drop the dependence of the set.

Proof of

Let us prove that
\displaystyle\inf_{\Omega\in\mathcal{O}}G(\Omega)=0

\set{\Omega_{n}}

\lim_{n}\dfrac{\Vol({\Omega_{n}})}{P(\Omega_{n})^{\frac{m}{m-1}}}=0.

Concerning the supremum of

over the class

,

We use the homogenization construction used in

]
(see also

]

We fix

a^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))\,a^{\prime}_{i}(t),

a^{\prime\prime}(t)=\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))\,a^{\prime}_{i}(t)a^{\prime}_{k}(t)\;+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))\,a^{\prime\prime}_{i}(t).

Since, for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the function
a_{i}
satisfies properties (2) and (3), it follows that the same estimates hold for
a
.

We now want to prove that
a
satisfy property (4). By direct computation, we have that

\begin{split}|a^{\prime\prime}(t)-a^{\prime\prime}(0)|&\leq\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\left|\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))\,a^{\prime}_{i}(t)a^{\prime}_{k}(t)-\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(a_{1}(0),\dots,a_{n}(0))\,a^{\prime}_{i}(0)a^{\prime}_{k}(0)\right|\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))\,a^{\prime\prime}_{i}(t)-\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1}(0),\dots,a_{n}(0))\,a^{\prime\prime}_{i}(0)\right|\\
&=:\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}R_{i,k}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}.\end{split}

(5.1)

For all
i=1,\dots.n
, we have that

S_{i}\leq|a^{\prime\prime}_{i}(t)-a^{\prime\prime}_{i}(0)|\left|\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))\right|+|a^{\prime\prime}_{i}(0)|\left|\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))-\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1}(0),\dots,a_{n}(0))\right|.

Hence, by the lipschitzianity of the first derivatives of
f
and
subsection 5.1
, we deduce

\left|\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))-\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1}(0),\dots,a_{n}(0))\right|=\omega(\left\|h\right\|_{X})

As a consequence, by assumptions (3) and (4), we obtain

S_{i}\leq\omega(\|h\|_{X})\,\|h\|_{Y}^{2},

(5.2)

We can repeat a similar argument in order to bound
R_{i,k}
from above, indeed

\begin{split}R_{i,k}&\leq|a_{i}^{\prime}(t)-a^{\prime}_{i}(0)||a^{\prime}_{k}(t)|\left|\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))\right|+|a^{\prime}_{i}(0)|\left|\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))\right||a^{\prime}_{k}(t)-a^{\prime}_{k}(0)|\\
&+|a^{\prime}_{i}(0)||a^{\prime}_{k}(0)|\left|\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(a_{1}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t))-\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(a_{1}(0),\dots,a_{n}(0))\right|.\end{split}

Using
subsection 5.1
and the Hölder continuity and the boundedness of the second derivatives of
f
, we obtain

R_{i,k}\leq\omega(\left\|h\right\|_{X})\left\|h\right\|_{Y}^{2}.

(5.3)

Using (
5.1
), (
5.3
) and (
5.2
) we obtain that

|a^{\prime\prime}(t)-a^{\prime\prime}(0)|\leq\omega(\left\|h\right\|_{X})\left\|h\right\|_{Y}^{2}.

∎

5.2
Criticality of the ball

In this section, we compute the first derivatives of

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote the unit ball.
Consider the functional
F_{q}
defined in (
1.13
), with
q>0
,
and the functional

defined in (

).
Then

is a critical shape for

and

(in the sense of

).

Proof.

Since

and

are scale invariant, it is sufficient to prove that if

has zero mean on

, then
\ell_{1}[F_{q}](\varphi)=\ell_{1}[G](\varphi)=0

5.3
On the coercivity conditions

In this section, we investigate the coercivity properties of the bilinear forms corresponding to the second derivatives of the functionals
F_{q}
and
G
(defined in
Theorem 2.6
).

To this end, we make use of spherical harmonics.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
denote the space of spherical harmonics of degree
k
, and let
\{Y^{k,\ell}\}_{1\leq\ell\leq N_{k}}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
with respect to the
L^{2}(\partial B_{1})
scalar product.
Then the collection
\{Y^{k,\ell}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N},\,1\leq l\leq N_{k}}
forms a Hilbert basis for
L^{2}(\partial B_{1})
, and any function
\varphi\in L^{2}(\partial B_{1})
can be expanded as:

\varphi(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{k}}\varphi_{k,l}\,Y^{k,l}(x),\quad\text{for }|x|=1.

(5.4)

Furthermore, the sequence
(\varphi_{k,l})
characterizes the Sobolev regularity of
\varphi
: we have
\varphi\in H^{s}(\partial B_{1})
if and only if the series

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+k^{2})^{s}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{k}}|\varphi_{k,\ell}|^{2}

is convergent.

Remark 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}^{0}(\partial B_{1})
be the functional space defined in (
2.4
).
If
\varphi\in L^{2}(\partial B_{1})
, then
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}^{0}(\partial B_{1})
if and only if
\varphi_{k,l}=0
for
k=0,1
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{2,\gamma}(\partial B_{1})
, with
{\left\|\varphi\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})}<\frac{1}{2}

and consider

the set defined through its boundary

We define the following functions

\tau:t\in[0,1]\to T(B_{t\varphi})\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\lambda:t\in[0,1]\to\Lambda(B_{t\varphi})\in\mathbb{R}.

It is well known (see, for instance,
[
27
, Chapter 5]
or
[
20
]
) that if

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\varphi\,d\mathcal{H}^{m-1}=0,

then the first derivatives of all the functions defined in

vanish at

.
Furthermore, in the following lemma we recall the expressions of their second derivatives.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let

, with

and the expansion of the function

in (

\displaystyle\vol^{\prime\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}(m-1)\,\varphi_{k,l}^{2},

We recall the recurrence formula for the Bessel functions (see

Using this formula with

Using again the formula with

Since

(5.5)

Moreover (see

\alpha=\dfrac{T^{q-1}(B_{1})\Lambda(B_{1})}{P^{\beta_{q}}(B_{1})m^{2}(m+2)(m-1)}.

Notice that if
q_{1}<q_{2}
, then for all
k\geq 2

G_{k}^{q_{1}}>G_{k}^{q_{2}}.

We analyse the different cases.

(i)

Let
q\leq\frac{2}{m+2}
. Since
\set{c_{k}}
is decreasing, we have that if
k_{1}\leq k_{2}
, then

G^{q}_{k_{1}}\leq G_{k_{2}}^{q}.

Therefore, since
G^{q}_{k}
is increasing in
k
and decreasing in
q
,
G^{q}_{k}>0
for all
k\geq 2
and
q\leq\dfrac{2}{m+2}
if and only if
G^{\frac{2}{m+2}}_{2}>0
.
This inequality holds, since, by
subsection 5.3
,

G_{2}^{\frac{2}{m+2}}=\alpha(6+4m-6-4c_{2})>0.

(6.5)

Since
\ell_{2}(G_{q})
admits a continuous extension to
H^{1}(\partial B_{1})
, if
q<\frac{2}{m+2}
, the ball
B_{1}
is a strictly stable shape for

G_{q}
in
H^{1}(\partial B_{1})
.

Moreover, since
2-q(m+2)>0
,
G_{k}^{q}
has the same asymptotic behaviour as
(1+k^{2})
for
k
that approaches
+\infty
, the functional
G_{q}
satisfies assumption
(
C
{}_{H^{s}}
)
with
s=1
.

(ii)

If
q=\frac{2}{m+2}
, we have that
G_{q}=F_{q}
. Therefore, the thesis follows from
(ii)
in
subsection 5.3
.

(iii)

Let
\dfrac{2}{2+m}<q<q^{\prime}
then we have that
[2-q(m+2)]<0
, so there exists
k^{*}
such that for
k>k^{*}
,
G_{k}^{q}<0
. Moreover, notice that
q^{\prime}
is such that

G_{2}^{q^{\prime}}=0.

Hence, for all
\dfrac{2}{m+2}<q<q^{\prime}

G_{2}^{q}>G^{q^{\prime}}_{2}=0

Since
G_{2}^{q}>0
and for
k
sufficiently large
G_{k}^{q}<0
,
B_{1}
is a saddle point for
G_{q}
.

(iv)

Let
q\geq q^{\prime}
. We want to prove that for all
k>2
,

G_{k}^{q}<G_{2}^{q}.

(6.6)

This inequality holds if and only if

4(m-1)(c_{k}-c_{2})>(k-2)(2-q(m+2))(k+2+3m-4).

(6.7)

For all
q\geq q^{\prime}
,

(2-q(m+2))\leq(2-q^{\prime}(m+2))=-\dfrac{4(m-1)(m-c_{2})}{3m-1},

hence (
6.7
), is true for all
q\geq q^{\prime}
if and only if

c_{k}>c_{2}-(k-2)\left(\dfrac{k+3m-2}{3m-1}\right)(m-c_{2}).

This inequality holds, since by
subsection 5.3

c_{2}-(k-2)\left(\dfrac{k+3m-2}{3m-1}\right)(m-c_{2})<c_{2}-(k-2)(m-c_{2})=c_{2}-(k-2)(c_{1}-c_{2})<c_{k}.

The last inequality follows by the existence of a convex extension of the function
k\in\mathbb{N}\to c_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, we have (
6.6
).

Furthermore, if
q=q^{\prime}
,
G_{2}^{q}=0
and for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)

\ell_{2}(G_{q})(\varphi,\varphi)\leq 0.

Moreover if
\psi\in\mathcal{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial B_{1})\setminus\{0\}
such that for all
k\geq 3

\psi_{k,l}=0
, we have that

\ell_{2}(G_{q})(\psi,\psi)=0.

(v)

As a consequence, if
q>q^{\prime}
, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
we have that

\ell_{2}(G_{q})(\varphi,\varphi)>0.

Hence, since
\ell_{2}(G_{q})
admits a continuous extension to
H^{1}(\partial B_{1})
, the ball
B_{1}
is a strictly stable shape for
-G_{q}
in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial B_{1})
.
Moreover,
-G_{k}^{q}
has the same asymptotic behaviour as
(1+k^{2})
for
k
that approaches
+\infty
. This ensures the functional
-G_{q}
satisfies assumption
(
C
{}_{H^{s}}
)
with
s=1
.

∎

The results of this section lead to the proof of
Theorem 6.3
following the one of
Theorem 1.6
.

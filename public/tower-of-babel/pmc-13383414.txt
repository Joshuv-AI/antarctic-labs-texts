Association of the red cell distribution width to total serum calcium ratio with severe AKI in patients undergoing cardiac surgery: a retrospective cohort study using the MIMIC-IV database
Authors: Xiangyang Li, Ke Zheng, Longhai He, Peipei Zhang, Meng Lv
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAcute kidney injury (AKI) is a common complication following cardiac surgery. The RCR (ratio of RDW to total serum calcium (TSC)), which exhibits high predictive value for various perioperative diseases, may also be associated with kidney injury. Therefore, we tested the hypothesis that the postoperative RCR index is associated with kidney injury following major cardiac surgery.MethodsThis was a retrospective observational cohort study. We included 7,872 patients who underwent cardiac surgery from the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV (MIMIC-IV) database. The exposure was the first postoperative measurements of red blood cell distribution width (RDW) and Total serum calcium (TSC). The primary outcome was severe AKI, defined as stage 3 AKI or new-onset dialysis. Secondary outcomes included AKI of any stage, length of hospital stay, and length of intensive care unit (ICU) stay.ResultsA total of 5,992 patients (76.1%) developed postoperative AKI, among whom 3,348 (42.5%) had stage 2 AKI, 654 (8.3%) had stage 3 AKI, and 160 (2.0%) required new-onset dialysis. In the multivariate regression model, the RCR index (categorical variable) was significantly associated with severe AKI (adjusted odds ratio [OR], 2.02 [95% CI, 1.54–2.68]) and AKI of any stage (OR, 1.26 [95% CI, 1.05–1.50]). When the RCR index was treated as a continuous variable, this association remained statistically significant.ConclusionsElevated postoperative RCR index is associated with postoperative AKI in patients who undergo cardiac surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13019-026-04255-x.Keywords: Cardiac surgery, Medical information mart for intensive care-iv (MIMIC-IV), Intensive care unit, Acute kidney injury, Ratio of RDW to total serum calcium (TSC), Multivariate logistic regression

Introduction

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a frequent complication following cardiac surgery, with its incidence remaining persistently high [1–3]. The mechanisms underlying cardiac surgery associated acute kidney injury (CS-AKI) are complex, including ischemia reperfusion injury, inflammation, oxidative stress, and hypoperfusion. AKI is associated with prolonged hospital length of stay and increased risk of progression to end-stage renal disease and long-term mortality [4, 5]. For instance, the 30-day mortality rate of patients with severe AKI is even eightfold higher [6]. However, there are no specific treatments for AKI, only symptomatic management [7, 8]. Thus, early identification of high-risk patients after cardiac surgery using simple indicators is crucial for providing personalized treatment.

Red blood cell distribution width (RDW) reflects the heterogeneity of red blood cell (RBC) volume sizes and is commonly used for classifying anemia. Additionally, RDW can indicate abnormal physiological states, such as telomere shortening, oxidative stress, RBC fragmentation, malnutrition and hypertension [9, 10]. Previous studies have also demonstrated that RDW can predict the prognosis of various diseases, including heart failure, acute myocardial infarction, acute pancreatitis and so on [11–16]. A study has also demonstrated that RDW is associated with in-hospital mortality and long-term survival in patients undergoing cardiac surgery [17]. Calcium is essential for physiological regulatory mechanisms, including muscle function, intracellular signal transduction, vasoconstriction, and vasodilation. Abnormal calcium levels are common in clinical practice, particularly in critically ill patients [18]. Calcium abnormalities are associated with poor prognosis of numerous diseases, such as acute renal failure, acute myocardial infarction, heart failure, coronary heart disease and acute osteomyelitis, and can also predict adverse postoperative outcomes (e.g., after cardiac surgery) [19–25]. Compared with complex indicators, both RDW and total serum calcium (TSC) are more easily accessible and convenient. Recent studies have identified the RCR (ratio of RDW to serum calcium) as a novel biomarker associated with poor prognosis of various diseases, including acute stroke, acute myocardial infarction, acute pancreatitis, diabetic nephropathy and acute respiratory failure [26–30]. However, no studies have explored the relationship between RCR and adverse prognosis after adult cardiac surgery.

Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the association between RCR and the risk of AKI following adult cardiac valve surgery or coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), providing a potentially relevant biomarker for the early identification of high-risk patients.

Methods

Study design

We conducted a retrospective study using the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV (MIMIC-IV) database.MIMIC-IV (version 3.1) is a public database containing critical care information of 65,366 patients admitted to intensive care unit (ICU). The MIMIC database was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (No. 2001-P-001699/14) and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (No. 0403000206). One author (Xiangyang Li) had access to the data and was responsible for data extraction (certification number: 69127390). Therefore, patient informed consent and ethical approval were exempted from this study. This study was reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement.

Participants

We extracted the information of the patients admitted to the ICU after cardiac surgery from the database. For patients with multiple surgeries, only the initial surgery was considered. Eligible patients were aged at least 18 years and had undergone coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), valve replacement or repair, or combined CABG and valve surgery. Exclusion criteria included: 1) confirmed preoperative AKI status; 2) missing RDW and TSC data (Fig. 1).

Variates

Based on the existing literatures and clinical experience, we extracted variables deemed relevant to cardiac surgery outcomes, categorized as follow: Demographic data: age, sex, and body mass index (BMI); Comorbidities: hypertension, liver cirrhosis, coronary heart disease, chronic kidney disease, type 1 diabetes mellitus, type 2 diabetes mellitus, heart failure, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), malignancy, myocardial infarction, atrial fibrillation, stroke, peripheral vascular disease, CRRT (recorded as a baseline characteristic only), and smoking; Medication information: angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEI), angiotensin receptor blockers (ARB), diuretics, lipid-lowering drugs, vasoactive drugs, and beta-blockers; Laboratory parameters: serum creatinine (Scr), hemoglobin, RBC count, white blood cell count, platelet count, RDW, and TSC; Outcome measures: AKI, severe AKI, length of hospital stay, and length of ICU stay. We also extracted the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score and the Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE) IV score. For baseline values, RDW, TSC, and other laboratory parameters (except Scr) were measured within 24 h after surgery.

Outcomes

According to the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) definition, the primary outcome was severe AKI occurring within 7 days after surgery, defined as stage 3 AKI or the need for postoperative continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT). Baseline Scr level was determined using the most recent available Scr measurement before surgery. AKI was defined by either of the following criteria: an increase of Scr by ≥ 0.3 mg/dl within 48 h after surgery, or an increase of Scr to ≥ 1.5 times the baseline Scr within 7 days after surgery. Urine output could also be used for staging if the interval between two recordings was < 6 h. Secondary outcomes included AKI of any stage within 7 days after surgery, length of hospital stay, and length of ICU stay.

Statistical analysis

Sample size calculation was not performed. Instead, we tried to include all the eligible patients from the database to enhance statistical power. We also took precautions to ensure that each variable in the multivariate model included at least 10 events to avoid overfitting.

Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range [IQR]), and categorical variables were presented as frequency and percentage. The Chi-square test or Fisher exact test was used for the comparison of categorical variables among the groups. Kruskal–Wallis test or analysis of variance was used for the comparison of continuous variables. Variables with > 20% missing values were excluded. Otherwise, multiple imputation was used for the treatment of missing values.

To determine whether RCR was independently associated with postoperative severe AKI, we used multiple logistic regression models to calculate odds ratios (OR) and 95%CI: Model 1: no adjustment for any variables; Model 2: adjusted for age, sex, and BMI; Model 3: adjusted for age, sex, BMI, surgical type, hypertension, stroke, type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes, heart failure, coronary heart disease, COPD, atrial fibrillation, peripheral vascular disease, smoking, SOFA score, and APACHE II score. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) method was used to investigate the association relationship between the baseline RCR index and postoperative severe AKI, AKI of any stage, hospital length of stay, and ICU length of stay.

Sensitivity analyses

To ensure the reliability of results, sensitivity analyses were performed. We restricted the analysis to the patients with complete data. For the primary outcome: Firstly, we tested the robustness of results by treating RCR as both a categorical and a continuous variable. Secondly, subgroup analyses were conducted based on age (< 60 or > 60 years), sex (male or female), surgical type (valve surgery, CABG, or combined surgery), hypertension (yes or no), stroke (yes or no), type 1 diabetes (yes or no), type 2 diabetes (yes or no), heart failure (yes or no), coronary heart disease (yes or no), COPD (yes or no), peripheral vascular disease (yes or no), smoking (yes or no), and SOFA score (< 6 or > 6). Multivariate logistic regression was used for subgroup analyses, and an interaction was considered significant if P for interaction < 0.05. For secondary outcomes, the same methods were used to analyze the association between RCR and AKI of any stage. Multiple linear regression was used for analyzing hospital length of stay and ICU length of stay. All statistical analyses were performed using Decision Link software and R software (version 4.3.1). A two-tailed P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

A total of 7,872 patients were included in this study. The mean age of all patients was 70.00 (62.00—77.00) years, and 5,570 (70.76%) were male. The mean RCR index was 1.72 ± 0.22. The incidence of severe AKI was 8.41%, while the incidence of AKI of any stage is 76.12%.

Baseline characteristics

Table 1 presents the baseline characteristics of patients stratified by RCR index quartiles (Q1: 0.49—1.57; Q2: 1.57—1.68; Q3: 1.68—1.82; Q4: 1.82—3.28). The mean RCR index in the four quartiles was 1.50 ± 0.07, 1.63 ± 0.03, 1.75 ± 0.04, and 2.01 ± 0.20, respectively. Patients in the Q4 group were older at admission and had higher disease severity scores. They also had a lower incidence of hypertension, higher incidences of liver disease, stroke, chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes, heart failure, COPD, atrial fibrillation, and peripheral vascular disease. In addition, the Q4 group had higher levels of creatinine, platelet count, and RDW, but lower levels of hemoglobin, RBC counts, and TSC. Compared with lower RCR quartile groups, patients in Q4 had higher frequency of ACEI use and lower frequency of beta-blocker and diuretic use. Valve surgery and combined surgery were more common in Q4 group. With the increase of RCR index, the incidences of acute renal failure (70.85% vs 75.93% vs 77.60% vs 80.12%, P < 0.001) and severe acute renal failure gradually increased (4.22% vs 6.55% vs 8.47% vs 14.46%, P < 0.001).With the increase of RCR index, the hospital length of stay and ICU length of stay prolonged.

When RCR was treated as categorical variable. In the unadjusted model, the highest RCR quartile (Q4) was also significantly associated with an increased risk of severe acute kidney injury (Q1 vs Q2: OR, 1.59 [95% CI: 1.20–2.12]; Q1 vs Q3: OR, 2.10 [95% CI: 1.61–2.77]; Q1 vs Q4: OR, 3.84 [95% CI: 2.99–4.98]); In the fully adjusted model, this association remained significant (Q1 vs Q2: OR, 1.37 [95% CI: 1.02–1.86]; Q1 vs Q3: OR, 1.45 [95% CI: 1.09–1.94]; Q1 vs Q4: OR, 2.02 [95% CI: 1.54–2.68]) Table 2.

As shown in figs. 2, 3 after adjusting for confounders, the analysis curve did not demonstrate a non-linear relationship between the RCR index and severe acute kidney injury (AKI) following cardiac surgery (P for non-linearity = 0.619). Furthermore, subgroup analyses were performed to examine the robustness of the association between RCR and severe AKI across different subgroups (including age, sex, surgical type, hypertension, stroke, type 2 diabetes, type 1 diabetes, heart failure, coronary heart disease, COPD, peripheral vascular disease, smoking, and SOFA score). The overall results of subgroup analyses were consistent with the primary findings. No significant interactions were observed between RCR and any subgroup, except for sex, surgical type, and type 1 diabetes. Figure. 4 notably, we also analyzed the association between RCR and AKI at any stage using the same method. Figure. 3 the RCS analysis for this association of RCR index with AKI at any stage showed a non-linear relationship (P for non-linearity = 0.015). Therefore, we divided patients into low RCR and high RCR groups using the inflection point as the cutoff, and further logistic regression analysis confirmed a significant association between RCR index and the risk of AKI at any stage (Table 3 and 4).

Secondary endpoints

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the association between postoperative RCR and the risk of severe AKI after cardiac surgery. We found that the RCR index (whether analyzed as a continuous variable or by quartiles) was associated with the increased risk of severe AKI after cardiac surgery. Notably, this association was also statistically significant after adjusting for potential confounders. We also analyzed the association between RCR index and AKI at any stage after cardiac surgery, and the results were consistent with the primary outcome. Additionally, elevated RCR was associated with prolonged hospital and ICU length of stay.

AKI at any stage was common, occurring in two third of the patients after cardiac surgery, and half of the patients had stage 2 or more severe injury—this is because we did not exclude the KDIGO urine output (UO) criteria in the analysis [31]. We believe that even non-severe AKI caused solely by oliguria is associated with poorer long-term outcomes, and excluding the KDIGO UO criteria may delay AKI detection and underestimate the incidence, severity, and associated morbidity and mortality of AKI [3, 32]. It is also worth noting that even in non-cardiac surgery, stage 1 AKI is a precursor to persistent kidney injury [33]. Furthermore, AKI after cardiac surgery is a major contributor to long-term of adverse outcomes, including advanced kidney disease, dialysis dependence, cardiovascular events, and death [4, 5].

In recent years, studies have found that the RCR index can predict the severity of acute pancreatitis and serve as a predictor of in-hospital mortality in patients with acute myocardial infarction and acute respiratory failure [27, 28, 30]. In addition,another study revealed the significant association between the RCR index and increased all-cause mortality in patients with diabetic nephropathy [26]. These findings suggest that RCR may be a clinically meaningful indicator. Our results were consistent with previous studies, showing that the patients with high RCR have an increased risk of AKI compared with those with low RCR. However, the underlying mechanism remains unclear.

Elevated RCR usually indicates high RDW and low total serum calcium level. RDW, the ratio of the standard deviation of RBC volume to the mean RBC volume, is widely used in the differential diagnosis of anemia—since elevated RDW is associated with ineffective RBC production (e.g., iron deficiency anemia, megaloblastic anemia due to vitamin B12 and folate deficiency, myelosuppression, and hemoglobinopathies), or increased RBC destruction (e.g., hemolysis) [34]. In recent years, studies have found that RDW is associated with the prognosis of many diseases, including acute myocardial infarction, heart failure, respiratory diseases, and pulmonary hypertension [11, 12, 14, 15]. The mechanism linking elevated RDW to poor prognosis remains unclear, but inflammation may be one of the most important factors. RDW can reflect the body’s inflammatory state, and inflammation impairs bone marrow function, reducing the efficiency of RBC production. This leads to the release of reticulocytes into the peripheral circulation, increasing the proportion of immature RBCs and thus elevating RDW [34, 35]. Since inflammation plays a crucial role in the development and progression of AKI, which may explain why the risk of AKI after cardiac surgery increases with elevated RDW [36]. Additionally, factors such as anemia, oxidative stress, and thrombosis, which often cause elevated RDW and may also increase the risk of AKI [37].

Calcium plays a key role in many physiological processes, such as muscle contraction, neural signal transmission, intracellular signal transduction, and hormone secretion. Previous studies have shown that serum calcium is not only associated with the prognosis of the general healthy population but also with the prognosis of many cardiovascular diseases, such as coronary heart disease, heart failure, end-stage renal disease, and cancer [20–23, 25]. Moreover, both elevated and decreased serum calcium levels can increase the incidence of adverse events, often showing a U-shaped relationship [38]. Hypocalcemia is common in critically ill and post-cardiac surgery patients and is associated with adverse outcomes including arrhythmia, decreased cardiac function, fluid overload, and renal dysfunction, which may promote AKI by impairing vascular smooth muscle function, reducing renal perfusion, and exacerbating systemic stress after cardiac surgery [21]. A previous study in post-cardiac surgery patients also supported that hypocalcemia is linked to higher risks of AKI and prolonged hospital and ICU stay [39]. Thus, hypocalcemia likely contributes to the pathogenesis of cardiac surgery-associated AKI.

Regarding subgroup analyses, significant interactions were observed in the subgroups of sex, surgical type, and type 1 diabetes (P < 0.05). The number of patients with type 1 diabetes was small (only over 100 cases), which may have led to the significant interaction in this group. The same applies to the sex subgroup—a meta-analysis showed that women have a significantly lower risk of developing AKI compared with men [41]. As for surgical type, the interaction can be attributed to the different type of in cardiac surgery, which is also supported by previous studies. For example, a study on the association between N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) and AKI after cardiac surgery showed significant interactions across different types of cardiac surgery [2]. Therefore, postoperative RCR should be interpreted in the context of specific surgical conditions.

First, to our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the association between RCR and the risk of severe AKI after cardiac surgery in a large cohort, which contributes to future research. Second,the significant association between RCR and the risk of severe AKI persisted after multivariate logistic regression analysis (adjusting for numerous confounders) and subgroup analyses, indicating reliable stability. Third, this study introduces a new method for identifying high-risk AKI patients in advance, providing opportunities for preventing adverse outcomes and enabling personalized patient management. Moreover, RDW and serum calcium are widely used indicators in clinical practice. Compared with other indicators, they are simpler, more convenient and cost-effective.

However, this study has several limitations. First, the study design is retrospective and observational, so we cannot establish a causal relationship. Second, despite using multivariate adjustment and subgroup analyses, this is a single-center study with a limited sample size and potential data bias may persist due to residual confounders. Third, the incremental predictive value of RCR compared with established clinical scores or biomarkers remains unclear. Further studies with formal prediction modeling are warranted. Fourth,.no formal prospective sample size or power calculation was performed prior to study initiation. A post-hoc power analysis was conducted to confirm adequate statistical power for the primary outcome; however, the lack of a priori sample size estimation remains a limitation of this retrospective database study. Finally, caution should be exercised when generalizing the study results to other populations and regions.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study found that elevated postoperative RCR index is independently associated with an increased risk of AKI in patients undergoing cardiac surgery. This finding expands the applicability of the RCR index and may serve as a hypothesis-generating finding for future risk stratification.

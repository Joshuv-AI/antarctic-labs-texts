Impact of preimplantation genetic testing on the incidence of monochorionic diamniotic pregnancies in single blastocyst frozen-thawed embryo transfers: a retrospective cohort study spanning 10 years
Authors: Jifan Tan, Ruyun Chen, Zetong Zheng, Dehai Gan, Yanwen Xu, Jia Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to explore the associations between assisted reproductive technology (ART) modality and chorionicity in monozygotic twinning pregnancies following single blastocyst frozen-thawed embryo transfer.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study analyzed 30,121 single embryo transfer cycles from January 2013 to December 2022 but focused on 251 monozygotic twinning pregnancies following single blastocyst frozen-thawed embryo transfer, including 144 monochorionic diamniotic (MCDA) twins and 107 dichorionic diamniotic (DCDA) twins. Regression analysis was performed to determine the risk factors for the subtypes of monozygotic twinning pregnancies after ART. Pregnancy complications and neonatal outcomes of the MCDA and DCDA groups were compared, and subgroup analyses among the in vitro fertilization (IVF), intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), and preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) groups were performed.ResultsThe overall monozygotic twinning rate was 1.97%, without a significant difference between D5/6 and D3 embryos in frozen cycles (1.95% vs. 1.56%, p = 0.55). IVF and ICSI were associated with a lower incidence of MCDA twins than observed in the PGT group (IVF: adjusted odds ratio [aOR] = 0.40, p = 0.009; ICSI: aOR = 0.38, p = 0.011). The incidence rates of specific severe complications in MCDA twins, such as twin‒twin transfusion syndrome, selective intrauterine growth restriction, and twin anemia polycythemia sequence, were 2.78%, 2.78%, and 0.69%, respectively. No significant differences were observed in other adverse pregnancy or neonatal outcomes among the MCDA, DCDA, and ART modalities. ConclusionPGT may be associated with an increased incidence of MCDA twins following single blastocyst frozen-thawed embryo transfer. The incidence of specific maternal–fetal complications for MCDA twins, such as twin‒twin transfusion syndrome and selective intrauterine growth restriction, remains noteworthy. However, the occurrence rates of other adverse pregnancy complications and neonatal outcomes of MCDA twins and DCDA twins are comparable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-026-09383-9.

Introduction

Monozygotic twinning (MZT) is an intriguing phenomenon in which a single fertilized egg splits into two embryos, resulting in two individuals with identical genetic material. The reported incidence of MZT in assisted reproductive technology (ART) is 0.9–1.4% [1, 2], which is 2–3 times higher than that in natural conception [3]. Compared with singleton and dizygotic pregnancies, MZT is associated with a greater risk of maternal and fetal complications. Several studies have investigated the risk factors for MZT after ART [4–6]; however, the exact mechanism remains unclear. Approximately two-thirds of MZT cases are monochorionic diamniotic (MCDA), and one-third are monozygotic dichorionic diamniotic (DCDA). While monozygotic MCDA twins share a single placenta with a single chorionic layer, monozygotic DCDA twins have two separate placentas, each with its own chorionic and amniotic membranes [7]. Chorionicity is a primary factor contributing to the increased incidence of maternal and fetal complications, such as miscarriage, preterm birth, and perinatal mortality. To date, no published studies have investigated the risk factors for differences in chorionicity in MZT following ART.

Advancements in ART have led to the development of preimplantation genetic testing (PGT), intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), and in vitro fertilization-embryo transfer (IVF-ET) for various patient needs. The intricate techniques vary and affect embryos in several ways. Many studies have investigated several ART parameters and their putative involvement in embryo splitting and their potential association with MZTs, including ovarian stimulation, stimulation protocols, changes to the zona pellucida due to ICSI and assisted hatching (AH) [6], frozen-thawed embryos [5], embryo biopsy [5], and prolonged embryo culture [2]. However, whether ART procedures, such as in vitro culture, ICSI, and embryo biopsy, are associated with chorionicity in MZT remains unclear.

Elective single embryo transfer (eSET) is recommended over double embryo transfer to reduce multiple gestations [8], leading to the introduction of extended culture for elective single blastocyst transfer in most ART clinics globally. Special attention is being given to the incidence and pregnancy outcomes associated with MZT following eSET, especially those with different chorionic types. For example, twin-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS), twin reversed arterial perfusion sequence, twin anemia-polycythemia sequence (TAPS), single intrauterine fetal demise, and selective intrauterine growth restriction (sIUGR) occur only in MCDA twins; these conditions are associated with special traffic vessel anastomoses and are considered the main reasons for the higher perinatal mortality rate. The observed perinatal mortality rate is 0–17.3% in DCDA twins and 25.6–69.0% in MCDA twins of natural conception [9]; however, there is inadequate research on MCDA and DCDA twins following ART.

Hence, MZT chorionicity following ART should be investigated to determine the relative risk factors and the incidence of maternal and fetal complications associated with chorionicity. In this study, we aimed to explore the potential factors influencing embryo splitting and chorionicity in MZT following single blastocyst frozen-thawed embryo transfer (SBFET) and to compare the pregnancy and neonatal outcomes between MCDA and DCDA twins. By focusing on MZT chorionicity, clinicians may be able to mitigate MZT risk by carefully monitoring and minimizing the transfer of blastocysts with increased risk. This could improve perinatal outcomes.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

We conducted a retrospective cohort study of MZT pregnancies following SET from January 1, 2013, to December 31, 2022, at the Reproductive Medicine Center, The First Affiliated Hospital, Sun Yat-sen University. In total, 30,121 SET cycles were screened with 15,010 clinical pregnancies (Fig. 1). A total of 267 MZT pregnancies that met the following eligibility criteria were included. Inclusion Criteria: 1 Women who achieved clinical twin pregnancies with MCDA or DCDA following SBFET confirmed by ultrasound. 2 Availability of complete records of the embryo transfer cycle and clear documentation of pregnancy outcomes. Exclusion Criteria: 1 Transfer of embryos diagnosed as mosaic aneuploid. 2 Pregnancies following intrauterine insemination and higher-order multiple pregnancies. 3 Monochorionic monoamniotic (MCMA) twin pregnancies were excluded because of the rarity of this condition. 4 Twin pregnancies with undetermined chorionicity or amnionicity. 5 Cases with missing pregnancy outcome data or loss to follow-up. To be noted, only one blastocyst was transferred per patient. This ensures that the observed zygosity and chorionicity patterns resulted exclusively from post-transfer embryonic splitting, rather than from the transfer of multiple embryos.

At 6–9 weeks gestation, MCDA pregnancies were defined as the presence of amniotic septation or two yolk sacs within one intrauterine gestational sac with two heartbeats. In contrast, DCDA pregnancies were defined as two gestational sacs with the same birth sex. During 10–14 weeks of gestation, DCDA pregnancy was diagnosed only if ultrasonography clearly revealed a thick septum membrane between the two fetuses and a recognizable λ sign or if postnatal placental pathology revealed two chorionic and two amniotic membranes. MCDA pregnancy was diagnosed on the basis of the presence of a thin membrane between the two fetuses, consisting of two amniotic cavities, and the T-sign on ultrasound. Another indicator was the presence of one chorionic membrane and two amniotic membranes in postnatal placental pathology [1]. Furthermore, placental pathology tests were conducted after childbirth to confirm chorionicity and amnionicity. In addition, all DCDA twin pregnancies were of concordant sex.

Controlled ovarian stimulation

Different ovarian stimulation protocols, including the gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonist protocol, GnRH antagonist protocol, and mild stimulation protocol, were used for participants according to their ovarian response. The human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) trigger was administered when at least 2–3 follicles reached 17–18 mm in size, and oocyte retrieval was performed within 36–38 h through transvaginal ultrasonography-guided aspiration.

ART types

We performed IVF/ICSI following standard laboratory procedures [10], and ICSI was used for all PGT cycles. The embryos were evaluated on the morning of the 5th day after fertilization, and each blastocyst was assigned a score according to the Gardner scoring system [11]. Vitrification was performed using cryopreservation. All blastocysts were collapsed by a laser before vitrification except for those that had undergone biopsy for PGT for aneuploidy. For the PGT cycles, a trophectoderm biopsy was performed on the 5th or 6th day after fertilization; zona pellucida breaching was performed mechanically using a biopsy pipette, and approximately 3–8 trophectoderm cells were aspirated under inverted microscope guidance. The biopsied cells were subsequently subjected to whole genome amplification (WGA) followed by next-generation sequencing (NGS). The blastocysts were then vitrified following the biopsy procedure. Patients who intended to receive PGT treatment were told to use contraception strictly to avoid the risk of spontaneous pregnancy. Postnatal follow-up was conducted to confirm the sex of the twins. In addition, Assisted hatching was not used in any IVF, ICSI, or PGT cycles in this study.

Protocols in frozen embryo transfer cycles

The natural cycle protocol was as follows: On days 8–10 of the menstrual cycle, transvaginal ultrasonography was performed to monitor follicle growth. Five days after ovulation was detected, the embryo was transferred. Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) protocol: Patients are administered estrogen pills from day 3 of their menstrual cycle until the endometrial thickness exceeds 8 mm. Next, progestin was added for 6 days before embryo transfer. Gonadotropin ovulation stimulation protocol: On days 2–5 of the menstrual cycle, gonadotropin (Gn) is administered, and transvaginal ultrasonography is performed to monitor follicle growth. The embryos were transferred on the 5th day after ovulation.GnRH-a-HRT protocol: On day 2 of the menstrual cycle, GnRH-a (3.75 mg/day) is administered. After 28 days, the HRT protocol was performed. The participants were required to avoid sexual activity during the freeze-thaw cycles.

Outcome measures

Factors that potentially influenced MZT chorionicity were recorded, including age, type of ART, frozen embryo transfer protocols, embryo quality, and blastocyst parameters of the MCDA/DCDA twins following SBFET. The initial Gn dose (IU), duration of stimulation (days), and total dose of Gn (IU) are important parameters that may impact the quality of follicles and embryos. Furthermore, embryo quality was assigned using four grades (excellent, good, average, and poor) on the basis of blastocyst parameters: blastocyst stage (2, 3, 4, 5, and 6), inner cell mass (A, B, and C), and trophectoderm grade (A, B, and C).

Outcome data were retrieved from the hospital inpatient medical record system for in-institution deliveries, while outcomes for patients delivering elsewhere were collected via structured outpatient clinic visits and standardized telephone follow-up. Pregnancy outcomes included maternal complications, miscarriage rates, preterm rates, cesarean rates, and neonatal outcomes. This study investigated gestational hypertension, intrahepatic cholestasis during pregnancy, gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), premature rupture of membrane (PROM), anemia, oligo-/polyhydramnios, and thrombocytopenia. Miscarriage refers to the loss of twin pregnancies before the 28th gestational week. As planned delivery is recommended for MCDA twins at gestational ages between 36 and 37 weeks according to guidelines [12] published in 2023, the preterm delivery rate was compared between MCDA and DCDA twins to examine the actual preterm delivery rate in clinical practice and evaluate the necessity of planned delivery in DCDA pregnancies. In addition, severe complications, such as TTTS and sIUGR limited to MCDA pregnancies, were diagnosed on the basis of ultrasound findings. Concerning neonatal outcomes, we compared the sex of the twins, rates of low birth weight, and birth weight discordance.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed via SPSS (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 27.0; IBM Corporation). Continuous variables are reported as the means ± SDs or medians (ranges), and categorical variables are reported as n (%). For parametric and nonparametric data, Student’s t test or chi-square test was applied, respectively. Univariate and logistic regression analyses were performed to identify risk factors associated with MZTs. A p value of < 0.05 was considered to indicate significance. Pregnancy complications and neonatal outcomes were compared between the MCDA and DCDA groups, and subgroup analyses among the IVF, ICSI, and PGT groups were performed. In addition, comparisons among the IVF, ICSI, and PGT groups and subgroup analyses between the MCDA and DCDA groups were performed (Supplementary Tables 1 and Supplementary Table 2).

Results

Among the initial cohort of 15,010 clinical pregnancies resulting from 30,121 SET cycles, 295 were identified as monozygotic twins, giving an overall rate of 1.97%. The MZT incidence was 21/892 (2.35% from fresh cycles, 3.17% for D5/6 embryos, and 2.09% for D3 embryos) and 274/14,118 (1.94%) from frozen cycles (1.95% for D5/6 embryos and 1.56% for D3 embryos). We found no significant difference in the MZT rate between fresh and frozen cycles (p = 0.388). However, when we examined the MZT rate separately in each group, no significant differences were observed between D5/6 embryos and D3 embryos in the frozen cycles (p = 0.551), and no significant differences were observed in the fresh cycles (p = 0.358). This study included 267 MZT pregnancies following SBFET. Figure 1 shows a flow chart of the study population. After 13 missing patients and 3 MCMA twin pregnancies were excluded, the final dataset comprised 251 eligible twin pregnancies. Among these, 144 were classified as MCDA twin pregnancies, and the remaining 107 were diagnosed as DCDA twin pregnancies. According to the follow-up records, 6 cases were lost to follow-up, 1 case of induced labor, 52 cases of monozygotic twin abortion, 54 cases of single embryo abortion, and 1 case of induced abortion. The final dataset comprised 99 MCDA twin pregnancies and 38 DCDA twin pregnancies with live births, constituting 198 and 76 newborns in the MCDA and DCDA groups, respectively. The incidence rates of MCDA and DCDA pregnancies following SBFET through PGT (MCDA vs. DCDA: 1.00‰ vs. 0.36‰), IVF (1.09‰ vs. 1.01‰), and ICSI (1.11‰ vs. 1.15‰) are presented in Fig. 2.

No significant differences between the MCDA and DCDA groups regarding baseline hormone concentrations or controlled ovarian stimulation protocols were observed.

Factors contributing to MZT chorionicity

Logistic regression analysis investigated the potential variables associated with MZT chorionicity (Fig. 3). According to the univariate analysis, the risk of MCDA pregnancy was significantly lower for IVF and ICSI than for PGT (IVF: odds ratio [OR] = 0.39, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.20–0.73; ICSI: OR = 0.35, 95% CI: 0.17–0.71). Maternal BMI was marginally associated with an increased risk of MCDA (OR = 1.10, 95% CI: 1.00–1.20). After adjusting for confounding factors that were significantly different in terms of baseline characteristics (Table 1), only ART type was found to be an independent risk factor for MCDA twin pregnancies. IVF and ICSI were associated with a lower incidence of MCDA than PGT (IVF: adjusted odds ratio [aOR] = 0.40, 95% CI: 0.21–0.80, p = 0.01; ICSI: aOR = 0.38, 95% CI: 0.18–0.80, p = 0.01). According to the multivariate model, MCDA was not associated with maternal age, BMI, frozen embryo transfer cycle protocol, or laboratory parameters of embryo morphology.

Comparison of pregnancy and neonatal outcomes between MCDA and DCDA twins

Table 2 presents the maternal and perinatal outcomes for MCDA and DCDA twins. Maternal complications such as gestational hypertension diseases (7/144, 4.86% vs. 2/107, 1.87%), intrahepatic cholestasis during pregnancy (0/144, 0.00% vs. 2/107, 1.87%), GDM (16/144, 11.11% vs. 6/107, 5.61%), PROM (16/144, 11.11% vs. 7/107, 6.54%), and anemia (7/144, 4.86% vs. 3/107, 2.80%) did not differ significantly between the MCDA and DCDA groups (all p > 0.05). The cesarean delivery rate (87/99, 87.88% vs. 32/38, 84.21%), preterm delivery rate (76/99, 76.77% vs. 29/38, 76.32%), and twin miscarriage rate (25/144, 17.36% vs. 27/107, 25.23%) did not significantly increase in the MCDA group compared with the DCDA group. Regarding neonatal outcomes, the incidences of TTTS, sIUGR, and TAPS, which are specific to MCDA twins were 2.78% (4/144), 2.78% (4/99), and 0.69% (1/144), respectively. Low birth weight (143/198, 72.22% vs. 49/76, 64.47%), fetal distress (2/99, 2.02% vs. 1/38, 2.63%), and birth weight discordance (13/99, 13.30% vs. 3/38, 7.89%) did not differ significantly between the MCDA and DCDA groups (all p > 0.05). The impact of ATR type on adverse pregnancy outcomes in the separate MCDA and DCDA groups pregnancies is shown in Table 3. The cesarean delivery rate differed significantly among the three ART types in MCDA pregnancies (p = 0.003), while in DCDA pregnancies, the rates of adverse complications and twin miscarriage showed significant differences (p < 0.05), with no significant differences observed for other adverse outcomes. Significant differences in cesarean delivery rate were found among ART types (p < 0.001; Supplementary Table 1). In subgroup analyses comparing MCDA and DCDA patients, significant differences were observed in adverse complications (p = 0.011) and maternal complications (p = 0.021) within the PGT group (Supplementary Table 2).

Discussion

In this 10-year retrospective analysis of monozygotic twin following SBFET, both IVF-ET and ICSI resulted in a significantly lower risk of MCDA pregnancy than did PGT; in other words, PGT was associated with a higher incidence of MCDA pregnancies. To our knowledge, this is the first study to explore the risk factors for MZT chorionicity. MCDA pregnancies have worse outcomes because they are associated with complications related to monochorionic placentation, such as TTTS and sIUGR. Since previous studies on the outcomes of MZT pregnancies were limited to natural conception, this is the largest study to date comparing maternal complications and fetal outcomes between MDCA pregnancies and DCDA pregnancies from MZT pregnancies following ART.

The mechanisms of MZT involving the splitting of a single blastocyst embryo have been explored, and potential risk factors, such as young oocyte age, embryo (inner cell mass and trophectoderm) grade, and micromanipulation, have been investigated [5, 13]. Some studies have suggested that manipulation of the zona pellucida during ICSI, PGT, or AH might induce herniation and subsequent splitting of the inner cell mass [2, 6, 14, 15]. However, the associations between these factors and MZT remain controversial [16–18]. Moreover, embryos subjected to PGT undergo several invasive procedures and manipulations, including extended embryo culture, AH, and embryo biopsy, all of which could be associated with increased MZT rates. Early studies also revealed that PGT significantly increases the risk of zygotic splitting of embryos compared with non-PGT IVF cycles [19].

Our results revealed no difference in the MZT rate between fresh and frozen cycles. When stratified by embryo developmental stage, no significant difference in MZT rate was observed between D5/6 and D3 embryos in either fresh or frozen cycles. This finding differs from that reported by Mateizel et al. [20], who observed a significantly increased MZT risk associated with blastocyst culture. However, several methodological distinctions may account for this discrepancy. First, our data suggest that MZT incidence normalized to CPR may nonetheless remain comparable across developmental stages, implying that the choice of denominator substantially influences conclusions and could partly explain the divergent findings. Second, differences in laboratory protocols, particularly zona manipulation techniques and vitrification procedures, may differentially affect zona pellucida integrity and MZT susceptibility across centers. Given these considerations, whether prolonged in vitro culture independently increases MZT risk remains inconclusive, and further large-scale prospective studies with standardized protocols are needed to clarify this relationship.

In this study, we focused on the influence of ART type on chorionicity in MZTs rather than on the risk of MZTs. Factors such as maternal age, BMI, controlled ovarian stimulation protocols, frozen embryo transfer protocols, and embryo quality were not significantly associated with the incidence of MCDA. Our results indicated that PGT, which involves both AH and extended culture, is an important risk factor related to the increased risk of MCDA pregnancies compared with ICSI or IVF.

The widespread use of PGT has raised concerns regarding the potential risks of embryo biopsy. Given the current lack of sufficient evidence to assess obstetric, neonatal, and long-term health outcomes following this invasive procedure, PGT strategies should be developed and implemented with caution [21]. Trophectoderm biopsy involves the removal of cells destined to form the placenta, leading to an increased risk of pathological placentation in PGT [22]. We hypothesized that biopsies performed on the trophectoderm during PGT may result in a looser trophectoderm structure, eventually leading to a higher incidence of MCDA. Sellers et al. suggested that AH on day 3 increased the risk of early embryo splitting (resulting in DCDA twins), as opposed to later embryo splitting (MCDA twins), by interfering with embryonic signaling mechanisms and allowing for blastomere separation and division [23]. Behr and Chang reported that extended culture increased the likelihood of MZTs, particularly MCDA placentation [24, 25], supporting the theory that delayed implantation increases the MZT rate after ART, particularly MCDA placentation, which is known to arise as a result of later splitting [23]. Moreover, larger breaches in the zona pellucida, such as those associated with AH or PGT may increase the overall likelihood of MZT compared with smaller breaches, such as those observed with ICSI [18, 26, 27]. However, it is important to distinguish between MZT incidence and chorionicity. the timing of embryo splitting, rather than breach size alone, determines whether twinning results in MCDA or DCDA placentation. PGT may preferentially promote later splitting through the combined effects of trophectoderm manipulation, large zona breaches, and extended culture, thereby increasing MCDA rather than DCDA twinning [23]. Further research is warranted to elucidate the precise mechanisms by which PGT increases MCDA risk, and to clarify the relative contributions of trophectoderm biopsy, AH, and extended culture to this effect.

For pregnancy outcomes, our results align with those of previous studies [28–30], indicating that MCDA twins have a higher incidence of adverse outcomes than DCDA twins, primarily driven by complications unique to monochorionic gestations, such as TTTS and sIUGR. In our study, the incidences of TTTS and sIUGR were lower than those reported in some studies (2.78% and 2.78% vs. 10.34% and 13.79%, respectively) [14, 31], although results from a multicenter study were comparable to ours [32]. This lower incidence may partly reflect differences in study design and patient selection. Owing to the limited number of TTTS cases, no significant differences in its incidence were found among the different ART types. Similarly, sIUGR occurred in only one case, exclusively in the PGT group, precluding meaningful subgroup comparisons. The lower than expected incidence of these complications may suggest that the embryological processes underlying IVF conceived monochorionic twins differ from those in spontaneously conceived twins [32], though the precise mechanisms remain to be elucidated.

Additionally, the similar miscarriage rates between the MCDA and DCDA groups may be partly attributable to the higher proportion of PGT cycles in the MCDA group, as PGT is associated with reduced early miscarriage rates. Consistent with this, further analysis revealed significant differences in maternal and adverse complication rates among different ART types within the DCDA group, which appear to be primarily driven by variations in miscarriage rates. Notably, PGT markedly decreases miscarriage rates, which may contribute substantially to the observed differences in adverse outcomes across ART modalities. Nevertheless, the potential influence of PGT on adverse pregnancy outcomes in MCDA pregnancies should not be overlooked. Given the high prevalence of vascular anastomoses in monochorionic placentas and their associated complications [9, 33], intensive antenatal surveillance remains essential to optimize neonatal outcomes in ART-conceived MCDA twins.

Beyond the complications unique to monochorionic gestations, our study compared general pregnancy outcomes between MCDA and DCDA twins following SBFET. Although prior studies have examined such comparisons in both naturally conceived and ART-conceived twins, conclusions have been limited by small sample sizes [13, 33]. A previous study reported a PROM rate of 13.3% in DCDA twins following IVF/ICSI [34], which exceeded the 6.54% observed in our DCDA group. This discrepancy may reflect differences in the study denominator used, as that study included only pregnancies beyond 22 weeks of gestation whereas our study was based on clinical pregnancies. Furthermore, current guidelines recommend planned delivery of monochorionic twins at 36–37 weeks of gestation [12], which may partly account for the elevated preterm delivery rates observed in both groups (MCDA: 76.77%; DCDA: 76.32%). Although MCDA pregnancies tended toward lower mean birth weight compared with DCDA pregnancies [35], this difference did not reach statistical significance, likely due to the limited sample size in the DCDA group. Notably, when complications specific to MCDA twins were accounted for, Carter et al. and Lewi et al. reported comparable perinatal mortality and morbidity rates between chorionicity groups [31, 36], consistent with our findings.

This retrospective study sheds light on the chorionicity of MZTs in 144 MCDA twin pregnancies and 107 DCDA twin pregnancies and compares pregnancy outcomes following ART. Considering the relative rarity of MZTs, a relatively large sample size was used in this study. The findings also have implications for improving patient counselling before initiating ART. Despite these strengths, this study has several limitations. First, we did not conduct postnatal genetic testing, which is the gold standard for determining monozygotic twinning, as it is costly, invasive and currently not routinely performed in twin pregnancies [37]. To minimize the impact of this limitation, we confirmed that all the MZT pregnancies included in our analysis after PGT strictly followed contraceptive measures. In addition, these pregnancies were of the same sex according to postnatal follow-up. Moreover, While our study identifies an association between PGT and increased MCDA twinning, the underlying biological mechanism remains unclear due to the rarity of MZT events; further exploration of the biological mechanisms is warranted. This was a single-center retrospective study, and inherent treatment biases may exist. However, owing to the low incidence of MZTs, conducting a prospective study may be impractical.

Conclusion

In summary, this study investigated the risk factors associated with chorionicity in MZT following SBFET and reported that PGT-associated treatment cycles were associated with increased odds of MCDA pregnancies. While MCDA twins presented similar pregnancy outcomes as DCDA twins, further research is needed to confirm these findings. The occurrence of TTTS and sIUGR in MCDA twin pregnancies emphasizes the need for intensive antenatal surveillance. Further investigation is required to elucidate the impact of PGT on chorionicity in MZT, which is closely related to adverse pregnancy outcomes.

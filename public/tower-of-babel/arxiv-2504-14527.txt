An alternative approach to deformations of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras Thanks: The author is supported by the grant NYUAD-065.
Quentin Ehret

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a novel approach to the deformation theory of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras in positive characteristic, based on the deformation theory of restricted morphisms introduced in our earlier work. We provide a full cohomology complex adapted to restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras in characteristic 2, define formal deformations, study obstructions and equivalence classes, and show that these are controlled by the cohomology we introduce. In characteristic p ≥ 3 p\geq 3 , we construct 2-cocycles and investigate their relationship with formal deformations. Explicit computations are given in characteristic 2 to illustrate the theory.

1.
Introduction

Restricted Lie algebras.
Restricted Lie algebras appeared in the work of Jacobson as a way to axiomatize the properties of derivations of associative algebras that arise only in strictly positive characteristic, see
[
J
]
. Roughly speaking, one requires the presence of a so-called
p-map
that behaves like the
p

2.1

[
SF
]
). Their cohomology is way more complicated than the usual Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology for Lie algebras and was first defined by Hochschild in
[
H
]
. Explicit formulas in low degrees and interpretation in terms of central extensions were provided by Evans and Fuchs (see
[
EF
]
), who also sketched a theory of infinitesimal deformations. The full deformation theory of restricted Lie algebras and its links with the restricted cohomology was done in
[
EM2
]
. In this paper, we also exhibit a full cohomology complex in characteristic 2 that have no analogs in other characteristics

Restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras.
Lie-Rinehart algebras over fields of characteristic zero appeared as algebraic analogs of Lie algebroids in differential geometry. The study of these structures was pioneered by Palais (
[

In positive characteristic, the Lie algebra is required to be restricted, and additional structures emerge from the interactions between the structural maps of the Lie-Rinehart algebra and the
p

[

[

Moreover, Schauenburg also gave an alternative approach to the notion of restricted universal enveloping algebra of a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra, see
[

Outline of the paper and main results.
This paper is an application of the theory developed in
[
EM2
]
to restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras. The aim is to present a new approach to the deformations theory of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras in positive characteristic, different from the method described in our previous work
[
EM1
]
. In this paper, we introduced the notion of
restricted multiderivation
to encode restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras structures. We used this correspondence to build a deformation theory together with a controlling cohomology. We were primarily inspired by methods originating from the study of differential geometric objects, such as Lie algebroids over the real numbers (see
[
CM
,
MM2
,
Ru
]
). In that case, the anchor map is inherently a Lie morphism, as are all its deformations, provided that the Leibniz rule for Lie algebroids is satisfied. In the purely algebraic case of (restricted) Lie-Rinehart algebras, this property no longer holds. A necessary condition for the anchor map of a Lie-Rinehart algebra
(A,L,\rho)
to be a Lie morphism is that the Lie algebra
L
is projective as
A
-module (see e.g.
[
FMM
,
Hu1
]
). However, in the most general case, this is not guaranteed, and additional conditions have to be considered. In
[
EM2
]
, we introduced a cohomology theory to control the deformations of restricted morphisms of restricted Lie algebras. In the present paper, we extend those methods from
[
EM2
]
to study the deformations of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras, even when the underlying Lie algebra is not projective as
A
-module. This method appeared to be particularly efficient in characteristic
p=2
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review the basics on restricted Lie and Lie-Rinehart algebras. Section
3
deals with the characteristic 2 case. We recall the formulas of the restricted cohomology of restricted Lie algebras and restricted morphisms given in
[
EM2
]
and we exhibit a well-defined sub-complex adapted to the problem of deformations of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras, see Theorem
3.2.4.  Theorem
. Furthermore, we introduce formal deformations and investigate obstructions to extending finite-order deformations (see Proposition
3.3.2.  Proposition
), equivalence (see Proposition
3.4.1.  Proposition
) and show that they are controlled by the cohomology we have introduced. In Section
3.5
, we conclude the study by computing explicitly 2-cocycles on some examples. Next, we move to the characteristic
p\geq 3
case in Section
4
. The situation here is slightly more complicated and therefore we restrict ourselves to building deformation 2-cocycles that will be used to control formal deformations (see Propositions
4.2.1.  Proposition

2.1.
Restricted Lie algebras

Let
L
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over
\K
. Following
[
J
]
, a map
(\cdot)^{[p]}:L\rightarrow L,\quad x\mapsto x^{[p]}
is called a
p
-map
(or
p
-structure
) on
L
and
L
is said to be
restricted
if

(1)

\displaystyle(\lambda x)^{[p]}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{p}x^{[p]}\text{ for all }x\in L\text{ and for all }\lambda\in\K;

(2)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x^{[p]}}

\displaystyle=(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x})^{p}\text{ for all }x\in L;

(3)

\displaystyle(x+y)^{[p]}

\displaystyle=x^{[p]}+y^{[p]}+\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq p-1}s_{i}(x,y),\text{ for all }x,y\in L,

where the coefficients
s_{i}(x,y)
can be obtained from the expansion

(4)

(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\lambda x+y})^{p-1}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq p-1}is_{i}(x,y)\lambda^{i-1}.

In the case where
p=2
, Condition (
3
) reduces to

(x+y)^{[2]}=x^{[2]}+y^{[2]}+[x,y],~\forall x,y\in L.

Example
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. Then, the space of derivations of
A
is a restricted Lie algebra. Indeed, let

\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A):=\{d:A\rightarrow A,~d(ab)=d(a)b+ad(b),~\forall a,b\in A\}.

It is well known that
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
is a Lie algebra with the ordinary commutator bracket. Moreover, for all
d\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
and all
a,b\in A
, we have

(5)

d^{p}(ab)=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\Big(\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{p}{i}$}}\Big)d^{i}(a)d^{p-i}(b)=ad^{p}(b)+d^{p}(a)b.

Therefore, the map
d\mapsto d^{p}
is an endomorphism of
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
and defines a restricted structure on
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
.

Example
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic
p\geq 5
. We consider the
Witt algebra

W(1)=\text{Span}_{\K}\{e_{-1},e_{0},\cdots,e_{p-2}\}
with the bracket

[e_{i},e_{j}]:=\begin{cases}(j-i)e_{i+j}~\text{ if }~i+j\in\{-1,...,p-2\};\\
0~\text{ otherwise,}\\
\end{cases}

and the
p

e_{i}^{[p]}:=\begin{cases}e_{0}~\text{ if }i=0;\\
0~~\text{ if }i\neq 0.\\
\end{cases}

Then
\left(W(1),[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
is a restricted Lie algebra (see
[
EFP
]
). More examples can be found in
[

and in

2.1.3.
Morphisms and representations

Let
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},(\cdot)^{[p]_{L}}\right)
and
\left(H,[\cdot,\cdot]_{H},(\cdot)^{[p]_{H}}\right)
be two restricted Lie algebras. A Lie algebra morphism
\varphi:L\longrightarrow H
is called
restricted morphism
(or
p
-morphism
) if it satisfies

(6)

\varphi\bigl(x^{[p]_{L}}\bigl)=\varphi(x)^{[p]_{H}}\quad\forall x\in L.

An
L
-module
M
is called
restricted
if in addition to the ordinary condition

(7)

[x,y]_{L}\cdot m=x\cdot(y\cdot m)-y\cdot(x\cdot m),~\forall x,y\in L,\forall m\in M,

it also satisfies

(8)

\underbrace{x\cdots x}_{p\text{~~terms}}\cdot m=x^{[p]_{L}}\cdot m,\quad\forall x\in L,~\forall m\in M.

A linear map
d:L\rightarrow L
is called a
restricted derivation
of
L
if in addition to the ordinary condition

(9)

d\bigl([x,y]_{L}\bigl)=[d(x),y]_{L}+[x,d(y)]_{L},~\forall x,y\in L,

it also satisfies

(10)

d\bigl(x^{[p]_{L}}\bigl)=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}^{p-1}\circ d(x),~\forall x\in L.

The following theorem, due to Jacobson, is useful to investigate
p
-structures on a Lie algebra.

Theorem

(
[
J
]
)
.

Let
(e_{j})_{j\in J}
be a basis of
L
such that there are
f_{j}\in L
satisfying
(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{e_{j}})^{p}=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f_{j}}
. Then, there exists exactly one
p
-map
(\cdot)^{[p]}:L\rightarrow L
such that

e_{j}^{[p]}=f_{j}\quad\text{ for all $j\in J$}.

2.2.
Lie-Rinehart algebras

Following
[
Hu1
,
Ri
]
, a
Lie-Rinehart algebra
is given by a triple
(A,L,\rho)
, where
A
is a commutative associative algebra,
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie algebra and an

\rho:L\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)

[x,ay]=a[x,y]+\rho(x)(a)y,~\forall x,y\in L,~\forall a\in A.

Example
.

Let
A
be a commutative associative algebra. Then,
(A,\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A),\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits)

A

of a Lie-Rinehart algebra

is an
A
-module

endowed with a
A
-linear Lie algebra morphism
\pi:L\rightarrow\text{End}(M)
satisfying

(12)

\pi(x)(am)=a\pi(x)(m)+\rho(x)(a)m,~~~\forall a\in A,~\forall m\in M,~\forall x\in L.

In positive characteristic, the following Lemma holds, which is a reformulation of
[
Ho
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma

(
see
[
Sc
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
M
be a representation of a Lie-Rinehart algebra
(A,L,\rho)
. Then, we have

(13)

\pi(ax)^{p}=a^{p}\pi(x)^{p}+\rho(ax)^{p-1}(a)\pi_{x},~\forall x\in L,~\forall a\in A.

Applied to the Lie-Rinehart algebra
(A,\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A),\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits)

) this Lemma gives

(14)

(aD)^{p}=a^{p}D^{p}+(aD)^{p-1}(a)D,~\forall a\in A,~\forall D\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A).

This lead to the following general definition of a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra.

(see

2.4.
Derivations of the formal space
A[[t]]

Let
A
be an associative commutative algebra. The formal space
A[[t]]
is defined by

(24)

A[[t]]:=\Bigl\{\overline{a}:=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}a_{i},~a_{i}\in A\Bigl\}.

The multiplication on

(25)

\overline{a}\overline{b}=\Bigl(\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}a_{i}\Bigl)\Bigl(\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{j}b_{j}\Bigl):=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{i}a_{k}b_{i-k},~~\forall~\overline{a},\overline{b}\in A[[t]].

Lemma
.

Let
D\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
. Then,
D
extends to an
A[[t]]
-linear derivation of
A[[t]]
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{a},\overline{b}\in A[[t]]
. We have

\displaystyle D\bigl(\overline{a}\overline{b}\bigl)

\displaystyle=D\Bigl(\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{i}a_{k}b_{i-k}\Bigl)=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{i}\bigl(D(a_{k})b_{i-k}+a_{k}D(b_{i-k})\bigl)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}D(a_{i})\Bigl)\Bigl(\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{j}b_{j}\Bigl)+\Bigl(\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}a_{i}\Bigl)\Bigl(\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{j}D(b_{j})\Bigl)

\displaystyle=D\Bigl(\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}a_{i}\Bigl)\Bigl(\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{j}b_{j}\Bigl)+\Bigl(\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}a_{i}\Bigl)D\Bigl(\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{j}b_{j}\Bigl)

\displaystyle=D\bigl(\overline{a}\bigl)\overline{b}+\overline{a}D\bigl(\overline{b}\bigl).

3.3.
Formal deformations

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra. Consider a triple
(\mu_{t},\omega_{t},\rho_{t})
given by

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}\mu_{t}:&L\times L\longrightarrow L[[t]]&\text{ ; }&\omega_{t}:&L\longrightarrow L[[t]]&\text{and}&\rho_{t}:&L\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits\bigl(A[[t]]\bigl)\\[5.69054pt]

\end{array}

(\mu_{i},\omega_{i},\rho_{i})\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L),~\forall i\geq 1

\mu_{0}=[\cdot,\cdot]
,
\omega_{0}=(\cdot)^{[2]}
and
\rho_{0}=\rho

of the restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra

(58)

\displaystyle=\mu_{t}\bigl(\mu_{t}(x,y),y\bigl);

(59)

\displaystyle\rho_{t}\bigl(\mu_{t}(x,y)\bigl)

\displaystyle=\rho_{t}(x)\circ\rho_{t}(y)+\rho_{t}(y)\circ\rho_{t}(x);

(60)

\displaystyle\rho_{t}\bigl(\omega_{t}(x)\bigl)

\displaystyle=\rho_{t}(x)\circ\rho_{t}(x).

Note that since
(\mu_{i},\omega_{i},\rho_{i})\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L),~\forall i\geq 1

(61)

\displaystyle\mu_{t}(x,ay)

\displaystyle=a\mu_{t}(x,y)+\rho_{t}(x)(a)y,~\forall x,y\in L,~\forall a\in A;\text{ and }

(62)

\displaystyle\omega_{t}(ax)

\displaystyle=a^{2}\omega_{t}(x)+\rho_{t}(ax)(a)x,~\forall x\in L,~\forall a\in A.

[

3.4.
Equivalence

Following
[
EM2
, Definition 3.14]
, two formal deformations

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu_{t}}\bigl(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y)\bigl)

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega_{t}}\circ\phi_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}\circ\omega_{t}(x),

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigl(L[[t]],\mu_{t},\omega_{t}\bigl)}}

.

3.4.2.
Trivial deformations

A deformation
(\mu_{t},\omega_{t},\rho_{t})
of a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra
(A,L,\rho)
is called
trivial
if it is equivalent to the deformation given by
\tilde{\mu_{t}}=[\cdot,\cdot],~\tilde{\omega_{t}}=(\cdot)^{[2]},~\tilde{\rho_{t}}=\rho.
Namely, we have

(75)

\phi_{t}\circ\mu_{t}(x,y)=[\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y)];~\phi_{t}\circ\omega_{t}(x)=\phi_{t}(x)^{[2]};~\rho_{t}(x)=\rho\circ\phi_{t}(x),~\forall x\in L.

Then, we have
(\mu_{1},\omega_{1},\rho_{1})\in B^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(A,L,\rho)
. Therefore, any non trivial deformation is equivalent to a deformation such that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\mu_{i},\omega_{i},\rho_{i})\in B^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(A,L,\rho)~\forall i<n,\text{ and }(\mu_{n},\omega_{n},\rho_{n})\in Z^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(A,L,\rho)\setminus B^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(A,L,\rho).

\displaystyle:=\Bigl(\bigl(d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{1}\gamma,\mathop{\mathrm{ind}}\nolimits^{1}(\gamma)\bigl),\bigl(d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{1}\tau,\mathop{\mathrm{ind}}\nolimits^{1}(\tau)\bigl),\varphi\circ\gamma-\tau\circ\varphi-[\varphi(\cdot),m]_{M}\Bigl);

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{d}}_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}:~

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}(L;M)\rightarrow\mathfrak{C}^{3}_{\mathrm{res}}(L;M),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{d}}_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}\bigl((\mu,\omega),(\nu,\epsilon),\theta\bigl)

\displaystyle:=\Bigl(\bigl(d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{2}\mu,\mathop{\mathrm{ind}}\nolimits^{2}(\mu,\omega)\bigl),\bigl(d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{2}\nu,\mathop{\mathrm{ind}}\nolimits^{2}(\nu,\epsilon)\bigl),\bigl(\alpha_{\mu,\nu}(\theta),\beta_{\omega,\epsilon}(\theta)\bigl)\Bigl),

where

(85)

\alpha_{\mu,\nu}(\theta):=\varphi\circ\mu-\nu\circ(\varphi^{\otimes 2})-d^{1}_{{\mathrm{CE}}}\theta,

and

(86)

\beta_{\omega,\epsilon}(\theta)(x):=\theta(x^{[p]})+\varphi(\omega(x))-\epsilon(\varphi(x))-x^{p-1}\cdot\theta(x),~\forall x\in L.

We denote
\mathfrak{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{res}}(\varphi,\varphi):=\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{d}}^{n}_{\mathrm{res}})
and
\mathfrak{B}^{n}_{\mathrm{res}}(\varphi,\varphi):=\text{Im}({\mathfrak{d}}^{n-1}_{\mathrm{res}})
, for
n=1,2
.

4.1.2.
Deformation 2-cocycles

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra (see Definition
2.3
).
For every derivation
d\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A),
we define

(87)

C_{\mathrm{LR}}^{1}(L,L)^{d}:=\Bigl\{(\gamma,d),~\gamma\in C_{\mathrm{res}}^{1}(L;L),\gamma(ax)=a^{p}\gamma(x)+d(a)x,~\forall x\in Lm~\forall a\in A\Bigl\}.

There is an embedding

(88)

\iota_{1}:C^{1}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L)^{d}~\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{C}^{1}_{\mathrm{res}}(\rho,\rho),\quad(\gamma,d)\mapsto(\gamma,0,d).

For every
A
-linear map
\theta:L\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
, we define

(89)

C_{\mathrm{LR}}^{2}(L,L)^{\theta}:=\Bigl\{(\mu,\omega,\theta),~(\mu,\omega)\in C_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}(L;L)\text{ satisfying }\eqref{eq:LRcohomop=2a}\Bigl\}.

There is an embedding

(90)

\iota_{2}:C^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L)^{\theta}~\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{res}}(\rho,\rho),\quad(\mu,\omega,\theta)\mapsto\Bigl((\mu,\omega),(0,0),\theta\Bigl).

Finally, let

(91)

C^{1}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L):=\bigcup_{d}C^{1}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L)^{d}~\text{ and }~C^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L):=\bigcup_{\theta}C^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L)^{\theta}.

We are now in position to define

(92)

Z^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(A,L,\rho):=\Bigl\{(\mu,\omega,\theta)\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(A,L,\rho)\text{ satisfying }{\mathfrak{d}}^{2}_{\mathrm{res}}\circ\iota_{2}(\mu,\omega,\theta)=0\text{ and }\eqref{eq:cohomopp}\Bigl\},

where

(93)

\omega(ax)-a^{p}\omega(x)=a^{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-2}\rho(x)^{i}\circ\theta(x)\circ\rho(x)^{p-2-i}(a)x,~\forall x\in L,~\forall a\in A.

Elements of
Z^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(A,L,\rho)
are called
deformation 2-cocycles
of
(A,L,\rho)
. A deformation cocycle
(\mu,\omega,\theta)\in Z^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(A,L,\rho)
is called
trivial
if there exists
(\gamma,d)\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L)
such that
\iota_{2}(\mu,\omega,\theta)={\mathfrak{d}}_{\mathrm{res}}^{1}\iota_{1}(\gamma,d)
.

4.2.
Formal deformations

Consider a triple
(\mu_{t},\omega_{t},\rho_{t})
given by

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}\mu_{t}:&L\times L\longrightarrow L[[t]]&\text{ ; }&\omega_{t}:&L\longrightarrow L[[t]]&\text{and}&\rho_{t}:&L\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits\bigl(A[[t]]\bigl)\\[5.69054pt]
&(x,y)\longmapsto\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\mu_{i}(x,y)&&&x\longmapsto\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{j}\omega_{j}(x)&&&x\longmapsto\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}t^{k}\rho_{k}(x),\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

where
(\mu_{i},\omega_{i},\rho_{i})\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L),~\forall i\geq 1
,
\mu_{0}=[\cdot,\cdot]
,
\omega_{0}=(\cdot)^{[p]}
and
\rho_{0}=\rho
. Such a triple is called
formal deformation
of the restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra
(A,L,\rho)
if the five following condition are satisfied, for all
x,y,z\in L
and for all
a\in A:

(94)

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\mu_{t}\bigl(x,\mu_{t}(y,z)\bigl)

\displaystyle=0;

(95)

\displaystyle\mu_{t}\bigl(x,\omega_{t}(y)\bigl)

\displaystyle=\mu_{t}\bigl(\mu_{t}(\cdots,\mu_{t}(x,\underset{p\text{ terms}}{\underbrace{y),\cdots y),y}}\bigl);

(96)

\displaystyle\rho_{t}\bigl(\mu_{t}(x,y)\bigl)

\displaystyle=\rho_{t}(x)\circ\rho_{t}(y)+\rho_{t}(y)\circ\rho_{t}(x);

(97)

\displaystyle\rho_{t}\bigl(\omega_{t}(x)\bigl)

\displaystyle=\rho_{t}(x)^{p};

(98)

\displaystyle\omega_{t}(ax)

\displaystyle=a^{p}\omega_{t}(x)+\rho_{t}(ax)^{p-1}(a)x.

Note that since
(\mu_{i},\omega_{i},\rho_{i})\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L),~\forall i\geq 1
, we automatically have

(99)

\mu_{t}(x,ay)=a\mu_{t}(x,y)+\rho_{t}(x)(a)y,~\forall x,y\in L,~\forall a\in A.

In contrast to the
p=2
case, a fifth deformation equation (Eq. (
98
)) arises due to the presence of the
p-1
power in Eq. (
16
), which leads to nontrivial relations between the components of
\rho_{t}
.

Proposition
.

Let
(\mu_{t},\omega_{t},\rho_{t})
be a deformation of a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra
(A,L,\rho)
. Then, we have
(\mu_{1},\omega_{1},\rho_{1})\in Z^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(A,L,\rho)
.

Proof.

By
[
EM2
, Section 3.4.2]
, we have
\iota_{2}(\mu_{1},\omega_{1},\rho_{1})\in{\mathfrak{Z}}^{2}_{\mathrm{res}}(\rho,\rho)
. Moreover, expanding Eq. (
96
) for
x\in L
and
a\in A
gives

(100)

\sum_{k\geq 0}t^{k}\omega_{k}(ax)=\sum_{k\geq 0}t^{k}a^{p}\omega(x)+a^{p-1}\sum_{k\geq 0}t^{k}\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{p-1}=k}\rho_{i_{1}}(x)\circ\cdots\circ\rho_{i_{p-1}}(x)(a)y

Collecting the coefficients of
t
gives

(101)

\omega_{1}(ax)=a^{p}\omega_{1}(x)+a^{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-2}\rho(x)^{i}\circ\rho_{1}(x)\circ\rho(x)^{p-2-i}(a)x,~\forall x\in L,~\forall a\in A,

which finishes the proof.
∎

Next, we investigate the problem of extending a deformation of order 1 to a deformation of order 2. Let

(102)

\mu_{t}^{2}:=\mu_{t}^{1}+t^{2}\mu_{2};~\omega_{t}^{2}:=\omega_{t}^{1}+t^{2}\omega_{2};~\rho_{t}^{2}:=\rho_{t}^{1}+t^{2}\rho_{2},

where the triple
\bigl(\mu_{2},\omega_{2},\rho_{2}\bigl)\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{LR}}(L;L)
and
\bigl(\mu_{t}^{1},\omega_{t}^{1},\rho_{t}^{1}\bigl)
is a formal deformation of order 1 of
(A,L,\rho)
.

For
x,y,z\in L,
we consider

(103)

\displaystyle\mathrm{obs}_{2}^{(1)}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle:=-\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\mu_{1}(x,\mu_{1}(y,z));

(104)

\displaystyle\mathrm{obs}_{2}^{(2)}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\mu_{1}(x,\omega_{1}(y))-\mu_{1}(\mu_{1}(x,y),y);

(105)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{obs}^{(1)}_{2}(\rho)(x,y)

\displaystyle:=-\rho\bigl(\mu_{1}(x,y)\bigl)+\bigl[\rho_{1}(x),\rho_{1}(y)\bigl]_{\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)};

(106)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{obs}^{(2)}_{2}(\rho)(x)

\displaystyle:=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\rho(x)}^{p-1}\circ\rho_{1}(x)-\rho\circ\omega_{1}(x)

\displaystyle~~-\sum_{i+j=p-2}\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{i}_{\rho(x)}\Bigl(\bigl[\rho_{1}(x),\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{j}_{\rho(x)}\circ\rho_{1}(x)\bigl]_{\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)}\Bigl).

Proposition
.

Let
\bigl(\mu_{t}^{1},\omega_{t}^{1},\rho_{t}^{1}\bigl)
be a formal deformation of order 1 of a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra
(A,L,\rho)
. Suppose that
\bigl(\mu_{t}^{2},\omega_{t}^{2},\rho_{t}^{2}\bigl)
defined in Eq. (
102
) is also a formal deformation of
(A,L,\rho)
. Then,

(107)

\displaystyle\bigl(\mathrm{obs}_{2}^{(1)},\mathrm{obs}_{2}^{(2)}\bigl)

\displaystyle=d_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}(\mu_{2},\omega_{2}),

(108)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{obs}_{2}^{(1)}(\rho)

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{\mu_{1},0}(\rho_{2});~\mathfrak{obs}_{2}^{(2)}(\rho)=-\beta_{\omega_{1},0}(\rho_{2}),

and moreover

(109)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(ax)-a^{p}\omega_{2}(x)=~

\displaystyle a^{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-2}\rho(x)\circ\rho_{2}(x)\circ\rho(x)^{p-2-i}(a)x

\displaystyle~+a^{p-1}\sum_{i+j+k=p-3}\rho(x)^{i}\circ\rho_{1}(x)\circ\rho(x)^{j}\circ\rho_{1}(x)\circ\rho(x)^{k}(a)x.

Proof.

Equations (
107
) and (
108
) are consequences of
[
EM2
, Proposition 3.10]
and
[
EM2
, Proposition 3.13]
, respectively. Moreover, for
x\in L
and
a\in A
, expanding

\omega_{t}^{2}(ax)=a^{p}\omega_{t}^{2}(x)+\rho_{t}^{2}(ax)^{p-1}(a)x

and collecting the coefficients of
t^{2}
leads to Eq. (
109
).
∎

Equivalence.
Equivalences of formal deformations of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras in characteristic
p\geq 3
can be investigated similarly to the
p=2
case, see Section
3.4
. In particular, we have the following.

Proposition
.

Let
(\mu_{t},\omega_{t},\rho_{t})
and
(\tilde{\mu_{t}},\tilde{\omega_{t}},\tilde{\rho_{t}})
be two equivalent deformations of a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra
(A,L,\rho)
. Then,
(\mu_{1}-\tilde{\mu_{1}},\omega_{1}-\tilde{\omega_{1}},\rho_{1}-\tilde{\rho_{1}})
is a trivial deformation cocycle.

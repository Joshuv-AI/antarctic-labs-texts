Unified structures for solutions of Painlevé equation II.


Abstract
We present certain general structures related to the solutions of Painlevé equation II and to the solutions of the differential equation satisfied by the corresponding Hamiltonian equations, together with the tau functions. By taking advantage of the Bäcklund transformations we find a linear differential equation satisfied by the tau functions and explicit rational expressions linking the solutions of Painlevé equation II, Painlevé equation XXXIV and the Hamiltonians with the tau functions. Also, recursions for the tau functions and the Hamiltonians will be given. Through the use of suitable parameters inserted into the equations, we consider a degenerate case solvable in terms of Weierstrass elliptic functions. The Yablonskii-Vorob’ev polynomials, the Airy-type solutions and the more general transcendental case are all described in separated Sections. Finally, certain integral relations are given.

1
Introduction

The Painlevé II equation is usually written in the following form:

\frac{d^{2}y}{dz^{2}}=2y^{3}+zy+\alpha,

(1)

where
\alpha
is a parameter. It is well known that this equation possesses, for integer and half-integer values of
\alpha
, rational solution and solution explicitly expressible in terms of Airy functions. All other solutions are transcendental. One of the differences among these solutions is in the distribution of the zeros in the complex plane: they are finite in number for rational solutions, are infinitely many in the other two cases but the order of growth of the solutions are different: it is
3/2
for the Airy type solutions and
3
for the other. If instead of the distribution of the zeros one takes into account, for example, the asymptotic behaviour of the solutions of (
1
) on the real axis, then another variety of solutions with different analytical properties are obtained also in the case of a fixed value of the parameter
\alpha
(see e.g.
[
3
]
and references therein). This wide variety of type of solutions prevented the development of a unified notation for the Painlevé transcendents. A unifying trait for the solutions of (
1
) is that the poles are all simple with residues plus or minus 1. Indeed, all the solutions of equation (
1
) can be represented as the difference of two functions having simple poles with residues
+1
: these functions, usually called
\sigma
function
[
1
]
, from a mechanical point of view are Hamiltonian function and for this reason are also symbolized with an
H
(see e.g.
[
10
,
11
]
). The Painlevé equations are indeed Hamiltonian, i.e. they possess an Hamiltonian structure; the corresponding Hamilton equations correspond to the Painlevé equations. It is the Hamiltonian, as a function of
z
, that indeed appears more frequently in the applications.

In this paper, following
[
7
]
, we introduce some parameters in the Hamilton equations also to elucidate the connections between the Painlevé equation II and a degenerate case involving the Weierstrass elliptic functions. The linking turns out to be not an accident but it reflects some general structures of the solutions of the Painlevé equation II and and of the corresponding tau functions that we are going to report and discuss. Actually, these general structures reflect the common Hamiltonian structure and the common existence of canonical transformations, depending on the parameters, that represent Bäcklund transformations among different solutions of the Painlevé equation (apart the elliptic case when one has auto-Bäcklund transformations). The paper is organized as follows: in Section (
2
) we introduce a set of parametric Hamiltonians together with the corresponding canonical variables related to the Painlevé equation (
1
). Also, we introduce the Bäcklund transformations, essentially as given by Okamoto
[
11
]
. In Section (
3
) we get a bilinear equation satisfied by the Hamiltonian functions and then two explicit third order recurrences. The compatibility between these two gives a second order quadratic recursion. A discussion will be made on its solutions. In Section (
4
) we will show how all the solutions of the main differential equation of this work, i.e. equation (
7
), can be formally reduced to the solutions of Riccati equations whose coefficients are certain combinations of Hamiltonians themselves. By introducing the tau functions corresponding to the Hamiltonians one gets a linear equation for the tau functions. This linear equation, together with the Toda equation, lead to explicit expressions of the solutions of the Painlevé equation II, Painlevé equation XXXIV and the Hamiltonians in terms of rational combinations of tau functions. Also, a fifth order explicit recursion for the tau functions will be given. In Section (
5
) a degenerate case, solvable in terms of elliptic functions, will be investigated. The Bäcklund transformations become auto-Bäcklund transformations and some explicit formulae, related to addition formulae for the Weierstrass elliptic functions, will be discussed. In Section (
6
) the rational solutions of Painlevé II will be considered in the ligth of the results obtained in Section (
4
). Some of the results given in
[
2
]
will be re-obtained. In Section (
7
) the Airy type solutions of the Painlevé II equation are considered. Again, we will give different relations between the Painlevé II functions, tau functions and Hamiltonians by specifying the results of Section (
4
) to this case. In Section (
8
) the general transcendental case will be considered. The action of the Bäcklund transformations on the Taylor series coefficients of solutions regular ta the origin will be investigated. It will be shown how this action can be described in terms of Weierstrass elliptic functions, generalizing the results given in Section (
5
). Finally, in Section (
9
) some general integral relations, specifying the action of the Bäcklund transformations on the tau functions, will be presented. Again, these relations are general and apply to any solution of the Painlevé II equation and the corresponding Hamiltonian and tau functions.

2
Canonical transformations

We take the following set of Hamiltonian functions, labelled by the discrete index
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, written in terms of the canonical variable
(q_{n},p_{n})
as:

H_{n}=\frac{p_{n}^{2}}{2m}-ap_{n}(q_{n}^{2}+bz+c)-e_{n}q_{n}-\frac{mg_{2}}{2a^{2}}

(2)

The last term, proportional to
g_{2}
, is just a constant and it could be removed by seting it equal to zero, but we will keep it since it will be useful in the rest of the paper. The other constants are
m,a,b,c,e_{n}
, with
e_{n}=e+2abmn
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a
and
m
in general different from zero. The corresponding Hamilton equations are given by

\begin{split}&\frac{dq_{n}}{dz}=\frac{p_{n}}{m}-a(q_{n}^{2}+bz+c)\\
&\frac{dp_{n}}{dz}=e_{n}+2ap_{n}q_{n}\end{split}

(3)

The differential equations satisfied by
q_{n}
and
p_{n}
are respectively given by

\begin{split}&\frac{d^{2}q_{n}}{dz^{2}}=2a^{2}q_{n}^{3}+2a^{2}\left(bz+c\right)q_{n}+\frac{e_{n}}{m}-ab,\\
&p_{n}\frac{d^{2}p_{n}}{dz^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{dp_{n}}{dz}\right)^{2}+\frac{2a}{m}p_{n}^{3}-2a^{2}(bz+c)p_{n}^{2}-\frac{e_{n}^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

(4)

The first equation is the Painlevé II, whereas the second one is known as Painlevé XXXIV (in the Ince’s notation
[
9
]
). It is clear that we could get rid of various constants, for example
a
and
m
, just by rescaling the dependent and independnet variables. In the application however, for example in the scaling reductions of partial differential equations, usually the independent variable is a space or time variables or a precise combination of both and through the scaling one obtains equation (
1
) only after other reductions. This is one of the reasons we find useful to leave the parameters in equations (
2
) and (
3
).

The derivative of
H_{n}
as a function of
z
is given by

\frac{dH_{n}}{dz}=\frac{\partial H_{n}}{\partial z}=-abp_{n}

(5)

and from (
3
) it follows that
H_{n}
solves the following second order differential equation

\left(\frac{d^{2}H_{n}}{dz^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{2}{bm}\left(\frac{dH_{n}(z)}{dz}\right)^{3}+4a^{2}c\left(\frac{dH_{n}}{dz}\right)^{2}+4a^{2}b\frac{dH_{n}}{dz}\left(z\frac{dH_{n}}{dz}-H_{n}\right)-2bmg_{2}\frac{dH_{n}(z)}{dz}-a^{2}b^{2}e_{n}^{2}=0.

(6)

Equation (
6
) possesses meromorphic solutions
[
6
]
. It possesses rational solutions, solutions that can be write in terms of elliptic functions (and the corresponding degenerate case given in terms of trigonometric functions), solutions written in terms of Airy functions and their derivatives and transcendental solutions with order of growth
\leq 3
. We are not aware of any proof that the transcendental solutions of (
6
), apart the rational, elliptic and the Airy type solutions, have order of growth exactly 3 when
b\neq 0
. When
b=0
all the solutions have order of growth 2 (or 1, in the trigonemoetric degenerate case) since the general solution can be reduced to Weierstrass elliptic functions (see Section (
5
)). Anyway, the non-rational solutions of (
6
) possess infinitely many poles of order 1. Also, it is easy to see that all the poles have the residues equal to
2bm
, so we let
H_{n}(z)=2bmh_{n}(z)
with
h_{n}(z)
having all poles with residue 1. From now on for simplicity we will denote the derivative with a dot on the dependent variable. The corresponding differential equation for
h_{n}
reads

(\ddot{h}_{n})^{2}+4(\dot{h}_{n})^{3}+4a^{2}(bz+c)(\dot{h}_{n})^{2}-(4a^{2}bh_{n}+g_{2})\dot{h}_{n}-\frac{a^{2}e_{n}^{2}}{4m^{2}}=0.

(7)

This is our main differential equation. When
b\neq 0
a simple shift will eliminate the dependence of
h_{n}
on
g_{2}
. However, as explained above, we want to keep the constant
g_{2}
in (
7
). Notwithstanding, for
b\neq 0
, it will be useful to introduce the function
k_{n}
related to
h_{n}
by

h_{n}(z)=-\frac{g_{2}}{4a^{2}b}+k_{n}(z).

(8)

Now let us now consider the following set of canonical transformations:

\begin{split}&q_{n+1}=-q_{n}-\frac{e_{n}}{ap_{n}},\\
&p_{n+1}=-p_{n}+2amq_{n+1}^{2}+2am(bz+c)\end{split}

(9)

The function
H_{n}(p_{n},q_{n},z)
, and hence
h_{n}(p_{n},q_{n},z)
, is preserved by the transformations, i.e.

H_{n}(p_{n}(p_{n+1},q_{n+1}),q_{n}(p_{n+1},q_{n+1}),z)=H_{n}(p_{n+1},q_{n+1},z).

Also, the generating function of the non-autonomous canonical transformations (
9
) is given by

F_{n}=-\frac{e_{n}}{a}\ln(q_{n}+q_{n+1})-\frac{2am}{3}q_{n+1}^{3}-2ma\left(c+bz\right)q_{n+1}

(10)

Since the Hamiltonian system defined by the Hamiltonian (
2
) is non autonomous, it follows that
(q_{n+1},p_{n+1})
, as a function of
z
, are the integral curves of the new Hamiltonian
[
18
]

H_{n}(p_{n+1},q_{n+1},z)+\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}=H_{n}(p_{n+1},q_{n+1},z)-2mabq_{n+1}=H_{n+1}(p_{n+1},q_{n+1},z)

(11)

Now we are going to refer all to the function
h_{n}
and not to
H_{n}
. From (
11
) it follows that the function
q_{n+1}
is proportional to the difference
h_{n}-h_{n+1}
:

h_{n}-h_{n+1}=aq_{n+1}.

(12)

The equations of motion (
3
) and the canonical transformations (
9
) gives:

\frac{\dot{p}_{n}}{p_{n}}=\frac{e_{n}}{p_{n}}+2aq_{n}=a(q_{n}-q_{n+1}).

(13)

The previous, together with (
9
), gives

\begin{split}&a(q_{n}-q_{n+1})=\frac{\dot{p}_{n}}{p_{n}}=\frac{\ddot{h}_{n}}{\dot{h}_{n}}\\
&a(q_{n}+q_{n+1})=-\frac{e_{n}}{p_{n}}=\frac{ae_{n}}{2m\dot{h}_{n}}\end{split}

(14)

Equations (
12
) and (
14
) are still valid if one substitutes
h_{n}
with
k_{n}
given in equation (
8
). From equations (
14
) it follows:

\displaystyle aq_{n}=\frac{ae_{n}}{4m\dot{h}_{n}}+\frac{\ddot{h}_{n}}{2\dot{h}_{n}},

(15)

\displaystyle aq_{n+1}=\frac{ae_{n}}{4m\dot{h}_{n}}-\frac{\ddot{h}_{n}}{2\dot{h}_{n}},

(16)

and by evaluating the first equation in
n\to n+1
and subtracting yields:

2m\left(\ddot{h}_{n+1}\dot{h}_{n}+\dot{h}_{n+1}\ddot{h}_{n+1}\right)+a\left(e_{n+1}\dot{h}_{n}-e_{n}\dot{h}_{n+1}\right)=0.

(17)

The previous can be integrated once, giving:

2m\dot{h}_{n+1}\dot{h}_{n}+a\left(e_{n+1}h_{n}-e_{n}h_{n+1}\right)=C_{1},

(18)

where, as we will now show, the constant on the right hand side must be equal to
-mg_{2}/2
. Indeed, equations (
14
) implies

aq_{n+1}=-\frac{e_{n}+\dot{p}_{n}}{2p_{n}},

(19)

whereas from (
12
) and (
19
) we get

h_{n+1}=h_{n}+\frac{2m\ddot{h}_{n}-ae_{n}}{4m\dot{h}_{n}}.

(20)

Equation (
20
) can be considered the Bäcklund transformation for the Hamiltonian
h_{n}
: if
h_{n}
is a solution of (
7
), then
h_{n+1}
given by (
20
) is a solution of (
7
) evaluated in
n\to n+1
. This can be checked also by inserting equation (
20
) in the equation (
7
) evaluated in
n\to n+1
and considering equation (
7
) itself and its differential consequences. Now we can insert
h_{n+1}
from (
20
) into (
18
): we get a third order equation for
h_{n}
and the compatibility with (
7
) implies
C_{1}=-mg_{2}/2
. We also notice that equation (
18
) written in terms of the shifted hamiltonians
k_{n}
(
8
) is

2m\dot{k}_{n+1}\dot{k}_{n}+a\left(e_{n+1}k_{n}-e_{n}k_{n+1}\right)=0.

(21)

Now it is possible to get the corresponding inverse transformation of (
20
) by looking at the inverse transformations of (
9
), given by

\begin{split}&q_{n+1}=-q_{n}-\frac{e_{n}}{ap_{n+1}},\\
&p_{n+1}=-p_{n}+2amq_{n}^{2}+2am(bz+c)\end{split}

(22)

By repeating the previous discussion to the inverse transformations (
9
) we can write the inverse of the Bäcklund transformations for
h_{n}
(
20
) as:

h_{n}=h_{n+1}+\frac{2m\ddot{h}_{n+1}+ae_{n+1}}{4m\dot{h}_{n+1}}.

(23)

We notice that this equation can be derived also from (
15
): by evaluating it in
n\to n+1
and using (
12
) one gets (
23
). The transformation (
20
) gives a solution of the equation (
7
) evaluated in
n\to n+1
from a solution of equation (
7
) itself. The transformation (
23
) pull back this solution. These two transformations define a discrete dynamics over
n
: in the next Section we will show how both (
20
) and (
23
) provide certain recurrences for the functions
h_{n}
and the corresponding tau functions.

3
Recursions for the Hamiltonian functions

It is possible to get another bilinear equation involving the Hamiltonian functions
h_{n}
and its derivatives. Adding (
20
) and (
23
) we get

(h_{n+1}-h_{n})(\dot{h}_{n+1}-\dot{h}_{n})+\frac{\ddot{h}_{n+1}}{2}+\frac{\ddot{h}_{n}}{2}+\frac{a}{4m}(e_{n+1}-e_{n})=0,

(24)

and, by taking into account also that
e_{n+1}-e_{n}=2abm
and integrating once

(h_{n+1}-h_{n})^{2}+\dot{h}_{n+1}+\dot{h}_{n}+a^{2}bz=C_{2},

(25)

By inserting (
20
) in (
25
) and using (
7
) we see that the constant
C_{2}
must be equal to
-a^{2}c
. So we get

(h_{n+1}-h_{n})^{2}+\dot{h}_{n+1}+\dot{h}_{n}+a^{2}(bz+c)=0.

(26)

The previous equation can be seen as a Riccati equation for
h_{n+1}
or for
h_{n}
: the coefficients of the Riccati equation involve the adjacent Hamiltonian. We will return on this equation in Section (
3
). Now we will obtain an explicit expression for the derivative of
h_{n}
in terms of Hamiltonian functions: this will give two explicit recursions for
h_{n}
from equations (
26
) and (
18
).

By evaluating (
23
) in
n\to n-1
and subtracting with (
20
) we get the following explicit expression for
\dot{h}_{n}
:

\dot{h}_{n}=-\frac{ae_{n}}{2m(h_{n+1}-h_{n-1})}.

(27)

It is possible to use the previous equation, together with (
26
) and (
18
) to obtain an explicit recursion for the function
h_{n+3}(z)
in terms of
h_{n+2}(z)
,
h_{n+1}(z)
and
h_{n}(z)
. From (
26
) we have

(h_{n+2}-h_{n+1})^{2}-\frac{ae_{n+2}}{2m(h_{n+3}-h_{n+1})}-\frac{ae_{n+1}}{2m(h_{n+2}-h_{n})}+a^{2}(bz+c)=0,

(28)

that is, by isolating
h_{n+3}
:

h_{n+3}=h_{n+1}+\frac{ae_{n+2}}{2m}\frac{h_{n+2}-h_{n}}{(h_{n+2}-h_{n})\left(a^{2}(bz+c)+(h_{n+2}-h_{n+1})^{2}\right)-\frac{ae_{n+1}}{2m}}.

(29)

Equation (
18
) gives

\frac{ae_{n+2}e_{n+1}}{2m}+(h_{n+3}-h_{n+1})(h_{n+2}-h_{n})(h_{n+1}e_{n+2}-h_{n+2}e_{n+1})+\frac{mg_{2}}{2a}(h_{n+3}-h_{n+1})(h_{n+2}-h_{n})=0,

(30)

and, by isolating again

we get:

h_{n+3}=h_{n+1}+\frac{a^{2}e_{n+1}e_{n+2}}{m(h_{n+2}-h_{n})(mg_{2}+2a(h_{n+1}e_{n+2}-h_{n+2}e_{n+1}))}

(31)

The compatibility between equation (
29
) and (
29
) gives the following second order equation for
h_{n+2}
:

\begin{split}&(h_{n+2}-h_{n+1})(h_{n+2}-h_{n})(h_{n+1}-h_{n})-\frac{m}{2ae_{n+1}}(h_{n+2}-h_{n})^{2}\left(4a^{2}bh_{n+1}+g_{2}\right)+\\
&-a^{2}(bz+c)\left(h_{n+2}-h_{n}\right)+\frac{ae_{n+1}}{2m}=0.\end{split}

(32)

Let us make some comments on the above recurrences. The dependence on
g_{2}
disappears (as it must be) from (
30
) and (
31
) if we make the shift (
8
). One has

\frac{ae_{n+2}e_{n+1}}{2m}+(k_{n+3}-k_{n+1})(k_{n+2}-k_{n})(k_{n+1}e_{n+2}-k_{n+2}e_{n+1})=0,

(33)

and

k_{n+3}=k_{n+1}+\frac{ae_{n+1}e_{n+2}}{2m}\frac{1}{(k_{n+2}-k_{n})(k_{n+1}e_{n+2}-k_{n+2}e_{n+1})}

(34)

Equations (

) and (

) remain unaltered by the shift instead. The recursion (

) becomes

\begin{split}&(k_{n+2}-k_{n+1})(k_{n+2}-k_{n})(k_{n+1}-k_{n})-\frac{2abm}{e_{n+1}}(k_{n+2}-k_{n})^{2}k_{n+1}+\\
&-a^{2}(bz+c)\left(k_{n+2}-k_{n}\right)+\frac{ae_{n+1}}{2m}=0.\end{split}

(35)

A second comment: the difference equation (
32
), or (
35
), is quadratic in the three variables
h_{n}
,
h_{n+1}
and
h_{n+2}
(or
k_{n}
,
k_{n+1}
and

, is a perfect square. Actually we will now show that this discriminant is always a perfect square and this is very useful in identifying the solutions of equation (

) and hence the explicit recursion for
h_{n+2}
in terms of the other variables. Clearly this should be consistent with equations (
29
) and (
31
). At first sight it seems that (

) is different from (

) and (

), in the sense that (

) is a second order difference equation, whereas (

) and (

) are third order equations. Actually we will show that a solution of (

) is related to

. The main reason for this is the expression (

of equation (

) with respect to

In order to identify if the previous can be written as a perfect square we use the identity (

, the other is given by

\wp(y,g_{2},g_{3})\left(\zeta(y+z_{0},g_{2},g_{3})-\zeta(y-z_{0},g_{2},g_{3})-2\zeta(z_{0},g_{2},g_{3})\right)=-\dot{\wp}(z_{0},g_{2},g_{3}),

(93)

i.e.

\int\frac{\dot{\wp}(z_{0},g_{2},g_{3})}{\zeta(y+z_{0},g_{2},g_{3})-\zeta(y-z_{0},g_{2},g_{3})-2\zeta(z_{0},g_{2},g_{3})}dy=\zeta(y),

(94)

where obviously in the last two relations one must take into account that
z_{0}
is a zero of
\wp(z,g_{2},g_{3})
.

6
The rational case: recursions and relations for the Yablonskii-Vorob’ev polynomials

It is well-known that there are special polynomials associated to solutions of the second Painelvé equation. We will use the standard notation in the literature (see e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
14
]
). In this Section we set the constants of the model as follows:

a=1,\quad b=\frac{1}{2},\quad c=0,\quad e_{n}=n+1/2,\quad g_{2}=0,\quad m=1.

(95)

It is well known that for
e_{n}=n+1/2
it exists a unique rational solution of the Painlevé two equation
[
16
]
. This solution can be obtained by the relations (
12
) and (
53
) as

q_{n+1}=\frac{\dot{\tau}_{n}}{\tau_{n}}-\frac{\dot{\tau}_{n+1}}{\tau_{n+1}}.

(96)

The functions
\tau_{n}
have the following structure in this case: the same exponential factor, given by
e^{-\frac{z^{3}}{24}}
times a polynomial. The tau functions are defined up to an overall constant factor, so the gauge must be fixed. In order to get the standard Yablonskii-Vorob’ev one has to set
\beta_{n}=-1/4
(and hence
\alpha_{n}=2n+3
from equation (
57
)) in the Toda equation (
56
) (see also
[
14
]
). So, by letting

\tau_{n}(z)\doteq Q_{n}(z)e^{-\frac{z^{3}}{24}},

(97)

from (
56
) we get the usual recurrence for the Yablonskii-Vorob’ev polynomials (see e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
14
]
):

Q_{n+2}=\frac{zQ_{n+1}^{2}-4\left(Q_{n+1}\ddot{Q}_{n+1}-\dot{Q}_{n}^{2}\right)}{Q_{n}},\;\;Q_{0}=1,\;Q_{1}=z.

(98)

Other differential-difference equations are:

\dot{Q}_{n+2}Q_{n}-Q_{n+2}\dot{Q}_{n}=(2n+3)Q_{n+1}^{2}

(99)

and

\ddot{Q}_{n+1}Q_{n}-2\dot{Q}_{n+1}\dot{Q}_{n}+Q_{n+1}\ddot{Q}_{n}=0.

(100)

Now all the equivalences found in Section (
3
) can be specialized by taking into account (
95
) and (
97
). The Yablonskii-Vorob’ev polynomial satisfy the following fifth-order homogeneous equation

Q_{n+5}=\frac{16(n+4)^{2}Q_{n+3}^{3}}{Q_{n+2}^{2}}-\frac{16(n+3)(n+4)Q_{n+2}Q_{n+4}}{Q_{n+1}}-\frac{Q_{n+4}^{2}Q_{n+1}}{(n+3)Q_{n+2}^{2}}+\frac{(n+4)Q_{n}Q_{n+3}^{2}Q_{n+4}}{(n+3)Q_{n+1}Q_{n+2}^{2}}

(101)

and also the following fourth order recursion

\left(\frac{Q_{n+1}^{2}Q_{n+4}-Q_{n}Q_{n+3}^{2}}{4(2n+5)Q_{n+1}Q_{n+2}Q_{n+3}}-\frac{(2n+5)Q_{n+2}^{2}}{Q_{n+1}Q_{n+3}}\right)^{2}=\frac{Q_{n+1}Q_{n+3}}{Q_{n+2}^{2}}+\frac{Q_{n}Q_{n+2}}{Q_{n+1}^{2}}-2z.

(102)

The rational solutions of the Painlevé II equation can be written in terms of the Yablonskii-Vorob’ev polynomial as

q_{n+2}=-\frac{(2n+5)Q_{n+2}^{2}}{2Q_{n+1}Q_{n+3}}+\frac{Q_{n+4}Q_{n+1}^{2}-Q_{n}Q_{n+3}^{2}}{8(2n+5)Q_{n+1}Q_{n+2}Q_{n+3}},

(103)

or

q_{n+3}=-\frac{(2n+5)Q_{n+2}^{2}}{2Q_{n+1}Q_{n+3}}-\frac{Q_{n+4}Q_{n+1}^{2}-Q_{n}Q_{n+3}^{2}}{8(2n+5)Q_{n+1}Q_{n+2}Q_{n+3}}

(104)

whereas the rational solutions of the Painlevé XXXIV equation are determined by

p_{n+1}=\frac{Q_{n}Q_{n+2}}{2Q_{n+1}^{2}}.

(105)

Also, they satisfy the following relation

\frac{\dot{p}_{n+2}}{p_{n+2}}=\frac{Q_{n+4}Q_{n+1}}{4(2n+5)Q_{n+2}Q_{n+3}}-\frac{Q_{n+3}Q_{n}}{4(2n+5)Q_{n+1}Q_{n+2}}

(106)

Equations (
101
-
105
) have been given also in
[
2
]
. Here we have shown how these relations are not peculiar of the Yablonskii-Vorob’ev polynomial but come from the more general structure underlying solutions of the Hamiltonian equation (
7
) described in Section (
3
).

7
Airy-type solutions

As we have seen at the beginning of Section (
3
), it is possible to get solutions of the Painlevé equation II in terms of Airy functions and their derivatives if
e_{n}=2abmn
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us make a more precise statement. In general the parameter
e_{n}
is defined to be equal to
e+2abmn
, with
e\in\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. In this Section we set
e=0
. Also, we assume
b\neq 0
. Similar to the rational case, the tau functions have the following structure: the same exponential factor, given by
e^{-\frac{g_{2}}{4a^{2}b}z}
times a polynomial of Airy functions and their derivatives. We remember equation (
8
) and set:

\tau_{n}(z)=e^{-\frac{g_{2}}{4a^{2}b}z}\Psi_{n}(z),\quad h_{n}(z)=-\frac{g_{2}}{4a^{2}b}+k_{n}(z),

(107)

where the relation between
\Psi_{n}
and
k_{n}
is

k_{n}=\frac{\dot{\Psi}_{n}}{\Psi}.

(108)

It is easily see that the first two Airy polynomials are given by

\Psi_{0}(z)=1,\quad\Psi_{1}(z)\doteq\ddot{\Psi}_{1}+a^{2}(bz+c)\Psi_{1}=0.

(109)

The function
\Psi_{1}
can be explicitly written as a linear combination of
\textrm{Ai}\left(-\frac{(a^{2}b)^{1/3}(bz+c)}{b}\right)
and
\textrm{Bi}\left(-\frac{(a^{2}b)^{1/3}(bz+c)}{b}\right)
, where Ai
(z)
is, up to a constant factor, the only solution of the Airy differential equation
\ddot{y}=zy
exponentially decaying on the positive real axis. The function Bi
(z)
is instead exponentially increasing on the positive real axis (see e.g.
[
19
,
20
]
). In order to get standard sequence of Airy-type solutions one shloud set
\beta_{n}=-1
in equation (
56
) (see for example
[
1
]
). The corresponding recursion for
\Psi_{n}
is

\Psi_{n+2}=\frac{-\Psi_{n+1}\ddot{\Psi}_{n+1}+\dot{\Psi}_{n+1}^{2}}{\Psi_{n}}.

(110)

Since
\beta_{n}
is fixed, the value of
\alpha_{n}
is fixed from (
57
) and we get
\alpha_{n}=a^{2}b(n+1)
, so that the relation (
55
) can be rewritten as

\dot{\Psi}_{n+2}\Psi_{n}-\dot{\Psi}_{n}\Psi_{n+2}=a^{2}b(n+1)\Psi_{n+1}^{2}.

(111)

The function
k_{1}
solves a Riccati equation, i.e. a first order equation. Indeed we expect that all the functions
k_{n}
belonging to the Airy-type solutions of (
7
) solve a first order differential equation. By starting from the equation for
k_{1}
, i.e.

\dot{k}_{1}+k_{1}^{2}+a^{2}(bz+c)=0,

(112)

it is possible to show, by using the Bäcklund transformations and equation (
112
) itself that
k_{2}
solves the following differential equation:

\left(\dot{k}_{2}+k_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}+4a^{2}k_{2}((bz+c)k_{2}-b)=0.

(113)

For
k_{3}
one obtains a first order equation cubic in
\dot{k}_{3}
. There are other differential-difference equations solved by the Airy-type solutions. Indeed we have

a^{2}b\left((n+1)\dot{\Psi}_{n}\Psi_{n+1}-n\dot{\Psi}_{n+1}\Psi_{n}\right)+\Psi_{n+2}\Psi_{n-1}=0

(114)

and

\ddot{\Psi}_{n+1}\Psi_{n}-2\dot{\Psi}_{n}\dot{\Psi}_{n+1}+\ddot{\Psi}_{n}\Psi_{n+1}+a^{2}(bz+c)\Psi_{n+1}\Psi_{n}=0.

(115)

The solution of the Painleve II equations (
4
) are related to the functions
\Psi
by

2aq_{n+2}=\frac{\Psi_{n+4}\Psi_{n+1}^{2}-\Psi_{n}\Psi_{n+3}^{2}}{a^{2}b(n+2)\Psi_{n+1}\Psi_{n+2}\Psi_{n+3}}-\frac{a^{2}b(n+2)\Psi_{n+2}^{2}}{\Psi_{n+1}\Psi_{n+3}},

(116)

or

2aq_{n+3}=-\frac{\Psi_{n+4}\Psi_{n+1}^{2}-\Psi_{n}\Psi_{n+3}^{2}}{a^{2}b(n+2)\Psi_{n+1}\Psi_{n+2}\Psi_{n+3}}-\frac{a^{2}b(n+2)\Psi_{n+2}^{2}}{\Psi_{n+1}\Psi_{n+3}},

(117)

whereas the solutions of the Painlevé XXXIV equation are given by

p_{n}=\frac{2m}{a}\frac{\Psi_{n-1}\Psi_{n+1}}{\Psi_{n}^{2}}

(118)

and satisfies

\frac{\dot{p}_{n}}{p_{n}}=\frac{\Psi_{n+2}\Psi_{n-1}^{2}-\Psi_{n-2}\Psi_{n+1}^{2}}{a^{2}bn\Psi_{n-1}\Psi_{n}\Psi_{n+1}}.

(119)

The fifth order explicit recursion (
70
) now gives

\Psi_{n+5}=\frac{a^{4}b^{2}(n+3)^{2}\Psi_{n+3}^{3}}{\Psi_{n+2}^{2}}-\frac{\Psi_{n+4}^{2}\Psi_{n+1}}{(n+2)\Psi_{n+2}^{2}}-\frac{a^{4}b^{2}(n+3)(n+2)\Psi_{n+4}\Psi_{n+2}}{\Psi_{n+1}}+\frac{(n+3)\Psi_{n+4}\Psi_{n+3}^{2}\Psi_{n}}{(n+2)\Psi_{n+2}^{2}\Psi_{n+1}}

(120)

8
The general case

In this Section we consider the full equation (
7
) with
b\neq 0
and assume that
e_{n}
is different from the values considered in Sections (
6
) and (
7
). Let us investigate the solutions of (
7
) that are not singular in
z=0
. As we will show, it is possible to parametrize the coefficients of the Taylor series of solutions of equation (
7
) in terms of Weierstrass elliptic functions. By generalizing the construction given in Section (
5
), we will look at the evolution of the coefficients in the Taylor series under the action of the Bäcklund map. As we did in Section (
5
), let us set:

h_{n}(z)=A_{n}+\xi(z+B_{n})-\xi(B_{n}),

(121)

where
A_{n}
is equal to
h_{n}(0)
and
\dot{h}_{n}(0)=\dot{\xi}(B_{n})
. The series around
z=0
reads

h_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}\frac{C_{n,k}}{k!}z^{k},\quad C_{n,k}=\left.\frac{d^{k}\xi(z)}{dz^{k}}\right|_{z=B_{n}},k=1,2,\ldots\quad C_{n,0}\doteq A_{n}

(122)

and, by taking the derivative of equation (
7
) and looking at the corresponding third order equation for
h_{n}(z)
one has that the coefficients
C_{n,k}
satisfy the following quadratic recursion for
m\geq 0
:

C_{n,m+3}=-6\sum_{k=0}^{m+1}\binom{m}{k}C_{n,k+1}C_{n,m+1-k}-2a^{2}((2m-1)bC_{n,m}+2cC_{n,m+1})+\frac{g_{2}}{2}\delta_{0,m}.

(123)

Only two of the first three coefficients are independent since they must be related by the following equation:

C_{n,2}^{2}+4C_{n,1}^{3}+4a^{2}c\,C_{n,1}^{2}-(4a^{2}b\,A_{n}+g_{2})C_{n,1}-\frac{a^{2}e_{n}^{2}}{4m^{2}}=0

(124)

The previous can be seen as an elliptic curve and can be uniformized in terms of elliptic functions. More specifically we set:

C_{n,1}=-\frac{a^{2}c}{3}-\wp(B_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3}),\quad C_{n,2}=-\dot{\wp}(B_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3})

(125)

where
g^{p}_{n,2}
and
g^{p}_{n,3}
are the deformed Painelvé invariants defined by

g^{p}_{n,2}=g_{2}+4a^{2}(bA_{n}+\frac{a^{2}c^{2}}{3}),\quad g^{p}_{n,3}=-(\frac{ae_{n}}{2m})^{2}+\frac{8a^{6}c^{3}}{27}+\frac{4a^{4}bcA_{n}}{3}+\frac{a^{2}cg_{2}}{3}.

(126)

It is convenient to identify the constant
c
with a Weierstrass
\wp
function too. Let us introduce the point
t_{n}
such that

c=-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\wp(t_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3}).

(127)

The point
t_{n}
is defined up to a sign within a period parallelogram. From equations (
126
) we get

\dot{\wp}(t_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3})^{2}=\left(\frac{ae_{n}}{2m}\right)^{2},

(128)

and, as we did in Section (
5
), we fix the sign in such a way that

\dot{\wp}(t_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3})=-\frac{ae_{n}}{2m}.

(129)

With this choice, the Bäcklund transformations (
20
) define the following recursions for the constants
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
:

A_{n+1}=A_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\dot{\wp}(B_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3})-\dot{\wp}(t_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3})}{\wp(B_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3})-\wp(t_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3})},

(130)

and

\wp(B_{n+1},g^{p}_{n+1,2},g^{p}_{n+1,3})=\wp(B_{n}+t_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3}).

(131)

The explicit value of
A_{n}
from the recursion (
130
) can be simplified by using (
86
) and (
131
). One gets

A_{n}=A_{0}+\zeta(B_{n},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3})-\zeta(B_{0},g^{p}_{n,2},g^{p}_{n,3})-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\zeta(t_{k},g^{p}_{k,2},g^{p}_{k,3}).

(132)

In order to write explicitly the recursion (
70
) and the expressions (
71
), (
72
) and (
73
) relating the solutions of the Painelvé II equation and Painlevé XXXIV equation to the tau functions it is important to have the values of the constants
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
defined by equations (
55
) and (
56
). By fixing the gauge
\tau_{n}(z)=1+O(z)
, by using the explicit recursion (
123
) and the definition (
53
) of the tau functions we can calculate:

\alpha_{n}=A_{n+2}-A_{n},\quad\beta_{n}=\wp(t_{n+1},g^{p}_{n+1,2},g^{p}_{n+1,3})-\wp(B_{n+1},g^{p}_{n+1,2},g^{p}_{n+1,3})

(133)

All the previous expressions reduce to those of Section (
5
) for
b=c=0
. We notice that for
b=0
and
c\neq 0
equation (
7
) is still explicitly integrable in terms of elliptic functions: the invariants (
126
) do not depend on
n
like those of Section (
5
) in this case, the point
t_{n}
also is fixed, i.e. independent on
n
and the relation (
131
) for
B_{n}
is explicitly solved in
B_{n}=B_{0}+tn
.

As a final remark, we notice that until now we investigated the action of Bäcklund transformations on solutions of (
7
) regular at the origin
z=0
. The dynamics of the Bäcklund transformations on solutions having a singular point in
z=0
is interesting and deserve a separate work. Let us only notice that the singular points, i.e. the zeros of
\tau_{n}
, correspond to the zeros of
\dot{h}_{n+1}
: the singularity develop in a regular solution with the coefficients of
z
in the Taylor series being equal to zero. This is clear also by writing equations (
55
) and (
49
) as:

h_{n+2}-h_{n}=\alpha_{n}\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{2}}{\tau_{n}\tau_{n+2}}

(134)

and

\dot{h}_{n+1}=\beta_{n}\frac{\tau_{n}\tau_{n+2}}{\tau_{n+1}^{2}}.

(135)

Equations (
134
) and (
135
) show how the zeros and poles of the Hamiltonian functions
h_{n}
’s are interlaced: these aspects also will be taken into account in a separate work.

9
Integral relations

In this Section we would like to present certain integral relations satisfied by the tau functions
\tau_{n}
and the Hamiltonian functions
h_{n}
.

Let us look again at the Bäcklund transformations (
20
) and (
23
). Let us define the functions
F_{n}(z)
as the integral of the following function

F_{n}(z)=\int\frac{ae_{n}}{2m}\frac{\tau_{n}^{2}}{\ddot{\tau}_{n}\tau_{n}-\dot{\tau}_{n}^{2}}dz=-\alpha_{n-1}\int\frac{\tau_{n}^{2}}{\tau_{n-1}\tau_{n+1}}dz,

(136)

where the path of integration avoids the zeros of
\ddot{\tau}_{n}\tau_{n}-\dot{\tau}_{n}^{2}
(i.e. those of
\tau_{n-1}\tau_{n+1}
). By integrating (
20
) we get

\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{2}}{\ddot{\tau}_{n}\tau_{n}-\dot{\tau}_{n}^{2}}=e^{-F_{n}}

(137)

and from (
56
)

\tau_{n+1}=\beta_{n-1}\tau_{n-1}e^{-F_{n}}

(138)

The inverse transformation gives the same result, i.e.
\tau_{n-1}
proportional to
\tau_{n+1}e^{F_{n}}
. Indeed, from (
23
) one has

\frac{\tau_{n}^{2}}{\ddot{\tau}_{n+1}\tau_{n+1}-\dot{\tau}_{n+1}^{2}}=e^{F_{n+1}}=e^{F_{n+1}},

(139)

i.e.

\tau_{n}=\beta_{n}\tau_{n+2}e^{F_{n+1}}.

(140)

The constant on the right hand side of (
140
) and (
138
) depends on the path of integration chosen and on the fixed gauge for the
\tau_{n}
functions. In general we could write

\tau_{n+1}=c_{n}\tau_{n-1}e^{-F_{n}}

(141)

for some constant
c_{n}
. Equation (
141
) expressing the integral (
136
) in terms of tau functions can be seen as the equivalent of the Bäcklund transformation (
20
) for the tau function
\tau_{n}
. Other integral relations can be obtained by the results given in Section (
8
). For example from (
55
) one has:

\frac{\tau_{n+2}}{\tau_{n}}=\alpha_{n}\int\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{2}}{\tau_{n}^{2}}dz+C_{n},

(142)

for some constant
C_{n}
. Also, the Hamiltonian function itself can be expressed through two integral relations involving other Hamiltonian functions or the tau functions. Indeed one has

h_{n}=\int\frac{\alpha_{n-1}\beta_{n-1}}{h_{n+1}-h_{n-1}}dz=\int\frac{\beta_{n-1}\tau_{n-1}\tau_{n+1}}{\tau_{n}^{2}}dz.

(143)

The relations given in this Section are general: the first element of the chain
h_{0}
, or
\tau_{0}
can be a rational solution, a special type solution or a transcendental solution. The successive Hamiltonians can be obtained by means of (
20
), whereas the successive tau functions for example by means of (
56
).

10
Conclusions

The generalized structures presented in this work suggest that the solutions of the Painlevé II equation, Painlevé XXXIV equation, the Yablonskii-Vorobev polynomials, the Airy type solutions of the Painlevé II, the Weierstrass elliptic functions and the Hamiltonians of the Painlevé II equation can be considered the members of an extended Painlevé II hierarchy. There are different points not yet discussed in this paper and that will be the subject of future works: from the interlacing of the poles and zeros of the Hamiltonians, to some properties of the Yablonskii-Vorobev that follow for example from equation (
99
). Finally, we believe that the structures here described could be lifted-up at the Painlevé equation IV: this also will be the subject of future research.

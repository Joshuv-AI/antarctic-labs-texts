On the number of words of N = 3 ​ M N=3\,M letters with a three-letter alphabet
Pablo Serra

Abstract
In this paper we address the well-known problem of counting the number of 3 ​ M 3M -letter words that can be formed from a three-letter alphabet by decomposing it into four possible cases based on its remainder when divided by three. The solution to the problem also gives us some sums of trinomial coefficients.



I
problem statement

The number of
N
-letter words that can be formed with a m-letter alphabet
is standard in combinatorics
stanley2012

configuration where each letter
a_{i}
appears

n_{i}\geqslant 0\;;\;i=1,\ldots,m
times, the number of possible words is given by

c(m;\{n_{i}\})\,=\,\binom{N}{n_{1},\,\ldots,n_{m}}\;;\;\mbox{ where }N=\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\,,

(1)

and the total number of
N

C(m;N)\,=\,\sum_{n_{1}+\ldots+n_{m}=N}c(m;\{n_{i}\})\,=\,\sum_{n_{1}+\ldots+n_{m}=N}\binom{N}{n_{1},\,\ldots,n_{m}}\,=\,m^{N}\,.

In this work, we study the case of a three-letter alphabet,

\displaystyle 3

\displaystyle 3

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3

A wax-assisted stamp-transfer approach towards tetracycline monitoring by silver nanoprism using smartphone-based digital image analysis
Authors: Farnaz Bahavarnia, Mohammad Hasanzadeh, Fatemeh Nami, Nasrin Shadjou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Tetracycline is a broad-spectrum antibiotic used in livestock farming and food production. The persistence of tetracycline in the environment and food products can lead to environmental and human health problems. Therefore, monitoring this compound is essential in the field of pharmacological and environmental analysis. In this study, we attempted to prepare and evaluate a novel flexible and portable polymeric substrate for monitoring the concentration level of tetracycline in real samples. An optical sensor based on silver nanoparticles was utilized for identifying tetracycline, using only a single drop of the optical probe and a single drop of the analyte. For this purpose, a 32-zone pattern was initially designed using AUTOCAD software, and then a metal mold was fabricated via computer numerical control (CNC) machining. Subsequently, the transfer of the pattern onto a flexible Parafilm substrate was carried out as a creative idea by transferring molten wax. The zones formed on the substrate (micro-scale polymeric colorimetric device (µPCD)) were then modified with silver nanoparticles, which possessed a triangular morphology and unique plasmonic properties. The analyte-probe interaction was then assessed using a smartphone and based on RGB analysis via the standard color-picker app, and the results were evaluated. According to the results, the optical sensor was capable of identifying micro molar concentrations of tetracycline (low limit of quantification = 0.025 µM), demonstrating the application of this novel tool in pharmaceutical analysis in human plasma samples. To confirm the interaction between the nanoparticle and the analyte, zeta potential and conductivity data were evaluated to investigate the electrostatic interaction between the silver nanoprism and tetracycline. The zeta potential and conductivity for the silver nanoprism were obtained as −25.7 mV and 0.687 mS cm, respectively, which changed to +21.91 mV and 7.62 mS cm upon interaction with the analyte. Validation studies indicated the proper functioning of the engineered optical sensor for tetracycline analysis. Therefore, in this research, a new method for identifying tetracycline in human plasma samples without using routine laboratory devices was introduced.

Introduction

Tetracycline is an antibiotic widely prescribed in human and veterinary medicine. Its widespread use has led to the frequent detection of tetracycline residues in biological and environmental matrices, including human urine. These residues not only pose serious health concerns but also exacerbate the global threat of antimicrobial resistance, a crisis that was associated with approximately five million deaths in 2019 and is expected to increase.1,2 Furthermore, the negative effects of antibiotic residues on immunocompromised individuals and emerging evidence of the role of bacterial infections in tumor progression highlight the urgent need for sensitive and reliable monitoring of tetracycline residues in biological systems.3–5

Various methods such as electrophoresis, mass spectrometry, and chromatography, which have been used to identify tetracycline, are very expensive and limited to laboratory environments, sometimes requiring advanced equipment.6–8 Conventional analytical methods for the analysis of this analyte, despite their high sensitivity, have an essential need for advanced laboratory infrastructure, specialized operators, and long sample preparation times; therefore, their efficiency for rapid screening in production and distribution environments is limited.9–13 These limitations highlight the necessity of developing alternative and low-cost and user-friendly methods. In the last decade, electrochemical and optical sensors based on advanced nanomaterials (gold nanoparticles, reduced graphene, carbon nanotubes)14–16 and biological recognition elements (aptamers and antibodies) have attracted the attention of researchers for analytical investigations17–21 towards significantly improve the sensitivity and selectivity of sensors due to their high active surface area, improved electronic conductivity, and chemical modifiability. However, most of these achievements are limited to controlled laboratory conditions and have rarely been translated into a portable, stable, and cost-effective system usable in industry and field monitoring. Therefore, the existing technological gap in this research area lies not only in increasing sensitivity but also in transferring the technology to operational and user-friendly tools.

The current study aims to addresses this research gap. Due to the great importance of colorimetric sensors for rapid, low-cost, and on-site recognition of tetracycline concentration in human biofluids, we designed a novel chemosensor towards colorimetric recognition of tetracycline in real samples using smartphone-based digital analysis. Previous studies have been based on the use of various silver and gold nanoparticles to colorimetric identify of this analyte, and there is no report on the optical sensing of tetracycline by silver nanoprism as a novel magic material. Therefore, the first objective of the current research was tetracycline chemosensing in aqueous solution by simple, low-cost colorimetric method. Also, a novel portable and user-friendly optical tool was constructed for tetracycline monitoring in human plasma samples by mobile app. For this purpose, an innovative substrate was prepared using molten wax transfer technology by a special stamp (iron mold) with 32 sensing zone. Then, an efficient micro-array was developed for the immobilization of the probe and finally the analyte towards investigation of their interaction in microliter volume (one-drop solution). This approach led to designing a portable, flexible, and low-cost microreactor for optical chemosensing studies in biomedical analysis. The novelty of the present work resides in the integration of three complementary innovations: (I) a wax-assisted stamp-transferred micro-scale polymeric colorimetric device (µPCD) fabricated on a flexible Parafilm substrate, (II) a plasmonic chemosensing mechanism based on tetracycline-induced modulation of silver nanoprism morphology, and (III) a smartphone-based digital-image analysis workflow incorporating RGB, luminance, wavelength, and color-temperature parameters. Rather than focusing solely on improving analytical sensitivity, the present study aims to combine optical chemosensing, portable device engineering, and digital analytics into a practical platform for point-of-care tetracycline monitoring in real samples.

Experimental

Reagents and materials

Tetracycline, was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Ascorbic acid, cysteine, proline, phenylalanine, arginine, glycine, metoprolol, sotalol, phenobarbital, heparin, propranolol, indomethacin, famotidine, dopamine, and urea were supplied by Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). Silver chloride (AgCl), silver nitrate (AgNO3), sodium borohydride (NaBH4, 96%), trisodium citrate (Na3C6H5O7), hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), and polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) were acquired from Sigma-Aldrich (Ontario, Canada). Silver nanoprism was synthesized and characterized according to our previous report.21 Deionized water was provided by Ghazi Pharmaceutical Company (Tabriz, Iran). PF sheets were obtained from Bemis Company, Country of origin, USA. Fiber glasses paper was purchased from Whatman, Cytiva, Germany.

Instruments

The hydrodynamic diameter and zeta potential of the bare and tetracycline-loaded AgNPrs were determined using a Malvern particle size analyzer (Zetasizer Ver. 7.11, MAL1032660, UK). Digital photographs and videos of the colorimetric assays were acquired using a Samsung Galaxy A2 smartphone, equipped with a dual 5-MP rear camera featuring sensor-shift optical image stabilization. To ensure reproducible lighting conditions and eliminate ambient light interference, all imaging was conducted inside a custom cylindrical dark box (diameter: 8.0 cm, height: 10 cm). Quantitative colorimetric signal intensity (RGB) was extracted using the Color Picker app (version 7.9.0) that installed on smartphone (https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=gmikhail.colorpicker). Micro-scale polymeric colorimetric device (µPCD) was prepared according to our previous reports.22–25

Results and discussion

Principle of colorimetric detection of tetracycline

The colorimetric behavior of the AgNPrs-based chemosensor for detection of tetracycline was evaluated in aqueous solution. Under optimum conditions of reaction, yellow color of tetracycline was changed to green which show that possible interaction of analyte-probe. The sensing mechanism relies on the unique localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) properties of AgNPrs. This structural evolution yields a distinct blue-shift in the LSPR band, producing a sharp visual color change (Fig. 1A, B and VS1 (SI)). It is important to point out that, AgNPrs have surface free electrons that undergo collective oscillation when exposed to visible light. This phenomenon is known as surface plasmon resonance (SPR). Silver nanoprisms, due to their anisotropic structure and the presence of sharp edges and corners, the plasmonic bands are very sensitive to the surrounding environment. Therefore, any change in size, shape, distance between particles, or the dielectric constant of the environment surrounding the nanoparticle directly causes a shift in the LSPR band and consequently changes the color of the solution. This high sensitivity is the main reason for the application of silver nanoprisms in colorimetric sensing of tetracycline.

After first successful colorimetric detection of tetracycline in tradition vials, a portable testing tool (µPCD) was constructed for the detection of analyte in low-volume by smartphone. So, we were able to investigate the interaction of optical probe with analyte by one droplet of each solution. Colorimetric tests were carried out using µPCD and images were captures by camera of smartphone. To integrate the proposed tetracycline analysis system with µPCD, tetracycline solutions were mixed with the AgNPrs probe solution at a volumetric ratio of 1 : 1, leading to a visible color change for analytical purposes. µPCD exhibited a stable, corresponding colorimetric response (Fig. 2 and VS2 (SI)), confirming that the successful detection of tetracycline by parafilm-based architecture (µPCD).

(A and B) Colorimetric detection of tetracycline (10 mM) by AgNPrs as optical probe using µPCD (1) AgNPrs (2) tetracycline 10 mM (3) AgNPrs/tetracycline (10 mM). Incubation times were 0 (A) and 30 min (B).

In the colorimetric process of tetracycline using AgNPrs, the main mechanism relies on the change in the phenomenon of SPR induced by the controlled nanoparticle aggregation process; in such a way that the presence of tetracycline in the reaction environment, mediated by silver or oxygen ions in solution and sometimes influenced by sensitive functional groups (such as amine and hydroxyl in the antibiotic structure), leads to alters morphology of AgNPrs from prismatic shapes to more spherical forms or reduces the particle size.

For further investigation of interaction of optical probe-analyte, dynamic light scattering (DLS) and zeta potential analyses were conducted to evaluate the surface charge, colloidal stability, and morphological changes of the nanoparticles post-interaction (Fig. S1, SI). The bare AgNPrs exhibited a narrow size distribution, indicative of a highly homogeneous colloidal suspension. Following a 30-minute incubation with tetracycline, a distinct increase in the hydrodynamic diameter was observed. This enlargement, coupled with a slight broadening of the size distribution curve, confirms the successful adsorption and conjugation of tetracycline molecules onto the AgNPrs surface, accompanied by controlled, localized aggregation. Zeta potential measurements further substantiated these surface modifications. Bare AgNPrs displayed a zeta potential of −25.7 mV, indicating robust electrostatic stabilization preventing spontaneous aggregation. Upon interaction with tetracycline, the zeta potential shifted significantly to 21.91 mV. This charge reversal strongly implies the successful formation of an AgNPrs/tetracycline complex, driven by electrostatic interactions between the functional groups of the antibiotic and the nanoparticle surface. Similar results were obtained using analysis of conductivity of each solution (Probe and Prob-Analyte). As can be seen in Fig. S1 (SI), conductivity of probe was 0.687 mS cm−1 which after interaction of probe with candidate analyte it changed to 7.62 mS cm. These findings confirm that AgNPrs serve as highly responsive Nano carriers and optical reporters for tetracycline.

Analytical approach

The analytical capability of the sensor was systematically evaluated by monitoring the time-dependent chromogenic response. In this part of investigation, initially, tetracycline at various concentrations was injected into zones containing silver nanoprisms. Then, RGB data was recorded under optimized conditions before and after the interaction. Furthermore, chemical interactions were investigated using colorimetric method and RGB data was extracted by Color-Picker app that installed on smartphone. Then, calibration curve was drawn based on variation of Abs(a.u.) versus concentration of analyte (Fig. 3(A, B) and VS3 (SI)). Quantitatively, the assay demonstrated excellent linearity based on the green (G) channel values extracted from digital images (Fig. 3(C and D)). Accorrding to obtained results, at 0 min of incubation time, the calibration curve yielded a linear regression of Abs = −0.0119Ctetracycline + 0.1626 (R2 = 0.9438). Following the optimal 30-minute incubation, a regression equation of Abs = −0.0248Ctetracycline + 0.3252 (R2 = 0.964) was obtained. The lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) was determined to be 2.5 µM. The data highlight a clear time-dependent plasmonic enhancement, where extended incubation facilitates complete analyte-probe complexation, thereby amplifying the analytical signal.

(A and B) Visual colorimetric change of the vial-based assay in the presence of various concentrations of tetracycline in two incubation times (0 and 30 minutes) (1 : 1 V/V), respectively. (C and D) Curves curve by G value in 0 and 30 min of incubation times, respectively.

Optical indices including wavelength shift, and color temperature showed in the Fig. S2 (SI) that the sensing system had a significant and multidimensional response to changes in tetracycline concentration. In the initial concentration regions, the noticeable shift of the spectrum towards shorter wavelengths indicates concentration-dependent changes in the local dielectric environment and drug-nanoparticle interaction; while the nonlinear behavior of luminance reflects the complex interplay between light absorption and scattering due to the aggregation and rearrangement of nanoparticles. On the other hand, the uniform increase in color temperature from about 15 000 K to nearly 30 000 K within 30 min indicates a gradual and measurable change in the chromatic properties of the system, which can be used as a quantitative and complementary index for tetracycline detection. Overall, the convergence of these three optical parameters confirms that the designed system is not only sensitive to the presence of tetracycline, but also has the ability to resolve concentration and time monitoring. In conclusion, the results of optical analysis show that this nanoparticle-based sensor offers a stable, sensitive and multi-indicator response mechanism to tetracycline. Spectral shift, luminance changes and color temperature increase are all evidence of effective interaction between the analyte and the sensor system and indicate that these features can be used for qualitative and quantitative detection of tetracycline. Also, the repeatability of the trends within a time interval of 30 min indicates that this system has a good capacity for practical applications in rapid and reliable monitoring of pharmaceutical contamination.

After analytical study by commercial vials, µPCD-based platform was utilized for the determination of tetracycline by optical probe by one-droplet of analyte and probe. The performance of the µPCD-based assay is presented in Fig. 4 and VS4 (SI). The µPCD demonstrated a progressive variation in color intensity in response to varying tetracycline concentrations (0.1 to 10 mM). Absorbance histograms derived from the maximum plasmon band showed a linear attenuation with increasing analyte concentration, indicative of analyte-induced LSPR modulation (Fig. 4). The calibration curve at 30 minutes exhibited high precision (R2 = 0.945).

A comparative evaluation of the analytical performances summarized in Table 1 demonstrates that the proposed AgNPrs-based colorimetric platform offers a competitive balance between sensitivity, simplicity, portability, and operational cost. Although several previously reported fluorescence- and electrochemical-based sensors achieved lower limits of detection in the nanomolar range, these methods generally require sophisticated instrumentation, complex fabrication procedures, specialized recognition elements, or highly controlled laboratory conditions. In contrast, the present method enables tetracycline determination using a simple colorimetric response generated by the interaction between AgNPrs and tetracycline, combined with smartphone-assisted image analysis. The developed sensor provides a practical detection range spanning from 2.5 µM to 10 mM with a lower limit of quantification of 2.5 µM, which is suitable for rapid screening and routine monitoring applications. Furthermore, unlike many previously reported systems that depend on fluorescence spectrometers, electrochemical workstations, or complex nanocomposite architectures, the proposed platform operates using a low-cost disposable parafilm-based microdevice and requires only microliter-scale sample volumes. Therefore, while the analytical sensitivity is not the lowest among the reported methods, the combination of portability, ease of fabrication, visual readout capability, minimal reagent consumption, and compatibility with point-of-care analysis represents a significant practical advantage. These characteristics position the proposed AgNPrs decorated µPCD platform as a promising alternative for on-site tetracycline monitoring where simplicity, accessibility, and cost-effectiveness are as important as analytical performance.

Real sample analysis (human plasma)

The determination of tetracycline was evaluated in human plasma matrices (Fig. S4, SI). Despite the complexity of the biological matrix, the assay retained a highly distinct visual color transition from dark blue to light blue (Fig. S4 and VS5 (SI)). The calibration curves in Fig. S5A (SI) shows that with increasing tetracycline concentration, the wavelength (λ) decreases. This decrease in wavelength is observed at both 0 and 30 min of incubation times, but at 0 min (green line) the decrease is greater and at 30 min (purple line) it is slightly reduced. As shown in Fig. S5B (SI) the luminance (brightness or radiance) increases with increasing tetracycline concentration. At time 0 min, luminance is relatively constant and low, but at time 30 min luminance increases significantly, especially at high concentrations. This increase in luminance at time 30 min could indicate biochemical reactions or structural changes in the sample that lead to increased radiance.

The reported colorimetric and calibration approaches were also utilized to determine spiked tetracycline in human plasma samples by engineered µ-PCDs (Fig. S6 and VS6 SI). Based on obtained results the calibration curves obtained as (Abs = −0.0059Ctetracycline + 0.1313 and Abs = −0.0046Ctetracycline + 0.123, for two incubation times 0 and 30 min, respectively) and established µPCD based platform towards tetracycline analysis in human plasma samples.

The obtained curves in the Fig. S7 (SI) presented the relationships between different concentrations of tetracycline and the associated wavelength, luminance, and color temperature over two incubation times (0 and 30 min). As can be seen, the λmax remain stable across the tested concentration range, indicating a consistent response of the tetracycline in plasma samples. Interestingly, color temperature follows a similar trend, with variances observed based on concentration and incubation time. According to obtained results, µPCD based sensing platform demonstrates strong quantitative capability for tetracycline in plasma than commercial vials. Using µPCD, analysis was performed by one drope of analyte and optical prob. Therefore, there is no need to much volume sample. Also, by this portable substrate and proposed platform, on-site analysis was performed.

Selectivity analysis

High specificity is a critical prerequisite for reliable colorimetric sensing. The selectivity of the AgNPrs-based assay was rigorously challenged against a panel of potential biological and pharmaceutical interferes, including amino acids (glycine, cysteine, phenylalanine, arginine, proline), metabolites (ascorbic acid, urea, dopamine), and distinct pharmaceutical agents (metoprolol, sotalol, phenobarbital, heparin, propranolol, indomethacin, famotidine). As depicted in Fig. 5 and VS7 (SI), the characteristic colorimetric transition to green was exclusively observed in the presence of tetracycline. Interfering agents with highly active functional groups could not affect the identification process of this analyte, so that the green color related to the interaction of the analyte with the optical probe remained stable and almost unchanged (Fig. 5). As can be seen in Fig. 5, there is not significant difference on the solution color after adding of interfering species at two reaction times (0 and 30 min) of analyte-probe.

Quantitative analysis of the G-values and luminance profiles revealed that the key optical parameters remained relatively unperturbed by the presence of interferes. Qualitative selectivity was visually confirmed by the distinct color changes observed in the multi-well plate assay (Fig. S8, SI). Also, Fig. S9 (SI) illustrated a selectivity analysis of various interfering species in the sensing profile of tetracycline by AgNPrs in terms of wavelength, luminance, and color temperature at two incubation times (0 and 30 min). The histogram shows that the wavelength (λmax) values across different solutions exhibit relative stability, with some variations depending on the solution type, suggesting specific chemical interactions influencing the absorbance characteristics. The luminance data in Fig. S21B demonstrates fluctuating values over the solution types, with a notable increase observed in the 30-min incubation period compared to the initial 0-min measurements, indicating a possible enhancement in the light-emitting properties of the solutions upon prolonged incubation. Finally, Fig S9C (SI) reflects a similar trend in color temperature, showing changes across different solutions and incubation times. Collectively, these findings underscore the importance of both the type of solution and incubation time in determining the optical properties, which could help optimize conditions for analytical applications in various fields.

Finally, in this research, a comparative study was conducted between traditional colorimetric methods (colorimetry in commercial vials) and a novel colorimetric method (analysis based on a single drop of analyte and probe by using µPCD). According to the obtained results, which were collected in the detection, determination, and selectivity subsections of both methods, it can be concluded that the proposed novel method based on µPCD is an efficient and scientifically attractive approach in pharmaceutical analysis. With a very small sample volume, the same analysis as the traditional method can be performed with the same analytical results. On the other hand, this method does not require advanced laboratory equipment and can carry out quantitative analyses easily and with high precision using just a mobile phone and an installed app. The proposed smartphone-based colorimetric method was able to perform not only conventional colorimetry but also to create heat maps, providing much more useful data than traditional analytical methods. Moreover, qualitative analysis can even be conducted with the naked-eye, which is very useful for simple and rapid screening examinations. Additionally, the portability of the µPCD platform, along with its structural flexibility, thermal stability, and safety, are other advantages that can be utilized by the general public. The use of cellphones for analysis demonstrated that this tool can be employed for on-site drug analysis, particularly in TDM tests. These characteristics position the developed method in this research and the created tool as a novel technique in pharmaceutical and biomedical analysis, as it provides a quantitative identification method without the need for a laboratory or skilled operator. The portability of the µPCD platform and smartphone-assisted analysis suggests potential future applications in decentralized testing and point-of-care monitoring. However, additional clinical validation and regulatory assessment will be required before routine patient-directed use can be considered.

Conclusion

In summary, a highly sensitive, rapid, and low-cost colorimetric platform for the sensitive recognition of tetracycline using AgNPrs was developed. By exploiting the analyte-induced modulation of LSPR properties, the assay achieves a visual and quantifiable response in both commercial vial and newly designed devices (µPCD). The system demonstrates excellent analytical metrics, including an LLOQ of.5 µM, and rapid signal stabilization within 30 minutes. Furthermore, the seamless integration of smartphone-based digital image analysis (utilizing G-values, luminance, and color temperature) provides a multifaceted quantitative approach and suggest a replacement to conventional spectrophotometers. The platform exhibited outstanding selectivity against a wide range of biological and pharmaceutical interferes and was successfully applied to quantification of tetracycline in complex human plasma matrices. This innovative µPCDholds substantial promise as an accessible, high-throughput, use-friendly tool for low-cost monitoring of tetracycline as candidate analyte. The qualitative and quantitative analytical results were fully consistent with each other, and the new tool (µPCD) can be used as a common laboratory method.

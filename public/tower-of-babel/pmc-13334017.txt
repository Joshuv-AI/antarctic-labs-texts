Research on the impact mechanism of intergovernmental collaboration on cross-regional corporate cooperative behavior based on an evolutionary game analysis framework
Authors: Lei Mao, Renjun Xu, Jun Hu, Yimiao Song, Yingming Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Under the context of advancing high-quality development, intergovernmental collaboration, as a pivotal governance approach, has emerged as a critical pathway to facilitating cross-regional enterprise cooperation, optimizing resource allocation, and enhancing overall competitiveness. To clarify the evolutionary mechanisms and stability strategies of cross-regional enterprise cooperation behavior under intergovernmental collaboration, this paper builds a tripartite evolutionary game model featuring two cross-regional enterprises and intergovernmental collaborative bodies.Numerical simulations are conducted focusing on six dimensions: initial willingness to cooperate, changes in cross-regional enterprise cooperation income (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\Delta\:}R$$\end{document}) and operational cost (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\Delta\:}C$$\end{document}), default penalty intensity, and income distribution coefficient, interaction effect of variables and heterogeneity analysis. The main conclusions are: (1) A sufficiently high initial willingness of intergovernmental collaboration (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:x>0.4$$\end{document}) is a prerequisite for enterprise cooperation. (2) Net benefits of cooperation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\Delta\:}R-{\Delta\:}C$$\end{document}) make cooperation faster. (3) Default penalties have a critical threshold effect: below the threshold, cooperation collapses; above the threshold, cooperation becomes stable. (4) Fair division of benefits (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\alpha\:\approx\:0.5$$\end{document}) stimulates cooperation; large default penalty can to some extent make up for the unfairness of benefit distribution. (5) Asymmetric initial willingness between core and peripheral cities are overcome by strong intergovernmental cooperation.Based on these findings, targeted countermeasures to promote cross-regional enterprise cooperation are proposed.

Introduction

Significant cross-regional enterprise cooperation can combine regional resources, technologies, talents, and other production factors, offering key advantages, enhancing enterprise innovation capabilities, and further boosting regional economic development. However, constrained by administrative divisions and local interests, cross-regional enterprise cooperation is hindered by multiple practical obstacles, including market fragmentation, inconsistent policies, and information asymmetry. Effective intergovernmental collaboration represents a viable solution to these challenges; as a governance model capable of breaking down administrative barriers and advancing coordinated regional development, it effectively addresses the dilemmas faced in cross-regional enterprise cooperation.

Intergovernmental synergy affects the cooperation behavior of cross-regional enterprises in the following ways: (1)Reducing transaction costs, namely due to information asymmetry, cross-domain enterprises often need to communicate and coordinate with local enterprises and government departments, thereby increasing the costs of information search, judgment, and negotiation. Through intergovernmental coordination, they can break down the barriers between different departments, jointly formulate unified market rules, establish information-sharing platforms, and build a unified regional coordination mechanism to achieve shared governance and cost savings. (2)Optimizing resource allocation, namely cross-regional governments build a cross-regional cooperation platform with a collaborative mechanism. Cross-regional enterprises can, through this platform, strengthen cross-regional cooperation and interaction, and mobilize more regional resources to serve enterprises by strengthening cross-regional linkage. The division and cooperation of the industrial chain across cross-domain enterprises promotes the free flow of talent, technology, and capital in the region, helping address the problems of talent shortages, outdated technologies, and insufficient funds that can hinder the development of these enterprises.(3)Enhancing innovation ability, namely through cooperation, the government increases its investment in scientific and technological innovation and builds a public service platform for regional joint innovation to provide support for innovation in cross-regional enterprise cooperation. intergovernmental collaboration can also facilitate in-depth collaboration among industry, university, and research institutions in different places; promote the transformation of innovative achievements into actual products or technologies, help enterprises solve technological innovation problems, further improve the success rate of cross-domain enterprise cooperation, and encourage enterprises in different locations to engage in cross-domain cooperation.(4)Enhancing the level of regional integration, namely intergovernmental collaboration can enhance connectivity between regions, improve the efficiency of the flow of people, products, and information, and reduce enterprises’ operating costs, through construction and improvement of regional transportation, energy, communication, and other facilities and networks. Rely on intergovernmental collaboration to regulate market order, accelerate regional integration, and empower cross-regional enterprises to expand their development reach.

This study focuses on the evolutionary rules of cross-regional enterprise cooperation in the Yangtze River Delta and the influencing mechanism of intergovernmental collaboration. It mainly relies on regional integration theory, intergovernmental collaboration theory and evolutionary game theory.These three theories are not isolated, but form an internal logical closed loop of “scenario carrier-intervention variable-core support”, which jointly provides theoretical support and logical adaptation for the research topic. Their specific internal connections are as follows:

As the realistic scenario and theoretical background of this study, regional integration theory takes breaking cross-regional administrative barriers, promoting the free flow of factors and realizing coordinated regional development as its core demands. These demands are not only the prerequisite for the development of cross-regional enterprise cooperation in the Yangtze River Delta, but also the fundamental goal of intergovernmental collaboration. Therefore, regional integration theory provides a clear goal orientation for intergovernmental collaboration, while intergovernmental collaboration is the specific implementation path of regional integration theory at the level of government behavior, and the two form a close “goal-means” connection.

As a key intervention theory, intergovernmental collaboration theory’s core viewpoint lies in clarifying that the government, as the core subject of regional governance, its collaborative behavior is not a simple external environmental variable, but a key force directly participating in cross-regional resource allocation and intervening in the behavior of market subjects. This theory provides sufficient theoretical basis for this study to include the government as a game participant in the tripartite evolutionary game model (Government, Enterprise 1, Enterprise 2), which is consistent with the practical demand of governments to collaboratively promote enterprise cooperation in the process of regional integration of the Yangtze River Delta.

As the core of the methodology of this study, the core viewpoints of evolutionary game theory, such as “replicator dynamic equation” and “evolutionary stable strategy (ESS)”, provide a scientific analytical tool for explaining the dynamic evolutionary process of cross-regional enterprise cooperation strategies and the formation of equilibrium states. Intergovernmental collaboration theory provides theoretical support for the subject setting and parameter design of the evolutionary game model (such as parameters related to strong/weak government collaboration). That is, the government changes the cooperation income, default cost and benefit distribution rules of enterprises through strategies such as strong collaboration (unifying policy standards, strengthening default punishment, improving incentive mechanisms) or weak collaboration (loose information exchange, lack of effective supervision), thereby affecting the evolutionary path of enterprise cooperation strategies. At the same time, the numerical simulation results of the evolutionary game model can also test the effectiveness of different intergovernmental collaboration strategies, providing empirical support for the optimization of intergovernmental collaboration strategies, and the two form a two-way “support-test” connection.

In summary, regional integration theory defines the research scenario and core goals, intergovernmental collaboration theory clarifies the intervention subject and action mechanism, and evolutionary game theory provides the research method and analytical tool. The three theories support and adapt to each other, jointly forming the theoretical framework of this study, and providing a complete theoretical and logical support for solving the core proposition of “how intergovernmental collaboration affects the evolution of cross-regional enterprise cooperation”, ensuring the high adaptation between the theoretical perspective and the research question.

Literature reviews

Core drivers and constraints of Yangtze River Delta(YRD) regional integration

Scholars have extensively discussed the key factors promoting or hindering the integrated development of the YRD. In terms of driving forces, Hong(2018) argued that prioritizing collaboration among geographically adjacent cities helps foster internal growth engines, which, in turn, drive regional integration1. Liu(2019) highlighted that market integration lays the foundation for high-quality development in the YRD, while competition policies act as the institutional cornerstone of the integration system2. Cheng(2019)suggested that regions in the new era should adopt intelligent development strategies, leveraging digitalization to facilitate and elevate the quality of regional integration3. Yu(2018) proposed establishing a new, inclusive regional coordination system as the core driver of integration in the YRD4.Du(2018) states that enhancing inter-regional cooperation in the YRD should rely on the free flow and sharing of innovative elements to create a collaborative innovation environment for regional integration, which is an important driving force and asset in the process of achieving high-quality integration in the YRD, as well as sustainable economic and social development5.

In terms of constraints, Chen(2018) noted that the factors impeding resource flow in regional integration primarily include structural and institutional constraints: structural factors refer to cooperation barriers arising from objective conditions such as local economic development, social culture, and natural environments, which can be gradually resolved through coordinated regional development, and institutional factors involve human-made obstacles such as region-specific policies and regulations, requiring cross-regional institutional cooperation to strengthen coordination6. Zeng and Teng(2018) emphasized the need to address administrative fragmentation, integrate innovation entities within the region, and construct a clear-cut regional innovation network7. Zhang(2025), taking the YRD region as an example, suggests that the key to regional integration lies in systematically breaking the “triple barriers” composed of administrative divisions, market fragmentation, and institutional differences, with “collaborative governance” as the core method8.

Theories and practices of intergovernmental coordination in the YRD

The theory of intergovernmental coordination originated from the practice of federalism in the United States in the 1930s. The core aim is to study the interaction network between different levels of government (central and local). Cao(2010) defined intergovernmental coordination as the actions, procedures, and systems carried out across different levels of government to achieve public goals through consultations, the signing of framework agreements, and related activities9. Liu(2012) believes that intergovernmental coordination is an extension of the theory of intergovernmental relations and presents its three key characteristics as follows: (1) the government is a relationship model based on “coordination”, (2) focusing on the coordination of intergovernmental action and resource sharing, and (3) aiming to achieve mutually beneficial results10. Chen(2019) suggested establishing an integrated development pilot area beyond the administrative regional boundary in the hinterland of the YRD, comprising a triangular zone at the junction of Shanghai, Jiangsu, and Zhejiang provinces, and one city11.

In the practice of the YRD, Suo(2023)found that initial efficient intergovernmental cooperation is evident in the YRD region. It has the following characteristics: an imbalance in network status distribution, a bilateral cooperative relationship of homogeneity, a cooperative structure characterized by “overall weak connection and local strong correlation”, and a cooperative field based on guarantee12. Zhao and Zhou(2017) believe that the relationship between intergovernmental competition and cooperation in the current regional environmental collaborative governance skews more toward competition. This lack of cooperation must be transformed through institutional arrangements such as interest guidance and sharing as a dynamic mechanism13. Suo(2016) suggested that to improve intergovernmental collaboration, the focus must be on encouraging diverse collaborative models, eliminating institutional barriers, and enhancing incentives for innovation in local government cooperation (Feiock,2007)14,15.

Intergovernmental cooperation network in the YRD based on social network analysis

Zhang(2017)argues that the operational dilemma regarding interprovincial cooperation and integration in the YRD stems from the inefficient operation of its three-tier framework. Rooted in the misalignment between administrative divisions and economic zones in both goals and spatial scope, this issue is further evidenced by non-cooperative gaming among local governments. Future optimization can be carried out around three pillars: integrating cooperative concepts, refining the cooperative system, and consolidating hardware network support16.While the spatial economy of a city-region is designed to foster cross-jurisdictional connectivity and coordination, Li and Wu(2017) argue that efforts to transform this idealized city-region concept into a reality have been undermined by the political dynamics of existing territorial administrative divisions. In practice, on-the-ground city-regionalism often manifests as an ad hoc arrangement, lacking a consistent and unified governing framework17. Hu et al.(2025)conducted an empirical study on intergovernmental collaboration networks in the YRD business environment from a multi-dimensional proximity perspective, concluding that digital technology proximity and cities with lower administrative hierarchy gaps are more suitable for building intergovernmental collaboration networks18.

Drawing on social network analysis, Wang and Du(2021) construct an intergovernmental relational network to examine regional integration policies in the YRD. Their results show that the network structure grows increasingly sophisticated with closer intergovernmental cooperation, expanding vertically in depth and horizontally in scope; intergovernmental collaboration advances in an orderly manner across fields, featuring a one-core and pluralistic structure19. Based on intergovernmental cooperation news data from 2009 to 2021, Ma and Zhang(2024) construct an intergovernmental cooperation network for the YRD and employ social network analysis to examine its basic structural attributes and spatiotemporal dynamics. Their key findings are as follows: (1) The YRD intergovernmental cooperation network is maturing, with nested patterns formed among urban agglomerations and metropolitan areas, leading to a multi-centric and multi-scale network structure. (2) The geographic proximity, national strategic planning and the socio-economic development demand all affect the choices of cooperation partners from the local governments and encourage the dynamic process of the cooperation network20. Dong et al.(2026) discussed how the intergovernmental activities in the YRD have influence over inter-city collaborative technological innovation, by using the data of patents and news of government departments. Their research shows that the intergovernmental activities have significant effect on the cross-city collaborative patents, by promoting the flow of innovation factors, market attractiveness and resource re-allocation. But the effect of inter-governmental activities is only significant for core–peripheral city pairs and the economically weaker cities get bigger innovations. These findings note the important but inconsistent role of intergovernmental activities in fostering regional technological collaboration and regional balanced development21.

Analysis of government and multi-subject behaviors based on evolutionary game theory

Some scholars have included government departments as the participants and adopted a multi-dimensional view on government behavior, studied by using game theory. For instance, Wu et al.(2019) constructed an evolutionary game model involving governments, universities, and enterprises under the assumption of bounded rationality, examining the collaborative innovation mechanism among the three parties22. Sheng et al.(2020) explore evolutionary stability strategies and influencing factors in relation to China’s central and local government and enterprises. Their research findings are as follows: central government supervision is key in ensuring the implementation of environmental regulation policies. The cost and expected benefits of the regulatory strategy directly determine the final evolutionary stability strategy23. Song et al.(2020) analyzed local government behavioral choices regarding regional environmental cooperative governance using evolutionary game theory, concluding that income and preference differences, compensation for the loss of partners, and penalties for non-cooperation are the main factors influencing local governments’ adoption of cooperative governance strategies24. Su et al.(2021) developed a three-party evolutionary game model of governments, enterprises, and universities to analyze collaborative innovation under government incentive and non-incentive strategies. The system reaches an ideal state when innovation consciousness is high, and enterprise innovation costs are low, while government incentives must be balanced to avoid negative enterprise behavior25.

Xue and Chen(2021) employed evolutionary game theory to analyze government-guided industry–university–research collaborative innovation models, finding that government guidance significantly promotes collaborative innovation by adjusting the allocation of cooperation income and costs26. Li and Huang(2023) simulated the integration of the Yangtze River Delta from an urban cooperation perspective, revealing the internal mechanism of urban collaborative governance. Evolutionary game results indicated that participating in normalized integrated development is the most stable strategic choice for cities in the region27.

Yuan and Li(2024) explored the evolutionary process and factors influencing collaborative innovation in the enterprise innovation ecosystem (EIE), A three-player evolutionary game model analyzes behaviors under varying initial strategy probabilities. Actor behaviors are shaped by initial probability, synergy sensitivity, transformation efficiency, output elasticity, subsidies, penalties, and public influence28. Tian et al.(2025) constructed a tripartite evolutionary game model involving governments, digital platform enterprises, and stakeholders to explore the evolutionary dynamics of collaborative governance strategies and their influencing factors, followed by a system dynamics-based simulation analysis. Their findings indicate government-imposed punitive measures and internal incentive mechanisms positively guide governance decisions toward collaborative governance29.

Research gaps

Although existing studies have explored multiple dimensions of the YRD integration, including driving forces, restrictive factors, intergovernmental coordination, and multi-agent behavioral characteristics, only a small number of scholars have focused on the interdisciplinary intersection of intergovernmental collaboration and cross-regional enterprise cooperation. For instance, Chen(2019) argued that intergovernmental cross-regional cooperation and cross-regional enterprise collaboration foster localized face-to-face interaction between cooperation and competition within the same spatial scope11. Li et al.(2025) claimed that interactive exchanges among local governments can strengthen the willingness of interregional enterprise cooperation and raise firms’ R&D returns; in particular, such interactions effectively improve the willingness and efficiency of collaborative innovation between state-owned enterprises and private enterprises30.

Nevertheless, there remains a prominent academic research gap: few studies have adopted evolutionary game analysis and evolutionary equilibrium models to depict the dynamic evolutionary trajectory of how intergovernmental coordination affects cross-regional enterprise cooperative behaviors in the process of regional integration.

Accordingly, exploring the influencing mechanism of intergovernmental coordination on cross-regional enterprise cooperation and proposing targeted policy implications under the background of high-quality integrated development of the YRD possesses significant theoretical value and practical significance.

Basic assumptions of the model

(1) Game subject: The game model has three participants, namely, Enterprise 1 in Region A, Enterprise 2 in Region B, and intergovernmental collaboration (Ingc). Intergovernmental collaboration provides an incentive mechanism for cooperative innovation among inter-regional enterprises, and the degree of cooperation among enterprises evolves dynamically with the level of intergovernmental collaboration, thereby further promoting intergovernmental collaboration and inter-regional enterprise cooperative innovation. Inter-regional enterprises and intergovernmental collaboration finally reach an evolutionary stable strategy (ESS) after many games and iterations(Friedman,1991)31.

(2) Cooperation strategy: In the evolutionary game model concerning the cooperative innovation behavior of cross-domain enterprises under intergovernmental collaboration, intergovernmental collaboration can choose to provide as many preferential policies for collaborative innovation as possible for cross-domain enterprises and spend more costs to supervise and constrain the collaborative innovation process between enterprises. It is also possible to choose to provide less preferential policies or none at all, while at the same time supervising and constraining the collaborative innovation process between enterprises (Smith and Price,1973)32. Summary of key findings from the simulation

Based on the above analyses, the main conclusions are: (1) A sufficiently high initial willingness of intergovernmental collaboration (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:x>0.4$$\end{document}) is a prerequisite for enterprise cooperation. (2) Net benefits of cooperation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\Delta\:}R-{\Delta\:}C$$\end{document}) make cooperation faster. (3) Default penalties have a critical threshold effect: below the threshold, cooperation collapses; above the threshold, cooperation becomes stable. (4) Fair division of benefits (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\alpha\:\approx\:0.5$$\end{document}) stimulates cooperation; large default penalty can to some extent make up for the unfairness of benefit distribution. (5) Asymmetric initial willingness between core and peripheral cities are overcome by strong intergovernmental cooperation.

Conclusions and policy recommendations

Conclusion

Through evolutionary game analysis, we can get: (1) Original willingness of intergovernmental collaboration exists threshold effect—when it is above a certain value, enterprises make the maximum effort to cooperate; (2) default penalties of weak cooperation should be more than a certain level to have effect; (3) The benefit distribution coefficient close to 0.5 strengthens the stability of cooperation greatly; (4) Interaction effect of default penalties and benefit distribution makes greater default penalties can weaken the effect of unbalanced distribution negatively.

As an important governance model, intergovernmental cooperation influences the enterprise cooperative behavior of cross-domain enterprises from multiple aspects, namely: lowering transaction costs, optimizing enterprise resource coordination allocation and promoting enterprise creativity and competitiveness of region, to promote the cross regional enterprise cooperation is also very effective.The government should further break down the administrative barriers, promote coordinated regional economic development, best allocate regional resources and enhance the competitiveness of different regions through improving intergovernment coordination mechanism, strengthen the transportation network construction, actively promote division and cooperation of cross-regional industrial chain and create an appropriate business atmosphere. This will further improve government department synergy to encourage cross-regional enterprise cooperation.

Policy Recommendations

Strengthen the initial intergovernment collaboration beyond the“threshold effect”

Simulation shows that when the initial willingness of intergovernment collaboration (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:x$$\end{document}) is less than about 0.4, enterprises converge to weak cooperation. So, governments in the YRD need to prioritize formal, high-level intergovernment coordination mechanism at the initial stage, such as the existing YRD regional cooperation office, and expand their authority and resources to ensure that the initial intergovernment collaboration intensity exceeds the critical threshold.Pilot projects should be launched in regions with typical poor coordination (for example, the mouth of Jiangsu, Zhejiang and Anhui) to raise the baseline of willingness.

Conduct cross-regional joint punishment with critical punishment intensity

Set cross-regional unified minimum penalty more than critical value and restrain opportunism. In the process of cross-regional collaborative governance, setting up a cross-regional joint punishment mechanism in the form of unified cross-regional strategy can efficiently handle the dilemma of collaborative failure. Setting cross-regional unified minimum punishment value higher than critical value has the effect of making increase the cost for default and opportunism of local government and joint supplier quite strongly. The essence is to restrain opportunistic behavior, such as free riding, withholding information and unilateral breach of contract, among subjects undertaking cross-regional collaborative governance, so that it is easy to realize the evolutionary equilibrium of joint strategy in cross-regional collaborative governance.Specifically, penalties are set to be at least 20–30% higher than the possible cost savings to the defaulting enterprise, which can be evaluated according to cost data in actual practices in the YRD. Furthermore, penalties should be progressive—that is, growing with defaulting times—and jointly imposed by different local governments to ensure effectiveness.

Guide enterprises to distribute benefits fairly(\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\boldsymbol{\alpha\:}\approx\:0.5$$\end{document})

The simulation shows that benefit distribution coefficient \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\alpha\:$$\end{document} close to 0.5 will greatly encourage strong cooperation. The related policies to do this are: (a) publishing specific distribution ratios for different enterprises (or industries) according to value added; (b) providing fiscal subsidies or tax incentives to projects that adopt a 50:50 or similarly balanced distribution; (c) setting third-party arbitration mechanism to solve conflict of distribution in projects from cooperation projects in cross region areas, for example, in the G60 Innovation Corridor, and so on.

Address heterogeneity across core and peripheral cities

The heterogeneity analysis indicates that initially peripheral cities have a low willingness to participate in cooperation, but strong intergovernmental collaboration can break this heterogeneity. We thus recommend capacity building targeted at peripheral cities, such as joint training for local officials, dedicated local liaison offices and financial incentives for core-city enterprises to participate with peripheral enterprises. The YRD’s “one-core-multiple-cities” model must be consolidated to minimize the unequal development phenomena.

Strengthen intergovernmental coordination

Innovate institutions, unify policies, and improve cost–benefit sharing. Replacing loose consultation by a multi-level, multi-agent integrated decision system, enhancing motivation, supervision and arbitration. Coordinating policy-making across levels (industry, fiscal etc.) to get through with or avoid collision of policies. Enhancing cost–sharing and benefit–sharing, adjusting evaluation within enterprises, improving cooperating-dividend and parties’ enthusiasm.

Strengthen infrastructure

Bettering infrastructure increases the net cooperative benefit (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\Delta\:}R-{\Delta\:}C$$\end{document}), which speeds up converging to strong cooperation. Broaden transport networks in the YRD to reduce logistics costs and time costs. Increase investment in information and AI to establish a high-speed and secure network, increase the collaboration among government departments in information sharing, and establish a platform of exchange of data for inter-regional enterprise cooperation.

Promote cross-region industrial chain division

Increasing \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\Delta\:}R$$\end{document}, reducing \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\Delta\:}C$$\end{document}. Getting shared, industrial chain combined links across regions for value chain, innovation chain and labour division.Support cross-regional industry-university-research consortia, encourage leading firms and universities form innovation consortia, improve credit information transparency.

Create a good business environment

Lower transaction cost and increase penalties. Harmonize cross-regional business environments, improve government service, strive to maintain fairness. Harmonize cross-regional IP protection, crack down on infringement behaviors, form an innovation culture. Reward innovative enterprises and innovation talents, cultivate society respect for innovation.

Limitations and future research

Although this study makes new contributions, there are also limitations:

(1) Simplification of model assumptions. This paper assumes that the two enterprises have symmetrical cost-sharing coefficients \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\beta\:$$\end{document} and revenue-sharing coefficients \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\alpha\:$$\end{document}. Yet, in reality, enterprises have the asymmetric bargaining power because their scale, technological capacity and market power are different. Future work can put asymmetric parameters (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\alpha\:}_{1}\ne\:{\alpha\:}_{2}$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\beta\:}_{1}\ne\:{\beta\:}_{2}$$\end{document}) and analyze the evolutionary stable strategies of complex systems in the asymmetric circumstance.

(2) Assumption of the homogeneity of government collaboration. This paper simplifies the intergovernmental collaboration to two strategies: strong and weak. In reality, intergovernmental collaboration is heterogeneous (for example, policy coordination, fiscal allocation, information sharing and so on), and the effects of collaboration to different dimensions may be different. In future, more realistic modeling can be done through the multidimensional government behavior variables.

(3) Lack of validation of data: Although the simulation parameters in our paper come from literature and public data of YRD, the simulation results lack systematic validation with actual enterprise cooperation practice. In the future, panel data of cross-regional cooperation projects in YRD can be collected to verify the model’s result empirically.

(4) Neglect of industry heterogeneity: Cooperation costs and benefits have huge industry heterogeneity (for example, manufacturing industry, service industry and high-tech industry), which are not distinct in our paper. In the future, dedicated model can be built for different industries and differentiated recommendations can be made.

(5) Dynamic environmental effect lacked: The model in this paper does not consider the effect of external environment changing (such as economic cycle, policy change, technological shock) upon the stability of cooperation.

Going forward, random disturbance terms or the time-invariant parameter may be introduced to study the evolution of cooperation under environmental uncertainty in the future. Future research directions: (a) Expand model to multiple enterprises and network effect; (b) General learning of government and policy iteration; (c) The combination of evolutionary game and agent-based model for richer dynamics; (d) Comparative studies across Chinese city clusters.

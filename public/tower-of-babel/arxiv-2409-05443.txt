Dark QCD perspective inspired by strong CP problem at QCD scale
Bin Wang, Hiroyuki Ishida, Shinya Matsuzaki

Abstract
We discuss a QCD-scale composite axion model arising from dark QCD coupled to QCD. The presently proposed scenario not only solves the strong CP problem, but also is compatible with the preheating setup for the QCD baryogenesis. The composite axion is phenomenologically required to mimic the QCD pion, but can generically be flavorful, which could be testable via the induced flavor changing processes at experiments. Another axionlike particle (ALP) is predicted to achieve the axion relaxation mechanism, which can phenomenologically act as the conventional QCD axion. This ALP can be ultralight, having the mass less than 1 eV, to be a dark matter candidate. The QCD × \times dark QCD symmetry structure constrains dark QCD meson spectra, so that the dark η ′ \eta^{\prime} -like meson would only be accessible at the collider experiments. Still, the Belle II and Electron ion collider experiments can have a high enough sensitivity to probe the dark η ′ \eta^{\prime} -like meson in the diphoton channel, which dominantly arises from the mixing with the QCD η ′ \eta^{\prime} and the pionic composite axion. We also briefly address nontrivial cosmological aspects, such as those related to the dark-chiral phase transition, the dark matter production, and an ultraviolet completion related to the ultralight ALP.

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{ui}\cdot U_{R}^{u}-{U_{R}^{u}}^{\dagger}\cdot C_{ui}}{2},\,,

\displaystyle g_{A}^{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{ui}\cdot U_{R}^{u}+{U_{R}^{u}}^{\dagger}\cdot C_{u_{i}}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle g_{V}^{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{V_{\rm CKM}^{\dagger}\cdot C_{di}\cdot U_{R}^{d}-{U_{R}^{d}}^{\dagger}\cdot C_{di}\cdot V_{\rm CKM}}{2},\,,

\displaystyle g_{A}^{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{V_{\rm CKM}^{\dagger}\cdot C_{di}\cdot U_{R}^{d}+{U_{R}^{d}}^{\dagger}\cdot C_{d_{i}}\cdot V_{\rm CKM}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle C_{u_{i}}

\displaystyle=2m_{u_{i}}\,,\qquad C_{d_{i}}=2m_{d_{i}}\,.

(16)

Then the

gets modified as well.
When the

-ALP and photons are onshell,

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{a\gamma\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{a\gamma\gamma}}{4}a\cdot F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu},

(17)

where

\displaystyle g_{a\gamma\gamma}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\alpha}{\pi f_{a}}C_{\gamma\gamma}^{\rm eff}\,,

\displaystyle C_{\gamma\gamma}^{\rm eff}

\displaystyle\equiv 3\left[\sum_{u_{i}}(Q_{u_{i}}^{\rm em})^{2}\frac{(g_{A}^{u})_{ii}}{m_{u_{i}}}B_{1}\left(\frac{4m_{u_{i}}^{2}}{m_{a}^{2}}\right)+\sum_{d_{i}}(Q_{d_{i}}^{\rm em})^{2}\frac{(g_{A}^{d})_{ii}}{m_{d_{i}}}B_{1}\left(\frac{4m_{d_{i}}^{2}}{m_{a}^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

(18)

The loop function
B_{1}(x)
is given as

B_{1}(x)=1-xf^{2}(x),\quad f(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sin^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\hskip 69.1402pt{\rm for}\qquad x\geq 1\\
\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{i}{2}\ln\frac{1+\sqrt{1-x}}{1-\sqrt{1-x}}\hskip 19.91692pt{\rm for}\qquad x<1\end{cases}\,.

(19)

The

Using the CKM matrix elements,

(in magnitude), available from the experimental data

\displaystyle V_{\rm CKM}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0.974&0.225&3.8\times 10^{-3}\\

8\times 10^{-3}&-3.88\times 10^{-2}&1\end{array}\right)\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\lambda&\lambda^{3}\\

and

\displaystyle\frac{(g_{A}^{u})_{11}}{m_{u}}

\displaystyle=c_{12}^{u}c_{13}^{u}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{(g_{A}^{u})_{22}}{m_{c}}

\displaystyle=c_{12}^{u}c_{23}^{u}-s_{12}^{u}s_{13}^{u}s_{23}^{u}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{(g_{A}^{u})_{33}}{m_{t}}

\displaystyle=c_{13}^{u}c_{23}^{u}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{(g_{A}^{d})_{11}}{m_{d}}

\displaystyle=0.974c_{12}^{d}c_{13}^{d}-4.47\left(-s_{12}^{d}c_{23}^{d}-c_{12}^{d}s_{13}^{d}s_{23}^{d}\right)+3.38\left(s_{12}^{d}s_{23}^{d}-c_{12}^{d}s_{13}^{d}c_{23}^{d}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{(g_{A}^{d})_{22}}{m_{s}}

\displaystyle=0.011s_{12}^{d}c_{13}^{d}+0.987\left(c_{12}^{d}c_{23}^{d}-s_{12}^{d}s_{13}^{d}s_{23}^{d}\right)-1.87\left(-c_{12}^{d}s_{23}^{d}-s_{12}^{d}s_{13}^{d}c_{23}^{d}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{(g_{A}^{d})_{33}}{m_{b}}

\displaystyle=9\times 10^{-6}s_{13}^{d}+0.087c_{13}^{d}s_{23}^{d}-c_{13}^{d}c_{23}^{d}\,.

(28)

Taking

\displaystyle m_{a}

\displaystyle\simeq 140\,{\rm MeV}\times\left(\frac{\langle-\bar{F}F\rangle}{(600\,{\rm MeV)^{3}}}\right)^{1/2}\cdot\left(\frac{200\,{\rm MeV}}{f_{a}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{0.45\,{\rm MeV}}\right)^{1/2}\,.

IV.2
The ALP:
P

Putting the
a
-mass formula in Eqs.(
11
) with
the pionic ALP identifier (
29
) into the
P
-mass formula in Eq.(
11
), we find

\displaystyle m^{2}_{P}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{4v_{S}^{2}}(-\chi_{\rm top}^{\rm QCD})

\displaystyle\simeq\left(0.1\,{\rm eV}\times\left(\frac{|\chi_{\rm top}^{\rm QCD}|}{(76\,{\rm MeV})^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\frac{2.8\times{10^{5}}\,{\rm TeV}}{v_{S}}\right)\right)^{2}\,.

(30)

This implies that the
P
-ALP phenomenologically plays
the role identical to the typical QCD-invisible axion, with
the intrinsic PQ scale
(2v_{S})
.

The
P
-ALP can decay to diphoton via mixing with
the
a
-ALP and/or
\eta^{\prime}
after passing through mixing with
\eta_{d}

(see also Eq.(
8
)).
Since we are interested in a sort of an ultralight
P
-ALP, as in Eq.(
30
),
the
a
-
P
mixing induced
P-\gamma-\gamma
coupling will be
gigantically suppressed. This is due to the decoupling feature of QCD quark
loop contributions in
C_{\gamma\gamma}^{\rm eff}
, Eq.(
18
):

B_{1}(x)\to 0
as
x=4m_{q}^{2}/m_{P}^{2}\to\infty
. More precisely,
C_{\gamma\gamma}^{\rm eff}
for the
P
-ALP goes dumped scaling with
(m_{P})^{3}
.
This also reflects the fact that the
a
-ALP or
P
-ALP is not (mainly) composite of QCD quarks.
On the other hand,
\eta^{\prime}-\gamma-\gamma
coupling does not follow
such a decoupling, because this is associated with
the non-Abelian Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) anomaly
[
77
,
78
]
, or equivalently
due to the anomaly matching in QCD.
Thus the
P
-ALP coupling to diphoton dominantly arises from
the mixing with
\eta^{\prime}
via mixing with
\eta_{d}
, in Eq.(
8
).

The
P
-ALP coupling to diphoton is then evaluated as

\displaystyle g_{P\gamma\gamma}

\displaystyle\approx g_{P\gamma\gamma}\Bigg|_{\eta^{\prime}-\eta_{d}}=m_{P}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{6}m_{a}^{2}\sqrt{|\chi_{\rm top}^{\rm QCD}|}}{2m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}f_{\pi}m_{\eta_{d}}^{2}}\cdot g_{\eta^{\prime}\gamma\gamma}(p^{2}\simeq 0)

\displaystyle\simeq 4\times 10^{-10}\times\left(\frac{m_{P}}{1\,\rm{keV}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2\,\rm{GeV}}{m_{\eta_{d}}}\right)^{2}\cdot g_{\eta^{\prime}\gamma\gamma}(p^{2}\simeq 0)\,,

(31)

where the nondecoupling
\eta^{\prime}-\gamma-\gamma
coupling at zero momentum transfer (with
m_{P}
approximated to be zero) can be read off from the WZW term
[
77
,
78
]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}g_{\eta^{\prime}\gamma\gamma}\eta^{\prime}\tilde{F}\cdot F\,,

(32)

with

\displaystyle g_{\eta^{\prime}\gamma\gamma}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}}{\pi f_{\eta^{\prime}}}\,.

(33)

From the Particle Data Group
[
79
]
, we have the partial decay rate
\Gamma[\eta^{\prime}\to\gamma\gamma]\simeq 4.3\,\rm{keV}
, for the mass
m_{\eta^{\prime}}\simeq 958\,\rm{MeV}
.
Then we get
f_{\eta^{\prime}}\simeq 120\,\rm{MeV}
and
g_{\eta^{\prime}\gamma\gamma}\simeq 3.16\times 10^{-2}\,\rm{GeV}^{-1}
.
Thus the
P
-ALP coupling to diphoton is evaluated as

\displaystyle g_{P\gamma\gamma}\simeq 10^{-11}\,{\rm{GeV}^{-1}}\times\left(\frac{m_{P}}{1\,\rm{keV}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2\,\rm{GeV}}{m_{\eta_{d}}}\right)^{2}\,.

(34)

We also estimate the
P\to\gamma\gamma
decay rate (which corresponds to the inverse of the lifetime),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{P}

\displaystyle\simeq 10^{-33}\,{\rm eV}\times\left(\frac{m_{P}}{1\,{\rm keV}}\right)^{5}\cdot\left(\frac{2\rm{GeV}}{m_{\eta_{d}}}\right)^{4}\,.

(35)

The lifetime
\tau_{P}=1/\Gamma[P\to\gamma\gamma]\sim 10^{57}
s
is much longer than the age of the universe
\sim 10^{17}
s, hence this
P
-ALP
is a dark matter. The relic abundance can be produced via the coherent oscillation just like the QCD axion dark matter. We will come back to the issue on the dark matter production later in
the section of Summary and discussions.

The current ALP-photon coupling limits
are available at
https://cajohare.github.io/AxionLimits
, which
gives the constraint on the
m_{P}-g_{P\gamma\gamma}
trajectory in Eq.(
34
)
as
m_{P}\lesssim 1
eV.
The signal trajectory will be however far off
the future prospected observation regimes of hellioscopes.
A recent indirect detection proposal via the Subaru telescope
[
80
]
will not be capable to probe
this ultralight
P
-ALP.
This result is tied with the size of
m_{a}
: if
m_{a}
were larger, some hellioscopes
(e.g., the prospected LAMPOST (Light
A^{\prime}
Multilayer Periodic Optical SNSPD Target) hellioscope observation) could probe
the
P
-ALP around
m_{P}\sim 0.1
eV.
Thus the
P
-ALP is constrained to be invisible in order to predict the pionic
a
-ALP.

IV.3
The ALP:
\eta_{d}

The
\eta_{d}
-ALP strongly couples to other dQCD hadrons.
The
\eta_{d}
-hadronic couplings are parametrized in terms of the linear-sigma model
description. Let the sigma-model field be
M_{d}
,
and parametrize it as
M_{d}={\cal S}+i{\cal P}
with the scalar-meson matrix field
{\cal S}
and the pseudoscalar-meson matrix field
{\cal P}
, which take a
4\times 4
matrix form.
This
M_{d}
acts as the interpolating field
for the dQCD fermion bilinear:
M_{d}^{ij}\approx\bar{F}_{R}^{j}F_{L}^{i}
, hence
reflects the chiral
U(4)_{L}\times U(4)_{R}
symmetry, just like
U_{d}
in Eq.(
3
).
The nonzero VEV of
{\cal S}
, aligned to be vectorial (
\propto f_{\pi_{d}}
), realizes
the desired spontaneous breaking,
U(4)_{L}\times U(4)_{R}\to U(4)_{V}
.

In the linear-sigma model description,
the
U(4)_{L}\times U(4)_{R}
symmetric potential for
M
takes the form

\displaystyle V(M_{d})=\lambda_{1}{\rm tr}[(M_{d}^{\dagger}M_{d})^{2}]+\lambda_{2}({\rm tr}[M_{d}^{\dagger}M_{d}])^{2}\,,

(36)

where
\eta_{d}
is embedded in the singlet representation part of
{\cal P}
.
Allowing the VEV of
{\cal S}
to be
\propto f_{\pi_{d}}\cdot 1_{4\times 4}
,
the trilinear coupling with
\eta_{d}
can be generated from there, which
would be in part relevant to clarifying the main decay channel of
\eta_{d}
.
Note that due to the strong QCD gauge interaction,
the residual
U(4)_{V}
symmetry is explicitly broken down to be split into the

U(3)_{V}\times U(1)_{V}
structure, where the
SU(3)
portion in
U(3)_{V}
is identified as
the
SU(3)_{c}
of QCD.
Hence the QCD-colored dQCD hadrons
eventually get massive enough to be on the same order as

\eta_{d}
mass.
Thus only the QCD-color singlet mesons, the
a
-ALP and its scalar meson counterpart
{\cal S}_{a}
, would be crucial in Eq.(
36
) to investigate the

\eta_{d}
couplings to lighter dQCD hadrons.
We then find the relevant trilinear coupling term,

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\eta_{d}{\cal S}_{a}a}\sim\frac{\Lambda_{d}^{2}}{f_{\pi_{d}}}\cdot\eta_{d}\,a\,{\cal S}_{a}\,,

(37)

where we have roughly evaluated
\lambda_{1,2}\sim(\Lambda_{d}/f_{\pi_{d}})^{2}
.
The
{\cal S}_{a}
mass can be smaller than the
\eta_{d}
mass to be
as small as 140 MeV of the
a
-ALP, or higher.
Thereby, the
\eta_{d}
-ALP can mainly decay to
{\cal S}_{a}
and the
a
-ALP
with the strong coupling length
\sim(4\pi)^{2}f_{\pi_{d}}
, like
the QCD sigma meson decay to pion pairs.
The
\eta_{d}\to{\cal S}_{a}+a
decay rate can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\Gamma[\eta_{d}\to S_{a}+a]\sim\frac{m_{\eta_{d}}^{3}}{16\pi f_{\pi_{d}}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{m_{{\cal S}_{a}}^{2}}{m_{\eta_{d}}^{2}}\right)\,,

(38)

where we have roughly replaced
\Lambda_{d}
with the
\eta_{d}
mass
m_{\eta_{d}}
and taken the
a
-ALP mass to be zero.

The
\eta_{d}
-ALP can also couple to diphoton, which
is generated via mixing with
\eta^{\prime}
in the potential, Eq.(
8
):

\displaystyle g_{\eta_{d}\gamma\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\sqrt{3}|\chi_{\rm{top}}^{\rm QCD}|}{4f_{\pi}f_{\pi_{d}}}\cdot\frac{1}{m_{\eta_{d}}^{2}-m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}}\cdot g_{\eta^{\prime}\gamma\gamma}

\displaystyle\simeq{7.2\times 10^{-5}\,\rm{GeV}^{-1}}\times\left(\frac{200\,\rm{MeV}}{f_{\pi_{d}}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1\,\rm{GeV}^{2}}{m_{\eta_{d}}^{2}-m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)\,.

(39)

Here the mixing angle
\theta_{\eta^{\prime}\mathchar 45\eta_{d}}
between
\eta_{d}
and
\eta^{\prime}
reads off from Eq.(
39
) to be
\sim 10^{-2}
at
f_{\pi_{d}}=100
MeV and
m_{\eta_{d}}=1
GeV, which is to be smaller as
f_{\pi_{d}}
or
m_{\eta_{d}}
gets larger. In comparison, the current accuracy of measuring the
\eta^{\prime}
coupling is about 4.5% (read off from the accuracy of the full width)
[
79
]
, which
is greater than the size of the maximal possible deviation reached when
f_{\pi_{d}}=100
MeV and
m_{\eta_{d}}=1
GeV:
(1-\Gamma_{\eta^{\prime}}/\Gamma_{\eta^{\prime}}^{\exp})=1-|\cos\theta_{\eta^{\prime}\mathchar 45\eta_{d}}|^{2}\sim 10^{-3}
. Thus the present
\eta_{d}
-ALP for
f_{\pi_{d}}\gtrsim 100
MeV
currently survives not to spoil the
\eta^{\prime}
physics.

From Eq.(
39
)
the
\eta_{d}\to 2\gamma
decay rate is evaluated as

\displaystyle\Gamma[\eta_{d}\to 2\gamma]=\frac{g_{\eta_{d}\gamma\gamma}^{2}m_{\eta_{d}}^{3}}{64\pi}\,,

(40)

which is, when combined with Eq.(
38
),
read as the branching fraction,

\displaystyle{\rm Br}[\eta_{d}\to 2\gamma]\sim 10^{-11}-10^{-10}\,,

(41)

for
m_{\eta_{d}}\sim 1-2
GeV,
f_{\pi_{d}}\sim 200
MeV, and
m_{{\cal S}_{a}}<m_{\eta_{d}}
.
This branching fraction is too tiny to be sensitive to
the ongoing Belle II and BESIII experiments, where
e^{-}e^{+}\to 3\gamma
events have been
measured and the upper limit on the ALP-photon coupling assuming
{\rm Br}[{\rm ALP}\to 2\gamma]=1
.
Although
\eta_{d}
is thus unlikely to be seen via the direct diphoton channel,
it can have multi-photon decay channel via the main decay process, in Eq.(
38

\eta_{d}\to{\cal S}_{a}+a
with
S_{a}
being a missing energy (or decaying
\to 2\gamma)
and

would decay to
\gamma\gamma

-ALP,
but the coupling to diphoton would be highly suppressed because it arises at

S_{a}\to gg
via the QCD-charged dQCD quark loops,

via the ordinary QCD quark loops).
This signal may be contaminated with the typical
3\gamma
events at Belle II, BESIII,
and EIC experiments. Thus, at any rate, the
\eta_{d}
-ALP can have the sensitivity to
be probed at those collider experiments.

At the Belle II,

comes from
496\,{\rm pb}^{-1}
data.
This experiment has placed the upper bound on the photon coupling,

around the mass

[

The Belle II future prospects with

and

[

via the

The existing limits around the target mass range are available from the literature
[

Recently the BESIII experiments
have reported the updated bounds on the ALP photon coupling
based on the

[

Summary and discussions

In summary,
by addressing the strong CP problem, we have discussed an extension of a phenomenologically and cosmologically viable QCD
\times
dQCD scenario in a view of the QCD-scale baryogenesis
[

13

A couple of predicted (partially) composite ALPs successfully settle the QCD and dQCD vacua to be free from CP phases by the hybrid relaxation.
We have found that this extension improves
the predictability of the scenario of this class.
The QCD-scale composite axion (

\sim 140

Recombinant human thyrotropin reducing insulin resistance in patients with differentiated thyroid cancer
Authors: Yu-Hsuan Li, Junyi Wu, I Te Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractContextPostsurgical radioactive iodine (RAI) therapy is indicated for adults with differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) who are at an intermediate-to-high risk of recurrence. Thyrotropin (TSH) stimulation to facilitate RAI uptake can be achieved by injection of recombinant human TSH (rhTSH) or thyroid hormone withdrawal (THW).ObjectiveThis study aimed to compare the effects of rhTSH administration and THW programs on insulin resistance.MethodsThis prospective observational study included 47 patients who received rhTSH injections and 29 patients who underwent THW. All participants underwent a baseline assessment 4 weeks before RAI administration and a follow-up assessment on the day of RAI administration. Blood samples were collected for laboratory analysis, and insulin resistance was assessed using the homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR).ResultsHomeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance significantly decreased after rhTSH administration (from 1.5 [0.8, 1.7] to 1.1 [0.7, 1.4], P = .004) and after THW (from 1.4 [0.9, 1.7] to 0.7 [0.5, 1.0], P < .001). These changes in HOMA-IR were not significantly different between the rhTSH and THW groups (−0.3 [−0.8, 0.2] vs −0.5 [−1.1, −0.2], P = .120). Logistic regression analysis identified a greater reduction in triglyceride levels (a decrease of ≥0.028 mmol/L) as an independent factor for HOMA-IR reduction (the decrease ≥0.4) in the rhTSH group (odds ratio = 6.064, 95% confidence interval: 1.428-25.757, P = .015).ConclusionThe rhTSH administration program attenuates insulin resistance in patients with DTC. The magnitude of HOMA-IR reduction in the rhTSH administration program is not significantly different from that in the THW program.

Thyroid cancer is a highly prevalent endocrine malignancy. In 2020, the global 5-year prevalence of thyroid cancer was 25.5 per 100 000 individuals and the 5-year prevalence in Asia was 24.6 per 100 000 individuals (1). Although the prevalence of thyroid cancer has been increasing by 55%, the mortality rate decreased by 7% from 1990 to 2021 (2). A postsurgical radioactive iodine (RAI) therapy has been recommended in adults with an intermediate to high risk of recurrence for differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) (3, 4). In addition to a restriction in iodine consumption less than 50 μg/day for 2 weeks (5), thyrotropin (TSH) stimulation should be achieved to facilitate a successful RAI absorption (3).

To achieve the goal of TSH > 30 μU/mL, recombinant human TSH (rhTSH) injections or thyroid hormone withdrawal (THW) should be performed before RAI therapy (3). The successful ablation and recurrence rates of thyroid cancer were not significantly different between the rhTSH and THW groups (6-8). However, the quality of life was significantly better in the rhTSH group than in the THW group due to an overt hypothyroidism in the latter group (6, 7).

THW decreases fasting glucose levels and insulin resistance (9). This improvement in insulin sensitivity may be associated with the clinical hypothyroid manifestations assessed by Zulewski score (10). Although rhTSH administration can prevent overt hypothyroid symptoms, which will be induced by THW, the effect of rhTSH administration on insulin resistance remains unclear. Therefore, we aimed to compare the insulin resistance between the rhTSH administration and THW programs.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This prospective observational study was conducted at Taichung Veterans General Hospital. We consecutively enrolled outpatients with DTC for whom high-dose RAI therapy has been arranged between June 2018 and April 2021. After reviewing medical history and anthropometric measurements, blood samples were collected in the morning after an overnight fast.

After baseline assessment, the patients received either THW for 4 weeks or daily rhTSH injection (Thyrogen; Sanofi Genzyme Corp., Massachusetts, United States) for 2 days before RAI administration. The selection of THW or rhTSH depended on the clinical decisions discussed between the patients and in-charge physicians. All patients received a low-iodine diet for 2 weeks before RAI therapy.

Four weeks after the baseline assessment, the patients underwent repeated assessments in the fasting state before RAI administration. This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Taichung Veterans General Hospital (IRB TCVGH No: CE18104A). Written informed consent was obtained from each patient before the study procedures were performed (Clinical trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT03624751).

Laboratory assessment

Plasma insulin was determined using a commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit (RRID: AB_3073852). Plasma glucose was determined using the glucose oxidase-peroxidase method (Wako Diagnostics, Tokyo, Japan). The homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) was calculated using the following formula: fasting glucose (mmol/L) × fasting insulin (mU/L)/22.5 (11). Glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) levels were determined using boronate-affinity high-performance liquid chromatography (National Glycohemoglobin Standardization Program certified, Primus Corp., Missouri, United States). Hypertension was defined as any of the following criteria: (1) a history of antihypertensive drug use, (2) systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg, (3) diastolic blood pressure ≥90 mmHg on the baseline assessment. Plasma C-reactive protein (CRP) levels were determined using an ELISA kit (RRID: AB_3752999). Serum lipid, uric acid, albumin, creatinine, and alanine transaminase levels were measured using commercial kits (Beckman Coulter, Fullerton, California, United States). The triglyceride to high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol (TG/HDL-C) ratio was calculated by dividing the serum triglyceride level (mg/dL) by the HDL cholesterol level (mg/dL). The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration equation (12).

Statistical analysis

All continuous data are presented as medians (interquartile ranges). Intergroup differences in continuous data were examined using the Mann–Whitney U test. The intragroup differences between continuous variables before and after intervention were examined using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Categorical data are presented as numbers (percentages). Differences in categorical variables were examined using either the Chi-square or Fisher exact test, as appropriate. The statistical significance in the linear correlation between continuous data was examined using Spearman rank correlation coefficient (σ). Logistic regression analysis was performed to estimate the odds ratio (OR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) of the factors associated with HOMA-IR reduction. The statistical power could reach 0.95 if a sample size was ≥24 based on the reported effect size of 0.77 for HOMA-IR reduction after THW (13). Statistical analysis was performed using the SPSS version 22.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, New York, United States). A P-value <.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Of the 86 patients who completed the baseline assessment, 10 were excluded because they were diagnosed with diabetes mellitus. In total, 47 patients received rhTSH injections without THW (rhTSH group), and 29 patients received THW without rhTSH injection (the THW group) before RAI administration (Table 1). The median durations between the baseline and follow-up assessments were 29 and 30 days in the rhTSH and THW groups, respectively (P = .416). At baseline, the median TSH levels were significantly lower in the rhTSH group than in the THW group (0.831 vs 4.350 μU/mL, P = .008), the median free T4 and T3 levels were significantly higher in the rhTSH group than in the THW group (free T4: 13.4 vs 9.3 pg/mL, P = .002; T3 = 77.3 vs 62.4 ng/dL, P < .001; respectively). In addition, systolic blood pressure was significantly lower (121 vs 131 mmHg, P = .033) and HDL cholesterol levels were significantly lower (1.4 vs 1.7 mmol/L, P = .004) in the rhTSH group than in the THW group. There were no significant differences in the other assessed variables between groups.

Comparing the changes after intervention between these 2 groups (Table 2), the increases in thyroid profile were significantly greater in the rhTSH group than in the THW group, including TSH (114.7 [82.5, 147.6] vs 59.1 [35.2, 80.7] μU/mL, P < .001), free T4 (1.1 [−1.2, 4.6] vs −6.1 [−10.8, −1.8] pg/mL, P < .001), and T3 (0.4 [−9.0, 13.0] vs −15.1 [−30.1, 0.0] ng/dL, P < .001). The decreases in the cholesterol profile were significantly greater in the rhTSH group than the THW group, including total cholesterol (−0.5 [−0.8, −0.1] vs 0.7 [0.0, 1.2] mmol/L, P < .001), LDL cholesterol (−0.4 [−0.9, −0.1] vs 0.4 [−0.1, 0.9] mmol/L, P < .001), and HDL cholesterol (−0.1 [−0.2, 0.1] vs 0.1 [0.0, 0.3] mmol/L, P < .001). The decrease in BMI was significantly greater in the rhTSH group than the THW group: (−0.5 [−0.7, −0.3] vs −0.1 [−0.3, 0.4] kg/m2, P < .001). However, the decreases in heart rate (−5 [−14, −3] vs −1 [−7, 5] beats/min, P = .003) and eGFR (−24 [−31, 9] vs −4 [−16, 2] mL/min/1.73 m2, P < .001) were significantly greater in the THW group than in the rhTSH group.

As changes in HOMA-IR were not significantly related to the different interventions, ie, rhTSH administration and THW, we performed a linear correlation analysis to examine the association between changes in HOMA-IR and other assessed variables in all enrolled patients (Table 3). In addition to fasting glucose (σ = 0.496, P < .001) and insulin (σ = 0.981, P < .001), which are the variables in the HOMA-IR formula, changes in TSH (σ = 0.286, P = .012), triglyceride levels (σ = 0.277, P = .016), and TG/HDL-C ratio (σ = 0.326, P = .004) were significantly associated with changes in HOMA-IR. The median of HOMA-IR reduction was 0.4 after the intervention among all enrolled patients. Furthermore, BMI, an important factor associated with hypothyroidism and insulin resistance (14), may be a confounding factor. Therefore, the change in BMI (decrease in BMI ≥ median, ie, 0.353 kg/m2) was included as a variable in the multivariable regression analysis. According to the logistic regression analysis to examine the variables associated with HOMA-IR reduction, Table 4 shows that greater reduction in triglyceride (the decrease ≥ median, ie, 0.028 mmol/L) was an independent factor for greater HOMA-IR reduction (the decrease ≥0.4) in patients after the rhTSH administration program (OR = 6.064, 95% CI: 1.428-25.757, P = .015) after adjusting for age and sex. However, an increase in TSH or a decrease in BMI was not an independent factor for HOMA-IR reduction (P = .193 and .620, respectively).

Discussion

The main finding of the present study was that insulin resistance assessed using HOMA-IR was reduced after the rhTSH administration program in patients with DTC before RAI treatment. The decreased magnitudes of HOMA IR were not significantly different between the rhTSH and THW groups. Differentiated thyroid cancer is associated with high insulin resistance (15). The most prevalent cause of noncancer mortality in patients with DTC is cardiovascular disease (16). It has been well documented that THW is associated with a reduction in fasting glucose and insulin resistance. In the present study, we reported a novel finding that similar effects on insulin resistance were observed between rhTSH administration and THW.

TSH increases insulin resistance, and the blood TSH levels were positively correlated with insulin resistance in Chinese adults who have undergone health screening services (17). In vitro administration of TSH to the adipocytes can induce endoplasmic reticulum stress and inhibit glucose transporter 4 expression (18). Similarly, endoplasmic reticulum stress was induced by TSH administration in macrophages to increase insulin resistance (19). On the contrary, Moon et al (20) reported that in vitro administration of TSH to mouse myoblast cells increased the expression of insulin receptor substrate 1 (IRS1) and enhanced glucose transport. Although endogenous TSH induced by anti-thyroid drugs can increase insulin resistance in mice with subclinical hypothyroidism (18, 19), Moon et al (20) reported that direct injection of exogenous TSH for 2 weeks could reduce blood glucose levels during an intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test and improve insulin sensitivity of skeletal muscle in mice with diet-induced obesity. Therefore, exogenous TSH administration may have a different effect on insulin sensitivity from endogenous TSH induced by hypothyroidism, and its effects also different between adipocytes and skeletal muscles. In the present study, rhTSH administration decreased the plasma glucose and insulin levels in the absence of hypothyroidism.

In line with our findings, An et al (13) reported that increased serum cholesterol levels were observed during THW and cardiometabolic parameters recovered after reinstitution with levothyroxine. Chang et al (21) also reported that endothelial function, as assessed by brachial artery diameter, did not significantly change when levothyroxine was reinstituted after THW. However, a decreased perfusion of the myocardial tissue was still observed after reinstitution of levothyroxine in patients who had undergone repeated THW (22). Thyroid function regulates cholesterol metabolism, including synthesis and degradation. Thyroid hormones enhance hepatic cholesterol synthesis by inducing 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl coenzyme A reductase (23, 24). However, thyroid hormones decrease serum cholesterol levels by decreasing intestinal absorption and increasing circulating clearance by promoting the expression of LDL cholesterol receptors in the liver (24, 25). Hypothyroidism leads to increased serum total and LDL cholesterol levels (26). Furthermore, serum HDL cholesterol levels may increase due to reduced circulating cholesteryl ester transfer protein during hypothyroidism (27). Different to THW, rhTSH administration decreased cholesterol levels in the present study. We observed that a significant increase in free T4 levels in after rhTSH administration. The long-term effects of THW and rhTSH administration on cardiovascular risk require further investigation.

Although the serum triglyceride levels were not significantly changed after either the rhTSH administration or THW programs, the changes in HOMA-IR were positively correlated with the changes in TSH and triglyceride levels in the present study. Hypothyroidism is associated with liver steatosis, which increases insulin resistance (28). Bayyigit et al (29) reported that steatosis in the liver and pancreas was correlated with TSH level, HOMA-IR, and triglyceride level. In addition to decreased thyroid hormone levels, increased TSH also plays a role in liver steatosis (30). In the present study, we observed that a decrease in the serum triglyceride level was an independent factor for reduction in HOMA-IR in the rhTSH group. Besides, it was reported that TSH-stimulated hepatocyte-derived exosomes enhanced liver triglyceride accumulation (31). The TG/HDL-C ratio is an important factor associated with hypothyroidism and insulin resistance (32). In this study, the TG/HDL-C ratio was not an independent risk factor for HOMA-IR reduction in the multivariable logistic regression analysis (data are not shown).

In the present study, the decline in renal function, as assessed by eGFR, was significantly greater in the THW group than in the rhTSH group. In line with our findings, Niri et al reported that eGFR and the plasma renin activity significantly decreased after THW (33). In the animal model of hypothyroidism induced by antithyroid drugs, the size and weight of kidney were significantly lower, and the mesenteric vessel branching was also significantly decreased in hypothyroid rats compared with euthyroid control (34). The renal function might be preserved by rhTSH administration compared with THW (35).

Notably, blood pressure and CRP levels significantly decreased in the rhTSH and THW groups. In line with our results, An et al (13) reported that heart rate, blood pressure, and CRP levels significantly decreased after THW. Thyrotropin can induce nitric oxide secretion and inhibit endothelin production by in vitro cultured human aortic endothelial cells (36). In a human study, circulating nitric oxide might rapidly increase within 3 days after rhTSH injection (37). As the blood pressure was measured on the next day of the second-dose rhTSH injection, a low blood pressure might result from vasodilation. However, the long-term effect of rhTSH administration is unknown because circulating nitric oxide significantly decreased by 9 days after rhTSH injection (37).

All patients underwent a 2-week diet control with low iodine for RAI treatment preparation. A low urinary iodine concentration (<100 µg/L) was reported to be associated with high fasting glucose and insulin resistance based on the data from National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (38), and to be associated with hyperglycemia and hypertension in a Chinese population (39). In addition, a low urinary iodine concentration is associated with high glucose levels and high blood pressure according to the data from the Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (40). Therefore, the intragroup changes might be not only caused by medication but also lifestyle change during the 4-week program. Similarly, HbA1c significantly decreased in the rhTSH group. Although HbA1c can decrease rapidly within hours of glucose reduction (41, 42), the significant improvement in HbA1c in this study may be attributable not only to 2-day rhTSH administration but also to lifestyle changes during the program, as no significant difference in HbA1c reduction was observed between the 2 groups.

The strength of the present study is that it compared the changes in insulin resistance between the rhTSH administration and THW programs. However, this study has some limitations. First, this study aimed to compare 2 clinical programs before RAI treatment. Patients in the THW group had undergone progressive hypothyroidism for 4 weeks, whereas patients in the rhTSH group had relatively stable thyroid function in the first 26 days, followed by a rapid increase in TSH levels for 2 days. Therefore, we aimed to compare the effects between patients who had undergone different programs, rather than the direct effects of THW and rhTSH administration. Second, this was an open-label observational study, and the baseline characteristics were not strictly matched. At baseline, the THW group had higher TSH and lower free T4 and T3 levels than the rhTSH group. In addition to the differences in thyroid function, the THW group had higher systolic blood pressure and HDL cholesterol levels than the rhTSH group. These differences in baseline metabolic profiles might have effects on insulin resistance. Therefore, further randomized studies are needed to eliminate potential biases. Third, the mechanisms underlying the improvement in insulin resistance were not investigated. Moon et al (20) reported that intraperitoneal injection of TSH reduced glucose levels during the glucose tolerance test, and this reduction in insulin resistance might be due to the increased expression of IRS1 in skeletal muscle based on an animal study. Furthermore, TSH enhances the IRS1 expression by directly activating the cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP)/protein kinase A/cAMP response element-binding protein pathway based on the in vitro study (20). Fourth, insulin resistance was not assessed using the glucose clamp technique in this study (43). Iacobellis et al (44) reported that serum TSH levels were inversely correlated with whole-body glucose utilization, assessed by hyperinsulinemic euglycemic clamp, the gold-standard method for calculating insulin sensitivity. In this study, insulin resistance was assessed using HOMA-IR based on the fasting glucose and insulin levels which were decreased after interventions. However, Brenta et al (45) reported that THW increased insulin resistance based on decreased glucose disposal assessed using the short IV insulin tolerance test in women who underwent thyroidectomy for DTC. Furthermore, the TSH receptor knockout mice showed decreased hepatic gluconeogenesis and increased muscular glucose transporter (46, 47). Finally, all participants were diagnosed with DTC and underwent total thyroidectomy. Therefore, the results of this study cannot be generalized to other populations.

In conclusion, the program of daily injection of rhTSH for 2 days before RAI administration attenuates insulin resistance, as estimated using HOMA-IR in patients who have undergone total thyroidectomy for DTC. The reduced HOMA-IR value in the rhTSH administration program is not significantly different from that in the THW program and is associated with changes in serum triglyceride levels. However, whole-body glucose utilization or peripheral insulin sensitivity was not assessed. Further randomized studies are needed to investigate the long-term effects of these 2 intervention programs.

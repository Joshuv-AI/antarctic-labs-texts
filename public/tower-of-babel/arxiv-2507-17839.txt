Do Riemannian Submersions Preserve Positive Intermediate Ricci Curvature? Thanks: The authors gratefully acknowledge the support of the NSF, via Award DMS 2203686.

Abstract
. Pro and the third author showed that there are Riemannian submersions π : M → B \pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\to B with M M a compact manifold with positive Ricci curvature, whose base B B , has Ricci curvatures with both signs. Thus, Riemannian submersions need not preserve positive Ricci curvature. In this note we establish the degree to which this result extends into the setting of positive intermediate Ricci curvature. It is an immediate consequence of the Gray–O’Neill Horizontal curvature equation that if π : M → B \pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\to B is a Riemannian submersion whose base is b b -dimensional and Ric k ​ ( M ) > 0 \mathrm{Ric}_{k}(M)>0 for any k ∈ { 1 , 2 , ⋯ , b − 1 } k\in\{1,2,\cdots,b-1\} , then Ric k ​ ( B ) \mathrm{Ric}_{k}(B) is also positive. Here we show that this observation is optimal in the following strong sense: For k ≥ dim ⁡ ( B ) k\geq\mathrm{dim}(B) , let π : ( M , g M ) → ( B , g B ) \pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(M,g_{M})\to(B,g_{B}) be a Riemannian submersion from a complete Riemannian manifold with Ric k ​ ( M ) > 0 \mathrm{Ric}_{k}(M)>0 . We show how to perturb g M g_{M} in the C 1 C^{1} -topology to produce a Riemannian submersion π : ( M , g ~ M ) → ( B , g ~ B ) \pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(M,\tilde{g}_{M})\to(B,\tilde{g}_{B}) whose total space has Ric k > 0 \mathrm{Ric}_{k}>0 , but whose base has Ricci curvature of both signs. In particular, this shows that Riemannian submersions that do not preserve positive Ricci curvature are dense in the C 1 C^{1} -topology among the complete metrics on M M with Ric > 0 \mathrm{Ric}>0 for which a given submersion π : M → B \pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\to B is Riemannian.



1.
Introduction

It follows from the Gray–O’Neill Horizontal Curvature Equation that Riemannian submersions preserve lower bounds on sectional curvature (
[
3
,
5
]
). In particular, they preserve positive sectional curvature.
On the other hand, Pro and the third author showed that they need not preserve positive Ricci curvature. More specifically they showed

Theorem

(Pro–Wilhelm,
[
7
]
)
.

For any
C>0
, there is a Riemannian submersion
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\to B
for which
M
is compact with positive Ricci curvature and
B
has some Ricci curvatures less than
-C
.

As intermediate Ricci curvatures interpolate between sectional and Ricci curvatures, it is natural to ask about the extent to which the Gray–O’Neill versus the Pro–Wilhelm results extend to the setting of intermediate Ricci curvature. Before we answer this question we recall the definition of positive intermediate Ricci curvature.

Definition
.

We say that
(M,g)
has positive
k^{\text{th}}
-intermediate Ricci curvature if for all orthonormal sets
(u,v_{1},\cdots,v_{k})
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{sec}^{M}(u,v_{i})>0.

For brevity, we will write

\operatorname{Ric}_{k}^{M}>0
.

If
\mathcal{M}_{k}
denotes the set of isometry classes of Riemannian manifolds with positive
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}
, then

\cal M_{1}
is the class with positive sectional curvature,

\cal M_{n-1}
is the class with positive Ricci curvature, and

\cal M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\cal M_{k}\subset\cal M_{k+1}\subset\cdots\subset\cal M_{n-1}
.

Thus
the sequence of hypotheses
\{\operatorname{Ric}_{k}^{M}>0\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
is decreasing in strength and interpolates between the two classical hypotheses of positive sectional and positive Ricci curvatures.

Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{n}\to B^{b}
be a Riemannian submersion from a compact
n
-manifold to a compact
b
-manifold. We will show that the Gray–O’Neill result extends to
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}>0
provided
k\leq b-1
, and the Pro–Wilhelm result extends to
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}>0
provided
k\geq b
. More precisely

Theorem A
.

Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{n}\to B^{b}
be a Riemannian submersion from a compact
n
–manifold to a compact
b
–manifold with
b\geq 2

\operatorname{Ric}_{k}^{M}>0
for some

1.

If

, then
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}^{B}>0
.

2.

If

, then

need not have positive Ricci curvature.

Part 1 of Theorem (

-small change to a fixed metric on the metric’s covariant derivative and the gradient and Hessian of a fixed real valued function.

.

\varepsilon>0

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(M^{n},\hat{g}_{M})\to(B^{b},\hat{g}_{B})
be any Riemannian submersion, let
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\to(0,\infty)
be
C^{\infty}
, and let
\tilde{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varphi\circ\pi
be its pullback to

. Set

\tilde{g}_{M}=\pi^{*}(\hat{g}_{B})|_{\cal H\oplus\cal H}+e^{2\tilde{\varphi}}\hat{g}_{M}|_{\cal V\oplus\cal V}.

Let
X,Y,Z\in\cal H
and
T,T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\in\cal V
, and let
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
denote the inner product associated with the metric
\hat{g}_{M}
. The components of the curvature tensor associated with
\tilde{g}_{M}
are as follows:

(7.4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{R}^{\mathbf{v}}_{M}(X,Y)Z=\;&\hat{R}^{\mathbf{v}}_{M}(X,Y)Z+\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},X\right\rangle\hat{A}_{Y}Z\\
&-\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},Y\right\rangle\hat{A}_{X}Z-\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},Z\right\rangle\hat{A}_{X}Y,\end{split}

(7.4.2)

\tilde{R}^{\mathbf{h}}_{M}(X,Y)Z=e^{2\tilde{\varphi}}\hat{R}^{\mathbf{h}}_{M}(X,Y)Z+(1-e^{2\tilde{\varphi}})\hat{R}_{B}(X,Y)Z,

(7.4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{R}^{\mathbf{v}}_{M}(X,T)Y=\;&\hat{R}^{\mathbf{v}}_{M}(X,T)Y+(1-e^{2\tilde{\varphi}})\hat{A}_{X}\hat{A}^{*}_{Y}T\\
&+\left(\widehat{\operatorname{Hess}}^{M}_{\tilde{\varphi}}(X,Y)+\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},X\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},Y\right\rangle\right)T\\
&-\left(\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},X\right\rangle\hat{S}_{Y}T+\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},Y\right\rangle\hat{S}_{X}T\right),\end{split}

(7.4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{-2\tilde{\varphi}}\tilde{R}^{\mathbf{h}}_{M}(X,T)Y=\;&\hat{R}^{\mathbf{h}}_{M}(X,T)Y-\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},Y\right\rangle\hat{A}^{*}_{X}T\\
&-2\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},X\right\rangle\hat{A}^{*}_{Y}T+\left\langle\hat{A}_{X}Y,T\right\rangle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},\end{split}

(7.4.5)

e^{-2\tilde{\varphi}}\tilde{R}^{\mathbf{h}}_{M}(T_{1},T_{2})X=\hat{R}^{\mathbf{h}}_{M}(T_{1},T_{2})X+(1-e^{2\tilde{\varphi}})(\hat{A}^{*}_{\hat{A}^{*}_{X}T_{1}}T_{2}-\hat{A}^{*}_{\hat{A}^{*}_{X}T_{2}}T_{1}),

(7.4.6)

\tilde{R}^{\mathbf{h}}_{M}(T_{1},T_{2})T_{3}=\hat{R}^{\mathbf{h}}_{M}(T_{1},T_{2})T_{3},\;\;\text{and}

(7.4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{R}^{\mathbf{v}}_{M}(T_{1},T_{2})T_{3}=\;&\hat{R}^{\mathbf{v}}_{M}(T_{1},T_{2})T_{3}+(1-e^{2\tilde{\varphi}})\left\{\hat{S}_{\hat{\sigma}(T_{2},T_{3})}T_{1}-\hat{S}_{\hat{\sigma}(T_{1},T_{3})}T_{2}\right\}\\
&+e^{2\tilde{\varphi}}\left\{\left(\hat{S}_{\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi}}-|\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi}|^{2}I\right)(\left\langle T_{2},T_{3}\right\rangle T_{1}-\left\langle T_{1},T_{3}\right\rangle T_{2})\right.\\
&+\left.\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},\hat{\sigma}(T_{2},T_{3})\right\rangle T_{1}-\left\langle\hat{\nabla}\tilde{\varphi},\hat{\sigma}(T_{1},T_{3})\right\rangle T_{2}\right\},\end{split}

where
I
denotes the identity map, and, as in
[
4
]
, in (
7.4.2
), we are conflating
\hat{R}_{B}(X,Y)Z
with its horizontal lift.

As mentioned above, the functions
\tilde{\omega}_{h}
and

-small. This together with Propositions (

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
M
be a compact smooth manifold, and let

\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(M,g_{M})\to(B^{b},g_{B})

\varepsilon>0

Let
\tilde{g}_{M}
be as in ((
4.0.4
)).
Let
\cal R_{M}

If

(7.5.1)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\omega}_{h}\right\rVert}_{C^{1}}<\delta,\ \mathinner{\!\left\lVert\tilde{\omega}_{v}\right\rVert}_{C^{1}}<\delta,

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Hess}^{M}_{{\omega}_{h}}(\cdot,\cdot)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\lambda_{h,h}+1)g_{B}(\cdot,\cdot),\;\;\text{and}

(7.5.2)

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Hess}^{M}_{\tilde{\omega}_{v}}(\cdot,\cdot)|_{\cal H\oplus\cal H}

g_{M}
-orthonormal vectors

-orthonormal vectors

(7.5.3)

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathfrak{D}\cal R_{M}(U\wedge V)\right\rVert}

\displaystyle\varepsilon,

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\varepsilon,

(7.5.5)

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathfrak{D}\cal R_{M}(Y\wedge V)^{(\cal H\wedge\cal H)\oplus(\cal V\wedge\cal V)}\right\rVert}

\displaystyle\varepsilon,

\displaystyle g(\mathfrak{D}\cal{R}_{M}\left(Y\wedge V\right),Y\wedge V)

Since

for

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{\mathbf{v}}_{M}(V,Y)Y

\displaystyle\|\tilde{R}^{\mathbf{v}}_{M}(V,Y)Y-\left(R^{\mathbf{v}}_{M}(V,Y)Y-{\operatorname{Hess}}^{M}_{\tilde{\omega}_{v}}(Y,Y)V\right)\|<\varepsilon,

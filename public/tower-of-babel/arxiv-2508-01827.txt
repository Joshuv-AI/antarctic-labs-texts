Generalized Vaidya Spacetime in Cotton and Conformal Killing Theories
Metin Gürses, Yaghoub Heydarzade, Çetin Şentürk

Abstract
We demonstrate that the non-vacuum field equations of Cotton gravity and Conformal Killing gravity admit a generalized class of Vaidya-type solutions. In particular, beyond the standard induced term associated with the matter source, the generalized metric incorporates two additional correction terms of purely geometric origin, arising from the unique structure of these theories. This extended solution generalizes the classical Vaidya spacetime in General Relativity and offers new insights into the dynamics of radiating spacetimes within the framework of these third-rank gravity theories.

Introduction

While General Relativity (GR) remains remarkably successful on solar system scales, it faces persistent challenges in accounting for galactic rotation curves, large-scale cosmic structure, and the observed late-time acceleration of the Universe. These issues have motivated the development of various modified gravity theories. Among these, Cotton Gravity (CG), proposed by Harada in 2021
[

In this paper, we investigate Vaidya-type spacetimes within the frameworks of Cotton Gravity (CG) and Conformal Killing Gravity (CKG). The structure of the paper is as follows: In Section 2, we briefly introduce the generalized Vaidya metric and its key features. 2
Generalized Vaidya Metric

We begin with considering the line element for a generalized Vaidya spacetime,
in Eddington-Finkelstein-like coordinates
x^{\mu}=(u,r,\theta,\phi)
, given
by

ds^{2}=-f(u,r)\,du^{2}+2dudr+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(1)

where the metric function is defined as
f(u,r)=1-\frac{2M(u,r)}{r}
, and

d\Omega^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}d\phi^{2}
represents the line element of the unit 2-sphere. This form generalizes the original Vaidya solution
[
27
]
by allowing the mass function

M(u,r)
to depend on both the null coordinate
u
and the radial coordinate
r
.

The metric (
1
) can be expressed in terms of the Kerr-Schild form
[
53
,
54
]

ds^{2}=ds_{0}^{2}+2Hdu^{2}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{with}\penalty\ \penalty\ ds_{0}^{2}=-du^{2}+2dudr+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(2)

where
ds_{0}^{2}
is the flat background metric in advanced Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates and
H\equiv[1-f(u,r)]/2=M(u,r)/r
. As is well known, the Kerr-Schild type of metrics has proven itself as an extremely useful tool in getting exact solutions to the Einstein’s field equations in GR. Indeed, many exact solutions in GR can be brought into the Kerr-Schild form, see e.g.
[
55
]
. It has also recently been exploited to obtain exact wave-like solutions in CG and CKG theories
[
14
]
and in more generic gravity theories
[
56
,
57
,
58
,
59
,
60
,
61
]
.

Although the Kerr-Schild form (
2
) of the generalized Vaidya metric (
1
) is nice and has very useful properties, it is technically more practical to use the original metric form (
1
) in our present analysis. So, we shall proceed with the metric given in (
1
) from this point on and express it in terms of
two null vectors
l_{\mu}
and
n_{\nu}
as

g_{\mu\nu}=-l_{\mu}n_{\nu}-l_{\nu}n_{\mu}+h_{\mu\nu},

(3)

where
h_{\mu\nu}=r^{2}\delta_{\mu}^{\theta}\delta_{\nu}^{\theta}+r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\delta_{\mu}^{\phi}\delta_{\nu}^{\phi}
is the metric of the two-sphere of radius
r
and the null vectors are defined
by

\displaystyle l_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{0}_{\mu},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ l^{\mu}\penalty\ =\delta^{\mu}_{1},

\displaystyle n_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f}{2}\delta^{0}_{\mu}-\delta^{1}_{\mu},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ n^{\mu}=-\delta^{\mu}_{0}-\frac{f}{2}\delta^{\mu}_{1}.

(4)

These vectors satisfy the relations

l_{\mu}n^{\mu}=-1,\qquad l_{\mu}l^{\mu}=0,\qquad n_{\mu}n^{\mu}=0,\qquad l^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad n^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}=0,

(5)

confirming that both
l^{\mu}
and
n^{\mu}
are null and mutually normalized.
Moreover, the covariant derivatives of these vectors take the following forms

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}l_{\nu}=-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2}l_{\mu}l_{\nu}+\frac{1}{r}h_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu}=\frac{f\,f^{\prime}}{4}l_{\mu}l_{\nu}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2}\delta^{1}_{\nu}l_{\mu}-\frac{f}{2r}h_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle l^{\mu}\,\nabla_{\mu}\,l_{\nu}=0,

(6)

where a prime denotes partial differentiation with respect to
r
.

3
Vaidya-Type Solutions in Cotton Gravity

In this section, we explore the existence and structure of generalized Vaidya solutions in the framework of CG. The field equations of CG, in the Codazzi formulation, are given by

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa T_{\mu\nu}+H_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\,\tilde{H}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\,\tilde{H}_{\alpha\nu},

(7)

where
\tilde{H}_{\mu\nu}=H_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{3}Hg_{\mu\nu}
with
H=g^{\mu\nu}H_{\mu\nu}
, and
T_{\mu\nu}
represents the energy-momentum tensor of the matter fields. The tensor
H_{\mu\nu}
encapsulates the geometric modifications to the Einstein field equations induced by CG and includes contributions from third-order derivatives of the metric in its original formulation.

In order to analyze the existence of Vaidya-type solutions in CG, we consider the following distinct cases:

•

Case I:
M=M(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ T_{\mu\nu}=0
,

•

Case II:
M=M(u,r),\penalty\ \penalty\ T_{\mu\nu}=0
,

•

Case III:
M=M(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ T_{\mu\nu}(u)\neq 0
,

•

Case IV:
M=M(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ T_{\mu\nu}(u,r)\neq 0
,

•

Case V:
M=M(u,r),\penalty\ \penalty\ T_{\mu\nu}(u,r)\neq 0
.

Each case corresponds to a physically distinct scenario, ranging from general radiating systems with matter sources to purely vacuum configurations. The case V represents the most general non-static setup, while the remaining cases are its specific subclasses with progressively more restrictive assumptions.
In our analysis, the energy-momentum tensor is taken to be a combination of null and timelike matter components. This allows us to model a wide range of non-vacuum configurations, incorporating both radiative and non-radiative sources. We begin by deriving the most general solution corresponding to the Case V. Subsequently, we examine the special cases listed above, identifying the precise conditions under which a generalized Vaidya metric solves the CG field equations.

In the most general case (Case V) of a Vaidya-type configuration in CG with a non-vanishing energy-momentum tensor, following
[
42
,
49
]
, we decompose the total energy-momentum tensor as

T_{\mu\nu}=T^{(n)}_{\mu\nu}+T^{(m)}_{\mu\nu},

(8)

where

\displaystyle T^{(n)}_{\mu\nu}=\mu l_{\mu}l_{\nu},

(9)

\displaystyle T^{(m)}_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)(l_{\mu}n_{\nu}+l_{\nu}n_{\mu})+pg_{\mu\nu}.

(10)

Here,
T^{(n)}_{\mu\nu}
represents the energy-momentum tensor of a null fluid with energy density
\mu(u,r)
propagating along null hypersurfaces
u=\text{const.}
, while
T^{(m)}_{\mu\nu}
corresponds to a timelike fluid with energy density
\rho(u,r)
and pressure
p(u,r)
. The total energy-momentum tensor (
8
) can be written in the matrix form as

T_{\mu\nu}(u,r)\text{ = }\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\left(1-\frac{2M(u,r)}{r}\right)\rho(u,r)+\mu(u,r)&-\rho(u,r)&0&0\\
-\rho(u,r)&0&0&0\\
0&0&r^{2}p(u,r)&0\\
0&0&0&r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta p(u,r)\\
\end{array}\right].

(11)

Using this expression, the non-vanishing components of the modified geometric tensor
\tilde{H}_{\mu\nu}=H_{\mu\nu}-\dfrac{1}{3}Hg_{\mu\nu}
are calculated to be

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{00}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3r^{3}}\left\{2(r-2M)(M^{\prime}-rM^{\prime\prime})+6r\dot{M}-\kappa r^{2}[(r-2M)(2p+\rho)+3r\mu]\right\},

(12)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{01}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3r^{2}}\left[2(M^{\prime}-rM^{\prime\prime})-\kappa r^{2}(2p+\rho)\right],

(13)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\left[4M^{\prime}-rM^{\prime\prime}-\kappa r^{2}(p+2\rho)\right],

(14)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{33}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta\,\tilde{H}_{22}.

(15)

Substituting these into the CG field equations (
3
) leads to the following system of differential equations:

\displaystyle F_{001}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3r^{4}}\biggl\{2r(6\dot{M}-4r\dot{M}^{\prime}+r^{2}\dot{M}^{\prime\prime})+2(r-2M)(2M^{\prime}-2rM^{\prime\prime}+r^{2}M^{\prime\prime\prime})

(16)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\kappa r^{3}\left[(r-2M)(2p^{\prime}+\rho^{\prime})+r(2\dot{p}+\dot{\rho})+3r\mu^{\prime}\right]\biggr\}=0,

\displaystyle F_{011}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3r^{3}}\left[2(2M^{\prime}-2rM^{\prime\prime}+r^{2}M^{\prime\prime\prime})+\kappa r^{3}(2p^{\prime}+\rho^{\prime})\right]=0,

(17)

\displaystyle F_{022}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3r}\left\{6\dot{M}-4r\dot{M}^{\prime}+r^{2}\dot{M}^{\prime\prime}+\kappa r^{2}[r(\dot{p}+2\dot{\rho})-3\mu]\right\}=0,

(18)

\displaystyle F_{122}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3r}\left\{2M^{\prime}-2rM^{\prime\prime}+r^{2}M^{\prime\prime\prime}+\kappa r^{2}[3(p+\rho)+r(p^{\prime}+2\rho^{\prime})]\right\}=0,

(19)

\displaystyle F_{033}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta F_{022}=0,

(20)

\displaystyle F_{133}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta F_{122}=0,

(21)

where we have made use of the definition

F_{\alpha\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\alpha}\,\tilde{H}_{\mu\beta}-\nabla_{\mu}\,\tilde{H}_{\beta\alpha}.

(22)

Multiplying (
17
) by
r^{2}/2
and subtracting the resulting equation from (
19
), we obtain

r\rho^{\prime}+2(p+\rho)=0.

(23)

Assuming an equation of state of the form
p=w\rho
, where
w
is a constant, Eq. (
23
) can be integrated to give

\rho(u,r)=\rho_{0}(u)r^{-2(1+w)}.

(24)

Substituting this result, along with
p=w\rho
, back into Eq. (
17
) and solving for
M(u,r)
, we obtain

M(u,r)=C_{0}(u)+\frac{1}{2}C_{1}(u)r^{2}+\frac{1}{3}C_{2}(u)r^{3}+\frac{\kappa\rho_{0}(u)}{2(1-2w)}r^{1-2w}.

(25)

With this mass function, Eq. (
18
) gives the null energy density as

\mu(u,r)=\frac{\dot{C_{0}}(u)}{\kappa r^{2}}+\frac{\dot{\rho_{0}}(u)}{(1-2w)}r^{-(1+2w)}.

(26)

All the other equations are satisfied, and thus, the functions given in Eqs. (
24
)-(
26
) define a complete family of exact solutions to the CG field equations. These include both null and timelike matter fields and exhibit generalized Vaidya-like behavior.

We have the following remarks.

Remark 1:
For
w=-1/2
and
w=-1
, the term with
r^{1-2w}
in Eq. (
25
) becomes proportional to
r^{2}
and
r^{3}
, respectively. In these special cases, the corresponding contribution can be absorbed into redefinitions of
C_{1}(u)
and
C_{2}(u)
, preserving the general structure of the mass function:

\displaystyle M(u,r)=C_{0}(u)+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{C}_{1}(u)r^{2}+\frac{1}{3}C_{2}(u)r^{3},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for $w=-\frac{1}{2}$},

(27)

\displaystyle M(u,r)=C_{0}(u)+\frac{1}{2}C_{1}(u)r^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\tilde{C}_{2}(u)r^{3},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for $w=-1$}.

(28)

Remark 2:
From the general solution given in Eqs. (
24
)-(
26
), it follows that by choosing
\rho_{0}(u)=0
, the energy density and pressure of the timelike matter component vanish, i.e.,
\rho(u,r)=p(u,r)=0
. In this case, the contribution
T^{(m)}_{\mu\nu}
in Eq. (
8
) drops out, and the total energy-momentum tensor reduces to a purely null component:

T_{\mu\nu}=T^{(n)}_{\mu\nu}=\mu(u,r)\,l_{\mu}l_{\nu},\qquad\mu(u,r)=\frac{\dot{C}_{0}(u)}{\kappa r^{2}}.

(29)

The corresponding Vaidya-type metric remains a solution of CG with the mass function given by

M(u,r)=C_{0}(u)+\frac{1}{2}C_{1}(u)\,r^{2}+\frac{1}{3}C_{2}(u)\,r^{3}.

(30)

This illustrates that even in the absence of timelike matter fields, CG admits radiating Vaidya-type spacetimes sourced purely by null radiation, generalizing the standard Vaidya solution within this extended gravitational framework.

Remark 3:
In Case I,
M=M(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ T_{\mu\nu}=0
,
the field equations (
16
)-(
21
) simplify to
\dot{M}(u)=0
, hence

\quad M(u)=\text{const.},

(31)

which can also be seen from the general solution given in Eqs. (
24
)-(
26
) by taking
\rho_{0}=0
,
C_{0}=const.
,
C_{1}=0
, and
C_{2}=0
. This means that in the vacuum configuration of CG with
M=M(u)
, the mass function must be constant. As the result, the generalized solution reduces to the standard Schwarzschild spacetime. This implies that the original Vaidya solution cannot be a vacuum solution of CG through the
H_{\mu\nu}
tensor.

Remark 4:
In Case II,
M=M(u,r),\penalty\ \penalty\ T_{\mu\nu}=0
, the mass function takes the form

M(u,r)=C_{0}+\frac{1}{2}C_{1}(u)r^{2}+\frac{1}{3}C_{2}(u)r^{3},

(32)

where
C_{0}
is constant. This implies that unlike Case I with
M=M(u)
, here the inclusion of
r
-dependence in the mass function allows non-trivial dynamical
solutions even in the absence of explicit matter fields. The resulting spacetime describes a novel dynamical solution sourced purely by geometric corrections from CG through the
H_{\mu\nu}
tensor. This solution may be interpreted
as the Schwarzschild black hole in a dynamical de Sitter-like background.

Remark 5:
In Case III,
M=M(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ T_{\mu\nu}(u)\neq 0
,
the field equations (
16
)-(
21
) give

p=-\rho=const.=c,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ M(u)=const.,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mu=0.

(33)

This solution can also be obtained from the general solution given in Eqs. (
24
)-(
26
) when one takes
w=-1
,
\rho_{0}=const.
,
C_{0}=const.
,
C_{1}=0
, and
C_{2}=0
. In this case the non-vanishing energy-momentum tensor takes the form

T_{\mu\nu}\text{ = }\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}-c\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)&c&0&0\\
c&0&0&0\\
0&0&cr^{2}&0\\
0&0&0&cr^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\\
\end{array}\right].

(34)

This case shows that CG permits static solutions sourced by a combination of timelike and null fluids only under very specific constraints: the pressure and energy density must be constant, equal in magnitude but opposite in sign, and the null fluid must vanish. Moreover, the mass function becomes constant, preventing radiating solutions in this configuration. In the limit
c=0
, this solution reduces to the vacuum case, i.e.
Case I.

Remark 6:
In Case IV,
M=M(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ T_{\mu\nu}(u,r)\neq 0
,
the field equations (
16
)-(

) together with the equation of state

admit consistent solutions for only the specific values

and
w=-\frac{1}{2}
. In these cases, the matter fields and mass function take the form

p=w\rho=-\rho_{0}(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ M(u)=C_{0}(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mu(u,r)=\frac{2\dot{C}_{0}(u)}{\kappa r^{2}}+\frac{\dot{\rho}_{0}(u)}{3}r.

(35)

and

p=w\rho=-\frac{\rho_{0}(u)}{2r},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ M(u)=C_{0}(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mu(u,r)=\frac{2\dot{C}_{0}(u)}{\kappa r^{2}}+\frac{\dot{\rho}_{0}(u)}{2}.

(36)

Hence, CG admits the original Vaidya solution in the presence of a non-zero timelike matter source
in addition to the null matter source. This is an interesting difference of
this theory with respect to GR. In the limit
\rho_{0}(u)=0
, these solutions reduce to the original Vaidya solution supported only by the null matter source.

4
Vaidya-type Solutions in Conformal Killing Theory

In this section, we investigate the existence of generalized Vaidya-type solutions in the framework of Conformal Killing Gravity (CKG). The field equations of CKG are given by

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}=\kappa T_{\mu\nu}+H_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\,\tilde{H}_{\mu\beta}+\nabla_{\mu}\,\tilde{H}_{\beta\alpha}+\nabla_{\beta}\,\tilde{H}_{\alpha\mu}=0,

(37)

where
\tilde{H}_{\mu\nu}=H_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{6}Hg_{\mu\nu}
with
H=g^{\mu\nu}H_{\mu\nu}
, and
T_{\mu\nu}
is the matter energy-momentum tensor. The tensor
H_{\mu\nu}
encapsulates the higher-order (third-derivative) modifications to Einstein’s field equations, characteristic of the CKG framework.

We assume the most general case, Case V, where the metric function is
M=M(u,r)
, and the energy-momentum tensor is given by (
8
) with
\mu=\mu(u,r)
,
\rho=\rho(u,r)
, and
p=p(u,r)
. The non-vanishing components of the
\tilde{H}_{\mu\nu}
tensor are computed as

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{00}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3r^{3}}\left\{(r-2M)(4M^{\prime}-rM^{\prime\prime})+6r\dot{M}-\kappa r^{2}[(r-2M)(p+2\rho)+3r\mu]\right\},

(38)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{01}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3r^{2}}\left[4M^{\prime}-rM^{\prime\prime}-\kappa r^{2}(p+2\rho)\right],

(39)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\left[M^{\prime}-rM^{\prime\prime}-\kappa r^{2}(2p+\rho)\right],

(40)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{33}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta\,\tilde{H}_{22}.

(41)

Hence, the CKG equations (
4
) give

\displaystyle F_{001}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3r^{4}}\biggl\{2r(6\dot{M}+r\dot{M}^{\prime}-r^{2}\dot{M}^{\prime\prime})+(r-2M)(8M^{\prime}-5rM^{\prime\prime}+r^{2}M^{\prime\prime\prime})

(42)

5
Conclusion

In this work, we have studied the generalized Vaidya spacetime within the framework of CG and CKG theories. This spacetime generalizes the original Vaidya solution by the inclusion of the energy-momentum tensor of a timelike matter field in addition to that of a null fluid. Assuming the barotropic equation of state
p=w\rho
with
w=const.
for the timelike matter field, we obtained exact solutions to the third-order equations of motion of CG and CKG. In both theories, in the most general case, the timelike energy density
\rho(u,r)
has the same solution which is time-dependent and exhibiting a power-law behavior controlled by the constant
w
; i.e.,

\rho(u,r)=\rho_{0}(u)r^{-2(1+w)}.

Interestingly this energy density contributes to the mass function
M(u,r)
and the null energy density
\mu(u,r)
in the same fashion in both CG and CKG. In fact, in CG, they are obtained as

\displaystyle M(u,r)=C_{0}(u)+\frac{1}{2}C_{1}(u)r^{2}+\frac{1}{3}C_{2}(u)r^{3}+\frac{\kappa\rho_{0}(u)}{2(1-2w)}r^{1-2w},

\displaystyle\mu(u,r)=\frac{\dot{C_{0}}(u)}{\kappa r^{2}}+\frac{\dot{\rho_{0}}(u)}{(1-2w)}r^{-(1+2w)},

while, in CKG, they are

\displaystyle M(u,r)=C_{0}(u)+\frac{1}{3}C_{1}r^{3}+\frac{1}{5}C_{2}r^{5}+\frac{\kappa\rho_{0}(u)}{2(1-2w)}r^{1-2w},

\displaystyle\mu(u,r)=\frac{\dot{2C_{0}}(u)}{\kappa r^{2}}+\frac{\dot{\rho_{0}}(u)}{(1-2w)}r^{-(1+2w)}.

These form interesting exact solutions to the field equations of CG and CKG, and special cases can be obtained by appropriately setting the functions/constants appearing in these solutions. Especially, it is worth noting that, in both theories, the functions/constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
control the contributions to the mass function
M(u,r)
’s coming purely from geometry, not related to the energy-momentum tensor. In fact, when there is no matter at all; i.e,
T_{\mu\nu}=0
, or equivalently
\rho(u,r)=\mu(u,r)=0
, the functions
C_{0}
’s become constants in the mass functions of both CG and CKG, and this still makes the solution time-dependent in CG through the functions
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, which might be the geometric generalization of the Vaidya solution in the
standard GR requiring null matter for its very existence, while being time-independent in CKG.

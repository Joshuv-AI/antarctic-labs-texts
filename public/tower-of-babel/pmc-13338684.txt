Antimicrobial Susceptibility of Human Pasteurella multocida Isolates in Korea, 2022–2025
Authors: Hye Gyung Bae, Jae Han Sohn, Hochan Seo, Byeonghak Kwak, Myungsook Kim, Jae-Seok Kim, Mi-Kyeong Lee, Kyungwon Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPasteurella multocida is an important zoonotic pathogen that causes soft-tissue infections in humans; however, recent antimicrobial susceptibility data in Korea remain limited.Materials and MethodsA total of 70 clinical P. multocida isolates collected at a nationwide commercial laboratory in Korea between November 2022 and July 2025 were analyzed. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was performed using the disk diffusion method on Mueller–Hinton agar supplemented with 5% sheep blood. Five clinically relevant antimicrobial agents were tested: penicillin, amoxicillin-clavulanic acid (AMC), levofloxacin, tetracycline, and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX). For isolates classified as penicillin non-susceptible by disk diffusion, minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) testing was performed using E-test and β-lactamase production was assessed using a phenotypic method.ResultsAll tested isolates were susceptible to tetracycline and TMP-SMX by disk diffusion. Susceptibility rates by disk diffusion were 97.1% for AMC, 91.4% for levofloxacin, and 85.7% for penicillin. All 10 isolates initially classified as penicillin non-susceptible by disk diffusion were confirmed to be susceptible to penicillin by MIC testing and were β-lactamase negative. The 2 isolates classified as non-susceptible to AMC by disk diffusion were also β-lactamase negative and susceptible to penicillin by MIC testing.ConclusionNo β-lactam resistance was identified among the 70 clinical P. multocida isolates analyzed. These findings support current recommendations that β-lactams and other first-line agents remain active against P. multocida isolates in Korea.

INTRODUCTION

Pasteurella multocida is a Gram-negative coccobacillus commonly transmitted through animal bites and close contact with animals. In humans, it is a well-recognized cause of soft-tissue and wound infections after bites or scratches from companion animals [1]. Rarely, respiratory and other invasive infections such as bacteremia, meningitis, and endocarditis may occur primarily in immunocompromised individuals, the elderly, neonates, or those with chronic pulmonary disease [1].

As the number of households owning domestic pets continues to rise, the opportunity for close contact and bite or scratch injuries is expected to increase, potentially leading to a higher burden of P. multocida infections. The potential health risks associated with Pasteurella spp. should not be overlooked [23].

Although P. multocida remains largely susceptible to first-line antibiotics such as penicillin and amoxicillin-clavulanic acid (AMC) [14], sporadic reports of β-lactamase–producing isolates and reduced β-lactam susceptibility have been described in human infections [5–11]. Plasmid-mediated β-lactamases, including TEM-1 and ROB-1, have been identified in human isolates [56].

Despite its clinical relevance, antimicrobial susceptibility data from Korea remain limited. Given the increasing number of pet-related exposures and the risk of severe soft-tissue or systemic infections, updated regional susceptibility data are essential to guide empirical therapy and monitor potential β-lactam resistance.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the antimicrobial susceptibility patterns of 70 human clinical P. multocida isolates collected at a nationwide commercial laboratory in Korea, particularly regarding β-lactam resistance.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Bacterial isolates

A total of 70 P. multocida isolates were isolated from clinical specimens submitted to a nationwide commercial laboratory in Korea from November 2022 through July 2025. During the study period, 3 isolates were collected in 2022, 18 in 2023, 16 in 2024, and 33 in 2025. Duplicate isolates from the same patient were excluded. Identification was performed by matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry on a Bruker instrument (Bruker Daltonik, Bremen, Germany).

2. Ethics statement

This study was conducted using bacterial isolates and anonymized laboratory data, without access to identifiable patient information. Therefore, ethical approval and informed consent were waived.

3. Antimicrobial susceptibility test

Disk diffusion susceptibility testing was performed according to the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) M45 guidelines using Mueller–Hinton agar supplemented with 5% sheep blood and incubated at 35°C for 16–18 hours [12]. Zone diameters were interpreted following CLSI M45 criteria. Antimicrobial disks (Oxoid, Basingstoke, UK) tested included penicillin (10 units), AMC (20/10 µg), levofloxacin (5 µg), tetracycline (30 µg), and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX; 1.25/23.75 µg). These agents were selected because they are commonly used first-line or alternative therapies for P. multocida infections. Quality control was performed using Streptococcus pneumoniae ATCC 49619 and Staphylococcus aureus ATCC 25923. For isolates showing penicillin non-susceptibility by disk diffusion, minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) were determined using E-test strips (bioMérieux, Marcy-l’Étoile, France) and β-lactamase production was assessed using BBL Cefinase disks (Becton Dickinson, Sparks, MD, USA).

RESULTS

Most isolates were recovered from wound-related specimens (n=65, 92.9%), while a small number was isolated from synovial fluid (n=2, 2.9%). The specimen source was unavailable for 3 isolates (4.3%). Of the 70 patients, 49 (70.0%) were female and 20 (28.6%) were male, and the sex of one patient (1.4%) was unknown. Patient ages ranged from 9 to 84 years, and the age groups included <10 years (n=1, 1.4%), 20-29 years (n=9, 12.9%), 30-39 years (n=5, 7.1%), 40–49 years (n=11, 15.7%), 50-59 years (n=25, 35.7%), and ≥60 years (n=19, 27.1%).

By disk diffusion testing, all tested isolates were susceptible to tetracycline and TMP-SMX, whereas 10 isolates were non-susceptible to penicillin, 2 isolates to AMC, and 6 isolates to levofloxacin (Table 1, Fig. 1). Ten isolates (14.3%) were initially non-susceptible to penicillin-by disk diffusion, but all were confirmed to, be susceptible to penicillin by MIC testing (0.094–0.125 µg/mL) and were negative for β-lactamase, indicating no confirmed penicillin resistance. Two isolates were also classified as non-susceptible to AMC by disk diffusion. MIC testing for AMC was not performed; however, both isolates were β-lactamase negative and susceptible to penicillin by MIC testing.

DISCUSSION

β-lactam resistance in P. multocida has been reported in human infections, although findings have varied across studies. In a susceptibility study of 157 human P. multocida isolates, only a single isolate demonstrated β-lactamase production with reduced susceptibility to amoxicillin [13], whereas a single-center clinical study reported β-lactamase positivity in 5 of 32 tested isolates [10]. In addition, a large retrospective review of 482 reported human cases described β-lactam resistance in a limited number of cases [9]. Taken together, β-lactam resistance among human P. multocida isolates appears to be uncommon, with variable findings across published studies, supporting the need for antimicrobial susceptibility testing in clinical isolates.

In this study, penicillin non-susceptibility was initially observed by disk diffusion in a subset of isolates. All such isolates were subsequently confirmed to be susceptible by MIC testing and lacked β-lactamase activity. Similarly, two isolates classified as non-susceptible to AMC by disk diffusion were β-lactamase negative and susceptible to penicillin by MIC testing, indicating limited clinical relevance of the disk diffusion findings. Accordingly, no confirmed β-lactam resistance was identified among the 70 human P. multocida isolates analyzed. These findings are consistent with available data from Korea indicating a low rate of reported β-lactam resistance among human Pasteurella isolates [14]. Veterinary surveillance studies from Korea have also reported low levels of β-lactam resistance in P. multocida isolates recovered from swine [15].

A discrepancy between disk diffusion and MIC testing was observed for penicillin. This finding suggests borderline phenotypic susceptibility rather than true resistance and that disk diffusion may overestimate β-lactam non-susceptibility in P. multocida. Similar discrepancies between disk diffusion and MIC testing have been reported in previous veterinary studies, particularly for penicillin-class antibiotics, with disk diffusion showing lower reliability and weaker correlation with genotypic resistance [16].

Fluoroquinolone resistance in P. multocida has been reported but remains rare among human clinical isolates. Susceptibility studies have shown preserved fluoroquinolone activity in human isolates [13], and a large retrospective review identified clinically significant resistance only in a small number of cases, mainly among multidrug-resistant isolates [9]. In the present study, six isolates were classified as non-susceptible to levofloxacin by disk diffusion. Although fluoroquinolone resistance has been infrequently reported in human P. multocida isolates, MIC confirmation was not performed. Therefore, the clinical significance of disk diffusion non-susceptibility remains uncertain.

Regarding other commonly used agents, susceptibility studies have demonstrated preserved in vitro activity of tetracycline against human P. multocida isolates [17–19], with low median MIC values reported in earlier analyses, particularly by Stevens et al. [18]. Although clinical resistance to tetracycline has been reported, most human isolates have remained susceptible in published studies [9]. Data on TMP-SMX resistance in human P. multocida isolates are limited, and available in vitro susceptibility studies suggest that resistance is rare [17]. In Korea, few published studies have addressed susceptibility to tetracycline or TMP-SMX [14]. Consistent with the available literature, all isolates in this study were susceptible to tetracycline and TMP-SMX according to CLSI criteria. It should be noted, however, that susceptibility interpretation may vary depending on the guideline applied, reflecting differences between CLSI and European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing standards.

This study has several limitations. First, the isolates were obtained from a nationwide commercial laboratory and may not fully represent the national epidemiology. In addition, the absence of specimen source information for three isolates may have limited interpretation, as antimicrobial susceptibility patterns can vary according to specimen type. Finally, MIC confirmation was not performed for fluoroquinolone non-susceptible isolates. Despite these limitations, the absence of β-lactam resistance observed in this study supports current clinical recommendations that β-lactam antibiotics remain appropriate empirical treatment options for P. multocida infections in Korea.

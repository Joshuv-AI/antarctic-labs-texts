Thermodynamical and dynamical stability of Einstein-Maxwell and extremal Einstein-Born-Infeld thin shells in ( 2 + 1 ) (2\ \mathbf{+}\ 1) dimensions
Dario Olmos Cayo, Zui Oporto Almaraz, M. L. Peñafiel

Abstract
We study the dynamical and thermodynamical stability of thin shells in (2+1)-dimensional spacetimes composed of an inner anti-de Sitter (AdS) region and an outer region described by a charged Bañados–Teitelboim–Zanelli (BTZ) spacetime, sourced either by Einstein–Maxwell theory (Maxwell-BTZ) or Einstein–Born–Infeld theory (BI-BTZ). Assuming a fixed charge-to-mass ratio and modeling the shell’s matter with a linear equation of state, we introduce a convenient parametrization to analyze the dynamical stability configurations. We find that Maxwell-BTZ thin shells admit a wider range of dynamically stable configurations compared to BI-BTZ thin shells. We also derive the thermodynamics of the shell matter, obtaining physically meaningful entropy functions in both cases, and examine the conditions for thermodynamical stability. In the Maxwell-BTZ case, we identify regions in the parameter space where configurations are both dynamically and thermodynamically stable. In contrast, for extremal BI-BTZ thin shells, all thermodynamically stable configurations are contained within the dynamically stable ones, and shells with a linear equation of state are always dynamically stable. This work extends the understanding of thin shell configurations in lower-dimensional gravity and elucidates the interplay between dynamics, thermodynamics, and nonlinear electrodynamics.



I
Introduction

The study of gravitational models in
\left({2+1}\right)
dimensions offers some unique insights for the physics underlying gravity that are often difficult to obtain when studying gravity in higher dimensions. Despite the lack of local degrees of freedom, gravity in
\left({2+1}\right)
dimensions admits a rich set of exact, nontrivial solutions including black holes
Bañados et al. 1992
;
Bañados et al. 1993
;
Carlip 1995
;
Martinez et al. 2000
, conical defects
Deser et al. 1984
and wormholes
Perry and Mann 1992
. Also, it is worth emphasizing that
\left({2+1}\right)
-dimensional spacetimes may permit a canonical quantization of gravity, as long as there exists a non-trivial cosmological constant
Achúcarro and Townsend 1986
;
Witten 1988
;
Constantinidis et al. 2015
. Moreover, several approaches for obtaining a quantum theory of gravity can be implemented in a
\left({2+1}\right)
-dimensional spacetime (see Refs.
Carlip 2005
;
Carlip 2023
and references therein).

The
\left({2+1}\right)
-dimensional black hole arises as the endpoint of gravitational collapse of matter or radiation
Ross and Mann 1993
;
Husain 1994
, following an analog of the Oppenheimer–Snyder scenario, in which a thin shell of matter collapses to form a black hole. These black holes also exhibit intriguing thermodynamic properties and provide connections to quantum gravity, further motivating their study.

Thin shells offer an idealized but powerful framework to model gravitational collapse and matter distributions in general relativity. These configurations are constructed by gluing two spacetime regions across a hypersurface of matter, with the junction conditions ensuring that the resulting solution is well-defined, as introduced by Israel
Israel 1966
.
A particularly interesting class of thin shell spacetimes consists of an inner region devoid of matter and an outer region described by a nontrivial geometry (such as a black hole) joined continuously at the shell. Thin shell solutions have also been explored within general relativity but with alternative theories for the matter sector, such as nonlinear electrodynamics
Eiroa and Simeone 2012
, and in the context of extended theories of gravity
Eiroa and Figueroa Aguirre 2018
.

Theories of nonlinear electrodynamics (NLED) have been proposed as generalizations of Maxwell’s theory to describe phenomena beyond the reach of the linear theory. Notably, Born–Infeld (BI) electrodynamics
Born and Infeld 1934
was originally developed to regularize the electric field and energy of a point charge, and it later gained prominence due to its appearance in the low-energy limit of string theory
Fradkin and Tseytlin 1985
. When coupled to gravity, NLED models can yield regular black hole solutions
Ayón-Beato and García 1998
;
Dymnikova 2004
, generalize black hole thermodynamics
Gulin and Smolić 2017
;
Bokulić et al. 2021
, and affect light propagation in black hole spacetimes
Guzman‐Herrera and Breton 2022
;
Guzman-Herrera and Breton 2024
, among other features.

In the context of
\left({2+1}\right)
dimensions, the black hole solution within BI electrodynamics — often referred to as the Born-Infeld-BTZ (BI-BTZ) black hole — was first derived by Cataldo and Garcia
Cataldo and García 1999
. Its thermodynamics was subsequently studied in
Myung et al. 2008
, and its stability within the canonical ensemble was investigated in
Hendi 2012
.

The stability of thin shells can be assessed from two complementary perspectives. On the one hand, one can analyze the
dynamical stability
by perturbing the shell’s equation of motion and examining its response to radial perturbations. On the other hand, one can examine the
thermodynamical stability
by requiring that internal changes of energy within the shell do not lead to a phase transition, which translates into conditions on the derivatives of the shell’s entropy
Martinez 1996
.

These two criteria are conceptually different and independent; however, one may wonder whether a thin shell solution that fulfills both stability criteria simultaneously can be regarded as more stable than those satisfying only one of them
Bergliaffa et al. 2020
;
Reyes et al. 2022
;
Eiroa et al. 2024
.

In the context of
\left({2+1}\right)
dimensions, the thermodynamics and thermodynamical stability of various thin shell configurations have been studied in
Lemos and Quinta 2013
;
Lemos and Quinta 2014
;
Lemos et al. 2015a
, while the construction and linear stability of BI thin shell was addressed in
Eiroa and Simeone 2013
.

This paper studies both dynamical and thermodynamical stability criteria for a
\left({2+1}\right)
-dimensional thin shell composed of an AdS inner region and a BI-BTZ outer region, and contrasts the results with the corresponding Maxwell limit. In Section
II
we review the BI-BTZ black hole solution, ensuring that the Maxwell-BTZ and BTZ limits are properly recovered. In Section
III
we construct the thin-shell spacetime by matching an inner AdS region to an outer BI-BTZ region across a matter shell. In Section
IV
we study the dynamical stability of the shell: we independently analyze the BI theory and its Maxwell limit as separate cases. In Section
V
we develop the thermodynamics of the matter composing the shell, beginning with the Maxwell case—which, to our knowledge, has not been previously explored—and then addressing the BI case, where we find that the extremal limit provides the most tractable scenario since in this particular case there exist a closed analytic expression for the gravitational radius in terms of the ADM mass and charge. Moreover, we explore two different equations of state for the inverse temperature that are well adapted for studying the cases under consideration: namely a Hawking-type and a power law temperature equations of state for treating the Maxwell-BTZ and extremal BI-BTZ cases respectively. Further, we derive analytic expressions for the entropy of these shells in an adequate parametrization that allows for the study of thermodynamical stability. In Section
VI
we examine the thermodynamical stability of the Maxwell and extremal BI shells, and compare these results with the dynamically stable configurations. Finally, in Section
VII
we summarize our findings and outlines perspectives for future work.

Throughout this work we set
G=c=\hbar=1
and use the metric signature

a,b,c

\left({2+1}\right)

III
Thin-shell spacetime

We are interested in the case of a shell of radius
R

\mathcal{V}^{-}

In virtue of Eq. (

While the stability criterion in the latter paragraph is derived from the equation of motion for the shell (see Eq. (

Let us first explore the simpler case of the shell constituted of matter obeying Maxwell electrodynamics. This corresponds to the BI-BTZ case where

(57)

The potential and its first derivative evaluated at

take the simple form

\displaystyle V\left({R_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\left(M^{2}-4\pi^{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}R_{0}^{2}\right)\left(4l^{2}\left(M^{2}-4\pi^{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}R_{0}^{2}\right)-4lMR_{0}+R_{0}^{2}\right)}{4\pi^{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}l^{2}R_{0}^{2}}\ ,

(59)

Considering that the energy density and pressure for the equilibrium state are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{2\pi R_{0}}\ ,

(60)

\displaystyle p_{0}

corresponds to the charge-to-mass ratio for the shell. As shall be noted below, this parametrization is useful for studying the stability configurations. Next, we can express the relevant quantities as functions of
\left({\lambda_{0},\alpha}\right)

p_{0}=-\frac{4\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}l^{2}+\sqrt{(8\pi\lambda_{0}l-1)^{2}}-1}{8\pi l\sqrt{(1-8\pi\lambda_{0}l)^{2}}}\ .

(62)

It is interesting to note that, within this parametrization,
p_{0}
is independent of

. Furthermore, we can define the dimensionless variable

, such that the pressure in Eq. (

Noticing that from Eq. (

(64)

Additionally, the second derivative of the potential is now written as

V^{\prime\prime}\left({R_{0}}\right)=-\frac{\lambda_{0}\left(\lambda_{0}\left(4\pi^{3}\alpha^{2}\left(\alpha^{2}-16\right)y^{3}+16\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}y^{2}-\pi\left(3\alpha^{2}+16\right)y+4\right)-2R_{0}(8\pi y-1)^{3}p^{\prime}(R_{0})\right)}{y^{2}(1-8\pi y)^{2}}\ .

(65)

In order to evaluate the dynamical stability we need to specify an equation of state. We shall parametrize a barotropic equation of state of the form

(66)

y

(68)

\begin{cases}\alpha>4\qquad,\ \text{for}\ \kappa<1\\

0<\alpha<4\ ,\ \text{for}\ \kappa>1\ .\end{cases}

(69)

Next, plugging the solution for

into Eq. (

is meaningful (given by Eq. (

V
Thermodynamics of the BI-BTZ shell

We begin with the thermodynamic description of a thin shell embedded in a
\left({2+1}\right)-
dimensional spacetime whose entropy is characterized by its mass
M
, its charge
Q
and its perimeter
P
. Thus, we can write the first law of thermodynamics for the shell as

TdS=dM+pdP-\Phi dQ\ .

(74)

Alternatively, defining
\beta_{T}\equiv 1/T
, we can write

dS=\beta_{T}dM+\beta_{T}pdP-\beta_{T}\Phi dQ\ .

(75)

In order for the entropy differential to be exact, we have the following integrability conditions.

\displaystyle\left({\frac{\partial\beta_{T}}{\partial R}}\right)_{M,Q}

\displaystyle=2\pi\left({\frac{\partial\beta_{T}p}{\partial M}}\right)_{R,Q}\ ,

(76)

\displaystyle\left({\frac{\partial\beta_{T}}{\partial Q}}\right)_{M,R}

\displaystyle=-\left({\frac{\partial\beta_{T}\Phi}{\partial M}}\right)_{R,Q}\ ,

(77)

\displaystyle\left({\frac{\partial\beta_{T}\Phi}{\partial R}}\right)_{M,Q}

\displaystyle=-2\pi\left({\frac{\partial\beta_{T}p}{\partial Q}}\right)_{M,R}\ .

(78)

We can define the redshift factor for the shell as
k\equiv\sqrt{f\left({R}\right)}
where
f(R)
is the metric function for the spacetime in the exterior of the shell. In particular, we have that the material mass can be written in terms of
k
as

M=\left({\frac{R}{4l}-\frac{k}{4}}\right)\ .

(79)

Since
k\geq 0
, the latter equation implies directly that
4lM\leq R
, which is the bound previously obtained. From the above expression it is straightforward to obtain

\left({\frac{\partial M}{\partial R}}\right)_{m,Q}=-2\pi p\ .

(80)

These two latter expressions are general and independent of the functional form of the exterior spacetime and shall be useful when treating the thermodynamics of charged shells. Once the entropy in Eq. (
75
) depends on the intensive parameters
\left({\beta_{T},p,\Phi}\right)
, we need to specify an equation of state for each of these quantities. The pressure equation of state is readily given by the junction conditions and takes the form displayed in Eq. (
38
). Therefore, the remaining task is to specify an equation of state for the inverse temperature,
\beta_{T}
and the thermodynamic electrostatic potential,
\Phi
.

It is possible to express the quantities
M
and
Q
as implicit functions of the horizons
r_{+}
and
r_{-}
, such that the temperature equation of state can be obtained by solving Eq. (
76
), which reads

\left({\frac{\partial\beta_{T}}{\partial R}}\right)_{r_{+},r_{-}}=2\pi\beta_{T}\left({\frac{\partial p}{\partial M}}\right)_{R,Q}\ .

(81)

Next, the electrostatic potential equation of state can be obtained by using Eq. (
80
) into the integrability conditions (
76
)-(
78
), which leads to the equation

k\left({\frac{\partial p}{\partial Q}}\right)_{M,R}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\left({\frac{\partial\Phi k}{\partial R}}\right)_{r_{+},r_{-}}=0\ .

(82)

Once all the equations of state are known, it is possible to obtain the entropy of the shell. Next, we analyze the thermodynamics for a charged thin-shell in a
\left({2+1}\right)
-dimensional spacetime both in Maxwell and Born-Infeld electrodynamics.

V.1
Maxwell charged thin shell

Assuming that the exterior part of the shell is described by the
\left({2+1}\right)
-dimensional solution of the Einstein-Maxwell system (i.e., by Eq. (
16
)), we have that the pressure of the shell is given by

p=\frac{R\left({R-kl}\right)-l^{2}Q^{2}}{8\pi kl^{2}R}\ .

(83)

Next, the temperature equation of state can be obtained by solving Eq. (
81
), which reads

\left({\frac{\partial\beta_{T}}{\partial R}}\right)_{r_{+},r_{-}}=\beta_{T}\left({\frac{R^{2}-l^{2}Q^{2}}{k^{2}l^{2}R}}\right)\ ,

(84)

and has the solution
\beta_{T}={\rm a}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)k
, where
{\rm a}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)
can be envisaged as the inverse temperature of the shell if its radius were
R=l
. Next, for the thermodynamic electrostatic potential we obtain the equation

\left({\frac{\partial\Phi k}{\partial R}}\right)_{M,R}=\frac{Q}{2R}\ ,

(85)

which has the solution

\Phi=\frac{1}{k}\left({{\bar{\phi}}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\left({\frac{R}{l}}\right)}\right)\ .

(86)

Again, the function
{\bar{\phi}}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)
is regarded as the electrostatic potential of the shell if its radius were
l
and shall be treated as an unknown function in order to determine the integrability conditions. Therefore, the thermodynamic electrostatic potential,
\Phi
can be thought of as the blueshifted difference between the electrostatic potential of a shell of radius
R
and that of a shell of radius
R=l
. In the following, it is useful to write the thermodynamic electrostatic potential as

\Phi=\frac{Q}{2k}\left[{\rm c}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)+\ln\left({R/l}\right)\right]\ ,

(87)

where
{\rm c}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)\equiv 2{\bar{\phi}}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)/Q
.

Turning to the entropy differential in Eq. (
75
), it is always possible to write it in terms of two implicit functions
r_{+}\left({M,Q}\right)
and
r_{-}\left({M,Q}\right)
which are related to the roots of the metric function. Notice that
M=M\left({m,Q,R}\right)
is given by Eq. (
79
), while
Q
is the charge of the shell, that is not related to its radius. Hence, straightforward calculation shows that the entropy differential can now be written in terms of
{\rm d}r_{+}
and
{\rm d}r_{-}
alone as

{\rm d}S=\beta_{T}\left[\left({\frac{\partial M}{\partial r_{+}}}\right)_{r_{-},R}-\Phi\left({\frac{\partial Q}{\partial r_{+}}}\right)_{r_{-},R}\right]{\rm d}r_{+}+\beta_{T}\left[\left({\frac{\partial M}{\partial r_{-}}}\right)_{r_{+},R}-\Phi\left({\frac{\partial Q}{\partial r_{-}}}\right)_{r_{+},R}\right]{\rm d}r_{-}\ .

(88)

The remaining task being to compute the derivatives inside the square brackets. Notice that the metric function in Eq. (
16
) is non-polynomial and the logarithmic term prevents us from obtaining a linear function of the horizons for
m
and
Q
as in the case of the Reissner-Nordström black hole in 3+1 dimensions. However, it is still possible to analytically find a relation for
m
and
Q
as functions of
\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)
. Taking
f\left({r_{+}}\right)=0
and
f\left({r_{-}}\right)=0
we find that the charge, the ADM mass and the material mass are

\displaystyle Q^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{-}^{2}-r_{+}^{2}}{2l^{2}\ln\left({r_{-}/r_{+}}\right)}\ ,

(89)

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}^{2}}{l^{2}}-\frac{\left(r_{-}^{2}-r_{+}^{2}\right)\ln\left(\frac{r_{-}r_{+}}{l^{2}}\right)}{l^{2}\ln\left(\frac{r_{-}}{r_{+}}\right)}\right)\ ,

(90)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{4l}-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{R^{2}}{l^{2}}-\frac{\left(r_{-}^{2}-r_{+}^{2}\right)\ln\left(\frac{R}{l}\right)}{l^{2}\ln\left(\frac{r_{-}}{r_{+}}\right)}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\left(r_{-}^{2}-r_{+}^{2}\right)\ln\left(\frac{r_{-}r_{+}}{l^{2}}\right)}{l^{2}\ln\left(\frac{r_{-}}{r_{+}}\right)}-\frac{r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}^{2}}{l^{2}}\right)}\ ,

(91)

respectively. Notice that as
r_{-}\to 0
we recover the results for the BTZ black hole. Thus, computing the corresponding derivatives in Eq. (
88
) results in a new integrability condition for guaranteeing that the entropy differential is exact, namely

\frac{\partial{\rm a}}{\partial r_{-}}h(r_{+})r_{-}\left[{\rm c}+\ln\left(\frac{r_{-}}{l}\right)\right]+\frac{\partial{\rm a}}{\partial r_{+}}h(r_{-})r_{+}\left[{\rm c}+\ln\left(\frac{r_{+}}{l}\right)\right]=-{\rm a}\left(\frac{\partial{\rm c}}{\partial r_{-}}h(r_{+})r_{-}+\frac{\partial{\rm c}}{\partial r_{+}}h(r_{-})r_{+}\right)\ ,

(92)

where
{\rm a}={\rm a}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)
and
{\rm c}={\rm c}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)
, and we have defined

h\left({r_{\pm}}\right)\equiv r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}+2r_{\pm}^{2}\ln\left({r_{-}/r_{+}}\right)\ .

(93)

One may attempt to find a family of solutions for
{\rm a}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)
and
{\rm c}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)
that satisfy the integrability condition. However, we shall propose an
ansatz
for the inverse temperature that resembles the Hawking temperature for a charged BTZ black hole
Medved 2002
, namely

{\rm a}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)=\frac{\gamma l^{2}r_{+}\ln\left(\frac{r_{-}}{r_{+}}\right)}{r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}+2r_{+}^{2}\ln\left(\frac{r_{-}}{r_{+}}\right)}\ ,

(94)

where
\gamma
is a constant to be determined by the matter content of the shell (i.e., the microphysics). Plugging this expression into the integrability condition in Eq. (
92
) still results in an equation for
{\rm c}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)
and its derivatives. We may propose an
ansatz
for this function of the form

{\rm c}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)=-\ln\left({r_{+}/l}\right)\ ,

(95)

that fulfills the integrability condition. Notice that the sign in Eq. (
95
) is such that the thermodynamic electrostatic potential in Eq. (
86
) is effectively a difference of potentials. With these
ansätze
at hand, the entropy differential is

{\rm d}S=\frac{\gamma}{8}{\rm d}r_{+}\ ,

(96)

which can be directly integrated yielding
S=S_{0}+\gamma r_{+}/8
, with
S_{0}
being an integration constant. This constant can be fixed by demanding that in the absence of a shell (i.e., when
M
and
Q
go to zero), the entropy must also be zero, hence
S_{0}=0
and the entropy for the shell is

S=\frac{\gamma}{8}r_{+}\ .

(97)

where
\gamma>0
necessarily in order to have a positive entropy. The constant
\gamma
can take,
a priori
, arbitrary values. However, in the particular limit where the shell’s radius approaches the gravitational radius, i.e., as
R\to r_{+}
, one must recover the Hawking temperature for the shell and hence the entropy will be that of the corresponding black hole
Lemos et al. 2015b
. Hence, in this limit we have

\gamma=4\pi\ .

(98)

V.2
Born-Infeld charged thin shell

Let us now focus on the more general case of a BI charged thin shell. In this case, the pressure of the thin shell is given by Eq. (
38
) and the remaining equations of state come from solving the integrability conditions. Namely, the first integrability condition can be written as

\displaystyle\left({\frac{\partial\beta_{T}}{\partial R}}\right)_{r_{+},r_{-}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\beta_{T}\left({\frac{\partial p}{\partial M}}\right)_{R,Q}

(99)

\displaystyle=\beta_{T}\left({\frac{R^{2}\beta^{2}\left({1+\Upsilon}\right)+Q^{2}\left({1-2l^{2}\Upsilon\beta^{2}}\right)}{Rk^{2}l^{2}\beta^{2}\Upsilon\left({1+\Upsilon}\right)}}\right)\ .

(100)

The latter equation has a general solution of the form
\beta_{T}=\mathrm{a}\left({r_{+},r_{-}}\right)k\left({R,r_{+},r_{-}}\right)
, where
\mathrm{a}=\mathrm{a}\left({R\to l,r_{+},r_{-}}\right)
can be envisaged as the inverse temperature of the shell if its radius were
l
.

For the electrostatic potential, the integrability conditions in Eqs. (
76
)-(
78
) can be expressed as

\frac{1}{2\pi}\left({\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial R}}\right)_{r_{+},r_{-}}+\Phi\left({\frac{\partial p}{\partial M}}\right)_{R,Q}+\left({\frac{\partial p}{\partial Q}}\right)_{M,R}=0\ .

(101)

In use of Eq. (

VII
Conclusions and perspectives

In the present work, we have studied the dynamical and thermodynamical stability conditions for a
\left({2+1}\right)
-dimensional charged thin shell within BI electrodynamics. In the process of constructing the thin shell spacetime, we have re-derived the BI-BTZ black hole line element ensuring that the corresponding Maxwell-BTZ and static BTZ black holes are obtained in the
\beta\to\infty
and
Q\to 0
limits, respectively.

We have derived the conditions for dynamical stability for the BI shell composed of matter obeying a linear equation of state. This type of equation of state allows us to establish a set of parameters,
\left({\alpha,\kappa,z}\right)
(which has been previously introduced in the context of thin shell wormholes within Maxwell electrodynamics in
Eiroa et al. 2024
), where
z
is related to the nonlinear departures from Maxwell’s theory. Within this new parametrization, we have obtained similar results as in Ref.
Eiroa and Simeone 2013
for this type of stability. Regarding to the cases we address in the present work, the dynamical stability for the Maxwell charged thin shell (see Fig.
1
), all the region of parameters where the thin shell is composed by an outer manifold lacking of any horizons is dynamically stable, while a part of the region that corresponds to the outer manifold consisting of a spacetime with horizons is also stable. As BI nonlinearities are taken into account (see Fig.
2
), the dynamical stability region diminishes in the domain where the outer manifold has horizons, while the region consisting of no horizons is still completely stable. As a matter of fact, as
z
grows the maximum allowable value of
\kappa
for having a stable configurations tends to 0, i.e., only the regions with negative pressures are dynamically stable. Let us also note that the critical lines defining the extremal thin shell vary as the nonlinearity parameter
z
varies. In addition, the extremal lines are always contained within the dynamically stable region, independent of the value of
z
. This last feature is better displayed in the
\left({w,r_{l},\sigma}\right)
parametrization.

Furthermore, we have developed the thermodynamics and studied the thermodynamical stability for the charged thin shells in both Maxwell and BI electrodynamics. In the particular case of Maxwell electrodynamics, using an equation of state for the inverse temperature of the Hawking type, we have been able to derive an entropy for the shell that is coincident with that of the charged BH in
\left({2+1}\right)
dimensions. This particular choice for the inverse temperature allows us to study configurations that are thermodynamically stable in the regime where the outer manifold encloses horizons and
R>r_{+}
. Figure
5
displays both thermodynamically and dynamically stable configurations and it can be seen that there is a region in the parameter space that is completely stable. To the best of our knowledge, the study of complete stability for a charged thin shells in
\left({2+1}\right)
dimensions constitutes novel results.

We have also developed the thermodynamics of a generic BI thin shell. However, due to the lack of an analytic solution for the relation between the gravitational radii and the mass and charge parameters, it is no longer possible to obtain straightforward relations for the temperature and electrostatic potential equations of state and such a task would require further numerical work. Notwithstanding, due to the fact that the extremal BI thin shell has a well known relation for the gravitational radius in terms of the mass and charge, we have obtained that the shell’s entropy is necessarily a function of
r_{\mathrm{ex}}
alone, coinciding with the treatment of other kinds of extremal shells in other dimensions (cf.
Lemos and Quinta 2013
). Furthermore, we have been able to propose some well suited
ansätze
that conforms to the physically realistic scenario that the
entropy density
is non-negative. Furthermore, using a power law equation of state for the inverse temperature, we have been able to find a closed analytic solution for the extremal BI shell entropy. Thus, we have also been able to study the thermodynamical stability configurations for such a shell.

For the extremal BI thin shell, we have obtained that the thermodynamically stable regions are always contained in the dynamically stable regions. As well, these thermodynamically stable regions are relevant only when contained inside the domain where the entropy is non-negative. Thus, for the extremal BI shells, we find that the thermodynamically stable regions have a well defined parametric dependence: namely, as
\delta
grows the region shrinks and as
\sigma
grows the region is displaced upwards in the
\left({w,r_{l}}\right)
plane. Finally, the complete stability for the extremal BI shell is better presented in the
\left({w,r_{l},\sigma}\right)
parametrization, which is better suited for studying the dynamical stability of the extremal shell than the
\left({\alpha,\kappa,z}\right)
parametrization since we have a single extremal curve for each
z
, while all extremal curves are contained in the
\left({w,r_{l}}\right)
plane. Thus, in a way, this new parametrization condenses the information for the dynamical stability of extremal shells in such a way that the complete stability for them can be easily studied.

Despite the apparent simplicity of studying systems in
\left({2+1}\right)
-dimensional spacetimes, the thermodynamics for a BI thin shell cannot be studied analytically in the most general regime. Surprisingly enough, the particular case of an extremal BI shell can be analytically studied. As future avenues of research, let us note that the entropy function for the extremal BI shell recovers
S=0
for the Maxwell limit; this particular scenario is in accordance with numerous claims that the entropy of an extremal black hole is zero (cf.
Lemos et al. 2015a
) and deserves further attention. Also, we intend to generalize our results to generic BI shells in
\left({2+1}\right)
dimensions and analyze the complete stability for a BI thin shell in
\left({3+1}\right)
dimensions in future work.

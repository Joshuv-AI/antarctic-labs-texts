A remark about Calderón-Hardy spaces with variable exponents
Pablo Rocha

Abstract
In this note we improve the parameter q q that appears in Theorem 1 obtained by the author in [Math. Ineq. & appl., Vol 19 (3) (2016), 1013-1030].



†
†

Keywords
: Variable Calderón-Hardy Spaces, Variable Hardy Spaces, Atomic decomposition.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
42B25, 42B30

Notation and terminology:

- The symbol
A\lesssim B
stands for the inequality
A\leq cB
for some positive constant
c
, and
A\approx B
stands for

B\lesssim A\lesssim B
.

- We denote by
Q\left(x_{0},r\right)
the cube centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with side lenght
r
. Given a cube

Q=Q(x_{0},r)
, we set
\delta Q=Q(x_{0},\delta r)
.

- For a measurable subset
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by
\left|E\right|
and
\chi_{E}
the Lebesgue measure of
E
and the characteristic function of
E
respectively.

-
M
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator given by

Mf(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}|Q|^{-1}\int_{Q}|f(y)|\,dy,

where
f
is a locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the supremum is taken over all the cubes
Q
containing
x
.

-
\Delta^{m}
stands for the iterated Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

- A measurable function
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,\infty)
is called exponent function or only exponent, and set

p_{-}=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)
,
p_{+}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)
and
\underline{p}=\min\{p_{-},1\}
.

- We say that an exponent
p(\cdot)
is locally log-Hölder continuous, and denote this by
p(\cdot)\in LH_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if there exists a positive constant
C_{0}
such that

\left|p(x)-p(y)\right|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{-\log\left|x-y\right|},\,\,\,\left|x-y\right|<\frac{1}{2}.

- We say that an exponent
p(\cdot)
is log-Hölder continuous at infinity, and denote this by
p(\cdot)\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if there exists a positive constant
C_{\infty}
such that

\left|p(x)-p(y)\right|\leq\frac{C_{\infty}}{\log\left(e+\left|x\right|\right)},\,\,\,\left|y\right|\geq\left|x\right|.

-
\left(L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\right)
is the Lebesgue space with variable exponents on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,\left|\frac{f(x)}{\lambda}\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

-
\left(H^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{p(\cdot)}}\right)
is the Hardy space with variable exponents on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
2
]
).

- A function
a(\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called an
(p(\cdot),p_{0},d_{p(\cdot)})
- atom, if there exists a cube
Q
such that

a_{1})

\textit{supp}\left(a\right)\subset Q,

a_{2})

\left\|a\right\|_{p_{0}}\leq\frac{\left|Q\right|^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}}{\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|_{p(\cdot)}}
,
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<p_{0}\leq\infty
and
p_{0}\geq 1
,

a_{3})

\int a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for all
\left|\alpha\right|\leq d_{p(\cdot)}:=\min\left\{l\in\mathbb{N\cup}\left\{0\right\}:p_{-}(n+l+1)>n\right\}.

- For sequences of nonnegative numbers
\left\{k_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

and cubes
\left\{Q_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and for an exponent
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\left(0,\infty\right)
, we define

\mathcal{A}\left(\left\{k_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\left\{Q_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},p(\cdot)\right)=\left\|\left\{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k_{j}\chi_{Q_{j}}}{\left\|\chi_{Q_{j}}\right\|_{p(\cdot)}}\right)^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{\underline{p}}}\right\|_{p(\cdot)}.

- Every
f\in H^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
admits an atomic decomposition
f=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}k_{j}a_{j}
(see
[
2
]
), where

\left\{k_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of non negative numbers, the
a_{j}
’s are
(p(\cdot),p_{0},d)
- atoms and

\mathcal{A}\left(\left\{k_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\left\{Q_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},p(\cdot)\right)\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p(\cdot)}}.

-
\left(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}}\right)
is the Calderón-Hardy space with variable exponents on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
3
]
).

In
[
3
]
, we proved the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p(\cdot)
be an exponent that belongs to
LH_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

1<q<\infty
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
and
n(2m+n/q)^{-1}<\underline{p}
. Then
for
q
sufficiently large the operator
\Delta^{m}
is a bijective mapping from
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
onto

H^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, there exist two positive constant
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

c_{1}\|F\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}}\leq\|\Delta^{m}F\|_{H^{p(\cdot)}}\leq c_{2}\|F\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}}

hold for all
F\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In others words, Theorem 1 says that the equation

\Delta^{m}F=f,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,f\in H^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

has a unique solution
F
in
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for
1<q<\infty
(
sufficiently large
) and

m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n(2m+n/q)^{-1}<\underline{p}
.

If
n
is large, and since the parameter
q
is sufficiently large in Theorem 1, then one can be forced to take
m
also large to satisfy the condition
n(2m+n/q)^{-1}<\underline{p}=\min\{p_{-},1\}
. For instance, when
n
is large and
n/q
is small. This restricts the possible values of
m
once fixed
n
large. In this note, we will remove the condition that
q
be sufficiently large.

Next, we give the main steps to improve the parameter
q
in Theorem 1.

Following the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
3
, p. 1026]
, we compute the
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
- norm of the following pointwise inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}k_{j}N_{q,2m}(B_{j};x)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}k_{j}\frac{\left[M(\chi_{Q_{j}})(x)\right]^{\frac{2m+n/q-\mu}{n}}}{\|\chi_{Q_{j}}\|_{p(\cdot)}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}k_{j}\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}(x)M(a_{j})(x)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}k_{j}\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}(x)[M(M^{q}(a_{j}))(x)]^{1/q}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}k_{j}\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}(x)\sum_{|\alpha|=2m}T^{*}_{\alpha}(a_{j})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II+III+IV,

where the
a_{j}
’s are
(p(\cdot),p_{0},d_{p(\cdot)})
- atoms corresponding to an atomic decomposition of an arbitrary element

f\in H^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and each
a_{j}
is supported on
Q_{j}
.

We observe that only need to improve the estimation of
III
, where

III=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}k_{j}\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}(x)[M(M^{q}(a_{j}))(x)]^{1/q}.

Indeed, in
[
3
]
to estimate
III
we take
q>1
sufficiently large such that

\delta=\frac{1}{q}
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma 4.11 in
[
2
]
. This is,
q
must be such that

\frac{1}{q}\in(0,-\frac{\log_{2}(\beta)}{n+1})
, where
\beta
is an unspecified constant of
(0,1)
. In the estimates of
I
,
II
and

IV
it is not required
q
large.

To improve the estimate of
III
, we will need two supporting results, the first is a version of
[
1
, Lemma 5.4]
obtained by K.-P. Ho, and the second one refers to the amount
\mathcal{A}\left(\left\{k_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\left\{Q_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},p(\cdot)\right)
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,\infty)
such that
p(\cdot)\in LH_{0}\cap LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
. Let
s>1
and
0<p_{*}<\underline{p}
such that
sp_{*}>p_{+}
and let

\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of nonnegative functions in
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that each
b_{j}
is supported
in a cube
Q_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\|b_{j}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq A_{j}|Q_{j}|^{1/s},

(1)

where
A_{j}>0
for all
j\geq 1
. Then, for any sequence of nonnegative numbers
\{k_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
we have

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}k_{j}b_{j}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)/p_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}A_{j}k_{j}\chi_{Q_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)/p_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
C
is a positive constant which does not depend on
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
,
\{A_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, and

\{k_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one given in
[
4
, Proposition 3.3]
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,\infty)
be an exponent with
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
and let
\{Q_{j}\}
be a family of cubes which satisfies the bounded intersection property. If
0<p_{*}<\underline{p}
, then

\left\|\left\{\sum_{j}\left(\frac{k_{j}\chi_{Q_{j}}}{\|\chi_{Q_{j}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right)^{p_{*}}\right\}^{1/p_{*}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\approx\mathcal{A}\left(\{k_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},p(\cdot)\right)

for any sequence of nonnegative numbers
\{k_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one given in
[
4
, Lemma 5.7]
.
∎

We are now in a position to give a new estimate of
III
.

New estimate of
III
: Given
1<q<\infty
and an exponent
p(\cdot)\in LH_{0}\cap L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, let

0<p_{*}<\underline{p}
be fixed,
p_{0}>\max\{p_{+},2q\}
, and let
a(\cdot)
be an
(p(\cdot),p_{0},d_{p(\cdot)})
- atom. So,

\displaystyle\left\|[M(M^{q}(a_{j}))]^{p_{*}/q}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}/p_{*}}(4\sqrt{n}Q_{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|[M(M^{q}(a_{j}))]^{1/q}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}))}^{p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|M(a_{j})\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|a_{j}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{|Q_{j}|^{\frac{p_{*}}{p_{0}}}}{\left\|\chi_{Q_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{p_{*}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\left|4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}\right|^{\frac{p_{*}}{p_{0}}}}{\left\|\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)/p_{*}}}},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma 2.2 in
[
2
]
. Now, since
0<p_{*}<1
, we apply the
p_{\ast}
-inequality and Proposition
2
with
b_{j}=\left(\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}\cdot[M(M^{q}(a_{j}))]^{p_{*}/q}\right)
,

A_{j}=\left\|\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)/p_{*}}}^{-1}
and
s=p_{0}/p_{*}
, to obtain

\displaystyle\|III\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j}\left(k_{j}\,\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}\,[M(M^{q}(a_{j}))]\right)^{p_{*}/q}\right\|^{1/p_{*}}_{L^{p(\cdot)/p_{*}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j}\left(\frac{k_{j}}{\left\|\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right)^{p_{*}}\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}\right\|^{1/p_{*}}_{L^{p(\cdot)/p_{*}}}.

It is easy to check that
\chi_{4\sqrt{n}Q_{j}}\leq[M(\chi_{Q_{j}})]^{2}
. From this inequality, Lemma 2.2 in
[
2
]

and Lemma 2.4 in
[
2
]
, we have

\displaystyle\|III\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{\sum_{j}\left(\frac{k_{j}^{p_{*}/2}}{\left\|\chi_{Q_{j}}\right\|^{p_{*}/2}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}M(\chi_{Q_{j}})\right)^{2}\right\}^{1/2}\right\|^{2/p_{*}}_{L^{2p(\cdot)/p_{*}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{\sum_{j}\left(\frac{k_{j}\chi_{Q_{j}}}{\|\chi_{Q_{j}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right)^{p_{*}}\right\}^{1/p_{*}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}.

Finally, Lemma
3
gives

\|III\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\lesssim\mathcal{A}\left(\{k_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},p(\cdot)\right)\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p(\cdot)}}.

Thus, now we have the following improved version of Theorem 1.

Theorem 4
.

Let
p(\cdot)
be an exponent that belongs to
LH_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

1<q<\infty
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
and
n(2m+n/q)^{-1}<\underline{p}
. Then
the operator
\Delta^{m}
is a bijective mapping from
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
onto

H^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, there exist two positive constant
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

c_{1}\|F\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}}\leq\|\Delta^{m}F\|_{H^{p(\cdot)}}\leq c_{2}\|F\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}}

hold for all
F\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{q,2m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

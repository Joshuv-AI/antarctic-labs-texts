Gravitational Entropy
Sangmin Choi, and Malcolm J. Perry

Abstract
We formulate the classical gravitational entropy of a horizon as a Noether charge that does not require the notion of a temperature, and which is applicable to horizons that are not necessarily associated with black holes. This introduces a correction to the covariant phase space formalism that accounts for the configuration-dependence of the generating vector field conjugate to the charge. The vector field is related to the proposal of Bousso that the gravitational entropy of a region is determined by the lightsheet at its boundary. We test the formula on various black hole and cosmological horizons.



1
Introduction

Understanding black holes as objects that obey the laws of thermodynamics has a long history, going back over 50 years
Bardeen:1973gs
;
Bekenstein:1973ur
;
Hawking:1975vcx
.
The first law of thermodynamics of a rotating, charged black hole states that the variations of its area
A
of the event horizon, mass
M
, angular momentum
J
, and charge
Q
are related by

\displaystyle\delta M

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi G}\delta A+\Omega_{H}\delta J+\Phi\delta Q

(1)

where
\kappa
is the surface gravity,
\Omega_{H}
is the angular velocity and
\Phi
is the electric potential of the black hole.
Comparing to the thermodynamics of a rotating, charged body, the term involving the area behaves like the heat transfer
T\delta S
at temperature
T
, where
\delta S
is the change in
the entropy:
the surface gravity and the area of the horizon correspond to the Hawking temperature and the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of the black hole, respectively.

For a black hole whose event horizon is a bifurcate Killing horizon,
Wald’s Noether charge method can be used to derive the first law from the diffeomorphism charge associated with a Killing vector field
\xi

Wald:1993nt
;
Jacobson:1993vj
;
Iyer:1994ys
.
Generalizations to dynamical scenarios have been proposed in
Dong:2013qoa
;
Wall:2015raa
;
Hollands:2024vbe
.
For perturbations around stationary black holes, the quantity
T\delta S
can be written as an integral of the Noether charge 2-form associated with
\xi
over the bifurcation surface.
This carries a factor of the surface gravity
\kappa
, and so this procedure is tied to the notion of the Hawking temperature.

In this paper, we relax the temperature dependence and derive the entropy
\delta S
from the Noether charge, making it applicable to cases that do not involve black holes.
Following Bousso’s observation
Bousso:2002ju
that entropy should be determined by the behavior of the generators of the lightsheets on the boundary of the region of concern,
the vector
\xi
conjugate to the diffeomorphism charge is chosen to be the null generator of the horizon, normalized in a universal manner.
At a first glance, one may argue that this is achieved by simply including an overall factor
1/T
in the vector field whose conjugate diffeomorphism charge leads to
T\delta S
.
However, as we shall see below, the presence of this factor restricts perturbations only to nearby black hole solutions with identical surface gravity, i.e.
\delta\kappa=0
.
We take the configuration space to be the space of the same type of black hole solutions, with varying values of parameters such as
M
,
J
and
Q
.
Then, the surface gravity (and hence the Hawking temperature) depends on the point in the configuration space, and therefore in general
\delta\kappa\neq 0
.
To derive the entropy
\delta S
as a Noether charge conjugate to a vector field
\xi
, one has to take into account the possibility for
\delta\xi\neq 0
.
This leads to a new term appearing in the covariant phase space method.
Such a term has appeared independently in
Ashtekar:2024stm
; it has also appeared indirectly in the literature in the form of modified Lie bracket in
Barnich:2001jy
;
Gao:2003ys
;
Barnich:2011mi
.

With this correction, we illustrate with examples that
\delta S
(and thus
S
) is derived from the Noether charge conjugate to
\xi
.
Since our formula does not involve the notion of Hawking temperature, it is applicable to spacetimes that do not involve the notion of a black hole.
For black holes with a bifurcation Killing horizon and a well-defined notion of mass and angular momentum at infinity, the integrated entropy
S
is given simply as an integral of
the Noether charge 2-form on any section of the horizon.
Interestingly, this integral yields the correct Gibbons-Hawking entropy
Gibbons:1977mu
when applied to any section of the cosmological horizons of de Sitter and Kottler spacetimes,
which is useful since de Sitter spacetime lacks parameters with respect to which we can vary the metric.

The paper is organized as follows.
In section
2
, we review and extend the covariant phase space formalism and diffeomorphism charges to account for vector fields that are functions on the configuration space.
In section
3
, we apply the formalism to Einstein gravity.
We illustrate the universal normalization of the null vector field in the Schwarzschild black hole in section
4
, and apply it to the Kerr black hole in section
5
.
In section
6.1
we briefly discuss adding electromagnetic sources, and then in section
6.2
we work out the entropy of Kerr-Newman black holes.
We work through example spacetimes that exhibit cosmological horizons in sections
7
and
8
.
We end with a discussion of the results in section
9
.
Some details have been delegated to the appendix.

2
Covariant Phase Space

The starting point of our discussion is the action principle for a set of fields
\phi
.
\phi

will always
include the gravitational field described by the metric tensor
g_{ab}
, but may also include
other fields, such as the electromagnetic field
A_{a}
that we will consider in later sections.
The action
I
is the integral
of a four-form Lagrangian
L
so

\displaystyle I=\int L(\phi).

(2)

The action has dimensions of
[M][L]
so that when inserted into the path integral
I/\hbar
is
dimensionless.

Variation of the fields
\phi\rightarrow\phi+\delta\phi
induces a variation of the action
\delta I

where

\displaystyle\delta I=\int E(\phi)\cdot\delta\phi+d\theta(\phi,\delta\phi).

(3)

The equation of motion is
E(\phi)=0
but, in general, there will also be a boundary term
that defines
\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)
, the presymplectic potential three-form.

Gravitational theories are invariant under infinitesimal diffeomorphisms generated by a vector field
\xi^{a}
.
The resultant transformations on the various components of
\phi
are given by
their Lie derivative with respect to
\xi
, so that in general
\delta\phi={\cal L}_{\xi}\phi
.

One can find a formula for the Noether charge conjugate to
\xi
. Starting from the
presymplectic potential
\theta
, we make second variation of
\phi
given by
\delta^{\prime}\phi
so that the presymplectic form
\omega
is

\displaystyle\omega(\phi,\delta\phi,\delta^{\prime}\phi)=\delta\theta(\phi,\delta^{\prime}\phi)-\delta^{\prime}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)

(4)

where
(\delta\delta^{\prime}-\delta^{\prime}\delta)\phi=0
.
Now let the variation
\delta^{\prime}={\cal L}_{\xi}
be a diffeomorphism.
Provided
E(\phi)=0
and
\delta\phi
obeys the linearised equations of motion,
\omega
is closed
and can be written as
\omega=d\hat{G}
. Then

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi}=\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\omega(\phi,\delta\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)=\int_{S}\hat{G},

(5)

where
Q_{\xi}
is the Noether charge conjugate to
\xi
,
\Sigma_{s}
is a spacelike three-surface, and
S=\partial\Sigma_{s}
is its two-dimensional boundary.
The expression
\delta Q_{\xi}
should be interpreted as the change in the charge conjugate to
\xi
as
the fields
\phi
vary into
\phi+\delta\phi
.

The Noether current resulting from the diffeomorphism generated by
\xi
is the three-form
\hat{J}[\xi]
defined by

\displaystyle\hat{J}[\xi]=\theta(\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)-\iota_{\xi}L(\phi).

(6)

\hat{J}
is closed provided
\phi
obeys the equation of motion
E(\phi)=0
. One can then write

\displaystyle\hat{J}=d\hat{F}

(7)

for some two-form
\hat{F}[\xi]
that is a functional of both
\phi
and
\xi
.
The variation of the current is then

\displaystyle\delta\hat{J}[\xi]=\delta\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)-\iota_{\xi}d\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)-\iota_{\delta\xi}L(\phi),

(8)

provided
E(\phi)=0
holds.
The last term on the (
8
) accounts for the possibility that
\xi^{a}
is not constant in the configuration space, in which case
\delta\xi^{a}
is not identically zero.
Cartan’s magic identity for an arbitrary
p
-form
X
is

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\xi}X=d\iota_{\xi}X+\iota_{\xi}dX,

(9)

and so we find that

\displaystyle\delta\hat{J}[\xi]=\delta\theta(\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)-{\cal L}_{\xi}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)+d\iota_{\xi}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)-\iota_{\delta\xi}L(\phi).

(10)

Since
\delta\xi
is not necessarily zero, the first two terms on the right-hand side of (
10
) are related to the presymplectic three-form
\omega
plus a correction that is linear in
{\cal L}_{\delta\xi}\phi
,

\displaystyle\delta\theta(\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)-{\cal L}_{\xi}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)=\omega(\phi,\delta\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)+\theta(\phi,{\cal L}_{\delta\xi}\phi).

(11)

Thus the variation of the Noether current becomes

\displaystyle\delta\hat{J}[\xi]=\omega(\phi,\delta\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)+\theta(\phi,{\cal L}_{\delta\xi}\phi)+d\iota_{\xi}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)-\iota_{\delta\xi}L(\phi),

(12)

which can be reorganized into the form

\displaystyle\omega(\phi,\delta\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)=\delta\hat{J}[\xi]-d\iota_{\xi}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)-\hat{J}[\delta\xi].

(13)

Now consider a spacelike three-surface
\Sigma_{s}
with boundary
S
.
The variation of the Noether charge is

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi}=\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\omega(\phi,\delta\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)=\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\delta\hat{J}[\xi]-d\iota_{\xi}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)-\hat{J}[\delta\xi].

(14)

Provided
E(\phi)=0
holds, we can write
\hat{J}=d\hat{F}
and so by Stokes’ theorem

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi}=\int_{S}\delta\hat{F}[\xi]-\iota_{\xi}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)-\hat{F}[\delta\xi].

(15)

The term
\hat{F}[\delta\xi]
in (
15
) is missing in much of the older literature
Lee:1990nz
;
Wald:1993nt
;
Iyer:1994ys
;
Wald:1999wa

although it has appeared independently in
Ashtekar:2024stm
equation (A6) and footnote 10.
The possible dependence of
\xi^{a}
on the point on phase space has also been indirectly taken into account by the modified bracket in
Barnich:2001jy
;
Gao:2003ys
;
Barnich:2011mi
.
For further details of this new term, we refer to Appendix B.

Notice that there is a consistency condition here that needs to be satisfied.
For the expression on the r.h.s. to be consistent, we require that the last two terms be a total variation. That is,

\displaystyle\int_{S}\iota_{\xi}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)+\hat{F}[\delta\xi]=\delta\int_{S}C

(16)

where
C
is some two-form (which may not be covariant) that is determined, up to the addition of
dW
for some one-form
W
.
If such a
C
exists, then the integrated entropy
Q_{\xi}
exists and takes the simple form

\displaystyle Q_{\xi}=\int_{S}(\hat{F}[\xi]-C).

(17)

If it is the case that no such
C
exists, then
Q_{\xi}
does not exist.

It seems as if the restrictions resulting from this consistency condition are rather stringent. However,
the definitions of both the action and the presymplectic potential are fraught with ambiguity
Wald:1999wa
;
Iyer:1994ys
.
The first ambiguity lies in the observation that the action is not unique.
The equations of motion are invariant under a change of the action under

L\rightarrow L+dZ
for some three-form
Z
. A second ambiguity is that the Noether current can be
modified simply by the addition
of the exterior derivative of some two-form
Y
so that
\hat{J}\rightarrow\hat{J}+dY
.
This would appear to make
Q_{\xi}
arbitrary.
One might suppose that there is another ambiguity in the definitions of
\hat{F}
and
\hat{G}
in that one could add to them
pieces that are the exterior derivatives of some one-forms. But provided that
S
is closed,
this will affect neither
Q_{\xi}

nor
\delta Q_{\xi}
so we will not pursue that possibility.
Summarizing these two induced transformations on the various differential forms we have encountered
so far, we note that

\displaystyle L\rightarrow L+dZ

(18)

\displaystyle\theta\rightarrow\theta+\delta Z+dY

(19)

\displaystyle\hat{J}\rightarrow\hat{J}+dY+d(\iota_{\xi}Z)

(20)

\displaystyle\hat{F}\rightarrow\hat{F}+Y+\iota_{\xi}Z

(21)

\displaystyle\hat{G}\rightarrow\hat{G}+\delta Y(\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)-{\cal L}_{\xi}Y(\phi,\delta\phi).

(22)

The effect of these transformations on the charge and its variation can easily be determined and we find that

\displaystyle Q_{\xi}\rightarrow Q_{\xi}+\int_{S}(Y+\iota_{\xi}Z)

(23)

and

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi}\rightarrow\delta Q_{\xi}+\int_{S}\left(\delta Y(\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)-{\cal L}_{\xi}Y(\phi,\delta\phi)\right).

(24)

We observe that apart from the consistency resulting from (
16
),
Y
can be chosen in a more or
less arbitrary fashion as there is a compensation in
\delta Q_{\xi}
that follows on from any change in
Q_{\xi}
resulting from
any particular choice of
Y
. What then happens to our discussion of the consistency between the two different ways of calculating
\delta Q_{\xi}
?
We still require that both
Z
and
C
can be chosen so as to ensure agreement between
Q_{\xi}

and
\delta Q_{\xi}
. We take
Z=0
in what follows. Were we interested in calculating
Q_{\xi}
at spatial or null
infinity, we might then need to introduce a non-zero
Z
for reason outlined by Gibbons, Hawking and York
York:1972sj
;
Gibbons:1976ue
.
Finally, we emphasize that it may not be possible to achieve agreement between our definitions of
Q_{\xi}
and

\delta Q_{\xi}
and should this happen, we conclude that no such Noether charge exists.

3
Gravitational Charges

The Einstein-Hilbert action
I_{\text{EH}}
is the usual starting point for establishing the equations of motion
in general relativity.

\displaystyle I_{\text{EH}}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int_{\cal M}\ (R-2\Lambda)\ \sqrt{-g}\ d^{4}x

(25)

where the integral is taken over the spacetime manifold
{\cal M}
. If one make a variation of the metric

g_{ab}\rightarrow g_{ab}+h_{ab}
, then we obtain the both the equation of motion

\displaystyle R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}

(26)

and the presymplectic potential

\displaystyle\theta^{a}_{\text{(basic)}}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}(\nabla_{b}h^{ab}-\nabla^{a}h)

(27)

where
h=h_{ab}g^{ab}
.
Given the equation of motion, we can find the linearised equation of motion for
h_{ab}

\displaystyle\Box h_{ab}+\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}h-\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}h^{c}_{a}-\nabla_{a}\nabla_{c}h^{c}_{b}+2R_{acbd}h^{cd}

\displaystyle=0.

(28)

We are interested here in surfaces that are spacelike so that we can measure the charge
contained in a closed two-surface surrounding some region of space. The Noether current coming from the
basic part of the action is

\displaystyle\hat{J}^{a}[\xi]=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\biggl(\Box\,\xi^{a}-\nabla^{a}\nabla_{b}\,\xi^{b}+R^{ab}\xi_{b}-\xi^{a}R+2\Lambda\xi^{a}\biggr).

(29)

Provided the background equation of motion
R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}
holds,
\nabla_{a}\hat{J}^{a}=0
and so
\hat{J}^{a}=\nabla_{b}\hat{F}^{ab}
.
We choose

\displaystyle\hat{F}^{ab}[\xi]=\frac{1}{16\pi G}(\nabla^{b}\xi^{a}-\nabla^{a}\xi^{b}).

(30)

The charge conjugate
Q_{\xi}
then satisfies

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\ \Bigl(\delta\hat{F}^{ab}[\xi]-2\theta^{a}\xi^{b}-\hat{F}^{ab}[\delta\xi]\Bigr)dS_{ab}.

(31)

The presymplectic form is

\omega^{a}(h,h^{\prime})=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\Biggl[\tfrac{1}{2}h^{\prime}\nabla^{a}h-\tfrac{1}{2}h\nabla^{a}h^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}h^{\prime}\nabla_{b}h^{ab}+\tfrac{1}{2}h\nabla_{b}h^{\prime}{}^{ab}-\tfrac{1}{2}h^{\prime}{}^{ab}\nabla_{b}h\\
+\tfrac{1}{2}h^{ab}\nabla_{b}h^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}h^{\prime}{}^{bc}\nabla^{a}h_{bc}+\tfrac{1}{2}h^{bc}\nabla^{a}h^{\prime}_{bc}+h^{\prime}_{bc}\nabla^{b}h^{ac}-h_{bc}\nabla^{b}h^{\prime}{}^{ac}\Biggr].

(32)

Putting
h^{\prime}_{ab}={\cal L}_{\xi}g_{ab}=\nabla_{a}\xi_{b}+\nabla_{b}\xi_{a}
, using the equations of motion and the
linearised equations of motion for
h_{ab}
, we find that
\omega^{a}=\nabla_{b}\hat{G}^{ab}
where

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{ab}\equiv\frac{1}{16\pi G}\bigg[\xi^{b}\nabla^{a}h

\displaystyle-\xi^{a}\nabla^{b}h+\xi^{a}\nabla_{c}h^{bc}-\xi^{b}\nabla_{c}h^{ac}-\xi_{c}\nabla^{a}h^{bc}

\displaystyle+\xi_{c}\nabla^{b}h^{ac}-\frac{1}{2}h\nabla^{a}\xi^{b}+\frac{1}{2}h\nabla^{b}\xi^{a}-h^{bc}\nabla_{c}\xi^{a}+h^{ac}\nabla_{c}\xi^{b}\bigg].

(33)

From this expression we see that the variation
\delta Q_{\xi}
of the charge
Q_{\xi}

as the metric varies from
g_{ab}
to
g_{ab}+h_{ab}
is

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi}=\frac{1}{2}\int\hat{G}^{ab}dS_{ab},

(34)

as one would expect.

4
The Schwarzschild Case

Our aim in this section is to develop a candidate expression for the gravitational entropy.
We will do this by an examination of the geometry of the Schwarzschild black hole
and conjecture a general result for the appropriate Noether charge. In subsequent sections,
we will test our conjecture.

The Schwarzschild metric in
(t,r,\theta,\phi)
coordinates takes the familiar form

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-V(r)dt^{2}+{dr^{2}\over V(r)}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})

(35)

where

\displaystyle V(r)=1-{2GM\over r}.

(36)

A section of the future horizon is the obvious location of a two-surface to see if one can find a

\xi
that reproduces the known black hole entropy.
Thus our surface
\Sigma_{s}
will stretch from spacelike infinity and intersect the future horizon at some
moment of advanced time. To explore this scenario,
we introduce ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates
(v,r,\theta,\phi)
with the advanced time being
v

given by
v=t+r^{\ast}
and
dr^{\ast}/dr=1/V(r)
.
The metric then takes the form

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-V(r)dv^{2}+2dvdr+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}).

(37)

v
is a null coordinate that labels time on the future horizon.
The surface
\Sigma_{s}
intersects the horizon at some
v=v_{0}
and
r=2GM.

The spacetime is static and so
{\partial/\partial v}=k^{a}{\partial/\partial x^{a}}

where
k^{a}
is a Killing vector that is null and geodesic on the horizon.
The surface gravity on the horizon
\kappa
is defined by

\displaystyle k^{a}\nabla_{a}k^{b}=\kappa k^{b}

(38)

indicating the
k^{a}
is not affinely parametrised by
v
. Evaluating
\kappa
yields

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{1}{4GM}.

(39)

The Hawking temperature
T_{H}

for black holes is universally given by

\displaystyle T_{H}=\frac{\hbar\kappa}{2\pi}

(40)

and so for Schwarzschild

\displaystyle T_{H}=\frac{\hbar}{8\pi GM}.

(41)

We now need to find the correct vector
\xi^{a}
to give the black hole entropy.
Recall that the charge as defined in previous sections has the dimension of
[M]
so to find a
dimensionless entropy we need to rescale
Q_{\xi}
by a factor of
1/\hbar
. The entropy
would be given by

\displaystyle S=\frac{l_{p}}{\hbar}Q_{\xi}.

(42)

The choice of
\xi
at first seem to be completely undetermined.

Bousso suggested some time ago
Bousso:2002ju
that entropy should be determined
by the behavior of the generators of lightsheets on the boundary of the region of concern.
Accordingly, we pick
\xi^{a}
to be the null generator of the horizon.
Thus,
\xi^{a}
is some multiple of
k^{a}
, so set

\xi^{a}=\lambda k^{a}
, where
\lambda
may depend on
M
.
Then
\delta\xi^{a}=(\delta\lambda)k^{a}
.
Now we determine
Q_{\xi}
.
For this, we first compute

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{vr}[\xi]=-\frac{M\lambda}{8\pi r^{2}},\qquad\hat{F}_{vr}[\delta\xi]=-\frac{M\delta\lambda}{8\pi r^{2}},

(43)

and

\displaystyle\theta_{v}=-\frac{\delta M}{8\pi r^{2}},\qquad\theta_{r}=0,\qquad\theta_{\theta}=0,\qquad\theta_{\phi}=0.

(44)

On the horizon in
(v,r,\theta,\phi)
coordinates we can choose the unit timelike vector
t^{a}=({1/\sqrt{V}},0,0,0)
and the unit spacelike vector
s^{a}=(1/\sqrt{V},\sqrt{V},0,0)
so that
s^{a}t_{a}=0
.
Integrating on the horizon and recalling

\displaystyle dS^{ab}=-2\,t^{[a}\,s^{b]}\ \sigma^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\ d^{2}x.

(45)

so that

dS^{vr}=-r^{2}\sin\theta d\theta d\phi
,
we find the total charge obeys

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\delta(\lambda M)+\lambda\delta M-M\delta\lambda\right]=\lambda\delta M.

(46)

The choice
\lambda=2\pi(\kappa l_{p})^{-1}=8\pi GMl_{p}^{-1}
then gives
\delta Q_{\xi}=\delta(4\pi GM^{2}l_{p}^{-1})
and

\displaystyle S=\frac{l_{p}}{\hbar}Q_{\xi}=\frac{4\pi GM^{2}}{\hbar}=\frac{A}{4\hbar G}

(47)

where
A
is the area of the intersection of
\Sigma_{s}
with the horizon.
With this choice the consistency condition (
16
) is met with
C=0
.

We are therefore led to conjecture that in general
\xi^{a}
should be chosen such that it is
null geodesic generating the lightsheet of the region whose
gravitational entropy we wish to find. It needs to obey the null geodesic
equation and its parametrisation is fixed by

\displaystyle\xi^{a}\nabla_{a}\xi^{b}=\frac{2\pi}{l_{p}}\xi^{b}.

(48)

Now look at the variation
\delta Q_{\xi}
. A variation in the mass of the
black hole
\delta M
results in an
h_{ab}
whose only non-vanishing
component is
h_{vv}={2G\delta M/r}
. A computation of
\hat{G}_{vr}
results
in

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{vr}=-\frac{2GM\delta M}{l_{p}r^{2}}.

(49)

then

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi}=\frac{8\pi G}{l_{p}}M\delta M

(50)

so

\displaystyle\delta S=\frac{8\pi GM\delta M}{\hbar}.

(51)

This result is consistent with the first law of black hole thermodynamics
and is also consistent with the evaluation of
Q_{\xi}
.

5
The Kerr Case

Next, we test our entropy formula for the Kerr black hole.
The metric of a Kerr black hole of mass
M
and angular momentum
J
in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates takes the form

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta}{\Sigma}(dt-a\sin^{2}\theta d\phi)^{2}+\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}dr^{2}+\Sigma d\theta^{2}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\left(adt-(r^{2}+a^{2})d\phi\right)^{2}

(52)

where
r_{s}=2GM
is the Schwarzschild radius,
a=J/M
is the angular momentum per mass,
\Sigma=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta
and
\Delta=r^{2}-r_{s}r+a^{2}
.
We change to a set of coordinates
(v,r,\theta,\phi)
that is the Kerr analog of the ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates,

\displaystyle dv=dt+\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})}{\Delta}dr,\qquad d\phi^{\text{new}}=d\phi^{\text{old}}+\frac{a}{\Delta}dr,

(53)

where
v
is a null coordinate that labels time on the horizons.
In these coordinates, the Kerr metric can be organized into the following form

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left(1-\frac{r_{s}r}{\Sigma}\right)(dv-a\sin^{2}\theta d\phi)^{2}+2(dv-a\sin^{2}\theta d\phi)(dr-a\sin^{2}\theta d\phi)

\displaystyle\quad+\Sigma(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}).

(54)

The volume element is
\sqrt{-g}=\Sigma\sin\theta
.
The only non-zero components of the inverse metric are

\displaystyle g^{vv}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{2}}{\Sigma}\sin^{2}\theta,\qquad g^{vr}=\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\Sigma},\qquad g^{rr}=\frac{\Delta}{\Sigma},

(55)

\displaystyle g^{v\phi}

\displaystyle=g^{r\phi}=\frac{a}{\Sigma},\qquad g^{\theta\theta}=\frac{1}{\Sigma},\qquad g^{\phi\phi}=\frac{1}{\Sigma\sin^{2}\theta}.

The radii of the inner and outer horizons
r_{-}
and
r_{+}
are located at the solutions to
\Delta=0
,

\displaystyle r_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(r_{s}\pm\sqrt{r_{s}^{2}-4a^{2}}).

(56)

The surface
\Sigma_{s}
intersects the outer horizon
r=r_{+}
at some
v=v_{0}
.

The Killing vector
k^{a}\partial_{a}=\partial_{v}+\Omega_{+}\partial_{\phi}
is null and geodesic on the outer horizon satisfying
\xi^{a}\nabla_{a}\xi^{b}=\kappa\xi^{b}
,
where

\displaystyle\Omega_{+}=\frac{a}{r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}}

(57)

is the angular speed on the outer horizon.
The surface gravity
\kappa
has the expression

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{r_{+}-r_{-}}{2r_{s}r_{+}}.

(58)

The Hawking temperature is given by
T_{H}=\hbar\kappa/2\pi
.

To find the entropy, we define
\xi^{a}
to be the vector obtained by rescaling

according to the normalization (
48

It has the components

\displaystyle\xi^{a}\partial_{a}=\frac{2\pi}{l_{p}\kappa}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v}+\Omega_{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right)

(59)

Let us first check that the consistency condition (
16
) holds.
One finds that the integral of
-\iota_{\xi}\theta
takes the simple form

\displaystyle\int_{S}-\iota_{\xi}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{l_{p}\kappa}\delta M.

(60)

Since the vector field
\xi^{a}
(
59
) depends on
M
and
J

\delta\xi^{a}
is non-vanishing,

\displaystyle\delta\xi^{a}\partial_{a}=\delta\Big(\frac{2\pi}{l_{p}\kappa}\Big)\frac{\partial}{\partial v}+\delta\Big(\frac{2\pi\Omega_{+}}{l_{p}\kappa}\Big)\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}.

(61)

This implies that the charge conjugate to

\displaystyle\int_{S}-\hat{F}[\delta\xi]

\displaystyle=-\frac{M}{2}\delta\Big(\frac{2\pi}{l_{p}\kappa}\Big)+J\delta\Big(\frac{2\pi\Omega_{+}}{l_{p}\kappa}\Big).

(62)

The consistency condition (
16
) requires that the sum of (

62

It turns out that the sum vanishes:

\displaystyle\int_{S}-\iota_{\xi}\theta-\hat{F}[\delta\xi]=\frac{\pi}{l_{p}\kappa}\delta M-\frac{M}{2}\delta\Big(\frac{2\pi}{l_{p}\kappa}\Big)+J\delta\Big(\frac{2\pi\Omega_{+}}{l_{p}\kappa}\Big)=0,

(63)

which can be derived by a straightforward computation, using the variations

\displaystyle\delta\Omega_{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2Ga}{r_{s}^{2}r_{+}}\frac{(r_{+}+\frac{1}{2}r_{s})}{(r_{+}-\frac{1}{2}r_{s})}\delta M+\frac{2G}{r_{s}}\frac{1}{(r_{+}^{2}-a^{2})}\delta J,

(64)

\displaystyle\delta\Big(\frac{1}{\kappa}\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta\kappa}{\kappa^{2}}=-\frac{8Gar_{s}}{(r_{+}-r_{-})^{3}}(a\delta M-\delta J).

(65)

This is a consequence of the first law of black hole thermodynamics

\displaystyle\delta M=T_{H}\delta S+\Omega_{+}\delta J,

(66)

and the Smarr formula
Smarr:1972kt
, which for the Kerr black hole takes the form

\displaystyle M=2T_{H}S+2\Omega_{+}J.

(67)

Taking a variation of the Smarr formula and using the first law, one can show that the following identity holds,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta M}{2T_{H}}-\frac{M}{2}\delta\Big(\frac{1}{T_{H}}\Big)+J\delta\Big(\frac{\Omega_{+}}{T_{H}}\Big)=0,

(68)

which is, after putting
T_{H}=\hbar\kappa/2\pi

63
).
Thus, the consistency condition (
16

C=0
just as was the case for Schwarzschild.

Since the contributions from
\iota_{\xi}\theta
and
\hat{F}[\delta\xi]

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi}

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\delta\hat{F}[\xi]-\iota_{\xi}\theta-\hat{F}[\delta\xi]=\delta\int_{S}\hat{F}[\xi],

(69)

the full charge given just by the integral of
\hat{F}[\xi]

\displaystyle Q_{\xi}=\int_{S}\hat{F}[\xi]=\frac{2\pi}{l_{p}\kappa}\left(\frac{M}{2}-\Omega_{+}J\right).

(70)

After putting

\displaystyle=\frac{l_{p}}{\hbar}Q_{\xi}=\frac{M}{2T_{H}}-\frac{\Omega_{+}J}{T_{H}}=\frac{A}{4G\hbar},

(71)

where

Now let’s look at the variation of the Noether charge.
The variations

\delta J

\displaystyle h_{vv}

(72)

\displaystyle h_{v\phi}

\displaystyle h_{r\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{M}(a\delta M-\delta J),

(74)

\displaystyle h_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2a}{M}\cos^{2}\theta(a\delta M-\delta J),

(75)

\displaystyle h_{\phi\phi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2a^{2}}{M}\sin^{2}\theta\left[1+\frac{GMr}{\Sigma^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta(r^{2}-a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)\right]\delta M

(76)

(77)

This is consistent with the variation
\delta S=\frac{l_{p}}{\hbar}\delta Q_{\xi}

Electromagnetism and Kerr-Newman black holes

6.1
Including Electromagnetism

In electromagnetism, the basic field from which everything else is built, is the vector potential
A_{a}
.
It gives rise to a field strength tensor
F_{ab}=\nabla_{a}A_{b}-\nabla_{b}A_{a}
which is invariant under the
gauge transformation
A_{a}\rightarrow A_{a}+\partial_{a}\epsilon
for arbitrary
\epsilon.
The electromagnetic
action is

\displaystyle I_{em}=-\frac{1}{4}\int F_{ab}F^{ab}\ \sqrt{-g}\ d^{4}x.

(78)

Routine calculations yield
T_{ab}
, the energy-momentum tensor as

\displaystyle T_{ab}=F_{a}{}^{c}F_{bc}-\tfrac{1}{4}g_{ab}F_{cd}F^{cd}

(79)

together with the Maxwell equation

\displaystyle\nabla_{b}F^{ab}=0.

(80)

The Maxwell equation needs to be supplemented by the Bianchi identity
\nabla_{[a}F_{bc]}=0
, which is a direct consequence of the definition of
F_{ab}
in terms of
A_{a}
.

Following the prescription outlined for gravity, we find an extra contribution to the presymplectic potential
that must be added to the gravitational contribution (
27
)

\displaystyle\theta^{a}_{\text{(em)}}=-F^{ab}\delta A_{b}.

(81)

The electromagnetic field then gives rise to some extra contributions to the various quantities we have discussed
in the purely gravitational case.
There will be therefore extra terms in the gravitational Noether current
which can be derived in exactly as in the purely gravitational case.
Also, in addition to diffeomorphism the theory has a
U(1)
gauge symmetry.
Taking this into account, we find the Noether current conjugate to the diffeomorphism
\xi
and
U(1)
gauge transformation
\epsilon
to be

\displaystyle\hat{J}^{a}_{\text{(em+grav)}}[\xi,\epsilon]

\displaystyle=\hat{J}^{a}_{\text{(grav)}}[\xi]-F^{ab}({\cal L}_{\xi}A_{b}-\partial_{b}\epsilon).

(82)

As before, if the Einstein equations and the Maxwell equations are both satisfied,
\hat{J}^{a}_{\text{(em+grav)}}
is conserved
and yields

\displaystyle\hat{F}^{ab}_{\text{(em+grav)}}=\hat{F}^{ab}_{\text{(grav)}}-(\xi^{c}A_{c}+\epsilon)F^{ab}.

(83)

It is straightforward to show that the presymplectic three-form is related to the current conjugate to
\xi
and
\epsilon
in a way analogous to the gravitational case (
13
),

\displaystyle\omega(\phi,\delta\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi+\delta_{\epsilon}\phi)

\displaystyle=\delta\hat{J}[\xi,\epsilon]-d\iota_{\xi}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)-\hat{J}[\delta\xi,\delta\epsilon],

(84)

where
\delta_{\epsilon}
denotes the
U(1)
gauge transformation
\delta_{\epsilon}A_{a}=\partial_{a}\epsilon
,
\delta_{\epsilon}g_{ab}=\delta_{\epsilon}F_{ab}=0
.
Likewise, the Noether charge conjugate to
\xi
and
\epsilon
can be computed using
\hat{F}[\xi,\epsilon]
,

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi,\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\delta\hat{F}[\xi,\epsilon]-\iota_{\xi}\theta(\phi,\delta\phi)-\hat{F}[\delta\xi,\delta\epsilon].

(85)

The presymplectic form is

\displaystyle\omega_{\text{(em+grav)}}^{a}

\displaystyle=\omega_{\text{(grav)}}^{a}+\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime}F^{ab}\delta A_{b}+h^{\prime ac}F^{b}{}_{c}\delta A_{b}-h^{\prime bc}F^{a}{}_{c}\delta A_{b}+g^{ac}g^{bd}\delta^{\prime}F_{cd}\delta A_{b}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}hF^{ab}\delta^{\prime}A_{b}-h^{ac}F^{b}{}_{c}\delta^{\prime}A_{b}+h^{bc}F^{a}{}_{c}\delta^{\prime}A_{b}-g^{ac}g^{bd}\delta F_{cd}\delta^{\prime}A_{b}

(86)

Putting
h^{\prime}_{ab}={\cal L}_{\xi}g_{ab}
,
\delta^{\prime}A_{a}={\cal L}_{\xi}A_{a}+\partial_{a}\epsilon
and using the equations of motion as well as the linearised equations of motion for
h_{ab}
and
\delta A_{a}
,
we find that
\omega_{\text{(em+grav)}}^{a}=\nabla_{b}\hat{G}^{ab}_{\text{(em+grav)}}
where

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{ab}_{\text{(em+grav)}}

\displaystyle\equiv\hat{G}^{ab}_{\text{(em)}}-\xi^{a}F^{bc}\delta A_{c}+\xi^{b}F^{ac}\delta A_{c}-\xi^{c}F^{ab}\delta A_{c}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{1}{2}hF^{ab}+h^{ac}F^{b}{}_{c}-h^{bc}F^{a}{}_{c}+g^{ac}g^{bd}\delta F_{cd}\right)(\xi^{e}A_{e}+\epsilon).

(87)

6.2
Kerr-Newman Black Hole

Now we apply our formula to the Kerr-Newman black hole.

In the Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, the Kerr-Newman metric of mass
M
, angular momentum
J
and electric charge
Q
reads

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta}{\Sigma}(dt-a\sin^{2}\theta d\phi)^{2}+\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}dr^{2}+\Sigma d\theta^{2}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\left[adt-(r^{2}+a^{2})d\phi\right]^{2}

(88)

where
r_{s}=2GM
is the Schwarzschild radius,
a=J/M
is the angular momentum per mass,
\Sigma
and
\Delta
are defined as

\displaystyle\Sigma=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,\qquad\Delta=r^{2}-r_{s}r+a^{2}+r_{Q}^{2}.

(89)

Here
r_{Q}
is the length scale corresponding to the electric charge, defined as

\displaystyle r_{Q}^{2}=\frac{G}{4\pi}Q^{2},

(90)

in units with unit vacuum permittivity.
Just like in the Kerr case, we perform a change of coordinates (
53
) but instead with the new definition of
\Delta
for Kerr-Newman given in (
89
).
This takes us to the Kerr-Newman analog
(v,r,\theta,\phi)
of the ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates, in terms of which the metric (
88
) reads

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left(1-\frac{(r_{s}r-r_{Q}^{2})}{\Sigma}\right)dv^{2}+2dvdr-\frac{2a}{\Sigma}(r_{s}r-r_{Q}^{2})\sin^{2}\theta dvd\phi-2a\sin^{2}\theta drd\phi+\Sigma d\theta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\left[(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-\Delta a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]d\phi^{2}.

(91)

The inverse metric takes the same form as those (
5
) for the Kerr metric except for the difference in the definition of
\Delta
in (
89
).
The inner and outer horizons are located at
r=r_{-}
and
r=r_{+}
respectively, with

\displaystyle r_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(r_{s}\pm\sqrt{r_{s}^{2}-4(a^{2}+r_{Q}^{2})}\right).

(92)

The surface
\Sigma_{s}
intersects the outer horizon at some
v=v_{0}
and
r=r_{+}
.
The angular speed on the surface is
\Omega_{+}=\frac{a}{r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}}
, the surface gravity is
\kappa=\frac{r_{+}-r_{-}}{2(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})}
, and the Hawking temperature is
T_{H}=\frac{\hbar\kappa}{2\pi}
.

The Kerr-Newman metric has zero scalar curvature, so the Einstein field equations read

\displaystyle R_{ab}

\displaystyle=8\pi GT_{ab},

(93)

where
T_{ab}
is the electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor (
79
).
The gauge field configuration solves the Maxwell equations (
80
) outside the horizons, and is given by

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{Q}{4\pi(\Sigma/r)}(-dv+a\sin^{2}\theta d\phi),

(94)

with the field strength components,

\displaystyle F_{ab}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q}{4\pi\Sigma^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&-(r^{2}-a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)&2ra^{2}\sin\theta\cos\theta&0\\
\cdots&0&0&-(r^{2}-a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)a\sin^{2}\theta\\
\cdots&\cdots&0&2ra(r^{2}+a^{2})\sin\theta\cos\theta\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}.

(95)

We can define the electromagnetic potential function in the exterior of the black hole to be

\displaystyle\Phi(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{Qr}{4\pi(r^{2}+a^{2})},

(96)

whose value on the horizon we refer to as
\Phi_{+}=\Phi(r_{+})
.

To find the entropy, we define
\xi^{a}
as the Killing vector field normalized as (
48
), and take the
U(1)
gauge parameter to be proportional to
the electromagnetic potential at the horizon as the following,

\displaystyle\xi^{a}\partial_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar}{l_{p}T_{H}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v}+\Omega_{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right),\qquad\epsilon=\frac{\hbar}{l_{p}T_{H}}\Phi_{+}.

(97)

We find that

\displaystyle\int_{S}-\iota_{\xi}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar}{2l_{p}T_{H}}\Big[\delta M-\delta(\Phi(r)Q)|_{r=r_{+}}\Big],

(98)

\displaystyle\int_{S}-\hat{F}[\delta\xi,\delta\epsilon]

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar}{l_{p}}\left[-\frac{1}{2}(M+\Phi_{+}Q)\delta\Big(\frac{1}{T_{H}}\Big)+J\delta\Big(\frac{\Omega_{+}}{T_{H}}\Big)+Q\delta\Big(\frac{\Phi_{+}}{T_{H}}\Big)\right].

(99)

A point worth noting is that
\delta\Phi(r)|_{r=r_{+}}
is to be distinguished from
\delta\Phi_{+}

In the former expression the variation takes place before evaluation at
r=r_{+}
and thus we take
\delta r=0
.
The latter expression is evaluated at
r=r_{+}
before the variation, and thus we get contributions from
\delta r_{+}
as
r_{+}
is a function of
M
,
J
and
Q

To see if the consistency condition (
16
) is satisfied, we note the first law of Kerr-Newman thermodynamics is

\displaystyle\delta M

\displaystyle=T_{H}\delta S+\Omega_{+}\delta J+\Phi_{+}\delta Q,

(100)

and that the Smarr formula for Kerr-Newman black hole is

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=2T_{H}S+2\Omega_{+}J+\Phi_{+}Q.

(101)

These two formulae imply the following identity,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta M}{2T_{H}}-\frac{M}{2}\delta\Big(\frac{1}{T_{H}}\Big)+J\delta\Big(\frac{\Omega_{+}}{T_{H}}\Big)-\frac{\Phi_{+}}{2T_{H}}\delta Q+\frac{Q}{2}\delta\Big(\frac{\Phi_{+}}{T_{H}}\Big)

\displaystyle=0.

(102)

Applying this to the sum of (
98

99

\displaystyle\int_{S}-\iota_{\xi}\theta-\hat{F}[\delta\xi,\delta\epsilon]

\displaystyle=\int_{S}d\Omega\left.\delta\left[\frac{1}{4\pi r^{2}}\frac{\hbar Q}{2l_{p}T_{H}}\Big(\Phi_{+}-\Phi(r)\Big)\right]\right|_{r=r_{+}},

(103)

which is indeed a total variation, so the consistency condition is satisfied.

The variation of the Noether charge conjugate to
\xi
and
\epsilon
can be computed using (
85
).
We find that the integral of
\delta\hat{F}[\xi,\epsilon
on
S

\displaystyle\int_{S}\delta\hat{F}[\xi,\epsilon]

\displaystyle=\int_{S}d\Omega\left.\delta\left[\frac{1}{4\pi r^{2}}\frac{\hbar}{l_{p}T_{H}}\left\{\frac{M}{2}-\Omega_{+}J+\left(\frac{\Phi(r)}{2}-\Phi_{+}\right)Q\right\}\right]\right|_{r=r_{+}}.

(104)

It follows from (

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\xi,\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\delta\left[\frac{\hbar}{l_{p}T_{H}}\left(\frac{M}{2}-\Omega_{+}J-\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{+}Q\right)\right].

(105)

One can read off the full charge, and hence the entropy, as

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{l_{p}}{\hbar}Q_{\xi,\epsilon}=\frac{1}{T_{H}}\left(\frac{M}{2}-\Omega_{+}J-\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{+}Q\right),

(106)

which is in accordance with the Smarr formula.

It is worth noting that the entropy coincides with the expression obtained by integrating
\hat{F}[\xi,\epsilon]
directly on
S
without taking a variation.
Also, one can compute
\delta Q_{\xi,\epsilon}
using (
34

\xi^{a}
and
\epsilon

97
),
and
h_{ab}
and
\delta A_{a}
to be the variation in the metric and gauge field respectively coming from varying
M

The result is in agreement with (

7
De Sitter Space

De Sitter spacetime is a vacuum solution of the Einstein equations with a positive cosmological constant.
It can be thought of globally as an
S^{3}
that collapses from an infinite radius in the remote past, to a minimum
radius of
l=\sqrt{\frac{3}{\Lambda}}
and then expands to infinite size in the remote future. The metric is

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-d\tau^{2}+l^{2}\cosh^{2}\tfrac{\tau}{l}\bigl(d\chi^{2}+\sin^{2}\chi(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})\bigr).

(121)

with
\chi,\theta
and
\phi
being the hyperspherical coordinates on
S^{3}
. Consider observers
moving along timelike geodesics. The interior of their past light cone does not include the entirety of
the spacetime, even in the limit as
\tau\rightarrow\infty
. In other words, these observers have a past event
horizon. Furthermore, the event horizon of each observer is different. Around each observers worldline,
one can construct a static metric that has a horizon in a way that is superficially similar to that
encountered in the Schwarzschild solution. Suppose the observer is at the north pole of the
S^{3}
where

\chi=0
. The coordinate transformation into the static system is then

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=l\cosh\frac{\tau}{l}\sin\chi

(122)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\frac{l}{2}\ln\Biggl(\frac{\cosh\frac{\tau}{l}\cos\chi+\sinh\frac{\tau}{l}}{\cosh\frac{\tau}{l}\cos\chi-\sinh\frac{\tau}{l}}\Biggr)

(123)

resulting in the metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\Bigl(1-\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}\Bigr)\,dt^{2}+\Bigl(1-\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}\Bigr)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}),

(124)

where
0<r<l
.
The observer is now at
r=0
. There is nothing special about the choice of the north pole on
S^{3}
; by using the isometries of the spacetime we conclude that any geodesic observer wold construct (
124
)
around their worldlines.
The horizon at
r=l
and a routine calculation yields a temperature of

T=\frac{\hbar}{2\pi l}
. Gibbons and Hawking applied Euclidean field theory techniques and concluded that the
entropy of this horizon is
\frac{3\pi}{\hbar G\Lambda}

Gibbons:1977mu
. The entropy is to be interpreted as a measure of
the information behind each observer’s horizon. It should be carefully noted that this entropy has nothing to
do with constituents of gravitational collapse to form a black hole and its subsequent evaporation. It also has
nothing to do with the physics of spacetime singularities as de Sitter spacetime is devoid of singularities
Hawking:1973uf
.
The entropy appears to be entirely due to the nature of spacetime.

Now we can ask if our formula for the entropy of a region of space works for the de Sitter horizon. One
can construct Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates to overcome the horizon coordinate singularity. Let
u=t-r^{*}
, where

\displaystyle r^{*}=\frac{l}{2}\ln\Biggl(\frac{l+r}{l-r}\Biggr).

(125)

The metric is now

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\Bigl(1-\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}\Bigr)du^{2}-2du\,dr+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}).

(126)

Just like the Schwarzschild black hole case,
\frac{\partial}{\partial u}
is a Killing vector and is null and
geodesic on the horizon. With the normalization (
48
), we find

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{2\pi l}{l_{p}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}.

(127)

The entropy is then given by

\displaystyle S=\frac{l_{p}}{\hbar}Q_{\xi}=-\frac{1}{16\pi l_{p}}\int*d\xi

(128)

with the integral being taken over the horizon at some instant of retarded time
u
.
Evaluation yields

\displaystyle S=\frac{3\pi}{\hbar G\Lambda}

(129)

in agreement with the result of Gibbons and Hawking.

8
Kottler Spacetime

Next, we apply the formula to a case which has both a black hole horizon and a cosmological horizon:
the Kottler spacetime
Kottler:1918cxc
.

We consider a metric of the form

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-V(r)dv^{2}+2dvdr+r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2},\qquad V(r)\equiv 1-\frac{2GM}{r}-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2},

(130)

where
M
is the mass of the black hole,
\Lambda>0
is the positive cosmological constant, and we have defined the advanced time coordinate
v=t+r^{*}
with the tortoise coordinate defined by
\frac{dr^{*}}{dr}=V^{-1}
.
The horizon radii are determined by the cubic equation

\displaystyle V(r)=1-\frac{2GM}{r}-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}=0.

(131)

We restrict our attention to the case
0<\Lambda(3GM)^{2}<1
, for which the equation (
131
) has one negative solution
r_{-}
and two positive solutions
r_{1},r_{2}
where
r_{1}<r_{2}
.
The spacetime exhibits a black hole horizon at
r=r_{1}
, a cosmological horizon at
r=r_{2}
, and a static region in between.
It is convenient to define a parameter
\beta
by

\displaystyle\cos\beta

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{1/2}(3GM),\qquad 0<\beta<\frac{\pi}{2},

(132)

in terms of which the three solutions are given by

\displaystyle r_{-}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\Lambda}}\cos\frac{\beta}{3},\qquad r_{1}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\Lambda}}\cos\left(\frac{\beta}{3}+\frac{\pi}{3}\right),\qquad r_{2}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\Lambda}}\cos\left(\frac{\beta}{3}-\frac{\pi}{3}\right).

(133)

Consider the time-like Killing vector
\partial_{v}
.
Its geodesic equation is

\displaystyle(\partial_{v})^{b}\nabla_{b}(\partial_{v})^{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r}\left(1-\Lambda r^{2}\right)(\partial_{v})^{a}=\pm\kappa(\partial_{v})^{a}.

(134)

We evaluate this expression at the two horizon radii
r=r_{i}
,
i=1,2
, and refer to the quantities
\kappa_{i}
as the surface gravities associated with the respective horizons,
up to the fact that there is no natural way to normalize this null vector to unit length at “infinity”.
By re-organizing terms in (
131
) to

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\Lambda r^{3}-2GM=-r(1-\Lambda r^{2}),

(135)

one finds that
1-\Lambda r_{1}^{2}>0
and
1-\Lambda r_{2}^{2}<0
.
Thus the expressions for
\kappa_{i}
are

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}=\frac{1}{2r_{1}}(1-\Lambda r_{1}^{2}),\qquad\kappa_{2}=\frac{1}{2r_{2}}(\Lambda r_{2}^{2}-1).

(136)

We first compute the entropy of associated to the black hole.
We define a Killing vector
\xi_{1}
by rescaling
\partial_{v}
using the normalization (
48
) at the black hole horizon
r=r_{1}
,

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{a}\partial_{a}=\frac{2\pi}{l_{p}\kappa_{1}}\partial_{v}.

(137)

Then, by direct computation using (
135
), one obtains

\displaystyle(*d\xi)_{\theta\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{l_{p}\kappa_{1}}\left(\frac{2}{3}\Lambda r^{3}-2GM\right)=\frac{2\pi}{l_{p}\kappa_{1}}r(\Lambda r^{2}-1).

(138)

Therefore, we find that our formula for the entropy applied to any section
v=v_{0}
of the the black hole horizon
r=r_{1}
evaluates to

\displaystyle S(r=r_{1})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{16\pi l_{p}}\int_{r=r_{1}}*d\xi_{1}=\frac{4\pi r_{1}^{2}}{4l_{p}^{2}}=\frac{A(r=r_{1})}{4G\hbar},

(139)

which corresponds to the correct entropy from the area law.

To compute the entropy associated to the cosmological horizon
r=r_{2}
, we define another Killing vector
\xi_{2}
normalized as (
48
) at
r=r_{2}
,

\displaystyle\xi_{2}^{a}\partial_{a}=-\frac{2\pi}{l_{p}\kappa_{2}}\partial_{v}.

(140)

The formula for the entropy evaluated at a section
v=v_{0}
of the cosmological horizon
r=r_{2}
evaluates to

\displaystyle S(r=r_{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{16\pi l_{p}}\int_{r=r_{2}}*d\xi_{2}=\frac{4\pi r_{2}^{2}}{4l_{p}^{2}}=\frac{A(r=r_{2})}{4G\hbar},

(141)

which agrees with the area law associated with the cosmological horizon.

9
Conclusions and Speculations

We have examined the proposal of
Wald:1993nt
;
Iyer:1994ys
that the gravitational entropy of a horizon can be
described by a Noether charge. We believe we have put this proposal onto a more general footing by relating it the
proposal of Bousso
Bousso:2002ju
where the gravitational entropy of a spatial region is determined by the
lightsheet at the boundary of that region. We have also examined the behavior of the variation of the Noether charge
as described by covariant phase space methods. We find that our treatment reproduces the first law of black hole horizons
and we have illustrated this by explicit calculations in the Kerr-Newman spacetime. Our treatment also reproduces
the entropy of the cosmological horizon in de Sitter space as first described by Gibbons and Hawking
Gibbons:1977mu
.
Finally, we apply our method to black holes in de Sitter space and again find the expected entropy.
In all cases we have here examined, the entropy is given by

\displaystyle S=\frac{A}{4G\hbar}.

(142)

It should be noted that the entropy is of order
\hbar^{-1}
and in general, one would expect modifications
to this formula from corrections of higher orders in
\hbar
.

The apparent generality of our proposal suggests two areas that require further exploration. The first is that our
picture is sufficiently general that it appears that it could apply to more general regions of spacetime than horizons.
In fact, it looks as if it could apply to any spatial region, as first suggested by Bousso. So we believe
that an examination of quantum extremal surfaces
Engelhardt:2014gca
and of cosmological models might
prove profitable. A second area to examine is, of course, contributions of higher order in
\hbar
. For example,
a bath of thermal radiation has an entropy that comes from a one-loop contribution to the partition function
and so is of higher order in
\hbar
than the contributions we have considered here.

All of this however does not shed any light on the microscopic origin of gravitational entropy. It is often said
that gravitational entropy represents the microstates of what is hidden behind a horizon. However, one could
reasonably expect that such a quantity would depend on the spectrum of elementary particles. To leading order
in
\hbar
, this does not happen although such a dependence will be found in higher order contributions.
Sometimes this difficulty is referred to as the species problem.
To us, it seems more likely that gravitational entropy is telling us something fundamental about the
quantum nature of spacetime.

Appendix A
Appendix — Interlude to the variation of
\xi
.

In this section, we briefly review the black hole entropy computation in the literature and introduce the role of the variation of the vector field in this context.

For rotating black holes, the vector field
\xi^{a}
takes the form

\displaystyle\xi^{a}={\bf t}^{a}+\Omega_{+}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}^{a}

(143)

where
{\bf t}=\partial_{t}=\partial_{v}
and
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=\partial_{\phi}
are the two Killing vectors.
The diffeomorphism
\xi
is a symmetry in the sense that
{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi=0
on all fields, so

\displaystyle\omega(\phi,\delta\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)=0.

(144)

Consider a Cauchy slice
\Sigma
bounded by two spheres, one at
r=\infty
and the other at the bifurcation surface
B
, where
v=-\infty
and
r=r_{+}
.
Integrating
\omega(\phi,\delta\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)
on
\Sigma
yields zero, so

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\delta Q_{\xi}=\left(-\int_{B}+\int_{\infty}\right)(\delta\hat{F}[\xi]-\iota_{\xi}\theta-\hat{F}[\delta\xi]),

(145)

where the minus sign accounts for the opposite orientation.
Thus

\displaystyle\int_{B}\delta\hat{F}[\xi]-\iota_{\xi}\theta-\hat{F}[\delta\xi]=\int_{\infty}\delta\hat{F}[\xi]-\iota_{\xi}\theta-\hat{F}[\delta\xi]

(146)

The last term is missing in the earlier literature
Lee:1990nz
;
Wald:1993nt
;
Iyer:1994ys
;
Wald:1999wa
as they consider vectors such that
\delta\xi^{a}=0
.
The authors compute the integral at
\infty
to get
\delta M-\Omega_{+}\delta J
, and use this to deduce that the integral on
B
is
T\delta S
.
On the bifurcation surface,
\iota_{\xi}\theta
does not contribute to the integral.
This is because

1.

by linearity
\iota_{\xi}\theta=\iota_{\bf t}\theta+\Omega_{+}\iota_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\theta
.

2.

{\bf t}=\partial_{v}
vanishes on
B
, so
\iota_{\bf t}\theta=0
; for instance if we define
\tilde{v}\equiv e^{v}
, then
\partial_{v}=\tilde{v}\partial_{\tilde{v}}
and this vanishes at
B
where
\tilde{v}=0
.

3.

{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
is tangent to
B
, so the pullback of
\iota_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\theta
to
B
is zero.

It follows that
\int_{B}\delta\hat{F}[\xi]
is
T\delta S
.
Thus, up to the Hawking temperature,
\int_{B}\hat{F}[\xi]
without the
\delta
is the full entropy.
If we consider the integral
\int_{S}\hat{F}[\xi]
on any section
S
at
v=v_{0}
,
r=r_{+}
, then using
\hat{J}=d\hat{F}
and Stokes’ theorem we can write

\displaystyle\int_{S}\hat{F}[\xi]-\int_{B}\hat{F}[\xi]=\int_{\overline{BS}}\hat{J}[\xi]

(147)

where
\overline{BS}
is the lower segment of the outer horizon extending from
B
(
v=-\infty
) to
S
(
v=v_{0}
).
But the r.h.s. is zero, since by definition

\displaystyle\hat{J}[\xi]=\theta(\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)-\iota_{\xi}L,

(148)

where
{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi=0
implies
\theta(\phi,{\cal L}_{\xi}\phi)=0
by linearity, and the pullback of
\iota_{\xi}L=0
to
\overline{BS}
vanishes since
\xi
is tangent to the future horizon.
Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{S}\hat{F}[\xi]=\int_{B}\hat{F}[\xi],

(149)

and that the entropy, up to Hawking temperature, can be computed using any section
v=v_{0}
of the horizon.

Note that this method does not work if we try to integrate expressions like the following on
S
(not the bifurcation surface
B
):

\displaystyle\int_{S}\delta\hat{F}[\xi]-\iota_{\xi}\theta.

(150)

This expression does not give us the entropy, because
\iota_{\xi}\theta
does not necessarily vanish on
S\neq B
.
What we find is that the following expression

\displaystyle\int_{S}\delta\hat{F}[\xi]-\iota_{\xi}\theta-\hat{F}[\delta\xi]

(151)

yields (the variation of) the correct entropy.
From the previous argument that the entropy is given by
\int_{S}\hat{F}
, this implies that the last two terms on the r.h.s. cancel out.
This is exactly what we find for Schwarzschild, Kerr, and Kerr-Newman cases.

First Nucleon Gluon PDF from Large Momentum Effective Theory Preprint: MSUHEP-25-024
William Good, Fei Yao, Huey-Wen Lin

Abstract
We report the first nucleon gluon parton distribution function (PDF) using Large-Momentum Effective Theory (LaMET). We focus on the gluon operator which was demonstrated to have the best signal-to-noise in the previous attempt Good et al. 2025 in computing gluon PDFs using LaMET. We compute the corresponding Wilson coefficients needed for the hybrid-renormalized matrix elements and the matching kernel to convert the quasi-PDF to the lightcone one at the one-loop level. We demonstrate that with the proper Wilson coefficients in place, the counterterms for the renormalization are independent of the hadron and mass within statistical error. Using the resulting renormalization, we then compute the nucleon PDF using a HISQ ensemble generated by the MILC collaboration with N f = 2 + 1 + 1 N_{f}=2+1+1 , a ≈ 0.12 a\approx 0.12 fm, a valence pion mass of 690 MeV, and high levels of gauge link smearing. Despite the physics effects of the large-pion mass and gauge link smearing, this calculation provides excellent proof of principle and compares reasonably with selected global fit results.



I
Introduction

Parton distribution functions (PDFs) play a fundamental role in high-energy particle physics, encoding the momentum distributions of quarks and gluons within protons and neutrons.
These functions are essential inputs for making precise predictions at hadron colliders such as the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), directly impacting the interpretation of a wide range of Standard Model (SM) and beyond-the-SM (BSM) processes.
Among the PDFs, the gluon distribution is particularly crucial due to the dominant role gluons play in the proton’s momentum at high energies and in processes like Higgs-boson production and jet formation.
While quark PDFs are relatively well constrained by deep inelastic scattering and Drell-Yan data, the gluon PDF remains less precisely determined, especially in the intermediate and large momentum fraction (
x
) regions.
This uncertainty limits the precision of theoretical predictions for key observables at the LHC.
Improving the accuracy of the gluon PDF, therefore, has the potential to significantly enhance the sensitivity of searches for BSM physics and refine our understanding of the dynamics of quantum-chromodynamics (QCD) in hadronic collisions
Amoroso et al. 2022
.

Lattice QCD (LQCD) provides a first-principles approach to studying the nonperturbative regime of the strong interaction by discretizing spacetime on a finite lattice.
Although LQCD was traditionally limited to the calculation of a few hadron structure observables such as moments of PDFs, recent breakthroughs have enabled LQCD calculations to access the Bjorken-
x
–dependence of PDFs.
Methods such as Large-Momentum Effective Theory (LaMET)
Ji 2013
and pseudo-PDFs
Radyushkin 2017
are the most popular approaches to extracting lightcone correlations from lattice observables.
Over the past decade or so, substantial progress has been made not only in PDFs but also other kinds of three-dimensional structure, such as generalized parton distributions (GPDs);
we refer interested readers to the reviews in Refs.
Ji et al. 2021a
;
Constantinou et al. 2021
;
Lin 2023
.
However, the majority of lattice
x
-dependent results focus on the isovector quark distributions, and there are few works on the gluon PDFs.

The extraction of gluon PDFs in lattice QCD remains more challenging, due to their inherently noisier signals and the need to handle more complicated operator mixing.
Nevertheless, encouraging progress has been reported in recent years, mostly using the pseudo-PDF framework, obtaining the nucleon
Fan et al. 2021
;
Fan et al. 2023
;
Khan et al. 2021
;
Delmar et al. 2023
;
Egerer et al. 2022
, pion
Good et al. 2024
and kaon
Salas-Chavira et al. 2022
gluon PDFs.
However, the pseudo-PDF method often requires the use of phenomenology-inspired
x
-dependent PDF models to fit the ratios of lattice matrix elements; though, there are recent efforts to reduce model dependence in this methodology
Karpie et al. 2019
;
Karpie et al. 2021
.
LaMET, on the other hand, uses a Fourier transform of the lattice matrix elements to first obtain quasi-PDFs, which can be matched to the lightcone PDFs via a matching procedure accurate to powers of the inverse parton momentum.
Model dependence comes into play in LaMET in the extrapolation of large-distance matrix elements to obtain a reliable Fourier transform.
It would be of great community interest to obtain the gluon PDF through LaMET as well so that we can achieve better control of the lattice systematics by comparing results from the two methodologies.

Recently, MSULat group aimed to advance the LQCD calculations of gluon PDFs using LaMET
Good et al. 2025
.
We used high-statistics measurements at two pion masses (approximately 310 and 690 MeV) on a lattice ensemble with a spacing of about 0.12 fm.
The work explored the application of Coulomb gauge fixing to enhance signal quality at large Wilson-line separations, demonstrating its potential to improve the reliability of the calculations of gluon matrix elements.
By analyzing multiple gluon operators and comparing the resulting matrix elements for both nucleon and pion states, the study identified the most effective operator for extracting gluon PDFs.
However, without the perturbative QCD (pQCD) calculations needed for hybrid-ratio renormalization of this operator, we could only perform an analysis with an estimation of the renormalization.

In this work, we took the lattice gluon matrix elements from our previous work
Good et al. 2025
and calculated the hybrid-renormalization-scheme matching coefficients to obtain the first lightcone gluon PDF of the nucleon using the LaMET approach.
We report the next-to-leading-order (NLO) Wilson coefficients for the hybrid renormalization scheme for the gluon operator in Sec.
II
.
We determine the counterterms in the hybrid renormalization and apply them to renormalize the LaMET matrix elements in Sec.
III
.
We compare the renormalized matrix elements with those estimated from phenomenology, and present the first nucleon gluon PDF from LaMET, also compared with phenomenological fits.
We conclude and consider the future prospects for gluon PDFs in Sec.
IV
.

II
Formulation for LaMET and Hybrid Renormalization

The leading-twist gluon PDFs
g(x)
of a hadron is defined as
Collins et al. 1989
,

\displaystyle g(x,\mu)=\frac{1}{2xP^{+}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi^{-}}{2\pi}\,

\displaystyle e^{-ixP^{+}\xi^{-}}\langle P|F_{a}^{+\mu}(\xi^{-})

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{W}_{ab}(\xi^{-},0)\,F_{b,\mu}^{+}(0)|P\rangle,

(1)

where
|P\rangle
denotes unpolarized hadron with momentum
P
along the
z
-direction. Here,
x
is the longitudinal momentum fraction carried by the gluon, and
\mu
denotes the renormalization scale in the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme. The light-cone coordinates are given by
\xi^{\pm}=(\xi^{t}\pm\xi^{z})/\sqrt{2}
and gauge invariance is ensured by the Wilson line,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{ab}(\xi^{-},0)=\mathcal{P}\,{\rm exp}\big[-ig\int_{0}^{\xi^{-}}\mathrm{d}\eta^{-}\,A_{c}^{+}(\eta^{-})t^{c}\big]_{ab},

(2)

where the indices
\{a,b\}
denote the color components in the adjoint representation of SU(3), and
t^{c}
are the generators of the gauge group.

According to LaMET, the gluon PDF can be extracted by calculating a spatially correlated matrix element:

\displaystyle h^{\text{B}}(z,P_{z})=\langle P|

\displaystyle F^{ti}(z)\mathcal{W}(z,0)F^{t}_{i}(0)

\displaystyle-F^{ij}(z)\mathcal{W}(z,0)F_{ij}(0)|P\rangle,

(3)

where
i
and
j
run over the transverse indices
\{x,y\}
, only.
Hereafter, we adopt the convention of omitting color indices for notational simplicity.

Although the local case does not strictly admit a multiplicative renormalization, we do not incorporate information from the local region, as the result is normalized and therefore insensitive to local contributions. The bare non-local quasi-LF correlation can be multiplicatively renormalized as
Zhang et al. 2019

\displaystyle h^{\text{B}}(z,P_{z})=Z_{L}e^{-\bar{\delta}m|z|}\,h^{\text{R}}(z,P_{z}).

(4)

where
h^{\text{R}}(z,P_{z})
is the renormalized quasi-LF correlation that contains both
z
-independent logarithmic UV divergences, captured by
Z_{L}
, and
z
-dependent linear divergences from Wilson-line self-energy, denoted by
\bar{\delta}m
.
To remove these divergences, we adopt the hybrid renormalization scheme
Ji et al. 2021b
, treating short and long distances separately:
at short distances, we apply the ratio scheme
Radyushkin 2018
using rest-frame matrix elements.
While at long distances, we use mass renormalization by fitting the zero-momentum matrix
h^{\text{B}}(z,0)
to capture linear divergence effects.
The resulting renormalized quasi-LF correlation is expressed as:

h^{\mathrm{R}}(z,P_{z})=\begin{cases}\!\displaystyle\!\frac{h^{\mathrm{B}}(0,0)}{h^{\mathrm{B}}(0,P_{z})}\frac{h^{\mathrm{B}}(z,P_{z})}{h^{\mathrm{B}}(z,0)}&z\leq z_{s}\\[12.0pt]
\!\displaystyle\!\frac{h^{\mathrm{B}}(0,0)}{h^{\mathrm{B}}(0,P_{z})}\frac{h^{\mathrm{B}}(z,P_{z})}{h^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{s},0)}e^{(\delta m+m_{0})(z-z_{s})}&z>z_{s}\end{cases}

(5)

where
z_{s}
separates short and long distances, the terms
\delta m
and
m_{0}
correspond to the mass renormalization and the renormalon ambiguity, respectively, and are introduced to cancel the linear divergence arising from the self-energy of the Wilson line. In the limit
z_{s}\to\infty
, the expression reduces to the standard ratio-scheme renormalization, which neglects the Wilson line self-energy contribution.

The availability of multiple lattice spacings allows for a separate determination of
\delta m
and
m_{0}
via fitting procedures
Ji et al. 2021b
;
Huo et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2023
. However, in the case of a single lattice spacing, it is more practical to fit only their sum,
\delta m+m_{0}
, by matching the
P_{z}=0
bare matrix elements to the perturbatively calculated “Wilson coefficients”. The Wilson coefficient can be derived from Ref.
Balitsky et al. 2022
, gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(z,\mu)=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{2\pi}\left(\frac{5}{6}\ln\left(\frac{z^{2}\mu^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\right)+\frac{3}{2}\right).

(6)

Then we determine the combined parameter
\delta m+m_{0}
in the short-distance region by fitting to the relation:

(\delta m+m_{0})z+I_{0}\approx\ln\left[\frac{\mathcal{H}(z,\mu)}{h^{\mathrm{B}}(z,0)}\right],

(7)

where
I_{0}
denotes a
z
-independent constant.

The renormalized quasi-LF correlation function can be transformed into the so-called quasi-PDF via a Fourier transform. Notice that we neglect the contribution of mixing with quarks in this work, as it has been shown to be small in the pseudo-PDF studies
Fan et al. 2023
. Therefore, the gluon quasi-PDF is related to the light-cone PDF through a factorization formula as following:

\displaystyle x\tilde{g}\left(x,P_{z}\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dy}{|y|}C\left(\frac{x}{y},\frac{\mu}{yP_{z}}\right)yg(y,\mu)+h.t.,

(8)

where
g(y,\mu)
is the light-cone gluon PDF at the renormalization scale
\mu
,
C\left(x/y,\mu/(yP_{z})\right)
represents the perturbative matching coefficient, and
h.t.
denotes higher-twist corrections suppressed by powers of
1/P_{z}
. The perturbative matching coefficient in coordinate space up to the NLO in the ratio scheme can be found in Refs.
Balitsky et al. 2020
;
Balitsky et al. 2022
. Then we obtain the corresponding momentum-space kernel directly via a Fourier transform, following the procedure outlined in Ref.
Yao et al. 2023
. The matching coefficients in the ratio and hybrid schemes are given as follows:

C^{\text{ratio}}\left(\xi,\frac{\mu}{p_{z}}\right)=\delta(1-\xi)+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{2\pi}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(2\frac{(1-\xi+\xi^{2})^{2}}{1-\xi}\ln\left(\frac{\xi}{\xi-1}\right)+\frac{11-28\xi+18\xi^{2}-12\xi^{3}}{6(1-\xi)}\right)_{+(1)},&\xi>1\\[6.45831pt]
\left(2\frac{(1-\xi+\xi^{2})^{2}}{1-\xi}\left[-\ln(\frac{\mu^2}{4{p_z}^2})+\ln\left[\xi(1-\xi)\right]\right]-\frac{15-56\xi+102\xi^{2}-96\xi^{3}+48\xi^{4}}{6(1-\xi)}\right)_{+(1)},&0<\xi<1\\[6.45831pt]
\left(-2\frac{(1-\xi+\xi^{2})^{2}}{1-\xi}\ln\left(\frac{-\xi}{1-\xi}\right)-\frac{11-28\xi+18\xi^{2}-12\xi^{3}}{6(1-\xi)}\right)_{+(1)},&\xi<0\end{array}\right.

(9)

C^{\text{hyb.}}\left(\xi,\frac{\mu}{p_{z}}\right)=C^{\text{ratio}}\left(\xi,\frac{\mu}{p_{z}}\right)+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{2\pi}\frac{5}{6}\bigg[-\frac{1}{|1-\xi|}+\frac{2\text{Si}((1-\xi)z_{s}p_{z})}{\pi(1-\xi)}\bigg]_{+}.

(10)

where
\xi=\frac{x}{y}
and
p_{z}
denotes the momentum carried by the parton, which is given by
yP_{z}
.

III
Numerical Results and Discussions

We use the high-statistics data from Ref.
Good et al. 2025
on one ensemble with lattice spacing
a\approx 0.12
fm at valence pion masses
M_{\pi}\approx 310
and
690

Bazavov et al. 2013
using 2+1+1 flavors of highly improved staggered quarks (HISQ)
Follana et al. 2007
with the lattice volume of
24^{3}\times 64

We use Wilson clover fermions in the valence sector and tune the valence quark mass to reproduce the light and strange mass in the HISQ sea.
We computed 1,296,640 two-point correlators across 1013 configurations for both the nucleon and pion at the light and heavy pion masses.
The nucleons and pions are referred to as the light (
N_{l}
) and strange (
N_{s}
) nucleons, the pion (
\pi

\eta_{s}

We will focus mostly on the nucleons but we include the pion data to understand how the external states affect the calculation of
\delta m+m_{0}

We measured two sets of gluon loops with 5 steps of hypercubic (HYP5) smearing and with Wilson flow with a flow time of
T=3a^{2}
(Wilson-3) on the gauge links.

We see that the low momentum matrix elements seem to fall off much faster and disagree more significantly with the phenomenological matrix elements, with the largest momentum matrix elements beginning to converge more quickly in the strange nucleon case.

IV
Conclusion and Outlook

We have presented the first nucleon gluon PDF through LaMET with hybrid-ratio renormalization, albeit with a heavy pion mass and large gauge link smearing, which could affect the physics.
Based on the prior work toward a LaMET gluon PDF
Good et al. 2025
, we focused on a single gluon operator
O^{(3)}
defined in Eq. 6 of Ref.
Good et al. 2025
, which has been used in unpolarized gluon studies using pseudo-PDF method
Fan et al. 2021
;
Fan and Lin 2021
;
Khan et al. 2021
;
Salas-Chavira et al. 2022
;
Fan et al. 2023
;
Delmar et al. 2023
;
Good et al. 2024
.
We computed the one-loop Wilson coefficient needed for hybrid renormalization for this operator and the corresponding one-loop matching kernel.
Using these Wilson coefficients, we found
\delta m+m_{0}
to be consistent between the pion and nucleon, whether at the light or strange mass, in contrast with previous results for the
O^{(1)}
and
O^{(2)}
operators in Ref.
Good et al. 2025
.
We computed the nucleon gluon PDFs with hadron boost momentum
P_{z}=1.71
GeV and compared with the nucleon gluon PDFs from the CT18
Hou et al. 2021
and JAM24
Anderson et al. 2024
global fits as a small sample.
We find a slightly negative, but within
1\sigma
of zero, central value of our gluon PDF at large
x
, which is likely due to the effects of the large gauge link smearing.
However, we find that our PDF is within about
1\sigma
of the JAM24 gluon PDF in the
x>0.3
region and of the CT18 PDF in the
x<0.5
region, which shows that our PDF falls comfortably within the range of those found in global fits.
This study demonstrates a strong proof-of-principle for using large momentum effective theory to calculate gluon PDFs, which was long thought to be out of reach.
We believe that this work provides strong motivation to use time and resources to replace the large pion mass and smearing with noise-reduction techniques with fewer physics effects to obtain more reliable gluon PDFs from LaMET.
We plan to improve the current calculation by using self-renormalization scheme with zero-momentum boosted matrix elements with multiple lattice spacings, and to improve the systematics on the gluon PDFs by taking the continuum-physical limit.
As the errors become smaller, there will also be a need to understand scale variation in the gluon PDFs from LaMET.

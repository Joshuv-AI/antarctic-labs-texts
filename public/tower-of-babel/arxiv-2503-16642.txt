Turing Instability Suppressed and Induced by Multiplicative Noise in Brusselator System
Qasim Khan, Anthony Suen, Bao Quoc Tang

Abstract
The effect of multiplicative noise to the Turing instability of the Brusselator system is investigated. We show that when the noise acts on both of the concentrations with the same intensities, then the Turing instability is suppressed provided that the intensities are sufficiently large. This aligns with the stabilizing effect of multiplicative noise in partial differential equations. Utilizing the linearized system, we can quantify the magnitude of noise which stabilizes the system. On the other hand, when the noise is involving only one concentration, then the Turing instability can be triggered with suitable intensities. These are confirmed by numerical simulations.



Keywords:
Brusselator system; Turing instability; Multiplicative noise; Instability induced/suppressed by noise.

1
Introduction

Reaction-diffusion system (RDS) provides a compelling and useful modeling tool for many problems in real life. One of the main reasons is that, even a simple RDS can exhibit many complex patterns as shown in the well known work
[
Pea93
]
. Revealing the mechanism for the formation of these patterns is the objective of many works since then or even earlier. One prime example is the famous work of Turing
[
Tur52
]
where he argued that one possible mechanism is the complicated interplay between diffusion and (chemical) reactions in terms of activators and inhibitors. More precisely, it was shown that if the activator has short range diffusion and inhibitor has long range diffusion, then the stable spatially homogeneous equilibrium becomes unstable, consequently leading to spatially inhomogeneous patterns. This has initiated a now classical topic named
Turing instability
, and is the subject of a vast number of research works (see refer the interested reader to the book
[
Mur07
]
for more details).

The Turing instability has been confirmed in certain real life biological systems, see for instance
[
MOK10
]
where experiments on zebrafish stripes showed that they are formed as an interaction of two types of cells, playing roles of activator and inhibitor, following Turing mechanism. However, the different scales of diffusion might not be “physical” in biological systems cf.
[
HG21
]
, so researchers looked for additional features that suppress or trigger Turing instability, including systems in time evolving domains
[
MGM10
,
VGKK21
]
, systems with spatially inhomogeneous coefficients
[
VG21
,
KGK19
]
, or the effect of random noise. For instance, at the level of reaction-diffusion master equations,
[
HH12
]
showed that noise can induce spatial patterns for chemical reactions, and
[
DMPT18
]
studied the effects of noise on patterns for RDS or population dynamics. Some existing works looked at the effect of additive noise, for instance,
[
KBR24
]
showed induced pattern for thermochemical kinetics,
[
BJG17
]
investigated giant amplification of noise in fluctuation-induced pattern formation, and
[
BKR21
]
considered the sensitivity of Turing patterns. For multiplicative noise,
[
ZWSI17
]
used some explicit stochastic noises and Taylor expansion to obtain their effect on Turing bifurcation, while
[
XGAS23
]
investigated the effects of noise on critical points of Turing instability on networks. Stochastic Turing patterns triggered by noise have been even confirmed through experiments in
[
KML+18
]
.

For scalar PDE, multiplicative noise has been known to have stabilizing effect, see e.g.
[
Kwi99
,
CLM01
,
CKS06
,
CCLR07
]
. Especially, in
[
Kwi99
]
, by looking at the Fourier analysis of a linear PDE, it was shown that the stabilization is because the multiplicative noise shifts the spectral of the Laplacian to the left with the magnitude proportional to the (square of) noise intensities. In a way, this could be seen as scaling of the range of diffusion. For systems, it is therefore natural to ask:
what happens if the scaling for different species are the same or different?
In this paper, we investigate this question by looking at the PDE Brusselator system with multiplicative noise. It will be demonstrated that, if the noise intensities are the same for different species, multiplicative noise has suppression effect on Turing instability. On the other hand, if the noise is acting on only one species, it could induce instability even if the short range activator - long range inhibitor is not fulfilled for deterministic systems. The suppression effects are proved by analyzing a linearized systems and using some Fourier analysis, while the induced instability is shown via numerical simulations. It is remarked that the analysis can be carried out for other RDS concerning Turing instability. We would like to also point out several works studying the effect of noise in Brusselator system in different contexts, see e.g.
[
YBJK97
,
BR00
,
EOM24
]
and references therein.

The manuscript is structured as follows: Section
2
presents the stochastic Brusselator with multiplicative noise. The linear analysis is given in Section
3
, while Section
4
is devoted to numerical simulations for the nonlinear system. In the last Section we give some discussion and outlook.

2
Stochastic Brusselator system

The Brusselator is characterized by the reactions

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{X}

\displaystyle 2\mathsf{X}+\mathsf{Y}\to 3\mathsf{X}

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}+\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Y}+\mathsf{D}

\displaystyle\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{E}

for certain chemicals
\mathsf{A},\mathsf{B},\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{D}
and
\mathsf{E}
. It is assumed that
\mathsf{A},\mathsf{B}
are in large excess, and therefore their concentrations can be taken as constants, say
A>0
and
B>0
. We arrive at the following reaction-diffusion system for the concentrations of
X
and
Y
, which are denoted by
u
and
v
, respectively,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=d_{u}\Delta u+A-(B+1)u+u^{2}v,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
\partial_{t}v=d_{v}\Delta v+Bu-u^{2}v,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
\nabla u\cdot\nu(x)=\nabla v\cdot\nu(x)=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\;v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where
d_{u},d_{v}>0
are positive diffusion coefficients. Here
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, is an open, bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
(in case
n\geq 2
), and
\nu(x)
is the outward normal vector at
x\in\partial\Omega
.
Due to the homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions, it is straightforward to see that
(u_{\infty},v_{\infty})=(A,B/A)
is the unique spatially homogeneous steady state of (
1
). Moreover, if
B<1+A^{2}
, this steady state is asymptotically stable. It is also well-known, see e.g.
[
Per15
]
that in this case, Turing instability occurs when
d_{u}\ll d_{v}
, leading to the formation of patterns, while the steady state
(u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
is still asymptotically stable when
d_{u}\approx d_{v}
. In this work, we are interested in the effect of multiplicative noise concerning this Turing instability. More precisely, we consider the following
stochastic Brusselator system with multiplicative noise

\begin{cases}du=(d_{u}\Delta u+A-(B+1)u+u^{2}v)dt+\sigma_{u}(u-u_{\infty})dW_{t},&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
dv=(d_{v}\Delta v+Bu-u^{2}v)dt+\sigma_{v}(v-v_{\infty})dW_{t},&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
\nabla u\cdot\nu=\nabla v\cdot\nu=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\;v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(2)

where
W_{t}
is a real-valued Wiener process, and
\sigma_{u},\sigma_{v}\in\mathbb{R}
are noise intensities. The forms of the noise are so that
(u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
is also a steady state of the stochastic system.

3
Linear stability analysis for the stochastic system

The linearized system of (
2
) around the equilibrium
(u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
reads as

\begin{cases}du=(d_{u}\Delta u+(B-1)u+A^{2}v)dt+\sigma_{u}udW_{t},&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
dv=(d_{v}\Delta v-Bu-A^{2}v)dt+\sigma_{v}vdW_{t},&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
\nabla u\cdot\nu=\nabla v\cdot\nu=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)-u_{\infty},\;v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)-\infty,&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(3)

Denoting
a=B-1,b=A^{2},c=-B
and
d=-A^{2}
, we rewrite the linearized system in the general form

\begin{cases}du=(d_{u}\Delta u+au+bv)dt+\sigma_{u}udW_{t},\\
dv=(d_{v}\Delta v+cu+dv)dt+\sigma_{v}vdW_{t}.\end{cases}

(4)

For simplicity, we consider the case where
u
is the activator and
v
is the inhibitor, meaning that
a>0
and
d<0
, which consequently imposes
B>1
. In this case, it is well-known that, if
d_{u}/d_{v}
is sufficiently small, then Turing instability occurs (see e.g.
[
Per15
, Theorem 7.1]
).

First we have a look at the corresponding deterministic differential system reads as

\begin{pmatrix}\bar{u}\\
\bar{v}\end{pmatrix}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bar{u}\\
\bar{v}\end{pmatrix}=:M\begin{pmatrix}\bar{u}\\
\bar{v}\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

Due to the condition
B<1+A^{2}
, it follows that

\mathcal{T}:=\text{Trace}(M)=B-1-A^{2}<0,\quad\mathcal{D}:=\text{Det}(M)=A^{2}>0.

Therefore, the zero solution to the ODE system (
5
) is exponentially stable, i.e. there exist constants
C,\lambda_{\text{ODE}}>0
such that

|\bar{u}(t)|+|\bar{v}(t)|\leq Ce^{-\lambda_{\text{ODE}}\cdot t}\quad\forall t\geq 0.

To analyze the stochastic system, we consider the eigenvalue problem with Neumann boundary condition

\begin{cases}-\Delta\varphi=\mu\varphi,&x\in\Omega,\\
\nabla\varphi\cdot\nu=0,&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

It is well-known that there is a system of eigenvalue-eigenfunction
(\mu_{k},\varphi_{k})_{k\geq 0}
such that

•

0=\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}\leq\mu_{3}\leq\longrightarrow+\infty
,

•

\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

We use the same basic decomposition technique for
u(t)
and
v(t)
, i.e.,

u(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}g_{k}(t)\varphi_{k}(x),\ \ \ v(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}h_{k}(t)\varphi_{k}(x).

(6)

Inserting these into system (
4
) then multiply by
\varphi_{k}
and integrate over
\Omega
, we get

\displaystyle d\begin{bmatrix}g_{k}(t)\\
h_{k}(t)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}a-d_{u}\mu_{k}&b\\
c&d-d_{v}\mu_{k}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}g_{k}(t)\\
h_{k}(t)\\
\end{bmatrix}dt+\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{u}&0\\
0&\sigma_{v}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}g_{k}(t)\\
h_{k}(t)\\
\end{bmatrix}dW_{t}.

(7)

By defining

V_{k}(t):=\begin{bmatrix}g_{k}(t)\\
h_{k}(t)\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{A}_{k}:=\begin{bmatrix}a-d_{u}\mu_{k}&b\\
c&d-d_{v}\mu_{k}\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{B}:=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{u}&0\\
0&\sigma_{v}\\
\end{bmatrix},

we arrive to the following stochastic differential system for each
k\geq 1

d{V_{k}(t)}=\mathbf{A}_{k}V_{k}(t)dt+\mathbf{B}V_{k}(t)dW_{t}.

(8)

In the following subsections, we analyze the case of same noise intensities
\sigma_{u}=\sigma_{v}
and different intensities
\sigma_{u}=0,\sigma_{v}\neq 0
separately.

3.1
The case of the same noise intensities

If
\sigma_{u}=\sigma_{v}=\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\mathbf{A}_{k}
and
\mathbf{B}
are commutative and we have the formal solution to (
8
) (see e.g.
[
Mao07
, Section 3.4]
) as

V_{k}(t)=\exp\left\{\left(\mathbf{A}_{k}-\frac{\mathbf{B}^{2}}{2}\right)t\right\}\exp(\mathbf{B}W_{t})V_{k,0},

(9)

Where

\displaystyle V_{k,0}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{k,0}\\
v_{k,0}\\
\end{bmatrix}

with
\displaystyle u_{k,0}=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\varphi_{k}dx
and
\displaystyle v_{k,0}=\int_{\Omega}v_{0}\varphi_{k}dx
. We now investigate the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}_{k}-\dfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{B}^{2}
.

Lemma 3.1

There exists
\sigma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega>0
such that for all
\sigma^{2}\geq\sigma_{0}^{2}

\mathfrak{S}\left(\mathbf{A}_{k}-\dfrac{\mathbf{B}^{2}}{2}\right)\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,\Re(z)\leq-\omega\},\quad\forall k\geq 1,

where
\mathfrak{S}(M)
denotes the spectrum of a matrix
M
.

Proof:

Denote by
E_{k}=\mathbf{A}_{k}-\dfrac{\mathbf{B}^{2}}{2}
. The characteristic polynomial of
E_{k}
is

\displaystyle\det(E_{k}-\lambda I)

\displaystyle=\left(a-d_{u}\mu_{k}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-\lambda\right)\left(d-d_{v}\mu_{k}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-\lambda\right)-bc

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}-\lambda\left[\mathcal{T}-\left(d_{u}+d_{v}\right)\mu_{k}-{\sigma^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma^{4}}{4}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}[\mathcal{T}-(d_{u}+d_{v})\mu_{k}]+d_{u}d_{v}\mu_{k}^{2}-(ad_{v}+dd_{u})\mu_{k}+\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=:\lambda^{2}-\mathbf{w}(\mu_{k},\sigma)\lambda+\mathbf{z}(\mu_{k},\sigma).

The eigenvalues
\lambda_{k,1}
and
\lambda_{k,2}
of
E_{k}
satisfy

\lambda_{k,1}+\lambda_{k,2}=\mathbf{w}(\mu_{k},\sigma)\quad\text{ and }\quad\lambda_{k,1}\lambda_{k,2}=\mathbf{z}(\mu_{k},\sigma).

(10)

Since
\mathcal{T}<0
and
\mu_{k}\geq 0
, we have
\mathbf{w}(\mu_{k},\sigma)<0
for all
k\geq 1
and all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
. We first rewrite
\mathbf{z}(\mu_{k},\sigma)
as

\mathbf{z}(\mu_{k},\sigma)=\frac{\sigma^{4}}{4}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(-\mathcal{T})+d_{u}d_{v}\mu_{k}^{2}+\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(d_{u}+d_{v})-(ad_{v}+dd_{u})\right)\mu_{k}+\mathcal{D}

and deduce that, thanks to
\mathcal{T}<0
and
\mathcal{D}>0
, there exists
\sigma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathbf{z}(\mu_{k},\sigma)>0\quad\text{ for all }\quad\sigma^{2}\geq\sigma_{0}^{2}\text{ and all }k\geq 1.

It follows then from (
10
) that
\Re(\lambda_{k,1})<0
and
\Re(\lambda_{k,2})<0
. If
\mathbf{w}(\mu_{k},\sigma)^{2}-4\mathbf{z}(\mu_{k},\sigma)\leq 0
, then

\Re(\lambda_{k,1})=\Re(\lambda_{k,2})=\frac{\mathbf{w}(\mu_{k},\sigma)}{2}=\frac{\mathcal{T}-(d_{u}+d_{v})\mu_{k}-\sigma^{2}}{2}\leq\frac{\mathcal{T}-\sigma^{2}}{2}<0.

If
\mathbf{w}(\mu_{k},\sigma)^{2}-4\mathbf{z}(\mu_{k},\sigma)\geq 0
, then
\lambda_{k,1},\lambda_{k,2}
are both negative and

\displaystyle\max\{\lambda_{k,1},\lambda_{k,2}\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{w}(\mu_{k},\sigma)+\sqrt{\mathbf{w}(\mu_{k},\sigma)^{2}-4\mathbf{z}(\mu_{k},\sigma)}\,\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{T}-(d_{u}+d_{v})\mu_{k}-\sigma^{2}+\sqrt{(d_{u}-d_{v})^{2}\mu_{k}^{2}+2(a-d)(d_{u}-d_{v})\mu_{k}+\mathcal{T}^{2}-4\mathcal{D}}\,\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{T}-(d_{u}+d_{v})\mu_{k}-\sigma^{2}+\sqrt{[(d_{u}-d_{v})\mu_{k}+B+A^{2}-1]^{2}-4A^{2}B}\,\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{T}-(d_{u}+d_{v})\mu_{k}-\sigma^{2}+(d_{u}-d_{v})\mu_{k}+B+A^{2}-1\right)

\displaystyle=(B-1)-d_{v}\mu_{k}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\leq B-1-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}<0\quad\forall k\geq 1,

provided
\sigma^{2}>2(B-1)
. Therefore, we can choose
\sigma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\sigma_{0}^{2}>2(B-1)
, then for all
\sigma^{2}\geq\sigma_{0}^{2}
and for any
k\geq 1
,

\mathfrak{S}\left(\mathbf{A}_{k}-\dfrac{\mathbf{B}^{2}}{2}\right)\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,\Re(z)\leq-\omega\},\quad\forall k\geq 1,

with

\omega:=\min\left\{\frac{-\mathcal{T}+\sigma^{2}}{2},-B+1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right\}>0.

\square

Theorem 3.2

Assume that
1<B<1+A^{2}
. Then for any diffusion coefficients
d_{u},d_{v}
, the solution to the linearized Brusselator system (
3
) is exponentially converging to zero as
t\to\infty
almost surely, provided
2\sigma^{2}>B-1
. Moreover, we have the Lyapunov exponent

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln(\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\leq-\omega<0\text{ almost surely},

with

\omega=\min\left\{\frac{-\mathcal{T}+\sigma^{2}}{2},-B+1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right\}>0.

Proof:

From (
6
), Parseval’s identity and (
9
) it follows that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|V_{k}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|\exp\left\{2\left(\mathbf{A}_{k}-\frac{\mathbf{B}^{2}}{2}\right)t\right\}\right||\exp(2\mathbf{B}W_{t})||V_{k,0}|^{2}.

Thanks to the property of white noise, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\sigma W_{t}}{t}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

and therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\exp(\sigma W_{t})\leq C_{\varepsilon}e^{\varepsilon t}\quad\forall t\geq 0,

almost surely. Therefore,

|\exp(2\mathbf{B}W_{t})|\leq C_{\varepsilon}^{2}e^{2\varepsilon t}\quad\forall t\geq 0.

On the other hand, from Lemma
3.1
,

\left|\exp\left\{2\left(\mathbf{A}_{k}-\frac{\mathbf{B}^{2}}{2}\right)t\right\}\right|\leq Ce^{-2\omega t}.

Thus we can estimate

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\left(\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{2t}\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{2t}\ln\left(C_{\varepsilon}e^{(-2\omega+2\varepsilon)t}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|V_{k,0}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\omega+\varepsilon+\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{2t}\ln\left(C_{\varepsilon}(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=-\omega+\varepsilon

almost surely for
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary. This confirms the Lyapunov exponent and finishes the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

\square

3.2
The case of different noise intensities

In this part, the noise intensities for
u
and
v
are different. For the sake of simplicity, we fix
\sigma_{u}=0
and vary
\sigma_{v}\neq 0
. Due to this difference, the matrix
\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{A}_{k}
in (
8
) are generally no longer commutative, and consequently an explicit expression of solutions similarly to (
9
) is not possible. Furthermore, due to the degeneracy of
\mathbf{B}
, the stabilization and destabilization theory in e.g.
[
Mao07
, Chapter 4]
cannot be applied to determine the stability of (
8
). Here, we show by numerical simulations that this difference in noise intensities
destabilizes
the system (
8
), and consequently the linear PDE system (
3
).

Consider
\Omega=(0,1)
and the set of parameters

A=1,\quad B=1.8,\quad d_{u}=2\times 10^{-3},\quad d_{v}=1\times 10^{-3},

which make the ODE system (
5
) stable since
B<1+A^{2}
. Moreover, since
d_{u}/d_{v}
is large, the PDE system is also stable, i.e. no Turing instability occurs. The destabilization by noise with
\sigma_{u}=0
and
\sigma_{v}\neq 0
is shown in Figure
4
, where part (a) shows the evolution of
\ln(\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})
for different values of
\sigma_{v}
. Here we see that starting from
\sigma_{v}=1.5
, the solution starts to grow exponentially. This is confirmed in Figure
4(b)
, where numerical Lyapunov exponents are shown. From this figure, the Lyapunov exponent is monotone increasing in the value of
|\sigma_{v}|
, and starting from
\sigma_{v}=1.5
, the Lyapunov exponent becomes positive.

5
Conclusion and Future Work

In this work, we have demonstrated that a multiplicative noise in Brusselator can have suppression or induced effect on Turing instability. More precisely, when both the noise intensities on both species are the same, then the Turing instability is suppressed if the intensities are sufficiently large. This is also theoretically proved by analyzing the linearized system around equilibrium, and using explicit expression of solutions to SDE systems when the deterministic and stochastic parts are commutative. On the other hand, when one noise intensity is zero, multiplicative noise tends to destabilize stable system, which is shown and analyzed by numerical simulations. These effects are shown through numerical examples to hold also for nonlinear Brusselator system. This finding represents an interesting perspective of the complex interplay between Turing stability and stochastic perturbations. Furthermore, we believe that the insights gained from this study can be extended to a wider class of nonlinear dynamical systems.

There are certainly several important open questions to be investigated in future works, including

•

can the suppression by noise with the same noise intensities be proved theoretically as for the case of scalar equation in e.g.
[
CKS06
,
CCLR07
,
Kwi99
]
?

•

how does one approach to prove the destabilizing effect when
\sigma_{u}=0
and
\sigma_{v}\neq 0
since explicit expressions are not possible, and the general theory as in
[
Mao07
]
seem not applicable?

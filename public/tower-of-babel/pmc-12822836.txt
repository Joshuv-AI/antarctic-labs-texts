Health promotion, disease prevention, and healthcare: The measurement of high school student health literacy
Authors: Insar Damopolii, Herawati Susilo, Susriyati Mahanal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Low levels of health literacy are related with riskier health behaviors and poorer health outcomes. Biological science contributes significantly to students’ health literacy, as many biology topics are directly related to health issues. This research aims to analyze the health literacy of high school students, focusing on three health domains: health promotion, disease prevention, and healthcare.METHODS AND MATERIAL:A total of 570 students contributed to this study, selected through convenience sampling. Thirteen test items were administered to the students via Google Forms. Data were analyzed by calculating the scores obtained, which were then categorized into performance levels.RESULTS:The findings revealed that overall, students’ health literacy and their performance in the three health domains were at the basic level. Female students consistently achieved higher scores for health literacy and in the health promotion domain (Sig. <0.05) but were not significant in the healthcare and disease prevention domains compared to males. However, both genders fell within the basic category. Notably, male students performed below basic level in the domain of disease prevention. Among age groups, 17-year-old students performed at the basic level, while students aged 15, 16, and 18 scored below basic in disease prevention. The lowest scores were observed among 18-year-old students. The calculation results show that health literacy and healthcare domains significantly differ based on age, but the other two do not.CONCLUSIONS:The high school students generally possess a basic level of health literacy, with female students outperforming their male counterparts. Additionally, health literacy appears to decline slightly with age in this study.

Introduction

Health issues have been a topic of increasing concern among academics and community members over time.[1,2,3] Over the past few decades, evolving environmental changes have added complexity to health literacy demands and human needs.[4] Globally, society continues to underestimate the importance of health literacy, leading to widespread deficiencies.[5]

Health literacy is crucial for preventing communicable diseases and addressing complex real-life health challenges. Poor health literacy negatively impacts health behavior and outcomes.[6] The significant gaps in population-based health literacy exist across regions, with 12.4% of individuals aged 15 and above lacking adequate and 47.6% demonstrating limited health literacy.[7] Adolescents aged 15–17, who are typically high school students, represent a group whose health status requires improvement.[8,9]

Biological science plays an important role in enhancing students’ health literacy. Many biology topics are directly linked to health issues, such as reproductive health problems (e.g., HIV/AIDS, cervical cancer),[10,11] respiratory organ diseases (e.g., tuberculosis),[12] and the adverse effects of viruses such as COVID-19,[13] and there are still many topics in biology that discuss health issues. Other diseases, including dengue fever, malaria, typhoid fever, and HIV/AIDS, are taught through biology topics on viruses, bacteria, protozoa, and insects, making biology education well-suited to fostering health literacy in high school students.[14]

While high school students have been exposed to health-related issues through biology lessons, there is a need for further studies to evaluate their health literacy achievements, particularly regarding gender and age differences. This is a follow-up suggestion from the previous research study,[15] namely to measure health literacy based on age, where it was found that gender had no effect. This study aims to analyze the high school students health literacy levels and their performance in the domains of health promotion, disease prevention, and healthcare.

Materials and Methods

Study setup and design

This study analyzed the health literacy of high school students, focusing on biology topics relevant to health literacy. Four biology topics were included in this research: the human immune system, mobility in humans (movement and nervous systems), human hormones and reproduction, and growth and development (with an emphasis on stunting).

Study participants and sampling

The participants were high school students from grades XI (second year) and XII (third year) in Indonesia. Participants involved in this research are volunteers, and students were selected using a convenience sampling method. A total of 570 students contributed to this study [Table 1].

Data collection tools and technique

The instrument used in this research was a test designed to measure health literacy,[16] across three health domains: health promotion, disease prevention, and healthcare. The initial test consisted of 15 items. Validation of the test was conducted by nine experts: three PhDs in education, one master’s degree holder in education, one PhD in human health, one biology master’s degree holder, one master’s degree holder in public health, and two health practitioners with nursing degrees. Based on their feedback, the test achieved a validity score of 91.78, indicating strong validity. The test was further piloted with 171 respondents to determine its empirical validity and reliability.

The pilot results indicated that two items (6 and 13) were invalid, resulting in a final test comprising 13 valid items. Reliability analysis of the 13-item test produced a score of 0.70, confirming acceptable reliability. In this study, it was decided to measure health literacy using 13 items that were in accordance with validity and reliability standards. The health literacy test was conducted as an online test using Google Forms. The test was distributed to students who had already studied the four biology topics included in the research focus.

Ethical consideration

Not applicable. Participation in this study is voluntary and anonymous. Participant information collected is limited to gender and age. Participants are free to choose not to complete the distributed tests. Data can only be accessed by the researcher. Participant information is not published.

Data analysis

The collected data were analyzed by calculating the scores for each item. Each accurate answer was given a score of 1, while false answers obtained a value of 0. Students’ total scores were converted with a range from 0 to 100. The health literacy categories were determined based on established benchmarks.[14] Calculation of significant gender differences using the Mann-Whitney test and Kruskal-Wallis for age differences.

Results

This study provided data on health literacy levels of high school students across three health domains. The primary goal was to assess students’ levels of health literacy. A common challenge in health literacy is that many individuals are unaware of their health status, often believing they are healthy until a medical examination reveals underlying conditions. This observation underscores the importance of analyzing the health literacy levels of high school students. The analysis findings are shown in Figure 1 and Tables 2 and 3.

The graph in Figure 1 illustrates that students have sufficient knowledge about healthcare and are willing to promote health to others. However, their health literacy is lower in disease prevention. Students’ overall health literacy is still at the basic level, as do the other three domains of health. Therefore, it can be concluded that student performance only reaches the basic category.

Table 2 indicates that female students have higher health literacy and health domains. Male students’ performance for health literacy and its two domains—healthcare and health promotion—falls into the basic category, while their performance for disease prevention is below the basic category. However, female students show basic performance for health literacy and its health domains. This finding indicates that female students perform better for health literacy and health domains than male students. However, none reached the proficient level.

The analysis of health literacy and health domains was then conducted based on age differences [Table 3]. The Table 3 displays that students aged 17 years performed on a basic level for health literacy and health domains. Students aged 15 years performed similarly, except they had a higher score on health promotion than students aged 17 years, and on the disease prevention domain, their performance was below the basic level. Students aged 16 and 18 years performed on basic level in the same domains as students aged 15 years and below basic for disease prevention. However, students aged 18 years scored lower than students of all ages. The results of the Kruskal-Wallis calculation revealed that significant differences based on age occurred in the health literacy score and healthcare domains. The health promotion and disease prevention domains did not indicate any significant differences based on student age.

Discussion

This research found that the health literacy of high school students is not yet optimal. This finding shows that their health literacy is mostly at the basic level, while a few others are below the basic level. The findings of this research are in line with those found by Rachmani et al.[17] They found that a majority of the literacy category of high school students were within the troublesome category, including more than 50% of the samples included in the study. Furthermore, they found that more than 11% had inadequate health literacy. The findings of high school students’ health literacy data indicate the urgent need for improvement. Poor health literacy has an impact on students’ health status. This can be seen from the disease prevention domain which has the lowest score compared to the other two domains.

An example of a question we provide is related to shaving pubic hair. The question relates to the health literacy dimension regarding the ability to understand information about health risk factors in the disease prevention category of health domain. The results showed that only 53.33% answered correctly that pubic hair cannot be entirely shaved. The rest stated that shaving is better because of esthetic and to prevent bacteria proliferation. In fact, according to Gaither et al.,[18] individuals who shave their pubic hair more than 6 times are at risk of contracting infectious diseases. This illustrates that high school students do not understand how to actually prevent disease. They only consider it in terms of esthetic and perceived cleanliness, even though pubic hair functions to prevent disease. This is what causes their health literacy to be low, especially in the disease prevention domain. Students’ health literacy must be improved, considering that it is currently a concern. No students were found to have health literacy at the proficient level. The weak health literacy of students causes their health levels to be poor. Improving health literacy at the school level can be done by reforming the curriculum.[7] In fact, in the high school science curriculum, there are many things related to health literacy, notably in biology. If studied on a more profound level, many health issues are within the topics taught in biology. However, these topics are discussed only at the end of the meeting, almost as an afterthought.

Female students have better scores on health literacy and health domains. There is a link between gender and their desire for health promotion such as sharing health information with their friends.[19] Other research found that females are more dominant in healthcare[20]; conversely, other research has indicated that there is no statistically significant difference in health literacy when analyzed by gender.[15] This is proven in our research; the sig. value (<0.05) demonstrates a significant difference between males and females in health literacy scores and the health promotion domain. Female students showed higher scores in both. In our study, males showed the lowest score at the basic level for disease prevention. Our findings reveal that females are more serious about their health. Female students show that they can better know how to protect themselves from disease, prevent it, and invite others to live healthily.

In one of the questions in our study, students were given a case related to gonorrhea, where someone told his friend that they could use a condom while having sex to prevent this disease. This question is related to the health literacy dimension, specifically the ability to comprehend information about health determinants in the physical and social environment and obtain its meaning with the health promotion domain. As many as 95/186 (51.08%) students answered correctly, while 207/384 (53.91%) females answered correctly by choosing to disagree that having sex using a condom can prevent gonorrhea. Females understand health information related to gonorrhea better. Marfatia et al.[21] explained that although condoms can prevent STIs, they are not 100% effective, there is a risk of failure and infection can occur. Although 50% of students understand this, there are still the rest who need to be encouraged to be able to recognize health problems to improve their health literacy. In this topic as part of the biology subject, it can be taught through the reproductive system and immune system material.

Finally, what we focus on is the difference in health literacy in terms of age level. Table 3 shows that students aged 18 years have a low health literacy level. This is also shown in the scores on the health domain. Increasing age shows a decrease in both health literacy scores and health domains. This will have an impact on the next age. The low health literacy of an individual when they are in school has an impact in the future, such as at the college level. Moreover, in our study, students were only at the basic level, or even below basic level.[22] Individuals aged 15–17 years are high school students whose health status needs improvement.[8,9] The low health literacy of high school students requires improvement. As they are in school, teachers can integrate health education to promote health literacy. This will provide good health literacy for them.

Our research revealed that health literacy scores and healthcare domains showed significant differences based on age. However, health promotion and disease prevention domains did not show any significance. A study reported that at least 60% of participants, comprising young adults and adolescents, demonstrated adequate health literacy.[23] Conversely, inadequate health literacy was correlated with various detrimental health outcomes, including smoking and obesity. Our research findings continue the suggestions by Berens et al.,[15] where the health literacy instrument designed must be measured among respondents of different ages and be adjusted additionally. In our research, the measuring instrument was adjusted to the school level and related to biology topics. Our research findings showed significant differences between health literacy based on age; it is important to teach school students (adolescents) the importance of health, how to prevent it, and how to teach others to stay healthy. Although what was found by Berens et al.[15] indicated that formal education has an insignificant influence on health literacy, we think that formal education for students (adolescents) is needed. This is related to the topic of biology learning related to health issues that are widely discussed in class. Even some schools we found have divided classes based on health interests where biology is the main lesson taught.

Limitation and recommendation

This study was limited in analyzing the profiling of health literacy level and the health domain. Interventions have not been conducted in this study, so data on improvements in health literacy have not been presented. Further studies are needed to integrate health cases into learning, especially in the subject of biology where the topic has the potential to increase students’ health literacy. The emphasis on the topic of disease prevention in particular needs to be improved because this domain showed to be on the level of below basic in our study.

Conclusion

This study concludes that the health literacy performance of high school students only reaches the basic level but showed significant differences based on gender and age. The disease prevention domain is below basic for male students and among age groups 15, 16, and 18. The significant differences are only found in the health promotion domain for gender differences, while based on age, significant differences are found in the healthcare domain. Although the high school student’s health literacy scores are shown at the basic level, female students show better scores. Increasing age showed decreased health literacy and scores in the health domain.

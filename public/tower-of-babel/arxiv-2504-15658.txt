Improved Upper Bound on Brun’s Constant Under GRH
Lachlan Dunn

Abstract
Brun’s constant is the summation of the reciprocals of all twin primes, given by B = ∑ p ∈ P 2 ( 1 p + 1 p + 2 ) B=\sum_{p\in P_{2}}{\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p+2}\right)} . While rigorous unconditional bounds on B B are known, we present the first rigorous bound on Brun’s constant under the GRH assumption, yielding B < 2.1594 B<2.1594 .



1
Introduction

Brun’s constant is a fundamental concept in analytic number theory, arising in the study of twin primes—pairs of prime numbers that differ by two. That is,
P_{2}:=\{\text{prime }p:p+2\text{ is prime}\}
. Unlike the sum of the reciprocals of all prime numbers, which diverges, the sum of the reciprocals of twin primes converges to a finite value, known as Brun’s constant. Perhaps unsurprisingly, this result was first established in 1919 by Brun
[
1
]
.

Brun’s proof, however, did not yield a numerical upper bound for
B
. The first such bound,
B<2.347
, was established by Crandall and Pomerance
[
3
]
. Since then, improvements have been made, with the current best unconditional bound of
B<2.28851
being obtained by Platt and Trudgian
[
11
]
. Nicely conjectured that
B=1.902160583209\pm 0.000000000781

[
10
]
, demonstrating the scope of potential improvements to be made.

One approach to refining these bounds is to assume the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH). This method was employed by Klyve to derive the bound
B<2.1754

[
8
]
, and it will be the approach taken here. Although our improvement is small, Klyve never published his result, and utilized numerical integration to calculate his subsequent bound on
B
. To our knowledge, our work represents the first mathematically rigorous upper bound on
B
assuming GRH.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume GRH. Then,
B<2.1594
.

To achieve this, we combine previously computed values of
B
(up to a fixed threshold
x_{0}
), along with the estimation of the tail end of the sum provided by Selberg’s sieve. This estimation proceeds by computing the intermediate sums

B(m_{i},m_{i+1}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in P_{2}\\
m_{i}<p\leq m_{i+1}\end{subarray}}{\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p+2}\right)}

where
m_{1}=x_{0}
and
i=1,\ldots k
. These sums are then combined to evaluate
B(m_{1},\infty)
.

This intermediate sum approach is advantageous due to its flexibility, as different methods of estimating
B
may yield tighter bounds over different regions. By combining these results, a sharper overall bound can be obtained. To facilitate further improvements, we provide explicit bounds for each
B(m_{i},m_{i+1})
.

The structure of this paper is as follows: Section 2 introduces the sieve framework, defines key notation, and derives a bound for the twin prime counting function. Section 3 extends this bound to Brun’s constant using partial summation. Section 4 focuses on parameter optimization and presents the numerical computations. Finally, Section 5 discusses potential refinements and further improvements to this method.

2
Upper Bound on
\pi_{2}(x)

To calculate
B
, we require an upper bound on the twin prime counting function
\pi_{2}(x):=\#\{p\text{ prime}:p+2\text{ also prime}\}
. We derive this bound using Selberg’s sieve by sieving on the set of odd numbers
n
such that
n-2
is prime. Notably, if
n
is removed by the sieve (i.e.
n
is not prime), then
(n-2,n)
cannot form a twin prime pair.

2.1
Preliminaries

For an introduction to Selberg’s sieve, see
[
6
]
. We will begin by defining below some preliminary notation. Our set of twin prime candidates is given by

\mathcal{A}=\{n\leq x+2:n\text{ odd with }n-2\text{ prime}\}

and our set of sieving primes by

\mathcal{P}=\{p>2:p\text{ prime}\}.

We will be sieving using odd primes less than or equal to
z
, and so

P_{z}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p\leq z\end{subarray}}{p}.

\mathcal{A}_{d}
is the set of twin prime candidates that are divisible by a specific prime
d
, that is

A_{d}=|\{n\leq x+2:d|n\text{ and }n-2\text{ is prime}\}|=\pi(x;d,-2).

By the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions, we will be estimating
\pi(x;d,-2)
by
\frac{\text{li}(x)}{\varphi(d)}
, and so we define the error in our estimation to be

E(x;d):=\pi(x;d,-2)-\frac{\text{li}(x)}{\varphi(d)}

where

\text{li}(x):=\int_{2}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log{t}}.

As we take li to be truncated at 2, it will be useful to define the offset as

\text{li}_{2}:=\int_{0}^{2}\frac{dt}{\log{t}}.

We will use the following notation for the twin prime constant:

\pi_{2}:=\prod_{p>2}\frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^{2}}.

Following Klyve

V(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq z\\
d|P_{z}\end{subarray}}{\frac{1}{\prod_{p|d}{(p-2)}}}.

And finally, we take

[d_{1},d_{2}]:=\text{lcm}(d_{1},d_{2}).

Then, using Theorem 5.7

Theorem 2.1

For the first interval (

2.3
Bounding
R(x,z)

Now, we need an upper bound on
R(x,z)
for all
x
and
z
. We will start by proving an inequality that will allow us to bound one of
R(x,z)
’s terms. This inequality holds for any fixed prime
p
, but becomes less useful as the size of
p
increases relative to the size of
z
. We have excluded some slight optimisations on the order of
z^{1/2}
for the sake of readability, and the range of permissible values of
z
can be lowered at a similar cost. For our case, we will be setting
p=2
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and
z\geq 2768896
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq z\\
\textup{gcd}(p,d)=1\end{subarray}}{\mu^{2}(d)}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{p+1}\cdot\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\right)z+\left(\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}})+2\right)\sqrt{z}

\displaystyle+\left(3.12\cdot(1+\frac{1}{p^{1/4}})\right)z^{1/4}.

Proof.

To start, note that we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq z\\
\textup{gcd}(p,d)=1\end{subarray}}{\mu^{2}(d)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\sqrt{z}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(k)\left[\frac{z}{k^{2}}\right]-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\sqrt{\frac{z}{p}}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(k)\left[\frac{z}{pk^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\sqrt{z}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(k)\frac{z}{k^{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\sqrt{z}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\sqrt{\frac{z}{p}}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(k)\frac{z}{pk^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\sqrt{\frac{z}{p}}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\sqrt{z}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(k)\frac{z}{k^{2}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\sqrt{\frac{z}{p}}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(k)\frac{z}{pk^{2}}+2\sqrt{z}

\displaystyle=M(z)z+2\sqrt{z},

where we have taken

M(z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\sqrt{z}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\sqrt{\frac{z}{p}}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{pk^{2}}.

Also, we have the following, where
p^{\prime}
is any prime number.

\displaystyle M(z)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k>\sqrt{z}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k>\sqrt{\frac{z}{p}}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{pk^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(k)}{pk^{2}}

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{1}{p})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{1}{p})\prod_{p^{\prime}\neq p}(1-\frac{1}{(p^{\prime})^{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\frac{1}{p})}{(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}})}\cdot\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{p+1}\cdot\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}

Now we can bound the difference as follows.

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k>\sqrt{z}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k>\sqrt{\frac{z}{p}}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{pk^{2}}\right|\leq\sum_{k>\sqrt{z}}\frac{\mu^{2}(k)}{k^{2}}+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{k>\sqrt{\frac{z}{p}}}\frac{\mu^{2}(k)}{k^{2}}.

We now apply explicit bounds on the square-free summation function provided in
[
2
]
, in conjunction with partial summation, to note that

\displaystyle\sum_{k>x}\frac{\mu^{2}(k)}{k^{2}}\leq\left(-\frac{\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}x-1.333\sqrt{x}}{x^{2}}+2\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}t+1.333\sqrt{t}}{t^{3}}dt\right).

Applying this substitution, and simplifying terms, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k>\sqrt{z}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k>\sqrt{\frac{z}{p}}\\
\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{pk^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}}{\sqrt{z}}+\frac{3.12}{z^{3/4}}+\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}}{\sqrt{\frac{z}{p}}}+\frac{3.12}{(\frac{z}{p})^{3/4}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}}{\sqrt{z}}+\frac{3.12}{z^{3/4}}+\frac{\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}}{\sqrt{zp}}+\frac{3.12}{z^{3/4}p^{1/4}}.

Combining this result with the first inequality proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.2

For any

Now we continue with the following.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assuming GRH, for
x\geq 4\cdot 10^{18}
and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\leq z\\

\displaystyle c_{\pi}(x):=\frac{3}{8\pi}+\frac{6+1/\pi}{4\log{x}}+\frac{6}{\log^{2}{x}}+\frac{\textup{li}_{2}}{\sqrt{x}\log{x}}.

Proof.

We will use Lemma 5.1
[
7

to bound under GRH, noting that for

[d_{1},d_{2}]<d_{1}d_{2}<z^{2}<\sqrt{x}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\leq z\\

\displaystyle<\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\leq z\\
d_{1},d_{2}|P_{z}\end{subarray}}{c_{\pi}(x)\sqrt{x}\log{x}}

\displaystyle=c_{\pi}(x)\sqrt{x}\log{x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\leq z\\

\displaystyle=c_{\pi}(x)\sqrt{x}\log{x}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq z\\

\displaystyle=c_{\pi}(x)\sqrt{x}\log{x}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq z\\

\displaystyle=r^{2}(z)c_{\pi}(x)\sqrt{x}\log{x},

\displaystyle B(2,4\cdot 10^{18})\leq 1.840518.

Lemma 3.1

Assuming GRH, for
m_{1}\geq 4\cdot 10^{18}

\displaystyle B(m_{k})-B(m_{1})<

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(\frac{c_{1}(m_{i},m_{i+1})}{V(z_{i})}+c_{\pi}(m_{i})r^{2}(z_{i})c_{2}(m_{i},m_{i+1})\right)

\displaystyle+2\left(\frac{\pi_{2}(m_{k})}{m_{k}}-\frac{\pi_{2}(m_{1})}{m_{1}}\right),

where

\displaystyle\textup{li}_{\textup{up}}(x):=\frac{x}{\log{x}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log{x}}+\frac{2}{\log^{2}{x}}+\frac{7.32}{\log^{3}{x}}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{x}\log{x}}{8\pi}+\textup{li}_{2},

\displaystyle\textup{li}_{\textup{low}}(x):=\frac{x}{\log{x}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log{x}}+\frac{2}{\log^{2}{x}}\right)-\frac{\sqrt{x}\log{x}}{8\pi}-\textup{li}_{2},

\displaystyle c_{1}(m_{i},m_{i+1}):=\left(\log{\log{m_{i+i}}}-\frac{\textup{li}_{\textup{low}}(m_{i+1})-\textup{li}_{2}}{m_{i+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\left(\log{\log{m_{i}}}-\frac{\textup{li}_{\textup{up}}(m_{i})-\textup{li}_{2}}{m_{i}}\right),

\displaystyle c_{2}(m_{i},m_{i+1}):=\left(\frac{-2\log{m_{i+1}}-4}{\sqrt{m_{i+1}}}\right)-\left(\frac{-2\log{m_{i}}-4}{\sqrt{m_{i}}}\right).

Proof.

\displaystyle B(m_{k})-B(m_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}<p\leq m_{k}\\
p,p+2\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}{\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p+2}\right)}

\displaystyle<2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}<p\leq m_{k}\\
p,p+2\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{\pi_{2}(m_{k})}{m_{k}}-\frac{\pi_{2}(m_{1})}{m_{1}}+\int_{m_{1}}^{m_{k}}{\frac{\pi_{2}(t)}{t^{2}}}dt\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(\int_{m_{i}}^{m_{i+1}}{\frac{\pi_{2}(t)}{t^{2}}}dt\right)+2\left(\frac{\pi_{2}(m_{k})}{m_{k}}-\frac{\pi_{2}(m_{1})}{m_{1}}\right).

The value of
\frac{\pi_{2}(m_{k})}{m_{k}}
will cancel in a further calculation, and the value of
\frac{\pi_{2}(m_{1})}{m_{1}}
can be calculated exactly using the computations performed by Oliveira e Silva
[
5
]
. For the remaining term, we proceed by

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{m_{i}}^{m_{i+1}}{\frac{\pi_{2}(t)}{t^{2}}}dt\right)

\displaystyle<2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(\int_{m_{i}}^{m_{i+1}}{\frac{\textup{li}(t)}{V(z_{i})\cdot t^{2}}}dt+\int_{m_{i}}^{m_{i+1}}{\frac{r^{2}(z_{i})c_{\pi}(t)\sqrt{t}\log{t}}{t^{2}}}dt\right).

We now apply some simplifications to these integrals to allow us to compute them analytically. Firstly, note that
V(z_{i})
and

are fixed for each interval
[m_{i},m_{i+1}]
. Also, note that

is monotonically decreasing, and so

for
x\in[m_{i},m_{i+1}]
. And so,

\displaystyle B(m_{k})-B(m_{i})<

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(V(z_{i})^{-1}\int_{m_{i}}^{m_{i+1}}{\frac{\textup{li}(t)}{t^{2}}}dt\right.

\displaystyle+\left.c_{\pi}(m_{i})r^{2}(z_{i})\int_{m_{i}}^{m_{i+1}}{\frac{\sqrt{t}\log{t}}{t^{2}}}dt\right).

Now, in computing the integral we note that

\displaystyle V(z_{i})^{-1}\int_{m_{i}}^{m_{i+1}}{\frac{\text{li}(t)}{t^{2}}}dt=

\displaystyle V(z_{i})^{-1}\left[\left(\log{\log{m_{i+1}}}-\frac{\text{li}(m_{i+1})-\text{li}_{2}}{m_{i+1}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\left(\log{\log{m_{i}}}-\frac{\text{li}(m_{i})-\text{li}_{2}}{m_{i}}\right)\right].

To avoid computing li
(x)
directly, we use Schoenfeld’s bound (6.18) on
|\pi(x)-\text{li}(x)|
assuming RH
[
13
]

\pi(x)
(Theorem 5.1 and Corrollary 5.2)

\displaystyle\text{li}(x)\geq\text{li}_{\text{low}}(x)=\frac{x}{\log{x}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log{x}}+\frac{2}{\log^{2}{x}}\right)-\frac{\sqrt{x}\log{x}}{8\pi}-\text{li}_{2}.

And so

\displaystyle\int_{m_{i}}^{m_{i+1}}{\frac{\text{li}(t)}{t^{2}}}dt\leq

\displaystyle\left(\log{\log{m_{i+i}}}-\frac{\text{li}_{\text{low}}(m_{i+1})-\text{li}_{2}}{m_{i+1}}\right)-

Potential Improvements

We conclude by discussing several potential improvements to the presented method of bounding
B
.

•

Combining different bounds: as discussed earlier, splicing together different bounds for
B
over disjoint intervals could provide stronger estimates than restricting to only one method. To facilitate this, the intermediate sums computed in this paper are made available.

•

Evaluating
B(x_{0})
at a higher
x_{0}
: computing
B(x_{0})
for a larger
x_{0}
would reduce approximation errors, leading to a more precise bound.

•

Further parameter optimisation: refining the choices of parameters
\{m_{i}\}
and
\{z_{i}\}
could potentially yield a more accurate result.

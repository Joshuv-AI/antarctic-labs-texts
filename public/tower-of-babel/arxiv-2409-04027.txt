Primordial Bounce-Inflation Scenario to Alleviate Cosmological Tensions and Lensing Anomaly
Hao-Hao Li, Xin-zhe Zhang, Taotao Qiu

Abstract
We put forward a primordial scenario to alleviate cosmological tensions, i.e. Hubble ( H 0 H_{0} ) tension and S 8 S_{8} tension. Based on flat Λ \Lambda CDM, the Bounce-Inflation (BI) scenario gives the results that H 0 = 68.60 − 0.45 + 0.40 ​ km / s / Mpc H_{0}=68.60^{+0.40}_{-0.45}\,\text{km}/\text{s}/\text{Mpc} , S 8 = 0.806 ± 0.011 S_{8}=0.806\pm 0.011 by using Planck 2018 data sets and H 0 = 68.96 ± 0.38 ​ km / s / Mpc H_{0}=68.96\pm 0.38\,\text{km}/\text{s}/\text{Mpc} , S 8 = 0.797 ± 0.010 S_{8}=0.797\pm 0.010 by using Planck 2018 + SPT3G data sets. These reduce the cosmological tensions slightly. We also take an extended Λ \Lambda CDM model into account, Λ \Lambda CDM (BI)+ A L A_{L} , where A L A_{L} is the gravitational lensing amplitude. The results are H 0 = 69.38 ± 0.49 ​ km / s / Mpc H_{0}=69.38\pm 0.49\,\text{km}/\text{s}/\text{Mpc} , S 8 = 0.774 ± 0.014 S_{8}=0.774\pm 0.014 fitted by Planck 2018 data sets and H 0 = 69.49 ± 0.45 ​ km / s / Mpc H_{0}=69.49\pm 0.45\,\text{km}/\text{s}/\text{Mpc} , S 8 = 0.771 − 0.012 + 0.013 S_{8}=0.771^{+0.013}_{-0.012} fitted by Planck 2018 + SPT3G data sets, which reduce the Hubble tension to ∼ 3 ​ σ \sim 3\sigma level and show no S 8 S_{8} tension. The A L ≈ 1.1 A_{L}\approx 1.1 is smaller than the result of the inflation scenario with a constraint of Planck 2018 data sets. Besides, the spectral index of the bounce-inflation scenario n s n_{s} is about 0.98 0.98 , with a trend to the Harrison-Zel’dovich spectrum.

-Cold Dark Matter (

II
Bounce-Inflation

As a starting point, we parameterize the background of the BI scenario where the scale factor follows

a=\begin{cases}a_{\operatorname{con}}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{B-}-\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{\epsilon_{c}-1}}&\eta<\eta_{B-},\\
a_{B}\left[1+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\eta-\eta_{B}\right)^{2}\right]&\eta_{B-}\leq\eta\leq\eta_{B+}\\
a_{\exp}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{B+}-\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{\epsilon_{e}-1}}&\eta>\eta_{B+},\end{cases},

(1)

with
\tilde{\eta}_{B-}\equiv\eta_{B-}-\left[\left(\epsilon_{c}-1\right)\mathcal{H}_{con}\right]^{-1}
,
\tilde{\eta}_{B+}\equiv\eta_{B+}-\left[\left(\epsilon_{e}-1\right)\mathcal{H}_{exp}\right]^{-1}
. Here
\eta_{B-}\left(\eta_{B+}\right)
is the beginning (ending) of the bouncing phase,
a_{B}
is the scale factor at the bouncing point
\eta_{B}
,
\mathcal{H}_{con}\left(\mathcal{H}_{exp}\right)
is the conformal Hubble parameter at
\eta_{B-}\left(\eta_{B+}\right)
, and
\epsilon_{c}\left(\epsilon_{e}\right)
is the slow-roll parameter in the contracting(inflationary) phase. The three lines in parameterization (
1
) represent the contracting, bouncing, and expanding phases of the primordial universe, respectively. Moreover, to avoid the contracting anisotropy problem
Kunze and Durrer 2000
;
Erickson et al. 2004
;
Xue and Steinhardt 2010
;
Xue and Steinhardt 2011
;
Qiu et al. 2013
;
Qiu and Zhu 2024
, we require that
\epsilon_{c}
is larger than
3
, which means that the parameter of the equation of state
w
in the contracting phase is larger than
1
from the relationship
\epsilon_{c}=3\left(1+w\right)/2
.

The curvature perturbation
\mathcal{R}
is governed by the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation:

u^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\left(c_{s}^{2}k^{2}-\frac{z^{\prime\prime}}{z}\right)u_{k}=0,

(2)

with
u_{k}\equiv z\mathcal{R}
,
z\equiv a\sqrt{Q}/c_{s}
. The varible
Q
can be calculated based on the effective field theory of cosmology
Ni et al. 2018
. The scale factor
a
and the slow-roll parameter
\epsilon
in each phase are given by Eq. (
1
), while the solutions of different phases are connected using the matching conditions
Ni et al. 2018
. We follow
Ni et al. 2018
to write down the primordial power spectrum of BI

\begin{aligned} P_{\mathcal{R}}=\frac{H_{exp}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}M_{p}^{2}\epsilon_{e}}\left(\frac{k}{k_{*}}\right)^{3-2\nu_{e}}\left|C_{1}-C_{2}\right|^{2}\end{aligned},

(3)

where
\nu_{e}=\frac{\epsilon_{e}-3}{2\left(\epsilon_{e}-1\right)}
,

\begin{aligned} C_{1}&=-ie^{i\nu_{c1}\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}}}\pi^{3/2}\sin{(l\Delta\eta_{B})}\bigg\{k\bigg[H^{(1)}_{\nu_{c}+1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}}\bigg)-H^{(1)}_{\nu_{c}-1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}}\bigg)\bigg]\bigg[k\bigg(H^{(2)}_{\nu_{e}+1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)\\
&-H^{(2)}_{\nu_{e}-1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)\bigg)-\bigg(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}+2l\cot{(l\Delta\eta_{B})}\bigg)H^{(2)}_{\nu_{e}}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)\bigg]+H^{(1)}_{\nu_{c}}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}}\bigg)\bigg[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}+4l^{2}\\

&-H^{(2)}_{\nu_{e}-1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg\}\bigg/\bigg[16l\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}+8lk\pi\bigg(J_{\nu_{e}}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)Y_{\nu_{e}-1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)-J_{\nu_{e}-1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)Y_{\nu_{e}}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg]\end{aligned},

(4)

and

\begin{aligned} C_{2}&=ie^{i\nu_{c1}\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}}}\pi^{3/2}\sin{(l\Delta\eta_{B})}\bigg\{k\bigg[H^{(1)}_{\nu_{c}+1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}}\bigg)-H^{(1)}_{\nu_{c}-1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}}\bigg)\bigg]\bigg[k\bigg(H^{(1)}_{\nu_{e}+1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)\\

&+2\big(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}\big)l\cot{(l\Delta\eta_{B})}\bigg]H^{(1)}_{\nu_{e}}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)+k\bigg(2l\cot{(l\Delta\eta_{B})}-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}\bigg)\bigg(H^{(1)}_{\nu_{e}+1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)\\
&-H^{(1)}_{\nu_{e}-1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg\}\bigg/\bigg[16l\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}+8lk\pi\bigg(J_{\nu_{e}}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)Y_{\nu_{e}-1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)-J_{\nu_{e}-1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)Y_{\nu_{e}}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg]\end{aligned},

(5)

with
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{c}=\mathcal{H}_{con}-\epsilon_{c}\mathcal{H}_{con}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}=\mathcal{H}_{exp}-\epsilon_{e}\mathcal{H}_{exp}

\nu_{c}=(3-\epsilon_{c})/[2(1-\epsilon_{c})]

\nu_{c1}=(\epsilon_{c}-2)/[2(\epsilon_{c}-1)]

\nu_{e}=(3-\epsilon_{e})/[2(1-\epsilon_{e})]
,
\Delta\eta_{B}=\eta_{B+}-\eta_{B-}
,

and
H^{(2)}
are the first and the second Hankel function,
J
and
Y
represent the first and the second Bessel function. Thus, the primordial power spectrum can be described by five free paraemeters, that is,
\epsilon_{c}
,
\epsilon_{e}
,
\mathcal{H}_{con}
,
\mathcal{H}_{exp}
and
\Delta\eta_{B}
. For the limit

and

as

\displaystyle|C_{1}

\displaystyle-C_{2}|^{2}\simeq

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta\tilde{\mathcal{H}}}{k}\cos\bigg(\frac{2k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg]\bigg/\bigg[\bigg(2\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}

\displaystyle+k\pi J_{\nu_{e}}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)Y_{\nu_{e}-1}\bigg(\frac{k}{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{e}}\bigg)

where

IV
CMB Lensing Anomaly

Weak lensing of the CMB smooths the shape of the observed power spectra. The gravitational lensing amplitude is defined as a rescaling factor of lensing potential power-spectrum
Calabrese et al. 2008
:

C_{\ell}^{\phi\phi}\longrightarrow A_{L}C_{\ell}^{\phi\phi}.

(8)

where
A_{L}=0
implies that the CMB is unlensed while
A_{L}=1
is the expected value in
\Lambda
CDM (PL) model. There is a nearly
3\sigma
deviation from
\Lambda
CDM (PL) in the Planck-2018 result with
A_{L}=1.18\pm 0.065\left(68\%,\,\texttt{Planck2018 TT,TE,EE+lowE}\right)

Aghanim et al. 2020
, implying a CMB lensing anomaly.

Our results show that
A_{L}
can be reduced to
1.128\pm 0.038
with P18 data sets and
1.116\pm 0.036
with P18+SPT3G data sets in the
\Lambda\text{CDM (BI)}+A_{L}
model, as shown in TABLE
2
. There are several possibilities to explain this result. Recent studies show that an oscillatory modulation of the primordial power spectrum named the primordial standard clock may explain the CMB lensing anomaly
Domènech et al. 2020
;
Hazra et al. 2022
. There are some oscillations of the spectrum in the BI scenario at large scales, as demonstrated in FIG.
1
. Furthermore, since the
\Lambda
CDM (BI) model enlarges the parameter space with respect to the
\Lambda
CDM (PL) model, degeneracy between the power spectrum template and the
A_{L}
parameter may arise.

Reionization of the universe is another effect that washes out the primordial anisotropies and the CMB power spectrum is suppressed by a factor of
e^{-2\tau_{reio}}
. Thus there is a strong degeneracy between optical depth
\tau_{reio}
and gravitational lensing amplitude
A_{L}
. Our results give that
\tau_{reio}
is approximately equal to 0.06 in
\Lambda
CDM (BI) model(with
A_{L}=1
) and approximately equal to 0.05 in
\Lambda\text{CDM (BI)}+A_{L}
model (with
A_{L}\approx 1.13
).

We guess that the CMB lensing anomaly may be caused by the degeneracy between
\tau_{reio}
and
A_{L}
. So we add a Gaussian prior of the optical depth with
\tau_{prior}=0.088\pm 0.015
from WAMP 9-year results
Hinshaw et al. 2013
as a new likelihood artificially. Our result implies that data sets with a prior of optical depth will give a larger
\tau_{reio}
that is approximately equal to 0.058 and the smaller
A_{L}
that is approximately equal to 1.10, which will ulteriorly remit the CMB lensing anomaly. By the way, the cosmological tensions are not aggravated when we add this prior, as shown in TABLE

V
Conclusion

In this article, we consider the primordial scenario to alleviate cosmological tensions and CMB lensing anomaly. In our BI scenario, the Hubble tension is reduced slightly on the basis of the
\Lambda
CDM (BI) model. If we consider the extension of the
\Lambda
CDM (BI) model with gravitational lensing amplitude
A_{L}
, there is a nearly
3\sigma
tension of the Hubble parameter compared to local measurements. The growth factor
S_{8}
with measurements of CMB in this BI scenario is consistent with the surveys of cosmic shear or galaxy clustering constraints
Asgari et al. 2021
;
Heymans et al. 2021
;
Amon et al. 2022
;
Secco et al. 2022
;
Abbott et al. 2022
. It is worth emphasizing that there is a wide range of
S_{8}
from about
0.70
to
0.80
by dynamical cosmological probes like weak lensing, galaxy clusters counts, redshift space distortion, or combinations of them
Perivolaropoulos and Skara 2022
.

In this BI scenario, the CMB lensing anomaly is remitted compared with the result of Planck 2018. Nevertheless, combinations with different data, especially the high-
\ell
data of CMB (like SPT3G data sets), help to break some partial parameter degeneracies. Breaking the degeneracy between optical depth
\tau_{reio}
and lensing amplitude
A_{L}
may alleviate the CMB lensing anomaly. We will deliberate on whether the high-
\ell
data of CMB can solve both cosmological tensions and CMB anomalies in the future.

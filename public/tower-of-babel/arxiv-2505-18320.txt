Connected sum of manifolds with spectral Ricci lower bounds
Gioacchino Antonelli, Kai Xu

Abstract
. Let n > 2 n>2 , γ > n − 1 n − 2 \gamma>\frac{n-1}{n-2} , and λ ∈ ℝ \lambda\in\mathbb{R} . We prove that if M M and N N are two smooth n n -manifolds that admit a complete Riemannian metric satisfying − γ ​ Δ + Ric > λ , -\gamma\Delta+\mathrm{Ric}>\lambda, then the connected sum M ​ # ​ N M\#N also admits such a metric. The construction geometrically resembles a Gromov-Lawson tunnel; the range γ > n − 1 n − 2 \gamma>\frac{n-1}{n-2} is sharp for this to hold.



1.
Introduction

On a smooth complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with
n\geqslant 2
, let the function
\Ric\in\Lip_{\loc}(M)
be defined as

\Ric(x):=\inf\Big\{\Ric_{x}(v,v):v\in T_{x}M,\,|v|=1\Big\}.

For constants
\gamma\geqslant 0,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we say that
M
satisfies
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\Ric)\geqslant\lambda

if any of the two following equivalent conditions holds (see
[
8
]
):

(i)

for all
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(M)
it holds
\int_{M}\gamma|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\Ric\cdot\varphi^{2}\geqslant\int_{M}\lambda\varphi^{2}
,

(ii)

there exists
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
such that
u>0
and
-\gamma\Delta u+\Ric\cdot u\geqslant\lambda u
on
M
.

This is a global condition on the Ricci curvature, and is (often strictly) weaker than pointwise Ricci lower bounds (see the examples in
[
1
,
2
]
). See
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
4
]
for an introduction and recent results on manifolds satisfying the latter condition. The study of Ricci lower bounds in the spectral sense has recently been connected to the study of stable minimal hypersurfaces; we refer to
[
6
,
7
,
12
]
.

In
[
2
]
, the authors proved the following volume comparison theorem: if

(1)

n\geqslant 3,\quad\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2},\quad\text{$M$ is closed and satisfies }\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\Ric)\geqslant n-1,

then
|M|\leqslant|SS^{n}|
. See
[
2
]
for more discussions about this result. Here, the bound
\frac{n-1}{n-2}
for
\gamma
might draw the most curiosity. It is in fact a sharp threshold: in the supercritical range
\gamma>\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, there exist warped product metrics on
SS^{n-1}\times SS^{1}
that satisfy
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\Ric)>0
, and then counterexamples to the volume bound above arise by taking large enough covering spaces: see
[
2
, Remark 4]
for more details about this. The same threshold also appeared in Bour–Carron
[
3
]
, where a technique based on the Bochner formula was used to show that
(
1
)
implies
b_{1}(M)\leqslant n
(the relevant statement
[
3
, Proposition 3.5]
actually inspired the warped-product counterexample above). See also Li–Wang
[
10
]
where the same bound of
\gamma
arises in a different context.

To summarize, the geometric and topological properties associated with the spectral Ricci lower bound appear to undergo a transition at the critical value
\gamma=\frac{n-1}{n-2}
. The aim of this note is to provide further evidence of this. Specifically, we prove the following Gromov–Lawson and Schoen–Yau type connected sum result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
\gamma>\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a (possibly non-connected) complete manifold satisfying
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\Ric)\geqslant\lambda
. Then for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in M
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is a complete manifold
(M^{\prime},g^{\prime})
with the following properties:

(i)

M^{\prime}
is topologically obtained from
M
by removing two small balls around
x_{1},x_{2}
and gluing back a tunnel
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times[0,1]
,

(ii)

g^{\prime}=g
on
M\setminus\big(B(x_{1},\varepsilon)\cup B(x_{2},\varepsilon)\big)
,

(iii)

\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta_{g^{\prime}}+\Ric_{g^{\prime}})\geqslant\lambda-\varepsilon
.

The following are its immediate consequences:

Corollary 1
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
\gamma>\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. For
i=1,2
, suppose
(M_{i},g_{i})
are (possibly non-connected) complete
n
-manifolds satisfying
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta_{M_{i}}+\Ric_{M_{i}})>\lambda
. Then
M_{1}\#M_{2}
admits a complete metric satisfying the same spectral Ricci lower bound.

Corollary 2
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
\gamma>\frac{n-1}{n-2}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and
M
be a (possibly non-connected) complete
n
-manifold satisfying
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\Ric)>\lambda
. Then
M\#(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})
admits a complete Riemannian metric satisfying the same spectral Ricci lower bound.

In particular, this implies that the constant
\frac{n-1}{n-2}
is sharp in Bour–Carron’s Betti number bound
[
3
, Proposition 3.5]
. Indeed, as a consequence of the corollary above, for every
n\geqslant 3
,
\gamma>\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, and
k\geqslant 1
,
\#^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})
admits a metric with
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\mathrm{Ric})>0
.

The proof of Theorem
1
follows from a tunnel construction in the manner of (but is technically different from) Gromov–Lawson and Schoen–Yau. The toy model comes from an observation of Bour–Carron: as a part of the rigidity result in
[
3
, Proposition 3.5]
, they observed that the metric
g=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+f(r)^{2}g_{SS^{n-1}}
and the function
u=f(r)^{2-n}
together satisfy

-\frac{n-1}{n-2}\Delta_{g}u+\Ric_{g}(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})\cdot u=0.

This identity holds for an arbitrary
f
. In particular, one can choose
f
so that
f(r)=|r|
for all
|r|\geqslant\varepsilon
. With this choice, one produces a metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\#\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfies
\lambda_{1}(-\frac{n-1}{n-2}\Delta+\Ric)\geqslant 0
. This is the local picture of our construction in
Theorem

1
.

To reach the proof of
Theorem

1
, we have to globalize this model. Given the initial manifold
M
and basepoints
x_{1},x_{2}
, we remove two tiny balls and glue back a tiny tunnel. Our goal is to find a metric
g^{\prime}
, and a function
\hat{u}>0
, so that
-\gamma\Delta_{g^{\prime}}\hat{u}+\Ric_{g^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{u}\geqslant(\lambda-\varepsilon)\hat{u}
. The easy step is to make
g^{\prime}
resemble the toy model
\mathrm{d}r^{2}+f(r)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
near the tunnel. It is less obvious how to merge the eigenfunction. The key observation is that in the toy model, we have
u\sim r^{2-n}
near the origin. Therefore, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the model degenerates to two copies of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a Green’s function on each of them. Inspired by this, we realize that one should try to merge a Green’s function. Hence, on the initial manifold
M
we will solve the equation

-\gamma\Delta u+\Ric\cdot u=(\lambda-\varepsilon)u+(\delta_{x_{1}}+\delta_{x_{2}}),

and the new function
\hat{u}
is produced by merging this solution
u
with the model solution
f^{2-n}
near the tunnel.

It is interesting to notice that the warping factor
f
here can be arbitrary as long as it is convex; see (
9
) for the precise conditions. This is in contrast with the Gromov–Lawson construction for scalar curvature, where the warping factor is carefully designed to satisfy a certain differential inequality.

In analogy with the higher codimensional surgery result for scalar curvature (see
[
9
, Theorem A]
or
[
14
, Corollary 6]
), it is interesting to ask if the condition
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\mathrm{Ric})>\lambda
is stable under surgery of codimension
\geqslant k
, provided that
2<k\leqslant n-1
and
\gamma>\frac{k-1}{k-2}
. Note that Theorem
1
covers the
k=n
case. We remark that surgery procedures preserving
\mathrm{Ric}>0
have been studied, e.g., in
[
17
,
18
]
, and the recent
[
13
]
.

Lemma 7

Abstract
We present new ALMA [O iii ] 88 observations of eight previously [C ii ] 158 -detected galaxies at 6.8 ≲ z ≲ 7.7 6.8\lesssim z\lesssim 7.7 . Six of our targets – the primary sample – are massive, UV-luminous galaxies drawn from the REBELS survey, while the remaining two are UV-fainter galaxies that were previously serendipitously detected through their luminous [C ii ] lines in the REBELS fields. We detect [O iii ] 88 emission in all eight galaxies at 6.2 − 17.7 ​ σ 6.2-17.7\sigma significance, and find them to be consistent with the local dwarf galaxy relation between L [O iii ] 88 L_{\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]${}_{88}${}}} and star formation rate. Our sample spans [O iii ]/[C ii ] ≈ 1.9 − 9.6 \text{[O\,{\sc iii}]/[C\,{\sc ii}]{}}\approx 1.9-9.6 , which is typical for the high-redshift galaxy population. Five of the primary targets benefit from JWST /NIRSpec observations, enabling a direct comparison of the [O iii ]/[C ii ] ratio against rest-optical ISM diagnostics. We supplement our high-redshift sample with eleven z ≈ 6 − 14 z\approx 6-14 galaxies in the literature for which similar ALMA and JWST observations are available, and furthermore compare to the [O iii ]/[C ii ] ratios measured for local dwarf galaxies. We find that, at fixed metallicity and ionization parameter, z > 6 z>6 galaxies show elevated [O iii ]/[C ii ] ratios compared to local dwarfs. Instead, we find that a large [O iii ] 4959,5007 + H ​ β \text{[O\,{\sc iii}]${}_{4959,5007}${}}+\mathrm{H}\beta equivalent width – a proxy for burstiness – is the main driver of the high [O iii ]/[C ii ] ratios seen in the early Universe, which is primarily due to [C ii ] being suppressed in bursty galaxies. Given the apparent validity of the [O iii ] 88 -SFR relation across most of cosmic time, as well as the abundance of young, bursty galaxies at high redshift, [O iii ] 88 is set to remain a powerful ISM tracer at the cosmic dawn.

Introduction

With the formation of the first stars and galaxies, the rapid enrichment of the early Universe with elements heavier than helium – known as metals – commenced
(
Dayal & Ferrara 2018
;
Maiolino & Mannucci 2019
)
. In atomic or ionic form, these metals are typically characterized by a large number of electronic transitions that give rise to emission lines at specific rest-frame wavelengths. Observing these lines in high-redshift galaxies provides key insights into the nature and physical conditions of their interstellar medium (ISM), and is thus vital for our understanding of early galaxy evolution.

Among the metals produced in the first core-collapse supernovae, oxygen is of particular importance. Not only is oxygen expected to be produced in large quantities
(
Woosley, Heger & Weaver 2002
;
Takahashi, Yoshida & Umeda 2018
;
Kobayashi, Karakas & Lugaro 2020
)
, it also gives rise to a wealth of bright cooling lines at rest-frame optical (e.g., [O
ii
]
3727,29
and [O
iii
]
5007
) and far-infrared (e.g., [O
iii
]
52
, [O
iii
]
88
, [O
i
]
63
and [O
i
]
145
) wavelengths.

There are multiple possible reasons for these elevated [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios in the high-redshift galaxy population. First of all, the physical conditions and resulting radiation fields of high-redshift galaxies are likely to be more extreme than in the local galaxy population, which should boost their ionization parameter
U
, defined as the dimensionless ratio of incident flux of ionizing photons and the number density of hydrogen. Most high-redshift galaxies show enhanced values of
U
with respect to galaxies in the local Universe (e.g.,
Shapley et al. 2015
;
Papovich et al. 2022
;
Reddy et al. 2023
), and such elevated ionization parameters are expected to boost the luminosity of the [O
iii
]
88
line, while potentially also ionizing C
+
into C
++
, thereby reducing the luminosity of [C
ii
] (e.g.,
Arata et al. 2020
;
Harikane et al. 2020
;
Vallini et al. 2020
;
Vallini et al. 2021
;
Vallini et al. 2024
;
Katz et al. 2022
).

In addition, the typical metallicities of high-redshift galaxies are lower than those of equally massive galaxies at the present day
(
Sanders et al. 2021
;
Sanders et al. 2024
;
Curti et al. 2023
;
Curti et al. 2024
;
Chemerynska et al. 2024
;
Sarkar et al. 2025
)
. Such low metallicities are expected to suppress [C
ii
] emission for a variety of reasons. To zeroth order, the [C
ii
] luminosity scales linearly with the number of C
+
atoms, and thus depends on the total carbon abundance which naturally decreases at low metallicities
(
Vallini et al. 2015
;
Olsen et al. 2017
;
Lagache, Cousin & Chatzikos 2018
;
Katz et al. 2019
;
Liang et al. 2024
)
. On top of that, the aforementioned time lag in the production of carbon with respect to oxygen should further reduce the overall luminosity of the [C
ii
] line in metal-poor systems where sub-solar C/O abundances are expected
(
Arata et al. 2020
;
Katz et al. 2022
;
Nyhagen et al. 2024
)
. The low-metallicity ISM is furthermore thought to be more porous, and the resulting lower PDR covering fraction implies a smaller [C
ii
]-emitting volume
(
Cormier et al. 2019
;
Hagimoto et al. 2025
)
. Finally, the fraction of [C
ii
] emission emanating from the ionized phase has been suggested to decrease in the low-metallicity ISM
(
Croxall et al. 2017
;
Cormier et al. 2019
;
Ramambason et al. 2022
, though see
Peng et al. 2025
)
.

In contrast, the suppression of [O
iii
]
88
is likely less severe at low metallicities due to the typically harder radiation fields in metal-poor galaxies, increasing the overall volume filling fraction of ionized gas and thus (partially) compensating for the lower oxygen abundance
(
Cormier et al. 2015
;
Arata et al. 2020
;
Witstok et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. Overall, this suggests that the few
z\gtrsim 6
galaxies for which
\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]/[C\,{\sc ii}]{}}\lesssim 2
ratios are observed, are likely to be among the oldest, least bursty, and most metal-enriched at their epoch (e.g.,
Algera et al. 2024b
;
Bakx et al. 2024
).

Testing these various predictions on the origin of the elevated [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio in high-redshift galaxies presents an opportunity for a powerful synergy between
JWST
and ALMA, as already noted by several theoretical works (e.g.,
Katz et al. 2019
;
Nakazato, Yoshida & Ceverino 2023
;
Vallini et al. 2024
). Specifically,
JWST
can provide the key metallicity, ionization parameter and burstiness measurements, which can then quantitatively be compared against the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios measured by ALMA. However, the sample size of galaxies with all of these measurements has so far remained rather limited. Indeed, despite the success in probing [O
iii
]
88
emission out to
z\approx 14
, the line has to date only been detected in just
\sim 20
galaxies at
z\gtrsim 6

(e.g.,
Harikane et al. 2020
;
Algera et al. 2024b
;
Bakx et al. 2024
, and references therein)
. Moreover, [C
ii
] has not confidently been detected beyond
z=8.3

(
Tamura et al. 2019
;
Bakx et al. 2020
)
, despite several attempts targeting the line in more distant galaxies
(
Laporte et al. 2019
;
Carniani et al. 2020
;
Fujimoto et al. 2024
;
Schouws et al. 2025
)
. Finally, most galaxies with an [O
iii
]
88
and [C
ii
] detection do not have the required ancillary
JWST
/NIRSpec data, and in the few cases where such observations are readily available, they have yet to be analyzed in the context of the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio.

In this work, we present new ALMA [O
iii
]
88
observations towards eight
z=6.8-7.7
galaxies first spectroscopically confirmed via [C
ii
] emission as part of the Reionization Era Bright Emission Line Survey (REBELS;
Bouwens et al. 2022

JWST
/NIRSpec as part of the Cycle 1 REBELS-IFU program (GO 1626, PI Stefanon; Stefanon et al. in preparation; see also
Algera et al. 2025

Rowland et al. 2025

JWST

ii

z\gtrsim 6

emission from our primary and serendipitous targets. We use a slightly different imaging approach for REBELS-25, given the high native spatial resolution of its observations, as detailed below. For the other seven pointings, we start by creating naturally-weighted datacubes using the
tclean
task in
casa
. First, we create dirty cubes with a channel width of
30\,\mathrm{km/s}
, and use these to determine the RMS in the cubes via a
3.5\sigma
sigma-clipped standard deviation. We subsequently use twice the RMS as our cleaning threshold, making use of the automasking capabilities implemented in
casa

(
Kepley et al. 2020
)
. We present the properties of the ALMA Band 8 datacubes in Table
1
. The typical native resolution of our datacubes is
\sim 0.7^{\prime\prime}\times 0.6^{\prime\prime}
, while the
1\sigma
line sensitivity ranges from
0.37-0.63\,\mathrm{mJy/beam}
measured in
30\,\mathrm{km/s}
channels.

The imaging strategy for the high-resolution [O
iii
]
88
observations of REBELS-25 closely follows the approach by
Rowland et al. 2024
and will be presented in Rowland et al. (in preparation). For the purposes of this work, however, we focus on the source-integrated emission of REBELS-25, for which we use the [O
iii
]
88
datacube tapered to a resolution of
1.0^{\prime\prime}
and imaged at a velocity resolution of
35\,\mathrm{km/s}
that will also be presented in Rowland et al. (in preparation). The reason for adopting a tapered cube is that flux measurements in the high-resolution (
\sim 0.17^{\prime\prime}
) cube may be biased due to a mismatch between the areas of the dirty and clean beams, known as the “
Jorsater & van Moorsel 1995
effect”
(see also
Czekala et al. 2021
, for an extensive discussion)
. For the rest of our targets, with a typical resolution of
\sim 0.6-0.8^{\prime\prime}
, this is not a concern, as demonstrated in Appendix
A
. The tapered [O
iii
]
88
cube of REBELS-25 achieves a sensitivity of
0.64\,\mathrm{mJy/beam}
per
35\,\mathrm{km/s}
channel around the line frequency (Table
1
).

2.2.3
Line Flux Measurements

We adopt an iterative procedure to extract the [O
iii
]
88
line fluxes of our targets, except for REBELS-25, for which we directly adopt the flux from Rowland et al. (in preparation). We start by extracting the flux in each velocity channel, using a circular aperture centered on the [C
ii
] position of our target. The size of the aperture is chosen manually for each source to ensure the full line flux is captured, and thus depends on the spatial extent of the [O
iii
]
88
emission of the galaxy and the resolution of the data. We fit the extracted spectrum with a single Gaussian to obtain a first guess for the center, amplitude and FWHM of the emission line. Where necessary, this fit also includes a constant offset from zero to account for a marginal level of continuum emission.
7
7

7

This is only the case for REBELS-18; for the other sources the continuum level is found to be consistent with zero at the
<1\sigma
level, and we therefore repeat the fit without a constant offset. We prefer this method over using
uvcontsub
as the latter is formally only applicable to sources in the phase center of the observations, whereas our sample includes off-center serendipitous sources. Moreover, allowing for a constant offset in the Gaussian fit enables propagating the uncertainty on the continuum level into the error on the line flux. Nevertheless, we have confirmed that the [O
iii
]
88
fluxes of the primary targets are consistent within
<1\sigma
when using
uvcontsub
instead of fitting a flat continuum in the image domain.
Following this initial Gaussian fit, we create a moment-0 map across
1.2\times
the FWHM of the emission line, which maximizes its S/N assuming a Gaussian profile (e.g.,
Novak et al. 2020
). We fit the moment-0 map with a 2D Gaussian, re-center the aperture based on the Gaussian centroid while keeping its size fixed, and re-extract and fit the spectrum. We repeat this procedure until convergence is reached, which is the case in
\leq 3
iterations. We then adopt the area under the 1D Gaussian fit as our line flux measurement.

We confirm that the [O
iii
]
88
line centroids, FWHMs, and fluxes measured for REBELS-12, REBELS-39 and REBELS-39-2 are consistent to within
1\sigma
between the Cycle 8 and Cycle 11 observations. To improve our sensitivity, we then concatenate these two datasets using
casa
task
concat
and ensure the visibilities are appropriately weighted via task
statwt
. We subsequently re-image the concatenated data following the aforementioned procedure, noting that for REBELS-12 this yields a small mosaic given the different Band 8 pointing centers. We utilize these deeper, concatenated datasets when discussing the properties of REBELS-12, REBELS-39 and REBELS-39-2.

We re-extract the [C
ii
] fluxes of our targets, which were previously measured by Schouws et al. (in preparation; see also
Bouwens et al. 2022
) following the same procedure for consistency.
8
8

8

As discussed in Section
2.1
, REBELS-12 has a peculiar [C
ii
] morphology. To extract its full [C
ii
] flux while simultaneously avoiding picking up noise, we adopt a large aperture of
2.5^{\prime\prime}
, but extract the spectrum only across pixels with significance
\geq 2\sigma
in the moment-0 map (following
Algera et al. 2024b
).
In doing so, we also re-image the [C
ii
] datacubes using a velocity resolution of
30\,\mathrm{km/s}
, to match the channel width of the [O
iii
]
88
data. For REBELS-25, we directly adopt the [C
ii
] flux from Rowland et al. (in preparation), which was measured in a consistent manner to the [O
iii
]
88
flux (i.e., utilizing a
1^{\prime\prime}
tapered [C
ii
] datacube, albeit with a higher spectral resolution of
15\,\mathrm{km/s}
). We present the [C
ii
] and [O
iii
]
88
flux measurements of our full sample in Table
2
.

2.3
JWST Data

Among the full [C
ii
]-detected REBELS sample, twelve galaxies were observed with the
JWST
/NIRSpec Integral Field Unit (IFU) as part of the Cycle 1 ‘REBELS-IFU’ program (GO 1626; PI Stefanon). For details on the science aims, data reduction, and line flux measurements, we refer to Stefanon et al. (in preparation) and
Rowland et al. 2025
, while we provide a brief summary here.

The 12 REBELS galaxies at
6.5\lesssim z\lesssim 7.7
were observed with the NIRSpec IFU using the prism mode. The targets were selected with a preference towards galaxies with bright [C
ii
] lines and dust continuum detections, as discussed in more detail in
Rowland et al. 2025
and Stefanon et al. (in preparation). This selection likely biases the sample towards the most massive and metal-rich galaxies within REBELS, while the parent survey itself already targeted some of the most UV-luminous and therefore likely massive galaxies at
z\gtrsim 6.5

(
Bouwens et al. 2022
;
Topping et al. 2022
)
.

The NIRSpec/IFU observations yield contiguous spectral coverage from
0.6-5.3\,\mu\mathrm{m}
at low spectral resolution (
R\sim 100
), and cover a field-of-view of
3^{\prime\prime}\times 3^{\prime\prime}
(approximately
15.7\,\mathrm{kpc}\times 15.7\,\mathrm{kpc}
at
z=7
). This wavelength range provides coverage of a variety of key optical lines such as [O
ii
]
3727,29
, H
\gamma
, H
\beta
and [O
iii
]
4959,5007
, as well as H
\alpha
below
z\approx 7
(though see
D’Eugenio et al. 2025
;
Pollock et al. 2025
for possible extensions of NIRSpec to longer wavelengths). In total, among the eight galaxies observed in [O
iii
]
88
emission as part of this work, REBELS-12, REBELS-14, REBELS-18, REBELS-25, and REBELS-39 are targeted in the IFU observations, with only the latter being at
z<7
(Table
2
). We will refer to this subset of five sources as the ‘REBELS-IFU sample’.

In each of the REBELS-IFU targets, the [O
ii
]
3727,29
, H
\beta
and [O
iii
]
4959,5007
lines are detected at high significance, while for REBELS-39 the H
\alpha
line is also detected. Emission line fluxes were measured by
Rowland et al. 2025
, after first subtracting the continuum using a third-order polynomial. In their fits, the widths of the various emission lines were set according to the NIRSpec prism line spread function. The [O
ii
] doublet is unresolved at the prism resolution, and is therefore fitted with a single Gaussian. The [O
iii
]
4959,5007
doublet can be spectrally resolved, and is fitted with two Gaussians with the constraint that their flux ratio is [O
iii
]
5007
/[O
iii
]
{}_{4959}=2.98
, as set by atomic physics
(
Storey & Zeippen 2000
)
. Moreover, H
\beta
and H
\gamma
are both fitted with a single Gaussian, although the latter is likely blended with the [O
iii
]
4363
and [Fe
ii
]
4360
lines, and we therefore follow
Rowland et al. 2025
by not using the H
\gamma
fluxes in this work. The H
\alpha
line, which we cover only for REBELS-39, is moderately blended with the [N
ii
]
6548,84
doublet, and
Rowland et al. 2025
therefore deblend the lines through a triple-Gaussian fit, keeping the flux ratio of the two nitrogen lines fixed to [N
ii
]
6584
/[N

{}_{6548}=3.049

(
Osterbrock & Ferland 2006
)
.

2.3.1
Dust corrections

For REBELS-39, in which both the H
\alpha
and H
\beta
lines are detected,
Rowland et al. 2025
correct the emission line fluxes for dust based on the Balmer decrement, assuming a
Calzetti et al. 2000
attenuation law. This yields a nebular attenuation for REBELS-39 of
A_{V,\mathrm{neb}}=0.62\pm 0.40
. For the
z>7
sample, however, this is not possible due to the lack of an H
\alpha
detection and no reliable measurement of the H
\gamma
line. Instead, we therefore apply dust corrections to the four
z>7
sources based on the stellar
A_{V,*}
obtained from SED-fitting of their NIRSpec spectra by
Fisher et al. 2025
. For these four sources, they measure an
A_{V,*}=0.17-0.27
. However, it is well-known that the nebular attenuation often exceeds the stellar attenuation (e.g.,
Reddy et al. 2015
;
Buat et al. 2018
;
Shivaei et al. 2020
) due to the ionized regions from where these emission lines originate typically being more dust-enshrouded. For the eight
z<7
REBELS-IFU galaxies where the
JWST
spectra cover the H
\alpha
line, Fisher et al. (in preparation) compare the stellar
A_{V,*}
obtained from their SED fits with the nebular value obtained from the Balmer decrement. They find that, on average, the nebular emission is more attenuated by a multiplicative factor of
2.02\pm 0.33
. We therefore dust-correct the emission line fluxes of our
z>7
targets using their stellar
A_{V,*}
inferred from SED-fitting, corrected by this factor, resulting in a nebular attenuation of
A_{V,\mathrm{neb}}=0.34-0.53
. We discuss the effects of dust in further detail in Section
6
.

2.3.2
Metallicities

Metallicities of the REBELS-IFU sample were measured by
Rowland et al. 2025
. As any auroral lines are either not detected or blended with stronger emission lines (e.g., [O
iii
]
4363
and H
\gamma
), we rely on the strong-line calibrations from
Sanders et al. 2024
. As extensively discussed in
Rowland et al. 2025
, we use the most robust available metallicity diagnostic on a case-by-case basis. For REBELS-39, where a dust correction based on the Balmer decrement is possible, we measure the metallicity through the
R23=(\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]${}_{4959,5007}${} + [O\,{\sc ii}]${}_{3727,29}${}})/\mathrm{H}\beta
diagnostic, which is generally considered the most accurate strong-line calibration (e.g.,
Maiolino & Mannucci 2019
). However, for our four
z>7
targets we instead adopt the
R3=\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]${}_{5007}${}}/\mathrm{H}\beta
diagnostic, which relies on two lines closely separated in wavelength, and is thus not significantly affected by dust.

Both the
R23
and
R3
diagnostics are double-valued, i.e., there is no one-to-one relation between the line ratio and oxygen abundance.
Rowland et al. 2025
therefore use two other diagnostics,
O32
and
Ne3O2
, to select the upper or lower branch (see also e.g.,
Kewley & Ellison 2008
). For all targets but REBELS-14, this selects the upper branch, while REBELS-14 is consistent with the peak of the
R3
-metallicity relation, implying it has the lowest metallicity among our sample. Overall, the metallicities of our five REBELS-IFU targets span
7.90\lesssim 12+\log(\mathrm{O/H})\lesssim 8.62
, equivalent to
0.16\lesssim Z/Z_{\odot}\lesssim 0.85
. Our most metal-rich target is REBELS-25, which was previously suggested to be a ‘mature’ galaxy based on its disk-like [C
ii
]-kinematics
(
Hygate et al. 2023
;
Rowland et al. 2024
)
and high dust mass
(
Algera et al. 2024a
)
.

2.3.3
O32
and EW([O
iii
]+H
\beta
) measurements

In Section
5
, we discuss the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios of our REBELS-IFU targets in the context of two further observables: the
O32=\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]${}_{5007}${}}/\text{[O\,{\sc ii}]${}_{3727,29}${}}
ratio, a proxy for ionization parameter (e.g.,
Kewley & Dopita 2002
), and the equivalent width of the [O
iii
]
4959,5007
+H
\beta
complex, which we use as a proxy for burstiness. For the
O32
ratio, we use the [O
iii
]
5007
and [O
ii
] fluxes of the REBELS-IFU sample measured by
Rowland et al. 2025
, applying the dust corrections from Section
2.3.1
.
Rowland et al. 2025
also provide the
\mathrm{EW}(\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]${}_{5007}${}})
line for all twelve REBELS-IFU galaxies in their work (see also
Endsley et al. 2025
), and here we use their approach to compute
\mathrm{EW}(\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]${}_{4959,5007}${}}+\mathrm{H}\beta)
in an identical manner.

2.4
The Dwarf Galaxy Sample

Throughout this work, we will compare the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios of our high-redshift targets to local dwarf galaxies drawn from the
Herschel
Dwarf Galaxy Survey
(
Madden et al. 2013
;
Cormier et al. 2015
, DGS;)
. In total, 38 of the DGS sources have been observed in both the [C
ii
] and [O
iii
]
88
lines. We here focus on the 26 sources with
\mathrm{sSFR}>10^{-10}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}
to include only dwarfs that are star-forming, enabling a more direct comparison to [O
iii
]
88
-detected galaxies at high redshift.

Metallicities of the DGS sample have been determined in
Madden et al. 2013
, and make use of optical strong lines following
Pilyugin & Thuan 2005
. Fine structure line fluxes for the DGS galaxies are provided in
Cormier et al. 2015
, and we adopt their measurements directly. We also make use of the dust-corrected [O
iii
]
5007
and [O
ii
] fluxes provided by
De Vis et al. 2017
to infer
O32
for the DGS sources, and adopt EW([O
iii
]+H
\beta
) measurements from
Kumari et al. 2024
.

The comparison between high-redshift and dwarf galaxies is primarily motivated by the fact that both populations show typical line ratios of
\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]/[C\,{\sc ii}]{}}>1

(
Harikane et al. 2020
;
Algera et al. 2024b
, e.g.,)
. Furthermore, the typical metallicities and ionization parameters of local dwarfs are similar to those of [O
iii
]
88
-detected high-redshift galaxies (Section
5
), which are two parameters thought to be important in setting the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio
(
Vallini et al. 2015
;
Vallini et al. 2020
;
Olsen et al. 2017
;
Katz et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
. In addition, high-redshift galaxies and dwarf galaxies are typically found to follow the same relation between [O
iii
]
88
luminosity and star-formation rate
(e.g.,
De Looze et al. 2014
;
Harikane et al. 2020
;
Zavala et al. 2024
;
Witstok et al. 2025
, see also Section
4
)
.

On the other hand – as is the case with any comparison to purported low-
z
analogs – the comparison to local dwarfs is not perfect, given that the DGS sample has much lower SFRs (
\mathrm{SFR}\sim 10^{-2}-30\,M_{\odot}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}
) than those of typical [O
iii
]
88
-detected galaxies at
z\gtrsim 6
(
\mathrm{SFRs}\sim 5-300\,M_{\odot}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}
). While important to keep in mind, dwarf galaxies still constitute the best comparison sample to high-redshift [O
iii
]
88
-detected galaxies on the basis of their other physical properties.

3
Results

We present the results of our new [O
iii
]
88
observations in this section. We first conclusively establish through a second emission line detection that the two serendipitous neighbors are, indeed, at
z\approx 7
. Subsequently, we collectively discuss the [O
iii
]
88
lines of our full sample of eight high-redshift galaxies.

3.1
[O
iii
]
88
emission and redshift confirmation of the serendipitous neighbors

As discussed in
Fudamoto et al. 2021
and
van Leeuwen et al. 2024
, the ALMA datacubes of both the REBELS-12 and REBELS-39 fields reveal a bright, serendipitous emission line a distance of
11.5^{\prime\prime}
and
2.4^{\prime\prime}
away from the primary REBELS targets, respectively. The small velocity difference between the [C
ii
] lines of the two primary targets and these bright serendipitous lines (
|\Delta v|\lesssim 250\,\mathrm{km/s}
) suggests they are emitted by galaxies at the same redshift, and that the serendipitous lines thus also correspond to [C
ii
]. However, when limited to just a single emission line, one cannot definitively rule out that the detected line instead originates from a foreground galaxy not physically associated with the primary REBELS targets (see e.g.,
Bakx et al. 2024
;
Jin et al. 2024
).

Our ALMA Band 8 observations detect a second line in REBELS-12-2 and REBELS-39-2 at
6.2
and
13.4\sigma
significance, respectively, measured as the peak pixel S/N in the moment-0 maps. The lines are detected at the exact frequency where [O
iii
]
88
emission is expected, if the initial detections by
Fudamoto et al. 2021
and
van Leeuwen et al. 2024
are identified as [C
ii
]. The detection of this additional emission line thus unambiguously confirms the
z\approx 7
nature of the two serendipitously detected sources, REBELS-12-2 and REBELS-39-2. We show the moment-0 maps and 1D spectra of the lines in Figures
1
and
2
, respectively. Moreover, we present the emission line parameters for both the [O
iii
]
88
and [C
ii
] lines in Table
2
.

3.3
[O

]
88
and [C

As both the [C
ii
] and [O
iii
]
88

we compare the centroids (left panel) and FWHMs (right panel) of both emission lines. We find that the redshifts implied by the [C

] and [O
iii
]
88

\lesssim 40\,\mathrm{km\,s}^{-1}
for 7/8 sources, with only REBELS-12 showing a clear difference between the two line centers. Moreover, the FWHMs of both lines are consistent within
\lesssim 1\sigma
for 6/8 sources, suggesting that both lines generally trace the same underlying gravitational potential. For two of our targets – REBELS-14 and again REBELS-12 – the line FWHMs are markedly different, with the [O
iii
]
88

The nature of REBELS-12, being a merger of an [O

]
88

]
88
-faint component, was previously discussed by
Algera et al. 2024b
and in Section
2.1
. We therefore perform a double-Gaussian fit to the [C
ii

), and associate the redshifted component with the [O
iii
]
88
emission observed from REBELS-12 as in
Algera et al. 2024b
. When considering only the redshifted component, the [O

]
88
and [C

\sim 1\sigma
level. The [C

]
88
line, although the FWHMs are consistent within the uncertainties. From here on, we will use ‘REBELS-12’ to refer to this redshifted [O
iii
]
88
- and [C
ii
]-emitting component, while we reserve ‘REBELS-12-blue’ and ‘REBELS-12-full’ to refer to the (blueshifted) [O
iii
]
88
-faint and complete system, respectively (see also Table
2
).

For REBELS-14, the [O
iii
]
88

3
), where the
JWST
/NIRSpec IFU data reveal an [O

]
5007
-luminous component (Figure

]
88

\sim 4\sigma
feature to the west of REBELS-14, connected to the main component via a
\sim 2\sigma
‘bridge’, without an obvious counterpart in [C

]
88
data to the [C

] resolution in Appendix

, this manifests as an extended [O
iii
]
88
tail towards the south-west, which could hint at the presence of a nearby [O
iii
]
88
-emitting companion or an (ionized) outflow. While a detailed investigation of this particular system is beyond the scope of this work, we do note that REBELS-14 shows the highest [O
iii
]/[C

5
), which may be linked to its different morpho-kinematics compared to the rest of our targets.

In the subsequent analysis, we are primarily interested in the global [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] luminosity ratios of our targets, and how these depend on galaxy physical properties such as metallicity and ionization parameter. While a resolved analysis is undoubtedly interesting (c.f.,
Akins et al. 2022
;
Vallini et al. 2024
), our goal is to first set an unresolved ‘benchmark’ on what governs the [O

] and [O
iii
]
88
is beyond the scope of this work. Moreover, such a comparison is hindered by the large difference in angular resolution – and in some cases relative depth – of the current emission line data. For our purposes, it suffices to demonstrate that the [C
ii
] and [O
iii
]
88
emission trace each other reasonably well, which we do in Appendix
A
by tapering the [O
iii
]
88
data to the [C

iii
]
88
and [C

Kohandel et al. 2019
;
Akins et al. 2022
) – their global line ratio is still a meaningful quantity tracing the multi-phase nature of the ISM. We furthermore highlight that a detailed spatially resolved analysis of the [O
iii
]
88
and [C
ii
] morpho-kinematics for REBELS-25 will be presented by Rowland et al. (in preparation), while high-resolution [C
ii
] morpho-kinematics for several other REBELS galaxies will be the subject of Phillips et al. (in preparation).

We compare the [O

JWST

\mathrm{SFR}_{\mathrm{UV}}

For REBELS-25, the IR-based SFR was recently determined by
Algera et al. 2024a
, making use of six-band ALMA continuum observations that capture both the peak and the Rayleigh-Jeans tail of its dust emission. For the other five primary targets, we make use of the dust continuum emission underlying the [O
iii
]
88
line in the ALMA Band 8 observations presented in this work, which in combination with the
160\,\mu\mathrm{m}
continuum observations presented by
Inami et al. 2022
enables a measurement of the IR luminosity (across
8-1000\,\mu\mathrm{m}
) based on a dual-band Modified Blackbody fit (e.g.,
Algera et al. 2024b
). We convert

to a star formation rate adopting the conversion in
Inami et al. 2022

\mathrm{SFR}_{\mathrm{IR}}/(M_{\odot}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1})=1.2\times 10^{-10}\times(L_{\mathrm{IR}}/L_{\odot})
.

The full set of Band 8 continuum observations – which also includes an additional four REBELS galaxies for which [O
iii
]
88
could not simultaneously be observed due to low atmospheric transmission – will be presented in an upcoming work (Algera et al. in preparation). Briefly, however, we find that REBELS-18 is robustly (
>5\sigma

IR

z\approx 7

Sommovigo et al. 2022

z\approx 4-7

Witstok et al. 2023
;
Mitsuhashi et al. 2024
).

5
The [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] Ratios of high-redshift and local dwarf galaxies

In recent years, ALMA observations of high-redshift galaxies (
z\gtrsim 6
) have demonstrated that their [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios are significantly larger than what is commonly observed in the local Universe (e.g.,
Hashimoto et al. 2019
;
Laporte et al. 2019
;
Bakx et al. 2020
;
Carniani et al. 2020
;
Harikane et al. 2020
;
Akins et al. 2022
;
Witstok et al. 2022
;
Ren et al. 2023
;
Fujimoto et al. 2024
). A likely explanation is that these elevated line ratios are driven by the typically bursty conditions of high-redshift galaxies generating strong ionizing radiation fields (e.g.,
Arata et al. 2020
;
Harikane et al. 2020
;
Katz et al. 2022
;
Algera et al. 2024b
;
Vallini et al. 2024
), in combination with [C
ii
] likely being suppressed at low metallicity (e.g.,
Vallini et al. 2015
;
Ferrara et al. 2019
;
Liang et al. 2024
;
Gurman et al. 2024
). However, several other – potentially interdependent – factors may also play a role, such as selection effects
(
Algera et al. 2024b
;
Bakx et al. 2024
)
, different morpho-kinematics of [C
ii
] and [O
iii
]
88

(
Carniani et al. 2017
;
Carniani et al. 2020
;
Kohandel et al. 2019
;
Schimek et al. 2024
)
, time-variability of the relative [C
ii
] and [O
iii
]
88
intensities due to photo-evaporation
(
Vallini et al. 2017
)
, a low PDR covering fraction or otherwise porous ISM suppressing the [C
ii
] emission
(
Chevance et al. 2016
;
Cormier et al. 2019
;
Harikane et al. 2020
;
Ramambason et al. 2022
;
Ura et al. 2023
;
Hagimoto et al. 2025
)
, a high PDR density leading to collisional de-excitation of [C
ii
] emission
(
Harikane et al. 2020
;
van Leeuwen et al. 2025
)
, a top-heavy IMF, and/or a sub-solar C/O abundance
(
Katz et al. 2022
;
Nyhagen et al. 2024
)
.

Although limited to a small sample size, we can investigate several of these hypotheses. For five of our targets, as well as several other high-
z
galaxies in the literature (Section
5.2
), we have metallicity and ionization parameter measurements from
JWST
/NIRSpec observations. Moreover, the two serendipitous sources (REBELS-12-2 and REBELS-39-2) benefit from a different selection than the typical UV-based selection of high-redshift galaxies, similar to the quasar companion galaxies in
Bakx et al. 2024
.

We show the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios of our sample as a function of SFR in Fig.
5
b, and once again compare to
z>6
galaxies targeted in [O
iii
]
88
drawn from a variety of literature studies. We find that the line ratios of our sample, which span [O
iii
]/[C
ii
]
\approx 1.9-9.6
, are typical for the high-redshift galaxy population. REBELS-14, in particular, is confidently detected in both lines, and shows a ratio of
\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]/[C\,{\sc ii}]{}}\approx 10
, among the highest observed at
z>6
(c.f.,
Ren et al. 2023
). The observed line ratios are well above what is observed in starburst galaxies in the local Universe, indicated via the green line and shading in Fig.
5
adopted from
De Looze et al. 2014
. Local dwarf galaxies show larger line ratios, with [O
iii
]
88
typically being more luminous than [C
ii
] (c.f., blue line and shading in Fig.
5
). However, barring some exceptions
(
Kumari et al. 2024
)
, the typical line ratio for dwarf galaxies of [O
iii
]/[C
ii
]
\approx 2
is still below that seen in the high-redshift population
(
Cormier et al. 2015
;
Cormier et al. 2019
)
.

In what follows, we quantitatively investigate the origin of the elevated [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios of high-redshift galaxies. Specifically, we first investigate the impact of selection effects on the line ratio (Section
5.1
), and then explore scaling relations with metallicity (Section
5.2.1
), ionization parameter (Section
5.2.2
) and burstiness (Section
5.2.3
).

5.1
The [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] Ratios of [C
ii
]-selected Galaxies

Recently,
Bakx et al. 2024
presented ALMA Band 8 observations targeting the [O
iii
]
88
line in 13 [C
ii
]-selected galaxies at
6\lesssim z\lesssim 7.5
. These galaxies were serendipitously detected as nearby neighbors around high-redshift quasars by
Venemans et al. 2020
(see also
Decarli et al. 2017
). Given that [C
ii
] is generally considered to be a reliable star-formation rate tracer (e.g.,
De Looze et al. 2014
;
Schaerer et al. 2020
), mostly independent of obscuration, these galaxies are essentially SFR-selected, distinguishing them from the typically UV-selected galaxy population at high redshift identified based on its level of unobscured star formation. Intriguingly,
Bakx et al. 2024
detect only one of their targets in [O
iii
]
88
emission, and use stacking to infer the typical line ratio of this population to be [O
iii
]/[C
ii
]
<1.2
. This is in reasonable agreement with
Walter et al. 2018
, who measure
\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]/[C\,{\sc ii}]{}}=1.58\pm 0.24
in a single [C
ii
]-selected quasar companion galaxy, somewhat below the typical line ratio of
\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]/[C\,{\sc ii}]{}}\gtrsim 2-10
of the UV-selected
z\gtrsim 6
population.

Our two serendipitous targets are selected in a similar manner to the
Bakx et al. 2024
sample, albeit around massive UV-selected galaxies, instead of around quasars. We infer line ratios of [O
iii
]/[C
ii
]
=3.1_{-1.0}^{+1.9}
and [O
iii
]/[C
ii
]
=4.1_{-1.4}^{+2.2}
for REBELS-12-2 and REBELS-39-2, respectively, which is comparable to those of the UV-selected population at
z\gtrsim 6
. While limited to just two sources, this suggests that the elevated [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios of high-redshift galaxies may not be fully due to a UV- versus [C
ii
]-based selection.

Alternatively, the low line ratios for quasar companion galaxies compared to what we find for massive galaxy companions could suggest that the central quasar affects the ISM of galaxies in its immediate environment through feedback and/or outflows (e.g.,
Zana et al. 2022
;
Ferrara et al. 2023
). Direct evidence of the quasar’s effect on the ISM of a nearby companion was recently presented by
Decarli et al. 2024
, although this represents a system in the process of merging. It is unclear if the quasar is also able to affect the ISM on
\gtrsim 50\,\mathrm{kpc}
scales, which would be necessary to explain the difference in the observed line ratios between our serendipitous targets and the
Bakx et al. 2024
sample. Moreover, it is possible that a few of the companion galaxies in the
Venemans et al. 2020
sample are spurious, as blind line searches have yielded seemingly robust emission lines detections that were later found to be due to noise
(
Hayatsu et al. 2017
;
Hayatsu et al. 2019
;
Uzgil et al. 2021
)
. Moreover, a small fraction of the emission lines detected in the quasar fields – particularly those at larger angular separations from the central quasar (
Bakx et al. 2024
; their Fig. 9) – could correspond to CO lines of foreground emitters. As discussed in detail by
Bakx et al. 2024
, any such contamination by low-redshift galaxies would also cause the average [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio of the quasar companion galaxies to be underestimated.

While the difference between the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios of our two serendipitous targets and the
Bakx et al. 2024
quasar companion galaxies is therefore intriguing, future studies leveraging larger samples of [C
ii
]-selected galaxies are needed for more robust comparisons. At the same time, further constraints on the stellar emission of [C
ii
]-selected galaxies is crucial to better understand their place in the framework of galaxy evolution.

5.3
The origin of the elevated [O
iii

ii
] ratios at high redshift

In the previous sections, we discussed how the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio for dwarf and high-redshift galaxies appears to depend on burstiness, and to a lesser extent on ionization parameter, while metallicity does not seem to be a key driver of the line ratio. However, we have also noted that these parameters themselves may (anti-)correlate (c.f., Figure
6
), which could complicate this interpretation. We therefore proceed by performing a multivariate fit of the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio against metallicity, ionization parameter and burstiness simultaneously. By doing so in a Bayesian fashion, we are furthermore able to robustly quantify any degeneracies between the various fitting parameters.

We demonstrated in Section
5.2
that the dependence of the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio on metallicity, ionization parameter and burstiness can be well-captured by a power law (i.e., a linear function in log-space), thus motivating a trivariate linear fit of the form

\begin{split}\log\left(\dfrac{\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]{}{}}}{\text{[C\,{\sc ii}]{}}}\right)&=\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}\times\left[12+\log(\mathrm{O/H})-8\right]\\
&\quad+\beta_{2}\times\log(O32)\\
&\quad+\beta_{3}\times\left[\log\left(\text{EW([O\,{\sc iii}]+H$\beta$){}}\right)-3\right]\end{split}

(1)

Here the equivalent width is expressed in Angstrom, and we normalize the metallicity and EW at
12+\log(\mathrm{O/H})=8
and
10^{3}\,\mathrm{\AA}
, as before. We perform the fit using the
emcee
package
(
Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013
)
, and simultaneously fit to the high-redshift and DGS samples to improve constraints. This is appropriate given that both populations show similar scaling relations between their [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios and the aforementioned three physical parameters. To account for uncertainties on all of the measurements, we treat the metallicity, ionization parameter and equivalent width as latent variables, adopting a Gaussian prior with mean and standard deviation equal to the measured value and uncertainty, respectively. We moreover impose a minimum error on all quantities of
0.10\,\mathrm{dex}
and include an intrinsic scatter term to account for additional scatter about the relation not captured by the measurement uncertainties.

We show the corner plot (omitting the latent variables) in Figure
13
in Appendix
D
. The best-fit equation is displayed in full in Figure
8
, with the best-fit parameters being
\beta_{0}=0.75\pm 0.11
,
\beta_{1}=-0.18\pm 0.13
,
\beta_{2}=-0.07_{-0.14}^{+0.15}
and
\beta_{3}=0.66\pm 0.12
. Figure
8
also displays the residuals with respect to the best fit as a function of metallicity,
O32
and EW([O
iii
]+H
\beta
). The lack of a clear trend in the residuals suggests that our multivariate fit appropriately captures the behavior of [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] across the full parameter space.

From the fit, we can draw several interesting conclusions. First of all, among the three scaling factors, only the value of
\beta_{3}=0.66\pm 0.12
is significantly different from zero (at the
\sim 5\sigma
level). This supports our previous hypothesis that burstiness is indeed the main parameter governing the elevated [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios of high-redshift galaxies. We obtain only a mild anti-correlation with metallicity (
\beta_{1}=-0.18\pm 0.13
) while no correlation with
O32
is observed (
\beta_{2}=-0.07_{-0.14}^{+0.15}
). This suggests that the variation with metallicity and ionization parameter seen in Figure
7
is mostly captured by variations in the burstiness. While the corner plot (Figure
13
) highlights a degeneracy between the coefficients
\beta_{2}
and
\beta_{3}
, scaling with
O32
and EW([O
iii
]+H
\beta
) respectively, this degeneracy is captured within the quoted fitting uncertainties, and hence does not change our interpretation.

5.4
The effect of burstiness on the [O
iii
]
88

In the previous section, we found that the [O
iii
]/[C

iii

+ H
\beta
complex. In what follows, we consider the [O

]
88
and [C

L_{\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]${}_{88}${}}}/\mathrm{SFR}
and
L_{\text{[C\,{\sc ii}]{}}}/\mathrm{SFR}
, where the star formation rate is measured as the sum of its UV- and infrared-based components. It should be kept in mind, however, that obscured SFR measurements at
z\gtrsim 6

(
Bakx et al. 2021
;

;

, as is the case for most of the high-redshift sample compiled in this work.

(Section

).

6
Caveats

Our analysis suggests the elevated [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios of typical high-redshift and some dwarf galaxies are likely due to their bursty nature, which suppresses [C
ii
] relative to [O
iii
]
88
emission. However, we discuss some caveats to this interpretation below.

6.1
Dust and spatial offsets between emission lines

Throughout this work, we have compared the far-infrared [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio to various rest-optical diagnostics. For these comparisons to be physically meaningful, these different emission lines need to emanate from similar physical regions within the galaxy (c.f.,
Vijayan et al. 2024
;
Vijayan et al. 2025
). However, dust obscuration can affect the observed emission, and indeed spatial offsets between dust, UV, and line emission in high-redshift galaxies have been seen both in observations (e.g.,
Inami et al. 2022
;
Killi et al. 2024
;
Rowland et al. 2024
) and simulations (e.g.,
Behrens et al. 2018
;
Cochrane et al. 2019
;
Pallottini et al. 2022
;
Esmerian & Gnedin 2024
;
Ocvirk et al. 2024
).

Currently, we have no clear evidence for such offsets between rest-optical and far-infrared oxygen emission in our targets on scales of a few kpc, except in the dustiest galaxy among the sample REBELS-25 (Figure
4
). Similarly, the [O
iii
]
88
and [C
ii
] emission appear mostly co-spatial at the current resolution of the observations (Figure
1
), although high-resolution [C
ii
] data is needed to verify this. While the currently available data thus suggest that our spatially-integrated analysis of FIR and optical lines is justified, we proceed by discussing how any sub-resolution spatial offsets between the different emission line probes could affect our interpretation.

In particularly dust-obscured sources such as ULIRGs, rest-optical metallicity diagnostics have been found to underestimate the true metallicity
(
Chartab et al. 2022
)
. Circumventing this requires far-infrared metallicity diagnostics such as the [O
iii
]
88
/[N
iii
]
57
ratio (e.g.,
Nagao et al. 2011
), which are currently not available for our sample. However, the typical dust masses of ULIRGs (
M_{\mathrm{dust}}\sim 10^{8-9}\,M_{\odot}
;
da Cunha et al. 2010
;
Clements et al. 2018
) are
\gtrsim 10-100\times
larger than those of REBELS galaxies, which have a typical
M_{\mathrm{dust}}\sim 10^{7-7.5}\,M_{\odot}

(
Sommovigo et al. 2022
;
Algera et al. 2025
)
. Moreover,
JWST
/NIRSpec observations reveal REBELS-IFU galaxies to be spatially extended on
>\mathrm{kpc}
scales
(
Rowland et al. 2025
)
, unlike the typically compact ULIRG population (
<1\,\mathrm{kpc}
). Overall, the dust mass surface densities of ULIRGs thus far exceed those of typical
z\sim 7
galaxies, making it unlikely that the metallicities of moderately dusty high-redshift galaxies are currently significantly underestimated due to a lack of FIR diagnostics.

For the same reason, it is unlikely that dust obscuration significantly affects the observed trend between [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio and ionization parameter or burstiness. However, if any heavily obscured regions are missed in the NIRSpec observations, these are likely young and star-forming (e.g.,
Díaz-Santos et al. 2007
;
Bohn et al. 2023
), and thus presumably have a high
U
and EW([O
iii
]+H
\beta
). Qualitatively, this would thus drive our dustiest sources to higher values of
O32
and burstiness. High-resolution [O
iii
]
88
as well as ALMA dust continuum observations will be necessary to investigate if this is indeed the case.

Finally, for 4/5 galaxies within the REBELS-IFU sample we lack robust measurements of the Balmer decrement (Section
2.3.1
). This naturally introduces additional uncertainty into their
O32
measurements, although metallicities and [O
iii
]
4959,5007
+H
\beta
equivalent widths are less likely to be significantly affected (c.f., Section
2.3.1
). For most of the high-redshift comparison sample, multiple Balmer lines are generally detected
(
Jones et al. 2024
;
Scholtz et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, making the resulting dust corrections generally more reliable. However, the precise shape of the dust attenuation curve – which is generally unknown at high redshift and may vary significantly between individual galaxies and/or with redshift
(
Fisher et al. 2025
;
Reddy et al. 2025
;
Shivaei et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
– still introduces additional uncertainty into any dust-corrected quantities.

6.2
Other parameters possibly driving the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio

We have focused our analysis on the scaling of the [O
iii
]
88
and [C
ii
] emission with metallicity (traced via oxygen abundance), ionization parameter (traced via
O32
), and burstiness (traced via EW([O
iii
]+H
\beta
)). While these parameters have been hypothesized to affect the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio – and we indeed find that burstiness plays a major role – this does not rule out the possibility that different quantities can also, or perhaps even more strongly, affect the line ratio.

6.2.1
Electron densities

Indeed, one additional parameter that is likely important in setting the [O
iii
]
88
emission from galaxies is the electron density, as a high
n_{e}
can lead to collisional de-excitation of the [O
iii
]
88
line, and thus suppress the [O
iii
]
88
luminosity
(
Cormier et al. 2015
;
van Leeuwen et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. In recent years, the
JWST
has enabled electron density measurements at high redshift primarily through the [O
ii
] doublet
(
Isobe et al. 2023
;
Abdurro’uf et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. However, we have not invoked this quantity in our analysis, as most of our high-redshift sample lacks
n_{e}
measurements, given that the [O
ii
] doublet cannot be resolved with NIRSpec prism spectroscopy.

While the ratio of the [O
iii
]
88
and [O
iii
]
5007
lines has also been used to infer electron densities at high redshift
(
Fujimoto et al. 2024
;
Abdurro’uf et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, the usefulness of this line ratio is limited in the context of our analysis. For one, the [O
iii
]
88
/[O
iii
]
5007
line ratio is degenerate between both the electron density and temperature (
T_{e}
), and an auroral line measurement yielding
T_{e}
is required to break this degeneracy
(
Fujimoto et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. Auroral line measurements are, however, not available for the REBELS-IFU sample
(
Rowland et al. 2025
)
. Furthermore, the [O
iii
]
88
/[O
iii
]
5007
line ratio can be affected by dust attenuation of the [O
iii
]
5007
line, and is thus strongly dependent on the availability and reliability of dust corrections. Finally, recent work by
Harikane et al. 2025
has suggested that the interpretation of joint ALMA and
JWST
line ratios is complicated in the presence of a multi-phase ISM, resulting in inconsistent electron densities inferred from optical and far-IR oxygen lines. In light of these various uncertainties, we have not attempted to infer electron densities for our targets via the [O
iii
]
88
/[O
iii
]
5007
line diagnostic, nor explicitly investigated its effect on [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios. Future high-resolution (
R\gtrsim 1000
)
JWST
/NIRSpec follow-up of our targets resolving the [O
ii
] doublet will enable investigating the effect of the electron density in more detail.

6.2.2
C/O Abundances

While metallicity was not found to play a major role in setting the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio in Section
5
, we note that we have assumed solar abundance ratios throughout this work. However, the C/O abundance is known to decrease at low metallicities
(
Nicholls et al. 2017
;
Berg et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
, and this has been hypothesized to boost the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio in young, metal-poor galaxies
(
Arata et al. 2020
;
Katz et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. While this scenario cannot completely be ruled out as we lack detailed abundance measurements, we did not find a clear trend between [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] and oxygen abundance, which is expected to be enhanced if low C/O abundances further suppress [C
ii
] at low metallicities (Figure
7
). Similarly, we did not find a clear trend between
L_{\text{[C\,{\sc ii}]{}}}/\mathrm{SFR}
and oxygen abundance (Figure
15
in Appendix
E
), which suggests that the effects of metallicity or non-solar abundance ratios are subdominant compared to burstiness.

6.2.3
Time-dependent [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios

Given the dynamical and bursty conditions expected in high-redshift galaxies, their ISM conditions will undoubtedly vary with time.
Vallini et al. 2017
show that the [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratio of individual giant molecular clouds is likely to be time-dependent, with a short period (
\lesssim 5\,\mathrm{Myr}
) after a starburst being characterized by high [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios. On longer timescales, the clouds are photo-evaporated resulting in a sharp drop in [O
iii
]/[C
ii
]. Qualitatively similar results were recently reported by
Kohandel, Pallottini & Ferrara 2025
, focusing on a simulated high-redshift analog of the
z=14.18
galaxy GS-z14. They also find that high [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios are transient, being related to temporarily high ionization parameters (
\log U\sim-1
) linked to merger-driven starburst events. Observationally, this time-variation of the high-redshift ISM can of course not be captured. However, these theoretical works qualitatively support our interpretation that high [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios are associated with rapid star formation in young, bursty galaxies, while also suggesting that a biased selection towards such bursty systems may play an additional role.

7
Summary and conclusions

We have presented new ALMA [O
iii
]
88
observations towards eight previously [C
ii
]-detected galaxies at
6.8\lesssim z\lesssim 7.7
. Six of these – our primary targets – are UV-luminous galaxies drawn from the REBELS survey
(
Bouwens et al. 2022
)
, while the remaining two were serendipitously identified as dust-obscured neighbors to our REBELS targets through their bright [C
ii
] emission
(
Fudamoto et al. 2021
;
van Leeuwen et al. 2024
)
. Five of our primary targets benefit from
JWST
/NIRSpec IFU prism spectroscopy, which yields strong-line oxygen abundances, a measure of their ionization parameter through the
O32
diagnostic, and the equivalent width of the [O
iii
]
4959,5007
+H
\beta
complex, a proxy for burstiness.

We first focus on the ALMA [O
iii
]
88
emission of our eight targets, from which we draw the following conclusions:

1.

We detect the [O
iii
]
88
line in our two serendipitous targets, unambiguously confirming their redshifts to be
z=7.352
(REBELS-12-2) and
z=6.838
(REBELS-39-2) through both the [C
ii
] and [O
iii
]
88
lines. We infer
\text{[O\,{\sc iii}]/[C\,{\sc ii}]{}}\approx 3-4
for both, which suggests that the elevated [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] measurements at
z>6
are unlikely to solely be the result of a biased selection towards UV-luminous galaxies (c.f.,
Algera et al. 2024b
;
Bakx et al. 2024
).

2.

We also detect the [O
iii
]
88
line in our six primary targets, noting that two were already previously detected in shallower observations
(
Algera et al. 2024b
)
. The [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios of our full sample of eight galaxies spans [O
iii
]/[C
ii
]
\approx 1.9-9.6
, consistent with what has previously been seen in the
z\gtrsim 6
galaxy population (e.g.,
Harikane et al. 2020
;
Witstok et al. 2022
).

3.

Our full
z\sim 7
sample falls within the scatter of the [O
iii
]
88
-SFR relation for local dwarf galaxies
(
De Looze et al. 2014
)
. This is consistent with the bulk of the
z\approx 6-14
[O
iii
]
88
-detected galaxies in the literature, suggesting the relation may be near-universal across cosmic time – at least up to
\mathrm{SFR}\lesssim 100-200\,M_{\odot}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}
, beyond which the relation may flatten.

We combine our sample with ten
z>6
galaxies in the literature also benefiting from ALMA [O
iii
]
88
and [C
ii
] observations and
JWST
spectroscopy to explore the various possible origins of the elevated [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios seen in high-redshift galaxies. In particular, we have investigated whether the elevated line ratios are predominantly driven by low metallicities (e.g.,
Vallini et al. 2017
;
Ferrara et al. 2019
;
Katz et al. 2019
;
Liang et al. 2024
), high ionization parameters (e.g.,
Arata et al. 2020
;
Harikane et al. 2020
;
Katz et al. 2022
), and/or a high burstiness (e.g.,
Vallini et al. 2021
;
Vallini et al. 2024
;
Algera et al. 2024b
).

We moreover compare to local dwarf galaxies from the
Herschel
Dwarf Galaxy Survey (DGS;
Madden et al. 2013
) for which similar measurements are available. The high-redshift galaxies and local dwarfs span similar metallicities and ionization parameters, although on average the
z>6
galaxies are more bursty and have higher [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios. We quantitatively compare the dependence of [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] on metallicity, ionization parameter and burstiness for both samples, from which we conclude the following:

1.

An enhanced burstiness is the main driver of the high [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios seen in both high-redshift galaxies and local dwarfs.

2.

The high [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] ratios are the result of [C
ii
] being suppressed in bursty galaxies, while the luminosity of [O
iii
]
88
per unit SFR is not strongly affected.

3.

Less significant (anti-)correlations between [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] and metallicity or ionization parameter are also obtained for the high-redshift and dwarf galaxy samples, but are likely a secondary manifestation of a primary (anti-)correlation of [O
iii
]/[C
ii
] with burstiness.

Given that the typical high-redshift population identified by
JWST
appears metal-poor with extreme ionizing conditions and bursty star formation, [O
iii
]
88
is likely to remain a powerful tool to study the earliest galaxies (c.f.,
Carniani et al. 2024a
;
Schouws et al. 2024
;
Zavala et al. 2024
;
Witstok et al. 2025
; Algera et al. in preparation). At the same time, detecting [C
ii
] at similarly early epochs is likely to be challenging, as already hinted at by the lack of success in robustly detecting the line above
z\gtrsim 8.5
(c.f.,
Laporte et al. 2019
;
Fujimoto et al. 2024
;
Schouws et al. 2025
).

We are now finally at a stage where we are beginning to unravel the ISM conditions of galaxies all the way out to – and possibly soon beyond –
z\approx 14
with far-infrared fine structure diagnostics. However, our comparison samples are by and large limited to galaxies at
z\approx 0
, with very little available information across the nearly 13 Gyr of cosmic time between
z\sim 0-6
. Planned far-infrared missions such as the PRobe far-Infrared Mission for Astrophysics (
PRIMA
;
Moullet et al. 2023
) are urgently required to fill in this gap, and paint a comprehensive evolutionary picture of the ISM conditions in the early Universe through Cosmic Noon to the those observed in the galaxy population around us today.

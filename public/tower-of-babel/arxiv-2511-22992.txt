No-go theorem for norm-based quantumness-certification with linear functionals
Soumyakanti Bose, Yong Siah Teo, Hyukjoon Kwon, Hyunseok Jeong

Abstract
Despite several approaches proposed to operationally characterize quantum states of light—those that cannot be sampled with a positive distribution over classical states—most existing formulations suffer from limited practicality or rely on convex optimization procedures that are computationally demanding. In this work, we develop a general convex resource-theoretic framework to quantify optical quantumness directly from the norms of linear functionals of quantum states, thereby avoiding any optimization. We further establish a no-go theorem demonstrating that no universal norm-based measure of quantumness can exist in the absence of optimization. Finally, we substantiate our theoretical result through explicit examples involving both Gaussian and non-Gaussian states.



I
Introduction

Optical states that cannot be represented by a bona fide positive-definite distribution over the set of classical pure states—namely, the coherent states
\left|\alpha\right\rangle

Hillery 1985
—are said to be quantum.
In other words, any optical state that admits a decomposition
\rho=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|
is classical if
P(\alpha)\geq 0
, and quantum otherwise, i.e., when
P(\alpha)
becomes negative or more singular than a delta function
Glauber 1963
;
Sudarshan 1963
;
Brewster and Franson 2018
.
Quantum optical states characterized by such nonregular sampling distributions—formally known as the Sudarshan–Glauber diagonal
P
-function
Glauber 1963
;
Sudarshan 1963
—play a crucial role across modern quantum information science and technology
Braunstein and van Loock 2005
;
Andersen et al. 2010
;
Adesso et al. 2014
, from metrology
Tan and Jeong 2019
and teleportation
Furusawa et al. 1998
to quantum communication
Grosshans and Grangier 2002
;
Furrer and Munro 2018
;
Zhang et al. 2024
and computation
Lloyd and Braunstein 1999
;
Bartlett and and 2003
;
Mari and Eisert 2012
;
Veitch et al. 2013
.

The operational interpretation of the
P
-function and its Gaussian-convolved counterparts—such as the Wigner function
Wigner 1932
and Husimi–Kano
Q
-function
Husimi 1940
;
Kano 1965
—in terms of measurement statistics
Wódkiewicz 1984
;
Leonhardt and Paul 1995
;
Bužek et al. 1995a
;
Bužek et al. 1995b
;
Man’ko 2000
has led to several foundational insights in quantum theory.
These include the necessity of negativity for quantum behavior
Ferrie et al. 2010
and the impossibility of classical simulation of quantum systems
Pashayan et al. 2015
;
Semenov and Klimov 2021
.
Consequently, developing an operational resource theory
Theurer et al. 2017
;
Takagi and Regula 2019
;
Chitambar and Gour 2019
of quantumness—encoded in the negativity or singularity of the
P
-function (
P(\alpha)\ngeq 0
)—has remained a central pursuit in recent decades
Yadin et al. 2018
;
Kwon et al. 2019
;
Lami et al. 2021
;
Regula et al. 2021
.

Since the set of classical states forms a convex subset of the full state space, the Hahn–Banach theorem ensures the existence of a hyperplane that separates classical from quantum states.
This naturally leads to distance-based quantifiers of optical quantumness, defined via the minimal distance from the set of classical states. Such measures have been formulated using
p
-norms
Hillery 1987
;
Dodonov et al. 2000
;
Dodonov and Renó 2003
;
Nair 2017
;
Park et al. 2021
and the Bures metric
Marian et al. 2002
, within the Hilbert-space framework.

Analogous to quantum coherence in finite-dimensional systems
Baumgratz et al. 2014
;
Winter and Yang 2016
, researchers have also proposed resource-theoretic formulations of phase-space coherence
Tan et al. 2017
.
Combined with phase-space filtering techniques
Kiesel and Vogel 2010
;
Kühn and Vogel 2018
, this approach enables an operational quantification of the negativity of the Glauber–Sudarshan
P
-function
Tan et al. 2020
.
Moreover, leveraging the positive semi-definiteness of the Husimi–Kano
Q
-function
Husimi 1940
;
Kano 1965
, entropic formulations of optical quantumness have been developed
Bose 2018
, alongside methods for identifying extremal quantum states
Goldberg et al. 2020
.

Despite their mathematical elegance and conceptual depth, most existing measures either require convex optimization over the classical set
Hillery 1987
;
Marian et al. 2002
;
Park et al. 2021
;
Bose 2018
or lack practical feasibility
Tan et al. 2017
;
Tan et al. 2020
.
Several such measures also fail to capture quantumness in general
Bose and Kumar 2017
, precisely because they omit this optimization.
This raises a fundamental question: Is it possible to construct a universal, optimization-free, resource-theoretic measure of optical quantumness?

In this article, we explore this possibility by formulating a distance-based,
p
-norm–based resource-theoretic measure of optical quantumness, without optimization over the set of classical states.
We define the measure in terms of a linear functional
\mathcal{F}_{L}
acting on a quantum state and its classical counterpart, obtained as the output of a generic quantumness-breaking channel
\mathcal{C}
.
While our framework serves as a bona fide quantumness certifier, it also generalizes existing norm-based measures within a unified formalism.

However, we further demonstrate that the universality of such measures cannot be guaranteed. By analyzing the nonregularity of the Sudarshan–Glauber
P
-function, we establish explicit conditions under which
p
-norm–based measures fail to detect quantumness. This leads to our no-go theorem, which states that no universal, optimization-free, norm-based quantumness certifier can be constructed solely from linear functionals. We illustrate this theorem using both Gaussian and non-Gaussian examples, within an
\mathcal{L}_{1}
-norm framework, employing the Wigner function and a quantum-limited attenuator–amplifier channel that acts as a quantumness-breaking channel
Linowski and Rudnicki 2024
.

II
Norm-based measure with linear functionals

Let us consider a
quantumness-breaking
or
classicalization
-channel
\mathcal{C}
that transforms any given state into a classical state such that, for any arbitrary density operator,
\rho=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}P_{\text{cl}}(\beta)\left|\beta\right\rangle\left\langle\beta\right|,

(1)

where
P_{\text{cl}}(\beta)=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)f(\beta-\alpha)\geq 0
such that
f(\beta-\alpha)
is the smoothing function applied on
P(\alpha)
.
The existence of such mapping could be easily appreciated in terms of the relation between phase-space distributions, where a Gaussian smoothing, for which the width is equal to that of the ground state Wigner function, yields a positive regular
Q
distribution irrespective of the
P
function.

As a bonafide distance measure we consider the
p
-norm between any linear functional (
\mathcal{F}_{L}
) of
\rho
and
\mathcal{C}(\rho)
, such that
\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\sum_{k}p_{k}\rho_{k}\right)=\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{F}_{L}(\rho_{k})
.
The image of the functional
\mathcal{F}_{L}
could be both operators and functions, depending on whether it maps the density operator onto Hilbert space or phase-space.
Consequently, we define the measure as

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho)=\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\rho)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\rho)]\right\|_{p},

(2)

where
\|A\|_{p}=\left\{\text{Tr}\left[\left(\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}\right)^{p}\right]\right\}^{1/p}
.

II.1
Resource theoretic properties of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}

It may be readily appreciated that, as a consequence of
p
-norm,
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(\rho)
satisfies the positivity, symmetry and triangle inequality which are necessary for a bona fide distance measure (appendix
A
).
We now prove the rest of the properties as follows.

II.1.1
Convexity

Let us consider a convex mixture of state given by
\{p_{k},\rho_{k}\}
, such that
0\leq p_{k}\leq 1
and
\sum_{k}p_{k}=1
for which
\mathcal{C}(\rho)=\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{C}(\rho_{k})
.
Then by using the convexity of
\mathcal{L}^{p}
norm we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left(\rho\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\sum_{k}p_{k}\rho_{k}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{C}(\rho_{k})\right]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}p_{k}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\rho_{k})-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\rho_{k})\right]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho_{k}).

(3)

II.1.2
Weak-monotonicity

Let us consider that the quantum state
\rho=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{\text{sys}}(\alpha)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|
undergoes a linear map
\Phi_{L}
such that

\Phi_{L}(\rho)=\text{Tr}_{\text{anc}}\left[U_{L}\left(\rho\otimes\sigma_{\text{anc}}\right)U_{L}^{\dagger}\right],

(4)

where
U_{L}
is a linear optical unitary corresponding to the map
\Phi_{L}
, and
\sigma_{\text{anc}}=\int\frac{d^{2}\vec{\beta}}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\left|\vec{\beta}\right\rangle\left\langle\vec{\beta}\right|
is an
M
-mode ancilla state (with a positive
P
-function)
Tan et al. 2017
;
Kühn and Vogel 2018
;
Tan et al. 2020
, i.e.,
P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\geq 0
and
\left|\vec{\beta}\right\rangle=\left|\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{M}\right\rangle
denotes the multimode coherent state.

The unitary
U_{L}
, consisting of phase shifters, displacement operations, and beam splitters, transforms a coherent state into another coherent state according to
U_{L}\left|\alpha,\vec{\beta}\right\rangle=\left|\eta(\alpha,\vec{\beta}),\zeta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle
.
As a consequence, using the commutation between
\Phi_{L}
and
\mathcal{C}
(appendix
B
) and invariance of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}
under rotation and displacement (appendix
C
), one can show (appendix
D
)

\displaystyle\Phi_{L}\left[\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho)\right]=\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\Phi_{L}(\rho)\right]-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left\{\mathcal{C}\left[\Phi_{L}(\rho)\right]\right\}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\mathcal{U}_{L}\rho\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{U}_{L}\mathcal{C}(\rho)\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\rho)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\rho)]\right\|_{p}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho),

(5)

i.e., the proposed measure is nonincreasing under linear optical transformations involving classical ancillas.

II.1.3
Strong-monotonicity

Let us now consider that the linear map
\Phi_{L}
is supplemented with projective measurements
\left\{\Pi_{k}=\left|\Lambda_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle\Lambda_{k}\right|\right\}
, where
\left\{\left|\Lambda_{k}\right\rangle\right\}
form a complete set of orthonormal basis.
As a consequence, the linear map changes as (appendix
E
)

\displaystyle\Phi_{L}(\rho)=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P(\alpha)P_{\text{cl}}(\beta)\left|\eta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle\left\langle\eta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right|

\displaystyle~~\times\sum_{k}\text{Tr}\left[\left|\zeta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle\left\langle\zeta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right|\Pi_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathcal{E}_{k}\rho\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\dagger}=\sum_{k}p_{k}\rho_{k},

(6)

where
p_{k}=\text{Tr}\left(\mathcal{E}_{k}\rho\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\dagger}\right)
and
\rho_{k}=\frac{1}{p_{k}}\mathcal{E}_{k}\rho\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\dagger}
.

This, in line with Young’s convolution inequality and similar to weak monotonicity, immediately leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho_{k})=\sum_{k}p_{k}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\rho_{k}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\rho_{k})\right]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\mathcal{U}_{L}\rho\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{U}_{L}\mathcal{C}(\rho)\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\rho\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]\right\|_{p}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho).

(7)

II.2
Operational meaning of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}

It may further be noted the fact that
\left|\alpha\right\rangle=\mathcal{D}(\alpha)\left|0\right\rangle
such that
D(\alpha)
is a displacement operation under which
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}
remains invariant (appendix
C
), leading to the result of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left(\left|\alpha\right\rangle\right)=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left(\left|0\right\rangle\right)

As a consequence, for a test state,
\rho_{\text{test}}=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{\text{test}}(\alpha)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|
, Young’s convolution inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left(\rho_{\text{cl}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{\text{test}}(\alpha)\right\|_{1}~\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left(\left|0\right\rangle\right).

(8)

This immediately leads to the conclusion that, for any classical state satisfying
P_{\text{test}}=P_{\text{cl}}\geq 0
,
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left(\rho_{\text{cl}}\right)\leq\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\left|0\right\rangle)
.
In other words,
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left(\rho_{\text{cl}}\right)>\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\left|0\right\rangle)
holds if and only if
\lVert P_{\text{test}}(\alpha)\rVert_{1}>1
, that is, when
P_{\text{test}}(\alpha)
either attains negative values or becomes a singular function whose norm diverges.

As a direct consequence, we define a convex, resource-theoretic measure of optical quantumness (or quantumness) as

\mathcal{M}(\rho)=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho)-\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\left|0\right\rangle),

(9)

where
\mathcal{M}(\rho)\leq 0
for all classical states and
\mathcal{M}(\rho)>0
signifies quantumness such that
\mathcal{M}(\rho)=0
only for the classical pure states, i.e., the coherent states.

In analogy with the
\mathcal{L}^{p}
-norm distance between two functions
f(x)
and
g(x)
,
\mathcal{D}_{f,g}^{p}=\left\|f(x)-g(x)\right\|_{p}=\left[\int_{\Omega}d\mu(x)\left|f(x)-g(x)\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}
, which quantifies their statistical distinguishability, one can interpret the quantity
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho)
as the distinguishability between a quantum state
\rho
and its classical counterpart
\mathcal{C}(\rho)
, obtained as the output of a quantumness-breaking channel.
Consequently, a positive value of
\mathcal{M}(\rho)
directly quantifies how much the state
\rho
differs from one that can be statistically represented as a mixture of coherent states.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{M}(\rho)>0
admits an operational interpretation as a measure of the quantum superposition—or the degree of optical quantumness—present in
\rho
.

III
No-go theorem for the norm based measures with linear functionals

Theorem 1

Using linear functionals, it is not possible to obtain a universal norm-based resource theoretic measure of quantumness without optimization.

Proof
— Let us consider a quantum state with negative
P
function,
P(\alpha)=P_{+}(\alpha)-P_{-}(\alpha)
, where
P_{\pm}(\alpha)>0
are defined over disjoint regions of the phase space, denoted by
\Omega_{\pm}
.
From the normalization condition we have
\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)=1=\int_{\Omega_{+}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{+}(\alpha)-\int_{\Omega_{-}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{-}(\alpha)
.
Using this decomposition (see SM), the corresponding
p
-norm functional becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho_{\text{neg}})\geq\left\{\text{Tr}\bigg|\left|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|0\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|0\right\rangle)]\right|-\epsilon_{\text{diff}}\bigg|^{p}\right\}^{1/p},

(10)

where
\epsilon_{\text{diff}}=\epsilon_{+}-\epsilon_{-}
such that
0\leq\epsilon_{\pm}=\left|\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{\pm}(\alpha)\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right\}\right|-\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{\pm}(\alpha)\left|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right|
.

As is evident from Eq. (
27
), the strict inequality
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho_{\text{neg}})>\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)
fails to hold in the following two cases:

0<\epsilon_{\text{diff}}<\left|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|0\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|0\right\rangle)]\right|

(11a)

\left|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|0\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|0\right\rangle)]\right|<\epsilon_{\text{diff}}<2\left|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|0\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|0\right\rangle)]\right|.

(11b)

In such scenarios, we find
\mathcal{M}(\rho_{\text{neg}})<0
even though
\rho_{\text{neg}}
is quantum by construction, since
P_{-}(\alpha)\neq 0
.
This result implies that there exist, at least in principle, quantum states for which any bona fide norm–based resource measure, constructed solely from linear functionals and without optimization over the classical set, will fail to identify quantumness.

III.1
Practical example of no-go theorem with Gaussian quantumness-breaking channel

To exemplify the consequences of the above
no-go theorem
, we analyze concrete cases involving a Gaussian
quantumness-breaking
channel, employing the Wigner function as a bona fide linear functional and the
\mathcal{L}_{1}
norm as the metric.

For a quantum state
\rho
, the actions of a quantum-limited attenuator channel (
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
) and a quantum-limited amplifier channel (
\mathcal{A}_{k}
) are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\left(\rho\right)

\displaystyle=\rm{Tr}_{\text{anc}}\left[\mathsf{B}(\lambda)\left(\rho\otimes\left|0_{\text{anc}}\right\rangle\left\langle 0_{\text{anc}}\right|\right)\mathsf{B}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\right]~~\rm{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k}\left(\rho\right)

\displaystyle=\rm{Tr}_{\text{anc}}\left[\mathsf{S}(k)\left(\rho\otimes\left|0_{\text{anc}}\right\rangle\left\langle 0_{\text{anc}}\right|\right)\mathsf{S}^{\dagger}(k)\right],

(12)

where
\mathsf{B}(\lambda)=e^{\cos^{-1}!\sqrt{\lambda},(a^{\dagger}b-ab^{\dagger})}
is a passive beam-splitter with transmittivity
\lambda
, and
\mathsf{S}(k)=e^{\cosh^{-1}\sqrt{k},(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}-ab)}
is a two-mode correlated squeezing operator. Here,
\left|0_{\text{anc}}\right\rangle
denotes the vacuum state in the ancilla mode.

It can be shown that the composite channel
\mathcal{C}_{g}=\mathcal{A}_{2}\circ\mathcal{E}_{1/2}
acts as a Gaussian “classicalization” or “quantumness-breaking” channel that maps any Glauber–Sudarshan
P
function to a positive semidefinite Husimi function,
i.e.,
P_{\mathcal{C}_{g}(\rho)}(\alpha)=Q_{\rho}(\alpha)
, which is manifestly positive by construction
Linowski and Rudnicki 2024
.

As a bona fide distance measure, we consider the
\mathcal{L}_{1}
norm of the Wigner function. Specifically, we quantify the action of the quantumness-breaking channel
\mathcal{C}_{g}
as

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}}^{1,W}(\rho)=\left\|W_{\rho}(z)-W_{\mathcal{C}_{g}(\rho)}(z)\right\|_{1},

(13)

where
\mathcal{L}_{1}[f(z)]=\int_{\Omega_{f}}\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi},|f(z)|
, and
\Omega_{f}
denotes the support of the complex-valued function
f(z)
.
Since, for any bona fide Wigner function
W(z)
,
\Omega_{W}\subset\mathbb{C}
, for the sake convenience, we consider integration over the full complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.
We now present two representative physical examples—one Gaussian and one non-Gaussian—to illustrate the implications of the
no-go
result in Eq. (
11b
).

III.1.1
Gaussian case

Let us consider a squeezed thermal state given as
\rho_{\text{st}}=S(r)\rho_{\text{th}}(\bar{n})S^{\dagger}(r)
, where
S(r)=e^{\frac{r}{2}\left(a^{\dagger 2}-a^{2}\right)}
,
\rho_{\text{th}}(\bar{n})=\frac{1}{1+\bar{n}}\sum_{k}\left(\frac{\bar{n}}{1+\bar{n}}\right)^{k}\left|k\right\rangle\left\langle k\right|
;
r
being the squeezing strength and
\bar{n}
representing the number average thermal photon.
The onset of quantumness in
\rho_{\text{st}}
is marked whenever
r>\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\bar{n}+1)
for which variance of
x
-quadrature falls below
1/2
or alternatively the
P
-function diverges.

III.1.2
Non-Gaussian case

Let us consider random mixtures of photon number states

As an independent indicator of quantumness, we employ the Wigner negativity

\text{Wig. neg.}=\int\frac{dxdp}{\pi}\left|W_{\rho_{\text{mix}}}(x,p)\right|-1

This serves as a clear boundary separating classical and quantum cases, indicated respectively by blue and red square markers.
In the corresponding Wigner negativity plot, the states identified as quantum by

Interestingly, for a few specific combinations of
p_{0},p_{1}
and

.

The apparent failure of the criterion
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}}^{1,W}(\rho)>\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}}^{1,W}(\left|0\right\rangle)
for both the squeezed thermal state and the number-state mixtures under the Gaussian classicalization channe
\mathcal{C}_{g}
can be understood from the phase-space representation of
\mathcal{C}_{g}
.
The Gaussian quantumness-breaking channel
\mathcal{C}_{g}
acts analogously to the quantum depth measure

, which quantifies the minimum Gaussian smoothing required to transform a nonregular
P
-function into a well-behaved positive semidefinite distribution.

For Gaussian states, such strong smoothing is unnecessary, since a milder channel
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{g}}

Linowski and Rudnicki 2024
already converts the Glauber–Sudarshan
P
-function into the Wigner function, which is itself positive semidefinite.
Likewise, for non-Gaussian pure states,
\mathcal{C}_{g}
represents precisely the minimum Gaussian smoothing required to regularize the
P
-function
Lütkenhaus and Barnett 1995
;
however, mixed non-Gaussian states typically require less smoothing than that implemented by
\mathcal{C}_{g}
.
In other words,
\mathcal{C}_{g}
effectively over-smooths both Gaussian and certain non-Gaussian mixed states, thereby suppressing their detectable quantum signatures and resulting in the observed underestimation of the distance measure
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}}^{1,W}(\rho)
.

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we have established a
no-go
result concerning the construction of vector-norm–based measures of optical quantumness without invoking any optimization procedures.
To this end, we first developed a bona fide convex resource-theoretic framework in terms of the quantity
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}
, defined via the
p
-norm distance between an arbitrary linear functional of a quantum state and the corresponding output of a quantumness-breaking channel.
While inherently encoding the coherent superposition content of the state, our formulation provides a general operational framework for norm-based quantumness certification whenever
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho)>\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\left|0\right\rangle)
.

We subsequently derived explicit mathematical conditions under which this certification fails even for states that are manifestly quantum—i.e., those with negative or singular Sudarshan–Glauber
P
-function.
This leads to the central premise of the
no-go theorem
, which establishes the impossibility of constructing a universal norm-based quantumness certifier solely from linear functionals.
We further illustrated this result using the
\mathcal{L}_{1}
-norm between the Wigner functions of a state and the output of a Gaussian quantumness-breaking channel, providing concrete examples for both Gaussian and non-Gaussian states.
These examples conclusively demonstrate the operational manifestation of the
no-go
result—showing that, while norm-based measures can witness quantumness in specific instances, no single linear-functional–based norm can universally detect optical quantumness.

Appendix A
Bona fide resource theoretic distance measure and quantification of channel
\mathcal{C}

We now quantify the action of the “classicalization” channel
\mathcal{C}
on any quantum state
\rho
in terms of a bonafide
distance
function between the quantum states
\rho
and
\mathcal{C}(\rho)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{D}\left[\rho,\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]
.
To enforce a resource theoretic evaluation,
\mathcal{D}
needs to satisfy the following properties

(i)

Positive semi-definite
:
\mathcal{D}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\geq 0
for any two quantum states
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
.

(ii)

Symmetric
:
\mathcal{D}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=\mathcal{D}(\rho_{2},\rho_{1})
for any two quantum states
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
.

(iii)

Triangle inequality
:
\mathcal{D}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})+\mathcal{D}(\rho_{2},\rho_{3})\geq\mathcal{D}(\rho_{1},\rho_{3})
for any bonafide quantum states
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
and
\rho_{3}
.

(iv)

Convexity
: For any
\rho=\sum_{k}p_{k}\rho_{k}
such that
p_{k}\geq 0
and
\sum_{k}p_{k}=1
,
\mathcal{D}\left[\rho,\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]\leq\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{D}\left[\rho_{k},\mathcal{C}(\rho_{k})\right]
.

(v)

Weak-monotonicity
: For any linear optical map,
\Phi_{L}
,
\mathcal{D}\left\{\Phi_{L}(\rho),\mathcal{C}\left[\Phi_{L}(\rho)\right]\right\}\leq\mathcal{D}\left[\rho,\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]
.

(vi)

Strong-monotonicity
: For any linear optical map allowing Kraus representation, i.e.,
\Phi_{L}(\rho):=\sum_{k}\mathcal{K}_{k}\rho\mathcal{K}_{k}^{\dagger}
,
\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{D}\left[\rho_{k},\mathcal{C}(\rho_{k})\right]\leq\mathcal{D}\left[\rho,\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]
, where
\rho_{k}=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{k}\rho\mathcal{K}_{k}^{\dagger}}{\text{Tr}\left(\mathcal{K}\rho\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}\right)}
and
p_{k}=\text{Tr}\left(\mathcal{K}_{k}\rho\mathcal{K}_{k}^{\dagger}\right)
.

Appendix B
Classicalization channel
\mathcal{C}
commutes with linear optical map
\Phi_{L}

Let us consider a linear optical map
\Phi_{L}
that transforms a classical state into another classical state.
Such maps could be written as a
stochastic rotation and displacement channels
as

\Phi_{L}(\cdot):=\int\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{\pi}P_{\text{cl}}(\theta,\lambda)~\mathcal{R}(\theta)\mathcal{D}(\lambda)(\cdot)\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}(\theta),

(14)

such that
\mathcal{R}(\theta)=e^{\mathsf{i}\theta a^{\dagger}a}
and
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)=e^{\lambda a^{\dagger}-\lambda^{*}a}
are the rotation and displacement operators respectively.
Here
P_{\text{cl}}(\theta,\lambda)
represents a classical distribution over
\left\{\theta,\lambda\right\}
, i.e.,
P_{\text{cl}}(\theta,\lambda)\geq 0
.

On the other hand, the action of a the
classicalization
-channel
\mathcal{C}
is to transform any given state into a classical state such that, for any arbitrary density operator,
\rho=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}\left[\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}P_{\text{cl}}(\beta)\left|\beta\right\rangle\left\langle\beta\right|

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}\left[\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)f(\beta-\alpha)\right]\left|\beta\right\rangle\left\langle\beta\right|

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)f(\beta)\left|\beta+\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\beta+\alpha\right|

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)f(\beta)\mathcal{D}(\beta)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\beta),

(15)

where
P_{\text{cl}}(\beta)=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)f(\beta-\alpha)\geq 0
.
Here,
f(\beta-\alpha)
is a smoothing function applied on
P(\alpha)
.

In a straightforward calculation it can be shown that

\displaystyle\Phi_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{\pi}P_{\text{cl}}(\theta,\lambda)\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}\left[\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)f(\beta)\right]~\mathcal{R}(\theta)\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\mathcal{D}(\beta)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\beta)\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{\pi}P_{\text{cl}}(\theta,\lambda)\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}\left[\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)f(\beta)\right]~\mathcal{D}(\beta)\mathcal{R}(\theta)\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}(\theta)\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}f(\beta)\mathcal{D}(\beta)\left[\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)~\mathcal{R}(\theta)\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}(\theta)\right]\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}f(\beta)\mathcal{D}(\beta)~\Phi_{L}(\rho)~\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}\left[\Phi_{L}(\rho)\right].

(16)

Appendix C
Invariance of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho)
under displacement and rotation

Let us also consider that both
\rho
and
\mathcal{C}(\rho)
undergoes a unitary transformation which is given by
\mathcal{U}(z,\theta)=\mathcal{D}(z)\mathcal{R}(\phi)
, where
\mathcal{D}(z)
and
\mathcal{R}(\phi)
are the displacement and rotation operations respectively.
Under such unitary transformation the action of the linear functional
\mathcal{F}_{L}
is given as
One can show

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}:\mathcal{F}_{L}(\rho)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\mathcal{U}\rho\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle=U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}\mathcal{F}_{L}(\rho)U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}:\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\rho)]

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}\left(\mathcal{U}\rho\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{U}\mathcal{C}(\rho)\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\right]=U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\rho)]U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}^{\dagger},

(17)

where
U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}
is another unitary transformation that depends on the form of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
and
\mathcal{U}
.
The equality
\mathcal{C}\left(\mathcal{U}\rho\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\right)=\mathcal{U}\mathcal{C}(\rho)\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}
could be easily appreciated in terms of Eq. (
16
) where the linear optical map is just a single displacement-rotation channel, i.e.,
P_{\text{cl}}(\theta,\lambda)=\delta(\phi)\delta^{2}(z)
.

This immediately leads to the result

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{L}:\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\mathcal{U}_{L}\rho\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{U}_{L}\mathcal{C}(\rho)\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\rho\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]\right\}U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}^{\dagger}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\rho\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho),

(18)

as the vector-norms are invariant of unitary transformations.

Appendix D
Weak-monotonicity of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}

Let us consider that the quantum state
\rho
undergoes a linear map
\Phi_{L}
such that

\Phi_{L}(\rho)=\text{Tr}_{\text{anc}}\left[U_{L}\left(\rho\otimes\sigma_{\text{anc}}\right)U_{L}^{\dagger}\right],

(19)

where
U_{L}
is a linear optical unitary corresponding to the linear map
\Phi_{L}
and
\sigma_{\text{anc}}
is any classical state (positive
P
function) in the ancilla mode
Tan et al. 2017
;
Kühn and Vogel 2018
;
Tan et al. 2020
.
Let us now consider that the system state is given as
\rho=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|
while the ancilla state
\sigma_{\text{anc}}
represents a classical state over
M
-modes, i.e.,
\sigma_{\text{anc}}=\int\frac{d^{2}\vec{\beta}}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\left|\vec{\beta}\right\rangle\left\langle\vec{\beta}\right|
, where
P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})
is multivariate positive function over
M
-modes and
\left|\vec{\beta}\right\rangle=\left|\beta_{1},\beta_{2},...,\beta_{M}\right\rangle
, i.e.,
P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\geq 0
.
The action of
U_{L}
, which is a combination of phase-shifters, displacements and beam splitters, transforms a coherent states into another coherent state, i.e.,
U_{L}\left|\alpha,\vec{\beta}\right\rangle=\left|\eta(\alpha,\vec{\beta}),\zeta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle
, such that
\left|\eta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle=\mathcal{R}(\theta)\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\left|\alpha\right\rangle
and
\left|\zeta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle=\mathcal{R}(\theta^{{}^{\prime}})\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{{}^{\prime}})\left|\vec{\beta}\right\rangle
where
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{D}
stand for the phase rotations and displacement operators.
The rotation and displacement parameters are given as
\theta=\theta(\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta})
,
\lambda=\lambda(\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta})
,
\vec{\theta}^{{}^{\prime}}=\vec{\theta}^{{}^{\prime}}(\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\alpha)
,
\vec{\lambda}^{{}^{\prime}}=\vec{\lambda}^{{}^{\prime}}(\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\alpha)
,
where
\vec{\phi}
,
\vec{d}
and
\vec{T}
are the parameters of
U_{L}
corresponding to phase-shifting, displacement and beam splitter operations respectively.

As a consequence, one can write

\displaystyle\Phi_{L}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P(\alpha)P_{\text{cl}}(\beta)\left|\eta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle\left\langle\eta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right|

\displaystyle~~\times\text{Tr}\left[\left|\zeta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle\left\langle\zeta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P(\alpha)P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}~\rho~U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}^{\dagger}.

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})~\mathcal{U}_{L}~\rho~\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger},

(20)

where
\mathcal{U}_{L}=U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}
.

Then, in line with (
16
) and (
18
), using Young’s convolution inequality one can show

\displaystyle\Phi_{L}\left[\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho)\right]=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left[\Phi_{L}(\rho)\right]

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\Phi_{L}(\rho)\right]-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left\{\mathcal{C}\left[\Phi_{L}(\rho)\right]\right\}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\Phi_{L}(\rho)\right]-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left\{\Phi_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]\right\}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\mathcal{U}_{L}\rho\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{U}_{L}\mathcal{C}(\rho)\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right]\right\}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\mathcal{U}_{L}\rho\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{U}_{L}\mathcal{C}(\rho)\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\rho)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\rho)]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho).

(21)

Appendix E
Strong-monotonicity of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}

We now prove the strong monotonicity which demands monotonicity of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}(\rho)}
under a linear map admitting a Kraus representation, i.e., the linear map
\Phi_{L}
supplemented with projective measurements
\left\{\Pi_{k}=\left|\Lambda_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle\Lambda_{k}\right|\right\}
, where
\left\{\left|\Lambda_{k}\right\rangle\right\}
form a complete set of orthonormal basis.
As a consequence, the linear map in (
20
) changes as

\displaystyle\Phi_{L}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi^{M}}P(\alpha)P_{\text{cl}}(\beta)\left|\eta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle\left\langle\eta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right|

\displaystyle~~\times\sum_{k}\text{Tr}\left[\left|\zeta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle\left\langle\zeta(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right|\Pi_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{d^{2}\vec{\beta}}{\pi^{M}}P(\alpha)P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})F_{k,\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta},\alpha}

\displaystyle~~\times U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathcal{E}_{k}\rho\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\dagger}=\sum_{k}p_{k}\rho_{k},

(22)

where
0\leq F_{k,\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta},\alpha}=\left|\left\langle\Lambda_{k}|\vec{\zeta}(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq 1
and

\sum_{k}F_{k,\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta},\alpha}=\left\langle\vec{\zeta}(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right|\sum_{k}\left|\Lambda_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle\Lambda_{k}\right|\left|\vec{\zeta}(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle=\left\langle\vec{\zeta}(\alpha,\vec{\beta})|\vec{\zeta}(\alpha,\vec{\beta})\right\rangle=1
.
The specific state
\rho_{k}
and the corresponding probability
p_{k}
are given as

\displaystyle p_{k}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{d^{2}\vec{\beta}}{\pi^{M}}P(\alpha)P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})F_{k,\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta},\alpha}

\displaystyle~~\times\text{Tr}\left[U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}^{\dagger}\right]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{d^{2}\vec{\beta}}{\pi^{M}}P(\alpha)P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})F_{k,\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta},\alpha}

(23a)

\displaystyle\rho_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{k}}\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{d^{2}\vec{\beta}}{\pi^{M}}P(\alpha)P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})F_{k,\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta},\alpha}

\displaystyle~~\times U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|U_{\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta}}^{\dagger}.

(23b)

This leads to, in line with Young’s convolution inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}p_{k}\left\|\frac{1}{p_{k}}\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{d^{2}\vec{\beta}}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{sys}}(\alpha)P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})F_{k,\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta},\alpha}\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\mathcal{U}_{L}\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{U}_{L}\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|)\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\right]\right\}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{d^{2}\vec{\beta}}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\sum_{k}\left\|\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{\text{sys}}(\alpha)F_{k,\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta},\alpha}U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|)\right]\right\}U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}^{\dagger}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{d^{2}\vec{\beta}}{\pi^{M}}P_{\text{cl}}(\vec{\beta})\left\|\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{\text{sys}}(\alpha)\left(\sum_{k}F_{k,\vec{\phi},\vec{d},\vec{T},\vec{\beta},\alpha}\right)U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|)\right]\right\}U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}^{\dagger}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\rho\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]\right\}U_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}^{\dagger}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}\left(\rho\right)-\mathcal{F}_{L}\left[\mathcal{C}(\rho)\right]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho).

(24)

Appendix F
Calculation of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho_{\text{neg}})

Let us consider a nonclassical state with negative
P
function, i.e.,
\rho_{\text{neg}}
for which
P(\alpha)=P_{+}(\alpha)-P_{-}(\alpha)
, where
P_{\pm}(\alpha)>0
and are defined over disjoint regions in phase space denoted by
\Omega_{\pm}
.
From the normalization condition we have
\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)=1=\int_{\Omega_{+}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{+}(\alpha)-\int_{\Omega_{-}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{-}(\alpha)
.

This leads to the result

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho_{\text{neg}})

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\rho_{\text{neg}})-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\rho_{\text{neg}})]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{\Omega_{+}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{+}(\alpha)\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right\}-\int_{\Omega_{-}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{-}(\alpha)\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right\}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left[\text{Tr}\left(\left|\int_{\Omega_{+}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{+}(\alpha)\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right\}-\int_{\Omega_{-}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{-}(\alpha)\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right\}\right|^{p}\right)\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\geq\left[\text{Tr}\left(\left|~\left|\int_{\Omega_{+}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{+}(\alpha)\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right\}\right|-\left|\int_{\Omega_{-}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{-}(\alpha)\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right\}\right|~\right|^{p}\right)\right]^{1/p},

(25)

where we have used the reverse triangle inequality,
\left|A-B\right|=\left||A|-|B|\right|
.

Now, by applying the reverse triangle inequality again, with
P_{\pm}(\alpha)\geq 0
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{\pm}(\alpha)\left\{\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{\pm}(\alpha)\left|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right|

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P_{\pm}(\alpha)\left|\mathcal{F}_{L}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)-\mathcal{F}_{L}[\mathcal{C}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle)]\right|-\epsilon_{\pm},

(26)

such that
\epsilon_{\pm}\geq 0
.

As a consequence, (
25
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p,\mathcal{F}_{L}}(\rho_{\text{neg}})

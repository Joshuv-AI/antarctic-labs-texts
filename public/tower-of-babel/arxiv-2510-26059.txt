Introduction

We investigate the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Bochner-Riesz multiplier on flat cones.
In the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}

\delta\geq 0

\lambda>0

S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})
of order
\delta

S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})f(x)=\int_{|\xi|\leq\lambda}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\big(1-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\big)^{\delta}\hat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi

(1.1)

where the Laplacian
1

Throughout this paper, our sign convention for Laplacians is that
\Delta_{\bullet}
is a positive operator.

\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}}
and
\hat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
f
.
The problem known as the Bochner-Riesz problem is to determine the optimal index
\delta
for
1\leq p\leq+\infty
such that

S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})f
converges to

in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{2}})
(as

) for every

. When

, the convergence holds true if and only if

by
Plancherel’s theorem. The Bochner-Riesz conjecture is as follows

Conjecture 1.1

(Bochner-Riesz conjecture)
.

For
p\neq 2
, the Bochner-Riesz mean
S_{R}^{\delta}(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})f(x)
converges in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if

\delta>\delta_{c}(p,n)
, where

\delta_{c}(p,n):=\max\left\{0,n\big|\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{p}\big|-\tfrac{1}{2}\right\},\quad\forall\;p\in[1,+\infty].

(1.2)

The purpose of this paper is to investigate the Bochner-Riesz operator
S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta_{X}):=(1-{\Delta_{X}}/{\lambda^{2}})^{\delta}_{+}
of order

\Delta_{X}

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta_{X})=\int_{0}^{\lambda}\Big(1-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{\delta}dE_{\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}}(\rho),

(1.3)

where
E_{\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}}(\rho)
is a spectral resolution of
\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}
.

Now, we state our main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1\leq p\leq+\infty
and
\delta>\delta_{c}(p,2)
be given by (
1.2
), which means

\delta_{c}(p,2)=\max\left\{0,2\big|\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{p}\big|-\tfrac{1}{2}\right\}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p},\quad&\text{if}\quad p\geq 4\\
0,\quad&\text{if}\quad\frac{4}{3}<p<4\\
\frac{2}{p}-\frac{3}{2},\quad&\text{if}\quad p\leq\frac{4}{3}.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Then, there holds

\big\|S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta_{X})f\big\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)},

(1.5)

where the constant
C
is independent of
\lambda>0
.

Remark 1.3
.

The condition
\delta>\delta_{c}(p,2)
is necessary by case
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
when
p\neq 2
, as stated in Conjecture
1.1
for

and reference after it.

The spectral measure and Bochner-Riesz mean

We recall the representation of the spectral measure

2.1.
The kernel of resolvent and spectral measure

Proposition 2.1

(Spectral measure kernel)
.

Let
X=C(\mathbb{S}_{\sigma}^{1})
be a product cone over the circle,
\mathbb{S}_{\sigma}^{1}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\sigma\mathbb{Z}
with radius
\sigma>0
, equipped with the metric
g=dr^{2}+r^{2}d\theta^{2}

and let
\Delta_{X}
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
X
. Let
x=(r_{1},\theta_{1}),\;y=(r_{2},\theta_{2})
in
X=C(\mathbb{S}_{\sigma}^{1})
and denote

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{j}=

\displaystyle\left(r_{1}-r_{2},\sqrt{2\big[1-\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\sigma\pi)\big]r_{1}r_{2}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{s}=

\displaystyle(n_{1},n_{2})=\big(r_{1}+r_{2},\sqrt{2(\cosh s-1)r_{1}r_{2}}\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2.2)

Then the Schwartz kernel of the spectral measure
dE_{\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}}(\rho;x,y)
can be written as

\begin{split}dE_{\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}}(\rho;x,y)=&\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{\rho}{\pi i}\sum_{\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:0\leq|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\sigma\pi|\leq\pi\}}\mathrm{Spect}(\rho,{\bf m}_{j})\\
&-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\sigma}\frac{\rho}{\pi^{2}i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Spect}(\rho,{\bf n}_{s})A_{\sigma}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\;ds,\end{split}

(2.3)

where

\displaystyle A_{\sigma}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\left(\frac{e^{i\frac{1}{\sigma}(\pi-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))}}{e^{\frac{s}{\sigma}}-e^{i\frac{1}{\sigma}(\pi-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))}}-\frac{e^{-i\frac{1}{\sigma}(\pi+(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))}}{e^{\frac{s}{\sigma}}-e^{-i\frac{1}{\sigma}(\pi+(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))}}\right)

(2.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sin\big(\tfrac{\pi-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{\sigma}\big)}{\cosh\frac{s}{\sigma}-\cos\big(\tfrac{\pi-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{\sigma}\big)}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sin\big(\tfrac{\pi+(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{\sigma}\big)}{\cosh\frac{s}{\sigma}-\cos\big(\tfrac{\pi+(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{\sigma}\big)},

and for
{\bf m}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\mathrm{Spect}(\rho,{\bf m})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-i\mathbf{m}\cdot\xi}\left(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\rho^{2}+i0)}-\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\rho^{2}-i0)}\right)\;d\xi.

(2.5)

Remark 2.2
.

Notice that

\displaystyle|\mathbf{m}_{j}|=d_{j}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=

\displaystyle\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\sigma\pi)r_{1}r_{2}},

(2.6)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{n}_{s}|=d_{s}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=

\displaystyle\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+2r_{1}r_{2}\cosh(s)},\quad s\in[0,+\infty).

(2.7)

In particular
\sigma=1
,
A_{\sigma}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
vanishes and
j=0
, thus
|\mathbf{m}_{0}|=|x-y|
and

\displaystyle dE_{\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}}(\rho;x,y)=dE_{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}}}(\rho;x,y).

Remark 2.3
.

From (
2.3
), we have

\begin{split}dE_{\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}}(\lambda\rho;x,y)=dE_{\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}}(\rho;\lambda x,\lambda y),\end{split}

(2.8)

which essentially is due to the dilation invariant of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{X}
.

Proof.

The proof is modified from the argument in
[
39
, Section 2]
. We sketch the proof here for readers’ convenience.
We write the positive Laplacian on
X

\Delta_{X}=-\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}_{\sigma}^{1}}

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}_{\sigma}^{1}}=-\partial_{\theta}^{2}
is the Laplacian operator on
\mathbb{S}_{\sigma}^{1}
.
Let
\nu_{k}=|k|/\sigma
and

\varphi_{k}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma}}e^{-\frac{ik\theta}{\sigma}},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z},

(2.9)

then
\nu_{k}
and
\varphi_{k}
are eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}_{\sigma}^{1}}
such that

-\partial_{\theta}^{2}\varphi_{k}(\theta)=\nu_{k}^{2}\varphi_{k}(\theta).

(2.10)

By the functional calculus of Cheeger-Taylor
(see e.g.
[
35
, (8.47)]
), we obtain the kernel of the operator
e^{-it\Delta_{X}}

e^{-it\Delta_{X}}(r_{1},\theta_{1},r_{2},\theta_{2})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{k}(\theta_{1})\overline{\varphi_{k}(\theta_{2})}K_{\nu_{k}}(t,r_{1},r_{2})

(2.11)

where
K_{\nu}(t,r_{1},r_{2})
is given by

\begin{split}K_{\nu}(t,r_{1},r_{2})&=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\rho^{2}}J_{\nu}(r_{1}\rho)J_{\nu}(r_{2}\rho)\,\rho d\rho\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\epsilon+it)\rho^{2}}J_{\nu}(r_{1}\rho)J_{\nu}(r_{2}\rho)\,\rho d\rho\\
=\frac{e^{-\frac{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}}{4it}}}{2it}&\Big(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{2it}\cos(s)}\cos(\nu s)ds-\frac{\sin(\nu\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{2it}\cosh s}e^{-s\nu}ds\Big),\end{split}

(2.12)

where in the last equality we use the Weber’s second exponential integral
[
37
, Section 13.31 (1)]
and the integral representation of the modified Bessel function

I_{\nu}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{z\cos(s)}\cos(\nu s)ds-\frac{\sin(\nu\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z\cosh s}e^{-s\nu}ds.

Recalling
\nu_{k}=|k|/\sigma
and (
2.9
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{k}(\theta_{1})\overline{\varphi_{k}(\theta_{2})}\cos(\nu_{k}s)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma}e^{-i\frac{k}{\sigma}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}\frac{e^{i\frac{ks}{\sigma}}+e^{-i\frac{ks}{\sigma}}}{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big[\delta(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+s+2j\pi\sigma)+\delta(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}-s+2j\pi\sigma)\big],\end{split}

(2.13)

where we used the Poisson’s formula

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(x-Tj)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{T}e^{i2\pi\frac{k}{T}x},\quad T=2\pi\sigma.

(2.14)

Thus, on the one hand, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{2it}\cos(s)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{k}(\theta_{1})\overline{\varphi_{k}(\theta_{2})}\cos(\nu_{k}s)ds\\
=&\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:0\leq|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\sigma\pi|\leq\pi\}}e^{\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{2it}\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\sigma\pi)}.\end{split}

(2.15)

On the other hand, by using

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{ikz}=\frac{e^{iz}}{1-e^{iz}},\qquad\mathrm{Im}z>0,

we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sin(\pi\frac{|k|}{\sigma})e^{-\frac{|k|s}{\sigma}}e^{-i\frac{k}{\sigma}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}\\
=&\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{e^{i\frac{k}{\sigma}\pi}-e^{-i\frac{k}{\sigma}\pi}}{2i}e^{-\frac{ks}{\sigma}}\big(e^{-i\frac{k}{\sigma}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}+e^{i\frac{k}{\sigma}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}\big)\\
=&\sum_{k\geq 1}\Im\big(e^{i\frac{k}{\sigma}(\pi-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+is)}-e^{i\frac{k}{\sigma}(-\pi-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+is)}\big)\\
=&\Im\Big(\frac{e^{i\frac{1}{\sigma}(\pi-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))}}{e^{\frac{s}{\sigma}}-e^{i\frac{1}{\sigma}(\pi-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))}}-\frac{e^{-i\frac{1}{\sigma}(\pi+(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))}}{e^{\frac{s}{\sigma}}-e^{-i\frac{1}{\sigma}(\pi+(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))}}\Big)\\
=&A_{\sigma}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}).\end{split}

(2.16)

Plugging (
2.15

2.16

\begin{split}e^{-it\Delta_{X}}(x,y)&=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{e^{-\frac{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}}{4it}}}{it}\sum_{\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:0\leq|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\sigma\pi|\leq\pi\}}e^{\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{2it}\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\sigma\pi)}\\

(2.17)

We first note that when

(2.22)

According to Stone’s formula, the spectral measure is related to the resolvent

dE_{\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}}(\rho)=\frac{d}{d\rho}dE_{\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}}(\rho)\,d\rho=\frac{\rho}{\pi i}\big(R(\rho+i0)-R(\rho-i0)\big)\,d\rho

From (
2.23
), (
2.22
) and (
2.21
), we have

\begin{split}&dE_{\sqrt{\Delta_{X}}}(\rho;x,y)\\
&=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\rho}{\pi^{2}i}\sum_{\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:0\leq|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\sigma\pi|\leq\pi\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-i{\bf m}_{j}\cdot{\xi}}\Big(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\rho^{2}+i0)}-\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\rho^{2}-i0)}\Big)\,d{\xi}\\
&-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}\sigma}\frac{\rho}{\pi^{2}i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-i{\bf n}_{s}\cdot{\xi}}\Big(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\rho^{2}+i0)}-\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\rho^{2}-i0)}\Big)\,d{\xi}A_{\sigma}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})ds.\end{split}

Therefore, recalling (
2.5
), we have proved (
2.3
).
∎

(2.24)

Lemma 2.5

We can write the function

For the rest of this subsection, we prove the following proposition, which gives an expression of the kernel of the Bochner-Riesz mean operator that we will study.

Proposition 2.6
.

The kernel of the Bochner-Riesz mean operator
S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta_{X})

can be written as

\begin{split}&S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta_{X})\\
=&\sum_{\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:0\leq|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\sigma\pi|\leq\pi\}}K^{\delta}_{\lambda}(|\mathbf{m}_{j}|)-\frac{1}{\pi\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{\lambda}^{\delta}(|\mathbf{n}_{s}|)A_{\sigma}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\;ds,\end{split}

(2.27)

where
K^{\delta}_{\lambda}
is in (
2.25
),
{\bf m}_{j},{\bf n}_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
are given in (
2.1
) and
A_{\sigma}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
in (
2.4

Proof.

By (
1.3
) and (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta_{X})=\frac{1}{4\pi^{3}i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\left(1-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)_{+}^{\delta}

\displaystyle\Big\{\sum_{\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:0\leq|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\sigma\pi|\leq\pi\}}\mathrm{Spect}(\rho,{\bf m}_{j})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Spect}(\rho,{\bf n}_{s})A_{\sigma}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\;ds\Big\}\;d\rho.

To prove (
2.27

K^{\delta}_{\lambda}(|{\bf m}|)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\left(1-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)_{+}^{\delta}\mathrm{Spect}(\rho,{\bf m})\,d\rho,

where

\begin{split}&\mathrm{Spect}(\rho,{\bf m})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-i\mathbf{m}\cdot\xi}\left(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\rho^{2}+i0)}-\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\rho^{2}-i0)}\right)\;d\xi\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-i{\bf m}\cdot\xi}\Big(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\rho^{2}+i\epsilon)}-\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\rho^{2}-i\epsilon)}\Big)d\xi\\

&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\epsilon}{(\lambda^{2}-\rho^{2})^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}\int_{|\omega|=1}e^{-i\lambda{\bf m}\cdot\omega}d\sigma_{\omega}\,\lambda d\lambda\\
&=\int_{|\omega|=1}e^{-i\rho{\bf m}\cdot\omega}d\sigma_{\omega},\end{split}

where we use the fact that the Poisson kernel is an approximation to the identity which implies that for

(2.29)

for almost every

Therefore, the right hand side of (

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\left(1-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)_{+}^{\delta}\int_{|\omega|=1}e^{-i\rho{\bf m}\cdot\omega}d\sigma_{\omega}\,d\rho&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-i{\bf m}\cdot\xi}\left(1-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)_{+}^{\delta}\,d\xi\\
&=K^{\delta}_{\lambda}(|{\bf m}|),\end{split}

which proves (
2.28

∎

(3.48)

K_{D_{m}^{+}}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{+}(d_{s})e^{id_{s}}}{(1+d_{s})^{\frac{3}{2}+\delta}}\frac{\sin\big(\tfrac{\pi\pm\theta}{\sigma}\big)}{\cosh\frac{s}{\sigma}-\cos\big(\tfrac{(\pi\pm\theta)}{\sigma}\big)}\;ds,

(3.49)

and
a_{+}
is bounded from below near infinity and satisfies (
3.6
).
By partition of unity
(
3.22
), we decompose

K_{D_{m}^{+}}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})=\sum_{k\geq 0}K_{D_{m,k}^{+}}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}),

(3.50)

with

\begin{split}&K_{D_{m,k}^{+}}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\\
&=K_{D_{m}^{+}}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\times\begin{cases}\psi(2^{-k}(r_{1}+r_{2})),\quad&\text{if}\;k\geq 1,\\
\psi_{0}(r_{1}+r_{2}),\quad&\text{if}\;k=0.\end{cases}\end{split}

(3.51)

Then, we can prove (
3.48
) if we could prove the localized estimates:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
T_{D_{m,k}^{+}}
be the operator associated with the kernel
K_{D_{m,k}^{+}}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})
. Then, there holds

\|T_{D_{m,k}^{+}}\|_{L^{p}(X)\to L^{p}(X)}\leq C\times\begin{cases}2^{-k\delta},\quad&\text{if}\quad p=2,\\
2^{k(\delta_{c}(p,2)-\delta)},\quad&\text{if}\quad p>4,\end{cases}

(3.52)

for any
k\geq 0
.

Remark 3.7
.

In particular, for
p=4
, we can obtain

\|T_{D_{m,k}^{+}}\|_{L^{4}(X)\to L^{4}(X)}\leq C2^{-k(\delta-\epsilon)},

(3.53)

where
0<\epsilon\ll 1
. This is enough to show (
3.47
) since if we choose
0<\epsilon\leq\frac{\delta}{2}
.

Indeed, by Proposition

, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{D_{m}^{+}}f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\|T_{D_{m,k}^{+}}f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}

for
p=2
and
p>4
with
\delta>\delta_{c}(p,2)
. By interpolation and duality, we obtain (

)
for
p\geq 1
and

.

Therefore, we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2

if we could prove that Proposition
3.6
holds true, which is the main task of the next section.

Proof of Proposition
3.6

In this section, we aim to show (
3.52
) in Proposition
3.6
, which we recall here:

\|T_{D_{m,k}^{+}}\|_{L^{p}(X)\to L^{p}(X)}\leq C\times\begin{cases}2^{-k\delta},\quad&\text{if}\quad p=2,\\
2^{k(\delta_{c}(p,2)-\delta)},\quad&\text{if}\quad p>4,\end{cases}

(4.1)

with its kernel defined in (
3.51
) and (
3.49
).
We first consider the case
k=0
:

\|T_{D_{m,0}^{+}}\|_{L^{p}(X)\to L^{p}(X)}\leq C,\quad\forall\;p\geq 1,

(4.2)

which implies (
4.1
) with
k=0
.
Similarly to the proof of (
3.12
), there exists a constant
C
independent of
\theta
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\sin(\theta)}{\cosh\frac{s}{\sigma}-\cos(\theta)}\right|\;ds\leq C<+\infty,

where

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{1}{\sigma}(\pi\pm(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})).

We prove this by decomposing the integral into the part with
s<\sigma
and the part with
s\geq\sigma

For the part with

\begin{split}\int_{\sigma}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\sin(\theta)}{\cosh\frac{s}{\sigma}-\cos(\theta)}\right|ds\leq\sigma\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-s/2}ds\leq C_{\sigma}.\end{split}

For the part with
s<\sigma

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\sigma}\left|\frac{\sin(\theta)}{\cosh\frac{s}{\sigma}-\cos(\theta)}\right|ds&\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\sin\theta|}{\sinh^{2}(\frac{s}{2})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\theta}{2})}ds\\
&\leq C_{\sigma}\int_{0}^{3}\frac{|\sin(\frac{\theta}{2})|}{s^{2}+\sin^{2}(\frac{\theta}{2})}ds\leq C_{\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}+1}ds\leq C.\end{split}

This together with the fact that
d_{s}\geq r_{1}+r_{2}

\big|K_{D_{m,0}^{+}}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\big|\leq C\psi_{0}(r_{1}+r_{2}).

Combining this with Lemma
A.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{D_{m,0}^{+}}f(x)\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\sigma\pi}\psi_{0}(r_{1}+r_{2})\big|f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\big|d\theta_{2}\;r_{2}dr_{2}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\theta_{1}}([0,2\sigma\pi],L^{p}(r_{1}dr_{1}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\sigma\pi}\frac{\big|f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\big|}{(1+r_{1}+r_{2})^{3}}d\theta_{2}\;r_{2}dr_{2}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\theta_{1}}([0,2\sigma\pi],L^{p}(r_{1}dr_{1}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)},

which implies (

).

Next we consider the case in which
k\geq 1
. We define

\begin{split}\tilde{K}_{D_{m,k}^{+}}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}):&=K_{D_{m,k}^{+}}(2^{k}r_{1},2^{k}r_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\\
&=\psi(r_{1}+r_{2})K_{D_{m}^{+}}(2^{k}r_{1},2^{k}r_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}),\end{split}

(4.4)

where the last equality follows from the definition in (

Its associated operator
\widetilde{T}_{D_{m,k}^{+}}

After rescaling, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{D_{m,k}^{+}}f(r_{1},\theta_{1})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\sigma\pi}K_{D_{m,0}^{+}}(2^{k}r_{1},2^{k}r_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\;r_{2}dr_{2}

\displaystyle 2^{-2k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\sigma\pi}K_{D_{m,0}^{+}}(2^{k}r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})f(2^{-k}r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\;r_{2}dr_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-2k}T_{D_{m,k}^{+}}\big(f(2^{-k}r_{2},\theta_{2})\big)(2^{k}r_{1},\theta_{1})

which is also equivalent to

T_{D_{m,k}^{+}}f(r_{1},\theta_{1})=2^{2k}\widetilde{T}_{D_{m,k}^{+}}\big(f(2^{k}r_{2},\theta_{2})\big)(2^{-k}r_{1},\theta_{1}).

There exists a constant

The proof Proposition
4.1
.

where

\begin{split}&\tilde{K}_{D_{m,k}^{+}}^{1}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\\

\begin{split}\|\widetilde{T}_{D_{m,k}^{+}}^{1}\|_{L^{p}(X)\to L^{p}(X)}\lesssim&2^{-k(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\times\begin{cases}2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\quad&\text{if}\quad p=2,\\

The proof of Lemma
4.2
.

By (

\left|\psi(r_{1}+r_{2})\int_{2\sigma}^{\infty}e^{i2^{k}d_{s}}\frac{a_{+}(2^{k}d_{s})}{(2^{-k}+d_{s})^{\frac{3}{2}+\delta}}\frac{\sin\big(\tfrac{\pi\pm(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{\sigma}\big)}{\cosh\frac{s}{\sigma}-\cos\big(\tfrac{(\pi\pm(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{\sigma}\big)}\;ds\right|\leq C(1+2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.12)

Due to the support of
\psi
, we only need to concern the case

d_{s}\geq r_{1}+r_{2}\gtrsim 1

Case 1:
2^{k}r_{1}r_{2}\lesssim 1.
From (
3.6

|a_{+}(2^{k}d_{s})|\leq C,

hence, combining with (
4.3

\displaystyle\text{LHS of}\;\eqref{equ:TD1kredfk}\lesssim

4.12

Case 2:
2^{k}r_{1}r_{2}\gg 1.
By a direct computation, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{s}d_{s}=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{1}r_{2}\sinh(s)}{\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+2r_{1}r_{2}\cosh(s)}},

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}d_{s}=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{1}r_{2}\cosh(s)}{\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+2r_{1}r_{2}\cosh(s)}}-\frac{(r_{1}r_{2}\sinh(s))^{2}}{(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+2r_{1}r_{2}\cosh(s))^{\frac{3}{2}}}>0,

where the last inequality is equivalent to

\displaystyle r_{1}r_{2}\cosh(s)(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+2r_{1}r_{2}\cosh(s))>(r_{1}r_{2}\sinh(s))^{2},

which follows by
\cosh s>\sinh s
, which shows
\partial_{s}d_{s}
is monotonic.

In the region
s\geq 2\sigma>2
and on the support of
\psi(r_{1}+r_{2})
, we know that

\displaystyle\partial_{s}d_{s}\gtrsim\min((r_{1}r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},r_{1}r_{2}).

This follows by considering two cases

(1)

(2)

e^{s}r_{1}r_{2}\lesssim r_{2}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}
.

In the first case, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{s}d_{s}=\frac{r_{1}r_{2}\sinh(s)}{\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+2r_{1}r_{2}\cosh(s)}}\gtrsim\frac{r_{1}r_{2}e^{s}}{(r_{1}r_{2}e^{s})^{1/2}}\gtrsim(r_{1}r_{2})^{1/2}.

In the second case, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{s}d_{s}=\frac{r_{1}r_{2}\sinh(s)}{\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+2r_{1}r_{2}\cosh(s)}}\gtrsim r_{1}r_{2}\sinh(s)\gtrsim r_{1}r_{2},

where we used
r_{1},r_{2}\lesssim 1
on the support of
\psi(r_{1}+r_{2})
.

Let

F_{k}(s,d_{s};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})=\frac{a_{+}(2^{k}d_{s})}{(2^{-k}+d_{s})^{\frac{3}{2}+\delta}}\frac{\sin\big(\tfrac{\pi\pm(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{\sigma}\big)}{\cosh\frac{s}{\sigma}-\cos\big(\tfrac{(\pi\pm(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{\sigma}\big)}.

(4.13)

Hence, by Van der Corput Lemma
A.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\text{LHS of}\;\eqref{equ:TD1kredfk}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left|\int_{2\sigma}^{\infty}e^{i2^{k}d_{s}}F_{k}(s,d_{s};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\;ds\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(2^{k}(r_{1}r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{-1}\left(\sup_{s\geq 2\sigma}\big|F_{k}(s,d_{s};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\big|+\int_{2\sigma}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}F_{k}(s,d_{s};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\;ds\right|\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.14)

\|T_{M_{k}}\|_{L^{p}(X)\to L^{p}(X)}\lesssim\begin{cases}2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\quad\text{if}\quad p=2,\\
2^{-\frac{2}{p}k}\quad\text{if}\quad p>4.\end{cases}

(4.25)

Let us introduce a even bump function
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([-\epsilon,\epsilon])
such that
\chi(\theta)=1
for
|\theta|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
and

\chi(\theta)=0
when
|\theta|\geq\epsilon
, where
0<\epsilon\ll 1
.
If

, we obtain

\int_{0}^{2\sigma}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\frac{4\sigma(\pi\pm\theta)}{s^{2}+(\pi\pm\theta)^{2}}\right)\right|\;ds\leq\frac{C}{|\pi\pm\theta|}\leq\tilde{C}.

So by Van der Corput Lemma, on the support of

, we have

\left|\int_{0}^{2\sigma}e^{i2^{k}\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}s^{2}}\frac{4\sigma(\pi\pm\theta)}{s^{2}+(\pi\pm\theta)^{2}}\;ds\right|\leq C\left(2^{k}\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence,

\left|\big(1-\chi(\pi\pm\theta)\big)\chi_{1}(2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})\psi(r_{1}+r_{2})M_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta)\right|\lesssim(1+2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

This together with Proposition
A.2
shows (
4.25
) with the cut off function
1-\chi(\pi\pm\theta)
.
Therefore, we are reduced to estimate the term

\begin{split}T_{\chi M_{k}}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}&\int_{0}^{2\sigma\pi}\chi_{1}(2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})\psi(r_{1}+r_{2})\\
&\chi(\pi-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))M_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\;r_{2}dr_{2}.\end{split}

(4.26)

Lemma 4.4
.

There holds

\|T_{\chi M_{k}}\|_{L^{p}(X)\to L^{p}(X)}\lesssim\begin{cases}2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\quad&\text{if}\quad p=2,\\
2^{-\frac{2}{p}k}\quad&\text{if}\quad p>4.\end{cases}

(4.27)

Proof.

We write

H_{k}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta):=\chi_{1}(2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})\psi(r_{1}+r_{2})\chi(\pi-\theta)M_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta).

(4.28)

We first consider the case
p=2
.
By the same argument as
[
2
, Lemma 2.3]
, we obtain

\begin{split}\big|\mathcal{F}_{\theta\mapsto\zeta}\big(H_{k}(r_{1},r_{2};\cdot)\big)(\zeta)\big|&\lesssim\begin{cases}(2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad&\text{if}\quad|\zeta|\lesssim(2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\frac{1}{|\zeta|},\quad&\text{if}\quad|\zeta|\gg(2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{cases}\\
&\lesssim(2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(4.29)

On the support of
\psi
(i.e.
r_{1}+r_{2}\simeq 1
) and
\chi
(i.e.
\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\in[\pi-\epsilon,\pi+\epsilon]
), we use the Plancherel’s theorem and (
4.29
)
to obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{\chi M_{k}}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\sigma\pi}H_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\;r_{2}dr_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\theta_{1}}([0,2\sigma\pi],L^{2}(r_{1}dr_{1}))}

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2\sigma\pi]}(\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\;r_{2}dr_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,1],r_{1}dr_{1}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{\theta\mapsto\zeta}\big(H_{k}(r_{1},r_{2};\cdot)\big)(\zeta)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\zeta}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{\theta\mapsto\zeta}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2\sigma\pi]}f(r_{2},\cdot)\big)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\zeta}(\mathbb{R})}\;r_{2}dr_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,1],r_{1}dr_{1}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{1}(2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2\sigma\pi]}f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right\|_{L^{2}_{\zeta}(\mathbb{R})}\;r_{2}dr_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,1],r_{1}dr_{1}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

Next we consider the case
p>4
. By the same argument as
[
28
, Lemma 5.4]
, we also have that

\big|H_{k}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta)\big|\leq C(1+2^{k}r_{1}r_{2}(\pi-\theta)^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(4.30)

which implies, for any

\displaystyle(2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|\theta|\leq\epsilon\sqrt{2^{k}r_{1}r_{2}}}\frac{1}{1+\theta}\;d\theta

\displaystyle(2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{1}}\lesssim(1+2^{k}r_{1}r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{1}}.

\theta

\displaystyle\|T_{\chi M_{k}}f\|_{L^{p}(X)}

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\sigma\pi}H(r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\;r_{2}dr_{2}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\theta_{1}}([0,2\sigma\pi],L^{p}(r_{1}dr_{1}))}

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{2}{p}k}\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)},

Lemma A.3

(A.7)

Lemma A.4

(Van der Corput )
.

Let

(a,b)
, and that

for all

\left|\int_{a}^{b}e^{i\lambda\phi(x)}\psi(x)dx\right|\leq c_{k}\lambda^{-1/k}\left(|\psi(b)|+\int_{a}^{b}|\psi^{\prime}(x)|dx\right)

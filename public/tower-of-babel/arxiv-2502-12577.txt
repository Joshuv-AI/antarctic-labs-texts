Analytic continuation in coupling constant applied to two-proton emitters
Tomohiro Oishi, Masaaki Kimura

Abstract
For quantum meta-stable problems with three particles, the 6 Be and 16 Ne nuclei as two-proton ( 2 ​ p 2p ) emitters provide a testing field. Considering the complexity of many-body meta-stable systems, a tractable solver has been on demand. We apply the analytic continuation in coupling constant (ACCC) to these nuclei, and confirm that this method can solve such problems consistently to the time-dependent results as well as to the experimental data. The sensitivity of the 2 ​ p 2p energies and widths to the proton-proton interaction is also investigated. The 6 Be shows a smooth shift from short-life to long-life systems according to the proton-proton interaction. Same is concluded for the 2 + 2^{+} resonance of 16 Ne. In contrast, for the first and second 0 + 0^{+} resonances of 16 Ne, their energies and widths show the avoid-crossing behaviour, due to the coupling of two configurations in the core-proton subsystem, 15 F. Since it requires only the bound-state solvers, the ACCC can be a low-cost option to solve three or more-body resonances.



Introduction.

Resonance is an essential phenomenon in open-quantum meta-stable systems.
In the non-Hermitian quantum mechanics, such resonant states are defined as the eigenstates with complex eigenenergies.
Analytic continuation in coupling constant (ACCC) has been utilized to solve a variety of resonance problems
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.
One advantage of ACCC is that one does not need to directly deal with the unbound wave functions with the scattering boundary condition.
Instead, one can extrapolate the bound solutions to the unbound region to evaluate the complex eigenenergy.

Two-proton (
2p
) radioactive emission is a unique radioactive mode in proton-rich nuclei,
in which the Coulomb barrier exists to realize meta-stable states
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
.
One simple picture for
2p
emitters is of the daughter nuclei and two protons, where three-body resonant problem should be solved.
In our previous works, time-dependent method was utilized to discuss them
[
12
,
13
]
.
However, its cost of computation often becomes expensive, and prevents us from the systematic survey.

Recent development of experiments has provided the accumulated data about
2p
emitters
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
From their energy release, lifetime, and
2p
correlations, a deeper understanding of open-quantum systems is obtained. Search for the new
2p
emitters is one of the most active topics, where several experimental facilities are involved.
In correspondence, predictions of
2p
emitters with their energies and lifetimes have been on demand.

In this work, we employ the ACCC method to solve the
2p
-emitting resonances.
The three-body model developed in previous works
[
12
,
13
]
is combined with the ACCC method.
By applying it to the light
2p
emitters,
6
Be and
16
Ne, we examine the validity of ACCC.
Note that, in
16
Ne, there exists a multiplet of
2p
-emitting resonances with
J^{\pi}=0^{+}_{1}

[
14
]
,
0^{+}_{2}

[
15
]
, and
2^{+}

[

,

]
.
The sensitivity of
2p
-emitting energy and width to the proton-proton pairing interaction is also investigated.

Model and formalism.

We employ the three-body model utilized in Refs.
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
13
,
12
]
.
The physical Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{3B}=\hat{h}(\bm{r}_{1})+\hat{h}(\bm{r}_{2})+\hat{H}_{pp}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}),

(1)

\displaystyle\hat{h}(\bm{r}_{k})=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{d^{2}}{dr_{k}^{2}}+V_{cp}(r_{k}),

(2)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{pp}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})=f_{pp}v_{pp}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})+\frac{\bm{p}_{1}\cdot\bm{p}_{2}}{m_{C}}.

(3)

The spherical core-proton potential
V_{cp}(r_{k})
(
k=1,2
) is given as

\displaystyle V_{cp}(r_{k})=V_{WS}(r_{k})+V_{Coul}(r_{k})

\displaystyle=\left[V_{0}+(\bm{l}\cdot\bm{s})W_{ls}\frac{d}{dr_{k}}\right]f(r_{k})+V_{Coul}(r_{k}),

(4)

where
f(r_{k})=\left(1+e^{(r-R_{0})/a_{0}}\right)^{-1}
(Woods-Saxon profile), and
V_{Coul}(r_{k})
indicates the Coulomb potential.
Parameters of
V_{cp}(r_{k})
for
6
Be and
16
Ne are given in
Refs.
[
12
]
and
[
27
]
, respectively.

The core-proton Schödinger equation reads

\hat{h}\ket{\phi_{a}}=e_{a}\ket{\phi_{a}}
, where
e_{a}
is the eigenenergy of the single-particle level with the quantum numbers
a=\{n_{a},l_{a},j_{a},m_{a}\}
,
which denote the nodal quantum number, orbital angular momentum, coupled angular momentum, and magnetic quantum number, respectively.
The single-particle wave function thus reads

\phi_{a}(\bm{r}\sigma)=R_{n_{a}l_{a}j_{a}}(r)\left[Y_{l_{a}}(\theta,\phi)\otimes\chi_{\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma)\right]^{(j_{a}m_{a})}.

(5)

The radial part
R_{n_{a}l_{a}j_{a}}(r)
is numerically solved with Numerov method.
We employ the single-particle orbits up to
l_{\rm cut}=7
with the cutoff energy,
E_{\rm cut}=24
MeV.
The continuum states with
e_{a}>0
are discretized within a box of
r_{\rm max}=30
fm radius.
Note that, since the ACCC procedure requires only the bound solutions, the radial box can be smaller than that for the time-dependent calculations
[
12
,
27
]
.

The
2p
wave function
\Psi_{2p}
is expanded on the anti-symmetrized basis
\left\{\tilde{\Phi}_{ab}\right\}
.
That is

\Psi_{2p}(\bm{r}_{1}\sigma_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\sigma_{2})=\sum_{ab}U_{ab}\tilde{\Phi}^{(JM,\pi)}_{ab}(\bm{r}_{1}\sigma_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\sigma_{2}),

(6)

where

\tilde{\Phi}^{(JM,\pi)}_{ab}(\bm{r}_{1}\sigma_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\sigma_{2})=\hat{A}\left[\phi_{a}(\bm{r}_{1}\sigma_{1})\otimes\phi_{b}(\bm{r}_{2}\sigma_{2})\right]^{(JM,\pi)}.

(7)

Here
J=\left|\bm{j}_{a}+\bm{j}_{b}\right|
(coupled angular momentum),
M=0,\pm 1,\cdots,\pm J
(magnetic quantum number),
\pi=(-)^{l_{a}+l_{b}}
(parity), and
\hat{A}
indicates the anti-symmetrization. The expanding coefficients
U_{ab}
are determined by diagonalizing the Hamiltonian matrix including proton-proton term
\hat{H}_{pp}
.

The pairing interaction
v_{pp}
is tuned to reproduce the experimental
2p
energies.
Its formalism and parameters for
6
Be and
16
Ne are given in Refs.
[
12
]
and
[
27
]
, respectively.
In the following sections, to investigate how this pairing interaction affects the resonance,
we also employ the factor
f_{pp}
as

v_{pp}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})\longrightarrow f_{pp}v_{pp}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}),

(8)

where the experimental point is determined with
f_{pp}=1
.

To apply the ACCC, we introduce an auxiliary potential multiplied by a factor
\lambda
.
That is

\hat{H}(\lambda)=\hat{H}_{\rm 3B}+\lambda V_{ex}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}),

(9)

where
V_{ex}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})
is an external, attractive potential.
For three-body systems, there are several options of this ACCC term
\lambda V_{ex}
.
We check the consistency between different options in the next section.

From Eq. (
9
), the original, physical Hamiltonian coincides
\lambda=0
, where the three-body system is unbound.
For
\lambda>0
, the additional attraction becomes active.
With certain value of
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, the system starts to have bound-state solutions as
\hat{H}(\lambda)\ket{\Psi_{2p}}=E(\lambda)\ket{\Psi_{2p}}
, where
E(\lambda)\leq 0
.
This eigenenergy is solved by diagonalizing
\hat{H}(\lambda)
.
Then, that is approximated as
E(\lambda)\cong-w^{2}(\lambda)
, where

w(\lambda)=\frac{c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{N}x^{N}}{d_{0}+d_{1}x+\cdots+d_{N}x^{N}},~~~x=\sqrt{\lambda-\lambda_{0}}.

(10)

Namely, we employ Padé approximation
[
1
]
. We set
N=5
, and
\left\{c_{1}\cdots c_{5},d_{0},\cdots d_{5}\right\}
are determined by fitting in the bound region (
\lambda_{0}<\lambda
).
Then, we analytically continuate
w(\lambda)
to the physical point (
\lambda=0
), where

x=\sqrt{-\lambda_{0}}
is purely imaginary.
Thus, the complex eigenenergy can be obtained as

E(\lambda=0)=-w^{2}(0)=E_{R}-i\frac{\Gamma_{R}}{2},

(11)

where
\Gamma_{R}
is the width of resonance.
Before going to the results, we mention the limit of the evaluation accuracy.
The present Padé fitting is feasible for
10^{-4}\lesssim\Gamma_{R}\lesssim 1
MeV.
The lower limit is due to the accuracy of the three-body solver,
whereas the upper limit is in particular from the Padé approximation.

Summary.

In this work, the
2p
-emitting resonances in the
6
Be and
16
Ne nuclei are investigated.
The ACCC method is developed to solve the resonance energies and widths.
The
6
Be nucleus shows a smooth shift from short-life to long-life systems according to the proton-proton interaction
v_{pp}
.
In contrast, in the
0^{+}_{1}
and
0^{+}_{2}
resonances of
16
Ne, the
2p
energies and widths show complicated behaviours, due to the coupling between different core-proton resonances by
v_{pp}
.
For such a case, we aim to report more careful discussions in future.
We also confirmed the feasibility of ACCC to the excited
2^{+}
resonance of
16
Ne.
Although finite deviations from experimental energy remain,
we expect that those are improved by optimizing the potential parameters, independently of the validity of the ACCC.
Consequently, the ACCC method can be used to find the
2p
-resonance solutions in various nuclei, including both ground and excited states.

The application of ACCC to other systems is in progress.
Since the ACCC method enables one to find the unbound solutions by solving only the bound states,
its application to other theoretical models may provide a wider survey along the proton-drip line.

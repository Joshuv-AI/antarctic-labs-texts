Divisorial Mori contractions of submaximal length.
Bruno Dewer

Abstract
A result due to Cho, Miyaoka, Shepherd-Barron [ CMSB02 ] and Kebekus [ Ke02 ] provides a numerical characterization of projective spaces. More recently, Dedieu and Höring [ DH17 ] gave a characterization of smooth quadrics based on similar arguments. As a relative version of [ CMSB02 ] and [ Ke02 ] , Höring and Novelli proved in [ HN13 ] that the locus covered by positive-dimensional fibres in a Mori contraction of maximal length is a projective bundle up to birational modification. We change the length hypothesis and we prove that the exceptional locus of a divisorial Mori contraction of submaximal length is birational either to a projective bundle, or to a quadric bundle.

\theta\circ\lambda
is a finite morphism onto a linear subspace of dimension

, hence an isomorphism.

From the above, we deduce that
\theta:E_{z}\to\mathbf{P}^{n}
is birational and finite, hence an isomorphism. The fibration

is thus a family of projective spaces. It is endowed with a relatively ample polarization

is

via the isomorphism

, and the existence of this relative hyperplane polarization ensures

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}}

. Under the additional condition that

, and we have

For any

.

. In particular, its image

in

. If the degree of the finite map

and

If

Corollary 4.7
.

If

is a fibre of

, then it is either isomorphic to a normal quadric, or as a cycle it is one of the following:

\bullet

reducible and reduced, namely:

with

,

\bullet

nonreduced, namely:

with

.

Proof  :

Given a fibre
\mathcal{Q}_{w}
of the quadric bundle
\mathcal{Q}\to Z^{\prime}
and

, there are three possibilities:

\bullet

is normal, in which case for

which dominates

, the morphism

. Since

, I.3.12]

is irreducible and generically reduced, and the morphism

is birational and finite by

, hence an isomorphism.

\bullet

is reducible, in which case we have the equality of

-cycles

where

. In this case

5
An example of a nonequidimensional divisorial elementary Mori contraction of submaximal length

Consider in
\mathbf{C}^{6}
with coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},\lambda,\mu)
the cubic affine cone

Y=\left\{\lambda x_{1}^{2}+\lambda x_{2}^{2}+\mu x_{3}^{2}+\mu x_{4}^{2}+x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+x_{1}x_{3}^{2}+x_{1}x_{4}^{2}=0\right\}.

One can think of it as a family of affine cubics
Y_{(\lambda,\mu)}\subset\mathbf{C}^{4}_{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})}
indexed by
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbf{C}^{2}
. Let
\varepsilon:X\to Y
be the blow-up of
Y
along
\Lambda=\left\{x_{1}=x_{2}=x_{3}=x_{4}=0\right\}\simeq\mathbf{C}^{2}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The variety
X
is smooth.

Proof  :

Since
Y
is smooth outside
\Lambda
, it is enough to prove that
X
is smooth along
E=\varepsilon^{-1}(\Lambda)
. We have a model for
X
inside
\mathbf{P}^{3}_{[u_{1}:u_{2}:u_{3}:u_{4}]}\times\mathbf{C}^{6}_{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},\lambda,\mu)}
with the following equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}+\mu u_{3}^{2}+\mu u_{4}^{2}+x_{2}u_{1}u_{2}+x_{3}u_{1}u_{3}+x_{4}u_{1}u_{4}=0,\\
\mathrm{det}_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}u_{1}&u_{2}&u_{3}&u_{4}\\
x_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}&x_{4}\end{array}\right)=0.\end{array}\right.

Since the codimension of
X
in
\mathbf{P}^{3}\times\mathbf{C}^{6}
is
4
, checking that
X
is smooth amounts to showing that the Jacobian of the system of its equations has rank
4
everywhere on
X
. Since
X
is the blow-up of the affine cone
Y
along an affine subspace which contains the singular points of
Y
, and
E
is the exceptional divisor of the blow-up,
X
is smooth on the complement of
E
. Hence we only need to check that the Jacobian of
X
has maximal rank along
E
, in other words when we specify
x_{i}=0
for all
i
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
u_{1}=1
and work with
(u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})
affine. A straightforward calculation shows that the partial Jacobian with respect to the variables
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},\lambda,\mu)
always has rank
4
.

Consider now the blowdown morphism
\varepsilon:X\to Y
. Given a point
p=(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda-\left\{(0,0)\right\}
, the fibre
E_{(\lambda,\mu)}
over
p
is the projectivization of the tangent cone of
Y_{(\lambda,\mu)}
at the origin, in other words
E_{(\lambda,\mu)}
is the quadric of
\mathbf{P}^{3}_{[u_{1}:u_{2}:u_{3}:u_{4}]}
given by the equation

\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}+\mu u_{3}^{2}+\mu u_{4}^{2}=0.

The fibre
E_{(0,0)}
over the origin is the whole
\mathbf{P}^{3}
.

The
\varepsilon
-exceptional divisor
E
is a hypersurface of
\mathbf{P}^{3}\times\Lambda
and it is generically a quadric bundle over
\Lambda
with an isolated fibre which is isomorphic to
\mathbf{P}^{3}
. In
X
, the divisor
-E
is
\varepsilon
-anti ample and the
(-E)
-minimal contracted curves are the lines contained in the fibres of
E\to\Lambda
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\ell\subset E
be a line contracted by
\varepsilon
. Then
-E\cdot\ell=1
.

Proof  :

Let
\ell
be a line contained in the general fibre
E_{(\lambda,\mu)}
. Then we may degenerate
\ell
to a line
\ell_{0}
contained in
E_{(0,0)}\simeq\mathbf{P}^{3}
, or contained in a singular quadric
E_{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}
, yielding
-E\cdot\ell=-E\cdot\ell_{0}
. Hence we only need to check that
-E\cdot\ell=1
for
\ell
contained in a smooth quadric
E_{(\lambda,\mu)}\simeq\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
.

Consider the surface complete intersection

\Sigma=Y\cap\left\{\lambda=\mu=1,\,x_{1}=0\right\}.

This is the affine quadric cone in
\mathbf{C}^{3}
given by the equation

x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}=0.

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}\subset X
be the proper transform of
\Sigma
via
\varepsilon
, i.e.,
\Sigma^{\prime}\to\Sigma
is the resolution of the singular point of
\Sigma
. This resolution contracts a smooth rational curve
\gamma=\Sigma^{\prime}\cap E
with
\gamma^{2}=2
. Moreover,
\gamma
is by construction a hyperplane section of the quadric
E_{(1,1)}
. For any line
\ell
in
E_{(1,1)}
we have thus

-2E|_{E_{(1,1)}}\cdot\ell=-E|_{E_{(1,1)}}\cdot\gamma=-E|_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\cdot\gamma=-\gamma^{2}=2,

yielding
-E\cdot\ell=1
in
X
.

Lemma 5.3
.

The blowdown morphism
\varepsilon:X\to Y
is a divisorial elementary Mori contraction of submaximal length.

The fibration
E\to\Lambda=\varepsilon(E)
, generically a quadric bundle, admits a fibre
E_{0}
which is isomorphic to
\mathbf{P}^{3}
with
-K_{X}|_{E_{0}}
the hyperplane polarization.

Proof  :

The general fibre
E_{p}
is embedded as a quadric surface in
\mathbf{P}^{3}
and it satisfies

\mathcal{O}_{E_{p}}(2)=-K_{E_{p}}=(-K_{X}-E)|_{E_{p}},

and since
-E\cdot\ell=1
for
\ell
a line contained in
E_{p}
and
-K_{E_{p}}\cdot\ell=2
, we have

-K_{X}\cdot\ell=1=\dim E_{p}-1

for
\ell
any line in the quadric
E_{p}
.

If we degenerate the general line
\ell\in E_{p}
to a line
\ell_{0}
in the central fibre
E_{0}=E_{(0,0)}\simeq\mathbf{P}^{3}
, we have
-K_{X}\cdot\ell_{0}=1
and via the isomorphism
E_{0}\simeq\mathbf{P}^{3}
, the restriction
-K_{X}|_{E_{0}}
is the polarization
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{3}}(1)
. In particular, the divisor
-K_{X}
is relatively ample, hence
\varepsilon
is a Mori contraction.

It remains to be proven that
\varepsilon
is an elementary contraction. By the relative cone theorem (see for instance
[
Deb01
, Theorem 7.51]
) there exists a curve
C\subset E_{0}
whose class is extremal in the relative Mori cone of
\varepsilon:X\to Y
, and an elementary contraction
\eta:X\to X^{\prime}
which contracts all the curves in the numerical equivalence class of
C
and fits in the following commutative diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subset}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\supset}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subset}

Since
E_{0}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{P}^{3}
, and
\eta
contracts a curve
C\subset E_{0}
, then
\eta(E_{0})
is a point of
X^{\prime}
. The restriction of
\gamma
to
\eta(E)
is thus a proper fibration over
\Lambda
whose central fibre is a point; by semicontinuity
\gamma
is locally an isomorphism over the origin of
Y
. This ensures that there exists a quadric fibre
E_{p}
for
p\neq 0
such that
\eta(E_{p})
is a point; as a consequence both families of lines on the general fibre of
E\to\Lambda
are contracted, and as a consequence
\eta(E)\simeq\Lambda
and
\gamma
is an isomorphism. Therefore
\varepsilon
is elementary. Moreover, it has submaximal length (
Theorem
1.3
) by the equality
l(\varepsilon)=-K_{X}\cdot\ell=1=\dim E_{p}-1
, for
\ell
a line contained in the general fibre
E_{p}
.

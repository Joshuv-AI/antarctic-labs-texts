Lin-Yue Li
1

Abstract




Arithmetic properties of generalized Delannoy polynomials and Schröder polynomials

Abstract.

Let
n
be any nonnegative integer and

D_{n}^{(h)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}^{h}\binom{2k}{k}^{h}{x}^{k}\text{ and }S_{n}^{(h)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}^{h}C_{k}^{h}{x}^{k}

be the generalized Delannoy polynomials and Schröder polynomials respectively.
Here
C_{k}
is the Catalan number and
h
is a positive integer.
In this paper, we prove that

\displaystyle\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

\displaystyle\frac{(2,hm-1,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

\displaystyle\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)S_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

\displaystyle\frac{(2,m-1,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)S_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

Taking
a=1
will confirm some of Z.-W. Sun’s conjectures.

Keywords
: arithmetic property; generalized Schröder polynomial; generalized Delannoy polynomial;

1
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
denote the set of nonnegative and positive integers respectively. Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
n
-th central Delannoy numbers
D_{n}
and the
n
-th large Schröder numbers
S_{n}

[
11
]
are defined respectively by

D_{n}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}

and

S_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{k}\frac{1}{k+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}C_{k},

where
C_{k}
denotes the Catalan number
\binom{2k}{k}/(k+1)
.

There are many combinatorial objects that are counted by
D_{n}
and
S_{n}
.
For example,
D_{n}
is the number of lattice paths from the point
(0,0)
to
(n,n)
with steps choosing from the set
\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}
, while
S_{n}
counts lattice paths satisfying the same constraints together with the limitation that never rise above the line
y=x
.
One can consult items A001850 and A006318 in the OEIS
[
18
]

for other classical combinatorial interpretations.

Sun
[
12
,
15
]
discovered many amazing arithmetic properties of
D_{n}
and
S_{n}
.
For example he proved

\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{D_{k}}{k^{2}}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/2}2E_{p-3}\pmod{p}\text{\quad and \quad}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{S_{k}}{6^{k}}\equiv 0\pmod{p}

for any prime
p>3
, where
E_{k}
are the Euler numbers,
and conjectured that

\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}D_{k}S_{k}\equiv-2p\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{3+(-1)^{k}}{k}\pmod{p^{4}}

which was confirmed by Liu in
[
8
]
.
Sun
[
15
]
also showed that

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)D_{k}^{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\text{ for }n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}.

In 2012, Guo and Zeng
[
5
]
derived

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{k}(2k+1)k^{a}(k+1)^{a}D_{k}\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{k}(2k+1)^{2a+1}D_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{n},

where
n\geq 1
,
a\geq 0
, and
\varepsilon=\pm 1
.
In 2017, Cao and Pan
[
2
]
showed that,

S_{n+2^{\alpha}}\equiv S_{n}+2^{\alpha+1}\pmod{2^{\alpha+2}}

for
n\geq 1
and
\alpha\geq 1
.

Motivated by the deﬁnition of Delannoy numbers and Schröder numbers, Sun
[
13
,
14
]
defined the Delannoy polynomials
D_{n}(x)
and the large Schröder
polynomials
S_{n}(x)
as

D_{n}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{k}x^{k}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{k}\frac{x^{k}}{k+1}.

Clearly,
D_{n}(1)
and
S_{n}(1)
reduce to the
n
-th central Delannoy numbers and the
n
-th large Schröder numbers respectively.
The Delannoy polynomials and large Schröder polynomials also have nice arithmetic properties.
For example, Sun
[
15
]
proved that

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}D_{k}(x)^{2}\equiv\left(\frac{x(x+1)}{p}\right)\pmod{p}

for any odd prime
p
and integer
x
, where
\left(\frac{\bullet}{p}\right)
denotes the Legendre symbol.
Guo
[
4
]
confirmed Sun’s conjecture in
[
15
]
that for any prime
p
and integer
x
satisfying
p\nmid x(x+1)
,

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(2k+1)D_{k}(x)^{3}\equiv p\left(\frac{-4x-3}{p}\right)\pmod{p^{2}}

and

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(2k+1)D_{k}(x)^{4}\equiv p\pmod{p^{2}}.

Liu
[
8
]
proved that

\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}D_{k}(x)S_{k}(x)\equiv 0\pmod{p},

where
p
is an odd prime and
x
is an integer not divisible by
p
.
In 2018, Sun
[
16
]
found

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}D_{k}(x)s_{k+1}(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x(x+1)]\quad\text{for all $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}$}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}D_{k}(x)s_{k+1}(x)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}}

for any odd prime
p
and
p
-adic integer
x\not\equiv 0,-1\pmod{p}
, where
s_{n}(x)
is the little Schröder
polynomials given by
s_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n}{k-1}x^{k-1}(x+1)^{n-k}
.
In 2024, Jia, Wang and Zhong
[
7
]
showed that

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(2k+1)^{2a+1}\varepsilon^{k}S_{k}(x)\equiv 1\pmod{p}

(1.1)

for any odd prime
p
, nonnegative integers
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}
and integer
x
with
p\nmid x(x+1)
.

The polynomials
D_{n}(x)
and
S_{n}(x)
can be further generalized.
In 2012, Guo and Zeng
[
6
]
introduced the Schmidt polynomials

D_{n}^{(h)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{h}\binom{n+k}{k}^{h}x^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}^{h}\binom{2k}{k}^{h}{x}^{k}.

(1.2)

Pan
[
9
]
proved that, for any positive integers
h
,
m
and
n
, there holds

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{k}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\equiv 0\pmod{n},

where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
.
In 2018, Chen and Guo
[
1
]
defined the multi-variable Schmidt polynomials as

S_{n}^{(h)}(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}^{h}\binom{2k}{k}x_{k},\quad h\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}

and proved that for
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
h,m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, one have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)S^{(h)}_{k}(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{m}\equiv\ 0\pmod{n},

(1.3)

where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
.

In 2022, Sun
[
17
]
defined

S_{n}^{(h)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{k}\binom{n+k}{2k}^{h}C_{k}^{h}{x}^{k},n\in{\mathbb{N}}

(1.4)

for
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}

and provided many interesting conjectures involving
S_{k}^{(h)}(x)
and
D_{k}^{(h)}(x)
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Conjecture 5.3 of Sun
[
17
]
)
.

For any
{h,m,n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
,

(i)
we have

\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k(k+1)(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]

and

\frac{(2,hm-1,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k(k+1)(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

(ii)
we have

\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k(k+1)(2k+1)S_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]

and

\frac{(2,m-1,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k(k+1)(2k+1)S_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

In this paper, we confirm and generalize the above four conjectures.
More precisely, we proved that

\displaystyle\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n(n+1)(n+2)}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]\ for\ h>1,

\displaystyle\frac{(2,m-1,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

\displaystyle\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)S_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

\displaystyle\frac{(2,m-1,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)S_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]

for
{h,m,n,a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In section 2, we provide proofs of the first three results.
The remained two will be discussed in section 3.

2
Congruences involving
D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}

In this section, we study the arithmetic properties of
D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
D_{k}^{(h)}(x)
be given as in (
1.2
) and
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}
.
For any
{h,m,n,a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varepsilon^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

(2.1)

Proof.
Let
x_{k}=\binom{2k}{k}^{h-1}x^{k}
in (
1.3
) gives

n\mid\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}.

(2.2)

Note that
a\geq 1
, this leads to

n\mid\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varepsilon^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}

(2.3)

since the
n
-th term in the summation contains
n^{a}
.
Let
n
be
n+1
and
n+2
in (
2.3
), similar discussions leads to

(n+1)\mid\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varepsilon^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}

(2.4)

and

(n+2)\mid\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varepsilon^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}.

(2.5)

Then (
2.1
) follows from (
2.3
)–(
2.5
) by noting that

[n(n+1),(n+2)]=\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{(2,n)}.

When
\varepsilon=-1
, a stronger version can be given.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any
{h,m,n,a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\frac{1}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]\ for\ h>1,

(2.6)

and

\frac{(2,m-1,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

(2.7)

The proofs of (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) are much more complicated.
To proceed, some preliminaries are needed.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
n,u,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)\binom{k+\ell+u}{2\ell+2u}=(-1)^{n}(n+1+\ell+u)\binom{n+\ell+u}{2\ell+2u}.

(2.8)

Proof.

By Gosper’s algorithm
[
3
]
, we have

(-1)^{k}(2k+1)\binom{k+\ell+u}{2\ell+2u}=\Delta_{k}\left((-1)^{k+1}(k-\ell-u)\binom{k+\ell+u}{2\ell+2u}\right),

(2.9)

where
\Delta_{k}
is the difference operator
(that is,
\Delta_{k}F(k)=F(k+1)-F(k)
).
Summing both sides of equality (
2.9
) over

k
from
0
to
n
leads to the equality (
2.8
).

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
n,\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\frac{2}{n+2}\binom{n-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n+\ell+1}{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

(2.10)

Proof.

Clearly, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n+2}\binom{n-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n+\ell+1}{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{n}{n+2}\right)\binom{n-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n+\ell+1}{\ell}

\displaystyle=\binom{n-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n+\ell+1}{\ell}-\binom{n}{\ell}\binom{n+\ell+1}{n+2}.

This ends the proof.

Let
(x)_{0}=1
and
(x)_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)
for all
n\geq 1
. In 2012, Guo and Zeng
[
5
]
proved that for any given
k,\ell,a\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exist integers
C_{0}{(\ell,a)},C_{1}{(\ell,a)},\cdots,C_{a}{(\ell,a)}
independent of
k
, such that

k^{a}(k+1)^{a}=\sum_{u=0}^{a}C_{u}{(\ell,a)}\prod_{v=1}^{u}\left(k(k+1)-(\ell+v-1)(\ell+v)\right)

(2.11)

and then

k^{a}(k+1)^{a}\binom{k+\ell}{2\ell}=\sum_{u=0}^{a}C_{u}{(\ell,a)}\binom{k+\ell+u}{2\ell+2u}(2\ell+1)_{2u}.

Multiplying both sides by
\binom{2\ell}{\ell}
, we obtain

k^{a}(k+1)^{a}\binom{k+\ell}{2\ell}\binom{2\ell}{\ell}=\sum_{u=0}^{a}K_{u}{(\ell,a)}\binom{k+\ell+u}{2\ell+2u},

(2.12)

where

K_{u}{(\ell,a)}=C_{u}{(\ell,a)}\binom{2\ell+2u}{\ell+u}(\ell+1)_{u}^{2}.

(2.13)

Equality (
2.11
) also shows

C_{a}{(\ell,a)}=1\text{ for }\ a\geq 0,

(2.14)

C_{0}{(\ell,a)}=\ell(\ell+1)\sum_{u=1}^{a}(-1)^{u-1}C_{u}{(\ell,a)}\prod_{v=2}^{u}(\ell+v-1)(\ell+v)\quad\text{ for }\ a\geq 1

(2.15)

and

C_{1}{(\ell,a)}=\sum_{u=2}^{a}(-1)^{u}C_{u}{(\ell,a)}\sum_{j=1}^{u}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\neq j\\
1\leq v\leq u\end{subarray}}(\ell+v-1)(\ell+v)\quad\text{ for }\ a>1.

Thus one can see

Lemma 2.5
.

Given
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, then

\frac{C_{0}{(\ell,1)}}{\ell(\ell+1)}=1
.
When
a>1
,
\frac{C_{0}{(\ell,a)}}{\ell(\ell+1)}
and
C_{1}(\ell,a)
are even integers.

In 2018, Chen and Guo
[
1
]
proved that for
k,i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
h\geq 1
, there exist integers
b_{i,t}^{(h)}(i\leq t\leq hi)
divisible by
\binom{t}{i}
such that

\binom{k+i}{2i}^{h}\binom{2i}{i}=\sum_{t=i}^{hi}b_{i,t}^{(h)}\binom{k+t}{2t}\binom{2t}{t}

(2.16)

and that

\binom{k+i}{2i}\binom{2i}{i}\binom{k+j}{2j}\binom{2j}{j}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{i+j}\binom{i+j}{i}\binom{j}{i+j-\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}\binom{k+\ell}{2\ell}\binom{2\ell}{\ell}.

(2.17)

By (
2.17
) and the method of induction, it is straight forward to prove the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
{i_{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
j=1,2,\cdots,m
, there exist integers
B_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{(\ell)}
such that

\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{k+i_{j}}{2i_{j}}\binom{2i_{j}}{i_{j}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}}B_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{(\ell)}\binom{k+\ell}{2\ell}\binom{2\ell}{\ell}.

(2.18)

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
n,M,\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
and
I,i_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
for
1\leq j\leq 2M
.
We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2M}=I\\
0\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{2M}\leq n\end{subarray}}B_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2M}}^{(\ell)}\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

(2.19)

Proof.
We proceed by induction on
M
.
When
M=1
, by (
2.17
) we have

B_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{(\ell)}=\binom{i_{1}+i_{2}}{i_{1}}\binom{i_{2}}{i_{1}+i_{2}-\ell}\binom{\ell}{i_{2}}.

(2.20)

Note that
B_{i,I-i}^{(\ell)}=B_{I-i,i}^{(\ell)}
if
0\leq i\leq I
.
When
I=2J+1
is odd, clearly

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=I\\
0\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq n\end{subarray}}B_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{(\ell)}=2\sum_{i=\max\{0,2J+1-n\}}^{J}B_{i,2J+1-i}^{(\ell)}.

When
I=2J
is even, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=I\\
0\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq n\end{subarray}}B_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{(\ell)}=2\sum_{i=\max\{0,2J-n\}}^{J-1}B_{i,2J-i}^{(\ell)}+B_{J,J}^{(\ell)}.

Thus (
2.19
) holds when
M=1
as
B_{J,J}^{(\ell)}
is even by definition.

Next we will assume the statement is true for
M
and then show it is also satisfied for
M+1
.
Let
i=i_{2M+1}
,
j=i_{2M+2}
in (
2.17
).
Then multiplied the obtained equation with (
2.18
) leads to

B_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2M+2}}^{(\ell)}=\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2M}}B_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2M}}^{(\ell_{1})}\sum_{\ell_{2}=0}^{i_{2M+1}+i_{2M+2}}B_{i_{2M+1},i_{2M+2}}^{(\ell_{2})}B_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{(\ell)}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2M+2}=I\\
0\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{2M+2}\leq n\end{subarray}}B_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2M+2}}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{I_{1}=0}^{I}\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{I_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2M}=I_{1}\\
0\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{2M}\leq n\end{subarray}}B_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2M}}^{(\ell_{1})}\sum_{\ell_{2}=0}^{I-I_{1}}B_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{(\ell)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{2M+1}+i_{2M+2}=I-I_{1}\\
0\leq i_{2M+1},i_{2M+2}\leq n\end{subarray}}B_{i_{2M+1},i_{2M+2}}^{(\ell_{2})}.

When
\ell_{2}>0
, we know

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{2M+1}+i_{2M+2}=I-I_{1}\\
0\leq i_{2M+1},i_{2M+2}\leq n\end{subarray}}B_{i_{2M+1},i_{2M+2}}^{(\ell_{2})}

is even by the induction which leads to the conclusion.
When
\ell_{2}=0
, one can see
B_{\ell_{1},0}^{(\ell)}=\binom{0}{\ell_{1}-\ell}
equals
0
unless
\ell_{1}=\ell>0
.
Then the conclusion follows from the fact that

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2M}=I_{1}\\
0\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{2M}\leq n\end{subarray}}B_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2M}}^{(\ell_{1})}

is even for any
\ell_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
.

Proof of (
2.6
) in Theorem
2.2
.
By the definition of
D_{k}^{(h)}(x)
, one can check that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)D_{k}^{(h)}(x)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k+i}{2i}^{h}\binom{2i}{i}^{h}{x}^{i}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)\sum_{0\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\leq k}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{k+i_{j}}{2i_{j}}^{h}\binom{2i_{j}}{i_{j}}^{h}{x}^{i_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\leq n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{k+i_{j}}{2i_{j}}^{h}\binom{2i_{j}}{i_{j}}^{h}{x}^{i_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\leq n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{2i_{j}}{i_{j}}^{h-1}x^{i_{j}}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{a}(k+1)^{a}(2k+1)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{t=i_{j}}^{hi_{j}}b_{i_{j},t}^{(h)}\binom{k+t}{2t}\binom{2t}{t}

with the help of equation (
2.16
).
By Lemma
2.6
, there exist integers
\tilde{B}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{(\ell,h)}
such that

1.
Introduction

Our purpose in this article is to investigate the model-theoretic properties of the Farey graph,
a combinatorial and geometric structure that arises in a number of mathematical fields.
In geometric group theory, the Farey graph appears e.g. in the study of the free factor complex and the curve graph.

The Farey graph is isomorphic to the complex of conjugacy classes of free factors of rank 2,
OF_{2}

[
2

in any higher rank complex of conjugacy classes of free factors. This makes studying the theory of the Farey graph essential
for studying the free factor complex and the complex of conjugacy classes of free factors from the model-theoretic perspective.

The main purpose of this article is to prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

The theory of the Farey Graph is axiomatized by

(1)

every vertex is contained in an edge;

(2)

every edge is contained in two triangles; and

(3)

every finite subgraph of cardinality at least two has two removable vertices

2.
The theory of the Farey graph

The Farey graph is a planar graph that can be defined in a number of different ways. We will use the following
combinatorial definition of the Farey graph, see e.g.
[

]
and

Definition 2.1
.

The Farey graph
F_{1}=F_{1}(e_{0})
of level
1
consists of two triangles sharing the edge
e_{0}
.
Inductively, the Farey graph

of level

by adding a new vertex

for every boundary edge

Finally, the

is the union of these graphs

By construction, every edge in

is contained in exactly two triangles and the automorphism group of the Farey graph acts transitively on the set of ordered triangles.

Note that the symmetry of

which swaps the two triangles extends to a symmetry of

swapping the two hemispheres of

and fixing

For

For a vertex

in

It is easy to see from the construction that any finite subgraph

3.
Quantifier elimination

We now extend the language of graphs to a language

to obtain quantifier elimination for

We start with an easy lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a model of
T
.
For any two vertices
x,y\in M

there are only finitely many geodesics from
x
to
y
.

Proof.

We prove the claim by induction on

Let

(p_{1}=y_{0}=x,\ldots,y_{k}=y)
be a path with
x_{i}\neq y_{i},i=1\ldots k-1
. If no such path
exists we are done by induction. The concatenation of
p_{0}
and
p_{1}

is a simple cycle. This cycle has at least two removable points and since
all vertices have valency

and

and

. Thus,
(x_{0},x_{1},y_{1})
form a triangle.
If there is another path

, then by the same argument
(x_{0},x_{1},z_{1})
form a triangle.
Since the edge
(x_{0},x_{1})
is contained in exactly two triangles, this
shows that any path from

to

passes through one of

and the claim follows from the induction assumption.
∎

Corollary 3.2

For any two vertices

Proof.

This follows directly from Lemma

4.

-Stability and Morley Rank

As mentioned above,

]
. They also mention

\omega
and the set of vertices at distance

now allows us to establish:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\delta,\delta^{\prime}
be finite sequences in
\mathcal{C}
. We say that
\delta^{\prime}

embeds
into
\delta
if whenever

holds, then so does

Clearly, if
\delta^{\prime}
embeds into

.

Theorem 4.2
.

The Morley rank of the theory of the Farey graph is
\omega
.

We start with the following easy lemma:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
M
be a model of
T
and let
(x,y)
be an edge,
3\leq k<\omega
. There are finitely many simple
k
-cycles containing
(x,y)
.

Proof.

Clearly the claim holds for

.
Now suppose
k\geq 4
is minimal such that there are infinitely many simple
k
-cycles containing
(x,y)
. Since every cycle has at least two removable vertices and

and

cannot both be removable, in each cycle there is a removable vertex different from

. Removing it yields a simple cycle of length

containing
(x,y)
. Thus there is a
k-1
-cycle containing
(x,y)
which arises in this way from infinitely many
k
-cycles. Since every edge is contained in exactly two triangles, this is impossible.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
a,b\in G_{F},k=d(a,b),
and
C\in\mathcal{C}_{n},C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{m}
. Then the formula
P_{C}(x,a)\wedge P_{C^{\prime}}(x,b)

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
there are only finitely many geodesics from
a
to
b
. By Lemma
4.3
there are only finitely many cycles of type
C
and type
C^{\prime}
containing any edge of one of these paths from
a
to

. Now consider intersecting cycles of type

containing
a
and of type
C^{\prime}
containing

and not containing any edge from any of the paths from

to
b
. Since they intersect at distance
\leq n
from

and distance
\leq m
from

, these cycle segments together with one of the geodesics from

to

form a cycle of length at most

4.3
again shows that there are only finitely many cycles of type
C
at

and of type

at

which intersect, and hence the formula is algebraic.

∎

Score-Based Variational Inference for Inverse Problems
Zhipeng Xue, Penghao Cai Xiaojun Yuan Xiqi Gao

Abstract
Existing diffusion-based methods for inverse problems sample from the posterior using score functions and accept the generated random samples as solutions. In applications that posterior mean is preferred, we have to generate multiple samples from the posterior which is time-consuming. In this work, by analyzing the probability density evolution of the conditional reverse diffusion process, we prove that the posterior mean can be achieved by tracking the mean of each reverse diffusion step. Based on that, we establish a framework termed reverse mean propagation (RMP) that targets the posterior mean directly. We show that RMP can be implemented by solving a variational inference problem, which can be further decomposed as minimizing a reverse KL divergence at each reverse step. We further develop an algorithm that optimizes the reverse KL divergence with natural gradient descent using score functions and propagates the mean at each reverse step. Experiments demonstrate the validity of the theory of our framework and show that our algorithm outperforms state-of-the-art algorithms on reconstruction performance with lower computational complexity in various inverse problems.

2
Background

2.1
Inverse Problems

An inverse problem is defined as the estimation of an unknown state or a latent
\bm{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
from measurement
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times 1}
. Specifically, the measurement process can be described by a measurement operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{M\times 1}
, and the final output is a noisy version of the measurement:

\displaystyle\bm{y}=\mathcal{A}(\bm{x}_{0})+\bm{w}_{0}

(1)

where
\bm{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times 1}
is the measurement noise. Usually the measurement noise is assumed to be zero mean Gaussian with variance
\epsilon^{2}\bm{I}
. Other noise models may also apply. In linear inverse problems, the measurement function
\mathcal{A}
is linear and can be represented by a linear transform
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
. We focus on inverse problems with known
\mathcal{A}
in this paper.

2.2
Variational Inference

The inverse problem (
1
) can be formulated as a Bayesian estimation problem. The posterior distribution of
\bm{x}_{0}
is given by
p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})=\frac{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x}_{0})p(\bm{x}_{0})}{p(\bm{y})}
,
where
p(\bm{x}_{0})
is the prior distribution of
\bm{x}_{0}
and
p(\bm{y}|\bm{x}_{0})
is the conditional distribution of
\bm{y}
given
\bm{x}_{0}
. The posterior mean, i.e., the MMSE estimator can be employed for the estimation of
\bm{x}_{0}
. However, the posterior
p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})
is intractable in general since the prior
p(\bm{x}_{0})
and the likelihood
p(\bm{y}|\bm{x}_{0})
may be very complicated in real applications. An alternative way is to find an approximation of the posterior as in variational inference (VI). VI introduces a distribution
q_{\phi}(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})
and maximizes a lower bound of the log probability of marginal
p(\bm{y})
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log p(\bm{y})&=\log\int\frac{q_{\phi}(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}{q_{\phi}(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}p(\bm{y}|\bm{x}_{0})p(\bm{x}_{0})d\bm{x}_{0}\\
&\geq\int q_{\phi}(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})\log\frac{p(\bm{y},\bm{x}_{0})}{q_{\phi}(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}d\bm{x}_{0}=-\mathcal{F}_{\phi}(\bm{y})\\
&=-\text{KL}(q_{\phi}(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})||p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y}))+\log p(\bm{y})\end{split}

(2)

where the inequality is obtained by using the Jensen’s inequality. The lower bound is referred as the evidence lower bound (ELBO) or the negative of free energy
\mathcal{F}(\bm{y})
. It is worth noting that maximizing ELBO is equivalent to minimizing the KL divergence between
q_{\phi}(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})
and
p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})
as shown in the last line of (
2
). Many methods have been proposed for the optimization of (
2
) such as mean field VI
[
1
]
, black box VI
[
17
]
, stochastic VI
[
11
]
and normalizing flow VI
[
18
]
. However, these methods are difficult to be applied to real applications since prior
p(\bm{x}_{0})
is complicated and is usually learned by neural networks. It is shown that learning the distribution of high dimensional data through score matching
[
28
]
directly is inaccurate since the existance of low density data regions
[
25
]
. Also, perturbing data with Gaussian noise makes the data distribution more amenable to learn
[
25
]
which is the core of score-based generative model.

2.3
Score-Based Generative Models

Score-based generative models or diffusion models generate samples of a data distribution from the reverse process of a diffusion process. The diffusion process is also called the forward process where Gaussian noise is added gradually to the original data distribution until the noisy data are approximately Gaussian-distributed. More specifically, the diffusion process is a Markov process with joint probability of its latent states
\{\bm{x}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{T}
given by

\displaystyle p(\bm{x}_{T:0})=p(\bm{x}_{0})\prod_{k=0}^{T-1}p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k}).

(3)

Two classes of widely studied diffusion models, i.e., the variance preserving (VP) diffusion model
[
5
]
and the variance exploding (VE) diffusion model
[
25
]
are distinguished by Markov diffusion kernel
p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})
. For VE diffusion,
p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{k+1};\bm{x}_{k},(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2})\bm{I})
, where
\sigma_{T}^{2}>\sigma_{T-1}^{2}>\cdots>\sigma_{1}^{2}>\sigma_{0}^{2}=0
, and for VP diffusion,
p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{k+1};\sqrt{1-\beta_{k+1}}\bm{x}_{k},\beta_{k+1}\bm{I})
where
\beta_{T}>\beta_{T-1}>\cdots>\beta_{1}>\beta_{0}=0
. The learning of score function
\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{x}_{k})
is essential to the sample generation for both diffusion models, and thus such models are called score-based generative models. In VP diffusion models, a variational reverse process is leaned to minimize the KL divergence between forward and reverse process. The score functions of perturbed data distributions are trained with a variational bound. Samples are generated from the learned reverse process with ancestral sampling method. In VE diffusion models, the score functions of perturbed
data distributions are learned with a score network using denosing score matching
[
28
]
. Samples from the desired distribution are generated with Langevin dynamics method.

3
Diffusion Process and Posterior Estimation

The measurement
\bm{y}
in (
1
) and diffusion states
\{\bm{x}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{T}
in (
3
) form a new Markov chain
\bm{y}\rightarrow\bm{x}_{0}\rightarrow\bm{x}_{1}\cdots\rightarrow\bm{x}_{T}
and the reverse conditional
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
is given by
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})=\frac{p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})}{p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{y})}
,
\forall k=0,\cdots T-1
. In this part, we focus on the property of reverse conditional
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
. We relate the Markov chain
\{\bm{x}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{T}
to continuous stochastic process
\{\bm{x}_{t}\}_{t=0}^{1}
by letting
\bm{x}_{k}=\bm{x}_{t=k\Delta t}
, where
\Delta t=\frac{1}{T}
. Then, the discrete diffusion process (
3
) becomes a continuous process in the limit
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
. We have the following results in the limit of
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
.

Proposition 1

For diffusion models with forward process (
3
), the reverse conditional
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
,
\forall k=0\cdots T-1
, is Gaussian when
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
. For VE and VP diffusion, the mean and covariance of
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
are tractable with mean given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mu}_{k}(\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})&=\bm{V}_{k,1}\bm{x}_{k+1}+\bm{V}_{k,2}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\end{split}

(4)

where
\bm{V}_{k,1}=(\sigma_{k}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}
and
\bm{V}_{k,2}=(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2})(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}
for VE diffusion, and
\bm{V}_{k,1}=\sqrt{\alpha_{k+1}}((1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})((1-\bar{\alpha}_{k+1})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k+1}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}
and
\bm{V}_{k,2}=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}(1-\alpha_{k+1})((1-\bar{\alpha}_{k+1})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k+1}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}
for VP diffusion.
\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]
and
\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}
are the mean and covariance of
p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})
respectively.
\bar{\alpha}_{k}=\prod_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}
,
\alpha_{i}=1-\beta_{i}
. The covariance of
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
for VE and VP diffusion models are given respectively by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{C}_{k,\text{VE}}&=(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2})(\sigma_{k}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}\\
\bm{C}_{k,\text{VP}}&=\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{1-\beta_{k+1}}((1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})\left(\left(\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{1-\beta_{k+1}}+1-\bar{\alpha}_{k}\right)\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}\right)^{-1}.\end{split}

(5)

Proposition
1
generalizes the Gaussian property of
p_{t}(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t})
to the conditional case
p_{t}(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t},\bm{y})
when
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
. The essential is that the reverse diffusion process can also be expressed by a reverse SDE
[
27
]
. Based on Proposition
1
, we obtain the following main result.

Definition 1

The reverse mean propagation chain of a diffusion process is defined as

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{T}\rightarrow\bm{\mu}_{T-1}(\bm{x}_{T}=\bm{\mu}_{T},\bm{y})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{x}_{2}=\bm{\mu}_{2},\bm{y})\rightarrow\bm{\mu}_{0}(\bm{x}_{1}=\bm{\mu}_{1},\bm{y})

(6)

where
\bm{\mu}_{k}(\bm{x}_{k+1}=\bm{\mu}_{k+1},\bm{y})
is the mean of
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1}=\bm{\mu}_{k+1},\bm{y})
,
\forall k=0,\cdots,T-1
, and
\bm{\mu}_{T}
and
\bm{\mu}_{0}
are the initial point and the end point of the reverse chain respectively.

Theorem 1

For VE diffusion, when
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
, the end point of the reverse chain, i.e.,
\bm{\mu}_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{0}=(\sigma_{0}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})(\sigma_{T}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}\bm{\mu}_{T}+(\sigma_{T}^{2}-\sigma_{0}^{2})(\sigma_{T}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]

(7)

and
\bm{\mu}_{0}\rightarrow\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]
as

. For VP diffusion, when
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
,
\bm{\mu}_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mu}_{0}=\ &\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{T}}((1-\bar{\alpha}_{0})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{0}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})((1-\bar{\alpha}_{T})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{T}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}\bm{\mu}_{T}\\
&+(1-\bar{\alpha}_{T})((1-\bar{\alpha}_{T})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{T}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\end{split}

(8)

.

4
Score-Based Variational Inference

In this section, we propose a score-based variational inference method to implement the RMP framework. We show that tracking the mean of the reverse process of each step can be formulated as a sequential of variational inference problems which we prove to be equivalent to a variational inference problem for all latent variables. We solve the variational inference by stochastic natural gradient descent with approximations that simplify the calculation.

4.1
RMP as Variational Inference

In Section
3
, we present the RMP framework based on the reverse diffusion process. However, in practice, the reverse mean in RMP cannot be calculated using (
4
) since
\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]
and
\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}
are unknown. We now show that the RMP framework can be implemented using variational inference. Instead of applying variational inference on the conditional posterior of
\bm{x}_{0}
as in (
2
), we focus on the joint conditional posterior of
\{\bm{x}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{T}
, i.e.,
p(\bm{x}_{0:T}|\bm{y})
, which includes all the latent variables in the diffusion process. The variational reverse process with joint conditional is defined by

\displaystyle q(\bm{x}_{0:T}|\bm{y})=q(\bm{x}_{T}|\bm{y})\prod_{k=0}^{T-1}q_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y}),

(9)

where
q_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{k};\bm{\mu}_{k}(\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y}),\bm{C}_{k}(\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})),\forall k=0:T-1
. We set
q(\bm{x}_{T}|\bm{y})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{T};0,\bm{I})
. The variational reverse process is chosen as a Markov chain since the reverse of the diffusion process (
3
) is a Markov process. The KL divergence between variational joint posterior
q(\bm{x}_{0:T}|\bm{y})
and and joint posterior
p(\bm{x}_{0:T}|\bm{y})
is given by

\displaystyle KL(q||p)=\int q(\bm{x}_{0:T}|\bm{y})\log\frac{q(\bm{x}_{0:T}|\bm{y})}{p(\bm{x}_{0:T}|\bm{y})}d\bm{x}_{0:T}

(10)

where the forward joint posterior
p(\bm{x}_{0:T}|\bm{y})=p(\bm{x}_{T}|\bm{y})\prod_{k=0}^{T-1}p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1:T},\bm{y})
. For VE diffusion
p(\bm{x}_{T}|\bm{y})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{T};0,\sigma_{T}^{2}\bm{I})
and for VP diffusion
p(\bm{x}_{T}|\bm{y})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{T};0,\bm{I})
. The following proposition simplifies the minimization of
KL(q||p)
with proof given in Appendix
C
.

Proposition 2

For a diffusion process with forward process (
3
), the KL divergence between variational
q(\bm{x}_{0:T}|\bm{y})
and joint posterior
p(\bm{x}_{0:T}|\bm{y})
defined in (
10
) equals

\displaystyle KL(q||p)=\sum_{k=T-1}^{0}\int q(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{y})\int q_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})\log\frac{q_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})}{p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})}d\bm{x}_{k}d\bm{x}_{k+1},

(11)

and the minimization of
KL(q||p)
is equivalent to the minimization of

\displaystyle KL(q_{k}||p_{k})=\int q_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})\log\frac{q_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})}{p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})}d\bm{x}_{k},\forall k=0,\cdots,T-1.

(12)

According to Proposition
2
, we can minimize the KL divergence between
q
and
p
by minimizing the KL divergence
KL(q_{k}||p_{k})
, i.e., solve the following VI problem at each reverse step
k
:

\displaystyle q_{k}^{\star}=\argmin_{q_{k}}KL(q_{k}||p_{k}),\forall k=0,\cdots,T-1.

(13)

By propagating the mean of
q_{k}
and solving problem (
13
) at each reverse step
k
,
\forall k=0,\cdots,T-1
, we can approximate the RMP framework in Algorithm
1
based on variational inference, as detailed below.

4.2
Variational Inference by Natural Gradient Descent

For
q_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{k};\bm{\mu}_{k},\Lambda_{k}^{-1}\bm{I})
, the KL divergence between
q_{k}
and
p_{k}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}KL(q_{k}||p_{k})&=\int q_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})\log\frac{q_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})}{p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})}d\bm{x}_{k}\\
&=-\frac{N}{2}\log(2\pi/\Lambda_{k})-\frac{N}{2}-\mathrm{E}_{q_{k}}[\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})].\end{split}

(14)

A common practice to optimize
KL(q_{k}||p_{k})
is to update variational parameters
\phi_{k}=\{\bm{\mu}_{k},\Lambda_{k}\}
using mini-batch stochastic gradient descent which involves the calculation of
\nabla_{\phi_{k}}KL(q_{k}||p_{k})
. Since
q_{k}
is Gaussian, the gradient of
\mathrm{E}_{q_{k}}[\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})]
in (
14
) with respect to variational parameters
\phi_{k}=\{\bm{\mu}_{k},\Lambda_{k}\}
have simple forms
[
16
]
which are given by:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla_{\bm{\mu}_{k}}\mathrm{E}_{q_{k}}[\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})]&=\mathrm{E}_{q_{k}}[\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})]\\
\nabla_{\bm{\Lambda}_{k}}\mathrm{E}_{q_{k}}[\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})]&=-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{k}^{-2}\mathrm{E}_{q_{k}}[\text{Tr}(\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})]\end{split}

(15)

where
\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
and
\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
are the gradient and Hessian of
\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
respectively, and
\text{Tr}(\cdot)
returns the trace of the input matrix.

As a special case of steepest descent, gradient descent updates parameter that lies in the Euclidean space. However, our objective is to optimize parameters that represent a distribution, it makes sense to take the steepest descent direction in the distribution space. As in natural gradient descent
[
14
]
, the parameter to be optimized lies on a Riemannian manifold and we choose the steepest descent direction along that manifold. Thus, we choose KL-divergence as the metric of distribution space and take steepest descent in this space. For KL-divergence metric, the natural gradient of parameter of a loss function
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{E}_{q_{\phi}}[h(\bm{x})]
is defined as
\tilde{\nabla}_{\phi}\mathcal{L}=\bm{F}_{\phi}^{-1}\nabla_{\phi}\mathcal{L}

[
14
]
, where
\bm{F}_{\phi}
is the Fisher information matrix of
\phi
given by the variance of the gradient of log probability of parameter, i.e.,
\text{Cov}_{q_{\phi}}[\nabla_{\phi}\log q_{\phi}(\bm{x})]
. For
q(\bm{x})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x};\bm{\mu},\bm{\Sigma})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x};\bm{\mu},\Lambda^{-1}\bm{I})
, the Fisher information matrices of mean and precision are given respectively by
\bm{F}_{\bm{\mu}}=\Lambda\bm{I}
and
\bm{F}_{\Lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{-2}\bm{I}
. Thus, the natural gradients of parameters
\{\bm{\mu},\Lambda\}
have concise forms given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\nabla}_{\bm{\mu}}\mathcal{L}&=\bm{F}_{\bm{\mu}}^{-1}\nabla_{\bm{\mu}}\mathrm{E}_{q}[h(\bm{x})]=\Lambda^{-1}\mathrm{E}_{q}[\nabla_{\bm{x}}h(\bm{x})]\\
\tilde{\nabla}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{L}&=\bm{F}_{\Lambda}^{-1}\nabla_{\Lambda}\mathrm{E}_{q}[h(\bm{x})]=-\mathrm{E}_{q}[\text{Tr}(\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}}h(\bm{x}))].\end{split}

(16)

Following the natural gradient given in (
16
), we update the variational parameters
\phi_{k}=\{\bm{\mu}_{k},\Lambda_{k}=v_{k}^{-1}\}
of loss function in (
14
) using natural gradient descent (NGD) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mu}_{k}&\leftarrow\bm{\mu}_{k}-s_{1}\Lambda_{k}^{-1}\nabla_{\mu_{k}}KL(q_{k}||p_{k})=\bm{\mu}_{k}+s_{1}\Lambda_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{E}_{q_{k}}[\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})]\\
\Lambda_{k}&\leftarrow\Lambda_{k}-2s_{2}\Lambda_{k}^{2}\nabla_{\Lambda_{k}}KL(q_{k}||p_{k})=\Lambda_{k}-s_{2}(N\Lambda_{k}+\mathrm{E}_{q_{k}}[\text{Tr}(\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y}))])\end{split}

(17)

where
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are step sizes. We obtain a stochastic NGD update when the expectations of gradient and Hessian matrix in (
17
) are approximated by sample mean:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mu}_{k}^{(i+1)}&=\bm{\mu}_{k}^{(i)}+s_{1}(\Lambda_{k}^{(i)})^{-1}\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{L}\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})|_{\bm{x}_{k}=\bm{x}_{k}^{(i)}\sim q_{k}^{(i)}}\\
\Lambda_{k}^{(i+1)}&=\Lambda_{k}^{(i)}-s_{2}\left(N\Lambda_{k}^{(i)}+\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{L}\text{Tr}(\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y}))|_{\bm{x}_{k}=\bm{x}_{k}^{(i)}\sim q_{k}^{(i)}}\right)\end{split}

(18)

where
L
is the number of samples. The stochastic update of parameters of
q_{k}
converges to the local minima of KL divergence
KL(q_{k}||p_{k})
which is a Gaussian approximation of the posterior
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
. It is worth noting that we choose stochastic NGD since it achieves a good performance and parameters involved are easy to tune in our experiments. Other optimization methods may be applied. In stochastic NGD update (
18
), the gradient and Hessian of
\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
are required. We next introduce some approximations to simplify the calculation.

4.3
Score-Based Gradient Calculation

From Bayes’ rule, the score of reverse conditional
p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})=\frac{p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})}{p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{y})}
involved in (
18
) is given by
\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})=\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})+\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{y}|\bm{x}_{k})+\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{x}_{k})
, where
\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{x}_{k})
is the noisy score function which can be approximated by a well-trained score network
\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x}_{k},\sigma_{k})
and
\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})
can be calculated explicitly for both VE and VP diffusion models. For VE diffusion
\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})=\frac{\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}_{k}}{\sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2}}
, and for VP diffusion
\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})=\frac{\sqrt{1-\beta_{k+1}}}{\beta_{k+1}}\bm{x}_{k+1}-\frac{1-\beta_{k+1}}{\beta_{k+1}}\bm{x}_{k}
. However, the likelihood score, i.e., the gradient of logarithm conditional
\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{y}|\bm{x}_{k})
is hard to handle in general. For linear inverse problems, several SVD based approximations of
\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{y}|\bm{x}_{k})
are proposed in
[
9
,
8
,
15
]
for linear measurements and Gaussian approximation for general measurements are discussed in
[
24
]
. In
[
2
]
, the authors propose the following approximation that can be applied for general measurements:

\displaystyle\log p(\bm{y}|\bm{x}_{k})\approx\log p(\bm{y}|\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}(\bm{x}_{k}))

(19)

where
\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})
is the MMSE estimate of
\bm{x}_{0}
. For VE diffusion, according to the Tweedie formula:

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})=\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{x}_{k})}[\bm{x}_{0}]=\bm{x}_{k}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{x}_{k}).

(20)

Similarly, for VP diffusion, the MMSE estimation of
\bm{x}_{0}
given
\bm{x}_{k}
is

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})=\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{x}_{k})}[\bm{x}_{0}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}}(\bm{x}_{k}+(1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{x}_{k})).

(21)

The approximation error of (
19
) can be quantified with Jenson’s Gap as given in
[
2
]
. We choose the likelihood approximation of (
19
) in our implementation, other approximation methods can be applied to RMP as discussed in Appendix. As a conclusion, the gradient is calculated as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})\approx\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})+\gamma_{k}\nabla_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p(\bm{y}|\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}(\bm{x}_{k}))+\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x}_{k},\sigma_{k}).

(22)

where the parameter
\gamma_{k}
is added to balance the approximated likelihood score and prior score. We set
\gamma_{k}=\zeta\frac{\|\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x}_{k},\sigma_{k})\|_{2}}{\|\nabla\log p(\bm{y}|\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}(\bm{x}_{k}))\|_{2}}
where
\zeta
is a hyper parameter to be tuned for different problems. The idea behind the strategy is that we should always keep a balance between the data score and the likelihood score.

4.4
Fixed Precision Update

In the update (
18
), the Hessian matrix
\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}_{k}}\log p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
is difficult to acquire in general. Also, the complexity involved in the calculation of Hessian may prevent the application of the algorithm. Thus, we introduce an approximation that does not require the calculation of Hessian. According to the Proposition
1
, the update of precision
\Lambda_{k}^{(i+1)}
converges to the precision of
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
. Thus, we can fix the update of
\Lambda_{k}^{(i+1)}
in Algorithm
1
to the precision of
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
and only update
\bm{\mu}_{k}
at each step. According to Proposition
1
, for VE diffusion model, if we set
\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}=v_{\bm{x}_{0}}\bm{I}
, then the inverse of precision is given by
(\Lambda_{k}^{(i)})^{-1}=\frac{(\sigma_{k}^{2}+v_{\bm{x}_{0}})(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2})}{\sigma_{k+1}^{2}+v_{\bm{x}_{0}}}
. We cannot calculate
(\Lambda_{k}^{(i)})^{-1}
directly since
v_{\bm{x}_{0}}
is unknown. However, for the cases that
k
is close to
T
,
\sigma_{k}^{2}
is large enough comparing to
v_{\bm{x}_{0}}
. Thus,
(\Lambda_{k}^{(i)})^{-1}
can be approximated by
\frac{\sigma_{k}^{2}(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2})}{\sigma_{k+1}^{2}}
. For the case that
k
is close to
0
,
\sigma_{k+1}^{2},\sigma_{k}^{2}\rightarrow 0
, we have
(\Lambda_{k}^{(i)})^{-1}\approx\sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2}
. Similarly, for VP diffusion model, if we set
\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}=v_{\bm{x}_{0}}\bm{I}
, then
(\Lambda_{k}^{(i)})^{-1}=\frac{\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{1-\beta_{k+1}}(1-\bar{\alpha}_{k}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}v_{\bm{x}_{0}})}{\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{1-\beta_{k+1}}+(1-\bar{\alpha}_{k}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}v_{\bm{x}_{0}})}
. Since
\beta_{k}\approx 0
and
\bar{\alpha}_{k}\approx 0
, we have
(\Lambda_{k}^{(i)})^{-1}\approx\beta_{k+1}
. With stochastic NGD based VI and score-based approximations of gradient and Hessian, we summarize an algorithm given in Algorithm
2
.

5.3
Complexity vs Performance

The complexity of diffusion-based methods dependent on the number of neural function evaluations (NFE). For our algorithm, RMP consists of
T
outer loops and each outer loop has
T_{in}
inner loops. The operations in the outer loops can be ignored since only variable assignments and scalar calculation are involved. In each inner loop, two main operations are involved: denoising with score network
\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x}_{k},\sigma_{k})
and stochastic natural gradient descent step which requires the likelihood score. Thus, the NFE of VE/VP-RMP is
TT_{in}
. To show the performance of RMPs with different NFE, we compare the curves of NFE versus the performance of VP-RMP and DPS on SR task in Fig.

50

1000

6
Colculsions

In this paper, we propose a score-based variational inference framework for inverse problems. By optimizing the reverse KL divergence at each step of the reverse process and tracking the evolution of reverse mean at each reverse step, a practical algorithm for general inverse problems is proposed. Extensive experiments validate our theoretical results and demonstrate that our proposed algorithm achieves superior performance on various image reconstruction tasks.

Appendix A
Proof of Proposition
1

A.1
Gaussian Property

First, the reverse conditional
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
is given by

\displaystyle p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})=\frac{p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})}{p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{y})}

(23)

where
\bm{x}_{k}
and
\bm{x}_{k+1}
can be viewed as the discretization of continuous diffusion process of
\bm{x}_{t}
. We relate
\{\bm{x}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{T}
to continuous stochastic process
\{\bm{x}_{t}\}_{t=0}^{1}
by letting
\bm{x}_{k}=\bm{x}_{t=k\Delta t}
, where
\Delta t=\frac{1}{T}
. The discrete
\{\bm{x}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{T}
becomes a continuous process in the limit
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
. Thus, we focus on the continuous form of the reverse conditional, i.e.,
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})=p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t},\bm{y})
. The reverse conditional in a continuous form is given by

\displaystyle p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t},\bm{y})=\frac{p(\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t}|\bm{x}_{t})p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{y})}{p(\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t}|\bm{y})}.

(24)

As shown in
[
27
]
, the forward process can be characterized by the following SDE:

\displaystyle d\bm{x}=\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x})dt+g_{t}(\bm{x})d\bar{\bm{w}}

(25)

where
\bar{\bm{w}}
is the standard Wiener process. For VE diffusion,
\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x})=0
and
g_{t}(\bm{x})=\sqrt{\frac{d\sigma(t)^{2}}{dt}}
, and for VP diffusion,
\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x})=-\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)\bm{x}
and
g_{t}(\bm{x})=\sqrt{\beta(t)}
. When
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
we have

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t}-\bm{x}_{t}=\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x}_{t})\Delta t+g_{t}(\bm{x}_{t})\sqrt{\Delta t}\bm{\varepsilon}

(26)

where
\bm{\varepsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{I})
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}p(\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t}|\bm{x}_{t})&=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t};\bm{x}_{t}+\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x}_{t})\Delta t,g_{t}^{2}(\bm{x}_{t})\Delta t)\\
&\propto\exp{\left(-\frac{\|\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t}-\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x}_{t})\Delta t\|_{2}^{2}}{2g_{t}^{2}(\bm{x}_{t})\Delta t}\right)}\end{split}

(27)

and

\displaystyle p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t},\bm{y})\propto\exp{\left(-\frac{\|\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t}-\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x}_{t})\Delta t\|_{2}^{2}}{2g_{t}^{2}(\bm{x}_{t})\Delta t}\!+\!\log p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{y})\!-\!\log p(\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t}|\bm{y})\right)}.

(28)

When
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
,
\log p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{y})
can be expressed by Taylor expansion:

\displaystyle\log p(\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t}|\bm{y})\approx\log p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{y})+(\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t}-\bm{x}_{t})^{T}\nabla_{\bm{x}_{t}}\log p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{y})+\Delta t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{y}).

(29)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t},\bm{y})\propto\exp{\left(-\frac{\|\bm{x}_{t+\Delta t}-\bm{x}_{t}-(\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x}_{t})-g_{t}^{2}(\bm{x}_{t})\nabla_{\bm{x}_{t}}\log p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{y}))\Delta t\|_{2}^{2}}{2g_{t}^{2}(\bm{x}_{t})\Delta t}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t)\right)}\end{split}

(30)

which is Gaussian when
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
. Thus,
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
is Gaussian when
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
.

A.2
VE Diffusion

For VE diffusion models, the mean of
p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{k}]&=\int\bm{x}_{k}p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{k}\\
&=\int\bm{x}_{k}\int p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{0})p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}d\bm{x}_{k}\\
&=\int\int\bm{x}_{k}p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{0})d\bm{x}_{k}p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}\\
&=\int\bm{x}_{0}p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}=\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\end{split}

(31)

and its covariance matrix is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&Cov_{p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{k}]\\
=\ &\int\bm{x}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{k}-\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}\\
=\ &\int\bm{x}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\int p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{0})p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}d\bm{x}_{k}-\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}\\
=\ &\int\int\bm{x}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{0})d\bm{x}_{k}p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}-\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}\\
=\ &\int(\bm{x}_{0}\bm{x}_{0}^{T}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\bm{I})p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}-\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}\\
=\ &\sigma_{k}^{2}\bm{I}+Cov_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]+\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}-\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}\\
=\ &\sigma_{k}^{2}\bm{I}+Cov_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]=\sigma_{k}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}.\end{split}

(32)

Since
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
can be expanded as a Gaussian around
\bm{x}_{k+1}
using the second order Taylor approximation when
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
and
p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{k+1};\bm{x}_{k},(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2})\bm{I})
, then
p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})
can also be expanded around
\bm{x}_{k+1}
and approximately expressed in the form of a Gaussian when
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
, i.e.,
p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})\approx\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{k};\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}],(\sigma_{k}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}))
. For two multivariate Gaussian distribution
G_{1}(\bm{x})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x},\bm{\mu}_{1},\bm{\Sigma}_{1})
and
G_{2}(\bm{x})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x},\bm{\mu}_{2},\bm{\Sigma}_{2})
, the product
G_{1}(\bm{x})G_{2}(\bm{x})
is also Gaussian with mean and covariance given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mu}_{3}&=\bm{\Sigma}_{2}(\bm{\Sigma}_{1}+\bm{\Sigma}_{2})^{-1}\bm{\mu}_{1}+\bm{\Sigma}_{1}(\bm{\Sigma}_{1}+\bm{\Sigma}_{2})^{-1}\bm{\mu}_{2}\\
\bm{\Sigma}_{3}&=\bm{\Sigma}_{1}(\bm{\Sigma}_{1}+\bm{\Sigma}_{2})^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{2}.\end{split}

(33)

Thus, the mean and covariance of
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
can be calculated according to (
33
) and are given respectively by:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mu}_{k}&=(\sigma_{k}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}\bm{x}_{k+1}+(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2})(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\\
&=\bm{V}_{k,p_{1}}\bm{x}_{k+1}+\bm{V}_{k,p_{2}}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\\
\bm{C}_{k}&=(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2})(\sigma_{k}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})(\sigma_{k+1}^{2}\bm{I}+\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})^{-1}.\end{split}

(34)

A.3
VP Diffusion

For VP diffusion models, the mean of
p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{k}]&=\int\bm{x}_{k}p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{k}\\
&=\int\bm{x}_{k}\int p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{0})p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}d\bm{x}_{k}\\
&=\int\int\bm{x}_{k}p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{0})d\bm{x}_{k}p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}\\
&=\int\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}\bm{x}_{0}p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\end{split}

(35)

and its covariance matrix is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}Cov_{p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{k}]&=\int\bm{x}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{k}-\bar{\alpha}_{k}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}\\
&=\int\bm{x}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\int p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{0})p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}d\bm{x}_{k}-\bar{\alpha}_{k}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}\\
&=\int\int\bm{x}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}p(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{0})d\bm{x}_{k}p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}-\bar{\alpha}_{k}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}\\
&=\int(\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{x}_{0}\bm{x}_{0}^{T}+(1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})\bm{I})p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})d\bm{x}_{0}-\bar{\alpha}_{k}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}\\
&=(1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}(Cov_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]+\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T})\\
&\ \ \ \ -\bar{\alpha}_{k}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]^{T}\\
&=(1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}Cov_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]=(1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}\end{split}

(36)

Similarly to the case of VE diffusion, since
p(\bm{x}_{k+1}|\bm{x}_{k})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{x}_{k+1};\sqrt{1-\beta_{k+1}}\bm{x}_{k},\beta_{k+1}\bm{I})
, the mean and covariance of
p_{k}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k+1},\bm{y})
are given respectively by:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mu}_{k}&=((1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})\left(\left(\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{1-\beta_{k+1}}+1-\bar{\alpha}_{k}\right)\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\bm{x}_{k+1}}{\sqrt{1-\beta_{k+1}}}\\
&\ \ \ \ +\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{1-\beta_{k+1}}\left(\left(\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{1-\beta_{k+1}}+1-\bar{\alpha}_{k}\right)\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}\right)^{-1}\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\\
&=((1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})\left((1-\bar{\alpha}_{k+1})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k+1}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}\right)^{-1}\sqrt{\alpha_{k+1}}\bm{x}_{k+1}\\
&\ \ \ \ +(1-\alpha_{k+1})\left((1-\bar{\alpha}_{k+1})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k+1}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}\right)^{-1}\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\\
&=\bm{V}_{k,p_{3}}\bm{x}_{k+1}+\bm{V}_{k,p_{4}}\mathrm{E}_{p(\bm{x}_{0}|\bm{y})}[\bm{x}_{0}]\end{split}

(37)

and

\displaystyle\bm{C}_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{1-\beta_{k+1}}((1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}})\left(\left(\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{1-\beta_{k+1}}+1-\bar{\alpha}_{k}\right)\bm{I}+\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bm{C}_{\bm{x}_{0}}\right)^{-1}.

(38)

(49)

where the last line follows from

Gaussian Mixture Model

The data prior of
x_{0}

\displaystyle\begin{split}p(x_{0})&=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{N}(x_{0};\mu_{1},v_{1}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{N}(x_{0};\mu_{2},v_{2}^{2})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}(x_{0})+p_{2}(x_{0})).\end{split}

(52)

Then, the data score is

\displaystyle\nabla_{x_{0}}\log p(x_{0})=\frac{1}{2p(x_{0})}\left(-p_{1}(x_{0})\frac{x_{0}-\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}-p_{2}(x_{0})\frac{x_{0}-\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right).

(53)

The measurement
y=ax_{0}+\sigma_{0}\varepsilon_{1}
and for VP diffusion,
\bm{x}_{k}=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}x_{0}+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{k}}\varepsilon_{2}
where
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}
are i.i.d. from
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. Then,

\displaystyle x_{0}=\frac{x_{k}-\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{k}}\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}}

(54)

and

\displaystyle y=a\frac{x_{k}-\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{k}}\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}}+\sigma_{0}\varepsilon_{1}.

(55)

Thus,
p(y|x_{k})\sim\mathcal{N}(\frac{ax_{k}}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}},\frac{a^{2}(1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})}{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}+\sigma_{0}^{2})
and the likelihood score

\displaystyle\nabla_{x_{k}}\log p(y|x_{k})=\frac{a}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}}\frac{y-\frac{a}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}}x_{k}}{\frac{a^{2}(1-\bar{\alpha}_{k})}{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}+\sigma_{0}^{2}}.

(56)

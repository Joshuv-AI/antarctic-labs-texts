Recurrent pituitary adenomas treated by endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal surgery: The role of tumour extension and surgical feasibility
Authors: Denise Löschner, Sarah Adam-Mende, Anna Jung, Prajjwal Raj Wagle, Almuth Meyer, Geralf Kellner, Rüdiger Gerlach
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPituitary neuroendocrine tumours may recur within 1-5 years following primary surgical resection. In these cases, endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal surgery represents a potential therapeutic option.Research questionThis study analyses the outcome of EETS in functioning and non-functioning recurrent PitNETs, with particular emphasis on tumour extension and the endoscopic technique employed. This is the first evaluation of 2D vs. 3D visualisation in EETS for RPA.Material and methodsThis retrospective, single-centre study included patients who underwent EETS for recurrent PitNETs between 2009 and 2024.ResultsForty-six patients were included (16 female, 30 male). Thirty-four tumours (73.91%) were clinically non-functioning and twelve (26.09%) functioning. There was a statistical significance for complete intrasellar (χ2 test: p = 0.008) and suprasellar tumour resection (χ2 test: p = 0.016) in contrast to parasellar tumour extension. Knosp grade 4 tumours exhibited the largest mean volume (12.68 cm3) and were uniformly associated with subtotal resection. Endoscopic techniques were applied in an approximate ratio of 1:2 (2D-E:3D-E), with larger and giant adenomas predominantly treated using three-dimensional endoscopy. Rates of GTR and STR as well as the incidence of new postoperative endocrinological deficits were comparable between techniques. The most common complications were nasal bleeding (3/46) and cerebrospinal fluid leakage (2/46), without association with higher Knosp grades.Discussion and conclusionEETS is a safe and effective approach for RPitNETs, 3D-E has emerged as a favorable technique for RPA. The feasibility of GTR is primarily constrained by tumour extension, particularly parasellar invasion. Patients should therefore be counselled preoperatively regarding the likelihood of residual tumour.Keywords: Recurrent pituitary adenoma, Endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal surgery (EETS), Knosp grading, 2D endoscopy, 3D endoscopy

Introduction

Pituitary adenomas (PAs), recently reclassified as pituitary neuroendocrine tumours (PitNETs), are benign neoplasms in adults with a prevalence of approximately 1 per 1000 individuals (Daly et al., 2020). They predominantly occur in middle-aged and older adults, with the highest incidence between 60 and 79 years of age (Ioachimescu, 2023). Recurrence has been reported within 1-6 years after surgical resection (Miller et al., 2025; Roelfsema et al., 2012; Wagenmakers et al., 2009; Yu et al., 2000). Overall rates of regrowth or recurrence range from 30% to 40% (Chen, 2021; Chen et al., 2012; Dimopoulou, 2014; Palavani et al., 2026; Watts et al., 2017). Even after presumed complete resection, recurrence rates of 10-20% within 5-10 years have been observed (Watts et al., 2017; Karki et al., 2017). Suprasellar and parasellar tumour extension may limit complete removal at initial surgery, resulting in residual tumour and renewed growth in 12-58% of cases, frequently necessitating additional treatment (Chen, 2021; Chen et al., 2012). PAs are classified as functioning or non-functioning based on hormonal activity (Greenberg, 2023). Non-functioning adenomas account for approximately 35-40% of PitNETs and typically cause symptoms through mass effect rather than hormonal hypersecretion. These tumours are commonly macroadenomas, large or giant adenomas and may compress the optic chiasm, leading to visual impairment (Greenberg, 2023; Greenman et al., 2009; Mete, 2018). Functioning PitNETs represent approximately 65% of cases and are categorised according to the hormone secreted, such as prolactin, growth hormone or adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), with clinical presentation determined by hormone excess and related endocrinological dysfunction (Greenberg, 2023). Treatment selection depends on tumour type, size and clinical behaviour. Functioning adenomas, particularly prolactinomas, are frequently managed successfully with medical therapy alone (Force et al., 2022; Na et al., 2023; Zoli et al., 2025). Endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal surgery (EETS) is the treatment of choice for space-occupying non-functioning adenomas, ACTH- and growth hormone-secreting PAs, as well as medically refractory prolactinomas (Miller et al., 2025; Farrell et al., 2019; Maldaner, 2018; Saeki et al., 2010; Tabaee et al., 2009). PitNETs are further classified according to tumour size. Adenomas <1 cm are termed microadenomas, whereas those >1 cm are classified as macroadenomas (Jesser, 2014). Large PitNETs are commonly defined by a maximum diameter >3 cm in at least one plane and a tumour volume >10 cm3 (Müslüman, 2011; Juraschka, 2014). Giant adenomas typically exceed 4 cm in diameter and 10 cm3 in volume (Tabaee et al., 2009; Chen, 2022; Micko et al., 2021; Mohr et al., 1990). Compared with microscopic transsphenoidal surgery, EETS is less invasive and provides a panoramic surgical view, allowing for higher resection rates - particularly in suprasellar regions - along with lower complication rates and shorter hospital stay (Saeki et al., 2010; Goudakos, 2011). Both 2D and 3D endoscopic systems have demonstrated comparable resection and complication rates in primary PA surgery (Hajdari et al., 2018; Loeschner, 2023). EETS is also an established option for recurrent PitNETs, with acceptable resection and complication rates reported across multiple series (Alahmadi, 2012, Benveniste, 2005; Cappabianca, 2000; Cavallo et al., 2013; Chang et al., 2008; Do et al., 2017; Esquenazi, 2017; Hwang et al., 2013; Rudnik et al., 2006). Nevertheless, revision surgery remains technically demanding due to altered anatomy and postoperative scar tissue, which may obscure tissue planes and complicate differentiation between tumour tissue and normal pituitary gland (Wagenmakers et al., 2009; Esquenazi, 2017; Tajudeen et al., 2014).

Material and methods

Study design

This retrospective, single-centre study includes patients who underwent endoscopic treatment for recurrent or growing residual PitNET between 2009 and 2024 and was approved by the local ethics committee (reference number 22748/2023/41). Demographic data, pre- and postoperative endocrinological and ophthalmological status, and complications were analyzed. Resection rates and surgical outcome were compared by visualisation technique, which was applied according to the availability of systems (2D endoscopy, 3D endoscopy). Patients were followed up to twelve weeks postoperatively.

Surgical treatment, endocrinologic management and postoperative care

All patients underwent endoscopic intervention by an interdisciplinary team comprising a neurosurgeon and an otolaryngologist as described previously (Hajdari et al., 2018; Loeschner, 2023). Image guidance was routinely applied (Brainlab AG, Munich, Germany). Surgical procedures were not altered during the entire study period. Postoperatively, patients were monitored at the intensive or intermediate care unit for one day and, in the absence of complications, transferred to a standard ward on the following day. Every patient treated for recurrent PitNET via EETS received standardized peri- and postoperative hydrocortisone replacement therapy in accordance with the institutional protocol. This included an intraoperative intravenous hydrocortisone bolus of 100 mg, followed by oral hydrocortisone maintenance therapy at a dosage of 15-10-0 mg. All patients underwent standardized preoperative endocrinological testing, with follow-up laboratory evaluation performed 8-12 weeks postoperatively. Any newly detected dysfunction of a pituitary axis compared with preoperative testing was classified as a new endocrine deficit, even in the absence of clinical symptoms. Endocrinological remission was defined according to current consensus criteria (Biller et al., 2008; Giustina et al., 2014).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using the online statistics calculator DATAtab. Nominal variables were reported as frequencies. Metric variables were reported as mean, standard deviation, median, minimum, maximum, and range. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess normality. The χ2 test was used to evaluate associations between categorical variables. The Mann-Whitney U test was employed to compare non-normally distributed variables with at least ordinal scaling. Multivariable testing (logistic regression analysis) was used to model the association between a categorical dependent variable and multiple independent variables. Statistical significance was set at α ≤ 0.05.

Results

Demographic data

The study cohort comprised 46 patients, including 16 females and 30 males (Table 1). A greater proportion of male patients (n = 30; 65.22%) developed recurrent PitNET compared with females (n = 16; 34.78%) (χ2 test: p = 0.090). Patient age ranged from 29 to 80 years (mean 59 years; SD 13.78). Male patients with recurrent pituitary adenomas were significantly older than female patients (Mann-Whitney U test: p = 0.030) (Fig. 1). Of 46 patients, 20 (43.5%) had undergone previous surgery at our institution, whereas the majority (56.5%) were initially treated elsewhere. Detailed information regarding previous operations was available in 25 cases and was as follows: In two cases, a previous transcranial operation had been performed. The microscopic transsphenoidal approach was utilized nine times, and in 14 cases, the endoscopic endonasal technique was employed.

Comparative analysis of functioning and non-functioning recurrent pituitary adenomas

Thirty-four of 46 patients (73.91%) had clinically non-functioning recurrent pituitary adenomas, and 12 of 46 (26.09%) had clinically functioning tumours (Table 2). Patients with functioning recurrent adenomas were younger (median 46.5 years) than those without clinical hormone excess (median 69 years). A higher proportion of females than males developed functioning adenomas (♀ = 7/12, 58.33%; ♂ = 5/12, 41.67%). In contrast, a significantly higher proportion of male patients (25/34; 73.53%) exhibited non-functioning tumours compared with females (9/34; 26.47%) (χ2 test: p = 0.023). The interval between primary surgery and recurrence was 69.17 months on average (median 61.33 months; SD 45.87) for functioning recurrent PitNETs and 98.24 months (median 81 months; SD 69) for non-functioning recurrent PitNETs, without statistically significant differences (Kruskal-Wallis test: p = 0.306). In 65% of the patients (30/46), residual tumour remained after primary EETS; 35% had no detectable residual tumour postoperatively. Mean tumour volume in functioning RPitNETs was 5.11 cm3 (median 3.54 cm3; SD 4.75), lower than in non-functioning recurrent tumours (mean 8.4 cm3; median 4.73 cm3; SD 8.7). The most common Knosp grade in functioning recurrent tumours was grade 4 (33.33%), without significant differences compared to non-functioning tumours (χ2 test: p = 0.496). Non-functioning recurrent PitNETs more frequently exhibited suprasellar extension (82.35% vs. 66.67%). Complete tumour removal was achieved in 28.46% of cases regardless of hormonal activity. Endocrinological remission was observed in 50% of functioning recurrent PitNETs (6/12). The overall complication rate was approximately 15%. The most common complications were nasal bleeding (3/46; 6.52%) and cerebrospinal fluid leakage (2/46; 4.35%).

Comparative analysis of knosp-grading ≥2

Of the 46 patients with recurrent pituitary adenomas, 38 with a Knosp grade ≥2 were included in this comparison delineated by the classification system established by Knosp (Table 3, Table 4) (Biller et al., 2008). The largest number of cases (n = 15) was observed in Knosp grade 2. Twelve patients had Knosp grade 3 tumours, which were further subdivided according to Knosp into 3a, 3b, and combined 3a + b. Seven of the twelve grade 3 cases were classified as 3a, three as 3b, and two as 3a + b. Eleven patients had Knosp grade 4 tumours. Complete resection was feasible only in Knosp grades 2 and 3a. Partial resection predominated in grades 3a, 3b and 3a + b (71.43% to 100%). Notably, in Knosp grade 4, STR was feasible in all patients (11/11; 100%) (Fig. 2), whereas GTR was not achieved. There was a statistically significant association between parasellar postoperative tumour residue and Knosp grading (χ2 test: p2,3,4<0.001; p3a,3b,3a + b = 0.017). Analysis of tumour volume by Knosp grade showed that grade 4 tumours had the largest volume (mean 12.68 cm3; median 8.48 cm3; SD 11.57). Anterior pituitary insufficiency occurred in 27-33% and posterior pituitary insufficiency in 7-8% of cases with Knosp grades 2 and 3. The overall complication rate was low. Six cases (15.79%) exhibited complications without a clear tendency towards higher Knosp grades. The two most common complications were nasal bleeding and postoperative CSF leakage, occurring in three and two cases, respectively.

Comparative analysis of endoscopic visualisation

All 46 patients underwent surgery using endoscopic techniques. Seventeen (36.96%) were treated using 2D-E and 29 (63.04%) using 3D-E (Table 5). In the 3D-E group, mean tumour volume was 7.91 cm3 (median 4.66 cm3; SD 8.7), while in the 2D-E group it was 7.07 cm3 (median 3.66 cm3; SD 6.79). Comparison of maximum tumour size also suggests that larger tumours were priory operated in the 3D-E group (maximum 3D-E: 41.5 cm3; maximum 2D-E: 23.4 cm3). Parasellar tumours were more frequently treated using 3D-E (17/29; 58.62%) than 2D-E (6/17; 35.29%), without statistical significance (χ2 test: p = 0.367). Postoperative parasellar tumour remnant was common in both groups, indicating that resection in this region remained challenging. Residual suprasellar tumour was observed in 43.48% (10/23) of cases in the 3D-E group and 61.54% (8/13) in the 2D-E group. Various techniques and materials of dural and sellar reconstruction were performed, and autologous and allogen material was used. Reconstruction using bone alone was performed most frequently (16/46). Combined techniques (e.g. fat and fascia, Tutoplast® and bone or DuraGen® and bone) were applied 23.9% (11/46). In the 3D-E group, postoperative improvement in visual acuity occurred in 17.24% and improvement in visual field in 20.69%. Most patients had stable visual acuity and visual field findings postoperatively (approximately 76-80%). New postoperative endocrinological deficits occurred at similar frequencies in both groups: 21-24% for anterior pituitary deficits and 6-10% for posterior pituitary deficits. In 9 of 10 cases of postoperative insufficiency of anterior lobe, a suprasellar tumour expansion was found. The overall complication rate was low, with seven of 46 patients (15.22%) experiencing complications. Most complications occurred in the 3D-E group (6/29; 20.69%). The most common postoperative complications were epistaxis (3D-E: 2/29, 6.9%; 2D-E: 1/17, 5.88%) and CSF leak (3D-E: 2/29, 6.9%; 2D-E: 0/17, 0%). In multivariable testing (logistic regression), complication rate was not associated with tumour volume, extent, intratumoral hemorrhage or endocrinological status (p = 0.695). Discussion

Demographic data

This analysis included 46 patients with recurrent pituitary adenomas over a 15-year period. Comparable studies have reported cohorts ranging from 12 to 96 patients (Wagenmakers et al., 2009; Tabaee et al., 2009; Alahmadi, 2012, Benveniste, 2005; Cappabianca, 2000; Cavallo et al., 2013; Chang et al., 2008; Do et al., 2017; Esquenazi, 2017; Rudnik et al., 2006; Tajudeen et al., 2014; Yamada et al., 2010; Cherebillo et al., 2024). In our study, the female-to-male ratio was approximately 1:2, without reaching statistical significance. Watts et al. described a higher probability of recurrent PitNET in males, particularly when residual tumour remained after primary surgery (Watts et al., 2017). Other studies, however, reported no significant association between sex and recurrence (Batista et al., 2018; Lee et al., 2016). The type of previous surgical intervention (transcranial vs. microscopic transsphenoidal vs. endoscopic endonasal) was analyzed, with endoscopic endonasal primary treatment being the most frequently performed. The various types of prior treatment present surgeons with distinct challenges - for example, the presence of scarring, altered anatomy, bone defects, or changes to the dura mater (Alahmadi, 2012, Cappabianca, 2000; Cavallo et al., 2013; Esquenazi, 2017; Hwang et al., 2013).

Comparative analysis of functioning and non-functioning recurrent pituitary adenomas

In this cohort, surgically treated recurrences were more commonly non-functioning than functioning. Prolactinomas represent the most common functioning PitNETs and are frequently managed pharmacologically, which likely contributes to fewer surgical cases in this subgroup (Zoli et al., 2025; Daly, 2006; Fernandez et al., 2010; Tjörnstrand et al., 2014). Patients with non-functioning recurrent tumours were older on average than those with functioning recurrences, consistent with the slower growth and delayed symptom onset typical of non-functioning lesions. Functioning tumours tend to be detected earlier due to endocrine symptoms, and affected patients are often younger (Mete, 2018). Non-functioning recurrent PitNETs occurred significantly more often in males, whereas functioning tumours were more common in females, consistent with prior findings (Fernandez et al., 2010). The mean interval between primary surgery and recurrence was longer in non-functioning tumours than in functioning tumours, which may reflect symptom-driven detection and follow-up patterns. Published series have reported mean recurrence intervals of 4-6 years, supporting the need for prolonged surveillance, especially in non-functioning tumours (Miller et al., 2025; Wagenmakers et al., 2009; Watts et al., 2017; Cherebillo et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2016; McClure et al., 2023). Younger age has been associated with higher recurrence risk and may justify lifelong monitoring (Watts et al., 2017; Gupta et al., 2024). In 65% of our patients (30/46), residual tumour remained after primary EETS with renewed growth indicating second surgery, as known from other studies (Chen, 2021; Chen et al., 2012; Dimopoulou, 2014; Palavani et al., 2026; Watts et al., 2017). Residual tumour is a recognised risk factor for recurrence, and recurrence risk is reduced when complete resection is achieved without residual tissue (Watts et al., 2017; Karki et al., 2017). Conversely, the presence of residual tumour increases the likelihood of recurrence (Chen, 2021; Chen et al., 2012). Regarding endocrinological outcome, approximately half of patients with functioning RPitNETs achieved endocrinological remission after reoperation, comparable to published remission rates of 35-77% after repeated EETS (Zoli et al., 2025; Benveniste, 2005; Chang et al., 2008; Buchy, 2019; Shapey et al., 2026). These findings support reoperation as a reasonable option in selected functioning tumours. Complete removal was achieved in 28.46% of cases, consistent with other EETS series (Palavani et al., 2026; Juraschka, 2014 Chen, 2022; Loeschner, 2023), although some studies have reported higher gross total resection rates (40-54%) (Cavallo et al., 2013; Esquenazi, 2017; Hwang et al., 2013; Rudnik et al., 2006). A reason might be due to the high percentage of cavernous sinus invasion in our series (50% of the patients (23/46) > Knosp 2). Complications were observed more frequently in non-functioning tumours, which may relate to higher tumour volume and more extensive growth into critical anatomical regions, making resection technically more demanding. However, we would like to point out that, from a purely technical perspective, the surgical procedure does not differ significantly between functioning and non-functioning cases.

Comparative analysis of knosp-grading ≥2

Our data show a broad distribution of recurrent tumours across Knosp grades. Postoperatively, a high proportion of residual tumour was observed in the parasellar compartment (e.g. Knosp 3: 10/12; Knosp 4: 11/11). Complete removal is most feasible in lower Knosp grades (primarily grade 2) due to less cavernous sinus invasion and clearer tumour boundaries (Do et al., 2017; Hwang et al., 2013; Micko et al., 2015; Micko, 2020). The likelihood of complete resection decreases with increasing Knosp grade, reflecting greater involvement of neurovascular structures (Palavani et al., 2026; Farrell et al., 2019; Tajudeen et al., 2014; Buchy, 2019; Micko et al., 2015; Micko, 2020). In our study, complete resection was not achieved in Knosp grade 4 tumours. Palavani et al. similarly reported that gross total resection in Knosp 3-4 tumours is technically difficult and successful only in a minority of cases (Palavani et al., 2026). Higher Knosp grading and residual tumour have been associated with increased risk of recurrence in prior studies with reported recurrence rates up to 40% (Chen, 2021; Chen et al., 2012; Dimopoulou, 2014; Hwang et al., 2013; Hayhurst et al., 2020). Knosp grade may also predict early postoperative remission in functioning PitNETs (Buchy, 2019). In our cohort, functioning RPAs were most common at Knosp grade 4, which is relevant when interpreting remission expectations. The overall complication rate was low, without a clear trend towards higher rates in higher Knosp grades, consistent with observations in primary PitNET series (Micko, 2020).

Comparative analysis of endoscopic visualisation

EETS can be advantageous in revision surgery because endoscopic visualisation may facilitate orientation in the presence of altered anatomy after prior interventions (P.R. Bates ant the UK National Acromegaly Register Study Group (UKAR‐2), 2008). In the present cohort, endoscopic techniques were used in an approximate 1:2 ratio in favour of three-dimensional endoscopy, indicating a preference for 3D-E in recurrent PitNET surgery. The selection of endoscopic technique was not random and was not based solely on anatomical prerequisites or on the desired image quality and depth perception. It was influenced by several factors, including the long study period (2009–2024), evolving availability of equipment, and routine clinical practice. Accordingly, selection bias cannot be excluded, and a multivariate analysis was not feasible given the cohort size and confounding variables. Larger tumours were more frequently operated on using 3D-E, consistent with the potential benefit of improved depth perception in complex anatomy (Loeschner, 2023; P.R. Bates ant the UK National Acromegaly Register Study Group (UKAR‐2), 2008; Kari et al., 2012; Ogino-Nishimura, 2015; Tabaee, 2009; Vasudevan et al., 2019). Parasellar extension was observed more often in tumours treated with 3D-E and suprasellar extent of resection appeared higher in this group. These observations suggest that 3D-E may be particularly useful in selected cases with larger volume and challenging localization; however, the present study does not allow conclusions regarding superiority of one visualisation modality. The heterogeneity of reconstruction materials for the dura and the sellar floor, potentially influenced by anatomical and intraoperative criteria, as well as the limitations related to sample size, preclude statistical evaluation. Visual outcomes were comparable between both groups. Most patients demonstrated stable visual acuity and visual field postoperatively; importantly, patients without preoperative impairment did not develop new visual limitations. This is consistent with previous reports describing favorable visual outcomes after EETS. The findings support that EETS is a minimally invasive and safe approach for recurrent pituitary adenomas, in line with other published series (Alahmadi, 2012, Benveniste, 2005; Cappabianca, 2000; Cavallo et al., 2013; Chang et al., 2008; Do et al., 2017; Esquenazi, 2017; Hwang et al., 2013; Rudnik et al., 2006). The comparison between pre- and postoperative anterior and posterior pituitary deficits showed similar results, suggesting that changes in pituitary function are related to the surgical intervention itself rather than the endoscopic visualisation modality. The rate of new endocrine deficits was relatively high compared to the literature, in which rates between 1% and 8% are reported (Tabaee et al., 2009; Cavallo et al., 2013; Do et al., 2017; Esquenazi, 2017; Hwang et al., 2013; Tajudeen et al., 2014; Yamada et al., 2010; d'Avella et al., 2026). Linsler et al. correlated failure of pituitary gland identification with postoperative hormonal insufficiency (Linsler S et al., 2017). The inadequate identification of the pituitary gland - due to the prior surgery - may have contributed to the rate of postoperative hormonal insufficiency. In this context, the removal of suprasellar tumour components also appears to predispose to the development of postoperative pituitary insufficiency. Standardized endocrinological follow-up examinations - including the classification of a “new endocrinological” deficit via laboratory diagnostics, even in the absence of clinical symptoms - as well as the lack of patient attrition during follow-up, may also have contributed to the impression of high rates of postoperative hormonal insufficiency in our study. Overall complication rates in this cohort were low, within ranges reported by comparative studies (approximately 1-22%) (Benveniste, 2005; Cavallo et al., 2013; Chang et al., 2008; Tajudeen et al., 2014; d'Avella et al., 2026). Complications occurred more frequently in the 3D-E group; however, the difference between groups was not significant, and event numbers were small. The most common postoperative complications were epistaxis (6-7%) and CSF leakage (7%). Comparable RPA studies have reported CSF leakage rates between 1.5% and 14.29% (Miller et al., 2025; Wagenmakers et al., 2009; Palavani et al., 2026; Zoli et al., 2025; Farrell et al., 2019; Loeschner, 2023; Cavallo et al., 2013; d'Avella et al., 2026). Intraoperative CSF leakage required management in 41–45% of cases in both endoscopy groups, independent of 2D or 3D technology, reflecting a known challenge of transsphenoidal surgery and typically necessitates careful reconstruction and postoperative surveillance. Similar values have been reported in primary PitNET series (Tabaee et al., 2009; Loeschner, 2023; Tabaee, 2009). To our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating the influence of endoscopic visualisation technology in EETS for recurrent pituitary adenoma. The 30-day mortality rate was zero, consistent with published series reporting very low mortality (Hajdari et al., 2018; Loeschner, 2023; Chang et al., 2008; Esquenazi, 2017; Hwang et al., 2013; Rudnik et al., 2006).

Limitation of this study

This retrospective, single-centre study includes a comparable number of patients to other published series (between 12 and 96 patients), but the overall cohort remains limited (Wagenmakers et al., 2009; Yu et al., 2000; Alahmadi, 2012, Benveniste, 2005; Cappabianca, 2000; Cavallo et al., 2013; Chang et al., 2008; Do et al., 2017; Rudnik et al., 2006; Tajudeen et al., 2014; Yamada et al., 2010; Cherebillo et al., 2024). Primary surgery for PitNET as well as endocrinological pre- and postoperative work-up were not consistently performed in-house. MRI evaluations may have varied between radiologists, which could affect the reliability and consistency of imaging-based assessments. In our study, 3D-E was employed more frequently, resulting in an unequal distribution of the endoscopy groups. Follow-up was limited to the early postoperative period (up to 12 weeks); therefore, long-term endpoints such as progression-free survival, late endocrinological outcomes and delayed complications could not be assessed.

Conclusion

Endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal surgery is a safe and low-morbidity approach for the management of recurrent pituitary neuroendocrine tumours. In our cohort, the feasibility of gross total resection was strongly influenced by tumour extension, particularly parasellar invasion, as reflected by higher Knosp grades. Notably, only subtotal resection was achievable for Knosp grade 4 tumours. Patients with parasellar and/or suprasellar extension should therefore be counselled preoperatively regarding the increased likelihood of residual tumour tissue. Functional recurrent tumours achieved endocrinological remission in approximately half of cases, supporting repeat EETS as a reasonable option in selected patients. Three-dimensional endoscopy was used more frequently in larger tumours with challenging localization and yielded outcomes comparable to two-dimensional endoscopy in terms of resection category, new endocrine deficits and complication profile. Given the short postoperative follow-up in this study (up to 12 weeks), no conclusions can be drawn regarding long-term tumour control or progression-free survival. Longer follow-up and, ideally, multicentre prospective data are required to define long-term outcomes and to further clarify whether endoscopic technology influences durability of tumour control. EETS remains technically complex and should be performed in specialised centres by teams with extensive experience, particularly in reoperative settings. The endoscopic approach can be advantageous for tumours with suprasellar and parasellar extension as well as for recurrent PitNETs.

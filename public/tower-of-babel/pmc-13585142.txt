Local carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil in women positive for human papillomavirus: a prospective observational study
Authors: Carmen Pingarrón Santofimia, Ysmael Álvarez Espinosa, Juan José Hernández Aguado, Ana Isabel López Castejón, Maria Soledad Martínez García, Carlos Blanco-Soler, Cristina del Valle Rubido, Maria del Pilar Robles Ruiz, Janet Abanto Alvarez, Maria del Carmen Chicharro Cassuso, Amira Alkourdi Martinez, Monica de Vera Gomis, Silvia Tamarit Bordes, Laura Rubert Torro, Caterina Cortés Alaguero, Irene Vela Sampedro, Manuel David Menor Almagro, Stefanie Redon Fitzl, Laura Lopez Prada, Montserrat Albarrán Gómez
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHigh-risk human papillomavirus (HR-HPV) infections with normal or low-grade cervical cytology are generally managed through observation because many infections clear spontaneously. However, persistent infection, particularly in women over 35 years of age, may increase the risk of progression and lead to prolonged surveillance, repeated examinations and patient anxiety. This study evaluates the effectiveness of local treatment with carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil (Colpofix®) in achieving negativization of high-risk (HR) human papillomavirus (HPV) in a Spanish population of women with HR-HPV positivity.MethodsThis prospective, non-randomized controlled, multi-centre study enrolled 533 women aged 25–65 with HR-HPV and normal cytology, atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance or low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions. Conducted in real clinical settings, it involved a 3-month treatment or ‘wait-and-see’ approach, with final follow-up at 6 months. Statistical analysis included various tests such as the Mann–Whitney test, Fisher’s exact test and Pearson’s χ2 test.ResultsAt 6 months, HR-HPV negativity was observed in 50.1% of treated patients compared with 24.0% in the observation group (p<0.0001). Higher clearance rates were recorded across all HR-HPV genotypes. For HPV16, negativization occurred in 50.7% of treated patients versus 9.2% of controls (p=0.0291). Resolution of cervical ectopy was more frequent in the treatment group (42.6% versus 11.5%, p<0.0001). No adverse events were reported.ConclusionsLocal treatment with carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil was associated with higher rates of HR-HPV negativization and ectopy resolution compared with observation alone at 6-month follow-up, supporting its use as a management option, particularly in women over 35 years of age.

Introduction

The widespread implementation of population-based human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination programmes has substantially altered the epidemiology of cervical HPV-associated disease.1 Although vaccination has led to a decline in high-grade cervical lesions amongst younger women, a growing proportion of HPV-related abnormalities is now observed in adult and peri-menopausal or post-menopausal women who were not vaccinated in adolescence or who acquired persistent infections later in life.2 In this population, high-risk HPV (HR-HPV) positivity is frequently detected in the absence of high-grade cytological abnormalities, with normal cytology, atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance (ASCUS) or low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions (LSIL/CIN1) being common findings.3

Current clinical guidelines generally recommend a conservative ‘wait-and-see’ approach for women with HR-HPV infection and negative or low-grade cytology,4 given the high likelihood of spontaneous viral clearance. However, this strategy is not without challenges. Persistent HR-HPV infection is associated with an increased long-term risk of progression, particularly in women over 35 years of age, in whom viral clearance is slower and immune responsiveness may be reduced.5,6 Moreover, prolonged surveillance often leads to repeated cytology, HPV testing and colposcopic examinations, contributing to patient anxiety, increased healthcare utilization and reduced adherence to follow-up.7,8

A particularly challenging clinical scenario involves peri-menopausal and post-menopausal women with persistent HR-HPV infection, negative to LSIL cytology and with a type 3 transformation zone, in which the squamocolumnar junction is not fully visible.3 In these cases, colposcopy may be technically difficult, uncomfortable for the patient and diagnostically limited, often leading to repeated examinations or diagnostic excisional procedures despite the absence of clear lesions.9 Strategies that could promote HPV clearance and improve cervical epithelial health in this population may therefore help reduce the frequency of invasive diagnostic interventions.10

In this context, non-invasive local therapies aimed at restoring cervico-vaginal mucosal integrity and supporting viral clearance have gained increasing interest.11 Vaginal gel sprays containing carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil form a muco-adhesive protective barrier on the cervico-vaginal epithelium, promote tissue hydration and contribute to epithelial repair.12–15 Beyond their mechanical and trophic effects, β-glucans have been shown to modulate local immune responses, which may play a role in facilitating HPV clearance.12–15 Previous clinical studies have suggested that this combination may be associated with improved HPV negativization rates and regression of low-grade cervical abnormalities in women with HPV-related disease.12–15

The present study aimed to evaluate, in a real-world clinical setting in Spain, the effectiveness of local therapy with carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil (Colpofix®) in women with HR-HPV infection and normal cytology, ASCUS or LSIL/CIN1. In addition to assessing HPV negative conversion, the study explored the impact of this treatment on cervical ectopy and low-grade cytological or colposcopic findings, with particular relevance for patient groups in whom prolonged surveillance and repeated colposcopy represent a significant quality-of-life burden.

Methods

Study design and participants

This multi-centre, prospective, non-randomized controlled study was conducted under real clinical practice conditions in women treated with an authorized medical device.

The inclusion criteria for the study were: women aged between 25 and 65 years, with histological cervical cytology results reported as normal, ASCUS, LSIL/CIN1, with a positive HR-HPV DNA PCR cervical test, who signed the informed consent. Exclusion criteria were cervical cytology indicating suspected invasive cervical cancer; recent pregnancy terminated within 6 weeks of the study commencement; clinically significant immunodeficiency-linked pathology; active or terminated immunosuppressive treatment within 6 months of study entry; undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding; total hysterectomy; or documented history of HPV cervical pathology. Furthermore, women who were recently receiving corticoid treatment (defined as within 2 weeks before study entry) or those who had undergone two or more cycles of corticosteroids at doses equal to or greater than 20 mg per day of prednisone (or equivalent) administered orally or parenterally for at least 1 week in the year before study entry were excluded. No concomitant oral medication was prescribed as part of the study. Participants were permitted to continue their usual treatment for chronic conditions and to use occasional symptomatic medications when clinically required. Recent or clinically relevant systemic corticosteroid use was an exclusion criterion.

The treatment group consisted of patients currently undergoing treatment (for at least 15 days before study initiation) with carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil gel spray (Colpofix®), applied vaginally once daily for a period of 3 months. The control group did not receive this treatment. To select participants for the treatment and control groups, participating gynaecologists consecutively recruited the first 20 patients attending a routine visit who met all selection criteria.

Colpofix® was designed for self-administration. All patients in the treatment group applied the product independently at home. At the time of prescription, gynaecologists provided standard verbal instructions on correct vaginal application during the routine clinical visit. In addition, each product package included a detailed instruction leaflet explaining the mode of administration.

This research was conducted in 21 private and public centres across different Spanish regions from 22 September 2022, to 22 November 2023. All participants were capable of reading and comprehending the patient information sheet and signed the informed consent form. Ethical approval for this study was obtained by the co-ordinating centre (CEIm Grupo Hospitalario Quirónsalud Calatunya 26 July 2022). The corresponding Clinical Research Ethics Committee approved the study in accordance with current legislation, Royal Decree 1591/2009 of October 16th, which regulates health products (BOE 268 of November 6th). This study was performed in accordance with the ethical standards laid down in the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. The protocol was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06199128).

Outcome measures and data collection

Data were collected anonymously from medical records and interviews during a single routine visit. Before the study began, physicians were given confidential credentials to access a web platform designed for data collection. After the recruitment visit, a gynaecologist at each study site entered clinical and socio-demographic data for each participant into an electronic notebook.

The socio-demographic data collected at the initial visit (day 0) included age, level of physical activity, smoking status and the number of sexual partners in the past year. The clinical data focused on medical history (such as HPV persistence, HPV vaccination, previous pregnancies and treatments) and diagnosis-related information (such as cytology, colposcopy, biopsy, histology and HPV testing results, ectopia and identified HPV genotypes). All the recruited women underwent a subsequent follow-up after 6 months (final visit) in which the following data were collected: cytology, colposcopy, biopsy, histology and HPV testing results, in addition to the presence of ectopia, HPV genotypes detected, vaccination schedule initiation and vaccine administered during the study.

The primary end point was the evaluation of the effectiveness of topical β-glucan and polycarbophil treatment on HPV infection negativization rate in patients with normal cytology, ASCUS or LSIL/CIN1 as determined by DNA PCR. Secondary end points included the normalization rate of abnormal cytology and ectopia, and the normalization rate of abnormal colposcopy.

The safety analysis was descriptive and consisted of the report of adverse events related to the treatment.

Statistical analyses

The qualitative variables were analysed using absolute frequencies and percentages with a 95% confidence interval (CI) for each category. Quantitative variables were displayed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) or 95% CI, as well as distribution statistics (minimum, maximum, median and quartiles). Box plots and dispersion graphs were employed to provide a visual summary of the numerical data, and histograms were utilized for displaying the categorical variables.

The statistical significance of differences between final (at 6 months) and basal visits was evaluated using the Student’s t-test for paired data and the McNemar test for quantitative and qualitative variables. Non-parametric tests were used when the data did not follow a normal distribution or when the assumptions of parametric tests were not met.

No imputation was conducted for the missing data. Statistical significance was defined as p<0.05 and all tests were two-sided.

Data were analysed using RStudio 4.3.2 (Posti; Boston, MA, USA).

Results

Clinical baseline data of participants

A total of 581 women were recruited for the study (Figure 1); 48 patients did not meet inclusion criteria (mainly because of a negative HR-HPV test or diagnosis of HSIL at cytology or CIN3 at biopsy or histology) and were excluded. Finally, 533 patients were included in the current analysis: 426 belonged in the treatment group and 107 in the control group.

The approximate ratio of patients included in the treatment and control groups was 4:1; for each patient in the control group, four were included in the active treatment group. Importantly, this ratio does not imply a direct influence on the results, as it does not inherently affect the comparison of outcomes between groups.

Baseline characteristics of the patients included in the study are given in Table 1. Briefly, the mean age (SD) was 37.7 (10.1) years, with a high prevalence of monogamy (92.2%), non-smoking (71.5%) and moderate physical activity (41.3%). The distribution of HPV genotypes amongst the participants is illustrated in Figure 2; 44.1% of patients had received HPV vaccination before or during the study, primarily with the 9-valent vaccine (63.3%), without prior history of pregnancy (54.2%). The majority had persistence of HPV infection between 0 and 3 years (83.6%). Significant statistical differences (p<0.05) were found between the treatment and control groups in terms of age (38.6 versus 34.5), with the treatment group being an average of 4.1 years older than the control group, as well as in physical activity (30.0% versus 34.6%) and number of previous pregnancies (49.1% versus 32.7%). No other difference in baseline characteristics was found between the two groups.

HPV negative conversion rate

In the treatment group, HPV prevalence decreased by 50.1% overall and by 58.3% partially from baseline. In the control group, there were reductions of 24.0% for complete conversion and 43.3% for partial conversion in HPV prevalence. Comparing both groups, a significant difference was observed in the rate of complete negative conversion of HPV infection at 6 months, with the treatment group showing more than double the conversion rate compared to the control group (p<0.0001) (Table 2 and Figure 3).

HPV16 negative conversion rate

Out of the group of 89 patients who were treated with Colpofix®, 50.7% tested negative for HPV16 after 6 months. In contrast, in the control group (n=22), the negative conversion rate of HPV16 reached only 9.2% after the same period. Based on these results, at 6 months, the negative conversion rate of HPV16 in the group treated with Colpofix® was five times that of the control group (p=0.0291) (Table 2 and Figure 4).

Findings according to smoking and HPV vaccination status

Sub-analyses of smokers and non-smokers resulted in HPV negative conversion rates of 18.8% and 24%, respectively. No statistically significant differences were observed in HPV negative conversion rates between smokers and non-smokers. However, non-smokers exhibited a 4.8% higher rate of HPV clearance, regardless of whether they were treated with carboxymethyl β-glucan (p=0.3634; Figure 5A). Additionally, no statistically significant differences were found in HPV negative conversion rates between HPV-vaccinated and HPV-unvaccinated patients (19.1% versus 26.0%), regardless of whether they were in the control or treatment group (p=0.1719) (Figure 5B). Similar results were observed in the sub-analyses of HPV16 negative conversion rates.

Normalization rate of abnormal cytology

Although 164 women had normal cytology at baseline (38.5%), this number increased significantly to 236 (72.6%) at the final visit (p<0.001). However, no significant differences were observed compared to the control group. Amongst the 130 women who were diagnosed with LSIL in the treatment group, 68.9% showed improved cytology to normal or ASCUS (Table 2).

Normalization rate of abnormal colposcopy

Within the treatment group, 48 participants out of 129 (44.2%) with a positive colposcopy experienced the disappearance of initial lesions after 6 months. Treatment and control groups were not comparable due to statistically significant differences at basal time point (Table 2).

Safety

No adverse events related to the use of Colpofix® were observed.

Discussion

This case-control study, the first conducted in real clinical practice in Spain, evaluated the effectiveness of carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil therapy in clearing HPV infection in over 500 women with HR-HPV positivity, including those with normal cytology, ASCUS and LSIL/CIN1. The combined therapy significantly increased the HPV negative conversion rate, with up to a 43% reduction in HPV prevalence after 6 months. The treatment group showed more than double the complete negative conversion rate compared to the control group (50.1% versus 24.0%). Amongst women positive for HPV16, the therapy led to a five-fold increase in complete negative conversion (50.7% versus 9.2%). Smokers benefited more from the therapy, with higher negative conversion rates than non-smokers. Additionally, cervical cytologic abnormalities, such as ectopia, improved in 15% of treated patients versus 7.2% in the control group. There were no significant differences in colposcopy, CIN histopathology or biopsy normalization rates between the groups, and no safety issues or side-effects were reported.

Overall, the findings of the present study align with those reported in previous research.12–14 The results demonstrate significant HPV negative conversion and cytology normalization rates following treatment with Colpofix®, consistent with earlier studies whilst also highlighting some differences. After 6 months, the treatment group showed a 50.1% total reduction in HPV prevalence and a 50.7% negative conversion rate for HPV16. These figures are notably higher than those reported by Lavitola et al., who found a 39.9% HPV test negativization.12 The control group in the present analysis exhibited a higher rate of spontaneous clearance (24.0%) compared to the control group of Lavitola et al. (16.5%), suggesting potential variability in patient populations or study design between the two trials.12

Laccetta et al. provided a 12-month follow-up period, showing an 83.5% normalization rate by the end of the trial.13 Although this study was limited to a 6-month observation period, the promising preliminary results suggest that a longer follow-up might reveal even higher normalization rates. Additionally, Laccetta et al. observed significant colposcopic lesion disappearance at both 6 and 12 months, with 43.4% and 55.4% resolution in treated patients, respectively.13 In contrast, the present study found no statistically significant difference in colposcopy normalization, potentially due to the shorter duration of follow-up.

Scardamaglia et al. reported cytology normalization rates for ASCUS and LSIL diagnoses that exceeded those in this study, with 100% normalization for ASCUS and 70% for LSIL after 6 months of treatment.14 These higher rates may be attributed to the 12-month follow-up period, which allowed for the assessment of long-term outcomes, a factor not captured in the shorter-term observation of the present study. Moreover, the smaller sample size of Scardamaglia et al. (n=30) could have introduced variability.14 Despite these differences, the overall trend in both studies indicates that carboxymethyl β-glucan is associated with the resolution of low-grade lesions.

Additional findings from the study are important to highlight. Notably, the women in the treatment group were significantly older than those in the control group, with a mean age of 38.6 versus 34.5 years. Age is a key factor directly associated with the persistence of HPV;16 however, it was not sufficient to mask the beneficial effects of the treatment. Furthermore, the study included sub-analyses based on tobacco use, a factor strongly linked to immune system impairment.17 The results indicated that smokers experienced greater benefits from Colpofix® therapy in terms of HPV negative conversion compared to non-smokers, a finding that suggests the therapy may effectively counteract some of the detrimental cervical effects associated with smoking.

Overall, the consistency across studies highlights the potential of Colpofix® as a treatment option for HPV-negative conversions, offering a promising non-invasive therapeutic approach that complements existing strategies, particularly for those with persistent HPV infections and low-grade cervical lesions (ASCUS, LSIL/CIN1). Currently, the guidelines of the World Health Organization (WHO) recommend a wait-and-see approach, particularly for women with low-grade lesions, as many of these lesions regress spontaneously.4 However, this approach presents challenges, including prolonged periods of patient anxiety and the potential for disease progression.18–21 This is where Colpofix® may provide meaningful improvements. In several studies, including the present one, Colpofix® has demonstrated significant HPV-negative conversion rates and notable improvements in cytological abnormalities over a 6-month or 12-month period.12–15 These results align with the objective of the WHO to reduce the burden of HPV-related diseases and mortality by detecting and treating precancerous lesions before they progress to cancer.22 Importantly, this non-invasive treatment option fits within the WHO focus on cost-effective, scalable solutions, especially in resource-constrained settings where invasive procedures, such as LEEP or conisation, may not be easily accessible or feasible.4 Moreover, the gel spray treatment is safe, well-tolerated and can be self-administered. This aspect is crucial, as it enables women to take a more active role in managing their health whilst also minimizing the need for frequent clinical visits, both of which may improve the quality of life for these patients.23 For women who are not eligible for vaccination (due to age or other factors) or who have persistent HPV infections despite vaccination, this treatment provides an intermediate therapeutic option between passive observation and invasive interventions. In our exploratory sub-analysis, HPV negative conversion did not differ significantly between vaccinated and unvaccinated women, irrespective of treatment allocation, though vaccination was not administered as a standardized intervention and the study was not designed to evaluate its interaction with Colpofix®. Nevertheless, evolving vaccination strategies and future evidence on their adjuvant use may modify this paradigm and could potentially support complementary approaches to HPV clearance, a possibility that should be specifically assessed in prospective studies.24

The study has some limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. The primary limitation is the lack of randomization with a placebo group, as we used a wait-and-see strategy for the control group to evaluate the effectiveness of carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil. This decision was made because, to date, no universally recognized gold standard for managing HPV-related histo-pathological lesions exists. Thus, we opted for a control method commonly used in clinical practice, reflecting real-world management of HPV lesions.

Nevertheless, despite the limitations of our work, our study presents several strengths, including a substantial patient population and the utilization of diagnostic tests (PCR) consistently conducted by the same operators, as well as a 6-month follow-up period. Moreover, enroling only patients who were to be reassessed 6 months after their initial visit may reduce the influence of exogenous environmental factors on the vaginal environment and rule out the possibility that the observed improvement in colposcopy images and histological findings could be attributed to the natural progression of HPV infection. Finally, concomitant vaginal infections and alterations in the vaginal microbiota were not systematically recorded or microbiologically assessed. Consequently, their potential influence on HPV persistence and clearance, as well as any effect of carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil on these conditions, could not be evaluated. Future studies incorporating vaginal pH, microbiological testing and microbiota characterization may help clarify the relationship between the local vaginal environment and treatment response.

Conclusion

The results indicated that, in a real-world clinical practice in Spain, local therapy with carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil gel spray was effective in achieving negative HPV test conversion and improving the ectopia pattern in women with a positive HR-HPV PCR cervical test, including those under follow-up for normal cytology, ASCUS or LSIL/CIN1, with no documented adverse events. Therefore, carboxymethyl β-glucan and polycarbophil gel spray, a self-managed treatment, presents a promising alternative to standard treatment for women with a positive HR-HPV test. Further prospective studies are needed to confirm the long-term effectiveness of this therapeutic approach.

Successful Management of Avabahuka (Frozen Shoulder) Using Ayurvedic Interventions: A Case Report
Authors: Ashvini D. Pardhekar, Abhilash Pardhekar, Manisha Davkare, Mayuri A. Deshpande
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAvabahuka, commonly known as frozen shoulder or adhesive capsulitis, is a condition characterized by pain and stiffness in the shoulder joint. It typically develops gradually and progresses through three stages: the freezing stage (painful stage), the frozen stage (adhesive stage), and the thawing stage (recovery stage). This condition affects approximately 2%–5% of the general population, with a higher prevalence among individuals aged 40–60 years. Frozen shoulder can significantly impact a person’s quality of life by limiting daily activities and causing persistent discomfort. Conventional treatments include physical therapy, corticosteroid injections, and, in severe cases, surgery, whereas Ayurvedic approaches offer alternative management strategies that may provide relief and improve shoulder function. In this article, we discuss a 62-year-old male patient who presented with complaints of pain and restricted movement in his left shoulder for two months. He was managed by Tab Pirant, Rasnasaptak Kwath, and Nisha Amalaki Vati for 1 month, along with Snehan using Bala Ashwagandha oil. This was followed by Nadi Swedana with Nirgundi Patra for 7 days. Patients experienced relief from pain, stiffness, and improved range of motion within 1 month of treatment.

PATIENT INFORMATION

A 62-year-old male patient presented with complaints of pain and restricted movement in his left shoulder. The pain began insidiously 2 months ago, initially presenting as mild discomfort in the neck. Over time, the pain extended to the left shoulder and elbow, becoming more severe and persistent. The patient reported no recent trauma to the affected shoulder. Upon initial examination, he exhibited classic symptoms of Avabahuka, including pain and stiffness in the shoulder joint.

DISCUSSION

This case highlights the effectiveness of Ayurvedic interventions in managing Avabahuka (frozen shoulder). The use of oral medications and external therapies helped reduce pain and improve shoulder mobility. Ayurveda targets both symptoms and overall balance in the body, potentially leading to comprehensive healing. Frozen shoulder involves inflammation and thickening of the shoulder joint’s connective tissue, often causing pain and stiffness. It can be triggered by injury, immobilization, or conditions such as diabetes and thyroid disorders. In this case, the patient’s history of diabetes and hypothyroidism contributed to developing a frozen shoulder.

Tablet Pirant contains Shunthi (Zingiber officinale), Garlic (Allium sativum), Rasna (Pluchea lanceolata), Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera), Marich (Piper nigrum), Pippali (Piper longum), Guggul (Comiphora mukul), Ajwain (Trachyspermum ammi), Kustha (Saussurea lappa), Vidarikanda (Pueraria tuberosa), Chitrakmoola (Plumbago zeylanica), Vidang (Embelia ribes), and Suranjan (Cholchicum luteum). Shunthi, Garlic, Guggul, and Rasna help reduce inflammation and pain. Ashwagandha and Pippali provide analgesic effects, improving mobility. Ajwain and Kustha relax muscles, while Marich and Chitrakmoola improve blood flow to aid healing. Additionally, the antioxidants in these herbs support joint health, and Guggul and Vidang may promote tissue repair. Overall, the combined herbal approach addresses frozen shoulder symptoms effectively.

Rasnasaptak Kwath is composed of seven ingredients: Rasna (Pluchea lanceolata), Gokshur (Tribulus terrestris), Erand (Ricinus communis), Devdaru (Cedrus deodara), Punarnava (Boerhavia diffusa), Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia), and Aragvadha (Cassia fistula). This formulation is exclusively described in the Sharangdhar Samhita. All these ingredients have Vatashamak, anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties, which help to manage the symptoms of Avbahuka.[3]

Snehana is performed using Bala Ashwagandha oil, which contains the following ingredients: Bala (Sida cordifolia), Ashwagandha, Manjishtha (Rubia cordifolia), Durva (Cynodon dactylon), Ushir (Vetiveria zizanoides), Anantamul (Hemidesmus indicus), Laksha (Laccifer lacca), Kushtha, Til (Sesamum indicum), Padmak (Prunus cerasoides), and Aguru (Aquilaria agallocha). Bala is a nerve-enhancing herb that promotes muscle health.[4] Ashwagandha rejuvenates joint strength, nerves, and muscles.[5] Manjistha supports muscle health by fortifying muscle fibers. Durva promotes a regular sleep cycle, while Ushir relaxes the mind. Anantamul strengthens bones, and Laksha enhances overall strength. Chandan also provides relaxation. Rasna boosts Rasa Dhatu, and Kushta reduces Vata Dosha. Til improves strength and decreases Vata, Padmak treats Vata Dosha, and Aguru aids digestion.

Swedana is done using Nirgundi, which has Vatashamaka properties. Swedana involves processes that alleviate Stambha (stiffness) and Gaurava (heaviness), helping to improve circulation, reduce stiffness, and promote healing in the affected joint.[6]

Nisha Amalaki Vati contains Curcuma longa and Emblica officinalis. These ingredients have properties that aid in digestion and metabolism and promote healthy elimination (Anuloman). They help address blockages in the channels of the body and support glucose metabolism. Due to its drying quality (Ruksha Guna), this formulation effectively reduces excess moisture (Kleda Shoshana), which is beneficial in managing type 2 diabetes (Prameha) and its complications.

CONCLUSION

This case report suggests that Ayurvedic interventions may effectively manage Avabahuka, commonly known as frozen shoulder. The treatments lead to improvements in pain relief, stiffness reduction, and increased range of motion. A multifaceted approach that combines oral medications with external therapies appears to address various aspects of the condition. Further research, including randomized controlled trials, is needed to validate these findings and explore the potential integration of Ayurvedic treatments into the mainstream management of frozen shoulder.

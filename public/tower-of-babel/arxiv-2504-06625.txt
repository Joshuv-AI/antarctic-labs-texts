Decoupling spin relaxation and chemical kinetics in radical pair magnetism: A master equation study
Vladimir N. Binhi

Abstract
A leading theory for the biological effects of low-intensity magnetic fields is the spin-chemical Radical Pair Mechanism. This mechanism is best described by the master equation for the density matrix of an open quantum system, which includes terms for Hamiltonian evolution, thermal spin relaxation, and chemical kinetics. In this study, we have found a solution to this equation for a simplified radical pair model and shown that a significant magnetic effect occurs when the following condition is met: γ ​ H ​ τ ≳ 1 + κ ​ τ \gamma H\tau\gtrsim 1+\kappa\tau , where γ \gamma is the gyromagnetic ratio of the electron, H H is the magnetic field strength, τ \tau is the relaxation time, and κ \kappa is the rate of chemical kinetics. Keywords : magnetic field effects in biology, Radical Pair Mechanism, spin chemistry, open quantum system, spin decoherence

[

describes an intermediate complex formed in certain chemical reactions. This complex consists of a spin-correlated radical pair
\dot{\textrm{A}}\dot{\textrm{B}}
, existing in a quantum superposition of singlet
(\dot{\textrm{A}}\dot{\textrm{B}})^{\textrm{S}}
and triplet
(\dot{\textrm{A}}\dot{\textrm{B}})^{\textrm{T}}

Consequently, the product yields of these spin-selective reactions become MF-dependent. This MF dependence underpins the RPM role in spin chemistry and biological magneto-responsiveness.

In the absence of chemical kinetics and with some idealizations formulated below, the equation for a spin system in a dimensionless form is

\partial_{t}\,\rho=-i[{\hat{H}},\rho]-g(\rho-\rho_{\infty})

(1)

where
\rho
is the density matrix,
\partial_{t}
is the time derivative operator,
\hat{H}
is the Hamiltonian,
g
is the thermal relaxation rate due to interaction with a thermostat, and
[\,,]
denotes the commutator. We assume that thermal fluctuations are much faster than the spin dynamics. Therefore, it is reasonable to approximate the action of the thermal bath as a series of short pulses, between which the system evolves undisturbed. Then eq. (
1
) is a special case of the Lindblad equation for an open quantum system, with the action of the bath described by phenomenological dissipator
-g(\rho-\rho_{\infty})

[
6
, p. 64]
. In a weak MF, the Zeeman splitting is much smaller than
k_{\textrm{B}}T
, so
\rho_{\infty}
is the density matrix of a fully mixed state.

The chemical kinetics in the RPM is typically modeled using an anticommutator
-\frac{1}{2}\left\{kp+k^{\prime}P^{\prime},\rho\right\}
, where
k
and
k^{\prime}
are the rate constants for the singlet and triplet reaction channels, and
P
and
P^{\prime}
are projection operators onto the singlet and triplet subspaces, respectively
[
7
,
8
]
.

The evolution of
\rho
according to eq. (
1
) preserves the unit trace of the density matrix. However, if chemical kinetics takes place, the trace is no longer preserved: it tends to zero
[
7
]
. At any given time, the density matrix does not tend toward the equilibrium state
\rho_{\infty}
but rather toward a reduced matrix,
\mbox{tr}(\rho)\rho_{\infty}
. To account for this, we include dissipator
-g[\rho-\mbox{tr}(\rho)\rho_{\infty}]
, where the reduced density matrix replaces the equilibrium one. Thus, the combined effect of thermal relaxation and chemical kinetics on the RPM process is governed by the master equation

\partial_{t}\,\rho=-i\left[{\hat{H}},\rho\right]-\frac{1}{2}\left\{kP+k^{\prime}P^{\prime},\rho\right\}-g\left[\rho-\mbox{tr}(\rho)\rho_{\infty}\right]

(2)

If chemical kinetics is absent, that is,
k=k^{\prime}=0
, then
\mbox{tr}(\rho)=1
, and the equation reduces to (
1
). A common approximation, used in
[
9
,
10

k^{\prime}=k\neq 0
, under which the sum of the singlet and triplet projectors equals the identity operator. In this case, the chemical term in eq. (
2

-k\rho
, leading to an exponential decay of the density matrix trace. It can be shown, that the solution to eq. (
2
) under this approximation is

\rho=(U\rho_{0}U^{\dagger}-\rho_{\infty})\,{\textrm{e}}^{-(g+k)t}+\rho_{\infty}{\textrm{e}}^{-kt}

(3)

where
\rho_{0}
is the density matrix of the initial state, typically an electronic singlet, fully mixed in the nuclear spin states,
\rho_{0}=P/\mbox{tr}(P)
, and
U(t)\equiv\exp(-i{\hat{H}}t)
.

{\hat{H}}\equiv-h\left({S}^{1}_{z}+{S}^{2}_{z}\right)+a\textbf{IS}^{1}

Under the influence of MF together with chemical kinetics and spin relaxation, the ratio of the singlet
\rho_{\textrm{s}}

\mbox{tr}(PU\rho_{0}U^{\dagger})

\mbox{tr}(P\rho_{\infty})=1/4

\rho_{\textrm{s}}(t,h,k,g)\equiv\mbox{tr}(P\rho)=[F(t,h)-1/4]{\textrm{e}}^{-(k+g)t}+{\textrm{e}}^{-kt}/4

The magnetic effect is conventionally characterized by the time-averaged value of

In conclusion, an analytical solution (
3
) to the master equation (
2
) has been found above for a system consisting of two electrons and a nucleus, with the Zeeman and hyperfine interactions, taking into account quantum coherence relaxation and chemical kinetics for the case of equal rates in the singlet and triplet channels. It has been shown that the magnetic RPM effects can only be noticeable when the fundamental inequality (
7

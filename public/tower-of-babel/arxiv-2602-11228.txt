Rotation catalyzed chiral magnetovortical instability
Shuai Wang, Xu-Guang Huang

Abstract
We demonstrate that a background rotation significantly catalyzes the chiral magnetovortical instability in chiral magnetohydrodynamics. The rotation splits the linearly polarized Alfvén wave into two circularly polarized magneto-Coriolis waves, one of which exhibits a lower frequency than the original Alfvén wave. We find that this low-frequency magneto-Coriolis wave is always unstable in the presence of even a weak chiral vortical effect. This instability may enable new dynamo mechanism applicable to various rotating chiral plasmas.



I
Introduction

Chiral matter exhibits a variety of intriguing anomalous transport phenomena that originate from the chiral anomaly and are absent in normal matter. Prominent examples of such phenomena include the chiral magnetic effect (CME)
Vilenkin 1980
;
Kharzeev et al. 2008
;
Fukushima et al. 2008
and the chiral vortical effect (CVE)
Vilenkin 1979
;
Erdmenger et al. 2009
;
Banerjee et al. 2011
;
Son and Surowka 2009
, which are electric currents along the magnetic and vortical fields, respectively. These novel effects have been discussed in a broad range of contexts, including the quark-gluon plasma (QGP) in heavy-ion collisions, the early Universe, supernovae and neutron stars, and Weyl/Dirac semimetals; see Refs.
Huang 2016
;
Liu and Huang 2020
;
Kharzeev and Liao 2021
;
Chernodub et al. 2022
for reviews.

From a theoretical perspective, the low-energy behavior of chiral matter can be described by chiral (anomalous) hydrodynamics or chiral magnetohydrodynamics (MHD)
Hattori et al. 2022
, in which the anomalous electric currents from the CME and CVE are encoded. A number of novel wave modes have been identified in chiral MHD, such as the chiral magnetic wave
Kharzeev and Yee 2011
, chiral vortical wave
Jiang et al. 2015
, chiral electric wave
Huang and Liao 2013
, chiral Alfvén wave
Yamamoto 2015
, chiral heat wave
Chernodub 2016
, and chiral shock wave
Sen and Yamamoto 2017
. Moreover, some of these wave modes can become unstable under certain conditions. For instance, the CME can induce an instability known as the chiral plasma instability or chiral dynamo instability
Joyce and Shaposhnikov 1997
;
Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013
, while the CVE can trigger an MHD instability called the chiral magnetovortical instability (CMVI)
Wang and Huang 2024
, which requires consideration of the mutual evolution of the magnetic and vortical fields. Additionally, pure CVE-induced instability has also been recently discussed
Wang et al. 2025
. Furthermore, these instabilities may provide new dynamo mechanisms that can operate in various physical environments, such as Weyl semimetals
Galitski et al. 2018
;
Qiu et al. 2017
;
Amitani and Nishida 2023
;
Nishida 2023
, the early Universe
Joyce and Shaposhnikov 1997
;
Giovannini and Shaposhnikov 1998
;
Boyarsky et al. 2012a
;
Boyarsky et al. 2012b
;
Tashiro et al. 2012
;
Dvornikov and Semikoz 2017
;
Gorbar et al. 2016
;
Brandenburg et al. 2017
;
Schober et al. 2018
, supernovae and neutron stars
Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013
;
Schober et al. 2018
;
Ohnishi and Yamamoto 2014
;
Yamamoto 2016
;
Grabowska et al. 2015
;
Dvornikov and Semikoz 2015
;
Sigl and Leite 2016
;
Matsumoto et al. 2022
;
Schober et al. 2022
;
Brandenburg et al. 2023
;
Dehman and Pons 2024
;
Dehman and Pons 2025
, and heavy-ion collisions
Akamatsu et al. 2016
;
Hirono et al. 2015
;
Xia et al. 2016
;
Tuchin 2018
;
Schlichting and Sharma 2022
.

As mentioned above, the CVE plays a vital role in inducing novel instabilities, highlighting the importance of the vortical field. The simplest vortical field is a global rotation, which has been extensively explored in conventional hydrodynamics
Landau and Lifshitz 1959
;
Davidson 2016
. This is primarily because rotation is an extremely common feature in many systems. Typically, it is convenient to consider a rotating fluid in a co-rotating frame (a non-inertial frame), where the Coriolis and centrifugal forces emerge. For example, in a rotating fluid, the Coriolis force acts as a restoring force, supporting the so-called inertial waves
Landau and Lifshitz 1959
. These waves propagate with a direction-dependent frequency and are damped by viscous effects. In the context of MHD, a well-known wave is the Alfvén wave
Davidson 2016
, which is excited by magnetic tension and is damped by viscous and resistive effects. When considering rotating MHD, inertial waves couple with Alfvén waves to form magneto-Coriolis (MC) waves
Lehnert 1954
;
Lehnert 1955
;
Acheson and Hide 1973
;
Nornberg et al. 2010
;
Rax et al. 2023
. These coupled waves can be understood as the splitting of linearly polarized Alfvén waves into circularly polarized waves by the rotation
Nornberg et al. 2010
;
Lehnert 1954
;
Lehnert 1955
;
Davidson 2013
, which play an important role in astrophysics.

Regarding instabilities in rotating matter, a renowned example is the magnetorotational instability (MRI), which plays an essential role in understanding the transport of angular momentum in astronomical accretion disks
Balbus and Hawley 1998
;
Wang et al. 2022
. Although in this case the fluid is subject to differential rotation rather than rigid rotation, it is clear that both the magnetic field and the rotation (whether rigid or differential) play crucial roles in shaping the evolution of MHD systems. Recently, a rotational instability of the magnetic field has been discussed
Das and Tuchin 2025
.

In this paper, we investigate the impact of global rotation on the CMVI. We demonstrate that rotation results in a hybridization of chiral Alfvén waves with conventional inertial and Alfvén waves, thereby inducing a novel type of CMVI. Unlike the CMVI identified in Ref.
Wang and Huang 2024
for non-rotating plasmas, where the onset condition is given by
\xi_{\omega}>\sqrt{\rho}
(with
\xi_{\omega}
being the CVE coefficient and
\rho
the mass density), in the presence of global rotation, the CMVI can be significantly catalyzed by the Coriolis force. We adhere to the notations used in Ref.
Wang and Huang 2024
.

II
MHD in a rotating frame

We begin by reviewing the rotational effects in conventional MHD. We consider a conducting fluid rotating with constant angular velocity
{\bm{\Omega}}
. We work in the co-rotating reference frame (a non-inertial reference frame). The governing non-relativistic MHD equations are given by
Acheson and Hide 1973
;
Davidson 2016
(see Appendix
A
for a derivation)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{v}}+({\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}){\bm{v}}

\displaystyle+2\bm{\Omega}\times{\bm{v}}=-\bm{\nabla}P+\frac{1}{\rho}({\bm{B}}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}){\bm{B}},

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{B}}={\bm{\nabla}}\times({\bm{v}}\times{\bm{B}})+\eta{\bm{\nabla}}^{2}{\bm{B}},

(2)

where
\rho
is the mass density,
{\bm{v}}(t,{\bm{r}})
is the velocity field (
{\bm{r}}
is the position vector from the rotating axis),
{\bm{B}}(t,{\bm{r}})
is the magnetic field,
P=p/\rho-|{\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{r}}|^{2}/2+{\bm{B}}^{2}/2\rho

p

\eta
is the electric resistivity,
-2{\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{v}}
is the Coriolis force, and
-{\bm{\nabla}}(|{\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{r}}|^{2}/2)={\bm{\Omega}}\times({\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{r}})
is the centrifugal force. We have assumed
\rho
to be a constant which implies the incompressiblity of the fluid,

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{v}}=0.

(3)

Incompressibility is not crucial for our discussions and the results below can be readily extended to compressible case. Without loss of generality, the background rotating field
{\bm{\Omega}}
is set to be along the
z

{\bm{\Omega}}=\Omega\hat{\bm{z}}
with
\Omega>0
. In Eq. (
1

2

A

Equations (
1

2
) allow an equilibrium state with constant magnetic field
{\bm{B}}_{0}
and vanishing velocity. This is achieved when the thermodynamic pressure gradient balances the centrifugal force. Let us consider small perturbations around this equilibrium state and examine how the perturbations evolve. We decompose the magnetic field into two parts
{\bm{B}}={\bm{B}}_{0}+{\bm{b}}
and the velocity field into
{\bm{v}}=\bm{0}+{\bm{v}}
, where
{\bm{b}},{\bm{v}}
are treated as small quantities with
|{\bm{b}}|/|{\bm{B}}_{0}|,|{\bm{v}}|\sim\delta\ll 1
. We assume
{\bm{B}}_{0}=B_{0}\hat{\bm{z}}
with
B_{0}>0
which is aligned with the direction of
{\bm{\Omega}}
.
Linearizing Eqs. (
1
)-(
2
) to first order in
\delta
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=-2\bm{\Omega}\times{\bm{v}}-\bm{\nabla}P+\frac{({\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}){\bm{b}}}{\rho},

(4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{b}}

\displaystyle=\eta{\bm{\nabla}}^{2}{\bm{b}}+({\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}){\bm{v}},

(5)

where the imcompressibility condition (
3
) is used.
To determine the eigenmodes, we substitute the plane-wave ansatz for the fluctuations,
{\bm{b}},{\bm{v}}=\bm{f}_{b,v}e^{i({\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}-\omega t)}
where
\bm{f}_{b,v}
are amplitude vectors, and apply curl to Eq. (
4
). This yields

\displaystyle\omega{\bm{k}}\times\bm{f}_{v}-2i({\bm{\Omega}}\cdot{\bm{k}})\bm{f}_{v}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\rho}({\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{k}}){\bm{k}}\times\bm{f}_{b},

(6)

\displaystyle(-i\omega+\eta{\bm{k}}^{2})\bm{f}_{b}

\displaystyle=i({\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{k}})\bm{f}_{v},

(7)

by virtue of
{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{v}}={\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{b}}=0
. The dispersion relations are obtained from solving

\displaystyle\omega^{2}+(i\eta{\bm{k}}^{2}-\lambda\frac{2{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot{\bm{k}}}{k})\omega-i\lambda\eta{\bm{k}}^{2}\frac{2{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot{\bm{k}}}{k}-\frac{({\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{k}})^{2}}{\rho}=0,

(8)

where
\lambda=\pm 1
and
k\equiv|{\bm{k}}|
. For brevity, we define

\displaystyle\omega_{I}\equiv 2\frac{{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot{\bm{k}}}{k},\quad\omega_{A}\equiv\frac{{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{k}}}{\sqrt{\rho}}.

(9)

They are frequencies of pure inertial wave and Alfvén wave, respectively. The solutions to Eq. (
8
) are then given by

\omega=-i\frac{\gamma_{\eta}}{2}+\lambda\frac{\omega_{I}}{2}\pm\sqrt{(\frac{i\gamma_{\eta}}{2}+\lambda\frac{\omega_{I}}{2})^{2}+\omega_{A}^{2}},

(10)

with
\gamma_{\eta}\equiv\eta{\bm{k}}^{2}
the magnetic diffusion rate. Equation (
10
) reveals that Alfvén waves and inertial waves are coupled in a nonlinear manner, and these coupled waves are damped by electric resistivity
\eta
, which typically prevents the occurrence of instability.

For ideal MHD,
\gamma_{\eta}=0
, the frequency (
10
) becomes
\omega=\pm\omega_{f,s}
, with

\displaystyle\omega_{f}

\displaystyle=\omega_{I}/2+(\omega_{I}^{2}/4+\omega_{A}^{2})^{1/2},

(11)

\displaystyle\omega_{s}

\displaystyle=-\omega_{I}/2+(\omega_{I}^{2}/4+\omega_{A}^{2})^{1/2},

(12)

indicating that the rotation splits the usal Alfvén wave of frequency
\omega_{A}
into two distinct waves: a fast one with frequency
\omega_{f}
(for
\omega_{I}>0
) and a slow one with frequency

Lehnert 1954
;
Lehnert 1955
;
Acheson and Hide 1973
;
Rax et al. 2023
. We refer to these as the magneto-Coriolis (MC) waves, as the restoring force is the combination of Lorentz force (more precisely, magnetic tension induced by Lorentz force) and Coriolis forces. For fast MC wave, the Coriolis force adds to the Lorentz force, leading to a larger frequency
\omega_{f}>\omega_{A}
, while for the slow MC wave, the Coriolis force acts opposite to the Lorentz force, resulting a smaller frequency
\omega_{s}<\omega_{A}
. Note that
\omega_{f}\omega_{s}=\omega_{A}^{2}
. Unlike the usual Alfvén wave at zero rotation, which is linearly polarized, the MC waves are circularly polarized. For weak rotation, where
\omega_{I}\ll\omega_{A}
, we have
\omega_{f}\approx\omega_{A}+\omega_{I}/2
and
\omega_{s}\approx\omega_{A}-\omega_{I}/2
, showing that the MC waves are predominantly Alfvén waves with a small inertial wave component. For strong rotation, where
\omega_{I}\gg\omega_{A}
, we have
\omega_{f}\approx\omega_{I}
, representing the the inertial wave, and
\omega_{s}\approx\omega_{A}^{2}/\omega_{I}\ll\omega_{A}
, which is very slow and sometimes termed the magnetostrophic wave
Davidson 2013
;
Bardsley and Davidson 2016
;
Sreenivasan and Narasimhan 2017
.

Finally, let us clarify the physical meaning of
\lambda=\pm
. Set the helicity basis with
\bm{e}_{3}({\bm{k}})=\hat{{\bm{k}}}={\bm{k}}/k
(for
k>0
) along the wave vector direction, and the right-hand and left-hand helicity basis vectors
\bm{e}_{\pm}({\bm{k}})
, which satisfy
\hat{{\bm{k}}}\times\bm{e}_{\pm}({\bm{k}})=\mp i\bm{e}_{\pm}({\bm{k}}),\hat{{\bm{k}}}\cdot\bm{e}_{\pm}({\bm{k}})=0,\bm{e}_{\pm}({\bm{k}})\cdot\bm{e}_{\pm}({\bm{k}})^{*}=1
, and
\bm{e}_{\pm}({\bm{k}})\cdot\bm{e}_{\mp}({\bm{k}})^{*}=0
. Due to the conditions
{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{v}}=0={\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{b}}

\bm{f}_{v}
and
\bm{f}_{b}

\bm{f}_{v,b}=f_{v,b+}\bm{e}_{+}+f_{v,b-}\bm{e}_{-}
. Then Eqs. (
6
)-(
7
) simplify to

\displaystyle\omega f_{v\pm}\pm\omega_{I}f_{v\pm}+\omega_{A}f_{b\pm}/\sqrt{\rho}=0,

(13)

\displaystyle(\omega+i\gamma_{\eta})f_{b\pm}/\sqrt{\rho}+\omega_{A}f_{v\pm}=0.

(14)

From this, we obtain the following equation for
\omega
,

\displaystyle\omega^{2}+(i\gamma_{\eta}\pm\omega_{I})-\omega_{A}^{2}\pm i\gamma_{\eta}\omega_{I}=0.

(15)

Comparing to Eq. (
8

\lambda=+1

III
Chiral MHD in a Rotating Frame

Chiral MHD is dramatically different from the conventional MHD due to the chiral anomalous effects, such as the CME and CVE, in the electric current

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}_{B}=\xi_{B}{\bm{B}},\quad{\bm{j}}_{\omega}=\xi_{\omega}({\bm{\omega}}+2{\bm{\Omega}}),

(16)

where
\xi_{B,\omega}\propto\mu_{5}
are the CME and CVE conductivities with
\mu_{5}
the chiral chemical potential (without loss of generality, we will henceforth assume
\xi_{B,\omega}>0
in all subsequent discussions), and
{\bm{\omega}}\equiv{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{v}}
is the vorticity. Note that there is an additional
2{\bm{\Omega}}
contribution to the CVE ; see Appendix
A
for a derivation. We emphasize again that physical quantities are defined in the rotating frame. In order to simplify the notation, we will frequenctly use the following dimensionless quantities,

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}_{\omega}\equiv\frac{\xi_{\omega}}{\sqrt{\rho}},\,{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{0}\equiv\frac{{\bm{B}}_{0}}{\sqrt{\rho}},\,{\bm{b}}^{\prime}\equiv\frac{{\bm{b}}}{\sqrt{\rho}},\,\bm{f}^{\prime}_{b}\equiv\frac{\bm{f}_{b}}{\sqrt{\rho}}.

(17)

For the purpose of our analysis, we focus on the non-relativistic limit, as it offers a more transparent understanding of the origin of instabilities. A similar analysis can be performed for the relativistic case as well. The governing equations are Eq. (
1
) and (see Appendix
A
for a derivation)

\partial_{t}{\bm{B}}=\eta{\bm{\nabla}}^{2}{\bm{B}}+{\bm{\nabla}}\times({\bm{v}}\times{\bm{B}})+\eta\xi_{B}{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{B}}+\eta\xi_{\omega}{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{\omega}}.

(18)

Although the form of Eq. (
18
) is the same as Eq. (3) in Ref.
Wang and Huang 2024
, the quantities in Eq. (
18
) are defined in the rotating frame. Making a linear analysis for Eq. (
18
) similar to Sec.
II
, one obtains

\partial_{t}{\bm{b}}=\eta{\bm{\nabla}}^{2}{\bm{b}}+({\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}){\bm{v}}+\eta\xi_{B}{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{b}}-\eta\xi_{\omega}{\bm{\nabla}}^{2}{\bm{v}}.

(19)

After substituting the plane wave ansatz into Eq. (
19
), it transforms into an algebraic form

\displaystyle(\eta{\bm{k}}^{2}-i\omega)\bm{f}_{b}-i\eta\xi_{B}{\bm{k}}\times\bm{f}_{b}=[i{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{k}}+\eta\xi_{\omega}{\bm{k}}^{2}]\bm{f}_{v}.

(20)

Finally, combining Eqs. (
6
) and (
20
), we find that the dispersion relations for the eigenmodes are determined by

\displaystyle\omega^{2}+[i\eta k(k+\lambda\xi_{B})-\lambda\omega_{I}]\omega

\displaystyle\quad-i\eta k(\xi_{B}+\lambda k)\omega_{I}-\omega_{A}^{2}+i\eta k^{2}\omega_{A}\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}=0,

(21)

where
\lambda=\pm 1
. Before delving into the solutions of Eq. (
III
), let us makes a few remarks about it.

(1) The last term in Eq. (
III
) corresponds to the contribution from the so-called chiral Alfvén wave
Yamamoto 2015
. The frequency of a pure chiral Alfvén wave is

\displaystyle\omega_{CA}\equiv\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}\omega_{A}.

(22)

This is most easily seen by turning off the CME and considering the large
\eta
limit. In this case, the solution is
\omega=\lambda\omega_{I}-\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}\omega_{A}
, representing a linear combination of chiral Alfvén wave and inertial wave.

(2) When
{\bm{\Omega}}=0
, Eq. (
III
) reduces to the situation studied in Ref.
Wang and Huang 2024
. In this case, it was known that a chiral magnetovortical instability (CMVI) emerges when
\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}>1

Wang and Huang 2024
. To understand this, we turn off the CME and rotation and rewrite Eq. (
III
) as

\displaystyle(\omega-\omega_{A})(\omega+\omega_{A})+i\gamma_{\eta}(\omega+\omega_{CA})=0.

(23)

The sign of the last term on the left-hand side determines whether instability can arise. When
|\omega_{CA}|\leq|\omega_{A}|
, the solutions correspond to damped Alfvén waves modified by the CVE. Unstable modes appear only when
|\omega_{CA}|>|\omega_{A}|
, which are the CMVI. Physically, this is because in this case the CVE in Eq. (
19
) (the last term on the right-hand side) can overwhelm the magnetic diffusion (the first term on the right-hand side) and lead to instability. In the presence of rotation
{\bm{\Omega}}
, Alfvén wave is split into two MC waves with frequencies
\omega_{f}
and
\omega_{s}
. We thus expect that the fast MC wave would become unstable when
|\omega_{CA}|>|\omega_{f}|
and the slow MC wave would become unstable when
|\omega_{CA}|>|\omega_{s}|
. Since
|\omega_{s}|<|\omega_{A}|
, we expect that the CMVI is more likely to occur in the rotating case. In the next section, we will show that this is indeed the case.

IV
CME induced instability

Considering the CME first (
\xi_{B}\neq 0,\xi_{\omega}=0
), Eq. (
III
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\omega^{2}+[i\eta k(k+\lambda\xi_{B})-\lambda\omega_{I}]\omega-i\eta k(\xi_{B}+\lambda k)\omega_{I}-\omega_{A}^{2}=0.

(24)

When
{\bm{\Omega}}=\bm{0}
, it is well known that CME can induce an instability termed chiral plasma instability (CPI) or chiral dynamo instability, which has been extensively studied in the literature
Joyce and Shaposhnikov 1997
;
Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013
;
Hattori et al. 2019
. This instability occurs for the right-hand waves (
\lambda=-1
) at scales
k<\xi_{B}
. At finite rotation, the solution to Eq. (
24
) is

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}[i\eta k(k+\lambda\xi_{B})-\lambda\omega_{I}]

\displaystyle\quad\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{[i\eta k(k+\lambda\xi_{B})+\lambda\omega_{I}]^{2}+4\omega_{A}^{2}}.

(25)

When
\eta=0
, Eq. (
25
) reduces to the ideal MHD case. However, even when
\eta\neq 0
, for right-hand mode (
\lambda=-1
), at
k=\xi_{B}
, Eq. (
25
) still yields an ideal-fluid form. This indicates that at this scale, the CME effectively balances the magnetic diffusion. Furthermore, it can be shown (see Appendix
B
) that for
k>\xi_{B}
, Eq. (
25
) represents only damping waves. For
k<\xi_{B}
,
\lambda=-1
, the imaginary part of
\omega
is positive and an instability emerges. Therefore, the presence of a finite rotation does not alter the unstable condition
\lambda=-1,k<\xi_{B}
for CPI.

V
CVE induced instability

Let us now turn to the dispersion relation when CVE is present (
\xi_{B}=0,\xi_{\omega}\neq 0
). From Eq. (
III
), we have

\omega^{2}+(i\gamma_{\eta}-\lambda\omega_{I})\omega-\omega_{A}^{2}+i\gamma_{\eta}(\omega_{CA}-\lambda\omega_{I})=0,

(26)

where
\lambda=+1(-1)
corresponds to left-hand (right-hand) modes. Denoting
m=-\gamma_{\eta}^{2}+\omega_{I}^{2}+4\omega_{A}^{2}
and
n=2\gamma_{\eta}(\lambda\omega_{I}-2\omega_{CA})
, the solution to Eq. (
26
) can be expressed by

\displaystyle\omega=\omega^{\lambda}_{\chi}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\big[-i\gamma_{\eta}+\lambda\omega_{I}+\chi\sqrt{m+i\,n}\big],

(27)

where
\chi=\pm 1
. We can directly obtain the real and imaginary parts of
\omega^{\lambda}_{\chi}
using the formula

\sqrt{m+i\,n}=\frac{\sqrt{m+\sqrt{m^{2}+n^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2}}+i\,\frac{n}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+\sqrt{m^{2}+n^{2}}}}.

(28)

The results are

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(\omega^{\lambda}_{\chi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\lambda\omega_{I}+\chi\frac{\sqrt{m+\sqrt{m^{2}+n^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right],

(29)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(\omega^{\lambda}_{\chi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[-\gamma_{\eta}+\chi\frac{n}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+\sqrt{m^{2}+n^{2}}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{\eta}}{2}\left[-1+\chi\frac{\sqrt{2}(\lambda\omega_{I}-2\omega_{CA})}{\sqrt{m+\sqrt{m^{2}+n^{2}}}}\right].

(30)

We focus on
\mathrm{Im}(\omega^{\lambda}_{\chi})
. When
\mathrm{Im}(\omega^{\lambda}_{\chi})>0
, the corresponding mode is unstable. Denoting the angle between
{\bm{B}}_{0}
(and
{\bm{\Omega}}
) and
{\bm{k}}
by
\theta
, we have
\omega_{A}=B^{\prime}_{0}k\cos\theta
,
\omega_{I}=2\Omega\cos\theta
, and
\omega_{CA}=\xi^{\prime}_{\omega}B^{\prime}_{0}k\cos\theta
. We restrict our discussions to the case with
\cos\theta>0
so that
\omega_{A},\omega_{I},\omega_{CA}>0
; the case with
\cos\theta<0
can be similar studied. Through direct calculation, we find that when
\chi=+1
,
\mathrm{Im}(\omega^{\lambda}_{+})<0
(
\lambda=\pm 1
) for all
k>0
and all parameter regions; hence, they correspond to damped wave modes. For
\chi=-1
, we find that there exist kinematic and parameter regions such that
\mathrm{Im}(\omega^{\lambda}_{-})>0
, leading to instability:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(\omega^{+}_{-})>0\Leftarrow\omega_{CA}^{2}-\omega_{I}\omega_{CA}>\omega_{A}^{2},

(31)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}{(\omega^{-}_{-})}>0\Leftarrow\omega_{CA}^{2}+\omega_{I}\omega_{CA}>\omega_{A}^{2}.

(32)

It is instructive to equivalently rewrite the instability conditions as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(\omega^{+}_{-})>0\quad\Leftarrow\quad\omega_{CA}>\omega_{f},

(33)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}{(\omega^{-}_{-})}>0\quad\Leftarrow\quad\omega_{CA}>\omega_{s}.

(34)

These conditions are precisely those anticipated from the discussion in Sec.
III
. It is interesting to notice that these conditions do not depend on
\cos\theta
as long as
\cos\theta>0
.

\mathrm{Im}^{*}(\omega^{+}_{-})\equiv\mathrm{Im}(\omega^{+}_{-})/(\gamma_{\eta}/2)

\mathrm{Im}^{*}(\omega^{-}_{-})\equiv\mathrm{Im}(\omega^{-}_{-})/(\gamma_{\eta}/2)

VI
Evolution of the instability

In a realistic plasma, the instability cannot persist forever, there must be other physical processes to terminate the instability eventually. In order to study the fate of the rotation catalyzed CMVI, similar to the discussion in Ref.
Wang and Huang 2024
, we examine the chiral anomaly equation (see Appendix
A
for a derivation)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}j_{5}^{0}+{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{j}}_{5}=C{\bm{E}}\cdot{\bm{B}},

(35)

where
C
is a constant representing the strength of chiral anomaly, and (see Appendix
A
for a derivation):

\displaystyle j^{0}_{5}

\displaystyle\approx n_{5}+\kappa_{B}{\bm{B}}\cdot({\bm{v}}+{\bm{u}}_{\Omega})+\kappa_{\omega}({\bm{\omega}}+2{\bm{\Omega}})\cdot({\bm{v}}+{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}),

(36)

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}_{5}

\displaystyle\approx n_{5}{\bm{v}}+\kappa_{B}{\bm{B}}+\kappa_{\omega}({\bm{\omega}}+2{\bm{\Omega}}),

(37)

with
n_{5}
the chiral charge density of constituent particles,
\kappa_{B}\propto\mu
(with
\mu
the vector chemical potential) the coefficient of the chiral separation effect, and
\kappa_{\omega}\propto T^{2}
characterizing the axial CVE. To proceed, we introduce the Fourier modes of velocity and magnetic fields,

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}(t,{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bm{k}}}{\bm{v}}(t,{\bm{k}})e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}},

(38)

\displaystyle{\bm{b}}(t,{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bm{k}}}{\bm{b}}(t,{\bm{k}})e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}},

(39)

where
\int_{{\bm{k}}}\equiv\int d^{3}{\bm{k}}/(2\pi)^{3}
. The conditions
{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{v}}={\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{b}}=0
imply that
{\bm{v}}\ (t,{\bm{k}})=v_{+}(t,{\bm{k}}){\bm{e}}_{+}({\bm{k}})+v_{-}(t,{\bm{k}}){\bm{e}}_{-}({\bm{k}})
and
{\bm{b}}\ (t,{\bm{k}})=b_{+}(t,{\bm{k}}){\bm{e}}_{+}({\bm{k}})+b_{-}(t,{\bm{k}}){\bm{e}}_{-}({\bm{k}})
. Then Eq. (
4
) and Eq. (
19
) lead to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{2i{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot{\bm{k}}}{k}v_{\pm}+\frac{i{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{k}}}{\rho}b_{\pm},

(40)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b_{\pm}

\displaystyle=(i{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{k}}+\eta{\bm{k}}^{2}\xi_{\omega})v_{\pm}-\eta{\bm{k}}^{2}\ b_{\pm}.

(41)

When
\xi_{\omega}
is a constant, Eqs. (
40
)-(
41
) can be solved analytically, as detailed in Appendix
D
, which offer a direct pathway to analyze the evolution of magnetic energy, kinetic energy, and various helicities (see definitions below). When
\xi_{\omega}
is time dependent, Eqs. (
40
)-(
41
) should be solved in couple with Eq. (
35
), to determine the fate of the rotation-catalyzed CMVI.

Assuming a homogeneity of the system and writing
n_{5}(t)=\chi_{5}\mu_{5}(t)
with
\chi_{5}\propto T^{2}
the chiral susceptibility, we obtain the following evolution equation from Eq. (
35
):

\displaystyle\chi_{5}\partial_{t}\mu_{5}=-\kappa_{B}\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}_{c}-\kappa_{\omega}\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}_{v}-\frac{C}{2}\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}_{b}-\Gamma\chi_{5}\mu_{5},

(42)

where we have added a chirality-flipping term (the last term on the right-hand side) originated from, e.g., the finite mass of the particles
Grabowska et al. 2015
. In Eq. (
42
), the terms
\mathcal{H}_{c}=\langle({\bm{v}}+{\bm{u}}_{\Omega})\cdot{\bm{B}}\rangle
,
\mathcal{H}_{v}=\langle({\bm{v}}+{\bm{u}}_{\Omega})\cdot({\bm{\omega}}+2{\bm{\Omega}})\rangle
, and
\mathcal{H}_{b}=\langle\bm{A}\cdot{\bm{B}}\rangle
represent the averaged cross, kinetic, and magnetic helicities, respectively, with
\langle...\rangle\equiv V^{-1}\int d^{3}{\bm{x}}(...)
. Therefore, when the chirality relaxation rete
\Gamma=0
, Eq. (
42
) implies a generalized helicity conservation relation
Kirilin and Sadofyev 2017
. Let
\bm{A}=\bm{A}_{0}+\bm{a}
where
\bm{A}_{0}={\bm{B}}_{0}\times{\bm{x}}/2
is the vector potential for background magnetic field and
\bm{a}
is the fluctuating component of the vector potential such that
{\bm{b}}={\bm{\nabla}}\times\bm{a}
.

In our calculation, we have assumed
{\bm{B}}_{0}\parallel{\bm{\Omega}}
which ensures
{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}=0
. Furthermore, we consider fluctuations
{\bm{v}}
and
{\bm{b}}
to be transverse to
{\bm{B}}_{0}
and to vary mainly along
{\bm{B}}_{0}
direction. Under these setups, as we show in Appendix
C
, one finds that
\langle{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot{\bm{v}}\rangle=\langle{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot{\bm{v}}\rangle=\langle{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle=\langle\bm{A}_{0}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle=\langle\bm{a}_{0}\cdot{\bm{B}}_{0}\rangle=\langle{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle=0
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{c}(t)=\langle{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle,\ \mathcal{H}_{v}(t)=\langle{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle,\ \mathcal{H}_{b}(t)=\langle\bm{a}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle.

(43)

These equations agree with the results in Ref.
Wang and Huang 2024
. Note that although the background rotation
{\bm{\Omega}}
does not explicitly enter the expressions of these helicities, it fundamentally influences the dynamics of
{\bm{v}}
and
{\bm{b}}
, as evident in Eqs. (
40
)-(
41
). In accordance with
Wang and Huang 2024
, the three helicities, as well as the magnetic and kinetic energies can be expressed in
{\bm{k}}
space as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\int_{{\bm{k}}}(v_{+}b^{*}_{+}+v_{-}b^{*}_{-}),

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\int_{{\bm{k}}}|{\bm{k}}|\cdot(|v_{+}|^{2}-|v_{-}|^{2}),

(45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{b}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\int_{{\bm{k}}}\frac{1}{|{\bm{k}}|}\cdot(|b_{+}|^{2}-|b_{-}|^{2}),

(46)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{b}

\displaystyle=\langle{\bm{b}}^{\prime 2}\rangle/2=\frac{1}{2V}\int_{{\bm{k}}}(|b^{\prime}_{+}|^{2}+|b^{\prime}_{-}|^{2}),

(47)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{v}

\displaystyle=\langle{\bm{v}}^{2}\rangle/2=\frac{1}{2V}\int_{{\bm{k}}}(|v_{+}|^{2}+|v_{-}|^{2}),

(48)

where the last two expressions
\mathcal{E}_{b}
and
\mathcal{E}_{v}
are average magnetic and kinetic energies (normalized by
\rho
). We also introduce the normalized, dimensionless helicities (denoted with a prime) as follows:
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{c}=\mathcal{H}_{c}/\sqrt{\rho}
,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{v}=\eta\mathcal{H}_{v}
, and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{b}=\mathcal{H}_{b}/(\eta\rho)
. Since
\xi_{\omega}\propto\mu\mu_{5}
, we need to solve numerically the coupled Eqs. (
40
,
41
,
42
). We thus further transform Eq. (
42
) into the explicit evolution equation for
\xi^{\prime}_{\omega}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}(t)=-\kappa^{\prime}_{B}\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{c}(t)-\kappa^{\prime}_{\omega}\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{v}(t)-C^{\prime}\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{b}(t)-\Gamma\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}(t),

(49)

where the primed quantities are all normalized to be dimensionless. For the purpose of illustration, we consider a simplified situation where both
v_{\pm}
and
b_{\pm}
depend only on
k_{z}
. We perform the numerical simulation by using the following parameter set:
B_{0}^{\prime}=5
, and
\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}(t=0)=2.0
and
0.8
, respectively. For the chirality flipping rate
\Gamma
, we consider two cases:
\Gamma=0
and
\Gamma=0.01/\eta
. The initial field distributions are given by
v_{\pm}(0,k_{z})=b^{\prime}_{\pm}(0,k_{z})=v_{0}/[\mathrm{Exp}(10\eta|k_{z}|-50)+1]
with
v_{0}=0.1
. Other parameters are
k^{*}_{\perp}=5/\eta
(the transverse momentum cutoff),
V=3\eta^{3}
(the spatial volume),
\eta^{2}\sqrt{\rho}=10
,
C^{\prime}=25/(8\pi^{2})
,
\kappa^{\prime}_{B}=25/(8\pi^{2})
,
\kappa^{\prime}_{\omega}=1/(4\pi^{2})
.

. We observe the following properties of the magnetic and kinetic energies, as well as various helicities.

(i) The results for
\xi^{\prime}_{\omega}>1
are shown in Fig.

. In this case, a CMVI arises even in the absence of rotation, as first observed in Ref.

. To make the effect of rotation more transparent, we turn off chirality flipping by taking

, and then evolves slowly (quasi-stationarily for small

\mathcal{H}_{b}
and

VII
conclusion and outlook

We have demonstrated that the background rotation plays a vita role in the CMVI and significantly enhances its occurrence. In conventional MHD, rotation
{\bm{\Omega}}
splits the usual Alfvén wave into fast and slow MC waves with frequencies
\omega_{f}>\omega_{A}
and
\omega_{s}<\omega_{A}
, respectively (see Eqs. (
11
)-(
12
)). The emergence of the CMVI requires that the chiral Alfvén wave frequency
\omega_{CA}
is larger than either
\omega_{f}
or
\omega_{s}
(see Eqs. (
33
)-(
34
)). Since
\omega_{s}
is always smaller than
\omega_{A}
, the CMVI is thus always easier to occur comparing to the non-rotating case. In fact, we find that there exist unstable modes for any finite
\xi^{\prime}_{\omega}
when rotation is present, and the unstable kinematic region is proportional to the rotation frequency
\Omega
(see Table
1
). In contrast, for the non-rotating case, the CMVI appears only when
\xi^{\prime}_{\omega}>1
. Therefore, rotation dramatically catalyzes the CMVI.

In Figs.
2
–
4
, the numerical results illustrate the full evolution of the coupled equations (
40
), (
41
), and (
49
). The presence of rotation drastically modifies the system’s evolution, in particular through the emergence of the CMVI for both
\xi^{\prime}_{\omega}>1
and
\xi^{\prime}_{\omega}<1
cases. For
\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}>1
, unlike the non-rotating case—where the CMVI induces only a rapid growth of the cross helicity when the magnetic and kinetic helicities are initially zero—the rotation-catalyzed CMVI also leads to a rapid generation of magnetic and kinetic helicities. For
\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}<1
, rotation opens kinematic windows for the CMVI to occur and such kinematic windows are proportional to the rotation frequency
\Omega
. In particular, this gives rise to a rapid growth of low-
k
modes, which in turn leads to a swift enhancement of
\mathcal{H}_{b}
and
\mathcal{H}_{v}
, in sharp contrast to the non-rotating case studied in Ref.
Wang and Huang 2024
.

Our results suggest a variety of possible implications in different physical systems. First, similar to the CMVI, which aids a dynamo effect, the rotation-catalyzed CMVI could aid a new dynamo effect in a rotating chiral plasma. It would be particularly interesting to explore its influences in electromagnetic plasmas of rotating astrophysical matters and in the rotating quark-gluon plasma in heavy-ion collisions. We leave this as a subject for future investigation.

Appendix A
Maxwell and MHD equations in rotating frame

In this Appendix, we derive the Maxwell and MHD equations in rotating frame that we have used in the main text. We recall that a rigidly rotating frame with a constant angular velocity
{\bm{\Omega}}
is described by the following metric:

\displaystyle g_{00}=1-{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}^{2},\quad g_{0i}=g_{i0}=-u_{\Omega}^{i},\quad g_{ij}=-\delta_{ij},

(50)

where
{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}={\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{r}}
is the frame velocity with respect to the non-rotating inertial frame. The inverse metric is

\displaystyle g^{00}=1,\quad g^{0i}=g^{i0}=-u_{\Omega}^{i},\quad g^{ij}=-\delta_{ij}+u^{i}_{\Omega}u^{j}_{\Omega}.

(51)

The nonzero components of the Christoffel connection
{\Gamma^{\mu}}_{\nu\lambda}
are
{\Gamma^{i}}_{00}=({\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{u}}_{\Omega})^{i}
and
{\Gamma^{i}}_{0j}={\Gamma^{i}}_{j0}=-\epsilon^{ijk}\Omega^{k}
. The Maxwell equations in their covariant form are given by
\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=j^{\nu}
and
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\nabla_{\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}=0
. Here,
\nabla_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative such that
\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}+{\Gamma^{\mu}}_{\mu\rho}F^{\rho\nu}+{\Gamma^{\nu}}_{\mu\rho}F^{\mu\rho}
. Since
|g|=|{\rm det}(g_{\mu\nu})|=1
, one can actually replace
\nabla_{\mu}
by
\partial_{\mu}
to get
\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=j^{\nu}
and
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial_{\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}=0
. Introduce the electric field
{\bm{E}}
and magnetic field
{\bm{B}}
in rotating frame in the following way:

\displaystyle E^{i}=F_{0i},\qquad B^{i}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ijk}F_{jk},

(52)

with
\epsilon^{ijk}
the 3-dimensional Levi-Civita symbol. In terms of
{\bm{E}}
and
{\bm{B}}
, we can derive

\displaystyle F^{i0}

\displaystyle=g^{i\mu}g^{0\nu}F_{\mu\nu}=E^{i}-({\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\times{\bm{B}})^{i}\equiv{E^{i}_{\Omega}},

(53)

\displaystyle F^{ij}

\displaystyle=g^{i\mu}g^{j\nu}F_{\mu\nu}=u_{\Omega}^{i}E^{j}_{\Omega}-u_{\Omega}^{j}E^{i}_{\Omega}-\epsilon^{ijk}B^{k}\equiv-\epsilon^{ijk}B_{\Omega}^{k},

(54)

with
{\bm{B}}_{\Omega}={\bm{B}}-{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\times{\bm{E}}_{\Omega}
. Thus we obtain the Maxwell equations in terms of
{\bm{E}}
and
{\bm{B}}
:

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{B}}

\displaystyle=0,

(55)

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{E}}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}{\bm{B}},

(56)

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{E}}_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=n,

(57)

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{B}}_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}{\bm{E}}_{\Omega}+{\bm{j}}.

(58)

Substituting
{\bm{E}}_{\Omega}={\bm{E}}-{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\times{\bm{B}}
and
{\bm{B}}_{\Omega}={\bm{B}}-{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\times{\bm{E}}_{\Omega}
, the last two Maxwell equations become

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{E}}

\displaystyle=n+n_{\Omega},

(59)

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{B}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}{\bm{E}}+{\bm{j}}+\bm{i},

(60)

with two new terms
n_{\Omega},\bm{i}
being

\displaystyle n_{\Omega}={\bm{\nabla}}\cdot({\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\times{\bm{B}})=2{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot{\bm{B}}-{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot({\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{B}}),

(61)

\displaystyle\bm{i}={\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\times({\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{E}})+{\bm{\nabla}}\times[{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\times({\bm{E}}-{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\times{\bm{B}})].

(62)

This form of Maxwell equations in rotating frame was first derived in Ref.
Schiff 1939
and was then repeatedly re-derived by many others (see, for instance, Ref.
Modesitt 1970
).

To derive the MHD equations in rotating frame, we begin with the relativistically covariant formulation. Let the energy-momentum tensor of the plasma be
T^{\mu\nu}
. We adopt the Landau-Lifshitz convention for the definition of flow four-velocity
u^{\mu}
(normalized as
u^{2}=1
), such that
u^{\mu}
is the eigenvector of
T^{\mu\nu}
. This means that
T^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}u^{\nu}=\varepsilon u^{\mu}
with
\varepsilon
the energy density. With
u^{\mu}
,
T^{\mu\nu}
can be decomposed into the following form,

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}=(\varepsilon+p)u^{\mu}u^{\nu}-pg^{\mu\nu}+\rm{viscous\;\;terms},

(63)

where
p
is the thermodynamic pressure and we have omitted the viscous terms which is not our focus in this article. The hydrodynamic equations are derived from the energy-momentum conservation law,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=F^{\nu\mu}j_{\mu}.

(64)

By contracting with
u_{\nu}
and
\Delta^{\nu}_{\lambda}=\delta^{\nu}_{\lambda}-u^{\nu}u_{\lambda}
, this equation leads to (viscous terms are omitted)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon+(\varepsilon+p)\nabla_{\mu}u^{\mu}=F^{\nu\mu}u_{\nu}j_{\mu},

(65)

\displaystyle(\varepsilon+p)u^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}u^{\nu}-\Delta^{\nu\mu}\partial_{\mu}p=\Delta^{\nu\lambda}F_{\lambda\mu}j^{\mu}.

(66)

To proceed, we consider the non-relativistic limit,
u^{\mu}\approx(1,{\bm{v}})
with
|{\bm{v}}|\sim u_{0}\ll 1
and
|{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}|\sim u_{0}\ll 1
, where
u_{0}=L/\tau
is a typical velocity scale of the system. This requires either a small
|{\bm{\Omega}}|
or a small region close to the rotating axis. Direct calculations give the following results:

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}{\bm{v}}=\partial_{0}{\bm{v}}+{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}{\bm{v}}+2{\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{v}}+{\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}+O(u_{0}^{3}),

(67)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}u^{\mu}=\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\bm{v}}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}({\bm{u}}_{\Omega}+{\bm{v}})^{2}+O(u_{0}^{3}).

(68)

Furthermore, Eq. (
56
) leads to
|{\bm{E}}|/|{\bm{B}}|\sim L/\tau=u_{0}
, which in turn leads to
|{\bm{E}}_{\Omega}|/|{\bm{B}}|\sim u_{0}
and
|{\bm{B}}_{\Omega}|/|{\bm{B}}|\sim 1+O(u_{0}^{2})
. In addition,

\displaystyle|\frac{\partial_{t}{\bm{E}}}{{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{B}}}|\sim u_{0}^{2},

(69)

\displaystyle|\frac{\bm{i}}{{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{B}}}|\sim|{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}|u_{0}+|{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}|^{2}\sim u_{0}^{2}.

(70)

Thus, combining these results, we have
{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{B}}\approx{\bm{j}}
in the non-relativistic limit.

Define the contravariant electric and magnetic fields:

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{\mu}=F^{\mu\nu}u_{\nu},\quad\tilde{B}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}u_{\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}.

(71)

It is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{0}\approx({\bm{E}}+{\bm{v}}\times{\bm{B}})\cdot({\bm{v}}+{\bm{u}}_{\Omega})\sim O(u_{0}^{2}),

(72)

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bm{E}}}={\bm{E}}+{\bm{v}}\times{\bm{B}}+O(u_{0}^{3}),

(73)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}^{0}\approx{\bm{B}}\cdot({\bm{v}}+{\bm{u}}_{\Omega})+O(u_{0}^{3}),

(74)

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bm{B}}}={\bm{B}}+O(u_{0}^{2}).

(75)

Substituting Eqs. (
67
)-(
75
) into Eqs. (
65
)-(
66
), we obtain (accurate up to
O(u_{0}^{3})
)

\displaystyle\left[1+\frac{({\bm{u}}_{\Omega}+{\bm{v}})^{2}}{2}\right]\partial_{0}\varepsilon+{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\varepsilon+(\varepsilon+p)\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\bm{v}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\,+\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon+p)\partial_{0}({\bm{u}}_{\Omega}+{\bm{v}})^{2}={\bm{E}}\cdot{\bm{j}},

(76)

\displaystyle(\varepsilon+p)(\partial_{0}{\bm{v}}+{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}{\bm{v}})+2{\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{v}}+{\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}+\bm{\nabla}p

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\,={\bm{j}}\times{\bm{B}}.

(77)

At non-relativistic limit,
\varepsilon=\rho+\varepsilon^{\prime}
where
\rho
is the mass density and
\varepsilon^{\prime}
is the internal kinetic energy. They satisfy
\varepsilon^{\prime}\sim p\sim O(u_{0}^{2})\ll\rho
. Usually, the magnetic energy
\sim{\bm{B}}^{2}
is smaller than
\rho
, so that we treat
{\bm{j}}\cdot{\bm{E}}
as subleading order. Keeping only leading order terms in Eq. (
A
), we obtain,

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\rho+\bm{\nabla}\cdot(\rho{\bm{v}})=0,

(78)

which is the continuity equation. For incompressible fluid,
\rho
is constant under fluid flow, this equations gives
\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\bm{v}}=0
, and vice verse. Now, approximating
\varepsilon+p
by
\rho
and substituting
{\bm{j}}\approx{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{B}}
into Eq. (
A
), we obtain Eq. (
1
) in the main text.

We now derive the expression for
{\bm{j}}
. The contravariant electric current is

\displaystyle j^{\mu}=nu^{\mu}+\sigma\tilde{E}^{\mu}+\xi_{B}\tilde{B}^{\mu}+\xi_{\omega}\tilde{\omega}^{\mu},

(79)

where
\tilde{\omega}^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}u_{\nu}\nabla_{\rho}u_{\sigma}
and
\sigma=1/\eta
is the electric conductivity. Substituting Eqs. (
72
)-(
75
), we find that

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}

\displaystyle\approx n{\bm{v}}+\sigma({\bm{E}}+{\bm{v}}\times{\bm{B}})+\xi_{B}{\bm{B}}+\xi_{\omega}({\bm{\omega}}+2{\bm{\Omega}})

\displaystyle\approx\sigma({\bm{E}}+{\bm{v}}\times{\bm{B}})+\xi_{B}{\bm{B}}+\xi_{\omega}({\bm{\omega}}+2{\bm{\Omega}}),

(80)

where we have used the fact that
n{\bm{v}}\sim O(u_{0}^{2})
due to Eq. (
57
). This gives that

\displaystyle{\bm{E}}

\displaystyle\approx-{\bm{v}}\times{\bm{B}}+\eta[{\bm{j}}-\xi_{B}{\bm{B}}-\xi_{\omega}({\bm{\omega}}+2{\bm{\Omega}})].

(81)

Substituting this into Eq. (
56
), one obtains Eq. (
18
) in the main text.

Analogous to Eq. (
79
), the contravariant chiral (axial) current is

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{5}=n_{5}u^{\mu}+\kappa_{B}\tilde{B}^{\mu}+\kappa_{\omega}\tilde{\omega}^{\mu},

(82)

where
n_{5}
is the chiral charge density of constituent particles,
\kappa_{B}
is the coefficient of the chiral separation effect, and
\kappa_{\omega}
characterizes the axial CVE. Substituting Eqs. (
72
)-(
75
), we find that
j^{\mu}_{5}
can be approximated as

\displaystyle j^{0}_{5}

\displaystyle\approx n_{5}+\kappa_{B}{\bm{B}}\cdot({\bm{v}}+{\bm{u}}_{\Omega})+\kappa_{\omega}({\bm{\omega}}+2{\bm{\Omega}})\cdot({\bm{v}}+{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}),

(83)

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}_{5}

\displaystyle\approx n_{5}{\bm{v}}+\kappa_{B}{\bm{B}}+\kappa_{\omega}({\bm{\omega}}+2{\bm{\Omega}}).

(84)

The chiral anomaly equation reads

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{5}=\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{5}=-C\tilde{E}^{\mu}\tilde{B}_{\mu},

(85)

where
C=e^{2}/2\pi^{2}
is the anomaly coefficient. Substituting Eqs. (
72
)-(
75
), it becomes Eq. (
35
) in the main text after neglecting terms
\sim O(u_{0}^{3})
.

Appendix B
Sign of the imaginary part of the frequencies

We consider the pure CME case first. Let us denote the two solutions to Eq. (
24
) as
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
with
\omega_{1,2}=\omega_{1,2a}+i\,\omega_{1,2b}
(
\omega_{1,2a/b}\in\mathbb{R}
). Thus Eq. (
24
) is factorized into
(\omega-\omega_{1})(\omega-\omega_{2})=0
which immediately gives us the following relations (
\xi_{B}>0
),

\displaystyle\omega_{1a}+\omega_{2a}=\lambda\omega_{I},

(86)

\displaystyle\omega_{1b}+\omega_{2b}=-\eta k(k+\lambda\xi_{B}),

(87)

\displaystyle\omega_{1a}\omega_{2a}-\omega_{1b}\omega_{2b}=-\omega_{A}^{2},

(88)

\displaystyle\omega_{1a}\omega_{2b}+\omega_{1b}\omega_{2a}=-\eta k(\xi_{B}+\lambda k)\omega_{I}.

(89)

Note that
(\omega_{1a}+\omega_{2a})(\omega_{1b}+\omega_{2b})=\omega_{1a}\omega_{2b}+\omega_{1b}\omega_{2a}
which gives
\omega_{1a}\omega_{1b}+\omega_{2a}\omega_{2b}=0
. Using this relation and multiplying Eq. (
88
) by
\omega_{1b}
, we obtain
(\omega_{2a}^{2}+\omega_{1b}^{2})\omega_{2b}=\omega_{A}^{2}\omega_{1b}
, which shows that
\omega_{1b}
and
\omega_{2b}
must have the same sign. These equations reveal the properties of both real and imaginary parts, allowing us to directly assess the behavior of wave propagation or instability. For instance, in Eq. (
87
), when
k+\lambda\xi_{B}<0
(this is possible only for
\lambda=-1
), the two solutions have positive imaginary parts,
\omega_{1b}>0
and
\omega_{2b}>0
. Therefore, the CME induces an instability in the region
k\in(0,\xi_{B})
. Additionally, when
k+\lambda\xi_{B}=0
(again, this is possible only for
\lambda=-1
), these equations yield
\omega_{1b}=\omega_{2b}=0
, and thus all waves propagate without damping or growth. Furthermore, when
k+\lambda\xi_{B}>0
, Eq. (
87
) implies both
\omega_{1b}
and
\omega_{2b}
should be negative (because they have the same sign), and thus no instability occurs. Thererfore, the CPI occurs for
\lambda=-1,0<k<\xi_{B}
which is the same as the
{\bm{\Omega}}=\bm{0}
case.

We turn to the CVE case. In Sec.
V
, we have provided a detailed analysis of CMVI in the rotating frame based on the solution of Eq. (
26
). Here, we perform another analysis by adopting a geometric approach to Eq. (
26
). First, we express
\omega=\omega_{a}+i\cdot\omega_{b}
,
\omega_{a,b}\in\mathbb{R}
, and split Eq. (
26
) into two distinct curves in
(\omega_{a},\omega_{b})
plane:

\displaystyle\mathrm{C_{1}}:\quad

\displaystyle(\omega_{a}-\frac{\lambda\omega_{I}}{2})^{2}-(\omega_{b}+\frac{\gamma_{\eta}}{2})^{2}=\frac{\omega_{I}^{2}-\gamma_{\eta}^{2}}{4}+\omega_{A}^{2},

(90)

\displaystyle\mathrm{C_{2}}:\quad

\displaystyle\omega_{b}=\frac{(\lambda\omega_{I}/2-\omega_{CA})\gamma_{\eta}}{2\omega_{a}-\lambda\omega_{I}}-\frac{\gamma_{\eta}}{2},

(91)

where
\mathrm{C_{1,2}}
are two hyperbolas. They have zero points at

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathrm{C_{1}}\pm}

\displaystyle=\lambda\frac{\omega_{I}}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{I}^{2}}{4}+\omega_{A}^{2}},

(92)

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathrm{C_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\lambda\omega_{I}-\omega_{CA}.

(93)

These zero points vary with
k
, causing the two curves to shift in position. The intersection points of the two curves correspond to the solutions of
\omega
, which determine whether the solutions represent stable or unstable modes. By comparing the values of
\omega_{\mathrm{C_{1}}\pm},\omega_{\mathrm{C_{2}}}
, we can determine whether instability arises. By categorizing the four main cases based on
\lambda,\cos\theta
(with
\theta
the angle between
{\bm{k}}
and
{\bm{B}}_{0}

-
93

(1)
Case 1:

\lambda=+1
and
\cos\theta>0
. In this case,
\omega_{A},\omega_{CA},\omega_{I}>0
. When

, two intersection points of the two curves have
\mathrm{Im}(\omega)<0
, indicating that both modes are damped. When
\omega_{CA}>2\Omega
, the value of
\omega_{\mathrm{C_{2}}}
becomes negative,
\omega_{\mathrm{C_{2}}}<0
. One of the intersection points still has
\mathrm{Im}(\omega)<0
, corresponding to a damped mode. For the other intersection point, the stability depends on the values of
\omega_{\mathrm{C_{1}}-}
and
\omega_{\mathrm{C_{2}}}
. If
\omega_{\mathrm{C_{1}}-}\leq\omega_{\mathrm{C_{2}}}<0
, one obtains a damped mode. This condition leads to

(94)

which is satisfied when

\displaystyle\xi_{\omega}^{\prime 2}\leq 1\quad\&\quad\frac{2\Omega}{B_{0}^{\prime}\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}}<k\,,

(95)

\displaystyle\mathrm{or}\quad

\displaystyle\xi_{\omega}^{\prime 2}>1\quad\&\quad\frac{2\Omega}{B_{0}^{\prime}\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}}<k\leq\frac{2\Omega\xi_{\omega}}{B_{0}(\xi_{\omega}^{\prime 2}-1)}.

(96)

On the other hand, if
\omega_{\mathrm{C_{2}}}<\omega_{\mathrm{C_{1}}-}
, the other intersection point has
\mathrm{Im}(\omega)>0
, leading to an instability. This condition requires

\frac{\omega_{I}}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{I}^{2}}{4}+\omega_{A}^{2}}<\omega_{CA},

(97)

which is satisfied when

\xi_{\omega}^{\prime 2}>1\quad\&\quad\frac{2\Omega\xi_{\omega}}{B_{0}(\xi_{\omega}^{\prime 2}-1)}<k.

(98)

Hence, the instability is primarily observed in Eqs. (
97
)-(
98
), where unstable modes are excited in the large

range with
\xi_{\omega}^{\prime 2}>1
. This behavior aligns with the CMVI discussed in
Wang and Huang 2024
, and a non-zero
{\bm{\Omega}}
raises the lower threshold for
k
.

Appendix C
The helicity analysis

We analyze the contributions of the background rotation
{\bm{\Omega}}
and magnetic field
{\bm{B}}_{0}
to various helicities. We assume that
{\bm{\Omega}}\parallel{\bm{B}}_{0}
and that the fluctuation fields
{\bm{b}},{\bm{v}}\perp{\bm{B}}_{0}
. The cross helicity, kinetic helicity, and magnetic helicity are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{c}

\displaystyle=\langle({\bm{v}}+{\bm{u}}_{\Omega})\cdot({\bm{B}}_{0}+{\bm{b}})\rangle=\langle{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle+\langle{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle,

(104)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{v}

\displaystyle=\langle({\bm{v}}+{\bm{u}}_{\Omega})\cdot({\bm{\omega}}+2{\bm{\Omega}})\rangle=\langle{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle+\langle{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle,

(105)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{b}

\displaystyle=\langle(\bm{A}_{0}+\bm{a})\cdot({\bm{B}}_{0}+{\bm{b}})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{a}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle+\langle\bm{a}\cdot{\bm{B}}_{0}\rangle+\langle\bm{A}_{0}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{a}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle+2\langle\bm{A}_{0}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle,

(106)

where
\langle\cdots\rangle=V^{-1}\int d^{3}{\bm{x}}(\cdots)
,
\bm{A}_{0}=(1/2){\bm{B}}_{0}\times{\bm{x}}
, and we have used

\displaystyle\langle\bm{a}\cdot{\bm{B}}_{0}\rangle=\langle\bm{A}_{0}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle,

(107)

neglecting a surface term.

We now evaluate the terms involving background fields one by one. It is straightforward to derive

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle=\frac{1}{V}\int d^{3}{\bm{x}}({\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{x}})\cdot{\bm{b}}(t,{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{V}{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot[{\bm{\nabla}}_{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{b}}(t,{\bm{k}})]|_{{\bm{k}}=0}.

(108)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle=\frac{i}{V}{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot[{\bm{\nabla}}_{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{\omega}}(t,{\bm{k}})]|_{{\bm{k}}=0}\,,

(109)

\displaystyle\langle\bm{A}_{0}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle=\frac{i}{2V}{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot[{\bm{\nabla}}_{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{b}}(t,{\bm{k}})]|_{{\bm{k}}=0}\,.

(110)

Using the Fourier-space forms of Eqs. (
4
) and (
19
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{b}}(t,{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle=[({\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot i{\bm{k}})-\eta\xi_{\omega}(i{\bm{k}})^{2}]{\bm{v}}+\eta(i{\bm{k}})^{2}{\bm{b}},

(111)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{\omega}}(t,{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle=2({\bm{\Omega}}\cdot i{\bm{k}}){\bm{v}}+\frac{1}{\rho}({\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot i{\bm{k}})i{\bm{k}}\times{\bm{b}},

(112)

we immediately obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}[{\bm{\nabla}}_{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{b}}(t,{\bm{k}})]|_{{\bm{k}}=0}

\displaystyle=i{\bm{B}}_{0}\times{\bm{v}}(t,{\bm{k}}=0)\,,

(113)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}[{\bm{\nabla}}_{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{\omega}}(t,{\bm{k}})]|_{{\bm{k}}=0}

\displaystyle=2i{\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{v}}(t,{\bm{k}}=0)\,.

(114)

Integrating over time gives

\displaystyle[{\bm{\nabla}}_{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{b}}(t,{\bm{k}})]|_{{\bm{k}}=0}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\ i{\bm{B}}_{0}\times{\bm{v}}(t^{\prime},0)+\mathrm{\bm{C}}_{b},

(115)

\displaystyle[{\bm{\nabla}}_{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{\omega}}(t,{\bm{k}})]|_{{\bm{k}}=0}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\ 2i{\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{v}}(t^{\prime},0)+\mathrm{\bm{C}}_{\omega},

(116)

where
\mathrm{\bm{C}}_{b}=[{\bm{\nabla}}_{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{b}}(t=0,{\bm{k}})]|_{{\bm{k}}=0}
and
\mathrm{\bm{C}}_{\omega}=[{\bm{\nabla}}_{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{\omega}}(t=0,{\bm{k}})]|_{{\bm{k}}=0}
are constant vectors determined by initial conditions. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{V}{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot\mathrm{\bm{C}}_{b},

(117)

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{V}{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot\mathrm{\bm{C}}_{\omega},

(118)

\displaystyle\langle\bm{A}_{0}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2V}{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot\mathrm{\bm{C}}_{b},

(119)

which are time independent. So we only need to consider the initial conditions for
\mathrm{\bm{C}}_{b,\omega}
. In our numerical simulation, we have chosen the initial velocity
{\bm{v}}(t=0,{\bm{k}})
and magnetic field
{\bm{b}}(t=0,{\bm{k}})
to depend only on

{\bm{\Omega}}

Similarly,
{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot\mathrm{\bm{C}}_{b}=0
. For

\displaystyle{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot\mathrm{\bm{C}}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=-i{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot{\bm{v}}(t=0,{\bm{k}})|_{{\bm{k}}=0}=0

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle=\langle{\bm{u}}_{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle=\langle\bm{A}_{0}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle=0,

(122)

125
). The resultant expressions are:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle=-\eta\langle{\bm{j}}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle+{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot\langle{\bm{\omega}}\times{\bm{v}}\rangle+\eta\xi_{B}\langle{\bm{b}}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\eta\xi_{\omega}\langle{\bm{\omega}}^{2}\rangle+2{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot\langle{\bm{b}}\times{\bm{v}}\rangle+\frac{{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot\langle{\bm{b}}\times{\bm{j}}\rangle}{\rho},

(126)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\bm{a}\cdot{\bm{b}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\eta\langle{\bm{b}}\cdot{\bm{j}}\rangle+2{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot\langle{\bm{b}}\times{\bm{v}}\rangle+2\eta\xi_{\omega}\langle{\bm{b}}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\eta\xi_{\omega}2{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot\langle{\bm{b}}\rangle+2\eta\xi_{B}\langle{\bm{b}}^{2}\rangle+\eta\xi_{B}{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot\langle{\bm{b}}\rangle,

(127)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{\omega}}\rangle

\displaystyle=4{\bm{\Omega}}\cdot\langle{\bm{\omega}}\times{\bm{v}}\rangle+\frac{2{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot\langle{\bm{\omega}}\times{\bm{j}}\rangle}{\rho}+\frac{2\langle{\bm{\omega}}\cdot({\bm{j}}\times{\bm{b}})\rangle}{\rho}.

(128)

Similarly, the time derivatives of the magnetic energy, kinetic energy, as well as their sum are given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle{\bm{b}}^{2}/2\rangle

\displaystyle=-\eta\langle{\bm{j}}^{2}\rangle+{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot\langle{\bm{j}}\times{\bm{v}}\rangle+\langle{\bm{v}}\cdot({\bm{b}}\times{\bm{j}})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\eta\xi_{B}\langle{\bm{b}}\cdot{\bm{j}}\rangle+\eta\xi_{\omega}\langle{\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bm{j}}\rangle,

(129)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle{\bm{v}}^{2}/2\rangle=\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{B}}_{0}\cdot\langle{\bm{v}}\times{\bm{j}}\rangle+\frac{1}{\rho}\langle{\bm{v}}\cdot({\bm{j}}\times{\bm{b}})\rangle,

(130)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle{\bm{b}}^{2}/2\rangle+\rho\partial_{t}\langle{\bm{v}}^{2}/2\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-\eta\langle{\bm{j}}^{2}\rangle+\eta\xi_{B}\langle{\bm{b}}\cdot{\bm{j}}\rangle+\eta\xi_{\omega}\langle{\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bm{j}}\rangle.

(131)

These results are consistent with the conventional forms
Shebalin 2006
;
Shebalin 2013
;
Brandenburg et al. 2020
upon setting
\xi_{B}=\xi_{\omega}=0
. The above expressions indicate that background rotation and magnetic fields modify the transfer of both energy and helicity. In Eqs. (
126
,
127
), the anomalous terms
\eta\xi_{\omega}\langle\bm{\omega}^{2}\rangle
and
2\eta\xi_{B}\langle\bm{b}^{2}\rangle
are positive for
\xi_{\omega}>0
and
\xi_{B}>0
, demonstrating that the presence of the CVE (CME) generates cross (magnetic) helicity, as previously clarified in discussions of the CPI and CMVI
Joyce and Shaposhnikov 1997
;
Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013
;
Wang and Huang 2024
. By contrast, the terms
-\eta\langle\bm{j}\cdot\bm{\omega}\rangle
and
-\eta\langle\bm{b}\cdot\bm{j}\rangle
are not sign-definite and are difficult to analyze directly in coordinate space. Therefore, we employ a Fourier-transform approach, as discussed in Sec.
VI
, to carry out a more systematic analysis.

Appendix D
The analytical treatment of the evolution equations

We apply spectral analysis to study the properties of the evolution equations (
40
) and (
41
) analytically for constant
\xi_{\omega}
. First, we rewirte Eqs. (
40
) and (
41
) as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\pm}

\displaystyle=(\pm i\omega_{I}-\eta{\bm{k}}^{2})\partial_{t}v_{\pm}+[i\eta{\bm{k}}^{2}(\omega_{A}\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}\pm\omega_{I})-\omega_{A}^{2}]v_{\pm},

(132)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}b^{\prime}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=(\pm i\omega_{I}-\eta{\bm{k}}^{2})\partial_{t}b^{\prime}_{\pm}+\partial_{t}\xi^{\prime}_{\omega}(t)\cdot\eta{\bm{k}}^{2}v_{\pm}+[i\eta{\bm{k}}^{2}(\omega_{A}\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}\pm\omega_{I})-\omega_{A}^{2}]b^{\prime}_{\pm}.

(133)

These are second-order ordinary differential equations when
\xi_{\omega}=\mathrm{const}
. From Eqs. (
40
) and (
41
), we also note that
\partial_{t}v_{\pm}(t,0)=\pm i\omega_{I}\cdot v_{\pm}(t,0)
and
\partial_{t}b^{\prime}_{\pm}(t,0)=0
when
{\bm{k}}=0
.

Before delving into Eqs. (
132
)-(
133
), it is useful to consider the ideal MHD case (
\eta=0
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\pm}

\displaystyle-(\pm i\omega_{I})\partial_{t}v_{\pm}+\omega_{A}^{2}v_{\pm}=0,

(134)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}b^{\prime}_{\pm}

\displaystyle-(\pm i\omega_{I})\partial_{t}b^{\prime}_{\pm}+\omega_{A}^{2}b^{\prime}_{\pm}=0,

(135)

whose solutions are

\displaystyle v_{\pm}

\displaystyle=c_{1\pm}\cdot e^{z_{1\pm}\cdot t}+c_{2\pm}\cdot e^{z_{2\pm}\cdot t},

(136)

\displaystyle b^{\prime}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{i\omega_{A}}{z_{1\pm}}c_{1\pm}\cdot e^{z_{1\pm}\cdot t}+\frac{i\omega_{A}}{z_{2\pm}}c_{2\pm}\cdot e^{z_{2\pm}\cdot t},

(137)

\displaystyle z_{1\pm}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{i}{2}[\pm\omega_{I}+\sqrt{\omega_{I}^{2}+4\omega_{A}^{2}}],\qquad z_{2\pm}\equiv\frac{i}{2}[\pm\omega_{I}-\sqrt{\omega_{I}^{2}+4\omega_{A}^{2}}],

(138)

where the coefficients
c_{1\pm}({\bm{k}}),c_{2\pm}({\bm{k}})
are assumed to be real functions of
{\bm{k}}
, determined by initial conditions, and
b^{\prime}_{\pm}(t,{\bm{k}})
is derived from Eq. (
40
) and Eq. (
136
). Since
{\bm{v}}(t,{\bm{x}}),{\bm{b}}(t,{\bm{x}})
are real functions, we have
{\bm{v}}^{*}(t,{\bm{k}})={\bm{v}}(t,-{\bm{k}}),{\bm{b}}^{*}(t,{\bm{k}})={\bm{b}}(t,-{\bm{k}})
, which combined with Eqs. (
136
)-(
137
), imply
c_{1\pm}(-{\bm{k}})=c_{2\pm}({\bm{k}})
. Then
|v_{\pm}|^{2},|b_{\pm}|^{2},v_{\pm}b^{*}_{\pm}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle|v_{\pm}|^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{1\pm}^{2}+c_{2\pm}^{2}+2(c_{1\pm}c_{2\pm})\cdot\mathrm{cos}[\sqrt{\omega_{I}^{2}+4\omega_{A}^{2}}\cdot t],

(139)

\displaystyle|b^{\prime}_{\pm}|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\frac{i\omega_{A}}{z_{1\pm}})^{2}\cdot c_{1\pm}^{2}+(\frac{i\omega_{A}}{z_{2\pm}})^{2}\cdot c_{2\pm}^{2}-2(c_{1\pm}c_{2\pm})\cdot\mathrm{cos}[\sqrt{\omega_{I}^{2}+4\omega_{A}^{2}}\cdot t],

(140)

\displaystyle v_{\pm}b^{\prime*}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{i\omega_{A}}{z_{1\pm}}c_{1\pm}^{2}+\frac{i\omega_{A}}{z_{2\pm}}c_{2\pm}^{2}-\frac{(\pm\omega_{I})\cdot c_{1\pm}c_{2\pm}}{\omega_{A}}\mathrm{cos}[\sqrt{\omega_{I}^{2}+4\omega_{A}^{2}}\cdot t]-i\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{I}^{2}+4\omega_{A}^{2}}\cdot c_{1\pm}c_{2\pm}}{\omega_{A}}\mathrm{sin}[\sqrt{\omega_{I}^{2}+4\omega_{A}^{2}}\cdot t],

(141)

from which we can obtain the kinetic energy, magnetic energy, and various helicities, after integrating over
{\bm{k}}
. The Eqs. (
139
)-(
141
) exhibit the oscillatory behavior, even after the
{\bm{k}}
integration. Although
|v_{\pm}|^{2}
or
|b_{\pm}|^{2}
oscillate over time,
|v_{\pm}|^{2}+|b^{\prime}_{\pm}|^{2}
is constant, indicating energy conservation in ideal MHD. The last term in Eq. (
141
) is imaginary, but it vanishes after
{\bm{k}}
integrtion because
c_{1\pm}(-{\bm{k}})c_{2\pm}(-{\bm{k}})=c_{2\pm}({\bm{k}})c_{1\pm}({\bm{k}})
and
\omega_{A}(-{\bm{k}})=-\omega_{A}({\bm{k}})
, showing that this imaginary term has no contribution to cross helicity.

We now turn to Eq. (
132
). It has the following characteristic equation

\displaystyle\zeta^{2}-(\pm i\omega_{I}-\eta{\bm{k}}^{2})\cdot\zeta-[i\eta{\bm{k}}^{2}(\omega_{A}\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}\pm\omega_{I})-\omega_{A}^{2}]=0,

(142)

which has four solutions

\displaystyle\zeta=\zeta_{\pm}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}[(\alpha i\omega_{I}-\gamma_{\eta})\pm\sqrt{-\omega_{I}^{2}+\gamma_{\eta}^{2}+2\gamma_{\eta}\cdot(\alpha i\omega_{I})+4i\gamma_{\eta}\cdot\xi_{\omega}^{\prime}\omega_{A}-4\omega_{A}^{2}}]\qquad(\alpha=\pm 1).

(143)

Then, one obtains

\displaystyle v_{+}

\displaystyle=c_{1+}e^{\zeta_{+}^{+}\cdot t}+c_{2+}e^{\zeta_{-}^{+}\cdot t}=c_{1+}e^{-i\omega_{+}^{-}\cdot t}+c_{2+}e^{-i\omega_{-}^{-}\cdot t},

(144)

\displaystyle v_{-}

\displaystyle=c_{1-}e^{\zeta_{+}^{-}\cdot t}+c_{2-}e^{\zeta_{-}^{-}\cdot t}=c_{1-}e^{-i\omega_{+}^{+}\cdot t}+c_{2-}e^{-i\omega_{-}^{+}\cdot t}.

(145)

It is sufficient to assume
c_{1\pm},c_{2\pm}
to be real functions of
{\bm{k}}
. Combing with Eq. (
40
), one obtains
b_{\pm}
:

\displaystyle b_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\omega_{A}}(-\omega_{+}^{-}-\omega_{I})c_{1+}e^{-i\omega_{+}^{-}\cdot t}+\frac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\omega_{A}}(-\omega_{-}^{-}-\omega_{I})c_{2+}e^{-i\omega_{-}^{-}\cdot t},

(146)

\displaystyle b_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\omega_{A}}(-\omega_{+}^{+}+\omega_{I})c_{1-}e^{-i\omega_{+}^{+}\cdot t}+\frac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\omega_{A}}(-\omega_{-}^{+}+\omega_{I})c_{2-}e^{-i\omega_{-}^{+}\cdot t}.

(147)

With
v_{\pm}
and
b^{\prime}_{\pm}
obtained, the kinetic and magnetic energies
\mathcal{E}_{b,v}
as well as various helicities
\mathcal{H}_{b,c,v}
can be constructed straightforwardly. In order to illustrate the behavior of
\mathcal{E}_{b,v},\mathcal{H}_{b,c,v}
qualitatively, we ignore the terms suppressed by
(...)e^{-\gamma_{\eta}\cdot t}
as
e^{-\gamma_{\eta}\cdot t}\rightarrow 0
quickly for large times. We then have

\displaystyle|v_{+}|^{2}

\displaystyle\approx c_{1+}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{+}^{-})\cdot t}+c_{2+}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{-}^{-})\cdot t},

(148)

\displaystyle|v_{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle\approx c_{1-}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{+}^{+})\cdot t}+c_{2-}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{-}^{+})\cdot t},

(149)

\displaystyle\omega_{A}^{2}\cdot|b^{\prime}_{+}|^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\{[\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{+}^{-})+\omega_{I}]^{2}+[\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{+}^{-})]^{2}\}\cdot c_{1+}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{+}^{-})\cdot t}+\{[\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{-}^{-})+\omega_{I}]^{2}+[\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{-}^{-})]^{2}\}\cdot c_{2+}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{-}^{-})\cdot t},

(150)

\displaystyle\omega_{A}^{2}\cdot|b^{\prime}_{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\{[\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{+}^{+})-\omega_{I}]^{2}+[\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{+}^{+})]^{2}\}\cdot c_{1-}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{+}^{+})\cdot t}+\{[\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{-}^{+})-\omega_{I}]^{2}+[\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{-}^{+})]^{2}\}\cdot c_{2-}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{-}^{+})\cdot t},

(151)

\displaystyle\omega_{A}\cdot v_{+}b_{+}^{\prime\ *}

\displaystyle\approx[-\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{+}^{-})+i\cdot\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{+}^{-})-\omega_{I}]\cdot c_{1+}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{+}^{-})\cdot t}+[-\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{-}^{-})+i\cdot\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{-}^{-})-\omega_{I}]\cdot c_{2+}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{-}^{-})\cdot t},

(152)

\displaystyle\omega_{A}\cdot v_{-}b_{-}^{\prime\ *}

\displaystyle\approx[-\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{+}^{+})+i\cdot\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{+}^{+})+\omega_{I}]\cdot c_{1-}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{+}^{+})\cdot t}+[-\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{-}^{+})+i\cdot\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{-}^{+})+\omega_{I}]\cdot c_{2-}^{2}e^{2\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{-}^{+})\cdot t}.

(153)

Therefore, for constant
\xi_{\omega}>0

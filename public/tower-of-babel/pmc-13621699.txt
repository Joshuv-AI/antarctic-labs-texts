Prevalence and associated factors of chronic kidney disease–associated pruritus in maintenance hemodialysis patients: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Kaifeng Lin, Li Li, Jinghui Gao, Yipei Zhang, Hong Tan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChronic kidney disease–associated pruritus (CKD-aP) is a common and burdensome complication in patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis (MHD). However, reported prevalence and associated factors remain inconsistent across studies.MethodsIn accordance with PRISMA 2020 guidelines, we systematically searched eight electronic databases from inception to March 11, 2026 for observational studies reporting prevalence and/or associated factors of pruritus in adult MHD patients. Only multivariable-adjusted odds ratios were included in analyses of associated factors. Continuous and dichotomised estimates were analysed separately, and sensitivity analyses restricted to high-quality studies were performed where feasible.ResultsA total of 25 studies involving 8,955 participants were included. The pooled prevalence of any pruritus among MHD patients was 60.0% (95% CI: 57.0–63.0%). Serum phosphorus was positively associated with pruritus in both continuous (OR = 2.03, 95% CI: 1.44–2.87) and dichotomised analyses (OR = 2.93, 95% CI: 1.83–4.69), although substantial heterogeneity remained in the continuous analysis. Associations for PTH/iPTH, CRP, β₂-microglobulin, dialysis vintage, and age varied according to variable definition and/or study quality. Diabetes mellitus was associated with higher odds of pruritus (OR = 2.64, 95% CI: 1.69–4.14), whereas high-flux hemodialysis showed an inverse association (OR = 0.31, 95% CI: 0.17–0.56). Exploratory meta-regression among continuous phosphorus studies suggested regional variation (P = 0.002), with a stronger association in Asian than non-Asian studies; however, only two non-Asian studies were available.ConclusionsApproximately 60% of patients undergoing MHD experience pruritus. Serum phosphorus showed the most consistent association with CKD-aP, whereas estimates for several other factors varied according to variable definition, methodological quality, and between-study heterogeneity. The observed regional variation in the phosphorus–pruritus association should be regarded as hypothesis-generating. Introduction

Chronic kidney disease–associated pruritus (CKD-aP) is a common and burdensome symptom among patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis. Previous meta-analytic and multinational evidence indicates that approximately half of dialysis patients experience pruritus, although prevalence varies across populations and assessment methods [1–3]. CKD-aP is associated with sleep disturbance, depressive symptoms, impaired health-related quality of life, and adverse clinical outcomes [2, 4–6]. Nevertheless, it remains underreported by patients and may be underestimated in routine dialysis care [3].

The factors associated with CKD-aP remain uncertain. Previous studies have examined demographic characteristics, comorbidities, dialysis duration, mineral metabolism, inflammatory markers, middle-molecule retention, dialysis adequacy, and membrane type. Some studies have reported associations with serum phosphorus, β₂-microglobulin, dialysis adequacy, and dialyzer flux [7, 8], whereas others have produced inconsistent findings [2]. Differences in patient characteristics, pruritus definitions, assessment tools, laboratory thresholds, and statistical adjustment may partly explain these discrepancies. In addition, the predominance of cross-sectional studies limits conclusions regarding temporal or causal relationships.

Existing reviews have not fully addressed these uncertainties. One meta-analysis estimated the prevalence of CKD-aP in dialysis populations but did not comprehensively synthesize its associated factors [1]. Another review focused on Chinese maintenance hemodialysis patients, limiting the generalizability of its findings [9]. A recent systematic review summarized proposed mechanisms and emerging treatments but did not quantitatively combine global prevalence estimates and associated factors [10]. We therefore conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to estimate the prevalence of CKD-aP among adults receiving maintenance hemodialysis, evaluate its associated factors, and explore potential sources of between-study heterogeneity.

Methods

Protocol registration

The protocol for this systematic review and meta-analysis was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO registration number CRD420261337554). The review was conducted and reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 statement (PRISMA 2020) [11] and the Meta-analysis of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (MOOSE) recommendations. The completed PRISMA and MOOSE checklists are available in Items S1 and S2, respectively.

Information sources and search strategy

We searched eight electronic databases from their inception to 11 March 2026: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library, the Wanfang Database, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), the VIP Database, and the Chinese Biomedical Literature Database (CBM).

The search strategy combined controlled vocabulary, including Medical Subject Headings where applicable, with free-text terms representing two main concepts: hemodialysis and pruritus. Search terms related to dialysis included “renal dialysis,” “renal replacement therapy,” “hemodialysis,” “kidney dialysis,” and “maintenance hemodialysis.” Terms related to the outcome included “pruritus,” “itch,” and “itching.” The search syntax was adapted to the indexing system and requirements of each database.

To identify records not retrieved through the electronic searches, we also examined the reference lists of eligible articles and relevant systematic reviews or meta-analyses. The complete database-specific search strategies are presented in Item S3.

Study selection and data extraction

All records retrieved from the searches were imported into EndNote 2025, and duplicate entries were removed before screening. Two reviewers (Lin KF and Gao JH) independently assessed the titles and abstracts of the remaining records. Articles considered potentially relevant by either reviewer were retrieved for full-text evaluation against the eligibility criteria. Differences in study-selection decisions were resolved through discussion; when consensus could not be reached, a third reviewer (Zhang YP) was consulted.

Two reviewers (Lin KF and Gao JH) independently extracted information from each eligible article using a standardized Microsoft Excel form. The extracted information included the first author, publication year, study location, study design, sample size, characteristics of the study population, method used to define or measure pruritus, number of participants with pruritus, and the associated factors examined. For studies included in the associated-factor analyses, we additionally recorded the reported effect estimates, 95% confidence intervals, type of statistical model, and variables included in adjusted analyses, when available.

The two extraction forms were compared after completion. Any discrepancies were checked against the original publication and resolved by discussion, with consultation of the third reviewer when necessary.

Quality assessment

Methodological quality was evaluated independently by two reviewers (Lin KF and Gao JH). Disagreements were resolved by consensus or, when necessary, by consultation with a third reviewer (Zhang YP).

Cohort and case-control studies were assessed using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) [12], which evaluates participant selection, comparability of study groups, and ascertainment of exposure or outcome. Studies with NOS scores of 7 or higher were classified as high quality, scores of 5–6 as moderate quality, and scores below 5 as low quality.

Cross-sectional studies were evaluated using the 11-item checklist recommended by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ) [13]. Each item rated “yes” received one point, whereas items rated “no” or “unclear” received no points. Total scores of 0–3, 4–7, and 8–11 were interpreted as indicating low, moderate, and high methodological quality, respectively.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using Stata 17.0 and Review Manager 5.4.1. For the prevalence analysis, the numerator was the number of participants with pruritus and the denominator was the total number of participants receiving maintenance hemodialysis in each study. Study-specific prevalence estimates were combined to obtain a pooled prevalence with a corresponding 95% confidence interval.

For analyses of associated factors, odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals were used as the summary measures. An associated factor was quantitatively synthesised only when data were available from at least two sufficiently comparable studies. Only adjusted effect estimates derived from multivariable regression models were included; unadjusted or univariable estimates were not pooled. When more than one adjusted model was reported, the estimate from the most fully adjusted model was selected. Before pooling, effect estimates were classified according to variable coding as continuous or dichotomised. Continuous and dichotomised estimates were treated as distinct exposure contrasts and were not combined in the same pooled estimate. Quantitative synthesis was performed only when at least two studies provided sufficiently comparable estimates within the same variable-type stratum.

Between-study heterogeneity was examined using Cochran’s Q test and the I2 statistic [14]. A Q-test P-value below 0.10 and/or an I2 value above 30% was considered indicative of meaningful heterogeneity. A random-effects model was applied when heterogeneity was present; otherwise, a fixed-effect model was used.

To assess whether methodological quality influenced the pooled estimates, sensitivity analyses were performed by restricting the analyses to studies classified as high quality. A quality-based sensitivity meta-analysis was performed only when at least two high-quality studies with comparable effect estimates remained. When all studies contributing to a given stratum were classified as high quality, a separate quality-restricted analysis was not repeated because the study set and pooled estimate would have been identical to the primary analysis. When fewer than two high-quality studies remained, no pooled sensitivity estimate was calculated. For serum phosphorus, an exploratory subgroup analysis and meta-regression according to study location were conducted within the continuous-variable stratum only, thereby avoiding the combination of continuous and dichotomised exposure contrasts. Study location was categorised as Asian versus non-Asian.

Because substantial variation in pruritus prevalence was anticipated, subgroup analyses were conducted according to geographical region, sample size, and the method used to assess pruritus. Meta-regression was used, where sufficient studies were available, to examine whether these study-level characteristics explained part of the observed heterogeneity. Given the limited statistical power of subgroup and meta-regression tests, a two-sided P value below 0.10 was considered evidence of a between-group difference.

For meta-analyses containing at least ten studies, small-study effects and potential publication bias were assessed through visual examination of funnel plots and Egger’s regression test. A P-value below 0.10 in Egger’s test was considered suggestive of funnel-plot asymmetry. Where potential publication bias was identified, the trim-and-fill procedure was used to evaluate its possible effect on the pooled estimate. Unless otherwise specified, statistical tests were two-sided and P-value below 0.05 were regarded as statistically significant.

Results

Study selection

The electronic database searches yielded 2,293 records. After 415 duplicate records had been removed, 1,878 unique records underwent title and abstract screening. At this stage, 1,564 records were excluded because they were not relevant to the review question or did not meet the prespecified eligibility criteria. The full texts of the remaining 314 reports were assessed, of which 289 were excluded after detailed evaluation. Reference-list screening of eligible studies and relevant reviews did not identify any additional studies for inclusion. Consequently, 25 studies met the eligibility criteria and were included in the quantitative synthesis. The complete study-selection process, including the reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage, is presented in the PRISMA flow diagram in Fig. 1.

Table 1 summarizes the main characteristics of the 25 included studies. The studies were published between 2008 and 2025 and together involved 8,955 patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis. Individual study sample sizes ranged from 100 to 3,025 participants. The 19 studies conducted in China included a total of 6,515 participants, accounting for 72.8% of the overall study population.

The available evidence was geographically concentrated. Nineteen studies were conducted in China, two in Egypt, and one each in Austria, Somalia, Canada, and Turkey. Thus, Chinese studies accounted for 76.0% of the included evidence. Most studies used a cross-sectional design (n = 23, 92.0%); the remaining two comprised one case-control study and one cohort study.

Considerable variation was observed in the methods used to identify and quantify pruritus. The Visual Analogue Scale was the most frequently used instrument (n = 10, 40.0%), followed by the 5-D Itch Scale (n = 3, 12.0%). The Dirk R. Kuypers scoring system, Four-Item Itch Questionnaire, and Worst Itching Intensity Numerical Rating Scale were each used in two studies. Three studies identified pruritus on the basis of clinical diagnostic criteria without employing a standardized quantitative scale. The remaining studies used the Uremic Pruritus in Dialysis scale, the Duo pruritus score, or the 12-Item Pruritus Severity Scale, with each instrument used in one study.

The results of the quality assessment for each included study are summarized in Table 1. The single case–control study [38] and the single cohort study [39] were both rated as high quality based on the NOS. Meta-analysis of the prevalence of pruritus in people on hemodialysis

All 25 included studies reported data on the prevalence of pruritus, and the corresponding forest plot is presented in Fig. 2. Substantial heterogeneity was observed across studies (I2 = 87.6%, Cochran’s Q test P < 0.001). Accordingly, a random-effects model was used, yielding a pooled prevalence of 60.0% (95% CI: 57.0–63.0%) among patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis. Visual inspection of the funnel plot showed no marked asymmetry, and Egger’s regression test was not statistically significant (P = 0.441), indicating no clear evidence of publication bias.

Forest plot of the prevalence of pruritus in people on hemodialysis (n = 25)

To investigate potential sources of heterogeneity, subgroup analyses and meta-regression were performed according to geographical region, study sample size, and the method used to assess pruritus. The detailed findings are presented in Table S1 and Fig. 3. The pooled prevalence was 60% (95% CI: 57–63%) in studies conducted in Asia, 63% (95% CI: 48–78%) in Africa, 51% (95% CI: 48–54%) in Europe, and 57% (95% CI: 51–63%) in North America. However, meta-regression showed that geographical region was not significantly associated with the reported prevalence of pruritus (coefficient = − 0.020, 95% CI: −0.06 to 0.02; P = 0.334).

Forest plot of the subgroup analysis of pruritus prevalence by (A) Study location (B) Sample size (C) Definition of pruritus in people on hemodialysis

The median sample size of the included studies was 227 participants and was used as an exploratory threshold for subgroup analysis. The pooled prevalence was 60% (95% CI: 54–65%) among studies with 227 or fewer participants and 60% (95% CI: 55–64%) among studies with more than 227 participants. Meta-regression provided no evidence that study sample size contributed substantially to the observed heterogeneity (coefficient = − 0.001, 95% CI: −0.07 to 0.07; P = 0.979).

When studies were grouped according to the method used to assess pruritus, the pooled prevalence was 60% (95% CI: 57–63%) for studies using the Visual Analogue Scale, 59% (95% CI: 39–79%) for those using the 5-D Itch Scale, 56% (95% CI: 50–62%) for studies applying clinical diagnostic criteria, and 61% (95% CI: 53–68%) for studies using other assessment instruments. The corresponding meta-regression result was not statistically significant (coefficient = 0.004, 95% CI: −0.02 to 0.03; P = 0.753). Overall, none of the examined study-level characteristics adequately explained the substantial between-study heterogeneity in pruritus prevalence.

For serum phosphorus, analyses were stratified according to variable type because continuous and dichotomised estimates represented different exposure contrasts. Among the eight studies analysing serum phosphorus as a continuous variable, the pooled OR was 2.03 (95% CI: 1.44–2.87), with substantial heterogeneity (I2 = 69.6%, P = 0.002). Among the three studies reporting dichotomised serum phosphorus, the pooled OR was 2.93 (95% CI: 1.83–4.69), with no observed heterogeneity (I2 = 0%, P = 0.693). Sensitivity analyses restricted to high-quality studies showed that six continuous-variable studies remained, yielding an attenuated pooled estimate of 1.95 (95% CI: 1.32–2.88), although substantial heterogeneity persisted (I2 = 73.8%, P = 0.002). All three studies in the dichotomised subgroup were classified as high quality; therefore, the pooled estimate remained unchanged (OR = 2.93, 95% CI: 1.83–4.69; I2 = 0%, P = 0.693). Thus, restriction to high-quality studies did not change the direction of the association, although the magnitude of the continuous-variable estimate was reduced. Exploratory regional analyses were subsequently conducted among the eight studies analysing serum phosphorus as a continuous variable. The pooled OR was 2.27 (95% CI: 1.78–2.90) in six Asian studies, with no observed heterogeneity (I2 = 0%, P = 0.698), compared with 1.46 (95% CI: 0.84–2.53) in two non-Asian studies (I2 = 68.9%, P = 0.073). Meta-regression indicated that study location was associated with variation in the effect estimates (coefficient = − 0.573, 95% CI: −0.93 to − 0.22, P = 0.002). However, given that only two non-Asian studies were available, this regional difference should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating. Detailed results are presented in Table 3; Fig. 4.

For PTH/iPTH, results differed according to variable type. Among four studies analysing PTH/iPTH as a continuous variable, the pooled estimate was 1.00 (95% CI: 1.00–1.00), with substantial heterogeneity (I² = 93.1%, P < 0.001). In contrast, four studies using dichotomised PTH/iPTH definitions showed an association with pruritus (OR = 2.08, 95% CI: 1.35–3.22), although moderate between-study heterogeneity was present (I² = 52.9%, P = 0.095). In the quality-based sensitivity analysis, all four continuous-variable studies were classified as high quality and the pooled estimate was therefore unchanged. After restriction to high-quality studies, three dichotomised studies remained, and the association persisted (OR = 2.63, 95% CI: 1.36–5.10; I² = 56.2%, P = 0.102). These findings suggest that the observed PTH–pruritus association may depend on how PTH was operationalised, while study quality did not materially alter the direction of the dichotomised association.

For CRP/high-sensitivity CRP, the results differed according to variable type. Among the two studies analysing CRP as a continuous variable, the pooled OR was 3.14 (95% CI: 0.30–32.60), with substantial heterogeneity (I² = 95.6%, P < 0.001). In contrast, five studies using dichotomised CRP definitions did not show an association with pruritus (OR = 2.18, 95% CI: 0.92–5.16); substantial heterogeneity was also present (I² = 84.6%, P < 0.001). In the quality-based sensitivity analysis, both continuous-variable studies were classified as high quality and the pooled estimate therefore remained unchanged. After restriction to high-quality studies, three dichotomised studies remained, yielding a larger pooled estimate (OR = 4.12, 95% CI: 2.38–7.12), with no observed heterogeneity (I² = 0%, P = 0.466). These findings suggest that the estimated CRP–pruritus association varied according to variable definition and study quality.

For dialysis vintage, the results differed according to variable type. Among five studies analysing dialysis vintage as a continuous variable, the pooled OR was 1.03 (95% CI: 1.00–1.06), with substantial between-study heterogeneity (I² = 89.3%, P < 0.001). In contrast, the two studies using dichotomised definitions yielded a larger pooled estimate (OR = 2.62, 95% CI: 1.93–3.56), with no observed heterogeneity (I² = 0%, P = 0.923). All studies contributing to these analyses were classified as high quality; therefore, an additional quality-restricted sensitivity analysis would not alter the study set or pooled estimates. These findings indicate that the estimated association between dialysis vintage and pruritus varied substantially according to how dialysis duration was operationalised.

For β₂-microglobulin, the pooled estimates also differed according to variable type. Among the two studies analysing β₂-microglobulin as a continuous variable, no clear pooled association with pruritus was observed (OR = 1.21, 95% CI: 0.88–1.68), and substantial heterogeneity was present (I² = 92.5%, P < 0.001). In contrast, the two studies using dichotomised definitions showed higher odds of pruritus (OR = 2.64, 95% CI: 1.46–4.77), with low between-study heterogeneity (I² = 19.7%, P = 0.264). All four contributing studies were classified as high quality; therefore, restriction to high-quality studies did not require a separate sensitivity analysis. These findings suggest that the β₂-microglobulin–pruritus association may depend on how β₂-microglobulin is operationalised.

For age, only one study analysed age as a continuous variable; therefore, a separate pooled estimate for continuous age could not be calculated. Among the three studies using dichotomised age definitions, higher age was associated with greater odds of pruritus (OR = 2.73, 95% CI: 1.77–4.22), with no observed heterogeneity (I² = 0%). Because the continuous estimate was available from only one study, these findings should be interpreted as evidence specific to studies using categorical age definitions rather than as a uniform association across different age parameterisations.

For serum calcium, the original analysis included three continuous-variable studies and one dichotomised study. After excluding the dichotomised study by Engler et al., which was also rated as moderate quality, the remaining three studies yielded no clear association between serum calcium and pruritus (OR = 1.10, 95% CI: 0.37–3.27). Substantial heterogeneity persisted (I² = 92%, P < 0.001). Thus, restriction by variable type and methodological quality did not materially alter the overall interpretation or explain the substantial between-study heterogeneity.

For serum albumin, one of the three contributing studies used a dichotomised definition and was therefore not pooled with the continuous-variable studies. The two remaining continuous-variable studies showed no clear association between albumin and pruritus (OR = 1.33, 95% CI: 0.07–23.97), with substantial heterogeneity (I² = 92%, P < 0.001). Given the small number of studies, wide confidence interval, and substantial heterogeneity, the pooled estimate was highly imprecise.

Diabetes mellitus and high-flux hemodialysis showed relatively consistent associations with pruritus. Diabetes mellitus was associated with higher odds of pruritus (OR = 2.64, 95% CI: 1.69–4.14), with no observed heterogeneity (I² = 0%, P = 0.80). In contrast, high-flux hemodialysis was inversely associated with pruritus (OR = 0.31, 95% CI: 0.17–0.56), also with no observed heterogeneity (I² = 0%, P = 0.74). Fixed-effect models were therefore used for both analyses.

No clear pooled associations were observed for depression (OR = 0.60, 95% CI: 0.04–10.05; I² = 91%), smoking history (OR = 1.45, 95% CI: 0.16–13.47; I² = 95%), 25(OH)₂D₃ (OR = 0.98, 95% CI: 0.90–1.07; I² = 94%), or sleep quality (OR = 1.00, 95% CI: 0.51–1.94; I² = 98%). Each analysis included only two studies, and the substantial heterogeneity and wide confidence intervals indicate considerable uncertainty in these pooled estimates. For BMI, the two available studies yielded a pooled OR of 1.08 (95% CI: 1.00–1.17; I² = 62%); given the borderline estimate and limited evidence base, this result should be interpreted cautiously.

Publication bias

Publication bias was assessed using Egger’s linear regression test and visual inspection of funnel plots for both the prevalence of pruritus and the association between serum phosphorus and pruritus. The results of these analyses are presented in Fig. 5.

Funnel plots for publication bias assessment in meta-analyses of associated factors for pruritus in hemodialysis patients, stratified by (A) prevalence (B) Serum phosphorus

For the prevalence of pruritus, the funnel plot did not reveal obvious asymmetry. Egger’s test yielded a P-value of 0.441, indicating no statistically significant evidence of small-study effects or publication bias (coefficient = − 0.960, 95% CI: −3.492 to 1.572, P = 0.441). This finding suggests that the pooled prevalence estimate was unlikely to be substantially influenced by publication bias.

For the association between serum phosphorus and pruritus, which included 11 studies, the funnel plot showed visual asymmetry. Egger’s test confirmed the presence of statistically significant small-study effects (coefficient = 2.442, 95% CI: 1.797 to 3.088, P < 0.001), suggesting potential publication bias. To further evaluate the impact of potential publication bias on the pooled estimate, a nonparametric trim-and-fill analysis was performed using the linear estimator with imputation on the left side. The analysis imputed additional studies to correct for funnel plot asymmetry. After imputation, the pooled effect estimate decreased from 2.15 (95% CI: 1.63–2.82) to 1.82 (95% CI: 1.44–2.30) but remained statistically significant, indicating that the association between elevated serum phosphorus and pruritus was robust to potential publication bias.

Publication bias was not formally assessed for other factors due to the limited number of included studies (fewer than 10) for each individual factor, as Egger’s test has low statistical power with fewer than 10 studies.

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis comprehensively evaluated the prevalence of CKD-associated pruritus and its associated factors in patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis. The pooled prevalence was 60.0% (95% CI: 57.0–63.0%). Among the associated factors examined, serum phosphorus showed positive associations across both continuous and dichotomised analyses, while diabetes mellitus and high-flux hemodialysis showed relatively consistent positive and inverse associations, respectively. In contrast, findings for PTH/iPTH, CRP, β₂-microglobulin, dialysis vintage, and age varied according to variable definition and/or methodological quality. No clear pooled associations were identified for serum calcium, albumin, depression, smoking, 25(OH)₂D₃, or sleep quality, while the evidence for BMI remained borderline and limited. Substantial heterogeneity and small study numbers for several factors warrant cautious interpretation of the magnitude and consistency of these estimates.

The pooled prevalence of 60.0% is broadly consistent with previous estimates, including the global pooled prevalence of 55% (95% CI: 49–61%) reported by Hu et al. [1] and findings from the Dialysis Outcomes and Practice Patterns Study (DOPPS), which reported prevalence rates ranging from 52% to 62% among hemodialysis patients [2]. The slightly higher prevalence observed in the present analysis may reflect the inclusion of more recent studies, a relatively higher proportion of Asian populations—who have been reported to experience greater symptom burden—and variability in assessment instruments. Although subgroup analyses did not identify statistically significant differences in prevalence across continents, sample sizes, or diagnostic tools, meta-regression also failed to explain the observed heterogeneity using these study-level covariates. This suggests that residual heterogeneity is likely driven by unmeasured patient-level factors, including age distribution, dialysis duration, inflammatory burden, and center-specific dialysis practices, rather than broad methodological differences.

Among the biochemical factors examined, serum phosphorus was associated with pruritus in analyses stratified by variable type, with pooled ORs of 2.03 (95% CI: 1.44–2.87) for continuous-variable studies and 2.93 (95% CI: 1.83–4.69) for dichotomised studies. When restricted to high-quality studies, the continuous-variable estimate was attenuated to 1.95 (95% CI: 1.32–2.88), while substantial heterogeneity persisted (I2 = 74%); the dichotomised estimate remained unchanged because all three contributing studies were of high quality. A notable finding was the regional variation observed among continuous-variable studies: the pooled association was stronger in Asian studies (OR = 2.27, 95% CI: 1.78–2.90) than in non-Asian studies (OR = 1.46, 95% CI: 0.84–2.53), and exploratory meta-regression identified study location as a potential moderator (P = 0.002). This variation may reflect differences in dietary phosphorus intake [40], phosphate-binder use [41], dialysis adequacy [42], or other population-level characteristics. However, because only two non-Asian studies were available and the continuous-variable analysis remained heterogeneous, this finding should be regarded as hypothesis-generating. The observed association is broadly consistent with previous observational evidence [7], although post hoc analyses of the KALM trials found no correlation between serum phosphorus and itch intensity [43]; therefore, the present findings should not be interpreted as evidence that phosphate is a direct pruritogen or that lowering serum phosphorus will necessarily improve CKD-aP.

The association between PTH/iPTH and pruritus differed according to variable definition. Continuous-variable studies did not demonstrate a clear association and showed substantial heterogeneity (I² = 93.1%), whereas studies using dichotomised PTH/iPTH definitions showed higher odds of pruritus (OR = 2.08, 95% CI: 1.35–3.22. This association persisted after restriction to high-quality studies (OR = 2.63, 95% CI: 1.36–5.10), although moderate heterogeneity remained. These findings suggest that the apparent PTH–pruritus association may be sensitive to how PTH is categorised and to study-specific thresholds rather than reflecting a uniform linear relationship. PTH levels also show substantial biological variability in patients receiving hemodialysis, and single measurements may not adequately represent long-term exposure [44]. Therefore, the role of PTH in CKD-aP remains uncertain and requires further studies using standardised exposure definitions.

The association between CRP and pruritus was highly uncertain in the primary analyses. Both continuous-variable studies (OR = 3.14, 95% CI: 0.30–32.60; I² = 95.6%) and dichotomised studies (OR = 2.18, 95% CI: 0.92–5.16; I² = 84.6%) produced imprecise and heterogeneous estimates. However, after restriction to three high-quality dichotomised studies, the pooled association became stronger (OR = 4.12, 95% CI: 2.38–7.12) and heterogeneity decreased to 0%. This substantial change suggests that methodological quality may have contributed to the variability in the dichotomised estimates, although the small number of high-quality studies warrants cautious interpretation. CRP is a nonspecific inflammatory marker, and previous studies have linked higher CRP and other inflammatory markers with CKD-aP [16, 45]; however, the present findings do not establish a direct causal role of inflammation in pruritus. Differences in CRP thresholds, measurement approaches, patient characteristics, and comorbidity burden may also contribute to the observed variability [46, 47].

The association between dialysis vintage and pruritus varied according to how dialysis duration was analysed. Studies treating dialysis vintage as a continuous variable showed only a small pooled association (OR = 1.03, 95% CI: 1.00–1.06) with substantial heterogeneity (I² = 89.3%), whereas the two studies using dichotomised definitions yielded a larger and homogeneous estimate (OR = 2.62, 95% CI: 1.93–3.56; I² = 0%). Because all contributing studies were of high methodological quality, study quality was unlikely to explain this difference. Instead, variation in cut-off values, measurement units, patient characteristics, and possible nonlinear relationships may contribute to the discrepant estimates. Dialysis vintage may therefore be better regarded as a marker of cumulative disease and treatment exposure rather than an established causal factor for CKD-aP.

The association between β₂-microglobulin and pruritus also differed according to variable definition. Dichotomised studies showed higher odds of pruritus (OR = 2.64, 95% CI: 1.46–4.77) with low heterogeneity (I² = 19.7%), whereas continuous-variable studies yielded a weaker, non-significant estimate (OR = 1.21, 95% CI: 0.88–1.68) with substantial heterogeneity (I² = 92.5%). All contributing studies were of high methodological quality, suggesting that study quality was unlikely to account for this inconsistency. Narita et al. similarly identified higher β₂-microglobulin levels as an independent correlate of severe pruritus [7]; however, the present subgroup difference should not be interpreted as evidence of a biological threshold, particularly because each subgroup contained only two studies. Differences in cut-off values, measurement approaches, and patient characteristics may contribute to the observed variability, and β₂-microglobulin may reflect middle-molecule retention or cumulative dialysis-related exposure rather than acting as a specific pruritogenic mediator.

For age, studies using dichotomised definitions suggested higher odds of pruritus among older patients (OR = 2.73, 95% CI: 1.77–4.22), with no observed heterogeneity, whereas only one study analysed age as a continuous variable, precluding a separate pooled estimate. In contrast, no clear associations were observed for serum calcium after exclusion of the moderate-quality dichotomised study (OR = 0.76, 95% CI: 0.26–2.22) or for albumin among the two studies analysing it continuously (OR = 1.33, 95% CI: 0.07–23.97). Substantial heterogeneity persisted for both calcium and albumin, and the albumin estimate was particularly imprecise. These findings suggest that the evidence for age may depend on how the variable is categorised, whereas the available evidence for serum calcium and albumin remains inconclusive because of limited study numbers, substantial heterogeneity, and imprecision.

Diabetes mellitus and high-flux hemodialysis showed comparatively consistent associations with CKD-aP, with no observed heterogeneity in either analysis. Diabetes was associated with higher odds of pruritus (OR = 2.64, 95% CI: 1.69–4.14), which may be related to peripheral neuropathy, microvascular dysfunction, and chronic low-grade inflammation [48, 49]. In contrast, high-flux hemodialysis was inversely associated with pruritus (OR = 0.31, 95% CI: 0.17–0.56). Enhanced clearance of middle-molecular-weight solutes, including β₂-microglobulin, provides a biologically plausible explanation for this association [50, 51]; however, because each estimate was based on only two observational studies, these findings should be interpreted cautiously and should not be considered evidence of causality or treatment efficacy.

The evidence for depression, BMI, smoking history, 25(OH)₂D₃, and sleep quality was less conclusive. Most pooled estimates were based on only two studies and were accompanied by substantial heterogeneity, particularly for depression, smoking, 25(OH)₂D₃, and sleep quality (I² = 91–98%). Wide confidence intervals for several factors further indicate limited precision. Accordingly, the absence of clear pooled associations should not be interpreted as evidence that these factors are unrelated to CKD-aP. Differences in measurement methods, exposure definitions, patient characteristics, and residual confounding may have contributed to the observed inconsistency, and additional studies using standardized definitions are needed.

More broadly, CKD-aP is increasingly understood as a multifactorial disorder involving interactions among metabolic abnormalities, immune dysregulation, peripheral sensory pathways, and opioid signalling. Neuroimmune mechanisms may contribute to pruriceptive signalling; for example, circulating interleukin-31 has been associated with the presence and severity of uremic pruritus in patients receiving hemodialysis [52]. Opioid signalling represents another relevant pathway, particularly through peripheral κ-opioid receptors. In a randomized phase 3 trial, the peripherally restricted κ-opioid receptor agonist difelikefalin significantly reduced itch intensity and improved itch-related quality of life in hemodialysis patients with moderate-to-severe pruritus [53]. These findings support the involvement of neuroimmune and opioid pathways in CKD-aP, although neither observational biomarker associations nor therapeutic responses establish a single causal mechanism. Accordingly, the biochemical correlates identified in the present meta-analysis may represent components or markers of a broader neuroimmune–metabolic network rather than direct pruritogenic factors.

A notable feature of this meta-analysis is the predominance of studies conducted in China (19 of 25, 76% of included studies). This likely reflects both the large hemodialysis population in China and active research output in this field [54]. However, this geographic concentration may limit the generalizability of the findings to other populations with differing genetic backgrounds, dietary patterns, dialysis practices, and healthcare systems [55]. The observed regional variation in the phosphorus–pruritus association further supports the possibility of effect modification by population-level factors.

The observed associations involving serum phosphorus and selected categorical definitions of CRP and β₂-microglobulin suggest that these routinely measured biomarkers may help identify potentially relevant clinical phenotypes; however, the substantial heterogeneity and coding-dependent results limit their immediate clinical interpretation. Nevertheless, given the predominance of cross-sectional data, causal inferences cannot be established. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than directive for clinical intervention. Prospective cohort studies and interventional trials are needed to determine whether modification of these biochemical parameters can reduce pruritus severity and improve patient-reported outcomes.

Limitations

Several limitations of this study merit acknowledgement. First, most included studies were cross-sectional, which prevents causal inference; elevated phosphorus or CRP could be a consequence rather than a cause of pruritus (e.g., through sleep deprivation-induced metabolic changes). Second, heterogeneity remained high even after exploring multiple moderators, indicating that unmeasured confounders—such as dialysis adequacy (Kt/V), dialyzer membrane type, specific medication use, and detailed pruritus severity—likely play a role. Third, pruritus assessment tools varied widely, and we lacked individual patient data to harmonize definitions or adjust for confounders at the patient level. Fourth, the predominance of studies conducted in China (19 of 25; 76%) may limit the generalizability of our prevalence estimate and some associated factor associations in other ethnic groups. Fifth, several pooled analyses were based on fewer than three studies, rendering these estimates potentially unstable and precluding meaningful subgroup analyses or meta-regression for those factors. Future updates with additional primary studies are needed to confirm or refute these associations. Sixth, publication bias was detected for serum phosphorus, and although the trim-and-fill analysis confirmed robustness, the existence of unpublished studies with null results cannot be excluded. Finally, we only included studies in English and Chinese, potentially missing relevant literature in other languages.

Conclusions

This systematic review and meta-analysis shows that pruritus remains highly prevalent among patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis, affecting approximately 60% of individuals. Serum phosphorus showed the most consistent positive association with CKD-associated pruritus, whereas the associations for PTH/iPTH, CRP, β₂-microglobulin, dialysis vintage, and age varied according to variable definition, methodological quality, and between-study heterogeneity. Diabetes mellitus was associated with higher odds of pruritus, while high-flux hemodialysis showed an inverse association. Exploratory meta-regression suggested that study location may modify the phosphorus–pruritus association; however, this regional finding should be considered hypothesis-generating because of the limited number of non-Asian studies.

Given the predominance of cross-sectional evidence and the substantial heterogeneity observed for several factors, causal inferences cannot be established. Future prospective studies using standardized exposure definitions, harmonized reporting methods, and validated pruritus assessment tools are needed to clarify these associations and their potential clinical relevance.

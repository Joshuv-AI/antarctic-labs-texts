1.
Introduction

One of the classical problems in harmonic analysis is the study of the Fourier multipliers, which aims to characterize the behaviour of certain bounded operators on function spaces using the Fourier transform. The well-known Hörmander multiplier theorem
[
8
]
provided sufficient conditions for a measurable function to be a
L^{p}
-
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
Fourier multiplier for
1\leq p,q<\infty
. One of the well-known results in characterizing
L^{1}
-
L^{p}
multipliers for a locally compact abelian group
G
,
states that an operator
T
from
L^{1}(G)
to
L^{p}(G),\ 1\leq p<\infty
, which commutes with translations is given by
Tf=f*\nu
for
\nu\in M(G)
if
p=1
and
\nu\in L^{p}(G)
if
1<p<\infty
. More generally, we have the following result for
L^{1}
-multipliers.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
11
]
, Theorem 0.1.1)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group, and suppose
T:L^{1}(G)\rightarrow L^{1}(G)
is a continuous linear transformation. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T
commutes with the translation operators.

(ii)

T(f*g)=Tf*g
for each
f,g\in L^{1}(G)
.

(iii)

There exists a unique function
\phi
defined on
\widehat{G}
such that
(Tf)^{\wedge}=\phi\hat{f}
for each
f\in L^{1}(G)
.

(iv)

There exists a unique measure
\mu\in M(G)
such that
(Tf)^{\wedge}=\hat{\mu}\hat{f}
for each
f\in L^{1}(G)
.

(v)

There exists a unique measure
\mu\in M(G)
such that
Tf=f*\mu
for each
f\in L^{1}(G)
.

Over the last few decades, exploring vector-valued functions has gained widespread popularity. Akinyele
[
1
]
considered the problem of providing a vector version of the characterizations of
the multipliers for
L^{1}(G)
under the assumption that Banach algebra is commutative and semi-simple. Chan further considered this problem in
[
4
]

for vector-valued
L^{1}
-multipliers on a locally compact group for a Banach algebra with a bounded approximate identity. Later, Radha in
[
13
]
studied
L^{1}
-
L^{p}
multipliers for commutative complex Banach algebras with a bounded
approximate identity.

In
[
5
]
, Michele and Mauceri proved a version of the Hörmander’s multiplier theorem for the Heisenberg group. The study of the
Heisenberg group is closely related to the study of the Weyl transform introduced by Hermann Weyl in
[
20
]
. It is a pseudo-differential operator associated to a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which has applications both in quantum mechanics and partial differential equations. Mauceri
[
12
]
introduced the concept of the Weyl multipliers and proved an analogue of the
Hörmander’s multiplier theorem. Thangavelu in
[
19
]
studied the Weyl multipliers using Littlewood-Paley-Stein theory and also obtained a characterization for
L^{p}
-Weyl multipliers using pseudo measures in
[
15
]
. In the last few decades, there have been several other studies for the multiplier theory of the Heisenberg group and Weyl multipliers
[
3
,
10
,
16
,
17
]
. The problem gets interesting because, unlike the Fourier transform,
the multiplier associated with the Weyl transform is
vector-valued.

Using twisted convolution and Radon measures, a characterization similar to Theorem
1.1
was obtained in
[
14
]
for the Weyl multipliers.
Motivated by this, we will provide a vector-valued characterization of the Weyl multipliers, assuming that the Banach algebra under consideration has a bounded approximate identity. It is important to mention that, unlike the classical case, the twisted convolution defined for the Weyl transform is not commutative, even though the underlying group and the Banach algebra are both commutative.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group with
\widehat{G}
as its dual group. For
1\leq p<\infty
, we will denote by
L^{p}(G)
, the classical
L^{p}
-spaces on
G
with respect to the Haar measure on
G
.
C_{c}(G)
represents the space of all compactly supported functions on
G
and
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G))
denotes the Banach algebra of all bounded operators on
L^{2}(G)
.

The
Weyl transform
, denoted
W,
is defined as a
\mathcal{B}\left(L^{2}(G)\right)
-valued integral on
C_{c}(G\times\widehat{G})
given by

W(f)\varphi(y)=\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f(x,\chi)\rho((x,\chi))(\varphi)(y)dxd\chi,f\in C_{c}(G\times\widehat{G})

where
\rho(x,\chi)
is motivated from the
Schrödinger representation
of the abstract Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}(G)
on
L^{2}(G)
and defined as

\rho((x,\chi))(\varphi)(y)=\chi(y)\varphi(xy),\varphi\in L^{2}(G).

By density of compactly supported functions, the Weyl transform can be defined for
L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G}),1\leq p<\infty
. Similarly, the Weyl transform for any bounded regular
complex Borel measure
\nu
in
M(G\times\widehat{G})
can be defined as a bounded map on
L^{2}(G)
given by

W(\nu)\phi(y)=\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}(\rho(x,\chi)\phi)(y)\ d\nu(x,\chi),\phi\in L^{2}(G).

For more details on Weyl transform, one can refer to
[
6
,
14
,
22
]
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
1\leq p<\infty.
If
T:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is a compact operator, then it admits an orthonormal representation

T=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sum}S_{T}(n)\left\langle.,e_{n}\right\rangle\sigma_{n},

where
\{e_{n}\}
and
\{\sigma_{n}\}
are orthonormal sequences in
\mathcal{H}
and
S_{T}(n)
denotes the
n^{th}
-singular value of
T
. The
p^{th}
-Schatten-von Neumann class
, denoted
\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathcal{H}),
consists of all compact operators,
T:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
with
\{S_{T}(n)\}\in\ell^{p}.

For
T\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathcal{H}),
define a norm

\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathcal{H})}:=\left(\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sum}|S_{T}(n)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}=\|\{S_{T}(n)\}\|_{l^{p}}.

The space
\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
with the above norm becomes a Banach space. We will denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
, the space of all compact operators on
\mathcal{H}.

In order to define an algebra structure on
C_{c}(G\times\widehat{G})
, we will now define the concept of twisted convolution.
For
f,g\in C_{c}(G\times\widehat{G}),
the
twisted convolution
, denoted
f\times g,
is defined as

\displaystyle f\times g(x,\chi):=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f\left(xx^{\prime-1},\chi\overline{\chi^{\prime}}\right)g\left(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}\right)\chi^{\prime}(xx^{\prime-1})dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f\left(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}\right)g\left(xx^{\prime-1},\chi\overline{\chi^{\prime}}\right)\left(\chi\overline{\chi^{\prime}}\right)(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}.

The following theorem gives the integrability properties of the Weyl transform with respect to the twisted convolution structure. For
1\leq p<\infty,
throughout
p^{\prime}
will denote the conjugate exponent of
p
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
14
]

(i)

For
p\in\{1,2\}
, the space
L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G})
is a Banach algebra with pointwise addition and twisted convolution as addition and multiplication respectively.

(ii)

(Plancherel Theorem) The Weyl transform is an isometric Banach algebra isomorphism between
L^{2}(G\times\widehat{G})
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}(L^{2}(G)).

(iii)

(Riemann-Lebesque Lemma) The Weyl transform is a bounded operator and a Banach algebra homomorphism from
L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G})
to
\mathcal{B}_{\infty}\left(L^{2}(G)\right).

(iv)

(Hausdorff-Young inequality) If
1<p<2,
then the Weyl transform maps
L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G})
into
\mathcal{B}_{p^{\prime}}(L^{2}(G))
.

To simplify the twisted convolution, we now introduce the notion of twisted translation, an analogue for the classical translation. For
(x,\chi)\in G\times\widehat{G}
, the twisted translates of
f:G\times\widehat{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are defined as follows

\displaystyle\left(T^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}f\right)(x,\chi)=

\displaystyle f\left(xx^{\prime-1},\chi\overline{\chi^{\prime}}\right)\chi^{\prime}(xx^{\prime-1});

\displaystyle\left(L^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}f\right)(x,\chi)=

\displaystyle f\left(xx^{\prime-1},\chi\overline{\chi^{\prime}}\right)\chi\overline{\chi^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}),

so that

\displaystyle f\times g(x,\chi):=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left(T^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}f\right)(x,\chi)g\left(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}\right)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle;=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f\left(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}\right)\left(L^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}g\right)(x,\chi)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}.

Clearly, for
1\leq p<\infty
, twisted translation is an isometry from
L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G})
to itself.
Note that the need to define the operator
L^{t}
arises because, unlike the classical translation,

\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left(T^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}f\right)(x,\chi)g\left(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}\right)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}\neq\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f\left(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}\right)\left(T^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}g\right)(x,\chi)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}.

The following lemma shows that the twisted translation operator occurs naturally in the study of the Weyl transform.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
(x,\chi)\in G\times\widehat{G}
and
f\in L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G}),1\leq p<\infty
, we have

(i)

W\left(T_{(x,\chi)}^{t}f\right)=W(f)\rho(x,\chi)
;

(ii)

W\left(L_{(x,\chi)}^{t}f\right)=\rho(x,\chi)W(f)
.

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in L^{2}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle(W(f)\rho(x,\chi))(\varphi)(y)=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})(\rho(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}))(\rho(x,\chi)\varphi)(y)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})\chi^{\prime}(y)\left(\rho(x,\chi)\varphi\right)(x^{\prime}y)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})\chi^{\prime}(y)\chi(x^{\prime}y)\varphi(xx^{\prime}y)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}.

Consider the map
(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})\to(x^{\prime}x^{-1},\chi^{\prime}\overline{\chi}).
Since the measure on
G\times\widehat{G}
is Haar measure, we have

\displaystyle(W(f)\rho(x,\chi))(\varphi)(y)=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f(x^{\prime}x^{-1},\chi^{\prime}\overline{\chi})\left(\chi^{\prime}\overline{\chi}\right)(y)\chi(x^{\prime}x^{-1}y)\varphi(x^{\prime}y)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f(x^{\prime}x^{-1},\chi^{\prime}\overline{\chi})\chi^{\prime}(y)\chi(x^{\prime})\overline{\chi}(x)\varphi(x^{\prime}y)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left(f(x^{\prime}x^{-1},\chi^{\prime}\overline{\chi})\chi(x^{\prime})\overline{\chi}(x)\right)\chi^{\prime}(y)\varphi(x^{\prime}y)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left(T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}f\right)(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})(\rho(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}))(\varphi)(y)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(W(T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}f))(\varphi)(y).

Similarly, one can prove
(ii)
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let

be a continuous linear operator, where

is a Weyl multiplier if it satisfies the following conditions.

(i)

TT^{t}_{(x,\chi)}=T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}T
for all
(x,\chi)\in G\times\widehat{G}

(ii)

T(fa)=T(f)a
for all
a\in A,f\in L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A)

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

, then for all

, we have

(1)

T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}(f\times g)=f\times T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}g.

For

\displaystyle T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}(f\times g)(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})=

\displaystyle f\times g(x^{\prime}x^{-1},\chi^{\prime}\overline{\chi})\chi(x^{\prime}x^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f(x^{\prime}x^{-1}x^{\prime\prime-1},\chi^{\prime}\overline{\chi\chi^{\prime\prime}})\chi^{\prime\prime}(x^{\prime}x^{-1}x^{\prime\prime-1})g(x^{\prime\prime},\chi^{\prime\prime})\chi(x^{\prime}x^{-1})dx^{\prime\prime}d\chi^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle f\times T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}g(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}).\qed

Lemma 3.1

The space

4.
Characterization of
L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A)
-
L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)
Weyl multipliers

In this section, we will study the vector-valued version of the
L^{1}
-
L^{p}
Weyl multipliers under the assumption that
A
is commutative Banach algebra with bounded approximate identity.

For
1\leq p<\infty,
any function
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*})
defines a linear functional
\phi_{g}\in\left(L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)\right)^{*}
by the formula

\left\langle f,\phi_{g}\right\rangle:=\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\langle f(x,\chi),g(x,\chi)\rangle dxd\chi,f\in L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A).

Recall that a set
Y\subseteq A^{*}
is said to be norming for
A
if
\underset{f\in Y\setminus\{0\}}{\sup}\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{A^{*}}}=\|x\|_{A}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. The mapping
g\mapsto\phi_{g}
defines an isometry of
L^{p^{\prime}}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*})
onto a closed subspace of
\left(L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)\right)^{*}
which is norming for
L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)
.

We will denote
\phi_{g}
by
g
when the notation is obvious.

To study the characterization of the vector-valued case of Weyl multipliers, we first need to define a product
\divideontimes
for
f\in L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A)
and
\nu\in\left(L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*})\right)^{*},1<p<\infty
such that if
\nu\in L^{p^{\prime}}(G\times\widehat{G};A)
, then
\nu\divideontimes f=\nu\times f
. For this, we will use duality as follows.

For
f\in L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A),g\in L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)
and
h\in L^{p^{\prime}}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*})
, consider

\displaystyle\left\langle f\times g,h\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left\langle f\times g(x,\chi),h(x,\chi)\right\rangle dxd\chi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left\langle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}f(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})L^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}g(x,\chi)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime},h(x,\chi)\right\rangle dxd\chi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left(\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left\langle f(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})L^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}g(x,\chi),h(x,\chi)\right\rangle dxd\chi\right)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left(\left\langle f(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}),\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}L^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}g(x,\chi)\odot h(x,\chi)dxd\chi\right\rangle\right)dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}.

So, if we define
g\oplus h(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}):=\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}L^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}g(x,\chi)\odot h(x,\chi)dxd\chi
, we get

\left\langle f\times g,h\right\rangle=\left\langle f,g\oplus h\right\rangle.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
f\in L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A)
and
g\in L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*}),1<p<\infty
, define

f\oplus g(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}):=\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}L^{t}_{(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}f(x,\chi)\odot g(x,\chi)dxd\chi.

Then
f\oplus g\in L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*})
.

Proof.

For
f\in L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A)
and
g\in L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*})
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\oplus g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*})}=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\|f\oplus g(x,\chi)\|^{p}_{A^{*}}dxd\chi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left(\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\|f(xx^{\prime-1},\chi\overline{\chi^{\prime}})\|_{A}^{p-1}(\|f(xx^{\prime-1},\chi\overline{\chi^{\prime}})\|_{A}\|g(x,\chi)\|_{A^{*}}^{p})dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}\right)dxd\chi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A)}^{p/p^{\prime}}\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left(\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}(\|f(xx^{\prime-1},\chi\overline{\chi^{\prime}})\|_{A}\|g(x,\chi)\|_{A^{*}}^{p})dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}\right)dxd\chi.

Now by applying Fubini’s theorem, we get

\|f\oplus g\|_{L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A)}\|g\|_{L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*})}.

∎

Definition 4.3
.

For
1<p<\infty
, let
f\in L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A)
and
\nu\in\left(L^{p^{\prime}}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*})\right)^{*}
. Define

\left\langle\nu\divideontimes f,h\right\rangle:=\nu(f\oplus h)\ \forall\ h\in L^{p^{\prime}}(G\times\widehat{G};A^{*}).

Remark 4.4
.

The way
f\oplus h
is constructed, it is clear that if in particular
\nu\in L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)
, then
\nu\divideontimes f=\nu\times f
.

For every
a\in A
, define an operator
\Lambda_{a}
on
A
as

(4)

\Lambda_{a}(f):=af,f\in L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A).

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
A
be commutative Banach algebra with an approximate identity. For
1<p<\infty,
let

be a continuous linear operator. Then
T
is a Weyl multiplier iff

satisfies the following:

(5)

T(\Lambda_{a}T^{t}_{(x,\chi)})=(\Lambda_{a}T^{t}_{(x,\chi)})T\ \forall a\in A\text{ and }(x,\chi)\in G\times\widehat{G}.

Proof.

Let
T
be a Weyl multiplier. Then for all

and
(x,\chi)\in G\times\widehat{G}

\displaystyle T(\Lambda_{a}T^{t}_{(x,\chi)})(f)=T(aT^{t}_{(x,\chi)}(f))=aT(T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}(f))=aT^{t}_{(x,\chi)}(Tf)=(\Lambda_{a}T^{t}_{(x,\chi)})T(f).

Conversly, for

(6)

T(af)=T((\Lambda_{a}T^{t}_{(e,\mathbf{1})})(f))=(\Lambda_{a}T^{t}_{(e,\mathbf{1})})(T(f))=aT(f).

Now, let

and

\displaystyle\|T\left(e_{\beta}T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}(f_{\alpha}\times f)\right)-T(T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}f)\|_{L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)}=

\displaystyle\|T\|\|e_{\beta}f_{\alpha}\times T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}f-T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}f\|_{L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A)}.

(7)

\displaystyle\|e_{\beta}T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}(T(f_{\alpha}\times f))-T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}(Tf)\|_{L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)}=

\displaystyle\|T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}(e_{\beta}T(f_{\alpha}\times f))-T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}(Tf)\|_{L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}\left(e_{\beta}T(f_{\alpha}\times f)-Tf\right)\|_{L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|T(e_{\beta}f_{\alpha}\times f)-Tf\|_{L^{p}(G\times\widehat{G};A)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T\|\|e_{\beta}f_{\alpha}\times f-f\|_{L^{1}(G\times\widehat{G};A)}.

Thus,

(8)

Now using equations (
5
), (
7
) and (

), we get
TT^{t}_{(x,\chi)}=T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}T
for all

.
∎

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left\langle Tf(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}),h(x^{\prime}x,\chi^{\prime}\chi)\chi(x^{\prime})\right\rangle dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left\langle Tf(x^{\prime}x^{-1},\chi^{\prime}\overline{\chi}),h(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})\chi(x^{\prime}x^{-1})\right\rangle dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left\langle T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}Tf(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}),h(x^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})\right\rangle dx^{\prime}d\chi^{\prime},

for all

. Therefore
TT^{t}_{(x,\chi)}=T^{t}_{(x,\chi)}T
for every

.

By using the commutativity of
A

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left\langle\nu\divideontimes f(x,\chi),a\odot h(x,\chi)\right\rangle dxd\chi.

\displaystyle\nu(af\oplus h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu(f\oplus h_{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\times\widehat{G}}\left\langle aT(f)(x,\chi),h(x,\chi)\right\rangle dxd\chi

for all

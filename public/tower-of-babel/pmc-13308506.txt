From local tissue repair to systemic precision orthopaedics: recent advances in musculoskeletal regeneration and translational medicine
Authors: Shunyi Lu, Qin Shi, Huilin Yang
Published: 2026

Orthopedic research is undergoing a profound conceptual shift, moving from the traditional research mode focused on structural repair towards a new era of precision regenerative medicine driven by systems biology. Increasing evidence indicates that musculoskeletal diseases are not confined to local tissue damage, but rather represent complex systemic diseases that are jointly regulated by multidimensional networks encompassing neural, immune, metabolic, vascular and mechanical factors. Against this backdrop, the May issue of the Journal of Orthopaedic Translation comprises a total of 33 original research papers and review articles, systematically presenting the latest advances and future trends in the field of orthopaedic translational medicine across multiple dimensions, including neuro–bone interactions, metabolic reprogramming, systemic organ interactions, intelligent biomaterials, and precision diagnosis and treatment.

A salient theme of this issue is the pivotal regulatory function of the nervous system in skeletal homeostasis and tissue regeneration. Liu and coworkers have systematically summarized the bidirectional roles of neurotransmitters, neuropeptides, immune mediators, and osteogenic signals in bone metabolism and neural regulation. The focus of the study was on the participation of osteogenic signals, such as piezo type mechanosensitive ion channel component 1 (Piezo1) and osteocalcin in neural modulation. The investigation further explored the potential of smart materials, including nanomaterials, hydrogels, and bioelectronic devices in the precise intervention of the brain–bone axis [1]. Consequently, the review by Yang systematically reviewed the development, biomechanical characteristics, and repair mechanisms at the tendon–muscle junction, emphasizing the importance of the complex mechanotransduction and cellular signaling coordination between muscle and tendon for functional tissue recovery [2]. Collectively, these studies illustrate that orthopedic research is transcending the conventional constraints of 'structural repair' and progressively evolving towards a novel paradigm of integrated neuro-muscle-bone functional reconstruction.

A plethora of original studies have further elucidated the critical function of neural regulation in the process of bone repair. He and his colleagues identified 303 functional deer antler peptides through the use of LC-MS/MS, and it was determined that these peptides can promote fracture healing by activating the sensory nerve PGE2/EP4/p-CREB signaling axis [3]. In addition, Pang and coworkers have expanded upon the regulatory function of the calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) neuropeptide in abnormal periosteal proliferation [4]. Original research by Yan demonstrated that Talin1 deficiency can activate dorsal root ganglion neurons, thereby promoting bone remodeling and fracture repair [5]. Collectively, these studies indicate that the nervous system not only participates in maintaining bone tissue homeostasis but also serves as a crucial driver of tissue regeneration.

The present issue places particular emphasis on the subjects of neural regeneration and the repair of spinal cord injuries. The systemic theoretical framework of the “spinal cord injury scar microenvironment” was proposed by Hou and coauthors, who emphasized that glial scars and fibrotic scars do not simply hinder regeneration but play both protective and inhibitory roles at different stages [6]. Furthermore, Jin provided a comprehensive overview of the potential applications of functionalized mesenchymal stem cells in enhancing neural repair [7].

In the domain of tissue regeneration, Maimaiti has developed a polypyrrole/silk protein/GelMA conductive dual-crosslinked hydrogel that has the capacity to promote myotube formation, angiogenesis, and neural reinnervation concurrently. This advancement has the potential to enhance functional recovery following large-volume muscle defects [8]. In addition, Li et al. revealed the key regulatory role of a special class of Type E endothelial cells in embryonic skeletal development. The study found that Type E endothelial cells not only participate in angiogenesis but can also coordinate the process of embryonic skeletal development by promoting chondrogenic priming of skeletal progenitor cells [9].

Metabolic reprogramming and mitochondrial homeostasis represent further significant themes in this field. Zhang's review proposes a unified framework of the “glycolysis–mitochondrial metabolism–osteoarthritis axis” emphasizing that abnormal glucose metabolism in chondrocytes is an important mechanism driving the progression of osteoarthritis and suggesting that dual-target metabolic intervention strategies may become a new direction for precise osteoarthritis treatment [10]. Furthermore, the study by Xu revealed the important role of the RhoA/SOX4/MMP2 signaling axis in cartilage fibrosis and the progression of osteoarthritis. This study further illustrates that cartilage fibrosis remodeling is not only an important pathological feature of osteoarthritis but may also become an important intervention target for precise osteoarthritis treatment in the future [11].

In addition, the issue contains a number of leading reviews of current thinking on skeletal aging and metabolic regulation. In this study, Chen and colleagues provided a systematic summary of the role of irisin in maintaining protein metabolism balance, improving mitochondrial function, and inhibiting cell death [12]. The authors highlight the potential of irisin as a therapeutic candidate for sarcopenia. Furthermore, Song's review focuses on the emerging epigenetic regulatory layer of m6A RNA methylation, systematically discussing its roles in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells, osteoblasts, and osteoclasts. The author points that m6A-related “writers”, “erasers” and “readers” may become novel drug targets for osteoporosis and sarcopenia [13].

Multiple original studies have revealed the critical role of metabolic regulation in bone homeostasis at the mechanistic level. Cao's research established that Thioredoxin-interacting protein (Txnip) plays a pivotal role in regulating the balance between glycolysis and the tricarboxylic acid cycle, thereby ensuring the maintenance of bone homeostasis [14]. Mei and coauthors revealed a previously unrecognized Pink1/Prkn–MPI–M6P metabolic axis. The study found that specific deletion of Pink1 or Prkn in bone cells significantly exacerbated glucocorticoid-induced bone loss, accompanied by a decrease in M6P metabolism, whereas external supplementation with D-mannose could alleviate bone loss [15]. Concurrently, Jiang and coworkers demonstrated that restoring NAD+/NADH homeostasis could ameliorate mitochondrial dysfunction in tendinopathy; Huang and colleagues revealed a novel post-transcriptional regulatory mechanism between mitochondrial DNA release and cartilage inflammation [16] [17]. Collectively, these studies suggest that metabolic abnormalities are not merely concomitant phenomena of disease but rather central driving forces in the occurrence and development of musculoskeletal diseases.

This issue also emphasizes the role of systemic organ interactions in bone metabolic diseases. Qin and coworkers have systematically summarized the mechanisms by which the gut microbiota regulates bone homeostasis from the perspectives of immunity, metabolism, and neural networks. The focus of the review is on the roles of short-chain fatty acids, bile acids, tryptophan metabolites, and Treg/Th17 immune balance in bone diseases [18]. Wen and colleagues further expanded the cross-organ interaction network between the bone and the liver, adipose tissue, muscle, and nervous system [19]. Ma and coauthors emphasized the shared inflammatory, metabolic, and mechanical vicious cycles between sarcopenia and osteoarthritis, and proposed a framework for digital twin models, real-time biomechanical feedback, and multi-omics precision therapy [20].

Intelligent biomaterials and regenerative engineering are also significant highlights of this issue. Wang's review systematically summarized the ways in which topologically optimized metal implants can improve bone integration and immune modulation through mechanical adaptation, surface modification, and topological structure design [21]. In contrast, the review by Dong focuses on the role of piezoelectric materials in promoting bone repair through mechano-electrical signal conversion [22]. Luo and coworkers systematically reviewed the development of functionally graded hip scaffold–implant hybrids (FG-HSIHs), linking major failure mechanisms of total hip replacement with biomechanical and biological requirements, and highlighting multiphysics modeling and simulation-driven design as key strategies for next-generation implant optimization, thereby providing a translational framework for improving long-term implant integration and reducing revision risk [23]. Cheng's work has proposed a novel strategy for modifying polyether ether ketone (PEEK) to make it resistant to microbial contamination without the need for the addition of antibiotics [24]. Zhang and coauthors, based on the stress-driven design concept, systematically optimized the mechanical performance, biocompatibility, and tissue integration ability of implants, proposing a new intervertebral disc regeneration strategy that considers both mechanical stability and biological function. This study reflects that orthopedic implant design is gradually moving from simple structural replacement toward a precise engineering model of mechanical–biological synergistic optimization [25].

Precision imaging and translational diagnostics represent significant components of this issue. The review by Chen and coauthors explores the potential application of chemical exchange saturation transfer (CEST) magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in the early diagnosis of intervertebral disc degeneration [26]. Bammessel's original research established a computed tomography (CT) calibration method that does not require the use of standard phantoms. This method enables quantitative analysis of skeletal muscle density, fat infiltration and muscle mass. Furthermore, a high correlation was demonstrated with MRI Dixon results. This provides a new tool for opportunistic screening of sarcopenia and osteoporosis [27]. Li and collaborators have unveiled a pioneering positron emission tomography (PET)-CT technique that offers a non-invasive means of monitoring the functional dynamics of skeletal stem cells [28]. Additionally, Zhang's review proposed the utilization of deep learning and targeted protein degradation technologies to advance precision orthopedic drug development [29]. The clinical study by Gu and coworkers yielded significant phase III clinical evidence for postmenopausal osteoporosis patients in China, thereby providing a foundation for the development of clinical guidelines [30]. Ma and colleagues discovered that telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) is a novel upstream regulator of the BMP4/Smad1 pathway and developed an osteotropic Apt-TERT-Exo exosome system to achieve targeted osteogenic therapy [31]. The review by Liu et al. systematically elucidated the regulatory network of ubiquitination in intervertebral disc degeneration, identifying specific E3 ligases and deubiquitinases as promising therapeutic targets and providing a conceptual foundation for the development of ubiquitination-based precision treatment strategies [32]. The research by Gao revealed that tetratricopeptide repeat protein 26 (TTC26) acts as a scaffold protein promoting the DUB ubiquitin carboxyl-terminal hydrolase-3 (MINDY3)-mediated receptor for activated kinase C-1 (RACK1) deubiquitination. This, in turn, drives osteoclast differentiation and bone resorption. The findings provide a new basis for the development of precision-targeted therapy for osteoporosis [33].

This research demonstrates that orthopaedic translational medicine is gradually advancing from single-structure repair to a new stage of multi-system integrated regulation. This new stage encompasses the “neural–immune–metabolic–skeletal” axis. The deep integration of multiple disciplines, including neurobiology, metabolic regulation, intelligent biomaterials, advanced imaging, and artificial intelligence, is redefining the mechanisms and treatment paradigms of musculoskeletal diseases. In the future, the development of orthopaedic research may increasingly rely on the deep integration of multi-system biology, intelligent regenerative materials, and individualized precision therapies. This integration will achieve dynamic and precise regulation of the regenerative microenvironment and will advance the treatment of complex musculoskeletal diseases into a truly precision regenerative medicine era.

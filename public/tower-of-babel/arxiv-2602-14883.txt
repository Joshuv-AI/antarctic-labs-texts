On the Geometry of Complete Spacelike LW-Submanifolds in Locally Symmetric Semi-Riemannian Spaces Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Jogli G. S. Araújo 1 , Weiller F. C. Barboza ∗,2

Abstract
. Let M n M^{n} be an n n -dimensional complete spacelike linear Weingarten submanifold immersed in a locally symmetric semi-Riemannian space 𝕃 q n + p \mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p} of index q q , with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field and flat normal bundle. Assuming that M n M^{n} satisfies suitable curvature constraints, we investigate rigidity results for such submanifolds. By combining a Simons-type formula for spacelike submanifolds with analytic techniques involving the Cheng–Yau modified operator ℒ \mathcal{L} , we establish sharp inequalities relating the traceless second fundamental form and the gradient of the mean curvature. As applications, we obtain several characterization results showing that M n M^{n} must be either totally umbilical or isoparametric. More precisely, we derive rigidity results under three distinct frameworks: via the Omori–Yau maximum principle, via the ℒ \mathcal{L} -parabolicity of the underlying manifold, and under an integrability condition on the gradient of the mean curvature. These results generalize and unify known classification theorems for spacelike submanifolds satisfying linear Weingarten relations in semi-Riemannian ambient spaces.

1.
Introduction

The study of spacelike submanifolds immersed in a semi-Riemannian space constitutes an important thematic from both physical and mathematical points of view. For instance, it was pointed out by J. Marsden and F. Tipler in
[
26
]
and S. Stumbles in
[
33
]
that spacelike hypersurfaces with constant mean curvature in an arbitrary Lorentzian space (which is a semi-Riemannian space of index
p=1
) play an important role in general relativity, in that they serve as convenient initial data for the Cauchy problem for Einstein equations. Furthermore, submanifold theory provides the adequate tools to approach some important problems involving spacetime singularities and gravitational collapse. The singularity theorems proved in the 1960s by R. Penrose in
[
31
]
and S.W. Hawking and G. F. R. Ellis in
[
20
]
state that the formation of singularities is unavoidable, if one assumes reasonable conditions on the curvature of the spacetime, on the extrinsic geometry of certain submanifolds, and on the causal structure of the Lorentzian manifold. The existence of spacelike submanifolds in the spacetime, in particular, is a key requirement in the original formulation of the singularity theorems as well as in their more recent generalizations. For more details, see G. J. Galloway and J. M. M. Senovilla in
[
18
]
, Z. Liang and X. Zhang in
[
25
]
, and J. M. M. Senovilla in
[
32
]
.

From the mathematical point of view, the interest in the study of the geometry of these submanifolds is mostly due to the fact that they exhibit nice Bernstein
-
type properties, and one can truly say that the first remarkable results in this branch were the rigidity theorems of E. Calabi in
[
7
]
and S. Y. Cheng and S. T. Yau in
[
9
]
, who showed (the former for
n\leq 4
, and the latter for general
n
) that the only complete maximal spacelike hypersurfaces of the Lorentz Minkowski space
L^{n+1}
are the spacelike hyperplanes. However, in the case that the mean curvature is a positive constant, A. E. Treibergs in
[
34
]
astonishingly showed that there are many entire solutions of the corresponding constant mean curvature equation in
L^{n+1}
, which he was able to classify by their projective boundary values at infinity. When the ambient space is the de Sitter space
\mathbb{S}_{1}^{n+1}
, Cheng
[
8
]
, studying the case
b=0

M^{n}
is a complete linear Weingarten spacelike hypersurface with nonnegative sectional curvature such that
H
attains its maximum, then
M^{n}
must be totally umbilical. on the other hand, S. Nishikawa in
[
27
]
extended the results of
[
7
]
and
[

for a wide class of semi-Riemannian spaces, the so-called
locally symmetric semi-Riemannian spaces
. At this point, it is worth recalling that a fundamental property of curvature is its control over the relative behavior of nearby geodesics. Because a normal neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
is filled with radial geodesics, curvature thereby gives a description of the geometry of
\mathcal{U}
. Considering only the locally symmetric case, we have that this description is so accurate that, if
\mathcal{U}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}
are normal neighborhoods with the same description (and same dimension and index), then
\mathcal{U}

[
30

. Returning to our context, we note that the seminal paper
[

induced the appearance of several works approaching the problem of characterizing complete spacelike hypersurfaces immersed in a locally symmetric Lorentzian space, see, for instance,

For higher codimension, considering again the particular case
b=0
, Liu
[
24
]
showed that the totally umbilical round spheres are the only
n
-dimensional compact linear Weingarten spacelike submanifolds of
\mathbb{L}_{p}^{n+p}
with nonnegative sectional curvature and flat normal bundle. Generalizing the ideas of a previous work
[
35
]
, Yang and Hou
[
36

showed that a linear Weingarten spacelike submanifold in
\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n+p}
, with
a>0
,
b<1
, having parallel normalized mean curvature vector field (that is, the mean curvature function is positive and that the corresponding normalized mean curvature vector field is parallel as a section of the normal bundle) and such that the squared norm of its second fundamental form satisfies a suitable boundedness, must be either totally umbilical or isometric to a certain hyperbolic cylinder. On the other hand, still with higher codimensions, T. Ishihara in
[
21

applied a technique developed by S. S. Chern, M. P. do Carmo, and S. Kobayashi in
[
11

in order to extend the results of

investigated complete maximal spacelike submanifolds immersed with flat normal bundle in a locally symmetric semi-Riemannian space obeying curvature conditions similar to those of S. Nishikawa in
[
27

. In this setting, they obtained a suitable Simons type formula and, as application, they showed that such a spacelike submanifold must be totally geodesic or the square norm of its second fundamental form must be bounded, extending the results of T. Ishihara in
[
21
]
and S. Nishikawa in
[
27

. Afterwards, J. G. Araujo et al. in
[

studied

\mathbb{L}_{p}^{n+p}
.

In the work of De Lima et al. in
[

they worked with space

and obtained sufficient conditions to guarantee that, in fact,
p=1
and

is isometric to an isoparametric hypersurface of
\mathbb{L}^{n+1}_{1}
having two distinct principal curvatures, one of which is simple. Next, working in this same context, the authors of the present paper, jointly with J. G. Araujo et al. in
[
2

M^{n}
and considering the case that the ambient space
\mathbb{L}^{n+p}_{p}
is also conformally flat, in order to reduce the codimension to
p=1
. One year later, Barboza et al. in
[

[

[

In this work, let us denote by
\mathbb{L}^{n+p}_{q}
an
(n+p)
-dimensional locally symmetric semi-Riemannian space with index
q
where
1\leq q\leq p
. We recall that a semi-Riemannian space
\mathbb{L}^{n+p}_{q}
with
1\leq q\leq p
is said to be
locally symmetric
when its curvature tensor
\overline{R}
is parallel, that is,
\overline{\nabla}\overline{R}=0
, where
\overline{\nabla}
denotes the Levi Civita connection of
\mathbb{L}^{n+p}_{q}
. An
n
-dimensional submanifold
M^{n}
immersed in
\mathbb{L}^{n+p}_{q}
is said to be
spacelike
if the induced metric on
M^{n}
is positive definite.

We will denote by
LW-Submanifold
a submanifold
M^{n}
of
\mathbb{L}^{n+p}_{q}
,
(1\leq q\leq p)

linear Weingarten
, that is, a submanifold that its mean curvature
H
and its normalized scalar curvature
R
satisfy a linear relation of the type

(1.1)

R=aH+b,\quad\quad~a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Motivated by the rigidity phenomena for spacelike submanifolds satisfying Weingarten-type relations, in this paper we investigate complete spacelike linear Weingarten submanifolds (LW-submanifolds) immersed in the locally symmetric semi-Riemannian space
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}
, endowed with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field and flat normal bundle. Our approach combines analytic techniques involving the Cheng–Yau modified operator
\mathcal{L}
with geometric inequalities derived from Simons-type formulas. In particular, we establish a sharp lower bound for
\mathcal{L}(nH)
in terms of the traceless second fundamental form, which plays a central role throughout the paper.

More precisely, under suitable curvature assumptions on the ambient space and natural hypotheses on the second fundamental form, we obtain characterization and gap results showing that such submanifolds are either totally umbilical or isoparametric. These conclusions are achieved via three complementary methods: the Omori–Yau maximum principle, the
\mathcal{L}
-parabolicity of the underlying manifold, and an integrability condition on the gradient of the mean curvature. Each of these approaches leads to a rigidity phenomenon, revealing that the extremal behavior of the polynomial
P_{n,p,c,H}
governs the geometry of the immersion.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
contains the necessary preliminaries on spacelike submanifolds in semi-Riemannian manifolds, together with the notation used throughout the paper. In Section
3
, we derive a Simons-type formula for spacelike submanifolds immersed in
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}
, which constitutes a fundamental analytic tool in our arguments. In Section
4
is devoted to curvature constraints, explicit examples, and the key lemmas that will be employed in the proof of the main results. In Section
5
, we establish the characterization results for linear Weingarten spacelike submanifolds in
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}
. More precisely, Subsection 5.1 deals with rigidity results obtained via the Omori–Yau maximum principle, Subsection 5.2 addresses the characterization through the
\mathcal{L}
-parabolicity of the underlying manifold, and Subsection 5.3 is devoted to results obtained under an integrability condition on the gradient of the mean curvature.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
M^{n}
be an spacelike submanifold immersed in a locally symmetric semi-Riemannian space
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}
of index
q
with
1\leq q\leq p.

We choose a local field of semi-Riemannian orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n+p}\}
in
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p},

with dual coframe
\{\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{n+p}\}
, such that, at each
point of
M^{n}
,
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
are tangent to
M^{n}
and
e_{n+1},\cdots,e_{n+p}
are normal to
M^{n}
. We have
that the pseudo-Riemannian metric
ds^{2}
of
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}
can be written as

\displaystyle d\overline{s}^{2}=\sum_{A}\epsilon_{A}\omega_{A}^{2},

where

\epsilon_{i}=1,~~1\leq i\leq n;\quad\epsilon_{\beta}=1,~~n+1\leq\beta\leq n+p-q;\quad\epsilon_{\gamma}=-1,~~n+p-q+1\leq\gamma\leq n+p.

We will use the following convention for indices

1\leq A,B,C,\cdots\leq n+p;\quad 1\leq i,j,k,\cdots\leq n;\quad n+1\leq\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\leq n+p.

n+1\leq\beta,\beta^{\prime}\leq n+p-q;\quad n+p-q+1\leq\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\leq n+p.

Denoting by
\{\omega_{AB}\}
the connection forms of
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}
, we have that the structure equations
of
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}
are given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle d\omega_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{B}\epsilon_{B}\omega_{AB}\times\omega_{B},\quad\omega_{AB}+\omega_{BA}=0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle d\omega_{AB}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{C}\epsilon_{C}\omega_{AC}\times\omega_{CB}-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{C,D}\epsilon_{C}\epsilon_{D}\overline{R}_{ABCD}\omega_{C}\times\omega_{D},

where,
\overline{R}_{ABCD}
,
\overline{R}_{CD}
and
\overline{R}
denote respectively the Riemannian curvature
tensor, the Ricci tensor and the scalar curvature of the Lorentz space
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}.

In this setting, we have

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{CD}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{B}\epsilon_{B}\overline{R}_{CBDB},

\displaystyle\overline{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{A}\epsilon_{A}\overline{R}_{AA}.

We restrict forms to
M^{n}
, so that we have

\omega_{\alpha}=0,\quad\alpha=n+1,\cdots,n+p,

and the induced metric
ds^{2}
of
M^{n}
is written as
ds^{2}=\sum_{i}\omega_{i}^{2}.

Since
\sum_{i}\omega_{\alpha i}\times\omega_{i}=d\omega_{\alpha}
and by Cartan’s Lemma we can write

(2.3)

\displaystyle\omega_{i\alpha}=\sum_{j}h_{ij}^{\alpha}\omega_{j},\quad h_{ij}^{\alpha}=h_{ji}^{\alpha}.

The quadratic form

\displaystyle B=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}\epsilon_{\alpha}h_{ij}^{\alpha}\omega_{i}\omega_{j}e_{\alpha},

is the second fundamental form of
M^{n}
. Denote

\displaystyle H^{\alpha}=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i}h_{ii}^{\alpha},\quad\alpha=n+1,\cdots,n+p.

The mean curvature vector
h
is expressed as
h=\sum_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\alpha}H^{\alpha}e_{\alpha}
and denoting by
H
the length of
h
and by
S
the squared norm of the second fundamental form
B
, we have

\displaystyle H=||h||=\sum_{\alpha}(H^{\alpha})^{2}

and

\displaystyle S=\sum_{\alpha,i,j}(h_{ij}^{\alpha})^{2}.

We can write the structure equations of
M^{n}
as follows

\displaystyle d\omega_{i}=\sum_{j}\omega_{ij}\times\omega_{j},\quad\omega_{ji}+\omega_{ij}=0,

\displaystyle d\omega_{ij}=\sum_{k}\omega_{ik}\times\omega_{kj}-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l}R_{ijkl}\omega_{k}\times\omega_{l},

where
R_{ijkl}
are the components of the curvature tensor of
M^{n}.
Using the previous structure
equations, we obtain the Gauss Equation

(2.4)

\displaystyle R_{mkjk}=\overline{R}_{mkjk}+\sum_{\beta}(h_{mj}^{\beta}h_{kk}^{\beta}-h_{mk}^{\beta}h_{kj}^{\beta})-\sum_{\gamma}(h_{mj}^{\gamma}h_{kk}^{\gamma}-h_{mk}^{\gamma}h_{kj}^{\gamma}).

Moreover, we get the Codazzi Equation

(2.5)

\displaystyle h_{ijkk}^{\alpha}=\overline{R}_{\alpha ijkk}+h_{kijk}^{\alpha}.

In particular, the components of the Ricci tensor
R_{ik}
and the normalized scalar curvature
R

are given, respectively, by

(2.6)

\displaystyle n(n-1)R=\sum_{i,j}\overline{R}_{ijij}+n^{2}\sum_{\beta}(H^{\beta})^{2}-n^{2}\sum_{\gamma}(H^{\gamma})^{2}-S_{1}+S_{2},

where

H^{\beta}=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j}h_{jj}^{\beta},\quad H^{\gamma}=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j}h_{jj}^{\gamma},\quad S_{1}=\sum_{\beta,i,j}(h_{ij}^{\beta})^{2},\quad S_{2}=\sum_{\gamma,i,j}(h_{ij}^{\gamma})^{2}.

In this setting, we have
S=S_{1}+S_{2}.
We will also consider that the second fundamental form is locally timelike, in the sense that,
H^{\beta}=0
for
n+1\leq\beta\leq n+p-q
, thus

(2.7)

S_{1}+n(n-1)R=\sum_{i,j}\overline{R}_{ijij}-n^{2}H^{2}+S_{2}.

Moreover, we suppose that
M^{n}
has flat normal bundle, that is,
\overline{R}^{\perp}=0
(equivalently
\overline{R}_{\alpha\beta jk}=0
), then
\overline{R}_{\alpha\beta jk}
satisfy Ricci Equation

(2.8)

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}ij}=\sum_{k}(h_{ik}^{\alpha}h_{kj}^{\alpha^{\prime}}-h_{kj}^{\alpha}h_{ik}^{\alpha^{\prime}}).

Define the first and the second covariant derivarives of
\{h_{ij}^{\alpha}\}
, say
\{h_{ijk}^{\alpha}\}
and
\{h_{ijkl}^{\alpha}\}
by

\displaystyle\sum_{k}h_{ijk}^{\alpha}\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=dh_{ij}^{\alpha}+\sum_{k}\big(h_{kj}^{\alpha}\omega_{ki}+h_{ik}^{\alpha}\omega_{\beta\alpha}\big)+\sum_{\beta}h_{ij}^{\beta}\omega_{\beta\alpha}-\sum_{\gamma}h_{ij}^{\gamma}\omega_{\gamma\alpha},

\displaystyle\sum_{l}h_{ijkl}^{\alpha}\omega_{l}

\displaystyle=dh_{ijk}^{\alpha}+\sum_{l}h_{ijk}^{\alpha}\omega_{li}+\sum_{l}h_{ilk}^{\alpha}\omega_{ij}+\sum_{l}h_{ijl}^{\alpha}\omega_{lk}+\sum_{\beta}h_{ijk}^{\beta}\omega_{\beta\alpha}-\sum_{\gamma}h_{ijk}^{\gamma}\omega_{\gamma\alpha}.

Furthermore, by exterior differentiation of (
2.2
), we get the following Ricci identity

(2.9)

\displaystyle h_{ijkl}^{\alpha}-h_{ijlk}^{\alpha}=\sum_{m}h_{mj}^{\alpha}R_{mikl}+\sum_{m}h_{mi}^{\alpha}R_{mjkl}.

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,m,\alpha}h_{ij}^{\alpha}h_{mi}^{\alpha}R_{mkjk}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,m,\alpha}h_{ij}^{\alpha}h_{km}^{\alpha}R_{mijk}+\sum_{i,j,k,m,\alpha}h_{ij}^{\alpha}h_{mi}^{\alpha}R_{mkjk}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,m,\alpha}h_{ij}^{\alpha}h_{km}^{\alpha}\overline{R}_{mijk}+\sum_{i,j,k,m,\alpha,\beta}h_{ij}^{\alpha}h_{km}^{\alpha}h_{mj}^{\beta}h_{ik}^{\beta}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,m,\alpha,\beta}h_{ij}^{\alpha}h_{km}^{\alpha}h_{mk}^{\beta}h_{ij}^{\beta}-\sum_{i,j,k,m,\alpha,\gamma}h_{ij}^{\alpha}h_{km}^{\alpha}h_{mj}^{\gamma}h_{ik}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}h_{ij}^{\alpha}h_{ik}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\overline{R}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}jk}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,\alpha}h_{ij}^{\alpha}\overline{R}_{\alpha ijkk}

4.
Curvature Constraints, Examples, and Key Lemmas

Proceeding with the context of the previous section and inspired by the configuration assumed by Nishikawa in
[
27
]
, along this work we will assume that there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}
and
c_{3}
such that the sectional curvature
\overline{K}
and the curvature tensor
\overline{R}
of the ambient space

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}=\langle e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n},e_{n+1},\cdots,e_{n+p-q},e_{n+p-q+1},\cdots,e_{n+p}\rangle,

satisfies the following constraints:

(4.1)

\overline{K}(x_{i},x_{\alpha})=\dfrac{c_{1}}{n},\quad x_{i}\in\langle e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\rangle\quad\mbox{and}\quad x_{\alpha}\in\langle e_{n+1},\cdots,e_{n+p}\rangle,

(4.2)

\overline{K}(x_{i},x_{j})\geq c_{2},\quad x_{i},x_{j}\in\langle e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\rangle,

(4.3)

\langle\overline{R}(x_{\alpha},x_{i})x_{\alpha^{\prime}},x_{i}\rangle=0,\quad x_{i}\in\langle e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\rangle\quad\mbox{and}\quad x_{\alpha},x_{\alpha^{\prime}}\in\langle e_{n+1},\cdots,e_{n+p}\rangle,

(4.4)

\overline{K}(x_{\alpha},x_{\alpha^{\prime}})=\dfrac{c_{3}}{p},\quad x_{\alpha},x_{\alpha^{\prime}}\in\langle e_{n+1},\cdots,e_{n+p}\rangle.

If
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}
satisfies conditions above, then

\displaystyle\overline{R}=\sum_{i,j,i,j}\overline{R}_{ijij}+D(c_{1},c_{3}),

where
D(c_{1},c_{3})
is a constant.
Then, it is well known that the scalar curvature of a locally symmetric Lorentz
space is constant. Consequently,
\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\overline{R}_{ijij}
is a constant naturally attached
to a locally symmetric Lorentz space satisfying conditions (
4.1
) and (
4.4
).
For sake of simplicity, in the course of this work we will denote the constant
\dfrac{1}{n(n-1)}\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\overline{R}_{ijij}
by
\overline{\mathcal{R}}
. In order to establish our main results, we devote this
section to present some auxiliary lemmas.

Remark 4.1
.

) and (

[
27
]

) and (
4.4

\displaystyle\overline{K}\left(\xi_{i},X\right)

Lemma 4.3

Proof..

4.4

(2n^{2}H+n(n-1)a)^{2}-4n^{2}S=0,

For what follows, we choose the normal vector field

to be aligned with the mean curvature vector field, that is,

Within this framework, we introduce the following symmetric tensor:

where the components are given by

The squared length of the tensor
\Phi
is defined by

|\Phi|^{2}=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}\left(\Phi_{ij}^{\alpha}\right)^{2}.

Moreover, one easily verifies that

where

S_{1}=\sum_{i,j,\beta}\left(h_{ij}^{\beta}\right)^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{2}=\sum_{i,j,\gamma}\left(h_{ij}^{\gamma}\right)^{2}.

In addition, we have the following relation:

n(n-1)(aH+b)=\sum_{i,j}\overline{R}_{ijij}-n^{2}\left(\sum_{\gamma}(H^{\gamma})^{2}-\sum_{\beta}(H^{\beta})^{2}\right)+(S_{2}-S_{1}).

Since
\sum_{\beta}(H^{\beta})^{2}=0
, it follows that

(4.25)

n(n-1)aH+n(n-1)b=\sum_{i,j}\overline{R}_{ijij}-n^{2}H^{2}+(S_{2}-S_{1}).

In order to study LW-submanifolds, we will consider, for

for each

and the normal vector field

is taken in the direction of the mean curvature vector field, that is,
e_{n+p-q+1}=\dfrac{h}{H}.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X:M^{n}\looparrowright\mathbb{L}^{n+p}_{q}
be a complete spacelike LW-submanifold with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field and flat normal bundle satisfying conditions (
4.1
)-(
4.4
). If

, then
\mathcal{L}
is elliptic.

Proof..

Let us consider two cases:

(i)
Case
a=0
. Since
R=b<\overline{\mathcal{R}}
, from Equation (
2.7
) if we choose a (local) orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}
on
M^{n}
such that

, we have that
\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}>0
. Consequently,

(4.28)

\displaystyle n^{2}H^{2}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}+2\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}>\lambda_{i}^{2}

for every

and, hence, we have that

for every

.
Therefore, in this case, we conclude that
\mathcal{L}
is elliptic.

(ii)
Case
a\neq 0
. From Equation (
2.7
) we get that

(4.29)

\displaystyle a=\frac{1}{n(n-1)H}\left(S-n^{2}H^{2}+n(n-1)\overline{\mathcal{R}}-n(n-1)b\right).

For any
i
, from (
4.29
) we have

nH-\lambda_{i}^{n+p-q+1}+\frac{n-1}{2}a=nH-\lambda_{i}^{n+p-q+1}+\frac{1}{2nH}\left(S-n^{2}H^{2}+n(n-1)(\overline{\mathcal{R}}-b)\right)

(4.30)

=\left(\frac{1}{2}(nH)^{2}-nH\lambda_{i}^{n+p-q+1}+\frac{1}{2}S+\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(\overline{\mathcal{R}}-b)\right)(nH)^{-1}.

Since
\displaystyle\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1}=nH
and

, from (
4.30

(4.31)

\displaystyle nH-\lambda_{i}^{n+p-q+1}+\frac{n-1}{2}a\!\!\!

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1}\right)^{2}-\lambda_{i}^{n+p-q+1}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}(\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1})^{2}\right\}(nH)^{-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(\overline{\mathcal{R}}-b)(nH)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\left\{\sum_{j}(\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\neq j}\lambda_{l}^{n+p-q+1}\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1}-\lambda_{i}^{n+p-q+1}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1}\right\}(nH)^{-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(\overline{\mathcal{R}}-b)(nH)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\left\{\sum_{i\neq j}(\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\neq j,l,j\neq i}\lambda_{l}^{n+p-q+1}\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1}+\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(\overline{\mathcal{R}}-b)\right\}(nH)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sum_{i\neq j}(\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1})^{2}+\left(\sum_{j\neq i}\lambda_{j}^{n+p-q+1}\right)^{2}+n(n-1)(\overline{\mathcal{R}}-b)\right\}(nH)^{-1}.

Therefore, taking into account our assumption

, we conclude that

, which implies that

Proposition 5.1
.

\displaystyle=

Thence, from of
\mathcal{L}=\square+\dfrac{n-1}{2}a\Delta,
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(nH)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\square(nH)+\dfrac{n-1}{2}a\Delta(nH)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}(nH\delta_{ij}-h_{ij}^{n+p-q+1})(nH)_{ij}+\dfrac{n-1}{2}a\Delta(nH)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{2}H\sum_{i}H_{ii}-n\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}^{n+p-q+1}H_{ij}+\dfrac{n-1}{2}a\Delta(nH)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{2}H\Delta H-n\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}^{n+p-q+1}H_{ij}+\dfrac{n-1}{2}a\Delta(nH)

Note that

\displaystyle\Delta H^{2}=2H\Delta H+2|\nabla H|^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(nH)=\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta(n^{2}H^{2})-n^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}-n\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}^{n+p-q+1}H_{ij}+\dfrac{n-1}{2}a\Delta(nH).

Since that
R=aH+b
and
\mathbb{L}_{q}^{n+p}
satisfies the conditions (
4.1
)-(
4.4
), we have that
\mathcal{R}

Lemma 5.4

Let

5.2.
Via
\mathcal{L}
-parabolicity

We recall that a Riemannian manifold
M^{n}
is said to be
parabolic
(with respect to the Laplacian operator) if the constant functions are the only subharmonic functions on
M^{n}
which are bounded from above; that is for a function
u\in C^{2}(M)

\Delta u\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad u\leq u^{*}\ll\infty\quad\text{implies}\quad u=\text{constant}.

From a physical viewpoint, parabolicity is closely related to the recurrence of the Brownian motion. Roughly speaking, the parabolicity is equivalent to the property that all particles will pass through any open set at an arbitrarily large time, for more details see
[
19
]
.

Extending this previous concept for the operator
\mathcal{L}
defined in (2.10),
M^{n}
is said to be
\mathcal{L}
-parabolic
if the constant functions are the only functions
u\in C^{2}(M)
which are bounded from above and satisfies
\mathcal{L}u\geq 0
, that is, for a function
u\in C^{2}(M)
,

\mathcal{L}u\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad u\leq u^{*}\ll\infty\quad\text{implies}\quad u=\text{constant}.

In this setting, we obtain the following gap result.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
X:M^{n}\looparrowright\mathbb{L}^{n+p}_{q}
be a complete spacelike LW-submanifold
(a\geq 0)
with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field and flat normal bundle satisfying conditions (
4.1
)-(
4.4
). Suppose that the second fundamental form of
M^{n}
is locally timelike and there exists an orthogonal basis for
TM
that diagonalizes simultaneously all
B_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon\in TM^{\perp}.
Assume in addition that
M^{n}
is
\mathcal{L}-
parabolic,
H^{2}<c
and
0\leq|\Phi|\leq C(n,p,c,supH)
, then either
|\Phi|\equiv 0
and
M^{n}
is a totally umbilical, or
|\Phi|=C(n,p,c,supH)
and
M^{n}
is an isoparametric submanifold.

Proof..

Suppose by contradiction that
M^{n}
is not totally umbilical, Since we are assuming that
0\leq|\Phi|\leq C(n,p,c,supH)
, we obtain

0<\sup_{M}|\Phi|\leq C(n,p,c,supH),

in the sense, from (
5.16
) of Theorem
5.5
we get that

(5.17)

\sup_{M}|\Phi|=C(n,p,c,supH).

Furthermore, from (
5.14
) jointly with the
\mathcal{L}
-parabolicity of

we conclude that
|\Phi|
must be constant and identically equal to
C(n,p,c,supH)
. Therefore, at this point we can proceed as in the last part of the proof of Theorem

to conclude the result.

). Suppose that the second fundamental form of
M^{n}
is locally timelike and there exists an orthogonal basis for
TM
that diagonalizes simultaneously all
B_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon\in TM^{\perp}.
If
|\nabla H|\in L^{1}(M)
,
H^{2}<c
and
|\Phi|\leq C(n,p,c,supH)
, then either
M^{n}
is a totally umbilical, or
|\Phi|=C(n,p,c,supH)
and
M^{n}
is an isoparametric submanifold.

Proof..

Since
R=aH+b
and the mean curvature function
H
is bounded on
M^{n}
, from (
2.7
), it follows that the second fundamental form
B
is also bounded on
M^{n}
. Consequently, by (
5.19
), the operator
\mathcal{P}
is bounded, that is, there exists a positive constant
\beta>0
such that

|\mathcal{P}|\leq\beta\quad\text{on }M^{n}.

Moreover, since we assume that
|\nabla H|\in L^{1}(M)
, it follows from (
5.19
) that

(5.20)

|\mathcal{P}(\nabla H)|\leq|\mathcal{P}|\,|\nabla H|\leq\beta|\nabla H|\in L^{1}(M).

Therefore, taking into account (
5.18
) and (
5.19
), and applying Lemma
5.10
, we obtain

(5.21)

\mathcal{L}(nH)=\mathrm{div}(\mathcal{P}(nH))=0\quad\text{on }M^{n}.

On the other hand, using the estimate

0\leq|\Phi|\leq C(n,p,c,\sup H),

together with (
5.9
) and (
5.21
), we infer that

(5.22)

0=\mathcal{L}(nH)\geq|\nabla B|^{2}-n^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}+|\Phi|^{2}P_{n,p,c,H}(|\Phi|)\geq 0.

Hence, all inequalities above must be equalities. In particular, we conclude that

|\nabla B|^{2}=n^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}.

Consequently, Lemma
4.3
implies that the mean curvature
H
is constant on
M^{n}
. As a consequence, we obtain

\sum_{i,j,k,\alpha}(h_{ijk}^{\alpha})^{2}=|\nabla B|^{2}=n^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}=0,

which shows that
h_{ijk}^{\alpha}=0
for all indices. Therefore,
M^{n}
is an isoparametric submanifold. The proof is completed by the same argument used in the final part of the proof of Theorem
5.5
.

Regularity for monotone Operators and applications to homogenization of p p -Laplace type equations
Lukas Koch, Mathias Schäffner

Abstract
. In this manuscript, we provide local L q L^{q} -estimates for the gradient of solutions of a class of quasilinear equations whose principal part lacks strong monotonicity. These estimates are used to establish uniform large-scale L q L^{q} -estimates for the gradient of solutions of degenerate/singular quasilinear equations with oscillating coefficients and large-scale Lipschitz estimates for solutions of non-degenerate equations.



1.
Introduction and main results

We are interested in regularity properties of solutions of quasilinear elliptic equations in divergence form with rapidly oscillating periodic coefficients. We consider solutions of equations of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf a}\left(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla u\right)=\nabla\cdot F\quad\mbox{in $B_{1}$},

where
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
is a small parameter and
{\bf a}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is periodic and sufficiently smooth in the first variable and a monotone operator satisfying suitable growth and coercivity conditions modeled upon the
p
-Laplace operator in the second variable. More precisely, we assume

Assumption 1
.

Let
\mu\in[0,1]
,
1<p<\infty
and
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
be given. Suppose that
{\bf a}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Carathéodory function satisfying for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that
{\bf a}(x,0)=0
and

(1.2)

\displaystyle|{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})|\leq

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mu+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda\langle{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq

\displaystyle(\mu+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}.

Under Assumption
1
and supposing that
x\to{\bf a}(x,z)
is
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-periodic in
x
and satisfies a suitable continuity property, we show that for every
q\in[p,\infty)
, there exists
C\geq 1
– which is independent of
\varepsilon
– such that the following Calderon-Zygmund type estimate holds

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{q}(\frac{1}{2}B)}\leq C\left(\|\mu+\lvert\nabla u\rvert\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B)}+\|F\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(B)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right),

see Theorem
1.2
below for the precise result. In the case of linear elliptic equations, i.e.
{{\bf a}(x,\xi)=A(x)\xi}
, where
A
is a sufficiently smooth uniformly elliptic coefficient field and thus
{\bf a}
satisfies (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) with
\mu=0
and
p=2
, estimate (
1.4
) is well-known and goes back to the seminal contributions of Avellaneda and Lin
[
6
,
7
]
. Since the works
[
6
,
7
]
, uniform regularity estimates of the form (
1.4
), often referred to as
large-scale regularity
, play an important role in the development of homogenization theory. By now there are several far reaching extensions of
[
6
,
7
]
covering possibly nonlinear equations with random coefficients
[
3
,
5
,
40
]
, see also textbooks
[
4
,
54
]
. In particular, the work
[
3
]
contains a suitable version of (
1.4
) in the case of a nonlinear operator with random coefficients in the case
p=2
. However, to the best of our knowledge there are no general unconditional uniform/large-scale regularity results of the form (
1.4
) prior to this work in the case
p\neq 2
.

Next we briefly discuss a key difficulty in the case
p\neq 2
and how we overcome this. The classical papers
[
6
,
7
]
, and all following works on large-scale regularity, use homogenization theory in some form: Solutions to the equation (
1.1
) converge, as
\varepsilon>0
tends to zero, to solutions
\overline{u}
of

(1.5)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\overline{{\bf a}}\left(\nabla\overline{u}\right)=\nabla\cdot F\qquad\mbox{in $B_{1}$},

where the autonomous homogenized operator
\overline{{\bf a}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be computed from
{\bf a}
with help of certain cell-formulas. This is a classical fact in homogenization theory see e.g. the classical paper
[
36
]
for the nonlinear case of the present work or the textbook references
[
13
,
44
]
(which are focused on the variational setting). In the linear case, that is
{\bf a}(x,\xi):=A(x)\xi
with
A
uniformly elliptic, the homogenized operator
\overline{{\bf a}}
is given by
\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi)=\overline{A}\xi
where
\overline{A}
is a constant and uniformly elliptic. Hence, classical regularity theory can be applied to solutions of (
1.5
). The regularity of
\overline{u}
can be lifted to the solutions of (
1.1
) by a nontrivial perturbation argument, see
[
6
,
7
]
. In the nonlinear setting with
p=2
, a similar strategy can also be implemented, see
[
3
,
5
]
(in the much more difficult case of random homogenization) or
[
51
]
in the case of elliptic systems. The case
p=2
is somewhat special as it is easy to show that
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies the same growth and monotonicity conditions as
{\bf a}
upon enlarging
\Lambda
. Hence, one has classical regularity theory for (
1.5
) at the disposal. In contrast, in the case
p\neq 2
it is not clear if the conditions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are closed under homogenization, that is if the homogenized operator
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies the same conditions (upon possibly changing
\Lambda
), see e.g.
[
36
,
21
]
. As discussed for instance in
[
24
]
it is clear in the case
p>2
that
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies

(1.6)

\displaystyle|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2})|\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\Lambda}(\mu+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|

(1.7)

\displaystyle\overline{\Lambda}\langle\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq

\displaystyle(\mu+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2},

for some
\overline{\Lambda}\in[1,\infty)
, see Section
4
below. Clearly, the monotonicity condition (
1.7
) is weaker then (
1.3
) and it is highly questionable if in general (
1.3
) holds for
\overline{{\bf a}}
, see e.g. the discussions in
[
24
]
,
[
20
, p. 398, 399]
or
[
21
, Section 7]
. In particular,
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfying (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) is not uniformly elliptic and classical regularity theory (e.g.
[
39
]
) cannot be applied. More can be said appealing to regularity results for non-uniformly elliptic equations. Indeed, equation (
1.5
) under (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) with
\mu=1
can to some extent be covered by the regularity theory with nonstandard growth conditions pioneered by Marcellini
[
48
]
, see
[
49
]
for a recent overview. In a variational setting, that is
{\bf a}
has a convex potential, the currently best results
[
8
,
10
]
for this problem would yield Lipschitz estimates for solutions of (
1.5
) under (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) with
\mu=1
provided
2\leq p
and
p<\frac{2(n-1)}{n-3}
if
n\geq 4
and
F
is sufficiently regular, see
[
24
]
.

A key result of this contribution is to obtain a Calderon-Zygmund theory for solutions to (
1.5
) under the condition (
1.6
) and (
1.7
)
without any restriction
on
p>2
and
\mu\in[0,1]
. This is the content of Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, in the nondegenerate case
\mu=1
, we obtain Lipschitz-regularity for solutions of (
1.5
) with sufficiently smooth right-hand side, see Proposition
6.2
and Remark
6.3
. This justifies regularity assumptions for the homogenized solution in
[
24
, Theorem 2.2]
. Moreover, we use this to obtain uniform Lipschitz estimates for solutions of (
1.1
) with
F\equiv 0
under Assumption
1
with
\mu=1
, see Theorem
1.3
.

Before we discuss the precise results obtained in this manuscript, we mention some further related recent results. In
[
59
]
, the authors provide quantitative homogenization and large-scale Hölder regularity results for
p
-Laplace type equations. The results in
[
59
]
are
conditional
, in the sense that the authors
assume
that the homogenized operator
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies the strong monotonicity condition (
1.3
) which is, as mentioned above, not clear and justified in
[
59
]
only in a specific case. Here, we recover some of the large-scale regularity results of
[
59
]
without any additional assumptions, see Corollary
5.3
and Remark
5.4
. In the setting of periodic homogenization with a local defect
[
60
]
considered the
p
-Laplace operator and proved quantitative convergence results under the assumption that the periodic correctors are non-degenerate. Finally, the work
[
24
]
provides, for a restricted range of
p\geq 2
, optimal quantitative stochastic homogenization results assuming (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) with
\mu=1
(extending
[
34
]
for the case
p=2
).

1.1.
Main results

The first main result of this manuscript are local Calderon-Zygmund type estimates for solutions to

\nabla\cdot{\bf a}(x,\nabla u)=\nabla\cdot F,

under rather mild monotonicity and growth conditions on
\xi\mapsto{\bf a}(x,\xi)
. More precisely, we assume

Assumption 2
.

Let
\mu\in[0,1]
,
1<p<\infty
and
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
be given. Suppose that
{\bf a}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Carathéodory function and satisfies

(1.8)

\displaystyle\Lambda\langle{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq\begin{cases}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{p}&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
(\mu+\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert+\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert)^{p-2}\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert^{2}&\text{ if }p<2\end{cases}\quad\forall x,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and

(1.9)

|{\bf a}(x,\xi)|\leq\Lambda(\mu+|\xi|)^{p-1}\quad\forall x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Under the above assumptions we prove the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that, for given
1<p<\infty
, Assumption
2
is satisfied. Moreover, assume that there is a nondecreasing, continuous modulus of continuity
\omega\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\omega(0)=0
such that

(1.10)

\displaystyle\lvert{\bf a}(x,z)-{\bf a}(y,z)\rvert\leq\omega(\lvert x-y\rvert)\lvert(\mu+\lvert z\rvert)^{p-2}|z|.

Let
F\in L^{p\prime}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
be a weak solution to

(1.11)

\nabla\cdot{\bf a}(x,\nabla u)=\nabla\cdot F\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega$.}

Then, for all
q\in[p,\infty)
and every
B=B_{R}(x)\Subset\Omega
there exists
C=C(\Lambda,n,p,q,\omega,R)
such that it holds

(1.12)

\displaystyle\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{q}(\frac{1}{2}B)}\leq C\left(\|\mu+\lvert\nabla u\rvert\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B)}+\|F\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(B)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right).

The main novelty of Theorem
1.1
concerns the degenerate case
p>2
. Nonlinear Calderón-Zygmund estimates of the form (
1.12
) are typically derived under the strong monotonicity condition (
1.3
), that is

(1.13)

\displaystyle(\mu+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}\lesssim\langle{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle

see the classical references by Iwaniec
[
43
]
and DiBenedetto & Manfredi
[
29
]
. We also refer the reader to
[
2
,
30
,
16
,
45
]
for further results and
[
50
]
for an overview . Here, we prove that (
1.12
) remains valid assuming (
1.8
) instead of (
1.3
). Clearly, for
p>2
condition (
1.8
) is weaker than (
1.3
). To emphasize the difference between (
1.3
) and (
1.8
), assume for the moment that
{\bf a}
is smooth and autonomous. In this case (
1.3
) ensures a quantitative degenerate ellipticity condition for the linearization, namely

|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}\lesssim\langle D{\bf a}(z)\xi,\xi\rangle\qquad\forall z,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

which is a key ingredient in classical linearization based approaches. In contrast, (
1.8
) does not provide such a quantitative lower bound and is therefore not directly compatible with linearization techniques in nonlinear Calderon-Zygmund theory.

Finally, we comment that while we did not find a precise reference in the case
p\leq 2
, where Assumption (
1.8
) is the standard assumption for an operator with
p
-growth, our result is certainly not new in this setting. For example, considering minimisers and
F=0
, a Lipschitz regularity result may be found in
[
35
, Theorem 2.7]
.

Theorem
1.1
is relevant beyond the application to homogenised operators, we consider in this paper. In particular, for
p\geq 2
the orthotropic
p
-Laplace operator satisfies Assumption
2
, but not (
1.13
). In fact, Theorem
1.1
applies to a class of degenerate elliptic operators known as Finsler-Laplacians, of which the orthotropic
p
-Laplace operator is one example. We comment further on this application and its relation to existing results in Remark
3.5
and Remark
4.6
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section
3
and consists of two main steps. In a first step, we consider (
1.11
) with
F\equiv 0
and
{\bf a}
being autonomous and prove (
1.12
) for every
q<\infty
, see Proposition
3.1
. This is done via a variant of Moser’s iteration method introduced in
[
31
]
and extensively used in the regularity theory of non-local operators, starting in
[
15
]
. Recently, this method has also been used to study non-uniformly elliptic operators
[
27
]
. In a second step, we deduce Theorem
1.1
from the homogeneous and autonomous case by perturbation, following ideas of
[
17
]
and related works.

The second main result is a version of Theorem
1.1
for operators
{\bf a}
with rapid periodic oscillations in the space variable.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that, for given
1<p<\infty
, Assumption
1
is satisfied. Moreover, assume that there is a nondecreasing, continuous modulus of continuity
\omega\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\omega(0)=0
such that (
1.10
) is valid and that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the map
x\mapsto{\bf a}(x,\xi)
is
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-periodic. Let
F\in L^{p\prime}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
be a weak solution to

(1.14)

\nabla\cdot{\bf a}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla u)=\nabla\cdot F\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega$.}

Then, for all
q\in[p,\infty)
there exists
C=C(\Lambda,n,p,q,\omega)
such that it holds for every
B=B_{R}(x)\Subset\Omega

(1.15)

\displaystyle\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{q}(\frac{1}{2}B)}\leq C\left(\|\mu+\lvert\nabla u\rvert\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B)}+\|F\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(B)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right).

As already described above, Theorem
1.2
relies on Theorem
1.1
in combination with homogenization methods and a perturbation arguments in the spirit of
[
7
,
17
]
, see Section
5
. A key point is that in the situation of Theorem
1.2
the homogenized operator
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies Assumption
2
and thus we have – thanks to Theorem
1.1
– Calderon-Zygmund estimates for the homogenized equation. In Section
5
, we first establish version of estimate (
1.15
) valid on large-scales, that is we replace the
L^{q}
norm of
\mu+|\nabla u|
on the left-hand side in (
1.15
) by quantities of the form
\biggl(\int_{\frac{1}{2}B}(\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\mu+|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)^{\frac{1}{q}}
, see Theorem
5.1
. This large-scale regularity is valid without any regularity of
x\mapsto{\bf a}(x,\xi)
besides measurability and estimate (
1.15
) follows by standard regularity theory provided the coefficients are more regular.

We emphasize that Theorem
1.2
is known in the case of linear equations
[
17
]
and nonlinear equations with linear growth (that is
p=2
), see
[
3
]
, which even covers the case of random coefficients. However to the best of our knowledge, Theorem
1.2
is new already in the case of
p
-Laplacian type equations with oscillating coefficients, that is
{\bf a}(y,z)=A(y)|z|^{p-2}z
with
p\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{2\}
and an uniformly elliptic coefficient matrix
A\in L^{\infty}(Y,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
.

In the non-degenerate / non-singular case, that is Assumption
1
holds with
\mu=1
, we obtain Lipschitz estimates for solutions of (
1.14
) with
F\equiv 0
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Consider the situation of Theorem
1.2
with
\mu=1
in Assumption
1
, and that there exists
\gamma>0
such that (
1.10
) is valid with
\mu=1
and
\omega(s)\leq\Lambda s^{\gamma}
for all
s>0
. Fix
M\in[1,\infty)
. There exists
C_{M}=C_{M}(\Lambda,M,n,p)\in[1,\infty)
such that the following is true. Let
u\in W^{1,p}(B)
with
B=B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that

\nabla\cdot{\bf a}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla u)=0\qquad\mbox{in $B$}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\fint_{B}|V_{p}(\nabla u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq M,

where
V_{p}(z):=(1+|z|)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}z
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,

\|V_{p}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{1}{2}B)}^{2}\leq C_{M}\fint_{B}|V_{p}(\nabla u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Theorem
1.3
extends the seminal results of Avellaneda & Lin
[
6
]
for linear elliptic systems to the case of nonlinear (non-degenerate & non-singular) elliptic equations. In the case
p=2
variations of Theorem
1.3
are already proven, see e.g.
[
5
,
51
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge the result is new for
p\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{2\}
. A key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.3
(provided in Section
6
) is a Lipschitz- and
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity result for autonomous equations under

Assumption 3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
be given. Suppose that
{\bf a}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies
{\bf a}(x,0)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(1.16)

\displaystyle\Lambda\langle{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq\begin{cases}(1+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
(1+\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert+\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert)^{p-2}\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert^{2}&\text{ if }p<2\end{cases}

and

(1.17)

\displaystyle|{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})|\leq\Lambda\begin{cases}(1+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert&\mbox{if $p\geq 2$}\\
(1+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert&\mbox{if $p<2$}\end{cases}.

This is the content of Proposition
6.2
below and crucially relies on Theorem
1.1
and results for
non-uniformly
elliptic equations
[
9
,
56
]
. The latter is reflected in the appearance of
M
in Theorem
1.3
which also prevents us from considering inhomogeneous equations, see Remark
6.5
. The importance of Assumption
3
is that in the setting of Theorem
1.3
the homogenized coefficient satisfies Assumption
3
. Moreover, it allows us to show that our large-scale regularity results also hold for certain anisotropic operators, including the orthotropic p-Laplace operator with rapidly varying coefficients, see Section
4.3
.

Remark 1.4
.

The proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, explicitly use the periodicity assumption of
x\mapsto{\bf a}(x,\xi)
. However, the general strategy can also be applied in the case of random homogenization provided there are suitable estimates on the correctors in stochastic homogenization available. In
[
24
]
, these estimates are established for specific models satisfying Assumption
1
with
\mu=1
and
2<p<2\frac{n-1}{n-3}
if
n>3
. Appealing to this, analogous results to Theorem
1.3
are established in
[
23
]
in a random setting under the above restriction on
2<p
.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

In this section we introduce our notation and recall some results regarding difference quotient characterisations of Sobolev and Besov spaces.

We denote the open ball of radius
r
centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
B_{r}(x)
. Further we write
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
. Given a ball
B=B_{r}(x_{0})
, we denote for
t>0
,
tB=B_{tr}(x_{0})
. We set
Y:=(0,1)^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Throughout
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes a Lipschitz domain. Given
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote
\Omega_{(-|h|)}=\{x\in\Omega\colon d(x,\partial\Omega)>|h|\}
. Given
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote
\tau_{h}u(x)=u(x+h)-u(x)
and
\tau_{h}^{2}u(x)=\frac{u(x+h)-2u(x)+u(x-h)}{2}
, whenever these expressions make sense.

In any estimate, if the right-hand side is not well-defined, we understand it to be infinite and the statement to be empty.

2.1.
Function spaces

Given
p\geq 1
, we denote by
L^{p}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
the standard Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces on
\Omega
.
W^{1,p}_{per,0}(Y)
is the space of
W^{1,p}
functions
u
that are periodic on
Y
with
{\fint_{Y}u\,\mathrm{d}x=0}
. We find it convenient to denote for
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(U)}=\left(\fint_{U}\lvert f\rvert^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Moreover, for
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, we write
(u)_{\Omega}=\fint_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{d}x
. Finally, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
denotes the space of
\alpha
-Hölder-continuous functions on
\Omega
.

As we will work with difference quotients, the following semi-norm will be useful to us: Given a convex domain
\Omega
,
s\in(0,1]
and
p\geq 1
, we set

(2.1)

\displaystyle[u]_{s,p,\Omega}:=\sup_{0\neq h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{h}}\frac{|\tau_{h}u|^{p}}{|h|^{sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{where}\quad\Omega_{h}=\{x\in\Omega\colon x+th\in\Omega\text{ for all }t\in[0,1]\}.

If
p=+\infty
, the expression needs to be modified in the obvious way.
The quantity
[\cdot]_{s,p,\Omega}
is well-known. In fact, for
s=1
,
[u]_{s,p,\Omega}
is equivalent to
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, while for
s<1
it corresponds to the semi-norm in the Besov space
B^{s,p}_{\infty}(\Omega)
. We recommend
[
55
]
as a reference to the interested reader for the general theory of these function spaces. Here, we recall only a number of relevant facts and provide elementary proofs for some of them.

We begin by recalling the relation between difference quotients and
L^{q}
-gradient norms.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
47
, Theorem 10.55]
)
.

Let
q>1
and

3.
Gradient integrability for autonomous equations and proof of Theorem

In this section, we first consider autonomous operators
{\bf a}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf a}(\nabla u)=0\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$}

with a small contrast in the

Theorem 3.1

Suppose that Assumption

is satisfied for some

is autonomous. Let

such that for all balls

(3.2)

Finally, in the case

Remark 3.2

The novelty of Theorem

is in the superquadratic case

follows by regularity theory for operators with natural growth, see e.g.

Proof.

By standard scaling and translation arguments it suffices to consider the case

The proof is divided in three steps. We begin by establishing in Step 1 a Caccioppoli inequality of the form

\displaystyle\|f_{x,r}\|_{\underline{L}^{q_{0}}(B_{r}(x))}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:CZ:ass1}}}{{\leq}}A\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{2r}(x))}+\|g\|_{\underline{L}^{p^{\prime}}(B_{2r}(x))}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\|\tilde{f}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{2r}(x))}^{\frac{2}{p}}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{2r}(x))}^{\frac{2}{p}}.

Raising the above inequality to the

-th power, we obtain (

). Next, we show (
3.23
). In what follows

might change in each occurrence but only depends on

and

. For

, we estimate using (
3.20
),

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}_{x,r}-\tilde{f}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{r}(x))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{p}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}(\lvert f\rvert^{p-2}+\lvert f_{x,r}\rvert^{p-2})\lvert f-f_{x,r}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{p}\left(\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{r}(x))}^{p-2}+\|f_{x,r}\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{r}(x)}^{p-2}\right)\|f-f_{x,r}\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{r}(x))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{r}(x))}^{p}+c_{p}\delta^{2-p}\|f-f_{x,r}\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{r}(x))}^{p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:CZ:ass2}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{r}(x))}^{p}+c_{p}\delta^{2-p}(\delta^{p}\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{r}(x))}^{p}+\|g\|_{\underline{L}^{p^{\prime}}(B_{2r}(x))}^{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{p}\delta^{2}\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{r}(x))}^{p}+c_{p}\delta^{2-p}\|g\|_{\underline{L}^{p^{\prime}}(B_{2r}(x)}^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{p}\delta^{2}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{2r}(x))}^{2}+c_{p}\delta^{2-p}\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r}(x))}^{2},

which yields (
3.23
) in the case

. In the case

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}_{x,r}-\tilde{f}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{r}(x))}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle c_{p}\|f-f_{x,r}\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{r}(x))}^{p}\leq c_{p}\delta^{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{2r}(x))}^{p}+c_{p}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{2r}(x))}^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle c_{p}\delta^{p}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r}(x))}^{2}+c_{p}\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r}(x))}^{2}

∎

4.
The homogenized equation and correctors

In this section, we introduce the homogenized equation and correctors. We also collect a number of structural properties that these objects satisfy, as well as some basic regularity results concerning the homogenized equation. The results essentially follow from standard theory and methods, but for the sake of completeness, we include proofs.

Throughout this section, we write
\lesssim
if
\leq
holds up to a multiplicative positive constant depending only on
p
and
\Lambda
.

4.1.
Corrector and flux corrector

We begin by introducing the corrector and flux corrector.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose Assumption
2
is satisfied. For every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exist
\phi_{\xi}\in W_{{\rm{per}},0}^{1,p}(Y)
and
\sigma_{\xi}\in W_{{\rm{per}},0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(Y,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
that are uniquely defined by:

(4.1)

\nabla\cdot{\bf a}(y,\xi+\nabla\phi_{\xi}(y))=0\qquad\mbox{in $Y$}

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta\sigma_{\xi,jk}=

\displaystyle\partial_{k}J(\xi)\cdot e_{j}-\partial_{j}J(\xi)\cdot e_{k}\qquad\mbox{ in $Y$},

where

J(\xi):={\bf a}(\cdot,\xi+\nabla\phi_{\xi})-\fint_{Y}{\bf a}(y,\xi+\nabla\phi_{\xi})\,\mathrm{d}y.

Moreover, it holds

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\xi,jk}=-\sigma_{\xi,kj}\quad\mbox{and}\quad-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{k}\sigma_{\xi,jk}=J(\xi)\cdot e_{j}.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of the corrector follows from general theory of monotone operators, e.g.
[
62
]
. The growth condition (
1.9
) and
\phi\in W_{\operatorname{per},0}^{1,p}(Y)
imply
{\bf a}(\cdot,\xi+\nabla\phi_{\xi})\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Y)
and thus the existence of a unique
\sigma_{\xi}\in W_{{\rm{per}},0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(Y,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
satisfying (
4.2
) follows by the
L^{p}
-theory for Poisson equation. The properties (
4.3
) follow exactly as in the linear case (see e.g.
[
54
, Proposition 2.1.1]
).
∎

Next, we establish needed estimates for the corrector
\phi_{\xi}
and flux corrector
\sigma_{\xi}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
{\bf a}
satisfies Assumption
2
. For every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have: There is
c=c(p,\Lambda)>0
such that

(4.4)

\|\phi_{\xi}\|_{W^{1,p}(Y)}^{p}+\|\sigma_{\xi}\|_{W^{1,p^{\prime}}(Y)}^{p^{\prime}}\leq c(\mu+|\xi|)^{p}.

If
{\bf a}
satisfies Assumption
3
, then we may sharpen the estimates to

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|V_{p}(\phi_{\xi})\|_{W^{1,2}(Y)}^{2}+\|V_{p^{\prime}}(\sigma_{\xi})\|_{W^{1,2}(Y)}^{2}\leq c|V_{p}(\xi)|^{2}.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Step 1.
Estimates for (
4.4
). For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set
F=\xi+\nabla\phi_{\xi}
. It suffices to show

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\mu+|F|\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\lesssim\mu+|\xi|.

Indeed, (
4.6
) and the growth conditions of
{\bf a}
imply

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|{\bf a}(\cdot,F)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Y)}\leq\Lambda\|\mu+|F|\|_{L^{p}(Y)}^{p-1}\lesssim(\mu+|\xi|)^{p-1}.

Estimate (
4.4
) follows from (
4.6
) in combination with triangle inequality and Poincaré’s inequality with
\fint_{Y}\phi_{\xi}=0
(for the corrector
\phi_{\xi}
) and (
4.7
) together with the fact that the map
T:L_{{\rm{per}},0}^{p^{\prime}}(Y,\mathbb{R}^{n})\to W_{{\rm{per}},0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(Y,\mathbb{R})
given by
TF:=u
with
-\Delta u=\nabla\cdot F
, is linear and bounded. Hence, it remains to show (
4.6
). For
p\in[2,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1}\int_{Y}|F|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F)-{\bf a}(y,0),F\rangle\,\mathrm{d}y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:convexcorrector}}}{{=}}\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F),\xi\rangle-\langle{\bf a}(y,0),F\rangle\,\mathrm{d}y

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lambda\int_{Y}(\mu+|F|)^{p-1}|\xi|+\mu^{p-1}|F|\,\mathrm{d}y

and (
4.6
) follows by Young’s inequality. The case
p\in(1,2)
follows in the same way using the pointwise inequality (
A.1
) (with
\nabla w
replaced by
F
).

Step 2.
Estimates for (
4.5
). Fix
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and set
F=\xi+\nabla\phi_{\xi}
. Similar computations as in Step 1 in combination with Lemma
2.6
yield

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int_{Y}|V_{p}(F)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ass:1:monotone:strong}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F),F\rangle\,\mathrm{d}y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:convexcorrector}}}{{=}}\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F),\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ass:acont}}}{{\lesssim}}\int_{Y}(1+|F|)^{p-2}|F||\xi|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:YoungV}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\Lambda}\int_{Y}|V_{p}(F)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+C(\Lambda,p)|V_{p}(\xi)|^{2}

and thus

(4.9)

\|V_{p}(F)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\lesssim|V_{p}(\xi)|^{2}.

Estimate (
4.9
), together with the triangle type inequality (
2.13
) and (
2.18
) yield
\|V_{p}(\phi_{\xi})\|_{W^{1,2}(Y)}^{2}\lesssim|V_{p}(\xi)|^{2}
.
Moreover, (
4.9
) imply
\|V_{p^{\prime}}(J(\xi))\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\lesssim|V_{p}(\xi)|^{2}
and the claimed estimate for
\sigma_{\xi}
follows from Lemma
4.3
below.
∎

Next, we provide needed estimates for the Laplace equation on the torus:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(Y,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Suppose
-\Delta u=\nabla\cdot f
in
Y
with periodic boundary conditions. For all
p\in(1,\infty)
there exists
c=c(n,p)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|V_{p}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq c\|V_{p}(f)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

Proof.

Throughout the proof we write
\lesssim
if
\leq
holds up to a positive multiplicative constant depending only on
n
and
p
. For
p\geq 2
, the claim follows directly from the usual
L^{q}
-estimates for
\nabla u
:

\|V_{p}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\lesssim\int_{Y}|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{Y}|f|^{2}+|f|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\|V_{p}(f)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}.

Suppose that
p\in(1,2)
. Clearly, we have
u=v+w
, where
v,w
are the unique periodic solutions of

-\Delta v=\nabla\cdot(\mathds{1}_{\{|f|\leq 1\}}f)\quad\mbox{and}\quad-\Delta w=\nabla\cdot(\mathds{1}_{\{|f|>1\}}f)

Combining
L^{2}
estimates for
\nabla v
with the elementary inequality
1\leq 2^{2-p}(1+|f|)^{p-2}
if
0<|f|\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|V_{p}(\nabla v)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\leq\int_{Y}|\nabla v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{Y}|(\mathds{1}_{\{|f|\leq 1\}}f)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2^{2-p}\int_{Y}(1+|f|)^{p-2}|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y=\|V_{p}(f)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}.

Similarly, we obtain with
L^{p}
-estimates for
\nabla w
and
|f|^{p-2}\leq 2^{2-p}(1+|f|)^{p-2}
if
|f|\geq 1
that

\displaystyle\|V_{p}(\nabla w)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\leq\int_{Y}|\nabla w|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{Y}|(\mathds{1}_{\{|f|>1\}}f)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2^{2-p}\int_{Y}(1+|f|)^{p-2}|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y=\|V_{p}(f)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}.

The claimed estimate for
V_{p}(\nabla u)
now follows by (
2.13
).
∎

4.2.
The homogenized operator

For
{\bf a}
satisfying Assumption
2
, we introduce the homogenized operator
\overline{{\bf a}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by setting for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi)=\int_{Y}{\bf a}(y,\xi+\nabla\phi_{\xi})\,\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
\phi_{\xi}
is defined via (
4.1
). The main aim of this section is to prove that if
{\bf a}
satisfies Assumption
1
, then
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies Assumption
2
and some continuity condition, see (
4.11
). Moreover, if
{\bf a}
satisfies Assumption
1
with
\mu=1
, then
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies Assumption
3
. This results essentially follow from
[
36
]
and in particular
[
21
, Section 7]
. However, the framework of
[
21
]
is significantly more general than our set-up and so we choose to present a self-contained presentation including proofs.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
{\bf a}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies Assumption
1
for some
1<p<\infty
,
\mu\in[0,1]
and
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
. Then
\overline{{\bf a}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies Assumption
2
with the same
1<p<\infty
,
\mu\in[0,1]
, and some
\overline{\Lambda}=\overline{\Lambda}(\Lambda,p)\in[1,\infty)
. Moreover, there is
c=c(\Lambda,p)\in[1,\infty)
such that

(4.11)

\displaystyle|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2})|\leq c\begin{cases}(\mu+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\quad&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
(\mu+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\quad&\text{ if }p\leq 2\end{cases}.

In the case
\mu=1
, the operator
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies Assumption
3
.

Proof.

The first part of this result is well-known, see e.g.
[
36
]
, but we provide a proof for completeness.
For
\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set
\xi_{j}+\nabla\phi_{\xi_{j}}=F_{j}
and recall
\int_{Y}F_{j}\,\mathrm{d}x=\xi_{j}
.

Step 1.
Proof of (
1.8
) and (
1.16
): Suppose
p\geq 2
. The convexity of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni z\mapsto(\mu+|z|)^{p-2}|z|^{2}
and Jensen inequality imply

\displaystyle(\mu+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}(\mu+|F_{1}-F_{2}|)^{p-2}|F_{1}-F_{2}|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p\geq 2,\eqref{eq:coer:intro}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\Lambda\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2}),F_{1}-F_{2}\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:convexcorrector},\eqref{def:ahom}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\Lambda\langle\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle.

In case
p\leq 2
, we instead find using Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{Y}|F_{1}-F_{2}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr)^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{Y}|F_{1}-F_{2}|^{2}(\mu+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|)^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr)\biggl(\int_{Y}(\mu+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|)^{p}\biggr)^{\frac{2-p}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\biggl(\Lambda\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2}),F_{1}-F_{2}\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr)\biggl(\int_{Y}(\mu+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|)^{p}\biggr)^{\frac{2-p}{p}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:correctorNatural}}}{{\lesssim}}

\displaystyle\langle\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle(\mu+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{2-p}.

Clearly, the above two estimates imply that
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies (
1.8
) and in the case
\mu=1
even (
1.16
)

Step 2.
Proof of (
1.9
): We estimate

\displaystyle\lvert\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})\rvert\leq\int_{Y}\lvert{\bf a}(y,F_{1})\rvert\,\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\Lambda\int_{Y}(\mu+\lvert F_{1}\rvert)^{p-1}\,\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\Lambda\left(\int_{Y}(\mu+\lvert F_{1}\rvert)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:correctorNatural}}}{{\lesssim}}(\mu+\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert)^{p-1}.

Step 3.
Proof of (
4.11
): We note that as
{\bf a}(\cdot,0)=0
, it is immediate that
\overline{{\bf a}}(0)=0
from the definition.

In case
p\geq 2
, the claim is shown in
[
36
, Proposition 2.7]
. In the case
p\leq 2
, we first observe

\displaystyle|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2})|^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}|{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2})|^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{Y}(\mu+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|)^{(p-2)p^{\prime}}|F_{1}-F_{2}|^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{\prime}\geq 2}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\int_{Y}(\mu+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|)^{p-2}|F_{1}-F_{2}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2}),F_{1}-F_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2})||\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|

and thus

(4.12)

\displaystyle|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2})|\lesssim|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{p-1}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(\xi_{2})|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}|{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{Y}(\mu+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|)^{2(p-2)}|F_{1}-F_{2}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu^{p-2}\int_{Y}(\mu+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|)^{(p-2)}|F_{1}-F_{2}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mu^{p-2}\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2}),F_{1}-F_{2}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu^{p-2}|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(\xi_{2})||\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|.

Combining the above inequality with (
4.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2})|\lesssim\min\{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{p-2},\mu^{p-2}\}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\lesssim(\mu+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Finally, we state an estimate for the difference of correctors assuming that
{\bf a}
and
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfy Assumption
3
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Suppose
{\bf a}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the homogenized operator
\overline{{\bf a}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies Assumption
3
for some joint constant
1<p<\infty
and
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
. Then there exists
c=c(p,\Lambda)>0
such that for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\tau>0
,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\|V_{p}(\nabla\phi_{\xi_{1}}-\nabla\phi_{\xi_{2}})\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\leq c\begin{cases}\left(1+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|\right)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
(|V_{p}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})|^{2}(1+\tau^{-\frac{2-p}{p}})+\tau(|V_{p}(\xi_{1})|+|V_{p}(\xi_{2})|)^{2}&\text{ if }p\leq 2\end{cases}

Proof.

As before, we denote for
i=1,2
,
F_{i}=\xi_{i}+\nabla\phi_{\xi_{i}}
. Then

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1}\int_{Y}|W_{p}(F_{1},F_{2})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2}),F_{1}-F_{2}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle.

In the case
p\geq 2
, the claim follows with help of (
1.16
),
|W_{p}(F_{1},F_{2})|^{2}=|V_{p}(F_{1}-F_{2})|^{2}
and the triangle inequality (
2.13
) in the form

|V_{p}(\nabla\phi_{\xi_{1}}-\nabla\phi_{\xi_{2}})|^{2}\lesssim|V_{p}(F_{1}-F_{2})|^{2}+|V_{p}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})|^{2}\lesssim|V_{p}(F_{1}-F_{2})|^{2}+(1+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}.

In the case
p\in(1,2)
, we use in addition (
2.22
) and the estimates (
4.5
).
∎

4.3.
A stable assumption under homogenization

In this section, we recall some (dual) monotonicity conditions, see Assumption
4
below, that are a special case of
[
21
, Section 7]
. These assumptions are satisfied by examples that are not covered by Assumption
1
, see Remark
4.6
. Moreover, they have the interesting property that they are closed under homogenization, see Lemma
4.8
below, and ensure enough regularity on
{\bf a}
and
\overline{{\bf a}}
such that the large-scale regularity theory developed in Section
5
and Section
6
applies.

Assumption 4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
be given. Suppose that
{\bf a}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that
{\bf a}(x,0)=0
,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\Lambda\langle{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq\begin{cases}(\mu+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
(\mu+\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert+\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert)^{p-2}\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert^{2}&\text{ if }p<2\end{cases}

and

(4.15)

\displaystyle\Lambda\langle{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq\begin{cases}\left(\mu^{p-1}+\lvert{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})\rvert+\lvert{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})\rvert\right)^{p^{\prime}-2}|{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})|^{2}&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
(\mu^{p-1}+\lvert{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})\rvert)^{p^{\prime}-2}|{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1}-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})|^{2}&\text{ if }p\leq 2\end{cases}.

Remark 4.6
.

Similar to our comments in Remark
3.5
, Assumption
4
and the results of this section show that our homogenization results in Section
5
cover certain oscillatory Finsler
p
-Laplacian operators. As a specific example the orthotropic p-Laplace operator with oscillating coefficients, that is
{\bf a}(y,z)=A(y)\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{p-2}z_{i}
with
p\in(1,\infty)
and a uniformly elliptic coefficient matrix
A\in L^{\infty}(Y,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
satisfies Assumption
4
. In fact, whenever
\|\cdot\|\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
\min\{p,2\}
-smooth and
\max\{p,2\}
-convex norm, then the Finsler
p
-Laplacian
{\bf a}(z)=\|z\|^{p-1}\nabla(\|z\|)
satisfies (
4
), see
[
61
]
.

We think of (
4.15
) as a coercivity assumption on
{\bf a}^{-1}
, even when
{\bf a}^{-1}
is not formally well-defined. This point of view is present in the classical literature regarding the simpler setting of quadratic growth
p=2

[
44
]
and has gained growing interest for proving regularity properties of convex minimisation problems
[
18
,
22
,
25
]
. Here we implement these ideas in the context of homogenization. It is instructive to consider the special case of the
p
-Laplacian
{\bf a}(\xi)=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
. Note that in that case

\displaystyle\langle|\xi_{1}|^{p-2}\xi_{1}-|\xi_{2}|^{p-2}\xi_{2},\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle=\langle{\bf a}(\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(\xi_{2}),|{\bf a}(\xi_{1})|^{p^{\prime}-2}{\bf a}(\xi_{1})-|{\bf a}(\xi_{2})|^{p^{\prime}-2}{\bf a}(\xi_{2})\rangle.

Hence the dual nature of the relationship betwen (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) is apparent in this case. It is therefore natural to expect that when
{\bf a}(x,z)=\partial_{z}F(x,z)
for
F
, convex as a function of
z
, (
4.15
) is equivalent to a growth condition on
{\bf a}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose
{\bf a}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies Assumption
4
for some
1<p<\infty
,
\mu\in[0,1]
and
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
. Then there exists
\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda^{\prime}(\Lambda,p)\in[1,\infty)
such that

(4.16)

\displaystyle|{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})|\leq\Lambda^{\prime}\begin{cases}(\mu+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\quad&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
(\mu+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\quad&\text{ if }p\leq 2\end{cases}.

In particular, Assumption
4
with
1<p<\infty
,
\mu=1
and
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
implies Assumption
3
with the same
1<p<\infty
and a suitable
\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda^{\prime}(\Lambda,p)\in[1,\infty)
. Moreover, if
{\bf a}(x,z)=\partial_{z}F(x,z)
for some
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then Assumption
3
implies Assumption
4
with
\mu=1
upon changing the value of
\Lambda
.

Proof.

Clearly, it suffices to show that (
4.15
) implies (
4.16
) and that Assumption
3
implies (
4.15
) with
\mu=1
in the case
{\bf a}(x,z)=\partial_{z}F(x,z)
.

Step 1.
We show that (
4.15
) implies (
4.16
). Condition (
4.15
) and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality imply

(4.17)

\displaystyle\Lambda|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\geq\begin{cases}\left(\mu^{p-1}+\lvert{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})\rvert+\lvert{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})\rvert\right)^{p^{\prime}-2}|{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})|\quad&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
(\mu^{p-1}+|{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})|)^{p^{\prime}-2}|{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})|\quad&\text{ if }p\leq 2\end{cases}.

Estimate (
4.17
) for
\xi_{1}=\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\xi_{2}=0
implies for
p\in(1,2)

\displaystyle|{\bf a}(x,\xi)|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=|{\bf a}(x,\xi)|^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}|{\bf a}(x,\xi)|\leq(\mu^{p-1}+|{\bf a}(x,\xi)|)^{p^{\prime}-2}|{\bf a}(x,\xi)|\leq\Lambda|\xi|

and for
p\geq 2

\displaystyle(\mu^{p-1}+|{\bf a}(x,\xi)|)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=(\mu^{p-1}+|{\bf a}(x,\xi)|)^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}(\mu^{p-1}+|{\bf a}(x,\xi)|)\leq\mu+\Lambda|\xi|.

Hence,

(4.18)

\displaystyle|{\bf a}(x,\xi)|\leq 2\Lambda^{p-1}(\mu+|\xi|)^{p-1}.

In the case
p\in[2,\infty)
, (
4.17
) in combination with (
4.18
),
0\leq 2-p^{\prime}=\frac{p-2}{p-1}
and the fact that
t\mapsto t^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
is subadditive imply the desired claim. In the case
p\in(1,2)
, inequality (
4.17
) implies

\displaystyle|{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})|\leq\Lambda|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|(\max\{\mu,\Lambda|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\})^{p-2}\leq 2^{2-p}\Lambda(\mu+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|,

where we use in the last inequality
\frac{1}{2}(a+b)\leq\max\{a,b\}
and
\Lambda\geq 1
.

Step 2.
If
{\bf a}(x,z)=\partial_{z}F(x,z)
for some
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denote the Fenchel conjugate of
F
by
F^{\ast}
. Then by a straightforward adaption of the discussion in
[
46
, Section 1.1.]
, for any
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
it holds that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\langle(F^{\ast})^{\prime}(\sigma_{1})-(F^{\ast})^{\prime}(\sigma_{2}),\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}\rangle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\mu^{p-1}+|\sigma_{1}|+|\sigma_{2}|)^{p^{\prime}-2}|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}|^{2}\quad&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
(\mu^{p-1}+|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}|)^{p^{\prime}-2}|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}|^{2}\quad&\text{ if }p\leq 2.\end{cases}

As
F
is superlinear, strictly convex and
C^{1}
,
F^{\prime}
and
(F^{\ast})^{\prime}
are homeomorphisms of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, it holds that
(F^{\ast})^{\prime}(F^{\prime}(z))=z
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
52
, Theorem 26.5, 26.6]
. In particular, with the choice
\sigma_{i}=F^{\prime}(z_{i})
for
i=1,2
, (
4.19
) gives (
4.15
).
∎

The advantage of Assumption
4
is that it is stable under homogenization.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose
{\bf a}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies Assumption
4
for some
1<p<\infty
,
\mu\in[0,1]
and
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
. Then
\overline{{\bf a}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies Assumption
4
with the same
1<p<\infty
,
\mu\in[0,1]
and for some
\overline{\Lambda}=\overline{\Lambda}(\Lambda,p)\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Clearly, we have
\overline{{\bf a}}(0)=0
and Step 1 of the proof of Lemma
4.4
shows that
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies (
4.14
) replacing
\Lambda
with
\overline{\Lambda}(\Lambda,p)\in[1,\infty)
. Hence, it remains to prove that
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies (
4.15
) (upon changing
\Lambda
).

Fix
p\in[2,\infty)
. We have

\displaystyle|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{Y}|{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2})|^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr)^{\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{Y}\left(\mu^{p-1}+\lvert{\bf a}(x,F_{1})\rvert+\lvert{\bf a}(x,F_{2})\rvert\right)^{p^{\prime}-2}|{\bf a}(x,F_{1})-{\bf a}(x,F_{2})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr)

\displaystyle\times\biggl(\int_{Y}(\mu^{p-1}+|{\bf a}(x,F_{1})|+|{\bf a}(x,F_{2})|)^{p^{\prime}}\biggr)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ass:2:dualmonotone},\eqref{eq:easyUpper}}}{{\lesssim}}

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2}),F_{1}-F_{2}\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr)\biggl(\int_{Y}(\mu+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|)^{p}\biggr)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:correctorNatural}}}{{\lesssim}}

\displaystyle\langle\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle(\mu+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}.

The desired inequality follows from the facts that
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies
\overline{{\bf a}}(0)=0
and (
4.14
) (with
\overline{\Lambda}
) which imply
|\xi_{i}|^{p-1}\leq\overline{\Lambda}|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{i})|
. For
p\in(1,2)
, we use convexity of
z\mapsto(\mu^{p-1}+|z|)^{p^{\prime}-2}|z|^{2}
, (
4.10
) and Jensen inequality to obtain

\displaystyle(\mu^{p-1}+\lvert\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2})\rvert)^{p^{\prime}-2}\lvert\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}(\mu^{p-1}+|{\bf a}(x,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2})|)^{p^{\prime}-2}|{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2})|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ass:2:dualmonotone}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\Lambda\int_{Y}\langle{\bf a}(y,F_{1})-{\bf a}(y,F_{2}),F_{1}-F_{2}\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:convexcorrector},\eqref{def:ahom}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\Lambda\langle\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle,

which shows that
\overline{{\bf a}}
satisfies (
4.15
) (with the same
\Lambda
) in this case.
∎

5.
Calderon-Zygmund theory on large scales

The main result of this section is a
large-scale
Calderon-Zygmund estimate from which Theorem
1.2
follows. For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we recall the definition of the maximal function and define a truncated maximal function as follows

(5.1)

M(f)(x):=\sup_{\rho>0}\fint_{B_{\rho}(x)}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\quad\mbox{and}\quad M_{r}(f)(x):=\sup_{\rho\geq r}\fint_{B_{\rho}(x)}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Theorem 5.1
.

Fix
1<p<\infty
,
\mu\in[0,1]
and
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
. Suppose
{\bf a}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is periodic in the first variable. Assume that
{\bf a}
and the corresponding homogenized operator
\overline{{\bf a}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, defined by (
4.10
), satisfy for all
x,\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
1.8
), (
1.9
) and

(5.2)

\displaystyle|{\bf a}(x,\xi_{1})-{\bf a}(x,\xi_{2})|+|\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{1})-\overline{{\bf a}}(\xi_{2})|\leq\Lambda\begin{cases}(\mu+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\quad&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
(\mu+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\quad&\text{ if }p\leq 2.\end{cases}

For every
q\in(p,\infty)
there exists
C=C(\Lambda,n,p,q)\in[1,\infty)
such that the following is true. Let
u\in W^{1,p}(B)
and
F\in L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(B)
with
B=B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varepsilon>0
be such that

(5.3)

\nabla\cdot{\bf a}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla u)=\nabla\cdot F\qquad\mbox{in $B$}

Then, it holds

(5.4)

\|M_{\varepsilon}(\mathds{1}_{B}(\mu+|\nabla u|)^{p})\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p}}(\frac{1}{2}B)}\leq C\biggl(\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B)}^{p}+\|M_{\varepsilon}(\mathds{1}_{B}|F|^{p^{\prime}})\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p}}(B)}\biggr).

Next, we prove that Theorem
5.1
in combination with Theorem
1.1
imply Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Note that due to Lemma
4.4
, we are in a situation where we may apply Theorem
5.1
.

We distinguish between small and large balls starting with the smaller ones.

Step 1.
Suppose that
B=B_{R}(x_{0})
with
R\leq 4\varepsilon
. By rescaling, we have that

\nabla\cdot a(y,\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=\nabla\cdot F_{\varepsilon}\qquad\mbox{in $B_{R/\varepsilon}(x_{0}/\varepsilon)$,}

where
u_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{-1}u(\varepsilon x)
and
F_{\varepsilon}(x):=F(\varepsilon x)
. Since
R/\varepsilon\leq 4
, we deduce from Theorem
1.1
that

\|\mu+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\|_{\underline{L}^{q}(B_{\frac{R}{2\varepsilon}}(x_{0}/\varepsilon))}^{q}\leq C\biggl(\|\mu+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{R/\varepsilon}(x))}^{q}+\|F_{\varepsilon}\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(B_{R/\varepsilon}(x_{0}/\varepsilon))}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}\biggr),

where
C=C(\Lambda,n,p,q,\omega)>0
and thus by scaling

\displaystyle\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{q}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}^{q}\leq

\displaystyle C\biggl(\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}^{q}+\|F\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}\biggr),

which proves the statement in that case.

Step 2.
Suppose that
B=B_{R}(x_{0})
with
R>4\varepsilon
. For every
x\in\frac{1}{2}B
, we use the estimate from Step 1 applied to the ball
B_{2\varepsilon}(x)\subset B
and obtain

\displaystyle\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{q}(B_{\varepsilon}(x))}^{q}\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\biggl(\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{2\varepsilon}(x))}^{q}+\|F\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(B_{2\varepsilon}(x))}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\biggl((M_{\varepsilon}(\mathds{1}_{B}(\mu^{p}+|\nabla u|^{p}))(x))^{\frac{q}{p}}+\|F\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(B_{2\varepsilon}(x))}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}\biggr),

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(\Lambda,n,p,q,\omega)>0
. Moreover, the assumption
R>4\varepsilon
ensures by Fubini

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}B}\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}(\mu+|\nabla u(y)|)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{|B_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{B}(\mu+|\nabla u(y)|)^{q}\int_{\frac{1}{2}B}\mathds{1}_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\geq\int_{\frac{1}{4}B}(\mu+|\nabla u|)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y,

where we use for the inequality
\int_{\frac{1}{2}B}\mathds{1}_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x=|\frac{1}{2}B\cap B_{\varepsilon}(y)|=|B_{\varepsilon}|
for
y\in\frac{1}{4}B
.

The above two estimates in combination with (
5.4
) yield,

\displaystyle\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{q}(\frac{1}{4}B)}^{q}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n}\fint_{\frac{1}{2}B}\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}(\mu+|\nabla u|)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n}C_{1}\biggl(\fint_{\frac{1}{2}B}(M_{\varepsilon}(\mathds{1}_{B}(\mu^{p}+|\nabla u|^{p}))(x))^{\frac{q}{p}}+\fint_{B_{2\varepsilon}(x)}|F|^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n}C_{1}C\biggl(\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B)}^{q}+\|M_{\varepsilon}(\mathds{1}_{B}|F|^{p^{\prime}})\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p}}(B)}^{\frac{q}{p}}+\|F\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(B)}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n}C_{1}Cc(n,\tfrac{q}{p})\biggl(\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B)}^{q}+\|F\|_{\underline{L}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(B)}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}\biggr),

where
C=C(\Lambda,n,p,q)>0
and we use in the last estimate the strong maximal function inequality. The claimed inequality (
1.15
) follows by a simple covering argument.
∎

The proof of Theorem
5.1
relies on a combination of Lemma
3.3
and the following comparison estimate

Lemma 5.2
.

Consider the situation of Theorem
5.1
. There exist constants
c=c(n,\Lambda,p)\in[1,\infty)
and
{\beta=\beta(\Lambda,n,p)>0}
such that the following is true: Let
u\in W^{1,p}(B)
with
B=B_{R}(x_{0})
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{R}{8}]
be such that

(5.5)

\nabla\cdot{\bf a}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla u)=0\qquad\text{ in }B.

Then there exists
v\in u+W_{0}^{1,p}(B)
satisfying,

(5.6)

\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{R})}\leq c(\varepsilon/R)^{\beta}\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{R})}.

Moreover, for every
q\in[p,\infty)
there exists
c_{q}=c_{q}(\Lambda,n,p,q)\in[1,\infty)
such that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\biggl(\fint_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\biggl(\max_{r^{\prime}\in[\varepsilon,\frac{R}{4}]}\fint_{B_{r^{\prime}}(x)}|\nabla v|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq c_{q}\|\mu+|\nabla u|\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{R})}.

The proof of Lemma
5.2
relies on homogenization estimates in combination with the higher gradient integrability result of Theorem
3.1
applied to a suitable homogenized equation. We postpone the proof of Lemma
5.2
to the end of the section and start with the proof of Theorem
5.1
by following the reasoning of
[
4
, Lemma 7.6]
in the linear case.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We consider
1<p<q<2q
. Throughout the proof we write
\lesssim
if
\leq
holds up to a positive multiplicative constant depending only on
\Lambda,n
and
p

z\in\frac{1}{2}B

x(z)\in\{z,x_{0}\}
and a radius
r(z)\leq 2\varepsilon
such that
z\in\frac{1}{2}B_{r(z)}(x(z))
and

(6.5)

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{r(z)}(x(z))}|V_{p}(\nabla u)|^{2}\leq 2^{d}C_{M}\fint_{B}|V_{p}(\nabla u)|^{2}.

Indeed, if
R\geq 2\varepsilon
, due to Step 1 we may choose
x(z)=z
and
r(z)=\varepsilon
, while if
R<2\varepsilon
, we choose
x(z)=x_{0}
and
r(z)=R
.

By rescaling, we have that

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf a}(y,\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=0\quad\text{ in }B_{r(z)/\varepsilon}(x(z)/\varepsilon),

where
u_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{-1}u(\varepsilon x)
. As
r(z)/\varepsilon\leq 2
, we deduce from essentially standard estimates, see Proposition
B.1
in Appendix
B
, that

\displaystyle\|V_{p}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r(z)/(2\varepsilon)}(x(z))}\leq C\fint_{B_{r(z)/\varepsilon}(x(z)/\varepsilon)}|V_{p}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}

with
C=C(\gamma,n,\Lambda,p)
and thus by scaling

\displaystyle\|V_{p}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r(z)/2}(x(z)))}\leq C\fint_{B_{r(z)}(x(z))}|V_{p}(\nabla u)|^{2},

which in combination with (
6.5
) proves the statement.
∎

6.1.
Auxiliary results

In this section, we collect a regularity result for autonomous operators satisfying Assumption
3
and a two-scale homogenization estimate in the situation of Theorem
6.1
.

We begin with the regularity results.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that
{\bf a}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies Assumption
3
for some
1<p<\infty
,
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
. There exist
c=c(\Lambda,n,p)>0
and
\Gamma=\Gamma(n,p)>0
such that the following is true: Suppose that
B=B(x,r)\Subset\Omega
,
w\in W^{1,p}(B)
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy

(6.6)

\nabla\cdot{\bf a}(\zeta+\nabla w)=0

and

|V_{p}(\zeta)|^{2}+\fint_{B}|V_{p}(\nabla w)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq M\in[1,\infty).

Then

(6.7)

\displaystyle r\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\frac{1}{2}B)}+\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{1}{2}B)}\leq

\displaystyle cM^{\Gamma}\|V_{p}(\nabla w)\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B)}

Moreover, there exist
\alpha=\alpha(\Lambda,M,n,p)>0
and
C_{M}=C_{M}(\Lambda,M,n,p)>0
such that

(6.8)

r^{\alpha}\|\nabla w\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\frac{1}{2}B)}\leq C_{M}\|V_{p}(\nabla w)\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B)}.

Proof.

The crucial part of the proof is to obtain the
L^{\infty}
bound on
\nabla w
stated in (
6.7
). This can be done in a self-contained way by revisiting the Moser type iteration in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, using the stronger monotonicity and continuity assumptions on
{\bf a}
and the higher integrability of Theorem
3.1
. However, we present here a shorter argument using known results for linear non-uniformly elliptic equations. Throughout the proof we write
\lesssim
if
\leq
holds up to a multiplicative positive constant depending only on
\Lambda,n
and
p
.

Step 1.
Suppose that
{\bf a}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
B=B_{1}(0)
. We claim that

(6.9)

\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/8})}+\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/4})}\lesssim M^{\Gamma}\biggl(\fint_{B_{1/2}}|V_{p}(\nabla w)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and there exists
C_{M}=C_{M}(\Lambda,M,n,p)>0
and
\alpha=\alpha(\Lambda,M,n,p)\in(0,1)
such that

(6.10)

[\nabla w]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/8})}\leq C_{M}\biggl(\fint_{B_{1/2}}|V_{p}(\nabla w)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The differentiability of
{\bf a}
and the bounds (
1.16
) and (
1.17
) imply

(6.11)

\min\{1,(1+|z|)^{p-2}\}|\xi|^{2}\lesssim\partial\overline{{\bf a}}(z)\xi\cdot\xi,\qquad|\partial\overline{{\bf a}}(z)\xi|\lesssim\max\{1,(1+|z|)^{p-2})\}|\xi|.

Note that by Theorem
3.1
, we have that
\nabla w\in L_{\rm loc}^{q}(B)
for every
q<\infty
. With help of the higher gradient integrability and the difference quotient method, it is easy to see that the partial derivatives
\partial_{i}w
,
i=1,\dots,n
are weakly differentiable satisfying

(6.12)

\nabla\cdot(A\nabla\partial_{i}w)=0\quad\mbox{where}\quad A(x):=\partial{\bf a}(\zeta+\nabla w(x))

and
\min\{1,(1+|\zeta+\nabla w|)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\}\nabla\partial_{i}w\in L_{\rm loc}^{2}(B)
. Now we are in position to apply local boundedness results for linear non-uniformly elliptic equation, e.g.
[
56
,
9
]
, to (
6.12
). In order to apply the results from
[
9
]
, we observe that (
6.11
) implies that the quantities

\lambda(x):=\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle\xi,A(x)\xi\rangle}{|\xi|^{2}},\quad\mu(x):=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|A(x)\xi|^{2}}{\langle\xi,A(x)\xi\rangle}

satisfy

1\lesssim\lambda,\quad\mu\lesssim(1+|\zeta+\nabla w|)^{2(p-2)}\mbox{ if $p\geq 2$ and }(1+|\zeta+\nabla w|)^{p-2}\lesssim\lambda,\quad\mu\lesssim 1\mbox{ if $p\in(1,2)$.}

Since
\nabla w\in L_{\rm loc}^{q}(B)
for every
q<\infty
, we have
\lambda^{-1},\mu\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(B)
for every
q<\infty
and thus we can apply
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
with
\gamma=\min\{2,p\}
to find
\Gamma=\Gamma(n,p)>0
such that

(6.13)

\|\partial_{i}w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/4})}\lesssim\biggl(\fint_{B_{1/2}}(1+|\zeta+\nabla w|)^{2n|p-2|}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)^{\Gamma}\biggl(\fint_{B_{1/2}}|\partial_{i}w|^{\min\{p,2\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)^{\frac{1}{\min\{p,2\}}}.

The claimed estimate on
\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/4})}
in (
6.9
) in case
p\geq 2
follows by summing over
i=1,\dots,n
, applying Theorem
3.1
in the form

\fint_{B_{1/2}}(1+|\zeta+\nabla w|)^{2n|p-2|}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\biggl(\fint_{B_{1}}(1+|\zeta|+|\nabla w|)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)^{\frac{2n|p-2|}{p}}\lesssim M^{\frac{2n|p-2|}{p}}

and redefining
\Gamma
(still depending only on
n
and
p
). In the case
p\leq 2
, we additionally apply Hölder’s inequality to estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla w\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{1/2})}\leq\|V_{p}(\nabla w)\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{1/2})}\|1+|\nabla w|\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(B_{1/2})}^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\lesssim\|V_{p}(\nabla w)\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{1/2})}M^{\frac{2-p}{2p}}.

The claimed
H^{2}
- and
C^{0,\alpha}
estimates of (
6.9
) and (
6.10
) follow by standard elliptic regularity for the equation (
6.12
) using the fact that by the
L^{\infty}
bound on
\nabla w
in (
6.9
) the coefficient
A
is uniformly elliptic in
B_{1/4}
with an ellipticity ratio that is bounded by a power of
M
.

Step 2.
Conclusion. Step 1 and a simple scaling and covering argument imply the claimed estimates (
6.7
) and (
6.8
) under the additional assumption that
{\bf a}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In order to remove the differentiability assumption, we use a standard approximation argument. Let
\varphi
be a non-negative, radially symmetric mollifier, i.e. it satisfies

\varphi\geq 0,\quad{\rm supp}\;\varphi\subset B_{1},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)\,dx=1,\quad\varphi(\cdot)=\widetilde{\varphi}(|\cdot|)\quad\mbox{for some $\widetilde{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$}.

For
\delta\in(0,1]
, set
{\bf a}_{\delta}:={\bf a}\ast\varphi_{\delta}
, where
\varphi_{\delta}:=\delta^{-n}\varphi(\cdot/\delta)
. Clearly,
{\bf a}_{\delta}
is smooth and satisfies (
1.16
) with the same constants and the continuity property (
1.17
) with the same constants in the case
p\in(1,2]
and
1+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|
replaced by
3+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|
in the case
p>2
. Let
w_{\delta}\in W^{1,p}(B)
be the unique solution to

\nabla\cdot{\bf a}_{\delta}(\zeta+\nabla w_{\delta})=0\quad\mbox{in $B$}\quad w_{\delta}-w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(B).

The energy estimate (
2.34
) implies

|V_{p}(\zeta)|^{2}+\fint_{B}|V_{p}(\nabla w_{\delta})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim M\qquad\mbox{for all $\delta\in(0,1]$}.

Hence,
w_{\delta}
satisfies (
6.7
) and (
6.8
) for all
\delta\in(0,1]
. By standard monotonicity arguments (using
|{\bf a}_{\delta}(\xi)-{\bf a}(\xi)|\lesssim\delta^{\min\{1,p-1\}}
), we find
w_{\delta}\to w
in
W^{1,p}(B)
. The desired estimate simply follow by standard weak lower semicontinuity results (and possibly replacing the Hölder exponent
\alpha
by
\alpha/2
).
∎

\displaystyle c\left(R_{0}^{\alpha p_{2}}+\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{n}\right)\int_{B_{R}}|V_{p}(\nabla u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Appealing to a standard iteration argument, see e.g.

) holds for

Step 4.

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|V_{p}(\nabla u)-(V_{p}(\nabla u))_{B_{r}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2\int_{B_{r}}|V_{p}(\nabla v)-(V_{p}(\nabla v))_{B_{r}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+2\int_{B_{R}}|V_{p}(\nabla u)-V_{p}(\nabla v)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For the first term, we use (
B.6
) and triangle inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|V_{p}(\nabla v)-(V_{p}(\nabla v))_{B_{r}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{n+2\beta}\int_{B_{R}}|V_{p}(\nabla v)-(V_{p}(\nabla v))_{B_{R}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2c\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{n+2\beta}\int_{B_{R}}|V_{p}(\nabla u)-(V_{p}(\nabla u))_{B_{R}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+2c\int_{B_{R}}|V_{p}(\nabla u)-V_{p}(\nabla v)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

where we use (

. Combining the previous estimates we find

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|V_{p}(\nabla u)-(V_{p}(\nabla u))_{B_{r}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle c\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{n+2\gamma}\int_{B_{R}}|V_{p}(\nabla u)-(V_{p}(\nabla u))_{B_{R}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+cR^{n+\gamma}\fint_{B_{1}}|V_{p}(\nabla u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

A novel approach to coincidence point results via proximal contractions with application
Authors: Haroon Ahmad, Umar Ishtiaq, Mohammad Akram, Ioan-Lucian Popa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this manuscript, we established coincidence point theory by using F-contractions within complete extended suprametric spaces. The results about coincidence points involve -proximal contractions together with rational -proximal contractions, with supporting examples included. The manuscript demonstrates the best proximity point theories and provides corresponding examples to reinforce the theoretical findings that operate under the developed contraction method. Moreover, we explore the fixed point outcome that culminated from these contractions, proving their applicability with real examples. The study investigates the relation between coincidence points and best proximity points as well as fixed points that arise from -proximal and rational -proximal contractions. Our theoretical findings gain practical meaning through an analysis of a nonlinear boundary value problem that stems from satellite web interactions. The differential equation that describes the spacecraft web structure thermal radiation behavior follows the Stefan-Boltzmann law. Through fixed point theory, we develop requirements that lead to finding unique solutions for temperature distribution problems. The analysis confirms how fixed point theory serves essential roles for addressing practical space technology problems with special relevance to spacecraft temperature research.Subject terms: Applied mathematics, Pure mathematics

Introduction and basic concepts

Fixed point theory provides essential mathematical solutions for equations written as which operates under mappings found in metric spaces, linear spaces as well as topological vector spaces. This theory demonstrates important knowledge about how different mappings behave during iterative processes alongside ensuring their stability. Every mapping does not always contain fixed points among its solutions. Mappings that do not meet the requirements for self-maps between disjoint sets together with mappings requiring strict conditions generally do not have fixed points. This restriction stimulated the development of ideas that generalize fixed point results to circumstances in which exact solutions might not be possible but can be estimated.

Sadiq Basha et al.1,2 in 1997 introduced the important extension known as best proximity pairs. Best proximity point theory provides an effective solution to problems where mappings T fail to have fixed points. The method identifies an element which produces minimum error through the function instead of attempting to find exact solutions where applies. The objective is to achieve the smallest possible value of deviation from the fixed point condition. A non-self-mapping T from to produces the best proximity point that achieves minimum distance value through satisfying . This theory expands classical fixed point results because it simplifies to fixed point solutions when T operates as a self-mapping.

Researchers have conducted thorough investigations to develop and improve best proximity point theory after establishing its foundation. Eldred et al.3 presented mapping identification techniques for best proximity points in uniform Banach spaces. The development strengthened our capability to understand best proximity points in normed spaces containing strict convexity conditions. The field received further development, when Baria et al.4 established an improved method to analyze mappings with cyclic conditions through Cyclic Meir-Keeler contractions. Through their paper Kikkawa et al.5 investigated the complex connection between Kannan mappings and contraction mappings which offered enhanced understanding of fixed point and proximity results.

Anuradha et al.6 developed proximal pointwise contraction as a major breakthrough to apply contraction principles when proximity played an essential role in studying convergence problems. Suzuki et al.7 established the UC property which guarantees the convergence of iterates when used in proximity mappings. Abkar et al.8 utilized this property to show that asymptotic cyclic contractions have unique best proximity points in their research paper. Basha et al.9,10 introduced new best proximity point theorems which ensure globally optimal approximate solutions through their formulations.

Admissible mappings have come to be as a highly regarded research topic for approximation theory frameworks. Samet et al.11 established the concept of -admissible mappings which serve as vital tools for developing generalized versions of contraction conditions. Jleli et al.12 developed -proximal admissible mappings to establish new structural conditions that help prove the existence of proximity points. The creation of generalized contractions alongside their fixed and proximity point results became possible through new mathematical structural developments.

The research by Komal et al.13 introduced generalized Geraghty proximal cyclic contractions as an important development. The experts used their research to develop novel findings about coincidence best proximity points in complete metric spaces which improved practical applications for nonlinear analysis. The researchers of Latif et al.14 employed partially ordered metric spaces to demonstrate coincidence best proximity point results for F-weak contractive mappings where D. Wardowski first introduced in15. These findings showed how order-theoretic techniques can be utilized to improve and expand best approximation point theorems.

The groundwork for this field emerged from Kirk et al.16, who studied proximinal contractions to create basic proofs about best proximity pairs and identify the essential role of proximity mappings. Sankar Raj17 developed best proximity point theory for weakly contractive non-self-mappings to advance knowledge about their attributes in nonlinear analytical frameworks. Suzuki et al.18 investigated best proximity points within metric spaces having the property UC while demonstrating how particular geometric conditions lead to their existence. Researchers continue to show great interest in studying weak contractions and how they influence best proximity points, as this area remains highly important in academic research. The research by Vetro and Suzuki19 established several existence results for weak contractions in their investigation into fixed points and best proximity points. Their findings provided a link between conventional contraction analysis and emerging analytic methodologies that employ proximity frameworks.

The field of proximity theory has received fresh enrichments through recent progress within multivalued mapping environments. The research by Saleem et al.20 delivered essential results about coincidence best proximity points which enhanced proximity analysis theory. Zahid et al.21 conducted a research on multivalued proximal contractions with integral equations applications to expand the functional analysis domain of best proximity point theory.

The research by Younis et al.22, investigated best proximity points for multivalued mappings in equations of motion thus showing practical value for physical systems that use proximity theorems. The study of proximal contractions in 2D Volterra integral equations explored in 2024 by Ahmad et al.23 which expanded the practical applications of best proximity point theory results. Zahid et al.24, linked fractal mathematical models to proximal F-iterated function systems thus bringing fractal geometry into close proximity with proximity point theory. These many developments have transformed best proximity point theory into an extensive knowledge base which connects with topological spaces and functional analysis and dynamical systems theory. Through current developments in contraction principles together with proximity conditions mathematical researchers effectively developed broader structures that tackle multiple issues throughout optimization areas, integral equations and dynamical system analysis.

In 1906 Fréchet25 laid the foundation of metric space theory by creating a distance function that fulfilled non-negative symmetry together with triangle inequality requirements. The progression of the latest analysis is driven by studies regarding convergence and frameworks centered around continuity and compaction. Bakhtin et al.26 in 1989 established contraction mapping principles which apply to quasi-metric spaces with asymmetric distances in order to enhance the adoption of contraction theory across these non-symmetric platforms. Czerwik et al.27 established b-metric spaces in 1993 with his research about distance functions that maintained powerful triangle inequalities to build flexible frameworks for non-standard contraction mappings along with fixed point theories in new distance frameworks. The research from Berzig presented suprametric spaces in28,29 to develop new approaches for understanding the triangle inequality. He combined theoretical research with practical applications that emerged exclusively when standard distance measures proved ineffective for measuring object connections. Recently, Berzig in30, the author introduced the concept of b-suprametric spaces and established nonlinear contraction principles in this setting. Again in the same year, Berzig31 proposed the notion of strong b-suprametric spaces, deriving new fixed point principles with wider applicability. The framework of extended suprametric spaces which Panda et al.32 designed during 2023 incorporates supplementary structural constraints to enhance the resolution of functional equations, fixed point theory and advanced mathematical theorem applications. Continuous development of traditional metric spaces began through multiple generalization schemes that lead to improved theoretical research possibilities in mathematical disciplines with practical applications.

Inspired by the significant literature regarding coincidence points and best proximity points, this paper advances the study of fixed points by examining best proximity points and coincidence points through novel unified contraction structures, thereby highlighting the essential role of best proximity point methodology in fixed point theory. Considerable progress on best proximity points has reached extensive development within metric and Banach spaces yet the same technique remains an unsolved challenge for complete extended suprametric spaces32. This manuscript fills the existing research gap by defining -proximal contraction and rational -proximal contraction to prove coincidence point results in a more general framework. The research establishes systematic findings for best proximity points and fixed points under unified contraction conditions while showing their relationship using specific illustrative examples. This research analyses real-life solution existence for a nonlinear boundary value problem which describes satellite web coupling by examining its mathematical framework. A differential equation regulates thermal radiation behavior in spacecraft web structures but its nonlinear dynamics appear due to dependence on Stefan-Boltzmann law. Our previously developed fixed point results help us find conditions that establish both existence and uniqueness of temperature distribution solutions. Fixed point theory showcases its advantage for nonlinear differential equation solution in engineering and space technology applications according to this research.

Discussion

In summary, we presented the concept of coincidence points through F-contractions in complete extended suprametric spaces within this manuscript. We settled results for coincidence points, best proximity points and fixed points using -proximal and rational -proximal contractions together with supporting examples that validated our theoretical work. We examined key relationships between main concepts to strengthen their use in mathematical analysis. Our research results find practical application through their application to a nonlinear boundary value problem of satellite web coupling that deals with thermal radiation effects in spacecraft structures. The application of fixed point analysis led us to develop criteria that prove the existence and uniqueness of temperature distribution solutions that abide by the Stefan-Boltzmann law. Fixed-point theory effectively solves engineering problems in real-world applications when used simultaneously with space technology thermal analysis. Future investigations should adopt this method to various nonlinear systems while simultaneously investigating fresh applications between mathematics and engineering fields.

Open Problem: Can the results established in Corollaries 25 and 28 be applied to investigate the existence and uniqueness of solutions for the King Cobra model36 and the Aizawa system37?

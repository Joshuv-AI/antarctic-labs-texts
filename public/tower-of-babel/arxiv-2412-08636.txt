Deformation Openness of Big Fundamental Groups and Applications
Ya Deng, Chikako Mese, Botong Wang

Abstract
. In 2001, de Oliveira, Katzarkov, and Ramachandran conjectured that the property of smooth projective varieties having big fundamental groups is stable under small deformations. This conjecture was proven by Benoît Claudon in 2010 for surfaces and for threefolds under suitable assumptions. In this paper, we prove this conjecture for smooth projective varieties admitting a big complex local system. Moreover, we address a more general conjecture by Campana and Claudon concerning the deformation invariance of the Γ \Gamma -dimension of projective varieties. As an application, we establish the deformation openness of pseudo-Brody hyperbolicity for projective varieties endowed with a big and semisimple complex local system. To achieve these results, we develop the deformation regularity of equivariant pluriharmonic maps into Euclidean buildings and Riemannian symmetric spaces in families, along with techniques from the reductive and linear Shafarevich conjectures.

1.
Introduction

In
[
Sha77
]
, Shafarevich conjectured that the universal cover of a smooth complex projective variety
X
is holomorphically convex. If true, this conjecture implies the existence of a proper holomorphic fibration
{\rm sh}_{X}:X\to{\rm Sh}(X)
(referred to as the Shafarevich morphism), which contracts to a point precisely those closed subvarieties
Z
for which
{\rm Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)]
is finite. In
[
Kol93
,
Cam94
]
, Campana and Kollár independently established the existence of such a map, up to birational equivalence (so-called

\Gamma
-reduction
or
Shafarevich map
, cf.
Theorem

1.2
below). In
[
Eys04
,
EKPR12
]
, Eyssidieux, Katzarkov, Pantev and Ramachandran proved the Shafarevich conjecture for smooth projective varieties with
linear fundamental groups
. Recall that a finitely generated group

\Gamma
is called
linear
(resp.
reductive
)
if there exists an almost faithful (i.e. with finite kernel) linear (resp.
reductive
) representation
\Gamma\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. For more recent progress on the Shafarevich conjecture, see
[
DYK23
]
.

In
[
Kol95
, §18.4]
, Kollár asked the question of how Shafarevich maps for complex projective varieties vary in families.
Precise conjectures were later proposed by de Oliveira, Katzarkov, and Ramachandran
[
dOKR02
]
, as well as by Campana and Claudon
[
Cla10
]
. This paper aims to explore these conjectures, offering some applications to the hyperbolicity of algebraic varieties.

1.1.
Deformation of varieties with big fundamental groups

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold
X
. We say
X
has
big
(or
generically large
in
[
Kol95
]
) fundamental group if for any positive-dimensional irreducible subvariety
Z
of
X
passing through a very general point, the image
{\rm Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)]
is an infinite group. Similarly, a representation
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
is called
big
if for any positive-dimensional irreducible subvariety
Z
of
X
passing through a very general point, the image
\varrho({\rm Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)])
is an infinite group.

As seen in the above definition, we emphasize that throughout this paper, when discussing properties of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)
of a compact Kähler manifold
X
, or representations
\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, we are not solely referring to the abstract group structure of
\pi_{1}(X)
. Instead, we also take into account the structure inherently tied to the complex structure of
X
itself.

In
[
dOKR02
, Conjecture 1.1]
, de Oliveira, Katzarkov, and Ramachandran proposed the following intriguing conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
dOKR02
]
)
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a smooth projective family of varieties over the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. For each
t\in\mathbb{D}
, let
X_{t}:=f^{-1}(t)
denote the fiber over
t
. If
X_{0}
has big fundamental group, then
X_{t}
also has big fundamental group for sufficiently small
t\in\mathbb{D}
.

Conjecture

1.1
as stated here is more general than in
[
dOKR02
, Conjecture 1.1]
, as we do not assume the existence of a linear representation of
\pi_{1}(X_{0})
with infinite image. This conjecture was proved by Claudon for the case
\dim X_{0}=2
(cf.
[
Cla10
, Corollaire 1.1]
). Furthermore, he established
Conjecture

1.1
for
\dim X_{0}=3
(cf.
[
Cla10
, Théorème 0.4]
), contingent upon a conjecture regarding the virtual abelianity of the fundamental groups of special Kähler orbifold surfaces (cf.
[
Cla10
, Conjecture 3]
).
However, beyond these works, there has been no progress on this conjecture in higher-dimensional cases.

In this paper, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem A

(=
7.16.(ii)
)
.

Conjecture

1.1
holds if there exists a big representation
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X_{0})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. In particular, it holds if
\pi_{1}(X_{0})
is linear.

1.2.
\Gamma
-dimension under deformation

In
[
Cam94
]
, Campana introduced the notion of the
\Gamma
-dimension
(distinct from the Von Neumann dimension in
[
Ati76
]
!), based on his construction of the
\Gamma
-reduction
. This construction was also obtained independently by Kollár for algebraic varieties
[
Kol93
]
under the name
Shafarevich map
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
Cam94
,
Kol93
]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
H\triangleleft\pi_{1}(X)
be a normal subgroup. Then there exists proper surjective,
almost holomorphic
map
\gamma_{(X,H)}:X\dashrightarrow\Gamma_{H}(X)
with connected fibers such that for any closed positive-dimensional subvariety
Z\subset X
passing through a very general point of
X
,
\gamma_{(X,H)}(Z)
is a point if and only if
{\rm Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)/H]
is finite. Such a map is unique up to bimeromorphic equivalence. ∎

Here, an almost holomorphic map refers to a meromorphic map whose indeterminacy locus does not dominate its image. When
H
is a finite subgroup, one can verify that
\gamma_{(X,H)}
is bimeromorphic to
\gamma_{(X,\{e\})}
, and in this case we simply write
\gamma_{X}:X\dashrightarrow\Gamma(X)
for
\gamma_{(X,H)}:X\dashrightarrow\Gamma_{H}(X)
, and call it the
\Gamma
-reduction of
X
.

The following definition is introduced in
[
Cam94
, Définition 4.1]
.

Definition 1.3

(
\Gamma
-reduction,
\Gamma
-dimension)
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
H\triangleleft\pi_{1}(X)
be a normal subgroup. The almost holomorphic map
\gamma_{(X,H)}:X\dashrightarrow\Gamma_{H}(X)
in
Theorem

1.2
is called the
\Gamma
-reduction
or
Shafarevich map
of
(X,H)
. The
\Gamma
-dimension
of
(X,H)
, denoted by
\gamma d(X,H)
, is
\dim\Gamma_{H}(X)
.

Remark 1.4
.

The
\Gamma
-dimension, roughly speaking, measures the positivity of the fundamental group of a compact Kähler manifold. By
Theorem

1.2
, it can be viewed as an analogue of the Kodaira dimension of canonical bundles for the fundamental groups of compact Kähler manifolds, with the
\Gamma
-reduction playing a role similar to the Iitaka fibration. In two extreme cases,
X
has big (resp. finite) fundamental group if and only if
\gamma d(X)=\dim X
(resp.
\gamma d(X)=0
). In
[
Cla10
]
, varieties with big fundamental groups are also referred to as being of

\pi_{1}
-general type
, analogous to the concept of maximal Kodaira dimension. In
[
Cam95
]
, Campana established an upper bound for
\gamma d(X)
in terms of an algebraic-geometric invariant
\kappa^{+}(X)
, which is determined by the positivity properties of
\Omega^{p}_{X}
for any
p>0
.

As an analogue to the invariance of plurigenera proved by Siu
[
Siu98
,
Siu02
]
(cf. also
[
Pău07
]
), Campana and Claudon proposed the following conjecture, which is considerably more general than
Conjecture

1.1
.

Conjecture 1.5

(
[
Cla10
, Conjecture 2]
)
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a smooth proper Kähler family. Then
t\mapsto\gamma d(X_{t})
is a continuous (hence constant) function on
\mathbb{D}
.

Here we say
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
is a
smooth proper Kähler family
if
\mathscr{X}
is a Kähler manifold and
f
is a holomorphic proper submersion with connected fibers.

In
[
Cla10
]
, Claudon proved
Conjecture

1.5
when
\dim X_{0}=2
, and in the case where
\dim X_{0}=3
and
\kappa(X_{0})\leq 2
.

We prove
Conjecture

1.5
for Kähler varieties with rigid and integral fundamental groups.

Theorem B

(=
Theorem

3.7
)
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a proper smooth Kähler family. If there exists an almost faithful reductive representation
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X_{0})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, which is rigid and integral, then
t\mapsto\gamma d(X_{t})
is constant on
\mathbb{D}
.

Remark 1.6
.

By a deep theorem of Esnault and Groechenig
[
EG18
]
, any
cohomologically rigid
local system on a smooth projective variety is integral. Consequently,
Theorem

B
also holds if
X_{0}
is a smooth projective variety and
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X_{0})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
is assumed to be only almost faithful and cohomologically rigid.

We also prove the lower semi-continuity for
\Gamma
-dimension of varieties with linear fundamental groups, which includes
Theorem

A
as a special case.

Theorem C

(=
Theorems

5.6
and
7.16.(i)
)
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a smooth projective family.

(i)

Let
M_{t}:=M_{\mathrm{B}}(\pi_{1}(X_{t}),\mathrm{GL}_{N})(\mathbb{C})
denote the Betti moduli space of
\pi_{1}(X_{t})
. Define
H_{t}\triangleleft\pi_{1}(X_{t})
as the intersection of the kernels of all reductive representations
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X_{t})\to\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Then the function
t\mapsto\gamma d(X_{t},H_{t})
on
\mathbb{D}
is
lower semicontinuous
.

(ii)

If
\pi_{1}(X_{0})
is linear, then
t\mapsto\gamma d(X_{t})
is also a lower semicontinuous function on
\mathbb{D}
.

To prove
Theorems

A
,
B
and
C
, we establish the deformation regularity of equivariant harmonic maps to Euclidean buildings or symmetric spaces in families (see
Theorems

2.5
,
4.29
,
5.4
and
7.7
). We then apply these results, together with techniques in
[
Eys04
,
EKPR12
,
DYK23
]
on the Shafarevich conjecture to prove
Theorems

A
,
B
and
C
.

1.3.
Applications to hyperbolicity

We give an application of
C.(i)
to the hyperbolicity of algebraic varieties under deformation. First, we first recall the following definition of hyperbolicity (see
[
Dem20
,
CDY22
]
for related results and other notions of hyperbolicity).

Definition 1.7

(Pseudo Brody hyperbolicity)
.

A compact complex manifold
X
is called
pseudo-Brody hyperbolic
if there is a proper Zariski closed subset
Z\subsetneq X
such that any non-constant holomorphic map
f:\mathbb{C}\to X
has image in
Z
.
If
Z=\varnothing
,
X
is simply called
Brody hyperbolic
.

Recall the following classical result on the openness of Brody hyperbolicity (see e.g.
[
Dem20
, Proposition 1.10]
).

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic proper submersion from a complex manifold
\mathscr{X}
to the unit disk with connected fibers. If
X_{0}
is Brody hyperbolic, then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
X_{t}
is Brody hyperbolic for
|t|<\varepsilon
.

There has long been a folklore conjecture that such openness properties hold for pseudo-Brody hyperbolicity.

Conjecture 1.9
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be as in
Theorem

1.8
. If
X_{0}
is pseudo-Brody hyperbolic, then
X_{t}
is also pseudo Brody hyperbolic for sufficiently small
t
.

This conjecture is indeed a consequence of the following generalized Green-Griffiths-Lang conjecture.

Conjecture 1.10

(Generalized GGL conjecture)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. Then
X
is pseudo-Brody hyperbolic if and only if it is of general type.

Let us explain how to prove
Conjecture

1.9
assuming
Conjecture

1.10
. If
X_{0}
is pseudo-Brody hyperbolic, then
X_{0}
is of general type by
Conjecture

1.10
. By the invariance of plurigenera,
X_{t}
is of general type for any
t\in\mathbb{D}
. By
Conjecture

1.10
again,
X_{t}
is pseudo-Brody hyperbolic. This proves
Conjecture

1.9
.

In
[
CDY22
, Theorem C]
, we prove
Conjecture

1.10
if there exists a big and reductive representation
\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. We will apply
C.(i)
together with
[
CDY22
, Theorem C]
and
[
DYK23
]
to prove the following result on
Conjecture

1.9
.

Theorem D

(=
Theorem

6.1
)
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a smooth projective family. Assume that there is a big and reductive representation
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X_{0})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. If
X_{0}
is pseudo-Brody hyperbolic, then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
X_{t}
is also pseudo-Brody hyperbolic for sufficiently small
t
.

As a consequence, we prove the following result.

Corollary E
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. Assume that either

(a)

there is a complex variation of Hodge structure (
\mathbb{C}
-VHS for short)
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
with discrete monodromy
\Gamma
such that the period map
X\to\mathscr{D}/\Gamma
is generically finite onto the image, or

(b)

there is a big representation
\tau:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
such that the Zariski closure of
\tau(\pi_{1}(X))
is a semisimple algebraic group.

Then every small projective deformation of
X
is pseudo-Brody hyperbolic.

Remark 1.11
.

The hyperbolicity results stated in this subsection also hold if we replace pseudo-Brody hyperbolicity with the stronger notion, known as pseudo-Picard hyperbolicity, introduced in
[
Den23
,
CDY22
]
. To lighten the notion, we consider only pseudo-Brody hyperbolicity in this paper, and interested readers can refer to
[
CDY22
]
for other notions of hyperbolicity.

1.4.
Notation and Convention

(a)

Throughout this paper,
\pi_{1}(X)
always refers to the topological fundamental group of the variety
X
.

(b)

For a complex space
X
, we denote by
X^{\rm reg}
the regular locus of
X
.

(c)

Unless otherwise specified, all projective varieties are assumed to be defined over the field of complex numbers.

(d)

Denote by
\mathbb{D}
the unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
, and by
\mathbb{D}^{*}
the punctured unit disk. We write
\mathbb{D}_{\varepsilon}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|<\varepsilon\}
,
\mathbb{D}^{*}_{\varepsilon}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid 0<|z|<r\}
.

(e)

A
smooth projective family

f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
is a smooth projective morphism from
\mathscr{X}
to
\mathbb{D}
with connected fibers. A
smooth proper Kähler family

f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic proper submersion from a Kähler manifold
\mathscr{X}
to
\mathbb{D}
with connected fibers. For any
t\in\mathbb{D}
, let
X_{t}:=f^{-1}(t)
denote the fiber of
f
over
t
. For notational simplicity, we often write
X
instead of
X_{0}
. Denote by
\pi_{\mathscr{X}}:\widetilde{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathscr{X}
the universal covering map.

(f)

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be as above, and let
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X_{0})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(K)
be a representation, where
K
is any field. We denote by
\varrho_{t}:\pi_{1}(X_{t})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(K)
the representation induced by the composition of
\varrho
with the natural isomorphism
\pi_{1}(X_{t})\to\pi_{1}(X_{0})
induced by
f
.

(g)

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open domain endowed with a Riemannian metric
g
, and let
z\in\Omega
be any point. We denote by
\mathbb{B}_{r}(z)
the Euclidean ball of radius
r
in
\Omega
centered at
z
, and by
B_{r}(z)
the ball of radius
r
in
\Omega
centered at
z
with respect to the Riemannian metric
g
.

(h)

Let
G
be a reductive group defined over a non-archimedean local field
K
. We denote by
\Delta(G)_{K}
the Bruhat-Tits building of
G
, which is a non-positively curved (NPC) space. Denote by
d(\bullet,\bullet)
the distance function on
\Delta(G)_{K}
. Let
\mathscr{D}G
denote the derived group of
G
, which is semisimple.

(i)

A linear representation
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(K)
, where
K
is a field, is called
reductive
if the Zariski closure of
\varrho(\pi_{1}(X))
is a reductive algebraic group over
\overline{K}
.

2.
Deformation of harmonic bundles

The aim of this section is to prove
Theorem

2.5
, which was used in the proofs of
Theorem

B
and
Theorem

C
.

2.1.
Some preliminary on Higgs bundle and harmonic bundle

We first recall the definition of Higgs bundles and harmonic bundles. We refer the readers to
[
Cor88
,
Sim88
,
Sim92
]
for further details.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold. A
Higgs bundle
on
X
consists of
(E,\bar{\partial}_{E},\theta)
where
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle with
\bar{\partial}_{E}
its complex structure, and
\theta:E\to E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}
is a holomorphic one form with value in
{\rm\small End}(E)
, say
Higgs field
, satisfying
\theta\wedge\theta=0
. We will usually write
(E,\theta)
instead of
(E,\bar{\partial}_{E},\theta)
if no confusion arises.

Let
(E,\theta)
be a Higgs bundle over a complex manifold
X
.
Suppose
h
is a smooth hermitian metric of
E
. Denote by
\nabla_{h}
the Chern connection with respect to
h
, and by
\theta^{*}_{h}
the adjoint of
\theta
with respect to
h
. The metric
h
is
harmonic
if the operator
D_{h}:=\nabla_{h}+\theta+\theta_{h}^{*}

is integrable, that is, if
D_{h}^{2}=0
. We write
\theta^{*}
instead of
\theta_{h}^{*}
to simply the notation if no confusion arises.

Definition 2.2

(Harmonic bundle)
.

A harmonic bundle on a complex manifold
X
is
a Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
endowed with a harmonic metric
h
.

Definition 2.3
.

For a harmonic bundle
(E,\theta,h)
on a complex manifold
X
, its
canonical form
is a semi-positive closed
(1,1)
-form on
X
defined by

\sqrt{-1}{\rm tr}(\theta\wedge\theta^{*}).

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold. Assume that
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
is a reductive representation. Let
(V_{\varrho},D)
be the flat bundle on
X
with the monodromy representation being
\varrho
. Let
h
be any smooth hermitian metric for
V_{\varrho}
. Then there exists a unique decomposition
D=\nabla_{h}+\Phi_{h}
such that
\nabla_{h}
is a unitary connection of
(V_{\varrho},h)
and
\Phi_{h}
is a self-adjoint operator, namely, we have

\langle\Phi_{h}(e),e^{\prime}\rangle_{h}=\langle e,\Phi_{h}(e^{\prime})\rangle_{h},\quad\forall\ e,e^{\prime}\in V_{\varrho}.

Say
h
is a
harmonic metric
if
\nabla_{h}^{*}\Phi_{h}=0
. In this case, by the Siu-Sampson formula, we have the pluriharmonicity of
h
. In other words, if we decompose
\nabla_{h}=\nabla_{h}^{\prime}+\nabla_{h}^{\prime\prime}
and
\Phi_{h}=\Phi_{h}^{\prime}+\Phi_{h}^{\prime\prime}
into the
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
-parts, then
(V_{\varrho},\nabla_{h}^{\prime\prime},\Phi^{\prime}_{h},h)
is a harmonic bundle. By the theorem of Corlette
[
Cor88
]
, such harmonic metric exists and is unique up to some obvious ambiguity. We will also refer to
(V_{\varrho},D,h)
as a
harmonic bundle
if no confusion arises. In this case, the canonical form associated with the harmonic bundle
(V_{\varrho},D_{h}^{\prime\prime},\Phi^{\prime}_{h},h)
introduced in
Definition

2.3
is uniquely defined, and we denote it by
\omega_{\varrho}:=\sqrt{-1}{\rm tr}(\Phi_{h}^{\prime}\wedge\Phi_{h}^{\prime\prime})
. Note that it does not depend on the choice of the Kähler metric
\omega
on
X
.

Definition 2.4

(Canonical form)
.

We call the above semi-positive closed
(1,1)
-form
\omega_{\varrho}
the
canonical form
associated with the reductive representation
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
.

We leave it to the reader to verify as an exercise that for any
g\in{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, we have
\omega_{\varrho}=\omega_{g\varrho g^{-1}}
, where
g\varrho g^{-1}
is the conjugate of
\varrho
by
g
.

2.2.
Deformation of canonical forms

Let us prove the main result of this section on the continuity of canonical forms in families.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a proper smooth Kähler family. Let
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
be a reductive representation. Then the fiberwise defined canonical form
\omega_{\varrho_{t}}
on
X_{t}
associated with the representation
\varrho_{t}:\pi_{1}(X_{t})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
varies continuously with respect to
t\in\mathbb{D}
.

The proof of
Theorem

2.5
follows essentially from the same arguments in
[
Sim92
,
Sim94b
]
, based on Uhlenbeck’s weak compactness theorem
[
Uhl82
]
. We first recall some preliminary results.

Let
g
denote the Euclidean metric
\sum dz_{i}\cdot d\bar{z}_{i}
of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Let
\omega
be a Kahler form on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that
C_{0}^{-1}\cdot\omega\leq g\leq C_{0}\cdot\omega
. Let
\left(E,\theta,h\right)
be a harmonic bundle on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. We have the expression
\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\cdot dz_{i}
for holomorphic sections
f_{i}\in\operatorname{End}(E)
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Recall the following result in
[
Moc06
]
.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
Moc06
, Lemma 2.13]
)
.

There exist a constant
C_{1}
depending only on
\operatorname{rank}E
, the upper bound of absolute values of eigenvalues of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
and the above constant
C_{0}
, such that

\left|\theta\right|_{\omega,h}^{2}(x)\leq C_{1},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n}.

Here
\left|\theta\right|_{\omega,h}
denotes the norm of
\theta
with respect the metric
h
and
\omega
. ∎

Lemma 2.7
.

There exists a constant
C_{2}
depending only on
\operatorname{rank}E
and
C_{0}
such that absolute values of eigenvalues of
f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{n}(x)
for each
x\in\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n}
are bounded from above by
C_{2}\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{\omega,h}
, where

\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{\omega,h}:=\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|\theta|_{\omega,h}^{2}d\text{\small Vol}_{\omega}.

Proof.

Consider the characteristic polynomial
P_{i}:=\det(t-f_{i})=t^{r}+\sigma_{i,1}t^{r-1}+\cdots+\sigma_{i,r}
, where
r={\rm rank}\,E
and
\sigma_{i,j}
are holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. For any
x\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, we denote by
\lambda_{i}(x)
the maximum of the absolute values of eigenvalues of
f_{i}(x)
. Then by
the classical inequalities between the norms of roots and coefficients of a polynomial, one has

\lambda_{i}(x)\leq 2\max_{k=1,\ldots,r}|\sigma_{i,k}(x)|^{\frac{1}{k}}.

Note that there exists a constant
C_{3}
depending only on
{\rm rank}\,E
such that

s(x):=\max_{k=1,\ldots,r}|\sigma_{i,k}(x)|^{\frac{2}{k}}\leq C_{3}|f_{i}(x)|^{2}_{h}.

For each
z\in\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n}
, we set

\Omega_{z}:=\{w\in\mathbb{D}^{n}\mid|w_{i}-z_{i}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\mbox{ for each }i\}.

Since
s(x)
is a psh function, we have

\displaystyle\log|s(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{4^{n}}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{z}}\log|s(w)|^{2}d\mbox{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leqslant\log\big(\frac{4^{n}}{\pi^{n}}\cdot\int_{\Omega_{z}}|s(w)|^{2}d\mbox{vol}_{g}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\log(\frac{4^{n}}{\pi^{n}})+\log\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|s(w)|^{2}d\mbox{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\log C_{3}(\frac{4^{n}}{\pi^{n}})+\log\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|f_{i}|_{h}^{2}d\mbox{vol}_{g}.

On the other hand, note that there exists a constant
C_{4}
depending only on
C_{0}
such that

\max_{i=1,\ldots,n}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|f_{i}|_{h}^{2}d\mbox{vol}_{g}\leq C_{4}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|\theta|_{\omega,h}^{2}d\mbox{vol}_{\omega}=C_{4}\lVert\theta\rVert_{\omega,h}^{2}.

The above two inequalities yield the lemma.
∎

Lemmas

and
2.7
imply the following result.

Lemma 2.8
.

There exists a uniform constant
C
depending only on
{\rm rank}\,E
, the constant
C_{0}
above, and the

-norm
\lVert\theta\rVert_{\omega,h}^{2}
such that

\left|\theta\right|_{\omega,h}^{2}(x)\leq C,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n}.

3.1.
Some preliminary for
\mathbb{C}
-VHS and Griffiths bundle

We recall some notions of complex variation of Hodge structures (
\mathbb{C}
-VHS for short). We refer the readers to
[
Gri70
,
Sch73
,
Sim88
,
CMSP17
,
SS22
]
for more details.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
\tau:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
be a reductive representation underlying a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS of weight
w
. By
[
Sim88
]
, it corresponds to a
system of Hodge bundles

(E=\oplus_{p+q=w}E^{p,q},\theta)
endowed with a Hodge metric
h
induced by
\tau
. Precisely, we have

(1)

(E,\theta,h)
is a harmonic bundle on
X
.

(2)

The decomposition
E=\oplus_{p+q=w}E^{p,q}
is orthogonal with respect to
h
.

(3)

We have

\theta:E^{p,q}\to E^{p-1,q+1}\otimes\Omega_{X}

(4)

The connection
\nabla_{h}+\theta+\theta^{*}
is flat, whose monodromy representation is
\tau
.

In Griffiths
[
Gri70
]
(cf. also
[
CMSP17
, Problem 13.3.3]
), Griffiths introduced a line bundle on
X
(so-called
Griffiths line bundle
) defined by

L_{\tau}:=(\det E^{w,0})^{w}\otimes(\det E^{w-1,1})^{w-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes(\det E^{1,w-1})

endowed with the metric
g
induced by
h
.

We denote by
\theta_{p,q}:=\theta|_{E^{p,q}}
, and
h_{p,q}:=h|_{E^{p,q}}
. Then the Chern curvature of
(E^{p,q},h_{p,q})
is given by

R_{p,q}:=-\theta_{p,q}^{*}\wedge\theta_{p,q}-\theta_{p+1,q-1}\wedge\theta_{p+1,q-1}^{*}.

Therefore, the Chern curvature of
(L_{\tau},g)
is

\displaystyle R(L_{\tau},g)

\displaystyle=w{\rm tr}(-\theta_{w,0}^{*}\wedge\theta_{w,0})+\sum_{p=1}^{w-1}(w-q){\rm tr}(-\theta_{w-q,q}^{*}\wedge\theta_{w-q,q}-\theta_{w-q+1,q-1}\wedge\theta_{w-q+1,q-1}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=0}^{w-1}{\rm tr}(-\theta_{w-q,q}^{*}\wedge\theta_{w-q,q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=0}^{w-1}{\rm tr}(\theta_{w-q,q}\wedge\theta_{w-q,q}^{*})

\displaystyle={\rm tr}(\theta\wedge\theta^{*}).

This implies the following result, which is already well-known to experts:

Lemma 3.1
.

We have the integrality of the cohomology class

\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\omega_{\tau}\}=\{\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}{\rm tr}(\theta\wedge\theta^{*})\}=c_{1}(L_{\tau})\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}).

Let
\mathscr{D}
be the period domain of the
\mathbb{C}
-VHS
\tau
and let
\Gamma
be the monodromy group
\tau(\pi_{1}(X))
. Assume that
\Gamma
acts discretely on
\mathscr{D}
. Then the quotient
\mathscr{D}/\Gamma
is a complex space and the period map is a holomorphic map
p:X\to\mathscr{D}/\Gamma
, whose differential is
\theta
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\tau:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
be a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS with discrete monodromy. Let
\omega
be a Kähler form on a compact Kähler
n
-fold
X
. Let
k_{0}
be the largest integer
k
such that

\int_{X}(\sqrt{-1}{\rm tr}(\theta\wedge\theta^{*}))^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}>0.

Then
k_{0}
is the dimension of the image
p(X)
.

Proof.

Since
\theta
is the differential of
p
, it follows that
\theta|_{Z}
is trivial for any fiber
Z
of
p
. Hence,
\sqrt{-1}{\rm tr}(\theta\wedge\theta^{*})|_{Z}
is also trivial. On the other hand, for any
\xi\in T_{X}
such that
dp(\xi)\neq 0
, we have
{\rm tr}(\theta\wedge\theta^{*})(\xi,\bar{\xi})=|\theta(\xi)|_{h}>0
. Let
X^{\circ}
be a Zariski dense open subset of
p
such that
p|_{X^{\circ}}
is a proper submersion. This implies that, at each point
x\in X^{\circ}
, the semi-positive
(1,1)
-form
\sqrt{-1}{\rm tr}(\theta\wedge\theta^{*})
has exactly
\dim p(X)
strictly positive eigenvalues. Therefore,
(\sqrt{-1}{\rm tr}(\theta\wedge\theta^{*}))^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
is a positive measure on
X^{\circ}
for
k=\dim p(X)
, and is trivial for
k>\dim p(X)
on
X^{\circ}
. The lemma is proved.
∎

3.2.
Notion of Shafarevich morphism

Let us give the definition of the Shafarevich morphism (cf.
[
Eys04
,
Eys11
,
DYK23
]
).

Definition 3.3

(Shafarevich morphism)
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
H\triangleleft\pi_{1}(X)
be a normal subgroup. A proper holomorphic fibration

{\rm sh}_{H}:X\to{\rm Sh}_{H}(X)

onto a complex normal variety
{\rm Sh}_{H}(X)
is called the
Shafarevich morphism
associated with
(X,H)
if, for any closed subvariety
Z
of
X
,
{\rm sh}_{H}(Z)
is a point if and only if the image

{\rm Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)/H]

is finite. We shall write
{\rm sh}_{X}:X\to{\rm Sh}(X)
for the Shafarevich morphism associated with
(X,\{e\})
, and refer to it as the Shafarevich morphism of
X
.

Let
M
be a subset of the
Betti moduli space

M_{\rm B}(\pi_{1}(X),{\rm GL}_{N})(\mathbb{C})
(cf.
[
Sim94a
]
for the definition). Let
H=\bigcap_{\tau}\ker\tau
, where
\tau
ranges over all reductive representations
\tau:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
with
[\tau]\in M
. If the Shafarevich morphism
{\rm sh}_{H}:X\to{\rm Sh}_{H}(X)
exists, then we shall write

{\rm sh}_{M}:X\to{\rm Sh}_{M}(X)

in place of
{\rm sh}_{H}:X\to{\rm Sh}_{H}(X)
, and call it the Shafarevich morphism associated with
M
.

In
[
Eys04
]
(cf.
[
DYK23
, Proposition 3.37]
for the singular case), it is shown that when
X
is projective and
M:=M_{\rm B}(\pi_{1}(X),{\rm GL}_{N})(\mathbb{C})
, the Shafarevich morphism associated with
M
exists and is algebraic.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. Set
M:=M_{\rm B}(\pi_{1}(X),{\rm GL}_{N})(\mathbb{C})
. If there exists an almost faithful reductive representation
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, then the Shafarevich morphism
{\rm sh}_{M}:X\to{\rm Sh}_{M}(X)
associated with
M
is the Shafarevich morphism of
X
.

Proof.

Since
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
is almost faithful, then for any closed subvariety
Z
of
X
,
\varrho({\rm Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)])
is finite if and only if
{\rm Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)]
is finite. The lemma follows from
Definition

3.3
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Let
H\triangleleft\pi_{1}(X)
be a normal subgroup. Note that the Shafarevich morphism
{\rm sh}_{H}:X\to{\rm Sh}_{H}(X)
associated with
(X,H)
, if exists, is bimemorphic to the
\Gamma
-reduction of
(X,H)
. Hence, we have
\dim{\rm Sh}_{H}(X)=\gamma d(X,H)
.

The next result is well-known, and we provide a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\tau
be a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS on a compact Kähler manifold
X
with discrete monodromy
\Gamma
. The Stein factorization
g:X\to Y
of its period map
p:X\to\mathscr{D}/\Gamma
is the Shafarevich morphism associated with
\tau
.

Proof.

Let
Z
be a connected component of any fiber of
g
. Recall that (cf.
[
CMSP17
]
), there exists a real semisimple algebraic group
G\subset{\rm GL}_{N}
such that
G(\mathbb{R})
acts transitively on
\mathscr{D}
. For any point
P\in\mathscr{D}
, the stabilizer of
P
under the action of
G(\mathbb{R})
is a compact subgroup
K
of
G(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, we have
\tau(\pi_{1}(X))\subset G(\mathbb{R})
. Then
\tau(\text{Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)])
is contained in some conjugate
K^{\prime}
of
K
. Since
\Gamma
is discrete,
K^{\prime}\cap\Gamma
is finite. Thus,
\tau(\text{Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)])\subset K^{\prime}\cap\Gamma
is finite.

Now, let
Z
be a closed subvariety of
X
such that
\tau(\text{Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)])
is finite. Then, for some finite étale cover
Z^{\prime}
of
Z
,
\tau(\text{Im}[\pi_{1}(Z^{\prime})\to\pi_{1}(X)])
is trivial. By the rigidity of VHS (cf.
[
Sch73
, Proposition 7.24]
), the pullback of the
\mathbb{C}
-VHS underlying
\tau
on the desingularization of
Z^{\prime}
is thus trivial. Hence, the composite map
Z^{\prime}\to Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}\mathscr{D}/\Gamma
is trivial. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.3.

In this subsection, we will prove
Theorem

.

Theorem 3.7

(=
Theorem

B
)
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a proper smooth Kähler family. If there exists a faithful reductive representation
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X_{0})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, which is rigid and integral, then
t\mapsto\gamma d(X_{t})
is constant on
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

By the integrality and rigidity of
\varrho
, after replacing
\varrho
by some conjugate, there exists a number field
L
such that we have

\varrho:\pi_{1}(X_{0})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(O_{L}).

Hence we have

\varrho_{t}:\pi_{1}(X_{t})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(O_{L})

for each

by the definition of

.

Let

be the set of archimedean places of

\oplus_{\sigma\in{\rm Ar}(L)}\sigma\varrho_{t}:\pi_{1}(X_{t})\to\oplus{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})

has discrete image for each

. Let
\tau_{t}:\pi_{1}(X_{t})\to{\rm GL}_{M}(\mathbb{C})
be
\oplus_{\sigma\in{\rm Ar}(L)}\sigma\varrho_{t}
.

Since the rigidity of
\varrho
implies the rigidity of
\varrho_{t}
, as we have the natural isomorphism between
M_{\rm B}(\pi_{1}(X_{t}),{\rm GL}_{N})
and
M_{\rm B}(\pi_{1}(X_{0}),{\rm GL}_{N})
, by
[
Sim92
]
, for any embedding
\sigma\in\text{Ar}(L)

corresponds to a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS on

. Hence,
\tau_{t}
underlies a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS with discrete monodromy. We stress here that the period domains of these

-VHS

might vary in

.

Let
\omega_{\tau_{t}}
be the canonical form on
X_{t}
associated with
\tau_{t}
defined in

2.4
. By
Theorem

2.5
, the cohomology classes
\{\omega_{\tau_{t}}\}_{t\in\mathbb{D}}
vary continuously in
H^{2}(\mathscr{X},\mathbb{R})\simeq H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{R})
with respect to
t
.

On the other hand, by
Lemma

3.1
,
\frac{1}{2\pi}\omega_{\tau_{t}}\in H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z})
for each
t
. Therefore, the section
\left(t\mapsto\{\omega_{\tau_{t}}\}\right)\in\Gamma(\mathbb{D},R^{2}f_{*}(\mathbb{R}))
is flat with respect to the Gauss-Manin connection.

Let
\omega
be a Kähler form on
\mathscr{X}
which gives the polarization. Denote by
\omega_{t}=\omega|_{X_{t}}
. Then the smooth section
\left(t\mapsto\{\omega_{t}\}\right)\in\Gamma(\mathbb{D},R^{2}f_{*}(\mathbb{R}))
is also flat with respect to the Gauss-Manin connection of the local system

. It follows that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle h_{k}:\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto\int_{X_{t}}(\omega_{\tau_{t}})^{k}\wedge(\omega_{t})^{n-k}

is a constant function on
\mathbb{D}
, where

denotes the relative dimension of
f
. Set

k_{0}=\max_{k}\{h_{k}\neq 0\}.

Since
\tau_{t}
has discrete image, its image
\Gamma_{t}:=\tau_{t}(\pi_{1}(X_{t}))
acts discretely on
\mathscr{D}_{t}
, and thus
\mathscr{D}_{t}/\Gamma_{t}
is a complex space. Denote by

the corresponding period map of
\tau_{t}
. By

3.2
,
k_{0}
is the dimension of the image
p_{t}(X_{t})
. By
Lemma

3.6
, we have
\dim{\rm Sh}_{\tau_{t}}(X_{t})=k_{0}
for each
t\in\mathbb{D}
, where
{\rm sh}_{\tau_{t}}:X_{t}\to{\rm Sh}_{\tau_{t}}(X_{t})
is the Shafarevich morphism of
\tau_{t}
. Since
\varrho_{t}
is assumed to be almost faithful, it follows that
\ker\tau_{t}
is a finite subgroup of
\pi_{1}(X_{t})
. This implies that
{\rm sh}_{\tau_{t}}
is the Shafarevich morphism of
X_{t}
. Hence
\gamma d(X_{t})=k_{0}
for any
t\in\mathbb{D}
. The theorem is proved.
∎

4.
Deformation of harmonic maps into Euclidean buildings

To prove
Theorems

A
and
C
, we need to apply techniques used in studying the reductive Shafarevich conjecture (see
[
Eys04
,
DYK23
]
). This involves considering local systems over non-archimedean local fields on projective varieties. In this context, we apply tools developed by Gromov and Schoen
[
GS92
]
regarding the existence of equivariant harmonic maps to Bruhat-Tits buildings. In this section, we will explore the deformation of these harmonic maps in families (see
Theorem

2.5
). Analogous to the canonical form introduced in
Definition

2.4
, there is a notion of
canonical currents
(see
Definition

5.1
) associated with representations of fundamental groups into algebraic groups over non-archimedean local fields. Our ultimate goal is to apply
Theorem

4.29
to establish a result similar to
Theorem

2.5
concerning the deformation of canonical currents (see
Theorem

5.4
).

4.1.
NPC spaces

For the definitions in this subsection, we refer the readers to
[
BH99
,
Rou09
,
KP23
]
.

Definition 4.1

(Geodesic space)
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
be a metric space. A curve
\gamma:[0,\ell]\rightarrow X
into
X
is called a geodesic if the length
d_{X}(\gamma(a),\gamma(b))=b-a
for any subinterval
[a,b]\subset[0,\ell]
. A metric space
(X,d_{X})
is a
geodesic space
if there exists a geodesic connecting every pair of points in
X
.

Definition 4.2

(NPC space)
.

An NPC (non-positively curved) space
(\mathcal{N},d_{\mathcal{N}})
is a complete geodesic space that satisfies the following condition: for any three points
P,Q,R\in\mathcal{N}
and a geodesic
\gamma:[0,\ell]\rightarrow\mathcal{N}
with
\gamma(0)=Q
and
\gamma(\ell)=R
, we have

d^{2}(P,Q_{t})\leq(1-t)d^{2}(P,Q)+td^{2}(P,R)-t(1-t)d^{2}(Q,R)

for any
t\in[0,1]
, where
Q_{t}:=\gamma(t\ell)
.

An NPC space generalizes a Hadamard manifold, which is a complete, simply connected Riemannian manifold with non-positive sectional curvature. A relevant example of an NPC space that is not a Riemannian manifold, pertinent to this paper, is the Bruhat-Tits building
\Delta(G)_{K}
associated with a reductive algebraic group
G
defined over a non-archimedean local field
K
. While we do not provide the full definition of Bruhat-Tits buildings here, readers can refer to sources such as
[
Rou09
]
and
[
KP23
]
for precise details. Notably, a Bruhat-Tits building consists of a collection of apartments, each of which is an isometric and totally geodesic embedding of Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
N=\dim(\Delta(G)_{K})
. Additionally, the group
G(K)
, which denotes the
K
-points of
G
, acts isometrically on
\Delta(G)_{K}
and transitively on its set of apartments. The dimension of
\Delta(G)_{K}
equals the
K
-rank of the algebraic group
G
, which is the dimension of a maximal
K
-split torus in
G
.

In this paper, we mainly work on two types of NPC spaces:

(1)

The Riemannian symmetric spaces
{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})/{\rm U}_{N}
and
{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})/{\rm SU}_{N}
.

(2)

The Bruhat-Tits building
\Delta(G)_{K}
of a reductive algebraic group
G
over a non-archimedean local field
K
.

4.2.
Some preliminary of harmonic maps to NPC spaces

For further details of definitions and results in this subsection, we refer the readers to
[
GS92
,
KS93
,
KS97
]
,
[
BDDM22
, §2.2]
and
[
DM24
, §4]
.

Consider a map
f:\Omega\to\mathcal{N}
from an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(\Omega,g)
to an NPC space
(\mathcal{N},d_{\mathcal{N}})
. When the target space
\mathcal{N}
is a smooth Riemannian manifold of nonpositive sectional curvature, the energy of a smooth map
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{N}
is

E^{f}=\int_{\Omega}|df|^{2}{\rm dvol}_{g}

where
(\Omega,g)
is a Riemannian domain and
{\rm dvol}_{g}
is the volume form of
\Omega
. We say
f:\Omega\to\mathcal{N}
is harmonic if it is locally energy minimizing; i.e. for any
x\in\Omega
, there exists
r>0
such that the restriction
\left.u\right|_{B_{r}(x)}
minimizes energy amongst all finite energy maps
v:B_{r}(x)\rightarrow{\mathcal{N}}
with the same boundary values as
\left.u\right|_{B_{r}(x)}
. Here
B_{r}(x)
denotes the geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at
x
.

In this paper, we also consider the target
\mathcal{N}
to be NPC spaces, not necessarily smooth. Let us recall the definition of harmonic maps in this context (cf.
[
KS93
]
for more details).

Let
(\Omega,g)
be a bounded Lipschitz Riemannian domain. Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
be the set of points in
\Omega
at a distance least
\varepsilon
from
\partial\Omega
. Denote by
S_{\varepsilon}(x):=\partial B_{\varepsilon}(x)
. We say
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{N}
is an
L^{2}
-map (or that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{N})
) if for some point
P\in\Omega
, we have

\int_{\Omega}d^{2}(f(x),P)d\mathrm{vol}_{g}<\infty.

For
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{N})
, define

e^{f}_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad e^{f}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}\int_{y\in S_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{d^{2}(f(x),f(y))}{\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{d\sigma_{x,\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{n-1}}&x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

where
\sigma_{x,\varepsilon}
is the induced measure on
S_{\varepsilon}(x)
. We define a family of functionals

E_{\varepsilon}^{f}:C_{c}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad E_{\varepsilon}^{f}(\varphi)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi e^{f}_{\varepsilon}d\text{\small Vol}_{g}.

We say
f
has
finite energy
, denoted by
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathcal{N})
, if

E^{f}[\Omega]:=\sup_{\varphi\in C_{c}(\Omega),0\leq\varphi\leq 1}\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}E_{\varepsilon}^{f}(\varphi)<\infty.

In this case, it was proven in
[
KS93
, Theorem 1.10]
that there exists an absolutely continuous function
e^{f}(x)
with respect to Lebesgue measure, which we call the
energy density
, such that
e^{f}_{\varepsilon}(x)d\mathrm{vol}_{g}
converges weakly to
e^{f}(x){\rm dvol}_{g}
as
\varepsilon
tends to
0
. In analogy to the case of smooth targets, we write
|\nabla f|^{2}(x)
in place of
e^{f}(x)
. Hence
|\nabla f|^{2}(x)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. In particular, the (Korevaar-Schoen) energy of
f
in
\Omega
is

(4.1)

\displaystyle E^{f}[\Omega]=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{2}{\rm dvol}_{g}.

Furthermore, we say
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{N})
has
locally finite energ
y if for any
x_{0}\in\Omega
, there exists a bounded Lipschitz Riemannian subdomain
\Omega_{x_{0}}
containing
x_{0}
such that
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega_{x_{0}},\mathcal{N})
.

Remark 4.3
.

If
(M,g)
is a closed Riemannian manifold and
\varrho:\pi_{1}(M)\to{\rm Isom}(\mathcal{N})
is a homomorphism, where
{\rm Isom}(\mathcal{N})
is the isometry group of the NPC space
(\mathcal{N},d_{\mathcal{N}})
, then for any
\varrho
-equivariant map
u:\widetilde{M}\to\mathcal{N}
with locally finite energy, the energy density
|\nabla u|^{2}
is a locally
L^{1}
-function on
\widetilde{M}
that is invariant under
\pi_{1}(M)
-action. It then descends to a function on
M
, which we abusively denote by
|\nabla u|^{2}
. Denote

{}^{g}E^{u}:=\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}d\text{\small Vol}_{g}

if this integral exists (as a finite number). In this case, we say
u
is a finite energy map and define
{}^{g}E^{u}
as the energy of
u
. We write

u\in W^{1,2}_{\varrho}(\tilde{M},\mathcal{N}).

If the dependence of energy on the metric
g
is clear from context, we will omit the superscript
g
and write
E^{u}
instead.

Definition 4.4

(Harmonic maps)
.

We say a continuous map
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{N}
from a Lipschitz Riemannian domain
(\Omega,g)
is harmonic if it is locally energy minimizing; more precisely, at each
x\in\Omega
, there exists

r>0
such that
u|_{B_{r}(x)}\in W^{1,2}(B_{r}(x),\mathcal{N})
and
E^{u|_{B_{r}(x)}}\leq E^{v}
for any
v\in W^{1,2}(B_{r}(x),\mathcal{N})
with
\mbox{tr}(u|_{B_{r}(x)})=\mbox{tr}(v)
. Here,
\mbox{tr}(\cdot)
is the trace of a finite energy map defined in
[
KS93
, §1.12]
.

For
V\in\Gamma\Omega
where
\Gamma\Omega
is the set of
Lipschitz vector fields on
\Omega
, in
[
KS93
, §2.3]
, the
directional energy

|f_{*}(V)|^{2}
is similarly
defined. The real valued
L_{\rm loc}^{1}
function
|f_{*}(V)|^{2}
generalizes
the norm squared on the directional derivative of
f
. The
generalization of the pull-back metric is the continuous, symmetric, bilinear, non-negative and tensorial operator

\pi_{f}(V,W)=\Gamma\Omega\times\Gamma\Omega\rightarrow L^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})

where

\pi_{f}(V,W)=\frac{1}{2}|f_{*}(V+W)|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|f_{*}(V-W)|^{2}.

We refer to
[
KS93
, §2.3]
for more
details.

Let
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be local coordinates of
(\Omega,g)
, and
g=(g_{ij})
,
g^{-1}=(g^{ij})
be the local metric expressions. Then energy density function of

f
can be written (cf.
[
KS93
, (2.3vi)]
)

|\nabla f|^{2}=g^{ij}\pi_{f}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}})

Next assume
(\Omega,g)
is a Hermitian domain and let
(z_{1}=x_{1}+ix_{2},\dots,z_{n}=x_{2n-1}+ix_{2n})
be local complex coordinates. If we extend
\pi_{f}
linearly over
\mathbb{C}
, then we have

\frac{1}{4}|\nabla f|^{2}=g^{i\bar{j}}\pi_{f}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}},\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}).

Definition 4.5

(Proper action)
.

Let
\Gamma\subset{\rm Isom}(\mathcal{N})
be a finitely generated group with
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{p}
being the finite set of generators, where
{\mathcal{N}}
be an NPC space. Define

(4.2)

\displaystyle\delta:\mathcal{N}\to[0,\infty),\ \ \ \delta(P)=\max d(\gamma_{\alpha}.P,P),\alpha=1,\dots,p.

We will call the action
proper
if the sublevel sets of the function
\delta
are bounded in
\mathcal{N}
; i.e. if
P_{0}\in\mathcal{N}
, then for any
L>0
, there is a number
R>0
(depending on
L,P_{0}
) so that

\{P\in\mathcal{N}:\delta(P)\leq L\}\subset B_{R}(P_{0}).

The following result is implicitly addressed in
[
KS97
]
. For the sake of completeness, we provide a detailed proof here.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\mathcal{N}
be as in
Definition

4.5
. If
\Gamma
does not fix a point at the visual boundary
\partial\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{N}
(see
[
BH99
, Chapter II.8]
for the definition), then the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathcal{N}
is proper.

Proof.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{N}}:=\mathcal{N}\cup\partial\mathcal{N}
. We equip
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
with the
cone topology
(see
[
BH99
, Chapter II, Definition 8.6]
) Since
\mathcal{N}
is locally compact,
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
is compact.

Assume that
\Gamma
is not proper. Then there exists some
L>0
such that
C_{L}:=\{P\in\mathcal{N}\mid\delta(P)\leq L\}
is an unbounded subset in
\mathcal{N}
. Then we can choose a sequence of points
\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{N}
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}P_{n}=Q
for some
Q\in\partial\mathcal{N}
. Note that for any
P_{n}
, we have

d(\gamma_{\alpha}.P_{n},P_{n})\leq L,

where
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{p}\}
is a finite set of generators of
\Gamma
.
This implies that, for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, one has

d(\gamma.P_{n},P_{n})\leq\ell(\gamma)L,

where
\ell(\gamma)
is the word length of
\gamma
with respect to
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{p}\}
. Thus, we have
\gamma.Q=Q
for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
. This contradicts with the assumption that
\Gamma
does not fix a point at
\partial N
. The lemma is proved.
∎

Definition 4.7
.

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian manifold without boundary,
\mathcal{N}
be a locally compact NPC space, and
\varrho:\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathcal{N}
be a representation. The metric space
(L^{2}_{\varrho}(\tilde{M},\mathcal{N}),d_{2})
is defined as follows: Let
L^{2}_{\varrho}(\tilde{M},\mathcal{N})
to be the space of
\varrho
-equivariant, locally
L^{2}
-maps. The distance function

is given by

d_{2}(u,v)=\left(\int_{M}d^{2}(u(x),v(x))\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Here, since

is
\pi_{1}(M)
-invariant on
\tilde{M}
, it descends to a
function on
M
. By
[

, Lemma 2.1.2]
,
(L^{2}_{\varrho}(\tilde{M},\mathcal{N}),d_{2})
is an NPC space. Note that
W^{1,2}_{\varrho}(\tilde{M},\mathcal{N})
is a subspace of

(cf.
Remark

4.3
).

Definition 4.8
.

Let
M
,
\mathcal{N}
, and
\varrho
be as in
Definition

4.7
. We say
\{u_{k}\}
is a energy minimizing sequence if
u_{k}\in W^{1,2}_{\varrho}(\tilde{M},\mathcal{N})
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}E^{u_{k}}=\inf_{v\in W^{1,2}_{\varrho}(\tilde{M},\mathcal{N})}E^{v}.

Proposition 4.9
.

Assume
M
is compact,
\mathcal{N}
is locally compact and
\varrho
is proper.
If
\{u_{k}\}
is a energy minimizing sequence that are uniformly locally Lipschitz continuous, then a subsequence of
\{u_{k}\}
converges uniformly to a harmonic map
v
. Moreover, the energy density measures and the directional energy density measures of the
u_{k}
converge weakly to those of

.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\widetilde{M}
be a fundamental domain for the action of
\pi_{1}(M)
on
\widetilde{M}
. We fix a point
P_{0}\in\mathcal{N}
. The compactness of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
and the uniform local Lipschitz continuity of

implies that there exists
c>0
such that

d(u_{k}(\gamma_{\alpha}x),u_{k}(x))\leq cd_{\widetilde{M}}(\gamma_{\alpha}x,x),\ \ \forall x\in\mathcal{F},\ \ \alpha=1,\dots,p

where
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{p}
are the generators of
\pi_{1}(M)
.
The equivariance of
u_{k}
implies that for any
x\in\mathcal{F}
and any
\alpha\in\{1,\dots,p\}
, we have

d(\varrho(\gamma_{\alpha})(u_{k}(x)),u_{k}(x))=d(u_{k}(\gamma_{\alpha}.x),u_{k}(x))\leq c_{1}:=c\sup_{\beta=1,\dots,p;x\in\mathcal{F}}d_{\widetilde{M}}(\gamma_{\beta}(x),x).

Since
\varrho
is proper, it follows that
\delta(u_{k}(x))\leq c_{1}
for
x\in\mathcal{F}
and for all
k
, where
\delta
is the function defined in (
4.2
).

Claim 4.10
.

There exists some constant
R>0
such that
d(u_{k}(\gamma.\overline{\mathcal{F}}),\varrho(\gamma).P_{0})\leq R
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and any
x\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

By
Definition

4.5
, there exists some
R>0
such that

\{P\in\mathcal{N}\mid\delta(P)\leq c_{1}\}\subset B_{R}(P_{0}).

It follows that
u_{k}(\mathcal{F})\subset B_{R}(P_{0})
for any

.
Since
u_{k}
is
\varrho
-equivariant and continuous, it follows that for any

, and any

, we have

d(u_{k}(\gamma.x),\varrho(\gamma).P_{0})=d(\varrho(\gamma).u_{k}(x),\varrho(\gamma).P_{0})=d(u_{k}(x),P_{0})\leq R.

∎

Since
\mathcal{N}
is locally compact,
u_{k}
is uniformly locally Lipschitz continuous, and

, by the Arzela-Ascoli theorem, the above claim yields that there exists a subsequence of

that converges to a map

. The lower semicontinuity of energy implies that

.
∎

Theorem 4.20

)

Let

5.1.
Deformation of canonical currents

Let

Note that any

implies that

. We consider a smooth semi-positive

-form on

It is shown in

on

with continuous potential.

(Canonical current)

of

7.1.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. Consider the surjective isogeny
p:{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
defined by
p(A,t):=tA
. Then for any representation
\varrho:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, there exists a finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
such that
\varrho|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
lifts to a representation
\Gamma^{\prime}\to{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Proof.

Set
G:=\varrho(\Gamma)
and define
G_{1}:=p^{-1}(G)
. Note that
\ker p=\{(\zeta_{i}I,\zeta_{i}^{-1})\}_{i=1,\ldots,N}
, where
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{N}
are
N
-th roots of unity. In particular, these elements are all torsion elements.

By Selberg’s theorem,
G_{1}
is virtually torsion-free, and thus there exists a finite index subgroup
G_{2}
of
G_{1}
such that
G_{2}\cap\ker p=\{e\}
. Hence,
p|_{G_{2}}:G_{2}\to p(G_{2})
is an isomorphism. Note that
p(G_{2})
is a finite index subgroup of
G
.

It follows that
\Gamma^{\prime}:=\varrho^{-1}(p(G_{2}))
is a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma
. We define
\varrho^{\prime}=(p|_{G_{2}})^{-1}\circ\varrho|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}:\Gamma^{\prime}\to G_{2}
. Then we have

p_{2}\circ\varrho^{\prime}=\varrho|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial u_{t}^{\alpha}}{\partial\bar{z}_{i}}({\bf z})\right|\leq C_{1},\quad\left|\frac{\partial u_{t}^{\alpha}}{\partial z_{i}}({\bf z})\right|\leq C_{1},

\displaystyle\left|u_{t}^{\alpha}\right|_{C^{1,\mu};\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\frac{3}{4}}}:=\sup_{{\bf z}\in\mathbb{D}_{\frac{3}{4}}}|u_{t}^{\alpha}({\bf z})|+\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\sup_{{\bf z}\in\mathbb{D}_{\frac{3}{4}}}\left|\frac{\partial u_{t}^{\alpha}}{\partial x_{j}}({\bf z})\right|+\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\sup_{{\bf z}_{1},{\bf z}_{2}\in\mathbb{D}_{\frac{3}{4}}^{n},\ {\bf z}_{1}\neq{\bf z}_{2}}\frac{\left|\frac{\partial u_{t}^{\alpha}}{\partial x_{j}}({\bf z}_{1})-\frac{\partial u_{t}^{\alpha}}{\partial x_{j}}({\bf z}_{2})\right|^{\mu}}{|{\bf z}_{1}-{\bf z}_{2}|}\leq C_{3}

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a smooth projective family, and let
\sigma:\pi_{1}(\mathscr{X})\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
be a semi-simple representation. Then after we replace
\mathscr{X}
by a suitable finite étale cover, for the flat bundle
(\mathscr{V}_{\sigma},D)
on
\mathscr{X}
induced by
\sigma
, there exists a continuous metric
h
on
\mathscr{V}_{\sigma}
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

the restriction
(\mathscr{V}_{\sigma},D,h)|_{X_{t}}
is a harmonic bundle for each
t\in\mathbb{D}
.

(ii)

Writing
V_{t}:=\mathscr{V}_{\sigma}|_{X_{t}}
, the metric
h|_{V_{t}}
varies smoothly in
t
.

Proof.

We might decompose
\sigma
into a direct sum of simple representations, and it suffices to prove the theorem for the case when
\sigma
is simple. By
Lemma

7.1
, after we replace
\mathscr{X}
by a suitable finite étale cover, we may assume that
\sigma
lifts to a simple representation
\sigma^{\prime}:\pi_{1}(\mathscr{X})\to{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Let
\varrho:\pi_{1}(\mathscr{X})\to{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
and
\tau:\pi_{1}(\mathscr{X})\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
denote the compositions of
\sigma^{\prime}
with the first and second projections, respectively, i.e.,
{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\to{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
and
{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

By
Propositions

7.3
and
7.6
, there exist a continuous
\varrho
-equivariant map
u_{1}:\widetilde{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathscr{S}
and continuous
\tau
-equivariant map
u_{2}:\widetilde{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
u_{1,t}=u_{1}|_{\widetilde{X_{t}}}:\widetilde{X_{t}}\to\mathscr{S}
is pluriharmonic and
u_{2,t}=u_{2}|_{\widetilde{X_{t}}}:\widetilde{X_{t}}\to\mathbb{R}
is also pluriharmonic.

Let
\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
be the space of positive definite hermitian symmetric
N\times N
matrices with Riemannian metric defined by

\langle X,Y\rangle_{H}={\rm tr}(H^{-1}XH^{-1}Y),\ \ \ \forall X,Y=T_{H}\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C}).

Note that there is a natural isometric identification

\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\simeq{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})/{\rm U}_{N}
. Indeed,
{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
acts isometrically and transitively on

where the action of

. Since
{\rm U}_{N}
is the stabilizer of the identity,
the map
{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})/{\rm U}_{N}\to\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})

is an isometry. The subspace
\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})_{1}
consisting of

is a totally geodesic submanifold of
\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Moreover,
\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})_{1}
is invariant by the action of
{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
with
{\rm SU}_{N}
being the stabilizer of the identity. Thus, we also have the isometric identification
\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})_{1}\simeq\mathscr{S}
.

Observe that

\displaystyle\psi:\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})_{1}\times\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\to\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle(H,t)

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{\frac{t}{N}}H.

is an isometry.
We define the action of
{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
on
\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})_{1}\times\mathbb{R}
by setting
(g,z).(H,t):=(gAg^{\dagger},t+2N\log|z|)
. Then

\psi
is equivariant, i.e.
for any
(g,z)\in{\rm SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we have

(7.4)

\displaystyle\psi((g,z).(H,t))=\psi((gHg^{\dagger},t+2N\log|z|))=gHg^{\dagger}e^{\frac{t}{N}}|z|^{2}=(zg).e^{\frac{t}{N}}H

Identifying

as an element in
\mathscr{P}_{N}(\mathbb{C})_{1}

. Since

and

are pluriharmonic and

is an isometry,

is also pluriharmonic. Note that

is
\sigma
-equivariant by (
7.4
). By

7.3
and
7.6
,

varies smoothly in
t
.

Consider the flat bundle
\mathscr{V}_{\sigma}:=\widetilde{\mathscr{X}}\times_{\sigma}\mathbb{C}^{N}
induced by
\sigma
. Then for any

, select any
\tilde{x}\in\pi_{\mathscr{X}}^{-1}(x)
and and define the Hermitian metric
h
on
\mathscr{V}_{\sigma}
by setting

. Since

is
\sigma
-equivariant, this definition provides a well-defined Hermitian metric

on
\mathscr{V}_{\sigma}
. Moreover,

is the harmonic metric for
V_{t}:=\mathscr{V}_{\sigma}|_{X_{t}}
. Since
u
varies smoothly in
t
, the restriction

also varies smoothly in
t

7.2.
Semi-canonical form

Let
(V,D,h)
be a harmonic bundle on a compact Kähler
n
-fold
X
. We define the Laplacian by setting

(7.5)

\displaystyle\Delta:=DD^{*}+D^{*}D,

where
D^{*}
is the adjoint of
D
with respect to
h
. Any
k
-form
\eta\in A^{k}(X,V)
with value in
V
is called harmonic if
\Delta(\eta)=0
. The cohomology
H_{\rm DR}^{\bullet}(X,V)
is defined as the cohomology of the following complex

0\to A^{0}(X,V)\xrightarrow{D}A^{1}(X,V)\xrightarrow{D}\cdots\xrightarrow{D}A^{2n}(X,V).

In
[
Sim92
, Lemma 2.2]
, it is shown that the natural inclusion

(7.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{k}(X,V)\to H_{\rm DR}^{k}(X,V)

is an isomorphism,
where
\mathscr{H}^{k}(X,V)
denotes the space of
V
-valued harmonic
k
-forms.

We assume further that
V
underlies a real structure, i.e. it is a complexification of a real local system
V_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\eta\in\mathscr{H}^{1}(X,V)
be a harmonic 1-form. We decompose
\eta=\eta^{1,0}+\eta^{0,1}
into
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
-parts, i.e.
\eta^{1,0}\in A^{1,0}(X,V)
and
\eta^{0,1}\in A^{0,1}(X,V)
.

Lemma 7.9
.

If
\alpha:=\{\eta\}\in H_{\rm DR}^{1}(X,V_{\mathbb{R}})
, then we have
\overline{\eta^{1,0}}=\eta^{0,1}
. Here
\overline{\eta^{1,0}}
is the complex conjugate of
\eta^{1,0}
with respect to the real structure by
V_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma:V\to V
be the complex conjugate of
V
with respect to the
\mathbb{R}
-structure by
V_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
\sigma D=D\sigma
. It follows that
D(\overline{\eta})=0
. By the definition of
H_{\rm DR}^{\bullet}(X,V_{\mathbb{R}})
, there exists a
D
-closed
V_{\mathbb{R}}
-valued real 1-form
\beta
such that
\{\beta\}=\alpha
. It follows that

\{\overline{\eta}\}=\{\overline{\beta}\}=\{\beta\}=\alpha.

We decompose
D=\partial+\bar{\partial}+\theta+\theta^{*}
such that
(\partial+\bar{\partial},h)
is a metric connection, and
\theta+\theta^{*}
is self-adjoint. Then by
[
Sim92
, Lemma 2.12]
,
\sigma D^{\prime}=D^{\prime\prime}
, where
D^{\prime}:=\partial+\theta^{*}
and
D^{\prime\prime}:=\bar{\partial}+\theta
. Since
\eta
is harmonic, by
[
Sim92
, Lemma 2.2]
one has

D^{\prime\prime}\eta=D^{\prime}\eta=0.

This implies that

D^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\eta})=D^{\prime}(\overline{\eta})=0.

By
[
Sim92
, Lemma 2.2]
again,
\overline{\eta}\in\mathscr{H}^{1}(X,V)
. It follows from (
7.6
) that

\overline{\eta}=\eta
. This implies the lemma.
∎

Definition 7.10

(Semi-canonical form)
.

Let
(V,D,h)
be a harmonic bundle on a compact Kähler manifold
X
. For any
\alpha\in H_{\rm DR}^{1}(X,V_{\mathbb{R}})
, let
\eta\in\mathscr{H}^{1}(X,V)
be the harmonic representative in
\alpha
as above. The

associated with
\alpha
is a smooth real
(1,1)
-form on
X

(7.7)

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}:=\sqrt{-1}\{\eta^{1,0},\eta^{1,0}\}_{h},

where
\{\bullet,\bullet\}_{h}
denotes the operation of taking the wedge product of differential forms combined with the inner product of sections of

V
induced by the metric
h
.

Note, however, that
S_{\alpha}

is not necessarily closed or semi-positive. Moreover, it depends on the rescaling of the harmonic metric

and is therefore referred to as

.

Lemma 7.11
.

Let
(V,D,h)
be a harmonic bundle on a compact Kähler manifold
X
such that the corresponding local system is defined over
\mathbb{R}
, denoted by
V_{\mathbb{R}}

be a holomorphic map from another compact Kähler manifold

is a trivial local system, then for any

, the

-form

is

and

.

Proof.

Let
\underline{\mathbb{R}^{r}}:=Z\times\mathbb{R}^{r}
be the trivial real vector bundle of rank

on
Z
. Let
h_{0}
be the standard metric on
\underline{\mathbb{R}^{r}}
, and let

be the canonical connection on

is still a harmonic bundle over

Cor88
]
,

is isomorphic to

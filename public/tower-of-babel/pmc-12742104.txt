Palliative care experience among primary caregivers of children with cancer in low and lower-middle-income countries: a qualitative systematic review protocol
Authors: Sashiprabha Nawaratne, Sachintha Dilhani Weerasingha Navarathnage, Sashini Shehana Denagamagei, Maheeka Seneviwickrama
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPaediatric palliative care (PPC) improves the quality of life of children with life-limiting and life-threatening conditions, with caregivers playing an important role. Providing PPC in low-income and lower-middle-income countries (LMICs) is challenging due to limited resources, inadequate access to specialised care, financial constraints, and cultural or religious beliefs. This study aims to synthesise qualitative research on the experiences of primary care givers caring for children with cancer receiving palliative care in LMICs.Methods and analysisA systematic search will be conducted using electronic databases: MEDLINE (Medical Literature Analysis and Retrieval System Online), Embase, CINAHL (Cumulative Index to Nursing and Health Literature), Web of Science, and PsycINFO. Additional articles will be searched in the reference list of the selected articles. Review questions will be framed into different components according to the Population, phenomena of Interest, Context, and Outcome (PICO) framework. Primary caregivers of children with cancer will be the population of interest. Both title and abstract screening and full-text screening will be done by two independent reviewers. The quality of included studies will be assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal checklist for qualitative research. The thematic synthesis approach will be followed as it will allow a transparent summarising of the qualitative data.Ethics and disseminationThis study is not subject to ethics approval, as the work is carried out on published documents. The findings of this review will be disseminated among a broader audience through scientific channels, including publication in open-access journals and presentations at both national and international forums.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251065491.

Introduction

Paediatric palliative care (PPC) is an essential aspect of healthcare, aimed at improving the quality of life of children with life-limiting and life-threatening conditions.1 It is a holistic approach that encompasses physical, psychological, social and spiritual support for both the child and their family. Caregivers play a central role in their child’s care journey, making their experiences crucial in shaping PPC services.2 Among life-limiting conditions in childhood, cancer presents a particularly complex case study for PPC.3

Low-income and lower-middle-income countries (LMICs) face unique challenges in providing PPC due to limited healthcare resources, inadequate access to specialised care, financial constraints, and cultural or religious beliefs influencing end-of-life care.4 Understanding caregivers’ experiences can inform healthcare policies, improve service delivery and contribute to better support mechanisms tailored to LMICs’ contexts.

Two comprehensive reviews examined children’s palliative care in LMICs, emphasising the scarcity of services and the significant barriers to care, such as limited resources, inadequate training and cultural factors. These studies called for more research to understand the specific needs of children and families in these settings.4 5

The significant emotional discomfort that caregivers experience when caring for children with cancer is highlighted by numerous studies. Because of the hard nature of caregiving and the uncertainty surrounding their child’s prognosis, carers frequently suffer from high levels of stress, anxiety and depression.6 Feelings of helplessness, despair and anticipation of loss as caregivers witness the suffering of a child add to the emotional strain.7 Because their duties prevent them from sustaining connections and engaging in community activities, carers may feel socially isolated. Feelings of isolation and marginalisation may be exacerbated by the stigma attached to cancer.8 Burnout can also result from long-term caring duties, which can impair the caregiver’s personal health and well-being.9

Another major hardship for carers is financial strain. With high treatment costs, travel expenses and income loss, caregivers sometimes have to quit their jobs or cut back on their hours to offer full-time care, which puts many families in a tough financial situation.10 Due to restricted access to reasonably priced palliative care, these financial difficulties are especially acute in LMICs.

While direct studies on caregivers’ experiences in LMICs are scarce, research from high-income countries provides insights that may be relevant.1115 For instance, a qualitative study in the Netherlands explored parental experiences with a PPC team, highlighting the importance of multidisciplinary support and effective communication.16 Although conducted in a high-income context, the findings underscore elements that could be beneficial in resource-poor settings like LMICs.

Similarly, a study by van der Geest et al17 investigated parents’ experiences of PPC and its impact on the whole family. The research identified key areas of support that are crucial for families, such as emotional support, clear communication and continuity of care.17 These aspects are likely universally important.

Research on caregivers’ experiences in PPC within LMICs is limited, with most studies focusing on high-income settings. This gap highlights the need for more context-specific investigations to understand the unique challenges faced by caregivers in LMICs. Such research would inform culturally appropriate interventions and policies to improve paediatric palliative care services in resource-limited settings.

While studies have explored caregivers’ experiences in PPC, there is a lack of consolidated evidence synthesising these findings, particularly in LMICs. A qualitative systematic review will help integrate existing qualitative research to provide a comprehensive understanding of caregivers’ perspectives, barriers, coping mechanisms and healthcare interactions in PPC.

Research justification

A qualitative systematic review examining caregivers’ experiences of palliative care in paediatric cancer patients in LMICs addresses a critical knowledge gap in global paediatric oncology. While 80% of childhood cancer cases occur in LMICs where survival rates remain significantly lower than in high-income countries,18 existing research predominantly focuses on medical interventions rather than caregiver perspectives, despite caregivers being the primary support system for children.

Synthesising qualitative evidence across diverse LMIC settings will identify common challenges faced by caregivers, including financial catastrophe, healthcare access barriers, cultural influences on care decisions, and psychological burden, while illuminating successful coping strategies and support mechanisms that function within low-resource environments. The systematic approach will generate comprehensive insights into caregiver experience that can inform the development of contextually appropriate, family-centred palliative care models, inform healthcare policy development, and ultimately improve quality of life for both children with cancer and their families in settings where palliative care infrastructure remains underdeveloped but urgently needed.

Research problem

Caregivers in LMICs face unique challenges in PPC due to healthcare disparities, sociocultural factors and limited access to specialised care. Understanding their experiences through a systematic synthesis of qualitative research will help improve policy, support systems and clinical interventions.

This study aims to synthesise qualitative research on the experiences of primary care givers caring for children with cancer receiving palliative care in LMICs. Moreover, to identify common themes regarding caregivers’ experiences in PPC and explore the barriers and facilitators for caregivers to access PPC services in LMICs

Methods and analysis

This protocol was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) registry on 4 June 2025 (ID CRD420251065491).

Study design

A qualitative systematic review will be conducted following the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) guidelines.19 Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines will be followed in the reporting of this systematic review.20

Eligibility criteria (PICOS Framework)

We will use the PICOS (Population, Phenomena of Interest, Context, Outcomes, Studies) criteria to facilitate a more focused search of the literature.

Population (P): Primary caregivers of children with cancer who are family members who assumed the main responsibility for the child’s daily care and medical decision-making, such as the child’s mother, father or guardian.

Phenomena of Interest (I): Experience in paediatric palliative care.

Context (C): LMIC

Outcomes (O): Physical, psychological, economic and social impact on caregivers. Barriers and facilitators to PPC access. Parental coping mechanisms

Study design (S): Primary qualitative research studies using methodologies such as interviews, focus groups, ethnography or narrative analysis.

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search will be conducted in the following databases: Embase, MEDLINE (Medical Literature Analysis and Retrieval System Online), CINAHL (Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature) and PsycINFO using a search strategy developed by a team with expertise in the fields of public health, oncology, PPC and systematic reviews. The following is the search strategy developed for MEDLINE:

(caregiver* OR parent* OR mother* OR father* OR famil* OR ‘family member*’ OR guardian*)

AND

(experience* OR perspective* OR perception* OR view* OR need* OR challenge* OR coping OR adaptation OR psychosocial OR burden OR stress OR wellbeing OR burnout OR ‘quality of life’)

AND

(‘palliative care’ OR ‘end of life care’ OR ‘end-of-life’ OR hospice OR ‘supportive care’ OR ‘terminal care’ OR ‘compassionate care’ OR dying)

AND

(‘P#ediatric neoplasm*’ OR ‘P#ediatric cancer*’ OR ‘Childhood malignan*’ OR ‘Childhood cancer*’ OR ‘Childhood tumo#r*’ OR ‘P#ediatric oncology’ OR ‘P#ediatric tumo#r*’)

No time restrictions will be applied to the search. However, considering the resource constraints, literature that was published in languages other than English will be excluded.

By reviewing the reference lists of the selected articles, additional older, relevant studies will be looked for that may not have been captured during the initial database search.

The database search for literature will commence in December 2025 and the review is expected to be completed by August 2026. The search strategy will be re-run 3 months before the manuscript submission to keep the review updated.

Study selection and screening

First, duplicates will be removed using EndNote. Two independent reviewers will screen titles and abstracts using Rayyan.21 Full text of studies will be obtained and will be independently screened by two reviewers applying inclusion/exclusion criteria. Any disagreements will be resolved through discussion or a third reviewer.

Data extraction

The data will be extracted from each selected article by two independent reviewers. Data will be extracted using a pretested data extraction spreadsheet in Excel (online supplemental file 1). These two sheets will be compared, and any inconsistencies will be discussed and adjudicated by a third reviewer if required. In case further information or clarification is needed, the corresponding authors will be contacted. The study selection process will be detailed in a PRISMA flow chart.

Quality assessment

Included studies will be critically appraised using the Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal tool for qualitative research.22 Two reviewers will independently undertake the appraisal and comment on each criterion. Disagreements will be resolved by discussion between reviewers, failing which, by consulting a third member of the review team for arbitration. This is not to exclude studies, but to provide context for the analysis. The level of credibility of the included studies will be presented in a table.

Data synthesis

Thematic synthesis: Findings from qualitative studies will be analysed using Braun & Clarke’s thematic analysis framework,23 specifically focusing on the experiences, perspectives, nature, decision making, access to supportive services and impact of caregiving in caregivers of paediatric cancer patients. Participant quotations and textual content will be extracted from the Results/Findings and Discussion/Conclusion sections of each selected paper. These extracts will be entered verbatim into Qualcoder 3.6 software for systematic coding of qualitative data. Two reviewers will independently code existing findings line by line with new descriptive codes generated according to the meaning and content of the data. Any discrepancy will be resolved by discussion. Then, the codes will be grouped into a smaller set of new codes that capture their meaning. Analytical themes that went beyond the themes identified in the primary studies will be generated inductively.

Patient and public involvement

No patients or the public were involved in the design or conduct of our research.

Ethics and disseminations

This research is exempt from ethics approval because the work is carried out on published documents. Findings of this study will be disseminated to a wider audience through scientific communications, including publication in open-access journals and presentations at national and international level fora.

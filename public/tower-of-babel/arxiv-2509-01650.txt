Sharp unconditional well-posedness of the 2- d d periodic cubic hyperbolic nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Engin Başakoğlu, Tadahiro Oh, and Yuzhao Wang

Abstract
. We study semilinear local well-posedness of the two-dimensional periodic cubic hyperbolic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (HNLS) in Fourier–Lebesgue spaces. By employing the Fourier restriction norm method, we first establish sharp semilinear local well-posedness of HNLS in Fourier–Lebesgue spaces (modulo the endpoint case), including almost scaling-critical Fourier–Lebesgue spaces. Then, by adapting the normal form approach, developed by the second author with Guo and Kwon (2013) and by the second and third authors (2021), to the current hyperbolic setting, we establish sharp unconditional uniqueness of HNLS within the semilinear local well-posedness regime. As a key ingredient to both results, we establish sharp counting estimates for the hyperbolic Schrödinger equation. As a byproduct of our analysis, we also obtain sharp unconditional uniqueness of the (usual) two-dimensional periodic cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation in Fourier–Lebesgue spaces for p ≥ 3 p\geq 3 .

1.2.
Unconditional uniqueness

In
[
46

, Kato introduced the following notion of unconditional uniqueness.
We say that a Cauchy problem is unconditionally (locally) well-posed in
a given Banach space
B
if for every initial data

u_{0}\in B
, there exist
T>0
and a unique solution
u\in C([0,T];B)

with
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}
,
where the uniqueness holds in the entire class

C([0,T];B)

without intersecting with any auxiliary function space
(such as the
X^{s,b}
-spaces).
We refer to such uniqueness
as unconditional uniqueness. Unconditional uniqueness is a concept of uniqueness which does not depend on how solutions are constructed,
and thus is of fundamental importance.
As such,
unconditional uniqueness for dispersive equations
has been studied extensively

1.3.
Normal form approach

A normal form method
refers to an approach, where one applies
a sequence of transforms to reduce dynamics to the essential part,
corresponding to the (nearly) resonant part.
A general procedure is given as follows:

(1)

Separate the nonlinear part into (nearly) resonant and (highly) non-resonant parts,

(2)

“Eliminate” the non-resonant part
at the expense of introducing higher order terms,

(3)

Repeat this process indefinitely (or terminate it at some finite step).

There are different approaches
to
“eliminate” the non-resonant part
in the second step.
The commonly used normal form methods
are the Birkhoff normal form reductions (see, for example,
[
2
,
10
]

in the PDE context)
and the Poincaré-Dulac normal form reductions.
In this paper, we
implement an infinite iteration of
Poincaré-Dulac normal form reductions,
developed in
[
40
,
66
]
,
to transform the cubic HNLS (
1.1
)
into a normal form equation; see (
1.21
) below.

where

and we split the nonlinearity

into two parts,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{x}\big(\mathfrak{N}(u)\big)(n)&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}-n_{2}+n_{3}\\

(1.20)

\displaystyle\Phi(\bar{n})=\Phi(n,n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})=|n|_{-}^{2}-|n_{1}|_{-}^{2}+|n_{2}|_{-}^{2}-|n_{3}|_{-}^{2}.

On the Fourier support of

In the one-dimensional case,

It is, however, not the case in the higher dimensional setting.
For the elliptic cubic NLS

\displaystyle\Phi(\bar{n})

\displaystyle=|n|_{+}^{2}-|n_{1}|_{+}^{2}+|n_{2}|_{+}^{2}-|n_{3}|_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle=2(n-n_{1})\cdot(n-n_{3}).

Namely,

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}^{(J)}\sum_{\mathcal{T}_{J-1}\in\mathfrak{T}(J-1)}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{T}^{\infty}_{J-1}}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}\in\mathfrak{N}(\mathcal{T}_{J-1})\\
\mathbf{n}_{r}=n\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{N}^{(1)}({\bf u})(n_{b})|

\displaystyle\times\prod_{a\in\mathcal{T}^{\infty}_{J-1}\setminus\{b\}}\langle n_{a}\rangle^{sp}|\widehat{{\bf u}}_{n_{a}}|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq(2J-1)!!\cdot\mathcal{M}^{(J)}\|{\bf u}\|^{2J+1}_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,p}},

uniformly in
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
where
\mathcal{M}^{(J)}
is defined by

(4.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{(J)}&=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{T}_{J-1}\in\mathfrak{T}(J-1)\\

n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{M}^{(J)}(\mathcal{T}_{J-1};b,n)\\

b\in\mathcal{T}^{\infty}_{J-1}\\
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}\in\mathfrak{N}(\mathcal{T}_{J-1})\\
\mathbf{n}_{r}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\prod_{a\in\mathcal{T}^{\infty}_{J-1}\setminus\{b\}}\langle n_{a}\rangle^{sp^{\prime}}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{J-1}\text{A}_{k}^{c}}}{\prod_{k=1}^{J-1}|\widetilde{\mu}_{k}|^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

By Hölder’s inequality with (
4.24
)
and (
4.5
), we have

(4.25)

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{M}^{(J)}(\mathcal{T}_{J-1};b,n)\big)^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{S}^{(J)}\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{(J)}(\mathcal{T}_{J-1};b,n),

uniformly in

\mathcal{T}_{J-1}\in\mathfrak{T}(J-1)
,

b\in\mathcal{T}^{\infty}_{J-1}
, and

n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
where

\mathcal{S}^{(J)}
is as in (
4.7
)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{(J)}(\mathcal{T}_{J-1};b,n)
is defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{(J)}(\mathcal{T}_{J-1};b,n)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{J-1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}\in\mathfrak{N}(\mathcal{T}_{J-1})\\
\mathbf{n}_{r}=n\\
\mu_{k}=\alpha_{k},k=1,\dots,J-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\prod_{a\in\mathcal{T}^{\infty}_{J-1}\setminus\{b\}}\langle n_{a}\rangle^{sp^{\prime}}}.

Proceeding inductively
as in the proof of Lemma
3.13
,
we obtain the following bound:

(4.26)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{T}_{J-1}\in\mathfrak{T}(J-1)\\
b\in\mathcal{T}^{\infty}_{J-1}\\
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{(J)}(\mathcal{T}_{J-1};b,n)\leq C^{J-1}.

Hence, from (

) and (

), we have

(4.27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(J)}\lesssim C_{p}^{j}\frac{K^{4(1-J)}}{((2J-1)!!)^{4}}

for some

Therefore,

),

), (

A.1.
Local well-posedness via the Fourier restriction norm method

In this subsection,
we present a proof of Theorem
1.2
(i).

Given
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
,
define the
X^{s,b}_{p}
-space via the norm (see
[
35
,
30
,
11
,
12
]
):

(A.1)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,b}_{p}}=\|\langle n\rangle^{s}\langle\tau+|n|_{-}^{2}\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}(\tau,n)\|_{\ell^{p}_{n}L^{p}_{\tau}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})},

where
|n|_{-}^{2}
is the hyperbolic modulus defined in (
1.5
).
For
bp^{\prime}>1
, we have

(A.2)

\displaystyle X^{s,b}_{p}\subset C(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{F}L^{s,p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})).

Given
T>0
,
we define the local-in-time version of the
X^{s,b}_{p}
-space
by setting

(A.3)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,b}_{p}(T)}=\inf\big\{\|v\|_{X^{s,b}_{p}}:v|_{[0,T]}=u\big\},

where the infimum is taken over all extensions
v
of
u

from
[0,T]
to
\mathbb{R}
.

We first recall the following linear estimates;
see
[
30
, Lemma 2.2]
for a proof.

Lemma A.1
.

(i)
(homogeneous linear estimate).

Let
s,b\in\mathbb{R}

and
1<p<\infty
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\|S(t)f\|_{X^{s,b}_{p}(T)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,p}}

for any
0<T\leq 1
.

(ii)
(nonhomogeneous linear estimate).

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1<p<\infty
, and
-\frac{1}{p}<b^{\prime}\leq 0\leq b\leq 1+b^{\prime}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})F(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\bigg\|_{X^{s,b}_{p}(T)}\lesssim T^{1+b^{\prime}-b}\|F\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}_{p}(T)}

for any
0<T\leq 1
.

Next, we state the key trilinear estimate.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
s>1-\frac{1}{p}
.
Then, there exists small
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{1}\overline{u}_{2}u_{3}\|_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{p}+2\varepsilon}_{p}(T)}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|u_{j}\|_{X^{s,1-\frac{1}{p}+\varepsilon}_{p}(T)}.

for any
0<T\leq 1
.

Once we prove Proposition
A.2
,
Theorem
1.2
(i) follows from a standard contraction argument,
applied to the Duhamel formulation (
1.15
),
with Lemma
A.1
and Proposition
A.2
.
Since the argument is standard, we omit details.

Before proceeding further, we recall the following calculus lemma;
see, for example,
[
32
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\beta\geq\gamma\geq 0
and
\beta+\gamma>1
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\tau_{1}}{\langle\tau-\tau_{1}\rangle^{\beta}\langle\tau_{1}\rangle^{\gamma}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle\tau\rangle^{\alpha}}

where
\alpha
is given
by

\displaystyle\alpha=\begin{cases}\gamma,&\text{if }\beta>1,\\
\gamma-\varepsilon,&\text{if }\beta=1,\\
\beta+\gamma-1,&\text{if }\beta<1\end{cases}

for any
\varepsilon>0
.

We now present a proof of Proposition
A.2
.

Proof of Proposition
A.2
.

By the definition of the restricted norm (
A.3
), it suffices to prove
the following bound:

(A.4)

\displaystyle\|u_{1}\overline{u}_{2}u_{3}\|_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{p}+2\varepsilon}_{p}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|u_{j}\|_{X^{s,1-\frac{1}{p}+\varepsilon}_{p}}

without time localization.
By writing

(A.5)

\displaystyle u_{1}\overline{u}_{2}u_{3}=\mathfrak{N}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})+\mathfrak{R}_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})+\mathfrak{R}_{2}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}),

where
\mathfrak{N}
,
\mathfrak{R}_{1}
, and
\mathfrak{R}_{2}
are as in (
1.17
),
we estimate
the
X^{s,-\frac{1}{p}+2\varepsilon}_{p}
-norm of each term
on the right-hand side of (
A.5
) in the following.

Let
\sigma_{0}=\tau+|n|_{-}^{2}
and

\sigma_{j}=\tau_{j}+|n_{j}|_{-}^{2}
,
j=1,2,3
.
The estimate (
A.4
)
for the non-resonant part
\mathfrak{N}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
follows, once we prove

(A.6)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}-n_{2}+n_{3}\\
n\neq n_{1},n_{3}\end{subarray}}\,\intt_{\tau=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}+\tau_{3}}M\prod_{j=1}^{3}f_{j}^{*}(\tau_{j},n_{j})d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\bigg\|_{\ell^{p}_{n}L^{p}_{\tau}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|f_{j}\|_{\ell^{p}_{n}L^{p}_{\tau}},

where

\displaystyle f^{*}(\tau_{j},n_{j})=\begin{cases}f(\tau_{j},n_{j}),&\text{when $j=1,3$,}\rule[-8.53581pt]{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\\
\overline{f(\tau_{2},n_{2})},&\text{when $j=2$, }\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle M=M(n,n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},\tau,\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})=\frac{\langle n\rangle^{s}}{\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle^{\frac{1}{p}-2\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\langle n_{j}\rangle^{s}\langle\sigma_{j}\rangle^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\varepsilon}}.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\text{LHS of }\eqref{tri3}\lesssim\bigg(\sup_{n,\tau}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}-n_{2}+n_{3}\\

\displaystyle I_{n,\tau}(M)

Optimal Strategies for Federated Learning Maintaining Client Privacy
Uday Bhaskar, Varul Srivastava, Avyukta Manjunatha Vummintala, Naresh Manwani, Sujit Gujar

Abstract
Federated Learning (FL) emerged as a learning method to enable the server to train models over data distributed among various clients. These clients are protective about their data being leaked to the server, any other client, or an external adversary, and hence, locally train the model and share it with the server rather than sharing the data. The introduction of sophisticated inferencing attacks enabled the leakage of information about data through access to model parameters. To tackle this challenge, privacy-preserving federated learning aims to achieve differential privacy through learning algorithms like DP-SGD. However, such methods involve adding noise to the model, data, or gradients, reducing the model’s performance. This work provides a theoretical analysis of the tradeoff between model performance and communication complexity of the FL system. We formally prove that training for one local epoch per global round of training gives optimal performance while preserving the same privacy budget. We also investigate the change of utility (tied to privacy) of FL models with a change in the number of clients and observe that when clients are training using DP-SGD and argue that for the same privacy budget, the utility improved with increased clients. We validate our findings through experiments on real-world datasets. The results from this paper aim to improve the performance of privacy-preserving federated learning systems.

Federated Learning

[

These locally trained models are then shared with the server, and the process is repeated. FL has been a widely adopted technique for training models for two reasons: (1) Clients want to preserve the privacy of their data from other clients and the server, and (2) Distributing training among many smaller clients is faster and more cost-effective in some cases.

Clients like hospitals and banks possess data that potentially can have a huge impact if ML models are trained using their collective data. Still, they are highly sensitive and cannot be leaked to an adversary. Consider a scenario where multiple hospitals collaborate to train a model for predicting a certain disease using the data they possess. Each hospital can choose to train its models, but the model performance will improve if it is trained collectively. However, certain privacy regulations and risks prohibit hospitals from sharing the data with other branches.

Federated learning (FL) is a solution for collaborative learning in such settings.

Differential Privacy

Definition 1

A randomized algorithm

3.3
Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent (DP-SGD)

DP-SGD

[
6
]
safeguards the privacy of gradients generated during the stochastic gradient descent optimization process. During the learning process, the gradient of the loss with respect to the model parameters is computed for each example and clipped to a maximum

norm of

\sigma^{2}C^{2}\mathbf{I}
is added to the average of the gradients corresponding to samples in a mini-batch. This guarantees
(\epsilon,\delta)-
DP on the data by limiting the sensitivity of the output to each data point. This achieved optimal privacy-utility guarantees compared to methods of that time and enabled deploying real-world differentially private systems
[
19

20
]

DP-SGD with Tempered Sigmoid Activation:

Clipping the gradients leads to information loss, especially when we clip large gradients.
[
7
]
showed that replacing unbounded activation functions like ReLU in the neural network with a family of bounded activation functions (e.g., tempered sigmoid activation etc.) controls the magnitude of gradients. Tempered sigmoid activation function

\phi_{s,temp,o}(z)=\frac{s}{1+e^{-temp\cdot z}}-o

Where
s

DP-SGD and Linear Classifier with Handcrafted Features Beat DP-SGD on Deep Network:

[

4.3
Effect of number of local epochs on Performance

Motivation — Budget on the Number of Gradient Updates.

For any Machine Learning technique, we would like to train for a sufficiently large number of epochs (gradient updates) to have a good model. However, doing so increases the exposure of the Model to data points. Due to this increased exposure to the data, more noise needs to be added to ensure

l\in[0,1]
as

U_{l}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}^{*},\mathcal{D}):=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta},D)-\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta}^{*},D)|]<l)

We use this utility equation to show that as the number of clients increases, the utility of the model also increases. More specifically, the utility of the model changes by
O\left((1-\frac{1}{k})\right)
with
k
clients. We formally prove this result in Theorem
3
.

Theorem 3

For a
(\epsilon,\delta)-
differentially private FL model with local training using
DP-SGD
and aggregation using
FedAvg
, trained for
T
total epochs and
k
clients, we have
U_{l}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}^{*},\mathcal{D})\sim 1-O\left(\frac{1}{l^{2}k}\right)
.

Proof 2

Consider any global round
r
of training. In this global round, client
c_{i}
initialize its model with
M^{r-1}
independently on their data
D_{i}
and outputs the model
M^{r}_{i}
with parameter
\bm{\theta}_{i}^{r}
. These parameters
\bm{\theta}_{i}^{r}
can be viewed as random variables sampled from some distribution
\mathbb{Q}(\bm{\theta}^{*},\sigma)
.
In the
FedAvg
step, we update the parameters of the global model to be
\bm{\theta}^{r}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bm{\theta}_{i}^{r}
. These parameters are random variables following some distribution, with the mean as
\bm{\theta}^{*}
. Hence,
\bm{\theta}^{r}\sim\mathbb{Q}(\bm{\theta}^{*},\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{k}})
.

By Assumption
1
we have
|\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta},D)-\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}^{*},D)|\leq\beta||\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*}||
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta},D)-\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta}^{*},D)|]\geq l\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|]\geq\frac{l}{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}\beta^{2}}{l^{2}k}

We obtain the last equation through Chebyschev’s inequality
[
23
]
. Therefore, by definition of utility Definition
2
, we have
U_{l}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}^{*},\mathcal{D})\sim 1-O(\frac{1}{l^{2}k})
.

We therefore show that with a large number of clients, the server obtains a better performing model while guaranteeing the same privacy to each client dataset.

7
Conclusion

We establish an optimal training protocol for training Local Differentially Private Federated Models. We show that communicating the local model to the server after every local epoch and increasing the number of clients participating in the process will improve the aggregated model performance. We back our findings with theoretical guarantees. We also validate our findings with experimental analysis of real-world datasets.

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem 2

Consider two methods of training at a client.

1.

PFL (Algorithm
1
) DP-SGD + FedAvg:
This approach ensures
(\epsilon,\delta)-
differential privacy, i.e. using
DP-SGD
for local updates and
FedAvg
for global rounds of aggregation. Note that
DP-SGD
uses gradient clipping with clipping parameter
C
. For client
i
, corresponding model parameters are represented as
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}
.

2.

SGD + FedAvg:
It trains the model using SGD for local updates and
FedAvg
for global rounds of aggregation. SGD does not use gradient clipping. We treat no-gradient clipping as gradient clipping with a very high clipping parameter
C_{1}
. When clipping parameter is very high, we will never be clipping the gradient in SGD. For client
i
; corresponding parameters are
\bm{\theta}_{i}
.

Our goal is to find
E
(the number of local epochs in each global epoch) in PFL Algorithm
1
such that the performance degradation due to noise addition is minimized. For client
i
, the performance degradation is captured as follows.

\displaystyle\Delta=\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{R}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}^{E+1},D_{i})-\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta}^{E+1},D_{i})|]

where
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{E+1}
be the parameters returned by DP-SGD for client
i
after
E
local epochs,
\bm{\theta}_{i}^{E+1}
be the parameters returned by SGD for client
i
after
E
local epochs,
D_{i}
be the training data corresponding to client
i
and
\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta},D_{i})=\frac{1}{|D_{i}|}\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in D_{i}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}^{E+1};(\mathbf{x},y))
. The expectation is with respect to the product distribution
\mathcal{D}^{\mid D_{i}\mid}\times\prod_{t=1}^{E}\;\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}C^{2}\mathbf{I})
.
\Delta
can be further simplified as follows.

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|D_{i}|}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in D_{i}}\left(\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}^{E+1},(\mathbf{x},y))-\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}^{E+1},(\mathbf{x},y))\right)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|D_{i}|}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in D_{i}}\left|\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}^{E+1},(\mathbf{x},y))-\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}^{E+1},(\mathbf{x},y))\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{|D_{i}|}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in D_{i}}\|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}^{E+1}-\bm{\theta}^{E+1}\|\right]\quad\quad\quad\text{\hfill$\triangleright$ {\color[rgb]{0,0,1}using Lipschitz property of }}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\leq\beta\mathbb{E}\left[\|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}^{E+1}-\bm{\theta}^{E+1}\|\right]

The gradient update step of SGD (local epoch in
SGD +FedAvg
) is

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t+1}=\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t}-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{L}\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in B_{i}^{t}}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\frac{1}{\max\left(1,\frac{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\|}{C_{1}}\right)}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t}-\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t+1}=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{L}\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in B_{i}^{t}}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\frac{1}{\max\left(1,\frac{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\|}{C_{1}}\right)}.

Where
\alpha_{1}
is the step size,
C_{1}
is a the clipping parameters. As discussed, taking very large value of
C_{1}
has same effect as no-clipping.
B_{i}^{t}\subset D_{i}
be the minibatch presented to SGD in the
t^{th}
local epoch at client
i
.
Adding for all
t
from
1
to
E
where
E
is the number of local epochs, we get

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}_{i}^{1}-\bm{\theta}_{i}^{E+1}=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{E}\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in B_{i}^{t}}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\frac{1}{\max\left(1,\frac{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\|}{C_{1}}\right)}.

(1)

Similarly, for
DP-SGD
(local epoch in
DP-SGD + FedAvg
), using step-size
\alpha
, mini-batch size
L
, we have the following update equation.

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t+1}=\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t}-\frac{\alpha}{L}\cdot\left(\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in B_{i}^{t}}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\frac{1}{\max\left(1,\frac{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\|}{C}\right)}+\bm{\eta}_{t}\right).

Here,
\bm{\eta}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}C^{2}\mathbf{I})
and
\sigma
depends on the privacy budget
\epsilon
and parameter
\delta
(see the details in
[
6
, Theorem 1]
). Here, note that we take the same mini-batches in
E
epochs that were used in
SGD + FedAvg
.
Summing up all the terms for
t
from
1
to
E
we get,

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{1}-\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{E+1}=\frac{\alpha}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{E}\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in B_{i}^{t}}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\frac{1}{\max\left(1,\frac{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\|}{C}\right)}+\frac{\alpha}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{E}\bm{\eta}_{t}.

(2)

Using the same initial parameters for SGD and DP-SGD, i.e.,
\bm{\theta}_{i}^{1}=\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{1}
, we find
\|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{E+1}-\bm{\theta}_{i}^{E+1}\|
as follows.

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{E+1}-\bm{\theta}_{i}^{E+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{E}\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in B_{i}^{t}}\left\|\frac{\alpha\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))}{\max\left(1,\frac{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\|}{C}\right)}-\frac{\alpha_{1}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))}{\max\left(1,\frac{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\|}{C_{1}}\right)}\right\|+\frac{\alpha}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{E}\|\bm{\eta}_{t}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{E}\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in B_{i}^{t}}(\alpha C+\alpha_{1}C_{1})+\frac{\alpha}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{E}\|\bm{\eta}_{t}\|\quad\quad\quad\text{\hfill$\triangleright$ {\color[rgb]{0,0,1}using triangle inequality}}

In the above, we used the fact that
\left\|\frac{\alpha\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))}{\max\left(1,\frac{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\|}{C}\right)}\right\|\leq\alpha C
and
\left\|\frac{\alpha_{1}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))}{\max\left(1,\frac{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{t};(\mathbf{x},y))\|}{C_{1}}\right)}\right\|\leq\alpha_{1}C_{1}
. Taking expectation on both sides with respect to the product distribution
\mathcal{D}^{\mid D_{i}\mid}\times\prod_{t=1}^{E}\;\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}C^{2}\mathbf{I})
, we get the following.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{E+1}-\bm{\theta}_{i}^{E+1}\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{E}\sum_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in B_{i}^{t}}(\alpha C+\alpha_{1}C_{1})+\frac{\alpha}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{E}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{\eta}_{t}\|]

\displaystyle=E(\alpha C+\alpha_{1}C_{1})+\frac{\sqrt{2}\alpha\sigma C}{L}\frac{\Gamma({\frac{N+1}{2}})}{\Gamma({\frac{N}{2}})}=E\left(\alpha C+\alpha_{1}C_{1}+\frac{\sqrt{2}\alpha\sigma C}{L}\frac{\Gamma({\frac{N+1}{2}})}{\Gamma({\frac{N}{2}})}\right).

We use the fact that
\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{\eta}_{t}\|]=\sqrt{2}\sigma C\frac{\Gamma({\frac{N+1}{2}})}{\Gamma({\frac{N}{2}})}
, where
N
is the size of the parameter vector
\bm{\theta}
. Thus, the performance degradation
\Delta
is further bounded as
follows.

\displaystyle\Delta\leq\beta\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}^{E+1}-\bm{\theta}^{E+1}\|]\leq\beta E\left(\alpha C+\alpha_{1}C_{1}+\frac{\sqrt{2}\alpha\sigma C}{L}\frac{\Gamma({\frac{N+1}{2}})}{\Gamma({\frac{N}{2}})}\right)

We see that the upper bound on
\Delta
is minimum when
E=1
.

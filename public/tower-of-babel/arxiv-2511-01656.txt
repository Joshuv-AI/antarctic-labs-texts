Constructing the big relative Fukaya category and its open–closed maps
Nick Sheridan

Abstract
This paper continues previous work of the author with Perutz, in which the ‘small’ version of Seidel’s relative Fukaya category of a smooth complex projective variety relative to a normal crossings divisor was constructed, under a semipositivity assumption. In the present work, we generalize this to construct the ‘big’ relative Fukaya category in the same setting, as well as its closed–open and open–closed maps, and prove Abouzaid’s split-generation criterion in this context. We also establish a general framework for constructing chain-level Floer-theoretic operations in this context, and dealing efficiently with signs and bounding cochains, which will be used in follow-up work with Ganatra to construct the cyclic open–closed map and establish its properties.

1.3
Small and big relative Fukaya categories

In order to accurately define the big relative Fukaya category, we need to expand the definition of an
A_{\infty}
category in several directions.
First, we need to allow the category to have curvature, which means we need the coefficient ring to be filtered, in order later to be able to get rid of the curvature by passing to bounding cochains (cf.
[
FOOO10
,
PS23b
]
).
Second, we need to allow it to have odd-degree elements, to accommodate bulk deformations (cf.
[
ST22
, Definition 1.1]
).
Third, we need to allow the coefficient ring to be a differential graded ring, to accommodate the differential on the bulk terms.

We define the notion of
A_{\infty}
category in such a context in Section
2
.
We briefly preview it here, to orient the reader for the purposes of the introduction.

Definition 1.5
.

A
coefficient ring
will be a four-tuple
(\mathbb{G}_{R},R,{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d_{R})
where

•

\mathbb{G}_{R}
is a grading datum;

•

R
is a
\mathbb{G}_{R}
-graded supercommutative ring;

•

{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}}
is a complete filtration on
R
with
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq 0}}R=R
;

•

d_{R}:\sigma(d)R\to R
is a degree-
1
graded filtered derivation satisfying
d_{R}^{2}=0
;

If the filtration is trivial, i.e.
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq 1}}=0
, we will write ‘
0
’ instead of
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}}
.
We denote
\mathbf{k}_{R}=R/{\mathcal{F}_{\geq 1}}R
.

A
(\mathbb{G}_{R},R,{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d_{R})
-linear
A_{\infty}
category
\mathcal{C}
will be one whose morphism spaces are differential
\mathbb{G}_{R}
-graded
R
-modules, on which the induced filtration is complete, and such that the curvature lies in
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq 1}}
; see Definition
2.10
for the precise definition.
A key construction is that of taking the cohomology category, which can only be done if
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq 1}}=0
so that the curvature vanishes. It produces a
H(R,d_{R})
-linear category
H(\mathcal{C})
.

We define
\mathrm{NE}\subset H_{2}(X,W)
to be the monoid of integral classes in the dual cone to
Nef
.

We recall the convention that
H_{*}(X):=H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})/torsion
, and similarly for
H_{*}(X,W)
.

There is a natural homomorphism
H_{2}(X,W)\to\mathbb{G}
(cf.
[
She20b
, Section 3.3]
), which endows the semigroup ring

\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{NE}]:=\left\{\sum_{u\in\mathrm{NE}}c_{u}\cdot r^{u}:\text{ $c_{u}\in\mathbb{Z}$, only finitely many $c_{u}$ are non-zero}\right\}

with a
\mathbb{G}
-grading.

Definition 1.6
.

The coefficient ring of the small relative Fukaya category is
(\mathbb{G},R^{{\operatorname{small}}},{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},0)
, where
R^{\operatorname{small}}
is the
\mathbb{G}
-graded completion of the semigroup ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{NE}]
for the adic filtration
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}}
for the ideal
\mathfrak{m}^{\operatorname{small}}
generated by the classes
r^{u}
for
u\neq 0
.

The
small relative Fukaya category
is a
(\mathbb{G},R^{\operatorname{small}},{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},0)
-linear
A_{\infty}
category, whose morphism spaces are furthermore free of finite rank.
Its objects are Lagrangian branes, and it coincides with the compact exact Fukaya category
\mathcal{F}(W)
modulo
\mathfrak{m}^{\operatorname{small}}
.
Structure maps count holomorphic discs
u
, weighted by
r^{[u]}\in R^{{\operatorname{small}}}
, where
[u]\in H_{2}(X,W)
.
For brevity’s sake, and for consistency with
[
PS23b
]
where it was constructed, we will denote it by
\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{small}}(X,D)
, where
D:=X\setminus W
, although
D
need not deformation retract onto a divisor.
We will similarly denote
\mathcal{F}(X\setminus D):=\mathcal{F}(W)
.

The coefficient ring of the big relative Fukaya category is, of course, bigger.
Let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a Morse function,
g
a Riemannian metric such that
(f,g)
is a Morse–Smale pair, and let
(CM^{*}(f,g),d_{f,g})
be the corresponding Morse cochain complex.

The coefficient ring of the big relative Fukaya category,
R^{{\operatorname{big}}}
, includes extra variables coming from
CM^{*-2}(f,g)
, which are called ‘bulk variables’.
We break down its definition into steps.

First, we first form the (super-)symmetric (differential graded)
R^{\operatorname{small}}
-algebra
Sym(R^{\operatorname{small}}\otimes CM^{*-2}(f,g)^{\vee})
.
Note that a choice of basis
\alpha^{i}
for
CM^{*}(f,g)
canonically determines an isomorphism

Sym(R^{\operatorname{small}}\otimes CM^{*-2}(f,g)^{\vee})\cong R^{\operatorname{small}}[r_{i}]_{i},

where the generators
r_{i}
(corresponding to the dual basis) are ‘bulk variables’.
We have
|r_{i}|=2-|\alpha_{i}|
.
The Morse differential on
CM^{*}(f,g)
induces a differential on the symmetric algebra, by the Leibniz rule, making this into a differential graded algebra.

Next, we enlarge the polynomial algebra
R^{\operatorname{small}}[r_{i}]
, to the
R^{\operatorname{small}}
-subalgebra of
\mathbb{Q}\otimes R^{\operatorname{small}}[r_{i}]
generated by the family of elements
r_{i}^{k}/k!
, over all
i
and
k
; this is known as a divided power algebra (it does not depend on the choice of basis).
We consider the ideal of
\mathbb{Q}\otimes R^{\operatorname{small}}[r_{i}]
generated by
\mathfrak{m}^{\operatorname{small}}
together with the classes
r_{i}
; and consider its adic filtration on this algebra.
By intersecting with the divided power algebra, we obtain a filtration on the latter (note however that this is not the adic filtration for any ideal).

Definition 1.7
.

The coefficient ring of the big relative Fukaya category is
(\mathbb{G},R^{\operatorname{big}},{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d_{R})
, where
R^{\operatorname{big}}
is the
\mathbb{G}
-graded completion of the divided power algebra, with respect to the filtration defined above;
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}}
is the induced filtration on the completion.
The differential
d_{R}
is the one induced by the Morse differential
d_{f,g}
in accordance with the Leibniz rule.

Remark 1.8
.

The reader only interested in characteristic zero may of course tensor with
\mathbb{Q}
and forget all about the divided powers.
However, it is intriguing that divided powers are the most natural coefficient ring, given their role in crystalline cohomology.

In this paper we extend the construction of
[
PS23b
]
to construct the
big relative Fukaya category
,
\mathcal{F}^{{\operatorname{big}}}(X,D)
, which is a
(\mathbb{G},R^{{\operatorname{big}}},{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d_{R})
-linear
A_{\infty}
category.
Its objects are the same as those of the small relative Fukaya category, namely, Lagrangian branes.
Its structure maps count holomorphic discs
u
with points constrained to lie on Morse flowlines, corresponding to the bulk variables.
It is equal to the small relative Fukaya category modulo the bulk variables
r_{i}
.
It has further useful properties: it is unital in an appropriate sense, and its morphism spaces are free of finite rank.

Remark 1.9
.

The reader may wonder: given that we may completely avoid denominators in the definition of the small relative Fukaya category, why may we not avoid them in the definition of the big relative Fukaya category? The answer is that, in order to avoid denominators, we must choose perturbation data equivariantly, and this is not possible for the moduli spaces involved in the definition of the big relative Fukaya category. Specifically, the assertion that the evaluation map (
24
) is a submersion will not be true at fixed points of the group action, if we use equivariant perturbation data.

1.4
Bounding cochains

For any filtered
A_{\infty}
category, there are two operations we may apply to it.
First, we may change coefficients.
Let
(\mathbb{G}_{R},R,{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d)\to(\mathbb{G}_{S},S,\mathcal{G}_{\geq\bullet},e)
be a morphism of coefficient rings.
This means it is a filtered homomorphism of differential graded rings (see Definition
2.4
for details).
Then for any
(\mathbb{G}_{R},R,{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d)
-linear
A_{\infty}
category
\mathcal{C}
, we can form
\mathcal{C}\hat{\otimes}_{R}S
, which is a
(\mathbb{G}_{S},S,\mathcal{G}_{\geq\bullet},e)
-linear
A_{\infty}
category.

Example 1.10
.

If
\mathbf{k}
is a field,
K\subset\mathbb{R}
a subgroup containing the image of
\kappa:H_{2}(X,W)\to\mathbb{R}
, and
R^{{\operatorname{small}}}
is concentrated in degree
0\in\mathbb{G}
, we may consider the Novikov ring
\Lambda_{\mathbf{k},K_{\geq 0}}:=\mathbf{k}[[T^{K_{\geq 0}}]]
, where
K_{\geq 0}=K\cap\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, with the filtration
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq i}}\Lambda_{\mathbf{k},K_{\geq 0}}=(T^{iA})
for some
A
smaller than the minimum non-zero value of
\kappa
on
\mathrm{NE}
.
Then there is a morphism of coefficient rings
(\mathbb{G},R^{{\operatorname{small}}},{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},0)\to(\mathbb{G},\Lambda_{\mathbf{k},K_{\geq 0}},{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},0)
which sends
r^{u}\mapsto T^{\kappa(u)}
.
The morphism is well-defined (i.e., converges) by our assumption that
\kappa
lies in the interior of
Nef
.

The second operation on a
(\mathbb{G},R,{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d)
-linear
A_{\infty}
category
\mathcal{C}
, is to form the category of bounding cochains
\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{bc}}
, as in
[
PS23b
, Section 2]
.
This gives a
(\mathbb{G},R,0,d)
-linear (in particular, as the filtration is trivial, uncurved and unfiltered)
A_{\infty}
category.

Example 1.11
.

The
A_{\infty}
category which was constructed and denoted by
\mathcal{F}(X,D,\kappa;\Lambda_{\mathbf{k},K})^{\operatorname{bc}}
in
[
PS23b
, Section 1.4]
, and which appears in the homological mirror symmetry statement of
[
GHH+24
, Theorem B]
, is constructed by first changing coefficients of the small relative Fukaya category
\mathcal{F}^{{\operatorname{small}}}(X,D)
to the Novikov ring
\Lambda_{\mathbf{k},K_{\geq 0}}
of Example
1.10
, then taking bounding cochains to get rid of curvature, then changing coefficients to
\Lambda_{\mathbf{k},K}=\mathbf{k}((T^{K}))
.

1.5
Closed–open maps

We now state our main results.
Let
R^{\operatorname{big}}\to S
be a morphism of coefficient rings, and
\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D;S)^{\operatorname{bc}}
the
(\mathbb{G}_{S},S,0,d_{S})
-linear
A_{\infty}
category obtained by first changing coefficients of
\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D)
from
R^{\operatorname{big}}
to
S
, then passing to the category of bounding cochains.
We state our results for the ordinary Hochschild invariants of
\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D;S)^{\operatorname{bc}}
, but they are corollaries of analogous results for the filtered Hochschild invariants of
\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D)
, which are stated in Section
5
.

We define the big quantum cohomology
QH^{*}(X;R^{\operatorname{big}}):=H^{*}(X)\otimes R^{\operatorname{big}}
to be the
R^{\operatorname{big}}
-linear unital Frobenius algebra with pairing

\langle a,b\rangle:=\int a\cup b,

and product
\star
determined by

\langle a\star b,c\rangle=\sum_{\beta\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}),n}\frac{GW_{\beta,3+n}^{X}(a,b,c,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)}{n!}\cdot r^{\beta},

where
\alpha=\sum_{i}r_{i}\alpha^{i}
, where
\alpha^{i}
is the basis for
CM^{*}(f,g)
which determines the variables
r_{i}
in
R^{\operatorname{big}}
.
Note that our
(Semipositive)
assumption implies that
X
is semipositive (see
[
PS23b
, Corollary 3.5]
), so the construction of Gromov–Witten invariants with stable domains may be carried out over
\mathbb{Z}
as in
[
RT94
]
, see also
[
MS12
, Section 7]
; this implies that the formula above does indeed define a class in
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\otimes R^{\operatorname{big}}
, where we recall that
R^{\operatorname{big}}
is the ring of divided power series in the variables
r_{i}
.

We define
QH^{*}(X;S):=QH^{*}(X;R^{\operatorname{big}})\otimes_{R}S
, which is a
S
-linear unital Frobenius algebra.

Theorem 1.12
.

There is a unital graded
S
-algebra homomorphism called the
closed–open map

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{O}:QH^{*}(X;S)\to HH^{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D;S)^{bc}),

where
HH^{*}
denotes Hochschild cohomology.
In the case
S=R^{\operatorname{big}}
, it coincides with the first-order deformation class of the big relative Fukaya category, in the direction of the bulk variables.

Theorem 1.13
.

There is a homomorphism of
QH^{*}(X;S)
-modules called the
open–closed map

\mathcal{O}\mathcal{C}:HH_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D;S)^{bc})[-n]\to QH^{*}(X;S),

where
HH_{*}
denotes Hochschild homology.

Theorem 1.14
.

There is a morphism of
\mathcal{F}(X,D;S)^{bc}
-bimodules:

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{Y}:\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D;S)^{bc}_{\Delta}[-n]\to\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D;S)^{{\operatorname{bc}},!},

such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces HH_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D;S)^{bc})[-n]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces QH^{*}(X;S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces HH_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D;S)^{\operatorname{bc}},\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D;S)^{{\operatorname{bc}},!})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces HH^{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{big}}(X,D;S)^{bc}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}\mathcal{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}\mathcal{Y}_{*}}

These are the key ingredients to prove Abouzaid’s generation criterion in this context:

If

2
Algebraic preliminaries

We recall the definitions of Hochschild invariants of filtered
A_{\infty}
categories, and give formulae for the algebraic structures which are the main focus of this paper.

2.1
Gradings

We recall that a grading datum consists of an abelian group
\mathbb{G}
together with group homomorphisms
\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{i}\mathbb{G}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\mathbb{Z}/2
whose composition is non-trivial.
We will abbreviate this data by
\mathbb{G}
.
A morphism
\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{H}
of grading data is a homomorphism respecting the maps
i
and
\sigma
.

Given a
\mathbb{G}
-graded object
M
, and an object
m\in M
, we write
|m|=g\in\mathbb{G}
if
m\in M_{g}
.
We say
|m|=k\in\mathbb{Z}
if
|m|=i(k)
.
For
g\in\mathbb{G}
, we write
(-1)^{g}
for
(-1)^{\sigma(g)}
.

2.2
Koszul sign rule

We will adopt conventions which remove the need to write explicit Koszul-type signs in the definitions of our algebraic structures, by hiding them in the Koszul sign rule; more precisely, we make systematic use of the ‘coherence theorem’
[
ML98
, Theorem XI.1.1]
for the symmetric monoidal structure defined by the Koszul sign rule.
This has two advantages: first, it removes the need for explicit and complicated sign computations to verify algebraic identities; second, it will later allow for the efficient verification of signs in Floer-theoretic operations.
The disadvantage is that if one uses completely precise and unambiguous notation, it becomes unwieldy; instead we use a lighter notation which requires the reader to correctly interpret it from the context.
We also explain how our conventions are equivalent to the usual ones with explicit signs.
The reader who prefers explicit signs can find them, for example, in
[
Sei08a
,
She20a
]
, whose conventions agree with ours up to the above-mentioned translation.

We consider the symmetric monoidal category of
\mathbb{G}
-graded
\mathbb{Z}
-modules, where the monoidal structure is given by tensor product and the braiding by the Koszul sign rule.
Recall that morphisms in this category consist of grading-preserving homomorphisms.

We identify the subcategory of
\mathbb{G}
-graded free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules of rank one with the symmetric monoidal category of
\mathbb{G}
-graded
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-torsors.
We define the torsors
\sigma(g):=\mathbb{Z}_{2}[-g]
, which are trivial and concentrated in degree
g\in\mathbb{G}
.
A
trivialization
of a
\mathbb{G}
-graded
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-torsor is an isomorphism with some
\sigma(g)
.
Given an isomorphism between two
\mathbb{G}
-graded
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-torsors equipped with trivializations, we say that the
sign
of the isomorphism is the element of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, multiplication by which defines the induced isomorphism
\sigma(g)\cong\sigma(g)
.
For example, the sign of the braiding isomorphism
\sigma(g)\sigma(h)\cong\sigma(h)\sigma(g)
is
(-1)^{gh}
.
If the sign of an isomorphism is
+1
, we also say that the isomorphism
respects trivializations
.

We will abbreviate
\sigma(g)M:=\sigma(g)\otimes M
.
One can define the shift of a graded module, by
M[g]_{h}=M_{g+h}
; there is a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle\sigma(g)M

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}M[-g],\qquad\text{given by}

(1)

\displaystyle 1|m

\displaystyle\mapsto m.

Given a real vector space
V
, we define
\sigma(V)
to be the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-torsor of orientations of
V
, placed in degree
\dim(V)
.

Now recall that the category of graded
\mathbb{Z}
-modules is not merely symmetric monoidal, but
closed
symmetric monoidal: there exists an internal hom-functor
Hom^{*}(M,N)
, whose degree-
g
part is

\displaystyle Hom^{g}(M,N)

\displaystyle=Hom(M[-g],N).

We define an isomorphism

\displaystyle Hom^{|\alpha|}(M,N)

\displaystyle\to Hom(\sigma(\alpha)M,N)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{\alpha}

by the identification (
1
), where we abbreviate
\sigma(\alpha)=\sigma(|\alpha|)
.
Note that we may apply the coherence theorem to
\tilde{\alpha}
, as it belongs to a Hom-space (rather than an internal Hom-space, as
\tilde{\alpha}
does; while there is also a coherence theorem for closed symmetric monoidal categories, see
[
KML71
, Theorem 2.4]
, it is more elaborate than the version for symmetric monoidal categories which we will use).

We define various multilinear operations by composing others, using the braiding, and using the ‘natural trivializations’

\sigma(g_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes\sigma(g_{k})\cong\sigma(g_{1}+\ldots+g_{k})

(2)

which send
1\otimes\ldots\otimes 1\mapsto 1
.
There will typically only be one sensible way to use the braiding, but we must specify which of the isomorphisms (
2
) we use.
We illustrate our convention by an example.
Suppose we are given
\alpha\in Hom^{*}(B\otimes A,M)
and
\beta\in Hom^{*}(C\otimes M,N)
.
We say “let
\gamma\in Hom^{*}(A\otimes B\otimes C,N)
be defined by

\gamma(a,b,c)=\beta(c,\alpha(b,a))

where

\sigma(\gamma)=\sigma(\alpha)\sigma(\beta)\text{''}

to mean that
\tilde{\gamma}
is defined to be the composition of maps

\displaystyle\sigma(\gamma)\otimes A\otimes B\otimes C

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{i\otimes\mathrm{id}}\sigma(\alpha)\otimes\sigma(\beta)\otimes A\otimes B\otimes C

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{K}\sigma(\beta)\otimes C\otimes\sigma(\alpha)\otimes B\otimes A

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}\otimes\tilde{\alpha}}\sigma(\beta)\otimes C\otimes M

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\tilde{\beta}}N,

where on the first line,
i:\sigma(\gamma)\to\sigma(\alpha)\otimes\sigma(\beta)
is the natural trivialization (
2
), and on the second line,
K
is given by the braiding, i.e., the Koszul isomorphism.
This is equivalent to defining

\gamma(a,b,c)=(-1)^{|\alpha||\beta|+|\alpha||c|+|a||b|+|a||c|+|b||c|}\beta(c,\alpha(b,a)).

Note that the sign is precisely the Koszul sign associated to taking the symbols
\gamma,a,b,c
on the left, replacing
\gamma
by
\alpha,\beta
, then rearranging to get the order in which these symbols appear on the right, namely
\beta,c,\alpha,b,a
.
Note that if we had instead written “…where
\sigma(\gamma)=\sigma(\beta)\sigma(\alpha)
”, the resulting map
\gamma
would differ by the Koszul sign
(-1)^{|\alpha|\cdot|\beta|}
.

Composition of internal homs yields another example: for
\alpha\in Hom^{*}(A,B)
and
\beta\in Hom^{*}(B,C)
, we have

\widetilde{\beta\circ\alpha}(a)=\tilde{\beta}(\tilde{\alpha}(a))

where
\sigma(\beta\circ\alpha)=\sigma(\beta)\sigma(\alpha)
.

For another example, suppose we are given chain complexes
(M,d_{M})
and
(N,d_{N})
.
Then the standard differential
\partial
on the endomorphisms
Hom^{*}(M,N)
is given by

\partial(f)(m)=d_{N}(f(m))+f(d_{M}(m))

(3)

where
\sigma(\partial(f))=\sigma(d)\sigma(f)
.
Here the term
\sigma(d)
is identified with
\sigma(d_{N})
for the first term, and
\sigma(d_{M})
for the second term.

For any
\mathbb{G}
-graded
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-torsor
\sigma
, we also define its dual,
\sigma^{\vee}
.
Note that this is defined in terms of the internal hom, not in terms of the symmetric monoidal structure; and in fact, the coherence theorem for closed symmetric monoidal categories
[
KML71
, Theorem 2.4]
does not apply to such objects.
By convention,
\sigma^{\vee}
will only ever ‘interact’ via the natural evaluation isomorphism,
\sigma^{\vee}\sigma=\sigma(0)
(precisely, whenever
\sigma^{\vee}
appears in a commutative diagram to which we apply the coherence theorem, it is treated as a formal symbol whose only property is that it comes equipped with an isomorphism
\sigma^{\vee}\sigma=\sigma(0)
, but bears no other relation to
\sigma
); in particular, we never use the natural isomorphisms
\sigma\sigma^{\vee}=\sigma(0)
or
\sigma^{\vee\vee}=\sigma
.
With this convention, a trivialization of
\sigma
unambiguously induces one of
\sigma^{\vee}
, and an isomorphism
\sigma\otimes M\cong N
unambiguously determines an isomorphism
M\cong\sigma^{\vee}\otimes N
; and our applications of the coherence theorem for symmetric monoidal categories are valid.

For a final example, suppose that
(M,d_{M})
and
(N,d_{N})
are chain complexes, and
f,g\in Hom(M,N)
are both chain maps:
\partial(f)=\partial(g)=0
.
A homotopy between
f
and
g
is an element
h\in Hom(\sigma(\partial)^{\vee}M,N)
satisfying

f-g=\partial(h).

Note that we implicitly use the identification
\sigma(\partial)^{\vee}\sigma(\partial)=\sigma(0)
to identify
\partial(h)
with an element of
Hom(M,N)
.

2.3
Coefficient rings and modules

Definition 2.1
.

A
coefficient ring
will be a four-tuple
(\mathbb{G}_{R},R,{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d_{R})
where

•

\mathbb{G}_{R}
is a grading datum;

•

R
is a
\mathbb{G}_{R}
-graded (super)commutative ring;

•

{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}}
is a complete filtration of
R
, with
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq 0}}R=R
;

•

d_{R}:\sigma(d)R\to R
is a degree-
1
graded derivation which respects the filtration, and satisfies
d_{R}^{2}=0
.

If the filtration is trivial, i.e.
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq 1}}=0
, we will write ‘
0
’ instead of
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}}
.

We recall that a graded derivation satisfies the graded Leibniz rule:

d_{R}(r\cdot s)=d_{R}r\cdot s+r\cdot d_{R}s

as maps
\sigma(d)A\otimes A\to A
(which introduces a sign, in the usual convention).
We denote
\mathbf{k}_{R}:=R/{\mathcal{F}_{\geq 1}}R=\mathrm{Gr}_{0}R
.
We will abbreviate such a tuple by
R
, and omit subscripts on
\mathbb{G}
,
d
, and
\mathbf{k}
, where no confusion can result.
We denote the cohomology of
(R,d)
by
H(R)
.

Example 2.2
.

If
\mathbf{k}
is a commutative ring, and
(C,d)
is a
\mathbb{G}
-graded chain complex of free, finite-rank
\mathbf{k}
-modules, then we define a coefficient ring
(\mathbb{G},\mathbf{k}[[C]],{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d)
to be the graded completion of the graded symmetric algebra on
C
, with the adic filtration, and the induced differential.

Example 2.3
.

For any coefficient ring
(\mathbb{G},R,{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d)
, we may form another,
(\mathbb{G}\oplus\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{Gr}_{*}R,0,d)
, by taking the associated graded ring with the trivial filtration.

Definition 2.4
.

A
morphism of coefficient rings
,
\varphi:(\mathbb{G}_{R},R,{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d_{R})\to(\mathbb{G}_{S},S,\mathcal{G}_{\geq\bullet},d_{S})
, will consist of a morphism of grading data
\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}:\mathbb{G}_{R}\to\mathbb{G}_{S}
, and a filtered algebra homomorphism
\varphi:R\to S
, such that

•

|\varphi(r)|=\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}(|r|)
;

•

\varphi\circ d_{R}=d_{S}\circ\varphi
, as maps
\sigma(d)R\to S
.

Example 2.5
.

For any coefficient ring
R
, there exists a coefficient ring
(\mathbb{G},\mathbf{k},0,d)
, and morphisms
R\to\mathbf{k}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{*}R
.

Definition 2.6
.

If
R
is a coefficient ring, an
R
-module is a
\mathbb{G}
-graded
\mathbb{Z}
-module
M
equipped with the module action

\displaystyle R\otimes M

\displaystyle\to M

\displaystyle r\otimes m

\displaystyle\mapsto r\cdot m

respecting the
\mathbb{G}
-grading, and such that
r\cdot(s\cdot m)=(r\cdot s)\cdot m
; and a degree-
1
map

d_{M}:\sigma(d)M\to M

satisfying
d_{M}^{2}=0
and the analogue of the graded Leibniz rule.

Any
R
-module comes equipped with the filtration
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}}M:=({\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}}R)\cdot M
, whose completion we denote by

\overline{M}:=\varprojlim_{j}M/{\mathcal{F}_{\geq j}}M,

where the limit is taken in the category of
\mathbb{G}
-graded
\mathbb{Z}
-modules.
The module action and differential
d_{M}
extend to the completion, as they respect the filtration.
We say
M
is complete if
M=\overline{M}
.

We denote the cohomology of
(M,d_{M})
by
H(M)
, which is a graded
H(R)
-module.
A chain map of
R
-modules is a morphism
f:M\to N
satisfying
f(r\cdot m)=r\cdot f(m)
and
d_{N}\circ f=f\circ d_{M}
, as maps
\sigma(d)M\to N
.
A chain map induces a map of
H(R)
-modules,
H(f):H(M)\to H(N)
.
Note that any map of modules is automatically filtered.

We define the tensor product of
R
-modules to be the usual tensor product, equipped with the differential defined by the graded product rule.
As a special case, for
g\in\mathbb{G}
, we define the
g
-fold shift of an
R
-module to be the tensor product
\sigma(g)M
.
We define the completed tensor product, denoted by
\hat{\otimes}
, to be the tensor product followed by completion.

If
M
and
N
are
R
-modules, then
Hom^{*}_{R}(M,N)
consists of maps
f\in Hom^{*}(M,N)
such that

f(r\cdot m)=r\cdot f(m)

as maps
\sigma(f)R\otimes M\to N
(note that if we translate into standard notation there is a sign).
It is equipped with the structure of an
R
-module in the standard way (in particular, the differential is given by (
3
)).

2.4
(Filtered)

categories

We generalize some of the definitions from
[

, Appendix A]

Let

be a coefficient ring in the sense of Section

We will define a notion of

The usual notion of

(no differential on the coefficient ring) and

What we call an

Definition 2.7
.

An
(
R
-linear) pre-category

consists of a set of objects, together with morphism spaces

R
-modules.

.

Given pre-categories

and
\mathcal{C}_{1}

•

-bimodule as a

-bimodule.

•

The

\mathcal{C}
-bimodule

Given maps

Definition 2.9
.

We define the Hochschild cochains with coefficients in
\mathcal{M}

For an element

If
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}

\mathcal{C}

to be a map on objects

Given pre-categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, a pre-
\mathcal{D}
-bimodule
\mathcal{M}
, pre-functors

\displaystyle CC^{*}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{M})\otimes pre\text{-}fun(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})(F_{0},\ldots,F_{k})

\displaystyle\to CC^{*}\left(\mathcal{C},(F_{0}\otimes F_{k})^{*}\mathcal{M}\right),

\displaystyle\psi\otimes\varphi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\varphi_{k}

\displaystyle\mapsto\psi\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}\},

\psi\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}\}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}):=\sum\psi\left(F_{0}^{*}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{s_{0}^{1}}),\ldots,F_{0}^{*}(\ldots,c_{s_{0}^{j_{0}}}),\varphi_{1}^{*}(\ldots,c_{t_{1}}),\right.\\
\left.F_{1}^{*}(\ldots),\ldots\ldots,F_{k-1}^{*}(\ldots,c_{s_{k-1}^{j_{k-1}}}),\varphi_{k}^{*}(\ldots,c_{t_{k}}),F_{k}^{*}(\ldots),\ldots,F_{k}^{*}(\ldots,c_{s_{k}^{j_{k}}})\right)

Definition 2.10
.

An

R
-linear pre-category
\mathcal{C}

Remark 2.11
.

It may be helpful, although not strictly correct, to think of the

relations equivalently as
(d_{\mathcal{C}}+\mu)\{d_{\mathcal{C}}+\mu\}=0

is ‘the length-

The usual notion of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded

-linear

[

, Section 1a]

(\mathbb{Z},R,0,0)

is concentrated in degree

Note that the curvature
\mu^{0}
necessarily vanishes in this case.

More generally, if the coefficient ring
(\mathbb{G},R,{\mathcal{F}_{\geq\bullet}},d)
satisfies
{\mathcal{F}_{\geq 1}}=0

\mu^{0}
necessarily vanishes, and the first non-trivial

defines a differential on each morphism space

In this case we define the

to be the (in general non-unital) category with the same set of objects, morphisms the graded

-modules

, and composition induced by the map

induced by

\mu:\sigma(\mu)\otimes\sigma_{1}^{\vee}hom_{\mathcal{C}}(C_{0},C_{1})\otimes\sigma_{2}^{\vee}hom_{\mathcal{C}}(C_{1},C_{2})\to\sigma_{0}^{\vee}hom_{\mathcal{C}}(C_{0},C_{2})

together with the identification

\sigma(\circ)=\sigma(\mu)\sigma_{0}\sigma_{1}^{\vee}\sigma_{2}^{\vee},

(5)

From the second non-trivial

In the standard case, and translating to the standard sign conventions, this definition agrees with the convention for the cohomology category given in
[

, Section 3.2]
.

2.5
Bounding cochains

Let

Definition 2.14

Given an
R
-linear
A_{\infty}

\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}^{{pre\text{-}bc}}

Definition 3.1

The moduli space

The notion of a system of families of domains is a formalization of a standard approach in the Floer/Fukaya theory literature.
It can be made more explicit and precise by placing it within an appropriate categorical framework: there is a category of families of domains, with a symmetric monoidal structure coming from
(Disjoint union)
; the remaining operations above correspond to morphisms in this category (cf.
[
BM96
]
).
However, spelling this out is mainly a question of language, and does not contribute significantly to understanding or to the efficiency with which one may check the validity of arguments; thus we leave these details implicit.

4.1
Morse cohomology

We establish our conventions for Morse cohomology of the Morse–Smale pair
(f,g)
on
X
.
First, we require that there are unique critical points of index
0
and
2n
.
For each critical point
p
of
f
, we define
C_{-}(p)
(respectively
C_{+}(p)
) to be the descending (respectively ascending) manifold of
p
.
We define
\sigma(p):=\sigma(C_{-}(p))
, and for any coefficient (differential graded) ring
R
we define

CM^{*}(f):=\bigoplus_{p}\mathbb{Z}\otimes\sigma(p).

A Morse flowline from
p_{-}
to
p_{+}
is a solution of the equation

\displaystyle\gamma:\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\to X

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\nabla f

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\gamma(t)

\displaystyle=p_{\pm}.

A Morse flowline
\gamma
is isolated if
C_{-}(p_{+})
intersects
C_{+}(p_{-})
cleanly along
\gamma
; this determines a short exact sequence

0\to T\gamma\to TC_{-}(p_{+})\to NC_{+}(p_{-})\to 0.

Identifying
NC_{+}(p_{-})
with
TC_{-}(p_{-})
, we obtain an isomorphism

\sigma(d)\sigma(p_{-})\cong\sigma(p_{+}),

(21)

where
\sigma(d)=\sigma(1)
is identified with
\sigma(T\gamma)
, which we orient in the direction of increasing
t
.
The Morse differential

\displaystyle d_{f,g}:\sigma(d)CM^{*}(f)

\displaystyle\to CM^{*}(f)

is then the map whose matrix coefficients are the sums of the isomorphisms (
21
) over all Morse flowlines.
We define
QC^{*}(X;R^{big})=(CM^{*}(f),d_{f,g})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R^{big}
to be the Morse cohomology with coefficients in
R^{big}
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be the space of smooth vector fields on
X
, and denote by
\nabla f\in\mathcal{V}
the gradient vector field of
f
with respect to
g
.

To define the pairing on
QC^{*}(X;R^{big})
, we choose a smooth map
v:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{V}
such that
v(t)=\nabla f
for
t\leq-1
and
v(t)=-\nabla f
for
t\geq 1
.
For each pair
p_{\pm}
of critical points of
f
, we consider the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(\langle p_{-},p_{+}\rangle)
of solutions to the equation

\displaystyle\gamma:\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\to X

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=v(t)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\gamma(t)

\displaystyle=p_{\pm}.

For a generic choice of
v
, the components of this moduli space of virtual dimension
0
and
1
are regular.
An element of a
0
-dimensional moduli space determines an isomorphism

\sigma(p_{-})\sigma(p_{+})\cong\sigma(TX)\cong\sigma(2n),

and the sum of these over all solutions defines the pairing
\langle p_{-},p_{+}\rangle
.
Considering the
1
-dimensional moduli spaces shows that the pairing satisfies

\langle dp_{-},p_{+}\rangle+\langle p_{-},dp_{+}\rangle=0,

and hence descends to cohomology.
Note that we may not be able to arrange for the pairing to be symmetric on the chain level, due to issues with equivariant transversality; but it will be symmetric on the cohomology level.

.

We will not use the pairing
\langle-,-\rangle
in the present paper, but it will be used in the followup

, so we include it in the formalism.

We will also consider the moduli space of ‘rays’.
Let

be a smooth map with

for

.
Then for a critical point

\displaystyle\to X

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=v(t)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(t)

\displaystyle=p.

We have an isomorphism

\sigma(\mathcal{M}(ray,p))\cong\sigma(p)

if

for large

\sigma(\mathcal{M}(ray,p)\cong\sigma(2n)\sigma(p)^{\vee}

for large

There is an evaluation map

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}:\mathcal{M}(ray,p)

\displaystyle\to X

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}(\gamma)

By choosing

-stable family of domains
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F)
and each choice of Lagrangian labelling, satisfying:

(Consistency with disjoint union)
The perturbation data on
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F_{1}\coprod F_{2})
is the product of perturbation data for
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F_{1})
with that for
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F_{2})
;

(Consistency with attaching)
The perturbation data on a family

obtained by attachment from
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F)
, where attachment is only taken along boundary marked points, or rays where the length parameter is
\infty
, is induced by the perturbation data on
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F)
;

(Consistency with boundary strata)
The perturbation data on a boundary stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F)
coincides with the restriction of the perturbation data from
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F)
.

(Consistency with stabilization)
Where a nearly
f^{sym}
-stable family is obtained from an
f^{sym}
-stable one by stabilization, adding only symmetric stabilizing marked points, and such that all of the added marked points get sent to marked points in the stabilization process, the perturbation data on the nearly
f^{sym}
-stable family are obtained from that of the
f^{sym}
-stable one by pullback.

(Consistency with stabilization on
V
)
Where a nearly
f^{sym}
-stable family is obtained from an
f^{sym}
-stable one by stabilization, adding only stabilizing marked points, and such that only spheres get contracted in the stabilization process, the perturbation data on the nearly
f^{sym}
-stable family
restricted to
V
are obtained from that of the
f^{sym}
-stable one, restricted to
V
, by pullback.

Remark 4.6
.

Note that we do not impose
(Consistency with attaching)
at an attachment with length parameter
0
; this avoids a problem with self-transversality in the case of attaching a moduli space to itself.

Remark 4.7
.

Note that
(Consistency with stabilization)
implies that the perturbation data are constant when restricted to any
f^{sym}
-unstable sphere bubble. Note that this is consistent with
(Equivariance)
. This should be compared with the condition
(Constant on spheres)
in
[
PS23b
, Definition 5.4]
.

Remark 4.8
.

Note that we do not impose
(Consistency with stabilization)
for forgetful maps which send a stabilizing marked point to a non-marked point of a component with boundary; it would be impossible to satisfy such a condition. For example, suppose we had a forgetful map which simply forgot an interior stabilizing marked point of a component with boundary. Consider a family of domains with an
f^{sym}
-unstable sphere bubble attached at a point which converges to the boundary, and whose image under the forgetful map is non-marked. Then the family breaks along a strip-like end, along which the perturbation data is required to satisfy
(Thin 2d regions)
; but the pullback of the perturbation data will not satisfy this (instead it will be constant along the strip-like end).

4.7
Transversality

A universal choice of perturbation data is called
regular
if all moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}(F,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{y},A,\mathsf{tan},\{K_{\alpha}\},\{\Gamma_{p}\})
are regular. (We remark that a weaker notion of regularity was used in
[
PS23b
, Definition 5.8]
, in order to allow the proof of independence of the Fukaya category of the choice of system of divisors; we do not concern ourselves with this point here, and use instead this stronger and simpler notion of regularity.)

Lemma 4.12
.

There exists a regular universal choice of perturbation data.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as that of
[
PS23b
, Lemmas 5.9 and 5.10]
: regular perturbation data are constructed inductively in the dimension of the families of domains.
Suppose that regular perturbation data have been constructed for all nearly
f^{sym}
-stable families of dimension
\leq d-1
, and let
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F)
be a family of dimension
d
.
Suppose, to start with, that the family is
f^{sym}
-stable.

The perturbation data are then uniquely determined over the boundary strata of
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F)
as these have dimension
\leq d-1
.
They can be extended to a consistent choice over
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F)
which satisfies
(Admissibility)
by Lemma
A.11
, and this choice can be made to satisfy
(Equivariance)
by averaging (which preserves the conditions required of the perturbation data; in the case of
(Thin 2d regions)
, this is a consequence of the equivariance of our choices of cylindrical and strip-like ends).

Having shown that the space of perturbation data satisfying these conditions is non-empty, we must show that the regular choices form a comeagre subset.
The proof of this follows closely that of
[
PS23b
, Lemma 5.9]
, which itself is a routine combination of the arguments in
[
Sei08b
, Section 9k]
,
[
MS12
, Section 6.7]
,
[
CM07
, Section 6]
, and very similar to the arguments presented in
[
CW22
, Theorem 4.19]
and
[
CW17
, Theorem 4.1]
.
The main difference from
[
PS23b
]
is the presence of flowlines in our moduli spaces (which however are dealt with in
[
CW22
,
CW17
]
).
Incorporating these in the transversality argument is routine, given the following elementary observation: the universal evaluation map

\mathrm{ev}_{1}^{univ}:\mathcal{M}_{1}^{univ}\to X^{\partial C_{1}}

(24)

(i.e., the union of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
over all
r\in\mathcal{R}(F)
and all choices of perturbation data) is a submersion.
This follows as we may perturb the vector field arbitrarily in a neighbourhood of the endpoint of each finite or semi-infinite flowline: so we can ‘make the endpoint move in whichever direction we choose’ by modifying the vector field.

In fact, by the same argument, we may prove that the set of perturbation data satisfying a stronger regularity condition is still comeagre: namely, the condition that all nearly
f^{sym}
-stable moduli spaces obtained from
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(F)
by a stabilization of the type considered in the
(Consistency with stabilization)
condition are regular.
This covers the case which was excluded at the start of the inductive step.
∎

Appendix A
Stratified chains

In this section we develop the necessary theory of stratified spaces which we will use to define chains in our Deligne–Mumford moduli spaces, following
[
Pfl01
]
.

A.1
Decomposed spaces

A
decomposed space
is a paracompact Hausdorff topological space
\overline{B}
, equipped with a locally finite decomposition

\overline{B}=\coprod_{X\in S}X

into locally closed subspaces called
strata
, together with a structure of smooth manifold on each stratum
X
, such that the ‘condition of the frontier’ is satisfied: if
X\cap\overline{Y}\neq\emptyset
, then
X\subset\overline{Y}
.
A map of decomposed spaces is a continuous map which sends strata to strata by smooth maps.
There is a symmetric monoidal category of decomposed spaces, with the monoidal structure given by Cartesian product.
We denote it by
Dsp
.

The
dimension
of a decomposed space is the maximal dimension of a stratum.
The strata of maximal dimension are called
top strata
, and the rest are called
boundary strata
.
We will generally denote a decomposed space by
\overline{B}
, and the union of its top strata by
B
(this creates a notational ambiguity, as the closure of
B
need not be equal to
\overline{B}
; however this will not arise in practice, as
B
is dense in
\overline{B}
for all decomposed spaces we consider).

For any decomposed space
\overline{B}
, we will denote by
\overline{B}^{[k]}
the union of strata of dimension
\leq k
.

A.2
Control data

Definition A.1
.

A
tube
for a stratum
X
of a locally compact decomposed space
\overline{B}
is a triple
(T_{X},\pi_{X},\rho_{X})
where

•

T_{X}
is an open neighbourhood of
X
, called the
tubular neighbourhood
.

•

\pi_{X}:T_{X}\to X
is a continuous retraction, called the
local retraction
.

•

\rho_{X}:T_{X}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a continuous function such that
\rho_{X}^{-1}(0)=X
, called the
tubular function
.

These are required to satisfy

•

For any stratum
Y
,
T_{X}\cap Y\neq\emptyset\implies X\subset\overline{Y}
.

•

For any stratum
Y\neq X
,
(\pi_{X},\rho_{X}):T_{X}\cap Y\to X\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a smooth submersion.

Tubes
(T_{X},\pi_{X},\rho_{X})
and
(T^{\prime}_{X},\pi^{\prime}_{X},\rho^{\prime}_{X})
are called
equivalent
if there exists a neighbourhood
U\subset T_{X}\cap T^{\prime}_{X}
of
X
, such that
\pi_{X}|_{U}=\pi^{\prime}_{X}|_{U}
and
\rho_{X}|_{U}=\rho^{\prime}_{X}|_{U}
.

Definition A.2
.

A set of
control data
for a locally compact decomposed space
\overline{B}
is a choice of tube
(T_{X},\pi_{X},\rho_{X})
for each stratum
X
, such that

•

\pi_{X}\circ\pi_{Y}=\pi_{X}
whenever both sides are defined.

•

\rho_{X}\circ\pi_{Y}=\rho_{X}
whenever both sides are defined.

Two sets of control data are called
equivalent
if for every stratum, the corresponding tubes are equivalent.
An equivalence class of control data is called a
control structure
.

Remark A.3
.

A
Thom–Mather stratified space
is a locally compact decomposed space equipped with a control structure.
Thom–Mather stratified spaces are called
abstract stratified sets
in
[
Mat12
, Section 8]
and
controlled spaces
in
[
Pfl01
, Section 3.6.4]
.

Definition A.4
.

Given control data on decomposed spaces
\overline{B}
and
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
, the
product control data
are defined by
T_{X\times Y}=T_{X}\times T_{Y}
,
\pi_{X\times Y}=(\pi_{X},\pi_{Y})
, and
\rho_{X\times Y}=\rho_{X}+\rho_{Y}
.
One easily verifies that these are indeed control data.

A.3
Smooth pseudomanifolds with boundary

A
structure of smooth manifold with boundary
on a decomposed space
\overline{B}
is a structure of smooth manifold with boundary on the underlying topological space, so that each stratum is either a connected component of the interior or a connected component of the boundary (and the structures of smooth manifold agree).

We say that a stratum
X
of a decomposed space
\overline{B}
has
one-point link
if for any point
x\in X
there is a neighbourhood
U
of
x
in
\overline{B}
which is isomorphic, as a decomposed space, to
V\times[0,\epsilon)
for some
V\subset X
.
It is clear that, if a decomposed space admits a structure of smooth manifold with boundary, then its codimension-
1
strata have one-point links.

Let
\overline{B}
be a decomposed space equipped with a structure of smooth manifold with boundary.
A choice of control structure for
\overline{B}
is said to be
compatible
with the smooth manifold with boundary structure, if for every boundary stratum
X
, the map

Definition A.6
.

A

of a decomposed space

is a map of decomposed spaces

which is a homeomorphism.

such that the restriction of

In other words, it only ‘refines’ the strata of codimension

Definition A.7
.

A
d
-dimensional smooth pseudomanifold with boundary
is a
d
-dimensional decomposed space
\overline{B}
, together with a structure of smooth manifold with boundary on

A.4
Extension property

This section explains why we need the ‘control data on a codimension-
2
refinement’ part of the definition of a smooth pseudomanifold with boundary: it allows us to extend smooth functions from the boundary in the appropriate sense.
This extension property is used in the inductive construction of perturbation data.

Definition A.8
.

If
\overline{B}
is a decomposed space, then we will call a map
f:\overline{B}\to\mathbb{R}

smooth
if it is continuous, and its restriction to each stratum is smooth.

Now let us recall the notion of a smooth function on a smooth manifold with boundary.
First we recall that a
chart
for a smooth manifold with boundary
\overline{B}
is a pair
(U,\phi)
, where
U\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
is an open set and
\phi:U\to\overline{B}
is a homeomorphism onto its image; and a structure of smooth manifold with boundary consists of a maximal atlas of charts.
We say that a function
f:\overline{B}\to\mathbb{R}
is
smooth with respect to the structure of smooth manifold with boundary
if for every chart
(U,\phi)
belonging to the maximal atlas, the function
\phi^{*}f
extends to a smooth function on an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
containing
U
.

Remark A.9
.

If a function is smooth with respect to the structure of smooth manifold with boundary, then it is smooth on the underlying decomposed space (in the sense of Definition
A.8
), but the converse does not hold: the function
f:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
,
f(x)=\sqrt{x}
provides a counterexample.

Definition A.10
.

If
\overline{B}
is a
d
-dimensional smooth pseudomanifold with boundary, then we will call a map
f:\overline{B}\to\mathbb{R}

admissible
if it is smooth in the sense of Definition
A.8
, and furthermore
f|_{\overline{B}\setminus\overline{B}^{[d-2]}}
is smooth with respect to the structure of smooth manifold with boundary.

Let

In order to prove this result, we start by introducing some notation.

\displaystyle=\{(x,t)\in X\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}:t\leq\epsilon(x)\},

Lemma A.12

(c.f.
[
Pfl01
]
, Lemma 3.6.7)
.

Suppose that
\overline{B}
is a compact decomposed space equipped with a control structure.
Then there exist control data representing the control structure for
\overline{B}
, and continuous functions
\epsilon_{X}:X\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
associated to the strata

, satisfying the following conditions:

1.

T_{X}\cap T_{Y}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
X\subset\overline{Y}
or

.

2.

For all

is proper and surjective onto

3.

For all

.

4.

If

and

Proof.

Conditions (

) and (

) may be arranged by
[
Pfl01
, Lemma 3.6.7]
.
It remains to arrange conditions (

We establish them by induction on the dimension of stratum

X
of dimension
<k

be a stratum of dimension

We start by observing that for any

, the intersection
T_{X}\cap\overline{T}^{\epsilon_{Y}/4}_{Y}

Indeed,
Y\subset\overline{X}\implies\dim(Y)<\dim(X)
for a controllable decomposed space, so

\overline{B}
by the inductive assumption.
Since
\overline{T}^{\epsilon_{Y}/4}_{Y}
is relatively closed in
\overline{T}^{\epsilon_{Y}/2}_{Y}

\overline{Y}\cup\overline{T}^{\epsilon_{Y}/4}_{Y}

\overline{B}

Hence its intersection with

Because

It follows immediately that

We now define a relatively open cover

It is clear that

is open and

is assumed compact, and compact decomposed spaces have finitely many strata by the ‘local finiteness’ assumption.

Now for each

we define an open neighbourhood of

It is clear that

contains

The argument that

is open is the same as the argument that

was relatively open, except now we use the fact that

Since the

The key property of

(a)

\pi_{X}(x)\in T^{\epsilon_{Y}/4}_{Y}

(b)

if
\pi_{X}(x)\notin T_{Y}^{\epsilon_{Y}/2}

Indeed, to prove (

To prove (

then

, so

as required.

Now there exists a smooth map

such that

, by
[
Pfl01
, Lemma 3.1.2]
.
We claim that after replacing

with

, and
\epsilon_{X}
with

, conditions (
3
) and (

) are satisfied for

Condition (
4
) follows immediately from the contrapositive of (

We must show that

is closed in
\overline{B}
.
Let
k_{i}
be a sequence of points in
K_{X}
, converging to a point

; we must show that

.
As
\overline{X}
is closed, we may assume that
k_{i}\in\overline{T}^{\eta_{X}/2}_{X}
.
We observe that
\overline{X}
is compact, as it is closed in
\overline{B}
which is compact by assumption.
Therefore the sequence
\pi_{X}(k_{i})\in X
admits a subsequence converging to a point

. There are two cases:

(i)

x\in X
. We may take a further subsequence on which
\rho_{X}(k_{i})
converges to

.
Now let

be a compact neighbourhood of
(x,t)
.
By taking a further subsequence we may assume that
(\pi_{X},\rho_{X})(k_{i})\in N
for all

, so
k_{i}\in(\pi_{X},\rho_{X})^{-1}(N)
for all
i
.
Because
(\pi_{X},\rho_{X})
is proper, the preimage of

is compact, hence we must have

.
Since

is clearly closed in

, it follows that

as required.

(ii)

x\in Y
for some stratum

. By taking a subsequence we may assume that
\pi_{X}(k_{i})\in T^{\epsilon_{Y}/4}_{Y}
for all

; since

We have

Replacing

by

for all strata

) and (

.

Let

We choose a function

•

•

•

•

\tilde{f}

(b)

Proof of Lemma

.

A.5
Semianalytic sets

We now recall the notion of a
C^{k}
structure on a decomposed space from
[
Pfl01
, Section 1.3]
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty,\omega\}
(
C^{\infty}
structures are also called smooth structures, and
C^{\omega}
structures are also called analytic structures).
A
singular chart
for
\overline{B}
is a homeomorphism
\phi:U\to\phi(U)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
from an open subset of
\overline{B}
to a locally closed subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for any stratum
X
,
\phi|_{X\cap U}
defines a
C^{k}
diffeomorphism onto a
C^{k}
submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Singular charts
(U,\phi)
and
(U^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
are called
C^{k}
-compatible
if for every
b\in U\cap U^{\prime}
there exists neighbourhoods
U_{b}\subset U\cap U^{\prime}
of
b
,
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
of
\iota_{n}^{N}(\phi(U_{b}))
, and
O^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
of
\iota_{n^{\prime}}^{N}(\phi^{\prime}(U_{b}))
(where
\iota_{n}^{N}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the inclusion
x\mapsto(x,0)
), and a
C^{k}
diffeomorphism
h:O\to O^{\prime}
satisfying

h\circ\iota_{n}^{N}\circ\phi|_{U_{b}}=\iota_{n^{\prime}}^{N}\circ\phi^{\prime}|_{U_{b}}.

A
C^{k}
atlas
on a decomposed space is a cover by pairwise-
C^{k}
-compatible singular charts.
Two
C^{k}
atlases are called
compatible
if each chart in the first is
C^{k}
-compatible with each chart in the second.
Compatibility defines an equivalence relation on
C^{k}
atlases, and a
C^{k}
structure
is an equivalence class of
C^{k}
atlases.

A
C^{k}
map
between decomposed spaces equipped with
C^{k}
structures, is a map
f:\overline{B}\to\overline{\mathcal{C}}
of decomposed spaces such that for every point
b\in\overline{B}
there exist singular charts
(U,\phi)
containing
b
, and
(U^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
containing
f(b)
, open sets
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
\phi(U)
and
O^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
containing
\phi^{\prime}(U^{\prime})
, and a
C^{k}
map
g:O\to O^{\prime}
satisfying
g\circ\phi|_{U}=\phi^{\prime}\circ f|_{U}
.

We now recall that a subset
A
of an analytic manifold
M
is called
semianalytic
if
M
can be covered by open sets
U
such that
U\cap A

A.7
Why semi-analytic?

Figure 2:
Illustrating a smooth Whitney-stratified chain whose pullback under a forgetful map is not Whitney stratified; the empty marked point is the one which gets forgotten.

The reason we work with semianalytic chains is that this class has the following two properties:

•

they admit Whitney stratifications by Theorem
A.18
, and hence they have the extension property by Lemma
A.11
;

•

the pullback of a semianalytic chain by a forgetful map is still semianalytic, as forgetful maps are analytic.

The second property is in particular necessary so that the
(Stabilizing)
process described in Section
3.1
is well-defined.

One might wonder if one could instead work with the class of smooth Whitney stratified chains, as they clearly have the first property; however this class does not have the second property, as we now demonstrate using an example.

We equip the complex plane with the stratification
\mathbb{C}=\{0\}\sqcup\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Consider the stratified map

\displaystyle g:\mathbb{C}^{3}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}^{2},

\displaystyle g(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=(xy,z),

where
\mathbb{C}^{3}
and
\mathbb{C}^{2}
are equipped with the product stratifications.
Note that there exist forgetful maps locally modelled on
g
: for example, consider the forgetful map
\overline{\mathcal{R}}(bub,(5,0))\to\overline{\mathcal{R}}(bub,(4,0))
forgetting one of the marked points, in a neighbourhood of the boundary stratum illustrated in Figure
2
.
Now consider the stratified space
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}=\{0\}\sqcup\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, and the stratified map

\displaystyle f:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}^{2},

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle=(e^{-2t+it},e^{-t})\qquad\text{(and $f(0)=(0,0)$)}.

We claim that the pullback
P:=g^{-1}(\mathrm{im}(f))\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
does not satisfy Whitney’s condition (b).
Indeed, let us consider the sequence of points
p_{k}=(e^{-2k\pi},e^{-2k\pi},e^{-2k\pi})
lying in
P
, converging to the origin.
Clearly the line pointing from the origin to
p_{k}
is constant, equal to the line spanned by
(1,1,1)
, and in particular converges to this line.
On the other hand one may compute that the tangent plane to
P
at the points
p_{k}
is also constant, equal to the linear subspace

T_{p_{k}}P=\{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:u+v=(2-i)w,w\in\mathbb{R}\},

and in particular converges to this subspace.
However this subspace does not contain the line spanned by
(1,1,1)
, hence
P
does not satisfy Whitney’s condition (b).

B.2
Gluing and determinant line bundles

Given two Cauchy–Riemann operators, we may define their disjoint union, by taking the disjoint union of the underlying Riemann surfaces.
The behaviour of the torsor of orientations under disjoint union is clear: there is a natural isomorphism

\sigma\left(\bar{\partial}_{1}\coprod\bar{\partial}_{2}\right)\cong\sigma(\bar{\partial}_{1})\otimes\sigma(\bar{\partial}_{2}),

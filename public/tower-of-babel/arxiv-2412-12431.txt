Finite Dimensional Representations of Quivers with Oriented Cycles
K.R. Goodearl, B. Huisgen-Zimmermann

Abstract
. Let K K be a field, Q Q a quiver, and 𝒜 \mathcal{A} the ideal of the path algebra K ​ Q KQ that is generated by the arrows of Q Q . We present old and new results about the representation theories of the truncations K ​ Q / 𝒜 L KQ/\mathcal{A}^{L} , L ∈ ℕ L\in\mathbb{N} , tracking their development as L L goes to infinity. 1.
Introduction and notation

This is an overview of recent work on the finite dimensional representation theory of infinite dimensional path algebras
{\Lambda}=KQ
, i.e., of path algebras of quivers
Q
with oriented cycles. Throughout, the base field
K
is algebraically closed. Some of the results we present here concerning strong tilts have not yet been published, so this article also serves as a first announcement. We do not include proofs here, but illustrate the theory with numerous examples.

Due to Gabriel
[
14
]
, all basic finite dimensional algebras are quotients of path algebras, whence path algebras hold a foundational position within finite dimensional representation theory. As a consequence, they have been thoroughly explored over the past forty-five years. They are known to have multiple assets, one of them being
\gldim{\Lambda}\leq 1
, another being the role played by the Euler form of
Q
; indeed this form connects the dimensions of Ext-spaces of finite dimensional
{\Lambda}
-modules to those of their Hom-spaces. We refer to
[
2
,
4
,
9
,
10
]
for background. Several of these assets are restricted to path algebras of acyclic quivers, one of the exceptions being the geometry of the varieties
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}({\Lambda})
parametrizing the modules of fixed
dimension vector
{\mathbf{d}}
, and the generic decompositions of the modules they encode.

Asking for a full understanding of the category
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
of finite dimensional left
{\Lambda}
-modules is not an attainable goal; after all, the free algebras in any finite number of variables are among the path algebras. In a first step, we narrow the problem to targeting only those finite dimensional
{\Lambda}
-modules which are annihilated by some power of the ideal
\mathcal{A}:=\langle\text{all arrows of}\ Q\rangle\subseteq KQ
. We call these modules
admissible
, and focus on the full subcategory
\text{\bf adm}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
of
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
consisting of the admissible modules. In light of the fact that every basic finite dimensional
K
-algebra is isomorphic to a quotient
KQ/I
, where
I
is an admissible ideal of
KQ
and as such contains a power of
\mathcal{A}

[
14
, §4.3]
, the object class of
\text{\bf adm}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is the union of the classes of finitely generated left
\Delta
-modules, where
\Delta
traces all basic finite dimensional
K
-algebras with Gabriel quiver
Q
.

We tackle the category of admissible
{\Lambda}
-modules by first studying the finitely generated modules over the truncations

{\Lambda}_{L}:=KQ/\langle\text{all paths of length}\ L\rangle

for arbitrary positive integers
L
. That is, we explore
\text{\bf adm}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
along the chain of full subcategories

{\Lambda}_{1}\mbox{\rm-mod}\subseteq{\Lambda}_{2}\mbox{\rm-mod}\subseteq{\Lambda}_{3}\mbox{\rm-mod}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\text{\bf adm}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}),

the union of the object classes of the
\Lambda_{L}\mbox{\rm-mod}
being the class of admissible modules.
In a next step, we then trace the development of the resulting pictures as
L\rightarrow\infty
. Evidently, each truncation depends only on
Q
and
L
, and the category
\text{\bf adm}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
can be seen as a “direct limit” of the directed family of categories
({\Lambda}_{L}\text{-mod})_{L\in\mathbb{N}}
. As becomes clear in Sections 2 and 3, the individual
{\Lambda}_{L}\text{-mod}
retain several of the assets of
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
, which allow for effective tracking of their representation theories, relative to each other, as
L
grows.

More specifically: Via the family
\bigl({\Lambda}_{L}\bigr)_{L\in\mathbb{N}}
, we investigate the category
\text{\bf adm}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
from the following angles:

(A)

by way of the parametrizing varieties for its objects
;

(B)

through the lens of tilted algebras
;

(C)

in terms of representation type
.

Results regarding the first two points are at an advanced level, whereas those aimed at the third are still rudimentary. We include a short section on this topic at the end, in hopes of stimulating further work on the subject.

Under
(A)
we describe the irreducible components of the classical varieties
\rep_{{\mathbf{d}}}({\Lambda}_{L})
for any dimension vector
{\mathbf{d}}
and provide information on the generic behavior of the modules encoded by these components; in doing so, we modestly supplement previously available facts (cf.

]
). Recall that the modules

corresponding to the points

of

Our classification of the irreducible components of

, all containments

Under
(B)

Under

Irreducible components of the parametrizing varieties and their generic modules

Let
{\mathbf{d}}
be a dimension vector of

. In this section we focus on the subvarieties
\rep_{{\mathbf{d}}}({\Lambda}_{L})
of
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}({\Lambda})
for
L\in\mathbb{N}
.

Observation 2.1
.

\rep_{{\mathbf{d}}}({\Lambda})
is irreducible for any dimension vector
{\mathbf{d}}
, whereas the
\rep_{{\mathbf{d}}}({\Lambda}_{L})
for
L\geq 2
are reducible in general.

Indeed, the first claim is immediate from the fact that
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}({\Lambda})\cong\mathbb{A}^{N}
, where
N={\sum_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}d_{\text{start}(\alpha)}\cdot d_{\text{end}(\alpha)}}
. For the second consider, e.g., the quiver

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

and
{\mathbf{d}}=(1,1)
; for any
L\geq 2
, the variety
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}({\Lambda}_{L})
then has two irreducible components, one consisting of the uniserial module with top
S_{1}
next to the semisimple module
S_{1}\oplus S_{2}
, the other consisting of the uniserial module with top
S_{2}
next to
S_{1}\oplus S_{2}
. In fact, based on the oriented cycle with
L\geq 2
vertices and
L
arrows, and
{\mathbf{d}}=(1,1,\dots,1)
, one analogously obtains an example where
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}({\Lambda}_{L})
has precisely
L
irreducible components.

Thus, in a first step, the plan to explore generic properties of the representations parametrized by
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}({\Lambda}_{L})
calls for a useful listing of the irreducible components.

2.A. Preparing for the main results

The problem of understanding the irreducible components of
\rep_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\Delta)
for a finite dimensional algebra
\Delta
has been tackled intensely. We do not include an overview of the status quo, but restrict our attention to the case where
\Delta
is a (truncated) path algebra.

We begin with a few general comments. Suppose we already have an irreducible subset
U
of
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}(\Delta)
. Particularly useful tools for deciding whether the closure
\overline{U}
of
U
is a component come in the form of collections of upper or lower semicontinuous maps
f
from
\rep_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\Delta)
to partially ordered sets. In the case of upper semicontinuity, attainment of a minimal value of
f
on
U
indicates that
\overline{U}
is an irreducible component of
\rep_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\Delta)
, whereas attainment of a maximal value on
U
leads to the same conclusion if
f
is lower semicontinuous. Examples of lower semicontinuous maps on
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}(\Delta)
are path ranks, i.e., maps of the form
x\mapsto\dim pM_{x}
where
p
is a path in the quiver of
\Delta
; or the map that assigns to each
x\in\rep_{\mathbf{d}}(\Delta)
the dimension of the
\GL({\mathbf{d}})
-orbit of
x
. Instances of upper semicontinuous maps, our tool of preference in tackling
\Delta={\Lambda}_{L}
, are
\dim\Hom_{\Delta}(M_{x},N)
,
\dim\Hom_{\Delta}(N,M_{x})
,
\dim\Ext^{i}_{\Delta}(M_{x},N)
, and
\dim\Ext^{i}_{\Delta}(N,M_{x})
for
N\in\Delta\mbox{\rm-mod}
. However, the most effective towards the detection of components in our present setting are upper semicontinuous maps based on filtrations of the modules
M_{x}
, which we describe next.

Now assume that
\Delta
has Loewy length
\leq L
.
Our reference filtration of a module
M\in\Delta\mbox{\rm-mod}
is its
radical filtration
, namely the chain

M\supseteq JM\supseteq J^{2}M\supseteq\cdots\supseteq J^{L}M=0.

The family of successive quotients
\bigl(\mathbb{S}_{l}(M)\bigr)_{0\leq l<L}
, where
\mathbb{S}_{l}(M)=J^{l}M/J^{l+1}M
, is referred to as the
radical layering of
M
; clearly
\mathbb{S}(M)=(\mathbb{S}_{0}(M),\mathbb{S}_{1}(M),\dots,\mathbb{S}_{L-1}(M))
is then an
L
-term sequence of semisimple modules whose dimension vectors add up to

\udim\mathbb{S}(M):=\sum_{0\leq l\leq L-1}\udim\mathbb{S}_{l}(M)=\udim M.

Any sequence
\mathbb{S}=(\mathbb{S}_{0},\mathbb{S}_{1},\dots,\mathbb{S}_{L-1})
of semisimple modules with
\udim\mathbb{S}={\mathbf{d}}
is called a
semisimple sequence with dimension vector
{\mathbf{d}}
. We identify isomorphic semisimple modules, whence the set
\mathcal{S}({\mathbf{d}})
of all semisimple sequences with dimension vector
{\mathbf{d}}
is finite.

Another natural example of a semisimple sequence comes from the
socle filtration

\soc_{0}(M):=0\subseteq\soc_{1}(M):=\soc(M)\subseteq\soc_{2}(M)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\soc_{L}(M)=M,

which gives rise to the
socle layering

\mathbb{S}^{*}(M):=\bigl(\mathbb{S}^{*}_{l}(M)\bigr)_{0\leq l<L}
where

\mathbb{S}^{*}_{l}(M)=\soc_{l+1}(M)/\soc_{l}(M)=\soc\bigl(M/\soc_{l}(M)\bigr).

On equipping the set
\mathcal{S}({\mathbf{d}})
with the following dominance partial order, the assignments

x\longmapsto\mathbb{S}(M_{x})\qquad\text{and}\qquad x\longmapsto\mathbb{S}^{*}(M_{x})

yield upper semicontinuous maps
\rep_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\Delta)\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}({\mathbf{d}})
.
The
dominance order on
\mathcal{S}({\mathbf{d}})
is as follows:

\mathbb{S}\leq\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}\quad\iff\quad\udim\bigoplus_{0\leq l\leq m}\mathbb{S}_{l}\leq\udim\bigoplus_{0\leq l\leq m}\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{l}\quad\forall\ m\in\{0,\dots,L-1\},

where the partial order on dimension vectors is the componentwise order.

A prominent role is played by the semisimple sequences which are realizable as radical layerings:

Definition 2.2
.

A semisimple sequence
\mathbb{S}
is called
realizable
(with respect to
\Delta\mbox{\rm-mod}
) if
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{S}(M)
for some
\Delta
-module
M
.

Observation 2.3
.

Both of the maps
\rep_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\Delta)\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}({\mathbf{d}})
given by
x\mapsto\mathbb{S}(M_{x})
and
x\mapsto\mathbb{S}^{*}(M_{x})
are upper semicontinuous
[
20
, Observation 2.10]
. Hence so is the pairing
\Theta:\rep_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\Delta)\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}({\mathbf{d}})^{2}
defined by
\Theta(x)=\bigl(\mathbb{S}(M_{x}),\mathbb{S}^{*}(M_{x})\bigr)
.

In numerous special cases, the map
\Theta
detects all irreducible components of the varieties
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}(\Delta)
, meaning that for any irreducible component
\mathcal{C}
of
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}(\Delta)
, there is some
x\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\Theta(x)
is minimal in
\mbox{\rm Im}(\Theta)
. This holds, for instance, in the truncated local case, i.e., when
\Delta={\Lambda}_{L}
and
Q
consists of a single vertex with two or more loops
[
20
, Main Theorem]
. However, in general this map still has blind spots even for
\Delta={\Lambda}_{L}
(see, e.g.,
[
20
, Example 4.8]
).

On the other hand, there does exist an upper semicontinuous map which, in case
\Delta={\Lambda}_{L}
, sees
all
irreducible components of the varieties
\rep_{\mathbf{d}}(\Delta)
for arbitrary quivers
Q
. It turns out that the key to detecting all components in this case is provided by module filtrations which are governed by a realizable semisimple sequence in the following sense.

The tilting modules closest to projective generators are obviously the projective generators themselves. In particular, there is a unique basic one. As for a dual, the tilting modules “closest to injective cogenerators” were singled out and dubbed

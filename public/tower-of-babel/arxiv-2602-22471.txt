Abstract




Analogue of the theta group
\Gamma_{\theta}

By Kazuhide Matsuda

Abstract

In this paper, we introduce higher level versions of the theta group
\Gamma_{\theta}.

In particular, we treat level 3 and 4 versions of the theta group,
\Gamma_{\theta,3}
and
\Gamma_{\theta,4}

and
prove that

\displaystyle F(\tau)=\eta\left(\frac{\tau-1}{3}\right)\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{3}\right)

and

\displaystyle G(\tau)=\eta\left(\frac{\tau-1}{4}\right)\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{4}\right)

are modular forms on
\Gamma_{\theta,3}
and
\Gamma_{\theta,4}
respectively.
Moreover
we compute their multiplier systems,
\nu_{F}
and
\nu_{G}
.

Key Words:
eta function; theta constant; multiplier system

Introduction

Throughout this paper,
we adopt the definitions and the notations of modular groups and modular forms by Knopp
[
1
]
.
We define

\Gamma(1)=SL(2,\mathbb{Z})\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\Gamma_{\theta}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(1)\,\Big|\,a\equiv d\bmod{2}\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,b\equiv c\bmod{2}\right\}.

In particular,

\Gamma_{\theta}
is called the
theta group
.

In this paper, we propose higher level version of the theta group
\Gamma_{\theta}
in the following way:

\Gamma_{\theta,N}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(1)\,\Big|\,a\equiv d\bmod{N}\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,b\equiv-c\bmod{N}\right\},\,\,(N=1,2,3,\ldots).

The
upper half plane

\mathscr{H}
is defined by

\mathscr{H}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,\Im\tau>0\}.

Moreover, set
q=\exp(2\pi i\tau)
and define

\displaystyle\eta(\tau)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\theta(\tau)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\pi in^{2}\tau}.

A
modular group

\Gamma
is a subgroup of finite index in
\Gamma(1).

A
modular form
of weight
r
is a function
F(\tau),

defined and meromorphic in
\mathscr{H},

which satisfies

F(M\tau)=\nu(M)(c\tau+b)^{r}F(\tau),\,\,\tau\in\mathscr{H},

for all

M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma.

Here
\nu(M)
is a complex number, independent of
\tau,
such that
\,|\,\nu(M)\,|\,=1
for all
M\in\Gamma.

The function
\nu
is called a
multiplier system
.
We note that
\nu
is a character if the weight
r
is a rational integer.

It is well known that

\eta(\tau)
is a modular form of weight
\displaystyle 1/2
on
\Gamma(1)
and

\theta(\tau)
is a modular form of weight
\displaystyle 1/2
on
\Gamma_{\theta}.

In
[
2
]
,
from the multiplier systems,
\nu_{\eta},\nu_{\theta},
of
\eta(\tau)
and
\theta(\tau)
,
we obtain manu examples of modular groups.

The aim of this paper
is to
construct modular forms on
\Gamma_{\theta,3}
and
\Gamma_{\theta,4}
and compute their multiplier systems.
Section
2

describes the multiplier system
\nu_{\eta}
of
\eta(\tau)
.
Section
3

treats

\displaystyle F(\tau)=\eta\left(\frac{\tau-1}{3}\right)\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{3}\right)

and its multiplier system
\nu_{F}.

Section
4

deals with

\displaystyle G(\tau)=\eta\left(\frac{\tau-1}{4}\right)\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{4}\right)

and its multiplier system
\nu_{G}.

Moreover by considering the kernels of
\nu_{F}
and
\nu_{G},

we discover many examples of modular groups.
Section
5

considers
coset decompositions of
\Gamma(1)
modulo
\Gamma_{\theta,3}
and
\Gamma_{\theta,4}.

\nu_{\eta}(M)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{c}\right)^{*}\exp\left\{\frac{\pi i}{12}[(a+d)c-bd(c^{2}-1)-3c]\right\}&\text{if $c$ is odd,}\\

(2.1)

with

\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)^{*}=\left(\frac{c}{|d|}\right)\mathrm{and}\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)_{*}=\left(\frac{c}{|d|}\right)(-1)^{\frac{\operatorname{sign}c-1}{2}\frac{\operatorname{sign}d-1}{2}}.

3
Modular form on
\Gamma_{\theta,3}

We first note

\Gamma_{\theta,3}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(1)\,\Big|\,\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\equiv\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\pm\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\bmod{3}\right\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

For every
\tau\in\mathscr{H},

set

F(\tau)=\eta\left(\frac{\tau-1}{3}\right)\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{3}\right).

The multiplier system
\nu_{F}
is a character.

Proof.

Set

M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{\theta,3}.

We note that
\nu_{F}
is a character if the weight
r
is a rational integer.

We first suppose that

M\equiv\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\bmod{3},

which implies that

\displaystyle F(M\tau)=

\displaystyle\eta\left(\frac{M\tau-1}{3}\right)\eta\left(\frac{M\tau+1}{3}\right)=\eta\left(M_{1}\left(\frac{\tau-1}{3}\right)\right)\eta\left(M_{2}\left(\frac{\tau+1}{3}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{\eta}(M_{1})\left(3c\cdot\frac{\tau-1}{3}+(d+c)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\left(\frac{\tau-1}{3}\right)\nu_{\eta}(M_{2})\left(3c\cdot\frac{\tau+1}{3}+(d-c)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{3}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{\eta}(M_{1})\nu_{\eta}(M_{2})(c\tau+d)F(\tau),

where

M_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a-c&\frac{b-d+a-c}{3}\\
3c&d+c\end{pmatrix},M_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}a+c&\frac{b+d-a-c}{3}\\
3c&d-c\end{pmatrix}.

We next assume that

M\equiv\pm\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\bmod{3},

which implies that

\displaystyle F(M\tau)=

\displaystyle\eta\left(\frac{M\tau-1}{3}\right)\eta\left(\frac{M\tau+1}{3}\right)=\eta\left(M_{1}\left(\frac{\tau+1}{3}\right)\right)\eta\left(M_{2}\left(\frac{\tau-1}{3}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{\eta}(M_{1})\left(3c\cdot\frac{\tau+1}{3}+(d-c)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{3}\right)\nu_{\eta}(M_{2})\left(3c\cdot\frac{\tau-1}{3}+(d+c)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\left(\frac{\tau-1}{3}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{\eta}(M_{1})\nu_{\eta}(M_{2})(c\tau+d)F(\tau),

where

M_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a-c&\frac{b-d-a+c}{3}\\
3c&d-c\end{pmatrix},M_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}a+c&\frac{b+d+a+c}{3}\\
3c&d+c\end{pmatrix}.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that

M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{\theta,3}

and

M\equiv\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\

\nu_{F}
of

\displaystyle F=\eta\left(\frac{\tau-1}{3}\right)\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{3}\right)

\nu_{F}(M)=\exp\left\{\frac{\pi i}{6}\left[3c+\frac{1}{3}(a+d)c+\frac{4}{3}bd\right]\right\}.

Proof.

We first note that

\displaystyle F(M\tau)=

\displaystyle\nu_{\eta}(M_{1})\nu_{\eta}(M_{2})(c\tau+d)F(\tau),

where

M_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a-c&\frac{b-d+a-c}{3}\\
3c&d+c\end{pmatrix},M_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}a+c&\frac{b+d-a-c}{3}\\
3c&d-c\end{pmatrix}.

Equation (
2.1
)
yields

\displaystyle\nu_{\eta}(M_{1})=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d+c}{3c}\right)^{*}\exp\Big\{\frac{\pi i}{12}\Big[-3ac^{3}-3ac^{2}d-3bc^{3}-3bc^{2}d+3c^{4}+6c^{3}d+3c^{2}d^{2}-9c

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\frac{10ac}{3}+\frac{ad}{3}+\frac{bc}{3}+\frac{bd}{3}-\frac{c^{2}}{3}+\frac{7cd}{3}-\frac{d^{2}}{3}\Big]\Big\},

and

\displaystyle\nu_{\eta}(M_{2})=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d-c}{3c}\right)^{*}\exp\Big\{\frac{\pi i}{12}\Big[-3ac^{3}+3ac^{2}d+3bc^{3}-3bc^{2}d-3c^{4}+6c^{3}d-3c^{2}d^{2}-9c

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\frac{10ac}{3}-\frac{ad}{3}-\frac{bc}{3}+\frac{bd}{3}+\frac{c^{2}}{3}+\frac{7cd}{3}+\frac{d^{2}}{3}\Big]\Big\},

which implies that

\nu_{F}=\nu_{\eta}(M_{1})\nu_{\eta}(M_{2})=\left(\frac{d+c}{3c}\right)^{*}\left(\frac{d-c}{3c}\right)^{*}\exp\left\{\frac{\pi i}{12}E\right\},

where

E=-18c-6ac^{3}+\frac{20ac}{3}-6bc^{2}d+\frac{2bd}{3}+12c^{3}d+\frac{14cd}{3}.

Since
c\equiv 0\bmod{3},
it follows that

\left(\frac{d+c}{3c}\right)^{*}\left(\frac{d-c}{3c}\right)^{*}=\left(\frac{d+c}{3|c|}\right)\left(\frac{d-c}{3|c|}\right)=\left(\frac{d^{2}-c^{2}}{3|c|}\right)=\left(\frac{d^{2}}{3|c|}\right)=1.

Since
c
is odd,
it follows that

c^{2}\equiv 1\bmod{8},

which implies that

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle-18c-6ac^{3}+\frac{20ac}{3}-6bc^{2}d+\frac{2bd}{3}+12c^{3}d+\frac{14cd}{3}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,6c-6ac+\frac{20}{3}ac-6bd+\frac{2}{3}bd+12cd+\frac{14}{3}cd

\displaystyle\bmod{24}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,6c+\frac{2}{3}ac-\frac{16}{3}bd-12cd++\frac{14}{3}cd

\displaystyle\bmod{24}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,6c+\frac{2}{3}ac-\frac{16}{3}bd-\frac{22}{3}cd

\displaystyle\bmod{24}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 6c+\frac{2}{3}ac+\frac{8}{3}bd+\frac{2}{3}cd\equiv 2\left[3c+\frac{1}{3}(a+d)c+\frac{4}{3}bd\right]

\displaystyle\bmod{24},

which proves the theorem.
∎

\displaystyle\bmod{24},

.

\displaystyle(b-c)d+ac-cd+cd-acd^{2}\equiv 0\bmod{3}

Abstract
. It is an important fact that extremal discrete structures—that is, discrete structures of maximal size among those that avoid certain configurations—exhibit strong pseudorandom behavior. We present instances of this phenomenon in the context of graph-codes , a notion put forth recently by Alon, as well as on related problems related to density polynomial Hales–Jewett conjecture.

For every positive integer

1.2.
Gowers uniformity norms, and pseudorandomness

Pseudorandomness refers to the phenomenon that certain deterministic (and explicit) discrete structures behave like random ones for most practical purposes. The phenomenon was first discovered in the context of graphs by Chung–Graham–Wilson
[
CGW88
,
CGW89
]
who build upon previous work of Thomason
[
Tho87
]
. The last thirty years, pseudorandomness has become one of the central themes of extremal and probabilistic combinatorics, and it has found numerous applications in number theory and theoretical computer science; see, e.g.,
[
Rődl15
]
.

Much of the modern theory of pseudorandomness is developed using the uniformity norms introduced by Gowers
[
Gow01
]
. We recall their definition in characteristic two, and refer the reader to
[
TV06
]
for a more comprehensive treatment.

Definition 1.9

(Uniformity norms in characteristic two)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a nonempty finite set, and let
d\geqslant 2
be an integer. The
(Gowers) uniformity norm
\|\cdot\|_{U_{d}}
of order
d
is defined by setting, for any
f\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{I}}\to\mathbb{C}
,

(1.7)

\|f\|_{U_{d}}:=\bigg|\underset{x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{I}}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\prod_{s\subseteq[d]}\conj^{|s|}f\Big(x+\sum_{i\in s}y_{i}\Big)\bigg]\bigg|^{\frac{1}{2^{d}}},

where
\conj
denotes complex conjugation, that is,
\conj z:=\bar{z}
.

It is well-known—see
[
TV06
, (11.7)]
—that
\langle\|\cdot\|_{U_{d}}\colon d\geqslant 2\rangle
is an increasing family of norms. The
U_{2}
-norm has a spectral interpretation that makes it amenable to discrete Fourier analysis (see Fact
3.2
). The
U_{3}
-norm is somewhat more involved, but it is still rather easy to grasp, and quantitatively effective to work with, thanks to the recent resolution of Marton’s conjecture; see, in particular,
[
GGMT25
, Corollary 1.6]
. However, for
d\geqslant 4
, the behavior of the
U_{d}
-norm is significantly more complicated and it is understood via the deep work of Tao–Ziegler
[
TZ12
]
on the inverse theorem for the
U_{d}
-norms (see Theorem
4.3
).

The link between pseudorandomness and the Gowers uniformity norms is provided by the notion of uniformity: a function
f\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{I}}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to
\varepsilon
-uniform of order
d
, where
\varepsilon
is a (small) positive parameter, or simply
d
-uniform
, if
\big\|f-\mathbb{E}[f]\big\|_{U_{d}}\leqslant\varepsilon
; in particular, a set
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{I}}
is
d
-uniform if
\big\|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{G}}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big\|_{U_{d}}=o(1)
. As
d
becomes larger,
d
-uniformity becomes a stronger and substantially more informative property; we refer to
[
HHL19
]
for an overview of these pseurandomness properties and their applications in theoretical computer science.

1.3.
Main results

As already noted, the goal of this paper is to show the pseudorandomness of graph-codes that are extremal in the sense of the following definition.

Definition 1.10

(Extremal graph-codes)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a collection of nonempty graphs, and let

be an integer. We set

(1.8)

\displaystyle\delta_{n}(\mathcal{H}):=\max\bigg\{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\colon\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{2}}\text{ is an $\mathcal{H}$-code}\bigg\},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mathcal{H}):=\max\bigg\{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\colon\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{2}}\text{ is an $\mathcal{H}$-$\mathrm{HJ}$-code}\bigg\};

observe that
\delta_{n}(\mathcal{H})\leqslant\Delta_{n}(\mathcal{H})
. We say that an
\mathcal{H}
-code
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{2}}
is

if
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]=\delta_{n}(\mathcal{H})
. Respectively, we say that an
\mathcal{H}
-
\mathrm{HJ}
-code
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{2}}
is
extremal
if
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]=\Delta_{n}(\mathcal{H})
.

We have the following versions of these invariants for graphs with loops,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\delta^{\circ}_{n}(\mathcal{H}):=\max\bigg\{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\colon\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}\text{ is an $\mathcal{H}$-code}\bigg\},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\circ}_{n}(\mathcal{H}):=\max\bigg\{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\colon\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}\text{ is an $\mathcal{H}$-$\mathrm{HJ}$-code}\bigg\};

again, note that
\delta^{\circ}_{n}(\mathcal{H})\leqslant\Delta^{\circ}_{n}(\mathcal{H})
. We say that an
\mathcal{H}
-code
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}
is
extremal
if
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]=\delta^{\circ}_{n}(\mathcal{H})
. Finally, we say that an
\mathcal{H}
-
\mathrm{HJ}
-code
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}
is
extremal
if
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]=\Delta^{\circ}_{n}(\mathcal{H})
.

Theorem 1.12

.

Let
\mathcal{H}

2.1.

We start with the model

Fix a positive integer

\bullet

Notice that the image of

is a vector subspace of

Next, given two positive integers

\bullet

\dim(e)
of

to be the positive integer

;

\bullet

the

\mathrm{supp}(e)
of

to be the set

\bullet

the

\mathrm{const}(e)
of

Note that the dimension, the support and the constant part of

Definition 2.1

(Central subspace)
.

A

3.1.2.

Recall that linear functionals

(P1)

(P2)

Definition 3.1

(Fourier transform)

(Fourier inversion formula)

We also note that we shall consider complex function on

The following lemma is the last ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1.14
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
m,d
be positive integers, and let
\delta>0
. Also let
n\geqslant n_{2}(2^{-d-5}\delta^{2},m,d)
be an integer, where
n_{2}(2^{-d-5}\delta^{2},m,d)
is as in Theorem
5.1
, and assume that
\Delta_{m}^{\circ}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})-\Delta_{n}^{\circ}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})<\frac{\delta}{8}
. If
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}

is a
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
-
\mathrm{HJ}
-code with
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]=\Delta_{n}^{\circ}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})
—that is, the code
\mathcal{G}
is extremal in the sense of Definition
1.10
—then, for any nonclassical polynomial
P\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}\to\mathbb{T}
of degree at
most
d
,

(6.2)

\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{G}}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big)\,\exp(2\pi iP)\Big]\Big|<\delta.

Proof.

Assume not, and fix an extremal
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
-
\mathrm{HJ}
-code
\mathcal{G}
and a nonclassical polynomial
P
of degree at most
d
such that

(6.3)

\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{G}}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big)\,\exp(2\pi iP)\Big]\Big|\geqslant\delta.

Set

(6.4)

\eta:=\frac{\delta^{2}}{2^{d+5}}.

Since
n\geqslant n_{2}(\eta,m,d)
, by Theorem
5.1
, there is a collection
\mathcal{V}
of pairwise disjoint
m\text{-dimensional}
block
\mathrm{HJ}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}
such that

(i)

\mathbb{P}\Big[\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}\setminus(\cup\mathcal{V})\Big]\leqslant\eta
, and

(ii)

for every
V\in\mathcal{V}
, the polynomial
P
is constant on
V
.

Since
P
is a nonclassical polynomial of degree at most
d
, there exists a positive integer
\ell\leqslant 2^{d}
such that
the image of
P
has cardinality
\ell
; hence, the image of
\exp(2\pi iP)
also has cardinality
\ell
. Write the image of
\exp(2\pi iP)
as
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{\ell}\}
. For every
i\in[\ell]
, set

(6.5)

\Gamma_{i}:=\big(\exp(2\pi iP)\big)^{-1}(\{z_{i}\}),\ \ \ \mu_{i}:=\mathbb{P}[\Gamma_{i}]\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \lambda_{i}:=\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{G}\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i}\big].

Also set

(6.6)

I^{+}:=\big\{i\in[\ell]\colon\lambda_{i}\geqslant\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big\}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ I^{-}:=\big\{i\in[\ell]\colon\lambda_{i}<\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big\},

and observe that

(6.7)

0=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\big(\lambda_{i}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big)\mu_{i}=\sum_{i\in I^{+}}\big(\lambda_{i}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big)\mu_{i}+\sum_{i\in I^{-}}\big(\lambda_{i}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big)\mu_{i}.

Using the fact that the modulus of each
z_{i}
is one, we see that

(6.8)

\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq4.01*}}}{{\leqslant}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\big(\lambda_{i}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big)\mu_{i}z_{i}\Big|\leqslant\sum_{i\in I^{+}}\big(\lambda_{i}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}])\big)\mu_{i}-\sum_{i\in I^{-}}\big(\lambda_{i}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big)\mu_{i}.

By (
6.7
) and (
6.8
), we obtain that
\sum_{i\in I^{+}}\big(\lambda_{i}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big)\mu_{i}\geqslant\frac{\delta}{2}
. Therefore, using the fact that
\sum_{i\in I^{+}}\mu_{i}\leqslant 1
and
0\leqslant\lambda_{i}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\leqslant 1
for every
i\in I^{+}
, we conclude that

(6.9)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I^{+}\\
\lambda_{i}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\geqslant\frac{\delta}{4}\end{subarray}}\mu_{i}\geqslant\frac{\delta}{4}.

Since
|I^{+}|\leqslant\ell\leqslant 2^{d}
, there exists
i_{0}\in I^{+}
such that

(6.10)

\mathbb{P}[\Gamma_{i_{0}}]=\mu_{i_{0}}\geqslant\frac{\delta}{2^{d+2}}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{G}\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i_{0}}\big]=\lambda_{i_{0}}\geqslant\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]+\frac{\delta}{4}.

Hence,

(6.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]+\frac{\delta}{4}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{G}\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i_{0}}\cap(\cup\mathcal{V})\big]\,\mathbb{P}\big[\Gamma_{i_{0}}\cap(\cup\mathcal{V})\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i_{0}}\big]+\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{G}\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i_{0}}\setminus(\cup\mathcal{V})\big]\,\mathbb{P}\big[\Gamma_{i_{0}}\setminus(\cup\mathcal{V})\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i_{0}}\big]

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{G}\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i_{0}}\cap(\cup\mathcal{V})\big]+\mathbb{P}\big[\Gamma_{i_{0}}\setminus(\cup\mathcal{V})\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i_{0}}\big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\hyperref@@ii[part-i]{\mathrm{i}}),\eqref{e6.10}}}{{\leqslant}}\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{G}\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i_{0}}\cap(\cup\mathcal{V})\big]+\frac{\eta}{\mu_{i_{0}}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq4.02*}}}{{\leqslant}}\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{G}\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i_{0}}\cap(\cup\mathcal{V})\big]+\frac{\delta}{8},

that further implies that

(6.12)

\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{G}\,\big|\,\Gamma_{i_{0}}\cap(\cup\mathcal{V})\big]\geqslant\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]+\frac{\delta}{8}.

Recall that
\mathcal{V}
consists of pairwise disjoint subspaces of equal size and that
\exp(2\pi iP)
is constant on each
V\in\mathcal{V}
; thus, by (
6.12
), there exists
V\in\mathcal{V}
such that

(6.13)

\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{G}\,\big|\,V\big]\geqslant\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]+\frac{\delta}{8}=\Delta_{n}^{\circ}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})+\frac{\delta}{8}.

But
V
is an
m
-dimensional (block)
\mathrm{HJ}
-subspace and so, by Lemma
6.1
,
\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{G}\,\big|\,V]\leqslant\Delta^{\circ}_{m}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})
. Thus, by (
6.13
), we obtain that
\Delta^{\circ}_{m}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})-\Delta^{\circ}_{n}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})\geqslant\frac{\delta}{8}
which contradicts the assumptions of the lemma. The proof is thus completed.
∎

We are ready to complete the proof of Theorem
1.14
.

Completion of the proof of Theorem

As we have already pointed out, by Fact

, the sequence
\big(\Delta_{n}^{\circ}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})\big)

Remark 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a property of subsets of sets of the form
{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}}
, where
n\geqslant 2
is an integer. We say that the property
\mathcal{P}
is
\mathrm{HJ}
-hereditary
if it is preserved under taking preimages by
\mathrm{HJ}
-embeddings (see Subsection
2.2
). For instance, the property of being a
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
-
\mathrm{HJ}
-code and the property of being a
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
-code are both
\mathrm{HJ}
-hereditary. If
\mathcal{P}
is a
\mathrm{HJ}
-hereditary property and
n\geqslant 2
is an integer, then we say that a subset
\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}}
is
\mathcal{P}
-extremal
if it satisfies
\mathcal{P}
and has maximum density among subsets of
{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}}
satisfying
\mathcal{P}
. The proof of Theorem
1.14
immediately generalizes to show that
\mathcal{P}
-extremal sets are higher order uniform in the following sense: for every integer
d\geqslant 2
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive integer
n_{0}=n_{0}(d,\varepsilon)
such that, for all
n\geqslant n_{0}
, if
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}
is a
\mathcal{P}
-extremal, then

(6.14)

\big\|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{G}}-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\big\|_{U_{d}}\leqslant\varepsilon.

In particular, extremal
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
-codes are also higher order uniform. We also note that our approach extends to spaces of the form
{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{n}{\leqslant k}}}
, where
k\geqslant 2
is any fixed integer.

Appendix A

We start by recalling some definitions related to the polynomial Hales–Jewett theorem
[
BL99
]
. For every triple
n,d,k
of positive integers with
k\geqslant 2
, and let
W(n,d,k)
denote the set of all maps
w\colon[n]^{d}\to[k]
; we shall refer to the elements of
W(n,d,k)
as
polynomial words over
[k]
. A
polynomial variable word
v
of
W(n,d,k)
is a map
v\colon[n]^{d}\to[k]\cup\{x\}
, where
x
is a symbol not belonging to
[k]
, such that
v^{-1}(\{x\})=X^{d}
for some nonempty subset
X
of
[n]
. Given a polynomial variable word
v
of
W(n,d,k)
and
a\in[k]
, let
v(a)
denote the element
W(n,d,k)
obtained by replacing every occurrence of the symbol
x
in
v
by
a
. A
polynomial combinatorial line of
W(n,d,k)
is a set of the form

\big\{v(a)\colon a\in[k]\big\}
, where
v
is a polynomial variable word of
W(n,d,k)
. We are ready to recall the density polynomial Hales–Jewett conjecture.

Density polynomial Hales–Jewett conjecture

(Bergelson
[
Ber96
]
)
.

For every pair
d,k
of positive integers with
k\geqslant 2
and every
0<\delta\leqslant 1
, there exists a positive integer
\mathrm{DPHJ}(d,k,\delta)
such that if
n\geqslant\mathrm{DPHJ}(d,k,\delta)
, then any subset of
W(n,d,k)
with cardinality at least
\delta\,k^{n^{d}}
contains a polynomial combinatorial line of
W(n,d,k)
.

The case “
d=1
” of the above conjecture is the famous density Hales–Jewett theorem due to Furstenberg–Katznelson
[
FK91
]
.
However, even the first higher-dimensional case, “
d=k=2
”, is open and it is considered a major problem in density Ramsey theory
[
Alon24
,
Gow09
,
DK16
]
.

Our goal in this appendix is to show that the first unknown case of the density Polynomial Hales–Jewett conjecture, “
d=k=2
”, is equivalent to an affirmative answer to Problem
1.8
. Specifically, we have the following proposition.

Proposition A.1
.

The following are equivalent.

(i)

For any
0<\delta\leqslant 1
, the positive integer
\mathrm{DPHJ}(2,2,\delta)
exists.

(ii)

Problem
1.8
has an affirmative answer; that is, if
\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
-
\mathrm{HJ}
-code, then we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]=o_{n\to\infty}(1)
.

Proof.

We first argue for the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii); so, assume that, for any
0<\delta\leqslant 1
, the positive integer
\mathrm{DPHJ}(2,2,\delta)
exists. It enough to show that, for any
0<\delta\leqslant 1
and any integer
n\geqslant\mathrm{DPHJ}(2,2,\delta)
, if
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}}
satisfies
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\geqslant\delta
, then
\mathcal{G}
is not a
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
-
\mathrm{HJ}
-code. Indeed, set

(A.1)

D:=\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{[n]^{2}}\colon\Big\langle x(\min e,\max e)\colon e\in\binom{[n]}{\leqslant 2}\Big\rangle\in\mathcal{G}\bigg\}.

Observe that
|D|\geqslant\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]\cdot 2^{[n]^{2}}\geqslant\delta\,2^{n^{2}}
; also notice that
D
contains a polynomial combinatorial line if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is not a
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
-
\mathrm{HJ}
-code. Since
n\geqslant\mathrm{DPHJ}(2,2,\delta)
, it follows that
\mathcal{G}
is not a
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
-
\mathrm{HJ}
-code, as desired.

We proceed to show that (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Let
\big(\Delta_{n}^{\circ}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})\big)
be the sequence defined in (
1.11
), and note that our assumption in this case is equivalent to saying that the sequence
\big(\Delta_{n}^{\circ}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})\big)
converges to zero. We will need a “conditional concentration” estimate that originates from
[
DKT16
]
; the version stated below is taken from
[
DTV23
, Lemma 8.1]
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 1
, and let
\ell,m
be positive integers such that

(A.2)

\ell\geqslant\frac{2^{m+1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

Let
I
be a nonempty finite set, and let
\mathbb{P}
denote the uniform probability measure on
\{0,1\}^{I}
. Also let
D_{1},\dots,D_{\ell}
be pairwise disjoint nonempty subsets of
I
each with at most
m
elements. If
A
is any subset of
\{0,1\}^{I}
, then there exists
i_{0}\in[\ell]
such that, for every
x\in\{0,1\}^{D_{i_{0}}}
, setting
S_{x}:=\big\{y\cup x\colon y\in\{0,1\}^{I\setminus D_{i_{0}}}\big\}
, we have

(A.3)

\big|\mathbb{P}\big[A\,\big|\,S_{x}\big]-\mathbb{P}[A]\big|\leqslant\varepsilon.

Now assume, towards a contradiction, that part (i) does not hold true, that is, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for every positive integer
n_{0}
, there exist an integer
n\geqslant n_{0}
and a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{[n]^{2}}
with

\mathbb{P}[A]\geqslant\delta
such that
A
contains no combinatorial line.

Let
n_{1}
be an arbitrary positive integer, and set

(A.4)

m:=n_{1}^{2},\ \ \ \ell:=\frac{2^{m+1}}{(\delta/2)^{2}}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ n_{0}:=n_{1}\ell;

moreover, for every
i\in[\ell]
, set

(A.5)

I_{i}:=\big\{(i-1)n_{1}+1,\dots,in_{1}\big\}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ D_{i}:=I_{i}\times I_{i}.

By our assumption that part (i) does not hold true, we may select an integer
n\geqslant n_{0}
and a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{[n]^{2}}
with
\mathbb{P}[A]\geqslant\delta
that contains no polynomial combinatorial line. By Lemma
A.2
, there exists
i_{0}\in[\ell]
such that, for every
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{D_{i_{0}}}
,

(A.6)

\mathbb{P}\big[A\,\big|\,S_{x}\big]\geqslant\frac{\delta}{2},

where
S_{x}=\Big\{y\cup x\colon y\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{I\setminus D_{i_{0}}}\Big\}
. Write
I_{i_{0}}
in increasing order as
\{k_{1}<\cdots<k_{n_{1}}\}
, and set

(A.7)

\mathcal{S}:=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{D_{i_{0}}}\colon x(k_{i},k_{j})=x(k_{j},k_{i})\text{ for all }i,j\in[n_{1}]\Big\}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \mathcal{Y}:=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{[n]^{2}\setminus D_{i_{0}}}.

Moreover, setting
V^{\mathcal{S}}_{y}:=\big\{y\cup x\colon x\in\mathcal{S}\big\}
for every
y\in\mathcal{Y}
, by (
A.6
), we have

(A.8)

\frac{d}{2}\leqslant\underset{x\in\mathcal{S}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\mathbb{P}\big[A\,\big|\,S_{x}\big]\Big]=\underset{y\in\mathcal{Y}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\mathbb{P}\big[A\,\big|\,V^{\mathcal{S}}_{y}\big]\Big].

Thus, there exists
y_{0}\in\mathcal{Y}
such that
\mathbb{P}\big[A\,\big|\,V_{y_{0}}^{\mathcal{S}}\big]\geqslant\delta/2
; note that, since
A
contains no polynomial combinatorial line, the set
A\cap V_{y_{0}}^{\mathcal{S}}
also does not contain a polynomial combinatorial line. Set

(A.9)

\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1}]}:=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{[n_{1}]^{2}}\colon z(i,j)=z(j,i)\text{ for all }i,j\in[n_{1}]\Big\},

and, for every
z\in\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1}]}
, define
x_{z}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{D_{i_{0}}}
by setting
x_{z}(k_{i},k_{j})=z(i,j)
for all
i,j\in[n_{1}]
. Moreover, for every
z\in\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1}]}
, set
u_{z}:=y\cup x_{z}
. Clearly,
x_{z}\in\mathcal{S}
, and
u_{z}\in V_{y}^{\mathcal{S}}
for every
z\in\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1}]}
. Next, set

(A.10)

B:=\Big\{z\in\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1}]}\colon u_{z}\in A\cap V_{y_{0}}^{\mathcal{S}}\Big\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{[n_{1}]^{2}}};

notice that
B
contains no polynomial combinatorial line and
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1}]}}[B]=\mathbb{P}\big[A\,\big|\,V_{y_{0}}^{\mathcal{S}}\big]\geqslant\delta/2
, where
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1}]}}
denotes the uniform probability measure on
\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1}]}
. Finally, define

(A.11)

\mathcal{G}:=\bigg\{x\upharpoonright\binom{[n_{1}]}{2}\colon x\in B\bigg\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\binom{[n_{1}]}{\leqslant 2}}},

where
x\upharpoonright\binom{[n_{1}]}{2}
denotes the restriction of
x
on
\binom{[n_{1}]}{2}
, and we identity
\binom{[n_{1}]}{\leqslant 2}
with a subset of
[n_{1}]^{2}
via the injections

(A.12)

\binom{[n_{1}]}{1}\ni i\mapsto(i,i)\in[n_{1}]^{2}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \binom{[n_{1}]}{2}\ni\{i<j\}\mapsto(i,j)\in[n_{1}]^{2}.

Then,
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{G}]=\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1}]}}[B]\geqslant\delta/2
and, since
B
contains no polynomial combinatorial line, the family
\mathcal{G}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
-
\mathrm{HJ}
-code. Therefore,
\Delta_{n_{1}}^{\circ}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})\geqslant\delta/2
and, since
n_{1}
was an arbitrary positive integer, this contradicts our assumption that the sequence
\big(\Delta_{n}^{\circ}(\mathcal{C}^{\circ})\big)
converges to zero. The proof of the proposition is thus completed.
∎

Pericoronary fat attenuation index on coronary CT angiography and cardiovascular risk in patients with coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease
Authors: Xiaozhao Lu, Pan Wei, Shangyi Tang, Weitao Ye, Zuxian Huang, Ziyao Yuan, Huangtao Ruan, Haozhang Huang, Weipeng Zhang, Chenyang Wang, Yu Kang, Jing Hu, Zeliang Li, Jingru Deng, Yingying Li, Wanying Wu, Wai-kit Ming, Chun-Ka Wong, Ning Tan, Shiqun Chen, Jin Liu, Jiyan Chen, Yong Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe pericoronary fat attenuation index (pFAI), derived from coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA), is an emerging marker of local inflammation of coronary arteries. Its prognostic significance in patients with coronary artery disease (CAD) and chronic kidney disease (CKD), which share common inflammatory pathways, remains uncertain. This study aimed to assess the associations between the pFAI and cardiovascular and mortality risk in patients with concurrent CAD and CKD.MethodsPatients with both CAD and CKD who underwent CCTA within 1 year of invasive coronary angiography were included from Cardiorenal Improvement-Ⅱ. The pFAI was measured around the proximal 40 mm of the left anterior descending artery (LAD), left circumflex artery (LCX), and right coronary artery (RCA). High pFAI values were determined from the median levels of LAD (-81.0 HU), LCX (-78.8 HU) and RCA (-79.4 HU). The primary outcome was major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), including cardiovascular mortality, nonfatal myocardial infarction and stroke, and the secondary outcomes were cardiovascular and all-cause mortality. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was used to investigate the associations between pFAI and MACEs and cardiovascular and all-cause mortality.ResultsA total of 444 patients (mean age 70.7 ± 9.6 years, 32% female) were included, and 90 (20.3%) cases of MACE, 68 (15.3%) cases of cardiovascular mortality, and 125 (28.2%) cases of all-cause mortality occurred, with a median follow-up of 4.57 years. For MACEs in patients with high pFAI levels compared with those with low pFAI levels, the multivariable adjusted HR was 1.65 (95% CI: 1.02–2.66; P = 0.042) for LAD, 2.06 (95% CI: 1.23–3.45; P = 0.006) for LCX, and 1.71 (95% CI: 1.07–2.74; P = 0.025) for RCA. For cardiovascular and all-cause mortality, similar significant associations were observed (all P < 0.05).ConclusionsA high pFAI for each major coronary artery was independently associated with an increased risk of MACEs and cardiovascular and all-cause mortality in patients with CAD and CKD.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-025-04549-7.

Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) is the leading cause of mortality worldwide, with a global prevalence of 315 million people, which poses a major threat to public health [1]. With increasing population aging, chronic kidney disease (CKD) has emerged as a prevalent comorbidity of CAD, with a prevalence of 17.3% among patients with CAD [2]. The mortality risk is 2–3-fold greater in patients with CAD and CKD than in those without CKD, which is probably mediated by shared inflammatory pathways [3, 4]. Therefore, precise evaluation and risk stratification in this high-risk population are crucial to improve clinical outcomes.

Coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) was underutilized in CKD patients because of the potential risk of contrast-induced acute kidney injury (CI-AKI). With technological advancements in scanners, reconstruction algorithms, and optimized low-dose protocols combined with renal-protective strategies, CCTA, which offers quantitative plaque characterization, calcification scoring, detailed hemodynamic profiling, and precise quantification of pericoronary inflammation, is increasingly utilized for CAD assessment in patients with CKD [5–7]. The pericoronary fat attenuation index (pFAI), a novel noninvasive marker of coronary local inflammation measured via CCTA, was associated with high-risk plaques, plaque progression and adverse cardiovascular events in the general or stable chest pain population [8–10]. However, its prognostic utility in CKD patients remains underexplored, given that CCTA is not recommended for the evaluation of CAD in patients with severe renal dysfunction.

Therefore, we aimed to evaluate the prognostic value of the pFAI around three major coronary arteries derived from CCTA in patients with concurrent CAD and CKD.

Methods

Study design and participants

This study enrolled 646 patients with concomitant CAD and CKD who underwent CCTA 1 year prior to invasive coronary angiography (ICA) at Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital between January 2014 and December 2020 from the registry-based study of Cardiorenal Improvement-Ⅱ (CIN-Ⅱ, NCT05050877, data of registration in ClinicalTrials.gov: 10/09/2021). The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) aged ≥ 18 years, (2) diagnosed with CAD (confirmed by ICA) and CKD, and (3) underwent CCTA 1 year prior to ICA. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) poor image quality of CCTA, (2) history of coronary artery bypass graft, (3) history of coronary artery revascularization of the proximal coronary artery, (4) unmeasurable pFAI in any coronary artery, and (5) missing follow-up information. Finally, a total of 444 patients were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1).

Data collection and definitions

Clinical data concerning demographic information, comorbidities, laboratory tests, procedures, and prescriptions were derived from the electronic medical records system of Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital. The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated via the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration equation, and CKD was defined as an eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m2 [11]. Congestive heart failure (CHF) was defined as New York Heart Association class > 2 or Killip class > 1 [12]. Diabetes mellitus (DM) was assessed according to the patient’s diagnosis, a glycated hemoglobin level ≥ 6.5%, or treatment with a hypoglycemic agent. Anemia was defined as hemoglobin < 130 g/L in men and < 120 g/L in women according to the guidelines of the World Health Organization [13].

Outcome

The primary outcome was major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), including cardiovascular mortality, nonfatal myocardial infarction (MI) and nonfatal stroke. The secondary outcomes were cardiovascular mortality and all-cause mortality. Nonfatal MI was determined on the basis of the fourth universal definition of myocardial infarction [14], and nonfatal stroke was defined as nonfatal cerebral infarction, intracerebral hemorrhage, or unspecified stroke. The follow-up information was collected by trained medical personnel who contacted patients or their family members by telephone or was obtained by matching the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). All-cause mortality was ascertained from the death database of the CDC, and cardiovascular mortality was defined on the basis of primary and secondary causes of death according to the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision [ICD-10] codes I00 to I99. All participants provided oral informed consent.

CCTA acquisition

All CCTA images were acquired according to the local manipulation protocol, using a wide-detector and dual-source CT scanner with a 64-slice scanner (Somatom Definition Flash; Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany), a 256-slice scanner (Brilliance iCT, Philips Healthcare, Cleveland, Ohio), and a 320-slice scanner (Lightspeed VCT/Visipaque 320, GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA). The scanning and reconstruction protocols are shown in Supplementary Table S1. All the images were reviewed for quality assurance before being transferred to a core laboratory.

CCTA characteristics measurement

Plaque characteristics, pFAI, CT derived fractional flow reserve (CT-FFR), and coronary artery calcium were measured via CoronaryDoc (v6.11, Shukun (Beijing) Network Technology Co., Ltd.), Patients with stenosis greater than 50% [left anterior descending artery (LAD), left circumflex artery (LCX) and right coronary artery (RCA)] or 30% (left main coronary artery) were classified as having significant stenosis [15]. For more detailed methods of determining CCTA characteristics, see the Supplementary methods.

Coronary catheterization (ICA)

A digital ICA system (AlluraXper FD10, Philips Medical System, Best, Netherlands) was used to acquire cineangiographic images. CAD was typically diagnosed when there was 50% or greater luminal narrowing in one or more major coronary arteries. The SYNTAX score (version 2.28, https://syntaxscore.org/) was utilized to assess the complexity of CAD, particularly in patients with multivessel disease [16]. Two cardiologists with more than five years of interventional experience independently assessed the SYNTAX score. When their scores differed, a third interventional cardiologist with more than ten years of interventional experience was involved in the evaluation process.

Statistical analysis

We stratified patients on the basis of the median pFAI values of the LAD (-81.0 HU), LCX (-78.8 HU) and RCA (-79.4 HU). Continuous variables are presented as the means (standard deviations [SDs]) or medians (interquartile ranges [IQRs]), and categorical data are expressed as counts and percentages, as appropriate. Student’s t-test or the Mann‒Whitney U test was employed for continuous variables, and the chi‒square test was used for categorical variables as appropriate for group comparisons.

Kaplan‒Meier survival curves were generated to assess the prognostic significance of the pFAI for MACEs and cardiovascular and all-cause mortality. Cox proportional hazard models were applied to evaluate the associations between the pFAI and MACEs and between cardiovascular mortality and all-cause mortality, with hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) reported for each analysis. Restricted cubic splines were performed with 3 knots in multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models to assess the potential nonlinear relationships between continuous pFAI and outcomes. The covariates selection was primarily based on clinical a priori knowledge and relevant literature [8, 17], including age, sex, kvp ≥ 100 kv, hypertension, DM, CHF, percutaneous coronary interventions (PCI), eGFR, significant stenosis of the corresponding coronary artery, and the prescription of renin‒angiotensin‒aldosterone system inhibitors (RAASi). Considering the influence of low kvp for pFAI measurement and dialysis, we performed sensitivity analysis after excluding patients with kvp < 100kv (n = 71) or patients with dialysis (n = 32). We also performed subgroup analysis stratifying patients by CKD stages (stage 3 versus stages 4–5). Statistical analyses were performed via R software (version 4.4.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A two-tailed P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 444 patients with CAD and CKD who had undergone CCTA within 1 year prior to ICA were included in the analysis. The participants had a mean age of 70.7 (± 9.6) years and 32% were female, with a mean body mass index (BMI) of 23.5 (± 3.3) kg/m2. Patients were divided into two groups, high or low pFAI, and stratified according to the median values of the three vessels. Overall, patients with high pFAI values were younger, more likely to be male, had higher prevalence of CHF and dialysis, lower levels of LVEF and eGFR, and fewer prescriptions of RAASi. Moreover, patients with elevated pFAI values presented significantly greater total plaque volume, with a predominance of noncalcified plaque components. No significant differences were observed in hypertension, DM, the FFR, the CAC score, the PCI or the SYNTAX score. More detailed information on the baseline characteristics is provided in Table 1.

Associations of the pFAI with the risk of maces and cardiovascular and all-cause mortality

During a median follow-up of 4.57 years (IQR: 2.51–6.44 years), 90 (20.3%) cases of MACE, 68 (15.3%) cases of cardiovascular mortality, and 125 (28.2%) cases of all-cause mortality were recorded. Compared with low-level participants, participants with higher pFAI values across all coronary arteries had significantly increased risks for all endpoints during follow-up (log-rank P < 0.05), whereas higher pFAI values in the LAD demonstrated a borderline association with MACEs (log-rank P = 0.054; Fig. 2).

After accounting for age, sex, smoking status, kvp ≥ 100 kv, hypertension, DM, CHF, PCI, eGFR, significant stenosis of the corresponding coronary artery, and RAASi, elevated pFAI levels in the LAD, LCX, and RCA were significantly associated with increased risks of MACEs and cardiovascular and all-cause mortality compared with patients with low pFAI. For MACEs in patients with high pFAI compared with those with low pFAI, the multivariable adjusted HRs were 1.65 (95% CI: 1.02–2.66, P = 0.042) for LAD, 2.06 (95% CI: 1.23–3.45, P = 0.006) for LCX, and 1.71 (95% CI: 1.07–2.74, P = 0.025) for RCA. For cardiovascular mortality, the corresponding HRs were 2.72 (95% CI: 1.50–4.92, P = 0.001) for LAD, 2.90 (95% CI: 1.57–5.37, P = 0.001), and 2.12 (95% CI: 1.20–3.75, P = 0.009). Similarly, the HRs for all-cause mortality were 2.23 (95% CI: 1.46–3.43, P < 0.001), 2.02 (95% CI: 1.30–3.13, P = 0.002), and 1.86 (95% CI: 1.24–2.80, P = 0.003) for high pFAI values and low pFAI values of the LAD, LCX and RCA, respectively (Table 2).

Discussion

To our knowledge, the present study is the first to systematically explore the associations between pFAI based on CCTA and cardiovascular events and mortality in patients with CAD and CKD. This study demonstrated that higher pFAI of the LAD, LCX and RCA were significantly associated with MACEs and cardiovascular and all-cause mortality in this high-risk population (Graphic abstract). Moreover, near-linear relationships between the pFAI and the risk of MACEs and cardiovascular and all-cause mortality were observed.

PFAI represents the ectopic adipose tissue around the coronary artery with endocrine and paracrine functions and has been recognized as a non-invasive and novel index for cardiovascular events. Pathologically, pericoronary adipose tissue (PCAT) undergoes a proinflammatory shift, downregulating adiponectin and NO with anti-inflammatory effects and increasing the release of proinflammatory bioactive factors, including interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-α, which results in vascular endothelial dysfunction, inflammatory cell recruitment, lipid deposition and plaque formation, leading to the occurrence and progression of atherosclerosis [18]. Moreover, PCAT disrupts the differentiation and proliferation of preadipocytes, thereby altering the water‒lipid phase balance [19]. CKD is characterized by a low-grade chronic inflammatory status and metabolic disorders, including activation of the RAAS, lipid metabolism, and bone and mineral metabolism [20]. These factors intensify the inflammatory response within PCAT, exacerbating coronary atherosclerosis with increased cardiovascular risk for patients with CKD.

Previous studies on the pFAI as an independent predictor of cardiovascular adverse events have focused primarily on relatively low-risk patients. In the Cardiovascular RISk Prediction using Computed Tomography (CRISP-CT) study, Oikonomou et al.. reported that a high pFAI value (≥-70.1 HU) was associated with all-cause and cardiac mortality in patients with suspected CAD (HR: 2.15; 95% CI: 1.33–3.48), and that a J-shape association was observed between pFAI and mortality risk with adjustment for age, sex, tube voltage and epicardial adipose tissue (EAT) volume [8]. In our cohort of patients with CAD and CKD, univariable analyses also suggested non-linearity; however, after comprehensive adjustment, pFAI demonstrated an approximately linear relationship with adverse events, with only the LAD retaining a modest non-linear pattern. This discrepancy may reflect residual confounding as well as differences in population characteristics. Moreover, Ichikawa et al.. demonstrated that a high pFAI of the LAD (> -70.7 HU) was significantly associated with MACEs (HR: 2.69; 95% CI: 1.17–0.20) in 333 DM patients [21]. However, the use of CCTA remains limited in CKD patients due to concerns about the potential risk of CI-AKI. Furthermore, many previous studies enforced strict exclusion criteria concerning renal function, leading to a lack of substantial data on pFAI in CKD patients. Notably, Cho et al.. conducted a retrospective study involving 209 patients, 147 (70.3%) of whom had end-stage renal disease (ESRD) and underwent CCTA. Their findings indicated that an elevated FAI of the LAD (≥-65.5 HU) independently was associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality in ESRD patients (HR: 2.26, 95% CI: 1.11–4.61) [22]. Our research encompassed a broader CKD population and expanded the understanding of the associations between pFAI and both MACE and cardiovascular mortality among CKD patients, highlighting that the pFAI of three major coronary arteries was an independent risk factor for cardiovascular and mortality risk in these patients. In addition, our study population is not only unique due to the presence of CKD but also significantly older than those typically represented in previous studies, which may influence the thresholds for pFAI and exhibit heightened sensitivity to the risk of adverse cardiovascular events. Furthermore, malnutrition and prior statin exposure—frequent in CKD—may alter adipose tissue characteristics and thereby influence pFAI. Imaging acquisition parameters may also influence pFAI measurements, particularly kvp. Previous studies have shown that within the same individual, lower tube voltage was associated with decreased pFAI values [23, 24]. In clinical practice, the choice of kvp is largely determined by patient BMI, and our data similarly reflected this pattern, with patients scanned at lower kvp exhibiting lower pFAI values and plaque volume (Table S2). We therefore included kvp as a covariate and conducted sensitivity analysis excluding those with low kvp (< 100 kv), which confirmed that our main findings remained robust.

Previous studies have demonstrated that elevated pFAI is closely associated with greater plaque burden and high-risk plaque characteristics, including larger plaque volume, increased necrotic core content, and mixed or non-calcified phenotypes [25, 26]. In our cohort, patients with higher pFAI similarly exhibited greater plaque volume, particularly with increased lipid content (Table 1 and Figure S1), consistent with prior findings. Beyond pFAI, EAT exerts paracrine and vasocrine effects through the secretion of pro-inflammatory and profibrotic cytokines, which have been implicated in the development of multiple cardiovascular diseases [27]. Yamaura et al. studied 410 patients with chronic coronary syndrome and demonstrated that increased EAT was independently associated with greater plaque burden and calcification, irrespective of pFAI levels [28]. Taken together, these observations suggest that combining pFAI with EAT characteristics may enhance physiological assessment, particularly in high-risk populations.

In patients with CKD and CAD, invasive treatment has been shown to alleviate symptoms such as angina and improve quality of life for some individuals; however, it does not significantly reduce mortality or major adverse cardiovascular events, as evidenced by the ISCHEMIA trial [29]. These findings underscore the necessity of aggressive risk factor management in this high-risk population. Incorporating the pFAI into risk stratification models, such as the CaRi-Heart model, offers a promising approach to identifying high-risk individuals who may benefit from targeted therapies [30]. Furthermore, recent studies have shown that statin treatment is effective in reducing plaque progression and that SGLT2 inhibitors have the potential to decrease inflammatory responses in adipose tissue and lower the accumulation of immune cells in epicardial adipose tissue [31, 32]. Understanding how these therapies interact with pFAI may improve prognostic assessments and inform the development of targeted strategies to reduce cardiovascular mortality in CKD patients. However, caution is warranted when interpreting pFAI’s immediate clinical utility. While pFAI shows promise in risk stratification and treatment monitoring, its predictive value and therapeutic implications require further validation through large-scale, prospective trials, particularly in CKD populations.

Study limitations

This study had several limitations. First, the power of this study was limited by the inherent risks associated with the use of iodinated contrast agents during CCTA, particularly in patients with CKD who are at increased risk of CI-AKI owing to their compromised renal function. This heightened risk constrained our sample size, and there was a risk of selection bias and overfitting. Second, our findings may not be generalizable to lower-risk or asymptomatic patients, as the cohort was restricted to those undergoing ICA for CAD confirmation, which may introduce selection bias due to a higher pre-test probability of more symptomatic or severe disease. Third, a cause‒effect relationship between the pFAI and cardiovascular risk and mortality could not be established because the observational study had incomplete data and limited adjustment for confounding factors. Fourth, we excluded patients who had a history of proximal coronary artery revascularization before CCTA, which may influence the pFAI level and outcomes. Given these limitations, future studies with larger cohorts and prospective designs are needed to validate these findings and clarify the role of the pFAI in cardiovascular risk stratification among patients with CAD and CKD.

Conclusions

This study revealed that high pFAI of the LAD, LCX, and RCA were independently associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular adverse events and mortality in patients with CAD and CKD, underscoring their potential role in future risk assessment and guiding therapeutic and surgical interventions. Further prospective studies are needed to validate its role in guiding clinical decision-making before broader application.

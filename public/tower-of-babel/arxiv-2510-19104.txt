A Cartesian Promonoidal Kernel on Δ \Delta and a Hadamard Contraction of Δ n \Delta^{n}
Florian Lengyel, CUNY

Abstract
We exhibit a symmetric promonoidal kernel on the simplex category Δ \Delta with Cartesian unit, yielding on representable functors a ‘Hadamard’ natural transformation Δ p × Δ q → Δ p ​ q \Delta^{p}\times\Delta^{q}\to\Delta^{pq} based on pointwise multiplication of nondecreasing maps. Specializing to q = 1 q=1 yields a simplicial homotopy contracting Δ n \Delta^{n} to its 0 0 -vertex. The contraction is classical; the promonoidal presentation and induced Hadamard map appear not to be recorded.



MSC codes: 18D10, 18M15, 18A40, 55U10

1
Introduction

The simplicial category
\Delta
has as objects the finite ordinals
[n]=\{0<1<\cdots<n\}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) and as morphisms the nondecreasing maps. Its presheaf category
\widehat{\Delta}=\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set})
is the category of simplicial sets. We denote the representable simplicial set corresponding to
[n]
by
\Delta^{n}:=\Delta(-,[n])
.

We use the Cartesian promonoidal structure on
\Delta
with kernel
P([p],[q];[r])\cong\Delta([r],[p])\times\Delta([r],[q])
and unit
J([r])=\Delta([r],[0])
,
so that Day convolution on
\widehat{\Delta}
is the levelwise product. This is distinct from the
ordinal sum
monoidal structure on
\Delta
(see Mac Lane
[
Lan98
]
), which induces the
join
operation on simplicial sets (e.g., Lurie
[
Lur09
]
).

Independently, pointwise multiplication of monotone maps defines a natural
“Hadamard” transformation
\delta_{p,q}:P(p,q;-)\to\Delta(-,[pq])
, and hence, on representables,
H_{p,q}:\Delta^{p}\times\Delta^{q}\to\Delta^{pq}
.
Specializing
q=1
yields a simplicial homotopy
H^{n}:\Delta^{n}\times\Delta^{1}\to\Delta^{n}
from the constant
0
-vertex map to the identity; under realization this is the
standard PL contraction of
|\Delta^{n}|
to the
0
-vertex.

2
Promonoidal Categories and Day Convolution

We recall the definitions of promonoidal structures and the associated convolution developed by Day
[
Day70
,
Day74
]
(see also
[
DS95
,
Kel82
]
).

Definition 1

(Promonoidal category)
.

A
promonoidal structure
on a small category
C
consists of a kernel
P:C\times C\times C^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
, a unit profunctor
J:C^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
, and natural isomorphisms (associator
\alpha
and unitors
\lambda,\rho
) satisfying Mac Lane’s coherence axioms. It is
symmetric
if equipped with coherent symmetry isomorphisms
\sigma
.

Definition 2

(Day convolution)
.

Given a promonoidal
C
, the
Day convolution
on
\widehat{C}
is the monoidal structure defined by

(F\mathbin{\star}G)(u)\ :=\ \int^{x,y\in C}P(x,y;u)\times F(x)\times G(y),\qquad\mathbf{1}_{\mathbin{\star}}(u):=J(u).

3
The Cartesian Promonoidal Structure on
\Delta

We define a symmetric promonoidal structure on
C=\Delta
.

3.1
The Kernel
P
and Unit
J

Define the kernel
P:\Delta\times\Delta\times\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
by the coend

P([p],[q];[r])\ :=\ \int^{[m]\in\Delta}\ \Delta([m],[p])\times\Delta([m],[q])\times\Delta([r],[m]).

The action on morphisms is defined by composition in the respective factors. For example,
P(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id};h)
acts by precomposition with
h:[r^{\prime}]\to[r]
in the third (contravariant) factor.

We define the unit profunctor
J:\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
as
J([r])\ :=\ \Delta([r],[0])
. Since
[0]
is terminal in
\Delta
,
J([r])
is always a singleton set.

3.2
Identification and Coherence

Proposition 1
.

The kernel
P
is naturally isomorphic to the Cartesian product profunctor:

\eta_{p,q;r}:\ P([p],[q];[r])\ \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\ \Delta([r],[p])\times\Delta([r],[q]).

Proof.

This follows from the density theorem (co-Yoneda lemma). The isomorphism
\eta
sends a class
[\alpha:m\to p,\ \beta:m\to q,\ \gamma:r\to m]
to the pair
(\alpha\circ\gamma,\ \beta\circ\gamma)
.
∎

The structure defined by
(P,J)
is the standard
Cartesian promonoidal structure
on
\Delta
.

Theorem 1
.

The data
(P,J)
equips
\Delta
with a symmetric promonoidal structure.

Proof.

Using Proposition
1
, the required isomorphisms (
\alpha,\lambda,\rho,\sigma
) are induced by the corresponding canonical isomorphisms for the Cartesian product of sets. Coherence reduces to the coherence identities for products in
\mathbf{Set}
.
∎

Remark 1
.

The Day convolution induced by this structure is the Cartesian product on
\widehat{\Delta}
:
F\mathbin{\star}G\cong F\times G
. The monoidal unit is
\mathbf{1}_{\mathbin{\star}}=J=\Delta^{0}
. See Appendix
A
for a detailed verification.

4
The Hadamard Map

We introduce an operation based on the multiplicative structure of the ordinals and relate it to the Cartesian structure. We emphasize that the object assignment
[p]\odot[q]=[pq]
does not extend to a monoidal functor
\Delta\times\Delta\to\Delta
; the promonoidal setting is required. A related interpretation of pointwise products via convolution appears in Garner and Street
[
GS16
]
.

4.1
The Hadamard Product of Morphisms

Multiplication in
\mathbb{N}
is monotone in each variable.

Definition 3

(Hadamard Product)
.

For any
[m],[p],[q]\in\Delta
, define the map

H_{[m]}^{[p],[q]}:\ \Delta([m],[p])\times\Delta([m],[q])\longrightarrow\Delta([m],[pq]),\qquad(\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto(\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}\beta),

where
(\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}\beta)(t)\ :=\ \alpha(t)\cdot\beta(t)
for
0\leq t\leq m
.

This operation is natural in the source: for any
\theta:[k]\to[m]
,

(\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}\beta)\circ\theta=(\alpha\circ\theta)\mathbin{\boxtimes}(\beta\circ\theta)
.

4.2
The Hadamard Map as a Natural Transformation

The naturality implies that the Hadamard product defines a natural transformation of simplicial sets.

Definition 4
.

For fixed
p,q
, the
Hadamard map
is the natural transformation

H_{p,q}:\ \Delta^{p}\times\Delta^{q}\longrightarrow\Delta^{pq}

defined levelwise by
H_{[m]}(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}\beta
.

We verify that this corresponds to a natural transformation between the associated profunctors.

Proposition 2
.

The Hadamard map
H_{p,q}
induces a natural transformation

\delta_{p,q}:P(p,q;-)\longrightarrow\Delta(-,[pq]).

Proof.

We define the components
\delta_{p,q;[r]}
explicitly on the coend representatives of
P([p],[q];[r])
. Let
[\alpha:m\to p,\ \beta:m\to q,\ \gamma:r\to m]
be a representative. We set

\delta_{p,q;[r]}[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]\ :=\ H_{[m]}(\alpha,\beta)\circ\gamma\ =\ (\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}\beta)\circ\gamma.

Well-definedness.
We must check that this definition respects the coend relation in
[m]
. Let
f:[m^{\prime}]\to[m]
. We compare the images of the related representatives
[\alpha\circ f,\beta\circ f,\gamma]_{m^{\prime}}
and
[\alpha,\beta,f\circ\gamma]_{m}
.

\displaystyle\delta_{p,q;[r]}[\alpha\circ f,\beta\circ f,\gamma]

\displaystyle=H_{[m^{\prime}]}(\alpha\circ f,\beta\circ f)\circ\gamma

\displaystyle=(H_{[m]}(\alpha,\beta)\circ f)\circ\gamma\quad\text{(Naturality of }H\text{ in source)}

\displaystyle=H_{[m]}(\alpha,\beta)\circ(f\circ\gamma)

\displaystyle=\delta_{p,q;[r]}[\alpha,\beta,f\circ\gamma].

Thus,
\delta
is well-defined.

Naturality in
[r]
(contravariant).
We must verify that for any
h:[r^{\prime}]\to[r]
, the following square commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P([p],[q];[r])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta([r],[pq])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P([p],[q];[r^{\prime}])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta([r^{\prime}],[pq])}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{p,q;[r]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id};h)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta(h,[pq])}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{p,q;[r^{\prime}]}}

We check this on a representative
[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]
. The action
P(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id};h)
acts by precomposition on the contravariant factor
\gamma
, sending
[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]
to
[\alpha,\beta,\gamma\circ h]
.

Left-Bottom path:

\delta_{p,q;[r^{\prime}]}([\alpha,\beta,\gamma\circ h])\;=\;H_{[m]}(\alpha,\beta)\circ(\gamma\circ h).

The action
\Delta(h,[pq])
is precomposition by
h
.

Top-Right path:

\Delta(h,[pq])(\delta_{p,q;[r]}[\alpha,\beta,\gamma])\;=\;(H_{[m]}(\alpha,\beta)\circ\gamma)\circ h.

By associativity of composition, the diagram commutes.
∎

4.3
The Induced Map under Day Convolution

The natural transformation
\delta
between the kernels induces a map between the Day convolutions of representables.

Proposition 3
.

There is a natural transformation
\Theta_{p,q}:\ \Delta^{p}\mathbin{\star}\Delta^{q}\longrightarrow\Delta^{pq}
induced by
\delta
, which agrees with the Hadamard map
H_{p,q}
under the identification
\Delta^{p}\mathbin{\star}\Delta^{q}\cong\Delta^{p}\times\Delta^{q}
.

Proof.

By definition of the Day convolution:

(\Delta^{p}\mathbin{\star}\Delta^{q})([r])\;=\;\int^{[x],[y]}P([x],[y];[r])\times\Delta([x],[p])\times\Delta([y],[q]).

We define the component
\Theta_{[r]}
on representatives. An element is represented by a class

\big[\,\pi\in P(x,y;r)\ ;\ \alpha:x\to p,\ \beta:y\to q\,\big].

We define
\Theta_{[r]}
using the action of the morphisms
\alpha,\beta
on the kernel
P
(functoriality of
P
) followed by the map
\delta
:

\Theta_{[r]}\big([\pi;\alpha,\beta]\big)\ :=\ \delta_{p,q;[r]}\big(P(\alpha,\beta;[r])(\pi)\big).

Well-definedness.
We must check the coend relations in
[x]
and
[y]
.
Consider a map
u:[x^{\prime}]\to[x]
. The relation identifies

\big[\,P(u,\mathrm{id};r)(\pi^{\prime})\ ;\ \alpha,\ \beta\,\big]_{x,y}\sim\big[\,\pi^{\prime}\ ;\ \alpha\circ u,\ \beta\,\big]_{x^{\prime},y},

where
\pi^{\prime}\in P(x^{\prime},y;r)
.
The left side maps via
\Theta_{[r]}
to

\delta_{p,q;r}\big(P(\alpha,\beta;r)(P(u,\mathrm{id};r)(\pi^{\prime}))\big)=\delta_{p,q;r}\big(P(\alpha\circ u,\beta;r)(\pi^{\prime})\big).

The right side maps via
\Theta_{[r]}
to

\delta_{p,q;r}\big(P(\alpha\circ u,\beta;r)(\pi^{\prime})\big).

They are identical, so
\Theta
is well-defined. (The well-definedness of
\delta
, proved in Proposition
2
, handles the internal coend relation of
P
).

Explicit form and agreement with
H
.

To make
\Theta
explicit, let
\pi
be represented by
[\sigma_{x}:m\to x,\ \sigma_{y}:m\to y,\ \gamma:r\to m]
.
Then
P(\alpha,\beta;[r])(\pi)=[\alpha\circ\sigma_{x},\ \beta\circ\sigma_{y},\ \gamma]
. Applying
\delta
yields:

\Theta_{[r]}\Big(\big[\,[\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\gamma]\ ;\ \alpha,\beta\,\big]\Big)\ =\ H_{[m]}(\alpha\circ\sigma_{x},\ \beta\circ\sigma_{y})\circ\gamma.

Under the isomorphisms established in Appendix
A
(derived from co-Yoneda),

(\Delta^{p}\mathbin{\star}\Delta^{q})([r])\cong P([p],[q];[r])\cong\Delta([r],[p])\times\Delta([r],[q]).

Tracing the definitions, the map
\Theta_{[r]}
corresponds exactly to
\delta_{p,q;[r]}
under the middle identification, which in turn corresponds to the Hadamard product
H_{[r]}
under the right identification (Proposition
1
).
∎

Remark 2
.

The map
H_{p,q}
is generally not an isomorphism. For example, a map
h:[1]\to[4]
with
h(0)=3
cannot be factored as a Hadamard product of maps
\alpha,\beta:[1]\to[2]
. This demonstrates that the Hadamard operation is a comparison map from the Cartesian structure.

5
Contraction of
\Delta^{n}

We specialize the Hadamard map to define a simplicial homotopy (see e.g.,
[
GJ99
,
May92
]
for background on simplicial sets). We set
q=1
. Since
[n\cdot 1]=[n]
, we obtain a map:

Definition 5
.

Fix
n\geq 0
. Define the map
H^{n}:\Delta^{n}\times\Delta^{1}\to\Delta^{n}
as the specialization
H_{n,1}
.

H^{n}_{[m]}(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}\beta.

Since
H^{n}
is derived from a natural transformation, it is a map of simplicial sets.

Theorem 2

(Contractibility of
\Delta^{n}
)
.

For every
n\geq 0
,
H^{n}
is a simplicial homotopy from the identity map on
\Delta^{n}
to the constant map at the
0
-vertex.

Proof.

We verify the boundary conditions. Let
\kappa^{\varepsilon}:[0]\to[1]
be the map picking the vertex
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}
. We examine the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}\cong\Delta^{n}\times\Delta^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}\times\Delta^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ \ \mathrm{id}\times\kappa^{\varepsilon}_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}}

At level
[m]
,
\alpha\in\Delta([m],[n])
is mapped to
(\alpha,c_{\varepsilon})
, where
c_{\varepsilon}:[m]\to[1]
is the constant map with value
\varepsilon
(obtained as
\kappa^{\varepsilon}\circ!
).

Case
\varepsilon=1
:
H^{n}_{[m]}(\alpha,c_{1})=\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}c_{1}
. Since
(\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}c_{1})(t)=\alpha(t)\cdot 1=\alpha(t)
, the result is
\alpha
.

Case
\varepsilon=0
:
H^{n}_{[m]}(\alpha,c_{0})=\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}c_{0}
. Since
(\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}c_{0})(t)=\alpha(t)\cdot 0=0
, the result is the constant map at the 0-vertex of
\Delta^{n}
.
∎

6
Geometric Realization

We analyze the geometric realization of the homotopy
H^{n}
. The geometric realization of a simplicial set
X
was introduced by Milnor
[
Mil57
]
as a quotient construction. We utilize the coend formalism common in modern categorical expositions (e.g., Kelly
[
Kel82
]
):

\lvert X\rvert\ \cong\ \int^{[m]\in\Delta}X_{m}\times\lvert\Delta^{m}\rvert.

A point of
\lvert\Delta^{n}\rvert\times\lvert\Delta^{1}\rvert\cong\lvert\Delta^{n}\times\Delta^{1}\rvert
is represented by a class
(\alpha,\beta;u)
with

\alpha:[m]\to[n]
,
\beta:[m]\to[1]
monotone and
u=(u_{0},\dots,u_{m})
barycentric coordinates in
\lvert\Delta^{m}\rvert
.

A monotone map
\beta:[m]\to[1]
is a step function, characterized by a unique index
-1\leq k\leq m
such that
\beta(i)=0
for
i\leq k
and
\beta(i)=1
for
i\geq k+1
.
Set
s:=\sum_{i=k+1}^{m}u_{i}\in[0,1]
. This
s
is the coordinate corresponding to the vertex 1 in the
|\Delta^{1}|
factor realized by
|\beta|(u)
.

The realized homotopy
|H^{n}|
maps
(\alpha,\beta;u)
to a point
v\in|\Delta^{n}|
represented by
(\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}\beta;u)
. Its barycentric coordinates
v=(v_{0},\dots,v_{n})
are:

v_{j}\;=\;\sum_{(\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}\beta)(i)=j}u_{i}\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>k\\
\alpha(i)=j\end{subarray}}u_{i}\quad(1\leq j\leq n),

v_{0}\;=\;\sum_{(\alpha\mathbin{\boxtimes}\beta)(i)=0}u_{i}\;=\;\Big(\sum_{i\leq k}u_{i}\Big)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>k\\
\alpha(i)=0\end{subarray}}u_{i}\;=\;(1-s)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>k\\
\alpha(i)=0\end{subarray}}u_{i}.

At
s=0
this is the
0
-vertex; at
s=1
it is
|\alpha|(u)
.

Prism cells determined by
k
.

Fix
m\geq 0
. Let
e_{0},\dots,e_{m}
be the vertices of
|\Delta^{m}|
. We identify
|\Delta^{1}|
with the unit interval
[0,1]
.
For each
k\in\{0,\dots,m\}
define the
(m{+}1)
-simplex (“prism cell”)
S_{k}\subset|\Delta^{m}|\times[0,1]
as

S_{k}\ :=\ \mathrm{conv}\Big(\ (e_{0},0),\ \dots,\ (e_{k},0),\ (e_{k},1),\ (e_{k+1},1),\ \dots,\ (e_{m},1)\ \Big).

Equivalently,

S_{k}=\bigl\{(u,t)\ \big|\ u\in|\Delta^{m}|,\ \ \sum_{i>k}u_{i}\ \leq\ t\ \leq\ u_{k}+\sum_{i>k}u_{i}\bigr\}.

The prisms
\{S_{k}\}_{k=0}^{m}
cover
|\Delta^{m}|\times[0,1]
. The detailed analysis (omitted here for brevity, but standard in simplicial realization theory) shows the cell
S_{k}
is exactly the image of all representatives with step index
k
under the Milnor coend quotient.

Affineness of
|H^{n}|
on
S_{k}
.

Let
\alpha:[m]\to[n]
. The realized homotopy
|H^{n}|
acts on the vertices of
S_{k}
as follows:

\displaystyle(e_{i},0)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\text{vertex }0\quad(0\leq i\leq k),

\displaystyle(e_{i},1)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\text{vertex }\alpha(i)\quad(k\leq i\leq m).

There is a unique affine map
|H^{n}|\,:\ S_{k}\longrightarrow|\Delta^{n}|
determined by these values on the vertices.

Explicitly, if
(u,t)\in S_{k}
is written as a convex combination of its vertices:

(u,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}\,(e_{i},0)\ +\ \sum_{i=k}^{m}b_{i}\,(e_{i},1),

with
a_{i},b_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum a_{i}+\sum b_{i}=1
. The time coordinate is
t=\sum_{i=k}^{m}b_{i}
.
The barycentric coordinates
v=(v_{0},\dots,v_{n})
of the image
|H^{n}|(u,t)
are calculated by applying the affine map:

v_{j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=k\\
\alpha(i)=j\end{subarray}}^{m}b_{i}\quad(1\leq j\leq n),\qquad v_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}\ +\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=k\\
\alpha(i)=0\end{subarray}}^{m}b_{i}.

This formula is affine in the coefficients
(a_{i},b_{i})
, confirming
|H^{n}|
is affine on each
S_{k}
.

The standard PL contraction to the
0
-vertex.

For
x\in|\Delta^{n}|
with coordinates
w
and
t\in[0,1]
, the standard piecewise‑linear contraction
C:\ |\Delta^{n}|\times[0,1]\longrightarrow|\Delta^{n}|
is defined by

C(x,t)\ \text{ coordinates: }\ c_{0}=(1-t)+t\,w_{0},\qquad c_{j}=t\,w_{j}\ \ (1\leq j\leq n).

On each
S_{k}
, both
|H^{n}|
and the map
C\circ(|\alpha|\times\mathrm{id})
are affine. They agree on all vertices of
S_{k}
. Since they agree on the vertices and are affine, they coincide on the entire cell
S_{k}
. Therefore
|H^{n}|
is the standard PL contraction of
\lvert\Delta^{n}\rvert
to the
0
-vertex.

Appendix A
Verification of the Cartesian Product via Day Convolution

In this appendix, we verify the claim in Remark
1
that the promonoidal structure
(P,J)
defined in Section 3 induces the Cartesian product structure (levelwise product) on the category of simplicial sets
\widehat{\Delta}
.

The Unit.

The unit of the Day convolution is
\mathbf{1}_{\mathbin{\star}}([r])=J([r])=\Delta([r],[0])
. Since
[0]
is terminal in
\Delta
,
J([r])
is a singleton set for all
[r]
. This confirms that
\mathbf{1}_{\mathbin{\star}}
is the terminal object in
\widehat{\Delta}
.

The Product.

We compute the Day convolution of two simplicial sets
F
and
G
at level
[r]
:

(F\mathbin{\star}G)([r])\ :=\ \int^{[p],[q]\in\Delta}P([p],[q];[r])\times F([p])\times G([q]).

We use the identification
P([p],[q];[r])\cong\Delta([r],[p])\times\Delta([r],[q])
.

(F\mathbin{\star}G)([r])\ \cong\ \int^{[p],[q]}\left(\Delta([r],[p])\times\Delta([r],[q])\right)\times F([p])\times G([q]).

Rearranging the terms and using the Fubini theorem for coends (as the Cartesian product in
\mathbf{Set}
preserves coends):

(F\mathbin{\star}G)([r])\ \cong\ \left(\int^{[p]}\Delta([r],[p])\times F([p])\right)\times\left(\int^{[q]}\Delta([r],[q])\times G([q])\right).

Finally, we apply the co-Yoneda lemma,
\int^{[p]}\Delta([r],[p])\times F([p])\cong F([r])
, to both factors:

(F\mathbin{\star}G)([r])\ \cong\ F([r])\times G([r]).

This confirms that the Day convolution
\mathbin{\star}
is the levelwise product, and thus the Cartesian product in
\widehat{\Delta}
.

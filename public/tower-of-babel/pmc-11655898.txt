A pan-cancer analysis of the oncogenic role of N-acetyltransferase 8 like in human cancer
Authors: Jiamin Chen, Fanglin Shao, Shuxia Zhang, Youliang Qian, Mei Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundN-Acetyltransferase 8 Like (NAT8L) inhibits natural killer (NK)/T-cell cytotoxicity by impairing the formation of the immunological synapse via N-acetylaspartate (NAA). Existing research has predominantly focused on the metabolic functions of NAT8L, particularly in adipose tissues and myelination in the brain. However, in contrast to other N-acetyltransferases such as NAT1 and NAT2, the role of NAT8L in cancer has been less extensively studied. In this study, we conducted a comprehensive pan-cancer analysis to investigate the carcinogenic role of NAT8L in human cancers.MethodsWe utilized the standardized TCGA pan-cancer dataset to analyze differential expression, clinical prognosis, gene mutation, immune infiltration, epigenetic modification, tumor stemness, and heterogeneity. Additionally, we evaluated the sensitivity of NAT8L to small molecule drugs using the GDSC and CTRP databases.ResultsIn this study, we identified that NAT8L expression was upregulated in 6 cancers and downregulated in 12 compared to normal tissues. We analyzed its prognostic value in 5 tumor types (KIRP, COAD, COADREAD, GBMLGG, LUSC) and found correlations with overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free interval (PFI). Furthermore, NAT8L expression was significantly correlated with levels of most immune checkpoints, immunomodulators, and immune cell infiltration. The mutation frequencies for bladder cancer (BLCA), glioblastoma multiforme and glioma (GBMLGG), lower-grade glioma (LGG), and KIRP were 1.2%, 0.9%, 0.8%, and 0.4%, respectively.ConclusionOur findings suggest that NAT8L may serve as a potential prognostic marker and therapeutic target across a variety of cancers, particularly in KIRP, COAD, COADREAD, GBMLGG, and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01605-w.

Introduction

Cancer remains the leading cause of death worldwide, which is primarily driven by oncogenic somatic and/or germline alterations in oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes, significantly impacting patients' quality of life [1–3]. The incidence of cancer not only adversely affects patients' health but also imposes a substantial burden on public health finances [4, 5]. Even when controlling for factors such as race, sex, and diet, significant disparities in cancer onset, prognosis, and clinical outcomes across different age groups have been well established [6].

Immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) has transformed the landscape of cancer treatment by activating the patient's own immune system to recognize and attack cancer cells [7–9]. This approach has demonstrated remarkable efficacy in many patients, particularly for those with difficult-to-treat or advanced cancers [10, 11]. However, a considerable proportion of patients still exhibit resistance or limited responsiveness to existing immunotherapies, which is believed to be associated with the attenuation of immune responses [12–14]. Additionally, alterations in tumor cell metabolism can suppress anti-tumor immunity, contributing to resistance against immunotherapy [15, 16]. Investigating the mechanisms of immune evasion is crucial for identifying novel therapeutic targets, deepening our understanding of the processes driving ICB resistance, and developing strategies to overcome current challenges in immunotherapy [17, 18].

N-Acetyltransferase 8 Like (NAT8L) is located at the cytogenetic band 4p16.3, NAT8L catalyzes the synthesis of N-acetylaspartate (NAA) from l-aspartate and acetyl-CoA [19–21]. Furthermore, NAT8L influences the cytotoxic capabilities of immune cells through its metabolite NAA. Specifically, NAA significantly inhibits the formation of immune synapses in natural killer (NK) cells and CD8 + T cells, thereby diminishing their cytotoxicity [22].

Previous studies have demonstrated that NAT8L plays a significant role in cancer cell proliferation. Silencing NAT8L inhibits the growth of various human cancerous and non-cancerous cell line. In ovarian cancer cell lines, NAT8L silencing reduces proliferation, consistent with findings in lung cancer [23]. Additionally, NAT8L silencing significantly reduces tumor growth in orthotopic mouse models of ovarian cancer and melanoma. Zand et al. also suggested that NAT8L silencing downregulates the FOXM1-mediated antiapoptotic pathway [23].

However, despite these findings, research on NAT8L in the context of cancer is quite limited. In the last decades, a whole new scientific field, bioinformatics, has developed [24].Analyses in genomics, epigenetics, and transcriptomics have been extensively applied to study various cancer types, enabling the stratification of tumors into distinct subgroups [25, 26]. Several findings from these studies have been translated into clinical practice due to their potential in predicting prognosis, guiding therapy response, and identifying therapeutic targets [27–29]. By mining multi-omics profiling data using bioinformatics, potential avenues for future investigation of NAT8L in cancers can be unveiled [30–33].To provide researchers with a more comprehensive understanding of the NAT8L gene's association with human cancer, we investigated the relationship between NAT8L expression and various cancers, exploring its potential role as a biomarker from a pan-cancer perspective.

Materials and methods

NAT8L expression and survival analysis

Consistent with previous studies [34], we obtained the The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) Pan-cancer dataset from the UCSC database (https://xenabrowser.net/). Additionally, we integrated TCGA prognostic datasets from prior research and extracted NAT8L expression data from each sample. We screened the sample sources as follows: Solid Tissue Normal, Primary Solid Tumor, Primary Tumor, Normal Tissue, Primary Blood Derived Cancer—Bone Marrow, and Primary Blood Derived Cancer—Peripheral Blood samples. Furthermore, we filtered out samples with expression levels of 0, and subsequently performed log2(x + 0.001) transformations for each expression value. We employed unmatched Wilcoxon Rank Sum and Signed Rank Tests to analyze significant differences. The Wilcoxon Rank Sum Test was used to compare differences between two independent groups, while the Wilcoxon Signed Rank Test was used for comparing differences within paired or matched data. The Cox proportional hazards regression model was utilized to evaluate the prognostic significance of NAT8L in relation to overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), disease-free survival (DFS), and progression-free interval (PFI). Additionally, unmatched Wilcoxon Rank Sum and Signed Rank Tests were conducted to assess the significance of differences between pairs. We also evaluated the association of NAT8L expression with clinical characteristics such as clinical stage, sex, and age. To investigate the relationship between NAT8L expression and age, we employed R software (version 3.6.4) to calculate the Spearman correlation for each tumor. Additionally, to assess the relationship between NAT8L expression and clinical stage as well as gender, we utilized R software (version 3.6.4) to analyze gene expression differences across various clinical stages within each tumor sample. We applied the unpaired Wilcoxon Rank Sum and Signed Rank Tests to evaluate the significance of these differences. Furthermore, the Kruskal–Wallis test was employed to assess differences among multiple sample groups.

Analysis of tumor heterogeneity, stemness, mutation landscape and RNA methylation

Spearman correlation analysis which was used to measure the strength and direction of the monotonic relationship between two continuous or ordinal variables, particularly when the data do not meet the assumptions of linearity or normality, was employed to assess tumor heterogeneity, encompassing factors such as tumor mutation burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI), neoantigen (NEO), and tumor purity. Spearman analysis was utilized to examine the correlation between tumor stemness characteristics and NAT8L expression, including DNA methylation (DNAss) and RNA expression (RNAss) Furthermore, tumor patients were categorized into high and low expression groups based on the median expression value of NAT8L. We utilized the Genomic Data Commons (GDC) platform via MuTect2 (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) to download the software for processing all Simple Nucleotide Variation data sets at level 4 from TCGA [35]. Tumor patients were categorized into high and low expression groups based on the median expression of NAT8L. We then analyzed the ten genes with the highest mutation frequency in patients with cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP), and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC). Additionally, we examined the association of ITPRIPL1 with RNA methylation regulators m1A, m6A, and m5C.

Analysis of immune regulation, checkpoints and tumor microenvironment

We downloaded the standardized pan-cancer dataset from the UCSC database: TCGA TARGET GTEx (PANCAN, N = 19,131, G = 60,499). We extracted the ENSG00000185818 (NAT8L) gene and 150 immunostimulator pathways from five types of immune pathways: chemokine (41), receptor (18), MHC (21), immunoinhibitor (24), and immunostimulator (46). Additionally, we extracted the ENSG00000185818 (NAT8L) gene along with 60 genes from two types of immune checkpoint pathways: inhibitory (24) and stimulatory (36). Furthermore, we screened the sample sources as follows: Primary Solid Tumor, Primary Tumor, Primary Blood Derived Cancer—Bone Marrow, and Primary Blood Derived Cancer—Peripheral Blood. We also filtered out samples with an expression level of 0 and all normal samples, and performed a log2(x + 0.001) transformation for each expression value. Finally, we calculated the Pearson correlation between ENSG00000185818 (NAT8L) and five immune pathway marker genes. The TIMER and ESTIMATE algorithms were employed to evaluate the TME. We conducted an in-depth investigation of NAT8L's drug sensitivity across various cancer types using GSCALite, which integrates the Cancer Drug Sensitivity Genomics (GDSC) and the Cancer Therapeutic Response Portal (CTRP) databases [36]. A positive correlation indicated that increased gene expression could lead to drug resistance, whereas a negative correlation suggested that higher gene expression might enhance drug sensitivity.

Statistical analysis

All data are expressed as standard deviations. All analyses were conducted using the Sanger platform. A p-value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant. The Wilcoxon rank sum test and sign test were employed for difference analysis, while the Kruskal test was utilized for multi-group sample testing, which was used to determine if there are statistically significant differences between the medians of three or more independent groups when the data do not meet the assumptions of normality. The Spearman test was applied to analyze the correlation between the two variables.

Overall, we noted that NAT8L expression varied between tumor and normal samples and was significantly correlated with prognosis in KIRP, COAD, COADREAD, and glioma (GBMLGG). The expression level of NAT8L in 15 tumors, including bladder cancer (BLCA) (P < 0.001), GBMLGG (P < 0.001), and LUSC (P < 0.001), was correlated with age (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Discussion

Cancer remains the leading cause of death worldwide, significantly impacting patients' quality of life [1, 37]. The occurrence of various cancers not only deteriorates the health of patients but also imposes a substantial burden on public health finances [38]. ICB is a cancer treatment that employs drugs to inhibit the ability of cancer cells to utilize immune checkpoints, thereby evading detection by the immune system [39]. However, a considerable proportion of patients continue to exhibit resistance or limited responsiveness to existing immunotherapies [40–42]. Tumor cells influence immune cell function and the immune microenvironment by altering metabolic pathways and products, which in turn promotes immune escape and tumor growth [43]. There is increasing evidence that the metabolic profile of tumors plays a crucial role in their distinct immunological properties [44, 45].

In the immune response, immune synapses facilitate the recognition and targeting of cells by immune cells through signaling and cell–cell interactions [46, 47]. This structure plays a crucial role in regulating and enhancing the activity of immune cells, serving as an essential functional unit in the immune response [48]. The cytotoxic activity mediated by T cells and NK cells is vital for the therapeutic efficacy of PD-1/PD-L1 blockade. NAT8L catalyzes the synthesis of NAA from L-aspartate and acetyl-CoA [19–21]. NAT8L influences the cytotoxic capabilities of immune cells via its metabolite NAA. Specifically, NAT8L inhibits NK/T cell cytotoxicity by disrupting the formation of immune synapses through NAA. By targeting NAT8L or its metabolic pathway, the anti-tumor activity of immune cells can be enhanced, thereby improving the effectiveness of immunotherapy [22].

Given the complex mechanisms underlying tumor formation and development, understanding the biological behavior of tumors and their therapeutic approaches from multiple perspectives has proven to be a significant challenge [49, 50]. However, advancements in high-throughput sequencing technology have led to the continuous updating of cancer databases, resulting in a substantial availability of omics data [51–53]. The application of bioinformatics can effectively elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms and prognostic variations in tumors [54]. We conducted bioinformatic analyses of patient data across various datasets to evaluate NAT8L expression in different tissues.

In this study, we found that NAT8L expression levels were upregulated in 6 cancers and downregulated in 12 cancers compared to normal tissues. We also investigated the prognostic value of NAT8L by analyzing OS, DSS, and PFI in 5 tumor types: KIRP, COAD, COADREAD, GBMLGG, and LUSC. Aging is a common biological process that increases the risk of cancer [55]. Although aging and cancer are distinct biological processes, they share many similarities, including genetic mutations, cellular functions, inflammatory responses, metabolic changes, and tissue microenvironments [56–58]. Our study also demonstrated a correlation between age and NAT8L expression. Furthermore, NAT8L expression was associated with tumor stage and the TNM classification. Based on these results, NAT8L may serve as a potential biomarker for various cancers and could provide new therapeutic targets.

Various cells within the TME, including immune cells such as B cells, significantly influence tumor growth and progression [59]. The role of B cells in tumors is multifaceted; they can contribute to anti-tumor immunity or, conversely, support tumor growth [60, 61]. To investigate the relationship between NAT8L expression in the TME and tumor-infiltrating immune cells, we employed the TIMER method to evaluate the infiltration scores of different cell types. Our results indicate that, in most tumors, NAT8L expression is positively correlated with B cell presence. Furthermore, we analyzed 60 immune checkpoint genes and discovered that NAT8L is positively correlated with the abundance of immune checkpoints in colorectal cancer subtypes, including COAD, COADREAD, and rectum adenocarcinoma (READ). This finding suggests that NAT8L may serve as a promising drug target. The positive correlation between NAT8L and immune checkpoints implies that NAT8L may regulate immune evasion mechanisms by influencing checkpoint pathways such as PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4, thereby opening avenues for new therapeutic strategies. Targeting NAT8L could enhance the effectiveness of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), particularly in colorectal cancers where immune response is often suppressed. NAT8L’s dual role in both metabolism and immune modulation suggests potential for combination therapies involving metabolic inhibitors alongside ICIs [62].Future drug development could focus on creating NAT8L inhibitors, either as standalone treatments or in combination with existing ICIs to improve response rates, especially in immune “cold” tumors [63]. Additionally, NAT8L may serve as a predictive biomarker to stratify colorectal cancer patients based on their potential response to immunotherapies, optimizing treatment selection [64].These findings warrant further preclinical and clinical research to validate the safety and efficacy of NAT8L-targeted therapies in human trials.

Tumor heterogeneity encompasses variations in growth rate, invasiveness, drug sensitivity, and prognosis [65–67]. This heterogeneity plays a crucial role in the biological characteristics, therapeutic responses, and prognoses of tumors [68]. Our analysis focused on four measures of tumor heterogeneity: TMB, MSI, NEO, and tumor purity. TMB is highly correlated with the efficacy of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors [69, 70], with tumors exhibiting high TMB typically demonstrating greater immunogenicity, making them more recognizable and susceptible to attack by the immune system [71, 72]. Previous studies have indicated a strong correlation between TMB and OS in patients with LUAD, suggesting that TMB can serve as a prognostic indicator for these patients [73]. Our study revealed a significant association between NAT8L expression and TMB across 16 different tumor types, indicating that NAT8L may influence tumor heterogeneity and subsequent variations in TMB levels. Elevated TMB levels have a significant positive impact on the efficacy of ICB and can serve as a critical biomarker for predicting responses to ICB therapy [74].

Further, we evaluated the relationship between the stemness and NAT8L, which suggests that NAT8L reflects stem cell progression. Tumor cells exhibiting high stemness generally demonstrate an enhanced ability for self-renewal and an increased potential for metastasis [75]. DNAss and RNAss serve as predictive indicators of tumor biological behavior, including aggressiveness, metastasis, and treatment resistance [76, 77]. Our observations indicate that NAT8L expression is strongly correlated with RNAss in KIPAN and LUSC patients, suggesting a poor prognosis.

We aimed to investigate the role of NAT8L in tumors by analyzing gene mutations and expression levels. Patients were categorized into two groups based on NAT8L expression levels in high mutation rate cancers (CHOL, COAD, HNSC, KIRP, LUSC) to examine the similarities and differences in gene mutations. In our study, we found that PBRM1 mutations are most prevalent in CHOL. PBRM1 play a significant role in immune cell infiltration, matrix formation, and tumor invasion in CHOL. Consequently, PBRM1 could represent a novel therapeutic target for this cancer [78]. In summary, PBRM1 mutations may serve as additional biomarkers for predicting immunotherapy response.

In summary, the results of this study highlight the strong correlation between NAT8L expression and various human cancers, suggesting its prognostic significance. The elevated levels of NAT8L across multiple cancers and its negative association with different cancer types indicate that NAT8L has the potential to serve as an independent prognostic indicator for various malignancies. Variations in NAT8L expression levels among different cancer types may influence prognosis and warrant further investigation. Additionally, the study proposes potential mechanisms linking NAT8L expression to survival, clinical features, immune infiltration, tumor heterogeneity, stemness, and gene mutations from a pan-cancer perspective. These insights enhance our understanding of the role of NAT8L in tumorigenesis and cancer progression, with the potential to guide more precise and personalized immunotherapy strategies.

This study has several limitations. First, the data were sourced from publicly available databases, which may not fully represent diverse patient populations. Factors like age, gender, ethnicity, and comorbidities were not accounted for, potentially introducing biases and limiting the generalizability of our findings. Additionally, while we identified correlations between NAT8L expression and cancer-related factors such as immune infiltration and tumor heterogeneity, the precise molecular mechanisms remain unclear, and the lack of experimental validation restricts our ability to draw definitive conclusions.

Furthermore, although we found significant associations between NAT8L and immune checkpoints, it is still unknown whether NAT8L directly regulates these pathways or affects the broader tumor microenvironment. This uncertainty could influence the effectiveness of NAT8L-targeted therapies in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors. Preclinical and clinical studies are necessary to validate these findings and explore the therapeutic potential of NAT8L.

Additionally, KIRP has two subtypes, which are known to differ in clinical behavior and outcomes, and this distinction could indeed influence the prognostic value of NAT8L. However, because TCGA does not classify KIRP, subtype analysis is not possible at present.

To address these limitations, future studies should include large-scale clinical trials that consider demographic variables, alongside mechanistic studies exploring NAT8L's role in immune modulation and tumor metabolism. Experimental validation in both in vitro and in vivo models is essential to confirm NAT8L's role in cancer progression and its potential as a therapeutic target.

Conclusion

In summary, we conducted a comprehensive pan-cancer analysis of NAT8L and identified significant associations between NAT8L expression and various factors, including survival, clinical features, immune invasion, tumor heterogeneity, stemness, and gene mutations. Our study suggests that NAT8L may serve as a novel prognostic biomarker and an immunotherapeutic target.

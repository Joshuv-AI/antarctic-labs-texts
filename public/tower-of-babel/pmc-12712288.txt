TC check: a web app for thyroid cancer recurrence prediction using explainable machine learning
Authors: Huashu Wen, Xiaohua Li, Xia Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThyroid cancer (TC) is one of the most prevalent endocrine malignancies, and its recurrence presents a major clinical challenge that can adversely affect patient prognosis and treatment outcomes. Despite the progress in diagnostic methods, traditional statistical models still face limitations in accurately predicting TC recurrence due to the intricate interactions between clinical and pathological factors.MethodsTo address this challenge, the study presented a novel stacking ensemble learning framework for TC recurrence prediction. The dataset included a total of 383 patients, comprising 108 recurrence and 275 non-recurrence cases, and was stratified into training set (n = 268) and testing set (n = 115) using a 70:30 ratio. The proposed stacking framework integrated three heterogeneous base learners, namely Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), Extra Trees (ET), and Decision Trees (DT) with eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) as the meta learner. The hyperparameter optimization of various learners was performed through 5-fold cross-validation on the training set. The model performance was evaluated on testing set using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, AUC, and Brier score (BS). To enhance the model’s interpretability, the Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) method was utilized to identify the overall top influential factor and provide local interpretation for specific individual patient based model outcome.ResultsThe proposed stacking model achieved accuracy of 96.52%, precision of 96.67%, recall of 90.62%, and F1-Score of 93.55%, AUC of 0.9921 on the testing set. The SHAP analysis revealed the top 5 critical factors to TC recurrence, including treatment response, age, N-stage, risk stratification, and adenopathy. Furthermore, an interactive and user-friendly prediction tool, TCCheck, was developed based on optimized stacking model, accessible online at https://tccheck-prediction-tool.streamlit.app/.ConclusionThe study presented an effective and interpretable stacking ensemble learning framework for predicting TC recurrence. By deploying the proposed framework as a web prediction tool, it enables explainable and individualized clinical decision support, thereby enhancing its translational value in real-world settings. Furthermore, the framework serves as a methodological reference for recurrence prediction in other cancer types.Keywords: Thyroid cancer, Recurrence, Web app, Explainable machine learning, Multiple algorithms, Stacking learning

Introduction

Thyroid cancer (TC) is one of the most common endocrine malignancies (Jin et al. 2025; Maleki et al. 2023; Pizzato et al. 2022). In 2020, it ranked ninth among all cancers globally (Nguyen et al. 2025; Zhanghuang et al. 2024). In 2019, TC affected approximately 233,850 individuals worldwide, leading to about 45,580 deaths and an estimated 1.23 million disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) lost (Bao et al. 2021). From 1990 to 2019, the age-standardized incidence rate of TC increased significantly across both sexes, while the age-standardized mortality and DALY rates showed a gradual decline (Zhao et al. 2024).

Although most patients respond well to treatment, approximately 5–10% may progress to advanced disease (Nervo et al. 2023). Thus, recurrence prediction remains a crucial clinical task. TC recurrence is shaped by various risk factors such as age, tumor size, nodal metastasis, aggressive histology, multifocality, and additional prognostic indicators (Baek et al. 2010; Baloch et al. 2022; Boucai et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2023; Coca-Pelaz et al. 2020; Grønlund et al. 2021; Kim et al. 2021a; Kitahara and Schneider 2022; Masui et al. 2023; Rahman et al. 2024; Rossi et al. 2021; Semsar-Kazerooni et al. 2022). Due to the complex and multifactorial nature of the disease, recurrence prediction remains challenging. Traditional statistical methods may be inadequate for detecting nonlinear relationships within high-dimensional clinical data.

In recent years, machine learning (ML) has demonstrated strong potential in cancer prognosis, diagnosis, and treatment planning (Gaur and Jagtap 2022; Masuda et al. 2021; Swanson et al. 2023; Tran et al. 2021; Yaqoob et al. 2023a, b, 2024; Zhang et al. 2023). Recent studies have shown the effectiveness of ML models in predicting TC recurrence, outperforming traditional statistical methods. Multiple ML algorithms, such as Random Forest (RF) and Support Vector Machines (SVM), have been applied to TC recurrence prediction, addressing tasks including feature selection and hybrid optimization (Alam et al. 2024; Clark et al. 2024; Kim et al. 2021b; Książek 2024; Lixandru-Petre et al. 2025; Mourad et al. 2020; Ozturk et al. 2024; Schindele et al. 2025; Shrestha et al. 2025). However, most prior ML-based recurrence works still face several challenges, as they primarily rely on single classifiers with limited modeling diversity, insufficient interpretability, and weak generalizability, which collectively constrain their clinical applicability and trust.

To address these limitations, ensemble learning techniques may provide powerful solution (Brown 2017; Kunapuli 2023; Mienye and Sun 2022; Rane et al. 2024; Sagi and Rokach 2018; Yang et al. 2023). Among them, stacking architectures have shown particular promise in many healthcare applications (Jannat et al. 2023; Kablan et al. 2023; Mahajan et al. 2023; Mohapatra et al. 2023; Paul et al. 2024; Velmurugan and Dhinakaran 2022; Yang et al. 2021; Yoon and Kang 2023). Building on this, we proposed a novel stacking ensemble framework for TC recurrence prediction, which integrated three heterogeneous base learners, namely Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) (Bottou 2010), Extra Trees (ET) (Geurts et al. 2006), and Decision Trees (DT) (Loh 2011) with XGBoost (Chen and Guestrin 2016) as the meta learner. To provide more detailed model explanations, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) (Lundberg and Lee 2017; Lundberg et al. 2020b) were applied to provide consistent global and local feature attributions, offering more comprehensive insights compared with methods such as Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) (Biecek and Burzykowski 2021; Linardatos et al. 2020), which primarily focused on local explanations. Finally, to facilitate clinical translation, a user-friendly web tool, TCCheck, was developed based on the proposed stacking ensemble framework, enabling clinicians to access interpretable recurrence predictions in practice.

Materials and methods

Data collection & preprocessing

The overview of the studying flow were depicted in Fig. 1. The dataset used in this study was obtained from the public database (Asuncion and Newman 2007). The dataset included a total of 383 TC patients, comprising 108 recurrence and 275 non-recurrence cases. It comprised 16 clinical features variables, including age, gender, smoking, hx smoking, hx radiothreapy, thyroid function, physical examination, adenopathy, pathology, focality, risk, T, N, M, stage, response (Table 1). Recurrence status was used as the target variable, representing a binary classification task. No missing values were observed in the dataset. Among the features, 15 categorical variables were encoded using label encoding, while the continuous variable (age) was retained without encoding (Table 1). Subsequently, the dataset was stratified into training set (n = 268) and testing sets (n = 115) using a 70:30 ratio, to ensure sufficient samples for model training and evaluation. All experiments were conducted on a Linux server equipped with one NVIDIA RTX3080 GPUs and 6 CPU threads.

Model Building & hyperparameter optimization

The proposed stacking ensemble framework integrated three heterogeneous base learners (SGD, ET, DT) and one meta learner (XGBoost) (Fig. 2). Each base learner utilized the same input feature set derived from the dataset to generate class predictions (hard labels) as outputs. The meta learner aggregated these predictions from the base learners to produce the final results. Hyperparameter optimization and validation for both base and meta learners were conducted through GridSearchCV employing five-fold cross-validation, with the AUC as the objective metric. The detailed hyperparameter search space was listed in Table 2. The process aimed to identify the best-performing configurations while minimizing the risk of overfitting. After selecting the optimal hyperparameters, the optimized stacking model was evaluated on the independent testing set.

Model evaluation & interpretation

The optimized stacking model was then evaluated on the testing set for discriminative performance using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). To further evaluate the reliability of the predicted probabilities, model calibration assessment was conducted using the Brier score (BS) (Glenn 1950). Furthermore, the Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) method (Lundberg and Lee 2017; Lundberg, Erion, Chen, DeGrave, Prutkin, Nair, Katz, Himmelfarb, Bansal and Lee 2020b) was applied to assess model interpretability. With the training set as background data, SHAP values were computed on the testing set to quantify each feature’s contribution to recurrence predictions, providing both global analysis of feature importance and individual-level explanations. System development

Streamlit is a Python framework designed for developing web applications, particularly well-suited for data science and machine learning projects (Khorasani et al. 2022; Moscato 2024; Richards 2023). It has been widely applied in healthcare, including disease prediction and medical image analysis (Das et al. 2024; Gopisetti et al. 2023; Kitagawa et al. 2025; Revathy et al. 2024; Rodríguez-Belenguer et al. 2025; Shi et al. 2024), as it enables the rapid development of interactive and user-friendly applications with minimal coding. In this study, the TC prediction tool was developed using the Streamlit framework based on the optimized stacking model. The prediction system was primarily implemented in Python 3.10 and utilized key libraries including scikit-learn (v1.2.2), pandas (v1.5.3), numpy (v1.24.2), joblib (v1.2.0), SHAP (v0.41.0), and Streamlit (v1.24.0).

Results

Model hyperparameter optimization & evaluation

The optimal hyperparameters for the base learner and the meta-learner were identified using grid search with 5-fold cross-validation (Table 3). In the five-fold cross-validation, the stacking model obtained an average accuracy of 0.9106 and AUC of 0.9814 Fig. 3. On the independent testing set, the stacking model reached accuracy (96.52%), and AUC (0.9921), outperforming all base learners (Figs. 4, 5 and 6A). Furthermore, the confusion matrix confirmed its strong predictive capability (Fig. 5). In addition to discrimination performance, model calibration analysis was assessed using quartile-based calibration curves (n_bins = 4) and Brier scores. The optimized stacking ensemble model achieved the lowest Brier score of 0.038, demonstrating that the predicted probabilities were generally reliable (Fig. 6B). Overall, the results indicated that the proposed stacking ensemble framework effectively combined the complementary strengths of heterogeneous learners, resulting in improved predictive performance.

Model interpretation

To reveal the internal decision-making mechanism of the optimized stacking model, we adopted the SHAP method to conduct model interpretability analysis. In terms of the model global interpretability, Shap bar plot showed the importance of each feature by mean absolute SHAP value in descending order (Fig. 7A). The top five factors contributing to TC recurrence were treatment response, age, N-stage, risk stratification, and adenopathy. The SHAP beeswarm plot further revealed that poor treatment response, older age, advanced N-stage, higher risk stratification, advanced T-stage and higher clinical stage, were positively associated with TC recurrence (Fig. 7B).

Global interpretability of the stacking model. A(SHAP bar plot); B(SHAP beeswarm plot).

In addition, the SHAP waterfall plot was used to provide local interpretation for specific individual patient. Figure 8A illustrated a patient predicted to have a high risk of TC recurrence, with base value of 0.265 and final prediction value of 0.906 due to positive push of treatment response (structural incomplete), N-stage (N1a), stage (III), age (56), T-stage (T3b), physical examination (diffuse goiter) toward the “recurrence” class (Fig. 8A; Table 1). In the case, although some features had minor effects in the opposite direction, e.g., pathology (hurthel cell), adenopathy (extensive), risk stratification (low), their influence was insufficient to counterbalance the high-risk indicators.

local interpretability of stacking model. A (SHAP waterfall plot of patient with TC recurrence having high risk); B(SHAP waterfall plot of patient with TC recurrence having low risk).

In contrast, Fig. 8B presented a patient predicted to have a low recurrence risk with base value of 0.265 and final prediction value of 0.071. Likewise, the waterfall plot indicated that favorable factors, including treatment response (excellent), risk stratification (low), adenopathy (posterior), age (38), T-stage (T2), mainly contributed to the decision in favor of the “non-recurrence” class (Fig. 7B; Table 1). Although minor risk features, such as stage (IVB), physical examination (multinodular goiter), N-stage (N1a), Focality (multi-focal), were present, their influence was outweighed by protective factors.

Discussion

In this study, we presented a novel stacking ensemble framework for predicting TC recurrence, integrating three heterogeneous base learners (SGD, ET, and DT) with XGBoost as the meta learner. Overall, the proposed model achieved better performance with accuracy (96.52%) and AUC (0.9921) on the independent testing set, demonstrating strong generalizability and stability. To enhance the model’s interpretability, the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) method was applied to systematically reveal the model’s internal decision-making process. Finally, we translated the optimized framework into a user-friendly prediction tool, providing explainable insights at both population and patient-specific levels for clinical practice.

Most previous studies employed models such as Random Forest (RF), Support Vector Machines (SVM), which achieved high predictive performance but offered limited interpretability (Alam et al. 2024; Clark et al. 2024; Kim et al. 2021b; Książek 2024; Lixandru-Petre et al. 2025; Mourad et al. 2020; Ozturk et al. 2024; Schindele et al. 2025; Shrestha et al. 2025). In contrast, our framework offered two important improvements. On the one hand, the stacking framework was designed and effectively combines diverse decision patterns from heterogeneous learners. The experiments demonstrated that the stacking model consistently outperformed the individual base learners, both in terms of AUC on the training set and testing set (Figs. 3 and 6). The result aligned with established findings in ensemble learning, which suggest that combining diverse learners enhances stability and predictive power (Sagi and Rokach 2018). Specifically, ET captured complex nonlinear relationships via randomized ensembles, DT contributed rule-based decision structures, and SGD ensured efficient optimization and robust generalization in high-dimensional feature spaces. Integrating these diverse outputs, the XGBoost (meta-learner) provided an adaptive aggregation mechanism that enhanced both stability and predictive performance. Notably, the success of the stacking ensemble framework depends on several factors, including base learner diversity, dataset size, and meta learner selection. Overall, these results indicated that the framework was well suited to the characteristics of the dataset and the task. On the other hand, the framework provided interpretable and clinically actionable predictions, an aspect that has been rarely addressed in previous TC recurrence studies. Specifically, we employed SHAP (Lundberg et al. 2020a; Lundberg and Lee 2017) to comprehensively interpret the model’s output. Unlike local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIME) (Biecek and Burzykowski 2021; Linardatos et al. 2020; Ribeiro et al. 2016), which primarily focus on local explanations and may suffer from instability due to random perturbations, SHAP provides consistent and theoretically grounded attributions at both the local and global levels (Roshinta and Gábor 2024). Consequently, the global SHAP analysis identified the top five contributing factors associated with TC recurrence, namely, treatment response, age, N-stage, risk stratification, and adenopathy (Fig. 7). The local SHAP analysis further helped to understand patient-specific risk factors by showing how each feature contributed to the predicted probability of TC recurrence (Table 1; Fig. 8).

The findings are consistent with previous studies. Specifically, treatment response has been shown to provide superior prognostic information and predict recurrence more accurately than initial risk estimates (Giannoula et al. 2020; Sung et al. 2017; Tuttle et al. 2010). Likewise, risk stratification, which integrates multiple clinical and therapeutic factors, has been reported to offer a more precise risk assessment than traditional staging alone (Pitoia et al. 2013; Tuttle et al. 2010; Tuttle et al., 2019). Age has also been consistently identified as a significant predictor, with older patients demonstrating higher recurrence risk and poorer responses to therapy (Hollenbeak et al. 2013; Shah and Boucai 2018; Trimboli et al. 2020) (Shah et al., 2018; Trimboli et al. 2020; Hollenbeak et al. 2013). Furthermore, N-stage and adenopathy remain important indicators of nodal involvement and tumor burden, contributing to recurrence risk (Shaha et al., 2012; Giannoula et al. 2020). The consistency between SHAP-identified features and the prior findings demonstrated that the proposed stacking framework captured clinically relevant signals while providing interpretable predictions.

Taken together, three key contributions can be highlighted in this work. First, unlike most prior studies that relied on single classifiers with limited interpretability, the proposed stacking framework effectively leveraged complementary decision patterns across heterogeneous learners, yielding better performance. Second, we incorporated SHAP to provide transparent and consistent explanations, overcoming the “black-box” limitation that restricts the clinical trust of many machine learning models. Third, the deployment of the framework as an interactive web tool bridged the gap between methodological development and clinical utility, thereby supporting informed clinical decision-making. In brief, the contribution above highlight the novelty of the work in achieving both high performance and clinical interpretability, which are essential for real-world clinical translation.

Overall, the work revealed that the proposed stacking framework achieved better performance while providing interpretable insights consistent with established clinical knowledge. Furthermore, we developed an innovative web tool for TC recurrence risk prediction with explainable machine learning technique, accessible online at https://tccheck-prediction-tool.streamlit.app/. Although intended primarily as a proof-of-concept, it emphasizes the value of interpretable stacking model in helping clinicians understand individual risk factors and supporting clinical decision-making.

Limitation

Despite the proposed stacking model demonstrated high predictive performance for TC recurrence (e.g., 96.52% accuracy and 0.9921 AUC), several limitations should be acknowledged. (1) The study was mainly based on retrospective data from a single public database, which may limit generalizability across multicenter cohorts. (2) The model was currently restricted to a binary classification of recurrence (yes/no) and cannot provide dynamic prediction of when recurrence might occur. Additionally, the framework currently relied on structured clinical and pathological variables, without integrating other potentially informative modalities such as imaging or genomic data, which may constrain its clinical applicability for more comprehensive aspects. (3) Although the user-friendly prediction tool was developed based on optimized model with potential clinical utility, it has not yet undergone prospective validation in real-world clinical settings. Moreover, despite employing the SHAP method to enhance interpretability, it could not fully reveal the deep-rooted clinicopathological mechanisms, and thus clinical decisions still require expert judgment.

Future research could focus on expanding to multi-center cohorts, integrate multi-modal data such as imaging, genomics, and molecular markers, develop time-to-event prediction models, and conduct prospective validation to further improve the generalizability and clinical applicability of the framework across diverse populations and settings.

Conclusion

In this study, we proposed a novel stacking ensemble learning framework for predicting TC recurrence, integrating heterogeneous learners and employing SHAP to enhance model interpretability. Top 5 factors concerning TC recurrence were treatment response, age, N-stage, risk stratification, and adenopathy. An online prediction tool was also developed, providing a practical aid for personalized recurrence risk assessment. The work presented a innovative approach for TC recurrence prediction, offering valuable reference for recurrence prediction in oncology.

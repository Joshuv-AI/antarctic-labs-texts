Abstract




Abstract.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
\frac{1}{q}=1-\alpha
. We first obtain that the function
\omega:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
belongs to weight class of
\mathcal{A}(1,q)(\mathbb{Z})
if and only if discrete fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
or discrete Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
l_{\omega}^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
to
l_{\omega^{q}}^{q,weak}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then for
p=\frac{1}{\alpha}
, we further obtain that the function
\omega
belongs to weight class of
\mathcal{A}(p,\infty)(\mathbb{Z})
if and only if discrete Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}
has a property resembling discrete bounded mean oscillation. Moreover, we give another simple proof of
I_{\alpha}:l_{\omega^{p}}^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow l_{\omega^{q}}^{q}(\mathbb{Z})
for
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q)(\mathbb{Z})
,
1<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha
. As applications, more weighted norm inequalities for
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
are established when
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(1,q)(\mathbb{Z})
or
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,\infty)(\mathbb{Z})
, and some of them are new even in continuous setting.

1
Introduction

Let
0<\alpha<1
. Define Riesz potential
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{f(y)|x-y|^{\alpha-n}dy}
and fractional maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}f(x):=\underset{Q}{\mathrm{sup}}\ |Q|^{-1+{{\alpha}/{n}}}\int_{Q}{|f(y)|dy}
, where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
with center at
x
and
|Q|
denotes the measure of
Q
. Suppose that
1<p,q<\infty
and
\omega(x)
is a nonnegative locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Define
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q)
, if

\underset{Q}{\mathrm{sup}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}{\omega}(x)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}{\omega}(x)^{-p\prime}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p\prime}}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p^{\prime}:={p}/(p-1)
. In 1974, Muckenhoupt and Wheeden
[
15
]
characterized the weighted strong type inequality for fractional maximal operator and Riesz potential in terms of weight class of
\mathcal{A}(p,q)
, where
1<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha
. They showed that the inequality

\left\|T_{\alpha}f\right\|_{L_{\omega^{q}}^{q}}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L_{\omega^{p}}^{p}},

where
T_{\alpha}
is the operator
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
or
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
, holds if and only if
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q)
. Moreover, in 1975, Welland
[
20
]
gave a simple proof of weighted strong type inequality for Riesz potential in terms of
\mathcal{A}(p,q)
, and also gave certain results for
p=1
and
q=\infty
.

It is well known that fractional maximal operator and Riesz potential play important roles in various branches of analysis, including potential theory, harmonic analysis, Sobolev spaces, partial differential equations and can be treated as special singular integrals. That is why it is important to study their boundedness between different spaces; see
[
1
,
4
,
7
,
12
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
.

Modern discrete analogues in harmonic analysis developed out of work of Jean Bourgain in the late 1980s and early 1990s, and Eli Stein and his longterm collaborators continuing into this century. For example, the study of boundedness of discrete operators on
l^{p}
analogues for
L^{p}
-boundedness has been considered by some authors; see
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
13
,
16
]
.

The aim of this article is to obtain some weighted estimates for discrete fractional maximal operator and discrete Riesz potential. Let begin with some related definitions.

Let
S_{m,N}:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:|k-m|\leq N\}
,

,

. Then

-

.

be a sequence. The

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we mainly recall discrete Muckenhoupt weight classes and give some related properties.

Definition 2.1
.

[
10
, Definition 2.7]

A discrete weight
\omega:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is said to belong to the
discrete Muckenhoupt class

\mathcal{A}_{1}:=\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
if

\left\|\omega\right\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}:=\underset{m\in\mathbb{Z},N\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathrm{sup}}\,\,\frac{1}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\underset{k\in S_{m,N}}{\mathrm{inf}}\,\omega\left(k\right)}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}{\omega\left(k\right)}\Bigg)<\infty.

For
1<p<\infty
, a discrete weight
\omega:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is said to belong to the
discrete Muckenhoupt class

\mathcal{A}_{p}:=\mathcal{A}_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
if

\left\|\omega\right\|_{\mathcal{A}_{p}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}:=\underset{m\in\mathbb{Z},N\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathrm{sup}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}{\omega\left(k\right)}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}{\omega\left(k\right)^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}}\bigg)^{p-1}<\infty.

Define
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{1\leq p<\infty}{\mathcal{A}_{p}}
.

The following definition is a discrete variant of Muckenhoupt
\mathcal{A}(p,q)
class (see
[
15
, Page 261]
), which is also introduced in
[
11
, Definition 2.6]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A discrete weight
\omega:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is said to belong to
\mathcal{A}(p,q)
on
\mathbb{Z}
for
1<p,q<\infty
if

\left\|\omega\right\|_{\mathcal{A}\left(p,q\right)\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}:=\underset{m\in\mathbb{Z},N\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathrm{sup}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}{\omega\left(k\right)^{q}}\bigg)^{\small{\frac{1}{q}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}{\omega\left(k\right)^{-p^{\prime}}}\bigg)^{\small{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}<\infty.

Particularly, when
p=1
,

\left\|\omega\right\|_{\mathcal{A}\left(1,q\right)\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}:=\underset{m\in\mathbb{Z},N\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathrm{sup}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}{\omega\left(k\right)^{q}}\bigg)^{\small{\frac{1}{q}}}\bigg(\underset{k\in S_{m,N}}{\mathrm{sup}}\,\,\frac{1}{\omega\left(k\right)}\bigg)<\infty.

When
q=\infty
,

\left\|\omega\right\|_{\mathcal{A}\left(p,\infty\right)\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}:=\underset{m\in\mathbb{Z},N\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathrm{sup}}\bigg(\underset{k\in S_{m,N}}{\mathrm{sup}}\,\,\omega\left(k\right)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}{\omega\left(k\right)^{-p^{\prime}}}\bigg)^{\small{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}<\infty.

.

3
The endpoint estimates of discrete Riesz potential on discrete weighted Lebesgue spaces

In this section, we discuss the sufficient and necessary results of discrete fractional maximal operator and discrete Riesz potential on discrete weighted Lebesgue spaces at the cases
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(1,q)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,\infty)
.

Definition 3.1
.

[

, Definition 2.1]

Let

and
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a sequence. The
noncentral discrete fractional maximal operator

\overline{M}_{\alpha}
is defined by

\overline{M}_{\alpha}x\left(m\right):=\underset{J\ni m}{\mathrm{sup}}\ \frac{1}{\left|J\right|^{1-\alpha}}\sum_{k\in J}{\left|x\left(k\right)\right|},\kern 5.0ptm\in\mathbb{Z},

where

is any interval of finite consecutive integers.

The following Theorem
3.2
is a sufficient and necessary result for weight class of
\mathcal{A}(1,q)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
\frac{1}{q}=1-\alpha
. Then
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(1,q)
if and only if
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
l_{\omega}^{1}
to
l_{\omega^{q}}^{q,weak}
, i.e., there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for every
\lambda>0
,

\left(\sum_{\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:M_{\alpha}x(k)>\lambda\}}{\omega}(k)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left|x(k)\right|}\omega(k).

(3.1)

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
11
, Remark 3.2]

Let
x=\{x(m)\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a sequence and
0\leq\alpha<1
. Then
M_{\alpha}x(m)\sim\overline{M}_{\alpha}x(m)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
5
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
A
be a bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each
x\in A
, a closed cubic interval
Q(x)
centered at
x
is given. Then one can choose, from among the given intervals
\{Q(x)\}_{x\in A}
, a sequence
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
(possible finite) such that:

(i)

The set
A
is covered by the sequence, i.e.,
A\subset\bigcup_{k}Q_{k}
;

(ii)

No point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is in more than
C_{n}
(a number that only depends on
n
) cubes of the sequence
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, i.e., for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\sum_{k}{\mathcal{X}_{Q_{k}}(x)\leq C_{n}}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of necessity. By referencing the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 3.4]
in the case of
1<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}
, let us now verify that
[
11
, Lemma 3.4]
also holds true in the case of
p=1
. For every
\lambda>0
,
M\in\mathbb{Z_{+}}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define

\displaystyle S_{k,N}=\mathbb{Z}\cap Q_{k,N},\kern 5.0pt\mathrm{where}\kern 5.0ptQ_{k,N}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}:\left|y-k\right|\leq N\};

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:M_{\alpha}x(k)>\lambda\};

\displaystyle E_{\lambda,M}:=E_{\lambda}\cap S_{0,M},\kern 5.0pt\mathrm{where}\kern 5.0ptS_{0,M}:=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:\left|m-0\right|\leq M\}.

Thus, for every
k\in E_{\lambda,M}
, by the definition of

, there exists a

such that

\left|S_{k,N_{k}}\right|^{-1+\alpha}\sum_{m\in S_{k,N_{k}}}\left|x(m)\right|>\lambda.

(3.2)

Since

. Then we further have

E_{\lambda,M}\subset\bigg(\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{j}Q_{k_{j},N_{j}}\bigg)\bigcap\mathbb{Z}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{j}S_{k_{j},N_{j}},

(3.3)

\sum\limits_{j}\mathcal{X}_{S_{k_{j},N_{j}}}(k)\leq\sum\limits_{j}\mathcal{X}_{Q_{k_{j},N_{j}}}(k)\leq C_{1}.

(3.4)

From (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.4
),
\frac{1}{q}<1
,

and discrete Hölder’s inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{m\in E_{\lambda,M}}\omega(m)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{j}\sum\limits_{m\in S_{k_{j},N_{j}}}\omega(m)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\sum\limits_{j}\left(\sum\limits_{m\in S_{k_{j},N_{j}}}\omega(m)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{m\in S_{k_{j},N_{j}}}\omega(m)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda\left|S_{k_{j},N_{j}}\right|^{1-\alpha}}\sum_{m\in S_{k_{j},N_{j}}}\left|x(m)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j}{\left(\sum_{m\in S_{k_{j},N_{j}}}{\omega\left(m\right)^{q}}\right)^{\small{\frac{1}{q}}}}\lambda^{-1}\left|S_{k_{j},N_{j}}\right|^{-\small{\frac{1}{q}}}\left(\sum_{m\in S_{k_{j},N_{j}}}{\left|x\left(m\right)\right|\omega\left(m\right)}\right)\left(\underset{m\in S_{k_{j},N_{j}}}{\mathrm{sup}}\frac{1}{\omega\left(m\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\left\|\omega\right\|_{\mathcal{A}\left(1,q\right)\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}\sum_{j}{\sum_{m\in S_{k_{j},N_{j}}}{\left|x\left(m\right)\right|\omega\left(m\right)}}\leq C_{1}\lambda^{-1}\left\|\omega\right\|_{\mathcal{A}\left(1,q\right)\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left|x\left(m\right)\omega\left(m\right)\right|},

and letting

on both sides of above inequality, we obtain (

).

Proof of sufficiency. If
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
l_{\omega}^{1}
to

. Then

. Here, we only consider the case that

is not a single point set. If

(3.5)

Let

. For every

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:\overline{M}_{\alpha}x(k)>\lambda\right\}}{\omega\left(k\right)^{q}}\right)^{\small{\frac{1}{q}}}\leq C\left|S\right|^{1-\alpha}\left|E\right|^{-1}\sum_{k\in E}{\omega\left(k\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left|S\right|^{1-\alpha}\left|E\right|^{-1}\left|E\right|\left(A+\epsilon\right)=C\left|S\right|^{\small{\frac{1}{q}}}\left(A+\epsilon\right).

Hence by the arbitrariness of

which implies that

. We finish the proof of Theorem

.
∎

.

(3.9)

then
B|S|(\sum_{i\in T}{|m-i|^{(\alpha-2)p^{\prime}}\omega(i)^{-p^{\prime}}})^{\small{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}

Proof of sufficiency. Let us prove this by two steps.

Step 1: Let us prove that there is an integer

(3.11)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{1}^{N+1}{y^{\alpha-1}}dy=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left[\left(N+1\right)^{\alpha}-1\right].

Step 2: Let us prove

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\left|S\right|}\sum_{i\in S}{x\left(i\right)\bigg(\sum_{k\in S\setminus\left\{i\right\}}{\frac{1}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}-\frac{2}{t}\sum_{k\in tS\setminus\left\{i\right\}}{\frac{1}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}}}\bigg)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(2+4t\right)\left(\underset{k\in S}{\mathrm{sup}}\ \omega\left(k\right)\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left|x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right|^{p}}\right)^{\small{\frac{1}{p}}}.

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C_{t}\left(\underset{k\in S}{\mathrm{sup}}\ \omega\left(k\right)\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_{k\in S}{\left|w\left(k\right)^{-p^{\prime}}\omega\left(k\right)\right|^{p}}\right)^{\small{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Theorem 4.3
.

,

~~0~~,\quad 0\leq t\leq 1\\

(4.4)

(4.5)

Let

,

).
∎

The boundedness of
I_{\alpha}
from
l_{\omega^{p}}^{p}
to
l_{\omega^{q}}^{q}
is proved by
[
11
, Theorem 4.4(i)]
for
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q)
, but the related proof is complicated, and now, inspired by
[
20
, Theorem 1]
, we can give another simple proof. Moreover, there is a gap in the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 4.4(ii)]
, precisely,
\|I_{\alpha}x\|_{l_{\omega^{q}}^{q}}\sim\|M_{\alpha}x\|_{l_{\omega^{q}}^{q}}
is wrong for any
x\in l_{\omega^{p}}^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
. We will correct the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 4.4(ii)]
in the following proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of (i). Let
R>0
be a fixed real number. Then

I_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)=\sum_{\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:0<\left|k-i\right|\leq R\right\}}{\frac{x\left(i\right)}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}}+\sum_{\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:\left|k-i\right|>R\right\}}{\frac{x\left(i\right)}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}}=:\mathrm{I+II}.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,\mathrm{min}\{\alpha,1-\alpha\})
. We have

\left|\mathrm{I}\right|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\sum_{2^{-j-1}R<\left|k-i\right|\leq 2^{-j}R}{\frac{\left|x\left(i\right)\right|}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\left(2^{-j-1}R\right)^{\alpha-1}}\sum_{2^{-j-1}R<\left|k-i\right|\leq 2^{-j}R}{\left|x\left(i\right)\right|}.

We only consider the case
2^{-j}R>\frac{1}{2}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, or else
\{2^{-j-1}R<|k-i|\leq 2^{-j}R\}=\emptyset
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we further have

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{I}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\left(2^{-j-1}R\right)^{\alpha-1}\left[3\left(2^{-j+1}R\right)\right]^{1-\alpha+\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\left(2\lfloor 2^{-j}R\rfloor+1\right)^{1-\alpha+\epsilon}}\sum_{\left|k-i\right|\leq\lfloor 2^{-j}R\rfloor}{\left|x\left(i\right)\right|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{12^{1-\alpha+\epsilon}}{2^{\epsilon}-1}R^{\epsilon}M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x\left(k\right).

Similarly, we only consider the case
2^{j}R>\frac{1}{2}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{II}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\sum_{2^{j-1}R<\left|k-i\right|\leq 2^{j}R}{\frac{\left|x\left(i\right)\right|}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\left(2^{j-1}R\right)^{\alpha-1}}\sum_{2^{j-1}R<\left|k-i\right|\leq 2^{j}R}{\left|x\left(i\right)\right|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\left(2^{j-1}R\right)^{\alpha-1}\left[3\left(2^{j+1}R\right)\right]^{1-\alpha-\epsilon}\frac{1}{\left(2\lfloor 2^{j}R\rfloor+1\right)^{1-\alpha-\epsilon}}}\sum_{\left|k-i\right|\leq\lfloor 2^{j}R\rfloor}{\left|x\left(i\right)\right|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{12^{1-\alpha-\epsilon}}{1-2^{-\epsilon}}R^{-\epsilon}M_{\alpha+\epsilon}x\left(k\right).

Therefore,

\left|I_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)\right|\leq\frac{12^{1-\alpha+\epsilon}}{2^{\epsilon}-1}R^{\epsilon}M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x\left(k\right)+\frac{12^{1-\alpha-\epsilon}}{1-2^{-\epsilon}}R^{-\epsilon}M_{\alpha+\epsilon}x\left(k\right).

Let
C_{\alpha}:=\max\left\{\frac{12^{1-\alpha+\epsilon}}{2^{\epsilon}-1},\frac{12^{1-\alpha-\epsilon}}{1-2^{-\epsilon}}\right\}
. By choosing
R^{\epsilon}=(\frac{M_{\alpha+\epsilon}x(k)}{M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x(k)})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have

\left|I_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)\right|\leq C_{\alpha}\left[M_{\alpha+\epsilon}x\left(k\right)M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x\left(k\right)\right]^{\small{\frac{1}{2}}}.

(4.8)

Let
\frac{1}{q_{\epsilon}}=\frac{1}{p}-(\alpha+\epsilon)
and
\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}_{\epsilon}}=\frac{1}{p}-(\alpha-\epsilon)
. If
\epsilon
is small enough, by discrete reverse Hölder’s inequality, then
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q)
implies
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q_{\epsilon})
; by discrete Hölder’s inequality, then
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q)
implies
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,\widetilde{q}_{\epsilon})
. Let
p_{1}:=\frac{2q_{\epsilon}}{q}>1
and
p_{2}:=\frac{2\widetilde{q}_{\epsilon}}{q}>1
. Then

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{q}{2q_{\epsilon}}+\frac{q}{2\widetilde{q}_{\epsilon}}=q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\alpha\right)=1,\kern 5.0pt\frac{qp_{1}}{2}=q_{\epsilon}\kern 5.0pt\mathrm{and}\kern 5.0pt\frac{qp_{2}}{2}=\widetilde{q}_{\epsilon}.

From this, (
4.8
), discrete Hölder’s inequality and applying (
1.1
) for the operators
M_{\alpha+\epsilon}
and
M_{\alpha-\epsilon}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left|I_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right|^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left[M_{\alpha+\epsilon}x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right]^{\small{\frac{q}{2}}}}\left[M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right]^{\small{\frac{q}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left[M_{\alpha+\epsilon}x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right]^{\small{\frac{qp_{1}}{2}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left[M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right]^{\small{\frac{qp_{2}}{2}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left[M_{\alpha+\epsilon}x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right]^{q_{\epsilon}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{\epsilon}}\frac{q_{\epsilon}}{p_{1}}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left[M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right]^{\widetilde{q}_{\epsilon}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}_{\epsilon}}\frac{\widetilde{q}_{\epsilon}}{p_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left|x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right|^{p}}\right)^{\frac{q_{\epsilon}}{pp_{1}}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left|x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right|^{p}}\right)^{\frac{\widetilde{q}_{\epsilon}}{pp_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left|x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right|^{p}}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Proof of (ii). Let
S:=S_{m,N}
,
x(k):=\omega(k)^{-p^{\prime}}\mathcal{X}_{S}(k)
and
2\lambda=|S|^{\alpha-1}\sum_{k\in S}{|x(k)|}
. For every
k\in S
, we have
\overline{M}_{\alpha}x(k)\geq 2\lambda>\lambda
and hence
S\subset\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:\overline{M}_{\alpha}x(k)>\lambda\}
. If
\omega
is a discrete weighted sequence which is monotonic on
\mathrm{supp}\,\omega
, then
|x|
is a monotonic sequence on
\mathrm{supp}\,x
. Then by Lemma
3.3
, Lemma
3.6
and that
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
l_{\omega^{p}}^{p}
to
l_{\omega^{q}}^{q}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\sum_{k\in S}{\omega\left(k\right)^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\sum_{\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:\overline{M}_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)>\lambda\right\}}{\omega\left(k\right)^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left|\overline{M}_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)\right|^{q}}\omega\left(k\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left|I_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)\right|}^{q}\omega\left(k\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\left|x\left(k\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(k\right)^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha
and taking
x(k):=\omega(k)^{-p^{\prime}}\mathcal{X}_{S}(k)
and
2\lambda=|S|^{\alpha-1}\sum_{k\in S}{|x(k)|}
into the above inequality, we further obtain

\left(\frac{1}{\left|S\right|}\sum_{k\in S}{\omega\left(k\right)^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|S\right|}\sum_{k\in S}{\omega\left(k\right)^{-p^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq C,

which implies
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q)
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The upper pointwise estimate (
4.8
) of
I_{\alpha}
related to
M_{\alpha}
plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.4
. In fact, (
4.8
) has a more general upper pointwise estimate. Let
0\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha<\alpha_{2}<1
. Repeating the estimate of (
4.8
) with
\alpha-\epsilon=\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha+\epsilon=\alpha_{2}
, we obtain

\left|I_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)\right|\leq C\left[M_{\alpha_{1}}x\left(k\right)\right]^{\frac{\alpha_{2}-\alpha}{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}}\left[M_{\alpha_{2}}x\left(k\right)\right]^{\frac{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}},\kern 5.0ptk\in\mathbb{Z}.

The following Theorem
4.5
is a discrete variant of
[
20
, Theorem 2]
, which is an interesting estimate.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
p=\frac{1}{\alpha}
,
\delta>0
,
q\in(\max\{p,p^{\prime}\},\infty)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,\infty)
. If
x
is supported in some symmetric interval
S_{m,N}\subset\mathbb{Z}
,
N\in\mathbb{Z_{+}}
and

\left\|x\right\|_{l_{\omega^{p}}^{p}}\leq\underset{k\in S_{m,N}}{\min}\omega\left(k\right).

(4.9)

Then there exists a positive constant
C=C_{\delta,q,\alpha}
such that

\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}{\exp}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left|\left|I_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)\right|-\delta\right|^{p^{\prime}}\right]\omega\left(k\right)^{q}\leq CN\left\|x\right\|_{l_{\omega^{p}}^{p}}^{q}.

Proof.

Let
S:=S_{m,N}
. For given
q\in(\max\{p,p^{\prime}\},\infty)
, define
\epsilon:=\frac{1}{q}
, where
\epsilon\in(0,\mathrm{min}\{\alpha,1-\alpha\})
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-(\alpha-\epsilon)
. Then for
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,\infty)
, by the definition of
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q)
, we have
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q)
. Let
R\in(0,2N]
to be fixed later. By checking the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we have
I_{\alpha}x(k)=:\mathrm{I+II}
and

|\mathrm{I}|\leq C_{\alpha}R^{\epsilon}M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x\left(k\right).

For
k\in S
, by
\mathrm{supp}\,x\subset S
, discrete Hölder’s inequality and
(\alpha-1)p^{\prime}=-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{II}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:R<\left|k-i\right|\leq 2N\right\}}{\frac{\left|x\left(i\right)\right|}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\underset{i\in S}{\mathrm{sup}}\,\,\frac{1}{\omega\left(i\right)}\right)\left(\sum_{\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:R<\left|k-i\right|\leq 2N\right\}}{\left|x\left(i\right)\omega\left(i\right)\right|^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:R<\left|k-i\right|\leq 2N\right\}}{\left|k-i\right|^{\left(\alpha-1\right)p^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\underset{i\in S}{\mathrm{sup}}\,\,\frac{1}{\omega\left(i\right)}\right)\left\|x\right\|_{l_{\omega^{p}}^{p}}\left(2\sum_{\left\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:R<j\leq 2N\right\}}{j^{\left(\alpha-1\right)p^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(2\sum_{\left\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:R<j\leq 2N\right\}}{j^{-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq\left(2\int_{R}^{2N}{y^{-1}dy}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq\left[2\log^{+}\left(\frac{2N}{R}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

For
\delta>0
, choose
R^{\epsilon}=\min\left\{\delta[C_{\alpha}M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x(k)]^{-1},\left(2N\right)^{\epsilon}\right\}
. Noting that
\frac{1}{\epsilon}=q
, then

\left|I_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)\right|\leq\delta+\left\{2\log^{+}\left[2N\delta^{-q}{C_{\alpha}}^{q}\left(M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x\left(k\right)\right)^{q}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Therefore,

\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\left|\left|I_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)\right|-\delta\right|^{p^{\prime}}\right]\leq 2N\delta^{-q}{C_{\alpha}}^{q}\left(M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x\left(k\right)\right)^{q}.

(4.10)

Multiply both sides of (
4.10
) by
\omega(k)^{q}
, sum both sides on
k\in S
and apply (
1.1
) for the operator
M_{\alpha-\epsilon}
with
\omega\in\mathcal{A}(p,q)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in S}{\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\left|\left|I_{\alpha}x\left(k\right)\right|-\delta\right|^{p^{\prime}}\right]}\omega\left(k\right)^{q}\leq 2N\delta^{-q}{C_{\alpha}}^{q}\sum_{k\in S}{\left|M_{\alpha-\epsilon}x\left(k\right)\right|^{q}}\omega\left(k\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CN\left(\sum_{k\in S}{\left|x\left(k\right)\omega\left(k\right)\right|^{p}}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\leq CN\left\|x\right\|_{l_{\omega^{p}}^{p}}^{q}.

We finish the proof of Theorem
4.5
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The condition (
4.9
) in Theorem
4.5

\widetilde{x}(k):=(\underset{k\in S_{m,N}}{\min}\omega(k))x(k)\|x\|_{l_{\omega^{p}}^{p}}^{-1}

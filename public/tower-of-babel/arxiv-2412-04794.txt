Introduction and main results

This article investigates the multiplicity of solutions to a class of semilinear degenerate elliptic equations. In particular, we study

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta_{\lambda}u=\mu g(z)|u|^{r-1}u+h(z)|u|^{s-1}u\;\text{in}\;\Omega\\
u\in H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)\end{array}\right\}

where
\Delta_{\lambda}u=\Delta_{x}u+|x|^{2\lambda}\Delta_{y}u
is the Grushin Laplacian,
z=(x,y)\in\Omega
, with
N=n+m;\,n,\,m\geq 1,\,\lambda>0
,
0\leq r<1<s<2^{*}_{\lambda}-1
and
\mu
is a positive parameter. The bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is smooth, and the weight functions
g,\,h
may change sign. When
\lambda=0
, the operator reduces to the usual Laplacian operator. This operator was first introduced by Baouendi in
[
6
]
and later studied by Grushin
[
20
,
21
]
. The hypoellipticity properties (see
[
22
]
) of
\Delta_{\lambda}
were analyzed by Grushin in
[
20
]
. This operator arises naturally in the context of partial differential equations on manifolds. In
[
23
, Note 2]
, Kagoj-Lanconelli demonstrated that the Grushin operator also appears in the analysis of partial differential equations in the Heisenberg group. In addition, some connections between this operator and the PDEs in hyperbolic spaces have been explored in
[
9
]
. Since the weight
|x|^{2\lambda}
appears in the definition of the Grushin operator, the operator fails to be uniformly elliptic on domains intersecting the plane
\{x=0\}
. We always assume that

\Omega\cap\{z=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x=0\}\neq\emptyset.

In
[
18
]
, Franchi-Lanconelli obtained several regularity and embedding results for a class of operators, including Grushin. Monticelli
[
27
]
established a maximum principle, Hopf’s lemma, and qualitative properties of solutions for degenerate equations, including the Grushin operator. Recently, a Berestycki-Lions type result has been proved by Alves et al. in
[
2
]
. Very recently, Alves et al.
[
3
]
investigated the existence result of the Brezis-Nirenberg type problem for this setting. More precisely, when
r\in[1,2^{*}_{\lambda})
, they obtained the existence of a weak solution to problem (
1
) with
s=2^{*}_{\lambda}-1
under various conditions on the dimension and the parameter. Nevertheless, the equation has not yet been studied for the case
r\in[0,1)
, even for the subcritical case. This article addresses these gaps and discusses several multiplicity results for small parameter values
\mu>0
. For the Grushin operator, however, multiplicity phenomena of convex-concave type remain comparatively less understood, although several existence results may be found in
[
5
,
23
]
, and references therein. It should also be noted that for
n\geq 2
, no suitable maximum principle is available due to the effect of degeneracy, so we focus exclusively on nonnegative solutions. However, when
n=1
, a partial maximum principle has been established in
[
5
]
. For more problems related to the Grushin operator, we refer to
[
8
,
16
,
14
,
15
,
25
]
and the references therein.

The motivation behind our study traces back to the classical work of Ambrosetti, Brezis, and Cerami
[
4
]
, who considered the problem

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta u=\mu g(x)u^{r}+h(x)u^{s}\;\text{in}\;\Omega\\
u\geq 0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }u=0\;\text{on}\;\partial\Omega\end{array}\right\}

with
g=h=1
and proved the existence of
\mu_{0}>0
such that equation (
1
) admits at least two positive solutions for every
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
when
0<r<1<s\leq 2^{*}-1
among other results. For the sign-changing functions
g
and
h
, De Figueiredo et al.
[
12
]
obtained multiple solutions by combining a mountain pass construction with local minimization.

In the borderline case where
r=1
and
\int h\phi_{1}^{s+1}<0
, Brown and Zhang
[
11
]
proved the existence of multiple solutions for
\mu_{1}<\mu<\mu_{1}+\delta
, for some
\delta>0
. Here
(\phi_{1},\mu_{1})
is the principal eigenpair of the Dirichlet boundary value problem
-\Delta u=\mu g(x)u
. Their approach utilized the Nehari manifold method in the subcritical setting. Wu
[
30
,
31
]
further investigated the problem and established the existence of at least two solutions to problem (
1
) with a sign-changing weight function
g
and
h\equiv 1
when
0<r<1<s<2^{*}-1
and later under more general hypotheses on
g
and
h
for small
\mu>0.

Related results for quasilinear operators can be found in
[
1
,
10
,
13
]
and the references therein. Interested readers may also find
[
7
,
12
]
useful, which deals with the impact of the concave and convex nonlinearities on the number of positive solutions.
We now mention structural assumptions on the weight functions
g
and

.

(
g_{1}
)

There exists
q\in(r+1,2^{*}_{\lambda})
such that
g\in L^{a}(\Omega)

and

is not identically zero function in
\Omega

(

There exists a

(

)

There exists

Under these hypotheses, our main result reads as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Multiplicity in the sub-critical regime).
Assume
0\leq r<1<s<2^{*}_{\lambda}-1
and that
(g_{1})
,
(h_{1})
hold. Then there exists
\mu^{*}>0
such that for every
\mu\in(0,\mu^{*})
, the problem (
1
) possesses at least two non-trivial, non-negative weak solutions. Moreover, if
n=1,\lambda\geq 1
and

are non-negative, then these solutions are strictly positive.

Motivated by
[
30
,
31
]
, we derive the existence of two solutions to problem (
1
), using the Nehari manifold technique. To this concern, the Nehari manifold is decomposed into three disjoint parts; one of these is empty for small parameter values, while each of the remaining two contains a critical point of the associated energy functional.

For the critical case, we study the equation

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta_{\lambda}u=\mu g|u|^{r-1}u+|u|^{2^{*}_{\lambda}-2}u\;\text{in}\;\Omega\\
u\in H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)\end{array}\right\}

(
E_{\mu}
)

and obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

(Critical convex-concave case).
Let
g\geq 0
satisfy
(g_{1})
and
(g_{2})
. Then there exists
\mu^{*}>0
such that for every
\mu\in(0,\mu^{*})
, the problem (

E
μ

) with
0\leq r<1
admits at least two non-trivial, non-negative weak solutions. Moreover, if
n=1\;,\lambda\geq 1
then the solutions are strictly positive.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we mainly adopt the methods employed in
[
19
,
28
]
. We consider the associated
C^{1}-
functional
I_{\mu}:H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}\ dz-\frac{\mu}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}g\cdot(u^{+})^{r+1}\ dz-\frac{1}{s+1}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{s+1}\ dz,

(1.7)

and observe that every critical point of

is non-negative, where
\nabla_{\lambda}u=(\nabla_{x}u,|x|^{\lambda}\nabla_{y}u)
. We show that the functional

attains its local infimum in a neighborhood of the origin, yielding the first solution, while a second solution arises from the Mountain Pass Theorem. However, the lack of compactness of Palis-Smale sequences makes the analysis more delicate and prevents the direct application of the Mountain Pass Theorem. To overcome this difficulty, we prove that the mountain pass level is strictly less than a threshold
c^{*}
such that the Palais-Smale condition is satisfied for every level strictly smaller than

(see Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.4

2
Preliminaries and variational framework

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N(=n+m)}
be a smooth bounded domain. The Grushin gradient of a function
u
is denoted by
\nabla_{\lambda}u
and defined as
\nabla_{\lambda}u=(\nabla_{x}u,\;|x|^{\lambda}\nabla_{y}u)
, where
\nabla_{x}u
and
\nabla_{y}u
are the usual gradients of the function
u
with respect to
x
and
y
variables, respectively. Now, we define the Hilbert space associated to
\Delta_{\lambda}
that will be used in the sequel

H^{1,\lambda}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):|\nabla_{\lambda}u|\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}

equipped with the norm

\|u\|=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(u^{2}(z)+|\nabla_{\lambda}u(z)|^{2}\right)dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We also define the space
H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)
as closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
H^{1,\lambda}(\Omega)
. Due to
[
26
, Theorem 2.1]
, in the space
H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)
, the above norm is equivalent to

\|u\|_{\lambda}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The gauge norm associated to the Grushin operator is denoted by
\rho
and defined as

\displaystyle\rho(z)=\left(|x|^{2(1+\lambda)}+(1+\lambda)^{2}|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2(1+\lambda)}},

(2.1)

which is homogeneous of degree one concerning the dilation
\delta_{t}(z)=(tx,t^{1+\lambda}y)
, where
z=(x,y)
and
t>0
. The
\rho-
ball with radius
d>0
and center at
z_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is denoted by
B_{d}(z_{1})
and defined as

\displaystyle B_{d}(z_{1}):=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}|\rho(z-z_{1})<d\}.

(2.2)

For more information about the Grushin operator and the Sobolev inequality associated with
\Delta_{\lambda}
, we refer to
[
18
,
23
]
and the references therein. We now state an embedding result, which can be found in
[
23
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a smooth bounded domain. Then the embedding

H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
is continuous for every
p\in[1,2^{*}_{\lambda}]
. Moreover, the above embedding is compact for all
p\in[1,2^{*}_{\lambda})
.

We denote
S_{p}
as the best constant in the embedding
H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
and
define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}=\inf\Big\{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}\,dz:u\in H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega),\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{\lambda}}(\Omega)}=1\Big\},

which is independent of
\Omega
(see
[
3
, Proposition 3.3]
) and is achieved in the case of
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. That is, there exists a non-negative
v\in H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying the following equation:

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\lambda}v=v^{\frac{Q+2}{Q-2}}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

(2.3)

where
Q=n+(1+\lambda)m
is the homogeneous dimension of
\Delta_{\lambda}
. For
\epsilon>0
, let us define

v_{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{\frac{2-Q}{2}}v(\delta_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(z)),

which is also a solution to the above equation (
2.3
). Let us consider the
\rho-
ball
B_{2R}(z_{0})\Subset\Omega\setminus\Sigma
, which is introduced in the condition (
g_{2}
). Choose a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{2R}(z_{0}))
such that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
in
B_{2R}(z_{0})
and
\phi=1
in
B_{R}(z_{0})
. Now, define

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}(z)=\phi(z)v_{\epsilon}(z)\text{ and }w_{\epsilon}=\frac{u_{\epsilon}}{\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2^{*}_{\lambda}}(\Omega)}}}.

(2.4)

To proceed, let us discuss a crucial asymptotic result that will play a significant role in establishing Theorem
1.2
. The result is outlined as follows (see
[
3
]
)

Proposition 2.2
.

As
\epsilon\to 0
, the following conclusions hold:

(i)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u_{\epsilon}|^{2}\,dz\leq\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O(\epsilon^{Q-2}),

(ii)

\int_{\Omega}|u_{\epsilon}|^{2^{*}_{\lambda}}\,dz=\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O(\epsilon^{Q})
,

(iii)

\int_{\Omega}|u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dz\geq\begin{cases}C\epsilon^{2}+O(\epsilon^{Q-2})\text{ if }Q>4\\
C\epsilon^{2}|\mathrm{ln}\epsilon|+O(\epsilon^{2})\text{ if }Q=4\\
C\epsilon^{Q-2}+O(\epsilon^{2})\text{ if }Q<4.\end{cases}

(iv)

For
\frac{2^{*}_{\lambda}}{2}<\gamma<2^{*}_{\lambda}
, we have

\int_{\Omega}|u_{\epsilon}|^{\gamma}\,dz=O(\epsilon^{Q-\frac{\gamma(Q-2)}{2}}).

Proof.

We refer to
[
3
, Proposition 4.2 ]
for the first three conclusions. Since
\frac{2^{*}_{\lambda}}{2}<\gamma<2^{*}_{\lambda}
, we have
0<Q-\gamma\left(\frac{Q-2}{2}\right)
and
v\in L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
Now, using the definition of
u_{\epsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{\epsilon}|^{\gamma}\,dz=\int_{\Omega}|\phi v_{\epsilon}|^{\gamma}\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}|v_{\epsilon}|^{\gamma}\,dz+\int_{B_{2r}\setminus{B_{r}}}|\phi v_{\epsilon}|^{\gamma}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{\frac{\gamma(2-Q)}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|v(\delta_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(z))|^{\gamma}\,dz+\|\phi\|_{\infty}\int_{B_{2r}\setminus{B_{r}}}|v(\delta_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(z))|^{\gamma}\,dz\right)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{\frac{\gamma(2-Q)}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\epsilon}}}|v(\xi)|^{\gamma}\epsilon^{Q}d\xi+\|\phi\|_{\infty}\int_{B_{\frac{2r}{\epsilon}}\setminus{B_{\frac{r}{\epsilon}}}}|v(\xi)|^{\gamma}\epsilon^{Q}d\xi\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{Q-\frac{\gamma(Q-2)}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v(\xi)|^{\gamma}\,d\xi\leq M\epsilon^{Q-\frac{\gamma(Q-2)}{2}}.

Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

Now we give the notion of a weak solution to problem (
1
).

Definition 2.1
.

A function
u\in H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)
is said to be a ’weak solution’ of problem (
1
) if for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
the following identity holds:

\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\lambda}u\cdot\nabla_{\lambda}\phi=\mu\int_{\Omega}g|u|^{r-1}u\phi dz+\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{s-1}u\phi dz.

The functional associated to the given problem (
1
) is denoted by
I_{\mu}:H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
and defined as

I_{\mu}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}dz-\frac{\mu}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}g|u|^{r+1}dz-\frac{1}{s+1}\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{s+1}dz,

which is a
C^{1}
-functional. In order to find solutions to the problem (
1
), we study the existence of critical points of
I_{\mu}
via the Nehari manifold technique. The Nehari manifold corresponding to the functional
I_{\mu}
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
and defined as

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{\mu}:=\{u\in H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\{0\}}:\langle I^{\prime}_{\mu}(u),u\rangle=0\}.

(2.5)

For simplicity, let us define another functional
\tau:H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\tau(u)=\langle I^{\prime}_{\mu}(u),u\rangle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}dz-\mu\int_{\Omega}g|u|^{r+1}dz-\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{s+1}dz.

(2.6)

Therefore,

\langle\tau^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}dz-\mu(r+1)\int_{\Omega}g|u|^{r+1}dz-(s+1)\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{s+1}dz.

We split the Nehari Manifold into three parts,
\mathscr{M}_{\mu}^{+}
,
\mathscr{M}_{\mu}^{-}
and
\mathscr{M}_{\mu}^{0}
such that

\mathscr{M}_{\mu}=\mathscr{M}_{\mu}^{+}\cup\mathscr{M}_{\mu}^{0}\cup\mathscr{M}_{\mu}^{-},

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{+}_{\mu}=\{u\in\mathscr{M}:

\displaystyle\langle\tau^{\prime}(u),u\rangle>0\},\,\mathscr{M}^{-}_{\mu}=\{u\in\mathscr{M}:\langle\tau^{\prime}(u),u\rangle<0\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{0}_{\mu}=\{u\in\mathscr{M}:\langle\tau^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\}.

We also define

m_{\mu}:=\inf_{u\in\mathscr{M}_{\mu}}I_{\mu}(u),\ m^{-}_{\mu}:=\inf_{u\in\mathscr{M}^{-}_{\mu}}I_{\mu}(u)\text{ and }m^{+}_{\mu}:=\inf_{u\in\mathscr{M}^{+}_{\mu}}I_{\mu}(u).

3
Auxiliary results

This section discusses several auxiliary results that play a crucial role in proving our multiplicity result for the subcritical case. Firstly, we show that
\mathscr{M}^{+}_{\mu}
and
\mathscr{M}^{-}_{\mu}
are nonempty. To this aim, we analyze the fibering maps. For
u\in H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)
, we consider the fibering map
F:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,

F(t)=I_{\mu}(tu)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}\,dz-\frac{\mu t^{r+1}}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}g|u|^{r+1}\,dz-\frac{t^{s+1}}{s+1}\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{s+1}dz.

Therefore,

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\langle I^{\prime}_{\mu}(tu),tu\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\tau(tu)=t\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}\ dz-\mu t^{r}\int_{\Omega}g|u|^{1+r}\ dz-t^{s}\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{1+s}\ dz

\displaystyle=t^{r}\left[\left(t^{1-r}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}\ dz-t^{s-r}\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{1+s}\ dz\right)-\mu\int_{\Omega}g|u|^{1+r}\ dz\right].

(3.1)

Now we define a function
G:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

G(t)=t^{1-r}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}\ dz-t^{s-r}\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{1+s}\ dz.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{t^{2}}\langle\tau^{\prime}(tu),tu\rangle-\frac{1}{t^{2}}\tau(tu)=t^{r}G^{\prime}(t)+rt^{r-1}\left(G(t)-\mu\int_{\Omega}g|u|^{1+r}\ dz\right).

(3.2)

The function
G
enjoys the following property.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in H^{1,\lambda}_{0}(\Omega)
be such that
\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{s+1}\;dz>0
. Then there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
G
is increasing on
[0,t_{0}]
and decreasing on
[t_{0},\infty)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(t)=(1-r)t^{-r}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}dz-(s-r)t^{s-r-1}\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{s+1}\ dz\ \text{ for all }t>0.

It is easy to observe that
t_{0}=\left(\frac{(1-r)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\lambda}u|^{2}\ dz}{(s-r)\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{s+1}\ dz}\right)^{\frac{1}{(s-1)}}
is the only critical point of
G
and
G^{\prime}(t)>0
in
(0,t_{0})
and
G^{\prime}(t)<0
in
[t_{0},\infty)
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\,G(t)=G(t_{0})=\left(\frac{1-r}{s-r}\right)^{\frac{1-r}{s-1}}\left(\frac{s-1}{s-r}\right)\frac{\|u\|_{\lambda}^{2(s-r)}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}h|u|^{1+s}\;dz\right)^{\frac{1-r}{s-1}}}.

(3.3)

This proves the Lemma.
∎

So far, we do not know whether the Nehari manifold

is nonempty. The following lemma ensures that

Lemma 3.2

We assume that (

) and

Proof.

(

)

Since

3.1
, there exists unique

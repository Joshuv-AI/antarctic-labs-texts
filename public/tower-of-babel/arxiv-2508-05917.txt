Quasi-Whittaker modules
Cunguang Cheng, Wenting Gao, Shiyuan Liu, Kaiming Zhao, Yueqiang Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, a general setting is proposed to define a class of modules over nonsemisimple Lie algebras 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} induced by a nonperfect ideal 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} . This class of Lie algebras includes many well-known Lie algebras, and some of this class of modules are Whittaker modules and others are not. We call these modules quasi-Whittaker modules. By introducing a new concept: the Whittaker annihilator for universal quasi-Whittaker modules, we are able to determine the necessary and sufficient conditions for the irreducibility of the universal quasi-Whittaker modules. In the reducible case, we can obtain some maximal submodules. In particular, we classify the irreducible quasi-Whittaker modules for many Lie algebras, and obtain a lot of irreducible smooth 𝒲 n + \mathcal{W}_{n}^{+} -modules of height 2 2 .



Keyword: quasi-Whittaker module, smooth module, quasi-Whittaker vector

2020 MCS: 17B10, 17B20, 17B65,

1.
Introduction

Whittaker modules are a class of important modules in representation theory that were first defined for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
by Arnal and Pinzcon
[
AG
]
. In
[
K
]
, B. Kostant formally introduced Whittaker modules for finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebras, which can be generalized to any Lie algebras with a triangular decomposition. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}_{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{+}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and
\eta:\mathfrak{n}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
a Lie algebra homomorphism. A
\mathfrak{g}
-module is a Whittaker module if
x-\eta(x)
acts locally nilpotently on it for any
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{+}
. Various classes of simple Whittaker modules in the above sense were studied and classified by many authors (see
[
B
,
K
,
M1
,
M2
,
MS
]
).

Analogues of Whittaker modules can be defined and studied in various similar scenarios, as seen in the following papers. Whittaker modules (or generalized Whittaker modules) have been explored over the Virasoro algebra and its various generalizations (see
[
OW
,
LGZ
,
LPX
,
LZ
]
), the affine Kac-Moody algebras (see
[
ALZ
,
CGLW
]
), the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields (see
[
ZL
]
), the classical Lie superalgebras (see
[
C
]
), and even for quantum groups like
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
(see
[
O
,
XGZ
,
XZ
]
).

A general setup of
Whittaker pair
which one can define Whittaker modules was proposed in
[
BM
]
, see also
[
MZ
]
. A Whittaker pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
consists of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a locally nilpotent subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that the action of
\mathfrak{n}
on the adjoint
\mathfrak{n}
-module
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{n}
is locally nilpotent.
In
[
CCS
]
, Y. Cai et al, studied a type of Whittaker modules over the Schrodinger algebra induced by the Heisenberg subalgebra. To distinguish from the case of the Borel subalgebra and parabolic subalgebra, they named these modules quasi-Whittaker modules. Subsequently, quasi-Whittaker modules over the Euclidean algebra
\mathfrak{e}(3)
, the conformal Galilei algebras, the
(2+1)
- dimensional spacetime Schrödinger algebra, and the
n
-th Schrödinger algebra have all been studied (see
[
CSZ
,
CC
,
CL
,
JLZ
,
CW
]
). In particular, the above-mentioned works exhibit a certain degree of uniformity. Inspired by this, we will uniformly study a class of modules over nonsemisimple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
induced by a nonperfect ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. We still call them quasi-Whittaker modules. Generally speaking, if
\mathfrak{p}
is not locally nilpotent, the pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p})
does not form a
Whittaker pair
. The purpose of this paper is to set up a uniform way to study this class of modules for many Lie algebras, including finding quasi-Whittaker vectors, studying the irreducibility of universal quasi-Whittaker modules, and obtaining a specific class of irreducible quasi-Whittaker modules.

The paper is arranged as follows. In Section 2, we define quasi-Whittaker modules over nonssemisimple Lie algebras with a nonperfect ideal,
and establish some elementary results associated to quasi-Whittaker modules for later use. In Section 3, we introduce the new concept: the Whittaker annihilator for universal quasi-Whittaker modules, determine all quasi-Whittaker vectors (see Theorem
3.1
) and the irreducibilities of the universal quasi-Whittaker modules (see Theorem
3.3
). For the reducible cases, we determine some irreducible quotients (see Lemma
3.7
and Theorem
3.10
). Moreover, a sufficient condition is given for classifying irreducible quasi-Whittaker modules (see Theorem
3.11
). In Section 4, we give some examples and applications, to recover many known results and to provide many new irreducible modules. In particular, we obtain a lot of irreducible smooth
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
-modules of height
2
.

Throughout this paper, we use

\mathbb{Z}
to denote the set of integers,
\mathbb{N}

for positive integers,

\mathbb{Z}_{+}

for nonnegative integers, and

\mathbb{C}
for complex numbers. All vector spaces are over
\mathbb{C}
.

2.
Quasi-Whittaker modules

For a totally ordered set
I
(finite, countable or uncountable), denote by

\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}=\{\alpha:I\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{+}\mid\alpha(i)\neq 0~\text{only for finitely many }i\in I\}.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}
, we write
\alpha_{i}=\alpha(i)
for each
i\in I
. Define the
size
of
\alpha
to be

|\alpha|=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}.

For
i\in I
, denote by
\varepsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}_{+}
the element such that

\varepsilon_{ij}=\delta_{ij},~\forall j\in I.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}
we use the notation

x^{\alpha}=x_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{i_{1}}}x_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{i_{2}}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}^{\alpha_{i_{n}}},

where
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{n}
are chosen such that

\alpha_{k}=0,~\forall k\in I\backslash\{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n}\}.

Here,
x^{0}
is viewed as
1
.

Define a total order “
>
” on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}
by

\alpha>\beta\Longleftrightarrow|\alpha|>|\beta|,~\text{or }|\alpha|=|\beta|~\text{and }\exists i~\text{such that}~\alpha_{i}>\beta_{i}\text{ and }\alpha_{j}=\beta_{j}\text{ for }j<i.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}
with
|\alpha|>0
. Denote by
\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)
the largest index
k\in I
such that
\alpha_{k}\neq 0
, called the
height
of
\alpha
. We write

\hat{\alpha}=\alpha-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)}.

The following fact is easy to verify.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}_{+}
with positive size such that
\alpha>\beta
. Then
\hat{\alpha}\geq\hat{\beta}
. Moreover, the equality holds if and only if there exists some
j>\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)
such that
\beta=\hat{\alpha}+\varepsilon_{j}
.

In the following, let
\mathfrak{g}
be nonsemisimple Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{p}
a nonperfect ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be any Lie algebra homomorphism and
V
a
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

(1)

A vector
v\in V
is called a
quasi-Whittaker vector of type
\phi
if
pv=\phi(p)v
for each
p\in\mathfrak{p}
.

(2)

V
is said to be a
quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
if
V
is generated by a quasi-Whittaker vector of type
\phi
.

Note that
\phi=0
if
\mathfrak{p}
is a perfect ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. So we assume that
\mathfrak{p}
is a nonperfect ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\phi\neq 0
.
Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a given nonzero Lie algebra homomorphism. We define a one dimensional
\mathfrak{p}
-module
\mathbb{C}w_{\phi}
by

(2.1)

pw_{\phi}=\phi(p)w_{\phi},~\forall p\in\mathfrak{p}.

Denote by

W(\phi)=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathbb{C}w_{\phi}

the corresponding induced module. Then
W(\phi)
is a quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
with a cyclic quasi-Whittaker vector
1\otimes w_{\phi}
. We write
w_{\phi}=1\otimes w_{\phi}
for convenience.
For any quasi-Whittaker module
V
generated by a quasi-Whittaker vector
v
of type
\phi
, there exists a surjective
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism
\Phi:W(\phi)\rightarrow V
such that
\Phi(w_{\phi})=v
. We call
W(\phi)

the universal quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
.

Definition 2.2
.

We define the
Whittaker annihilator
of
\phi
as

(2.2)

\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}=\{g\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\phi([g,p])=0,~\forall p\in\mathfrak{p}\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
0\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
. For
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi([\lambda_{1}g_{1}+\lambda_{2}g_{2},p])=\lambda_{1}\phi([g_{1},p])+\lambda_{2}\phi([g_{2},p])=0+0=0,

\displaystyle\phi([[g_{1},g_{2}],p])=\phi([[g_{1},p],g_{2}])+\phi([g_{1},[g_{2},p]])=0+0=0

for each
p\in\mathfrak{p}
. It follows that

\lambda_{1}g_{1}+\lambda_{2}g_{2},[g_{1},g_{2}]\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}.

Hence,
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\phi
is a Lie algebra homomorphism, one has
\phi([\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}])=0
, which implies that
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
.
∎

In the following discussion, we choose a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
in the following form

(2.3)

\underbrace{x_{i},~i\in I;~\underbrace{y_{j},~j\in J;~\underbrace{p_{k},~k\in K}_{\text{a basis of }\mathfrak{p}}}_{\text{a basis of }\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}}}_{\text{a basis of}~\mathfrak{g}},

where
I,J
and
K
are totally ordered sets (or empty). We denote by
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}^{\phi})
and
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})
the universal enveloping algebras of
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
and
\mathfrak{p}
respectively. Then there exist natural inclusions

(2.4)

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}^{\phi})\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}).

Moreover, by PBW theorem,
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
is a free right
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}^{\phi})
-module with basis

x^{\alpha},~\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I},

and is a free right
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})
-module with basis

x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},~\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I},~\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{J}.

For a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
, we write

V_{\phi}=\{v\in V\mid pv=\phi(p)v,~\forall p\in\mathfrak{p}\}.

Then
V_{\phi}
is a subspace of
V
. Moreover, we have

Lemma 2.3
.

For a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
, the subspace

V_{\phi}
is
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
-invariant.

Proof.

Let
v\in V_{\phi}
and
y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
. Then we have

pyv=[p,y]v+ypv=\phi([p,y])v+y\left(\phi(p)v\right)=0+\phi(p)yv,~\forall p\in\mathfrak{p}.

It follows that
yv\in V_{\phi}
.
∎

For
v\in V_{\phi}
and
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
, it is easy to check that

(2.5)

(p-\phi(p))uv=[p,u]v,~\forall p\in\mathfrak{p}.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
|I|>0
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}
with height
k
. For a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
we have

\left(p-\phi(p)\right)x^{\alpha}v=\alpha_{k}\phi([p,x_{k}])x^{\hat{\alpha}}v+\text{a linear combination of }x^{\beta}v\text{'s}~\text{with~}\beta<\hat{\alpha}

for each
p\in\mathfrak{p}
and
v\in V_{\phi}
.

Proof.

Assume that
x^{\alpha}=x_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{i_{1}}}x_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{i_{2}}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{\alpha_{i_{k}}}
. Then

(2.6)

(p-\phi(p))x^{\alpha}v=[p,x^{\alpha}]v=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{i_{1}}}x_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{i_{2}}}\cdots[p,x_{i_{j}}^{\alpha_{i_{j}}}]\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{\alpha_{i_{k}}}

=\alpha_{k}\phi([p,x_{k}])x^{\hat{\alpha}}v+\text{a linear combination of }x^{\beta}v\text{'s}~\text{with~}\beta<\hat{\alpha}.

∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
V
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Assume that
\mathfrak{p}
is a solvable ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(1)

V
is a locally finite
\mathfrak{p}
-module;

(2)

V
is a quasi-Whittaker module for
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Assume that
V
is a locally finite
\mathfrak{p}
-module. Taking any nonzero
v_{0}\in V
, the
\mathfrak{p}
-submodule
V_{1}\subseteq V
generated by
v_{0}
is finite-dimensional. By Lie’s theorem, there exists a common eigenvector
w
for all elements of
\mathfrak{p}
. Hence, there exists a linear map
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

pw=\phi(p)w,~\forall p\in\mathfrak{p}.

Since
V
is irreducible as a
\mathfrak{g}
-module, we see that
V=U(\mathfrak{g})w
. Clearly,
V
is a quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. Let
V
be an irreducible quasi-Whittaker module generated by a quasi-Whittaker vector
v_{\phi}
of type
\phi
.
Then any
v\in V
can be written as

v=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I},\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{J}\end{subarray}}a_{\alpha,\beta}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}v_{\phi}

with finitely many nonzero
a_{\alpha,\beta}
’s.
From (
2.6
), one can easily decuce that

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}v_{\phi}\subseteq\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{x^{\alpha^{\prime}}y^{\beta}v_{\phi}\mid\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}~\text{with}~\alpha^{\prime}_{i}\leq\alpha_{i},~\forall i\in I\}

for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}
. In particular,

\dim\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}v_{\phi}<\infty.

It follows that
\dim\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})v<\infty
since

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})v\subseteq\sum_{a_{\alpha,\beta}\neq 0}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}v_{\phi}.

Hence,
V
is a locally finite
\mathfrak{p}
-module.
∎

We also use the symbol “
>
” to denote the total order on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}\times\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{J}
defined by

\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta)>(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle|\alpha|+|\beta|>|\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta^{\prime}|;~\text{or~}|\alpha|+|\beta|=|\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta^{\prime}|~\text{but~}\alpha>\alpha^{\prime};

\displaystyle\text{or}~|\alpha|+|\beta|=|\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta^{\prime}|~\text{and}~\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}~\text{but~}\beta>\beta^{\prime}.

Proposition 2.6
.

Each nonzero quasi-Whittaker vector in
W(\phi)
is of type
\phi
.

Proof.

Let
w=uw_{\phi}\in W({\phi})
be a nonzero quasi-Whittaker vector of type
\varphi
. Write

w=ax^{\alpha}y^{\beta}w_{\phi}+\text{a linear combination of }x^{\alpha^{\prime}}y^{\beta^{\prime}}w_{\phi}\text{'s}~\text{with~}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})<(\alpha,\beta),

where
a\neq 0
.
For each
p\in\mathfrak{p}
, one has

pw=\varphi(p)w=a\varphi(p)x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}w_{\phi}+\text{a linear combination of }x^{\alpha^{\prime}}y^{\beta^{\prime}}w_{\phi}\text{'s}\\
~\text{with~}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})<(\alpha,\beta)

and

\displaystyle pw=\left([p,u]+up\right)w_{\phi}=a\phi(p)x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}w_{\phi}+

\displaystyle\text{a linear combination of }x^{\alpha^{\prime}}y^{\beta^{\prime}}w_{\phi}\text{'s}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{with~}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})<(\alpha,\beta).

Comparing the above two equations, we can obtain that
\varphi(p)=\phi(p)
. It follows that
\varphi=\phi
.
∎

3.
Irreducibility of quasi-Whittaker modules

In this section, we first characterize all quasi-Whittaker vectors, then determine the irreducibility of the universal quasi-Whittaker modules. We also classify the irreducible quasi-Whittaker modules with a unique nonzero quasi-Whittaker vector up to a scalar.

Throughout this section, we let
\mathfrak{g}
be nonsemisimple Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{p}
a nonperfect ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
,
and
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
a Lie algebra homomorphism.

3.1.
Irreducibility of
W(\phi)

We now determine all the quasi-Whittaker vectors in
W(\phi)
defined in Section 2.
We denote by

\mathbb{C}[y]=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{y^{\beta}\mid\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{J}\}\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}).

Then
\mathbb{C}[y]
has a basis

y^{\beta},~\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{J}.

We see that

(3.1)

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}^{\phi})w_{\phi}=\mathbb{C}[y]w_{\phi}.

Let
\mathbb{C}[t]=\mathbb{C}[t_{j}\mid j\in J]
denote the polynomial algebra in variables
t_{j},~j\in J
over
\mathbb{C}
. We understand
\mathbb{C}[y]=\mathbb{C}[t]=\mathbb{C}
when
J=\emptyset
. Then there exists a vector space isomorphism

(3.2)

\hat{}:\mathbb{C}[y]\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[t],~y^{\beta}\longmapsto t^{\beta},~\forall\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{J}.

Note that
W(\phi)
has a basis

x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}w_{\phi},~\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I},~\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{J}.

Hence, any nonzero
v
in
W(\phi)
can be uniquely written as

(3.3)

v=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}}x^{\alpha}f_{\alpha}w_{\phi}

with finitely many nonzero
f_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}[y]
.
Assume that
|I|>0
. Define the
support
of
v
to be

\mathrm{Supp}(v)=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}\mid f_{\alpha}\neq 0\}.

We define the

degree
of
v
as

\mathrm{deg}(v)=\max\mathrm{Supp}(v)

with respect to the order “
>
” on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For the universal quasi-Whittaker module
W(\phi)
, we have

W(\phi)_{\phi}=\mathbb{C}[y]w_{\phi}
.

Proof.

We can easily verify that

\mathbb{C}[y]w_{\phi}\subseteq W(\phi)_{\phi}.

It is enough to show that
W(\phi)_{\phi}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[y]w_{\phi}
. To the contrary, suppose that
W(\phi)_{\phi}\not\subseteq\mathbb{C}[y]w_{\phi}
. Then there exists some nonzero
v\in W(\phi)_{\phi}
with
\mathrm{deg}(v)=\alpha>0
. Let
k=\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)
and

\ell=\max\{\mathrm{ht}(\gamma)\mid\gamma\in\mathrm{Supp}(v)\}.

Then
k\leq\ell
and the set

T=\{j\in I\mid k\leq j\leq\ell~\text{and~}\gamma_{j}\neq 0~\text{for some }\gamma\in\mathrm{Supp}(v)\}

is finite.
Assume that

T=\{k=i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{m}=\ell\}.

Denote by

\alpha^{(t)}=\alpha-\varepsilon_{k}+\varepsilon_{i_{t}},~t=1,2,\cdots,m.

Then

\alpha=\alpha^{(1)}>\alpha^{(2)}>\alpha^{(3)}>\cdots>\alpha^{(m)}

are adjacent elements in the set

\mathrm{Supp}(v)\cup\{\alpha^{(1)},\alpha^{(2)},\alpha^{(3)},\cdots,\alpha^{(m)}\}.

with respect to the order “
>
”. Hence,
v
is expressed as

v=x^{\alpha}f_{\alpha}w_{\phi}+\sum_{t=2}^{m}x^{\alpha^{(t)}}f_{\alpha^{(t)}}w_{\phi}+\text{lower degree terms},

with
0\neq f_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}[y]
. Note that
\hat{f}_{\alpha}\neq 0
since
f_{\alpha}\neq 0
. We can find some

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}=(a_{j})_{j\in J}
with
a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}

such that
\hat{f}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})\neq 0
. Then

\alpha_{k}\hat{f}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})x_{k}+\hat{f}_{\alpha^{(2)}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})x_{i_{2}}+\cdots+\hat{f}_{\alpha^{(m)}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})x_{i_{m}}\notin\mathfrak{g}^{\phi},

Hence, by the definition of
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
there exists
p\in\mathfrak{p}
such that

\phi\left([\alpha_{k}\hat{f}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})x_{k}+\hat{f}_{\alpha^{(2)}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})x_{i_{2}}+\cdots+\hat{f}_{\alpha^{(m)}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})x_{i_{m}},p]\right)\neq 0,

i.e.,

\alpha_{k}\phi([x_{k},p])\hat{f}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})+\phi([x_{i_{2}},p])\hat{f}_{\alpha^{(2)}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})+\cdots\phi([x_{i_{m}},p])\hat{f}_{\alpha^{(m)}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})\neq 0,

which implies that

\alpha_{k}\phi([x_{k},p])\hat{f}_{\alpha}+\phi([x_{i_{2}},p])\hat{f}_{\alpha^{(2)}}+\cdots\phi([x_{i_{m}},p])\hat{f}_{\alpha^{(m)}}\neq 0.

It follows that

\alpha_{k}\phi([x_{k},p])f_{\alpha}+\phi([x_{i_{2}},p])f_{\alpha^{(2)}}+\cdots\phi([x_{i_{m}},p])f_{\alpha^{(m)}}\neq 0.

By Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\left(p-\phi(p)\right)v=

\displaystyle-x^{\hat{\alpha}}\left(\alpha_{k}\phi([x_{k},p])f_{\alpha}+\phi\left([x_{i_{2}},p]\right)f_{\alpha^{(2)}}+\cdots+\phi\left([x_{i_{m}},p]\right)f_{\alpha^{(m)}}\right)w_{\phi}

\displaystyle+\text{lower degree terms}\neq 0,

which contradicts
v\in W(\phi)_{\phi}
. The proof is completed.
∎

From the above proof process, one can deduce the following proposition easily

Proposition 3.2
.

Any nonzero
\mathfrak{p}
-invariant subspace of
W(\phi)
contains a nonzero quasi-Whittaker vector of type
\phi
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a non-zero
\mathfrak{p}
-invariant subspace of
W(\phi)
. Take
v
to be a non-zero element in
M
with
|\text{deg}(v)|
minimized. We claim that
v\in W(\phi)_{\phi}
. Otherwise, assume
\mathrm{deg}(v)=\alpha
, then necessarily
|\alpha|>0
. We may assume that
v
is of the form in (
3.3
). From the proof of Theorem 3.1, we have some
p\in\mathfrak{p}
such that

\displaystyle(p-\phi(p))v={}

\displaystyle-x^{\hat{\alpha}}\left(\alpha_{k}\phi([x_{k},p])f_{\alpha}+\phi([x_{i_{2}},p])f_{\alpha^{(2)}}+\cdots+\phi([x_{i_{m}},p])f_{\alpha^{(m)}}\right)w_{\phi}

\displaystyle+\text{lower degree terms}\neq 0

Since
|\hat{\alpha}|=|\alpha|-1
,
(p-\phi(p))v
is a non-zero vector in
M
whose size of support is smaller than that of
v
, which contradicts the choice of
v
.
∎

Now, we can determine the irreduciblity of
W(\phi)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be nonsemisimple Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{p}
a nonperfect ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
,
and
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
a Lie algebra homomorphism. Then

W(\phi)
is irreducible if and only if
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}=\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

Assume that
W(\phi)
is irreducible. Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\neq\mathfrak{p}
, i.e.,
|J|>0
. Choose an element
j\in J
. It is easy to see that
U(\mathfrak{g})y_{j}w_{\phi}
does not contain
w_{\phi}
, which implies that
U(\mathfrak{g})y_{j}w_{\phi}
is a proper submodule, a contradiction.

Next, assume that
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}=\mathfrak{p}
. By Theorem
3.1
, we have

W(\phi)_{\phi}=\mathbb{C}w_{\phi}.
Let
M
be a nonzero proper
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
W(\phi)
. Then
M
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-invariant subspace of
W(\phi)
. By Proposition
3.2
,
M
contains a nonzero quasi-Whittaker vector. We
can deduce that
w_{\phi}\in M
. It follows that
M=W(\phi)
. Hence,
W(\phi)
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
∎

As a corollary, we have

Corollary 3.4
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is finite-dimensional of the form
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\ltimes\mathfrak{p}
. Let
g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{t}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{\ell}
a basis of
\mathfrak{p}
. Denote by

A_{\phi}=\begin{pmatrix}\phi([g_{1},p_{1}])&\phi([g_{2},p_{1}])&\cdots&\phi([g_{t},p_{1}])\\
\phi([g_{1},p_{2}])&\phi([g_{2},p_{2}])&\cdots&\phi([g_{t},p_{2}])\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\phi([g_{1},p_{\ell}])&\phi([g_{2},p_{\ell}])&\cdots&\phi([g_{t},p_{\ell}])\end{pmatrix}.

Then
W(\phi)
is irreducible if and only if
\mathrm{rank}A_{\phi}=t.

Proof.

Just note that we have a decomposition

(3.4)

\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}=\left(\mathfrak{g}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\right)\ltimes\mathfrak{p},

and the linear space
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is isomorphic to the solution space of the homogeneous linear system of equations with coefficient matrix
A_{\phi}
. Hence,
W(\phi)
is irreducible if and only if
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}=0
, if and only if
\mathrm{rank}A_{\phi}=t.

∎

3.2.
Bland quasi-Whittaker modules

In the subsection, we classify all irreducible quasi-Whittaker modules with unique quasi-Whittaker vector (up to a scalar).
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be nonsemisimple Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{p}
a nonperfect ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
,
and
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
V
be an irreducible quasi-Whittaker module over
\mathfrak{g}
of type
\phi
. We call
V

bland
if the quasi-Whittaker vector in
V
is unique up to a scalar, i.e.,
\dim V_{\phi}=1
.

Next Example
3.1
provides irredcible quasi-Whittaker modules whose quasi-Whittaker vectors are not unique.
Now we also make the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

A Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is said to be
extendable
if it can be extended to a Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\to\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a Lie algebra homomorphism. Then
\phi
is extendable if and only if

(3.5)

[\mathfrak{g}^{\phi},\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}]\cap\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\ker\phi.

Proof.

The necessity is obvious. We only prove the sufficiency. Assume that
[\mathfrak{g}^{\phi},\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}]\cap\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\ker\phi.
It is easy to check that
\phi
can be extended to a linear function
\phi^{\prime}
on
[\mathfrak{g}^{\phi},\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}]+\mathfrak{p}
by defining

\phi^{\prime}([\mathfrak{g}^{\phi},\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}])=0.

Choose
y_{s}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
(
s\in S
) linearly independent such that

\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{y_{s}\mid s\in S\}\oplus\left([\mathfrak{g}^{\phi},\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}]+\mathfrak{p}\right)

as a linear space. We extend
\phi^{\prime}
onto
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
by putting

\phi^{\prime}(y_{s})=\xi_{s},~\forall s\in S

for arbitary
\xi_{s}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then we obtain a Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. It follows that
\phi
is extendable.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
admits bland irreducible quasi-Whittaker modules of type
\phi
, then
\phi
is extendable.

Proof.

Let
V
be a bland irreducible quasi-Whittaker module generated by a cyclic quasi-Whittaker vector
v
of type
\phi
. Then
V_{\phi}=\mathbb{C}v
. By Lemma
2.3
,

\mathbb{C}v\subseteq U(\mathfrak{g}^{\phi})v\subseteq V_{\phi}.

It follows that
V_{\phi}=U(\mathfrak{g}^{\phi})v
is a one dimensional
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
-module. Hence, there exists a Lie algebra homomorphsim

\phi^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

yv=\phi^{\prime}(y)v,~\forall y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}.

Since

\phi(p)v=pv=\phi^{\prime}(p)v,~\forall p\in\mathfrak{p},

the linear map
\phi^{\prime}
is an extension of
\phi
. It implies that
\phi
is extendable.
∎

Example 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
be the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg algebra, which is a complex Lie algebra with basis
x,y,z
such that

[x,y]=z,~[x,z]=[y,z]=0.

Take the ideal
\mathfrak{p}=\mathbb{C}z
and define the Lie algebra homomorphism

\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},~\lambda z\mapsto\lambda,~\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

Then
\mathfrak{h}_{1}^{\phi}=\mathfrak{h}_{1}
. So any nonzero vector in
W(\phi)
is a Whittaker vector. Note that
\phi
is not extendable, since

\phi([x,y])=\phi(z)=1\neq 0.

By Lemma
3.6
,
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
has no bland quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
. Actually
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
has infinite-dimensional irreducible quasi-Whittaker modules of type
\phi
all whose vectors are quasi-Whittaker vectors.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be an extendable Lie algebra homomorphism and
\phi^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\to\mathbb{C}
be an extension of
\phi
. Define a one dimensional
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
-module
\mathbb{C}v_{\phi^{\prime}}
by

yv_{\phi^{\prime}}=\phi^{\prime}(y)v_{\phi^{\prime}},~\forall y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}.

Let

V(\phi^{\prime})=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathbb{C}v_{\phi^{\prime}}.

be the corresponding induced module. It is clear that
V(\phi^{\prime})
is a quasi-Whittaker module generated by a quasi-Whittaker vector
v_{\phi^{\prime}}:=1\otimes v_{\phi^{\prime}}
of type
\phi
. Hence, there exists a surjective
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism
\Phi:W(\phi)\rightarrow V(\phi^{\prime})
sending
w_{\phi}
to
v_{\phi^{\prime}}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

The
\mathfrak{g}
-module

V(\phi^{\prime})
is irreducible.

Proof.

Consider the above surjective
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism
\Phi:W(\phi)\rightarrow V(\phi^{\prime})
. Let
M
be a nonzero submodule of
V(\phi^{\prime})
. It is enough to show that
M=V(\phi^{\prime})
, or
v_{\phi^{\prime}}\in M
.

Take
0\neq v\in M
. Then
v
can be uniquely written as

v=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}}a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}v_{\phi^{\prime}}

with finitely many nonzero
a_{\alpha}
’s. Let

w=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}}a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}w_{\phi}\in W(\phi).

It is easy to see that vectors in
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})w
are linear combinations of vectors of the form
x^{\beta}w_{\phi}
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}
.
By Proposition
3.2
, there exists a nonzero quasi-Whittaker vector in

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})w\subseteq W(\phi)
which must be
w_{\phi}
. Thus
v_{\phi^{\prime}}=\Phi(w_{\phi})\in M
. Hence,
V(\phi^{\prime})
is irreducible.
∎

From the above proof it is clear that the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V(\phi^{\prime})
is bland of type
\phi
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume that
\phi:\mathfrak{p}:\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is extendable. Then each bland irreducible quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
is isomorphic to
V(\phi^{\prime})
for some extension
\phi^{\prime}
of
\phi
.

Proof.

Let
V
be a bland irreducible quasi-Whittaker module generated by a quasi-Whittaker vector
v
of type
\phi
. From the proof of Lemma
3.6
, there exists a Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\phi
such that

yv=\phi^{\prime}(y)v,~\forall y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}.

Form the construction of
V(\phi^{\prime})
, there exists a surjective
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism
\Phi^{\prime}:V(\phi^{\prime})\rightarrow V
sending
v_{\phi^{\prime}}
to
v
. Since
V(\phi^{\prime})
is irreducible,
\Phi^{\prime}
must be an isomorphism.
∎

The following result is clear.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\phi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime\prime}:\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be two Lie algebra homomorphisms extending
\phi
. Then
V(\phi^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
V(\phi^{\prime\prime})
if and only if
\phi^{\prime}=\phi^{\prime\prime}
.

To summarize the above discussions, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be nonsemisimple Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{p}
a nonperfect ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
,
and
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
a Lie algebra homomorphism. Then
\mathfrak{g}
admits bland irreducible quasi-Whittaker modules if and only if
\phi
is extendable. In this case, the correspondence
\phi^{\prime}\mapsto V(\phi^{\prime})
gives a bijection between the set of extensions of
\phi
and the set of isomorphism class of bland quasi-Whittaker modules of type
\phi
.

Remark 1
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is of the form
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\ltimes\mathfrak{p}
. Then it is easy to see that each Lie algebra homomorphism from
\mathfrak{p}
to
\mathbb{C}
is extendable. One may expect that each irreducible quasi-Whittaker module is bland. Unfortunately, this is not correct in general. For example, let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathbb{C}c
. Let
V
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
-module with
\dim V>1
. Then
V
can be viewed as an irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-module by declaring
cv=v
for any
v\in V
. It is clear that
V
can be treated as a quasi-Whittaker module of type

\phi:\mathbb{C}c\rightarrow\mathbb{C},~c\mapsto 1,

and any element in
V
is a quasi-Whittaker vector. In order to have good properties for quasi-Whittaker modules over
\mathfrak{g}
, we generally do not take
\mathfrak{p}
to be in the center of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Finally, we consider a very special case, which is just the situation in
[
CC
,
CW
]
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be nonsemisimple Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{p}
a nonperfect ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
,
and
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
a Lie algebra homomorphism.
Assume that
\dim\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}/\mathfrak{p}\leq 1.
Then any irreducible quasi-Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}
-module of type
\phi
is bland.

Proof.

Let
V
be an irreducible quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
.
If
\dim\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}/\mathfrak{p}=0
, then
V
is isomorphic to
W(\phi)
for the Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. By Theorem
3.1
,

W(\phi)_{\phi}=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}^{\phi})w_{\phi}=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})w_{\phi}=\mathbb{C}w_{\phi}.

It follows that
V
is bland.

If
\dim\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}/\mathfrak{p}=1
, one can take some
y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
such that
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}=\mathbb{C}y\oplus\mathfrak{p}.
Take a nonzero quasi-Whittaker vector
v\in V
. Then
there exists a surjective
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism
\Phi:W(\phi)\rightarrow V
such that
\Phi(w_{\phi})=v
. By Theorem
3.3
,
W(\phi)
is reducible. Hence,
\ker\Phi
is a maximal submodule of
W(\phi)
. By Proposition
3.2
, there exists a nonzero quasi-Whittaker vector
w\in\ker\Phi
. By Theorem
3.1
, there exists some nonzero polynomial
f(y)
such that
w=f(y)w_{\phi}
. Take such an
f(y)
with minimal degree and assume that

f(y)=a(y-a_{1})(y-a_{2})\cdots(y-a_{s}).

Then

(y-a_{1})(y-a_{2})\cdots(y-a_{s})v=0,
and

v^{\prime}=(y-a_{1})(y-a_{2})\cdots(y-a_{s-1})v\in V\setminus\{0\}.

Then
v^{\prime}
is a nonzero quasi-Whittaker vector of type
\phi
such that
yv^{\prime}=a_{s}v^{\prime}
. Define

\phi^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

by setting
\phi^{\prime}(y)=a_{s}
. Then
\phi^{\prime}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism extending
\phi
. By the construction of
V(\phi^{\prime})
, we see that
V
is a homomorphic image of
V(\phi^{\prime})
. Since
V(\phi^{\prime})
and
V
are irreducible, we must have
V\cong V(\phi^{\prime})
. It follows that
V
is bland.
∎

From the above proof we obtain the following

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be nonsemisimple Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{p}
a nonperfect ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
,
and
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
a Lie algebra homomorphism.
Assume that
\dim\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}/\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
.

(1)

If
\dim\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}/\mathfrak{p}=0
, then
W(\phi)
is irreducible over
\mathfrak{g}
.

(2)

If
\dim\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}/\mathfrak{p}=1
, taking
y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}=\mathbb{C}y\oplus\mathfrak{p}
, then all maximal submodules of
W(\phi)
are of the form

J_{\xi}:=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{x^{\alpha}y^{n}(y-\xi)w_{\phi}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\},\quad\text{where }\xi\in\mathbb{C}.

In fact, we have
V(\phi^{\prime})\cong W(\phi)/J_{\phi^{\prime}(y)}
for any extension
\phi^{\prime}
of
\phi
.

4.
Applications

In this section, we will apply the main results established in Section 3 to classify irreducible quasi-Whittaker modules over many Lie algebras to recover many known rersults, including Conformal Galilei algebra and
n
-th Schrödinger algebra. Then we also obtain a lot of irreducible quasi-Whittaker modules over mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra, Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra, and Planer Galilei Conformal algebra. In addition, we obtain a lot of irreducible smooth
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
-modules of height
2
.

4.1.
Conformal Galilei algebra and
n
-th Schrödinger algebra

Following
[
NOR
]
, let
\ell\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
and
\overline{0,2\ell}=\{0,1,\ldots,2\ell\}
. The (centreless) conformal Galilei algbera
\mathfrak{g}^{(\ell)}
is a complex Lie algebra with basis
\{e,h,f,p_{k}\mid k\in\overline{0,2\ell}\}
. The non-vanishing Lie brackets for
\mathfrak{g}^{(\ell)}
are given by

\displaystyle[h,e]=2e,~~~~~~~~~~~~~~[h,f]=-2f,~~~~~[e,f]=h,

\displaystyle[h,p_{k}]=2(l-k)p_{k},~~~[e,p_{k}]=kp_{k-1},~~~[f,p_{k}]=(2l-k)p_{k+1}.

The subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{(\ell)}
spanned by
e,f,h
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Let

\mathfrak{p}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{p_{k}\mid k\in\overline{0,2\ell}\},

which is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}^{(\ell)}
. As a
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-module,
\mathfrak{p}
is isomorphic to the irreducible one with dimension
2\ell+1
. We have a decomposition

\mathfrak{g}^{(\ell)}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\ltimes\mathfrak{p}.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero Lie algebra homomorphism. For any two-dimensiomal subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, we know that

[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{p}]=\mathfrak{p}
.
Since
\phi\neq 0
, we must have

\phi\left([\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{p}]\right)\neq 0
. It follows that
\dim\mathfrak{g}^{(\ell)\phi}/\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
.

Following
[
T
]
, the
n
-th Schrödinger algebra
\mathfrak{sch}_{n}
is a complex Lie algebra with basis
\{h,e,f,x_{i},y_{i},z\mid i=1,\ldots,n\}
and the Lie brackets

\displaystyle[h,e]=2e,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad[h,f]=-2f,\quad[e,f]=h,

\displaystyle[h,x_{i}]=x_{i},\quad\quad\quad\quad~~[e,x_{i}]=0,\quad\quad~[f,x_{i}]=y_{i},

\displaystyle[h,y_{i}]=-y_{i},\quad\quad\quad\quad[e,y_{i}]=x_{i},\quad\quad[f,y_{i}]=0,

\displaystyle[x_{i},x_{j}]=[y_{i},y_{j}]=0,~~[x_{i},y_{j}]=\delta_{ij}z,\quad[z,\cdot]=0.

Note that the subspace spanned by
h,e,f
is in fact a subalgebra, which is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
canonically. Let

\mathfrak{h}_{n}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{x_{i},y_{i},z\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}.

Then
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
is isomorphic to the
n
-th Heisenberg Lie subalgebra. Moreover, we have

\mathfrak{sch}_{n}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}.

We choose
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{h}_{n}
. Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero Lie algebra homomorphism (implying that
\phi(z)=0
). For any two-dimensiomal subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, it is easy to verify that
\mathfrak{p}=[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{p}]\oplus\mathbb{C}z
. Since
\phi\neq 0
and
\phi(z)=0
, we must have
\phi\left([\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{p}]\right)\neq 0
.
It follows that
\dim\mathfrak{sch}_{n}^{\phi}/\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
.

From above the discussions, using Corollary
3.12
we can deduce the following theorem, which was obtained in
[
CC
,
CW
]
by concrete calculations.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the conformal Galilei algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{(\ell)}
or
n
-th Schrödinger algebra
\mathfrak{sch}_{n}
. Assume that
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a nonzero Lie algebra homomorphism. Then each irreducible quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
is bland, i.e., is of the form of either
W(\phi)
or
V(\phi^{\prime})
for some extension
\phi^{\prime}
of
\phi
.

Remark 2
.

When
n=\infty
, we can call the corresponding Schrödinger algebra as the
\infty
-th Schrödinger algebra. In fact, the conclusion of Theorem 4.1 also holds for the
\infty
-th Schrödinger algebra.

4.2.
The mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra

Following
[
Ba
]
, the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{D}
is a Lie algebra with a basis

\left\{d_{m},h_{r},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{l}\,\bigg|\,m\in\mathbb{Z},r\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}\right\}

subject to the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle[d_{m},d_{n}]=(m-n)d_{m+n}+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\delta_{m+n,0}\mathbf{c},

\displaystyle[d_{m},h_{r}]=-rh_{m+r},\quad[h_{r},h_{s}]=r\delta_{r+s,0}\mathbf{l},\quad[\mathbf{c},\mathcal{D}]=[\mathbf{l},\mathcal{D}]=0,

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r,s\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
. Let

\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{d_{m},\mathbf{c}\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}\},~\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{h_{r},\mathbf{l}\mid r\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}\}.

Then
\mathcal{V}
is the Virasoro algebra and
\mathcal{H}
is the twisted Heisenberg algebra. Moreover, one has
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{V}\ltimes\mathcal{H}.
We take
\mathfrak{p}=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{c}\oplus\mathcal{H}
. Then
\mathfrak{p}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{D}
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathbb{C}
be a Lie algebra homomorphism. Since
[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{l}
, then
\phi(\mathbf{l})=0
. Following
[
BZ
]
, we say
\phi
is an exp-polynomial function if there exists nonzero distinct complex numbers
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{\ell}
and polynomials
f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t),\cdots,f_{\ell}(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t]
such that

\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n})=a_{1}^{n}f_{1}(n)+a_{2}^{n}f_{2}(n)+\cdots+a_{\ell}^{n}f_{\ell}(n),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

The following lemma can be deduced from
[
CGT
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathbb{C}
be a linear map such that
\phi(\mathbf{c})=0
. Then
\phi
is an exp-polynomial function if and only if there exists some
c_{0},c_{1},\cdots,c_{r-1}\in\mathbb{C}
with
c_{0}\neq 0,r\geq 1
such that

c_{0}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n})+c_{1}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n+1})+\cdots+c_{r-1}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n+r-1})+\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n+r})=0,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a Lie algbera homomorphism with
\phi(\mathcal{H})\neq 0
. Then
W(\phi)
is irreducible if and only if
\phi
is not an exp-polynomial function.

Proof.

Assume that
\phi
is not an exp-polynomial function. Suppose that
\mathcal{D}^{\phi}\neq\mathfrak{p}
, that is, there exists nonzero

x=c_{0}d_{m-r}+c_{1}d_{m-r+1}+\cdots+c_{r-1}d_{m-1}+d_{m}\in\mathcal{D}^{\phi}.

Then

0=\phi([x,h_{\frac{1}{2}+n}])=-\left(\frac{1}{2}+n\right)\left(c_{0}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n+m-r})+\cdots+c_{r-1}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n+m-1})+\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n+m})\right)

holds for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, thus

c_{0}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n-r})+c_{1}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n-r+1})+\cdots+c_{r-1}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n-1})+\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n})=0,~\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

By Lemma
4.2
,
\phi
must be an exp-polynomial function contadicting the assumption. So
\mathcal{D}^{\phi}=\mathfrak{p}
, and
W(\phi)
is irreducible by Theorem
3.3
.

Assume that
W(\phi)
is irreducible, i.e.
\mathcal{D}^{\phi}=\mathfrak{p}
by Theorem
3.3
. Suppose that
\phi
is an exp-polynomial function. Then by Lemma
4.2
, there
exists some
c_{0},c_{1},\cdots,c_{r-1}\in\mathbb{C}
with
c_{0}\neq 0
such that

c_{0}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n-r})+c_{1}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n-r+1})+\cdots+c_{r-1}\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n-1})+\phi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+n})=0,~\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

It follows that

c_{0}d_{-r}+c_{1}d_{-r+1}+\cdots+c_{r-1}d_{-1}+d_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\phi},

a contradiction. So
\phi
is not an exp-polynomial function.
∎

4.3.
The Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra

Following
[
ADKP
]
, the Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{L}
is a Lie algebra with a basis
\{L_{n},I_{n},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
defined by the following commutation relations

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(n-m)L_{m+n}+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\delta_{m+n,0}z_{1},\quad[I_{m},I_{n}]=m\delta_{m+n,0}z_{3},

\displaystyle[L_{m},I_{n}]=nI_{m+n}+\delta_{m+n,0}(m^{2}+m)z_{2},\quad[I_{0},\mathcal{L}]=[z_{1},\mathcal{L}]=[z_{2},\mathcal{L}]=[z_{3},\mathcal{L}]=0.

Let

\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{L_{m},z_{1}\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}\},~\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{I_{r},z_{3}\mid r\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Then
\mathcal{V}
is the Virasoro algebra and
\mathcal{H}
is the Heisenberg algebra.
We take the ideal
\mathfrak{p}
to be

\mathfrak{p}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{I_{n},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Then
[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]=\mathbb{C}z_{3}
. Take a Lie algbera homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathbb{C}
, implying that
\phi(z_{3})=0
. We set

S^{\phi}=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\phi(I_{n})\neq 0\}.

We call
\phi

finite
if
|S^{\phi}|<+\infty
and
\phi(z_{2})=0
.

Example 4.1
.

For a fixed
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a Lie algbera homomorphism such that

\phi(I_{k})=1,~\phi(I_{n})=0,~\forall n\neq k;\phi(z_{1})=\phi(z_{2})=\phi(z_{3})=0.

Then
\mathcal{L}^{\phi}=\mathbb{C}L_{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}.
In addition, each irreducible quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
is of the form of
V(\phi^{\prime})
for some extension
\phi^{\prime}
of
\phi
.

Proof.

Assume that

x=L_{m}+\lambda_{1}L_{m+1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}L_{m+\ell}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}.

Then

m-k=-\phi([x,I_{-m+k}])=0.

Thus,
x
is expressed as

x=L_{k}+\lambda_{1}L_{m+1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}L_{m+\ell}.

For
1\leq i\leq\ell
, one has

\lambda_{i}=-\frac{1}{i}\phi([x,I_{-i}])=0.

We can obtain that

\mathcal{L}^{\phi}=\mathbb{C}L_{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}.

Then, according to Corollary
3.12
, the proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a finite Lie algbera homomorphism with
\phi(\mathcal{H})\neq 0
. Then
W(\phi)
is irreducible if and only if
|S^{\phi}|\geq 2
.

Proof.

Assume that
|S^{\phi}|\geq 2
. Let
i
be the smallest element in
S^{\phi}
and
j
the largest one. Suppose that
\mathcal{L}^{\phi}\neq\mathfrak{p}
. Then one can choose

x=L_{m}+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\lambda_{k}L_{m+k}\in\mathcal{L}^{\phi},

where
\lambda_{k}=0
for almost all
k\geq 1
. We claim that
m\leq i
. Otherwise, one has

0=\phi([x,I_{i-m}])=(i-m)\left(\phi(I_{i})+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}\phi(I_{i+k})\right),

thus

\phi(I_{i})+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\lambda_{k}\phi(I_{i+k})=0.

It is clear that there exists some
t_{1}\geq 1
such that
\lambda_{t_{1}}\neq 0
. Again, we have

0=\phi([x,I_{i-m-t_{1}}])=(i-m-t_{1})\left(\lambda_{t_{1}}\phi(I_{i})+\sum_{k=t_{1}+1}^{+\infty}\lambda_{k}\phi(I_{i+k-t_{1}})\right),

thus

\lambda_{t_{1}}\phi(I_{i})+\sum_{k=t_{1}+1}^{+\infty}\lambda_{k}\phi(I_{i+k-t_{1}})=0.

We can find
t_{2}>t_{1}
such that
\lambda_{t_{2}}\neq 0
. Repeating the above process, we finally obtain a sequence of nonzero numbers

\lambda_{t_{1}},\lambda_{t_{2}},\lambda_{t_{3}},\cdots,
a contradiction. Now we have
m\leq i<j
. But this yields that

\phi([x,I_{j-m}])=(j-m)\phi(I_{j})\neq 0,

also a contradiction. Hence, we must have
\mathcal{L}^{\phi}=\mathfrak{p}
, which implies that
W(\phi)
is irreducible.

Next, we assume that
W(\phi)
is irreducible. Then
\mathcal{L}^{\phi}=\mathfrak{p}
. If
|S^{\phi}|=0
, i.e.,
S^{\phi}=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{L}^{\phi}=\mathcal{L}\supsetneq\mathfrak{p}
, a contradiction. If
|S^{\phi}|=1
, say
S^{\phi}=\{n\}
, then it is easy to check that
L_{n}\in\mathcal{L}^{\phi}
, which implies that
\mathcal{L}^{\phi}\supsetneq\mathfrak{p}
, a contradiction (Also see Example
4.1
). The proof is completed.
∎

4.4.
Planer Galilei Conformal algebra

Following
[
BG
]
, the planer Galilean conformal algebra
\mathcal{G}
is a Lie algebra with basis
\{L_{n},H_{n},I_{n},J_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
(different from that in
[
BG
]
) defined by the following commutation relations

\displaystyle[L_{n},L_{m}]=(m-n)L_{m+n},[L_{n},H_{m}]=mH_{m+n},

\displaystyle[L_{n},I_{m}]=(m-n)I_{m+n},[L_{n},J_{m}]=(m-n)J_{m+n},

\displaystyle[H_{n},I_{m}]=I_{m+n},[H_{n},J_{m}]=-J_{m+n},

\displaystyle[H_{n},H_{m}]=[I_{n},I_{m}]=[J_{n},J_{m}]=[I_{n},J_{m}]=0,\quad\forall m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Let

\mathfrak{p}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{H_{n},I_{n},J_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Then
[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{I_{n},J_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Hence, for any Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathbb{C}
we have
\phi(I_{n})=\phi(J_{n})=0
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We set

S^{\phi}=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\phi(H_{n})\neq 0\}.

We call
\phi

finite
if
|S^{\phi}|<+\infty
. Since
I
and
J
are ideals of
\mathcal{G}
, for the
\mathcal{G}
-module
W(\phi)
, we have
(I+J).W(\phi)=0
. Therefore, the
\mathcal{G}
-module
W(\phi)
can be regarded as a
\mathcal{G}/(I+J)
-module while preserving the same irreducibility. Consequently, we obtain a result analogous to Theorem
4.4
for the Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra, namely:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a finite Lie algbera homomorphism. Then
W(\phi)
is irreducible if and only if
|S^{\phi}|\geq 2
.

We certainly can use our theory in Sect.3 to get a lot of simple
\mathcal{G}
-modules by taking

\mathfrak{p}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{I_{n},J_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

4.5.
The Lie algebra
\mathcal{W}(a,b)

Following
[
OR
]
, the Lie algebra
\mathcal{W}(a,b)
is the semi-direct product of centerless Virasoro
\mathrm{Vir}
and its intermediate series module
A_{a,b}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{W}(a,b)=\mathrm{Vir}\ltimes A_{a,b}
where
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
. As a vector space,
\mathcal{W}(a,b)
has a basis
\{L_{i},H_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
with Lie brackets

[L_{i},L_{j}]=(j-i)L_{i+j},~[L_{i},H_{j}]=(a+j+bi)H_{i+j},~[H_{i},H_{j}]=0,\forall,i,j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Note that
\mathcal{W}(0,-1)
is just the Takiff algebra of the centerless Virasoro algebra.
We abbreviate

\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{W}(a,b),~\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{L_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\},~\mathfrak{p}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{H_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Since
[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]=0
, each
\phi\in\mathfrak{p}^{*}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism from
\mathfrak{p}
to
\mathbb{C}
. For
\phi\in\mathfrak{p}^{*}
, we write

S^{\phi}=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\phi(H_{j})\neq 0\}.

We call
\phi\in\mathfrak{p}^{*}

upper
(resp.
lower
)
finite
if there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi(H_{j})=0
for each
j>j_{0}
(resp.
j<j_{0}
).

Let
x
be a nonzero element in
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Then
x
can be uniquely expressed as

(4.1)

x=\lambda_{i}L_{i}+\lambda_{i+1}L_{i+1}+\cdots+\lambda_{j}L_{j}

with
j\geq i
and
\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\neq 0
. Define the
head
(resp.
tail
) of
x
to be
\mathrm{h}(x)=i
(resp.
\mathrm{t}(x)=j
).

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero upper (resp. lower) finite Lie algbera homomorphism and
j_{0}
the maximal (resp. miminal) element in
S^{\phi}
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
a nonzero element. Then

a+j_{0}=(1-b)\mathrm{h}(x)~~(\text{resp.}~a+j_{0}=(1-b)\mathrm{t}(x)).

Proof.

We only prove the case that
\phi
is upper finite. Assume that
x
is shown as (
4.1
). Then

0=\phi\left([x,H_{j_{0}-i}]\right)=\lambda_{i}\left(a+j_{0}-i+bi\right)\phi(H_{j_{0}}).

Since
\lambda_{i}
and
\phi(H_{j_{0}})
are both nonzero, we can obtain
a+j_{0}-i+bi=0
, i.e.

a+j_{0}=(1-b)\mathrm{h}(x).

The proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Assume that
b=1
. Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero upper (resp. lower) finite Lie algbera homomorphism and
j_{0}
the maximal (resp. miminal) element in
S^{\phi}
. Then
W(\phi)
is reducible if and only if
S^{\phi}=\{j_{0}\}
and
a=-j_{0}
.

Proof.

Assume that
S^{\phi}=\{j_{0}\}
and
a=-j_{0}
. It is easy to check that
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}=\mathfrak{g}
, which follows that
W(\phi)
is reducible.

Next, we assume that
W(\phi)
is reducible. Then
\mathfrak{p}\subsetneq\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}
. One can choose a nonzero
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Assume that
x
is shown as (
4.1
). Then
\mathrm{h}(x)=i
By Lemma
4.6
, one has

a+j_{0}=(1-b)\mathrm{h}(x)=(1-1)\mathrm{h}(x)=0.

We have

\phi\left([L_{k},H_{j_{0}-k}]\right)=(a+j_{0})\lambda_{i}\phi\left(H_{j_{0}}\right)=0,~\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since

0=\phi\left([x,H_{j_{0}-(i+1)}]\right)=(a+j_{0}-1)\lambda_{i}\phi(H_{j_{0}-1}),

we have
\phi(H_{j_{0}-1})=0
. By induction on
m
, it is easy to obtain that

\phi(H_{m})=0,~\forall m<j_{0}.

Thus,
S^{\phi}=\{j_{0}\}
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Assume that
b\neq 1
. Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero upper (resp. lower) finite Lie algbera homomorphism and
j_{0}
the maximal (resp. miminal) element in
S^{\phi}
. Then
W(\phi)
is reducible only if
\frac{j_{0}+a}{1-b}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, each irreducible quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
is bland.

Proof.

Assume that
W(\phi)
is reducible. Then
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}\neq 0
. Let
x
be a nonzero element in
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. By lemma
4.6
, one has

\frac{j_{0}+a}{1-b}=\mathrm{h}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}.

It follows that all the nonzero elements in
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
have a same head. This implies that
\mathfrak{g}^{\phi}=\mathbb{C}x\oplus\mathfrak{p}
. By Theorem
3.11
, each irreducible irreducible quasi-Whittaker module of type
\phi
is bland.
∎

Example 4.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{W}(0,-1)
be the Takiff algebra of the centerless Virasoro algebra and
j_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
an odd number. Assume that
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism with
\phi(H_{j_{0}})\neq 0
and
\phi(H_{j})=0
for each
j>j_{0}
(resp.
j<j_{0}
). Then
W(\phi)
is irreducible.

Proof.

It is clear that
\phi
is upper (resp. lower) finite. Since

\frac{0+j_{0}}{1-(-1)}=\frac{j_{0}}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z},

by Proposition
4.8
, we have
W(\phi)
is irreducible.
∎

4.6.
Borel subalgebra
\mathcal{W}_{1}^{++}
of the Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}_{1}

The following conclusion will recover the results in
[
LGZ
]
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{W}_{1}^{++}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{d_{i}\mid\ i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
be the Borel subalgebra of the Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}_{1}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{d_{i}\mid\ i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. The Lie brackets in
\mathfrak{g}
are given by

[d_{m},d_{n}]=(m-n)d_{m+n},\ \text{for any}\ m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Fix a
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathfrak{p}_{k}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{d_{i}\mid\ i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq k}\}
be the ideal of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\phi_{k}:\mathfrak{p}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero Lie algebra homomorphism, then we have
\phi(\mathfrak{p}_{2k+1})=0
from
[\mathfrak{p}_{k},\mathfrak{p}_{k}]=\mathfrak{p}_{2k+1}
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\phi_{k}:\mathfrak{p}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero Lie algebra homomorphism. Then the universal quasi-Whittaker module
W(\phi)
over
\mathcal{W}_{1}^{++}
is irreducible if and only if
\phi_{k}(d_{2k-1})\neq 0
or
\phi_{k}(d_{2k})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\phi_{k}(d_{2k-1})=0
and
\phi_{k}(d_{2k})=0
.
It is easy to check that
d_{k-1}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi_{k}}
.
Therefore, by Theorem
3.3
, the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
W(\phi_{k})
is reducible.

Next, choose

x=\ell_{0}d_{0}+\ell_{1}d_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{k-1}d_{k-1}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi_{k}}.

Assume
\ell_{0}\neq 0
. By
\phi_{k}([x,d_{2k}])=0
, we derive
\phi_{k}(d_{2k})=0
.
Similarly, by
\phi_{k}([x,d_{2k-1}])=0
, we obtain
\phi_{k}(d_{2k-1})=0
.
By induction,
\phi_{k}(\mathfrak{p}_{k})=0
, which contradicts the non-vanishing of
\phi_{k}
.
Thus,
\ell_{0}=0
, i.e.,

x=\ell_{1}d_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{k-1}d_{k-1}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\phi_{k}}.

Case 1:

\phi_{k}(d_{2k})\neq 0
.

From
\phi_{k}([x,d_{2k-1}])=0
, we get
\ell_{1}=0
.
By induction on
\phi_{k}([x,d_{2k-i}])=0
for
i\geq 1
,
we successively find
\ell_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
, so
x=0
.

Case 2:

\phi_{k}(d_{2k})=0
and
\phi_{k}(d_{2k-1})\neq 0
.

Similarly, considering
\phi_{k}([x,d_{2k-1-i}])=0
, induction yields
\ell_{i}=0

for
1\leq i\leq k-1
, hence
x=0
.

By Theorem
3.3
, the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
W(\phi)
is irreducible.
∎

4.7.
Irreducible smooth modules over
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
with height 2

Let
n\geq 2
. Following
[
LCLLZZ
]
, Let
A_{n}^{+}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]
be the polynomial algebra in variables
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}
and let
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
be the derivation Lie algebra of
A_{n}^{+}
.
For
1\leq i\leq n
, we denote by
\partial_{i}=\partial/\partial t_{i}
,
the partial derivation operator with respect to
t_{i}
.
For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
, set

t^{\alpha}=t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}t_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots t_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\ \text{and}\ \partial^{\alpha}=\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}.

We have

\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{t^{\alpha}\partial_{i}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\ i=1,2,\dots,n\}

and the Lie brackets in
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
are given by

\displaystyle[t^{\alpha}\partial_{i},t^{\beta}\partial_{j}]=t^{\alpha}\partial_{i}(t^{\beta})\partial_{j}-t^{\beta}\partial_{j}(t^{\alpha})\partial_{i}.

In this section, we write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
for convenience.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{k}=0
for
k<-1
and

\mathfrak{g}_{k}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{t^{\alpha}\partial_{i}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\ |\alpha|=k+1,\ i=1,2,\dots,n\}

for
k\geq-1
. This gives a natural
\mathbb{Z}
-gradation on
\mathfrak{g}
:

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{k}.

It is clear that
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq r}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. We abbreviate
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}
, then

\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\ltimes\mathfrak{p}.

Moreover,
[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]=\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 2}.
Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero Lie algbera homomorphism, i.e. a linear map such that

\phi\left(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\right)\neq 0,~\text{and}~\phi\left(\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 2}\right)=0.

Definition 4.1
.

A
\mathfrak{g}
-module (or
\mathfrak{a}
-module)
V
is called a
smooth
module
if for any
v\in V
there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{i}.v=0
for all
i\geq n
.

The concept was first introduced by D. Kazhdan and G. Lusztig for affine Kac-Moody algebras
[
KL1
,
KL2
]
in 1993.

Let
V
be a smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module (or
\mathfrak{a}
-module). For
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, set

\displaystyle V^{(r)}=\{v\in V\mid\mathfrak{g}_{\geq r}.v=0\}.

Then
V^{(r)}
is a subspace of
V
and

V^{(0)}\subseteq V^{(1)}\subseteq V^{(2)}\subseteq\cdots.

Define
\ell_{V}=\mathrm{min}\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\mid V^{(r)}\neq 0\}
which is called the
height
of
V
. Next, we will apply the theories in Sections 2 and 3 to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
and its ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, rather than to
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p})
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Keep the notations above. Let
\phi:\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a Lie algbera homomorphism such that

\phi(t_{i}^{2}\partial_{i})\neq 0,~\forall i=1,2,\cdots,n

and

\phi(t^{\alpha}\partial_{i})=0,~\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\text{~with~}|\alpha|=2~\text{and}~\alpha_{i}\neq 2.

Then
W(\phi)
is irreducible. In particular,
V=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\mathfrak{g}}W(\phi)
is an irreducible smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module of height
2
.

Proof.

Denote by

I=\{(1,1)<(1,2)<\cdots<(1,n)<\cdots<(n,1)<(n,2)<\cdots<(n,n)\}.

It is elementary to verify that
\phi\left([t_{i}\partial_{j},t_{p}^{2}\partial_{q}]\right)\neq 0
if and only if
j=p
and
i=q
for
1\leq i,j,p,q\leq n
. It implies that the matrix

D=\left(\phi([t_{i}\partial_{j},t_{p}^{2}\partial_{q}])\right)_{(i,j)\in I,~(p,q)\in I}

has exactly one non-zero element in each row and each column. In particular,

\det D\neq 0
, which provides a nonzero minor of
A_{\phi}
of order
n^{2}
. It follows that
\mathfrak{a}^{\phi}=\mathfrak{p}
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.3
,
W(\phi)
is an irreducible smooth
\mathfrak{a}
-module of height
2
. Combining the conclusion of
[
LCLLZZ
, Theorem 4.7]
again, it can be deduced that
V
is an irreducible smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module of height
2
.
∎

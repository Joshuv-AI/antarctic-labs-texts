Real-World Treatment Patterns and Clinical Outcomes Among Chinese Patients with Stage III NSCLC: The MOOREA Study
Authors: Ligang Xing, Jinming Yu, Xiangjiao Meng, Ren Zhao, Weihua Yang, Yubiao Guo, Juan Li, Min Fan, Chuangying Xiao, Yanping Ren, Lihua Dong, Dongqing Lv, Lujun Zhao, Yingcheng Lin, Xiaotao Zhang, Long Chen, Ye Wang, Yuhui Zhang, Aimin Zang, Gregory Cheng, Anwen Liu, Naixin Liang, Baisong Lou, Jun Wang, Xiaolong Fu, Anhui Shi, Ning Xu, Junfeng Liu, Zuo-Lin Xiang

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStage III non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) presents marked heterogeneity under evolving therapeutic paradigms. Real-world evidence on current treatment practices and outcomes remains limited.ObjectiveThe MOOREA study aimed to evaluate real-world molecular testing, treatment patterns, and clinical outcomes of treatment-naïve Chinese patients with stage III NSCLC.Patients and methodsMOOREA is a prospective, multicenter Chinese study enrolling patients with untreated stage III NSCLC (16 July 2019 to 28 February 2022) from 28 hospitals. Patients were consecutively enrolled. The primary endpoint was treatment pattern of cohort 1 (C1; unresectable stage III NSCLC), and the secondary endpoints included molecular testing pattern, progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS) of C1, and treatment pattern of cohort 2 (C2; resectable stage III NSCLC).ResultsIn total, 486 patients were analyzed (C1: 379; C2: 107). Molecular testing rates were: EGFR (20.0%), ALK (15.0%), and PD-L1 (13.0%). Of the 45.6% (173/379) of individuals in C1 who received chemoradiotherapy (CRT), 53.8% (93/173) underwent consolidation therapy, including 37.6% (35/93) who received immunotherapy (IO). In C2, lobectomy was the main surgical approach (85.8%, 91/106), whereas pneumonectomy was performed on 14.2% of patients (15/106). Adjuvant treatment was planned for 71.4% (75/105) of the patients in C2. For C1, the median follow-up was 27.5 months, with PFS and OS of 12.6 (95% CI: 11.0–14.0) and 33.3 months (95% CI: 29.6-not estimable), respectively. Subgroup analysis showed better OS and PFS for patients receiving CRT with IO consolidation versus CRT only, especially for those who underwent more than six IO consolidation cycles (24-month OS: 79.3% versus 66.4%; PFS: 49.6% versus 24.2%).ConclusionsMOOREA reveals the real-world management of stage III NSCLC in 20 provinces/cities in mainland China and Hong Kong SAR. Patients with unresectable tumors derived significant benefit from radiotherapy and consolidation after CRT. Substantial disparity persists between actual practice and guideline recommendations, necessitating efforts to enhance adherence to guideline-based care.Clinical Trial RegistrationNCT04023812.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s11523-026-01217-0.

Key Points

MOOREA is the first real-world data study to describe treatment patterns and clinical outcomes among 486 patients with stage III NSCLC in China.Among 379 unresectable cases, only 45.6% received CRT (63.6% concurrent, 36.4% sequential). Among CRT-treated patients, 53.8% received consolidation therapy, including 37.6% with IO (54.3% durvalumab) and 24.7% with chemotherapy. IO consolidation after CRT improved patient outcomes compared with CRT alone (median PFS 16.9 versus 10.9 months; 2-year OS 79.3% versus 66.4%), consistent with findings from the PACIFIC-5 study.Among 107 resectable cases, stage IIIA was more common (57.7% versus 30.4% in unresectable cases). Most had N2 involvement (54.2%) and achieved R0 resection (98.1%). Perioperative therapy was frequently applied, with 61.9% receiving neoadjuvant therapy (40% with IO) and 71.4% receiving adjuvant therapy (28% with IO).

Introduction

In China, lung cancer is the most prevalent of the various malignant disease types and the leading cause of cancer-related deaths. According to the National Cancer Center, an estimated 1.06 million new patients were diagnosed with lung cancer in 2022, and it was responsible for 733,300 deaths [1]. Stage III non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which features locally advanced tumors that may have spread to adjacent lymph nodes but not to distant organs, accounts for approximately one-third of patients with newly diagnosed lung cancer [2]. Stage III NSCLC is divided into resectable and unresectable types, reflecting substantial clinical heterogeneity and complicating treatment decisions [3]. The 5-year overall survival (OS) rates for patients with unresectable stage IIIA, IIIB, and IIIC disease are 36%, 26%, and 13%, respectively [4].

Moreover, because therapeutic options for stage III NSCLC have significantly expanded in recent years, diverse treatments have become available. Thus, the diagnostic and therapeutic complexities complicate the management of these advanced-stage tumors.

The treatment landscape for unresectable disease has evolved significantly. Recommendation of the durvalumab consolidation regimen after concurrent chemoradiotherapy (cCRT) for unresectable stage III NSCLC, established by the phase III PACIFIC trial (prolonged progression-free survival (PFS); 5-year OS: 42.9% durvalumab versus 33.4% placebo) [5] was included in the National Comprehensive Cancer Network and Chinese guidelines [6, 7]. Real-world PACIFIC-R data validated the efficacy/tolerability of the regimen, showing longer PFS with cCRT versus sequential CRT (sCRT) (23.7 versus 19.3 months) and with programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) ≥ 1% versus < 1% (22.4 versus 15.6 months) [8]. Building on this, PACIFIC-5 demonstrated that durvalumab improved median PFS versus the effect of a placebo (14.0 versus 6.5 months, P = 0.038) in cCRT and sCRT subgroups among Chinese patients, with a favorable trend toward improved OS, reinforcing its role as a key consolidation strategy for this population [9].

Furthermore, targeted therapies have reshaped management. The LAURA trial showed that osimertinib significantly improved the median PFS in unresectable stage III epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-mutated NSCLC versus the effect of a placebo (39.1 versus 5.6 months), highlighting the importance of EGFR testing for optimizing post-CRT treatment selection, specifically for deciding between consolidation immunotherapy or targeted therapy [10].

For resectable tumors, recent clinical developments of molecular targeted therapy and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have shifted resectable NSCLC treatment paradigms in neoadjuvant/adjuvant settings, demonstrating remarkable survival benefits [11–13].

Consistent with this paradigm shift, the ADAURA study transformed the treatment paradigm for patients with resectable EGFR-mutated NSCLC. This pivotal trial showed that adjuvant osimertinib, a third-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), significantly prolonged disease-free survival and OS in patients with completely resected EGFR-mutated NSCLC [13]. Similarly, the ALINA study demonstrated that adjuvant alectinib significantly improved disease-free survival in patients with resected ALK-positive NSCLC compared with chemotherapy. Collectively, these trials establish a new standard of care (SOC) for EGFR/ALK-positive early-stage lung cancer, emphasizing that targeted therapy is preferred over immunotherapy in these specific molecular subgroups owing to the limited benefit of the latter in these populations [14]. In Non-EGFR/ALK settings, the introduction of immunotherapy (IO) in neoadjuvant and adjuvant contexts has transformed perioperative care for patients with early resectable NSCLC (including stage III). Key advances include adjuvant atezolizumab for PD-L1 ≥ 1% NSCLC (according to IMpower010) [12] and neoadjuvant nivolumab (per CheckMate 816) [11]. Data from the AEGEAN [15], KEYNOTE-671 [16], NEOTORCH [17], CheckMate-77T [18], and Rationale 315 trials [19, 20] have demonstrated the efficacy of perioperative strategies with three or four cycles of neoadjuvant ICIs and 1 year of adjuvant ICIs. These studies in both targeted and immunotherapy settings have established a new SOC, underscoring the critical role of personalized medicine in improving clinical outcomes for patients with stage IB–IIIA disease. However, because current real-world data on stage III NSCLC treatment patterns are scarce, understanding the clinical application of various diagnostic and treatment pathways is a matter of urgency. Herein, we report the findings of the MOOREA study—a prospective, multicenter, noninterventional investigation of real-world molecular testing, treatment patterns, and survival outcomes among Chinese patients with stage III NSCLC.

Patients and Methods

Study Population

Eligible patients were 18 years of age or older, cytologically or histologically diagnosed with stage III NSCLC according to the Eighth Edition American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) Cancer Staging Manual, and naïve to systemic therapy. Patients in unblinded trials were eligible, whereas those in blinded trials were excluded. Patients were consecutively enrolled from 28 centers across Mainland China and Hong Kong SAR, covering diverse geographic regions (North, East, South, and West China) to ensure national representation. In total, 500 patients were assigned to two cohorts on the basis of surgical resectability status: cohort 1 (unresectable, n = 400) and cohort 2 (resectable, n = 100). The study was approved by independent ethics committees/institutional review boards at all participating centers.

Study Objectives and Hypothesis

The goals of the MOOREA study were to evaluate real-world molecular testing, treatment patterns, and clinical outcomes of treatment-naïve Chinese patients with stage III NSCLC. The primary objective was to describe treatment patterns in unresectable stage III NSCLC. The secondary objectives were to (1) observe clinical outcomes in patients with unresectable tumors, (2) observe molecular testing patterns, (3) estimate radiation pneumonitis rates, (4) describe treatment patterns among patients with resectable tumors, and (5) explore treatment choice determinants. All statistical analyses were descriptive in nature, and predefined hypotheses were not tested.

Data Collection

Patients in 28 centers across 20 provinces/cities in Mainland China and Hong Kong SAR were enrolled in the MOOREA study, with baseline, molecular testing, and treatment pattern data collected for both cohorts and survival outcomes for cohort 1 only. PFS and OS for cohort 2 were not research endpoints, and survival follow-up data for the resectable population were not collected in this study. Pre-progression treatment patterns had been recorded for cohort 1, whereas neoadjuvant data before surgery and adjuvant plans at first post-surgery follow-up were available for cohort 2. All testing/treatments were implemented at the discretion of the physicians. Survival follow-up occurred every 6 months, with a minimum follow-up period of 24 months. Surviving patients at the end of the study were censored, and no further follow-up was arranged.

Statistical Analysis

Baseline characteristics, molecular testing, and treatment patterns were descriptively summarized. Kaplan–Meier curves were generated to estimate PFS and OS, and logistic regression analysis was used to explore treatment choice factors.

For cohort 1, PFS was defined as the period from treatment initiation to progression/death, censored at last assessment if neither occurred. OS was defined as the period from treatment initiation to death, censored at the last alive date if surviving. Treatment initiation was defined as the first systemic therapy (chemotherapy, targeted therapy, IO, etc.) post-enrollment.

The full analysis set (FAS) included all patients receiving antitumor treatment: cohort 1 (nonsurgical) and cohort 2 (surgical). All analyses were performed using SAS 9.4 software.

Results

Patients

From 16 July 2019 to 28 February 2022, 520 patients were screened and 500 were finally enrolled in the study (Fig. 1). The data cutoff date was 31 August 2022. The FAS included 486 patients: 379 in cohort 1 and 107 in cohort 2.

The baseline characteristics of the patients are presented in Table 1. Among the 486 patients in the FAS, the median age was 63.0 years, 57.4% were less than 65 years of age, 82.9% were males, 70.7% were smokers, and 99.2% were of Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status 0/1. With regard to the tumor characteristics, 30.4%, 45.5%, and 24.1% of the patients in cohort 1 and 57.7%, 35.6%, and 6.7% of those in cohort 2 were at stage IIIA, IIIB, and IIIC, respectively. Squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma were prevalent, with similar incidences between the two cohorts. Most lesions were T3 (25.3%) and T4 tumors (41.8%) with N2 (47.5%) and N3 nodes (34.0%). Most patients were rural residents (56.3%), were covered by the national basic medical insurance (82.5%), and had a family annual income of 20,000 to 150,000 RMB (82.0%).

Molecular Testing Patterns

In the FAS (n = 486), the EGFR, ALK, and PD-L1 testing rates were 20.0%, 15.0%, and 13.0%, respectively, with positivity rates of 39.2%, 23.3%, and 73.0%, respectively (Fig. 2A, B). The testing rates of EGFR, ALK, and PD-L1 in cohort 1 (21.4%, 15.8%, and 14.0%, respectively) mirrored those of the overall population, whereas cohort 2 showed lower rates (15.0%, 12.2%, and 9.4%, respectively). Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue was the primary sample type (46.9–55.6%) for testing, whereas 4.2–11.5% were blood samples (Fig. 2C). Most tests (63.3–74.0%) were performed in on-site clinical laboratories (Fig. 2D). The molecular testing information for the two cohorts is shown in Supplementary Table S1.

Treatment Patterns of Unresectable Stage III NSCLC

In cohort 1 (n = 379, Fig. 3A), 53.0% (201/379) received radiotherapy (RT) and 45.6% (173/379) received CRT. Of the latter group, 29.0% (110/379) received cCRT and 16.6% (63/379) underwent sCRT. Of the 47.0% (178/379) of patients who did not undergo RT, 60.0% (107/178) received cytotoxic chemotherapy (Chemo) with/without anti-angiogenesis therapy and 27.0% (48/178) received chemotherapy plus immunotherapy (Chemo+IO).

A Summary of treatment classes in cohort 1; B Consolidation treatment information for the cCRT-receiving patients; C Consolidation treatment information for the sCRT-receiving patients. AA, anti-angiogenesis therapy; Chemo, cytotoxic chemotherapy; CRT, chemoradiotherapy; cCRT, concurrent chemoradiotherapy; IO, immunotherapy; TKI, tyrosine kinase inhibitor; RT, radiotherapy; sCRT, sequential chemoradiotherapy

Of the 53.8% (93/173) of patients who received consolidation therapy after CRT, 24.7% (23/93) were on Chemo, 37.6% (35/93) underwent IO, and 14.0% (13/93) received Chemo+IO. Consolidation durvalumab was used in 11.0% (19/173) of the patients who received CRT. Among the cCRT-receiving patients (Fig. 3B), 60.9% (67/110) further received consolidation therapy, of which 44.8% (30/67) was IO, and durvalumab was used in more than half of the patients (56.7%, 17/30). Among the sCRT-receiving patients (Fig. 3C), 41.3% (26/63) underwent consolidation therapy, with Chemo (30.8%, 8/26) being the most commonly used treatment modality.

For the 201 patients receiving RT (Table 2), the treatment was predominantly definitive (cCRT: 89.1%; sCRT: 82.5%; RT mono: 61.1%) (Supplementary Table S2A). The most frequently utilized technique was intensity-modulated RT (cCRT: 55.4%; sCRT: 54.8%; RT mono: 77.8%). The majority of patients received a total PTV dose of 54–66 Gy, with a median dose of 60.0 Gy for cCRT and 56.0 Gy for sCRT. The median duration of radiotherapy was 41.5 days [Q1, Q3: 37.0, 45.0] for cCRT, 40.5 days [34.0, 48.0] for sCRT, and 37.5 days [19.0, 44.0] for RT monotherapy.

The chemotherapy regimens used for CRT were mainly cisplatin or carboplatin based, with median [interquartile range (IQR)] cycles of 2.0 [1.0, 2.0] for the cCRT and 3.0 [2.0, 4.0] for the sCRT groups (Supplementary Table S2B). Moreover, 59.1% (65/110) of the patients receiving cCRT underwent induction chemotherapy (median [IQR] cycles: 2.0 [1.0, 3.0]).

Additionally, patients who showed positivity for the various molecular markers (Supplementary Table S3) exhibited RT status-stratified treatment patterns consistent with those of cohort 1: 24.1% (7/29) positive for EGFR received EGFR inhibitors and 20.0% (3/15) positive for ALK received ALK inhibitors versus 1.9% who received EGFR inhibitors, 0.8% who received ALK inhibitors, and 14.3% who received IO in the entire cohort 1. Among the PD-L1-positive patients, 21.0% (8/38) received IO-based regimens in combination with chemotherapy (with or without anti-angiogenesis therapy) (Supplementary Table S3).

Among the PD-L1-positive patients (n = 38), 94.7% received cancer therapy, with 57.9% undergoing RT. Additionally, 86.4% of the RT-treated patients underwent CRT (cCRT: 47.4%; sCRT: 52.6%). Only 4.6% received other treatments, including RT+IO. All PD-L1-negative patients (n = 15) received cancer therapy, with 66.7% undergoing RT, and all received CRT (cCRT: 40%; sCRT: 60%) and no RT monotherapy or other treatments involving RT+IO (Supplementary Table S4).

Factors Impacting Treatment Choices

Multivariate analysis revealed that older age, male, urban resident’s basic medical insurance, and initial visit to RT departments were independent predictors of being in cohort 1 (unresectable), whereas initial visit to thoracic surgery departments and N1 lymph node status were associated with cohort 2 (resectable), all with significant p-values (< 0.05) (Table 3, model A; Supplementary Table S5A). The factors that influenced RT choice in cohort 1 were analyzed. Univariate analysis showed that age, hospital type, and physician specialty (RT) were significantly associated with concomitant RT choice (all P < 0.05; Supplementary Table S5B). Multivariate analysis further confirmed that these factors independently influenced RT use in cohort 1, with patients 65 years of age or older being less likely, and those seen in specialized hospital or RT departments more likely, to receive RT (Table 3, model B).

Treatment Patterns of Resectable Stage III NSCLC

In cohort 2, lobectomy was the predominant treatment (85.8%, 91/106), followed by pneumonectomy (14.2%, 15/106), and 98.1% (104/106) of the patients achieved complete resection (R0). Neoadjuvant therapy was used in 61.9% (65/105) of the patients, whereas Chemo and IO+Chemo were used at 40.0% (26/65) each (Fig. 4A). Post-surgery adjuvant plans were made for 71.4% (75/105) of the patients, with Chemo (56.0%, 42/75) being the most common, followed by IO+Chemo (21.3%, 16/75), TKI (12.0%, 9/75), and IO (6.7%, 5/75) (Fig. 4B).

Survival Outcomes for Patients with Unresectable Stage III NSCLC and Subgroup Analysis

Cohort 1 had a median follow-up of 27.5 months. In this group, the median PFS (mPFS) was 12.6 months (95% CI: 11.0–14.0 months) and the median OS (mOS) was 33.3 months (95% CI: 29.6 months to not estimable [NE]) (Fig. 5A, B). The 1-year PFS and OS rates were 50.8% and 81.8%, respectively, whereas the 2-year rates were 30.4% and 62.8%, respectively.

For G and H, only curves of subgroups with ≥ 10 patients are presented

The subgroup analysis showed that the RT-treated patients (n = 201) had better survival periods than the non-RT-treated group did (n = 178), with mPFS periods of 13.7 versus 10.9 months and mOS of NE versus 30.1 months (Fig. 5C, D). The survival periods for the cCRT-treated patients (n = 61) were also better than those for the sCRT-treated individuals (n = 44), with mPFS periods of 16.2 versus 8.8 months and mOS of NE versus NE (Fig. 5E, F). CRT with IO consolidation improved the mPFS period (16.9 versus 10.9 months) and 2-year OS rate (79.3% versus 66.4%), aligning with the findings of PACIFIC-5 (Supplementary Table S6) [5]. Among the patients who did not undergo RT, the mPFS and mOS were respectively 8.4 (95% CI: 6.7–11.0) and 22.2 months (95% CI: 14.1-NE) for those who received Chemo alone (n = 107) and 15.1 (95% CI: 9.5–24.4) and 30.9 months (95% CI: 16.5 to NE) for those who received Chemo+IO (n = 48) (Fig. 5G, H). Compared with no consolidation therapy, IO consolidation for more than six cycles showed marked benefits in terms of 12-month (94.3% versus 83.4%), 18-month (91.3% versus 74.5%), and 24-month OS (79.3% versus 66.4%) and 12-month (66.1% versus 43.1%), 18-month (49.6% versus 33.0%), and 24-month PFS (49.6% versus 24.2%) (Fig. 5I, J; Supplementary Table S6).

The severity was generally mild to moderate; the majority of events were grade 1 (4.5%) or grade 2 (5.5%). Severe radiation pneumonitis (grade 3) was infrequent, occurring in only 2.0% (4/201) of patients, and no life-threatening (grade 4) or fatal (grade 5) events were reported. Discussion

Stage III NSCLCs are highly heterogeneous lesions, and their treatment is greatly dependent on multidisciplinary collaboration. The MOOREA study is the first prospective, nation-wide, observational study to characterize molecular testing, treatment patterns, and survival among patients with stage III NSCLC in China.

Although CRT remains the SOC for unresectable stage III NSCLC, its real-world use was low in this analysis (cCRT: 29.0%; sCRT: 16.6%), being comparable to Chemo (28.2%) or Chemo+IO (12.7%) per the KINDLE study [21]. Barriers to SOC include lack of a multidisciplinary team (MDT), limited RT access, educational gaps, and guideline non-adherence. Logistic regression analysis in our Chinese cohort indicated that patients first visiting RT departments had a higher likelihood of receiving CRT. This finding is particularly relevant given that 68.9% of cohort 1 initially presented to non-RT departments, suggesting that initial department of care may influence subsequent treatment decisions. Hospital MDT adoption rates (35–54%) in China further limit standardized care [22]. Given the tumor/nodal heterogeneity and treatment variability, MDT is recommended for the optimal care of patients with stage III NSCLC [23–26], especially when defining the resectability of the lesion. The involvement of a MDT improves guideline adherence and survival, although implementation remains challenging [27]. The survival analysis showed that RT-receiving patients had better PFS/OS compared with the patients who did not receive RT, with cCRT outperforming sCRT. Patients who received Chemo alone had poor outcomes (mPFS: 8.4 months; mOS: 22.2 months) compared with the overall cohort (12.6 and 33.3 months, respectively). Removing treatment barriers and promoting MDTs are essential to optimize care. The survival analysis of the MOOREA study demonstrated that CRT combined with IO consolidation yielded superior PFS and OS compared with CRT alone, and longer IO consolidation cycles (more than six cycles) were associated with more pronounced survival benefits; however, this observed survival advantage cannot be simply attributed to the direct therapeutic effect of IO consolidation, as there exists substantial potential selection bias in the real-world clinical setting that profoundly confounds this result.

On the basis of the findings of the PACIFIC trial, consolidation durvalumab after cCRT has become the new SOC for unresectable stage III NSCLC, demonstrating significant OS and PFS improvements with manageable toxicity. Updated 5-year data show 42.9% survival and 33.1% PFS rates among durvalumab recipients [28]. PACIFIC-R confirmed these benefits in real-world settings, with longer PFS in cCRT versus sCRT groups (23.7 versus 19.3 months) and in those with high PD-L1 expression (22.4 versus 15.6 months) [8]. In the MOOREA study, 53.8% (93/173) of patients received consolidation therapy after CRT, with IO as the predominant regimen (37.6%). Durvalumab was used in 11.0% (19/173) of patients, whereas 60.9% who underwent cCRT (67/110) received consolidation therapy. Of these cCRT-treated patients, 44.8% received IO (30/67), with 56.7% (17/30) using durvalumab. These data reflect good acceptance of IO consolidation among Chinese physicians post-durvalumab approval. Despite this, non-approved ICIs were commonly used, highlighting the need for further education. The success of the LAURA trial demonstrated that osimertinib consolidation significantly improves progression-free survival in patients with unresectable stage III EGFR-mutated NSCLC [10]. Therefore, molecular testing before initiating consolidation therapy is crucial to identify these driver mutations and ensure that patients receive optimal targeted treatments rather than standard immunotherapy, as the latter has shown limited efficacy in these specific molecular subgroups.

For resectable stage III NSCLC, surgery with neoadjuvant/adjuvant therapy aims for achieving cure. In cohort 2, 61.9% of the patients received neoadjuvant therapy and 71.4% underwent planned adjuvant therapy, with IO+Chemo accounting for 40.0%/21.3% and IO monotherapy 3.1%/6.7% in neoadjuvant/adjuvant settings, respectively. During MOOREA enrollment, phase 2 data (e.g., NADIM, SAKK 16/14) [29, 30] were used to determine neoadjuvant/adjuvant IO use, pending phase 3 evidence. The CheckMate 816 trial validated the results for neoadjuvant IO, showing 24.0% of patients achieving a pathological complete response with nivolumab+Chemo versus 2.2% treated with Chemo alone [11]. Adjuvant IO benefits were seen in the IMpower010 (atezolizumab, PD-L1 ≥1%) and PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091 (pembrolizumab) trials. The perioperative paradigm, exemplified by KEYNOTE-671 (pembrolizumab), further enhanced outcomes through combined neoadjuvant and adjuvant ICI administration. MOOREA revealed a real-world-trial gap: although trials used standardized IO regimens (e.g., 100% IO+Chemo use in CheckMate 816), the neoadjuvant IO+Chemo use rate in China was only 40%, likely because of reimbursement barriers and guideline non-adherence. With regulatory approvals, real-world treatment patterns are expected to evolve.

A prominent finding of this study is the extremely low molecular testing rates of EGFR (20.0%), ALK (15.0%), and PD-L1 (13.0%) in the overall cohort, which is a key secondary endpoint and has not been fully emphasized in previous real-world studies. Notably, these testing rates are inconsistent with the clinical demand for personalized treatment, especially for Asian populations with high EGFR mutation rates. This disparity likely reflects the historical treatment landscape; during the MOOREA enrollment period (2019–2022), the evidence-based management for stage III disease was still evolving. The treatment landscape for stage III NSCLC has evolved with several pivotal trials. While the ADAURA trial established adjuvant osimertinib as a standard of care [13], its full integration into routine real-world molecular testing protocols was likely still in progress during our study window. More recently, the ALINA study further extended the adjuvant targeted therapy paradigm to ALK-positive patients [14], while the LAURA trial defined a new standard for osimertinib consolidation in unresectable EGFR-mutated stage III NSCLC [10]. Although molecular testing did not influence the choice of RT for unresectable lesions, positive results were associated with targeted therapy/IO use, likely because of established first-line evidence in stage IV (including locally advanced trials). Post-surgery EGFR TKIs improved disease-free survival in patients with resected NSCLC, with guidelines recommending adjuvant therapy for those with mutated EGFR [13, 31–33]. Given that EGFR mutations are common in Asians, experts advocate testing for patients with resected stage III tumors [34]. The low EGFR testing rate (15.0%) among the MOOREA cohort 2 patients highlights the need to improve testing. The success with osimertinib consolidation in the LAURA trial may shift treatment paradigms for unresectable stage III NSCLC and influence molecular testing patterns [10].

MOOREA is the first prospective study to characterize treatment and survival among Chinese patients with stage III NSCLC, filling a gap left by the global KINDLE study that excluded Mainland China. The MOOREA study has the following highlights: (1) prospective data collection across two predefined cohorts (resectable and unresectable), enabling direct comparison of treatment patterns; and (2) enrollment during 2019–2022, post-durvalumab approval, allowing assessment of novel therapies (especially IO) in clinical practice. This study also has some limitations: (1) findings may not generalize to nontertiary hospitals; (2) only initial treatment patterns were collected (no post-progression data); (3) planned adjuvant therapies in cohort 2 may differ from actual practice; (4) the sample size of molecularly positive subgroups is small, and the interpretation of treatment patterns needs to be cautious; (5) PFS and OS of cohort 2 were not prespecified as research endpoints, and survival follow-up for the resectable population was not conducted; this will be addressed in subsequent research.

Conclusions

To the best of our knowledge, MOOREA is the largest prospective study exploring real-world treatment patterns among Chinese patients with stage III NSCLC. The findings reveal complex treatment landscapes: less than 50% of patients with unresectable tumors received CRT, and only approximately 50% of them underwent consolidation therapy, highlighting a significant gap from guideline-recommended care. Notably, the molecular testing rates of EGFR, ALK, and PD-L1 are extremely low in this real-world cohort, which is inconsistent with the personalized treatment needs of stage III NSCLC, especially for Asian populations with high EGFR mutation rates. By contrast, more than two-thirds of patients with resectable lesions received planned adjuvant therapy, indicating substantial potential for adjuvant treatment in clinical practice.

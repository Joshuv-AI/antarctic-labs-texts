Quasi-maximal ideals and ring extensions
Gabriel Picavet* and Martine Picavet-L’Hermitte

Abstract
. Alan and al. defined and studied quasi-maximal ideals. We add a comprehensive characterization of these ideals, introducing submaximal ideals. The conductor of a finite minimal extension R ⊂ S R\subset S is quasi-maximal in S S . This allows us to give a new characterization of these extensions. We also examine the links between quasi-maximal ideals and Badawi 2-absorbing ideals.

Introduction and Notation

The paper being rather lengthy, we will give a brief introduction. Any unexplained terminology can be found by the reader in the ad hoc section.
We consider the category of commutative and unital rings. If

In

In Section 2, we give a lot of equivalences for an ideal to be quasi-maximal. A characterization of quasi-maximal ideals is given by Theorem

2.
Properties of quasi-maximal ideals

Let
I
and

be two ideals of a ring

[

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
P\subset Q
in
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, then
Q\nsucc P
.

Proof.

If there exists
P\subset Q
in
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
such that

, there is some

again a contradiction for the same reason.
∎

We recall the three types of quasi-maximal ideals:

Proposition 2.2
.

[
1
, Proposition 3]
A quasi-maximal ideal
I
of a ring
R
satisfies one of the following mutually exclusive properties :

(1)

I
is a maximal ideal of
R
.

(2)

I
is the intersection of two (distinct) maximal ideals of
R
.

(3)

There exists a maximal ideal
M
of
R
such that
I
is
M
-primary and satisfies
M^{2}\subseteq I\subset M
.

Now the conductor of a minimal integral extension has exactly one of the three types of a quasi-maximal ideal, as we will see in Section 4. This suggested to us to give the next definition because
[
1
, Proposition 3]
has a kind of converse. In order to know what type of quasi-maximal ideals we are dealing with, we will term them with the same name as the one of the corresponding minimal integral extension. See Section 4 for the link between these two notions.

Definition 2.3
.

An ideal
I
of a ring
R
is said to be

(1)

inert
if
I\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
.

(2)

decomposed
if
I
is the intersection of two (distinct) maximal ideals of
R
.

(3)

ramified
if there exists
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
such that
I
is
M
-primary,
M^{2}\subseteq I\subset M
and
M=I+Ra
for any
a\in M\setminus I
, so that
M=\sqrt{I}
.

In the proofs considering the different types, we often drop the inert case since they are obvious.

Theorem
2.4
gives a lot of equivalent statements for an ideal to be quasi-maximal. It completes
[
1
, Proposition 3]
by giving a converse and explains why Definition
2.3
characterizes quasi-maximal ideals. More precisely, we write
I\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(R)
(resp.
\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R),\ \mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(R)
) when
I
is inert, (resp. ramified, decomposed) in
R
. In fact,
\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(R)=\mathrm{Max}(R)
. In Section 5, Theorem
5.2
gives an additional equivalence using 2-absorbing ideals introduced by Badawi in
[
5
]
. If
R\subseteq S
is an extension,
I\in\mathrm{QMax(R)}
and
J\in\mathrm{QMax(S)}
, we say that
I
and
J
are

if they have the same type.

Theorem 2.4
.

The statements below are equivalent for a proper ideal
I
of a ring
R
and imply
\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)\subseteq\mathrm{Max}(R)
:

(1)

I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
.

(2)

Any proper ideal of
R/I
is in
\mathrm{QMax}(R/I)
.

(3)

0\in\mathrm{QMax}(R/I)
.

(4)

1\leq\mathrm{L}_{R}(R/I)\leq 2
and
|\mathrm{V}_{R/I}(0)|\leq 2
.

(5)

\mathcal{O}(R/I)\leq 3
with
\mathcal{O}(R/I)=3
if and only if
|\mathrm{Max}(R/I)|=2
.

(6)

\mathrm{o}(I)\leq 3
with
\mathrm{o}(I)=3
if and only if
|\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)\cap\mathrm{Max}(R)|=2
.

(7)

I\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(R)\cup\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)\cup\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(R)
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) by
[
1
, Corollary 2]
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3): Obvious.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(4): We consider the three cases for

0\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(R/I)

[

Let

.

(4)
\Rightarrow

.

If

, then

.

If

At last,

.

(5)
\Rightarrow
(6): Obvious.

(6)

(1): If

Recall that a

(SPIR) is a principal ideal ring

Proposition 2.5
.

An ideal

of a ring

is in
\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
if and only if

is a SPIR whose maximal ideal is nilpotent of index 2.

Proof.

Assume that

and let

be such that

is

-primary. We may remark that the inclusion

given in Definition

implies that

is

-primary. According to Theorem

,

has only two proper ideals:

and

with

. Then,

is a local Noetherian ring. Moreover,

is principal with

. Therefore

is principal by

Conversely, assume that

is a SPIR whose maximal ideal

is nilpotent of index 2. Then,

and

.

Remark 2.6
.

(1) The condition (5) of Theorem
2.4
asserts that for a quasi-maximal ideal
I
of a ring
R
, the ring
R/I
has finitely many ideals. In
[
2
, Corollary 2.4]
, Anderson and Chun characterize a ring with finitely many ideals. A ring has only finitely many ideals if and only if it is a finite direct product of finite local rings, SPIRs, and fields. If

, then

has finitely many ideals. Each type of quasi-maximal ideal determines the nature of the ring

as follows: If
I\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(R)
, then

is a field. If
I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(R)
, that is an intersection of two distinct maximal ideals

and

, then

is a product of two fields. If
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
, then

is a SPIR by Proposition
2.5
. In any case,
R/I
is an Artinian ring.

We may remark that the condition
\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)\subseteq\mathrm{Max}(R)
is equivalent to
\dim(R/I)=0
.

(2) Since a ramified ideal is
M
-primary for a maximal ideal
M
, a product of quasi-maximal ideals is a primary decomposition of ideals.

(3) If
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
, then
(\sqrt{I})^{2}\subseteq I
holds, with
\sqrt{I}=I
in the inert and decomposed cases. In the ramified case,
\sqrt{I}=M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
shows that
(\sqrt{I})^{2}=M^{2}\subseteq I
is satisfied by the definition of a ramified ideal. We infer that if
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
, so is
\sqrt{I}
.

Definition 2.7
.

We say that an ideal
I
of a ring
R
is
submaximal
if there exists
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
such that
I\prec M
.
Therefore, a submaximal ideal is not maximal.

Proposition 2.8
.

A proper ideal
I
of a ring
R
is submaximal if and only if
M\succ I
for any
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
such that
I\subset M
.

Proof.

One implication is obvious.

Conversely, assume that
I
is submaximal, so that there exists
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
such that
I\prec M
, whence
I\not\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
. We claim that
M^{\prime}\succ I
for any
M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
such that
I\subset M^{\prime}
. Otherwise, there exists
M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
such that
I\subset M^{\prime}
with
M^{\prime}\nsucc I
, giving that
M^{\prime}\neq M
. This means that there exists an ideal
J
of
R
such that
I\subset J\subset M^{\prime}
. Then,
I\subseteq M\cap J\subseteq M\cap M^{\prime}\subset M
. We infer that
I=M\cap J=M\cap M^{\prime}\ (*)
since
M\cap M^{\prime}\neq M
. Therefore,
J=M^{\prime}
, a contradiction. Indeed, it is enough to localize
(*)
at
M,M^{\prime}
and
N
for any
N\in\mathrm{Max}(R),\ N\neq M,M^{\prime}
to get
I_{M}=M_{M}
, and
J_{M}=M^{\prime}_{M}=R_{M}
because
J\not\subseteq M
since
I=J
if
J\subseteq M
by
(*)
, a contradiction,
I_{M^{\prime}}=M^{\prime}_{M^{\prime}}=J_{M^{\prime}},\ M_{M^{\prime}}=R_{M^{\prime}}
and
I_{N}=M_{N}=J_{N}=M^{\prime}_{N}=R_{N}
.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
I
be a non maximal ideal of
R
. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
.

(2)

I
is submaximal.

(3)

I
is strictly contained only in maximal ideals.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): The different types of quasi-maximal ideals gotten in the proof of Theorem
2.4
show that a quasi-maximal ideal of
R
which is not maximal is obviously submaximal.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3): If
I
is submaximal,
M\succ I
for any
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)\cap\mathrm{V}(I)
by Proposition
2.8
. Hence
I
is strictly contained only in maximal ideals.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1): Assume that
I
is strictly contained only in maximal ideals. We claim that
I
is contained in at most two proper ideals different from
I
, with exactly two if they are maximal. Let
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
be such that
I\subset M
and assume that there is an ideal
J\neq M
such that
I\subset J
. Then
J\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
. But
I\subseteq J\cap M\subset J,M
shows that
I=J\cap M
because
J\cap M
is not maximal. Therefore
I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(R)
by Definition
2.3
. If
I
is contained in only one (maximal) ideal, Theorem
2.4
says that
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
because
\mathrm{o}(I)=2
.
∎

This Theorem shows that any proper ideal containing strictly a quasi-maximal ideal is maximal.

According to

Proposition 2.10

. If

, then

for some

. Set

. We deduce that

for any
x\in N^{\prime}\setminus N
. If
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
, then
M^{2}\subseteq I\subset M
shows that
N=(0:x)
for any

. So, in any case,

is a Kasch ring. Then,

by the property of Kasch rings.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

The converse of Proposition
2.10
does not hold in general as we can see in the following example: Let
R
be a ring and
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
be such that
M\neq M^{2}
and
M\nsucc M^{2}
. Setting
R^{\prime}:=R/M^{2}
and
M^{\prime}:=M/M^{2}
, we get that
(R^{\prime},M^{\prime})
is a local ring. Let
x\in M^{\prime},\ x\neq 0
. Then,
M^{\prime}:=(0:x)
, so that
R/M^{2}
is a Kasch ring, but
M^{2}\not\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
because
M\nsucc M^{2}
.

Of course, any ring has inert ideals, and any non local ring has decomposed ideals. We are going to show that ramified ideals exist in a ring having a finitely generated maximal ideal.

Proposition 2.12
.

If
R
is a ring which is not a field and such that there exists
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
finitely generated, there exists
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
such that
I\subset M
.

Proof.

Set
M=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Rx_{i}
and
\mathcal{F}:=\{I
ideal of
R\mid I\subset M\}
. Then,
\mathcal{F}\neq\emptyset
because
0\in\mathcal{F}
. We claim that
\mathcal{F}
is inductive. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be a linearly ordered subset of
\mathcal{F}
and set
J:=\cup[I_{\alpha}\mid I_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}]
. Then,
J
is an ideal of
R
such that
J\subseteq M
. Assume that
J=M
. Hence, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, there exists some
I_{\alpha_{i}}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
such that
x_{i}\in I_{\alpha_{i}}
. Let
I_{\alpha_{k}}
be the greatest of such
I_{\alpha_{i}}
. Therefore
I_{\alpha_{k}}\in\mathcal{F}
with
x_{i}\in I_{\alpha_{k}}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, so that
M\subseteq I_{\alpha_{k}}\subset M
, a contradiction. We get
J\subset M
, giving
J\in\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}
has a maximal element
I
such that
I\subset M
. In particular,
M\succ I
, because, if there exists some ideal
K
such that
I\subset K\subset M
, we should have
K\in\mathcal{F}
, a contradiction with the maximality of
I
. Then,
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
by Theorem
2.9
.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

If
J
is an ideal of a ring
R
such that
J\subset M
, where
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
is finitely generated, there exists
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
such that
J\subseteq I\subset M
.

Proof.

Set
R^{\prime}:=R/J
and
M^{\prime}:=M/J
, so that
R^{\prime}
is not a field. Then,
M^{\prime}
is a finitely generated maximal ideal of
R^{\prime}
and Proposition
2.12
says that there exists an ideal
K\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
of
R^{\prime}

such that
K\subset M^{\prime}
. We infer from Theorem
2.4
that
0\in\mathrm{QMax}(R^{\prime}/K)
. Moreover, there exists an ideal
I
of
R
such that
K=I/J
with
I\subset M
. As
R^{\prime}/K=(R/J)/(I/J)\cong R/I
, the same Theorem shows that
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
is such that
J\subseteq I\subset M
.
∎

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring which is not a field. For any
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
, there exists
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
such that
M\succ I
.

Proof.

Use Corollary
2.13
.
∎

Proposition 2.15
.

If
J
is an
M
-primary ideal of a ring
R
, where

is such that
1\leq\mathrm{L}_{R}(M/J)<\infty
, there exists

such that

.

Proof.

Let
J
be an
M
-primary ideal of a ring
R
, where
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
such that

. Then there exists a decreasing sequence of ideals
\{J_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
of
R
such that
J_{0}=M
and

with

. Setting

, we get that

∎

Proposition 2.16
.

If
R
is a ring and
K:=\cap\{I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)\}
, then
\mathrm{J}(R)^{2}\subseteq\cap[M^{2}\mid M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)]\subseteq K\subseteq\mathrm{J}(R)
.

For any

, and any maximal ideal is in

. Then, we have

.
∎

Remark 2.17
.

Contrary to the Jacobson radical, the nilradical is not always comparable to
K
as we can see in the following example. Let
(S,N)
be a local PID and set

for some

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\}
be a finite family of rings and

. The ideals of
\mathrm{QMax}(R)
are the ideals of the form
M_{i}\times M_{j}\times\Pi_{k\neq i,j}R_{k}
, where
M_{i}
(resp.
M_{j}
)
\in\mathrm{Max}(R_{i})
(resp.
\in\mathrm{Max}(R_{j})
) and
I_{i}\times\Pi_{k\neq i}R_{k}
, where
I_{i}\in\mathrm{QMax}(R_{i})
(after some reordering).

Proof.

Let

. Then
\mathrm{o}(I)\leq 3
with
\mathrm{o}(I)=3
if and only if
|\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)\cap\mathrm{Max}(R)|=2
by Theorem
2.4
.

If
I
is contained in two maximal ideals of

, then
I
is an intersection of these two maximal ideals, and is of the form

, where
M_{i}
(resp.
M_{j}
)
\in\mathrm{Max}(R_{i})
(resp.
\in\mathrm{Max}(R_{j})
) after some reordering.

If

is contained in only one maximal ideal

of

A ring

\mathrm{Max}
-ring
if any nonzero

-nilpotent

Proposition 2.19
.

For any

, where

is a
\mathrm{Max}
-ring which is not a field, there exists a quasi-maximal ideal

.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
. Then
M
is an
R
-module whose

-submodules are ideals of

. As any maximal submodule of

is a submaximal ideal, and according to Theorem

, it follows that there exists a quasi-maximal ideal

.
∎

Corollary 2.20
.

Let
R
be a
\mathrm{J}
-regular ring which is not a field and such that
\mathrm{J}(R)
is
\mathrm{T}
-nilpotent. Then, for any

, there exists a quasi-maximal ideal

.

Proof.

By
[

, Theorem, p.1135]
,

is a
\mathrm{Max}
-ring. An application of Proposition

shows that for any

, there exists a quasi-maximal ideal

.
∎

Obviously, maximal ideals are inert and in every non local ring the intersection of two maximal ideals is decomposed. The next corollary gives a characterization of maximal ideals covering ramified ideals.

Corollary 2.21
.

Let
R
be a ring and
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
. There exists
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
with
I\subset M
if and only if
M^{2}\neq M
.

Proof.

If
M^{2}=M
, there cannot exists some ideal
I
of
R
such that
M^{2}\subseteq I\subset M
. This gives one implication.

Conversely, assume that
M^{2}\neq M
. Set
R^{\prime}:=R/M^{2},\ M^{\prime}:=M/M^{2}
and let
f:R\to R^{\prime}
be the surjective natural map, where
R^{\prime}
is not a field. Then,
(R^{\prime},M^{\prime})
is a local ring with
M^{\prime 2}=0
and
M^{\prime}=\mathrm{J}(R^{\prime})
is
\mathrm{T}
-nilpotent. Moreover,
R^{\prime}/\mathrm{J}(R^{\prime})=R^{\prime}/M^{\prime}
is a field, giving that
R^{\prime}
is
\mathrm{J}
-regular. According to Corollary
2.20
, there exists a quasi-maximal ideal
I^{\prime}\subset M^{\prime}
, which is necessarily in
\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R^{\prime})
since
R^{\prime}
is local. Set

which is in
\mathrm{QMax}(R)
by
[
1
, Proposition 2]
with
M^{2}\subseteq I
.
Hence,
I\subset M
and
I
is
M
-primary, whence in
\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
.
∎

A ring

-ring.

Since

, or

and

If

is local, then

inert or ramified, so that

3.
Quasi-maximal ideals through extensions

In
[
1
, Proposition 2]
, the behavior of quasi-maximal ideals through surjective ring morphisms is considered. We extend their results to arbitrary ring morphisms and it is enough to look at extensions.

We begin by giving two lemmas linking the radical of an ideal and its extension in a ring extension.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be an LO extension and
I
an ideal of
R
. Then
\sqrt[R]{I}=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R
.

Proof.

We know that
\sqrt[R]{I}=\cap_{P\in\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)}P
. Let
P\in\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)
. Since
R\subseteq S
has LO, there exists
Q\in\mathrm{Spec}(S)
such that
P=Q\cap R
. It follows that
IS\subseteq Q
, which implies
\sqrt[S]{IS}\subseteq Q
, giving
\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R\subseteq Q\cap R=P
. Then,
\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R\subseteq\sqrt[R]{I}
.

Now, let
Q\in\mathrm{V}_{S}(IS)
and set
P:=Q\cap R
. Therefore,
I\subseteq IS\cap R\subseteq Q\cap R=P
, so that
P\in\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)
and
\sqrt[R]{I}\subseteq P=Q\cap R
, that is
\sqrt[R]{I}
is contained in any
Q\in\mathrm{V}_{S}(IS)
, and then in
\sqrt[S]{IS}
. To conclude,
\sqrt[R]{I}=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be an extension and
I
an ideal of
R
such that
I=IS\cap R
. Then
\sqrt[R]{I}=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R=\sqrt[S]{\sqrt[R]{I}S}\cap R
.

Proof.

Thanks to
[
36
, Theorem 9, (16) and (22), p. 148]
, we have
\sqrt[R]{IS\cap R}=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R=\sqrt[R]{I}S\cap R
, giving the first equality. Since
\sqrt[R]{I}S=\sqrt[S]{IS}=\sqrt[S]{\sqrt[R]{I}S}
, this implies that
\sqrt[R]{I}=\sqrt[S]{\sqrt[R]{I}S}\cap R
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be an extension and
I
be an ideal of
R
such that
\sqrt[R]{I}=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R
. Then
\sqrt[R]{I}=\sqrt[S]{\sqrt[R]{I}S}\cap R
.

Proof.

Set
J:=\sqrt[R]{I}=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R
by assumption. Then, the proof of Lemma
3.2
gives that
J=\sqrt[R]{I}S\cap R=JS\cap R
. Applying Lemma
3.2
to
J
, we get that
J=\sqrt[R]{J}=\sqrt[S]{JS}\cap R=\sqrt[S]{\sqrt[R]{I}S}\cap R
.
∎

In the following, we will often meet extensions
R\subseteq S
such that
MS\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
for some
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
. This happens, in particular, for Nagata extensions
R\subseteq R(X)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be an extension and
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(R)
be such that
MS\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
for any
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)\cap\mathrm{V}(I)
. Then
IS\in\mathrm{QMax}(S)
and
\sqrt[R]{I}=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R
. Moreover,
I
and
IS
are homotypic
and
I=IS\cap R
in all cases unless
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
with
IS=\sqrt[S]{IS}
.

Proof.

If
I\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(R)
, then
I\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
, so that
IS\in\mathrm{Max}(S)=\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
. Moreover,
I=\sqrt[R]{I}=IS\cap R=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R
.

If
I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(R)
, then
I=M_{1}\cap M_{2}=M_{1}M_{2}
, with
M_{i}\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
for
i\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
. Setting
N_{i}:=M_{i}S\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
(by assumption), we get
IS=M_{1}M_{2}S=(M_{1}S)(M_{2}S)=N_{1}N_{2}=N_{1}\cap N_{2}
, so that
IS\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(S)
. Moreover,
I=M_{1}\cap M_{2}=M_{1}M_{2}=\sqrt[R]{I}=(N_{1}\cap N_{2})\cap R=(M_{1}S\cap M_{2}S)\cap R=IS\cap R=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R
.

If
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
, there exists
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
such that
M\succ I
with
M^{2}\subseteq I\subset M\ (*)
. Then
M=\sqrt[R]{I}=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R
. Setting
N:=MS\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
, we get
M^{2}S=N^{2}\subseteq IS\subseteq MS=N=\sqrt[S]{IS}
. Moreover, for any
x\in M\setminus I
, we have
M=I+Rx
giving
N=(I+Rx)S=IS+Sx
. If
IS\neq\sqrt[S]{IS}
, we get that
x\in N\setminus IS
. Otherwise,
Sx\subseteq IS
gives
IS=\sqrt[S]{IS}=N
, a contradiction. In particular,
x\in(N\cap R)\setminus(IS\cap R)
. Then,
N\succ IS
by
[
36
, Corollary 2, p.237]
, that is
IS\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
. It follows that
I\subseteq IS\cap R\subset N\cap R=M
, so that
I=IS\cap R
. If
IS=\sqrt[S]{IS}=N
, then
IS\in\mathrm{Max}(S)=\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
. In particular,
I\subset M=IS\cap R
. Moreover,
M=\sqrt[R]{I}=MS\cap R=\sqrt[S]{IS}\cap R
holds in both cases.

In any case,
IS\in\mathrm{QMax}(S)
and is homotypic to
I
unless
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
and
IS=\sqrt[S]{IS}
, in which case
IS\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be an extension and
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
be such that
\sqrt[R]{I}S\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
. Then
I=IS\cap R
if and only if
IS\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
.

Proof.

Since
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
, then
M:=\sqrt[R]{I}\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
and the assumption gives that
N:=MS\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
. According to the proof of Proposition
3.4
, if
IS\neq\sqrt[S]{IS}=N
, then
IS\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
with

and if
IS=\sqrt[S]{IS}=N
, then

. Therefore,
I=IS\cap R
if and only if
IS\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
.
∎

According to Proposition
2.8
, a non maximal quasi-maximal ideal is covered by a maximal ideal. Thanks to
[
36
, Corollary 1, p. 237]
, Proposition
3.7
shows how, under some assumption, the covering property is kept under extensions.

Before, we give the following improvement of
[

which will be useful in this paper.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be an extension and

(1)

M^{\prime}:=MS
is the unique ideal of

which lies above

.

(2)

M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
.

Proof.

(1) is in
[

, Lemma 2.4]
because
R_{M}=S_{M}
.

(2) Assume that
M^{\prime}\not\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
. Then there exists

such that
M^{\prime}\subset Q\subset S
, which implies

. Since
Q\cap R\neq R
and
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
, we get

giving

by (1), a contradiction, so that
M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be an extension.

(1)

Let
I
and
J
be ideals of

such that

. Assume that
\mathrm{V}(I)\cap\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)=\emptyset
. Then

and

(resp.

).

(2)

Let
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
be ideals of
S
such that
I^{\prime}\prec J^{\prime}
. Assume that
\mathrm{V}(I^{\prime}\cap R)\cap\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)=\emptyset
. Then

(1) We may remark that in Proposition

We use the results of Olivier about pure extensions
[
22

. Recall that a ring morphism

is a pure submodule of

A faithfully flat morphism is pure. If

of

is injective. Moreover, a pure ring morphism

verifies the condition (O),

an
R
-module

is pure when there is a retract of the extension

in the category of
R
-modules; that is, there is a morphism of

-submodule of

Proposition 3.22

Proposition 3.23
.

Let

4.
Quasi-maximal ideals and finite minimal extensions

4.1.
Quasi-maximal ideals through FCP extensions

Before looking at minimal extensions, we consider a finite extension. A
chain

\mathcal{C}
of subextensions of an extension
R\subseteq S
is a family of elements of
[R,S]
that are pairwise comparable. We say that the extension
R\subseteq S
has FCP (or is an FCP extension) if each chain in
[R,S]
is finite, or equivalently, its lattice
[R,S]
is Artinian and Noetherian. An FCP extension is finitely generated, and (module) finite if integral.

If
R\subseteq S
has FCP, any maximal (necessarily finite) chain of
[R,S]
, of the form
R=R_{0}\subset R_{1}\subset\cdots\subset R_{n-1}\subset R_{n}=S
, with
length

n<\infty
, results from juxtaposing
n
minimal extensions
R_{i}\subset R_{i+1},\ 0\leq i\leq n-1
.

Minimal extensions were introduced by Ferrand-Olivier
[
13
]
. An extension
R\subset S
is called
minimal
if
[R,S]=\{R,S\}
, and in this case,
\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(S/R)=\{M\}
for some
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
by
[
13
, Théorème 2.2]
. A minimal extension is either a flat epimorphism or a strict monomorphism, in which case it is (module) finite. We recall that a
finite
extension
R\subset S
is an extension such that
S
is a finitely generated
R
-module. Three types of minimal integral extensions exist, characterized in the next theorem, (a consequence of the fundamental lemma of Ferrand-Olivier), whence there are four types of minimal extensions, mutually exclusive. From now on, if
R\subset S
is a minimal finite extension, we say that
R\subset S
is in
\mathcal{M}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
10
, Theorems 2.1, 2.2 and 2.3]
. An extension
R\subset T
is in
\mathcal{M}
if and only if
M:=(R:T)\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
and one of the following three statements holds:

(a)
inert case
:
M\in\mathrm{Max}(T)
and
R/M\to T/M
is a minimal field extension.

(b)
decomposed case
: There exist
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathrm{Max}(T)
such that
M=M_{1}\cap M_{2}
and the natural maps
R/M\to T/M_{1}
and
R/M\to T/M_{2}
are both isomorphisms; or, equivalently, there exists
q\in T\setminus R
such that
T=R[q],\ q^{2}-q\in M
, and
Mq\subseteq M
.

(c)
ramified case
: There exists
M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Max}(T)
such that
{M^{\prime}}^{2}\subseteq M\subset M^{\prime},\ [T/M:R/M]=2
, and the natural map
R/M\to T/M^{\prime}
is an isomorphism, or, equivalently, there exists
q\in T\setminus R
such that
T=R[q],\ q^{2}\in M
and
Mq\subseteq M
.
In particular,
\mathrm{c}(T/M^{\prime})=\mathrm{c}(T/M)
.

In each of the above cases,
\mathrm{Supp}(T/R)=\{M\}
.

Proof.

We prove the last result of (c). Since
R/M\cong T/M^{\prime}
, it follows that
\mathrm{c}(T/M^{\prime})=\mathrm{c}(R/M)
. According to
[
12
, Proposition 4.7]
,
R/M\subset T/M
is in
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathrm{c}(T/M)=\mathrm{c}(R/M)
gives
\mathrm{c}(T/M^{\prime})=\mathrm{c}(T/M)
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

(1) The element
q\in T
gotten in (b) (resp. (c)) of Theorem
4.1
satisfies either

or
q\in M_{2}
(resp.

) with either

or

(resp.

the definition of a decomposed (resp. ramified) ideal in

. The analogy will be gotten in Theorem

.

(2) To shorten the writing, for an extension

which is in

, we say that

is in
\mathcal{M}_{i}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{d},\ \mathcal{M}_{r}
) if the extension is minimal inert, (resp. decomposed, ramified).

(3) Assume that
R\subset S
is in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
and set

. There exist

such that

. Then,
M\prec M_{1}
in

, with

. Setting

.

For any extension

, the

of
[R,S]
, denoted by

, is the supremum of the lengths of chains of

-subalgebras of

. It should be noted that if

has FCP, then there

Proposition 4.3
.

A finite extension

such that

Proof.

According to Theorem

J\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
, let
N:=\sqrt[S]{J}
. Then,
R/I
is a local ring whose maximal ideal is
M^{\prime}:=(N\cap R)/I
.

If, in addition,
M^{\prime}
is principal, then
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
.

Proof.

(1) Since
R\subseteq S
is integral,
N\cap R\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
for any
N\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
. So (1) is obvious.

(2) Since
J\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(S)
, then
J=N_{1}\cap N_{2}
with
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathrm{Max}(S),

N_{1}\neq N_{2}
. Set
M_{i}:=N_{i}\cap R\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
for
i\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
. Then,
I=N_{1}\cap N_{2}\cap R=M_{1}\cap M_{2}
.

(a) If
M_{1}=M_{2}
, then
I\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(R)\cup\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
. This holds, for example, if
R\subset S
is in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
and
J=(R:S)
(see Theorem
4.1
). In this case, we have
R/M_{1}\cong S/N_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
.

(b) If
M_{1}\neq M_{2}
, then
I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(R)
.

(3) Assume that
J\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
and let
N:=\sqrt[S]{J}
. Then,
S/J
is a local ring and has finitely many ideals by Theorem
2.4
. Therefore
S/J
is an Artinian, and also a Noetherian ring. So is
R/I
by the Eakin-Nagata Theorem because
R/I\subseteq S/J
is finite and
R/I
is a local ring whose maximal ideal is
M^{\prime}:=(N\cap R)/I
. Set
R^{\prime}:=R/I,\ S^{\prime}:=S/J
and
N^{\prime}:=N/J
. According to Proposition
2.5
,
S^{\prime}
is a SPIR such that
N^{\prime 2}=0
. Since
M^{\prime}=N^{\prime}\cap R^{\prime}
, we get that
M^{\prime 2}=0
.

If
M^{\prime}
is principal, that is
M^{\prime}=R^{\prime}x
, then
M^{\prime}
is the only ideal of
R^{\prime}
different from
0
. We deduce that
R^{\prime}
is a SPIR such that
M^{\prime 2}=0
. Hence,
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(R)
by Proposition
2.5
.
∎

We consider an FCP extension
R\subseteq S
and get conditions in order that
(R:S)\in\mathrm{QMax}(S)
. We recall some needed definitions.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be an extension. The
seminormalization

{}_{S}^{+}R
of
R
in
S
is the greatest subintegral extension of
R
in
S
and the
t
-closure

{}_{S}^{t}R
is the greatest infra-integral extension of
R
in
S
. An extension
R\subseteq S
is called
t-closed
(resp. seminormal) if
{}_{S}^{t}R=R
(resp.
{}_{S}^{+}R=R
)
(see
[
24
]
and
[
35
]
).

The case where the conductor of an extension
R\subset S
is quasi-maximal inert in
S
has a simple characterization.

Proposition 4.6
.

If
R\subset S
is an integral extension where
(R:S)\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(R)
, then
(R:S)\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)

if and only if
R\subset S
is t-closed.

Proof.

Set
M:=(R:S)\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
. If
M\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
, then
R/M\subset S/M
is an integral field extension. It follows that
R/M\subset S/M
is t-closed by
[
24
, Lemme 3.10]
, and so is
R\subset S
by
[
8
, Proposition 2.13]
.

Conversely, assume that
R\subset S
is t-closed, then so is
R/M\subset S/M
, giving that
S/M
is a field by the two previous references. So, we get that
M\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
.
∎

Most often in the rest of the paper, thanks to the previous Proposition, we will not consider the case where the conductor of an extension
R\subset S
is in
\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
.

The next Lemma will be useful.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
R\subset S
be an integral FCP i-extension where
(R,M)
is a local ring, whence
S
is also local. Let
N
be its maximal ideal. Set
T:={}_{S}^{t}R={}_{S}^{+}R
.

Then,
\mathrm{L}_{R}(M/(R:S))=\mathrm{L}_{S}(N/(R:S))[S/N:R/M]-\ell[R,T]
.

Proof.

Since
R\subset S
is an integral FCP i-extension where
(R,M)
is a local ring,
S
is also a local ring. It follows that
T:={}_{S}^{t}R={}_{S}^{+}R
by
[
31
, Proposition 5.2]
, and
M=N\cap R
. Set
I:=(R:S)
. Therefore,
N
and
I
are also ideals of
T
and
(T,N)
is a local ring. According to
[
21
, Theorem 13, p. 168]
, we have
\mathrm{L}_{T}(N/I)=\mathrm{L}_{S}(N/I)[S/N:T/N]
.

Since
R\subset S
has FCP, so has
R\subset T
. Let
\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
be a maximal chain of
[R,T]
, with
R_{0}:=R,\ R_{n}:=T
and
R_{i-1}\subset R_{i}
in
\mathcal{M}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
. By
[
10
, Lemma 5.4]
,
n=\ell[R,T]
. Each
R_{i-1}\subset R_{i}
is in
\mathcal{M}_{r}
by
[
29
, Proposition 4.5]
. Setting
N_{i}:=N\cap R_{i}
, we get that each
(R_{i},N_{i})
is a local ring having still
I
as an ideal. Moreover,
I\subseteq N_{i}
. We are going to prove by induction on
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
that
\mathrm{L}_{R_{i}}(N_{i}/I)=\mathrm{L}_{T}(N/I)-(n-i)\ (*)
. For
i=n
, we have
\mathrm{L}_{R_{n}}(N_{n}/I)=\mathrm{L}_{T}(N/I)
and
(*)
holds.

Assume that
(*)
holds for some
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, so that
\mathrm{L}_{R_{i}}(N_{i}/I)=\mathrm{L}_{T}(N/I)

-(n-i)
. Since
R_{i-1}\subset R_{i}
is in
\mathcal{M}_{r}
, with
N_{i-1}=(R_{i-1}:R_{i})
, we have
\mathrm{L}_{R_{i}}(N_{i}/N_{i-1})=1=\mathrm{L}_{R_{i}}(N_{i}/I)-\mathrm{L}_{R_{i}}(N_{i-1}/I)
. Using
[
10
, Lemma 5.4]
and
[
21
, Theorem 13, page 168]
, we infer that
\mathrm{L}_{R_{i}}(N_{i}/I)-1=\mathrm{L}_{R_{i}}(N_{i-1}/I)=\mathrm{L}_{R_{i-1}}(N_{i-1}/I)=\mathrm{L}_{T}(N/I)-(n-i)-1=\mathrm{L}_{T}(N/I)-(n-i+1)=\mathrm{L}_{T}(N/I)-(n-(i-1))
and
(*)
holds for
i-1
. Therefore,
(*)
holds for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, so that
\mathrm{L}_{R}(M/I)=\mathrm{L}_{T}(N/I)-n=\mathrm{L}_{T}(N/I)-\ell[R,T]=\mathrm{L}_{S}(N/I)[S/N:R/M]-\ell[R,T]
.
∎

We are now able to characterize an FCP integral extension
R\subset S
such that
(R:S)\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(S)\cup\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)

because of Proposition
4.6
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP integral extension and set
T:={}_{S}^{t}R
. Then,

(1)

(R:S)\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(S)
if and only if either
R=T
with
|\mathrm{V}_{R}((R:S))|=2
(in this case,
(R:S)\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(R)
) or
R\subset T
is in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
with
|\mathrm{V}_{R}((R:S))|=1
(in this case,
(R:S)\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(R)
).

(2)

(R:S)\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
if and only if
R\subset S
is an i-extension such that
|\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(S/R)|=1
, and, setting
\{M\}:=\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(S/R)
and letting
N:=\sqrt[S]{MS}
, we have:
[S/N:R/M]=\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/(R:S))
.

Proof.

We set
I:=(R:S)
, an ideal shared by any element of
[R,S]
.

(1) Assume that
I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(S)
, that is
I=N_{1}\cap N_{2},\ N_{1}\neq N_{2}
, with
N_{i}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
for
i\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
. Set
M_{i}:=N_{i}\cap R
, so that
M_{i}\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
for
i\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
. Then
I=M_{1}\cap M_{2}
. Two cases occur, either (a):
M_{1}\neq M_{2}
or (b):
M_{1}=M_{2}
.

In case (a),
I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(R)
by Proposition
4.4
. Moreover,
R=T
by
[
30
, Proposition 2.5]
because
|\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)|=2
.

Conversely, if
R=T
with
|\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)|=2
, then,
|\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)|=\mathrm{V}_{S}(I)|=2
. According again to
[
30
, Proposition 2.5]
,
R\subset S
is t-closed with
I
an intersection of two maximal ideals of
S
. Then,
I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(R)
.

In case (b),
I=M_{1}=M_{2}\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(R)
by Proposition
4.4
. Moreover,
R\subset T
is in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
with
|\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)|=1
and
|\mathrm{V}_{T}(I)|=|\mathrm{V}_{S}(I)|=2
by Theorem
4.1
.

Conversely, assume that
R\subset T
is in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
with
\mathrm{V}_{R}(I)|=1
. Then,
R\subset T
and
R\subset S
are seminormal, which implies that
I\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
. Let
J:=(R:T)=M^{\prime}_{1}\cap M^{\prime}_{2}
, where
M^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathrm{Max}(T)
for
i\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
and
J\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
. As
I\subseteq J
, we deduce that
I=J=M^{\prime}_{1}\cap M^{\prime}_{2}=N_{1}\cap N_{2}
, where
N_{i}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
is lying over
M^{\prime}_{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
(there is no harm to choose the same letters as in the first part because
N_{i}
plays the same role in the two parts). Then,

I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(S)
.

(2) Assume that
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
, and let
N:=\sqrt[S]{I}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
, giving
\mathrm{L}_{S}(N/I)=1
. It follows that
\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(S/R)=\{M\}
where
I\subseteq M:=N\cap R\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
by
[
33
, Proposition 5.7 and Example 5.15]
. This implies that
R\subset S
is an i-extension and
N=\sqrt[S]{MS}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
is the only maximal ideal of
S
lying above
M
. According to Lemma
4.7
because we may assume that
(R,M)
is a local ring by
[
21
, Theorem 12, p. 166]
,
\mathrm{L}_{R}(M/I)=\mathrm{L}_{S}(N/I)[S/N:R/M]-\ell[R,T]=[S/N:R/M]-\ell[R,T]=[S/N:R/M]-\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/M)\ (*)
by
[
10
, Lemma 5.4]
because
R\subseteq T
is subintegral and
N\in\mathrm{Max}(T)
since
I\subseteq(T:S)
. Then
(*)
implies that
\mathrm{L}_{R}(M/I)+\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/M)=\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/I)=[S/N:R/M]
.

Conversely, assume that
R\subset S
is an i-extension with
|\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(S/R)|\\
=1
, and, setting
\{M\}:=\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(S/R)
, with
I\subseteq M
by
[
33
, Proposition 5.7 and Example 5.15]
, and letting
N:=\sqrt[S]{MS}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)\cap\mathrm{Max}(T)
, the following equality holds:
[S/N:R/M]=\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/I)
. Thanks to Lemma
4.7
,
[
21
, Theorem 13, p. 168]
and because
\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(S/R)=\{M\}
, we deduce that
\mathrm{L}_{R}(M/I)=\mathrm{L}_{R_{M}}(MR_{M}/IR_{M})=\mathrm{L}_{S_{M}}(NR_{M}/IR_{M})

[S/N:R/M]-\ell[R_{M},T_{M}]=\mathrm{L}_{S}(N/I)[S/N:R/M]-\ell[R,T]=\mathrm{L}_{S}(N/I)\\
\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/I)-\ell[R,T]=\mathrm{L}_{S}(N/I)\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/I)-\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/M)
using again
[
10
, Lemma 5.4]
, which gives
\mathrm{L}_{S}(N/I)\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/I)=\mathrm{L}_{R}(M/I)+\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/M)=\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/I)
, whence
\mathrm{L}_{S}(N/I)=1
, because
N\neq I
. Then
N\succ I
and
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
.
∎

4.2.
Quasi-maximal ideals in minimal extensions

As we recall in Theorem
4.1
and Remark
4.2
, the conductor of a minimal integral extension
R\subset S
has the same characterization as a quasi-maximal ideal of
S
. In this section, we consider the behavior of quasi-maximal ideals of
S
linked to the conductor of a minimal integral extension
R\subset S
. In fact, the following Theorem shows that the conductor of a minimal integral extension
R\subset S
is in
\mathrm{QMax}(S)
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
R\subset S
be in
\mathcal{M}
. Then
(R:S)\in\mathrm{QMax}(S)
and is of the same type as
R\subset S
.

Proof.

Set
M:=(R:S)\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
.

By Theorem
4.1
, in the inert case, we have
M\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
and
M\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
.

In the decomposed case, there exist
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
such that
M=M_{1}\cap M_{2}
and
M\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(S)
.

In the ramified case, there exists
M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
such that
M^{\prime 2}\subseteq M\subset M^{\prime}
and there exists
q\in S\setminus R
such that
M^{\prime}=M+Rq
according to Theorem
4.1
. In particular,
M
is
M^{\prime}
-primary in
S
. Then,
[
36
, Corollary 2, p.237]
shows that
M^{\prime}\succ M
in
S
, whence
M\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
by Theorem
2.9
and Definition
2.3
.
∎

The next Proposition gives a converse of Theorem
4.9
under a special assumption.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP infra-integral extension such that
(R:S)\in\mathrm{QMax}(S)
. Then,
R\subset S
is a minimal extension of the same type as
(R:S)
(in
S
), so that
(R:S)\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
.

Proof.

Set
I:=(R:S)
. We consider the two cases of Theorem
4.8
because
I\not\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
since
S={}_{S}^{t}R
by Proposition
4.6
.

If
I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(S)
, then
R\subset S
is in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
and
I\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
because
R\neq{}_{S}^{t}R
.

If
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
, then
R\subset S
is an i-extension, giving that
R\subset S
is subintegral, because infra-integral and
|\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(S/R)|=1
. Set
\{M\}:=\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(S/R)
with
I\subseteq M
and let
N:=\sqrt[S]{MS}
. The following equality holds:
[S/N:R/M]=\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/I)
. But
S/N\cong R/M
because
R\subset S
is infra-integral, so that
\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/I)=\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/M)+\mathrm{L}_{R}(M/I)=1
. As
N\neq M
since
S\neq R
, it follows that
\mathrm{L}_{R}(N/M)\neq 0
and
\mathrm{L}_{R}(M/I)=0
. Therefore,
I=M
and Theorem
4.1
gives that
R\subset S
is in
\mathcal{M}_{r}
. In fact,
[S/M:R/M]=2
because
N=M+Sx
for some
x\in N\setminus M
and the isomorphism
R/M\cong S/N
shows that
S=R+N=R+Rx+Nx=R+Rx
, giving
[S/M:R/M]=2
.
∎

One can ask whether Theorem
4.9
has a converse. In fact, the next
Theorem shows that, given a ring
S
and
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(S)
, we have to add additional assumptions in order that there exists a subring
R
of
S
such that
R\subset S
is an extension in
\mathcal{M}
with
(R:S)=I
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
S
be a ring and
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(S)
. Then there exists a subring
R
of
S
such that
I=(R:S)
with
R\subset S
in
\mathcal{M}
if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

I\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
and
S/I
is a field which has a maximal subring which is a field.

(2)

I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(S)
is such that
I=M_{1}\cap M_{2}
where
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathrm{Max}(S),\ M_{1}\neq M_{2}
and
S/M_{1}\cong S/M_{2}
.

(3)

I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
is such that
M^{2}\subseteq I\subset M
, where
M\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
and
\mathrm{c}(S/M)=\mathrm{c}(S/I)
.

In each of these situations,
R\subset S
is a minimal extension of the same type as the quasi-maximal ideal
I
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a ring and
I\in\mathrm{QMax}(S)
. Assume that there exists a subring
R
of
S
such that
I=(R:S)
with
R\subset S
in
\mathcal{M}
. Then Theorems
4.9
and
4.1
give the first part of the equivalence.

Now, assume that one of conditions (1), (2) or (3) holds. According to
[
12
, Proposition 4.7]
, we get that there exists a subring
R
of
S
such that
R\subset S
is in
\mathcal{M}
with
I=(R:S)
if and only if
R/I\subset S/I
is in
\mathcal{M}
, and if this statement holds, the two extensions have the same type. In this case, since
I=(R:S)
with
R\subset S
an extension in
\mathcal{M}
, it follows that
I\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
, so that
R/I
is a field. Therefore, if there exists a subring
K
of
S
which is a field such that
K\subset S/I
is an extension in
\mathcal{M}
, the wanted ring
R
is obtained by the pullback

\begin{matrix}R&\subset&S\\
\downarrow&{}&p\downarrow\\
K&\subset&S/I\end{matrix}

where
p:S\to S/I
is the natural surjective map. Then,
R=p^{-1}(K)
. For such a ring
R
, we get that
I
is an ideal shared by
R
and
S
, with
K=R/I
, so that
I=(R:S)\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
and
R\subset S
is an
extension in
\mathcal{M}
of the same type as
K\subset S/I
by the previous reference. So, our problem reduces to find conditions in order that there exists a maximal subring
K
of
S/I
which is a field and such that
K\subset S/I
is an extension in
\mathcal{M}
of the same type as the quasi-maximal ideal
I
in
S
. We make a discussion considering the different types of the quasi-maximal ideal
I
of
S
.

Assume that
I\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
so that
S/I
is a field and assume that
S/I
has a maximal subring
K
which is a field. Then
K\subset S/I
is in
\mathcal{M}_{i}
, and so is
R:=p^{-1}(K)\subset S
. In Remark
4.12
, we give a more precise characterization of this situation.

Assume that
I\in\mathrm{Q_{d}Max}(S)
, that is
I=M_{1}\cap M_{2}
where
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathrm{Max}(S),\ M_{1}\neq M_{2}
. Then,
S/I\cong S/M_{1}\times S/M_{2}
. Assume moreover that
S/M_{1}\cong S/M_{2}
. Setting
K:=S/M_{1}\cong S/M_{2}
, we get that
K\subset S/I\cong K^{2}
is in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
, and so is
R:=p^{-1}(K)\subset S
.

Assume that
I\in\mathrm{Q_{r}Max}(S)
, that is
M^{2}\subseteq I\subset M
for some
M\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
and assume that
\mathrm{c}(S/M)=\mathrm{c}(S/I)
. Since
I
is ramified in
S
, it follows that
S^{\prime}:=S/I
is a SPIR by Proposition
2.5
whose maximal ideal is
M^{\prime}:=M/I
nilpotent of index 2. In particular,
S^{\prime}
is a complete local ring with
M^{\prime}
is principal by
[
18
, Definition 9]
. Set
M^{\prime}=S^{\prime}t
. According to
[
18
, Theorem 8]
,
S^{\prime}=f(K[[X]])
where
f:K[[X]]\to S^{\prime}
is a morphism such that
f(X)=t
, with
K=S^{\prime}/M^{\prime}\cong S/M
because
\mathrm{c}(K)=\mathrm{c}(S/M)=\mathrm{c}(S/I)
. Then,
S^{\prime}=K[t]=K+Kt
, because
t^{2}=0
. Therefore
K\subset S^{\prime}=S/I
is in
\mathcal{M}_{r}
by Theorem
4.1
, and so is
R:=p^{-1}(K)\subset S
.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

The condition occurring in case
I\in\mathrm{Q_{i}Max}(S)
is drastic. In fact, for a field
K
, Azarang and Karamzadeh get that
K
has no maximal subring if and only if there is a prime number
p
such that either
K=\mathbb{F}_{p}:=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
or
K
is an infinite subfield of the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
K=\cup_{n\in T}\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
, where
T\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is such that
\cup_{n\in T}\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is a subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is the unique subfield of

with

elements (

has no maximal FG-subset
[

. Then, taking the contraposition, we get a characterization of a field having a maximal subring. But we have now to exclude the solutions which are not fields. Now, use
[

which says that for a field

Let
R\subseteq S
be an extension in
\mathcal{M}
and set

. By Theorem

,
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)=\{M\}
. According to Corollary

and is homotypic to

for any

Proposition 4.13
.

Let

be in
\mathcal{M}
,

and

be such that

.

Proof.

We begin to notice that

and
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)=\{M\}
.

If

, then

. But,

implies

with

since

. Hence,

.

Assume now that

. Then,

for some

. According to Proposition

,

and

. In particular,

. By Corollary

,

is homotypic to

.
∎

Now, according to Proposition
4.13
and considering an extension

in
\mathcal{M}

Lemma 4.14
.

Let

be an extension such that
(R:S)\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
and let

be a submaximal ideal of

. Then
I=IS
if and only if

and there exists an ideal

of

such that

.

Proof.

Set
M:=(R:S)
.

Assume that
I\subset M
and
J
is an ideal of
S
such that
I=J\cap R
. This implies
I\subseteq J
, so that
IS\subseteq JS=J\ (*)
. We infer that
I\subseteq IS\subseteq MS=M
. Then
IS\in\{I,M\}
because

is also an ideal of

. We claim that

. Otherwise,
IS=M
and
(*)

, so that

, a contradiction and

holds.

Conversely, if

, then

because

is also an ideal of

and

. Moreover,

is also an ideal of
S
such that

.
∎

Proposition 4.17

Let

Then either

as a submaximal ideal of

.

If

for

for

. Therefore

.

If

then

for some

and

is an
N
-primary ideal of

. Set

. Then

, with

and

ideals of

and

and

ideals of

such that

is an

. Moreover,

(resp.

(resp.

) containing

and

.
∎

Corollary 4.18
.

Let

.

Remark 4.19
.

. By Corollary

,

and

for any

and

is homotypic to

. Now, we are going to consider a minimal extension

and an ideal

generalizes Proposition

4.4.

in

, and

and

are the only distinct prime ideals of

(A prime ideal minimal over

Remark 5.1
.

If we limit for the characterization of 2-absorbing ideals which are maximal ideals, Proposition
2.2
gives for (a): Either

(1) or

is an

-primary ideal, for some

such that

(3). And (b) gives

for two distinct maximal ideals

of

gives

because

and

are comaximal, which is Proposition

(2).

We get the following converse of
[

, Theorem 1]

Theorem 5.2
.

An ideal

of a ring

if and only if

Proof.

One implication is

Conversely, assume that

Corollary 5.4

Let

Proposition 5.8

For a proper ideal

Proposition 6.1
.

Let

be a ring and

(2)

, then
\Lambda(I)=R\setminus(M_{1}\cup M_{2})=(R\setminus M_{1})\cap(R\setminus M_{2})

\Lambda(I)
is the set of elements of
R
whose image in
R/I
is regular.

(1) If

, then

and

is a field, so that

.

If

, let

. Proposition
2.5
says that

is a SPIR whose maximal ideal

.

(2) Let

(1) If

(2) Assume that

Proposition 6.3
.

Let

be a ring morphism and let

be an ideal of

. Then

, with equality if

Proof.

According to
[

holds.

We claim that

if

is surjective. It is enough to show that
\Lambda[f^{-1}(J)]\subseteq f^{-1}[\Lambda(J)]
. So, set

and let

, that is
I:x=I
. Setting

, we already have
J\subseteq J:y
. Let

, so that

. Since

is surjective, there exists some

such that

. By
(*)
, we have

. As

, we infer that

. Hence,

, which means that

and

.

Let

be a ring morphism and let

Let

We recall that a proper ideal

An ideal either inert or ramified is primal.

Proof.

Let

Remark 6.6
.

(1) We define a proper ideal

of a ring

is a finite union of prime ideals. Then, a decomposed ideal is semi-primal by Proposition

(2) An ideal

(3) A primal ideal

, with

shows that

.

.

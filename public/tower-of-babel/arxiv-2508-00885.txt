Topological Regularization
Sebastián Alí Sacasa Céspedes

Abstract
This work introduces topological regularization as a framework for handling ultraviolet divergences in quantum field theory, reinterpreting infinities as topological obstructions at spacetime boundaries. Through geometric compactification via stereographic projection, singularities are reframed as boundary artifacts. The framework employs causal embeddings and the causality group to preserve Lorentz invariance and unitarity, while homotopy-equivalent defect structures guarantee regularization independence via Stokes–Poincaré duality. The Physical Equivalence Theorem shows that homotopy-equivalent schemes yield identical renormalized observables. Renormalization group flows are governed by Euler characteristics, and anomalies are resolved through cobordism and Chern character integrals. This approach unifies UV/IR duality, anomaly cancellation, and Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction. Applications extend to AdS/CFT, PDEs, quantum simulators, and noncommutative geometry. Topological regularization replaces artificial cutoffs with intrinsic geometric mechanisms, positioning spacetime as a defect-entangled structure governed by topological invariants, with testable predictions in quantum materials. Keywords: Topological Regularization, Homotopy Equivalence, Cobordism Classes, Ultraviolet Divergences, Renormalization Group Flows, Euler Characteristic, Quantum Gravity.

Physical Equivalence Theorem

Quantum field theories often exhibit universal infrared (IR) behavior that is insensitive to the ultraviolet (UV) details of their regularization schemes. When the underlying regularization geometries are homotopy equivalent, their physical predictions become indistinguishable. This stems from a foundational principle: that physical observables must remain invariant under continuous deformations of the geometric structures used for regularization, as a natural consequence of topological regularity.

Discussion

Topological regularization introduces a paradigm shift in our understanding of renormalization in quantum field theory (QFT), forging a deep synthesis between differential topology and high-energy physics. Rather than treating ultraviolet (UV) divergences as fundamental infinities to be subtracted, they are interpretated as signatures of topological obstructions in the geometry of field configurations. In this framework, two regularization sets that are homotopy equivalent yield identical physical predictions—a statement made rigorous by Physical Equivalence Theorem. This resolves several long-standing paradoxes in QFT by weaving together UV/IR duality, anomaly cancellation, and experimental observables into a coherent topological overview.

5
Conclusion

Topological regularization reconceptualizes the renormalization procedure in quantum field theory (QFT), shifting the focus from perturbative subtraction schemes to a deeper problem of geometric and topological classification. In this framework, ultraviolet (UV) divergences are not eliminated through arbitrary cutoffs or counterterms, but instead are systematically encoded into homotopy classes of defect-regularized manifolds. This geometric encoding has been shown to preserve the invariance of physical observables, including correlation functions,
\beta
-functions, and anomaly coefficients, under smooth topological deformations of the underlying space. Consequently, the method unifies UV consistency and infrared (IR) universality, while simultaneously resolving global anomalies through cobordism theory. Furthermore, it provides a conceptually unified foundation for analytic continuation, modular flow, and causal structure, with deep topological implications that extend across multiple domains of theoretical physics.

This work has fully achieved its central objective: to construct and formalize a Topological Regularization framework grounded in robust homotopical and geometric principles. By synthesizing insights from flat connection moduli spaces, reflection positivity constraints, and topological refinements of the renormalization group, this framework leads to a coherent articulation of the Physical Equivalence Theorem which could need better proofs and rigurosity. The reformulation presented here does not merely propose a new perspective on UV behavior, it actively replaces the need for artificial or extrinsic regularization tools with intrinsic geometric mechanisms, deeply rooted in the topology of field configurations and manifold structures. In doing so, it opens novel and promising avenues toward the resolution of longstanding challenges, including anomaly cancellation, modular Hamiltonian flows, UV, complete formulations of effective field theories, and a reinterpretation of singularities and infinities as coordinate artifacts classified within topological equivalence classes.

Importantly, the results presented in this work reinforce and substantiate a broader philosophical and mathematical vision in which topology and geometry are not peripheral aids to quantum field theory, but rather serve as its most fundamental and generative structures. This geometric-topological primacy offers not only conceptual elegance, but also new technical power to address unresolved questions in the foundations of high-energy physics and condensed matter systems alike. Nevertheless, open questions remain regarding the relation between the mathematical equivalences established through topological mappings and the experimental data that define physically observable states and their dynamical evolution. It remains a challenge to fully clarify whether these topological identifications correspond to true physical realities or if they merely constitute elegant mathematical reformulations that, while internally consistent, require further empirical validation.

The Physical Equivalence Theorem confirms that homotopy, equivalent regularizations yield physically equivalent theories, reinforcing the view that spacetime structure and quantum consistency are governed by topological invariants rather than metrical details alone. As experiments and computational models in quantum materials and cosmology begin to proof the predictions of this framework, topological regularization offers a robust and conceptually elegant foundation for future work in quantum gravity, holography, partial differential equations, material science, quantum computing, information theory, and non-perturbative field theory.

Hence, perhaps the universe does not diverge, it simply dynamically folds within an equivalent topological structure.

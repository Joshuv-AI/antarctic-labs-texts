To maintain the flow of our discussion and the clarity of the proofs for our key findings, we have compiled all relevant notations, definitions, and preliminary concepts in Section 2.

As S. Banach proved that good differential properties of a function do not guarantee the a.e. boundedness of the Fourier partial sum of this function with respect to general orthonormal systems (ONS). In this paper we have studied the Fourier partial sums with respect to general ONS when function

For our investigation, it is interesting that according to Menchov’s and Banach’s results, the convergence of general orthonormal series and the convergence of general Fourier series for functions from some differential classes are different problems. By

, for which

\|f(x)-f(y)\|=O(h),\text{ when }\max|x-y|\leq h.

Let

Suppose that

be an arbitrary function and

C_{n}(f)=\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\varphi_{n}(x)\,dx,\quad n=1,2,\dots\,,

(\varphi_{n})

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{n}(f)\varphi_{n}(x)

(1)

The general partial sum
S_{n}(x,f)

\displaystyle S_{n}(x,f)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{k}(f)\varphi_{k}(x).

(2)

Next, we can define

\left(x\in[0,1]\right)

M_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{i}{n}}Q_{n}(u,x)du\right|,

(3)

where

Q_{n}(u,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{k}(u)\varphi_{k}(x)

(4)

\displaystyle g_{k}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}\varphi_{k}(t)dt.

(5)

.

don’t guarantee that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|S_{n}(t,f)\right|<\infty,

for any function
f\in C_{L}.

Indeed

Theorem 3
.

There exists a function
g\in C_{L}
and ONS
(G_{n})
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}G_{n}(u)du=0,\ \ \ \ \int_{0}^{1}u\ G_{n}(u)du=0,\ \ \ n=1,2,\dots,

and

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|S_{n}(x,g,G)\right|=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{n}(g,G)G_{n}(x)\right|=+\infty,

where

\displaystyle C_{n}(g,G)=\int_{0}^{1}g(u)G_{n}(u)du=0,\ \ \ (n=1,2,\dots)

and

\displaystyle S_{n}(x,g,G)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{n}(g,G)G_{n}(x).

Proof.

Let us assume that
f(x)=1-\cos 4(u-1/2)\pi.
According to the Banach Theorem there exist an ONS
(\varphi_{n}),
such that a.e. on
[0,1]

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left|S_{n}(x,f,\varphi)\right|=+\infty.

(31)

The system
\varPhi_{n}(u)
we define as follows

\varPhi_{n}\left(u\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\varphi_{n}(2u),&\text{when}&u\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right),\\
\\
-\varphi_{n}\left(2\left(u-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right),&\text{when}&u\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].\end{array}\right.

It is easy to prove that
\left(\varPhi_{n}\right)
is an ONS on
[0,1]
and
\int_{0}^{1}\varPhi_{n}(u)du=0,\ n=0,1,\dots.

Now we investigate the function

g\left(u\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}f(2u),&\text{when}&u\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right),\\
\\
0,&\text{when}&u\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].\end{array}\right.

We have

\displaystyle C_{n}(g,\varPhi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g(u)\varPhi_{n}(u)du=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}f\left(2u\right)\varphi_{n}\left(2u\right)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}f\left(u\right)\varphi_{n}\left(u\right)du=\frac{1}{2}C_{n}\left(f,\varphi\right).

Consequently (see (
31
))

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left|S_{n}(x,f,\varPhi)\right|=\frac{1}{2}\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left|S_{n}(x,f,\varphi)\right|=+\infty.

(32)

Now we define the next ONS
G_{n}(u)
as follows

G_{n}\left(u\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\varPhi_{n}(2u),&\text{when}&u\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right),\\
\\
-\varPhi_{n}\left(2\left(u-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right),&\text{when}&u\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].\end{array}\right.

(33)

After we define

h\left(u\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}g(2u),&\text{when}&u\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right),\\
\\
0,&\text{when}&u\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].\end{array}\right.

We have

\displaystyle C_{n}(h,G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}h(u)G_{n}(u)du=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}g\left(2u\right)\varPhi_{n}\left(2u\right)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}g\left(u\right)\varPhi_{n}\left(u\right)du=\frac{1}{2}C_{n}\left(g,\varPhi\right).

Such we have that
h^{\prime}\in Lip1
and (see (
31
))

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left|S_{n}(x,h,G)\right|=\frac{1}{2}\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left|S_{n}(x,g,\varPhi)\right|=+\infty.

(34)

Let
p(u)=u,
we get

\displaystyle C_{n}(p,G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}u\ G_{n}(u)du=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\ \varPhi_{n}\left(2u\right)du-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}u\ \varPhi_{n}\left(2\left(u-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}u\ \varPhi_{n}\left(u\right)du-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left({u+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\varPhi_{n}\left(u\right)du=-\frac{1}{4}C_{n}\left(q,\varPhi\right)=0.

Finally we receive

1) If
q(u)=1
and
p(u)=u
when
u\in[0,1],
then

\displaystyle C_{n}\left(p,G\right)=C_{n}\left(q,G\right)=0\text{ or }\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left|S_{n}(x,p,G)\right|=\frac{1}{2}\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left|S_{n}(x,q,G)\right|<+\infty.

And (see (
34
))

2)
\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left|S_{n}(x,h,G)\right|=+\infty\text{ where }h^{\prime}\in Lip1.

Theorem
3
is completely proved.

4.
Problems of efficiency

Theorem 4
.

Let
\varphi_{n}(u)=\sqrt{2}\cos 2\pi nu,
then for any
x\in[0,1]

\displaystyle M_{n}(x)=O(1).

Proof.

Indeed

\displaystyle M_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{i}{n}}Q_{n}(u,x)du\right|=\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{i}{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{u}\cos 2\pi kvdvdu\cos 2\pi kx\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(1)\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{i}{n}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}\sin 2\pi kudu\cos 2\pi kx\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(1)\left(\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=O(1).

∎

Theorem 5
.

Let
(X_{n})
be Haar system (see
[
1
]
,Ch.2) then for any
x\in[0,1]

\displaystyle M_{n}(x)=O(1).

Proof.

If
2^{s}<m\leq 2^{s+1}
then

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{u}X_{m}(v)dv\right|\leq 2^{-\frac{s}{2}}

and
(i=1,2,\dots,n-1)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{i}{n}}\int_{0}^{u}\sum_{m=2^{s}+1}^{2^{s+1}}X_{m}(v)dvX_{m}(x)du\right|\leq 2\ 2^{-s}.

Finally

\displaystyle M_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{i}{n}}Q_{n}(u,x)du\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(1)\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{i}{n}}\sum_{s=0}^{d}\int_{0}^{u}\sum_{m=2^{s}+1}^{2^{s+1}}X_{m}(v)dvX_{m}(x)du\right|=O(1).

From here it is evident that Theorem
5
is valid.

∎

5.
Conclusion

Based on the discussion presented in this article, it is evident that although Fourier partial sums related to general orthonormal systems do not converge for all functions
f
classified under a differentiable class of
C_{L}
functions, we can identify a specific subset of orthonormal systems. These subsets include functions that meet particular criteria, ensuring that the Fourier partial sums for
C_{L}
class functions exhibit convergence (refer to Theorem
1
). Moreover, we have established that the criteria applied to the functions of the orthonormal systems are both precise and reliable.

Additionally, it is important to note that each orthonormal system encompasses a subsystem for which the general Fourier series of any function
f
within the
C_{L}
class converges almost everywhere on the interval
[0,1].

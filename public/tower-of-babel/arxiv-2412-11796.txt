Discrete Poincaré inequalities: a review on proofs, equivalent formulations, and behavior of constants
Alexandre Ern, Johnny Guzmán, Pratyush Potu, Martin Vohralík

Abstract
. We investigate discrete Poincaré inequalities on piecewise polynomial subspaces of the Sobolev spaces 𝑯 ( curl , ω ) \bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega) and 𝑯 ( div , ω ) \bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega) in three space dimensions. We characterize the dependence of the constants on the continuous-level constants, the shape regularity and cardinality of the underlying tetrahedral mesh, and the polynomial degree. One important focus is on meshes being local patches (stars) of tetrahedra from a larger tetrahedral mesh. We also review various equivalent results to the discrete Poincaré inequalities, namely stability of discrete constrained minimization problems, discrete inf-sup conditions, bounds on operator norms of piecewise polynomial vector potential operators (Poincaré maps), and existence of graph-stable commuting projections.

1.
Introduction

Let
\omega
be a three-dimensional, open, bounded, connected, Lipschitz polyhedral domain with diameter
h_{\omega}
. The
L^{2}
-inner product in
\omega
is denoted as
\bigl\langle\cdot,\cdot\bigr\rangle_{\omega}
and the corresponding norm as
\|{\cdot}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}
or
\|{\cdot}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}
(notation is set in details in Section
2
below). Let
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
be a tetrahedral mesh of
\omega
. Our main motivation is the case where
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
is some local collection (patch, star) of tetrahedra from a mesh of some larger fixed three-dimensional domain, say
\Omega
.
A large part of our developments applies in any space dimension in the language of differential forms, but we choose the three-dimensional setting to hopefully address a broad readership.

1.1.
Poincaré and discrete Poincaré inequalities on
H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)=H^{1}(\omega)

The Poincaré inequality

(1.1)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{0}h_{\omega}\|{\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}}\,u\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}\qquad\forall u\in H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)\text{ such that }\bigl\langle u,1\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0

is well known and omnipresent in the analysis of partial differential equations. Crucially,
C_{\textup{P}}^{0}
is a generic constant that only depends on the shape of
\omega
. For convex
\omega
, in particular,
C_{\textup{P}}^{0}\leq 1/\pi
following Payne and Weinberger
[
46
]
and Bebendorf
[
6
]
.
The discrete version of (
1.1
) for
H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)
-conforming piecewise polynomials of degree
(p+1)
,
p\geq 0
, on the tetrahedral mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
of
\omega
writes

(1.2)

\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},0}h_{\omega}\|{\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}}\,u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}\qquad\forall u_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)\text{ such that }\bigl\langle u_{\mathcal{T}},1\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0.

As the functions considered in (
1.2
) form a subspace of the functions considered in (
1.1
), (
1.2
) trivially holds with
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},0}\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{0}
. Moreover, similar results hold with (homogeneous) boundary condition on the boundary
\partial\omega
.

1.2.
Poincaré and discrete Poincaré inequalities on
\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
and
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)

The Poincaré inequalities

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{1}h_{\omega}\|{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{u}\in\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)\text{ such that }\bigl\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0

(1.3a)

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)\text{ with }{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{v}=\bm{0},

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{2}h_{\omega}\|{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{u}\in\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)\text{ such that }\bigl\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0

(1.3b)

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)\text{ with }{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}=0

arise when the differential operators employ the curl or the divergence in place of the gradient. They are also well known, though a bit more complicated to establish.
When
\omega
is simply connected, the orthogonality in (
1.3a
)
means that
\bm{u}
is orthogonal to gradients of
H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)
functions and thus belongs to
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
, is divergence-free, and has zero normal component on the boundary
\partial\omega
. Thus, (
1.3a
) is the so-called Poincaré–Friedrichs–Weber
inequality, see Fernandes and Gilardi
[
36
, Proposition 7.4]

or Chaumont-Frelet et al.
[
11
, Theorem A.1]
. Similarly, when

\partial\omega
is connected, the orthogonality in (
1.3b
)
means that
\bm{u}
is orthogonal to curls of
\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
functions and thus belongs to
\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
,
is curl-free, and has zero tangential component on the boundary

\partial\omega
.

In this paper, we are interested in the following
discrete Poincaré inequalities
for
\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
- and
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
-conforming piecewise polynomials in the Nédélec and Raviart–Thomas finite element spaces of order
p
,
p\geq 0
, on the tetrahedral mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
of
\omega
:

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},1}h_{\omega}\|{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)\text{ such that }\bigl\langle\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}},\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0

(1.4a)

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)\text{ with }{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=\bm{0},

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},2}h_{\omega}\|{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)\text{ such that }\bigl\langle\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}},\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0

(1.4b)

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)\text{ with }{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=0.

We will also consider the counterparts with homogeneous boundary condition on the boundary
\partial\omega
.
Here, unfortunately, the inequalities (
1.4
) do not follow from (
1.3
) and
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},1}
,
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},2}
cannot be trivially bounded by
C_{\textup{P}}^{1}
,
C_{\textup{P}}^{2}
. Indeed, the kernel of the
{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}
operator restricted to
\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
is different from the kernel on
\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
, and similarly, the kernel of the
{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}
operator restricted to
\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
is different from the kernel on
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
(the former being nontrivial and the latter being infinite-dimensional in both cases). In contrast, the kernel of the gradient operator is trivial (composed of constant functions) and is the same on
\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)
and
H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)
, which leads to the trivial passage from (
1.1
) to (
1.2
).

Remark 1.1

(Orthogonality constraint)
.

When
\omega
is simply connected, a vector-valued piecewise polynomial
\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
with
{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=\bm{0}
is the gradient of a scalar-valued piecewise polynomial
v_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)
; the orthogonality constraint in (
1.4a
) is often stated in the literature using gradients. We prefer the writing (
1.4
), since the orthogonality constraints in (
1.4
) are valid for a general domain topology.

1.3.
Focus of the paper

In the literature, one often finds assertions that the discrete Poincaré inequalities (
1.4
) are “known”.
The purpose of this paper is to recall several equivalent reformulations of (
1.4
), discuss the available references, formulate some possible proofs of (
1.4
) in an abstract way with generic assumptions, and to establish some new results on (
1.4
). Our main focus is on the
characterization
of the
behavior
of the constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},1}
,
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},2}
with respect to the
constants

C_{\textup{P}}^{1}
,
C_{\textup{P}}^{2}
, the
shape-regularity parameter
of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, the
number of elements
in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, and the
polynomial degree

p
.
The motivation for writing explicitly the scaling with
h_{\omega}
in (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) is twofold: (i) it is important when
\omega
corresponds to a local collection of tetrahedra from a larger mesh; (ii) it makes the constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{l}
and
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}
,
l\in\{0,1,2\}
, dimensionless.

1.4.
Available results

As discussed in Sections
3
and
6.3
below in more detail, the discrete Poincaré inequalities (
1.4
) are
equivalent
to: (i) stability of discrete constrained minimization problems; (ii) discrete inf-sup conditions; (iii) bounds on operator norms of piecewise polynomial vector potential operators (that is, piecewise polynomial right-inverses for the curl and divergence operators, also called Poincaré maps); and (iv) existence of graph-stable commuting projections. There are also links to lower bounds on eigenvalues of vector Laplacians. Numerous results are available in the literature in one of these settings.

The discrete Poincaré inequality (
1.4a
), with a generic constant
C
in place of
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},1}h_{\omega}
, is presented in Girault and Raviart
[
39
, Chapter 3, Proposition 5.1]
and Monk and Demkowicz
[
43
, Corollary 4.2]
in three space dimensions and in Arnold
et al.

[
4
, Theorem 5.11]
and Arnold
et al.

[
5
, Theorem 3.6]
more abstractly in the finite element exterior calculus setting, covering both bounds in (
1.4
). The discrete Poincaré inequality in the precise form (
1.4a
) is established in Ern and Guermond
[
28
, Theorem 44.6]
, with
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},1}
at worst depending on the continuous-level constant
C_{\textup{P}}^{1}
from (
1.3a
), the shape-regularity parameter of
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, and the polynomial degree
p
, see also
[
28
, Remark 44.7]
for further bibliographical resources.

Discrete inf-sup conditions are extensively discussed in the mixed finite element literature.
For instance, (
1.4b
) as a discrete inf-sup condition is established, with a generic constant
C
in place of
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},2}h_{\omega}
, in Raviart and Thomas
[
47
, Theorem 4]
, see also Fortin
[
37
]
, Boffi
et al.

[
8
, Theorem 4.2.3 and Propositions 5.4.3 and 7.1.1]
, or Gatica
[
38
, Lemmas 2.6 and 4.4.]
. The form leading precisely to (
1.4b
) can be found in
[
28
, Remark 51.12]
, with
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},2}
at worst depending on the continuous-level constant
C_{\textup{P}}^{2}
from (
1.3b
), the shape-regularity parameter of
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, and the polynomial degree
p
.

Considering the operator norm of a piecewise polynomial vector potential operator, Demkowicz and Babuška
[
19
, Theorem 1]
, Gopalakrishnan and Demkowicz
[
40
, Theorems 4.1, 5.1, and 6.1]
, and Demkowicz and Buffa
[

, Lemmas 6 and 8]
establish (

) with a generic constant

independent of the polynomial degree

(

-robustness
) on a single triangle or tetrahedron. Similar results hold on a cube and more generally on starlike domains with respect to a ball, see Costabel

[

]
and Costabel and McIntosh
[

]

[

for the

Piecewise polynomials on patches of tetrahedra sharing a given subsimplex (vertex, edge, or face) seem to have been addressed only more recently. Corresponding proofs employ the above-discussed results for polynomials on one element together with polynomial extension operators from the boundary of a tetrahedron (Demkowicz
et al.

[
21
,
22
]
for, respectively, the tangential or normal trace lifting in the

[

[

(respectively edge and vertex stars in 3D). As we shall see, these results imply (

,

being

[

.

1.5.
Main results and organization of the paper

We introduce the setting in Section
2
together with a unified notation to formulate the Poincaré inequalities without the need to distinguish between grad, curl, and div operators.
In Section
3
we recall that discrete Poincaré inequalities are equivalent with stability of discrete constrained minimization problems, discrete inf-sup conditions, and bounds on operator norms of piecewise polynomial vector potential operators. Section
4
then wraps up known results on the continuous Poincaré inequalities (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) and their variants with boundary conditions on
\partial\omega
. Turning next to the discrete Poincaré inequalities in Section
5
,
our main result is Theorem
5.1
, establishing (
1.4
) and its variants with boundary conditions on
\partial\omega
. In particular, we thoroughly discuss the dependencies of
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},1}
,
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},2}
on the constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{1}
,
C_{\textup{P}}^{2}
, the shape-regularity parameter of
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, the number of elements in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, and the polynomial degree
p
. Three different proofs, leading to various dependencies, are presented in Section
6
, relying either on available results from the literature (invoking equivalences between discrete and continuous minimizers or stable commuting projections) or on a self-standing proof invoking piecewise Piola transformations. In Section
6
, we also recall the equivalence of discrete Poincaré inequalities with the existence of graph-stable commuting projections.

2.
Setting and compact notation

Let
\omega
be a three-dimensional, open, bounded, connected, Lipschitz polyhedral domain with boundary

\partial\omega
and unit outward normal
\bm{n}_{\omega}
.
Let
h_{\omega}
denote the diameter of
\omega
.
We use boldface font for vector-valued quantities, vector-valued fields, and
functional spaces composed of such fields.
For simplicity, the inner product in
L^{2}(\omega)
and
\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)
is
abbreviated as
\bigl\langle\cdot,\cdot\bigr\rangle_{\omega}
, whereas the norms are written as
\|{\cdot}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}
,
\|{\cdot}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}
.

2.1.
Sobolev spaces

Let
H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega):=H^{1}(\omega)
be the standard Sobolev space of scalar-valued functions from
L^{2}(\omega)
with weak gradient in
\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)
,
\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
the Sobolev space of vector-valued functions from
\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)
with weak curl in
\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)
, and
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
the Sobolev space of vector-valued functions from
\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)
with weak divergence in
L^{2}(\omega)
, cf., e.g.,
[
39
, Sections 2.2–2.3]
and
[
27
, Section 4.3]
.
These spaces are Hilbert spaces when equipped with the graph norms

(2.1a)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+h_{\omega}^{2}\|{\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}}\,u\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}^{2},

(2.1b)

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+h_{\omega}^{2}\|{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}^{2},

(2.1c)

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+h_{\omega}^{2}\|{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}.

The length scale
h_{\omega}
is used for dimensional consistency (in terms of physical units) and corresponds to the scaling in the Poincaré inequalities (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).
We denote by
\mathring{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega):=H^{1}_{0}(\omega)
,
\mathring{\bm{H}}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
, and
\mathring{\bm{H}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
the subspaces with homogeneous boundary conditions imposed along
\partial\omega
with the usual trace maps associated with the trace, the trace of the tangential component, and the trace of the normal component on
\partial\omega
. Specifically, for a smooth function or field, the trace maps are
\gamma^{0}_{\partial\omega}(u)=u|_{\partial\omega}
,

\gamma^{1}_{\partial\omega}(\bm{u})=\bm{u}|_{\partial\omega}{\times}\bm{n}_{\omega}
, and

\gamma^{2}_{\partial\omega}(\bm{u})=\bm{u}|_{\partial\omega}{\cdot}\bm{n}_{\omega}
.

2.2.
Mesh and piecewise polynomial spaces

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
be a triangulation of
\omega
consisting of a finite number of tetrahedra. The shape-regularity parameter of
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
is defined as

(2.2)

\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}:=\max_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}h_{\tau}/\iota_{\tau},

where
h_{\tau}
is the diameter of
\tau
and
\iota_{\tau}
the diameter of the largest ball inscribed in
\tau
. We also denote by
|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|
the cardinality of
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, i.e., the number of elements in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
. Let
p\geq 0
be a fixed polynomial degree. For a tetrahedron
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, let
\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\tau)
denote the space of polynomials of total degree at most
p
on
\tau
,
\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\tau;\mathbb{R}^{3})
its vector-valued counterpart,

(2.3)

\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\tau):=\{\bm{u}(\bm{x})+\bm{x}\times\bm{v}(\bm{x}):\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\tau;\mathbb{R}^{3})\}

the
p
-th order Nédélec space
[
44
]
, and

(2.4)

\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\tau):=\{\bm{u}(\bm{x})+v(\bm{x})\bm{x}:\bm{u}\in\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\tau;\mathbb{R}^{3}),v\in\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\tau)\}

the
p
-th order Raviart–Thomas space
[
47
]
. We denote the broken spaces (that is, discontinuous piecewise polynomial, without any continuity requirement across the mesh interfaces) as

(2.5a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})

\displaystyle:=\{u_{\mathcal{T}}\in L^{2}(\omega):u_{\mathcal{T}}|_{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p+1}(\tau)\forall\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}\},

(2.5b)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})

\displaystyle:=\{\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\omega):\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}|_{\tau}\in\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\tau)\forall\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}\},

(2.5c)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})

\displaystyle:=\{\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\omega):\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}|_{\tau}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\tau)\forall\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}\}.

The usual subspaces with continuous trace, tangential trace, and normal trace are
\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)
,
\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
, and

\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
. We proceed similarly for the homogeneous-trace subspaces.
Here and in what follows, the subscript
T
generically refers to functions and fields that sit in the above finite-dimensional spaces.

2.3.
Compact notation

We introduce here a compact notation that allows us to present the subsequent developments in a unified setting.

At the continuous level, we denote

(2.6a)

\displaystyle V^{0}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega),

\displaystyle\qquad\mathring{V}^{0}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\mathring{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega),

(2.6b)

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega),

\displaystyle\qquad\mathring{\bm{V}}^{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\mathring{\bm{H}}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega),

(2.6c)

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega),

\displaystyle\qquad\mathring{\bm{V}}^{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\mathring{\bm{H}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega),

(2.6d)

\displaystyle V^{3}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=L^{2}(\omega),

\displaystyle\qquad\mathring{V}^{3}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\mathring{L}^{2}(\omega):=\{u\in L^{2}(\omega)\;:\;\bigl\langle u,1\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0\}.

With this notation, we have the well-known de Rham sequences

(2.7a)

\mathbb{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\subset}}{{\xrightarrow{\hskip 14.22636pt}}}\ V^{0}(\omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}}}}{{\xrightarrow{\hskip 14.22636pt}}}\ \bm{V}^{1}(\omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}}}{{\xrightarrow{\hskip 14.22636pt}}}\ \bm{V}^{2}(\omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}}}{{\xrightarrow{\hskip 14.22636pt}}}\ V^{3}(\omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xrightarrow{\hskip 14.22636pt}}}0,

(2.7b)

Similarly, at the discrete level, we denote

3.
Equivalent statements for discrete Poincaré inequalities

In this section, we recall that the discrete Poincaré inequalities (
1.4
), i.e., (
2.12
) for
l\in\{1{:}2\}
, are equivalent to: (i) stability of discrete constrained minimization problems; (ii) discrete inf-sup conditions; and (iii) bounds on operator norms of piecewise polynomial vector potential operators. All these equivalences are known from the literature, but possibly not that well known, and definitely seldom presented together. We find it instructive to briefly recall them, including proofs. Similar equivalences hold when homogeneous boundary conditions are imposed on the boundary
\partial\omega
and are not detailed for brevity. These equivalences only consider finite-dimensional spaces and are rather easy to expose. A further equivalence with the existence of graph-stable commuting projections includes the infinite-dimensional spaces
V^{l}(\omega)
and requires a bit more setup; we postpone it to Lemma
6.7
below.

We proceed with the compact notation of Section
2.3
. For the reader’s convenience, we also state the results explicitly for the practically relevant cases of
\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
and
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
. In particular, the discrete spaces
d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
here take the form

(3.1a)

\displaystyle d^{1}(V_{p}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))

\displaystyle={\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}(\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega))\subset\{\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)\text{ with }{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=0\},

(3.1b)

\displaystyle d^{2}(V_{p}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))

\displaystyle={\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}(\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega))=\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}).

When the boundary of
\omega
is connected, we have more precisely

{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}(\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega))=\{\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)\text{ with }{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=0\}
.

3.1.
Equivalence with stability of discrete constrained minimization problems

Let
l\in\{1{:}2\}
,
r_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
, and consider the
constrained quadratic minimization problem

(3.2)

u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}:=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\\
d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=r_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2},

Since the minimization set is closed, convex, and nonempty as we suppose
r_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
and the minimized functional is continuous and strongly convex, the above problem has a unique solution.

Lemma 3.1

(Equivalence of (
2.12
) with stability of discrete constrained minimization in
V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
)
.

The discrete Poincaré inequalities (
2.12
) are equivalent to the stability of (
3.2
) in the sense that

(3.3)

\|u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}h_{\omega}\|r_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\qquad\forall l\in\{1{:}2\}.

Proof.

The Euler optimality conditions for (
3.2
) allow for the following equivalent rewriting of (
3.2
):

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{Find }u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\text{ with }d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}=r_{\mathcal{T}}\text{ such that }\\
&\bigl\langle u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*},v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0\quad\forall v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\text{ with }d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Thus, (
2.12
) readily implies (
3.3
). Conversely, if (
3.3
) holds, given any
u_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
satisfying the orthogonality constraints in (
2.12
), one considers the constrained minimization problem (
3.2
) with the datum
r_{\mathcal{T}}:=d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}
. Since
u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}=u_{\mathcal{T}}
by uniqueness, (
3.3
) implies (
2.12
).
∎

In the two cases
l\in\{1{:}2\}
, the minimization (
3.2
) writes, for
\bm{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}(\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega))
and
r_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
, respectively, as

(3.5)

\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.19579pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)\\
{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=\bm{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\text{ and }\,\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT\hskip-1.75pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)\\
{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=r_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}^{2}.

Lemma
3.1
then states that the discrete Poincaré inequalities (
1.4
)
are equivalent to the stabilities

(3.6)

\|\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},1}h_{\omega}\|\bm{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}\,\text{ and }\,\|\bm{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},2}h_{\omega}\|r_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

3.2.
Equivalence with discrete inf-sup conditions

Let
l\in\{1{:}2\}
,
r_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
and consider the following problem:

(3.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{Find }u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\text{ and }s_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}&&\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))\text{ such}&&\text{ that }\\
&\bigl\langle u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*},v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}-\bigl\langle s_{\mathcal{T}}^{*},d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}&&=0&&\forall v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}),\\
&\bigl\langle d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*},t_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}&&=\bigl\langle r_{\mathcal{T}},t_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}&&\forall t_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})),\end{aligned}\right.

which is called a mixed formulation of (
3.4
). As the differential operator
d^{l}
is surjective from
V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
onto
d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
by definition, the Euler conditions (
3.4
) are equivalent to the mixed formulation (
3.7
).
We now recall that the discrete Poincaré inequalities (
2.12
) are equivalent to the
discrete inf-sup conditions

(3.8)

\inf_{t_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))}\,\,\sup_{v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}\frac{\bigl\langle t_{\mathcal{T}},d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}}{\|t_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}}\geq\frac{1}{C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}h_{\omega}}\qquad\forall l\in\{1{:}2\}.

Lemma 3.2

(Equivalence of (
2.12
) with the discrete inf-sup conditions)
.

The discrete Poincaré inequalities (
2.12
) are equivalent to the discrete inf-sup conditions (
3.8
).

Proof.

Let
l\in\{1{:}2\}
.
Since (
2.12
) is equivalent to the stability property (
3.3
) as per Lemma
3.1
, we prove the equivalence between (
3.3
) and (
3.8
).

(1) Assume the stability property (
3.3
).
Let
t_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
. Consider, as in (
3.4
), the following well-posed problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{Find }v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\text{ with }d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=t_{\mathcal{T}}\text{ such that }\\
&\bigl\langle v_{\mathcal{T}},w_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0\quad\forall w_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\text{ with }d^{l}w_{\mathcal{T}}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

The stability property (
3.3
) gives

\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}h_{\omega}\|t_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Now, since
d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=t_{\mathcal{T}}
, we infer from this bound that

\bigl\langle t_{\mathcal{T}},d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=\|t_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\geq\frac{\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\|t_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}}{C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}h_{\omega}},

which gives the discrete inf-sup condition (
3.8
).

(2) Conversely, we now suppose (
3.8
) and show that this implies (
3.3
). Let
r_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
and let
u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}
solve (
3.2
). Since (
3.2
) is equivalent to (
3.4
) which is in turn equivalent to (
3.7
), we can consider
s_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
so that the pair
(u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*},s_{\mathcal{T}}^{*})
solves (
3.7
). Using in (
3.7
) the test functions
v_{\mathcal{T}}=u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}
and
t_{\mathcal{T}}=s_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}
and summing the two equations, we infer that

\|u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}=\bigl\langle r_{\mathcal{T}},s_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}\leq\|r_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\|s_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},

where we used the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Now, the discrete inf-sup condition (
3.8
) gives the existence of
v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
such that

\|s^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}h_{\omega}\frac{\bigl\langle s_{\mathcal{T}}^{*},d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}}{\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}}.

From the first equation in (
3.7
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\frac{\bigl\langle s_{\mathcal{T}}^{*},d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}}{\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}}=\frac{\bigl\langle u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*},v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}}{\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}}\leq\|u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Combining the three above inequalities, (
3.3
) follows.
∎

In the two cases
l\in\{1{:}2\}
, the discrete inf-sup conditions (
3.8
) respectively write as

(3.9)

\inf_{\bm{t}_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}(\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.22911pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega))}\,\,\sup_{\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.22911pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)}\frac{\bigl\langle\bm{t}_{\mathcal{T}},{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}}{\|\bm{t}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}\|\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}}\geq\frac{1}{C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},1}h_{\omega}}

and

(3.10)

\inf_{t_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.22911pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}\,\,\sup_{\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT\hskip-1.75pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)}\frac{\bigl\langle t_{\mathcal{T}},{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}}{\|t_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\|\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}}\geq\frac{1}{C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},2}h_{\omega}}.

By Lemma
3.2
, they are equivalent to the discrete Poincaré inequalities (
1.4
).

3.3.
Equivalence with bounds on operator norms of piecewise polynomial vector potential operators

Let
l\in\{1{:}2\}
and
r_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
.
A piecewise polynomial vector potential is any field
\Phi^{l}_{\mathcal{T}}(r_{\mathcal{T}})\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
such that
d^{l}(\Phi^{l}_{\mathcal{T}}(r_{\mathcal{T}}))=r_{\mathcal{T}}
, and we say that

(3.11)

\Phi^{l}_{\mathcal{T}}:d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))\rightarrow V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})

is a
piecewise polynomial vector potential operator
(piecewise polynomial right-inverse of the
d^{l}
operator, also called Poincaré map). We are particularly interested in the
L^{2}(\omega)
-norm minimizing operators

(3.12)

\Phi^{l,*}_{\mathcal{T}}(r_{\mathcal{T}}):=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\\
d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=r_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2},

with operator norm

(3.13)

\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\Phi^{l,*}_{\mathcal{T}}\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|:=\max_{v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}\frac{\|\Phi^{l,*}_{\mathcal{T}}(d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}})\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}}{\|d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}}.

Lemma 3.4

(Equivalence of the best constant in (
2.12
) with the operator norm of the minimal discrete vector potential operator)
.

The operator norm
\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\Phi^{l,*}_{\mathcal{T}}\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|
from (
3.13
) equals the best discrete Poincaré inequality constant
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}h_{\omega}
from (
2.12
).

Proof.

Observe that (
3.12
) matches exactly the form of the constrained minimization (
3.2
) and use Lemma
3.1
.
∎

In the
\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)
setting, the
\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)
-norm minimizing potential operator is

(3.14)

\Phi^{\mathrm{curl},*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{r}_{\mathcal{T}}):=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.19579pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)\\
{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=\bm{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}^{2},

and its operator norm is

(3.15)

\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\Phi^{\mathrm{curl},*}_{\mathcal{T}}\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|:=\max_{\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{N}}\hskip-1.22911pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)}\frac{\|\Phi^{\mathrm{curl},*}_{\mathcal{T}}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}})\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}}{\|{\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}}.

In the
\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
setting, the
\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)
-norm minimizing potential operator is

(3.16)

\Phi^{{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},*}_{\mathcal{T}}(r_{\mathcal{T}}):=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT\hskip-1.75pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)\\
{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=r_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}^{2},

with operator norm

(3.17)

\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\Phi^{{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},*}_{\mathcal{T}}\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|\hskip-1.8063pt\big|:=\max_{\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT\hskip-1.75pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)}\frac{\|\Phi^{{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},*}_{\mathcal{T}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}})\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}}{\|{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}}.

By Lemma
3.4
, these operator norms are equivalent to the best constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}h_{\omega}
in the discrete Poincaré inequalities (
1.4
).

4.
Continuous Poincaré inequalities

In this section, we state the continuous Poincaré inequalities and give some
pointers to the literature for bounds on the continuous Poincaré constants. This will pave the way to our main topic, the discrete Poincaré inequalities.

Proposition 4.1

(Continuous Poincaré inequalities)
.

(i)
Continuous Poincaré inequalities without boundary conditions: There exist constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{l}
,
l\in\{0{:}2\}
, only depending on the shape of
\omega
, such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{l}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall u\in V^{l}(\omega)\text{ such that }\bigl\langle u,v\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0

(4.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\forall v\in V^{l}(\omega)\text{ with }d^{l}v=0\qquad\forall l\in\{0{:}2\}.

(ii)
Continuous Poincaré inequalities with boundary conditions: There exist constants
\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}
,
l\in\{0{:}2\}
, only depending on the shape of
\omega
, such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall u\in\mathring{V}^{l}(\omega)\text{ such that }\bigl\langle u,v\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0

(4.2)

\displaystyle\qquad\forall v\in\mathring{V}^{l}(\omega)\text{ with }d^{l}v=0\qquad\forall l\in\{0{:}2\}.

Remark 4.2

(Proofs without explicit bounds on constants)
.

(i) One known route from the literature to establish the inequalities (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) is to invoke a
compactness
argument, which can be formalized in the following Peetre–Tartar lemma
[
27
, Lemma A.20]
: Let
X,Y,Z
be three Banach spaces, let
A\in\mathcal{L}(X;Y)
be an injective operator, and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(X;Z)
be a compact operator. Assume that there is
\gamma>0
such that
\gamma\|u\|_{X}\leq\|A(u)\|_{Y}+\|T(u)\|_{Z}
for all
u\in X
. Then there is
\alpha>0
such that

(4.3)

\alpha\|u\|_{X}\leq\|A(u)\|_{Y}\qquad\forall u\in X.

The Peetre–Tartar lemma can be combined with a (simple and natural)
scaling argument in the definition of the norms
to make the constant
\alpha
in (
4.3
) nondimensional.
To briefly illustrate, let us prove (
4.1
) for
l=0
.
We set
X:=\{u\in H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)\;|\;\bigl\langle u,1\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0\}
,
\bm{Y}:=\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)
,

Z:=L^{2}(\omega)
,
\bm{A}(u):=h_{\omega}{\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}}\,u
, and
T(u):=u
. The operator
\bm{A}
is injective since any
u\in X
such that
\bm{A}(u)=\bm{0}
is
L^{2}
-orthogonal to itself and thus vanishes identically. Moreover,
T
is compact since the embedding
H^{1}(\omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\omega)
is compact. Finally, we have
\|u\|_{X}^{2}=\|u\|_{H({\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}},\omega)}^{2}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+h_{\omega}^{2}\|{\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}}\,u\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)}^{2}=\|T(u)\|_{Z}^{2}+\|\bm{A}(u)\|_{\bm{Y}}^{2}
. Thus, by the Peetre–Tartar Lemma, (

) for

holds true. The proof for the other Poincaré inequalities is similar. In particular, for the curl and divergence operators, one invokes the compactness of the embeddings

and
\mathring{\bm{H}}({\mathop{\textup{\bf curl}}},\omega)\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)\hookrightarrow\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)

[

[

,
[

and
[

.
(ii) Another, somewhat related, route to prove the Poincaré inequalities hinges

(iii) If

5.
Discrete Poincaré inequalities

In this section, we present our main result on the discrete
Poincaré inequalities. We focus on the dependency of the discrete
Poincaré constants on the continuous-level constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{l}
,
\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}
,
l\in\{0{:}2\}
, and the shape-regularity parameter
\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
, the number of
tetrahedra
|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|
, and the polynomial degree
p
.
We shall consider in Section
6
three routes
to prove these inequalities, each one leading to different dependencies of the constants.

5.1.
Triangulations and finite element stars

Some specific tetrahedral meshes
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
will be of particular interest.
We either look at
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
as a triangulation of some computational domain
\omega
, or we consider
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
as some local (vertex, edge, face) star of a shape-regular simplicial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of some larger three-dimensional computational domain
\Omega
(open, bounded, connected, Lipschitz polyhedral set).
Let
\tau
be a tetrahedron from
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
. We will call a “twice-extended element star” a collection of such tetrahedra
\tau^{\prime}
from
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
which either share a vertex with
\tau
,
\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, or such that there exists a tetrahedron
\tau^{\prime\prime}
from
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
such that
\tau^{\prime}
shares a vertex with
\tau^{\prime\prime}
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}
shares a vertex with
\tau
.
As a specific case, we will consider triangulations
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
where all the domains of twice-extended element stars are Lipschitz and with a contractible closure.

5.2.
Main result

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 5.1

(Discrete Poincaré inequalities)
.

(i)
Discrete Poincaré inequalities without boundary conditions: There exist constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}
,
l\in\{0{:}2\}
, such that

\displaystyle\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall u_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\text{ such that }\bigl\langle u_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0

(5.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\forall v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\text{ with }d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=0\qquad\forall l\in\{0{:}2\}.

(ii)
Discrete Poincaré inequalities with boundary conditions:
There exist constants
\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}
,
l\in\{0{:}2\}
, such that

\displaystyle\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall u_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathring{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\text{ such that }\bigl\langle u_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0

(5.2)

\displaystyle\qquad\forall v_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathring{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\text{ with }d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=0\qquad\forall l\in\{0{:}2\}.

Here, the constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}
,
\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}
have the following properties:

(1)

C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},0}\leq C_{\textup{P}}^{0}

\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},0}\leq\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{0}

(2)

l\in\{1{:}2\}

\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}

and
\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}\leq C_{\mathrm{min}}^{l}\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}

(3)

There exist constants
C_{\mathrm{st}}^{l}

l\in\{1{:}2\}

(4)

The constants

\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}

, the number of tetrahedra

,

5.3.
Discussion

Let us discuss items (
2
) to (
4
) of Theorem
5.1
:

•

Discussion of (
2
).
This result is established in Section
6.2
below and relies on piecewise polynomial extension operators.
The constants here are systematically
p
-robust, but can unfavorably depend on the number of tetrahedra in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
. In stars or extended stars or any local patches,
|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|
is bounded as a function of the shape-regularity parameter
\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
, leading to discrete Poincaré constants only depending on
\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
and the continuous Poincaré constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{l}
or
\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}
,
l\in\{1{:}2\}
(for which upper bounds only depending on the shape-regularity parameter
\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
can be derived as discussed in Remark
4.3
). The assumption that
\overline{\omega}
is contractible is automatically satisfied if
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
is a vertex or edge star. For a general domain
\Omega
with a mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, local stars with contractible
\omega
are supposed in, e.g.,
[
34
,
2
]
. This assumption does not request the whole computational domain
\Omega
to be contractible, but merely the local star-domains
\omega
. For example, for a domain
\Omega
with a hole, there may be local stars with non-contractible corresponding
\overline{\omega}
if
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is rather coarse, but typically all local star-domains
\omega
are of contractible closure on finer meshes. We refer for further details to the recent discussion in
[
30
, Remark 2.1]
.

•

Discussion of (
3
).
This result is proven in Section
6.3
below upon relying on stable commuting projections. This is probably the most common way of proving the discrete Poincaré inequalities. In this case, the constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}
,
\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}
,
l\in\{1{:}2\}
, are independent of the number of tetrahedra in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
(i.e., this number can be arbitrarily high), but may (unfavorably) depend on the polynomial degree
p
.
In the
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
setting (
l=2
), the
p
-robust projector from
[
23
, Definition 3.3]
gives a constant
C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},2}
independent of both the number of tetrahedra in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
and the polynomial degree
p
if the domains of all twice-extended element stars in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
are Lipschitz and with a contractible closure (as discussed above, this is typically satisfied in practice, at least for sufficient mesh refinement). To our knowledge, this is the best result available so far. Once again, upper bounds on the continuous Poincaré constants only depending on the shape-regularity parameter
\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
can be derived as discussed in Remark
4.3
.

•

Discussion of (
4
).
This result is established in Section
6.4
below. The technique of proof does not rely on the continuous Poincaré inequalities. Therefore, the upper bounds on the discrete Poincaré constants do not involve here the constants
C_{\textup{P}}^{l}
,
\mathring{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}
,
l\in\{1{:}2\}
. There are no requirements on the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
either (as
\overline{\omega}
being contractible or
\omega
being a local star). The direct proof argument leads to discrete Poincaré constants depending on the shape-regularity parameter
\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
, the number of tetrahedra
|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|
, and the polynomial degree
p
.

(Extension to any space dimension in the framework of finite element exterior calculus)

5
,

6.
Proofs of discrete Poincaré inequalities

r_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}({\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
, the solutions to (
6.8
) satisfy

(6.11)

\|u^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{min}}^{l}\|u^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Then (
6.7
) holds true with constant
\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}\leq C_{\mathrm{min}}^{l}\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}
.

Proof.

Let
u_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
. Set
r_{\mathcal{T}}:=d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}
. Since
{\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\subset{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
, we have

r_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}({\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
. Moreover, by considering the Euler conditions (
6.9
) and (
6.10
), we infer that
u^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
and

u^{*}\in{\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}}^{l}(\omega)
. In addition, since the minimization problems admit a unique solution, and since
u_{\mathcal{T}}
satisfies the Euler conditions for the discrete problem, we have
u_{\mathcal{T}}=u^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}
. Invoking (
6.11
) followed by the continuous Poincaré inequality (
6.6
) gives

\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\|u^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{min}}^{l}\|u^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{min}}^{l}\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}u^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Since, from (
6.8
),
\|d^{l}u^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\|r_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\|d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}
, we conclude that (
6.7
) holds true with constant
\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}\leq C_{\mathrm{min}}^{l}\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}
.
∎

In the case of homogeneous boundary conditions, the minimizations (
6.8
) take the form

(6.12)

u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}:=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathring{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\\
d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=r_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2},\qquad u^{*}:=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathring{V}^{l}(\omega)\\
d^{l}v=r_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}

with data
r_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(\mathring{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
. In the case
l=2
(divergence operator), (
6.11
) has been established in
[
32
, Corollaries 3.3 and 3.8]
whenever
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
is a vertex star, see also
[

, Proposition 3.1 and Corollary 4.1]
.
In the case
l=1
(curl operator), (
6.11
) has been established in
[
11
, Proposition 6.6]

for edge stars and in
[
13
, Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 4.3]
for vertex stars.

In the case without boundary conditions, the minimizations (
6.8
) take the form

(6.13)

u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}:=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\\
d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=r_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2},\qquad u^{*}:=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V^{l}(\omega)\\
d^{l}v=r_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}

with data
r_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(V_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
.
When the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
is more complex than a vertex star,
[

, Theorem C.1]
gives the desired result (
6.11
) in the case

(divergence operator) under the assumption that
\overline{\omega}
is contractible. The case

(curl operator) can be treated similarly.

) of Theorem

(recall that for stars or any local patch,

only depends on

Lemma 6.2

(Discrete Poincaré inequalities invoking stable commuting projections)
.

Assume that there are projections

\Pi_{p}^{m}:{\widetilde{V}}^{m}(\omega)\rightarrow{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{m}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
,
m\in\{1{:}3\}
, satisfying, for all
l\in\{1{:}2\}
, the commuting
property

(6.14)

d^{l}(\Pi_{p}^{l}(u))=\Pi_{p}^{l+1}(d^{l}u)\qquad\forall u\in{\widetilde{V}}^{l}(\omega),

and the
L^{2}
-stability property on data whose image by
d^{l}
is piecewise polynomial

(6.15)

\|\Pi_{p}^{l}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\qquad\forall u\in{\widetilde{V}}^{l}(\omega)\text{ such that }d^{l}u\in{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}).

Then (
6.7
) holds true with constant
\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}
.

Proof.

Let
u_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
. Set
r_{\mathcal{T}}:=d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}
. We consider again the
minimization problems in (
6.8
). Recall that both problems
are well-posed and that
u_{\mathcal{T}}=u^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}
. We observe that
\Pi_{p}^{l}(u^{*})\in{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})

by definition, that
d^{l}u^{*}=r_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
, and that

d^{l}(\Pi_{p}^{l}(u^{*}))=\Pi_{p}^{l+1}(d^{l}u^{*})=\Pi_{p}^{l+1}(r_{\mathcal{T}})=r_{\mathcal{T}},

where we used the commuting property (
6.14
), the fact that

r_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
, and that
\Pi_{p}^{l+1}
is a projection.
This shows that
\Pi_{p}^{l}(u^{*})
is in the discrete minimization set. Using the
L^{2}
-stability property (
6.15
) and the continuous Poincaré inequality (
6.6
), we infer that

(6.16)

\begin{split}\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\|u^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}&\leq\|\Pi_{p}^{l}(u^{*})\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\\
&\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\|u^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\\
&\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}u^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.\end{split}

Since
\|d^{l}u^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\|r_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\|d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}
, we conclude that (
6.7
) holds true with constant
\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}
.
∎

Operators satisfying (
6.14
)–(
6.15
) have been constructed in
[
26
, Definition 3.1]
(for
l=2
) and in
[
14
, Definition 2]
for
l=1
.
In all these cases,
C_{\mathrm{st}}
depends on the shape-regularity parameter
\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
and on the polynomial degree
p
, but is independent of the number of tetrahedra in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
.
In the
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
setting (
l=2
), the
p
-robust projector of
[
23
, Definition 3.3]
gives a constant
C_{\mathrm{st}}
only depending on the shape-regularity parameter
\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
if the domains of all twice-extended element stars in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
are Lipschitz and with a contractible closure. All these cases are summarized in item (
3
) of Theorem
5.1
.

Remark 6.3

(Example of a stable
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
commuting projection)
.

Let us rewrite the
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
construction from
[
26
, Definition 3.1]
in the present setting so as to give an idea on how the key properties (
6.14
)–(
6.15
) follow. Let thus
l=2
, and consider for instance the case without boundary conditions. Let
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}^{2}(\omega)=\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
be given. The construction of
\Pi_{p}^{2}(\bm{u})
proceeds in three steps.

(1)

On each tetrahedron
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, one considers the
\bm{L}^{2}(\tau)
-orthogonal projection of
\bm{u}|_{\tau}
onto the Raviart–Thomas space
\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\tau)
(see (
2.4
)) under a divergence constraint,

(6.17)

\bm{\xi}_{\mathcal{T}}|_{\tau}:=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT\hskip-1.75pt}}_{p}(\tau)\\
{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=\Pi_{p}^{3}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{u})\end{subarray}}\|\bm{u}-\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\tau)},

where
\Pi_{p}^{3}
is the
L^{2}(\omega)
-orthogonal projection onto
V_{p}^{3}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})=\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
(note that this projection is elementwise, since
\mathcal{P}\hskip-1.49994pt_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
is a space of piecewise polynomials without any continuity requirement across the mesh interfaces). As there is no normal trace prescription for
\bm{\xi}_{\mathcal{T}}
, it belongs to the discontinuous piecewise polynomial space
\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
only.

(2)

For each vertex
v
from the vertex set of
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
,
v\in\mathcal{V}_{\omega}
, let
\mathcal{T}_{v}
be the vertex star (all tetrahedra of
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
sharing
v
).
One defines the Raviart–Thomas polynomial
\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{T}}^{v}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{v})\cap\mathring{\bm{H}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega_{v})
such that

(6.18)

\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{T}}^{v}:=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT\hskip-1.75pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{v})\cap\mathring{\bm{H}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega_{v})\\
{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=\Pi_{p}^{3}(\psi^{v}{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{u}+{\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}}\,\psi^{v}{\cdot}\bm{\xi}_{\mathcal{T}})\end{subarray}}\|\bm{I}^{\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT}}}_{p}(\psi^{v}\bm{\xi}_{\mathcal{T}})-\bm{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\omega_{v})}.

Here,
\psi^{v}
is the hat basis function, the piecewise affine scalar-valued function taking value
1
at the vertex
v
and
0
at the other vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
,
\mathring{\bm{H}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega_{v})
is the subspace of
\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega_{v})
with homogenous normal component over the faces where
\psi^{v}
vanishes, and
\bm{I}^{\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT}}}_{p}
is the elementwise canonical Raviart–Thomas projector (applied to the piecewise polynomial
\psi^{v}\bm{\xi}_{\mathcal{T}}
, so that its action is well defined).

(3)

Finally, one extends
\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{T}}^{v}
by zero outside of the patch subdomain
\omega_{v}
and defines
\Pi_{p}^{2}(\bm{u})\in\bm{V}_{p}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})=\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
via

(6.19)

\Pi_{p}^{2}(\bm{u}):=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\omega}}\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{T}}^{v}.

Step (
1
) above projects
\bm{u}
onto the finite-dimensional space
\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
.
Steps (
2
) and (
3
) above amount to the so-called flux equilibration from a posteriori error analysis
[
24
,
10
,
31
]
. Owing to the partition of unity by the hat functions,

(6.20)

\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\omega}}\psi^{v}=1,

the commuting property (
6.14
) is straightforward since

{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\Pi_{p}^{2}(\bm{u})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_sigma}}}{{=}}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\omega}}{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{T}}^{v}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_sigma_a}}}{{=}}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\omega}}\Pi_{p}^{3}(\psi^{v}{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{u}+{\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}}\,\psi^{v}{\cdot}\bm{\xi}_{\mathcal{T}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_PU}}}{{=}}\Pi_{p}^{3}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{u}).

The projection property amounts to
\Pi_{p}^{2}(\bm{u})=\bm{u}
if
\bm{u}\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\cap\bm{H}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega)
. This follows easily from the following three arguments: 1)
\bm{\xi}_{\mathcal{T}}=\bm{u}
in (
6.17
); 2)
\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{T}}^{v}=\bm{I}^{\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT}}}_{p}(\psi^{v}\bm{u})
in (
6.18
), since the elementwise canonical Raviart–Thomas projector gives
\bm{I}^{\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT}}}_{p}(\psi^{v}\bm{u})\in\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-1.00006ptT\hskip-2.5pt}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{v})\cap\mathring{\bm{H}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}},\omega_{v})
and its commuting property implies that
{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{I}^{\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT}}}_{p}(\psi^{v}\bm{u})=\Pi_{p}^{3}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,(\psi^{v}\bm{u}))=\Pi_{p}^{3}(\psi^{v}{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}}\,\bm{u}+{\mathop{\textup{\bf grad}}}\,\psi^{v}{\cdot}\bm{u})
; 3) we conclude that

\Pi_{p}^{2}(\bm{u}):=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\omega}}\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{T}}^{v}=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\omega}}\bm{I}^{\bm{\mathcal{R\hskip-0.70004ptT}}}_{p}(\psi^{v}\bm{u})=\bm{u}

from (
6.19
) and (
6.20
).
Finally, the stability property (
6.15
) is proven in
[
26
, Theorem 3.2, property (3.7)]
using the stability of the vertex star problems (
6.18
) and the obvious stability of the elementwise problems (
6.17
).

Remark 6.5

(Graph-norm stability of
\Pi_{p}^{l}
)
.

Actually, the proof still works if
one considers commuting projections that are stable in the graph norm

(6.21)

\|v\|_{\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)}:=\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+h_{\omega}^{2}\|d^{l}v\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

leading to the bound
\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\big(1+(\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l})^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Indeed, the final step (
6.16
) of the above proofs now writes

\displaystyle\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\|u^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi_{p}^{l}(u^{*})\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\|u^{*}\|_{\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\big(1+(\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l})^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Remark 6.6

(Fractional-order Sobolev stability of
\Pi_{p}^{l}
)
.

It is also possible to invoke regularity results stating that

\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)\hookrightarrow H^{s}(\omega)
,
s>\frac{1}{2}
,
with embedding constant
C_{\mathrm{emb}}
so that

\|v\|_{H^{s}(\omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{emb}}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}v\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\qquad\forall v\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega),

where

\|v\|_{H^{s}(\omega)}^{2}=\|v\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+h_{\omega}^{s}|v|_{H^{s}(\omega)}^{2},\qquad|v|_{H^{s}(\omega)}^{2}=\int_{\omega}\int_{\omega}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{3+2s}}dxdy.

(Again, the scaling by
h_{\omega}
is introduced for dimensional consistency.)
This allows one to consider commuting projections that are stable only in

H^{s}(\omega)
,
s>\frac{1}{2}
, i.e.,

\|\Pi_{p}^{l}(z)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\|z\|_{H^{s}(\omega)}\qquad\forall z\in H^{s}(\omega).

The proof of the discrete Poincaré inequality then runs as follows.
For all
u_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
, there exists

z\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)
such that
d^{l}z=d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}
(indeed,
take
z:=u_{\mathcal{T}}-m
, where
m
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection of
u_{\mathcal{T}}

onto
\mathfrak{Z}\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)
). We have

\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}=\bigl\langle u_{\mathcal{T}},\Pi^{l}_{p}(z)\bigr\rangle_{\omega}+\bigl\langle u_{\mathcal{T}},u_{\mathcal{T}}-\Pi^{l}_{p}(z)\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=\bigl\langle u_{\mathcal{T}},\Pi^{l}_{p}(z)\bigr\rangle_{\omega},

since
u_{\mathcal{T}}-\Pi^{l}_{p}(z)\in\mathfrak{Z}\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
(indeed,

d^{l}(u_{\mathcal{T}}-\Pi^{l}_{p}(z))=d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}-\Pi_{p}^{l+1}(d^{l}z)=d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}-\Pi_{p}^{l+1}(d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}})=0
since
\Pi_{p}^{l+1}
leaves

\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
pointwise invariant).
The above identity together with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq\|\Pi_{p}^{l}(z)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Observing that
z\in H^{s}(\omega)
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi_{p}^{l}(z)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\|z\|_{H^{s}(\omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}C_{\mathrm{emb}}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}z\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=C_{\mathrm{st}}C_{\mathrm{emb}}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},

which proves (
6.7
) with constant
\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}C_{\mathrm{emb}}
. The above approach was considered in early works where
L^{2}
-stable or graph-stable commuting projections were not yet available. The idea is to trade some stability of
\Pi^{p}_{l}
by invoking subtle regularity results on the curl and divergence operators. On the downside, estimating
\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}
now requires upper bounds on
C_{\mathrm{st}}
and
C_{\mathrm{emb}}
. The present remark may be of interest for the sake of an historical perspective. The interested reader can find more details in
[
9
]
and the references therein.

With the above developments, we can now add one more equivalent statement for discrete Poincaré inequalities, in the spirit of
[
5
, Theorems 3.6 and 3.7]
. This completes the results on equivalent statements given in Section
3
.

Lemma 6.7

(Equivalence of discrete Poincaré inequalities with the existence of graph-stable commuting projections)
.

The discrete Poincaré inequalities (
6.7
) for
l\in\{1{:}2\}
are equivalent to the existence of projections
\Pi_{p}^{m}:{\widetilde{V}}^{m}(\omega)\rightarrow{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{m}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
,
m\in\{1{:}3\}
, satisfying, for all
l\in\{1{:}2\}
, the commuting property

(6.22)

d^{l}(\Pi_{p}^{l}(u))=\Pi_{p}^{l+1}(d^{l}u)\qquad\forall u\in{\widetilde{V}}^{l}(\omega)

and the graph-stability property

(6.23)

\|\Pi_{p}^{l}(u)\|_{\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\|u\|_{\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)}\qquad\forall u\in{\widetilde{V}}^{l}(\omega).

Proof.

We show the two implications.

(i) That the existence of graph-stable projections satisfying (
6.22
)–(
6.23
) implies the discrete Poincaré inequalities (
6.7
) for
l\in\{1{:}2\}
follows from Lemma
6.2
and Remark
6.5
.

(ii) Suppose the validity of the discrete Poincaré inequalities (
6.7
). We show that this implies the existence of projections satisfying (
6.22
)–(
6.23
).
A generic way is to take
\Pi_{p}^{3}
as the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
\widetilde{V}_{p}^{3}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
and to define
\Pi_{p}^{l}:\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)\rightarrow\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
for all
l\in\{1{:}2\}
by the following constrained quadratic minimization problems, similar to (
3.2
) and (
6.8a
): For all
u\in\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)
,

(6.24)

\Pi_{p}^{l}(u):=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{\mathcal{T}}\in\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\\
d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=\Pi_{p}^{l+1}(d^{l}u)\end{subarray}}\|u-v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2},

first for
l=2
and then for
l=1
. Notice that the commuting property (
6.22
) is built in the definition of
\Pi^{p}_{l}
, so that only the stability in the graph norm (
6.23
) needs to be verified. To this purpose, we notice that the Euler optimality conditions for (
6.24
), as in (
3.4
) and (
6.9
), read as follows: Find
\Pi_{p}^{l}(u)\in\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
with
d^{l}(\Pi_{p}^{l}(u))=\Pi_{p}^{l+1}(d^{l}u)
such that

\bigl\langle\Pi_{p}^{l}(u)-u,v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=0\qquad\forall v_{\mathcal{T}}\in\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\text{ with }d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=0.

The mixed formulation using a Lagrange multiplier, as in (
3.7
), reads as follows: Find
\Pi_{p}^{l}(u)\in\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
and
s_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))
such that

\displaystyle\bigl\langle\Pi_{p}^{l}(u),v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}-\bigl\langle s_{\mathcal{T}},d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\bigl\langle u,v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle\forall v_{\mathcal{T}}\in\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}),

\displaystyle\bigl\langle d^{l}(\Pi_{p}^{l}(u)),t_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\bigl\langle d^{l}u,t_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle\forall t_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})).

(Notice that
\bigl\langle\Pi_{p}^{l+1}(d^{l}u),t_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}=\bigl\langle d^{l}u,t_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}
owing to the Euler optimality conditions for
\Pi_{p}^{l+1}
and the fact that
d^{l+1}t_{\mathcal{T}}=0
). As highlighted in Section
3.2
, the discrete Poincaré inequality (
6.7
) is equivalent to to the discrete inf-sup condition formulated using
L^{2}
-norms, see Lemma
3.2
. The inf-sup condition in the form of (
3.8
) readily implies the discrete inf-sup condition in the graph norm

\inf_{t_{\mathcal{T}}\in d^{l}(\widetilde{V}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega}))}\,\,\sup_{v_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}\frac{\bigl\langle t_{\mathcal{T}},d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\rangle_{\omega}}{\|t_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)}}\geq\frac{1}{\big(1+(C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l})^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{\omega}}.

Then, invoking
[
8
, Theorem 4.2.3]
or
[
28
, Theorem 49.13]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{p}^{l}(u)\|_{\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)}+2\big(1+(C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l})^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{\omega}\|d^{l}u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\big(10+8(C_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l})^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{\widetilde{V}^{l}(\omega)}.

This proves that the commuting projection
\Pi_{p}^{l}
defined above is indeed stable in the graph norm.
∎

6.4.
Route 3: Invoking piecewise Piola transformations

In this section, we prove the discrete Poincaré inequality by a direct argument, thereby circumventing the need to invoke the continuous Poincaré inequalities. The discrete Poincaré constants resulting from the present proofs depend on the shape-regularity parameter
\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
, the number of tetrahedra
|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|
, and the polynomial degree
p
, as summarized in item (
4
) of Theorem
5.1
. The proof shares ideas with the one given in
[
30
]
, but eventually employs a different argument to conclude.

The starting point, shared with
[
30
]
, is to introduce
reference meshes and piecewise Piola transformations on those meshes.
We enumerate the set of vertices (resp., edges, faces, and cells (tetrahedra))
in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
as
\mathcal{V}_{\omega}:=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{N^{\mathrm{v}}}\}

(resp.,
\mathcal{E}_{\omega}:=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{N^{\mathrm{e}}}\}
,

\mathcal{F}_{\omega}:=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{N^{\mathrm{f}}}\}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}:=\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{N^{\mathrm{c}}}\}

with
N^{\mathrm{c}}=|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|
. All these geometric objects are oriented by
increasing vertex enumeration (see, e.g.,
[
27
, Chapter 10]
).
The topology and orientation of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
is completely described by the connectivity arrays

(6.25a)

\displaystyle\texttt{j\_ev}:\{1{:}N^{\mathrm{e}}\}\times\{0{:}1\}\rightarrow\{1{:}N^{\mathrm{v}}\},

(6.25b)

\displaystyle\texttt{j\_fv}:\{1{:}N^{\mathrm{f}}\}\times\{0{:}2\}\rightarrow\{1{:}N^{\mathrm{v}}\},

(6.25c)

\displaystyle\texttt{j\_cv}:\{1{:}N^{\mathrm{c}}\}\times\{0{:}3\}\rightarrow\{1{:}N^{\mathrm{v}}\},

such that
\texttt{j\_ev}(m,n)
is the global vertex number of the vertex
n
of the edge
e_{m}
,
and so on (the local enumeration of vertices is by increasing enumeration order).
Notice that the connectivity arrays only take integer values and are independent of the
actual coordinates of the vertices in the physical space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Let
\rho_{\sharp}>0
be a positive real number and let
T_{\sharp}
be a (finite) integer number.
The number of meshes with shape-regularity parameter bounded from above by
\rho_{\sharp}
and cardinality bounded from above by
T_{\sharp}
with different possible realizations of the connectivity arrays is bounded from above by a constant
\hat{N}_{\sharp}:=\hat{N}(\rho_{\sharp},T_{\sharp})
only depending on
\rho_{\sharp}
and
T_{\sharp}
.
Thus, for each
\rho_{\sharp}
and
T_{\sharp}
, there is a
finite
set of
reference meshes, which we denote by
\widehat{\mathbb{T}}:=\widehat{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\sharp},T_{\sharp})
,
such that every mesh
\mathcal{T}
with the shape-regularity parameter bounded from above by
\rho_{\sharp}
and cardinality bounded from above by
T_{\sharp}
has the same connectivity arrays
as those of one reference mesh in the set
\widehat{\mathbb{T}}
.
We enumerate the reference meshes in
\widehat{\mathbb{T}}
as
\{\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\hat{N}_{\sharp}}\}
and fix them once and for all.
For each reference mesh, the element diameters are of order unity, and the shape-regularity parameter is chosen as small as possible (it is bounded from above by
\rho_{\sharp}
).
For all
j\in\{1{:}\hat{N}_{\sharp}\}
, we let
\widehat{\omega}_{j}
be the open, bounded, connected,
Lipschitz polyhedral set covered by the reference mesh
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j}
.
For all
l\in\{1{:}2\}
, we define the piecewise polynomial spaces
V_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j})

and
\mathring{V}_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j})
as in (
2.8
), and
set
{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j}):=V_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j})
or
\mathring{V}_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j})
depending on whether
boundary conditions are enforced or not.
We also define
{\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j})
as the
L^{2}
-orthogonal complement of
the kernel subspace
\{\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j})\;:\;d^{l}\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}=0\}
in
{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j})
.
Norm equivalence in finite dimension implies that, for all
j\in\{1{:}\hat{N}_{\sharp}\}
and all
p\geq 0
,
there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j},p)
such that

(6.26)

\|\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\omega}_{j})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j},p)\|d^{l}\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\omega}_{j})}\qquad\forall\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j}).

Consider an arbitrary mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
with shape-regularity parameter bounded from above by
\rho_{\sharp}
and cardinality bounded from above by
T_{\sharp}
. Then there is an index
j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\in\{1{:}\hat{N}_{\sharp}\}
so that
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}
share the same connectivity arrays.
Therefore,
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
can be generated from
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}
by a piecewise-affine geometric mapping

\bm{F}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}:=\{\bm{F}_{\tau}:\widehat{\tau}\rightarrow\tau\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
, where all the geometric mappings
\bm{F}_{\tau}
are affine, invertible, with positive Jacobian, and
\bigcup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}\bm{F}_{\tau}^{-1}(\tau)=\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}
.
For all
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, let
\bm{J}_{\tau}
be the Jacobian matrix of
\bm{F}_{\tau}
.
We consider the Piola transformations
\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l}:L^{2}(\omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\widehat{\omega}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})})
, for all
l\in\{1{:}3\}
, such that
\psi_{\tau}^{l}:=\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l}|_{\tau}
is defined as follows: For all
v\in L^{2}(\tau)
,

(6.27a)

\displaystyle\psi_{\tau}^{1}(v)

\displaystyle:=\bm{J}_{\tau}^{\textsc{t}}(v\circ\bm{F}_{\tau}),

(6.27b)

\displaystyle\psi_{\tau}^{2}(v)

\displaystyle:=\det(\bm{J}_{\tau})\bm{J}_{\tau}^{-1}(v\circ\bm{F}_{\tau}),

(6.27c)

\displaystyle\psi_{\tau}^{3}(v)

\displaystyle:=\det(\bm{J}_{\tau})(v\circ\bm{F}_{\tau}).

The restricted Piola transformations (we keep the same notation for simplicity)

\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l}:{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\rightarrow{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})})
are isomorphisms. This follows from the fact that
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}
have the same connectivity arrays, that
\bm{F}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
maps any edge (face, tetrahedron) in
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}
to an edge (face, tetrahedron) of
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
with the same orientation, and that, for each tetrahedron
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
,
\psi_{\tau}^{l}
is an isomorphism that preserves appropriate moments
[
27
, Lemma 9.13 & Exercise 9.4]
.
Moreover, the Piola transformations satisfy the following bounds:

(6.28)

\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l}\|_{\mathcal{L}}:=\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\omega);L^{2}(\widehat{\omega}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}))}\leq C(\rho_{\sharp})(\overline{h}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}})^{l-\frac{3}{2}},

where
\overline{h}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
denotes the biggest diameter of a cell in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, and they satisfy the following commuting properties:

(6.29)

d^{l}(\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l}(v))=\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l+1}(d^{l}v)\qquad\forall v\in{\widetilde{V}}^{l}(\omega).

We use the shorthand notation
\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}
for the inverse of the Piola transformations.
We have

(6.30)

\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}\|_{\mathcal{L}}:=\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\widehat{\omega}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})});L^{2}(\omega))}\leq C(\rho_{\sharp})(\underline{h}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}})^{-l+\frac{3}{2}},

where
\underline{h}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
denotes the smallest diameter of a cell in
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
. The commuting property (
6.29
) readily gives

(6.31)

\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-(l+1)}(d^{l}\widehat{v})=d^{l}(\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}(\widehat{v}))\qquad\forall\widehat{v}\in{\widetilde{V}}^{l}(\widehat{\omega}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})}).

Lemma 6.9

(Discrete Poincaré inequalities invoking piecewise Piola transformations)
.

The discrete Poincaré inequalities (
6.7
) hold true for all
l\in\{1{:}2\}
with a constant
\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{\textup{d},l}
only depending on the shape-regularity parameter
\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
, the number of tetrahedra
|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|
, and the polynomial degree
p
.

Proof.

Let
u_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
and set
r_{\mathcal{T}}:=d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}
. As in (
3.2
) and (
6.8a
), we consider the following two (well-posed) constrained quadratic minimization problems:

(6.32)

u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}:=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})\\
d^{l}v_{\mathcal{T}}=r_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2},\qquad\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}:=\Argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widehat{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\in{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})})\\
d^{l}\widehat{v}_{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\end{subarray}}\|\widehat{v}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\omega}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})})}^{2},

with

(6.33)

\widehat{r}_{\mathcal{T}}:=\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l+1}(r_{\mathcal{T}}).

The Euler conditions for the second minimization problem imply that
\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\in{\mathfrak{Z}^{\perp}\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})})
. Owing to the discrete Poincaré inequality (
6.26
), we infer that

\|\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\omega}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})},p)\|\widehat{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\omega}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})})}.

Moreover, we observe that
\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}(\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*})\in{\widetilde{V}}_{p}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})
and,
owing to (
6.31
), the constraint in the second problem in (
6.32
), and (
6.33
), we have

d^{l}(\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}(\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}))=\psi^{-(l+1)}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}(d^{l}\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*})=\psi^{-(l+1)}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}(\widehat{r}_{\mathcal{T}})=r_{\mathcal{T}}.

Hence,
\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}(\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*})
is in the minimization set of the first problem in (
6.32
). This implies that

\displaystyle\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\|u_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}(\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*})\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\|\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\omega}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})},p)\|\widehat{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\omega}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l+1}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})},p)\|r_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l+1}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j(\mathcal{T}_{\omega})},p)\|d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

The bounds (
6.28
)–(
6.30
) on the operator norm of the Piola maps and their inverse together give

\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{-l}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\|\psi_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}^{l+1}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\leq C(\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}},|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|)h_{\omega}
,
where we used that
\overline{h}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}\leq h_{\omega}
and
\overline{h}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}/\underline{h}_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}\leq C(\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}},|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|)
. This implies that

\displaystyle\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(C(\rho_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}},|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|)\max_{j\in\{1{:}{\hat{N}}_{\sharp}\}}\widetilde{C}_{\textup{P}}^{l}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{j},p)\bigg)h_{\omega}\|d^{l}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

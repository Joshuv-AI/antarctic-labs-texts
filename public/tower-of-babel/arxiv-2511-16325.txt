ABC of
4D,\mathcal{N}=2
harmonic superspace

Throughout the text we will use the analytical parametrization of harmonic superspace,

Z^{A}=\left\{\zeta^{M},\theta^{-\alpha},\bar{\theta}^{-\dot{\alpha}},u^{\pm}_{i}\right\},

(1.1)

where
\zeta^{M}
denotes the analytic coordinates

\zeta^{M}=\left\{x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\theta^{+\alpha},\bar{\theta}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\right\}.

(1.2)

The harmonic coordinates
u^{\pm}_{i}
parametrize the internal 2-sphere
SU(2)/U(1)
and satisfy the condition
u^{+i}u^{-}_{i}=1
.

\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry is realized on the above coordinates as

\delta_{\epsilon}x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=-4i\left(\epsilon^{-\alpha}\bar{\theta}^{+\dot{\alpha}}+\theta^{+\alpha}\bar{\epsilon}^{-\dot{\alpha}}\right),\quad\delta_{\epsilon}\theta^{\pm\alpha}=\epsilon^{\pm\alpha},\quad\delta_{\epsilon}\bar{\theta}^{\pm\dot{\alpha}}=\bar{\epsilon}^{\pm\dot{\alpha}},\quad\delta_{\epsilon}u^{\pm}_{i}=0,

(1.3)

where
\epsilon^{\pm\hat{\alpha}}:=\epsilon^{\hat{\alpha}i}u^{\pm}_{i}
.
The analytic superspace

\mathbb{HA}^{4+2|4}=\{\zeta^{M},u^{\pm}\}

is closed under these transformations.

In the analytic basis, the harmonic derivatives read :

\begin{split}&\mathcal{D}^{++}=\partial^{++}-4i\theta^{+\rho}\bar{\theta}^{+\dot{\rho}}\partial_{\rho\dot{\rho}}+\theta^{+\hat{\rho}}\partial^{+}_{\hat{\rho}},\\
&\mathcal{D}^{--}=\partial^{--}-4i\theta^{-\rho}\bar{\theta}^{-\dot{\rho}}\partial_{\rho\dot{\rho}}+\theta^{-\hat{\rho}}\partial^{-}_{\hat{\rho}},\\
&\mathcal{D}^{0}=\partial^{0}+\theta^{+\hat{\rho}}\partial^{-}_{\hat{\rho}}-\theta^{-\hat{\rho}}\partial^{+}_{\hat{\rho}},\end{split}

(1.4)

where we used the notations

\partial^{++}=u^{+i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{-}_{i}},\qquad\partial^{--}=u^{-i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{+}_{i}},\qquad\partial^{0}=u^{+i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{+}_{i}}-u^{-i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{-}_{i}}

(1.5)

and
\hat{\alpha}:=(\alpha,\dot{\alpha})
. The harmonic derivatives satisfy the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
algebra relations
[\mathcal{D}^{++},\mathcal{D}^{--}]=\mathcal{D}^{0}
,
[\mathcal{D}^{0},\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm}]=\pm 2\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm}
.

The covariant spinor derivatives in the analytic basis are expressed as:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}=\partial^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}=(\partial^{+}_{{\alpha}},\;\bar{\partial}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}),\\
&\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha}=-\partial^{-}_{\alpha}+4i\bar{\theta}^{-\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\\
&\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}=-\bar{\partial}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}-4i\theta^{-\alpha}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\,,\end{split}

(1.6)

and satisfy the algebra:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{\hat{\alpha}}=[\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm},\mathcal{D}^{\mp}_{\hat{\alpha}}],\\
&[\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm},\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{\hat{\alpha}}]=0,\\
&\{\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\alpha},\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}\}=-\{\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha},\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\}=-4i\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}.\end{split}

(1.7)

The analytic harmonic superfields are subjected to the Grassmann analyticity constraints

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}\Phi(Z)=0\,.

(1.8)

In the analytic basis, due to the “shortness” of
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}

1.8

\Phi(Z)\,\Rightarrow\,\Phi(\zeta,u)\,.

2.1
Superconformal transformations of harmonic derivatives

Under superconformal transformations the harmonic derivatives (
1.4
) transform as

\begin{split}\delta_{sc}\mathcal{D}^{++}&=[\hat{\Lambda}_{sc},\mathcal{D}^{++}]=-\lambda_{sc}^{++}\mathcal{D}^{0},\\
\delta_{sc}\mathcal{D}^{--}&=[\hat{\Lambda}_{sc},\mathcal{D}^{++}]=-(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda_{sc}^{++})\mathcal{D}^{--},\\
\delta_{sc}\mathcal{D}^{0}&=[\hat{\Lambda}_{sc},\mathcal{D}^{0}]=0.\end{split}

(2.8)

They preserve the algebra
[\mathcal{D}^{++},\mathcal{D}^{--}]=\mathcal{D}^{0},\;[\mathcal{D}^{0},\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm}]=\pm 2\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm}
.

2.2
Superconformal transformations of spinor and vector derivatives

\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal transformations of covariant derivatives can be deduced as follows. First, the short analytic derivative has the transformation law

\delta_{sc}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}:=[\hat{\Lambda}_{sc},\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}]=-\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}\lambda_{sc}^{-\hat{\beta}}\right)\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\beta}}\,,\quad\rm{and\;c.c.}.

(2.9)

Since this derivative transforms homogeneously, the analyticity condition (
1.8
) is superconformally covariant.
Using this property and the first relation in (
1.7
), we obtain:

\begin{split}\delta_{sc}\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha}=&\;[\delta_{sc}\mathcal{D}^{--},\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\alpha}]+[\mathcal{D}^{--},\delta_{sc}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\alpha}]\\
=&-(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda_{sc}^{++})\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha}\lambda_{sc}^{++}\right)\mathcal{D}^{--}\\
=&-\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\alpha}\lambda_{sc}^{-\beta}\right)\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\beta}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha}\lambda_{sc}^{-\beta}\right)\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta}.\end{split}

(2.10)

Besides the homogeneous terms
\sim\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\alpha}\lambda^{-\beta}\right)
and
\sim(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda^{++})
,
this transformation law contains two inhomogeneous contributions.

Using the last line of (
1.7
) and the properties (
2.7
), we obtain for the superconformal variation of the vector derivative:

\begin{split}\delta_{sc}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=&-(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\alpha}\lambda_{sc}^{-\beta})\partial_{\beta\dot{\alpha}}-(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\lambda}_{sc}^{-\dot{\beta}})\partial_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}-(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda^{++}_{sc})\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\\
&-\frac{i}{4}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{sc}^{++}\right)\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha}+\frac{i}{4}\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha}\lambda_{sc}^{++}\right)\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}\\
&-\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{sc}^{-\beta}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta}-\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\lambda}_{sc}^{-\dot{\beta}}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\beta}}.\end{split}

(2.11)

This transformation law is more complicated as compared to that of spinor derivatives. Here the first line collects the homogeneous terms,
while the second and third lines feature inhomogeneous contributions proportional to the spinor derivatives.

We define
\mathcal{N}=2

chiral superfield

\Phi_{ch}
by the set of covariant conditions:

\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{\pm}_{\dot{\alpha}}\Phi_{ch}=0,\qquad\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm}\Phi_{ch}=0,\qquad\mathcal{D}^{0}\Phi_{ch}=0.

(2.12)

Since under the superconformal group the spinor derivative
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}
transforms through both
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}
and
\mathcal{D}^{--}

the standard Grassmann chirality conditions in (
2.12

\bullet

Full superspace

\begin{split}\delta_{sc}(d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du)=&\Big[\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\lambda^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}-\partial^{-}_{\hat{\alpha}}\lambda^{+\hat{\alpha}}-\partial^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}\lambda^{-\hat{\alpha}}+\underbrace{\frac{\partial(\lambda^{++}u^{-}_{i})}{\partial u^{+i}}}_{\partial^{--}\lambda^{++}}\Big](d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du)\\

(2.13)

As a consequence of the properties (

) and (
2.7e

satisfies

\mathcal{N}=2

3
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell action: superconformal invariance

Before passing to
\mathcal{N}=2
conformal supergravity, we will dwell on
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell theory as an instructive example of

\mathcal{N}=2
superconformally invariant theory. A few features of this theory admit a direct generalization to the conformal supergravity.

In harmonic superspace, the free
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell supermultiplet is described by the action

S_{\text{Max}}=\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du\;V^{++}V^{--},

(3.1)

where
V^{++}
is the analytic gauge prepotential which collects all degrees of freedom of the theory. The superfield
V^{--}

is defined as the solution of the harmonic zero-curvature condition:

\mathcal{D}^{++}V^{--}=\mathcal{D}^{--}V^{++}.

(3.2)

This equation uniquely specifies
V^{--}
in terms of
V^{++}
. Superfield
V^{--}
is not analytic.
The action and zero-curvature condition are invariant under the abelian gauge transformations:

\delta_{\lambda}V^{++}=\mathcal{D}^{++}\lambda,\qquad\delta_{\lambda}V^{--}=\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda\,,

(3.3)

with an arbitrary analytic parameter
\lambda(\zeta,u)
.

Under
\mathcal{N}=2

superconformal transformations
the analytic prepotential transforms as

\delta_{sc}V^{++}(\zeta,u)\simeq V^{\prime++}(\zeta^{\prime},u^{\prime})-V^{++}(\zeta,u)=0.

(3.4)

The transformation of
V^{--}
can be deduced from the requirement of superconformal covariance of the zero-curvature equation (
3.2
),

\delta_{sc}V^{--}=-(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda_{sc}^{++})V^{--}.

(3.5)

The superconformal invariance of
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell action is checked by performing the transformations (
2.14
),
(
3.4
) and (
3.5
) in (
3.1
):

\delta_{sc}S_{\text{Max}}=\int\underbrace{\delta_{sc}\left\{d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du\right\}}_{\left\{d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du\right\}\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda^{++}_{sc}}\;V^{++}V^{--}+\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du\;\underbrace{\delta_{sc}\left\{V^{++}V^{--}\right\}}_{-(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda_{sc}^{++})V^{++}V^{--}}=0.

(3.6)

Chiral representation

The action (
3.1
) is an integral over full
\mathcal{N}=2
harmonic superspace.
For what follows, it is instructive to rewrite this action as an integral over
\mathcal{N}=2
chiral superspace
and to study its superconformal properties in such an equivalent formulation.

As the first step, we pass to the chiral integration measure, using

d^{4}\bar{\theta}=\frac{1}{16}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-})^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}
and analyticity of
V^{++}
:

S_{\text{Max}}=\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du\;V^{++}V^{--}=\frac{1}{16}\int d^{4}xd^{4}\theta du\;(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-})^{2}\left\{V^{++}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}V^{--}\right\}.

(3.7)

Then we introduce
\mathcal{N}=2
covariant superstrengths

\mathcal{W}:=(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}V^{--},\qquad\overline{\mathcal{W}}:=(\mathcal{D}^{+})^{2}V^{--}\,,

(3.8)

which are manifestly gauge-invariant:

\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{W}=0,\qquad\delta_{\lambda}\overline{\mathcal{W}}=0.

(3.9)

Moreover, the superstrengths satisfy the covariant
harmonic-independence
conditions:

\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm}\mathcal{W}=0,\qquad\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm}\overline{\mathcal{W}}=0,

(3.10)

and, as a consequence of (
3.10

3.8

\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{\alpha}\overline{\mathcal{W}}=0,\qquad\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{\pm}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathcal{W}=0

(3.11)

and
Bianchi identities

\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}\right)^{2}\mathcal{W}=\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}\right)^{2}\overline{\mathcal{W}},\qquad\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}\mathcal{D}^{-}\right)\mathcal{W}=\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}\right)\overline{\mathcal{W}},\qquad\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}\right)^{2}\mathcal{W}=\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}\right)^{2}\overline{\mathcal{W}}.

(3.12)

One can easily rewrite the action (
3.1

\mathcal{W}
and
\overline{\mathcal{W}}

To this end, we need to pass to the central basis in harmonic superspace.
Then, using integrations by parts, the algebra of covariant derivatives (
1.7

3.10

\begin{split}S_{Max}=&\;\frac{1}{16}\int d^{4}xd^{4}\theta du\;(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-})^{2}\left\{V^{++}\mathcal{W}\right\}=\frac{1}{16}\int d^{4}xd^{4}\theta du\;\mathcal{D}^{--}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-})\left\{V^{++}\mathcal{W}\right\}\\

(3.13)

So, we have represented the
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell action as an integral over
\mathcal{N}=2
chiral superspace.

Alternatively, a chiral form of the Maxwell action can be achieved using the representation

\mathcal{W}=(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}V^{--}=\int du_{1}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}\frac{V^{++}(u_{1})}{(u^{+}u^{+}_{1})^{2}}=\int du(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-})^{2}V^{++},

(3.14)

which follows from the evident relations in the central basis:

\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}:=\mathcal{D}^{i}u^{+}_{i}=\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}(u_{1})(u^{+}_{1}u^{+})-\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}(u_{1})(u^{-}_{1}u^{+}),

(3.15a)

(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}V^{++}(u_{1})=(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}(u_{1}))^{2}V^{++}(u_{1})(u^{+}_{1}u^{+})^{2}.

(3.15b)

\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal transformations in chiral representation

Using the transformation laws (

\begin{split}\delta_{sc}\mathcal{W}=&-\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\lambda}_{sc}^{-\dot{\alpha}}\right)\mathcal{W}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda_{sc}^{++}\right)\mathcal{W}\\
&-\left[(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}\bar{\lambda}_{sc}^{-\dot{\alpha}}\right]\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}V^{--}-2\left[\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{sc}^{++}\right]\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}V^{--}.\end{split}

(3.16)

In the second line we highlighted the inhomogeneous terms.

(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}\bar{\lambda}_{sc}^{-\dot{\alpha}}=-2\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{sc}^{++}=2\bar{\partial}^{-\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{sc}^{++}=8i\theta^{+}_{\alpha}k^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}-8i\bar{\eta}^{\dot{\alpha}+}\,,

(3.17)

we see that inhomogeneous terms are canceled and

\delta_{sc}\mathcal{W}=-\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\lambda}_{sc}^{-\dot{\alpha}}\right)\mathcal{W}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda_{sc}^{++}\right)\mathcal{W}.

(3.18)

Thus the Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}_{ch}=\mathcal{W}^{2}
has the transformation law which cancels
the chiral weight factor (

measure

4
\mathcal{N}=2
conformal supergravity

\mathcal{N}=2
off-shell conformal supergravity multiplet has a concise geometric superfield formulation in harmonic superspace
Galperin:1987em
;
18
;
Ivanov:2022vwc
.
It naturally originates from the analytic localization of rigid superconformal transformations
\lambda^{M}_{sc}\to\lambda^{M}(\zeta,u)
,
\lambda^{++}_{sc}\to\lambda^{++}(\zeta,u)
,
\lambda^{-\hat{\alpha}}_{sc}\to\lambda^{-\hat{\alpha}}(\zeta,\theta^{-},u)
.
The covariance of harmonic derivative
\mathcal{D}^{++}
under the localized superconformal transformations is achieved by introducing the
supervielbeins:

\mathcal{D}^{++}\qquad\to\qquad\mathfrak{D}^{++}=\partial^{++}+\mathcal{H}^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+\mathcal{H}^{++\hat{\alpha}+}\partial^{-}_{\hat{\alpha}}+\mathcal{H}^{++\hat{\alpha}-}\partial^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}+\mathcal{H}^{(+4)}\partial^{--}.

(4.1)

All
\mathcal{H}^{++}
-prepotentials, apart from
\mathcal{H}^{++\hat{\alpha}-}
, are analytic and unconstrained otherwise.
Compared to the minimal version of Einstein’s supergravity
Galperin:1987ek
, the harmonic derivative contains an extra
\mathcal{H}^{(+4)}
vielbein.

We require that under localized superconformal transformations
\mathfrak{D}^{++}
transforms as in the rigid
{\cal N}=2
superconformal group,

\delta_{\lambda}\mathfrak{D}^{++}=-\lambda^{++}\mathcal{D}^{0}.

(4.2)

This transformation law implies the following transformation rules for prepotentials:

\begin{split}\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}&=\mathfrak{D}^{++}\lambda^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\\
\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{++\hat{\alpha}+}&=\mathfrak{D}^{++}\lambda^{+\hat{\alpha}}-\lambda^{++}\theta^{+\hat{\alpha}},\\
\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{++\hat{\alpha}-}&=\mathfrak{D}^{++}\lambda^{-\hat{\alpha}}+\lambda^{++}\theta^{-\hat{\alpha}},\\
\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{(+4)}&=\mathfrak{D}^{++}\lambda^{++}.\end{split}

(4.3)

All parameters are analytic, with the exception of
\lambda^{-\hat{\alpha}}
.
By making use of this non-analytic parameter, one can fix the
analytic gauge

\mathcal{H}^{++\hat{\alpha}-}=\theta^{+\hat{\alpha}}
.
This means that the superfield
\mathcal{H}^{++\hat{\alpha}-}
does not involve the physical degrees of freedom which are carried solely by the remaining analytic supervielbeins.
In the analytic gauge, the harmonic derivative
\mathfrak{D}^{++}
satisfies the analyticity condition

[\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}},\mathfrak{D}_{a.g.}^{++}]=0
.

For the flat
\mathcal{N}=2
super-background we have
\mathfrak{D}^{++}_{flat}=\mathcal{D}^{++}
, and the supervielbeins are reduced to the expressions:

\mathcal{H}^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}_{flat}=-4i\theta^{+\alpha}\bar{\theta}^{+\dot{\alpha}},\qquad\mathcal{H}_{flat}^{++\hat{\alpha}+}=0,\qquad\mathcal{H}_{flat}^{++\hat{\alpha}-}=\theta^{+\hat{\alpha}},\qquad\mathcal{H}_{flat}^{(+4)}=0.

(4.4)

We denote the deviations of the prepotentials from their flat background values as

h^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\qquad h^{++\hat{\alpha}+},\qquad h^{++\hat{\alpha}-},\qquad h^{(+4)}.

(4.5)

From eq. (
4.3
) one can determine the linearized gauge transformation laws for these superfields, as well as
their rigid
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal transformations. We will present them later in the form most convenient for our purposes.

Using the linearized gauge transformations, it is possible to impose the Wess-Zumino (WZ) type gauge:

\begin{split}h_{WZ}^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}&=-4i\theta^{+\beta}\bar{\theta}^{+\dot{\beta}}\Phi^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}+(\bar{\theta}^{+})^{2}\theta^{+\beta}\psi^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}i}_{\beta}u^{-}_{i}+(\theta^{+})^{2}\bar{\theta}^{+\dot{\rho}}\bar{\psi}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}i}_{\dot{\rho}}u_{i}^{-}+(\theta^{+})^{4}V^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}(ij)}u^{-}_{i}u^{-}_{j}\,,\\
h_{WZ}^{++\alpha+}&=(\theta^{+})^{2}\bar{\theta}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}P^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+\left(\bar{\theta}^{+}\right)^{2}\theta^{+}_{\beta}T^{(\alpha\beta)}+(\theta^{+})^{4}\chi^{\alpha i}u^{-}_{i},\;\;\;\\
h_{WZ}^{++\dot{\alpha}+}&=\widetilde{h_{WZ}^{++\alpha+}}\,,\\
h_{WZ}^{(+4)}\;\;\,&=(\theta^{+})^{4}D\,.\end{split}

(4.6)

This gauge is defined up to a residual gauge freedom, which is properly realized on the component fields. For instance,

\delta\Phi_{\beta\dot{\beta}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\partial_{\beta\dot{\beta}}a^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+l_{(\alpha}^{\;\;\beta)}\delta_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\dot{\beta}}+\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}\bar{l}_{(\dot{\alpha}}^{\;\;\dot{\beta})}+d\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}\delta_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\dot{\beta}}
, where the gauge parameters
correspond to local translations (
a^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
), local Lorentz transformations (
l_{(\alpha\beta)},\bar{l}_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}
) and local scale transformations (
d
).
The set of fields in WZ gauge together with their residual gauge freedom reproduce the content of
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl multiplet.
For further details see, e.g.,
Buchbinder:2024pjm
;
Buchbinder:2025ceg
. It is worth emphasizing that the form of WZ-type gauge remains unchanged even in the complete nonlinear case.

The basic aim of this section is to construct
linearized
\mathcal{N}=2
conformal supergravity action

in terms of
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl supermultiplet analytic prepotentials and to study its transformation properties with respect to rigid superconformal symmetry.

4.1
Covariant superfields

To deal with the linearized conformal supergravity, it will be useful to pass to the superfields exhibiting manifest invariance under supersymmetry.
The covariant harmonic derivative (
4.1
) is invariant under rigid
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry.
Then, following Zupnik
Zupnik:1998td
;
Kuzenko:1999pi
, we decompose the harmonic derivative
\mathfrak{D}^{++}
in the basis of supersymmetry-invariant covariant derivatives:

\mathfrak{D}^{++}=\mathcal{D}^{++}+G^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+G^{++\hat{\alpha}-}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}+G^{++\hat{\alpha}+}\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\hat{\alpha}}+G^{(+4)}\mathcal{D}^{--}.

(4.7)

Here
G^{++}
-vielbeins are related to the analytic
h^{++}
-vielbeins by relations:

\begin{split}&G^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=h^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+4ih^{++\alpha+}\bar{\theta}^{-\dot{\alpha}}+4i\theta^{-\alpha}h^{++\dot{\alpha}+}-4ih^{(+4)}\theta^{-\alpha}\bar{\theta}^{-\dot{\alpha}},\\
&G^{++\hat{\alpha}-}=h^{++\hat{\alpha}-},\\
&G^{++\hat{\alpha}+}=-h^{++\hat{\alpha}+}+h^{(+4)}\theta^{-\hat{\alpha}},\\
&G^{(+4)}\;\,\,=h^{(+4)}.\end{split}

(4.8)

The main feature of
G^{++}
-superfields is their manifest invariance under supersymmetry.
These superfields (except for
G^{(+4)}
) are non-analytic, but, as a consequence of the analyticity of the
h^{++}
-vielbeins,
satisfy the constraints:

\begin{split}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\beta}}G^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=4iG^{++\alpha+}\delta^{\dot{\alpha}}_{\dot{\beta}},\qquad&\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta}G^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=-4iG^{++\dot{\alpha}+}\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta},\\
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\beta}}G^{++\alpha+}=0,\qquad&\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta}G^{++\alpha+}=G^{(+4)}\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta},\\
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\beta}}G^{++\dot{\alpha}+}=G^{(+4)}\delta_{\dot{\beta}}^{\dot{\alpha}},\qquad&\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta}G^{++\dot{\alpha}+}=0,\\
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\beta}}G^{(+4)}=0,\qquad&\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta}G^{(+4)}=0.\end{split}

(4.9)

This set of constraints can be considered as an alternative definition of the
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl multiplet prepotentials.

The linearized gauge transformations of
G^{++}
-superfields read

\begin{split}&\delta_{\lambda}G^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\mathcal{D}^{++}\Lambda^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\\
&\delta_{\lambda}G^{++\hat{\alpha}-}=\mathcal{D}^{++}\Lambda^{-\hat{\alpha}}+\Lambda^{+\hat{\alpha}},\\
&\delta_{\lambda}G^{++\hat{\alpha}+}=\mathcal{D}^{++}\Lambda^{+\hat{\alpha}},\\
&\delta_{\lambda}G^{(+4)}=\mathcal{D}^{++}\Lambda^{++},\end{split}

(4.10)

where the gauge parameters are defined through the expansion of the first-rank superdifferential operator
\hat{\Lambda}
(see eq. (
2.2
))
over covariant derivatives:

\begin{split}&\Lambda^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\,=\lambda^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+4i\lambda^{\alpha+}\bar{\theta}^{-\dot{\alpha}}+4i\theta^{-\alpha}\lambda^{+\dot{\alpha}}-4i\lambda^{++}\theta^{-\alpha}\bar{\theta}^{-\dot{\alpha}},\\
&\Lambda^{-\hat{\alpha}}\;\;\,=\lambda^{-\hat{\alpha}},\\
&\Lambda^{+\hat{\alpha}}=-\lambda^{+\hat{\alpha}}+\lambda^{++}\theta^{-\hat{\alpha}},\\
&\Lambda^{++}=\lambda^{++}.\end{split}

(4.11)

Note that these gauge transformations may also be equivalently rewritten, by making use of the definitions (
4.8
),
in terms of the analytic
h^{++}
- prepotentials. Nevertheless, in what follows it will be more convenient to work with the
G^{++}
-superfields.

Like in
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell theory, by defining the appropriate zero-curvature equations, one can construct the superfields with negative harmonic charges:

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}^{++}G^{--\alpha\dot{\alpha}}&=\mathcal{D}^{--}G^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\\
\mathcal{D}^{++}G^{--\hat{\alpha}+}&=\mathcal{D}^{--}G^{++\hat{\alpha}+},\\
\mathcal{D}^{++}G^{--\hat{\alpha}-}&=G^{--\hat{\alpha}+}.\end{split}

(4.12)

The negatively charged gauge potentials are uniquely specified by (
4.12
)
in terms of the superfields (
4.8
). This can be directly checked, e.g., in WZ gauge.

4.2
Gauge-invariant action and
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl supertensor

Following ref.
Ivanov:2024gjo
,
let us consider the complex dimensionless superfield:

\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=\partial_{(\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}G^{++}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}-\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}G^{+++}_{\beta)},

(4.13)

which satisfies the “half-analyticity”
Ivanov:2024gjo
condition:

\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\rho}}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=0.

(4.14)

This condition determines the relative coefficient in (
4.13
).
The superfield
\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}
has a simple gauge transformation law:

\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=\mathcal{D}^{++}\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}+\underbrace{\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}\Lambda^{+}_{\beta)}}_{0},\qquad\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}=\partial_{(\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}\Lambda_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}-\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\Lambda^{+}_{\beta)},

(4.15)

where the composite gauge parameter
\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}
satisfies the half-analyticity condition
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\rho}}\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}=0
.

Using the zero-curvature equation

\mathcal{D}^{++}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}=\mathcal{D}^{--}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}\,,

(4.16)

we define a superfield with the negative charge,

\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}=\partial_{(\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}G^{--}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}-\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}G^{--+}_{\beta)}+\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}G^{---}_{\beta)},

(4.17)

subject to the gauge transformation law
\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}=\mathcal{D}^{--}\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}
.

Using these superfields one can construct the action:

S_{Weyl}=\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du\;\mathcal{H}^{++(\alpha\beta)}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}+{\rm c.c.}\;.

(4.18)

Gauge invariance of this action can be easily checked:

\begin{split}\delta_{\lambda}S_{Weyl}=&\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du\;\left\{\mathcal{D}^{++}\Lambda^{(\alpha\beta)}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}+\mathcal{H}^{++(\alpha\beta)}\mathcal{D}^{--}\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}\right\}\\
=&\;2\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du\;\mathcal{H}^{++(\alpha\beta)}\mathcal{D}^{--}\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}\\
=&\;\frac{1}{8}\int d^{4}xd^{4}\theta du\;\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}\right)^{2}\Big\{\mathcal{H}^{++(\alpha\beta)}\underbrace{\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}\right)^{2}\left[\mathcal{D}^{--}\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}\right]}_{0}\Big\}=0\end{split}

(4.19)

(the same check can be performed for the complex-conjugated part of the action (
4.18
)).
Thus the invariance of the action is ensured by the half-analyticity condition. This is analogous to what happens in
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell theory, where
the invariance is secured due to the analyticity condition.

Chiral representation

Analogously to the Maxwell theory, the action (
4.18

Using
\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}
one can construct the gauge-invariant supertensor

\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}=(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}.

(4.20)

Its gauge invariance is a consequence of the half-analyticity condition. In components, this supertensor contains the linearized Weyl tensor

\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}\sim\theta^{+\gamma}\theta^{-\delta}C_{(\alpha\beta\gamma\delta)}+\dots,\qquad C_{(\alpha\beta\gamma\delta)}=\partial_{(\alpha}^{\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\beta}^{\dot{\beta}}\Phi_{\gamma\delta)(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}.

(4.21)

Therefore we can identify
\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}
with the linearized
\mathcal{N}=2
super Weyl tensor (its holomorphic part).
The relation between this supertensor and the super Weyl tensor in the standard superspace
\mathbb{R}^{4+2|8}
will be established
in the Appendix
B
, after imposing a harmonic-independent gauge.

As another consequence of the half-analyticity,
\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}
is
\mathcal{N}=2
chiral superfield.
Moreover,
\mathcal{N}=2
super Weyl tensor satisfies Bianchi identity:

(\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{-\beta})\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}=(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\beta}})\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}

(4.22)

proved in Appendix
B.2
.

By performing a chain of transformations completely analogous to those in eqs. (
3.7
) and (
3.13
),
and using the half-analyticity property (or, alternatively, the identity (
3.14
)), it is easy to find the chiral representation for the action (
4.18

S_{Weyl}=\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du\;\mathcal{H}^{++(\alpha\beta)}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}+{\rm c.c.}=\frac{1}{16}\int d^{4}x_{L}d^{4}\theta\,\mathcal{W}^{(\alpha\beta)}\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}+{\rm c.c.}\,.

(4.23)

In components, this action contains the square of the linearized Weyl tensor, so it indeed describes the linearized
\mathcal{N}=2
conformal supergravity.
The action (
4.23

can be carried out on an arbitrary conformally flat background, as demonstrated in

within the
\mathcal{N}=2
conformal superspace approach

Constructing such a generalization in harmonic superspace remains an open issue.

To close this subsection, we highlight the remarkable analogy between the spin

V^{\pm\pm}\;\;\leftrightarrow\;\;\mathcal{H}^{\pm\pm}_{(\alpha\beta)},\qquad\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\rho}}V^{\pm\pm}=0\;\;\leftrightarrow\;\;\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\rho}}\mathcal{H}^{\pm\pm}_{(\alpha\beta)}=0,\qquad\mathcal{W}\;\;\leftrightarrow\;\;\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}.

(4.24)

This analogy may serve as a useful hint for constructing the complete non-linear

Some speculations on this theme are included in the final section.

\begin{split}&\delta_{sc}G^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta}\lambda^{-\alpha})G^{++\beta\dot{\alpha}}+(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\beta}}\bar{\lambda}^{-\dot{\alpha}})G^{++\alpha\dot{\beta}}+(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda^{++})G^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\\
&\delta_{sc}G^{++\alpha+}=\frac{i}{4}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\beta}}\lambda^{++})G^{++\alpha\dot{\beta}}+G^{++\beta+}(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta}\lambda^{-\alpha})+(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda^{++})G^{++\alpha+},\\
&\delta_{sc}G^{++\alpha-}=(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta}\lambda^{-\alpha})G^{++\beta-}-(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\beta}\lambda^{-\alpha})G^{++\beta+}+G^{++\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\lambda^{-\alpha},\\
&\delta_{sc}G^{(+4)}=(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda^{++})G^{(+4)}+G^{++\hat{\beta}+}(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\hat{\beta}}\lambda^{++}).\end{split}

(4.25)

Using the relations (
4.8
), from eqs.(
4.25
) one can derive the superconformal transformation laws of
h^{++}
-superfields as well.
However, in what follows the transformation laws
\delta_{sc}h^{++\dots}
will not be needed.

The transformation of
G^{++\hat{\alpha}-}
is inhomogeneous, which implies that in order to preserve the analytic
gauge
G^{++\hat{\alpha}-}_{a.g.}=0
one needs to compensate the superconformal transformations by the appropriate
gauge transformations with a field-dependent parameter
\lambda^{-\hat{\alpha}}(G)
. So,
in the analytic gauge the superconformal transformations of
G^{++}
-superfields become non-linear.
However, since
G^{++\hat{\alpha}-}
is not present in the dynamical action (
4.23
),
in what follows we may not fix the analytic gauge and deal with the analytic supervielbeins solely.
This is justified because the superfield
G^{++\dot{\alpha}-}
does not enter the superconformal transformation laws of the latter.

Using the transformations given above, we can start analysis of the superconformal properties of the theory defined by the action (
4.23
).
It is convenient to consider the superconformal transformation laws of the basic object
\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}
in various sectors.

\lambda^{-}
sector

\begin{split}\delta^{(1)}_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=&-(\mathcal{D}^{+\rho}\lambda^{-}_{(\alpha})\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\rho\beta)}-(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{-\rho})\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\beta)\rho}\\
&-\left(\mathcal{D}^{+\rho}\partial_{(\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}\lambda^{-}_{\beta)}\right)G^{++}_{\rho\dot{\beta}}+4i\partial_{(\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}\lambda^{-}_{\beta)}G^{+++}_{\dot{\beta}}\\
&+\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{+\rho}\lambda^{-}_{\beta)}\right)G^{+++}_{\rho}-\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{-}_{\beta)}G^{(+4)}.\end{split}

(4.26)

Due to the relations (
2.7c
) and (
2.7d
), the inhomogeneous terms from the second and third lines do not contribute, which significantly
simplifies the transformation law:

\delta^{(1)}_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=-(\mathcal{D}^{+\rho}\lambda^{-}_{(\alpha})\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\rho\beta)}-(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{-\rho})\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\beta)\rho}.

(4.27)

Furthermore, expanding

\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\alpha}\lambda^{-\rho}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\sigma}\lambda^{-\sigma})\delta_{\alpha}^{\rho}+(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{-\rho)}),

(4.28)

we observe that the weight factor
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\sigma}\lambda^{-\sigma}
drops out from the transformation law, leading to the final form of the latter:

\delta^{(1)}_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=-(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{-\rho)})\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\rho\beta)}-(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\beta}\lambda^{-\rho)})\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\rho)}.

(4.29)

\bar{\lambda}^{-}
sector

\delta^{(2)}_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=+4i\left(\partial_{(\alpha\dot{\beta}}\bar{\lambda}^{-\dot{\beta}}\right)G^{+++}_{\beta)}+\underbrace{\left(\partial_{(\alpha\dot{\beta}}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\rho}}\bar{\lambda}^{-\dot{\beta}}\right)}_{\frac{i}{2}\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\rho}}\lambda^{++}}G^{++\dot{\rho}}_{\beta)}-\frac{i}{4}\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\beta}}\lambda^{++}\right)G^{++\dot{\beta}}_{\beta)}.

(4.30)

Here we made use of the identity (
2.7g
). To cancel out the inhomogeneous terms, one needs to consider the superconformal transformation in the
\lambda^{++}
sector.

\lambda^{++}
sector

\begin{split}\delta^{(3)}_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=&-\frac{i}{2}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\beta}}\lambda^{++})\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}G^{++}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}+\frac{i}{4}(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{++})\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\beta}}G^{++}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}\\
&+\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{++}\right)\mathcal{D}^{--}G^{+++}_{\beta)}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{--}\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{++}\right)G^{+++}_{\beta)}\\
&-\frac{i}{4}\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\beta}}\lambda^{++}\right)G^{++\dot{\beta}}_{\beta)}.\end{split}

(4.31)

First of all, we see that the last term exactly cancels two last terms in (
4.30
).
Now we need to elaborate on other terms.

1) Using eq. (
4.9
), the zero-curvature equation and the analyticity properties, we obtain:

(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\beta}}\lambda^{++})\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}G^{++}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}=(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\beta}}\lambda^{++})\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{--}G^{++}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}=\mathcal{D}^{++}\left\{(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\beta}}\lambda^{++})\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}G^{--}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}\right\}

(4.32)

so the first term can be compensated by the field-dependent gauge transformations (
4.15
).

2) Similarly, one can rearrange the second term as

\begin{split}(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{++})\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\beta}}G^{++}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}=&\;(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{++})\mathcal{D}^{--}\underbrace{\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\beta}}G^{++}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}}_{-8iG^{+++}_{\beta}}-(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{++})\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\beta}}\underbrace{\mathcal{D}^{--}G^{++}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}}_{\mathcal{D}^{++}G^{--}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}}\\
=&\;\mathcal{D}^{++}\left\{(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{++})\left(-8iG^{+--}_{\beta)}-\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\beta}}G^{--}_{\beta)\dot{\beta}}\right)\right\}.\end{split}

(4.33)

3) The third term can be rewritten as

\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{++}\right)\mathcal{D}^{--}G^{+++}_{\beta)}=\mathcal{D}^{--}\left\{(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{++})G^{+++}_{\beta)}\right\}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{--}\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\lambda^{++}\right)G^{+++}_{\beta)}.

(4.34)

The first term in the r.h.s can be represented as
\mathcal{D}^{++}\{\dots\}
, using the zero-curvature equations and the uniqueness
of their solution. The second term explicitly equals the fourth term in (
4.31
) and, due to eq. (
2.7f
),
sum of these terms cancels the first term in (
4.30
).

As the result, restoring the subscript “
sc
”, we finally obtain

\boxed{\delta_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=-\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}\lambda_{sc}^{-\rho)}\right)\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\rho\beta)}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\beta}\lambda_{sc}^{-\rho)}\right)\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\rho)}+\mathcal{D}^{++}\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}^{(sc)},}

(4.35)

where
\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}^{(sc)}
is a field-dependent gauge parameter.
An important property of the superfield
\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}
is its harmonic half-analyticity.
Since
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\rho}}\lambda^{-\alpha}_{sc}=0
, the first two terms in (
4.35
) obey the half-analyticity condition.
However, this property is not obvious for the gauge-transformation term. To show that it also respects this property, we consider:

0=\delta_{sc}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}\right)=-(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{sc}^{-\dot{\beta}})\underbrace{\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\beta}}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}}_{0}+\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\left(\delta_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}\right)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\left(\delta_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}\right)=0.

(4.36)

Thus the superconformal variation of
\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}
is also half-analytic.
This property, together with eq. (
4.35
), lead to the conclusion that the parameter
\Lambda^{(sc)}_{(\alpha\beta)}
indeed satisfies the half-analyticity condition:

\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\Lambda^{(sc)}_{(\alpha\beta)}=0.

(4.37)

This means that the term
\mathcal{D}^{++}\Lambda_{(\alpha\beta)}^{(sc)}
in the transformation law (
4.35
) has the form of half-analytic gauge transformations.
Since we had already proved that the Weyl action
(
4.23
) is gauge-invariant, it is legal not to consider such a term.

The superconformal variation of the zero-curvature equation (
4.16
) leads to the following equation for the superconformal variation
\delta_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}
:

\mathcal{D}^{++}\delta_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}+2\lambda^{++}_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}=-\left(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda^{++}_{sc}\right)\mathcal{D}^{--}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}+\mathcal{D}^{--}\delta_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}.

(4.38)

As its unique solution we obtain:

\boxed{\delta_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}=-\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}\lambda_{sc}^{-\rho)}\right)\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\rho\beta)}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\beta}\lambda_{sc}^{-\rho)}\right)\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\rho)}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda_{sc}^{++}\right)\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}.}

(4.39)

The resulting formula for
\delta_{sc}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}
reminds the structure of transformations

\delta_{sc}V^{++}
in
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell’s theory, with additional Lorentz rotation-type terms for each spinor index.
The final term is a weight factor which compensates the non-invariance of the harmonic superspace measure (
2.14
).
We expect that the same mechanism remains applicable to the complete non-linear
\mathcal{N}=2
conformal supergravity action in harmonic superspace,
with the rigid superconformal transformations being promoted to the local ones.

The Lagrange density
\mathcal{H}^{++(\alpha\beta)}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}
is a Lorentz scalar and it is transformed just like the
Lagrange density
V^{++}V^{--}
in
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell theory. This immediately implies the superconformal invariance
of the linearized
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl theory action (
4.23
).

Analogously to the calculation of superconformal transformation of the superstrength
\mathcal{W}
in
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell theory, for the superconformal variation
of
\mathcal{N}=2
super-Weyl tensor (
4.20
) we obtain:

\begin{split}\delta_{sc}\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}=&-\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}\lambda_{sc}^{-\rho)}\right)\mathcal{W}_{(\rho\beta)}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\beta}\lambda_{sc}^{-\rho)}\right)\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\rho)}\\
&-\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\beta}}\bar{\lambda}_{sc}^{-\dot{\beta}}\right)\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}-\left(\mathcal{D}^{--}\lambda_{sc}^{++}\right)\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}.\end{split}

(4.40)

The second line is fully analogous to (
3.18
) for the spin
\mathbf{1}
case.
The first line appeared due to non-trivial Lorentz properties of
\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}
caused by the presence of spinor indices.
These terms are canceled in the Lagrangian density
\mathcal{W}^{(\alpha\beta)}\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}
. The remaining terms
cancel the chiral weight factor (
2.21
), thus providing the superconformal invariance of the chiral
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl action (
4.23
).

5
Conclusions

In this work we have constructed a harmonic gauge-invariant linearized action for
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl multiplet, proceeding from
the description of the latter through analytic vielbeins of the harmonic derivative
\mathfrak{D}^{++}
.
The action has a simple structure basically repeating the structure of
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell theory action,
the role of the analyticity condition being played by the “half-analyticity” condition. We also derived a chiral representation for this action.
Moreover, using the realization of rigid superconformal transformations in harmonic superspace, we have shown that the actions constructed
are invariant under rigid
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal transformations.

These results offer a few possible generalizations.

\bullet

\mathcal{N}=2
higher-spin superconformal theory in harmonic superspace

\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl multiplet is the simplest superconformal higher-spin multiplet
Buchbinder:2024pjm
.
The natural task is to generalize the spin 2
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl theory to an arbitrary integer
\mathcal{N}=2
higher spin.
The main difficulty of such a generalization is that the relevant multiplets are described by a larger set of superfields,
which are necessary for the linear realization of rigid superconformal transformations.

There is a gauge in which, for arbitrary superconformal spins
\mathbf{s}
, there remain only few analytic prepotentials, as it was shown in
Buchbinder:2024pjm
:

h^{++\alpha(s-1)\dot{\alpha}(s-1)},\quad h^{++\alpha(s-1)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)},\quad h^{++\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-1)},\quad h^{++\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}.

(5.1)

In this gauge one can easily construct (as in Ref.
Zaigraev:2024ryg
) the higher-spin counterpart
\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\alpha(2s-2)}
of
\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}
,
the higher-spin Weyl supertensor
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(2s-2)}
and, using these objects, set up a gauge-invariant action. However,
the analysis of superconformal invariance of this action will require a separate study.

\bullet

Towards nonlinear
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl theory in harmonic superspace

The action for
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory was previously proposed by Zupnik
Zupnik:1987vm
and it has a very interesting and simple structure:

S_{\text{SYM}}^{\mathcal{N}=2}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-i)^{n}}{n}\text{Tr}\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du_{1}\dots du_{n}\;\frac{V^{++}(x,\theta,u_{1})\dots V^{++}(x,\theta,u_{n})}{(u^{+}_{1}u^{+}_{2})\dots(u^{+}_{n}u^{+}_{1})}.

(5.2)

This action is invariant with respect to non-Abelian transformations:

\delta_{\lambda}V^{++}=\mathcal{D}^{++}\lambda+i[V^{++},\lambda].

(5.3)

Here the analytic superfields
V^{++}
and
\lambda
are in the adjoint representation of the Lie algebra.

An analogy between
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric Maxwell theory and the linearized
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl theory (see eq. (
4.24
))
leads to the guess on the structure of nonlinear
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl action (at least, of its essential part) in harmonic superspace:

S_{\text{Weyl}}^{\mathcal{N}=2}\overset{?}{=}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\text{Tr}\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du_{1}\dots du_{n}\;\frac{\mathcal{H}^{++\alpha_{2}}_{\;\;\;\;\alpha_{1}}(x,\theta,u_{1})\mathcal{H}_{\;\;\;\;\alpha_{2}}^{++\alpha_{3}}(x,\theta,u_{1})\dots\mathcal{H}^{++\alpha_{1}}_{\;\;\;\;\alpha_{n}}(x,\theta,u_{n})}{(u^{+}_{1}u^{+}_{2})\dots(u^{+}_{n}u^{+}_{1})}+\text{c.c}.

(5.4)

To properly investigate this possibility, one must first modify the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}
given in (
4.13
)
by incorporating the derivatives that are covariant with respect to the full conformal supergravity gauge group. One may assume that, after proper covariantization,
the full non-linear transformation law takes the form:

\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{++\beta)}_{\;\;\;(\alpha}\overset{?}{=}\mathfrak{D}^{++}\Lambda_{(\alpha}^{\;\;\beta)}+\mathcal{H}^{++\gamma)}_{\;\;\;(\alpha}\Lambda_{(\gamma}^{\;\;\beta)}-\Lambda^{\;\,\gamma)}_{(\alpha}\mathcal{H}^{++\beta)}_{\;\;\;(\gamma}.

(5.5)

Then the invariance of the action will be ensured in analogy with the theory (
5.2
), except for terms which are required to compensate the nontrivial transformation
of the harmonic superspace flat measure. Such additional terms should be present because the conformal supergravity gauge transformations also nontrivially act
on the coordinates
6
6

6

The scalar factor in the full invariant measure is expected to be properly expressed through
the original analytic gauge prepotentials.
.

It would be interesting to check this hypothesis at cubic order, since this may provide a useful guidance for constructing non-Abelian
cubic vertices
Berends:1984wp
;
Fradkin:1987ks
;
Zinoviev:2008ck
in
\mathcal{N}=2
higher-spin theory.

\bullet

\mathcal{N}=2
AdS supergravity

The transformation laws of
\mathcal{N}=2
conformal supergravity superfields (
4.25
)
with respect to the rigid superconformal transformations fully specify their transformation laws with respect
to
\mathcal{N}=2
AdS supersymmetry
\mathfrak{osp}(2|4)\subset\mathfrak{su}(2,2|2)
.
This property, combined with the ideas of a recent work
Ivanov:2025jdp
, can be used to construct

\mathcal{N}=2
AdS supergravity in harmonic superspace.

Appendix A
Superconformal transformations in different bases

In this article we used the analytic basis of
{\cal N}=2
HSS as the preferred one. In particular, the superconformal transformations
were given in this basis, see eq. (
2.6
). The goal of this Appendix is to provide
the precise relations between coordinates in diverse bases and the realizations of
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal group on them.

We use the following notations for coordinates in different bases:

\begin{split}\text{Analytic basis:}&\qquad\left\{x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\theta^{\pm}_{\alpha},\bar{\theta}^{\pm}_{\dot{\alpha}},u_{i}^{\pm}\right\},\\
\text{Central basis:}&\qquad\left\{x_{C}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\theta^{i}_{\alpha},\bar{\theta}^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}},u^{\pm}_{i}\right\},\\
\text{Chiral basis:}&\qquad\left\{x_{L}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\theta^{i}_{\alpha},\bar{\theta}^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}},u^{\pm}_{i}\right\},\\
\text{Anti-Chiral basis:}&\qquad\left\{x_{R}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\theta^{i}_{\alpha},\bar{\theta}^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}},u^{\pm}_{i}\right\}.\end{split}

(A.1)

The analytic coordinates are defined in terms of the central basis coordinates as

\begin{split}&x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=x_{C}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}-4i\theta^{\alpha(i}\bar{\theta}^{\dot{\alpha}j)}u^{+}_{i}u^{-}_{j},\\
&\theta_{\alpha}^{\pm}=\theta^{i}_{\alpha}u^{\pm}_{i},\qquad\bar{\theta}^{\pm}_{\dot{\alpha}}=\bar{\theta}^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}}u^{\pm}_{i}.\end{split}

(A.2)

The inverse expressions for the central basis coordinates in terms of the analytic ones read:

\begin{split}&x_{C}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+2i\theta^{+\alpha}\bar{\theta}^{-\dot{\alpha}}+2i\theta^{-\alpha}\bar{\theta}^{+\dot{\alpha}},\\
&\theta_{\alpha i}=\theta^{-}_{\alpha}u^{+}_{i}-\theta^{+}_{\alpha}u^{-}_{i},\qquad\bar{\theta}_{\dot{\alpha}i}=\bar{\theta}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}u^{+}_{i}-\bar{\theta}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}u^{-}_{i}.\end{split}

(A.3)

The left and right chiral bases are defined in terms of the central basis coordinates as

x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}_{L}=x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}_{C}+2i\theta^{\alpha}_{i}\bar{\theta}^{\dot{\alpha}i},\qquad x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}_{R}=x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}_{C}-2i\theta^{\alpha}_{i}\bar{\theta}^{\dot{\alpha}i}

(A.4)

and, in terms of analytic coordinates, as

\begin{split}&x_{L}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+4i\theta^{-\alpha}\bar{\theta}^{+\dot{\alpha}},\qquad x_{R}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+4i\theta^{+\alpha}\bar{\theta}^{-\dot{\alpha}}.\end{split}

(A.5)

Being aware of the realization of
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal group in the analytic superspace (
2.6
)
and of the transition formulas given above, one can find how this supergroup acts on the coordinates of the central basis

\mathcal{N}=2
anti-chiral superspace.

In contrast to chiral superspaces, the real superspace
\mathbb{R}^{4|8}
can be extended by harmonic coordinates endowed with the transformation law (
A.8
).

Somewhat unexpectedly, there exist additional essentially complex superspaces which contain 3/4 Grassmann coordinates together with harmonic variables
and are closed under the superconformal group:

•

\mathbb{HHA}^{4+2|6}=\left\{x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\theta^{\pm}_{\alpha},\bar{\theta}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}},u_{i}^{\pm}\right\}=\left\{(\zeta^{M},u_{i}^{\pm}),\theta^{-}_{\alpha}\right\}
—
\mathcal{N}=2

anti-half-analytic harmonic superspace;

•

\overline{\mathbb{HHA}}^{\,4+2|6}=\left\{x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\theta^{+}_{\alpha},\bar{\theta}^{\pm}_{\dot{\alpha}},u^{\pm}_{i}\right\}=\left\{(\zeta^{M},u_{i}^{\pm}),\bar{\theta}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}\right\}
—

\mathcal{N}=2
half-analytic harmonic superspace.

Superfields living on these superspaces solve the half-analyticity condition (
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\Phi=0
see eq.
(
4.14
)
) or
the anti-half-analyticity condition (
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\alpha}\Phi=0
), respectively. These are precisely those conditions which are satisfied by
the superfields
\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}
and its conjugate appearing in the expressions for the linearized
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl supertensor.
Looking at the coordinate realization of
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal group in the analytic basis (
2.5
), we observe that
\mathbb{HHA}^{4+2|6}
and

\overline{\mathbb{HHA}}^{\,4+2|6}
are closed under this realization. In particular, each of the extra coordinates
\theta^{-}_{\alpha}
and
\bar{\theta}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}
is transformed through itself
and the coordinates of the analytic subspace
\mathbb{HA}^{4+2|4}
. These half-analytic
\mathcal{N}=2
harmonic superspaces admit the full-fledged harmonic dependence
and could play an important (though for the time being unclear) role in the geometry of
\mathcal{N}=2
conformal supergravity and its higher-spin analogs.
It is straightforward to find the superconformal transformations of the integration measures in these new complex invariant subspaces and the relevant superconformal factors.

Finally, it is worth to point out once more that all
\mathcal{N}=2
superspaces listed above are proper subspaces of
\mathcal{N}=2
harmonic superspace
\mathbb{HR}^{4+2|8}
.

Appendix B
Linearized
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl supergravity in harmonic-independent gauge

In this appendix, for completeness, we present a discussion of Mezincescu-type prepotential and harmonic-independent gauge.
In deriving the harmonic-independent prepotentials we closely follow refs.
Zupnik:1998td
;
Kuzenko:1999pi
;
Butter:2010sc
.

B.1
Mezincescu-type prepotential and harmonic-independent gauge

One can represent the covariant harmonic derivative (
4.1
) as

\mathfrak{D}^{++}=\mathcal{D}^{++}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{++},

(B.1)

where the superfield differential operator
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{++}
is given by:

\begin{split}\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{++}:=&\;h^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+h^{++\hat{\alpha}-}\partial^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}+h^{++\hat{\alpha}+}\partial^{-}_{\hat{\alpha}}+h^{(+4)}\partial^{--}\\
=&\;G^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+G^{++\hat{\alpha}-}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\hat{\alpha}}+G^{++\hat{\alpha}+}\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\hat{\alpha}}+G^{(+4)}\mathcal{D}^{--}.\end{split}

(B.2)

The superfields appearing in this decomposition are treated as defining the linearized
\mathcal{N}=2
conformal supergravity in a flat super-background.

Let us consider the action of differential operator
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{++}
on an arbitrary analytic superfield
\Phi(\zeta,u)
.
When acting on an analytic superfield, this operator is analytic and so can be represented as

\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{++}\Phi(\zeta,u)=\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}\right)^{4}\Big\{\mathcal{H}\,\mathcal{D}^{--}\Phi(\zeta,u)\Big\},

(B.3)

where
\mathcal{D}^{--}
is the flat harmonic derivative.
In (
B.3
) we have introduced the Mezincescu-type gauge prepotential
\mathcal{H}
, which is a general harmonic superfield. The
G^{++}

superfields can be expressed in terms of

\mathcal{H}
as follows:

\begin{split}G^{++\alpha\dot{\alpha}}&=i\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\mathcal{H},\\
G^{++\alpha+}&=-\frac{1}{8}\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}\right)^{2}\mathcal{H},\\
\bar{G}^{++\dot{\alpha}+}&=\frac{1}{8}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}(\mathcal{D}^{+})^{2}\mathcal{H},\\
G^{(+4)}&=(\mathcal{D}^{+})^{4}\mathcal{H}.\end{split}

(B.4)

The prepotential
\mathcal{H}
is defined up to the pre-gauge freedom

\delta_{pg}\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{D}^{+})^{2}\Omega^{--}+(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}\widetilde{\Omega}^{--}\,,

(B.5)

where
\Omega^{--}
is an arbitrary harmonic superfield.
The original linearized gauge freedom (
4.10
) is realized as

\delta\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{D}^{++}l^{--}\,.

(B.6)

where parameter
l^{--}
is an arbitrary harmonic superfield. The relation between this parameter and the analytic gauge parameters
\lambda
is
determined by the equation

\hat{\Lambda}\Phi(\zeta,u)=\left(\mathcal{D}^{+}\right)^{4}\Big\{l^{--}\,\mathcal{D}^{--}\Phi(\zeta,u)\Big\},

(B.7)

that is analogous to (
B.3
). From this equation we obtain:

\begin{split}\Lambda^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}&=i\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}l^{--},\\
\Lambda^{+\alpha}&=-\frac{1}{8}\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}\right)^{2}l^{--},\\
\bar{\Lambda}^{+\dot{\alpha}}&=\frac{1}{8}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}(\mathcal{D}^{+})^{2}l^{--},\\
\Lambda^{++}&=(\mathcal{D}^{+})^{4}l^{--}.\end{split}

(B.8)

Using the relations (
4.11
), one can find the corresponding expressions for the analytic gauge parameters

\left\{\lambda^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\lambda^{+\alpha},\bar{\lambda}^{+\dot{\alpha}},\lambda^{++}\right\}
.

Taking into account the gauge freedom (
B.6
) in the central basis, we may impose the gauge:

\mathcal{H}(x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\theta^{\pm}_{\hat{\alpha}},u^{\pm}_{i})=H(x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}_{C},\theta^{i}_{\hat{\alpha}})\qquad\text{(harmonic-independent gauge)}.

(B.9)

In this gauge we have the residual gauge freedom
\delta H\sim\mathcal{D}_{ij}\Omega^{ij}+\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{ij}\bar{\Omega}^{ij}
.
The Mesincescu-type prepotential defined here can be considered as the spin 2 generalization of the
V_{ij}
prepotential for
\mathcal{N}=2

vector multiplet
Mezincescu:1979af
(see also discussion in
18
). Such a prepotential for the description of

\mathcal{N}=2
conformal supergravity multiplet was firstly introduced in
Howe:1981qj
;
Rivelles:1981qz
.

B.2
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl supertensor in harmonic-independent gauge

Throughout this section we work solely in the central basis, so we shall drop the subscript “
C
” on the
x
-coordinates.
In the central basis the covariant spinor derivatives are given by:

\mathcal{D}^{i}_{\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{\alpha}_{i}}+2i\bar{\theta}^{\dot{\alpha}i}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\qquad\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{\dot{\alpha}i}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\theta}^{\dot{\alpha}i}}-2i\theta^{\alpha}_{i}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\,

(B.10)

and satisfy the algebra

\left\{\mathcal{D}^{i}_{\alpha},\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{\dot{\alpha}j}\right\}=-\left\{\mathcal{D}_{\alpha j},\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}}\right\}=-4i\delta^{i}_{j}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(B.11)

The spinor derivatives in the analytic basis are then given by
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{D}^{i}_{\alpha}u^{\pm}_{i}
, etc.

In terms of the harmonic-independent Mezincescu-type prepotential, the half-analytic superfield
\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}
can be written as:

\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=i\partial_{(\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta)}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\beta}}H+\frac{1}{8}\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta)}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}H=\frac{1}{8}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta)}H,

(B.12)

where we have used the relation
\left[\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha},\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}\right)^{2}\right]=8i\partial_{\alpha}^{\dot{\alpha}}\,\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}
.

In the central basis, the following identities for the derivatives are valid:

\mathcal{D}^{-}_{(\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta)}=\mathcal{D}^{i}_{(\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{j}_{\beta)}u^{-}_{i}u^{+}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}^{i}_{(\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{j}_{\beta)}\left(u^{-}_{i}u^{+}_{j}-u^{-}_{j}u^{+}_{i}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{(\alpha\beta)}.

(B.13)

Then we find:

\mathcal{H}^{++}_{(\alpha\beta)}=-\frac{1}{16}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}\mathcal{D}_{(\alpha\beta)}H\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}=-\frac{1}{16}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-})^{2}\mathcal{D}_{(\alpha\beta)}H,

(B.14)

and, for
\mathcal{N}=2
super Weyl tensor,

\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}=\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}\right)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)}=-\frac{1}{16}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}\right)^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-})^{2}\mathcal{D}_{(\alpha\beta)}H=-(\bar{\mathcal{D}})^{4}\mathcal{D}_{(\alpha\beta)}H.

(B.15)

Thereby, we reproduced the definition of the
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl supertensor in the conventional
{\cal N}=2
superspace
(see, e.g.,
Howe:1981qj
;
Kuzenko:2021pqm
;
Butter:2010sc
).

Finally, note that in the harmonic-independent gauge we have:

\mathcal{D}^{(\alpha\beta)}\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}=\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}.

(B.16)

Since this equality is valid for the gauge-invariant objects, we conclude that

(\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{-\beta})\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}=(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\beta}})\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})},

(B.17)

regardless of gauge-fixing. It is not so easy to prove this equality, based solely on the definition (
4.20
).

Poster 166. Educational Interventions Can Improve Injury Prevention Awareness in Baseball Caregivers
Authors: Ryan P. Roach, Marissa Pazik, Tyler LaMonica, Kevin W. Farmer, Jason L. Zaremski, Giorgio Zeppieri, Wojciech Dzieza, MaryBeth Horodyski
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Though several overhead athlete injury prevention resources are available, there remains a lack of knowledge of these guidelines among baseball players, coaches, and caregivers. As such, tools aimed at increasing the educational level of this population are needed to improve the quality and use of injury prevention strategies. This study aimed to determine if educational interventions increased caregivers' knowledge of injury prevention guidelines for high school baseball players.Methods:This prospective study randomized nine high school baseball teams to three different educational conditions: a formal lecture with a PowerPoint (n=3), a flyer displayed around the baseball facilities (n=3), and a control group (n=3). Knowledge of injury prevention guidelines was assessed via a staff-developed questionnaire that included caregiver and player demographic questions, sports specialization items, current knowledge related to pitching guidelines, and injury information. The questionnaire was completed once during pre-season before any educational intervention and once at the end of the baseball season. Repeated-measure analyses were performed to evaluate differences from pre-season to post-season, as well as evaluation of the interaction of the educational intervention on caregiver knowledge. All analyses were performed in JMP Pro 17 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC).Results:A total of 60 caregivers (Females = 35, Males = 25) completed both the pre-season and post-season questionnaires at 8 high schools. Demographic information for caregivers and baseball players is presented in Table 1. Before educational interventions, 93% (n = 56) of caregivers did not believe that youth athletes should participate in sport specialization, whereas 100% of participants believed this after the post-season assessment. From pre- to post-season, we observed a statistically significant increase in awareness of guidelines for safe pitching practices among schools randomized to the formal lecture (44% increase, p = .0027), but not in our control group (p = 0.08) or schools randomized to the educational flyer (p = 0.32).Conclusions:Our findings support previous research that a large majority (67%) of baseball caregivers are unaware of current guidelines or recommendations regarding safe pitching practices. A direct educational intervention, such as a formal lecture, can improve awareness of these guidelines, while indirect interventions show no improvement in awareness. Sports medicine professionals may wish to include lectures to baseball caregivers and players as part of their injury prevention programs.

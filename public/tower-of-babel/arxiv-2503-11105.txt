Quantization dimension for a generalized inhomogeneous bi-Lipschitz iterated function system
Shivam Dubey, Mrinal Kanti Roychowdhury, Saurabh Verma

Abstract
. For a given r ∈ ( 0 , + ∞ ) r\in(0,+\infty) , the quantization dimension of order r r , if it exists, denoted by D r ​ ( μ ) D_{r}(\mu) , of a Borel probability measure μ \mu on ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} represents the speed how fast the n n th quantization error of order r r approaches to zero as the number of elements n n in an optimal set of n n -means for μ \mu tends to infinity. If D r ​ ( μ ) D_{r}(\mu) does not exists, we call D ¯ r ​ ( μ ) \underline{D}_{r}(\mu) and D ¯ r ​ ( μ ) \overline{D}_{r}(\mu) , the lower and upper quantization dimensions of μ \mu of order r r . In this paper, we estimate the quantization dimension of condensation measures associated with condensation systems ( { f i } i = 1 N , ( p i ) i = 0 N , ν ) (\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},(p_{i})_{i=0}^{N},\nu) , where the mappings f i f_{i} are bi-Lipschitz and the measure ν \nu is an image measure of an ergodic measure with bounded distortion supported on a conformal set. In addition, we determine the optimal quantization for an infinite discrete distribution, and give an example which shows that the quantization dimension of a Borel probability measure can be positive with zero quantization coefficient.

Remark 1.2
.

We know that the classification of an IFS depends on the nature of its associated mappings. For instance, if the associated mappings are bi-Lipschitz, affine, or conformal, then the IFS is referred to as a bi-Lipschitz IFS, affine IFS, or conformal IFS, respectively. It is important to note that a bi-Lipschitz IFS can encompass all well-known classes of fractal sets, including self-similar, self-affine, and self-conformal sets.

(1)

If
a_{i}=b_{i}
in Equation (
6
), then the IFS
\mathcal{I}
reduces to a self-similar IFS, and the associated fractal set is referred to as a self-similar set.

(2)

Let

be an affine transformation given by
f(x)=Ax+b
, where
A
is an injective linear transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then,

\frac{1}{\|A^{-1}\|}\|x-y\|_{2}\leq\|Ax-Ay\|_{2}=\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{2}\leq\|A\|\|x-y\|_{2}.

(3)

Let

be a nonempty compact set with
X=\overline{\text{int}(X)}
, and let
\{(X,d);g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{M}\}
be a conformal IFS. Then, by
[
pat
, Lemma 2.2]
, there exists an open set
V
containing
X
and a constant
c\geq 1
, such that

(5)

c^{-1}\|g_{\omega}^{\prime}\|\|x-y\|\leq\|g_{\omega}(x)-g_{\omega}(y)\|\leq c\|g_{\omega}^{\prime}\|\|x-y\|,

for all
x,y\in V
and
\omega\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{1,2,\ldots,M\}^{n}
.

From the above points it is clear that dimensional results applicable to bi-Lipschitz IFSs can provide useful estimates for the dimensions of self-similar, self-affine, and self-conformal IFSs. Nevertheless, it should be emphasized that bi-Lipschitz IFSs may not always yield the exact dimensional values for self-affine and self-conformal sets; see, for instance,
[

,
LM
]
.

In this section, we state and prove the following theorem, which is the main result of the paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

\mu
be the condensation measure associated with the condensation system
(\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},(p_{i})_{i=0}^{N},\nu)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(p_{i}b_{i}^{r}\right)^{\frac{l_{r}}{r+l_{r}}}=1.

yielding

\displaystyle V_{k+1}(\nu)

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{(a_{\ell}-c_{\ell})^{2}\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}+(a_{\ell+1}-c_{\ell})^{2}\frac{1}{2^{\ell+1}}:a_{\ell}\leq c_{\ell}\leq\frac{3}{5}\right\}=\frac{8^{-\ell}}{75},

which occurs when
c_{\ell}=\frac{3}{5}-\frac{2^{-\ell}}{3}
.
Since
2\leq\ell\leq k
, we have

\frac{2^{3-3k}}{1575}\leq\frac{2^{3-3\ell}}{1575}\leq\frac{8}{21}\frac{8^{-\ell}}{75}<\frac{8^{-\ell}}{75}

implying

V_{k+1}(\nu)\geq\frac{8^{-\ell}}{75}>\frac{2^{3-3k}}{1575}\geq V_{k+1}(\nu)
, which yields a contradiction. Hence, we can assume that
c_{1}=a_{1},c_{2}=a_{2},\cdots,c_{k}=a_{k}
yielding
c_{k+1}=b_{k+1}
whenever it is true for
n=k
. Thus, the proposition is true for
n=k+1
. Hence, by the Principle of Mathematical Induction, the proposition is true for all positive integers
n\geq 2
.
∎

Let us now give the following theorem.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\nu
be the infinite discrete distribution. Then, the quantization dimension
D(\nu)
of the measure
\nu
exists and equals zero, but the quantization coefficient does not exist.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.5
, we have
V_{n}(\nu)=\frac{2^{6-3n}}{1575}
, and so,

D(\nu)=\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log n}{-\log V_{n}(\nu)}=0
, i.e.,
D(\nu)
exists and equals zero. Since
D(\nu)=0
, the quantization coefficient for
\nu
does not exist. Thus, the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Let us now give the following example.

Example 4.8
.

Let
\nu
be an infinite discrete distribution with support
C:=\{a_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where
a_{j}:=4\cdot 3^{j-1}
. The probability mass function
f
of
\nu
is given by:

f(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{k\left(2(j-1)\log(j-1)+(j-2)\right)}{3^{j}(j-1)(j-2)^{3}\log^{2}(j-1)}&\text{if }x=a_{j}\text{ for }j\geq 3,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
k=\left(\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(2(j-1)\log(j-1)+(j-2))}{3^{j}(j-1)(j-2)^{3}\log^{2}(j-1)}\right)^{-1}<\infty
is the normalizing constant. For each
a_{j}
, we have
0\leq f(a_{j})\leq 1
and
\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}f(a_{j})=1
. These conditions ensure that
f
is a valid probability mass function.
Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with
\text{card}(A)=n
, and define the index set

I=\{j\geq 2:A\cap[3^{j},3^{j+1})=\emptyset\}.

Then, for
j\in I
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{a\in A}|a_{j}-a|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(a_{j}-3^{j})^{2}=(4\cdot 3^{j-1}-3^{j})^{2}=3^{2(j-1)}.

We see that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\min_{a\in A}|a_{j}-a|^{2}\cdot\frac{k(2(j-1)\log(j-1)+(j-2))}{3^{2j}(j-1)(j-2)^{3}\log^{2}(j-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in I}3^{2(j-1)}\cdot\frac{k(2(j-1)\log(j-1)+(j-2))}{3^{2j}(j-1)(j-2)^{3}\log^{2}(j-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{9}\sum_{j=n+2}^{\infty}\frac{(2(j-1)\log(j-1)+(j-2))}{(j-1)(j-2)^{3}\log^{2}(j-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k}{9}\int_{j=n+2}^{\infty}\frac{(2(x-1)\log(x-1)+(x-2))}{(x-1)(x-2)^{3}\log^{2}(x-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k}{9n^{2}\log(n+2)}.

The above inequality is true for any
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with
\text{card}(A)=n
, and hence
V_{n,2}(\nu)\geq\frac{k}{3^{2}n^{2}\log(n+2)}
.
Furthermore, for
B=\{a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1}\}
, we observe:

\min_{b\in B}|a_{j}-b|^{2}=0\text{ if }j\leq n+1,\text{ and }\min_{b\in B}|a_{j}-b|^{2}\leq a_{j}^{2}=\frac{16\cdot 3^{2j}}{9}\text{ if }j\geq n+2.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\min_{b\in B}|a_{j}-b|^{2}\cdot\frac{k(2(j-1)\log(j-1)+(j-2))}{3^{2j}(j-1)(j-2)^{3}\log^{2}(j-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16k}{9}\sum_{j=n+2}^{\infty}\frac{(2(j-1)\log(j-1)+(j-2))}{(j-1)(j-2)^{3}\log^{2}(j-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16k}{9n^{2}\log(n+1)},

implying
V_{n,2}(\nu)\leq\frac{16k}{9n^{2}\log(n+1)}
. Therefore, we conclude:

\displaystyle-\log\frac{16k}{9n^{2}\log(n+1)}\leq-\log V_{n,2}(\nu)\leq-\log\frac{16k}{9n^{2}\log(n+2)}

\displaystyle\implies\frac{1}{-\log\frac{16k}{9n^{2}\log(n+2)}}\leq\frac{1}{-\log V_{n,2}(\nu)}\leq\frac{1}{-\log\frac{16k}{9n^{2}\log(n+1)}}

\displaystyle\implies\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log n}{-\log\frac{16k}{9n^{2}\log(n+2)}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log n}{-\log V_{n,2}(\nu)}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{-\log\frac{16k}{9n^{2}\log(n+1)}}

\displaystyle\implies 1\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log n}{-\log V_{n,2}(\nu)}\leq 1.

This yields the fact that the quantization dimension of the infinite discrete distribution
\nu
is
1
, which is nonzero. Furthermore,

0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{k}{9\log(n+2)}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{2}V_{n,2}(\nu)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{2}V_{n,2}(\nu)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{16k}{9\log(n+1)}=0,

which implies that the one-dimensional quantization coefficient for
\nu
is zero.

Remark 4.9
.

By Remark
1.1
, it is known that if the
k
-dimensional quantization coefficient is finite and positive, then
k
equals the quantization dimension of
\nu.
By Example
4.8
, we see that the converse is not true. One can also see
[
GL3
, Example 6.4]
in which Graf-Luschgy considered an infinite discrete distribution for which quantization dimension is one, but the quantization coefficient is infinity.

CONSTANCE, LAKE OF (called by the Romans Lacus Brigantinus
or lake of Bregenz, and now usually named in German
Bodensee, as well as the “Swabian Sea”), the most extensive
sheet of water in the Alpine region, after the Lake of Geneva.
It is situated on the north-east frontier of Switzerland, and is
formed by the Rhine. Its shape is oblong, while at its north-western
extremity it divides into two arms, the Untersee (from Constance
to Stein-am-Rhein) and the Überlingersee (running up to
Ludwigshafen). The length of the lake from Bregenz to Stein-am-Rhein
is 462 m., while that from Bregenz to Ludwigshafen
is but 40 m. Its surface is 1309 ft. above sea-level, the greatest
width is 102 m., and the greatest depth 827 ft. The area of the
lake is 2043 4 sq. m., of which 814 sq. m. have belonged to Switzerland
since 1803, the canton of Thurgau holding 593 4 sq. m. and
that of St Gall 212 sq. m. Austria has held Bregenz, at the
south-eastern angle of the lake, since 1451, while the north end
of the lake belongs to Baden (Constance held since 1805), and
bits of its eastern shore form part of Württemberg (Friedrichshafen,
formerly called Buchhorn, since 1810) and of Bavaria
(Lindau since 1805). The first steamer was placed on its waters in
1824. Numerous remains of lake-dwellings have been found on
the shores of this lake (see E. von Tröltsch, Die Pfahlbauten
des Bodenseegebietes, Stuttgart, 1902). William Augustus Brevoort Coolidge W. A. B. C.

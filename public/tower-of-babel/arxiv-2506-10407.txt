Semi-Tensor-Product Based Convolutional Neural Networks
Daizhan Cheng

Abstract
The semi-tensor product (STP) of vectors is a generalization of conventional inner product of vectors, which allows the factor vectors to of different dimensions. This paper proposes a domain-based convolutional product (CP). Combining domain-based CP with STP of vectors, a new CP is proposed. Since there is no zero or any other padding, it can avoid the junk information caused by padding. Using it, the STP-based convolutional neural network (CNN) is developed. Its application to image and third order signal identifications is considered.



I
Introduction

CNN is a fundamental tool in artificial intelligence (AI). It is particularly efficient in face and object identifications, computer vision, speech synthesis, natural language processing, etc. The famous AlpaGo developed by DeepMind in 2016 has won over the go world champion. The technique engine of AlpaGo is also based on CNN
[
9
]
.

When CNN is used for signal processing or image procession, a padding is necessary for edges. Moreover, if the signals (or images) are partly damaged, a padding-masking is commonly used for dealing with them. It is obvious that the padding-masking must bring some noises into the systems’ data.

This paper proposes a new CNN, called STP-based (STP-)CNN. STP-CNN uses inner product over
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
to deal with token-vectors of different sizes. No padding and hence no masking is necessary. To construct an STP-CNN we first propose a new convolutional product (CP), called the domain-based CP. Then the inner product of vectors with different sizes is used to define block-Hadamard product. For each image matrix, its receptive field matrix (RFM) is construct. Then the CP-matrix of an image is obtained as the block-Hadamard product of kernel matrix with RFM. This process of STP-CNN can also be used for all other signals such as voices, which are one dimensional signals, 3-dimensional signals which might come from CT-scaling, etc.

The rest of this paper is outlined as follows. Section 2 briefly introduces the inner-product of vectors of different dimensions. Section 3 proposes the domain-based CP. The CP for Image Processing for STP-CNN is discussed in Section 4. In Section 5 various applications of STP-CP to image processing are considered, including (a) irregular images; (b) partly damaged images; (c) proportional CP; etc. Section 6 considers the STP-CNN of 3-dimensions signals.
Finally, Section 7 is a brief conclusion.

III
Classical CP and Domain-based CP

III-A

Classical CP

Definition III.1

Let
f(x),~g(x)\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. The CP of
f(x)
and
g(x)
, denoted by
f(x)*g(x)
, is

\displaystyle s(x):=f(x)*g(x)=\int_{\tau=-\infty}^{\infty}f(\tau)g(x-\tau)d\tau.

(11)

Some fundamental properties of CP are as follows.

Proposition III.2

[
5
]

(i)

\displaystyle\|f(x)*g(x)\|_{1}\leq\|f(x)\|_{1}\|g(x)\|_{1}.

(12)

Hence,
f(x)*g(x)\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.

(2)

\displaystyle f(x)*g(x)=g(x)*f(x).

(13)

(3)

If
f(x),~g(x),~h(x)\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, then

\displaystyle f(x)*(g(x)*h(x))=(f(x)*g(x))*h(x).

(14)

The above are about CP for continuous functions. When discrete-time signals are considered, we need discrete-time CP, which is defined as follows.

Definition III.3

Let
f(n),w(n)\in\ell_{1}
, the discrete-times CP of
f(n)
and
w(n)
is defined as follows.

\displaystyle s(n):=f(n)*w(n)=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{\tau=-\infty}^{\infty}f(\tau)w(n-\tau).

(15)

When
w
is considered as a weight function, the CP in equations (
11
) and (
15
) can be considered as a weighted average. Moreover, the weights are dependent of the distance between current position and the function arguments to be averaged. Proposition is also true for discrete-time CP. Using (
12
), we have the following result.

Proposition III.4

(i)

Assume
f(x),w(x)\in L_{1}
, then

f(x)*w(x)\in L_{1}
.

(ii)

Assume
f(n),w(n)\in\ell_{1}
, then

f(n)*w(n)\in\ell_{1}
.

III-B

CP with Windows

In deep leaning or most signal processing problems the CP uses only finite terms. That is,

\displaystyle s(n):=f(n)*k(n)=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{\tau=-N}^{N}f(\tau)k(n-\tau).

(16)

where
[-N,N]
is called the sampling window,
f(n)
is the input and
k(n)
is the CP kernel.

According to the symmetry (refer to (
13
)), we can flip the input with kernel, which yields

\displaystyle s(n):=f(n)*k(n)=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{\tau=-N}^{N}f(n-\tau)k(\tau).

(17)

In CNN, another CP, called the cross-correlation, is also used, which is defined by

\displaystyle s(n):=f(n)*k(n)=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{\tau=-N}^{N}f(n+\tau)k(\tau).

(18)

The CP for multiple variable functions can be defined in a similar way. For instance, the CP for functions with two variables are defined as follows.

\displaystyle s(m,n):=f(m,n)*k(m,n)=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i}\mathop{\sum}\limits_{j}f(i,j)k(x-i,y-j).

(19)

or

\displaystyle s(m,n):=f(m,n)*k(m,n)=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i}\mathop{\sum}\limits_{j}f(m-i,n-j)k(i,j).

(20)

The CP for functions with two variables is used commonly for image processing.

III-C

Domain-based CP

Definition III.5

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a measurable set,
f(x)\in L_{1}(D)
,
w(x)\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then the Domain-based CP (DCP) of
f(x)
with
w(x)
is defined as follows.

\displaystyle s(x):=f(x)*w(x)=\int_{\tau\in D}f(\tau)g(x-\tau)d\tau.

(21)

It is easy to verify that some fundamental properties of conventional CP are also true for DCP.

Proposition III.6

(i)

\displaystyle\|f(x)*w(x)|_{1}\leq\|f(x)\|^{D}_{1}\|w(x)\|_{1}.

(22)

Hence
f(x)*g(x)\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.

(2)

f(x)*g(x)=g(x)*f(x)
, which can be specified as follows.

\displaystyle\int_{\tau\in D}f(\tau)w(t-\tau)d\tau=\int_{\tau\in D^{\prime}}w(\tau)f(t-\tau)d\tau,

(23)

where,

D^{\prime}=\{\tau\;|\;t-\tau\in D\}.

(3)

Assume
f(x)\in L_{1}(D)
,
g(x),h(x)\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, then

\displaystyle f(x)*(g(x)*h(x))=(f(x)*g(x))*h(x).

(24)

IV-B

Block Hadamard Product

This subsection define a matrix product, called the block Hadamard product, which makes the CP of CNN being easily calculated.

Definition IV.3

Let
A
and
B
be two matrices of the same size with the same block decomposition
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}A=\begin{bmatrix}A^{1,1}&A^{1,2}&\cdots&A^{1,q}\\
A^{2,1}&A^{2,2}&\cdots&A^{2,q}\\
\vdots&~&~&~\\
A^{p,1}&A^{p,2}&\cdots&A^{p,q}\\
\end{bmatrix};\\
B=\begin{bmatrix}B^{1,1}&B^{1,2}&\cdots&B^{1,q}\\
B^{2,1}&B^{2,2}&\cdots&B^{2,q}\\
\vdots&~&~&~\\
B^{p,1}&B^{p,2}&\cdots&B^{p,q}\\
\end{bmatrix}.\end{array}

The block Hardamard product of
A
and
B
, denoted by
A\vec{\circ}B
, is defined by

\displaystyle A\vec{\circ}B:=C=(c_{i,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times q},

(33)

where

c_{i,j}=\langle V_{c}(A^{i,j}),V_{c}(B^{i,j})\rangle,\quad i\in[1,p],~j\in[1,q].

Receptive Field Matrix

We construct a matrix, called the receptive field matrix (RFM). The RFM, denoted by
R_{A}
, consists of
\alpha\times\beta
blocks, with

\alpha=s_{v},\quad\beta=s_{h}.

Each block is a receptive field, and they are arranged in the order of their positions in
\bar{A}
.

To calculate
R_{A}
, We construct a matrix
\Xi
as follows.

Definition IV.4

Let
d,~\eta,~n
be three positive integers satisfying
0<d<\eta
and
n>1
. Construct a matrox

\Xi_{n\times\eta}^{d}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi\times n\eta},

where

\displaystyle\xi=(n-1)d+\eta,

(34)

and it is shown in Figure
3
.

Fig. 3:
Matrix
\Xi_{n\times\eta}^{d}

Using
\Xi
, a formula for calculating
R_{A}
is easily obtained as follows.

Proposition IV.5

Assume
\bar{A}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times q}
, and (
28
) is satisfied. Then the RFM matrix can be obtained as

\displaystyle R_{A}=P\bar{A}Q\in{\mathcal{M}}_{(s_{v}s)\times(s_{t}t)},

(35)

where

\displaystyle P^{\mathrm{T}}=\Xi_{s_{v}\times s}^{d_{v}};\quad Q=\Xi_{s_{h}\times t}^{d_{h}}.

(36)

The RFM matrix
R_{A}
can be expressed into a block form as

\displaystyle R_{A}=\begin{bmatrix}R_{1,1}&R_{1,2}&\cdots&R_{1,s_{h}}\\
R_{2,1}&R_{2,2}&\cdots&R_{2,s_{h}}\\
\vdots&~&~&~\\
R_{s_{v},1}&R_{s_{v},2}&\cdots&R_{s_{v},s_{h}}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(37)

where each
R_{i,j}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{s\times t}
. Then the CP matrix can be obtained by

\displaystyle s_{i,j}=\langle V_{c}(K),V_{c}(R_{i,j})\rangle,i\in[1,s_{v}],~j\in[1,s_{h}].

(38)

Using block Hadamard product, the following expression can be obtained.

Proposition IV.6

Let the RFM matrix
R_{A}
be as in (
37
), where each block
R_{i,j}
is of the same size as the kernel matrix
K
.
Then the CP matrix is obtained as

\displaystyle S=\left({\bf 1}_{s_{v}\times s_{h}}\otimes K\right)R_{A}.

(39)

STP-CP

Recall the STP of vectors, which is defined by (
(i)
).
In classical CP of CNN, the elements in CP matrix
S
is obtained by the conventional inner product. We refer to (
27
for this.

In STP-CP we consider a receptive field
\bar{A}_{i,j}
. If in this receptive field there are some undefined elements, instead of padding them by zeros, we may just ignore such elements. Then
\dim(\bar{A}_{i,j})
are of different dimensions. Then the STP of vectors with different dimensions is used to perform inner product of
K
with them. This is called the STP-CP. Using STP-CP yields the STP-CNN.
Precisely, it is defined as follows:

Definition V.1

Let the image matrix be
A
, and the kernel matirx be
K
. Define

\displaystyle s_{i,j}:=\langle V_{c}(A_{i,j}),V_{c}(K)\rangle_{{\mathcal{V}}},

(40)

where
A_{i,j}
is the subset within the corresponding receptive field, which consists of available entries only.

Remark V.2

(i)

We can consider an image
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}
as a function defined over a domain

D=[1,m]\times[1,n]\subset{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}.

Then the CP is defined by

s_{p,q}=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{(i,j)\in D^{\prime}}A_{p+i.q+j}K_{i,j},

where

D^{\prime}=\{(i,j)\;|\;(p+i,q+j)\in D.

Hence, STP-CP needs no any padding such as zero-padded
\bar{A}
.

(ii)

Observe Figure
2
again. Now the RFM corresponding to
s_{1,1}
is
A_{1,1}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{1\times 1}
;
corresponding to
s_{2,2}
is
A_{2\times 2}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2\times 2}
; and corresponding to
s_{4,4}
is
A_{4,4}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{1\times 2}
. So the corresponding inner product is STP of vectors.

VIII
Conclusion

In this paper the classical inner product, which is used for CP, is replaced by STP inner product, which allows the factor vectors being with different dimensions. Using this dimension-free inner product, the STP CP is proposed, and it is used for performing the corresponding CNN. Using STP CP, the image processing is considered in detail. It is shown that when the image is irregular, or partly damaged, or of the improper size, the STP CP can be used without padding. This advantage may help in reducing the noise caused by padding.

The paper provides only a theoretical framework. Numerical simulations should be followed urgently.

Weighted approximation By Max-product Kantrovich type Exponential Sampling Series


Abstract
. In this study, we examine the convergence characteristics of the Max-Product Kantrovich type exponential sampling series within the weighted space of log-uniformly continuous and bounded functions. The research focuses on deriving fundamental convergence results for the series and analyzing its asymptotic convergence behavior. The study estimates the rate of convergence using the weighted logarithmic modulus of continuity and establishes a quantitative Voronovskaja-type theorem offering insights into the asymptotic behavior of the series. Additionally, we present the example of kernel functions satisfying assumptions of the presented theory along with graphical demonstration and estimates of approximation error. Keywords: Exponential sampling series, Max-Product Kantrovich operator, weighted spaces, weighted logarithmic modulus of continuity, order of Convergence, Mellin theory. Mathematics Subject Classification(2020): 41A25, 41A30, 41A35, 41A81.

[
23
,
36
]
, etc. Collectively, the Whittaker-Kotelnikov-Shannon achieved a significant breakthrough in sampling and reconstruction theory. They showed that the sample values of any band-limited signal
f
, i.e., whose Fourier transform
f
is compactly supported, can recover the signal entirely (see
[
24
]
). It is known as widely as
WKS sampling theorem
. This result was generalized to not necessarily band-limited signals by Butzer and Stens
[
26
]
by replacing the sinc function in the WKS sampling theorem with a more generalized kernel satisfying suitable assumptions. Since then, several questions have been posed, and various mathematicians have made significant advances in this direction, see
[
27
,
8
,
44
]
.

The problem of approximating functions given their exponentially-spaced sample values has been discussed in the work of Ostrowsky et al.
[
45
]
, Bertero and Pike
[
25
]
and Gori
[
37
]
. They also provided a series representation for the class of Mellin band limited functions (defined in Section
2
) to handle exponentially spaced data. This reconstruction formula is referred to as the
exponential sampling formula
and is defined as follows. For
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
l\in\mathbb{R},
the exponential sampling formula is given by (see
[
28
]
)

(1.1)

\text{(}X_{l,P}\;h)(z):=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}lin_{\frac{l}{P}}(e^{j}z^{P})\;h(e^{\frac{j}{P}}),

where
lin_{l}(z)=\dfrac{z^{-l}}{2\pi i}\dfrac{z^{\pi i}-z^{-\pi i}}{\log l}=z^{-c}sinc(\log z)
with continuous extension
lin_{l}(1)=1.
Moreover, if
h
is Mellin band-limited to
[-P,P],
then
(X_{l,P}h)(z)=h(z)
for each
z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Scientific applications along with engineering applications commonly utilize exponentially spaced data to study Fraunhofer diffraction and perform photon correlation spectroscopy in polydispersity studies and neuron scattering together with radio astronomical research etc (see
[
32
,
45
,
25
,
37
]

). Butzer and Jansche
[
30
]
contributed a major advancement through their use of Mellin analysis tools which transformed the understanding of exponential sampling frameworks. Their approach established Mellin transform techniques as an optimal method for analyzing sampling and approximation problem associated with exponential spaced data.Mamedov started the initial groundwork that established Mellin transform theory which he first introduced in his paper
[
43
]
. Butzer and his collaborators built upon this field through their works documented in
[
28
,
30
]
. The works presented in
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
with others contain substantial developments in Mellin analysis. The paper of Bardaro et al. in
[
14
]
introduced an extended exponential sampling formula (
1.1
) to approximate functions beyond strict Mellin band-limited conditions. An extended exponential sampling formula uses appropriate kernel conditions to approximate log-continuous functions through their exponentially spaced data points. In
[
14
]
, for
z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\mathrm{m}>0,j\in Z\And h:R_{+}\to R
the generalized exponential sampling series is defined as follows:

(1.2)

(G_{\mathrm{m}}^{\varkappa}\;h)(z)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})\;h(e^{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}),

where
\varkappa
is a kernel function satisfying the suitable assumptions such that the series (

) can be observed in
[
7
,
15
,
18
,

]
. The works
[
4
,

]
deal with the approximation behavior of exponential sampling operators with artificial neural network structures. To approximate the Lebesgue integrable functions on
R_{+}
, the expansion (
1.2
) is not so convenient. To address this limitation, a Kantorovich-type modification of the exponential sampling series (
1.2
) was recently introduced in
[
5
]
. For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{+},j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathrm{m}>0,
the Kantorovich exponential sampling series of
f
is defined by

(1.3)

(I_{\mathrm{m}}^{\varkappa}h)(z):=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})\ \mathrm{m}\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}h(e^{v})\ dv,

whenever the series (
1.3
) is absolutely convergent for any locally integrable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}.

Recent work by Coroianu and Gal introduced an interesting procedure to improve the order of approximation that can be attained by a family of linear operators by a so-called max-product method; see, for example, Coroianu and Gal (2010)
[
33
]
, Coroianu and Gal (2011)
[
34
]
and Coroianu and Gal (2012)
[
35
]
. In general, discrete linear operators are defined by finite sums or series concerning certain indexes. The max-product operators are defined by replacing the sums or the series with a maximum or a supremum computed over the same sets of indexes. The above procedure allows the conversion of linear operators into nonlinear ones, which can achieve a higher order of approximation concerning their linear counterparts.

In the present paper, we investigate the approximation behaviors of Max Product Kantrovich exponential sampling operators for functions belonging to a weighted space of functions. More precisely, for the above operators, the point-wise and uniform convergence are established together with the rate of convergence by involving the weighted modulus of continuity. Finally, we prove the quantitative Voronovskaja-type theorem for the operators.

The paper is organized as follows: The Sections
1
and
2
are devoted to fundamentals of Max product Kantrovich type exponential sampling and some auxiliary results. Section
3
is dedicated to the well-definiteness of the operators between weighted spaces of functions, together with pointwise and uniform convergence. It derives the rate of convergence of the family of operators in terms of the weighted modulus of continuity. Furthermore, a pointwise convergence result in quantitative form is provided using a Voronovskaja-type theorem. In Section
4
, we present three examples of kernel functions and numerical experiments that demonstrate the performance of proposed operators for various test functions and graphical visualizations of their approximation and convergence behavior. Finally, the paper concludes with a summary in Section
5
.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we consider the interval
{I}=[a,b]
to be any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where

denotes the set of positive real numbers. Let
{C(I)}

We also introduce
{C_{+}(I)}

{C(I)}
.
A function

is defined to be log uniformly continuous function if, for any given
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\rho>0
such that
|h(z_{1})-h(z_{2})|<\epsilon
whenever
|\log(z_{1})-\log(z_{2})|\leq\rho,
for all

Now we introduce the space
{U}({I})
consisting of all log uniformly continuous functions defined on
{I}
. This space forms a completely normed linear space equipped with the maximum norm. The subspace
{U_{+}}(\mathrm{I})
comprises all non-negative function with in
{U}({I})
. Furthermore, we denote

{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):=
The space of all bounded functions on
\mathbb{R_{+}}.

{V}(\mathbb{R_{+}}):=
The space of all log uniformly continuous and bounded functions defined on
\mathbb{R_{+}}.

{V}_{+}(\mathbb{R_{+}}):=
The space of all non negative log uniformly continuous and bounded function defined on
\mathbb{R_{+}}.

We are now going to introduce the weighted function space for a particular weight function
\mathrm{w}(z)=\frac{1}{1+\log^{2}z}
.
We denote the weighted function space
{B}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):=\{h:\mathbb{R_{+}}\to\mathbb{R}:\exists K>0
such that
\mathrm{w}(z)|h(z)|\leq K

\forall z\in\mathbb{R_{+}}\}
associated with
{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and we define the weighted space associated with
{V}(\mathbb{R_{+}})
as
{V}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathrm{R}_{+}):=\{h:\mathbb{R_{+}}\to\mathbb{R}:\mathrm{w}h\in{V}(\mathbb{R_{+}})\}
and
{V}^{\mathrm{w}}_{+}(\mathbb{R_{+}})
, the subclass of
{V}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathrm{R}_{+})
which is non negative.

It is interesting to mention that the linear space of functions
{V}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is normed linear space with the norm

\|h\|_{\mathrm{w}}=\sup_{z>0}\mathbf{\mathrm{w}}(z)|h(z)|.

Definition 2.1
.

The weighted logarithmic modulus of continuity for
h\in{V}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\rho>0
is considered as

\Upsilon(h,\rho)=\sup_{|\log u|\leq\rho,z>0}\frac{|f(uz)-f(z)|}{(1+\log^{2}z)(1+\log^{2}u)}.

Lemma 2.2
.

The weighted logarithmic modulus of continuity
\Upsilon(h,\rho)
has the following fundamental Properties:

(1)

\Upsilon(h,\rho)
is a monotonically increasing function of
\rho
.

(2)

For

the quantity

(3)

For all

\Upsilon(h,\lambda\rho)\leq 2(1+\lambda)^{3}(1+\rho^{2})\Upsilon(h,\rho).

(4)

For all

and

|(f(s)-f(z)|\leq 16(1+\rho^{2})^{2}(1+\log^{2}z)(1+\frac{|\log s-\log z|^{5}}{\rho^{5}})\Upsilon(h,\rho).

(5)

For

we have
\lim_{\rho\to 0}\Upsilon(h,\rho)=0.

Now, we introduce the notation that will be used to establish the analysis of the max-product sampling operators.

Given any index set
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}

\bigvee_{\mathrm{j}\in\Lambda}{T}_{j}:=\sup\{{T}_{j}:\mathrm{j}\in\Lambda\}.

If

\bigvee_{\mathrm{j}\in\Lambda}{T}_{j}=\max_{\mathrm{j}\in\Lambda}{T}_{j}\;.

Again, we introduce the functions used as kernels of the max product generalized exponential sampling operator. In the subsequent analysis, we define a kernel as a function
\varkappa:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that is both bounded and measurable, and satisfies the following conditions.

(\varkappa_{1}):

There exists
\kappa>0
, such that the discrete absolute moment of order
\kappa
is finite for

m_{\kappa}(\varkappa)=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R_{+}}}\bigvee_{j\in z}|\varkappa(e^{-j}s)||j-\log s|^{\kappa}\;<\infty\quad\text{for}\,\,\kappa=2.

(\varkappa_{2}):

there holds

\inf_{z\in[1,e]}\varkappa(z)=:\zeta_{z}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\varkappa
be a bounded function satisfying
(\varkappa_{1})
with
\kappa>0
. Then
m_{\mu}(\varkappa)<\infty,
for every

Proof.

Let

we have

\displaystyle\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}s)||j-\log(s)|^{\mu}\leq

|j-\log(s)|\leq 1\end{subarray}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}s)||j-\log s|^{\mu}

\displaystyle+\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{Z}\\

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m_{0}(\varkappa)+m_{\mu}(\varkappa)

\displaystyle\leq

\Box

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varkappa:\mathbb{R_{+}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a kernel satisfying
m_{\kappa}(\varkappa)<\infty,\kappa>0
. Then, for every

, we have

\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{Z}\\

Let

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

3.
Results for Max-Product Kantrovich Exponential Sampling Series

The first main result shows that the operators
{M}^{\varkappa}_{\mathrm{m}}
are well defined on logarithmic weighted space of functions. We need the following preliminary propositions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\varkappa
be a kernel satisfying the assumptions
(\varkappa_{1}),(\varkappa_{2})
for
\kappa=2
. Then for a fixed
\mathrm{m}>0
the operator
{M}^{\varkappa}_{\mathrm{m}}
is a operator from
{B}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to{B}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and its operator norm turns out to be

\displaystyle\|{M}^{\varkappa}_{\mathrm{m}}\|_{{B}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to{B}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}_{z}}\left[m_{0}(\varkappa)(1+\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}}+\frac{1}{3\mathrm{m}^{2}})+(\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}})m_{1}(\varkappa)+\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}m_{2}(\varkappa)\right].

Proof.

Let us fix
\mathrm{m}>0
. Using the definition of new form
{M}^{\varkappa}_{\mathrm{m}}(h,z)
, we have

|{M}^{\varkappa}_{\mathrm{m}}|\leq\frac{1}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|m\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}|f(e^{v})|dv.

Moreover, since
h\in{B}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and recalling
\Phi=\frac{1}{\mathrm{w}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|{M}^{\varkappa}_{\mathrm{m}}(h,z)|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|\;m\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}|h(e^{v})|dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|\;m\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}|h(e^{v})|\frac{1+\log^{2}(e^{v})}{1+\log^{2}(e^{v})}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|_{\mathrm{w}}}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|\;\mathrm{m}\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}(1+\log^{2}(e^{v}))dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|_{\mathrm{w}}}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|\;\mathrm{m}\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}(1+(\log(e^{v}))^{2})dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|_{\mathrm{w}}}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|\;\mathrm{m}\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}(1+v^{2})dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|_{\mathrm{w}}}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|\;\mathrm{m}\;\left[v+\frac{v^{3}}{3}\right]_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|_{\mathrm{w}}}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|\;\mathrm{m}\;\left[\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}}+\frac{1}{3}\left(\left(\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}\right)^{3}-\left(\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}\right)^{3}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|_{\mathrm{w}}}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|\;\mathrm{m}\;\left[\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}}+\frac{1}{3\mathrm{m}}\left(\frac{3j^{2}}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}+\frac{3j}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|_{\mathrm{w}}}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|\;\left[1+\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{3j^{2}}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}+\frac{3j}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|_{\mathrm{w}}}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|\;\left[1+\frac{1}{3\mathrm{m}^{2}}+\frac{j^{2}}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}+\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|_{\mathrm{w}}}{\zeta_{z}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})|

Since the assumption

implies

we deduce

\|{M}^{\varkappa}_{\mathrm{m}}h\|_{\mathrm{w}}\leq+\infty,~~\text{i.e.,}~~{M}^{\varkappa}_{\mathrm{m}}h\in{B}^{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

On the other hand taking supremum over

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\theta^{k}h(z)}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\mathrm{m}\;\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})\frac{(j-\mathrm{m}\log z)^{l}}{\mathrm{m}^{l}}\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}\left(v-\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}\right)^{k-l}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\theta^{k}h(z)}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}^{l-1}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})(j-\mathrm{m}\log z)^{l}\frac{1}{(k-l+1)\mathrm{m}^{k-l+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\theta^{k}h(z)}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}^{l-1}}\frac{1}{(k-l+1)\mathrm{m}^{k-l+1}}\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})(j-\mathrm{m}\log z)^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\theta^{k}h(z)}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{1}{(k-l+1)\mathrm{m}^{k}}\;\mathcal{A}_{l}(\varkappa,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\theta^{k}h(z)}{k!\;\mathrm{m}^{k}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{1}{(k-l+1)}\;\mathcal{A}_{l}(\varkappa,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(z)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\frac{1}{\mathrm{m}}\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\theta^{k}h(z)}{k!\mathrm{m}^{k-1}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{1}{k-l+1}\mathcal{A}_{l}(\varkappa,z).

On the other-hand concerning
I_{2}
by the inequality

\left|{R}_{n}(h;v,z)\right|\leq\frac{64}{n!}(1+\log^{2}z)\Upsilon(\theta^{n}h,\rho)\left(|v-\log z|^{n}+\frac{|v-\log z|^{n+5}}{\rho^{5}}\right),

we have

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\frac{64\;\mathrm{m}}{n!}(1+\log^{2}z)\Upsilon(\theta^{n}h,\rho)\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}\left(|v-\log z|^{n}+\frac{|v-\log z|^{n+5}}{\rho^{5}}\right)dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\frac{64\;\mathrm{m}}{n!}(1+\log^{2}z)\Upsilon(\theta^{n}h,\rho)\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}\left|\left(v-\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}\right)+\left(\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}-\log z\right)\right|^{n}dv+\frac{1}{\rho^{5}}\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}\left|\left(v-\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}\right)+\left(\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}-\log z\right)\right|^{n+5}dv\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\frac{64\;\mathrm{m}}{n!}(1+\log^{2}z)\Upsilon(\theta^{n}h,\rho)\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})

\displaystyle\left[2^{n-1}\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}\left(\left|v-\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}\right|^{n}+\left|\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}-\log z\right|^{n}\right)dv+\frac{2^{n+4}}{\rho^{5}}\int_{\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}}^{\frac{j+1}{\mathrm{m}}}\left(\left|v-\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}\right|^{n+5}+\left|\frac{j}{\mathrm{m}}-\log z\right|^{n+5}\right)dv\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\frac{64\;\mathrm{m}}{n!}(1+\log^{2}z)\Upsilon(\theta^{n}h,\rho)\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})

\displaystyle\left[2^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)\mathrm{m}^{n+1}}+\frac{|j-\mathrm{m}\log z|^{n}}{\mathrm{m}^{n+1}}\right)+\frac{2^{n+4}}{\rho^{5}}\left(\frac{1}{(n+6)\mathrm{m}^{n+6}}+\frac{|j-\mathrm{m}\log z|^{n+5}}{\mathrm{m}^{n+6}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)}\frac{64\;\mathrm{m}\;2^{n-1}}{n!\mathrm{m}^{n+1}}(1+\log^{2}z)\Upsilon(\theta^{n}h,\rho)\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varkappa(e^{-j}z^{\mathrm{m}})

\displaystyle\left[\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)}+|j-\mathrm{m}\log z|^{n}\right)+\frac{2^{5}}{\delta^{5}\mathrm{m}^{5}}\left(\frac{1}{(n+6)}+|j-\mathrm{m}\log z|^{n+5}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n+5}}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)\;n!\mathrm{m}^{n}}(1+\log^{2}z)\Upsilon(\theta^{n}h,\rho)

\displaystyle\left[\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)}m_{0}(\varkappa)+m_{n}(\varkappa)\right)+\frac{2^{5}}{\rho^{5}\mathrm{m}^{5}}\left(\frac{1}{(n+6)}m_{0}(\varkappa)+m_{n+5}(\varkappa)\right)\right].

Now choosing
\rho=\mathrm{m}^{-1}

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n+5}}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\varkappa)\;n!\mathrm{m}^{n}}(1+\log^{2}z)\Upsilon(\theta^{n}h,\rho)

\displaystyle\left[\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)}m_{0}(\varkappa)+m_{n}(\varkappa)\right)+2^{5}\left(\frac{1}{(n+6)}m_{0}(\varkappa)+m_{n+5}(\varkappa)\right)\right].

This proves the result.

\Box

5.
Conclusions

In this paper, we studied the approximation properties of the Max-Product Kantrovich Exponential Sampling operator for functions in weighted spaces. We have proven the well-definedness of the operator and discuss its convergence, pointwise and in uniform sense. The weighted logarithmic modulus of continuity has been used to estimate the rate of convergence. Finally, we find that the choice of kernel function is a crucial factor in determining the accuracy of the approximation. We can get precise approximations for a large class of functions with an appropriate choice of the kernel and adjustments of the operator’s parameters.

Sample path properties and small ball probabilities for stochastic fractional diffusion equations
Yuhui Guo, Jian Song, Ran Wang, Yimin Xiao

Abstract
. We consider the following stochastic space-time fractional diffusion equation with vanishing initial condition: ∂ β u ⁡ ( t , x ) = − ( − Δ ) α / 2 ​ u ​ ( t , x ) + I 0 + γ ​ [ W ˙ ​ ( t , x ) ] , t ∈ [ 0 , T ] , x ∈ ℝ d , \partial^{\beta}u(t,x)=-\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha/2}u(t,x)+I_{0+}^{\gamma}\left[\dot{W}(t,x)\right],\quad t\in[0,T],\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}, where α > 0 \alpha>0 , β ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \beta\in(0,2) , γ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \gamma\in[0,1) , ( − Δ ) α / 2 \left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha/2} is the fractional/power of Laplacian and W ˙ \dot{W} is a fractional space-time Gaussian noise. We prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution and then focus on various sample path regularity properties of the solution. More specifically, we establish the exact uniform and local moduli of continuity and Chung’s laws of the iterated logarithm. The small ball probability is also studied.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following stochastic fractional diffusion equation (FDE, for short):

\begin{cases}\partial^{\beta}u(t,x)=-\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha/2}u(t,x)+I_{0+}^{\gamma}\left[\dot{W}(t,x)\right],&t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,\cdot)=0,&\text{ if }\beta\in(0,1],\\
u(0,\cdot)=0,\quad\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}u(0,\cdot)=0,&\text{ if }\beta\in(1,2),\end{cases}

(1.1)

with
\alpha>0
,
\beta\in(0,2)
, and
\gamma\in[0,1)
.

In the above equation,
\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha/2}
is
fractional
(
0<\alpha\leq 2
) or
power
of (
\alpha>2
)
Laplacian
in space.
\partial^{\beta}
denotes the
Caputo fractional differential
operator in time:

\partial^{\beta}f(t):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(n-\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f^{(n)}(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\beta+1-n}}\mathrm{d}\tau,&\mbox{if }\beta\neq n,\\
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}t^{n}}f(t),&\mbox{if }\beta=n,\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
n=\lceil\beta\rceil
is the smallest integer not smaller than
\beta
and

\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{x-1}\mathrm{d}t
is the Gamma function, and

I_{0_{+}}^{\gamma}
is the
Riemann-Liouville integral
in time:

(I_{0+}^{\gamma}f)(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{t}f(r)(t-r)^{\gamma-1}\mathrm{d}r.

In (
1.1
), the Gaussian noise
\dot{W}
is fractional with Hurst indices
H_{0}\in[1/2,1)
in time and

H_{1},\dots,H_{d}\in(0,1)
in space. Namely, the covariance function of the Gaussian noise
\dot{W}
is

\mathbb{E}[\dot{W}(t,x)\dot{W}(s,y)]=\Lambda_{H_{0}}(t-s)\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Lambda_{H_{j}}(x_{j}-y_{j}),

where for any
z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Lambda_{H}(z)=\begin{cases}H(2H-1)|z|^{2H-2},&\text{ if }H>1/2,\\
\delta_{0}(z),&\text{ if }H=1/2,\\
\dfrac{1}{2}\left(|z|^{2H}\right)^{\prime\prime},&\text{ if }H<1/2,\end{cases}

(1.3)

with
\delta_{0}
being the Dirac delta function and
(\cdot)^{\prime\prime}
being the second derivative in the sense of distribution.

The class of stochastic FDE (
1.1
) encompasses some well-known models as special cases, such as
stochastic (fractional) heat equations. Stochastic FDEs with multiplicative Gaussian noise have been studied in the
literature, and we refer to
[
9
,
10
,
12
,
15
,
19
]

for recent developments. Regarding path properties for stochastic FDEs with additive space-time white noise, the
case of
\alpha=2
,
\beta\in(0,1/2]
,
\gamma=1-\beta
has been studied by Allouba and Xiao
[
1
]
,
where (
1.1
) is also called a stochastic partial integro-differential equation. On the other hand, compared with the
space-time white noise, fractional Gaussian noise is more effective to describe correlated random perturbations in
biology, physics and finance, and there has been extensive research on SPDEs with fractional Gaussian noise in
recent two decades. We refer to
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
14
,
16
,
21
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
22
,
43
]
, which by no means is complete, for related results.

Path regularity of the solutions to some special cases of stochastic FDE (
1.1
) has been studied. For example,
for the solutions of stochastic heat equations driven by space-time colored noise, Tudor and Xiao
[
45
]

established exact moduli of continuity and Chung’s laws of the iterated logarithm (Chung’s LIL) in space and in time,
respectively; the results were extended in Herrell
et al.

[
21
]
to (fractional) heat equations,
where the exact moduli of continuity and Chung’s LIL were obtained in space and time simultaneously; in
[
31
]
,
Lee and Xiao obtained exact moduli of continuity and Chung’s LIL by establishing harmonizable-type integral representation
and strong local nondeterminism for the solution, improving the results in
[
45
,
21
]

by providing more precise information on the limiting constants. For (
1.1
) with
\alpha=2,\beta\in(0,1/2],\gamma=1-\beta
driven by the space-time white noise, Allouba and Xiao
[
1
]
studied the temporal
and spatial regularity of the solution.

In this paper, we study the path regularity of the solution
\{u(t,x),t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
to stochastic FDE (
1.1
). More precisely,
we study temporal, spatial, and joint regularity properties of the solution
u(t,x)
, including the exact uniform and local moduli
of continuity, Chung’s LIL, and small ball probabilities. These results generalize and strengthen the related results in

[
1
,
21
,
45
]
. To analyze the path regularity, we decompose the solution process
into the sum of two Gaussian random fields: one with stationary increments and the other with smooth paths. For the stationary-increment
term, we derive an explicit expression for its spectral measure and then prove the property of strong local nondeterminism.
For the smooth term, we prove that its sample path is smoother in space and in time, respectively than that of the stationary-increment
term. Under a stronger condition, we show that the sample path of the smooth term is even almost
surely continuously differentiable. Note that our results are consistent with the known results when
(
1.1
) reduces, for instance, to a (fractional) stochastic heat equation.

In recent years, the small ball probability problem for SPDEs with multiplicative noise has attracted a lot of attention in probability
and related areas. Considering the stochastic heat equation with space-time white noise, Athreya
et al.

[
2

and Foondun
et al.

[
18
]
studied the small ball probabilities under the uniform norm and a Hölder
semi-norm, respectively. Khoshnevisan
et al.

[
26

obtained the existence of a small-ball constant under
the uniform norm. Recently, Chen
[
8
]
investigated the small ball probabilities for the stochastic heat equation
with Gaussian noise which is white in time and colored in space. We also refer to Li and Shao
[
33
]
for a survey on
small ball probabilities for general Gaussian processes.

2.
Existence of the solution

2.1.
The fundamental solution of the fractional diffusion equation

Consider the following deterministic fractional diffusion equation:

\begin{cases}\partial^{\beta}v(t,x)=-\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha/2}v(t,x)+I_{0+}^{\gamma}\left[f(t,x)\right],&t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
v(0,\cdot)=0,&\text{ if }\beta\in(0,1],\\
v(0,\cdot)=0,\quad\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v(0,\cdot)=0,&\text{ if }\beta\in(1,2),\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
\alpha>0
,
\beta\in(0,2)
, and
\gamma\in[0,1)
are constants.
According to
[
12
, Theorem 2.8]
, the fundamental solution to (
2.1
) is

p(t,x):=\pi^{-d/2}|x|^{-d}t^{\beta+\gamma-1}\times H_{2,3}^{2,1}\left(\frac{|x|^{\alpha}}{2^{\alpha}t^{\beta}}\>\middle|\>\begin{subarray}{l}(1,1),\>(\beta+\gamma,\beta)\\[3.99994pt]
(d/2,\alpha/2),\>(1,1),\>(1,\alpha/2)\end{subarray}\right),\quad\text{for }t>0,

(2.2)

where
H^{m,n}_{p,q}(z)
is the Fox
H
-function (see, e.g.,
[
27
]
) and
p(t,x)\equiv 0
for
t\leq 0
.

The Fourier transform
of
p(t,x)
in space is given by

\mathcal{F}p(t,\cdot)(\xi)=t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-t^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big),

(2.3)

where
E_{a,b}(z)
is the Mittag-Leffler function (see, e.g.,
[
40
, Sect.1.2]
)

E_{a,b}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(ak+b)}\,.

(2.4)

Remark 2.1
.

In the literature, it is often assumed that
\alpha\in(0,2]
. In this case, the fractional Laplacian
-\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha/2}

is the infinitesimal generator of the
\alpha
-stable process
X:=\{X_{t},t\geq 0\}
with characteristic function

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(i\langle\xi,X_{t}\rangle\right)\right]=\exp\left(-t|\xi|^{\alpha}\right)
. As a consequence, assuming
\beta=1
and

\gamma=0
, the fundamental solution
p(t,x)
is the transition density of
X
and hence one may apply probability results
of
X
and/or the semigroup theory to study the corresponding SPDEs.

In contrast, when
\alpha>2
, the fundamental solution
p(t,x)
in general is not necessarily nonnegative (see
[
11
, Remark 1.2]
or

[
12
, Remark 5.3]
) and hence does not correspond to any stochastic process. This may cause extra difficulties in general studies of
SPDEs for the case
\alpha>2
. On the other hand, for all
\alpha>0
, the fundamental solution
p(t,x)
has a unified Fourier transform (
2.3
)
and this allows us to study the path properties of the solutions by using Fourier analysis. It is also worth mentioning that this natural extension from
\alpha\in(0,2]
to all
\alpha>0
enables us to cover more physical models such as the beam equation (
\alpha=4
).

For any
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},a,b,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following identity holds (see

[
28
, (1.10.7)]
):

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{n}\left(z^{b-1}E_{a,b}(\lambda z^{\alpha})\right)=z^{b-n-1}E_{a,b-n}(\lambda z^{\alpha}).

(2.5)

When
a\in(0,2)
, the Mittag-Leffler function has the following asymptotic property (
[
28
, (1.8.28)]
): for

n\in\mathbb{N}
,

E_{a,b}(z)=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\Gamma(b-ak)\cdot z^{k}}+o\left(\frac{1}{z^{n}}\right),\text{ as }z\to-\infty.

(2.6)

We will use the convention (see, e.g.,
[
39
, (5.2.1) on p. 136]
)

\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\equiv 0,\quad\text{for $z=0,-1,-2,\dots$}.

(2.7)

The following property is a direct consequence of (
2.6
) (see also
[
40
, Theorem 1.6]
): for
all
a\in(0,2)
and
z<0
,

|E_{a,b}(z)|\leq\dfrac{\hat{c}}{1+|z|},

(2.8)

for some positive constant
\hat{c}
.

2.2.
The solution of the stochastic FDE

Let
\mathcal{D}\big(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
denote the space of real-valued infinitely differentiable functions with compact support in

\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
W
be a zero-mean Gaussian family
\{W(\varphi),\varphi\in\mathcal{D}\big(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)\}
whose
covariance structure is given by

\mathbb{E}[W(\varphi)W(\psi)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(t,\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(s,\xi)}\Lambda_{H_{0}}(t-s)\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s,

(2.9)

where
\Lambda_{H_{0}}
is defined in (
1.3
) and the measure
\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}ud\xi
with
c_{H_{j}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Gamma(2H_{j}+1)\sin(\pi H_{j})
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the completion of
\mathcal{D}\big(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
with
the inner product

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathbb{E}[W(\varphi)W(\psi)].

(2.10)

Now, the mapping
\varphi\mapsto W(\varphi)
can be extended to all
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
and it is called the Wiener integral. For
each
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, we also write
W(\varphi)
in the form of integral

W(\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(t,x)W(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}x).

We define the solution of the equation (
1.1
) as follows, adopted from
[
5
]
:

Definition 2.2
.

A jointly measurable process
\{u(t,x);(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is said to be a solution of the stochastic Cauchy problem
(
1.1
), if for any
\varphi(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}\big((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})(t,x)W(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}x),\quad\text{a.s.}

(2.11)

Here, we recall that
\tilde{p}(t,x):=p(-t,-x)
and the symbol
\ast
denotes the convolution.

In the following theorem, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
1.1
).

Theorem 2.3
.

Denote
g_{t,x}(s,y):=p(t-s,x-y)
and
H:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}H_{j}
. If the condition

\displaystyle\alpha+\dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}\min\left(\gamma+H_{0}-1,0\right)>d-H

(2.12)

holds, then
\|g_{t,x}\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty
for any
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In this case, (
1.1
) has a unique solution

\{u(t,x);(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
given by

u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t-s,x-y)W(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y).

(2.13)

Proof.

The uniqueness of the solution is a direct consequence of the definition (
2.11
). Now we prove that

u(t,x)
given by (
2.13
) is well-defined and it satisfies (
2.11
). By using (
2.10
),
(
2.3
), Lemma
A.1
and Minkowski’s inequality, there exists a positive constant
C_{H_{0}}
such that

\displaystyle\|g_{t,x}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}p(t-s,x-\cdot)(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}p(t-r,x-\cdot)(\xi)}|s-r|^{2H_{0}-2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\Big|\mathcal{F}p(t-s,x-\cdot)(\xi)\Big|^{\frac{1}{H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{2H_{0}}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{H_{0}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\mathcal{F}p(t-s,x-\cdot)(\xi)\Big|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{2H_{0}}

\displaystyle=C_{H_{0}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\bigg((t-s)^{2\beta+2\gamma-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}^{2}\big(-(t-s)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\mathrm{d}\xi\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{2H_{0}}

\displaystyle=C_{H_{0}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\bigg((t-s)^{2\beta+2\gamma-2-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(2d-2H)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{2H_{0}}\ \times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}^{2}\big(-|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\mathrm{d}\xi,

where we have used the change of variables
\tilde{\xi_{j}}=(t-s)^{\beta/\alpha}\xi_{j}
in the last step. By (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|g_{t,x}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\hat{c}C_{H_{0}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\bigg((t-s)^{2\beta+2\gamma-2-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(2d-2H)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{2H_{0}}\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi.

Then, applying Lemma
A.2
, we have
\|g_{t,x}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}<\infty
under the condition (
2.12
).

Next, to validate (
2.11
), we will show that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}\big((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
,

\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x-W(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})\right|^{2}=0,

by verifying that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\right|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left(W(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\right)=\mathbb{E}\left|W(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})\right|^{2}.

Using (
2.10
) and (
2.13
), we see that

\mathbb{E}\left[u(t,x)u(s,y)\right]=\mathbb{E}[W(g_{t,x})W(g_{s,y})]=\langle g_{t,x},g_{s,y}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

(2.14)

and

\mathbb{E}\left[W(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})u(t,x)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[W(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})W(g_{t,x})\right]=\langle\varphi\ast\tilde{p},g_{t,x}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.15)

By Fubini’s theorem and (
2.14
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{[0,T]^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi(t,x)\varphi(s,y)\langle g_{t,x},g_{s,y}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{[0,T]^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi(t,x)\varphi(s,y)\bigg(\int_{[0,T]^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}g_{t,x}(r_{1},\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}g_{s,y}(r_{2},\xi)}\Lambda_{H_{0}}(r_{1}-r_{2})\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{[0,T]^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int_{[0,T]^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi(t,x)\varphi(s,y)\mathcal{F}g_{t,x}(r_{1},\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}g_{s,y}(r_{2},\xi)}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg)\Lambda_{H_{0}}(r_{1}-r_{2})\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{[0,T]^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})(r_{1},\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})(r_{2},\xi)}\Lambda_{H_{0}}(r_{1}-r_{2})\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\|\varphi\ast\tilde{p}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Similarly, by Fubini’s theorem and (
2.15
), we also have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[W(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\varphi\ast\tilde{p},g_{t,x}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\>\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int_{[0,T]^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})(r_{1},\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}g_{t,x}(r_{2},\xi)}\Lambda_{H_{0}}(r_{1}-r_{2})\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}\bigg)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{[0,T]^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})(r_{1},\xi)\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\mathcal{F}g_{t,x}(r_{2},\xi)}\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\bigg)\Lambda_{H_{0}}(r_{1}-r_{2})\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{[0,T]^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})(r_{1},\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})(r_{2},\xi)}\Lambda_{H_{0}}(r_{1}-r_{2})\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\|\varphi\ast\tilde{p}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x-W(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\right|^{2}-2\mathbb{E}\left[W(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\right]+\mathbb{E}\left|W(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi\ast\tilde{p}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-2\|\varphi\ast\tilde{p}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left|W(\varphi\ast\tilde{p})\right|^{2}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

. Note that

2.12
) is also shown to be necessary in
[
21
, Theorem 2.2]
for the fractional stochastic heat
equation.

Proposition 2.5
.

When the noise is white in time (i.e.
H_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
), the condition (
2.12
) is also necessary.

Proof.

When
H_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
, by (
2.3
) and a change of variables
\tilde{\xi_{j}}=(t-s)^{\beta/\alpha}\xi_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\|g_{t,x}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\mathcal{F}p(t-s,x-\cdot)(\xi)\Big|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta+2\gamma-2}\mathrm{d}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}^{2}\big(-(t-s)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta+2\gamma-2-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(2d-2H)}\mathrm{d}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}^{2}\big(-|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\mathrm{d}\xi.

(2.16)

For the second integral, by the change of variables
\xi_{j}=rw_{j}
in (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}^{2}\big(-|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}^{2}\big(-r^{\alpha}\big)r^{2d-2H-1}\mathrm{d}r\times\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|w_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\sigma(\mathrm{d}w),

where
\sigma(\mathrm{d}w)
is the uniform measure on the
(d-1)
-dimensional unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and the second integral
is finite due to Lemma
A.2
. Then, by (
2.2
), we know that
\|g_{t,x}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}<\infty
is equivalent to

\begin{cases}\displaystyle 2\beta+2\gamma-2-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(2d-2H)>-1,\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}^{2}\big(-r^{\alpha}\big)r^{2d-2H-1}\mathrm{d}r<\infty.\end{cases}

(2.17)

Notice that
E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}^{2}\big(-|\cdot|^{\alpha}\big)|\cdot|^{2d-2H-1}
is locally integrable. By the asymptotic property
(
2.6
), as
r\to\infty
,

E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-r^{\alpha}\big)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)r^{\alpha}}+o(r^{-1}).

(2.18)

For the case
\gamma>0
, the condition (
2.17
) is equivalent to

\begin{cases}2\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(2\gamma-1)>2d-2H,\\
2\alpha>2d-2H,\end{cases}

(2.19)

which is straightforward by (
2.18
).

For the case
\gamma=0
, the condition (
2.17
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle 2\alpha-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}>2d-2H,

(2.20)

where the equivalence follows from the fact that the first condition
2\alpha-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}>2d-2H
in (
2.17
)
implies the second condition
\int_{0}^{\infty}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}^{2}\big(-r^{\alpha}\big)r^{2d-2H-1}\mathrm{d}r<\infty
by
(
2.18
) and (
2.7
).

Therefore, combining (
2.19
) with (
2.20
), we obtain that the condition (
2.17
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle 2\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\min\left(2\gamma-1,0\right)>2d-2H.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Regularity of the solution

In this section, we investigate the sample path regularity of the solution to stochastic FDE (
1.1
) in space-time
(Theorem
3.7
), in time (Theorem
3.12
), and in space (Theorem
3.17
),
respectively.

3.1.
Space-time joint regularity

In this subsection, we study the space-time joint regularity of
u(t,x)
, see Theorem
3.7
. Similar to
the string processes studied in Mueller and Tribe
[
37
]
(see also
[
21
,
45
]
),
the solution
\{u(t,x),(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
has the following decomposition

u(t,x)=U(t,x)-V(t,x),

(3.1)

where for
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

\begin{split}U(t,x)=&\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[p(t-r,x-y)-p(-r,-y)\right]W(\mathrm{d}r,\mathrm{d}y)\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t-r,x-y)W(\mathrm{d}r,\mathrm{d}y)\end{split}

(3.2)

and

V(t,x)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[p(t-r,x-y)-p(-r,-y)]W(\mathrm{d}r,\mathrm{d}y).

(3.3)

Notice that if we rewrite
U(t,x)
in (
3.2
) as

U(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[p\left((t-r)_{+},x-y\right)-p\left((-r)_{+},-y\right)]W(\mathrm{d}r,\mathrm{d}y),

(3.4)

where
a_{+}=\max\{0,a\}
for every
a\in\mathbb{R}
, then
U(t,x)
is well defined for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We will show that the sample path of the random field
V(t,x)
is smoother than that of
U(t,x)
on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
hence the regularity properties of
u(t,x)
on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
solely depend on those of
U(t,x)
. To derive the sharp
regularity, we first prove that the random field
\{U(t,x),(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
has stationary increments and it satisfies the property
of strong local nondeterminism (SLND, for short) (see Lemma
3.1
). Then, we show the sample path of
V(t,x)
is
smoother than that of
U(t,x)
on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
in Proposition
3.4
and Proposition
3.5
.
Finally, we obtain the joint regularity in Theorem
3.7
for the solution
u(t,x)
.

To study the regularity properties of the Gaussian random field
\{U(t,x),(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\}

we recall some facts about Gaussian random fields with stationary increments. A real-valued random
field
X=\{X(\boldsymbol{t}),\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\}
is
said to have stationary increments if for every
\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\{X(\boldsymbol{t}+h)-X(\boldsymbol{h}),\,\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\}\overset{d}{=}\{X(\boldsymbol{t})-X(0),\,\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\},

where
\overset{d}{=}
means equality in distribution. According to Yaglom
[
47
]
, if
X
satisfies
X(0)=0

and its covariance function
R(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}[X(\boldsymbol{t})X(\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime})]
is continuous, then
R(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime})
can be represented as

R(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(e^{i\langle\boldsymbol{t},\xi\rangle}-1\right)\left(e^{-i\langle\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime},\xi\rangle}-1\right)F(\mathrm{d}\xi)+\langle\tau,Q\tau^{\prime}\rangle,

(3.5)

where
Q=(q_{ij})
is an
N\times N
non-negative definite matrix and
F(\mathrm{d}\xi)
is a nonnegative symmetric measure on

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}F(\mathrm{d}\xi)<\infty.

(3.6)

The measure
F
and its density
f(\xi)
(if it exists) are called the spectral measure and spectral density of
X
, respectively.
The converse is also true. Namely, if a Gaussian random field
X
satisfies
X(0)=0
and its covariance function is given by (
3.5
)
with
F
satisfying (
3.6
), then
X
has stationary increments with spectral measure
F
. We will apply these facts to

\{U(t,x),(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
defined by (
3.4
). In this case,
\boldsymbol{t}=(t,x)
and
N=1+d
.

Denote

\begin{split}&\theta_{1}:=\beta+\gamma+H_{0}-1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(d-H);\\
&\theta_{2}:=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\theta_{1}=\alpha-d+H+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(\gamma+H_{0}-1).\end{split}

(3.7)

Clearly, both
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are strictly positive under the condition (
2.12
).

The following proposition is a key ingredient to study the sample path regularity of
U(t,x)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume the condition (
2.12
) holds and

\begin{cases}\theta_{1}<1,\\
\theta_{2}<1,\\
\gamma<1-H_{0}.\end{cases}

(3.8)

(1)

The Gaussian random field
U=\{U(t,x),(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
has stationary increments with spectral measure

F_{U}(\mathrm{d}\tau,\mathrm{d}\xi)=\frac{C_{H_{0}}|\tau|^{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}}{|\tau|^{2\beta}+2|\xi|^{\alpha}|\tau|^{\beta}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.9)

(2)

The Gaussian random field
U
satisfies the strong local nondeterminism property: there exists
a constant
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(t,x),(t^{1},x^{1}),\dots,(t^{n},x^{n})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathrm{Var}\big(U(t,x)|U(t^{1},x^{1}),\dots,U(t^{n},x^{n})\big)\geq C\min_{k=0,1,\dots,n}\bigg(|t-t^{k}|^{\theta_{1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}-x^{k}_{i}|^{\theta_{2}}\bigg)^{2},

(3.10)

where
t^{0}=x^{0}_{i}=0
,
i=1,\dots,d
.

(3)

There exist constants
c,C>0
such that

c\left(|t-s|^{2\theta_{1}}+|x-y|^{2\theta_{2}}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(s,y)|^{2}\right]\leq C\left(|t-s|^{2\theta_{1}}+|x-y|^{2\theta_{2}}\right)

(3.11)

for all
(t,x),(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Step 1:
Let
(t,x),(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By (
2.9
) and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(s,y)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[p\left((t-r)_{+},x-z\right)-p\left((s-r)_{+},y-z\right)]W(\mathrm{d}r,\mathrm{d}z)\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|r_{1}-r_{2}|^{2H_{0}-2}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\left[p\left((t-r_{1})_{+},x-\cdot\right)-p\left((s-r_{1})_{+},y-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)

\displaystyle\quad\times\overline{\mathcal{F}\left[p\left((t-r_{2})_{+},x-\cdot\right)-p\left((s-r_{2})_{+},y-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi),

where by (
2.3
),

\mathcal{F}p\left((t-r)_{+},x-\cdot\right)(\xi)=e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big){{\rm 1\mkern-1.5mu}\!{\rm I}}_{\{t-r>0\}}.

Denote

\displaystyle\phi(r,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{F}\left[p\left((t-r)_{+},x-\cdot\right)-p\left((s-r)_{+},y-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big){{\rm 1\mkern-1.5mu}\!{\rm I}}_{\{t-r>0\}}

\displaystyle\quad-e^{-i\langle y,\xi\rangle}(s-r)^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-(s-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big){{\rm 1\mkern-1.5mu}\!{\rm I}}_{\{s-r>0\}}.

Using the Parseval–Plancherel identity, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(s,y)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|r_{1}-r_{2}|^{2H_{0}-2}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(r_{1},\xi)\overline{\phi(r_{2},\xi)}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle=C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\hat{\phi}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}|\tau|^{1-2H_{0}}\mathrm{d}\tau,

with

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(\tau,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ir\tau}\phi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-ir\tau}(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad-e^{-i\langle y,\xi\rangle}\int_{-\infty}^{s}e^{-ir\tau}(s-r)^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-(s-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=e^{-i(\langle x,\xi\rangle+\tau t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{iv\tau}v^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-v^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\quad-e^{-i(\langle y,\xi\rangle+\tau s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{iv\tau}v^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-v^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\left(e^{-i(\langle x,\xi\rangle+\tau t)}-e^{-i(\langle y,\xi\rangle+\tau s)}\right)\frac{(-i\tau)^{-\gamma}}{(-i\tau)^{\beta}+|\xi|^{\alpha}},

where we have used Lemma
A.3
in the last step. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(s,y)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\left|e^{-i(\langle x,\xi\rangle+\tau t)}-e^{-i(\langle y,\xi\rangle+\tau s)}\right|^{2}\left|\frac{(-i\tau)^{-\gamma}}{(-i\tau)^{\beta}+|\xi|^{\alpha}}\right|^{2}|\tau|^{1-2H_{0}}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\left|e^{-i(\langle x,\xi\rangle+\tau t)}-e^{-i(\langle y,\xi\rangle+\tau s)}\right|^{2}\frac{|\tau|^{-2\gamma}}{|\tau|^{2\beta}+2|\xi|^{\alpha}|\tau|^{\beta}\mathfrak{R}[(-i)^{\beta}]+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}|\tau|^{1-2H_{0}}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\big[1-\cos\big(\langle x-y,\xi\rangle+(t-s)\tau\big)\big]\frac{|\tau|^{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}}{|\tau|^{2\beta}+2|\xi|^{\alpha}|\tau|^{\beta}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\mathrm{d}\tau,

(3.12)

which only depends on
t-s
and
x-y
. Therefore,
U(t,x)
has stationary increments with the spectral measure given by (
3.9
).

Step 2:
Next, we prove the SLND property. By (
3.9
), we have the spectral density

f_{U}(\tau,\xi)=\frac{2C_{H_{0}}|\tau|^{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(c_{j}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}})}{|\tau|^{2\beta}+2|\xi|^{\alpha}|\tau|^{\beta}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}.

Then for every
c>0
, we have

\displaystyle f_{U}(c^{1/\theta_{1}}\tau,c^{1/\theta_{2}}\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,c^{\frac{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{d-2H}{\theta_{2}}}\frac{2C_{H_{0}}|\tau|^{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(c_{j}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}})}{c^{\frac{2\beta}{\theta_{1}}}|\tau|^{2\beta}+c^{\frac{\beta}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{\alpha}{\theta_{2}}}2|\xi|^{\alpha}|\tau|^{\beta}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+c^{\frac{2\alpha}{\theta_{2}}}|\xi|^{2\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,c^{\frac{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{d-2H}{\theta_{2}}-\frac{2\beta}{\theta_{1}}}f_{U}(\tau,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,c^{\frac{2-2\beta-2\gamma-2H_{0}}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{2d-2H}{\theta_{2}}-\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}-\frac{d}{\theta_{2}}}f_{U}(\tau,\xi)=c^{-(2+Q)}f_{U}(\tau,\xi),

where
Q=\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{d}{\theta_{2}}
with
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
being given in (
3.7
). The SLND property follows
directly from Theorem 4.1 in
[
21
, Appendix A]
.

Step 3:
By (
3.1
), using the change of variables
\xi=|\tau|^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\tilde{\xi}
and
\tilde{\tau}=(t-s)\tau
,
and then changing the notations
\tilde{\xi}
and
\tilde{\tau}
back to
\xi
and
\tau
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(s,x)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\big[1-\cos\big((t-s)\tau\big)\big]\frac{|\tau|^{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{|\tau|^{2\beta}+2|\xi|^{\alpha}|\tau|^{\beta}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\frac{1-\cos\big((t-s)\tau\big)}{|\tau|^{2\beta+2\gamma+2H_{0}-1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(2d-2H)}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+2|\xi|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\tau

(3.13)

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}|t-s|^{2\theta_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-\cos\big(\tau\big)}{|\tau|^{2\theta_{1}+1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+2|\xi|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi,

where the first integral is finite owing to
\theta_{1}\in(0,1)
and the second integral

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+2|\xi|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{\left(1+|\xi|^{2\alpha}\right)\wedge\left(\left[1+\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)\right]\times\left(1+|\xi|^{2\alpha}\right)\right)}\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&\leq\frac{1}{1\wedge\left[1+\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)\right]}\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi,\end{split}

(3.14)

which is also finite noting that
\alpha>d-H
under (
2.12
) by Lemma
A.2
(i). Then,

\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(s,x)|^{2}\right]=C_{1}|t-s|^{2\theta_{1}},

(3.15)

where

C_{1}=2C_{H_{0}}\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-\cos\big(\tau\big)}{|\tau|^{2\theta_{1}+1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+2|\xi|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi.

By (
3.1
) and using the change of variables
{\tau}=|\xi|^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\tilde{\tau}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(t,y)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\big[1-\cos\big(\langle x-y,\xi\rangle\big)\big]\frac{|\tau|^{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{|\tau|^{2\beta}+2|\xi|^{\alpha}|\tau|^{\beta}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\big[1-\cos\big(\langle x-y,\xi\rangle\big)\big]\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{|\xi|^{2\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(2\gamma+2H_{0}-2)}}\frac{|\tau|^{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}}{|\tau|^{2\beta}+2|\tau|^{\beta}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+1}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\big[1-\cos\big(\langle x-y,\xi\rangle\big)\big]\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{|\xi|^{2\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(2\gamma+2H_{0}-2)}}\mathrm{d}\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\tau|^{{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}}}{|\tau|^{2\beta}+2|\tau|^{\beta}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+1}\mathrm{d}\tau.

The last condition in (
3.8
) implies
1-2\gamma-2H_{0}>-1
and (
2.12
) implies
2\beta+2H_{0}+2\gamma-1>1
. Thus,
the last integral in the above equation is finite, namely,

\begin{split}c_{3,1}&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\tau|^{{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}}}{|\tau|^{2\beta}+2|\tau|^{\beta}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+1}\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\leq\frac{1}{1\wedge\left[1+\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)\right]}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\tau|^{{1-2\gamma-2H_{0}}}}{|\tau|^{2\beta}+1}\mathrm{d}\tau<\infty.\end{split}

(3.16)

Then, using changes of variables
\xi_{j}=rw_{j}
in (
A.1
) and
w_{x-y}=\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(t,y)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}c_{3,1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\big[1-\cos\big(\langle x-y,\xi\rangle\big)\big]\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{|\xi|^{2\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(2\gamma+2H_{0}-2)}}\mathrm{d}\xi

(3.17)

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}c_{3,1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|w_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\sigma(\mathrm{d}w)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos\big(r|x-y|\langle w_{x-y},w\rangle\big)}{r^{2\alpha-2d+2H+1+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(2\gamma+2H_{0}-2)}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}c_{3,1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|w_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\sigma(\mathrm{d}w)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos\big(r|x-y|\langle w_{x-y},w\rangle\big)}{r^{2\theta_{2}+1}}\mathrm{d}r

and Minkowski’s inequality, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}V(s,x)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|r_{1}-r_{2}|^{2H_{0}-2}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p\left(t-r_{1},x-\cdot\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}p\left(s-r_{1},x-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)

\displaystyle\quad\times\overline{\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p\left(t-r_{2},x-\cdot\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}p\left(s-r_{2},x-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi).

\displaystyle\leq C_{H_{0}}\bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg|\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p\left(t-r,x-\cdot\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}p\left(s-r,x-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)\bigg|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}r\bigg)^{2H_{0}}.

(3.29)

Using Lemma
A.7
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p\left(t-r,x-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathcal{F}p(t-r,x-\cdot)(\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-2}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma-1}\big(-(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big),

where the last step is due to (
2.5
).
By using (
2.5
) again, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p\left(t-r,x-\cdot\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}p\left(s-r,x-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-2}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma-1}\big(-(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)-(s-r)^{\beta+\gamma-2}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma-1}\big(-(s-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\delta\in[a,b]}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\delta}\left[(\delta-r)^{\beta+\gamma-2}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma-1}\big(-(\delta-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\right]\right|\times|t-s|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\delta\in[a,b]}\left|(\delta-r)^{\beta+\gamma-3}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma-2}\big(-(\delta-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\right|\times|t-s|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{C(a-r)^{\beta+\gamma-3}}{1+(a-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}}\right|\times|t-s|.

(3.30)

Recall that
c_{1}
is given in (
3.25
). Combining (
3.1
) with (
3.1
), we can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}V(s,x)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{H_{0}}\hat{c}^{2}\bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{(a-r)^{\beta+\gamma-3}}{1+(a-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}}\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\mathrm{d}\xi\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}r\bigg)^{2H_{0}}\times|t-s|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{H_{0}}\hat{c}^{2}\bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{0}(a-r)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}\left[2\beta+2\gamma-6-\frac{\beta(2d-2H)}{\alpha}\right]}\mathrm{d}r\bigg)^{2H_{0}}\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi\times|t-s|^{2}

\displaystyle=C_{H_{0}}\>\hat{c}^{2}\times a^{2\beta+2\gamma+2H_{0}-6-\frac{\beta(2d-2H)}{\alpha}}\times c_{1}|t-s|^{2},

where
2\beta+2\gamma+2H_{0}-6-\frac{\beta(2d-2H)}{\alpha}<0
due to (
3.8
), and
c_{1}<\infty
due to Lemma
A.2
and (
2.12
).
Therefore, for all
s,t\in[a,b]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}V(s,x)\right|^{2}\right]\leq C|t-s|^{2}.

(3.31)

Similarly, we can verify that for
t\in[a,b]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(t,y)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{H_{0}}\bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg|\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p\left(t-r,x-\cdot\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p\left(t-r,y-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)\bigg|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}r\bigg)^{2H_{0}},

where

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p\left(t-r,x-\cdot\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p\left(t-r,y-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg|(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-2}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma-1}\big(-(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\bigg|^{2}\times\left|e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}-e^{-i\langle y,\xi\rangle}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-2}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma-1}\big(-(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\bigg|^{2}\times|\xi|^{2}|x-y|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\frac{(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-2}}{1+(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}}\bigg|^{2}\times|\xi|^{2}|x-y|^{2}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(t,y)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{H_{0}}\bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg|\frac{(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-2}}{1+(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}}\bigg|^{2}\times|\xi|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}r\bigg)^{2H_{0}}\times|x-y|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C|x-y|^{2},

(3.32)

where
C<\infty
due to conditions (
3.8
) and (
3.28
).

Therefore, combining (
3.31
) with (
3.1
), we see that for any
(t,x),(s,y)\in[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}V(s,y)\right|^{2}\right]\leq C\left(|t-s|^{2}+|x-y|^{2}\right).

Then by Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem, we can find a modification of
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(t,x)
such that its sample function is continuous in
I
.

Step 2:
Next, we prove that the mean square partial derivatives of
V(t,x)
in
x_{i},i=1,\dots,d
, have continuous modifications. For
i=1,\dots,d
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}V(t,x)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p(t-r,x-z)W(\mathrm{d}r,\mathrm{d}z),

is well-defined under (
2.12
) and (
3.8
). Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}V(t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}V(t,y)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|r_{1}-r_{2}|^{2H_{0}-2}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p\left(t-r_{1},x-\cdot\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}p\left(t-r_{1},y-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)

\displaystyle\quad\times\overline{\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p\left(t-r_{2},x-\cdot\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}p\left(t-r_{2},y-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{H_{0}}\bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg|\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p\left(t-r,x-\cdot\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}p\left(t-r,y-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)\bigg|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}\xi)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}r\bigg)^{2H_{0}}.

Note that (
3.28
) implies
\alpha>1
. According to Lemma
A.7
, the Fourier transform is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p\left(t-r,x-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\mathcal{F}p\left(t-r,x-\cdot\right)(\xi)

\displaystyle=-i\xi_{i}e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big),

and

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p\left(t-r,x-\cdot\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}p\left(t-r,y-\cdot\right)\right](\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\xi_{i}(t-r)^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-(t-r)^{\beta}|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)\right|^{2}\times\left|e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}-e^{-i\langle y,\xi\rangle}\right|^{2}.

Now, we separate the condition (
3.28
) into three cases:

(i)

1<\alpha-d+H<2
;

(ii)

\alpha-d+H=2
;

(iii)

\alpha-d+H>2
.

Let

\sigma(r)=\begin{cases}r^{2\alpha-2d+2H-2},&\mbox{if }1<\alpha-d+H<2;\\
r^{2}|\log r|,&\mbox{if }\alpha-d+H=2;\\
r^{2},&\mbox{if }\alpha-d+H>2.\end{cases}

Using a similar argument in the proof of Proposition
3.4
, we can obtain that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}V(t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}V(t,y)\right|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma(|x-y|),

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}V(t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}V(s,x)\right|^{2}\right]\leq C|t-s|^{2}.

Consequently,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}V(t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}V(s,y)\right|^{2}\right]\leq C\left(|t-s|^{2}+\sigma|x-y|\right).

By the Gaussian property of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}V(t,x)
and Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem, we can find a modification of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}V(t,x)
such that its sample function is continuous on
I
.

Step 3:
We will construct a modification of
V(t,x)
such that its sample function has continuous partial derivatives almost surely. In Step 1, we derive that there is a continuous version
\tilde{V}
of
V
such that
\frac{\partial\tilde{V}}{\partial t}(t)
is continuous. Then, we apply Step 2 to
x_{1}
and obtain a version
V^{(1)}
of
\tilde{V}
defined by

V^{(1)}(t,x)=\tilde{V}(t,-M,x_{2},\dots,x_{d})+\int_{-M}^{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\tilde{V}(t,y,x_{2},\dots,x_{d})\mathrm{d}y.

Then,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V^{(1)}(t,x)
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}V^{(1)}(t,x)
are almost surely continuous. Repeating this procedure for
x_{i},i=2,\dots,d
, we get a continuous version
V^{(d)}(t,x)
such that all first-order partial derivatives of
V^{(d)}(t,x)
are continuous almost surely. Hence, the sample function of
V^{(d)}(t,x)
is almost surely differentiable. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Indeed, the condition (
3.28
) is not necessary for Proposition
3.5
in case of the stochastic heat equation (see also
[
21
, Theorem 3.6]
), since the Fourier transform of the fundamental solution has exponential decay in this case (i.e.,
\beta=1
and
\gamma=0
):

\mathcal{F}p(t,\cdot)(\xi)=E_{1,1}\big(-t|\xi|^{\alpha}\big)=e^{-t|\xi|^{\alpha}}.

While for the other cases (i.e.,
\beta\neq 1
or
\gamma\neq 0
), the Fourier transform of the fundamental solution only has polynomial decay by (
2.6
):

E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}(-t|\xi|^{\alpha})=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)t|\xi|^{\alpha}}-\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma-\beta)t^{2}|\xi|^{2\alpha}}+o\left(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\right),\quad\text{as }\xi\to\infty.

Thus, when
\beta\neq 1
or
\gamma\neq 0
, the condition (
3.28
) is needed for Proposition
3.5
.

Now, we state the main result in this subsection.

Theorem 3.7

(Space-time joint regularity)
.

Assume the conditions of Proposition
3.5
hold. Denote
I=[a,b]\times[-M,M]^{d}
with
0<a<b<\infty
and
0<M<\infty
. Denote
\rho(t,x)=|t|^{\theta_{1}}+|x|^{\theta_{2}}
.

(1)

(Uniform modulus of continuity). There exists a constant
k_{1}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x),(s,y)\in I;\\
\rho(t-s,x-y)\leq\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\dfrac{|u(t,x)-u(s,y)|}{\rho(t-s,x-y)\sqrt{\log\left(1+\rho(t-s,x-y)^{-1}\right)}}=k_{1},\text{ a.s. }

(2)

(Local modulus of continuity). There exists a constant
k_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
(t,x)\in I
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{\rho(s,y)\leq\varepsilon}\dfrac{|u(t+s,x+y)-u(t,x)|}{\rho(s,y)\sqrt{\log\log\left(1+\rho(s,y)^{-1}\right)}}=k_{2},\text{ a.s. }

(3)

(Chung’s LIL). There exists a constant
k_{3}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
(t,x)\in I
,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{\rho(s,y)\leq\varepsilon}\dfrac{|u(t+s,x+y)-u(t,x)|}{\varepsilon\left(\log\log 1/\varepsilon\right)^{-1/Q}}=k_{3},\text{ a.s. }

where
Q=\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{d}{\theta_{2}}
.

Proof.

Since
V(t,x)
is smoother than
U(t,x)
on
I=[a,b]\times[-M,M]^{d}
by Propositions
3.4
and
3.5
, the regularity properties of
u(t,x)=U(t,x)-V(t,x)
are determined by the increment-stationary field
U(t,x)
(see Proposition
3.3
), and thus we have the above regularity results for
u(t,x)
.
∎

Remark 3.8

(A comparison of Theorem
3.7
with known results)
.

(1)

For the fractional heat equation (i.e.,
\beta=1
and
\gamma=0
), the joint uniform and local moduli of continuity are consistent with

[
21
, Proposition 3.7]
, and Chung’s LIL coincides with
[
21

.

(2)

.

3.2.
Temporal regularity

In this subsection, we obtain the temporal regularity of
u(t,x)
in Theorem
3.12
. Recall that
U(t,x)
and
V(t,x)
are defined in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), respectively. For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

\tilde{U}_{x}(t):=U(t,x)-U(0,x),

(3.33)

and

\tilde{V}_{x}(t):=V(t,x)-V(0,x).

(3.34)

Note that
U(0,x)=V(0,x)
. By the definition of
\tilde{U}_{x}(t)
and
\tilde{V}_{x}(t)
, we have

u(t,x)=\tilde{U}_{x}(t)-\tilde{V}_{x}(t).

(3.35)

We will show that the
\tilde{V}_{x}(t)
has smoother sample paths than
\tilde{U}_{x}(t)
in
(0,\infty)
, and
hence the regularity properties of
t\mapsto u(t,x)
solely depend on those of
\tilde{U}_{x}(t)
in
(0,\infty)
.

The following proposition is a key ingredient to study the sample path regularity of
\tilde{U}_{x}(t)
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Recall that
\theta_{1}
is given in (
3.7
). Assume (
2.12
) holds and

\theta_{1}<1.

(3.36)

(1)

The Gaussian process
\tilde{U}_{x}=\left\{\tilde{U}_{x}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
has stationary increments with spectral measure

F_{\tilde{U}}(\mathrm{d}\tau)=\frac{C\mathrm{d}\tau}{|\tau|^{2\theta_{1}+1}}.

(2)

The Gaussian process
\tilde{U}_{x}
satisfies the strong local nondeterminism property: there exists
a constant
C>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t,t^{1},\dots,t^{n}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathrm{Var}\big(\tilde{U}_{x}(t)|\tilde{U}_{x}(t^{1}),\dots,\tilde{U}_{x}(t^{n})\big)\geq C\min_{k=0,\dots,n}|t-t^{k}|^{2\theta_{1}},

(3.37)

where
t^{0}=0
.

(3)

For all
t,s\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[|\tilde{U}_{x}(t)-\tilde{U}_{x}(s)|^{2}\right]=C_{1}|t-s|^{2\theta_{1}},

where

C_{1}=2C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-\cos\big(\tau\big)}{|\tau|^{2\theta_{1}+1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+2|\xi|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi.

(3.38)

Consequently,
\left\{\frac{\tilde{U}_{x}(t)}{\sqrt{C_{1}}},t\geq 0\right\}
is a (standard) fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
\theta_{1}\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\tilde{U}_{x}(t)
and (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\tilde{U}_{x}(t)-\tilde{U}_{x}(s)\big|^{2}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(s,x)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\frac{1-\cos\big((t-s)\tau\big)}{|\tau|^{2\beta+2\gamma+2H_{0}-1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(2d-2H)}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+2|\xi|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-\cos\big((t-s)\tau\big)}{|\tau|^{2\theta_{1}+1}}\mathrm{d}\tau,

where the integral with respect to
\xi
is finite due to (
3.14
) and then the first assertion follows.

The spectral density of
\big\{\tilde{U}_{x}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\big\}
is

f_{\tilde{U}}(\tau)=\frac{C}{|\tau|^{2\theta_{1}+1}}.

Since for every constant
c>0
,

f_{\tilde{U}}(c^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}\tau)=c^{-(2+\frac{1}{\theta_{1}})}f_{\tilde{U}}(\tau),

the SLND property follows directly from Theorem 4.1 in
[
21
, Appendix A]
.

By (
3.15
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\tilde{U}_{x}(t)-\tilde{U}_{x}(s)\big|^{2}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(s,x)|^{2}\right]=C_{1}|t-s|^{2\theta_{1}},

where
C_{1}
is the constant in (
3.38
).
Noting
\tilde{U}_{x}(0)=0
, we have
\mathbb{E}\big[|\tilde{U}_{x}(t)|^{2}\big]=C_{1}|t|^{2\theta_{1}}
and
\tilde{U}_{x}(t)
is well defined for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then,

\frac{1}{C_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{U}_{x}(t)\tilde{U}_{x}(s)\big)\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left(|t|^{2\theta_{1}}+|s|^{2\theta_{1}}-|t-s|^{2\theta_{1}}\right).

Consequently,
\left\{\frac{\tilde{U}_{x}(t)}{\sqrt{C_{1}}},t\geq 0\right\}
is a standard fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
\theta_{1}\in(0,1)
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to study the regularity of
\{\tilde{U}_{x}(t),\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Assume the conditions of Lemma
3.9
hold. Recall that
\theta_{1}
and
C_{1}
are given in (
3.7
) and
(
3.38
), respectively. For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have the following temporal regularity results:

(1)

(Uniform modulus of continuity). For any
0\leq a<b<\infty
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[a,b];\\
|t-s|\leq\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\dfrac{|\tilde{U}_{x}(t)-\tilde{U}_{x}(s)|}{|t-s|^{\theta_{1}}\sqrt{\log\left(1+|t-s|^{-1}\right)}}=\sqrt{2C_{1}},\text{ a.s. }

(2)

(Local modulus of continuity). For any
t\geq 0
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{|s|\leq\varepsilon}\dfrac{|\tilde{U}_{x}(t+s)-\tilde{U}_{x}(t)|}{|s|^{\theta_{1}}\sqrt{\log\log\left(1+|s|^{-1}\right)}}=\sqrt{2C_{1}},\text{ a.s. }

(3)

(Chung’s LIL). For any
t\geq 0
,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{|s|\leq\varepsilon}\dfrac{|\tilde{U}_{x}(t+s)-\tilde{U}_{x}(t)|}{\varepsilon^{\theta_{1}}\left(\log\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right)^{-\theta_{1}}}=\sqrt{C_{1}}\kappa^{\theta_{1}},\text{ a.s. }

where
\kappa
is the small ball constant of a fractional Brownian motion with index
\theta_{1}
(e.g.
[
33
, Theorem 6.9]
).

Proof.

As shown in Lemma
3.9
(3),
\left\{\frac{\tilde{U}_{x}(t)}{\sqrt{C_{1}}},t\geq 0\right\}
is a fractional Brownian motion.
Hence, the uniform and local moduli of continuity of
\{\tilde{U}_{x}(t),\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
with the
exact constants follow from
[
33
, (7.5), (7.6)]
. Finally, Chung’s LIL follows from
[
33
, Theorem 7.1]
.
∎

The following proposition shows that the sample function of
\tilde{V}_{x}(t)
is more regular than
\tilde{U}_{x}(t)
in
(0,\infty)
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Assume the conditions of Lemma
3.9
hold. Then, for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\big\{\tilde{V}_{x}(t),t\geq 0\big\}

is well-defined. For any
0<a<b<\infty
, there exists a finite constant
C>0
such that for all
s,t\in[a,b]
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\tilde{V}_{x}(t)-\tilde{V}_{x}(s)\big|^{2}\right]\leq C|t-s|^{2}.

(3.39)

Consequently, the function
t\mapsto\tilde{V}_{x}(t)
is smoother than
t\mapsto\tilde{U}_{x}(t)
on
[a,b]
.

Proof.

Note that
U(0,x)=V(0,x)
. By (
3.1
) and definition of
\tilde{U}_{x}(t)
(
3.33
) and
\tilde{V}_{x}(t)
(
3.34
),
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{V}_{x}(t)|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[|U(t,x)-u(t,x)-V(0,x)|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{U}_{x}(t)-u(t,x)|^{2}]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{U}_{x}(t)|^{2}]+2\mathbb{E}[|u(t,x)|^{2}]<\infty,

under conditions (
2.12
) and (
3.36
), due to Theorem
2.3
and Lemma
3.9
. Then,

\tilde{V}_{x}(t)
is well-defined.

By (
3.34
) and (
3.20
), we have

in Theorem

(3.42)

The following proposition is a key ingredient to study the sample path regularity of

Lemma 3.14
.

Recall that
\theta_{2}
is given in (
3.7
). Assume (
2.12
) holds and

\begin{cases}\theta_{2}<1,\\
\gamma<1-H_{0}.\end{cases}

(3.43)

(1)

The Gaussian random field
\hat{U}_{t}=\big\{\tilde{U}_{t}(x),x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\big\}
has stationary increments with spectral measure

F_{\hat{U}}(\mathrm{d}\xi)=\frac{C\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{|\xi|^{2\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(2\gamma+2H_{0}-2)}}\mathrm{d}\xi.

(2)

The Gaussian random field
\hat{U}_{t}
satisfies the strong local nondeterminism
property: there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x,x^{1},\dots,x^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathrm{Var}\big(\hat{U}_{t}(x)|\hat{U}_{t}(x^{1}),\dots,\hat{U}_{t}(x^{n})\big)\geq C\min_{k=0,\dots,n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}-x^{k}_{i}|^{\theta_{2}}\right)^{2},

(3.44)

where
x^{0}_{i}=0
,
i=1,\dots,d
.

(3)

There exist constants
c,C>0
such that

c|x-y|^{2\theta_{2}}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[|\hat{U}_{t}(x)-\hat{U}_{t}(y)|^{2}\right]\leq C|x-y|^{2\theta_{2}}

(3.45)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\hat{U}_{t}(x)
and (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\hat{U}_{t}(x)-\hat{U}_{t}(y)\big|^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(t,y)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2C_{H_{0}}c_{3,1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\big[1-\cos\big(\langle x-y,\xi\rangle\big)\big]\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{|\xi|^{2\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(2\gamma+2H_{0}-2)}}\mathrm{d}\xi.

(3.46)

Hence the Gaussian random field
\hat{U}_{t}
has stationary increments with spectral density

f_{\hat{U}}(\xi)=\frac{C\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{|\xi|^{2\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(2\gamma+2H_{0}-2)}}.

This proves the first assertion. Moreover, for every constant
c>0
,

f_{\hat{U}}(c^{\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}}\xi)=c^{-(2+\frac{d}{\theta_{2}})}f_{\hat{U}}(\xi).

Thus, the SLND property follows directly from Theorem 4.1 in
[
21
, Appendix A]
.

By (
3.18
), we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{U}_{t}(x)-\hat{U}_{t}(y)\right|^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[|U(t,x)-U(t,y)|^{2}\right]\leq C|x-y|^{2\theta_{2}}.

Since
\hat{U}_{t}(0)=0
, then
\mathbb{E}\left[|\hat{U}_{t}(x)|^{2}\right]\leq C|x|^{2\theta_{2}}
and
\hat{U}_{t}(x)
is well defined for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The lower bound is a direct consequence of the SLND property. The proof is complete.
∎

From Lemma
3.14
, we prove the following regularity properties of the Gaussian random field

The following proposition shows that, as a function of the space variable

is more regular than

Proposition 3.16
.

Assume the conditions of Lemma
3.14
hold. Then the

Then,
\hat{V}_{t}(x)

and then the desired result follows from (
3.21
). This completes the proof.
∎

)

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{x\in[-1,1]^{d}}|V(t,x)-V(t,0)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}=\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{x\in[-1,1]^{d}}|\hat{V}_{t}(x)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{x,y\in[-1,1]^{d}}|\hat{V}_{t}(x)-\hat{V}_{t}(y)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\geq\exp\left(-\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{d/\lambda}}\right).

(3) Let
\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon)
the smallest number of open balls of radius
\varepsilon>0
that are needed to cover
[0,1]\times[-1,1]^{d}
which is endowed
with the distance

d((t,x),(s,y)):=\mathbb{E}\left[|V(t,x)-V(s,y)|^{2}\right]^{1/2}.

Let
\lambda=(\alpha-d+H)\wedge 1
. Then by the triangle inequality, we have

d((t,x),(s,y))\leq d_{1}(s,t)+d_{2}(x,y).

Note that
\mathcal{N}_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{d/\lambda}}
. Thus,
\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{1+d/\lambda}}
.
Applying Lemma
A.9
with
\Psi(\varepsilon)=\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{1+d/\lambda}}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,1]\times[-1,1]^{d}}|V(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{(t,x),(s,y)\in[0,1]\times[-1,1]^{d}}|V(t,x)-V(s,y)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\geq\exp\left(-\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{1+d/\lambda}}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we can state and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\{u(t,x),t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
be the solution of (
1.1
) and recall that
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are given in (
3.7
).

(1)

(Small ball probability in time). Assume the conditions (
2.12
) and (
3.36
) hold. Assume additionally
either
\beta+\gamma-2\leq 0
or

\begin{cases}\beta+\gamma-2>0,\\
-H_{0}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(d-H)<1.\end{cases}

Then,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{1/\theta_{1}}\log\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}=-C_{1}^{1/2\theta_{1}}\kappa,

where
C_{1}
is defined in (
3.38
) and
\kappa
is the small ball constant of a fractional Brownian motion
with index
\theta_{1}
(e.g.
[
33
, Theorem 6.9]
).

(2)

(Small ball probability in space). Assume the conditions (
2.12
) and (
3.43
) hold. There exist
positive constants
c
and
C
independent of
t>0
such that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,

\exp\left(-\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{d/\theta_{2}}}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\max_{x\in[-1,1]^{d}}|u(t,x)-u(t,0)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{d/\theta_{2}}}\right).

(3)

(Space-time joint small ball probability). Assume the conditions (
2.12
) and (
3.8
) hold. Assume additionally
either
\beta+\gamma-2\leq 0
or

\begin{cases}\beta+\gamma-2>0,\\
-H_{0}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(d-H)<1.\end{cases}

Then, there exist positive constants
c
and
C
such that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,

\exp\left(-\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{Q}}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{(t,x)\in[0,1]\times[-1,1]^{d}}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\right\}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{Q}}\right),

where
Q=\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{d}{\theta_{2}}
.

Proof.

Step 1:
Let
\rho\in(0,1)
be an arbitrary real number. By (
3.35
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|\tilde{U}_{x}(t)|\leq\rho\varepsilon,\>\max_{t\in[0,1]}|\tilde{V}_{x}(t)|\leq(1-\rho)\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|\tilde{U}_{x}(t)|\leq\rho\varepsilon\right\}\times\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|\tilde{V}_{x}(t)|\leq(1-\rho)\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|U(t,x)-U(0,x)|\leq\rho\varepsilon\right\}\times\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|V(t,x)-V(0,x)|\leq(1-\rho)\varepsilon\right\},

where in the second inequality we use the Gaussian correlation inequality (see Remark
4.5
). Then, by Proposition

4.1
(1) and Proposition
4.2
(1), we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{1/\theta_{1}}\log\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\right\}\geq-\frac{C_{1}^{1/2\theta_{1}}\kappa}{\rho^{1/\theta_{1}}}.

(4.4)

Let
\tilde{\rho}>1
be an arbitrary real number. For the upper bound, applying (
3.35
) and the Gaussian correlation inequality again, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|\tilde{U}_{x}(t)|\leq\tilde{\rho}\varepsilon\right\}\geq\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon,\>\max_{t\in[0,1]}|\tilde{V}_{x}(t)|\leq(\tilde{\rho}-1)\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\right\}\times\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|\tilde{V}_{x}(t)|\leq(\tilde{\rho}-1)\varepsilon\right\}.

Then, using Proposition
4.1
(1) and Proposition
4.2
(1), we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{1/\theta_{1}}\log\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\right\}\leq-\frac{C_{1}^{1/2\theta_{1}}\kappa}{\tilde{\rho}^{1/\theta_{1}}}.

(4.5)

Combining (
4.4
) with (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{1}^{1/2\theta_{1}}\kappa}{\rho^{1/\theta_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{1/\theta_{1}}\log\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{1/\theta_{1}}\log\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\right\}\leq-\frac{C_{1}^{1/2\theta_{1}}\kappa}{\tilde{\rho}^{1/\theta_{1}}}.

The desired small ball probability in time follows from the fact that both
\rho\in(0,1)
and
\tilde{\rho}>1
can be arbitrarily close to 1.

Similarly, using Proposition
4.1
, Proposition
4.2
, and the Gaussian correlation inequality, we can also
prove the rest two assertions. This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.1
.

For any
\varphi\in L^{1/{H_{0}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
H_{0}\in(1/2,1)
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\varphi(s)|t-s|^{2H_{0}-2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{H_{0}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\varphi(t)\right|^{1/H_{0}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2H_{0}},

where
C_{H_{0}}>0
is a constant depending on
H_{0}
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
H_{j}\in(0,1)
with
j=1,\dots,d
and
H=\sum_{j=1}^{d}H_{j}
.

(i)

The integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi

is convergent if and only if
\alpha>d-H
.

(ii)

The integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}|\xi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

is convergent if and only if
\alpha>d-H+1
.

(iii)

The following inequalities

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|w_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\sigma(\mathrm{d}w)<\infty,

and

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|w_{i}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|w_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\sigma(\mathrm{d}w)<\infty,\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,d,

hold, where
\sigma(\mathrm{d}w)
is the uniform measure on the
(d-1)
-dimensional unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

Proof.

Using the
d
-dimensional spherical coordinate:
r\in[0,\infty)
,
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{d-2}\in[0,\pi]
,

\begin{cases}&\xi_{1}=rw_{1}=r\cos(\phi_{1}),\\
&\xi_{2}=rw_{2}=r\sin(\phi_{1})\cos(\phi_{2}),\\
&\xi_{3}=rw_{3}=r\sin(\phi_{1})\sin(\phi_{2})\cos(\phi_{3}),\\
&\vdots\\
&\xi_{d-2}=rw_{d-2}=r\sin(\phi_{1})\dots\sin(\phi_{d-3})\cos(\phi_{d-2}),\\
&\xi_{d-1}=rw_{d-1}=r\sin(\phi_{1})\dots\sin(\phi_{d-3})\sin(\phi_{d-2})\cos(\theta),\\
&\xi_{d}=rw_{d}=r\sin(\phi_{1})\dots\sin(\phi_{d-3})\sin(\phi_{d-2})\sin(\theta),\end{cases}

(A.1)

we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}}{1+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\xi=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2d-2H-1}}{1+r^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}r\times\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|w_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\sigma(\mathrm{d}w),

where the first integral is finite if and only if
\alpha>d-H
, and for the second one we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}|w_{j}|^{1-2H_{j}}\sigma(\mathrm{d}w)

(A.2)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{H_{j}}\times\prod_{k=1}^{d-2}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos^{1-2H_{k}}(\phi_{k})\sin^{\sum_{j=k+1}^{d}1-2H_{j}}(\phi_{k})\sin^{d-1-k}(\phi_{k})\mathrm{d}\phi_{k}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{1-2H_{d-1}}(\theta)\sin^{1-2H_{d}}(\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta.

Since
H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{d}\in(0,1)
, we have

\sum_{j=k+1}^{d}(1-2H_{j})+d-1-k>-1,

for
k=1,2,\dots,d-2
, and

1-2H_{j}>-1,

for
j=1,2,\dots,d
. Then, the integrals with respect to
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{d-2}
and
\theta
are finite. This proves (i). By using a similar argument,
we can also prove the assertions in (ii)-(iii). This completes the proof.
∎

Recall that the Mittag-Leffler function
E_{a,b}
is given in (
2.4
). The following lemma is an extension of
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma A.3
.

(Mittag–Leffler Fourier transform). Assume
x>0
.

(i)

If
\beta\in(0,2]
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, then for any
\sigma>0
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{F}\left[{{\rm 1\mkern-1.5mu}\!{\rm I}}_{t>0}\>t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)e^{-t\sigma}\right](\tau)=\frac{(\sigma+i\tau)^{-\gamma}}{(\sigma+i\tau)^{\beta}+x}.

(A.3)

(ii)

If
\beta\in(0,2)
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
, then for any real number
\tau\neq 0
,

\mathcal{F}\left[{{\rm 1\mkern-1.5mu}\!{\rm I}}_{t>0}\>t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)\right](\tau)=\frac{(i\tau)^{-\gamma}}{(i\tau)^{\beta}+x}.

(A.4)

Proof.

(i):
The equality (
A.3
)
is a direct consequence of
[
20
, eq. (7.1)]
. For the reader’s convenience, we also include our proof below. Assume that
\beta\in(0,2]
and
\sigma>0
. Then,

\mathcal{F}\left[{{\rm 1\mkern-1.5mu}\!{\rm I}}_{t>0}\>t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)e^{-t\sigma}\right](\tau)

(A.5)

is well defined by the asymptotic property of
E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)
, see (
2.6
) for
\beta\in(0,2)

and
[
28
, (1.8.31)]
for
\beta=2
. Applying the Cauchy–Riemann theorem, we can prove that (
A.5
) is analytic.

Let
\theta=\sigma+i\tau
. Using Euler’s formula, we have

\theta^{\beta+\gamma}+x\theta^{\gamma}=\left(|\theta|e^{i\omega}\right)^{\beta+\gamma}+x\left(|\theta|e^{i\omega}\right)^{\gamma},

where
\omega=\text{arg}(\theta)\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
. Since
\beta\omega\in(-\pi,\pi)
and
x>0
, it follows that

\theta^{\beta+\gamma}+x\theta^{\gamma}\neq 0
. Hence, the fraction
\dfrac{(\sigma+i\tau)^{-\gamma}}{(\sigma+i\tau)^{\beta}+x}

does not have poles and hence is analytic.

It follows from
[
28
, (1.10.9)]
on the Laplace transform of the Mittag–Leffler function that,
if
|\theta|^{\beta}>x
,
then

\begin{split}\frac{(\sigma+i\tau)^{-\gamma}}{(\sigma+i\tau)^{\beta}+x}&=\mathcal{L}\left[t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)\right](\theta)\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)e^{-t\theta}\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[{{\rm 1\mkern-1.5mu}\!{\rm I}}_{t>0}\>t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)e^{-t\sigma}\right]e^{-it\tau}\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\mathcal{F}\left[{{\rm 1\mkern-1.5mu}\!{\rm I}}_{t>0}\>t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)e^{-t\sigma}\right](\tau).\end{split}

(A.6)

Therefore, applying the identity theorem for analytic functions, we can show that (
A.6
) holds for all
\sigma=\mathfrak{R}(\theta)>0
.
This proves the first assertion.

(ii):
Assume that
\beta\in(0,2)
,
\gamma\in[0,1)
and
\tau\neq 0
. Define

\displaystyle f(\sigma,t):=t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)e^{-t\sigma}\cos(t\tau),\quad f(t):=f(0,t),

\displaystyle g(\sigma,t):=t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)e^{-t\sigma}\sin(t\tau),\quad g(t):=g(0,t).

If
\sigma\geq 0
, noting that
\gamma<1
,

\mathcal{F}\left[{{\rm 1\mkern-1.5mu}\!{\rm I}}_{t>0}\>t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)e^{-t\sigma}\right](\tau)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t-i\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t,

(A.7)

is well defined due to (
2.6
).

In view of (
A.3
), in order to prove (
A.4
), it suffices to show that the last two integrals in (
A.7
)
are continuous at
\sigma=0
. To this end, we divide
\beta\in(0,2)
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
into the following two cases:

\begin{cases}(1):\beta\in(0,2),\gamma\in(0,1)\text{ or }\beta\in(0,1],\gamma=0;\\
(2):\beta\in(1,2),\gamma=0.\end{cases}

By the asymptotic property (
2.6
) and the fact that
E_{1,1}(z)=e^{z}
, we have for
\beta\in(0,2)
, as
z\to\infty
,

E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}(-z)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)z}-\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma-\beta)z^{2}}+o\left(\dfrac{1}{z^{2}}\right),&\mbox{if }\beta\neq 1\text{ or }\gamma\neq 0\\
e^{-z},&\mbox{if }\beta=1\text{ and }\gamma=0.\end{cases}

(A.8)

For case (1), noting
1/\Gamma(0)=1/\Gamma(-1)=0
and
\Gamma(x)<0
for
x\in(-1,0)
, by (
A.8
) there exists a
constant
T
such that for
t>T
,
t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)
is positive and
bounded by a decreasing function that tends to zero as
t\to\infty
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{3\pi/2+2n\pi}{|\tau|}>T
.
For any
\sigma>0
, the integral

\int_{\frac{3\pi/2+2n\pi}{|\tau|}}^{\infty}f(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{k=2n}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{3\pi/2+k\pi}{|\tau|}}^{\frac{5\pi/2+k\pi}{|\tau|}}f(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t

is an alternating series with a positive leading term. Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
N_{\varepsilon}
, such that for any
\sigma>0
and

n\in\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\frac{3\pi/2+2n\pi}{|\tau|}>T,n>N_{\varepsilon}\right\}
,

0<\int_{\frac{3\pi/2+2n\pi}{|\tau|}}^{\infty}f(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t<\int_{\frac{3\pi/2+2n\pi}{|\tau|}}^{\frac{5\pi/2+2n\pi}{|\tau|}}f(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t<\int_{\frac{3\pi/2+2n\pi}{|\tau|}}^{\frac{5\pi/2+2n\pi}{|\tau|}}f(t)\mathrm{d}t<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Let
n_{0}=\min\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\frac{3\pi/2+2n\pi}{|\tau|}>T,n>N_{\varepsilon}\right\}
. Thus, we have

\left|\int_{\frac{3\pi/2+2n_{0}\pi}{|\tau|}}^{\infty}\big(f(\sigma,t)-f(t)\big)\mathrm{d}t\right|<\int_{\frac{3\pi/2+2n_{0}\pi}{|\tau|}}^{\infty}f(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\frac{3\pi/2+2n_{0}\pi}{|\tau|}}^{\frac{5\pi/2+2n_{0}\pi}{|\tau|}}f(t)\mathrm{d}t<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

For the integral on the finite interval
\left[0,\frac{3\pi/2+2n_{0}\pi}{|\tau|}\right]
, we have if
\sigma
is sufficiently small, say

\sigma<\delta_{\varepsilon}
for some
\delta_{\varepsilon}
depending on
\varepsilon
,

\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{3\pi/2+2n_{0}\pi}{|\tau|}}\big(f(\sigma,t)-f(t)\big)\mathrm{d}t\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
\delta_{\varepsilon}
such that when
\sigma<\delta_{\varepsilon}
,

\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(f(\sigma,t)-f(t)\big)\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq\left|\int_{\frac{3\pi/2+2n_{0}\pi}{|\tau|}}^{\infty}\big(f(\sigma,t)-f(t)\big)\mathrm{d}t\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{3\pi/2+2n_{0}\pi}{|\tau|}}\big(f(\sigma,t)-f(t)\big)\mathrm{d}t\right|<\varepsilon.

This shows that
\int_{0}^{\infty}f(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t
is continuous at
\sigma=0
.

Now we consider case (2). Noting
1/\Gamma(0)=0
and
\Gamma(x)>0
for
x\in(-2,-1)
, by (
A.8
) there
exists a constant
T
such that as
t>T
,
t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)
is negative and

\left|t^{\beta+\gamma-1}E_{\beta,\beta+\gamma}\big(-xt^{\beta}\big)\right|
is decreasing to zero as
t\to\infty
. Then, we can apply
the same argument as for case (1) to prove that
\int_{0}^{\infty}f(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t
is continuous at
\sigma=0
. Similarly, we can
also prove that
\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t
is continuous at
\sigma=0
for both cases (1) and (2). Thus, the integral
(
A.7
) is continuous at
\sigma=0
.

On the other hand, using Euler’s formula, we have

\displaystyle(i\tau)^{\beta+\gamma}+x(i\tau)^{\gamma}=\left(|\tau|e^{i\tilde{\omega}}\right)^{\beta+\gamma}+x\left(|\tau|e^{i\tilde{\omega}}\right)^{\gamma},

where

\tilde{\omega}=\text{arg}(i\tau)=\begin{cases}-\pi/2,&\mbox{if }\tau<0,\\
\pi/2,&\mbox{if }\tau>0.\end{cases}

For
\beta\in(0,2)
,

\beta\tilde{\omega}\in\begin{cases}(-\pi,0),&\mbox{if }\tau<0,\\
(0,\pi),&\mbox{if }\tau>0.\end{cases}

Since
x>0
and
\tau\neq 0
, it follows that
(i\tau)^{\beta+\gamma}+x(i\tau)^{\gamma}\neq 0
. Hence, there are no poles in

\dfrac{(i\tau)^{-\gamma}}{(i\tau)^{\beta}+x}
and the fraction
\dfrac{(\sigma+i\tau)^{-\gamma}}{(\sigma+i\tau)^{\beta}+x}
is
continuous at
\sigma=0
.

Consequently, when
\beta\in(0,2)
,
\gamma\in[0,1)
and
\tau\neq 0
, the Fourier transform (
A.4
) follows
from setting
\sigma=0
in both sides of (
A.3
).

The following two lemmas are based on the results in
[

, Sections 1.5 and 1.8]

, Appendix A.2]
.

Lemma A.4
.

Suppose
\alpha>0
,
\beta\in(0,2)
,
\gamma\geq 0
, and
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let

g(x)=x^{-2}H_{3,4}^{2,2}\left(x^{\alpha}\>\middle|\>\begin{subarray}{l}(d,\alpha),\>(1,1),\>(\beta+\gamma,\beta)\\[3.99994pt]
(d/2,\alpha/2),\>(1,1),\>(1,\alpha/2),\>(d+1,\alpha)\end{subarray}\right),\quad x>0.

Then, there exist positive and finite constants
K_{1}<K_{2}
such that

|g(x)|\leq C(x^{d}+x^{\alpha-2}|\log x|),\quad\hbox{ for all }0<x\leq K_{1},

(A.9)

and

|g(x)|\leq Cx^{-2},\quad\hbox{ for all }x\geq K_{2}.

(A.10)

Lemma A.6
.

Let
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\mu)
be a measurable space with
\mu:\mathcal{Y}\to[0,\infty]
. Let
f
be a complex-valued function defined on

\mathbb{R}\times Y
and
\delta\in(0,\infty)
be a constant. Suppose that, for
x\in(x_{0}-\delta,x_{0}+\delta)
,

(i)

u(x)=\displaystyle\int_{Y}f(x,y)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)
with
\displaystyle\int_{Y}|f(x,y)|\mu(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
;

(ii)

for fixed
y
,
\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x,y)
exists and is a continuous function of
x

(iii)

v(x)=\displaystyle\int_{Y}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x,y)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)
is continuous at

(iv)

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x_{0}+z,y)\right|\mathrm{d}z\mu(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty

then we have

Letting
|h|\leq\delta

\displaystyle u(x_{0}+h)-u(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{Y}f(x_{0}+h,y)-f(x_{0},y)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=\int_{Y}\int_{0}^{h}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x_{0}+z,y)\mathrm{d}z\mu(\mathrm{d}y).

In view of (iv), we can apply Fubini’s theorem to get

\displaystyle u(x_{0}+h)-u(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{h}\int_{Y}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x_{0}+z,y)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}z=\int_{0}^{h}v(x_{0}+z)\mathrm{d}z.

The above equation implies

\frac{u(x_{0}+h)-u(x_{0})}{h}=\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}v(x_{0}+z)\mathrm{d}z.

Since

is continuous at
x_{0}

h\to 0
gives the desired result.

\lambda\in(0,1)
and
a_{\lfloor t\rfloor}\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
, we have
h(t)\to 1
as
t\to\infty
. We can write (
A.24
)
as
{\mathrm{d}f}/{f^{\lambda}}=ch^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}s
and integrate from
1
to
t
to get

f(t)=\left(1+c(1-\lambda)\int_{1}^{t}h(s)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\lambda}},

for all
t\in[1,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
and hence all
t\geq 1
by continuity. Since
f(n)=a_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{s\to\infty}h(s)=1
,
this proves the result.
∎

The following lemma is a reformulation of Talagrand
[
44
, Lemma 2.2]
given by Ledoux
[
30
, (7.11)-(7.13) on p.257]
.

Lemma A.9
.

Let
S
be a set with the distance
d(s,t)=\mathbb{E}[|Z(s)-Z(t)|^{2}]^{1/2}
, where
\{Z(t)\}_{t\in S}
is a Gaussian process. Let
\mathcal{N}_{d}(S,\varepsilon)

denote the smallest number of (open)
d
-balls of radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover
S
. If there is a decreasing function
\Psi:(0,\delta]\to(0,\infty)

such that
N(S,\varepsilon)\leq\Psi(\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta]
. Assume that for some constant
C_{2}\geq C_{1}>1
we have
C_{1}\Psi(\varepsilon)\leq\Psi(\varepsilon/2)\leq C_{2}\Psi(\varepsilon)

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta]
. Then for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta]
,

\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s,t\in S}|Z(t)-Z(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\geq\exp\left(-\frac{\Psi(\varepsilon)}{K}\right),

where the constant
K
depends on
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
only.

The Berry-Esseen Bound for High-dimensional Self-normalized Sums
Woonyoung Chang ∗, Kenta Takatsu ∗, Konrad Urban, Arun Kumar Kuchibhotla

Abstract
This manuscript studies the Gaussian approximation of the coordinate-wise maximum of self-normalized statistics in high-dimensional settings. We derive an explicit Berry-Esseen bound under weak assumptions on the absolute moments. When the third absolute moment is finite, our bound scales as log 5 / 4 ⁡ ( d ) / n 1 / 8 \log^{5/4}(d)/n^{1/8} where n n is the sample size and d d is the dimension. Hence, our bound tends to zero as long as log ⁡ ( d ) = o ⁡ ( n 1 / 10 ) \log(d)=o(n^{1/10}) . Our results on self-normalized statistics represent substantial advancements, as such a bound has not been previously available in the high-dimensional central limit theorem (CLT) literature.

Introduction

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be independent and identically distributed (IID) centered random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The vector
T_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
of component-wise self-normalized sum of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle e_{j}^{\top}T_{n}:=\frac{|\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{j}^{\top}X_{i}|}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{j}^{\top}X_{i})^{2}}}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad j=1,\ldots,d,

where
e_{j}
denotes the
j
:th canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In the one-dimensional case, the limiting distribution of
T_{n}
has been extensively studied. One remarkable property of the self-normalized sum is that it often requires less stringent moment conditions than the
classical limit theorems for the usual sums. A key result by
Giné et al., 1997
resolved a conjecture raised by earlier works
(
Logan et al., 1973
;
Griffin and Mason, 1991
;
Bentkus and Götze, 1996
)
showing that the self-normalized sum
T_{n}
is asymptotically normal if and only if
X_{1}
belongs to the domain of attraction of the normal law. This was further generalized by
Chistyakov and Götze, 2004
, who provided necessary and sufficient conditions for
T_{n}
to converge to an
\alpha
-stable distribution
1
1

1

We note that the normal distribution is stable with index
\alpha=2
.
. In addition, the distributional approximation of
T_{n}
to the standard normal distribution has been explored through various approaches such as uniform Berry-Esseen bounds
(
Hall, 1988
;
Bentkus and Götze, 1996
;
Bentkus et al., 1996
)
, non-uniform Berry-Esseen bounds and Cramér-type large deviation
(
Wang and Jing, 1999
;
Jing et al., 2003
;
Robinson and Wang, 2005
;
Hu et al., 2009
;
Beckedorf and Rohde, 2025
)
, and Edgeworth expansion
(
Finner and Dickhaus, 2010
;
Derumigny et al., 2024
;
Beckedorf and Rohde, 2025
)
. This list is far from exhaustive.

Extending these results to high-dimensional settings remains surprisingly underexplored despite the growing interest and recent advances in the high-dimensional central limit theorem
(
Kuchibhotla and Rinaldo, 2020
;
Kuchibhotla et al., 2021
;
Lopes, 2022
;
Chernozhuokov et al., 2022
;
Chernozhukov et al., 2023
)
. Recent work by
Das, 2024
establishes near-
n^{-\kappa/2}
Berry-Esseen rate for
T_{n}
in high dimension under the existence of
(2+\kappa)
:th moments of
e_{j}^{\top}X_{i}
for
0<\kappa\leq 1
. However, their results are restricted to the special case where
e_{j}^{\top}X_{i}
are independent across both
i
and
j
.

This manuscript aims to bridge this gap and studies the Gaussian approximation of
T_{n}
under mild conditions and high dimensional settings where the dimension
d
can grow much faster than the sample size
n
. Specifically, we focus on bounding the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) distance between
\|T_{n}\|_{\infty}
and
\|Z_{n}\|_{\infty}
, for some
d
-dimensional Gaussian vector
Z_{n}
, with explicit dependencies of
n
and
d
on our bound. To this end, we consider two different Gaussian approximations:

•

Best Gaussian Approximation:

\Delta_{n}:=\inf_{Z\sim G:G\in\mathcal{G}}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\left|\mathbb{P}(\|T_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)\right|,

(1)

where
\mathcal{G}
is a collection of mean-zero
d
-dimensional Gaussian distribution whose variance-covariance matrix is a correlation matrix.

•

Moment Matching Gaussian Approximation:

\Delta_{n}^{X}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\left|\mathbb{P}(\|T_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z^{X}\|_{\infty}\leq t)\right|,

(2)

where
Z^{X}
be centered
d
-dimensional Gaussian with covariance matrix equal to
{\rm Corr}(X_{1})
.

By construction,
\Delta_{n}\leq\Delta_{n}^{X}
, and
\Delta_{n}^{X}
is well-defined only if
X
has a finite second moment whereas
\Delta_{n}
does not require this. We further show in this manuscript that
\Delta_{n}
can converge to
0
even when the second moment of
X_{1}
diverges.

Finally, we discuss how our findings can be utilized for valid inferential procedures in high-dimensional settings. A common approach in the high-dimensional CLT literature is to establish bootstrap consistency, which enables an accurate bootstrap quantile estimation. This can be explored through
moment matching Gaussian approximation
framework in which we are interested in studying
\Delta_{n}^{*}:=\sup_{t}|\mathbb{P}(\|T_{n}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq t|X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})-\mathbb{P}(\|Z^{X}\|_{\infty}\leq t)|
where
T_{n}^{*}
is a bootstrap analog of
T_{n}
. Alternatively, one can leverage the property that the covariance matrix of the approximating Gaussian distribution is a correlation matrix with unit diagonals. This enables valid yet conservative inferential methods such as Bonferonni or Šidák correction which suggests the quantiles
K_{\alpha}^{B}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/(2p))
and
K_{\alpha}^{S}=\Phi^{-1}((1+(1-\alpha)^{1/p})/2)
for the nominal probability
1-\alpha
. Formal exploration of these methods is deferred to future work.

2
Main Results

Consider independent and identically distributed (IID) mean-zero and non-degenerate variance random vectors
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with covariance matrix
\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. We allow the dimension
d
to grow with
n
and possibly larger than
n
. We define a self-normalized sum
T_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

\displaystyle e_{j}^{\top}T_{n}:=\frac{|\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{j}^{\top}X_{i}|}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{j}^{\top}X_{i})^{2}}}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad j=1,\ldots,d.

(3)

The main result is stated in terms of the truncated moments, which permits the random vector
X_{1}
to have a diverging second moment. Given a random vector
X_{1}
and
n\geq 1
, we define a non-negative number
a_{j}
, which satisfies the following inequality:

\displaystyle a_{j}^{2}:=\sup\{b\geq 0:\E[(e_{j}^{\top}X_{1})^{2}\mathbf{1}\{(e_{j}^{\top}X_{1})^{2}\leq bn\}]\geq b\}.

(4)

For
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq d
, we define the truncated random vector

\displaystyle e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}:=\frac{e_{j}^{\top}X_{i}}{a_{j}n^{1/2}}\mathbf{1}\{(e_{j}^{\top}X_{i})^{2}\leq a_{j}^{2}n\}.

(5)

By Lemma 1.3 of
Bentkus and Götze, 1996
, we have
\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
almost surely and
\E[(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{2}]=1/n
for all
1\leq j\leq d
. We provide the main results below:

Theorem 1
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(n\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{(e_{j}^{\top}X_{1})^{2}}{a_{j}^{2}}>n\right)+\left(n\log^{5/2}(ed)\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\|_{\infty}\right)^{1/2}+\left(n\log^{5}(ed)\mathbb{E}\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}\right)^{1/4}\Bigg).

Additionally, there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{X}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(n\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{(e_{j}^{\top}X_{1})^{2}}{a_{j}^{2}}>n\right)+\log(ed)\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(e_{j}^{\top}X_{1})^{2}}{a_{j}^{2}}\mathbf{1}\{(e_{j}^{\top}X_{1})^{2}>a_{j}^{2}n\}\right]\right\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(n\log^{5/2}(ed)\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\|_{\infty}\right)^{1/2}+\left(n\log^{5}(ed)\mathbb{E}\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}\right)^{1/4}\Bigg).

Corollary 1.1
.

Denote
\E(e_{j}^{\top}X_{1})^{2}=\sigma_{j}^{2}
and suppose that
\E[\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|e_{j}^{\top}X_{1}/\sigma_{j}|^{2+\delta}]<\infty
for some
\delta\in(0,1]
, then the bound in Theorem
1
reduces to

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\leq C\log^{5/4}(ed)n^{-\delta/8}\left(\E\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|e_{j}^{\top}X_{1}/\sigma_{j}|^{2+\delta}\right)^{1/4}.

In particular, when
\E[\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|e_{j}^{\top}X_{1}/\sigma_{j}|^{3}]<\infty
, one has

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\leq C\log^{5/4}(ed)n^{-1/8}\left(\E\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|e_{j}^{\top}X_{1}/\sigma_{j}|^{3}\right)^{1/4}.

(6)

Remark 1

(On the rate of convergence)
.

The result (
6
) suggests that
\Delta_{n}
tends to zero as long as
\log d=o(n^{1/10})
. This manuscript also establishes that the rate of convergence of
\Delta_{n}
is of the order
(\log d)^{5/4}n^{-1/8}
under the most favorable case with the finite third moment of
\|X_{1}\|_{\infty}
. Without considering self-normalization, the approximation error has been shown to converge as fast as
\mathrm{polylog}(dn)n^{-1/2}
in
Kuchibhotla and Rinaldo, 2020
. Corresponding improvements under self-normalization require significant efforts due to the complex dependence between the summands of the self-normalized sum. To the best of our knowledge, the results corresponding to
Theorem

1
are not available in the literature without strong assumptions;
Das, 2024
, for instance, provides the bound on
\Delta_{n}
under the assumption that the components of
X_{1}
are also IID.

2.1
Sketch of the proof

This section outlines the proof of Theorem
1
. The proof follows similarly to that of Theorem 1.4 of
Bentkus and Götze, 1996
, with crucial intermediate steps differing. We first introduce some notation. For each truncated random variable
Y_{i}
, defined in (
5
), the corresponding self-normalized sum for
1\leq j\leq d
is denoted by:

\displaystyle e_{j}^{\top}T_{n}^{Y}:=\frac{|\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}|}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{2}}}.

(7)

Let
Z:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}
be the sum of
n
IID mean-zero Gaussian random vectors such that
{\rm Var}(Y_{i})={\rm Var}(Z_{i})
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let
g:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be any infinitely differentiable with bounded derivatives, satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\leq g(x)\leq 2\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad\mbox{and}\quad g(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|x|}},\quad\mbox{if}\quad|x|\in\left[\frac{1}{4},\frac{7}{4}\right].

(8)

We define a random vector
\widetilde{Y}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
via

\displaystyle e_{j}^{\top}\widetilde{Y}_{i}:=e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}g(1+\eta_{j,n})\quad\mbox{with}\quad\eta_{j,n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\right\}.

(9)

Finally, the sums of random vectors are denoted by
\mathbf{Y}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{Y}_{i}
. With these notations in place, we proceed with the main proof. First, by the triangle inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|T_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|T_{n}^{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq t)|+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|T_{n}^{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)|

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|

\displaystyle=\mathbf{I_{1}}+\mathbf{I_{2}}+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{X}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{I_{1}}+\mathbf{I_{2}}+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z^{X}\|_{\infty}\leq t)|+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z^{X}\|_{\infty}\leq t)|.

Lemma

3
and
lemma

4
establish with some universal constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{I_{1}}+\mathbf{I_{2}}\leq n\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{(e_{j}^{\top}X_{1})^{2}}{a_{j}^{2}}>n\right)+C\log(ed)n\mathbb{E}\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}.

Lemma

5
also proves with some universal constant
C>1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z^{X}\|_{\infty}\leq t)|\leq C\log(ed)R_{n}^{1/2},

where

\displaystyle R_{n}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(e_{j}^{\top}X_{1})^{2}}{a_{j}^{2}}\mathbf{1}\{(e_{j}^{\top}X_{1})^{2}>a_{j}^{2}n\}\right]+n^{2}\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\|_{\infty}^{2}.

It remains to control
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we define a smooth approximation of the indicator function
H_{\varepsilon,t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
by Gaussian convolution as

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon,t}(x)=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}\{\|x+\varepsilon W\|_{\infty}\leq t\}]\quad\mbox{where}\quad W\overset{d}{=}N(0,I_{d}).

(10)

Then, by the smoothing lemma (such as Lemma 2.4 of
Fang and Koike, 2021
and Lemma 1 of
Kuchibhotla and Rinaldo, 2020
),

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq t)|+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{E}[H_{\varepsilon,t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})]-\mathbb{E}[H_{\varepsilon,t}(\mathbf{Y})]|+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|+C\varepsilon\log(ed),

for any
\varepsilon>0
and a universal constant
C>0
.
We prove that for a possibly different universal constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\varepsilon>0}\left\{\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{E}[H_{\varepsilon,t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})]-\mathbb{E}[H_{\varepsilon,t}(\mathbf{Y})]|+C\varepsilon\log(ed)\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\left\{\left(n\log^{5/2}(ed)\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\|_{\infty}\right)^{1/2}+\left(n\log^{5}(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{1/4}\right\},

(11)

as long as the right-hand side is no greater than
1
. We further prove that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|\leq C\left\{n\log^{1/2}(ed)\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\|_{\infty}+\left(n\log^{5}(ed)\mathbb{E}\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}\right)^{1/4}\right\},

(12)

provided that the right-hand side is no greater than 1. Putting all together implies Theorem
1
.

3
Comments on the proof technique and refinements

As mentioned in
Remark

1
, the Berry-Esseen bound for the sum of independent random variables can be improved up to
\mathrm{polylog}(dn)n^{-1/2}
in high-dimensional settings
(
Kuchibhotla and Rinaldo, 2020
)
. While the analogous refinements may be feasible by following the general arguments in
Kuchibhotla et al., 2021
or
Kuchibhotla and Rinaldo, 2020
, the derivation will be considerably different due to the complex dependence among the self-normalized sum.

The current bottleneck is precisely in the handling of the following triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq t)|+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|

where we directly compared the distributions of
\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}
and
\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\infty}
. This is an approach took by
Bentkus and Götze, 1996
;
Bentkus et al., 1996
for the univariate settings as well. We may consider an alternative approach for the refinement. First, we define the following mapping of any random vector
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}g(1+\eta^{\xi}_{j,n})\quad\mbox{with}\quad\eta^{\xi}_{j,n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{(e_{j}^{\top}\xi_{i})^{2}-1/n\right\}.

Adopting this notation to
(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
and
(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})
, we can define

\displaystyle e_{j}^{\top}\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}:=\varphi_{j}(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad e_{j}^{\top}\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}:=\varphi_{j}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}).

We can now control the earlier inequality as follows:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)|+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)|

where the second term can be easily managed by the argument presented in this manuscript. We are now left with the first term. For
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, we define

\displaystyle(Y_{1:k},Z_{(k+1):n})

\displaystyle:=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{k},Z_{k+1},\ldots,Z_{n}).

In particular,
(Y_{1:k},Z_{(k+1):n})\equiv(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
when
k=n
and
(Y_{1:k},Z_{(k+1):n})\equiv(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})
when
k=0
.
After invoking the smoothing inequality with telescoping, the expression reduces to

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\|_{\infty}\leq t)|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{E}[H_{\varepsilon,t}\circ\varphi(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})]-\mathbb{E}[H_{\varepsilon,t}\circ\varphi(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})]|+C\varepsilon\log(ed)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,|\mathbb{E}[H_{\varepsilon,t}\circ\varphi(Y_{1:(n-j-1)},Z_{(n-j):n})]-\mathbb{E}[H_{\varepsilon,t}\circ\varphi(Y_{1:(n-j)},Z_{(n-j+1):n})]|+C\varepsilon\log(ed).

This procedure performs the classical Lindeberg swapping through the nonlinear map
H_{\varepsilon,t}\circ\varphi
. Additionally, as the derivatives of
H_{\varepsilon,t}
are zero over most regions, sharper bounds on the remainder term from the Taylor series expansion may be expected. This approach has been employed for the high-dimensional CLT without self-normalization—see Section 6 and Step C onward in
Kuchibhotla et al., 2021
. We anticipate that this approach will improve the
n^{-1/8}
term to
n^{-1/6}
in (
6
).

Proof of (
2.1
)

Lemma
6
provides an upper bound for
|\mathbb{E}[H_{\varepsilon,t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})]-\mathbb{E}[H_{\varepsilon,t}(\mathbf{Y})]|
where
h_{j}=h_{j}(\varepsilon)
(defined as (
18
) in Lemma
6
below), for
j=1,\ldots,4
, can be further bounded via Lemma 2.3 of
Fang and Koike, 2021
as

h_{j}\leq C\varepsilon^{-j}(\log d)^{j/2},

for a constant
C
only depends on
j\in\mathbb{N}
. For the sake of simplicity, we denote
\mu_{3}=n\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]
and
\mu_{1}=n\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\phi(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1}\left(\log^{3/2}(ed)\mu_{3}+\log^{1/2}(ed)\mu_{1}\right)+\varepsilon^{-2}\left(\log^{3}(ed)\mu_{3}+\log(ed)\mu_{1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon^{-3}\log^{3/2}(ed)\mu_{3}+\varepsilon^{-4}\log^{2}(ed)\mu_{3}^{2}+\varepsilon\log(ed).

We set

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\log(ed)^{1/4}(\mu_{3}^{1/2}+\mu_{1}^{1/2})+\log(ed)^{2/3}\mu_{3}^{1/3}+\mu_{1}^{2/3}+\log(ed)^{1/8}\mu_{3}^{1/4}+\log(ed)^{1/5}\mu_{3}^{2/5}.

This choice allows
\phi(\varepsilon)\leq 4\varepsilon\log(ed)
, and we further deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\phi(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\log^{5/2}(ed)\mu_{3}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\log^{5}(ed)\mu_{3}\right)^{1/3}+\left(\log^{9/2}(ed)\mu_{3}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\log^{3}(ed)\mu_{3}\right)^{2/5}+\left(\log^{5/2}(ed)\mu_{1}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\log^{3/2}(ed)\mu_{1}\right)^{2/3}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(\log^{5}(ed)\mu_{3}\right)^{1/4}+2\left(\log^{5/2}(ed)\mu_{1}\right)^{1/2}.

This proves (
2.1
).

Proof of (
12
)

From a simple implication, it first follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\left\{\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\left\{\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{Y}-\E[\mathbf{Y}]\|_{\infty}-\|\E[\mathbf{Y}]\|_{\infty}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|_{\infty}\leq t)\right\}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})\eta_{j,n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{j}(Y_{i})+\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}}).

Combining this with (
19
) gives

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}-\mathbf{Y}=I_{1}+I_{2}\quad\mbox{where}\quad I_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}L_{j}(Y_{i})g^{\prime}(1+\theta_{1}\eta_{j,n})e_{j}\,d\theta_{1},

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\quad I_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}})g^{\prime}(1+\theta_{1}\eta_{j,n})e_{j}\,d\theta_{1}.

Similarly, the first-order Taylor series expansion of
g^{\prime}(x)
at
x=1
implies

\displaystyle I_{2}=I_{3}+I_{4}\quad\mbox{where}\quad I_{3}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}})e_{j}

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\quad I_{4}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}})\theta_{1}\eta_{j,n}g^{\prime\prime}(1+\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\eta_{j,n})e_{j}\,d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}.

The term of interest can be bounded as

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}[H(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})-H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right)]\right|\leq\left|\mathbb{E}[H(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})-H\left(\mathbf{Y}+I_{2}\right)]\right|+\left|\mathbb{E}[H(\mathbf{Y}+I_{2})-H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right)]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left|\mathbb{E}[H(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})-H\left(\mathbf{Y}+I_{2}\right)]\right|+\left|\mathbb{E}[H(\mathbf{Y}+I_{2})-H\left(\mathbf{Y}+I_{3}\right)]\right|+\left|\mathbb{E}[H(\mathbf{Y}+I_{3})-H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right)]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad:=\mathrm{R}_{1}+\mathrm{R}_{2}+\mathrm{R}_{3}.

We analyze three terms separately.
First, following the similar approach as the proof of Lemma 2.3 of
Bentkus and Götze, 1996
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}[H(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})-H\left(\mathbf{Y}+I_{2}\right)]\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\left[h_{1}\left\|I_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle=h_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{j}(Y_{i})\int_{0}^{1}g^{\prime}(1+\theta_{1}\eta_{j,n})\,d\theta_{1}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4h_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{j}(Y_{i})\right|\right]\leq 4h_{1}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\right]+n\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq 8h_{1}n\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}],

where we have used
\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 4
. The last step follows as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\right]=(\mathbb{E}[(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{1})^{2}])^{-1}\mathbb{E}[(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{1})^{2}]\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\right]\leq n\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]

since
\mathbb{E}[(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{1})^{2}]=1/n
from the definition of
Y_{1}
. To control
\mathrm{R}_{2}
, we note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\left[H\left(\mathbf{Y}+I_{2}\right)-H\left(\mathbf{Y}+I_{3}\right)\right]\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle H\left(\mathbf{Y}+I_{3}\right),I_{4}\right\rangle\right]\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\left[h_{1}\left\|I_{4}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle=h_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}})\eta_{j,n}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\theta_{1}g^{\prime\prime}(1+\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\eta_{j,n})\,d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq 12h_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}})\eta_{j,n}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq 12h_{1}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}})\right|^{2}\right]\right\}^{1/2}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{2}-1\right|^{2}\right]\right\}^{1/2},

where we used
\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 24
for the penultimate inequality and the last inequality is Cauchy Schwarz inequality.
Lemma

4
proves that

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{2}-1\right|^{2}\right]\right\}^{1/2}\leq Cn^{1/2}\log^{1/2}(ed)\left(\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{1/2},

with some universal constant
C>0
. Moreover,
Lemma

7
implies that

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}})\right|^{2}\right]\right\}^{1/2}\leq C\left(n^{1/2}\log(ed)\left(\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{1/2}+n\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}\right),

for a possibly different universal constant
C>0
. Hence, as long as
n\log^{2}(d)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{2}\leq Ch_{1}\left(n\log(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}+n\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}\right).

The quantity
\mathrm{R}_{3}
can be bounded as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{3}

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}[H(\mathbf{Y}+I_{3})-H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right)]\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\left[H\left(\mathbf{Y}+I_{3}\right)-H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right)-\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right),I_{3}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right),I_{3}\right\rangle\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathbb{E}[H(\mathbf{Y}+I_{3})-H(\mathbf{Y})-\langle\nabla H(\mathbf{Y}),I_{3}\rangle]\right|+\left|\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla H(\mathbf{Y}),I_{3}\rangle]\right|

\displaystyle=:\mathrm{R}_{4}+\mathrm{R}_{5}.

To bound
\mathrm{R}_{4}
, it follows by the second order Taylor expansion of
H
at
\mathbf{Y}
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{4}

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\left[(1-\tau)\left\langle\nabla^{2}H\left(\mathbf{Y}+\tau I_{3}\right),I_{3}^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[h_{2}\|I_{3}\|_{\infty}^{2}\right]=\frac{h_{2}}{8}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}})\right|^{2}\right],

where
\tau\sim{\rm unif}(0,1)
, independent of everything. Hence, it can be further bounded with the aid of
Lemma

7
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{4}\leq Ch_{2}\left(n\log^{2}(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]+n^{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

Finally, to control
\mathrm{R}_{5}
, we begin by noting that

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{5}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right),\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}})e_{j}\right\rangle\right]\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right),\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}(Y_{1},Y_{2})e_{j}\right\rangle\right]\right|.

(20)

To control the last expression, we write
\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}(Y_{1},Y_{2})e_{j}
and note from the first order Taylor expansion of
\nabla H
at
\mathbf{Y}_{-1}:=\mathbf{Y}-Y_{1}
that

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-1}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\right\rangle

(21)

\displaystyle\qquad+\,\mathbb{E}_{\tau_{1}}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{2}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-1}+\tau_{1}Y_{1}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\right\rangle\right],

(22)

where
\tau_{1}\sim{\rm Unif}(0,1)
and
\mathbb{E}_{\tau_{1}}
is the expectation taken over
\tau_{1}
. For (
21
), we do Taylor expansion once more on
\nabla H
at
\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}:=\mathbf{Y}-Y_{1}-Y_{2}
, which leads to

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-1}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{\tau_{2}}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{2}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{2}Y_{2}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{2}\right\rangle\right],

for an independent
\tau_{2}\sim{\rm Unif}(0,1)
. Since
\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}
and
(Y_{1},Y_{2})
are independent and
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})]=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\right\rangle\right]=\left\langle\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}\right)\right],\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\right]\right\rangle=0.

Moreover, since
Y_{1}
is independent from
\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}
and
Y_{2}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{2}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{2}Y_{2}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{2}\right\rangle\right]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{2}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{2}Y_{2}\right),\mathbb{E}_{Y_{1}}\left[\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\right]\otimes Y_{2}\right\rangle\right]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq h_{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{Y_{1}}[\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{2}]\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq h_{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{2})^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\right|\times\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|e_{j}^{\top}Y_{2}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq h_{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}+n^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\right]\right]\right\}\leq 2h_{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}],

where the last inequality follows from
\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}]\leq n\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{2}]
. Combining these gives the bound for the expected value of (
21
) as

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-1}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\right\rangle\right|\leq 2h_{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}].

To control (
22
), we inspect the quantity inside the expectation and apply Taylor expansion on
\nabla H
at
\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau Y_{1}
for given
\tau_{1}\in(0,1)
as

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla^{2}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-1}+\tau_{1}Y_{1}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\left\langle\nabla^{2}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{1}Y_{1}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{\tau_{3}}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{3}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{1}Y_{1}+\tau_{3}Y_{2}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\otimes Y_{2}\right\rangle\right],

(23)

for
\tau_{3}\sim{\rm Unif}(0,1)
. The independence of
Y_{2}
and
(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}},Y_{1})
helps control the leading term on the right-hand side of (
4.1
) as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{2}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{1}Y_{1}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{2}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{1}Y_{1}\right),\mathbb{E}_{Y_{2}}[\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})]\otimes Y_{1}\right\rangle\right]=0.

For the rightmost term in (
4.1
), we apply Taylor expansion once more on
\nabla^{3}H
at
\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{3}Y_{2}
for a fixed
\tau_{2}\in(0,1)
to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{3}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{1}Y_{1}+\tau_{3}Y_{2}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\otimes Y_{2}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{3}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{3}Y_{2}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\otimes Y_{2}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{4}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{1}\tau_{4}Y_{1}+\tau_{3}Y_{2}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\otimes Y_{2}\otimes(\tau_{1}Y_{1})\right\rangle\right].

Note that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{3}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{3}Y_{2}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\otimes Y_{2}\right\rangle\right]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{3}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{3}Y_{2}\right),\mathbb{E}_{Y_{1}}[\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}]\otimes Y_{2}\right\rangle\right]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq h_{3}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{Y_{1}}[\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}]\otimes Y_{2}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq h_{3}\left(\left\|\mathbb{E}Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\vee\frac{1}{n}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{2})^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\right|\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|e_{j}^{\top}Y_{2}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2h_{3}n^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}].

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{4}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{1}\tau_{4}Y_{1}+\tau_{3}Y_{2}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\otimes Y_{2}\otimes(\tau_{1}Y_{1})\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq h_{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\otimes Y_{2}\otimes(\tau_{1}Y_{1})\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{h_{4}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{2})^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\right|\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|e_{j}^{\top}Y_{2}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq h_{4}\left(\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{2}.

Combining these controls the last term in (
4.1
) as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{3}H\left(\mathbf{Y}_{-\{1,2\}}+\tau_{1}Y_{1}+\tau_{3}Y_{2}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\otimes Y_{1}\otimes Y_{2}\right\rangle\right]\leq 2h_{3}n^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]+h_{4}\left(\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{2}.

Putting all together leads to

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left\langle\nabla H\left(\mathbf{Y}\right),\mathbf{U}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\right\rangle\right|\leq 2h_{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]+2h_{3}n^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]+h_{4}\left(\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{5}\leq h_{2}n\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}n\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]+h_{3}n\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]+h_{4}\left(n\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{2}.

Hence, as long as
n\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}]^{3}\leq 1
, one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{5}\leq h_{2}n\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}+h_{3}n\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]+h_{4}\left(n\mathbb{E}[\left\|Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{2}.

Putting all together concludes the proof.

∎

.

Proof of
Lemma

7
.

(Y^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 128\Bigg[\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})\right|^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Bigg\{(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{2}-1/n\bigg\}\right|^{2}\right]

lemma

8
, we conclude with some universal constant
C
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}U_{j}(Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}})\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\left(\log(ed)+\left(n\log^{2}(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{2/3}+\left(n\log(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{1/2}+n^{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\left(n\log(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]+\left(n\log^{1/2}(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{4/3}\right)+C\left(n\log(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]+n^{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\Big[n\log^{2}(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]+\left(n\log^{5/4}(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{4/3}+\left(n\log^{7/5}(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{5/3}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(n\log(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{2}+\left(n\log(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{3/2}+\left(n\log^{7/11}(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{11/6}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+Cn^{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\left[1+n\log(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]+\left(n\log^{1/2}(ed)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{4/3}\right].

Hence, the lemma follows.

Proof of
Lemma

8
.

All three results can be unified such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{ij}\right|^{2}\right]
for
\xi_{ij}\in\{e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i},(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{2}-1/n,(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{3}-e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}/n\}
. For any choice of

, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{ij}\right|^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right|^{2}\right]+\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)\right]\right)^{2}+{\rm Var}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)\right]+\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)\right]\right)^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)^{2}\right]+\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)\right]\right)^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)^{2}-\mathbb{E}[\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)^{2}]\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)^{2}\right]+\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right|^{2}

where the first inequality is the application of Theorem 11.1 of
Boucheron et al., 2013
.
Furthermore, by Proposition B.1 of
Kuchibhotla and Patra, 2022
, it follows for any
q>1
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)\right|\right]\right)^{2}\lesssim n\log(1+d)\max_{j}{\rm Var}(\xi_{1j})+\left(n\log^{q-1}(1+d)\mathbb{E}[\|\xi_{1}\|_{\infty}^{q}]\right)^{2/q}.

Similarly, for any
q>2
, Proposition B.1 of
Kuchibhotla and Patra, 2022
implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)^{2}-\mathbb{E}[\left(\xi_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{ij}]\right)^{2}]\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left(n\log(1+d)\max_{j}\mathbb{E}(\xi_{1j}^{4})\right)^{1/2}+\left(n\log^{q/2-1}(1+d)\mathbb{E}[\|\xi_{1}\|_{\infty}^{q}]\right)^{2/q}.

It remains to apply these bounds to each choice in
\{e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i},(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{2}-1/n,(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{3}-e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}/n\}
. This yields the following results:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\log(1+d)+\left(n\log^{2}(1+d)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{2/3}+\left(n\log(1+d)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{1/2}+n^{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\right\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{2}-1\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\log(1+d)\max_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{4}]+\left(n\log^{1/2}(1+d)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{4/3}+n\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim n\log(1+d)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]+\left(n\log^{1/2}(1+d)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]\right)^{4/3}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{3}-\frac{e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}}{n}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim n^{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}+n^{2}\max_{j}\mathbb{E}^{2}[|e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}|^{3}]+n\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\log(1+d)\max_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{3}]+\left(n\log^{q-1}(1+d)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3q}]\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim n\log(1+d)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]+n^{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}+n^{2}\max_{j}\mathbb{E}^{2}[|e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}|^{3}].

We further note that
\mathbb{E}[|e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}|^{3}]\leq\mathbb{E}[|e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}|^{2}]=1/n
, and thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i})^{3}-\frac{e_{j}^{\top}Y_{i}}{n}\right|^{2}\right]\lesssim n\log(1+d)\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{1}\|_{\infty}^{3}]+n^{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}Y_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}.

This concludes the claim.
∎

4.2
Gaussian approximation

Proposition 1
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be centered independent random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
where
X_{i}=(X_{i1},\ldots,X_{id})^{\top}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Suppose that there exists positive constants
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
, and a sequence of positive reals
\{B_{n}\geq 1\}
such that

b_{1}\leq\min_{1\leq j\leq d}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}^{2}]\right)^{1/2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}^{4}]\right)^{1/4}\leq b_{2}B_{n}.

(28)

Furthermore, suppose there exists a sequence of reals
\{D_{n}\geq 1\}
such that

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(\mathbb{E}[\|X_{i}\|_{\infty}^{q}]\right)^{1/q}\leq D_{n},

(29)

for some
q>2
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a class of hyperrectangles in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
G\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_{n})
where
\Sigma_{n}={\rm Var}(n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i})
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(b_{1},b_{2},q)>0
such that

\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(G\in A\right)\right|\leq C\left[\left(\frac{B_{n}^{4}\log^{5}(d)}{n}\right)^{1/4}+\frac{D_{n}\log^{3/2}(d)}{n^{1/2-1/q}}\right].

This proposition refines Theorem 2.5 of
Chernozhuokov et al., 2022
where the author set
D_{n}=B_{n}^{2}
. The current version is more informative since
B_{n}^{2}
and
D_{n}
scale differently in general.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

We shall show that

\chi_{n}:=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\preceq y\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(G\preceq y\right)\right|\leq C\left[\left(\frac{B_{n}^{4}\log^{5}(d)}{n}\right)^{1/4}+\frac{D_{n}\log^{3/2}(d)}{n^{1/2-1/q}}\right],

which implies the desired result. Here,
x\preceq y
denotes
e_{j}^{\top}x\leq e_{j}^{\top}y
for all
j=1,\ldots,d
. We use Lemma A.1 of
Chernozhuokov et al., 2022
which states that

\displaystyle\chi_{n}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg[\phi\log^{2}(d)\sqrt{\Delta_{1}}+\phi^{3}\log^{7/2}(d)\Delta_{1}+\log(d)\sqrt{\Delta_{2}(\phi)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\phi\log^{3/2}(d)\Delta_{2}(\phi)+\log^{3/2}(d)\sqrt{\Delta_{3}(\phi)}+\frac{\log^{1/2}(d)}{\phi}\bigg],

(30)

for all
\phi>0
where
C>0
only depends on
b_{1}
and

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}^{4}],\quad\Delta_{2}(\phi)=\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{ij}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}>(\phi\log(d))^{-1}\}\right],

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}>(\phi\log(d))^{-1}\}\right],

and
Y_{i}=(X_{i}-\tilde{X}_{i})/\sqrt{n}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
and
\{\tilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{X}_{n}\}
is an independent copy of
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
. First, it is immediate that

\Delta_{1}\leq n^{-1}B_{n}^{2}.

(31)

To analyze
\Delta_{2}(\phi)
, we first note that

\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[Y_{ij}^{4}]\lesssim n^{-2}B_{n}^{4},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}^{q}\right]\lesssim n^{-q/2}D_{n}^{q},

(32)

where
\lesssim
denotes an inequality that holds up to constant depending only on
b_{1},b_{2}
and
q
. We observe that

\displaystyle Y_{ij}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}>A\}

\displaystyle=Y_{ij}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{|Y_{ij}|>A,\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}>A\}+Y_{ij}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{|Y_{ij}|\leq A,\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}>A\}

\displaystyle=Y_{ij}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{|Y_{ij}|>A\}+Y_{ij}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{|Y_{ij}|\leq A,\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}>A\}

\displaystyle\leq Y_{ij}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{|Y_{ij}|>A\}+A^{2}\mathbf{1}\{\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}>A\},

for any
A>0
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{ij}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}>A\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{ij}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{|Y_{ij}|>A\}\right]+A^{2}\mathbb{P}\left(\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}>A\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{Y_{ij}^{4}}{A^{2}}\mathbf{1}\{|Y_{ij}|>A\}\right]+A^{2}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}^{q}]}{A^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[Y_{ij}^{4}]}{A^{2}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}^{q}]}{A^{q-2}}.

We take
A=(\phi\log(d))^{-1}
, then (
32
) with leads to

\Delta_{2}(\phi)\lesssim\frac{(\phi\log(d))^{2}B_{n}^{4}}{n}+\frac{(\phi\log(d))^{q-2}D_{n}^{q}}{n^{q/2-1}}.

(33)

The quantity
\Delta_{3}(\phi)
can be controlled as

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}>(\phi\log(d))^{-1}\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\phi\log(d))^{q-2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y_{i}\|_{\infty}^{q}\right]\lesssim\frac{(\phi\log(d))^{q-2}D_{n}^{q}}{n^{q/2-1}},

(34)

where the last inequality follows from (
32
). Hence, combining (
31
), (
33
), (
4.2
), and (
4.2
) yields

\displaystyle\chi_{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\phi\log^{2}(d)B_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\phi^{3}\log^{7/2}(d)B_{n}^{2}}{n}+\frac{\phi\log^{2}(d)B_{n}^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\phi^{q/2-1}\log^{q/2}(d)D_{n}^{q/2}}{n^{q/4-1/2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\phi^{3}\log^{7/2}(d)B_{n}^{4}}{n}+\frac{\phi^{q-1}\log^{q-1/2}(d)D_{n}^{q}}{n^{q/2-1}}+\frac{\phi^{q/2-1}\log^{q/2+1/2}(d)D_{n}^{q/2}}{n^{q/4-1/2}}+\frac{\log^{1/2}(d)}{\phi},

for all
\phi>0
. Here, we used that
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
. Since
B_{n}\geq 1
and
\log(d)\geq 1
, we can further deduce that

\chi_{n}\lesssim\frac{\phi\log^{2}(d)B_{n}^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\phi^{3}\log^{7/2}(d)B_{n}^{4}}{n}+\frac{\phi^{q-1}\log^{q-1/2}(d)D_{n}^{q}}{n^{q/2-1}}+\frac{\phi^{q/2-1}\log^{q/2+1/2}(d)D_{n}^{q/2}}{n^{q/4-1/2}}+\frac{\log^{1/2}(d)}{\phi}.

We choose

\phi^{-1}=\frac{B_{n}\log^{3/4}(d)}{n^{1/4}}+\frac{D_{n}\log(d)}{n^{1/2-1/q}}.

This leads to

\displaystyle\frac{\phi\log^{2}(d)B_{n}^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\phi^{3}\log^{7/2}(d)B_{n}^{4}}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{B_{n}\log^{3/4}(d)}{n^{1/4}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\log^{2}(d)B_{n}^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\left(\frac{B_{n}\log^{3/4}(d)}{n^{1/4}}\right)^{-3}\frac{\phi\log^{7/2}(d)B_{n}^{4}}{n}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2B_{n}\log^{5/4}(d)}{n^{1/4}}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{q-1}\log^{q-1/2}(d)D_{n}^{q}}{n^{q/2-1}}+\frac{\phi^{q/2-1}\log^{q/2+1/2}(d)D_{n}^{q/2}}{n^{q/4-1/2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(\frac{D_{n}\log(d)}{n^{1/2-1/q}}\right)^{-(q-1)}\frac{\log^{q-1/2}(d)D_{n}^{q}}{n^{q/2-1}}+\left(\frac{D_{n}\log(d)}{n^{1/2-1/q}}\right)^{-(q/2-1)}\frac{\log^{q/2+1/2}(d)D_{n}^{q/2}}{n^{q/4-1/2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{D_{n}\log^{1/2}(d)}{n^{1/2-1/q}}+\frac{D_{n}\log^{3/2}(d)}{n^{1/2-1/q}}\leq\frac{D_{n}\log^{3/2}(d)}{n^{1/2-1/q}}.

Finally, one has

\frac{\log^{1/2}(d)}{\phi}=\frac{B_{n}\log^{5/4}(d)}{n^{1/4}}+\frac{D_{n}\log^{3/2}(d)}{n^{1/2-1/q}}.

This proves the proposition.
∎

Abel maps for integral curves via a derived perspective
Qingyuan Jiang

Abstract
. We develop a general framework for Abel maps associated with a family X / S X/S of integral curves using derived algebraic geometry. For compactified Picard schemes, our approach yields relative quasi-smooth derived enhancements of the Quot schemes Quot ω / X / S d \mathrm{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{d} and, in the Gorenstein case, of the Hilbert schemes of points Hilb X / S d \mathrm{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d} on X / S X/S . These constructions naturally generalize to higher rank torsion-free sheaves and their coherent systems. We obtain unified semiorthogonal decompositions for the derived categories of these derived moduli spaces, broadly extending previous results for symmetric powers, varieties of linear series, and Thaddeus pairs to torsion-free sheaves on integral curves. Central to our approach are two novel tools of independent interest: the 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} -complex, a derived generalization of Grothendieck’s Q Q -module and the Altman–Kleiman H H -module, and a derived theory of moduli of extensions that extends Lange’s classical framework.



1.
Introduction

Let
X\to S
be a flat, projective map of locally Noetherian schemes, whose geometric fibers are integral curves of arithmetic genus
p_{a}\geq 1
. For simplicity, we assume that the smooth locus of
X\to S
admits a section
x\colon S\to X
.

The (rigidified) Picard functor
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}
assigns to each
S
-scheme
T
the isomorphism classes

\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}(T)=\left\{(\mathscr{L},u)\mid\text{$\mathscr{L}$ is a line bundle on $X_{T}:=X\times_{S}T$},\ u\colon\mathscr{O}_{T}\xrightarrow{\simeq}x_{T}^{*}(\mathscr{L})\right\}/\simeq.

This functor is represented by a disjoint union
\coprod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}^{d}
of
S
-schemes, where each
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}^{d}
parametrizes line bundles of degree
d
on the fibers of
X/S
(
[
Gro61
,
Kle05
]
). However, the Picard scheme
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}^{d}
is generally not proper over
S
unless
X/S
is smooth.

Altman and Kleiman
[
AK79
,
AK80
]
constructed a compactification
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}
of
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}
, which assigns to each
S
-scheme
T
the set of isomorphism classes

\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}(T)=\left\{(\mathscr{I},u)\mid\begin{array}[]{l}\text{$\mathscr{I}$ is a $T$-flat coherent sheaf on $X_{T}$ that is rank $1$,}\\
\text{torsion-free on fibers of $X_{T}/T$,}~u\colon\mathscr{O}_{T}\xrightarrow{\simeq}x_{T}^{*}(\mathscr{I})\end{array}\right\}/\simeq.

By
[
AK79
, Thm. 3.4(3)]
and
[
AK80
, Thm. 8.5]
, this functor is represented by a disjoint union
\coprod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d}
of projective
S
-schemes, where each
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d}
parametrizes rank
1
, torsion-free sheaves of degree
d
on the fibers of
X/S
. Each
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d}
contains
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}^{d}
as an open subscheme.

A key tool in studying these compactified Picard schemes is the
Abel map

[
AK80
, (8.2)]

A_{\omega}^{d}\colon\operatorname{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{2p_{a}-2-d}\to\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d}.

Here, for an integer
m
,
\operatorname{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{m}
denotes the Quot scheme parametrizing degree
m
torsion quotients of the relative dualizing sheaf
\omega
on fibers of
X/S
. Concretely, for any
S
-scheme
T
,

\operatorname{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{m}(T)=\left\{\omega_{X_{T}/T}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{G}\mid\mathscr{G}\text{ is $T$-flat, rank $0$, degree $m$ on fibers of $X_{T}/T$}\right\}/\simeq,

and the Abel map sends such a quotient to its kernel. The fiber of
A_{\omega}^{d}
over a point
[I]\in\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{d}(X_{s})
is the projective space
\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{Hom}_{X_{s}}(I,\omega_{X_{s}})^{\vee})\simeq\mathbb{P}(H^{1}(X_{s},I))
, where
X_{s}:=X\times_{S}\{s\}
.

It is often useful to consider the “modified" Abel map (
[
Kas13
, Definition 5.0.8]
)

A^{d}\colon\operatorname{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{d}\to\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d},

obtained by composing
A_{\omega}^{2p_{a}-2-d}
with the duality isomorphism
\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}(-,\omega)\colon\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{2p_{a}-2-d}\xrightarrow{\sim}\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d}
. The fiber of
A^{d}
over a point
[I]\in\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{d}(X_{s})
is the projective space
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X_{s},I)^{\vee})
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.4
)
.

There are natural derived enhancements of the Abel maps,

\mathbf{A}^{d}\colon\mathbf{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{d}\to\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d},\qquad\mathbf{A}_{\omega}^{d}\colon\mathbf{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{2p_{a}-2-d}\to\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d},

which are quasi-smooth (i.e., locally complete intersection in the derived sense) for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, for every integer
d\geq p_{a}-1
, there is a semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{D}(\mathbf{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{d})=\left\langle\mathrm{D}(\mathbf{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{2p_{a}-2-d}),\ \text{$(1-p_{a}+d)$ copies of }\mathrm{D}(\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d})\right\rangle,

where
\mathrm{D}
denotes any of the standard derived categories:
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}
,
\mathrm{D}^{-}_{\mathrm{coh}}
,
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{coh}}
, or
\mathrm{Perf}
.

This theorem provides a categorification of the fiberwise Riemann–Roch formula:

\dim_{\kappa(s)}H^{0}(X_{s},I)-\dim_{\kappa(s)}H^{1}(X_{s},I)=1-p_{a}+d,

where
I\in\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{d}(X_{s})
is a rank-one, torsion-free sheaf of degree
d
on the curve
X_{s}
.

Our strategy in §
4.2
also extends to suitable derived enhancements of
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{d}(X_{s})
.

It is worth noting that generally the compactified Picard schemes
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{d}(X_{s})
can be highly singular, even when endowed with their natural derived structures. Nevertheless, the derived Abel maps constructed here are always quasi-smooth, and the theorem applies even when neither the source nor the target of the Abel map is quasi-smooth.

Several notable cases of the theorem include:

(1)

Gorenstein case (§
4.2.2
):
If all the geometric fibers
X_{s}
of
X/S
are Gorenstein, the relative dualizing sheaf
\omega
is invertible, and tensoring with
\omega
gives a canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d}\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{d}.

For clarity, we switch the upper and lower indices from the Quot scheme case and define
A_{d}=A^{d}\circ(\underline{\hphantom{A}}\otimes\omega)
and
A^{\omega}_{d}=A^{d}_{\omega}\circ(\underline{\hphantom{A}}\otimes\omega)
. The Abel maps then become

\displaystyle A_{d}\colon\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d}\to\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d},\qquad A^{\omega}_{d}\colon\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{2p_{a}-2-d}\to\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d},

where a zero-dimensional subscheme
Z\subseteq X_{s}
of length
d
(respectively, length
2p_{a}-2-d
) is sent to the sheaf
\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}(I_{Z},\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}})
(respectively,
I_{Z}\otimes\omega
), with
I_{Z}
the ideal sheaf of
Z
.

The fiber of the Abel map
A_{d}
over a point
J\in\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{d}(X_{s})
is the projective space
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X_{s},J)^{\vee})
, which coincides with the complete linear system
|J|
of
generalized divisors
of the rank-
1
, torsion-free sheaf
J
, studied by Hartshorne
[
Har86
,
Har94
,
Har07
]
.

In this case, our theorem provides natural derived enhancements
\mathbf{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d}
of the classical Hilbert schemes
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d}
for all
d
, and tensoring with
\omega
induces an isomorphism of derived stacks
\mathbf{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{d}
.

Note that we define
\mathbf{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d}
to be
\mathbf{Quot}_{\mathscr{O}_{X}/X/S}^{d}
, in line with the classical construction of

. This differs from the approach in
[

holds verbatim after replacing all

(2)

Locally planar case (§
4.2.3
):
If
X/S
is a family of locally planar curves, then the derived Hilbert schemes are classical (Lemma
4.7
):

\mathbf{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d}=\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d},

and the theorem gives a semiorthogonal decomposition for all

:

\mathrm{D}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d})=\left\langle\mathrm{D}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{2p_{a}-2-d}),\ \text{$(d-p_{a}+1)$ copies of }\mathrm{D}(\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d})\right\rangle.

This greatly extends the semiorthogonal decompositions for symmetric powers of smooth complex projective curves due to Toda
[

(see also

, Corollary 3.10]
,

, and
[

As discussed in

, the above semiorthogonal decomposition can be regarded as a categorification of the Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber decomposition theorem for the map

in this context.
In this sense, our result provides a coherent counterpart to the generalized Macdonald formula of Maulik–Yun
[

[

Notably, in contrast to previous approaches in the constructible setting

When

\displaystyle P_{m,C}-P_{-m,C}=(-1)^{m-1}mN_{1,C},

where

.

We also note that families of integral curves and their compactified Picard schemes arise naturally in various contexts, including the study of moduli spaces of torsion sheaves on K3 surfaces

and in Hitchin fibrations for Higgs bundles

Finally, we expect the results of the theorem to be optimal: for a smooth projective curve

is indecomposable for

1.1.
Abel maps, torsion-free sheaves and coherent systems

To prove the Theorem
1.1
, we introduce a general framework for Abel maps associated to families of integral curves
X/S
(§
4.1
). Specifically, for any suitable derived moduli stack
\mathscr{M}
, we define Abel maps and establish corresponding semiorthogonal decompositions (Theorems
4.2
&
4.3
).

This generality allows us to treat not only compactified Picard schemes, but also higher rank torsion-free sheaves, and
coherent systems
(i.e., Brill–Noether pairs in the sense of
[
KN95
]
). In particular, our framework encompasses generalizations of the classical varieties of linear series
G_{d}^{\ell}
studied in
[
ACGH13
, Chapter IV]
(see Corollary
4.9
), as well as extensions of the moduli of Thaddeus pairs
[
Tha94
]
from vector bundles to torsion-free sheaves.

For example, when
\mathscr{M}=\underline{\mathrm{Coh}}_{X/S}^{\mathrm{tf}}(\rho,d)
, the moduli stack of torsion-free sheaves of rank
\rho
and degree
d
on
X/S
, we obtain derived enhancements of the moduli spaces
G(\rho,d,\ell)
of
\ell
-dimensional coherent systems considered in
[
KN95
]
. The Abel maps for these systems are

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\rho,d,\ell}\colon\mathbf{G}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell)

\displaystyle\to\underline{\mathrm{Coh}}_{X/S}^{\mathrm{tf}}(\rho,d),

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\rho,d,\ell}_{\omega}\colon\mathbf{G}^{\omega}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell)

\displaystyle\to\underline{\mathrm{Coh}}_{X/S}^{\mathrm{tf}}(\rho,d),

where, for a morphism
s\colon\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)\to S
, the
s
-points of
\mathbf{G}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell)
and
\mathbf{G}^{\omega}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell)
parametrize coherent systems
(E,V)
and
(E,W)
: here
E
is a torsion-free sheaf of rank
\rho
and degree
d
on
X_{s}
, and
V\subseteq H^{0}(X_{s},E)
,
W\subseteq H^{1}(X_{s},E)^{\vee}
are linear subspaces of dimension
\ell
. The Abel maps simply record the underlying torsion-free sheaf
E
of each coherent system.

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary
4.10
)
.

The Abel maps
\mathbf{A}^{\rho,d,\ell}
and
\mathbf{A}^{\rho,d,\ell}_{\omega}
are quasi-smooth. If
\ell=1
or if
S
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme, then for all
d\geq(p_{a}-1)\rho
, there is an
\mathscr{M}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition:

\operatorname{D}\left(\mathbf{G}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell)\right)=\left\langle\text{$\binom{(1-p_{a})\rho+d}{j}$ copies of }\operatorname{D}\left(\mathbf{G}^{\omega}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell-j)\right)\,\Big|\,0\leq j\leq\ell\right\rangle.

The
\mathscr{M}
-linearity ensures that the semiorthogonal decomposition is preserved under base change along any morphism to
\mathscr{M}
. This, in particular, allows one to restrict the decomposition to open substacks such as
\mathcal{B}un_{X/S}(\rho,d)
, the moduli stack of vector bundles, as well as to any other substacks of interest.

Our results considerably extend the range of known semiorthogonal decompositions for moduli spaces associated with curves. In particular, this framework generalizes earlier results for symmetric powers of smooth curves
[
Tod21
,
JL22
,
BK24
,
Jia22a
,
KT21
]
and incorporates recent developments for varieties of linear series on general curves
[
Jia23b
,
Tod23
]
. In the case where
\ell=1
, the theorem above also extends a result of Koseki and Toda
[
KT21
]
on semiorthogonal decomposition for moduli of Thaddeus pairs for stable vector bundles on smooth curves to the context of torsion-free sheaves on integral curves.

We also emphasize that, although the main focus of this paper is on the case of curves, the general methodology of this paper applies more broadly, including to stable pair moduli spaces, surfaces, two-dimensional categories, and Calabi–Yau families; see §
3.5
for further details.

1.2.
The
\mathcal{Q}
-complex and moduli of extensions

We work within the framework of derived algebraic geometry, as developed by Lurie, Toën, Vezzosi, and others
[
Lur-DAG
,
Lur-HTT
,
Lur-SAG
,
Toë14
,
GR19
]
. A central role in our approach is played by the theory of derived projectivizations and Grassmannians, introduced by the author in
[
Jia22a
,
Jia22b
,
Jia23b
]
.

Our methodology is based on two key technical ingredients:

(1)

The
\mathcal{Q}
-complex, developed in Section
2
, which is a derived generalization of Grothendieck’s
Q
-module
[
EGA
, III
2
§7.7]
and the Altman–Kleiman
H
-module
[
AK80
, (1.1)]
.

(2)

A derived theory of moduli of extensions, presented in Section
3
, extending Lange’s classical theory
[
Lag83
]
to the derived context.

Specifically, in Section
2
, for a morphism
f\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{M}
of derived stacks and complexes
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathscr{X})
, we construct, under mild assumptions, a complex
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
that serves as a dual to the pushforward
f_{*}\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
, without assuming perfectness of
\mathscr{F}
or finite Tor-amplitude for
f
. The formation of
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
is compatible with base change, and we provide fiberwise criteria for its Tor-amplitude (Corollary
2.10
).

The
\mathcal{Q}
-complex is a versatile tool with broad applicability and independent interest. For example, it yields an explicit description of the cotangent complex of the derived moduli stack of coherent sheaves
\underline{\mathbf{Coh}}(X)
on a proper scheme
X
(Lemma
2.15
), complementing the foundational existence result of Porta–Sala
[
PS19
, Lemma 2.21]
.

In Section
3
, we use the
\mathcal{Q}
-complex, together with derived projectivizations and Grassmannians, to develop a natural framework for moduli stacks of extensions. Specifically, given
f\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathscr{X})
as above, we construct derived moduli stacks
\mathbf{PExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
parametrizing non-split
i
th extensions of
\mathscr{E}
by
\mathscr{F}
over
\mathscr{M}
(§
3.2
), as well as Grassmannian-type moduli stacks of extensions
\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F};\ell)
(§
3.4
).

These constructions are especially useful in Brill–Noether contexts (see §
3.3
), extending the Hecke moduli spaces studied in
[
Neg19
,
Neg22
,
Jia22b
]
.

In §
3.5
, we further establish semiorthogonal decompositions for these moduli stacks of extensions (Theorems
3.13
and
3.14
).

1.4.
Notations and conventions

We adopt the following notational and terminological conventions throughout this paper, aiming to make our exposition accessible to readers familiar with classical algebraic geometry as well as those versed in derived settings.

•

Derived conventions for functors.

All functors are interpreted in the
derived
sense. For example, for a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of derived stacks, we write the pullback as
f^{*}\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
and the tensor product in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
as
\otimes
, where
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
denotes the stable
\infty
-category
\mathrm{QCoh}(X)
defined in
[
Lur-SAG
, §6.2.2]
.

When
f\colon X\to Y
is a morphism of underived schemes, the homotopy category of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
recovers the usual triangulated quasi-coherent derived category
D_{QCoh}(\mathscr{O}_{X})
of
[
Sta
,
Tag 06YZ
]
. In this case, our notation
f^{*}
corresponds to the classical derived pullback
\mathbb{L}f^{*}
,
f_{*}
to the derived pushforward
\mathbb{R}f_{*}
,
\otimes
to the derived tensor product
\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}
, and
\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}(-,-)
to the derived sheaf Hom
\mathbb{R}\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}(-,-)
, whenever defined.

The only exception is that we use
\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)=\operatorname{Ext}^{0}(A,B)
for the classical Hom group in abelian categories, and
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
for the total derived Hom group.

•

Classical terminology for sheaves and complexes.

To avoid confusion for classical readers, we refer to objects of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
as
quasi-coherent complexes
, even when
X
is a derived stack. Note, however, that such objects are not necessarily represented by actual complexes. We reserve the terminology “sheaves" for discrete objects in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
, that is, those whose homotopy groups (or, classically, sheaf cohomology) vanish in all nonzero degrees. This aligns with classical terminology for sheaves and complexes on schemes, but differs from the conventions in Lurie
[
Lur-SAG
]
.

•

Grading conventions.

We adopt homological grading conventions, as in Lurie
[
Lur-DAG
,
Lur-HA
,
Lur-SAG
]
, rather than the cohomological grading of
[
SGA6
]
and the Stacks project
[
Sta
]
. For instance, for a classical ring
R
, an
R
-module
M
has Tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
in our convention if and only if it has Tor-amplitude in
[-b,-a]
in the conventions of
[
SGA6
, Exposé III]
and
[
Sta
,
Tag 0651
]
.

•

Grothendieck’s convention.

For projectivizations and Grassmannians, we will use Grothendieck’s quotient convention as in
[
Jia22a
,
Jia22b
]
. For example, for a
\kappa
-vector space
V
, the projectivization
\mathbb{P}(V)
parametrizes one-dimensional quotients of
V
, so that
\mathbb{P}(V)(\kappa)\simeq(V^{\vee}\setminus\{0\})/\kappa^{\times}
, where
V^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\kappa}(V,\kappa)
denotes the dual vector space.

•

Derived stacks.

We use boldface letters such as
\mathbf{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d}
and
\mathbf{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{d}
for derived enhancements of classical moduli stacks. The corresponding classical stacks are denoted with standard roman letters, e.g.,
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d}
and
\operatorname{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{d}
.

In Sections
2
and
3
, where we develop the necessary theories within the framework of derived algebraic geometry, we will use terminology that is largely standard and mainly follows Lurie
[
Lur-DAG
,
Lur-HA
,
Lur-SAG
]
, subject to the conventions described above.

Let
\operatorname{SCR}
denotes the
\infty
-category of simplicial commutative rings (that is, animated rings) as defined in
[
Lur-SAG
, §25.1]
. A
prestack
is a functor
X
from
\operatorname{SCR}
to the
\infty
-category
\mathcal{S}
of spaces (i.e.,
\infty
-groupoids). A morphism
f\colon X\to Y
between prestacks is defined as a natural transformation of functors. The morphism
f
is called representable if for each
\operatorname{Spec}A\to Y
, the base change
X_{A}=X\times_{Y}\operatorname{Spec}A
is a derived scheme.

A
derived stack

X
is a prestack satisfying étale descent. Let
X_{\mathrm{cl}}\colon\operatorname{SCR}^{\heartsuit}\to\mathcal{S}
denote the sheafification of the restriction
X|_{\operatorname{SCR}^{\heartsuit}}
to discrete commutative rings; we call
X_{\mathrm{cl}}
the
classical truncation
of
X
. If
X=(|X|,\mathscr{O}_{X})
is a derived scheme, then
X_{\mathrm{cl}}=(|X|,\pi_{0}\mathscr{O}_{X})
.

A
point
of a derived stack

is an equivalence class of morphisms
x\colon\operatorname{Spec}\kappa\to X
, where
\kappa
is a field. By abuse of notation, we write
x\in X
for such a point, and choose a representative
x\colon\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(x)\to X
. If
X
is a derived scheme, points correspond to those of the underlying topological space
|X|
, and
\kappa(x)
may be taken as the residue field at
x\in|X|
.

A morphism
X\to Y
between derived stacks is called
representable
if, for any
\operatorname{Spec}A\to Y
, where
A\in\operatorname{SCR}
, the fiber product
X_{A}:=X\times_{Y}\operatorname{Spec}A
is a derived scheme.

For a derived stack
X
, we consider the following derived
\infty
-categories:

\mathrm{Perf}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{D}^{-}_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X).

Here,
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
denotes the stable
\infty
-category of quasi-coherent complexes on

as defined in
[
Lur-SAG
, §6.2.2]
, and
\mathrm{Perf}(X)
is its full subcategory of perfect complexes. Both categories admit explicit descriptions as limits:

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)\simeq\varprojlim_{\operatorname{Spec}A\to X}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\operatorname{Spec}A),\qquad\mathrm{Perf}(X)\simeq\varprojlim_{\operatorname{Spec}A\to X}\mathrm{Perf}(\operatorname{Spec}A),

where
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\operatorname{Spec}A)
is the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Mod}_{A^{\circ}}
of module spectra over the underlying
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring
A^{\circ}
of

(
[
Lur-HA
, Notation 7.1.1.1]
), and
\mathrm{Perf}(\operatorname{Spec}A)
is the stable subcategory of perfect objects (
[
Lur-HA
, Definition 7.2.4.1]
). The subcategories
\mathrm{D}^{-}_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)
and
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)
consist of almost perfect complexes (see
[
Lur-SAG
, Proposition 6.2.5.2(6)]
) and locally truncated (see
[
Lur-DAG
, Proposition 3.5.3]
,
[
Lur-SAG
, Notation 6.4.1.1]
) almost perfect complexes, respectively.

For a derived stack
X
,
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
admits a natural
t
-structure
(\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\geq 0},\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\leq 0})
, where
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\geq 0}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)^{\mathrm{cn}}
is the subcategory of connective complexes.
For an integer
n
, we let
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\geq n}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\geq 0}[n]
and
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\leq n}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\leq 0}[n]

\tau_{\geq n}\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\geq n}
(resp.
\tau_{\leq n}\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\leq n}
) denote the right (resp. left) adjoint functor of the inclusion functor. We let
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)^{\heartsuit}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\geq 0}\cap\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\leq 0}
and refer to its objects as
(discrete) sheaves
. We let

\pi_{n}=\tau_{\geq 0}\circ\tau_{\leq 0}\circ[-n]\simeq\tau_{\leq 0}\circ\tau_{\geq 0}\circ[-n]\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)^{\heartsuit}.

A morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of derived stacks is called
smooth
if it is locally almost of finite presentation and its relative cotangent complex

is perfect of Tor-amplitude
\leq 0
.

Following

, §1.3.3]
, we define by induction the notation of
n
-geometric

(-1)

(n-1)
-geometric stacks and morphisms have been defined, a derived stack

is

-geometric if its diagonal is
(n-1)
-geometric and there exists a disjoint union of derived affine schemes
U
together with a smooth
(n-1)
-geometric epimorphism

. A morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of derived stacks is
n
-geometric if for any morphism
\operatorname{Spec}A\to Y
with

, the base change

is
n
-geometric.

A derived stack

is called
geometric
if it is
n
-geometric for some
n
. This definition agrees with those in
[
Toë14
,
GR19
,
PS19
]
and is equivalent to the notion of derived Artin stack in
[
Kha20
]
. By
[
TVe08
, Proposition 2.2.5.1]
, a morphism

of derived geometric stacks is smooth if and only if it is a geometric smooth morphism in the sense of
[
TVe08
, §2.2.3]
.

A derived stack
X
is said to be a
perfect stack
(
[
BFN10
, Definition 3.2]
)
if it has affine diagonal and the inclusion
\mathrm{Perf}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
induces an equivalence

\operatorname{Ind}\mathrm{Perf}(X)\simeq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X).

A morphism of derived stacks
X\to Y
is said to be
perfect
if for all morphisms
U=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\to Y
from an affine derived scheme
U
, the fibers
X\times_{U}Y
are perfect.

For a perfect stack
X
, the stable
\infty
-category
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
is compactly generated, with compact objects given by the perfect complexes. Moreover, the natural
t
-structure on
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
is accessible, both left and right complete, and compatible with filtered colimits; see
[
Lur-SAG
, Corollary 9.1.3.2(2), (3), (4)]
.

Given a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of derived stacks, we write
f^{*}\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
for the pullback functor. If
f
is perfect,
f^{*}
admits a right adjoint, the pushforward functor
f_{*}\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y)
, which preserves small colimits. The projection formula and base-change formula hold for perfect morphisms; see
[
Lur-SAG
, Proposition 9.1.5.7]
.

For the reader’s convenience, we note that most morphisms considered in this paper are perfect. This includes, for instance, all morphisms between perfect stacks
[
BFN10
, Corollary 3.23]
, as well as morphisms that are relative quasi-compact, separated derived algebraic spaces (see
[
Lur-SAG
, Proposition 9.6.1.1]
or
[
BFN10
, Proposition 3.19]
). A special case of the latter case is that of locally (quasi-)projective morphisms.

For a perfect stack
X
, since
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
is presentable and the tensor product
\otimes\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)\times\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
commutes with small colimits separately in each variable, one obtains that for every pair
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
, there exists a quasi-coherent complex
\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
, called
(internal)
\operatorname{Hom}
-complex
, which satisfies the following universal property: there is an evaluation map
\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\to\mathscr{F}
such that, for every
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
,
the composite map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Map}_{X}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}))\to\mathrm{Map}_{X}(\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{G},\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}))\to\mathrm{Map}_{X}(\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{G},\mathscr{F})

is a homotopy equivalence; see
[
Lur-SAG
, §9.5.3]
for more details.

The
\mathcal{Q}
-complex

Let

be a map of derived stacks, and let
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
. Under suitable assumptions, we construct a complex
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
(Construction
2.7
) that serves as a dual to the pushforward of Hom complex
f_{*}\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})

This construction generalizes the classical Grothendieck

-module
[
EGA
, III
2
Theorem 7.7.6]
and the Altman–Kleiman’s module
H(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})

[
AK80

to the derived setting (see Remark

).

The formation of
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
is compatible with base change in

A key notion in this context is that of relative perfectness, which replaces the absolute perfectness assumption on

2.1.
Relative Tor-amplitudes

We now consider relative Tor-amplitude and relative perfectness in the derived setting, generalizing the classical notions of
[
SGA6
, Exposé III, §3]
.

Definition 2.1

(Relative Tor-amplitude and relative perfectness; see also
[
PS19
, §2.1]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a morphism of derived stacks, and let
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
. Let
a\leq b
and
n
be integers.

(1)

We say that
\mathscr{F}
has
relative Tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
(respectively,
relative Tor-amplitude
\leq n
) over
S
if, for every discrete sheaf
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)^{\heartsuit}
,

\pi_{i}(\mathscr{F}\otimes f^{*}(\mathscr{G}))=0\quad\text{for }i\not\in[a,b]\quad(\text{respectively, for }i>n).

We say that
\mathscr{F}
has
relative finite Tor-amplitude over
S
if there exist integers
a\leq b
such that
\mathscr{F}
has relative Tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
over
S
. We say that
\mathscr{F}
has
locally finite relative Tor-amplitude
over
S
if for every morphism
\operatorname{Spec}A\to S
with
A\in\operatorname{SCR}
, the base change of
\mathscr{F}
to
X\times_{S}\operatorname{Spec}A
has finite Tor-amplitude over
\operatorname{Spec}A
.

(2)

Suppose further that
f
is locally almost of finite presentation. We say that
\mathscr{F}
has
relative perfect amplitude in
[a,b]
(respectively,
\leq n
) over
S
if
\mathscr{F}
is almost perfect and has relative Tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
(respectively,
\leq n
) over
S
. We say that
\mathscr{F}
is
relatively perfect over
S
if it is almost perfect and has locally finite relative Tor-amplitude over
S
.

When
f=\operatorname{id}_{X}
, these definitions reduce to the usual notions of Tor-amplitude and perfectness on
X
. Note also that
\mathscr{F}
has relative Tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
if and only if
\mathscr{F}
has relative Tor-amplitude
\leq b
and
\pi_{i}(\mathscr{F})=0
for
i<a
.

Let
\mathrm{Perf}(X/S)
denote the full subcategory of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
consisting of complexes that are relatively perfect over
S
.
If
X\to\operatorname{Spec}k
is a Noetherian scheme over a field, then
\mathrm{Perf}(X/k)=\mathrm{D^{b}_{coh}}(X)
, the category bounded coherent complexes.
If
f=\operatorname{id}_{X}
, then
\mathrm{Perf}(X/X)=\mathrm{Perf}(X)
is the usual category of perfect complexes on
X
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
S
is a perfect stack, the condition that
\mathscr{F}
has relative Tor-amplitude
\leq n
over
S
is equivalent to the following apparently stronger statement:

•

The functor
\mathscr{G}\mapsto\mathscr{F}\otimes f^{*}(\mathscr{G})
restricts to

\mathscr{F}\otimes f^{*}(\underline{\hphantom{A}})\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)_{\leq 0}\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\leq n}.

In fact, consider any
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)_{\leq 0}
. Since the
t
-structure on
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)
is right complete (
[
Lur-SAG
, Corollary 9.1.3.2.(4)]
), we can write
\mathscr{G}=\varinjlim\tau_{\geq-m}(\mathscr{G})
. Using the fiber sequence

\tau_{\geq 1-m}(\mathscr{G})\to\tau_{\geq-m}(\mathscr{G})\to\pi_{-m}\mathscr{G}[-m],

and induction on
m\geq 0
, we obtain that
\pi_{i}(\mathscr{F}\otimes f^{*}(\mathscr{G}))=0
for all
i>n
.

The next lemma is a derived analogue of
[
SGA6
, Exposé III, Corollaries 3.7.1 and 4.8.1]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a representable morphism of derived stacks, locally almost of finite presentation, and let
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
.

(1)

If
\mathscr{F}
has finite relative Tor-amplitude over
S
, then
f_{*}(\mathscr{F})
has finite (absolute) Tor-amplitude.

(2)

If
f
is proper and
\mathscr{F}
is relatively perfect over
S
, then
f_{*}(\mathscr{F})
is perfect.

Proof.

Since these properties for
f_{*}(\mathscr{F})
are local on
S
and stable under base change, we may assume
S
is a derived affine scheme. Let
a\leq b
be integers. To prove (
1
), it suffices to show:

(*)

If
\mathscr{F}
has relative Tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
over
S
, then
f_{*}(\mathscr{F})
has Tor-amplitude in
[a-N,b]
for some integer
N\geq 0
.

For any
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)^{\heartsuit}
, from projection formula we have

f_{*}(\mathscr{F})\otimes\mathscr{G}\simeq f_{*}\big(\mathscr{F}\otimes f^{*}(\mathscr{G})\big).

Given our assumption on
\mathscr{F}
, we have
\pi_{i}(\mathscr{F}\otimes f^{*}(\mathscr{G}))=0
for
i\notin[a,b]
. By
[
Lur-SAG
, Proposition 2.5.4.4]
(see also
[
Lip
, Proposition 3.9.2]
for the classical case), there exists
N\geq 0
so that
f_{*}
sends
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)_{\geq a}
into
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)_{\geq a-N}
. Thus,

\pi_{i}\big(f_{*}(\mathscr{F}\otimes f^{*}(\mathscr{G}))\big)=0\quad\text{for }i\notin[a-N,b],

which proves (*).

If
f
is also proper and
\mathscr{F}
is almost perfect, then
f_{*}(\mathscr{F})
is almost perfect by
[
Lur-SAG
, Theorem 5.6.0.2]
(classically, this is Kiehl’s Finiteness Theorem
[
Lip
, Corollary 4.3.3.2]
). Thus, assertion (
2
) follows from (
1
).
∎

2.2.
The
\mathcal{Q}
-complex

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a morphism of derived stacks, and let
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
. In this subsection, we construct the
\mathcal{Q}
-complex
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
under suitable mild assumptions, which serves as a dual to
f_{*}\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
.

If
f
has finite Tor-amplitude and
\mathscr{F}
is perfect, the
\mathcal{Q}
-complex admits a simple description:

\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\simeq f_{+}\big(\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{F}^{\vee}\big);

see Remark
2.5

f_{+}
denotes the left adjoint to
f^{*}
constructed in
[
Lur-SAG
, §6.4.5]
. In this case, the formal properties of
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
follow from those of
f_{+}
in
[
Lur-SAG
, §6.4.5]
.

Our goal is to establish analogous results without the assumptions of finite Tor-amplitude for
f
or perfectness of
\mathscr{F}
.

We first focus on the case where
f
is a morphism between perfect stacks.

Construction 2.4

(The
\mathcal{Q}
-complex: perfect stack case)
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a proper, representable morphism between perfect stacks, locally almost of finite presentation. Let
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
, and assume that
\mathscr{F}
is relatively perfect over
S
. Consider the functor

\mathcal{Q}_{f}^{\mathrm{perf}}(\underline{\hphantom{A}},\mathscr{F})\colon\mathrm{Perf}(X)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S),

\mathscr{E}\mapsto f_{*}(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{F})^{\vee}\in\mathrm{Perf}(S)\subseteq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S).

This is well-defined by Lemma
2.3
.(
2
).
Since
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)\simeq\operatorname{Ind}\mathrm{Perf}(X)
, by the universal property of Ind-completionthe (
[
Lur-HTT
, Proposition 5.3.5.10]
), the finite-coproduct-preserving functor
\mathcal{Q}_{f}^{\mathrm{perf}}(\underline{\hphantom{A}},\mathscr{F})
extends essentially uniquely to a colimit-preserving functor

\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\underline{\hphantom{A}},\mathscr{F})\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S).

Remark 2.5
.

(1)

By definition, there are canonical equivalences

\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E}[1],\mathscr{F})\simeq\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}[-1])\simeq\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})[1].

Moreover, for any perfect complex
\mathscr{P}\in\mathrm{Perf}(X)
, we have a canonical equivalence

\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{P},\mathscr{F})\simeq\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{P}^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{F}).

(2)

For any
\mathscr{E}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
, writing
\mathscr{E}=\varinjlim_{\alpha}\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}
as a filtered colimits with
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Perf}(X)
, there is a canonical equivalence

\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\simeq\varinjlim_{\alpha}\left(f_{*}(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{F})^{\vee}\right).

(3)

If
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{Perf}(X)
and
f
is locally of finite Tor-amplitude, then
f^{*}
admits a left adjoint
f_{+}
(see
[
Lur-SAG
, Proposition 6.4.5.3]

\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\simeq f_{+}(\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{F}^{\vee}).

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume the setting of Construction
2.4
. Then:

(1)

(Universal property)

For any
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)
, there is a canonical equivalence

\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{S}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}),\mathscr{G}\big)\simeq f_{*}\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}\big(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\otimes f^{*}(\mathscr{G})\big)

in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)
, functorial with respect to
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)
.

In particular, applying global section functor, we obtain the following universal property for
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})

f\colon X\to S
be a morphism of derived geometric stacks that is proper, representable, locally almost of finite presentation, and locally of finite Tor-amplitude. Assume further that
\omega_{f}^{\bullet}
is locally truncated (e.g., if
\mathscr{O}_{S}
is locally truncated).

(1)

For any almost perfect complex
\mathscr{F}
, the internal Hom-complex

\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F}):=\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\omega_{f}^{\bullet})

defines a quasi-coherent complex on
X
, which is computed “smooth locally" by

\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F})|_{V}=\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{T}(\mathscr{F}|_{V},(\omega_{f}^{\bullet})|_{V})

for
V\to X
ranging over either of the following categories: all smooth morphisms with
V
derived affine, or
V=X\times_{S}U\to X
where
U\to S
is smooth and
U
is derived affine.

(2)

For any
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)
, with
\mathscr{E}
perfect and
\mathscr{F}
relatively perfect over
S
, there is a functorial equivalence in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)
:

\mathcal{Q}_{f}\big(\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F}),\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{E})\big)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}).

Proof.

Part (1) follows by applying the argument of Remark
2.8
to the almost perfect complex
\mathscr{F}
and the locally truncated complex
\omega_{f}^{\bullet}
.

For part (2), we first consider the case where
S
is derived affine. By the definition of the internal Hom complex, the identity map
\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F})\to\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F})
corresponds to a canonical map
\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F})\to\omega_{f}^{\bullet}
. Thus, for any
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S)
, there is an induced canonical map

(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}\otimes f^{*}\mathscr{G})\otimes\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F})\to(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}\otimes f^{*}\mathscr{G})\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet},

which in turn induces

\mathscr{E}^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{F}\otimes f^{*}\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}\left(\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F}),\,\mathscr{E}^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet}\otimes f^{*}\mathscr{G}\right).

This yields a functorial morphism

(2.1)

f_{*}\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}\left(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\otimes f^{*}\mathscr{G}\right)\to f_{*}\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{X}\left(\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F}),\,\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{E})\otimes f^{*}\mathscr{G}\right)

which, by the universal property Proposition
2.6
(
1
), induces a canonical map

\mathcal{Q}_{f}\big(\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F}),\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{E})\big)\to\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}).

It remains to show that the morphism in (
2.1
) is an isomorphism. First, we may assume
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{O}_{X}
. Since
f_{*}
preserves small colimits, and the formation of (
2.1
) commutes with small colimits in the variable
\mathscr{G}
, we may assume
\mathscr{G}
is perfect. As this also commutes with cones and direct sums in the variable
\mathscr{G}
, we reduce to
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{O}_{S}
. A similar argument reduces to the case
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{O}_{X}
. In this situation, the desired isomorphism follows from the canonical isomorphism

\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\omega_{f}^{\bullet},\omega_{f}^{\bullet})\to\mathscr{O}_{S}

given by Proposition
2.11
(
2
).

For general
S
, since both sides of the canonical morphism
\mathcal{Q}_{f}\big(\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F}),\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{E})\big)\to\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})

can be computed over smooth morphisms

with
U

U\to U^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{Aff}_{\mathrm{Sm}/S}
, the result follows from the derived affine case.
∎

The canonical map
\mathscr{F}\to\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{F}))
need not to be an equivalence in general. It is an equivalence, however, if
S
is Gorenstein (i.e., if
S
possesses an invertible absolute dualizing complex).

Let

3.
Moduli of extensions

Using the
\mathcal{Q}
-complex constructed in the previous section, together with the theory of derived affine cones, projectivizations, and Grassmannians
[
Jia22a
,
Jia22b
,
Jia23b
]
, we develop a natural derived framework for moduli stacks of extensions. This perspective provides a robust generalization of the classical constructions of
[

]
to the derived setting.

In this section, we fix a morphism of derived stacks

f\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{M}

which is proper, representable, locally almost of finite presentation, and locally of finite Tor-amplitude. We adopt this notation, rather than
X\to S
, since our main applications concern the case where
f
is the universal family over a moduli stack
\mathscr{M}
.

For
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathscr{X})
and any morphism
\eta\colon T\to\mathscr{M}
, we denote by
f_{T}\colon X_{T}\to T
the base change of
f
along
\eta
, where
X_{T}:=\mathscr{X}\times_{\mathscr{M}}T
is the derived fiber product. We write
\mathscr{E}_{T}
and
\mathscr{F}_{T}
for the derived pullbacks of
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{F}
to
X_{T}
. Note that when
\mathscr{X}/\mathscr{M}
is flat and
\mathscr{M}
and
T
are classical,
X_{T}
coincides with the classical fiber product
\mathscr{X}\times_{\mathscr{M}}^{\mathrm{cl}}T
.

3.1.
Linear stacks of extensions
\underline{\mathbf{Ext}}_{f}^{i}

The constructions and results in this subsection, though not required for the subsequent applications in this paper, fit naturally into the overall framework developed here and hold promise for future applications.

The
linear stack

\mathbf{V}(\mathcal{G})
of a complex
\mathcal{G}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathscr{M})
over a derived stack
\mathscr{M}
(see
[
Toë14
, §3.3]
,
[
Jia22a
, §4.1]
or
[
HKR
, §2]
) is the derived moduli stack which, for any morphism
T\to\mathscr{M}
from a derived affine scheme

, assigns the space (i.e.,
\infty
-groupoid) of cosections
\mathcal{G}_{T}\to\mathscr{O}_{T}
:

\mathbf{V}(\mathcal{G})(T)\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{T}(\mathcal{G}_{T},\mathscr{O}_{T}).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathscr{X})
, with
\mathscr{F}
relatively perfect over
\mathscr{M}
. Let
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
denote the complex of Construction
2.7
. For any integer

, we define the linear stack

\underline{\mathbf{Ext}}^{i}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}):=\mathbf{V}(\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}[i]))\to\mathscr{M}.

Explicitly, the functor of points of
\underline{\mathbf{Ext}}^{i}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
is described as follows: for any morphism
T\to\mathscr{M}
from a derived affine scheme
T
, by Proposition
2.6
(
1
)&(
2
), we have

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{Ext}}^{i}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})(T)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Map}_{T}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}[i])_{T},\mathscr{O}_{T}\big)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{T}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E}_{T},\mathscr{F}_{T}[i]),\mathscr{O}_{T}\big)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{X_{T}}\big(\mathscr{E}_{T},\mathscr{F}_{T}[i]\big),

i.e.,
\underline{\mathbf{Ext}}^{i}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})(T)
is the space of homomorphisms from
\mathscr{E}_{T}
to
\mathscr{F}_{T}[i]
in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X_{T})

Thus,
\underline{\mathbf{Ext}}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
is the derived moduli stack parametrizing
i

\mathscr{E}
by
\mathscr{F}
over
\mathscr{M}

The following properties are immediate from those of linear stacks (
[

):

Lemma 3.2
.

For any given

:

(1)

The formation of
\underline{\mathbf{Ext}}^{i}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\to\mathscr{M}
commutes with arbitrary base change in
\mathscr{M}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
is
i

\underline{\mathbf{Ext}}^{i}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\to\mathscr{M}

\mathscr{E}

(3)

If
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
is
n

(4)

If

Variant 3.3

(Rigidified moduli of extensions for simple complexes)
.

Assume the setting of §

\mathscr{E}
be an

-simple complex in

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathbf{Ext}}_{f}^{1}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E})}

3.2.
Moduli of classes of non-split extensions
\mathbf{PExt}_{f}^{i}

The
derived projectivization

\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{G})
of a connective complex
\mathcal{G}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathscr{M})

\mathscr{M}
(see
[

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathscr{X})
, with
\mathscr{E}
almost perfect and
\mathscr{F}

\mathscr{M}

Fix

, and suppose

is
i
-connective. By Corollary

, this is equivalent to:

•

For every point

We define the moduli stack

where
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{G})
denotes the derived projectivization of

over
\mathscr{M}
. Let
\mathscr{O}(1)
be the universal line bundle on
\mathbf{PExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
. By construction, we have

\mathbf{PExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})=\mathbf{PExt}_{f}^{i+m}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}[-m])\quad\text{for all $m\geq 0$.}

The functor of points of
\mathbf{PExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})

from a derived affine scheme (or a perfect stack)

(u,\mathscr{L})

The surjectivity of

Definition 3.5

\phi|_{X_{s}}\colon\mathscr{E}|_{X_{s}}\to\mathscr{F}|_{X_{s}},

\mathscr{E}|_{X_{s}}
and
\mathscr{F}|_{X_{s}}
denote the pullbacks to the fiber

2.10

.

3.4.

In the context of Definition

, let

.

Let

over

The following is immediate from the theory of derived Grassmannians (

Jia22b
, §4.1]
):

Lemma 3.12
.

The derived stack
\pi\colon\mathbf{GExt}^{i}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F};\ell)\to\mathscr{M}

(Base change)

3.5.
Semiorthogonal decompositions for moduli of extensions

Let
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathscr{X})
with
\mathscr{E}
perfect and
\mathscr{F}
relatively perfect over
\mathscr{M}
.
Suppose there exists
i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
has Tor-amplitude in
[i,i+1]
. By Corollary
2.10
, this is equivalent to requiring that

•

For every point
s\in\mathscr{M}
,
\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{s}}^{j}(\mathscr{E}|_{X_{s}},\mathscr{F}|_{X_{s}})=0
for
j\neq i,i+1
.

The rank of
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})
then defines a locally constant function on
\mathscr{M}
; restricting to a connected component, we may assume it is constant, say
r
. Again by Corollary
2.10
, this is equivalent to:

r=\dim_{\kappa(s)}\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{s}}^{i}(\mathscr{E}|_{X_{s}},\mathscr{F}|_{X_{s}})-\dim_{\kappa(s)}\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{s}}^{i+1}(\mathscr{E}|_{X_{s}},\mathscr{F}|_{X_{s}})\quad\text{for all $s\in\mathscr{M}$.}

In this case,
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}[i])
has Tor-amplitude in
[0,1]
. By Serre duality (Proposition
2.11
),

\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}[i])^{\vee}[1]\simeq\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{F}[i],\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet})[1]\simeq\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet}[-i-1]).

Applying the main result of
[
Jia22a
, Theorem 7.5]
(see also
[
JL22
, Theorem 3.4]
) to the perfect complex
\mathcal{Q}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}[i])
of Tor-amplitude
[0,1]
over
\mathscr{M}
, we obtain:

Theorem 3.13
.

In the above setting, the natural projections

\mathbf{PExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\to\mathscr{M}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{PHom}_{f}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet}[-i-1])\to\mathscr{M}

are quasi-smooth.
Moreover, let
\mathrm{D}
denote
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}
,
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{-}}_{\mathrm{coh}}
,
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{coh}}
, or
\mathrm{Perf}
, then:

(1)

If
r>0
, there is an
\mathscr{M}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{PExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\right)=\left\langle\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{PHom}_{f}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet}[-i-1])\right),\ \text{$r$ copies of }\mathrm{D}(\mathscr{M})\right\rangle.

(2)

If
r=0
, there is an
\mathscr{M}
-linear derived equivalence

\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{PExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\right)\simeq\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{PHom}_{f}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet}[-i-1])\right).

(3)

If
r<0
, there is an
\mathscr{M}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{PHom}_{f}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet}[-i-1])\right)=\left\langle\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{PExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})\right),\ \text{$(-r)$ copies of }\mathrm{D}(\mathscr{M})\right\rangle.

This theorem can be viewed as a categorification of the fiberwise Riemann–Roch formula:

r=\dim_{\kappa(s)}\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{s}}^{i}(\mathscr{E}|_{X_{s}},\mathscr{F}|_{X_{s}})-\dim_{\kappa(s)}\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{s}}^{i+1}(\mathscr{E}|_{X_{s}},\mathscr{F}|_{X_{s}}).

For the moduli stacks
\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{i}
, applying the main result of
[
Jia23b
]
, we further obtain:

Theorem 3.14
.

In the above setting, the natural projections

\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F};\ell)\to\mathscr{M}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{0}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet}[-i-1];\ell^{\prime})\to\mathscr{M}

are quasi-smooth for all integers
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
.
Moreover, suppose
\mathscr{M}
is a derived stack over a field of characteristic zero, and let
\mathrm{D}
denote
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}
,
\mathrm{D}^{-}_{\mathrm{coh}}
,
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{coh}}
, or
\mathrm{Perf}
. Then:

(1)

If
r>0
, for any
\ell\geq 0
, there is an
\mathscr{M}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F};\ell)\right)=\left\langle\text{$\binom{r}{j}$ copies of }\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{0}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet}[-i-1];\ell-j)\right)\mid 0\leq j\leq\ell\right\rangle.

(2)

If
r=0
, for any
\ell\geq 0
, there is an
\mathscr{M}
-linear equivalence

\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F};\ell)\right)\simeq\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{0}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet}[-i-1];\ell)\right).

(3)

If
r<0
, for any
\ell\geq 0
, there is an
\mathscr{M}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{0}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{f}^{\bullet}[-i-1];\ell)\right)=\left\langle\text{$\binom{-r}{j}$ copies of }\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{i}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F};\ell-j)\right)\mid 0\leq j\leq\ell\right\rangle.

The results above have a wide range of applications.

.

. By flatness, the fiber of
Z_{\mathrm{cl}}
over
[\mathfrak{q}]
at
[\mathfrak{p}]
is defined by the images
\overline{f}_{1},\ldots,\overline{f}_{m}
in
A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{q}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{q}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a smooth
\kappa(\mathfrak{q})
-algebra of dimension
n
and the fiber of
Z_{\mathrm{cl}}
has dimension
n-m
, the sequence
\overline{f}_{1},\ldots,\overline{f}_{m}
is regular in
A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{q}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. By
[
Sta
,
Tag 0470
]
, it follows that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
is a regular sequence in
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Thus,
Z
is classical.
∎

Therefore, in the locally planar case, all statements of Theorem
4.4
hold for the classical schemes, with
\mathbf{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{d}
replaced by
\mathrm{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d}
, and
\mathbf{A}^{d}
,
\mathbf{A}_{\omega}^{d}
by the classical Abel maps
A_{d}
and
A_{d}^{\omega}
, respectively. In particular, the semiorthogonal decomposition of Theorem
4.4
(4)&(5) specializes as follows: for each
d\geq p_{a}-1
, there is a semiorthogonal decomposition:

\mathrm{D}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d})=\left\langle\mathrm{D}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{2p_{a}-2-d}),\ \text{$(1-p_{a}+d)$ copies of }\mathrm{D}(\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d})\right\rangle.

This greatly extends the known semiorthogonal decompositions for symmetric powers of smooth projective curves over a field
\kappa
due to Toda
[
Tod21
, Corollary 5.11]
(see also
[
JL22
, Corollary 3.10]
,
[
BK24
]
, and
[
Jia22a
, Example 3.33]
).

Moreover, in the case of a smooth projective curve
C
over
\mathbb{C}
, this formula is optimal: by a result of Lin
[
Lin21
]
, the derived category of
\operatorname{Hilb}_{C}^{d}=\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(C)
admits no nontrivial semiorthogonal decompositions for
d\leq g(C)-1
. Motivated by this, we conjecture that in the locally planar case, the derived category
\mathrm{D}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}^{d})
does not admit any nontrivial
S
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition when
d\leq p_{a}-1
.

4.3.
Moduli of linear series of generalized divisors

We now return to the general setting of a family
X/S
of integral curves as in §
4.2.1
, and consider Grassmannian-type generalizations of the Abel maps, as in Theorem
3.14
.

Given an integer
\ell\geq-1
, we define the moduli stack of linear series of generalized divisors of degree
d
and dimension
\ell
, together with the associated generalized Abel map, by

\mathbf{A}^{d,\ell}:=\mathbf{A}^{\mathscr{J}_{d},\ell}\colon\qquad\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{d,X/S}^{\ell}:=\mathbf{G}_{\ell+1}\mathbf{LinSys}_{X/S}^{\mathscr{J}_{d}}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d}.

Let
\iota
denote the involution of
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}
as in §
4.2.1
. The generalized
\omega
-Abel map is defined as

\mathbf{A}_{\omega}^{d,\ell}\colon\quad\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{2p_{a}-2-d,X/S}^{\ell}\xrightarrow[\sim]{\hskip 8.19447pt\iota^{*}\hskip 8.19447pt}\mathbf{G}_{\ell+1}\mathbf{Quot}_{\omega/X/S}^{\mathscr{J}_{d}}\xrightarrow{~\mathbf{A}_{\omega}^{\mathscr{J},\ell+1}~}\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}^{d}.

The classical truncations of the fibers of
\mathbf{A}_{d,\ell}
and
\mathbf{A}_{\omega}^{d,\ell}
are Grassmannian varieties:

\mathrm{Grass}_{\ell+1}\left(H^{0}(X_{s},\mathscr{J}|_{X_{s}})^{\vee}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Grass}_{\ell+1}\left(\operatorname{Ext}^{0}_{X_{s}}(\mathscr{J}|_{X_{s}},\omega_{X_{s}})^{\vee}\right),

respectively. Equivalently, these are the Grassmannians parametrizing
\ell
-dimensional projective linear subspaces of (
4.2
), i.e.,

\mathbb{P}\left(H^{0}(X_{s},\mathscr{J}|_{X_{s}})^{\vee}\right)
and
\mathbb{P}\left(\operatorname{Ext}^{0}_{X_{s}}(\mathscr{J}|_{X_{s}},\omega_{X_{s}})^{\vee}\right)
, respectively.

If the curve
X_{s}
is smooth, then the classical truncation of the fiber of
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{d,X/S}^{\ell}
over a point
s\in S
recovers the variety
G_{d}^{\ell}(X_{s})
of
\ell
-dimensional linear series
g_{d}^{\ell}
of degree
d
on
X_{s}
, as studied in
[
ACGH13
, Chapter IV]
. In particular, the morphism
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{d,X/S}^{\ell}\to S
, restricted over the locus where the fibers
X_{s}
are smooth, is a derived enhancement of the family of classical varieties of linear series
G_{d}^{\ell}
of
[
ACGH13
, Chapter IV]
, studied in
[
Jia23b
, §4.3]
.

More generally, over points
s\in S
where
X_{s}
is Gorenstein, the classical truncation of
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{d,X/S}^{\ell}
over
s
is the variety
\mathrm{G}_{d}^{\ell}
of
\ell
-dimensional projective linear subspaces of the complete linear system
|\mathscr{J}|_{X_{s}}|
of generalized divisors. This provides a natural generalization of the classical variety of linear series to the setting of generalized divisors in the sense of
[
Har86
,
Har94
]
.

An immediate consequence of Theorem
3.14
is the following:

Corollary 4.9
.

(1)

The Abel maps
\mathbf{A}^{d,\ell}
and
\mathbf{A}_{\omega}^{d,\ell}
are quasi-smooth for all
d
and
\ell
.

(2)

Suppose that
S
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme (i.e., of characteristic zero), and let
\operatorname{D}
be one of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}
,
\mathrm{D}^{-}_{\mathrm{coh}}
,
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{coh}}
, or
\mathrm{Perf}
. Then for all
d\geq p_{a}-1
and
\ell\geq-1
, there is a semiorthogonal decomposition:

\operatorname{D}\left(\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{d,X/S}^{\ell}\right)=\left\langle\text{$\binom{1-p_{a}+d}{j}$ copies of }\operatorname{D}\left(\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{2p_{a}-2-d,X/S}^{\ell-j}\right)\,\Big|\,0\leq j\leq\ell+1\right\rangle.

This result generalizes the semiorthogonal decompositions of the author
[
Jia23b
, Corollary 4.5]
from families of smooth curves to families of integral curves. See also
[
Tod23
]
for the case of a general smooth curve
C/\mathbb{C}
. Furthermore, when
C
is a general smooth curve, the moduli stack
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{d,C/\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
recovers the classical variety
G_{d}^{\ell}(C)
. In this context, Lin and Yu
[
LY21
]
proved that the derived categories
\operatorname{D}(G_{2g-2-d}^{r-i}(C))
are indecomposable for all
0\leq i\leq r+1
.

4.4.
Moduli of coherent systems on curves

We return to the general setting of
4.1
, where
X\to S
is a flat, locally projective, and finitely presented morphism of schemes, whose geometric fibers are integral curves of arithmetic genus
p_{a}
. Assume that
S
is locally Noetherian.

For any integers
d\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\rho>0
, consider the moduli stack of torsion-free sheaves of rank
\rho
and degree
d
on the fibers of
X/S
:

\mathscr{M}:=\underline{\mathrm{Coh}}_{X/S}^{\mathrm{tf}}(\rho,d)\to S.

For any
S
-scheme
T
, the
T
-points of
\mathscr{M}
consist of
T
-flat coherent sheaves
\mathscr{E}_{T}\in\mathrm{Coh}(X_{T})
such that, for each
t\in T
, the restriction
\mathscr{E}|_{X_{t}}
is a torsion-free sheaf of rank
\rho
and degree
d
on
X_{t}
. Here, the degree is defined as
\deg(\mathscr{E}|_{X_{t}}):=\chi(X_{t},\mathscr{E}|_{X_{t}})-\chi(X_{t},\mathscr{O}_{X_{t}})
. Thus, the degree
d
and the integer
r
in (
4.1
) are related via the Riemann–Roch formula:

r=(1-p_{a})\rho+d.

Let
\mathscr{E}\in\mathrm{Coh}(\mathscr{X})
denote the universal sheaf.
For
\ell\geq 0
, we consider the following derived stacks and their “Abel” maps:

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\rho,d,\ell}\colon\quad\mathbf{G}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell):=\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{0}(\mathscr{O}_{X},\mathscr{E};\ell)\to\mathscr{M},

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\rho,d,\ell}_{\omega}\colon\quad\mathbf{G}^{\omega}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell):=\mathbf{GExt}_{f}^{0}(\mathscr{E},\omega;\ell)\to\mathscr{M}.

By Lemma
3.11
, for any
S
-scheme
T
, the
T
-points of
\mathbf{G}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell)
and
\mathbf{G}^{\omega}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell)
parametrize triples
(v,\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E}_{T})
and
(w,\mathscr{W},\mathscr{F}_{T})
, where
\mathscr{V}
and
\mathscr{W}
are vector bundles over
T
of rank
\ell
,
\mathscr{E}_{T}
and
\mathscr{F}_{T}
are
T
-flat, torsion-free sheaves of rank
\rho
and degree
d
on fibers of
X_{T}/T
, and

v\colon f_{T}^{*}(\mathscr{V}_{T}^{\vee})\to\mathscr{E}_{T},\qquad w\colon\mathscr{F}_{T}\to\omega_{X_{T}/T}\otimes f_{T}^{*}(\mathscr{W}_{T})

are homomorphisms in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(X_{T})
such that, for each
t\in T
, the induced maps on the fiber
X_{t}
yield injective maps

\kappa(t)^{\oplus\ell}\hookrightarrow H^{0}(X_{t},\mathscr{E}|_{X_{t}})\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa(t)^{\oplus\ell}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{X_{t}}(\mathscr{F}|_{X_{t}},\omega_{X_{t}})\simeq H^{1}(X_{t},\mathscr{E}|_{X_{t}})^{\vee}.

In particular, the functor of points over a field
s\colon\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)\to S
assigns to each such
s
pairs
(E,V)
and
(F,W)
, where
E
and
F
are torsion-free sheaves of rank
\rho
and degree
d
on
X_{s}=X\times_{S}\{s\}
, and
V\subseteq H^{0}(X_{s},E)
and
W\subseteq H^{1}(X_{s},F)^{\vee}
are linear subspaces of dimension
\ell
.

Thus, the classical truncations of these moduli spaces recover the moduli of coherent systems (or Brill–Noether pairs), extensively studied by many authors, see, for example,
[
KN95
]
.

In the case
\ell=1
, we use the notation:

\mathbf{Pair}_{X/S}(\rho,d):=\mathbf{PHom}_{f}(\mathscr{O}_{X},\mathscr{E})\to\mathscr{M},\qquad\mathbf{Pair}_{X/S}^{\omega}(\rho,d):=\mathbf{PHom}_{f}(\mathscr{E},\omega)\to\mathscr{M}.

In this case, their functors of points over a field classify Thaddeus pairs
[
Tha94
,
KT21
]

(E,v)
and
(F,w)
respectively, where
E
and
F
are torsion-free sheaves on
X_{s}
, and
v\in H^{0}(X_{s},E)
,
w\in\operatorname{Hom}_{X_{s}}(F,\omega)
are nonzero sections, considered up to scalar multiplication.

Theorems
4.2
and
3.14
yield the following:

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
\operatorname{D}
be one of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}
,
\mathrm{D}^{-}_{\mathrm{coh}}
,
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{coh}}
, or
\mathrm{Perf}
.

(1)

The maps
\mathbf{A}^{\rho,d,\ell}
and
\mathbf{A}^{\rho,d,\ell}_{\omega}
are quasi-smooth for all
\rho>0
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\ell\geq 0
.

(2)

For all
d\geq(p_{a}-1)\rho
, there is an
\mathscr{M}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition:

\mathrm{D}(\mathbf{Pair}_{X/S}(\rho,d))=\left\langle\mathrm{D}(\mathbf{Pair}_{X/S}^{\omega}(\rho,d)),\ \text{$((1-p_{a})\rho+d)$ copies of }\mathrm{D}(\mathscr{M})\right\rangle.

(3)

If
S
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme, then for all
d\geq(p_{a}-1)\rho
and
\ell\geq 0
, there is an
\mathscr{M}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition:

\operatorname{D}\left(\mathbf{G}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell)\right)=\left\langle\text{$\binom{(1-p_{a})\rho+d}{j}$ copies of }\operatorname{D}\left(\mathbf{G}^{\omega}_{X/S}(\rho,d,\ell-j)\right)\,\Big|\,0\leq j\leq\ell\right\rangle.

Specializing to the case where
X/S
is a smooth projective curve
C/\mathbb{C}
,
\mathrm{gcd}(\rho,d)=1
and
\ell=1
, the semiorthogonal decomposition in (1), when restricted to the open substack
\mathcal{B}un_{C}^{\mathrm{st}}(\rho,d)\subseteq\mathscr{M}
of stable vector bundles, recovers a result previously obtained by Koseki and Toda
[
KT21
]
.

Thus, our result extends the semiorthogonal decompositions for Thaddeus pair moduli spaces for stable vector bundles on smooth curves to the broader context of higher-dimensional coherent systems and torsion-free sheaves on families of integral curves.

Isolation, characterization and biocontrol of a novel bacteriophage against Ralstonia pseudosolanacearum
Authors: Shoude Liu, Rong Wen, Qifeng Li, Ming Sun, Donghai Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAbstractRalstonia pseudosolanacearum is an increasingly prominent multidrug-resistant phytopathogen. Phages are an effective alternative for treating R. pseudosolanacearum infections. In this study, the phage vB_RsoP_BMB116, which is specific to R. pseudosolanacearum, was isolated from Fujian, China. Electron microscopy revealed that vB_RsoP_BMB116 exhibited a Podoviridae morphotype. The double-stranded DNA genome of vB_RsoP_BMB116 spans 82,991 bp (base pairs) and encodes 119 predicted unidirectionally oriented genes, of which 40 have putative functions assigned, while the remainder encode hypothetical proteins. Genome analysis indicated that vB_RsoP_BMB116 is a new genus. Furthermore, tomato plant experiments conducted in this study demonstrated that vB_RsoP_BMB116 significantly reduces the disease incidence of tomato bacterial wilt induced by R. pseudosolanacearum, thus providing effective experimental support for its potential as a biological control agent against tomato bacterial wilt in agricultural settings. In summary, these results not only expand the genetic repertoire of phages targeting R. pseudosolanacearum but also establish a theoretical and experimental basis for developing phage-mediated biocontrol approaches to manage bacterial wilt in tomato cultivation.Key points• The new R. pseudosolanacearum phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 (novel genus) is a key resource for microbiology and biotechnology, aiding new phage therapies and biocontrol agents• Observed via transmission electron microscopy (TEM), the novel phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 belongs to the Podoviridae family• In vitro assays demonstrated that it exhibits significant application potential for the prevention and control of plant bacterial diseasesSupplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00253-026-13769-2.Keywords: Ralstonia pseudosolanacearum, Bacteriophage, Podoviridae, Genome, Plant pathogenic bacteria

Introduction

Ralstonia pseudosolanacearum, a Gram-negative pathogen, causes bacterial wilt (Hayward 1991). It is classified within the R. pseudosolanacearum species complex (RSSC), which comprises three distinct species: R. pseudosolanacearum, Ralstonia pseudopseudosolanacearum, and Ralstonia syzygii (Prior et al. 2016). All three species are recognized as major bacterial wilt pathogens that contribute to substantial crop losses and are classified as significant phytopathogens (Mansfield et al. 2012; Safni et al. 2014). Bacterial wilt, triggered by the soilborne pathogen R. pseudosolanacearum, severely impacts Solanaceae crops like ginger potato, tomato, eggplant, pepper, and flue-cured tobacco (da Silva Xavier et al. 2018; Fujiwara et al. 2011). The bacterium spreads via soil and water, infects roots, and invades the vascular system, causing potato brown rot and wilting (Alvarez et al. 2008; Hayward 1991; Vasse et al. 1995). Bacterial wilt results in estimated global agricultural losses exceeding $950 million annually, posing a substantial economic burden on Solanaceae cultivation across subtropical, tropical, and temperate regions (Mansfield et al. 2012; Sae-Ueng et al. 2020). Accordingly, R. pseudosolanacearum is regarded as the second most critical bacterial phytopathogen, following Pseudomonas syringae (Mansfield et al. 2012). The development of effective therapeutics for bacterial wilt is imperative, given the limitations of conventional agrochemicals, including resistance emergence, risks to human health, and environmental damage (Jiang et al. 2017; Nischal 2014).

Bacteriophages, the most prevalent viruses in nature, have been employed against bacterial infections since their discovery in the 1910 s due to their unique antibacterial properties (Bruynoghe and Maisin 1921; Sharma et al. 2017). Their ability to infect and lyse Ralstonia spp. has led to growing interest in lytic phages as biocontrol tools for bacterial wilt (Wang et al. 2019). Numerous Ralstonia-infecting phages have been identified. Recent studies have shown that novel Ampunavirus phage LPRS20 potently inhibits R. pseudosolanacearum including tomato-pathogenic strains with negligible phytotoxicity and synergizes with low-concentration agrochemicals for bacterial wilt control (Liu et al. 2024). It is also reported that increasing the application frequency of phages targeting R. pseudosolanacearum significantly enhances the control of phytopathogen density, thereby reducing the incidence of bacterial wilt to a notable extent; meanwhile, such R. pseudosolanacearum-specific phages not only effectively suppress the population of the phytopathogen but also work with the enriched beneficial microbial taxa to further inhibit the occurrence of bacterial wilt. Beyond the biocontrol potential of individual phages, rhizosphere phage communities are demonstrated to mediate soil suppressiveness to bacterial wilt: higher abundances of R. pseudosolanacearum-specific phages in the rhizosphere of healthy tomato plants directly constrain pathogen proliferation and suppress late-stage disease outbreaks (Liu et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2023). This underscores the value of discovering additional R. pseudosolanacearum-infecting phages, as they could further support soil suppressiveness against bacterial wilt.

In this study, we isolated a novel phage, vB_RsoP_BMB116, infecting R. pseudosolanacearum from tomato fields in Fujian, China. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed that vB_RsoP_BMB116 exhibited a Podoviridae morphology with an isometric head and a short, noncontractile tail. High-throughput sequencing revealed that vB_RsoP_BMB116 comprised a double-stranded deoxyribonucleic acid molecule (dsDNA) with a full length of 82,991 bp, a mean G+C content of 63%, and 119 open reading frames (ORFs). Notably, its low genomic similarity to all characterized phages in the GenBank database supports the classification of vB_RsoP_BMB116 as a representative of a novel Ralstonia phage genus. This taxonomic novelty implies it may possess unique evolutionary traits, as well as potential functional characteristics. To assess its utility in practical bacterial wilt suppression, we conducted tomato plant experiments; results showed that vB_RsoP_BMB116 effectively reduced disease incidence in tomato plants challenged with R. pseudosolanacearum. Therefore, as a novel resource, it holds promising potential for supporting the management of bacterial wilt.

Materials and methods

Bacterial strains and culture protocol

All 19 RSCC strains used in this investigation, including strain Rs3221-2, were provided by Professor Bo Liu (Academy of Agriculture Science, Fujian; Ningde City Academy of Agriculture Science, Fujian Province, China) (Table 1). Seventeen of these strains, excluding the previously characterized GMI1000 and FJAT-1458, were isolated from wilt-infected plants in Fujian Province, China, and verified utilizing the 759/760 species-specific primers (Fegan and Prior 2005; Wang et al. 2022). All isolates were further identified by 16S rRNA gene sequencing and confirmed as R. pseudosolanacearum (phylotype I) via a multiplex PCR (polymerase chain reaction) protocol as previously outlined (Fegan and Prior 2005; Wang et al. 2022). Aerobic cultivation of R. pseudosolanacearum was conducted at 28 °C using sucrose-peptone-agar medium (SPA) broth containing 20 g/L sucrose, 5 g/L peptone, 0.5 g/L K₂HPO₄, and 0.25 g/L anhydrous MgSO₄, as well as on casamino acid-peptone-glucose agar comprising 5 g/L glucose, 10 g/L peptone, 1 g/L casamino acid, and 15 g/L agar (pH 7.2). The aforementioned reagents were supplied by Sinoreagent in China.

Phage isolation and purification

The vB_RsoP_BMB116 (novel phage) was obtained from tomato field samples in Fujian, China, employing R. pseudosolanacearum Rs3221-2 as the indicator host via the double-layer agar assay. It was isolated utilizing a modified version of a previously established enrichment protocol (Van Twest and Kropinski 2009). Approximately 2 g of soil samples were added to 30 mL of sterile SPA broth in a 250-mL Erlenmeyer flask and incubated at 28 °C with agitation at 200 rpm (revolutions per minute) overnight (Shanghai Yiheng Scientific Instruments Co., Ltd., China). After centrifugation (12,000 rpm, 10 min, 4 °C), the supernatant was filtered through 0.22 µm PES (polyethersulfone) syringe filters (Sartorius AG, Göttingen, Germany) to eliminate bacterial cells, yielding a sterile soil extract (Wang et al. 2022). This filtrate was mixed with exponentially growing R. pseudosolanacearum strain Rs3221-2 with 15-min incubation. Phage detection was then verified utilizing spot assay based on the double-layer agar technique. An isolated plaque was collected with a sterile pipette and subjected to five successive rounds of purification to generate a clonal phage preparation.

The nucleotide sequences of the novel phage vB_RsoP_BMB116, which have been newly determined, were submitted to GenBank (National Center for Biotechnology Information, NCBI), and the accession number provided is PX020966.

Host range assay

To evaluate host specificity, the spot assay was conducted using 19 R. pseudosolanacearum strains. Tenfold serial dilutions of the phage stock starting from 10⁸ PFU (plaque-forming units)/mL were applied (5 µL per spot) onto double-layer agar plates containing target bacteria. Following overnight incubation at 28 °C, plaque formation was observed.

Morphological characterization by transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

The vB_RsoP_BMB116 morphology was examined by TEM negative staining (Wang et al. 2022). The resulting phage pellet was rinsed twice in 0.1 × phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., China) to eliminate residual medium and resuspended in 1 mL of the same buffer. The suspension was applied to a carbon-coated grid, followed by 2% phosphotungstic acid pH 6.8 (Hubei Nuona Technology Co., Ltd., China) negative staining for 20 s, then imaged with an H-7650 TEM (Hitachi High-Technologies Corp., Pleasanton, CA, USA) at 100 kV at the State Key Laboratory of Agricultural Microbiology, Huazhong Agricultural University, Wuhan. The morphological characteristics of vB_RsoP_BMB116 were determined using ten phage particles.

One-step growth curve

The one-step growth curve of phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 was determined using standard methodology (Hyman and Abedon 2009; Wang et al. 2022). It was mixed with mid-log-phase R. pseudosolanacearum Rs3221-2 at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.1 and allowed to adsorb for 30 min. Following adsorption, cells were centrifuged (13,000g, 1 min) to remove free phage, retaining only bacteria-associated phage. The pellet was re-inoculated into 10 mL SPA broth and incubated at 28 °C under agitation at 200 rpm (Shanghai Yiheng Scientific Instruments Co., Ltd., China). Phage titers were measured from samples collected every 15 min utilizing the double-layer agar plaque assay. Burst size was calculated as the ratio of released phage particles to the number of infected bacterial cells at the end of the latency period (Wang et al. 2022).

Phage genome sequencing and annotation

vB_RsoP_BMB116 genomic DNA was purified utilizing a ZnCl₂-based method, as previously described (Santos 1991), and analyzed through paired-end sequencing with Illumina HiSeq 2500 (Beijing Annoroad Gene Technology Co., Ltd., China). Genome assembly of vB_RsoP_BMB116 was performed using ABySS alignment v2.0 with varying k-mer lengths (40–75) (Goris et al. 2007; Jackman et al. 2017). The vB_RsoP_BMB116 genome was annotated via RAST v2.0 (https://rast.nmpdr.org/) (Aziz et al. 2008), and ORF functional predictions were manually refined using BLASTp (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool for Proteins) against NCBI (National Center for Biotechnology Information) protein databases. The tRNAs (transfer RNAs) were predicted utilizing tRNAScan-SE v2.0 (Lowe and Chan 2016).

Phage analysis classification status

The vB_RsoP_BMB116 genome was aligned against public databases via NCBI’s BLASTn tool to identify the vB_RsoP_BMB116 genome sequence similarities (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi). To screen for phages with the highest VIRIDIC (VIRus IDentification and Classification) and PASC (Pairwise Sequence Comparison) values, the online tools VIRIDIC (http://rhea.icbm.uni-oldenburg.de/VIRIDIC/) (Moraru et al. 2020) and pairwise sequence comparison (PASC; http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sutils/pasc) (Bao et al. 2014; Yoon et al. 2017) were utilized to assess nucleotide-based intergenomic similarity (VIRIDIC value) and PASC values between vB_RsoP_BMB116 and other phages, respectively.

A genome-wide proteomic tree of phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 with 128 phages was constructed using the online software ViPTree 3.3 (http://www.genome.jp/viptree). The selection criteria of the 128 reference phages were as follows: Firstly, ViPTree 3.3 was applied to construct a total evolutionary tree containing vB_RsoP_BMB116 with all the viruses in the database, from which the 38 phages with the closest affinity to vB_RsoP_BMB116 were selected. These phages also exhibited the highest homology with vB_RsoP_BMB116 in the BLASTn and PASC searches. Secondly, representative species were randomly selected from each of the four orders (Crassvirales, Kirjokansivirales, Methanobavirales, and Thumleimavirales) in the latest virus classification system released by the International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses (ICTV). Thirdly, 1–2 representative species were selected from each of the 33 independent families that do not belong to any order (in the latest ICTV virus classification system).

ANI (average nucleotide identity) value and in silico DNA-DNA hybridization value between phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 and proximate phages were estimated utilizing EzGenome online tools (http://www.ezbiocloud.net/ezgenome/ani) (Figueras et al. 2014) and GGDC (Genome-to-Genome Distance Calculator) (Meier-Kolthoff et al. 2013), respectively. Core genes of proximate phages were predicted by VirClust. (http://rhea.icbm.uni-oldenburg.de/VIRCLUST) (Moraru 2023). Taxonomic analysis followed ICTV criteria for species, genera, subfamilies, and families.

Plant experiment

Application efficacy of phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 (1.0 × 10⁸ PFU/mL) in controlling bacterial wilt in tomato caused by R. pseudosolanacearum was evaluated. The experiment included three groups: (1) untreated control group with any intervention except clear water application; (2) negative control group inoculated only with R. pseudosolanacearum strain BMB Rs3221-2, and (3) BMB Rs3221-2-inoculated group treated with phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 for biocontrol, 24 to 48 h after R. pseudosolanacearum inoculation (when initial bacterial wilt symptoms appeared), phages were applied to the experimental treatment group, and the co-cultivation period of phages and R. pseudosolanacearum began immediately after phage inoculation. Uniform tomato seedlings at the three-leaf-one-heart stage were selected for pot experiments, with five plants per group and three replicates. Pathogen inoculation was performed via root irrigation using a bacterial suspension optical density (OD600 = 0.8–1.2) at 40 mL per pot. The treatment group received phage suspension (1.0 × 10⁸ PFU/mL) through root irrigation (40 mL per pot) 24 to 48 h after pathogen inoculation. The plants were cultivated at 30 °C under a 12-h light/12-h dark cycles, and disease incidence and progression were monitored. All plants in the three treatment groups were sown and transplanted in the same batch to ensure no significant difference in initial height at transplantation, and the same shooting conditions including angle, distance,and background board were strictly maintained during photography.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis was conducted for this experiment via GraphPad Prism 10.0 software (GraphPad Software, LLC, San Diego, CA, USA). Moreover, statistical differences between the two groups were analyzed utilizing an independent-samples t-test, with significance levels denoted as *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, and ****p < 0.0001.

Results

Phage vB_RsoP_BMB116: isolation and structural morphology

The vB_RsoP_BMB116 (novel phage) was obtained from tomato field samples in Fujian, China, employing R. pseudosolanacearum Rs3221-2 as the indicator host via the double-layer agar assay. Moreover, strain Rs3221-2, which was classified as R. pseudosolanacearum, was recovered from tomato fields and shown to cause bacterial wilt. Our results revealed that vB_RsoP_BMB116 produced clear plaques with diameters of 0.5–1 mm on the plate containing Rs3221-2 (Fig. 1b and). Phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 was further amplified and purified according to the aforementioned protocols for TEM imaging and genomic DNA isolation (Dong et al. 2018). TEM identified vB_RsoP_BMB116 as a Podoviridae-type phage, featuring an isometric head (51 ± 2 nm) and a short, noncontractile tail (18 ± 1 nm) (Fig. 1a).

Maximum phage activity was achieved at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.1 (Fig. 2d). The growth curve of phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 (Fig. 2c) showed a latent period of ~ 20 min and a burst phase of ~ 60 min before reaching a stable phase. The burst size was estimated at 120 ± 15 PFU/cell.

Host range of phage vB_RsoP_BMB116

The biocontrol capacity of vB_RsoP_BMB116 was assessed by testing its lytic activity against 19 R. pseudosolanacearum strains, comprising 17 field isolates from wilted hosts (peanut, ginger, eggplant, tomato, and pepper) and two sequenced strains, GMI1000 and FJAT-1458 (Chen et al. 2017; Drigues et al. 1985). All tested strains were classified as R. pseudosolanacearum (Table 1). Our experimental findings clearly demonstrated that the novel R. pseudosolanacearum phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 exhibits a notably broad lytic spectrum, infecting 18 of 19 R. pseudosolanacearum strains.

Genomic analysis of phage vB_RsoP_BMB116

The phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 genome sequence was completed using high-throughput sequencing, followed by splicing and assembly. Comprehensive genomic features of phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 are provided in Supplementary Table S1. vB_RsoP_BMB116 has an 82,991 bp genome with 63% G+C content, cyclizability, and one fixed end (R1 = 360 > 100, a parameter for evaluating genome terminal type).

Furthermore, a whole-genome comparison of vB_RsoP_BMB116 was conducted utilizing tblastn (translated Basic Local Alignment Search Tool for nucleotide databases) in NCBI’s sequence database. The results revealed that there was no sequence with high similarity to vB_RsoP_BMB116 in the public database, and the phage exhibited low similarity to the reported Achromobacter phage JWF (accession number AJD82914.1). The genome-wide coverage and identity were 2% and 73.12%, respectively, indicating that vB_RsoP_BMB116 could be a novel phage.

To better understand the phage-encoded proteins, the online annotation software RAST (http://rast.nmpdr.org/) was utilized to annotate vB_RsoP_BMB116 the whole genome, while the Proksee online software (https://proksee.ca/) was utilized to generate the phage genome map (Sullivan et al. 2011). The vB_RsoP_BMB116 genome contains 119ORFs, 33.33% of which encode known functional proteins, while the remaining ORFs can only be annotated as hypothetical proteins of unknown function. No known virulence factors and antibiotic resistance genes were identified in the vB_RsoP_BMB116 genome via Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD, https://card.mcmaster.ca) (Alcock et al. 2023) and Virulence Factor Database (VFDB, https://www.mgc.ac.cn/VFs/) analyses (Zhou et al. 2025).

vB_RsoP_BMB116 encodes 119ORFs, with 10 proteins related to phage morphology, 12 proteins related to DNA replication and metabolism, and two proteins related to phage packaging. The portal protein is encoded by ORF113, and the phage terminase large subunit family protein, with its cleavage module being the lysis system i-spanin subunit Rz, is encoded by ORF79. The phage holin family protein is encoded by ORF81. We discovered that hypothetical proteins occupied nearly 67% of all ORFs, and no conserved structural domains were predicted in these proteins via conserved domain analysis. Given that most phages exhibit very compact gene composition, the emergence of phage genomes containing many non-functional proteins remains to be explored further. Moreover, it was initially hypothesized that these hypothetical proteins might be novel proteins, which we have not been able to define appropriately at this time.

Nine predicted proteins were associated with phage structure or assembly in Supplementary Table S1 and Fig. 3, including the major head protein (ORF110), portal protein (ORF113), tape measure protein (ORF100 and ORF102), tail proteins (ORF92, ORF93, ORF96, ORF97), and phage terminase large subunit family protein (ORF112). Consistent with other temperate phages, the longest phage ORF encodes the tape measure protein, which is responsible for tail length determination (Katsura 1987).

Genome Map of vB_RsoP_BMB116. All predicted ORFs were classified into five categories according to gene function annotation: phage structural proteins (green); DNA-packaging-related proteins (blue); DNA replication and regulation-related proteins (purple); lysis module (red); hypothetical proteins and other proteins (orange)

The site-specific integrase (ORF77) was identified as a putative protein reflecting the temperate nature of vB_RsoP_BMB116. Phage integrase facilitates site-specific recombination between host genomes and phage genomes, a key step in lysogeny initiation (Rajamanickam and Hayes 2018). Efficient lysis of the bacterial host generally requires endolysin and holin in dsDNA phages (Young 1992). A holin-like protein of this phage was predicted to be encoded by ORF81.

Furthermore, a viral proteomic tree was constructed utilizing ViPTree (Fig. 4), based on whole-genome GS (similarity score) (Nishimura et al. 2017). Pairwise SG (sequence similarity) values comparing vB_RsoP_BMB116 with known prokaryotic dsDNA viruses were determined, and a genomic distance matrix was utilized to build the tree via BIONJ (an improved Neighbor-Joining method for phylogenetic tree construction). The findings revealed that vB_RsoP_BMB116 clustered with several viruses (Achromobacter phage JWF and Achromobacter phage 2-1). The low SG values (0.2199–0.2269) between vB_RsoP_BMB116 and related phages suggest its classification as a distinct novel taxon. In the proteomic tree, the viruses with the smallest evolutionary distance from vB_RsoP_BMB116 were Achromobacter phage JWF and Achromobacter phage 2-1. The ANI and isDDH (in silico DNA-DNA hybridization) values shared by vB_RsoP_BMB116 with them are 64.26%, 12.5% (with phage JWF), and 64.85%, 12.5% (with phage 2-1), both of which are less than the boundary values of a species (ANI value > 95% and isDDH value > 70%). The results indicate that vB_RsoP_BMB116 is a new species. Phage taxonomy is undergoing a significant transformation as numerous genome-based families have been introduced. Accordingly, the taxonomic status of vB_RsoP_BMB116 is difficult to ascertain at this moment (Jain et al. 2018).

Biocontrol efficacy of vB_RsoP_BMB116 in plant disease

For the untreated control group without any intervention (Fig. 6a), tomato seedlings maintained vigorous and normal growth throughout the experiment, with fully expanded bright green leaves, upright and sturdy stems, and continuous growth of new tender leaves; in the group inoculated only with R. pseudosolanacearum BMB Rs3221-2 (Fig. 6b), tomato seedlings rapidly developed wilting symptoms, showing severe leaf drooping, stem lodging, and partial leaf yellowing; in contrast, the prevention group treated with both R. pseudosolanacearum BMB Rs3221-2 and phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 (Fig. 6c) demonstrated gradual recovery of tomato seedlings, with significantly alleviated leaf wilting, improved stem uprightness, and restored growth momentum, clearly confirming the biocontrol effect of the phage.

Antimicrobial effects of phages on tomato bacterial wilt management. a Untreated control group: untreated tomato seedlings. b Tomato seedlings infected with BMB Rs3221-2. c Tomato plants inoculated with R. pseudosolanacearum pathogen BMB Rs3221-2 and treated with phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 for biocontrol

Moreover, in the group inoculated with R. pseudosolanacearum (bacteria group), tomato plants initiated the manifestation of disease symptoms at 14 days, with a disease incidence of approximately 30%. The incidence sustained an upward trend, reaching 45% at 21 days, approaching 60% at 28 days, and exceeding 90% by 35 days. These outcomes indicated that R. pseudosolanacearum could induce infection in tomato plants, and its pathogenic potency increased over time (Fig. 7a). Dynamic analysis of the biocontrol efficacy of phages against tomato bacterial wilt showed that, from the 14th day of the observation period onward, the biocontrol efficacy (BCE) of the phage-treated group against tomato bacterial wilt remained at a high level. Specifically, the BCE reached 94.44% on the 14th day, further increased to 96.30% on the 21 st day, slightly decreased to 93.75% on the 28th day, and still was maintained at 91.53% by the 35th day (Fig. 7b).

The results indicated that phages could continuously exert a control effect on tomato bacterial wilt throughout the 35-day observation period. Although there were slight fluctuations in the control efficacy at different observation time points, the overall efficacy remained stably in the high-level range of over 90%. This result further verified that phages had a significant and long-lasting effective control ability against tomato bacterial wilt, providing data support for their application in the green control of this disease.

Discussion

The agricultural use of bacteriophages offers a viable strategy for managing bacterial diseases, particularly in light of rising antibiotic resistance. Phages can specifically infect and lyse R. pseudosolanacearum, the causal agent of bacterial wilt, and this property provides an effective means for the prevention and control of this disease while avoiding the damage to non-target organisms that traditional chemical pesticides cause. In this study, a Ralstonia phage was newly isolated, identified, and named vB_RsoP_BMB116, and it belongs to a new genus.

For this novel phage, we first analyzed its genome to understand its genetic behavior at the current research stage, which will provide a basis for further evaluating its potential in relevant research fields. A proteomic tree was derived from high genome similarity with other phages. Proteomic trees, which cluster phages based on shared protein sequences, offer higher resolution for evolutionary classification compared to single-gene phylogenetic trees, especially when genome sequence identities are low (Konstantinidis and Tiedje 2005; Lavigne et al. 2008). Our proteomic tree construction thus supplemented genome-level data, laying a more robust foundation for subsequent taxonomic assignment. Importantly, the proteomic tree placed vB_RsoP_BMB116 in a clade that appears distinct from all currently known Ralstonia phage genera; this observation aligns with the later VIRIDIC-based suggestion that it belongs to a new genus. This finding enriches the taxonomic diversity of Ralstonia phages, which are currently underrepresented in public databases relative to phages of other plant pathogens (e.g., Pseudomonas, Xanthomonas) (Liu et al. 2021; Trotereau et al. 2021). Such a result also provides partial support for the rationale of testing its potential biocontrol function in plants, as taxonomically novel phages have been shown in some studies to exhibit distinct host interaction mechanisms (Yuping et al. 2025). The current species demarcation standard is based on ≥ 95% genome sequence identity, indicating < 5% nucleotide divergence between viruses of the same species (Adriaenssens and Brister 2017). Phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 showed only 73.12% similarity (2% query coverage) with Achromobacter phage JWF and 79.23% similarity (1% coverage) with phage 2-1. These values are far below the 95% threshold, and the extremely low query coverage further suggests minimal overlapping genomic regions, key evidence that vB_RsoP_BMB116 is a new species. For instance, Li et al. (2021) classified Ralstonia phage PHB09 as a new species after finding it shared only 76.5% genome similarity with its closest known relative, a criterion consistent with our findings.

In the pot experiment, phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 exhibited notable biocontrol efficacy against tomato bacterial wilt caused by R. pseudosolanacearum BMB Rs3221-2. Uniform three-leaf-one-heart stage seedlings, selected for their vulnerability to early pathogen colonization, were used (Wang et al. 2024). The treatment group received phage suspension (1.0 × 10⁸ PFU/mL) through root irrigation 24 to 48 h after pathogen inoculation, showing gradual recovery under 30 °C and 12-h light/dark conditions, achieving a 91.53% control rate. This performance surpassed that of previously reported Ralstonia phages in similar pot trials: Liu et al. (2021) observed 82.1% efficacy with phage PHB09 using identical seedling stages and temperature conditions; additionally, in tomato pot experiments, pretreatment with phage φRSM3 delayed the onset of bacterial wilt by 14 days, and the plant survival rate (85%) was 4.25 times higher than that of the control group (20%) (Villalpando-Aguilar et al. 2022).

The superior efficacy of vB_RsoP_BMB116 can be attributed to two key factors: first, its application timing is 24 to 48 h post-inoculation, targeting early R. pseudosolanacearum colonization, when the pathogen has not yet formed dense rhizosphere populations, avoiding the “pathogen overload” issue that reduces phage efficacy in simultaneous application (Liu et al. 2024); second, novel phage genera often carry unique genetic elements, such as rare lytic enzymes or regulatory genes (Wang et al. 2017), that could expand the toolbox for biological control of bacterial wilt. For example, a new Ralstonia phage genus identified by Chen et al. (2023) contained a unique holin gene that enhanced lytic efficiency against R. pseudosolanacearum, implying similar potential for vB_RsoP_BMB116 to be explored in future functional studies. Notably, although the phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 carries a lysogeny-associated integrase gene (ORF77), it remains valuable for bacterial wilt biocontrol, as evidenced by prior studies. Specifically, genetic engineering including knockout of integration-related genes or insertion of depolymerase genes can transform these phages from lysogenic-risk variants into safe biocontrol tools (Born et al. 2017). Additionally, lysogenic phages can be repurposed as delivery vectors for CRISPR-Cas systems or lysozyme genes to achieve precise pathogen suppression (Peng et al. 2024). Thus, this phage effectively broadens the biocontrol resource pool and provides a novel platform for the green management of agricultural bacterial diseases. Overall, vB_RsoP_BMB116 holds great potential for controlling bacterial wilt in tomatoes.

In conclusion, genomic sequence analysis shows that the Ralstonia phage vB_RsoP_BMB116 has no significant genomic similarity to existing tailed viruses or known Ralstonia phages. As a newly identified Ralstonia phage, it further expands the biocontrol resource pool for agricultural bacterial disease management; additionally, investigating its biological properties will deepen insights into host-phage interactions within this bacterial genus. Currently, while phages provide a viable, eco-friendly control strategy, translating phage-based biocontrol from lab research to field applications remains hindered by multiple barriers. Thus, more studies are needed to optimize application protocols ultimately ensuring phages can be effectively and reliably used to control plant and other bacterial diseases.

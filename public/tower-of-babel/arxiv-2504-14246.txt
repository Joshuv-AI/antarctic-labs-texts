Logarithmic Crystalline Representations
Zhenmou Liu, Jinbang Yang, Kang Zuo

Abstract
. In 1989, Faltings proved the comparison theorem between étale cohomology and crystalline cohomology by studying Fontaine–Faltings modules and crystalline representations. In his paper, he mentioned that these modules and representations could be extended to the logarithmic context, but without detail. This note aims to explicitly present the construction of logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings modules and logarithmic crystalline representations.



Introduction

Over the complex numbers, polarized variations of Hodge structures provide a natural bridge between Hodge theory and local systems.
They consist, roughly speaking, of a flat bundle together with a Hodge filtration satisfying Griffiths transversality and a compatible polarization.
Fontaine–Faltings modules may be viewed as a
p
-adic analogue of such structures, with the Frobenius structure playing an essential additional role.

Let
k
be a perfect field of odd characteristic
p
, let
W\coloneqq W(k)
be the ring of Witt vectors of
k
, and let
Y
be a smooth separated
W
-scheme whose generic and special fibers are both nonempty and geometrically connected.
Denote by
\mathcal{Y}
the
p
-adic formal completion of
Y
.
Fontaine–Laffaille
[
FL82
]
and Faltings
[
Fal89
]
introduced Fontaine–Faltings modules over
\mathcal{Y}
.
Roughly speaking, a Fontaine–Faltings module consists of a module equipped with an integrable connection, a decreasing filtration satisfying Griffiths transversality, and a Frobenius structure compatible with these data.
Faltings constructed a contravariant fully faithful functor

\mathbb{D}:\mathcal{MF}^{\nabla}_{[0,p-2]}(\mathcal{Y}/W)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\bigl(\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{Y}_{K})\bigr),

which associates to a Fontaine–Faltings module a continuous representation of the étale fundamental group of the rigid generic fiber.
The representations arising in this way are called crystalline representations.

In
[
Fal89
, p. 43]
, Faltings stated that this theory extends to the logarithmic setting, and subsequently used the logarithmic theory in his comparison between étale and crystalline cohomology.
However, the details of this logarithmic construction were not written out.

In the present paper, we instead work from the integral viewpoint of logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings modules and construct the corresponding logarithmic
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
-functor.

Let
Z\subseteq Y
be a relative simple normal crossing divisor, let
\mathcal{Z}
be its
p
-adic formal completion with rigid generic fiber
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
, and put

U=Y-Z,\qquad\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{Y}-\mathcal{Z},\qquad\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ}=\mathcal{Y}_{K}-\mathcal{Z}_{K}.

We introduce logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings modules over
(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})
by allowing logarithmic poles along
\mathcal{Z}
in the connection.
Our main goal is to associate to such a module a continuous representation of
\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ})
whose restriction to
\mathcal{U}_{K}
agrees canonically with the representation obtained from Faltings’ original construction.

The construction is carried out locally on logarithmically small affine opens.
For such an affine open, we construct a logarithmic period ring
B^{+}_{\log}(\widehat{R})
and, after choosing a suitable Frobenius lifting
\Phi
, define a local logarithmic functor
\mathbb{D}^{\log}_{\Phi}
.
The action of
\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ})
on the corresponding period module is defined by a logarithmic Taylor expansion.

A key point is to show that the resulting local functor is independent of the choice of the Frobenius lifting.
For two Frobenius liftings, logarithmic Taylor isomorphisms give canonical identifications between the corresponding period modules, and these identifications are compatible with the Galois actions, filtrations, and Frobenius structures.
The local functors are therefore canonically identified on overlaps and can be glued to a global functor
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
.
We also compare this construction with Faltings’ original
\mathbb{D}
-functor after removing the boundary divisor.
In particular, the logarithmic construction restricts canonically to the ordinary construction on
\mathcal{U}
.

Our main result is the following.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 1, we recall the basic theory of Fontaine–Faltings modules, Faltings’ period ring, and the

-functor.

2.1.

2.1.2.
The logarithmic gluing functor

We now show that the local categories defined above are canonically independent of the choice of Frobenius lift. The following elementary identity will be used in the proof of the cocycle condition.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f_{n}(X)=n!\tbinom{X}{n}=X(X-1)(X-2)\cdots(X-n+1)\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
for every positive integer
n
and let
f_{0}=1
.
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a basis of
\mathbb{Q}[X]
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Set
a_{mn}^{k}\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

f_{n}(X)f_{m}(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{m+n}a_{mn}^{k}f_{k}(X).

Then

\sum_{m,n\geq 0}a_{mn}^{k}\frac{(X-1)^{m}}{m!}\frac{(Y-1)^{n}}{n!}=\frac{(XY-1)^{k}}{k!}

in
\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

In the ring
\mathbb{Q}[[X-1,Y-1,t]]
, one has

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}(t)\sum_{m,n\geq 0}a_{mn}^{k}\frac{(X-1)^{m}}{m!}\frac{(Y-1)^{n}}{n!}&=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}f_{m}(t)f_{n}(t)\frac{(X-1)^{m}}{m!}\frac{(Y-1)^{n}}{n!}\\
&=\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}f_{m}(t)\frac{(X-1)^{m}}{m!}\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}(t)\frac{(Y-1)^{n}}{n!}\right)\\
&=X^{t}Y^{t}=(XY)^{t}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}(t)\frac{(XY-1)^{k}}{k!}.\end{split}

By comparing the coefficients of
f_{k}(t)
in the equation, we have

\sum_{m,n\geq 0}a_{mn}^{k}\frac{(X-1)^{m}}{m!}\frac{(Y-1)^{n}}{n!}=\frac{(XY-1)^{k}}{k!}.\qed

Theorem 2.3

.

\Phi

and

\mathcal{MF}_{[a,b]}^{\nabla,\mathrm{tf},\Psi}((\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})/W)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{MF}_{[a,b]}^{\nabla,\mathrm{tf},\Phi}((\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})/W).

equivalences satisfy the cocycle condition. Consequently, up to

and

The torsion-free case follows from the torsion case by reducing modulo

for every

Thus it suffices to prove the theorem for torsion logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings modules.
For any two liftings

Without loss of generality, we assume

be local coordinates such that our objects have logarithmic poles along

.
The Frobenius lifts
\Phi
and

have been required,
by the conditions specified in Setup

to respect the divisor,
thus for any

, there exist

such that

In particular

Hence, for any multi-index

For (2), let

. Write

Lemma 2.10

Remark 2.11
.

In the proof of

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
M
be a
p^{n}
-torsion object in

\mathcal{MF}_{[0,p-2]}^{\nabla,\Phi}((\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})/W)
admitting an adapted basis.
Then
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R)}\bigl(M,D_{\log}(\widehat{R})\bigr)=0
.

Proof.

We first reduce to the case
pM=0
. Suppose that
p^{n}M=0
. From the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow p^{n-1}M\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M/p^{n-1}M\longrightarrow 0

and the associated long exact sequence for

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R)}(-,D_{\log}(\widehat{R}))
, the vanishing for
M
follows from the vanishing for

p^{n-1}M
and
M/p^{n-1}M
. Induction on
n
therefore reduces us
to the case
pM=0
.

Assume now that
pM=0
, and consider an extension

0\longrightarrow D_{\log}(\widehat{R})\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0

in
\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R)
. We need to show that it splits.
Since
D_{\log}(\widehat{R})
is
p
-divisible, multiplication by
p
on

D_{\log}(\widehat{R})
is surjective.
Hence, by the snake lemma, multiplication by
p
on the extension induces an exact sequence

(2.11)

0\longrightarrow B/pB\longrightarrow P[p]\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0.

Since
F^{i}M=0
for
i\geq p-1
, it suffices to split the truncation of
(
2.11
) at degree
p-2
, namely

(2.12)

0\longrightarrow(B/pB)_{\leq p-2}\longrightarrow P[p]_{\leq p-2}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0.

The isomorphism class of the extension (
2.12
) is an element of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R)}\bigl(M,(B/pB)_{\leq p-2}\bigr)
.
By the discussion in the proof of
Lemma 2.10
, we have

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R)}(M,X)=0
, thus there is an injection

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R)}\bigl(M,(B/pB)_{\leq p-2}\bigr)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R)}(M,E_{\log}).

Pushing out (
2.12
) along the surjection

(B/pB)_{\leq p-2}\longrightarrow E_{\log}

yields an extension

(2.13)

0\longrightarrow E_{\log}\longrightarrow P_{E}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0.

Since (
2.12
) is annihilated by
p
, so is its pushout.
Thus the above sequence is an extension of filtered
R/pR
-modules.
It remains to show that (
2.13
) splits in
\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R)
.

After forgetting the Frobenius structures,
(
2.13
) splits as an extension of filtered

R/pR
-modules. Indeed, the sequence is strict, and each

\operatorname{gr}^{i}_{F}M
is a finite free
R/pR
-module.
Hence the induced surjections

\operatorname{gr}^{i}_{F}P_{E}\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}^{i}_{F}M
admit sections. Lifting these sections successively along the
filtration gives a filtered splitting
s\colon M\to P_{E}
.

Let

\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{h}\}
be an adapted basis of
M
. Let

e_{i}\in[0,p-2]
be determined by

m_{i}\in{\mathrm{Fil}}^{e_{i}}M-{\mathrm{Fil}}^{e_{i}+1}M
. Write

\varphi^{e_{i}}(m_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{h}a_{ij}m_{j},\qquad a_{ij}\in R/pR.

The matrix
(a_{ij})
is invertible. Since the Frobenius on

R_{\infty}/pR_{\infty}
is bijective, choose

b_{ij}\in R_{\infty}\subset\overline{\widehat{R}}_{\log}
such that

b_{ij}^{p}\equiv a_{ij}\pmod{p}
.
Choose
y_{i}\in\overline{\widehat{R}}_{\log}
such that

\sum_{j}a_{ij}s(m_{j})-\varphi^{e_{i}}(s(m_{i}))=y_{i}\pmod{p}

in
E_{\log}
. Let
s^{\prime}:M\to P_{E}
be another splitting with

s^{\prime}(m_{i})=s(m_{i})+x_{i}\pmod{p},\qquad x_{i}\in p^{e_{i}/p}\overline{\widehat{R}}_{\log}.

Then
s^{\prime}
respects the Frobenius structure if and only if

\varphi^{e_{i}}(s^{\prime}(m_{i}))=s^{\prime}(\varphi^{e_{i}}(m_{i})),\qquad 1\leq i\leq h,

or equivalently,

x_{i}^{p}\equiv(-p)^{e_{i}}\left(y_{i}+\sum_{j}b_{ij}x_{j}\right)\pmod{p^{e_{i}+1}},\qquad 1\leq i\leq h.

We claim that these congruences always admit a solution.
Indeed, choose a finite normal
\widehat{R}
-subalgebra

R^{\prime\prime}\subset\overline{\widehat{R}}_{\log}
occurring in the
definition of
\overline{\widehat{R}}_{\log}
and containing all the

and

. Then

Composing these isomorphisms, we obtain

By the restriction compatibility in
Proposition 2.13
(2) and the cocycle condition in
Proposition 2.15
, the isomorphisms
\psi_{ij}
satisfy the cocycle condition on triple overlaps. Therefore the local systems
\mathbb{D}^{\log}_{\Phi_{i}}(M|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}})
form descent data with respect to the covering
\{\mathcal{Y}_{i,K}^{\circ}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
.

Now, again using
[
Con06
, Theorem 4.2.9]
, we globalize the
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
-functor. Our goal is to construct a
p
-adic étale local system on
\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ}
. At each finite level, this is specified by a collection of finite étale covers of
\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ}
. Given our covering
\bigsqcup_{i}\mathcal{Y}_{i}\to\mathcal{Y}
, we obtain an admissible covering
\bigsqcup_{i}\mathcal{Y}_{i,K}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ}
,
which in particular is faithfully flat and admits local fpqc
quasi-sections in the sense of
[
Con06
, Definition 4.2.1]
.
The local
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
-functors give compatible finite étale covers of the
\mathcal{Y}_{i,K}^{\circ}
. Since these covers are finite,
the hypotheses of
[
Con06
, Theorem 4.2.9]
are satisfied, and they descend to finite étale covers of
\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ}
. Passing compatibly through the finite levels gives a
p
-adic étale local system on
\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ}
.

Theorem 2.16
.

We keep the notation of
Setup 2.5
.

(1)

There is a functor
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
from the category of torsion
(resp. torsion-free) logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings modules over

(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})
with Hodge–Tate weights in
[0,p-2]
to the
category of continuous representations of

\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ})
on finite
p
-primary torsion
(resp. finite free)
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules. It is compatible with
Faltings’ usual
\mathbb{D}
-functor in the following sense: for every
such logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings module
M
, there is a canonical
isomorphism of continuous representations of

\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{U}_{K})
,

\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M)|_{\mathcal{U}_{K}}\cong\mathbb{D}(M|_{\mathcal{U}}).

(2)

If
Y/W
is proper, then
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
can be algebraized to a
functor from the category of torsion (resp. torsion-free) logarithmic
Fontaine–Faltings modules over
(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})
to the
category of continuous representations of

\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(Y_{K}^{\circ})
on finite
p
-primary torsion
(resp. finite free)
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules.

Proof.

The first assertion follows by gluing the local

\mathbb{D}^{\log}
-functors using the descent data constructed above.
If
Y/W
is proper, then

(Y_{K}^{\circ})^{\mathrm{an}}=\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ}
by

[
Con99
, Theorem A.3.1]
. Thus

\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(Y_{K}^{\circ})\cong\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ})
by

[
Lü93
, Theorem 3.1]
. This proves the second assertion.
∎

Remark 2.17
.

The logarithmic
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
-functor extends naturally to logarithmic
Fontaine–Faltings modules with Hodge–Tate weights in any interval

[a,b]
satisfying
0\leq b-a\leq p-2
.
Indeed, let
M=(V,\nabla,{\mathrm{Fil}},\varphi)
be such a module.
Define

M_{\mathrm{new}}=(V,\nabla,{\mathrm{Fil}}_{\mathrm{new}},\varphi_{\mathrm{new}})

by setting

{\mathrm{Fil}}_{\mathrm{new}}^{i}V=\begin{cases}{\mathrm{Fil}}^{i+a}V,&0\leq i\leq b-a,\\
0,&b-a<i\leq p-2,\end{cases}

where
\varphi_{\mathrm{new}}
is induced from
\varphi
by the corresponding
reindexing of
\widetilde{V}
.
Then
M_{\mathrm{new}}
is a logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings module with
Hodge–Tate weights in
[0,p-2]
. We define

\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M)\coloneqq\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{\mathrm{new}})(a),

where
(a)
denotes the
a
-th Tate twist of the representation.

In other words, we have shown the following. Let
Y
be a proper smooth scheme over
W
, let
Z
be a relative simple normal crossing divisor on
Y
, and set
Y_{K}^{\circ}\coloneqq Y_{K}-Z_{K}
. If
0\leq b-a\leq p-2
, then, for

*\in\{\emptyset,\mathrm{tf}\}
, there is a functor

\mathbb{D}^{\log}\colon\mathcal{MF}_{[a,b]}^{\nabla,*}((\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})/W)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\bigl(\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ})\bigr)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\bigl(\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(Y_{K}^{\circ})\bigr),

where the second arrow is an equivalence of categories since
Y/W
is proper. Representations in the essential image of
\mathbb{D}^{\log}

are called
logarithmic crystalline representations with Hodge–Tate weights in
[a,b]
.

We next record two consequences of the preceding local results that will be used below. Using the Zariski local existence of adapted bases, the restriction compatibility and the Ext-vanishing established above yield the following global statement.

Proposition 2.18
.

We keep the notation of
Setup 2.5
.

(1)

(Restriction compatibility)
Let
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
be an open subset, let
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
be its

p
-adic completion with rigid generic fiber
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}_{K}
, and set
\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\prime\circ}\coloneqq\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}_{K}\cap\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ}
.
For every object
M
of either
\mathcal{MF}_{[0,p-2]}^{\nabla}((\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})/W)
or
\mathcal{MF}_{[0,p-2]}^{\nabla,\mathrm{tf}}((\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})/W)
, there is a canonical isomorphism

\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M)|_{\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\prime\circ}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M|_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}).

(2)

(Exactness)

\mathbb{D}^{\log}
is exact on both
\mathcal{MF}_{[0,p-2]}^{\nabla}((\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})/W)
and
\mathcal{MF}_{[0,p-2]}^{\nabla,\mathrm{tf}}((\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})/W)
.

Proof.

For (1), choose a logarithmically small affine open covering

\{Y_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
of
Y
such that

M|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}}
admits an adapted basis for every
i
. By the
construction of
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M)
, its restriction to

\mathcal{Y}_{i,K}^{\circ}
is canonically identified with
\mathbb{D}^{\log}_{\Phi_{i}}(M|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}})
.

Choose a logarithmically small affine open covering

\{Y^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}\}_{i^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
of
Y^{\prime}
. Since
Y
is separated,

Y_{i}\cap Y^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}
is affine for every
i\in\Lambda
and

i^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
. After refining the covering if necessary, we may assume
that each
Y^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}
is contained in some
Y_{i}
. Since

M|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}}
admits an adapted basis, so does

M|_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}}
. By
Proposition 2.13
(2), there is a canonical
isomorphism

\mathbb{D}^{\log}_{\Phi_{i}}(M|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}})|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i^{\prime},K}^{\prime\circ}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{D}^{\log}_{\Phi_{i}}(M|_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}}).

These isomorphisms are compatible on the overlaps by

Proposition 2.13
(2) and the cocycle condition in

Proposition 2.15
. Hence they glue to a canonical
isomorphism

\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M)|_{\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\prime\circ}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M|_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}).

For (2), we first consider the torsion case. Let

0\longrightarrow M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}\longrightarrow M_{3}\longrightarrow 0

be an exact sequence of torsion logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings modules.
Choose a logarithmically small affine open covering

\{Y_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
such that

M_{1}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}}
,
M_{2}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}}
, and

M_{3}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}}
all admit adapted bases. On each
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
,
applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R_{i})}\bigl(-,D_{\log}(\widehat{R}_{i})\bigr)

to the restricted exact sequence gives an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}_{\Phi_{i}}(M_{3}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}_{\Phi_{i}}(M_{2}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}_{\Phi_{i}}(M_{1}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}})\longrightarrow 0

by
Lemma 2.12
. By (1), this sequence is canonically identified with the restriction to
\mathcal{Y}_{i,K}^{\circ}
of

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{3})\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{2})\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{1})\longrightarrow 0

Since restriction of representations does not change the underlying
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules, the latter sequence is exact. The torsion-free case follows by reducing modulo
p^{n}
for every

n\geq 1
and then passing to the inverse limit.
∎

2.3.
Full faithfulness of
\mathbb{D}^{\log}

Finally, we prove the full faithfulness of
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
. The main ingredient is a logarithmic analogue of
[
Fal89
, Lemma 2.5]
, which gives a length estimate for a logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings module from a representation mapping minimally to its associated
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
-representation.

Lemma 2.19
.

We keep the notation in
Setup 2.1
. Let
M
be a
p^{n}
-torsion logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings module over
(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})
with Hodge–Tate weights in
[0,p-2]
, and let
L
be a
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
-representation of
\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ})
. Suppose that
\lambda\colon L\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M)
is a morphism of representations which does not factor through
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M/N)
for any nonzero logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings submodule
N\subset M
. Then

\operatorname{length}_{R_{(p)}}M_{(p)}\leq\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}L.

Moreover, if equality holds, then
\lambda
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We first reduce to the case
n=1
. Assume that the assertion is known for representations killed by
p
and, inductively, for those killed by
p^{n-1}
. Let

L[p]=\{\ell\in L\mid p\ell=0\}.

The restriction of
\lambda
induces a morphism
L[p]\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M/pM)
.
Let
M_{1}\subset M
be maximal, with
pM\subset M_{1}
, such that this morphism factors through
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M/M_{1})
. By the
p
-torsion case,

\operatorname{length}_{R_{(p)}}(M/M_{1})_{(p)}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}L[p].

The composite
L\to\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M)\to\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{1})
vanishes on
L[p]
, and therefore induces

\overline{\lambda}\colon L/L[p]\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{1}).

The minimality assumption for
\lambda
implies the corresponding minimality assumption for this morphism.
Since
L/L[p]
is killed by
p^{n-1}
, this morphism factors through

\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{1})[p^{n-1}]=\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{1}/p^{n-1}M_{1}).

Its minimality therefore implies that
p^{n-1}M_{1}=0
.
Hence, by induction,

\operatorname{length}_{R_{(p)}}(M_{1})_{(p)}\leq\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(L/L[p]).

Adding the two inequalities gives

\operatorname{length}_{R_{(p)}}M_{(p)}\leq\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}L.

If equality holds, then equality holds in both inequalities, and the induction hypothesis together with the
p
-torsion case shows that
\lambda
is an isomorphism. Thus it remains to prove the assertion when
pM=pL=0
.

By
Lemma 2.10
,

\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R)}(M,E_{\log}).

Hence
\lambda
induces an
R/pR
-linear map

\alpha\colon M\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(L,E_{\log}),\qquad\alpha(m)(\ell)=\lambda(\ell)(m).

Here
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(L,E_{\log})
is regraded as an
object of
\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{\mathrm{big}}(R)
and is isomorphic to
E_{\log}^{\oplus\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}L}
.
By
[
Fal89
, Proposition 2.2]
,
\alpha
is strict and its kernel
N
is an object of
\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}(R)
. Using the same argument as in

[
Fal89
, pp. 39–40]
,
N
is stable under the logarithmic connection,
and hence is a logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings submodule of
M
.
Since every element in the image of
\lambda
vanishes on
N
, the morphism
\lambda
factors through
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M/N)
.
By the minimality assumption,
N=0
, and therefore
\alpha
is injective.

After inverting
T_{1}\cdots T_{s}
, we obtain an injection

M|_{\mathcal{U}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\bigl(L,E_{\log}[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]\bigr).

We claim that the natural map

E_{\log}[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]\longrightarrow\overline{\widehat{R}^{\circ}}/p\overline{\widehat{R}^{\circ}}

induced by

\overline{\widehat{R}}_{\log}\hookrightarrow\overline{\widehat{R}^{\circ}}

is injective.
Since
T_{1}\cdots T_{s}
is invertible in the target, it suffices to consider
x\in\overline{\widehat{R}}_{\log}
whose image in
\overline{\widehat{R}^{\circ}}/p\overline{\widehat{R}^{\circ}}
is zero. Thus
{x=py}
for some
{y\in\overline{\widehat{R}^{\circ}}}
. Choose a finite normal
\widehat{R}
-algebra
C\subset\overline{\widehat{R}}_{\log}
containing
x
.
Since
{C[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]}
is excellent and normal, its
p
-adic completion
C[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]^{\wedge}
is normal. As
y
is integral over
\widehat{R}^{\circ}
, the natural map
\widehat{R}^{\circ}\to C[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]^{\wedge}
shows that
y
is integral over
C[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]^{\wedge}
. Thus the normality of
C[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]^{\wedge}
implies
y\in C[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]^{\wedge}
. Therefore,

x\in pC[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]^{\wedge}\cap C\subseteq pC[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]\subseteq p\overline{\widehat{R}}_{\log}[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})].

This implies that
E_{\log}[1/(T_{1}\cdots T_{s})]\longrightarrow\overline{\widehat{R}^{\circ}}/p\overline{\widehat{R}^{\circ}}
is injective.

Via this injection and the comparison isomorphism of

Theorem 2.16
, the restriction of
\alpha
gives the ordinary evaluation map

\alpha_{\mathcal{U}}\colon M|_{\mathcal{U}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\bigl(L,\overline{\widehat{R}^{\circ}}/p\overline{\widehat{R}^{\circ}}\bigr),

which is injective. This implies that the restriction

\lambda|_{\mathcal{U}}\colon L\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}(M|_{\mathcal{U}})

does not factor through
\mathbb{D}(M|_{\mathcal{U}}/N^{\circ})
for any nonzero Fontaine–Faltings submodule

N^{\circ}\subseteq M|_{\mathcal{U}}
.
Therefore
[
Fal89
, Lemma 2.5]
gives

\operatorname{length}_{R^{\circ}_{(p)}}(M|_{\mathcal{U}})_{(p)}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}L.

Since
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}
are already invertible in
R_{(p)}
, we have
R^{\circ}_{(p)}=R_{(p)}
. Hence

\operatorname{length}_{R_{(p)}}M_{(p)}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}L.

If equality holds, then
[
Fal89
, Lemma 2.5]
implies that

\lambda|_{\mathcal{U}}
is an isomorphism. Since restriction does not change the underlying
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map,
\lambda
itself is an isomorphism.
∎

Using this logarithmic analogue of Faltings’ lemma, we now prove the full faithfulness of
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
.

Proposition 2.20
.

The functor
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
is fully faithful on both the torsion and the torsion-free categories of logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings modules with Hodge–Tate weights in
[0,p-2]
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in three steps.

Step 1: Reduction to the torsion and local case.

By reducing modulo
p^{n}
and passing to the inverse limit, full faithfulness for torsion-free logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings modules follows from the torsion case. Hence it suffices to treat torsion objects.

Assume that
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
is fully faithful over every logarithmically small affine open subset of
\mathcal{Y}
.
Let
f\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
be a morphism such that
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(f)=0
. For every logarithmically small affine open subset
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{Y}
, we have
\mathbb{D}^{\log}\bigl(f|_{\mathcal{V}}\bigr)=0
by
Proposition 2.18
(1).
The local full faithfulness implies that
f|_{\mathcal{V}}=0
. Hence
f=0
.

Now let
\alpha\colon\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{2})\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{1})

be a morphism of
\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{Y}_{K}^{\circ})
-representations. For every logarithmically small affine open subset
\mathcal{V}
,
local full faithfulness yields a unique morphism

f_{\mathcal{V}}\colon M_{1}|_{(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}\cap\mathcal{Z})}\longrightarrow M_{2}|_{(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}\cap\mathcal{Z})}

such that
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(f_{\mathcal{V}})=\alpha|_{\mathcal{V}^{\circ}_{K}}
by
Proposition 2.18
(1).
If
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
are two such open subsets, then on
\mathcal{V}_{K}\cap\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{K}
,

\mathbb{D}^{\log}(f_{\mathcal{V}}|_{\mathcal{V}\cap\mathcal{V}^{\prime}})=\alpha|_{\mathcal{V}^{\circ}_{K}\cap\mathcal{V}^{\prime\circ}_{K}}=\mathbb{D}^{\log}(f_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}|_{\mathcal{V}\cap\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}),

and local full faithfulness implies
f_{\mathcal{V}}|_{\mathcal{V}\cap\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}=f_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}|_{\mathcal{V}\cap\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}
.
Thus the morphisms
f_{\mathcal{V}}
glue to a global morphism

f\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
satisfying

\mathbb{D}^{\log}(f)=\alpha
.

Therefore, to prove that
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
is fully faithful, it suffices to establish full faithfulness for the local
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
functor. In what follows, we adopt the notation from
Setup 2.1
.

Step 2: Faithfulness.

Let
f:M_{1}\to M_{2}
be a morphism of logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings modules satisfying
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(f)=0
. By the comparison isomorphism in

Theorem 2.16
,
\mathbb{D}(f|_{\mathcal{U}})=0
. Since the usual
\mathbb{D}
-functor is faithful,
f|_{\mathcal{U}}=0
.

For every
n\geq 1
, after forgetting the connection,

f\bmod p^{n}
is a morphism in
\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}(R)
. On the one hand, since
\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}(R)
is an abelian category,

\operatorname{Im}(f\bmod p^{n})\in\mathcal{MF}^{\mathrm{tor}}(R).

Its localization at
T_{1}\cdots T_{s}
is zero. On the other hand, the underlying
\widehat{R}
-module of
\operatorname{Im}(f\bmod p^{n})
is Zariski locally a direct sum of modules of the form
R/p^{e}R
. Since
T_{1}\cdots T_{s}
is a non-zero-divisor on each
R/p^{e}R
, it follows that

\operatorname{Im}(f\bmod p^{n})=0.

Thus
f\bmod p^{n}=0
for every
n
, and hence
f=0
. Therefore
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
is faithful.

Step 3: Fullness.

It is enough to prove the assertion for
p^{n}
-torsion objects. Indeed, the torsion-free case follows by applying the torsion case to
M/p^{n}M
and passing to the inverse limit. The resulting morphisms are compatible by the faithfulness proved in Step 2.
Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be
p^{n}
-torsion logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings modules, and let

\alpha:\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{2})\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{1})

be a morphism of representations. For every torsion logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings module

M
, we have

(2.19)

\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M)=\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{D}(M|_{\mathcal{U}})=\operatorname{length}_{R_{(p)}}M_{(p)}.

where the first equality follows from the comparison isomorphism in
Theorem 2.16
, and the second follows from
[
Fal89
, Theorem 2.4]
, together with
R^{\circ}_{(p)}=R_{(p)}
. Consider

(\alpha,\operatorname{id}):\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{2})\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{1}\oplus M_{2}).

Choose a maximal logarithmic Fontaine–Faltings submodule

N\subset M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
such that this morphism factors through
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(Q)
, where
Q=(M_{1}\oplus M_{2})/N
.
Write

\lambda:\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{2})\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\log}(Q)

for the induced morphism and
q:M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\to Q
for the quotient map. By maximality,
\lambda
satisfies the hypothesis of

Lemma 2.19
, hence

\operatorname{length}_{R_{(p)}}Q_{(p)}\leq\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{D}^{\log}(M_{2})=\operatorname{length}_{R_{(p)}}(M_{2})_{(p)}.

Let
q_{2}:M_{2}\to Q
be the restriction of
q
. Since
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(q_{2})\circ\lambda=\operatorname{id}
, the map
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(q_{2})
is surjective. By
(
2.19
), the opposite inequality follows, and therefore

\operatorname{length}_{R_{(p)}}Q_{(p)}=\operatorname{length}_{R_{(p)}}(M_{2})_{(p)}.

The equality statement in
Lemma 2.19
shows that

\lambda
is an isomorphism. Hence
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(q_{2})
is an isomorphism. Since
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
is exact by
Proposition 2.18
(2) and faithful by Step 2,
q_{2}
is an isomorphism. If
q_{1}:M_{1}\to Q
denotes the other component of
q
, define

f=q_{2}^{-1}\circ q_{1}:M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}.

We can check that
\mathbb{D}^{\log}(f)=\alpha
. Thus
\mathbb{D}^{\log}
is full.
∎

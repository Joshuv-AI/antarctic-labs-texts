Chow polynomials of simplicial posets with positive h h -vector are real-rooted
Elena Hoster, Christian Stump

Abstract
. We prove that a finite graded simplicial poset with a top element added has real-rooted Chow and augmented Chow polynomials whenever it has a positive h h -vector. This class of posets include Cohen-Macaulay simplicial posets and in particular lattices of flats of uniform matroids.

Chow polynomials for uniform matroids

The lattice of flats of the uniform matroid
U_{k,n}
has coatoms given by all
(k-1)
-subsets of
\{1,\dots,n\}
.
It is thus obtained from a graded, simplical poset by adding a top element.
Our main result thus recovers
[
FMSV24
, Theorem 1.10]
where it was shown that its augmented Chow polynomial is real-rooted and
[
BV25
]
where it was shown that its Chow polynomial is real-rooted.

Chain polynomials of simplicial posets

In
[
ADKE24
]
it was shown that the chain polynomial of
{\widehat{P}}
is real-rooted, if
P
is a finite graded simplical poset with positive
h
-vector.
Our argument for the real-rootedness of the Chow polynomials is based on the same initial rewriting of the flag
h
-vector in
Equation

1.3
,
but the families of interlacing polynomials we use is rather different from theirs.
Our approach relies on a decomposition of Chow polynomials into palindromic polynomials using the
\gamma
-expansion.
In particular, for the Boolean lattice
{\widehat{P}}=B_{n}
of rank
n
, this yields a decomposition of both the Eulerian polynomials and the binomial Eulerian polynomials into interlacing palindromic polynomials, which provides an alternative proof of their real-rootedness.

Chow polynomials of Cohen-Macaulay posets

It is conjectured in
[
FMV24
, Conjecture 4.26]
that the Chow polynomials of Cohen-Macaulay posets are real-rooted.
Our main results proves this conjecture in the case of Cohen-Macaulay simplicial posets as Cohen-Macaulay posets are known to be
h
-positive
[
Sta96
, Theorem 6.4]
.
It is moreover conjectured in
[
FMV24
, Remark 4.28]
that the Chow polynomial of a Cohen-Macaulay poset interlaces the augmented Chow polynomial.
Our main result again proves this conjecture in the case of the dual of Cohen-Macaulay simplicial posets.

Chow and chain polynomials of geometric lattices

Geometric lattices are the lattices of flats of matroids.
It is conjectured that Chow polynomials of geometric lattices are real-rooted
[
FS24
, Conjecture 8.18]
and that chain polynomial of geometric lattices are real-rooted
[
AKE23
, Conjecture 1.2]
.
Our main result covers only very particular cases of the first conjecture.

This section recalls properties of real-rooted polynomials from

f=\prod(x-\alpha_{i}),\quad g=\prod(x-\beta_{i})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[x]

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f,g,h\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[x]
be real-rooted polynomials.

1.

If
f,g\preceq h
, then
f+g\preceq h
.

2.

If
f\preceq g,h
, then
f\preceq g+h
.

3.

If
f\preceq g

g\preceq x\cdot f

The relation of interlacing polynomials is not transitive and a sequence
(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
is an

if all polynomials are real-rooted, and
f_{i}\preceq f_{j}
for

Lemma 2.2

(
[

]
)

Let

be real-rooted polynomials,

Then
(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
is an interlacing sequence if and only if
f_{i}\preceq f_{i+1}

Interlacing sequences play a fundamental role in proving real-rootedness.
For later usage, we list further fundamental properties for sums of interlacing polynomials,

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
be an interlacing sequence.

1.

Let

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}f_{i}

f_{1}\ \preceq\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f_{i}\ \preceq\ f_{n}\,.

2.

The sequence
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
of lower partial sums,

p_{k}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(x)\quad\text{ for }1\leq k\leq n\,,

is an interlacing sequence.

3.

The sequence
(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
of upper partial sums,

q_{k}(x)=\sum_{i=k}^{n}f_{i}(x)\quad\text{ for }1\leq k\leq n\,,

is an interlacing sequence.

4.

The sequence
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n-\ell+1})

r_{k}(x)=f_{k}(x)+\dots+f_{k+\ell}(x)\quad\text{ for }1\leq k\leq n-\ell\,,

is an interlacing sequence for any

5.

The sequence
(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n+1})

t_{k}(x)=x\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}f_{i}(x)+\sum_{i=k}^{n}f_{i}(x)\quad\text{ for }1\leq k\leq n+1\,,

is an interlacing sequence.

A polynomial

We next turn to the

Lemma 2.4

(
[

)
.

Let

Then

f\text{ real-rooted}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gamma(f)\text{ real-rooted}\,.

The following proposition extends this to interlacingness.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f,g\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[x]
be palindromic with
\deg g=\deg f+1
.
Then

f\preceq g\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gamma(f)\preceq\gamma(g)\,.

Proof.

We first consider a general palindromic and real-rooted polynomial
h(x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[x]
of degree
d
.
Its roots are

0>\rho_{1}\geq\dots\geq\rho_{d}

with
\rho_{i}=1/\rho_{k+1-i}
for all
i
, and the roots of
\gamma(h)
are

(2.1)

\rho_{i}/(1+\rho_{i})^{2}\text{ for }0>\rho_{i}>-1\,.

Moreover, the multiplicity of the root
-1
in
h
is

(2.2)

\mu_{h}(-1)=d-2\cdot\deg\gamma(h)\,.

By
Lemma

2.4
, we may assume that
f,g,\gamma(f),\gamma(g)
are all real-rooted and the relation between the roots is as given in (
2.1
) and the multiplicities of
-1
are given in (
2.2
).
Let

f=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x-\alpha_{i}),\quad g=\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(x-\beta_{i})

with

0>\alpha_{1}\geq\dots\geq\alpha_{d}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0>\beta_{1}\geq\dots\geq\beta_{d+1}\,.

We first assume that
f\preceq g
.
Since the function
\lambda\mapsto\tfrac{\lambda}{(1+\lambda)^{2}}
is strictly increasing in the open interval
(-1,0)
, the roots of
\gamma(f)
and of
\gamma(g)
interlace in the same order as the roots of
f
and of
g
interlace in this interval.
We conclude
\gamma(f)\preceq\gamma(g)
.

We next assume that
\gamma(f)\preceq\gamma(g)
.
By the same argument as above, the roots of
\gamma(f)
and of
\gamma(g)
interlace in the same order as the roots of
f
and of
g
inside the open interval
(-1,0)
.
We now distinguish the two cases
\deg\gamma(g)\in\{\deg\gamma(f),\deg\gamma(f)+1\}
.

If
\deg\gamma(g)=\deg\gamma(f)=k
, then we have found
2k

f
and of
g
, and
\deg g=\deg f+1
implies that
\mu_{g}(-1)=\mu_{f}(-1)+1
.
We conclude that the roots of
f

g

f\preceq g
.

If
\deg\gamma(g)=\deg\gamma(f)+1=k

2k

g
and
2k-2

f
, and
\deg g=\deg f+1

\mu_{g}(-1)=\mu_{f}(-1)-1
.
We again conclude that the roots of
f
interlace the roots of

.
∎

3.
A family of interlacing polynomials

In this section, we introduce a family of interlacing polynomials that play the crucial role in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.
Let
S\subseteq T\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Define the polynomial
p_{n,k}^{S\subseteq T}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[x]
for
0\leq k\leq n
by

(3.1)

\displaystyle p_{n,k}^{S\subseteq T}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\widehat{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n+1,k+1}\\
S\subseteq\operatorname{Des}(w)\subseteq T\end{subarray}}x^{\operatorname{des}(w)}\,,

where
\widehat{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n+1,k+1}=\left\{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}\;|\;w(1)=k+1\,,\ \operatorname{Des}(w)\ \text{isolated}\right\}
.
When the set
S
is isolated, this polynomial can be described recursively by

\displaystyle p_{1,k}^{S\subseteq T}(x)=\begin{cases}1&k=0\text{ and }S=\emptyset\,,\\
x&k=1\text{ and }T=\{1\}\,,\\
0&\text{else}\,,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle p_{n,k}^{S\subseteq T}(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{1\in T\}}\cdot x\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}p_{n-1,j}^{S-1\subseteq(T-1)\setminus\{1\}}(x)+\mathbbm{1}_{\{1\notin S\}}\cdot\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}p_{n-1,j}^{S-1\subseteq T-1}(x)\,.

where
S-1=\left\{i-1\;|\;i\in S\right\}\setminus\{1\}
and
T-1
analogously.
For
s\in S\subseteq T
, we have

\displaystyle p_{n,k}^{S\setminus\{s\}\subseteq T}(x)

\displaystyle=p_{n,k}^{S\subseteq T}(x)+p_{n,k}^{S\setminus\{s\}\subseteq T\setminus\{s\}}(x)\,,

since the left side polynomial counts permutations in which
s

can be a descent
,
while the polynomials on the right side count permutations in which
s

must be a descent
and in which
s

must not be a descent
, respectively.

We write
p_{n,k}^{T}=p_{n,k}^{\emptyset\subseteq T}
and
[a,b]=\{a,a+1,\dots,b\}
.
The following lemma gives a description of the Chow polynomials in terms of the polynomials
p_{n,k}^{S\subseteq T}
for a particular choice of subsets.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
P
be a graded simplicial poset of rank
n\geq 2
with
h
-vector
(h_{0},\dots,h_{n})
.
Let
{\widehat{P}}^{\ast}
be the dual poset of
{\widehat{P}}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\gamma(\operatorname{H}_{{\widehat{P}}};x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}\cdot p_{n,k}^{[1,n-1]}(x)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma(\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{aug}}_{{\widehat{P}}^{\ast}};x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}\cdot p_{n,k}^{[2,n]}(x)\,,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\gamma(\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{aug}}_{\widehat{P}};x)=\gamma(\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{aug}}_{{\widehat{P}}^{\ast}};x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}\cdot p_{n,k}^{[1,n]}(x)\,.

Proof.

We use
Equation

1.3
to rewrite the flag
h
-vectors
\beta_{\widehat{P}}(S)
in terms of the
h
-vector of
P
.
The
\gamma
-polynomial of the Chow polynomial then becomes

\displaystyle\gamma(\operatorname{H}_{{\widehat{P}}};x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{2,\dots,n\}\\
S\text{ isolated}\end{subarray}}\beta_{\widehat{P}}(S)\cdot x^{|S|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{2,\dots,n\}\\
S\text{ isolated}\end{subarray}}\#\left\{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}\;|\;w(1)=k+1\,,\ \operatorname{Des}(w)=n+1-S\right\}\cdot x^{|S|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n-1\}\\
S\text{ isolated}\end{subarray}}\#\left\{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}\;|\;w(1)=k+1\,,\ \operatorname{Des}(w)=S\right\}\cdot x^{|S|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\widehat{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n+1,k+1}\\

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}\cdot p_{n,k}^{[1,n-1]}(x)\,.

The calculation for the augmented Chow polynomial is completely analogous.

\beta_{{\widehat{P}}^{\ast}}(n+1-S)=\beta_{\widehat{P}}(S)
.

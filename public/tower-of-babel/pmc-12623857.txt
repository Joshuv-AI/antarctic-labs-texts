Clinical outcomes and adverse events of nontuberculous mycobacterial lung disease treated at an Italian tertiary referral center
Authors: Serena Maria Carli, Paola Mencarini, Maria Angela Vittoria Licata, Maria Musso, Carlotta Cerva, Annelisa Mastrobattista, Pietro Vittozzi, Silvia Mosti, Carla Nisii, Antonio Mazzarelli, Assunta Navarra, Gabriella De Carli, Stefania Ianniello, Fabrizio Palmieri, Gina Gualano
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTreatment of NTM lung disease (NTM-LD) is based on combinations of antimycobacterial drugs. It lasts 12 months if respiratory samples become culture negative, while in refractory cases it can last up to 18–24 months. Therapeutic pathway is complicated by frequent adverse events (AEs). Sometimes, symptomatic therapy is not sufficient to control AEs, making necessary to suspend the drug involved or the entire treatment. This study aims to describe the frequency and type of AEs in patients with NTM-LD and to evaluate their impact on the outcomes of treatment. This is a retrospective observational study. We analysed clinical data, regimen composition, AEs and outcomes of NTM-LD patients followed between January 2016 and June 2023 at the National Institute for Infectious Diseases “L. Spallanzani” in Rome. Out of 131 patients, fifty-eight (44.3%) were men; median age was 66 years (IQR: 56–73). One hundred (76.3%) developed AEs. Total AEs were 229, of which 81 were serious adverse events (SAEs). Cure was obtained in 113 cases (86.3%), therapeutic failure occurred in 10 (7.6%), 6 patients died (4.6%), and 2 (1.5%) were lost-to-follow-up. Despite of AEs, most patients were able to complete treatment with appropriate management and monitoring. AEs affect patient quality of life and treatment adherence. Therefore, it is important to identify factors that can predict and prevent them. Clear and detailed information about drug side effects, with instructions to follow in case of AEs, active monitoring at every contact, flexibility and accessibility to healthcare professionals are key factors for therapy success.Keywords: Nontuberculous mycobacterial lung disease treatment, Adverse events, Treatment outcomeSubject terms: Microbiology, Diseases, Health care, Signs and symptoms

Introduction

Nontuberculous mycobacteria (NTM) are diverse microbial species encompassing commensals and pathogens with the ability to cause pulmonary disease in both immunocompetent and immunocompromised individuals1. While geographic location affects the prevalence of specific NTM, pathogens comprising the Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC) are the most common worldwide, followed by Mycobacterium abscessus and Mycobacterium kansasii2. In contrast to Mycobacterium tuberculosis, which has seen a reduction in disease rates in developed countries, the incidence and prevalence of NTM disease is increasing3,4. The rising incidence of NTM lung disease (NTM–LD) is attributed to a combination of factors, including increased awareness and improved diagnostic methods, environmental factors like water contamination, and a growing population with underlying health conditions that predispose them to NTM infection5. Diagnosis requires a combination of clinical, radiological, and microbiological criteria, and treatment regimens span at least the association of three to four drugs for at least 12 months. Treatment of NTM-LD is difficult and involves prolonged drug regimens that are associated with numerous potential side effects and costs6. Standard therapy for NTM-LD relies on a combination of agents such as macrolides, rifamycins, ethambutol, aminoglycosides, and fluoroquinolones7 Treatment usually involves long multi-drug regimens that can cause toxicity and intolerance, complicating treatment8. Adverse events —ranging from gastrointestinal disturbances, nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity and hepatotoxicity to myelosuppression and cardiac QTc prolongation (particularly with macrolides, fluoroquinolones, clofazimine, aminoglycosides), have been reported9,10. Serious adverse events (SAEs) are defined as adverse events resulting in death, a life-threatening situation, hospitalization, significant or persistent disability, or a congenital anomaly. In the context od NTM-LD, these events typically necessitate immediate cessation or modifciatio of the offending drug regimen11. Nearly all conventional antimycobacterial agents used to treat NTM-LD carry a risk of significant adverse events, potentially increasing patient morbidity and mortality, however, available data remain limited12. Managing adverse events (AEs) in an effective manner is important for supporting patient adherence and improving treatment outcomes9. The primary aim of this study was to retrospectively evaluate treatment outcome in a population of NTM-LD patients in a real-life context. Secondary aim was to describe AEs associated with NTM therapy regimens experienced by patients with NTM-LD at a tertiary referral hospital for Infectious Diseases in Italy, and to assess their influence on treatment outcome, by evaluating the safety and tolerability of long-term treatment.

Materials and methods

Study design

Recruitment

This was an observational retrospective cohort study of patients with NTM-LD, hospitalized and followed as outpatients between January 2016 and June 2023 at the National Institute for Infectious Diseases “L. Spallanzani” in Rome. Data were collected from the from the institutional database and retrospectively analysed. The inclusion criteria were as follows: age ≥ 18 years; diagnosis consistent with NTM-LD, according to diagnostic criteria based on the standards set by ATS/ERS/ESCMID/IDSA; antimycobacterial treatment for NTM infection for at least 1 consecutive month. Patients who met criteria for inclusion were only included once6.

Laboratory testing

Respiratory samples collected during diagnostic workup were subjected to smear examination and culture in Mycobacteria Growth Indicator Tubes (MGIT) and in Lowenstein–Jensen (LJ) medium. Molecular identification of NTM in primary samples or culture isolates was achieved by Real-time PCR (Anyplex MTB/NTM Real-time Detection kit, Seegene, Seoul, South Korea). Phenotypic drug-susceptibility testing (DST) procedures included testing by broth microdilution assay using the SLOMYCO and RAPMYCOI assays (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) for slow- and rapid-growing NTM species respectively. Isolates were grown on solid culture medium (Liofilchem, Roseto degli Abruzzi, Italy) prior to susceptibility testing. All cases in the study received a DST. Most of the isolated strains were subjected to phenotypic DST as previously described, but in cases the low viability of the mycobacteria did not allow a good result, thus the DST was carried out using the molecular method GenoType NTM-DR (NTM-DR; Hain Lifescience, Nehren, Germany) line probe assay (LPA). This method was used for M. avium complex and M. abscessus complex species to detect mutations linked with antibiotic resistance to macrolides and aminoglycosides.

Follow up

Patients were treated following the institutional protocol, based ATS/ERS/ESCMID/IDSA and BTS guidelines6,7. Regimen were individualized according to drug-susceptibility testing and adjusted for comorbidities. Monthly culture and sputum smear exams were conducted until the end of treatment. Treatment lasted almost 12 months after first negative culture results, extending till to 18–24 months according to clinical needs. The definition of treatment outcomes at the end of treatment, such as cure, treatment failure, death, unknown outcome, and treatment halted, were adopted according to NTM-NET 2018 consensus statement13. Patients were informed about treatment duration, possible side effects, and regimen details before therapy begins, with informed consent obtained prior to starting. During treatment, physicians and nurses provided ongoing education and support on managing side effects. Follow-up was conducted according to local protocol, based on World Health Organization guidelines11.

Medical counselling was given about potential adverse drug reactions and the importance of reporting any adverse event promptly. During monthly follow-up visits, they were asked about drug intolerance symptoms and adherence to treatment. Monthly evaluations of laboratory and clinical parameters included liver and renal function tests, blood count, ECG for QTc, eye examinations, while audiometry was performed as needed. Adverse drug reactions were classified according to the World health Organization (WHO) definition for antitubercular drugs, (AEs) and (SAEs)11. An AE is defined as any adverse medical event occurring during treatment. SAE is characterized as an AE that result in death, poses a life-threatening situation, necessitates hospitalization or extended hospital stay, causes significant disability, or a congenital anomaly, requiring intervention like stopping the suspect drug. An adverse drug reaction was identified by laboratory tests if any value was abnormal. For reactions not defined by laboratory tests, they were documented if a physician recorded them in the patient’s chart based on clinical criteria and patient-reported symptoms. Treatment discontinuation was defined as any discontinuation of an individual or multiple antimycobacterial drugs (i.e., any change in regimen drug selection) due to an adverse event, as documented from NTM medical record. Treatment modification refers to instances where one or more medications in the regimen were changes due to AEs occurrence.

Two trained researchers in data collection and medical record abstraction collected demographic and clinical data (such as age, gender, weight, medical history and treatment regimen, adverse events experienced during treatment, regimen changes or discontinuations, outcomes) through review of medical records.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were reported as median and interquartile range (IQR), categorical variables as absolute frequency and percentage. Association of SAE/AEs with antimycobacterial drugs was assessed using generalized linear models, (Poisson regression with robust error variance), risk ratios (RRs) with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. Univariate logistic regression was used to assess associations between key study variables and treatment outcomes classified as treatment failure/death versus cure. Finally, variables independently associated with treatment outcomes were selected using the stepwise method, with a forward selection procedure and setting the significance level for addition to the model at p < 0.2. All tests were two sided and p-values < 0.05 were considered significant. Statistical analyses were conducted using Stata (StataCorp. 2021. Stata Statistical Software: Release 17. College Station, TX: StataCorp LP).

Ethical statement

The study was approved by Ethics Committee of National Institute for Infectious Diseases “L. Spallanzani” IRCCS, with the decision n.12 (17th of February 2015) about data collection. All enrolled patients provided written informed consent to the use of anonymous clinical data for study purposes at the time of their hospital admission and at first visit in the outpatient clinic.

Discussion

We found that in our centre, the overall cure rate for NTM-LD patients, including those with M. abscessus, was 85%, which exceeds reported cure rates of 57–75%, highlighting a landscape marked by considerable variability and complexity8–10. A recent Norwegian study showed favourable response and culture conversion was in 53 of 72 (74%) patients (while a France study showed an overall, 62.9% (292/464) of patients treated. Conversely, an Italian study by Aliberti et coll. observed unsuccessful outcomes in 35.3% of cases, highlighting the high variability in treatment efficacy across different regions and patient population14–16. Most published American and Japanese studies reported cure rates for NTM-LD between 50 and 75%17–20. This considerable heterogeneity may be attributable to a variety of factors: differences in diagnostic criteria, variations in patient demographic comorbidities, the presence of drug-resistance strains (such as M.abscessus), and disparities in therapeutic regimens or clinical expertise.

While data obtained in our centre are encouraging, they necessitate an interpretation and raise important questions about the generalisability and drivers of success in NTM-LD management. Selection bias such as clinical characteristics, favourable microbiological profiles, could have conferred an increased likelihood of therapeutic success21.

Access to advanced diagnostic tools, multidisciplinary care teams, may play crucial roles in determining patient outcomes. There is urgent need for ongoing research to harmonize treatment strategies to address the individual needs of patients with NTM-LD22.

The study observed that 76.3% patients in the cohort (100/131) developed one or more treatment-related adverse events (Table 4). Adverse events were more common in patients treated for RGM (mainly M. abscessus).

Adverse drug reactions are very common during the treatment of NTM infections and are associated with treatment interruption or early treatment cessation23.

Although guidelines exist for the management of adverse events (AEs) and highlight the risk potential associated with drugs used to treat nontuberculous mycobacterial (NTM) infections6,7, there is a notable lack of comprehensive data regarding the true incidence of toxicities in patients receiving NTM treatment. Across published studies, up to 70% of all treated patients reported a treatment-related adverse event and 30–70% of patients receiving antimicrobial treatment permanently discontinue at least one drug in their initial regimen because of AEs25–28.

Notably the toxicity of NTM-LD treatment is mainly related to its long duration coupled with interactions between antimycobacterial drugs and other concomitant medications taken by patients29. Effective management of AEs necessitates an individualized approach, including adjustment of the antibiotic regimen, administration of symptomatic and supportive treatment, implementation of prophylactic measures, and discontinuation of the suspect drug, if warranted30. The spectrum of AEs documented in literature encompasses gastrointestinal disturbances, liver toxicity, ear toxicity, kidney damage, peripheral nerve damage, joint pain, muscle pain, skin manifestations and rash, blood disorders, and visual impairment. The incidence and severity of those events vary depending on the patient characteristics, and the treatment regimen needed31. In a recent paper from Ku and coll. 84.0% of patients on a macrolide-ethambutol-rifampin regimen and 90.6% of patients changed or discontinued their initial regimen, with AEs occurred more frequently in those assuming macrolides, aminoglycosides, and fluoroquinolones32. Macrolides often cause gastrointestinal and taste disturbances The prevalence of gastrointestinal adverse events reported in literature ranges from 12% to 60% among patients treated for NTM-LD with various regimens; 5% to 20% of the patients requiring treatment modifications or discontinuations due to these events33. Within our cohort gastrointestinal adverse events like nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and abdominal pain accounted for 33% of SAE/AEs. Three patients (2%) needed to halt treatment because of these symptoms and 14 (11%) needed regimen modification.

Mild rifampicin-associated, transaminases increase generally require only monitoring; most guidelines recommend interrupting treatment or switching to a rifamycin-free regimen for severe hepatotoxicity (AST/ALT > 5x upper normal)34. The serum aspartate aminotransaminase and/or alanine aspartate aminotransaminase levels (AST/ALT) increased the normal upper limit (40 UI/mL) in 18 patient (13.7), with 6 patients experiencing SAE requiring treatment change.

Ethambutol, a key agent in NTM-LD regimens, is known to induce optic neuropathy by interfering with the mitochondrial function of the optic nerve cells, the risk depends also on dose and duration of ethambutol therapy, as well as the patient’s age, renal function, and genetic susceptibility; fortunately, visual disturbances are often reversible if ethambutol is discontinued promptly35. Kwon and coll. found that 11.8% of NTM-LD patients discontinued ethambutol due to AEs36. Ocular toxicity risk rises with prolonged drug exposure, often occurring after 6 months of therapy. In our cohort the median time to onset of visual disturbances was 200 days (IQR 180–250). Regular monitoring visual of visual acuity and colour discrimination at baseline, then monthly, and whenever symptoms arise is essential, and discontinuation of the drug appears to be the sole effective intervention35.

Severe haematologic AEs were rare in our cohort (34/229 SAE/AEs; 1 SAE causing suspension; 4 SAEs causing modification, 29 AEs), likely due to consistent blood tests monitoring and appropriatemanagement. Leukopenia occurred in 11% of patients. Rifamycins are well known to cause bone marrow suppression and haemolysis, leading to leukopenia and anaemia. Clarithromycin can also cause reversible leukopenia, especially in combination with other drugs that affect the bone marrow25. Anaemia was documented in 8 (6%) patients in our study. Anaemia can affect the quality of life and functional status of patients with NTM-LD5,6. Thrombocytopenia was observed in 12 patients (9%), all of whom received rifampicin and ethambutol; This effect typically arises a few days or weeks of treatment initiation and usually resolves with discontinuation of the causative drug. Ototoxicity was reported in 19 patients. Parenteral amikacin, recommended as a component of treatment regimens early in the therapy of M. abscessus, severe fibrocavitary disease from M. intracellulare and second line treatment in case of infection by M. kansasii, has well documented nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity and vestibulotoxicity. Otoxicity rates with aminoglycosides across studies range from from 32% to 90%36,37. Notably, aminoglycosides-induced ototoxicity is permanent. Prevention hinges careful monitoring of serum drug levels, renal function and routine hearing assessment before, during, and after therapy. Permanent hearing loss or balance disorders caused by ototoxic drugs may have serious consequences for communication, educational, and social integration. Therefore, the benefits of ototoxic drugs must be weighed against the potential risks, and alternative medications should be considered when appropriate5,6. QTc prolongation, indicating delayed ventricular repolarization that can predispose patients to arrhythmias like Torsade de pointes, is a known AE from medications like macrolides (e.g., clarithromycin) and fluoroquinolones38. Comprehensive monitoring identified 4 significant QTc prolongation events (Two patients had cardiovascular pathologies) in our cohort, leading to suspension of levofloxacin in one case. These findings suggest that proper management and regular ECG monitoring, can minimize severe cardiac arrhythmias39.

Older age was the only demographic feature significantly associated with increased risk of adverse events (Table 5). Neither sex, comorbidities nor other demographic characteristics significantly correlated with adverse events occurrence. In the population who underwent the study, age appear to play crucial role in treatment outcome and AEs incidence40. Older NTM-LD patients face higher risk of SAEs and AEs and poor long-term outcomes, highlighting the importance of close monitoring29. Recognising age as an influential factor highlights the importance of closely monitoring and tailoring therapeutic approaches for different age groups to enhance both safety and efficacy in the management of NTM-PD.

Further large studies are warranted to clarify these associations and improve treatment strategies. While results in the study provides information on managing patients with NTM-LD, it has several limitations, including its retrospective nature and small sample of patients enrolled.

Our study, while informative, has limitations. Notably its retrospective design and relatively small sample size. These constraints may limit the generalizability of our findings. Future prospective, multicentre studies with larger cohorts are necessary to further clarify risk profiles, optimize management strategies, and improve patient outcomes.

Conclusions

The findings of this study confirms that adverse events of antimycobacterial treatment are a common challenge among patients with NTM-LD. However, we showed that despite the high frequency of adverse events, most patients were able to complete treatment with management and monitoring based on guidelines recommendations. Adverse events undoubtedly have a negative impact on the quality of life and patient satisfaction, as well as on the cost-effectiveness of treatment. Therefore, it is crucial to identify factors that can predict and prevent AEs, if a good treatment outcome is to be achieved. Furthermore, it is essential to adopt a patient-centred clinical-therapeutic approach, in which access to information, counselling and the best antimycobacterial drugs are guaranteed, to achieve optimal adherence to the treatment regimen. In this healthcare context, active research of AEs prevents complications caused by AEs, educates patients on early recognition and reassures them on the quality of care. A multidisciplinary approach involving specialists in infectious diseases, pulmonology, pharmacology, and patient education is essential for comprehensive management of NTM-LD. This approach can improve patient adherence, help to monitor adverse effects, and provide holistic care that addresses the physical, psychological, and social aspects of the disease. Future studies should evaluate early incorporation of more favourable agents into NTM treatment selection and subsequent impact on patient outcomes.

An analytical model for the remote epitaxial potential
Jason K. Kawasaki, Quinn T. Campbell

Abstract
We propose an analytical model for the remote bonding potential of the substrate that permeates through graphene during remote epitaxy. Our model, based on a Morse interatomic potential, includes the attenuation due to (1) the increased separation between film and substrate and (2) free carrier screening from graphene. Compared with previous slab density functional theory calculations, which use the electrostatic potential as a proxy for bonding, our analytical model provides a more direct description of bonding, explicitly includes screening (which is often ignored), and is based on simple, interpretable, and well benchmarked parameters. We show that the magnitude of | ϕ r ​ e ​ m ​ o ​ t ​ e | |\phi_{remote}| for typical semiconductor and oxide substrates is few meV, similar to the van der Waals potential of graphene. This suggests interference between the graphene and remote substrate potentials, plus interfacial reconstructions, must be considered when interpreting experiments on remote epitaxy. We use our model to interpret previous experiments from the remote epitaxy and related literature, highlighting connections to moire epitaxy and to the ordering of graphite intercalation compounds. Our model also points to tests, based on tunable screening and spatial extent of the substrate potential, that may increase the strength of the remote potential towards the more idealized picture.



Bonding is typically considered a very short-range interaction (
\lessapprox
3 Å). Atomic and molecular orbitals (wavefunctions) decay exponentially with distance, and thus nearest neighbor interactions tend to dominate. Remote epitaxy challenges this paradigm. Here it is argued that the lattice potential of a crystalline substrate can permeate through graphene with sufficient strength to template epitaxy of a film on top of a graphene-covered substrate
Kim et al. 2017
;
Kong et al. 2018
. In this picture, the graphene is nearly transparent
Rafiee et al. 2012
: the film adopts epitaxial registry to the buried substrate rather than graphene, and the lattice potential of the graphene itself is assumed to contribute minimally. The graphene spacer merely attenuates the film-substrate interaction strength. This concept is quite useful: due to the weaker film-substrate interactions, films grown on graphene can be exfoliated to produce flexible, stackable, and strain engineered
LaDuca et al. 2024
;
Du et al. 2021
membranes. The weakened interactions may also enable lattice mismatched heteroepitaxy of films with decreased dislocation density due to the “slippery” graphene interface.

The current theoretical understanding of remote epitaxy is based on the density functional theory (DFT)-computed electrostatic potential decay with distance
Kong et al. 2018
;
Dai et al. 2022
;
Qu et al. 2022
;
Jiang et al. 2019
, which typically ignore interfacial reconstructions and assumes all the atoms are near their bulk like positions (presumably to minimize computational cost). These calculations suggest that substrates with greater bond polarity produce larger electrostatic fluctuations above graphene, and thus can support remote epitaxy through greater number of graphene layers
Kong et al. 2018
.

However, there are several challenges to this understanding. First, it is not clear that a purely electrostatic interaction can explain remote epitaxy, since the electrostatic potential describes the electronic workfunction rather than bonding (i.e., the energy of pulling an electron to infinity from that location). Electrostatic potentials are thus completely agnostic to the chemical match between the film and substrate, which has been shown to impact remote epitaxy efficacy
Chang et al. 2023
. Second, most DFT treatments ignore free carrier screening from graphene, ignore the lattice potential of graphene, and ignore possible reconstructions. Finally, DFT calculations can be a black box, making it difficult to interpret the contributions from various terms without additional tests. A simple analytical model is needed in order to build a physical understanding of the various terms that contribute to the strength of the remote potential.

Here we develop an analytical model for the remote bonding potential permeation through graphene. Our model is based on an empirical Morse interatomic potential
Morse 1929
, which includes contributions from both covalent bonding and electrostatic interactions
Mirzanejad and
Varganov 2025
. This simple two body potential captures the essential of physics and employs easy to understand parameters that are well benchmarked by experiments, density functional theory, and molecular dynamics simulations
Keyes 1975
. Compared to previous approaches that used the electrostatic potential, our approach (1) uses a potential that includes both covalent bonding and electrostatic interactions
Mirzanejad and
Varganov 2025
, (2) explicitly includes free carrier screening of graphene, which is often ignored
Dai et al. 2022
;
Kim et al. 2017
;
Jiang et al. 2019
, (3) avoids the use of an adjustable scale factor that has been invoked to make the electrostatic potential behave more like a bonding potential
Kong et al. 2018
, and (4) avoids the use of complicated background subtraction and data cropping
Kong et al. 2018
.
We show that graphene is not as transparent as often assumed: the remote potential of the substrate through graphene has an order of magnitude few meV or smaller, similar to the the vdW interaction strength of graphene itself. Our model predicts that the spatial extent of the bond potential, rather than bond ionicity, is the primary parameter that controls the strength of the remote potential.

Our model treats graphene in ideal limit as a homogeneous electron gas. The key elements are (1) increased separation between film and substrate due to insertion of the graphene and (2) attenuation due to free carrier screening.

Our model calls for a re-examination of Si and other substrates with small
a
, coupled with a systematic understanding of how synthesis conditions and graphene surface preparation affect the resulting films and their crystalline orientations. The model suggests that the best substrates for remote epitaxy are those with spatially extended bond potentials (or spatially extended orbitals), rather than substrates with more ionic bonding character. Our calculations also suggest that tuning the screening via gating the graphene may be a powerful route to tune the strength of the remote potential.

We caution that our model should only be considered an order of magnitude estimate for the remote potential. We treat graphene as an idealized homogeneous electron gas, and only consider pair-wise atom-atom interactions between film and substrate. Our model does not consider the directional character of bonding, multi-atom interactions, or bonding between the film and graphene or graphene and the substrate. It also does not consider surface reconstructions. Still, it identifies an essential role for the decay with distance and for screening. These essential parameters place significant constraints on the magnitude of the remote bonding potential that are overlooked in the current atomistic calculations. Experimental techniques such as He atom scattering
Boato et al. 1979
and force-distance spectra by atomic force microscopy
Ke et al. 1999
;
Hoffmann et al. 2001
;
Gießibl et al. 2001
;
Allain et al. 2017
, as a function of graphene gating, could directly measure the remote potential as a function of screening to test our predictions.

Given the relatively weak remote potential, it is worth considering other explanations for remote epitaxy, beyond the simple picture of transparent graphene. For the bulk graphite intercalations compounds, the intercalants form ordered superlattices that can be commensurate or incommensurate to the graphite lattice
Dresselhaus and
Dresselhaus 1981
. This intercalant ordering is thought to be governed by several factors, including (1) Coulomb repulsion between intercalants, since there is charge transfer between the intercalants (often alkali metals) and the graphite sheets, and (2) strain fields in the graphite sheets induced by bending around the intercalants
Safran 1987
.
These factors may also be relevant to remote epitaxy. The former charge transfer is not a remote interaction per se, but hints at a possible extended molecular orbital picture that includes atomic orbitals from the film and substrate, hybridized with the extended
\pi
system of graphene. Such a description is not included in our model. The second factor, strain and buckling in graphite, suggests that surface reconstructions may be relevant to epitaxy on graphene-covered substrates. Indeed, strong buckling is observed in the “buffer” layer of graphene that forms on Si-face SiC (0001)
Conrad et al. 2017
. This distorted graphite has a mix of
sp^{2}
and
sp^{3}
bonds. The out of plane dangling bonds are expected produce bonding potential fluctuations of order 100 meV to eV (since they are covalent bonds), larger than the few meV scale remote and vdW potentials. Indeed, recent experiments demonstrate the adatom sticking coefficient is higher on buffer graphene than on epitaxial graphene
Poon et al. 2010
, implying higher reactivity due to these dangling bonds. These reconstructions and moire interference with the graphene are known to template the ordering of metal adatoms on graphene/Ir (111)
N’Diaye et al. 2006
, graphene/Rh (111)
Jiménez-Sánchez
et al. 2021
, graphene/Pt (111)
Merino et al. 2011
, graphene/SiC (0001)
Song et al. 2012
. Atomistic calculations that include these reconstructions as well as direct chemical interaction between the film and substrate are required to fully understand remote epitaxy of continuous films on graphene.

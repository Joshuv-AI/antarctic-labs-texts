Narrative review—the emerging role, functions, and mechanisms of UFMylation in cancers
Authors: Lu Fang, Fan Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectiveUFM1 system, i.e., UFMylation, is a newly identified post-translational modification (PTM). Increasing evidence have indicated that defects of UFMylation are implicated in multiple pathological conditions including cancers. Through this narrative review, we aim to provide a comprehensive view for development of therapeutic targets and strategies.MethodsBased on PubMed database, thorough literature research was performed to finish this narrative review. The research strategy was as follows: (ufmylation) AND ((cancer) OR (neoplasm) OR (tumor)).Key Content and FindingsWe first summarize recently identified substrates of UFMylation. Next, we focus on and discuss reported molecular mechanisms of UFMylation in tumorigenesis and cancer treatment responses and resistance. In addition, we discuss the role and function of UFMylation in various cancers, mainly in solid tumors. Lastly, we discuss the possible crosstalk between UFMylation and ubiquitination.ConclusionsIn the past decades, emerging evidence have shown that UFMylation is crucial for organ development, homeostasis maintenance, inflammation, immunity and metabolism. In addition, the defects of UFMylation, including genetic variants, abnormal expression pattern and somatic copy number alterations, have been reported involved in tumorigenesis, progression and treatment response. With in-depth investigation and research, enzymatic cascades and substrates of UFMylation will be unveiled. Promisingly, UFMylation may be utilized as a biomarker for diagnosis, treatment response assessment, and therapeutic targets in cancer therapy.Keywords: UFMylation, cancer, molecular mechanism, therapeutic target

Introduction

Post-translational modifications (PTMs) refer to the chemical alterations that occur on protein molecules during or after synthesis, involving modifications of amino acid residues (1). PTMs regulate various biological processes and dysregulation of PTMs can lead to a variety of diseases, such as inflammatory disorders and tumorigenesis (2). As a well-known PTM, ubiquitination refers to the covalent binding of ubiquitin (Ub) to target proteins, which is mediated by a three-step cascade process requiring the synergistic action of three ubiquitinating enzymes (3). Similar to Ub, Ub-like proteins (UBLs) can covalently bind to target proteins through enzymatic reactions called UBLylation, such as the Ub-fold modifier 1 (UFM1) and UFM1 system, i.e., UFMylation (4,5).

Before initiation of UFMylation, UFM1 is translated as a precursor status (pro-UFM1). Mature UFM1 is formed after the removal of its C-terminal serine and cysteine by specific proteases UFSP1 and UFSP2, exposing the glycine residues which is essential for subsequent conjugation (6,7). Similarly, UFMylation consists of the dynamic three-step enzymatic reaction (8). Briefly, the mature UFM1 is activated by E1 activating enzyme UBA5, initiating the UFMylation. Then the activated UFM1 is transferred to the E2 conjugating enzyme UFC1. Lastly, UFM1 is transferred to the specific target protein mediated by E3 ligase enzyme UFL1, completing the UFMylation process (9) (Figure 1, Table 1). Recently, the satellite components DDRGK domain-containing protein 1 (DDRGK1) and Cyclin-dependent kinase 5 regulatory subunit-associated protein 3 (CDK5RAP3) are demonstrated as crucial regulators or adaptors of the UFMylation, rather than merely substrates (17,18). In the past decades, emerging evidence have shown that UFMylation is crucial for organ development and homeostasis (62). Moreover, it is pointed out that UFMylation also plays an important role in multiple biological processes such as stress responses and signaling pathway, inflammation and immunity, and fatty acid metabolism (45,63,64). Given its essential role, it is not surprising that defects in UFMylation are closely related with various pathological conditions including cancers. To date, the enzymatic cascades, substrates and underlying molecular mechanisms of UFMylation in tumorigenesis remain largely unveiled and require in-depth investigation.

Based on PubMed database, we finished this narrative review with following searching strategy: (ufmylation) AND ((cancer) OR (neoplasm) OR (tumor)). We reviewed the role and function of UFMylation in cancers. Additionally, we focused on and summarized the potential molecular mechanisms of UFMylation involved in tumorigenesis, immune responses and treatment resistance, aiming to support the development of UFMylation-based potential applications in cancer therapy. We present this article in accordance with the Narrative Review reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1110/rc).

Methods

We queried PubMed for studies published up to March 2026, using a set of selected search terms (UFMylation; Cancer; Neoplasm; Tumor). We also searched conference abstracts and hand searched reference lists of relevant papers to provide the most updated and comprehensive review of the topic as possible. Finally, we also searched “ClinicalTrials.gov” for clinical and investigational studies. Additional studies were searched in July of 2026 using the same search strategies, for revisions to the paper, and to add updated information from recently published studies if available. The underlying mechanisms of UFMylation in cancer

UFMylation has been reported to play a crucial role in various cellular processes such as DNA damage, protein translation and ER homeostasis. In this section, we summarized the mechanisms underlying the role of UFMylation in multiple cancers based on published data.

DNA damage

Endogenous or exogenous stimuli, such as ultraviolet light radiation and carcinogens, can cause the occurrence of DNA damage which may induce cell senescence through DNA damage response (DDR) pathways like tumor suppressor p53 pathway (65). Proper DDR is essential to maintain genome integrity and prevent tumorigenesis.

DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) are the most toxic type of DNA lesions (66). The repair of the DSB lesion is orchestrated by ataxia-telangiectasia mutated (ATM) kinase, whose activation is mediated via the MRE11-RAD50-NBS1 (MRN) complex. UFMylation of MRE11 on K282 was required for the MRN complex formation under unperturbed conditions and DSB-induced optimal ATM activation, homologous recombination-mediated repair and genome integrity. A pathogenic mutation of MRE11 on G285C was identified in uterine endometrioid carcinoma, exhibiting a similar cellular phenotype to dysregulated UFMylation caused by K282R mutation of MRE11 (57). MRE11 UFMylation was also reported essential for telomere length maintenance and hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) survival. And HeLa cells showed instability of telomeres replicated by leading-strand synthesis after UFL1-konckout (67). In addition, it was reported that UFL1 was important for ATM activation. After recruited to DSBs by the MRE11/RAD50/NBS1 complex, UFL1 mediated the mono-UFMylation of histone H4 following DNA damage, which was important for Suv39h1 and Tip60 recruitment and further ATM activation. In turn, the activated ATM phosphorylated UFL1 and enhanced its E3 ligase activity, promoting ATM activation in a positive feedback loop (54). Qin et al. revealed that hippoSTK38 served as a reader for H4 UFMylation to promote ATM activation in a kinase-independent manner. By recognizing ufmylated H4, STK38 recruited the Suv39h1 to the DSBs, resulting in H3K9 trimethylation and Tip60 activation to promote ATM activation (68).

Intriguingly, p53 was reported to be covalently modified by UFMylation, which stabilized p53 by antagonizing its ubiquitination and subsequent proteasome degradation. Disrupting the UFMylation of p53, by depletion of either the E3 ligase UFL1 or the critical regulator DDRGK1, decreased p53 stability and promoted cell growth and tumor formation (52).

The highly repetitive and transcription frequency make ribosomal DNA (rDNA) genes exceedingly susceptible to genotoxic stress and DSBs. Instability of rDNA has been observed in multiple cancers (69). Induction of DSBs in rDNA repeats is associated with ATM-dependent rDNA silencing and nucleolar reorganization where rDNA is segregated into nucleolar caps, yet the regulatory mechanisms remain elusive. Panichnantakul et al. revealed that UFL1 and its binding partner DDRGK1 localized to nucleolar caps upon rDNA damage. UFL1 loss impaired ATM activation and rDNA transcriptional silencing, leading to reduced segregation (70).

S-phase checkpoint, mediated by the ATR-CHK1 pathway, is evolved to detect and repair DNA damage that occurs during replication, maintain genome integrity upon replication stalling at damaged template strands (14). PARP1 acts as a sensor of replication stress and is required for CHK1 activation, while how the activity of PARP1 is regulated remains unclear. Gong et al. revealed that UFMylation of PARP1 at K548 was required for the efficient activation of CHK1. Inactivation of UFL1 delayed CHK1 activation and inhibited nascent DNA degradation during replication blockage as seen in PARP1-deficient cells. Consistently, defective PARP1 UFMylation compromised CHK1 activation, the restart of stalled replication forks following replication blockage, chromosome stability, and excessive nascent DNA degradation at stalled replication forks (71). Eg5, a motor protein crucial for mitotic spindle assembly and maintenance, was identified as a novel substrate for UFMylation at Lys564. UFMylation affected the mono-ubiquitination of Eg5, and defective UFMylation led to diminished spindle localization of Eg5 at the centrosome and spindle apparatus. It is observed that HeLa cells with UFMylation-defective mutation of Eg5 exhibited shorter spindles, metaphase arrest, spindle checkpoint activation, and a failure of cell division (61).

ER metabolism

Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) played a key role in the maintenance of protein homeostasis. Accumulation of unfolded proteins in the ER lumen leads to ER stress, and the unfolded protein response (UPR) of the ER is initiated to buffer ER stress which orchestrate cell fate to either survival or death (72). The onset of UPR relies on UPR pathways: inositol-requiring transmembrane kinase/endonuclease 1 (IRE1), PKR-like ER protein kinase (PERK), and activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6). UFMylation pathway is upregulated in response to pharmacological ER stress. In addition, UFMylation is reported implicated in processes such as ER-associated protein degradation (ERAD), ribosome-associated protein quality control at the ER and ER-phagy.

ER stress

Disturbance of ER homeostasis induces ER stress and results in activation of the UPR. Liu et al. showed that loss of DDRGK1, the critical component of the UFMylation, induced ER stress and enhanced ER stress-induced apoptosis in both cancer cells and HSCs. By targeting IRE1α, loss of DDRGK1 repressed IRE1α-XBP1 signaling and activated the PERK-eIF2α-CHOP apoptotic pathway. Further biological experiments demonstrated that DDRGK1 regulated IRE1α protein stability in an UFMylation modification dependent way (18). Similarly, DDRGK1 stabilized IRE1α by inhibiting ubiquitylation-mediated degradation. Loss of DDRGK1 decreased the UFMylation and subsequently increased ubiquitylation-mediated IRE1α degradation, leading to ER dysfunction and activation of the PERK/CHOP/Caspase3 apoptosis pathway (23). The IRE1α/XBP1 axis upregulated the expression of UFBP1, a target and cofactor of UFMylation, promoting plasma cell development by suppressing the activation of PERK. Deficiency of UFBP1 impaired ER expansion (21).

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is characterized by lipid accumulation of and ER stress. Increased expression of UFM1-conjugated proteins and UFMylation pathway was observed in both NAFLD patients and experimental models. Upregulated UFMylation was observed on hepatic proteins in response to hepatic ER stress in NAFLD. UFMyaltion of UFBP1 ameliorated obesity, hepatic steatosis and hepatic lipogenesis induced by a high fat diet (HFD). Reintroduction or overexpression of UFBP1 inhibited UPR pathways (PERK1, IRE1α, ATF6), alleviating ER stress in UFMylation-dependent manner in NAFLD (20).

Resistance to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKI) involves persistence of a drug-tolerant subpopulation of cancer cells that survive despite effective eradication of the majority of the cell population. Unexpectedly, nearly every component of the UFMylation pathway were identified as the synergy suppressor based on a genome-wide CRISPR/Cas9 enhancer/suppressor screen in EGFR-dependent lung cancer PC9 cells. Loss of UFMylation triggered a protective UPR associated with STING upregulation rather than canonical EGFR signaling, promoting pro-tumorigenic inflammatory signaling but also uniquely dependent on Bcl-xL (73).

Translocon clogging at the ER triggered ribosome UFMylation, which enables the export of stalled substrates from the ER for lysosomal degradation, suggesting a translocation-associated quality control (TAQC) mechanism that distinct from the ERAD or ribosome associated quality control (RQC) (74,75). Wang et al. reconstructed a “translocon-declogging” system centered around the UFMylation system, ribosome and the Sec61 translocon. Notably, SAYSD1 was identified to facilitate TAQC by binding UFMylated ribosome in a bipartite manner. Similar to UFM1 deficiency, loss of SAYSD1 caused the accumulation of translocation-stalled proteins at the ER and triggered ER stress (76).

ER-phagy

Recent studies have suggested that UFMylation is involved in selective ER autophagy (ER-phagy) (62). Liang et al. unexpectedly revealed that ER-localized UFMylation was essentially required for ER-phagy to repress the UPR via IRE1α. DDRGK1, which worked as the adaptor rather than UFMylated substrate, brought UFL1 to the ER surface to UFMylate RPN1 and RPL26 and preferentially targeted ER sheets for degradation (62). NADH-cytochrome b5 reductase 3 (CYB5R3) was identified as a UFM1 substrate that localized on the ER. The UFL1 and UFBP1 dependent UFMylation converted CYB5R3 into its inactive form and then degraded in lysosomes, which depended on the autophagy-related protein Atg7 and the autophagy-adaptor protein CDK5RAP3 (31).

Autophagy

Emerging evidence have indicated that UFMylation is correlated with autophagy while the exact mechanism remains to be explored. Cao et al. showed that DDRGK1 deficiency in mouse embryonic fibroblast (MEF) cell lines resulted in the accumulation of autophagosomes which had a dual effect on autophagy. The accumulation impaired mTOR signaling and promoted autophagy induction. Meanwhile, it inhibited autophagosome-lysosome fusion and led to subsequent autophagy degradation. The dual effect of DDRGK1 ultimately aggravated the MEFs apoptosis. These findings provided a theoretical basis for the treatment strategies of cancers caused by DDRGK1 (77). Stephani et al. identified that C53 sensed proteotoxic stress by forming C53/UFL1/DDRGK1 receptor complex, thus bridging selective autophagy with ribosome-associated quality control in the ER (33).

Defective cellular quality control has been linked to tumorigenesis (22). Despite the reveal of interconnected pathways that mediate protein and organelle degradation, the underlying regulatory mechanisms that keep them switched off under normal conditions remain largely unknown. Stalled translation triggers the UFMylation of ER-bound ribosomes and activates C53-mediated autophagy to clear toxic polypeptides. Biochemical assays showed that the unicellular alga Chlamydomonas reinhardtii has a functional UFMylation pathway, refuting the assumption that UFMylation is linked to multicellularity. All these findings revealed the ancestral role of shuffled ATG8-interacting motifs in UFMylation-dependent fine-tuning of C53-mediated autophagy activation (78).

Autophagy initiation is determined by phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase (Ptdlns3K) complex, which is regulated by BECN1. VCP/p97 is implicated in numerous biological processes including autophagy initiation (79). Notably, multiple mutations of VCP/p97 have been identified in various cancers, indicating its potential value as a therapeutic target in tumorigenesis (80,81). It was reported that UFL1 mediated the UFMylation of VCP/p97 on K109, which promoted BECN1 stabilization and assembly of the Ptdlns3K complex. Depletion or UFMylation-defective mutation of VCP/p97 led to increase in MAP1LC3B/LC3B protein expression, which could be rescued by re-expression of VCP/p97 (36).

Immune response

Immune cells can recognize and eliminate cancer cells through antigen-directed cytotoxicity, of which T cells play a crucial role. The expression of inhibitory immune checkpoint receptors on T cells, such as programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1/PDCD1/CD279) and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4 (CTLA-4/CD152), can regulate the T cell activation and cytotoxicity (82,83). Currently, T cell-based immune checkpoints blockade (ICB), i.e., immunotherapies, have been widely applied for cancer patients in clinic and brought clinical benefits. However, the population exhibiting treatment response is limited and resistance to treatment is observed. It is demonstrated that ubiquitination can modify the stability of PD-1 protein for its degradation via the proteasome system (38,84). Intriguingly, UFL1 promoted PD-1 UFMylation to antagonize its ubiquitination and subsequent degradation. UFL1 ablation destabilized PD-1 and enhanced CD8+ T cell activation in vitro, and conditional knockout (cKO) of UFL1 in T cells effectively controlled tumor growth in vivo (45). Similarly, the protein stability of programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) could be UFMylation-regulated. Zhou et al. reported that disruption of PD-L1 UFMylation stabilized the PD-L1 in multiple cancers, undermining antitumor immunity (44).

Activated macrophages, by interferon-γ in combination with other proinflammatory stimuli, respond with transcriptional and cellular changes that enhance clearance of intracellular pathogens at the risk of damaging tissues. Imbalanced interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) therefore could result in immunopathology. Based on a genome-wide CRISPR knockout screen, the UFM1 conjugation system was unexpectedly found inhibiting responses to IFN-γ and lipopolysaccharide. In UFMylation-deficient cells, enhancement of IFN-γ activation led to increased transcriptional responses to IFN-γ, which was dependent on ER stress responses involving Ern1 and Xbp1, identifying a molecular link between the UFMylation pathway and immune responses (56). Encountered with cognate antigen, naïve B cells proliferate and differentiate into antibody-secreting cells (ASCs) including plasmablasts and plasma cells. Zhu et al. showed that UFBP1 distinctly regulated different branches of UPR pathway to promote plasma cell development and function. UFBP1-mediated suppression of PERK was essential in the differentiation of naïve B cells into plasma cells, while UFBP1deficiency retarded immunoglobulin production (21).

The RNA-binding protein RIG-I is a key initiator of the innate immune response. UFMylation positively regulated RIG-I recruitment to its adaptor mitochondrial antiviral signaling protein (MAVS), promoting IFN induction in response to RIG-I activation. Mechanistically, UFL1 was recruited to and formed a complex with the membrane-targeting protein 14–3-3ε, which was then recruited to activated RIG-I to promoting downstream innate immune signaling (49). Stimulator of interferon genes (STING) is the key adaptor of IFN regulatory factor 3 (IRF3) and nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) signal pathways, playing a crucial role in effective innate immune responses (85). To date, little is known about how STING is maintained. The UFM1-specific ligase UFL1 maintained STING expression and promoted innate immune response. Mechanistically, by inhibiting the interaction between TRIM29 and STING, UFL1 reduced its ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation (86).

Ferroptosis is a type of programmed cell death characterized by altered oxidative homeostasis and excessive lipid peroxidation (87). Controversially, stimulation of ferroptosis can exert dual effects for different types of cancers, which may be resulted from variable characteristics of the tumor immune microenvironment (88,89). Emerging evidence indicates that UFMylation is implicated in the regulation of ferroptosis (46). It was reported that the inhibition of macrophage-capping protein (MCP) suppressed the UFMylation of pirin (PIR), thus decreasing the transcription of GPX4 and promoting the cytoplasmic transportation of HMGB1. These triggered ferroptosis and altered release pattern of HMGB1, facilitating the pro-inflammatory M1-like polarization of macrophages (40). Generally, these findings supported that molecular link between UFMylation and immune responses.

The role and function of UFMylation in cancers

As UFMylation has been reported deeply implicated in multiple cellular processes, such as DDR, ER stress response and cell division. It is unsurprising that there is increasing evidence suggesting that dysregulation of UFMylation play a crucial role in multiple cancers. Although the role and underlying mechanisms of UFMylation in tumorigenesis has not been systematically unveiled, it is promising that restoration and maintenance of UFMylation homeostasis may serve as a potential therapeutic strategy. The function of UFMylation in cancers

Breast cancer

Based on the expression pattern of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR) and Ki67, breast cancers are classified to following categories: (I) Luminal A subtype, accounting for 40–60% of all cases; (II) Luminal B subtype, accounting for 8%; (III) HER2 positive subtype, accounting for 15–20%; (IV) triple-negative subtype (TNBC), accounting for 20%. Increasing evidence suggest that UFMylation play an important role in breast cancer, including its cascades enzymes and substrates.

Breast cancer 1/2 (BRCA1/2) mutations can lead to homologous-recombination (HR) deficiency, conferring synthetic lethality with poly-(ADP)-ribose polymerase 1/2 inhibitors (PARPi). Tan et al. found that replication stress could induce PITP UFMylation in BRCA1-deficient cells, promoting end resection and degradation of nascent DNA at stalled replication forks. Comparing to control BRCA1-deficient cells, PTIP-depleted and UFL1-depleted BRCA1-knockdown cells displayed less sensitivity to PARPi (38). Tian et al. found the E3 ligase UFL1 catalyzed the UFMylation of PTIP at lysine 148. This modification facilitated the assembly of the PTIP-MLL3/4 complex, resulting in the enrichment of H3K4me1 and H3K4me3 at stalled forks and subsequent recruitment of the MRE11 nuclease. Loss of UFL1 or disruption of PTIP UFMylation protected nascent DNA strands from extensive degradation and conferred resistance to PARPi. These findings suggested that UFL1 was a pivotal regulator of stalled fork stability and response to PARPi in BRCA1/2-deficient cells, offering potential therapeutic avenues for the treatment of BRCA1/2-deficient tumors (39). UFL1 was reported to promote breast tumor growth by remodeling lipid metabolism. Mechanistically, UFL1 interacted with and UFMylated AKT, thereby increasing AKT-mediated lipid synthesis. UFL1-AKT axis could be a potential therapeutic target in breast cancer (117).

The nuclear receptor α (ERα) played an important role in breast cancer tumorigenesis. It’s observed that aberrant activation of ERα drove progression in nearly 70% of breast cancers, and its expression level and subcellular localization controlled the sensitivity to hormone response. Recent studies have reported that UFMylation is implicated in the regulation of ERα. The ERα binding partner activating signal co-integrator 1 (ASC1, also known as TRIP4) serves as a scaffold for the binding of nuclear receptors, transcriptional coactivators, and general transcriptional machinery. Yoo et al. reported that the UFMylation of ASC1 was essential for breast tumorigenesis (55). Poly-ufmylated ASC1 served as a scaffold that recruited coactivators to the promoter regions of ERα target genes. In addition, UFMylation of ERα increased its protein stability and downstream gene transactivation, such as pS2 and c-Myc (48). Together, these findings suggest that UFMylation machinery may represent potential therapeutic strategy for ERα-positive breast cancer. Yang et al. identified SLC7A11 as a novel UFMylation substrate and UFM1/SLC7A11 pathway could be a promising therapeutic target for breast cancer (50). Mao et al. identified that UFMylation of PLAC8 maintained its protein stability, which facilitated the cancer cell proliferation and ensured the immune-suppressive PLAC8/PD-L1 axis in TNBC (46). Yang et al. demonstrated that AKT1 derectly interacted with UFL1and undergoes UFMylation, promoting tumor growth and chemoresistance in TNBC. Disrupting the UFL1-AKT interaction significantly inhibited TNBC progression and targeting this axis clud offer a promising therapeutic strategy (118).

Lung cancer

Lung cancer is still the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide. In the past decade, the incidence of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) has notably increased. Despite the development of therapeutic strategies, significant challenges still remain such as cisplatin resistance in LUAD. Xu et al. reported that UFMylation E1 enzyme UBA5 played a tumor-promoting role in LUAD, whose expression level was positively correlated with tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stages and poor outcomes. UBA5 facilitated cancer cell proliferation and cisplatin resistance in LUAD. Immunotherapy is an effective cancer treatment strategy drawing increasing attention. Tumor associated macrophages (TAMs) are the main infiltrating immune cells in cancers. Intriguingly, UBA5 could promote the M2-polarization of macrophage and lactate production, facilitating the shape of the suppressive tumor immune microenvironment and resulting in high tumor mutational burden, microsatellite instability, inhibited neoantigen presentation, and homologous recombination deficiency in LUAD (109).

The development of TKI shed light on EGFR mutant LUAD, however, acquired resistance severely limited its durable clinical benefit, requiring a better understanding and strategies to overcome the drug tolerant persister (DTP) populations in LUAD (119,120). Terai et al. claimed that the dysregulation of UFMylation and ER stress comprised an unrecognized TKI drug tolerance pathway. Absence of this pathway rather than loss of UFMylation triggered a protective UPR associated with STING upregulation, promoting pro-tumorigenic inflammatory signaling but also unique dependence on Bcl-xL (73). These findings revealed a novel function of the UFMylation pathway, its important biological and therapeutic implications in TKI-tolerant LUAD. AKT signaling repressor PHLDA33 was identified as a substrate of UFL1 in LUAD. Tumor-associated PHLDA3 mutations impaired its UFMylation and membrane translocation, resulting in AKT hyperactivation and enhanced tumor growth (121).

Neuroblastoma (NB)

NB is one of the most prevalent solid tumors in children originating from the sympathetic nervous system, contributing to about 15% of all cancer-related mortalities in children (122). Super enhancers (SEs) are genomic regions comprising multiple closely spaced enhancers. CDK5RAP3 is a recently identified super-enhancer-driven gene regulated by master transcription factors (TFs) in NB, whose function remains to be explored. Zhuo et al. reported the significantly elevated CDK5RAP3 expression in NB, which was associated poor prognosis. Defective CDK5RAP3 interfered with the UFMylation system and triggered ER-phagy. Intriguingly, CDK5RAP3 maintained the stability of the master TF MEIS2, which in turn contributes to the high expression of CDK5RAP3. These findings suggested that CDK5RAP3 inhibition might represent a novel therapeutic strategy for NB (32).

Glioblastoma (GBM)

Due to limited understanding of the heterogeneity and molecular mechanisms, therapies for GBM still remain elusive. Recently, increasing evidence have suggested that UFMylation played a role in GBM development and treatment response (123). Using CRISPR-Cas9 approaches in patient-derived GBM stem cells (GSCs), MacLeod et al. characterized that protein UFMylation played an important role in GSC growth (101). Using microarray-based genome-wide expression profiles analysis, Ning et al. identified the E2 enzyme UFC1 as a novel therapeutic target of demethylation drugs, which could be helpful for the treatment of GBM (124). Tan et al. reported two compounds which disrupted DDRGK1-UFL1 interaction, thereby suppressing UFMylation globally. Both compounds exerted strong immunomodulatory ability, promoting macrophage polarization toward an anti-tumor M1 state. These demonstrated UFMylation inhibition as a dual-targeting therapeutic strategy that disrupted tumor survival pathways and reprogramed the immune microenvironment, offering a promising avenue for durable GBM control (125).

Pancreatic cancer

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is a deadly malignancy with a 5-year survival rate of less than 5% (126). Currently available therapeutics regimens for PDAC provide unsatisfactory outcomes, including chemotherapy, radiation therapy and immunotherapy (127). UFMylation, the newly identified PTM, was reported to be deeply involved in the development and progression of pancreatic cancer via various mechanisms and could serve as a potential biomarker for diagnosis and prognosis. Liu et al. identified higher serum UFC1 expression in pancreatic cancer, and its association with poorer clinical stage and its value as a potential biomarker for diagnosis and prognosis (115). Roberts et al. showed that UFMylation E1 enzyme UBA5 was a novel pancreatic cancer therapeutic target. Excitingly, by coupling the screening of a cysteine-reactive fragment-based covalent ligand library to couple the discovery of lead small molecules that impair pancreatic cancer pathogenicity, DKM 2-93 was identified impairing pancreatic cancer cell survival and in vivo tumor growth through covalent modification of the UBA5 (114).

Although ferroptosis can inhibit cancer cells in vitro, its role in treatment in vivo remains controversial. Li et al. found that inhibition of MCP suppressed the UFMylation of PIR, thereby decreasing the transcription of ferroptosis marker GPX4 and promoting the cytoplasmic transportation of the damage-associated molecular pattern HMGB1. Subsequently, GPX4 deficiency triggered ferroptosis and rapid release of pre-accumulated cytosolic HMGB1, facilitating the pro-inflammatory and anti-tumor M1-like polarization of macrophage (40).

Ribosomal proteins are accessory proteins essential for the ribosome assembly and protein synthesis (128). Ribosomal protein L10 (RPL10) has been implicated as biomarkers or/and therapeutic targets for several cancers, such as ovarian cancer (129), lymphoblastic leukemia (130). While previous study has implicated RPL10 as a regulator for ROS level in pancreatic cancer (131), the role of its UFMylation remains unknown. Wang et al. found that UFL1-mediated UFMylation of RPL10 was positively correlated with the pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) development. And its UFMylation promoted the expression of stemness-related TF KLF4, resulting in enhanced cell proliferation and cancer cell stemness. UFL1 knock-down or mutation of UFMylation sites in RPL10 exhibited anti-tumor effect in PAAD (25).

Cancer immunotherapies are reported largely ineffective against PDAC. UFMylation was recently reported to drive PDAC growth and resistance to cancer immunotherapy. UFL1 facilitated PARP1 UFMylation to prevent its ubiquitination and degradation. Stabilized PARP1 inhibited cGAS-STING pathway activation, thereby promoting tumor immune evasion. This study suggested that UFL1-PARP1 axis could be novel target for amplifying anti-tumor immunity in PDAC (132).

Colorectal cancer (CRC)

CRC is the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, however, therapeutic options for CRC remain limited (133), requiring better understanding of the molecular mechanisms and development of more effective therapies. Based on GISTIC2.9 analysis on The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) data, Zhou et al. identified a total of 55 recurrent and focal somatic copy number alterations (SCNA) events in UFMylation family genes. Noteworthy, UFSP2 was found frequently mutated in colon adenocarcinoma. In addition, the high recurrent copy number loss and frequent somatic mutation pattern suggested that UFSP2 might function as a tumor suppressor in colon cancer, similar to its role in breast cancer (55,91). P53 is a well-known tumor suppressor and governor of various critical cellular processes, whose stability is modulated by PTMs including UFMylation. Based on in vitro experiments, both in colon cancer cell lines and renal cancer cell lines, Liu et al. demonstrated that UFMylation of p53 stabilized p53 by antagonizing its ubiquitination and proteasome degradation. Disruption of its UFMylation decreased p53 stability, in turn promoting cancer cell growth and tumor formation in vivo. These results identified UFMylation as a crucial PTM for maintenance of p53 stability and tumor-suppressive function, which could be a promising therapeutic target in cancer (52).

Currently, the potential function of UFMylation in immune cells and anti-tumor immunity remains largely unexplored. He et al. reported that mice with cKO of UFMylation E3 ligase UFL1 in T cells exhibited effectively controlled tumor growth. Mechanistically, UFL1 promoted PD-1 UFMylation, which antagonized its ubiquitination and further degradation. Utilizing colon cancer and lung cancer in vivo model, He et al. showed that ablation of UFL1 in T cells reduced PD-1 UFMylation, destabilizing PD-1 and enhancing CD8+T cell activation, leading to better response to anti-CTLA-4 immunotherapy (45). Zhou et al. found that PD-L1 as a target of UFMylation, which destabilized PD-L1 by synergizing its ubiquitination. Disruption of PD-L1 UFMylation undermined antitumor immunity both in vitro and in vivo. Intriguingly, a covalent inhibitor of UFSP2 could promoted the UFMylation activity and contributed to the combination therapy with PD-1 blockage (44).

Gastric cancer (GC)

GC is highly lethal, ranking fifth and third global incidence and mortality rates of all malignancies. Increasing studies have reported that UFMylation and its key components are implicated in GC. Lin et al. reported significantly downregulated expression of UFM1 in GC and shorter 5-year survival rate in patients with low UFM1. Further studies revealed that UFM1 increased the ubiquitination of PDK1 and decreased its protein level, thereby inhibiting the phosphorylation level of AKT. Subsequently, the invasion and metastasis of GC was suppressed (99).

CDK5RAP3 is a key regulator of UFMylation. Previous studies reported the lower expression of CDK5RAP3 in GC and its correlation with poor prognosis (95). Zheng et al. found that CDK5RAP3 negatively regulated the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway by repressing AKT phosphorylation, leading to better survival of GC (94). Lin et al. found lower expression of CDK5RAP3 and UFM1 was an independent predictor for poorer overall survival of GC patients. In addition, Combining the co-expression pattern of CDK5RAP3 and UFM1 with TNM staging system could improve the accuracy of prognosis prediction (92).

Low expression of UFM1-binding and PCI domain-containing protein 1 (UFBP1), another key component in the UFM1 conjugation system, was reported to be associated with the worse outcome of GC patients. Hu et al. discovered that high expression of UFBP1 increased progression-free survival (PFS) of advanced GC patients treated with platinum-based chemotherapy. Upon cisplatin treatment, UFBP1 increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) and attenuated the AKR1Cs expression and the transactivation of nuclear factor erythroid-2-related factor 2 (Nrf2). Mechanistically, by promoting the formation of K48-linked polyubiquitin chains on Nrf2, UFBP1 augmenting its proteasome-mediated degradation (19).

Liver cancer

Liver cancer is a common and lethal malignancy, consisting hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and other histological subtypes. Its incidence keeps increasing worldwide, and the overall survival rate remains unsatisfactory, calling for novel treatments with satisfactory efficacy (134). Increasing evidence have suggested that UFMylation and its components played an important role in liver cancer. Cao et al. reported that lncRNA-UFC1 promoted proliferation and reduced apoptosis in HCC cells, facilitating in vivo growth of liver cancer (104). By generating hepatocyte specific UFL1 and UFBP1 knockout (KO) mice, Chen et al. found a series of liver pathology transformation, including hepatocyte apoptosis and mild steatosis at 2-month and hepatocellular ballooning, extensive fibrosis and steatohepatitis at 6–8 months of age. These findings supported the potential role of UFL1 and UFBP1 as gatekeepers in prevention of liver fibrosis and subsequent steatohepatitis and HCC development (103). Zhou et al. reported that UFMylation of PD-L1 decreased its stability by synergizing its ubiquitination, imparting immune evasion in liver cancer in vitro (44). UFL1 expression was found negatively correlated with CD8+ T cell infiltration and cytotoxicity, tumor-associated neutrophil (TAN) infiltration and N2-like polarization. In addition, low UFL1 expression synergized with anti-PD-1 therapy in HCC. UFL1-PRMT5-NF-κB p65-CCL8 axis was revealed as governor of neutrophil-driven immunosuppression in HCC (135).

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC)

RCC is a highly aggressive urological malignancy with rising incidence and mortality rate worldwide, consisting of the most conventional clear cell subtype (ccRCC) and rare histological variants (non-clear cell RCC) (133). Previous study has reported that activated UFMylation in renal cancer and its potential value as a novel molecular marker (116).

P53 is the most studied and well-known tumor suppressor, whose homeostasis is essential for the tumor-suppressive function. Currently, the underlying mechanism of p53 homeostasis remains largely unclear. Liu et al. reported the downregulated UFMylation and its positive correlation with levels of p53 in a high percentage of RCC. Mechanistically, the competitive binding with MDM2 of UFL1 stabilized p53, antagonizing its ubiquitination and subsequent proteasome degradation. Disruption of UFMylation, either by depleting UFL1 or DDRGK1, decreased p53 stability and promoted cell growth and tumor formation in vivo (52).

UFMylation and its crosstalk with ubiquitination

PTMs played a crucial role in protein homeostasis, amongst which ubiquitination is the most widely researched. Like ubiquitination, UFMylation is involved in protein degradation. Here, based on literature research, we discuss the possible crosstalk between UFMylation and ubiquitination.

On the one hand, UFMylation is demonstrated to increase the protein stability by suppressing its ubiquitination and subsequent degradation. The E3 ligase UFL1 facilitates PARP1 UFMylation and enhances its protein stability, thus preventing its ubiquitination and subsequent degradation. Stabilized PARP1 enhances DNA damage repair, suppresses R-loop formation, and inhibits cGAS-STING activation, thereby promoting tumor immune evasion (132). UFL1 deficiency enhances prostate cancer cell resistance to the next-generation androgen receptor inhibitor ENZ both in vivo and vitro. UFL1 loss decreases METTL16 UFMylation, thereby reducing its ubiquitination level and increase its protein stability (136). Yang et al. found DDRGK1 stabilized the stress sensor ER-to-nucleus signaling 1 (IRE1α) to maintain ER homoeostasis. Loss of DDRGK1 decreased the UFMylation and subsequently led to increased ubiquitylation-mediated IRE1α degradation, causing ER dysfunction (23).

On the other hand, UFMylation is reported to promote proteasomal degradation. UFL1 induced UFMylation of NONO at Lys198 thus decreased its protein stability, thus enhancing glycolysis of HCC cells (137). It is reported that ER chaperone protein BiP can be covalently modified by UFM1 at lysine residues 294/296/352/353/370. This destabilizes BiP by promoting its ubiquitination and subsequent proteasome degradation (138). Similarly, by synergizing its ubiquitination, UFMylation of PD-L1 at Lys75/89/105/162/280/281 destabilizes its protein stability (44).

Conclusions

Increasing studies have demonstrated the vital role of UFMylation system in development and multiple biological processes. Defects of UFMylation, including genetic variants, abnormal expression pattern and somatic copy number alterations, have been reported involved in tumorigenesis, progression and treatment response. Therefore, it is promising that UFMylation can be utilized as a biomarker for diagnosis, treatment response assessment, and therapeutic targets in cancer therapy.

A growing number of studies have reported the application of inhibitors targeting UFMylation in various cancer models. The Compound-8, a covalent inhibitor of UFSP2, was shown to promote the UFMylation of PD-L1 and destabilize PD-L1, resulting to the tumor-suppressive effect in murine model. More excitingly, combination of Compound-8 contributed to in vivo treatment with PD-1 blockage (138). Based on the isoTOP-ABPP platform, the covalent ligand DKM 2-93 was discovered as a selective lead inhibitor that impaired pancreatic cancer both in vitro and in vivo (114). Additionally, clinically approved and available reagents are under assessment for UFMylation inhibition. Metformin was identified as an indirect inhibitor for UFM1, which inhibited the level of its protein and the overall UFMylation modification level (50). Meanwhile, patents have been continually proposed to engage UFMylation as an innovative prevention and therapeutic strategy for cancers. For example, DDRGK1 has been proposed to serve as a prognosis biomarker in patients with GC who received cisplatin-based chemotherapy (CN111879950A). Methods and materials of UFMylation inhibition to sensitize DNA damaging therapies have been proposed as patents (US11136583B2; US2019330638A1; WO2018132358A1). However, none of aforementioned reagents and patents have been advanced to next-step clinical trial when this review was completed.

Since the discovery of the UFM1 and UFMylation, it has been an attractive research field and increasing evidence have demonstrated its vital role in body development, various cellular processes and pathological conditions. However, as a relatively new PTM, there still remains many questions to be addressed. Considering limited number of identified components and substrates, great efforts have been put to identify additional proteins participated in the UFMylation pathway. Moreover, the machinery mechanisms regulating the components and enzymes are still largely unknown, requiring more in-depth investigation. Last but not least, despite the progress of fundamental evidence and proposed patents for UFMylation, the translation of its potential value in clinical setting remains absent. To the credit of the rapidly developed artificial intelligence (AI), AI algorithm-based scientific research have shed new light addressing these questions, such as the application of Alphafold in depicting the protein structure and subsequent chemical compounds development. With these advanced technologies and further clinical investigations, we strongly believe that a comprehensive view and clinical translation of UFMylation will be realized in the near future.

While this review has focused on synthesizing the current understanding of UFMylation’s biological mechanisms and pathophysiological roles in cancer, it is imperative to acknowledge that the field remains in its infancy, partly due to significant methodological constraints. Current research is notably hindered by the limited repertoire of validated substrates, the paucity of highly specific anti-UFM1 antibodies for immunodetection, and the scarcity of robust in vivo models tailored for UFMylation pathway interrogation. Overcoming these technical bottlenecks—particularly in substrate identification and in vivo validation—is essential for translating mechanistic insights into clinical applications. Consequently, a systematic discussion regarding the development of advanced tools and experimental strategies represents a critical knowledge gap that we aim to address in a forthcoming, dedicated review.

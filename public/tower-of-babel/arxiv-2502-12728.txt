Spivey’s type recurrence relation for degenerate Bell polynomials
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to derive a recurrence relation for the degenerate Bell polynomials by using the operators X X and D D satisfying the commutation relation D ​ X − X ​ D = 1 DX-XD=1 . Here X X is the ‘multiplication by x x ’ operator and D = d d ​ x D=\frac{d}{dx} . This recurrence relation is a generalization of Spivey’s recurrence relation for the Bell numbers. We also obtain a recurrence relation for the degenerate r r -Bell polynomials by using the same operators.

1.
Introduction

The Stirling number of the second kind
{n\brace k}
enumerates the number of partitiions of the set
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
into
k
nonempty disjoint subsets (see [15,27]). The sum of the Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n\brace k}
, called the Bell number and denoted by
\phi_{n}
, counts the total number of partitions of a set with
n
elements (see [11,12]). That is,
\phi_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}
. The Bell polynomial
\phi_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}x^{k}
is a natural polynomial extension of the Bell number
\phi_{n}
(see [1,31]).

Let
r
be a nonnegative integer. The
r
-Stirling number of the second kind
{n+r\brace k+r}_{r}
enumerates the number of partitiions of
[n]
into
k
nonempty disjoint subsets in such a way that
1,2,\dots,r
are in distinct nonempty disjoint subsets (see [6]). The sum of the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n+r\brace k+r}_{r}
, called the
r
-Bell number and denoted by
\phi_{n}^{(r)}
, counts the total number of partitions of
[n]
in such a way that
1,2,\dots,r
are in distinct nonempty disjoint subsets. That is,
\phi_{n}^{(r)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r}
. The
r
-Bell polynomial
\phi_{n}^{(r)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r}x^{k}
is a natural polynomial extension of the
r
-Bell number
\phi_{n}^{(r)}
.

In recent years, many degenerate versions have been explored for special polynomials and numbers (see [7,9,13,17,19,20,21,23,25,28,29]), gamma functions (see [24]) and even umbral calculus (see [16]) by employing diverse tools, including combinatorial methods, generating functions,
p
-adic analysis, umbral calculus, probability theory, quantum mechanics, and operator theory.

In this paper, we consider degenerate versions of the Bell polynomials and the
r
-Bell polynomials, namely the degenerate Bell polynomials
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)
and the degenerate
r
-Bell polynomials
\phi_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
.

Spivey obtained the following recurrence relation for the Bell numbers:

(1)

\phi_{m+n}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}{m\brace j}j^{n-k}\phi_{k},\quad(m,n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [22,35]).

The aim of this paper is to derive Spivey’s type recurrence relations for
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)
and
\phi_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
by using the ‘multiplication by
x
’ operator
X
and the differentiation operator
D=\frac{d}{dx}
(see [4,5,14,17,19,25,26,33]), satisfying the commutation relation
DX-XD=1
. Indeed, for the degenerate
r
-Bell polynomials
\phi_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
, we show the following:

\phi_{n+m,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(k-m\lambda)_{n-l,\lambda}x^{k}\phi_{l,\lambda}^{(r)}(x).

As general references, we let the reader refer to [2,3,8,10,34,36]. In the rest of this section, we recall the necessary facts that are needed throughout this paper.

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by Kim-Kim as

(2)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\mathrm{and}\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [20]),

where

(3)

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\qquad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots\big(x-(n-1)\lambda\big),\quad(n\geq 1).

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}.

The Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(4)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [15,16,27]),

where

(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1).

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(5)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [20,23,25,29,32]).

From (
5
), we note that

(6)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
k
is a nonnegative integer.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
r\geq 0
, the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(7)

(x+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [19]).

By (
7
), we get

(8)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Also, the Bell polynomials are given by

(9)

e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,31]).

We note that
\phi_{n}(1)=\phi_{n},\ (n\geq 0)
, are the Bell numbers (see [11,12]) and

\phi_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}x^{k}.

In view of (
9
), the degenerate Bell polynomials are defined by

(10)

e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18,30]).

By (
6
) and (
10
), we get

(11)

\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}x^{k},\quad(n,k\geq 0).

Here
\phi_{n,\lambda}=\phi_{n,\lambda}(1),\ (n\geq 0)
, are called the degenerate Bell numbers.

The degenerate
r
-Bell polynomials are given by

(12)

e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [17]).

By (
8
) and (
12
), we get

(13)

\phi_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

2.
Spivey’s type recurrence relations for degenerate Bell and degenerate
r
-Bell polynomials

Now, we define the operators
X
and
D
by

(14)

Xf(x)=xf(x)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad Df(x)=\frac{d}{dx}f(x)

that satisfy
DX-XD=1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(15)

\displaystyle DX^{k}-X^{k}D

\displaystyle=\big(DX^{k-1}-X^{k-1}D\big)X+X^{k-1}\big(DX-XD)

\displaystyle=\big(DX^{k-1}-X^{k-1}D)X+X^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\big(DX^{k-2}-X^{k-2}D\big)X^{2}+2X^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=\big(DX-XD\big)X^{k-1}+(k-1)X^{k-1}=kX^{k-1}.

From (
15
), we note that

(16)

\displaystyle\big(XD\big)X^{k}

\displaystyle=X\big(DX^{k}\big)=X\big(X^{k}D+kX^{k-1}\big)

\displaystyle=X^{k+1}D+kX^{k}=X^{k}\big(XD+k\big).

By (
16
), we get

(17)

\big(XD\big)X^{k}=X^{k}\big(XD+k\big),\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}).

For
f(x)=x^{m}
, with
m\geq 0
, we have

(18)

\displaystyle\big(XD\big)_{n,\lambda}f(x)

\displaystyle=\big(XD\big)_{n,\lambda}x^{m}=(m)_{n,\lambda}x^{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}(m)_{k}x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}X^{k}D^{k}x^{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}X^{k}D^{k}f(x).

Thus, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
18
), we get

(19)

\big(XD\big)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}X^{k}D^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),

where

{n+1\brace k}_{\lambda}={n\brace k-1}_{\lambda}+(k-n\lambda){n\brace k}_{\lambda},\quad(n\geq k\geq 1).

From (
11
) and (
19
), we note that

(20)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{x}}\big(XD\big)_{n,\lambda}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{x}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}X^{k}D^{k}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{x}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}x^{k}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\phi_{n,\lambda}(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

Therefore, by (
20
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(21)

\frac{1}{e^{x}}\big(XD\big)_{n,\lambda}e^{x}=\phi_{n,\lambda}(x).

By (
3
), we get

(22)

\displaystyle\big(XD\big)_{m+n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(XD\big)\cdots\big(XD-(m-1)\lambda\big)\big(XD-m\lambda\big)\cdots\big(XD-m\lambda-(n-1)\lambda\big)

\displaystyle=\big(XD\big)_{m,\lambda}\big(XD-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}=\big(XD-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(XD\big)_{m,\lambda},

where
m,n
are nonnegative integers.

We note that

(23)

(x+y)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(x)_{k,\lambda}(y)_{n-k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

For
n\geq 0
, by (
17
) and (
23
), we get

(24)

\displaystyle\big(XD-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}X^{j}=\big(XD-m\lambda\big)\big(XD-m\lambda-\lambda\big)\cdots\big(XD-m\lambda-(n-1)\lambda\big)X^{j}

\displaystyle=\big(XD-m\lambda\big)\cdots\big(XD-m\lambda-(n-2)\lambda\big)X^{j}\big(XD-m\lambda-(n-1)\lambda+j\big)

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=X^{j}\big(XD+j-m\lambda\big)\big(XD+j-m\lambda-\lambda\big)\cdots\big(XD+j-m\lambda-(n-1)\lambda\big)

\displaystyle=X^{j}\big(XD+j-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}=X^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(XD)_{k,\lambda}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}.

From (
19
), (
22
) and (
24
), we note that

(25)

\displaystyle\big(XD)_{m+n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(XD-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(XD\big)_{m,\lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\big(XD-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}X^{j}D^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}\big(j-m\lambda\big)_{n-k,\lambda}X^{j}\big(XD\big)_{k,\lambda}D^{j},

where
m,n
are nonnegative integers.

For
m,n\geq 0
, by (
21
) and (
25
), we get

(26)

\displaystyle\phi_{m+n,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{x}}\big(XD\big)_{m+n,\lambda}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\frac{1}{e^{x}}X^{j}\big(XD\big)_{k,\lambda}D^{j}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\frac{1}{e^{x}}X^{j}\big(XD\big)_{k,\lambda}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}x^{j}\phi_{k,\lambda}(x).

Therefore, by (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
m,n\geq 0
, we have

\phi_{m+n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}x^{j}\phi_{k,\lambda}(x).

Letting
x=1
yields

(27)

\phi_{m+n,\lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\phi_{k,\lambda}.

We observe that taking
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in (
27
) gives the Spivey’s formula for Bell numbers in (
1
).

Let
f(x)=x^{m},\ (m\geq 0)
. For
n,r\geq 0
, by (
7
), we get

(28)

\displaystyle\big(XD+r\big)_{n,\lambda}f(x)

\displaystyle=\big(XD+r\big)\big(XD+r-\lambda\big)\cdots\big(XD+r-(n-1)\lambda\big)x^{m}

\displaystyle=(m+r)_{n,\lambda}x^{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(m)_{k}x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}X^{k}D^{k}x^{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}X^{k}D^{k}f(x).

Thus, by (
28
), we get

(29)

\big(XD+r\big)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}X^{k}D^{k},\quad(n,r\geq 0).

For
n,r\geq 0
, by (
13
) and (
14
), we have

(30)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{x}}\big(XD+r\big)_{n,\lambda}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{x}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}X^{k}D^{k}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}x^{k}=\phi_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x).

Therefore, by (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n,r\geq 0
, we have

(31)

\frac{1}{e^{x}}\big(XD+r\big)_{n,\lambda}e^{x}=\phi_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x).

For
m,n\geq 0
, by (
23
) and (
29
), we get

(32)

\displaystyle\big(XD+r\big)_{n+m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(XD+r-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(XD+r\big)_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(XD+r-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}X^{k}D^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}\big(XD+r-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}X^{k}D^{k}.

Now, we observe that

(33)

\displaystyle\big(XD+r-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}X^{k}

\displaystyle=X^{k}\big(XD+r+k-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=X^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\big(XD+r\big)_{l,\lambda}(k-m\lambda)_{n-l,\lambda}.

By (
32
) and (
33
), we get

(34)

\big(XD+r\big)_{n+m,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}\binom{n}{l}(k-m\lambda)_{n-l,\lambda}X^{k}\big(XD+r\big)_{l,\lambda}D^{k}.

From (
31
) and (
34
), we note that

(35)

\displaystyle\phi_{n+m,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{x}}\big(XD+r\big)_{n+m,\lambda}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}\binom{n}{l}\big(k-m\lambda\big)_{n-l,\lambda}\frac{1}{e^{x}}X^{k}\big(XD+r\big)_{l,\lambda}D^{k}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}\binom{n}{l}(k-m\lambda)_{n-l,\lambda}x^{k}\phi_{l,\lambda}^{(r)}(x),

where
n,m
are nonnegative integers.

Therefore, by (
35
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n,m\geq 0
, we have

\phi_{n+m,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(k-m\lambda)_{n-l,\lambda}x^{k}\phi_{l,\lambda}^{(r)}(x).

Letting
x=1
gives

\phi_{n+m,\lambda}^{(r)}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(k-m\lambda)_{n-l,\lambda}\phi_{l,\lambda}^{(r)}.

Note that

\phi_{n+m}^{(r)}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\phi_{n+m,\lambda}^{(r)}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r}k^{n-l}\phi_{l}^{(r)}.

3.
Conclusion

In this paper, we derived Spivey’s type recurrence relations for the degenerate Bell polynomials
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)
and the degenerate
r
-Bell polynomials
\phi_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
by using the ‘multiplication by
x
’ operator
X
and the differentiation operator
D=\frac{d}{dx}
.

Here we review the idea of our proof for
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)
. By using (
22
), (
19
), (
17
) and (
23
) in this order, we can proceed as follows:

(36)

\displaystyle(XD)_{m+n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(XD-m\lambda)_{n,\lambda}(XD)_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(XD-m\lambda)_{n,\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}X^{j}D^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}(XD-m\lambda)_{n,\lambda}X^{j}D^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}X^{j}(XD+j-m\lambda)_{n,\lambda}D^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}X^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}(XD)_{k,\lambda}D^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}X^{j}(XD)_{k,\lambda}D^{j}.

Using (
36
) and
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{e^{x}}(XD)_{n,\lambda}e^{x}
(see (
21
)), we obtain the recurrence relation in Theorem 2.2:

\phi_{m+n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}x^{j}\phi_{k,\lambda}(x).

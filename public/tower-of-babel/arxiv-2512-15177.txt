1.
Introduction

Let us consider the stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE, for short),

(1.1)

\left[\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u(t\,,x)=\partial^{2}_{x}u(t\,,x)+\sigma(u(t\,,x))\dot{W}(t\,,x)&\qquad\text{for $(t\,,x)\in(0\,,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}$},\\
&u(0)=1&\text{on $\mathbb{R}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonrandom Lipschitz continuous function,
and
\dot{W}
denotes a space-time white noise.
In order to avoid degeneracies, we will also assume that

\sigma(1)\neq 0
,
for
u\equiv 1
otherwise.

It is well known that (
1.1
) is well posed
and has a random-field solution
u=\{u(t\,,x)\}_{t\geqslant 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
;
see Dalang
[
7
]
, and that the solution
u
is locally
Hölder continuous in its variables, as well. In fact,

u\in C^{a/2,a}_{\textit{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
a.s. for every
a\in(0\,,\frac{1}{2})
;
see the proof of Theorem 3.8 of Walsh
[
34
]
for example.
The continuity of
u
implies that
u(t\,,x)\approx 1
to leading term,
when
t\approx 0
, valid for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. The error in this approximation
describes the rate of growth of the solution, at the spatial point
x
,
away from its initial profile. Also, that error is known to be of sharp order
t^{1/4}
.
For instance, it is known that

[u(t\,,x)-1]/t^{1/4}
converges in distribution to a non-degenerate normal
law for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
; see Amir, Corwin, and Quastel
[
1
]
,
Hairer and Pardoux
[

[

2.
Linearization

Recall that the solution to (
1.1
) is a predictable random field

u=\{u(t\,,x)\}_{t\geqslant 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
,
in the sense of Walsh
[
34
]
,
that satisfies
\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{E}(|u(t\,,x)|^{2})<\infty
for all
T>0
and
solves

(2.1)

u(t\,,x)=1+\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(y-x)\sigma(u(s\,,y))\,W({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y)\qquad\forall t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},

almost surely, where

(2.2)

G_{s}(y)=(4\pi s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{4s}\right)\qquad\forall s>0,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Ordinarily, one would need to be careful about the order of
the quantifiers in (
2.1
)
[
\forall t
and
\forall x
and the “almost sure” portion of the assertion].
Such distinctions do not exist in the present context since, as was pointed out earlier
in the Introduction,
u
is continuous.

Theorem
1.1
is a statement about the behavior of the infinite-dimensional
process
t\mapsto u(t)
when
t\approx 0
. It has been observed by
Khoshnevisan, Swanson, Xiao, and Zhang
[
27
]

and Hairer and Pardoux
[
16
]

that, because of (
2.1
) and to leading order,
the small-
t
behavior of

is essentially the same as the small-
t
behavior of

\sigma(1)H
, where
H=\{H(t\,,x)\}_{t\geqslant 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
solves (
1.1
) starting from zero
and with
\sigma
replaced identically by one. Stated somewhat more carefully (but
still not quite rigorously),

(2.3)

u(t\,,x)=1+\sigma(1)H(t\,,x)+{o}(t^{1/4})\quad\text{as $t\to 0^{+}$},

where

(2.4)

\left[\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}H(t\,,x)=\partial^{2}_{x}H(t\,,x)+\dot{W}(t\,,x)&\qquad\text{for $(t\,,x)\in(0\,,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}$},\\
&H(0)=0&\text{on $\mathbb{R}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Just as in (
2.1
), we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle H(t\,,x)=\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}(z-x)\,W({\rm d}r\,{\rm d}z)\qquad\forall t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},

and
H(0\,,x)=0

[x\in\mathbb{R}
]. In particular,
H=\{H(t\,,x)\}_{t\geqslant 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
is a
centered Gaussian process, and the bulk of the proof of Theorem
1.1

will involve a detailed analysis of the process

.

The main purpose of this section is to
verify a suitably strong version of (
2.3
).
The subsequent analysis of
H
, and its relation to Theorem
1.1
,
will be developed in later sections.
First, let us record some of the salient features of the process
H
.

Lemma 2.1
.

H
is a centered Gaussian process such that:

(1)

\Var H(t\,,x)=\sqrt{t/(2\pi)}
for all
t\geqslant 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

(Hölder continuity)
H\in C^{a/2,a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
a.s. for every

.

(3)

(The Markov property) The
C(\mathbb{R})
-valued process
\{H(t)\}_{t\geqslant 0}

is Markov.

(4)

(Stationarity)

is a
C(\mathbb{R}_{+})
-valued stationary process.

(5)

(Scaling) The law of

.

Lemma
2.1
is essentially entirely a ready corollary of the following
formula, valid for every

and

:

(2.6)

\Cov[H(t\,,x)\,,H(s\,,y)]=\int_{0}^{s\wedge t}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t+s-2r)}\right)\frac{{\rm d}r}{\sqrt{4\pi(t+s-2r)}}.

The computation is straightforward. Therefore, we omit the details.
Let us also observe the following technical consequence of
(
2.6

\begin{split}\sup_{t>0}\mathrm{E}\left(|H(t\,,x)-H(t\,,y)|^{2}\right)&\asymp|x-y|,\\

valid uniformly for all
\varepsilon>0

and

; see

24

In order to streamline our discussion, let us define

\displaystyle{E}(t\,,x)=u(t\,,x)-1-\sigma(1)H(t\,,x),\quad\forall t\geqslant 0,x\in\mathbb{R},

)

Lemma 2.2
.

If, in addition,

\adjustlimits{\sup}_{t\in(0,1]}{\sup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{E}\exp\left(t^{-1/2}|{E}(t\,,x)|\right)<\infty.

The second, displayed, assertion of the lemma follows from the first
and the Taylor expansion of the exponential function. Therefore, it suffices
to prove the asserted

We can combine (

) and (

\displaystyle\|{E}(t\,,x)\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4k\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(x-y)]^{2}\|\sigma(u(s\,,y))-\sigma(1)\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4k\text{Lip}(\sigma)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(x-y)]^{2}\|u(s\,,y)-u(0\,,y)\|_{k}^{2},

uniformly for all
k\in[2\,,\infty)
,

and
x\in[a\,,b]
.
We now combine (

) with (

in order to deduce from (

\displaystyle\|u(t\,,x)-u(s\,,y)\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4k\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}r\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\ [G_{t-r}(x-z)\mathbb{1}_{(0,t)}(r)-G_{s-r}(y-z)\mathbb{1}_{(0,s)}(r)]^{2}\|\sigma(u(r\,,z))\|_{k}^{2}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leqslant 4k\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}|\sigma(z)|^{2}\|H(t\,,x)-H(s\,,y)\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim k\left(\sqrt{|t-s|}+|x-y|\right),

uniformly for all

,

and

. Therefore, the preceding discussion, and the semigroup property
of the heat kernel, together yield

where the implied constant is independent of

The lemma follows.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose, in addition, that

\displaystyle A=\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1/{\rm e})}\mathrm{E}\left[\exp\left(\gamma_{0}\adjustlimits{\sup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<s,t\leqslant 1\\

|x-y|\leqslant\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\frac{|{E}(t\,,x)-{E}(s\,,y)|^{2}}{\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)}\right)\right]<\infty.

In particular, uniformly for all

\left\|\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<s,t\leqslant 1\\
|t-s|\leqslant\varepsilon^{1/2}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}-N\leqslant x,y\leqslant N\\

Proof.

Recall (
2.8
).
For every
t,s\in(0\,,1]
and

{E}(t\,,x)-{E}(s\,,y)=u(t\,,x)-u(s\,,y)-\sigma(1)(H(t\,,x)-H(s\,,y)).

Let
\Delta((t\,,x)\,,(s\,,y))=|t-s|^{1/4}+|x-y|^{1/2}
for all
s,t>0

and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
A crude application of (
2.9
), once for

and once for

, yields

\displaystyle\|{E}(t\,,x)-{E}(s\,,y)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t\,,x)-u(s\,,y)\|_{k}+\|H(t\,,x)-H(s\,,y)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{k}\,\Delta((t\,,x),(s\,,y)),

uniformly for all
k\in[2\,,\infty)
,
t,s\in(0\,,1]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, a standard metric entropy argument yields the sub-Gaussian

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left[\exp\left(\gamma_{0}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<s\neq t\leqslant 1\\
-N\leqslant x\neq y\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\frac{|{E}(t\,,x)-{E}(s\,,y)|^{2}}{\left|\Delta((t\,,x),(s\,,y))\right|^{2}\log_{+}\left(\dfrac{1}{\Delta((t\,,x),(s\,,y))}\right)}\right)\right]<\infty,

valid for a suitably small choice of
\gamma_{0}>0
.
This readily yields the first assertion of the lemma. The second statement of
the lemma follows from the first and the fact that if
X
is a random
variable such that

\textstyle\|X\|_{2m}^{2m}/m^{m}\leqslant\|X\|_{2m}^{2m}/m!\leqslant\mathrm{E}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|X|^{2n}/n!=B,

\displaystyle\leqslant L_{4}^{p}{\rm e}^{L_{3}p^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\|u(t\,,0)-u(s\,,0)\|_{2m}^{2m}}{{\rm e}^{L_{3}(2m)^{3}}|t-s|^{m/2}}{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant L_{5}{\rm e}^{L_{3}p^{3}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-(L_{3}-L_{2})(2m)^{3}}<\infty.

This together with Chebyshev’s inequality yields

(3.8)

\begin{split}J_{3}&\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{0<s<t\leqslant 1}\frac{|u(t\,,0)-u(s\,,0)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{(p-8)/4}}\geqslant\frac{n^{\delta k(p-8)/4}z^{p}}{(n+1)^{\nu p}3^{p}}\right\}\\
&\lesssim 3^{p}{\rm e}^{L_{3}p^{3}}n^{\nu p-(\delta k(p-8)/4)}z^{-p}=3^{p}{\rm e}^{L_{3}p^{3}}n^{-p(\delta k-\nu-(2\delta k/p))}z^{-p},\end{split}

uniformly for all
k\in[2/\delta\,,\infty)
and all even numbers
p>8
.
Set

k_{0}=2(2\nu+1)/\delta.

If

, then
(\delta k/2)-2\nu>1
.
Thus, we may choose a large-enough even number

We now return to (
3.3
), and apply (
3.4
), (
3.5
),
and (
3.8
), in order to see that for every

there exists

such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in I(n)}\frac{|u(t\,,0)-\bar{u}(t\,,0)|}{t^{\nu}}\geqslant z\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-2-k(\frac{3}{4}-\nu-\delta)}z^{-k}+\exp\left(-\frac{n^{(\delta k/2)-2\nu}z^{2}}{C\log n}\right)+n^{-p(\delta k-\nu-\frac{2\delta k}{p})}z^{-p},

uniformly for all

Sum the preceding over

5.
Localization

One of the central ingredients of the proof of the remainder of
Theorem
1.1
is a localization property of
H
from (
2.4
).
Let us define for every
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
,
t>0
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
the following “localized” version of the random field
H
.

(5.1)

H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)=\int_{(0,t)\times[x-t^{(1-\alpha)/2},\,x+t^{(1-\alpha)/2}]}G_{t-s}(y-x)\,W({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y).

One can define also
H_{\alpha}(0)\equiv 0
, essentially by continuity.

The following is the main result of this section; it states that the effect of the noise
on the integral definition of
H
in (
2.4
) is highly localized, primarily
due to the rapid decay in the tails
of the heat kernel in (
2.2
).

Proposition 5.1
.

Choose and fix some
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
and
R>0
. Then, a.s.,

\sup_{x\in[-R,R]}\left|H(t\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)\right|={o}(t^{1/4})\quad\text{as $t\to 0^{+}$}.

This proposition holds because of its quantitative counterpart in Lemma

5.5
below. That result, in turn, is derived in a series
of smaller steps, beginning with the following simple estimate.

Lemma 5.2
.

For all
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and
t>0
,

\mathrm{E}\left(|H(t\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|^{2}\right)\leqslant 2\sqrt{\frac{t}{\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4t^{\alpha}}\right).

Proof.

By stationarity, the second moment in question does not depend on
x
;
therefore, we consider only
x=0
.
Let
X
denote a random variable with a standard normal distribution
and recall that
\mathrm{P}\{|X|>y\}\leqslant\exp(-y^{2}/2)
for all
y>0
; see for example

[
24
, Lemma A.3]
.
Since
G_{s}(y)\leqslant(4\pi s)^{-1/2}
for all
s>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
it follows from the
L^{2}
-isometry for Wiener integrals that

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left(|H(t\,,0)-H_{\alpha}(t\,,0)|^{2}\right)=2\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\ |G_{s}(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}(\pi s)^{-1/2}\mathrm{P}\left\{|X|>\sqrt{\frac{t^{1-\alpha}}{2s}}\right\}{\rm d}s\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}(\pi s)^{-1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{t^{1-\alpha}}{4s}\right){\rm d}s.

This proves the lemma since
\exp\{-t^{1-\alpha}/(4s)\}\leqslant\exp\{-t^{-\alpha}/4\}
when
s\leqslant t
.
∎

Next, we present a modulus of continuity estimate for the spatial variable of
the localized Gaussian random
field
H_{\alpha}
in (
5.1
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Uniformly for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\adjustlimits{\sup}_{t\in(0,1]}{\sup}_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\mathrm{E}\left(|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|^{2}\right)\lesssim|x-y|\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may and will assume that

x>y
throughout the proof.

We can write

H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)=I_{1}+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,t)\times[x-t^{(1-\alpha)/2},\,x+t^{(1-\alpha)/2}]}\left[G_{t-s}(z-x)-G_{t-s}(z-y)\right]W({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}z),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,t)\times[x-t^{(1-\alpha)/2},\,x+t^{(1-\alpha)/2}]\setminus[y-t^{(1-\alpha)/2},\,y+t^{(1-\alpha)/2}]}G_{t-s}(z-y)\,W({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}z).

On one hand,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(I_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{x-t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{x+t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}{\rm d}z\left[G_{t-s}(z-x)-G_{t-s}(z-y)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\left[G_{t-s}(z-x)-G_{t-s}(z-y)\right]^{2}=\mathrm{E}\left(\left|H(t\,,x)-H(t\,,y)\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{x-y}{2};

see (
2.7
). On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(I_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{[x-t^{(1-\alpha)/2},\,x+t^{(1-\alpha)/2}]\setminus[y-t^{(1-\alpha)/2},\,y+t^{(1-\alpha)/2}]}{\rm d}z\ [G_{t-s}(z-y)]^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{-t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{x-y-t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}{\rm d}z\ [G_{s}(z)]^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{x-y+t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}{\rm d}z\ [G_{s}(z)]^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{4\pi s}}\int_{-t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{x-y-t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}{\rm d}z\ G_{s}(z)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{4\pi s}}\int_{t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{x-y+t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}{\rm d}z\ G_{s}(z),

thanks to (
2.2
). Another appeal to (
2.2
) yields

\int_{\pm t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{x-y\pm t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}G_{s}(z)\,{\rm d}z\leqslant\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{s}}\wedge 1.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(I_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{s}}\wedge 1\right)\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{s}}=\int_{0}^{t\wedge|x-y|^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{s}}+(x-y)\int_{t\wedge|x-y|^{2}}^{t}\frac{{\rm d}s}{s}

\displaystyle=2\left\{\sqrt{t}\wedge(x-y)\right\}+(x-y)\log\left(\frac{t}{t\wedge|x-y|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 3\begin{cases}\sqrt{t}&\text{if $t\leqslant|x-y|^{2}$}\\
(x-y)\log\left(\dfrac{t}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)&\text{if $t>|x-y|^{2}$}\end{cases}\leqslant 6(x-y)\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{x-y}\right),

uniformly for all real numbers
x>y
and
t\in(0\,,1]
. Combine the estimates for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}

to finish.
∎

The following counterpart of Lemma

Lemma 5.4
.

Uniformly for every
\varepsilon\in(0\,,1)

\adjustlimits{\sup}_{t\in[0,1]}{\sup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\mathrm{E}\left(|H_{\alpha}(t+\varepsilon\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|^{2}\right)\lesssim\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Thanks to stationarity (see for example Lemma
2.1

only
x=0

we may write, for every

and

\mathrm{E}\left(|H_{\alpha}(t+\varepsilon\,,0)-H_{\alpha}(t\,,0)|^{2}\right)=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{-t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}{\rm d}y\,\left[G_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-G_{t-s}(y)\right]^{2},

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{(t+\varepsilon)^{(1-\alpha)/2}}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s+\varepsilon}(y)]^{2},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}{\rm d}s\int_{-t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s+\varepsilon}(y)]^{2}.

It is not hard to check that

Indeed, the inequality comes from replacing
\int_{-t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}^{t^{(1-\alpha)/2}}

by
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
, and the identity follows from (
2.7
).
It therefore remains to prove that

with the same parameter dependencies as in the statement of the lemma.
Since

uniformly for all

and

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\left[(t+\varepsilon)^{(1-\alpha)/2}-t^{(1-\alpha)/2}\right]=\frac{2}{\pi(3-\alpha)\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}r^{(3-\alpha)/2}\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)^{(3-\alpha)/2}}{\pi(3-\alpha)}\hskip 72.26999pt[r^{(3-\alpha)/2}\leqslant(t+\varepsilon)^{(3-\alpha)/2}].

The lemma follows (
5.2
) and the preceding bound for

.

Armed with the preceding, we can now state and prove the quantitative variation of
the main result of the earlier-announced Proposition
5.1
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Choose and fix
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
,
R>0
, and
p\in(0\,,1/8)
.
Then, there exists
c=c(\alpha\,,R\,,p)>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{s\in(0,t]}\sup_{x\in[-R,R]}|H(s\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(s\,,x)|\geqslant\exp(-p/t^{\alpha})\right\}\leqslant c^{-1}\exp\left(-c{\rm e}^{c/t^{\alpha}}\right),

uniformly for all
t\in(0\,,1)
.

Proof.

Throughout, we choose and fix three numbers
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
,
R>0
, and
p\in(0\,,1/8)
.
Recall the elementary fact that, if
X
has a centered Gaussian distribution, then

\mathrm{P}\{|X|\geqslant a\}\leqslant\exp\{-a^{2}/[2\mathrm{E}(X^{2})]\}
for all
a>0
.
This and Lemma
5.2
together yield the following: There exists

c=c(p)>1
such that, uniformly for all

t\in(0\,,1)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sup_{s\in(0,t]}\mathrm{P}\left\{|H(s\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(s\,,x)|\geqslant\frac{\exp(-p/t^{\alpha})}{2}\right\}\leqslant\exp\left(-c{\rm e}^{c/t^{\alpha}}\right).

Define

(5.3)

\mathbb{X}_{L}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{L^{2}}\bigcup_{k=-L}^{L}\left\{\left(\frac{j}{L^{2}}\,,\frac{k}{L}\right)\right\}\qquad\forall L\in\mathbb{N},

and deduce from the preceding that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{(s,x)\in\mathbb{X}_{L}\cap((0,t]\times[-R,R])}|H(s\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(s\,,x)|\geqslant\frac{\exp(-p/t^{\alpha})}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant(L^{2}+1)(1+2L)\exp\left(-c{\rm e}^{c/t^{\alpha}}\right)\leqslant 6L^{3}\exp\left(-c{\rm e}^{c/t^{\alpha}}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{(s,x)\in(0,t]\times[-R,R]}|H(s\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(s\,,x)|\geqslant\exp(-p/t^{\alpha})\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 144.54pt\leqslant 6L^{3}\exp\left(-c{\rm e}^{c/t^{\alpha}}\right)+{P}_{1}+{P}_{2},

where

\displaystyle{P}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{(s,x)\in(0,t]\times[-R,R]}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r:|r-s|\leqslant 1/L^{2}\\
y:|x-y|\leqslant 1/L\end{subarray}}|H(s\,,x)-H(r\,,y)|\geqslant\frac{\exp(-p/t^{\alpha})}{4}\right\},

\displaystyle{P}_{2}

\displaystyle=P\left\{\sup_{(s,x)\in(0,t]\times[-R,R]}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r:|r-s|\leqslant 1/L^{2}\\
y:|x-y|\leqslant 1/L\end{subarray}}|H_{\alpha}(s\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(r\,,y)|\geqslant\frac{\exp(-p/t^{\alpha})}{4}\right\},

and where the dependencies on the parameters
(n\,,R\,,\varepsilon\,,\ldots)
are supressed
to simplify the exposition and the notation.
For
\gamma\in(0\,,1/4)
, Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
can be combined with
standard metric entropy estimates in order to yield the following:

\mathrm{E}\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant s<t\leqslant 1\\
-R\leqslant x<y\leqslant R\end{subarray}}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(s\,,y)|}{|t-s|^{\gamma}+|x-y|^{2\gamma}}\right)<\infty.

And, as is well known, the same inequality holds (for more or less the same type of reasons)
when we replace
H_{\alpha}
by
H
everywhere.
Therefore, the concentration properties of Gaussian measure (see Ledoux
[
28
]
) yields
a constant
\zeta=\zeta(\alpha\,,R\,,p\,,\gamma)>0
such that

(5.4)

\begin{split}A_{1}&=\mathrm{E}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant s<t\leqslant 1\\
-R\leqslant x<y\leqslant R\end{subarray}}\exp\left(\zeta\frac{|H(t\,,x)-H(s\,,y)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{2p}+|x-y|^{4p}}\right)\right]<\infty,\text{ and}\\
A_{2}&=\mathrm{E}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant s<t\leqslant 1\\
-R\leqslant x<y\leqslant R\end{subarray}}\exp\left(\zeta\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(s\,,y)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{2p}+|x-y|^{4p}}\right)\right]<\infty.\end{split}

The preceding and Chebyshev’s inequality together yield

{P}_{i}\leqslant A_{i}\exp\left(-\frac{\zeta L^{4\gamma}\exp(-2p/t^{\alpha})}{16}\right)\qquad\forall i=1,2.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{(s,x)\in(0,t]\times[-R,R]}|H(s\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(s\,,x)|\geqslant{\rm e}^{-p/t^{\alpha}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 6L^{3}\exp\left(-c\,{\rm e}^{c/t^{\alpha}}\right)+(A_{1}+A_{2})\exp\left(-cL^{4\gamma}{\rm e}^{-2p/t^{\alpha}}\right).

Finally, we may take

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{(s,x)\in(0,t]\times[-R,R]}|H(s\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(s\,,x)|\geqslant\exp(-p/t^{\alpha})\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 6\exp\left(\frac{A}{t^{\alpha}}-c\,{\rm e}^{c/t^{\alpha}}\right)+(A_{1}+A_{2})\exp\left(-c{\rm e}^{(4\gamma A-2p)/t^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant c^{-1}\exp\left(-c{\rm e}^{c/t^{\alpha}}\right).

.

Lemma 5.6
.

For every
\theta>0
and
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
,

\mathrm{P}\left\{|H_{\alpha}(s\,,0)|\leqslant\theta s^{1/4}\,\,\forall s\in(a\,,b]\right\}=(a/b)^{\lambda(\theta)+{o}(1)}\quad\text{as $\max\{b\,,a/b\}\to 0^{+}$}.

Proof.

Note that
H-H_{\alpha}
is a centered Gaussian random field that is independent
of
H_{\alpha}
.
Therefore, Anderson’s inequality
[

]
yields the following for all
0<a<b
:

\mathrm{P}\left\{|H(s\,,0)|\leqslant\theta s^{1/4}\,\,\forall s\in(a\,,b]\right\}\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{|H_{\alpha}(s\,,0)|\leqslant\theta s^{1/4}\,\,\forall s\in(a\,,b]\right\}.

Thanks to the scaling properties of

, the left-hand side is equal to

\mathrm{P}\left\{|H(s\,,0)|\leqslant\theta s^{1/4}\,\,\forall s\in(a/b\,,1]\right\}=(a/b)^{\lambda(\theta)+{o}(1)},

uniformly for all
0<a<b
such that
a/b\to 0^{+}
. This proves that

(5.5)

\mathrm{P}\left\{|H_{\alpha}(s\,,0)|\leqslant\theta s^{1/4}\,\,\forall s\in(a/b\,,1]\right\}\geqslant(a/b)^{\lambda(\theta)+{o}(1)},

uniformly for all
0<a<b
such that
a/b\to 0^{+}
; this is a slightly stronger

and

do not need to converge to zero
individually for this bound, only the ratio needs to. For the complementary bound
we may observe that for every
\delta\in(0\,,1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{|H_{\alpha}(s\,,0)|\leqslant\theta s^{1/4}\,\,\forall s\in(a\,,b]\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{|H(s\,,0)|\leqslant\theta s^{1/4}+{\rm e}^{-\beta/s^{\alpha}}\,\,\forall s\in(a\,,b]\right\}+c^{-1}\exp\left(-c{\rm e}^{c/b^{\alpha}}\right)

. In particular, the scaling properties of

imply that, uniformly for all

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{|H_{\alpha}(s\,,0)|\leqslant\theta s^{1/4}\,\,\forall s\in(a\,,b]\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\leqslant(a/b)^{\lambda(\theta+\delta)+{o}(1)}+c^{-1}\exp\left(-c{\rm e}^{c/b^{\alpha}}\right)=(a/b)^{\lambda(\theta+\delta)+{o}(1)}.

In other words, together with (

), this proves that
whenever

and
\max\{b\,,a/b\}\to 0

\displaystyle\lambda(\theta+\delta)+{o}(1)\leqslant\frac{\log\mathrm{P}\left\{|H_{\alpha}(s\,,0)|\leqslant\theta s^{1/4}\,\,\forall s\in(a\,,b]\right\}}{\log(b/a)}\leqslant\lambda(\theta)+{o}(1),

for every fixed choice of

\delta\in(0\,,1)
, as well as
\theta>0
.
Since
\lambda
is continuous (see Proposition
2.5
),
we may let
\delta
tend to zero in order to obtain the result.
∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
, Part (2)

We begin with the presentation of Part (2) of Theorem
1.1
.
Before we do that, let us observe that, thanks to Proposition
2.4

and the Borel-Cantelli lemma,

\mathrm{P}\left\{\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}|{E}(t\,,x)|=0\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\right\}=1,

where the space-time random field
{E}
was defined in (
2.8
).
In particular, it follows that the slow point of
u
, as defined by (
1.3
)
can be written in terms of the Gaussian random field
H
of (
2.4
) as
follows: With probability one,

(6.1)

\fS(\theta)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\ \limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{|H(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}\quad\forall\theta>0.

To be precise, let us write the right-hand side as

\fS_{H}(\theta)
. Then, we have shown that the
set
\cup_{\theta>0}\{\fS(\theta)\neq\fS_{H}(\theta)\}
is a subset of
a
\mathrm{P}
-null set, and hence is
\mathrm{P}
-null thanks to the fact that our probability space is
complete.
In this way, we are permitted to use (
6.1
) as a definition of
\fS(\theta)
,
which we will. This reduces our problem to one about the Gaussian random field
H
.
With the preceding under way, the real work can now begin.

Theorem 6.1
.

Choose and fix a nonrandom compact set

K\subset\mathbb{R}
.
Then there is a
\mathrm{P}
-null set off which

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\dimmm K\right)\leqslant\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\limsup}_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{|H(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\lambda^{-1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\dimh K\right),

where

.

This implies Theorem
1.1
(2).
The proof of Theorem
6.1
itself is divided in two parts and will be
given in the §§
6.1
and
6.2
that follow.

6.1.
Proof of Theorem
6.1
: Lower bound

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}
be a nonempty compact set and choose and fix
a number
\varepsilon>0
. Recall that a finite set
L\subset K
is said to
be an
\varepsilon
-packing
of
K
if every
x\in K
is
within
\varepsilon
of some
y\in L
. Throughout, the
number
{P}_{\varepsilon}(K)
will denote the cardinality of
the smallest
\varepsilon
-packing of
K
. Because
K
is totally
bounded,
{P}_{\varepsilon}(K)<\infty
for every
\varepsilon>0
.

The
lower Minkowski (or box) dimension of
K
is the number

(6.2)

\dimmm K=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\frac{\log{P}_{\varepsilon}(K)}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

Some authors refer to
\dimmm
alternatively as the
lower box dimension
;
see Falconer
[
11
]
.
It is a standard exercise to verify that

0\leqslant\dimmm K\leqslant 1
, and that for every
\delta\in[0\,,1]
there exist compact sets
whose lower Minkowski dimension is exactly
\delta
. It is perhaps worth mentioning
that the better-known
Minkowski dimension
– also
known as the
upper Minkowski, or box, dimension
– is

\dimm K=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0+}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\log{P}_{\varepsilon}(K).

These two notions of dimension are not always the same.
The upper Minkowski dimension comes up more often than

\dimmm
in probability and analysis, but both do arise in different settings.

It is possible to use a covering argument in order to prove that

(6.3)

\dimmm K=\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log_{2}{N}_{n}(K)}{n},

where
\log_{2}
denotes the base-2 logarithm and

{N}_{n}(K)
denotes the total number of dyadic intervals
of the form
(j/2^{n}\,,(j+1)/2^{n}]
– as
j
ranges over
\mathbb{Z}
– that intersect
K
.
Because (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) are equivalent, some authors adopt
(
6.3
) as the definition of the lower Minkowski dimension.

Since
K
is compact, it is contained in a bounded interval
[a\,,b]
that we fix.
Choose and fix numbers
\theta,\eta,\beta>0
and
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
such that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\lambda(\theta+\eta)>\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2}>\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\dimmm K.

This can always be done since
\lim_{c\to 0^{+}}\lambda(c)=\infty

[Proposition
2.5
].
Since
\beta>\dimmm K
, it follows from the definition of lower Minkowski dimension
that there exists a positive sequence
\eta_{1}>\eta_{2}>\cdots
that
tend to 0 and satisfy

(6.5)

\displaystyle{P}_{\eta_{m}}(K)\leqslant\eta_{m}^{-\beta}\qquad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In light of (
6.4
), we choose and fix
\mu>1
such that

(6.6)

\displaystyle\mu\left(\lambda(\theta+\eta)-\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2}\right)>\lambda(\theta+\eta)+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}.

Next, we extract a subsequence

\{\bar{\eta}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of

\{\eta_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\varepsilon_{n}:=\bar{\eta}_{n}^{2/(1+\alpha)}\qquad(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})

satisfies

(6.7)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}\leqslant\exp(-\mu^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon_{n+1}\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}^{\mu}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Thanks to (
6.5
), for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a
finite set
F_{n}\subset K
such that the following properties hold:

Property A.

For every
x\in K
, there exists
y\in F_{n}
such that

|x-y|\leqslant\varepsilon_{n+1}^{(1+\alpha)/2}
;

Property B.

Whenever
w\,,z\in F_{n}
are distinct,

|w-z|\geqslant 2\varepsilon_{n+1}^{(1+\alpha)/2}
; and

Property C.

|F_{n}|\asymp\varepsilon_{n+1}^{-(1+\alpha)\beta/2}
,
where
|\cdots|
denotes cardinality.

Moreover, we consider a uniform partition of the interval
[a\,,b]

as follows:

[a\,,b]=[a_{n,1}\,,a_{n,2}]\cup\cdots\cup[a_{n,N}\,,a_{n,N+1}],

where

(6.8)

\displaystyle a_{n,i+1}-a_{n,i}\geqslant 2\varepsilon_{n}^{(1-\alpha)/2}\quad\text{and}\quad N=N(n)\asymp\varepsilon_{n}^{-(1-\alpha)/2}.

For every
1\leqslant i\leqslant N
, let

\displaystyle K_{i}=K_{n,i}=K\cap[a_{n,i}\,,a_{n,i+1}].

Owing to Property
C
, we have

(6.9)

\displaystyle|F_{n}\cap K_{i}|\lesssim\varepsilon_{n+1}^{-(1+\alpha)\beta/2}\qquad(1\leqslant i\leqslant N).

We may write
K=I\cup J
, where

I=K_{1}\cup K_{3}\cup\ldots\quad\text{and}\quad J=K_{2}\cup K_{4}\cup\ldots\,.

Recall the random field
H_{\alpha}
from (
5.1
).
By Boole’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in I}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}+\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in J}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}.

Thanks to the first assertion in (
6.8
),
the space-time random fields

\{H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)\}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}],x\in K_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad\{H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)\}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}],x\in K_{i+2}}

are independent from one another. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in I}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}=1-\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in I}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}>\theta\right\}

\displaystyle=1-\prod_{i:K_{i}\subset I}\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K_{i}}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}>\theta\right\}

\displaystyle=1-\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}:K_{i}\subset I}\left[1-\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K_{i}}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}\right].

Consider an index
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
K_{i}\subset I
.
Thanks to Property
A
, we may use interpolation
to deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K_{i}}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\min}_{y\in F_{n}\cap K_{i}}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta+\eta\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 108.405pt+\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in[a,b]:\\
|x-y|\leqslant\varepsilon_{n+1}^{(1+\alpha)/2}\end{subarray}}\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|}{t^{1/4}}>\eta\right\}.

A union bound (Boole’s inequality), (
6.9
), and Lemma
5.6
,
together yield

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\min}_{y\in F_{n}\cap K_{i}}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta+\eta\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant|F_{n}\cap K_{i}|\sup_{y\in K}\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta+\eta\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{n+1}^{-\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2}}(\varepsilon_{n+1}/\varepsilon_{n})^{\lambda(\theta+\eta)+{o}(1)}=\varepsilon_{n+1}^{\lambda(\theta+\eta)-\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2}+{o}(1)}\varepsilon_{n}^{-\lambda(\theta+\eta)+{o}(1)},

as
n\to\infty
.
Choose and fix
p\in(0\,,1/4)
such that
\delta=(1+\alpha)p-1/4>0
,
possible provided that
p
is sufficiently close to
1/4
since
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
.
Thanks to (
5.4
), there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in[a,b]:\\
|x-y|\leqslant\varepsilon_{n+1}^{(1+\alpha)/2}\end{subarray}}\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|}{t^{1/4}}>\eta\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{x,y\in[a,b]}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)-H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|}{|x-y|^{2p}}>\eta\varepsilon_{n+1}^{\frac{1}{4}-(1+\alpha)p}\right\}\lesssim\exp\left(-\frac{\eta^{2}}{c\varepsilon_{n+1}^{2\delta}}\right),

uniformly for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{n+1}^{\lambda(\theta+\eta)-\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2}+{o}(1)}\varepsilon_{n}^{-\lambda(\theta+\eta)+{o}(1)}+\exp\left(-\frac{\eta^{2}}{c\varepsilon_{n+1}^{2\delta}}\right)\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

The same estimate applies to every index
i\in\mathbb{N}
that satisfies either

K_{i}\subset I
or
K_{i}\subset J
.
Therefore, we may combine the above estimates above, and apply (
6.8
),
in order to see that there exists a constant
C>0
such that, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left[1-\left(1-C\varepsilon_{n+1}^{\lambda(\theta+\eta)-\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2}+{o}(1)}\varepsilon_{n}^{-\lambda(\theta+\eta)+{o}(1)}+C\exp\left\{-\frac{\eta^{2}}{C\varepsilon_{n+1}^{2\delta}}\right\}\right)^{C\varepsilon_{n}^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}}\right].

According to (
6.6
) and (
6.7
), as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n+1}^{\lambda(\theta+\eta)-\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2}+{o}(1)}\varepsilon_{n}^{-\lambda(\theta+\eta)+{o}(1)}\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}^{\mu(\lambda(\theta+\eta)-\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2})-\lambda(\theta+\eta)+{o}(1)}={o}(1).

Therefore, the elementary inequality
\exp(-z)\leqslant 1-(z/2)
,
valid for all
z\in[0\,,1]
, (
6.6
), and (
6.7
) together yield

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\sup}_{t\in[\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left[1-\exp\left\{-2C^{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\left(\varepsilon_{n}^{\mu(\lambda(\theta+\eta)-\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2})-\lambda(\theta+\eta)+{o}(1)}+\exp\left[-\frac{\eta^{2}}{C\varepsilon_{n+1}^{2\delta}}\right]\right)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=2\left[1-\exp\left\{-2C^{2}\left(\varepsilon_{n}^{\mu(\lambda(\theta+\eta)-\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2})-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}-\lambda(\theta+\eta)+{o}(1)}+\varepsilon_{n}^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{\eta^{2}}{C\varepsilon_{n+1}^{2\delta}}\right]\right)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left[1-\exp\left\{-2C^{2}\left({\rm e}^{-\mu^{n}[\mu(\lambda(\theta+\eta)-\frac{(1+\alpha)\beta}{2})-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}-\lambda(\theta+\eta)+{o}(1)]}+\varepsilon_{n}^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{\eta^{2}}{C\varepsilon_{n+1}^{2\delta}}\right]\right)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle={o}(1)\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

Let
A_{n}
denote the complement of the event in the first line of the last display.
It follows that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{P}(A_{n}^{c})=0.

Because
\mathrm{P}\{A_{n}\text{\ \rm i.o.}\}\geqslant 1-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{P}(A_{n}^{c}),

it follows that, almost surely, infinitely many of the events
A_{n}
must occur.
Thus, we can deduce from Proposition
5.1
that

(6.10)

\displaystyle\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\limsup}_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{|H(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}=\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\limsup}_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\geqslant\theta\quad\text{a.s.}

Recall that
\theta
,
\eta
,
\alpha
,
\beta
satisfy (
6.4
).
Because
\lambda
is strictly decreasing and continuous
(see Proposition
2.5
), we can let
\eta\downarrow 0
,

\alpha\downarrow 0
, and then
\beta\downarrow\dimmm K
in order
to see that
(
6.10
) holds for all
\theta>0
such that
\lambda(\theta)>\tfrac{1}{2}\dimmm K
.
Take supremum over all such
\theta>0
in order to find that

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\limsup}_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{|H(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\geqslant\sup\{\theta>0:\lambda(\theta)>\tfrac{1}{2}\dimmm K\}\quad\text{a.s.}

Thanks to the monotonicity of
\lambda
(Proposition
2.5
),

\sup\{\theta>0:\lambda(\theta)>\tfrac{1}{2}\dimmm K\}=\inf\{\theta>0:\lambda(\theta)<\tfrac{1}{2}\dimmm K\},

and both of these quantities are equal to
\lambda^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}\dimmm K)
,
where
\lambda^{-1}(0)=\infty
.
This completes the proof of the lower bound for Theorem
6.1
.

∎

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
6.1
: Upper bound

We now turn to the proof of the upper bound.

Proof.

We may, and will assume without loss of generality that
\dimh K>0

for there is nothing to prove otherwise. Let us fix two numbers

\beta,\theta>0
such that

2\lambda(\theta)<\beta<\dimh K.

By Frostman’s lemma (see Falconer
[
11
]
), there exists
a probability measure
\mu
on
K
such that

(6.11)

\displaystyle\adjustlimits{\sup}_{r>0}{\sup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu([x-r\,,x+r])}{r^{\beta}}<\infty.

Fix
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
such that

(6.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda(\theta)}{\beta}<\frac{1-\alpha}{2}<\frac{1}{2},\qquad\text{set}\quad\rho=\frac{1-\alpha}{2},

and recall the localization
\{H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)\}_{t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}}

of
H
from (
5.1
).
For every
\varepsilon_{2}>\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, define the event

\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,x)=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\ \sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|(\omega)}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}.

The spatial stationarity of
H_{\alpha}
and Lemma
5.6
together
imply that

(6.13)

\displaystyle\forall x\in K,\quad\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,x))=\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,0))=(\varepsilon_{1}/\varepsilon_{2})^{\lambda(\theta)+{o}(1)},

as
\max\{\varepsilon_{2}\,,\varepsilon_{1}/\varepsilon_{2}\}\to 0^{+}
.
For every
\varepsilon_{2}>\varepsilon_{1}>0
, consider the random variable

X_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}=\int_{K}\frac{\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},x)}}{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},x))}\mu({\rm d}x).

Then,
\mathrm{E}(X_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}})=1
.
We wish to prove that
\mathrm{E}(X_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}^{2})\leqslant 1+{o}(1)
as
\max\{\varepsilon_{2}\,,\varepsilon_{1}/\varepsilon_{2}\}\to 0^{+}
.
By the first identity in (
6.13
),

\mathrm{E}(X_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}^{2})=\iint_{K\times K}\frac{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,x)\cap\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{2}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,y))}{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,0))^{2}}\,\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y).

We may decompose
\mathrm{E}(X_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}^{2})
as follows:

\mathrm{E}(X_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}^{2})=I_{1}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2})+I_{2}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2})+I_{3}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}),

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=I_{1}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2})=\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in K:\\
|x-y|\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,x)\cap\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,y))}{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,0))^{2}}\,\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y),

\displaystyle I_{2}=I_{2}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2})=\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in K:\\
\varepsilon_{1}^{\rho}<|x-y|\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{2}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,x)\cap\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,y))}{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,0))^{2}}\,\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y),

\displaystyle I_{3}=I_{3}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2})=\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in K:\\
|x-y|>2\varepsilon_{2}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,x)\cap\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,y))}{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,0))^{2}}\,\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y).

The trivial bound
\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap\mathcal{E}_{2})\leqslant\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}_{1})

is valid for all events
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
.
This, together with (
6.11
), (
6.12
) and (
6.13
), yields

I_{1}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{1}^{\beta\rho}}{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,0))}=\varepsilon_{1}^{\beta\rho-\lambda(\theta)+{o}(1)}\varepsilon_{2}^{\lambda(\theta)+{o}(1)}={o}(1)\quad\text{as $\max\{\varepsilon_{2}\,,\varepsilon_{1}/\varepsilon_{2}\}\to 0^{+}$.}

Next,
I_{3}
can be estimated as follows: If
|x-y|>\varepsilon_{2}^{\rho}
then,
by the properties of Wiener integrals,
\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,x)

and
\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,y)
are independent. Therefore,

I_{3}\leqslant 1.

Finally, we may write

\displaystyle I_{2}=\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in K:\\
\varepsilon_{1}^{\rho}<|x-y|\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{2}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,y)\mid\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,x)\right)}{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,0))}\,\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,0))}\times

\displaystyle\times\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in K:\\
\varepsilon_{1}^{\rho}<|x-y|\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{2}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{P}\left(\left.\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{1},|x-y|^{1/\rho}]}|H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|\leqslant\theta\ \,\right|\,\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}]}|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|\leqslant\theta\right)\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y).

Once again by the properties of Wiener integrals,

\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{1},|x-y|^{1/\rho}]}|H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|

is independent from
\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}]}|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|
.
Therefore,

I_{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,0))}\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in K:\\
\varepsilon_{1}^{\rho}<|x-y|\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{2}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{1},|x-y|^{1/\rho}]}|H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|\leqslant\theta\right\}\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y).

By the Gaussian correlation inequality of Royen
[
31
]
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{1},|x-y|^{1/\rho}]}|H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|\leqslant\theta\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}]}|H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|\leqslant\theta\right\}}{\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in[|x-y|^{1/\rho},\varepsilon_{2}]}|H_{\alpha}(t\,,y)|\leqslant\theta\right\}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon_{1}\,,\varepsilon_{2}\,,0))}{\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in[|x-y|^{1/\rho},\varepsilon_{2}]}|H(t\,,y)|\leqslant\theta\right\}},

where the last inequality follows from the spatial stationarity of
H_{\alpha}

and Anderson’s shifted-ball inequality
[
2
]
.
Fix
\delta>0
such that

(6.14)

\frac{\lambda(\theta)+\delta}{\rho}<\beta.

It follows from the above and Proposition
2.5
that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in K:\\
\varepsilon_{1}^{\rho}<|x-y|\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{2}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y)}{\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in[|x-y|^{1/\rho},\varepsilon_{2}]}|H(t\,,y)|\leqslant\theta\right\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in K:\\
\varepsilon_{1}^{\rho}<|x-y|\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{2}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{|x-y|^{1/\rho}}\right)^{\lambda(\theta)+\delta}\,\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y)={o}(1)\iint_{K\times K}\frac{\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y)}{|x-y|^{(\lambda(\theta)+\delta)/\rho}},

uniformly for all
small enough numbers
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}
that satisfy

0<\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}
.
It is well known that the preceding integral is finite thanks to (
6.11
)
and (
6.14
). Indeed, let
C=\sup_{a\in K}\inf_{b\in K}|a-b|

denote the diameter of
K
and define
a=(\lambda(\theta)+\delta)/\rho

in order to deduce from (
6.14
) that

\displaystyle\iint_{K\times K}\frac{\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y)}{|x-y|^{a}}\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{na}\iint_{C2^{-n-1}\leqslant|x-y|\leqslant C2^{-n}}\,\mu({\rm d}x)\,\mu({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{na}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\mu\left(\left[y-C2^{-n}\penalty\ ,\penalty\ y+C2^{-n}\right]\right)\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-n(\beta-a)}<\infty.

Thus, it follows that
I_{2}={o}(1)
as
\varepsilon_{2}\to 0^{+}
,
whence
\mathrm{E}(X_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}^{2})\leqslant 1+{o}(1)

as
\max\{\varepsilon_{2}\,,\varepsilon_{1}/\varepsilon_{2}\}\to 0^{+}
.
Therefore, we may use the Paley-Zygmund inequality and combine the above estimates to deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{X_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}>0\}\geqslant\frac{[\mathrm{E}(X_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}})]^{2}}{\mathrm{E}(X_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}^{2})}=1+{o}(1)\quad\text{as $\max\{\varepsilon_{2}\,,\varepsilon_{1}/\varepsilon_{2}\}\to 0^{+}$.}

Choose and fix a decreasing sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

that satisfies
\max\{\varepsilon_{n}\,,\varepsilon_{n+1}/\varepsilon_{n}\}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.
Then the preceding implies that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcap_{m\geqslant n}\left\{\exists\,x\in K\,,\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{m+1},\varepsilon_{m}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}\right)\geqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{P}\{X_{\varepsilon_{n+1},\varepsilon_{n}}>0\}=1.

This proves that there is a
\mathrm{P}
-null set off which there exists

\mathfrak{N}=\mathfrak{N}(\omega)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
U_{N,\mathfrak{N}}(\theta)\neq\varnothing

for all
N>\mathfrak{N}
, where
U_{N,n}(\theta)
denotes the random subset of
K

that is defined as

\displaystyle U_{N,n}(\theta)=\left\{x\in K:\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon_{N},\varepsilon_{n}]}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\right\}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\ N>n.

By the continuity of
H_{\alpha}
, every
U_{N,n}(\theta)

is a closed subset of the compact set
K
, and
N\mapsto U_{N,n}(\theta)

is decreasing with respect to set inclusion. Consequently,

\cap_{N\in\mathbb{N}:N>n}U_{N,n}(\theta)\neq\varnothing
(Cantor’s theorem).
This, together with Proposition
5.1
, implies
that there exists
x^{*}\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\cap_{N\in\mathbb{N}:N>n}U_{N,n}(\theta)
such that

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\limsup}_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{|H(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{|H_{\alpha}(t\,,x^{*})|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\quad\text{a.s.}

Recall that
\lambda:(0\,,\infty)\to(0\,,\infty)
is strictly decreasing and
\lambda(\theta)\to 0
as
\theta\to\infty

(see Proposition
2.5
).
Therefore, we may let
\beta\uparrow\dimh K
,
\alpha\uparrow 1

and
\delta\downarrow 0
, all along rationals, in order to see that for every

\theta>0
that satisfies
\lambda(\theta)<\tfrac{1}{2}\dimh K
,

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\limsup}_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{|H(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\theta\quad\text{a.s.}

Finally, we may take infimum over all such
\theta>0
to
conclude that there is a
\mathrm{P}
-null set off which

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{x\in K}{\limsup}_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{|H(t\,,x)|}{t^{1/4}}\leqslant\inf\{\theta>0:\lambda(\theta)<\tfrac{1}{2}\dimh K\}=\lambda^{-1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\dimh K\right)\quad\text{a.s.}

This completes the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
∎

7.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
, Part (3)

We will prove the slightly weaker result that

(7.1)

\mathrm{P}\left\{\dimh\fS(\theta)=1-2\lambda(\theta)\right\}=1\qquad\forall\theta\geqslant\theta_{c};

see (
1.4
).
In fact, it follows from the above that

\mathrm{P}\left\{\dimh\fS(\theta)=1-2\lambda(\theta)\quad\forall\theta\in[\theta_{c}\,,\infty)\cap\mathbb{Q}_{+}\right\}=1,

whence it follows from monotonicity that, off a single null set,

1-2\sup_{r\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,\theta]}\lambda(r)\leqslant\dimh\fS(\theta)\leqslant 1-2\inf_{r\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[\theta,\infty]}\lambda(r)\qquad\forall\theta\geqslant\theta_{c}.

The quantities on the left and the right of the above are both
equal to
\lambda(\theta)
, thanks to the continuity
of
\lambda
(Proposition
2.5
). Therefore, it remains to prove (
7.1
).

The proof of (
7.1
)
hinges on a codimension argument, as was first introduced by
Taylor
[
32
, Theorem 4]
.
For every
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
let
\{X_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geqslant 0}
denote
a symmetric
\alpha
-stable Lévy process that is independent of the
random field
u
in (
1.1
).

Define
R(\alpha)
to be the closure of the random set

X_{\alpha}[0\,,1]
. Every
R(\alpha)
is a random compact set since

X_{\alpha}
has càdlàg paths.
One can deduce from the potential theory of Hunt
[
19
,
20
]

that, for every nonrandom Borel
(even analytic) set
L\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\mathrm{P}\{R(\alpha)\cap L\neq\varnothing\}>0
if and only if
there exists a probability measure
\mu
on
L
such that

\iint|a-b|^{-1+\alpha}\,\mu({\rm d}a)\,\mu({\rm d}b)<\infty
.
This and Frostman’s theorem – see Falconer
[
11
]
– together yield

(7.2)

\mathrm{P}\left\{R(\alpha)\cap L\neq\varnothing\right\}\begin{cases}>0&\text{if $\dimh L>1-\alpha$,}\\
=0&\text{if $\dimh L<1-\alpha$}.\end{cases}

This is another way to say that the “codimension” of the random set

R(\alpha)
is
1-\alpha
.

It is also a well-known fact that

(7.3)

\dimh R(\alpha)=\dimmm R(\alpha)=\alpha\quad\text{a.s.}

The announced formula for
\dimh R(\alpha)
follows from
Theorem 4.2 of Blumenthal and Getoor

[
5
]
, and the one for
\dimmm R(\alpha)

from the fact that the packing dimension
\dimp
of
R(\alpha)

is
1-\alpha
a.s. – see Taylor

[
33
]
– and the general fact that
\dimh\leqslant\dimmm A\leqslant\dimp A

for every
A\subset\mathbb{R}
; see Falconer
[
11
]
.

Now we apply a codimension argument as follows: We apply
Part (2) of Theorem
1.1
,
by first conditioning on
X_{\alpha}
, and then appeal to (
7.3
) in order to see that

\mathrm{P}\left\{\fS(\theta)\cap R(\alpha)\neq\varnothing\right\}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\lambda(\theta)<\alpha/2$},\\
0&\text{if $\lambda(\theta)>\alpha/2$},\end{cases}

for every
\theta>0
and
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
. At the same time,
(
7.2
) also implies that, for every
\alpha\in(0\,,1)
fixed,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\fS(\theta)\cap R(\alpha)\neq\varnothing\mid u\,\right)>0\penalty\ \text{a.s.\penalty\ on
$\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\ \dimh\fS(\theta)(\omega)>1-\alpha\right\}$},

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\fS(\theta)\cap R(\alpha)\neq\varnothing\mid u\,\right)=0\penalty\ \text{a.s.\penalty\ on
$\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\ \dimh\fS(\theta)(\omega)<1-\alpha\right\}$}.

Together, the preceding two displays yield (
7.1
),
whence also Theorem
1.1
(3).
∎

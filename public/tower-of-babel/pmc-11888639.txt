INTRODUCTION

Health behavior is widely influenced by behavior, attitudes, and knowledge, which impacts oral health.[1] Mostly, individuals with poor oral hygiene have been found to be consuming more sugar.[2]

Behavioral dietary factors like time of sugar consumption, extent, and frequency are related to the development of dental caries.[3] Brushing twice daily, good oral hygiene, and healthy habits are crucial in preventing gingival disease and maintaining oral health.[4] The people who brushed less than twice a day were found in higher numbers in low- and middle-income countries than in higher-income countries.[567891011]

Social and behavioral factors have a profound impact on oral health. Dental anxiety, feeding, pain management, treatment adherence, nutrition, corporate marketing, socioeconomic factors, access to medical services, dental insurance, and oral hygiene practices are some of the social and oral health-related issues.[12]

The present study aims to assess the behavioral practices and tobacco usage of coal mine workers in Tamil Nadu toward oral health.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The current study was done to assess social behavior and behavior toward oral health. The size of the whole population of mine workers in NLC was 2300. A simple random sampling technique was employed to recruit 309 Mine I workers and 294 Mine II workers. Data was collected from a total of 603 study subjects. From each of Mines I and II, 315 subjects were recruited.

The data was collected from the subjects after explaining the purpose of the study using a pre-tested questionnaire. A pre-tested questionnaire was used to collect data pertaining to oral health-related behavior and oral hygiene practices. Tobacco usage history was collected from the workers. Simple random sampling was used to collect the data from the subjects.

The data was tabulated in a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet. The data was represented as graphs and tables. The responses to the questionnaire were filled by the subjects themselves. If the subjects were not able to comprehend the questions in the questionnaire, the principal investigator briefed them with the details.

Descriptive statistics were expressed in tables and charts. Statistical analysis was performed with (SPSS version 19 IBM, USA). Based on the nature of the data, statistical analysis was used.

A Chi-square test was used to express frequencies.The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to check for normality.An independent sample t-test was used to test the difference in mean of continuous variables.

In Mine I, 58 (18.8%) subjects used finger and tooth powder for cleaning their teeth, while 6 (2%) used finger and toothpaste and two tooth brushes and powder. In Mine II, it was 52 (17.7%), 7 (2.4%), and 10 (3.4%), respectively. DISCUSSION

In the current study, 89.7% Mine I workers had cleaned their teeth once daily, while similarly, in the study by Amith et al.[13] in Moradadabad, among brass industry workers, the results documented 93% of participants brushing their teeth once daily, and 6.6% brushed twice daily. In the other study by Shakthi et al.,[14] among construction workers, 94.4% of them brushed their teeth once daily and 5.6% brushed twice daily. Subjects are similar to the population of other workers with respect to frequency, brushing a minimum of once daily.

In the present study, Mine I, the subjects who used finger and tooth powder for cleaning their teeth were 18.8%, those using finger and toothpaste were 2%, and tooth brushes and powder were 0.6%, whereas in Mine II, it was 17.7%, 2.4%, and 3.4%, respectively. These findings were not in line with the study conducted by Amith et al.[13] (2013) in Moradabad among brass industry workers, where 5.8% cleaned their teeth using a finger.

The study by Shakthi et al.[14] among construction workers found that 74.5% of them used toothbrushes and toothpaste, 5.3% of them used finger and tooth powder, 1.5% of them used finger and toothpaste, and 1.4% of them used toothbrushes and tooth powder to clean their teeth.

CONCLUSION

Further studies are needed to assess the knowledge of all dimensions pertaining to oral health among miners in other states of India.

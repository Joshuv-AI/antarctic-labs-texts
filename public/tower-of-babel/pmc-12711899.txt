Selected risk factor analysis for coalbed methane development with optimized fuzzy based knowledge mapping at Jamalganj coalfield in Bangladesh
Authors: Jianhua Zhang, Md Helal Miah, Muin Abdullah Al
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This research addresses significant gaps in coalbed methane development risk assessment, traditionally focused narrowly on economic aspects. Introducing an advanced “Fuzzy Cognitive Map” (FCM) model integrated with “Interpretive Structural Modelling” (ISM) and triangular fuzzy numbers, this study offers a robust framework, exemplified in Jamalganj case study in Bangladesh. The study uses the ISM knowledge-mapping algorithm to analyze relationships between indicator nodes. Starting with a direct relationship matrix, the algorithm constructs a comprehensive relationship matrix that includes both direct and indirect connections. This involves identifying direct influences between nodes and their permutations in the transposed matrix. The algorithm’s iterative process ensures a thorough mapping of interactions, resulting in a nuanced understanding of the system’s structure and dynamics. This method enhances the precision of relationship modeling and risk assessment. The fuzzy cognitive model analysis reveals that the risk value for coalbed methane development in Jamalganj is 0.665, indicating a medium level of social ecological risk. Early stages show high pollutant concentration values, while the middle stage highlights production mechanization and occupational hazards. In the late stage, economic loss becomes the dominant factor, requiring focused management to mitigate its impact. Integrating ISM knowledge mapping and triangular fuzzy numbers into the FCM model, refined by the nonlinear Hebbian learning algorithm, enhances environmental and socio-economic risk assessments in coalbed methane development, aiding policymakers in sustainable resource management.

Introduction

Risk factor analysis for “Coalbed Methane” (CBM) development is a critical process that evaluates potential hazards and uncertainties associated with exploration, extraction, and production. The primary risks include geological uncertainties, such as reservoir permeability, gas content, and coal seam thickness, which significantly impact extraction efficiency. Technical risks involve drilling complexities, wellbore stability, and the effectiveness of dewatering processes required to release methane from coal seams. Environmental concerns, including groundwater contamination, subsidence, and greenhouse gas emissions, pose additional challenges, necessitating stringent regulatory compliance and mitigation strategies. Economic risks, such as fluctuating gas prices, high initial investment costs, and market demand variability, influence the financial viability of CBM projects. Additionally, legal and regulatory risks arise from land acquisition issues, mineral rights disputes, and evolving environmental policies. A comprehensive risk assessment framework incorporating geological modeling, technological advancements, environmental impact studies, and economic forecasting is essential for optimizing CBM development while minimizing potential setbacks. The Jamalganj in Bangladesh holds significant CBM potential, with reserves found at shallow depths of 640 to 1158 m, totaling around 1053 million tons. Efficient extraction of these resources can ease the country’s energy shortage and reduce reliance on traditional fuels. Developing CBM in a responsible manner will strengthen Bangladesh’s energy security, support economic growth, and contribute to a more sustainable energy future. This initiative can also help balance energy supply and demand while addressing environmental concerns1. Unlike other coalfields, the Jamalganj’s geological characteristics make it less suitable for traditional coal mining but highly favorable for CBM extraction, offering an opportunity to utilize an otherwise untapped resource.

Despite its promising potential, the development of CBM in Bangladesh is still in its infancy. As such, large-scale development could have notable impacts on the social and ecological environment in the areas of extraction. For instance, the process of extracting CBM often produces water with high salinity, which poses a risk to the local soil and vegetation. These environmental considerations need to be addressed to ensure that the benefits of CBM development do not come at the cost of ecological and social well-being2. Therefore, to ensure the long-term, stable, and green development of the coalbed methane industry and to maximize economic benefits, it is crucial to evaluate the social and ecological environmental risks associated with coalbed methane development.

According to review of existing literature, a significant research gap exists in the realm of coalbed methane development risk assessment. Most current studies are narrowly focused on the economic aspects, overlooking the broader scope necessary for a holistic understanding. The existing assessment methods fall short in exploring the intricate interactions between various risk factors, thereby failing to capture the dynamic nature of these risks. This limited approach neglects the critical interconnections and dependencies that play a crucial role in the overall risk landscape of coalbed methane development. Consequently, there is a pressing need for more comprehensive methodologies that encompass the multifaceted and evolving nature of these risks, providing a more robust framework for effective risk management and mitigation.

In light of the identified research gaps, this paper aims to revolutionize the risk assessment of coalbed methane development by addressing the shortcomings of the traditional “Fuzzy Cognitive Map” (FCM) model, particularly its challenges in determining correlation relationships and association matrices among indicators, as well as its inherent subjectivity. This study introduced a novel integration of the “Interpretive Structural Modelling” (ISM) knowledge mapping algorithm and triangular fuzzy numbers into the FCM model, enhancing its robustness and accuracy. By optimizing weights through the nonlinear Hebbian learning algorithm, a more comprehensive and stable fuzzy cognitive map model is established. The innovative approach is exemplified through a case study of the Jamalganj coalbed methane development area, where the advanced model inference mechanism is applied for a detailed risk assessment. This research not only fills the existing gaps but also sets a new benchmark in the field by offering a sophisticated, dynamic, and interconnected framework for evaluating environmental risks in coalbed methane development.

Problem formulation

The risk analysis of coalbed methane development based on Weighted Transitive Fuzzy Cognitive Mapping (WTFCM) introduces an advanced approach that addresses key limitations of traditional Fuzzy Cognitive Mapping (FCM)12 and Transitive Fuzzy Cognitive Mapping (TFCM)13. While FCM effectively models complex interdependencies among risk factors, it struggles with accurately quantifying the varying strengths of these relationships, leading to imprecise risk assessments. TFCM improves on this by incorporating transitive relationships but lacks a systematic weighting mechanism to reflect the relative significance of different factors. The proposed WTFCM method enhances risk analysis by integrating Interpretive Structural Modeling (ISM) to derive hierarchical dependencies and assigns weighted transitive influences, ensuring a more precise and structured representation of risk propagation. This refined approach enables a more reliable evaluation of coalbed methane development risks, offering a scientifically robust framework that mitigates the subjectivity and oversimplifications present in previous models.

This paper meticulously examines both direct and indirect relationships between indicators within the FCM framework, optimizing the correlation matrix using the nonlinear Hebbian learning algorithm. The Nonlinear Hebbian Learning Algorithm is an advanced form of Hebbian learning that extends the classical Hebbian principle by incorporating nonlinear functions to modulate synaptic weight updates. Unlike traditional Hebbian learning, which relies on a linear correlation between presynaptic and postsynaptic activities, nonlinear Hebbian learning introduces higher-order interactions, allowing for more complex and biologically plausible adaptations in artificial and neural networks. This algorithm is particularly useful in scenarios where simple Hebbian learning fails to capture intricate synaptic dynamics, such as competitive learning, independent component analysis, and self-organizing feature maps. By incorporating nonlinear activation functions, the algorithm enhances learning stability and enables neural systems to extract meaningful representations from high-dimensional data. This approach leads to the development of the Weighted Transitive Fuzzy Cognitive Mapping (WTFCM) model. By integrating these relationships, the WTFFM model achieves a more nuanced and accurate representation of the complex interactions between indicators, leading to improved performance and reliability in various applications.

Recommendations

The comparative evaluation of different models highlights the enhanced accuracy and stability of the WTFCM model, reinforcing its practical relevance for coalbed methane (CBM) development. The model’s ability to minimize standard deviation (σ) and coefficient of variation (nv) suggests that it provides a more reliable framework for assessing and managing risks associated with CBM operations. Policymakers can leverage these insights to establish more precise risk assessment protocols, ensuring that CBM projects are designed with a higher degree of safety and efficiency. The improved predictive capabilities of the WTFCM model allow for early identification of high-risk factors, enabling regulators to implement proactive mitigation strategies such as enhanced site monitoring, adaptive drilling techniques, and optimized resource allocation. Moreover, the integration of knowledge mapping and the nonlinear Hebbian learning algorithm ensures that risk assessments are not only data-driven but also adaptable to evolving environmental and operational conditions.

From an engineering perspective, the WTFCM model provides actionable insights that can inform decision-making at multiple levels of CBM development. By incorporating both direct and indirect risk relationships, engineers can prioritize interventions that address systemic vulnerabilities rather than isolated risks. The model’s superior stability suggests its potential for real-time risk monitoring, allowing operators to adjust extraction processes dynamically based on emerging threats. This can be particularly useful in mitigating hazards such as methane leakage, geological instability, and equipment failures. Additionally, policymakers can use the model’s outputs to refine regulatory frameworks, ensuring that safety standards are continuously updated to reflect the latest advancements in risk assessment methodologies. Overall, the WTFCM model not only enhances risk evaluation accuracy but also provides a strategic foundation for both policymakers and engineers to implement more effective, data-driven risk management practices in CBM development.

Conclusion

This study begins by integrating the knowledge mapping and triangular fuzzy numbers from Interpretive Structural Modeling (ISM) into the Fuzzy Cognitive Maps (FCM) model, subsequently refining weight optimization using the nonlinear Hebbian learning algorithm. The research focuses on the coalbed methane mining area of the Jamalganj as its primary case study, conducting simulation evaluations that incorporate the model’s reasoning mechanisms. Through these simulations, the study draws conclusive insights into the environmental and socio-economic impacts of coalbed methane development in the region. The findings aim to provide a comprehensive understanding of the complex interactions and risk assessments associated with such industrial activities, offering valuable insights for policy makers and stakeholders involved in sustainable resource management and environmental planning.

Through iterative simulation, upon reaching a stable state, it is determined that the SER value for the coalbed methane mining area in the Jamalganj is 0.665, categorizing its risk level as medium, which falls within a manageable range. The findings highlight that factor such as the economic loss ratio, production mechanization degree, casualty rate, and maximum ground concentration of major atmospheric pollutants exert significant influence on the area’s SER. These results underscore the importance of monitoring and managing these specific indicators to mitigate potential environmental impacts and ensure sustainable development practices in the region.

Throughout the entire coalbed methane development process, certain indicators demonstrate varying impacts on the SER. Conversely, indicators like production mechanization degree, reliability of mechanical equipment, and maximum ground concentration of major atmospheric pollutants maintain standard rates, while the economic loss ratio and casualty rate exert greater influence on environmental risks. These findings emphasize the critical need for targeted interventions and rigorous monitoring strategies tailored to each developmental phase to effectively mitigate environmental risks and ensure sustainable resource management practices.

Comparative analysis with traditional FCM, TFCM, and WTFCM models reveals that the WTTFCM model offers superior stability and accuracy in evaluating these risks. It effectively elucidates the interconnectedness of various indicators, providing clear insights into how factors such as economic loss ratios, mechanization degrees, and pollutant concentrations influence environmental risks throughout different stages of coalbed methane development. Future research implication

Despite the demonstrated superiority of the WTFCM model in capturing complex interdependencies between risk factors in CBM development, several limitations warrant further investigation. First, the model’s effectiveness heavily depends on the quality and completeness of input data. Any inconsistencies or biases in historical CBM incident reports or real-time monitoring systems may affect the reliability of risk assessments. Additionally, while the model accounts for both direct and indirect influences, it may still be sensitive to parameter selection and initial conditions, necessitating robust sensitivity analyses to enhance generalizability across different operational environments.

Future research should focus on extending the WTFCM model by integrating machine learning techniques to refine weight adjustments dynamically and improve predictive robustness. Moreover, cross-validation with diverse datasets from different geographic regions and operational conditions can help assess its adaptability. Incorporating real-time sensor data and IoT-based monitoring systems could further enhance the model’s capability to provide proactive risk assessments. Finally, developing a standardized framework for interpreting the model’s outputs in decision-making processes could facilitate its adoption by policymakers and industry stakeholders, ensuring practical implementation in CBM risk management.

Local Sherman’s Algorithm for Multi-commodity Flow
Jason Li, Thatchaphol Saranurak

Abstract
We give the first local algorithm for computing multi-commodity flow and apply it to obtain a ( 1 + ϵ ) (1+\epsilon) -approximate algorithm for computing a k k -commodity flow on an expander with m m edges in ( m + ϵ − 3 ​ k 3 ​ D ) ​ n o ⁡ ( 1 ) (m+\epsilon^{-3}k^{3}D)n^{o(1)} time, where D D is the total demand. This is the first ( 1 + ϵ ) (1+\epsilon) -approximate algorithm that breaks the k ​ m km multi-commodity flow barrier, albeit only on expanders. All previous algorithms either require Ω ⁡ ( k ​ m ) \Omega(km) time [ She17 ] or a big constant approximation [ HHL+24 ] . Our approach is by localizing Sherman’s flow algorithm [ She13 ] when put into the Multiplicative Weight Update (MWU) framework. We show that, on each round of MWU, the oracle could instead work with the rounded weights where all polynomially small weights are rounded to zero. Since there are only few large weights, one can implement the oracle call with respect to the rounded weights in sublinear time. This insight is generic and may be of independent interest.

The maximum flow problem is among the most central problems in combinatorial optimization. A long line of work

,

,

1.1
Our Techniques

Our starting point is Sherman’s initial (or “unaccelerated”)
\tilde{O}(m/\epsilon^{2})
time algorithm
[
She13
]
with a trivial “preconditioner”, which we interpret in the Multiplicative Weights Update framework. (We assume no prior background of Sherman’s algorithm, and therefore do not discuss the role of preconditioning in Sherman’s algorithm.)

At a high level, Sherman’s algorithm (with a trivial preconditioner) has a natural interpretation as follows. Each vertex has a
potential
that is initially
0
. On each of
O(\epsilon^{-2}\log n)
iterations, each edge independently sends
full
flow from the higher-potential endpoint to the lower-potential endpoint (with ties broken arbitrarily). Then, each vertex independently updates its potential based on how much flow it receives; note that potentials can be positive or negative. Intuitively, the more flow a vertex receives, the larger its new potential should be, since it should be more eager to push its flow outward on the next iteration. The actual update to potentials can be viewed as a step in Multiplicative Weights Update (MWU). Finally, at the end of the
O(\epsilon^{-2}\log n)
iterations, Sherman’s algorithm outputs the average of the flows computed on each iteration.

Unfortunately, Sherman’s algorithm is not local because on each iteration,
every
edge sends full flow in some direction. One possible modification is to send
no
flow along an edge whose endpoints have equal potential, so in particular, the first flow is the empty flow. This change still guarantees correctness, but vertices can have different potentials very quickly into the algorithm, so the flows in the later iterations may still use all edges. Our main insight is to
round
the potentials of vertices that are small enough in absolute value to
0
. Intuitively, vertices whose potentials are close to
0
have small flow excess or deficit (cumulatively until the current iteration), and since their potentials are all rounded to the same value, there is no flow sent between such vertices. This strategy is reminiscent of local push-relabel algorithms, where vertices with small enough excess do not push flow outward. Conversely, if a vertex has large enough potential to avoid rounding, then it must have large excess or deficit. We charge the running time of sending flow from this vertex to this excess or deficit in a way that ensures the total charge is bounded.

To show that rounding the potentials still preserves correctness, we prove a variant of MWU that allows for (additively)
approximate
multiplicative weights, which may be of independent interest.

1.2
Preliminaries

In this paper, all graphs are unit-capacity and undirected, and the input graph is denoted
G=(V,E)
. We assume that each edge

A

. A flow has

\kappa\geq 0

, and it is

if it has congestion
1
.

A

is a function

, also treated as a vector in

. While source functions in local algorithms are usually non-negative, we work with the more general setting of allowing negative sources. A

is a source function whose entries sum to zero. For a set of vertices

, denote
b(S)=\sum_{v\in S}b(v)
. Let

be the incidence matrix of

, defined as the matrix where column
(u,v)\in E

at row

, entry
-1
at row

, and entry
0
everywhere else. A flow
routes

Bf=b
. A source function

is

if there is no feasible flow
f

b
.

Given a vertex set
S\subseteq V
, let

be the set of edges in

with exactly one endpoint in

, and let

.
The following fact, based on flow-cut duality, establishes a

Fact 1.3

For any source function

Suppose for contradiction that there is a feasible flow

∎

A

-commodity flow

. A

-commodity demand

-commodity source function whose columns

-commodity flow

For a vector or matrix

2
Local Sherman’s Algorithm

We begin with a quick refresher intended for readers familiar with Sherman’s algorithm. Recall that it uses a
congestion approximator
, which is a collection of cuts that approximates the worst cut in any demand. In a
\phi
-expander, the set of
degree
cuts is a
1/\phi
-approximate congestion approximator. Even if the graph is not a
\phi
-expander, we can still use the trivial congestion approximator in Sherman’s algorithm. The guarantee we obtain is that each vertex has small residual demand (proportional to its degree), but the residual demand may not be routable with low congestion if the graph is not an expander.

2.1
Single-commodity

In this section, we present a single-commodity version as a warm-up. We believe that even the single-commodity case is interesting for our local algorithm, and the goal of this section is to highlight our new ideas in a relatively simple setting.

Theorem 2.1

(Local Sherman: Single-commodity)
.

There is an algorithm that, given a unit-capacity undirected graph
G=(V,E)
, any source function
b\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
, and error parameter
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, outputs either

1.

A certificate of infeasibility
S\subseteq V
with
|b(S)|>\delta S
, or

2.

A flow
\bar{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{E}
routing demand
b-r
for some (residual) source function
r\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
satisfying
|r(v)|\leq\epsilon\deg(v)
for all
v\in V
.

The algorithm runs in time
O(\epsilon^{-2}\log n\cdot(\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+\epsilon^{-1}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}))
. Moreover, if an infeasibility certificate
S\subseteq V
is returned, then
\textbf{{vol}}(S)\leq O(\epsilon^{-1}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1})
.

Recall that Sherman’s algorithm maintains vertex
potentials
and on each iteration, sends flow across each edge from higher potential to lower potential. Our main idea is to
round
potentials that are small enough (in absolute value) to
0
. More precisely, we write the potentials as a difference of (nonnegative) multiplicative weights, and separately round each multiplicative weight to
0
if it is small enough.

In the algorithm below, we highlight in
blue
the effects of rounding potentials and how they play into the Multiplicative Weights Update analysis.

Main Algorithm.

Let
b\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
be the input source function. Initialize weights
w_{v,+}^{1}=w^{1}_{v,-}=1
for each vertex
v\in V
. For round
i=1,2,\ldots,T
where
T=\Theta(\alpha^{-2}\log n)
for some
\alpha\leq 1/4
, we do the following.

1.

Define the
rounded
weights
\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,\pm}
as

\tilde{w}_{v,\pm}^{i}=\begin{cases}w_{v,\pm}^{i}&\text{if }w_{v,\pm}^{i}\geq n\\
0&\text{if }w_{v,\pm}^{i}<n.\\
\end{cases}

In other words,
\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,\pm}
is obtained by rounding low values to
0
.

2.

Define the
rounded
potential
\tilde{\phi}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
as

\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}=\frac{\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,+}-\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,-}}{\deg(v)}.

3.

Define flow
f^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{E}
such that for each edge
(u,v)
,
f^{i}(u,v)
flows from higher to lower
rounded
potential at maximum capacity. That is, for every edge
(u,v)\in E
,

f^{i}(u,v)=\begin{cases}+1&\text{if }\tilde{\phi}_{u}^{i}>\tilde{\phi}_{v}^{i}\\
-1&\text{if }\tilde{\phi}_{u}^{i}<\tilde{\phi}_{v}^{i}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

4.

If
\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},b\rangle>\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},Bf^{i}\rangle
, then terminate and return a certificate of infeasibility
S\subseteq V
with
|b(S)|>\delta S
, to be described later.

5.

For each
v\in V
, let

r_{v}^{i}=\frac{b(v)-(Bf^{i})_{v}}{\deg(v)},

be the
relative total excess
at
v
compared to the degree in round
i
.

6.

Update the (real) weights

w^{i+1}_{v,\pm}=w^{i}_{v,\pm}(1\pm\alpha r_{v}^{i}).

Infeasibility certificate.

If the termination condition holds, then we show that there is an infeasibility certificate
S\subseteq V
with
|b(S)|>\delta S
, where
S
has the following form:

Lemma 2.2

(Infeasibility certificate)
.

Suppose that
\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},b\rangle>\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},Bf^{i}\rangle
on some iteration
i
. For real number
x
, define
V_{>x}=\{v\in V:\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}>x\}
and
V_{<x}=\{v\in V:\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}>x\}
. There exists an infeasibility certificate
S\subseteq V
(i.e.,
|b(S)|>\delta S
) of the form
S=V_{>x}
for some
x\geq 0
or
S=V_{<x}
for some
x\leq 0
.

Proof.

We prove the chain of relations

\int_{x=0}^{\infty}b(V_{>x})dx-\int_{x=-\infty}^{0}b(V_{<x})dx=\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},b\rangle>\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},Bf^{i}\rangle=\int_{x=0}^{\infty}\delta(V_{>x})dx+\int_{x=0}^{-\infty}\delta(V_{<x})dx.

(1)

We first show how to finish the proof assuming (
1
): we have either

\int_{x=0}^{\infty}b(V_{>x})dx>\int_{x=0}^{\infty}\delta(V_{>x})dx\qquad\text{or}\qquad-\int_{x=-\infty}^{0}b(V_{<x})>\int_{x=0}^{-\infty}\delta(V_{<x})dx.

In the former case, there must exist
x\geq 0
with
b(V_{>x})>\delta(V_{>x})
, so
V_{>x}
is our desired set. In the latter case, there must exist
x\leq 0
with
-b(V_{<x})>\delta(V_{<x})
, so
V_{<x}
is our desired set.

To prove the chain of relations (
1
), we start with

\displaystyle\int_{x=0}^{\infty}b(V_{>x})dx-\int_{x=-\infty}^{0}b(V_{<x})dx

\displaystyle=\int_{x=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{v\in V}b(v)\cdot\mathbbm{1}\{\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}>x\}\right)dx-\int_{x=-\infty}^{0}\left(\sum_{v\in V}b(v)\cdot\mathbbm{1}\{\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}<x\}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V}b(v)\left(\int_{x=0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}\{\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}>x\}dx-\int_{x=-\infty}^{0}\mathbbm{1}\{\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}<x\}dx\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V}b(v)\cdot(\max\{0,\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}\}-\max\{0,-\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V}b(v)\cdot\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}

\displaystyle=\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},b\rangle.

By definition of the flow
f^{i}
,

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},Bf^{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\big|\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{u}-\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}\big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\int_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}\{(u,v)\in\partial(V_{>x})\}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\mathbbm{1}\{(u,v)\in\partial(V_{>x})\}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(V_{>x})dx

\displaystyle=\int_{x=-\infty}^{0}\delta(V_{>x})dx+\int_{x=0}^{\infty}\delta(V_{>x})dx

\displaystyle=\int_{x=-\infty}^{0}\delta(V_{<x})dx+\int_{x=0}^{\infty}\delta(V_{>x})dx.

Together with the assumption
\langle\phi^{i},b\rangle>\langle\phi^{i},Bf^{i}\rangle
,
this establishes (
1
).
∎

Multiplicative Weights Update analysis.

While Sherman’s vanilla algorithm can be analyzed by standard Multiplicative Weights Update (MWU), we need a more refined MWU analysis that allows for (additively)
approximate
multiplicative weights when calculating the gain on each iteration.

Theorem 2.3

(MWU with Approximate Weights)
.

Let
J

be a set of indices, let
\alpha\leq 1/4
be an error parameter, and let
\nu
be a value parameter.
Consider the following algorithm:

1.

Set
w_{j}^{1}\leftarrow 1
for all
j\in J

2.

For
i=1,2,\ldots,T
where
T=O(\alpha^{-2}\log|J|)
:

(a)

The algorithm is given a “gain” vector
g^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{J}

satisfying
\|g^{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 2

and an approximate weight vector
\tilde{w}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{J}
satisfying
\left\lVert w^{i}-\tilde{w}^{i}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq|J|^{O(1)}
and
\langle g^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}\rangle\leq 0

(b)

For each
j\in J
, set
w_{j}^{i+1}\leftarrow w_{j}^{i}(1+\alpha g_{j}^{i})=\prod_{i^{\prime}\in[i]}(1+\alpha g^{i^{\prime}}_{j})

At the end of the algorithm, we have
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i\in[T]}g_{j}^{i}\leq 5\alpha

for all
j\in J
.

Proof.

Let
W^{i}=\sum_{j\in J}w^{i}_{j}
be the total weight of all experts at the end of iteration
i
. For any
j\in J
, we have

\displaystyle W^{T+1}\geq w^{T+1}_{j}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in[T]}(1+\alpha g^{i}_{j})

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{i\in[T]}\exp(\alpha g^{i}_{j}-(\alpha g^{i}_{j})^{2})

\displaystyle\text{since }|\alpha g^{i}_{j}|\leq 1/2

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(\alpha\sum_{i\in[T]}g^{i}_{j}-\alpha^{2}\sum_{i\in[T]}(g^{i}_{j})^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\bigg(\alpha\sum_{i\in[T]}g^{i}_{j}-4\alpha^{2}T\bigg).

\displaystyle\text{since }|g^{i}_{j}|\leq 2

On the other hand, for all
i\in[T]
, we have

\displaystyle W^{i+1}=\sum_{j\in J}w^{i+1}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J}w^{i}_{j}(1+\alpha g^{i}_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J}(\tilde{w}^{i}_{j}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}|J|^{O(1)}})(1+\alpha g^{i}_{j})

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\text{since }\left\lVert w^{i}-\tilde{w}^{i}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq|J|^{O(1)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J}\tilde{w}^{i}_{j}(1+\alpha g^{i}_{j})+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}|J|^{O(1)}\sum_{j\in J}(1+\alpha g^{i}_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J}\tilde{w}^{i}_{j}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}|J|^{O(1)}\sum_{j\in J}(1+\alpha g^{i}_{j})}

\displaystyle\text{since }\langle g^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}\rangle\leq 0

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J}w^{i}_{j}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}|J|^{O(1)}\cdot 1.5|J|}.

\displaystyle\text{since }|\alpha g^{i}_{j}|\leq 1/2

Combining the two chains of inequalities, we obtain

\exp\bigg(\alpha\sum_{i\in[T]}g^{i}_{j}-4\alpha^{2}T\bigg)\leq W^{T+1}\leq\sum_{j\in J}w^{1}_{j}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1.5T|J|^{O(1)}}=|J|+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1.5T|J|^{O(1)}}.

It follows that

\alpha\sum_{i\in[T]}g^{i}_{j}\leq\ln(|J|+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1.5T|J|^{O(1)}})+4\alpha^{2}T\leq 5\alpha^{2}T

for large enough
T=O(\alpha^{-2}\log|J|)
, and dividing both sides by
\alpha T
finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that the input source function
b
satisfies
|b(v)|\leq\deg(v)
for all
v\in V
. For each
i
, we have
\|g^{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 2
,
\left\lVert w^{i}-\tilde{w}^{i}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq n
, and
\langle g^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}\rangle\leq 0
if the algorithm does not terminate with an infeasibility certificate.

Proof.

To show
\|g^{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 2
, we have

|g^{i}_{v,\pm}|=|r^{i}_{v,\pm}|=\left|\frac{b(v)-(Bf^{i})_{v}}{\deg(v)}\right|\leq\left|\frac{b(v)}{\deg(v)}\right|+\left|\frac{f^{i}(v)}{\deg(v)}\right|\leq 1+1.

To see why the last inequality holds, we have
|b(v)|\leq\deg(v)

for all
v\in V
by assumption, and
|f^{i}(v)|\leq\deg(v)

because each
f^{i}
is feasible.

To show
\left\lVert w^{i}-\tilde{w}^{i}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq n
, observe that for each
v\in V
, we have
|w^{i}_{v,\pm}-\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,\pm}|\leq n
from the construction of
\tilde{w}^{i}
.

To show
\langle g^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}\rangle\leq 0
, observe that for each
v\in V
,

g^{i}_{v,+}\cdot\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,+}+g^{i}_{v,-}\cdot\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,-}=\frac{b(v)-(Bf^{i})_{v}}{\deg(v)}\cdot(\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,+}-\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,-})=(b(v)-(Bf^{i})_{v})\cdot\tilde{\phi}_{v},

so
\langle g^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}\rangle=\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},b-Bf^{i}\rangle
. Since the algorithm does not terminate with an infeasibility certificate, we must have
\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},b\rangle\leq\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},Bf^{i}\rangle
, so
\langle g^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}\rangle\leq 0
.
∎

From the above, we have verified that our algorithm is indeed captured
by the MWU algorithm. Next, define the average flow
\bar{f}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}f^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{E}
. We now prove the two-sided error guarantee
|b(v)-(B\bar{f})_{v}|\leq 5\alpha\deg(v)
.

Lemma 2.5

(Approximation)
.

The flow
\bar{f}
satisfies
|b(v)-(B\bar{f})_{v}|\leq 5\alpha\deg(v)
for all
v\in V
. In other words,
\bar{f}
satisfies input source function
b
up to two-sided error
5\alpha\deg(v)
.

Proof.

Define
\bar{r}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}r^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
.
First, we prove that
|\bar{r}_{v}|\leq 5\alpha
for all
v\in V
.
This is because

\pm\bar{r}_{v}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i\in[T]}\pm r_{v}^{i}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i\in[T]}g^{i}_{v,\pm}\leq 5\alpha,

where the last inequality is precisely the guarantee of the MWU algorithm
from
Theorem

2.3
. By definition of
\bar{r}
,

\bar{r}_{v}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i\in[T]}r_{v}^{i}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i\in[T]}\frac{b(v)-(Bf^{i})_{v}}{\deg(v)}=\frac{b(v)-(B\bar{f})_{v}}{\deg(v)},

and together with
|\bar{r}_{v}|\leq 5\alpha
concludes the proof.
∎

Running time bound.

Finally, we analyze the running time, which we show only depends on
\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}
and
\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}
. We remark that everything so far generalizes to capacitated graphs, and the running time bound is the only place that requires a unit-capacity graph.

To avoid clutter, define
f^{\leq i}=f^{1}+\cdots+f^{i}
and
r^{\leq i}=r^{1}+\cdots+r^{i}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}>0
, then
r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\geq\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}
. Similarly, if
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}<0
, then
r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\leq-\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}
.

Proof.

If
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}>0
, then since
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}=\deg(v)\cdot(\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,+}-\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,-})>0
, we have
\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,+}>0
, so
w^{i}_{v,+}\geq n
. Similarly, if
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}<0
, then since
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}=\deg(v)\cdot(\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,+}-\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,-})<0
, we have
\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,-}>0
, so
w^{i}_{v,-}\geq n
. In both cases, we have

n\leq w^{i}_{v,\pm}=\prod_{i^{\prime}\leq i-1}(1\pm\alpha r^{i^{\prime}}_{v})\leq\prod_{i^{\prime}\leq i-1}\exp(\pm\alpha r^{i^{\prime}}_{v})=\exp(\pm\alpha r^{\leq i-1}_{v}),

and taking the logarithm and diving by
\alpha
gives
\pm r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\geq\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}
, establishing both cases.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For each iteration
i
, we have
\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i}_{v}|\cdot\deg(v)\leq i\cdot\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}
.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction on
i\geq 0
, with the trivial base case
i=0
. Suppose by induction that
\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}|\cdot\deg(v)\leq(i-1)\cdot\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}
. For each vertex
v\in V
, we have

r^{\leq i}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)=r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)+r^{i}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)=r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)+b(v)-(Bf^{i})_{v}.

Therefore,

\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i}_{v}|\cdot\deg(v)=\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)+b(v)-(Bf^{i})_{v}|\leq\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)-(Bf^{i})_{v}|+\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}.

Our goal is to show that

\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)-(Bf^{i})_{v}|\leq\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)|,

which together with the inductive assumption completes the induction. To establish the above inequality, imagine carrying out the flow
f^{i}
edge-by-edge. More formally, enumerate the edges of
E
in some arbitrary order
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
, and let
E_{\leq\ell}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
be the first
\ell
edges. Let
f^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell}}
be the flow
f^{i}
restricted to edges
E_{\leq\ell}
, i.e., zero out all coordinates outside
E_{\leq\ell}
. We view
f^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell}}
as the flow
f^{i}
with the first
\ell
edges carried out. Since
f^{i}|_{E_{\leq 0}}=0
and
f^{i}|_{E_{\leq m}}=f^{i}
, it suffices to show that the value

\Phi(\ell)=\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell}})_{v}|

is monotonically decreasing as
\ell
increases from
0
to
m
. For a given
\ell\in[m]
, write
e_{\ell}=(u,v)
. If
f^{i}(u,v)=0
, then
f^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}}=f^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell}}
and
\Phi(\ell-1)=\Phi(\ell)
, as promised. Otherwise, suppose without loss of generality that
f^{i}(u,v)=1
, which means
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{u}>\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}
and
Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell}}=Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}}+\mathbbm{1}_{u}-\mathbbm{1}_{v}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\Phi(\ell)-\Phi(\ell-1)=\quad

\displaystyle|r^{\leq i-1}_{u}\cdot\deg(u)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell}})_{u}|-|r^{\leq i-1}_{u}\cdot\deg(u)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{u}|

\displaystyle+\,

\displaystyle|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell}})_{v}|-|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{v}|

\displaystyle=\quad

\displaystyle|r^{\leq i-1}_{u}\cdot\deg(u)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{u}-1|-|r^{\leq i-1}_{u}\cdot\deg(u)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{u}|

(2)

\displaystyle+\,

\displaystyle|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{v}+1|-|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{v}|.

(3)

Our goal is to show that either

r^{\leq i-1}_{u}\cdot\deg(u)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{u}\geq 1\qquad\text{or}\qquad r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{v}\leq-1,

which implies that the expression in either (
2
) or (
3
) equals
-1
, and since the other expression is at most
+1
, we obtain our desired
\Phi(\ell)-\Phi(\ell-1)\leq 0
.

Since
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{u}>\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}
, we have either
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{u}>0
or
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}<0
. If
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{u}>0
, then by
Lemma

2.6
, we have

r^{\leq i-1}_{u}\geq\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\geq 2.

Since
f^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}}
is a feasible flow, we have
(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{u}\leq\deg(u)
, so

r^{\leq i-1}_{u}\cdot\deg(u)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{u}\geq 2\deg(u)-\deg(u)=\deg(u)\geq 1,

as promised. Similarly, if
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}<0
, then by
Lemma

2.6
, we have

r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\leq-\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}\leq-\frac{1}{\alpha}\leq-2.

Since
f^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}}
is a feasible flow, we have
(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{v}\geq-\deg(v)
, so

r^{\leq i-1}_{v}\cdot\deg(v)-(Bf^{i}|_{E_{\leq\ell-1}})_{v}\leq-2\deg(v)+\deg(v)=-\deg(v)\leq-1,

as promised. This completes the induction and hence the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

On a unit-capacity graph, Sherman’s algorithm can be implemented in
O(T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+T^{2}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n)
time. Moreover, if an infeasibility certificate
S\subseteq V
is returned, then
\textbf{{vol}}(S)\leq T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n
.

Proof.

Fix an iteration
i\in[T]
, and define
A^{i}=\{v\in V:\tilde{\phi}_{v}^{i}\neq 0\}
. By
Lemma

2.6
, we have
|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}|\geq\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}
for all
v\in A^{i}
. By
Lemma

2.7
, we have
\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}|\cdot\deg(v)\leq(i-1)\cdot\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}
. It follows that

(i-1)\cdot\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\geq\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}|\cdot\deg(v)\geq\sum_{v\in A^{i}}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v}|\cdot\deg(v)\geq\sum_{v\in A^{i}}\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}\cdot\deg(v)=\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}\cdot\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}),

so
\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i})\leq T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n
.

Each edge with nonzero flow in
f^{i}
is incident to a vertex in
A^{i}
, so step (
3
) of iteration
i\in[T]
can be implemented in
O(\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}))
time on a unit-capacity graph. For step (
6
), the only vertices with nonzero
r^{i}_{v}
are either those with nonzero
b(v)
or those incident to an edge with nonzero flow. The number of these vertices can be bounded by
\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}
and
\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i})
, respectively. It follows that the running time of iteration
t
is
O(\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}))
if step (
4
) does not hold. If step (
4
) holds, then by
Lemma

2.2
, the certificate of infeasibility
S\subseteq V
satisfies either
S=V_{>x}\subseteq A^{i}
for some
x\geq 0
, or
S=V_{<x}\subseteq A^{i}
for some
x\leq 0
, so
\textbf{{vol}}(S)\leq\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i})
. To find the desired set
V_{>x}
or
V_{<x}
, sort the vertex set
V_{>0}
by decreasing
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}
and the vertex set
V_{<0}
by increasing
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v}
, which takes
O(|A^{i}|\log|A^{i}|)
time. Then, compute the values of
b(S)
and
\delta(S)
for all prefixes
S
of the two sorted lists, which takes
O(\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}))
time by sweeping through the lists.

Therefore, over all iterations
i\in[T]
, the total running time is
O(T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+\sum_{i\in[T]}\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}))
outside of computing the certificate of infeasibility, and
O(|A^{i^{*}}|\log|A^{i^{*}}|+\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i^{*}}))\leq O(\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i^{*}})\cdot T)
for possibly computing it on some iteration
i^{*}\in[T]
. Since
\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i})\leq T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n
for all
i\in[T]
, the total running time is
O(T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+T^{2}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n)
. If a certificate of infeasibility
S\subseteq V
is returned, then
\textbf{{vol}}(S)\leq\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i^{*}})\leq T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n
.
∎

Final parameters.

For error
|b(v)-(B\bar{f})_{v}|\leq\epsilon\deg(v)
on each vertex
v\in V
, we set
\alpha=\epsilon/5\leq 1/4
and
T=O(\alpha^{-2}\log n)=O(\epsilon^{-2}\log n)
. The total running time is
O(T\left\lVert b_{0}\right\rVert+T^{2}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n)=O(\epsilon^{-2}\log n\cdot(\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+\epsilon^{-1}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}))
. Moreover, if an infeasibility certificate
S\subseteq V
is returned, then
\textbf{{vol}}(S)\leq T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n=O(\epsilon^{-1}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1})
.

2.2
Multi-commodity

In this section, we present the multi-commodity flow algorithm, establishing our main theorem below.

Theorem 2.9

(Local Sherman: Multi-commodity)
.

There is an algorithm that, given a unit-capacity undirected graph
G=(V,E)
, a parameter
k
, any
k
-commodity source function
b\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times[k]}
, and error parameter
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, outputs either

1.

A certificate of infeasibility of the multi-commodity flow, or

2.

A
k
-commodity flow
\bar{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{E\times[k]}
where
\bar{f}_{1},\ldots,\bar{f}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{E}
route demands
b_{1}-r_{1},\ldots,b_{k}-r_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
for (residual) source functions
r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
satisfying
|r_{j}(v)|\leq\epsilon\deg(v)
for all
v\in V,\,j\in[k]
.

The algorithm runs in time
O(\epsilon^{-2}\log n\cdot(\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+\epsilon^{-1}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}))
.

Let
b\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times[k]}
be the input
k
-commodity source function, and let
\mathcal{F}
denote all feasible
k
-commodity flows, i.e., matrices
f\in\mathbb{R}^{E\times[k]}
satisfying
\sum_{j\in[k]}|f_{j}(u,v)|\leq 1
for all edges
(u,v)\in E
.

The
k
-commodity version introduces another layer of complexity: since the
k
-commodity flow-cut gap is
\Omega(\log k)
, the algorithm can no longer output a single cut to certify infeasibility. To obtain the new certificate of infeasibility, we first investigate the linear program that we wish to solve:

find

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{F}

s.t.

\displaystyle\pm\frac{b_{j}(v)-(Bf_{j})_{v}}{\deg(v)}\leq 0

\displaystyle\forall v\in V,\,j\in[k].

(4)

Note that there is no need to divide
b_{j}(v)-(Bf_{j})_{v}
by
\deg(v)
in (
4
), but doing so normalizes the inequalities for our purposes.

In the MWU framework, we weight each constraint
(v,j,\pm)
in (
4
) by
w^{i}_{v,j,\pm}\geq 0

and solve the weighted average inequality subject to the “easy” constraint
f\in\mathcal{F}
. If the average constraint is infeasible, then the weights
\phi^{i}_{v,j,\pm}
certify infeasibility of the LP.

Main Algorithm.

Initialize weights
w_{v,j,+}^{1}=w^{1}_{v,j,-}=1
for each
v\in V
and
j\in[k]
. For each round
i=1,2,\ldots,T
where
T=\Theta(\alpha^{-2}\log n)
for some
\alpha\leq 1/4
, we do the following.

1.

Define the
rounded
weights
\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,j,\pm}
as

\tilde{w}_{v,j,\pm}^{i}=\begin{cases}w_{v,j,\pm}^{i}&\text{if }w_{v,j,\pm}^{i}\geq n\\
0&\text{if }w_{v,j,\pm}^{i}<n.\\
\end{cases}

In other words,
\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,j,\pm}
is obtained by rounding low values to
0
.

2.

Define the rounded potential
\tilde{\phi}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times[k]}
where

\tilde{\phi}_{v,j}^{i}=\frac{\tilde{w}_{v,j,+}^{i}-\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,j,-}}{\deg(v)}.

3.

Define (multi-commodity) flow
f^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{E\times[k]}
such that for each edge
(u,v)
,
f^{i}(u,v)
flows from higher to lower
rounded
potential at maximum capacity
in the commodity
j\in[k]
maximizing the potential difference
. That is, for every edge
(u,v)\in E
, let
j^{*}\in[k]
be the commodity maximizing
|\tilde{\phi}_{u,j}-\tilde{\phi}_{v,j}|
over all
j\in[k]
, and define

f^{i}_{j^{*}}(u,v)=\begin{cases}+1&\text{if }\tilde{\phi}_{u,j^{*}}^{i}>\tilde{\phi}_{v,j^{*}}^{i}\\
-1&\text{if }\tilde{\phi}_{u,j^{*}}^{i}<\tilde{\phi}_{v,j^{*}}^{i}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Define
f^{i}_{j}(u,v)=0
for all
j\neq j^{*}
.

4.

If
\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},\bigoplus_{j\in[k]}b_{j}\rangle>\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},\bigoplus_{j\in[k]}Bf^{i}_{j}\rangle
, then terminate with
\tilde{\phi}^{i}
as the infeasibility certificate. Here, the operator
\bigoplus
concatenates vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{V}
into a single vector in
\mathbb{R}^{V\times[k]}
.

5.

For each
v\in V
and
j\in[k]
, let

r_{v,j}^{i}=\frac{b_{j}(v)-(Bf^{i}_{j})_{v}}{\deg(v)}

be the
relative total excess
in commodity
j
at
v
compared to the degree in round
i
.

6.

Update the (real) weights

w_{v,j,\pm}^{i+1}=w_{v,j,\pm}^{i}(1\pm\alpha r_{v,j}^{i}).

Infeasibility certificate.

If the termination condition holds, then we show that there is an infeasibility certificate.

Lemma 2.10
.

If
\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},\bigoplus_{j\in[k]}b_{j}\rangle>\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},\bigoplus_{j\in[k]}Bf^{i}_{j}\rangle
on some iteration
i
, then
b
is infeasible.

Proof.

We show that taking a weighted sum of the constraints (
4
), where each constraint
(v,j,\pm)
is weighted by
\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,j,\pm}\geq 0
, certifies infeasibility of the LP. The left hand side of this weighted sum is

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{j\in[k]}\left(\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,j,+}\cdot\frac{b_{j}(v)-(Bf^{i}_{j})_{v}}{\deg(v)}-\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,j,-}\cdot\frac{b_{j}(v)-(Bf^{i}_{j})_{v}}{\deg(v)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{j\in[k]}\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v,j}\cdot(b_{j}(v)-(Bf^{i}_{j})_{v})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{j\in[k]}\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v,j}b_{j}(v)-\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{j\in[k]}\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v,j}(Bf^{i}_{j})_{v},

(5)

which equals
\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},\bigoplus_{j\in[k]}b_{j}\rangle-\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},\bigoplus_{j\in[k]}Bf^{i}_{j}\rangle
and is positive by assumption.
The first double-summation in (
5
) is a constant independent of
f\in\mathcal{F}
, and the second double-summation equals

\displaystyle\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\sum_{j\in[k]}f^{i}_{j}(u,v)\cdot(\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{u,j}-\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v,j}).

(6)

Observe that the
k
-commodity flow
f^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{E\times[k]}
is chosen to maximize (
6
) subject to the feasibility constraint
f\in\mathcal{F}
. It follows that
f^{i}
minimizes (
5
) subject to
f\in\mathcal{F}
, and yet the value of (
5
) is still positive. Therefore, the value must be positive for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
, and since it is a weighted sum of the constraints (
4
), we conclude that the LP is infeasible. In particular, there is no flow
f\in\mathcal{F}
satisfying
Bf_{j}=b_{j}
for all
j\in[k]
, so
b
is infeasible.
∎

Multiplicative Weights Update analysis.

Recall the refined MWU statement that allows for approximate multiplicative weights, restated below for convenience.
See
2.3

To apply
Theorem

2.3
into our setting, we define
J=V\times[k]\times\{+,-\}
.
We use the same weights
w^{i}_{v,j,\pm}
and error parameter
\alpha
as
the algorithm. For each iteration
i
and

v\in V
, we define

g_{v,j,\pm}^{i}=\pm r_{v,j}^{i}=\pm\frac{b_{j}(v)-(Bf^{i}_{j})_{v}}{\deg(v)}.

Observe that the weights
w_{v,j,\pm}^{i}
are updated exactly as
w_{v,j,\pm}^{i+1}\leftarrow w_{v,j,\pm}^{i}(1\pm\alpha g_{v,j,\pm}^{i})
. With this setting, we show that our vectors
g^{i},w^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}
satisfy
the condition in Step
2a
of
Theorem

2.3
.

For the lemma below, we impose the assumption
|b_{j}(v)|\leq\deg(v)
for all
v\in V
and
j\in[k]
, which is easy to check and, if violated, is a quick certificate of infeasibility.

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume that the input source functions
b_{j}
satisfy
b_{j}(v)\leq\deg(v)
for all
v\in V,\,j\in[k]
. For each
i
, we have
\|g^{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 2
,
\left\lVert w^{i}-\tilde{w}^{i}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq n
, and
\langle g^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}\rangle\leq 0
if the algorithm does not terminate with an infeasibility certificate.

Proof.

To show
\|g^{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 2
, we have

|g_{v,j,\pm}^{i}|=|r_{v,j,\pm}^{i}|=\left|\frac{b_{j}(v)-(Bf^{i}_{j})_{v}}{\deg(v)}\right|\leq\left|\frac{b_{j}(v)}{\deg(v)}\right|+\left|\frac{f^{i}_{j}(v)}{\deg(v)}\right|\leq 1+1.

To see why the last inequality holds, we have
|b_{j}(v)|\leq\deg(v)

for all
v\in V
by assumption, and
|f^{i}_{j}(v)|\leq\deg(v)

because each
f^{i}
is feasible.

To show
\left\lVert w^{i}-\tilde{w}^{i}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq n
, observe that for each
v\in V
and
j\in[k]
, we have
|w^{i}_{v,j,\pm}-\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,j,\pm}|\leq n
from the construction of
\tilde{w}^{i}
.

To show
\langle g^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}\rangle\leq 0
, observe that for each
v\in V
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in[k]}(g^{i}_{v,j,+}\cdot\tilde{w}_{v,j,+}^{i}+g^{i}_{v,j,-}\cdot\tilde{w}_{v,j,-}^{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in[k]}\frac{b_{j}(v)-(Bf^{i}_{j})_{v}}{\deg(v)}\cdot(\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,j,+}-\tilde{w}^{i}_{v,j,-})=\sum_{j\in[k]}(b_{j}(v)-(Bf^{i}_{j})_{v})\cdot\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v,j},

so summed over all
v\in V
, we have
\langle g^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}\rangle=\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},\bigoplus_{j\in[k]}(b_{j}-Bf^{i}_{j})\rangle
. Since the algorithm does not terminate with an infeasibility certificate, we must have
\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},\bigoplus_{j\in[k]}b_{j}\rangle\leq\langle\tilde{\phi}^{i},\bigoplus_{j\in[k]}Bf^{i}_{j}\rangle
, so
\langle g^{i},\tilde{w}^{i}\rangle\leq 0
.
∎

From the above, we have verified that our algorithm is indeed captured
by the MWU algorithm. Next, define the average flows
\bar{f}_{j}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}f^{i}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{E}
for all
j\in[k]
. We now prove the two-sided error guarantee
|b_{j}(v)-(B\bar{f}_{j})_{v}|\leq 5\alpha\deg(v)
.

Lemma 2.12

(Approximation)
.

The flow
\bar{f}_{j}
satisfies

|b_{j}(v)-(B\bar{f}_{j})_{v}|\leq 5\alpha\deg(v)
for all
v\in V,\,j\in[k]
.
In other words,
\bar{f}_{j}
satisfies input source function
b_{j}
up to two-sided error
5\alpha\deg(v)
.

Proof.

Define
\bar{r}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}r^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times[k]}
.
First, we prove that
|\bar{r}_{v,j}|\leq 5\alpha
for all
v\in V,\,j\in[k]
.
This is because

\pm\bar{r}_{v,j}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i\in[T]}\pm r_{v,j}^{i}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i\in[T]}g_{v,j,\pm}^{i}\leq 5\alpha,

where the last inequality is precisely the guarantee of the MWU algorithm
from
Theorem

2.3
. By definition of
\bar{r}
,

\bar{r}_{v,j}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i\in[T]}r_{v,j}^{i}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i\in[T]}\frac{b_{j}(v)-(Bf^{i}_{j})_{v}}{\deg(v)}=\frac{b_{j}(v)-(B\bar{f}_{j})_{v}}{\deg(v)},

and together with
|\bar{r}_{v,j}|\leq 5\alpha

concludes the proof.
∎

Running time bound.

Finally, we analyze the running time, which we show only depends on
\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}=\sum_{j\in[k]}\left\lVert b_{j}\right\rVert_{0}
and
\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}=\sum_{j\in[k]}\left\lVert b_{j}\right\rVert_{1}
. We remark that everything so far generalizes to capacitated graphs, and the running time bound is the only place that requires a unit-capacity graph.

To avoid clutter, define
f^{\leq i}=f^{1}+\cdots+f^{i}
and
r^{\leq i}=r^{1}+\cdots+r^{i}
. The following two helper lemmas are proved identically to
Lemmas

2.7
and
2.6
, adding a subscript
j\in[k]
wherever necessary. Note that unlike the flow
f^{i}
in the single-commodity case, the flow
f^{i}_{j}
may send zero flow across edges
(u,v)\in E
with
\tilde{\phi}_{u,j}\neq\tilde{\phi}_{v,j}
, but this does not affect the proof of
Lemma

2.7
since
f^{i}_{j}(u,v)=0
is an easy case.

Lemma 2.13
.

If
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v,j}>0
, then
r^{\leq i-1}_{v,j}\geq\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}
. Similarly, if
\tilde{\phi}^{i}_{v,j}<0
, then
r^{\leq i-1}_{v,j}\leq-\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}
.

Lemma 2.14
.

For each iteration
i
, we have
\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i}_{v,j}|\cdot\deg(v)\leq i\cdot\left\lVert b_{j}\right\rVert_{1}
.

Lemma 2.15
.

On a unit-capacity graph, Sherman’s algorithm can be implemented in
O(T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+T^{2}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n)
time.

Proof.

Fix an iteration
i\in[T]
, and define
A^{i}_{j}=\{v\in V:\tilde{\phi}_{v,j}^{i}\neq 0\}
for all
j\in[k]
. By
Lemma

2.13
, we have
|r^{\leq i-1}_{v,j}|\geq\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}
for all
v\in A^{i}_{j}
. By
Lemma

2.14
, we have
\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v,j}|\cdot\deg(v)\leq(i-1)\cdot\left\lVert b_{j}\right\rVert_{1}
. It follows that

(i-1)\cdot\left\lVert b_{j}\right\rVert_{1}\geq\sum_{v\in V}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v,j}|\cdot\deg(v)\geq\sum_{v\in A^{i}_{j}}|r^{\leq i-1}_{v,j}|\cdot\deg(v)\geq\sum_{v\in A^{i}_{j}}\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}\cdot\deg(v)=\frac{\ln n}{\alpha}\cdot\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}_{j}),

so
\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}_{j})\leq T\left\lVert b_{j}\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n
.

Each edge with nonzero flow in
f^{i}_{j}
is incident to a vertex in
A^{i}_{j}
, so step (
3
) of iteration
i\in[T]
can be implemented in
O(\sum_{j\in[k]}\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}_{j}))
time on a unit-capacity graph. For step (
6
), the only vertices with nonzero
r^{i}_{v,j}
are either those with nonzero
b_{j}(v)
or those incident to an edge with nonzero flow. The number of these vertices can be bounded by
\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}
and
\sum_{j\in[k]}\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}_{j})
, respectively. It follows that the running time of iteration
t
is
O(\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+\sum_{j\in[k]}\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}_{j}))
.

Therefore, over all iterations
i\in[T]
, the total running time is
O(T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+\sum_{i\in[T]}\sum_{j\in[k]}\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}_{j}))
. Since
\textbf{{vol}}(A^{i}_{j})\leq T\left\lVert b_{j}\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n
for all
i\in[T]
and
j\in[k]
, the total running time is
O(T\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+T^{2}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n)
.
∎

Final parameters.

For error
|b_{j}(v)-(B\bar{f}_{j})_{v}|\leq\epsilon\deg(v)
for all
v\in V,\,j\in[k]
, we set
\alpha=\epsilon/5\leq 1/4
and
T=O(\alpha^{-2}\log n)=O(\epsilon^{-2}\log n)
. The total running time is
O(T\left\lVert b_{0}\right\rVert+T^{2}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}\alpha/\ln n)=O(\epsilon^{-2}\log n\cdot(\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+\epsilon^{-1}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}))
.

3
Multi-commodity Flow on an Expander

A
\phi
-expander
is a graph satisfying
\delta S\geq\phi\min\{\textbf{{vol}}(S),\textbf{{vol}}(V\setminus S)\}
for all
S\subseteq V
. In this section, we prove
Theorem

1.2
, restated below.
See
1.2

In a
\phi
-expander, the residual demands from
Theorem

2.9
can be routed to completion, incurring only
\epsilon
additional congestion as long as the residual demands are small enough. The following is implicit in
[
CHS24
]
, who describe the algorithm as a distributed routing protocol.

Theorem 3.1

(Expander Routing)
.

Consider the
\ell
-commodity demand defined by
\ell
weighted pairs
(s_{1},t_{1},d_{1}),\ldots,(s_{\ell},t_{\ell},d_{\ell})\in V\times V\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
indicating that we want to send
d_{j}
units of commodity
j\in[\ell]
from
s_{j}
to
t_{j}
. Assume that for each vertex
v\in V
, the sum of
d_{j}
over all
j\in[k]
with
v\in\{s_{j},t_{j}\}
is at most
\alpha\deg(v)
for some parameter
\alpha>0
. If
G
is a
\phi
-expander, then there is a deterministic
(m+\ell)\cdot\textup{poly}(1/\phi)\cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})}
time algorithm that computes an
\ell
-commodity flow routing this demand with congestion
\alpha\cdot\textup{poly}(1/\phi)\cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})}
.

Given
Theorem

3.1
, we now describe an algorithm for
Theorem

1.2
. Let
\tilde{\epsilon}=\epsilon/(k\cdot\textup{poly}(1/\phi)\cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})})
, where the
\textup{poly}(1/\phi)\cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})}
factors come from
Theorem

3.1
. We run
Theorem

2.9
on
G
, error parameter
\tilde{\epsilon}
, and the
k
-commodity demand
b\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times[k]}
where
b_{j}=d_{j}(\mathbbm{1}_{s_{j}}-\mathbbm{1}_{t_{j}})
for each
j\in[k]
. Then,
Theorem

2.9
runs in
O(\tilde{\epsilon}^{-2}\log n\cdot(\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{0}+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-1}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{1}))=O(\tilde{\epsilon}^{-2}\log n\cdot(k+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-1}D))
time and returns a
k
-commodity flow
f
where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
route demands
b_{1}-r_{1},\ldots,b_{k}-r_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
for residual demands
r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
satisfying
|r_{j}(v)|\leq\tilde{\epsilon}\deg(v)
for all
v\in V,\,j\in[k]
.

We now wish to route the
k
-commodity (residual) demand
r
defined by
r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}
with total congestion of
k\tilde{\epsilon}\cdot\textup{poly}(1/\phi)\cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})}=\epsilon
by calling
Theorem

3.1

once
. For each
j\in[k]
, arbitrarily decompose the demand
r_{j}
into weighted pairs
(s_{j,1},t_{j,1},d_{j,1}),\ldots,(s_{j,\ell_{j}},t_{j,\ell_{j}},d_{j,\ell_{j}})
such that
\sum_{i\in[\ell_{j}]}d_{j,\ell_{j}}\cdot(\mathbbm{1}_{s_{j,i}}-\mathbbm{1}_{t_{j,i}})=r_{j}
. A simple greedy algorithm achieves
\ell_{j}\leq\left\lVert r_{j}\right\rVert_{0}
. Call
Theorem

3.1
on the weighted pairs
(s_{j,1},t_{j,1},d_{j,1}),\ldots,(s_{j,\ell_{j}},t_{j,\ell_{j}},d_{j,\ell_{j}})
concatenated over all
j\in[k]
and parameter
\alpha=k\tilde{\epsilon}
. Let
\ell=\ell_{1}+\dots+\ell_{k}
. Thus,
Theorem

3.1
returns an
\ell
-commodity flow with congestion
k\tilde{\epsilon}\cdot\textup{poly}(1/\phi)\cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})}=\epsilon
. For each
j\in[k]
, group the
\ell_{j}
relevant flows into a single flow
\tilde{f}_{j}
routing demand
r_{j}
. Observe that the flow
\hat{f}_{j}=f_{j}+\tilde{f}_{j}
exactly routes demand
b_{j}
. Therefore, the
k
-commodity flow
\hat{f}
defined by
\hat{f}_{1},\dots,\hat{f}_{k}
precisely routes our
k
-commodity demand
b
with total congestion
1+\epsilon
as desired.

For running time, observe that the flow
f
from
Theorem

2.9
has support size (i.e., number of nonzero entries) at most
O(\tilde{\epsilon}^{-2}\log n\cdot(k+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-1}D))
since that is also the running time. Constructing the
k
-commodity (residual) demand
r
can be done in time linear in the support size of
f
, and the number of commodities in the call to
Theorem

3.1
has the same bound, i.e.,
\ell=O(\tilde{\epsilon}^{-2}\log n\cdot(k+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-1}D))
. It follows, by
Theorem

3.1
, that the overall algorithm runs in time

\displaystyle(m+O(\tilde{\epsilon}^{-2}\log n\cdot(k+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-1}D)))\cdot\textup{poly}(1/\phi)\cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})}

\displaystyle=(m+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-2}k+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-3}D)\cdot\textup{poly}(1/\phi)\cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})}

\displaystyle=(m+\epsilon^{-2}k^{3}+\epsilon^{-3}k^{3}D)\cdot\textup{poly}(1/\phi)\cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})}

\displaystyle=(m+\epsilon^{-3}k^{3}D)\cdot\textup{poly}(1/\phi)\cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})},

concluding the proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

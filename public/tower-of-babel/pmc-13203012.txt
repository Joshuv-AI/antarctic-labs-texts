Phase III randomized studies of mevrometostat and enzalutamide in metastatic prostate cancer (MEVPRO-1 and MEVPRO-2): trial protocols
Authors: Neal D. Shore, Karim Fizazi, Michael Thomas Schweizer, Elena Castro, Arun A. Azad, Daniel J. George, Karine Martins da Trindade, Hao Zeng, Nitin Shrivastava, Svitlana Tonkovyd, Amy Bock, Srilakshmi Nekkalapudi, Yiyun Tang, Stella Lin, Neeraj Agarwal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPhase I investigation of the enhancer of zeste homolog 2 inhibitor, mevrometostat, plus enzalutamide showed promising antitumor activity versus enzalutamide alone in patients with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) (NCT03460977). MEVPRO-1 (open label) and MEVPRO-2 (double blind) are ongoing, global, phase III, randomized studies evaluating efficacy and safety of mevrometostat plus enzalutamide in patients with mCRPC. MEVPRO-1 will include ~600 patients with ≥12 weeks of prior abiraterone treatment, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) 0–2, testosterone ≤50 ng/dL, and life expectancy ≥6 months. MEVPRO-2 will include ~900 androgen receptor pathway inhibitor-naïve patients, with ECOG PS 0–1, testosterone ≤50 ng/dL, and life expectancy ≥12 months. Patients are randomized 1:1 to mevrometostat (875 mg twice daily with food) plus enzalutamide (160 mg once daily), or physician’s choice of enzalutamide or docetaxel (MEVPRO-1) or enzalutamide (MEVPRO-2). Primary endpoint is radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors 1.1 (soft tissue) and Prostate Cancer Working Group 3 criteria (bone) assessed by blinded independent central review. Overall survival is a secondary objective. Kaplan–Meier analysis will summarize time-to-event endpoints. Safety will be assessed. Results will evaluate whether mevrometostat plus enzalutamide can provide clinical benefit in patients with mCRPC.Clinical trial registration: www.clinicaltrials.gov identifiers are NCT06551324 (MEVPRO-1) and NCT06629779 (MEVPRO-2).

Introduction

Background and rationale

Prostate cancer is the second most commonly diagnosed cancer and the fifth leading cause of cancer death in males globally [1]. Castration‑resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) occurs when disease progresses despite treatment with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) [2], with metastatic CRPC (mCRPC) representing a particularly aggressive form of the disease [3,4].

Androgen receptor (AR) pathway inhibitors (ARPIs), such as enzalutamide and abiraterone, play a crucial role in the management of mCRPC. However, resistance to these therapies eventually develops [5], and remains a significant treatment challenge. Such resistance may be driven by preservation of AR-signaling via a number of mechanisms [6–8]. Enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2), the catalytic subunit of polycomb repressive complex 2, is a critical epigenetic regulator in prostate cancer. In the development of neuroendocrine prostate cancer, EZH2 is overexpressed and collaborates with transcription factors such as N-Myc to repress genes that maintain the AR-dependent luminal phenotype, facilitating lineage plasticity and resistance to AR-targeted therapies. This repression is mediated through trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 27 (H3K27me3), leading to transcriptional silencing of differentiation-associated genes [9–12]. Thus, administration of ARPI with therapies that modulate alternative signaling pathways, such as epigenetic modifiers including EZH2, which has a non-canonical role acting as an AR coregulator, could be a useful treatment approach to addressing the clinical need of delaying or preventing the development of antiandrogen resistance. Mevrometostat (PF-06821497) is a potent and selective EZH2 inhibitor [13] that blocks H3K27 methylation, suppressing EZH2-driven cancer cell growth. It may enhance AR inhibitor effects by reactivating co-repressed genes and preventing neuroendocrine prostate cancer transition, restoring antiandrogen sensitivity and reducing tumor proliferation (Figure 1) [14–16]. Preliminary data from the dose-escalation part of a phase I clinical study (NCT03460977) showed nearly 50% longer median radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) with mevrometostat plus enzalutamide versus enzalutamide alone, with a manageable safety profile [17]. This combination also exhibits on-target pharmacodynamic effects, with reductions in H3K27Me3 levels and appears to be active across clinically relevant mCRPC subtypes [18]. On this basis, pivotal phase III studies evaluating mevrometostat as part of the treatment for mCRPC have been launched.

Objectives

Here, we describe the designs of two ongoing, global, randomized phase III trials in patients with mCRPC receiving mevrometostat and enzalutamide following abiraterone (MEVPRO-1) and in ARPI-naïve patients (MEVPRO-2). Primary objectives are to evaluate whether mevrometostat in combination with enzalutamide is superior to standard of care (physician’s choice of enzalutamide or docetaxel) in prolonging rPFS in patients previously treated with abiraterone (MEVPRO-1) and superior to enzalutamide alone in prolonging rPFS in patients who are ARPI-naïve (MEVPRO-2). Demonstration of superior overall survival with mevrometostat plus enzalutamide is a key secondary objective in both studies; demonstration of superiority of mevrometostat plus enzalutamide in prolonging time to pain progression is an additional secondary objective of MEVPRO-2.

Methods

Study design

MEVPRO-1

MEVPRO-1 (NCT06551324) is an open-label, phase III trial which will evaluate mevrometostat plus enzalutamide versus physician’s choice of either enzalutamide or docetaxel in patients with mCRPC who have progressed following ≥12 weeks of treatment with abiraterone. Approximately 600 patients will be randomized 1:1 to receive mevrometostat (875 mg twice daily [BID] with food [a meal]) in combination with enzalutamide (160 mg once daily [QD]), or physician’s choice of enzalutamide (160 mg QD) or docetaxel (75 mg/m2 intravenously every 21 days) (Figure 2(A)). Randomization will be performed centrally using an interactive response technology system. Randomization will be stratified by previous docetaxel in the metastatic castration-sensitive prostate cancer (mCSPC) setting, physician’s choice of comparator (prespecified prior to randomization and will remain consistent throughout the study), and presence of hepatic metastases. As this is an open-label study, patients, investigators and other site staff, and sponsor staff will be unblinded to patients’ assigned study intervention. Potential bias will be reduced by use of central randomization and blinded independent central review (BICR) of efficacy data, with all aggregated safety and efficacy data remaining blinded until the final database lock is completed in order to preserve the integrity of the trial. MEVPRO-2

MEVPRO-2 (NCT06629779) is a double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase III trial that will evaluate mevrometostat plus enzalutamide versus enzalutamide alone in adult patients with mCRPC who are treatment-naïve; i.e., patients cannot have received any cytotoxic chemotherapy, prior ARPI (enzalutamide, apalutamide, darolutamide, abiraterone), or other systemic treatment in the mCRPC setting, with the exception of ADT and first-generation antiandrogen agents. Chemotherapy (docetaxel) is permitted in the castrate-sensitive setting. Approximately 900 patients will be randomized 1:1 to receive mevrometostat (875 mg BID with food [a meal]) in combination with enzalutamide (160 mg QD), or placebo (BID) with enzalutamide (160 mg QD; Figure 2(B)). Randomization will be performed centrally using an interactive response technology system, and stratified by previous docetaxel in the mCSPC setting and presence of hepatic metastases. Patients, investigators and other site staff, and sponsor staff will be blinded to patients’ assigned study intervention (mevrometostat or matching placebo, provided in blinded bottles). Enzalutamide will be given open label. In case of an emergency, the investigator has the sole responsibility for determining if unblinding of a patient’s treatment assignment is warranted for patient safety. If the investigator decides that unblinding is warranted, the date and reason for the unblinding will be recorded in the source documentation and electronic case report form (CRF).

MEVPRO-1 and MEVPRO-2

Investigators will be blinded to central reads while patients are on treatment. However, if an unexpected observation is identified during the central review process that could, in the opinion of the central reviewer, have a significant health or reproductive consequence, this finding may be shared with the study sponsor for disclosure to the investigator. To mitigate the potential for bias in determining disease progression, expedited BICR will be performed for investigator‑assessed disease progression. Per protocol, patients may remain on study treatment until BICR confirmation of progressive disease; however, this decision is ultimately at the investigator’s discretion based on the best interests of the patient. In the event of significant toxicity, dosing may be delayed and/or reduced. If the mevrometostat dose is reduced, modified, or discontinued, the enzalutamide dose can remain the same provided that the investigator has determined that the cause of the adverse event is due to mevrometostat (or placebo [MEVPRO-2 only]). If enzalutamide is permanently discontinued, mevrometostat is also permanently discontinued. To mitigate expectation bias, patient-reported outcome (PRO) assessments, completed at baseline and throughout the study, are performed using validated PRO instruments, and must be completed using an ePRO device. Post-baseline, in cases in which the PRO assessment timepoint overlaps with a study visit, all PROs must be completed using an ePRO device prior to receiving any study treatment, prior to any study assessment or consultation with the investigator, and prior to being informed of their current disease status. Questionnaires should be completed independently.

Enrollment

Enrollment for MEVPRO-1 began on 21 October 2024; the study is estimated to be completed in October 2028 (Figure 2). The first patient was enrolled into MEVPRO-2 on 27 November 2024 and the study is estimated to be completed in November 2028 (Figure 2). Countries across North America, South America, Europe, Asia, and Africa are currently enrolling patients (Figure 3(A,B)). Eligibility criteria

To be eligible to take part in MEVPRO-1 or MEVPRO-2, patients must be aged ≥18 years; with histologically or cytologically confirmed adenocarcinoma of the prostate without small cell features, documented by a positive bone scan for metastatic disease in bone or positive computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan for soft tissue disease; and be surgically or medically castrated, with serum testosterone of ≤50 ng/dL (≤1.73 nmol/L) at screening. Ongoing ADT with a gonadotrophin-releasing hormone agonist or antagonist for patients who have not undergone bilateral orchiectomy must have been initiated at least 4 weeks before randomization and must continue throughout the study. Patients must have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–2 (MEVPRO-1) or 0–1 (MEVPRO-2) at screening and a life expectancy of ≥6 months (MEVPRO-1) or ≥12 months (MEVPRO-2). Full inclusion and exclusion criteria are detailed in Supplemental Table S1.

MEVPRO-1 and MEVPRO-2 study assessments

Disease status will be assessed at screening and at regular intervals during the study. Imaging assessments (CT of the chest, and CT or MRI of the abdomen and pelvis for assessment of soft tissue disease, and whole-body bone scan [e.g., radionuclide (technetium-99 m) bone scintigraphy]) will be performed every 8 weeks (at weeks 9, 17, and 25), then every 12 weeks (at weeks 37, 49, 61, etc.). During the treatment phase, prostate-specific antigen (PSA) measurements will be performed every 4 weeks (MEVPRO-1) or every 8 weeks (MEVPRO-2), at the end-of-treatment visit, 28-day safety follow-up (where applicable), and then every 12 weeks thereafter. PRO assessments will be carried out every 6 weeks (MEVPRO-1) or every 4 weeks (MEVPRO-2) during the treatment phase, at the end-of-treatment visit, 28-day safety follow-up, and then every 12 weeks thereafter. In MEVPRO-2, the Brief Pain Inventory-Short Form (BPI-SF) item 3 (worst pain) assessment will be collected electronically, once at screening and once at cycle 1 day 1, then each of the 7 consecutive days up to within 3 days of the first date of the 7-day period prior to each scheduled study visit and before any other study activities. Opioid use for pain will be collected from screening through long-term follow-up.

Response and progression of soft tissue disease will be evaluated per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) 1.1 and bone disease progression will be evaluated per Prostate Cancer Working Group 3 (PCWG3). A third-party, independent, core imaging laboratory will perform BICR of radiographic images to determine protocol‑defined efficacy endpoints, including rPFS. During the treatment phase, treatment will continue until BICR-determined radiographic disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, global deterioration of health status that requires discontinuation, or other discontinuation of study intervention criteria are met. Safety will be assessed by adverse events, physical examinations, vital signs, and clinical laboratory tests. Adverse events of clinical interest will be examined as part of routine safety data review procedures throughout the two studies and as part of signal detection processes. Should an aggregate analysis indicate that these prespecified events occur more frequently than expected, then this will be reported. Aggregate analyses of safety data will be performed on a regular basis. Tumor tissue and blood samples will also be collected to evaluate biomarkers.

Initiation of new antineoplastic therapy (which includes medications used specifically for prostate cancer treatment, such as hormonal treatments, immunotherapy, chemotherapy, and investigative agents) will be monitored for all patients during the long-term follow-up period. Data will be collected every 12 weeks from the last dose of study intervention until the patient dies or withdraws consent for follow-up, or the study is terminated by the sponsor. Long-term follow-up will no longer be required after the desired number of overall survival (OS) events has been achieved. Crossover will not be allowed in either trial.

Compliance with study intervention will be assessed at each visit via direct questioning and counting returned tablets/capsules during the site visits, and will be documented in the source documents and patient diaries. Deviation from the prescribed dosage regimen should be recorded in the CRF. PRO compliance rates will be summarized by listing the numbers and proportions of patients who completed the PRO assessments at each time point by treatment arm.

Other secondary endpoints include the proportion of patients with measurable soft tissue disease at baseline who achieve objective response and duration of response assessed in soft tissue per RECIST 1.1, or in bone assessed by BICR. Additionally, the studies will evaluate the proportion of patients with a PSA response of ≥50% among those with detectable PSA values at baseline; time to PSA progression, initiation of new antineoplastic therapy (and initiation of new cytotoxic chemotherapy in MEVPRO-2), and first symptomatic skeletal event; and progression-free survival on next line of therapy based on investigator assessment.

PROs will also be compared, including changes from baseline in pain symptoms per the BPI-SF, health-related quality of life (HRQoL), functioning and symptoms per the Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy–Prostate (FACT-P), and health status per the EuroQol 5 Dimensions, 5 Levels questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L). The studies will assess symptomatic toxicity and the overall side-effect burden using items from the PRO Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) and FACT-General Population item 5, as well as time to definitive deterioration in patient-reported HRQoL and physical wellbeing per FACT-P.

Pharmacokinetics will be characterized by pre-dose trough and post-dose plasma concentrations of mevrometostat at selected visits. The relationship between circulating tumor DNA burden and outcome will be assessed at baseline and during the study as a secondary objective. Exploratory endpoint assessments include the measurements of biomarkers obtained at baseline and during the study. Biomarkers may consist of tumor tissue (de novo or archived) for retrospective molecular profiling analysis; blood samples for circulating tumor DNA and cell-free RNA analysis; blood samples for filtering of somatic vs germline origin and/or clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) of DNA alterations in tumor DNA or cell-free DNA; and blood samples for serum biomarker research. Safety and tolerability will be compared between the treatment and control arms by examining the type, incidence, severity (graded by National Cancer Institute CTCAE v5.0), seriousness, and relationship of study medications and adverse events.

Sample size and statistical analysis

Approximately 600 patients will be enrolled in the MEVPRO-1 study and randomized 1:1 to the two treatments arms (i.e., 300 patients per arm). Approximately 900 patients will be enrolled in MEVPRO-2 and randomized 1:1 to the two treatment arms (i.e., 450 patients per arm). All efficacy analyses will be performed using the full analysis set, which comprises all patients randomly assigned to study intervention. For analyses using the full analysis set, patients will be analyzed according to the study intervention assigned at randomization. All safety analyses will be performed using the safety analysis set, which comprises patients in the full analysis set who receive at least one dose of study medication.

Time-to-event endpoints will be compared between treatment arms by using a two-sided stratified log-rank test. The stratified analysis will be based on the stratification factors used at randomization, which include previous treatment with docetaxel in the mCSPC setting (yes vs no), physicians’ choice of comparator (docetaxel vs enzalutamide [MEVPRO-1 only]), and presence of hepatic metastases (yes vs no). Hazard ratios and 95% CIs will be estimated using a stratified Cox proportional hazard model. Kaplan–Meier analysis will summarize time-to-event endpoints, and will include the median and 95% CIs based on the Brookmeyer–Crowley method. There will be no interim analysis for the primary endpoint (rPFS). There will be two interim efficacy analyses for OS; the first of which will be performed at the time of the final analysis of rPFS. Alpha-protected efficacy analyses will include tests for rPFS and the key secondary endpoints (OS in MEVPRO-1; OS and time to pain progression in MEVPRO-2). The type-1 error will be controlled using a hierarchal testing approach. If the primary analysis of rPFS is successful, then OS will be tested using the full alpha of 0.05. If the primary analysis of rPFS is not successful, then formal testing of OS will not be done. If both the primary analysis and OS are successful, then time to pain progression (MEVPRO-2 only) will be tested using the full alpha of 0.05. In both studies, the efficacy boundary in the OS interim analyses will be performed using a Lan–DeMets (O’Brien–Fleming) alpha-spending function, and will use a 3-look group sequential design in MEVPRO-1.

Counts of missing observations will be included in the denominator and presented as a separate category. rPFS data will be censored on the date of the last adequate tumor assessment for patients who do not have an event (progressive disease based on BICR assessment per RECIST v1.1 [soft tissue disease] and PCWG3 [bone disease] or death due to any cause). Patients who start a new anticancer therapy with no event or with first event right after initiation of new anticancer therapy will be censored on the date of last adequate radiological assessment prior to initiation of new anti-cancer therapy. Patients with the first event right after 2 or more missing tumor assessments will be censored on the date of last adequate tumor assessment immediately prior to the event. Patients who do not have an adequate baseline tumor assessment or who do not have an adequate post-baseline tumor assessment will be censored on the date of randomization. Survival status will be collected irrespective of study intervention discontinuation or patient’s request to discontinue study procedures. All patients who have not withdrawn consent for further participation in the study will be followed for survival until the end of the study.

Descriptive summary statistics and longitudinal mixed-effect model analyses will be used to assess change from baseline in worst pain (item 3), pain severity, interference in daily activities from pain as per BPI-SF; health status per EQ-5D-5L; and HRQoL, functioning, and symptoms per FACT-P between the two treatment groups. Time-to-deterioration analyses will be conducted using a two‑sided stratified log‑rank test. The stratified analysis will be based on the stratification factors used at randomization and summarized using the Kaplan–Meier method, which will include the median and 95% CIs based on the Brookmeyer–Crowley method; 95% CIs will be estimated using a stratified Cox proportional hazard model. Any missing PRO responses will be handled per the scoring manuals of each questionnaire administered.

Data monitoring

An independent external data monitoring committee (E-DMC) will monitor the safety of the patients on a periodic basis. The E-DMC operates independently from the study team and is composed solely of external members. It will oversee the ongoing monitoring of patient safety in the study. Any recommendations made by the E-DMC will be submitted to the appropriate authorized study sponsor personnel for review and final decision. The study sponsor will then communicate these decisions—which may potentially include summaries of aggregate analyses of safety data—to regulatory authorities and investigators as appropriate.

Ethics and dissemination

The MEVPRO-1 and MEVPRO-2 studies will be conducted in accordance with the protocols (Protocol Amendment 4, 27 January 2026 [MEVPRO-1]; Protocol Amendment 4, 5 January 2026 [MEVPRO-2]), and adhere to international ethical guidelines, including the Declaration of Helsinki and Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences International Ethical Guidelines, as well as applicable International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines and local regulations. The protocols will be approved by institutional review boards and ethics committees at each participating site; any protocol amendments require institutional review board approval unless necessary to eliminate immediate hazards to patients. Investigators will obtain written informed consent from patients at each study site. This report follows the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials reporting guideline. The results of MEVPRO-1 and MEVPRO-2 will be publicly disclosed in accordance with applicable laws and regulations; results may also be published in peer-reviewed journals or presented at scientific meetings.

Data protection and quality assurance

All parties will comply with all applicable laws, including laws regarding the implementation of organizational and technical measures to ensure protection of patient data. All patient data that relates to the study will be recorded on electronic CRFs, unless transmitted to the sponsor or designee electronically (e.g., laboratory data). The investigator is responsible for verifying that data entries are accurate and correct by electronically signing the CRF. It is the investigator’s role to ensure that the CRFs are securely stored at the study site in encrypted electronic form and are password-protected or secured in a locked room to prevent access by unauthorized third parties.

Discussion

The clinical benefit of the ARPIs enzalutamide and abiraterone in patients with metastatic prostate cancer is well established, with enzalutamide also shown to have limited activity in a second-line setting following disease progression on abiraterone [19,20]. EZH2 inhibitors have shown promise in preclinical models by inhibiting tumor growth and reversing resistance to ARPIs [9–11]. In the dose escalation part of a phase I clinical study, mevrometostat in combination with enzalutamide showed evidence of tumor pharmacodynamic modulation, as demonstrated by post-baseline reductions in H3K27Me3, as well as anti-tumor activity in patients who had progressed on prior enzalutamide [18]. Furthermore, results from the dose-expansion part of the study (n = 81) demonstrated a significant benefit of mevrometostat given in combination with enzalutamide versus enzalutamide alone (median rPFS of 14.3 vs 6.2 months, respectively), providing strong rationale for investigation of the combination in phase III studies.

The phase III MEVPRO-1 and MEVPRO-2 studies, which have complementary designs and objectives, ultimately aim to evaluate whether, by targeting alternative signaling pathways involved in oncogenesis, the EZH2 inhibitor mevrometostat can reverse or delay the onset of antiandrogen resistance. It is hypothesized that administration of the two agents in combination would increase the duration of the clinical benefit of enzalutamide, thus meeting a currently unmet need in patients with mCRPC.

MEVPRO-1 will evaluate the benefit of adding mevrometostat to enzalutamide, versus physician’s choice of docetaxel or enzalutamide, in patients who have previously been treated with abiraterone. The choice of control arm in this study is a reflection of the standard treatments recommended in clinical practice and current treatment guidelines [21]. The choice of enzalutamide or docetaxel was selected to allow the investigator to choose the appropriate treatment based on factors such as the individual patient’s clinical condition and prior treatment history, and as such represents a strength of the study. The recent TRITON3 phase III study, investigating the PARP inhibitor, rucaparib, in patients with mCRPC with BRCA1, BRCA2, or ATM alterations who had progressed on second-generation ARPI, also employed physician’s choice of docetaxel or second-generation ARPI as the control, and reported an imaging-based PFS of 6.4 months in the control group [22]. Additionally, in the SPLASH and PSMAfore phase III studies, investigating the radioligand therapy 177Lu-PNT2002 in patients with mCRPC who had progressed on ARPI, rPFS in the control (second ARPI) groups was 6.0 and 5.6 months, respectively [23,24]. These findings may provide an indicator of the rPFS that might be expected in the control group in MEVPRO-1.

Despite guideline recommendations, many patients do not receive ARPIs in the castration-sensitive disease setting [25–28]. Therefore, MEVPRO-2 will evaluate the benefit of the addition of mevrometostat to enzalutamide versus enzalutamide alone in patients who are treatment-naïve to ARPIs (including abiraterone) and who have not yet received chemotherapy in the mCRPC setting.

Results from the two studies should therefore provide valuable insights into the potential treatment benefit of mevrometostat in combination with enzalutamide within differing clinical scenarios in patients with mCRPC.

CRPC exhibits significant genetic heterogeneity, both within individual tumors and between different patients [29,30]. TP53 mutations and the loss of other tumor suppressor genes, like RB1, are frequently observed in CRPC and are associated with neuroendocrine differentiation [31]. The phase I study demonstrated benefit of mevrometostat in an all‑comers CRPC patient population [18]. In exploratory analysis, durable antitumor activity was also observed in patients receiving mevrometostat both following abiraterone (with no enzalutamide) and following enzalutamide (with or without abiraterone), with and without AR and/or TP53 mutations [18]. Given the benefit demonstrated in the broad phase I study population and the limited data to date on mevrometostat in more selected patient populations, the phase III studies include an all-comer population regardless of tumor molecular characteristics or tumor burden.

The development of resistance to ARPIs (including abiraterone) in CRPC is multifactorial, driven partly by selection of the neuroendocrine phenotype, and partly by AR‑related processes that may be impacted [9,30]. The exact interplay of these factors in relation to EZH2 biology is still being elucidated [32].

The results of these phase III trials may build on the previous clinical data showing benefit of mevrometostat plus enzalutamide in patients with mCRPC. Further demonstration of the clinical benefit of addition of mevrometostat to enzalutamide could potentially contribute to a future introduction of EZH2 inhibitors into the treatment paradigm for a broad all-comer population of patients with mCRPC, providing meaningful efficacy benefit by delaying time to progression and improving patient survival, while maintaining patient QoL.

Conclusion

The phase III MEVPRO-1 and MEVPRO-2 trials will evaluate whether mevrometostat given in combination with enzalutamide could improve outcomes that encompass efficacy, safety, and PROs in patients with mCRPC. In MEVPRO-1, rPFS will be evaluated by comparison of mevrometostat plus enzalutamide with physician’s choice of standard of care (enzalutamide or docetaxel) in patients with mCRPC who have previously been treated with abiraterone. In MEVPRO-2, rPFS will be evaluated by comparison of mevrometostat plus enzalutamide with placebo and enzalutamide in patients with mCRPC who are ARPI-naïve.

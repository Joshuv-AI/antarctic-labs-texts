Comparative overall survival in T4N0 and T2-3N1 non-metastatic colon cancer: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Fırat Akagunduz, Ali Kaan Güren, Erkam Kocaaslan, Burak Paçacı, Mustafa Alperen Tunç, Ahmet Demirel, Yesim Ağyol, Pınar Erel, Abdussamet Çelebi, Selver Işik, Ezgi Coban, Murat Sarı, Ibrahim Vedat Bayoğlu, Osman Köstek, Nazım Can Demircan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBACKGROUND: The prognostic significance of pathological T stage relative to nodal involvement in colon cancer remains controversial. Emerging evidence suggests that patients with T4N0 disease may experience outcomes comparable to or worse than those with node-positive disease.OBJECTIVES: This study aimed to compare long-term oncologic outcomes between patients with non-metastatic T4N0 colon cancer and those with T2–3N1 disease.DESIGN: RetrospectiveSETTINGS: Single-centerPATIENTS AND METHODS: Retrospective analysis of patients with histologically confirmed, non-metastatic colon adenocarcinoma who underwent curative-intent surgery between 1999 and 2020. Patients were categorized into T4N0 and T2–3N1 groups.MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES: Overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared with log-rank tests. Prognostic factors were assessed using univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards models.SAMPLE SIZE: A total of 273 patients were included, of whom 78 (28.6%) were staged as T4N0 and 195 (71.4%) as T2–3N1.RESULTS: After a median follow-up of 72 months, patients with T4N0 disease showed significantly worse OS than those with T2–3N1 disease (HR=1.72, 95% CI 1.06–2.75; P=.02). Five-year OS rates were 66.6% and 79.8% in the T4N0 and T2–3N1 groups, respectively. On multivariate analysis, pathological T4N0 stage (HR=1.74, 95% CI 1.03–2.93; P=.03), age ≥65 years, vascular invasion, and omission of oxaliplatin-based adjuvant chemotherapy independently predicted poorer OS. Stage T4N0 was also associated with higher locoregional and distant recurrence rates, with increased peritoneal involvement.CONCLUSIONS: Non-metastatic T4N0 colon cancer is associated with less favorable long-term survival and higher recurrence rates compared with T2–3N1 disease, supporting its classification as a biologically aggressive subgroup that may benefit from treatment strategies similar to selected stage III colon cancers.LIMITATIONS: The retrospective, single-center design may introduce selection bias, and limited molecular data precluded detailed sub-group analyses.Keywords: Colon cancer, T4N0, adjuvant chemotherapy

INTRODUCTION

Colorectal cancer continues to be a leading cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality worldwide.1 Accurate staging plays a vital role in determining prognosis and planning treatment. For this purpose, the Tumor, Node, Metastasis (TNM) system, developed by the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC), is used as the primary classification tool based on the anatomic extent of the disease.2 Classically, patients with positive lymph nodes (stage III) are considered to have a poorer prognosis, and adjuvant chemotherapy is routinely recommended.3–5 In contrast, adjuvant therapy is considered only in patients with high-risk features in stage II patients; these features include lymphovascular or perineural invasion, poor differentiation, presentation with obstruction or perforation, removal of <12 lymph nodes, and tumor stage T4.6

In recent years, a remarkable finding has been highlighted by numerous studies: patients with T4N0 (stage IIB/IIC) colon cancer demonstrate survival outcomes that are similar to or even worse than those of patients with T2–3N1 (stage IIIA/B) disease, despite having no lymph node involvement. Large-scale database analyses have demonstrated that, even after adjustment for factors such as adequate lymph node retrieval and the receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy, patients with T4N0 disease exhibit inferior overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) compared with those with stage IIIA disease.7,8 This persistent “survival paradox” suggests that T4 lesions may possess inherently more aggressive biological behavior, and that the current staging system may not adequately reflect this biological heterogeneity.

In light of these findings, the necessity of evaluating and managing T4N0 colon cancers in a manner similar to stage III disease has been increasingly discussed in recent literature. Accordingly, in the present study, we aimed to evaluate the long-term oncologic outcomes of patients diagnosed with non-metastatic T4N0 colon cancer at our center and to compare these outcomes with those of patients with T2–3N1 disease.

PATIENTS AND METHODS

Study population

Patients diagnosed with colon cancer between January 1, 1999, and December 31, 2020, at the Department of Medical Oncology, Marmara University Pendik Training and Research Hospital were retrospectively reviewed. Patients included in the study were those with histopathologically confirmed colon adenocarcinoma, without evidence of distant metastasis at diagnosis, who underwent curative-intent surgical resection and had pathologic T4N0 or T2–3N1 disease. Patients with tumors located at the rectosigmoid junction or more distal locations were excluded. Eligible patients were categorized into two groups according to pathological staging: T4N0 and T2–3N1. Adjuvant chemotherapy was administered to eligible patients according to contemporary National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) guidelines applicable at the time of treatment, considering the long study period (1999–2020). The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (approval date: 21 November 2025; protocol no: 09.2025.25–1019) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Data collection

Demographic characteristics, including age at diagnosis and sex, were recorded. Clinicopathological data collected included primary tumor location, presentation with bowel perforation or obstruction, histopathological tumor features, TNM stage, number of lymph nodes retrieved, receipt of adjuvant therapy and treatment regimens, development of disease recurrence or distant metastasis during follow-up, and mortality status, including the date of death when applicable. Tumor location was classified as right-sided colon cancer (cecum, ascending colon) or left-sided colon cancer (transverse colon, descending colon and sigmoid colon). Histopathological characteristics evaluated included tumor size, presence of vascular, lymphatic, and perineural invasion, and the total number of lymph nodes retrieved.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were given as n and percentages (%) whereas continuous variables were shown as median with interquartile range (IQR). Comparisons between the two groups were conducted using the Chi-square test and Student's t-test for categorical and continuous variables, respectively. DFS was defined as the interval between the date of surgery and the date of documented recurrence, distant metastasis, or death from any cause. OS was defined as the time from the date of diagnosis to the date of death or the last follow-up, whichever occurred first. Survival was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and survival curves were compared using the log-rank test. Median follow-up was calculated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were used to evaluate the impact of prognostic factors on survival outcomes. Variables with statistical significance or a trend (P<.2) in univariate Cox regression analyses were subsequently included in the multivariate model to identify independent prognostic factors, and results were reported with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). To address potential confounding related to treatment heterogeneity, additional subgroup analyses were performed among patients who received oxaliplatin-based adjuvant chemotherapy. Overall survival and disease-free survival were re-evaluated in this subgroup using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared with the log-rank test. A two-sided P<.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Overall, 273 patients with a median age of 60 years (IQR: 53–70) were included in the analysis, of whom 60.8% (n=166) were males and 53.8% (n=147) had left-sided primary tumors. Seventy-eight cases (28.6%) had T4N0 and 195 (71.4%) had T2–3N1 disease. Among patients with T4 disease, information on T4 sub-classification was available only for a subset of cases, as this distinction was not consistently reported in earlier pathology records. Among patients with available data, 30 were classified as T4a and 3 as T4b. While there were no statistically significant differences observed in age, gender and primary tumor location between the two groups, some clinicopathological features differed notably. A higher incidence of obstruction was noted in the T4N0 group compared with the T2–3N1 group (35% vs. 14.0%, P=.002). On the other hand, a higher rate of lymphatic invasion was observed in the T2–3N1 group (70% vs. 51.4%, P=.009). The median follow-up of the overall population was 72.0 months (95% CI 62.4–81.6). The median follow-up duration was 74.6 (range, 66.5–82.7) months for patients in the T4N0 group and 72.0 (range, 60.2–83.6) months for those in the T2–3N1 group. The median OS was 92 months (range, NR) in the T4N0 group and 153.8 (range, 99.1–208.4) months in the T2–3N1 group. Five-year OS rates were 66.6% and 79.8% in the T4N0 and T2–3N1 groups, respectively. Kaplan–Meier survival curves demonstrated significantly poorer OS in the T4N0 group compared with the T2–3N1 group (HR=1.72, 95% CI 1.06–2.75; P=.02) (Figure 1).

During follow-up, patients with T4N0 disease experienced significantly higher rates of distant metastasis (28% vs. 16.4%, P=.04) and locoregional recurrence (18% vs. 7.2%, P=.01) than those with T2–3N1 disease. During follow-up, metastatic disease developed in 22 patients in the T4N0 group and in 32 patients in the T2–3N1 group. As several patients had metastases involving more than one anatomical site, the distribution of metastatic involvement was analyzed based on the total number of metastatic sites rather than the number of patients. In the T4N0 group, a total of 33 metastatic sites were identified. The most frequent site of metastasis was the intra-abdominal lymph nodes (n=10, 30%), followed by the peritoneum (n=8, 24%) and the liver (n=8, 24%). In contrast, the T2–3N1 group demonstrated a different metastatic pattern, with a total of 41 metastatic sites recorded. The liver was the most common site of metastasis (n=21, 51.2%), followed by the lung (n=7, 17.1%) and the peritoneum (n=5, 12.2%).

Five-year DFS rates were 61% for the T4N0 group and 74.4% for the T2–3N1 group. T4N0 stage was associated with an increased risk of disease recurrence (HR: 1.66, 95% CI 1.03–2.65; P=.03) (Figure 2). In univariate Cox regression analysis, age ≥65 years (HR 1.69, 95% CI 1.07–2.65; P=.02), pathological stage T4N0 (HR 1.66, 95% CI 1.04–2.66; P=.04), vascular invasion (HR 1.97, 95% CI 1.21–3.20; P=.006), and omission of oxaliplatin-based adjuvant chemotherapy (HR 2.21, 95% CI 1.41–3.46; P<.001) were significantly associated with worse survival outcomes. In multivariate analysis, vascular invasion (HR 2.03, 95% CI 1.10–3.74; P=.02) and not receiving oxaliplatin-based adjuvant chemotherapy (HR 2.00, 95% CI 1.22–3.29; P=.006) remained independent predictors of poor survival. Although age ≥65 years (HR 1.52, 95% CI 0.93–2.48; P=.09) and pathological stage T4N0 (HR 1.65, 95% CI 0.98–2.77; P=.06) showed a trend toward worse outcomes, these associations did not reach statistical significance after adjustment for confounding factors (Table 4).

In subgroup analyses restricted to patients who received oxaliplatin-based adjuvant chemotherapy, disease-free survival was shorter in the T4N0 group compared with the T2–3N1 group (mean DFS: 132.4 months [95% CI, 104.9–159.9] vs 204.9 months [95% CI, 188.9–220.9], respectively), with borderline statistical significance (log-rank p=0.088). In contrast, overall survival did not differ significantly between the groups in this subgroup (mean OS: 132.8 months [95% CI, 105.2–160.3] vs 166.6 months [95% CI, 142.6–190.5], respectively; log-rank p=.456).

DISCUSSION

Our study found that the OS outcomes of patients diagnosed with T4N0 colon cancer who underwent curative resection were significantly worse survival than T2-3N1 patients. Even after adjusting for age and other clinical-pathological factors in multivariate analyses, the T4N0 pathological stage remained an independent adverse prognostic factor for OS. This key finding constitutes the main result of our study and suggests that the prognostic impact of tumor depth may be more dominant than nodal involvement in some patient groups. Our results shed light on the T4N0–T2–3N1 survival paradox, which has been debated in the literature for a long time, and its related clinical endpoints.

In large-scale population-based studies, it has been demonstrated that the survival outcomes of T4N0 patients are worse than those of selected node-positive (particularly stage IIIA) patients, even when adequate lymph node dissection and adjuvant chemotherapy are administered. Current and comprehensive NCDB analyses have revealed that this paradox persists even in the 8th edition of the AJCC TNM classification and have suggested that the T stage may be more predictive than nodal status in some subgroups.7–9 Our study, supported by real-world data, once again emphasizes that T4N0 disease is a clinically high-risk subgroup.

This survival difference between subgroups may be partly due to heterogeneity in treatment patterns. In our study, the higher proportion of patients not receiving adjuvant chemotherapy in the T4N0 group and the relatively lower use of oxaliplatin-based regimens suggest that the risk of early recurrence and mortality may not have been sufficiently suppressed in this group. This situation demonstrates that the perception of T4N0 disease as lower risk in daily clinical practice due to its nodal negativity may affect treatment intensity and, consequently, oncological outcomes. Similarly, the literature suggests that the heterogeneous use of adjuvant therapy among high-risk stage II patients may further contribute to disparities in survival outcomes.9 On the other hand, lymphatic invasion and the number of lymph nodes removed (<12), which were significant in univariate analysis in our study, lost their significance in multivariate models, supporting that the observed survival difference cannot be explained solely by inadequate surgical staging.

Another important observation supporting the overall survival findings in our study is the disease-free survival analyses. At 5 years of follow-up, the DFS rate was 61% in the T4N0 group and 74.4% in the T2–3N1 group. Pathological stage T4N0 was associated with an increased risk of disease recurrence compared with T2–3N1 disease (HR=1.66; 95% CI 1.03–2.65; P=.03). The borderline significance after adjustment suggests that the effect on DFS may be partially mediated by interactions with other clinical-pathological factors and treatment patterns. However, the literature reports that T4 tumors are associated with a higher risk of recurrence and shorter DFS regardless of nodal status, and that this unfavorable course may persist even in patient groups who have undergone adequate lymph node dissection and adjuvant therapy. One possible mechanism is the aggressive local biology of T4 tumors. Complete invasion of the bowel wall, direct contact with the peritoneal surface, and involvement of adjacent structures may contribute to early disease recurrence by paving the way for microscopic peritoneal spread that is clinically and radiologically undetectable at the time of diagnosis. Current studies suggest that the fact that a significant proportion of recurrences in T4 disease show a peritoneal pattern may be one of the key factors explaining the loss of DFS.11,12 These findings underscore the importance of meticulous surgical technique, adequate resection margins, and heightened intra-abdominal surveillance in patients with T4N0 disease.

In our study, the identification of advanced age (≥65 years) as an independent poor prognostic factor for OS is consistent with the literature. In large population-based analyses, age has been considered a marker associated not only with biological aging but also with comorbidity burden, access to treatment, and the feasibility of adjuvant chemotherapy.13 This finding suggests that the aggressive biology of T4N0 disease may lead to more pronounced clinical outcomes in the elderly population. The underrepresentation of elderly patients in clinical randomized trials has led to more cautious adjuvant treatment decisions in this population. Although the addition of oxaliplatin has improved disease-free and overall survival in the general patient population, careful patient selection is recommended due to the significantly increased risk of toxicity and the more limited or uncertain survival benefit in elderly patients.14–17 In this context, the fact that a significant proportion of patients in our study were elderly can be considered one of the possible reasons for the lower preference for adjuvant chemotherapy regimens containing oxaliplatin.

In our study, vascular invasion was found to be an independent prognostic factor for both OS and DFS, consistent with the literature.18,19 This can be explained by its direct reflection of hematological spread biology. In colon tumors, venous drainage is largely directed to the liver via the portal vein system and creates an anatomical “shortcut” that increases the risk of early systemic recurrence, particularly liver metastasis, in the presence of venous invasion. Furthermore, venous invasion/EMVI is considered a biomarker indicating more aggressive subclones capable of passing critical steps in the metastasis cascade, such as intravasation, survival in circulation, and colonization in distant organs.20,21 In contrast, the loss of independence of lymphatic invasion and the number of lymph nodes removed (<12) in multivariate analyses may be attributed to the fact that the prognostic impact of these parameters is primarily related to staging accuracy in T3N0 (stage IIA) patients. The literature also indicates that the prognostic importance of inadequate lymph node sampling is limited to T3N0 patients; in T4N0 and node-positive (T2–3N1) disease, tumor biology predominantly determines the prognostic effect.8,22 The fact that lymphatic invasion is often collinear with nodal involvement may reduce its independent prognostic contribution when nodal status is included in the model; however, vascular invasion reflects systemic spread capacity independently of nodal status and therefore retains its prognostic power. This biological framework offers one possible mechanism that could explain why the survival paradox in T4N0 disease persists even when corrected by factors such as adequate lymph node dissection and adjuvant therapy. In our cohort, vascular invasion emerged as the most consistent independent predictor across both OS and DFS models, suggesting that tumor biology rather than nodal status alone primarily drives prognosis in T4N0 disease.

In this study, not receiving adjuvant chemotherapy or receiving only fluoropyrimidine (FP) therapy was an independent poor prognostic factor for both OS and DFS is consistent with the current literature for both subgroups. When analyses were restricted to patients who received oxaliplatin-based adjuvant chemotherapy, the difference in overall survival between the groups was not significant, whereas disease-free survival remained shorter in the T4N0 group with borderline significance. This pattern suggests that treatment heterogeneity may partly contribute to the overall survival difference observed in the full cohort, while the higher risk of recurrence in T4N0 disease may persist even under standard treatment. These findings support the contribution of tumor biology in addition to treatment-related factors. In T2–3N1 (stage III) colon cancer, strong randomized evidence demonstrates that oxaliplatin-based adjuvant therapy (FOLFOX or CAPOX) provides long-term DFS and OS benefits, whereas FP monotherapy or complete omission of adjuvant therapy in this patient group is associated with inadequate systemic control.3,4 Although the IDEA consortium demonstrated that treatment duration could be shortened based on risk, it emphasizes that regimens containing oxaliplatin remain important, particularly in high-risk stage III biology, and that reducing treatment intensity may increase the risk of recurrence.23 Similarly, in T4N0 disease, the T4 stage alone is associated with a high risk of recurrence and mortality, and current guidelines recommend considering adjuvant therapy in this group. Large observational series and reviews show that not receiving adjuvant therapy or limiting treatment to FP alone in T4N0 patients is associated with a loss of DFS and OS; in some subgroups, this risk may be similar to or worse than that seen in selected stage III patients.7–9,24

Our study has some limitations. The retrospective and single-center design carries the risk of selection bias. The limited availability of data on molecular markers and the inability to analyze the T4a–T4b subgroups separately are important limitations. The subdivision of T4 tumors into T4a and T4b has been incorporated into the AJCC TNM staging system since the 7th edition; however, this distinction was not consistently reported in pathology records in earlier years of our cohort. As a result, T4 sub-classification was available only for a limited number of patients, precluding meaningful subgroup analyses. Again, because differentiation/grade and microsatellite instability were not consistently reported in the pathology report, we were unable to assess their effect on survival. Although mismatch repair (MMR) status could be assessed in a limited number of patients, no significant imbalance was found between the groups. This finding suggests that the observed survival difference cannot be explained solely by MSI/dMMR biology. The lack of consistent documentation of EMVI, tumor deposits, and margin status in historical pathology reports limited their inclusion in survival modeling, which may have influenced risk stratification accuracy. The limited sample size in subgroup analyses may have reduced the statistical power to detect significant differences. However, the long follow-up period, the use of OS as the primary endpoint, the supportive presentation of DFS analyses, and the use of multivariate statistical approaches constitute the strengths of our study.

In conclusion, this study suggests that T4N0 colon cancer represents a biologically aggressive subgroup associated with poorer long-term survival compared to T2–3N1 disease, and that the current TNM staging system may not adequately reflect the prognosis of this patient group. It may be rational to manage T4N0 patients with more intensive and standardized strategies similar to those used for T2–3N1 disease. We recommend that peritoneal metastasis be thoroughly evaluated prior to treatment in T4N0 disease. Prospective, multicenter studies that thoroughly evaluate molecular profiling, recurrence patterns, and treatment responses will clarify risk classification and adjuvant treatment decisions in this high-risk patient group.

Full-stage simulation and validation of multi-body incremental launching mechanics for complex curve bridges
Authors: Lin Xiao, Hehui Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To address mechanical analysis challenges of multi-body incremental launching for complex curve bridges in urban reconstruction, traditional methods suffer from incomplete working condition coverage, geometric model distortion, and inadequate core component simulation. This study establishes a refined full-stage calculation system: proposes a mathematical expression of the launching process and automatic constraint state judgment method, develops a finite element strategy (rapid 1D linear modeling and accurate 3D spatial mapping), and designs a Python-based automated program for MIDAS MCT command stream generation. Validated by the Guangzhou-Shenzhen Expressway reconstruction project, it covers 771 working conditions, accurately extracts key mechanical responses (support reactions, ductile hinge shear forces, displacements, stresses), and quantifies the influence of linear curvature and torsion. The method mitigates the shortcomings of traditional approaches in terms of computational efficiency, completeness of working condition coverage, and geometric fidelity, establishing an efficient automated analysis process that covers the entire construction stage. It promotes the digital and intelligent evolution of incremental launching mechanical analysis, offering technical support for safe and precise bridge construction in complex environments.

Introduction

The incremental launching method (ILM) is widely recognized as a preferred construction technology for bridge engineering in complex urban environments, thanks to its advantages of minimal under-bridge space occupation, high construction automation, and reduced interference with existing traffic1,2. Zhao et al.3 reviewed the research status and development trend of ILM, noting that its application in complex curve bridges is a core direction for future development. As infrastructure development shifts from large-scale new construction to renovation, expansion, and performance upgrading, especially in densely populated urban areas and mature expressway networks, bridge construction faces stringent constraints from existing structures, underground pipelines, and continuous traffic flow4,5. To adapt to such complex spatial limitations, newly built bridges often adopt sophisticated composite alignments with spatial variable curves, posing unprecedented challenges to traditional monolithic ILM6.

Traditional monolithic ILM tends to generate significant lateral deviations when applied to spatial variable-curve bridges, mainly due to the mismatch between structural alignment and linear launching path, ultimately failing to reach the designed position7,8. Granata9 confirmed that lateral stability control is the key bottleneck of curved bridge launching through research on small-radius continuous curved girders under complex conditions. To address this, the innovative multi-body coordinated incremental launching technology has been proposed and applied in engineering practice. By incorporating ductile hinges between adjacent girder segments, it enables limited relative rotation, transforming the entire structure into a semi-rigid flexible system composed of multiple substructures10. During launching, each substructure can adaptively adjust to spatial curve variations via hinge rotation, realizing continuous unidirectional launching for long-span variable-curvature bridges and overcoming the limitation of narrow construction sites where multiple assembly points cannot be set. Typical cases like the overline incremental launching of S-shaped complex curved steel trough girders have verified its engineering feasibility11.

However, the introduction of ductile hinges and multi-body coordination significantly increases mechanical analysis complexity. The structural system dynamically evolves from a statically determinate continuous girder to a time-varying girder-hinge multi-body system, where internal force states, support reactions, and constraint conditions change synchronously with the launching process and hinge connection status9,12. Conventional simplified analysis methods relying on manually preset key working conditions can no longer meet the requirements of high-precision full-process design and safety control for such complex structures. A review of existing research reveals three core limitations in the mechanical analysis of multi-body coordinated incremental launching for spatial variable-curve bridges.

First, insufficient coverage of construction working conditions. The actual incremental launching process involves hundreds or even thousands of stepping stages, but traditional methods only select limited typical conditions for verification. This experience-dependent selection leads to incomplete calculation coverage, failing to capture the true most unfavorable mechanical states and introducing potential safety hazards13. For example, Wu et al6. applied ILM to curved steel box continuous girder bridges but only verified 8 key stages, resulting in unforeseen local stress concentration. Similarly, Wu14 adopted intelligent stepping multi-point launching for approach steel box girders but ignored transient stress responses during segment docking due to limited condition selection, deviating from actual monitoring data.

Second, geometric model distortion in numerical simulation. To simplify modeling, most existing studies reduce spatial variable-curve bridges to 2D planar or 1D linear models, ignoring the effects of spatial alignment (especially torsion deformation of small-curvature-radius girders) on internal force redistribution, support reaction distribution, and hinge stress10. Relevant studies15–17 have shown that model simplification leads to deviations in stress and deformation prediction, and only 3D models can fully capture coupled flexural and torsional effects18, while 2D modeling fails to reflect the bending-torsion coupling of special section girders19.

Third, inadequate simulation of core ductile hinges. As a novel component in ILM, ductile hinges lack direct simulation literature. Their mechanical characteristics (semi-rigid connection, multi-directional constraint release, shear-rotation coupling) differ from fully rigid or flexible connections, requiring accurate simulation of constraint release and vertical shear transmission10. Although studies on semi-rigid connections and plastic hinges exist20, they have not integrated multi-body coordinated movement mechanisms with hinge properties, leaving a gap in dedicated simulation methods for such systems.

In terms of automated analysis, recent studies have made preliminary progress but have limitations. Gu et al.21 proposed a rapid construction stage definition method for ILM mechanical analysis but confined it to planar straight bridges. Their method calculates construction stages using matrix displacement and idealized boundary conditions, without addressing spatial curve effects or multi-body coordination. In contrast, our method explicitly incorporates 3D spatial coordinate mapping from 1D linear models, enabling full geometric fidelity for spatial variable-curve bridges. Zhou22 developed an automated simulation script based on ABAQUS secondary development for single main structures, which struggles to simulate multi-body collaborative systems with complex connections like ductile hinges. Our method, by contrast, specifically addresses the ductile hinge as a semi-rigid connector through a dedicated simplified simulation approach, filling the gap in dedicated hinge modeling for multi-body launching systems. Thus, an integrated automated analysis tool covering full-stage, all-working-condition, and high-precision mechanical simulation for spatial variable-curve bridges with multi-body coordination is lacking.

To address these gaps, this study systematically establishes a refined full-stage mechanical calculation method system for multi-body incremental launching of complex curve bridges. The main objectives are:To propose a mathematical expression of the entire launching process and an automatic state judgment method for structures and supports, laying a foundation for programmatic analysis;To develop a finite element modeling strategy combining rapid 1D linear modeling and precise 3D spatial coordinate mapping, balancing efficiency and geometric fidelity;To design a Python-based automated program for MIDAS MCT command stream generation, realizing batch construction stage definition and full-working-condition traversal calculation;To verify the method’s effectiveness through a real-world engineering case and quantify the influence of spatial alignment on mechanical responses.

To simulate the spatial support of walking-type incremental launching equipment and the effects of alignment curvature and torsional deformation on the structural mechanical behavior, fishbone nodes are set on both sides of the main girder, which are connected to the main girder nodes via rigid links. The fishbone nodes corresponding to activated piers serve as support nodes, and compression-only vertical constraints can be applied through the node elastic support command.

Ductile hinge simulation and spatial geometric mapping method

As a semi-rigid connection structure, the ductile hinge permits limited relative rotation and longitudinal/transversal displacement between adjacent girder segments during incremental launching, while functioning to transmit vertical shear forces. To achieve accurate simulation of the ductile hinge in finite element models, the following simplified simulation method is proposed: a hinge core node Nhinge is set at the center of the cantilever girder at the portal frame, which is connected to the lower fishbone node Nfishbone via an elastic connection with only axial SDx constraint; Nhinge is connected to the fishbone node Nroot at the root of the cantilever girder via a rigid link to ensure coordinated deformation between the hinge core and the girder body. The simplified simulation method is illustrated in Fig. 3. The justification for this simplification is as follows: First, the primary mechanical function of the ductile hinge during incremental launching is vertical shear transfer, which is the dominant internal force at the hinge location. Second, the relative rotation between adjacent segments is inherently accommodated by the structural design of the hinge (i.e., the clearance in the pinned connection), and thus does not require explicit rotational constraint modeling in the elastic connection. Third, the rigid link between Nhinge and Nroot ensures coordinated deformation between the hinge core and the girder body, effectively capturing the overall coupling behavior of the hinge zone.Fig. 3Simplified simulation of ductile hinges. The elastic connection stiffness in the SDx direction is rigid, and the rigid link has infinite stiffness in all six degrees of freedom.

Simplified simulation of ductile hinges. The elastic connection stiffness in the SDx direction is rigid, and the rigid link has infinite stiffness in all six degrees of freedom.

The adequacy of this hinge simulation approach is indirectly supported by the overall agreement between simulated support reactions and preliminary field monitoring data, the consistency of simulated hinge shear force patterns with expected mechanical behavior (e.g., shear force reversal before and after passing piers), and the successful application of similar elastic connection-based simulation methods in prior bridge engineering studies10,20. However, direct experimental validation of the hinge model through laboratory testing remains an important future work direction, particularly to quantify the effects of shear-rotation coupling and fatigue loading on hinge performance.

To balance modeling efficiency and geometric fidelity, an equidistant node model is first established on a one-dimensional straight line, and all construction stages are defined accordingly. Subsequently, the node mileages in the linear model are mapped to the corresponding 3D spatial coordinates based on the actual horizontal and vertical alignments of the bridge, thereby accurately restoring the spatial variable-curve geometric configuration of the structure. The specific implementation of the coordinate mapping is as follows: For each node in the 1D linear model with mileage l_i, the corresponding 3D spatial coordinates (Xi, Yi, Zi) are determined based on the bridge’s horizontal and vertical alignment parameters. The horizontal alignment is described by a chain of circular curves and transition curves (clothoids), where the planar coordinates (Xi, Yi) are computed by numerical integration of the heading angle along the alignment. The vertical alignment is described by parabolic vertical curves, where the elevation Zi is computed from the gradient and vertical curve parameters at the corresponding mileage. To evaluate the mapping accuracy, the mapped coordinates of 42 pier locations and 10 hinge positions were compared with the original design coordinates, showing maximum deviations of less than 1 mm in all three directions, which confirms the accuracy and reliability of the mapping process.

While this simplified simulation approach has proven effective in engineering practice, it should be noted that the SDx-only constraint does not explicitly model potential shear-rotation coupling effects at the hinge. Future work should include experimental validation of the hinge model and, if necessary, incorporation of multi-degree-of-freedom coupling in the elastic connection to improve simulation fidelity.

Engineering application

Engineering background

This study takes the upper viaduct of the Taiping-Wudianmei Interchange in the reconstruction and expansion project of the Suiwan Section of the Guangzhou-Shenzhen Expressway (under construction) in China as the application object. The bridge is located in Humen Town, Dongguan City, serving as a three-dimensional expansion project of the expressway main line, with a total length of 3.773 km. The main body of the bridge adopts an integral single-box three-chamber steel box girder structure, with a standard deck width of 33.5 m. The girder height is divided into 2.5 m (standard span) and 3.6 m (long-span section). The horizontal and vertical alignments of the bridge (Fig. 5) are complex, exhibiting significant spatial variable-curve characteristics. After comprehensive comparison and selection, traditional technologies such as span-by-span incremental launching and segmented multi-point incremental launching were excluded due to high safety risks, low construction efficiency, or failure to meet the conditions for setting up temporary piers. Therefore, the innovative multi-body collaborative incremental launching construction technology was adopted. By installing multiple sets of ductile hinges on the entire bridge, the structure is transformed into a multi-body collaborative system, enabling each girder segment to adapt to alignment changes during the launching process. Thus, under extremely restricted site conditions, the safe and precise erection of the ultra-long, variable-curve steel box girder is realized. Full-stage finite element modeling for multi-body collaborative incremental launching

Based on the calculation method proposed in this paper, a full-stage structural finite element analysis model for the multi-body collaborative incremental launching of the spatial variable-curve bridge in this project is established. Both steel box girders and launching noses are simulated using beam elements, and they are connected via the co-node method. The supports provided by each launching pier to fishbone nodes are simulated using compression-only elastic supports. To prevent rigid body displacement in the model and account for the friction effect between the bottom of steel girders and launching equipment, a lateral constraint stiffness of 1 kN/mm (in SDX and SDY directions) is assigned at support nodes, while all rotational degrees of freedom are released21. In the model, the front and rear launching noses are simplified based on the principle of equivalent bending stiffness and self-weight, and a single main girder element is adopted to simulate the actual separated double-main-girder plus cross-beam system. Only the self-weight of the structure is considered in the load application. The top and bottom plates of the main girder are made of Q355D steel, and the web is made of Q420D steel; the launching noses are made of Q355B steel, with the elastic modulus of all steel materials taken as 2.06 × 106 MPa. The finite element model is shown in Fig. 10.Fig. 10Full-stage structural analysis MIDAS model of multi-body coordination incremental launching.

Full-stage structural analysis MIDAS model of multi-body coordination incremental launching.

The model comprises a total of 61,313 nodes and 20,430 elements, with the element length for both the front and rear launching noses and the steel box girders uniformly set to 0.1 m. A total of 40,902 elastic connections are associated with the fishbone girders and ductile hinges. In actual construction, steel box girder alignment adjustment is performed after the walking-type jacks complete 10 consecutive small launching steps (with a step distance of 0.45 m). To reduce the computational scale, a launching step of 4.5 m is adopted in the model, and a total of 771 launching working conditions are simulated, which basically cover the most unfavorable states of various key parameters. Furthermore, to quantitatively evaluate the influence of the spatial variable-curve alignment on structural internal force redistribution, the one-dimensional linear fishbone girder model generated during the modeling process is used as a reference to conduct a comparative analysis of the degree of influence of the spatial alignment on support reactions and shear forces at ductile hinges. It should be noted that while the 4.5 m launching step captures most unfavorable static states during the launching phase, it may not fully capture transient peak responses during segment docking moments, as the actual construction involves 10 consecutive small steps of 0.45 m each between alignment adjustments. However, the most critical mechanical states (e.g., maximum support reactions and hinge shear forces) typically occur during the launching phase rather than the docking moment, as confirmed by the 771 working conditions analyzed in this study.

Shear force analysis of ductile hinges

Among all 10 groups of ductile hinges, the shear force distribution exhibits a clear pattern: hinges located at or near the long-span section (Hinges 8–10) generally experience higher shear forces than those at standard sections, primarily due to the increased girder weight (girder height of 3.6 m vs. 2.5 m) and longer span lengths in this region. The maximum shear force of each hinge is closely related to the span length of the adjacent support, with larger spans producing higher peak shear forces. This distribution pattern is consistent with the structural mechanics principle that shear forces at hinge locations are proportional to the load and span characteristics of the adjacent structural segments.

Analysis shows that the left shear force of Hinge 10 reaches the largest peak value. The shear force curve at this hinge versus launching distance (Fig. 13) reveals that the shear force of ductile hinges is closely related to the passing-pier state. The envelope curves of stresses for the main girder and launching noses are extracted (Fig. 18). Analysis of the influence of spatial alignment on calculation results

To accurately evaluate the influence of spatial alignment on structural internal forces, the left and right support reactions of LS7 support and the left and right shear forces of Hinge 5 (with the largest difference in left and right shear forces among all hinges) are selected for comparison with the calculation results of the corresponding linear model. Difference curve between reaction force of LS7 and linear model.

Difference curve between left and right shear force of hinge 5 and linear model.

The influence mechanism of spatial alignment on structural internal forces can be attributed to three factors: (1) Horizontal curves induce torsional deformation in the girder cross-section, leading to uneven distribution of support reactions between the left and right sides, as evidenced by the complementary characteristics shown in Fig. 19; (2) Vertical curves affect the longitudinal force distribution by modifying the effective span geometry and the angle between the girder axis and support directions, contributing to the shear force deviations observed at ductile hinges; (3) The coupling effect of horizontal and vertical curves generates additional internal forces, particularly torsion and lateral bending moments, that are not captured by the linear model. The relatively small deviations observed in this study (1.4% for support reactions, 7.1% for hinge shear forces) are primarily attributed to the large radii of the horizontal and vertical curves in the second section, which limit the magnitude of torsional and geometric coupling effects.

The analysis indicates that affected by the horizontal and vertical curves of the route, the difference in left and right support reactions of the launching support relative to the linear model exhibits complementary characteristics, while the difference in left and right shear forces of the hinge is basically symmetric about the zero axis, reflecting the internal force redistribution effect. The maximum deviation of the total support reaction of LS7 relative to the linear model is 121.6 kN, accounting for 1.4% of the maximum value in the linear model; the maximum deviation of the left and right shear forces of Hinge No.5 relative to the linear model is 234.5 kN, accounting for 7.1%. Due to the large radii of the horizontal and vertical curves of the launching path in the second section, the overall influence of alignment is relatively small. The method proposed in this paper can quantitatively analyze the influence of route geometric characteristics on structural internal forces and deformations, providing reliable data support and a technical path for the refined design of temporary structures and construction control.

Research limitations

While the method proposed in this study has been successfully applied in the Guangzhou-Shenzhen Expressway reconstruction and expansion project, several limitations remain and warrant further investigation in future work:To balance computational efficiency with engineering practicality, several simplifications were adopted in the analysis. For instance, the ductile hinge was simulated using an elastic connection that constrains only the axial direction, which did not fully account for its own structural deformation, slip along the length of the cantilever girder, or potential fatigue effects. Furthermore, the analysis considered only the self-weight of the structure and did not include environmental and construction loads such as temperature variations, wind loads, or the dynamic response of the launching equipment.To manage computational scale, a launching step of 4.5 m was used in the model (merging 10 actual consecutive small steps). While this can capture most unfavorable static states, it may smooth out peak dynamic responses during certain transient construction phases, such as the moment of segment docking.The current analysis is based on ideal geometry and perfect construction sequences. Practical factors like fabrication and installation tolerances, uneven foundation settlement, and synchronization deviations of launching equipment could induce additional structural forces. The present method does not yet integrate these uncertainties or simulate feedback control adjustments based on real-time monitoring. Additionally, while preliminary field monitoring data from strain gauges and displacement sensors at selected piers and ductile hinges have shown general agreement with simulation results (deviations within 10–15%), comprehensive monitoring data covering all construction stages are not yet available due to the ongoing nature of the project. A full validation against field monitoring data will be conducted upon project completion and reported in future work.The automated workflow is deeply reliant on the generation of MIDAS MCT command streams. Its migration to other general-purpose finite element platforms would require additional development efforts. These limitations may impact the generalizability of the findings in the following ways: the ductile hinge simplification may not be directly applicable to bridges with significantly different hinge designs or loading conditions; the 4.5 m launching step may not capture all critical transient states for bridges with more complex alignment transitions; and the MIDAS-dependent workflow limits immediate applicability to projects using other finite element platforms.

Conclusions

Addressing the limitations of traditional methods in working condition coverage, geometric simulation, and hinge joint simulation, this paper proposes and establishes a systematic and refined calculation method for incremental launching construction. The main research conclusions and achievements are summarized as follows:A mathematical expression for the entire incremental launching process is proposed, clarifying the dynamic correlation logic of "activated components—support constraints—hinge node states", which solves the problem of difficult programmatic description of the multi-body collaborative incremental launching process for long-span continuous multi-span bridges. An innovative finite element model generation strategy of "first one-dimensional linear modeling, then spatial coordinate mapping" is developed, which not only avoids the complexity of direct modeling of spatial curves but also ensures geometric fidelity through precise coordinate restoration, effectively overcoming the result distortion caused by traditional dimension reduction analysis.An automated generation program for MIDAS MCT command streams based on Python is developed, realizing full-process automation from basic data reading, model construction, construction stage definition to full-working-condition traversal calculation. This program achieves automation from basic data to full working condition calculation, substantially improving analysis efficiency. By covering 771 construction stages, it significantly reduces the risk of omitting the most unfavorable states due to empirical selection of working conditions, thereby enhancing the systematicity and safety of the analysis.Through case verification on the reconstruction and expansion project of the Suiwan Section of the Guangzhou-Shenzhen Expressway (with a launching weight of 36,000 tons and a launching mileage of 3,508 m), full-process mechanical responses such as support reactions (maximum 10,721 kN), ductile hinge shear forces (maximum 2,081 kN), main girder displacements (−100.9 ~ 4.9 mm) and stresses (−83.1 ~ 39.9 MPa) are successfully extracted, and all indicators meet the design requirements. Comparative analysis quantifies the influence of spatial alignment: the maximum deviation of support reactions is 1.4%, and the maximum deviation of hinge shear forces is 7.1%, providing accurate data support for the design of temporary structures and construction control.The automated analysis program constructed in this study provides a feasible solution for achieving full-stage mechanical analysis of multi-body incremental launching for complex curve bridges. This tool contributes to enhancing the safety and controllability of bridge construction under harsh conditions such as urban areas and complex alignments, and plays a positive role in promoting the transformation of mechanical analysis for incremental launching construction towards dynamic refinement and digital-intelligent analysis.

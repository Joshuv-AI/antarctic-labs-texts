Abstract
. In the present paper we characterize the removable sets for solutions of the fractional heat equation satisfying some parabolic BMO or Lip α \text{Lip}_{\alpha} normalization conditions. We do this by introducing associated fractional caloric capacities, that we show to be comparable to a certain parabolic Hausdorff content. AMS 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 42B20 (primary); 28A12 (secondary). Keywords: Fractional heat equation, singular integrals.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we characterize removable sets for solutions of the fractional heat equation under certain parabolic BMO or Lip
α
normalization conditions. Our main motivation stems from the results obained in
[
MaPrTo
]
and
[
MaPr
]
.
The study conducted by Mateu, Prat and Tolsa in
[
MaPrTo
]
explores removable singularities for regular
(1,1/2)-
Lipschitz solutions of the classical heat equation, associated with the operator

\Theta:=(-\Delta_{x})+\partial_{t},\;\mbox{ where }\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Here
(-\Delta_{x})
is the usual Laplacian, computed with respect to the spatial variables. In
[
MaPr
]
, the authors extend the study to the fractional heat equation, defined via the
s
-heat operator

\Theta^{s}:=(-\Delta_{x})^{s}+\partial_{t},\;s\in(0,1].

For
s=1
, we recover the classical heat equation, while for
s<1
, the operator
(-\Delta_{x})^{s}
, commonly referred to as the
s
-fractional Laplacian or
s
-Laplacian, requires an alternative definition. It is typically introduced through its Fourier transform:

\widehat{(-\Delta_{x})^{s}}f(\xi,t)=|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{f}(\xi,t),

or via the singular integral representation

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{x})^{s}f(x,t)

\displaystyle=c_{n,s}\;\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x,t)-f(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=c_{n,s}^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x+y,t)-2f(x,t)+f(x-y,t)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

These representations are equivalent and highlight that
(-\Delta_{x})^{s}
is no longer a local operator and that as
s\to 1
, one recovers the expression of
(-\Delta_{x})
. The reader may consult
[
DPV
, §3]
or
[
St
]
for details on the properties of
(-\Delta_{x})^{s}
.

To study removable sets in this context, we introduce the
s
-
parabolic distance
between two points
\overline{x}:=(x,t),\,\overline{y}:=(y,\tau)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, defined as

|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}=\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{x},\overline{y}):=\max\big\{|x-y|,|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2s}}\big\},\qquad\text{for }\,0<s\leq 1.

This leads naturally to the notions of
s
-parabolic cubes and
s
-parabolic balls. We convey that
B(\overline{x},r)
will be the
s
-
parabolic ball
centered at
\overline{x}
with radius
r
, where the spatial coordinates are contained in a Euclidean ball
B_{1}
of radius
r
, while the temporal coordinate lies in a real interval
I
of length
(2r)^{2s}
. On the other hand, an
s
-
parabolic cube

Q
of side length
\ell
is a set of the form

I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{n}\times I_{n+1},

where
I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}
are intervals of length
\ell
, while
I_{n+1}
is another interval of length
\ell^{2s}
. We write
\ell(Q)=\ell
.

Let us recall that a function
f
is said to be
(1,1/2)
-Lipschitz regular if, as precised in
[
MaPrTo
]
, it is such that

(1.1)

\|\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}<\infty,\hskip 28.45274pt\|\partial_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{\ast,p_{1}}<\infty.

Here, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\ast,p_{1}}
stands for the usual BMO norm of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
but computed with respect to
1
-
parabolic
cubes. As shown by Hofmann and Lewis
[
Ho
, Lemma 1]
,
[
HoL
, Theorem 7.4]
, such functions satisfy

\|f\|_{\text{Lip}_{1/2},t}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
t,u\in\mathbb{R},t\neq u\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(x,t)-f(x,u)|}{|t-u|^{1/2}}\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}+\|\partial_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{\ast,p_{1}}.

Thus a
(1,1/2)-
Lipschitz function is Lipschitz in the spatial variables and 1/2-Lipschitz in time. This explains the term
(1,1/2)-
Lipschitz caloric capacity introduced in
[
MaPrTo
]
, defined for a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as

\Gamma_{\Theta}(E)=\sup\{|\langle\Theta f,1\rangle|\textbf{}\},

the supremum taken over all
(1,1/2)-
Lipschitz regular functions
f
satisfying the heat equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus E
and with the norms in (
1.1
) smaller or equal than one.
A key result in
[
MaPrTo
]
establishes the equivalence between the removability of the compact set
E
for
(1,1/2)-
Lipschitz solutions to the heat equation and the fact that
\Gamma_{\Theta}(E)
vanishes.

In this paper, we aim at characterizing different variants of the previous Lipschitz caloric capacity, replacing the previous estimates with parabolic BMO or a
\text{Lip}_{\alpha}
conditions for
\nabla_{x}f
and
\partial_{t}^{1/2}f
. More generally, we analyze removable sets for solutions of the
s
-fractional heat equation with
s
-parabolic gradient
(\nabla_{x},\partial_{t}^{\frac{1}{2s}})
satisfying either an
s
-parabolic
BMO
or
\text{Lip}_{\alpha}
condition. The reader who is not familiar with the notion of removability may conceive removable sets as those which “do not matter” when solving the
\Theta^{s}
-equation,
0<s\leq 1
. This has to be understood in the sense that any solution defined on their complement that satisfies the above
(1,\frac{1}{2s})
-gradient estimates, can be extended to verify the
\Theta^{s}
-equation throughout the entire domain, including the set itself.

Our main result characterizes removability in terms of two different capacities: one requiring solutions to the
\Theta^{s}
-equation satisfy
s
-parabolic BMO estimates, and another one requiring solutions satisfy
s
-parabolic
\text{Lip}_{\alpha}
bounds. These capacities, denoted by
\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},\ast}
and
\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},\alpha}
respectively, are related to certain
s
-parabolic Hausdorff content
\pazocal{H}^{m}_{\infty,p_{s}}
, which is defined as in the Euclidean case (see
[
Mat
]
, for instance), just replacing the Euclidean distance by the parabolic distance introduced above. Our main result reads as follows:

Theorem
.

Let
s\in(1/2,1]
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
compact set. Then,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},\ast}(E)

\displaystyle\approx_{n,s}\pazocal{H}^{n+1}_{\infty,p_{s}}(E),

\displaystyle\text{if $\alpha<2s-1$,}\quad\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},\alpha}(E)

\displaystyle\approx_{n,s,\alpha}\pazocal{H}^{n+1+\alpha}_{\infty,p_{s}}(E).

Moreover, the nullity of these capacities is equivalent to the removability of the corresponding compact set for solutions satisfying
(1,\frac{1}{2s})
-gradient estimates in either
s
-parabolic
BMO
or
\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha}
, assuming
\alpha<2s-1
in the latter case.

We further study the same type of question for a generalization of the capacities presented by Mateu and Prat in
[
MaPr
, §4 & §7]
. That is, we will ask for a characterization of removable sets for solutions to the
\Theta^{s}
-equation satisfying conditions of the form

\|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}f\|<\infty,\hskip 21.33955pt\|\partial_{t}^{\sigma/s}f\|<\infty,\qquad\text{$s\in(0,1]$ and $\sigma\in[0,s)$}.

Here the symbols
\|\cdot\|
can refer both to
s
-parabolic
BMO
norms or both to
s
-parabolic
\text{Lip}_{\alpha}
seminorms. We prove the following result:

Theorem
.

For any
s\in(0,1]
,
\sigma\in[0,s)
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
compact set,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Theta^{s},\ast}^{\sigma}(E)

\displaystyle\approx_{n,s,\sigma}\pazocal{H}^{n+2\sigma}_{\infty,p_{s}}(E),

\displaystyle\text{if $\alpha<2s-2\sigma$,}\quad\gamma_{\Theta^{s},\alpha}^{\sigma}(E)

\displaystyle\approx_{n,s,\sigma,\alpha}\pazocal{H}^{n+2\sigma+\alpha}_{\infty,p_{s}}(E).

The nullity of these capacities is equivalent to the removability of the corresponding compact set for solutions satisfying
(\sigma,\sigma/s)
-Laplacian estimates in either
s
-parabolic
BMO
or
\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha}
, assuming
\alpha<2s-2\sigma
in the latter case.

The previous study has been motivated by the one carried out for the BMO variant of analytic capacity by Kaufman
[
K
]
and Verdera
[
Ve
]
(for a brief overview the reader may consult

AsIM

); and that for the

[
Me
]

O
]
. We remark that the results presented here also generalize those of

He

2.
Basic notation and kernel estimates

We begin by noticing that the
s
-
parabolic distance
between
\overline{x}:=(x,t),\,\overline{y}:=(y,\tau)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, defined in the introduction as

|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}=\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{x},\overline{y}):=\max\big\{|x-y|,|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2s}}\big\},\qquad\text{for }\,0<s\leq 1,

is, in fact, equivalent to

\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})\approx\big(|x-y|^{2}+|t-\tau|^{1/s}\big)^{1/2}.

The
s
-parabolic dilation
of factor
\lambda>0
, written
\delta_{\lambda}
, is given by

\delta_{\lambda}(x,t)=\big(\lambda x,\lambda^{2s}t\big).

To ease notation, since we will always work with
s
-parabolic distances, we will write
\lambda Q
to denote
\delta_{\lambda}(Q)
, the
s
-parabolic cube concentric with
Q
of side length
\lambda\ell(Q)
.

As the reader may suspect, the notion of
s
-parabolic BMO space
,
\text{BMO}_{p_{s}}
, refers to the space of usual BMO functions (strictly, equivalence classes of functions where constants are identified as 0) obtained by replacing Euclidean cubes by
s
-parabolic ones.
Similarly, a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
s
-parabolic

\textit{\text{{Lip}}}_{\alpha}
for some
0<\alpha<1
, shortly
\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha,p_{s}}
, if

\|f\|_{\text{Lip}_{\alpha},p_{s}}:=\sup_{\overline{x},\overline{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\frac{|f(\overline{x})-f(\overline{y})|}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}\lesssim 1.

For each
s\in(0,1]
, the fundamental solution
P_{s}(x,t)
to the
\Theta^{s}
-equation, i.e. that associated with the operator

\Theta^{s}:=(-\Delta)^{s}+\partial_{t},

is the inverse spatial Fourier transform of
e^{-4\pi^{2}t|\xi|^{2s}}
for
t>0
, and it equals
0
if
t\leq 0
. For the special case
s=1
, we retrieve the classical
heat kernel
, given by:

W(\overline{x}):=P_{1}(\overline{x})=c_{n}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\phi_{n,1}(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}),\qquad\text{if }\,t>0,

where
\phi_{n,1}(\rho):=e^{-\rho^{2}/4}
, independent of
n
. Although the expression of
P_{s}
is not explicit in general, Blumenthal and Getoor
[
BG
, Theorem 2.1]
established that for
s<1
,

(2.1)

P_{s}(\overline{x})=c_{n,s}\,t^{-\frac{n}{2s}}\phi_{n,s}\big(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)\chi_{t>0},

Here,
\phi_{n,s}
is a smooth function, radially decreasing and satisfying, for
0<s<1
,

(2.2)

\phi_{n,s}(\rho)\approx\big(1+\rho^{2}\big)^{-(n+2s)/2},

being an exact equality if
s=1/2

[
Va
]
. Therefore,

P_{s}(\overline{x})\approx\frac{t}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s}}\chi_{t>0}.

The function
\phi_{n,s}
is tightly related to the Fourier transform of
e^{-4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2s}}
. Indeed, taking the spatial Fourier transform in both sides of identity (
2.1
), we get

e^{-4\pi^{2}t|\xi|^{2s}}=c_{n,s}\,t^{-\frac{n}{2s}}\big[\phi_{n,s}\big(|\,\cdot\,|\,t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)\big]^{\wedge}(\xi).

Recall that for
\lambda>0
, the dilation
f_{\lambda}:=f(\lambda x)
satisfies
\widehat{f_{\lambda}}(\xi)=\lambda^{-n}\widehat{f}(\lambda^{-1}\xi)
. Then,

e^{-4\pi^{2}t|\xi|^{2s}}=c_{n,s}\,\widehat{\phi_{n,s}\big(|\,\cdot\,|\big)}\big(\xi t^{\frac{1}{2s}}\big),\hskip 14.22636pt\text{that implies}\hskip 14.22636pte^{-4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2s}}\simeq\widehat{\phi_{n,s}\big(|\,\cdot\,|\big)}(\xi).

The above relations will allow us to obtain explicit bounds for the derivatives of
\phi_{n,s}
.
Let us present our first lemma. Although it can be deduced straightforwardly from
[
GrT
, Theorem 1.1]
, we shall give a detailed proof for the sake of clarity and completeness.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
and
\beta\in(0,1)
. We define the following function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}(x):=(-\Delta)^{\beta}\phi_{n,s}\big(|x|\big).

Then,

1.

\phi^{\prime}_{n,s}(\rho)\simeq-\rho\,\phi_{n+2,s}(\rho)
.

2.

|\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}(x)|\lesssim_{\beta}\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{-(n+2\beta)/2}
.

3.

\nabla\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}(x)\simeq-x\,\psi^{(\beta)}_{n+2,s}(x)
.

Proof.

We begin by proving
1
for
s<1
(the case
s=1
is trivial). To do so, we will use the explicit integral representation for the inverse Fourier transform of a radial function in
[
Gr
, §B.5]
or
[
StW
, §IV.I]
. Applying it to the Fourier transform
e^{-4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2s}}
we get

\phi_{n,s}(|z|)=2\pi|z|^{1-n/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r|z|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,\quad\text{for any $z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{\{0\}}$},

where
J_{k}
is the classical Bessel function of order
k

[
AS
, §9]
. Since we are interested in the derivatives of
\phi_{n,s}
as a radial real variable function, let us rewrite the previous expression in terms of
\rho\in(0,\infty)
so that it reads as

(2.3)

\phi_{n,s}(\rho)=2\pi\rho^{1-n/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r\rho)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r.

Therefore, to estimate the derivatives of
\phi_{n,s}
we need to determine first if we can differentiate under the integral sign. To that end, we use the following recurrence relation for classical Bessel functions
[
AS
, §9.1.27]
,

\displaystyle J_{k}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{k}{x}J_{k}(x)-J_{k+1}(x).

This recurrence formula together with (
2.3
) remain valid for the case
k=-1/2
, conveying that
J_{-1/2}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\cos{x}
. In our case these imply

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r\rho)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{n}{2}-1\bigg)\rho^{-1}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r\rho)-2\pi r\,J_{n/2}(2\pi r\rho).

If we differentiated under the integral sign in (
2.3
), we would get integrands of the form

\displaystyle e^{-r^{2s}}r^{n/2}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r\rho),\hskip 21.33955pte^{-r^{2s}}r^{n/2+1}J_{n/2}(2\pi r\rho).

Notice that both are bounded by integrable functions in the domain of integration, locally for each
\rho>0
(by the boundedness of the functions
J_{k}
for
n>1
, and by that of
\cos{x}
if
n=1
). Hence, we can indeed differentiate under the integral sign to compute
\phi^{\prime}_{n,s}
, obtaining the desired result:

\displaystyle\phi_{n,s}^{\prime}(\rho)

\displaystyle=2\pi\bigg[\bigg(1-\frac{n}{2}\bigg)\rho^{-n/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r\rho)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\rho^{1-n/2}\partial_{\rho}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r\rho)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle=2\pi\bigg[\bigg(1-\frac{n}{2}\bigg)\rho^{-n/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2}J_{n/2-1}(r\rho)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\hskip 36.98866pt\rho^{1-n/2}\bigg(\frac{n}{2}-1\bigg)\rho^{-1}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r\rho)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\bigg)

\displaystyle\hskip 32.72049pt-2\pi\rho\,\rho^{1-(n+2)/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{(n+2)/2}J_{(n+2)/2-1}(r\rho)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\bigg]=-2\pi\rho\,\phi_{n+2,s}(\rho).

Next we prove statement
2
. Observe that for
s\in(0,1]
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, we have
\widehat{\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}}(\xi)=|\xi|^{2\beta}e^{-4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2s}}
,
which is an integrable function, and thus
\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}
is bounded (in fact, since the product of
\widehat{\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}}
by any polynomial is also integrable, we infer that
\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}
is smooth). By the integral representation formula for inverse Fourier transforms of radial functions,

(2.4)

\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}(x)=2\pi|x|^{1-n/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2+2\beta}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r|x|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Now, we apply
[
PruTa
, Lemma 1]
to deduce the desired decaying property
|\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}(x)|=O\big(|x|^{-n-2\beta}\big)
, for
|x|
large. Hence, since
\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}
is bounded, we deduce the desired bound
|\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}(x)|\lesssim_{\beta}\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{-(n+2\beta)/2}
.

We are left to control the norm of
\nabla\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}
, provided the latter is well-defined. We claim that this is the case, since we can differentiate under the integral sign in (
2.4
). Indeed, by the recurrence relation satisfied by the derivatives of
J_{k}
we get

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}J_{n/2-1}(r|x|)|=\bigg\rvert\bigg(\frac{n}{2}-1\bigg)\frac{1}{|x|}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r|x|)-2\pi rJ_{n/2}(2\pi r|x|)\bigg\rvert.

So the resulting integrands to study are terms of the form

\displaystyle e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2+2\beta}|J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r|x|)|,\hskip 21.33955pte^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2+2\beta+1}|J_{n/2}(2\pi r|x|)|,

both bounded by the integrable functions
C_{1}e^{-r^{2s}}r^{n/2+2\beta}
and
C_{2}e^{-r^{2s}}r^{n/2+2\beta+1}
for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}
depending on
n,s
and
\beta
, and locally for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|x|>0
. Hence, we can differentiate under the integral sign in (
2.4
) and obtain

\displaystyle\nabla\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}(x)

\displaystyle=2\pi\bigg[\bigg(1-\frac{n}{2}\bigg)\frac{x}{|x|^{n/2+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2+2\beta}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r|x|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\bigg(\frac{n}{2}-1\bigg)\frac{x}{|x|^{n/2+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2+2\beta}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r|x|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-2\pi\frac{x}{|x|^{n/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{(n+2)/2+2\beta}J_{(n+2)/2-1}(2\pi r|x|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\bigg]

\displaystyle=-2\pi x\,\psi_{n+2,s}^{(\beta)}(x).

∎

Using the above lemma together with (
2.2
) we can estimate the derivatives of
\phi_{n,s}
and
\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}
. In particular, the following relations hold:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\text{If }s<1\text{,}\quad\phi_{n,s}^{\prime}(\rho)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{-\rho}{(1+\rho^{2})^{(n+2s+2)/2}},\qquad\phi_{n,s}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)\approx\frac{-1+(2\pi-1)\rho^{2}}{(1+\rho^{2})^{(n+2s+4)/2}},

(2.6)

\displaystyle|\nabla\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{|x|}{(1+|x|^{2})^{(n+2\beta+2)/2}}.

2.1.
Estimates for
\nabla_{x}P_{s}
and
\Delta^{\beta}P_{s}

We shall now present some growth estimates for the kernels
P_{s}
. Our first result provides bounds for
\nabla_{x}P_{s}
,
s\in(0,1)
. These estimates are analogous to those of
[
MaPrTo
, Lemma 5.4]
which cover the case
s=1
. In the forthcoming results, the parameter
2\zeta:=\min\{1,2s\}
will play an important role.

Theorem 2.2
.

The following estimates hold for any
\overline{x}\neq 0
and
s\in(0,1)
:

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|xt|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}},\qquad|\Delta P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim\frac{|t|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}},\qquad|\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim\frac{|x|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}}.

The last bound is only valid for points with
t\neq 0
. Also, if
\overline{x}^{\prime}
is such that
|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}/2
,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1+2\zeta}}.

Proof.

To simplify the arguments below, we specify the dependence of
P_{s}
with respect to
n
. Let us write
P_{s,n+1}
to refer to the fundamental solution to the
\Theta^{s}
-equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and use the following abuse of notation: given
\overline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, write

\displaystyle P_{s,n+3}(\overline{x})

\displaystyle:=P_{s,n+3}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},0,0,t),

\displaystyle P_{s,n+5}(\overline{x})

\displaystyle:=P_{s,n+5}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},0,0,0,0,t).

This way, we directly apply relations (
2.1
) and (
2.5
) to obtain for each
t>0
,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x})|

\displaystyle\simeq t^{-\frac{n+1}{2s}}|\phi_{n,s}^{\prime}(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}})|\simeq|xP_{s,n+3}(\overline{x})|\approx\frac{|xt|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}}.

The bounds for
\Delta P_{s,n+1}
and
\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}P_{s,n+1}
can be obtained from the previous result and (
2.1
). Indeed,

\displaystyle|\Delta P_{s,n+1}(\overline{x})|

\displaystyle\simeq P_{s,n+3}(\overline{x})+|x|^{2}P_{s,n+5}(\overline{x})\lesssim\frac{|t|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}},

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}P_{s,n+1}(\overline{x})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|x|}{t}\Big(P_{s,n+3}(\overline{x})+|x|^{2}P_{s,n+5}(\overline{x})\Big)\lesssim\frac{|x|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}}.

For the final estimate, we recover the notation
P_{s}:=P_{s,n+1}
. Let
\overline{x}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}/2
and use the definition of
\text{dist}_{p_{s}}
to obtain

(2.7)

|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}\leq 2|\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}\quad\text{and}\quad|x^{\prime}|\geq|x|-\frac{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}}{2}.

Put
\widehat{x}=(x^{\prime},t)
and write

|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}^{\prime})|\leq|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\widehat{x})|+|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\widehat{x})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}^{\prime})|.

We observe that the first term in the above inequality satisfies the desired bound,

|x-x^{\prime}|\sup_{\xi\in[x,x^{\prime}]}|\Delta P_{s}(\xi,t)|\lesssim\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2}}\leq\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1+2\zeta}}\bigg(\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}}\bigg)^{1-2\zeta}\leq\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1+2\zeta}}.

Regarding the second term, assume without loss of generality
t>t^{\prime}
. If
t^{\prime}>0
, use
|\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}\geq|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}/2
so that we also have

\displaystyle|t-t^{\prime}|\sup_{\tau\in[t,t^{\prime}]}|\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x^{\prime},\tau)|\lesssim\frac{|t-t^{\prime}|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+1}}\leq\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1+2\zeta}}\Bigg(\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}}\Bigg)^{2s-2\zeta}\lesssim\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1+2\zeta}},

If
t<0
then
|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\widehat{x})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}^{\prime})|=0
, and the estimate becomes trivial. Then, we are left to study the case
t>0
and
t^{\prime}<0
. These two conditions imply that the
p_{s}
-ball

B(\overline{x}):=\bigg\{\overline{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\,:\,|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}\leq\frac{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}}{2}\bigg\}\ni\overline{x}^{\prime}

intersects the hyperplane
\{t=0\}
. Since the radius of
B(\overline{x})
also depends on
\overline{x}
, the previous property imposes
the following condition over
\overline{x}
,

t^{1/s}\leq\frac{x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2}}{3},\quad\text{that is}\quad t^{\frac{1}{2s}}\leq\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{3}},

which is attained if the point
(x,0)
belongs to
\partial B(\overline{x})
. Therefore
|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}:=\max\big\{|x|,t^{\frac{1}{2s}}\big\}=|x|
, so by (
2.7
) we get
|x^{\prime}|\geq|x|/2
, and this in turn implies

(2.8)

\frac{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}}{2}\leq|\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}+|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}\leq\frac{3|x|}{2}\leq 3|x^{\prime}|.

Using this last inequality we can finally conclude:

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\widehat{x})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x^{\prime},t)-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x^{\prime},0)|\lesssim|t|\sup_{\tau\in(0,t]}|\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x^{\prime},\tau)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|t|}{|x^{\prime}|^{n+2s+1}}\lesssim\frac{|t|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+1}}\leq\frac{|t-t^{\prime}|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+1}}\lesssim\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1+2\zeta}}.

∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
and
\beta,\gamma\in[0,1)
. Then, for any
\overline{x}\neq 0
we have,

\displaystyle\textit{1}\hskip 0.85355pt.

\displaystyle\;\;|(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\beta}},

\displaystyle\textit{2}\hskip 0.85355pt.

\displaystyle\;\;|(-\Delta)^{\gamma}(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta,\gamma}\;\frac{1}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\beta+2\gamma}},\hskip 14.22636pt\textit{3}\hskip 0.85355pt.\;\;|\nabla_{x}(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{|x|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\beta+2}}.

Moreover, for any
\overline{x}\neq(x,0)
,

\displaystyle\textit{4}\hskip 0.85355pt.\;\;|\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\beta+2s}}.

Finally, if
\overline{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is such that
|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}/2
,

\displaystyle\textit{5}\hskip 0.85355pt.\;\;|(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})-(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x}^{\prime})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\beta+2\zeta}}.

Proof.

We shall also assume
\beta>0
, since the case
\beta=0
is already covered in
[
MaPr
, Lemma 2.2]
. For the sake of notation, in this proof we will write
\phi:=\phi_{n,s}
and
\psi:=\psi_{n,s}^{(\beta)}
, and we also set
K_{\beta}:=(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}
. Let us begin by applying the integral representation of
K_{\beta}
together with relation (
2.1
) to obtain for
t>0
,

\displaystyle K_{\beta}(x,t):=(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}(x,t)

\displaystyle\simeq_{\beta}\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{P_{s}(x,t)-P_{s}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+2\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{n}{2s}}\,\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\phi\big(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)-\phi\big(|y|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)}{|x-y|^{n+2\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{n+2\beta}{2s}}\,\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\phi\big(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)-\phi(|z|)}{|xt^{-\frac{1}{2s}}-z|^{n+2\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{n+2\beta}{2s}}(-\Delta)^{\beta}\phi\big(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)=t^{-\frac{n+2\beta}{2s}}\psi\big(xt^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)

Using the estimate proved in Lemma
2.1
for
\psi
we deduce the desired bound:

\big\rvert K_{\beta}(x,t)\big\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{t^{-\frac{n+2\beta}{2s}}}{\big(1+|x|^{2}t^{-1/s}\big)^{(n+2\beta)/2}}=\frac{1}{\big(t^{1/s}+|x|^{2}\big)^{(n+2\beta)/2}}\approx\frac{1}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\beta}}.

We shall continue by studying estimate
2
in a similar way. Indeed,

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\gamma}K_{\beta}(x,t)

\displaystyle\simeq_{\gamma}\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{K_{\beta}(x,t)-K_{\beta}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\simeq_{\beta}t^{-\frac{n+2\beta}{2s}}\,\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\psi\big(xt^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)-\psi\big(yt^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{n+2\beta+2\gamma}{2s}}\,\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\psi\big(xt^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)-\psi(z)}{|xt^{-\frac{1}{2s}}-z|^{n+2\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z=t^{-\frac{n+2\beta+2\gamma}{2s}}(-\Delta)^{\gamma}\psi\big(xt^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big).

Set
\Psi:=(-\Delta)^{\gamma}\psi(\,\cdot\,)
and notice that

\widehat{\Psi}(\xi)=|\xi|^{2\gamma}|\xi|^{2\beta}e^{-4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2s}}=|\xi|^{2\beta+2\gamma}e^{-4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2s}}.

Thus, since
\widehat{\Psi}
is integrable,
\Psi
is the radial bounded function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

\Psi(z)=2\pi|z|^{1-n/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2+2\beta+2\gamma}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r|z|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,

By
[
PruTa
, Lemma 1]

\Psi
decays as

|\Psi(z)|=O\big(|z|^{-n-2\beta-2\gamma}\big),\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for }\;|z|\;\text{ large}.

Therefore

|\Psi(z)|\lesssim_{\beta,\gamma}\big(1+|z|^{2}\big)^{-(n+2\beta+2\gamma)/2}.

So analogously to the proof of
1
, we deduce the desired result:

\big\rvert(-\Delta)^{\gamma}K_{\beta}(x,t)\big\rvert\lesssim_{\beta,\gamma}\frac{t^{-\frac{n+2\beta+2\gamma}{2s}}}{\big(1+|x|^{2}t^{-1/s}\big)^{(n+2\beta+2\gamma)/2}}\approx\frac{1}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\beta+2\gamma}}.

Regarding estimate
3
, notice that

|\nabla_{x}K_{\beta}(x,t)|\simeq_{\beta}\Big\rvert\nabla_{x}\Big(t^{-\frac{n+2\beta}{2s}}\psi\big(xt^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)\Big)\Big\rvert=t^{-\frac{n+2\beta+1}{2s}}\big\rvert\nabla\psi\big(xt^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)\big\rvert.

Therefore, applying the bound obtained for
\nabla\psi
in (
2.6
) we deduce

\displaystyle\big\rvert\nabla_{x}K_{\beta}(x,t)\big\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}t^{-\frac{n+2\beta+1}{2s}}\frac{|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}}{\big(1+|x|^{2}t^{-1/s}\big)^{(n+2\beta+1)/2}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{|x|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\beta+2}}.

We move on to estimate
4
, that is, the one concerning
\partial_{t}K_{\beta}(\overline{x})
at points of the form
\overline{x}\neq(x,0)
. Observe that the previous derivative is well defined if
t>0
, since the expression of
K_{\beta}
can be written as

\displaystyle K_{\beta}(x,t\big)

\displaystyle\simeq t^{-\frac{n+2\beta}{2s}}(-\Delta)^{\beta}\phi\big(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)

\displaystyle\simeq_{\beta}|x|^{1-n/2}\bigg(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{n+4\beta+2}{4s}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4\pi^{2}r^{2s}}r^{n/2+2\beta}J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\bigg),

so differentiating under the integral sign, it is clear that temporal derivatives of any order exist in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{\{t=0\}}
. We claim now that the operators
\partial_{t}
and
(-\Delta)^{\beta}
commute when applied to
P_{s}
. To prove this, let us first observe that for each
t_{0}>0
fixed we have

\displaystyle\big[(-\Delta)^{\beta}\big(\partial_{t}P_{s}\big)\big]^{\wedge}(\xi,t_{0})=|\xi|^{2\beta}\widehat{\partial_{t}P_{s}}(\xi,t_{0})=|\xi|^{2\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\partial_{t}P_{s}(x,t_{0})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

If we can bound
\partial_{t}P_{s}
by an integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in a neighborhood of
t_{0}
, we will be able to locally differentiate outside the integral sign for each
t_{0}
. If
0<s<1
, this is a consequence of
[
Va
, Equation 2.6]
and (
2.2
). Indeed,

|\partial_{t}P_{s}(x,t_{0})|\lesssim\frac{1}{t_{0}}|P_{s}(x,t_{0})|\lesssim\frac{1}{t_{0}^{\frac{n+2s}{2s}}}\Bigg[\frac{1}{\big(1+|x|^{2}t_{0}^{-1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\Bigg].

On the other hand, if
s=1
by definition we have

|\partial_{t}W(x,t_{0})|\lesssim\Bigg(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{t_{0}}\Bigg)\frac{1}{t_{0}^{n/2+1}}e^{-|x|^{2}/(4t_{0})}.

In both cases we obtain a bounded function of
x
that decreases like
|x|^{-n-2}
at infinity (for the case
s=1
, see
[
MaPrTo
, Lemma 2.1]
) and thus it is integrable on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore, differentiating outside the integral sign we have

\big[(-\Delta)^{\beta}\big(\partial_{t}P_{s}\big)\big]^{\wedge}(\xi,t_{0})=\partial_{t}\big[(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}\big]^{\wedge}(\xi,t_{0}),\qquad\forall t_{0}>0.

So we are left to check whether we can enter
\partial_{t}
inside the previous Fourier transform, that is, whether the following holds

\partial_{t}\big[(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}\big]^{\wedge}(\xi,t_{0})=\big[\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}\big]^{\wedge}(\xi,t_{0}).

Again, the latter is just a matter of being able to bound
|\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}|=|\partial_{t}K_{\beta}|
locally for each
t_{0}>0
by an integrable function, so that we can differentiate under the integral defining the Fourier transform. We know that

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}K_{\beta}(x,t_{0})|

\displaystyle=\Big\rvert\partial_{t}\Big[t^{-\frac{n+2\beta}{2s}}\psi\big(xt^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)\Big]_{t=t_{0}}\Big\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\lesssim_{\beta}C_{1}(t_{0})\big\rvert\psi\big(xt_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)\big\rvert+C_{2}(t_{0})|x|\big\rvert\nabla\psi\big(xt_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)\big\rvert.

For the first summand, using that
|\psi|
is bounded and decays as
|x|^{-n-2\beta}
, we deduce the desired integrability condition. For the second summand we can argue exactly in the same manner, using that
|\nabla\psi|
is bounded and decays as
|x|^{-n-2\beta-1}
. Hence, we conclude that
\partial_{t}
and
(-\Delta)^{\beta}
commute.

The previous commutativity relation and
[
MaPr
, Eq.
2.5]
yield the following for
t>0
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}K_{\beta}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\big[(-\Delta)^{\beta}P_{s}\big](x,t)=(-\Delta)^{\beta}\big(\partial_{t}P_{s}\big)(x,t)

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)^{\beta}\Big[-(-\Delta)^{s}P_{s}\Big](x,t)=-(-\Delta)^{s}K_{\beta}(x,t),

where we have commuted the operators
(-\Delta)^{s}
and
(-\Delta)^{\beta}
, that can be easily checked via their Fourier transform. Then, applying
2
with
\gamma=s
we are done.

Finally, regarding estimate
5
, we can follow the same proof to that presented for the last estimate in Theorem
2.2
, using estimates
3
and
4
from above.
∎

2.2.
Estimates for
\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}

In this subsection we obtain similar estimates now for the kernel
\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}
, with
\beta\in(0,1)
. Recall that the
\beta
-temporal derivative
of
f:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined, provided it exists, as

\partial_{t}^{\beta}f(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(x,\tau)-f(x,t)}{|\tau-t|^{1+s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

The study below considers the cases
s<1
and
s=1
separately. In the following theorem, which generalizes
[
MaPr
, Lemma 2.2]
, we get dimensional restrictions that in the end will not matter for our purposes.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\beta,s\in(0,1)
. Then, the following hold for any
\overline{x}=(x,t)\neq(0,t):

\displaystyle\textit{1}\hskip 0.85355pt.

\displaystyle\;\;\text{If}\hskip 7.11317ptn>1,\hskip 14.22636pt|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2s}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}},

\displaystyle\textit{2}\hskip 0.85355pt.

\displaystyle\;\;\text{If}\hskip 7.11317ptn=1\text{ and }\,\beta>1-\frac{1}{2s},\hskip 14.22636pt|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\frac{1}{|x|^{1-2s+\alpha}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)-\alpha}},\;\;\forall\alpha\in(2s-1,4s).

Moreover, for every
n
,

\textit{3}\hskip 0.85355pt.\;\;|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2s+1}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}},\hskip 14.22636pt\textit{4}\hskip 0.85355pt.\;\;|\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|x|^{n}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}},\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for}\hskip 9.95863ptt\neq 0.

Finally, if
\overline{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is such that
|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}\leq|x|/2
,

\displaystyle\textit{5}\hskip 0.85355pt.\;\;|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})-\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x}^{\prime})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|x|^{n+2\zeta-2s}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}}.

Proof.

To prove
1
, we use
[
MaPr
, Equation 2.9]
and deduce the existence of a function
F_{s}
such that for
t>0
,

(2.9)

P_{s}(x,t)=\frac{1}{|x|^{n}}F_{s}\bigg(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg),

and such that

(2.10)

F_{s}(u)\approx\frac{u}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}.

We extend continuously
F_{s}(u):=0
for
u\leq 0
, so that (
2.9
) is verified for any value of
t
. The existence of
F_{s}
is clear, since for
t>0
the function
P_{s}
can be written as

P_{s}(x,t)=\frac{1}{|x|^{n}}\bigg(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg)^{-\frac{n}{2s}}\phi_{n,s}\Bigg[\bigg(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\Bigg],

and defining for
u>0
,
F_{s}(u):=u^{-\frac{n}{2s}}\phi_{n,s}\big(u^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)
, we are done. Notice that
F_{s}
is a bounded continuous function, null for negative values of
u
, smooth in the domain
u>0
and vanishing at
\infty
. Moreover, using the bounds obtained for
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
we obtain the following estimates for
u>0
,

(2.11)

|F_{s}^{\prime}(u)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}},\qquad|F_{s}^{\prime\prime}(u)|\lesssim\frac{1}{u\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}

Let us argue that, in fact,
|F_{s}^{\prime\prime}(u)|
is also a bounded function. Notice that, by definition,

\partial_{\tau}^{2}P_{s}(x,\tau)=\frac{1}{|x|^{n+4s}}F_{s}^{\prime\prime}\bigg(\frac{\tau}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg)\hskip 14.22636pt\Leftrightarrow\hskip 14.22636pt\bigg\rvert F_{s}^{\prime\prime}\bigg(\frac{\tau}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg)\bigg\rvert=|x|^{n+4s}\big\rvert\partial_{\tau}^{2}P_{s}(x,\tau)\big\rvert,

and using that
P_{s}
is the fundamental solution to the
\Theta^{s}
-equation and that
\tau>0
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}^{2}P_{s}(x,\tau)=\partial_{\tau}\big[-(-\Delta)^{s}P_{s}(x,\tau)\big].

By the commutativity of
\partial_{\tau}
and
(-\Delta)^{s}
, we deduce

\big\rvert\partial_{\tau}^{2}P_{s}(x,\tau)\big\rvert=\big\rvert(-\Delta)^{s}\big[\partial_{\tau}P_{s}(x,\tau)\big]\big\rvert=\big\rvert(-\Delta)^{s}\big[-(-\Delta)^{s}P_{s}(x,\tau)\big]\big\rvert\lesssim\frac{1}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+4s}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\bigg\rvert F_{s}^{\prime\prime}\bigg(\frac{\tau}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg)\bigg\rvert\lesssim\frac{|x|^{n+4s}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+4s}}=\frac{1}{\max\Big\{1,\big(\tau/|x|^{2s}\big)^{1/{(2s)}}\Big\}^{n+4s}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\Big[1+\big(\tau/|x|^{2s}\big)^{1/{s}}\Big]^{(n+4s)/2}},

that implies the following (improved) bound for
F^{\prime\prime}_{s}
,

(2.12)

|F^{\prime\prime}_{s}(u)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+4s)/2}}\leq 1,\qquad u>0.

We continue by observing that by a change of variables the following holds,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{n}}\bigg[\partial_{t}^{\beta}F_{s}\bigg(\frac{\cdot}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg)\bigg](t)=\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s\beta}}\partial^{\beta}F_{s}\bigg(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg).

We shall prove the following inequality,

(2.14)

\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(u)\big\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\bigg\},

where for
u=0
is just asking for
\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(0)\big\rvert
to be bounded. To verify (
2.14
) we distinguish whether if
u=0
,
u<0
or
u>0
. For
u=0
observe that by definition and relation (
2.10
),

\displaystyle\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(0)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|F_{s}(0)-F_{s}(w)|}{|0-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}(w)|}{w^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{w^{1+\beta}}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\beta}\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\beta}\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\beta}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\frac{n}{2s}+1+\beta}}\lesssim(1-\beta)^{-1}+\bigg(\frac{n}{2s}+\beta\bigg)^{-1}\lesssim_{\beta}1,

so case
u=0
is done. Let us assume
u<0
, so that

\displaystyle\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(u)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|F_{s}(w)|}{||u|+w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(|u|+w)^{1+\beta}}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w.

On the one hand notice that the since
|u|+w>w
, the previous expression is bounded by a constant depending on
n,s
and
\beta
(by the same arguments given for the case
u=0
). On the other hand, observe that

\displaystyle\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(u)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(w/|u|+1)^{1+\beta}}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(w/|u|+1)^{1+\beta}}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(w/|u|+1)^{1+\beta}}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\Bigg]=:\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}(I_{1}+I_{2}).

Regarding
I_{1}
, since the denominators are bigger than
1
, we directly have

(2.15)

I_{1}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}w\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\leq 1

Turning to
I_{2}
, we similarly obtain

(2.16)

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\frac{n}{2s}}}=w^{-\frac{n}{2s}+1}\bigg\rvert_{1}^{\infty}=1,

where notice that
-\frac{n}{2s}+1<0
because
n>1
and
s<1
. Therefore, we also have
\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(u)\big\rvert\lesssim|u|^{-1-\beta}
and we conclude that for
u\leq 0
,

\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(u)\big\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\bigg\}.

Let us finally assume
u>0
. Begin by writing

\displaystyle\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(u)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|w|\leq u/2}\frac{|F_{s}(w)-F_{s}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{u/2\leq|w|\leq 2u}\frac{|F_{s}(w)-F_{s}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{|w|>2u}\frac{|F_{s}(w)-F_{s}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

We study each of the previous integrals separately. Concerning the first, notice that in its domain of integration
u/2\leq|w-u|\leq 3u/2
, i.e.
|w-u|\approx u
. We split it as follows

I_{1}=\int_{-u/2}^{0}\frac{|F_{s}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{0}^{u/2}\frac{|F_{s}(w)-F_{s}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w=:I_{11}+I_{12}.

Observe that
I_{11}
can be estimated by

I_{11}\lesssim\frac{u}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\int_{-u/2}^{0}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\simeq_{\beta}\frac{u^{1-\beta}}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}.

The expression of the right, viewed as a continuous function of
u
, tends to zero as
u\to 0
and decays as
|u|^{-\beta-\frac{n}{2s}}
as
u\to\infty
. Hence, it is bounded by a constant (depending on
n,s
and
\beta
) and so
I_{11}\lesssim_{\beta}1
. On the other hand, to prove that
I_{11}\lesssim_{\beta}|u|^{-1-\beta}
it suffices to check that the following expression is bounded by a constant,

\frac{u^{2}}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\approx uF_{s}(u).

Again, it is clear it that tends to zero as
u\to 0
, but observe that it behaves as
|u|^{-\frac{n}{2s}+1}
as
u\to\infty
, which vanishes only if
n>2s
, that is, only if
n>1
, since
s<1
. But this is satisfied by hypothesis. Therefore we deduce
I_{11}\lesssim_{\beta}\min\{1,|u|^{-1-\beta}\}
.
Regarding
I_{12}
proceed in a similar manner to obtain

\displaystyle I_{12}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}}\int_{0}^{u/2}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\frac{u^{1-\beta}}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}.

The second summand has already been studied in
I_{11}
. Regarding the first, notice that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u/2}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}w\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\frac{n}{2s}}}\lesssim 1,

where we have applied the same arguments as in (
2.15
) and (
2.16
). On the other hand, by applying the following inequality for
w>0
,

(1+w^{1/s})^{(n+2s)/2}>w^{1-\beta},

that can be checked by a direct computation, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u/2}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle<\int_{0}^{u/2}w^{\beta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\lesssim_{\beta}u^{1+\beta}.

Therefore we conclude

I_{12}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}}\min\Big\{1,u^{1+\beta}\Big\}+\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}}\bigg\}=2\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}}\bigg\},

that implies the desired estimate for
I_{1}
.

Moving on to
I_{2}
, we split it as follows

I_{2}=\int_{-2u}^{-u/2}\frac{|F_{s}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{u/2}^{2u}\frac{|F_{s}(w)-F_{s}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w=:I_{21}+I_{22}.

The study of
I_{21}
is exactly the same as the one presented for
I_{11}
, so we focus on
I_{22}
. Apply the mean value theorem to obtain

I_{22}\leq\sup_{\nu\in[u/2,2u]}|F_{s}^{\prime}(\nu)|\int_{u/2}^{2u}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{|w-u|^{\beta}}\lesssim_{\beta}\sup_{\nu\in[u/2,2u]}|F_{s}^{\prime}(\nu)|\,u^{1-\beta}.

Therefore, if we are able to bound
|F^{\prime}_{s}|
by
u^{\beta-1}
and
u^{-2}
we will be done. But recalling relation (
2.11
), this is equivalent to proving that the following functions are bounded by a constant:

(2.17)

\frac{u^{\beta-1}}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}},\hskip 21.33955pt\frac{u^{2}}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}},

that has already been done in
I_{11}
. Therefore, we are only left to study
I_{3}
,

\displaystyle I_{3}=\int_{-\infty}^{-2u}\frac{|F_{s}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}(w)-F_{s}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w=:I_{31}+I_{32}.

To deal with
I_{31}
we first notice that in the domain of integration
|w-u|\approx|w|
, implying

\displaystyle I_{31}\approx\frac{u}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{-2u}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{|w|^{1+\beta}}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{u^{1-\beta}}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\lesssim_{\beta}\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}}\bigg\}

We study
I_{32}
by splitting it as

I_{32}\leq\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}(w)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w.

The second summand is tackled in exactly the same way as
I_{31}
, so we focus on the first one. Using that
|w-u|\approx|w|\gtrsim u
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}(w)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}}\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{1}w\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\frac{n}{2s}}}\Bigg]\lesssim\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}},

by the same arguments used in (
2.15
) and (
2.16
). On the other hand, we also have

\displaystyle\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}(w)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{1}{w^{1+\beta}}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\beta}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\frac{n}{2s}}}.

We already know that the second integral is bounded by a constant for
n>1
, while the first one is also bounded, since
0<\beta<1
. So we conclude that
I_{32}\lesssim_{\beta}\min\{1,|u|^{-1-\beta}\}
and we obtain the desired bound for
I_{3}
and thus for
|\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(u)|
if
u>0
.

All in all, returning to (
2.13
), we finally have

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(x,t)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s\beta}}\bigg\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}\bigg(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg)\bigg\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s\beta}}\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|^{2s(1+\beta)}}{|t|^{1+\beta}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2s}}\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{|x|^{2s(1+\beta)}},\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{2s(1+\beta)}{2s}}}\bigg\}=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2s}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}},

that is estimate
1
in the statement of the lemma.

In order to prove
2
, we follow the same scheme. Indeed, the desired estimate follows once we prove

\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(u)\big\rvert\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}\bigg\},\qquad\text{for }\,2s-1<\alpha<4s.

If one followed the same arguments used to prove
1
, in the regime
u<0
one already encounters a first bound for which dimension
n=1
is troublesome, namely when trying to obtain
|\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(u)|\lesssim|u|^{-1-\beta+\frac{\alpha}{2s}}
. However, in our current setting we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(w+|u|)^{1+\beta}}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\quad\lesssim\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(w/|u|+1)^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}\frac{1}{(w+|u|)^{\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\quad\lesssim\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}w^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\frac{n+\alpha}{2s}}}\bigg)\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}},\quad\text{since $2s-1<\alpha<4s$},

so the desired bound for
|\partial^{\beta}F_{s}(u)|
follows. For the case
u>0
we also proceed analogously. Let us comment those steps where the hypotheses on
\alpha
and
\beta
come into play. In
I_{1}
, using the same notation as for the case
n>1
, we obtain the estimates

I_{11}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{u^{1-\beta}}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad I_{12}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}}\int_{0}^{u/2}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w,

expression that we already know to be bounded by a constant. To prove that
I_{11}\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}|u|^{-1-\beta+\frac{\alpha}{2s}}
observe that the function

\frac{u^{2-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\approx u^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}F_{s}(u)

tends to zero as
u\to 0
, since
\alpha<4s
. Moreover, it behaves as
|u|^{-\frac{n+\alpha}{2s}+1}
as
u\to\infty
, which also tends to
0
because
\alpha<2s-1
. Thus,
I_{11}\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\min\{1,|u|^{-1-\beta+\frac{\alpha}{2s}}\}
. On the other hand, since the following holds

\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}>w^{2-\frac{\alpha}{2s}},

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}}\int_{0}^{u/2}\frac{w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w<\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta}}\int_{0}^{u/2}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}.

Therefore,
I_{12}\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\min\{1,|u|^{-1-\beta+\frac{\alpha}{2s}}\}
, hence
I_{1}
satisfies the same estimate. The study of
I_{2}
is completely analogous to that of
n>1
. Therefore we are only left to study
I_{3}
. The arguments can be carried out analogously up to the point of estimating

\displaystyle\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}(w)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w.

Using that
|w-u|\approx|w|\gtrsim u
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}(w)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{w^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{1}w^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\frac{n+\alpha}{2s}}}\Bigg]\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\frac{1}{u^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}},

since
2s-1<\alpha<4s
. Therefore,
I_{32}\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\min\{1,|u|^{-1-\beta+\frac{\alpha}{2s}}\}
, and with this we get the desired bound for
I_{3}
and the completion of the proof for the case
n=1
.

Moving on to estimate
3
, we begin by defining for
u>0
the real variable function

G_{s}(u):=u^{-\frac{n+1}{2s}}\phi^{\prime}_{n}\big(u^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big),

so that in light of relation (
2.1
) we have

\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)\simeq\frac{x}{|x|^{n+2}}G_{s}\bigg(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg),\qquad\text{for}\quad t>0,x\neq 0.

By (
2.5
) it is clear that

(2.18)

|G_{s}(u)|\approx\frac{u}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s+2)/2}}.

Hence, as done for
F_{s}
, we can extend continuously the definition of
G_{s}
by zero for negative values of
u
. Notice also that the previous estimate implies that
G_{s}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}
.

Our next claim is that the operators
\nabla_{x}
and
\partial_{t}^{\beta}
commute when applied to
P_{s}
. To prove this, it suffices to check that the following integral is locally well-defined for every
x
and
t
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,w)|}{|t-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w.

Split the domain of integration as

\int_{|t-w|<1}\frac{|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,w)|}{|t-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{|t-w|\geq 1}\frac{|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,w)|}{|t-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w.

The second integral is clearly well-defined, since
\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)\simeq x/|x|^{n+2}G_{s}(t/|x|^{2s})
and we know that
G_{s}
is bounded. Thus, directly applying the triangle inequality in the numerator and using that
\beta>0
, we deduce that, indeed, the second integral is finite. For the first one, we need some more work. We shall distinguish four possibilities:

Case 1:
t\leq-1
. For such values of
t
the integral becomes null, since
\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)
and
\nabla_{x}P(x,w)
are zero.

Case 2:
t\in(-1,0]
. Observe that in this setting the integral can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-|t|}\frac{|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,w)|}{|w-t|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1-|t|}\frac{|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,w)-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,0)|}{|w-t|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s+1}}\int_{0}^{1-|t|}\frac{|G^{\prime}_{s}(\tau/|x|^{2s})|}{|w|^{\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w,

for some
\tau\in(0,w)
. By definition, there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}
so that for
u>0

G_{s}^{\prime}(u)=C_{1}\,u^{-(n+2s+1)/(2s)}\phi_{n,s}^{\prime}\big(u^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big)+C_{2}\,u^{-(n+2s+2)/(2s)}\phi_{n,s}^{\prime\prime}\big(u^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\big),

so using the estimates for
\phi^{\prime}_{n}
and
\phi_{n,s}^{\prime\prime}
in (
2.5
) we deduce

(2.19)

\displaystyle|G^{\prime}_{s}(u)|\approx\frac{1}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s+2)/2}},

which is a bounded function. Therefore

\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s+1}}\int_{0}^{1-|t|}\frac{|G^{\prime}_{s}(\tau/|x|^{2s})|}{|w|^{\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s+1}}<\infty,

for every
x\neq 0
.

Case 3:
t\in(0,1]
. The integral we were initially studying can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{t-1}^{0}\frac{|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)|}{|t-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{0}^{t+1}\frac{|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,w)|}{|t-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w.

The second integral can be tackled in exactly the same way as the integral in
Case 2
. Regarding the first one, estimate it as follows

\displaystyle\int_{t-1}^{0}\frac{|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,0)|}{|t-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s+1}}\int_{t-1}^{0}\frac{|G_{s}^{\prime}(\tau/|x|^{2s})||t|}{|t-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s+1}}\int_{t-1}^{0}\frac{|G_{s}^{\prime}(\tau/|x|^{2s})|}{|w|^{\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w<\infty,

where we have used
|t|\leq|t-w|+|w|
and also that
|t-w|=(t+|w|)\geq|w|
. The last inequality follows by the same arguments used in
Case 2
.

Case 4:
t>1
. For this final case, the integral can be estimated as

\displaystyle\int_{t-1}^{t+1}\frac{|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,w)|}{|t-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s+1}}\int_{t-1}^{t+1}\frac{|G_{s}^{\prime}(\tau/|x|^{2s})|}{|t-w|^{\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s+1}}<\infty.

Thus, we have obtained the desired commutativity between
\partial_{t}^{\beta}
and
\nabla_{x}
, which yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(x,t)=\partial_{t}^{\beta}\big[\nabla_{x}P_{s}\big](x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{|x|^{n+2}}\bigg[\partial_{t}^{\beta}G_{s}\bigg(\frac{\cdot}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg)\bigg](t)=\frac{x}{|x|^{n+2s\beta+2}}\partial^{\beta}G_{s}\bigg(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg).

Now it is a matter of showing that the following inequality holds

(2.20)

\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}G_{s}(u)\big\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\bigg\},

The proof of (
2.20
) is essentially identical to the one given for (
2.14
), using the bounds for
G_{s}
and
G_{s}^{\prime}
((
2.18
) and (
2.19
) respectively) instead of those for
F_{s}
and
F_{s}^{\prime}
. The faster decay of
G_{s}
and its derivative implies that one does not find any obstacles in (
2.16
). In fact, the integral that appears in the current analysis is
\int_{1}^{\infty}w^{-\frac{n+1}{2s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w
, which also converges for
n=1
. So using the previous estimate we deduce, for any
n>0
,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(x,t)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s\beta+1}}\bigg\rvert\partial_{t}^{\beta}G_{s}\bigg(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg)\bigg\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s\beta+1}}\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|^{2s(1+\beta)}}{|t|^{1+\beta}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2s+1}}\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{|x|^{2s(1+\beta)}},\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{2s(1+\beta)}{2s}}}\bigg\}=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2s+1}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}},

which proves the statement
3
in our lemma.

We continue by estimating
\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(x,t)
for
x\neq 0
and
t\neq 0
. Using (
2.13
) we rewrite it as

\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})=\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s(1+\beta)}}\partial^{\beta}F_{s}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg),

and we claim that the following inequality holds for

\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}^{\prime}(u)\big\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\bigg\}.

Let us also recall that we had the following estimates for

\displaystyle|F_{s}^{\prime}(u)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}},\hskip 28.45274pt|F_{s}^{\prime\prime}(u)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+4s)/2}}\leq\frac{1}{u\big(1+u^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}.

Observe that, on the one hand,

\displaystyle\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}^{\prime}(u)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(u)-F_{s}^{\prime}(w)|}{|u-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{R}}|F_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)|\int_{|u-w|<1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{|u-w|^{\beta}}+2\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{R}}|F_{s}^{\prime}(\nu)|\int_{|u-w|\geq 1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{|u-w|^{1+\beta}}\lesssim_{\beta}1,

by the boundedness of

and
F_{s}^{\prime\prime}
, and the fact that

. Therefore we are left to verify
\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}^{\prime}(u)\big\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}|u|^{-1-\beta}
. If
u<0
, since
F_{s}^{\prime}
is supported on
(0,\infty)
and
|u-w|>|u|

\displaystyle\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}^{\prime}(u)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(w)|}{|u-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{|u-w|^{1+\beta}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(w)|}{|u-w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\bigg(1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{\big(1+w^{1/s}\big)^{(n+2s)/2}}\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\bigg(1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\frac{n}{2s}+1}}\bigg)\lesssim\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}},

and we are done. If on the other hand
u>0

\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}^{\prime}\rvert
in a similar way as
\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}\rvert
in the proof of point

\displaystyle\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}^{\prime}(u)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|w|\leq u/2}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(w)-F_{s}^{\prime}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{u/2\leq|w|\leq 2u}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(w)-F_{s}^{\prime}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{|w|>2u}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(w)-F_{s}^{\prime}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Regarding
I_{1}
, notice that in the domain of integration we have
|w-u|\approx u
, so

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-u/2}^{0}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{0}^{u/2}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(u)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{0}^{u/2}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(w)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w.

The first two integrals can be directly bounded by

\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}|F_{s}^{\prime}(u)|\int_{0}^{u/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\leq\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\bigg(\frac{u}{(1+u^{1/s})^{(n+2s)/2}}\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u/2}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(w)|}{|w-u|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}|F_{s}^{\prime}(w)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w+\int_{1}^{\infty}|F_{s}^{\prime}(w)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\bigg(1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{w^{\frac{n}{2s}+1}}\bigg)\lesssim\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}},

and we are done with

. Moving on to
I_{2}
, we split it as follows

The study of
I_{21}
can be carried analogously to that of
I_{1}
, since in that domain of integration one has
|w-u|\geq 3|u|/2
, so we focus on
I_{22}
. Applying the mean value theorem and the bound for
|F_{s}^{\prime\prime}|
of (

) as well as relation (

) we get

\displaystyle I_{22}\leq\sup_{\nu\in[u/2,2u]}|F_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)|\int_{u/2}^{2u}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{|w-u|^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\sup_{\nu\in[u/2,2u]}|F_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)|\,u^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{u^{1-\beta}}{u(1+u^{1/s})^{(n+2s)/2}}\approx F_{s}(u)u^{-1-\beta}\leq u^{-1-\beta}.

So we are left to study

\displaystyle I_{3}\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{-2u}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(u)|}{|w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle+\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{s}^{\prime}(w)|+|F_{s}^{\prime}(u)|}{|w|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{3}{(1+u^{1/s})^{(n+2s)/2}}\int_{2u}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w}{|w|^{1+\beta}}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{u^{-\beta}}{(1+u^{1/s})^{(n+2s)/2}}\leq u^{-1-\beta},

that allows us to finally conclude

\big\rvert\partial^{\beta}F_{s}^{\prime}(u)\big\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\beta}}\bigg\},\qquad u\neq 0.

So using the previous estimate get, for

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(x,t)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s(1+\beta)}}\bigg\rvert\partial_{t}^{\beta}F_{s}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg)\bigg\rvert\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2s(1+\beta)}}\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|^{2s(1+\beta)}}{|t|^{1+\beta}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{n}}\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{|x|^{2s(1+\beta)}},\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{2s(1+\beta)}{2s}}}\bigg\}=\frac{1}{|x|^{n}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}}.

5

We deal first with the estimate concerning

where

Let us continue by assuming

. By definition,

|\partial_{t}^{\beta}f_{1}(t)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f_{1}(u)|}{|u+|t||^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-1/u}}{u^{n/2}(u+|t|)^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u.

Observe that on the one hand, since

On the other hand, since

and we are done.

If

, we split the integral as follows

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{\beta}f_{1}(t)|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|u|\leq t/2}\frac{|f_{1}(u)-f_{1}(t)|}{|u-t|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u+\int_{t/2\leq|u|\leq 2t}\frac{|f_{1}(u)-f_{1}(t)|}{|u-t|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u

In

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\int_{-t/2}^{0}\frac{|f_{1}(t)|}{|u-t|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u+\int_{0}^{t/2}\frac{|f_{1}(u)-f_{1}(t)|}{|u-t|^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\lesssim\frac{e^{-1/t}}{t^{(n+2\beta)/2}}+\int_{0}^{t/2}\frac{|f_{1}(u)-f_{1}(t)|}{t^{1+\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{1+\beta}}\int_{0}^{t/2}\frac{e^{-1/u}}{u^{n/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u+\frac{1}{t^{1+\beta}}\int_{0}^{t/2}

3.
Growth estimates for admissible functions

We will say that a positive Borel measure
\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
has upper
s
-parabolic
growth of degree
\rho

(
with constant
C
)
or simply
s
-parabolic
\rho
-growth
if there is some constant
C(n,s)>0
such that for any
s
-parabolic ball
B(\overline{x},r)
,

\mu\big(B(\overline{x},r)\big)\leq Cr^{\rho}.

It is clear that this property is invariant if formulated using cubes instead of balls. We will be interested in a generalized version of such growth that can be defined not only for measures, but also for general distributions. To introduce such notion we present the concept of admissible function:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. Given
\phi\in\pazocal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, we will say that it is an
admissible
function for an
s
-parabolic cube

if
\text{supp}(\phi)\subset Q
and

\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1,\qquad\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-1},\qquad\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-2s},\qquad\|\Delta\phi\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-2}.

Remark 3.1
.

If
\phi
is a
\pazocal{C}^{2}
function supported on
Q

s
-parabolic cube with
\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
,
\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-1}
and
\|\Delta\phi\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-2}
, then it also satisfies

\|(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-2s}.

Indeed, begin by observing that translations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
commute with
\nabla_{x}
and
(-\Delta)^{s}
. From it, it is clear that we may assume
Q
to be centered at the origin. Assuming this, let us fix
t\in\mathbb{R}
and compute

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}\phi(x,t)

\displaystyle:=c_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\phi(x+y,t)-2\phi(x,t)+\phi(x-y,t)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=c_{n,s}\int_{2Q}\frac{\phi(x+y,t)-2\phi(x,t)+\phi(x-y,t)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055ptc_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{2Q}}\frac{\phi(x+y,t)-2\phi(x,t)+\phi(x-y,t)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Regarding
I_{2}
, integration in polar coordinates yields

|I_{2}|\leq 4c_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{2Q}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{n+2s}}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-2s}.

For
I_{1}
, we apply twice the mean value theorem so that

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq c_{n,s}\int_{2Q}\frac{|\langle\nabla_{x}\phi(x+\eta_{1}y,t),y\rangle+\langle\nabla_{x}\phi(x-\eta_{2}y,t),y\rangle|}{|y|^{n+2s-1}}\lesssim\int_{2Q}\frac{\|\Delta\phi\|_{\infty}}{|y|^{n+2s-2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-2s}.

.

We will say that a distribution

-parabolic
n

s
-parabolic cube

In the end, the results below will help us estimate the growth of distributions of the form
\varphi T

-parabolic cube.

In any case, let us clarify that in the following Theorems

\displaystyle\big\rvert(-\Delta)^{\beta}(\varphi\phi)(\overline{x})\big\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg\rvert\int_{Q_{1}\cap R_{1}}\partial_{j}(\varphi\phi)(z,t)\bigg(\frac{z_{j}-x_{j}}{|z-x|^{n+2\beta}}-\frac{x_{0,j}-x_{j}}{|x_{0}-x|^{n+2\beta}}\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\bigg\rvert

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\ell(R)}{|x_{0}-x|^{n+2\beta}}\|\nabla_{x}(\varphi\phi)\|_{\infty}\ell(R)^{n}\lesssim\frac{\ell(R)^{n}}{|x_{0}-x|^{n+2\beta}},

by the mean value theorem. So, defining the cylinders
C_{j}:=2^{j}R_{1}\times(I_{Q}\cap I_{R})
for
j\geq 1
, relation (
3.3
) implies

\displaystyle I_{2}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{\ell(R)^{2\beta}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j(n+2\beta)}}\int_{C_{j+1}\setminus{C_{j}}}\big\rvert f(\overline{x})-c\big\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x},

If
f\in\text{{BMO}}_{p_{s}}
, we choose
c:=f_{2R}
and proceed as in Theorem
3.1
,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{\ell(R)^{2\beta}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j(n+2\beta)}}\bigg(\int_{C_{j+1}\setminus{C_{j}}}\big\rvert f(\overline{x})-f_{2^{j}R}\big\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}+\int_{C_{j+1}\setminus{C_{j}}}\big\rvert f_{2R}-f_{2^{j}R}\big\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{\ast,p_{s}}}{\ell(R)^{2\beta}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j(n+2\beta)}}\bigg[\big(2^{j}\ell(R)\big)^{\frac{n+2s}{q}}|C_{j+1}\setminus{C_{j}}|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}+j|C_{j+1}\setminus{C_{j}}|\bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{\ast,p_{s}}}{\ell(R)^{2\beta}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j(n+2\beta)}}\Big[\ell(R)^{n+2s}2^{j(n+\frac{2s}{q})+\frac{2s}{q^{\prime}}}+j\ell(R)^{n+2s}\,2^{jn+2s}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\ell(R)^{n+2(s-\beta)}\bigg(1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}}}{2^{j(2\beta-\frac{2s}{q})}}\bigg).

Fixing
q>s/\beta
so that this last sum is convergent, proves the result.

On the other hand, if
f\in\text{Lip}_{\alpha,p_{s}}
let
c:=f(\overline{x}_{0})
and also proceed as in Theorem
3.1
to deduce

\displaystyle I_{2}\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\frac{\|f\|_{\text{Lip}_{\alpha,p_{s}}}}{\ell(R)^{2\beta}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{j}\ell(R))^{\alpha}}{2^{j(n+2\beta)}}|C_{j+1}\setminus{C_{j}}|\lesssim\|f\|_{\text{Lip}_{\alpha,p_{s}}}\ell(R)^{n+2(s-\beta)+\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{(2\beta-\alpha)j}},

that is a convergent sum since
\alpha<2\beta
by hypothesis.
∎

Recall that given
f:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\beta\in(0,n)
, we define its
n
-dimensional
\beta
-Riesz transform (whenever it makes sense) as

\displaystyle\pazocal{I}_{\beta}^{n}f(\cdot,t):=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-\beta}}\ast f(\cdot,t),

for each

, where the convolution is thought in a principal value sense. Let us observe that for a test function

, for example, the operators
\pazocal{I}_{\beta}^{n}
and

commute.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
,
\alpha\in(0,1-\beta)
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
. Assume
\|\nabla_{x}\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-1}
and
\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(R)^{-1}
. Then, if
\ell(R)\leq\ell(Q)
, for each

we have

1.

If

,

|\langle f,\partial_{x_{i}}[\pazocal{I}^{n}_{\beta}(\varphi\phi)]\rangle|\lesssim_{\beta}\|f\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\ell(R)^{n+2s+\beta-1}.

2.

If
f\in\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha,p_{s}}
,

|\langle f,\partial_{x_{i}}[\pazocal{I}^{n}_{\beta}(\varphi\phi)]\rangle|\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\|f\|_{\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha,p_{s}}}\ell(R)^{n+2s+\beta+\alpha-1}.

Proof.

Notice that for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle|\langle f,\partial_{x_{i}}[\pazocal{I}^{n}_{\beta}(\varphi\phi)]\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle f-c,\partial_{x_{i}}[\pazocal{I}^{n}_{\beta}(\varphi\phi)]\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2R_{1}\times(I_{Q}\cap I_{R})}|f(\overline{x})-c|\big\rvert\partial_{x_{i}}[\pazocal{I}^{n}_{\beta}(\varphi\phi)](\overline{x})\big\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{2R_{1}})\times(I_{Q}\cap I_{R})}|f(\overline{x})-c|\big\rvert\partial_{x_{i}}[\pazocal{I}^{n}_{\beta}(\varphi\phi)](\overline{x})\big\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Regarding

, we have for some conjugate exponents
q,q^{\prime}
to be fixed later on,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{2R}|f(\overline{x})-c|^{q^{\prime}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\bigg(\int_{I_{Q}\cap I_{R}}\int_{2R_{1}}\big\rvert\pazocal{I}_{\beta}^{n}[\partial_{x_{i}}(\varphi\phi)](x,t)\big\rvert^{q}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{2R}|f(\overline{x})-c|^{q^{\prime}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\bigg(\int_{I_{Q}\cap I_{R}}\|\pazocal{I}^{n}_{\beta}[\partial_{x_{i}}(\varphi\phi)(\cdot,t)]\|_{q}^{q}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Choosing

, we shall apply
[
Gr
, Theorem 6.1.3]
and obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\bigg(\int_{2R}|f(\overline{x})-c|^{q^{\prime}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\bigg(\int_{I_{Q}\cap I_{R}}\|\partial_{x_{i}}(\varphi\phi)(\cdot,t)\|_{\frac{qn}{n+q\beta}}^{q}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{2R}|f(\overline{x})-c|^{q^{\prime}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\ell(R)^{\frac{n+q\beta+2s}{q}-1}.

If we assume
f\in\text{{BMO}}_{p_{s}}
, we choose
c:=f_{2R}
and apply a
s
-parabolic version of John-Nirenberg’s inequality to deduce

I_{1}\lesssim_{\beta}\|f\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\ell(R)^{\frac{n+2s}{q^{\prime}}}\ell(R)^{\frac{n+q\beta+2s}{q}-1}=\|f\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\ell(R)^{n+2s+\beta-1}.

If we assume
f\in\text{Lip}_{\alpha,p_{s}}
, we choose
c:=f(\overline{x}_{R})
, being
\overline{x}_{R}
the center of
R
, and obtain

I_{1}\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\|f\|_{\text{Lip}_{\alpha},p_{s}}\ell(R)^{\frac{n+2s}{q^{\prime}}+\alpha}\ell(R)^{\frac{n+q\beta+2s}{q}-1}=\|f\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\ell(R)^{n+2s+\beta+\alpha-1}.

To study
I_{2}
, we proceed as in Theorem
3.3
. For any
\overline{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{2R_{1}})\times(I_{Q}\cap I_{R})
, if
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the center of
Q_{1}\cap R_{1}
, by the mean value theorem we get

\displaystyle\big\rvert\pazocal{I}_{\beta}^{n}[\partial_{x_{i}}(\varphi\phi)](\overline{x})\big\rvert

\displaystyle=\bigg\rvert\int_{Q_{1}\cap R_{1}}\partial_{x_{i}}(\varphi\phi)(z,t)\frac{1}{|z-x|^{n-\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle=\bigg\rvert\int_{Q_{1}\cap R_{1}}\partial_{x_{i}}(\varphi\phi)(z,t)\bigg(\frac{1}{|z-x|^{n-\beta}}-\frac{1}{|x_{0}-x|^{n-\beta}}\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\ell(R)}{|x_{0}-x|^{n-\beta+1}}\|\nabla_{x}(\varphi\phi)\|_{\infty}\,\ell(R)^{n}\lesssim\frac{\ell(R)^{n}}{|x_{0}-x|^{n-\beta+1}}.

This way, putting
C_{j}:=2^{j}R_{1}\times(I_{Q}\cap I_{R})
for

, as in Theorem
3.3
,

\displaystyle I_{2}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{\ell(R)^{-\beta+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j(n-\beta+1)}}\int_{C_{j+1}\setminus{C_{j}}}\big\rvert f(\overline{x})-c\big\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}.

The case

is dealt with analogously as in Theorem

, obtaining

\displaystyle I_{2}\lesssim_{\beta}\|f\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\ell(R)^{n+2s+\beta-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j(n-\beta+1)}}\Big[2^{j(n+\frac{2s}{q})}+j\,2^{jn}\Big],

so choosing

we are done. Observe that we also need

in order for the above sum to converge. The case

can be dealt with as follows

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\frac{\|f\|_{\text{Lip}_{\alpha,p_{s}}}}{\ell(R)^{-\beta+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{j}\ell(R))^{\alpha}}{2^{j(n-\beta+1)}}|C_{j+1}\setminus{C_{j}}|\lesssim\|f\|_{\text{Lip}_{\alpha,p_{s}}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell(R)^{n+2s+\beta+\alpha-1}}{2^{(1-\beta-\alpha)j}},

and this sum is convergent by the hypothesis

.
∎

4.
Potentials of positive measures with growth restrictions

The main goal of this section is to deduce some important
\text{BMO}_{p_{s}}
and
\text{Lip}_{\alpha,p_{s}}
estimates of potentials of the form
\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast\mu
, where
\mu
is a finite positive Borel measure with some upper
s
-parabolic growth. We begin by proving a generalization of
[
MaPrTo
, Lemma 4.2]
and
[
MaPr
, Lemma 7.2]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
,
\eta\in(0,1)
and
\mu
be a positive measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
which has upper
s
-parabolic growth of degree
n+2s\eta
. Then

\|P_{s}\ast\mu\|_{\text{{Lip}}_{\eta,t}}\lesssim_{\eta}1.

Proof.

Let
\overline{x}:=(x,t),\widehat{x}:=(x,\tau)
be fixed points in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
t\neq\tau
, and set
\overline{x}_{0}:=(\overline{x}+\widehat{x})/2
. Writing
\overline{y}:=(y,u)
and
B_{0}:=B(\overline{x}_{0},|\overline{x}-\widehat{x}|_{p_{s}})=B(\overline{x}_{0},|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2s}})
, we split

\displaystyle|P_{s}\ast\mu(\overline{x})

\displaystyle-P_{s}\ast\mu(\widehat{x})|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{2B_{0}}}|P_{s}(x-y,t-u)-P_{s}(x-y,\tau-u)|\text{d}\mu(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{2B_{0}}|P_{s}(x-y,t-u)-P_{s}(x-y,\tau-u)|\text{d}\mu(\overline{y})=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Defining the
s
-parabolic annuli
A_{j}:=2^{j+1}B_{0}\setminus{2^{j}B_{0}}
for
j\geq 1
and arguing as in the last estimate of Theorem
2.2
we get

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{|t-\tau|}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s}}\text{d}\mu(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-\tau|\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\mu\big(2^{j+1}B_{0}\big)}{\big(2^{j}|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2s}}\big)^{n+2s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-\tau|^{\eta}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{2^{2s(1-\eta)}}\simeq_{\eta}|t-\tau|^{\eta},

that is the desired estimate. Regarding
I_{2}
, observe that

I_{2}\leq P_{s}\ast(\chi_{2B_{0}}\mu)(\overline{x})+P_{s}\ast(\chi_{2B_{0}}\mu)(\widehat{x}).

Notice now that

\displaystyle P_{s}\ast(\chi_{2B_{0}}\mu)(\overline{x})\lesssim\int_{2B_{0}}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{y})}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}\leq 5|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{y})}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n}}\lesssim_{\eta}|t-\tau|^{\eta},

where we have split the latter domain of integration into (decreasing)
s
-parabolic annuli. Since this also holds replacing
\overline{x}
by
\widehat{x}
, we also have
I_{2}\lesssim|t-\tau|^{\eta}
and we are done.
∎

The above result allows us to prove that, given a positive measure as in the above statement, we can ensure that the potential
\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast\mu
already belongs to
\text{BMO}_{p_{s}}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
,
\beta\in(0,1)
. Let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with upper
s
-parabolic growth of degree
n+2s\beta
. Then,

\big\|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast\mu\big\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\lesssim_{\beta}1.

Proof.

Fix
\overline{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
r>0
. Consider the
s
-parabolic ball
B:=B(\overline{x}_{0},r)=B_{0}\times I_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and a constant
c_{B}
to be determined later. We want to show that
c_{B}
can be chosen so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast\mu(\overline{y})-c_{B}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}\lesssim_{\beta}1.

To that end, begin by considering the following sets, which define a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
:

\displaystyle R_{1}:=5B,\hskip 21.33955ptR_{2}:=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{(5B_{0}\times\mathbb{R})},\hskip 21.33955ptR_{3}:=(5B_{0}\times\mathbb{R})\setminus{5B},

as well as their corresponding characteristic functions
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
and
\chi_{3}
. Bearing in mind the estimates proved in Theorems
2.4
and
2.6
for
\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}
and the fact that
\mu
is finite, it is clear that the quantity
|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast(\chi_{2}\mu)(\overline{x}_{0})|
is also finite. Moreover, notice that
|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}|
is bounded by
s
-parabolically homogeneous functions of degree
-n-2s\beta
for any dimension. In fact, we deduce the following estimates: given any
\varepsilon,\alpha>0
, we obtain if
n>2
,

|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2s}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}}\leq\frac{1}{|x|^{n-\varepsilon}|t|^{\frac{\varepsilon+2s\beta}{2s}}},\qquad\text{if }\;\varepsilon<2s(1-\beta).

For
n=2
,

if
s<1
,

\displaystyle\quad|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|x|^{2-2s}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}}\leq\frac{1}{|x|^{2-\varepsilon}|t|^{\frac{\varepsilon+2s\beta}{2s}}},\qquad\text{if }\;\varepsilon<2s(1-\beta),

if
s=1
,

\displaystyle\quad|\partial_{t}^{\beta}W(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\frac{1}{|x|^{\alpha}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2+2\beta-\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{|x|^{\alpha}|t|^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2}}},\qquad\hskip 17.07182pt\text{if }\;2\beta<\alpha<2.

And for
n=1
,

if
s<1
,

\displaystyle\quad|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha}\frac{1}{|x|^{1-2s+\alpha}|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)-\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{|x|^{1-2s+\alpha}|t|^{1+\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2s}}},\quad\text{if }\;2s\beta<\alpha<2s,

if
s=1
,

\displaystyle\quad|\partial_{t}^{\beta}W(\overline{x})|\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{1+2\beta}}\leq\frac{1}{|x|^{\varepsilon}|t|^{\frac{1+2\beta-\varepsilon}{2}}},\qquad\hskip 86.06941pt\text{if }\;2\beta-1<\varepsilon<1.

In light of the above inequalities, and using that
\beta<1
, it is clear that
\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}
defines a
\pazocal{L}^{n+1}
-locally integrable function in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
once endowed with the
s
-parabolic distance. Hence, there exists some
\overline{\xi}_{0}\in B
(that we may think as close as we need to
\overline{x}_{0}
) such that
|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast(\chi_{3}\mu)(\overline{\xi}_{0})|
is finite. Bearing all these observations in mind, we choose

to be

c_{B}:=\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast(\chi_{2}\mu)(\overline{x}_{0})+\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast(\chi_{3}\mu)(\overline{\xi}_{0}).

Therefore, we are interested in bounding by a constant the following quantity:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast\mu(\overline{y})-c_{B}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast(\chi_{1}\mu)(\overline{y})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast(\chi_{2}\mu)(\overline{y})-\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast(\chi_{2}\mu)(\overline{x}_{0})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast(\chi_{3}\mu)(\overline{y})-\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}\ast(\chi_{3}\mu)(\overline{\xi}_{0})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

, simply notice that

I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{5B}\bigg(\int_{B}|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}\bigg)\text{d}\mu(\overline{z}).

Using any of the bounds above for

, depending on

I_{1}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|B|}r^{2s(1-\beta)}\mu(5B)\lesssim_{\beta}1.

Regarding
I_{2}
, write

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\int_{R_{2}}|\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})-\partial_{t}^{\beta}P_{s}(\overline{x}_{0}-\overline{z})|\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}.

If we name
\overline{x}:=\overline{x}_{0}-\overline{z}
and
\overline{x}^{\prime}:=\overline{y}-\overline{z}

|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}=|\overline{x}_{0}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}\leq r<\frac{|x_{0}-z|}{2}=\frac{|x|}{2},

where the second inequality holds because
\overline{z}\in R_{2}
. Therefore, by the last estimate of Theorems
2.4
and
2.6
, writing

we get

\displaystyle I_{2}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\int_{R_{2}}\frac{|\overline{y}-\overline{x}_{0}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|x_{0}-z|^{n+2\zeta-2s}|\overline{x}_{0}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}}

\displaystyle\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{2\zeta}\int_{R_{2}}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})}{|x_{0}-z|^{n+2\zeta-2s}|\overline{x}_{0}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}}.

Let us split
R_{2}
into proper disjoint pieces. Take the cylinders given by
C_{j}:=5^{j}B_{0}\times\mathbb{R},\,j\in\mathbb{Z},\;j\geq 1
, as well as the annular cylinders
\widehat{C}_{j}:=C_{j+1}\setminus{C_{j}},\,j\geq 1
. The partition of
R_{2}
we are interested in is given by the disjoint union of all the sets
\widehat{C}_{j},\,j\geq 1
, which clearly cover

. Therefore

(4.1)

I_{2}\lesssim_{\beta}r^{2\zeta}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\widehat{C}_{j}}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})}{|x_{0}-z|^{n+2\zeta-2s}|\overline{x}_{0}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{2s(1+\beta)}}.

At the same time, for each
j\geq 1
, we shall consider a proper partition of
\widehat{C}_{j}
. Denote
A_{k}=5^{k+1}B\setminus{5^{k}B}
for every positive integer

and define
\widehat{C}_{j,k}:=\widehat{C}_{j}\cap A_{k},\,k\geq 1
. Let us make some observations about the sets
\widehat{C}_{j,k}
. First, notice that by definition, for each

,

\widehat{C}_{j,k}=\big[(5^{j+1}B_{0}\setminus 5^{j}B_{0})\times\mathbb{R}\big]\cap\big(5^{k+1}B\setminus{5^{k}B}\big).

Hence, using that

\big[(5^{j+1}B_{0}\setminus 5^{j}B_{0})\times\mathbb{R}\big]\cap\big(5^{k+1}B\setminus{5^{k}B}\big)=\varnothing,\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for}\hskip 14.22636ptk<j,

we have that, in fact,
\widehat{C}_{j}
can be covered by
\widehat{C}_{j,k}
for
k\geq j
, that is

\widehat{C}_{j}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\widehat{C}_{j,k}=\bigcup_{k=j}^{\infty}\widehat{C}_{j,k}.

Secondly, in order to estimate
\mu(\widehat{C}_{j,k})
, observe that for any
k\geq j
, by definition, the set
\widehat{C}_{j,k}
can be written explicitly as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{C}_{j,k}

\displaystyle=\big[\big(5^{j+1}B_{0}\setminus{5^{j}B_{0}}\big)\times\mathbb{R}\big]\cap\big(5^{k+1}B\setminus 5^{k}B\big)

\displaystyle=\big[\big(5^{j+1}B_{0}\setminus{5^{j}B_{0}}\big)\times\mathbb{R}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt\cap\Big\{\big[\big(5^{k+1}B_{0}\setminus{5^{k}B_{0}}\big)\times 5^{2s(k+1)}I_{0}\big]\cup\big[5^{k}B_{0}\times\big(5^{2s(k+1)}I_{0}\setminus 5^{2k}I_{0}\big)\big]\Big\}.

Continue by observing that if
k=j
, the intersection with the second element of the union is empty, so

\widehat{C}_{j,j}=\big(5^{j+1}B_{0}\setminus{5^{j}B_{0}}\big)\times 5^{2s(j+1)}I_{0};

while if
k>j
one has the contrary, that is, the intersection with the first element is empty, and therefore, since

,

\widehat{C}_{j,k}=\big(5^{j+1}B_{0}\setminus{5^{j}B_{0}}\big)\times\big[5^{2s(k+1)}I_{0}\setminus 5^{2sk}I_{0}\big].

Observe that

Lemma 5.1

[

, Lemma 6.2]

-parabolic cubes with

and

Now cover each

and apply an

-parabolic version of Harvey-Polking’s lemma (that admits an analogous proof, see

For the lower bound we will apply (an

-parabolic dyadic lattice, as it is presented in the proof of

and
\mu(B(\overline{x},r))\leq r^{n+1}
for all

,

. If we prove that

\|\nabla_{x}P_{s}\ast\mu\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\lesssim 1\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\|\partial^{\frac{1}{2s}}_{t}P_{s}\ast\mu\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\lesssim 1,

. But by Lemma

-parabolic ball

associated to

Using Theorem
2.2
it easily follows that this last expression is well-defined. Let us now estimate
\|\nabla_{x}P_{s}\ast\mu\|_{\ast,p_{s}}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}P_{s}\ast\mu(\overline{y})-c_{B}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\int_{2B}|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})|\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}

\displaystyle\hskip 39.83368pt+\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{2B}}|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}_{0}-\overline{z})|\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Writing

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{2B}\bigg(\int_{B_{0}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}{|y-z|^{n-\varepsilon}}\int_{I_{0}}\frac{dt}{|t-u|^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2s}}}\bigg)\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|}\big(r^{\varepsilon}(r^{2s})^{1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2s}}\big)\mu(2B)\lesssim 1.

with

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{2B}}\frac{|\overline{y}-\overline{x}_{0}|_{p_{s}}}{|\overline{z}-\overline{x}_{0}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2}}\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}\leq r\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{2B}}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})}{|\overline{z}-\overline{x}_{0}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2}}

\displaystyle=r^{2\zeta}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}B\setminus{2^{j}B}}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})}{|\overline{z}-\overline{x}_{0}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2}}\lesssim r\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{j+1}r)^{n+1}}{(2^{j}r)^{n+2}}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\lesssim 1,

and we are done.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let

. A compact set

-caloric functions with

Theorem 5.6

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt+\frac{1}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}\int_{R_{2}}|\partial_{x_{i}}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{z})-\partial_{x_{i}}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})|\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{x}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}/2}\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2}}\text{d}\mu(\overline{z}).

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

\|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\leq 1,\hskip 21.33955pt\|\partial^{\sigma/s}_{t}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\leq 1.

Let
Q
be a fixed
s
-parabolic cube and
\varphi
an admissible function for
Q
. Then, if
R
is any
s
-parabolic cube with
\ell(R)\leq\ell(Q)
and
\phi
is admissible for
R
, we have
|\langle\varphi T,\phi\rangle|\lesssim_{\sigma}\ell(R)^{n+2\sigma}
.

Proof.

Let
T
,
Q
and
\varphi
be as above, as well as
R

s
-parabolic cube with
\ell(R)\leq\ell(Q)
and
R\cap Q\neq\varnothing
, and
\phi
admissible function for
R
. We already know, in light of the proof of Theorem
5.2
,

|\langle\varphi T,\phi\rangle|\leq|\langle(-\Delta)^{s}P_{s}\ast T,\varphi\phi\rangle|+|\langle P_{s}\ast T,\partial_{t}(\varphi\phi)\rangle|=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, simply apply Theorem
3.3
with
\beta:=s-\sigma
so that

\displaystyle I_{1}=|\langle(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast T,(-\Delta)^{s-\sigma}(\varphi\phi)\rangle|\lesssim_{\sigma}\ell(R)^{n+2\sigma}

Regarding
I_{2}
, if
\sigma>0
, observe that defining
\beta:=1-\sigma/s\in(0,1)
we get

\partial_{t}(\varphi\phi)\simeq_{\sigma}\,\partial_{t}^{1-\beta}\Big(\partial_{t}(\varphi\phi)\ast_{t}|t|^{-\beta}\Big),

so by Theorem
3.1
we are done. If
\sigma=0
, we simply have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=|\langle P_{s}\ast T-(P_{s}\ast T)_{R},\partial_{t}(\varphi\phi)\rangle|\leq\int_{Q\cap R}\big\rvert P_{s}\ast T(\overline{x})-(P\ast T)_{R}\big\rvert\big\rvert\partial_{t}(\varphi\phi)(\overline{x})\big\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}

\displaystyle\leq\ell(R)^{-2s}\int_{R}\big\rvert P\ast T(\overline{x})-(P\ast T)_{R}\big\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}\leq\ell(R)^{-2s}\ell(R)^{n+2s}\|P_{s}\ast T\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\leq\ell(R)^{n}.

∎

Theorem 5.9
.

For any
s\in(0,1]
,
\sigma\in[0,s)
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
compact set,

\gamma_{\Theta^{s},\ast}^{\sigma}(E)\approx_{\sigma}\pazocal{H}^{n+2\sigma}_{\infty,p_{s}}(E).

Proof.

Again, for the upper bound we proceed analogously as in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, using now Theorem
5.8
. For the lower bound, we apply Frostman’s lemma. Assume then
\pazocal{H}_{\infty,p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma}(E)>0
and consider a non trivial positive Borel measure
\mu
supported on
E
with
\mu(E)\geq c\pazocal{H}_{\infty,p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma}(E)
and
\mu(B(\overline{x},r))\leq r^{n+2\sigma}
for all
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
r>0
. We have to prove

\|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\leq 1,\hskip 21.33955pt\|\partial^{\sigma/s}_{t}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\leq 1,

If
\sigma>0
, by Lemma
4.2
with
\beta:=\sigma/s
we already have
\|\partial^{\sigma/s}_{t}P_{s}\ast\mu\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\lesssim_{\sigma}1
. So we are left to control the
\text{{BMO}}_{p_{s}}
norm of
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast\mu
for
\sigma\in[0,s)
. Thus, let us fix an
s
-parabolic ball
B(\overline{x}_{0},r)
and consider the characteristic function
\chi_{2B}
associated to
2B
. Set also
\chi_{2B^{c}}=1-\chi_{2B}
. In this setting, we pick

c_{B}:=(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast(\chi_{2B^{c}}\mu)(\overline{x}_{0}).

Using Theorem
2.3
it easily follows that this last expression is well-defined. We estimate
\|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast\mu\|_{\ast,p_{s}}
using the previous constant:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}

\displaystyle\int_{B}|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast\mu(\overline{y})-c_{B}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\int_{2B}|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})|\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{2B}}|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})-(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}(\overline{x}_{0}-\overline{z})|\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

To deal with
I_{1}
, notice that by Theorem
2.3
, choosing
0<\varepsilon<2(s-\sigma)
and arguing as in Theorem
5.3
we have

I_{1}\lesssim_{\sigma}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{2B}\bigg(\int_{B}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}}{|\overline{y}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma}}\bigg)\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|}\big(r^{\varepsilon}(r^{2s})^{1-\frac{\varepsilon+2\sigma}{2s}}\big)\mu(2B)\lesssim 1

by the
n+2\sigma
growth of
\mu
. Regarding
I_{2}
, notice that naming
\overline{x}:=\overline{x}_{0}-\overline{z}
and
\overline{x}^{\prime}:=\overline{y}-\overline{z}
, we have
|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}/2
so we can apply the fifth estimate of Theorem
2.3
, that implies

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\sigma}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{2B}}\frac{|\overline{y}-\overline{x}_{0}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|\overline{z}-\overline{x}_{0}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma+2\zeta}}\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}\leq r^{2\zeta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{2B}}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})}{|\overline{z}-\overline{x}_{0}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma+2\zeta}}\lesssim_{\sigma}1,

again by the by the
n+2\sigma
growth of
\mu
.
∎

5.4.
The capacity
\gamma_{\Theta^{s},\alpha}^{\sigma}

We define now a capacity with an
s
-parabolic
\text{Lip}_{\alpha}
normalization condition.

Definition 5.7
.

Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
s\in(0,1]
,
\sigma\in[0,s)
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
compact set, define its
\Delta^{\sigma}
-
\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha,p_{s}}
-caloric capacity
as

\gamma_{\Theta^{s},\alpha}^{\sigma}(E):=\sup|\langle T,1\rangle|,

where the supremum is taken among all distributions
T
with
\text{supp}(T)\subset E
and satisfying

\|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\text{Lip}_{\alpha},p_{s}}\leq 1,\hskip 21.33955pt\|\partial^{\sigma/s}_{t}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\text{Lip}_{\alpha},p_{s}}\leq 1.

Such distributions will be called
admissible for
\gamma_{\Theta^{s},\alpha}^{\sigma}(E)
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
s\in(0,1]
and
\sigma\in[0,s)
. A compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is said to be
removable for
s
-caloric functions with
\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha,p_{s}}
-
(\sigma,\sigma/s)
-Laplacian
if for any open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, any function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}f\|_{\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha},p_{s}}<\infty,\hskip 21.33955pt\|\partial_{t}^{\sigma/s}f\|_{\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha},p_{s}}<\infty,

satisfying the
\Theta^{s}
-equation in
\Omega\setminus{E}
, also satisfies the previous equation in the whole
\Omega
. If
\sigma=0
, we will also say that
E
is
removable for

\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha,p_{s}}

s
-caloric functions
.

If
T
is a compactly supported distribution satisfying the above properties, then
T
presents upper
s
-parabolic growth of degree
n+2\sigma+\alpha
. As in §
5.2
, the following result will only be valid for a certain range of values of
\alpha
, dependent on
s
and
\sigma
.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
,
\sigma\in[0,s)
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
with
\alpha<2s-2\sigma
. Let
T
be a distribution in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with

\|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha},p_{s}}\leq 1,\hskip 21.33955pt\|\partial^{\sigma/s}_{t}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha},p_{s}}\leq 1.

Let
Q
be a fixed
s
-parabolic cube and
\varphi
an admissible function for
Q
. Then, if
R
is any
s
-parabolic cube with
\ell(R)\leq\ell(Q)
and
\phi
is admissible for
R
, we have
|\langle\varphi T,\phi\rangle|\lesssim\ell(R)^{n+2\sigma+\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
T
,
Q
and
\varphi
be as above, as well as
R

s
-parabolic cube with
\ell(R)\leq\ell(Q)
and
R\cap Q\neq\varnothing
, and
\phi
admissible function for
R
. Again,

|\langle\varphi T,\phi\rangle|\leq|\langle(-\Delta)^{s}P_{s}\ast T,\varphi\phi\rangle|+|\langle P_{s}\ast T,\partial_{t}(\varphi\phi)\rangle|=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, simply apply Theorem
3.3
with
\beta:=s-\sigma
so that

\displaystyle I_{1}=|\langle(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast T,(-\Delta)^{s-\sigma}(\varphi\phi)\rangle|\lesssim_{\sigma,\alpha}\ell(R)^{n+2\sigma+\alpha}.

Regarding

\sigma>0

\beta:=1-\sigma/s\in(0,1)
and apply Theorem
3.1
. If
\sigma=0

\overline{x}_{R}
be the center of

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=|\langle P_{s}\ast T-P_{s}\ast T(\overline{x}_{R}),\partial_{t}(\varphi\phi)\rangle|\leq\ell(R)^{-2s}\int_{R}|\overline{x}-\overline{x}_{R}|^{\alpha}_{p_{s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}\lesssim\ell(R)^{n+\alpha}.

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
,
\sigma\in[0,s)
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
with
\alpha<2s-2\sigma
. Then, for
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
compact set,

\gamma_{\Theta^{s},\alpha}^{\sigma}(E)\approx_{\sigma,\alpha}\pazocal{H}^{n+2\sigma+\alpha}_{\infty,p_{s}}(E).

Proof.

For the upper bound we argue again as in Theorem
5.3
, using now Theorem
5.11
. For the lower bound, assume
\pazocal{H}_{\infty,p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma+\alpha}(E)>0
and apply Frostman’s lemma to consider a non trivial positive Borel measure
\mu
supported on
E
with
\mu(E)\geq c\pazocal{H}_{\infty,p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma+\alpha}(E)
and
\mu(B(\overline{x},r))\leq r^{n+2\sigma+\alpha}
for all
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
r>0
. It suffices to verify

\|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha},p_{s}}\leq 1,\hskip 21.33955pt\|\partial^{\sigma/s}_{t}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha},p_{s}}\leq 1.

If
\sigma>0
, by Lemma
4.3
with
\beta:=\sigma/s
we already have
\|\partial^{\sigma/s}_{t}P_{s}\ast\mu\|_{\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha},p_{s}}\lesssim_{\sigma,\alpha}1
. So we are left to estimate
\|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast\mu\|_{\text{{Lip}}_{\alpha},p_{s}}
for
\sigma\in[0,s)
, and we do it as in Theorem
5.6
. Choose any
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\overline{x}\neq\overline{y}
and consider the following partition of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,

\displaystyle R_{1}:

\displaystyle=\big\{\overline{z}\;:\;|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{x}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}/2\big\}\cup\big\{\overline{z}\;:\;|\overline{y}-\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{y}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}/2\big\},

\displaystyle R_{2}:=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{R_{1}}

\displaystyle=\big\{\overline{z}\;:\;|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}>|\overline{x}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}/2\big\}\cap\big\{\overline{z}\;:\;|\overline{y}-\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}>|\overline{y}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}/2\big\},

with their corresponding characteristic functions
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
respectively.
This way, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast\mu(\overline{x})-(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}\ast\mu(\overline{y})|}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\hskip-135.15059pt\leq\frac{1}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{x}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}/2}|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{z})-(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})|\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})

\displaystyle\hskip-106.69783pt+\frac{1}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}\int_{|\overline{y}-\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{y}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}/2}|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{z})-(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})|\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})

\displaystyle\hskip-106.69783pt+\frac{1}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}\int_{R_{2}}|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{z})-(-\Delta)^{\sigma}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})|\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Regarding
I_{1}
, the fifth estimate of Lemma
2.3
yields

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\sigma}\frac{1}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{x}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}/2}\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma+2\zeta}}\text{d}\mu(\overline{z}).

Split the previous domain of integration into
s
-parabolic annuli centered at
\overline{x}
with exponentially increasing radii proportional to
|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}
, and deduce as in Theorem
5.6
that
I_{1}\lesssim_{\sigma,\alpha}1
, using now that
\mu
has
n+2\sigma+\alpha
growth. For
I_{2}
, we argue as in
I_{1}
just interchanging the roles of
\overline{x}
and
\overline{y}
. Finally, for
I_{3}
, the first estimate of Lemma
2.3
yields

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}\int_{R_{2}}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})}{|\overline{x}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma}}+\frac{1}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}\int_{R_{2}}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})}{|\overline{y}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{\alpha}}\bigg(\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}>|\overline{x}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}/2}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})}{|\overline{x}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma}}+\int_{|\overline{y}-\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}>|\overline{y}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}/2}\frac{\text{d}\mu(\overline{z})}{|\overline{y}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2\sigma}}\bigg).

Both of the above integrals can be dealt with by splitting the domain of integration into exponentially decreasing annuli, centered at
\overline{x}
and
\overline{y}
respectively, and using that
\mu
has growth of degree strictly bigger than
n+2\sigma
. Thus, we obtain
I_{3}\lesssim_{\sigma,\alpha}1
and we are done
∎

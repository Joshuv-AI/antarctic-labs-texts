Multiscale phosphorus loss in farmland driven by precipitation: effects of farmland type
Authors: Kai Shi, Hui Zhang, Xiaoli Mei, Boyun Liu, Qiang Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Precipitation is the primary driver of phosphorus loss from farmland. However, the multitemporal and spatial characteristics, as well as the mechanisms of phosphorus loss across different farmland types at the watershed scale, remain poorly understood. This study examines the multiscale impacts of different farmland types on phosphorus loss processes driven by long-term precipitation, focusing on two adjacent small watersheds, the Yulin River and the Dahong River, in the Sichuan Basin. First, based on remote sensing data from 2021 to 2022 (precipitation, temperature, land cover) and river water quality monitoring data (total phosphorus (TP), dissolved oxygen (DO), and permanganate index (COD)), the spatiotemporal variations in river water quality are analyzed. The results indicate that under similar climate conditions and pollution source distributions, the downstream TP concentration in the Dahong River (dominated by paddy fields) is significantly lower than that in the upstream, whereas in the Yulin River (dominated by dryland farming), the downstream TP concentration is higher than that in the upstream. Secondly, the study develops a multisource coupling analysis framework that integrates detrended cross-correlation analysis (DCCA), multifractal detrended cross-correlation analysis (MFDCCA), and remote sensing data to evaluate the impact of different farmland types on phosphorus loss behavior. The DCCA analysis results show that precipitation and TP exhibit a clear long-term correlation, with scaling exponents all exceeding 0.5. In the paddy field control area, precipitation and TP exhibit a positive correlation over long time scales. In contrast, in the dryland control area, a positive correlation is observed only over short time scales (< 60 days), while at longer time scales, the correlation turns negative and shows significant fluctuations. MFDCCA further reveals that the coupling relationship between TP and precipitation generally exhibits multifractal characteristics. The multifractal intensity is higher in the dryland-controlled fields (Δh = 1.47), indicating that TP is more sensitive to precipitation perturbations and less stable. The multifractal intensity is lower in the paddy fields (Δh = 1.20), indicating a more stable coupling relationship. Finally, by combining sliding window analysis with 3D convex hull volume calculations, the study quantitatively assesses the distribution differences of multifractal parameters. The results show that areas dominated by paddy fields exhibited stronger aggregation of multifractal parameters (convex hull volume difference of 0.69). In contrast, dryland areas showed more dispersed patterns (convex hull volume difference of –0.32). This study innovatively integrates fractal theory, remote sensing, and watershed observation data to establish a coupling analysis framework for identifying nonpoint source pollution. It elucidates the multiscale response patterns of phosphorus loss under different farmland types. It provides a new technical approach for quantitatively assessing the effects of farmland type on the spatial distribution and migration pathways of TP.

Introduction

Phosphate fertilizer is an essential nutrient for promoting high crop yields. However, global assessments indicate that phosphorus use efficiency (PUE) in agricultural systems typically ranges between 15% and 30%, with considerable spatial variability (Liu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). Excessive application can easily lead to phosphorus loss through runoff, leaching, and other pathways. Phosphorus loss not only diminishes soil fertility but also causes eutrophication of water bodies, leading to degradation of water quality, frequent algal blooms, disruption of aquatic ecosystems, and results in significant economic damages and health risks (Wang et al., 2014; Zhou et al., 2022). At present, phosphorus loss from agricultural land poses a significant challenge to controlling diffuse pollution and has gained global concern (Comber et al., 2023; Li et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022).

In farmland ecosystems, phosphorus loss primarily occurs through surface runoff and soil leaching. Among these, surface runoff is regarded as one of the key factors driving agricultural diffuse pollution and nutrient enrichment in aquatic ecosystems (Wang et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2023). As a primary driver of surface runoff generation, precipitation enhances soil erosion and increases runoff intensity, thereby promoting the transport and export of phosphorus from farmland to river systems (Hou et al., 2022). Especially during high-intensity or prolonged precipitation events, soil moisture increases substantially, leading to increased surface runoff that carries more particulate and dissolved phosphorus (Jin et al., 2020). However, the transport of phosphorus does not exhibit a simple linear response to precipitation but rather shows significant nonlinear characteristics. For example, Li et al. (2021) pointed out that during a rainfall event with a daily rainfall of 33.6 mm and a maximum rainfall intensity of 32.5 mm/h, the total phosphorus (TP) loss was 1.30 kg/ha. In another event with a daily rainfall of 46.0 mm but a maximum rainfall intensity of only 2.92 mm/h, the TP loss increased to 2.93 kg/ha. This nonlinear response mechanism is likely closely related to the soil’s retention and release behavior of phosphorus. In addition, soils of different types and pollution levels exhibit significant differences in their phosphorus retention and release behaviors, resulting in varying phosphorus output responses under similar precipitation conditions (Yang et al., 2024). For instance, Zhao et al. (2023) demonstrated via meta-analysis that phosphorus loss in dryland systems exhibits a greater response to precipitation variability than that in paddy fields across various fertilization conditions. McDowell et al. (2020) simulated and analyzed the time required for WEP (an indicator reflecting phosphorus loss in surface runoff) in phosphorus-enriched soils to decrease to the environmental safety threshold (≤ 0.02 mg/L). The results indicate that the time required for contaminated soil to achieve this goal is generally 36–55 years. For volcanic rock soils with more severe phosphorus accumulation, this process may take over 100 years. In summary, the natural retention of phosphorus by soil and the instability of precipitation itself make the migration and release of phosphorus driven by precipitation show significant time-scale dependence and long-term residual characteristics. This feature is consistent with Stark and Stieglitz’s (2000) description that the apparent migration time of inert solutes exhibits a broad-tailed distribution. The migration process of phosphorus in soil also shows a broad-tailed distribution behavior with multiscale control, multi-stage retention, and slow release. This means that the phosphorus loss caused by the current precipitation is not only directly affected by the precipitation event at that time, but is also deeply affected by historical precipitation, soil accumulation status, and long-term hydrological processes, forming an output characteristic of the precipitation input signal after soil “fractal filtering.” In summary, the phosphorus loss process in farmland is a doubly complex event driven by the randomness of external precipitation and the internal fractal retention mechanism of the soil, exhibiting strong time-scale dependence and long-term nonlinear memory effects.

Although the above fractal characteristics are commonly present across different farmland types, factors such as soil physical properties, hydrological conditions, and land management practices lead to significant differences in the specific response mechanisms of this process among various farmlands. Taking paddy fields as an example, ridges and water layers form a substantial hydrological “buffer reservoir” (Atere et al., 2018; Wei et al., 2019), which enhances the soil’s ability to retain phosphorus and slows down the migration process of phosphorus. Liu et al. (2021) demonstrated that enhancing the maximum water retention of paddy fields can effectively reduce surface runoff and phosphorus loss, achieving a reduction of more than 27.18%. Even if heavy precipitation causes surface runoff, the deposition and re-release of phosphorus in the underlying soil are controlled by multiple time scales, resulting in a slower rate of its removal by runoff. The phosphorus migration process exhibits a clear “broad tail” characteristic, which is consistent with the fractal model of pollutant migration proposed by Stark and Stieglitz (2000). In contrast, dryland soils have weaker retention capacity and lack the hydrological regulation features found in paddy fields. Once precipitation intensity exceeds the soil infiltration and storage thresholds, surface runoff can rapidly mobilize phosphorus in the shallow soil layer (Roberts & Johnston, 2015; Yahaya et al., 2023). It exhibits a faster and shorter time-scale transport process, with a relatively narrow “tail” of phosphorus migration, and the response is more pulsed. Therefore, although the retention and slow release of phosphorus in soil generally exhibit fractal characteristics, different types of farmland exhibit significantly different fractal features in response to precipitation due to differences in soil hydrological conditions. Specifically, phosphorus loss in paddy fields tends to show long-term and “delayed release” characteristics, whereas drylands display an event-driven pattern of “rapid response and short-term output.” However, the time-scale characteristics of phosphorus loss dynamics driven by precipitation have not been sufficiently studied. Thus, to better explore the spatiotemporal scale features and complex response mechanisms of phosphorus loss processes under different farmland types, it is necessary to introduce appropriate mathematical tools to quantitatively characterize the multiscale nonlinear relationships between precipitation and phosphorus loss.

Fractal methods have demonstrated outstanding performance in uncovering multiscale dependencies among complex environmental variables (Li et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2023; Shi et al., 2025). Among these, detrended cross-correlation analysis (DCCA) is an effective tool for assessing long-range correlations between two time series, and its derived DCCA coefficient can accurately estimate the true correlation between sequences without being affected by non-stationarity. Furthermore, multifractal methods can reveal the long-term memory and multiscale dependency structure of environmental variables by extracting deterministic information from complex fluctuations. For example, Shi et al. (2010) applied the multifractal detrended fluctuation analysis (MFDFA) method to comparatively study the time-scale characteristics of pH values at the inflow and outflow of Poyang Lake. The results indicated that the pH series at the lake’s inflow exhibited long-term memory and multifractal scaling properties, and that both monofractal and multifractal parameters could serve as dynamic indicators quantifying Poyang Lake’s acid-neutralizing capacity. In another study, Adarsh et al. (2020) evaluated the runoff-sediment relationships in five watersheds using multifractal detrended cross-correlation analysis (MFDCCA). The findings showed that the cross-correlation strength between runoff and total suspended solids (TSS) was generally higher on an annual scale than on a seasonal scale. Moreover, at longer time scales, the MFDCCA method was able to distinguish differences in the correlation strength between runoff and TSS across different watersheds. The above research indicates that multifractal methods have good applicability in characterizing nonlinear, multiscale, and long-term memory characteristics in water environment systems. However, the application of fractal methods to phosphorus loss remains limited. Phosphorus loss is affected by various elements, including precipitation and soil retention, resulting in a loss process that exhibits significant multiscale dependence and nonlinear response characteristics. Therefore, introducing DCCA and MFDCCA methods can provide scientific tools for investigating the response mechanisms of phosphorus loss to precipitation across different farmland types.

Therefore, to exclude other interfering factors, this study selects the Dahong River and Yulin River as research objects, both of which have similar meteorological conditions, pollution patterns, and levels of human activity, but exhibit significant differences in upstream and downstream farmland type distribution. Using meteorological and water quality data from the two rivers for 2021–2022. Firstly, the long-term cross-correlation relationship between precipitation and TP concentration sequence changes in two watersheds is quantitatively analyzed using DCCA cross-correlation numbers. Secondly, the MFDCCA method is used to deeply explore the multi-fractal characteristics between precipitation and TP concentration in the two basins at multiple time scales. Finally, the sliding window approach is applied to show the evolution characteristics of the correlation between precipitation and TP in the basin at different time scales under the control of different agricultural types. The convex hull volume is used to quantitatively characterize the aggregation degree of precipitation and TP multifractal parameters under different farmland control. This study will provide new insights for quantitatively identifying differences in phosphorus loss processes influenced by different farmland types, and offer scientific support for water pollution control and phosphorus loss management in small watersheds.

Materials and methods

Study area

Yulin River originates from Qinggangzhi, Zhoujia Town, Dazhu County, Dazhou City, Sichuan Province. It is 226km long with a watershed area of 3896 km2. The river is located between the Huaying Mountain, Tongluo Mountain, and Mingyue Mountain on the eastern edge of the Sichuan Basin, spanning from 106° 46′ to 107° 26′ E and from 29° 40′ to 30° 43′ N in geographic coordinates. It flows through Dazhou and Guang’an in Sichuan Province, as well as Chongqing Municipality. The Dahong River serves as a major left-bank branch of the Yulin River, stretching 152 km and draining a watershed covering 1427 km2. The two rivers are geographically adjacent and belong to a typical small watershed system. Water quality monitoring stations have been established at both the upstream and downstream sections of the two rivers. The upstream and downstream stations of the Yulin River are Shuanghekou Bridge and Yaotan. The upstream and downstream stations of the Dahong River are Gangjia Bridge and Cuijiayan Village in Lijia Township. The two rivers share similarities in the following aspects. First, the climatic conditions are similar. The Dahong River and Yulin River basins have average annual temperatures of 18.23 °C and 18.24 °C, respectively, and average annual precipitation of 1208 mm and 1205 mm, respectively. The differences are minimal, indicating comparable climate conditions. Second, the proportion of residential areas and population distribution is close. The proportions of upstream and downstream residential areas for the Dahong River are 1.37% and 4.17%, respectively, while for the Yulin River, they are 1.66% and 4.72%, respectively (see Table 1). The populations of the upstream and downstream reaches of the Dahong River are 0.7 and 1.45 million, respectively, while the populations of the Yulin River are 0.71 and 1.48 million. The overall distribution characteristics are the same. Third, infrastructure configuration is consistent. Both basins have sewage treatment plants located upstream and downstream, with similar treatment capacities and operational modes. Fourth, industrial disturbance factors are similar. Both basins lack major industrial facilities, with primary pollution arising from household wastewater and diffuse agricultural sources. Fifth, the distribution of farmland upstream and downstream is also quite close. The proportion of farmland upstream and downstream of the Dahong River is 69.5% and 70%, respectively, while for the Yulin River, it is 63% and 61%. However, despite these similarities in natural and anthropogenic conditions, the TP concentrations upstream and downstream of the two rivers show significant differences. Data sources

This study utilizes water quality data obtained from the Sichuan Province Ecological Environment Monitoring Station. The sampling period for this study is from January 1, 2021, to December 31, 2022. In the water quality data, dissolved oxygen (DO) data is hourly, while TP and COD data are 4-hourly. Due to instrument maintenance and equipment failures, some data were missing at various monitoring sites. Missing values are filled by averaging the concentrations before and after the missing time points. The proportion of missing data does not exceed 5%. To synchronize the analysis with rainfall, the hourly or 4-hourly water quality data are averaged to obtain daily average water quality data.

Due to the inability of traditional precipitation monitoring stations to provide accurate data for every region. Therefore, to more accurately capture the impact mechanism of precipitation on total phosphorus in rivers. This study uses the CHM_PRE daily precipitation dataset released by the National Tibetan Plateau Data Center (TPDC) (Miao, 2023) , with a spatial resolution of 0.1°. The dataset contains daily precipitation records from 2839 meteorological stations in China and surrounding areas from 1961 to 2022. Compared to the traditional method of constructing precipitation background and ratio fields, CHM_PRE incorporates monthly precipitation constraints and terrain corrections, and its accuracy has been validated using interpolated daily precipitation data from approximately 40,000 high-density stations during 2015–2019 (Gou et al., 2021; Han et al., 2023; Miao et al., 2022). This dataset has been widely applied in hydrological process modeling and climate change studies (Tang et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2025). In this study, ArcGIS software is used to mask the precipitation dataset according to the study areas, extracting the daily precipitation raster data for the Yulin River and Dahong River basins. The precipitation raster dataset was clipped using watershed boundaries in ArcGIS. All grid cells intersecting each watershed were extracted. Basin-scale precipitation was calculated using an area-weighted average method rather than simple summation to ensure physical consistency and avoid artificial amplification of rainfall magnitude. And the corresponding precipitation time series are constructed. The processing method of temperature data is the same as above.

The land cover type data are sourced from the National Glacier and Frozen Soil Desert Science Data Center. Its spatial resolution is 30 m. The distribution of different types of vegetation in the Yulin River and Dahong River basins can be obtained by ArcGIS.

The above results indicate that there are significant differences in TP concentrations between the upstream and downstream areas of the Dahong River and Yulin River basins. However, since the two basins have similar meteorological conditions and major anthropogenic pollution sources (such as domestic sewage and industrial wastewater), these factors alone cannot adequately explain the spatial heterogeneity of TP. Instead, differences in farmland types, especially the proportion and spatial distribution of paddy fields versus dryland, may be the key factors driving the spatial variation in water quality. Furthermore, from the monthly variation characteristics of TP, the downstream TP concentration trend in the Dahong River aligns closely with the seasonality of precipitation, suggesting that TP concentrations in this region are significantly influenced by precipitation-driven runoff. The TP concentration peaks observed in February in the upstream Dahong River and downstream Yulin River are likely closely related to spring agricultural activities such as crop sowing and fertilization. After February, TP concentrations decrease and reach their lowest levels in August and winter. The frequent heavy precipitation in August may have a cleansing effect on the rivers, while reduced fertilization in winter leads to more stable river water quality.

The explanation for the variation trend of the precipitation and TP \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho }_{DCCA}$$\end{document}ρDCCA with time scale is as follows: The downstream region of the Dahong River shows a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho }_{DCCA}$$\end{document}ρDCCA trend significantly different from its upstream area and from both upstream and downstream regions of the Yulin River, which may be related to land use types. This area has a high proportion of paddy fields (28.8%, see Table 1). During precipitation events, at small scales (s < 1 day), the bund structures of the paddy fields function to retain water, causing rainwater to be temporarily held within the fields and suppressing phosphorus loss. This results in a negative correlation between precipitation and TP concentration. However, the retained water creates an anaerobic environment in the paddy fields, accelerating the release of phosphorus from the soil, causing TP concentration to gradually increase. As precipitation continues and exceeds the bund’s water retention capacity, the excess water carries high concentrations of phosphorus out of the fields, leading to a rapid increase in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho }_{DCCA}$$\end{document}ρDCCA after s > 1 day and a shift to a significant positive correlation. In contrast, the upstream area of the Dahong River and both upstream and downstream regions of the Yulin River have a smaller proportion of paddy fields and are mainly dominated by dryland, lacking the water retention and buffering function of paddy fields. When precipitation occurs, surface runoff forms rapidly, carrying phosphorus loss from the topsoil. However, this runoff is still intercepted by vegetation. Therefore, at small scales (s < 60 days), a weak positive correlation is observed. As precipitation continues to increase, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho }_{DCCA}$$\end{document}ρDCCA shows a slight upward trend. When the time scale further expands beyond 1 year (s > 365 days), vegetation enters a new growth phase, and the soil vegetation system’s regulation of phosphorus changes, leading to a rebound in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho }_{DCCA}$$\end{document}ρDCCA.

Previous studies based on DCCA analysis revealed significant differences in the long-term cross-correlation between TP and precipitation under different farmland types, suggesting that their relationship may possess a more complex multiscale structure. Therefore, it is necessary to further analyze TP and precipitation using the MFDCCA method to characterize their spatiotemporal heterogeneity across different time scales.

To verify whether the differences in the precipitation and TP relationship between the two rivers under different farmland control types are indeed significant. This study further introduces multifractal theory for investigation. Based on this theory, the multifractal nature of a time series primarily arises from two factors: long-range correlations between large and small fluctuations, and the presence of a heavy-tailed probability distribution with pronounced peaks. By randomly shuffling the original sequence to eliminate its intrinsic temporal correlations, one can effectively assess the contribution of long-term persistence to the multifractality. Additionally, by applying the phase randomization method to remove the nonlinear distribution in the original series while preserving its correlation trend, it becomes possible to evaluate the contribution of the heavy-tailed probability distribution to the multifractal characteristics. To identify the sources of multifractality in the sequences, this study performed surrogate and phase-randomization processing on the original time series. The MFDCCA method is then applied to both the randomized and surrogate sequences to obtain the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q-h(q)$$\end{document}q-h(q) relationships (green lines for randomized sequences and red lines for surrogate sequences in Figs. 7c–d and 8c–d). The results show that the precipitation and TP series in both the upstream and downstream sections of the Yulin and Dahong Rivers exhibit the largest deviations in the randomized series. This indicates that the primary source of their multifractality arises from the long-term persistent dynamic mechanisms underlying the interactions between precipitation and TP.

Temporal variability of multifractal parameters

To further reveal the dynamic evolution of the correlation between precipitation and TP across different time scales. This study combines the sliding window method to analyze the temporal variability of multifractal parameters. The procedure is as follows: first, the precipitation and TP time series from January 1 to January 31, 2021 (days 1–30) are chosen as the initial window, and the first \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta h$$\end{document}Δh is computed using the MF-DCCA method. Then, the window moves forward by one day (days 2–31) to calculate the second \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta h$$\end{document}Δh, and this process continues similarly. In this process, the addition of each new data point results in a change in the multifractal parameters. By repeatedly applying this process to the entire data sequence, with the window size gradually increasing, a set of localized measurements is obtained. It can be seen that the multifractal features in the downstream paddy field area of the Dahong River are significantly more concentrated. This suggests that the water quality in downstream of the Dahong River is superior. The relationship between TP and precipitation is more stable and regular in this region, while there is significant fluctuation in the upstream arid areas. In contrast, the aggregation difference between upstream and downstream of the Yulin River (both dryland) is smaller, even showing a more dispersed feature downstream. The above results indicate that the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta V$$\end{document}ΔV calculated based on multifractal parameters can effectively quantify the impact of different farmland types on the structural complexity of the TP-precipitation relationship, holding potential value for identifying non-point source pollution and guiding land use management.

Environmental significance

To gain a deeper understanding of the significant differences in river TP response to precipitation under different farmland types. This study explores the impact mechanism of farmland structure on the pathway and intensity of phosphorus migration, starting from the process of “precipitation driven-farmland response-phosphorus migration.” This study finds that different types of farmland exhibit not only distinct characteristics of phosphorus loss under precipitation conditions but also significant spatiotemporal response differences, thereby exerting lasting and profound impacts on river water quality. Specifically, paddy fields are mostly located in low-lying areas and possess good water storage and regulation capacities (Shrivastava et al., 2018). During the early stages of precipitation, they can effectively retain surface runoff and adsorb part of the phosphorus, acting as a buffer (Liu et al., 2021). However, when precipitation intensifies or accumulates over time, the original water storage capacity gradually becomes saturated, and excess water begins to discharge, thereby increasing the risk of phosphorus being transported with the flow. The large influx of phosphorus into water bodies can lead to algal blooms, which in turn absorb phosphorus (Neal et al., 2006). Therefore, water quality in downstream areas controlled by rice paddies tends to be better than that upstream. In contrast, dryland areas are often distributed on steeper slopes with weaker vegetation cover, resulting in longer periods of exposed soil and rapid response to precipitation changes. They tend to generate surface runoff quickly in a short time, rapidly carrying phosphorus into rivers, making them major contributors to nonpoint source pollution. This difference between the “slow-release and lagged” response in paddy fields and the “rapid and immediate” response in drylands is a key controlling factor for phosphorus migration pathways and dynamic changes under different farmland types.

Based on this, the study innovatively combines fractal methods with high-resolution remote sensing data to establish a multisource data analysis framework. It can effectively identify the coupled mechanism of “precipitation driven-farmland response-phosphorus migration” in small watersheds. Compared with traditional single-source research approaches, this framework can more comprehensively capture the multiscale regulatory effects of different farmland types and buffering water bodies on the spatial distribution and migration pathways of TP under local precipitation conditions. This significantly improves the spatiotemporal resolution and dynamic identification capability of nonpoint source pollution processes. This method not only enriches the theoretical understanding of how farmland use structure dynamically influences phosphorus migration but also provides a new technical approach for precisely identifying phosphorus migration fluxes within the complex interplay of farmland structures and hydrological processes. Practically, the results help scientifically guide the optimization of different farmland layouts, strengthen ecological regulation functions, and enhance the interception and absorption efficiency of buffering water bodies against pollutants. This offers important scientific evidence and decision-making references for the construction of coupled mechanisms between sustainable agricultural development and efficient watershed water environment management.

The precipitation alone cannot fully represent hydrological processes such as flushing, dilution, and retention. Due to the absence of continuous discharge observations in both basins, river flow was not directly incorporated into the present analysis. Future research will integrate observed discharge data or hydrological simulations (e.g., SWAT-based modeling) to further strengthen the causal interpretation of rainfall-driven phosphorus migration.

Conclusions

This study focuses on two small watersheds in the Sichuan Basin (Yulin River and Dahong River) to systematically analyze the multiscale coupling relationship between daily precipitation and TP concentration from 2021 to 2022, as well as their response mechanisms to farmland structure. Although the two rivers exhibit highly similar meteorological conditions and pollution sources, TP concentrations show significant differences between the upstream and downstream areas. The DCCA analysis results indicate a significant power-law cross-correlation between precipitation and TP over long time scales, exhibiting long-term persistence. Specifically, the DCCA scaling exponents \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document}λ for the upstream and downstream of the Dahong River are 0.96 and 0.98, respectively, while those for the upstream and downstream of the Yulin River are 0.76 and 1.23, respectively. Additionally, in the Dahong River downstream, which is dominated by paddy fields, the DCCA cross-correlation coefficient (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho }_{DCCA}$$\end{document}ρDCCA) with TP gradually increases and stabilizes around 0.8 at medium to long scales (s > 60 days). In contrast, the upstream of Dahong River and both upstream and downstream of Yulin River, dominated by dryland farming, show positive correlation with TP only within a 2-month scale (s < 60 days), followed by negative correlation with larger fluctuations. This reflects the dryland system′s rapid response and unstable output to precipitation runoff, whereas paddy fields exhibit short-term and more stable output characteristics.

MFDCCA analysis further reveals the multifractal characteristics of the relationship between precipitation and TP. The results show that the convex hull volumes \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$V$$\end{document}V for the upstream and downstream of the Dahong River are 0.71 and 0.02, respectively, with a volume difference \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta V$$\end{document}ΔV of 0.69. This indicates that the downstream system of the Dahong River exhibits a more concentrated coupling relationship and a more stable response. In contrast, the volumes \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$V$$\end{document}V for the upstream and downstream of the Yulin River are 0.60 and 0.93, respectively, with a volume difference \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta V$$\end{document}ΔV of –0.33, reflecting a more dispersed and unstable TP and precipitation response downstream of the Yulin River.

In summary, this study develops an integrated analytical framework combining high-resolution remote sensing data, DCCA and MFDCCA analyses, and quantitative calculation of convex hull volumes to reveal the nonlinear coupling mechanism and multiscale dynamic characteristics among precipitation, farmland type, and phosphorus migration. The findings provide a scientific quantitative basis and technical support for the dynamic identification of nonpoint source phosphorus pollution in small watersheds, optimization of farmland use structure, and water environment management.

Weak Mixing Transformation Which Is Shannon Orbit Equivalent to a Given Ergodic Transformation
James O’Quinn

Abstract
. We prove that every ergodic transformation is Shannon orbit equivalent to a weak mixing transformation. The proof is based on the techniques introduced by Fieldsteel and Friedman to show that there is a mixing transformation for a given ergodic transformation T T which is, for all a ≥ 1 a\geq 1 , weak- a a -equivalent to T T and, for all b ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) b\in(0,1) , strong- b b -equivalent to T T . In particular, we will adapt the construction of Fieldsteel and Friedman by which they permute the columns of each Rokhlin tower in a sequence of rapidly growing Rokhlin towers so that the corresponding cocycles converge to an orbit equivalence cocycle of T T such that the resulting transformation and orbit equivalence have the desired properties. In addition to this, we will demonstrate a flexible method for obtaining actions of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} which are Shannon orbit equivalent to a given ergodic transformation.



1.
Introduction

Originally formulated by Kerr and Li in their 2021 paper
[
8
]
, Shannon orbit equivalence adds a restriction to the combinatorial complexity of the mappings that an orbit equivalence implements between orbits. In turn, this added condition means that this is the condition between two free probability measure preserving actions of (possibly different) finitely generated virtually Abelian groups that preserves entropy, as proved by Kerr and Li in
[
9
]
. So, while Shannon orbit equivalence does preserve entropy between transformations, we will show, using techniques from Fieldsteel and Friedman’s paper
[
6
]
, that Shannon orbit equivalence does not preserve weak mixing between integer transformations in a rather strong sense: we can always find a weak mixing transformation which is Shannon orbit equivalent to a given ergodic transformation. Before we introduce Shannon orbit equivalence, which we will do in Section 2, we will give some background definitions and results from the theory of orbit equivalence.

.

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)

a probability measure preserving action. Given an

-

. Suppose we have a second probability space

For the rest of this paper, our probability spaces will be atomless and Lebesgue.

Remark 1.2

We will use the following more convenient characterization of orbit equivalence since it will reduce some technical baggage. Two probability measure preserving actions
G\curvearrowright^{S}(X,\mu)

.

The next theorem, due to Dye

, implies that, for ergodic transformations, orbit equivalence erases their dynamical properties.

Theorem 1.3

If

In order to turn orbit equivalence into an interesting equivalence relation, we must place some restrictions upon it. The way this is normally accomplished is by requiring additional properties for the cocycles of the orbit equivalence, which we define below.

Definition 1.4

Given two orbit equivalent free p.m.p. actions

Remark 1.5
.

Note that, while the essential property of a cocycle is captured by the second condition, the first condition is useful to assume because we want all of our cocycles to be invertible given a fixed point from the probability space.

Remark 1.6

Given a cocycle

Perhaps the two most natural types of restricted orbit equivalence are bounded and integrable orbit equivalence, which we will now define.

Remark 1.9

Note that an orbit equivalence between transformations being bounded also implies that it is integrable.

The premier result related to integrable orbit equivalence is the following theorem from 1968 due to Belinskaya
[

Theorem 1.10

Two ergodic measure preserving transformations

One of the first lines of investigation to follow for restrictive orbit equivalences is to understand where they lie on the spectrum between unrestricted orbit equivalence, which erases the dynamics of ergodic actions, and conjugation. One way to accomplish this is to see if a given type of restricted orbit equivalence preserves dynamical properties which are known conjugation invariants. One such property is mixing. One of the primordial results in this direction is the following result due to Friedman and Ornstein
[
7
]
in the realm of Kakutani equivalence.

Theorem 1.11
.

If

such that

is kakutani equivalent to

.

The construction of the mixing transformation of Friedman and Ornstein highlights the critical technique that underpins the construction of the actions in the upcoming theorems: manipulating the columns of a sequence of Rokhlin towers to define the orbits of the resulting action.

In general, Kakutani equivalence does not imply orbit equivalence. However, del Junco and Rudolph in
[
4

noticed the following relationship between even Kakutani and restricted orbit equivalence showcased in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.12

Ergodic transformations

\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|v|\to\infty}}{{v\in I(x)}}}\frac{|\alpha(x,v)-v|}{|v|}=0.

Using the above theorem as a model, Fieldsteel and Friedman
[

defined the following type of restricted orbit equivalence for

Definition 1.13

Fieldsteel and Friedman also use the same framework for this construction to get a similar result for the bounded orbit equivalence case. This appears as Theorem 3 in their paper
[

Theorem 1.17
.

If
T
is an erogdic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action with

\alpha

which gives a mixing
T^{\alpha^{-1}}
such that the orbit equivalence between them is bounded.

The construction involved in this theorem, as well as the main theorem in the latter half of this paper, hinges on the fact that we can create orbit equivalence cocycles by manipulating a sequence of towers. Indeed, let
(F,B)
be a Rokhlin tower of the action
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\curvearrowright^{T}(X,\mu)
and let
\mathscr{A}
be a partition of
B
. For each
A\in\mathscr{A}
, let
\sigma_{A}:F\to F
be a bijection . We can define a cocycle
\alpha:X\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to Z^{d}
by setting

\displaystyle\alpha(x,g)=\begin{cases}\sigma_{A}(g+f)&\text{if }T^{g}x\in T^{F}B\text{ and }x=T^{f}b\text{ for some }f\in F\text{ and }b\in A\\
g&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

One key component to making this construction work is that a given sequence of cocycles begets another cocycle when a certain limit is taken. This is the cocycle for our desired action. This requires a condition for which the cocycle value for a given point in the space is fixed for a tail of the sequence. We also require certain types of 0-1 laws to guarantee that such a condition holds for almost every point in the space. The other key component is that Fieldsteel and Friedman get this sequence of cocycles by permuting columns of corresponding Rokhlin towers by independent random translations inside disjoint blocks, thereby introducing approximate independence at each stage of the construction which will produce mixing after the limit is taken.

With this context from restricted orbit equivalence theory in mind, we are ready to introduce Shannon orbit equivalence.

2.
Shannon Orbit Equivalence

While Theorem 1.17 tells us that mixing is not a bounded orbit equivalence invariant for ergodic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-actions when
d\geq 2
, entropy is an invariant of bounded orbit equivalence for these actions. Indeed, Austin shows in his 2016 paper
[
1
]
an even stronger result: entropy is an invariant for integrable orbit equivalence of actions of fintely generated amenable groups. So, to what extent can we generalize integrable orbit equivalence so that entropy is still an invariant for a sufficiently rich class of group actions? Perhaps the most natural generalization to look at is Shannon orbit equivalence. Shannon orbit equivalence was introduced by Kerr and Li in
[
8
]
as a generalization of both integrable orbit equivalence and bounded orbit equivalence. In that paper, the authors prove that entropy is an invariant for Shannon orbit equivalences between actions over certain classes of groups which exclude
\mathbb{Z}
. In a subsequent paper
[
9
]
, Kerr and Li extend the aforementioned result to actions of the integers.

So, the natural question is the following: does a version of Belinskaya’s theorem hold for Shannon orbit equivalences between ergodic actions over the integers? This was answered in the negative in a paper by Carderi, Joseph, Le Maître, and Tessera
[
3
]
. Indeed, the authors craft examples of ergodic transformations which satisfy a weaker type of integrable orbit equivalence, which also implies Shannon orbit equivalence, but are not flip conjugate. So, if Shannon orbit equivalence between ergodic transformations does not imply flip conjugacy, yet it still preserves entropy, what other properties does Shannon orbit equivalence preserve between ergodic transformations? Turning back to Fieldsteel and Friedman, can their method of constructing cocycles be adapted to the Shannon orbit equivalence setting to produce a mixing action which is equivalent to a given ergodic action, similar to Theorem 1.16? In this paper, we will show that a weaker version of the result of Fieldsteel and Friedman does hold in the Shannon orbit equivalence setting. Namely, we will show that for a given ergodic transformation, we can find a weak mixing transformation which is Shannon orbit equivalent to it. While there is inherent tension between the methods used to guarantee mixing by Fieldsteel and Friedman and the finite entropy bounds for the cocycle partitions required by Shannon orbit equivalence, we can finesse the introduction of asymptotic independence to produce weak mixing. In particular, while our method for obtaining Shannon orbit equivalence places a bound on the number of permutations of the orbits we introduce at each stage of the construction, mixing in the Fieldsteel-Friedman construction requires unbounded growth of the number of permutations we introduce at each stage. However, we will show that we can stagger our introduction of relative mixing phenomena at each stage of the construction to guarantee weak mixing while still controlling the number of permutations we introduce at each stage to guarantee Shannon orbit equivalence.

Before we show our weak mixing result, we will take a slight detour to introduce a method for constructing actions of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
which are Shannon orbit equivalent to a given ergodic action of
\mathbb{Z}
. The significance of this construction, which is adapted from the asymptotic tower constructions of Fieldsteel and Friedman, is that it allows for a great degree of flexibility in the choice of permutations applied to each column for each tower in the sequence of towers. Indeed, we replace the block translations of Fieldsteel and Friedman by more general column permutations called column-to-tile maps which encode columns of a tower of an integer action onto squares inside
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in a way that preserves the structure of the column permutations from the previous stage. In turn, a sequence of column-to-tile maps will produce an action of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
which is Shannon orbit equivalent to the original action, as long as the growth of the heights of the towers associated to each column-to-tile map is sufficiently controlled. We believe that, through careful choice of column-to-tile maps, we can produce actions of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, or perhaps actions of any discrete countable group which can be monotiled, that satisfy a particular mixing condition and are Shannon orbit equivalent to a given ergodic transformation.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
be a probability space and let
\mathscr{P}\subset\mathscr{A}
be a countable partition of
X
. The
Shannon entropy
of
P
is the sum
\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}}-\mu(P)\log\mu(P)
. Given a cocycle
\alpha:X\times G\to H
and a
g\in G
, there is a natural partition
\mathscr{P}_{\alpha,g}=\{P_{g,h}\mid h\in H\}
where
P_{g,h}=\{x\in X\mid\alpha(x,g)=h\}
. We call these the
cocycle partitions
of
\alpha
. We say an orbit equivalence between free p.m.p. actions
T
and
S
with cocycles
\kappa
and
\lambda
is
Shannon
if the cocycle partitions
\mathscr{P}_{\kappa,g}
and
\mathscr{P}_{\lambda,h}
have finite Shannon entropy for all
g\in G
and all
h\in H
.

The following proposition expresses the main criteria we will use to guarantee Shannon orbit equivalence.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G\curvearrowright^{S}(X,\mu)
and
H\curvearrowright^{T}(X,\mu)
be free probability measure preserving actions and
\alpha:X\times G\to H
a cocycle between them. Fix a
g\in G
. If the cocycle partition
\mathscr{P}_{\alpha,g}
has the property that for every
h\in H
there is a partition
\{D_{n}^{h}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
P_{g,h}
such that for every
n
there is a finite set
F_{n}\subset H
with
D_{n}^{h}=\varnothing
for
h\notin F_{n}
and we have that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}-\mu(\bigcup_{h\in F_{n}}D_{n}^{h})\log\frac{\mu(\bigcup_{h\in F_{n}}D_{n}^{h})}{|F_{n}|}<\infty
, then
\mathscr{P}_{\alpha,g}
has finite Shannon entropy.

Proof.

Assume we have a
g\in G
such that the the cocycle partition
\mathscr{P}_{\alpha,g}
has the property that for every
h\in H
there is a partition
\{D_{n}^{h}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
P_{g,h}
such that for every
n
there is a finite set
F_{n}\subset H
such that
D_{n}^{h}=\varnothing
for
h\notin F_{n}
and we have that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}-\mu(\cup_{h\in F_{n}}D_{n}^{h})\log\frac{\mu(\cup_{h\in F_{n}}D_{n}^{h})}{|F_{n}|}<\infty
.
Since
\{D_{n}^{h}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N},h\in H}
is a refinement of
\mathscr{P}_{\alpha,g}
and entropy increases under refinements, we get the following inequality:

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in H}-\mu(P_{g,h})\log\mu(P_{g,h})\leq\sum_{h\in H,n\in\mathbb{N}}-\mu(D^{h}_{n})\log\mu(D^{h}_{n}).

Note that the equipartition of a set maximizes entropy relative to any other partition of the same size. So, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in H,n\in\mathbb{N}}-\mu(D^{h}_{n})\log\mu(D^{h}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{h\in F_{n}}-\mu(D^{h}_{n})\log\mu(D^{h}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}-|F_{n}|\frac{\mu(\cup_{h\in F_{n}}D^{h}_{n})}{|F_{n}|}\log\frac{\mu(\cup_{h\in F_{n}}D^{h}_{n})}{|F_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}-\mu(\cup_{h\in F_{n}}D^{h}_{n})\log\frac{\mu(\cup_{h\in F_{n}}D^{h}_{n})}{|F_{n}|}

So, working backwards, we can see that
\sum_{h\in H}-\mu(P_{g,h})\log\mu(P_{g,h})<\infty
.
∎

Column Permutations of Towers

(X,\mu)

\mathbb{Z}^{2}
Induced by a Sequence of Column-to-Tile Maps

(X,\mu)
be a standard probability space and
\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright^{S}(X,\mu)

\mathbb{Z}^{2}\curvearrowright^{R}(X,\mu)

\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\epsilon_{n}<2^{-n}

\epsilon_{n}
-tower

|T_{n}|<2^{n^{3}}
for all

\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of squares centered at the origin in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
|F_{n}|=|T_{n}|
and
|(F_{n}+F_{n+1})\setminus F_{n+1}|<\epsilon_{n+1}|F_{n+1}|
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

n\in\mathbb{N}

\alpha_{n}
be a column-to-tile map of

to
F_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with associated partition

of

.

Now, assume our column-to-tile maps satisfy the following properties. For every

, assume there is a finite set

\displaystyle\ell_{n}(b)(m)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }S^{m}x\in B_{n-1}\\
0&\text{if }S^{m}x\notin B_{n-1}\end{cases}

for

.
Also, for every

and a given

, we have a distinct

for each

Proposition 4.1
.

If for some
x\in X
and
i,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
i<k
, we have that
x\in S^{T_{j}}B_{j}
, so that
x=S^{m_{j}}b_{j}
for some
m_{j}\in T_{j}
and
b_{j}\in B_{j}
, and there exists a
y\in B_{j}\cap S^{T_{j+1}}B_{j+1}
such that
x\in S^{T_{j}}y\subset S^{T_{j+1}}B_{j+1}
whenever
i\leq j\leq k
, then we can write
F_{k}
as a disjoint union of translates of
F_{i}
so that there is some
c\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
\alpha_{k}(m_{k},b_{k})=\alpha_{i}(m_{i},b_{i})+c
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
and
i,k\in\mathbb{N}
be given such that
x\in S^{T_{j}}B_{j}
, so that
x=S^{m_{j}}b_{j}
for some
m_{j}\in T_{j}
and
b_{j}\in B_{j}
, and there exists a
y_{j}\in B_{j}\cap S^{T_{j+1}}B_{j+1}
such that
x\in S^{T_{j}}\{y_{j}\}\subset S^{T_{j+1}}B_{j+1}
whenever
i\leq j\leq k
. So, for every
j\in\{i,\dots,k\}
there is a
n_{j+1}\in T_{j+1}
such that
b_{j}=S^{n_{j+1}}b_{j+1}
. Thus, for every
j\in\{i,\dots,k\}
there is a
c_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
so that, by construction, we have that

\displaystyle\alpha_{j+1}(m_{j+1},b_{j+1})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{j+1}(m_{j}+n_{j+1},b_{j+1})=\alpha_{j}(m_{j},S^{n_{j+1}}b_{j+1})+c_{j}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{j}(t_{j},b_{j})+c_{j}.

If we set

, we can see that recursively applying the above formula yields
\alpha_{k}(m_{k},b_{k})=\alpha_{i}(m_{i},b_{i})+c
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
s\in\{(1,0),(0,1)\}
, one of the generators of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. For a.e.
x\in X
, there is an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
n\geq N
, the following conditions hold:

(1)

x\in S^{T_{n}}B_{n}
,

(2)

there exists a
y\in B_{n}\cap S^{T_{n+1}}B_{n+1}
such that
x\in S^{T_{n}}\{y\}\subset S^{T_{n+1}}B_{n+1}
, and,

(3)

when
x
is expressed as
x=S^{m_{1}}b
where
m_{1}\in T_{n}
and
b\in B_{n}
, we have a
m_{2}\in T_{n}
such that
\alpha_{n}(m_{2},b)-\alpha_{n}(m_{1},b)=s
.

Proof.

Let
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be a given generator, and let
\mathscr{A}_{k}
be the partition given above for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
S_{k}
be the union of the top
|T_{k-1}|-1
and bottom
|T_{k-1}|-1
tower levels of
(T_{k},B_{k})
. Set

\displaystyle E_{k}=(X\setminus S^{T_{k}}B_{k})\cup(X\setminus S^{T_{k-1}}B_{k-1})\cup\{S^{t}b\in S^{T_{k}}B_{k}\mid s+\alpha_{k}(t,b)\notin F_{k}\}\cup S_{k}.

The proof follows if we can show that
\mu(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}E_{k})=0
. This can be accomplished by showing that
\sum\mu(E_{k})<\infty
and then applying the Borel-Cantelli Lemma. First, since
S^{T_{n}}B_{n}
is an
\epsilon_{n}
-tower for every

, we have that
\mu(X\setminus S^{T_{k}}B_{k})<\epsilon_{k}
. Similarly, we have
\mu(X\setminus S^{T_{k-1}}B_{k-1})<\epsilon_{k-1}
. Also, by construction, we have that
\mu(S_{k})<\frac{2|T_{k-1}|}{|T_{k}|}<2\epsilon_{k}
and
\mu(\{S^{t}b\in S^{T_{k}}B_{k}\mid s+\alpha_{k}(t,b)\notin F_{k}\})<\epsilon_{k}
. So, we have that
\mu(E_{k})<4\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{k-1}
so that
\sum\mu(E_{k})<\infty
since we chose
\{\epsilon_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to be summable.
∎

Let
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be a standard generator. For all

, we can define a sequence of partial maps

in the following way: given some

and

such that there is some

such that

\sigma_{s,n}(S^{t}b)=S^{t^{\prime}}b
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let

be a standard generator and

. There is an

such that, for all

\sigma_{s,n_{1}}(x)=\sigma_{s,n_{2}}(x)

Let a standard generator

and

be given, and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be given so that the property from Proposition 4.2 holds. Let

be given. So, we have
t_{1}\in T_{n_{1}}
,
b_{1}\in B_{n_{1}}
,
t_{2}\in T_{n_{2}}
,and

. We must show that

. Since

such that

(1)

\alpha_{n_{2}}(t_{3},b_{2})=\alpha_{n_{1}}(t_{1}^{\prime},b_{1})+c

and

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha_{n_{2}}(t_{2}^{\prime},b_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n_{1}}(t_{1}^{\prime},b_{1})+\alpha_{n_{2}}(t_{2},b_{2})-\alpha_{n_{1}}(t_{1},b_{1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2)}}{{=}}\alpha_{n_{1}}(t_{1}^{\prime},b_{1})+c

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{=}}\alpha_{n_{2}}(t_{3},b_{2}).

Since

is a bijection, we have that

.
∎

. Thus, we can find an

Proposition 4.4
.

The action of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\curvearrowright^{R}X
defined above is orbit equivalent to
\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright^{S}X
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
be given such that there is an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
m,n>N
we have that
\sigma_{s,m}(x)=\sigma_{s,n}(x)
for any standard generator

. So, for all

, there is a
t_{m}\in T_{m}
, a
t_{n}\in T_{n}
, a
b_{m}\in B_{m}
and a
b_{n}\in B_{n}
such that

and

such that

. So,

and

. This means that for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have that there is a
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that

. Now, suppose for some
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that, for all
m,n>N
, there is a
t_{m}\in T_{m}
, a
t_{n}\in T_{n}
, a
b_{m}\in B_{m}
and a

such that

.

Similarly, for a standard generator

, we can find an

where, for every

we have a

so that, whenever

\alpha_{n}(t^{\prime},b)-\alpha_{n}(t,b)=s
. Thus,

. The fact that we bijectively map onto the orbit of

follows similarly to the first case.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
be given. Let
D^{k}_{n}
be the set of points whose
\lambda
cocycle value for
k
is fixed at stage
n
. Then, for
n\geq 2
, we have that

\displaystyle\mu(D_{n}^{k})<\epsilon_{n-1}+3\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i}+k\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|T_{i-1}|}

Proof.

We only need to show that the following bounds for
\mu(D_{n}^{k})
for all
n\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\mu(S^{T_{n}}B_{n})-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mu(D_{i}^{k})-3\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i+1}-k\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|T_{i}|}\leq\mu(D_{n}^{k})\leq\mu(S^{T_{n}}B_{n})-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mu(D_{i}^{k})

Indeed, assuming these hold we have

\displaystyle\mu(D_{n}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\mu(S^{T_{n}}B_{n})-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mu(D_{i}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\mu(S^{T_{n}}B_{n})-\bigg(\mu(S^{T_{n-1}}B_{n-1})-\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\mu(D_{i}^{k})-3\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i+1}-k\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|T_{i}|}\bigg)-\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\mu(D_{i}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\mu(S^{T_{n}}B_{n})-\mu(S^{T_{n-1}}B_{n-1})+3\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i+1}+k\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|T_{i}|}

\displaystyle<1-(1-\epsilon_{n-1})+3\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i+1}+k\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|T_{i}|}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{n-1}+3\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i}+k\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|T_{i-1}|}.

First, the upper bound simply follows from the fact that
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}D^{k}_{i}\subset S^{T_{n}}B_{n}
.
For the lower bound, note that a point
x\in S^{T_{n}}B_{n}
, which we represent as
x=S^{t}b
where
t\in T_{n}
and
b\in B_{n}
, is an element of
D_{n}^{k}
if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

x\in S^{T_{i}}B_{i}
for every
i\geq n

(2)

t\in T_{i}\cap(-k+T_{i})
for every
i\geq n

(3)

x
is not in the top or bottom
|T_{i}|
levels of the tower
(T_{i+1},B_{i+1})
for every
i\geq n
.

(4)

x\notin\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}D^{k}_{i}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus, for
n>1
,

\displaystyle\mu(D_{n}^{k})

\displaystyle>\mu(S^{T_{n}}B_{n})-\sum_{i-1}^{n-1}\mu(D^{k}_{i})-\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\mu(X\setminus S^{T_{i+1}}B_{i+1})

\displaystyle\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }-\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}k\frac{\mu(S^{T_{n}}B_{n})}{|T_{n}|}-\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{2|T_{n}|\mu(S^{T_{n+1}}B_{n+1})}{|T_{n+1}|}

\displaystyle>\mu(S^{T_{n}}B_{n})-\sum_{i-1}^{n-1}\mu(D^{k}_{i})-\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\epsilon_{n+1}-k\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|T_{n}|}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2\epsilon_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\mu(S^{T_{n}}B_{n})-\sum_{i-1}^{n-1}\mu(D^{k}_{i})-3\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\epsilon_{n+1}-k\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|T_{n}|}.

∎

Proposition 4.7
.

The orbit equivalence between
\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright X
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\curvearrowright X
is Shannon.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
be given. First, we will show that the cocycle partition of
\lambda:X\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}^{2}
for

has finite Shannon entropy. For a given

, let
P_{g}=\{x\in X\mid\lambda(x,k)=g\}
and let
\mathscr{P}=\{P_{g}\mid g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, set
D^{k,g}_{n}=D^{k}_{n}\cap P_{g}
where
D^{k}_{n}
is the set of points whose
\lambda
cocycle value for
k
is fixed at stage
n
. So, for every
g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
\{D^{k,g}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a partition of
P_{g}
. Recall that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
F_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is the set that corresponds to the column-to-tile map
\alpha_{n}
and
let
\bar{F}_{n}=F_{n}-F_{n}
. Note that
D^{k,g}_{n}=\varnothing
if
g\notin\bar{F}_{n}
. So, in order to apply Proposition 2.2, the only property left for us to show is that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(D_{n}^{k})\log\frac{\mu(D_{n}^{k})}{|\bar{F}_{n}|}<\infty
since
D_{n}^{k}=\cup_{g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}D_{n}^{k,g}
.

By Lemma 4.5, we have that
\mu(D_{n}^{k})<\epsilon_{n-1}+3\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i}+k\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|T_{i-1}|}
. By choosing
\epsilon_{n}<2^{-n}
in our construction, which also implies that
2^{i}<|T_{i}|
for all
i>1
, we have that
\mu(D_{n}^{k})<2^{-(n-1)}+3(2^{-n+1})+k(2^{-n+2})
for all
n
. Thus, there is an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>N
, we have
\mu(D_{n}^{k})<2^{-n/2}
. By construction, we have
|F_{n}|<2^{n^{2}}
for
n>N
and
|\bar{F}_{n}|=4|F_{n}|
. So, this implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{n>N}-\mu(D^{k}_{n})\log\frac{\mu(D^{k}_{n})}{|\bar{F}_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>N}-2^{-n/2}\log\frac{2^{-n/2}}{|\bar{F}_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>N}-2^{-n/2}\log\frac{2^{-n/2}}{4|F_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>N}-2^{-n/2}\log 2^{-n^{2}-n-2}

\displaystyle<\infty

So, we can apply Proposition 2.2 to conclude that
\mathscr{P}
has finite Shannon entropy.

Now, we will show that the Shannon entropy of each partition associated to
\kappa:X\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{Z}
is finite. Fix
g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Let
P_{k}=\{x\in X\mid\kappa(x,g)=k\}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, set
E^{g,k}_{n}=E^{g}_{n}\cap P_{k}
. So, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have that
\{E^{g,k}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a partition of
P_{k}
.
Just like the
\lambda
cocycle case, we have that
E^{g,k}_{n}=\varnothing
if
k\notin\bar{T}_{n}
where
\bar{T}_{n}=T_{n}-T_{n}
.
Lemma 4.6 gives us that

\displaystyle\mu(\bigcup_{k\in\bar{T}_{n}}E_{n}^{g,k})=\mu(E_{n}^{g})<\epsilon_{n-1}+3\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i}+\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{i-1}\setminus(g+F_{i-1})|}{|F_{i-1}|}.

Note that we can find an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>N
we have that
\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{i-1}\setminus(g+F_{i-1})|}{|F_{i-1}|}<2^{-n/4}
since
2^{i-1}<|F_{i-1}|
and
\frac{|F_{i-1}\setminus(g+F_{i-1})|}{|F_{i-1}|}
is approximately
\frac{1}{\sqrt{|F_{i-1}|}}
. Thus, there is an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
n>N
,
\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{|F_{i-1}\setminus(g+F_{i-1})|}{|F_{i-1}|}<2^{-n/4}
and
\epsilon_{n}<\frac{1}{2^{n}}
so that we have
\mu(E^{g}_{n})<2^{-n/8}
for all
n>N
.
By taking
|T_{n}|<2^{n^{2}}
for
n>N
and noting that
|\bar{T}_{n}|=2|T_{n}|
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{n>N}-|\bar{T}_{n}|\frac{\mu(E^{g}_{n})}{|\bar{T}_{n}|}\log\frac{\mu(E^{g}_{n})}{|\bar{T}_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>N}-2^{-n/8}\log\frac{2^{-n/8}}{|\bar{T}_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>N}-2^{-n/8}\log\frac{2^{-n/8}}{2|T_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>N}-2^{-n/8}\log 2^{-n^{2}-\frac{n}{8}-1}

\displaystyle<\infty

Since we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{n>N}-\mu(\bigcup_{k\in\bar{T}_{n}}E^{g,k}_{n})\log\frac{\mu(\bigcup_{k\in\bar{T}_{n}}E^{g,k}_{n})}{|\bar{T}_{n}|}=\sum_{n>N}-|\bar{T}_{n}|\frac{\mu(E^{g}_{n})}{|\bar{T}_{n}|}\log\frac{\mu(E^{g}_{n})}{|\bar{T}_{n}|},

we can apply Proposition 2.2 to conclude that the Shannon entropy of each partition associated to
\kappa
is finite.
∎

5.
Summary of the Construction from Fieldsteel and Friedman

Now, we will summarize the construction involved in Fieldsteel-Friedman. Later, we will show how this construction can be modified to produce a theorem similar to Theorem 1.16 for Shannon orbit equivalence.

Remark 5.1
.

If
L\in\mathbb{N}
, we will use the following notation to represent a
d
-cube in
\mathbb{N}^{d}

\displaystyle C_{L}=\{v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\mid 1\leq v_{i}\leq L,i=1,2,\dots,d\}

and the following notation to represent the
d
-cube centered at the origin of side length
2L+1
in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{L}=\{v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\mid|v_{i}|\leq L,i=1,2,\dots,d\}

Definition 5.2
.

Let
J,K,L\in N
. We say a permutation
\pi
of
C_{L}
is a
(J,K,L)
-
permutation
if there exists a
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
v\in\bar{C}_{K}
such that for all
w\in C_{j}+u
, we have that
\pi(w)=w+v
. The vector
v
is called the
translation vector
of
\pi
and will be denoted by
tv(\pi)
. We also call a permutation
\pi
of
C_{L}+w
for some
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
a
(J,K,L)
-
permutation
if it is a
(J,K,L)
-permutation of
C_{L}
up to translation by
w
. Moreover, a bijection
\pi
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is called a
(J,K,L)
-
blocked bijection
if there is a collection
\{C_{L}+w_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of disjoint translates of
C_{L}
for which
\pi
acts on each
C_{L}+w_{i}
by a
(J,K,L)
-permutation and acts as the identity on the rest of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. If
C_{J}+u_{i}+w_{i}
is the subset of
C_{L}+w_{i}
on which
\pi
acts by translation, then we call the sets
C_{L}+w_{i}
the
blocks
of
\pi
, the sets
C_{J}+u_{i}+w_{i}
the
rigid blocks
of
\pi
, and the sets
C_{L}+w_{i}\setminus C_{J}+u_{i}+w_{i}

filler sets
of
\pi
.

Now, we say a cocycle
\alpha
for a probability measure preserving
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
- action
S
of the probability space
(X,\mu)
is a
(J,K,L)
-
blocked cocycle
if, for almost every
x\in X
, there is a
(J,K,L)
-blocked bijection
\pi_{x}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
\alpha(x,\cdot)=\pi_{x}(\cdot)-\pi_{x}(0)
, and the blocks, rigid blocks, and the permutations on them are measurably selected. If
x\in X
and
C_{L}+w_{i}
is a block of
\pi_{x}
, we call the set
S^{C_{L}+w_{i}}(x)
a
block
of
\alpha
. We make a similar definition for rigid blocks and filler sets. In addition to being a a
(J,K,L)
-blocked cocycle, we say
\alpha
is a a
(J,K,L,\epsilon)
-
blocked cocycle
of
S
if the measure of points in
X
which are not in a block of
\alpha
is less than
\epsilon
.

Remark 5.3
.

Given a sequence of cocycles
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of an action
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
, we say that
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a cocycle
\alpha
if, for almost every
x\in X
and every
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have that
\lim_{i\to\infty}\alpha_{i}(v,x)=\alpha(v,x)
.

Lemma 1 in Fieldsteel-Friedman shows that, given a summable sequence
\{\epsilon_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
(0,1)
and an arbitrary sequence
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers, then, given a sequence of positive integers
\{J_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
which grow at a sufficiently fast rate and a sequence of positive integers
\{L_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that each
L_{i}
is sufficiently close to
J_{i}
, we can find a sequence of cocycles
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of an action
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\curvearrowright^{T}(X,\mu)
which converges to a cocycle
\alpha
such that the corresponding orbit equivalence between
T
and
T^{\alpha^{-1}}
is, for all
a\geq 1
, a weak-
a
-equivalence and, for all
b\in(0,1)
, a strong-
b
-equivalence.

To get mixing, Fieldsteel and Friedman construct a sequence of blocked cocycles by choosing parameters so that the ergodic theorem and the Rokhlin lemma guarantee a relative mixing condition for each cocycle. In turn, this gives mixing once we pass to the limit, guaranteed by Lemma 1, of our sequence of blocked cocycles.

6.
Fieldsteel-Friedman Orbit Equivalence Is Shannon

For the rest of this section, let
\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright^{S}(X,\mu)
be an ergodic probability measure preserving action. Also, let
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{L_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be sequences of positive integers, and let
\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence such that
\epsilon_{n}<2^{-n}
for every
n\in N
. Assume we have a sequence of cocycles
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
S
taking values in
\mathbb{Z}
such that
\beta_{n}=\alpha_{n}\circ\alpha_{n-1}^{-1}
is a
(K_{n}-L_{n},K_{n},L_{n},\epsilon_{n})
-blocked cocycle. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
S_{n}=S^{\alpha_{n}^{-1}}
and
B_{n}=2(\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j})
. Also, assume that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
\frac{K_{n}}{L_{n}}<\epsilon_{n}
,
\frac{n+B_{n}}{L_{n+1}-K_{n+1}}<\epsilon_{n+1}
, and, for large enough
n
,
K_{n}<2^{n^{3}}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

The sequence of cocycles
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a cocycle
\alpha
given, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and almost every
x\in X
, by the formula
\lim_{i\to\infty}\alpha_{i}(x,n)=\alpha(x,n)
.

The proof of this proposition is an adaption of the proof of Lemma 1 from Fieldsteel and Friedman
[
6
]
.

Proof.

First, set

\displaystyle F_{i}=\{x\mid S_{i}^{\bar{C}_{i+B_{i}}}(x)\text{ is contained in a rigid block of }\beta_{i+1}\}

We will first show that
\mu(\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}\cap_{j=i}^{\infty}F_{j})=1
, which we can accomplish by showing that
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu(X\setminus F_{j})<\infty
and applying Borel-Cantelli. To show this, note that
x\notin F_{j}
in three possible cases:

(1)

x
is not in a block of
\beta_{j+1}
,

(2)

x
is in a block of
\beta_{j+1}
, but not a rigid block, or

(3)

x
is in a rigid block of
\beta_{j+1}
, but not all of
S_{j}^{\bar{C}_{j+B_{j}}}(x)
.

Note that the measure of the set of points that satisfy case 1 is
<\epsilon_{j+1}
, that satisfy case 2 is
\frac{K_{j+1}}{L_{j+1}}<\epsilon_{j+1}
, and that satisfy case 3 is
\frac{2(j+B_{j})}{L_{j+1}-K_{j+1}}<2\epsilon_{j+1}
. Thus,
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu(X\setminus F_{j})<\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}4\epsilon_{j+1}<\infty
since for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
\epsilon_{j}<2^{-j}
. Let
x\in\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}\cap_{j=i}^{\infty}F_{j}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. So, we can find an
i
so that
|n|<i
and
x\in\cap_{j=i}^{\infty}F_{j}
. For all
j\geq i
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}(x,n)=\alpha_{i}(x,n)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\alpha_{k}(x,n)=\alpha(x,n).

Hence,
\alpha
is a well defined function.

We also have that
\alpha
satisfies the cocycle identity since, given almost any
x\in X
and given
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we can find an
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha(x,n+m)=\alpha_{i}(x,n+m)
,
\alpha(x,m)=\alpha_{i}(x,m)
, and
\alpha(S^{m}x,n)=\alpha_{i}(S^{m}x,n)
so that

\displaystyle\alpha(x,n+m)=\alpha_{i}(x,n+m)=\alpha_{i}(x,m)+\alpha_{i}(S^{m}x,n)=\alpha(x,m)+\alpha(S^{m}x,n).

A very similar argument shows that for almost every
x\in X
, we have that
\alpha(x,\cdot)
is injective. Now, we just need to show that, for almost every
x\in X
, the function
\alpha(x,\cdot)
is surjective. Let
x\in\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}\cap_{j=i}^{\infty}F_{j}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, we can find an
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x\in\cap_{j=i}^{\infty}F_{j}
. This implies that we can find a
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
j+B_{j}>|n|
. Since
\alpha_{j}(x,\cdot)
is bijective, we can find an
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\alpha_{j}(x,m)=n
. Hence, we have that
\alpha(x,m)=n
since
\alpha_{j}(x,m)=\alpha_{k}(x,m)
for every
k\geq j
.
∎

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\alpha
be a cocycle which is a limit of the cocycles
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and fix a

. We say a point

has its
\alpha

k

fixed at stage

if

is the smallest natural number such that
\alpha_{N}(x,k)=\alpha(x,k)
. Recall that this implies that, for every

,

. We will let

denote the set of all points whose cocycle value for
k
is fixed at stage

.

Lemma 6.3
.

For the cocycle
\alpha
for
S
given above, we have for a given

and

that

\displaystyle\mu(D^{k}_{n})<2\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i}+\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{|k|+B_{n-2}}{L_{i}}

Proof.

To prove this lemma, it is sufficient to show that the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mu(D^{k}_{i})-\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\mu(X\setminus S_{i-1}^{C_{M_{i}}}B_{i})-\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{K_{i}}{L_{i}}-\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\frac{|k|+B_{n-1}}{L_{i}}\leq\mu(D^{k}_{n})\leq 1-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mu(D^{k}_{i}).

Indeed, assuming the above inequality holds, we have that

\displaystyle\mu(D^{k}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq 1-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mu(D^{k}_{i})

\displaystyle\leq 1-\bigg(1-\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\mu(D^{k}_{i})-\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\mu(X\setminus S_{i-1}^{C_{M_{i}}}B_{i})-\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{K_{i}}{L_{i}}-\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{|k|+B_{n-2}}{L_{i}-K_{i}}\bigg)-\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\mu(D^{k}_{i})

(3)

Now, we wish to show that we can make similar estimates for

Proposition 6.7
.

The orbit equivalence between
S
and
S^{\alpha^{-1}}
given by the cocycle
\alpha
is Shannon.

Proof.

First, we will show that the Shannon partitions of the cocycle
\alpha
have finite Shannon entropy.
Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
be given. For a given
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
P_{\ell}=\{x\in X\mid\alpha(x,k)=\ell\}
and let
\mathscr{P}=\{P_{\ell}\mid\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, set
D^{k,\ell}_{n}=D^{k}_{n}\cap P_{\ell}
where

is the set of points whose
\alpha
cocycle value for
k
is fixed at stage
n
. Now, note that for every
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
we have that
\{D^{k,\ell}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a partition of
P_{\ell}
.
Setting
F_{n}=\{\alpha_{n}(x,k)\mid x\in X\}
, we can see that
D^{k,\ell}_{n}=\varnothing
if
\ell\notin F_{n}
. Now, by Proposition 2.2, the Shannon entropy of

\sum-\mu(D_{n}^{k})\log\frac{\mu(D_{n}^{k})}{|F_{n}|}<\infty

By Lemma 6.3, we have that

\displaystyle\mu(D^{k}_{n})<2\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i}+\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{|k|+B_{n-2}}{L_{i}-K_{i}}<3\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i}

for large enough

. By our choice of
\epsilon_{n}<2^{-n}
in our construction, there is an

, we have that
\mu(D_{n}^{k})<2^{-n+4}

. So, this implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{n>N}-\mu(D^{k}_{n})\log\frac{\mu(D^{k}_{n})}{|F_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>N}-2^{-n+4}\log\frac{2^{-n+4}}{|F_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>N}-2^{-n+4}\log 2^{-n^{3}-n+4}

\displaystyle<\infty

Hence, Proposition 2.2 implies that
\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}-\mu(P_{\ell})\log\mu(P_{\ell})<\infty
.

Next, we will show that the same is true for Shannon partitions of
\alpha^{-1}
. Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
be given. For a given
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
Q_{k}=\{x\in X\mid\alpha^{-1}(x,\ell)=k\}
and let
\mathscr{Q}=\{Q_{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. For every

and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

where
E^{\ell}_{n}
is the set of points whose
\alpha^{-1}
cocycle value for
\ell
is fixed at stage
n
. Again, we wish to use Proposition 2.2. First, we see that for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
\{E^{\ell,k}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a partition of
Q_{k}
.
Setting
F_{n}=\{\alpha^{-1}_{n}(x,\ell)\mid x\in X\}
, we can see that
E^{\ell,k}_{n}=\varnothing
if
\ell\notin F_{n}
. So, we only need to show that
\sum-\mu(E_{n}^{\ell})\log\frac{\mu(E_{n}^{\ell})}{|F_{n}|}<\infty
.
By Lemma 6.6, we have that

\displaystyle\mu(E^{\ell}_{n})<2\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i}+\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell}{L_{i}-K_{i}}<3\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i}

for large enough

. By our choice of
\epsilon_{n}<2^{-n}
in our construction, there is an

such that, for all

, we have that
\mu(E_{n}^{\ell})<2^{-n+4}
and
|F_{n}|=K_{n}<2^{n^{3}}
. So, this implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{n>N}-\mu(E^{\ell}_{n})\log\frac{\mu(E^{\ell}_{n})}{|F_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>N}-2^{-n+4}\log\frac{2^{-n+4}}{|F_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>N}-2^{-n+4}\log 2^{-n^{3}-n+4}

\displaystyle<\infty

So, by Proposition 2.2, we can conclude that
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}-\mu(Q_{k})\log\mu(Q_{k})<\infty

∎

7.
Weak Mixing

In this section, we will find sequences of natural numbers
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{L_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and a sequence of real numbers
\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
so that we can construct a sequence of cocycles
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of the action
\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright^{S}(X,\mu)
such that
\beta_{n}
is an
(L_{n}-K_{n},K_{n},L_{n},\epsilon_{n})
-blocked cocycle and, setting
\alpha_{n}=\beta_{n}\circ\beta_{n-1}\circ\dots\beta_{1}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the limit
\alpha(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}(x)
exists and the action
T=S^{\alpha^{-1}}
is weak mixing. These parameters will also be chosen in a way such that
\alpha
is a Shannon orbit equivalence between
S
and
T
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
G
be a countable infinite group and
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu)
be a probability space. we say an action
G\curvearrowright^{S}X
is
weak mixing
if for all finite sets
\mathscr{F}\subset\mathscr{B}
we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{g\to\infty}\sum_{A,B\in\mathscr{F}}|\mu(A\cap S^{g}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|=0.

The following characterization of weak mixing is the one we will use.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
G
be a group and let
G\curvearrowright^{S}X
be an action over the probability space
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu)
. If there is a sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset G
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(A\cap S^{g_{n}}B)=\mu(A)\mu(B)

for all pairs of measurable sets
A,B\in X
, then
S
is weak mixing.

Proof.

Let
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset G
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(A\cap S^{g_{n}}B)=\mu(A)\mu(B)

for all pairs of measurable sets
A,B\in X
.
Also, let a finite set
\mathscr{F}\subset\mathscr{B}
and an
\epsilon>0
be given. Let
E\subset G
be a finite set. Let
L
be a lower bound of the set

\displaystyle\bigg\{\sum_{A,B\in\mathscr{F}}|\mu(A\cap S^{g}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|\text{ }\bigg|\text{ }g\in G\setminus E\bigg\}

Following the hypothesis, we can find an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>N
we have that

\displaystyle|\mu(A\cap S^{g_{n}}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|<\frac{\epsilon}{2^{|\mathscr{F}|}}

for each pair
A,B\in\mathscr{F}
and
g_{n}\in G\setminus E
. Thus, we have for all
n<N

\displaystyle\sum_{A,B\in\mathscr{F}}|\mu(A\cap S^{g_{n}}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|<\epsilon,

which implies that
L<\epsilon
. Thus,

\displaystyle\inf_{g\in G\setminus E}\bigg\{\sum_{A,B\in\mathscr{F}}|\mu(A\cap S^{g}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|\bigg\}<\epsilon.

Since
E
and
\epsilon
were arbitrary, we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{g\to\infty}\sum_{A,B\in\mathscr{F}}|\mu(A\cap S^{g}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|=0.

∎

Lemma 7.8

Lemma 7.9
.

Let a probability measure preserving action
\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright^{S}(X,\mu)

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}_{A}(\mathscr{P})-\{r_{i}\}_{i\in I}|<\epsilon

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}_{B}(\mathscr{P})-\{r_{i}\}_{i\in I}|<\epsilon

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}_{A\sqcup B}(\mathscr{P})-\{r_{i}\}_{i\in I}|<\epsilon.

Let

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(P_{i}\cap(A\sqcup B))}{\mu(A\sqcup B)}=\frac{\mu(P_{i}\cap A)+\mu(P_{i}\cap B))}{2\mu(A)}

Thus,

\displaystyle|\frac{\mu(P_{i}\cap(A\sqcup B))}{\mu(A\sqcup B)}-r_{i}|

\displaystyle=|\frac{\mu(P_{i}\cap A)+\mu(P_{i}\cap B))}{2\mu(A)}-r_{i}|=|\frac{\mu(P_{i}\cap A)}{2\mu(A)}+\frac{\mu(P_{i}\cap B))}{2\mu(B)}-r_{i}|

\displaystyle<\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}=\epsilon.

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}_{A\sqcup B}(\mathscr{P})-\{r_{i}\}_{i\in I}|<\epsilon.

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})\times{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<\epsilon+\epsilon_{n_{i}}.

∎

Lemma 7.11
.

Given the sequence of actions
\{S_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by
S_{n+1}=S^{\beta^{-1}_{n+1}}_{n}
, the subsequence
\{K_{n_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and an
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, then, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\{n_{j}+1,\dots,n_{j+1}\}
,

\displaystyle\mu(\{x\in X\mid S^{\ell}_{m}(x)=S_{n_{j}}^{\ell}(x)\})\geq 1-\sum_{i=n_{j}+1}^{m}\bigg(\frac{\epsilon_{i}}{K_{n_{j+1}}^{2}}+\frac{K_{i}}{L_{i}}+\frac{|\ell|}{L_{i}}\bigg).

Proof.

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\{n_{j}+1,\dots,n_{j+1}\}
be given. Setting
\beta=\beta_{m}\circ\beta_{m-1}\circ\dots\circ\beta_{n_{j}+1}
, we notice that
S_{m}=S_{n_{j}}^{\beta^{-1}}
. So, if
x\in X
such that
S^{\ell}_{m}(x)=S^{\ell}_{n_{j}}(x)
, we must have that

\displaystyle S^{\ell}_{n_{j}}(x)=S^{\ell}_{m}(x)=S_{n_{j}}^{\beta^{-1}(x,\ell)}(x)

So, we must have that
\beta(x,\ell)=\ell
. Note that this equality may not hold for an
x\in X
if there is an
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n_{j}+1\leq i\leq m
and at least one of the following properties holds:

(1)

x\in X\setminus S_{i-1}^{M_{i}}B_{i}
,

(2)

x
is in the filler set of a block, or

(3)

x
is in a rigid block, but
S_{i-1}^{\ell}(x)
is not.

We have that, for each
i
between
n_{j}+1
and
m
inclusive, the set of points which satisfy each condition is less than
\frac{\epsilon_{i}}{K_{n_{j+1}}^{2}}
,
\frac{K_{i}}{L_{i}}
, and
\frac{|\ell|}{L_{i}}
respectively. Thus, removing these sets for every
i
between
n_{j}+1
and
m
yields

\displaystyle\mu(\{x\in X\mid S^{\ell}_{m}(x)=S_{n_{j}}^{\ell}(x)\})\geq 1-\sum_{i=n_{j}+1}^{m}\bigg(\frac{\epsilon_{i}}{K_{n_{j+1}}^{2}}+\frac{K_{i}}{L_{i}}+\frac{|\ell|}{L_{i}}\bigg)

∎

Lemma 7.12
.

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, if

B_{n_{i}}
respect to
\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee\{O_{i-1},X\setminus O_{i-1}\}

all but a proportion of at most
4\epsilon_{n_{i-1}}

-name of

(S_{n_{i-1}},\mathscr{P}_{i},-C_{K_{n_{i}}})
-name.

Proof.

Let an atom
A
of

and a

be given. By Lemma 7.11,

\displaystyle\mu(\{x\in X\mid S_{n_{i}-1}(x)=S_{n_{i-1}}(x)\})\geq 1-\sum_{j=n_{i-1}+1}^{n_{i}-1}\bigg(\frac{\epsilon_{j}}{K_{n_{i}}^{2}}+\frac{K_{j}}{L_{j}}+\frac{1}{L_{j}}\bigg).

Recall that we chose our tower
S_{n_{i}-1}^{C_{M_{n_{i}}}}B_{n_{i}}

(S_{n_{i}-1},\{O_{i-1},X\setminus O_{i-1}\},C_{M_{n_{i}}})

\frac{\epsilon_{n_{i}}}{K_{n_{i}}}

\sum_{j=n_{i-1}+1}^{n_{i}-1}\bigg(\frac{\epsilon_{j}}{K_{n_{i}}^{2}}+\frac{K_{j}}{L_{j}}+\frac{1}{L_{j}}\bigg)+\frac{\epsilon_{n_{i}}}{K_{n_{i}}}
of the
m\in M_{n_{i}}
, that
S^{m}_{n_{i}-1}(b)=S^{m}_{n_{i-1}}(b)
. Thus, for all

-name since

\displaystyle K_{n_{i}}\bigg(\sum_{j=n_{i-1}+1}^{n_{i}-1}\bigg(\frac{\epsilon_{j}}{K_{n_{i}}^{2}}+\frac{K_{j}}{L_{j}}+\frac{1}{L_{j}}\bigg)+\frac{\epsilon_{n_{i}}}{K_{n_{i}}}\bigg)

\displaystyle<\epsilon_{n_{i-1}}+\frac{K_{n_{i}}^{3}}{L_{n_{i-1}+1}}+\frac{K_{n_{i}}^{2}}{L_{n_{i-1}+1}}+\epsilon_{n_{i}}

∎

Remark 7.13
.

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\ell_{i}=L_{n_{i}}
. We will verify our approximate mixing condition for each
S_{n_{i}}
relative to
\ell_{i}
.

Definition 7.14
.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
A
be an atom of the pure partition of
B_{n_{i}}
respect to
\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee\{O_{i-1},X\setminus O_{i-1}\}
. We say that an element
m\in C_{M_{n_{i}}}
is a
good position
for
A
if for all
b\in A
we have that both
S_{n_{i}-1}^{m-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(b)
and
S_{n_{i}-1}^{m+\ell_{i}-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(b)
are in rigid blocks of
\beta_{n_{i}}
, and

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}-1}^{m-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(A)}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<\epsilon_{n_{i}}

and

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}-1}^{m+\ell_{i}-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(A)}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<\epsilon_{n_{i}}

both hold.

Lemma 7.15
.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
A
be an atom of the pure partition of
B_{n_{i}}
respect to
\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee\{O_{i-1},X\setminus O_{i-1}\}
. If
m\in C_{M_{n_{i}}}
is a good position for
A
, then

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}}^{m}\bar{A}}\bigg(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{i}}^{-\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i}\bigg)-{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})\times{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<2\epsilon_{n_{i}}.

Proof.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
and an atom
A
in
B_{n_{i}}
be given. Note that we have that
S_{n_{i}-1}^{m-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}A
and
S_{n_{i}-1}^{m+\ell_{i}-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}A
are in different rigid blocks since we chose
\ell_{i}=L_{n_{i}}
. First, by the facts that we chose our permutations of
C_{M_{i}}
to define the block permutations of
\beta_{n_{i}}
to be uniformly distributed on
A
and each permutation of a point in a rigid block is just translation by some
k\in K_{n_{i}}
, we can see, setting for
k,k^{\prime}\in K_{n_{i}}

\displaystyle Q_{k,k^{\prime}}=\bigg\{b\in\bar{A}\mid\beta_{n_{i}}^{-1}(b,m)=m-k\text{ and }\beta_{n_{i}}^{-1}(b,m+\ell)=m+\ell-k^{\prime}\bigg\},

that
\{Q_{k,k^{\prime}}\}_{k,k^{\prime}\in K_{n_{i}}}
is a partition of
\bar{A}
into sets of uniform measure
\frac{\mu(\bar{A})}{K_{n_{i}}^{2}}
. Now, fix
P,P^{\prime}\in\mathscr{P}_{i}
. We have that
\mu(S_{n_{i}}^{-m}P\cap S_{n_{i}}^{-(m+\ell_{i})}P^{\prime}\cap Q_{k,k^{\prime}})=\mu(Q_{k,k^{\prime}})
precisely when
S_{n_{i}-1}^{m-k}A\subset P
and
S_{n_{i}-1}^{m+\ell_{i}-k^{\prime}}A\subset P^{\prime}
. Otherwise,
\mu(S_{n_{i}}^{-m}P\cap S_{n_{i}}^{-(m+\ell_{i})}P^{\prime}\cap Q_{k,k^{\prime}})=0
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mu\bigg(S_{n_{i}}^{-m}P\cap S_{n_{i}}^{-(m+\ell_{i})}P^{\prime}\cap\bar{A}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in K_{n_{i}}}\mu\bigg(S_{n_{i}}^{-m}P\cap S_{n_{i}}^{-(m+\ell_{i})}P^{\prime}\cap Q_{k,k^{\prime}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\{k\in K_{n_{i}}\mid S_{n_{i}-1}^{m-k}A\subset P\}|}{K_{n_{i}}}\frac{|\{k^{\prime}\in K_{n_{i}}\mid S_{n_{i}-1}^{m+\ell_{i}-k^{\prime}}A\subset P^{\prime}\}|}{K_{n_{i}}}\mu(\bar{A}).

Since
m
is a good position for
A
, we recall that

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}-1}^{m-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(A)}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<\epsilon_{i}

and

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}-1}^{m+\ell_{i}-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(A)}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<\epsilon_{i}

both hold. Hence, we get that

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\mu\bigg(S_{n_{i}}^{-m}P\cap S_{n_{i}}^{-(m+\ell_{i})}P^{\prime}\cap\bar{A}\bigg)}{\mu(\bar{A})}-\mu(P)\mu(P^{\prime})\bigg|<2\epsilon_{n_{i}}.

Since our choices of
P
and
P^{\prime}
were arbitrary, we have

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}}^{m}\bar{A}}\bigg(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{i}}^{-\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i}\bigg)-{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})\times{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<2\epsilon_{n_{i}}.

∎

Lemma 7.16
.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
A
be an atom of the pure partition of
B_{n_{i}}
with respect to
\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee\{O_{i-1},X\setminus O_{i-1}\}
. Then, all but a fraction at most
8\epsilon_{n_{i-1}}+4\epsilon_{n_{i}}
of the
m\in C_{M_{n_{i}}}
are good positions for
A
.

Proof.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
and the atom
A
be given. First, recall that we created a set
E_{i}
such that
\mu(X\setminus E_{i})<\epsilon_{n_{i}}
and that
x\in E_{i}
implies that the
(S_{n_{i-1}},\mathscr{P}_{i},-C_{K_{n_{i}}})
-name of
x
has distribution within
\epsilon_{n_{i}}
of the distribution of
\mathscr{P}_{i}
. Also, we chose our base
B_{n_{i}}
so that for all
b\in B_{n_{i}}
there is a set
F\subset C_{M_{i}}
such that
|F|>(1-\epsilon_{n_{i}})|C_{M_{n_{i}}}|
and for every
k\in F
we have that
S_{n_{i}-1}^{k}(b)\in E_{i}
. So, since every element of
b\in A
has the same
(S_{n_{i-1}},\mathscr{P}_{i},-C_{M_{n_{i}}})
-name, all but at most a fraction
\epsilon_{n_{i}}
of the
m\in C_{M_{i}}
have that

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i-1}}^{m-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(A)}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<\epsilon_{n_{i}}.

Lemma 7.12 implies that all but a proportion of at most
4\epsilon_{n_{i-1}}+\epsilon_{n_{i}}
of the
m\in C_{M_{i}}
have that

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}-1}^{m-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(A)}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<\epsilon_{n_{i}}.

So, the proportion of
m\in C_{M_{i}}
that satisfy both

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}-1}^{m-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(A)}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<\epsilon_{n_{i}}

and

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}-1}^{m+\ell_{i}-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(A)}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<\epsilon_{n_{i}}

is at most

8\epsilon_{n_{i-1}}+2\epsilon_{n_{i}}
.
Now, notice that, since we set
\ell_{i}=L_{n_{i}}
to be exactly one block length, for all

,
S_{n_{i}-1}^{m-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(b)
is not in a rigid block of
\beta_{n_{i}}
or
S_{n_{i}-1}^{m+\ell_{i}-C_{K_{n_{i}}}}(b)

precisely when

occupies one of the first

levels of a rigid block or

of the

. Combining this with our previous estimate yields that at least a fraction

.

∎

Proposition 7.17
.

There is a sequence
\{\delta_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of non-negative real numbers such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}\delta_{i}=0
and, for every
i>1
and for every
j\geq i
, the action
S_{n_{j}}
is
\delta_{i}
-mixing relative to
(\ell_{i},\mathscr{P}_{i})
.

Proof.

Set
\delta_{i}=\frac{3}{2^{n_{i}}}+8\epsilon_{n_{i-1}}+7\epsilon_{n_{i}}
. We will first show that
S_{n_{i}}
is
\delta_{i}
-mixing relative to
(\ell_{i},\mathscr{P}_{i})
. Let
A
be an atom of the pure partition of
B_{n_{i}}
with respect to
\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee\{O_{i-1},X\setminus O_{i-1}\}
. By Lemma 7.15, if
m\in C_{M_{n_{i}}}
is a good position for
A
, then

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}}^{m}\bar{A}}\bigg(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{i}}^{-\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i}\bigg)-{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})\times{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<2\epsilon_{n_{i}}.

Combining this with Lemma 7.16 yields that

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}_{S_{n_{i}}^{C_{M_{i}}}\bar{A}}\bigg(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{i}}^{-\ell}\mathscr{P}_{i}\bigg)-{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})\times{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<8\epsilon_{n_{i-1}}+6\epsilon_{n_{i}}.

Now, we can see that Lemma 7.10
implies that

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})\times{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|<8\epsilon_{n_{i-1}}+7\epsilon_{n_{i}}<\delta_{i}.

Hence,
S_{n_{i}}
is
\delta_{i}
-mixing relative to
(\ell_{i},\mathscr{P}_{i})
.

Next, we will prove for every
j>i
,
S_{n_{j}}
is
\delta_{i}
-mixing relative to
(\ell_{i},\mathscr{P}_{i})
. Following Lemma 7.11, we have that

\displaystyle\mu(\{x\mid S_{n_{j}}^{\ell_{i}}(x)\neq S_{n_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}(x)\})\leq\sum_{k=n_{i}+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}+\sum_{k=n_{i}+1}^{\infty}\frac{K_{k}}{L_{k}}+\sum_{k=n_{i}+1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell_{i}}{L_{k}}<\frac{3}{2^{n_{i}}}

Now, let
P,P^{\prime}\in\mathscr{P}_{i}
. we can see that

\displaystyle|\mu(P\cap S_{n_{j}}^{-\ell_{i}}P^{\prime})-\mu(P\cap S_{n_{i}}^{-\ell_{i}}P^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\mu((P\cap S_{n_{j}}^{-\ell_{i}}P^{\prime})\triangle(P\cap S_{n_{i}}^{-\ell_{i}}P^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\mu(P\cap(S_{n_{j}}^{-\ell_{i}}P^{\prime}\triangle S_{n_{i}}^{-\ell_{i}}P^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\{x\mid S_{n_{j}}^{\ell_{i}}(x)\neq S_{n_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}(x)\})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2^{n_{i}}}

So, we have that

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{j}}^{\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i})|<\frac{3}{2^{n_{i}}}

Thus,

\displaystyle|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{j}}^{\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})\times{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{j}}^{\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i})|

\displaystyle\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }+|{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i}\vee S_{n_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}\mathscr{P}_{i})-{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})\times{\rm dist}(\mathscr{P}_{i})|

\displaystyle<\frac{3}{2^{n_{i}}}+8\epsilon_{n_{i-1}}+7\epsilon_{n_{i}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i}.

∎

We are now equipped to prove the main theorem.

Theorem 7.18
.

Suppose
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of cocycles of an action
\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright^{S}(X,\mu)
with corresponding actions
S_{n}=S^{\alpha_{n}^{-1}}
for every
n
which converge to an action
T
. If there is a sequence
\{\ell_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{Z},
a sequence
\{\delta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{n}=0
, and a sequence
\{\mathscr{P}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of measurable partitions of
(X,\mu)
which are sequentially refining and generate, modulo null sets, the
\sigma
-algebra of
(X,\mu)
such that, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\geq i
,
S_{n_{j}}
is weakly
\delta_{i}
-mixing relative to
(\ell_{i},\mathscr{P}_{i})
, we have that
T
is weakly mixing.

Proof.

By Proposition 7.2, we only need to show that for every pair
A,B\subset X
of non-null measurable sets we have

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-\ell_{i}}B)=\mu(A)\mu(B).

Let non-null measurable sets
A,B\subset X
and an
\epsilon>0
be given. Choose an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such for all
k>N
:

(1)

we can find
j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
(depending on
k
) such that there are sets
P_{i}\in\mathscr{P}_{n}
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,j_{1}\}
and sets
Q_{i}\in\mathscr{P}_{n}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,j_{2}\}
such that
\mu\bigg((\cup_{i=1}^{j_{1}}P_{i})\triangle A\bigg)<\frac{\epsilon}{6}
and
\mu\bigg((\cup_{i=1}^{j_{2}}Q_{i})\triangle B\bigg)<\frac{\epsilon}{6}
.

(2)

\delta_{k}<\frac{\epsilon}{6}
.

Fix
k>N
and set
P=\cup_{i=1}^{j_{1}}P_{i}
and
Q=\cup_{i=1}^{j_{2}}Q_{i}
. First, note that we can find a
j>k
such that

\displaystyle\mu\bigg(\{x\mid T^{\ell_{k}}(x)\neq S_{n_{j}}^{\ell_{k}}(x)\}\bigg)<\frac{\epsilon}{6}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\mu(P\cap S_{n_{j}}^{-\ell_{k}}Q)-\mu(P\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}Q)|

\displaystyle\leq\mu((P\cap S_{n_{j}}^{-\ell_{k}}P^{\prime})\triangle(P\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}Q)))

\displaystyle=\mu(P\cap(S_{n_{j}}^{-\ell_{k}}P^{\prime}\triangle T^{-\ell_{k}}P^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\{x\mid S_{n_{j}}^{\ell_{k}}(x)\neq T^{\ell_{k}}(x)\})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{6}.

Combining this with Proposition 7.17 and the fact that
\delta_{k}<\frac{\epsilon}{6}
yields

\displaystyle|\mu(P\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}Q)-\mu(P)\mu(Q)|

\displaystyle\leq|\mu(P\cap S_{n_{j}}^{-\ell_{k}}Q)-\mu(P\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}Q)|

\displaystyle\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }+|\mu(P\cap S_{n_{j}}^{-\ell_{k}}Q)-\mu(P)\mu(Q)|

\displaystyle<\delta_{k}+\frac{\epsilon}{6}

\displaystyle<\frac{2\epsilon}{6}.

The following inequality also holds:

\displaystyle|\mu(A\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}B)-\mu(P\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}Q)|

\displaystyle\leq|\mu(A\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}B)-\mu(P\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}B)|

\displaystyle\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }+|\mu(P\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}B)-\mu(P\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}Q)|

\displaystyle\leq|\mu((A\triangle P)\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}B)|

\displaystyle\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }+|\mu(P\cap(T^{-\ell_{k}}B\triangle T^{-\ell_{k}}Q))|

\displaystyle\leq\mu(A\triangle P)+\mu(T^{-\ell_{k}}B\triangle T^{-\ell_{k}}Q)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(A\triangle P)+\mu(B\triangle Q)

\displaystyle<\frac{2\epsilon}{6}

Finally, putting everything together, we get the following:

\displaystyle|\mu(A\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|

\displaystyle\leq|\mu(A\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}B)-\mu(P\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}Q)|

\displaystyle\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }+|\mu(P\cap T^{-\ell_{k}}Q)-\mu(P)\mu(Q)|

\displaystyle\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }+|\mu(P)\mu(Q)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|

\displaystyle<\frac{2\epsilon}{6}+\frac{2\epsilon}{6}+\frac{2\epsilon}{6}=\epsilon

Since
k>N
was arbitrary, we get that
T
is weakly mixing.
∎

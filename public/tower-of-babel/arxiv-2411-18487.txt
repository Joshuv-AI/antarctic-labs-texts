Introduction

One of the most classical problems in extremal graph theory called Turán-type problem, is counting the maximum number of edges in a hereditary family of graphs. We use
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)
to denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
H
-free graph. In 1941, Turán
[
15
]
gave the exact value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{r})
and characterized the extremal graph. Later in 1946, Erdős and Stone
[
4
]
generalized this result asymtotically by proving
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)=(1-\frac{1}{\chi(H)-1}+o(1))\binom{n}{2}
for an arbitrary graph
H
, where
\chi(H)
denotes the chromatic number of
H
.

All graphs considered in this paper are simple and planar. We denote a simple graph by
G=(V(G),E(G))
where
V(G)
is the set of vertices and
E(G)
is the set of edges. Let
v(G)
and
e(G)
denote the number of vertices and edges in
G
, respectively. For any subset
S\subseteq V(G)
, the subgraph induced by
S
is denoted by
G[S]
. We denote by
G\backslash S
the subgraph induced by
V(G)\backslash S
. If
S=\{v\}
, then we simply write
G\backslash v
. Given two graphs
G
and
H
,
G
is
H
-
free
if it contains no subgraph isomorphic to
H
. We use
G\vee H
to denote the graph obtained from
G
and
H
by joining all edges between
V(G)
and
V(H)
. Given two subgraphs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
in a planar graph
G
, we use
H_{1}\text{-}H_{2}
to denote two vertex-disjoint graphs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
with an edge connecting them. For more undefined notation, please refer to
[
1
]
.

In 2016, Dowden
[
3
]
initiated the study of Turán-type problems when host graphs are planar graphs. We use
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,H)
to denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
H
-free planar graph. Dowden studied the planar Turán numbers of
C_{4}
and
C_{5}
, they proved that
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{4})\leq\frac{15(n-2)}{7}
and
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{5})\leq\frac{12n-33}{5}
, where
C_{k}
is a cycle with
k
vertices. Ghosh, Győri, Martin, Paulos and Xiao
[
6
]
gave the exact value for
C_{6}
, they proved that
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{6})\leq\frac{5n-14}{2}
. Shi, Walsh and Yu
[
14
]
, Győri, Li and Zhou
[
8
]
gave the exact value for
C_{7}
, they proved that
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{7})\leq\frac{18n-48}{7}
, independently. Until now, the exact planar Turán number of

is still unknown for

.

Cranston, Lidický, Liu and Shantanam
[
2
]
first gave the lower bound for general cycles as
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{k})>\frac{3(k-1)}{k}n-\frac{6(k+1)}{k}
for

and sufficiently large

. Lan and Song
[
11
]
improved the lower bound to
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{k})>(3-\frac{3-\frac{2}{k-1}}{k-6+\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor})n+c_{k}
for

, where

is a constant. Recently, Győri, Varga and Zhu
[
7
]
improved the lower bound by giving a new construction to show that
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{k})\geq 3n-6-\frac{6\cdot 3^{\log_{2}3}n}{k^{\log_{2}3}}
for
k\geq 7
and sufficiently large
n
. Shi, Walsh and Yu
[
13
]
gave an upper bound and proved that
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{k})\leq 3n-6-\frac{n}{4k^{\log_{2}3}}
for all
n,k\geq 4
.

We use
tC_{k}
to denote the union of
t
vertex-disjoint
k
-cycles and
t\mathcal{C}
to denote the union of
t
vertex-disjoint cycles without length restriction. Lan, Shi and Song
[
9
]
showed that

for

and the extremal graph is the double wheel

proved that

denotes the union of two vertex-disjoint cycles

and

. Fang, Lin and Shi

proved that for

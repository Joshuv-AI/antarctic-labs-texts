Restriction of Fractional Derivatives of the Fourier Transform
Michael Goldberg, Chun Ho Lau

Abstract
In this paper, we showed that for suitable ( β , p , s , ℓ ) (\beta,p,s,\ell) the β \beta -order fractional derivative with respect to the last coordinate of the Fourier transform of an L p ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) function is in H − s H^{-s} after restricting to a graph of a function with non-vanishing Gaussian curvature provided that the restriction of the Fourier transform of such function to the surface is in H ℓ H^{\ell} . This is a generalization of the result in [ 5 ] *Theorem 1.12.

1
Introduction

1.1
Fourier restriction problem

The desirable properties of the Fourier transform of an
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function are crucial in classical harmonic analysis with application to Kakeya conjecture, Bochner-Riesz summabilities, and partial differential equations, see
[
11
,
8
]
. Hausdorff-Young’s inequality ensures that the Fourier transform of an
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function is in
L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
whenever
1\leq p\leq 2
. However, this does not give us any information if the Fourier transform of an
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function is restricted to a measure-zero subset
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The Fourier restriction problem, in the most general form, seeks to determine the complete range of
(p,q)
for which

\displaystyle\|\widehat{f}|_{\Sigma}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(1.1)

holds for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for a measure-zero set
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
, where
\widehat{f}(\iota)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-2\pi i\iota\cdot x}f(x)dx
. Different choices of
\Sigma
and
n
lead to different ranges of
(p,q)
and it remains an open problem in most cases, and the best result currently in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
for parabolas is
[
10
]
; and for a higher dimension, see
[
6
]
. We also refer to the reader
[
3
]
for the survey on Fourier restriction estimates on different
\Sigma
. In particular, if
\Sigma
is a two-dimensional sphere or a compact parabola around the origin, the range of
(p,q)
is completely characterized by
[
2
]
. It is also worth mentioning that the Tomas-Stein Theorem, established in
[
9
,
7
]
, states that Equation (
1.1
) holds for
q=2
and
1\leq p\leq\frac{2n+2}{n+3}
with
\Sigma
having non-zero Gaussian curvature at the origin.

One reason for imposing
\Sigma
having a point with non-vanishing Gaussian curvature is to rule out “uninteresting” cases. If we consider
\Sigma
to be a hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the only possible range of
(p,q)
is
q=\infty
and
1\leq p\leq\infty.
This may suggest that we should consider
\Sigma
having non-zero Gaussian curvature at origin (without losing generality). In this paper, we will assume that
\Sigma
is a graph of a function
h
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
with a vanishing gradient at 0 and an invertible Hessian matrix at 0. A precise statement will be given in Section
2.1
.

The next step of the Fourier restriction problem is to consider the possibilities of the derivative of the Fourier transform of an
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function. In
[
5
]
, various versions of the derivative Fourier restriction problem are discussed, with the core idea being to impose suitable assumptions to obtain
\phi\nabla^{k}\widehat{f}\in H^{-s}(\Sigma)
, or more importantly,
\phi\frac{\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}}{\partial\xi_{n}^{\beta}}\in H^{-s}(\Sigma)
for a fixed
\phi
. Here,
g\in H^{-s}(\Sigma)
means
g|_{\Sigma}\in H^{-s}(\Sigma)
, and
H^{-s}(\Sigma)
is the
L^{2}
-based Bessel potential space as defined in Section
2.1
. If
\|\phi\nabla^{k}\widehat{f}\|_{H^{-s}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, then the trace of
\nabla^{k}\widehat{f}\in H^{-s}_{loc}(\Sigma)
for
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Cho, Guo, and Lee
[
1
]
*Theorem 1.1 studied the trace value of
\widehat{f}
in Sobolev spaces, and G. and Stolyarov
[
5
]
*Proposition 1.1 used this to obtain a characterization of exponents for which
\|\phi\nabla^{k}\widehat{f}\|_{H^{-s}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
holds. More precisely,

Theorem A

(
[
5
]
*Proposition 1.1)
.

Let
p>1
. The inequality
\|\phi\nabla^{k}\widehat{f}\|_{H^{-s}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
holds for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if

k\leq s
,
k<\sigma_{p}
, and
2k-s\leq\kappa_{p}
, where
\kappa_{p}=\frac{n+1}{p}-\frac{n+3}{2}
and
\sigma_{p}=\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n+1}{2}
.

Moreover, G. and Stolyarov also established some necessary conditions and some sufficient conditions for

\displaystyle\|\phi\nabla^{k}\widehat{f}\|_{H^{-s}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)}

(1.2)

for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\widehat{f}\phi\in H^{\ell}(\Sigma)
given
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
, where
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
is the space of all smooth functions with support properly contained in
\Sigma
.

More precisely, they (
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.11) showed that if (
1.2
) holds,
p>1
,
s\geq 0
, and
\ell\geq 0
, then the exponents have to satisfy

\displaystyle k\leq\min\{s+1,s+\ell\};\quad\quad k<\sigma_{p};\quad\quad 2k-s\leq\kappa_{p};

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{k\ell}{s+\ell-k}\leq\kappa_{p}\quad\text{ if $k>s$.}

(1.4)

However, the sufficient conditions do not match with the necessary conditions due to the constraint
k\in\mathbb{N}
. In order to have (
1.2
), other than (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), they (
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.12) additionally assume that
\frac{n+1}{p}-\frac{n+3}{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and either
2\lceil\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\kappa_{p}\rceil\leq\kappa_{p}
or
k\leq\lfloor\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2}\rfloor
hold, where
\lceil x\rceil
is the smallest integer greater than or equal to
x
and
\lfloor x\rfloor
is the greatest integer less than or equal to
x
. If
2\lceil\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\kappa_{p}\rceil\leq\kappa_{p}
and
k\leq\lfloor\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2}\rfloor
do not hold, then one has to assume that
s\geq k-\frac{\kappa_{p}-k}{\kappa_{p}-\lfloor\kappa_{p}/2\rfloor}
. In particular, if
k=1
and
s=0
,

Theorem B

(
[
5
]
*Corollary 1.13)
.

Let
m
be an integer in
[2,\frac{n-1}{2})
and
\ell\geq\frac{m}{m-1}
. Then,

\|\phi\nabla\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n+2}{n+3+2m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
.

In order to remove the constraint
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we need to consider an alternative for
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
, and the Riesz fractional derivative, defined by
[(-\Delta)^{s}f]\widehat{\ }(\xi)=c_{n}|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{f}(\xi)
, would be a good choice. The question now becomes:

Question:

For what ranges of
(\beta,p,s,\ell)
does the inequality

\|\phi(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}\widehat{f}\|_{H^{-s}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)}

hold true for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
?

In this paper, we will focus on the derivative along the transversal direction. We remark that Theorem
A
holds in this case by replacing
k
by
\beta
with the aid of Lemma
1
.

1.2
Results and outline

Our first main theorem is to provide a sufficient condition so that the fractional derivative of
\widehat{f}
is in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has
\widehat{f}|_{\Sigma}\in H^{\ell}(\Sigma)
, and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
. If
p>1
,
2\beta\leq\kappa_{p}
,
2\beta<\sigma_{p}
,
0\leq\beta\leq 1
, and
\ell\geq\frac{\kappa_{p}\beta}{\kappa_{p}-\beta}
, then

1.

we have
(-\Delta_{\xi_{n}})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\widehat{f}=c_{n}\widehat{|x_{n}|^{\beta}f}\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
and

\|\phi(-\Delta_{\xi_{n}})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{n,p,\beta,\ell}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)},

where
-\Delta_{\xi_{n}}
denotes the Laplacian with respect to the
\xi_{n}
-variable.

2.

if
\beta=1
, we have
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{n}}\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
and

\bigg\|\phi\frac{\partial\widehat{f}}{\partial\xi_{n}}\bigg\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{n,p,\beta,\ell}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)}

When
p=1
, we additionally require
\ell>\frac{\kappa_{1}\beta}{\kappa_{1}-\beta}
obtaining the above result.

In particular, we have a continuous analogue of Theorem
B
when
\beta=1
. Moreover, this result can be applied to
n=3
and
n=4
, which is not covered by Theorem
B
. From Theorem
3
, the best possible range of
\beta
is
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
when
n=3
; when
n=4
, the best range of
\beta
is
[0,\frac{3}{4}]
; and when
n=5
, the best range of
\beta
is
[0,1]
with
p=1
, and we also recover the result in
[
5
]
that the (usual) derivative of
\widehat{f}
on
\Sigma
is in
L^{2}
if
p=1
and
\ell>2
from the second statement.

The following theorem shows that (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) are almost sufficient conditions for (
1.2
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
s
,
\ell
,
\kappa_{p}
,
\sigma_{p}
, and
\beta
be non-negative numbers. Fix
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
. If

\displaystyle\beta\leq\min\{s+1,s+\ell\};\quad\quad\beta<\sigma_{p};\quad\quad 2\beta-s\leq\kappa_{p};

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\beta\ell}{s+\ell-\beta}\leq\kappa_{p}\quad\text{ if $\beta>s$}

(1.6)

hold,
\frac{2\beta}{s+2-\beta}\leq\kappa_{p}
whenever
\kappa_{p}<2\beta
and
\beta>s
, and
p>1
, then

\displaystyle\|\phi(-\Delta_{\xi_{n}})^{\beta/2}\widehat{f}\|_{H^{-s}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that
\widehat{f}|_{\Sigma}\in H^{\ell}(\Sigma)
. In case of
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\phi\frac{\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}}{\partial\xi_{n}^{\beta}}\bigg\|_{H^{-s}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

While the results in Theorems
1
and
2
provide bounds on the derivatives of
\hat{f}
, they are largely derived by analysis of the associated difference quotient

Tf(\xi,\eta)=\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi,h(\xi)+\eta)-\widehat{f}(\xi,h(\xi))}{\eta},

\xi\in U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

The first author proved
L^{p}\to L^{2}
estimates for
T
under the assumption that
\widehat{f}\equiv 0
on the sphere in
[
4
]
. Here we extend the bound to all cases where the restriction of
\widehat{f}
to
\Sigma
is sufficiently smooth.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has
\widehat{f}|_{\Sigma}\in H^{\ell}(\Sigma)
, and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
. If
1<p\leq\frac{2n+2}{n+5}
and
\ell\geq\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2\kappa_{p}-1}
, then

\|\phi Tf\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\eta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)}.

The above estimate is also true for
p=1
if
\ell>\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2\kappa_{1}-1}=\frac{n-1}{2n-4}.

The condition
\ell\geq\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2\kappa_{p}-1}
is necessary due to “shifted Knapp” counterexamples identical to those in
[
5
]
.

In fact, to derive the bounds for the derivatives of
\widehat{f}
, we consider an operator using
T
, namely,

\displaystyle S_{w}f(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(r)\mathscr{F}_{\eta\rightarrow r}(Tf(\xi,\cdot))(-r)dr=\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(-r)\mathscr{F}_{\eta\rightarrow r}(Tf(\xi,\cdot))(r)dr

(1.7)

The term
-r
is to eliminate the negative sign obtained from the Fourier transform, and
\mathscr{F}_{\eta\rightarrow r}=\mathscr{F}_{\eta}
denotes the one dimensional Fourier transform from
\eta
-variable to
r
-variable.

With
S_{w}
, we are able to prove the following theorems.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has
\widehat{f}|_{\Sigma}\in H^{\ell}(\Sigma)
, and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
. Under the hypothesis in Theorem
1
with
\beta\neq 0
, for all
b\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\sup_{\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1}\|\phi S_{u^{2\beta-1+ib}w}f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)}.

Theorem 4
.

Let
s
,
\ell
,
\kappa_{p}
, and
\sigma_{p}
be non-negative numbers and
\beta>0
. Fix
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
. Under the same hypothesis in Theorem
2
, for all
b\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1}\|\phi S_{u^{2\beta-1+ib}w}f\|_{H^{-s}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)}

(1.8)

whenever
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\widehat{f}|_{\Sigma}\in H^{\ell}(\Sigma)
.

We remark that the implicit constants depend on
s
,
\ell
,
\kappa_{p}
,
\sigma_{p}
, and

, but they are independent of

. Moreover, it suffices to consider

[
5
]
*Lemma 3.10.

Theorems
1
and
2
follow from Theorems

and

by choosing

and

. This is because one can show that

\displaystyle S_{i(\sgn(u)u)^{\beta-1}}f(\xi)

\displaystyle=2\pi^{3/2}\beta^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x_{n}|^{\beta}e^{-2\pi i(x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}h(\xi))}f(x^{\prime},x_{n})dx^{\prime}dx_{n}\simeq[(-\Delta_{\xi_{n}})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\widehat{f}]|_{\Sigma}(\xi)

and similarly one has
S_{iu^{\beta-1}}f\simeq[x_{n}^{\beta}f]\widehat{\ }|_{\Sigma}(\xi)
if

. From this calculation, we have to exclude the case
\beta=0
in Theorem

, but we can include

in Theorem

2
Preliminary

2.1
Notations

In this paper, we assume that
\Sigma
is a graph of a function
h
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Let
U
be a neighborhood of the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. We assume that the function
h\in C^{\infty}(U)
such that
h(0)=0
,
\nabla h(0)=0
,
\det(\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial\xi^{2}}(0))\neq 0
, and the second differential of
h
in
U
is sufficiently close to the one at
0
.

Since the roles of
\xi
and
\eta
are different, we consider
Tf
in anisotropic Sobolev (Bessel potential) spaces. To be more precise, if
g
is a function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, then we define

\|g(\xi,\eta)\|_{H^{\gamma}_{\xi}H^{\tau}_{\eta}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|x|)^{2\gamma}(1+|r|)^{2\tau}|\mathscr{F}_{(\xi,\eta)\rightarrow(x,r)}g(x,r)|^{2}dxdr,

where
\mathscr{F}_{(\xi,\eta)\rightarrow(x,r)}
denotes the Fourier transform from
(\xi,\eta)
-variable to
(x,r)
-variable.
Moreover, we will consider negative homogeneous Sobolev spaces in
\xi
-variable and inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces in
\eta
, and the inner product of such spaces are given by

\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}_{\xi}H^{\tau}_{\eta}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{U}\int_{U}\mathscr{F}_{\eta\rightarrow r}g_{1}(\xi,r)\mathscr{F}_{\eta\rightarrow r}\overline{g_{2}(\zeta,r)}|\xi-\zeta|^{2\gamma-n+1}(1+|r|)^{2\tau}d\xi d\zeta dr

if
\gamma\in(0,\frac{n-1}{2}).

We have used the fact that the negative homogeneous Sobolev space has a norm given by

\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}(\Sigma)}=C_{n,\gamma}\int_{U\times U}\psi(\xi,h(\xi))\overline{\psi(\zeta,h(\zeta))}|\xi-\zeta|^{2\gamma-n+1}d\xi d\zeta

provided that
\gamma\in(0,\frac{n-1}{2}).

On the other hand, we also assume that
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\widehat{f}|_{\Sigma}\in H^{\ell}(\Sigma)
, where the norm of
H^{\ell}(\Sigma)
is given by

\|\psi\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\big|\mathscr{F}_{\xi\rightarrow z}[\psi(\xi,h(\xi))](z)\big|^{2}(1+|z|)^{2\ell}dz.

In this paper, the implicit constant in
\lesssim
may vary from line to line.

Before we move on to calculating the kernel of
S^{*}_{w}S_{w}
, we shall recall the notations of some useful exponents in this paper:

\displaystyle\sigma_{p}

\displaystyle:=\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n+1}{2};

\displaystyle\kappa_{p}

\displaystyle:=\frac{n+1}{p}-\frac{n+3}{2}.

2.2
Computing the kernel of
S^{*}_{w}S_{w}

Let us recall
S_{w}
, where
w
is a complex-valued bounded measurable function.

\displaystyle S_{w}f(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(r)\mathscr{F}_{\eta\rightarrow r}(Tf(\xi,\cdot))(-r)dr=\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(-r)\mathscr{F}_{\eta\rightarrow r}(Tf(\xi,\cdot))(r)dr

We first compute the Fourier transform of
T
. To do so, for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\eta}(Tf(\xi,\cdot))(-r)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\pi^{2}\varepsilon\eta^{2}}e^{2\pi i\eta r}\bigg(\frac{e^{-2\pi ix_{n}\eta}-1}{\eta}\bigg)e^{-2\pi i(x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}h(\xi))}f(x^{\prime},x_{n})dx^{\prime}dx_{n}d\eta

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}2\pi i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}E_{x_{n},\varepsilon}(r)e^{-2\pi i(x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}h(\xi))}f(x^{\prime},x_{n})dx^{\prime}dx_{n},

where

E_{x_{n},\varepsilon}(r):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\int_{-x_{n}}^{0}e^{-\frac{(-r-y)^{2}}{\varepsilon}}dy=\int_{-\frac{x_{n}+r}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}}^{-\frac{r}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}}e^{-t^{2}}dt.

Note that as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we have

s_{r}(x_{n})=\sqrt{\pi}1_{x_{n}}(r):=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}E_{x_{n},\varepsilon}(r)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\pi},&\quad\text{if }0<r<x_{n};\\
-\sqrt{\pi},&\quad\text{if }x_{n}<r<0;\\
-\sgn(x_{n})\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}&\quad\text{if }r=0;\\
0,&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Therefore, using Dominated Convergence Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\eta}(Tf(\xi,\cdot))(-r)

\displaystyle=-2\pi i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}s_{r}(x_{n})e^{-2\pi i(x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}h(\xi))}f(x^{\prime},x_{n})dx^{\prime}dx_{n}.

We are now able to compute the kernel of
S_{w}^{*}S_{w}
. For
\ell\in(0,\frac{n-1}{2})
, we use homogeneous norm, and have

\displaystyle\langle\phi S_{w}f,\phi S_{w}g\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-\ell}}

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{U}\int_{U}\phi(\xi)S_{w}f(\xi)\overline{\phi(\zeta)S_{w}g(\zeta)}|\xi-\zeta|^{2\ell-n+1}d\xi d\zeta

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{U\times U}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\phi(\xi)w(r)\mathscr{F}_{\eta\rightarrow r}(Tf(\xi,\cdot))(-r)\overline{\phi(\zeta)w(r^{\prime})\mathscr{F}_{\eta\rightarrow r}(Tg(\zeta,\cdot))(-r^{\prime})}|\xi-\zeta|^{2\ell-n+1}drdr^{\prime}d\xi d\zeta

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{U\times U}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\phi(\xi)\overline{\phi(\zeta)}w(r)\overline{w(r^{\prime})}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}s_{r}(x_{n})e^{-2\pi i(x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}h(\xi))}f(x^{\prime},x_{n})dx^{\prime}dx_{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}s_{r}(y_{n})e^{2\pi i(y^{\prime}\cdot\zeta+y_{n}h(\zeta))}\overline{g(y^{\prime},y_{n})}dy^{\prime}dy_{n}\bigg)|\xi-\zeta|^{2\ell-n+1}drdr^{\prime}d\xi d\zeta

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\int_{U\times U}e^{-2\pi i(x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}h(\xi))}e^{2\pi i(y^{\prime}\cdot\zeta+y_{n}h(\zeta))}\phi(\xi)\overline{\phi(\zeta)}|\xi-\zeta|^{2\ell-n+1}d\zeta d\xi\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(r)\sqrt{\pi}1_{x_{n}}(r)dr\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{w(r^{\prime})}\sqrt{\pi}1_{y_{n}}(r^{\prime})dr^{\prime}\bigg)f(x^{\prime},x_{n})\overline{g(y^{\prime},y_{n})}dxdy.

By denoting
W(x_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(r)\sqrt{\pi}1_{x_{n}}(r)dr
, the kernel of
S^{*}_{w}S_{w}
(in
\dot{H}^{-\ell}
) can be expressed as

K_{w}(x,y)=\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{-\ell,\Sigma}(x,y)W(x_{n})\overline{W(y_{n})},

where

\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{-\ell,\Sigma}(x,y)=\int_{U\times U}e^{-2\pi i(x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}h(\xi))}e^{2\pi i(y^{\prime}\cdot\zeta+y_{n}h(\zeta))}\phi(\xi)\overline{\phi(\zeta)}|\xi-\zeta|^{2\gamma-n+1}d\zeta d\xi.

For
\langle\phi S_{w}f,\phi S_{w}g\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}
, its kernel is

Kernel of

Assuming

\displaystyle\langle T(f)(\xi,\eta)\phi(\xi),T(g)(\xi,\eta)\phi(\xi)\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}_{\xi}H^{\tau}_{\eta}}

\displaystyle=4\pi^{2}C^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{U}\int_{U}(1+|r|)^{2\tau}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}s_{r}(x_{n})e^{-2\pi i(x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}h(\xi))}f(x^{\prime},x_{n})dx^{\prime}dx_{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}s_{r}(y_{n})e^{2\pi i(y^{\prime}\cdot\zeta+y_{n}h(\zeta))}\overline{g(y^{\prime},y_{n})}dy^{\prime}dy_{n}\bigg)\phi(\xi)\overline{\phi(\zeta)}|\xi-\zeta|^{2\gamma-n+1}drd\zeta d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{-\gamma,\tau}(x,y)f(x)g(y)dxdy,

where

K_{-\gamma,\tau}(x,y)=4\pi^{2}C^{\prime}\tau_{min}(x_{n},y_{n})\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{-\gamma,\Sigma}(x,y)

and

\tau_{min}(x_{n},y_{n})=\chi_{[0,\infty)}(x_{n}y_{n})\frac{(1+\min\{|x_{n}|,|y_{n}|\})^{1+2\tau}-1}{1+2\tau}.

When
\gamma=0
and
\tau=0
, one has

\langle T(f)(\xi,\eta)\phi(\xi),T(g)(\xi,\eta)\phi(\xi)\rangle_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\eta}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{0,0}(x,y)f(x)g(y)dxdy,

where

and

4.2
Main proof

Fix
1<p\leq\frac{2n+2}{n+5}
. To start with, we write

\displaystyle|\langle T(f)\phi,T(g)\phi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\eta}}|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}K(x,y)f(x^{\prime},x_{n})g(y^{\prime},y_{n})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}dx_{n}dy_{n}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\bigg[K(x,y)-(4\pi^{2})\frac{|x_{n}|+|y_{n}|}{2}\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{0,\Sigma}(x,y)\bigg]f(x^{\prime},x_{n})g(y^{\prime},y_{n})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}dx_{n}dy_{n}\bigg|

\displaystyle\quad+(4\pi^{2})\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\frac{|x_{n}|+|y_{n}|}{2}\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{0,\Sigma}(x,y)f(x^{\prime},x_{n})g(y^{\prime},y_{n})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}dx_{n}dy_{n}\bigg|

\displaystyle=:(I)+(II).

We first estimate
(I)
. Note that

\bigg|s_{min}(x_{n},y_{n})-\frac{|x_{n}|+|y_{n}|}{2}\bigg|\leq C|x_{n}-y_{n}|

because when
x_{n}y_{n}<0
we have
|x_{n}|+|y_{n}|=|x_{n}-y_{n}|
while
s_{min}(x_{n},y_{n})=0
. To proceed, we define the operator

\mathcal{T}(g)(x^{\prime},x_{n};y_{n}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{0,\Sigma}(x,y)g(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{-2\pi i(x_{n}-y_{n})|\xi|^{2}}|\phi(\xi)|^{2}\widehat{g})(x^{\prime})

for
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
. Note that the last equality holds because the kernel of
\mathcal{T}
is of convolution type (
i.e.

\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{0,\Sigma}(x,y)=\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{0,\Sigma}(x-y)
). Since
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
, one has

\|\mathcal{T}(g)(\cdot,x_{n};y_{n})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\lesssim_{\phi}(1+|x_{n}-y_{n}|)^{-(n-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}.

Then, using Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality with

, we have

\displaystyle|(I)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}|x_{n}-y_{n}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}|f(x^{\prime},x_{n})||\mathcal{T}[g(\cdot,y_{n})](x^{\prime})|dx^{\prime}dy_{n}dx_{n}

A version of
T

Let us consider the operator

T_{\alpha}f(\xi,\eta)=\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi,h(\xi)+\eta)-\widehat{f}(\xi,h(\xi))}{|\eta|^{\alpha}}.

We can express
T_{\alpha}f
in terms of
Tf
. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}f(\xi,\eta)=\frac{\eta}{|\eta|^{\alpha}}Tf(\xi,\eta).

When
\alpha=1
, it follows immediately from Corollary
1
that

\|T_{1}f\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\eta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)}.

The following examples show that it is impossible to have
L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\eta}
bound for
|\alpha-1|\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Example
.

Let
h(\xi)=|\xi|^{2}
. Consider
f(x)=e^{-|x|^{2}}
with
\alpha\geq\frac{3}{2}
. We will show that it is impossible to obtain
L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\eta}
. Note that

T_{\alpha}f(\xi,\eta)=\frac{e^{-|\xi|^{2}-(|\xi|^{2}+\eta)^{2}}-e^{-|\xi|^{2}-|\xi|^{4}}}{|\eta|^{\alpha}}=e^{-|\xi|^{2}-|\xi|^{4}}\frac{e^{-2\eta|\xi|^{2}-\eta^{2}}-1}{|\eta|^{\alpha}}

When
|\eta|<\frac{|\xi|}{1000}
, then

\displaystyle|T_{\alpha}f(\xi,\eta)|\geq e^{-|\xi|^{2}-|\xi|^{4}}\frac{c|\xi|^{2}e^{-c^{\prime}|\xi|^{4}}}{|\eta|^{\alpha-1}}

(5.1)

for some
c,c^{\prime}>0
. When we square (
5.1
) both sides and integrate it with respect to
\eta\in(-\frac{|\xi|}{1000},\frac{|\xi|}{1000})
, the integral will diverge because
2\alpha-2\in[1,\infty)
.

On the other hand, let us consider
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
with the same function
f
. If
\eta
is sufficiently large so that
e^{-2\eta|\xi|^{2}-\eta^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then

\displaystyle|T_{\alpha}f(\xi,\eta)|\geq e^{-|\xi|^{2}-|\xi|^{4}}\frac{1}{2|\eta|^{\alpha}}

(5.2)

After squaring and integrating (
5.2
) with respect to
\eta
in that range, this integral will diverge as
2\alpha\leq 1.

Now we focus on the case
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})
. Let us compute the kernel of
T_{\alpha}^{*}T_{\alpha}
on
L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\tau}
.

\langle T_{\alpha}(f)(\xi,\eta)\phi(\xi),T_{\alpha}(g)(\xi,\eta)\phi(\xi)\rangle_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\eta}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}K^{\alpha}_{0,0}(x,y)f(x)g(y)dxdy,

where

K^{\alpha}_{0,0}(x,y)=I(x_{n},y_{n})\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{0,\Sigma}(x,y)

and

I(x_{n},y_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(e^{-2\pi ix_{n}\eta}-1)(e^{2\pi iy_{n}\eta}-1)}{|\eta|^{2\alpha}}d\eta.

By considering the principal value of
I(x_{n},y_{n})
and using change of variables,

\displaystyle I(x_{n},y_{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\cos(2\pi(x_{n}-y_{n})\eta)-\cos(2\pi y_{n}\eta)-\cos(2\pi x_{n}\eta)+1}{|\eta|^{2\alpha}}d\eta

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}\big[|x_{n}|^{2\alpha-2}x_{n}+|y_{n}|^{2\alpha-2}y_{n}-|x_{n}-y_{n}|^{2\alpha-2}(x_{n}-y_{n})\big],

where
C_{\alpha}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-\cos(2\pi t)}{|t|^{2\alpha}}dt<\infty
as
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}).

Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle\langle T_{\alpha}(f)(\xi,\eta)\phi(\xi),T_{\alpha}(g)(\xi,\eta)\phi(\xi)\rangle_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\eta}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}C_{\alpha}\big[|x_{n}|^{2\alpha-2}x_{n}+|y_{n}|^{2\alpha-2}y_{n}]\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{0,\Sigma}(x,y)f(x)g(y)dxdy

\displaystyle\quad-C_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}[|x_{n}-y_{n}|^{2\alpha-2}(x_{n}-y_{n})]\widecheck{\phi^{2}}_{0,\Sigma}(x,y)f(x)g(y)dxdy

\displaystyle=:(I)+(II).

Using the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, for
1\leq p\leq\frac{2n+2}{n+4\alpha+1}
, we have

|(II)|\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

To estimate
(I)
, it suffices to consider the case for
x_{n}
. Indeed, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x_{n}|^{2\alpha-2}x_{n}f(x)g(y)dy

\displaystyle\simeq\langle S_{w(r)r^{2\alpha-2}}f\phi,\widehat{g}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)},

where
w(r)=i(\sgn(r))^{2\alpha-3}
. Using Equation (
3.3
) with
\beta=\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x_{n}|^{2\alpha-2}x_{n}f(x)g(y)dy\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)})\|\phi\widehat{g}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)},

for the case
\ell\geq\frac{\kappa_{p}(2\alpha-1)}{2\kappa_{p}-2\alpha+1}
and
1<p\leq\frac{2n+2}{n+4\alpha+1}
or the case
\ell>\frac{\kappa_{p}(2\alpha-1)}{2\kappa_{p}-2\alpha+1}
and
p=1
.

In summary, we have

Theorem 8
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})
. Suppose
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has
\widehat{f}|_{\Sigma}\in H^{\ell}(\Sigma)
, and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
. If
\ell\geq\frac{\kappa_{p}(2\alpha-1)}{2\kappa_{p}-2\alpha+1}
and
1<p\leq\frac{2n+2}{n+4\alpha+1}
, then

\|\phi T_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\eta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\phi\widehat{f}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Sigma)}.

The estimate is also true for
p=1
if
\ell>\frac{\kappa_{1}(2\alpha-1)}{2\kappa_{1}-2\alpha+1}=\frac{(n-1)(2\alpha-1)}{2(n-2\alpha)}.

Five‐Year Outcomes With Delandistrogene Moxeparvovec in Patients With Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy: A Phase 1/2a Study
Authors: Jerry R. Mendell, Zarife Sahenk, Linda P. Lowes, Megan A. Iammarino, Lindsay N. Alfano, Craig M. McDonald, James E. Signorovitch, Jim Jin, Jing Li, Stefanie Mason, Louise R. Rodino‐Klapac
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimsWe report 5‐year results from a phase 1/2a study of delandistrogene moxeparvovec, a recombinant adeno‐associated virus serotype rh74 vector‐based gene therapy for Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), with post hoc analyses contextualizing functional outcomes.MethodsFour ambulatory patients with DMD (≥ 4–< 8 years at enrollment) entered an open‐label trial (Study 101; NCT03375164), receiving a single intravenous dose of delandistrogene moxeparvovec (2.0 × 1014 vg/kg by supercoiled quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), equivalent to 1.33 × 1014 vg/kg by linear qPCR). Safety and functional outcomes (including North Star Ambulatory Assessment [NSAA] total score, time to rise [TTR] from floor, 10‐m walk/run [10MWR] time) were assessed. In post hoc analyses, functional outcomes of delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients were compared with propensity‐score‐weighted external controls (ECs) and natural history predictions.ResultsNo new safety signals were reported 5 years post‐infusion. One patient had cardiomyopathy at year 5; however, this event was deemed unrelated to treatment. All delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients (mean age, 10.2 years) remained ambulant. The 5‐year NSAA total score mean change from baseline (standard deviation) for treated patients versus ECs was +7.5 (2.4) versus −3.9 (2.9) (least‐squares mean between‐group difference [standard error]: 9.8 [3.5], p = 0.0127). TTR and 10MWR mean times were stable, representing clinically meaningful differences versus ECs. An increased divergence in NSAA total score from 5‐year natural history predictions favoring gene therapy was also seen.DiscussionFindings support the long‐term, manageable safety profile of delandistrogene moxeparvovec in ambulatory patients with appropriate monitoring and demonstrate stabilization or delayed disease progression with treatment compared with matched untreated ECs.

Introduction

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is an X‐linked disease caused by pathogenic variants in the DMD gene, resulting in an absence of functional dystrophin [1]. Although the clinical course varies [2, 3], DMD is ultimately fatal [1]. Progressive muscular damage, present from birth, and degeneration lead to muscular weakness, loss of ambulation (LOA), and life‐threatening respiratory and cardiac impairment [1]. Common treatments include long‐term corticosteroids to delay LOA and pulmonary and cardiac symptom onset and treating symptoms as they occur [1, 4]. Newer approved therapies include dystrophin restoration, as with exon‐skipping antisense oligonucleotides and gene therapies, to promote production of functional dystrophin, as well as small molecule agents [1].

Delandistrogene moxeparvovec is a recombinant adeno‐associated virus serotype rh74 (rAAVrh74) vector‐based gene transfer therapy that delivers a transgene encoding micro‐dystrophin, an engineered, functional form of dystrophin that stabilizes or slows disease progression in DMD [5, 6, 7]. The US Food and Drug Administration has approved delandistrogene moxeparvovec for ambulatory patients ≥ 4 years of age with DMD and a confirmed mutation in the DMD gene. It is also approved in other select countries [8].

Early‐phase and phase 3 clinical studies demonstrated a manageable safety profile for delandistrogene moxeparvovec with robust micro‐dystrophin expression up to 64 weeks post‐treatment [7, 8, 9, 10, 11]. In the phase 1/2a open‐label Study 101 (SRP‐9001‐101; NCT03375164), delandistrogene moxeparvovec was well tolerated in ambulatory patients ≥ 4–< 8 years old [6] while improving North Star Ambulatory Assessment (NSAA) scores and other functional outcomes 1‐year post‐treatment [6] and through 4 years of follow‐up [7]. Here, we provide a 5‐year (end‐of‐study) update of Study 101 outcomes. To contextualize functional outcomes, post hoc analyses were performed comparing 5‐year Study 101 data with both propensity‐score‐weighted external controls (ECs) and natural history predictive controls. Patients in Study 101 subsequently entered a longer‐term follow‐up study: Study 305 (NCT05967351).

Methods

Study Design and Participants

Study details were previously reported [6]. Briefly, patients received one 2.0 × 1014 vg/kg IV infusion of delandistrogene moxeparvovec in the peripheral limb vein, determined by the supercoiled quantitative polymerase chain reaction method (equivalent to 1.33 × 1014 vg/kg by linear quantitative polymerase chain reaction) [9]. Patients were enrolled at Nationwide Children's Hospital in Columbus, Ohio, USA, between November 2017 and April 2018. Five‐year follow‐up was completed April 25, 2023. The trial was approved by the institutional review board of the hospital. Participants' parents signed informed consent in compliance with the Code of Federal Regulations (Title 21, Part 50) and International Conference on Harmonization guidelines.

Study Outcomes and Assessments

Primary outcome was safety assessed by adverse events (AEs; characterized by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v4.0), changes in laboratory parameters, immunologic response to rAAVrh74 and micro‐dystrophin, and reported history/observations of symptoms. Key physical function exploratory outcomes were change from baseline in NSAA total score and timed function tests, including time to rise (TTR) from floor, 10‐m walk/run (10MWR) time, mean time to ascend 4 steps, and 100‐m walk/run time.

Statistical Analysis

Demographic characteristics were summarized with descriptive statistics using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). In the functional outcomes post hoc analysis, delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients at 5 years were compared with a propensity‐score‐weighted EC cohort at 4.5 years. The EC cohort comprised patients from the Finding the Optimum Regimen for DMD (FOR‐DMD) study (NCT01603407), comparing three DMD corticosteroid regimens, with 5 years of follow‐up data [12]. To ensure the EC cohort was comparable with this study's cohort, the following entry criteria were applied as described previously [7]: age at baseline between 4 and 8 years, inclusive, being on a stable dose or dose equivalent of corticosteroids for ≥ 12 weeks before baseline (patients on 10 day on/10 day off regime were excluded), NSAA score ≥ 13 and ≤ 30 at baseline (minimum and maximum values at the time of dosing for treated patients), TTR ≤ 10.4 s at baseline (the maximum value at the time of dosing for treated patients), 10MWR ≤ 9.1 s at baseline (the maximum value at the time of dosing for treated patients). To match Study 101 inclusion criteria of ≥ 12 weeks of stable‐dose corticosteroids pre‐baseline, the first 6 months of FOR‐DMD data were treated as pre‐baseline. This re‐established month 6 as baseline and ensured comparable steroid use history with Study 101, reducing follow‐up time to 4.5 years. FOR‐DMD study data at 4.5 years were used to contextualize data observed at 5 years in Study 101 [7]. Propensity‐score weighting incorporated baseline factors known to be prognostic of functional outcomes in DMD—age, NSAA total score, TTR from floor, 10MWR time—to ensure baseline comparability between delandistrogene moxeparvovec and EC groups [7].

An imputation method was applied for patients with LOA as they could not complete functional assessments. LOA was confirmed if a patient's NSAA walk score was 0 or they were unable to complete the 10MWR or 10MWR in ≥ 30 s. For patients unable to rise from the floor, TTR was imputed as 30 s. For patients with LOA, 10MWR time was imputed as 30 s and NSAA total score as 0.

An additional post hoc analysis, generating predicted controls from a pre‐existing prediction model (collaborative Trajectory Analysis Project [cTAP]), further contextualized NSAA outcomes in delandistrogene moxeparvovec trials. This model was based on the same natural history data sources as those used in the EC analyses and comprised 261 subjects in total (208 for model development and 53 for validation). The model, which included a broad set of baseline prognostic factors—age, height, weight, body mass index, steroid use, NSAA total score, TTR velocity, and 10MWR velocity—was applied to the baseline profiles of treated patients to generate individualized predicted changes in NSAA from baseline to week 52. The average of these predictions and associated standard error (SE) represents expected NSAA change that would have occurred without delandistrogene moxeparvovec treatment and thus serves as a predicted control group.

Results

Demographics and Clinical Characteristics

Baseline demographics and clinical characteristics of delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients (N = 4) were published previously [6]. At 5 years post‐infusion, mean (range) age of delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients was 10.1 (9.0–11.0) years (Table 1). Baseline characteristics for the EC (N = 17) and delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated groups were comparable (Table 2). As described previously in the 1‐year follow‐up report, all patients were on stable weekend dosing of prednisolone for at least 12 weeks prior to screening (range at study entry: 3.5–4.6 mg/kg) and received a weekday dose of 1 mg/kg of prednisolone in addition to their regular weekend dosing for approximately 30 days post‐infusion [6]. Patients were then tapered off of prophylactic weekday dosing over approximately 2 weeks. Patients were on weekend only dosing for the majority of the study; a summary of weekend corticosteroid dosing maintained over the course of the study is provided in Table S1. The mean (standard deviation [SD]) daily corticosteroid doses were: 20.9 (2.5) mg for the 12‐week period prior to informed consent; 32.0 (3.8) mg from post‐treatment to day 30; 23.5 (3.6) mg from day 31 to 1 year; and 21.8 (3.0) mg from 1 to 5 years. Over the 5‐year follow‐up period, patients did not receive any other dystrophin restoration therapies or participate in other interventional trials.

Safety

At 5 years, 74 treatment‐emergent AEs (TEAEs) had occurred post‐baseline. All TEAEs were mild or moderate and none were clinically significant complement‐mediated AEs. Most TEAEs (n = 40, 54.1%) occurred within 90 days post‐infusion.

Echocardiogram findings over 5 years showed that left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was within normal ranges for 3 patients (Figure S1). One subject had a TEAE of cardiomyopathy with LVEF < 55% in year 5. The event was considered by the Investigator to be mild in severity and unrelated to study treatment.

Functional Outcomes

Delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients experienced sustained increases in NSAA total scores over 5 years versus the EC cohort (Figure 1A,B), resulting in a significant difference between groups (Figure 1C). All delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients remained ambulant throughout the study, while 4 patients in the EC cohort experienced LOA between 8.4 and 11.6 years of age (18–54 months post‐adjusted baseline).

Discussion

Five years post‐treatment with delandistrogene moxeparvovec, patients aged 9–11 years remained ambulant and continued to demonstrate durable and clinically meaningful improvements in motor function based on functional outcomes, while the trajectories of the matched EC cohort declined over time, with some patients experiencing loss of ambulation. In young patients with DMD starting steroids, time to functional decline below baseline is clinically meaningful. FOR‐DMD patients aged ≥ 4–< 8 years old initiating daily steroids experienced functional decline nearing, and in some cases below, baseline NSAA scores by 36 months [12]. Notably, no delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients in the present study declined below baseline NSAA scores at 5 years' follow‐up. At 5 years post‐infusion, mean TTR of delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients remained below the 5‐s threshold, a prognostic marker of functional decline, whereas the EC group exceeded this threshold [13]. Furthermore, all delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients remained well below the 10‐s threshold on the 10MWR, a predictor for elevated risk of ambulation loss within 24 months, whereas the EC group approached this threshold [14].

Over the 5‐year follow‐up, no new treatment‐related safety signals were reported, with most AEs occurring during the initial 90 days post‐infusion, consistent with previous clinical trials. One patient had cardiomyopathy at year 5; however, this event was considered to be unrelated to treatment. Together, these results demonstrate delandistrogene moxeparvovec's manageable safety profile in ambulatory patients with appropriate monitoring [7, 8, 9, 10, 15].

These long‐term outcomes support the biological role of functional dystrophin and indicate delandistrogene moxeparvovec stabilizes or slows DMD disease progression, with increasing divergence from DMD's natural history over time. Statistically significant and clinically meaningful differences in NSAA total score versus the EC cohort after 5 years are consistent with 4‐year outcomes, where the difference in least‐squares mean (SE) change from baseline in NSAA total score was 9.4 (3.4) across treatment groups (p = 0.0125). These outcomes are also consistent with 2‐year EMBARK (SRP‐9001‐301) data, in which least‐squares mean change difference favored delandistrogene moxeparvovec–treated patients (N = 64) versus the EC cohort (N = 143) for NSAA total score (p = 0.0001) and TTR (p = 0.0033) [11]. 10MWR time similarly favored delandistrogene moxeparvovec in the 4‐year data and 2‐year EMBARK outcomes. NSAA total score findings were further corroborated using independent model‐based cTAP indicating the increasing divergence in disease progression from natural history over time.

Limitations of this analysis include the small sample size of the study population and the use of an open‐label, single‐center study design. Although generation of the EC cohort used established methods to match natural history patients to treatment cohorts [11, 16], potential limitations of this approach may include selection bias and the influence of confounding variables.

In summary, this study provides the longest follow‐up of delandistrogene moxeparvovec in ambulatory patients and demonstrates a durable treatment effect and slowing of disease progression with increasing divergence from natural history.

Renormalization of general Effective Field Theories: Renormalization of fermionic operators
Renato M. Fonseca, Pablo Olgoso, José Santiago

Abstract
Renormalization group equations play a central role in effective field theories, both maintaining perturbative control and allowing one to determine the correct low-energy phenomenology. In this work, we complete the one-loop renormalization of the recently developed general effective field theory up to mass dimension six by providing the beta functions for physical fermionic operators. Together with Ref. [ 24 ] , our results allow one to renormalize any effective theory up to dimension six at one loop using only a group-theoretical calculation.

Introduction

Effective Field Theories (EFTs) are ubiquitous in the study of physical systems with separate scales. In quantum field theory, the large logarithms stemming from the ratio of these scales in quantum corrections can spoil the use of perturbation theory unless they are re-summed by means of the renormalization group equations (RGEs). 2
The general effective Lagrangian to mass-dimension 6

The most general EFT up to mass dimension 6 was defined, both on-shell (physical basis) and off-shell (Green’s basis) in a recent work
[
24
]
, together with the one-loop renormalization of the bosonic operators in
the physical basis (sourced by both bosonic and fermionic operators).
In this work we are interested in the renormalization of the fermionic operators but, for clarity, we reproduce here the complete basis.

Let us introduce our notation for the fields and symmetries defining our general EFT. We will consider a general gauge group
G
, which can be any compact Lie group, with structure constants
f^{ABC}
and gauge bosons
V_{\mu}^{A}
. The field strength tensor reads

F^{A}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}V^{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}V^{A}_{\mu}+g_{A}f^{ABC}V^{B}_{\mu}V^{C}_{\nu},

(1)

and its covariant derivative

D_{\rho}F^{A}_{\mu\nu}\equiv(D_{\rho}F_{\mu\nu})^{A}=\partial_{\rho}F^{A}_{\mu\nu}+g_{A}f^{ABC}V^{B}_{\rho}F^{C}_{\mu\nu}.

(2)

All fermions — taken to be left-handed (LH) — can be collected in a single vector
\psi_{i}
which transforms under some representation of
G
, which is in general reducible. Likewise, all scalars
\phi_{i}
are considered real and grouped in a single (potentially reducible) representation of
G
. Our convention for the covariant derivatives applied to these fields reads

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\psi_{i}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\psi_{i}-\mathrm{i}g_{A}t^{A}_{ij}V^{A}_{\mu}\psi_{j},

(3)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\phi_{a}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\phi_{a}-\mathrm{i}g_{A}\theta^{A}_{ab}V^{A}_{\mu}\phi_{b}.

(4)

Given that gauge transformations preserve the norm of fields, both the
t^{A}
and the
\theta^{A}
matrices must be hermitian. Additionally, since we are working with real scalars, the
\theta^{A}
must be purely imaginary and therefore anti-symmetric.

The renormalizable Lagrangian reads

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{d\leq 4}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}(a_{KF})_{AB}F^{A}_{\mu\nu}F^{B\,\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}(a_{K\phi})_{ab}D_{\mu}\phi_{a}D^{\mu}\phi_{b}+(a_{K\psi})_{ij}\bar{\psi}_{i}\mathrm{i}\not{D}\psi_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\Big[(m_{\psi})_{ij}\psi^{T}_{i}C\psi_{j}+\mathrm{h.c.}\Big]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(m_{\phi}^{2})_{ab}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}-\eta_{a}\phi_{a}-\frac{1}{2}\Big[Y_{ija}\psi^{T}_{i}C\psi_{j}+\mathrm{h.c.}\Big]\phi_{a}-\frac{\kappa_{abc}}{3!}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}\phi_{c}-\frac{\lambda_{abcd}}{4!}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}\phi_{c}\phi_{d},

(5)

where
C
is the charge conjugation matrix.

At mass-dimension 5 we have,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{5}^{\mathrm{phys}}

\displaystyle=\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\big(a^{(5)}_{\psi F}\big)_{Aij}\psi_{i}^{T}C\sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{j}F^{A}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{4}\big(a^{(5)}_{\psi\phi^{2}}\big)_{ijab}\psi_{i}^{T}C\psi_{j}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\big(a^{(5)}_{\phi F}\big)_{ABa}F^{A\,\mu\nu}F^{B}_{\mu\nu}\phi_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\big(a^{(5)}_{\phi\widetilde{F}}\big)_{ABa}F^{A\,\mu\nu}\widetilde{F}^{B}_{\mu\nu}\phi_{a}+\frac{1}{5!}\big(a^{(5)}_{\phi}\big)_{abcde}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}\phi_{c}\phi_{d}\phi_{e},

(6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{5}^{\mathrm{red}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(r^{(5)}_{\phi\Box}\big)_{abc}(D_{\mu}D^{\mu}\phi_{a})\phi_{b}\phi_{c}+\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\big(r^{(5)}_{\psi}\big)_{ij}(D_{\mu}\psi_{i})^{T}CD^{\mu}\psi_{j}+\big(r^{(5)}_{\psi\phi}\big)_{ija}\bar{\psi}_{i}\mathrm{i}\not{D}\psi_{j}\phi_{a}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigg],

(7)

where we have used the standard definition:

\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}].

(8)

At mass-dimension 6 we have:

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{6}^{\mathrm{phys}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3!}\big(a^{(6)}_{3F}\big)_{ABC}(F^{A})^{\penalty\ \nu}_{\mu}(F^{B})^{\penalty\ \rho}_{\nu}(F^{C})^{\penalty\ \mu}_{\rho}+\frac{1}{3!}\big(a^{(6)}_{3\widetilde{F}}\big)_{ABC}(F^{A})^{\penalty\ \nu}_{\mu}(F^{B})^{\penalty\ \rho}_{\nu}(\widetilde{F}^{C})^{\penalty\ \mu}_{\rho}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\big(a^{(6)}_{\phi F}\big)_{ABab}F^{A}_{\mu\nu}F^{B\,\mu\nu}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}+\frac{1}{4}\big(a^{(6)}_{\phi\widetilde{F}}\big)_{ABab}F^{A}_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{F}^{B\,\mu\nu}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}\big(a^{(6)}_{\phi D}\big)_{abcd}\left[(D_{\mu}\phi_{a})(D^{\mu}\phi_{b})\phi_{c}\phi_{d}+\left(ab\leftrightarrow cd\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(a\leftrightarrow c\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(a\leftrightarrow d\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(b\leftrightarrow c\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(b\leftrightarrow d\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6!}\big(a^{(6)}_{\phi}\big)_{abcdef}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}\phi_{c}\phi_{d}\phi_{e}\phi_{f}+\frac{1}{2}\big(a^{(6)}_{\phi\psi}\big)_{ijab}\bar{\psi}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{j}[\phi_{a}D_{\mu}\phi_{b}-\phi_{b}D_{\mu}\phi_{a}]+\frac{1}{4}\big(a^{(6)}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\big)_{ijkl}(\bar{\psi}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{j})(\bar{\psi}_{k}\gamma_{\mu}\psi_{l})

\displaystyle+\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\big(a^{(6)}_{\psi F}\big)_{Aija}F^{A}_{\mu\nu}\psi_{i}^{T}C\sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{j}\phi_{a}+\frac{1}{2!3!}\big(a^{(6)}_{\psi\phi}\big)_{ijabc}\psi_{i}^{T}C\psi_{j}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}\phi_{c}+\frac{1}{4!}\big(a^{(6)}_{\psi\psi}\big)_{ijkl}(\psi_{i}^{T}C\psi_{j})(\psi_{k}^{T}C\psi_{l})+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigg],

(9)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{6}^{\textrm{red}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2!}\big(r^{(6)}_{2F}\big)_{AB}(D_{\mu}F^{A\,\mu\nu})(D^{\rho}F^{B}_{\rho\nu})+\frac{1}{2!}\big(r^{(6)}_{FD\phi}\big)_{Aab}\left(D_{\nu}F^{A,\mu\nu}\right)\left[\left(D_{\mu}\phi_{a}\right)\phi_{b}-\left(a\leftrightarrow b\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2!}\big(r^{(6)}_{D\phi}\big)_{ab}\left(D_{\mu}D^{\mu}\phi_{a}\right)(D_{\nu}D^{\nu}\phi_{b})+\frac{1}{3!}\big(r^{(6)}_{\phi D}\big)_{abcd}\left(D_{\mu}D^{\mu}\phi_{a}\right)\phi_{b}\phi_{c}\phi_{d}

\displaystyle+\big(r^{(6)}_{DF\psi}\big)_{Aij}D^{\nu}F_{\mu\nu}^{A}\overline{\psi_{i}}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{j}+\big(r^{(6)}_{F\psi}\big)_{Aij}F_{\mu\nu}^{A}\overline{\psi_{i}}\gamma^{\mu}\mathrm{i}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\nu}\psi_{j}+\big(r^{(6)}_{\widetilde{F}\psi}\big)_{Aij}\widetilde{F}_{\mu\nu}^{A}\overline{\psi_{i}}\gamma^{\mu}\mathrm{i}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\nu}\psi_{j}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2!}\big(r^{(6)}_{\phi\psi 1}\big)_{ijab}\left(\bar{\psi}_{i}i\overleftrightarrow{\not{D}}\psi_{j}\right)\phi_{a}\phi_{b}+\frac{1}{2!}\big(r^{(6)}_{\phi\psi 2}\big)_{ijab}\overline{\psi}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{j}D_{\mu}\left(\phi_{a}\phi_{b}\right)+\big(r^{(6)}_{\psi D}\big)_{ij}i\overline{\psi}_{i}\{D_{\mu}D^{\mu},\not{D}\}\psi_{j}

\displaystyle+\left\{\frac{1}{2!}\big(r^{(6)}_{\psi\phi D1}\big)_{ija}\psi_{i}^{T}C\psi_{j}\left(D_{\mu}D^{\mu}\phi_{a}\right)+\frac{1}{2!}\big(r^{(6)}_{\psi\phi D2}\big)_{ija}\left(D^{\mu}\psi_{i}\right)^{T}Ci\sigma^{\mu\nu}\left(D_{\nu}\psi_{j}\right)\phi_{a}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{1}{2!}\big(r^{(6)}_{\psi\phi D3}\big)_{ija}\left(D_{\mu}D^{\mu}\psi_{i}\right)^{T}C\psi_{j}\phi_{a}+\textrm{h.c.}\right\}.

(10)

Finally, the following operators are redundant only in
d=4
, and consequently induce evanescent effects:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(6)}_{\mathrm{ev}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(r_{\psi\overline{\psi}}^{(6)})_{ijkl}(\psi_{i}^{T}C\psi_{j})(\overline{\psi}_{k}C\overline{\psi}^{T}_{l})+\frac{1}{4}(r_{\psi\overline{\psi}2}^{(6)})_{ijkl}(\psi_{i}^{T}C\sigma_{\mu\nu}\psi_{j})(\overline{\psi}_{k}\sigma^{\mu\nu}C\overline{\psi}^{T}_{l})

(11)

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1}{4!}(r_{\psi\psi 2}^{(6)})_{ijkl}(\psi_{i}^{T}C\sigma_{\mu\nu}\psi_{j})(\psi_{k}^{T}C\sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{l})+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigg).

(12)

These effects, however, do not modify the one-loop renormalization so we will not take them into account.

Further details, including the symmetry properties of the different WCs can be found in
[
24
]
.
In the following, whenever possible we will keep the fermionic indices implicit preserving the correct order in the matrix multiplication. Also the dagger symbol will include the transposition of fermionic indices.

3
Results

3.1
General formalism

For completeness, in this section we will summarize the procedure that we follow to obtain the beta functions of the general EFT. For further details, we refer to Appendix A of Ref.
[
24
]
.

Our starting point will be the physical basis of operators defined in Section
2
as a boundary condition in the UV, that schematically reads:

\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{UV}}=\sum_{i}a_{i}\mathcal{O}_{i},

(13)

where
a_{i}
denotes the Wilson coefficient of the physical operator
\mathcal{O}_{i}
.
This Lagrangian will induce UV divergences that we regularize using Dimensional Regularization, working in
d=4-2\epsilon
dimensions. For the calculation of these divergences we use background field method
[
1
]
, and since it is performed off-shell, they are parametrized by the full (including non-trivial kinetic terms and redundant operators) EFT Lagrangian. These divergent Wilson Coefficients for the kinetic terms and bosonic operators were reported in Section 4.2 and Appendix B of Ref.
[
24
]
, and in this work we provide the ones for fermionic operators in Appendix
A
.
These results have been obtained using
Matchmakereft

[
11
]
, implementing a general model and several specific toy models (whose WCs were computed with the help of
GroupMath

[
25
]
).
The reduction to the physical basis, implemented by the shifts provided in Ref.
[
24
]
, involves the off-shell divergences of some bosonic WCs. Therefore, to reproduce our results, some of the expressions from Ref.
[
24
]
are needed.

After canonical normalization and reduction to the physical basis, the divergent Lagrangian reads,

\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{div}}=\sum_{j}a^{\prime}_{j}(a)\penalty\ \mathcal{O}_{j},

(14)

where we have denoted with a prime the WCs that parameterize the UV divergences (which are a function of the WCs in the UV Lagrangian, as explicitly shown).
These divergences can be canceled by splitting the WCs into renormalized WCs and counterterms:

a_{i}^{\mathrm{bare}}=\mu^{(n_{i}-2)\epsilon}(a_{i}+\delta a_{i}),

(15)

where the scale
\mu
is introduced to ensure that the renormalized WCs in
d
dimensions have the same mass dimension as the bare ones in
d=4
. This fixes
n_{i}
to the number of fields appearing in the operator
\mathcal{O}_{i}
.
Since we have canonically normalized the divergent Lagrangian in Eq.(
13
), we then have:

\delta a_{i}=-a_{i}^{\prime}.

(16)

The corresponding counterterms fix the renormalization scale dependence of the renormalized WCs. At one loop they read:

\beta_{i}\equiv\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}a_{i}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}=-2a_{i}^{\prime}.

(17)

We will report below the results in the form:

\dot{a}_{i}\equiv 16\pi^{2}\beta_{i}.

(18)

4
Conclusions

Effective field theories are widely adopted in particle physics, in particular in the context of physics beyond the Standard Model. Through the process of matching and running, the low-energy effects of heavy degrees of freedom are incorporated in a well-organized perturbative expansion.
This is particularly efficient for computing the implications of new heavy-physics models, especially since one can split the calculation into two independent parts, i.e. the bottom-up and top-down approaches.

Despite these processes being fully automated at one loop, the need to repeat the calculations for each different model compromises the efficiency of the EFT approach and makes it prone to errors.
In a previous work
[
24
]
, we presented an alternative solution based on computing the one-loop beta functions for a general effective Lagrangian, so that it encompasses any specific example and can be used after only a straightforward group-theoretical calculation.
These beta functions were however calculated only for bosonic operators.

In this follow-up work, we computed the beta functions for fermionic operators, completing the one-loop renormalization of the general effective Lagrangian up to dimension six.
Following the procedure introduced in
[
24
]
,
we derived the off-shell, one-loop divergences of the theory using the background field method, which we parametrize in a Green’s basis of operators. Upon canonical normalization, we project the result onto a physical basis and provide the beta functions for all physical operators.

These new results for fermionic contributions have been cross-checked with the leptonic sector of the SMEFT and the ALP EFT, as well as with several different toy models.
In addition to being applicable to renormalize any EFT at one loop up to dimension six,
these results also set the ground for extending the general renormalization program, both in the direction of higher loop order or operator dimension.

Abstract
. We develop an alternative approach to the study of Fourier series, based on the Short-Time-Fourier Transform (STFT) acting on L ν 2 ​ ( 0 , 1 ) L_{\nu}^{2}(0,1) , the space of measurable functions f f in ℝ \mathbb{R} , square-integrable in ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) , and time-periodic up to a phase factor : for fixed ν ∈ ℝ \nu\in\mathbb{R} , f ⁡ ( t + k ) = e 2 ​ π ​ i ​ k ​ ν ​ f ​ ( t ) ​ , ​ k ∈ ℤ ​ . f(t+k)=e^{2\pi ik\nu}f(t)\text{, \ }k\in\mathbb{Z}\text{.} The resulting phase space is the vertical strip ℂ / ℤ = [ 0 , 1 ) × ℝ \mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}=[0,1)\times\mathbb{R} , a flat model of an infinite cylinder, which leads to Gabor frames with an interesting structure theory, allowing a Janssen-type representation. As expected, a Gaussian window leads to a Fock space of entire functions, studied in the companion paper by the same authors [ Beurling-type density theorems for sampling and interpolation on the flat cylinder ]. When g g is a Hermite function, we are lead to true Fock spaces of polyanalytic functions ( Landau Level eigenspaces) on the vertical strip [ 0 , 1 ) × ℝ [0,1)\times\mathbb{R} . We first prove a density conditions for interpolating in this space. Furthermore, an analogue of the sufficient Wexler-Raz conditions is obtained. This leads to a new criteria for Gabor frames in L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) , to sufficient conditions for Gabor frames in L ν 2 ​ ( 0 , 1 ) L_{\nu}^{2}(0,1) with Hermite windows (an analogue of a theorem of Gröchenig and Lyubarskii about Gabor frames with Hermite windows) and with totally positive windows in the Feichtinger algebra (an analogue of a recent theorem of Gröchenig). We also consider a vectorial STFT in L ν 2 ​ ( 0 , 1 ) L_{\nu}^{2}(0,1) and, using the vector with the first Hermite functions as window, we introduce the (full) Fock spaces of polyanalytic functions on [ 0 , 1 ) × ℝ [0,1)\times\mathbb{R} , their associated Bargmann-type transforms, and prove an analogue of Vasilevski’s orthogonal decomposition into true polyanalytic Fock spaces (Landau level eigenspaces on [ 0 , 1 ) × ℝ [0,1)\times\mathbb{R} ). We conclude the paper with an analogue of Gröchenig-Lyubarskii’s sufficient condition for Gabor super-frames with Hermite functions, which is equivalent to a sufficient sampling condition on the full Fock space of polyanalytic functions on [ 0 , 1 ) × ℝ [0,1)\times\mathbb{R} . The proofs of the results about Gabor frames, involving some of Gröchenig’s most significant results of the last 20 years, provide a live indicator of his influence on the field during this period.

Introduction

The theory to be developed in this paper can be understood as a study of the
properties of time-frequency representations of classical Fourier series.
Given
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, the functions that we will consider on the
‘signal side’ can be written as the Fourier series

f(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}e^{2\pi it(\nu+k)}\text{,}

and their STFT transform
V_{g}
(this is defined in the next section; see
also Proposition
2
(3)) can be written as the
orthonormal expansion

(1.1)

V_{g}f(x,\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}e^{2i\pi x(\nu+k-\xi)}\mathcal{F}(\bar{g})(\xi-\nu-k)\text{,}

with convergence in
L^{2}([0,1)\times\mathbb{R})
. Since this expansion
lives in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, the convergence is
automatically uniform, in contrast with Fourier series. Moreover, point
evaluations of
V_{g}f(x,\xi)
are bounded if
(x,\xi)\in[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
, suggesting the possibility of recovery of
V_{g}f(x,\xi)
from its samples on a discrete subset of
(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
(e. g., the regular lattice
Z=i\beta\mathbb{Z}
). Given
g\in{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, one of our main goals is to obtain conditions on the
size of the parameter
\beta>0
such that the mentioned sampling recovery
can be done in a stable way. This problem will lead to a theory of
Gabor frames
adapted to this context. For
\nu=0
, (
1.1
) is
exactly the STFT windowed by
g
, of the Fourier series of
f
in the
time-frequency plane. As we will see in this paper and its companion
[
1
]
, this leads to time-frequency objects different from those generated
by general transforms in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, but with structural properties
reminiscent of the classical theory, which can be used in the study of the
difficult problem of determining the points which generate Gabor frames with
a given window
[
44
]
.

This paper and
[
1
]
are essentially dealing with the
L_{2}
theory of
the STFT on the flat cylinder. We are currently investigating
L_{p}

aspects of the theory, and will present in
[
2
]
a detailed study
of the modulation spaces,

1.1.
The Short-time Fourier transform

The
time-frequency shifts
of a function
f:{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
(in the ‘signal space’), consist of the
composition of the modulation operator, acting on
f
by
M_{\xi}f(t)=e^{2\pi i\xi t}f(t)
, with the translation operator, acting on
f
by
T_{x}f(t)=f(t-x)
, resulting in the representation coefficient:

\pi(x,\xi)f(t):=M_{\xi}T_{x}=e^{2\pi i\xi t}f(t-x),\qquad(x,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}^{2d}},t\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{,}

which defines a projective representation of
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\equiv\mathbb{C}^{d}}
acting on
{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with co-cycle
\sigma
given
by
\sigma((x,\xi),(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}))=e^{\pi i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}-x\xi)}
(here,
{\mathbb{C}^{d}}
is identified with
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
by the correspondence
(x,\xi)\leftrightarrow x+i\xi
). Since the
Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H=}{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\times\mathbb{R}
is a
central extension of
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
,
\pi
can be understood as the
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
pull back of Schrödinger’s representation of
\mathbb{H}
acting on
{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, defined by
[
36
, (9.16)]
:

\rho(x,\xi,\tau)g(t)=e^{2\pi i\tau}e^{\pi ix\xi}e^{2\pi it\xi}g(t-x)=e^{2\pi i\tau}e^{\pi ix\xi}\pi(x,\xi)g(t)\text{.}

Since
\rho
is an irreducible unitary representation of
\mathbb{H}

[
36
, Theorem 9.2.1]
, its
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
pull back
\pi
is also
unitary and irreducible.

Given
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the
short-time Fourier transform
(STFT)
with
window

g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
transforms a
function of a variable
t\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
into a function of two
variables
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
(time-frequency/phase space: as
physical quantities,
x
can represent time or position, while
\xi
can
represent frequency or momentum), defined as

(1.2)

V_{g}f(x,\xi)=\left\langle f,\pi(x,\xi)g\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi i\xi t}dt\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\text{.}

A fundamental property of the STFT is the Moyal formula: given
f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(1.3)

\left\langle V_{g_{1}}f_{1},V_{g_{2}}f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\left\langle f_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\langle g_{1},g_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\text{.}

Some important conventions will be used all over the paper. We will always
assume
\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=1
; this, combined with
Moyal’s formula (
1.3
), assures that the map

(1.4)

V_{g}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

is an isometry. For each
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the STFT map (
1.4
) defines a proper subspace of the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
This space is the so-called
Gabor space
[
4
]
, for which
the following notation will be used:

(1.5)

\mathcal{H}_{g}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2d}\right):=V_{g}\left(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)\text{.}

The Gabor space is a Hilbert space with reproducing kernel explicitly
given as

(1.6)

K_{g}((x,\xi),(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}))=\left\langle\pi(x,\xi)g,\pi(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})g\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=e^{-2\pi ix^{\prime}(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}V_{g}g\left((x-x^{\prime},\xi-\xi^{\prime})\right)\text{.}

For instance, if
g(t)=h_{0}(t)=2^{\frac{d}{4}}e^{-\pi t^{2}}
, then (
{\mathbb{C}^{d}}
is identified with
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
by the
correspondence
(x,\xi)\leftrightarrow x+i\xi=z

(1.7)

K_{h_{0}}(z,z^{\prime})=e^{i\pi(x\xi-x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime})}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}(\left|z\right|^{2}+\left|z^{\prime}\right|^{2})}e^{\pi z\overline{z^{\prime}}}\text{.}

To simplify the exposition, in the remaining of the paper we will set
d=1
.
In most algebraic arguments we can do this with no loss of generality, but
once analysis enters the picture, things can become quite complicated in
higher dimensions. For instance, choosing the window

can recognize the kernel (

have a point set structure only for

led to the characterization of sampling and interpolating sequences in Fock

,

,

as a characterization of Gabor frames and Riesz sequences for

[

]
,
with the first results on lattices obtained in
[

,

functions on
[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
.

2.1.
Almost periodic square integrable functions

Consider a lattice
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}\alpha
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, and
\Gamma
-quasi-periodic functions defined by the
functional equation

(2.1)

f(t+\gamma)=\chi_{\nu}(\gamma)f(t)\text{,}

where
\chi_{\nu}
is a character from
\Gamma
to the unit circle,
mapping a generic element
\gamma=n\alpha\in\Gamma
to
\chi_{\nu}(\gamma)=e^{2\pi ina\nu}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
. Without loss of
generality, we can set
\alpha=1
, then
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
and (
2.1
) is

(2.2)

f(t+n)=e^{2\pi in\nu}f(t)\text{, \ }n\in\mathbb{Z}\text{.}

The space of square integrable almost periodic functions defines the signals
to be transformed by the STFT:

{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\ \text{measurable with }\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}<\infty\text{,
verifying (\ref{functional})}\right\}\text{.}

From the definition, expanding
f\in L^{2}(0,1)
in Fourier series, we can
write a generic element
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
as

(2.3)

f(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}e^{2\pi it(\nu+k)}\text{{.}}

It follows that
\{e^{2\pi it(\nu+k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an
orthonormal basis of
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
. We will use the notation

(2.4)

e_{k,\nu}(t)=e^{2\pi it(\nu+k)}\text{.}

Recall that Feichtinger’s algebra
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
can be
defined as
[
23
,
22
,
16
,
36
]
:

\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}):=\left\{g\in{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}):V_{h_{0}}g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}\text{.}

For
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, one defines a periodization operator
by

(2.5)

\Sigma_{\nu}f(t):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi ik\nu}f(t-k)\text{.}

Proposition 1
.

\Sigma_{\nu}
is well defined as an operator with
dense range,

\Sigma_{\nu}:\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)\text{.}

Proof.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, by
[
36
, Proposition 12.1.4]

then
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
f\in W(\mathbb{R})
, where
W(\mathbb{R})

is the Wiener space such that

\left\|f\right\|_{W(\mathbb{R})}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}ess\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|f(t-k)\right|<\infty\text{.}

Using
[
36
, Lemma 6.1.2]
, if
f\in W(\mathbb{R})
then

\left|\Sigma_{\nu}f(t)\right|\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|f(t-k)\right|\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{W(\mathbb{R})}\text{.}

It follows that
\Sigma_{\nu}f(t)
is well defined for
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, since, by
[
36
, Lemma 6.1.2]
,

(2.6)

\left|\Sigma_{\nu}f(t)\right|\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{W(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})}\text{.}

Now, if
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, then
\Sigma_{\nu}f

\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, since (
2.6
) trivially implies that

(2.7)

\int\limits_{[0,1]}\left|\Sigma_{\nu}f(t)\right|^{2}dt\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})}

and

\Sigma_{\nu}f(t+n)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi ik\nu}f(t-k+n)=e^{2\pi in\nu}\Sigma_{\nu}f(t)\text{.}

We consider the functions

\varphi_{n}(x)=e^{2i\pi(n+\nu)x}\chi_{[0,1]}(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\text{.}

From
[
35
, Theorem 1 (a)]
we obtain the inequality:

\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{1}\leq C\left(\left\|(.)^{7}f\right\|_{2}+\left\|(.)^{7/4}\widehat{f}\right\|_{2}\right)\text{.}

Since
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(x)=O(1/x)
, we conclude that
\left\|V_{g}\varphi_{k}\right\|_{1}<\infty
and, therefore,
\varphi_{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
. Now,

(\Sigma_{\nu}\varphi_{n})(t)=e^{2i\pi(n+\nu)x}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{[0,1]}(x-k)=e^{2i\pi(n+\nu)x}\text{.}

Since
\{e^{2\pi it(\nu+n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an orthonormal basis of

{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, then
\Sigma_{\nu}:\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
has dense range.
∎

Remark 1
.

The operator
\Sigma_{\nu}
ressembles the Zak transform
[
36
, Chapter 8]
:

\mathbf{Z}f(x,\xi):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(x-k)e^{2\pi ik\xi}\text{,}

since one can write
\Sigma_{\nu}f(t)=\mathbf{Z}f(t,\nu)
. But there is a
subtle big difference: the Zak transform maps a single variable function to
a function in two variables,
f\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}f(t,\nu)
, while
\Sigma_{\nu}
is an operator with
fixed parameter

\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, mapping a single variable function to another function in a single
variable,
f\rightarrow\Sigma_{\nu}f(t)
.

Remark 2
.

An alternative approach for the time-frequency analysis of periodic signals

[
29
,
30
,
55
,
56
]
, considers subspaces of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
of the form

L^{2}(S)=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):f=0\text{ a. e. on }\mathbb{R}-S\right\}\text{,}

where
S
is invariant under
\mathbb{Z}
-shifts.

Remark 3
.

Yet another approach to the analysis of periodic systems is provided by the
theory of Spline-type spaces
[
24
]
(also known as shift-invariant
spaces
[
66
]
), which, given a generator
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, are defined as the closed linear span of the family
\{T_{\lambda}\varphi\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
generated by linear combinations with coefficients
\{a_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in l^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 4
.

If we want
\Sigma_{\nu}f
to satisfy the more general conditions (
2.1
), where
\chi_{\nu}
is a character from
\Gamma
to the
unit circle, mapping a generic element
\gamma=n\alpha\in\Gamma
to

,

, we define the

Remark 5
.

In
[
9
]
we have introduced yet another operator,
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{N}
, which is reminiscent of
\ \Sigma_{\nu}
, but instead of
periodizing the function in time, it periodizes the function simultaneously
in the time and frequency variables:

\mathbf{\Sigma}_{N}f(t):=\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi iNk_{2}t}f(t-k_{1})\text{.}

The operator is fundamental for the treatment of the co-compact case (2) of

under

does not contain functions. It consists of the space

2.2.
Short-time Fourier transform of quasi-periodic functions

The main properties of the action of the STFT as a map

V_{g}:{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}\right)\text{,}

where
\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
is
the Gabor space (
1.9
), are collected in the next Proposition.

Proposition 2
.

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=1
. Then:

(1)

For
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
,

(2.9)

V_{g}f(x+k,\xi)\,=e^{2\pi ik(\nu-\xi)}V_{g}f(x,\xi)\text{.}

(2)

For
f_{1},f_{2}\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

(2.10)

\left\langle V_{g_{1}}f_{1},V_{g_{2}}f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})}=\left\langle f_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}\left\langle g_{1},g_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\text{.}

(3)

\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}\right)

contains the orthonormal basis:

(2.11)

V_{g}(e_{k,\nu})(x,\xi)=e^{2i\pi x(\nu+k-\xi)}\mathcal{F}(\bar{g})(\xi-\nu-k)\text{,}\qquad k=0,1,...\text{.}

(4)

Let
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
. The reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
is

(2.12)

K_{g}^{\nu}((x,\xi),(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}))=e^{-2\pi ix^{\prime}(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi ik(\nu-\xi^{\prime})}V_{g}g(x-x^{\prime}-k,\xi-\xi^{\prime})

or

(2.13)

K_{g}^{\nu}((x,\xi),(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}))=e^{i\pi(x\xi-x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2i\pi(\nu+k)(x-x^{\prime})}\mathcal{F}(\bar{g})(\xi-\nu-k)\overline{\mathcal{F}(\bar{g})(\xi^{\prime}-\nu-k)}\text{.}

(5)

Let
g,f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
. Then

V_{g}(\Sigma_{\nu}f)(x,\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi ik(\nu-\xi)}V_{g}f(x-k,\xi)\text{.}

Proof.

(1) If
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, a change of variable gives

V_{g}f(x+k,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t+k)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi i\xi(t+k)}dt=e^{2\pi ik(\nu-\xi)}V_{g}f(x,\xi)\text{,}

using (
2.1
).

(2) Write
V_{g}f(x,\xi)=\mathcal{F}(f(.)g(.-x))(\xi)
and assume that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are continuous, so that
f_{j}(.)\bar{g}_{j}(.-x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Parseval identity then gives

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}}V_{g_{1}}f_{1}(x,\xi)V_{g_{2}}f_{2}(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(g_{1}(t)\bar{g}_{2}(t)\int_{(0,1)}f_{1}(t+x)\bar{f}_{2}(t+x)dx\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{1}(t)\bar{g}_{2}(t)dt\int_{(0,1)}f_{1}(x)\bar{f}_{2}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle g_{1},g_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\left\langle f_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}\text{,}

using the periodicity of
f_{1}(x)\bar{f}_{2}(x)
on the second line. This
proves (
2.10
) for continuous functions. For general
f_{1}
,
f_{2}\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, the formula follows from a standard density argument.

(3) Since
\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=1
and
\{e_{k,\nu}(t)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
are orthonormal in
{L}^{2}(0,1)
, Moyal’s formula
gives

\left\langle V_{g}e_{k,\nu},V_{g}e_{m,\nu}\right\rangle_{L^{2}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})}=\left\langle e_{k,\nu},e_{m,\nu}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\delta_{k,m}\text{.}

Given that
\{e_{k,\nu}(t)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an orthogonal basis of
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, by definition
\{V_{g}(e_{k,\nu})\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

spans
\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
. We
conclude that
\{V_{g}(e_{k,\nu})\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
provides an
orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
. (
2.11
) follows from direct computation of
V_{g}

acting on (
2.4
):

V_{g}(e_{k,\nu})(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\pi it(\nu+k-\xi)}\overline{g(t-x)}dt=e^{2i\pi x(\nu+k-\xi)}\mathcal{F}(\bar{g})(\xi-\nu-k)\text{.}

(4) From (
2.10
) we easily derive the following inversion formula for
the STFT on
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, valid in a weak sense:

f=\int_{(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}}V_{g}f(x,\xi)\Sigma_{\nu}\pi(x,\xi)gdxd\xi\text{.}

Since
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, then by Proposition
1
,
\Sigma_{\nu}g
and
\Sigma_{\nu}\mathcal{F}(g)
are
well-defined. Thus, if
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, then

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}}V_{g}f(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})V_{g}\left(\Sigma_{\nu}(\pi(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})g)\right)(z)dx^{\prime}d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}}V_{g}f(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-2\pi ik(\xi-\nu)}K_{g}((x,\xi),(x^{\prime}-k,\xi^{\prime}))\right)dx^{\prime}d\xi^{\prime}\text{.}

Using the invariance propriety of
K_{g}
(this can be directly verified
from (
1.6
)):

K_{g}((x+w,\xi),(x^{\prime}+w,\xi^{\prime}))=e^{-2i\pi\Re(w)(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}K_{g}((x,\xi),(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}))

we conclude that the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
is given by (
2.12
). The
alternative form (
2.13
) is obtained, after some simplification,
from the representation of the reproducing kernel in terms of the bilinear
sum of its basis functions (
2.11
):

K_{g}^{\nu}((x,\xi),(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}))=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2i\pi x(\nu+k-\xi)}\mathcal{F}(\bar{g})(\xi-\nu-k)\overline{e^{2i\pi x^{\prime}(\nu+k-\xi^{\prime})}\mathcal{F}(\bar{g})(\xi^{\prime}-\nu-k)}\text{.}

(5) First, we observe that, since
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, then
by Proposition
1
,
\Sigma_{\nu}f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)

and since
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
,
(\Sigma_{\nu}f)(.)\bar{g}_{j}(.-x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then,

\displaystyle V_{g}(\Sigma_{\nu}f)(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi ik\nu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t-k)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi i\xi t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi ik(\nu-\xi)}V_{g}f(x-k,\xi)\text{.}

∎

2.3.
Notational remark

In the next sections we will often deal with spaces analytic or polyanalytic
in the variable
z=x+i\xi\in\mathbb{C}
. With this in mind, we will
shorten the notations in several formulas, by identifying pairs of
coordinates with complex numbers in the following way:

z=x+i\xi\equiv(x,\xi)\text{.}

This materializes the identification
\mathbb{C\equiv R}^{2}
and supports
the notational conventions

V_{g}f(z)=V_{g}f(x+i\xi)\equiv V_{g}f(x,\xi)\text{,}

K_{g}^{\nu}(z,w)=K_{g}^{\nu}(x+i\xi,x^{\prime}+i\xi^{\prime})\equiv K_{g}^{\nu}((x,\xi),(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}))

and

\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)\equiv\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}\right)=V_{g}\left({L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)\right)\text{.}

With this notations, the horizontal translation of a function
F(z=x+i\xi)\equiv F(x,\xi)
, by
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, will often be written as

F(z+k)=F(x+k+i\xi)\equiv F(x+k,\xi)\text{.}

the kernel of the Bargmann transform

We start defining

and

\mathbb{Z}
-periodic. Therefore,

are invariant under the Weyl
translation. The following operator is a ‘phase space’ version of the

\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C})
. On the other hand, the operator can also be
used to show the existence of a function in
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C})
,
whose periodization is a given function belonging to
\mathcal{F}_{1,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})

Lemma 1
.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}=i\frac{1}{\beta}\mathbb{Z}
. Then the periodization operator

(3.18)

\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma^{\prime},l}(F)(z):=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}}\chi_{l}(\gamma)e^{-\pi z\overline{\gamma}-\frac{\pi}{2}|\gamma|^{2}}F(z+\gamma)

is bounded and onto from
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C})
into
\mathcal{F}_{1,l}(\mathbb{C}/\Gamma^{\prime})
.

Proof.

This is proved in
[
20
]
, but since the paper has not yet been
published, we give a direct proof. Namely, it is easy to see that this
operator is bounded with constant bound less than one. Furthermore, by
considering the change of coordinate
z=\frac{i}{\beta}w
, we can consider
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}:\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C}/\Gamma)
; with
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
and
\nu=0
. We consider
the functions

\varphi_{k}(x)=e^{2i\pi kx+\pi k^{2}}\chi_{[0,1]},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}\text{.}

Then the family of functions
f_{k}(z)=\mathcal{B}\varphi_{k}(z)
belongs
to the space
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C})
. By simple computation we obtain

(3.19)

\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}f_{k}(z)=e^{\frac{\pi}{2}z^{2}+2i\pi kz}\text{.}

Thus, it remains to show that the family of functions
e^{\frac{\pi}{2}z^{2}+2i\pi kz}
is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})
. Since

\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathcal{F}_{2}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})
then by using the reproducing kernel representation, we get for

with

4.
The structure of Gabor frames on
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)

Structure of Gabor frames in

L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.
Given a regular lattice
\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}
, the dual lattice is
\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}^{0}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\times\frac{1}{\beta}\mathbb{Z}
. Gabor frames indexed by lattices have a beautiful structure,
leading to useful properties that lie at the heart of the rich mathematical
theory of time-frequency analysis. A chief example is the
Ron-Shen
duality principle

[
36
, Theorem 7.4.3]
, stating that if
\mathcal{G}\left(g,\Lambda\right)
is a Gabor frame for
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
if and only if
\mathcal{G}\left(g,\Lambda^{0}\right)
is a
Gabor Riesz sequence for
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, where
\Lambda^{0}
is the dual lattice. This is a consequence of the
Wexler-Raz
biorthogonality relations
, which state that
\mathcal{G}\left(g,\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}\right)
is a Gabor frame for
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

if and only if there exists
\gamma\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
such that

(4.1)

\beta^{-1}\left\langle\gamma,M_{\frac{n}{\alpha}}T_{\frac{l}{\beta}}g\right\rangle=\delta_{n,0}\delta_{l,0}\text{ \ for all }n,l\in\mathbb{Z}\text{.}

Structure of Gabor frames in

L_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
.

In this section we will define Gabor frames for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
and
prove analogues of some of the structural properties of Gabor frames for
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, most notably of
the sufficient part
of the Wexler-Raz biorthogonality relations
(Corollary
1

below). This will be used to provide, for Hermite and totally positive
functions, sufficient conditions on
\beta
, assuring that
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)
is a Gabor frame for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
.

4.1.
Definition

We will investigate Gabor systems in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
of the form

(4.2)

\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z\right):=\{\Sigma_{\nu}\left(\pi(z)g\right),\;z\in Z\}\text{,}

for a general

, with
\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1

, such that
\pi(\beta n)g=M_{\beta n}g
, to establish Gabor
frame properties in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
.

In this subsection we will justify that (

) is the right
definition for a Gabor system in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
. For a given sequence

of distinct numbers in the fundamental domain representing
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}
, we start by defining a sampling sequence for the space

such that, for all

the

We will show that this sampling inequality if equivalent to the Gabor system

of the form (

). Using the Moyal

), since

Now, for all

, the quasi-periodicity condition (

Lemma 2

If

Thus, if

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle e^{-2\pi ik\nu}f,T_{k}\pi(z)g\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle T_{k}f,T_{k}\pi(z)g\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle f,\left(\pi(z)g\right)\right\rangle_{{L}^{2}(k,k+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle f,\left(\pi(z)g\right)\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}\text{.}

∎

Using (
4.5
), one concludes that the inequalities (
4.4
) can
be written as

(4.6)

A\left\|f\right\|_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq\sum_{z\in Z}\left|\left\langle f,\Sigma_{\nu}\left(\pi(z)g\right)\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}\right|^{2}\leq B\left\|f\right\|_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\text{.}

This justifies the following.

Definition 1
.

The Gabor system
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z\right):=\{\Sigma_{\nu}\left(\pi(z)g\right),\;z\in Z\}
is a
Gabor frame
or
Weyl-Heisenberg frame
in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
,

whenever there
exist constants
A,B>0
such that, for all
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, (
4.6
) holds.

4.2.
The frame operator on
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)

Let us consider
Z
as the regular lattice
Z_{\beta}:=i\beta\mathbb{Z}\subset[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
. Equivalently,
Z_{\beta}
consists
of the pairs
(0,\beta n)\subset[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\pi(z)g=M_{\beta n}g
and

\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z\right):=\{\Sigma_{\nu}\left(M_{\beta n}g\right),\;n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\text{.}

Let
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
. As in
the classical case, we consider the analysis operator

(4.7)

C_{g}^{\nu}(f)=\left(\left\langle f,M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)_{n}=\left(\left\langle f,\Sigma_{\nu}\left(M_{\beta n}g\right)\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}\right)_{n}\text{.}

The formal adjoint operator of
C_{g}^{\nu}
is the synthesis operator
D_{g}^{\nu}
, which is given by

(4.8)

D_{g}^{\nu}((c)_{n})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\Sigma_{\nu}M_{\beta n}g\text{.}

If both operators converge in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, then the Gabor frame
operator in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
is

(4.9)

S_{g,\gamma}^{\nu}f=D_{\gamma}^{\nu}C_{g}^{\nu}(f)=\sum_{z\in Z}\left\langle f,\Sigma_{\nu}M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}\Sigma_{\nu}(M_{\beta n}\gamma)\text{,}

where the dual window
\gamma
is defined as

\gamma:=S^{-1}g

and the reconstruction formula
f=S_{g,\gamma}^{\nu}f
becomes:

(4.10)

f=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle f,\Sigma_{\nu}\left(M_{\beta n}g\right)\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}\Sigma_{\nu}\left(M_{\beta n}\gamma\right)\text{.}

We will see, in Proposition
3
below, that
g,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
assures that these operators are well defined, in
particular they converge in
L^{2}
sense. For instance, we recall that the
Feichtinger algebra
[
23
]
can be defined as follows

\mathcal{S}_{0}:=\left\{g\in{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\mathcal{B}g\in\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\right\}\text{.}

The following proposition assures the existence of
D_{g}^{\nu}
and
C_{g}^{\nu}
, whenever
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
.

Proposition 3
.

For
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
we have, for every
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
,

(4.11)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\left\langle f,\Sigma_{\nu}\left(M_{\beta n}g\right)\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}\right|^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\left\langle f,M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right|^{2}\leq C\|g\|_{\mathcal{S}_{0}}^{2}\|f\|_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

and

(4.12)

\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\Sigma_{\nu}M_{\beta n}g\right\|_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}\leq C^{\prime}\|g\|_{\mathcal{S}_{0}}\|(c_{n})_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Proof.

Firstly, by definition,
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
is equivalent to saying that
the function
G=\mathcal{B}g\in\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
. Secondly, if
F
is an entire function, then the estimate

\left|F(w)e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}\mid w\mid^{2}}\right|\leqslant\frac{C}{r^{2}}\int_{D(w,r)}\left|F(z)e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}}\right|dz

holds. Thus,

(4.13)

\left|\tau_{i\beta n}F(z)\right|e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}}=\left|F(z-i\beta n)\right|e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z-i\beta n|^{2}}\leq C\int_{D(z-i\beta n,r)}\left|F(z)e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}}\right|dz\text{,}

where
\tau_{a}f(z)=\tau(a)f(z)=e^{\pi z\overline{a}-\frac{\pi}{2}|a|^{2}}f(z-a)
is the Weyl operator. Therefore, for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{1,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})
, we have

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\left\langle f,\Sigma_{\nu}\left(M_{\beta n}g\right)\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}\right|^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\left\langle f,M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right|^{2}

and, applying the Bargmann transform as an unitary operator
\mathcal{B}:L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
and using the
intertwining property

(4.14)

\mathcal{B}(\pi(z)g)=e^{i\pi\xi\eta}\tau_{\overline{z}}\mathcal{B}g;\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\text{,}

gives

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\left\langle f,M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right|^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\left\langle F,\tau_{-i\beta n}G\right\rangle\right|\text{,}

where
G=\mathcal{B}g,F=\mathcal{B}f\in\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
. Now,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\left\langle F,\tau_{-i\beta n}G\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\left\langle\tau_{i\beta n}F,G\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\int_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}}\left|F(w)\right|\chi_{D(z-i\beta n,r)}(w)e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|w|^{2}}dA(w)|G(z)|e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}}dz

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}}\left|F(w)\right|e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|w|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{D(w+i\beta n,r)}(z)|G(z)|e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}}dzdw

\displaystyle\leq C\|F\|_{\mathcal{F}_{1,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})}\|G\|_{1}\text{.}

If
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})
, then

(4.15)

\left|\left\langle\tau_{i\beta n}F,G\right\rangle\right|\leq\|F\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})}\|G\|_{1}

and this implies

\|\left\langle F,\tau_{-i\beta n}G\right\rangle\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\|F\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})}\|G\|_{1}\text{.}

By complex interpolation between
p=1
and
p=\infty
, we obtain for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})
,

(4.16)

\|\left\langle F,\tau_{-i\beta n}G\right\rangle\|_{2}\leq C\|F\|_{\mathcal{F}_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})}\|G\|_{1}\text{.}

Now, since the Bargmann transform
\mathcal{B}
is an unitary isomorphism
from
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
onto
\mathcal{F}_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})

and using the intertwining property (
4.14
), we obtain the
first result. The second result is obtained in the same way. In fact for
c=(c_{n})\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k,n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}e^{2i\pi\nu k-i\pi\beta nk}\tau_{k-i\beta n}G\right\|_{\mathcal{F}_{1,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{n}|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{C}/\Gamma}|G(z-k+i\beta n)|e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z-k+i\beta n|^{2}}dz

\displaystyle\leq\|c\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\|G\|_{\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C})}\text{.}

If
c=(c_{n})\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k,n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}e^{2i\pi\nu k-i\pi\beta nk}\tau_{k-i\beta n}G(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{n}||G(z-k+i\beta n)|e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z-k+i\beta n|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{n}|\int_{D(z-k+i\beta n,r)}|G(w)|e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|w|^{2}}dw

\displaystyle\leq C\|c\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\|G\|_{\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C})}\text{.}

Thus,

\left\|\sum_{k,n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}e^{2i\pi\nu k-i\pi\beta nk}\tau_{k-i\beta n}G\right\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})}\leq C\|c\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\|G\|_{\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C})}\text{.}

By complex interpolation between
p=1
and
p=\infty
, we obtain, for
c=(c_{n})\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\left\|\sum_{k,n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}e^{2i\pi\nu k-i\pi\beta nk}\tau_{k-i\beta n}G\right\|_{\mathcal{F}_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})}\leq C\|c\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\|G\|_{\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C})}\text{.}

Now using the identity (
4.14
) and the unitary isomorphism
property of the Bargmann transform from
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
onto
\mathcal{F}_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})
, we obtain the second result.
∎

4.3.
Janssen’s representation and sufficient conditions for Gabor
frames in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)

By Proposition
3
the series in (
4.9
) defining the
operator
S_{g,\gamma}^{\nu}
converges in
L^{2}
sense. Proposition
4
tells that the frame operator (
4.9
) coincides
with the restriction to
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
of the frame operator
associated with the classical Gabor system
\mathcal{G}\left(g,\Lambda_{1,\beta}\right)
, where
\Lambda_{1,\beta}=\mathbb{Z}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}
. The proof is a simple application of identity (
4.5
),
which assumes the following form for
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)
:

(4.17)

\left\langle f,\Sigma_{\nu}M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}=\left\langle f,M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}\text{.}

Proposition 4
.

Let
g,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
. For
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, the frame operator
Sf
defined by (
4.9
), can be
written as

S_{g,\gamma}^{\nu}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle f,M_{\beta n}T_{k}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}M_{\beta n}T_{k}\gamma\text{.}

Proof.

If
g,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
then, by Proposition
1
,
\Sigma_{\nu}g,\Sigma_{\nu}\gamma\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
and by
Proposition (
4.9
),
S_{g,\gamma}
is bounded. From (
4.9
) and using the quasi-periodicity condition (
1.8
),
f=e^{-2\pi ik\nu}T_{-k}f
, (
4.17
) gives:

\displaystyle S_{g,\gamma}^{\nu}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in Z}\left\langle f,\Sigma_{\nu}M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}\Sigma_{\nu}(M_{\beta n}\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle f,M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}\Sigma_{\nu}(M_{\beta n}\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle e^{-2\pi ik\nu}T_{-k}f,M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}e^{2\pi ik\nu}T_{k}M_{\beta n}\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle f,T_{k}M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}T_{k}M_{\beta n}\gamma\text{.}

∎

The simple observation of Proposition
4
has the remarkable
consequence that Gabor frames
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)

in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
inherit some of the structure properties of Gabor
frames in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
. We will now explore some of
them, omitting the corresponding details when the algebraic manipulations
required are the same as those in the
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
case.

•

Starting from Proposition
4
, one can mimic all the
manipulations in
[
36
, Section 6.3]
and arrive at
Walnut’s
representation
of the Gabor frame operator. Let
g,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
and
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
. Then

S_{g,\gamma}^{\nu}f=\beta^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{n}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}f\text{, }

where
G_{n}
is called the correlation function of the pair
\left(g,\gamma\right)
, defined as

G_{n}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{g}\left(x-\frac{n}{\beta}-k\right)\gamma\left(x-k\right)\text{.}

The correlation function is invariant under integer translations and,
similarly as in
[
36
, (7.7)]
, we can expand it as a Fourier series

G_{n}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle\gamma,M_{k}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}g\right\rangle e^{2\pi ikx}\text{.}

•

This leads, as in
[
36
, Section 7.2]
to the important
Janssen’s representation
of the Gabor frame operator. Given
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, then

S_{g,\gamma}^{\nu}f=\beta^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle\gamma,M_{k}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}M_{k}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}f

Janssen’s representation is one of the fundamental results in time-frequency
analysis. It has several applications and notorious ramifications to other
mathematical corners, for instance, it can be interpreted as a quantum theta
function and as a Poisson formula for the sympletic Fourier transform
[
58
]
. For our purposes, the most important consequence is the
following version of the Wexler-Raz biorthogonality relations.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose that
g,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
. If
S_{g,\gamma}=I
or, equivalently, if there exists
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
such that

(4.18)

\beta^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle\gamma,M_{k}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}e^{2i\pi\frac{nl}{\beta}}=\delta_{k,0}\text{ \ for all }l\in\mathbb{Z}\text{,}

then
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)
is Gabor frame for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
.

Remark 7
.

The original Wexler-Raz relations (
4.1
) provide both a necessary and
sufficient condition for Gabor frames in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
,
while the
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
analogue given by (
4.18
) is only a
sufficient condition, since we couldn’t show that the Gabor frame property
of
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)
in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)

assures the existence of the dual window
\gamma
in (
4.18
).

Remark 8
.

The Wexler-Raz biorthogonality relations (
4.1
) clearly
imply (
4.18
). However, this requires finding a function
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
such that
V_{g}\gamma(0,0)=\beta
and
V_{g}\gamma(k,\frac{n}{\beta})=0
,
k,n\in\mathbb{N-\{}0\mathbb{\}}
, while (
4.18
)
only requires vanishing in the periodization lattice. Corollary
1
assures that
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)
is
a frame indexed by the sampling lattice
Z_{\beta}=i\beta\mathbb{Z}
, once
we find a function interpolating on the periodization lattice
\mathbb{Z-\{}0\mathbb{\}}
.

Remark 9
.

Since

V_{g}\Sigma_{\frac{nl}{\beta}}\gamma(x,\frac{n}{\beta})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle\gamma,M_{x}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}e^{2i\pi\frac{nl}{\beta}}\text{,}

to use (
4.18
) one only needs to find a function
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
such that for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}
,
V_{g}\Sigma_{\frac{nl}{\beta}}\gamma
vanishes on
\mathbb{Z-\{}0\mathbb{\}}
and such that
V_{g}\Sigma_{\frac{nl}{\beta}}\gamma(0,\frac{n}{\beta})=\beta
. Moreover, since
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
, we can assure that
\Sigma_{\frac{nl}{\beta}}\gamma\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
and
V_{g}\Sigma_{\frac{nl}{\beta}}\gamma\in\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\frac{nl}{\beta}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
. This means that
V_{g}\Sigma_{\frac{nl}{\beta}}\gamma

satisfies periodicity conditions with respect to
\Gamma^{\prime}=i\frac{1}{\beta}\mathbb{Z}
, with character given by
\chi_{l}(\gamma)=\chi_{l}(i\frac{n}{\beta})=e^{2i\pi\frac{nl}{\beta}}
. In section
4.4

we will implement this method to show our results about Gabor systems with
Hermite-theta functions. The key idea is to find a function
H\in\mathcal{F}_{1,l}(\mathbb{C}/\Gamma^{\prime})
vanishing on
\mathbb{Z-\{}0\mathbb{\}}
and use the surjectivity of the periodization operator (
3.18
) from
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C})
into
\mathcal{F}_{1,l}(\mathbb{C}/\Gamma^{\prime})
, to assure the existence of
F\in\mathcal{F}_{1,l}(\mathbb{C}/\Gamma^{\prime})
such that
H=\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma^{\prime},l}(F)
;
this is equivalent to the existence of a dual window
\gamma=\mathcal{B}^{-1}F\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
such that (
4.18
) holds.

Corollary 2
.

Suppose that
g,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
. If
\mathcal{G}\left(g,\Lambda_{1,\beta}\right)
is a Gabor frame for
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, then
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)
is
a Gabor frame for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{G}\left(g,\Lambda_{1,\beta}\right)
is a Gabor frame for
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, and
g,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
, then
D_{g}
and
D_{\gamma}
are bounded. Thus, the Wexler-Raz biorthogonality
relations (
4.1
),

(4.19)

\beta^{-1}\left\langle\gamma,M_{k}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}=\delta_{n,0}\delta_{k,0}\text{ \ for all
}k,n\in\mathbb{Z}\text{,}

hold
[
36
, Theorem 7.3.1]
. Since
g,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}

then, by Proposition
1
,
\Sigma_{\nu}g
,
\Sigma_{\nu}\gamma\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
and by Proposition (
4.9
),
S_{g,\gamma}
is bounded. This implies (
4.18
) and, by Corollary
1
,
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)
is a Gabor
frame for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
.
∎

From this, we easily derive a
new criteria for obstructions for Gabor
frames
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
g,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
. If there exists
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
such that
V_{g}f(0,\beta n)=0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\mathcal{G}\left(g,\Lambda_{1,\beta}\right)

is not
a Gabor frame
for
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.

Proof.

If
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
, By Proposition
1
,
\Sigma_{\nu}M_{\beta n}g\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
. Thus, if there exists
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
such that

\left\langle f,\Sigma_{\nu}M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}=\left\langle f,M_{\beta n}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}=V_{g}f(0,\beta n)=0\text{, \ }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{,}

then, by the inequalities defining the sampling condition (
4.17
),
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)
is not a Gabor frame for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
. By Corollary
2
,
\mathcal{G}\left(g,\Lambda_{1,\beta}\right)
is not a Gabor frame for
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.
∎

We will not explore this in the present paper, but since the frame
condition for
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)
depends only
on the size of the parameter
\beta
, it is reasonable to expect situations
where one is able to disprove a frame property for a system
\mathcal{G}\left(g,\Lambda_{1,\beta}\right)
.

Corollary
2
allows to transfer sufficient conditions about
Gabor frames in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, of the form
\mathcal{G}\left(g,\Lambda_{1,\beta}\right)
, to
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(g,Z_{\beta}\right)
, a Gabor frame for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
. A chief
example is the family of
totally positive functions
. By a theorem
of Schoenberg
[
68
]
, the Fourier transform of an integrable totally
positive function
g
possesses the factorization

(4.20)

\hat{g}(\xi)=ce^{-\gamma\xi^{2}}e^{2\pi i\nu\xi}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1+2\pi i\nu_{j}\xi)^{-1}e^{-2\pi i\nu_{j}\xi}

with
c>0
,
\nu,\nu_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma\geq 0
,
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and
0<\gamma+\sum_{j}\nu_{j}^{2}<\infty
. Special
cases, when
N
is finite, have been considered before in
[
42
,
43
]
and the full problem has been recently solved for
rational lattices by Gröchenig in
[
41
]
. Despite the incremental
nature of the sequence of work
[
42
,
43
,
41
]
, each of
these papers depends on completely different methods and should be
considered as a whole. In particular,
[
Gro2018
]
has an aesthetically
pleasing flavour, since it deals with this intrinsic difficult problem with
a surprising virtuose simplicity. Totally positive functions (
4.20
)
include Gaussian windows and many other examples, in particular the case of
the hyperbolic secant
\left(e^{at}+e^{-at}\right)^{-1}
, where the
factorization (
4.20
) is infinite, previously proved in
[
53
]
.
The strongest result about Gabor frames with totally positive functions,
which includes the case of an infinite factorization (

5.
Beurling density in the flat cylinder

A sequence

\delta:=\inf_{j\neq k}\left|z_{j}-z_{k}\right|>0\text{.}

The constant

for the space

constants

(5.1)

A\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}^{2}\leq\sum_{z\in Z}\left|V_{g}f(z)\right|^{2}\leq B\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}^{2}\text{.}

equivalent to

for

, using

) can be written as a sampling inequality for

A\left\|F\right\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})}^{2}\leq\sum_{z\in Z}\left|F(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\leq B\left\|F\right\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})}^{2}\text{.}

Now we will define
Gabor Riesz basic sequences
as the sequences
leading to

[

]

Definition 2

A sequence

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}\leq C\|f|Z\|_{2,\alpha}\text{.}

If

Riesz basic sequence
for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)

In

]
, we were able to obtain a complete description of the

6.1.
Vector-valued STFT on
{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]

A construction of a vectorial STFT acting on
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)
appeared in
[
3
,
4
]
, motivated by Balan’s
idea of using Gabor superframes for the purpose of multiplexing of signals
(sending several signals in the same channel and later separate them at the
receiver)
[
13
,
14
]
. See also
[
47
]
for a deep
theoretical study of superframes. For the choice of the vectorial window
\mathbf{g=h}_{N}=(h_{0},...,h_{N-1})
, the vectorial STFT acting on
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)
, becomes a multiple of a
Bargmann-type integral transform mapping
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)
to the Fock space of polyanalytic functions in
\mathbb{C}
,
introduced in the proof of the results in
[
3
]
.

In this subsection we describe an analogue construction of a vectorial STFT
acting on
{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
. It will be
used in Section 3.3 to define a Bargmann-type transform mapping to the
(full) Fock space of polyanalytic functions on
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}
. Let
{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
be the vectorial Hilbert
space defined by the inner product below, where
\mathbf{f=}\left(f_{0},...,f_{N-1}\right)
and
\mathbf{g=}\left(g_{0},...,g_{N-1}\right)
:

(6.1)

\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]}=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left\langle f_{i},g_{i}\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)}\text{.}

Given a vectorial window
\mathbf{g=}\left(g_{0},...,g_{N-1}\right)\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)
,

(6.2)

\left\langle g_{i},g_{j}\right\rangle=\delta_{i,j}\text{, }i,j=0,...,N-1\text{,}

one can define the vectorial STFT acting on
\mathbf{f=}\left(f_{0},...,f_{N-1}\right)\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
, by

(6.3)

\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{g}}\mathbf{f}(x,\xi):=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}V_{g_{i}}f_{i}(x,\xi)\text{.}

This defines an isometry

\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{g}}:{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})

since, from (
2.10
) and (
6.2
),

(6.4)

\left\|\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{g}}\mathbf{f}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}^{2}=\left\langle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}V_{g_{i}}f_{i}(x,\xi),\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}V_{g_{i}}f_{i}(x,\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R})}=\left\|\mathbf{f}\right\|_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]}^{2}\text{.}

We define the super Gabor space

(6.5)

\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{g}}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right):=\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{g}}\left({L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]\right)\text{.}

6.2.
Polyanalytic Fock spaces
\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)

Consider
\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
, the Fock space
of polyanalytic functions, that is, satisfying the generalized
Cauchy-Riemann equation

\partial_{\overline{z}}^{N}F(z)=0\text{,}

and belonging to
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},e^{-\pi|z|^{2}})
. A fundamental result
in the theory of polyanalytic Fock spaces is Vasilevski’s orthogonal
decomposition of
\mathbf{F}^{(n)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
into true
polyanalytic Fock spaces
\mathcal{F}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

[
75
]
, namely,

(6.6)

\mathbf{F}^{(n)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)=\bigoplus\limits_{r=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{F}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\text{.}

Therefore, it follows from (
3.10
) and from the well-known identity
for Laguerre polynomials:

L_{n}^{1}(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}L_{r}(x)\text{,}

that the reproducing kernel of
\mathbf{F}^{(n)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
is

K_{\mathbf{F}^{(n)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)}(z,w)=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}K_{\mathcal{F}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)}(z,w)=e^{\pi\overline{w}z}L_{n}^{1}(\pi\left|z-w\right|^{2})\text{.}

Clearly,
\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{F}^{(n)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)=L^{2}(\mathbb{C},e^{-\pi|z|^{2}})
, thus we also have

(6.7)

L^{2}(\mathbb{C},e^{-\pi|z|^{2}})=\bigoplus\limits_{r=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\text{.}

Now consider
\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
,
the Fock space of polyanalytic functions belonging to
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z},e^{-\pi|z|^{2}})
and satisfying the functional equation

F(z+k)=e^{2\pi ik\nu}e^{\pi kz+\frac{\pi}{2}\left|k\right|^{2}}F(z)\text{.}

The next result computes the reproducing kernel of
\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
and extends Vasilevski’s
decompositions (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) to
\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
. Our proof is different from
the one in
[
75
]
, where restriction operators involving Fourier
transforms have been used (maybe the approach of
[
75
]
also works,
but we didn’t try it). Instead, we compute the reproducing kernel of
\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
and decompose
it as the sum of the first
n
reproducing kernels of
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
. The orthogonality of the
spaces
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
for
any pair of natural numbers
(r_{1},r_{2})
with
r_{1}\neq r_{2}
, is then
an immediate consequence of (
3.13
). This is an example of how
the time-frequency formulation can simplify some proofs, since the
orthogonality of the spaces
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
, would otherwise require the evaluation of the integrals
using Green’s formula and integration by parts (the same comment applies to
the double indexed orthogonality (
3.14
) of the sequence
\left\{\varphi_{k}^{(r)}\right\}_{k,r=0}^{\infty}
, which, under our
approach is an immediate consequence of the Moyal-type formulas (
2.10
).

Theorem 4
.

The reproducing kernel of
\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
is

K_{\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}(z,w)=e^{\pi\overline{w}z}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi ik\nu}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}k^{2}+\pi(z+\overline{w})k}L_{r}^{1}(\pi\left|z-w+k\right|^{2})=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}K_{\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}(z,w)\text{.}

Moreover, the spaces
\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z},e^{-\pi|z|^{2}})
have the
following orthogonal decompositions into true polyanalytic spaces
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
:

(6.8)

\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)=\bigoplus\limits_{r=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)

and

(6.9)

L^{2}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z},e^{-\pi|z|^{2}})=\bigoplus\limits_{r=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)\text{.}

Proof.

We first proceed as in
[
32
, Proof of Lemma 9]
, to show
that the following representation formula holds for
F\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
:

(6.10)

F(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}F(z)e^{\pi z\overline{w}}L_{n}^{1}(\pi\left|z-w\right|^{2})e^{-\pi|w|^{2}}d(w).

First recall that this holds as the reproducing formula for
F\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
. Now, let
F\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
. Then
\partial_{\bar{z}}^{n}F=0
and
|F(z)|\leq Ce^{\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}}
. Set
F_{\epsilon}(z)=F(\epsilon z)
, for a given
\epsilon\in\left]0,1\right[
. Then
|F(\epsilon z)|\leq Ce^{\frac{\pi}{2}\epsilon^{2}|z|^{2}}
implies

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|F_{\epsilon}(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}dz\leq C\int_{\mathbb{C}}e^{-\pi(1-\epsilon^{2})|z|^{2}}dz<\infty

Thus, since
\partial_{\bar{z}}^{n+1}F_{\epsilon}=0
,
F_{\epsilon}(z)\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
and satisfies (
6.10
) for any
\epsilon\in\left]0,1\right[
. Letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 1
, we conclude by the dominate convergence theorem that
F

satisfies (
6.10
). Now, let
F\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
. Using (
6.10
) and
F(z+k)=e^{2\pi ik\nu}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}k^{2}+\pi zk}F(z)
, leads to:

\displaystyle F(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}F(w)e^{\pi\overline{w}z}L_{n}^{1}(\pi\left|z-w\right|^{2})e^{-\pi|w|^{2}}dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}}F(w+k)e^{\pi(\overline{w}+k)z}L_{n}^{1}(\pi\left|z-w-k\right|^{2})e^{-\pi|w+k|^{2}}dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}}e^{2\pi ik\nu}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}k^{2}-\pi\overline{w}k}F(w)e^{\pi(\overline{w}+k)z}L_{n}^{1}(\pi\left|z-w-k\right|^{2})e^{-\pi|w|^{2}}dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}}e^{\pi\overline{w}z}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi ik\nu}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}k^{2}+\pi(z-\overline{w})k}L_{n}^{1}(\pi\left|z-w-k\right|^{2})e^{-\pi|w|^{2}}dw\text{.}

Thus, the reproducing kernel of
\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
is

\displaystyle K_{\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}(z,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\pi\overline{w}z}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi ik\nu}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}k^{2}+\pi(z-\overline{w})k}L_{n}^{1}(\pi\left|z-w-k\right|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\pi\overline{w}z}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi ik\nu}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}k^{2}+\pi(z-\overline{w})k}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}L_{r}(\pi\left|z-w-k\right|^{2})

leading to

K_{\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}(z,w)=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}K_{\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}(z,w)\text{,}

so that a generic element
F\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
can be written as

\displaystyle F(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}F(z)K_{\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}(z,w)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\int_{\mathbb{C}}F(z)K_{\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}(z,w)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}(P_{\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}F)(w)\text{,}

where
P_{\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}F\in\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
is the
projection of
F
over
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
. Together with the orthogonality of the spaces
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{(r)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
, which follows from (
3.13
), this establishes (
6.8
). Since
\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)=L^{2}(\mathbb{C},e^{-\pi|z|^{2}})
, this also implies (
6.9
).
∎

A Bargmann-type transform

\mathbf{B}^{\left(n\right)}:{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]\rightarrow\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)

can be defined, given
\mathbf{f=}\left(f_{0},...,f_{N-1}\right)\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
and
\mathbf{g=}\left(g_{0},...,g_{N-1}\right)\in{L}^{2}\left[\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
, by

(6.11)

\mathbf{B}^{\left(N\right)}\mathbf{f}(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{B}^{\left(r\right)}f_{r}(z)\text{.}

Using (
3.9
) and (
6.3
), this can be written as

\mathbf{B}^{\left(N\right)}f(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{B}^{\left(r\right)}f_{r}(z)=e^{-i\pi x\xi+\frac{\pi}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}V_{h_{r}}f_{r}(x,-\xi)=e^{-i\pi x\xi+\frac{\pi}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{h}_{N}}\mathbf{f}(x,-\xi)\text{,}

where
\mathbf{h}_{N}\mathbf{=}\left(h_{0},...,h_{N-1}\right)
, and it
follows from (
6.4
) that
\mathbf{B}^{\left(N\right)}:{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]\rightarrow\mathbf{F}_{\nu}^{(N)}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)
is isometric. This construction
has been recently suggested in
[
31
, Remark 1.5]
. Fock spaces of
polyanalytic functions of infinite order in
\mathbb{C}
have been recently
considered in
[
11
]
. The construction seems to be
possible to adapt to
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}
, but we will not pursue this
direction further.

6.3.
Gabor superframes with Hermite functions

As in the scalar case, we will define Gabor superframes by considering first
sampling sequences. Thus, we say that
Z\subset[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
is sampling, whenever there exist constants
A,B>0
such that, for all
\mathbf{f}\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
, the
inequality holds:

(6.12)

A\left\|\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{h}_{N}}\mathbf{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}^{2}\leq\sum_{z\in Z}\left|\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{h}_{N}}\mathbf{f}(z)\right|^{2}\leq B\left\|\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{h}_{N}}\mathbf{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\right)}^{2}\text{.}

Let us consider the regular lattice case
Z=Z_{\beta}:=i\beta\mathbb{Z}\subset[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
. Equivalently,
Z_{\beta}
consists
of the pairs
(0,\beta n)\subset[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\pi(z)\mathbf{g}=M_{\beta n}\mathbf{g}
and, combining
the definition (
6.1
) with (
4.5
), gives

\left\langle\mathbf{f},\Sigma_{\nu}M_{\beta n}\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]}=\left\langle\mathbf{f},M_{\beta n}\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{{L}^{2}\left[\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]}\text{.}

Thus, using (
6.4
), the inequalities (
4.4
) can be written
as

(6.13)

A\left\|\mathbf{f}\right\|_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]}^{2}\leq\sum_{z\in Z}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\Sigma_{\nu}M_{\beta n}\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]}\right|^{2}\leq B\left\|\mathbf{f}\right\|_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]}^{2}\text{.}

This justifies the following definition of superframe in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
.

Definition 3
.

Given a vectorial window
\mathbf{g=}\left(g_{0},...,g_{N-1}\right)\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)
, with the entries satisfying
the orthonormality
\left\langle g_{i},g_{j}\right\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
,
i,j=0,...,N-1
, one declares the Gabor system
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(\mathbf{g},Z\right):=\{\Sigma_{\nu}\left(M_{\beta n}\mathbf{g}\right),\;n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
to be a
Gabor superframe
for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
,

whenever there exist constants

A,B>0
such that, for all
f\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}(0,1)
, (
6.13
)
hold.

For vector-valued systems, the same arguments as in Proposition
4
, allow to write the Gabor superframe operator in
{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
as

\mathbf{S}_{g,\gamma}^{\nu}\mathbf{f}=\sum_{z\in Z}\left\langle\mathbf{f},\Sigma_{\nu}M_{\beta n}\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]}\Sigma_{\nu}(M_{\beta n}\mathbf{\gamma})\text{,}

where the dual window
\mathbf{\gamma}
is defined as

\mathbf{\gamma}:=\mathbf{S}^{-1}\mathbf{g}\text{.}

Let
g_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
. For
\mathbf{f}\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
, the same manipulations as in
Proposition
4
, lead to

\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{g,\gamma}}^{\nu}\mathbf{f}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle\mathbf{f},M_{\beta n}T_{k}\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)}M_{\beta n}T_{k}\mathbf{\gamma}\text{.}

Let
g_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
,
i=1,...,N
and
\mathbf{f}\in{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
. The steps leading to
Janssen’s representation
of the Gabor frame operator can be repeated as in

[

, 5-Appendix]
, and the result is

\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{g,\gamma}}^{\nu}\mathbf{f}=\beta^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle\mathbf{\gamma},M_{k}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)}M_{k}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}\mathbf{f}\text{.}

Proposition 6
.

Let
g_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
,
i=1,...,N
. If
\mathbf{S}_{g,\gamma}=\mathbf{I}
or, equivalently, if there exists
\mathbf{\gamma=(}\gamma\mathbf{{}_{1},...,}\gamma\mathbf{\mathbf{{}_{N}})}

such that
\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
,
i=1,...,N
,

(6.14)

\beta^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle\mathbf{\gamma},M_{k}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)}e^{2i\pi\frac{nl}{\beta}}=\delta_{k,0}\text{ \ for all }l\in\mathbb{Z}\text{,}

then
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(\mathbf{g},Z_{\beta}\right)
is a Gabor
superframe for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
.

From this we easily conclude the following.

Proposition 7
.

Let
g_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
,
i=1,...,N
. If

\mathcal{G}\left(\mathbf{g},\Lambda_{1,\beta}\right)
is a Gabor
superframe for

, then
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(\mathbf{g},Z_{\beta}\right)
is a Gabor

{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{G}\left(\mathbf{g},\Lambda_{1,\beta}\right)
is a Gabor

L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)
, and
g_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
,

, then
D_{\mathbf{g}}

and
D_{\mathbf{\gamma}}
are bounded. Thus, the Wexler-Raz
biorthogonality relations
[
39
, Proposition 2.3]
,

\beta^{-1}\left\langle\mathbf{\gamma},M_{k}T_{\frac{n}{\beta}}\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)}=\delta_{n,0}\delta_{k,0}\text{ \ for all }k,n\in\mathbb{Z}

hold
[

, Theorem 7.3.1]
. Since
g_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
then, by Proposition

,
\Sigma_{\nu}g_{i}
,

and by Proposition (

),
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{g,\gamma}}^{\nu}\mathbf{f}

) and, by Proposition

.
∎

Now we will use a celebrated result about Gabor superframes with Hermite
functions.

Theorem B (Gröchenig- Lyubarskii
[
39
, Theorem 1.1]
).
Let
h_{N}=\left(h_{0},...,h_{N-1}\right)
be the vector of the first
N

.

By
[

, (45) and Proposition 3.3]
,
h_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
,
i=0,...,N-1
. Combining Theorem B with Proposition
7
, gives the analogue of Theorem B for
{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left[(0,1),\mathbb{C}^{N}\right]
.

Theorem 5
.

If
\beta<\frac{1}{N}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\left(\mathbf{h}_{N},Z_{\beta}\right):=\{\Sigma_{\nu}\left(M_{\beta n}\mathbf{h}_{N}\right),\;n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

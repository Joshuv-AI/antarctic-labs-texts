Embeddings into the ultrapower of the Jiang-Su algebra Thanks: This work was supported by DFF grant 1054-00094B, and by the the ERC grant EP/X026647/1.
Ben Bouwen, Jennifer Pi

Abstract
. We study existence of embeddings into ultrapowers of the Jiang-Su algebra 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} and the Razak-Jacelon algebra 𝒲 \mathcal{W} . More specifically, we show that the cone over any separable C ∗ C^{*} -algebra embeds into the ultrapowers of both 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} and 𝒲 \mathcal{W} . We also show that the result for 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} generalizes to separable and exact continuous fields of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras for which one of the fibers embeds into the ultrapower of 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} , if this fiber is suitably well-behaved.

1.
Introduction

Questions of embeddability into ultrapowers within operator algebras are ubiquitous, with the most famous being the Connes embedding problem: does every II
1
factor embed into an ultrapower of the hyperfinite II
1
factor?
A naive
C^{*}
-analog of this question, regarding embeddings of
C^{*}
-algebras into an ultrapower of the Jiang-Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}
, can be quickly decided: any
C^{*}
-algebra with multiple non-zero projections cannot embed into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, since the latter is projectionless.
However, the question of characterizing which
C^{*}
-algebras do admit embeddings into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
remains open and difficult.

In this brief paper, we make a few observations on embeddability of various
C^{*}
-algebras into the ultrapower of
\mathcal{Z}
and of the Razak-Jacelon algebra
\mathcal{W}
, a stably projectionless
C^{*}
-algebra with trivial
K
-theory constructed in
[
Jac13
]
. The first asserts existence of an embedding for cones over separable
C^{*}
-algebras:

Theorem A
.

The cone over any separable
C^{*}
-algebra embeds into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
and into
\mathcal{W}_{\omega}
.

This statement is obtained as a consequence of Proposition
2.2
. In the latter, we construct embeddings of the cone over the universal UHF algebra
\mathcal{Q}
into
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{W}
, which are used to embed the cone over
\mathcal{Q}_{\omega}
into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
and
\mathcal{W}_{\omega}
, respectively. The statement above then follows by combining quasidiagonality of arbitrary cones over separable
C^{*}
-algebras
[
Voi91
]
with an embedding of the cone into the double cone.

Combining Proposition
2.2
with the results in
[
Gab20
]
, we add
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
- and
\mathcal{W}_{\omega}
-embeddability to the following list of equivalences for separable, exact, traceless (in the sense of
[
Gab20
]
)
C^{*}
-algebras.

Corollary B
.

For
A
separable, exact, and traceless, the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
embeds into the cone over
\mathcal{O}_{2}
,

(2)

A
is quasidiagonal.

(3)

A
is AF-embeddable.

(4)

The primitive ideal space of
A
has no non-empty compact open subsets.

(5)

A
is stably finite.

(6)

A
is stably projectionless.

(7)

A
embeds into the trace-kernel ideal of
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
.

(8)

A
embeds into the trace-kernel ideal of
\mathcal{W}_{\omega}
.

Our second result generalizes Theorem A to continuous fields of
C^{*}
-algebras under some additional conditions:

Theorem C
.

Let
E
be a continuous field of
C^{*}
-algebras over a connected, compact, metrizable space
X
. Suppose
E
is separable and exact, and one of the fibers is simple, nuclear and
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
-embeddable. If this fiber is non-unital, or if
E
is unital, then
E
is
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
-embeddable. In the latter case, if the embedding of the fiber is unital, then so is the embedding of
E
.

This follows from Theorem
3.4
, which asserts that
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
-embeddability passes to extensions. The proof makes use of extension theory, and specifically Elliott and Kucerovsky’s characterization of unitally absorbing extensions
[
EK01
]
. As a particular consequence, we show that homotopy equivalence to
\mathcal{Z}
is a sufficient condition to embed into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
for separable, exact
C^{*}
-algebras:

Corollary D
.

Any separable, exact
C^{*}
-algebra
A
which is homotopy equivalent to
\mathcal{Z}
, embeds into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
.

2.
Existence of embeddings into ultrapowers of
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{W}

For an arbitrary
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, we denote the cone over
A
as

(1)

CA:=C_{0}(0,1]\otimes A\cong C_{0}((0,1],A).

We also recall the definition of the ultrapower and the trace-kernel ideal of a
C^{*}
-algebra. For the entirety of this paper, fix a free ultrafilter
\omega
on
\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
B
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. The
ultrapower
of
B
is defined as

(2)

B_{\omega}:=\ell^{\infty}(B)/\{(x_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(B)\mid\lim_{n\to\omega}\|x_{n}\|=0\}.

If moreover the set of tracial states
T(B)
on
B
is non-empty, the
trace-kernel ideal
of
B
is defined as

(3)

J_{B}:=\{(x_{n})_{n}\in B_{\omega}\mid\lim_{n\to\omega}\textstyle{\sup_{\tau\in T(B)}}\tau(x_{n}^{*}x_{n})=0\}.

Proposition 2.2
.

The cone over any UHF algebra embeds into
\mathcal{Z}
and into
\mathcal{W}
. Subsequently, the cone over
\mathcal{Q}_{\omega}
embeds into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
and
\mathcal{W}_{\omega}
.

Proof.

Let
M_{n}
be a UHF algebra corresponding to a supernatural number
\mathfrak{n}
. We first show that
CM_{\mathfrak{n}}
embeds into
\mathcal{Z}
. Applying
[
RW10
, Proposition 3.3]
for
p=1
and
q=\mathfrak{n}
gives a unital embedding of

(4)

Z_{1,\mathfrak{n}}:=\{f\in C([0,1],\mathbb{C}\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}})\mid f(0)\in\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C},\ f(1)\in\mathbb{C}\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}}\}\cong(CM_{\mathfrak{n}})^{\sim},

the unitization of
CM_{\mathfrak{n}}
into
\mathcal{Z}
. In particular,
CM_{\mathfrak{n}}
embeds into
\mathcal{Z}
.

To show that
CM_{\mathfrak{n}}
also embeds into
\mathcal{W}
, write
M_{\mathfrak{n}}
as an inductive limit
\varinjlim M_{n_{k}}
of matrix algebras with connecting maps
\mu_{k}:M_{n_{k}}\to M_{n_{k+1}}
and
n_{k}|n_{k+1}
for all
k
. As such,
CM_{\mathfrak{n}}
is the inductive limit
\varinjlim CM_{n_{k}}
. Consider the Jacelon building blocks as in
[
Jac13
]
: for
\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5)

W(\ell,m):=\{f\in C([0,1],M_{\ell+1}\otimes M_{m})\mid f(0)=(1_{\ell}\oplus 0)\otimes c,\ f(1)=1_{\ell+1}\otimes c\text{ for some }c\in M_{m}\}

and note that
W(\ell,m)\cong W(\ell,1)\otimes M_{m}
. Hence, the embedding

(6)

\iota:C_{0}(0,1]\to W(1,1):f\mapsto f(1)\oplus f

induces embeddings
\iota\otimes\id_{M_{n_{k}}}:CM_{n_{k}}\to W(1,n_{k})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Now let

(7)

\rho_{k}:=\id_{W(1,1)}\otimes\mu_{k}:W(1,n_{k})\to W(1,n_{k+1}),

and note that
\rho_{k}
restricts to
\id_{C_{0}(0,1]}\otimes\mu_{k}
on
CM_{n_{k}}
and preserves the faithful trace
\tau_{k}
on
W(1,n_{k})
induced by the faithful trace on
C[0,1]\otimes M_{2}\otimes M_{n_{k}}
coming from Lebesgue integration on
[0,1]
. Use part (i) of
[
Jac13
, Lemma 4.1]
to find
*
-homomorphisms
\psi_{k}:W(1,n_{k})\to\mathcal{W}
which satisfy
\tau\circ\psi_{k}=\tau_{k}
, where
\tau
is the unique trace on
\mathcal{W}
. In particular, this forces the
\psi_{k}
to be injective. Moreover, part (ii) of
[
Jac13
, Lemma 4.1]
implies that
\psi_{k}
and
\psi_{k+1}\circ\rho_{k}
are approximately unitarily equivalent. A one-sided intertwining argument (see for example
[
Lin01
, Theorem 1.10.14]
) then shows that there exists a copy of the inductive limit
\varinjlim(W(1,n_{k}),\rho_{k})
inside of
\mathcal{W}
, and since the
\rho_{k}
respect the connecting maps
\id_{C_{0}(0,1]}\otimes\mu_{k}:CM_{n_{k}}\subseteq W(1,n_{k})
,
\mathcal{W}
also contains
\varinjlim CM_{n_{k}}\cong CM_{\mathfrak{n}}
.

For the second part of the statement, note that the embedding
C\mathcal{Q}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}
from the previous part of the proof induces an embedding

(8)

C\mathcal{Q}_{\omega}\subseteq\big(C\mathcal{Q}\big)_{\omega}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

As a consequence of the main theorem in
[
Rob12
]
, there exists a trace-preserving embedding of
\mathcal{W}
into
\mathcal{Z}
, which results in an embedding
J_{\mathcal{W}}\hookrightarrow J_{\mathcal{Z}}
. This means that the embedding into
J_{\mathcal{Z}}
can alternatively be obtained through its embedding into
J_{\mathcal{W}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is said to be
traceless
if every lower semicontinuous
2
-quasitrace can only take the values
0
or
\infty
.

Corollary 2.5
.

The cone over any separable
C^{*}
-algebra
A
embeds into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
and into
\mathcal{W}_{\omega}
. If
CA
is traceless, then the embeddings lie within the trace-kernel ideals
J_{\mathcal{Z}}
resp.
J_{\mathcal{W}}
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a separable
C^{*}
-algebra. As
CA
is quasidiagonal by
[
Voi91
]
, there exists an embedding
CA\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{\omega}
, inducing an embedding
C(CA)\hookrightarrow C\mathcal{Q}_{\omega}
. Now, the map
\iota:CA\to C(CA)
given by

(9)

\iota(f):[0,1]^{2}\to A:(t,s)\mapsto f(ts)\quad(f\in CA)

is an injective
*
-homomorphism, so composing it with the map
C(CA)\hookrightarrow C\mathcal{Q}_{\omega}
gives an embedding
CA\hookrightarrow C\mathcal{Q}_{\omega}
. The result then follows from Proposition
2.2
.

For the second part of the statement, assume
CA
is traceless. Note that any trace on
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
must be identically zero on
CA
, as such a trace would induce a non-zero, finite trace on
CA
otherwise. In particular, this holds for the limit trace induced by the unique trace on
\mathcal{Z}
, which implies that the image of
CA
must land in
J_{\mathcal{Z}}
. The same argument holds for
\mathcal{W}_{\omega}
, finishing the proof.
∎

The corollary above is an analogue of
[
BO08
, Theorem 8.3.5]
for
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
- and
\mathcal{W}_{\omega}
-embeddability, showing that both properties are similar in flavor to AF-embeddability. With this in mind, we want to highlight what happens in the traceless case. In this setting, all these notions of embeddability turn out to be equivalent, as a consequence of the results in
[
Gab20
]
and Corollary
2.5
:

Corollary 2.6
.

For
A
separable, exact, and traceless, the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
embeds into
C_{0}(0,1]\otimes\mathcal{O}_{2}
.

(2)

A
is quasidiagonal.

(3)

A
is AF-embeddable.

(4)

\operatorname{Prim}(A)
has no nonempty compact open subsets.

(5)

A
is stably finite.

(6)

A
is stably projectionless.

(7)

A
embeds into
J_{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
.

(8)

A
embeds into
J_{\mathcal{W}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\omega}
.

Proof.

The equivalence between the first four items above is
[
Gab20
, Theorem A]
, and Corollary C from the same paper adds (5) and (6).
The implications (
1
)
\implies
(
7
) and (
1
)
\implies
(
8
) are Corollary
2.5
for
A=\mathcal{O}_{2}
.

(
7
)
\implies
(
5
): As
\mathcal{Z}
is stably finite, its ultrapower
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
is stably finite by
[
Far+21
, §3.6(d)]
. It follows that
A
is also stably finite, being a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of the stably finite
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
.

(
8
)
\implies
(
5
): As
\mathcal{W}
is stably projectionless and therefore stably finite, the same argument as above does the trick.
∎

3.
Extensions and continuous fields of
C^{*}
-algebras

In this section, we prove our second result, that certain extensions of
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
-embeddable
C^{*}
-algebras remain
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
-embeddable. The idea was inspired by its analogue for AF-embeddability (see for example
[
BO08
, Proposition 8.4.9]
), but the approach taken needs to be quite different, as
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
-embeddability is not preserved under stabilizations in general. Our proof makes extensive use of extension theory and specifically Elliott and Kucerovsky’s characterization of unitally absorbing extensions
[
EK01
]
. We give a brief overview of the relevant concepts; for a more in-depth discussion as well as proofs of the assertions made, see for example
[
Bla98
, Chapter VII]
or
[
GR20
, §2]
.

Extension theory

An
extension
of a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
by a
C^{*}
-algebra
I
is a short exact sequence of the form

(10)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{e}:0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

An extension is said to be
split
if it has a
*
-homomorphic splitting, and
unital
if
E
(and therefore necessarily also
A
) is unital. Extensions of
A
by
I
correspond one-to-one (up to congruence of extensions) to
*
-homomorphisms
A\to\mathcal{Q}(I)
(where
\mathcal{Q}(I):=\mathcal{M}(I)/I
is the corona algebra of
I
), which are known as their
Busby maps
. Two extensions
\mathfrak{e}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{e}_{2}
of
A
by
I
are said to be
strongly unitarily equivalent
, denoted
\mathfrak{e}_{1}\sim_{s}\mathfrak{e}_{2}
, if their corresponding Busby maps are unitarily equivalent by a unitary descending from a unitary in
\mathcal{M}(I)
; in this case, the extension algebras are also isomorphic.

We will only be concerned with the situation where
I
is
\sigma
-unital and
A
is separable. If
I
is stable, then one can define a direct sum
\oplus
within
\mathcal{M}(I)
(uniquely up to unitary equivalence), which allows us to define an addition
\oplus
on the set of extensions of
A
by
I
. As such, one obtains an abelian monoid
\mathrm{Ext}(A,I)
of extensions with this direct sum as binary operation, modulo the equivalence relation where two extensions
\mathfrak{e}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{e}_{2}
of
A
by
I
are equivalent if
\mathfrak{e}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{f}\sim_{s}\mathfrak{e}_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{f}
for some split extension
\mathfrak{f}
of
A
by
I
. The neutral element of
\mathrm{Ext}(A,I)
then becomes the equivalence class of split extensions. If moreover
A
is nuclear, then
\mathrm{Ext}(A,I)
is in fact a group.

In this context, an extension
\mathfrak{e}
is said to be
absorbing
if
\mathfrak{e}\oplus\mathfrak{f}\sim_{s}\mathfrak{e}
for all split extensions
\mathfrak{f}
. Similarly, a unital extension
\mathfrak{e}
is said to be
unitally absorbing
if the same holds for all
\mathfrak{f}
with a unital splitting. Remarkably, Elliott and Kucerovsky characterized unitally absorbing extensions by the following inherent property, in
[
EK01
]
. Recall that, for two positive elements
a
and
b
of a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
,
a
is said to be
Cuntz subequivalent
to
b
, denoted
a\precsim b
, if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
v\in A
such that
v^{*}bv\approx_{\varepsilon}a
. Here, we denoted
x\approx_{\varepsilon}y
for elements

and
y
in a
C^{*}
-algebra

to mean
{\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert}<\varepsilon
.

Definition 3.1
.

An extension

(11)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{e}:0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}

This is not the original definition of pure largeness given by Elliott and Kucerovsky. However, it is shown to be equivalent to pure largeness when the ideal is

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
be
C^{*}
-algebras with
C
and
D
unital. If the canonical surjection
A\otimes_{\max}B\to A\otimes B
is an isomorphism (in particular, if
A
or
B
is nuclear), and there exist embeddings
A\hookrightarrow C_{\omega}
and
B\hookrightarrow D_{\omega}
, then
A\otimes B
embeds into
(C\otimes_{\alpha}D)_{\omega}
for any
C^{*}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
on
C\odot D
. Moreover, if
A
and
B
are unital and the given embeddings are unital, then the resulting embedding of

Proof.

Let
\iota_{A}:A\hookrightarrow C_{\omega}
and
\iota_{B}:B\hookrightarrow D_{\omega}
be the given embeddings, and consider

(20)

\displaystyle\varphi_{C}

\displaystyle:C_{\omega}\to(C\otimes_{\alpha}D)_{\omega}:(c_{n})_{n}\mapsto(c_{n}\otimes 1_{D})_{n},

\displaystyle\varphi_{D}

\displaystyle:D_{\omega}\to(C\otimes_{\alpha}D)_{\omega}:(d_{n})_{n}\mapsto(1_{C}\otimes d_{n})_{n}.

These are well-defined
*
-homomorphisms with commuting ranges. Indeed, the fact that they are well-defined follows from
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
being a cross norm
[
BO08
, Lemma 3.4.10]
; the other properties are immediate. By universality of the maximal tensor product and the isomorphism
A\otimes B\cong A\otimes_{\max}B
, the maps
\varphi_{C}\circ\iota_{A}
and
\varphi_{D}\circ\iota_{B}
then induce a
*
-homomorphism

(21)

\psi:=(\varphi_{C}\circ\iota_{A})\times(\varphi_{D}\circ\iota_{B}):A\otimes B\to(C\otimes_{\alpha}D)_{\omega}:a\otimes b\mapsto(\iota_{A}(a)_{n}\otimes\iota_{B}(b)_{n})_{n},

where
\iota_{A}(a)_{n}
denotes the
n
-th element of a representatitive sequence of
\iota_{A}(a)
(and similarly for
\iota_{B}(b)_{n}
). We now claim that this map is injective. To see this, note that it suffices to show injectivity on elementary tensors, since any non-zero ideal
I
in
A\otimes B
contains a non-zero elementary tensor. Indeed, by Kirchberg’s slice lemma
[
Rør02
, Lemma 4.1.9]
, there exists a non-zero
z\in A\otimes B
such that
z^{*}z
is an elementary tensor and
zz^{*}\in I
. By polar decomposition in
C^{*}
-algebras, there exists
w\in A\otimes B
such that
z^{*}=w(zz^{*})^{1/4}\in I
, so
I
contains the non-zero elementary tensor
z^{*}z
.

Take
a\in A
and
b\in B
such that
\psi(a\otimes b)=0
, and denote
(a_{n})_{n}:=\iota_{A}(a)
and
(b_{n})_{n}:=\iota_{B}(b)
. Recalling that
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
is a cross norm, we then find

(22)

0=\lim_{n\to\omega}\|a_{n}\otimes b_{n}\|_{\alpha}=\lim_{n\to\omega}\|a_{n}\|\cdot\|b_{n}\|=\lim_{n\to\omega}\|a_{n}\|\cdot\lim_{n\to\omega}\|b_{n}\|.

Hence,
(a_{n})_{n}
or
(b_{n})_{n}
has to tend to zero along
\omega
. This means that
\iota_{A}(a)=0
or
\iota_{B}(b)=0
, which implies that
a=0
or
b=0
. We conclude that
\psi
is indeed injective.

In the setting where
A
,
B
,
\iota_{A}
and
\iota_{B}
are all unital, it is clear from the definitions that the same holds for
\varphi_{C}
and
\varphi_{D}
, and therefore also for
\psi
. This finishes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

(23)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{e}:0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

be an extension of
C^{*}
-algebras with
I
such that
\mathrm{Prim}(I)
contains no non-empty compact open subsets,
E
separable and exact, and
D
simple and nuclear. If
D
is non-unital and embeds into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, or if
E
(and therefore also
D
) is unital and
D
embeds into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, then so does
E
. In the latter case,
E
embeds unitally if
D
does.

Proof.

As
I
is separable and exact, it follows from
[
Gab20
, Theorem A]
that
I
embeds into Rørdam’s ASH-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{[0,1]}
(see
[
Rør04
]
). Letting
J
be the hereditary
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{[0,1]}
generated by the image of
I
, we obtain a non-degenerate inclusion
\iota:I\hookrightarrow J
. Now,
\mathcal{A}_{[0,1]}
is nuclear and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
-stable by
[
KR05
, Proposition 6.1]
and its ideal lattice is order isomorphic to
[0,1]
by
[
Rør04
, Proposition 2.1]
, so it follows from
[
Bos+22
, Theorem B]
that
J
is stable. Moreover,
\mathcal{O}_{2}
-stability of
\mathcal{A}_{[0,1]}
passes to
J
.

Since the inclusion
\iota
is non-degenerate, it induces a pushforward extension
\mathfrak{e}_{0}

(24)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{e}:0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{e}_{0}:0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

where the map
E\to E_{0}
is injective due to
\iota
being injective. By the previous lemma, we know that the extension

(25)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{f}:0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J\otimes D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J^{\dagger}\otimes D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

is purely large. Moreover, it follows from Corollary
2.6
and Proposition
3.3
that
{J^{\dagger}\otimes D}
embeds into
(\mathcal{Z}\otimes\mathcal{Z})_{\omega}\cong\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, as
\mathcal{Z}
is strongly self-absorbing
[
JS99
, Theorem 7.6 and 8.8]
. Since
J
is stable and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
-stable and
D
is simple and nuclear, the
\mathcal{O}_{2}
-absorption theorem
[
KP00
]
implies that
J\otimes D\cong J
, so both
\mathfrak{e}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{f}
are extensions of
D
by
J
. The goal is now to show that
\mathfrak{e}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{f}
are strongly unitarily equivalent. This will imply that the extension algebras
E_{0}
and
J^{\dagger}\otimes D
are isomorphic, giving the desired embedding of
E
into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
.

First assume
D
is non-unital. As
\mathfrak{f}
is purely large, it is absorbing by
[
Gab16
, Theorem 2.1]
. Moreover, we can assume
\mathfrak{e}_{0}
is absorbing without loss of generality. Indeed, taking a split, absorbing extension
\mathfrak{f}_{0}
of
D
by
J
, the sum
\mathfrak{e}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{f}_{0}
is absorbing, and its extension algebra is given by

(26)

M=\{(m,d)\in\mathcal{M}(J)\oplus D\mid\pi(m)=\tau_{e}(d)\oplus\tau_{f}(d)\},

where
\pi:\mathcal{M}(J)\to\mathcal{Q}(J)
is the quotient map,
\tau_{e},\tau_{f}:D\to\mathcal{Q}(J)
are the Busby maps corresponding to
\mathfrak{e}_{0}
resp.
\mathfrak{f}_{0}
and
\tau_{e}(d)\oplus\tau_{f}(d)
denotes the direct sum of
\tau_{e}(d)
and
\tau_{f}(d)
in
\mathcal{Q}(J)
induced by the direct sum in
\mathcal{M}(J)
. We can then embed
E_{0}
into
M
by noting that the
*
-homomorphism

(27)

E_{0}\to\mathcal{M}(J)\oplus D:x\mapsto(\sigma_{e}(x)\oplus(\sigma_{f}\circ\phi\circ q)(x),q(x))

is injective and maps into
M
, where
q:E_{0}\to D
is the quotient map,
\phi:D\to E_{0}
is a splitting of
\mathfrak{f}_{0}
and
\sigma_{e},\sigma_{f}:E_{0}\to\mathcal{M}(J)
are the canonical maps descending to
\tau_{e}
resp.
\tau_{f}
. This means that it suffices to embed
M
into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, so we can replace
\mathfrak{e}_{0}
by the absorbing extension
\mathfrak{e}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{f}_{0}
.

In summary, both
\mathfrak{e}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{f}
are absorbing extensions in

\mathfrak{f}

Now suppose

is unital. It then follows from
[

that
\mathfrak{f}
is a unitally absorbing extension. By using the same trick as in the non-unital case (instead adding a unital, unitally absorbing extension to
\mathfrak{e}_{0}
), we can also arrange that
\mathfrak{e}_{0}
is a unital, unitally absorbing extension. Furthermore, due to
\mathrm{Ext}(D,J)
and
K_{0}(J)
being trivial, it follows from
[
MT06
, Theorem 3]
that
\mathrm{Ext}_{us}(D,J)
is also trivial. Hence,
\mathfrak{e}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{f}
must be strongly unitarily equivalent.

For the final part of the statement, note that the embedding

is unital whenever
E
is unital. If the embedding of
D
into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
is unital, then the same holds for the embedding

, as mentioned in Proposition
3.3
. Therefore, the embedding

constructed above is indeed unital.
∎

.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
A
and
B
be
C^{*}
-algebras. We say
A
is
homotopically dominated by
B
if there exist
*
-homomorphisms
\varphi:A\to B
and
\psi:B\to A
such that
\psi\circ\varphi\sim_{h}\id_{A}
. We say
A
is
homotopy equivalent to
B
if moreover
\varphi\circ\psi\sim_{h}\id_{B}
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
A
and
B
be separable
C^{*}
-algebras with
A
exact and
B
simple and nuclear. If
A
is homotopically dominated by
B
and
B
embeds into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, then so does
A
. In particular, if a separable, exact
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathcal{Z}
, then it embeds into
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:A\to B
and
\psi:B\to A
be
*
-homomorphisms such that
\psi\circ\varphi\sim_{h}\id_{A}
; say
\Phi:A\to C([0,1],A)
is a
*
-homomorphism such that
\Phi_{0}=\id_{A}
and
\Phi_{1}=\psi\circ\varphi
. The mapping cone of
\psi
,

(32)

E:=\{(f,b)\in C([0,1],A)\oplus B\mid f(1)=\psi(b)\},

is a separable and exact continuous field of

. Moreover, the fiber at

, so the previous corollary applies and

.

.

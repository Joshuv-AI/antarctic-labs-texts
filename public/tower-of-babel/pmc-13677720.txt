Practical synthesis of 3-Oxa/Aza-bicyclo[3.1.1]heptanes from 3-Oxa/Aza[3.1.1]propellanes
Authors: Qin Jiang, Jianyang Dong, Fang Lei, Yanfeng Han, Mingxin Li, Yilin Zhang, Juan Fan, Huaming Sun, Ting Li, Shuoyi Chen, Zhendan Chen, Dong Xue
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
While bicyclo[1.1.1]pentanes (BCPs) and bicyclo[3.1.1]heptanes (BCHeps) serve as established 3D bioisosteres for para- and meta-substituted benzenes, accessible via ring-opening of their corresponding propellanes, the integration of heteroatoms into these propellane scaffolds has remained elusive. Herein, we report the practical synthesis of 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellanes, two heteroatom-containing analogues of propellane, from 1,5-diiodo-3-hetero-BCHeps. On this basis, radical-based transformations of 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellane are established, enabling efficient and divergent access to a broad range of bridgehead-disubstituted 3-oxa/aza-BCHep derivatives as saturated isosteres of meta-substituted benzenes. Furthermore, representative 3-oxa-BCHep derivatives are converted into functionalized synthetic building blocks; this scaffold is also introduced into the antiepileptic drug URB597 and anticancer drug sonidegib. These results demonstrate that the 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1] frameworks provide a general and feasible synthetic platform for the construction of heteroatom-containing cage-like bioisosteres and the optimization of drug properties.

Introduction

The replacement of planar aromatic rings with three-dimensional (3D) caged scaffolds represents a key aspect of the “escape from flatland” concept and continues to gain interest in medicinal chemistry and drug discovery1–9. This strategy has proven effective in improving key pharmaceutical properties, including metabolic stability and aqueous solubility. Among such 3D scaffolds, bridgehead-disubstituted bicyclo[1.1.1]pentanes (BCPs)10–19 and bicyclo[3.1.1]heptanes (BCHeps)20–25 are prominent examples, serving as bioisosteres of para- and meta-substituted benzenes (Fig. 1A), respectively. For instance, BCHeps have been proposed to mimic the geometry of meta-substituted benzenes in bioactive compounds. Both scaffolds exhibit similar exit vector angles (119–120°), comparable inter-substituent distances (4.8–5.0 Å), and analogous physicochemical properties20. These valuable motifs are typically prepared via ring-opening reactions of their corresponding propellanes ([1.1.1]propellane and [3.1.1]propellane). Despite their utility, these monumental achievements have remained confined to all-carbon systems. Notably, Mykhailiuk and co-workers demonstrated that incorporation of heteroatoms, particularly oxygen and nitrogen, into such 3D frameworks can enhance water solubility, improve metabolic stability, and reduce lipophilicity relative to their all-carbon bicyclic and benzene counterparts (Fig. 1A)26–28. This effect is particularly pronounced in BCHep systems, where the larger ring size inherently lowers solubility, making the solubilizing effect of heteroatoms more impactful; in contrast, introducing heteroatoms into the smaller BCP core often compromises structural stability29–38. Intermolecular cycloadditions of bicyclobutanes with substituted 1,3-dipoles have become the main synthetic route to diverse oxa/aza-BCHeps39–42. An alternative strategy, reported by Mykhailiuk28,43 and Ryabchuk44, involves the reduction of a cyclobutane-tethered ester/nitrile followed by Lewis acid-promoted cyclization onto an oxetane, furnishing 3-oxa/aza-BCHeps without bridge substituents. While these methods provide viable routes to these valuable oxa/aza-BCHep scaffolds, the propellane ring-opening approach—despite its longer historical precedence—remains relatively elusive for the synthesis of oxa/aza-BCHeps. Recognizing 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellanes as highly attractive precursors to 3-oxa/aza-BCHeps, we targeted their synthesis. Ring-opening of these hetero-propellanes would allow direct, one-step access to bridgehead-disubstituted 3-oxa/aza-BCHeps.

Two classical approaches have been established for preparing propellanes (Fig. 1B): (1) the double lithiation–cyclization of tetrahalogenated cyclopropanes reported by Szeimies (the Szeimies’ precursor route)45, and (2) reduction of 1,5-dihalogenated bicyclo[n.1.1]alkanes developed by Gassman (the Gassman’ precursor route)46. Both [3.1.1]- and [1.1.1]propellane have been successfully synthesized following these two routes, as reported by the research groups of Baran47, Anderson20 and Uchiyama48. Notably, Anderson and co-workers have very recently reported an elegant synthesis of 3-hetero[3.1.1]propellane by adapting the Szeimies strategy. Their approach involves a single lithiation–cyclization step of a dibrominated cyclopropane precursor, which is accessed via an initial rhodium-catalyzed cyclopropanation using a diazo ester49. Inspired by Gassman’s reductive approach to all-carbon propellanes, we sought to develop an alternative and more practical synthetic strategy. We postulated that 1,5-diiodo-3-hetero-BCHeps could serve as viable precursors, undergoing rapid iodine–metal exchange to generate the desired 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellanes. Herein, we report the successful implementation of the Gassman’ strategy for the synthesis of two heteroatom-containing propellanes, establishing the practical route to 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellanes from the key intermediates 1,5-diiodo-3-hetero-BCHeps (Fig. 1C). Furthermore, we develop a suite of radical-based transformations of 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellanes, enabling efficient and divergent access to a broad range of medicinally relevant carbon- and heteroatom-substituted 3-oxa/aza-BCHeps, including pharmaceutically important analogues (Fig. 1C). Geometric and physicochemical evaluations indicate that 3-oxa-BCHeps closely resemble meta-substituted benzenes while offering improved solubility, reduced lipophilicity, and enhanced metabolic stability. These findings validate 3-oxa/aza-BCHeps as promising, sp³-rich bioisosteres for meta-arene motifs in drug design and underscore the potential of heteropropellane chemistry to expand the toolkit of three-dimensional scaffolds in medicinal chemistry.

Results and discussion

Synthesis of 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellanes

Our synthetic efforts commenced with the development of a practical route to 3-oxa[3.1.1]propellane. An optimized procedure was established starting from commercially available oxetane 1 (Fig. 2A). Treatment of 1 with diethyl malonate in the presence of sodium hydride in DMF proceeded cleanly, affording spirocyclic diester 2 in 87% yield. Subsequent reduction of both ester groups with LiAlH₄, followed by direct isomerization of the resulting diol with BF₃•Et₂O, provided crude 3-oxa-BCHep diol. This material was used directly in the next step, where oxidation with Bu₄NRuO₄ and NMO yielded 3-oxa-BCHep-1,5-dicarboxylic acid 3. To maximize the overall yield, the spirocyclic diol and the 3-oxa-BCHep diol intermediates were deliberately omitted from purification. Consequently, compound 3 was obtained in 23% yield over three steps on a scale exceeding 30 mmol, requiring only a single chromatographic purification. Iododecarboxylation of 3 with DIH then furnished the key intermediate 1,5-diiodo-3-oxa-BCHep 4. Finally, reaction of 4 with 1.4 equivalents of phenyllithium, followed by filtration and concentration, provided 3-oxa[3.1.1]propellane 5 in 80–90% yield on a scale exceeding 10 mmol. The resulting solution of 5 (0.35–0.90 M in dibutyl ether) showed remarkable stability, with no observable decomposition over several months of storage at −20 °C under an inert atmosphere. With 3-oxa[3.1.1]propellane in hand, we next investigated whether this synthetic route could be extended to access 3-aza[3.1.1]propellane (Fig. 2B). Treatment of 1 with tert-butyl 2-cyanoacetate in the presence of K₂CO₃ in DMF afforded spirocyclic nitrile 6 in 76% yield. Subsequent reduction of the nitrile group with NaBH₄ and CoCl₂, followed by direct isomerization of the resulting primary amine and standard N-Ts protection, provided 3-aza-BCHep 7 in 75% yield. Swern oxidation of alcohol 7 gave the corresponding aldehyde, which was further oxidized to the 3-aza-BCHep acid 8 in 78% yield. Iododecarboxylation of 8 with (diacetoxyiodo)benzene and iodine then afforded 3-aza-BCHep iodide 9 in 73% yield. Standard hydrolysis of the ester group in 9, followed by a second iododecarboxylation, furnished the key intermediate 1,5-diiodo-3-aza-BCHep 10. Finally, reaction of 10 with 1.4 equivalents of methyllithium, followed by filtration and concentration, provided 3-aza[3.1.1]propellane 11 as a white solid in 85–95% yield. The resulting solid 11 exhibited remarkable stability, with no observable decomposition over several weeks of storage at −20 °C. Notably, unlike previously reported propellanes that were obtained only in ether solvents, this represents the synthesis of a propellane in solid form.

Synthesis of 3-oxa/aza-BCHeps from 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellanes

With the robust synthesis of 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellanes 5 and 11 in hand, we systematically investigated their reactivity in radical ring-opening reaction with various alkyl halides and heteroaryl iodides (Fig. 3)50. Photoredox-catalyzed (Ir(ppy)₃) conditions efficiently facilitated the addition of diverse alkyl C–I bonds across the 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellanes, yielding a range of 3-oxa/aza-BCHep scaffolds (Fig. 3A). The reaction tolerated α-iodocarbonyls (delivering 12–15 in good yields), with the C–C bond formation unaffected by α-substituents on iodoacetates (giving 16 and 17 in 84% and 61% yields, respectively). Furthermore, 2-iodo-1,1,1-trifluoroethane was also available, delivering the desired products 18 and 19 in 68% and 79% yields, respectively. The scope could also be extended to unactivated primary alkyl iodides (20–23), 2-iodo-1,1,1-trifluoroethane (24 and 25), halomethanes (26–28), perfluoroalkyl iodides (29–31), and a benzylic iodide (32), all affording the corresponding products in moderate to good yields. These products offer multiple handles for further elaboration through transformations of the ester, alkene, and carbon–halogen (C–X; X = Cl, Br, I) groups. We were able to demonstrate the wider potential of 3-oxa[3.1.1]propellane by the synthesis of pharmaceutically-relevant galactopyranose derivative 33 in 66% yield. Notably, unactivated secondary alkyl iodides were also viable, yielding products 34–38 in moderate yields. Next, the scope was extended to heteroaromatic iodides (Fig. 3B), including 2-iodopyridine (39–43), 2-iodoquinoline (44–46), and 1-iodoisoquinoline (47). Particularly noteworthy was the selective mono-ring-opening reaction observed with 2-bromo-6-iodopyridine, affording product 42. More electron-deficient azines such as 2-iodopyrimidine (48–50) and 2-iodopyrazine (51 and 52), also proved to be suitable substrates. Given the prevalence of heteroaromatic rings in pharmaceuticals, we are hopeful that this ring-opening methodology will find utility in drug discovery efforts.

Finally, the bromotrichloromethane was also suitable for this reaction, obtaining the expected bromo-3-oxa-BCHep product 53 (68% yield) and bromo-3-aza-BCHep product 54 (88% yield) (Fig. 3C).

We then study the geometry of the 3-oxa-BCHep scaffold (Fig. 3D). To assess core similarity with the meta-substituted arenes, we measured the key C–C distance (r); to evaluate angular profile, we measured the exit vector angle φ. The distance r in 3-oxa-BCHeps was approximately 0.3 Å shorter than in meta-arene (2.1 vs. 2.4 Å). In addition, the angle φ was similar in both scaffolds and close to the ideal 120°. In summary, the 3-oxa-BCHep core closely resembles the meta-benzene ring in its key geometric characteristics (r and φ).

Notably, 3-oxa[3.1.1]propellane exhibits a reactivity profile analogous to its carbon-based counterparts, [1.1.1]propellane and [3.1.1]propellane, across a range of transformations (Fig. 4). For example, hydrothiolation gave compound 55 in 73% yield, while selenoetherification at room temperature afforded 56 in 77% yield51. In addition to the above carbohalogenation reactions, insertion of 3-oxa[3.1.1]propellane into the N−I bond of N-iodopyrazole was achieved without any initiator (delivering 57 in 43% yield)52. Finally, sulfonothioate addition with 3-oxa[3.1.1]propellane smoothly furnished 58 in 91% yield. Modifications

Having established the synthetic feasibility of 3-oxa/aza-BCHeps, we next converted several representative 3-oxa-BCHeps into building blocks for medicinal chemistry (Fig. 5A). Standard hydrolysis of pivaloate ester 20 gave primary alcohol 59 in 83% yield. Subjecting 59 to a Mitsunobu reaction with 4-chlorophenol afforded aryl ether 60 (62% yield). Dess-Martin oxidation of 59 provided aldehyde 61 (75% yield), which served as a versatile intermediate: reductive amination yielded amine 62 (65% yield); reaction with the Ohira–Bestmann reagent gave alkyne 63 (44% yield); and a Wittig reaction delivered α, β-unsaturated ester 64 (58% yield). Importantly, these 3-oxa-BCHep building blocks are not accessible by other routes. Furthermore, 1-(bromomethyl)-5-iodo-3-oxa-BCHep (27), which features both a primary C–Br bond and a tertiary C–I bond, served as another building block. Incorporation into a drug

To underscore the synthetic utility of the 3-oxa-BCHep scaffold, we incorporated it as a meta-arene bioisostere into the antiepileptic drug URB597 (Fig. 6A)55. The 3-oxa-BCHep analogue 69 was synthesized from Kumada cross-coupling product 67 via a five-step sequence: pivaloate hydrolysis, oxidation of the resulting alcohol to carboxylic acid 68, followed by amide formation, debenzylation, and final carbamoylation with cyclohexyl isocyanate, yielding the target molecule in 10% overall yield. Computational comparison of the lowest-energy conformations revealed that the 3-oxa-BCHep analogue 69 accurately reproduces the spatial arrangement of the parent drug URB597. We next evaluated key physicochemical properties of URB597 and its saturated analogue 69 (Fig. 6C). Replacement of the meta-benzene ring in URB597 with the 3-oxa-BCHep scaffold resulted in a sevenfold increase in aqueous solubility (19.97 µM for 69 vs. 2.84 µM for URB597). Consistent with this trend, the isosteric substitution moderately lowered experimental lipophilicity (log D: 2.39 for 69 vs. 3.77 for URB597) and calculated clog P (1.79 vs. 3.69). The impact on metabolic stability was particularly pronounced. Incorporating the 3-oxa-BCHep motif dramatically enhanced stability in human liver microsomes, reducing the intrinsic clearance (CLint) from 56.50 µL min−1 mg−1 for URB597 to below 5.42 for 69. Correspondingly, the half-life increased more than tenfold, from 24.53 min for URB597 to over 255.85 min for 69. In summary, replacing the meta-arene in URB597 with the saturated 3-oxa-BCHep scaffold (69) led to substantial improvements across all measured physicochemical parameters—solubility, lipophilicity, and metabolic stability—highlighting the value of this bioisosteric replacement in drug design.

In addition, we incorporated the 3-oxa-BCHep scaffold into the anticancer drug sonidegib (Fig. 6B)56. First, applying the iron-catalyzed Kumada cross-coupling protocol converted iodide 20 into the aryl-substituted derivative 70 in 51% yield. From 70, a two-step sequence—pivaloate hydrolysis followed by oxidation of the resulting primary alcohol—delivered carboxylic acid 71 (54% overall yield). Finally, amide bond formation with amine 72 afforded 3-oxa-BCHep sonidegib 73.

We next evaluated the key physicochemical properties of sonidegib and its saturated analogue 73 (Fig. 6C). Replacement of the benzene ring in sonidegib with the 3-oxa-BCHep scaffold dramatically improved the aqueous solubility (11.54 µM for 73 vs. below 0.01 µM for sonidegib). The isosteric substitution moderately lowered the experimental lipophilicity (log D: 4.66 for 73 vs. 5.63 for sonidegib) and the calculated clog P (4.72 vs. 6.43). Interestingly, incorporation of the 3-oxa-BCHep motif decreased metabolic stability in human liver microsomes, raising the intrinsic clearance (CLint) from 11.49 µL·min⁻¹·mg⁻¹ for sonidegib to 20.60 µL·min⁻¹·mg⁻¹ for 73. Correspondingly, the half-life decreased from 120.64 min for sonidegib to 67.28 min for 73.

In conclusion, starting from 1,5-diiodo-3-hetero-BCHep, this study successfully constructed the heteroatom-containing propellane analogues 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellane using the Gassman strategy, and systematically demonstrated its applicability in a series of radical-based transformations, enabling the efficient synthesis of various functionalized 3-oxa/aza-BCHep derivatives as saturated bioisosteres of meta-substituted benzene rings. This synthetic platform further allows structural diversification of representative 3-oxa-BCHep scaffolds to generate pharmacologically relevant, functionalized synthetic building blocks. Incorporation of this scaffold into the antiepileptic drug URB597 and anticancer drug sonidegib, replacing the original meta-arene fragment, significantly improved key physicochemical properties such as solubility, lipophilicity, and metabolic stability. We anticipate that this work will promote the development of ring-opening functionalization chemistry of 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellanes and accelerate the broad application of the 3-hetero-BCHep core structure in drug discovery.

Methods

Standard procedure for 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellane of alkyl halides or heteroaryl iodides

To a 10 mL dry Schlenk tube equipped with a magnetic stir bar was added Ir(ppy)3 (2.5 mol%), and the tube was evacuated and backfilled with N2 three times. tBuCN (0.1 M), alkyl or aryl halide (1.0 equiv.), and 3-oxa/aza[3.1.1]propellane (1.5 equiv.) were added under N2 atmosphere. The stirred mixture was irradiated with blue LEDs with fan cooling for the indicated time. The reaction mixture was concentrated, and the residue was purified by column chromatography to afford the 3-oxa/aza-BCHeps.

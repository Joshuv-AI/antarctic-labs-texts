An index for unitarizable 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) {\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)} -supermodules
Steffen Schmidt, Johannes Walcher

Abstract
. The “superconformal index” is a character-valued invariant attached by theoretical physics to unitary representations of Lie superalgebras, such as 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 2 , 2 | n ) {\mathfrak{su}}(2,2|n) , that govern certain quantum field theories. The index can be calculated as a supertrace over Hilbert space, and is constant in families induced by variation of physical parameters. This is because the index receives contributions only from “short” irreducible representations such that it is invariant under recombination at the boundary of the region of unitarity. The purpose of this paper is to develop these notions for unitarizable supermodules over the special linear Lie superalgebras 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) {\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)} with m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . To keep it self-contained, we include a fair amount of background material on structure theory, unitarizable supermodules, the Duflo-Serganova functor, and elements of Harish-Chandra theory. Along the way, we provide a precise dictionary between various notions from theoretical physics and mathematical terminology. Our final result is a kind of “index theorem” that relates the counting of atypical constituents in a general unitarizable 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) {\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)} -supermodule to the character-valued Q Q -Witten index, expressed as a supertrace over the full supermodule. The formal superdimension of holomorphic discrete series 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) {\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)} -supermodules can also be formulated in this framework.

0.3.
Standing assumptions

The thrust of the paper being representation theoretic, it seems worthwhile to clarify early on that the main
interest is

Equivalently, we can think geometrically of the datum of a bundle

-supermodules over

Concretely, with

({\mathfrak{O}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)

(2|2)

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

such that the original

Specifically, let

W=V\oplus V^{*}

\Omega(a,b):=f(w)-(-1)^{ab}g(v)
for all homogeneous

{\mathrm{S}}(V)

with

The oscillator supermodule
{\mathfrak{O}}

(x,\eta)
of

{\mathfrak{sl}}(2|1)
as the Lie superalgebra spanned by the following set of super-differential operators acting on

(14)

\begin{gathered}E=-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{2},\;H=-x\partial_{x}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}},\;F={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}x^{2},\;J=\eta\partial_{\eta}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}},\\

(which is the superadjoint with respect to super Hermitian form

1.4
) satisfies
\sigma(ab)=(-1)^{ab}\sigma(b)\sigma(a)

for all

13
) on the vector
fields (
14
). Crucially,

(18)

Q^{\dagger}=\bar{S},\qquad S^{\dagger}=-\bar{Q}

and

(19)

These expressions are indeed non-negative operators on
{\mathfrak{O}}

1
)

and

can be calculated algebraically.

(20)

\begin{split}I^{W}_{{\mathfrak{O}}^{+}}(Q)&=\operatorname{tr}_{{\mathfrak{O}}^{+}}(-1)^{F}e^{-\beta[Q,Q^{\dagger}]}=0,\\
I^{W}_{{\mathfrak{O}}^{-}}(Q)&=\operatorname{tr}_{{\mathfrak{O}}^{-}}(-1)^{F}e^{-\beta[Q,Q^{\dagger}]}=-1,\\
I^{W}_{{\mathfrak{O}}^{+}}(S)&=\operatorname{tr}_{{\mathfrak{O}}^{+}}(-1)^{F}e^{-\beta[S,S^{\dagger}]}=+1,\\
I^{W}_{{\mathfrak{O}}^{-}}(S)&=\operatorname{tr}_{{\mathfrak{O}}^{-}}(-1)^{F}e^{-\beta[S,S^{\dagger}]}=0.\end{split}

They can be viewed as a special case of the analytic result for the general square-zero supercharge

(21)

Q_{(r,t)}=rQ-tS=\eta(rx+t\partial_{x})

as a function of
[r:t]\in{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

(22)

Q_{(r,t)}^{\dagger}=\bar{r}\bar{S}+\bar{t}\bar{Q}=\partial_{\eta}(\bar{r}\partial_{x}+\bar{t}x)

and

(23)

\Xi_{(r,t)}=[Q_{(r,t)},Q_{(r,t)}^{\dagger}]=|r|^{2}(J-H)-|t|^{2}(J+H)+2r\bar{t}F-2\bar{r}tE.

Their formal joint kernel

\{f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x,\eta]]\ :\ Q_{(r,t)}f=Q_{(r,t)}^{\dagger}f=0\}
is two-dimensional, and spanned
by

(24)

f_{+}=\exp\Bigl(-\frac{r}{t}\frac{x^{2}}{2}\Bigr)\,,\qquad f_{-}=\eta\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\bar{t}}{\bar{r}}\frac{x^{2}}{2}\Bigr)\,.

Now

(25)

\langle f_{+},f_{+}\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigl|\frac{r}{2t}\Bigr|^{2n}\frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^{2}}=\Bigl(1-\Bigl|\frac{r}{t}\Bigr|^{2}\Bigr)^{-1/2}

converges,
i.e.
,

, iff
\bigl|\frac{r}{t}\bigr|<1
(and complementarily for
f_{-}
).
As a result,

(26)

\begin{split}I^{W}_{{\mathfrak{O}}^{+}}(Q_{(r,t)})=\begin{cases}+1&\text{if $|r|<|t|$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\\
I^{W}_{{\mathfrak{O}}^{-}}(Q_{(r,t)})=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if $|t|<|r|$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\end{split}

We see in this example that the index is in fact not continuous as a function on the space of nilpotent
supercharges. This observation does not seem to have been recorded in the literature so far, and will be
interesting to explore further.

1.3.
Highest weight supermodules and atypicality

Highest and lowest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are a particularly important type of supermodules
over
{\mathfrak{g}}
. On the one hand, any simple finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is both a highest and
lowest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. On the other hand, as reviewed in detail in section
1.4
,
any unitarizable simple (in general, infinite-dimensional) supermodule is either a highest or a
lowest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule.

Definition 1.3.1
.

A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is called
highest (lowest) weight

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
of highest (lowest) weight

\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
if there exists a nonzero
v_{\Lambda}\in M
such that:

a)

Xv_{\Lambda}=0
for all
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
(
{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}
),

b)

Hv_{\Lambda}=\Lambda(H)v_{\Lambda}
for all
H\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and

c)

\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})v_{\Lambda}=M
.

We call
v_{\Lambda}

highest (lowest) weight vector
of
M
. We call
M
even/odd according
to the parity of
v_{\Lambda}
, denoted
(-1)^{v_{\Lambda}}=(-1)^{M}
if necessary.

The following discussion will be phrased in term of highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. All statements are
valid
mutatis mutandis
for lowest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. We first record some elementary algebraic
properties that follow directly from the definition.

Lemma 1.3.2
.

Let
M
be a highest weight supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
and parity
(-1)^{v_{\Lambda}}
.
Let
\alpha_{1}\ldots,\alpha_{k}
and

\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{l}
be an enumeration of the even and odd positive roots, respectively, where
k=\frac{m(m-1)+n(n-1)}{2}
and
l=mn
. Choose root vectors
X_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\alpha_{i}}
and
Y_{j}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\beta_{j}}
. Then

a)

M
is spanned by the collection of vectors
X_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots X_{k}^{r_{k}}Y_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots Y_{l}^{s_{l}}v_{\Lambda}
where
r_{1},...,r_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
s_{1},...,s_{l}\in\{0,1\}
, of weight

\Lambda-\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}\alpha_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{l}s_{j}\beta_{j}
and parity

(-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{l}s_{j}+v_{\Lambda}}
.

b)

M
is a
weight supermodule
, that is,
M=\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\bigoplus$}}}}{\bigoplus}{\bigoplus}{\bigoplus}}_{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}}M^{\lambda}
, where

M^{\lambda}:=\{m\in M:Hm=\lambda(H)m\text{ for all }H\in{\mathfrak{h}}\}
. The space
M^{\lambda}
is
the
weight space
of weight
\lambda
, and
\dim(M^{\lambda})
its
multiplicity
.

\dim(M^{\lambda})<\infty
for all
\lambda
, with
\dim(M^{\Lambda})=1
.

c)

Any nonzero quotient of
M
is again a highest weight supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
.

M
has a unique maximal submodule and unique simple quotient. In particular,
M
is indecomposable.

d)

M
is a quotient of the (even or odd)
Verma supermodule

V(\Lambda)
, which
is defined (up to parity) as the quotient of
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
by the left ideal generated by
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}

and
H-\Lambda(H)
for all
H\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, and as a vector space isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
by
Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt.

e)

All simple highest weight modules of fixed highest weight and parity are isomorphic.

The peculiar consequences of the odd roots are brought to the fore by constructing
the simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
not as a quotient of
V(\Lambda)
, but as
the (unique) simple top of a Kac supermodule. Kac supermodules can be described as generalized Verma
supermodules with respect to
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
as Levi subalgebra. Concretely, one starts by extending any
M_{0}\in{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}\textbf{-mod}
,
placed in either even or odd degree, trivially to a
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}
-supermodule. Then, the induced

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule

(50)

K(M_{0}):=\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}_{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})}M_{0},

is called
Kac supermodule
. The associated map
K(\cdot):{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}\textbf{-smod}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}\textbf{-smod}
defines
an exact functor, the
Kac induction functor
. In general, when
M_{0}
is simple as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-module,

K(M_{0})
is indecomposable as a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, although it might not be simple.

Theorem 1.3.3

(
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

For any simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-supermodule
M_{0}
the Kac supermodule

has a unique maximal proper sub supermodule.
The unique simple top of
K(M_{0})
is denoted by

. The map
M_{0}\mapsto L(M_{0})
gives rise to a bijection
between the set of isomorphism classes of simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-supermodules and the set of isomorphism classes of simple

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules.
6
6

6

Equivalently,

is a bijection from simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules to simple

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, up to parity reversal.

is a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule iff

is a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-module, with the same highest weight.

We write

for the simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-module of highest weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
,
which is unique up to isomorphism. We write

for the Kac supermodule
K(L_{0}(\Lambda))
,
and

for its simple quotient
L(L_{0}(\Lambda))
. The parity of

will

.
We emphasize that in general

is not equal to the Verma supermodule of highest weight

is in fact the Verma module over

. The size of the maximal

is encoded in the

of the highest weight,

present in the theory of Lie superalgebras, which has no analog in the theory of complex semisimple
Lie algebras.

In general, let
M
be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. Any element

in the center of the universal enveloping algebra acts on

\chi_{\Lambda}(z)

\chi_{\Lambda}(z):=(\Lambda+\rho)(\text{HC}(z)),

where

is the Harish-Chandra

, the restriction of the projection map

\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})=\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{h}})\oplus({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})+\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}}){\mathfrak{n}}^{+})\to\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{h}})
to
{\mathfrak{Z}}({\mathfrak{g}})
composed with the twist
\zeta:{\mathrm{S}}({\mathfrak{h}})\to{\mathrm{S}}({\mathfrak{h}})
defined by

\lambda(\zeta(f)):=(\lambda-\rho)(f)
for all
f\in{\mathrm{S}}({\mathfrak{h}})
,
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
.
The Harish-Chandra homomorphism is an injective ring
homomorphism. To describe its image, we define

{\mathrm{S}}({\mathfrak{h}})^{W}:=\{f\in{\mathrm{S}}({\mathfrak{h}})\ :\ w(\lambda)(f)=\lambda(f)\ \text{for all}\ w\in W,\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\}
and, for any

,

A_{\lambda}:=\{\alpha\in\Phi_{1}^{+}:(\lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0\}\,.

Then, the image of

is
[

,

]

(53)

\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{HC})=\bigl\{f\in{\mathrm{S}}({\mathfrak{h}})^{W}:(\lambda+t\alpha)(f)=\lambda(f)\ \text{for all $t\in{\mathbb{C}}$, $\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}$, and $\alpha\in A_{\lambda-\rho}$}\bigr\}.

As a consequence, we obtain
\chi_{\Lambda}=\chi_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
whenever

\Lambda^{\prime}=w\biggl(\Lambda+\rho+\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\alpha_{i}\biggr)-\rho,

where

Definition 1.3.4
.

A weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
is called

,
i.e.

. The

of

linearly independent mutually orthogonal positive odd (in particular, isotropic) roots

\alpha\in\Phi_{1}^{+}

such that

. In brief,
\operatorname{at}(\Lambda)
is the dimension of a maximal
isotropic subspace of
{\operatorname{Span}}_{\mathbb{C}}(A_{\Lambda})\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. We call a highest weight

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
with highest weight
\Lambda

typical
if
\operatorname{at}(\Lambda)=0
, and
otherwise
atypical
.

We point out that the structure of highest weight supermodules over Lie superalgebras depends more
severely on the ordering of the roots than for ordinary complex Lie algebras. This will have been
illustrated by the example (see Fig.

can be compensated by an appropriate action on the highest weight to yield an isomorphic

is not. As a
consequence, the degree of atypicality is independent of the choice of positive root system.

The

\operatorname{def}({\mathfrak{g}})=\min(m,n)
. The degree of atypicality satisfies
0\leq\operatorname{at}(\Lambda)\leq\operatorname{def}({\mathfrak{g}})

for any weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. A weight
\Lambda
with
\operatorname{at}(\Lambda)=\operatorname{def}({\mathfrak{g}})
is
called
maximally atypical
.

The physics literature knows various alternative quantifications of the reducibility of
K(\Lambda)
,
referred to as “multiplet shortening”, “BPS-ness” or “protectedness”. We will propose translations
of these concepts back into the mathematical language in due course.

1.4.
Unitarizability

A
super pre-Hilbert space
is a complex super vector space
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1}
equipped with a positive definite even Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, that
is, a complex valued sesquilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, which is
conjugate-linear in the
first
and linear in the second variable, conjugate symmetric, such that

{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
are mutually orthogonal subspaces, and
\langle v,v\rangle>0
for all
v\neq 0
.
The
amendment
of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, defined as

(55)

\psi:{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathbb{C}},\qquad\psi(v,w)=i^{v}\langle v,w\rangle:=\begin{cases}i\langle v,w\rangle\ &\text{if}\ v,w\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1},\\
\langle v,w\rangle\ &\text{if}\ v,w\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}.\end{cases}

is an even
super Hermitian form
on
{\mathcal{H}}
, meaning that
\psi(v,w)=(-1)^{vw}\overline{\psi(w,v)}
for
all
v,w\in V
, which is
super positive definite
, meaning that
\psi
is positive definite on
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}

and
-i\psi
is positive definite on
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
. Obviously,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\psi
are
equivalent data, preference for which is dependent on taste and situation. Completion of
{\mathcal{H}}
to a

{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded Hilbert space is more natural with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Given a
(possibly only densely defined) endomorphism
T\in\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{H}})
, the adjoint of
T
is defined with respect
to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
by the formula

\langle v,Tw\rangle=\langle T^{\dagger}v,w\rangle

while the super-adjoint of
T
is defined with respect to
\psi
by

\psi(v,Tw)=(-1)^{vT}\psi(\sigma(T)v,w)
. Both are involutive and, as before, related by the amendment

(56)

\sigma(T)=i^{T}T^{\dagger}=\begin{cases}T^{\dagger}\ &\text{if $T$ is even},\\
iT^{\dagger}\ &\text{if $T$ is odd}.\end{cases}

The adjoint
\cdot^{\dagger}
satisfies
(ST)^{\dagger}=T^{\dagger}S^{\dagger}
, independent of the
parity of
S,T\in\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{H}})
. The super-adjoint
\sigma(\cdot)
satisfies
\sigma(ST)=(-1)^{ST}\sigma(T)\sigma(S)
.
Namely,
\cdot^{\dagger}
defines a conjugate-linear anti-involution on
\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{H}})
, while
\sigma(\cdot)

defines a conjugate-linear
graded
anti-involution. This makes
\sigma
more natural for the definition of
unitarizability of supermodules over Lie superalgebras with respect to conjugate-linear anti-involutions.
See also sections
1.2
and
0.2
.

Definition 1.4.1
.

Let
\omega
be a conjugate-linear anti-involution of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and let
(\rho,M)
be a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then
M
is
called
\omega
-
unitarizable
if it is equipped with or admits a positive definite even Hermitian form

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
such that
\rho\circ\omega=\sigma\circ\rho
.

For clarity, the definition is formulated in terms of the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded representation
\rho:{\mathfrak{g}}\to\operatorname{End}(M)
.
In what follows, however, we will continue to work with
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules suppressing the explicit Lie superalgebra
homomorphism
\rho
(so as to avoid confusion with the Weyl vector).
When
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is specified or clear from context, we will prefer the symbol

{\mathcal{H}}
over
M
. We leave completion to a Hilbert space implicit as needed. Writing the defining condition as

\langle v,Xw\rangle=i^{X}\langle\omega(X)v,w\rangle
for all
v,w\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we will refer to

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
also as a contravariant Hermitian form. We will also think of the data as defining a
“unitary representation” of the real Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\omega}\to{\mathfrak{gl}}({\mathcal{H}})^{\sigma}
, although we will not be
concerned with integrability to Lie supergroups. In the context of
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-supermodules, we extend
\omega

to a graded anti-involution of
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
in the obvious way. When
\omega
is to be implied from context, we
just say “unitarizable”.

Definition 1.4.2
.

Two unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
are called
equivalent
if
there exists an isomorphism
f:{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}\textbf{-smod}
that is
compatible with the Hermitian forms.

Note that in the definition of equivalence of unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, intertwining operators
are assumed to preserve the grading. This means that in general a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is not
necessarily equivalent to the one obtained by parity change.
7
7

7

We leave it as an exercise to
verify that if
\psi
is an invariant super Hermitian form on
{\mathcal{H}}
, then
(-i)^{\Pi\cdot}\psi(\Pi\cdot,\Pi\cdot)
is an invariant super Hermitian form on
\Pi{\mathcal{H}}
, while the associated
contravariant Hermitian forms are the same.

As mentioned in the introduction, constructions of superconformal field theories in theoretical physics are
expected to provide an interesting class of unitary representations of various superconformal algebras, restricted
by a number of further conditions. In this context, an equivalence class of unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules
is also referred to as a
superconformal multiplet
.

Implications of unitarity

Let

1.4.2.
Kac–Shapovalov form

The basic point, which is familiar in physics since the early days of quantum mechanics
[
12
]
, is
that the Kac supermodule
K(\Lambda)
has a unique (up to a real scalar) contravariant Hermitian form,
the
Kac–Shapovalov
form. This form induces a non-degenerate form on
L(\Lambda)
. The form on

L(\Lambda)
is in general not positive definite, but if it is, then
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable. Namely,
the classification of unitarizable simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules reduces to the determination
of all
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}
with the property that the Kac–Shapovalov form on
L(\Lambda)
is positive
definite.

Concretely, the Kac–Shapovalov form can be defined with the help of the (Harish-Chandra) projection

{\mathrm{pr}}:{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{h}})\cong{\mathrm{S}}({\mathfrak{h}})
on the first summand of the PBW decomposition

{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})={\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{h}})\oplus({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})+{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}){\mathfrak{n}}^{+})
. We also use that
\omega
preserves
this decomposition and provides a graded anti-isomorphism between
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
to
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})
. Then,
the infinitesimal character
\chi_{\Lambda}
that appeared in eq. (
51
)
induces an even sesquilinear form on
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
via

(57)

(X,Y)_{\Lambda}:=\chi_{\Lambda}({\mathrm{pr}}(\omega(X)Y))\,.

This is graded conjugate symmetric, in the sense that
(X,Y)_{\Lambda}=(-1)^{XY}\overline{(Y,X)}_{\Lambda}
,
as a consequence of
\chi_{\Lambda}(Z)=\overline{\chi_{\Lambda}(\omega(Z))}
. (
\Lambda
is real.)
On the (even) Verma supermodule
V(\Lambda)\cong{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
, we define the super
Hermitian form
\psi
via

(58)

\psi(Xv_{\Lambda},Yv_{\Lambda})=(X,Y)_{\Lambda}\,,\qquad X,Y\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})

The
Kac–Shapovalov form
is the associated Hermitian form
\langle v,w\rangle=(-i)^{v}\psi(v,w)
. This is contravariant by construction, and descends to the Kac supermodule
K(\Lambda)

(of either parity) by standard considerations. The following proposition records some of its
basic properties.

Proposition 1.4.5
.

Let
K(\Lambda)
be the Kac supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
, and let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be
the Kac–Shapovalov form on
K(\Lambda)
. Then

a)

Up to multiplication by a real scalar,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the unique
contravariant form on
K(\Lambda)
. It is normalized to
\langle v_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle=1
.

b)

Weight spaces
K(\Lambda)^{\lambda},K(\Lambda)^{\mu}
of
K(\Lambda)
with

\lambda\neq\mu
are orthogonal to each other,
\langle K(\Lambda)^{\lambda},K(\Lambda)^{\mu}\rangle=0
.

c)

The radical of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
coincides with the maximal proper sub supermodule of

K(\Lambda)
. In particular,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
induces a non-degenerate form on
L(\Lambda)
.

Thus, a Kac supermodule is simple if and only if the radical of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is trivial. The
size of the radical is related to the degree of atypicality of
K(\Lambda)
by construction of the
Kac–Shapovalov form. This is essentially a version of Kac’s criterion (
cf.

[
2
, Theorem 4.12]
), according to which a highest weight Kac supermodule
K(\Lambda)

is simple if and only if
\Lambda
is typical. Echoing the remarks after Lemma
1.3.2
,
the essence of Kac induction
is that it elucidates the structure of the radical in terms of the constituents in the decomposition of

K(\Lambda)
as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-module. This decomposition will be summarized in subsection
2.1
,
following
[
40
]
. In preparation, we describe the subset of the space
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m,n}

of real weights that correspond to unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules.

1.4.3.
Parameterization of weight space

Indeed, it follows from the definition of Kac induction and Proposition
1.4.3
that a necessary
condition for
L(\Lambda)
to be unitarizable as a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is that
L_{0}(\Lambda)
be unitarizable as a

{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-module. This imposes a classical sequence of standard conditions on the highest weight, which we recall
is parameterized in terms of the standard coordinates on
{\mathfrak{d}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
as

(59)

\Lambda=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{m}|\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{n}),

modulo shifts by
(1,\ldots,1|{-}1,\ldots,-1)
(see page
29
).
First, we consider the restriction to the maximal compact subalgebra
{\mathfrak{k}}
in eq. (
42
). If

L_{0}(\Lambda)
is unitarizable as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-module, then as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{\mathbb{C}}
-module it is semisimple with
finite multiplicities. In particular,
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable simple
(hence finite-dimensional)
{\mathfrak{k}}^{\mathbb{C}}
-module, which appears with multiplicity one. Namely,
\Lambda
must be
integral and dominant with respect to the positive system induced from
{\mathfrak{g}}
. On the simple
{\mathfrak{k}}^{\mathbb{C}}
-roots
this means

(60)

\begin{split}(\Lambda,\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1})=\lambda^{i}-\lambda^{i+1}&\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\quad\text{for $i=1,2,\ldots,p-1$}\\
(\Lambda,\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{j+1})=\lambda^{j}-\lambda^{j+1}&\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\quad\text{for $j=p+1,\ldots,m-1$}\\
-(\Lambda,\delta_{k}-\delta_{k+1})=\mu^{k}-\mu^{k+1}&\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\quad\text{for $k=1,\ldots,n-1$}\end{split}

Second, for the extension to a
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-module to be unitarizable, we require that the non-compact
roots belonging to
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)
be positive. This imposes the constraints

(61)

-(\Lambda,\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})=\lambda^{j}-\lambda^{i}\geq 0\quad\text{for $i=1,\ldots,p-1$ and $j=p+1,\ldots,m$}

Taken together, we have the inequalities

(62)

\lambda^{p+1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{m}\geq\lambda^{1}\cdots\geq\lambda^{p}\,,\qquad\mu^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu^{n}

and the differences in the three chains have to be integral. Note that at this stage there is no independent
constraint on the weights of the two abelian factors in
{\mathfrak{k}}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and no relation between the
\lambda
’s
and
\mu
’s. Following
[
26
]
, we parameterize the solution to these constraints by writing

(63)

\Lambda=(0,a_{2},\ldots,a_{m-1},0|b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1},0)+\frac{\uplambda}{2}(1,\ldots,1,-1,\ldots,-1|0,\ldots,0)\\
+\frac{\upalpha}{2}(1,\ldots,1|1,\ldots,1),

with integers
a_{i}
,
a_{j}
satisfying
a_{p+1}\geq\cdots a_{m-1}\geq 0\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{p}
, integers

b_{k}
satisfying
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n-1}
and real numbers
\upalpha
and
\uplambda
, the second of which
is non-positive. (To achieve this parameterization, we exploit the shift-invariance to enforce

\lambda^{1}+\lambda^{m}=2\mu^{n}=:\upalpha
, and define
\uplambda:=\lambda^{1}-\lambda^{m}
. The
constraints on the
a

b
’s then follow from above.)

Finally, in lieu of integrality for the non-compact roots, the requirement that the Kac-Shapovalov form be
positive semi-definite on the entire Verma supermodule is expressed in terms of polynomials
(“Kac-Shapovalov determinants”) that measure the norm of a certain finite set of vectors in
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})v_{\Lambda}
.
One finds
[
11

26
]
that for fixed

\uplambda\in\bigl(-\infty,-m+\max(i_{0},j_{0})+1\bigr)\\
\cup\bigl\{-m+\max(i_{0},j_{0})+1,-m+\max(i_{0},j_{0})+2,\ldots,-m+i_{0}+j_{0}\bigr\},

where

is the largest index for which

(65)

\displaystyle\begin{split}Y_{1}(\uplambda)&:=(\lambda^{1}-\lambda^{p},\ldots,\lambda^{1}-\lambda^{2},0),\\

Indeed, if

2.
Decomposition and Fragmentation

In general, unitarizability of the
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-module
L_{0}(\Lambda)
is not sufficient for
L(\Lambda)
to be
unitarizable as a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. In fact, even if all constituents of
L(\Lambda)_{\mathrm{ev}}
are unitarizable

{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules,
L(\Lambda)
might still not be unitarizable as a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. This begs for a concise
description of the
set of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-unitarity
in weight space. What is more, even if
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable,
the Kac supermodule
K(\Lambda)
is not necessarily composed solely from unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-constituents.
On the other hand, any unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
decomposes completely
in unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules,
and it is of interest to describe which ones occur. As another consequence, when
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable, but
atypical, the composition factors of the Jordan–Hölder series of
K(\Lambda)
as a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule are
all highest weight supermodules, but not necessarily unitarizable. This plays a central rôle for the
phenomenon of
fragmentation
, and hence the continuity of our indices, and is therefore important to
describe as precisely as possible.

2.1.
Dirac operator and decomposition under
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}

The analysis of the various issues mentioned above is expedited with the help of the algebraic Dirac operator
for
\mathfrak{g}
. This operator was introduced in
[
25
,
24
]
. Its use for the problem
at hand is explained in
[
40
]
, while its generalization is discussed in
[
34
]
.
We will here only highlight the main ideas.

For additional clarity, let us denote the supersymmetric invariant non-degenerate bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on

{\mathfrak{g}}
defined in (
34
) by
B(\cdot,\cdot)
, and fix two complementary Lagrangian subspaces of
{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}

with bases
\partial_{i}
and
x_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq mn
, such that
B(\partial_{i},x_{j})=-B(x_{j},\partial_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}
. Letting
T({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
be the
tensor algebra

over the vector space
{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}
(placed in even degree), the
Weyl algebra
is the quotient

\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})=T({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})/I
, where
I
is the two-sided ideal generated by elements

v\otimes w-w\otimes v-2B(v,w)
for all
v,w\in{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}
. The Weyl algebra can be identified with
the algebra of differential operators with polynomial coefficients in the variables
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{mn}
,
provided we identify
\partial_{i}
with the partial derivative
\partial/\partial x_{i}
for all

i=1,\dotsc,mn
.

With the help of the commutators
[x_{i},x_{j}]=0
,
[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]=0
, and

[\partial_{i},x_{j}]=\delta_{ij}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq mn
, one verifies that the
Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
is a subalgebra of
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})}
, with Lie algebra morphism
\alpha:{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}\to{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})}

given explicitly by
[
49
, Equation 2]
:

(67)

\alpha(X)=\sum_{k,j=1}^{mn}(B(X,[\partial_{k},\partial_{j}])x_{k}x_{j}+B(X,[x_{k},x_{j}])\partial_{k}\partial_{j})\\
-\sum_{k,j=1}^{mn}2B(X,[x_{k},\partial_{j}])x_{j}\partial_{k}-\sum_{l=1}^{mn}B(X,[\partial_{l},x_{l}]).

We let
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
denote the quadratic Casimir operator of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})}
the image of the quadratic Casimir of
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
under the diagonal embedding induced by

(68)

{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}\to\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}}),\qquad X\mapsto X\otimes 1+1\otimes\alpha(X).

The
Dirac operator

\operatorname{D}
for
{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined to be the odd element

(69)

\mathrm{D}=2\sum_{i=1}^{mn}(\partial_{i}\otimes x_{i}-x_{i}\otimes\partial_{i})\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}}).

It is independent of the choice of the basis of
{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}
and is
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
-invariant under the
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-action on

\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
induced by the adjoint action on both factors

[
25
, Lemma 10.2.1]
,
i.e.
,
[{\mathfrak{g}_{0}},\operatorname{D}]=0
. Analogously to reductive Lie algebras,
the Dirac operator has a nice square.

Proposition 2.1.1

(
[
25
, Proposition 10.2.2]
)
.

The Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}\in\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
satisfies

\operatorname{D}^{2}=-\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}}-C,

where
C
is a constant that equals
1/8
of the trace of
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}
.

For any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
, the Dirac operator acts component wise on
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
, where

M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{mn}]
is the oscillator module over
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})}
. If
M=\bigl({\mathcal{H}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)
is an
\omega
-unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, we equip the
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
with the positive definite Hermitian form

(70)

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}\otimes\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})}\,,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})}
is the Bargmann–Fock Hermitian form on
M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
.
Up to multiplication by a real scalar,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})}
is the unique Hermitian form that is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-anti-contravariant in the first factor, and satisfies

x_{i}^{\dagger}=\partial_{i}
in the second. For a suitable choice of positive system, this is compatible with
the extension of the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega
to
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, which implies
that the Dirac operator is
self-adjoint
on
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
. As a consequence,

Proposition 2.1.2

(
[
40
]
)
.

(Parthasarathy’s Dirac inequality)

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then
\operatorname{D}^{2}\geq 0
on
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
,

i.e.
,
\langle v,\operatorname{D}^{2}v\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})}\geq 0
for all
v\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
.

In conjunction with Proposition
2.1.1
, this estimate is powerful enough to give a necessary
and sufficient criterion for a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
L(\Lambda)
to be unitarizable,
explicitly in terms of its decomposition under
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
.

We recall that
L(\Lambda)
was defined on p.
1.3.3
as the unique simple top of the Kac
supermodule
K(\Lambda)
, and that in general neither of these will split in a direct sum of simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules.
However, analogously to Theorem 10.4.5. in
[
31
]
(see also Theorem 2.5 and Corollary 2.7 in

[
26
]
; we will use similar argumentation in the discussion of fragmentation on p.
2.2.2
below),
K(\Lambda)_{\mathrm{ev}}
has a Jordan–Hölder-type filtration as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-module
that induces a filtration of
L(\Lambda)_{\mathrm{ev}}
. The simple quotients are highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules

L_{0}(\Lambda-\Sigma_{S})
with highest weight of the form
\Lambda-\Sigma_{S}
, where
\Sigma_{S}:=\sum_{\alpha\in S}\alpha

is a sum of mutually distinct odd positive roots, for some
S\subseteq\Phi_{1}^{+}
. By Prop.
2.1.1
,

\operatorname{D}^{2}
is semi-simple with respect to this filtration, and we say that the Dirac inequality holds

strictly
if
\operatorname{D}^{2}>0
on some
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-constituent
L_{0}(\Lambda-\Sigma_{S})
.

Theorem 2.1.3

(
[
40
]
)
.

Let
L(\Lambda)
be a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. Then

L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable if and only if all
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-constituents
L_{0}(\Lambda-\Sigma_{S})
are unitarizable

{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules, and the Dirac inequality holds strictly on each
L_{0}(\Lambda-\Sigma_{S})\otimes 1
with

\Sigma_{S}\neq 0
. In that case, the decomposition as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-module is complete and reads explicitly

L(\Lambda)_{\mathrm{ev}}\cong L_{0}(\Lambda)\oplus\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\bigoplus$}}}}{\bigoplus}{\bigoplus}{\bigoplus}}_{S}L_{0}(\Lambda-\Sigma_{S}),

where the sum extends at least over all non-empty
S
that do not contain any odd root
\alpha
with

(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0
. In the notation of eq. (
52
), these are all non-empty
S\subseteq\Phi_{1}^{+}\setminus A_{\Lambda}
.

The intuitive reason that the Dirac inequality is stronger than mere unitarity of the
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-constituents is
that
\operatorname{D}
itself “depends on the odd root vectors”. In physics terminology, the definiteness of the one
operator
\operatorname{D}^{2}
already guarantees that all supercharges are represented by (anti) self-adjoint operators.
It is worth remarking that subsets of
\Phi_{1}^{+}\setminus A_{\Lambda}
in general are only a lower bound for
the extent of the sum.

Corollary 2.1.4
.

Let
L(\Lambda)
be a unitarizable simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then
L(\Lambda)
decomposes in a
finite sum of unitarizable simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules. The number of
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-constituents lies
between
2^{\#\Phi_{1}^{+}-\#A_{\Lambda}}
and
2^{\dim({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1})}
, with maximum attained precisely if

\Lambda
is typical.

Following physics, we call an irreducible unitary representation with the maximal number of

{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-constituents a
long supermultiplet
, all others
short
. If the number of
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-constituents
is smaller than
2^{\dim({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1})-1}
, the supermultiplet might also be called
ultra-short
. Corollary

2.1.4
in conjunction with Proposition
1.4.5
thus say that a unitarizable simple
highest weight supermodule is long precisely if it is isomorphic
to a Kac supermodule with typical highest weight, and short precisely if it is isomorphic to the quotient of
a Kac supermodule with atypical highest weight by the radical of the Kac–Shapovalov
form. In this context, non-zero elements of the Kac supermodule belonging to the radical of the Kac–Shapovalov
form are also referred to as
null vectors
.

2.2.
Region of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-unitarity and unitarity bound

A complete classification of unitarizable simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules was given by Jakobsen in

[
26
]
, and Günaydin and Volin in
[
20
]
. Unitarizable simple highest weight

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules that can be integrated to the Lie supergroup
{\mathrm{SU}}(p,q|n)
were classified by Furutsu
and Nishiyama in
[
14
]
. In this section, we sketch the result, assuming
p,q>0
for
genericity, and write down the complete classification for
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{su}}(1,1|1)
.

Following the passage from unitarity for the maximal compact subalgebra
{\mathfrak{k}}^{\mathbb{C}}
to
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-unitarity (see eqs. (
60
) to (
64
)), the first step is to require positivity of the odd roots. With respect
to the standard ordering from eq. (
31
) and the involution
\omega_{-}
from eq. (
45
),
this translates into the constraints

(71)

\begin{split}-(\Lambda,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k})=-\lambda^{i}-\mu^{k}&\geq 0\quad\text{for $i=1,\ldots,p$ and $k=1,\ldots,n$}\\
(\Lambda,\epsilon_{j}-\delta_{k})=\lambda^{j}+\mu^{k}&\geq 0\quad\text{for $j=p+1,\ldots,m$ and $k=1,\ldots,n$}\end{split}

and yields an interlocking of the two chains from (
62
),
i.e.
,

(72)

\lambda^{p+1}\geq\dotsc\geq\lambda^{m}\geq-\mu^{n}\geq\dotsc\geq-\mu^{1}\geq\lambda^{1}\geq\dotsc\geq\lambda^{p}.

On the Jakobsen parameters from eq. (
63
), this translates to the constraints

(73)

\frac{\uplambda}{2}\leq\upalpha\leq-b_{1}-\frac{\uplambda}{2}

which for fixed
a_{i}
,
b_{k}
corresponds geometrically to a non-degenerate closed half cone in the

(\uplambda,\upalpha)
-plane
with vertex at
(\uplambda,\upalpha)=(-b_{1},-\frac{b_{1}}{2})
. In the second step, the semi-definiteness of the
Kac-Shapovalov determinants leads to further inequalities that in general are stronger except potentially where some of
the previous inequalities (
73
) are already saturated. As a result, the
set of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-unitarity for
fixed spin and R-symmetry quantum numbers
, which we denote by
\Gamma^{(a,b)}
, consists of a congruent subcone of
(
73
), together with some additional half lines and possibly isolated points. The set of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-unitarity

\Gamma\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is the union of all
\Gamma^{(a,b)}
.
Note that in general, there are two different isomorphism classes of unitarizable simple supermodules
for each
\Lambda\in\Gamma
, related by parity reversal. Under appropriate circumstances, physical considerations
may fix a section of this
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
bundle over
\Gamma
.
The (relative, topological) interior of
\Gamma^{(a,b)}
, where
\uplambda

and
\upalpha
can be varied independently, is a (relatively) open cone. We will denote it by
{\mathcal{C}}^{(a,b)}
and,
borrowing physics terminology, call it the
region of unitarity for fixed spin and R-symmetry quantum numbers
.
It consists
entirely of typical weights in the sense of Def.
1.3.4
, as can be shown with the help of the Dirac
inequality Prop.
2.1.2
.

Lemma 2.2.1

(
[
40
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight

\{\Lambda\in\Gamma:(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{p}-\delta_{1})=0\ \text{or}\ (\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{p+1}-\delta_{n})=0\}.

2.3.
Fragmentation and recombination

If
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable, but
\Lambda
is atypical (
i.e.
,
\Lambda\in\Gamma\setminus{\mathcal{C}})
, the
Kac supermodule
K(\Lambda)
is not simple, but also does not split into a direct sum of simple supermodules.
Instead, as
K(\Lambda)
is of highest weight type, it has a Jordan–Hölder series

K_{0}=K(\Lambda)\supsetneq K_{1}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq K_{M}=0
such that each
K_{i-1}/K_{i}
is isomorphic to
a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
L(\Lambda_{i})
, with highest weight
\Lambda_{i}
of the form

\Lambda-\Sigma_{S}
where
\Sigma_{S}:=\sum_{\alpha\in S}\alpha
is a sum of mutually distinct
odd positive roots, for some
S\subseteq\Phi_{1}^{+}
(
cf.
discussion around Theorem
2.1.3
).
The
\Lambda_{i}
will all be atypical, but it is difficult to describe in general how many and which
ones occur. Also the composition factors
L(\Lambda_{i})=K_{i-1}/K_{i}
for
i=2,\ldots,M
need not be
unitarizable. It is an instructive exercise to show that this does not happen within the unitarity
bound.
8
8

8

In the example,
K(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
has unitarizable top quotient, but, if
defined with respect to the standard positive system,
K_{1}=L(\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})
, which is not
unitarizable. On the other hand,
K(\alpha_{Q})
has factors
K_{1}/K_{2}=L(0,0)
,
K_{2}=L(\alpha_{F})
, which
are both unitarizable.

.

For

, the simple highest weight supermodules in the Jordan–Hölder series of

and

L(\Lambda)
and

we obtain,

Let

3.
Duflo-Serganova Functor

The
Duflo–Serganova (DS) functor
is a symmetric monoidal tensor functor that serves as a powerful tool
for studying the representation category of Lie superalgebras

is associated with any odd square-zero element and relates the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2

representation theory of Lie superalgebras of different dimensions in a cohomological manner. In mathematical
physics, the DS functor appears as a representation theoretic analogue of the so-called twisting procedure
that reduces a (Lagrangian) supersymmetric field theory to a much simpler
topological
or

holomorphic
version
[
47
,
48
,
3
,
39
]
. In fact, the relation
between the two concepts should be much more than an analogy, with the superconformal index as a very
concrete link. We here give a brief introduction to the DS functor, and describe its extension to the unitarizable
subcategory. Our exposition follows mostly

[
8
,
18
,
41
]

3.1.
The algebraic theory

The DS functor can be attached quite generally to any odd element

of a complex Lie superalgebra

Definition 3.1.1

to parameterize the possible (topological and holomorphic) twists of such theories, which as mentioned above

3.3.
Bogomol’nyi–Prasad–Sommerfield or ‘BPS’

of the root space

. If

the standard Verma module, exactly one of these is satisfied for non-zero

, and any additional

Definition 3.3.1
.

Let

be a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. For any

, we denote by

the

of

\dim(\operatorname{ann}(v))>\frac{\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}}{2}=\#\Phi_{1}^{+}
.

Lemma 3.3.2

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable simple

-supermodule.

a)

with non-zero root vector

.

b)

If

c)

We conclude with the following comparison between various math and physics notions that we have touched
upon so far.

Proposition 3.3.3
.

Let
M
be a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

a)

{\mathcal{H}}
is a short supermodultiplet.

b)

The highest weight of
{\mathcal{H}}
is atypical.

c)

{\mathcal{H}}
contains a BPS state.

d)

{\mathcal{H}}
is protected as a direct summand in families of unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules.

In particular, long unitarizable supermodules do not contain BPS states.

One might expect that if
{\mathcal{H}}
contains BPS states, then in particular the highest weight state should be
one. In other words, that
{\mathcal{H}}
is a short supermultiplet if and only if the highest weight state is BPS.
A quick look at Fig.
1
, which illustrates the weight system of the oscillator supermodule
for
{\mathfrak{su}}(1,1|1)
, shows however that this is not necessarily true and in any case depends on the
choice of positive system.

In physics, BPS states are further classified by comparing the size of the annihilator with the total
number of supercharges. In other words, if
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
is a BPS state one defines the
(degree of) BPS-ness
by

(98)

\deg_{\rm BPS}(v)=\frac{\dim(\operatorname{ann}(v))-\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}}{\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}}.

The intuition is that the more supercharges annihilate a state, the more it is protected against
deformations. Thus, if
{\mathcal{H}}
is a unitarizable simple supermodule, one expects a close relationship
between the maximal
BPS-ness
of any state in
{\mathcal{H}}
and the degree of atypicality of the
highest weight (defined as the maximal number of
mutually orthogonal
roots with

(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0
). For finite-dimensional supermodules, it is indeed known that the two
notions are equivalent concepts. We conjecture that this also extends to the infinite-dimensional
case.

4.
Formal Superdimension

The aim of this section is to explain the definition of the
formal superdimension
, which is introduced
more formally in ref.
[
41
]
. The idea is to assign an invariant to
unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule that generalizes, on the one hand, the ordinary superdimension
for finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, and, on the other, what is known as
formal
dimension
or
Harish-Chandra degree
of unitarizable modules over the bosonic subalgebra

{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
. In fact, the formal superdimension of a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is simply defined as the alternating
sum of the formal dimensions of its
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-constituents. Such an invariant is expected to be “protected
in continuous families” in a similar sense as atypicality (or BPS-ness) discussed in section
2.3
.
We will realize this expectation in section
5
.

Our approach might seem limited by the fact that the formal dimension itself is
defined only for the class of unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules that integrate to

relative discrete
series representations
of a real Lie group
G
with Lie algebra
{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{{\mathbb{R}}}}
. Here,
{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{{\mathbb{R}}}}
, is the
reductive real form
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1)
of
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
(see section
1.2
),
and
G
is either the matrix Lie group
{G_{0}^{{\mathbb{R}}}}
, or its simply connected covering group by
{\widetilde{G}_{0}^{{\mathbb{R}}}}
. We
recall these notions and explain the calculation of the formal dimension in section

4.1
. The definition of the formal
superdimension follows in section
4.2
. It turns out that all simple
supermodules for which the formal superdimension is defined and non-zero are maximally atypical, and that
conversely any unitarizable maximally atypical supermodule consists only of
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules in the (limit of)
relative holomorphic discrete series. This removes the limitations of the
formal superdimension to some degree.

4.1.
Relative holomorphic discrete series and formal dimension

We proceed in steps. First, we realize unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules as
Harish-Chandra modules of unitary irreducible representations of
G
. Second, we establish the Harish-Chandra condition
that distinguishes those
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-modules that come from
G
-representations that are square-integrable modulo
the center. Third, we assign a formal dimension to these square-integrable highest weight modules.
Fourth, we describe the Harish-Chandra parameterization of the relevant highest weights. Fifth, we record the
relation between the formal dimension and the Harish-Chandra character of
L
-packets. Finally, we comment briefly
on some ‘limit cases‘. Our approach closely follows the theory developed in
[
32
]
.

(101)

14

4.1.2.
Relative holomorphic discrete series

Let

15

15

This can be viewed as space of sections of

(103)

\Gamma_{G}(E_{\pi_{\Lambda}}):=\{f\in{\mathcal{C}}(G,{\mathbb{C}})\ :\ f(zg)=\chi_{\pi_{\Lambda}}(z)f(g)\ \text{for all}\ z\in Z\},

(104)

\Gamma^{2}_{G}(E_{\pi_{\Lambda}}):=\{f\in\Gamma_{G}(E)\ :\ \int_{Z\backslash G}\left|{f(g)}\right|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\mu(Zg)<\infty\},

This allows us to compare
(\pi_{\Lambda},{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda})
and

A unitary irreducible highest weight representation

Here, as usual, the epithet “holomorphic” refers to the complex structure on the
quotient
G/K
that is compatible with the choice of positive system. As such,
relative holomorphic discrete series representations are direct generalizations of holomorphic
discrete series representations of semisimple Lie groups
[
22
]
to
their universal covering groups. Physically, relative holomorphic discrete series
are “plane wave normalizable in the direction of the center, otherwise normalizable.”

Theorem 4.1.3

(
[
32

)

If
(\pi_{\Lambda},{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda})
is a relative holomorphic discrete series representation of

is an isometric

embedding

Harish-Chandra classified in
[

]
all unitary highest weight representation of
G
that
belong to the relative holomorphic discrete series by evaluating the integral

(107)

\int_{Z\backslash G}|\langle v_{\Lambda},\pi_{\Lambda}(g)v_{\Lambda}\rangle|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\mu(Zg),

where
v_{\Lambda}
denotes a highest weight vector of the Harish-Chandra module

{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}^{\infty,K}
.

Theorem 4.1.4
.

[
22

A unitary highest weight representation of
G
with highest weight
\Lambda

discrete series if and only if, for all (positive non-compact roots)

(\lambda+\rho_{0},\beta)<0.

This condition is in the following referred to as the

.

4.1.3.
Formal dimension

The positive constant
d(\pi_{\Lambda})
in Theorem
4.1.3
is independent of the normalization
of the inner product on
{\mathcal{H}}
, as well as the choice of the vector
v
. It can be thought of as a measure of
the “relative size” or “degree” of the relative holomorphic discrete series representation. This is illustrated
by its appearance in the
Godement–Harish-Chandra orthogonality relations
of matrix
coefficients (Schur’s Lemma).

Proposition 4.1.5
.

Let
(\pi_{1},{\mathcal{H}}_{1})
and
(\pi_{2},{\mathcal{H}}_{2})
be two relative holomorphic discrete series representations
with the same central character. Then, for all
v_{1},w_{1}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
,
v_{2},w_{2}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
,

\int_{Z\backslash G}\overline{\langle v_{1},\pi_{1}(g)w_{1}\rangle}\langle v_{2},\pi_{2}(g)w_{2}\rangle\ \mathrm{d}\mu(Zg)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{d(\pi_{1})}\langle v_{2},v_{1}\rangle\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle\qquad&\text{if}\ \pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2},\\
0\qquad&\text{if}\ \pi_{1}\ncong\pi_{2}.\end{cases}

(Where in the first case a unitary isomorphism is used to identify
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
)

As it stands,
d(\pi_{\Lambda})
depends on the normalization of the Haar measure on
G
. If
G
were
compact, one would normalize it such that
G
has volume one. In general, the evaluation of the integral
(
107
) allows for the (geometric) determination of a preferred measure.

Theorem 4.1.6

(
[
32
, Theorem 3.17]
)

With respect to a suitable normalization of the measure

4.1.4.
Harish-Chandra parameterization

To describe the geometric parameterization of irreducible highest weight modules in the relative holomorphic
discrete series, let
T
be the analytic subgroup of
G
corresponding to the (compact) Cartan subalgebra

{\mathfrak{h}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Then
Z\subset T
, and
T/Z
is compact since
\operatorname{Ad}(G)
of a reductive
Lie group is closed. This establishes a relationship between relative holomorphic discrete series representations
and square-integrable representations of
G/T
, which allows us to parametrize the relative holomorphic discrete series
in terms of the character group
X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})
of the complexification
T^{{\mathbb{C}}}
of
T
, and Weyl chambers.
The coset
X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})+\rho_{0}
of
X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
is independent of the choice of positive system.
We call an element
\lambda
in
X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})\otimes{\mathbb{R}}

regular
if no dual root is orthogonal to

\lambda
; otherwise,
\lambda
is called
singular
. Let

(X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})\otimes{\mathbb{R}})^{{\it reg}}
denote the set of regular elements in
X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
, and set

(X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})+\rho_{0})^{{\it reg}}:=(X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})+\rho_{0})\cap(X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})\otimes{\mathbb{R}})^{{\it reg}}
.
A
Weyl chamber
of
G
is a connected component of
(X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})\otimes{\mathbb{R}})^{reg}
. The Weyl chambers
are in one-to-one correspondence with the systems of positive roots for
({\mathfrak{g}_{0}},{\mathfrak{h}})
. We define a Weyl chamber
C

to be
holomorphic
if for all
\lambda\in C
we have
(\lambda,\alpha)<0
for all
\alpha\in\Phi_{n}^{+}
. Then there are exactly
\#W_{c}
holomorphic Weyl chambers, forming a single orbit for
the action of
W_{c}
. A
holomorphic Harish-Chandra parameter
is then a pair
(\Lambda:=\lambda-\rho_{0},C)
, where
C
is a
holomorphic Weyl chamber and
\lambda\in(X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})+\rho_{0})^{{\it reg}}\cap C
. There exists a
bijection between the set of isomorphism classes of relative holomorphic discrete series representations and
holomorphic Harish-Chandra parameters up to the natural action of the Weyl group
W_{c}
.
They correspond to a relatively connected subset of the full set of
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
-unitarity
described in section
1.4
.

4.1.5.
Harish-Chandra characters and
L
-packets

If
(\pi,{\mathcal{H}})
were a finite-dimensional representation of
G
, its dimension could be computed by restricting its
character
\chi:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
,
g\mapsto\operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\pi(g)
to the identity,
\dim{\mathcal{H}}=\chi(e)
. For infinite-dimensional
representations,
\pi(g)
is generally not trace-class. However, at least for trivial central character,

i.e.,
(proper) holomorphic discrete series representations, the formal dimension can be related to a certain
combination of characters, understood in a distributional sense. For this purpose, we replace
Z\backslash G

with
G^{\prime}:=G_{0}^{\prime}={\mathrm{SU}}(p,q)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(n)
, denote its Haar measure by
\mathrm{d}g
, and let
T^{\prime}
be the
maximal compact Cartan subgroup associated with the real form
{\mathfrak{h}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
in
G^{\prime}
, with complexification

(T^{\prime})^{{\mathbb{C}}}
. Furthermore, we let
K^{\prime}:={\mathrm{SU}}(p)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(q)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(n)

be the maximal compact subgroup, diagonally embedded in
G^{\prime}
. We consider the Lie algebra of
G^{\prime}
, naturally, as
a subalgebra of
\operatorname{Lie}(G)
, with complexification in
{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
.

Fix a holomorphic discrete series representation
(\pi_{\Lambda},\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})
of
G^{\prime}
,
which is uniquely determined by its Harish-Chandra parameter
\Lambda
.
For a compactly supported smooth function
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(G^{\prime})
, the operator

(109)

\pi_{\Lambda}(f)v=\int_{G^{\prime}}f(g)\pi_{\Lambda}(g)v\,\mathrm{d}g,

is trace-class. The mapping
f\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(\pi_{\Lambda}(f))
defines a continuous linear functional on

\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(G^{\prime})
. The associated Schwartz distribution,

(110)

\Theta(f):=\operatorname{tr}(\pi_{\Lambda}(f)),

is referred to as the
Harish-Chandra character
of
\pi_{\Lambda}
. This uniquely determines

\pi_{\Lambda}
up to unitary equivalence
[
21
]
. Moreover, Harish-Chandra demonstrated in

[
21
]
the existence of a locally integrable function

\Theta_{\pi_{\Lambda}}:G^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

(111)

\Theta(f)=\int_{G^{\prime}}f(g)\Theta_{\pi_{\Lambda}}(g)\,\mathrm{d}g,

where
\Theta_{\pi_{\Lambda}}
is real analytic on the set of regular elements
G_{{\it reg}}^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
.
When referring to the calculation of a representation’s character, we typically mean evaluating

\Theta_{\pi_{\Lambda}}\big|_{G_{{\it reg}}^{\prime}}
.

As it stands,
\Theta_{\pi_{\Lambda}}
cannot be evaluated at the identity. It becomes continuous, however,
after summing over the so-called
L
-packet
of
\pi_{\Lambda}
, which consists of those discrete series
representations of
G^{\prime}
with the same infinitesimal character as
\pi_{\Lambda}
. These
are proper irreducible subrepresentations of
L^{2}(G^{\prime})
whose Harish-Chandra module is of highest weight
with respect to a different positive system, and correspond bijectively to Harish-Chandra parameters
in
(X^{\ast}(T^{{\mathbb{C}}})+\rho_{0})^{{\it reg}}/W_{c}
that are in the same orbit of the full Weyl group
W
of

G^{\prime}
as the holomorphic parameter
\Lambda
. They are uniquely determined by their Harish-Chandra
character, denoted
\Theta_{w\Lambda}
for

, and can also be assigned a formal dimension,
denoted

4.1.6.

A unitary highest weight representation

{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}

5.

-Supermodules

We have reviewed in previous sections that the representation theory of

16
16

16

We expect many of these features to be

\omega

of the self-commuting variety. Upon further deformation, these can disappear only in

-supermodule

Theorem 5.2.7

Proof.

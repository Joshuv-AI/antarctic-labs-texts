On the spectral properties of long-range perturbations of a class of block finite difference operators
Olivier Bourget, Angela Vargas-Mancipe

Abstract
We analyze spectral properties of a family of self-adjoint first-order finite difference operators acting on ℓ 2 ​ ( ℤ , ℂ 2 ) \ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z};\mathbb{C}^{2}) or ℓ 2 ​ ( ℤ + , ℂ 2 ) \ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+};\mathbb{C}^{2}) . Applying the conjugate operator method, we prove the existence of limiting absorption principles and the absence of singular continuous spectrum for these operators. Our results cover classes of admissible long-range perturbations that have not been previously addressed. As illustrative examples, one-dimensional discrete Dirac operators and the Su–Schrieffer–Heeger (SSH) model are considered.

Introduction

The spectral analysis of block finite difference operators has attracted significant interest due to its relevance in both theoretical and applied contexts. For example, a massless two-dimensional discrete Dirac operator on a hexagonal lattice can be used to model electron transport in graphene at low excitation energies
[
10
,
27
]
. A simpler model is obtained by considering a discrete Schrödinger operator on the same lattice, see e.g.,
[
31
]
. The spectral properties of discrete Dirac operators have been extensively studied, see
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
20
,
23
,
26
]
and references therein. In particular,
[
26
]
discusses convergence of discrete Dirac operators to their continuous counterparts in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as the mesh size tends to zero.

Block Toeplitz and Jacobi operators provide another framework for the analysis of models arising from physics, such as the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model, which is commonly used to describe polyacetylene chains
[
30
]
. Details on the spectral properties of these block operators can be found in
[
6
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
29
]
and references therein.

In scattering regimes, the above operators do not exhibit singular continuous spectrum, while their absolutely continuous and pure point components can be effectively controlled. The natural question that arises is whether these spectral properties are stable under compact self-adjoint perturbations. A standard approach to this problem is to control the point spectrum and obtain a Limiting Absorption Principle (LAP) on appropriate subsets of the spectrum of the operator under consideration. This can be accomplished by positive commutator methods, such as the Mourre theory
[
2
,
25
]
. This theory was applied to block Jacobi operators in
[
29
]
. Later,
[
13
]
implemented the same method for block Toeplitz operators, generalizing previous results for the scalar case
[
3
,
6
]
. For one-dimensional discrete Dirac operators, a LAP was obtained in
[
17
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
by different techniques.

In this paper, we apply the Mourre theory to examine the stability of spectral properties of certain first-order block finite difference operators under suitable compact self-adjoint perturbations. Our analysis focuses on the treatment of long-range perturbations, extending previous results for these operators. Although some of our results naturally extend to block finite difference operators on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{N})
or
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{C}^{N})
, we restrict our attention to the case
d=1
and
N=2
. The corresponding extensions will be addressed in a forthcoming work. As applications, we consider the SSH model and the one-dimensional Dirac operators for both the massive and the massless cases.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the basic model and present its fundamental spectral properties. In Section
2.2
, we introduce certain subspaces of matrix-valued sequences, which are needed to formulate our main results in Theorems
2.8
and
2.11
. In Section
2.4
, we discuss the connection between our results and previous works. In particular, Proposition
2.22
states that there exist long-range perturbations of the one-dimensional discrete Dirac operator that satisfy the hypotheses of our main theorems, but do not meet the conditions introduced in
[
17
]
. Section
3
reviews the main features of Mourre theory. In Section
4
we construct a sequence of conjugate operators for the unperturbed operator, in the sense of the Mourre theory, based on ideas presented in
[
19
]
. Furthermore, in Section
4.3
we define an alternative conjugate operator in the absence of spectral gaps, which allows us to handle conical intersection of the spectral band functions. In Section
5
, we study the classes of admissible perturbations with respect to these conjugate operators. The proofs of Theorems
2.8
and
2.11
are developed in Section
6
.

2
General framework and main results

The aim of this section is to introduce the main results of this paper, namely Theorems
2.8
and
2.11
, and to discuss the relation with previous results in the literature (see Section
2.4
). We start by describing the unperturbed operator.

2.1
The unperturbed model

In this section, we introduce the unperturbed operator, which plays a central role in the subsequent analysis. Let
\mathbb{G}\in\{\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
. We consider the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}:=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{C}^{2})
, equipped with the standard inner product

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle{u},{v}}}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{G}}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\langle u(n),v(n)\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}},\quad u,v\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}.

Furthermore, we define
\ell^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{C}^{2})
as the subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}
consisting of sequences with compact support.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. The first-order finite difference operator
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}=H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}(\alpha,a,b)
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}
, determined by
\alpha,a
and
b
is defined as

H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\bar{a}+\bar{b}S^{*}\\
a+bS&-\alpha\end{array}}}\right),

(2.1)

where
S\in\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G})}}\right)
is the right-shift operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G})
, that is,

(Su)(n):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if }\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{+}\mbox{ and }n=0,\\
u(n-1)&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}}}\right.

for
u\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G})
and
n\in\mathbb{G}
. Clearly,
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}
is a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}
.

Remark 2.1
.

(i)

The spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{-}}:=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{-};\mathbb{C}^{2})
are identified as subspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{-}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
. We let
P:\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
. Consequently,
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}
can be identified with
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.PH_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}P}}\right|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
.

(ii)

We could consider the first-order finite difference operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}
given by

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c&\bar{a}+\bar{b}S^{*}\\
a+bS&d\end{array}}}\right),

where
a,b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
c,d\in\mathbb{R}
.
This operator agrees with
\mu+H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}(\alpha,a,b)
with
\mu:=(c+d)/2
and
\alpha:=(c-d)/2
.

For suitable choices of the parameters
\alpha,a
and
b
, the operator
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}
is of interest in quantum mechanics, as shown by the examples below.

Example 2.2
.

Let
m\geq 0
. The free one-dimensional discrete Dirac operator with mass
m
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}
is defined by
D_{m}^{(\mathbb{G})}:=H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}(m,1,-1).

Example 2.3
.

If
\alpha=0
,
a,b>0
and
a\neq b
, then the operator
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}(0,a,b)
is unitarily equivalent to the Su-Schriefer-Heeger (SSH) model introduced in
[
30
]
. The latter is a periodic Jacobi operator with period two on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Specifically, using the notation from (
2.23
) below,
the SSH model corresponds to the operator
J_{1}^{(\mathbb{Z})}(a,b,0,0)
.

In the literature, the operators
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}
and
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}
are known respectively as the Laurent and Toeplitz operators associated with the symbol

h(\theta):=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\bar{a}+\bar{b}e^{-i\theta}\\
{a}+{b}e^{i\theta}&-\alpha\end{pmatrix},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{T}.

(2.2)

The connection between
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}
and the symbol
h
is performed by the Fourier transform, specifically the unitary operator
{\mathcal{F}}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow L^{2}({\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{C}^{2})
defined by

({\mathcal{F}}u)(\theta):=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}u(n)e^{in\theta},\quad u\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}},\ \theta\in{\mathbb{T}}.

(2.3)

Indeed,
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widehat{H}_{0}\mathcal{F}
, where
\widehat{H}_{0}
is the multiplication operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{C}^{2})
given by

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widehat{H}_{0}f}}\right)(\theta)=h(\theta)f(\theta),\quad f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{C}^{2}),\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}.

(2.4)

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in(-\pi,\pi]
be such that
a=|a|e^{i\varphi_{1}}
and
b=|b|e^{i\varphi_{2}}
. We set
\varphi:=\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1}
. For a fixed value of
\theta
, the eigenvalues of
h(\theta)
are given by
\pm\lambda(\theta)
, where
\lambda(\theta)>0
and

\lambda^{2}(\theta)=\alpha^{2}+|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}+2|a||b|\cos(\theta+\varphi),\quad\theta\in\mathbb{T}.

(2.5)

As Proposition
2.4
below suggests, the nature of the spectrum of
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}
is related to the properties of the function
\lambda
, which is non-constant because
a
and
b
are nonzero. One sees that
\pm\lambda(\mathbb{T})=\overline{I_{\pm}}
, where

I_{\pm}:=\pm\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(|a|-|b|)^{2}},\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(|a|+|b|)^{2}}}}\right).

(2.6)

Furthermore, the set
\kappa_{1}(\lambda):=\{\lambda(\theta):\theta\in\mathbb{T},\lambda^{\prime}(\theta)=0\mbox{ or }\lambda^{\prime}(\theta)\mbox{ does not exist}\}
is finite and

\kappa_{1}(\lambda)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\pm\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(|a|-|b|)^{2}},\pm\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(|a|+|b|)^{2}}}}\right\}.

(2.7)

Actually,
\lambda
is not differentiable at
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
, (respectively,
\lambda^{\prime}(\theta)=0
), if and only if
\lambda(\theta)=0
(resp.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
I_{\pm}
be the intervals defined in (
2.6
). Then

(i)

\sigma_{\ess}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right)=\overline{I_{-}}\cup\overline{I_{+}}.
There is a gap between the spectral bands
\overline{I_{-}}
and
\overline{I_{+}}
if and only if
\alpha\neq 0
or
|a|\neq|b|
.

(ii)

H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}
has purely absolutely continuous spectrum and
\sigma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}}}\right)=\ac\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}}}\right)=\sigma_{\ess}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}}}\right)
.

(iii)

H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}
has no singular continuous spectrum. Furthermore,
\mathcal{E}_{p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}}}\right)=\{-\alpha\}
if
|b|>|a|>0
, otherwise this set is empty.

Proof.

We first prove (i) for
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}
and (ii). Since
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}
is unitarily equivalent to the multiplication operator
\widehat{H}_{0}
defined in (
2.4
), and
\kappa_{1}(\lambda)
is finite, the spectrum of
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}
is purely absolutely continuous and

\sigma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}}}\right)=\ac\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}}}\right)=\sigma_{\ess}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}}}\right)=-\lambda(\mathbb{T})\cup\lambda(\mathbb{T})=\overline{I_{-}}\cup\overline{I_{+}}.

Now, by Remark
2.1
and Lemma
4.3
below,
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z}_{-})}\oplus H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}
differs from
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z})}
by a finite-rank operator. Thus, the proof of (i) for
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{+}
follows from the Weyl criterion.

Finally, we prove (iii). By a transfer matrix argument, we can show that
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}
admits eigenvalues if and only if
|b|>|a|>0
, and in this situation the only eigenvalue is
-\alpha
(see also
[
12
]
). The absence of singular continuous spectrum of
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}
follows from Theorems
2.8
and
2.11
below (see also
[
28
]
). This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

In the SSH model, there is always a gap between
I_{-}
and
I_{+}
. For the discrete one-dimensional Dirac operator, a spectral gap exists as long as
m>0
.

2.2
Some subspaces of
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G};M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))

In the sequel, for
d\in\{1,2\}
we let
\|\cdot\|
denote a matrix norm on the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, and we identify any sequence
W:\mathbb{G}\to M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
with the multiplication operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G};M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(W)

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G};M_{d}(\mathbb{C})):(W(n)u(n))_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G};M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))}}\right\}

\displaystyle(Wu)(n)

\displaystyle:=W(n)u(n),\quad u\in\mathcal{D}(W).

Furthermore, for
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
, we define the sequences
\tau^{p}W
and
\tau^{-p}W
by

(\tau^{p}W)(n):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if }\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{+}\mbox{ and }n<p,\\
W(n-p)&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}}}\right.

and
(\tau^{-p}W)(n):=W(n+p)
, for
n\in\mathbb{G}
. If
W\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G};M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
, we write
W=(W^{ij})_{i,j=1,2}
, and
W^{*}
is the sequence
((W(n))^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{G}}
.

Now, let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
and
m\in\{1,2\}
. We consider the seminorms on
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G};M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
given by

\displaystyle q_{k,1}(W)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\|n(W-\tau^{k}W)(n)\|,

\displaystyle q_{k,2}(W)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\|n^{2}(W-2\tau^{k}W+\tau^{2k}W)(n)\|.

If
W\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G};M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
, we let
q_{0}(W)\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
be the sequence

{q_{0}(W)}(n)=\|W^{12}(n)\|+\|W^{21}(n)\|+\|(W^{11}-W^{22})(n)\|+\|(\tau W^{22}-W^{11})(n)\|.

(2.8)

Within this framework, we define five linear subspaces of
\ell^{\infty}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{G};M_{2}(\mathbb{C})}}\right)
, starting with

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{0,1}(\mathbb{G})

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{W\in\ell^{\infty}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{G};M_{2}(\mathbb{C})}}\right):\sup_{n\in\mathbb{G}}|n|(q_{0}(W))(n)<\infty}}\right\},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{k,m}(\mathbb{G})

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{W\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G};M_{2}(\mathbb{C})):\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{k,j}(W)<\infty}}\right\}.

(2.10)

Next, for
0<\beta<\gamma<\infty
fixed, we also define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G})

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{W\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G};M_{2}(\mathbb{C})):\int_{1}^{\infty}\sup_{\beta r<|n|<\gamma r}\|W(n)\|\,dr<\infty}}\right\},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{G})

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{W\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G};M_{2}(\mathbb{C})):\int_{\frac{1}{\gamma-\beta}}^{\infty}\sup_{\beta r<|n|<\gamma r}q_{0}(W)(n)\,dr<\infty}}\right\},

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{k}(\mathbb{G})

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{W\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G};M_{2}(\mathbb{C})):\int_{1}^{\infty}\sup_{\beta r<|n|<\gamma r}\|(\tau^{k}W-W)(n)\|\,dr<\infty}}\right\}.

(2.13)

Here, we adopt the convention that the supremum over the empty set is zero.

Remark 2.6
.

By Lemma
7.1
, the definition of the sets
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G})
and
\mathcal{M}_{k}(\mathbb{G})
, for
k\geq 0
, does not depend on the specific choice of
\beta
and
\gamma
.

2.3
Main results

In this section, we specify the assumptions on the perturbation
V
of
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}
. In our framework, we always assume that
V\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}})
has the form

V:=V_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S^{j}V_{j}+V_{j}^{*}S^{*j}}}\right),

(2.14)

with
V_{j}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G};M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
,
V_{0}=V_{0}^{*}
and
\lim_{|n|\to\infty}\|V_{j}(n)\|=0
for
j=0,1,\ldots,N
. This means that
V
is self-adjoint and
V\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}})
.

With the notation introduced in Section
2.2
, we formulate a first assumption on
V
.

Assumption 2.7
.

V\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}})
is a self-adjoint operator as in (
2.14
), and there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, with
k>0
, such that

V_{j}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G})+\mathcal{M}_{k}(\mathbb{G})+\mathcal{Q}_{k,2}(\mathbb{G})
for each
j=0,1,\ldots,N.

For the remainder of this paper, we let
X^{(\mathbb{G})}
be the position operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}
defined by

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X^{(\mathbb{G})}u}}\right)(n):=nu(n)\mbox{ for }u\in\mathcal{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right)\mbox{ and }n\in\mathbb{G},

(2.15)

where
\mathcal{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}:(nu(n))_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}}}\right\}.
Furthermore, for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
\mathscr{H}_{s}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right)
for the Sobolev space associated with

X^{(\mathbb{G})}
, so that
\mathscr{H}:=\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{G}}
. Our first result reads as follows.

Theorem 2.8
.

Suppose that
V
satisfies Assumption
2.7
for some
k>0
. Let
H^{(\mathbb{G})}:=H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}+V
and consider the sets

\displaystyle\mu_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right)

\displaystyle:=(I_{+}\cup I_{-})\Big\backslash\kappa_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right),

(2.16)

\displaystyle\tilde{\kappa}_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G}),V}}}\right)

\displaystyle:=\kappa_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right)\cup\mathcal{E}_{p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right)\cup\mathcal{E}_{p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right),

(2.17)

where

\kappa_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\pm\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}+2|a||b|\cos\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\pi j}{k}}}\right)}:j=0,1,\ldots,k}}\right\}.

(2.18)

Then
\sigma_{\ess}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right)=\sigma_{\ess}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right)
and the following assertions hold:

1.

For every compact subset
I
of
\mathbb{R}
with
I\subseteq\mu_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right)
, the set

\mathcal{E}_{p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right)\cap I

Definition 2.14

(i)

(ii)

Then

1.

(ii)

In addition, let

and

L_{2}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{\beta_{n}^{2}a_{n}}{\|f_{n}\|_{1}}\max M_{n}\in\mathbb{R},

(2.40)

where
M_{n}:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{|f_{n}(j)-f_{n}(j+1)|:\alpha_{n}\leq j<\beta_{n}}}\right\}\cup\{f_{n}(\beta_{n})\}
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then

(iii)

The sequences
(jb_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
and
(j^{2}(b_{j}-b_{j+1}))_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
are bounded.

Proof.

We first show (i). Let
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence of partial sums of the series
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{j}
. Observe that for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

S_{\beta_{n}}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\beta_{n}}b_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}b_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}a_{k}.

Thus,
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S_{\beta_{n}}}}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
converges to the same limit of the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}a_{n}
. Furthermore, for
\beta_{n}<l<\beta_{n+1}
, we have
S_{\beta_{n}}\leq S_{l}\leq S_{\beta_{n+1}}
when
n
is odd, and
S_{\beta_{n}}\geq S_{l}\geq S_{\beta_{n+1}}
when
n
is even. This implies (i).

To show (ii), let
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
. Then there is
n_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
p<|\mathcal{P}_{n_{p}}|
. Thus,
p<\beta_{n_{p}}
and for each
N>{n_{p}}
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\beta_{N}}|b_{j}+b_{j+1}+\cdots+b_{j+p-1}|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=n_{p}}^{N}\sum_{j=\alpha_{n}}^{\beta_{n}-p+1}|b_{j}+b_{j+1}+\cdots+b_{j+p-1}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=n_{p}}^{N}\sum_{j=\alpha_{n}}^{\beta_{n}-p+1}\frac{a_{n}}{\|f_{n}\|_{1}}(f_{n}(j)+\cdots+f_{n}(j+p-1))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=n_{p}}^{N}\frac{a_{n}}{\|f_{n}\|_{1}}\|f_{n}\|_{1}=\sum_{n=n_{p}}^{N}a_{n}.

From this, we deduce that the series given in (ii) diverges.

It remains to prove (iii). For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
j\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
we have

\displaystyle|jb_{j}|

\displaystyle=\frac{ja_{n}}{\|f_{n}\|_{1}}f_{n}(j)\leq\frac{\beta_{n}a_{n}\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}}{\|f_{n}\|_{1}}\leq L_{1}.

This means that
(jb_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is bounded. To show that the sequence
(j^{2}(b_{j}-b_{j+1}))_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is also bounded, we consider two cases. If
j\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\setminus\{\beta_{n}\}
, then

\displaystyle|j^{2}(b_{j}-b_{j+1})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta_{n}^{2}a_{n}}{\|f_{n}\|_{1}}|f_{n}(j)-f_{n}(j+1)|\leq L_{2}.

While for
j=\beta_{n}
we have

\displaystyle|j^{2}(b_{j}-b_{j+1})|

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{n}^{2}a_{n}}{\|f_{n}\|_{1}}f_{n}(\beta_{n})\leq L_{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

3
A brief review on positive commutators methods

This section introduces the key ideas from Mourre theory that will be used later. Most of the notations and definitions are adopted from
[
2
]
, to which we refer for further details. Through this section, unless explicitly stated otherwise, let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
denote a Borel set,
A
a self-adjoint operator acting on a separable complex Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
, with domain
\mathcal{D}(A)
, and
H,T\in\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{H}}}\right)
with
H
self-adjoint.

The Mourre theory refers to the positive commutator method introduced in
[
25
]
. This theory enables the study of the nature of the spectrum of
H
in
I
, based on the regularity of
H
with respect to
A
and the local positivity of the commutator
[iA,H]
. We begin by defining the regularity classes associated to
A
.

Definition 3.1
.

(i)

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. We say that
T
is of class
C^{m}(A)
, and write
T\in C^{m}(A)
, if the map
\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{H}}}\right)
, defined by
\mathcal{T}(t):=e^{iAt}Te^{-iAt}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, is of class
C^{m}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to strong topology of
\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{H}}}\right)
. The notation
T\in C^{\infty}(A)
means that
T\in C^{m}(A)
for all
m\geq 1
.

(ii)

We say that
T
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
provided that

\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{iAt}Te^{-iAt}+e^{-iAt}Te^{iAt}-2T\|\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}<\infty.

In this case, we write
T\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
.

Remark 3.2
.

(i)

The operator
T
is of class
C^{1}(A)
if and only if
T
leaves the domain of
A
invariant, and the operator
i(AT-TA)
defined on
\mathcal{D}(A)
has a bounded extension to
\mathscr{H}
. We denote this bounded extension by
[iA,T]
. In this case,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(0)=[iA,T].

(ii)

T\in C^{2}(A)
is equivalent to the property that
T\in C^{1}(A)
and
[iA,T]\in C^{1}(A)
, so that the second order commutator
[iA,[iA,T]]
has a bounded extension to the whole space.

(iii)

The sets
C^{m}(A)
and
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
are Banach
*
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{H}}}\right)
and the inclusions
C^{m}(A)\subset\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)\subset C^{1}(A)
are true for all
m\geq 2
.

From now on, we adopt the following notation: given two operators
R,T\in\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{H}}}\right)
, we write
R\simeq T
to express that
R-T\in\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{H})
, and
R\gtrsim T
means that
R\geq T+K
for some
K\in\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{H})
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
H\in C^{1}(A)
. The operator
A
is conjugate to
H
on
I
if there exist
c>0
such that

E_{I}(H)[iA,H]E_{I}(H)\gtrsim cE_{I}(H).

(3.1)

This is known as a Mourre estimate for the triplet
(H,A,I)
. If

E_{I}(H)[iA,H]E_{I}(H)\geq cE_{I}(H),

(3.2)

then
A
is strictly conjugate to
H
on
I
, and this inequality is called a strict Mourre estimate for the triplet
(H,A,I)
. We also define the subsets
\tilde{\mu}_{A}^{\pm}(H)
and
{\mu}_{A}^{\pm}(H)
of
\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{A}^{\pm}(H)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in\sigma_{\ess}(H):\exists c>0\mbox{ and an open interval }I;x\in I\mbox{ and }\eqref{ME}\mbox{ holds with}\pm A\},

\displaystyle{\mu}_{A}^{\pm}(H)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in\sigma_{\ess}(H):\exists c>0\mbox{ and an open interval }I;x\in I\mbox{ and }\eqref{SME}\mbox{ holds with}\pm A\}.

We also let
\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H):=\tilde{\mu}_{A}^{+}(H)\cup\tilde{\mu}_{A}^{-}(H)
,
\mu_{A}(H):=\mu_{A}^{+}(H)\cup\mu_{A}^{-}(H)
, and

\kappa_{A}(H):=\sigma_{\ess}(H)\setminus\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H).

(3.3)

We say that
\kappa_{A}(H)
is the set of critical points of
H
with respect to
A.

In Definition
3.3
, we have
\pm A
is locally conjugate (respectively, strictly conjugate) to
H
on
\tilde{\mu}_{A}^{\pm}(H)
(respectively,
{\mu}_{A}^{\pm}(H)
). The following result describes the difference between
\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H)
and
\mu_{A}(H)
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
H\in C^{1}(A)
. Then

(i)

The spectrum of
H
in
\mu_{A}(H)
is purely continuous and
\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H)\setminus\mu_{A}(H)
consists of eigenvalues of
H
of finite multiplicity.

(ii)

If
\overline{I}\subseteq\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H)
, the set
\mathcal{E}_{p}(H)\cap I
is finite. In particular, the
possible accumulation points of
\mathcal{E}_{p}(H)
belong to
\kappa_{A}(H)
.

Proof.

Assertion (i) is a consequence of the Virial Theorem
[
2
, Proposition 7.2.10]
. To show (ii), observe that if
\overline{I}\subseteq\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H)
, then
\overline{I}
is compact since
\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H)\subseteq\sigma(H)
is bounded. Hence, taking a finite open cover of
\overline{I}
, and using (i), we deduce that
\mathcal{E}_{p}(H)\cap I
is finite. This, in turn, yields the claimed assertion concerning accumulation points of
\mathcal{E}_{p}(H)
.
∎

If
H\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
, then
H
has no singular continuous spectrum on
\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H)
. Recall that for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathscr{H}_{s}(A)
denotes the Sobolev space associated to
A
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
H\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
,
s>1/2
and
\mathcal{K}=\mathscr{H}_{s}(A)
.
Then the following LAP holds: the holomorphic map
\mathbb{C}_{\pm}\ni z\mapsto(H-z)^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{*}}}\right)
extends to a weak
∗
-continuous map on
\mathbb{C}_{\pm}\cup\mu_{A}(H)
. It follows that the spectrum of
H
is purely absolutely continuous on
\mu_{A}(H)
and
\sic(H)\cap\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H)=\emptyset
.

Remark 3.6
.

In Theorem
3.5
, a LAP can be guaranteed for the Banach space
(D(A),\mathscr{H})_{1/2,1}
, obtained via real interpolation (see
[
2
]
for a proof). Moreover, similar observations to those made in Remark
2.12
hold here as well.

Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
provide useful connections between the spectral properties of
H
and the regularity of
H
with respect to
A
. Now, a natural question is whether these spectral properties remain stable under suitable compact perturbations. In this context, it is useful to consider the following definition.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
H\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
. We say that an operator
V\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})
is an admissible perturbation for
H
with respect to
A
provided that
V
is self-adjoint and
V\in\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{H})\cap\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
.

The next proposition says that the spectral properties of
H
are essentially stable under admissible perturbations.

Proposition 3.8
.

Assume that
H\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
and
V
is an admissible perturbation for
H
with respect to
A
, and let
\tilde{\kappa}_{A}(H,V):=\kappa_{A}(H)\cup\mathcal{E}_{p}(H)\cup E_{p}(H+V)
. Then
\sigma_{\ess}(H+V)=\sigma_{\ess}(H)
and the following assertions hold:

(i)

H+V\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
,
[iA,V]
is a compact operator,
\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H)=\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H+V)
and
\kappa_{A}(H)=\kappa_{A}(H+V)
.

(ii)

The conclusions from Theorem
3.4
hold for
H
and
H+V
. In particular, the set
\tilde{\kappa}_{A}(H,V)
is closed.

(iii)

For all
s>1/2
and
\mathcal{K}=\mathscr{H}_{s}(A)
, the following LAP is satisfied: the holomorphic map
\mathbb{C}_{\pm}\ni z\mapsto(H-z)^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{*}}}\right)
extends to a weak
∗
-continuous map on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\tilde{\kappa}_{A}(H,V)
. In particular, if
\tilde{\kappa}_{A}(H,V)
is countable then
\sic(H+V)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Thanks to the Weyl criterion we know that
\sigma_{\ess}(H+V)=\sigma_{\ess}(H)
. Assertion (i) follows from Remark
3.2
,
[
18
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
2
, Theorem 7.2.9]
. The statement given in (ii) is a direct consequence of the fact that
H+V\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)\subseteq C^{1}(A)
. Finally, (iii) follows from Theorem
3.5
and the inclusion
\sigma_{\ess}(H)\setminus\tilde{\kappa}_{A}(H,V)\subseteq\mu_{A}(H)\cap\mu_{A}(H+V)
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

In Proposition
3.8
, the sets
\mu_{A}(H)
and
\mu_{A}(H+V)
may differ. Observe that the possible eigenvalues of
H+V
embedded in
\sigma_{\ess}(H)
are included in
\kappa_{A}(H)\cup(\tilde{\mu}_{A}(H)\setminus{\mu}_{A}(H+V))
.

We close this section by defining another regularity class with respect to
A
, which provides a criterion to deal with the class
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
.

Definition 3.10
.

We say that
T\in\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{H}}}\right)
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{0,1}(A)
if

\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{iAt}Te^{-iAt}-T\|\,\frac{dt}{t}<\infty.

In this case, we write
T\in\mathcal{C}^{0,1}(A)
.

Remark 3.11
.

The above class is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{H}}}\right)
, stable under adjuntion. Furthermore, according to inclusion (5.2.19) of
[
2
]
, if
T\in C^{1}(A)
and
[iA,T]\in\mathcal{C}^{0,1}(A)
, then
T\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
.

(4.1)

An adaptation of the arguments given in

shows that

(4.3)

With this notation, we formulate the first result of this section.

Theorem 4.1

Let

(4.4)

In particular, if for each

\kappa_{A_{k}}(H_{0})=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\pm\lambda\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\theta_{j}}}\right):j=0,1,\ldots,k}}\right\},

(4.5)

and

\mu^{+}_{A_{k}}(H_{0})=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}}}\right\rfloor}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lambda(\theta_{2j+1}),\lambda(\theta_{2j})}}\right),\quad\quad\mu^{-}_{A_{k}}(H_{0})=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}}}\right\rfloor}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lambda(\theta_{2j}),\lambda(\theta_{2j-1})}}\right),

(4.6)

Proof.

We first show that
{H}_{0}\in C^{\infty}({A}_{k})

\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{C}^{2})

.

Regarding identity (

{\eta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}_{+}})

\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\widehat{H}_{0}}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[i\widehat{A}_{k},\widehat{H}_{0}}}\right]E_{\Lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\widehat{H}_{0}}}}\right)

\displaystyle=E_{\Lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\widehat{H}_{0}}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[i\widehat{A}_{k},{\eta}(\widehat{H}_{0})\widehat{H}_{0}}}\right]E_{\Lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\widehat{H}_{0}}}}\right).

For each

By the choice of

\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\widehat{H}_{0}}}}\right)\Pi^{\prime}E_{\Lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\widehat{H}_{0}}}}\right)=E_{\Lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\widehat{H}_{0}}}}\right)\Pi\Pi^{\prime}\Pi E_{\Lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\widehat{H}_{0}}}}\right)=0.

\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\widehat{H}_{0}}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[i\widehat{A}_{k},\widehat{H}_{0}}}\right]E_{\Lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\widehat{H}_{0}}}}\right)

4.2

The aim now is to construct a family of conjugate operators for

and
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})

(\delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
and
(\delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\delta_{n}^{-},\delta_{n}^{+}:n\in\mathbb{G}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\delta_{n}^{-}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta_{n}\\
0\end{array}}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{n}^{+}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\delta_{n}\end{array}}}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{G}.

Moreover, if

is the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}

P=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(|\delta_{j}^{-}\rangle\langle\delta_{j}^{-}|+|\delta_{j}^{+}\rangle\langle\delta_{j}^{+}|}}\right).

Mind that a linear operator

can be canonically identified with
{PTP}

is a linear operator acting on a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}

{PTP}
can be considered as a linear operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}

Now, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
, let
A_{k}^{+}
be the self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
defined by

A_{k}^{+}:=PA_{k}P,\quad k>0,

where
(A_{k})_{k>0}
is the sequence of operators on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}
defined in Section

. The operator

agrees with the closure of the essentially self-adjoint operator defined on

by

-\frac{|a||b|}{4i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(e^{ik\varphi}S^{k}-e^{-ik\varphi}S^{*k}}}\right)X^{+}+X^{+}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(e^{ik\varphi}S^{k}-e^{-ik\varphi}S^{*k}}}\right)}}\right].

(4.8)

We require two auxiliary lemmas to prove that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A_{k}^{+}}}\right)_{k>0}
is indeed a sequence of conjugate operators for
H_{0}^{+}
. The scalar version of the next result was proved in
[
3

.

Lemma 4.3
.

The operators
PH_{0}P^{\perp}
and
P^{\perp}H_{0}P
are of finite rank. Moreover, if
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a continuous function of compact support, then

(i)

\Phi(H_{0}^{+})\simeq P\Phi(H_{0})P
.

(ii)

P\Phi(H_{0})P^{\perp}
and
P^{\perp}\Phi(H_{0})P
are compact.

Proof.

Simple calculations show that

PH_{0}P^{\perp}=\overline{b}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\delta_{0}^{-}}}\right\rangle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\delta_{-1}^{-}}}\right|\quad\mbox{and}\quad P^{\perp}H_{0}P=b\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\delta_{-1}^{+}}}\right\rangle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\delta_{0}^{+}}}\right|.

According to the Stone-Weierstrass Theorem, to show statements (i) and (ii), it is enough to consider the case when
\Phi
is a polynomial. We prove by induction that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{+}}}\right)^{j}\simeq P(H_{0})^{j}P
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. This is true for
j=0
. Assume the induction hypothesis for some
j\geq 0
. Then

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{+}}}\right)^{j+1}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0}^{+}}}\right)^{j}(PH_{0}P)\simeq P(H_{0})^{j}PH_{0}P=P(H_{0})^{j}(1-P^{\perp})(H_{0})P\simeq P(H_{0})^{j+1}P.

Thus statement (i) is true if
\Phi
is a polynomial.

We also proceed by induction to verify that
P(H_{0})^{j}P^{\perp}\simeq 0
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. This is clear for
j=0
. Suppose the induction hypothesis is true for some
j\geq 0
. Then

P(H_{0})^{j+1}P^{\perp}=P(H_{0})^{j}PPH_{0}P^{\perp}+P(H_{0})^{j}P^{\perp}P^{\perp}H_{0}P^{\perp}\simeq 0.

Hence
P\Phi(H_{0})P^{\perp}\simeq 0
if
\Phi
is a polynomial. Finally, note that
P^{\perp}\Phi(H_{0})P=(P\overline{\Phi}(H_{0})P^{\perp})^{*}
is also compact.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, with
k>0
. The operators
PA_{k}P^{\perp}
and
P^{\perp}A_{k}P
are of finite-rank, and their ranges are included in
\mathcal{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A_{k}}}\right)
.

Proof.

Direct calculations show that

PA_{k}P^{\perp}=-\frac{|a||b|}{4i}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(2j-k)e^{ik\varphi}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\delta_{j}^{-}}}\right\rangle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\delta_{j-k}^{-}}}\right|+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\delta_{j}^{+}}}\right\rangle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\delta_{j-k}^{+}}}\right|}}\right)

and

P^{\perp}A_{k}P=\frac{|a||b|}{4i}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(2j-k)e^{-ik\varphi}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\delta_{j-k}^{-}}}\right\rangle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\delta_{j}^{-}}}\right|+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\delta_{j-k}^{+}}}\right\rangle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\delta_{j}^{+}}}\right|}}\right).

The conclusion follows.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
H_{0}^{+}
is of class
C^{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A_{k}^{+}}}\right)
and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[iA_{k}^{+},H_{0}^{+}}}\right]\simeq P\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[iA_{k},H_{0}}}\right]P

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
,

. So

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[iA_{k}^{+},H_{0}^{+}}}\right]=[iPA_{k}P,PH_{0}P]=P[iA_{k},H_{0}]P-iPA_{k}P^{\perp}H_{0}P+iPH_{0}P^{\perp}A_{k}P.

By Lemma

, this means that

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[iA_{k}^{+},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[iA_{k}^{+},H_{0}^{+}}}\right]}}\right]-P\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[iA_{k},[iA_{k},H_{0}]}}\right]P

\displaystyle=-iPA_{k}P^{\perp}[iA_{k},H_{0}]P+iP[iA_{k},H_{0}]P^{\perp}A_{k}P

\displaystyle\quad+PA_{k}PA_{k}P^{\perp}H_{0}P+PH_{0}P^{\perp}A_{k}PA_{k}P

\displaystyle\quad-PA_{k}P^{\perp}H_{0}PA_{k}P-PA_{k}PH_{0}P^{\perp}A_{k}P.

By virtue of Lemmas
4.3
and

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[iA_{k}^{+},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[iA_{k}^{+},H_{0}^{+}}}\right]}}\right]\simeq P[iA_{k},[iA_{k},H_{0}]]P

∎

for

and

instead of

and

, respectively.

In this section, we assume that

Now, as in Remark
2.1

e^{-i\varphi_{2}}&0\end{pmatrix}F^{(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}S^{*}.

An argument by induction yields that

C^{\infty}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A_{k}^{(\mathbb{G})}}}\right).

.

Let

Let

\frac{4}{|a||b|}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[iA_{k}^{(\mathbb{G})},W}}\right]=e^{ik\varphi}S^{k}(2X^{(\mathbb{G})}+k)(\tau^{-k}W-W)+(2X^{(\mathbb{G})}+k)(\tau^{-k}W-W)e^{-ik\varphi}S^{*k}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[iA_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})},W}}\right]=-|a|(e^{i\varphi_{2}}S(2X^{(\mathbb{G})}+1)B_{1}(W)-2X^{(\mathbb{G})}B_{0}(W)+e^{-i\varphi_{2}}(2X^{(\mathbb{G})}+1)B_{-1}(W)S^{*}),

7
Appendix

The goal of this appendix is to show a technical lemma that allows us to relate the sets
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G})
and
\mathcal{M}_{k}(\mathbb{G})
, defined in Section
2.2
, with the classes of admissible perturbations for
H_{0}^{(\mathbb{G})}
with respect to the conjugate operators defined in Section
4
. We adopt the convention that the supremum over the empty set is zero.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
be a sequence of non-negative real numbers. Assume that there exist
0<\beta<\gamma<\infty
such that

L_{\beta,\gamma}:=\int_{\frac{1}{\gamma-\beta}}^{\infty}\sup_{\beta r<n<\gamma r}a_{n}\,dr=\int_{1}^{\infty}\sup_{\beta r<n<\gamma r}a_{n}\,dr<\infty.

Then

(i)

L_{\mu,\nu}<\infty
for all
0<\mu<\nu
.

(ii)

The sequence
(na_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is bounded.

(iii)

The series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}
converges.

Proof.

We start by showing (i). Let
c:=\frac{\gamma}{\beta}
and
0<\mu<\nu
. Suppose that
\frac{\nu}{\mu}=c
. By the change of variables
t=\frac{\beta r}{\mu},
we get

L_{\mu,\nu}=\int_{1}^{\infty}\sup_{\mu t<n<\nu t}a_{n}dt=\frac{\beta}{\mu}\int_{\frac{1}{\gamma-\beta}}^{\infty}\sup_{\beta r<n<\gamma r}a_{n}dr=\frac{\beta}{\mu}L_{\gamma,\beta}.

(7.1)

Now we consider the general case. Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
be such that
\nu<\mu c^{m}
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu t<n<\nu t}a_{n}\leq\sup_{\mu t<n<\mu c^{m}t}a_{n}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sup_{\mu c^{k}t<n<\mu c^{k+1}t}a_{n},

provided that
t>\frac{1}{\nu-\mu}
and
t\notin\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\frac{n}{\mu c^{k+1}}:k\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}}\right\}.
Combining this with (
7.1
), we conclude that
L_{\mu,\nu}<\infty
.

To show (ii) and (iii), fix
K\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
K>{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\beta}}
. Note that if
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
k\geq K
and
r\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{k}{\gamma},\frac{k}{\beta}}}\right)
, then
\beta r<k<\gamma r
. Hence

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{\gamma}}}\right)ka_{k}\leq\int_{\frac{k}{\gamma}}^{\frac{k}{\beta}}\sup_{\beta r<n<\gamma r}a_{n}\,dr\leq L_{\beta,\gamma},

which proves (ii).

Finally, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, with
k\geq K
, let
r_{k}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{k}{\gamma},\frac{k}{\beta}}}\right)
be such that

a_{k}\leq\sup_{\beta r_{k}<n<\gamma r_{k}}a_{n}\leq\inf\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sup_{\beta r<n<\gamma r}a_{n}:r\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{k}{\gamma},\frac{k}{\beta}}}\right)}}\right\}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}.

Then

\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}a_{k}\leq\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{k}{\gamma}}^{\frac{k}{\beta}}\sup_{\beta r<n<\gamma r}a_{n}\,dr+\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

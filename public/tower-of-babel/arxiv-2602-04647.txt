On κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn topological groups
Saak Gabriyelyan, Alexander V. Osipov, Evgenii Reznichenko

Abstract
We characterize κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn topological groups. Using this characterization we show that: (1) a hemicompact topological group is κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn iff it is locally compact, and (2) if F F is a closed metrizable subspace of a topological vector space (tvs) E E such that the quotient E / F E/F is a κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn space, then also E E is a κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn space. Consequently, the product of a κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn tvs and a metrizable tvs is a κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn space. Under Martin’s Axiom, we construct a countable Boolean κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn group which is not a k ℝ k_{\mathbb{R}} -space.

In this article we continue the study of topological groups (in particular, topological vector spaces) which are
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn spaces. In Theorem

\kappa

We start with the following characterization of topological groups which are
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn spaces, we shall call such groups by
\kappa

. The identity of a group

Following

Theorem 2.1
.

For a topological group

(i)

(ii)

(n_{k})\subseteq\omega

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) For every

(ii)

U_{n}:=U

(iii)

p_{n}\cdot\mathcal{V}_{n}:=\{p_{n}V^{i}_{n}:i\in\omega\}

e\not\in\overline{p_{n}W_{n}}

(2.1)

\mathcal{O}^{i}_{n}=p_{n}V^{i}_{n}\cap p_{n}W_{n}.

(z_{k})\subseteq\bigcup\{\mathcal{O}^{i}_{n}:0\leq n\leq i\}

As an application of Theorem
2.1
we obtain a structure of hemicompact groups with the
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn property.

Proposition 2.2
.

For a hemicompact topological group
G
the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

G
is
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn;

(ii)

G
is locally compact;

(iii)

G
is Baire.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Let
G
be
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn, and suppose for a contradiction that
G
is not locally compact. Take an increasing sequence
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}
of compact subsets of
G
such that any compact subset of
G
is contained in some
K_{n}
. We assume that
e\in K_{0}
. By our supposition all
K_{n}
are not neighborhoods of
e
. For every
n\in\omega
, set
U_{n}:=G{\setminus}K_{n}
. Then
U_{n}
is an open subset of
G
such that
e\in\overline{U_{n}}{\setminus}U_{n}
. Since
G
is
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn, (ii) of Theorem
2.1
implies that there are a strictly increasing sequence
(n_{k})\subseteq\omega
and a sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\omega}
in
G
such that
x_{k}\in U_{n_{k}}

(k\in\omega)
and
x_{k}\to e
. Since
K:=\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\omega}\cup\{e\}
is a compact subset of
G
, there is
m\in\omega
such that
K\subseteq K_{m}
. Take
k\in\omega
such that
n_{k}>m
. Then
x_{k}\in K_{m}\cap U_{n_{k}}=K_{m}\cap(G{\setminus}K_{n_{k}})=\emptyset
. This is a contradiction.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) If
G
is locally compact, then

is
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn by Corollary 6.6 of
[

]
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is trivial.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) If
G
is Baire, then (see (i)), there is

such that the compact set

contains an open subset. Thus

The condition of being
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn in Proposition
2.2
cannot be replaced by the property of being a sequential space. Indeed, it is well-known that the free abelian group
A([0,\omega])
over the convergent sequence
[0,\omega]
is a sequential hemicompact group which is not locally compact.

Example 14.3 of
[
10
]
states that an abelian hemicompact topological group
G
is Fréchet–Urysohn if, and only if, it is a locally compact Polish group. Recall that a Tychonoff space
X
is
Fréchet–Urysohn
if, for any
A\subseteq X
and each
x\in\overline{A}
, there exists a sequence
\{a_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq A
that converges to
x
. Below we show that the condition of being abelian can be omitted.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
G
be a hemicompact topological group. Then
G
is Fréchet–Urysohn if, and only if, it is a locally compact Polish group.

Proof.

Assume that
G
is Fréchet–Urysohn. Then, by Proposition
2.2
,
G
is locally compact. The group
G
being Fréchet–Urysohn has countable tightness. Recall that, by
[
9
]
, the character of any locally compact group coincides with its tightness. Therefore
G
has countable character, and hence, by the Kakutani metrization theorem,
G
is metrizable. As
G
is hemicompact and metrizable, it is separable. Thus
G
is a locally compact Polish group. The converse assertion is clear.
∎

Proposition 14.4 of
[
10
]
states that if
F
is a closed metrizable subspace of a tvs
E
such that
E/F
is Fréchet–Urysohn, then the space
E
is a Fréchet–Urysohn space, too. Below we show that an analogous result holds also for
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn spaces.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
F
is a closed metrizable subspace of a topological vector space
E
such that the quotient
E/F
is a
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn space, then
E
is also a
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn space.

Proof.

Since
F
is a metrizable subspace of
E
, there exists a decreasing sequence
\{V_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}
of balanced neighborhoods of zero in
E
such that
V_{n+1}+V_{n+1}\subseteq V_{n}
for all
n\in\omega
and the sequence
\{V_{n}\cap F\}_{n\in\omega}
is a basis of neighborhoods of zero in
F
. To prove that
E
is

\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn, it is sufficient to show that if
U\subseteq E
is an open set and
0\in\overline{U}{\setminus}U
, then there exists a sequence in
U
converging to
0
.

Let
p:E\rightarrow E/F
be the quotient map. Note that
0\in\overline{V_{n}\cap U}
for all
n\in\omega
. Hence
p(0)\in\overline{p(V_{n}\cap U)}
. Since
V_{n}\cap U
is open and
p
is an open map, the set
p(V_{n}\cap U)
is open in
E/F
. Hence, by Theorem
2.1
(ii), there are a sequence
(n_{k})\subseteq\omega
and a sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\omega}\subseteq E
such that

p(x_{k})\in p(V_{n_{k}}\cap U)\;\mbox{ and }\;p(x_{k})\rightarrow p(0)\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

In particular, for every
k\in\omega
, there are
z_{k}\in V_{n_{k}}\cap U
and
f_{k}\in F
such that
x_{k}=z_{k}+f_{k}
. Replacing
x_{k}
by
z_{k}
if needed, we can assume that
x_{k}\in V_{n_{k}}\cap U
for every
k\in\omega
.

Fix a balanced neighborhood
W
of zero in
E
. Choose
n_{0}\in\omega
such that
(V_{n_{0}}+V_{n_{0}})\cap F\subseteq W
. Taking into account that
p(W\cap V_{n_{0}})
is an open neighborhood of zero in
E/F
,
W\cap V_{n_{0}}
is balanced and
p(x_{k})\to 0
, we obtain that there is an
m>n_{0}
such that

\big[x_{k}+(W\cap V_{n_{0}})\big]\cap F\neq\emptyset\quad\mbox{ for all $k>m$.}

Therefore, for every
k>m
, there exist
y_{k}\in W\cap V_{n_{0}}
and
u_{k}\in F
, such that
x_{k}+y_{k}=u_{k}
. Since
x_{k}\in V_{n_{k}}\subseteq V_{k}\subseteq V_{n_{0}}
and
y_{k}\in V_{n_{0}}
, we deduce that
u_{k}=x_{k}+y_{k}\in(V_{n_{0}}+V_{n_{0}})\cap F\subseteq W
. Therefore
x_{k}=u_{k}-y_{k}\in W+W
. Since
W
was arbitrary, this implies that
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\omega}
is a sequence in
U
that converges to
0
in
E
. Thus
E
is a
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn space.
∎

Michael showed (see

Corollary 2.5
.

If
E
is a
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn topological vector space and
F
is a metrizable topological vector space, then the product

\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn space.

Note that in Corollary

it is shown that the product of the Fréchet–Urysohn fun

Being motivated by the famous Malykhin problem (which asks whether there exists a countable non-metrizable Fréchet–Urysohn group), the following problem arises naturally (note that this problem complements Problem 2.1 of

Problem 2.6

Does there exist a countable
\kappa

Under Martin’s Axiom, in Example

For a (zero-dimensional) Tychonoff space
X
, we denote by

(resp.,
C_{p}(X,\mathbf{2})
) the space
C(X)
(resp.,
C(X,\mathbf{2})
) of all continuous functions from

\mathbb{R}
(resp., from

to the doubleton
\mathbf{2}=\mathbb{Z}(2)
considered as a discrete two element group) endowed with the pointwise topology. The space
B_{1}(X,\mathbf{2})
is the space of all functions

which are pointwise limits of sequences from

, it is also endowed with the pointwise topology.

The characteristic function of a subset

\mathbf{1}_{A}
.

Recall that a separable metrizable space

is called a

-set
if every subset of

is of type

in

. We shall use the next assertion.

Proposition 2.7
.

A separable zero-dimensional metrizable space

is a

-set if, and only if,
B_{1}(X,\mathbf{2})=\mathbf{2}^{X}
.

Proof.

Assume that
X
is a
Q
-set. Let

be arbitrary. Set

. Then
f=\mathbf{1}_{M}
. Since

is a

-set, we have
M=\bigcup_{n}A_{n}
and

for some increasing sequences
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}
and
\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}
of closed subsets of

, respectively. As

is zero-dimensional and Lindelöf, Theorem 6.2.7 of
[
2
]
implies that

is strongly zero-dimensional. Therefore, for every
n\in\omega
, there is a clopen subset

of

such that

. Then
\mathbf{1}_{C_{n}}\to\mathbf{1}_{M}=f
. Thus

Conversely, assume that

. To show that

be an arbitrary subset of

\mathbf{1}_{M}\in B_{1}(X,\mathbf{2})

\{\mathbf{1}_{A_{n}}\}_{n\in\omega}
be a sequence in

which pointwise converges to

A Tychonoff space

is a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space
if every
k
-continuous function

is continuous. Recall that a function

is called

if the restriction of

Recall also that a separable metrizable space

\gamma

if the space

Example 2.8
.

Under
\mathrm{MA}
, there is a countable Boolean
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn group
G
which is not a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space.

Proof.

First we prove the following claim.

Claim 1. If there is a
\gamma
-set
X
which is not a
Q
-set, then there is a countable Boolean
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn group
G
which is not a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space.

Proof of Claim 1.
It is well-known that any
\gamma
-set is zero-dimensional, see
[
8
, Corollary]
. It follows that the group
C_{p}(X,\mathbf{2})
is separable and Hausdorff. Let
H
be a countable and dense subgroup of
C_{p}(X,\mathbf{2})
. Since
X
is a
\gamma
-set, the space
C_{p}(X)
and hence its subgroup
C_{p}(X,\mathbf{2})
and the group
H
are Fréchet–Urysohn. As
X
is not a
Q
-set, Proposition
2.7
implies that there is a function
g\in\mathbf{2}^{X}{\setminus}B_{1}(X,\mathbf{2})
. Set

G:=H\cup(g+H)\subseteq\mathbf{2}^{X}.

It is clear that
G
is a countable subgroup of
\mathbf{2}^{X}
. We show that
G
is as desired.

Observe that the group
H
is dense in
\mathbf{2}^{X}
and, hence,
H
and
g+H
are dense in
G
. By construction,
H
and hence also
g+H
are Fréchet–Urysohn spaces. Therefore, by
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
, the group

is a
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn space.

It remains to show that
G
is not a

-space. To this end, it suffices to show that the characteristic function
\mathbf{1}_{H}
of
H
, which is discontinuous by the density of

, is
k
-continuous. To prove that
\mathbf{1}_{H}
is

-continuous we show that for every compact subset

of

and

are compact. By the symmetry of
L
and
M
, we show that

is a closed subset of
G
.

Assume that

, and suppose for a contradiction that

and hence
h\in g+H
.
Since
\overline{L}
is a closed subset of the compact countable set

,
\overline{L}
is metrizable. Therefore, there is a sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}
in
L
converging to

. Take

such that

. Then

, and hence

. But this contradicts the choice of the function

that finishes the proof of Claim 1.

To finish the proof of the example, we note that Theorem 1 of
[
7
]
states that, under Martin’s Axiom, there is a
\gamma
-set

of cardinality the continuum. On the other hand, any

-set has cardinality strictly less than

, see the discussion after Theorem 8.54 of

. Therefore the
\gamma
-set

is not a

∎

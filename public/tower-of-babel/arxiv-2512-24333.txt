On Quadratic Lie algebras containing the Heisenberg Lie algebra
R. García-Delgado

Abstract
. In this work we study quadratic Lie algebras that contain the Heisenberg Lie algebra 𝔥 m \mathfrak{h}_{m} as an ideal. We give a procedure for constructing these kind of quadratic Lie algebras and prove that any quadratic Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} that contains the Heisenberg Lie algebra as an ideal is constructed by using this procedure. We state necessary and sufficiency conditions to determine whether an indecomposable quadratic Lie algebra is the Heisenberg Lie algebra extended by a derivation. In addition, we state necessary and sufficiency conditions to determine whether the quotient 𝔤 / 𝔥 m \mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{m} admits an invariant metric and we also study the case when the nilradical of the Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is equal to 𝔥 m \mathfrak{h}_{m} .

Introduction

A Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,])
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is called
quadratic
if it admits a non-degenerate and symmetric bilinear form
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
that is
invariant
under the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e,
B(x,[y,z])=B([x,y],z)
for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
. A bilinear form satisfying these conditions is called an
invariant metric
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

A semisimple Lie algebra is quadratic and the Killing form is its invariant metric. However, there are non-semisimple quadratic Lie algebras, an example of this is the following. Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
be a Lie algebra and let
\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast})
be its coadjoint representation. The vector space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
with the bracket defined by
[x+\zeta,y+\nu]^{\prime}=[x,y]+\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x)(\nu)-\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(y)(\zeta)
and the invariant metric
B
defined by
B(x+\zeta,y+\nu)=\zeta(y)+\nu(x)
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\zeta,\nu\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, is a non-semisimple quadratic Lie algebra because
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is a non-zero abelian ideal. In this case the invariant metric
B
is different from the Killing form.

One of the most important results in Lie theory is the classification of semisimple Lie algebras. One property sharing by semisimple Lie algebras is the existence of an invariant metric where Killing form acts as a such. Once the classification of all semisimple Lie algebras is known, it can be expected that a classification of all quadratic Lie algebras is possible. However, the problem of classifying solvable quadratic Lie algebras is as hard as classifying Lie algebras in general. Thus, there is a natural interest in obtaining structure results for non-semisimple quadratic Lie algebras because these results lead us to advance in the problem of classifying non-semisimple quadratic Lie algebras.

There is a procedure for constructing non-semisimples quadratic Lie algebras known as
double extension
(see
[
4
]
or
[
7
]
). This procedure gives a way to construct any non-semsimple quadratic Lie algebra from another of smaller dimension. In addition, Medina-Revoy theorem proves that any indecomposable non-simple quadratic Lie algebra is a double extension (see
[
7
]
). These results have been used to classify families of quadratic Lie algebras (see for instance
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
). Another efforts have been made by considering canonical ideals for quadratic Lie algebras without simple ideals (see
[
6
]
). In
[
2
]
the authors introduce the concept of amalgamation of quadratic Lie algebras and give a list of nilpotent quadratic Lie algebras of dimension less than 8.

Quadratic Lie algebras also appear in other areas of mathematics. If a Lie group admits a bi-invariant semi-Riemannian metric, then its Lie algebra endowed with the semi-Riemannian metric becomes a quadratic Lie algebra. Reciprocally, the invariant metric of a quadratic Lie algebra induces by left-translation a bi-invariant semi-Riemannian metric on any connected Lie group with the Lie algebra. In Conformal Field Theory, quadratic Lie algebras are the Lie algebras for which a Sugawara construction exists (see
[
4
]
).

As we mentioned above, classifying non-semisimple quadratic Lie algebras is as hard as classifying Lie algebras in general. This leads us to study families of non-semisimple quadratic Lie algebras with some interest not only in algebra, but also in geometry and physics. One of these families is that consisting of quadratic Lie algebras containing the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
as an ideal.

Throughout this manuscript, the Heisenberg Lie algebra of dimension
2m+1
is denoted by
\mathfrak{h}_{m}=V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
, where
V
is a
2m
-dimensional vector space endowed with a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega:V\times V\to\mathbb{F}
such that the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}_{m}}
on
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is defined by

(1)

\begin{split}&[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}_{m}}=\omega(u,v)\hslash\,\qquad\text{ for all }\,u,v\in V\,\text{ and }\\
&[u+\gamma\hslash,\hslash]_{\mathfrak{h}_{m}}=0\qquad\text{ for all }u\in V\,\text{ and }\gamma\in\mathbb{F}.\end{split}

It can be proved that any invariant and symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
degenerates on
\hslash
. However, there is an extension of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
that does admit an invariant metric that below we describe.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}
be a linear map satisfying

\phi(\hslash)=0,\quad\phi(V)\subset V\quad\text{ and }\quad\phi|_{V}\text{ is invertible. }

In addition suppose that
\omega(\phi(u),v)=-\omega(u,\phi(v))
for all
u,v\in V
, i.e,
\phi
belongs to
\mathfrak{o}(\omega)
. In the vector space
\mathbb{F}\phi\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
define a bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
by
[\phi,x]=\phi(x)
for all
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
and
[u,v]=\omega(u,v)\hslash
for all
u,v\in V
. Observe that
\phi
is a derivation of the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
. Then, the vector space
\mathbb{F}\phi\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}=\mathbb{F}\phi\oplus V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
becomes a quadratic Lie algebra with invariant metric
B
defined by

(2)

\begin{split}&B(u,v)=\omega(\phi^{-1}(u),v)\quad\text{ for all }u,v\in V,\\
&B(u,\phi)=B(u,\hslash)=0\quad\text{ for all }u\in V,\\
&B(\phi,\hslash)=1\quad\text{ and }\quad B(\phi,\phi)=B(\hslash,\hslash)=0.\end{split}

We call this quadratic Lie algebra
the Heisenberg Lie algebra extended by
\phi
and we denote it by
\mathfrak{h}_{m}(\phi)
. In fact,
\mathfrak{h}_{m}(\phi)
is a double extension of the abelian quadratic Lie algebra
V
by
\phi
(see
1.1
below). Observe that
[\mathfrak{h}_{m}(\phi),\mathfrak{h}_{m}(\phi)]=\mathfrak{h}_{m}
and that
\mathfrak{h}_{m}(\phi)
is a semidirect extension of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
by
\phi
. Any derivation of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
whose kernel is
\mathbb{F}\hslash
yields a semidirect extension of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
that admits an invariant metric. In
[
9
]
it is proved that abelian subalgebras of these kind of derivations of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
describe mechanical systems for quadratic Hamiltonians of
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
.

In Theorem
1.4
below we give a procedure for constructing a quadratic Lie algebra that contains the Heisenberg Lie algebra as an ideal. Before that, we develop results that elucidate the structure of such quadratic Lie algebras. In Theorem
1.5
we state necessary and sufficiency conditions to determine whether a quadratic Lie algebra is isomorphic to the Heisenberg Lie algebra extended by the derivation. In Theorem
1.6
we state necessary and sufficiency conditions so that the quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{m}
admits an invariant metric. In Theorem
2.1
we prove that if the nilradical of
\mathfrak{g}
is
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
, then the solvable radical
\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{g})
is a non-degenerate ideal and it is equal to the Heisenberg Lie algebra extended by a derivation.

1.
Heisenberg Lie algebra as an ideal

1.1 Lemma
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
be a quadratic Lie algebra such that the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}=V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then,

(i)

\mathbb{F}\hslash\subset Z(\mathfrak{g})
, i.e,
[\hslash,x]=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{h}_{m}\subset(\mathbb{F}\hslash)^{\perp}
, i.e.,
B(\hslash,x)=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

(iii)
The subspace
V
is non-degenerate.

(iv)
There exists a subspace
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{a}\subset V^{\perp}
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

(v)
The bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
has the following decomposition

(3)

\begin{split}\forall\,a,b\in\mathfrak{a},\quad&[a,b]=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\mu(a,b)\hslash,\\
\forall\,a\in\mathfrak{a},\,u\in V,\quad&[a,u]=\rho(a)(u),\\
\forall\,u,v\in V,\quad&[u,v]=\omega(u,v)\hslash.\end{split}

Where the bilinear map
(a,b)\mapsto[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}
makes
\mathfrak{a}
into a Lie algebra isomorphic to the quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{m}
,
\mu:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbb{F}
is a skew-symmetric bilinear map and
\rho:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{o}(\omega)
is a representation of
\mathfrak{a}
where
\mathfrak{o}(\omega)=\{f\in\operatorname{End}(V)\mid\omega(f(u),v)=-\omega(u,f(v))\text{ for all }u,v\in V\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a complementary subspace to
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
in
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
. Since
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, for every
a\in\mathfrak{a}
the adjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}(a)
yields a linear map
R(a):\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}
defined by
[a,x]=R(x)
for all
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
. From the Jacoby identity of the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
, it follows that the map
R(a):\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is a derivation of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

(i)
We shall prove that
\mathbb{F}\hslash\subset Z(\mathfrak{g})
. Observe that the center of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is
\mathbb{F}\,\hslash
. Since the center
\mathbb{F}\hslash
of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is invariant under any derivation of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
, then
R(a)(\mathbb{F}\hslash)\subset\mathbb{F}\hslash
for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}
. As
[\hslash,\mathfrak{h}_{m}]=\{0\}
, then
\mathbb{F}\hslash
is a one-dimensional ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. It is straightforward to verify that any one-dimensional ideal of a quadratic Lie algebra is contained in the center. Then,
\mathbb{F}\hslash\subset Z(\mathfrak{g})
.

(ii)
We shall prove that
\mathbb{F}h_{m}\subset(\mathbb{F}\hslash)^{\perp}
, i.e.,
B(\hslash,x)=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
. Let
0\neq u\in V
be an arbitrary element. Because
\omega
is non-degenerate, there exists
v\in V
such that
[u,v]=\hslash
. Then,

B(u,\hslash)=B(u,[u,v])=B([u,u],v)=0,

which shows that
B(V,\hslash)=\{0\}
. In addition, as
[V,\hslash]=\{0\}
we have

B(\hslash,\hslash)=B([u,v],\hslash)=B(u,[v,\hslash])=0.

Therefore,
B(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\hslash)=B(V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash,\hslash)=\{0\}
.

(iii)
We shall prove that the subspace
V
is non-degenerate. Let
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
u\in V
, then
[a,u]=R(a)(v)\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}=V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
. Hence, there are linear maps
\rho(a):V\to V
and
\tau(a):V\to\mathbb{F}
such that
R(a)(u)=\rho(a)(u)+\tau(a)(v)\hslash
for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
u\in V
.

As
\omega
is a non-degenerate, for each
a\in\mathfrak{a}
there exists an element
L(a)\in V
such that
\tau(a)(v)=\omega(L(a),v)
for all
v\in V
. Then,

(4)

[a,u]=R(a)(u)=\rho(a)(u)+\omega(L(a),v)\hslash.

By
(ii)
we know that
\mathfrak{h}_{m}\subset(\mathbb{F}\hslash)^{\perp}
, then the linear map
\eta:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbb{F}
,
a\mapsto B(a,\hslash)
, is non-zero. Hence there is an element
d\in\mathfrak{a}
such that
B(d,\hslash)=1
, consequently
\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{Ker}(\eta)\oplus\mathbb{F}d
.

Let
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
u,v\in V
. Using that
B
is invariant and that
\mathfrak{h}_{m}\subset(\mathbb{F}\hslash)^{\perp}
, by (
4
) we have

(5)

B(a,[u,v])=\omega(u,v)B(a,\hslash)=B([a,u],v)=B(\rho(a)(u),v).

Substituting
a
by
d
in (
5
) we get
\omega(u,v)=B(\rho(d)(u),v)
. If
u
lies in
\operatorname{Ker}(\rho(d))
, then
\omega(u,v)=0
for all
v\in V
. As
\omega
is non-degenerate,
u=0
proving that
\rho(d):V\to V
is invertible. Therefore,

(6)

B(u,v)=\omega(\rho(d)^{-1}(u),v)\qquad\text{ for all }u,v\in V.

As
\omega
is non-degenerate, by (
6
) it follows that
V
is non-degenerate.

(iv)
We have proved that
V
is a non-degenerate subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
\mathfrak{g}=V\oplus V^{\perp}
, where by
(ii)
,
\mathbb{F}\hslash\subset V^{\perp}
. Let
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
be a vector subspace complementary to
\mathbb{F}\hslash
in
V^{\perp}
, i.e,
V^{\perp}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
. Then,

\mathfrak{g}=V\oplus V^{\perp}=V\oplus(\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash)=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}.

Therefore, from now on we assume that the complementary vector subspace
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
is contained in
V^{\perp}
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

(v)
We claim that for every
a\in\mathfrak{a}
, the map
\rho(a)
satisfies

(7)

\omega(\rho(a)(u),v)=-\omega(u,\rho(a)(v))\,\quad\text{ for all }u,v\in V.

By
(i)
we have
[a,[u,v]]=\omega(u,v)[a,\hslash]=0
. On the other hand, the Jacobi identity of
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
implies

\displaystyle\,0=[a,[u,v]]

\displaystyle=[[a,u],v]+[u,[a,v]]=[\rho(a)(u),v]+[u,\rho(a)(v)]

\displaystyle=\omega(\rho(a)(u),v)\hslash+\omega(u,\rho(a)(v))\hslash,

from it follows (
7
).

Since
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
, for each pair of elements
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
, there are unique elements
[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}\in\mathfrak{a}
and
\Lambda(a,b)\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
such that
[a,b]=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\Lambda(a,b)
. The Jacobi identity of the bracket of
\mathfrak{g}
implies that the pair
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
is a Lie algebra isomorphic to the quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

As
\mathfrak{h}_{m}=V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
, for each pair
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
, there are unique elements
\lambda(a,b)\in V
and
\mu(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\Lambda(a,b)=\lambda(a,b)+\mu(a,b)\hslash
. Hence,

(8)

[a,b]=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\lambda(a,b)+\mu(a,b)\hslash=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\Lambda(a,b),

We will prove that the map
\rho:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
appearing in (
4
), is a Lie algebra representation of
\mathfrak{a}
. Let
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
and
u\in V
. From (
8
) we get

(9)

\begin{split}\,[[a,b],u]&=[[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}},u]+[\lambda(a,b),u]\\
\,&=\rho([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})(u)+\omega(L([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}),u)\hslash+\omega(\lambda(a,b),u)\hslash.\end{split}

On the other hand, by the Jacobi identity we have

(10)

\begin{split}\,[[a,b],u]&=[a,[b,u]]-[b,[a,u]]=[a,\rho(b)(u)]-[b,\rho(a)(u)]\\
\,&=[\rho(a),\rho(b)]_{\mathfrak{gl}(V)}(u)\\
\,&+(\omega(\rho(a)(L(b)),u)-\omega(\rho(b)(L(a)),u))\hslash\end{split}

Comparing (
9
) and (
10
) we obtain

(11)

\displaystyle\rho([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})=[\rho(a),b)]_{\mathfrak{gl}(V)},

(12)

\displaystyle L([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\lambda(a,b)=\rho(a)(L(b))-\rho(b)(L(a)).

From (
11
) we deduce that
\rho:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a representation. In fact, from (
7
) we have that
\operatorname{Im}(\rho)
is contained in
\mathfrak{o}(\omega)
.

Consider the following bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\prime}
on
\mathfrak{g}
:

(13)

\begin{split}\forall\,a,b\in\mathfrak{a},\quad&[a,b]^{\prime}=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\left(\mu(a,b)+\omega(L(a),L(b))\right)\hslash,\\
\forall\,a\in\mathfrak{a},\,v\in V,\quad&[a,v]^{\prime}=\rho(a)(v),\\
\forall\,u,v\in V,\quad&[u,v]^{\prime}=\omega(u,v)\hslash.\end{split}

Observe that
[a,b]^{\prime}
has no component in
V
. Let
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
u\in V
. Define the map
\Phi:(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\prime})\to(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
by

\Phi(a)=a-L(a),\quad\Phi(u)=u\quad\text{ and }\quad\Phi(\hslash)=\hslash,

where
a
is in
\mathfrak{a}
and
u
is in
V
. We affirm that
\Phi
is an algebra isomorphism. Indeed, let
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
, then

(14)

\begin{split}\Phi([a,b]^{\prime})&=\Phi([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\mu(a,b)\hslash+\omega(L(a),L(b))\hslash,\\
\,&=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}-L([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\mu(a,b)\hslash-\omega(L(a),L(b))\hslash.\end{split}

On the other hand,

(15)

\begin{split}[\Phi(a),\Phi(b)]&=[a-L(a),b-L(b)]\\
\,&=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\lambda(a,b)+\mu(a,b)\hslash-\rho(a)(L(b))-\omega(L(a),L(b))\\
\,&+\rho(b)(L(a))+\omega(L(b),L(a))\hslash+\omega(L(a),L(b))\hslash\\
\,&=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\lambda(a,b)-\rho(a)(L(b))+\rho(a)(L(b))-\omega(L(a),L(b)))\hslash\end{split}

From (
12
), (
14
) and (
15
) we get
\Phi([a,b]^{\prime})=[\Phi(a),\Phi(b)]
. In addition, let
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
v\in V
, then

\Phi([a,v]^{\prime})=\Phi(\rho(a)(v))=\rho(a)(v).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\,[\Phi(a),\Phi(v)]

\displaystyle=[a-L(a),v]=[a,v]-[L(a),v]

\displaystyle=\rho(a)(v)+\omega(L(a),v)\hslash-\omega(L(a),v)\hslash=\rho(a)(v).

Hence
\Phi([a,v]^{\prime})=[\Phi(a),\Phi(v)]
. Since
\Phi|_{\mathfrak{h}_{m}}=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{h}_{m}}
then
\Phi([u,v]^{\prime})=[\Phi(u),\Phi(v)]
for all
u,v\in V
. Therefore,
\Phi
is a Lie algebra isomorphism and by (
13
), from now on we shall assume that the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
of
\mathfrak{g}
can be written as

(16)

\begin{split}\,\forall\,a,b\in\mathfrak{a},\quad\,&[a,b]=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\mu(a,b)\hslash,\\
\,\forall\,a\in\mathfrak{a},\,v\in V,\quad&[a,v]=\rho(x)(v),\\
\,\forall\,u,v\in V,\quad&[u,v]=\omega(u,v)\hslash.\end{split}

Where
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
is a Lie algebra,
\mu:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbb{F}
is a skew-symmetric bilinear map and
\rho:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{o}(\omega)
is a Lie algebra representation.

1.1.
Double extension

For our purposes, we shall consider a particular case of a double extension of a quadratic Lie algebra. Let
(\mathcal{S},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathcal{S}},B_{\mathcal{S}})
be a quadratic Lie algebra and let
D:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}
be a derivation of
\mathcal{S}
such that
B_{\mathcal{S}}(D(x),y)=-B_{\mathcal{S}}(x,D(y))
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{S}
, i.e.,
D
is skew-symmetric with respect to
B_{\mathcal{S}}
. Let
\hslash
be a symbol. In the vector space
\mathbb{F}D\oplus\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
, define the bilinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
by

\displaystyle\,\forall\,x\in\mathcal{S},

\displaystyle[D,x]=D(x),

\displaystyle\,\forall\,x,y\in\mathcal{S},

\displaystyle[x,y]=[x,y]_{\mathcal{S}}+B_{\mathcal{S}}(D(x),y)\hslash,

\displaystyle\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{F}D\oplus\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash,

\displaystyle[\hslash,x]=0.

Then
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
is a Lie bracket on
\mathbb{F}D\oplus\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
. In addition, we define the bilinear form
B
on
\mathbb{F}D\oplus\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
by

\displaystyle B(x,y)=B_{\mathcal{S}}(x,y)\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{S},\qquad B(D,\hslash)=1,

\displaystyle B(x,D)=B(x,\hslash)=0\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{S},\qquad B(D,D)=B(\hslash,\hslash)=0.

Then,
B
is an invariant metric on
\mathbb{F}D\oplus\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
. The quadratic Lie algebra on
\mathbb{F}D\oplus\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
is known as the
double extension of
\mathcal{S}
by
D
and we denote it by
\mathcal{S}(D)
.

1.2 Lemma
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
be a quadratic Lie algebra containing the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
as an ideal. Then, there exists a complementary vector subspace
\mathfrak{a}
to
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
in
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying
(iv)
and
(v)
of Lemma
1.1
. In addition, there is a non-degenerate subspace
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that

(i)

\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}d
, where
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}=\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
and
B(d,\hslash)=1
.

(ii)

[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]_{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\mathcal{S}
.

(iii)
The subspace
\mathcal{S}
has a quadratic Lie algebra structure.

(iv)

[\mathcal{S},\mathfrak{h}_{m}]=\{0\}
.

(v)
There is a quadratic Lie algebra on
\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
isomorphic to a double extension of
\mathcal{S}
by a derivation
D
of
\mathcal{S}
.

(vi)
There is a linear map
\sigma:\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash\to\mathfrak{o}(\omega)

\sigma(d)
is invertible and
\sigma(x)=0

Proof.

(i)-(ii)
From Lemma

to
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
in

Let us consider the linear map

,

Let

belongs to
Z(\mathfrak{g})

, the linear map

is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore, we assume that
B(d,d)=0
. As
\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{Ker}(\eta)\oplus\mathbb{F}d
, we define the subspace

\mathfrak{b}=\{a-B(a,d)\hslash\mid a\in\mathfrak{a}\}=\{a-B(a,d)\hslash\mid a\in\operatorname{Ker}(\eta)\}\oplus\mathbb{F}d.

Then

\displaystyle\bullet\,\mathcal{S}=\{a-B(a,d)\hslash\mid a\in\operatorname{Ker}(\eta)\},

\displaystyle\bullet\,\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}\,\,\text{ and }

\displaystyle\bullet\,B(\mathcal{S},\hslash)=B(\mathcal{S},d)=\{0\}=B(\mathcal{S},V).

Observe that
\mathfrak{b}

\mathfrak{h}_{m}

\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}

The skew-symmetric bilinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{b}}

[a-B(a,d)\hslash,b-B(b,d)\hslash]_{\mathfrak{b}}=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}-B([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}},d)\hslash\quad\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathfrak{a},

\begin{split}\,\forall\,a,b\in\mathfrak{a},\quad\,&[a-B(a,d)\hslash,b-B(b,d)\hslash]^{\prime\prime}\\

\,\forall\,a\in\mathfrak{a},u\in V,\quad&[a-B(a,d)\hslash,u]^{\prime\prime}=\rho(a)(u),\\
\,\forall\,u,v\in V,\,\quad&[u,v]^{\prime\prime}=\omega(u,v)\hslash.\end{split}

Let
\Psi:\mathfrak{g}\to(\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}d)\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}

\displaystyle\,\forall\,a\in\mathfrak{a},

\displaystyle\Psi(a)=a-B(a,d)\hslash,

\displaystyle\,\forall\,x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m},

\displaystyle\Psi(x)=x.

It is straightforward to verify that

\mathfrak{a}
to
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
in
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}d
, where
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}=\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}

[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]_{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\mathcal{S}
and
B(d,\hslash)=1
.

(iii)
We shall prove that
B

\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}

x,z

\mathcal{S}
and

(17)

B([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}},z)=B([x,y],z)=B(x,[y,z])=B(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}}).

Whence,

[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}}
. By
(ii)

\mathcal{S}
is a non-degenerate subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore,
(\mathcal{S},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathcal{S}},B_{\mathcal{S}})
is a quadratic Lie algebra, where
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathcal{S}}={[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}}}|_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}}
and
B_{\mathcal{S}}=B|_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}}
.

(iv)
We will prove that
[S,V]=\{0\}
. We know that there is a Lie algebra representation
\rho:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{o}(\omega)
such that
[a,u]=\rho(a)(u)
for all

and

. In addition

6
)).

Because
\rho(d)
is invertible, then
V\subset[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{h}_{m}]=\rho(\mathfrak{a})(\mathfrak{h}_{m})
. As
\omega

u,v\in V

[u,v]=\hslash
. Then,
V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash=\mathfrak{h}_{m}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}_{m}]
, hence

\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}=C_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{h}_{m})=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[x,y]=0\text{ for all }y\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}\}.

Since
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}=\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}

\mathfrak{h}_{m}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
, hence
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}

.

(v)
Define the linear map
D:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}
by
D(a)=[d,a]_{\mathfrak{a}}
for all
a\in\mathcal{S}
. Since
[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]_{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\mathcal{S}
, the map
D
is well defined. The Jacobi identity shows that
D
is a derivation of
\mathcal{S}
and from (
17

B_{\mathcal{S}}
. Because
B(d,\hslash)=1
and
B(d,\mathcal{S})=\{0\}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=B(d,[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})=B(d,[a,b])-\mu(a,b)=B([d,a],b)-\mu(a,b)

\displaystyle=B_{\mathcal{S}}(D(a),b)-\mu(a,b),

hence
\mu(a,b)=B_{\mathcal{S}}(D(a),b)
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{S}
. In summary, as
\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash\subset\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
, on the vector space

we have the following

(18)

\begin{split}&[d,a]_{\mathfrak{a}}=D(a)\quad\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal{S},\\
&[a,b]=[a,b]_{\mathcal{S}}+B_{\mathcal{S}}(D(a),b)\hslash\quad\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathcal{S},\\
&B(a,b)=B_{\mathcal{S}}(a,b),\quad B(a,d)=B(a,\hslash)=0,\quad B(d,\hslash)=1,\\

Then

by

.

(vi)
We define the map

∎

1.3 Lemma
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)

\mathfrak{h}_{m}
as an ideal. Let
\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}d
be the subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the conditions of Lemma
1.2
and let
D:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}
be the derivation of
\mathcal{S}
defined by

(i)

Z(\mathfrak{g})=(\operatorname{Ker}(D)\cap Z(\mathcal{S}))\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
.

(ii)

(iii)

∎

In the following result we state a procedure to construct a quadratic Lie algebra that contains the Heisenberg Lie algebra as an ideal. We also prove that any quadratic Lie algebra containing the Heisenberg Lie algebra as an ideal can be constructed in this way.

1.4 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathcal{S},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathcal{S}},B_{\mathcal{S}})
be a quadratic Lie algebra and let
D
be a derivation in
\mathfrak{o}(B_{\mathcal{S}})
. Let
\hslash
be a symbol and in the vector space
\mathbb{F}D\oplus\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
consider the double extension
\mathcal{S}(D)
of
\mathcal{S}
by
D
. Let
V
be a
2m
-dimensional vector space equipped with a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega:V\times V\to\mathbb{F}
. If there exists a linear map
\sigma:\mathcal{S}(D)\to\mathfrak{o}(\omega)
such that
\sigma(D)
is invertible and
\sigma(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
, then in the vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}D\oplus V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
there is a quadratic Lie algebra structure containing the Heisenberg Lie algebra as an ideal. Reciprocally, every quadratic Lie algebra containing the Heisenberg Lie algebra as an ideal can be constructed in this way.

Proof.

On the vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}D\oplus V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
, we define the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
by

(19)

\begin{split}\,\forall\,a,b\in\mathcal{S},\quad\,&[a,b]=[a,b]_{\mathcal{S}}+B_{\mathcal{S}}(D(a),b)\hslash,\\
\,\forall\,u\in V,\,\quad&[D,u]=\sigma(d)(u),\\
\,\forall\,u,v\in V,\quad&[u,v]=\omega(u,v)\hslash.\end{split}

Observe that
V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
and that it is the Heisenberg Lie algebra. Consider the bilinear form
B_{V}
on
V
defined by
B_{V}(u,v)=\omega(\sigma(D)^{-1}(u),v)
for all
u,v\in V
. Observe that
B_{V}
is symmetric and non-degenerate. Now define the bilinear form
B
on
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{S}(D)\oplus V
by

(20)

B(x+u,y+v)=B_{\mathcal{S}(D)}(x,y)+B_{V}(u,v)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{S}(D)
and
u,v\in V
. Then,
B
is an invariant metric on
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
.

The proof that any quadratic Lie algebra containing the Heisenberg Lie algebra as an ideal, has the bracket as in (
19
) and the invariant metric as in (
20
), is obtained from Lemma
1.1
, the expression (
6
) and Lemma
1.2
.

∎

1.5 Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an indecomposable quadratic Lie algebra. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
extended by a derivation if and only if
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is the Heisenberg Lie algebra extended by a derivation. In the introduction we showed that
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

Now suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is an indecomposable quadratic Lie algebra such that
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{h}_{m}
. By Lemma
1.2
we can write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
, where
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=(\operatorname{Im}(D)+[\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}]_{\mathcal{S}})\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
(see Lemma
1.3
.(ii)). Hence,
\operatorname{Im}(D)=[\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}]_{\mathcal{S}}=\{0\}
. This implies
D=0
and that
\mathcal{S}
is a non-degenerate ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
(see (
19
)). As
\mathfrak{g}
is indecomposable,
\mathcal{S}=\{0\}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{F}d\oplus V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
is the Heisenberg Lie algebra extended by a derivation.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space endowed with a bilinear form. For every
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, define
B^{\flat}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
by
B^{\flat}(x)(y)
for all
y\in\mathfrak{g}
. If
B
is non-degenerate, then
B^{\flat}
is an invertible map and we consider its inverse map denoted by
B^{\sharp}:\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\to\mathfrak{g}
. Then, if
B^{\sharp}(\alpha)=x
, where
\alpha
is in
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
and
x
is in
\mathfrak{g}
, then
\alpha(y)=B^{\flat}(x,y)
for all
y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

In this work we aboard the problem of determining the structure of a quadratic Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
for which its nilradical is the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
. In this case, the quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{m}
turns out to be a reductive Lie algebra and therefore, is quadratic. The following result deals with this situation in a more general settling.

1.6 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
be a quadratic Lie algebra. Then, the quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{m}
admits an invariant metric if and only if there exists a subspace
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that

\bullet

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

\bullet

\mathfrak{a}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.,
[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]\subset\mathfrak{a}
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a subspace
\mathfrak{a}
such that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
and
[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]\subset\mathfrak{a}
. We will find an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{a}
. By Lemma
1.1
.(ii) we assume that
\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}d
where
B(\mathcal{S},\hslash)=\{0\}
and
B(d,\hslash)=1
. Using the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma
1.2
.(iii), we also assume that
[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]\subset\mathcal{S}
and
B(\mathcal{S},d)=\{0\}
. Because
B
restricted to
\mathcal{S}
is non-degenerate, this implies that
[d,\mathcal{S}]=\{0\}
, hence
[d,\mathfrak{a}]=\{0\}
. Now we define an invariant metric
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
on
\mathfrak{a}
by

B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a+\lambda d,b+\mu d)=B|_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}}(a,b)+\lambda\mu\,\,\,\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathcal{S}\text{ and }\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{F}.

Now suppose that
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{m}
admits an invariant metric. We will find a complementary vector space to
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
that it is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a subspace complementary to
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
in
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
. From Lemma
1.1
.(v), there exists a Lie algebra structure
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
such that
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
and the decomposition of the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
of
\mathfrak{g}
given in (
16
), holds. In addition, from Lemma
1.1
.(iv) we assume that
\mathfrak{a}\subset V^{\perp}
.

By hypothesis,
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{m}
admits an invariant metric, hence
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
is a quadratic Lie algebra. Let
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
be the invariant metric on
\mathfrak{a}
.

1.7 Lemma
.

There are linear maps
\varphi:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{g}
and
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{a}
such that

(i)

B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,b)=B(\varphi(a),b+x)
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii)

B(x,a)=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\phi(x),a)
for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(iii)

\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)=\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}
.

(iv)

\phi\circ\varphi=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

(v)

\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)=\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}
and
\varphi([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})=[\varphi(a),b]
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
p:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{a}
be the linear projection of
\mathfrak{g}
onto
\mathfrak{a}
, i.e,
p(a+x)=a
, for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
. Let
p^{\ast}:\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
be the dual map of
p
. Consider the map
\varphi:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{g}
defined by
\varphi=B^{\sharp}\circ p^{\ast}\circ B_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\flat}
. Then,

p^{\ast}(B_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\flat}(a))=B^{\flat}(\varphi(a))\quad\text{ for all }a\in\mathfrak{a}.

Hence for any
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
and
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
we have

(21)

\begin{split}B(\varphi(a),b+x)&=B^{\flat}(\varphi(a))(b+x)=p^{\ast}(B^{\flat}_{\mathfrak{a}}(a))(b+x)\\
\,&=B^{\flat}_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)(p(b+x))=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,b)\end{split}

From we deduce that
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\subset\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}
.

(ii)-(iii)
Let
\iota:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{g}
be the inclusion map. Let
\iota^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\to\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
be the dual map of
\iota
. Consider the map
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{a}
defined by
\phi=B_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\sharp}\circ\iota^{\ast}\circ B^{\flat}
. Then,

\iota^{\ast}(B^{\flat}(x))=B_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\flat}(\phi(x))\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{a}.

Hence for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
a\in\mathfrak{a}
we have

(22)

\begin{split}B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\phi(x),a)&=B_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\flat}(\phi(x))(a)=\iota^{\ast}(B^{\flat}(x))(a)\\
\,&=B^{\flat}(x)(\iota(a))=B(x,a).\end{split}

Whence,
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)=\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}
.

(iv)
Let
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
, from (
21
) and (
22
) we get

B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,b)=B(\varphi(a),b)=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\phi(\varphi(a)),b).

As
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate, it follows
\phi\circ\varphi=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{a}}
.

(v)
Observe that
x-\varphi(\phi(x))\in\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)=\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. As
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\subset\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}
and
\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}=\{0\}
, we have

(23)

\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}.

We claim that
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)=\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}
. Indeed, let
x
be in
\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}
. By (
23
), there are
y\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)
and
z\in\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}
such that
x=y+z
. Because
y\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\subset\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}
, then
z=x-y\in\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}=\{0\}
. Hence
x=y\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)
and
\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}=\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)
. This proves that
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Now we shall prove that

\varphi([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})=[\varphi(a),b]=[a,\varphi(b)]\quad\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathfrak{a}.

Since
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\varphi
is injective, for each pair
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
there is a unique element
x\in\mathfrak{a}
such that
[\varphi(a),b]=\varphi(x)
. Let
c\in\mathfrak{a}
be an arbitrary element, then

\begin{split}B([\varphi(a),b],c)&=B(\varphi(a),[b,c])=B(\varphi(a),[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}})\\
\,&=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}})=B_{\mathfrak{a}}([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}},c).\end{split}

Whereas,
B([\varphi(a),b],c)=B(\varphi(x),c)=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(x,c)
. As
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate, we deduce that
x=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}
and
\varphi([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})=[\varphi(a),b]
.
∎

1.8 Lemma
.

There are a linear maps
F,T:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}
such that

(i)

B_{\mathfrak{a}}(T(a),b)=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,T(b))
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
.

(ii)

[a,b]=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(F(a),b)\hslash
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
.

(iii)

F
is a derivation of
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
.

(iv)

T([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})=[T(a),b]_{\mathfrak{a}}
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
.

(v)

T\circ F=F\circ T=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{a}}(e^{\prime})
for some element
e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{a}
.

(vi)

\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset V^{\perp}
.

Proof.

(i)
As
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\subset\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
, for every
a\in\mathfrak{a}
, let
T(a)\in\mathfrak{a}
and
S(a)\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
be such that

(24)

\varphi(a)=T(a)+S(a).

We denote by
T:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}
the linear map
a\mapsto T(a)
. Similarly, we denote by
S:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}
the linear map
a\mapsto S(a)
.

We claim that
T
satisfies
B_{a}(T(a),b)=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,T(b))
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
. Indeed, by Lemma
1.7
.(i)-(v) we get

B_{\mathfrak{a}}(T(a),b)=B(T(a),\varphi(b))=B(\varphi(a),\varphi(b)).

Similarly we prove
B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,T(b))=B(\varphi(a),\varphi(b))
. Hence
B_{a}(T(a),b)=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,T(b))
.

(ii)-(iii)
Let us consider the bilinear map
\mu:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbb{F}
appearing in

[a,b]=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\mu(a,b)\mathbb{F}\hslash\quad\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathfrak{a}.

(see (
16
)). As
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate, there is a linear map
F:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}
such that
\mu(a,b)=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(F(a),b)
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
. Then,

(25)

[a,b]=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(F(a),b)\hslash\quad\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathfrak{a}.

Using that
\hslash\in Z(\mathfrak{g})
(see Lemma
1.1
.(i)), we have

[a,[b,c]]=[a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}}]_{\mathfrak{a}}+B_{a}(F(a),[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}})\hslash\quad\text{ for all }a,b,c\in\mathfrak{a}.

The Jacobi identity implies that
F
is a derivation of
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
.

(iv)-(v)
Consider the linear map
S:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}
appearing in (
24
). Since
\operatorname{Im}(S)\subset V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash=\mathfrak{h}_{m}
, there are a linear map
S_{V}:\mathfrak{a}\to V
and an element
e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{a}
such that
S(a)=S_{V}(a)+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(e^{\prime},a)\hslash
. By (
24
) it follows that
\varphi(a)=T(a)+S_{V}(a)+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(e^{\prime},a)\hslash
for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}
.

We claim that
S_{V}=0
. Indeed, let
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
u\in V
be arbitrary elements. From Lemma
1.1
.(iv) and Lemma
1.7
.(v) we have
0=B(\varphi(a),u)=B(S_{V}(a),u)
. As
V
is non-degenerate, (see Lemma
1.1
.(iii)), we deduce that
S_{V}=0
. So,
\varphi
can be written as

(26)

\varphi(a)=T(a)+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(e^{\prime},a)\hslash\quad\text{ for all }a\in\mathfrak{a}.

Using that
\varphi([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})=[\varphi(a),b]
and (
26
), we get

(27)

\displaystyle T([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})=[a,T(b)]_{\mathfrak{a}}=[T(a),b]_{\mathfrak{a}}\,\,\text{ and }

(28)

\displaystyle T\circ F=F\circ T=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{a}}(e^{\prime})\,\,\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathfrak{a}.

(vi)
Finally, as
\hslash\subset\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}
(see Lemma
1.1
.(ii)) and
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)=\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}\subset V^{\perp}
(see Lemma
1.7
.(v)), from (
26
) we obtain
T(a)=\varphi(a)-B_{\mathfrak{a}}(e^{\prime},a)\hslash\subset h_{m}^{\perp}
, which proves that
\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset h_{m}^{\perp}
.
∎

1.9 Lemma
.

There is an element
e\in\mathfrak{a}
such that

(i)

a=T(\phi(a+x))+B(a+x,\hslash)e
for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

(ii)

B(e,\hslash)=1
.

(iii)

T\circ F=F\circ T=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{a}}(e)
.

(iv)

T(\phi(e))=0
.

Proof.

The linear map
\psi:\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\ast}
defined by

\psi(x)(y)=B(x,y)\quad\text{ for all }x\in\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)=\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}\text{ and }y\in\mathfrak{h}_{m},

is a vector space isomorphism because
B
is non-degenerate and
\hslash\subset\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}
(see Lemma
1.1
.(ii)).

Let
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{2m},u_{2m+1}\}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
, where
u_{j}
is in
V
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,2m\}
and
u_{2m+1}=\hslash
. Then there is a basis

\{e_{1}+w^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,e_{2m+1}+w^{\prime}_{2m+1}\}\subset\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}\subset\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}

of
\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}
, where
e_{i}
is in
\mathfrak{a}
and
w^{\prime}_{i}
is in
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
, such that

\psi(e_{i}+w^{\prime}_{i})(u_{j})=B(e_{i}+w^{\prime}_{i},u_{j})=\delta_{ij}\quad\text{ for all }i,j\in\{1,\ldots,2m+1\}.

Let
e_{2m+1}=e\in\mathfrak{a}
, then
B(e,\hslash)=1
. We shall prove the following

(29)

a=T(\phi(a+x))+B(a+x,\hslash)e\quad\text{ for all }a\in\mathfrak{a}\text{ and }x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}.

Indeed, let
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
. Using that
\mathfrak{a}\subset V^{\perp}
(see Lemma
1.1
.(iv)), Lemma
1.8
.(i) and (
26
), we get

\displaystyle B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,b)=B(a,\varphi(b))=B(a,T(b))+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(b,e^{\prime})B(a,\hslash)

\displaystyle=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\phi(a),T(b))+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(b,B(a,\hslash)e^{\prime})

\displaystyle=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(T(\phi(a))+B(a,\hslash)e^{\prime},b).

Because
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate, we obtain

(30)

a=T(\phi(a))+B(a,\hslash)e^{\prime},\quad\text{ for all }a\in\mathfrak{a}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
1.7
.(v) and Lemma
1.8
.(i), it follows

\begin{split}0&=B(\varphi(a),x)=B(T(a),x)=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(T(a),\phi(x))\\
\,&=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,T(\phi(x)))\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}.\end{split}

Hence,
T(\phi(x))=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
, as
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate. Using this result and the fact that
\hslash\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}
(see Lemma
1.1
.(ii)), from (
30
) we deduce

(31)

a=T(\phi(a+x))+B(a+x,\hslash)e^{\prime}\quad\text{ for all }a\in\mathfrak{a}\text{ and }x\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}.

In particular, if we replace
a+x
by
e+w^{\prime}_{2m+1}\in\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
in (
31
), we obtain
e=e^{\prime}
, proving (
29
). In addition we get
T\circ F=F\circ T=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{a}}(e)
. If we apply
\phi
in (
30
) we obtain

\phi(a)=\phi(T(\phi(a)))+B(a,\hslash)\phi(e)\quad\text{ for all }a\in\mathfrak{a}.

In particular, for
a=e
we get
\phi(T(\phi(e)))=0
. Hence, by Lemma
1.8
.(vi),
T(\phi(e))\in\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)\cap\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\perp}=\{0\}

\phi(e)

\operatorname{Ker}(T)

∎

.

(i)

(ii)

F

2.
Heisenberg Lie algebras as the nilradical

2.1 Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a quadratic Lie algebra such that
\operatorname{Nil}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{h}_{m}
. Then,

\bullet

\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{g})
is isomorphic to the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
extended by a derivation.

\bullet

\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{g})
is a non-degenerate ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{g}/\operatorname{Nil}(\mathfrak{g})
is reductive, then it is a quadratic Lie algebra. By Theorem
1.6
, there is a vector subspace
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]\subset\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

We may assume that
\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathbb{F}d
, where
[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]_{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\mathcal{S}
. Let us consider the decomposition of the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
of
\mathfrak{g}
given in (
19
). As
\mathfrak{a}
is reductive, it follows that
d
belongs to the center of
\mathfrak{a}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{S}
is a non-degenerate ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}=\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
.

Now we shall prove that
\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{F}d\oplus V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
. Observe that
\mathbb{F}d\oplus V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
is solvable because

[\mathbb{F}d\oplus V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash,\mathbb{F}d\oplus V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash]=\mathfrak{h}_{m}

and
\mathfrak{h}_{m}=\operatorname{Nil}(\mathfrak{g})
is nilpotent. Then,

\operatorname{Nil}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}=\mathbb{F}d\oplus\operatorname{Nil}(\mathfrak{g}).

Whence,

\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Nil}(\mathfrak{g})<\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{g})\leq 1+\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Nil}(\mathfrak{g}).

Therefore,
\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}=\mathbb{F}d\oplus V\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
. By Lemma
1.3
.(iii),
\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{g})
is isomorphic to the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
extended by a derivation.
∎

2.2 Corollary
.

(i)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an indecomposable quadratic Lie algebra. If
\operatorname{Nil}(\mathfrak{g})
is the Heisenberg Lie algebra, then
\mathfrak{g}
is the Heisenberg Lie algebra extended by a derivation.

(ii)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a quadratic solvable Lie algebra. If
\,\operatorname{Nil}(\mathfrak{g})
is the Heisenberg Lie algebra, then
\mathfrak{g}
is the Heisenberg Lie algebra extended by a derivation.

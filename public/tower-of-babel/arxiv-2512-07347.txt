Self-adjoint realization of the harmonic oscillator in polar coordinates and some consequences
Krzysztof Stempak

Abstract
. We consider spectral decomposition of the harmonic oscillator in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} in terms of two different orthonormal bases in L 2 ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) consisting of its eigenfunctions. Then, using purely functional analysis tools we provide simple proofs of rotational symmetry of the Hermite projection operators studied by Kochneff, and Thangavelu’s Hecke-Bochner type identity.

Primary 33C50, 47B25; Secondary 33C55.

1.
Introduction

The harmonic oscillator

H=-\Delta+|x|^{2}

is a model example of an unbounded operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with discrete spectrum, whose spectral theory is completely understood.
Analysis of
H
, which is an important operator in mathematical physics (known as the quantum oscillator), was performed in numerous papers and monographs,
see for instance,
[
8
]
,
[
5
]
or
[
15
]
. Initially considered with domain
{\rm Dom}\,H:=C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the operator
H
is symmetric and nonnegative. Additionally,

H
is essentially self-adjoint (this is a consequence of a general theorem, see
[
8
, Theorem X.28]
), which means that its self-adjoint extension is unique.

The
multi-dimensional Hermite functions

\{h_{\alpha}\colon\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
,

h_{\alpha}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}h_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i}),\qquad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

form an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In addition,
h_{\alpha}
are eigenfunctions of the differential operator
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
,

\big(-\Delta+|x|^{2}\big)h_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}h_{\alpha},\qquad\lambda_{\alpha}=n+2|\alpha|,

where
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}
stands for the length of
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Hence, a well known procedure (see
[
3
, Lemma 1.2.2]
) shows that

\mathcal{H}
defined by

\displaystyle{\rm Dom}\,\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}|\lambda_{\alpha}\langle f,h_{\alpha}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\lambda_{\alpha}\langle f,h_{\alpha}\rangle h_{\alpha},\qquad f\in{\rm Dom}\,\mathcal{H},

is self-adjoint and its spectrum is discrete and equals
\{n+2k\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Since
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset{\rm Dom}\,\mathcal{H}
, it follows that
\mathcal{H}
is a self-adjoint
extension of
H
.

Less known is a different realization of
\mathcal{H}
given in terms of another orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consisting of eigenfunctions of
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
.
See Sommen
[
10
]
or Coulembier et al.
[
2
]
. The basis in question is

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}

L^{2}(X)

X=\mathbb{R}^{n}

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\sigma_{n-1}
will stand

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, to make this note self-contained, we first collect necessary facts on the systems of Laguerre functions and spherical harmonics.
We refer to Section
4
, (D), where the case of spherical harmonics in dimension one is discussed separately.

Let
\beta>-1
. The Laguerre functions

\ell^{\beta}_{k}(r)=\Big(\frac{2\Gamma(k+1)}{\Gamma(k+\beta+1)}\Big)^{1/2}L^{\beta}_{k}(r^{2})e^{-r^{2}/2},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N},\quad r>0,

where
L_{k}^{\beta}
stands for the Laguerre polynomial of order
\beta>-1
and degree
k
(see
[
7
, p.76]
), form an orthonormal basis in

L^{2}\big(r^{2\beta+1}\big):=L^{2}\big((0,\infty),r^{2\beta+1}dr\big)
and satisfy

\Big(-\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}-\frac{2\beta+1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}+r^{2}\Big)\ell^{\beta}_{k}=2(2k+\beta+1)\ell^{\beta}_{k}.

(2.1)

A comprehensive presentation of the theory of spherical harmonics can be found in
[
11
, Chapter IV]
or
[
4
, Chapter 2, H]
. Let
n\geq 1
be fixed.
We apply the convention that if
Y
is a solid harmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then its restriction to
\Sigma_{n-1}
will be denoted
\mathcal{Y}
and called the
spherical harmonic corresponding to
Y
.
H_{(s)}
will stand for the space of solid harmonics homogeneous of degree
s\in\mathbb{N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Thus, if
Y\in H_{(s)}
, then
Y(rx^{\prime})=r^{s}\mathcal{Y}(x^{\prime})
. We shall write
\mathcal{H}_{(s)}
for the space of restrictions of
Y\in H_{(s)}
to
\Sigma_{n-1}
;

\mathcal{H}_{(s)}
is a finite dimensional subspace of the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Sigma_{n-1}):=L^{2}(\Sigma_{n-1},\sigma_{n-1})
.

Let
d_{s}:={\rm dim}\,H_{(s)}={\rm dim}\,\mathcal{H}_{(s)}
. Recall (see the proof of

[
4
, (2.55) Corollary]
with slightly different notation) that
d_{s}={\rm dim}\,P_{s}-{\rm dim}\,P_{s-2}
, where
P_{s}
stands for the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
s
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any orthonormal basis
\mathcal{Y}_{s,1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{s,d_{s}}
of
\mathcal{H}_{(s)}
and
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{n-1}
it holds (see
[
4
, (2.57) Theorem, a) and b)]
)

\sum_{j=1}^{d_{s}}\mathcal{Y}_{s,j}(x^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{s,j}(y^{\prime})}=\mathcal{Z}^{x^{\prime}}_{s}(y^{\prime}).

(2.2)

Here
\mathcal{Z}^{x^{\prime}}_{s}\in\mathcal{H}_{(s)}
is the
zonal harmonic of degree

s

with pole at

x^{\prime}
, which means that for all
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathcal{H}_{(s)}

it holds
\mathcal{Y}(x^{\prime})=\langle\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}^{x^{\prime}}_{s}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{n-1})}
.

The following result is known and the proof is given only for completeness.

Lemma 2.1
.

The system
\{\phi_{k,s,j}\}
is an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and (
1.1
) holds.

Proof.

Using integration in polar coordinates, orthogonality and the normalization of the system easily follows from the fact that for every

s\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{\ell^{\frac{n}{2}-1+s}_{k}\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\{Y_{s,j}\colon s\in\mathbb{N},1\leq j\leq d_{s}\}
are orthonormal systems in
L^{2}(r^{2(\frac{n}{2}-1+s)+1})

and
L^{2}(\Sigma_{n-1})
, respectively. We note that for
n=1
integration in ‘polar coordinates’ takes a special form, see Section
4
, (D); this remark also applies in the remaining part of the proof.

It remains to verify completeness. For this we check that Parseval’s identity

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\sum_{k,s,j}\big|\langle f,\phi_{k,s,j}\rangle\big|^{2},\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(2.3)

is satisfied; this will be a simple consequence of Parseval’s identities for the orthonormal bases
\{\ell^{\beta}_{k}\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and

\{\mathcal{Y}_{s,j}\colon s\in\mathbb{N},1\leq j\leq d_{s}\}
in
L^{2}(r^{2\beta+1})
and
L^{2}(\Sigma_{n-1})
, respectively. Indeed, denoting
f_{r}(x^{\prime})=f(rx^{\prime})
,
x^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{n-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|^{2}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{n-1}}|f(rx^{\prime})|^{2}d\sigma(x^{\prime})r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{s,j}|\langle f_{r},\mathcal{Y}_{s,j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{n-1})}|^{2}r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,j}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\langle f_{r},\mathcal{Y}_{s,j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{n-1})}|^{2}r^{n-1}dr.

With
s
and
j
fixed, consider the function
F_{s,j}(r)=\langle f_{r},\mathcal{Y}_{s,j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{n-1})}
and write

F_{s,j}(r)=r^{s}G_{s,j}(r)\quad{\rm with}\quad G_{s,j}(r)=r^{-s}F_{s,j}(r).

Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\langle f_{r},\mathcal{Y}_{s,j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{n-1})}|^{2}r^{n-1}dr=\int_{0}^{\infty}|F_{s,j}(r)|^{2}r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|G_{s,j}(r)|^{2}r^{2(\frac{n}{2}-1+s)+1}dr

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\big|\langle G_{s,j},\ell_{k}^{\frac{n}{2}-1+s}\rangle_{L^{2}(r^{n-1+2s})}\big|^{2}.

But

\displaystyle\langle G_{s,j},\ell_{k}^{\frac{n}{2}-1+s}\rangle_{L^{2}(r^{n-1+2s})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-s}\int_{\Sigma_{n-1}}f(rx^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{s,j}(x^{\prime})}d\sigma(x^{\prime})\ell_{k}^{\frac{n}{2}-1+2s}(r)r^{n-1+2s}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{n-1}}f(rx^{\prime})\ell_{k}^{\frac{n}{2}-1+2s}(r)r^{s}\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{s,j}(x^{\prime})}d\sigma(x^{\prime})r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle=\langle f,\phi_{k,s,j}\rangle.

Combining the above finally gives (
2.3
).

Proving (
1.1
) we shall use the differential properties of the Laguerre functions
\ell^{\beta}_{k}
, see (
2.1
), and harmonicity and homogeneity of
Y_{s,j}
.
We first note that for
F(x)=f(|x|)
with
f(r):=\ell^{\frac{n}{2}-1+s}_{k}(r)
and
Y(x):=Y_{s,j}(x)
, since
Y
is harmonic, we obtain

-\Delta(FY)=(-\Delta F)Y-2\nabla F\cdot\nabla Y.

But
F
is radial and hence, with
r=|x|
,

\displaystyle(-\Delta+|x|^{2})F(x)

\displaystyle=\Big(-\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}-\frac{n-1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}+r^{2}\Big)f(r)

\displaystyle=\Big(-\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}-\frac{2(\frac{n}{2}-1+s)+1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}+r^{2}\Big)f(r)+\frac{2s}{r}\frac{d}{dr}f(r)

\displaystyle=(n+2(s+2k))f(r)+\frac{2s}{r}\frac{d}{dr}f(r),

where in the last step (
2.1
) was used.
To conclude verification of (
1.1
) we observe that

\frac{s}{r}f^{\prime}(r)Y(x)=\nabla F(x)\cdot\nabla Y(x).

Indeed,
\nabla F(x)=\frac{f^{\prime}(r)}{r}x
and
x\cdot\nabla Y(x)=sY(x)
since
Y
is homogeneous of degree
s
.
∎

3.
Main results

Let
\Pi_{n+2m}
stand for the orthogonal projection operator corresponding to the eigenvalue
n+2m
and associated to
\mathcal{H}
,

\Pi_{n+2m}f=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\langle f,h_{\alpha}\rangle h_{\alpha},\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

so that the spectral decomposition of
\mathcal{H}
is

\mathcal{H}f=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(n+2m)\Pi_{n+2m}f,\qquad f\in{\rm Dom}\,\mathcal{H}.

Clearly, the integral kernel of
\Pi_{n+2m}
is

\Phi_{m}(x,y)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}h_{\alpha}(x)h_{\alpha}(y),\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Analogously, for the parallel realization of
\mathcal{H}
, which we denoted
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
,

\widetilde{\Pi}_{n+2m}f=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\sum_{1\leq j\leq d_{m-2k}}\langle f,\phi_{k,m-2k,j}\rangle\phi_{k,m-2k,j},\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(3.1)

so that

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}f=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(n+2m)\widetilde{\Pi}_{n+2m}f,\qquad f\in{\rm Dom}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{H}},

holds. The integral kernel of
\widetilde{\Pi}_{n+2m}
, expressed in polar coordinates, is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}(rx^{\prime},uy^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\sum_{1\leq j\leq d_{m-2k}}\phi_{k,m-2k,j}(x)\overline{\phi_{k,m-2k,j}(y)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\ell^{\frac{n}{2}-1+m-2k}_{k}(r)\ell^{\frac{n}{2}-1+m-2k}_{k}(u)(ru)^{m-2k}\sum_{1\leq j\leq d_{m-2k}}\mathcal{Y}_{m-2k,j}(x^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{m-2k,j}(y^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\ell^{\frac{n}{2}-1+m-2k}_{k}(r)\ell^{\frac{n}{2}-1+m-2k}_{k}(u)(ru)^{m-2k}\mathcal{Z}^{x^{\prime}}_{m-2k}(y^{\prime}),

where
\mathcal{Z}^{x^{\prime}}_{m-2k}
is the zonal harmonic of degree
m-2k
with pole at
x^{\prime}
; we used (
2.2
) in the last step.

For any self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space its spectral decomposition is uniquely determined, see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 5.7]
. Therefore, since

\mathcal{H}=\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

\Pi_{n+2m}=\widetilde{\Pi}_{n+2m},\qquad m\in\mathbb{N};

(3.2)

notably
\Phi_{m}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}
. An explanation of (
3.2
) based on elementary means is contained in Section
4
, (B).

3.1.
Rotational symmetry of projection operators

This property, to be precise for
g\in SO(n)
only, was proved by Kochneff
[
6
]
and required some effort in the proof.

Theorem 3.1
.

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.4]
)

Let
T_{g}f(x)=f(gx)
for
g\in O(n)
. Then we have

\Pi_{n+2m}\circ T_{g}=T_{g}\circ\Pi_{n+2m}\qquad m\in\mathbb{N},\quad g\in O(n).

(3.3)

But (
3.3
) is just a simple consequence of the spectral theorem. More precisely, if on a Hilbert space a bounded operator
B
commutes with
a self-adjoint operator
S
(for an unbounded
S
this means the inclusion
BS\subset SB
), then
B
commutes with all spectral projections from
the spectral decomposition of
S
. See e.g.
[
9
, Proposition 5.15]
, where the mentioned result is included in a more general setting.

In our framework, since

\mathcal{H}\circ T_{g}=T_{g}\circ\mathcal{H},\qquad g\in O(n),

(3.4)

therefore (
3.3
) holds. The above commutation is naturally expected because
T_{g}
commutes with
H
so it should commute with the self-adjoint
extension of
H
. However, it requires a formal proof which is outlined in Section
4
, (C).

3.2.
Hecke-Bochner type identity for the Hermite projections

This identity was proved by Thangavelu
[
14
]
(an earlier paper
[
13
]
contains its proof only for
n
even). A shorter proof was provided by Kochneff

[
6
]
. In both cases the proofs relied on appropriate evaluations of integrals with Hermite and Laguerre functions involved.

After adjusting the present notation with that in
[
14
]
, the result is as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

(
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
)

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be of the form
f(x)=f_{0}(|x|)Y(x)
, where

is a solid harmonic of homogeneity

K\in\mathbb{N},

\Pi_{n+2(M+2K)}f(x)=\langle f_{0},\ell^{\frac{n}{2}-1+M}_{K}\rangle_{L^{2}(r^{\frac{n}{2}-1+M})}\ell^{\frac{n}{2}-1+M}_{K}(|x|)Y(x),

(3.5)

and
\Pi_{n+2m}f=0
when
m
is not of the form

for some

Again, we refer to Section

3.2
) and note that for

of the form

4.
Appendix

(A) To prove
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset{\rm Dom}\,\,\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
it suffices to check that for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and every
N\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

|\langle\varphi,\phi_{k,s,j}\rangle|\leq C_{\varphi,N}\lambda_{k,s}^{-N},

(4.1)

uniformly in
k,s\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,d_{s}\}
. This reduces to checking that

\langle\varphi,(-\Delta+|x|^{2})\phi_{k,s,j}\rangle=\langle(-\Delta+|x|^{2})\varphi,\phi_{k,s,j}\rangle.

Indeed, since
(-\Delta+|x|^{2})\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can repeat this argument to obtain

\lambda_{k,s}^{N}\langle\varphi,\phi_{k,s,j}\rangle=\langle(-\Delta+|x|^{2})^{N}\varphi,\phi_{k,s,j}\rangle

and then (
4.1
) follows.

Let
{\rm supp}\,\varphi\subset\{|x|\leq R-1\}
for some
R>1
. Obviously, it suffices to verify that

\langle\varphi,(-\Delta)\phi_{k,s,j}\rangle=\langle(-\Delta)\varphi,\phi_{k,s,j}\rangle.

But this follows from Green’s formula, see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem D.9, (D.6), p. 408]
because
\varphi
and
the directional outward normal derivative
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}
, vanish on the boundary of
\{|x|\leq R\}
.

(B) It is certainly pedagogical to deliver a proof of (
3.2
) by elementary means. For this, given
n=1,2,\ldots
, it suffices to check that
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
the projection spaces

{\rm lin}\,\{h_{\alpha}\colon|\alpha|=m\}\quad{\rm and}\quad{\rm lin}\,\{\phi_{k,m-2k,j}\colon 0\leq k\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor,1\leq j\leq d_{m-2k}\}

coincide. This is equivalent to checking that the corresponding spaces of polynomials,

V_{m}={\rm lin}\,\{H_{\alpha}\colon|\alpha|=m\},

and

\widetilde{V}_{m}=\quad{\rm lin}\,\{L_{k}^{\frac{n}{2}-1+m-2k}(|x|^{2})Y_{m-2k,j}(x)\colon 0\leq k\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor,1\leq j\leq d_{m-2k}\},

coincide. Since
V_{m}
and
\widetilde{V}_{m}

A resource-efficient machine learning framework for real-time non-intrusive load monitoring and performance optimization in solar-powered aviation systems
Authors: Aguida Mohammed Echarif, Yanbo Che, El Moundher Aouiche, Abdrabbi Bourezg, Muhammad Zain Yousaf, Wajid Khan, Mohit Bajaj, Oleksandr Rubanenko
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The integration of non-intrusive load monitoring (NILM) into solar-powered aviation systems presents a transformative approach for achieving sustainable, lightweight, and intelligent flight operations. However, the sector’s stringent constraints on weight, latency, and computational resources pose critical challenges to real-time NILM deployment. This study develops a resource-efficient machine learning framework that systematically evaluates six machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) models using high-resolution (200 kHz) power data that capture both transient and steady-state load characteristics across all flight phases. Advanced preprocessing techniques—comprising moving average smoothing and non-overlapping downsampling—were applied to suppress noise while preserving essential features. The comparative analysis reveals that K-Nearest neighbors (KNN) delivers the most effective balance between accuracy and computational cost, achieving an R² of 0.9403 with an execution time of 0.20 s, substantially outperforming ensemble models such as random forest (RF) and XGBoost in real-time feasibility. Conversely, the hybrid CNN-LSTM architecture attained the lowest mean squared error (MSE = 0.0048) and superior temporal sensitivity but required 271.53 s, demonstrating its suitability for offline analysis rather than onboard deployment. Through comprehensive hardware-in-the-loop validation using Opal-RT and Launchpad-F28379D DSP controllers, the framework verified appliance-level disaggregation accuracy under dynamic flight scenarios. The findings underscore a critical accuracy–efficiency trade-off in NILM model selection, establishing that traditional ML algorithms can outperform complex DL models when optimized for real-time, resource-constrained environments. This research provides actionable design insights for next-generation solar-powered aircraft energy management systems, demonstrating that model selection must prioritize computational efficiency, predictive reliability, and real-time responsiveness to enable sustainable and intelligent aviation energy control.

Introduction

Solar-powered aviation represents a significant advancement in eco-friendly flight technology, utilizing solar energy to power aircraft and reduce reliance on fossil fuels. By harnessing solar energy, this approach can significantly lower operational costs, extend flight durations often exceeding 24 h for UAVs and enable sustainable multi-day operations1. However, this rapid expansion has intensified the demand for advanced power management systems capable of balancing variable energy supply and demand under the sector stringent weight and reliability constraints2. A primary challenge lies in managing the highly variable power requirements across different flight phases, which peak during takeoff and stabilize during cruising, while also accurately detecting transient load events3.

A multi-layered strategy has emerged to address these challenges. Fundamentally, photovoltaic (PV) systems are strengthened through modeling and simulation to improve solar energy utilization and overall efficiency4,5. Operationally, performance is enhanced by aligning flight schedules with periods of optimal solar availability a strategy shown to reduce consumption by up to 30.84% and by directing solar power towards climbing and charging during peak sunlight hours3,6. These tactics are integrated through flight-phase-dependent Energy Management Systems (EMS) that optimize allocation across distinct operational phases, achieving outcomes like a 22.9% energy surplus and enabling lighter battery configurations7. This is complemented by optimizing the electrical system design to minimize excess capacity, further reducing aircraft weight, which is a decisive factor for efficiency8. At the system control level, solutions such as the integration of state machine control with droop control have been proposed to manage power allocation and ensure stable voltage in solar UAVs9. Despite these advancements, key challenges persist in scaling these systems for larger aircraft and maintaining efficiency under diverse environmental conditions.

While EMS focuses on macro-level energy allocation, effective load monitoring at the component level is equally crucial for reliable power management. NILM is particularly advantageous in this context, as it estimates individual component consumption from aggregate system data without invasive sensors10. This can reduce wiring requirements by up to 30%, directly supporting the critical goal of weight minimization3. However, adapting NILM to aviation demands advanced time-series analysis to address variable equipment behavior, transient loads, and dynamic operational conditions10,11. This requirement is becoming increasingly critical with the growing complexity of modern hybrid-electric aircraft power systems, which necessitate advanced NILM frameworks to manage diverse energy sources and highly dynamic load profiles12.

The adoption of NILM in aviation faces significant challenges related to computational weight, latency, and reliability. A principal hurdle is developing robust algorithms that maintain accuracy under dynamic operating conditions and with diverse load signatures13. This is particularly acute for DL models, which struggle to adapt to unpredictable environments due to their dependence on large, high-quality datasets and substantial computational resources14. Latency is another critical constraint, as the complex architectures of many NILM models are unsuitable for aviation real-time processing demands. To address these limitations, research has explored several pathways. Lightweight models, such as GraphNILM, utilize graph neural networks to reduce memory usage while maintaining performance, making them more suitable for aviation applications15. The successful deployment of NILM in aviation hinges on overcoming key challenges, including the exploration of promising but underutilized approaches like Federated Learning (FL), which could mitigate latency through decentralized, local data processing16.

ML techniques, particularly robust traditional methods like Random Forest and XGBoost, significantly enhance NILM accuracy for precise energy management in real-world aviation scenarios17,18. RF has demonstrated high effectiveness, achieving an identification accuracy of 96.8% for multiple load states19 and an R-squared value of 0.87 in energy applications20, with performance further enhanced when combined with optimization techniques19. Similarly, XGBoost exhibits strong predictive capability for energy consumption17 and superior performance in short-term load forecasting, achieving a lower mean average percentage error than traditional methods21. Its efficiency in handling large datasets and integrating historical data features makes it highly valuable for predicting load patterns and optimizing resource allocation in complex systems like aviation22,23.

In contrast, KNN is utilized for classifying loads based on active and reactive power characteristics. While it can enable effective energy consumption disaggregation potentially leading to behavioral changes that improve energy efficiency by up to 15%20 and can be adapted to forecast low-voltage loads under decentralized supply conditions24, it is generally less suited than RF or XGBoost for the high-dimensional and dynamic data typical of complex aviation environments.

DL has been applied to NILM to capture time-dependent load variations, which are particularly significant in complex systems such as aviation25. Among DL models, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks are effective in modeling temporal dependencies in sequential data; however, their high computational requirements often limit real-time deployment26,27. To address these limitations, hybrid architectures have emerged. The CNN-LSTM framework, for instance, combines Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for extracting local spatial features with LSTMs for modeling long-term temporal dependencies, achieving strong results such as 93.27% accuracy on benchmark datasets28.

The selection of models in this comparative study was guided by the goal of evaluating a diverse range of algorithms under strict real-time constraints. Accordingly, both tree-based ensemble ML models and a hybrid DL model (CNN-LSTM) were included. The CNN-LSTM was chosen as the representative hybrid approach due to its proven ability to concurrently extract spatial and temporal features from time-series data, making it well-suited for NILM applications28. Although advanced sequence models such as Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and Transformers are also relevant, their investigation is deferred to future work to maintain the focus and scope of this benchmarking study.

Recent advancements in NILM have incorporated sophisticated architectures to improve performance. The integration of attention mechanisms, such as bidirectional LSTM with attention, allows models to focus on critical time steps, thereby refining outputs and enhancing estimation accuracy29. Similarly, the use of multi-head attention to capture complex patterns and Focal Loss to address class imbalance has significantly boosted accuracy and F1-scores on low-frequency data24. More recently, transformer-based models and their hybrid variants have surpassed the performance of LSTMs, GRUs, and CNNs, achieving significant reductions in error metrics. To mitigate the computational demands of these complex models, techniques like structured pruning have been employed, reducing model size and computational load by up to 48.85%30.

Despite these advancements, significant challenges persist. The effectiveness of advanced models is highly dependent on the availability of large volumes of high-quality training data. Furthermore, issues of generalizability across different datasets and the inherent complexity of decomposing multi-state appliance loads remain active research problems31. To address the unresolved challenge of modeling time-dependent appliance usage, the present study evaluates a hybrid CNN-LSTM framework against traditional ML for NILM in solar-powered aviation, leveraging its combined spatial and temporal modeling capabilities. Experiment

The bus power control unit (BPCU) serves as a critical hub within the Aircraft Electrical Power System (EPS), engineered to consolidate heterogeneous power electronics to ensure stable and optimized energy distribution from generation sources. Its architecture incorporates DC/DC converters, DC/AC inverters, transformer rectifier units (TRUs), and energy storage subsystems, alongside advanced load regulation capabilities tailored for aviation operational demands. To address the unique challenges of solar-powered aircraft, a cable weight minimization strategy is proposed, leveraging Remote Power Distribution Units (RPDUs). These RPDUs are strategically positioned and energized via redundant three-phase feeders from the secondary power distribution unit (SPDU), optimizing spatial efficiency while accounting for distributed generation (DG) integration, system resilience, and mission-specific constraints32.

To evaluate and refine this optimization, a sophisticated HIL simulation framework is employed to analyze both the overall active power output and the specific energy consumption patterns of individual appliances. This investigation combines real-time system modeling with physical control mechanisms by integrating Launchpad-F28379D DSP controllers and the advanced simulation capabilities of the Opal-RT simulator software. This setup enables an accurate and detailed examination of the PDS, providing insights into dynamic power demands and real-time load variations during aircraft operations. Figure 1 illustrates the experimental setup, showcasing the integration of the DSP controller with the Opal-RT system for real-time analysis. By replicating the dynamic power demands and load variations encountered during flight, this combined approach supports the development of more efficient and reliable solar-powered aircraft systems.

The HIL environment was configured using the TI Launchpad-F28379D DSP coupled with a real-time simulation platform to emulate solar irradiance and electrical transients. The trained NILM models were executed on the DSP, receiving real-time sensor data streams (irradiance, voltage, current) identical to those in the simulated PV system. The HIL validation demonstrated close alignment between predicted and actual load power traces under dynamic irradiance levels at 0.5, 1.4, and 1.7 s, confirming model reliability and real-time consistency between software training and hardware operation.

The implementation utilized the Texas instruments launchpad-F28379D DSPs, shown in Fig. 2, chosen for their dual-core C28x architecture and ability to support deterministic real-time execution. This platform offers the computational capability necessary for advanced control algorithms, aided by a built-in floating-point unit for efficient handling of complex operations and 150 ps high-resolution PWM modules that enable precise power converter regulation under dynamic flight conditions. System reliability was strengthened through a synchronized time-triggered architecture that employed the enhanced PWM modules as timing masters, while the analog interfaces incorporated anti-aliasing filters tailored to the aircraft’s sensor characteristics.

Model training and evaluation for the intelligent control components were conducted on a desktop workstation (Intel Core i9, 3.8 GHz, 32 GB RAM) using Python 3.10, TensorFlow 2.12, and scikit-learn 1.3. The resulting trained models were deployed to the TI Launchpad-F28379D DSP for inference testing, where they produced consistent outputs with sub-2 ms latency. These results confirm the feasibility of integrating the proposed algorithms into embedded flight control hardware for real-time onboard energy management in solar-powered aviation.

High-resolution power data was collected over a simulated 4-hour flight using a HIL setup operating at a 200 kHz sampling rate. This configuration provided precise synchronization and enabled the capture of transient behaviors essential for detecting state transitions and distinguishing appliances with overlapping power profiles. The analysis examined both transient features associated with mode changes and steady-state variables such as active power, reactive power, current, and voltage. Among these, active power showed the strongest linear correlation with total power consumption and consistently ranked highest in feature importance across tree-based models. Although harmonics and phase angle offered limited discriminative value for some appliances, their overall contribution was marginal and introduced unnecessary complexity. To maintain simplicity and computational efficiency—key requirements for real-time deployment—the final models were developed using active power as the primary feature. Data preprocessing and feature selection

This specific section carefully outlines the advanced preprocessing and feature extraction techniques utilized to comprehensively examine both transient and steady-state behaviors, which play a vital role in enhancing the precision and dependability of power management systems. The thorough process involves several distinct steps, including the critical stages of data collection, preprocessing, and the application of smoothing methods designed to refine the dataset, thereby enabling the efficient training of the NILM model. To accurately capture the complex variations found in active power measurements, the dataset is meticulously collected at a significantly high sampling rate, ensuring that the subtleties of the data are maintained.

High-resolution data acquisition frequently brings forth significant noise and unnecessary variations, which conceal the true performance of the monitored devices. To tackle these obstacles, a preprocessing phase is employed to minimize noise and streamline the dataset, improving its readiness for subsequent analysis and interpretation. This preprocessing encompasses two primary techniques: data smoothing and non-overlapping moving average down sampling, both executed using MATLAB. The smoothing technique mitigates high-frequency noise and anomalies in the raw signal, enabling the fundamental device behavior to become more apparent. The preprocessing pipeline was quantitatively evaluated by measuring its effect on transient preservation through event detection rates. In the raw 200 kHz signal, approximately 98% of appliance state transitions were detected, and after applying the moving average filter and downsampling, the detection rate remained high at 95%. This confirms that the pipeline effectively suppresses high-frequency noise while retaining the transient features required for accurate NILM. To maintain the temporal integrity of the flight data, the dataset was partitioned chronologically for model evaluation: a 90% training and 10% testing split was used for conventional machine learning models, while deep learning models employed an 88% training and 12% testing split after restructuring the sequential data into time-lagged samples without disrupting temporal order.

ML techniques and hyperparameter configuration

This research employs ML models—specifically RF, KNN, and XGBoost to predict energy usage patterns and capture nonlinear relationships in steady-state characteristics. In parallel, advanced DL architectures such as the CNN-LSTM are utilized to model both spatial and temporal dependencies, enabling accurate load disaggregation and detection of transient behaviors in solar-powered aircraft.

Hyperparameter tuning is essential for maximizing the predictive performance of the models. This study used GridSearchCV to conduct exhaustive parameter searches combined with cross-validation (CV)35, targeting the minimization of Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the validation set. A 5-fold cross-validation scheme with negative MSE as the scoring metric was applied to achieve a balanced bias–variance trade-off and ensure strong generalization across different data subsets while keeping computational cost manageable.

The computational characteristics of each model family also shaped their suitability for deployment. KNN requires minimal training but its inference time grows with dataset size. Tree-based ensembles provide fast, stable inference, making them appropriate for real-time systems. In contrast, deep learning models such as CNN-LSTM incur significant computational load during both training and inference, reducing their feasibility for resource-constrained embedded platforms.

RF

The process of hyperparameter optimization in RF regression constitutes an essential component that can markedly improve the predictive efficacy of the model. This process involves optimizing various parameters such as the number of trees, tree depth, and the number of features considered for splitting at each node. Different strategies and methodologies proposed efficiently tune these hyperparameter, each with its own advantages and limitations36.

KNN

Regression necessitates meticulous hyperparameter optimization for enhanced efficacy. Crucial hyperparameter encompass the count of neighbors (k) and the distance metric (DM). Research indicates that the choice of these parameters significantly influences the model accuracy, particularly in relation to dataset characteristics. The optimal configuration for KNN regression influenced by factors such as sample size and the number of attributes, with specific recommendations for effective tuning37.

XGBoost

The implementation of XGBoost regression accompanied by hyperparameter optimization constitutes a robust methodology for augmenting predictive efficacy across diverse fields. Hyperparameter optimization involves fine-tuning key parameters such as learning rate, maximum depth, and subsample to enhance the operational performance of the XGBoost model, which can be achieved using techniques like GridSearchCV and RandomizedSearchCV38.

Linear regression

A fundamental statistical technique, linear regression models the relationship between predictor variables and a response variable by minimizing squared residuals to make predictions based on known data. This method has evolved with the advent of big data, leading to various enhancements and applications in diverse fields, including data science and nursing research39.

Lasso regression

Or least absolute shrinkage and selection operator, is a powerful statistical method used for feature selection and regularization in high-dimensional datasets. Its effectiveness and versatility in reducing overfitting by penalizing coefficients to promote sparsity is demonstrated by successful applications across various domains, including cancer diagnosis and sales prediction40.

LSTM

Networks have emerged as a powerful tool in NILM, enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of energy disaggregation. By leveraging LSTM ability to capture temporal dependencies in time-series data, researchers have developed various models that significantly improve appliance identification and energy consumption forecasting41.

CNN-LSTM

Hybrid architecture was selected for the regression model to effectively handle complex time-series data. This design leverages the complementary strengths of each network, the CNN excels at extracting short-term, local spatial features, while the LSTM network models long-range temporal dependencies. Comparative analysis against GRU and Transformer variants demonstrated that the hybrid model achieved comparable accuracy with significantly lower computational overhead. This efficiency makes it particularly suitable for deployment on DSPs in real-time flight applications, aligning with its proven promise across other demanding fields such as telecommunications, healthcare, and robotics42. Evaluation metrics

Overall model performance and the accuracy-speed trade-off

The primary objective was to identify a model that balances predictive accuracy with computational efficiency for real-time aviation constraints. The results, summarized in Table 4, reveal a clear trade-off. For real-time NILM, KNN emerged as the optimal candidate. It achieved the highest accuracy among the lightweight models (R² = 0.9403) while requiring a minimal execution time of only 0.20 s. While RF maintained comparable accuracy (R² = 0.9240), its ensemble structure incurred a significantly higher computational cost (22.62 s). XGBoost was also more intensive, diminishing its suitability for time-sensitive applications.

The statistical results confirm the robustness of our findings. Specifically, the 95% confidence interval (CI) for KNN (0.938–0.943) does not overlap with those of Random Forest (0.921–0.927) or XGBoost (0.907–0.916). This clear separation provides strong statistical evidence that KNN’s superior performance is consistent and not due to random chance in a single train-test split, thereby substantiating our claim that it is the top-performing model for this specific task.

In stark contrast, the deep learning models demonstrated superior pattern recognition capability, with the hybrid CNN-LSTM achieving the lowest MSE of 0.0048. However, this came at a prohibitive computational cost (271.53 s), rendering them unsuitable for real-time deployment on standard hardware. At the other extreme, linear models were computationally fastest but failed entirely to model the non-linear load patterns, achieving negligible R² values lower than 0.2179.

Conclusion

This research provides a comprehensive evaluation of multiple ML and DL algorithms for NILM in solar-powered aviation, introducing a resource-aware framework that balances predictive accuracy with computational feasibility under stringent aerospace constraints. Leveraging high-resolution (200 kHz) power data and rigorous hyperparameter optimization via GridSearchCV, the study establishes quantitative benchmarks for NILM performance in real-time aviation environments, revealing a clear trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency.

For real-time onboard energy management where latency, weight, and processor capability are critical KNN demonstrated an optimal compromise, achieving an R² of 0.9403 with a minimal execution time of 0.20 s, confirming its suitability for embedded aviation controllers. In contrast, while the hybrid CNN-LSTM deep learning model achieved the lowest MSE (0.0048) by capturing complex temporal patterns and detailed features during offline diagnostic and design-phase tasks, its 271.53-second computational overhead demonstrates that well-optimized traditional machine learning models can match or outperform deep learning in accuracy-to-efficiency trade-offs in resource-constrained settings. HIL validation on Opal-RT and TI Launchpad-F28379D DSP platforms further demonstrated the practical feasibility of the framework, enabling accurate appliance-level disaggregation and robust adaptability to dynamic flight-phase transitions. The KNN model, in particular, achieved a 2 ms inference time on the DSP, reinforcing its suitability for real-time embedded deployment.

Collectively, these findings establish resource-awareness as a guiding principle for NILM in aviation and support a hybrid deployment strategy: lightweight ML models like KNN for onboard real-time operation, complemented by sophisticated DL architectures such as CNN-LSTM for ground-based analysis and system design. Building on this foundation, future work will extend the framework to larger aircraft with complex load profiles, assess scalable approaches such as ensemble models (RF, LightGBM) on diverse embedded platforms, and investigate federated learning for privacy-preserving updates, providing practical design guidelines for computationally efficient, sustainable energy management systems and advancing autonomous, long-endurance solar-powered and next-generation electrified aviation.

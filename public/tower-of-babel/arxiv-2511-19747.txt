Chopping More Finely: Finite Countermodels in Modal Logic via the Subdivision Construction
Tenyo Takahashi

Abstract
We present a new method, the Subdivision Construction, for proving the finite model property (the fmp) for broad classes of modal logics and modal rule systems. The construction builds on the framework of stable canonical rules, and produces a finite modal algebra (finite modal space) that will be a finite countermodel of such rules, yielding the fmp. We apply the Subdivision Construction for proving the fmp for logics and rule systems axiomatized by stable canonical formulas and rules of finite modal algebras of finite height. We also observe that these logics and rule systems are union-splittings in corresponding lattices. As a consequence, we identify a class of union-splittings in 𝖭𝖤𝗑𝗍𝖪𝟦 \mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K4}} with the degree of Kripke incompleteness 1. 1 1 1 This paper is based on [ 31 , Chapter 4] .

Introduction

A normal modal logic
L
has the
finite model property
(the
fmp
, for short) if, for any formula that is not a theorem of
L
, there exists a finite countermodel of
L
that refutes the formula. Algebraically, this is equivalent to saying that the variety of modal algebras validating
L
is generated by its finite members. The fmp is one of the most important and well-studied properties of modal logics (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
and
[
12
]
). The fmp strictly implies Kripke completeness; a Kripke complete logic without the fmp can be found in
[
18
]
). Additionally, a finitely axiomatizable logic with the fmp is decidable
[
20
]
.

General fmp results for classes of modal logics have been thoroughly studied. We refer to
[
12
, Sections 5.8 and 11.8]
for a historical account. Early results include the following. Bull
[
10
]
and Fine
[
16
]
showed that every extension of
\mathsf{S4.3}
has the fmp. Segerberg
[
27
]
proved that every extension of
\mathsf{K4}
of finite depth has the fmp. Later, the interests shifted to identifying classes of logics with specific semantic properties that have the fmp, in other words, to finding sufficient conditions for having the fmp.
Subframe logics
in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K4}}
were defined and studied by Fine
[
14
,
15
]
, and Zakharyaschev
[
34
,
35
]
generalized them to
cofinal subframe logics
; all transitive subframe logics and cofinal subframe logics have the fmp. Moreover, Zakharyaschev
[
36
]
(see also
[
12
, Chapter 11]
) applied two combinatorial methods based on
canonical formulas
, the method of
inserting points
and the method of
removing points
, for proving the fmp for large classes of transitive logics. This, in particular, implies the fmp of extensions of
\mathsf{K4}
with modal reduction principles and the fmp of extensions of
\mathsf{S4}
with a formula in one variable. Recently, Bezhanishvili et al.
[
5
]
introduced
\mathsf{M}
-stable logics
and showed that they have the fmp if the logic
\mathsf{M}

admits filtration in the strong sense
. This assumption can be weakened to that of
\mathsf{M}
admitting
definable filtration

[
30
]
. Another noteworthy fmp result comes from the study of lattices of modal logics. Blok
[
9
]
showed that all
union-splittings
in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K}}
, the lattice of normal modal logics, have the fmp. The fmp is also studied for modal rule systems, or modal consequence relations. It was shown in
[
4
]
that all
stable rule systems
have the fmp.

In this paper, we introduce a novel combinatorial method, which we call the
Subdivision Construction
, to show the fmp for classes of logics and rule systems. The construction is based on the theory of
stable canonical rules
introduced in
[
4
]
. Every formula
\varphi
is semantically equivalent to finitely many stable canonical rules, say,
\{\rho(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):1\leq i\leq n\}
, where each
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
is a finite
L
-algebra and
D_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
. So, if
\varphi\notin L
, then there is an
L
-algebra
\mathfrak{B}
refuting
\varphi
, hence
\mathfrak{B}\not\models\rho(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i})
for some
i
. This, by the semantic characterization of stable canonical rules, means that there is a stable embedding from
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
to
\mathfrak{B}
satisfying the closed domain condition (CDC) for
D_{i}
(the details on these notions can be found in
[
4
]
; we will also introduce them in
Section

2
). Let
\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}
be the dual sapces of
\mathfrak{B}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
respectively. Dually, there is a surjective stable map from
\mathfrak{X}
onto
\mathcal{F}_{i}
satisfying CDC for a set
\mathcal{D}_{i}
of subsets of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. If we can find a finite modal space
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\models L
and there is a surjective map from
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
onto
\mathcal{F}_{i}
satisfying CDC for
\mathcal{D}_{i}
, then the dual algebra of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a finite
L
-algebra refuting
\rho(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i})
and thus refuting
\varphi
. This yeilds the fmp of
L
. In this way, we can reduce a refutation of an arbitrary formula to a refutation of a stable canonical rule, and further transform the question into one involving modal spaces, where combinatorial arguments come in handy. This method is used in
[
7
]
, for example, to provide an alternative proof of the Blok-Esakia theorem.

Now the Subdivision Construction applies to a stable map
f
from
\mathfrak{X}
onto
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, constructing the finite modal space
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
as follows. As
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is finite, the map
f
can be seen as a division of
\mathfrak{X}
into finitely many regions by considering preimages of each point in
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. We make this division finer by subdividing each region into more pieces. This is why we name the construction
Subdivision
. We will carry out this process point by point. For a point
s
in
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, we divide the preimage
f^{-1}(s)
into finitely many subregions, and then replace the point
s
with the corresponding number of points, yielding a finite modal space
\mathcal{F}_{s}^{\prime}
. Additionally, the construction induces a map from
\mathfrak{X}
to
\mathcal{F}_{s}^{\prime}
modifying
f
in sending each subregion to the corresponding point, and a map from
\mathcal{F}_{s}^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{F}_{i}
identifying the new points with
s
. This constitutes one step of the construction.

Stable canonical rules and formulas

We refer to
[
4
,
23
,
26
]
for inference rules and consequence relations in modal logic. We will only address modal multi-conclusion rules and normal modal multi-conclusion consequence relation (or normal modal multi-conclusion rule system), so we simply call them
rules
and
rule systems
. For a set
\mathcal{R}
of rules, let
\mathcal{S}_{0}+\mathcal{R}
be the least rule system containing
\mathcal{S}_{0}\cup\mathcal{R}
. For a logic
L
, let
\mathcal{S}_{L}
be the least rule system containing
L
. When
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{0}+\mathcal{R}
, we say that
\mathcal{S}
is

by
\mathcal{R}
over
\mathcal{S}_{0}

\mathcal{S}_{L}
is axiomatized by
\mathcal{R}
over
\mathcal{S}_{L_{0}}
, we say that
L
is axiomatized by
\mathcal{R}
over
L_{0}
. we say that
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are
equivalent
over
\mathcal{S}
if
\mathcal{S}+\rho=\mathcal{S}+\rho^{\prime}
. Each rule corresponds to a universal sentence, and each rule system to a universal class. We write
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})\coloneq\{\mathfrak{A}:\mathfrak{A}\models\mathcal{S}\}
for a rule system
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{U})\coloneq\{\rho:\mathcal{U}\models\rho\}
for a universal class
\mathcal{U}
. For a rule system
\mathcal{S}
, let
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
denote the lattice of normal extensions of
\mathcal{S}
. The notion of fmp applies to rule systems as well: a rule system
\mathcal{S}
has the fmp if the universal class
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})
is generated by its finite members. That is, for any rule
\rho\notin\mathcal{S}
, there is a finite
\mathcal{S}
-algebra (or, equivalently, a finite
\mathcal{S}
-space) that refutes
\rho
.

x\in\mathfrak{X}
such that
\mathrm{rank}(x)>|X|
. Then there is a path of length
>|X|
(starting at
x
) in
\mathfrak{X}
. Such a path must contain two identical points, which implies that
\mathfrak{X}
has a cycle. This shows
(1)\Rightarrow(3)
.
∎

The idea of the Subdivision Construction is as follows. Suppose that we want to show the fmp of a logic
L
. Let
\varphi\notin L
and
\mathfrak{X}
be an
L
-space that refutes
\varphi
. According to the theory of stable canonical rules, we may assume
\varphi
to be a stable canonical rule
\rho(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{D})
, and thus there is a stable map
f:\mathfrak{X}\twoheadrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{F}
. Using the information of
f
, we construct a finite modal space
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
out of
\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
respects more the structure of
\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{F}
. This is done by dividing the preimage of
f
of points in
\mathcal{F}
in an appropriate way, which is why we name the construction
Subdivision
. The output
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
will be a finite
L
-space refuting
\varphi
. Before introducing the construction in its full details, we present a simple example that illustrates how the construction works and the intuition behind it.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
be the following non-transitive modal space.

\bullet\>a
\bullet\>b
\bullet\>c

Let
\mathfrak{X}=(X,R)
be a modal space and
f_{1}:\mathfrak{X}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}_{1}
be a stable map. Then, all points in
f_{1}^{-1}(c)
are dead ends. A point in
f_{1}^{-1}(b)
can either see some point in
f_{1}^{-1}(c)
or be a dead end. However, this distinction is lost when applying
f_{1}
. We recover this information by letting
f_{1}
factor through the following modal space
\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

\bullet\>a
\bullet\>b_{1}
\bullet\>b_{2}
\bullet\>c

Let
f_{2}:\mathfrak{X}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}_{2}
be the stable map behaving the same way as
f_{1}
except for that
f_{2}
sends points in
f_{1}^{-1}(b)
that see some point in
f_{1}^{-1}(c)
to
b_{1}
and that are dead ends to
b_{2}
. One can verify that
f_{2}
is continuous, surjective (assuming that in
f_{1}^{-1}(b)
there are points that see some point in
f_{1}^{-1}(c)
and that are dead ends), and stable. Also, there is a canonical stable map
g_{2}:\mathcal{F}_{2}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}_{1}
, identifying
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
as
b
. Indeed, we have
f_{1}=g_{2}\circ f_{2}
. Moreover, all points in
f_{2}^{-1}(b_{1})
see some points in
f_{2}^{-1}(c)
and all points in
f_{2}^{-1}(b_{2})
are dead ends. In this sense, we recover the information that was lost when applying
f_{1}
directly.

Next, we proceed with
f_{2}^{-1}(a)
in a similar manner. In principal, there are eight possibilities for points in
f_{2}^{-1}(a)
, depending on whether they see some point in
f_{2}^{-1}(c)
,
f_{2}^{-1}(b_{1})
, and
f_{2}^{-1}(b_{2})
. But since
f_{2}
is stable, a point in
f_{2}^{-1}(a)
cannot see any point in
f_{2}^{-1}(c)
as
a
does not see
c
. So, we may consider the following modal space
\mathcal{F}_{3}
and construct a similar factorization of
f_{2}
as
g_{3}\circ f_{3}
.

\bullet\>a_{1}
\bullet\>a_{2}
\bullet\>a_{3}
\bullet\>a_{4}
\bullet\>b_{1}
\bullet\>b_{2}
\bullet\>c

The finite modal space
\mathcal{F}_{3}
and the maps
f_{3}:\mathfrak{X}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}_{3}
and
g_{3}:\mathcal{F}_{3}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}_{1}
are the results of the Subdivision Construction applied to the map
f_{1}:\mathfrak{X}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}_{1}
. One can verify from the construction that
f_{3}
is in fact a p-morphism. So,
\mathcal{F}_{3}
respects the structure of
\mathfrak{X}
more than
\mathcal{F}_{1}
does.

There may be two concerns about this example of the Subdivision Construction. First, not all possibilities are realized in a preimage. For example, there might be no dead end in
f_{2}^{-1}(a)
, so
f_{3}
might not be surjective. This issue can be overcome by adding points only when the corresponding possibility is realized in the preimage. One may also wonder what happens if we take the closed domain
\mathcal{D}
into account. As we will see in the following, the CDC works perfectly with this construction.

Moreover, it should be clear from this example that the Subdivision Construction only works for the cycle-free part, in particular, the irreflexive part, of a modal space. If
a
is reflexive, then points in
f_{2}^{-1}(a)
can see each other, and there is no appropriate way to define the relation in
\mathcal{F}_{3}
on
\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\}
. This concludes our example.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}=(X,R)
and
\mathfrak{Y}=(Y,Q)
be modal spaces and

be a stable map.

1.

For a point

, we call

a p-morphism for

such that

, then there exists some

and

.

2.

For a subset

, we call

a p-morphism for

is a p-morphism for
x
for all

.

A stable map

is a p-morphism for

iff it is a p-morphism in the usual sense.

4
Finite model property via the Subdivision Construction

In this section, we demonstrate how to use the Subdivision Construction (
Lemma

3.6
) to prove the fmp for logics and rule systems. In general, the Subdivision Construction does not preserve properties of modal spaces due to its combinatorial nature. But when it does, it will produce a finite countermodel that is useful for showing the fmp.

Definition 4.1
.

1.

Let
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be rule systems such that
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. We say that the Subdivision Construction
preserves
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
over
\mathcal{S}
if, whenever
\mathfrak{X}\models\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}\models\mathcal{S}
, the modal sapce
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
obtained in
Lemma

3.6
validates
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.

2.

Let
L
and
L^{\prime}
be logics such that
L\subseteq L^{\prime}
. We say that the Subdivision Construction
preserves
L^{\prime}
over
L
if, whenever
\mathfrak{X}
is topo-rooted (i.e., the dual algebra is s.i.),
\mathfrak{X}\models L^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{F}\models L
, the modal space
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
obtained in
Lemma

3.6
validates
L^{\prime}
.

We will prove the fmp for logics and rule systems of the following form; it turns out that they are union-splittings in the corresponding lattices.

•

A stable rule system + stable canonical rules of finite modal algebras of finite height,

•

A strictly stable logic + stable canonical formulas of finite s.i. modal algebras of finite height,

•

A
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable logic + stable canonical formulas of finite s.i.
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebra of finite height.

The fmp of these logics and rule systems is proved via the following general scheme.

Theorem 4.2
.

1.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a rule system that admits definable filtration, and

. Since

is stable, by the dual of

, we have
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\models\mathcal{S}
.

.

Corollary 4.8
.

Every stable rule system has the fmp.

Proof.

This follows from
Theorem

4.6
by taking
I=\emptyset
.
∎

A natural question is what these rule systems in
Theorem

4.6
are. It turns out that they form a lower part of the union-splittings of the lattice
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
and have a neat characterization. In particular, we observe that varying the closed domain condition and using finite modal algebras outside
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})
do not add more axiomatization power, as long as the algebras are of finite height. Recall that
\mathcal{FH}
is the class of modal algebras of finite height, and Jankov rules are stable canonical rules of the form
\rho(\mathfrak{A},A)
. The rule systems mentioned in
Theorem

4.6
are precisely those satisfying the condition (4) in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a stable rule system. For any rule system
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
, the following are equivalent:

1.

\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{FH}
,

2.

\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathrm{fin}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{FH}
,

3.

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is axiomatized over
\mathcal{S}
by Jankov rules of finite
\mathcal{S}
-algebras of finite height,

4.

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is axiomatized over
\mathcal{S}
by stable canonical rules of finite modal algebras of finite height,

5.

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}+\{\rho(\mathfrak{A},A):\mathfrak{A}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})_{\mathrm{fin}}\cap\mathcal{FH}\}
.

Moreover, any rule system
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
satisfying (one of) the above conditions is a union-splitting in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a stable rule system and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
. We first show the equivalence of the first four items.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: This is clear.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: Suppose that
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathrm{fin}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{FH}
. Let

\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{S}+\{\rho(\mathfrak{A},A):\mathfrak{A}\text{ is a finite $\mathcal{S}$-algebra such that }\mathfrak{A}\not\models\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\}.

Since
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathrm{fin}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{FH}
, a finite
\mathcal{S}
-algebra refuting
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
must be must be of finite height, so
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
is axiomatized over
\mathcal{S}
by Jankov rules of finite modal algebras of finite height, thus
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
has the fmp by
Theorem

4.6
. It suffices to show that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
.

If
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\not\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
, then by the fmp of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
, there is a finite
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\mathfrak{A}\not\models\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, which contradicts the definition of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
since
\mathfrak{A}\not\models\rho(\mathfrak{A},A)
. So,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
. Convsersely, if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}\not\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, then there is an
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
-algebra
\mathfrak{B}
such that
\mathfrak{B}\not\models\rho(\mathfrak{A},A)
for some finite
\mathcal{S}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\mathfrak{A}\not\models\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. So,
\mathfrak{A}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{B}
, and since
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})
is closed under subalgebras,
\mathfrak{A}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
, which contradicts
\mathfrak{A}\not\models\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. So,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, and therefore
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
.

(3)\Rightarrow(4)
: This is clear.

(4)\Rightarrow(1)
: Let
\mathfrak{B}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})
such that
\mathfrak{B}
is not of finite height. For any finite modal algebra
\mathfrak{A}
of finite height, since being of finite height reflects stable subalgebras (
Lemma

4.4
),
\mathfrak{A}\not\hookrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathfrak{B}
, so
\mathfrak{B}\models\rho(\mathfrak{A},D)
for any
D\subseteq A
. So, if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is axiomatized over
\mathcal{S}
by Jankov rules of finite modal algebras of finite height, then
\mathfrak{B}\models\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Thus,
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{FH}
.

Next, we show that
(3)\Rightarrow(5)
and
(5)\Rightarrow(2)
, and thus completing the proof.

(3)\Rightarrow(5)
: This is clear.

(5)\Rightarrow(2)
: Let
\mathfrak{B}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathrm{fin}}
such that
\mathfrak{B}
is not of finite height. By the a similar argument as in the case
(4)\Rightarrow(1)
, it follows that
\mathfrak{B}\models\mathcal{S}+\{\rho(\mathfrak{A},A):\mathfrak{A}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})_{\mathrm{fin}}\cap\mathcal{FH}\}
, so
\mathfrak{B}\models\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
by the assumption (5). Thus,
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathrm{fin}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{FH}
.

Finally, as stable rule systems have the fmp, it is a straightforward generalization of
Theorem

2.17
(see also
[
4
, Theorem 7.10]
) that,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a union-splitting of
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
iff
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is axiomatizable over
\mathcal{S}
by Jankov rules. So, the condition (3) implies being a union-splitting. Thus, any rule system
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
satisfying (one of) the conditions (1)-(5) is a union-splitting in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
.
∎

The subclass of the union-splittings of
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
in question is a lower part of the class of all union-splittings of
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
because of item (5) in
Theorem

4.9
.

Remark 4.10
.

For a stable rule system
\mathcal{S}
, it also follows from
Theorem

4.9
that this lower part of the union-splittings of
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
is decidable. More specifically, this means that there is an algorithm that, given finitely many rules
\rho_{0},\dots,\rho_{n}
, decides whether the rule system
\mathcal{S}+\rho_{0}+\dots+\rho_{n}
falls in this class. The item (3) implies that the decision problem is recursively enumerable, and the item (2) implies that the complement is recursively enumerable. A similar idea is used in
[
29
]
to prove the decidability of being a union-splitting logic in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K}}
.

It is unknown if all union-splittings in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
have the fmp for a stable rule system
\mathcal{S}
. This is expected since all finite algebras split
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathcal{S}}
, which is similar to the case of
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K4}}
and contrary to the case of
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K}}
.

Union-splittings of the lattice of extensions of strictly stable logics

Next, we turn to logics. Our first application is intended to generalize Blok’s fmp result that every union-splitting in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K}}
has the fmp. We start with a simple observation on
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-spaces. This is also helpful in the current case because the dual space of a modal algebra of finite height, in particular, a finite cycle-free modal space, is a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}

m\in\omega

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-space. A point
x\in\mathfrak{X}
is a topo-root iff it is a root.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{X}
is a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-space, we have
R^{<\omega}[x]=R^{\leq m}[x]
, which is closed. So, the closure of
R^{<\omega}[x]
coincides with itself, hence
x
is a topo-root iff
x
is a root.
∎

Note that this does not imply that being topo-rooted and being rooted are equivalent for

Unlike the other two cases, we cannot deal with all stable logics because, in general, their characterization is not as strong as that of stable rule systems or
\mathsf{K4}
-stable logics. If
L
is a
\mathsf{K4}
-stable logic, then the class
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
is finitely
\mathsf{K4}
-stable
[
5
]
, that is, it is closed under finite stable
\mathsf{K4}
-subalgebras. This fact generalizes to
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable logics (
Lemma

4.18
). However, for a stable logic
L
, the class
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
may not be closed under finite stable subalgebras.

Definition 4.13
.

A logic
L
is
strictly stable
if the class
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
is finitely stable, that is, it is closed under finite stable subalgebras.

Examples of striclty stable logics include
\mathsf{K}
,
\mathsf{KD}
, and
\mathsf{KT}
.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
L
be a strictly stable logic. If
\{\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}
is a set of stable canonical formulas (so that each
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
is a finite s.i. modal algebra of finite height and
D_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
), then the logic

L+\{\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}

is preserved by the Subdivision Construction over
\mathsf{K}
.

Proof.

Let
L^{\prime}=L+\{\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}
. Let
\mathfrak{X}=(X,R)
be a topo-rooted
L^{\prime}
-space,
\mathcal{F}
be a finite modal space,
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(F)
, and

be a stable map. Applying the Subdivision Construction (
Lemma

3.6
), we obtain a finite modal space

and a stable map

such that
f^{\prime}
is a p-morphism for
x\in\mathfrak{X}
with
\mathrm{rank}(f^{\prime}(x))<\omega
. Since
L
is strictly stable, the class
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
is finitely stable. The dual algebra of
\mathfrak{X}
is in
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
because
\mathfrak{X}
is topo-rooted and
\mathfrak{X}\models L
. So, the dual algebra of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is in
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
by the dual of
\mathfrak{X}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, hence
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\models L
.

Suppose for a contradiction that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\not\models\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i})
for some

. Let
\mathcal{F}_{i}
be the dual space of
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
and

. Then, by the dual of

2.15
, there is a (topo-)rooted closed upset

such that

. Since

. Let

. Let

. Then
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
is a closed upset of
\mathfrak{X}

is a root of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. Moreover, since every point in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}

, so

, and it follows that
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
is topo-rooted.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
L
be a strictly stable logic. If
\{\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}
is a set of stable canonical formulas, then the logic

L+\{\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}

has the fmp.

Proof.

This follows from
Theorem

4.2
with

4.14
and the fact that
\mathsf{K}
admits (definable) filtration.
∎

This immediately implies the following fmp result by Blok
[

]
.

Corollary 4.16
.

Every union-splitting in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K}}
has the fmp.

Proof.

\mathsf{K}
is clearly a strictly stable logic. Thus, the statement follows from
Theorems

2.20
and
4.15
.
∎

Similar to the case of rule systems, we can show that, for a strictly stable logic
L
, varying the closed domain condition and using finite s.i. modal algebras outside
\mathcal{V}(L)
do not add more axiomatization power, as long as the algebras are of finite height. The logics mentioned in
Theorem

4.15
are precisely those satisfying the condition (2) in the following theorem, and they form a subclass of the union-splittings of the lattice
\mathsf{NExt}{L}
. Here, Jankov formulas are stable canonical formulas of the form
\epsilon(\mathfrak{A},A)
.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
L
be a strictly stable logic. For any logic
L^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{L}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

L^{\prime}
is axiomatized over
L
by Jankov formulas of finite s.i.
L
-algebras of finite height,

(2)

L^{\prime}
is axiomatized over
L
by stable canonical formulas of finite s.i. modal algebras of finite height.

Moreover, any logic
L^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{L}
satisfying (one of) the above conditions is a union-splitting in
\mathsf{NExt}{L}
.

Proof.

The direction
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
is clear. For the converse, let

L^{\prime}=L+\{\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}

where each
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
is a finite s.i. modal algebra of finite height. Let

\displaystyle L^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=L+\{\epsilon(\mathfrak{A},A):\mathfrak{A}\text{ is a finite s.i.~$L$-algebra of finite height such that }

\displaystyle\text{\hskip 250.0pt}\mathfrak{A}_{i}\hookrightarrow_{D_{i}}\mathfrak{A}\text{ for some $i\in I$}\}.

It suffices to show that
L^{\prime}=L^{\prime\prime}
. Let
\mathfrak{B}
be an
L
-algebra and
\mathfrak{X}
be its dual space.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{B}\not\models L^{\prime\prime}
. Then
\mathfrak{B}\not\models\epsilon(\mathfrak{A},A)
for some finite s.i.
L
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
of finite height such that
\mathfrak{A}_{i}\hookrightarrow_{D_{i}}\mathfrak{A}
. By
Theorem

2.15
,
\mathfrak{A}
is a subalgebra of a s.i. homomorphic image
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{B}
. Since
\mathfrak{A}_{i}\hookrightarrow_{D_{i}}\mathfrak{A}
, we have
\mathfrak{A}\hookrightarrow_{D}\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
by composition, which implies
\mathfrak{B}\not\models\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i})
, hence
\mathfrak{B}\not\models L^{\prime}
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathfrak{B}\not\models L^{\prime}
. Then
\mathfrak{B}\not\models\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i})
for some
i\in I
, and
\mathfrak{A}_{i}\hookrightarrow_{D_{i}}\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
for a s.i. homomorphic image
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{B}
by
Theorem

2.15
. Let
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}
be the dual space of
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
respectively, and
\mathcal{D}_{i}=\sigma[D_{i}]
. Then
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
is a topo-rooted closed upset of
\mathfrak{X}
and there is a stable map
f:\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\mathcal{F}_{i}
. Since
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
is of finite height,
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
is so by
Lemma

4.4
, so
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}\models\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
for some
m\in\omega
. Since
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
is topo-rooted,
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
has a topo-root, which is also a root by
Lemma

4.11
, so
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
is rooted. Applying the Subdivision Construction (
Lemma

3.6
) to
f:\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\mathcal{F}_{i}
, we obtain a finite modal space
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, a stable map
f^{\prime}:\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
such that
f^{\prime}
is a p-morphism for
x\in\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
with
\mathrm{rank}(f^{\prime}(x))<\omega
, and a stable map
g:\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\mathcal{F}_{i}
such that
f=g\circ f^{\prime}
. Let
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
be the dual algebra of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Then
\mathfrak{A}_{i}\hookrightarrow_{D_{i}}\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
is of finite height,
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
is so by
Lemma

4.4
. So,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is cycle-free by
Proposition

4.3
. Thus,
f^{\prime}:\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a p-morphism. Since
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
is rooted and
f^{\prime}
is surjective and stable,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is also rooted. Also, dually, we have that
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
is a homomorphic image of
\mathfrak{B}
and
\mathfrak{B}\models L
, we obtain
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}\models L
. Then,
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
is a finite s.i.
L
-algebra of finite height such that
\mathfrak{A}_{i}\hookrightarrow_{D_{i}}\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
. Thus,
\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}^{\prime},A^{\prime})\in L^{\prime\prime}
by definition. Moreover, since
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{B}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
, we have
\mathfrak{B}\not\models\epsilon(\mathfrak{A}^{\prime},A^{\prime})
by
Theorem

2.15
. So,
\mathfrak{B}\not\models L^{\prime\prime}
, and therefore,
L^{\prime}=L^{\prime\prime}
.

Finally, we show that if
\mathfrak{A}
is a finite s.i.
L
-algebra (of finite height), then
(L+\epsilon(\mathfrak{A},A),\mathsf{Log}(\mathfrak{A}))
is a splitting pair in
\mathsf{NExt}{L}
. This implies that a logic
L^{\prime}
satisfying (1) is a union-splitting in
\mathsf{NExt}{L}
. For any logic
L^{\prime\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{L}
such that
L^{\prime}\not\subseteq L^{\prime\prime}
, there is a modal algebra
\mathfrak{B}
such that
\mathfrak{B}\models L^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathfrak{B}\not\models\epsilon(\mathfrak{A},A)
. So,
\mathfrak{A}
is a sbualgebra of a s.i. homomorphic image of
\mathfrak{B}
, which implies
L^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathsf{Log}(\mathfrak{B})\subseteq\mathsf{Log}(\mathfrak{A})
. Thus,
(L+\epsilon(\mathfrak{A},A),\mathsf{Log}(\mathfrak{A}))
is a splitting pair in
\mathsf{NExt}{L}
.
∎

If
L=\mathsf{K}

\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K}}
by
Theorem

2.20

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
m\geq 1
. If
L
is a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable logic, then the class
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
is finitely
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable.

Proof.

Let
L
be a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable logic. Then
\mathcal{V}(L)
is generated by a stable class
\mathsf{K}
of
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebras. We first show that
\mathcal{V}(L)
is generated by
\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
. It is clear that
\mathcal{V}(\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}})\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathsf{K})=\mathcal{V}(L)
. For the converse, let
\varphi\notin L
. Then
\mathfrak{D}\not\models\varphi
for some
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathsf{K}
. Since
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
adimits definable filtration
[
17
]
(see also
[
30
]
), there is a definable filtration
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{D}
such that
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}\models\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}\not\models\varphi
. Then,
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
since
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
is finite and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathsf{K}
is stable. So,
\mathcal{V}(\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}})\not\models\varphi
. Thus,
\mathcal{V}(\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}})=\mathcal{V}(L)
. Note that
\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is also stable.

Let
\mathfrak{B}\in\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
and
\mathfrak{A}
be a finite
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebra such that
\mathfrak{A}\hookrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathfrak{B}
. Let
\mathfrak{X}=(X,R)
and
\mathcal{F}=(F,Q)
be the dual space of
\mathfrak{B}
and
\mathfrak{A}
respectively. Then, there is a stable map
f:\mathfrak{X}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}
. Since
\mathfrak{B}
is s.i.,
\mathfrak{X}
is topo-rooted. Let
x_{0}
be a topo-root of
\mathfrak{X}
. If
s\notin Q^{<\omega}[f(x_{0})]
for some
s\in\mathcal{F}
, then
f^{-1}(s)
is a non-empty clopen subset of
\mathfrak{X}
such that
f^{-1}(s)\cap R^{<\omega}[x_{0}]=\emptyset
, so the closure of
R^{<\omega}[x_{0}]
is contained in
\mathfrak{X}{\setminus}f^{-1}(s)
, which contradicts
x_{0}
being a topo-root of
\mathfrak{X}
. Thus,
s\in Q^{<\omega}[f(x_{0})]
for all
s\in\mathcal{F}
, namely,
f(x_{0})
is a root of
\mathcal{F}
. So,
\mathcal{F}
is rooted, and
\mathfrak{A}
is s.i. since it is finite.

Since
\mathfrak{A}\hookrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathfrak{B}
and
\mathfrak{B}
is s.i.,
\mathfrak{B}\not\models\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A},\emptyset)
by
Theorem

2.12
, so
\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A},\emptyset)\notin L
by
\mathfrak{B}\models L
. Then, since
\mathcal{V}(L)
is generated by
\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
, there is some
\mathcal{C}\in\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
such that
\mathcal{C}\not\models\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A},\emptyset)
. So, there is some s.i. homomorphic image
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathfrak{A}\hookrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
by
Theorem

2.12
. Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be the dual space of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
respectively. Then,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is a closed upset of
\mathcal{G}
and there is a stable map
f:\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}
. If
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}
, then
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}
, so
\mathfrak{A}\in\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
since
\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is stable, and hence
\mathfrak{A}\in\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\neq\mathcal{G}
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{A}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be the modal space obtained by adding a fresh point
s_{0}
to
\mathcal{F}
such that
s_{0}
sees every point in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. It is easy to verify that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-space and
\mathcal{F}
is a closed upset of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. We define
f^{\prime}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
by
f^{\prime}(t)=f(t)
if
t\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime}(t)=s_{0}
otherwise. Then,
f^{\prime}
is surjective as
f
is so and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\neq\mathcal{G}
. Moreover,
f^{\prime}
is stable because
f
is so and
s_{0}
sees every point in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Thus, since a map between two finite modal spaces is always continuous, we have
f^{\prime}:\mathcal{G}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Let
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
be the dual algebra of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Then,
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
is a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebra such that
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{C}
, and
\mathfrak{A}
is a homomorphic image of
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
. This implies
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
and
\mathfrak{A}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{fin}})=\mathcal{V}(L)
. So, we conclude that the class
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
is finitely
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable.
∎

Lemma 4.19
.

Let
m\geq 1
and
L
be a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable logic. If
\{\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}
is a set of stable canonical formulas where each
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
is a finite s.i.
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebra of finite height, then the logic

L+\{\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}

is preserved by the Subdivision Construction over
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
.

Proof.

Let
L^{\prime}=L+\{\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}
. Let
\mathfrak{X}=(X,R)
be a topo-rooted
L^{\prime}
-space and
\mathcal{F}=(F,Q)
be a finite
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-space. Applying the Subdivision Construction (
Lemma

3.6
), we obtain a finite modal space
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=(F^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
, a stable map
f^{\prime}:\mathfrak{X}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
such that
f^{\prime}
is a p-morphism for
x\in\mathfrak{X}
with
\mathrm{rank}(f^{\prime}(x))<\omega
, and a stable map
g:\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{F}
such that
g
is the identity between the rank
\omega
part of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and that of
\mathcal{F}
, and
f=g\circ f^{\prime}
.

Claim 4.20
.

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\models\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
.

Proof.

Let
s_{0},\dots,s_{m+1}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be such that
s_{i}Q^{\prime}s_{i+1}
for each
0\leq i\leq m
. We show that
s_{0}Q^{\prime}s_{m+1}
. We divide cases by
\mathrm{rank}(s_{0})
.

If
\mathrm{rank}(s_{0})<\omega
, then
\mathrm{rank}(s_{i})<\omega
for all
0\leq i\leq m+1
. Since
f^{\prime}:\mathfrak{X}\twoheadrightarrow_{\emptyset}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is surjective and is a p-morphism for
x\in\mathfrak{X}
with
\mathrm{rank}(f^{\prime}(x))<\omega
, we obtain a chain
x_{0}R\cdots Rx_{m+1}
in
\mathfrak{X}
such that
f^{\prime}(x_{i})=s_{i}
for each
0\leq i\leq m+1
. Then
x_{0}Rx_{m+1}
since
\mathfrak{X}\models\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
by assumption, which implies
s_{0}Q^{\prime}s_{m+1}
since
f^{\prime}
is stable.

Suppose that
\mathrm{rank}(s_{0})=\omega
. Since
g:\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{F}
is stable, we have
g(s_{0})Q\cdots Qg(s_{m+1})
in
\mathcal{F}
. Then
g(s_{0})Qg(s_{m+1})
since
\mathcal{F}\models\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
by assumption. Also, since
\mathrm{rank}(s_{0})=\omega
, we have
\mathrm{rank}(g(s_{0}))=\omega
by
Lemma

3.2
. Then, it follows from the Subdivision Construction that
s_{0}
remains the same as
g(s_{0})
through the construction, and one of the following cases happens:

(1)

If
\mathrm{rank}(g(s_{m+1}))=\omega
, then
s_{m+1}
remains the same as
g(s_{m+1})
through the contruction, so
s_{0}Q^{\prime}s_{m+1}
since
g(s_{0})Q^{\prime}g(s_{m+1})
.

(2)

If
\mathrm{rank}(g(s_{m+1}))<\omega
, then
s_{m}
is added with
g(s_{m})
replaced in the steps of the construction, and we must have
s_{0}Q^{\prime}s_{m+1}
by
g(s_{0})Qg(s_{m+1})
.

Thus, in both cases we have
s_{0}Q^{\prime}s_{m+1}
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\models\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
.
∎

The class
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
is finitely
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable by
Lemma

4.18
. Since the dual algebra of
\mathfrak{X}
is in
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
and the dual algebra of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a finite
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebra by
4.20
, the dual algebra of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is in
\mathcal{V}(L)_{\mathrm{si}}
. In particular, we have
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\models L
.

Suppose for a contradiction that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\not\models\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i})
for some
i\in I
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{i}
be the dual space of
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
and
\mathcal{D}_{i}=\sigma[D_{i}]
. Then, by the dual of
Theorem

2.12
, there is a topo-rooted closed upset
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\twoheadrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\mathcal{F}_{i}
. A similar argument as in the proof of
Lemma

4.14
yields a topo-rooted closed upset
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{X}
such that
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\mathcal{F}_{i}
. By the dual of
Theorem

2.12
, this means
\mathfrak{X}\not\models\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i})
, which contradicts
\mathfrak{X}\models L^{\prime}
. Thus,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\models\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i})
for all
i\in I
, and therefore
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\models L^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem 4.21
.

Let
m\geq 1
and
L
be a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable logic. If
\{\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}
is a set of stable canonical formulas where each
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
is a finite s.i.
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebra of finite height, then the logic

L+\{\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A}_{i},D_{i}):i\in I\}

has the fmp.

Proof.

This follows from
Theorem

4.2
with
Lemma

4.19
and the fact that
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
admits definable filtration
[
17
]
(see also
[
30
]
).
∎

Similar to the previous two cases, we again obtain the result that, for a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable logic
L
, varying the closed domain condition and using finite s.i.
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebras outside
\mathcal{V}(L)
does not add more axiomatization power, as long as the algebras are of finite height. The logics mentioned in
Theorem

4.21
are precisely those satisfying the condition (2) in the following theorem, and they form a subclass of the union-splittings of the lattice
\mathsf{NExt}{L}
. Here, Jankov formulas are stable canonical formulas of the form
\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A},A)
.

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
m\geq 1
and
L
be a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable logic. For any logic
L^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{L}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

L^{\prime}
is axiomatized over
L
by Jankov formulas of finite s.i.
L
-algebras of finite height,

(2)

L^{\prime}
is axiomatized over
L
by stable canonical formulas of finite s.i.
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebras of finite height.

Moreover, any logic
L^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{L}
satisfying (one of) the above conditions is a union-splitting in
\mathsf{NExt}{L}
.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a finite s.i.
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebra of finite height, it follows from
Theorem

2.12
and
Theorem

2.15
that Jankov formulas as a special type of stable canonical formulas of the form
\gamma^{m}(\mathfrak{A},A)
and Jankov formulas as a special type of stable canonical formulas of the form
\epsilon(\mathfrak{A},A)
have the same semantic characterization for
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebras. Moreover, as
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable logics have the fmp
[
30
]
, it is a straightforward generalization of
Theorem

2.18
that, for a
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-stable logic
L
and a logic
L^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{L}
,
L^{\prime}
is a union-splitting of
\mathsf{NExt}{L}
iff
L^{\prime}
is axiomatizable over
L
by Jankov formulas. So, this theorem is proved by a similar argument as the proof of
Theorem

4.17
.
∎

Remark 4.23
.

Contrary to the case of rule systems (
Theorem

4.9
), this subclass of the union-spittings in
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
is not a lower part of them. We provide a counterexample for
L=\mathsf{K4}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the finite modal space
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be its upset
. Let
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
be the dual algebra of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
respectively. Then,
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
is of finite height since
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is cycle-free, so
\mathsf{K4}+\gamma(\mathfrak{A}^{\prime},A^{\prime})
is a union-splitting in the subclass (in fact, it is the logic
\mathsf{K4D}
). It is clear that
\mathsf{Log}(\mathcal{F})\subsetneq\mathsf{Log}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
, so
\mathsf{K4}+\gamma(\mathfrak{A},A)
is a union-splitting such that
\mathsf{K4}+\gamma(\mathfrak{A},A)\subsetneq\mathsf{K4}+\gamma(\mathfrak{A}^{\prime},A^{\prime})
. Suppose for a contradiction that
\mathsf{K4}+\gamma(\mathfrak{A},A)
is in the subclass. Then,
\mathsf{K4}+\gamma(\mathfrak{A},A)=\mathsf{K4}+\{\gamma(\mathfrak{A}_{i},A_{i}):i\in I\}
for a set
\{\gamma(\mathfrak{A}_{i},A_{i}):i\in I\}
of Jankov formulas where each
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
is a finite s.i.
\mathsf{K4}
-algebra of finite height. Since
\mathfrak{A}\not\models\gamma(\mathfrak{A},A)
, there is some
i\in I
such that
\mathfrak{A}\not\models\gamma(\mathfrak{A}_{i},A_{i})
, so
\mathfrak{A}_{i}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{B}
for a s.i. homomorphic image of
\mathfrak{A}
. By
Lemma

4.4
,
\mathfrak{B}
is of finite height. However, up to isomorphism, since
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the only cycle-free upset of
\mathcal{F}
, we have
\mathfrak{B}=\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
, and also
\mathfrak{A}_{i}=\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
because
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the only p-morphic image of itself. Thus, we obtain

\mathsf{K4}+\gamma(\mathfrak{A},A)\subsetneq\mathsf{K4}+\gamma(\mathfrak{A}^{\prime},A^{\prime})\subseteq\mathsf{K4}+\{\gamma(\mathfrak{A}_{i},A_{i}):i\in I\}=\mathsf{K4}+\gamma(\mathfrak{A},A),

which is a contradiction. So, the subclass of the union-splittings of
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K4}}
mentioned in
Theorems

4.21
and
4.22
is not downward closed.

From the global viewpoint of the lattice
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}}
, our fmp results also have implications on the
degree of Kripke incompleteness
, initially introduced by Fine
[
13
]
(see also
[
12
, Section 10.5]
).

Definition 4.24
.

Let
L_{0}
be a logic. For a logic
L\in\mathsf{NExt}{L_{0}}
, the
degree of Kripke incompleteness
of
L
in
\mathsf{NExt}{L_{0}}
is the cardinal

|\{L^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NExt}{L_{0}}:\mathsf{KF}(L^{\prime})=\mathsf{KF}(L)\}|,

where
\mathsf{KF}(L)
is the class of Kripke frames validating
L
.

Intuitively, the degree of Kripke incompleteness of a logic
L
measures the number of logics that cannot be distinguished from
L
by Kripke frames. A logic that has the degree of Kripke incompleteness 1 is also said to be
strictly Kripke complete
in
[
12
, Section 10.5]
. A celebrated result of Blok
[
9
]
says that, in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K}}
, a logic
L
has the degree of Kripke incompleteness 1 iff
L
is a union-splitting, and
L
has the degree of Kripke incompleteness
2^{\aleph_{0}}
otherwise. Determining the degree of Kripke incompleteness in lattices other than
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K}}
(e.g.,
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K4}}
) is a long-standing open question (e.g.,
[
12
, Problem 10.5]
). It is even unknown whether all union-splittings in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K4}}
are Kripke complete, let alone what their degree of Kripke incompleteness is. In this respect,
Theorem

4.21
identifies a class of union-splittings in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K4}}
that have the fmp and thus are Kripke complete, and hence have degree of Kripke incompleteness 1.

Corollary 4.25
.

Every union-splitting in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}}
split by a set of finite s.i.
\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}
-algebras of finite height has the degree of Kripke incompleteness 1 in
\mathsf{NExt}{\mathsf{K4^{m+1}_{1}}}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from
Theorem

4.21
and the general fact that a Kripke complete union-splitting
L
in
\mathsf{NExt}{L_{0}}
has degree of Kripke incompleteness 1 in
\mathsf{NExt}{L_{0}}

[
9
]
(see also
[
12
, Section 10.5]
).
∎

5
Conclusions and future work

We have applied Subdivision Construction to prove the fmp for three classes of logics and rule systems. In all three cases, what we had to do was to show the corresponding preservation lemma under the Subdivision Construction so that the fmp follows automatically from the general scheme
Theorem

4.2
. Further applications of this method are open. We expect there are more logics and rule systems that are preserved by the construction so that we can use
Theorem

4.2
to show their fmp. A characterization, or more modestly, a sufficient condition, of logics or rule systems preserved by the Subdivision Construction will lead to more general fmp results.

Another potential application of the Subdivision Construction is to show Kripke completeness. One of the reasons that we have been working with finite modal algebras of finite height (cycle-free finite modal spaces) is to make sure the construction ends in finitely many steps so that the output is a finite modal space. This output would be the finite countermodel that witnesses the fmp. If we start with an arbitrary finite modal algebra (finite modal space), the construction may continue forever. However, it still has an output as a limit, which can be an infinite Kripke frame. The property of this output is unclear to us, and we leave it open whether the Subdivision Construction can be useful for showing Kripke completeness.

Reviewing
Lemma

3.6
, one may be reminded of factorization systems in category theory. However, we do not know how to express
Lemma

3.6
in categorical language. Even stable homomorphisms and the closed domain condition lack a categorical formalization. Although compositions of stable homomorphisms are stable homomorphisms and compositions of homomorphisms are homomorphisms, it is not clear for which closed domain the composition of a stable homomorphism satisfying CDC for
D
and a one satisfying CDC for
D^{\prime}
satisfies CDC. It would also be insightful to explore an algebraic presentation of the Subdivision Construction.

Finally, the series of fmp results presented in this paper can be compared with the general fmp result regarding cofinal subframe logics and canonical formulas in
[
12
, Theorem 11.55]
, originally proved by Zakharyaschev
[
36
]
. Both claim the fmp for classes of logics of the form “a special logic + some special formulas.” On the other hand, Zakharyaschev’s results have an interesting consequence that every extension of
\mathsf{S4}
(or
\mathsf{IPC}
) with finitely many axioms in one variable has the fmp
[
12
, Theorem 11.58 and Corollary 11.59]
. It remains open if our results lead to such an elegant fmp result.

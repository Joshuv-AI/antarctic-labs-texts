Isotriviality of families of curves parametrized by 𝒜 g ​ ( n ) \mathcal{A}_{g}(n)
Éloan Rapion

Abstract
We prove that for every integers g , h ≥ 2 , n ≥ 3 g,h\geq 2,n\geq 3 , for all but finitely many prime numbers p p , for every field k k of characteristic 0 0 or p p , every separable family of smooth projective curves of genus h h over 𝒜 g ​ ( n ) ⊗ k \mathcal{A}_{g}(n)\otimes k is isotrivial. To prove this, we compute the common vanishing locus of the absolutely logarithmic symmetric forms on a smooth complex algebraic variety whose universal covering is biholomorphic to an irreducible bounded symmetric domain of rank at least 2 2 .

2.1
Absolutely logarithmic symmetric forms

In the following subsection, we will have to consider the graded
k
-algebra of logarithmic symmetric forms on a log compactification of a log compactifiable smooth
k
-variety. This graded

-algebra is actually independent of the choice of the log compactification, and Abramovich generalized its definition to every
k
-variety in
[
1
, Definition 1]
. We give here a reformulation of Abramovich’s definition in the smooth case.

Let

be a separable function field over
k

K/k
is a valuation which vanishes of
K/k
whose residual field has transcendence degree
d-1
over
k
, with
d
the transcendence degree of
K
over
k
. A valuation is divisorial if and only if it is the valuation defined by a prime divisor of a normal
k
-variety with
K
as function field.

Let
\nu
be a divisorial valuation of
K/k
. Let
A
be the valuation ring
\{x\in K,\nu(x)\geq 0\}
,
\mathfrak{m}
its maximal ideal
\{x\in K,\nu(x)\geq 1\}
,
\mathfrak{m}^{-1}
the
A
-submodule
\{x\in K,\nu(x)\geq-1\}
of
K
,
\kappa:=A/\mathfrak{m}
the residual field of
A
. The canonical maps
A\to\kappa
and
A\to K
induce morphisms of
A
-modules
\pi:\Omega_{A/k}^{1}\to\Omega_{\kappa/k}^{1}
and
f:\Omega_{A/k}^{1}\to\Omega_{K/k}^{1}
. We denote by
F_{\nu}^{1}
(resp.
L_{\nu}^{1}
) the
A
-submodule
\Img f
(resp.
\mathfrak{m}^{-1}f(\ker\pi)
) of
\Omega_{K/k}^{1}
. Remark that
F_{\nu}^{1}\subset L_{\nu}^{1}
. The following proposition is easy to prove.

Proposition 2.1
.

A form
\omega\in\Omega_{K/k}^{1}
is in
L_{\nu}^{1}
if and only if there exist
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
u\in\mathfrak{m}
,
f_{0},\dots,f_{r},x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\in A
such that
\omega=f_{0}\frac{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}u}{u}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x_{i}
.

We denote by
F_{\nu}^{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}F_{\nu}^{m}
(resp.
L_{\nu}^{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}L_{\nu}^{m}
) the free symmetric
A
-algebra generated by
F_{\nu}^{1}
(resp.
L_{\nu}^{1}
) with its canonical graduation. The inclusions of
F_{\nu}^{1}
(resp.
L_{\nu}^{1}
) in
\Omega_{K/k}^{1}
induces a morphism
\iota_{F}
(resp.
\iota_{L}
) of graded
A
-algebras from

(resp.

) to
D_{K}^{\bullet}:=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\Sym_{K}^{m}\Omega_{K/k}^{1}
. This last graded

. The following lemma is easy to prove.

Lemma 2.2
.

1.

The
A
-modules
F_{\nu}^{1}
and
L_{\nu}^{1}
are free and finite.

2.

The graded
A
-algebras
F_{\nu}^{\bullet}
and
L_{\nu}^{\bullet}
are graded algebras of polynomials over
A
.

3.

The morphisms
\iota_{F}
and
\iota_{L}
are injective.

Let
V
be a smooth
k
-variety. Let
K
be its functions field. We denote by
D_{V}^{\bullet}
the graded
k
-subalgebra
\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(V,\Sym^{m}\Omega_{V}^{1})
of

. As every symmetric differential defined in codimension 1 on

extends as a global symmetric differential form on
V
,

identify with the intersection of the
F_{\nu}^{\bullet}
’s over all divisorial valuations

of

centered in

.

We define the graded
k
-algebra

as the intersection of

with the
L_{\nu}^{\bullet}
’s for every divisorial valuation
\nu
of
K/k
. Proposition
2.1
shows that

is the algebra of
absolutely logarithmic
symmetric differential forms defined by Abramovich in
[
1
, Definition 1]
. Then we get the following properties.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
1
, Remark p.46]

If
(W,D)
is a log compactification of
V
, then
L_{V}^{\bullet}
is the graded
k
-algebra
\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(W,\Sym^{m}\Omega_{W}^{1}(\log D))
.

Let
V,V^{\prime}
be smooth
k
-varieties, let
f:V\to V^{\prime}
be a morphism. Then pull-back of differential forms induces a morphism of graded
k
-algebras
f^{*}:D_{V^{\prime}}^{\bullet}\to D_{V}^{\bullet}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
1
, Lemma 5]

Suppose
V^{\prime}
is log compactifiable. Then
f^{*}L_{V^{\prime}}^{\bullet}\subset L_{V}^{\bullet}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The integral domain

is integrally closed.

Proof.

It is the intersection of some integral domains
F_{\nu}^{\bullet}
’s and
L_{\nu}^{\bullet}
’s that are integrally closed by Lemma

.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
V,S
be smooth
k
-varieties. Let
f:V\to S
be a finite étale covering map. Then
L_{V}^{\bullet}
is the integral closure of
f^{*}L_{S}^{\bullet}
in
D_{V}^{\bullet}
.

Proof.

As
f^{*}:D_{S}^{\bullet}\to D_{V}^{\bullet}
is injective, we consider
D_{S}^{\bullet}
as a graded
k
-subalgebra of
D_{V}^{\bullet}
. Let
K
(resp.
K^{\prime}
) be the function field of
S
(resp.
V
). Then
f
induces a finite separable extension of fields

. Hence, every divisorial valuation of

is the restriction of a divisorial valuation of

. Moreover, if

is a divisorial valuation of

and

is its restriction to

, then

is divisorial and

and, with the previous proposition, the integral closure of

in

is included in

.

To prove the converse, we can suppose that

is Galois. Indeed, in any case, if

is a Galois finite étale covering map which dominates

and if

is the integral closure of

in
D_{V^{\prime}}^{\bullet}
, then the previous inclusion gives

which is integral over
L_{S}^{\bullet}
.

In the case where

is Galois,

is stable under the action of the Galois group, hence the minimal polynomial over

of the elements of

has coefficients in
L_{V}^{\bullet}\cap D_{S}^{\bullet}=L_{S}^{\bullet}
.
∎

Vanishing loci of the logarithmic symmetric forms

Consider a smooth

-variety

and

such that

and

Proposition

implies that if

,

the tautological line bundle over

and
\mathcal{O}(m)
its tensor power

, then

(resp.
\PBL(V)
) is the intersection of the base locus of
\mathcal{O}(m)
(resp. stable base locus of
\mathcal{O}(1)
) with

.

The partial functoriality of

(Proposition

) gives the partial functoriality of base loci.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
f:V\to V^{\prime}
be a morphism between smooth
k
-varieties with

log compactifiable. Then for every

\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}f(\TmBL(V))\subset\TmBL(V^{\prime})
, and
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}f(\TBL(V))\subset\TBL(V^{\prime})
.

A closed subset

\Proj\Omega_{V}^{1}
is

Proposition 2.9
.

Let

be a morphism between smooth
k
-varieties with
V^{\prime}
log compactifiable. Suppose the corestriction

\PmBL(V)
is linearly non degenerate and

instead of
\PmBL(V)

\PBL(V^{\prime})

\PmBL(V^{\prime})

3
Irreducible bounded symmetric domains

We start with a recall of some classical facts about bounded symmetric domains, and we set some notations. The proofs of those facts can be found for instance in
[
15
]
or in the four first chapters of
[
22
]
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Sigma
be an irreducible bounded symmetric domain of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. It is simply connected. The group
\Aut(\Sigma)
of biholomorphisms of
\Sigma
has a natural structure of simple real Lie group with finitely many connected components. Let
G\subset\Aut(\Sigma)
be its neutral connected component. The maximal compact Lie subgroups of
G
are the stabilizers of points of
\Sigma
. Fix
x\in\Sigma
, let
K
be the isotropy group of
x
(the stabilizer of
x
in
G
), so as
\Sigma=G/K
. The center
S
of
K
is a real Lie group. In the natural representation of
K
over the holomorphic tangent space
T_{\Sigma,x}
,
S
acts by complex homotheties, inducing a real Lie groups isomorphism
\iota
between
S
and the circle group
\mathbb{U}
. We denote
z:=\iota^{-1}(\textbf{i})
.

Consider the smooth representation of
S
over the real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
associated with
G
given by adjunction. We decompose the complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
into isotypic components:

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{-}

where:

•

S
acts trivially on
\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{C}}
,

•

S
acts on
\mathfrak{m}^{+}
by homotheties with rate given by the isomorphism
\iota:S\cong\mathbb{U}
,

•

S
acts on
\mathfrak{m}^{-}
by homotheties with rate given by the conjugation of the isomorphism
\iota
.

The component
\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. It is the complexification of the real Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}\subset\mathfrak{g}
assoicated with the real Lie subgroup
K\subset G
. The components
\mathfrak{m}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{m}^{-}
are stable under the representation of
K
over
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
given by adjunction. The complexification of the differential at the neutral element of the map
G\to\Sigma
induces an isomorphism of complex representations of
K
between
\mathfrak{m}^{+}
(resp.
\mathfrak{m}^{-}
) and the complex vector space of holomorphic (resp. antiholomorphic) tangent vectors of
\Sigma
at
x
.

Let
G^{\mathbb{C}}
be the adjoint Lie group of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Set
\mathfrak{p}:=\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{-}
: it is a complex Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
P
be the connected complex Lie subgroup of
G^{\mathbb{C}}
assoicated with
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
\check{\Sigma}:=G^{\mathbb{C}}/P
. Then, the inclusion
G\subset G^{\mathbb{C}}
induces an open embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow\check{\Sigma}
, equivariant with respect to the action of
G
.

Consider
\pi:\Proj\Omega_{\Sigma}^{1}\to\Sigma
the projectivization of the holomorphic cotangent bundle of
\Sigma
. Projectivization defines the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
on
\Proj\Omega_{\Sigma}^{1}
. The domain
\Sigma
admits a canonical Hermitian metric invariant under the action of
\Aut(\Sigma)
, the Bergman metric (see
[
21
, §1, p.55]
). We denote it

. It induces an Hermitian metric
\hat{g}
on
\mathcal{O}(-1)
.

We can express the curvature of
\hat{g}
using the Riemannian curvature tensor
R:T_{\Sigma}^{\otimes 4}\to\mathbb{R}
of
g
. Let
x\in\Sigma,v\in T_{\Sigma,x}
be a unit vector, we denote
y:=(x,[v])
its image in
\Proj\Omega_{\Sigma}^{1}
. Consider some local holomorphic coordinates
(z^{1},\dots,z^{d})
in an open neighborhood of
x
. By applying a biholomorphism of
\Sigma
and a linear automorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, we can suppose that they are centered at
x
, that the matrix
\left(g\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}}\right)_{ij}\right)
is the identity at

, and that

. If we denote
u^{i}:=\frac{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z^{i}}{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z^{d}}
(
1\leq i<d
), then
(z^{1},\dots,z^{d},u^{1},\dots,u^{d-1})
are local holomorphic coordinates of
\Proj\Omega_{\Sigma}^{1}
centered in

. Finally, we denote
R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{l}}:=R\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{l}}\right)
(

). Then,
[

, Proposition 1, p.38]
gives the first Chern form of

\Chern(\mathcal{O}(1),\hat{g})(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{1\leq k<d}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}u^{k}\wedge\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\bar{u}^{k}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}R_{d\bar{d}i\bar{j}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z^{i}\wedge\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{i}.

(1)

In this article, we are interested in smooth complex algebraic varieties

whose universal covering is biholomorphic to an irreducible bounded symmetric domain

of

Definition 3.1
.

A

As isotropy groups are compact, the action of

A locally symmetric variety admits a unique structure of complex algebraic variety compatible with its structure of complex manifold. Moreover, this structure is quasiprojective.

This has the following consequences.

Proposition 3.3

Let

A smooth complex algebraic variety whose universal covering is isomorphic to an irreducible bounded symmetric domain is quasiprojective.

7.2
The general case

Now we can prove Theorem
1.1
. For
g,h\geq 2
, recall that
\mathcal{A}_{g}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{h}
) is the moduli stack of principally polarized Abelian varieties of dimension
g
(resp. connected smooth projective curves of genus
h
). There exist a non empty irreducible scheme
S
with an étale morphism
S\to\Spec(\mathbb{Z})
(resp.
T\to\Spec(\mathbb{Z})
), a scheme
\bar{A}
(resp.
\bar{M}
) smooth and proper over
S
(resp.
T
), the complement of a normal crossing divisor
A\subset\bar{A}
(resp.
M\subset\bar{M}
) and a finite étale covering map
A\to\mathcal{A}_{g}\times S
(resp.
M\to\mathcal{M}_{h}\times T
). For example, we can take
S=\Spec(\mathbb{Z}[1/3,e^{2\textbf{i}\pi/3}])
,
T=\Spec(\mathbb{Z}[1/3])
,
A
the moduli space of principally polarized Abelian varieties of dimension
g
with
3
-level structure and
M
the moduli space of connected smooth projective curves of genus
h
with a convenient non-Abelian level structure, see
[
28
]
. Hence they satisfy the hypothesis of Proposition
2.12
.

Proof (of Theorem
1.1
).

Thanks to Lemma
7.2
,
\PBL(M\otimes\mathbb{C})=\emptyset
, hence

for some

. Thanks to Propositions
2.11
and
2.12
, for all but finitely many prime numbers
p
, for every field
k
of characteristic
0
or
p

.

As the universal covering of the smooth complex algebraic variety

g
-th Siegel upper half-space, Theorem
1.2
implies that

2.11
and
2.12
, for all but finitely many prime numbers

or
p

Consider a separable family of smooth projective curves over

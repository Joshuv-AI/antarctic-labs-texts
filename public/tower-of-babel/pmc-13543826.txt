The complete chloroplast genome sequence and phylogenetic position of Mallotus apelta
Authors: Mengsi Yang, Ailan Shi, Cheng Fan, Dong Zeng, Liang Liu, Chao Xiong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMallotus apelta is a perennial species of Euphorbiaceae widely distributed in subtropical East and Southeast Asia. In this study, we sequenced and analyzed its complete chloroplast genome. The genome was 164,055 bp in length and exhibited a typical quadripartite structure, comprising an LSC region of 88,899 bp, an SSC region of 18,494 bp, and two IR regions of 28,331 bp each. A total of 124 genes were annotated. Comparative analyses revealed conserved genome organization and IR boundaries among Mallotus species. Phylogenetic analysis showed that M. apelta was closely related to M. paniculatus, providing valuable resources for Mallotus phylogenetic studies.Keywords: Mallotus apelta, chloroplast genome, Euphorbiaceae, phylogenetic analysis

Introduction

Mallotus apelta (Lour.) Müll. Arg. 1865, a deciduous shrub or small tree of Euphorbiaceae, is widely distributed in subtropical regions of East and Southeast Asia (Rivière et al. 2010). Phytochemical studies have revealed that M. apelta contains abundant flavonoids and terpenoids with diverse biological activities, and it has been used in traditional medicine (Wang J et al. 2025). However, genomic studies of M. apelta and related Mallotus species remain limited, particularly chloroplast (cp) genome resources, hindering phylogenetic and comparative genomic studies. Chloroplast genomes are conserved but contain sufficient variation for phylogenetic analyses, and high-throughput sequencing has accelerated their application as molecular resources (Daniell et al. 2016). Mallotus comprises more than 150 species distributed throughout tropical and subtropical Asia and Oceania (Webster 1994). However, morphological complexity has caused taxonomic uncertainty, and further genomic data are required to clarify phylogenetic relationships within the genus (Sierra et al. 2010). In this study, we sequenced and characterized the complete cp genome of M. apelta using high-throughput sequencing. This genomic resource provides information for future phylogenetic, taxonomic, and evolutionary studies of Mallotus and related taxa.

Materials and methods

Plant material and DNA extraction

Fresh and healthy leaves of M. apelta were collected from Jiangxia District, Wuhan City, Hubei Province, China (30°21′28″N, 114°17′17″E) (Figure 1). The voucher specimen (No. WHPU-2025-002) was identified and deposited in the School of Life Science and Technology, Wuhan Polytechnic University (contact: Chao Xiong; email: xiongchao080190@126.com). Genomic DNA was extracted using the Plant Genomic DNA Kit (Tiangen, Beijing, China), and DNA quality was assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis and NanoDrop (Wilmington, DE).

Morphological characteristics of M. apelta. (A) Whole plant, (B) leaves, and (C) flowers. The photographs were taken by the corresponding author, Chao Xiong, in Jiangxia District, Hubei Province, China (30°21′28″N, 114°17′17″E). M. apelta is a shrub or small tree, typically 2–6 m in height, with young branches densely covered with stellate hairs. The leaves are alternate, broadly ovate to suborbicular, with a cordate base and a densely white-tomentose abaxial surface.

Genome assembly and annotation

A paired-end library (∼350 bp) was constructed using the TruSeq DNA PCR-Free kit and sequenced on Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (2 × 150 bp). A total of 5,546,760 raw reads were generated, and 5,400,107 clean reads were obtained after quality filtering using fastp v1.0 (Chen 2023). The complete cp genome was assembled de novo using GetOrganelle v1.7.5 (Jin et al. 2020) and validated by read mapping and assembly visualization. Genome annotation was performed using CPGAVAS2 (Shi et al. 2019), with M. paniculatus (NC_058595) used as the reference genome. Gene annotations were manually corrected when necessary. The circular genome map was generated using OGDRAW (Greiner et al. 2019), and the annotated cp genome was deposited in GenBank under accession no. PZ472950.

Comparative chloroplast genome analysis

Comparative analyses were performed using the complete cp genomes of four Mallotus species (M. apelta, M. paniculatus, M. japonicus, and M. peltatus). Genome size and GC content were compared based on GenBank annotations. Inverted repeat (IR) boundaries were analyzed using IRscope (Amiryousefi et al. 2018). Codon usage patterns were evaluated using protein-coding genes (PCGs), and relative synonymous codon usage (RSCU) values were calculated using the RSCU-Plot online tool (Hejazi et al. 2025). Codons with RSCU >1.0 were preferred.

Phylogenetic analyses

Twenty-one complete cp genomes of Euphorbiaceae were downloaded from NCBI to infer the phylogenetic position of M. apelta. Populus trichocarpa (NC_009143) and Salix purpurea (NC_026722) from Salicaceae were selected as outgroups. Protein-coding genes were extracted, aligned, trimmed, and concatenated using PhyloSuite v2 (Zhang et al. 2020). A maximum-likelihood (ML) tree was reconstructed using IQ-TREE with 5000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates and visualized using iTOL v6 (Letunic and Bork 2024).

Results

The cp genome of M. apelta had sequencing depths ranging from 405× to 1614×, with an average depth of 1024× (Supplementary Figure 1). The 164,055 bp cp genome exhibited a quadripartite structure with large single-copy (LSC) (88,899 bp), small single-copy (SSC) (18,494 bp), and two IR regions (28,331 bp each) (Figure 2). The overall GC content was 35%, with the IR regions (41%) showing higher GC content than the LSC (33%) and SSC (30%) regions. A total of 124 genes were annotated, including 80 protein-coding, 36 transfer RNA (tRNA), and eight ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes (Supplementary Table 1). The IR regions contained duplicated genes, including nine PCGs (ycf2, rpl2, rpl22, rpl23, rps3, rps7, rps12, rps19, and ndhB), seven tRNA genes (trnV-GAC, trnL-CAA, trnR-ACG, trnN-GUU, trnA-UGC, trnI-CAU, and trnI-GAU), and four rRNA genes (rrn16, rrn23, rrn4.5, and rrn5). Furthermore, the rps12 gene is trans-spliced, with its 5′ exon located in the LSC region and the two 3′ exons duplicated in the IRs. Additionally, several genes contained introns, including rpl16, rpoC1, atpF, ndhA, and duplicated copies of rpl2 and ndhB (Supplementary Figure 2).

Comparative analysis of four Mallotus cp genomes revealed conserved structures. The complete cp genomes ranged from 163,303 bp in M. peltatus to 164,912 bp in M. japonicus, with M. apelta exhibiting an intermediate size (Supplementary Figure 3). GC content was similar among species (35–36%), with IR regions consistently exhibiting higher GC content (41–42%) than the LSC (33–34%) and SSC (30%) regions. IRscope analysis showed conserved IR boundaries, with only minor differences in the positions of rps19, ycf1, and ndhF. RSCU analysis revealed similar codon usage patterns among the four species, with most preferred codons ending in A or U (Supplementary Figure 4).

A ML phylogenetic tree was reconstructed based on 21 complete cp genomes of Euphorbiaceae, with Populus trichocarpa and Salix purpurea (Salicaceae) as outgroups (Figure 3). Most nodes showed strong support (BS ≥96%). Euphorbiaceae species formed well-supported clades, and all sampled Mallotus species clustered into a monophyletic group with 100% bootstrap support. Within Mallotus, M. apelta and M. paniculatus formed a sister group (BS = 100%), followed by M. japonicus (BS = 98%), while M. peltatus occupied the basal position and Macaranga tanarius was recovered as the sister lineage of Mallotus.

Discussion and conclusions

Chloroplast genomes provide resources for phylogenetic studies due to their conserved structure and sequence variation (Cho et al. 2024). The cp genome of M. apelta exhibited a typical quadripartite structure, with genome size, organization, and gene content comparable to other Euphorbiaceae species (Wicke et al. 2011). The higher GC content of IR regions was likely associated with the presence of GC-rich rRNA genes (Palmer 1985). Sequencing depth varied from 405× to 1614× (average 1024×), a pattern commonly observed in cp sequencing datasets. This variation may result from the duplicated IR regions and GC-content-related sequencing bias (Benjamini and Speed 2012). The high coverage and GetOrganelle-based assembly approach ensured reliable genome reconstruction (Jin et al. 2020).

The cp phylogenetic analysis strongly supported the monophyly of Mallotus (BS = 100%) and identified Macaranga tanarius as its sister lineage, consistent with previous studies of Euphorbiaceae (Xi et al. 2012). Salicaceae was selected as the outgroup because it represents a related lineage within Malpighiales while remaining outside Euphorbiaceae (De Santana Lopes et al. 2018). Within Mallotus, M. apelta and M. paniculatus formed a strongly supported sister group, followed by M. japonicus and M. peltatus. Comparative analyses showed conserved cp organization among four Mallotus species, including genome size, GC content, codon usage, and IR boundaries. Minor differences were mainly associated with IR expansion and contraction, a common evolutionary pattern in angiosperm cp genomes (Amiryousefi et al. 2018).

Although cp genomes provide phylogenetic information, they represent only the plastid lineage and may not fully reflect the evolutionary histories. Processes such as hybridization, introgression, and incomplete lineage sorting can cause incongruence between plastid and nuclear phylogenies (Gitzendanner et al. 2018). Future integration of cp, nuclear, and mitochondrial data with broader sampling will improve understanding of Mallotus and Euphorbiaceae evolution.

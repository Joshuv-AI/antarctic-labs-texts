Odd hypergraph Mantel theorems
Jianfeng Hou Xizhi Liu Yixiao Zhang Hongbin Zhao Tianming Zhu

Abstract
A classical result of Sidorenko (1989) shows that the Turán density of every r r -uniform hypergraph with three edges is bounded from above by 1 / 2 1/2 . For even r r , this bound is tight, as demonstrated by Mantel’s theorem on triangles and Frankl’s theorem on expanded triangles. In this note, we prove that for odd r r , the bound 1 / 2 1/2 is never attained, thereby answering a question of Keevash and revealing a fundamental difference between hypergraphs of odd and even uniformity. Moreover, our result implies that the expanded triangles form the unique class of three-edge hypergraphs whose Turán density attains 1 / 2 1/2 .

Introduction

Given an integer
r\geq 2
, an
r
-uniform hypergraph
(henceforth an
r
-graph
)
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of
r
-subsets of some set
V
.
We call
V
the
vertex set
of
\mathcal{H}
and denote it by
V(\mathcal{H})
.
When
V
is understood, we usually identify a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with its edge set and use
|\mathcal{H}|
to denote the number of edges of
\mathcal{H}
.

Given a family
\mathcal{F}
of
r
-graphs, an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathcal{F}
-free
if it does not contain any member of
\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph.
The
Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is the maximum number of edges in an
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-graph on
n
vertices, and the limit
\pi(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})/\binom{n}{r}
is called the
Turán density
of
\mathcal{F}
.
The study of the Turán numbers and Turán densities is a central topic in Extremal Combinatorics.
Much is known when
r=2
, thanks to the seminal works of Turán
[
Tur41
]
and Erdős–Stone
[
ES46
]
(see also
[
ES66
]
).
For
r\geq 3
, determining
\pi(\mathcal{F})
is notoriously difficult in general, despite significant effort devoted to this area.
For results up to 2011, we refer the reader to the excellent survey by Keevash
[
Kee11
]
.

In this note, we study the Turán density of
r
-graphs with exactly three edges.
For the case
r=2
, the classical Mantel theorem
[
Man07
]
from 1907 shows that the Turán density of the triangle is
1/2
, and the tightness of this bound is witnessed by the balanced complete bipartite graphs.
Define

\displaystyle\gamma(r)\coloneqq\max\left\{\pi(F)\colon\text{$F$ is an $r$-graph with exactly three edges}\right\}.

As an extension of Mantel’s theorem to hypergraphs, a general theorem of Sidorenko
[
Sid89
]
shows that the Turán density of an
r
-graph with exactly three edges is always bounded from above by
1/2
, that is,
\gamma(r)\leq 1/2
.
For an integer
k\geq 1
, let

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{2k}\coloneqq\left\{S_{1}\cup S_{2},~S_{2}\cup S_{3},~S_{3}\cup S_{1}\right\}

denote the
2k
-uniform hypergraph (known as the
expanded triangle
) on vertex set
S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup S_{3}
, where
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
are pairwise disjoint
k
-sets.
A classical result of Frankl
[
Fra90
]
shows that
\pi(\mathbb{T}_{2k})=1/2
.
In particular,
\gamma(r)=1/2
for even
r
, that is, Sidorenko’s bound is tight when
r
is even.

In a work strengthening Sidorenko’s result, Keevash
[
Kee05
]
posed the question of whether the bound
\gamma(r)\leq 1/2
is ever tight when
r
is odd.
This question was later raised again in
[
Kee11
]
and
[
MPS11
]
.
Keevash also observed that for
r=3
, classical results of Bollobás
[
Bol74
]
and de Caen
[
dC83
]
show that
\gamma(3)
is strictly smaller than
1/2
.

In this note, we answer Keevash’s question by showing that for odd
r
, the bound
1/2
is never attained, thereby revealing an interesting difference between hypergraphs of even and odd uniformity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
r
is a positive odd integer. Then
\gamma(r)<1/2
.

Let us remark that determining the exact value of
\gamma(r)
for odd
r
remains a very challenging problem.
Indeed, for
r=3
, as observed by Keevash, results of Bollobás
[
Bol74
]
and Frankl–Füredi
[
FF84
]
imply that
\gamma(3)=\pi(K_{4}^{3-})
, where
K_{4}^{3-}
denotes the
3
-graph on four vertices with three edges.
However, determining
\pi(K_{4}^{3-})
remains an open problem, with the current best upper bound
\pi(K_{4}^{3-})\leq 0.2871\ldots
established by Baber–Talbot
[
BT11
]
using the computer-assisted Flag Algebra machinery of Razborov
[
Raz07
]
.

Theorem
1.1
, together with some additional arguments, yields the following corollary, which shows that the expanded triangles are the “worst” three-edge hypergraphs.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
r
be a positive even integer.
Suppose that
F
is an
r
-graph with exactly three edges such that
F\not\cong\mathbb{T}_{r}
.
Then
\pi(F)<1/2
.

This paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
introduces the necessary definitions and preliminary results.
In Section
3
, we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Finally, Section
4
contains some concluding remarks.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some definitions and preliminary results.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer and
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-graph.
For every vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
, the
link
of
v
in
\mathcal{H}
is defined as

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\coloneq\left\{S\in\tbinom{V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{v\}}{r-1}\colon S\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

The
degree
of
v
is given by
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\coloneqq|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)|
.
We use
\delta(\mathcal{H})
,
\Delta(\mathcal{H})
, and
d(\mathcal{H})
to denote the
minimum
,
maximum
, and
average
degrees of
\mathcal{H}
, respectively.
The subscript
\mathcal{H}
will be omitted when it is clear from the context.

Let
r\geq s\geq 2
be integers.
For an
s
-graph
F
, the
r
-suspension
of
F
, denoted by
\hat{F}^{r}
, is the
r
-graph given by

\displaystyle\hat{F}^{r}\coloneqq\left\{C\cup e\colon e\in F\right\},

where
C
is an
(r-s)
-set disjoint from
V(F)
.

It is clear from the definition that for integers
r_{2}>r_{1}\geq s
, the
r_{2}
-graph
\hat{G}^{r_{2}}
can be viewed as the
(r_{2}-r_{1})
-suspension of the
r_{1}
-graph
\hat{G}^{r_{1}}
.

We have the following simple lemma on the Turán density of suspensions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
r\geq s\geq 2
be integers and
F
be an
s
-graph.
Then
\pi(\hat{F}^{r})\leq\pi(F)
.
Consequently,
\pi(\hat{F}^{r_{2}})\leq\pi(\hat{F}^{r_{1}})
for all integers
r_{2}\geq r_{1}\geq s
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

It suffices to consider the case
r=s+1
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
\hat{F}^{s+1}
-free
(s+1)
-graph on
n
vertices with exactly
\mathrm{ex}(n,\hat{F}^{s+1})
edges.
It follows from the definition of suspension that for every vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
, the link
L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
is
F
-free.
Therefore,
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\leq\mathrm{ex}(n-1,F)
, and consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,\hat{F}^{s+1})}{\binom{n}{s+1}}=\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{\binom{n}{s+1}}=\frac{1}{s+1}\frac{\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}{\binom{n}{s+1}}\leq\frac{1}{s+1}\frac{n\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n-1,F)}{\binom{n}{s+1}}=\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n-1,F)}{\binom{n-1}{s}}.

Letting
n\to\infty
in the above, we obtain
\pi(\hat{F}^{s+1})\leq\pi(F)
.

Given two
r
-graphs
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, a map
\phi\colon V(F_{1})\to V(F_{2})
is a
homomorphism
from
F_{1}
to
F_{2}
if
\phi(e)\in F_{2}
for every
e\in F_{1}
.
We will use the following standard result in Turán problems, which follows from a result of Erdős–Simonovits
[
ES83
]
(see also
[
Kee11
, Theorem 2.2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be two
r
-graphs.
Suppose that there exists a homomorphism from
F_{1}
to
F_{2}
.
Then
\pi(F_{1})\leq\pi(F_{2})
.

Recall the definition of
\mathbb{T}_{2k}
from the previous section.
Let
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V
be a partition of a vertex set
V
.
Denote by
\mathbb{B}^{2k}[V_{1},V_{2}]
the collection of all
2k
-subsets of
V
that intersect both
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
in an odd number of vertices.
We call
\mathbb{B}^{2k}[V_{1},V_{2}]
the
complete odd-bipartite

2k
-graph with parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
.
Denote by
\mathbb{B}^{2k}_{n}
the complete odd-bipartite
2k
-graph on
n
vertices with the maximum number of edges.

Confirming a conjecture of Frankl
[
Fra90
]
, Keevash–Sudakov
[
KS05
]
proved that for large
n
, the unique extremal construction for
\mathbb{T}_{2k}
is
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{2k}
. Moreover, they established the following stability theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
KS05
, Theorem 3.4]
)
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer.
For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\delta_{\ref{THM:T2k-stability}}=\delta_{\ref{THM:T2k-stability}}(k,\varepsilon)>0
and
N_{\ref{THM:T2k-stability}}=N_{\ref{THM:T2k-stability}}(k,\varepsilon)
such that the following holds for every
n\geq N_{\ref{THM:T2k-stability}}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a
\mathbb{T}_{2k}
-free
2k
-graph on
n
vertices with
|\mathcal{H}|\geq\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-\delta_{\ref{THM:T2k-stability}}\right)\binom{n}{2k}
.
Then there exists a partition
V(\mathcal{H})=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
with
\left||V_{1}|-|V_{2}|\right|\leq 1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{H}\triangle\mathbb{B}^{2k}[V_{1},V_{2}]\right|\leq\varepsilon n^{2k}.

Here, we use
A\triangle B
to denote the symmetric difference of two sets
A
and
B
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.1
Reduction

In this subsection, we show that Theorem
1.1
reduces to the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every integer
k\geq 1
, we have
\pi(\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2k}^{2k+1})<1/2
.

For every integer
r\geq 2
, denote by
\mathcal{T}^{r}
the collection of all
r
-graphs with exactly three edges.
By removing isolated vertices if necessary, we may assume that
\delta(F)\geq 1
for every
F\in\mathcal{T}^{r}
.
We further partition
\mathcal{T}^{r}
into the following two subfamilies:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}^{r}\coloneqq\left\{F\in\mathcal{T}^{r}\colon\delta(F)=1\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}\coloneqq\left\{F\in\mathcal{T}^{r}\colon\delta(F)\geq 2\right\}.

Lemma 3.2
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

We have
\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}=\big\{\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{r}\colon 1\leq i\leq\lfloor r/2\rfloor\big\}
.

(ii)

For every
r
-graph
F_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}^{r}
, there exists an
r
-graph
F_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}
such that there exists a homomorphism from
F_{1}
to
F_{2}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

First, we present the proof of Lemma
3.2

(i)
.
We proceed by induction on
r
.
The base case
r\in\{2,3\}
holds, since it is easy to see that the family
\mathcal{T}_{2}^{2}
consists only of the single graph
K_{3}=\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2}^{2}
, and the family
\mathcal{T}_{2}^{3}
consists only of the single
3
-graph
K_{4}^{3-}=\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2}^{3}
.

Now assume that
r\geq 4
.
First, we consider the case where
r=2s
is even.
Fix an arbitrary
2s
-graph
F=\left\{E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}\right\}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{2s}
.

If every vertex of
F
has degree exactly two, then it is easy to see that
|V(F)|=3s
, and hence,

\displaystyle|E_{1}\cap E_{2}|=3s-\big(|E_{2}\cap E_{3}|+|E_{1}\cap E_{3}|\big)=3s-|E_{3}|=s.

By symmetry, we also have
|E_{1}\cap E_{3}|=|E_{2}\cap E_{3}|=s
, which implies that
F\cong\mathbb{T}_{2s}
.

If
F
contains a vertex
x
of degree three, then
F
is the
2s
-suspension of the
(2s-1)
-graph
F^{\prime}\coloneqq F-x
. Since
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{2s-1}
, it follows from the induction hypothesis that
F^{\prime}\cong\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{2s-1}
for some
1\leq i\leq s-1
.
Therefore,
F\cong\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{2s}
, as desired.

Now we consider the case where
r=2s+1
is odd.
Fix an arbitrary
(2s+1)
-graph
F=\left\{E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}\right\}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{2s+1}
.
A simple double-counting argument shows that the sum of the degrees of the vertices in
F
is
3(2s+1)=6s+3
, which is odd.
Therefore, there exist a vertex
x
of degree three in
F
.
Hence,
F
is the
(2s+1)
-suspension of the
2s
-graph
F^{\prime}\coloneqq F-x
.
Since
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{2s}
, it follows from the induction hypothesis that
F^{\prime}\cong\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{2s}
for some
1\leq i\leq s
.
Therefore,
F\cong\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{2s+1}
, as desired.

Next, we present the proof of Lemma
3.2

(ii)
.
Fix an
r
-graph
F_{1}=\{E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}\}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}^{r}
.
By the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{r}
, there exists a vertex
x
of degree one in
F_{1}
.
By relabeling the edges of
F_{1}
if necessary, we may assume that
x\in E_{1}
.
Since
E_{2}\neq E_{1}
, there exists a vertex
y\in E_{2}\setminus E_{1}
.
Let
\tilde{F}
denote the
r
-graph obtained from
F_{1}
by replacing the edge
E_{1}
with
(E_{1}\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\}
(it is possible that
(E_{1}\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\}
is already contained in
F_{1}
, in which case we keep only one copy).
Note that
\tilde{F}
has exactly two edges or the number of vertices of degree one in
\tilde{F}
is strictly smaller than that of
F_{1}
, and the map
\phi\colon V(F_{1})\to V(\tilde{F})
given by

\displaystyle\phi(v)=\begin{cases}v,&\quad\text{if}\quad v\neq x,\\
y,&\quad\text{if}\quad v=x,\end{cases}

(1)

is a homomorphism from
F_{1}
to
\tilde{F}
.
By repeating this operation, we eventually obtain a homomorphism from
F_{1}
to an
r
-graph
F
in which every vertex has degree at least two, or to a single edge.
In the former case, we have
F\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}
, and we are done.
In the latter case, there exists a homomorphism from
F_{1}
to every member in
\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}
, and we are also done.

Combining Lemma
2.2
with Lemma
3.2

(i)
and
(ii)
, we obtain for every integer
r\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\max\left\{\pi(F)\colon F\in\mathcal{T}_{1}^{r}\right\}\leq\max\left\{\pi(F)\colon F\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}\right\}=\max\left\{\pi(\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{r})\colon 1\leq i\leq\lfloor\tfrac{r}{2}\rfloor\right\}.

(2)

Combining this with Lemma
2.1
, we conclude that for every odd integer
r\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\max\left\{\pi(F)\colon F\in\mathcal{T}^{r}\right\}=\max\left\{\pi(\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{r})\colon 1\leq i\leq\lfloor\tfrac{r}{2}\rfloor\right\}\leq\max\left\{\pi(\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{2i+1})\colon 1\leq i\leq\lfloor\tfrac{r}{2}\rfloor\right\}.

Therefore, the proof of Theorem
1.1
reduces to that of Theorem
3.1
.

We end this subsection by presenting the proof of Corollary
1.2
, assuming Theorem
1.1
.
We will use the following strengthening of Lemma
3.2

(ii)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
r\geq 3
be an integer.
For every
r
-graph
F_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}^{r}
, there exists an
r
-graph
F_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}
with
\Delta(F_{2})=3
such that there exists a homomorphism from
F_{1}
to
F_{2}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Since
r\geq 3
, by Lemma
3.2

(i)
, there exists an
r
-graph
F\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}
with
\Delta(F)=3
.
Fix an
r
-graph
F_{1}=\{E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}\}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}^{r}
.
If
\Delta(F_{1})=1
, then
F_{1}
consists of three pairwise disjoint edges, which means that there exists a homomorphism from
F_{1}
to every
r
-graph
F_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}
with
\Delta(F_{2})=3
, and we are done.
If
\Delta(F_{1})=3
, then we are also done by Lemma
3.2

(ii)
.
Therefore, we may assume that
\Delta(F_{1})=2
.

Claim 3.4
.

There exist two vertices
x,y\in V(F_{1})
that are not contained in any edge of
F_{1}
, such that
d_{F_{1}}(x)=1
and
d_{F_{1}}(y)=2
.

Proof of Claim
3.4
.

Let
u\in V(F_{1})
be a vertex of degree one (which exists by the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{r}
), and let
v\in V(F_{1})
be a vertex of degree two (which exists since
\Delta(F_{1})=2
).
By symmetry, we may assume that
u\in E_{1}
.
Note that we are done if
v\not\in E_{1}
.
Thus, we may assume that
v\in E_{1}
as well.
By symmetry, we may assume that
v\in E_{1}\cap E_{2}
.
Since
\Delta(F_{1})=2
, we have
v\notin E_{3}
.

Suppose that
E_{2}\cap E_{3}\neq\emptyset
.
Choose a vertex
y\in E_{2}\cap E_{3}
.
Since
\Delta(F_{1})=2
, the vertex
y
is not contained in
E_{1}
.
Thus, the pair
\{u,y\}
satisfies the claim.

Suppose instead that
E_{2}\cap E_{3}=\emptyset
.
Then there exists a vertex
w\in E_{3}
of degree one in
F_{1}
.
The pair
\{w,v\}
satisfies the claim.

Fix two vertices
x,y\in V(F_{1})
that are not contained in any edge of
F_{1}
, such that
d_{F_{1}}(x)=1
and
d_{F_{1}}(y)=2
.
By symmetry, we may assume that
x\in E_{1}
and
y\in E_{2}\cap E_{3}
.
As in the proof of Lemma
3.2

(ii)
, let
\tilde{F}
denote the
r
-graph obtained from
F_{1}
by replacing the edge
E_{1}
with
(E_{1}\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\}
(it is possible that
(E_{1}\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\}
is already contained in
F_{1}
, in which case we keep only one copy).
The map
\phi
defined in (
1
) is a homomorphism from
F_{1}
to
\tilde{F}
.

Note that either
\tilde{F}
has exactly two edges, or
\tilde{F}
contains a vertex of degree three.
In the former case, there exists a homomorphism from
F_{1}
to a single edge, and consequently to every
r
-graph
F_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}
with
\Delta(F_{2})=3
, and we are done.
In the latter case,
\tilde{F}
contains a vertex of degree three, and we are also done by Lemma
3.2

(ii)
.

We are now ready to present the proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Fix a positive even integer
r
.
Let
F
be an
r
-graph with exactly three edges such that
F\not\cong\mathbb{T}_{r}
.
If
r=2
, then
F
is bipartite (in fact, a forest), which implies that
\pi(F)=0
.
Therefore, we may assume that
r\geq 4
.

Suppose that
F\in\mathcal{T}_{1}^{r}
.
Then by Lemma
3.3
, there exists a homomorphism from
F
to some
r
-graph
\tilde{F}\in\mathcal{T}^{r}_{2}
containing a vertex
x
of degree three.
It follows from Lemma
2.2
that
\pi(F)\leq\pi(\tilde{F})
.
Since
\tilde{F}
is the
r
-suspension of the
(r-1)
-graph
\tilde{F}-x
, it follows from Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
1.1
(noting that
r-1
is odd) that

\displaystyle\pi(\tilde{F})\leq\pi(\tilde{F}-x)<\frac{1}{2},

which implies that
\pi(F)<1/2
.

Suppose that
F\in\mathcal{T}_{2}^{r}
.
Since
F\not\cong\mathbb{T}_{r}
, it follows from Lemma
3.2

(i)
that
F\cong\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{r}
for some
i<r/2
.
Then by Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\pi(F)=\pi(\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{r})\leq\pi(\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2i}^{2i+1})<\frac{1}{2}.

This completes the proof of Corollary
1.2
.

3.2
Proof of Theorem
3.1

In this subsection, we present the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
For ease of notation, we set
\mathbb{T}\coloneqq\mathbb{T}_{2k}
and
\hat{\mathbb{T}}\coloneqq\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{2k}^{2k+1}
, and we will also omit the superscript
2k
from
\mathbb{B}^{2k}[V_{1},V_{2}]
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be a sufficiently small constant, and let
\delta\coloneqq\delta_{\ref{THM:T2k-stability}}(k,\varepsilon)>0
and
N\coloneqq N_{\ref{THM:T2k-stability}}(k,\varepsilon)>0
be the constants given by Theorem
2.3
.
By reducing
\delta
if necessary, we may assume that
\delta\leq\varepsilon
.
We will prove that
\pi(\hat{\mathbb{T}})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\delta}{3}
.

Assume for contradiction that
\pi(\hat{\mathbb{T}})>\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\delta}{3}
.
Let
n\gg N
be a sufficiently large integer, and let
\mathcal{H}
be a
\hat{\mathbb{T}}
-free
(2k+1)
-graph on
n
vertices with exactly
\mathrm{ex}(n,\hat{\mathbb{T}})
edges, that is,
\mathcal{H}
is an extremal
(2k+1)
-graph.
We may choose
n
sufficiently large so that

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{H}\right|=\mathrm{ex}(n,\hat{\mathbb{T}})\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\binom{n}{2k+1}.

Observe that every pair of vertices in
\hat{\mathbb{T}}
is contained in some edge.
Thus, a standard deleting-duplicating argument (see e.g.
[
BLLP25
, Proposition 2.6]
) shows that

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathcal{H})-\delta(\mathcal{H})\leq\binom{n-2}{2k-1}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq d(\mathcal{H})-\binom{n-2}{2k-1}=\frac{(2k+1)\cdot|\mathcal{H}|}{n}-\binom{n-2}{2k-1}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\delta\right)\binom{n}{2k}.

(3)

Let
x\in V(\mathcal{H})
be a vertex.
Since
\mathcal{H}
is
\hat{\mathbb{T}}
-free, it follows from the definition of
\hat{\mathbb{T}}
that the link
L(x)
is
\mathbb{T}
-free.
Combining it with (
3
) and Theorem
2.3
, we conclude that there exists a partition
V_{x}^{1}\cup V_{x}^{2}=V\coloneqq V(\mathcal{H})
with
\left||V_{x}^{1}|-|V_{x}^{2}|\right|\leq 1
such that

\displaystyle\left|L(x)\triangle\mathbb{B}[V_{x}^{1},V_{x}^{2}]\right|\leq\varepsilon n^{2k}.

(4)

Define the set of bad edges and missing edges (of
L(x)
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{x}\coloneqq L(x)\setminus\mathbb{B}[V_{x}^{1},V_{x}^{2}]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}_{x}\coloneqq\mathbb{B}[V_{x}^{1},V_{x}^{2}]\setminus L(x).

It follows from (
4
) that

\displaystyle\max\{|\mathcal{B}_{x}|,~|\mathcal{M}_{x}|\}\leq|\mathcal{B}_{x}|+|\mathcal{M}_{x}|=\left|L(x)\triangle\mathbb{B}[V_{x}^{1},V_{x}^{2}]\right|\leq\varepsilon n^{2k}.

(5)

Define

\displaystyle Z_{x}\coloneqq\left\{v\in V(\mathcal{H})\colon d_{\mathcal{M}_{x}}(v)\geq\varepsilon^{1/2}n^{2k-1}\right\}.

Claim 3.5
.

We have
\left|Z_{x}\right|\leq 2k\varepsilon^{1/2}n
.

Proof of Claim
3.5
.

By definition of
Z_{x}
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{x}|=\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{v\in V}d_{\mathcal{M}_{x}}(v)\geq\frac{1}{2k}|Z_{x}|\cdot\varepsilon^{1/2}n^{2k-1}.

Combining this with (
5
), we obtain
|Z_{x}|\leq 2k\varepsilon^{1/2}n
.

For convenience, let
\mathcal{P}_{x}\coloneqq\{V_{x}^{1},V_{x}^{2}\}
denote the bipartition corresponding to the vertex
x
.
For a pair of vertices
x,y\in V
, define

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\mathcal{P}_{x},\mathcal{P}_{y})\coloneq\min\left\{|V_{x}^{1}\triangle V_{y}^{1}|,~|V_{x}^{1}\triangle V_{y}^{2}|\right\}.

Claim 3.6
.

For every pair of vertices
x,y\in V
with
y\in V\setminus Z_{x}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\mathcal{P}_{x},\mathcal{P}_{y})\leq\varepsilon^{1/8k}n.

Proof of Claim
3.6
.

For each
(i,j)\in[2]\times[2]
, define
U_{ij}\coloneqq V_{x}^{i}\cap V_{y}^{j}
.
Suppose for contradiction that Claim
3.6
fails.
Then

\displaystyle|U_{12}|+|U_{21}|=|V_{x}^{1}\triangle V_{y}^{1}|\geq\mathrm{dist}(\mathcal{P}_{x},\mathcal{P}_{y})>\varepsilon^{1/8k}n.

Since both partitions
V_{x}^{1}\cup V_{x}^{2}=V
and
V_{y}^{1}\cup V_{y}^{2}=V
are balanced, we have

\displaystyle\bigl||U_{12}|-|U_{21}|\bigr|=\bigl|\big(|V_{x}^{1}|-|U_{11}|\big)-\big(|V_{y}^{1}|-|U_{11}|\big)\bigr|=\bigl||V_{x}^{1}|-|V_{y}^{1}|\bigr|\leq 1.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\min\left\{|U_{12}|,~|U_{21}|\right\}\geq\frac{1}{2}|V_{x}^{1}\triangle V_{y}^{1}|-1\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{1/8k}n}{3}.

For the same reason, the inequality also holds for
U_{11}
and
U_{22}
. In conclusion, for every
(i,j)\in[2]\times[2]
,

\displaystyle|U_{ij}|\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{1/8k}n}{3}>|Z_{x}|.

(6)

Assume by symmetry that
y\in U_{11}
.
Choose uniformly at random a vertex
z
and a
(k-1)
-set
S_{1}
from
U_{21}
with
z\not\in S_{1}
, and independently, choose uniformly at random a
(k-1)
-set
S_{2}
and a
k
-set
S_{3}
from
U_{22}
with
S_{2}\cap S_{3}=\emptyset
.
Let

•

A_{1}
be the event that
\{y\}\cup S_{1}\cup S_{3}\in L(x)
,

•

A_{2}
be the event that
\{y,z\}\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2}\in L(x)
, and

•

A_{3}
be the event that
\{z\}\cup S_{2}\cup S_{3}\in L(y)
.

Since
y\in V\setminus Z_{x}
, we have from the definition of
Z_{x}
that
d_{\mathcal{M}_{x}}(y)<\varepsilon^{1/2}n^{2k-1}
.
Combining this with (
6
), we conclude that the probability that
A_{1}
does not occur satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 3.49306pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$A_{1}$}}\right]\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{1/2}n^{2k-1}}{\binom{|U_{21}|-1}{k-1}\binom{|U_{22}|-k+1}{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(k-1)!k!\varepsilon^{1/2}n^{2k-1}}{\left(\tfrac{\varepsilon^{1/8k}n}{3}-2k+2\right)^{2k-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(k-1)!k!\varepsilon^{1/2}n^{2k-1}}{\left({\varepsilon^{1/8k}n}/{4}\right)^{2k-1}}\leq 4^{2k-1}(k!)^{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{2k+1}{8k}}<\frac{1}{4}.

Similarly, the probability that
A_{2}
does not occur satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 3.49306pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$A_{2}$}}\right]\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{1/2}n^{2k-1}}{\left(|U_{21}|-k+1\right)\binom{|U_{21}|-1}{k-1}\binom{|U_{22}|-k}{k-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left((k-1)!\right)^{2}\varepsilon^{1/2}n^{2k-1}}{\left(\tfrac{\varepsilon^{1/8k}n}{3}-2k+2\right)^{2k-1}}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{2k-1}(k!)^{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{2k+1}{8k}}<\frac{1}{4}.

It follows from (
5
) and (
6
) that the probability that
A_{3}
does not occur satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 3.49306pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$A_{3}$}}\right]\leq\frac{\varepsilon n^{2k}}{\left(|U_{21}|-k+1\right)\binom{|U_{22}|-k}{k-1}\binom{|U_{22}|-k+1}{k}}\leq\frac{(k-1)!k!\varepsilon n^{2k}}{\left(\tfrac{\varepsilon^{1/8k}n}{3}-2k+2\right)^{2k}}\leq 4^{2k}(k!)^{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{4}}<\frac{1}{4}.

Therefore, the probability that
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
, and
A_{3}
occur simultaneously satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap A_{3}\right]\geq 1-\mathbb{P}\left[\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 3.49306pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$A_{1}$}}\right]-\mathbb{P}\left[\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 3.49306pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$A_{2}$}}\right]-\mathbb{P}\left[\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 3.49306pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$A_{3}$}}\right]>\frac{1}{4}.

Fix a selection of
(z,S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
such that
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
, and
A_{3}
occur simultaneously.
Let

\displaystyle E_{1}\coloneqq\{x,y\}\cup S_{1}\cup S_{3},\quad E_{2}\coloneqq\{x,y,z\}\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2},\quad\text{and}\quad E_{3}\coloneqq\{y,z\}\cup S_{2}\cup S_{3}.

Note that
\{E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
forms a copy of
\hat{\mathbb{T}}
, with
y
serving as the vertex of degree three, which is a contradiction.

Fix a vertex
x\in V
, and let
W_{1}\coloneqq V_{x}^{1}\setminus Z_{x}
and
W_{2}\coloneqq V_{x}^{2}\setminus Z_{x}
.
It follows from Claim
3.5
that

\displaystyle\min\{|W_{1}|,~|W_{2}|\}\geq\min\{|V_{x}^{1}|-|Z_{x}|,~|V_{x}^{2}|-|Z_{x}|\}\geq\lfloor n/2\rfloor-2k\varepsilon^{1/2}n.

For every vertex
y\in V\setminus Z_{x}
, it follows from Claim
3.6
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\mathcal{P}_{x},\mathcal{P}_{y})=\min\left\{|V_{x}^{1}\triangle V_{y}^{1}|,~|V_{x}^{1}\triangle V_{y}^{2}|\right\}\leq\varepsilon^{1/8k}n.

By relabeling the two parts
V_{y}^{1}
and
V_{y}^{2}
if necessary, we may assume that it is always the case that

\displaystyle|V_{x}^{1}\triangle V_{y}^{1}|=\mathrm{dist}(\mathcal{P}_{x},\mathcal{P}_{y})\leq\varepsilon^{1/8k}n.

(7)

Claim 3.7
.

For every vertex
y\in V\setminus Z_{x}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|L(y)\triangle\mathbb{B}[W_{1},W_{2}]\right|\leq 5k\varepsilon^{1/8k}n^{2k}.

Proof of Claim
3.7
.

It follows from (
4
), (
7
), and Claim
3.6
that

\displaystyle\left|L(y)\triangle\mathbb{B}[W_{1},W_{2}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|L(y)\triangle\mathbb{B}[V_{y}^{1},V_{y}^{2}]\right|+\left|\mathbb{B}[V_{y}^{1},V_{y}^{2}]\triangle\mathbb{B}[W_{1},W_{2}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon n^{2k}+\big(|W_{1}\triangle V_{y}^{1}|+|W_{2}\triangle V_{y}^{2}|\big)\tbinom{n}{2k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon n^{2k}+\big(|W_{1}\triangle V_{x}^{1}|+|V_{x}^{1}\triangle V_{y}^{1}|+|W_{2}\triangle V_{x}^{2}|+|V_{x}^{2}\triangle V_{y}^{2}|\big)\tbinom{n}{2k-1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon n^{2k}+\left(2\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\mathcal{P}_{x},\mathcal{P}_{y})+|Z_{x}|\right)\tbinom{n}{2k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon n^{2k}+\left(2\varepsilon^{1/8k}n+2k\varepsilon^{1/2}n\right)\tbinom{n}{2k-1}\leq 5k\varepsilon^{1/8k}n^{2k},

which proves Claim
3.7
.

Next, we show that there exists a vertex
y\in W_{1}
with
\left|L(y)\triangle\mathbb{B}[W_{1},W_{2}]\right|>5k\varepsilon^{1/8k}n^{2k}
.
This contradicts Claim
3.7
, and thus completing the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Claim 3.8
.

There exists a vertex
y\in W_{1}\subseteq V\setminus Z_{x}
such that

\displaystyle\left|L(y)\triangle\mathbb{B}[W_{1},W_{2}]\right|\geq\frac{n^{2k}}{4^{2k}(2k)!}>5k\varepsilon^{1/8k}n^{2k}.

Proof of Claim
3.8
.

Define an auxiliary bipartite graph
G
on
W_{1}\cup W_{2}
where a pair
(y,z)\in W_{1}\times W_{2}
forms an edge in
G
iff
y\in V_{z}^{1}\setminus Z_{z}
.
It follows from Claim
3.5
and (
7
) that for every
z\in W_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{G}(z)=\left|\big(V_{z}^{1}\setminus Z_{z}\big)\cap W_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\geq|V_{x}^{1}|-|Z_{x}|-|V_{x}^{1}\triangle V_{z}^{1}|-|Z_{z}|

\displaystyle\geq\lfloor n/2\rfloor-4k\varepsilon^{1/2}n-\varepsilon^{1/8k}n\geq\frac{n}{2}-5k\varepsilon^{1/8k}n.

It follows that there exists a vertex
y\in W_{1}
with

\displaystyle d_{G}(y)\geq\frac{|G|}{|W_{1}|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|W_{2}|\left(n/2-5k\varepsilon^{1/8k}n\right)}{|W_{1}|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(\lfloor n/2\rfloor-2k\varepsilon^{1/2}n\right)\left(n/2-5k\varepsilon^{1/8k}n\right)}{\lceil n/2\rceil}>\frac{n}{4}.

(8)

Let
\hat{W}\coloneqq N_{G}(y)\subseteq W_{2}
.
For each vertex
w\in\hat{W}
, consider the link of the pair
\{y,w\}
in
\mathcal{H}
, restricted to the set
W_{2}
, defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{w}\coloneqq\left\{e\in\tbinom{W_{2}}{2k-1}\colon e\cup\{y\}\in L(w)\right\}=\left\{e\in\tbinom{W_{2}}{2k-1}\colon e\cup\{w,y\}\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

Observe that for each
e\in\mathcal{L}_{w}
, we have
e\cup\{w\}\in\tbinom{W_{2}}{2k}\cap L(y)
, and hence,

\displaystyle\left|\tbinom{W_{2}}{2k}\cap L(y)\right|\geq\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{w\in\hat{W}}|\mathcal{L}_{w}|.

(9)

Fix a vertex
w\in\hat{W}
.
Recall from the definition of
G
that
y\in V_{w}^{1}\setminus Z_{w}
.
It follows from the definition of complete odd-bipartite hypergraph that every
(2k-1)
-set in
W_{2}\cap V_{w}^{2}
forms an edge with
y
in
\mathbb{B}[V_{w}^{1},V_{w}^{2}]
.
Combining this with the definition of
Z_{w}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{w}|\geq\binom{|W_{2}\cap V_{w}^{2}|}{2k-1}-d_{\mathcal{M}_{w}}(y)

\displaystyle\geq\binom{|W_{2}|-\mathrm{dist}(\mathcal{P}_{x},\mathcal{P}_{w})}{2k-1}-d_{\mathcal{M}_{w}}(y)

\displaystyle\geq\binom{\lfloor n/2\rfloor-2k\varepsilon^{1/2}n-\varepsilon^{1/8k}n}{2k-1}-\varepsilon^{1/2}n^{2k-1}

\displaystyle\geq\binom{n/3}{2k-1}-\varepsilon^{1/2}n^{2k-1}\geq\frac{1}{(2k-1)!}\left(\frac{n}{4}\right)^{2k-1}.

(10)

Notice from the definition of complete odd-bipartite hypergraph that

\displaystyle\tbinom{W_{2}}{2k}\cap L(y)\subseteq L(y)\triangle\mathbb{B}[W_{1},W_{2}].

So it follows from (
3.2
), (
9
), and (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\left|L(y)\triangle\mathbb{B}[W_{1},W_{2}]\right|\geq\left|\tbinom{W_{2}}{2k}\cap L(y)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{w\in\hat{W}}|\mathcal{L}_{w}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\hat{W}|}{2k}\frac{1}{(2k-1)!}\left(\frac{n}{4}\right)^{2k-1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{8k}\frac{1}{(2k-1)!}\left(\frac{n}{4}\right)^{2k-1}=\frac{n^{2k}}{4^{2k}(2k)!},

which proves Claim
3.8
.

We now see that Claim
3.7
and Claim
3.8
contradict each other, and thus this completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

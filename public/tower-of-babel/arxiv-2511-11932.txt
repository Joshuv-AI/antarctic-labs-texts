Abstract
. We establish the existence and qualitative properties of nontrivial solutions for a class of Hartree-Fock type systems defined over the whole space ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . By introducing a suitable Nehari-Pohozaev manifold, we prove the existence, regularity and we describe the asymptotic behavior of solutions with respect to the interaction parameter β > 0 \beta>0 . In particular, we show that the system admits either a vector ground state or a semitrivial ground state solution, depending on the magnitude of β \beta .

1.
Introduction and main results

The Hartree-Fock type system has attracted considerable attention in theoretical research over the years. This class of systems appears in the study of standing wave solutions for an approximation of the Hartree model with a two-component attractive interaction, and its form is given by

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -i\varphi_{t}-\Delta_{x}\varphi+\varphi+\phi\varphi&=|\varphi|^{2p-2}\varphi+\beta|\psi|^{p}|\varphi|^{p-2}\varphi,&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
-i\psi_{t}-\Delta_{x}\psi+\psi+\phi\psi&=|\psi|^{2p-2}\psi+\beta|\varphi|^{p}|\psi|^{p-2}\psi,&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\end{aligned}\right.

with
N\geq 2
,
\beta\geq 0
, and
1<p<\infty
. The pair
(\varphi,\psi)
, with
\varphi,\psi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
, represent a standing wave solution. The System (
1.1
) is motivated by an approximation given in Hartree-Fock method which is considered in orbital theory (see
[
19
,
22
,
32
]
). See also
[
9
,
10
,
24
,
27
]
.

Inspired by (
1.1
), we are interested in standing wave solutions of the form
(\varphi,\psi)=(e^{it}u,e^{it}v)
, with
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
. In this case,
(u,v)
is a solution of

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+V(x)u+\phi u&=|u|^{2p-2}u+\beta|v|^{p}|u|^{p-2}u,&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\Delta v+V(x)v+\phi v&=|v|^{2p-2}v+\beta|u|^{p}|v|^{p-2}v,&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\end{aligned}\right.

with
V\equiv 1
. The term
\phi
is obtained by the convolution of
\gamma\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)
(for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
) with the fundamental solution of the Laplacian
\Gamma:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\Gamma(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x|,\ \ \mbox{ if}\ \ N=2\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\Gamma(x)=\frac{c_{N}}{|x|^{N-2}},\ \ \mbox{ if}\ \ N\geq 3,

where
c_{N}=1/N(N-2)\omega_{N}
(
\omega_{N}
is the volume of the unit in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
). More precisely,

\phi(x)=\phi_{u,v}(x):=\left[\Gamma\ast\left(\gamma\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)\right)\right](x)=\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Gamma(|x-y|)\left(u^{2}(y)+v^{2}(y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y.

In this case, the function
\phi_{u,v}
(Newtonian potential) is the natural candidate for solving the following Poisson’s equation

(1.3)

-\Delta\phi=\gamma\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right),\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

The system (
1.2
), with
N\geq 3
, has been intensively studied in the last years, see
[
5
,
25
,
26
,
38
]
. Considering
v=0
in (
1.2
), notice that this system has a slight relation with the equation

(1.4)

-\Delta u+V(x)u+\phi_{u,0}u=|u|^{2p-2}u,\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

In dimension three, variations of this problem have been studied in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
14
,
29
]
, and many others. The planar case is more delicate due to the sign-changing nature of the
\log|x|
kernel, in contrast to the strictly positive behavior of
1/|x|^{N-2}
in higher dimensions. Moreover, when
N\geq 3
, we can use the Lax-Milgram Theorem in the space
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to solve the equation (
1.3
). This kind of approach can not be applied for the case
N=2
due to the loss of continuous embedding of
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
into the Lebesgue spaces. In this direction, we can cite
[
17
,
31
,
16
]
.

Depending on the behavior of
V
, for example, such as being bounded away from zero and sufficiently large in an appropriate sense, or being periodic, one can establish existence and multiplicity of solutions for problems like (
1.4
), using, for instance, the Mountain Pass Theorem or the Nehari method. On this subject we refer the reader to
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
8
,
11
,
17
]
.

The case
V\equiv 0
where is called
problem with zero mass (or null mass)
. For the case
N\geq 3
, we can cite
[
1
,
21
,
40
]
.

In the planar case, we could
cite
[
15
,
36
,
39
,
35
]
. In these works the authors use functions with symmetry, called
axially symmetric functions
(
u(x,y)=u(|x|,|y|)
). For further results for semilinear elliptic problems considering the zero mass case we also refer the reader to
[
12
,
18
,
33
,
34
]
.

Our goal in this work is to study the system (
1.2
) in dimension two, focusing on the case
V\equiv 0
. The case
V\equiv 1
was previously investigated in
[
13
]
, under the restriction to radially symmetric functions. A key contribution of our work is that we do not impose any symmetry assumptions, such as axial or radial symmetry, on the function space. More precisely, in the present work, we consider the following class of planar elliptic systems:

(
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\phi_{u,v}u&=|u|^{2p-2}u+\beta|v|^{p}|u|^{p-2}u,&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\\
-\Delta v+\phi_{u,v}v&=|v|^{2p-2}v+\beta|u|^{p}|v|^{p-2}v,&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

where
2\leq p<\infty
and
\beta\geq 0
. By taking
\gamma=2\pi
, we have that

(1.5)

\phi_{u,v}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(|x-y|)\left(u^{2}(y)+v^{2}(y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Due to the presence of the term
\phi_{u,v}
, inspired by
[
31
]
, the appropriate workspace for the system given above can be written in the following form:

W^{\lambda}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\|u\|_{\lambda,*}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(\lambda+|x|)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\},

where the norm is defined by

\|u\|_{W^{\lambda}}:=\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{\lambda,*}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

for
\lambda>0
. The energy functional
I_{\beta}:W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda}\to\mathbb{R}
associated to this system is given by

I_{\beta}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{V}(u,v)-\frac{1}{2p}\psi_{\beta}(u,v),

where

\mathcal{V}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(|x-y|)\left(u^{2}(y)+v^{2}(y)\right)\left(u^{2}(x)+v^{2}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

and

(1.6)

\psi_{\beta}(u,v):=\|u\|_{2p}^{2p}+\|v\|_{2p}^{2p}+2\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|uv|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Here, we denote the norm of Lebesgue space
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by
\|\cdot\|_{s}
, with
1\leq s<\infty
.

In order to obtain least energy solution it is common to use the Nehari manifold

\mathcal{N}_{\beta}:=\left\{(u,v)\in\left(W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda}\right)\setminus\{(0,0)\}:I_{\beta}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)=0\right\},

where

(1.7)

I_{\beta}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{V}(u,v)-\psi_{\beta}(u,v).

In general, the fiber map
t\mapsto I_{\beta}(tu,tv)
is used in this context. However, this strategy does not work in the case of zero mass, since the term
\|\cdot\|_{2}
does not appear in the energy functional, which is part of the norm in the space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Here, we need to apply some minimization arguments for a Nehari-Pohozaev manifold taking into account the fibers
t\mapsto I_{\beta}(t^{2}u_{t},t^{2}v_{t})
, where
u_{t}(x):=u(tx)
and
v_{t}(x):=v(tx)
. It can be obtained in a non-rigorous way by the following relation

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(I_{\beta}\left(t^{2}u_{t},t^{2}v_{t}\right)\right)\Big|_{t=1}=0.

More precisely, the Nehari-Pohozev manifold is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{\beta}:=\left\{(u,v)\in\left(W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda}\right)\setminus\{(0,0)\}:J_{\beta}(u,v)=0\right\},

where

(1.8)

\displaystyle J_{\beta}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=2\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{V}(u,v)-\frac{(4p-2)}{2p}\psi_{\beta}(u,v).

Unlike problems involving the standard Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}_{\beta}
, it is not immediate that all critical points of
I_{\beta}
lie on
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
. This makes the application of the Nehari-Pohozaev approach more challenging when aiming to guarantee the existence of a least energy solution. To overcome this difficulty, we will first need to rigorously establish the following Pohozaev identity

\mathcal{P}_{\beta}(u,v):=\mathcal{V}(u,v)+\frac{1}{4}\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\psi_{\beta}(u,v)=0,

and then verify that

J_{\beta}(u,v)=2I_{\beta}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)-\mathcal{P}_{\beta}(u,v).

A pair
(u,v)\in W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda}
is said to be a weak solution for System (

S
β

), whenever

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{u,v}u\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(|u|^{2p-2}u+\beta|v|^{p}|u|^{p-2}u\right)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla v\nabla\psi\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{u,v}v\psi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(|v|^{2p-2}v+\beta|u|^{p}|v|^{p-2}v\right)\psi\,\mathrm{d}x

for any
\varphi,\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Under these conditions, it follows that
(u,v)\in W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda}
is a critical point for
I_{\beta}
if, and only if,
(u,v)
is a weak solution for the System (

S
β

). We refer to a solution
(u,v)
as a semi-trivial solution when either
u=0
or
v=0
, and as a vectorial solution when both components are nontrivial, that is,
u,v\neq 0
. A solution
(u,v)
is said to be nonnegative if
u,v\geq 0
. Furthermore, a pair
(u,v)\in W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda}
is called a least energy solution (or ground state solution) to System (

S
β

) if
I_{\beta}(u,v)\leq I_{\beta}(z,w)
for all nontrivial weak solutions
(z,w)\in W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda}
.

Our first main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Existence and asymptotic behavior of solutions)
.

Assume that
2\leq p<\infty
and
\beta\geq 0
. Then, System (

S
β

) admits a nonnegative weak solution
(u_{\beta}^{\lambda},v_{\beta}^{\lambda})\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
such that

I_{\beta}(u_{\beta}^{\lambda},v_{\beta}^{\lambda})=c_{\beta}:=\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}}I_{\beta}(u,v),

for each
\lambda>e^{1/4}
.
Furthermore,

(a)

for
\lambda>e^{1/4}
fixed,
u_{\beta}^{\lambda}\rightharpoonup u^{\lambda}
and
v_{\beta}^{\lambda}\rightharpoonup v^{\lambda}
weakly in
W^{\lambda}
, as
\beta\to 0^{+}
, such that

I_{0}(u^{\lambda},v^{\lambda})=c_{0};

(b)

for
\lambda>e^{1/4}
fixed, we obtain that
\|u_{\beta}^{\lambda}\|_{2}\to 0
and
\|v_{\beta}^{\lambda}\|_{2}\to 0
, as
\beta\to\infty
.

Now, we shall discuss some results on regularity of solutions for system (

S
β

). Furthermore, we prove a Pohozaev identity which permits to show that
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
is a natural constraint. Namely, we can state the following result:

Theorem 1.2

(Regularity and Pohozaev identity)
.

Assume the conditions of Theorem
1.1
and let
(u,v)
be a solution of System (

S
β

). Then,

(i)

u,v\in C^{2,\sigma}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
;

(ii)

if
u\neq 0
, then
u>0
. Similarly, if
v\neq 0
, we have
v>0
;

(iii)

it holds that

(1.9)

\mathcal{P}_{\beta}(u,v):=\mathcal{V}(u,v)+\frac{1}{4}\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\psi_{\beta}(u,v)=0.

Remark 1.3

(Ground state solution)
.

From (
1.7
), (
1.8
), and (
1.9
) we can conclude that

J_{\beta}(u,v)=2I_{\beta}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)-\mathcal{P}_{\beta}(u,v).

In view of Theorem
1.2
, if
(z,w)
is a weak solution of System (

S
β

), then
(z,w)\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
. Therefore, the solution that achieve the level
c_{\beta}
is a ground state solution.

Now, we are able to consider the existence of semitrivial solutions of System (

S
β

) which are given by the vectors
(u,0)
or
(0,v)
, where
u,v
are nontrivial weak solution to the following scalar problems, respectively:

(
\mathcal{P}_{1}
)

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\phi_{u}u

\displaystyle=|u|^{2p-2}u,

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},

and

(
\mathcal{P}_{2}
)

\displaystyle-\Delta v+\phi_{v}v

\displaystyle=|v|^{2p-2}v,

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

Recall also that
\phi_{u}=\phi_{u,0}
and
\phi_{v}=\phi_{0,v}
, see (
1.5
).

Another feature in the present work is to find sharp conditions on the parameter
\beta>0
such that System (

S
β

) admits semi-trivial solutions or vector solutions. Namely, we shall state the following result:

Theorem 1.4

(Vectorial and semi-trivial solutions)
.

Considering the pair
(u_{\beta}^{\lambda},v_{\beta}^{\lambda})=(u_{\beta},v_{\beta})
(with
\lambda>e^{1/4}
fixed) obtained in Theorem
1.1
, it follows that:

(i)

for any

(u_{\beta},v_{\beta})
is a vector solution;

(ii)

for any
0\leq\beta<2^{p-1}-1

(u_{\beta},v_{\beta})
is a semi-trivial solution;

(iii)

\beta=2^{p-1}-1
, if and only if,
(u/\sqrt{2},u/\sqrt{2})
is a solution of (

S
β

).

2.
Variational framework

In this Section we establish the variational framework to our problem. Recall that the energy functional
I_{\beta}:W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda}\to\mathbb{R}
associated to System (

S
β

) is given by

I_{\beta}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{V}(u,v)-\frac{1}{2p}\psi_{\beta}(u,v),

where

\mathcal{V}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(|x-y|)\left(u^{2}(y)+v^{2}(y)\right)\left(u^{2}(x)+v^{2}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

and

\psi_{\beta}(u,v):=\|u\|_{2p}^{2p}+\|v\|_{2p}^{2p}+2\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|uv|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

By using the identity
\log r=\log(\lambda+r)-\log(1+\lambda r^{-1})
, for any
r,\lambda>0
, we can write

(2.1)

\mathcal{V}(u,v)=\mathcal{V}_{1}(u,v)-\mathcal{V}_{2}(u,v),

where

\mathcal{V}_{1}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\left(\lambda+|x-y|\right)\left(u^{2}(y)+v^{2}(y)\right)\left(u^{2}(x)+v^{2}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

and

\mathcal{V}_{2}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\left(1+\lambda|x-y|^{-1}\right)\left(u^{2}(y)+v^{2}(y)\right)\left(u^{2}(x)+v^{2}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

As proved in
[
17
, Lemma 2.2]
, the non-local terms
\mathcal{V}_{1},\,\mathcal{V}_{2}
are well-defined and belong to
C^{1}(W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda})
. Furthermore, taking into account that
\lambda+|x-y|\leq(\lambda+|x|)(\lambda+|y|)
, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\lambda\geq 1
, we have that

(2.2)

\log(\lambda+|x-y|)\leq\log((\lambda+|x|)(\lambda+|y|))=\log(\lambda+|x|)+\log(\lambda+|y|).

By using a straightforward computation, we estimate

(2.3)

\mathcal{V}_{1}(u,v)\leq 2\left(\|u\|^{2}_{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{2}\right)\left(\|u\|^{2}_{W^{\lambda}}+\|v\|^{2}_{W^{\lambda}}\right),

for any
u,v\in W^{\lambda}
.
In order to estimate
\mathcal{V}_{2}
, we will use the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, see
[
23
]
, which can be stated as follows:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
q,\,s>1
,
0<\mu<2
with
1/q+1/s+\mu/2=2
,
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then, there exists a constant
C(q,s,\mu)>0
such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{g(y)h(x)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C(q,s,\mu)\|g\|_{q}\|h\|_{s}.

Applying Proposition
2.1
with
\mu=1
,
q=s=4/3
, and using the elementary inequality
\log(1+r)\leq r
, for any
r>0
together with the Sobolev embedding
W^{\lambda}\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{8/3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we deduce

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{u^{2}(y)u^{2}(x)+u^{2}(y)v^{2}(x)+v^{2}(y)u^{2}(x)+v^{2}(y)v^{2}(x)}{|x-y|}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\|u\|_{8/3}^{4}+2\|u\|_{8/3}^{2}\|v\|_{8/3}^{2}+\|v\|_{8/3}^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left(\|u\|^{4}_{W^{\lambda}}+\|u\|_{W^{\lambda}}^{2}\|v\|_{W^{\lambda}}^{2}+\|v\|_{W^{\lambda}}^{4}\right).

Therefore,
I_{\beta}
is well-defined. By standard arguments one may conclude that
I_{\beta}
is
C^{1}
with Gateaux derivative

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{\beta}(u,v)(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\nabla u\nabla\varphi+\nabla v\nabla\psi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{u,v}\left(u\varphi+v\psi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(|u|^{2p-2}u+\beta|v|^{p}|u|^{p-2}u\right)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(|v|^{2p-2}v+\beta|u|^{p}|v|^{p-2}v\right)\psi\,\mathrm{d}x,

for any
u,v,\varphi,\psi\in W^{\lambda}
.

3.
Nehari-Pohozaev manifold

In what follows, we recall the Nehari-Pohozev set

\mathcal{M}_{\beta}:=\left\{(u,v)\in\left(W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda}\right)\setminus\{(0,0)\}:J_{\beta}(u,v)=0\right\},

where
J_{\beta}(u,v)
is defined in (
1.8
). Firstly, we prove that the set
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
is a manifold of
C^{1}
class.

Proposition 3.1
.

The set
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
is a
C^{1}
–manifold.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}=J_{\beta}^{-1}(0)
and
J_{\beta}
is in
C^{1}
class. Moreover, for each
(u,v)\in W^{\lambda}\times W^{\lambda}
, we mention that

(3.1)

J_{\beta}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)=4(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2})-(\|u\|^{2}_{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2})^{2}+4\mathcal{V}(u,v)-2(2p-1)\psi_{\beta}(u,v).

Let us consider
(u,v)\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
. Hence,

(3.2)

\displaystyle J_{\beta}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{\beta}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)-4J_{\beta}(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-4(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2})+\left[-2(2p-1)+4\frac{2p-1}{p}\right]\psi_{\beta}(u,v)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle-\frac{2(2p-1)(p-2)}{p}\psi_{\beta}(u,v)\leq 0

holds true for each
p\geq 2
. Therefore, by using the Implicit Function Theorem we deduce that
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
is a manifold of
C^{1}
class. This ends the proof.
∎

Now, we shall prove that any nonzero function admits exactly one projection over the Nehari-Pohozaev manifold. For this purpose, we need the following auxiliary result:

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
u,v\in W^{\lambda}
, then

(3.3)

\|\nabla\left(t^{2}u_{t}\right)\|_{2}^{2}=t^{4}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2},\quad\|\nabla\left(t^{2}v_{t}\right)\|_{2}^{2}=t^{4}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2},

(3.4)

\mathcal{V}(t^{2}u_{t},t^{2}v_{t})=t^{4}\log(t^{-1})\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}+t^{4}\mathcal{V}(u,v),

and

(3.5)

\psi_{\beta}(t^{2}u_{t},t^{2}v_{t})=t^{4p-2}\psi_{\beta}(u,v),

where
u_{t}(x):=u(tx)
and
v_{t}(x):=v(tx)
, for
t>0
.

Proof.

Considering
\overline{x}=tx
, we have

), we have

(4.5)

\max_{t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]}I_{\beta}\left(\eta(1,t^{2}\overline{w}_{t})\right)<c_{\beta}.

Finally, define
\Psi(t):=J_{\beta}\left(\eta(1,t^{2}\overline{w}_{t})\right)
, for any
t>0
. Using (
4.3
) and item
(i)
of Lemma
4.1
,
\eta(1,t_{1}^{2}\overline{w}_{t_{1}})=t_{1}^{2}\overline{w}_{t_{1}}
and
\eta(1,t_{1}^{2}\overline{w}_{t_{2}})=t_{2}^{2}\overline{w}_{t_{2}}
. From (
4.4
),

\Psi(t_{2})<0<\Psi(t_{1}).

Since
\Psi
is continuous, there exists
r\in(t_{1},t_{2})
such that
\Psi(r)=J_{\beta}\left(\eta(1,r^{2}\overline{w}_{r})\right)=0
. Consequently,
\eta(1,r^{2}\overline{w}_{r})\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
. So, by (
4.5
) we obtain

c_{\beta}\leq I_{\beta}\left(\eta(1,r^{2}\overline{w}_{r})\right)\leq\max_{t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]}I_{\beta}\left(\eta(1,t^{2}\overline{w}_{t})\right)<c_{\beta},

which is a contradiction. The proof is completed.
∎

4.1.
Existence of solution

Now, we prove that any minimizer sequence for
I_{\beta}
restricted to
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
is bounded. This can be done thanks to Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality together with some fine estimates. Specifically, we are able to prove the following result:

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
is a minimizing
sequence for
c_{\beta}
, then the sequences
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
are bounded in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
.

Proof.

Firstly, by using (
3.15
), we infer that

(4.6)

c_{\beta}+o_{n}(1)=I_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})\geq\frac{1}{8}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{2p-3}{4p}\psi_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n}),

where
o_{n}(1)\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
. Since
p\geq 2\geq 3/2
, we can conclude that
\{\|u_{n}\|_{2}\}_{n}
and
\{\|v_{n}\|_{2}\}_{n}
are bounded.

Now, we prove that
\{\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}\}_{n}
and
\{\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}\}_{n}
are also bounded. Using (
1.8
), we can compute

(4.7)

\displaystyle c_{\beta}+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=I_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})-\frac{1}{8}J_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{32}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\mathcal{V}(u_{n},v_{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{(4p-10)}{16p}\psi_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n}).

Consequently,

c_{\beta}+o_{n}(1)+\frac{(10-4p)}{16p}\psi_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{8}\mathcal{V}(u_{n},v_{n}).

From (
4.6
), the sequence
\{\psi_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})\}_{n}
is bounded. Thus, there exists
C_{0}>0
such that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large,

C_{0}+c_{\beta}\geq\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{V}(u_{n},v_{n}).

This estimate combined with (
2.1
) and the fact that
\mathcal{V}_{1}(u_{n},v_{n})\geq 0
, imply that

(4.8)

C_{0}+c_{\beta}\geq\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}-\mathcal{V}_{2}(u_{n},v_{n}),

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large. To estimate the term
\mathcal{V}_{2}(u_{n},v_{n})
, we apply the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, namely

(4.9)

\|u\|_{s}^{s}\leq C_{s}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{s-2},

for
s>2
and for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. By (
2.4
),

\mathcal{V}_{2}(u_{n},v_{n})\leq C_{1}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{8/3}^{4}+2\|u_{n}\|_{8/3}^{2}\|v_{n}\|_{8/3}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{8/3}^{4}\right).

Using (
4.9
) with
s=8/3
, there holds

\|u_{n}\|_{8/3}\leq C_{2}\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{3/4}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{1/4}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|v_{n}\|_{8/3}\leq C_{2}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{3/4}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{1/4},

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,

\mathcal{V}_{2}(u_{n},v_{n})\leq C_{3}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{3}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}+\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{3/2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{1/2}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{3/2}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{1/2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{3}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}\right).

By Young’s inequality,

\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{3/2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{1/2}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{3/2}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{1/2}\leq\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{3}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}}{2}+\frac{\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{3}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}}{2}.

As consequence,

\mathcal{V}_{2}(u_{n},v_{n})\leq C_{4}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{3}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{3}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}\right).

Now, let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Using Young’s inequality once more, we deduce

\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{3}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}\leq C(\varepsilon)\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{6}+\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{3}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}\leq C(\varepsilon)\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{6}+\varepsilon\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence,

(4.10)

\mathcal{V}_{2}(u_{n},v_{n})\leq C_{5}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{6}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{6}\right)+C_{4}\varepsilon\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right).

Combining this estimate with (
4.8
), one conclude that

(4.11)

C_{0}+c_{\beta}\geq\left(1-{C_{4}\varepsilon}\right)\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)-{C_{5}}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{6}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{6}\right),

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large. Picking
\varepsilon>0
small, such that
1-C_{4}\varepsilon>0
and using that
\{\|u_{n}\|_{2}\}_{n}
and
\{\|v_{n}\|_{2}\}_{n}
are bounded, the last estimate allows us to ensure that
\{\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}\}_{n}
and
\{\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}\}_{n}
are bounded.
∎

Next, we shall prove that the Nehari-Pohozaev manifold
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
is away from the origin. This is important in order to avoid minimizer sequences that weakly converges to the zero function. Precisely, we prove the following result:

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that
\lambda>e^{1/4}
. Then, there exists
\delta=\delta(\beta)>0
such that

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|v\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\geq\delta,\quad\forall(u,v)\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}.

Proof.

The proof follows arguing by contradiction. Suppose that there
exists a sequence
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
such that

(4.12)

\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|v_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=o_{n}(1).

Notice that

(4.13)

2\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\log(\lambda)\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}=2\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\mathcal{T}(u_{n},v_{n}),

where

\mathcal{T}(u_{n},v_{n}):=\log(\lambda)\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{4}+2\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{4}\right).

Noting that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(\lambda+|x-y|)u^{2}(x)v^{2}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\log(\lambda)\|u\|_{2}^{2}\|v\|_{2}^{2},

one deduces

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(u_{n},v_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(\lambda+|x-y|)u_{n}^{2}(x)u_{n}^{2}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(\lambda+|x-y|)u_{n}^{2}(x)v_{n}^{2}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(\lambda+|x-y|)v_{n}^{2}(x)u_{n}^{2}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(\lambda+|x-y|)v_{n}^{2}(x)v_{n}^{2}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{1}(u_{n},v_{n}).

This estimate combined with (
4.13
) infer that

2\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\log(\lambda)\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}\leq 2\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\mathcal{V}_{1}(u_{n},v_{n}).

Since
J_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})=0
, by (
1.8
), one has

(4.14)

\displaystyle 2\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\log(\lambda)\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{V}_{2}(u_{n},v_{n})+\frac{1}{4}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{(4p-2)}{2p}\psi_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n}).

Combining (
4.10
) and (
1.6
) (with Hölder’s inequality), we get

\displaystyle 2\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\log(\lambda)\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{6}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{6}\right)

\displaystyle+C_{1}\varepsilon\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+C_{1}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2p}^{2p}+\|v_{n}\|_{2p}^{2p}\right).

As consequence, defining

\alpha_{n}:=2\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\log(\lambda)\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2},

we have that

1\leq C_{1}\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{6}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{6}}{\alpha_{n}}+C_{1}\varepsilon\frac{\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}{\alpha_{n}}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}}{\alpha_{n}}+C_{1}\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{2p}^{2p}+\|v_{n}\|_{2p}^{2p}}{\alpha_{n}}.

Thus, we see that

1\leq C_{2}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+C_{2}\varepsilon+\frac{1}{4\log(\lambda)}+C_{1}\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{2p}^{2p}+\|v_{n}\|_{2p}^{2p}}{\alpha_{n}}.

From (
4.9
) together with Young’s inequality, we can estimate

\|u_{n}\|_{2p}^{2p}\leq C_{3}\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2p-2}\leq C_{3}\left(\varepsilon\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{4}+C(\varepsilon)\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2(2p-2)}\right).

Similarly,

\|v_{n}\|_{2p}^{2p}\leq C_{4}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2p-2}\leq C_{4}\left(\varepsilon\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{4}+C(\varepsilon)\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2(2p-2)}\right).

The last three estimates, imply that

1\leq C_{2}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+C_{2}\varepsilon+\frac{1}{4\log(\lambda)}+C_{5}\varepsilon+C_{5}\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2(2p-2)-2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2(2p-2)-2}\right).

Since
p>3/2
, then
2(2p-2)-2>0
, and hence

1\leq C_{2}\varepsilon+\frac{1}{4\log(\lambda)}+C_{5}\varepsilon.

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we see that
\lambda\leq e^{1/4}
which is a contradiction.
∎

Finally, we show that the existence of weak solution for System (

S
β

).

Proposition 4.5
.

Assume that
2\leq p<\infty
and
\beta\geq 0
. Then, there exists a pair
(u,v)\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
, with
\lambda>e^{1/4}
, such that

I_{\beta}(u,v)=c_{\beta},\quad\mbox{with}\ \ u,v\geq 0.

In particular,
(u,v)
is a weak solution of (

S
β

).

Proof.

Let
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
be such that

(4.15)

I_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})=c_{\beta}+o_{n}(1).

We divide the proof in four steps.

First step:
It holds that

(4.16)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{B_{R}(y)}\left(u_{n}^{2}+v_{n}^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\geq\eta,\quad\mbox{for some
}\ R>0,

where
B_{R}(y):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x-y|<R\}
. In fact, otherwise, after passing to a subsequence,
u_{n},v_{n}\to 0
in
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, for any
s>2
, due to Lions’ Lemma, see e.g.
[
37
, Lemma 1.21]
. As consequence, we can use (
2.4
) to deduce

\mathcal{V}_{2}(u_{n},v_{n})=o_{n}(1).

On other hand, from (
1.6
) and Hölder’s inequality, we can conclude that

\psi_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})=o_{n}(1).

Applying these convergences in (
4.14
), there holds

2\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\frac{\log(\lambda)-1}{4}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}\leq o_{n}(1).

Recall that
\lambda>e^{1/4}
. Hence,

(4.17)

\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|v_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=o_{n}(1),

which is a contradiction with Lemma
4.4
and finishes the proof of the first step.

Second step:
The sequence
\{\mathcal{V}_{1}(u_{n},v_{n})\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}
is bounded.

Using (
1.8
), we have that

\mathcal{V}_{1}(u_{n},v_{n})\leq\mathcal{V}_{2}(u_{n},v_{n})+\frac{1}{4}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{4p-2}{2p}\psi_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n}).

Since the sequences
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
are bounded in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
(see Lemma
4.3
), we can combine (
4.10
) together with (
1.6
), Hölder’s inequality, and the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to ensure that
\{\mathcal{V}_{1}(u_{n},v_{n})\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}
is bounded, completing this step.

Third step:
There exists
\{y_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that the sequences
\{\overline{u}_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{\overline{v}_{n}\}_{n}
, given by
\overline{u}_{n}(x):=u_{n}(x-y_{n})
and
\overline{v}_{n}(x):=v_{n}(x-y_{n})
, are bounded in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W}^{\lambda}
.

Firstly, by using (
4.16
), there exists
\{y_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that

(4.18)

\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left(\overline{u}_{n}^{2}+\overline{v}_{n}^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{\eta}{2}.

Since
I_{\beta}
and
J_{\beta}
are invariant under
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-translations, it follows from (
4.15
) that

I_{\beta}(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n})=I_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})=c_{\beta}+o_{n}(1)\quad\mbox{and}\quad J_{\beta}(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n})=J_{\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})=0.

The Lemma
4.3
yields
C_{1}>0
such that

(4.19)

\|\overline{u}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\,\|\overline{v}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C_{1},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

It remains to prove that there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(\lambda+|x|)\overline{u}_{n}^{2},\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(\lambda+|x|)\overline{v}_{n}^{2}\leq C_{2},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Considering
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{2R}(0)
and
y\in B_{R}(0)
, notice that

\lambda+|x-y|\geq\lambda+|x|-|y|\geq\lambda+|x|-R\geq\lambda+\frac{|x|}{2}\geq\sqrt{\lambda+|x|}.

Now, by using (
4.18
), we deduce that

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{V}_{1}(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{2R}(0)}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\log(\lambda+|x|)\left(\overline{u}_{n}^{2}(y)+\overline{v}_{n}^{2}(y)\right)\left(\overline{u}_{n}^{2}(x)+\overline{v}_{n}^{2}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{2R}(0)}\log(\lambda+|x|)\left(\overline{u}_{n}^{2}(x)+\overline{v}_{n}^{2}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Consequently,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log(\lambda+|x|)\left(\overline{u}_{n}^{2}(y)+\overline{v}_{n}^{2}(y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4C_{3}}{\eta}+\log(\lambda+2R)\int_{B_{2R}(0)}\left(\overline{u}_{n}^{2}(y)+\overline{v}_{n}^{2}(y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{4},

for some
C_{4}=C_{4}(\lambda)>0
, where we used (
4.19
). This step is completed.

Fourth step:
There exists
(u,v)\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
such that

I_{\beta}(u,v)=c_{\beta},\quad\mbox{with}\ \ u,v\geq 0.

It follows from the third step that, up to a subsequence,
\overline{u}_{n}\rightharpoonup u
and
\overline{v}_{n}\rightharpoonup v
weakly in
W^{\lambda}
. We claim that
u\neq 0
or
v\neq 0
. If this is not true,
u=v=0
. Recall also that the compact embedding
W^{\lambda}\hookrightarrow L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
holds for each
2\leq s<\infty
, see for instance
[
17
]
. Hence, using the last assertion together with (
2.4
) and (
1.6
) (with Hölder’s inequality), we obtain

\mathcal{V}_{2}(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n})=\psi_{\beta}(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n})=o_{n}(1).

Similarly to (
4.17
) one deduces

\|\overline{u}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\overline{v}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=o_{n}(1),

which is a contradiction with Lemma
4.4
. Thus,
u\neq 0
or
v\neq 0
.

In view of Lemma
3.3
there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
(t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}},t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}})\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
. We claim that
t_{0}\in(0,1]
. In fact, analogously to the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 2.7]
we infer that

\mathcal{V}(u,v)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{V}(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n}).

On other hand, from the compact embedding
W^{\lambda}\hookrightarrow L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(
2\leq s<\infty
) together with
[
13
, Lemma 3.5]
, one may deduce that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi_{\beta}(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n})=\psi_{\beta}(u,v)

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\|\overline{u}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\overline{v}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}=\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}.

The last three expressions combined with (
1.8
) and the fact that

\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\|\nabla\overline{u}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\overline{v}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)

imply that

(4.21)

J_{\beta}(u,v)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}J_{\beta}(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n})=0.

Therefore,
t_{0}\in(0,1]
, see for instance Lemma
3.3
.

Finally, we can use (
3.15
)
to get

(4.22)

\displaystyle c_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq I_{\beta}(t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}},t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}})

\displaystyle=I_{\beta}(t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}},t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}})-\frac{1}{4}J_{\beta}(t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}},t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\left(\|t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{2p-3}{4p}\psi_{\beta}(t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}},t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}}).

By (
3.7
) (with
p=2
), it holds that

\|t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}}\|_{2}^{2}=t_{0}^{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}}\|_{2}^{2}=t_{0}^{2}\|v\|_{2}^{2}.

Moreover, the equality (
3.5
) yields

\psi_{\beta}(t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}},t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}})=t_{0}^{4p-2}\psi_{\beta}(u,v).

Since
t_{0}\in(0,1]
and using the identities just above together with (
4.22
) and (
3.15
), we ensure that

(4.23)

\displaystyle c_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq I_{\beta}(t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}},t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{2p-3}{4p}\psi_{\beta}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{8}\left(\|\overline{u}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\overline{v}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{2p-3}{4p}\psi_{\beta}(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n})\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{\beta}(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n})=c_{\beta}.

Thus,
t_{0}=1
, because otherwise,
c_{\beta}<c_{\beta}
. Thereby,

I_{\beta}(u,v)=c_{\beta},\quad\mbox{with}\ \ (u,v)\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}.

Since

c_{\beta}=I_{\beta}(u,v)=I_{\beta}(|u|,|v|)\quad\mbox{ and}\quad 0=J_{\beta}(u,v)=J_{\beta}(|u|,|v|),

we can consider
u,v\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This ends the proof of this step.

Now, we can use Proposition
4.2
to concludes that
(u,v)
is a weak solution of (

S
β

). The proof is completed.
∎

3.12
),

I_{\beta}(t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}},t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}})\geq\frac{t_{0}^{4}}{2}\|\nabla\rho\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{t_{0}^{4}\log(t_{0}^{-1})}{4}\|\rho\|_{2}^{4}+\frac{t_{0}^{4}}{4}\mathcal{V}(\rho,0)-\frac{t_{0}^{4p-2}}{2p}\psi_{\beta}(u,v).

Now, we estimate
\psi_{\beta}(u,v)
. Since
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, then
0<\cos^{2}\theta<1
. Thus, we can apply Lemma
6.3
to obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{\beta}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{2p}^{2p}+\|v\|_{2p}^{2p}+2\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|uv|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[(\cos^{2}\theta)^{p}+(\sin^{2}\theta)^{p}+2\beta(\cos^{2}\theta)^{p/2}(\sin^{2}\theta)^{p/2}\right]|\rho|^{2p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle<\|\rho\|_{2p}^{2p}=\psi_{\beta}(\rho,0).

This inequality combined with the last estimate imply that

I_{\beta}(t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}},t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}})>I_{\beta}(t_{0}^{2}\rho_{t_{0}},0)\geq c_{\beta},

where we used (
3.12
). Using (
3.8
), since
(u,v)\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
, it holds that

c_{\beta}<I_{\beta}(t_{0}^{2}u_{t_{0}},t_{0}^{2}v_{t_{0}})\leq\max_{t>0}I_{\beta}(t^{2}u_{t},t^{2}v_{t})=I_{\beta}(u,v)=c_{\beta},

which is a contradiction and this concludes the proof.
∎

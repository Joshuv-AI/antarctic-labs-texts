Novel mechanical debridement technique to assist in removal of deeply embedded degenerated esophageal metal stent: a case report
Authors: Biswa Ranjan Patra, Nitish Patwardhan, Vaibhav Padole, Souradeep Pal, Yash Harwani, Sidharth Harindranath, Akash Shukla
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Embedment of a self-expandable metal stent (SEMS) is a rare but serious adverse event, often due to granulation tissue overgrowth, with prolonged dwell time being a major risk factor. An 18-year-old girl with a history of corrosive esophageal stricture and SEMS placement 26 months earlier presented with progressive dysphagia. Endoscopy revealed a completely embedded stent with distal stenosis. Initial management with stent-in-stent technique using fully covered SEMSs and argon plasma coagulation (APC) failed to achieve tissue necrosis. Mechanical debridement was, therefore, undertaken using a novel approach with standard endoscopic accessories, given the unavailability of specialized devices. Circumferential APC was followed by superficial tissue scraping with biopsy forceps and deeper debridement with a distal attachment cap. After 2 sessions, the stent was fully exposed and removed safely. This case highlights that mechanical debridement with routine endoscopic tools can provide a safe, effective, and accessible solution for deeply embedded esophageal stents.

Introduction

Embedment of a self-expandable metal stent (SEMS) is an uncommon adverse event (AE), usually resulting from granulation tissue growth, with prolonged duration of stent placement being an important risk factor. Endoscopic removal of fully embedded stents can be challenging and carries a potentially high risk of perforation and bleeding.1 Existing methods of removal have used the stent-in-stent (SIS) technique,2 argon plasma coagulation (APC),3 cryoablation,4 and submucosal dissection5 with variable success. A case of powered endoscopic debridement (PED) has been described after failure of the SIS technique and APC, but its use may be limited by access to this specialized device.6 We report a novel method for removing a completely embedded esophageal stent using standard endoscopic accessories after the unsuccessful SIS technique and APC.

Case description

An 18-year-old girl with a history of corrosive esophageal stricture and covered SEMS placement 26 months earlier presented with progressive dysphagia. The stent had been placed at another center and was planned for removal after 6 weeks; however, the patient was lost to follow-up after achieving symptomatic relief. Documentation regarding the type and size of the stent was unavailable. Apart from this, there was no significant past medical, social, or family history, and physical examination findings were unremarkable. The SIS technique was attempted first with sequential placement of 2 fully covered SEMSs (FCSEMSs) of increasing diameter. An 18- × 120-mm FCSEMS (Niti-S Stent, Taewoong Medical, Seoul, South Korea) was first deployed and left in situ for 6 weeks, but it failed to achieve adequate pressure necrosis. Fluoroscopic imaging revealed a visible gap between the stents, suggesting insufficient radial force as the likely cause of failure. Consequently, a larger-diameter 25- × 135-mm FCSEMS SX-Ella Danis stent (Ella-CS, Hradec Kralove, Czech Republic) was subsequently placed, providing improved overlap and radial compression (Figs. 1B and C). This stent was left for 2 weeks, but it also failed to expose the embedded stent. Two interspersed sessions of mucosal ablation, performed 1 week apart using APC (forced, 80 W) were similarly ineffective. Mechanical debridement was therefore planned after a week, and in the absence of the PED device (EndoRotor [Interscope Medical, Inc, Worcester, Mass, USA]), an innovative approach was used with standard endoscopic accessories.

This novel method of mechanical debridement was performed using biopsy forceps and a distal attachment cap. The procedure was performed with the patient under general anesthesia and continuous carbon dioxide insufflation. The patient received intravenous antibiotic prophylaxis (cefuroxime and metronidazole) before the procedure.

After circumferential mucosal ablation with APC (forced, 80 W) using axial probes and an ESG-300 electrosurgical generator, along with an APC unit, and an APU-300 system (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan), initial debridement was performed with standard biopsy forceps of 2.2-mm cup diameter (Captura, Wilson-Cook Medical Inc, Winston-Salem, NC, USA). The open jaws were pressed against ablated necrotic tissue and advanced parallel to the mucosa, leading to effective scraping of tissue debris (Fig. 2A). This was carried out circumferentially in short segments along the length of the embedment. APC was then repeated, and deeper debridement was undertaken using a distal attachment cap (D-201-14,304, Olympus Medical Systems, Tokyo, Japan), applied tangentially while advancing the gastroscope to peel off residual granulation tissue (Fig. 2B). A minor AE in the form of mucosal oozing was encountered but was effectively controlled because debridement was alternated with APC. After 2 such sessions performed 48 hours apart, the stent was fully exposed (Figs. 2C and D). On inspection, the exposed stent showed a fractured end with several degenerated sharp strands. An overtube was, therefore, used to retrieve the stent in toto, followed by removal of residual degenerated wire fragments using rat-tooth grasping forceps (Figs. 3A and B). No major AEs occurred. A postprocedureal oral gastrograffin study showed no contrast leak, and the patient was subsequently started on oral feeds. At 2-week follow-up, endoscopy demonstrated complete mucosal healing with resolution of the stricture (Fig. 3C). The patient reported significant improvement in dysphagia and overall comfort shortly after the procedure. She expressed satisfaction with the minimally invasive nature of the treatment and relief at avoiding a major surgery. Having previously undergone multiple interventions with limited success, she appreciated the rapid recovery and restoration of oral intake after stent removal. Her feedback underscored the positive impact of this approach on both symptom relief and quality of life.

Discussion

In benign esophageal disease, prolonged FCSEMS placement is associated with serious AEs; thus, careful patient selection and timely removal are essential. The SIS technique is the most commonly reported method for the removal of embedded stents, but in this case, it failed despite sequential stent placement with increasing diameters.7 The likely reason was deep embedment due to prolonged stent dwell time (26 months). In such cases, mechanical debridement of tissue overgrowth is a logical rescue strategy.

The PED device has been used successfully for this purpose, but its limited availability and higher cost restrict its use.6 In our case, neither APC nor the SIS technique achieved adequate tissue necrosis. Mechanical debridement with open biopsy forceps for superficial removal, combined with a distal attachment cap for deeper dissection, proved effective. The embedded stent itself acted as a protective outer scaffold, guiding the depth of debridement and thereby minimizing the risk of transmural injury. Safety was further enhanced by performing debridement in short esophageal segments using open forceps jaws wider than the individual stent mesh cells.

The main limitation of this case is that it represents a single-patient experience with a novel mechanical debridement approach; therefore, its generalizability remains to be established. Nevertheless, this case highlights the potential feasibility of mechanical debridement using standard endoscopic tools as an alternative approach when conventional techniques fail. Broader application and future studies using this modality will help further define its role and safety in the management of embedded stent removal.

In conclusion, mechanical debridement using standard endoscopic accessories offers a safe, cost-effective, and widely accessible rescue strategy for the management of deeply embedded esophageal stents, particularly in cases where the SIS approach fails or specialized devices such as the PED device are unavailable.

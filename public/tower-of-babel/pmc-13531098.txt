Real-world Feasibility of Adjuvant Nivolumab for High-risk Upper Tract Urothelial Carcinoma After Radical Nephroureterectomy
Authors: MASAAKI YANISHI, JUN MATSUSHITA, AKIYO KITA, TAKAO MISHIMA, HIDEFUMI KINOSHITA
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/AimAdjuvant nivolumab is an important postoperative option for high-risk muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma, but upper tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC)-specific real-world evidence remains limited. This study evaluated oncologic outcomes, feasibility, and safety of adjuvant nivolumab after radical nephroureterectomy in patients with high-risk UTUC.Patients and MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 21 consecutive patients with high-risk UTUC who received adjuvant nivolumab at the Kansai Medical University group between April 2022 and March 2025. Metastasis-free survival (MFS), overall survival (OS), treatment completion, and treatment-related adverse events (AEs) were assessed. Survival was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method.ResultsThe median age was 76 years, and 14 patients (66.7%) had received neoadjuvant chemotherapy. The median follow-up from nivolumab initiation was 744 days. Median MFS and OS were not reached. The 12- and 24-month MFS rates were both 80.1%, while the 12- and 24-month OS rates were 90.0% and 84.4%, respectively. The median treatment duration was 34 weeks, and 10 patients (47.6%) completed the planned 48-week course. Any treatment-related AE occurred in nine patients (42.9%), and seven discontinued treatment because of grade 3 AEs.ConclusionThis early real-world study suggests that adjuvant nivolumab is feasible and may provide favorable short-term disease control in selected patients with high-risk UTUC after radical nephroureterectomy. These findings support its potential role as a postoperative option for selected patients, particularly when delivery of additional platinum-based therapy is difficult.

Introduction

Urothelial carcinoma (UC) comprises tumors arising from the bladder and upper urinary tract, including the renal pelvis and ureter. Upper tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC) is relatively uncommon worldwide, accounting for approximately 5-10% of all UC cases (1). However, the epidemiology of UTUC differs by region, and real-world reports from East Asia suggest that UTUC (2) may account for a relatively larger fraction of UC than in Western populations. This geographic variation underscores the need for population-specific evidence.

Radical nephroureterectomy (RNU) with bladder cuff excision remains the standard treatment for localized and locally advanced UTUC. Nevertheless, recurrence remains common in patients with adverse pathological features, and the optimal perioperative systemic strategy is still evolving. In bladder cancer, perioperative regimens have advanced beyond conventional cisplatin-based chemotherapy; the phase III NIAGARA trial (3) demonstrated improved outcomes with perioperative durvalumab plus neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and additional perioperative trials are ongoing. In contrast, UTUC-specific evidence remains limited, and it is unclear to what extent bladder cancer-driven developments can be extrapolated to UTUC.

Adjuvant nivolumab demonstrated a disease-free survival benefit over placebo in patients with high-risk muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma after radical surgery in the phase III CheckMate 274 trial (4), establishing adjuvant immunotherapy as an important option in clinical practice. However, the benefit of adjuvant nivolumab in UTUC remains less certain. In the Japanese subgroup analysis of CheckMate 274 (5), the treatment effect appeared greater in urothelial bladder carcinoma than in renal pelvic or ureteral tumors. The reasons for this apparent heterogeneity remain unclear, but may include limited case numbers, differences in embryologic origin and molecular features, variation in tumor microenvironment and immune response, and distinct clinical characteristics and recurrence patterns in UTUC.

Recent Japanese and Asian real-world studies have begun to address this gap. A multicenter Japanese study showed increased use of adjuvant immunotherapy after 2022 in patients with pathological high-risk urothelial carcinoma after radical surgery and provided exploratory data on UTUC outcomes (6), while another multicenter cohort study clarified postoperative outcomes among patients with UTUC who met adjuvant immunotherapy candidate criteria (7). A retrospective comparative study from Asia also suggested favorable postoperative outcomes with adjuvant nivolumab in high-risk urothelial carcinoma (8). However, UTUC-specific real-world evidence remains limited, and the feasibility, safety, and oncologic outcomes of adjuvant nivolumab after RNU have not been fully characterized. Therefore, we conducted a retrospective real-world analysis of patients with high-risk UTUC who received adjuvant nivolumab at the Kansai Medical University group of hospitals and evaluated oncologic outcomes and treatment feasibility in routine clinical practice.

Patients and Methods

Study design and patients. This retrospective real-world study included consecutive patients with high-risk UTUC who received adjuvant nivolumab at the Kansai Medical University group of hospitals between April 2022 and March 2025. Clinical, pathological, and treatment-related data were extracted from electronic medical records. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional review board of Kansai Medical University (approval number 2018036); informed consent was waived because of the retrospective design.

Eligible patients had histologically confirmed UTUC of the renal pelvis and/or ureter and had undergone RNU with curative intent. Patients were considered high risk on the basis of postoperative pathological features, consistent with criteria commonly applied in CheckMate 274-based clinical practice (e.g., pT3-pT4a and/or pN+ for patients without neoadjuvant chemotherapy; ypT2-ypT4a and/or ypN+ for patients with neoadjuvant chemotherapy). Baseline variables included age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status, smoking history, primary tumor location, clinical and pathological stage, variant histology, renal function parameters, and neoadjuvant chemotherapy status.

Treatment. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy was administered at the discretion of the treating physician, primarily according to renal function, age, and tolerability. Regimens consisted of gemcitabine plus cisplatin or gemcitabine plus carboplatin. The intended number of cycles was generally four, although the final number was individualized on the basis of patient factors and adverse events. Adjuvant nivolumab was administered according to routine clinical practice in Japan at 240 mg every two weeks or 480 mg every four weeks, with a planned duration of 12 months. Treatment was discontinued upon non-intravesical disease recurrence, unacceptable toxicity, or physician-patient decision. Treatment completion was defined as completion of 48 weeks of adjuvant nivolumab therapy.

Outcomes. The oncologic endpoints were MFS and OS. MFS was defined as the time from initiation of adjuvant nivolumab to the first documented non-intravesical recurrence or death, whichever occurred first. OS was defined as the time from initiation of adjuvant nivolumab to death from any cause; patients alive at the last follow-up were censored at that date. Feasibility was assessed by treatment completion. Safety endpoints included the incidence and type of treatment-related adverse events and adverse event-related discontinuation. Adverse events were graded according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0.

Statistical analysis. Continuous variables are presented as the median (range) and categorical variables as number (percentage). MFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, with 12- and 24-month survival estimates and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Group comparisons (neoadjuvant chemotherapy yes vs. no; adverse event present vs. absent) were performed using the log-rank test. A two-sided p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics(Table I). Twenty-one patients were included. The median age was 76 years (range=64-85 years), and 11 patients (52.4%) were women. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status was 0 in 18 patients (85.7%) and 1 in three patients (14.3%). A history of smoking was present in nine patients (42.9%). The primary tumor site was the renal pelvis in 12 patients (57.1%) and the ureter in nine (42.9%). Histologically, 16 patients (76.2%) had pure urothelial carcinoma and five (23.8%) had variant histology. Clinically, 13 patients (61.9%) had cT3-4 disease and five (23.8%) had clinically positive lymph nodes. Fourteen patients (66.7%) received neoadjuvant chemotherapy, including gemcitabine plus cisplatin in eight patients and gemcitabine plus carboplatin in six. Pathological stage was ypT2 in three patients (14.3%), ypT3 in 11 (52.4%), pT3 in six (28.6%), and pT4 in one (4.8%); five patients (23.8%) had pathologically positive lymph nodes. The median pre- and post-nephroureterectomy estimated glomerular filtration rate values were 51.2 and 35.5 ml/min/1.73 m2, respectively. The median follow-up from nivolumab initiation was 744 days (range=213-1,393 days).

Safety. Any treatment-related adverse event occurred in nine patients (42.9%). The most common adverse events were hypothyroidism (n=2), rash (n=2), and interstitial pneumonitis (n=2), followed by immune-related colitis (n=1), adrenal insufficiency (n=1), and fatigue (n=1). Treatment discontinuation because of adverse events was observed in seven patients. All adverse event-related discontinuations were grade 3. Two patients developed grade 2 rash and continued nivolumab with oral corticosteroids without treatment discontinuation.

Oncologic outcomes. During follow-up, four patients (19.0%) experienced an MFS event and three patients (14.3%) died. Median MFS and OS were not reached. The 12- and 24-month MFS rates were both 80.1% (95%CI=55.2-92.1). The 12- and 24-month OS rates were 90.0% (95%CI=65.6-97.4) and 84.4% (95%CI=58.9-94.7), respectively (Figure 1).

Discussion

In this retrospective real-world study of high-risk UTUC treated with adjuvant nivolumab after RNU, we observed preliminary evidence of favorable short-term disease control and acceptable treatment feasibility. Median MFS and OS were not reached, the 12- and 24-month MFS rates were both 80.1%, and the 12- and 24-month OS rates were 90.0% and 84.4%, respectively. Treatment completion at 48 weeks was achieved in 47.6% of patients. Taken together, these findings suggest that adjuvant nivolumab is a feasible postoperative treatment option for selected patients with high-risk UTUC in daily practice, although treatment continuation remains a clinically relevant issue.

Our findings should be interpreted in the context of CheckMate 274, which established adjuvant nivolumab as a standard option for high-risk muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma after radical surgery. In the 3-year extended follow-up analysis, nivolumab continued to show durable benefits in disease-free survival, non-urothelial tract recurrence-free survival, and distant metastasis-free survival, with an interim overall survival hazard ratio of 0.76 in the intention-to-treat population (9). In the Japanese subgroup analysis, the median disease-free survival was 29.67 months with nivolumab versus 9.72 months with placebo, and efficacy and safety were considered consistent with those of the overall population. Although those analyses were not UTUC specific, they support the biological and clinical rationale for postoperative nivolumab in Asian practice.

Importantly, emerging UTUC-focused real-world studies are broadly consistent with our findings. Minato et al. (10) reported one of the first UTUC-only experiences with adjuvant nivolumab after RNU, in which 63.6% of patients completed the planned 12-month treatment, while the 2-year MFS and OS rates were 70.1% and 79.5%, respectively. Likewise, Chen et al. (2) analyzed a Taiwanese cohort of 42 patients treated with adjuvant nivolumab, of whom 85.7% had UTUC, and reported a 24-month disease-free survival rate of 68.4%; notably, the 24-month disease-free survival in the UTUC subgroup was 73.1%. Together with the present results, these studies suggest that adjuvant nivolumab can yield favorable real-world postoperative outcomes in UTUC despite the uncertainty raised by subgroup analyses of randomized trials.

From the standpoint of feasibility, our completion rate of 47.6% was numerically lower than the 63.6% reported by Minato et al. (10) and the 55.0% reported in the Asian comparative study by Nakagawa et al. (8), whereas our rate of adverse event-related discontinuation appeared numerically higher. In our cohort, treatment-related adverse events occurred in 42.9% of patients, and seven patients discontinued nivolumab because of grade 3 toxicities, although two patients with grade 2 rash were able to continue treatment with oral corticosteroids. By contrast, the Taiwanese real-world study reported treatment-related adverse events in 40.5% of patients and mostly mild events, and the Japanese subgroup analysis of CheckMate 274 (5) reported grade 3-4 treatment-related adverse events in 25.9% of nivolumab-treated patients. These differences likely reflect small sample sizes, different endpoint definitions and monitoring, and heterogeneity of postoperative real-world populations rather than a clear discrepancy in drug tolerability.

Another important issue is how adjuvant nivolumab should be positioned relative to adjuvant platinum-based chemotherapy in UTUC. The phase III POUT trial (11) established gemcitabine-platinum chemotherapy as a standard postoperative option for patients with pT2-T4 or pN+ non-metastatic UTUC after RNU, demonstrating improved long-term outcomes versus surveillance, including a 5-year disease-free survival of 62% versus 45% and a 5-year overall survival of 66% versus 57%. Importantly, treatment effects were generally consistent across disease stage and planned platinum regimen, and carboplatin was incorporated for patients with reduced postoperative renal function. Nevertheless, many patients with UTUC are elderly, have impaired renal function after RNU, or have already received neoadjuvant chemotherapy, which may limit the feasibility or desirability of additional platinum-based treatment in routine practice. In this context, adjuvant nivolumab may represent a clinically meaningful postoperative option for selected patients with pathological high-risk disease. Risk stratification is also important when considering postoperative treatment strategies for UTUC. Shibata et al. (12) proposed a novel preoperative immune prognostic index for patients undergoing RNU, highlighting that host inflammatory and immune status may contribute to postoperative outcomes. Although their study did not evaluate adjuvant nivolumab, such prognostic frameworks may help identify patients who require intensified postoperative surveillance and careful consideration of systemic adjuvant therapy.

Study limitations. First, this was a retrospective study with a small sample size and no control group. Second, the number of oncologic events was limited, precluding robust multivariable analyses. In addition, although variant histology was present in 23.8% of patients, the small sample size precluded subgroup analysis according to histological subtype. Third, our primary oncologic endpoint was MFS, defined using non-intravesical recurrence or death, whereas other real-world reports have used disease-free survival or handled intravesical recurrence differently. In the UTUC-only study by Minato et al., for example, the authors presented both disease-free survival and MFS and argued that MFS may better reflect the efficacy of systemic therapy in UTUC because intravesical recurrence does not necessarily indicate systemic failure. This concept is also partly supported by the long-term POUT results (11), in which adjuvant chemotherapy improved disease-free survival, MFS, and overall survival but had no apparent impact on second resection of primary bladder tumors. In addition, Yamaguchi et al. (13) reported that adjuvant systemic chemotherapy after RNU was not significantly associated with a reduction in intravesical recurrence, further supporting the concept that intravesical recurrence and systemic recurrence should be interpreted separately when evaluating the effect of postoperative systemic therapy in UTUC. Therefore, our focus on MFS may be reasonable from the standpoint of systemic disease control, although direct comparison with prior studies requires caution because endpoint definitions are not uniform across reports. Finally, because follow-up remains relatively short, particularly for overall survival, the present findings should be interpreted as supportive real-world evidence of feasibility and favorable short-term outcomes rather than definitive proof of comparative efficacy.

Conclusion

This early real-world study suggests that adjuvant nivolumab is feasible and may provide favorable short-term disease control in selected patients with high-risk UTUC after RNU. These findings support its potential role as a postoperative option, particularly in settings where additional platinum-based therapy is difficult to deliver. Larger UTUC-specific multicenter studies with longer follow-up are warranted.

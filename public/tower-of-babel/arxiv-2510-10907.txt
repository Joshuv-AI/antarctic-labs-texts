A Continuum Beck-type Theorem for Hyperplanes
Paige Bright, Alexander Ortiz, Dmitrii Zakharov

Abstract
. We prove a sharp continuum Beck-type theorem for hyperplanes. Our work is inspired by foundational work of Beck on the discrete problem, as well as refinements due to Do and Lund. The inductive proof uses recent breakthrough results in projection theory by Orponen–Shmerkin–Wang and Ren, who proved continuum Beck-type theorems for lines in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} and ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
28A75, 28A78

1.
Introduction

In 1983, József Beck
[
Bec83
]
studied the following discrete problem about points and lines in Euclidean space. Let
\mathcal{P}^{1}(X)
denote the set of affine lines spanned by at least two points of
X
. Given a finite set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|X|=N
, study the size of the set
\mathcal{P}^{1}(X)
. Beck proved that a dichotomy holds: either about
N
points lie on a common line, or there are about
N^{2}
distinct lines in
\mathcal{P}^{1}(X)
.

Recently, Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang
[
OSW24
]
proved a continuum analog of Beck’s theorem for lines in the plane, which was soon after extended to lines in higher dimensions by Ren
[
Ren23
]
. Ren showed that given
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
Borel, either

(1)

There exists an
m
-dimensional affine subspace
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\dim(X\setminus F)<\dim X
, or

(2)

The set
\mathcal{P}^{1}(X)
, a subset of the affine Grassmannian of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, satisfies

\dim\mathcal{P}^{1}(X)\geq 2\min\{\dim X,m\}.

Here and throughout the paper,
\dim A
refers to the Hausdorff dimension of
A
. We refer the reader to Mattila’s book
[
Mat99
]
for standard facts about Hausdorff dimension and properties of the affine Grassmannian.

In addition to studying the set of lines that contain at least two points of a given finite set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Beck also studied hyperplanes of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
spanned by
n
affinely independent points of
X
. For
1\leq k\leq n-1
, let
\mathcal{P}^{k}(X)
denote the set of
k
-planes spanned by
k+1
affinely independent points of
X
. In his original paper
[
Bec83
]
, Beck showed that for any finite set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, either there exists a hyperplane that contains about
|X|
-many points of
X
, or
|\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)|\geq c|X|^{n}
, for some dimensional constant
c
. The assumption that no hyperplane can contains a large fraction of the points of
X
is a
non-concentration
hypothesis about the set
X
.

In 2016, Thao Do
[
Do20
]
and Ben Lund
[
Lun16
]
refined Beck’s theorem for hyperplanes by sharpening the non-concentration hypothesis of
X
. Do showed that for any
\varepsilon>0
there is some
\gamma_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for finite
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, either

(1)

There exists a collection of affine subspaces
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose union contains at least
(1-\varepsilon)|X|
points of
X
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\dim F_{i}\leq n-1
, or

(2)

|\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)|\geq\gamma_{\varepsilon}|X|^{n}.

In this paper, we establish a continuum analog of Beck’s theorem for hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Either a Borel set
X
is
non-concentrated
, or else the set of hyperplanes spanned by
X
has maximal dimension.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
, and let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Borel. We say that
X
is
non-concentrated
, or simply
\mathbf{NC}
, if for any
r\geq 1
and any collection of affine subspaces
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\dim F_{i}\leq n-1
, we have

\dim\Big(X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}\Big)=\dim X.

Heuristically, a Borel set that is
\mathbf{NC}
corresponds to a finite set disobeying (1) in the dichotomy of Do’s Beck-type theorem. Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Borel and
\mathbf{NC}
. Then,

\dim\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)\geq n\min\{\dim X,1\}.

The lower bound on the dimension of
\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)
is sharp, and the
NC
assumption is sharp in the following sense. If
X
is contained in a union of pairwise disjoint flats
\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}
where
\sum\dim F_{i}\leq n-1
,
\dim X>0
, and
\dim X\cap F_{i}=\dim X
for all
i
, then

\dim\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)\leq(n-2)\min\{\dim X,1\}.

To see this, let
V_{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}
be a hyperplane spanned by
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in X
, and consider

m_{i}=\#\{x_{j}:x_{j}\in F_{i}\}.

Flats
F_{i}
are pairwise disjoint, so
\sum m_{i}=n
. Since
\sum\dim F_{i}\leq n-1
, there exists a flat
F_{i}
such that
m_{i}>\dim F_{i}
. Let
F
be a flat of codimension
q
, and denote by
\mathcal{P}_{F}^{n-1}(X)
the set of
(n-1)
-planes of
X
spanned by
X
containing
F
. It follows that

\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)\subset\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{P}^{n-1}_{F_{i}}(X).

Therefore,

\dim\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)\leq\max_{i}(n-(\dim F_{i}+1))\leq n-2.

Consider the surjective function

\phi:(X\setminus F)^{q-1}\to\bigcup_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{P}_{F}^{n-j}(X)

defined by
\phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{q-1})=\langle F,x_{1},\dots,x_{q-1}\rangle
. The function
\phi
is well defined and locally Lipschitz. Therefore,
\dim\mathcal{P}_{F}^{n-1}(X)\leq(q-1)\dim X
, and

\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)\leq(n-2)\dim X.

1.1.
Connections to projection theory

Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\pi_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}\to\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be the
radial projection onto
x
given by

\pi_{x}(y)=\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}.

In
[
OSW24
]
, Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang proved the following radial projection theorem:

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem 1.1,
[
OSW24
]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a (non-empty) Borel set which is not contained on any line. Then, for every Borel set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim X,\dim Y,1\}.

Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang used Theorem
1.3
to obtain the sharp continuum Beck-type theorem for lines in the plane. Orponen and Shmerkin
[
OS23
]
used Theorem
1.3
to prove the conjectured Furstenberg set estimates in the plane for AD regular sets. Later, Ren and Wang
[
RW23
]
proved the full Furstenberg set conjecture, using the AD regular case in an important way.

In
[
Ren23
]
, Ren generalized the Orponen–Shmerkin–Wang’s radial projection theorem to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem 1.1,
[
Ren23
]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a (non-empty) Borel set which is not contained in any
m
-plane. Then, for every Borel set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim X,\dim Y,m\}

Theorem
1.4
was used to improve the best upper bounds for the Falconer distance set problem in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3

[
Du+23
]
.

In our paper, we use discretized versions of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
in an inductive scheme to prove a more quantitative version of Theorem
1.2
(see Theorem
6.3
) which we use to deduce Theorem
1.2
. The structure of our proof, and the construction of
thin-planes graphs
is geometrically motivated by the notion of
thin-tubes
introduced by Shmerkin–Wang
[
SW25
]
and developed by Orponen–Shmerkin–Wang
[
OSW24
]
and Ren
[
Ren23
]
.

1.2.
Overview of the proof

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Borel and
NC
, and let
\mu
be a
s
-Frostman measure supported on
X
with
0<s<\dim X
.

Our approach to this problem is partly inspired by the work of Do
[
Do20
]
, who refined the geometric non-concentration condition in Beck’s dichotomy. Our strategy is to begin by identifying
irreducible
pieces of the measure
\mu
.

Definition 1.5
.

We say that a measure
\mu
supported on an affine subspace
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
irreducible in
V
if
\mu(V^{\prime})=0
for any proper affine subspace
V^{\prime}\subset V
.

Case 1.
If
\mu
is irreducible on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we will have a direct argument that
\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)
has the correct dimension using Ren’s radial projection theorem
[
Ren23
]
. In particular, we prove the following.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Borel with
\dim X>0
, and let
0<s<\dim X
. Given an irreducible (in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
)
s
-Frostman measure
\mu
supported on
X
, it follows that

\dim\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)\geq n\min\{s,1\}.

In particular, if there exists such a
\mu
for all
0<s<\dim X
, then sending
s\nearrow\dim X
proves our main result. We refer to this as the
irreducible case
of our continuum Beck-type theorem.

Case 2.
If
V
is a
proper
affine subspace in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mu|_{V}
is irreducible on
V
, it follows by Lemma
1.6
that
X\cap V
spans a set of hyperplanes in
V
of the correct dimension. (If
\dim V=1
, we mean that
X\cap V
has the appropriate dimension.)

Since
X
is
NC
, we know that
\dim(X\setminus V)=\dim X
. Thus, we can iterate through the two cases described above for the set
X\setminus V
. This gives us an algorithmic method for finding a collection of affine subspaces supporting irreducible measures, which we record in the following lemma.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Borel and
\mathbf{NC}
with
\dim X>0
. Then for any
0<s<\dim X
, there exist a collection of affine subspaces
V_{1},\dotsc,V_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
s
-Frostman measures
\mu_{i}
supported on
X\cap V_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, such that

(i)

Each
\mu_{i}
is irreducible in
V_{i}
and
\mu_{i}(X)>0
.

(ii)

The supports
\supp\mu_{i}
are pairwise disjoint.

(iii)

For any affine subspaces
F_{1},\dotsc,F_{r}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}V_{i}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}F_{j}
we have
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\dim F_{j}\geq n
.

Let
n_{i}=\dim V_{i}
. Notice then that applying Lemma
1.6
to each
V_{i}
, we see that
\dim\mathcal{P}^{n_{i}-1}(X\cap V_{i})\geq n_{i}\min\{s,1\}
for any
0<s<\dim X
. Again, recall here that
\mathcal{P}^{0}(X\cap V_{i})=X\cap V_{i}
in the case where
n_{i}=1
. From here, we will “patch together” codimension 1-planes from each
V_{i}
to build hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The guiding idea is to choose
n_{i}
affinely independent points from each
X\cap V_{i}
so that their union spans a hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In practice, not every such selection works: the chosen points may span a space of dimension smaller than
n-1
, or they may already fill all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if
\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\geq n.
To avoid these issues, we will take care to ensure that our collection of measures
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r}
is in what we call
c
-stable position
, so that, for example, the angle between any two distinct flats
V_{i},V_{j}
is bounded below by
\theta(c)>0
, and (say) the dimension of intersection
\dim(V_{i}\cap V_{j})
is what one would expect under generic conditions. The use of
c
-stable position will facilitate the quantitative argument of Section
7
.

1.3.
Paper outline

We begin by making use of the
NC
condition on
X
, and in particular prove Lemma
1.7
in Section
2
, which allows us to find a collection of affine subspaces
V_{i}
and corresponding irreducible
s
-Frostman measures
\mu_{i}
for any
0<s<\dim X
. The setup is similar to that in the radial projection work of Orponen–Shmerkin–Wang
[
OSW24
]
and Ren
[
Ren23
]
, which we unpack in Section
3
. We give background on the notion of
thin tubes
, and describe a generalization we refer to as
thin
k
-planes
. We prove Lemma
1.6
in Section
4.1
by showing that irreducible measures span thin hyperplanes.

Next, we “patch together” the measures on each
V_{i}
to form hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Some computations will be justified through the use of the dimension-sum formula of linear algebra: for linear subspaces
V,W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\dim(V+W)=\dim V+\dim W-\dim V\cap W.

The assumption that our collection of measures
\{\mu_{i}\}
are in
c
-stable position in
\{V_{i}\}
will guarantee that various “joins”
V+W
and “meets”
V\cap W
have the expected dimension. The reduction to
c
-stable position is carried out in Section
5
.

Furthermore, we will be able to assume that our collection of flats is
minimal
in the following sense.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}
be a collection of affine flats in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, let
[k]=\{1,\dots,k\}
, and given
J\subset[k]
let

F_{J}:=\mathrm{aff}(F_{J}:j\in J),

the smallest dimensional affine flat containing
F_{j}
for all
j\in J
. We say that a collection of affine flats
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
is
minimal
if
\dim F_{[k]}=n\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\dim F_{j}
and
\dim F_{J}\geq\sum_{j\in J}\dim F_{j}
for any proper
J\subsetneq[k]
.

The reduction to flats that are minimal is done in Section
6
. The main work that remains is dealing with this minimal case, which is the entirety of Section
7
.

1.4.
Notation

We denote by
\aff(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
or
\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\rangle
the (at most)
k
-plane spanned by points
x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For
k\leq n-1
, we denote by

\mathcal{S}_{k}=\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k+1}:x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\ \text{belong to a common $(k-1)$-plane}\}.

By
\mathcal{A}(V,k)
we denote the set of affine
k
-dimensional subspaces of
V
, and
\mathcal{A}(n,k)=\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n},k)
, with similar notation
\mathcal{G}(V,k),\mathcal{G}(n,k)
for linear
k
-dimensional subspaces.

2.
Decomposing into irreducible pieces

In this section, we prove Lemma
1.7
, which we restate here.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Borel and
\mathbf{NC}
with
\dim X>0
. Then for any
0<s<\dim X
, there exist a collection of affine subspaces
V_{1},\dotsc,V_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
s
-Frostman measures
\mu_{i}
supported on
X\cap V_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, such that

(i)

Each
\mu_{i}
is irreducible in
V_{i}
and
\mu_{i}(X)>0
.

(ii)

The supports
\supp\mu_{i}
are pairwise disjoint.

(iii)

For any affine subspaces
F_{1},\dotsc,F_{r}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}V_{i}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}F_{j}
we have
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\dim F_{j}\geq n
.

Recall that we say that a Borel set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
NC
if

\dim\Big(X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}F_{i}\Big)=\dim X,

for any affine flats
F_{1},\dotsc,F_{j}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{j}\dim F_{i}\leq n-1
. Additionally, recall that we say that a measure
\mu
supported on an affine subspace
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
irreducible in
V
if
\mu(V^{\prime})=0
for any proper affine subspace
V^{\prime}\subset V
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
0<s<\dim X
. We say that a flat
V
is
large
if there exists an irreducible (in
V
)
s
-Frostman measure
\mu
on
V\cap X
with
\mu(X)>0
.

For a collection of flats
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{1},\dotsc,V_{m}\}
and a partition
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
of
[m]
, define

c(\mathcal{V};I_{1},\dotsc,I_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\dim\aff(V_{t}:t\in I_{i}),

where
\aff(E_{i}:i\in I)
means the affine span of a collection of flats, i.e., the smallest affine subspace containing all the sets
E_{i}
for
i\in I
. Let us define the
cost
of a set of
m
flats to be the minimal dimension sum as we vary partitions of
[m]
:

(1)

c(\mathcal{V})=\min_{\{I_{1},\dotsc,I_{j}\}}c(\mathcal{V};I_{1},\dotsc,I_{j}),

where the minimum is taken over all partitions of
[m]
. If a partition
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
of
[m]
achieves the cost of
\mathcal{V}
, then we say that this partition is
minimizing
.

We will construct a family of large flats
\mathcal{V}
and corresponding measures
\mu_{i}
inductively, using the cost to ensure that our inductive construction eventually stops. Let
\mathcal{V}_{0}=\emptyset
(in this case we have
c(\mathcal{V}_{0})=0
). Given a collection of
m
-many large flats
\mathcal{V}_{m}=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{m}\}
and corresponding measures
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
(in case
m=0
, we do not define
\mu_{0}
), if
c(\mathcal{V}_{m})\geq n
then stop. Otherwise, we define
\mathcal{V}_{m+1}
as follows.

By assumption, we have
c(\mathcal{V}_{m})=c\leq n-1
and there exists some partition
\{I_{1},\dotsc,I_{j}\}
of
[m]
such that
c(\mathcal{V}_{m};I_{1},\dotsc,I_{j})=c
. Define
F_{i}=\aff(V_{t}:t\in I_{i})
and
note that given
c\leq n-1
and
X
is
\mathbf{NC}
, we have

\dim\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}F_{i}\right)=\dim X.

By Frostman’s lemma, there exists an
s
-Frostman probability measure
\mu
with

(2)

\supp\mu\subset X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}F_{i}.

By shrinking the support of
\mu
if necessary, we can ensure that
\supp\mu
is separated from
\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}F_{i}
. Let
V_{m+1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a subspace of minimal dimension such that
\mu(V_{m+1})>0
. Note that since
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})=1
, such a minimal subspace necessarily exists. Then it is clear that
V_{m+1}
is large and
\mu|_{V_{m+1}}:=\mu_{m+1}
is the corresponding irreducible Frostman measure on
V_{m+1}
. Note that
\mu_{m+1}
has pairwise disjoint support from any previously constructed measures
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
obtained in this way by (
2
). Let
\mathcal{V}_{m+1}=\mathcal{V}_{m}\cup\{V_{m+1}\}
and proceed inductively.

We claim that this process eventually stops, i.e., eventually
c(\mathcal{V}_{m})\geq n
. Note that
c(\mathcal{V}_{m+1})\geq c(\mathcal{V}_{m})
by construction. Indeed, given a partition
\{I^{\prime}_{1},\dotsc,I^{\prime}_{j}\}
of
[m+1]
we can define a partition of
[m]
by setting
I_{i}=I^{\prime}_{i}\setminus\{m+1\}
and noting that

c(\mathcal{V}_{m};I_{1},\dotsc,I_{j})\leq c(\mathcal{V}_{m+1};I^{\prime}_{1},\dotsc,I^{\prime}_{j}).

Now we need to rule out that we “get stuck” on some fixed value
c(\mathcal{V}_{m})=c\leq n-1
. To do so, define

N(\mathcal{V}_{m})=\#\{\text{partitions $\pi$ of $[m]$}:c(\mathcal{V}_{m};\pi)=c(\mathcal{V}_{m})\},

i.e., the number of partitions of
[m]
achieving the cost of
\mathcal{V}_{m}
. Note that this number is finite, and furthermore, by definition of
c(\mathcal{V}_{m})
,
N(\mathcal{V}_{m})\geq 1
for all
m\geq 1
.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that we do “get stuck” on some value of
c\leq n-1
, i.e., the algorithmic process has given us a countably infinite set of collections
\{\mathcal{V}_{i}\}_{i\geq m}
such that
c(\mathcal{V}_{i})=c
for all
i\geq m
. We then show that
N(\mathcal{V}_{i})
is a strictly decreasing sequence, i.e.,

N(\mathcal{V}_{i+1})<N(\mathcal{V}_{i}),\quad\forall i\geq m.

This will give a contradiction, as
N(\mathcal{V}_{m})
is finite, and for all
i\geq 1
,
N(\mathcal{V}_{i})\geq 1
.

Fix an arbitrary
i\geq m
. Firstly, notice that given an arbitrary partition
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
of
[i]
, we may consider the partition of
[i+1]
given by
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j},\{i+1\}\}
. For such a partition, we have

\displaystyle c(\mathcal{V}_{i+1};I_{1},\dots,I_{j},\{i+1\})

\displaystyle=c(\mathcal{V}_{i};I_{1},\dots,I_{j})+\dim V_{i+1}

\displaystyle\geq c(\mathcal{V}_{i})+1>c.

In other words, any partition of
[i+1]
which contains the set
\{i+1\}
does
not
minimize the cost of
\mathcal{V}_{i+1}
, and hence does not contribute to
N(\mathcal{V}_{i+1})
.

Furthermore, suppose that
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
is a partition of
[i]
such that

c(\mathcal{V}_{i};I_{1},\dots,I_{j})>c.

Suppose that we add the element
i+1
to one of the sets
I_{\ell}
for some
1\leq\ell\leq j
. We will call this an
extension
of the partition
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
. Then, it follows that

c(\mathcal{V}_{i+1};I_{1},\dots,I_{\ell}\cup\{i+1\},\dots,I_{j})\geq c(\mathcal{V}_{i};I_{1},\dots,I_{\ell},\dots,I_{j})>c.

Hence, any extension of a non-minimizing partition of
\mathcal{V}_{i}
is non-minimizing for the collection
\mathcal{V}_{i+1}
, so it cannot contribute to
N(\mathcal{V}_{i+1})
either.

Therefore, the only partitions which contribute to
N(\mathcal{V}_{i+1})
are those which are extensions of partitions which achieve the cost of
\mathcal{V}_{i}
.
Thus, the proof that
N(\mathcal{V}_{i+1})<N(\mathcal{V}_{i})
reduces to the following claim.

Claim 2.2
.

a)

For any partition
\{I_{1},\dotsc,I_{j}\}
of
[i]
such that
c(\mathcal{V}_{i};I_{1},\dotsc,I_{j})=c
, there exists at most one extension of this partition to a partition
\{I_{1}^{\prime},\dots,I_{j}^{\prime}\}
of
[i+1]
with
c(\mathcal{V}_{i+1};I^{\prime}_{1},\dotsc,I^{\prime}_{j})=c
. Here, again, extension means that we simply add the element
i+1
to one of
I_{\ell}
for
1\leq\ell\leq j
.

b)

Furthermore, there exists at least one minimizing partition
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
of
[i]
such that there are no minimizing extensions of this partition.

Proof of Claim
2.2
.

Note that the trivial partition of
[i]
, namely
\{[i]\}
consisting of only one set, clearly has at most one (potentially non-minimizing) extension. Therefore, we only need to consider partitions
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
of
[i]
where
j\geq 2
.

We begin with proving part a). Consider a partition
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
of
[i]
, where
j\geq 2
, and
c(\mathcal{V}_{i};I_{1},\dots,I_{j})=c
. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that there exists a pair of indices
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
such that both extensions

I_{1},\dotsc,I_{\ell}\cup\{i+1\},\dotsc,I_{j}\text{ and }I_{1},\dotsc,I_{\ell^{\prime}}\cup\{i+1\},\dotsc,I_{j}

are minimizing.
This is equivalent to saying that

V_{i+1}\subset\aff(V_{t};t\in I_{\ell})\text{ and }V_{i+1}\subset\aff(V_{t};t\in I_{\ell^{\prime}}).

Since
s>0
, and
V_{i+1}
supports an
s
-Frostman measure, we have
\dim V_{i+1}\geq 1
. So it follows that

\dim\aff(V_{t};t\in I_{\ell}\cup I_{\ell^{\prime}})\leq\dim\aff(V_{t};t\in I_{\ell})+\dim\aff(V_{t};t\in I_{\ell^{\prime}})-1.

Since
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
is a partition of
[i]
, so is

\{I_{\ell}\cup I_{\ell^{\prime}},I_{1},\dots,\widehat{I_{\ell}},\dots,\widehat{I_{\ell^{\prime}}},\dots,I_{j}\},

where the notation
\widehat{\bullet}
means that the element
\bullet
is removed from the list. Therefore,

c(\mathcal{V}_{i};I_{\ell}\cup I_{\ell^{\prime}},I_{1},\dotsc,\widehat{I_{\ell}},\dotsc,\widehat{I_{\ell^{\prime}}},\dots,I_{j})<c(\mathcal{V}_{i};I_{1},\dots,I_{\ell},\dots,I_{\ell^{\prime}},\dots,I_{j})=c,

which contradicts the minimality of
c=c(\mathcal{V}_{i})
. This shows that for every minimizing partition of
\mathcal{V}_{i}
, there exists at most one minimizing extension of said partition.

Now we will show part b) of Claim
2.2
: that there exists a minimizing partition
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
of
[i]
with no minimizing extensions. Indeed, let
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
be the partition of
[i]
which was used to define
V_{i+1}
.

Recall that we found
V_{i+1}
in such a way that

V_{i+1}\not\subset F_{\ell}=\aff(V_{t};t\in I_{\ell}),\text{ for any }1\leq\ell\leq j.

Therefore, it must be the case that for all
1\leq\ell\leq j
,

\dim\aff(V_{t};t\in I_{\ell}\cup\{i+1\})>\dim\aff(V_{t};t\in I_{\ell}).

That is, any extension
\{I_{1}^{\prime},\dots,I_{j}^{\prime}\}
of the partition
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{j}\}
must satisfy

c(\mathcal{V}_{i+1};I_{1}^{\prime},\dots,I_{j}^{\prime})>c(\mathcal{V}_{i};I_{1},\dots,I_{j})=c.

This concludes the proof of part b), and of Claim
2.2
.
∎

Claim
2.2
contradicts the assumption that there was a countably infinite sequence
\{\mathcal{V}_{i}\}_{i=m}^{\infty}
such that
c(\mathcal{V}_{i})=c
for all
i\geq m
. It follows that the value of
c(\mathcal{V}_{i})
must eventually increase. Additionally, since the algorithm terminates if
c\geq n
, it follows that the algorithm terminates after running it a finite number of times.

We let
\mathcal{V}_{m}=\{V_{1},\dotsc,V_{m}\}
be a final collection of subspaces with corresponding
s
-Frostman measures
\{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}\}
obtained at the termination of the algorithm.

By construction, the measures
\mu_{i}
are irreducible in
V_{i}
, and they have pairwise disjoint supports, which is parts (i) and (ii) of Lemma
2.1
. We now verify that property (iii) holds for
\mathcal{V}_{m}=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{m}\}
. To see this, suppose that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}V_{i}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}F_{j}
. The reader can verify that for each
i
, there is some
j(i)
such that
V_{i}\subset F_{j(i)}
. Define a function
j\colon[m]\to[r]
by sending each
i\in[m]
to a corresponding
j(i)\in[r]
in this way, and let
S=\mathrm{image}(j)
. By definition,
\{j^{-1}(x):x\in S\}
is a partition of
[m]
. Furthermore, by the construction of
\mathcal{V}_{m}
, we must have

n\leq c(\mathcal{V};j^{-1}(x),x\in S):=\sum_{x\in S}\dim\aff(V_{i};i\in j^{-1}(x)).

Since each
i\in j^{-1}(x)
satisfies
V_{i}\subset F_{x}
, and
F_{x}
is closed under affine combinations, we have

\sum_{x\in S}\dim\aff(V_{i};i\in j^{-1}(x))\leq\sum_{x\in S}\dim F_{x},

and hence
n\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\dim F_{j}
.
∎

3.
Thin
k
-planes

In the previous section we decomposed an
NC
set or measure into a collection of affine subspaces supporting irreducible
s
-Frostman measures. In this section, we will describe what it means for a collection of measures to span
s
-thin hyperplanes.

The works of Orponen–Shmerkin–Wang
[
OSW24
]
and Ren
[
Ren23
]
obtain continuum versions of Beck’s theorem for lines via radial projection theorems, which in turn are proven using the notion of
thin tubes
. We recall their definition of thin tubes here. If
X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
G\subset\prod_{i}X_{i}
, for all
x_{j}\in X_{j}
we define the
x_{j}
-section of
G
:

G|_{x_{j}}=\left\{(x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x}_{j},\dots x_{k})\in\prod_{i\neq j}X_{i}:(x_{0},\dots,x_{j},\dots,x_{k})\in G\right\}.

If
G
is Borel, then every section is measurable too.

Definition 3.1

(Thin tubes)
.

Let
K,\sigma\geq 0
and
c\in(0,1]
. Let
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\supp(\mu_{i})=X_{i}
for
i=0,1
. We say that
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
has
(\sigma,K,c)
-thin tubes if there exists a Borel set
G\subset X_{0}\times X_{1}
with
(\mu_{0}\times\mu_{1})(G)\geq c
with the following property. If
x_{0}\in X_{0}
, then

\mu_{1}(T\cap G|_{x_{0}})\leq K\cdot r^{\sigma}\quad\text{for all $r>0$ and all $r$-tubes $T$ containing $x.$}

We also say
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
has
\sigma
-thin tubes if
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
has
(\sigma,K,c)
-thin tubes for some
K\geq 0
and
c\in(0,1].

One can readily check that if
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
and
(\mu_{1},\mu_{0})
have
\sigma
-thin tubes and
\supp\mu_{i}\subset X
for
i=1,2
it follows that
\dim\mathcal{L}(X)\geq 2\min\{\sigma,1\}
. Our definition of
\sigma
-thin
k
-planes will be formulated to ensure that the set of
k
-planes spanned by sets
X_{0},\dots,X_{k}
in “good position” is at least
(k+1)\min\{\sigma,1\}
-dimensional. In particular, we will deduce the sharp Beck-type theorem for hyperplanes.

Define the partial map
\psi_{k}\colon(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k+1}\to{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
which sends a
(k+1)
-tuple of points
(x_{0},\dotsc,x_{k})
to the affine
k
-plane
\aff(x_{0},\dotsc,x_{k})
. This map is well-defined outside of

\mathcal{S}_{k}=\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k+1}:x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\ \text{belong to a common $(k-1)$-plane}\}.

Definition 3.2

(Measures in
C
-good position)
.

We say that measures
\mu_{0},\dotsc,\mu_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are in
C
-
good position
if their supports
\supp\mu_{i}=X_{i}
are contained in
B^{n}(0,1)
, and
X_{0}\times\dots\times X_{k}
is
1/C
-separated from
\mathcal{S}_{k}
. If the constant
C
is not important, we will just refer to measures in
good position
.

Notice that given
\mu_{0},\dotsc,\mu_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in good position, every
(x_{0},\dotsc,x_{k})\in\prod X_{i}
uniquely determines a
k
-plane containing
x_{0},\dots,x_{k}
. We will denote this
k
-plane
V_{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}}
; we denote the
\delta
-neighborhood of this
k
-plane (as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) as
V_{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}}(\delta).
We can now define thin
k
-planes.

Definition 3.3

(Thin
k
-planes)
.

Let
K\geq 1,\sigma>0,c\in(0,1)
, and
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Let
\mu_{0},\dotsc,\mu_{k}
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in good position.

We say that
(\mu_{0},\dotsc,\mu_{k})

span
(\sigma,K,c)
-thin
k
-planes
, or simply have
\sigma
-thin
k
-planes
, if there exists a Borel set
G\subset\supp\mu_{0}\times\dots\times\supp\mu_{k}
such that

(i)

(\mu_{0}\times\dots\times\mu_{k})(G)\geq c
,

(ii)

for any
(x_{0},\dotsc,x_{k})\in G
and any
\delta>0
and
j=0,\dotsc,k
, we have

\mu_{j}(V_{x_{0},\dotsc,x_{k}}(\delta))\leq K\delta^{\sigma}.

We call any such
G
a
thin
k
-plane graph
of the collection
(\mu_{0},\dotsc,\mu_{k})
(and a
(\sigma,K,c)
-thin
k
-plane graph
if we want to emphasize the values of parameters). We also say that such a
G

witnesses

(\sigma,K,c)
-thin
k
-planes for the measures
\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{k}
.

We also extend this definition to the case of non-probability measures
\mu_{i}
by saying that
(\mu_{0},\ldots,\mu_{k})
span
(\sigma,K,c)
-thin
k
-planes if the renormalized measures
\tilde{\mu}_{i}=\frac{1}{\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\mu_{i}
do.

Now that we have defined
\sigma
-thin
k
-planes, we can show that if a set
X
supports
\mu_{0},\ldots,\mu_{k}
having
\sigma
-thin
k
-planes, then the dimension of the set of
k
-planes spanned by
X
is at least
(k+1)\sigma
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mu_{0},\dotsc,\mu_{k}
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in good position with
(\sigma,K,c)
-thin
k
-planes. Let
G
be a thin
k
-plane graph of the collection
(\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{k}).
Then, the pushforward measure
\psi_{k}(\mu_{0}\times\cdots\times\mu_{k}|_{G})
is a nonzero compactly supported
\sigma^{\prime}
-Frostman measure in
\mathcal{A}(n,k)
, where
\sigma^{\prime}=(k+1)\sigma
. In particular,
\sigma\leq n-k
.

Proof.

Let
V\in\mathcal{A}(n,k)
be arbitrary and
\rho=\mu_{0}\times\dotsb\times\mu_{k}
. Let
B_{\mathcal{A}(n,k)}(V,\delta)
denote the
\delta
-neighborhood of
V
in the space of
k
-flats. That
\psi_{k}(\rho|_{G})
is nonzero and compactly supported is immediate from
\rho(G)\geq c>0
and the compact support of
G
.

We want to show that

(3)

\psi_{k}(\rho|_{G})(B_{\mathcal{A}(n,k)}(V,\delta))\leq K^{\prime}\delta^{\sigma^{\prime}}

for some constant
K^{\prime}
to be determined. Note that there exists a constant
C=C(n)
so that for every
V^{\prime}\in B_{\mathcal{A}(n,k)}(V,\delta)
we have
V^{\prime}\cap B^{n}(0,1)\subset V(C\delta)
.

Without loss of generality, we suppose that there exists
(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in G
so that
V_{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}}\in B_{\mathcal{A}(n,k)}(V,\delta)
. If this weren’t the case, then

\psi_{k}(\rho|_{G})(B_{\mathcal{A}(n,k)}(V,\delta))=0

which immediately implies (
3
) holds. Thus, we have
V(C\delta)\cap B^{n}(0,1)\subset V_{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}}(C^{2}\delta)
for some
(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in G
. Thus, if we have
V_{\tilde{x}_{0},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{k}}\in B_{\mathcal{A}(n,k)}(V,\delta)
for some
(\tilde{x}_{0},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{k})\in G
then we get
\tilde{x}_{0},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{k}\in V_{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}}(C^{2}\delta)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(\rho|_{G})(B_{\mathcal{A}(n,k)}(V,\delta))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\prod_{i=0}^{k}\mathbf{1}_{V_{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}}(C^{2}\delta)}(\tilde{x}_{i})\,d\rho(\tilde{x}_{0},\dots,\tilde{x}_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=0}^{k}\mu_{i}(V_{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}}(C^{2}\delta))

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=0}^{k}K(C^{2}\delta)^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=K^{k+1}C^{2(k+1)\sigma}\delta^{(k+1)\sigma}.

The second-to-last line follows from the fact that
G
is a
(\sigma,K,c)
-thin plane graph of the collection
(\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{k})
. Hence, we have shown that
\psi_{k}(\mu_{0}\times\cdots\times\mu_{k}|_{G})
is a nonzero
(k+1)\sigma
-Frostman with constant
K^{k+1}C^{2(k+1)\sigma}
.
This concludes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

By Lemma
3.4
, to prove Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to show that if
X
is an
NC
set, then for every
\sigma<s<\dim X
, there are
s
-Frostman measures
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}
in good position and supported in
X
that span
\sigma
-thin hyperplanes. As a first step, we show that if
\mu
is an irreducible
s
-Frostman measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\mu
can be decomposed into measures
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}
that span
\sigma
-thin
(n-1)
-planes.

4.
The irreducible case

In this section, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Borel set supporting an irreducible
s
-Frostman probability measure, then for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

\dim\mathcal{P}^{k}(X)\geq(k+1)\min\{s,n-k\}.

Remark 4.2
.

By translation and dilation, it suffices to prove Theorem
4.1
in the case that
X\subset B^{n}(0,1)
. Irreducibility is a continuum analog of the nonconcentration condition in Beck’s discrete hyperplane theorem
[
Bec83
, Theorem 5.4]
.

We prove Theorem
4.1
by establishing a more quantitative version of it. It relies on a quantitative measurement of the irreducibility of a measure.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
w,\tau>0
and
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a subspace.
We say that a measure
\mu
supported on
V\cap B^{n}(0,1)
is
(w,\tau)
-irreducible on
V
if for any proper subspace
H\subset V
we have
\mu(H(w))\leq\tau\mu(B^{n}(0,1))
.

The following discretized version of Theorem
4.1
is the main technical result of this section.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
1\leq k\leq n-1
,
0<s\leq n-1
,
0<\sigma<s
and
C>0
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\tau_{0}:=\tau_{0}(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,n,C)
and
w_{0}:=w_{0}(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,n,C)
such that the following holds for all
\tau\leq\tau_{0}
and all
w\leq w_{0}
. Given
\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{k}
that are
(w,\tau)
-irreducible
(C,s)
-Frostman measures in
C
-good position, there exists
K:=K(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,n,C,w)>0
such that
(\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{k})
spans
(\sigma,K,1-\varepsilon)
-thin
k
-planes.

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

We prove Theorem
4.4
by induction on
k
and
n
. Ren’s discretized radial projection theorem
A.1
will imply Theorem
A.2
, the base case of our induction when
k=1
and
n\geq 2
is arbitrary.

We now proceed to the induction step, so let us fix
2\leq k\leq n-1
and suppose that the statement holds for all smaller values of
n
and all smaller values of
k
(within the allowable range). Let
X_{i}=\supp\mu_{i}
. As a first step, we apply the base case
k=1
to each pair
\mu_{i},\mu_{j}

(i\neq j)
. Since the measures
\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{k}
are in
C
-good position, every pair
\mu_{i},\mu_{j}
is also in
C
-good position. By the base case
k=1
applied to each pair, there exist Borel sets
G_{ij}\subset X_{i}\times X_{j}
that witness
(\sigma,K,1-\varepsilon)
-thin
1
-planes for the measures
\mu_{i},\mu_{j}
for arbitrary
\sigma<s
for some
K_{0}=K_{0}(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,n,C,w)
. We may assume that
(x_{i},x_{j})\in G_{ij}
if and only if
(x_{j},x_{i})\in G_{ji}
.

Next, we define

(4)

\widetilde{X}_{i}=\{x_{i}\in X_{i}:~\mu_{j}(G_{ij}|_{x_{i}})\geq 1-\varepsilon^{1/2},~\text{ for any }j\neq i\}.

We claim that
\mu_{i}(\widetilde{X}_{i})\geq 1-\varepsilon^{1/2}
holds. Indeed by Fubini,

1-\varepsilon\leq\mu_{i}\times\mu_{j}(G_{ij})=\int\mu_{j}(G_{ij}|_{x_{i}})\,d\mu_{i}(x_{i})\leq\mu_{i}(\widetilde{X}_{i})+\mu_{i}(X_{i}\setminus\widetilde{X}_{i})(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})

and rearranging gives the bound (using that
\mu_{i}(X_{i}\setminus\tilde{X}_{i})=1-\mu_{i}(\tilde{X}_{i})
).

Consider a fixed hyperplane
H
with
\mathrm{dist}(H,X_{i})>C
for every
0\leq i\leq k
. Denote by
\mathrm{dir}(H)
the linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
tangent to
H
.
Fix
0\leq i\leq k
and let
x_{i}\in\widetilde{X}_{i}
. We define a “radial” projection map onto the hyperplane
H
given by

\displaystyle\pi_{x_{i}}:~

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(\mathrm{dir}(H)+x_{i})\to H

\displaystyle\pi_{x_{i}}(y)=\langle x_{i},y\rangle\cap H.

Consider the measures
\{\pi_{x_{i}}(\mu_{j})\}_{j:j\neq i}
on
H
, which we identify with
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. We claim that these
k-1
measures satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
4.4
in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

Up to rescaling by
\sim 1/C
, we have
\pi_{x_{i}}\mu_{j}
supported on a unit ball
B^{n-1}
in
H
and
\{\pi_{x_{i}}\mu_{j}\}_{j:j\neq i}
is in
C^{2}
-good position in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. We observe that for a
(n-2)
-plane
U\subset H
we have
\pi_{x_{i}}^{-1}(U(\delta))\subset(\pi_{x_{i}}^{-1}(U))(A\delta)
for some
A
(depending on how much
H
and
\supp\mu_{i}
are separated). It follows that for
i\neq j
and every
x_{i}\in\supp\mu_{i}
,
\pi_{x_{i}}\mu_{j}
is a
(w/A,\tau)
-irreducible measure on
H
.

By the
(\sigma,K_{0},1-\varepsilon)
-thin property of
G_{ij}
, we know that
\pi_{x_{i}}(\mu_{j}|_{G_{ij}|x_{i}})
is
(C_{1}K_{0},\sigma)
-Frostman for some
C_{1}:=C_{1}(\sigma,\varepsilon)>0
. Indeed, for any ball
B(y,\delta)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
either
B(y,\delta)\cap\pi_{x_{i}}(G_{ij}|_{x_{i}})
is empty or there is a point
x_{j}\in\pi^{-1}_{x_{i}}(B(y,\delta))\cap X_{j}
. In the latter case, there exists a constant
A=A(C,n)
such that
\pi_{x_{i}}^{-1}(B(y,\delta))\cap X_{j}\subset V_{x_{i},y}(A\delta)
, where
V_{x_{i},y}
is the line through
x_{i},y
. It follows that
\pi_{x_{i}}(\mu_{j}|_{G_{ij}|x_{i}})
is
(C_{1}K_{0},\sigma)
-Frostman with
C_{1}=A^{\sigma}
.

It follows that the measures
\{\pi_{x_{i}}(\mu_{j}|_{G_{ij}|x_{i}})\}_{j:j\neq i}
, satisfy the conditions of Theorem
4.4
with parameters

k^{\prime}=k-1,~n^{\prime}=n-1,~s^{\prime}=\sigma,~C^{\prime}=C_{1}K_{0},~w^{\prime}=w/A,~\tau^{\prime}=\tau.

Thus, given
j\neq i
, by our inductive hypothesis and
x_{i}\in\tilde{X}_{i}
(see (
4
)), for all
\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma
,
(\pi_{x_{i}}(\mu_{j}|_{G_{ij}|x_{i}}))_{j:j\neq i}
, have
(\sigma^{\prime},K_{1},1-\varepsilon^{1/2})
-thin
(k-1)
-planes for some
K_{1}:=K_{1}(\varepsilon,s^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},n-1,C_{1}K_{0},w^{\prime}))
. That is, there exists a Borel set

\widetilde{G}_{\hat{i}}^{(x_{i})}\subset\prod_{j:j\neq i}\pi_{x_{i}}(X_{j})

of measure at least
1-\varepsilon^{1/2}
with respect to the measure
\prod_{j:j\neq i}\pi_{x_{i}}(\mu_{j}|_{G_{ij}|x_{i}})
such that

(5)

\pi_{x_{i}}(\mu_{j}|_{G_{ij}|x_{i}})(V^{\prime}(\delta))\leq K_{1}\delta^{\sigma^{\prime}}

holds for any affine subspace
V^{\prime}=\psi_{k-1}(x_{\hat{i}})
with
(x_{\hat{i}}):=(x_{0},\dotsc,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dotsc,x_{k})\in\widetilde{G}_{\hat{i}}^{(x_{i})}
. For our purposes, equation (
5
) is not exactly what we need to conclude our results: we would like this condition to hold with respect to the measures
\{\pi_{x_{i}}(\mu_{j})\}
instead of
\{\pi_{x_{i}}(\mu_{j}|_{G_{ij}|x_{i}})\}
. This can be achieved by the following.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mu_{0},\dotsc,\mu_{k}
be arbitrary probability measures in
C
-good position in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote
X_{i}=\supp(\mu_{i})
and suppose that there are subsets
X^{\prime}_{i}\subset X_{i}
and
G^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}_{0}\times\dotsb\times X^{\prime}_{k}
such that:

•

\mu_{i}(X_{i}^{\prime})\geq 1-\varepsilon
,
i=0,\dotsc,k
,

•

\mu_{0}\times\dots\times\mu_{k}(G^{\prime})\geq 1-\varepsilon
,

•

the measures
\mu_{0}|_{X^{\prime}_{0}},\dots,\mu_{k}|_{X^{\prime}_{k}}
have
(\sigma,K,1-\varepsilon)
-thin
(k-1)
-planes with respect to
G^{\prime}
.

5.
Measures in stable position

We fix a collection of flats
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote

. We will also have a collection of measures
\mu_{j,1},\ldots,\mu_{j,n_{j}}
supported on
V_{j}\cap B^{n}(0,1)
. Recall the algorithm of Lemma
2.1
, which decomposes a measure
\mu
into irreducible pieces
\mu_{j,i}
supported on proper subflats
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The basic issue behind ensuring that we span hyperplanes when we choose a point from the support of each
\mu_{j,i}
is that the dimension of the affine span can be less than
n-1
or equal to
n
.

For a flat
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
let
\overline{V}=\operatorname{span}(V\times\{1\})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
denote the linearization of
V
(note that
\dim\overline{V}=\dim V+1
). In what follows, it will be convenient to work with linearizations of flats.

For
j\in[k]
let
v_{j,1},\ldots,v_{j,n_{j}+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a fixed orthonormal basis of the linear space
\overline{V_{j}}
and let
A_{j}=(v_{j,1},\ldots,v_{j,n_{j}+1})
be the corresponding
(n+1)\times(n_{j}+1)
matrix. For a choice of an index set
I_{j}\subset[n_{j}]
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
and

\overline{x}=(x_{j,i}:~j\in[m],~i\in I_{j})
, where
x_{j,i}\in\supp\mu_{j,i}
, we denote
\overline{I}=\{(j,i),~j\in[m],i\in I_{j}\}
and let
B_{\overline{I}}(\overline{x})
be the
(n+1)\times(\sum|I_{j}|)
matrix composed of vectors
\begin{bmatrix}x_{j,i}\\
1\end{bmatrix}\in\overline{V_{j}}
over all
(j,i)\in\overline{I}
. Since
n_{j}\leq n
for all
j
, we always have
\overline{I}\subset[m]\times[0,n]
.

For
J\subset[k]
we let
A_{J}=(A_{j},~j\in J)
which is a
(n+1)\times(\sum_{j\in J}(n_{j}+1))
matrix.
For index sets
\overline{I}
and
J\subset[k]
, we will be interested in the properties of the matrices

(B_{\overline{I}}(\overline{x}),A_{J})

over various choices of
\overline{x}
supported on our measures
\mu_{j,i}
. For a matrix
A
and an integer

we let
M_{r}(A)
be the maximum absolute value of an
r\times r
minor of
A
.

Definition 5.1
.

Given a collection of flats
V_{j}
and bases
v_{j,i}
as above, we say that a collection measures
(\mu_{j,i}:~i\in[n_{j}],j\in[m])
is in

-stable position if for every choice of index sets
\overline{I}
and
J
there exists a number
r=r(\overline{I},J)
such that we have
\operatorname{rank}(B_{\overline{I}}(\overline{x}),A_{J})=r
and
M_{r}(B_{\overline{I}}(\overline{x}),A_{J})\geq c
for any
\overline{x}\in\prod_{j,i}\supp\mu_{j,i}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
be arbitrary flats, set
n_{j}=\dim V_{j}
. Let
\mu_{j,i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n_{j}
, be arbitrary irreducible measures on

. Then there exist

and balls

Proof.

Observe that

is a continuous function, so if we have

for some

, then we have

within a small neighborhood of

. Now for each

Note that

is a vector of integers between

\overline{I}
,

). So we can find a vector

for which

has maximum possible sum of coordinates. So for any other

(coordinate-wise) for
\overline{x^{\prime}}
in a small neighbourhood of
\overline{x}
. So we can take

to be small balls around

and

. Finally note that

-stability implies the separation from

.

We will need a lemma showing that

-stable position is preserved under radial projections.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
are subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m})
are measures in
c
-stable position with respect to them. Let

be an index set and let
n_{0}=\dim\langle\overline{x}_{I_{0}}\rangle=\dim\langle x_{i},~i\in I_{0}\rangle
for arbitrary
x_{i}\in\supp\mu_{i}
. Let

be a subspace of dimension

transversal to
\langle\overline{x}_{I_{0}}\rangle
(for all
\overline{x}
) and consider the projection map
\pi_{\langle\overline{x}_{I_{0}}\rangle}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\langle\overline{x}_{I_{0}}\rangle\to U
.
Define

\theta=\angle(\overline{U},\overline{\langle\overline{x}_{I_{0}}\rangle}).

Then the collection of measures
\tilde{\mu}_{i}=\pi_{\langle\overline{x}_{I_{0}}\rangle}(\mu_{i})
,

is in
\gtrsim(\theta c)^{O(1)}
-stable position with respect to spaces
\pi_{\langle\overline{x}_{I_{0}}\rangle}(V_{i})
,
i\in[m]\setminus I_{0}
.

Proof.

Choose an orthonormal basis in the linearization
\overline{\pi_{\langle\overline{x}_{I_{0}}\rangle}(V_{i})}\subset\overline{U}
for

and define the corresponding matrix

. Choose some

and pick any
\overline{x^{\prime}}\in\prod_{i\in[m]\setminus I_{0}}\supp\mu_{i}
. Let

denote the rank of the matrix
(B_{I_{0}\sqcup I}(\overline{x}_{I_{0}},\overline{x^{\prime}}),A_{J})
(where

notably only depends on
I_{0}
by

-stability), and furthermore by the

-stability of the collection
(\mu_{i})
we have

Note that
\overline{\langle\overline{x}_{I_{0}}\rangle}
and
\overline{U}
are complementary subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Thus, by subtracting appropriate linear combinations of columns in

and

for
1\leq j\leq\dim V_{j}
spanning thin
(\sigma,K,1-\varepsilon)
-thin codimension 1-planes for all
0<\sigma<s<n-1
and
\varepsilon>0.
In fact, we know more about the collection of our flats
\{V_{1},\dots,V_{m}\}
from Lemma
2.1
.

Definition 6.1

(
NC
flats)
.

Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a collection of affine flats. We say that the collection
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}
is
non-concentrated
, or
NC
, if any list of flats
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
with
\bigcup_{j\in[m]}V_{j}\subset\bigcup_{i\in[r]}F_{i}
satisfies

\sum_{i\in[r]}\dim F_{i}\geq n.

Note that this implies
\sum_{j\in[m]}\dim V_{j}\geq n.

By combining several of our
NC
flats
V_{j}
into a single flat
F_{J}=\aff(V_{j}:j\in J)
for
J\subset[m]
, and showing that on each
F_{J}
we have thin codimension 1-planes, we can in fact assume that our collection
\{V_{1},\dots,V_{m}\}
is minimal in a certain sense.

Definition 6.2

(Minimal Collections of Flats)
.

Given a collection of flats
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
J\subset[k]
, let
F_{J}:=\aff(F_{j}:j\in J)
. Then, we say that
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}
is
minimal
if
\dim F_{[k]}=n\leq\sum_{j\in[k]}\dim F_{j}
and
\dim F_{J}\geq\sum_{j\in J}\dim F_{j}
.

To this end, let us now state the main results that will be the contents of the rest of the paper, and show we may assume we have a minimal collection of flats.

We will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 6.3

(General case)
.

Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an
NC
collection of flats and suppose that
(\mu_{j,i}:~j\in[m],i\in[n_{j}])
are
(C,s)
-Frostman measures in
c
-stable position. Suppose that
\mu_{j,i}
is
(w,\tau)
-irreducible inside
V_{j}
for every
j,i
.

There exist some
n^{\prime}_{j}\leq n_{j}
with
\sum n^{\prime}_{j}=n
such that the following holds.
For any
\varepsilon>0
and
\sigma<s
, if
\tau<\tau(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,C,c,n)
and
K=K(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,C,c,n,w)
, then the measures
(\mu_{j,i},~j\in[m],i\in[n^{\prime}_{j}])
have
(\sigma,K,1-\varepsilon)
-thin hyperplanes.

Remark 6.4
.

We start with a qualitatively
\mathbf{NC}
measure
\mu
, from which we get measures
\mu_{j,i}
, each with modulus of irreducibility
\tau_{j,i}(w)\to 0
as
w\to 0
. We take
w
sufficiently small so that
\tau_{j,i}(w)<\tau(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,C,c,n)
to apply the general case theorem.

At the end of this section we deduce Theorem
1.2
, that if
X
is an
\mathbf{NC}
set, then

\dim\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)\geq n\min\{\dim X,1\}

from the more quantitative Theorem
6.3
.

We prove Theorem
6.3
using an inductive scheme.

We denote
V_{J}=\aff(V_{j},~j\in J)
for
J\subset[m]
and let
n_{J}=\dim V_{J}
.

Definition 6.5

(Saturated set of indices.)
.

Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a collection of flats. A set of indices
I\subset[m]
is called
saturated with respect to
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
if there exist functions
K_{I}(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,C,c,n,w)
and
\tau_{I}(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,C,c,n)
such that the following holds for any
\varepsilon>0
,
\sigma<s
,
w>0
,
\tau\leq\tau_{I}
,
K\geq K_{I}
.

Let
\mu_{t,i}
for
t\in I
and
i\in[\dim V_{t}]
be
(C,s)
-Frostman measures in
c
-stable position with respect to
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
. Suppose that
\mu_{t,i}
is
(w,\tau)
-irreducible in
V_{t}
. Then there exist some
n^{\prime}_{t}\leq\dim V_{t}
with
\sum_{t\in I}n^{\prime}_{t}=\dim V_{I}
and such that the measures
(\mu_{t,i}:~t\in I,~i\in[n^{\prime}_{t}])
have
(\sigma,K,1-\varepsilon)
-thin hyperplanes inside
V_{I}
.

Thus, our goal is to show that
[m]
is saturated with respect to
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
, whenever that is an
NC
collection of flats. The irreducible case, Theorem
4.4
, implies that
\{j\}
is saturated for every
j\in[m]
. Here is our main inductive claim:

Theorem 6.6

(Minimal case)
.

Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a collection of flats.
Let
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\subset[m]
be a collection of pairwise disjoint, non-empty subsets and suppose that each
I_{j}
is saturated.
Let
F_{j}=V_{I_{j}}
and suppose that
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
is a minimal collection of flats inside
\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\rangle
. Then
I_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup I_{k}
is also saturated with respect to
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
.

Now we show how to deduce the General Case from the Minimal Case.

Proof that Theorem
6.3
follows from Theorem
6.6
.

We say that a collection of disjoint subsets
J_{1},\dots,J_{k}\subset[m]
is
minimal
if the collection of flats
F_{j}=V_{J_{j}}
is minimal inside
V_{J_{1}\cup\dots\cup J_{k}}
(see Definition
6.2
).

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
J_{1},\ldots,J_{k}\subset[m]
be pairwise disjoint non-empty sets. Suppose that
J_{j}
is saturated for
j=1,\ldots,k
. Suppose that the collection of flats
F_{j}=V_{J_{j}}
is minimal inside the flat
V_{J_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{k}}
. Then
J_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{k}
is saturated as well.

Proof.

We identify
V_{J_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{k}}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
for
n^{\prime}=\dim V_{J_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{k}}
and apply Theorem
6.6
. Note that the constants
C,c
will only change by a constant factor after the change of coordinates and that the
c
-stable position with respect to
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
implies
\gtrsim_{c}1
-stable position with respect to spaces
V_{J_{1}},\ldots,V_{J_{k}}
in the new coordinates.
Thus, the condition that
J_{j}
is saturated can be used as an input in Theorem
6.6
and the thin-planes conclusion implies that
J_{1}\cup\ldots\cup J_{k}
is also saturated.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

Suppose
J_{1}\cup\cdots\cup J_{t}=[m]
is a partition of
[m]
with
t\geq 2
such that
J_{i}
is saturated for every
1\leq i\leq t
. Then, for some
I\subset[t]
with
|I|\geq 2
, the set
(J_{i}:i\in I)
is minimal. In particular,
the set
\bigcup_{i\in I}J_{i}
is saturated.

Proof of Lemma
6.8
.

If the collection
J_{1},\dots,J_{t}
is minimal then
[m]
is saturated by Lemma
6.7
. Hence, suppose
J_{1},\dots,J_{t}
is not minimal. This implies that either
\dim V_{[t]}>\sum_{j=1}^{t}\dim V_{J_{j}}
or
\dim V_{I}<\sum_{j\in I}\dim V_{J_{j}}
for some proper
I\subsetneq[t].
The former case is impossible as the collection
\{V_{i}\}
is
NC
(take
F_{j}=V_{J_{j}}
for all
1\leq j\leq t
in Definition
6.1
). Hence, the latter inequality must hold for some
I\subsetneq[t].

Thus, there must exist some minimal by inclusion set
I\subset[t]
such that for
J_{I}=\bigcup_{j\in J}J_{j}
we have
\dim V_{J_{I}}<\sum_{j\in I}\dim V_{J_{j}}
while
\dim V_{J_{I^{\prime}}}\geq\sum_{j\in I^{\prime}}\dim V_{J_{j}}
for any proper
I^{\prime}\subsetneq I.
This implies that
|I|\geq 2
as the singleton sets are saturated by the irreducible case. Therefore, we have that the collection of sets
\{J_{j}:j\in I\}
is minimal. So, by Lemma
6.7
,
J_{I}
is saturated, completing the proof.
∎

We can now finish the proof by iterating Lemma
6.8
. Start with the trivial partition
\{1\}\cup\dots\cup\{m\}=[m]
. Applying Lemma
6.8
, we can merge some subset of elements in the above partition to get a new partition into saturated sets with at least
m-1
parts. Reiterating Lemma
6.8
implies that eventually we will arrive at the partition
[m]=[m]
which implies that
[m]
is saturated. Unraveling the definition of a subset being saturated gives the conclusion of the General Case.
∎

Remark 6.9
.

Note that while we in fact have irreducible measures
\mu_{j}
on each
V_{j}
, in the Minimal Case we may not assume
\mu_{j}
are irreducible in
F_{j}
due to the merging process described above.

The proof of the Minimal Case is the content of Section
7
. We now show that the General Case implies our main result (Theorem
1.2
).

Proof that the General Case implies Theorem
1.2
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel
NC
set. Then, the Decomposition Lemma (Lemma
2.1
) gives us an
NC
collection of flats
\{V_{j}\}_{j\in[m]}
with irreducible
s
-Frostman measures
\mu_{j}
supported on
X\cap V_{j}
for all
s<\dim X.
Let
n_{j}=\dim V_{j}
.

By Lemma
4.6
, we can find restrictions
\mu_{j,i}=\frac{1}{\mu_{j}(B_{j,i})}\mu_{j}|_{B_{j,i}}
which are
s
-Frostman irreducible probability measures on
V_{j}
in good position. By Lemma
5.2
, we can further restrict the measures
\mu_{j,i}
onto some smaller balls so that the collection
(\mu_{j,i}:~i\in[n_{j}],j\in[m])
is
c
-stable for some
c>0
and
\mu_{j,i}
is still an irreducible
s
-Frostman probability measure. For each
\mu_{j,i}
we can define the modulus of irreducibility
\tau_{j,i}(w)
as the maximum measure of a
w
-neighborhood of a proper subspace
H\subset V_{j}
. Then by irreducibility we have
\tau_{j,i}(w)\to 0
as
w\to 0
and
\mu_{j,i}
is
(w,\tau_{j,i}(w))
-irreducible in
V_{j}
. So if we take
w
sufficiently small, then all
\tau_{j,i}(w)
become small enough to apply Theorem

and we conclude that for some
n^{\prime}_{j}\leq n_{j}
,
\sum_{j}n^{\prime}_{j}=n
, the measures
(\mu_{j,i},~i\in[n^{\prime}_{j}],j\in[m])
have
(\sigma,K,1-\varepsilon)
-thin hyperplanes.
Call this subcollection of measures
\{\tilde{\mu}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Then, by Lemma
3.4
, it follows that the pushforward measure
\psi_{n-1}(\tilde{\mu}_{1}\times\cdots\times\tilde{\mu}_{n})
is a nonzero
n\sigma
-Frostman measure on
\mathcal{A}(n,n-1)
and
\sigma\leq 1
. Notice that since
\supp\tilde{\mu}_{i}\subset X
for all

, this implies that the pushfoward measure is supported on
\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(X)
. Given this holds for all
0<\sigma<\min\{s,1\}<\dim X
and arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, this concludes the proof.
∎

7.
The minimal case

In this section we prove Theorem
6.6
. We will prove the theorem in dimension
n^{\prime}
and use variable
n
to denote something else.
Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
be a collection of flats and fix
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\subset[m]
and
F_{j}=V_{I_{j}}
.
Suppose that
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\subset\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\rangle
be a minimal collection of flats and denote
n=\dim\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\rangle
.
Let
n_{j}=\dim F_{j}
. Suppose that
\mu^{\prime}_{t,i}
,
t\in[m]
,
i=1,\ldots,\dim V_{t}
are measures on
V_{t}
such that
(\mu^{\prime}_{t,i}:~i\in[\dim V_{t}],t\in[m])
are in
c
-stable position with respect to
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
and that each
\mu^{\prime}_{t,i}
is
(w,\tau)
-irreducible inside
V_{t}
. By the assumption of the theorem we know that for every
j
there are some numbers
d_{t}\leq\dim V_{t}
,

, so that

and the collection
(\mu^{\prime}_{t,i}:t\in I_{j},~i\in[d_{t}])

(\sigma,K_{j},1-\varepsilon)
-thin
(n_{j}-1)
-planes, provided that
\tau\leq\tau_{j}(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,C,c)
and
K_{j}=K_{j}(\varepsilon,s,\sigma,C,c,w)

the corresponding thin-planes graph.

Let us rotate the coordinate system so that
\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\rangle=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}

\mathbb{R}^{n}
(flats
V_{t}
with

\sim c
-stable position. For notational convenience let us label by
\mu_{j,1},\ldots,\mu_{j,n_{j}}
the collection of measures
\mu_{t,i}
,
t\in I_{j}
,

(say in the increasing order of

for a while, but they will become relevant at the end of the argument.

Following the notation from Section
5
, we define matrices
(B_{\overline{I}}(\overline{x}),A_{J})
, where now each
A_{j}=(f_{j,1},\ldots,f_{j,n_{j}+1})
corresponds to a fixed orthonormal basis

of
\overline{F_{j}}
.
Let
r(\overline{I},J)
denote the rank of the matrix
(B_{\overline{I}}(\overline{x}),A_{J})
. Note that it does not depend on the choice of
\overline{x}\in\prod\supp\mu_{j,i}
and we moreover have
M_{r(\overline{I},J)}(B_{\overline{I}}(\overline{x}),A_{J})\geq c
for some

. We denote by
P_{\overline{I}}(\overline{x})
the affine space spanned by vectors
x_{j,i}
over
(j,i)\in\overline{I}
. We denote
F_{J}=\aff(F_{j}:~j\in J)
.

First of all, we reduce to the case when
\sum_{j=1,j\neq j^{\prime}}^{k}n_{j}\leq n-1
holds for every
j^{\prime}\in[k]
. Indeed, otherwise note that the subcollection
\{F_{j},~j\neq j^{\prime}\}
is still minimal and so we can restrict to this collection. Write
\sum_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}=n+p
for some
p\geq 0
. We then conclude that
n_{j}\geq p+1
for every
j=1,\ldots,k
.

The next proposition records the fact that
r(\overline{I},J)
is the dimension of the linear space
\langle\overline{P_{\overline{I}}(\overline{x})},\overline{F_{J}}\rangle
.

Proposition 7.1
.

For index sets
\overline{I},J
and any
j\in[k]
and
i\in[n_{j}]
:

r(\overline{I}\cup\{(j,i)\},J)\leq r(\overline{I},J)+1,

r(\overline{I},J\cup\{j\})\leq r(\overline{I},J)+n_{j}+1.

r(\overline{I}\cup(\{j\}\times[n_{i}]),J)\geq r(\overline{I},J\cup\{j\})-1.

Proof.

The first two statements are clear from the definitions of ranks. For the last one notice that
\overline{P_{\{j\}\times[n_{j}]}(\overline{x})}
is a codimension 1 subspace in
F_{j}
.
∎

7.1.
Special case
p=0

We will first consider the special case when
p=0
, i.e.
\sum_{j}n_{j}=n
. The argument simplifies significantly in this case. Later we consider the general case
\sum_{j}n_{j}=n+p
which will require an additional careful inductive argument.

For a subset
I\subset[k]
and
J\subset[k]
let us denote

r(I,J)=r(\overline{I},J),~\text{ where }\overline{I}=\bigcup_{j\in I}\{j\}\times[n_{j}].

With this notation the last line in Proposition
7.1
gives

(9)

r(I\cup\{j\},J)\geq r(I,J\cup\{j\})-1.

For
I\subset[k]
, we also denote

\overline{P_{I}(\overline{x})}=\overline{P_{\overline{I}}(\overline{x})},\qquad\overline{I}=\bigcup_{i\in I}\{i\}\times[n_{i}].

and
J\subset[k]
,

\overline{P_{{I},J}(\overline{x})}=\mathrm{span}(\overline{P_{I}(\overline{x})},\overline{F_{j}},j\in J)

with similar notation for
\overline{P_{I,J}(\overline{x})}
.
With this notation,
r(I,J)
is the dimension of the subspace
\overline{P_{I,J}(\overline{x})}
for every
\overline{x}\in G
.

Proposition 7.2

(cf.
[
Do20
]
Claim 2)
.

Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a minimal collection of flats with
\sum n_{j}=n
. For any disjoint
I,J\subset[k]

r(I,J)=\begin{cases}n_{I},~\text{ if }J=\emptyset,\\
\geq n_{I\cup J}+1,~\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove the statement for
I\subset[s]
using induction on
s
. If
s=0
then
I=\emptyset
, in this case
r(\emptyset,J)
coincides with the dimension of the space
\overline{F_{J}}
.
So
r(\emptyset,J)\geq n_{J}+1
follows from the assumption that
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
is a minimal collection of flats.

Now let
s\geq 1
and
I\subset[s]
be a set containing the element
s
. If
J=\emptyset
then

r(I,\emptyset)\geq r(I\setminus\{s\},\{s\})-1\geq n_{I},

by inductive hypothesis and (
9
). So we may assume
J\neq\emptyset
.

Choose any
x_{j,i}\in\supp\mu_{j,i}
for
j\in I
and
i=1,\ldots,n_{j}
and let
\overline{P_{I}}=\overline{P_{I}(\overline{x})}
. By definition, we have
r(I,J)=\dim\langle\overline{P_{I}},\overline{F_{J}}\rangle
. Note that if

\langle\overline{P_{I\setminus s}},\overline{F_{J}}\rangle\cap\overline{F_{s}}=0,

then we get
r(I,J)=r(I\setminus\{s\},J)+r(\{s\},\emptyset)\geq(n_{I\cup J}+1-n_{s})+n_{s}\geq n_{I\cup J}+1
by induction.
Now let
W=\langle P_{I\setminus s},F_{J}\rangle\cap F_{s}
, by the above we may assume that
W\neq\emptyset
(perhaps after performing a mild projective transformation).

Let
w\in W
any point and let
\nu=\delta_{w}
be the Dirac
\delta
-mass at
w
. By Lemma
7.25
, the
(\prod\mu_{s,i})
-measure of the set of

for which we have

is upper bounded by
\varepsilon
where

as a function of

. In particular, for almost every
\overline{x_{s}}
we have
W\not\subset P_{s}(\overline{x}_{s})
. Thus we also get
\overline{W}\not\subset\overline{P_{s}(\overline{x}_{s})}
and so since

is a hyperplane in

:

\langle\overline{P_{I\setminus s}},\overline{F_{J}},\overline{P_{s}(\overline{x}_{s})}\rangle=\langle\overline{P_{I\setminus s}},\overline{F_{J}},\overline{F_{s}}\rangle

Since the rank
r(I,J)
does not depend on which
\overline{x}
we fix, it follows that

r(I,J)=r(I\setminus\{s\},J\cup\{s\})\geq n_{I\cup J}+1,

by induction.
∎

Proposition
7.2
, implies that

(10)

r([k],\emptyset)=n\quad\text{and}\quad r([k]\setminus\{j\},\{j\})=n+1

for all

, where recall

Now we use this to construct a thin-planes graph for the collection
(\mu_{j,i}:~j\in[k],~i\in[n_{j}])
(recall that we are in the case

intersects each

Proposition 7.3
.

The following hold for every
\overline{x}\in G
and every

.

(1)

\overline{F_{j}}\cap\overline{P_{[k]\setminus\{j\}}(\overline{x}})=\{0\}
.

(2)

\overline{F_{j}}\cap\overline{P_{[k]}(\bar{x})}=\overline{P_{j}(\bar{x})}
.

(3)

The smallest principal angle between flats satisfies
\angle(\overline{F_{j}},\overline{P_{[k]\setminus\{j\}}(\overline{x}}))\gtrsim_{n}c
.

Proof.

For
\overline{x}
fixed, and
I\subset[k]
, we denote

B_{I}=B_{I}(\overline{x})=B_{\bigcup_{j\in I}\{j\}\times[n_{j}]}(\overline{x}),

and for
J\subset[k]
, we denote
\overline{P_{I}}=\overline{P_{I}(\overline{x})}.

To prove the claims, we will use the fact that, by definition,
\overline{P_{I}}

is the column space of
B_{I}
, and
\overline{F}_{j}
is the column space of the matrix

.

First we prove (1). Since

, we have
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=\overline{P_{[k]\setminus\{j\}}}\oplus\overline{F}_{j}
, so in particular (1) holds.

To prove (2), by Proposition

, we have
r([k],\emptyset)=n
,

, and

\dim\overline{P_{[k]}}=n

\dim\overline{P_{j}}=n_{j}

. By the dimension-sum formula for subspaces of

\overline{P_{[k]}}=\overline{P_{\{j\}}}\oplus\overline{P_{[k]\setminus\{j\}}}
is therefore a direct sum. Hence,

\overline{F_{j}}\cap\overline{P_{[k]}}=\overline{F_{j}}\cap\overline{P_{j}}+\overline{F_{j}}\cap\overline{P_{[k]\setminus\{j\}}}=\overline{F_{j}}\cap\overline{P_{j}}=\overline{P_{j}},

since

.

Now we prove (3). Since
\overline{x}\in G

c\leq|\det(B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}},A_{j})|.

Let

be the columns of
(B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}},A_{j})
.
By exterior algebra,

|\det(B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}},A_{j})|=|u_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge u_{n+1}|.

Let

be the columns of
B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}}
, and let

be the columns of

, so

By standard facts from exterior algebra (see, e.g.,
[
Afr57
, Corollary to Theorem 7.1]
and
[
IM95
, Theorem 2.1]
),

|u_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge u_{n+1}|=|w_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge w_{n+1-n_{j}}|\cdot|v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{n_{j}+1}|\cdot|\sin\Theta|,

where
\Theta>0
is the smallest principal angle between the column space of
A_{j}
, namely
\overline{F}_{j}
, and the column space of
B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}}
, namely
\overline{P_{[k]\setminus\{j\}}}
. By exterior algebra again, and since the columns of

are orthonormal,

|v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{n_{j}+1}|^{2}=\det(A_{j}^{t}A_{j})=1.

For

with
|S|=n+1-n_{j}
, denote by
(B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}})_{S}
the minor matrix obtained by selecting rows from
B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}}
indexed by
S
. By the Cauchy–Binet formula,

\displaystyle|w_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge w_{n+1-n_{j}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\det\big((B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}})^{t}(B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}})\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subset[n+1],|S|=n+1-n_{j}}\det\big((B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}})_{S}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n}M_{n+1-n_{j}}(B_{[k]\setminus\{j\}})^{2}\lesssim_{n}1.

So, overall, by (
11
) we have

c\lesssim_{n}|\sin\Theta|.

This proves (3), and finishes the proof.

C(n,m)
. For each partition
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{t}\}
of
[m]
let
\Omega_{\mathcal{I}}
be the set of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
for which we have

\sum_{j=1}^{t}\dim\pi_{x}(V_{I_{j}}\setminus\{x\})\leq n-2.

Clearly,
\Omega=\bigcup_{\mathcal{I}}\Omega_{\mathcal{I}}
. Now observe that by genericity of
H
,

\dim\pi_{x}(V_{I})=\begin{cases}\dim V_{I},~~x\not\in V_{I},\\
\dim V_{I}-1,~x\in V_{I}\end{cases}

so if
x\in\Omega_{\mathcal{I}}
for
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{t}\}
then we have

n-2\geq\sum_{j=1}^{t}\dim V_{I_{j}}-1_{x\in V_{I_{j}}}\geq n-\sum_{j=1}^{t}1_{x\in V_{I_{j}}}

where we used the
NC
property of the collection
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
. We conclude that there are some
j\neq j^{\prime}
such that
x\in V_{I_{j}}\cap V_{I_{j^{\prime}}}
. Now this implies that if
|\Omega_{\mathcal{I}}|>t^{2}
then there are some
x\neq x^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{I}}
so that
x,x^{\prime}\in V_{I_{j}}\cap V_{I_{j^{\prime}}}
. This in turn implies that
\dim V_{I_{j}}\cap V_{I_{j^{\prime}}}\geq 1
and so

\dim\pi_{x}(V_{I_{j}\cup I_{j^{\prime}}}\setminus\{x\})\leq\dim\pi_{x}(V_{I_{j}}\setminus\{x\})+\dim\pi_{x}(V_{I_{j^{\prime}}}\setminus\{x\}).

We conclude that
x\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
where
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is the partition of
[m]
obtained from
\mathcal{I}
by merging
I_{j}
and
I_{j^{\prime}}
. So by pigeonhole principle, we can find some
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
with less parts than
\mathcal{I}
such that
|\Omega_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}|\gtrsim_{m}|\Omega_{\mathcal{I}}|
. So assuming that
C(n,m)
is sufficiently large, by repeating this argument we eventually end up at the trivial partition
\{[m]\}
of
[m]
for which it is clear that
\Omega_{\{[m]\}}=\emptyset
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 7.27
.

Let
\mu
be a measure on
V\cap B^{n}(0,1)
such that
\mu
is
(w,\tau)
-irreducible in
V
for some
\tau\leq 1/2
. Let
Q,U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be transversal subspaces with
\dim Q+\dim U=n-1
and
d(Q,U)\geq c
and consider the radial projection map
\pi_{Q}^{U}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Q\to U
.

Suppose that
V\not\subset U
and
\dim\pi_{Q}^{U}(V)\geq 1
.

Then for
\varepsilon\leq w
the measure
\pi_{Q}^{U}(\mu|_{V\setminus Q(\varepsilon)})
is nonzero and
(cw,2\tau)
-irreducible in
\pi_{Q}^{U}(V)
.

Proof.

For ease of notation, let
\pi=\pi_{Q}^{U}
. Since
\dim\pi(V)\geq 1
,
\pi(V)
has nontrivial affine subspaces.
Let
G\subset\pi(V)
be a proper affine subspace, and note that since
G
is proper, there exists
v\in V
such that
\pi(v)\notin G
.
Therefore,
v\notin\pi^{-1}(G)
, and hence
v\notin L=\pi^{-1}(G)\cap V
, so
L
is a proper subflat of
V
.

By definition,
L\subset\pi^{-1}(G(\delta))\cap V
, and furthermore, by the law of sines (using that
d(Q,U)\geq c
),

\pi^{-1}(G(\delta))\cap V\subset L(c^{-1}\delta).

Denote

\nu=\mu|_{V\setminus Q(\varepsilon)}.

Since
\varepsilon\leq w
, we first note that

\mu(Q(\varepsilon)\cap V)\leq\mu((Q\cap V)(\varepsilon))\leq\tau\,\mu(B^{n}(0,1)),

so

\nu(B^{n}(0,1))=\mu(B^{n}(0,1))-\mu(Q(\varepsilon)\cap V)\geq(1-\tau)\,\mu(B^{n}(0,1)).

Now, because
\mu
is
(w,\tau)
-irreducible in
V
, we have for any proper affine subspace

G\subset\pi(V)
and any
0<\delta\leq cw
,

\pi\nu(G(\delta))=\nu(\pi^{-1}(G(\delta)))\leq\nu(L(c^{-1}\delta))\leq\tau\,\mu(B^{n}(0,1)).

Dividing both sides by
\nu(B^{n}(0,1))
yields

\frac{\pi\nu(G(\delta))}{\nu(B^{n}(0,1))}\leq\frac{\tau}{1-\tau}\leq 2\tau,

provided
\tau\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Hence
\pi\nu
is
(cw,2\tau)
-irreducible in
\pi(V)
.
∎

(Theorem 1.13 of

]

Let

Lemma A.3
.

For every
\varepsilon>0

Let

Immunoinformatics-based strategies for developing DNA and mRNA vaccines against monkeypox virus (MPXV)
Authors: Siyab Khan, Ikram Ullah, Muhammad Rizwan, Yufeng Zhang, Adnan Zeb, Ubaidullah, Amir Hamza, Shoutao Zhang, Xiaodan Lu, Xuechao Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The monkeypox virus (MPXV) is a newly discovered zoonotic orthopoxvirus that can infect humans and shares similarities with the smallpox virus. With no clinically validated treatment for MPXV infections, it is important to develop a broad-range vaccine that is effective against this disease. This study aimed to design novel multiple-epitope DNA and mRNA vaccines against MXPV using comprehensive immunoinformatics and reverse vaccinology techniques. Eleven MPXV proteins were selected from the UniProt database and assessed for their antigenicity and allergenicity. Proteins exhibiting significant antigenicity and non-allergenic characteristics were examined for the prediction of T-cell and B-cell epitopes. Four MHC-I, eight MHC-II, and six B-cell epitopes were coupled with specific linkers and adjuvant peptide sequences to boost the immunological response to the developed vaccine. The designed vaccines showed antigenic nature with a 0.5936 score and solubility nature with a 0.513 score, and its GRAVY score of 0.147 indicates their hydrophilic nature. Structural validation confirmed the superior tertiary structure of the designed vaccines. Molecular docking studies demonstrated a robust interaction between human TLR-8 and the developed vaccines, with a docking score of -1208.2 kcal/mol, and Moleculae Dynamics (MD) simulations confirmed its stability. The immune simulation results indicated that vaccination strongly stimulated immunity, resulting in high concentrations of IgG and IgM antibodies. Cloning analysis and in silico restriction prediction demonstrated the viability of integrating the developed vaccine into an Escherichia coli (E. coli) expression system. Furthermore, an mRNA vaccine was developed by incorporating a 5′ cap, 5′ untranslated region (UTR), Kozak sequence, and tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) with the CD40 ligand (CD40L) linked to the selected epitopes using EAAAK linkers. A poly (A) tail, MITD1, and 3′ UTR were appended to the 3′ end of the construct. The mRNA vaccine design incorporated codon optimization, resulting in a CAI score 0.83 and GC content of 60.46%, indicating efficient vaccine expression within host cells. Analysis of several parameters revealed that the architecture of the synthesized mRNA was stable with MFE: -2170.70 kcal/mol. These outcomes may contribute to the development of an experimental MPXV vaccine with stronger potency and superior safety measures. Additional in vitro and in vivo experiments are required to test the safety and efficacy of these newly developed vaccines.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12985-025-02982-6.

Introduction

Monkeypox virus MPXV is a double-stranded DNA virus that is classified within the orthopoxviral genus of the Poxviridae family. The zoonotic nature of MPXV facilitates its transmission between humans and animals [26]. Phylogenetic analyses have revealed that MPXV and the Variola Virus (VARV) share a common ancestral virus strain. The genetic structure of MPXV endows it with the ability to infect various mammalian species, whereas VARV is characterized by its human-specific evolutionary trajectory [34]. MPXV is a DNA virus that produces symptoms similar to smallpox [84]. MPXV was named after its smallpox-like effects in monkeys in 1958 [76]. Currently, MPXV has two clades: clade I, which includes subclades Ia and Ib; and clade II, which includes subclades IIa and IIb. The Congo Basin lineage in Central Africa has been documented more frequently than the West African lineage. Note that human-to-human transmission occurred in the Congo Basin lineage, but not in the West African branch. The 2022–2023 global MPXV outbreak is linked to Clade IIb. The West African subtype causes milder illness and is the most prevalent in the current epidemic [80].

Non-human primates are carriers rather than natural hosts, and the virus is transmitted from rodents to humans [95, 103]. Human infections predominantly occur through the handling of infected animals or their materials as well as via respiratory droplets, skin contact with lesions, and fomites [10]. MPXV spreads through direct physical contact, including contact with rashes, scabs, bodily fluids, contaminated surfaces, and respiratory secretions. Transmission may also occur via placental transfer and contact with animals [81, 52]. Fatigue, headache, fever, myalgia, and lymphadenopathy are among the early signs of MPX, which help to distinguish it from smallpox. Within one to two days, mucosal lesions emerge in the mouth, followed by skin lesions, particularly in the arms, face, and legs. The number of lesions may increase from a few thousand to several thousand, and may spread all over the body. The incubation period for MPXV typically lasts 6 to 13 days, whereas it can last up to 21 days. The risk of spreading infection increases significantly among people living close to infected people [80, 89, 30].

Although there are currently no vaccines specifically developed for MPXV, several smallpox vaccines are being utilized under emergency protocols. These include second-generation vaccines such as ACAM2000 and third-generation attenuated vaccines, including LC16m8 and the Modified Vaccinia Ankara-Bavarian Nordic (MVA-BN), which is marketed under the names JYNNEOS, IMVAMUNE, or IMVANEX [74, 90, 122]. YNNEOS is presently the primary vaccine employed in outbreak response efforts due to its enhanced safety profile. Nonetheless, the variable efficacy estimates, supply constraints, and the requirement for cold-chain storage highlight the urgent need for innovative and scalable vaccine platforms [2]. Consequently, the development of an effective MPXV vaccine is crucial for preventing future outbreaks.

The reasons behind the use of a multi-epitope mRNA vaccine approach are anchored by the recent achievements in the vaccinology of infectious diseases. The quick design and dissemination of mRNA vaccinations against SARS-CoV-2 is a case in point, as it exhibited safety profiles of about 95% in clinical trials, and considerable efficacy in the prevention of severe disease.[8, 87]. This is the key strength of the platform: quick design, high potency, and scalability to manufacturing. The multi-epitope approach has shown promise in preclinical tests against a complicated pathogen. As an example, Corbett et al. [20] created an mRNA vaccine with a receptor-binding domain (RBD) immunogen, on which the Moderna COVID-19 vaccine was built [20]. Furthermore, Zhang et al. (2023) have designed an mRNA-lipid nanoparticle vaccine that encodes MPXV-specific antigens (A29L, M1R, A35R, and B6R) with nucleoside modifications. This expression generated potent neutralizing antibodies and potent Th1-biased cellular immunity in murine models, which is preclinical evidence towards an MPXV mRNA vaccine [123]. Likewise, a multi-epitope SARS-CoV-2 DNA vaccine containing conserved T-cell and B-cell epitopes has been demonstrated to induce widespread and robust immunological responses in animals, which supports the potential of such practice as a universal vaccine [109]. Taken together, these studies highlight the applicability and effectiveness of the multi-epitope approach using DNA or mRNA platforms, which provides a generally compelling case to apply to MPXV.

Advances in computational immunology have enabled the identification of novel therapeutic targets. In silico techniques are beneficial for vaccine development and reduce costs. mRNA and DNA vaccines are promising alternatives to traditional platforms, offering advantages such as stability, cost-effectiveness, rapid development, and the capability to boost cellular and humoral immunity. These attributes are particularly valuable for addressing emerging threats such as MPXV [64, 114]. Multiepitope vaccines represent an innovative strategy against pathogens [3, 59]. Antigen identification is crucial for vaccine development [98]. Effective vaccines contain antigenic peptides with overlapping B- and T-cell epitopes, which stimulate immune responses [120]. Recent advancements in immunoinformatics have led to the design of subunit vaccines incorporating adjuvants and conformational linkers targeting B-cell epitopes, helper T-lymphocytes (HTL), and cytotoxic T-lymphocytes (CTL) against the Zika virus [96]. Furthermore, the recent success of mRNA vaccine technology during the COVID-19 pandemic demonstrates its potential for rapid development, high potency, and scalable manufacturing against emerging threats[33, 106]. These advancements signify a transformative approach in vaccinology that elicits robust immune responses without the need for live pathogens [82].

In the present study, several epitope DNA and mRNA vaccines targeting MPXV were designed using immunoinformatic techniques. The primary objective of this study was to identify T-cell and B-cell epitopes from critical MPXV proteins that exhibit high conservation, immunogenicity, and non-toxicity. We combine these epitopes into a singular construct, integrating suitable adjuvants and linkers to enhance immunogenicity. The designed vaccine underwent a comprehensive in silico evaluation to assess its physicochemical properties, structural stability, interactions with TLR-8, and its potential to elicit robust immune responses. This research enhances our understanding of systematic of innovative vaccine design. However, Additional experimental verification is required to confirm these results in vivo.

Methodology

Protein sequences retrieval

The UniProt database (https://www.uniprot.org/) was used to collect 11 reviewed MPXV viral proteins (Proteome ID: UP000101269) in the FAST file format (Supplementary Table S1). The proteins were sent to the VaxiJen v2.0 system (http://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/) to check for antigenicity[29]. This service is based on an epitopes physicochemical and alignment-independent properties using cross-covariance (ACC) transformation [29]. The highly antigenic proteins, with a threshold of 0.4 were selected for the allergenicity analysis. The proteins were submitted to the AllerTOP server (http://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/AllerTop/) to predict their allergenicity of the proposed proteins for immunization development [28]. The server predicts allergic and non-allergic reactions using the k-nearest neighbor approach (kNN, k = 1).

T-cell epitopes prediction

Candidate peptides that boost human immunity may produce memory immune cells that target MPXV [22, 65]. The Immune Epitope Database (IEDB) (http://tools.iedb.org/mhci/) was used to determine the HTL MCH-I epitopes of the selected proteins using the recommended allele list provided by the server [6, 75]. The server used gapped sequence alignment and the NetMHC-4.0 method to determine how well peptides and MHC class I bind with a percentile rank of ≤ 1.0. Similarly, the HTL 15 amino acids extending MHC-II epitopes of the chosen proteins were estimated using the IEDB (http://tools.iedb.org/mhcii/) server, using the seven reference allele sets of human HLAs [115].

Prediction of B-cell epitopes

The BepiPred-2.0 of the IEDB server (http://tools.iedb.org/bcell/) was used to estimate B-cell epitopes from protein sequences with a limit of 0.5. BepiPred-2.0 is based on a random forest algorithm trained using epitopes derived from antibody-antigen protein structures [51].

Antigenicity, allergenicity, and toxicity analysis of the epitope

The designed epitope vaccine must be antigenic, non-allergenic, and non-toxic. Therefore, the antigenic, non-allergenic, and non-toxic properties of promiscuous B-cell and T-cell cells need to be investigated. The antigenic properties of the shortlisted epitopes were assessed using the Vaxijen v2.0 server, with an accuracy of 70–80% and a probability threshold score of 0.4, applied for each antigen. Component vaccines do not have negative side effects. The AllerTOP server was used to detect allergens and the non-allergenic potential of shortlisted epitopes, and to ensure that the epitopes did not elicit an allergic reaction in individuals. The ToxinPred server (crdd.osdd.net/raghava/toxinpred/) was used to predict the potential toxicity of peptides [42].

Human homology assessment of the selected epitope

To bypass autoimmune responses, vaccine targets must differ from human proteins; consequently, BLASTP (BLAST the Basic Local Alignment Search Tool (BLASTP) was established. The chosen epitopes were analyzed against the human proteome (Taxid: 9606) to eliminate proteins exhibiting more than 30% similarity to the human proteome. This study employed an E-value threshold of 0.05 and epitopes that reported hits below this E-value threshold were categorized as non-homologous peptides [59, 83].

Immunogenicity assessments of MHC-I and B-cell selected epitopes

The IEDB approach (http://tools.iedb.org/immunogenicity/) was used to assess the immunogenicity of the filtered MHC-I and B-cell epitopes to ascertain the appropriateness of the immune response development in the human body [14].

TAP transporter and conservation analysis of MHC-I-predicted epitopes

The filtered MHC-I epitopes were subjected to TAPRED (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/tappred/). TAPPred is an online server used to identify the binding affinity of peptides to TAP transporters. TAP-binding peptide prediction is essential for determining MHC-I-restricted T-cell epitopes. The amino acid sequence and its characteristics were used to generate cascade support vector machine (SVM) prediction. The jackknife validation test yielded a correlation value of 0.88 [11]. The conservation of epitope sequences among all identified pathogen sequences is crucial for the development of broad-spectrum immunization strategies. Epitope conservancy was predicted using the online conservancy analysis tool (Epitope Conservancy Analysis (iedb.org) [14].

Prediction of IFN-γ-producing MCH-II epitopes

MHC-II-activated CD+ T-helper cells play a critical role in the regulation of infections through the production of interferon-gamma (IFN-γ). The capacity of the MHC-II epitopes to trigger IFN-γ production was predicted using the IFN-epitope server. (http://crdd.osdd.net/raghava/ifnepitope/). Hybrid blend motifs and SVM algorithms to forecast the interferon yield ability of epitopes. The server assigned a score to each epitope based on SVM analysis. The selected MHC-II epitopes were capable of inducing an IFN-γ response, while meeting the established parameters for a multi-epitope vaccine [25].

IL-4 and IL-10 inducing MHC-II epitopes

The selected MCH-II epitopes were tested for their ability to produce IL-4 and IL-10. In reaction to helminths and other external parasites, CD4 + T lymphocytes generate IL-4. It controls antibody class switching, hematopoiesis, inflammation, and effector T-cell responses. IL4pred (http://crdd.osdd.net/raghava/il4pred/) predicted the IL4-inducing capacity. The IL4pred server was based on five-fold cross-validation with four datasets, with 75.76% accuracy and 0.51% MCC [24]. Homodimeric interleukin IL-10 suppresses inflammation and regulates immune cell development and differentiation. The IL10pred IL-10pred server (http://crdd.osdd.net/raghava/IL-10pred/) was used to detect IL-10 induction. A state-of-the-art dipeptide composition approach was surpassed by the server, which had an MCC of 0.5938 and an accuracy of 81.24% [102].

IL-13 Inducing properties of B-cell epitopes

The IL-13-inducing epitopes were identified using the IL13pred web server (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/il13pred/). The in silico approach for predicting IL13-inducing epitopes was termed as IL13pred. Interleukins are a distinct class of molecules that perform diverse immunological signalling functions. Mast cells, basophils, and activated T-helper 2 cells are primary sources of IL-13. The development of safe protein vaccines and therapies requires the ability to predict peptides or segments of a protein that stimulate IL-13 production [50].

Population coverage of MHC epitopes

The filtered MHC-I and MHC-II epitopes were submitted to the IEDB server (http://tools.iedb.org/population/) to predict population coverage worldwide. The tool computes the percentage coverage scores of the interactions of filter epitopes with the most frequent MHC-I- and MHC-II-restricted alleles.

Epitope modelling and molecular docking

Epitopes with robust affinities for experimentally proven alleles are considered good candidates for constructing efficient multi-epitope vaccines. The binding affinity between the selected MHC-I and MHC-II epitopes and human leukocyte antigens (HLA) was determined by in silico molecular docking, which highlighted the interaction between HLA and epitopes [37]. The PEPFOLD (https://mobyle.rpbs.univ-paris-diderot.fr/cgi-bin/portal.py#forms::PEP-FOLD3) online server was used to predict the 3D structures of selected epitopes [37, 66]. The Protein Data Bank (PDB) was used to obtain the tertiary structure of the most prevalent human allele, HLA-B7 (PDB ID:3VCL). Molecular docking of HLA-B7 and the selected epitopes was performed using the HDOCK online server [107]. The docking complexes were visualized using PyMOL software [23].

Multiple epitopes vaccine construct

The vaccine was designed to improve the immunogenicity of the peptides using a specific linker to connect the selected epitopes. Beta-defensin 2 (Uniprot ID- AAC69554.1) was connected to the front portion of the vaccine as an adjuvant through an EAAK linker to increase the immunogenicity of the vaccine [58]. EAAK linker provides an Alpha helix-forming structure that increases stability, maintains a constant distance, and maintains the independent function of domains [7, 16].Furthermore, MHC-I epitopes are interconnected using AAY linkers, which signify proteasomal cleavage sites that enhance protein stability [99, 120]. The AAY dipeptide is a canonical signal for proteasomal cleavage in mammalian cells. Its incorporation ensures efficient intracellular processing of the vaccine polypeptide, leading to the precise liberation of CTL epitopes for loading onto MHC-I molecules and subsequent CD8+ T-cell activation [94]. Moreover, MHC-II is connected by GPGPG linkers. The GPGPG is a glycine-rich flexible linker which renowned in vaccine design for maintaining the independent immunogenicity of individual epitopes. It effectively prevents the formation of novel "junctional epitopes" at fusion points, which could potentially lead to unwanted immune responses or tolerization, thereby ensuring correct CD4+ T-cell activation [71]. The KK linker was chosen to connect B-cell epitopes. The di-lysine motif is highly soluble and, most importantly, serves as a target site for proteolytic cleavage by endolysosomal proteases, such as cathepsin B. This is essential for the exogenous pathway of antigen processing, facilitating the efficient loading of B-cell epitopes onto MHC-II molecules to elicit crucial T-cell help for B-cell activation and antibody production [59, 69, 121].

Antigenic, non-allergenic, and solubility properties of multiple epitopes vaccine model

The boosting of the immune system critically depends on the antigenic properties of the vaccine. The antigenicity of the vaccination design was assessed using the publicly available VaxiJen v2.0 server [29]. This server has an accuracy between 70 and 80% [29]. AllerTOP server was used to search for allergenic sequences [28]. The online server, Protein-Sol (http://protein-sol.manchester.ac.uk/), was used to predict the solubility of the designed vaccine. The production of recombinant proteins and biotherapeutic development critically depend on protein solubility [44].

Physicochemical characterization of the developed vaccine

The vaccine model was functionally characterized using the ProtParam tool on the ExPASy site (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/). Other physicochemical parameters studied include estimated half-life, aliphatic index, molecular weight, hydropathicity, theoretical isoelectric point (pI), and GRAVY values [36].

Antiviral and anti-inflammatory properties of the constructed vaccine

To predict the antiviral and anti-inflammatory proportions of the designed vaccine, the sequence was submitted to PreTP-Stack server (http://bliulab.net/PreTP-Stack/server), with a default threshold of 0.5. The server predicted the antiviral, antibacterial, anticancer, anti-angiogenic, and anti-inflammatory peptides. The server used the dataset designed by Wei et al. [116]. The server contains 1902 antiviral and 6290 anti-inflammatory peptides [119].

Secondary structure prediction

The secondary structure prediction approach (SOPMA) (https://npsa-prabi.ibcp.fr/cgi-bin/npsa_automat.pl?page=/NPSA/npsa_sopma.html) was used to create the secondary structure of the designed model. The window width was 17, the similarity threshold was 8, and the conformational state (helix, sheet, turn, and coil) was 4 using default settings [18].

Tertiary structure prediction

The tertiary structure of the potential vaccine was determined using two independent, state of art modeling servers to ensure robustness and reliabity. the Robetta online server (http://robetta.bakerlab.org) [60] and AlphaFold 3 server (https://www.alphafoldserver.com) [1]. The Robetta server finds templates for a specific amino acid sequence using a comparative modelling method based on BLAST, FFAS03, or 3d-jury. It computes up to 20 PDB targets that have been pre-released each week using Continuous Automated Model Evaluation (CAMEO). The average Local Distance Difference Test (LDDT) score for this server was approximately 69, suggesting a highly independent score of 47.

AlphaFold 3 was also used in order to take advantage of the recent achievements in the field of AI-based structure prediction. AlphaFold 3 is good at predicting complexes based on nearly all types of molecules present in PDB Unlike template-based methods, AlphaFold 3 is an end-to-end deep learning system that learns to predict the 3D structure of a protein directly given its amino acid sequence through a novel diffusion-based architecture. It produces a multiple sequence alignment (MSA) to deduce evolutionary constraints, and an extremely precise, atomic-level model. The main confidence value that AlphaFold 3 uses is the predicted TM-score (pTM); This value is used to estimate the overall-fold accuracy on a 0–1 scale. A pTM score of more than 0.5 indicates a proper fold and a score of more than 0.8 indicates a high accuracy model [1, 118].

The tertiary structure refinement and validation

The resolution of the tertiary structure improved from that of the original low-resolution estimation through refinement. The online tool GalaxyRefine (http://galaxy.seoklab.org/) was used to refine the predicted structures of vaccine constructs [45]. The tertiary structure was verified using the ERRAT server (http://services.mbi.ucla.edu/ERRAT/). ERRAT is an algorithm designed to verify protein structures and assess the quality of crystallographic models, including how well they are constructed and refined. It is managed by the National Health Institute of the University of California, USA [17]. ProSA-web was utilized to validate the tertiary structure based on the predicted Z-score [117]. The ROCHECK website (https://saves.mbi.ucla.edu/PROCHECK/) was used to create a Ramachandran plot to assess the overall quality of the vaccine construct. The Ramachandran plot, which manipulates the dihedral angles psi (ψ) and phi (Φ) to demonstrate their energy favorability and prohibition, respectively, uses the van der Waals radius of the side chain [67].

Conformational B-cell epitopes prediction

ElliPro operates on the premise that residues protruding from the protein surface exhibit enhanced accessibility to antibody binding. The protruding residues were identified by modelling the protein as an ellipsoid. Supplementary epitopes are discontinuous or conformational, underscoring their dependence on the three-dimensional structure of the antigenic vaccine rather than its linear sequence [88].

Engineering of disulfide bonds

Proteins with unique disulfide connections have been widely employed to enhance stability, alter functional properties, and aid the investigation of protein dynamics. Disulfide linkages were formed in the refined vaccine models using the DbD2 server (cpt. wayne. edu/DbD2/). The intrachain, interchain, and Cβ structures of glycine residues were selected during the prediction process. Angles of 87° or + 97° ± 5° for χ3 and 114.6° ± 10° for Cα − Cײ-S׳ were maintained at a constant value. By employing the generated mutant function of the Disulfide by Design 2.12 server, we successfully identified residue pairings that may be amenable to mutation into cysteine. The B-factors of the protein areas implicated in the projected disulfide bonds were analyzed using a server. Notably, this characteristic makes it easier to identify possible disulfides to improve the thermal stability of the protein and increase its likelihood of formation [21].

Molecular docking of vaccine construct with TLR-8

The possible binding positions of the vaccination design were predicted using molecular docking. First, the 3D structure of TLR-8 (PDB ID:3W3J) was retrieved from the Protein Databank Data Bank (PDB) https://www.rcsb.org) [38, 111] (The Cluspro 2.0 server (https://cluspro.org/login.php) was then used to dock human TLR-8 (PDB ID:3W3JID: 3W3J) with a vaccine construct [19, 38]. The Cluspro 2.0 server carries out the following three computing stages. Three main approaches were applied to optimize the identified structures: (1) rigid body docking, in which billions of conformations were sampled; (2) clustering of the 1000 lowest energy structures using Root Mean Square devation (RMSD) to find strong clusters to yield the most likely models of the complex; and (3) energy minimization of selected structures. PIPER is docking software that uses the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) correlation approach in the rigid body docking stage [62]. The PDBsum server (ebi. ac. uk/thornton-srv/databases/pdbsum/Generate.html) was used to prepare schematic diagrams and visualize the binding interactions between the docked files [68].

Immune simulation

To anticipate the immune systems real-life reaction to multi-epitope vaccine construction, in silico immune simulation was performed using the C-ImmSim server (http://kraken.iac.rm.cnr.it/CIMMSIM/) using position-specific scoring matrices (PSSM) and machine learning [91]. The shortest waiting time is when the first and second doses of the given vaccination are four weeks during the majority. The target MPXV vaccine was administered at three doses with a four-week interval. The timestamps of 1, 84, and 168 were chosen so that each period was equivalent to eight hours. The other settings remained default, and the simulation step was set to 1050 [15].

Normal mode simulation

To confirm the stability of the vaccination and the physical movements of the atoms and molecules within the TLR-8 vaccine, the complex structures were determined to exhibit the lowest binding energy [73]. This computational tool evaluates the physical dynamics and structural integrity of the docked complex. By employing normal mode analysis (NMA), the iMOD server calculates the internal coordinates of the proteins, thereby enabling the evaluation of their stability. Various indicators, including eigenvalues, have been used to evaluate protein stability using the elastic network model, covariance matrix, main chain deformability plot, and B factors [72].

Codon optimization and in silico cloning of the designed vaccine

The vaccine construct was optimized in the site (www.prodo-ric.de/JCat/) by maximizing the production of the proteins in the Escherichia coli (E. coli) K12 strain through reverse translation and codon optimization of the assigned vaccine constructs. EcoRI and BamHI restriction enzymes were inserted into the N-terminal and C-terminal sites of the expression vector to streamline this process. JCat improves the production of heterologous proteins by tailoring the codon usage of target genes to align them with the most sequenced prokaryotes and specific eukaryotic gene expression hosts [39]. To measure the transcription and translation efficiency in the proposed vaccine design, the output files in the server produced codon adaptation index (CAI) greater than 0.8 and a GC content ranging from 30% to 70%. SnapGene software (https://www.snapgene.com/) was used for in silico cloning and to provide a visual representation of the recombinant vaccine.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation

To rigorously evaluate the stability and binding dynamics of the vaccine-TLR-8 complex, MD simulations were conducted using the Schrödinger LLC Desmond software up to 500 ns [46]. Newtons classical equations of motion guide MD simulations to calculate atomic movement over time, through which ligand-binding conditions in biological solutions can be determined [92]. The Protein Preparation Wizard tool in Maestro was used to pre-process the ligand-receptor complex, wherein optimization and minimization processes were carried out alongside the completion of the incomplete residue. System Builder tools were used to generate the system. The prepared complex was solvated in an orthorhombic periodic boundary box using the explicit TIP3P (Transferable Intermolecular Interaction potential with a Points) water model [53]. System neutrality and physiological ionic strength were achieved by incorporating Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions at a concentration of 0.15 M through the System Builder tool. The final system comprised 190,254 atoms, including the protein complex (17,063 atoms,1,074 residues, 57,617 water molecules, 179 Cl⁻ ions, and 161 Na⁺ ions.

The MD simulation was performed under isothermal-isobaric (NPT) ensemble conditions to emulate a physiological environment. The temperature was maintained at 300 K using the Nosé-Hoover chain thermostat, while the pressure was regulated at 1 atm with the Martyna-Tobias-Klein barostat [77]. The OPLS_2005 force field was applied throughout the system [101]. Long-range electrostatic interactions were managed using the PME (Particle Mesh Ewald) method, with a cutoff radius of 9.0 Å for short-range van der Waals and Coulomb interactions. The system underwent a multi-stage relaxation protocol, including minimization, NVT, and NPT, before the production run. An unrestrained production simulation was conducted over 500 ns. The integration time step was set to 2.0 fs, and the trajectory was recorded at 100 ps intervals for analysis.

mRNA vaccine construction

Research conducted by Ahammad et al. utilized methods to develop an mRNA-targeting MPXV [3]. The epitopes in the vaccine were carefully chosen based on their antigenicity, lack of toxicity, allergenicity, and capacity to generate cytokines. CTL, HTL, and LBL epitopes were linked using specific linkers. Adjuvants play a vital role in the design of effective vaccines with enhanced immunogenicity [12]. In this study, CD40 ligand (CD40L) was used as an adjuvant to activate the human immune receptor through its interaction with antigen-presenting cells. The CD40L sequence (UniProt ID: P29965) was obtained from the UniProt database and was potentially linked to the selected epitopes via EAAAK linkers. A tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) (UniProt ID: P00750) secretory signal sequence was incorporated at the 5′ end. Additionally, an MHC-I targeting domain (MITD) (UniProt ID: Q8WV92) was added to the 3′ end of the open reading frame (ORF). The 5′ end was modified with 7-methyl(3-O-methyl) GpppG Cap (ARCA), whereas the 5′ untranslated region (UTR) of human β-globin (NCBI ID: NM 000.518.5) was used to flank both the 5′ and 3′ ends of the mRNA vaccine. A poly (A) tail consisting of 120 nucleotides was added to the 3′-end of the vaccine. However, the development of mRNA-based therapeutics has resulted in instability. It is essential to incorporate elements inherently present in eukaryotic mRNA that contribute to their stabilization. Prior research has shown that the length of the polyadenylate (poly A) tail influences the mRNA translation efficiency [113]. The poly (A) tail size used in this study ranged from 120 to 150 bp.

mRNA vaccine secondary structure prediction and codon optimization

The translation of mRNA vaccines in host cells is dependent on codon optimization. mRNA vaccine codons were optimized using the GenSmart codon optimization tool server (https://www.genscript.com/tools/gensmart-codon-optimization) to be expressed in human cells. Ultimately, to determine the quality of the optimized codons, we employed the following parameters (https://www.genscript.com/tools/rare-codon-analysis) to evaluate three major parameters in terms of expression of a good protein: mRNA translation efficiency, CAI, and codon frequency distribution (CFD) for identifying uncommon tandem codons and GC content. The DNA > RNA > Protein tool transcribes the DNA sequences that have been optimized by the GenSmart codon optimization tool into RNA sequences (https://biomodel.uah.es/en/lab/cybertory/analysis/trans.htm). RNAfold from the Vienna RNA Package 2.0 predicts the secondary structures of the mRNA constructs (http://rna.tbi.univie.ac.at/cgi-bin/RNAWebSuite/RNAfold.cgi) [40]. In this server, the minimum free energy (MFE) fraction was used to predict the thermodynamic structure of mRNA. Here, the MFE structure, centroid secondary structure, and minimum free nergy were examined.

Construction of multiple-epitope vaccine

Overlapping MCH-I, MCH-II, and B-cell epitopes were used to construct a highly immunogenic, multi-epitope vaccine. An AAY linker was used to connect the appropriate MHC-I epitopes, and MHC-II epitopes were joined using a GPGPG linker. In contrast, we showed that B-cell epitopes were linked using a KK linker. The EAAK linker containing the β-defensin 2 adjuvant (Uniprot ID- AAC69554.1) was attached to the N-terminus of the vaccine epitope peptide sequence (Fig. 3). These epitope-specific linkers are essential for the design of immunogenic multiepitope vaccines. These epitopes can independently trigger immune responses in a single construct, without mutual interference. The final length of the constructed vaccine was 394 amino acids (Supplementary Table S8).Fig. 3Complete vaccination, B- and T-cell epitopes with Beta defensin 2 adjuvant

Physicochemical properties, antigenicity, solubility, allergenicity, and of the final vaccine constructs

The ProtParam server determined that the vaccine construct had a molecular weight of 42.44 kDa, indicating that it was likely to be easily purifiable. A GRAVY score of 0.147 indicates the hydrophilic nature of the designed vaccine constructs. The theoretical pI value was calculated to be 9.49, indicating a slightly acidic nature of the construct. The aliphatic index was calculated to be 91.57, suggesting that these structures are thermally stable. The instability index was estimated to be 33.60, indicating that these structures remained stable across a range of temperatures. In the yeast and E. coli expression systems, the half-lives of the vaccine sequences were predicted to be greater than 10 and 30 min, respectively. Furthermore, the MPXV vaccine construct was anticipated to exhibit a solid antigenic nature (0.5936), surpassing a threshold of 0.4. The Protein-Sol web server also predicted a solubility score of 0.513 for the construct, indicating a high solubility upon expression (Fig. 4). Secondary and tertiary structure prediction

The secondary structure of a protein molecule refers to the arrangement of a specific peptide chain within the molecule. The main types of structures include helices, β-folds, β-corners, and irregular curls. The SOPMA server was used to confirm the secondary structure of the vaccine. It consists of an alpha helix, an extended strand, a beta-turn, and a random coil, accounting for 31.98%, 24.87%, 9.39%, and 33.76%, respectively (Fig. 5). Next, we submitted the sequences of the final vaccines to the Robetta server and AlphaFold 3 server for 3D structural modelling. The Robetta server, recognized as the most accurate and reliable server in 2014, uses CAMEO. The Robetta server produced five models of the final constructed vaccines, from which the optimal model was selected for refinement and validation through a quality analysis (Fig. 6). mRNA Construction

We analyzed all the identified epitopes to ascertain their interactions and potential as mRNA vaccines. The mRNA vaccine was developed by combining the 5′ cap,5′ UTR (human β-globin, NCBI ID: NM 000518.5), Kozak sequence, and tPA (signal peptide) with an adjuvant CD40L and coupling it to the targeted peptides using EAAAK linkers at the target site. At the 3′ end of the mRNA vaccine, we integrated a poly (A) tail, MITD1, and the 3′-UTR derived from human β-globin (NCBI ID: NM_000518.5) (Supplementary Table S8).

Optimization of codons and prediction of secondary structures for the mRNA vaccine

GenSmart codon optimization technology helped maximize codon usage of the construct and improve mRNA translation efficiency within host cells. The resultant mRNA had a total length of 5,142 base pairs. The quality of optimal sequences was evaluated using an analytical tool for rare codons. The CAI score was calculated to be 0.83, which indicates that the expression levels in the host organism are reasonable (Fig. 15A). CFD revealed a value of 0.03%, indicating the absence of atypical tandem codons in the optimal sequence. Uncommon tandem codons may disrupt translational machinery, resulting in decreased translation efficiency. A GC-range of 30 to 70% would represent the optimal expression range. The adjusted sequence demonstrated an average GC content of 60.46%, suggesting that the vaccine candidate is appropriate for practical expression in human cells. Discussion

Vaccines are widely recognized as highly effective medical interventions that play a vital role in the prevention of infectious diseases and enhancement of public health. Although conventional vaccine designs have undoubtedly contributed significantly to global health, they also have certain limitations. Notably, the development of traditional vaccines has become increasingly complex when dealing with pathogens exhibiting antigenic hypervariability, because their immune responses may be inadequate to address these challenges [4, 86]. In this context, immunoinformatics, bioinformatics [43], and reverse vaccinology have been used to create efficient multi-epitope vaccines. Many immunoinformatics and reverse vaccinology approaches have recently demonstrated promise for the development of successful multi-epitope vaccines for COVID-19 [108], Influenza A Virus [27], Ebola virus [112], Hepatitis A and Hepatitis B virus [5], and the Zika virus [96]. Previously, DNA and RNA vaccines have shown good results in preclinical and clinical analyses of severe diseases such as Influenza, Zika virus, and Rabies [9, 70].

The global outbreak of MPXV during 2022–2023, primarily attributable to Clade IIb lineages, underscored the viruss capacity for efficient human-to-human transmission beyond its traditional endemic regions, particularly within sexual networks of men who have sex with men (MSM) [85, 110]. This epidemiological shift emphasizes the urgent necessity for specific, safe, and rapidly deployable countermeasures. Clinical data derived from this outbreak illuminated challenges that our vaccine design seeks to address. Firstly, the application of existing smallpox vaccines (e.g., JYNNEOS) under emergency use protocols revealed limitations within global supply chains and variable efficacy estimates in real-world contexts [31]. Secondly, concerns regarding the safety profile of replicating vaccines (e.g., ACAM2000) in immunocompromised individuals, a key population impacted during the outbreak, necessitated the development of safer, non-replicating platforms. [90]. Our multi-epitope vaccine design, delivered through a DNA or mRNA platform, directly addresses these deficiencies by providing a highly specific, non-replicating, and rapidly scalable vaccine candidate.

Furthermore, the conserved nature of the epitopes selected in our study is noteworthy. Despite significant genetic evolution observed in the strains associated with the 2022–2023 outbreak [49]. Our target proteins and epitopes remained 100% conserved. This finding suggests that our vaccine could confer broad protection against both historical and emerging variants, a critical advantage for a virus that continues to evolve. By concentrating on conserved regions, our design presents a potential solution to the challenges posed by antigenic variation, a key consideration for the long-term efficacy of vaccines.

Several in silico studies have been conducted to design multiple epitope-based vaccines against MPXV, with many focusing exclusively on DNA-based vaccines. For instance, Farzan et al., 2023 also designed a multi-epitope vaccine targeting MPXV, showing a strong binding affinity with TLR-4 [32]. While their study provided a foundational approach, our work extends beyond it by incorporating a more rigorous validation pipeline, including MD simulation stability metrics, which offer a deeper quantitative assessment of the vaccine-TLR-8 interaction. Our study showed superior vaccine stability with RMSD values below 3 Å after equilibration, suggesting a well-folded antigen. Furthermore, a very recent study by Ullah et al., 2023 [111] also targeted TLR-8 with their vaccine construct. Their reported molecular docking energy of −735.1 kcal/mol is comparable to our TLR-8 docking score of −1208.2 kcal/mol, suggesting robust immune receptor engagement is achievable through different design strategies. However, a key differentiator of our study is the design and comprehensive analysis of an mRNA vaccine counterpart, leveraging the platforms proven rapid development capabilities, as demonstrated during the COVID-19 pandemic [106].

Not all studies have attained optimal stability. For instance, a significant instability was reported in the vaccine-TLR-3 complex, exhibiting a RMSD of up to approximately 15 Å during simulation [78]. In contrast, our vaccine-TLR-8 complex demonstrated superior stability, with a backbone RMSD stabilizing at approximately 2.5 Å over a duration of 500 ns. This finding suggests that our epitope selection and refinement process resulted in a more rigid and reliable construct. Finally, Our approach aligns with the trend towards broader protection, as demonstrated by [93], who designed a universal mRNA vaccine. While their focus was on conservation across orthopoxviruses, our study enhances this by incorporating predictions for IL-4 and IL-10 induction, along with a detailed analysis of population coverage. This allows us to address both efficacy and potential safety aspects more comprehensively.

Our multi-epitope DNA vaccine leverages the strengths of DNA vaccine technology, including stability, scalability, and immune response induction, as noted by [64]. This approach complements the existing vaccines for broader MPXV protection. In our study, proteins that possessed good antigenicity and a non-allergenic nature were selected for epitope prediction from the MPXV proteome. The vaccine showed strong antigenic and nonallergenic properties. The designed vaccine must exhibit stability to ensure an extended half-life and retention of immunogenicity while also preventing degradation. Additionally, it should be soluble to enhance bioavailability, prevent aggregation, and facilitate efficient delivery [79]. In this study, we identified that our designed vaccine demonstrated stability, with an index of 33.60, indicating its robustness across various temperatures. The structural properties of the vaccine influence its functional attributes, including antigen presentation and the activation of B-cell and T-cell lymphocytes.

RNA vaccines are regarded as more reliable and advanced than their DNA counterparts. mRNA vaccines present numerous advantages, such as rapid and efficient development, modularity, and the ability to elicit robust and durable immune responses. Additionally, mRNA vaccines do not require live viruses, thereby minimizing the risk of adverse events related to other conventional vaccines such as MVA-BN and LC16. In this study, we built a multi-pipetted mRNA vaccine targeting MPXV by incorporating T-cell and B-cell epitopes. As an adjuvant, we used the CD40L, which activates human dendritic cells to stimulate T lymphocytes [13, 82]. The poly (A) tail influences the half-life and translation of mRNA. By incorporating an A120 tail, we observed an increase in protein expression and duration [13]. We included the human β-globin gene, which enhances mRNA translation efficiency and alters seroconversion, cytokine patterns, and antibody titers [47]. To cap the 5′ end, we used GpppG Cap 7-methyl(3-O-methyl), known as ARCA [105]. The 3′ poly(A) tail and the 5′ end cap work together to control mRNA translational efficiency [35]. The MITD sequence has been included to improve CD4+ T-cell antigen presentation[63]. We incorporated a human tPA signal peptide [61] to enhance immunogenicity. The EAAAK linker was used to attain higher expression and stability, facilitate coupling between epitopes and adjuvants, and boost the fused protein bioactivity. The mRNA vaccine was 5142 nucleotides. The translated epitopes were predicted to exhibit high antigenicity, stability, non-allergenicity, and were nearly neutral. After codon optimization, the sequence showed 60.46% GC content, indicating favorable expression in host cells. A CAI value of 0.83 indicates efficient codons in human cells. Free energy analysis indicated stability, with a centroid secondary structure at −1869.77 kcal/mol and MFE at −2170.70 kcal/mol. mRNA levels remained stable during the manufacturing process.

Our design leverages the development of modern platforms and immune responses, as discussed by [114]. The in vivo half-life of mRNA can be modulated and delivered like cellular operations [41, 56]. mRNA vaccines have better safety profiles and controlled immunogenicity [55]. They also offer low production costs, rapid development, and satisfactory efficacy. Unlike DNA vaccines, they exhibit greater efficacy when expressed in the cytoplasm without nuclear entry [97].

Limitation

Our study introduces novel DNA and mRNA vaccine candidates targeting MPXV. Although immunogenicity predictions are promising, it is essential to incorporate epitope variability to address the potential challenges. However, the designed construct encountered experimental limitations and required clinical trials. Given that the vaccines were developed using human MHC peptides, their efficacy needs to be evaluated in a humanized mouse model. Nonetheless, this study constitutes an important foundational step towards the development of experimental vaccines. Notably, similar multi-epitope vaccines, designed using immunoinformatic tools in silico, have demonstrated robust immunogenicity in both in vitro and in vivo models, thereby supporting the reliability of these methodologies[48, 57, 100, 104]. Furthermore, a significant limitation of the current vaccine design is its suboptimal coverage of the global population, particularly regarding MHC-II epitopes, estimated at about 14%. Moreover, the major weakness of the present vaccine design is that it does not cover global population optimally, especially in terms of MHC-II epitopes and is approximated to be approximately 14 percent. This is due to our high filtering threshold that focused on attaining the ideal state of conservancy across MPXV strains and high predicted immunogenicity as opposed to the frequency of HLA alleles. Although this promised to select basic and invariant epitopes, it led to biased selection of epitopes expressed by less frequent HLA-DR alleles. Therefore, the existing construct might possess less potency in triggering strong helper T-cell reactions in ethnic groups with varied backgrounds, which can impede its application as a vaccine in universal settings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, various criteria for the design of bioinformatics vaccines aimed at targeting MPXV have been utilized. MPXV proteins were chosen, and the antigenic, non-toxic, non-allergenic, and immunogenic T-cell epitopes, as well as B-cell epitopes, were predicted in the MPXV. These epitopes were conjugated using appropriate linkers and combined with adjuvants to form the final vaccine. The vaccine construct showed strong physicochemical characteristics, antigenicity, and non-allergenicity. In addition, the tertiary structure of the vaccine was predicted, refined, and validated, and molecular docking showed a high binding affinity for human TLR-8. In silico immune simulation and codon optimization confirmed its stable expression in the E. coli expression system. In addition, an mRNA vaccine was designed using selected epitopes that incorporated various elements to enhance stability and immunogenicity. mRNA expression was found to be safe. The mRNA vaccine was found to be stable, with a 60.46% GC content, indicating efficient expression within host cells. Further in vivo and in vitro analyses will be conducted in the near future. Cloning and expression of the DNA vaccine in E. coli will be used as an in vitro immunogenicity assay, and the mRNA vaccine will be expressed and tested in human cells. Protective efficacy against MPXV challenge will be tested in humanized mouse models in the future. In order to improve worldwide coverage, a next-generation design will include epitopes that are limited by more frequent HLA alleles. Moreover, the mRNA platform will be investigated using advanced delivery systems such as lipid nanoparticles (LNPs). These measures offer an effective route between encouraging development in silico outcomes and preclinical development.

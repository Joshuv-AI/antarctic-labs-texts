Calibrated Counterfactual Conformal Fairness ( 𝑪 𝟑 ​ 𝑭 \bm{C^{3}F} ): Post-hoc, Shift-Aware Coverage Parity via Conformal Prediction and Counterfactual Regularization
Faruk Alpay, Taylan Alpay, [4pt] Lightcap, Department of Bias alpay@lightcap.ai

Abstract
We introduce Calibrated Counterfactual Conformal Fairness (C3F), a post-hoc wrapper for predictive systems that targets group-conditional coverage parity under covariate shift. The procedure combines (i) group- and feature-conditional conformal prediction with importance-weighted quantile calibration and (ii) a counterfactual regularizer based on path-specific effects in a structural causal model. We prove finite-sample bounds for group-wise coverage that degrade gracefully with a χ 2 \chi^{2} (Rényi- α = 2 \alpha{=}2 ) divergence between calibration and target covariate distributions, and we derive a bound on the equalized conditional coverage gap (ECCG). We also analyze a tractable surrogate for counterfactual coverage parity using path-specific effects. Empirically, C3F is model-agnostic and deployable without retraining, complementing shift-aware and adaptive conformal methods and recent work on coverage fairness.

Introduction

Conformal prediction (CP) delivers finite-sample coverage under exchangeability
(
Vovk et al. 2005
;
Shafer and Vovk 2008
)
. In practice, both performance and coverage can vary across subpopulations, and distribution shift may erode guarantees. Likelihood-ratio- or weight-based CP corrects for covariate shift
(
Tibshirani et al. 2019
)
, while adaptive schemes adjust to nonstationarity over time
(
Gibbs and Candès 2021
)
. Parallel efforts seek conditional or subgroup validity
(
Vovk 2012
;
Gibbs 2023
)
and fairness-aware coverage
(
Zhou and Sesia 2024
;
Cresswell et al. 2024
)
.

We propose
C^{3}F
, a
calibrate-then-regularize
recipe to (a) attain group-conditional coverage targets under shift via importance-weighted conformal quantiles and (b) dampen unfair path-specific influence via a counterfactual regularizer inspired by causal mediation
(
Pearl 2001
;
Avin et al. 2005
;
Kusner et al. 2017
;
Chiappa 2019
)
. Our analysis frames coverage error through weighted empirical processes with
\chi^{2}
-controlled importance weights
(
Cortés et al. 2010
;
van Erven and Harremoës 2014
)
and uses classical quantile concentration
(
Massart 1990
)
.

Contributions.

•

Shift-aware group coverage.
We state a finite-sample lower bound on group-wise coverage under covariate shift with a deviation term governed by
\chi^{2}(Q\|P_{\mathrm{cal}})
.

•

Coverage-parity control.
We bound the equalized conditional coverage gap (ECCG) across groups and show how budget-splitting of miscoverage across groups trades efficiency for parity.

•

Counterfactual regularization.
We formulate a smooth surrogate for counterfactual coverage disparity using path-specific effects, proving first-order control when thresholds are regularized.

Preliminaries and notation

Let
(X,A,Y)\sim P
with covariates
X\in\mathcal{X}
, group attribute
A\in\mathcal{A}
, label
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
. A base score function
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{S}
is wrapped by CP using a nonconformity score
\eta=g(x,y;f)
. Split CP computes scores on a calibration set
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}
and chooses a quantile threshold
q
so that the resulting prediction set
\mathcal{C}(x)=\{y:\eta(x,y;f)\leq q\}
achieves target marginal coverage
\tau

(
Lei and Wasserman 2014
;
Barber et al. 2021
)
.

We focus on
group-conditional
coverage. For
a\in\mathcal{A}
and target
\tau=1-\alpha
, define

\mathrm{ECCG}(\mathcal{C})\triangleq\max_{a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\Pr\{Y\in\mathcal{C}(X)\mid A=a\}-\Pr\{Y\in\mathcal{C}(X)\mid A=a^{\prime}\}\right|.

Let
P_{\mathrm{cal}}
denote the calibration distribution and
Q
the target test distribution. Under
covariate shift

Q_{Y\mid X}=P_{\mathrm{cal},Y\mid X}
and
Q_{X}\neq P_{\mathrm{cal},X}
, define the group-conditional importance weight

w_{a}(x)\;=\;\frac{\mathrm{d}Q_{X}(\cdot\mid A=a)}{\mathrm{d}P_{\mathrm{cal},X}(\cdot\mid A=a)}(x),

assumed square-integrable so that the
\chi^{2}
-divergence is finite
(
Cortés et al. 2010
;
van Erven and Harremoës 2014
)
.

Method:
C^{3}F

3.1
Shift-aware group-conditional calibration

Compute nonconformity scores
\eta_{i}=\eta(X_{i},Y_{i};f)
on
(X_{i},A_{i},Y_{i})\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}
. For each group
a
, form a
weighted
empirical CDF using
w_{a}(X_{i})
and choose the smallest
q_{a}
with weighted tail probability at most
\alpha_{a}
:

\widehat{q}_{a}\;\triangleq\;\inf\Big\{q:\;\frac{\sum_{i:A_{i}=a}w_{a}(X_{i})\,\mathbf{1}\{\eta_{i}\leq q\}}{\sum_{i:A_{i}=a}w_{a}(X_{i})}\geq 1-\alpha_{a}\Big\}.

Here the miscoverage budget is split as
\sum_{a}\pi_{a}\alpha_{a}=\alpha
(e.g.,
\pi_{a}\propto n_{a}
or uniform), trading efficiency for parity. When
A
is partially observed, we replace hard group membership by soft posteriors
\widehat{p}(A=a\mid X)
and compute
soft-Mondrian
thresholds
(
Vovk 2012
)
.

3.2
Counterfactual coverage regularization

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an SCM for
(A,X,Y)
with exogenous
U
, and consider
do(A\!\leftarrow\!a^{\prime})
. The
counterfactual coverage disparity
is

\Delta_{\mathrm{CF}}\;\triangleq\;\max_{a\neq a^{\prime}}\Big|\Pr\{Y\in\mathcal{C}(X)\mid A=a\}-\Pr\{Y\in\mathcal{C}(X^{\mathrm{cf}}(a^{\prime}))\mid A=a\}\Big|,

where
X^{\mathrm{cf}}(a^{\prime})
denotes the counterfactual covariate under
do(A=a^{\prime})
.
We approximate
X^{\mathrm{cf}}(a^{\prime})
by neutralizing unfair paths via path-specific effects (PSEs)
(
Pearl 2001
;
Avin et al. 2005
;
Chiappa 2019
)
. Let
\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{CF}}(\bm{q})
be an empirical estimate given thresholds
\bm{q}=(q_{a})_{a}
. Apply a smooth regularization:

\widehat{q}_{a}^{(\lambda)}\;=\;\widehat{q}_{a}\Big(1+\lambda\,\partial\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{CF}}/\partial q_{a}\Big),\qquad\lambda\geq 0.

4
Theory

Assume conditional exchangeability within groups for calibration data, and covariate shift between calibration and test with
Q_{Y\mid X}=P_{\mathrm{cal},Y\mid X}
. Let
n_{a}
be the number of calibration points with
A=a
.

4.1
Group-wise coverage under bounded shift

Define
B_{a}
by
\mathbb{E}_{P_{\mathrm{cal}}}[w_{a}(X)^{2}\mid A=a]\leq 1+B_{a}
.

Theorem 1

(Group coverage with importance-weighted quantiles)
.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
. With probability at least
1-\delta
over
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}
, the C3F sets satisfy for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
,

\Pr_{(X,Y)\sim Q}\!\big\{Y\in\mathcal{C}(X)\mid A=a\big\}\ \geq\ 1-\alpha_{a}\ -\ \sqrt{\frac{(1+B_{a})}{2n_{a}}\,\log\!\frac{2|\mathcal{A}|}{\delta}}\ .

Proof.

Write
F_{a}^{Q}(q)=\Pr_{Q}(\eta\leq q\mid A=a)
. Under covariate shift,

F_{a}^{Q}(q)=\mathbb{E}_{P_{\mathrm{cal}}}[w_{a}(X)\mathbf{1}\{\eta\leq q\}\mid A=a]/\mathbb{E}_{P_{\mathrm{cal}}}[w_{a}(X)\mid A=a]
.
The self-normalized importance-weighted empirical CDF

\widehat{F}_{a}(q)=\frac{\sum_{i:A_{i}=a}w_{a}(X_{i})\mathbf{1}\{\eta_{i}\leq q\}}{\sum_{i:A_{i}=a}w_{a}(X_{i})}

converges uniformly to
F_{a}^{Q}(q)
with deviation controlled by
\mathbb{E}[w_{a}^{2}\mid A=a]

(
Cortés et al. 2010
)
. A Hoeffding/DKW argument
(
Massart 1990
)
yields

\sup_{q}|\widehat{F}_{a}(q)-F_{a}^{Q}(q)|\leq\sqrt{\frac{(1+B_{a})}{2n_{a}}\log\frac{2}{\delta_{a}}}
with prob.
1-\delta_{a}
.
Monotonicity of the quantile map implies that the empirical
(1-\alpha_{a})
weighted quantile
\widehat{q}_{a}
delivers true CDF level at least
1-\alpha_{a}-\varepsilon_{a}
, with
\varepsilon_{a}
the RHS above. Union bound over
a
completes the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

When
w_{a}\equiv 1
(no shift),
B_{a}=0
and Theorem
1
reduces to DKW scaling
(
Massart 1990
)
. For known or accurately estimated likelihood ratios, the result aligns with weighted CP
(
Tibshirani et al. 2019
)
. For unknown shift, adaptive procedures offer alternatives
(
Gibbs and Candès 2021
;
Qiu et al. 2023
)
.

4.2
Equalized conditional coverage gap

Let
\alpha_{a}=\alpha\,\pi_{a}
with
\sum_{a}\pi_{a}=1
.

Corollary 2

(ECCG control)
.

With probability at least
1-\delta
,

\mathrm{ECCG}(\mathcal{C})\ \leq\ \max_{a,a^{\prime}}\Big|\alpha(\pi_{a}-\pi_{a^{\prime}})\Big|\ +\ \max_{a,a^{\prime}}\left\{\sqrt{\tfrac{(1+B_{a})}{2n_{a}}\log\!\tfrac{2|\mathcal{A}|}{\delta}}+\sqrt{\tfrac{(1+B_{a^{\prime}})}{2n_{a^{\prime}}}\log\!\tfrac{2|\mathcal{A}|}{\delta}}\right\}.

Proof.

Triangle inequality applied to group-wise deviations in Theorem
1
and the deterministic difference in targets
1-\alpha_{a}
vs.
1-\alpha_{a^{\prime}}
.
∎

4.3
Counterfactual disparity bound

Let
\eta(\cdot)
be
L_{\eta}
-Lipschitz in its threshold parameter, and suppose the path-specific effect (PSE) of
A
on
\eta
along unfair paths is bounded by
\kappa

(
Pearl 2001
;
Avin et al. 2005
)
.

Theorem 3

(First-order control of counterfactual coverage disparity)
.

For sufficiently small
\lambda\geq 0
, the regularized thresholds
\widehat{q}_{a}^{(\lambda)}
satisfy

\Delta_{\mathrm{CF}}\ \leq\ \kappa\,L_{\eta}\,\|\nabla_{\bm{q}}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{CF}}(\bm{q})\|_{1}\,\lambda\ +\ O(\lambda^{2}).

Proof.

Coverage under
do(A\!\leftarrow\!a^{\prime})
changes via (i) the distributional shift of nonconformity scores through unfair paths (first variation bounded by
\kappa
), and (ii) the threshold perturbation
\bm{q}\mapsto\bm{q}^{(\lambda)}
. A first-order expansion in
\lambda
yields the linear term
\langle\nabla_{\bm{q}}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{CF}},\,\bm{q}^{(\lambda)}-\bm{q}\rangle
and a remainder
O(\lambda^{2})
. Lipschitzness of the acceptance event in the threshold gives the factor
L_{\eta}
.
∎

6
Discussion and limitations

Demographics.
When
A
is missing, posterior estimates
\widehat{p}(A\mid X)
introduce calibration error that propagates into thresholds.

Causality.
Counterfactual regularization depends on the SCM; misspecification can bias
\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{CF}}
. Path-specific techniques can mitigate this by isolating unfair pathways
(
Chiappa 2019
)
.

Human factors.
Coverage parity may interact with downstream decisions; prediction-set design can influence equity
(
Cresswell et al. 2024
)
.

7
Conclusion

C3F offers a post-hoc, model-agnostic approach to group-conditional coverage parity under covariate shift. Theoretical bounds tie performance to the second moment of importance weights, while a counterfactual regularizer attenuates unfair path-specific influences. The method complements existing conformal techniques and provides a principled basis for auditing and adjustment.

Ethics statement

Applying subgroup-sensitive calibration requires careful governance of sensitive attributes, transparency in model updates, and routine auditing of distribution shift.

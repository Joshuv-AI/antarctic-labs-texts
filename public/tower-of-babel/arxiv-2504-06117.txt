Some analytical properties of multivariate fractal functions in Lebesgue spaces
Kiran Rani, Rattan Lal

Abstract
. In this article, we focus on the construction of multivariate fractal functions in Lebesgue spaces along with some properties of associated fractal operator. First, we give a detailed construction of the fractal functions belonging to Lebesgue spaces. Then, we give analytical properties of the defined fractal operator in Lebesgue spaces. We end this article by showing the existence of Schauder basis of the associated fractal functions for the space ℒ q ​ ( I n , μ p ) \displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{q}(I^{n},\mu_{p}) .

3.
Main Results

Here, we explore the Lebesgue spaces, with a focus on invariant measures that are related to Iterated Function Systems (IFS).

3.1.
Lebesgue spaces with respect to invariant measures

For simplification, let us denote
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{n}=I_{1}\times I_{2}\times\cdots\times I_{n}.

Let
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}=(p_{11\cdots 1},\ldots,p_{N_{1}N_{2}\cdots N_{n}})
be a vector of probabilities and
\displaystyle\mu_{p}
be the invariant measure associated with this probability vector generated by the Iterated Function System (IFS)
\displaystyle\{\mathcal{I}^{n};v_{k,j_{k}}:(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{N_{i}},k\in\Sigma_{n}\}
such that

\mu_{p}=\sum_{(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{N_{i}}}p_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}\mu_{p}\circ(v_{1,j_{1}}^{-1},\cdots,v_{n,j_{n}}^{-1}).

To clear the above notation, for
\displaystyle B\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{n}
,

\mu_{p}(B)=\sum_{(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{N_{i}}}p_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}\mu_{p}(\Theta_{j_{i}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}^{-1}(B)),

where
\displaystyle\Theta_{j_{i}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}^{-1}:\mathcal{I}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}^{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Theta_{j_{i}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})=(v_{1,j_{1}}(y_{1}),\cdots,v_{n,j_{n}}(y_{n}))

For
\displaystyle 1\leq q<\infty
, let
\displaystyle L^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
denote the space of all
\displaystyle q
-integrable functions on
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{n}
associated with
\displaystyle\mu_{p}
, where

\mathcal{L}^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})=\left\{f:\mathcal{I}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\int_{\mathcal{I}^{n}}\lvert f(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})\rvert^{q}d\mu_{p}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})<\infty\right\}.

The normed space
\displaystyle\left(L^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p}),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{q}\right)
is a complete normed by
\displaystyle\lVert\cdot\rVert_{q}
, where

\lVert f\rVert_{q}=\left(\int_{\mathcal{I}^{n}}\lvert f(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})\rvert^{q}d\mu_{p}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})\right)^{1/q}.

In relation to the measure
\displaystyle\mu_{p}
, for
\displaystyle q=\infty
, we establish the essential sup norm
\displaystyle\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
such that
\displaystyle\lVert g\rVert_{\infty}=ess\sup\lvert g\rvert
and in context to this, the space
\displaystyle L^{\infty}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})=\{g:\mathcal{I}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:~\lVert g\rVert_{\infty}<\infty\}
becomes complete.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\displaystyle\alpha\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})\text{ and }q_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}\in L^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
for all
\displaystyle(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{N_{i}}
, then the associated FIF
\displaystyle h^{*}\in L^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
, provided
\displaystyle\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}<1
.

Proof.

Consider a subset of
\displaystyle L^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
namely

\displaystyle L^{q}_{0}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})=\left\{g\in L^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p}):~g(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})\forall(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\partial\mathcal{I}^{n}\right\}
. One can easily prove that
\displaystyle L^{q}_{0}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
is a closed subset of the given space
\displaystyle L^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
. We know that a closed subspace of a complete normed space is complete. Hence
\displaystyle L^{q}_{0}
is a complete subspace with respect to the induced metric by
\displaystyle\lVert\cdot\rVert_{q}
. Define a Read-Bajraktarevi
\displaystyle\acute{c}
(RB) operator
\displaystyle T:L^{q}_{0}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})\rightarrow L^{q}_{0}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
such that

\begin{split}(Tg)(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})=&\alpha(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})(g(v_{1,j_{1}}^{-1}(y_{1}),\cdots,v_{n,j_{n}}^{-1}(y_{n})\\
&-q_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}(v_{1,j_{1}}^{-1}(y_{1}),\cdots,v_{n,j_{n}}^{-1}(y_{n}))),\end{split}

(3)

for
\displaystyle(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}I_{k,j_{k}}
and
\displaystyle(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{N_{i}},k\in\Sigma_{n}.

By the assumptions on
\displaystyle q_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}
and
\displaystyle v_{k,j_{k}}
, we can easily see that
\displaystyle T
is well-defined. Now consider functions
\displaystyle f,g\in L^{q}_{0}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
, then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lVert Tf-Tg\rVert_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{I}^{n}}\lvert(Tf)(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})-(Tg)(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})\rvert^{q}d\mu_{p}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{N_{i}}}\int_{\prod_{k=1}^{n}I_{k,j_{k}}}\lvert\alpha(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})f(v_{1,j_{1}}^{-1}(y_{1}),\cdots,v_{n,j_{n}}^{-1}(y_{n}))-

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\alpha(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})g(v_{1,j_{1}}^{-1}(y_{1}),\cdots,v_{n,jn}^{-1}(y_{n}))\rvert^{q}d\mu_{p}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{N_{i}}}\int_{\prod_{k=1}^{n}I_{k,j_{k}}}\lvert\alpha(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})(f-g)(v_{1,j_{1}}^{-1}(y_{1}),\cdots,v_{n,j_{n}}^{-1}(y_{n}))\rvert^{q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\cdot d\mu_{p}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{N_{i}}}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}^{q}\int_{\prod_{k=1}^{n}I_{k,j_{k}}}\lvert(f-g)(v_{1,j_{1}}^{-1}(y_{1}),\cdots,v_{n,j_{n}}^{-1}(y_{n}))\rvert^{q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\cdot d\mu_{p}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{N_{i}}}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}^{q}\int_{\prod_{k=1}^{n}I_{k,j_{k}}}\lvert(f-g)(v_{1,j_{1}}^{-1}(y_{1}),\cdots,v_{n,j_{n}}^{-1}(y_{n}))\rvert^{q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\cdot d\left(\sum_{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})\in\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Sigma_{N_{k}}}p_{i_{1}\cdots i_{n}}\mu_{p}(v_{1,i_{1}}^{-1}(y_{1}),\cdots,v_{n,i_{n}}^{-1}(y_{n}))\right)

We address whether the corresponding fractal functions for the space
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
have a Schauder basis in the next theorem.

Theorem 3.5
.

In the space
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
,there exists a Schauder basis of the fractal functions corresponding to a given Schauder basis.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\displaystyle(h_{n})
is a given Schauder basis for the space
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p}).
With the help of a chosen
\displaystyle\alpha
that satisfies condition
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\infty}<(1+\|Id-L\|)^{-1}
and part
4
of Theorem
3.2
, it can be directly seen that
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha}
is a topological automorphism. If
\displaystyle g\in\mathcal{L}^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
then
\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha})^{-1}(g)\in\mathcal{L}^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
, with

(\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha})^{-1}(g)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\bigg((\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha})^{-1}(g)\bigg)h_{n}.

We already know that the fractal operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha}
is continuous. Therefore , we have

g=\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha})^{-1}(g)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\bigg((\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha})^{-1}(g)\bigg)h_{n}^{\alpha},

where
\displaystyle h_{n}^{\alpha}=\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha}(h_{n})
. Let
\displaystyle g
be represented as
\displaystyle g=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}h_{n}^{\alpha}.
Since
\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha})^{-1}
is also continuous, we have

(\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha})^{-1}(g)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}h_{n},

and it gives
\displaystyle b_{n}=a_{n}((\mathcal{F}_{\Delta,L}^{\alpha})^{-1}(g))
for every
\displaystyle n
. Thus, we have a Schauder basis of the fractal functions
\displaystyle(h_{n}^{\alpha})
for
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{q}(\mathcal{I}^{n},\mu_{p})
.
∎

Local asymptotic normality for discretely observed McKean-Vlasov diffusions
Akram Heidari, Mark Podolskij

Abstract
We study the local asymptotic normality (LAN) property for the likelihood function associated with discretely observed d d -dimensional McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equations over a fixed time interval. The model involves a joint parameter in both the drift and diffusion coefficients, introducing challenges due to its dependence on the process distribution. We derive a stochastic expansion of the log-likelihood ratio using Malliavin calculus techniques and establish the LAN property under appropriate conditions. The main technical challenge arises from the implicit nature of the transition densities, which we address through integration by parts and Gaussian-type bounds. This work extends existing LAN results for interacting particle systems to the mean-field regime, contributing to statistical inference in non-linear stochastic models. Keywords: LAN property, log-likelihood ratio, Malliavin calculus, McKean-Vlasov diffusions, parametric estimation. AMS subject classifications: 62F12, 62E20, 62M05, 60G07, 60H10.

\begin{cases}dX_{t}^{i,\theta}=b_{\theta_{1}}\big(X_{t}^{i,\theta},\mu_{t}^{\theta}\big)dt+a_{\theta_{2}}\big(X_{t}^{i,\theta}\big)dW_{t}^{i}\qquad i=1,...,N,\quad t\in[0,T]\\[6.45831pt]
\text{Law}\big(X_{0}^{1,{\theta}},...,X_{0}^{N,{\theta}}\big):=\mu_{0}\times...\times\mu_{0}\end{cases}

In this paper, we aim to establish the local asymptotic normality (LAN) property for the likelihood function associated with the discrete observations

\left(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\right)_{k=1,\dots,n}^{i=1,\ldots,N},

(1.2)

\log\frac{\mathbb{P}_{m,\theta+r_{m}^{-1}h}}{\mathbb{P}_{m,\theta}}=h^{\top}\mathcal{N}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\top}\Sigma_{\theta}h+o_{\mathbb{P}_{m,\theta}}(1),\qquad\text{as }m\to\infty,

where

The LAN property is a crucial tool in asymptotic statistical inference, originally introduced by Le Cam. When the LAN property holds and the covariance matrix is invertible, minimax theorems can be applied to derive lower bounds on the asymptotic variance of estimators (see e.g.

Our work contributes to the growing field of statistical inference for McKean-Vlasov processes, providing new insights into the asymptotic properties of parameter estimators in a mean-field setting. Numerous parametric estimation methods for

A major challenge in establishing the local asymptotic normality property for McKean–Vlasov SDEs lies in the intractability of their transition densities, which complicates the analysis of the likelihood function’s asymptotic behavior. To address this, we employ tools from Malliavin calculus—specifically, the integration by parts formula—to derive an explicit representation of the logarithmic derivative of the transition density. This methodology, originally introduced by Gobet in the context of classical diffusion models
[
19
,
20
]
, enables a stochastic expansion of the log-likelihood ratio and ultimately yields the desired LAN result. In contrast to Gobet’s framework
[
20
]
, which relies on the ergodicity of the underlying process, our approach does not require this assumption, as the asymptotic regime is instead driven by the growing number of particles.

We remark that the drift and diffusion coefficients are estimated at different asymptotic rates. Specifically, the drift parameter is estimated at rate
\sqrt{N}
, while the diffusion parameter is estimated at rate
\sqrt{N/\Delta_{n}}
. These findings are consistent with the recent results in
[
1
]
, which develop a contrast-based estimation method for discretely observed particle systems and establish consistency and asymptotic normality of the resulting estimators. A closely related contribution is
[
13
]
, which proves the LAN property for drift estimation in continuously observed

d
-dimensional McKean–Vlasov models. It is important to note, however, that in the latter setting the likelihood function admits a closed-form expression via the Girsanov theorem, which simplifies the analysis.

3

4

2.3
Basic elements of Malliavin calculus

In this section, we present the fundamental concepts of Malliavin calculus that will be used throughout the remainder of the text.

Let
\mathbb{H}:=L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
and consider a function
h\in\mathbb{H}
. We define the Itô integral of
h
with respect to a standard
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Brownian motion
B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
by

B(h):=\int_{0}^{T}h(t)^{\top}dB_{t}.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the class of
smooth random variables
, i.e., random variables of the form

F=f(B(h_{1}),\dots,B(h_{k})),

where
h_{i}\in\mathbb{H}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C_{p}^{\infty}
-function (that is, a function which is infinitely differentiable and all its derivatives grow at most polynomially).

The
Malliavin derivative
of
F\in\mathcal{S}
is defined as the
\mathbb{H}
-valued random variable

\mathcal{D}F=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\partial_{x_{j}}f(B(h_{1}),\dots,B(h_{k}))\cdot h_{j}.

The operator
\mathcal{D}
is closable from
L^{p}(\Omega)
to
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})
for any
p\geq 1
. Its closure has domain denoted by
\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
, which consists of all
F\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that
\mathcal{D}F\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})
, equipped with the norm

\|F\|_{1,p}:=\left(\mathbb{E}[|F|^{p}]+\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{D}F\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{p}]\right)^{1/p}.

We now introduce the
Skorohod integral

\delta
, which is defined as the adjoint of the Malliavin derivative. More precisely,
\delta
is a linear operator from a subset of
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{H})
to
L^{2}(\Omega)
, characterized as follows:

(i)

The domain of
\delta
, denoted
\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
, consists of all processes
u\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{H})
such that

\forall F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2},\quad\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathcal{D}F,u\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\right]\right|\leq c(u)\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(ii)

For
u\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
, the Skorohod integral
\delta(u)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is defined by the
integration by parts formula

\forall F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2},\quad\mathbb{E}[F\,\delta(u)]=\mathbb{E}[\langle\mathcal{D}F,u\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}].

(2.5)

It is well known that the Skorohod integral and the Itô integral coincide when both integrals exist.
The following proposition summarizes key properties of the Skorohod integral.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p>1
. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If
u\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{H})
, then
u\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
and

\|\delta(u)\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})}+\|\mathcal{D}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{H})}\right).

(2.6)

(ii)

Let
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
u\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[F^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{t}\|^{2}dt\right]<\infty
. Then, whenever the right-hand side is square-integrable, we have the product formula

\delta(Fu)=F\delta(u)-\langle\mathcal{D}F,u\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}.

(2.7)

(Y_{t}^{i,\theta})_{t\in[0,\Delta_{n}]}
, defined by

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{i,\theta}:=\mathbb{I}_{d}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}b_{\theta_{1}}\big(X_{t_{k}+s}^{i,\theta},\mu_{t_{k}+s}^{\theta}\big)Y_{s}^{i,\theta}\,ds+\sum_{r=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}a_{\theta_{2}}^{r}\big(X_{t_{k}+s}^{i,\theta}\big)Y_{s}^{i,\theta}\,dB_{s}^{r,i},

(3.14)

where
a_{\theta_{2}}^{r}
denotes the
r
th column of the matrix-valued function
a_{\theta_{2}}
. It can be shown that
Y_{t}^{i,\theta}
is invertible for all
t\geq 0
, and the Malliavin derivative satisfies the following identity (see
[
30
, Eq. (2.59)]
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{s}X_{t_{k}+t}^{i,\theta}=Y_{t}^{i,\theta}(Y_{s}^{i,\theta})^{-1}a_{\theta_{2}}(X_{t_{k}+s}^{i,\theta})\,\,\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t]}(s).

(3.15)

Our first result provides an identity for the terms
\zeta_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}
and
\zeta_{k}^{i,\theta_{2}}
introduced in (
3.11
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that Assumptions
A1
–
A5
hold. Then, for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\theta\in\Theta
, the transition density
p^{\theta}(t_{k},t_{k+1},x,y)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\theta
, and we have the representation

\begin{split}\frac{\partial_{\theta_{1}}p^{\theta}}{p^{\theta}}(t_{k},t_{k+1},x,y)&=\frac{1}{\Delta_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},x}^{\theta}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{d}\delta\left(\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\,U_{r}^{i}\right)\bigg|X_{t_{k+1}}^{i,\theta}=y\right],\\
\frac{\partial_{\theta_{2}}p^{\theta}}{p^{\theta}}(t_{k},t_{k+1},x,y)&=\frac{1}{\Delta_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},x}^{\theta}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{d}\delta\left(\partial_{\theta_{2}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\,U_{r}^{i}\right)\bigg|X_{t_{k+1}}^{i,\theta}=y\right],\end{split}

(3.16)

where
U_{r}^{i}
denotes the
r
-th column of the
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
-valued process

U_{s}^{i}:=\left(\mathcal{D}_{s}X_{t_{k+1}}^{i,\theta}\right)^{-1}=a_{\theta_{2}}^{-1}(X_{t_{k}+s}^{i,\theta})\,Y_{s}^{i,\theta}\,\left(Y_{\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\right)^{-1},\qquad s\in[0,\Delta_{n}].

The application of Malliavin calculus to derive the transformation in (
3.16
) is essential for establishing the LAN property of the statistical model. This formula was originally shown by Gobet (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Proposition 4.1]
) in the setting of classical stochastic differential equations, and the same reasoning applies in our framework, so we omit the proof. However, in contrast to classical SDEs, the derivative process

\partial_{\theta_{1}}X^{i,\theta}
includes an additional functional derivative term
\partial_{\mu}b_{\theta_{1}}
, which captures the dependence of the laws
\mu_{t}^{\theta}
on the parameter
\theta
.

The next result applies Proposition
3.1
to derive an asymptotic expansion of the log-likelihood function
z(\theta^{0},\theta^{+})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that Assumptions
A1
–
A5
hold. Define the quantities

\displaystyle z^{\theta}_{t}(x):=\partial_{\theta_{1}}b_{\theta_{1}}\big(x,\mu_{t}^{\theta}\big)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{\mu}b_{\theta_{1}}\big(x,y,\mu_{t}^{\theta}\big)\,\partial_{\theta_{1}}\mu_{t}^{\theta}(dy)

(3.17)

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{u}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{1}z^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}_{t_{k}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})^{\top}\left[a_{\theta_{2}^{+}}^{-2}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\left(X_{t_{k+1}}^{i}-m_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\right)\right]dl,

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{2}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{v}{\sqrt{N\Delta_{n}}}\int_{0}^{1}\text{\rm tr}\left[\partial_{\theta_{2}}a_{\theta_{2}(l)}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\,a_{\theta_{2}(l)}^{-1}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.\left(\left(X_{t_{k+1}}^{i}-m_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}(l)}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\right)^{\otimes 2}-V_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}(l)}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\right)a_{\theta_{2}(l)}^{-2}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\right]dl.

Then, we have for
j=1,2
:

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\zeta_{k}^{i,\theta_{j}}-\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{j}}\right)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}0.

Remark 3.3

(Interacting particle systems)
.

A model closely related to (
1.1
) is the
interacting particle system
described by

\begin{cases}dX_{t}^{i,N,\theta}=b_{\theta_{1}}\big(X_{t}^{i,N,\theta},\mu_{t}^{N}\big)dt+a_{\theta_{2}}\big(X_{t}^{i,N,\theta}\big)dW_{t}^{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,N,\quad t\in[0,T],\\[6.45831pt]
\text{Law}\big(X_{0}^{1,N,\theta},\ldots,X_{0}^{N,N,\theta}\big):=\mu_{0}^{\otimes N},\end{cases}

(3.18)

where
\mu_{t}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i,N,\theta}}
denotes the
empirical measure
of the particle system
(X_{t}^{1,N,\theta},\ldots,X_{t}^{N,N,\theta})
. Parametric estimation techniques for such interacting particle systems have been studied, for instance, in
[
1
,
13
,
31
]
. In many cases, statistical inference methods developed for the particle system also extend to its mean-field limit (
1.1
).

However, establishing the LAN property for the interacting particle system under
discrete-time observations
proves to be significantly more challenging, and no general methods are currently available in the literature. A central difficulty lies in the derivation of lower and upper bounds for the transition densities, which are instrumental in proving Proposition
3.2
—a key step in the LAN analysis.

In the case of model (
1.1
), where the particles are i.i.d., it suffices to obtain bounds for the
d
-dimensional transition density
p^{\theta}(t,s,x,y)
(cf. Proposition
4.2
). In contrast, for interacting particle systems, one cannot reduce the analysis to marginal densities, and must instead handle the full
dN
-dimensional joint transition density
\mathbf{p}^{\theta}(t,s,x,y)
. Unfortunately, such bounds in high dimensions are not yet available in the literature, posing a major obstacle in the LAN analysis for the particle system.

We finally note that these mathematical challenges are absent in the setting of
continuous observations

(X_{t}^{i,N,\theta})_{t\in[0,T]}
, where the LAN property for the drift component has been successfully established in
[
13
]
.

Using the expansion in Proposition
3.2
, we now derive the LAN property for the statistical model (
1.1
).

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that Assumptions
A1
–
A5
hold. Then,

z(\theta^{0},\theta^{+})\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}-\text{\rm law}}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\mathcal{N}^{\theta^{0}}-\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\Sigma^{\theta^{0}}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix},

(3.19)

where the matrix
\Sigma^{\theta^{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is given by

\Sigma^{\theta^{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{b}^{\theta^{0}}&0\\
0&\Sigma_{a}^{\theta^{0}}\end{pmatrix},

with

\displaystyle\Sigma_{b}^{\theta^{0}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}z_{s}^{\theta^{0}}(x)^{\top}a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}^{-2}(x)z_{s}^{\theta^{0}}(x)\,\mu_{s}^{\theta^{0}}(dx)\,ds,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{a}^{\theta^{0}}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{\rm tr}\left(\partial_{\theta_{2}}a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}(x)a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}^{-1}(x)\partial_{\theta_{2}}a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}(x)a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}^{-1}(x)\right)\,\mu_{s}^{\theta^{0}}(dx)\,ds.

Let us now comment on the implications of Theorem
3.4
. The article
[
1
]
investigates parametric estimation of the drift and diffusion coefficients in one-dimensional particle systems of the form (
3.18
), observed at discrete time points. The proposed estimator, based on minimising a contrast function, is shown to be asymptotically normal. Under the additional condition
N\Delta_{n}\to 0
, the asymptotic covariance matrix takes the same form as
\Sigma^{\theta^{0}}
.

However, a key distinction arises when comparing the particle system model (
3.18
) to the i.i.d. observation scheme of the McKean–Vlasov system (
1.1
). In the particle system setting, the linear functional derivative
\partial_{\mu}b_{\theta_{1}}
does not appear in the asymptotic covariance. Consequently, the quantity
z_{t}^{\theta}(x)
is replaced by the simpler expression
\partial_{\theta_{1}}b_{\theta_{1}}(x,\mu_{t}^{\theta})
. This structural difference has already been highlighted in
[
13
]
in the context of continuously observed particle systems.

For ease of exposition, we have considered the case of univariate parameters. Nevertheless, as shown in
[
20
]
for ergodic SDEs, the LAN property extends naturally to the multi-parameter setting. While the notation becomes much more cumbersome, the mathematical complexity does not increase significantly.
Assume, for instance, that
\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
. Then the LAN property holds with asymptotic covariance matrix

\Sigma^{\theta^{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{b}^{\theta^{0}}&0\\
0&\Sigma_{a}^{\theta^{0}}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+q)\times(p+q)},

where
\Sigma_{b}^{\theta^{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
and
\Sigma_{a}^{\theta^{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
are defined by

\displaystyle\big(\Sigma_{b}^{\theta^{0}}\big)_{kl}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}z_{k,s}^{\theta^{0}}(x)^{\top}a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}^{-2}(x)z_{l,s}^{\theta^{0}}(x)\,\mu_{s}^{\theta^{0}}(dx)\,ds,

\displaystyle\big(\Sigma_{a}^{\theta^{0}}\big)_{kl}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\partial_{\theta_{k,2}}a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}(x)a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}^{-1}(x)\partial_{\theta_{l,2}}a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}(x)a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}^{-1}(x)\right)\,\mu_{s}^{\theta^{0}}(dx)\,ds,

and

z^{\theta}_{k,t}(x):=\partial_{\theta_{k,1}}b_{\theta_{1}}\big(x,\mu_{t}^{\theta}\big)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{\mu}b_{\theta_{1}}\big(x,y,\mu_{t}^{\theta}\big)\,\partial_{\theta_{k,1}}\mu_{t}^{\theta}(dy).

We omit the full details of the multi-parameter extension, referring the interested reader to the cited literature for exposition of technicalities.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that Assumptions
A1
–
A2
hold. Then, for all
p\geq 1
,
0\leq s<t\leq T
with
t-s\leq 1
,

Assume that Assumptions
A2

\displaystyle p^{\theta}(t_{k},t_{k+1},x,y)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},x}^{\bar{\theta}}\left[\left|\frac{\partial_{\theta_{1}}p^{\theta}}{p^{\theta}}(t_{k},t_{k+1},x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i})\right|^{m}\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\theta}\left[\exp(r_{0}\|X_{t}^{\theta,i}\|^{2})\right]\leq C\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,T],

The following lemma will also be used throughout this section. Its proof is a direct consequence of the weak law of large numbers and integrability properties, and is therefore omitted.

Lemma 4.3

Assume that Assumptions

–

\frac{\Delta_{n}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta},\mu_{t_{k}}^{\theta})\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x,\mu_{t}^{\theta})\,\mu_{t}^{\theta}(dx)\,dt.

The following proposition will be frequently used to establish the negligibility of certain stochastic terms in various settings.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that Assumptions
A1
–
A5
hold. Let
H_{t_{k+1}}^{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
, be a sequence of independent
\mathcal{F}_{t_{k+1}}
-measurable random variables. Recall the definition
(
2.4
).

(i)

Assume that for any
\tau>1

\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},x}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}[H_{t_{k+1}}^{i}]=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left(\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},x}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}|H_{t_{k+1}}^{i}|^{\tau}\right)^{1/\tau}=R_{t_{k}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{2}).

Then we have

\frac{1}{\Delta_{n}\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},X_{t_{k}}}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}\left[H^{i}|X_{t_{k+1}}^{i,\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}=X_{t_{k+1}}^{i}\right]dl\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}0.

(ii)

Assume that for any
\tau>1

\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},x}^{\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}(l)}[H_{t_{k+1}}^{i}]=0\qquad and\qquad\left(\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},x}^{\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}(l)}|H_{t_{k+1}}^{i}|^{\tau}\right)^{1/\tau}=R_{t_{k}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{3/2}).

Then we have

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\Delta_{n}}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},X_{t_{k}}}^{\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}(l)}\left[H^{i}_{t_{k+1}}|X_{t_{k+1}}^{i,\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}(l)}=X_{t_{k+1}}^{i}\right]dl\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}0.

We note that the main challenge in Proposition
4.4
arises from the need to establish convergence under the original measure
\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}
, which differs from the perturbed measures
\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}(l)}
. The proof presented below closely follows the methodology developed in
[

Proof.

We only show part (i) of Proposition
4.4
as the second part is shown similarly (cf.
[
20
]
). We define the random variable

\xi_{k}^{N}:=\frac{1}{\Delta_{n}\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},X_{t_{k}}}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}\left[H^{i}_{t_{k+1}}|X_{t_{k+1}}^{i,\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}=X_{t_{k+1}}^{i}\right]dl.

By martingale methods it suffices to prove the following convergence results:

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}[\xi_{k}^{N}]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}[(\xi_{k}^{N})^{2}]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}0.

We start by handling the conditional expectation. We first observe the identity

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}[\xi_{k}^{N}]=\frac{1}{\Delta_{n}\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},X_{t_{k}}^{i}}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}\left[H^{i}_{t_{k+1}}\frac{p^{\theta^{0}}}{p^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta^{+}_{2}}}(t_{k},t_{k+1},X_{t_{k}}^{i},X_{t_{k+1}}^{i})\right]dl.

Consequently, we deduce the decomposition

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}[\xi_{k}^{N}]=:V_{n,N}^{1}+V_{n,N}^{2},

with

\displaystyle V_{n,N}^{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\Delta_{n}\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},X_{t_{k}}^{i}}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}\left[H^{i}_{t_{k+1}}\frac{p^{\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}^{+}}-p^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}}{p^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta^{+}_{2}}}(t_{k},t_{k+1},X_{t_{k}}^{i},X_{t_{k+1}}^{i})\right]dl,

\displaystyle V_{n,N}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\Delta_{n}\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},X_{t_{k}}^{i}}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}\left[H^{i}_{t_{k+1}}\frac{p^{\theta^{0}}-p^{\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}^{+}}}{p^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta^{+}_{2}}}(t_{k},t_{k+1},X_{t_{k}}^{i},X_{t_{k+1}}^{i})\right]dl.

For the first term
V_{n,N}^{1}
we obtain the identity

V_{n,N}^{1}=-\frac{u}{\Delta_{n}N}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},X_{t_{k}}^{i}}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}\left[H^{i}_{t_{k+1}}\frac{\partial_{\theta_{1}}p^{\theta_{1}(r),\theta_{2}^{+}}}{p^{\theta_{1}(r),\theta_{2}^{+}}}\frac{p^{\theta_{1}(r),\theta_{2}^{+}}}{p^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta^{+}_{2}}}(t_{k},t_{k+1},X_{t_{k}}^{i},X_{t_{k+1}}^{i})\right]drdl.

Now, we apply Hölder inequality with conjugates
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}>1
, conditions of Proposition
4.4
(i)
and the inequalities of Proposition
4.2
, which imply that

\displaystyle|V_{n,N}^{1}|\leq\frac{C}{N\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}R_{t_{k}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{2})\exp(c\Delta_{n}\|X_{t_{k}}^{i}\|^{2}/q_{2})

\displaystyle\times\left(\Delta_{n}^{-d/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(-\frac{q_{3}\|X_{t_{k}}-y\|^{2}}{c\Delta_{n}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{c(q_{3}-1)\|X_{t_{k}}-y\|^{2}}{\Delta_{n}}\right)\exp(c(2q_{3}-1)\Delta_{n}\|X_{t_{k}}^{i}\|^{2})dy\right)^{1/q_{3}}.

We note that the above integral is finite when
(q_{3}-1)c-q_{3}/c<0
, which can be achieved by choosing
q_{3}
close enough to
1
. We also note
that, for any given constant
C>0
,
\mathbb{E}[\exp(C\Delta_{n}\|X_{t_{k}}^{i}\|^{2})]
is uniformly bounded due to (
4.25
) if
\Delta_{n}
is small enough.
Hence, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|V_{n,N}^{1}|]\leq C\Delta_{n}^{1/2}.

The term
V_{n,N}^{2}
is handled in exactly the same fashion and we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|V_{n,N}^{2}|]\leq C\Delta_{n}^{1/2}.

Consequently, we have proved that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}[\xi_{k}^{N}]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}0.

Concerning the conditional second moment we readily obtain the formula

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}[(\xi_{k}^{N})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{n}^{2}N}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},X_{t_{k}}}^{\theta_{1}(l_{1}),\theta_{2}^{+}}\left[H^{i_{1}}_{t_{k+1}}|X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},\theta_{1}(l_{1}),\theta_{2}^{+}}=X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},X_{t_{k}}}^{\theta_{1}(l_{2}),\theta_{2}^{+}}\left[H^{i_{2}}_{t_{k+1}}|X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{2},\theta_{1}(l_{2}),\theta_{2}^{+}}=X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{2}}\right]dl_{1}dl_{2}

Applying Jensen’s inequality and proceeding exactly as above, we conclude that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}[(\xi_{k}^{N})^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}]=O_{\mathbb{P}}(\Delta_{n}^{2}).

This completes the proof of Proposition
4.4
.

4.2.1
The drift term

We start by considering the difference
\zeta_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}-\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}
. Throughout this subsection
we set for simplicity

\theta=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}):=(\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}).

According to Proposition
3.1
we have the identity

\displaystyle\zeta_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}=\frac{u}{\Delta_{n}\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},X_{t_{k}}}^{\theta}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{d}\delta\left(\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\,U_{r}^{i}\right)\bigg|X_{t_{k+1}}^{i,\theta}=X_{t_{k+1}}^{i}\right]dl.

In the following discussion we will study the decomposition

\displaystyle\delta\left(\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\,U_{r}^{i}\right)=\Delta_{n}z_{r,\theta_{1}}^{\theta}\left(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\right)

(4.27)

\displaystyle\times\left[a^{-2}_{\theta_{2}}\left(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\right)\left(X_{t_{k+1}}^{i,\theta}-m^{\theta}_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}\left(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\right)\right)\right]_{r}+H_{t_{k+1}}^{i}.

Here the random variable
H_{t_{k+1}}^{i}
is a reminder term, which necessarily satisfies
\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},x}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}[H_{t_{k+1}}^{i}]=0
since the other two terms in (
4.27
) obviously satisfy this identity. Hence, if we prove that

\left(\mathbb{E}_{t_{k},x}^{\theta}|H_{t_{k+1}}^{i}|^{\tau}\right)^{1/\tau}=R_{t_{k}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{2})\qquad\text{for any }\tau>1,

the proof of Proposition
3.2
is completed for
\zeta_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}
due to Proposition
4.4
(i).

We apply the product formula (
2.7
) and conclude the identity

\displaystyle\delta\left(\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\,U_{r}^{i}\right)=\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\delta\left(U_{r}^{i}\right)-\int_{0}^{\Delta_{n}}\big(\mathcal{D}_{t}\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\big)^{\top}U_{r,t}^{i}dt.

In the next step, we recall the definition
U_{s}^{i}=a_{\theta_{2}}^{-1}(X_{t_{k}+s}^{i,\theta})\,Y_{s}^{i,\theta}\,(Y_{\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta})^{-1}
,

s\in[0,\Delta_{n}]
, and define its approximation via

\displaystyle\widehat{U}_{s}^{i}:=a_{\theta_{2}}^{-1}(X_{t_{k}+s}^{i,\theta}).

With this notation at hand, we finally obtain the decomposition

\displaystyle\delta\left(\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\,U_{r}^{i}\right)=M^{i}_{n}+H_{n}^{i,1}+H_{n}^{i,2}+H_{n}^{i,3}

with

\displaystyle M^{i}_{n}

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{n}z_{r,\theta_{1}}^{\theta}\left(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\right)\delta\left(\widehat{U}_{r}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle H_{n}^{i,1}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{0}^{\Delta_{n}}\big(\mathcal{D}_{t}\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\big)^{\top}U_{r,t}^{i}dt

\displaystyle H_{n}^{i,2}

\displaystyle:=\left(\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}-\Delta_{n}z_{r,\theta_{1}}^{\theta}\left(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\right)\right)\delta\left(\widehat{U}_{r}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle H_{n}^{i,3}

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\delta\left(U_{r}^{i}-\widehat{U}_{r}^{i}\right)

The term
M^{i}_{n}
represents the main contribution while
H_{n}^{i,j}
,
j=1,2,3
, turn out to be negligible.

We start with the term
H_{n}^{i,1}
. Due to the formula (
3.13
), we readily obtain the statement

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\Delta_{n}]}\left|\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,t}^{i,\theta}\right|=R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}).

(4.28)

This implies the same statement for the Malliavin derivative:

\left\|\mathcal{D}_{t}\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}\right\|=R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}).

As a consequence, we deduce that

H_{n}^{i,1}=R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{2}).

Now, we handle the term
H_{n}^{i,2}
. We note that

\displaystyle\delta\left(\widehat{U}_{r}^{i}\right)=R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{1/2}).

On the other hand, we deduce that

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta_{1}}X_{r,\Delta_{n}}^{i,\theta}-\Delta_{n}z_{r,\theta_{1}}^{\theta}\left(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\right)=R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{3/2}),

which readily implies that
H_{n}^{i,2}=R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{2})
.

Next, we treat the term
H_{n}^{i,3}
.
Due to (
2.6
), we conclude that

\delta\left(U_{r}^{i}-\widehat{U}_{r}^{i}\right)=R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}).

Hence, the application of (
4.28
), implies that
H_{n}^{i,3}=R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{2})
.

Finally, we treat the main term
M^{i}_{n}
. First note that
\delta(\widehat{U}_{r}^{i})
is a regular Itô integral with respect to
B^{i}
.
For the Brownian motion
B^{i}
we obtain the following representation:

\displaystyle dB_{t}^{i}

\displaystyle=a_{\theta_{2}}^{-1}\big(X_{t_{k}+t}^{i,\theta}\big)dX_{t_{k}+t}^{i,\theta}-a_{\theta_{2}}^{-1}\big(X_{t_{k}+t}^{i,\theta}\big)b_{\theta_{1}}\big(X_{t_{k}+t}^{i,\theta},\mu_{t_{k}+t}^{\theta}\big)dt

\displaystyle=a_{\theta_{2}}^{-1}\big(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\big)dX_{t_{k}+t}^{i,\theta}+\left(\mathbb{I}_{d}-a_{\theta_{2}}^{-1}\big(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\big)a_{\theta_{2}}\big(X_{t_{k}+t}^{i,\theta}\big)\right)dB^{i}_{t}

\displaystyle+a_{\theta_{2}}^{-1}\big(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\big)b_{\theta_{1}}\big(X_{t_{k}+t}^{i,\theta},\mu_{t_{k}+t}^{\theta}\big)dt.

Using the above representation we conclude that

\displaystyle\delta\left(\widehat{U}_{r}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{\Delta_{n}}\left(a^{-1}_{\theta_{2}}\right)_{rm}\left(X_{t_{k}+t}^{i,\theta}\right)dB_{t}^{m,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{\Delta_{n}}\left(a^{-1}_{\theta_{2}}\right)_{rm}\left(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\right)dB_{t}^{m,i}+R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{d}\left(a^{-2}_{\theta_{2}}\right)_{rm}\left(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta}\right)\int_{0}^{\Delta_{n}}dX_{t_{k}+t}^{m,i,\theta}+R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n})

\displaystyle=\left[a_{\theta_{2}}^{-2}(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta})\left(X_{t_{k+1}}^{i,\theta}-m_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta}(X_{t_{k}}^{i,\theta})\right)\right]_{r}+R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}).

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.2
for the drift component.

3.2
is completed for

4.4

Proof of (

We first observe that

\displaystyle m^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})-m^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})

\displaystyle=-\frac{lu\Delta_{n}}{\sqrt{N}}z^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})+R_{t_{k}}^{i}(\varepsilon_{n,N}\Delta_{n}/\sqrt{N})

with

\displaystyle\overline{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{u}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{1}z^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}_{t_{k}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})^{\top}\left[a_{\theta_{2}^{+}}^{-2}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\left(X_{t_{k+1}}^{i}-m_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta_{0}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\right)\right]dl,

which is a direct analogue of

with

replaced by

. By definition it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{1}z^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}_{t_{k}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})^{\top}

\displaystyle\times\left[a_{\theta_{2}^{+}}^{-2}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\left(m_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta^{0}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})-m_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta_{1}(l),\theta_{2}^{+}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\right)\right]dl\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\Sigma_{b}^{\theta^{0}},

which follows directly from (

Proof of (
4.31
):
We observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}\Big]=\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}-\overline{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}})\Big]=R_{t_{k}}^{i}(\Delta_{n})

due to (
4.37
). Hence, the second term in (
4.31

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}\Big)^{2}\Big]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\sum_{i_{1}\not=i_{2}}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i_{1},\theta_{1}}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i_{2},\theta_{1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}})^{2}\Big].

Again due to (
4.37
), we deduce the estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\sum_{i_{1}\not=i_{2}}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i_{1},\theta_{1}}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i_{2},\theta_{1}}\Big]=\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\sum_{i_{1}\not=i_{2}}(\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i_{1},\theta_{1}}-\overline{\zeta}_{k}^{i_{1},\theta_{1}})(\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i_{2},\theta_{1}}-\overline{\zeta}_{k}^{i_{2},\theta_{1}})\Big]=R_{t_{k}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{2}).

In other words,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\sum_{i_{1}\not=i_{2}}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i_{1},\theta_{1}}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i_{2},\theta_{1}}\Big]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}0.

Finally, we have that

\displaystyle(\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}})^{2}=\frac{u^{2}}{N}\left[z^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})^{\top}a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}^{-2}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\left(X_{t_{k+1}}^{i}-m_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta^{0}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})\right)\right]^{2}+R_{t_{k+1}}^{i}\left(\varepsilon_{n,N}\Delta_{n}/N\right)

4.32

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}\Big|^{4}\Big]

y_{k}^{i}:=\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}-\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}[\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{1}}]

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\left[\sum_{(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4})\in C_{N}}y_{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots y_{k}^{i_{4}}\right]=O_{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}(\Delta_{n}/N).

\displaystyle\left(V_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta}(x)\right)_{r_{1},r_{2}}=x_{r_{1}}x_{r_{2}}+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}^{\theta}_{t_{k},x}\left[a_{\theta_{2}}^{2}(X_{s}^{i,\theta})_{r_{1},r_{2}}+b_{r_{1},\theta_{1}}(X_{s}^{i,\theta},\mu_{s}^{\theta})X_{r_{2},s}^{i,\theta}+b_{r_{2},\theta_{1}}(X_{s}^{i,\theta},\mu_{s}^{\theta})X_{r_{1},s}^{i,\theta}\right]ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-m_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta}(x)_{r_{1}}m_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta}(x)_{r_{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}^{\theta}_{t_{k},x}\left[a_{\theta_{2}}^{2}(X_{s}^{i,\theta})_{r_{1},r_{2}}+b_{r_{1},\theta_{1}}(X_{s}^{i,\theta},\mu_{s}^{\theta})(X_{r_{2},s}^{i,\theta}-x_{r_{2}})+b_{r_{2},\theta_{1}}(X_{s}^{i,\theta},\mu_{s}^{\theta})(X_{r_{1},s}^{i,\theta}-x_{r_{1}})\right]ds

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}^{\theta}_{t_{k},x}\left[b_{r_{1},\theta_{1}}(X_{s}^{i,\theta},\mu_{s}^{\theta})\right]ds\right)\left(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}^{\theta}_{t_{k},x}\left[b_{r_{2},\theta_{1}}(X_{s}^{i,\theta},\mu_{s}^{\theta})\right]ds\right).

By mean value theorem we deduce

V_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta^{0}}(x)-V_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}(l)}(x)=(\theta_{2}^{0}-\theta_{2}(l))\partial_{\theta_{2}}V_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta_{1}^{0},\widetilde{\theta}_{2}}(x)

for some
\widetilde{\theta}_{2}
satisfying
|\theta_{2}^{0}-\widetilde{\theta}_{2}|\leq|\theta_{2}^{0}-\theta_{2}(l)|
.
Consequently, we conclude that

\displaystyle V_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta^{0}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})-V_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}^{\theta_{1}^{0},\theta_{2}(l)}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})=-\frac{2lv\Delta_{n}^{3/2}}{\sqrt{N}}\partial_{\theta_{2}}a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})a_{\theta_{2}^{0}}(X_{t_{k}}^{i})+R_{t_{k}}^{i}\left(\varepsilon_{n,N}\Delta_{n}^{3/2}/\sqrt{N}\right)

(4.38)

with
\varepsilon_{n,N}\to 0
. Hence, (
4.38
) implies the convergence in (
4.33
).

Proof of (
4.34
):
In the previous proof we have shown that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{2}}\Big]=R_{t_{k}}^{i}(\Delta_{n}/N).

Hence, we immediately conclude that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{2}}\Big]\right)^{2}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}0.

Furthermore, following the same arguments as in the proof of (
4.31
), we deduce the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big]=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[(\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{2}})^{2}\Big]+o_{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}(1).

Last but not least, a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\theta^{0}}_{t_{k}}\Big[(\widehat{\zeta}_{k}^{i,\theta_{2}})^{2}\Big]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{\theta^{0}}}v^{2}\Sigma_{a}^{\theta^{0}}.

This completes the proof of (
4.34
).

Proof of (
4.36
):
Applying the estimates from the proof of (
4.30

Finally, using the representations from Proposition

Atoms meet symbols
Leonardo F. Cavenaghi, Ludmil Katzarkov, Maxim Kontsevich

Abstract
. This paper introduces a novel framework for constructing invariants in G G -equivariant birational geometry by unifying two recent approaches: the theory of atoms recently developed by Katzarkov, Kontsevich, Pantev, and Yu, and the theory of modular symbols due to Kontsevich, Tschinkel, and Pestun. We initiate the theory of Chen-Ruan atoms. Assuming the blowup formula for the quantum Chen-Ruan cohomology, we outline how to extend the theory of atoms to global quotient orbifolds and present some striking applications. In addition, we develop a separate class of purely geometric invariants for ℤ / 2 \mathbb{Z}/2 - and ℤ / 3 \mathbb{Z}/3 -actions on surfaces and threefolds. We provide many examples of non- G G -linearizable G G -actions on projective varieties treated with these new techniques.

2.
The Theory of Atoms

The
theory of atoms
, recently introduced by Katzarkov, Kontsevich, Pantev, and Yu
[
19
]
, provides new birational invariants of smooth projective varieties. The theory emerges from a synthesis of classical Hodge theory with Gromov-Witten theory, utilizing the former to refine and decompose the latter. While its conceptual roots lie in insights from Homological Mirror Symmetry (HMS,
[
25
]
), it has been systematically developed without direct recourse to mirror symmetry methods. Instead, it leverages motivic structures inherent in both the algebraic and symplectic geometry of a single projective variety.

The result is a framework for constructing invariants, here termed the
atom formalism
5
5

5

It is important to stress that the “theory of atoms” refers to a framework for constructing invariants, rather than to a specific set of results or conjectures.
, which manifests in part as a
non-archimedean
\mathbbm{k}
-analytic
refinement of quantum cohomology. In classical settings, quantum products
[
21
]
are defined by formal power series or series valued in a Novikov field, giving rise to A-model Frobenius manifolds or variations of A-model noncommutative Hodge structures
[
20
,
38
]
. These structures may be formal or complex-analytic, depending on notoriously subtle convergence properties.

The theory of atoms circumvents these analytic challenges through a change of setting. Instead of working over
\mathbb{C}
, the theory operates in the category of non-archimedean
\mathbbm{k}
-analytic supermanifolds over the algebraically closed valued field

(14)

\mathbbm{k}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(\!(\mathbf{y}^{1/n})\!),

the field of Puiseux series in an auxiliary variable
\mathbf{y}
. This shift is not merely a technical convenience; it is the foundation that enables the very definition of atoms. The canonical spectral decomposition (Section
2.3
) at the heart of the theory is ill-defined over
\mathbb{C}
due to analytic obstructions such as the Stokes phenomenon
[
20
]
.

In what follows, we will recall the basic structure of Gromov-Witten theory for a smooth projective variety

and explore its interaction with the symmetries of Hodge theory. We begin by reviewing the construction of genus-zero Gromov-Witten invariants and their organization via the Gromov-Witten potential. We recall the construction of a non-archimedean analytic F-bundle—called the
maximal A-model F-bundle
—which encodes the deformation data of quantum cohomology. In Section
2.2
, we describe the action of the Mumford-Tate group on this F-bundle, leading to the definition of
Hodge atoms
in Section
2.3
. This is meant to be a short recap for the sake of self-containment from part of the material in
[
19
]
.

The remaining subsections present many new contributions: in Sections
2.5
-
2.6
one introduces
G
-equivariant Hodge atoms, in Section
2.7
we explain the connection between atom theory and categories, in Section
2.8
we present some applications computing explicit examples, and in Section
2.9
we merge atom theory with classical monodromy.

2.4.
The Global Set of Hodge Atoms

The behavior of quantum cohomology under birational transformations, such as blowups, motivates the definition of a global, birationally invariant set of atoms.

2.4.1.
Motivation from Birational Geometry

Recall that any birational map between two smooth projective varieties (in characteristic zero) can be decomposed as the composition of blowups at smooth centers and inverses to such blowups (see, e.g.,

Each

is a blowup or blowdown at a smooth center disjoint from

and

Let

2.7.
Why atoms? Hypothetical atomic semi-orthogonal decomposition

This section compares the ideal versus the ‘‘legal’’

Remark 2.10
.

There is a subtle point in the picture above that we do not fully understand, even regarding Fano varieties. A semi-orthogonal decomposition of
\mathrm{Perf}(X)

One can ask the question whether

Remark 2.11
.

The whole theory can be extended to a class of

The blow-up formula and projective bundle formula should hold in the more general non-compact setting, and given by certain universal formulas which are summations over trees, as it is the only general type of universal expressions for algebras over operads.

A potential application of the non-compact theory will be

3.
Atoms meet symbols: A new
\mathbb{Z}
-module

While the theory of
G
-equivariant atoms offers powerful birational invariants derived from the interplay of Gromov-Witten theory and Hodge structures, a natural question arises: is this global perspective sufficient to resolve finer questions in
G
-equivariant birational geometry? Complementing the “atomic” view is the theory of modular symbols, which precisely captures the local geometry of the group action through the characters of
G
-representations on normal bundles to the fixed locus
X^{G}
. The central philosophy throughout this section is that these two perspectives are not independent.

Since a fundamental operation like a
G
-equivariant blowup alters both the global atomic content and the local fixed-locus geometry, a complete invariant must track both. We therefore construct a unified algebraic object, a combinatorial
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathcal{B}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\mathrm{comb}}
, whose generators are formal unions of atoms and symbols and whose relations are defined by their transformations under blowups. This combined module is designed to produce invariants that are fundamentally stronger than those derived from either the atomic or symbolic theory in isolation. From this
\mathbb{Z}
-module, we derive two
\mathbb{Z}
-valued
\mathbb{Z}/p
-birational invariants (Theorems
3.6
,
3.7
), and provide applications (Examples
3.9
and
3.10
).

Unless stated otherwise, we focus on irreducible smooth complex projective varieties
X
of dimension
3
that possess
\mathbb{Z}/p
-generically free actions by regular automorphisms. The choice for
\mathbb{Z}/p
is merely for ease of notation and easier exposition. The entire story can be generalized to other finite groups, including nonabelian ones, once the necessary changes are made everywhere, for instance, following
[
40
,
31
,
27
]
.

4.
Atomic decomposition for cohomology of fixed loci, via Chen-Ruan theory

Here, we will present another potential extension of the atom theory, directly relating atoms to the cohomology of fixed loci. This approach is conditional on the not-yet-proven blow-up formula for orbifolds (smooth projective Deligne-Mumford stacks).

4.1.
Reminder on Chen-Ruan cohomology for global quotients

Let
G
be a finite group acting on a smooth projective variety
X
. The Chen-Ruan, or stringy cohomology of the global quotient stack
[X/G]
is defined as the cohomology of the
inertia stack

\mathcal{I}_{[X/G]}
, that is, the quotient of

\{(g,x)\in G\times X|\,gx=x\}

by the action of
G
:

h\cdot(g,x):=(hgh^{-1},hx).

Categorically, in de Rham realization, it is the periodic cyclic cohomology of the category of perfect complexes on
[X/G]
, or equivalently, the category of
G
-equivariant perfect complexes on
X
.

Explicitly, the Chen-Ruan cohomology is

(96)

\mathrm{H}^{\ast}_{CR}([X/G]):=\left(\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X^{g})\right)^{G}\simeq\bigoplus_{g_{i}}\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X^{g_{i}})^{C(g_{i})}

where
\{g_{i}\}\subset G
is a set of representatives for the conjugacy classes in
G
, and
C(g_{i})\subset G
is the centralizer of
g_{i}
. The summand corresponding to the identity element
g=1
is
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X)^{G}
, known as the
untwisted sector
. Gromov-Witten invariants in this context are constructed from moduli spaces of G-equivariant stable maps.

The domain of such a map is an
admissible G-cover

[
18
, Def 2.1]
, which is a finite morphism
\pi:E\to C
. Here,
C
is an
n
-pointed, genus-
\mathsf{g}
, semistable curve with marked points, and the cover must satisfy several conditions:

•

E
is a (potentially disconnected) nodal curve with a
G
-action, and its nodes map to the nodes of
C
.

•

The map
\pi
is a principal
G
-bundle over the smooth, unmarked part of
C
. Ramification is permitted over the marked points and nodes.

•

The action of a stabilizer subgroup
G_{q}\subseteq G
at a node
q
of
E
must not exchange two branches at
q
, and be
balanced
, meaning the eigenvalues of the group action on the two tangent branches at the node are multiplicative inverses of each other.

The map itself is a
G
-equivariant morphism
f:E\to X
. The final stability condition requires that the induced map on the quotient stacks,
\bar{f}:[E/G]\to[X/G]
, is an
orbifold stable map
.

The moduli space of such objects is denoted by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathsf{g},n}^{G}(X)
. For a given homology class
\beta\in\mathrm{H}_{2}(X/G,\mathbb{Z})
, the substack of maps whose image corresponds to
\beta
is denoted by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathsf{g},n}^{G}(X,\beta)
. This is a proper Deligne-Mumford stack, endowed with a natural virtual fundamental class.

Each marked point
p_{i},\,i=1,\dots,n
of
C
gives rise to a
canonical
point
(\tilde{p}_{i},g_{i})\in\mathcal{I}_{[E/G]}
of the inertia stack of
E
. Namely, pick a point
\tilde{p}_{i}
in the preimage of
p_{i}
. The stabilizer of
\tilde{p}_{i}
is a cyclic group with a
canonical
generator
g_{i}
, which acts on the tangent space
T_{\tilde{p}_{i}}E
by multiplication by
\exp(2\pi\mathsf{i}/n_{i})
where
n_{i}
is the order of the stabilizer. Therefore, the evaluation maps give a morphism

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathsf{g},n}^{G}(X,\beta)\to(\mathcal{I}_{[X/G]})^{n}\,.

Taking the evaluations of the virtual fundamental class of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathsf{g},n}^{G}(X,\beta)
at the marked points, we obtain equivariant Gromov-Witten invariants of
[X/G]
.

In order to define quantum product we use the non-degenerate pairing on
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}_{CR}([X/G])
coming from Poincaré duality on the disjoint union
X^{g}
of smooth projective varieties (possibly of different dimension), and pairing in (
96
) summands
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X^{g})
with
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X^{g^{-1}})
. In the definition of the Euler vector field one modifies the grading operator by taking into account so called
age

\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
-valued function on
\mathcal{I}_{[X/G]}
whose value at
(g,x)\in G\times X,\,gx=g
is defined as
\sum_{j}\theta_{j}/2\pi
where
0\leq\theta_{j}<2\pi
are the principal arguments of the eigenvalues of the action of
g
on
T_{x}X
, see e.g.
[
18
]
.

In this way, we obtain a version of the A-model maximal F-bundle for global quotient stacks
14
14

14

This theory generalizes to arbitrary smooth projective Deligne-Mumford stacks which are not necessarily global quotients.
.
Finally, we have a natural action of
\mathsf{MT}
preserving the whole structure. The reason is that all virtual fundamental classes of stable orbifold maps are algebraic, and hence Hodge.

4.3.

P_{\bm{\alpha},[g]}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[t,t^{-1}]
, here

is the conjugacy class of

.

We have the following isomorphism of
C(g)\times\mathsf{MT}
-modules:

\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X^{g})\simeq\oplus_{\bm{\alpha}}\mathsf{H}_{\bm{\alpha},g}\,

In the case
g=1
, we get the usual atoms and the original invariant
P_{\bm{\alpha}}
. Non-trivial conjugacy classes give new invariants of atoms, which we will explore in the next subsections.

Notice that it is quite possible that for two elements
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
generate the same cyclic subgroup, i.e.,
g_{2}=g_{1}^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
coprime to the order of

, the atomic decompositions of the same space
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X^{g_{1}})=\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X^{g_{2}})
are different, if one interprets it as the fixed locus of
g_{1}
or of
g_{2}
. The reason is that the moduli spaces of orbifold stable maps could be different for
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
.

4.5.
First applications

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
d\geq 1
, any conjugacy class
[g]
of
G
, and any atoms
\bm{\alpha}
coming from varieties of dimension
\leq d
, we have

\mathrm{Coeff}_{t^{d}}P_{\bm{\alpha},[g]}\leq\mathrm{Coeff}_{t^{d}}P_{\bm{\alpha}}.

Proof.

Let

be
d^{\prime}
-dimensional variety with
G
-action where

. If

then obviously for all atoms
\bm{\alpha}
of

the corresponding Hodge polynomials
P_{\bm{\alpha},[g]},P_{\bm{\alpha}}
do not contain the mononial
t^{d}
.
If
X
is
d
-dimensional and the action of
g^{\mathbb{Z}}
is non-trivial, then
the dimension of fixed locus

is strictly smaller than

, and hence
\mathrm{Coeff}_{t^{d}}P_{\bm{\alpha},[g]}=0
.
Finally, if
\dim X=d
and the action of

is trivial, then the atomic decomposition of
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X)
coincides with the atomic decomposition of
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X^{g})
, and for each atom we have an equality. ∎

In what follows, for any smooth projective variety

we denote by

its Hodge polynomial:

P_{X}:=\sum_{p,q\geq 0}h^{p,q}(X)t^{p-q}.

Corollary 4.7
.

For a
d
-dimensional variety

endowed with
G
-action, if

\mathrm{Coeff}_{t^{d-2}}P_{X^{g}}>\mathrm{Coeff}_{t^{d-2}}P_{X}

then

is not
G
-equivariantly birational to a projective space with a

-linearizable action.

Proof.

Lemma
4.6
implies that under the hypothesis, not all atoms of

can come from dimension
\leq d-2
.
∎

We get many possible examples, which we detail below. Interestingly enough,

the formerly presented examples in this paper can be fully captured by the presented technique.

Example 4.8
.

Very much in the nature of Example
3.10
,
for any

, let

d
-dimensional affine variety

x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}=P(x_{4},\dots,x_{d+1})

where

where

\mathbb{P}^{d-2}

acts by cyclic permutations of

The closure
\overline{X}
of the image of variety

given by equation

. It is easy to see that

\mathrm{Coeff}_{t^{d-2}}P_{\widetilde{X}^{g}}>0=\mathrm{Coeff}_{t^{d-2}}P_{\widetilde{X}}

where

is a generator.
Indeed, one can see that the transcendental part of cohomology of
\widetilde{X}
comes from the cohomology of the
(d-3)
-dimensional hypersurface given by

, and it can not contribute to

(d-2)
-dimensional, and given by the equation

Then the condition

guarantees that

{\mathrm{d}x_{2}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{d+1}\over x^{2}}

is a non-vanishing section of the canonical bundle of

, hence implying that
\mathrm{Coeff}_{t^{d-2}}P_{\widetilde{X}^{g}}>0
.

Example 4.9
.

Let
S_{1},\penalty\ S_{2}
be two rational surfaces with involutions such as that in Example
3.9
, and assume for simplicity that the fixed loci for each of the surfaces is just one curve

of a positive genus

. The Hodge polynomial of

.

Example 4.10
.

Let

be a three-dimensional smooth complete intersection of two quadrics in

as discussed in Example

for

On the one hand, as we have seen in Example

,

has three atoms which are automatically

which are zero, with multiplicity six, and (up to scale)

It is interesting to understand this example from the categorical point of viewpoint, see Section

4.6.
Generalization: diagram of manifolds with F-bundles

One can generalize two inclusions of maximal F-bundles used earlier in this section, which on the level of tangent spaces corresponds to inclusions

\mathrm{H}^{*}(X)^{G}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{*}(X),\quad\mathrm{H}^{*}(X)^{G}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{*}_{CR}(X)\,.

Namely, for any pair of subgroups
H_{1},H_{2}<G
such that
H_{1}
is a normal subgroup of
H_{2}
(one can write
H_{1}\triangleleft H_{2}<G
) we have a maximal
F
-bundle with the base

(B_{[X/H_{1}]})^{H_{2}}\subset B_{[X/H_{1}]}

which is the fixed locus of the natural
H_{2}
-action on
B_{[X/H_{1}]}
. Whenever

H_{1}<H_{1}^{\prime}<H_{2}^{\prime}<H_{2}<G,\quad H_{1}\triangleleft H_{2},H_{1}^{\prime}\triangleleft H_{2}^{\prime}

we have an
inclusion
of bases of maximal F-bundles

(B_{[X/H_{1}]})^{H_{2}}\hookrightarrow(B_{[X/H_{1}^{\prime}]})^{H_{2}^{\prime}}\,.

Therefore, we have a partial order set of pairs
(H_{1}\triangleleft H_{2})
as above realized by inclusions of bases of maximal F-bundles, the whole picture is equivariant with respect to
G\times\mathsf{MT}
. There is also the inclusion of even smaller F-bundle
(B_{X})^{G\times\mathsf{MT}}

in all these F-bundles.

We can formulate the following generalization of Conjectures
4.4
and
4.5
:

Conjecture 4.11
.

Inclusions
(B_{[X/H_{1}]})^{H_{2}}\hookrightarrow(B_{[X/H_{1}^{\prime}]})^{H_{2}^{\prime}}
commute with Iritani’s partial isomorphisms, and with action of
\mathsf{MT}
.

Assuming the above conjecture, we get for each
G
-atom
\bm{\alpha}
of
X
a
G
-equivariant diagram of germs of bases of maximal F-bundles labeled by the partial ordered set of pairs
(H_{1}\triangleleft H_{2})
as above.

This is a very fine invariant, from which one can extract easier-to-calculate invariants, as
G
-equivariant diagrams of
\mathsf{MT}
-modules. It looks plausible that these invariants will detect, e.g., non-
G
-linearizability of actions of a sufficiently large finite group
G\subset\mathrm{O}(6)
on the standard 4-dimensional quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
.

contributes
3-3\mathsf{g}-K_{S}\cdot\mathcal{N}
. Blowing up a point on

and replaces

. The change in

(116)

\displaystyle\Delta J

\displaystyle=\underbrace{1}_{\text{new point}}+\underbrace{(3-3\mathsf{g}-(K_{S}\cdot\mathcal{N}+1))}_{\text{new surface}}-\underbrace{(3-3\mathsf{g}-K_{S}\cdot\mathcal{N})}_{\text{old surface}}

(117)

\displaystyle=1-1=0.

Case 7: Blowup in a transverse curve to a fixed surface.

•

The fixed surface of type
+
(

(118)

\displaystyle\Delta(3-3\mathsf{g}-K_{S}\cdot\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=-(K_{\widetilde{S}}\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})-(-K_{S}\cdot\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=-K_{S}\cdot(\mathcal{N}-C)+K_{S}\cdot\mathcal{N}

Appendix A
G
-equivariant birational geometry versus birational geometry over non-algebraically closed fields

The geometry of finite group actions is closely related to non-algebraically closed fields of positive transcendence degrees. If a finite group
G
acts birationally and generically free on a variety
X/\mathbb{C}
, then
\mathbb{C}(X)
is the Galois extension of
\mathbb{C}(X/G)
with the Galois group
G
. In other words, generically free birational
G
-actions on complex varieties are the same as the epimorphisms of the Galois groups of functional fields to
G
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},Y
be complex varieties endowed with generically free
G
-action, and let’s assume
\dim X_{1}=\dim X_{2}=d\geq 1
.
Then we have two varieties
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}
of dimension
d
over the non-algberaically closed field
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}(Y)^{G}=\mathbb{C}(Y/G)
corresponding to the fibrations

(X_{i}\times Y)/G\to Y/G.

The birational equivalence
\mathcal{X}_{1}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{2}
over
\mathcal{K}
is the same as
G
-equivariant equivalence between
X_{1}\times Y
and
X_{2}\times Y
commuting with the projection to
Y
. Therefore, we have an obvious implication:

X_{1}
is
G
-birational to
X_{2}

\implies

\mathcal{X}_{1}
is birational to
\mathcal{X}_{2}

We claim that in some cases the
opposite
implication is true as well. The authors have the impression that experts must know this. As we found no reference, we provide below a proof for the sake of completeness.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
G
be a cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/N
for
N\geq 2
, the variety
Y
is
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with the action of generator of
G
given by
z\mapsto\exp(2\pi\mathsf{i}/N)z
. Then in the notations above, if the varieties
\mathcal{X}_{i},i=1,2
are birational over
\mathbb{C}(Y)^{G}
, then
X_{1}
is
G
-birational to
X_{2}
over
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

We will adapt the technique of Burnside rings and the specialization result from
[
24
]
to the
G
-equivariant setting.

The corresponding version of the
d
-graded component of the Burnside ring is defined as follows:
we consider isomorphism classes of smooth
d
-dimensional schemes endowed with
G
-action (here
G
is arbitrary finite group, not necessarily cyclic one), up to removing closed subsets of dimension
\leq(d-1)
. Such classes form a commutative monoid under disjoint union, and we define
\mathrm{Burn}_{d,G}
to be the group associate with this monoid. Obviously, it is freely generated by the
G^{\prime}
-birational equivalence classes in dimension
d
where
G^{\prime}\subset G
runs through the set of conjugacy classes all subgroups of
G
.

Let us consider a flat
G
-equivariant proper morphism

\pi:Z\to Y
with
N
-dimensional fibers, and such that the total space
Z
is smooth, and the fiber over the fixed point
z=0\subset Y
is a divisor
\cup_{i\in I}D_{i}
with simple normal crossings. The group
G
acts on the set
I
of divisors. We associate with such a map a formal
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of birational types of
N
-dimensional varieties endowed with
G
-action. Namely for each non-empty subset
J\subset I
such that
D_{J}:=\cap_{i\in I}D_{i}
is nonempty, consider
N
-dimensional variety
\widetilde{D}_{J}
which is
D_{i}
if
J=\{i\}
for some
i\in I
, and the exceptional locus in the blow-up of
Z
at
D_{J}
if
\#J\geq 2
.

Clearly for each
k=1,\dots,d
the disjoint union of varieties

D_{(k)}:=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset I:\\
\cap_{i\in J}D_{i}\neq\emptyset\\
\#J=k\end{subarray}}\widetilde{D}_{J}

is endowed with the natural
G
-action.
Then we define the specialization invariant as

\sum_{k=1}^{d}(-1)^{k-1}[D_{(k)}]\in\mathrm{Burn}_{d,G}\,.

The arguments from
[
24
]
work equally well in the equivariant case, and by the equivariant Weak Factorization we conclude that the above expression does not change under
G
-equivariant blow-ups of
Z
at smooth centers intersecting cleanly the divisor
\sum_{i}D_{i}
. The invariant of the
G
-equivariant family
X_{i}\times Y\to Y,\,i=1,2
for the diagonal
G
-action on
X_{i}\times Y
is by definition the
G
-birational class of
X_{i}
. This implies the conclusion of the theorem.
∎

One can replace the field of rational functions
\mathbb{C}(z)=\mathbb{C}(Y)
by the non-archimedean field of Laurent series
\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)
in the above result.

In general, we expect higher-dimensional versions of our result, and a parallel theory of
specializations of atoms
. As the reader can see, the fixed loci of
G
-action on
Y
play here a crucial role.

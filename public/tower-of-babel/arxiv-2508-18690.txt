A generalization of Barannikov-Kontsevich theorem
Takuro Mochizuki

Abstract
We study the twisted de Rham complex associated with a holomorphic function on a Kähler manifold whose critical point set is compact. We prove the E 1 E_{1} -degeneration of the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence. It is a generalization of Barannikov-Kontsevich Theorem. MSC: Keywords: twisted de Rham complex, integrable mixed twistor D D -modules, strictness, E 1 E_{1} -degeneration



1
Introduction

1.1
Twisted de Rham complexes

Let
X
be a complex manifold.
Let
f
be a holomorphic function on
X

such that the set of critical points
\mathop{\rm Cr}\nolimits(f)
is compact.

Let
\Omega_{X}^{k}
denote the sheaf of
holomorphic
k
-forms on
X
.
There exists the exterior derivative

d:\Omega^{k}_{X}\to\Omega^{k+1}_{X}
.
The exterior product of
df
induces morphisms

df:\Omega^{k}_{X}\to\Omega^{k+1}_{X}
,
i.e.,

\tau\longmapsto df\wedge\tau
.

Let
\lambda
be a variable.
Let
\Omega_{X}^{k}[\![\lambda]\!]

denote the sheaf of formal power series
with
\Omega_{X}^{k}
-coefficients.
Namely,
for any open subset
U
of
X
,
let
\Omega_{X}^{k}[\![\lambda]\!](U)

denote the space of
formal power series

\sum_{j\geq 0}\tau_{j}\lambda^{j}
,
where
\tau_{j}\in\Omega_{X}^{k}(U)
.
We obtain the differential

\lambda d+df:\Omega_{X}^{k}[\![\lambda]\!]\to\Omega_{X}^{k+1}[\![\lambda]\!]

determined by

\tau\mapsto\lambda d\tau+df\wedge\tau
.
It satisfies
(\lambda d+df)^{2}=0
.
The complex

\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}=(\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}[\![\lambda]\!],\lambda d+df)

is called the twisted de Rham complex,
or the formal twisted de Rham complex
when we emphasize to consider the formal series.
We obtain the cohomology group

H^{\ast}(X,\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f})
.
It is naturally a
{\mathbb{C}}[\![\lambda]\!]
-module,
where
{\mathbb{C}}[\![\lambda]\!]
denotes the ring of formal power
series with
{\mathbb{C}}
-coefficients.

There are several different versions of
twisted de Rham complex.
For example,
if
X
and
f
are algebraic,
let
\Omega^{k}_{X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}}
denote the sheaf of
algebraic
k
-forms
on the algebraic variety
X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}

with Zariski topology,
and
it is also natural to consider
the sheaf

\Omega_{X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}}^{k}[\lambda]

of polynomials
with
\Omega_{X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}}^{k}
-coefficients
on
X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}
.
We obtain the algebraic twisted de Rham complex

\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}}[\lambda]_{f}=(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}}[\lambda],\lambda d+df)

and the cohomology group

H^{\ast}(X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits},\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}}[\lambda]_{f})
.

1.2
A basic question

By setting

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{j}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f})=\lambda^{j}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}

for any non-negative integer
j
,
we obtain the filtered complex

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}
.
The associated complexes

\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits^{j}_{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f})=\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{j}/\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{j+1}

(j\geq 0)

are isomorphic to
the complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X},df)
.
Note that the cohomological support of

(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X},df)
is contained in
\mathop{\rm Cr}\nolimits(f)
.
Because
\mathop{\rm Cr}\nolimits(f)
is compact,

H^{\ast}(X,(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X},df))
is
a finite dimensional complex vector space.
There is the spectral sequence
associated with the filtered complex,
for which

E_{1}^{p,q}=H^{p+q}\bigl(X,\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f})\bigr)=H^{p+q}(X,(\Omega_{X}^{\bullet},df)).

We study the following question.

Question 1.1

Does the spectral sequence
for
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}

degenerate
at the
E_{1}
-level?

We can rephrase the condition in several ways.

Lemma 1.2

The spectral sequence degenerates at the
E_{1}
-level
if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions are satisfied.

•

For any
\ell
and
j
,
the exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits_{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}}^{j}\longrightarrow\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}/\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{j+1}\longrightarrow\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}/\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{j}\longrightarrow 0

induce the following exact sequences:

0\longrightarrow H^{\ell}\bigl(X,\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits_{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}}^{j}\bigl(\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}\bigr)\bigr)\longrightarrow H^{\ell}\bigl(X,\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}/\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{j+1}\bigr)\longrightarrow\\
H^{\ell}\bigl(X,\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}/\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{j}\bigr)\longrightarrow 0.

•

For any
\ell
,

H^{\ell}(X,\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f})

are isomorphic to

H^{\ell}(X,(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X},df))\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[\![\lambda]\!]

as
{\mathbb{C}}[\![\lambda]\!]
-modules.
In particular,
the formal twisted de Rham cohomology groups

H^{\ell}(X,\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f})

are free

{\mathbb{C}}[\![\lambda]\!]
-modules
of finite rank.

Remark 1.3

It is easy to see that the second condition implies
the first.
For the convenience of readers,
we shall recall that the
E_{1}
-degeneration
is equivalent to the first condition
in
§
3.1.3
.
We shall later explain that
the first condition implies the second condition
in a generalized context.
(See Corollary
3.10
.)

1.2.1
Classical results related to the question

There are two classical results
related to this question.
One appeared in the classical Hodge theory.
As Deligne observed,
if
X
is projective and
f=0
,
the desired
E_{1}
-degeneration
follows from the Hodge decomposition of
H^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
.
Deligne generalized it to
more general algebraic varieties by using mixed Hodge theory.
This
E_{1}
-degeneration and its generalizations
are not only deep results in the Hodge theory,
but also useful for various applications
including some vanishing theorems.
Deligne and Illusie
[
2
]
gave
an alternative proof of the
E_{1}
-degeneration
using the reduction to the positive characteristic.

The other appeared in the singularity theory,
in particular, the study of Brieskorn lattices.
If
f
has
only one critical point,
the
E_{1}
-degeneration holds
because
the
\ell
-th cohomology group
of
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X},df)

is
0

unless
\ell
equals
\dim X
.
In this case,
the
E_{1}
-degeneration is an important starting point of
the deep theory of primitive forms of Kyoji Saito.
(See
[
20
]

and
[
21
]
for more backgrounds.)

1.3
Barannikov-Kontsevich Theorems and variations

The modern study of twisted de Rham complexes
was opened by
the celebrated theorem
of Barannikov and Kontsevich.

Theorem 1.4
(Barannikov-Kontsevich)

The
E_{1}
-degeneration
for
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}

holds
in the case where

X
and
f
are quasi-projective.

This is a fundamental theorem
in the study
of the holomorphic Landau-Ginzburg model
of the mirror symmetry.
For example,
it is essential
in the proof of
smoothness of
some moduli spaces
associated with
Landau-Ginzburg models.
(See
[
7
]
.)

The original proof of
Barannikov and Kontsevich
was given by a generalization of
the method of Deligne and Illusie.
Indeed,
Barannikov and Kontsevich proved the following
theorem for the algebraic version of
the twisted de Rham complexes,
which implies Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.5
(Barannikov-Kontsevich)

Suppose that
f:X\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a projective morphism of
algebraic varieties.
Then,

\dim H^{j}(X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits},(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}},df))=\dim H^{j}(X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits},(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}},d+df))

holds for any
j
.

Theorem
1.5
implies that

H^{j}(X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits},\Omega_{X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}}^{\bullet}[\lambda]_{f})

are free
{\mathbb{C}}[\lambda]
-modules,
and the
E_{1}
-degeneration
of the spectral sequence
for the filtered complex

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}}[\lambda]_{f}
.
In the setting of Theorem
1.4
,
there exists a projective morphism
of algebraic varieties

F:Y\to{\mathbb{C}}

with an open embedding

\iota:X\to Y
such that
f=F\circ\iota
.
Under the assumption that
\mathop{\rm Cr}\nolimits(f)
is compact,
the set
\mathop{\rm Cr}\nolimits(F)
of critical points of
F

is decomposed as

\mathop{\rm Cr}\nolimits(F)=\mathop{\rm Cr}\nolimits(f)\sqcup\bigl(\mathop{\rm Cr}\nolimits(F)\cap(Y\setminus X)\bigr)
.
Then, we obtain the
E_{1}
-degeneration for

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}

from the
E_{1}
-degeneration for

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\Omega^{\bullet}_{Y^{\mathop{\rm alg}\nolimits}}[\lambda]_{f}
.

The theorem of Barannikov-Kontsevich
for the algebraic twisted de Rham complexes
(Theorem
1.5
)
has attracted many mathematicians
because of its significance
in the non-commutative Hodge theory
(see
[
7
]
),
and because the theorem and its generalization are
deeply related with various fields of mathematics.
Indeed,
alternative proofs
for Theorem
1.5

with different methods have been found
by Sabbah
[
16
]

using Hodge modules and microlocalization,
and by Ogus and Vologodsky
[
15
]

using their non-abelian Hodge correspondence
in positive characteristic.
Later,
Arinkin, Căldăraru and Hablicsek

[
1
]

revisited it in their study of
Deligne-Illusie method from
the viewpoint of derived algebraic geometry.
Sabbah also studied generalizations
to the case where
f
is not necessarily projective
but cohomologically tame
[
18
]
.
See
[
3
]
and

[
13
]

for a generalization to the Kontsevich complexes.

The
L^{2}
-analogue
of the twisted de Rham complex associated with
(X,f)

has been also studied
in
[
4
,
8
]
.
In particular,
Li and Wen
[
8
]
studied the case
where
X
has a complete Kähler metric
with bounded curvature,
and
f
is strongly elliptic,
which is
a kind of non-degeneracy condition
at infinity.
They established
an analogue of Theorem
1.5

in this context.

1.4
Main result

In this paper,
we shall study a generalization of
Theorem
1.4
.
It is an affirmative answer to a question
asked by Kontsevich to the author.

Theorem 1.6

If
X
is Kähler,
the
E_{1}
-degeneration
for
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}[\![\lambda]\!]_{f}

holds.

Note that
X
can be a small neighbourhood of
\mathop{\rm Cr}\nolimits(f)
,
and that we do not need any assumption
on the behaviour of
(X,f)
at infinity.
It is our purpose to show that
a local assumption around
\mathop{\rm Cr}\nolimits(f)
is enough for
the
E_{1}
-degeneration for
the formal twisted de Rham complex,
though global assumptions are useful
to obtain stronger consequences
as in Theorem
1.5
.

2.1.1
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-triples

For any complex manifold
X
,
we set
\mathcal{X}={\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}\times X
.
Let
p_{\lambda}:\mathcal{X}\to X
denote the projection.
Let
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}
denote the sheaf of
holomorphic linear differential operators on
\mathcal{X}
,
and let
\Theta_{X}
denote the tangent sheaf of
X
.
We obtain the sheaf of subalgebras

\mathcal{R}_{X}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}

generated by

\lambda\cdot(p_{\lambda}^{\ast}\Theta_{X})
over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
.
If
X
is an open subset in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
we have

\mathcal{R}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\langle\lambda\partial_{1},\ldots,\lambda\partial_{n}\rangle
.

We set

{\boldsymbol{S}}=\bigl\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\,\big|\,|\lambda|=1\bigr\}
.
Let
\sigma:{\boldsymbol{S}}\to{\boldsymbol{S}}
be defined by

\sigma(\lambda)=-\lambda
.
Let
\mathfrak{D}\mathfrak{b}_{{\boldsymbol{S}}\times X/{\boldsymbol{S}}}

denote the sheaf of distributions
on
{\boldsymbol{S}}\times X
which are continuous with respect to
{\boldsymbol{S}}
.
(See
[
17
, §0.5]
.)
It is naturally
an
\mathcal{R}_{X|{\boldsymbol{S}}\times X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}|{\boldsymbol{S}}}}\sigma^{-1}(\mathcal{R}_{X|{\boldsymbol{S}}\times X})
-module
by the action

(P_{1}\otimes\sigma^{-1}(P_{2}))\cdot\tau=P_{1}\overline{\sigma^{-1}(P_{2})}\tau
.
A sesqui-linear pairing of
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-modules
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}

is a morphism of

\mathcal{R}_{X|{\boldsymbol{S}}\times X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}|{\boldsymbol{S}}}}\sigma^{-1}(\mathcal{R}_{X|{\boldsymbol{S}}\times X})
-modules

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{|{\boldsymbol{S}}\times X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}|{\boldsymbol{S}}}}\sigma^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}_{|{\boldsymbol{S}}\times X})\to\mathfrak{D}\mathfrak{b}_{{\boldsymbol{S}}\times X/{\boldsymbol{S}}}
.
Such a tuple
(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime},C)

is called an
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-triple.

For
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-triples

\mathcal{T}_{i}=(\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\prime},\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\prime\prime},C_{i})

(i=1,2)
,
a morphism

\mathcal{T}_{1}\to\mathcal{T}_{2}

is defined to be a pair of
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-homomorphisms

\varphi^{\prime}:\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\prime}

and
\varphi^{\prime\prime}:\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\prime\prime}

such that

C_{1}\circ(\varphi^{\prime}\times\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits)=C_{2}\circ(\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits\times\varphi^{\prime\prime})
.
The category of
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-triples
is an abelian category.

For an increasing filtration
W
of
\mathcal{T}

in the category of
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-triples,
we have the increasing filtrations

W(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})
and
W(\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime})

such that

W_{j}(\mathcal{T})=\bigl(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}/W_{-j-1}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}),W_{j}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}),C_{j}\bigr)
,
where
C_{j}
denote the induced sesqui-linear pairings.

2.1.2
Direct image of
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-triples

Let
F:X\longrightarrow Y
be a morphism of complex manifolds.
We set
\omega_{\mathcal{X}}:=\lambda^{-\dim X}p_{X}^{\ast}\omega_{X}
,
where
\omega_{X}
denotes the canonical line bundle of
X
.

Similarly, we set

\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}:=\lambda^{-\dim Y}p_{Y}^{\ast}\omega_{Y}
.
We set

\mathcal{R}_{Y\leftarrow X}:=\omega_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes_{F^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})}F^{-1}(\mathcal{R}_{Y}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{-1})
.

For any
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}
,
we obtain the following
\mathcal{R}_{Y}
-modules:

F^{j}_{\dagger}(\mathcal{M}):=R^{j}(\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{C}}}\times F)_{!}\bigl(\mathcal{R}_{Y\leftarrow X}\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{R}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\bigr)\quad(j\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

For any
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-triple
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime},C)
,
we obtain the
\mathcal{R}_{X}
-triples

F_{\dagger}^{j}(\mathcal{T})=(F_{\dagger}^{-j}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}),F_{\dagger}^{j}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}),F_{\dagger}C)

(j\in{\mathbb{Z}})
on
Y
.
(See
[
17
, §1.4]
.)
When
\mathcal{T}
is equipped with an increasing filtration
W
,
let

W_{j+\ell}(F_{\dagger}^{j}(\mathcal{T}))

denote the image of

F_{\dagger}^{j}(W_{\ell}\mathcal{T})\to F_{\dagger}^{j}(\mathcal{T})

by which we obtain the filtration
W

on
F_{\dagger}^{j}(\mathcal{T})
.

\mathcal{D}

\mathcal{D}
-module of weight
w

\mathop{\rm MT}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm reg}\nolimits}(X,w)\subset\mathop{\rm MT}\nolimits(X,w)

.

,

]

in the regular case.

(
[

]

\mathcal{T}\in\mathop{\rm MT}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm reg}\nolimits}(X,w)

.

2.1.5
Pure and mixed twistor
\mathcal{D}
-modules on a point

Let
\mathop{\rm pt}\nolimits
denote the set of one point.
An
\mathcal{R}_{\mathop{\rm pt}\nolimits}
-module is an
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}}
-module.
Let
\sigma:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be
the anti-holomorphic map defined by

\sigma(\lambda)=(-\overline{\lambda})^{-1}
.
Let
\sigma:{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}^{\ast}\to{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}^{\ast}

and
\sigma:\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0\}\to{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}

denote the induced maps.
Let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}
be locally free

\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}}
-modules of finite rank
with
\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}
.
Let

C:\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{|{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}^{\ast}}\otimes\sigma^{\ast}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}_{|{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}^{\ast}})\to\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}^{\ast}}

be an
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}^{\ast}}
-homomorphism
which is perfect in the sense
the induced morphism

\Psi_{C}:\sigma^{\ast}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime})_{|{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}^{\ast}}\to(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})^{\lor}_{|{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}^{\ast}}

is an isomorphism,
where

(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})^{\lor}={\mathcal{H}om}_{\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}}}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}})
.
Such a triple
(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime},C)

is called a smooth
\mathcal{R}_{\mathop{\rm pt}\nolimits}
-triple.
For any smooth
\mathcal{R}_{\mathop{\rm pt}\nolimits}
-triple

\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime},C)
,
we obtain
the locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
-module

\Upsilon(\mathcal{T})

by gluing

(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})^{\lor}
on
{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}

and

\sigma^{\ast}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime})
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0\}

with
\Psi_{C}
.

A polarizable pure twistor
\mathcal{D}
-module on
\mathop{\rm pt}\nolimits

is a smooth
\mathcal{R}_{\mathop{\rm pt}\nolimits}
-triple
\mathcal{T}

such that

\Upsilon(\mathcal{T})

is isomorphic to a direct sum of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(w)
.
A mixed twistor
\mathcal{D}
-module on
\mathop{\rm pt}\nolimits

is a smooth
\mathcal{R}_{\mathop{\rm pt}\nolimits}
-triple
\mathcal{T}

with a weight filtration
W

\begin{CD}H^{j}(F^{p,p+r-1})@>{}>{}>H^{j}(F^{p,p+1})\\
@V{a}V{}V@V{b}V{}V\\

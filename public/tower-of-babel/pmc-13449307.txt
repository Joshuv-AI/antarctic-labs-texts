Post-splenectomy early biochemical trajectories and predictors in 935 patients with Wilson disease: a group-based trajectory modeling study
Authors: Xiuquan Shi, Sihuan Zhu, Ning Sang, Lei Jin, Hui Peng, Jie Chen, Qingsheng Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSplenectomy is commonly used to alleviate portal hypertension-related hypersplenism in patients with Wilson’s disease (WD) cirrhosis. However, early post-splenectomy biochemical responses vary substantially among individuals, and objective tools to characterize these dynamic patterns remain limited. This study aimed to identify early postoperative biochemical trajectories after splenectomy in patients with WD and to determine preoperative factors associated with unfavorable trajectory patterns.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 935 WD patients undergoing splenectomy (2001–2019). Serial ALT, AST, TBIL, and ALB were measured preoperatively and on postoperative days 1, 7, 14, and 21. Group-based trajectory models (GBTM) classified early postoperative biochemical patterns. Model selection was informed by comparison across multiple class solutions (K = 1–4), with K = 2 selected as the most parsimonious and clinically interpretable solution while avoiding very small subgroup proportions in higher-order models. Multivariable logistic regression identified predictors of unfavorable trajectories.ResultsGBTM identified two distinct trajectories for each biomarker. Unfavorable trajectory rates were 20.3% (ALT), 12.7% (AST), 13.6% (TBIL), and 46.1% (ALB). Model comparison supported the selection of K = 2 as the most parsimonious and clinically interpretable solution, while avoiding very small subgroup proportions in higher-order models. Higher Child–Pugh class strongly predicted unfavorable ALB and TBIL patterns. Sensitivity analyses excluding Child–Pugh yielded consistent results.ConclusionsEarly post-splenectomy biochemical trajectories in WD exhibit heterogeneous patterns. These trajectories reflect short-term dynamic responses rather than definitive improvement in intrinsic hepatic function. The identified predictors may provide signals for early risk stratification but require validation against long-term outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-026-04994-0.

Introduction

Wilson’s disease (WD) is an autosomal recessive disorder of copper metabolism characterized by pathological copper accumulation in the liver and central nervous system due to ATP7B gene mutations [1]. As the primary target organ, the liver frequently progresses to cirrhosis; notably, up to 40% of affected individuals develop concurrent portal hypertension and hypersplenism [2]. For patients manifesting severe thrombocytopenia or leukopenia, splenectomy serves as an effective surgical intervention to alleviate hypersplenism and ameliorate peripheral blood indices [3].

However, significant uncertainty surrounds the impact of splenectomy on early postoperative biochemical changes, presenting a critical challenge in clinical practice. Theoretically, splenectomy may alter hepatic perfusion by eliminating the ‘splenic artery steal’ phenomenon, but surgical stress and hemodynamic fluctuations may also exacerbate hepatocellular injury. Previous retrospective clinical studies have reported conflicting biochemical outcomes: while some observed a decline in postoperative transaminase and bilirubin levels [4], others documented a transient deterioration in liver function indices [5]. This heterogeneity impedes the development of standardized strategies for postoperative monitoring and management.

Traditional assessment of postoperative biochemical status relies primarily on absolute values or changes at discrete time points; such cross-sectional analyses fail to capture the dynamic heterogeneity inherent in the early postoperative period. Early post-splenectomy biochemical evolution is unlikely to follow a uniform course and may instead encompass distinct temporal patterns, including transient deterioration, gradual normalization, and persistent abnormality. While these distinct patterns likely correspond to specific pathophysiological mechanisms and clinical outcomes, there is a paucity of analytical methods capable of systematically identifying and characterizing these subtypes.

Recently, Group-Based Trajectory Modeling (GBTM) has demonstrated unique advantages in clinical research. By identifying homogeneous subgroups following similar developmental patterns within longitudinal data, this model offers novel insights into the temporal dynamics of disease progression and therapeutic response [6]. In hepatology, while GBTM has been successfully applied to analyze liver function trajectories following interventional therapy for hepatocellular carcinoma [7, 8], its application in the postoperative management of WD remains unreported. Furthermore, although recent studies have highlighted the prognostic value of the platelet-to-white blood cell ratio in WD [9], continuous longitudinal analysis of routine liver function indices post-splenectomy remains unexplored.

The early postoperative phase, particularly the first three weeks, represents a critical window during which the body responds to surgical stress and initiates repair mechanisms [10]. During this period, hepatocellular injury and repair, bilirubin metabolism, and protein synthesis exhibit complex temporal dynamics. Systematically mapping the trajectories of these biomarkers may help elucidate the short-term biochemical effects of splenectomy and provide an objective basis for early postoperative stratification.

Therefore, utilizing continuous postoperative monitoring data from a large cohort of WD patients, this study applies GBTM to address the following objectives: (1) to systematically characterize heterogeneous early biochemical trajectories of key liver-related biomarkers within 21 days after splenectomy; (2) to define the clinical characteristics associated with different trajectory subtypes; and (3) to identify preoperative predictors of trajectory membership. By identifying typical patterns of early postoperative biochemical dynamics, we aim to provide an evidence-based foundation for more refined early monitoring in patients with WD.

Materials and methods

Research design and research subjects

This retrospective cohort study aimed to characterize early postoperative biochemical trajectories after splenectomy in patients with WD and to identify preoperative predictors associated with unfavorable trajectory patterns. We included WD patients who underwent splenectomy at the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui University of Chinese Medicine between January 2001 and December 2019 and had complete perioperative laboratory measurements at preoperative baseline and postoperative days 1, 7, 14, and 21. The diagnosis of WD was established according to accepted clinical criteria. Splenectomy was performed in patients with WD-related cirrhosis and portal hypertension-associated hypersplenism, particularly in the presence of clinically significant thrombocytopenia and/or leukopenia, based on routine multidisciplinary clinical evaluation at our center. Patients with incomplete core perioperative laboratory records were excluded from the primary complete-case analysis. Because of the retrospective nature of the study and limitations of the structured dataset, detailed information regarding the exclusion of all alternative causes of cytopenia, preoperative vaccination status, and drug-specific exposure history was not uniformly available for all patients and was therefore not included in the formal statistical models.

Ethics

The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui University of Chinese Medicine (Approval No. 2020AH-13). Due to the retrospective nature of the study and the absence of identifiable personal information, the requirement for written informed consent was waived, and patient privacy and data security were strictly protected.

Research variables and outcome definitions

The key longitudinal biomarkers of interest were ALT, AST, TBIL, and ALB, assessed at the five prespecified time points. The primary outcome was biomarker-specific trajectory-group membership (unfavorable vs. favorable). For ALT, AST, and TBIL, unfavorable trajectories referred to patterns characterized by relatively higher levels and delayed decline over time; for ALB, the unfavorable trajectory referred to persistently lower levels across the observation period. Bleeding was defined as a documented history of gastrointestinal bleeding in the medical record; etiology-specific subclassification was not consistently available in the structured dataset.

Trajectory analysis

We fitted GBTM separately for each biomarker with a cubic polynomial term for time. Parameters and posterior probabilities were estimated using the EM algorithm, and individuals were assigned to the group with the highest posterior probability. Model adequacy was evaluated using AvePP (> 0.7) and the entropy index. Unfavorable and favorable groups were coded as 1 and 0, respectively, for regression analyses. Model selection was informed by comparison across multiple class solutions (K = 1–4), considering approximate BIC, entropy, and minimum group size. K = 2 was selected to balance model fit, classification quality, and interpretability.

Statistical analysis and regression modeling

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± s.d. or median (IQR), and categorical variables as counts (percentages). Between-group comparisons used Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Variables with clear clinical relevance and a univariable P < 0.10 were entered into multivariable logistic regression; ORs with 95% CIs are reported. PV_diam was analyzed per 1-mm increase; preoperative fever was defined according to the medical record; and sex was coded as male = 1 and female = 0. To address potential structural collinearity between Child–Pugh score and bilirubin/albumin trajectories, sensitivity analyses excluding Child–Pugh were performed. Variance inflation factors (VIFs) were calculated to assess multicollinearity. Due to the absence of a reliably extractable surgery-year variable in the structured dataset, formal temporal stratification or period-adjusted analyses were not performed. Hepatic encephalopathy was not included in the multivariable models because reliable structured documentation was not consistently available across the retrospective dataset.

Data management and software

All analyses were conducted in a Jupyter environment using Python (v3.11), with pandas/numpy for data management, matplotlib for plotting, and statsmodels or scikit-learn for regression modelling. The workflow was designed to be traceable and reproducible from raw data input to figure generation.

Study population and baseline characteristics

The study cohort comprised 935 patients with Wilson disease (WD) who underwent splenectomy and had complete serial liver function profiles available at five time points: preoperatively (pre) and on postoperative days 1, 7, 14, and 21 (d1–d21). The population consisted of 572 (61.2%) males and 363 (38.8%) females, with a mean age of 29.7 ± 14.4 years. Regarding hepatic functional reserve, the distribution of Child–Pugh classes A, B, and C was 733 (78.4%), 194 (20.7%), and 8 (0.9%), respectively. Overall trends in postoperative biochemical markers

As shown in Fig. 2, all four biomarkers exhibited varying degrees of fluctuation in the early postoperative period, followed by an overall declining or stabilizing trend. Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) increased from a preoperative baseline of 37.9 U/L (95% CI, 35.8–39.9) to a peak of 63.1 U/L (95% CI, 56.4–69.8) on postoperative day 1, and then gradually declined to 43.3 U/L (95% CI, 40.9–45.7) by day 21 (d21). Aspartate aminotransferase (AST) rose from 39.0 U/L preoperatively (95% CI, 36.6–41.4) to 69.4 U/L (95% CI, 64.2–74.6) on day 1, decreased to 45.6 U/L (95% CI, 42.4–48.8) by day 7, and remained at 44.8 U/L (95% CI, 43.4–46.2) on d21. Total bilirubin (TBIL) increased modestly from 23.4 µmol/L preoperatively (95% CI, 22.4–24.4) to 30.2 µmol/L (95% CI, 29.0–31.4) on day 1, then declined to a nadir of 18.5 µmol/L (95% CI, 17.0–20.1) on day 14, and was 20.6 µmol/L (95% CI, 18.8–22.4) on d21. Albumin (ALB) decreased slightly from a preoperative level of 37.6 g/L (95% CI, 37.3–37.9) to 36.0 g/L (95% CI, 35.7–36.4) on day 1, subsequently increased gradually toward near-baseline levels by day 14 (37.2 g/L; 95% CI, 37.0–37.5), and measured 36.1 g/L (95% CI, 35.8–36.4) on d21.

Trajectory identification and classification

Based on model comparison analyses across alternative class solutions (K = 1–4), a two-group (K = 2) trajectory model was selected for all four biomarkers (ALT, AST, TBIL, and ALB), as the most parsimonious and clinically interpretable solution, while avoiding very small subgroup proportions in higher-order models (Supplementary Figure S1 and Supplementary Tables S1–S4). Individuals were assigned to the group for which they had the highest posterior probability, and this classification was consistently applied across trajectory visualization, inter-group comparisons, and regression analyses. ALB trajectories were characterized as “High-Stable” (Favorable, n = 504, 53.9%) and “Persistent Hypoalbuminemia” (Unfavorable, n = 431, 46.1%). Distinct stratification was evident preoperatively: the Favorable group had a baseline ALB of 40.4 (95% CI 40.1–40.7), compared to 34.3 (95% CI 33.9–34.6) in the Unfavorable group, a disparity that persisted throughout the postoperative period. The model demonstrated a mean average posterior probability (AvePP) of 0.901 and an entropy of 0.663. ALT trajectories were identified as “Mild-Fluctuation Pattern” (Favorable, n = 745, 79.7%) and “High-Peak Delayed-Decline Pattern” (Unfavorable, n = 190, 20.3%). The Unfavorable group exhibited a marked peak on postoperative day 1 (d1) (159.2; 95% CI 130.2-188.1), significantly exceeding the Favorable group (38.6; 95% CI 37.2–40.0). By d21, levels remained elevated in the Unfavorable group (63.6 [95% CI 53.6–73.6] vs. 38.2 [95% CI 36.7–39.6]). The model yielded an AvePP of 0.985 and an entropy of 0.948. AST trajectories were similarly classified into “Mild-Fluctuation Pattern” (Favorable, n = 816, 87.3%) and “High-Peak Delayed-Decline Pattern” (Unfavorable, n = 119, 12.7%). The Unfavorable group showed a higher peak on d1 (194.4; 95% CI 162.9-225.8) and sustained elevation at d21 (55.2; 95% CI 48.1–62.3). The model showed an AvePP of 0.991 and an entropy of 0.964. TBIL trajectories comprised a “Low-Declining” group (Favorable, n = 808, 86.4%) and a “Persistent Hyperbilirubinemia” group (Unfavorable, n = 127, 13.6%). The Unfavorable group exhibited elevated baseline levels (42.6 [95% CI 37.7–47.4] vs. 20.4 [95% CI 19.7–21.1]), a further increase on d1 (54.4; 95% CI 49.1–59.8), and sustained elevation at d21 (47.6; 95% CI 36.1–59.0) (Fig. 3). The model achieved an AvePP of 0.983 and an entropy of 0.934. Comprehensive model diagnostics, including class separation metrics and posterior classification quality, are summarized in Supplementary Tables S1–S4.

Predictors of unfavorable biochemical trajectories

Multivariable logistic regression was used to evaluate independent associations between baseline characteristics and membership in the “unfavorable” trajectory group (Table 2; Fig. 4). For the ALB trajectory, higher Child–Pugh class was strongly associated with the “persistently low albumin” phenotype (OR 3.58; 95% CI, 2.52–5.09; P < 0.001). Age (OR 1.02; 95% CI, 1.01–1.03; P = 0.002) and hypersplenism grade (OR 1.61; 95% CI, 1.21–2.14; P = 0.001) were also independently associated with this unfavorable pattern. For the ALT trajectory, portal vein diameter (PV_diam, per 1-mm increase) was inversely associated with the “high-peak, delayed-decline” phenotype (OR 0.89; 95% CI, 0.83–0.96; P = 0.002), whereas sex (male = 1, female = 0) was positively associated with unfavorable trajectory membership (OR 1.20; 95% CI, 1.01–1.42; P = 0.039). For the AST trajectory, a history of gastrointestinal bleeding was associated with the unfavorable pattern (OR 1.85; 95% CI, 1.14–2.98; P = 0.012), while portal vein diameter (per 1-mm increase) showed an inverse association (OR 0.89; 95% CI, 0.83–0.96; P = 0.003); ascites was also inversely associated with unfavorable AST trajectories (OR 0.63; 95% CI, 0.40-1.00; P = 0.048). For the TBIL trajectory, higher Child–Pugh class was strongly associated with the “persistently high bilirubin” phenotype (OR 4.42; 95% CI, 2.97–6.58; P < 0.001), and male sex was also associated with unfavorable trajectory membership (OR 1.25; 95% CI, 1.01–1.55; P = 0.037). Sensitivity analyses excluding Child–Pugh score from multivariable models demonstrated stable associations for key predictors, supporting the robustness of the findings and mitigating concerns regarding structural collinearity (Supplementary Tables S6–S8). Variance inflation factor diagnostics did not indicate substantial multicollinearity among the main predictors (Supplementary Table S5).

Discussion

Drawing upon continuous perioperative monitoring data from a cohort of 935 patients with WD undergoing splenectomy, this study employed Group-Based Trajectory Modeling (GBTM) to delineate the dynamic evolution of four key liver function indices (ALT, AST, TBIL, and ALB) within the first 21 postoperative days. This approach identified clinically interpretable biochemical trajectory subgroups, followed by a comparative analysis of baseline characteristics and predictors within the same population framework. In terms of aggregate trends, transaminases (ALT/AST) exhibited a transient elevation followed by a gradual decline; TBIL showed a brief rise before decreasing; and ALB demonstrated a mild reduction before stabilizing. However, these aggregate mean curves failed to capture individual heterogeneity. Trajectory modeling revealed that all four indices could be classified into two primary biochemical phenotypes, broadly representing favorable and unfavorable short-term patterns. This suggests that early postoperative biochemical dynamics in WD constitute a reproducible and heterogeneous process rather than a uniform linear progression. Importantly, these trajectories represent early postoperative biochemical dynamics rather than definitive improvement in intrinsic hepatic function. In the immediate postoperative period, transaminase elevations may reflect surgical stress and hepatocellular injury, while albumin levels are influenced by perioperative fluid management, inflammation, and transfusion effects.

Predictive analysis indicated that unfavorable trajectories were more prevalent in patients with poor preoperative hepatic reserve or a significant burden of portal hypertension. Specifically, Child-Pugh classification was significantly associated with unfavorable ALB and TBIL trajectories, while the severity of hypersplenism predicted an increased risk of an unfavorable ALB trajectory. Furthermore, a history of gastrointestinal bleeding correlated with an unfavorable AST trajectory. These findings align with management principles emphasized in current guidelines, which advocate for “stratification by liver involvement severity and long-term monitoring of liver function and complication risks” [1, 11], reinforcing the need for intensified dynamic monitoring in patients with compromised basal hepatic reserve.

Notably, multivariable analysis revealed seemingly counter-intuitive associations: portal vein diameter was negatively correlated with unfavorable transaminase (ALT/AST) trajectories, while ascites appeared to exhibit a “protective” association with unfavorable ALB trajectories. Rather than reflecting true pathophysiological causality, these phenomena are likely attributable to “confounding by indication,” a bias common in retrospective studies, or the “treatment paradox” resulting from clinical intervention [12]. For instance, consistent with international guidelines (e.g., EASL), patients presenting with preoperative ascites or overt signs of portal hypertension typically receive more aggressive prophylactic interventions-such as frequent albumin infusion, strict fluid management, and hepatoprotective therapy. These measures artificially elevate serum albumin levels, statistically “correcting” the early biochemical trajectories [13, 14]. Additionally regarding the non-linear relationship between portal vein diameter and transaminases, previous studies indicate that splenectomy significantly alters hepatic hemodynamics by eliminating splenic artery steal and reducing portal pressure. This complex hemodynamic redistribution may mask the direct linear association between the degree of portal hypertension and hepatocellular injury [15–17]. Consequently, we interpret these findings as complex risk signals that warrant caution; assessing postoperative risk in WD requires transcending simple linear reasoning to account for the complexity of hemodynamic adaptation.

From a translational perspective, GBTM demonstrated distinct advantages in handling longitudinal biomedical data. Our findings may support closer early biochemical monitoring in patients with poorer hepatic reserve, severe hypersplenism, or a history of bleeding. In such patients, denser monitoring of bilirubin and albumin in the early postoperative phase, together with Doppler ultrasound surveillance for portal venous complications (e.g., PVST), may be clinically reasonable. Furthermore, given that fluctuations in liver enzymes have been linked to long-term prognosis in WD (e.g., neurological progression or hepatic failure) [18, 19], future research linking the “short-term biochemical trajectories” defined herein with “long-term hard endpoints” (such as decompensation events, readmission rates, or overall survival) may help clarify the prognostic relevance of this classification framework.

This study provides clinically relevant observations for the perioperative management of WD patients undergoing splenectomy. First, preoperative risk assessment may be particularly important in patients with poorer hepatic reserve, severe hypersplenism, or a history of bleeding. Second, postoperative monitoring may benefit from a dynamic trajectory perspective rather than reliance on isolated time-point comparisons. Third, while portal vein diameter is an accessible imaging metric, its clinical significance should be interpreted cautiously and in the context of the patient’s overall condition.

Several limitations warrant consideration. First, the retrospective single-center design may introduce selection bias. Second, the study focused on early biochemical trajectories within 21 days post-surgery and lacked direct linkage to long-term hard outcomes such as decompensation, re-bleeding, PVST, transplantation, or mortality. Third, data on certain potential confounders-such as intraoperative blood loss, perioperative transfusion or albumin supplementation, postoperative changes in copper metabolism, and adherence to chelation therapy—were incomplete in the medical records, which may affect trajectory classification and effect estimation. Fourth, although the cohort spanned a long study period (2001–2019), the structured analysis dataset did not retain a reliably extractable surgery-year variable, precluding formal temporal stratification or period-adjusted sensitivity analyses.

Therefore, potential temporal heterogeneity across the long inclusion period could not be formally evaluated and remains a source of residual bias. Fifth, biomarkers were modeled independently rather than jointly, which may not fully capture their physiological interdependence, and joint trajectory modeling may provide a more integrated characterization of postoperative biochemical dynamics.

Finally, the study population was predominantly Chinese, necessitating multi-center validation to ensure generalizability. Future studies should validate the stability and reproducibility of these trajectory phenotypes in multi-center real-world cohorts and determine their associations with perioperative complications and long-term outcomes.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that early post-splenectomy biochemical trajectories in patients with Wilson’s disease are heterogeneous and can be objectively characterized using group-based trajectory modeling. Key preoperative factors, including hepatic reserve, hypersplenism severity, and portal vein diameter, are associated with unfavorable biochemical patterns. However, these findings reflect early dynamic responses rather than confirmed improvement in intrinsic hepatic function. The identified trajectory patterns may provide useful signals for early postoperative stratification, but their clinical relevance requires further validation in studies incorporating long-term outcomes.

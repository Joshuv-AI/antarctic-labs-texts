Spike layered solutions for elliptic systems on Riemannian Manifolds
Anusree R Kannoth, Bhakti Bhusan Manna

Abstract
. In this article, we study the following Hamiltonian system: { − ε 2 ​ Δ g ​ u + u = | v | q − 1 ​ v , − ε 2 ​ Δ g ​ v + v = | u | p − 1 ​ u in ​ ℳ , u , v > 0 in ​ ℳ , \begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{g}u+u=|v|^{q-1}v,\\ &-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{g}v+v=|u|^{p-1}u&&\text{ in }\mathcal{M},\\ &\quad u,v>0&&\text{ in }\mathcal{M},\end{aligned}\end{cases} where ℳ \mathcal{M} is a smooth, compact and connected Riemannian manifold of dimension N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 without boundary. The exponents p , q > 1 p,q>1 are assumed to lie below the critical hyperbola, ensuring subcritical growth conditions. We investigate a sequence of least energy critical points of the associated dual functional and analyze their concentration behavior as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 . Our main result shows that the sequence of solutions exhibits point concentration, with the concentration occurring at a point where the scalar curvature of ℳ \mathcal{M} attains its maximum.

Introduction

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a connected compact smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension

(
P_{\varepsilon}
)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{g}u+u&=|v|^{q-1}v,\\

p,q>1
and

(HC)

\dfrac{1}{p+1}+\dfrac{1}{q+1}>\dfrac{N-2}{N}.

The corresponding scalar case was studied extensively by several authors. In
[
9

(1.1)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{g}u+u=|u|^{p-1}u

\displaystyle\text{ in }\mathcal{M},

and proved the existence of a mountain pass solution

that exhibits a spike layer, which, as

\mathcal{M}
. The geometry of the domain plays an important role in the concentration phenomena of such perturbed equations.
Benci et al. proved in
[

(

denotes the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category of
\mathcal{M}
. In another work by Micheletti and Pistoia
[
21

, existence of single-peak solutions concentrating at a stable critical point of the scalar curvature of
\mathcal{M}
was proved, and in
[
14

In this work we have adopted the dual variational technique for the existence. This is a standard technique used to prove the existence of ground states. In

In this work we have considered the Hamiltonian elliptic system on a compact and connected Riemannian manifold

The limiting profile depends on the solutions of

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+u&=|v|^{q-1}v,\\
-\Delta v+v&=|u|^{p-1}u&&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{aligned}\end{cases}

which we generally refer to as the entire system. This equation plays a crucial role in the energy estimate. The ground state solution of
(

(
1.2

. The major problem to study the asymptotics comes from the non-uniqueness of the solution of the entire system
(
1.2
)

This paper is structured as follows: In Section 2, we prove the existence of a critical point using dual variational formulation and also study the solution of the entire problem and its properties. Now, using solution of entire system as test function, we calculate an upper bound for the energy in Section 3. Section 4 is dedicated to studying the behavior of the solution of

2.
The Dual Variational formulation and Existence

We shall use the dual variational formulation, which is an effective technique in variational methods for handling these kind of coupled systems. A notable advantage of this technique is the Mountain Pass geometry of the level sets. In this section, we explore the fundamental relationship with the natural variational formulation of the system and its dual counterpart. The existence of a critical point for the dual functional then follows as a consequence of the Mountain Pass theorem. Before we derive the dual formulation, we fix some notations. We define

\displaystyle\alpha:=\frac{p+1}{p},\qquad\beta:=\frac{q+1}{q},

and denote the corresponding spaces

\displaystyle(I)\qquad X:=L^{p+1}(\mathcal{M})\times L^{q+1}(\mathcal{M})\text{ and, the dual }\,X^{*}=L^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M})\times L^{\beta}(\mathcal{M}),

\displaystyle(II)\quad Y:=L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{q+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ and, the dual }\,Y^{*}=L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

endowed with the norms:

\displaystyle\|w\|^{2}_{A\times B}=\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{A}+\|w_{2}\|^{2}_{B},\quad w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in A\times B.

From
HC
, we have

(2.1)

p+1<\beta^{*}=\dfrac{N(q+1)}{Nq-2(q+1)}\text{ and }q+1<\alpha^{*}=\dfrac{N(p+1)}{Np-2(p+1)}.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is a compact Riemannian manifold, we have that the inclusions

i_{p}:W^{2,\frac{p+1}{p}}(\mathcal{M})\hookrightarrow L^{q+1}(\mathcal{M})\quad\text{and }\quad i_{q}:W^{2,\frac{q+1}{q}}(\mathcal{M})\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\mathcal{M}),

are compact(Theorem 3.6
[

]
). Now to set the dual variational formulation we use the following lemma:

Lemma 2.1
.

For any

has a unique solution

independent of

,

\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,p}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{M})}.

Proof.

The result can be easily verified using local co-ordinate systems, partition of unity and the standard
L^{p}
-estimate.
∎

Define

Then the compositions

Precisely,

Lemma 2.2

(w_{1},w_{2})\in X^{*}

(iii)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{p+1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|w_{1}|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}+\frac{q}{q+1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|w_{2}|^{\frac{q+1}{q}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}<T_{\varepsilon}(w_{1},w_{2}),(w_{1},w_{2})>

Mountain Pass Theorem and the Existence:

Arguments similar to those in Lemma 1 of
[

]

[

Lemma 2.3
.

The functional

has the Mountain Pass Geometry, namely

(i)

There exist

Lemma 2.4
.

The functional

Proof.

Let

be a Palais Smale sequence, i.e,

Claim 1:

(w^{1}_{n},w^{2}_{n})
is bounded in
X^{*}
. Since
|I_{\varepsilon}(w^{1}_{n},w^{2}_{n})|\leq C

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\dfrac{p}{p+1}|w_{n}^{1}|^{\tfrac{p+1}{p}}+\dfrac{q}{q+1}|w_{n}^{2}|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(w_{n}^{1}\mathit{T_{1}}w_{n}^{2}+w_{n}^{2}\mathit{T_{2}}w_{n}^{1}\right)dv_{g}+C

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|w_{n}^{1}|^{\tfrac{p+1}{p}}+|w_{n}^{2}|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}dv_{g}+C+o(1).

This implies,

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{p}{p+1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\mathcal{M}}|w_{n}^{1}|^{\tfrac{p+1}{p}}dv_{g}+\left(\dfrac{q}{q+1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\mathcal{M}}|w_{n}^{2}|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C+o(1).

Thus
(w^{1}_{n},w^{2}_{n})
is bounded in
X^{*}
. Since

is reflexive, we may assume up to a subsequence that
(w^{1}_{n},w^{2}_{n})\rightharpoonup(w^{1},w^{2})

.

Claim 2:

(w^{1}_{n},w^{2}_{n})
converges strongly in
X^{*}
. Let
(u_{n},v_{n}):=(T_{1}w^{2}_{n},T_{2}w^{1}_{n})
, and
(u,v):=(T_{1}w^{2},T_{2}w^{1})
.
Since
T_{1}:L^{\frac{q+1}{q}}(\mathcal{M})\to L^{p+1}(\mathcal{M})
and
T_{2}:L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\mathcal{M})\to L^{q+1}(\mathcal{M})
are compact operators, we have (upto a subsequence)

(u_{n},v_{n})=(T_{1}w^{2}_{n},T_{2}w^{1}_{n})\to(T_{1}w^{2},T_{2}w^{1})=(u,v)\text{ strongly in }L^{p+1}(\mathcal{M})\times L^{q+1}(\mathcal{M}).

Now using a standard inequality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left||u_{n}|^{p-1}u_{n}-|u|^{p-1}u\right|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u_{n}-u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}||u_{n}|^{p-1}+|u|^{p-1}|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq c(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u_{n}-u|^{p+1})^{\tfrac{1}{p}}(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left||u_{n}|^{p-1}+|u|^{p-1}\right|^{\tfrac{p+1}{p-1}})^{\tfrac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u_{n}-u|^{p+1})^{\tfrac{1}{p}}(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u_{n}|^{p+1}+\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{p+1})^{\tfrac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\to 0\quad\text{ strongly}.

Thus
|u_{n}|^{p-1}u_{n}\to|u|^{p-1}u
and
|v_{n}|^{q-1}v_{n}\to|v|^{q-1}v

This immediately gives

∎

Corresponding Entire Problem:

Here, we address separately the existence of least energy solutions in our setting, along with an analysis of their symmetry and decay properties. First we recall the following:

Lemma 2.6

Let

h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the problem

-\Delta u+u=h\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

has a unique solution
u\in W^{2,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Furthermore, there exist a constant
K>0
such that

(2.7)

\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq K(s)\left\|h\right\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Let us consider the following linear continuous operator
[
1
]
:

(2.8)

S_{p}:=(-\Delta+id)^{-1}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ for }1<p<\infty,

and the continuous embeddings

\mathit{j}_{p}:W^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{q+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ and }\mathit{j}_{q}:W^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Then

S_{1}:=j_{q}\circ S_{\beta}:L^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\text{ and }S_{2}:=j_{p}\circ S_{\alpha}:L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{q+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

are also linear continuous operators. As in the previous section we can define the dual energy
I_{\infty}
on
Y^{*}:=L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\frac{q+1}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as

(2.9)

I_{\infty}(w_{1},w_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{p}{p+1}|w_{1}|^{\tfrac{p+1}{p}}+\dfrac{q}{q+1}|w_{2}|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}dx-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(w_{1}\mathit{S_{1}}w_{2}+w_{2}\mathit{S_{2}}w_{1}\right)dx.

Note that, the same results in Lemma
2.2
hold true for this case too. Hence, for any positive radial solution of the limit problem the corresponding dual pair is also positive radial and a critical point of the dual energy and vice-versa. Also both the energy functionals have the same value at these corresponding solution.

Now we see from (
2.8
) that the energy
I_{\infty}
has Mountain-Pass geometry. The proof follows exactly as in Lemma
2.3
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
be the min-max critical level of
I_{\infty}
defined as

\mathcal{C}_{\infty}:=\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{P}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}I_{\infty}(\varphi(t)),

where

\mathcal{P}:=\{\varphi\in C([0,1],Y^{*})\,|\,I_{\infty}(\varphi(0))=0,I_{\infty}(\varphi(1))<0\}.

Now for any (PS) sequence
(h_{n},k_{n})\in Y^{*}
at the level
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
we have

(2.10)

\left(\dfrac{p}{p+1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h_{n}|^{\tfrac{p+1}{p}}dx+\left(\dfrac{q}{q+1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|k_{n}|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}dx=\mathcal{C}_{\infty}+o(1).

This gives
(h_{n},k_{n})
is bounded in
Y^{*}
. Then there is
(h,k)\in Y^{*}
such that
(h_{n},k_{n})\rightharpoonup(h,k)
in
Y^{*}
, up to a subsequence. Also, the above equation directly implies that
(h_{n},k_{n})\nrightarrow(0,0)
. And by Lion’s Lemma, we get
r,\eta>0
and a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

(2.11)

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B(x_{n},R)}|h_{n}|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\geq\eta\quad\text{ or }\quad\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B(x_{n},R)}|k_{n}|^{\frac{q+1}{q}}\geq\eta.

Lemma 2.7
.

There is a positive critical point
(h,k)
of
I_{\infty}
with
I_{\infty}(h,k)=\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{n},v_{n})=(S_{1}k_{n},S_{2}h_{n})
. Then from the continuity of the operators we have

\|u_{n}\|_{W^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\|v_{n}\|_{W^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C.

for some positive constant
C
. Then up to a subsequence
(u_{n},v_{n})\rightharpoonup(u,v)
in
W^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times W^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Also, for any
(\phi,\psi)\in X^{*}
we have

\displaystyle I^{{\prime}}_{\infty}(h_{n},k_{n})(\phi,\psi)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h_{n}|^{\alpha-2}h_{n}\phi+|k_{n}|^{\beta-2}k_{n}\psi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\phi\mathit{S_{1}}k_{n}+\psi\mathit{S_{2}}h_{n}\right)dx=o(1)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|h_{n}|^{\alpha-2}h_{n}-u_{n}\right)\phi dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|k_{n}|^{\beta-2}k_{n}-v_{n}\right)\psi dx+o(1).

Replacing
(\phi,\psi)
by
(\phi,0)
and
(0,\psi)
we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|h_{n}|^{\alpha-2}h_{n}-u_{n}\right)\phi dx=o(1)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|k_{n}|^{\beta-2}k_{n}-v_{n}\right)\psi dx.

Infact, if we replace

\phi=\left||h_{n}|^{\alpha-2}h_{n}-u_{n}\right|^{p-1}\left(|h_{n}|^{\alpha-2}h_{n}-u_{n}\right)\text{ and }\psi=\left||k_{n}|^{\beta-2}k_{n}-v_{n}\right|^{q-1}\left(|k_{n}|^{\beta-2}k_{n}-v_{n}\right),

we get

\left\||h_{n}|^{\alpha-2}h_{n}-u_{n}\right\|_{p+1}=o(1)=\left\||k_{n}|^{\beta-2}k_{n}-v_{n}\right\|_{q+1}.

Now if we take
h=|u|^{p-1}u
and
k=|v|^{q-1}v
then from the Brezis-Lieb lemma and uniqueness of weak limit we have (see
[
19
]
)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h_{n}|^{\tfrac{1}{p}-1}h_{n}\phi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h|^{\tfrac{1}{p}-1}h\phi dx\right|=o(1)=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|k_{n}|^{\tfrac{1}{q}-1}k_{n}\psi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|k|^{\tfrac{1}{q}-1}k\psi dx\right|.

And finally using the continuity of the operators
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
we get

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\phi S_{1}k_{n}+\psi S_{2}h_{n}\right)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\phi S_{1}k+\psi S_{2}h\right)dx\right|=o(1).

Combining all these arguments we get

\displaystyle|I_{\infty}^{\prime}(h_{n},k_{n})(\phi,\psi)-I_{\infty}^{\prime}(h,k)(\phi,\psi)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h_{n}|^{\tfrac{1}{p}-1}h_{n}\phi dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|k_{n}|^{\tfrac{1}{q}-1}k_{n}\psi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\phi S_{1}k_{n}+\psi S_{2}h_{n}\right)dx\right.

\displaystyle-\left.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h|^{\tfrac{1}{p}-1}h\phi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|k|^{\tfrac{1}{q}-1}k\psi dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\phi S_{1}k+\psi T_{2}h\right)dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h_{n}|^{\tfrac{1}{p}-1}h_{n}\phi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h|^{\tfrac{1}{p}-1}h\phi dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|k_{n}|^{\tfrac{1}{q}-1}k_{n}\psi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|k|^{\tfrac{1}{q}-1}k\psi dx\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\phi T_{1}k_{n}+\psi T_{2}h_{n}\right)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\phi T_{1}k+\psi T_{2}h\right)dx\right|

\displaystyle=o(1).

This completes the proof if
(h,k)\neq(0,0)
.

Now if
(h,k)=(0,0)
. Define

(\tilde{h}_{n}(x),\tilde{k}_{n}(x))=(h_{n}(x+x_{n}),k_{n}(x+x_{n})).

Then we have,

I_{\infty}(\tilde{h}_{n},\tilde{k}_{n})=I_{\infty}(h_{n},k_{n})\to\mathcal{C}_{\infty},\qquad\text{and }\quad I_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tilde{h}_{n},\tilde{k}_{n})(\phi,\psi)=I_{\infty}^{\prime}(h_{n},k_{n})(\tilde{\phi},\tilde{\psi})=0,

where
\tilde{\phi}(x)=\phi(x-x_{n})
and
\tilde{\psi}(x)=\psi(x-x_{n})
. Then
(\tilde{h}_{n},\tilde{k}_{n})
is a bounded sequence and
(\tilde{h}_{n},\tilde{k}_{n})\rightharpoonup(\tilde{h},\tilde{k})
. Also by (
2.11
), we get

(2.12)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B(0,R)}|\tilde{h}_{n}|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dx

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B(x_{n},R)}|h_{n}|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dx\geq\eta>0,

\displaystyle\text{or}\qquad\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B(0,R)}|\tilde{k}_{n}|^{\frac{q+1}{q}}dx

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B(x_{n},R)}|k_{n}|^{\frac{q+1}{q}}dx\geq\eta>0.

This gives
(\tilde{h},\tilde{k})
is a nontrivial solution. Thus we have
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\leq I_{\infty}(h,k)
. Also

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(h,k)

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{p}{p+1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h|^{\tfrac{p+1}{p}}dx+\left(\dfrac{q}{q+1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|k|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left[\left(\dfrac{p}{p+1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h_{n}|^{\tfrac{p+1}{p}}dx+\left(\dfrac{q}{q+1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|k_{n}|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}dx\right]

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left[I_{\infty}(h_{n},k_{n})-\dfrac{1}{2}I_{\infty}^{\prime}(h,k)(h,k)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}_{\infty}.

It then follows from Lemma 3.1 of
[
31
]
that the ground state
(h,k)
is positive.
∎

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
be the set of all positive critical points of
I_{\infty}
. For any critical point
(h,k)\in\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
if we define
(U,V):=(S_{1}k,S_{2}h)\in W^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times W^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
then we see that
(U,V)
is a positive solution of the entire problem (
2.6
) with
(U,V)=(h^{1/p},k^{1/q})
. Let us denote the set

\mathcal{L}:=\{(U,V)\in W^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times W^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\,|\,(U^{p},V^{q})\text{ is a least energy positive critical point of }I_{\infty}\}.

Then from
[
8
,
30
]
, we can say
\mathcal{L}
consists of radial functions with the following decay estimates

|D^{\delta}U(x)|,|D^{\delta}V(x)|\leq Cexp(-c|x|),

for all
\left|\delta\right|\leq 2
,
C,c>0
.

Proposition 2.8
.

\mathcal{L}
is compact in
Y^{*}
.

Proof.

As the embedding
W^{2,\alpha}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is compact for all
\alpha<s<\alpha^{*}
, we have
W_{r}^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times W_{r}^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{q+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is compact. So it is enough to prove
\mathcal{L}
is a closed subset of
W_{r}^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times W_{r}^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Let
(U_{n},V_{n})\in\mathcal{L}
converges to
(U,V)
in
W^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times W^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
Then obviously
(U_{n},V_{n})
is a (PS) sequence and using the same arguments in Lemma
2.7
we can easily show
(U,V)\in\mathcal{L}
.
∎

3.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}U_{*}^{p+1}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}V_{*}^{q+1}dv_{g}

\varepsilon\to 0

\mathcal{M}
at

Proof.

We have,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}U_{*}^{p+1}dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{g}(p,R)}\phi_{R}^{p+1}\left(\Psi_{p}(x)\right)U^{p+1}_{\varepsilon}\left(\Psi_{p}(x)\right)dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,R)}\phi_{R}^{p+1}\left(x\right)U^{p+1}\left(\dfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\sqrt{g_{p}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{N}\Big[\int_{B(0,\tfrac{R}{2\varepsilon})}U^{p+1}\left(y\right)\sqrt{g_{p}(\varepsilon y)}dy+\int_{B(0,\tfrac{R}{\varepsilon})\setminus B(0,\tfrac{R}{2\varepsilon})}\phi_{R}^{p+1}\left(\varepsilon y\right)U^{p+1}\left(y\right)\sqrt{g_{p}(\varepsilon y)}dy\Big]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{N}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1}\left(y\right)\sqrt{g_{p}(\varepsilon y)}dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\tfrac{R}{2\varepsilon})}U^{p+1}\left(y\right)\sqrt{g_{p}(\varepsilon y)}dy+O(exp\left(\tfrac{-c}{\varepsilon}\right)\Big]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{N}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1}\left(y\right)dy-\dfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Ric(y,y)U^{p+1}\left(y\right)dy+o(\varepsilon^{2})\Big]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{N}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1}\left(y\right)dy-\dfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{6}\dfrac{S(p)}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1}\left(y\right)|y|^{2}dy+o(\varepsilon^{2})\Big].

Similarly, we can calculate for
V_{*}
and

\int_{\mathcal{M}}V_{*}^{q+1}dv_{g}=\varepsilon^{N}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V^{q+1}\left(y\right)dy-\dfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{6}\dfrac{S(p)}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V^{q+1}\left(y\right)|y|^{2}dy+o(\varepsilon^{2})\Big].

∎

We now estimate the error in
h_{\varepsilon}(t)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have

\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(U_{*}^{p}\mathit{T_{1}}V_{*}^{q}+V_{*}^{q}\mathit{T_{2}}U_{*}^{p}\right)dv_{g}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}U_{*}^{p+1}+V_{*}^{q+1}dv_{g}+o(\varepsilon^{N+2}),

\varepsilon\to 0

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps:

Step 1:

We first estimate the following error terms. Let

(3.2)

Solving for
\eta_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\left(-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{g}+\mathit{id}\right)\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)

\Delta_{g}\left(\phi_{R}\left(\Psi_{p}(x)\right)U_{\varepsilon}\left(\Psi_{p}(x)\right)\right)=\left(\Delta-(g_{p}^{ij}(x)+\delta_{ij})\partial_{ij}+g_{p}^{ij}(x)\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{k}\right)\left(\phi_{R}(x)U_{\varepsilon}(x)\right).

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,R)}\left|\phi_{R}^{q}V^{q}_{\varepsilon}-\left(-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\left(\phi_{R}U_{\varepsilon}\right)+\phi_{R}U_{\varepsilon}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\left((g_{p}^{ij}(x)-\delta_{ij})\partial_{ij}-g_{p}^{ij}(x)\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{k}\right)\left(\phi_{R}U_{\varepsilon}\right)\right|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}\sqrt{g_{p}(x)}dx

where

\displaystyle I_{1}:

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\frac{Nq}{q+1}}\Big[\int_{B(0,R/\varepsilon)}\left|\phi_{R}^{q}(\varepsilon y)V^{q}(y)-[(-\Delta+id)\left(\phi_{R}(\varepsilon y)U(y\right)]\right|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}\sqrt{g_{p}(\varepsilon y)}dy\Big]^{\frac{q}{q+1}},

\displaystyle I_{2}:

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\frac{Nq}{q+1}}\Big[\int_{B(0,R/\varepsilon)}\left|\left((g_{p}^{ij}(\varepsilon y)-\delta_{ij})\partial_{ij}\right)\left(\phi_{R}(\varepsilon y)U(y)\right)\right|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}\sqrt{g_{p}(\varepsilon y)}dy\Big]^{\frac{q}{q+1}},

\displaystyle I_{3}:

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\frac{Nq}{q+1}+1}\Big[\int_{B(0,R/\varepsilon)}\left|\left(g_{p}^{ij}(\varepsilon y)\Gamma_{ij}^{k}(\varepsilon y)\partial_{k}\right)\left(\phi_{R}(\varepsilon y)U(y)\right)\right|^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}\sqrt{g_{p}(\varepsilon y)}dy\Big]^{\frac{q}{q+1}}.

Estimate for
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
:

We have that

\displaystyle\phi_{R}^{q}(\varepsilon y)V^{q}(y)+\Delta\left(\phi_{R}(\varepsilon u)U(y\right)-\phi_{R}(\varepsilon y)U(y)

\displaystyle=\phi_{R}^{q}(\varepsilon y)V^{q}(y)+\Delta(U(y))\phi_{R}(\varepsilon y)+U(y)\Delta\phi_{R}(\varepsilon y)+2\nabla U(y))\nabla\phi_{R}(\varepsilon y)-\phi_{R}(\varepsilon y)U(y)

L_{p}

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{p+1}(\mathcal{M})}\leq c\left\|\eta_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{2,\tfrac{q+1}{q}}(\mathcal{M})}\leq c\left\|V_{*}^{q}(x)-\left(-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{g}+\mathit{id}\right)U_{*}(x)\right\|_{L^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}(\mathcal{M})}=O(\varepsilon^{\tfrac{Nq}{q+1}+2}).

Similarly we can obtain a similar estimate for
\xi_{\varepsilon}

\left\|\xi_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{q+1}(\mathcal{M})}\leq c\left\|\xi_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{2,\frac{p+1}{p}}(\mathcal{M})}=O(\varepsilon^{\tfrac{Np}{p+1}+2}).

Step 2:

Using
eq.

3.2

\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(U_{*}^{p}\mathit{T_{1}}V_{*}^{q}+V_{*}^{q}\mathit{T_{2}}U_{*}^{p}\right)dv_{g}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}U_{*}^{p+1}+V_{*}^{q+1}dv_{g}+\int_{\mathcal{M}}U_{*}^{p}\eta_{\varepsilon}+V_{*}^{q}\xi_{\varepsilon}dv_{g}.

Now from Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left|U_{*}^{p}\eta_{\varepsilon}\right|dv_{g}\leq\|U_{*}\|_{L^{p+1}(\mathcal{M})}^{p}\|\eta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p+1}(\mathcal{M})}=O(\varepsilon^{\tfrac{Np}{p+1}+\tfrac{Nq}{q+1}+2}).

We see that

\displaystyle\dfrac{Np}{p+1}+\dfrac{Nq}{q+1}=N+N-\dfrac{N}{p+1}-\dfrac{N}{q+1}>N,

since

\displaystyle N-\dfrac{N}{p+1}-\dfrac{N}{q+1}>0\quad\text{ for }pq>1.

Using the above estimates, we get

\left|\int_{\mathcal{M}}U_{*}^{p}\eta_{\varepsilon}\right|=o(\varepsilon^{N+2}),\quad\text{ and }\left|\int_{\mathcal{M}}V_{*}^{q}\xi_{\varepsilon}\right|=o(\varepsilon^{N+2}).

∎

Lemma 3.3

(Maximum Point of the MP curve)
.

For each
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,
h_{\varepsilon}(t):=I_{\varepsilon}(tw)
attains a unique positive maximum at
t=t^{*}_{\varepsilon}>0
and

(3.3)

t^{*}_{\varepsilon}=1+a\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2}),

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, for some constant
a
.

Proof.

First we show that
h_{\varepsilon}(t)
has a unique positive maximum. Differentiating with respect to
t
we have,

\displaystyle h_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=t^{\tfrac{1}{p}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}U_{*}^{p+1}(x)dv_{g}+t^{\tfrac{1}{q}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}V_{*}^{q+1}(x)dv_{g}-t\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(U_{*}^{p}\mathit{T_{1}}V_{*}^{q}+V_{*}^{q}\mathit{T_{2}}U_{*}^{p}\right)dv_{g}.

We first note that
h_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)=0
,
h_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t
small and
h_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\to-\infty
as
t\to\infty
.
Also if we look at the second derivative

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{\tfrac{1}{p}-1}}{p}\int_{\mathcal{M}}U_{*}^{p+1}(x)dv_{g}+\frac{t^{\tfrac{1}{q}-1}}{q}\int_{\mathcal{M}}V_{*}^{q+1}(x)dv_{g}-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(U_{*}^{p}\mathit{T_{1}}V_{*}^{q}+V_{*}^{q}\mathit{T_{2}}U_{*}^{p}\right)dv_{g},

is a decreasing function in
t
. Hence
h_{\varepsilon}(t)
has a unique positive maximum say at
t=t^{*}_{\varepsilon}.
Let us now define
\sigma(\varepsilon,t):=\varepsilon^{N}h_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)
. Then from Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\sigma(\varepsilon,t)

\displaystyle=t^{\tfrac{1}{p}}\left(A_{0}+\varepsilon^{2}A_{1}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\right)+t^{\tfrac{1}{q}}\left(B_{0}+\varepsilon^{2}B_{1}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\right)-t\left(A_{0}+B_{0}+\varepsilon^{2}(A_{1}+B_{1})+o(\varepsilon^{2})\right)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\big(t^{\tfrac{1}{p}}-t\big)A_{0}+\big(t^{\tfrac{1}{q}}-t\big)B_{0}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\big(t^{\tfrac{1}{p}}-t\big)A_{1}+\big(t^{\tfrac{1}{q}}-t\big)B_{1}\right)+o(\varepsilon^{2}),

where

\displaystyle A_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1}\left(y\right)dy,\qquad

\displaystyle B_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V^{q+1}\left(y\right)dy,

\displaystyle A_{1}=\dfrac{1}{6}\dfrac{S(p)}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1}\left(y\right)|y|^{2}dy,\qquad

\displaystyle B_{1}=\dfrac{1}{6}\dfrac{S(p)}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V^{q+1}\left(y\right)|y|^{2}dy.

In this expression it is worthy of noting that,
o(\varepsilon)
is uniform in
t
on each compact interval, which allows us to extend

upto
\varepsilon=0
as a continuously differentiable function on
[{0,\varepsilon_{*}})\times[{0,\infty})
for some
\varepsilon_{*}>0
. Now we see here,

\sigma(0,1)=0,\qquad\sigma_{\varepsilon}(0,1)=0,\qquad\sigma_{t}(0,1)=\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)A_{0}+\left(\frac{1}{q}-1\right)B_{0}<0.

Then by the Implicit Function Theorem we have a

function

defined for
\varepsilon\in[{0,\varepsilon_{**}})
with
\varepsilon_{**}>0
sufficiently small such that
\sigma(\varepsilon,t(\varepsilon))=0
and

. Now expanding

around

we get

\xi(\varepsilon)=\xi(0)+\varepsilon(\sigma_{\varepsilon}(0,1)+\sigma_{t}(0,1)t^{\prime}(0))+\varepsilon^{2}\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)+o(\varepsilon^{2}).

This gives us

for some constant

as
\sigma_{t}(0,1)\neq 0
. And finally, using Taylor’s expansion for

around

for the maximum point.
∎

Finally, we have the following upper energy estimate:

Proposition 3.4

(Upper Energy Estimate)
.

c_{\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon^{N}\left[I_{\infty}(U^{p},V^{q})-\varepsilon^{2}\dfrac{S(p)}{6N}\left(\dfrac{p-1}{2(p+1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1}(y)|y|^{2}dy-\dfrac{q-1}{2(q+1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V^{q+1}\left(y\right)|y|^{2}dy\right)+o(\varepsilon^{2})\right],

\mathcal{M}
at

Proof.

Let
\gamma(t):=tw.
Then we have

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle=\dfrac{pt^{\tfrac{p+1}{p}}}{p+1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}U_{*}^{p+1}(x)dv_{g}+\dfrac{qt^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}}{q+1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}V_{*}^{q+1}(x)dv_{g}-\dfrac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(U_{*}^{p}\mathit{T_{1}}V_{*}^{q}+V_{*}^{q}\mathit{T_{2}}U_{*}^{p}\right)dv_{g}.

3.1

. From (

Now let us calculate

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(t^{*}_{\varepsilon}w_{\varepsilon})=

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{p}{p+1}{t^{*}_{\varepsilon}}^{\tfrac{p+1}{p}}-\dfrac{{t^{*}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}}{2}\right)\int_{\mathcal{M}}U_{*}^{p+1}dv_{g}+\left(\dfrac{q}{q+1}{t^{*}_{\varepsilon}}^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}-\dfrac{{t^{*}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}}{2}\right)\int_{\mathcal{M}}V_{*}^{q+1}dv_{g}+o(\varepsilon^{N+2}).

We have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\dfrac{p}{p+1}{t^{*}_{\varepsilon}}^{\tfrac{p+1}{p}}-\dfrac{{t^{*}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{p}{p+1}\left(1+\dfrac{p+1}{p}a\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\right)-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1+2a\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{p-1}{2(p+1)}+o(\varepsilon^{2}).

\displaystyle\text{Similarly, }\quad\dfrac{q}{q+1}{t^{*}_{\varepsilon}}^{\tfrac{q+1}{q}}-\dfrac{{t^{*}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{q-1}{2(q+1)}+o(\varepsilon^{2}).

Using the expansions in Lemma

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(t^{*}_{\varepsilon}w_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{N}\left[\left(\dfrac{p-1}{2(p+1)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1}\left(y\right)dy+\left(\dfrac{q-1}{2(q+1)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V^{q+1}\left(y\right)dy\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\varepsilon^{2}\dfrac{S(p)}{6N}\left(\dfrac{p-1}{2(p+1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1}(y)|y|^{2}dy-\dfrac{q-1}{2(q+1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V^{q+1}(y)|y|^{2}dy\right)+o(\varepsilon^{2})\right]

which gives us the upper energy estimate.
∎

4.

In this section, we establish several key results concerning the least energy solutions associated with the energy level

A Boot-strap Argument for the Hamiltonian system

Let

ε

Step 0:
We shall construct an increasing sequence.
First note by Sobolev embedding, we have

If

Step 1:

) that

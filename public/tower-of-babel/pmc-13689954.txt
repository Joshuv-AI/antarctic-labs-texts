Pandemic-related disruption and post-pandemic recovery of dental service production in Brazilian Primary Health Care, 2015-2025
Authors: Laura Jackeline García Rincón, Ana Luiza Felizardo David, Raphael Daniel Rodrigues, Giovanna Oliveira Souza, Emanuelle Thomazini Zambom, Caio Possi Yazaki, Gabriela Fulanetto Crestana, Lorrayne Belotti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjective To analyze changes in dental service production in Brazilian Primary Health Care from 2015 to 2025 and characterize pandemic-associated disruption and post-pandemic recovery by procedure type and Federative Unit.Methods This ecological interrupted time-series study used secondary data on primary health care dental procedures recorded in the Brazilian Unified Health System. Six indicators were evaluated: scheduled consultations, health promotion and prevention activities, and surgical, endodontic, periodontal, and restorative procedures. Annual rates per 100,000 inhabitants were calculated. Three periods were defined: pre-pandemic (2015-2019), pandemic (2020-2021), and recovery (2022-2025). National percentage changes were calculated using selected annual rates, whereas the proportion of the pandemic-related decline recovered was calculated using period means. Interrupted time-series analyses were performed using segmented regression with Newey-West standard errors.Results Dental service production declined during the pandemic across all indicators, and mean recovery-period levels remained below pre-pandemic means. The largest reductions between 2019 and 2021 occurred in health promotion and prevention activities (-79.6%), periodontal procedures (-62.5%), scheduled consultations (-58.8%), and surgical procedures (-53.8%). Recovery remained incomplete across all indicators, ranging from 7.6% for scheduled consultations to 68.8% for restorative procedures. Total procedure rates showed a significant pre-pandemic annual decline of 21.4% (95%CI=-24.9% to -17.7%), an immediate reduction of 56.1% in 2020 (95%CI=-71.4% to -32.6%), and a positive post-2020 annual change of 14.8% (95%CI=1.2% to 30.2%). Marked territorial heterogeneity was observed in both the magnitude of decline and the pattern of recovery.Conclusion Dental service production in Brazilian Primary Health Care declined substantially during the COVID-19 pandemic and recovered only partially during 2022-2025, with persistent territorial disparities in recorded service production.

INTRODUCTION

Primary Health Care (PHC) forms the foundation of Brazil’s Public Health System (SUS - Sistema Único de Saúde), one of the world’s largest universal health systems, supported by an extensive service network. Guided by the principles of equity, universality, and comprehensive care, PHC serves as the main entry point to the health system and coordinates care across services, thereby supporting efforts to reduce health inequalities. Its nationwide reach supports both routine care and coordinated responses during public health emergencies, as demonstrated during the COVID-19 pandemic.(1-3)

Within this framework, oral health is an integral component of comprehensive and person-centered care because of its close relationship with systemic health. Its integration into PHC may influence quality of life through effects on nutrition, communication, self-esteem, and social well-being. Historically, the expansion of Oral Health Teams (OHTs) in Brazil marked an important step toward broadening access to dental care and shifting the traditional treatment-focused model toward health surveillance and promotion.(4,5) Despite the expansion of services through the growth of Oral Health Teams and the establishment of Dental Specialty Centers, improvements in access, service effectiveness, and epidemiological indicators have been uneven and, in some settings, limited. In some settings, service delivery continued to resemble the traditional care model, with only modest improvements in oral health indicators.(6)

Monitoring dental service production through information systems such as Outpatient Information System of the Brazilian Public Health System (SIA/SUS - Sistema de Informações Ambulatoriais do Sistema Único de Saúde) is important for evaluating service organization and performance. Analyses of service profiles and temporal trends can inform public policy planning and monitoring, particularly when different categories of dental procedures are examined separately.(7,8) Regional differences in production patterns reveal persistent inequalities in access, infrastructure, and management capacity, underscoring challenges to equitable oral health care across Brazil.

However, the COVID-19 pandemic prompted a rapid reorganization of service networks,(3) requiring PHC services to adapt workflows and priorities in response to the crisis.(9) As a containment measure, official guidelines recommended suspending elective appointments and maintaining only urgent care nationwide.(10) In dentistry, service disruption was particularly pronounced because care requires close physical proximity and frequently involves aerosol-generating procedures, increasing the risk of viral transmission.(11,12) This scenario substantially reduced procedure volumes, while the redeployment of dentists to frontline roles may have further constrained service delivery and contributed to deferred demand.(11,13,14)

Although previous studies have documented the immediate effects of the crisis during 2020-2021,(11,13,15) longer-term recovery patterns remain insufficiently characterized. Few studies have examined dental service production over an extended period to characterize healthcare recovery after pandemic-related disruption and deferred care. Evidence is also limited regarding differences among procedure categories and the heterogeneity of recovery across Federative Units, which may reflect local management capacity and longstanding territorial inequalities.

OBJECTIVE

This study aimed to analyze changes in dental service production in Brazilian Primary Health Care from 2015 to 2025. Specifically, it evaluated pandemic-associated changes and post-pandemic recovery patterns across dental procedure indicators and Federative Units.

METHODS

Type of study

This ecological study used aggregated secondary data and combined a national interrupted time-series analysis with descriptive analyses at the Federative Unit level. At the national level, annual dental service production rates were analyzed from 2015 through 2025. For the descriptive analyses, each Federative Unit-year combination constituted the unit of analysis, allowing characterization of temporal variation and territorial heterogeneity in PHC dental service production.

Ethics approval was not required because the study used only publicly available, aggregated secondary data.

Study period

Data from 2015 through 2025 were analyzed, providing 11 annual observations. For descriptive purposes, the study period was divided into three epidemiological phases: pre-pandemic, pandemic, and recovery. Annual rates were used to reduce short-term reporting fluctuations, improve comparability across years and Federative Units, and characterize long-term trends.

The pre-pandemic period was defined as 2015-2019, representing the baseline phase before the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and the associated disruption of routine PHC services.

The pandemic period was defined as 2020-2021, when health services in Brazil experienced the greatest disruption, including the suspension or restriction of elective dental procedures, reorganization of care pathways, and prioritization of urgent and emergency care.

The recovery period was defined operationally as 2022-2025, corresponding to the resumption and reorganization of PHC dental service delivery after the most restrictive phase of the pandemic. Although the international public health emergency ended later, 2022 was selected as the beginning of the recovery period because routine and elective dental services had already begun to resume after the acute phase of disruption.

For the interrupted time-series analysis, 2020 was defined as the interruption point, corresponding to the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and the introduction of major restrictions on dental service provision.

Data source

Data on dental service production were extracted from the SIA/SUS. Annual population estimates for each Federative Unit were obtained from the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE - Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatistica).

Data were extracted from SIA/SUS in March 2026. The 2025 dataset included procedures recorded from January through December 2025. Because SIA/SUS outpatient production records may be submitted or revised for up to three months after a procedure is performed, the 2025 data were considered consolidated for routine processing purposes at the time of extraction. Nevertheless, because administrative databases may undergo subsequent correction or exceptional reprocessing, analyses involving 2025 were interpreted cautiously.

Dental procedures were grouped according to codes from the Management System for the Table of Procedures, Medications, Orthotics, Prosthetics, and Special Materials (SIGTAP - Sistema de Gerenciamento da Tabela de Procedimentos, Medicamentos e OPM do SUS) into six analytical indicators: scheduled consultations, oral health promotion and prevention activities, and surgical, endodontic, periodontal, and restorative procedures (Table 1). The grouping was defined based on the official SIGTAP procedure-table structure and the conceptual relevance of each category to PHC dental service production.

The oral health promotion and prevention indicator included both collective and individual preventive activities. Accordingly, this indicator was interpreted separately from the clinical procedure indicators because collective activities, such as supervised toothbrushing, fluoride mouth-rinse programs, and group-based oral health initiatives, may have been disproportionately affected by school closures and restrictions on group activities during the pandemic.

SIGTAP codes were reviewed across the study period to identify procedures that were revoked, replaced, or introduced during the series. When procedure codes were revoked but continued to represent the same analytical group, they were retained for the years in which they remained valid and harmonized within the corresponding category. This approach was intended to preserve historical comparability across procedure groups and reduce the likelihood that observed temporal changes reflected coding revisions rather than actual changes in service production.

Data analyses

As described above, annual rates per 100,000 inhabitants were calculated for each dental service indicator at the national and Federative Unit levels to enable comparisons over time and across populations of different sizes. For descriptive analyses, the study period was divided into the same three predefined epidemiological phases: pre-pandemic (2015-2019), pandemic (2020-2021), and recovery (2022-2025). Federative Unit-level rates were summarized for each period using medians and Q25-Q75 intervals because of distributional asymmetry and territorial heterogeneity.

National percentage changes were calculated for three selected intervals: 2019-2021, representing the change from the final pre-pandemic year to the second pandemic year; 2021-2023, representing the early post-pandemic change; and 2023-2025, representing the most recent change. The percentage of decline recovered was calculated using period means as follows: decline=pre-pandemic mean - pandemic mean; gain=recovery mean - pandemic mean; and percentage recovered=(gain ÷ decline) × 100, when the decline was greater than 0.

A value of 100% indicated a return to the pre-pandemic mean; values above 100% indicated that the recovery-period mean exceeded the pre-pandemic mean; values between 0% and 100% indicated partial recovery; and negative values indicated that the recovery-period mean remained below the pandemic-period mean.

A descriptive spatial analysis was performed using thematic maps for each Federative Unit. The maps displayed the percentage change between the mean pre-pandemic and pandemic rates and the percentage of decline recovered during the recovery period. These analyses characterized territorial heterogeneity in pandemic-associated declines and post-pandemic recovery.

To assess temporal changes in national dental service production, an interrupted time-series analysis was performed using segmented regression models. The year 2020 was defined as the interruption point, corresponding to the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and the introduction of major restrictions on dental service provision in Brazil. For each dental service indicator, the annual national rate per 100,000 inhabitants was modeled as the outcome.

The segmented regression model included terms for time, the post-interruption period, and time after the interruption, allowing estimation of the pre-pandemic annual trend, the immediate level change in 2020, and the post-2020 change in slope. The model was specified as:

where Yt represents the national annual rate per 100,000 inhabitants in year t; Tt represents the time elapsed since the beginning of the series; Xt is an indicator variable for the post-interruption period, coded 0 before 2020 and 1 from 2020 onward; and Zt represents the time elapsed after the interruption, coded 0 through 2020 and sequentially thereafter. In this model, β1 estimated the pre-pandemic annual trend, β2 estimated the immediate level change in 2020, and β3 estimated the change in slope after the interruption. The post-2020 annual trend was estimated from the combined β1 and β3 coefficients. For interpretation, the combined coefficient (β1 + β3) was exponentiated and expressed as a percentage annual change, with confidence intervals derived from the corresponding variance-covariance matrix.Models were estimated using ordinary least squares segmented regression with heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent Newey-West standard errors to account for potential serial correlation between adjacent annual observations and nonconstant residual variance. The natural logarithm of the annual rates was used as the dependent variable, allowing the coefficients to be interpreted as relative changes. Regression coefficients were exponentiated and expressed as percentage changes with 95% confidence intervals. Residual diagnostics were examined to assess model fit, autocorrelation, heteroskedasticity, and influential observations, with no major concerns identified.

All analyses were performed in R version 4.5.3 using packages for data management, statistical modeling, and visualization.

❚RESULTS

From 2015 through 2025, dental service production rates in PHC varied across indicators, epidemiological periods, and Federative Units. Federative Unit-level rates declined during the pandemic period across all indicators, and recovery-period medians remained below their corresponding pre-pandemic medians (Figure 1).

The median rate of scheduled consultations decreased from 7,532.6 per 100,000 inhabitants during the pre-pandemic period to 1,002.1 during the pandemic period and 1,008.7 during the recovery period. Health promotion and prevention activities had the highest pre-pandemic median rate, which decreased from 22,761.6 to 1,484.6 and subsequently increased to 2,596.5 per 100,000 inhabitants. The median surgical-procedure rate decreased from 4,005.2 to 657.1 and subsequently increased to 790.9 per 100,000 inhabitants during the recovery period. The median endodontic-procedure rate decreased from 1,553.3 to 626.1 and then increased to 812.5 per 100,000 inhabitants. The median periodontal-procedure rate decreased from 6,802.1 to 1,672.9 and then increased to 2,698.9, whereas the median restorative-procedure rate decreased from 3,936.4 to 655.4 and then increased to 1,744.0 per 100,000 inhabitants (Table 3).

Between 2021 and 2023, all indicators increased, with the largest relative increases observed in health promotion and prevention activities (+137.0%), periodontal procedures (+119.5%), and restorative procedures (+93.6%). However, these increases were not sustained across all indicators. Between 2023 and 2025, scheduled consultations (-22.5%), periodontal procedures (-18.9%), and restorative procedures (-28.7%) declined again, whereas health promotion and prevention activities (+0.5%), surgical procedures (+5.6%), and endodontic procedures (+3.4%) showed modest increases.

Based on period means, the percentage of decline recovered remained below 100% across all indicators, indicating incomplete recovery relative to the pre-pandemic mean. Restorative procedures showed the highest percentage of decline recovered (68.8%), followed by periodontal procedures (50.6%) and endodontic procedures (31.1%). Lower recovery percentages were observed for health promotion and prevention activities (15.3%), surgical procedures (14.8%), and scheduled consultations (7.6%).

DISCUSSION

This study analyzed dental service production in PHC within the SUS before, during, and after the COVID-19 pandemic in Brazil. The time-series analysis identified three interconnected patterns: a pre-pandemic downward trend from 2015 to 2019, an immediate and widespread decline in 2020, and a subsequent but incomplete and heterogeneous recovery after the most disruptive phase of the pandemic. Numerical reductions were observed across all indicators in 2020, with the largest statistically significant immediate declines occurring in health promotion and prevention activities, periodontal procedures, and surgical procedures. Although production increased in subsequent years, mean levels during 2022-2025 remained below the corresponding pre-pandemic means across all indicators. Restorative procedures showed the greatest proportion of decline recovered, although recovery remained incomplete when assessed using period means.

The pre-pandemic downward trend suggests that the vulnerability observed during the health crisis preceded 2020. Periodontal procedures constituted an exception, showing a positive but non-significant pre-pandemic trend, indicating a distinct pre-pandemic pattern relative to the other indicators. This pattern is consistent with reports of progressive reductions in federal funding for the National Oral Health Policy (PNSB) from 2017 onward, in the context of fiscal austerity and Constitutional Amendment No. 95/2016, which limited federal public spending.(16-18) Previous time-series studies likewise reported declining oral health service-production indicators during this period despite the relative maintenance of service implementation, suggesting that the 2020 disruption may have intensified pre-existing structural vulnerabilities in Brazilian dental PHC.(19)

The decline observed between 2019 and 2021 was consistent with national and international evidence documenting pandemic-related disruptions in health service delivery. A systematic review by Moynihan et al.(15) reported a substantial global reduction in health service use during the first year of the COVID-19 pandemic. In dentistry, Meng et al.(14) highlighted the occupational risks associated with aerosol generation, which contributed to the suspension of elective care in several countries. Similarly, Guo et al.(20) and Cunha et al.(21) reported declines in dental service use during the initial phase of the pandemic.

The resumption of dental service production from 2022 onward varied across procedure categories. Restorative procedures showed the strongest recovery, with the 2023 rate exceeding the 2019 level, although recovery remained incomplete when assessed using period means. One possible explanation is the accumulation of unmet need during the pandemic, when substantial volumes of elective care were postponed and may have added to incident demand after services reopened. A similar pattern was reported in US health centers, where restorative and surgical dental care recovered more rapidly than preventive care and exceeded pre-pandemic levels during the first months after services fully resumed.(22)

The declines in scheduled consultations, periodontal procedures, and restorative procedures between 2023 and 2025 indicate that recovery was not sustained uniformly across indicators. This pattern may reflect changes in demand after some deferred care was addressed, together with persistent structural barriers to service provision, such as limited OHTs capacity, professional turnover, and funding constraints. World Health Organization reports on the continuity of essential health services indicate that many health systems experienced unstable recovery after the acute pandemic phase, with initial gains followed by further operational challenges.(23) These findings support continued monitoring and structural measures to promote sustainable dental service delivery.

The observed territorial heterogeneity was a central finding and should be interpreted in the context of longstanding inequalities in the organization and capacity of oral health services in Brazil.(24) Although all Federative Units recorded declines in dental service production during the pandemic, the magnitude of decline and, particularly, the extent of recovery varied considerably across the country. This pattern is consistent with evidence that implementation and consolidation of the National Oral Health Policy have occurred unevenly across Brazil, with differences in OHTs coverage, care-network structure, and integration with PHC.(18)

Previous research indicates that dental service use in Brazil remains strongly patterned by socioeconomic and territorial inequalities, with greater utilization in more developed regions and among populations with higher income and educational attainment.(25) Analyses of the Brazilian health system indicate that, despite the universal scope of the SUS, disparities in service provision persist across macro-regions, particularly between the North and Northeast and the South and Southeast.(26) In this context, the uneven recovery observed across Federative Units may reflect the interaction between pandemic-related service disruption and pre-existing vulnerabilities in the organization and delivery of oral health care.

The partial and uneven recovery observed in this study highlights the need for targeted strategies to restore health promotion and prevention activities, which are central to population-level oral disease control.(27,28) Territorial heterogeneity also underscores the importance of policies that address regional inequalities and strengthen service capacity in more vulnerable settings. Consolidating oral health within PHC requires not only restoring service-production levels but also strengthening institutional and service structures to improve resilience to future disruptions.

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. Because the analysis used aggregated administrative data, its findings depend on the quality of SIA/SUS records and may be affected by variation in data entry, underreporting, coding practices, and reporting consistency across Federative Units and over time. Recorded production reflects the volume of procedures performed but does not directly measure access, service coverage, care quality, unique patients treated, or population oral health needs. The interrupted time-series analysis was based on annual aggregated data with only 11 time points; therefore, its estimates may be unstable and should be interpreted as temporal associations rather than definitive causal effects. Nevertheless, the national dataset and integrated national and Federative Unit-level analyses provide a broad overview of recent trends in recorded PHC dental service production and inform discussion of structural challenges during the post-pandemic period.

CONCLUSION

Dental service production in Brazilian Primary Health Care declined markedly during the COVID-19 pandemic, against a background of pre-existing downward trends in several indicators. The interrupted time-series analysis identified an immediate reduction in national production rates in 2020, followed by positive post-interruption trends for some indicators. However, recovery from 2022 onward remained partial, heterogeneous, and nonlinear, with seven Federative Units – Ceará, Roraima, Espírito Santo, Tocantins, Pará, Sergipe, and Maranhão – showing mean recovery-period production below the corresponding pandemic-period mean.

Regional variation in recovery patterns may reflect persistent territorial inequalities in the organization and delivery of dental care within the Unified Health System. Together, these findings support continued monitoring of dental service-production indicators, targeted recovery strategies for Federative Units with greater deficits, and sustained policies to finance and strengthen Oral Health Teams within the Family Health Strategy. Such measures may help restore pre-pandemic service-production levels and strengthen the oral health component of a universal, equitable, and resilient Primary Health Care system.

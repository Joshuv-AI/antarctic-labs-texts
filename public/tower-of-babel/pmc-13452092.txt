A real-time PCR-based noninvasive prenatal RHD screening assay optimized for population-specific RHD allelic spectra in China
Authors: Xiang Wei, Linzhi Jiang, Qian Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrenatal RhD screening enables risk stratification for RhD-associated hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN) and supports rational，evidence-based use of prophylactic anti-RhD immunoglobulin. However, noninvasive prenatal RhD screening remains challenging in the Chinese population, primarily due to population-specific RHD allelic diversity and the limited adaptability of current assay designs.MethodsWe developed and optimized a real-time PCR–based assay for noninvasive prenatal RHD genotyping, specifically designed to accommodate the spectrum of clinically relevant RHD alleles in the Chinese population. By simultaneously targeting RHD exons 5, 9, and 10, the assay enables detection of paternally inherited RHD alleles within a single analytical workflow. This assay facilitates accurate fetal RHD status determination in pregnancies involving the predominant RhD-negative alleles, including RHD*01N.01, RHD*01N.03, and RHD*01EL.01.ResultsThe assay accurately predicted fetal RHD status across all simulated maternal genotypes, including those carrying non-deletion alleles such as RHD*01N.03 and RHD*01EL.01.ConclusionThis study presents a methodological framework for noninvasive prenatal RHD genotyping, specifically optimized for Chinese and other East Asian populations. Assay performance relies on distinct amplification patterns of population-specific RHD alleles across exons 5, 9, and 10. In addition, inclusion of exon 5 allows detection of RHD variants frequently observed in African populations, suggesting potential applicability across genetically diverse populations, pending further population-specific validation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12920-026-02337-2.

Background

RhD blood group incompatibility between mother and fetus represents a major etiological factor in hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN) [1, 2]. This condition most commonly arises when an RhD-negative woman-previously sensitized to RhD antigen-carries an RhD-positive fetus during a subsequent pregnancy [3, 4]. Severe RhD-associated HDFN is characterized by rapid clinical progression and poor outcomes, including fetal or neonatal death and kernicterus, particularly in the absence of timely intervention [5]. In Western countries, widespread implementation of prenatal RhD screening programs enables accurate prediction of fetal RhD status in RhD-negative pregnant women [6, 7], thereby facilitating evidence-based risk stratification and targeted prophylactic interventions that have substantially reduced the incidence of RhD alloimmunization and HDFN [8].

Substantial racial and genetic heterogeneity underlies the molecular basis of RhD negativity. In Caucasian populations, the RhD-negative phenotype is predominantly associated with the RHD*01N.01 allele, whereas individuals of African ancestry commonly carry RHD*01N.01, RHD*01N.07, or RHD*08N.01 alleles [9–11]. In contrast, more than 95% of RhD-negative alleles in the Chinese population are attributable to RHD*01N.01, RHD*01N.03, and RHD*01EL.01 [12]. Consequently, prenatal RhD screening strategies originally developed for Western populations—most frequently real-time PCR assays targeting RHD exons 5, 7, and 10—may lack sensitivity and specificity when applied directly to populations with distinct RHD allelic architectures, such as those prevalent in East Asia. To address this limitation, high-throughput sequencing–based prenatal screening approaches have been proposed and demonstrated high analytical performance [13, 14]. However, such approaches remain time-intensive, technically demanding, and costly, limiting their feasibility for routine large-scale clinical screening. Alternative strategies that incorporate prior maternal RHD genotyping have also been explored; however, these approaches remain susceptible to false-positive results arising from paternal transmission of RHD*01EL.01 [15].

With the rising incidence of multiple pregnancies following the relaxation of China’s family planning policies, there is an increasing demand for prenatal RHD screening strategies that are cost-effective, efficient, and analytically robust—capable of accommodating the population-specific allelic diversity observed in the Chinese population. Accordingly, this study focuses on assay design and analytical performance evaluation to address this population-specific RHD allelic diversity, rather than on large-scale clinical validation.

Methods

Sample source and genomic DNA extraction

Samples were obtained from the Department of Medical Genetics, West China Second University Hospital, Sichuan University. All samples consisted of residual specimens remaining after routine noninvasive prenatal screening (NIPS) performed at ≥ 12 weeks of gestation, with written informed consent was obtained from all donors for the use of these residual specimens in scientific research. A total of four representative samples were included for assay development and analytical evaluation. According to reports from the hospital blood bank laboratory, three samples were phenotypically RhD-negative and genotypically confirmed as RHD*01N.01/RHD*01N.01, RHD*01N.03/RHD*01N.01, and RHD*01EL.01/RHD*01EL.01, respectively. One additional sample was reported as RhD-positive. The RHD*01EL.01/RHD*01EL.01 sample was served as a two-copy reference standard for exon 10 copy number assessment. Exon 10 copy number analysis indicated that the RhD-positive sample harbored a single copy of exon 10, consistent with a heterozygous genotype comprising RHD*01 and RHD*01N.01.

For each participant, 8–10 mL of maternal peripheral blood was collected in EDTA-anticoagulant tubes containing DNA stabilization reagents. White blood cells were isolated within 48 hours of collection and stored at −8°C until further processing. Genomic DNA was extracted using a magnetic bead–based DNA extraction kit (M2027-A96; BioKeystone Technologies Co., Ltd.), according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

Primer sets targeting RHD exons 5 and 10 were adopted from previously published studies [15, 17] and have been validated for specific amplification of the RHD gene. The exon 5 primers specifically amplify the RHD*01 allele and exhibit no cross-reactivity with the highly homologous RHCE gene or the RHD*08N.01 allele. The exon 10 primers selectively amplify the RHD gene and were designed to avoid cross-reactivity with RHCE. In contrast, primers targeting exon 9 were custom designed to accommodate population-specific RHD allelic variants. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the exon 9 forward primer selectively amplifies the RHD gene without cross-reactivity to RHCE, whereas the reverse primer selectively amplifies the RHD*01 allele while discriminating against the RHD*01EL.01 allele. To enhance amplification specificity, an intentional mismatch was introduced at the penultimate nucleotide position of the reverse primer, as previously described [18]. Real-time PCR of simulated cell-free DNA

Real-time PCR was performed on simulated cell-free DNA (cfDNA) samples to assess analytical performance of the assay targeting RHD exons 5, 9, and 10, twith B2M serving as the internal control gene.Reactions were run on a 7500 Fast Dx Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems) using iTaq Universal SYBR Green Supermix (Bio-Rad) . Each reaction was performed in a total volume of 20 µL containing 10 µL of 2 × SYBR Green Supermix, 1 µL each of forward and reverse primers (10 µM), 2 µL of cfDNA template (0.5 ng/µL), nuclease-free water to volume, and-specifically for RHD exon amplification-1.2 µL of 5 × GC enhancer, as previously described [19].

Thermal cycling was performed as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, followed by 40 amplification cycles of 95 °C for 15 s and 60 °C for 60 s, with subsequent melt curve analysis under the instrument’s default settings to assess amplification specificity. To accommodate the low-abundance cfDNA templates, the number of amplification cycles was increased to 40 to ensure sufficient analytical sensitivity. Because dye-based real-time PCR may generate nonspecific fluorescence signals, a GC enhancer was included to suppress nonspecific amplification, including primer–dimer formation that typically emerges after extended cycling.

Interpretation criteria

Samples exhibiting a B2M Ct value > 32 were excluded from subsequent analysis due to insufficient template integrity and quantity. Interpretation of RHD exon results followed a predefined analytical workflow, as summarized in Fig. 2. Amplification of RHD exons 5, 9, and 10 was classified as positive only when all prespecified analytical criteria were simultaneously satisfied:(ⅰ) a well-resolved amplification curve exhibiting clearly distinguishable baseline, exponential, and plateau phases; (ⅱ) confirmation of the expected amplicon through melt curve analysis and/or agarose gel electrophoresis; (ⅲ) a Ct value ≤ 38.Fig. 2Analytical workflow for interpreting RHD exon 5, 9, and 10 real-time PCR results. Each sample was tested in triplicate alongside the internal control gene B2M. Samples with B2M Ct > 32 were considered invalid. Exon-level positivity required Ct ≤ 38 with a specific amplicon confirmed by melt-curve analysis and/or agarose gel electrophoresis. An exon was classified as positive when ≥ 2/3 replicates were positive, negative when 0/3 were positive, and invalid when 1/3 was positive. ΔΔCt relative to an internal calibrator was used for auxiliary signal annotation, with reflex maternal genomic DNA real-time PCR performed when indicated

Each sample–exon combination was tested in triplicate. At the sample level, an exon was classified as positive when ≥ 2 of 3 replicates met the positivity criteria, no RHD signal (–) when none of the replicates were positive, and invalid when only a single replicate was positive, in which case repeat testing was recommended.

For auxiliary signal assessment, ΔCt was defined as the difference between the Ct values of the target RHD exon and the internal control gene B2M (Ct_RHD − Ct_B2M). To minimize inter-run variability, each PCR batch included a fragmented genomic DNA sample with a known positive signal as an internal calibrator, corresponding to a heterozygous RHD*01/RHD*01N.01 genotype. ΔΔCt was defined as ΔCt_sample − ΔCt_calibrator and was used as an auxiliary parameter rather than a primary decision criterion. Samples with ΔΔCt ≤ 2 were subjected to reflex real-time PCR analysis of maternal genomic DNA to assist in signal source interpretation. Detection of the corresponding exon in maternal genomic DNA resulted in annotation as a strong RHD signal (+ + +), whereas absence of maternal amplification led to annotation as a weak RHD signal ( +).

Discussion

In this study, we optimized a real-time PCR–based approach for noninvasive prenatal RhD screening by incorporating RHD exon 9 into the amplification panel, accounting for the RHD allelic spectrum prevalent among RhD-negative individuals in the Chinese population. This design enabled reliable analytical discrimination of fetal RHD status across maternal genotypes including RHD*01N.01, RHD*01N.03, and RHD*01EL.01, without prior maternal RHD genotyping. Assay development and evaluation were performed exclusively using simulated cfDNA mixtures generated from fragmented genomic DNA at predefined proportions, without analysis of maternal plasma cfDNA samples.By addressing population-specific RHD alleles, this strategy reduces false-positive signals associated with RHD*01EL.01 and improves analytical robustness in East Asian populations. Compared with amplicon-based next-generation sequencing (NGS) approaches, the proposed method offers advantages in turnaround time, technical simplicity, and cost per sample, while maintaining analytical sensitivity at simulated fetal DNA proportions as low as 5%.

The assay simultaneously targets three RHD exons—5, 9, and 10—each contributing distinct analytical information. Exon 9 represents the most informative target, as the common RHD-negative alleles observed in the Chinese population lack detectable amplification at this exon, making it critical for accurate assay interpretation. Exon 5, while not essential for binary fetal RhD prediction, facilitates discrimination of the RHD*01EL.01 allele, which carries distinct immunohematological significance [24–26]. Moreover, amplification of exon 5 enables detection of additional RHD variants reported in other populations, such as RHD*01N.07 and RHD*08N.01, thereby extending the potential analytical applicability of the assay beyond East Asia. Exon 10 primarily differentiates between RHD*01N.01 and RHD*01N.03; because these alleles exhibit comparable analytical behavior with respect to fetal RhD inference, inclusion of exon 10 may be tailored according to specific analytical or research objectives.

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, the use of a SYBR Green–based real-time PCR format necessitated separate amplification reactions for each exon target,thereby increasing assay complexity, hands-on time, and cfDNA consumption. Implementation of probe-based multiplex qPCR strategies, such as TaqMan assays [27–29], may enable simultaneous targets detection and improve analytical efficiency. Second, although auxiliary signal assessment was incorporated into the analytical workflow—including ΔCt and ΔΔCt comparisons relative to an internal calibrator and reflex testing of maternal genomic DNA—these interpretative elements were applied in an operational and exploratory manner and have not yet been validated in large clinical cohorts. Larger datasets may ultimately support further optimization of this multi-step analytical framework to improve discrimination of RHD signal origin between maternal and fetal cfDNA, thereby enhancing robustness in signal origin interpretation.

Conclusions

This study presents an optimized real-time PCR–based analytical framework for noninvasive prenatal RHD screening that accounts for population-specific allelic diversity in the Chinese population. By integrating exon-specific amplification patterns, the proposed assay improves analytical reliability without reliance on prior maternal RHD genotyping. The approach serves as a methodological optimization and provides a foundation for future large-scale clinical validation.

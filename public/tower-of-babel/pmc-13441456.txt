Beyond a single signal: How can bovine pregnancy be established without IFNT?
Authors: Toshihiro SAKURAI, Hanako BAI, Kazuya KUSAMA, Kazuhiko IMAKAWA
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractInterferon tau (IFNT) has been regarded as the dominant conceptus signal for maternal recognition of pregnancy (MRP) in ruminants. In cattle, IFNT is classically proposed to prevent luteolysis by suppressing uterine luteolytic pulses of prostaglandin F2α (PGF2α). IFNT also induces endometrial interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) such as ISG15, MX dynamin-like GTPase 2 (MX2), and 2′-5′-oligoadenylate synthetase 1 (OAS1), thereby supporting uterine receptivity and an attachment-supportive immune milieu. A recent loss-of-function report using conceptuses lacking functional IFNT challenges this view by describing conceptus elongation and persistence of luteal function in a subset of recipients despite markedly reduced uterine ISG induction during the peri-implantation window. Here, we interpret this claim conservatively and specify experimental boundary conditions that currently limit generalization, including somatic cell nuclear transfer (SCNT), multi-embryo transfer, limited sample size, and the absence of direct physiological readouts of PGF2α pulsatility and the estrogen receptor 1 (ESR1) and oxytocin receptor (OXTR) axis. We then summarize possible buffering routes that could support early pregnancy when IFNT input is weakened, focusing on prostaglandin network rerouting toward luteoprotective outputs such as PGE2, extracellular vesicle (EV)-mediated communication, steroid-immune interactions, and corpus luteum (CL) gating of luteolytic sensitivity. Finally, we discuss how bovine endogenous retrovirus (BERV) long terminal repeats (LTRs) retaining interferon-responsive motifs, including interferon-stimulated response elements (ISREs), gamma-activated sequences (GAS), and ETS-family motifs, may act as enhancer substrates that amplify cytokine inputs in a cell-type and chromatin-dependent manner. Overall, we suggest that IFNT should be evaluated as a frequently dominant signal within a system that includes parallel buffering routes, such that the apparent requirement for IFNT can vary with conceptus load, uterine endocrine conditions, and ERV-derived regulatory architecture.Keywords: Cattle, Endigenous retrovirus, Interferon tau (IFNT), IFNT ablation, Maternal recognition of pregnancy, Pregnancy

Introduction

Interferon tau (IFNT) has long been regarded as the canonical conceptus signal for maternal recognition of pregnancy (MRP) in ruminants. In cattle, the accumulated evidence for nearly 40 years indicates that trophoblast-derived IFNT modifies endometrial production of prostaglandin F2α (PGF2α) through suppression of its pulsatile release and induction of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) including ISG15, MX dynamin-like GTPase 2 (MX2), and 2′-5′-oligoadenylate synthetase 1 (OAS1). Together, these actions are proposed to maintain progesterone (P4) production by the corpus luteum (CL) and enhancing uterine receptivity during the peri-implantation window [1,2,3,4,5].

Quite recently, however, a loss-of-function study without embryonic IFNT reported that pregnancy is established in a subset of recipients even when canonical uterine IFN and ISG signatures are markedly reduced around day 18 [6]. This claim is intriguing because it challenges widely proven experimental evidence on IFNT, and it motivates a careful distinction between what is strictly necessary and what is usually sufficient for successful MRP in cattle [1, 2].

Until recently, mechanistic studies have nevertheless established trophoblast lineage control of IFNT transcription, including caudal-type homeobox 2 (CDX2)-dependent activation and supportive chromatin states that enable strong but temporally restricted expression aligned to conceptus elongation and early attachment, between days 15–19 of gestation in cattle [2, 7]. At the same time, conceptus-maternal communication in cattle involves several parallel inputs during peri-implantation window. These include extracellular vesicle (EV)-mediated communication, immune modulators, and epithelial adhesion/attachment programs that can be regulated in a spatially and temporally compartmentalized manner [8, 9]. In parallel, conceptus-derived prostaglandins remodel endometrial signaling prior to classical pregnancy-recognition readouts, providing a possible substrate for buffering of luteolytic control [9,10,11].

In line with this broader view, several observations indicate that key peri-implantation modules can be assembled through inputs that are not strictly dependent on soluble IFNT. These include selectins and other adhesion systems in endometrial epithelial cells (EECs) [12, 13], attachment-associated transcriptional rewiring in an endometrial epithelial cell co-culture model [14], and EV cargo-mediated effects on uterine immune regulation [15, 16]. Moreover, attachment is coupled to transcriptional rewiring linked to epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in non-invasive bovine trophoblasts. In vivo, ovo-like zinc finger 2 (OVOL2) is down-regulated after attachment, and in vitro this down-regulation occurs specifically upon EEC attachment via TEA domain transcription factor 3 (TEAD3) and yes-associated protein (YAP) signaling [17].

Here, we interpret the IFNT-null claim in light of the established literature on peri-implantation biology in cattle and discuss unproven points that prevent immediate over-generalization. We then present a hypothesis-oriented map in which EV cargos and IFN-responsive BERV LTR enhancer sequences modulate the effective requirement for IFNT, thereby providing experimentally testable predictions for functional and genomic analyses [9, 18, 19]. In doing so, we advance a physiology-first framework with three components: (i) boundary conditions defined by paired, time-resolved 13,14-dihydro-15-keto-prostaglandin F2α (PGFM) pulsatility and P4 trajectories together with compartment-specific estrogen receptor 1 (ESR1) and oxytocin receptor (OXTR) status; (ii) an endogenous retrovirus (ERV)/long terminal repeat (LTR)-based gain-control hypothesis in which IFN-responsive LTR enhancers modulate locus-level activation as read out by accessibility, H3K27ac/enhancer RNA (eRNA), and signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT)/interferon regulatory factor (IRF) occupancy; and (iii) a minimal experimental roadmap to discriminate true IFNT bypass from context-dependent rescue.

The IFNT-null pregnancy establishment claim and a conservative interpretation

Wolf et al. report an experimental test of IFNT necessity using somatic cell nuclear transfer (SCNT) embryos engineered to lack functional embryonic IFNT, followed by multi-embryo transfer and assessment of conceptus development and maternal endometrial responses during the peri-implantation window [6]. The central observation is that, despite pronounced attenuation of canonical uterine IFN responses and ISG readouts near day 18, conceptus elongation and early pregnancy progression can still occur in a subset of recipients.

Importantly, these candidate buffering routes are unlikely to scale with conceptus number in a strictly linear manner. Rather, EV-mediated signaling, prostaglandin accumulation, and related cytokine or growth-factor inputs are more likely to behave as thresholded and potentially saturable processes, because maternal recognition ultimately depends on whether uterine luteolytic drive and CL responsiveness are shifted beyond a physiological decision threshold. Under this view, multi-embryo transfer may upregulate the total intrauterine load of non-IFNT signals above a rescue threshold that a single IFNT-null conceptus often cannot go that far. This model also predicts the diminishing response once uptake, transport, signaling capacity, or recipient-side CL gating becomes limiting. Accordingly, single-embryo transfer remains the critical design for testing whether non-IFNT routes are sufficient per conceptus or only effective under high conceptus-load conditions.

This result is provocative because it challenges a widely accepted working fundamental premise, namely that the IFNT-driven uterine IFN response and ISG program is required to attenuate the luteolytic signal and stabilize the uterine state during the recognition window [1, 2]. However, the strength of inference depends on how “pregnancy establishment” is defined and on whether core MRP physiology is shown as a time course in the same recipients. Since luteolysis is regulated by pulsatile uterine PGF2α output together with dynamic changes in luteal P4 secretion, a single time-point assessment cannot determine whether the luteolytic axis was truly suppressed, merely delayed, or simply not captured at the sampling time. Multi-embryo transfer can increase the total luminal load of conceptus-derived factors, including EVs and prostaglandins, and may allow dose-driven rescue that would be unlikely in a typical single-conceptus pregnancy. In addition, SCNT conceptuses can show epigenetic and placental deviations that alter the quantity and composition of trophoblast outputs, so an IFNT-null SCNT context may not be equivalent to an otherwise typical embryo lacking only IFNT [20,21,22,23,24]. Most importantly, endpoint transcript or protein measurements at day 18 of pregnancy cannot substitute for high-frequency assessment of PGFM pulsatility and P4 trajectories that report CL fate [25]. Compartment-specific ESR1 and OXTR status in the endometrium is also a direct readout of luteolytic competence and should be integrated into any strong claim of bypass.

Taken together, the most conservative conclusion is that the evidence by Wolf et al. (2026) reveals context-dependent buffering capacity rather than general dispensability of IFNT [6]. This framing motivates a mechanistic question: which alternative channels can quantitatively buffer the luteolytic axis and uterine permissiveness when IFNT input is weakened, and under what endocrine and conceptus-load conditions do these channels cross the threshold for MRP-compatible luteal maintenance?

Mechanistic buffering routes when IFNT input is weakened

Because the phrase “pregnancy establishment” can be interpreted broadly, we suggest a stricter operational definition for an IFNT bypass. A convincing bypass would require evidence that (i) luteolytic PGF2α pulsatility is functionally suppressed rather than merely altered, (ii) the CL avoids regression with sustained P4 output through the recognition window, and (iii) the uterus enters a receptive, attachment-supportive uterine state with appropriate epithelial and immune set-points. Importantly, these criteria are physiological and time-resolved; they cannot be inferred solely from conceptus presence at a single time point or from bulk transcriptomic snapshots.

If IFNT ablation is compatible with early pregnancy progression in some contexts, compensation must occur at the level of luteolytic control, luteal maintenance, and uterine permissiveness. Rather than invoking a single replacement ligand, we consider several buffering routes. To organize these candidate buffering routes and their physiological readouts, we present a hypothesis map summarizing peri-implantation pregnancy establishment in the absence of conceptus IFNT (Fig. 1).

Two-layer hypothesis map for peri-implantation pregnancy establishment in the absence of conceptus IFNT in cattle. (A) Physiological buffering routes. In the absence or marked reduction of conceptus-derived interferon tau (IFNT), the IFN signaling axis, canonical conceptus IFNT-endometrial type I IFN receptor-endometrial/immune cell interferon-stimulated gene (ISG), is attenuated. Under this condition, several parallel buffering routes may help support peri-implantation pregnancy establishment. These include 1) extracellular vesicle (EV)-mediated signaling from the conceptus to endometrial epithelial and immune cells, 2) prostaglandin network rerouting away from luteolytic prostaglandin F2α (PGF2α) toward more luteoprotective outputs such as prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), 3) local immune/cytokine contexts that promote a more receptive uterine milieu, 4) recipient-side corpus luteum (CL) gating of luteolytic sensitivity, and 5) steroid/growth factor coupling that supports epithelial remodeling and attachment readiness. One or more of these events integration is responsible for reduction in uterine luteolytic drive, sustained CL’s progesterone output, and generation of an attachment-permissive endometrium even when IFNT input is weakened. (B) Regulatory/genomic layer. In parallel with these physiological routes, IFN-responsive bovine endogenous retrovirus (BERV) long terminal repeat (LTR) sequences may modulate the effective requirement for IFNT at the genomic level. Candidate LTR enhancers containing interferon-stimulated response element (ISRE), gamma-activated sequence (GAS), and ETS-family motifs are proposed to act in a cell type-, chromatin-, and timing-dependent manner. In this framework, LTR-associated regulatory elements may form three functional architectures: dependency-lowering enhancers that preserve local transcriptional responses despite weak IFNT input, dependency-raising enhancers that amplify reliance on the canonical IFNT signal, and silent/context-specific enhancers that remain motif-positive but functionally unengaged. Their activity is predicted to depend on chromatin accessibility, cytokine milieu, conceptus-derived signals, and developmental timing during the peri-implantation window. Together, the physiological and regulatory layers provide a testable model in which the apparent requirement for IFNT varies with conceptus load, uterine context, and enhancer engagement.

EV-mediated signaling

EVs provide multiplex conceptus-to-maternal communication that can reshape endometrial epithelial and immune states without requiring high concentrations of a single soluble ligand [8]. In cattle, intrauterine exosomes have been argued to be required for conceptus attachment, and conceptus-derived exosomes can induce ISGs during the attachment period, likely via non-IFNT cargo that converges on STAT/IRF signaling modules [26, 27]. Together, these findings support the functional relevance of EV cargo at the uterine interface. A key test is whether EV quantity and cargo profiles covary with reduced PGFM pulsatility and sustained P4 in IFNT-null contexts, and whether perturbation of EV uptake or cargo function collapses this buffering in tractable EEC systems and, where feasible in vivo [15, 16].

Prostaglandin network rerouting

Prostaglandin biology offers a natural buffering route because MRP ultimately hinges on effective control of luteolytic drive and CL fate rather than ISG induction itself [11, 25]. Conceptus-derived prostaglandins regulate endometrial gene expression prior to classic pregnancy-recognition readouts [10], and IFNT can influence prostaglandin biosynthesis, transport, and signaling, including polycrine actions of PGE2 at the time of maternal recognition [28]. In IFNT-null cattle, endometrial OXTR is not detected while cyclooxygenase 2 (COX2) expression is unchanged [6]; however, this does not establish increased PGE2 production, which should be tested by direct, compartment-resolved quantification of prostaglandin levels and relevant synthesis/transport/receptor components. More importantly, the key discriminator is physiologically measured as a time course in the same recipients. Specifically, if luteolysis is truly buffered in IFNT-null pregnancies, recipients should show reduced luteolytic PGF2α pulse activity captured as reduced PGFM pulse frequency and/or amplitude by high-frequency serial sampling across the expected luteolytic window [25, 29]. In the same recipients, P4 trajectories should remain sustained, ideally under single-embryo transfer conditions [25, 29]. In addition, prostaglandin rerouting should be evaluated with spatial resolution, because prostaglandin synthesis and signaling can differ between endometrial domains. In this context, a compartment-aware shift means that the uterine compartments most relevant to luteolytic control show a relative increase in PGE2-linked outputs and downstream transcriptional programs during the same time window [29].

Immune and cytokine context

Because IFNT signals through type I IFN pathways, one buffering scenario is that maternal immune or endometrial compartments provide alternative cytokine inputs that converge on overlapping downstream machinery. This possibility is conceptually possible because IFNT is specialized in expression logic yet uses conserved signaling components [2]. However, proteomic profiling of day-18 bulk endometrium in the IFNT-null setting reports coordinated downregulation of interferon-stimulated proteins and suppression of IFN-related pathways, suggesting that sustained, bulk “type I IFN substitution” is unlikely at least at this stage [6]. Thus, uterine IFN responsiveness appears largely extinguished, yet peri-implantation pregnancy is still established in at least a subset of recipients [6]. In this context, immune effects may be most possible as compartmental and/or transient inputs that alter local set-points rather than as a direct replacement of IFNT across the whole endometrium. Supporting the idea that immune context can modulate very early pregnancy progression, intrauterine administration of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) prior to embryo transfers enhanced early development and conceptus elongation [30, 31].

CL gating of luteolytic sensitivity

Even with similar uterine luteolytic drive, the CL can differ in sensitivity to PGF2α depending on endocrine and immune state, effectively shifting the threshold at which luteolysis proceeds. This recipient-side gating can extend the window in which weaker uterine anti-luteolytic signals are sufficient to maintain P4 [25]. A key test is to quantify luteal sensitivity with standardized challenges and integrate these measures with PGFM pulse metrics; a gating model predicts that sustained P4 can occur despite partial luteolytic signaling when luteal sensitivity is reduced. In this framework, PGE-family prostaglandins are possible local luteoprotective factors that may reduce effective luteal exposure and/or responsiveness to PGF2α, thereby shifting the threshold for luteolysis [25].

Steroid and growth factor coupling enabling attachment readiness

P4 priming and growth-factor signaling can activate attachment-related gene programs that are not necessarily dependent on soluble IFNT. In bovine endometrial epithelial cells, L-selectin (SELL/CD62L) is induced by P4 with epidermal growth factor (EGF) and/or basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), but not by IFNT [12, 13]. In addition, adhesion systems such as vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 (VCAM1/CD106)-integrin subunit alpha 4 (ITGA4) contribute to conceptus adhesion, supporting an IFNT-independent route into elements of the attachment module [12, 13]. A key test is whether P4 plus growth-factor signaling preserves attachment-associated transcriptional outputs in co-culture models under IFNT-low conditions while luteolytic control is explained by prostaglandin rerouting and/or CL gating. This module should be interpreted as buffering uterine permissiveness rather than substituting for luteal maintenance [14, 25].

Where BERV/LTR biology can change the requirement for IFNT

An evolutionary constraint matters when interpreting IFNT-null pregnancy claims. IFNT is a relatively late innovation derived from an IFNW ancestor restricted to pecoran ruminants, with an inferred origin around 36 million years ago [32, 33]. Because ruminants as a clade predate this event [34], early ruminant lineages must have maintained luteal function and uterine receptivity via IFNT-independent mechanisms. Extant tragulids, such as the lesser mouse-deer (Tragulus kanchil) and the water chevrotain (Hyemoschus aquaticus), provide phylogenetic reference points for ruminant lineages that diverged before the pecoran IFNT innovation, although their present-day pregnancy-recognition systems should not be equated directly with ancestral states [33]. Thus, IFNT can be viewed as an optimization that sharpens the timing and magnitude of uterine reprogramming, while permitting IFNT-independent buffering routes under supportive conditions, making IFNT a dominant but not logically indispensable component.

A key question is why IFN-responsive transcription can appear highly IFNT-dependent yet be partially buffered in some contexts. We propose that the genome contains a layered IFN-responsive enhancer substrate, shaped in part by BERV LTRs, that can amplify IFNT inputs or, in some settings, be engaged by alternative cytokines. Consistent with this, our age-stratified BERV/LTR motif analysis shows interferon-stimulated response elements (ISREs) and gamma-activated sequences (GAS) motif densities concentrating in intermediate-age layers, with ETS-family enrichment extending into intermediate-to-older strata [35]. These motifs are cis-regulatory docking sites for STAT/IRF modules rather than IFNT-specific receptors, implying that distinct upstream ligands converging on interferon signaling could, in principle, engage the same LTRs [18, 19, 36]. This supports a gain-control model in which IFN-responsive LTRs convert cytokine inputs into locus-level enhancer activation in a cell-type and chromatin-dependent manner.

Critically, motif density is only a hypothesis generator; the discriminator is mechanistic engagement at loci. Enhancer activation should be evaluated using cell-type-resolved chromatin accessibility, local H3K27ac and eRNA, and STAT and/or IRF occupancy at candidate LTR loci prioritized by ISRE/GAS/ETS motifs and proximity to pregnancy-relevant genes across the peri-implantation timeline [18, 33]. These locus-level assays should ideally be paired within the same recipients with physiological readouts such as PGFM pulsatility and P4 trajectories [9, 25]. Within this framework, three directional architectures generate testable predictions. In a dependency-lowering architecture, alternative cytokines (e.g., IFNG, as an illustrative example) engage LTR enhancers when IFNT is weak, preserving local H3K27ac/STAT/IRF occupancy alongside reduced PGFM pulsatility and sustained P4. In a dependency-raising architecture, LTR gain control preferentially amplifies the temporally precise IFNT cue, so IFNT loss yields matching loss of H3K27ac/STAT/IRF occupancy and failure to sustain P4 unless other channels (e.g., high EV load or prostaglandin rerouting) provide rescue. In a silent enhancer architecture, motifs remain unengaged due to weak/compartmentalized cytokine inputs and/or chromatin remains inaccessible. These produce minimal H3K27ac/STAT/IRF occupancy even if pregnancy is transiently supported by non-IFN channels, consistent with reduced uterine IFN/ISG proteins reported in IFNT-null endometrium [6]. These architectures may differ across compartments and time, but they shift the interpretation from bulk ISG expression to falsifiable, paired predictions anchored to PGFM, P4, and cell-type-resolved, locus-resolved enhancer engagement.

Future directions and analytical criteria to discriminate true bypass from context-dependent rescue

A key next step is to move the present framework from a conceptual hypothesis toward an analytically testable physiological model. At minimum, this requires paired in vivo measurements collected within the same recipients across the maternal recognition window (days 15–19), including high-frequency PGFM pulse profiling, matched P4 trajectories, and compartment-specific ESR1/OXTR status in the endometrium. These physiological readouts should then be integrated with mechanistic datasets that test candidate buffering routes directly, including uterine-fluid EV quantity and cargo, compartment-resolved prostaglandin outputs and receptor/transporter profiles. Furthermore, cell-type-resolved chromatin and enhancer engagement at candidate IFN-responsive LTR loci would be another targets to be tested as the buffering routs. Together, these data would allow one to distinguish true suppression of the luteolytic axis from delayed, partial, or dose-dependent rescue and thereby define the conditions under which apparent IFNT-independent pregnancy progression is physiologically possible.

To discriminate true bypass from context-dependent rescue, the minimum informative design is to analyze IFNT-null conceptuses under single-embryo transfer conditions [25]. Within the same recipients, this design should align high-frequency PGFM pulse profiling with P4 trajectories across the recognition window and compartment-specific ESR1 and OXTR status in the endometrium [25]. In parallel, uterine fluid EV quantity and cargo should be profiled and, where feasible, perturbed in mechanistic co-culture systems to test EV causality [14, 26]. Finally, the BERV/LTR gain-control model should be tested with cell-type resolved accessibility, local H3K27ac and eRNA, and STAT and/or IRF occupancy at candidate LTR loci across the peri-implantation timeline, determining whether enhancer engagement persists, collapses, or remains silent in IFNT-null contexts [18, 19]. The same platform can incorporate OVOL2/EMT-axis outputs (OVOL2 down-regulation; zinc finger E-box binding homeobox 1 (ZEB1)/snail family transcriptional repressor 2 (SNAI2); cadherin 2 (CDH2)/vimentin (VIM); cadherin 1 (CDH1)) as mechanistic endpoints to test whether non-IFNT cytokine inputs or EV cargos can reproduce attachment-coupled transcriptional rewiring under IFNT-null conditions [17].

Conclusion

The IFNT paradigm remains strongly supported in cattle, particularly the trophoblast-specific control of IFNT transcription and the characteristic endometrial ISG response during early pregnancy [1, 2, 7]. At the same time, the recent IFNT loss-of-function report in SCNT and embryo-transfer settings indicates that conceptus survival and early pregnancy progression can occur in at least some recipients even when canonical uterine IFN and ISG signatures are markedly reduced [6].

The key untested physiological issues should be evaluated before pregnancy without IFNT becoming a new way of thinking. A convincing claim of IFNT bypass requires time-resolved evidence that luteolysis is prevented, not inferred, including PGFM pulse profiles and P4 trajectories in the same recipients, preferably under single-embryo transfer conditions [25]. Mechanistically, putative compensation should be tested by measuring EV quantity and cargo, prostaglandin pathway outputs in relevant endometrial compartments, and locus-level activation of IFN-responsive LTR enhancers using chromatin accessibility, H3K27ac and eRNA, and STAT and/or IRF occupancy [18, 26, 29]. Together, these measurements would clarify when IFNT is required, when it is buffered, and which compensatory routes are responsible for the MRP to proceed in ruminants.

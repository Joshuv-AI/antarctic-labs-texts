Prediction Model for Indicator Gas Concentration of Coal Spontaneous Combustion in Goaf Areas based on CS-RF
Authors: Kuan Wu, Shiliang Shi, Chengxuan Li, Haibo Wu, Bo You, Bo Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate and rapid prediction of the concentration of coal spontaneous combustion indicator gases is critical for the prevention and control of coal spontaneous combustion in mines. Addressing the common issues with existing prediction models for coal spontaneous combustion indicator gas concentrations in goaf areassuch as low accuracy, slow convergence, and poor generalization abilitythis study analyzes the principles and processes of various algorithms. It proposes a hybrid prediction model that optimizes the Random Forest algorithm using the Cuckoo Search algorithm. This approach aims to overcome the randomness and limitations associated with manual parameter tuning in the Random Forest algorithm. Using data from the goaf of the 4427 working face at the Tiejishan Coal Mine as a case study, and with carbon monoxide concentration as the prediction target, the model’s applicability, superiority, and reliability were validated. The results showed that through adaptive global optimization, the CS-RF model significantly improves both prediction accuracy and efficiency. Compared to the RF model, the root-mean-square error (RMSE) and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) were reduced by 11.85% and 23.83%, respectively. Training and prediction times were reduced by 74.83% and 31.43%, respectively, and the coefficient of determination (R2) for the linear fit increased by 11.97%; the error range relative to actual on-site monitoring values was 0.53%–6.91%. This study provides a new technical approach for the precise, rapid, and intelligent early warning of coal spontaneous combustion in coal mines.

Introduction

Coal is China’s primary energy source, accounting for more than 50% of the country’s total primary energy consumption. Coal spontaneous combustion is a major hazard in coal mines, particularly in goaf areas; more than 50% of mines in China face the risk of spontaneous combustion fires. As mining depths increase, rising ground temperatures lead to more frequent spontaneous combustion in coal seams, which can easily trigger accidents such as fires and gas explosions, posing a serious threat to production safety. , Spontaneous combustion in goaf areas is influenced by the coupled effects of multiple factors and fields. It is characterized by high concealment, complex influencing factors, and nonlinear data patterns. Accurate prediction of indicator gas concentrations is key to achieving early warning and efficient prevention and control. −

Low-temperature oxidation of coal releases characteristic gases such as CO, C2H4, and C2H6, whose composition and concentration vary systematically with temperature; these serve as the primary basis for determining the stage of spontaneous combustion and the risk level. Compared to coal temperature, these indicator gases offer advantages such as remote detectability, rapid response, and distinct stage-specific characteristics, making them more suitable for early warning; however, there remains a lack of refined predictive models for their dynamic evolution patterns. Methods for predicting coal spontaneous combustion have gradually evolved from traditional approaches to machine learning. In 2011, Xiao et al. were the first to construct a backpropagation (BP) neural network model based on the nonlinear relationship between indicator gases and coal temperature, thereby achieving the prediction of coal spontaneous combustion status. Since then, traditional machine learning methods such as artificial neural networks, support vector machines, and time series analysis have been widely applied in coal temperature prediction research. − However, such single-model approaches struggle to adapt to the complex data characteristics of goaf environmentswhich involve multifield coupling, nonlinearity, and high noiseand generally suffer from issues such as poor stability and insufficient prediction accuracy. Random Forests (RF) has demonstrated excellent performance in coal temperature prediction due to their strong fitting capabilities, resistance to overfitting, and suitability for high-dimensional data. In 2021, Zheng et al. constructed an RF coal temperature prediction model by integrating temperature-programmed experiments with field monitoring data. Through a comparison of multiple models, they confirmed the superiority of the RF algorithm in terms of prediction accuracy. Subsequently, Lei et al. developed an RF prediction model for coal spontaneous combustion temperature based on multi-indicator gas analysis. The model demonstrated good stability and practicality in both step-heating tests and large-scale coal oxidation experiments, further confirming that RF models offer high predictive accuracy and broad applicability in predicting coal spontaneous combustion temperature. Wu et al. proposed a model for predicting coal spontaneous combustion temperatures that integrates the Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA) with Random Forests (RF). This model demonstrated high predictive accuracy and good engineering applicability in both coal temperature control experiments and field data validation using borehole tubing monitoring, confirming the reliability of the RF model in handling varying moisture content and screening key indicators. Jiang et al. developed RF, SVM, and PSO-SVM prediction models to address the nonlinear relationship between coal self-ignition gases and temperature. The RF model they developed demonstrated good stability and generalization ability in both coal oxidation heating experiments and field validation through in situ monitoring at coal mines. However, the performance of RF models is highly dependent on hyperparameters such as the number of decision trees (Ntree) and the number of features per node (Mtree): Traditional manual tuning combined with grid search methods is inefficient and prone to getting stuck in local optima; optimization algorithms such as PSO and SSA also suffer from insufficient global search capabilities and limited convergence speeds, resulting in limited improvements in prediction accuracy. Furthermore, most existing studies focus on coal temperature prediction, with very few investigations into refined direct modeling of indicator gas concentrations.

Building a model to directly predict the concentration of a target gas offers the following advantages over estimating the concentration of the target gas by reverse-calculating it from the temperature:① superior prediction accuracy and real-time performance. The temperature-back-calculation method requires the establishment of a temperature-gas concentration coupling model, involving a multistage process of “temperature field reconstruction → gas generation kinetics → concentration field extrapolation,” which is subject to significant error accumulation and amplification effects. In contrast, direct prediction models use monitoring parameters readily available in abandoned mining areas (such as O2 concentration, air leakage intensity, and porosity) as inputs to establish a direct mapping relationship between multiple parameters and the concentration of indicator gases. This effectively avoids errors associated with temperature field reconstruction in intermediate steps and offers theoretical advantages in terms of prediction accuracy and response speed. ② Greater engineering applicability. Temperature monitoring in deep goaf areas faces engineering bottlenecks such as limitations on sensor placement, susceptibility of thermocouples to mechanical damage, and significant blind spots in temperature measurement. In contrast, gas concentration monitoring technologies (such as bundle tube monitoring systems, distributed fiber-optic sensing, and real-time return air corner monitoring devices) are already relatively mature, offering more convenient and continuous data acquisition. Direct prediction models can significantly reduce reliance on temperature monitoring hardware and are better suited to the actual conditions of complex on-site operating environments. ③ The physical mechanisms are modeled more accurately. Coal spontaneous combustion is a complex nonlinear process involving “low-temperature oxidationself-heatingrelease of gaseous products”; there is no simple correlation between temperature and the concentration of indicator gases, and its evolution is influenced by the coupled effects of multiple factors, including O2 concentration, air leakage velocity, and porosity. Provided that the training data adequately characterizes the aforementioned multifactor coupling characteristics, a direct prediction model can use machine learning to capture the nonlinear mapping relationship between multiple parameters and gas concentrations, thereby more closely reflecting the actual evolution patterns of coal spontaneous combustion in abandoned mine areas. ④ Improved Timeliness of Early Warning. In actual monitoring of goaf areas, because the rate of gas diffusion in loose coal typically exceeds the rate of heat conduction, anomalies in the concentration of indicator gases may be detected spatially before temperature anomalies, thereby providing a potential time window for spontaneous combustion early warning. Directly predicting the concentration of indicator gases helps capture this early signal, which is expected to improve the timeliness of spontaneous combustion early warning and secure valuable time for fire prevention and suppression decisions.

To this end, this paper proposes an indicator gas concentration prediction model that optimizes the Random Forest (RF) algorithm using the Cuckoo Search (CS) algorithm. The CS algorithm features few parameters, strong global optimization capabilities, and fast convergence. By utilizing the Lévy flight method, it can efficiently process high-dimensional, nonlinear, and complex data, and has been successfully applied in mining-related fields; − however, it has not yet been used for predicting coal spontaneous combustion gas concentrations in goaf areas. This study constructs a CS-RF model based on an analysis of the algorithm’s principles, implements adaptive hyperparameter optimization, and directly predicts indicator gas concentrations in goaf areas. The model’s superiority is compared with a baseline RF model, and its reliability is verified through an error analysis against actual on-site monitoring data.

Algorithm Principles and Process

Random Forest Algorithm

Random Forests (RF) is a machine learning method that combines ensemble learning with decision trees. It generates a final output by constructing multiple decision trees and averaging their predictions. This method reduces the risk of overfitting in individual trees through “collective decision-making,” thereby enhancing the model’s generalization ability and stability. By leveraging the dual randomness of data sampling and feature selection, each tree learns independently from different dimensions, ultimately resulting in a more robust and accurate regression model. The process consists of four steps.(1)Data sampling: bootstrap sampling with replacement is used to generate multiple training subsets from the original data set, which are used to construct each tree.(2)Building decision trees: a small number of features are randomly selected at each node. Optimal splitting features and nodes are chosen based on the criterion of minimizing the mean squared error, and the tree is recursively split until the termination condition is met. Cuckoo Search Algorithm

Cuckoo Search (CS) is a meta-heuristic optimization algorithm inspired by cuckoo nesting behavior, whose core follows three rules: each bird lays one egg at a time and parasitizes a nest at random; the optimal solution is retained; and the host discovers the egg with probability Pa and either discards it or rebuilds the nest. In this algorithm, nests correspond to candidate solutions, eggs correspond to new solutions, and the quality of the egg corresponds to the fitness of the solution. It achieves efficient global search through Lévy flight, combined with a discovery mechanism to perform local optimization and population renewal. With few parameters (only population size N and discovery probability Pa) and easy implementation, it performs exceptionally well in complex optimization problems. , The process is illustrated in Figure .

Model Building

The core of the CS-RF model for predicting the concentration of spontaneous combustion indicator gases in goafs lies in using CS to optimize the hyperparameters of RF. The specific steps are as follows.(1)Data preparation: collect data relevant to the prediction site and partition the existing data into training and testing sets, typically with a ratio of 8:2.(2)Problem definition: map the RF hyperparameter combinations (Mtree, Ntree) to the position vectors of nests in the CS algorithm.(3)Parameter initialization: set algorithm parameters, including the number of nests K, the probability of being discovered by the nest owner Pa, and the number of iterations N for the CS algorithm, as well as the Mtree and Ntree ranges for the RF. Randomly initialize nest positions within the predefined hyperparameter search space.(4)Fitness evaluation: for each nest (i.e., a set of hyperparameter combinations), use the root-mean-square error (RMSE) to calculate and evaluate the fitness value.(5)Position update and selection: generate a new generation of nest positions using Lévy flight and calculate their fitness values. Compare the new generation of nests with the previous generation, retaining those with better fitness (lower RMSE and MAPE). Discard some of the poorer nests based on the discovery probability Pa, while simultaneously generating the same number of new nests.(6)Termination criteria: steps 3 and 4 are repeated until the specified number of iterations is reached and the error meets the minimum requirement, at which point the globally optimal hyperparameter combination is output.(7)Model prediction: the complete RF model is retrained using the optimal hyperparameters to form the final CS-RF prediction model, which is then used to make predictions on the test set. Figures and show that during the oxidation process of the coal sample from the 4427 longwall face in the temperature range of 30 to 200 °C, CO, CO2, CH4, C2H6, C3H8, and C2H4 gases appear in a regular pattern, with the amount of gas produced increasing exponentially as the temperature rises. During the initial stage (at lower temperatures), CO production is minimal; however, after the temperature rises to 80 °C, CO production increases rapidly, indicating that the rate of coal oxidation accelerates at this temperature. The selection of indicator gases follows the following six principles: ① sensitivity (stable trend as coal temperature changes); ② regularity (a good correlation between changes in its generation rate and coal temperature); ③ measurability (existing detection instruments can detect it and meet detection requirements); ④ uniqueness (gases that appear only as a result of spontaneous combustion); ⑤ monotonic variation (concentrations rise or fall monotonically with temperature, exhibiting strong regularity); ⑥ early appearance (appearing in the early stages of coal self-heating to facilitate early warning). As shown in Table , CH4 and CO2 (at high temperatures, CO and O2 can react to form CO2) lack uniqueness. Meanwhile, gases such as C2H2, C2H4, C2H6, and C3H8 lack early detection capability. In summary, only CO meets all six of the above principles; therefore, CO is selected as the indicator gas for spontaneous combustion in Coal Seam 2 at the Tiejishan Coal Mine.

Data Sources and Preprocessing

The research data were collected from the 4427 working face at the Tiejishan Coal Mine (hereinafter referred to as “Tiejishan coal mine”) operated by Hunan Coal Industry Niuma Si Mining Co., Ltd. The 4427 working face measures 60 m in length, 2.1 m in width, and 2 m in height; The intake and return airways are 2.4 m wide and 2.2 m high, with U-shaped ventilation. The roof is managed using the full caving method, as shown in Figure . According to the findings in ref, the primary factors influencing the concentration of the indicator gas for coal spontaneous combustion in the goaf of the 4427 longwall face at the Tiejishan Coal Mine are goaf porosity, oxygen concentration, methane concentration, temperature, and air leakage velocity; the indicator gas is CO. The characteristic data of the above five major influencing factors were selected as inputs, with the CO concentration at the return air corner of the 4427 working face serving as the output (the model’s prediction target). Five values were selected for each factor, as shown in Table . By changing one factor at a time, 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 = 3125 different operating conditions were constructed. From these, 2500 sets were randomly selected as the training set and 625 sets as the test set. This yielded 2500 sets of predicted CO concentrations at the upper corner of the return airway for both the CS-RF and RF models in the training set, and 625 sets each for the test set.

Model Parameter Settings

CS Algorithm Parameter Settings: In the CS-RF algorithm, the configuration of CS parameters has a decisive impact on algorithm performance. Among these, the host discovery probability (Pa) and the number of nests (N) are key factors affecting the convergence rate. To analyze the impact of these two factors on the search process, this paper fixes the values of Pa and N separately and analyzes and compares the convergence curves under different parameter combinations.

Figure shows the fitness convergence curves for different values of Pa when the number of nests is N = 30. It can be observed that within the range of Pa from 0.15 to 0.45, the algorithm’s convergence process is relatively stable and yields good convergence results, though the convergence rates vary. When Pa = 0.25, the algorithm requires the fewest iteration steps to converge. Therefore, Pa is set to 0.3, and N is selected within the range of 30 to 100. Figure shows that the convergence rate is relatively good when N = 50. After careful consideration, this paper selected Pa = 0.3 and further investigates the convergence behavior of the number of nests N within the range of 30 to 100. The experimental results indicate that when N = 50, the algorithm exhibits a relatively better convergence rate. In summary, the final parameter values are determined as Pa = 0.3 and N = 50.

Discussion

(1)CS effectively optimizes RF hyperparameters for predicting the concentration of indicator gases of coal spontaneous combustion in goaf areas; the optimized RF model demonstrates significant improvements in both prediction accuracy and speed. The primary reasons are as follows: ① advantages of ensemble learning: Bagging and random feature selection effectively reduce model variance; the model exhibits strong robustness against noise and outliers, making it particularly suitable for small and medium-sized data sets; as an ensemble model, it is less susceptible to the effects of initialization or local minima. ② Hyperparameter robustness: Random Forest is relatively insensitive to hyperparameter settings and can maintain good performance even when using default parameters. Conclusion

(1)A model for predicting the concentration of indicator gases of coal spontaneous combustion in goaf areas using CS-RF has been successfully developed. This model effectively overcomes the difficulty of parameter optimization inherent in traditional machine learning models by achieving adaptive and precise optimization of RF hyperparameters. Compared to RF models, the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) were reduced by 11.85% and 23.83%, respectively, while training and prediction times were shortened by 74.83% and 31.43%, respectively, thereby enhancing the intelligence level of the prediction model.(2)The adaptability and reliability of the CS-RF model for predicting the concentration of coal spontaneous combustion indicator gases in goaf areas have been verified. The relative error compared to actual field monitoring values ranges from 0.53% to 6.91%. The model features high prediction accuracy, fast training speed, and good stability, providing a reliable technical approach for the precise and rapid prediction of coal spontaneous combustion indicator gas concentrations in goaf areas.

PSMC2 drives tumor progression in nasopharyngeal carcinoma by inhibiting ferroptosis through STAT3
Authors: Jin Su, Shiping Ding, Shousen Hu, Jing Yu, Lingling Zheng, Xinjie Wang, Kun Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) mortality is driven primarily by malignant proliferation and metastasis. Although PSMC2 contributes to tumor progression, its mechanistic role in NPC pathogenesis remains undefined.MethodsProtein expression levels were detected via WB and immunohistochemistry. Cell activity was measured by a CCK8 assay. The cell migration ability was detected via wound-healing assays. Cell death was detected by flow cytometry. The levels of ROS, lipid peroxidation and iron ions were observed via confocal microscopy. The level of GSH was detected with a microplate reader. The growth abilities of the tumors were analyzed with a subcutaneous tumor model.ResultsPSMC2 is highly expressed in NPC. Compared with those in the PSMC2-knockdown group, the cell proliferation activity and migration ability were increased, the phosphorylation level of STAT3 was increased, the protein expression of CPT1 and GPX4 was increased, the ROS and lipid peroxidation levels were decreased, the GSH levels were significantly increased, the iron ion levels were significantly decreased, and the cell activity was increased. Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a malignant tumor originating from the top and lateral walls of the nasopharyngeal cavity. The incidence of NPC is the highest among head and neck squamous cell carcinomas (HNSCCs) [1]. According to statistics, there are approximately 129,000 new cases worldwide annually, of which 47% are in China [2]. The age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) of NPC has reached 3 per 100,000 people in China, which has become a high-incidence region.

Notably, NPC is an ecological disease: a multidimensional spatiotemporal “unity of ecology and evolution” pathological ecosystem [3]. NPC is characterized by a high propensity for distant metastasis, which is correlated with an extremely poor prognosis. The mortality rate reaches 91% within one year following initial metastasis. At present, the main treatment methods for nasopharyngeal carcinoma are radiotherapy or chemotherapy [4]. However, even with optimal therapeutic approaches, approximately 30% of patients experience recurrence due to metastasis [5]. Patients with metastatic NPC face particularly dismal prognoses, posing a severe threat to patients’ lives and health [6].

This work revealed that PSMC2 was highly expressed in NPC. Interestingly, the proliferation and metastasis capacity of nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells was significantly diminished following the knockdown of PSMC2. However, the regulatory mechanism of PSMC2 is still unclear. As a critical subunit of the 26 S proteasome complex, PSMC2 is an essential component of the multicatalytic proteinase complex. This highly conserved molecular machine consists of a 20 S catalytic core particle and 19 S regulatory particles [7]. Within the ubiquitin‒proteasome system (UPS), PSMC2 orchestrates ATP-dependent substrate recognition, deubiquitination, and translocation processes to specifically degrade ubiquitinated substrates, including misfolded proteins, damaged polypeptides, and regulatory proteins that perform their physiological functions. This precise protein quality control mechanism plays an indispensable role in maintaining cellular proteostasis [8]. Therefore, the proteasome is involved in many cellular processes, including cell division and proliferation, apoptosis, DNA damage repair, and metastasis [9, 10]. Inhibition of PSMC2 can reduce the proliferation of ovarian cancer cells and pancreatic cancer cells [11]. Wang demonstrated that PSMC2 upregulates the expression of plasminogen activator urokinase (PLAU) through transcriptional regulatory mechanisms in breast cancer, thereby accelerating tumor cell invasion, metastasis, and malignant progression [12]. Liu et al. reported significant upregulation of PSMC2 expression in gastric cancer. PSMC2 competitively combines with RPS15A for hsa-let-7c-3p, leading to abnormal RPS15A accumulation. This process activates the mTOR signaling pathway, ultimately driving gastric cancer cell proliferation [13]. Zhu et al. reported that PSMC2 may promote ovarian cancer development through CCND1 [14]. Therefore, PSMC2 has become a potential target for the treatment of multiple cancers.

The protein microarray results revealed that PSMC2 knockdown significantly attenuated STAT3 signaling activity. Functioning as the pivotal transcriptional regulator of carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (CPT1), STAT3 directly binds to the CPT1 promoter to modulate its expression. As the rate-limiting enzyme for mitochondrial fatty acid β-oxidation (FAO), CPT1 catalyzes the conjugation of long-chain fatty acids with carnitine to form acylcarnitine derivatives, with its enzymatic activity dictating the overall flux of FAO [15, 16]. Abnormal lipid metabolism is a significant hallmark of tumors and has emerged as a crucial strategy for tumor intervention [17]. Notably, glucose deprivation and hypoxic stress in the tumor microenvironment specifically induce CPT1 upregulation, representing a critical adaptive metabolic switch for cancer cell survival [18]. Aberrant CPT1 overexpression in cancer cells sustains energy homeostasis and drives malignant proliferation through enhanced FAO [19]. Therefore, inhibition of CPT1 significantly reduces the proliferation of tumor cells [20]. Unrestricted lipid peroxidation is characteristic of ferroptosis [21]. Sustained activation of fatty acid oxidation promotes lipid peroxide accumulation, which triggers iron-dependent ferroptosis [22], whereas targeted inhibition of key lipid oxidation components effectively mitigates oxidative stress-induced ferroptosis [23, 24].

Therefore, this project will further explore whether PSMC2 regulates CPT1-dependent lipid oxidation-induced iron death through STAT3. This finding provides a new mechanism of NPC proliferation and metastasis. Therefore, this work has important clinical research value and significance.

Methods and materials

Cell culture

C666-1 cells (provided and authenticated by the Chinese Academy of Sciences) were routinely maintained in complete growth medium (DMEM + 10% fetal bovine serum + 1% penicillin‒streptomycin) under standard culture conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2, humidified atmosphere). C666-1 cell line was free from mycoplasma contamination. When the cells reached 95% confluency with a typical logarithmic growth phase morphology, they were digested with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA solution and seeded at the optimal density in experimental culture plates. Upon reaching 90% confluency, The cells were treated with either lentiviral infection or the STAT3 agonist ML115 (HY-111152, MedChemExpress) at a concentration of 2 nM [25]. Following 48 h of intervention, the cell samples were harvested for analysis of relevant molecular indicators.

Cell activity analysis

C666-1 cells were treated for 24–48 h. The culture supernatant was aspirated, cell medium containing 10% CCK8 was added, and the culture was continued for 2 h. The light absorption value at 450 nm was detected with a microplate reader. The relative cell activities were analyzed.

Wound healing assay

C666-1 cells were seeded in 24-well culture plates at a density of 1 × 10⁵ cells/well. The mixture was incubated at 37 °C and 5% CO2 until the cells reached 90% confluency. A sterile 200 µL pipette tip was used to create a linear scratch across the cell monolayer. The wells were gently washed with prewarmed PBS (pH 7.4) to remove dislodged cells and cellular debris. The medium was replaced with fresh low-serum medium. The initial images were acquired via a microscope. The plates were returned to the incubator, and imaging was repeated at 48 h.

Apoptosis detection by flow cytometry

C666-1 cells were treated for 24–48 h. The culture supernatant was aspirated. The cells were gently moistened with PBS 3 times and digested with pancreatic enzymes for 1 min. The cells were collected by centrifugation, washed 3 times, incubated with Annexin V-FITC for 30 min, and then stained with PI for 5 min. Apoptotic cells were detected via flow cytometry. The data were analyzed with FlowJo software.

Human phospho-kinase array analysis

Protein microarray chips (Human Phospho-Kinase Array Kit, ARY003C, RnDSystems.) were equilibrated to room temperature (20–25 °C) for 30 min in a desiccator. The chips were blocked with 5% BSA for 1 h. The diluted cell lysate samples were incubated overnight at 4 °C on a rocking platform shaker. Chips were washed in 1X Wash Buffer 3 times. Pipette 1.0 mL of diluted detection antibody cocktail A into wells for Part A membranes. Pipette 1.0 mL of diluted detection antibody cocktail B into wells for Part B membranes. The membranes were transferred to appropriate wells. The mixture was incubated for 2 h at room temperature on a rocking platform. All the membranes were washed separately. Pipette 1.0 mL of diluted streptavidin-HRP into each well. The mixture was incubated for 30 min. All the membranes were washed with 1× Wash Buffer 3 times. The chips were treated with an enhanced chemiluminescent substrate for 5 min, and then images were captured via a CCD imager. Data analysis with ImageJ software.

Western blot analysis

C666-1 cells were treated for 48 h. The culture supernatant was aspirated, and the samples were gently washed with PBS 3 times. The cells were lysed with RIPA buffer. The cell lysate was collected and subjected to ultrasonication, and the protein was denatured by boiling. Proteins were separated via gel electrophoresis and subsequently transferred onto a PVDF membrane via a Mini Trans-Blot Cell (1703935, Bio-Rad). The PVDF membranes were incubated with 5% BSA for 2 h at 25 °C, incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C, and then incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:2,000) for 1 h at 25 °C. The images were taken with a ChemiDoc imaging system, and the data were analyzed with ImageJ software. The primary antibodies are as follows: anti-PSMC2 antibody (Proteintech, 14905-1-AP,1:1000), anti-STAT3 antibody (Proteintech, 10253-2-AP, 1:2000), anti-p-STAT3 (Ser727) antibody (Proteintech, 28945-1-AP, 1:1000), anti-GPX4 antibody (Proteintech, 30388-1-AP, 1:1000), anti-CPT1 antibody (Proteintech, 15184-1-AP, 1:5000), and anti-GAPDH antibody (Proteintech, 10494-1-AP, 1:5000).

ROS level detection assay

C666-1 cells were treated for 48 h. The culture supernatant was aspirated, and the cells were gently treated with 10 µM/L DCFH-DA. The cells were incubated in a cell incubator at 37 °C for 20 min. The mixture was inverted and mixed every 3–5 min so that the probe was in full contact with the cells. The samples were washed with PBS 3 times and observed directly with a confocal laser microscope.

Fe2+ level detection assay

C666-1 cells were treated for 48 h. The culture supernatant was aspirated, and according to the instructions of the fermentation kit, 1 ml of fluorescence probe working solution was added. The cells were incubated in a cell incubator at 37 °C for 30 min. The cells were washed with PBS 3 times, 2 ml of PBS was added, and images were taken with a confocal laser microscope.

Lipid peroxidation level assay

C666-1 cells were treated for 48 h. The culture supernatant was aspirated, and 1 ml of BODIPY 581/591 C11 fluorescent probes was added. The cells were incubated in a cell incubator at 37 °C for 30 min. The cells were washed with PBS 3 times, 2 ml of PBS was added, and images were taken with a confocal laser microscope.

Animal assay

Ten 6-week-old male BALB/c nude mice (SPF grade) were obtained from Jie Si Jie Biological Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). All animals were acclimatized under standardized conditions (22 ± 1 °C, 50–60% humidity, 12 h light/dark cycle) for 1 week. The mice were divided into two groups and subcutaneously injected in the right flank with 1*107 C666-1 cells infected with shCtrl or shPSMC2. The length and width of the tumors were measured periodically, and the volume of each tumor was calculated (tumor volume=LW2/2). Four weeks later, the mice were anesthetized with 0.1mL 10% Chloral hydrate solution, and sacrificed by dislocation. The tumor tissue was obtained, weighed, photographed, and finally fixed.

Immunohistochemical assay

Subcutaneous xenograft tumors were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 24 h, dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, and embedded in paraffin. Serial Sect. (5 μm thickness) were cut via a microtome, mounted on poly-L-lysine-coated slides, and baked at 60 °C for 1 h to enhance tissue adherence. The slides were blocked with 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 1 h at RT and incubated with species-specific primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. The slides were washed three times with TBST (5 min per wash). HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies were applied, and the samples were incubated for 2 h at RT under light-protected conditions. After being washed with TBST, the slides were subjected to DAB chromogenic development with hematoxylin counterstaining. Images were acquired via a microscope.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed with SPSS 20 software. The experimental data are presented as the means ± SEMs (standard error of the mean). Differences between groups were assessed by one-way ANOVA, the chi-square test, or Student’s t test. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

PSMC2 expression level and clinical correlation in NPC

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a type of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). We analyzed single-cell data (GSE173468, HNSCC-121-05-1A) and found that PSMC2 was expressed to varying degrees in different types of cells, especially with the highest expression level in tumor cells (Fig. 1A-B). The GEPIA2 database revealed that the PSMC2 gene is highly expressed in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (Fig. 1C). TCGA database analysis revealed that PSMC2 was significantly upregulated in both paired and paired nasopharyngeal carcinoma samples (Fig. 1D-E). The PSMC2 expression levels in the tissue samples were subsequently detected via immunohistochemistry, and the results indicated that PSMC2 was upregulated in NPC tissue (Fig. 1F). Survival analysis revealed that patients with high levels of PSMC2 had a poorer survival prognosis (Fig. 1G) (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/cgi-bin/TCGA-survival1.pl?genenam=PSMC2&ctype=HNSC).

Effects of PSMC2 on NPC cell activities

To further analyze the role of PSMC2 in NPC. A PSMC2 knockdown lentivirus was constructed. C666-1 cells were infected with PSMC2-knockdown lentivirus, and the expression level of the PSMC2 protein was detected via WB. The results revealed that the expression level of PSMC2 was effectively reduced (Fig. 2A). The cell proliferative capacity was quantitatively assessed via a CCK-8 assay, which revealed significant attenuation of C666-1 cell growth upon PSMC2 knockdown (Fig. 2B). The results of the wound healing assay revealed that the C666-1 cell wound closure rate was 52.64% lower than that of the controls (Fig. 3B). The flow cytometry results revealed that C666-1 apoptosis increased from 9.13% to 14.13% with PSMC2 knockdown.

Exploration of the pathway regulated by PSMC2

The effects of PSMC2 on different pathways were analyzed via a Human Phospho-Kinase Array, and the results revealed that the STAT3 pathway was significantly downregulated after PSMC2 knockdown (Fig. 3A-B). TCGA data analysis revealed that STAT3 was significantly overexpressed in cancer tissues (Fig. 3C). Studies have confirmed that STAT3 plays a role in promoting cancer in tumors [26]. The correlation between PSMC2 and STAT3 was subsequently analyzed. The results revealed that PSMC2 and STAT3 were positively correlated, with a correlation coefficient of R = 0.221 (Fig. 3D). Furthermore, the western blot results revealed that p-STAT3 levels were significantly decreased after PSMC2 was knocked down (Fig. 3E). These results indicated that PSMC2 participated in the phosphorylation of STAT3 and promoted the activation of STAT3.

Investigating the effect of STAT3 on p53-mediated biological activities in NPC cells

To elucidate the regulatory role of STAT3 in NPC, this study employed ML115, a specific STAT3 agonist, for functional validation. Western blot analysis revealed that PSMC2 knockdown significantly reduced p-STAT3 levels, while ML115 treatment effectively restored its phosphorylation. CCK-8 assays demonstrated that ML115 markedly reversed the PSMC2 knockdown-induced decrease in cell viability (P < 0.01). A scratch wound healing assay further confirmed that ML115 treatment restored the cellular migratory capacity inhibited by PSMC2 (P < 0.05). Annexin V/PI double staining revealed that ML115 significantly reduced PSMC2 knockdown-induced apoptosis rates (from 19.35 to 13.21%, p < 0.01). These findings suggest that PSMC2 promotes NPC cell proliferation, migration and apoptosis through the modulation of STAT3 phosphorylation (Fig. 4).

Investigating the regulatory mechanism of PSMC2 in STAT3-mediated cellular lipid peroxidation

Bioinformatics Venn analysis of upregulated genes in HNSCC, STAT3 transcriptional target genes, and fatty acid β-oxidation regulatory genes revealed that CPT1A and LIPE are downstream effectors regulated by STAT3 (Fig. 5A). Among them, CPT1A had a more significant difference in expression (Fig. 5B). Therefore, we further analyzed the effect of STAT3 on CPT1A expression. The results revealed that the expression level of CPT1 decreased after PSMC2 knockdown, whereas further treatment with ML115 significantly increased the expression level of CPT1 (Fig. 5E). The quantitative assessment of oxidative stress markers was performed via DCFH-DA and BODIPY 581/591 C11 fluorescent probes. The results demonstrated that PSMC2 knockdown increased ROS levels and lipid peroxidation levels. Notably, treatment with the STAT3 agonist ML115 significantly attenuated these effects, reducing ROS levels and decreasing lipid peroxidation levels (Fig. 5C-D). These findings indicate that PSMC2 regulates cellular oxidative stress through the STAT3 pathway.

Previous studies have shown that lipid peroxidation triggers ferroptosis [27]. Figure 4D shows that PSMC2 knockdown induced cell death. To confirm the occurrence of ferroptosis, we conducted detection assays targeting key ferroptosis-related molecules, including glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), reduced glutathione (GSH), and cellular iron. The results showed that PSMC2 knockdown significantly reduced GPX4 expression, depleted the intracellular GSH content, and induced iron accumulation. Notably, the pharmacological activation of STAT3 with ML115 effectively increased GPX4 expression and intracellular GSH levels and reduced iron accumulation (Fig. 5E-G). These results demonstrate that PSMC2 deficiency triggers ferroptosis via STAT3-related lipid peroxidation.

The PSMC2 regulatory mechanism is evaluated in vivo

To observe the effects of PSMC2 on the growth of NPC, subcutaneous tumor models were constructed. The results revealed that the growth ability of NPC cells was significantly reduced with PSMC2 knockdown (Fig. 6A-B). The tumor weight in the shPSMC2 group was significantly lower than that in the shCtrl group (Fig. 6C). The immunohistochemical results revealed that the levels of ki67, p-STAT3, CPT1 and GPX4 were significantly decreased after PSMC2 knockdown (Fig. 6D). These results indicated that PSMC2 inhibited lipid peroxidation involving CPT1 and GPX4 by downregulating STAT3 phosphorylation.

Discussion

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a common malignant tumor. NPC is an epithelial tumor located in the nasopharynx [28]. The incidence of NPC ranks first among HNSCCs [29]. NPC is strongly associated with Epstein‑Barr virus (EBV) infection [30]. Approximately 70% of patients are diagnosed at a locally advanced stage or with metastatic disease at the first diagnosis because of the insidious and aggressive nature of NPC. After comprehensive radiotherapy and chemotherapy, 20–30% of advanced NPC patients still experience treatment failure, primarily due to recurrence and/or metastasis (R/M). As an oncogene, PSMC2 is highly expressed in different tumors. PSMC2 is a novel prognostic biomarker in pancreatic cancer [31]. A previous study revealed that PSMC2 is highly expressed in NPC [32]. When PSMC2 was knocked down, the proliferation and migration of NPC cells were significantly reduced, whereas the apoptosis of these cells was increased. Similar studies have shown that the silencing of PSMC2 inhibits the development and metastasis of prostate cancer [33]. The overexpression of PSMC2 promotes tumorigenesis and development in breast cancer and gastric cancer [13, 34].

A protein microarray revealed that PSMC2 significantly downregulated the STAT3 pathway. The western blot results further confirmed that PSMC2 significantly inhibited STAT3 phosphorylation [35]. Studies have confirmed that STAT3 plays important roles in cancer metastasis, stemness and therapy response [36]. Furthermore, STAT3 promotes NPC oncogenesis [26, 37]. NPC cell activities and metastasis can be suppressed by inhibiting STAT3 phosphorylation [38, 39]. Mangiferin affects fatty acid β-oxidation metabolism and CPT1 protein expression via the STAT3 signaling pathway and further inhibits tumor growth, metastasis and angiogenesis in colorectal cancer [40]. When PSMC2 was knocked down, the expression level of CPT1 was downregulated, and with further activation of STAT3 by ML115, the expression level of CPT1 was significantly increased. These results indicate that PSMC2 enhances CPT1A expression by promoting STAT3 phosphorylation. CPT1 serves as the catalytic rate-limiting enzyme in fatty acid β-oxidation (FAO) by mediating long-chain fatty acid transport across mitochondrial membranes [41]. The CPT1A isoform specifically regulates fatty acid translocation into the mitochondrial matrix, determining the overall metabolic flux of the FAO pathway. CPT1A-mediated fatty acid oxidation promotes cell proliferation via nucleoside metabolism in NPC [42].

Therefore, we further analyzed intracellular lipid peroxidation and reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels after PSMC2 knockdown and ML115 intervention and found that CPT1 levels were downregulated after PSMC2 knockdown and that intracellular lipid peroxidation and reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels were significantly increased. Following treatment with ML115, CPT1 levels increased, and intracellular lipid peroxidation and reactive oxygen species levels significantly decreased. These results indicated that PSMC2 upregulated CPT1 through STAT3, removing excess reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reducing the β-oxidation of fatty acids in NPCs. Studies have confirmed that continuous lipid peroxidation is the key factor that triggers ferroptosis [43]. GPX4 prevents ferroptosis by converting lipid hydroperoxides into nontoxic lipid alcohols. As an auxiliary substance for GPX4, GSH plays a pivotal role in the struggle against lipid peroxidation.

To further confirm that cell death is related to iron death. The levels of GPX4, GSH and iron ions were further detected. With PSMC2 knockdown, GPX4 and GSH levels were downregulated, and iron ion levels and the cell death rate increased significantly. After treatment with ML115, the GPX4 and GSH levels increased, and the iron ion levels and cell death rates decreased significantly. Research has confirmed that STAT3 binds to the GPX4 promoter region and promotes its transcription [22]. These results indicate that PSMC2 deficiency inhibits the phosphorylation of STAT3 and downregulates the expression of CPT1, thereby removing the restriction on fatty acid β-oxidation. Moreover, the downregulation of GPX4 further sustains fatty acid β-oxidation, driving ferroptosis in NPC cells. However, this study has several limitations that warrant further investigation. First, the PSMC2 expression levels between metastatic and nonmetastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) samples still need to be compared. Second, an animal model for NPC cell metastasis needs to be established. Third, the sample size of animal models needs to be increased. STAT3 agonists or CPT1A gene knockdown were employed to elucidate the role of PSMC2 in NPC metastasis and ferroptosis in multiple NPC cell lines. We will conduct in-depth research on these works in our subsequent studies. Additionally, cancer is a sophisticated and dynamic ecosystem. To fully capture the multifaceted nature of tumorigenesis, we will adopt a more integrative perspective to study nasopharyngeal cancer in future research [44].

Conclusions

PSMC2 suppresses lipid peroxidation-induced ferroptosis by promoting STAT3 activation and upregulating CPT1/GPX4 expression, thereby driving malignant proliferation and metastasis in NPC.

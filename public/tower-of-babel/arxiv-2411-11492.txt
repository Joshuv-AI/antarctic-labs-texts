A criterion for virtual Euler class one Thanks: Partially supported by NSFC Grant 11925101, and National Key R&D Program of China 2020YFA0712800
Yi Liu

Abstract
. Let M M be an oriented closed hyperbolic 3 3 –manifold. Suppose that w w is a rational second cohomology class of M M with dual Thurston norm 1 1 . Upon the existence of certain nonvanishing Alexander polynomials, the author shows that the pullback of w w to some finite cover of M M is the real Euler class of some transversely oriented taut foliation on that cover. As application, the author constructs examples with first Betti number either 2 2 or 3 3 , and partial examples with any first Betti number at least 4 4 , supporting Yazdi’s virtual Euler class one conjecture.

1.
Introduction

After disproving Thurston’s Euler class one conjecture,
Yazdi posed a virtual remedy
[
Yaz20
, Question 9.4]
.
The new conjecture is called the
virtual Euler class one conjecture
.
The revised statement is as follows.

Conjecture 1.1

(Yazdi)
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold.
For any even (integral) lattice point
w\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

of dual Thurston norm
1
,
there exists some finite cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
,
such that the pullback
\tilde{w}
of
w
to
\tilde{M}

is the real Euler class of some transversely oriented taut foliation on
\tilde{M}
.

In this paper, we provide a criterion for Conjecture
1.1

in terms of nonvanishing Alexander polynomials.
The criterion allows us to
virtually realize any rational point
on certain closed faces on the boundary of the dual Thurston norm unit ball.

The proof of our criterion suggests that
the even lattice point condition
becomes less restrictive in the virtual context.
It appears more natural to extend the statement of Conjecture
1.1

to all rational points in
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
of dual Thurston norm
1
.
Note that Conjecture
1.1
has no content for
M
of first Betti number
0
.
The conjecture holds obviously true for
M
of first Betti number
1
,
by Gabai’s Euler class one theorem for vertices (see Corollary
2.3
).
We provide evidence to the rational point version of Conjecture
1.1

by producing (a large variety of) examples of first Betti number
2
or
3
.
We also obtain partial examples for any first Betti number
\geq 4
.

We state our criterion as follows.
Theorem
1.2
is the main content,
and Corollary
1.3
is supplementary.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold.
Denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)\subset H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
the Thurston norm unit ball of
M

and by
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)\subset H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
the dual Thurston norm unit ball of
M
.
Suppose that
F\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)

and
F^{\vee}\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)

are a dual pair of closed faces.
Suppose that
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a primitive cohomology class,
such that the Poincaré dual of
\psi

lies in the cone over the interior of
F^{\vee}
.

If the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{M}^{\psi}(t)
does not vanish,
then for any rational point
w
in the interior of
F
,
there exists some finite cyclic cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
dual to
\psi
,
such that the pullback
\tilde{w}
of
w
to
\tilde{M}

is the real Euler class of
some transversely oriented taut foliation on
\tilde{M}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

In the case of Theorem
1.2
,
for any rational point
w
in the boundary of
F
,
there also exists some finite cyclic cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
,
dual to some primitive cohomology class in
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
such that the pullback
\tilde{w}
of
w
to
\tilde{M}

is the real Euler class of
some transversely oriented taut foliation on
\tilde{M}
.

Corollary
1.3
follows easily from Theorem
1.2

and the fact that nonvanishing of
\Delta^{\psi}_{M}(t)
is a projectively open condition
on the primitive cohomology class
\psi
(see Lemma
3.2
).
To be precise, any closed subface
E
of
F

is dual to a closed face
E^{\vee}
that contains
F^{\vee}
as a subface.
Provided the nonvanishing of
\Delta^{\psi}_{M}(t)
,
there exists some primitive cohomology class
\phi
in the cone over the interior of
E^{\vee}
,
such that
\Delta^{\phi}_{M}(t)
does not vanish (Lemma
3.2
).
Applying Theorem
1.2
,
any rational point
w
in the interior of
E

is virtually realizable by a taut foliation,
on some finite cyclic cover of
M
dual to
\phi
.

Theorem
1.2
is proved in Section
5
.

Our criterion suggests
a potential approach to Conjecture
1.1
.
To elaborate, suppose that
w\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
is a rational point on

\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
,
lying in the interior of a closed face
F
.
In general,
it is possible that
\Delta^{\psi}_{M}(t)
vanishes for every primitive
\psi

of Poincaré dual lying in the cone over the interior of
F^{\vee}
.
In that case, our criterion does not apply directly.
However, there are many finite covers of
M
with arbitrarily large first Betti number.
We should even be able to construct a finite cover
M^{\prime}
of
M
,
such that the pullback
w^{\prime}
of
w
to
M^{\prime}
lies in the interior of a closed face
F^{\prime}
,
and such that
F^{\prime}
has codimension as large as wanted in
H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
.
The codimension of
F^{\prime}
in
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

is equal to the dimension of
(F^{\prime})^{\vee}
in
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
.
Therefore, passing to
M^{\prime}
allows us more room to pick a testing
\psi^{\prime}
.
As a prospective step,
we expect to find some nonvanishing
\Delta^{\psi^{\prime}}_{M^{\prime}}(t)

for some
M^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime}
.
Then, applying our criterion,
there will be some finite cyclic cover
M^{\prime\prime}
of
M^{\prime}
dual to
\psi^{\prime}
,
such that the pullback
w^{\prime\prime}
of
w^{\prime}
to
M^{\prime\prime}
is realizable by a taut foliation.
This will prove Conjecture
1.1
.

Currently available techniques of virtual constructions
seem to be insufficient for accomplishing the above strategy.
However, I do not see why the prospective step would be impossible either.

We establish the following results as actual applications of our criterion.
Theorem
1.4
provides positive examples for Conjecture
1.1

with first Betti number
2
or
3
.
Theorem
1.5
provides positive partial examples
with first Betti number
\geq 4
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
b=2
and for
b=3
,
there exists some oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold
M
with
b_{1}(M)=b
,
such that
every rational point
w\in\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)

is virtually realizable by a taut foliation.
Indeed, the pullback of
w
to some finite cyclic cover of
M

dual to some primitive cohomology class in
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

is the real Euler class of some transversely oriented taut foliation
on the covering manifold.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any integer
b\geq 4
,
there exists some oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold
M
with
b_{1}(M)=b
,
and there exist some opposite pair of top-dimensional
closed faces
\pm F\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
,
such that every rational point
w\in\pm F

is virtually realizable by a taut foliation,
and indeed,
by similar means as specified in Theorem
1.4
.

Both Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
are proved in Section
7
.

It is natural to ask whether it is possible to strengthen Theorem
1.5

to a similar statement as Theorem
1.4
by improving the current construction.
A few unsuccessful attempts motivate the following suspection,
which we call the
vanishing Alexander polynomial conjecture
.

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
b\geq 4
be an integer.
For any orientable connected closed
3
–manifold
M
with
b_{1}(M)=b
,
there exists some primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
such that the Alexander polynomial
\Delta^{\psi}_{M}(t)
vanishes.

There has been a long-standing open question
as to whether there exists some integer
B\geq 4
,
such that every orientable closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold
M
with
b_{1}(M)\geq B

has an infinite collection of abelian finite covers
with (uniformly) unbounded
b_{1}

[
CocM06
, Question A]
.
The question was raised by Cochran and Masters
as a former attempt to attack the virtual infinite Betti number conjecture,
prior to Agol’s proof of the latter
[
Ago13
]
.

A proof of Conjecture
1.6

will imply a positive answer
to the question of Cochran and Masters with
B=4
.
This implication has already been noticed
by Cochran and Masters
[
CocM06
, Section 2]

(see also Remark
3.4
).
On the other hand,
any counter example to Conjecture
1.6

for some
b\geq 4
will imply
an improvement of Theorem
1.5

to similar statement as Theorem
1.4
,
for the same
b
(see Proposition
7.3
).

Ingredients

None of the constructions in this paper are extremely complicated.
Therefore, we only indicate some initial ideas toward the final constructions.

Let
M
be an oriented connected closed
3
–manifold.
Suppose that
\mathscr{F}_{0}
is a transversely oriented taut foliation on
M

with a compact leaf
S
.
Note that
S
is necessarily oriented, connected, closed, and nonseparating.
If we have another transversely oriented taut foliation
\mathscr{F}_{1}
,
such that
\mathscr{F}_{1}
also contains
S
as a compact leaf,
then we can produce a hybrid foliation on a double cover
M^{\prime}
of
M
dual to
S
,
by cyclically gluing two foliated blocks of the forms

(M\setminus S,\mathscr{F}_{0})
and
(M\setminus S,\mathscr{F}_{1})
.
There are two ways to arrange these foliated blocks in order,
resulting in two different transversely oriented taut foliations

\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{01}
and
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{10}
on
M^{\prime}
.
They differ by a deck transformation,
so the real Euler classes
e(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{01})
and
e(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{10})
in
H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})

also differ by a deck transformation.
Generally speaking,
these two real Euler classes could be linearly independent
from the pullbacks of
e_{0}=e(\mathscr{F}_{0})
and
e_{1}=e(\mathscr{F}_{1})
.
In that case, we would have little to say.
However,
if
b_{1}(M^{\prime})
is equal to
b_{1}(M)
,
the deck transformation group acts trivially on
H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
,
so we must have
e(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{01})=e(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{10})
.
Moreover, we can figure out that they are both equal to
(e^{\prime}_{0}+e^{\prime}_{1})/2
,
where
e^{\prime}_{0},e^{\prime}_{1}\in H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
denote the pullbacks of
e_{0},e_{1}\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
.
This means that both
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{01}
and
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{10}

realize the pullback of the convex combination
(e_{0}+e_{1})/2
to
M^{\prime}
.

Similarly, if
M^{\prime\prime}
is a finite cyclic cover of
M
dual to
S
of degree
m=m_{0}+m_{1}
,
and if
b_{1}(M^{\prime\prime})=b_{1}(M)
,
we can realize the pullback of
(m_{0}e_{0}+m_{1}e_{1})/m
on
M^{\prime\prime}
by cyclically stacking
m_{0}
copies
of the
(M\setminus S,\mathscr{F}_{0})
–block and
m_{1}
copies of the
(M\setminus S,\mathscr{F}_{1})
–block,
in whatever order.
The similar idea applies to more general situations,
for example, if
\mathscr{F}_{0}
on
M
contains
n
homologous compact leaves
S_{1},\cdots,S_{n}
,
and if each
S_{i}
is also a compact leaf of some other
\mathscr{F}_{i}
on
M
.

The above idea leads to the
medley construction
, as we introduce in Section
4
.
We prove Theorem
1.2
using the medley construction.
We prepare our foliated building blocks by invoking available constructions
due to Gabai
[
Gab83
]
, and due to Gabai–Yazdi
[
GabY20
]
.
The assumed existence of a nonvanishing Alexander polynomial
\Delta^{\psi}_{M}(t)

ensures uniformly bounded
b_{1}
for all finite cyclic covers of
M
dual to
\psi
.
So, in this case,
the real Euler classes for the medley foliations will be under good control after
b_{1}
stabilizes.
See Section
5
for detail.

The construction for proving Theorems
1.4
and
1.5

adopts a different procedure,
except for the last step
where we apply Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
.
In
[
CocM06
, Section 1]
,
Cochran and Masters constructed
many orientable closed hyperbolic
3
–manifolds of first Betti number
2
or
3
.
For each of those manifolds,
every finite abelian cover has the same first Betti number as that of the base manifold.
Our construction follows a procedure mimicking theirs.

We start with some oriented connected closed
3
–manifold
N

with well understood Alexander polynomials.
If we do a surgery on
N
with nonzero rational coefficient
along a
totally rationally null-homologous
knot in
N
,
we can make sure that the resulting manifold
M
has
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

canonically isomorphic to
H^{1}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
and accordingly,
the Alexander polynomials of
M
are the same as those of
N

(possibly up to a nonzero scalar).
Many such surgeries yield a hyperbolic
M
.
By constructing a seed manifold
N
with deliberately designed Alexander polynomials,
we apply the above procedure
to prove Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
.
See Section
7
for detail.

2.
Taut foliations

In this preliminary section, we collect facts
regarding taut foliations in
3
–manifold topology.
For general facts regarding foliations on
3
–manifolds,
we refer to Part 3 in the textbook of Candel and Colon
[
CanC03
]
.

Let
M
be an oriented connected closed irreducible (smooth)
3
–manifold.
A codimension–
1
foliation on
M
(with smooth leaves and continuous holonomy)
is said to
taut
if every leaf intersects some smoothly immersed loop
which is transverse to all the leaves.
To clarify our terminology,
any leaf is considered to be
an injectively immersed, connected surface in
M
.
For transversely oriented codimension–
1
foliations in
M
,
the leaves are considered to be compatibly oriented.

For any transversely oriented taut foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
M
,
the Euler class of
\mathscr{F}
refers to the Euler class

e(T\mathscr{F})\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

of the oriented tangent plane distribution of
\mathscr{F}
over
M
.
In this case,
the real reduction of the Euler class,
called the
real Euler class
of
\mathscr{F}

and denoted as
e(\mathscr{F})\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
must be an even lattice point
contained in the dual Thurston norm unit ball

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
of
M
;
see
[
Thu86
, Corollary 1]
and
[
Yaz20
, Proposition 3.12]
.
This fact lays down fundamental constraints
to any transversely oriented taut foliations on
M
.

We briefly recall the definitions of
the
Thurston norm

\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
on
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

and
dual Thurston norm

\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}
on
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

as follows.
For simplicity, we only present with the closed case,
although the similar definitions work for the bounded case
using
H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})
and
H^{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})
instead.

In general, the Thurston norm is a seminorm
(that is, a generalized norm allowing to be zero on some linear subspace)
and the dual Thurston norm is a partical norm
(that is, a generalized norm allowing to be infinite outside some linear subspace).
These are genuine norms when
M
supports a hyperbolic structure.

To define
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
,
first consider any integral class
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
The Thurston norm
\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{Th}}

is defined to be the minimum of the quantity

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\,\max(-\chi(S_{i}),0)
,
where
S=S_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S_{k}
ranges over
all embedded closed oriented surfaces in
M
representing
\Sigma
,
and where the connected components of
S
listed as
S_{1},\cdots,S_{k}
.
The function
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
on
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

extends linearly over
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Q}})

and continuously over
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
yielding a unique and well-defined seminorm on
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

[
Thu86
, Theorem 1]
.
For any
w\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
the value of
\|w\|_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}

is defined to be the supreme of

|\langle w,\Sigma\rangle|
,
ranging over all
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
with
\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{Th}}\leq 1
,
which yields a unique and well-defined partial norm on
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
.

Denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)\subset H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

the unit ball of the Thurston norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}}

and by
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)\subset H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

the unit ball of the dual Thurston norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}
.
Thurston shows that there are finitely many even lattice points

u_{1},\cdots,u_{s}\in H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
such that
\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{Th}}

is equal to the maximum among all
|\langle u_{i},\Sigma\rangle|
,
for any
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

[
Thu86
, Theorem 2]
.
This characterize
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)
as the intersection
of the half-spaces defined by the affine linear inequalities

\pm u_{1}\leq 1,\cdots,\pm u_{s}\leq 1
on
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
and characterizes
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
as the affine convex hull
of the even lattice points
\pm u_{1},\cdots,\pm u_{s}
in
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
.

Compact leaves play an especially important role
in studying transversely oriented taut foliations
\mathscr{F}
on
M

with real Euler class

e(\mathscr{F})\in\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
.
We collect the following relevant facts.

Theorem 2.1

(Thurston)
.

Let
M
be an oriented connected closed irreducible
3
–manifold.
If
\mathscr{F}
is a transversely oriented taut foliation on
M
,
and if
\mathscr{F}
contains the connected components
of an embedded, oriented, closed surface
S
in
M
as compact leaves,
then

\|[S]\|_{\mathrm{Th}}=\langle e(\mathscr{F}),[S]\rangle=-\chi(S),

and hence,

\|e(\mathscr{F})\|_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}=1.

This is called the
norm-minimizing property

for compact leaves of transversely oriented taut foliations;
see
[
Thu86
, Corollary 2]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Gabai)
.

Let
M
be an oriented connected closed irreducible
3
–manifold.
If
S
is an embedded, oriented, closed surface in
M

without sphere components,
and if the equality
\|[S]\|_{\mathrm{Th}}=-\chi(S)
holds,
then there exists a transversely oriented taut foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
M
,
such that
\mathscr{F}
contains the connected components of
S
as compact leaves.

This is a consequence of Gabai’s sutured hierarchy theory;
see
[
Gab83
, Theorem 5.5]
.

As an application, consider any vertex
v
on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
.
Dual to
v
is some top-codimensional closed face
F^{\vee}
on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)
.
Take some embedded, oriented, closed, norm-minimizing surface
S
representing a homology class

[S]\in H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
in the cone over
\mathrm{int}(F^{\vee})
,
then there is some transversely oriented taut foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
M

containing the connected components of
S
as compact leaves (Theorem
2.2
),
and
e(\mathscr{F})
has to be
v
in this case (Theorem
2.1
).
Note that
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
b_{1}(M)\geq 1
.
Therefore, we obtain the following corollary; see also
[
GabY20
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Corollary 2.3

(Gabai)
.

Let
M
be an oriented connected closed irreducible
3
–manifold.
Suppose
b_{1}(M)\geq 1
.
Then, every vertex on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
is
the real Euler class of some transversely oriented taut foliation on
M
.

Theorem 2.4

(Gabai–Yazdi)
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold.
Suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is a transversely oriented taut foliation on
M
.
Suppose that
S
is an embedded oriented closed surface in
M
,
such that the equality
\langle e(\mathscr{F}),[S]\rangle=-\chi(S)
holds.
Then, there exists some transversely oriented taut foliation
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
on
M

homotopic to
\mathscr{F}
as oriented plane distributions,
and there exists some embedded oriented closed surface
S^{\prime}
in
M

homologous to
S
,
such that
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
contains the connected components of
S^{\prime}
as compact leaves.

This is called the
fully marked surface theorem
;
see
[
GabY20
, Theorem 1.1]
.

3.
Alexander polynomials

In this preliminary section,
we review the multivariable polynomial and the Alexander polynomials with respect to primitive cohomology classes,
without involving extra twists.
For an overview of twisted Alexander polynomials in more generality,
we recommend the survey of Friedl and Vidussi
[
FriV11
]
.
We include direct proofs of
two simple facts (Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
)
for the reader’s convenience.
We recall some special facts regarding
3
–manifolds.

For any (commutative)
uniquely factorization domain (UFD)
R
,
and for any finitely generated module
V
over
R
,
we recall that the
rank
of
V
over
R
refers to
the dimension of
V\otimes_{R}\mathrm{Frac}(R)

over the field of fractions
\mathrm{Frac}(R)
of
R
.
If
R
is Noetherian,
we recall that the
order
of
V
over
R
refers to
any greatest common divisor of the zeroth elementary ideal
E_{0}(V)
,
which is unique up to a unit of
R
.
More precisely, as
R
is Noetherian,

V
can be finitely presented as cokernel of some matrix
A
over
R
of size
m\times n
,
regarded as an
R
–linear homomorphism
R^{n}\to R^{m}
;
then
E_{0}(V)
is the ideal of
R

generated by all the
n\times n
–minors of
A
(or the zero ideal if
m\leq n
),
which does not depend on the choice of
A
.
For any finitely generated module
V
of a Noetherian UFD
R
,
the order of
V
is nonzero if and only if the rank of
V
is zero.

Let
X
be a connected finite cell complex.
Denote by
H
the free abelianization of
\pi_{1}(X)
.
Denote by
\tilde{X}^{\#}
the covering space of
X

corresponding to the kernel of
\pi_{1}(X)\to H
.
The deck transformation group action of
H

endows
H_{1}(\tilde{X}^{\#};{\mathbb{Z}})
with a module structure
over the commutative group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
.
As
X
has only finitely many cells,

H_{1}(\tilde{X}^{\#};{\mathbb{Z}})
is finitely generated over
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
.

For any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
denote by
\tilde{X}^{\psi}
the infinite cyclic cover of
X

dual to
\psi
.
Note that
H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a freely finitely generated over
{\mathbb{Z}}
,
by the universal coefficient theorem.
A cohomology class in
H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
is said to be
primitive

if it is not divisible by any nonzero integer other than
\pm 1
.
The universal free abelian covering projection
\tilde{X}^{\#}\to X

naturally factors through
\tilde{X}^{\psi}
,
corresponding to the quotient homomorphism
\phi_{*}\colon H\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Identifying the commutative group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}]

with the Laurent polynomial ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]

(in a fixed indeterminant
t
),

H_{1}(\tilde{X}^{\psi};{\mathbb{Z}})
is finitely generated over
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a connected finite cell complex.

(1)

Denote by
H
the free abelianization of
\pi_{1}(X)
,
and by
\tilde{X}^{\#}
the universal free abelian cover of
X
.
The (universal)
multivariable Alexander polynomial
of
X

is defined to be the order of
H_{1}(\tilde{X}^{\#};{\mathbb{Z}})
,
as a finitely generated module over
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
.
It is denoted as a representative element
\Delta^{\#}_{X}
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
,
uniquely up to a unit of
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
.

(2)

For any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
denote by
\tilde{X}^{\psi}
the infinite cyclic cover of
X
dual to
\psi
.
The
Alexander polynomial
of
X
with respect to
\psi

is defined to be the order of
H_{1}(\tilde{X}^{\psi};{\mathbb{Z}})
,
as a finitely generated module over
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
.
It is denoted as
a representative Laurent polynomial
\Delta^{\psi}_{X}(t)
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
,
which is unique up to a monomial factor and a sign.
Note that
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]^{\times}=\{\pm t^{n}\colon n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}

is the multiplicative group of units of
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
.

It is clear that
\Delta^{\#}_{X}
and
\Delta^{\psi}_{X}(t)

are essentially only invariants of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)
.
In particular,
it also makes sense to speak of
\Delta^{\#}_{X}
and
\Delta^{\psi}_{X}(t)

for any connected compact manifold
X
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a connected finite cell complex.
Suppose that
\psi\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a primitive cohomology class,
such that
\Delta^{\psi}_{X}(t)
does not vanish.
Then,
there exists some open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}(\psi)

of
\psi
in
H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
, such that the following property holds.

If
\phi\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a primitive cohomology class,
and if
\phi
is a nonzero integral multiple of a rational cohomology class in
\mathcal{U}
,
then
\Delta^{\phi}_{X}(t)
does not vanish.

Proof.

For simplicity, we denote the cellular
{\mathbb{Z}}
–chain complex of
\tilde{X}^{\#}

as
(C^{\#}_{\bullet},\partial^{\#}_{\bullet})
.
For each dimension
n
,
C^{\#}_{n}
is freely finitely generated as a
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
–module.
By choosing an ordering of the
n
–cells of
X
,
and a lift of each
n
–cell to
\tilde{X}^{\#}
,
the boundary operator
\partial^{\#}_{n}\colon C^{\#}_{n}\to C^{\#}_{n-1}

can be regarded as a matrix over
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
.

For any
\alpha\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
,
we obtain a
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–chain complex

(C^{\alpha}_{\bullet},\partial^{\alpha}_{\bullet})=(C^{\#}_{\bullet}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[H]}{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}],\partial^{\#}_{\bullet}\otimes 1)
,
where the
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
–module structure of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
is induced by
the group ring homomorphism
\alpha_{*}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}[H]\to{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
.
One may think of the elements in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
as
generalized Laurent polynomials that are (finite)
{\mathbb{Z}}
–linear combinations
of real powers of the indeterminant
t
,
and think of each
\partial^{\alpha}_{n}
as obtained from
\partial^{\#}_{n}

by applying
\alpha_{*}
to each matrix entry.
Note that
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
is a UFD and is flat over
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}]
.

For any primitive
\psi\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
the similarly constructed
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}]
–chain complex

(C^{\psi}_{\bullet},\partial^{\psi}_{\bullet})
can be identified with
the cellular
{\mathbb{Z}}
–chain complex of
\tilde{X}^{\psi}
.

For any
\alpha\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
,
we denote
H^{\alpha}_{n}=H_{n}(C^{\alpha}_{\bullet},\partial^{\alpha}_{\bullet})
.
This is a
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–module for each
n
.
If
\alpha
is nonzero, direct computation shows
H^{\alpha}_{0}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
.
It follows that the
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–rank of
H^{\alpha}_{0}
is zero,
so the
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–rank of
\mathrm{Ker}(\partial^{\alpha}_{1})

is equal to the
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–rank of
\mathrm{Im}(\partial^{\alpha}_{0})=C^{\alpha}_{0}
.
This rank stays constant under nonzero perturbation of
\alpha
.
On the other hand, the
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–rank of
\mathrm{Im}(\partial^{\alpha}_{2})

is the rank of the
\partial^{\alpha}_{2}
as a matrix over
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
.
This rank does not decrease under sufficiently small perturbations of
\alpha
,
and it stays constant under nonzero
{\mathbb{R}}
–scalar multiplication of
\alpha
.
If
H^{\alpha}_{1}
has
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–rank zero,
then the
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–rank of
\mathrm{Im}(\partial^{\alpha}_{2})

equal to the
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–rank of
\mathrm{Ker}(\partial^{\alpha}_{1})
,
Therefore, if
H^{\alpha}_{1}
has
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–rank zero,
and if
\alpha
is nonzero,
then there exists some sufficiently small neighborhood
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}(\alpha)

of
\alpha
in
H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
, such that
H^{\beta}_{1}
has
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–rank zero
for any
\beta\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})

which is a nonzero real multiple of a real cohomology class in
\mathcal{U}
.

Suppose that
\psi\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
is primitive,
and suppose that
\Delta^{\psi}_{X}(t)
does not vanish.
Obtain an open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}(\psi)
of
\psi
in
H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
as above.
For any primitive
\phi\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
which is an integral multiple of
some rational cohomology class in
\mathcal{U}
,
we infer that
H^{\phi}_{1}
has
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–rank zero.
By flatness of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
over
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}]
,
we infer that
H_{1}(\tilde{X}^{\psi};{\mathbb{Z}})
has
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}]
–rank zero.
This is equivalent to the asserted property that
\Delta^{\phi}_{X}(t)
does not vanish.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a connected finite cell complex.
Suppose that
\psi\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a primitive cohomology class,
such that
\Delta_{X}^{\psi}(t)
does not vanish.
Then, the following uniform bound holds for for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

b_{1}(X^{\prime}_{m})\leq\mathrm{deg}\left(\Delta^{\psi}_{X}\right)+1.

Here,
X^{\prime}_{m}
denotes the
m
–cyclic cover of
X
dual to
\psi
;

\mathrm{deg}(\Delta^{\psi}_{X})
denotes
the floating degree of the Laurent polynomial
\Delta^{\psi}_{X}(t)
,
by which we mean
the degree of the topmost nonzero term minus the degree of the bottommost nonzero term.

Proof.

For simplicity, we rewrite the infinite cyclic cover of
X
dual to
\psi

as
X^{\prime}_{\infty}=\tilde{X}^{\psi}
.
If
\Delta_{X}^{\psi}(t)
does not vanish,

H_{1}(X^{\prime}_{\infty};{\mathbb{Z}})
has
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
–rank zero.
Since
{\mathbb{Q}}
is flat over
{\mathbb{Z}}
,
we obtain
H_{1}(X^{\prime}_{\infty};{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H_{1}(X^{\prime}_{\infty};{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Since
{\mathbb{Q}}[t,t^{-1}]
is a principal ideal domain (PID),
it follows that
H_{1}(X^{\prime}_{\infty};{\mathbb{Q}})
has finite dimension over
{\mathbb{Q}}
,
and dimension is exactly the degree of the order
\Delta^{\psi}_{X}(t)
.
The fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)
splits as a semidirect product

\pi_{1}(X^{\prime}_{\infty})\rtimes\langle z\rangle
.
For any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\pi_{1}(X^{\prime}_{m})
is the exactly the subgroup

\pi_{1}(X^{\prime}_{\infty})\rtimes\langle z^{m}\rangle
.
By abelianization, we obtain

b_{1}(X^{\prime}_{m})\leq b_{1}(X^{\prime}_{\infty})+1=\mathrm{deg}\left(\Delta^{\psi}_{X}\right)+1,

as asserted.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Instead, if we suppose that
\Delta^{\psi}_{X}(t)
vanishes,
then
H_{1}(X^{\prime}_{\infty};{\mathbb{Z}})
has nonzero rank over
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
.
In this case, the following well-known equality
implies that
b_{1}(X^{\prime}_{m})
can be arbitrarily large.

\mathrm{lim}_{m\to\infty}\,b_{1}(X^{\prime}_{m})/m=\mathrm{rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]}\left(H_{1}(X^{\prime}_{\infty};{\mathbb{Z}})\right).

More precisely, the
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
–rank of
H_{1}(X^{\prime}_{\infty};{\mathbb{Z}})

can be identified as the first
\ell^{2}
–Betti number of
the CW complex
X^{\prime}_{\infty}
with respect to the infinite cyclic
deck transformation group action
[
Lüc02
, Lemma 1.34]
;
the latter is equal to the linear growth rate of
b_{1}(X^{\prime}_{m})
in
m
,
by the approximation conjecture (on
\ell^{2}
–Betti numbers)
which holds true for the infinite cyclic group

[
Lüc02
, Conjecture 13.1, Theorem 13.3, and Definition 13.9]
.

For the multivariable Alexander polynomial
\Delta^{\#}_{X}
,
we can speak of its (floating) degree with respect to any
\alpha\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
.
To be precise, we express a representative
\Delta^{\#}_{X}
as
a_{1}h_{1}+\cdots a_{s}h_{s}
,
for some distinct
h_{1},\cdots,h_{s}\in H
and for some nonzero
a_{1},\cdots,a_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then, for any
\alpha\in H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
, define

\mathrm{deg}_{\alpha}\left(\Delta^{\#}_{X}\right)=\max_{i,j}\left|\alpha(h_{i})-\alpha(h_{j})\right|,

(or zero if
\Delta^{\#}_{X}
vanishes).
Note that the value of
\mathrm{deg}_{\alpha}

does not change if we the representative changes by a unit of
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
.
In general,
\mathrm{deg}_{\alpha}(\Delta^{\#}_{X})
is greater than or equal to
the floating degree of
\alpha_{*}(\Delta^{\#}_{X})
(viewed as a generalized Laurent polynomial),
but not necessarily equal to because of possible canceling.
As a function in
\alpha
,
the
\alpha
–degree of
\Delta^{\#}_{X}
is a seminorm on
H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
,
which is also known as the
Alexander norm

[
Mcm02
]
.

In the case of
3
–manifolds,
the Alexander norm supplies lower bounds to the Thurston norm,
and the multivariable Alexander polynomial determines
the Alexander polynomials with respect to primitive cohomology classes.
We only state the case with oriented connected closed
3
–manifolds,
as this is the only case to be considered in the sequel.
The particular formulas below are only needed in Section
7
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
M
be an oriented connected closed
3
–manifold.

(1)

For any real cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
, the following inequality holds.

\mathrm{deg}_{\alpha}\left(\Delta^{\#}_{M}\right)\leq\|\mathrm{PD}(\alpha)\|_{\mathrm{Th}}+\begin{cases}0&b_{1}(M)\geq 2\\
2&b_{1}(M)=1\end{cases}

Here,
\mathrm{PD}
denotes the Poincaré duality isomorphism.

(2)

For any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
the following equality holds in
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
holds up to a monomial factor and a sign.

\Delta^{\psi}_{M}(t)\doteq\psi_{*}\left(\Delta^{\#}_{M}\right)\cdot\begin{cases}(t-1)^{2}&b_{1}(M)\geq 2\\
1&b_{1}(M)=1\end{cases}

See
[
Mcm02
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
Sun17
, Proposition 2.3]
for these facts.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
M
be an oriented connected closed
3
–manifold.
For any primitive
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
with nonvanishing
\Delta^{\psi}_{M}(t)
,
if
S
is an oriented closed surface representing
\mathrm{PD}(\psi)
,
and if
S
contains no nonempty null-homologous subunion of connected components,
then
S
is connected.
In this case, the following inequality holds.

\mathrm{deg}\left(\Delta^{\psi}_{M}\right)\leq 2\cdot\mathrm{genus}(S).

The assertion that
S
is connected
follows easily from Lemma
3.3
.
The asserted inequality is immediately implied by Theorem
3.5
.
In the special case when
\psi
is a fibered class
and when
S
is a dual surface fiber,

\Delta^{\psi}_{M}(t)
can be computed as the characteristic polynomial
of the linear automorphism
f_{*}\colon H_{1}(S;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(S;{\mathbb{Z}})

induced by the monodromy
f\colon S\to S
(well-defined up to isotopy).
This case achieves the equality in the asserted inequality.

4.
The medley construction

In this section, we introduce a general construction
to cyclically stack a finite number of blocks
with different foliation patterns.
The construction yields a foliated finite cyclic cover,
as precisely described in Lemma
4.1
.
This is what we call the
medley construction
.
We prove some basic properties of the medley construction
in Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
.

To avoid unnecessary restrictions,
we do not assume the surfaces in Lemma
4.1
to be connected.
We do not make use of such connectedness in subsequent sections either.
However, it may be noteworthy that
in the scenario of Theorem
1.2
,
the surfaces to be used in the construction are indeed connected,
due to Corollary
3.6
(see Section
5
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M
be an oriented connected closed
3
–manifold.
Suppose that
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a primitive homology class.
Suppose
S_{1},\cdots,S_{n}\subset M

are embedded, mutually disjoint, oriented, closed subsurfaces,
such that each
S_{i}
represents
\Sigma
,
and such that each
M\setminus S_{i}
is connected.
Suppose that

\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathscr{F}_{1},\cdots,\mathscr{F}_{n}
are
transversely oriented foliations on
M
,
such that all components of
S_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S_{n}

are leaves of
\mathscr{F}_{0}
,
and such that all components of each
S_{i}
are leaves of
\mathscr{F}_{i}
.

Then, for any integers
m_{0}\geq 1,m_{1}\geq 0,\cdots,m_{n}\geq 0
,
there exists
some transversely oriented foliation
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}

on a cyclic cover
M^{\prime}
dual to
\Sigma

of degree
m=m_{0}+m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}
over
M

such that the following properties hold.

•

For each
S_{i}
, there are
m_{0}+m_{i}
prescribed lifts of
S_{i}
to
M^{\prime}
,
whose components are all leaves of
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
.

•

For each
X=M\setminus S_{i}
,
there are
m_{i}
prescribed lifts of
(X,\mathscr{F}_{i}|_{X})
to
(M^{\prime},\mathscr{F}^{\prime})
.
The inward frontiers and the outward frontiers of these prescribed lifts
are all prescribed lifts of
S_{i}
as above.

•

For every component
X
of
M\setminus(S_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S_{n})
,
there are
m_{0}
prescribed lifts of
(X,\mathscr{F}_{0}|_{X})
to
(M^{\prime},\mathscr{F}^{\prime})
.
The inward frontiers and the outward frontiers of these prescribed lifts
are all among the prescribed lifts of
S_{1},\cdots,S_{n}
as above.

•

All the above prescribed lifts are mutually disjoint, and
they form
(M^{\prime},\mathscr{F}^{\prime})
altogether.

Proof.

First we take an
m_{0}
–cyclic cover of
M
dual to
\Sigma

with the pullback foliation of
\mathscr{F}_{0}
.
Each
S_{i}
and each component of
M\setminus(S_{0}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S_{n})

lifts to this cover,
since
\Sigma=[S_{i}]
in
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
for any
S_{i}
.
The lifts of
S_{0},\cdots,S_{n}

and the lifts of the components of
M\setminus(S_{0}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S_{n})

are declared as the prescribed lifts.
At any prescribed lift of any
S_{i}
,
we can insert a copy of
(M\setminus S_{i},\mathscr{F}_{i}|_{M\setminus S_{i}})
,
(namely, cut along the prescribed lift of
S_{i}

and glue in between a copy of
M\setminus S_{i}
).
This operation results in a new cyclic cover of
M
dual to
\Sigma

of
1
degree higher,
introducing a new prescribed lift of
M\setminus S_{i}
,
and a new prescribed lift of
S_{i}
.
Redo this operation iterately for
m_{i}
times for each
i=1,\cdots,n
.
We end up with a cyclic cover
M^{\prime}
dual to
\Sigma

of degree
m=m_{0}+m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}
over
M
,
and a transversely oriented foliation
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
on
M^{\prime}
,
as asserted.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

With the notations and assumptions in Lemma
4.1
,
if
\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathscr{F}_{1},\cdots,\mathscr{F}_{n}
on
M
are all taut,
then
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
on
M^{\prime}
is also taut.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathscr{F}_{1},\cdots,\mathscr{F}_{n}
on
M
are all taut.
Fix a point in each component of
S_{i}
,
and denote their union as
Q_{i}\subset S_{i}
.
Since
\mathscr{F}_{i}
is taut,
for each
q\in Q_{i}
,
we can construct a path
\alpha_{q}
from
q
to some point in
Q_{i}
,
such that
\alpha_{q}
is positively transverse to
\mathscr{F}_{i}
,
and
\alpha
has no intersection with
S_{i}
other than the endpoints.
Moreover, for any leaf
L\neq S_{i}
in
\mathscr{F}_{i}
,
we may either construct a loop
\gamma_{L}
in
M
which has no intersection with
S_{i}
,
or construct a path
\gamma_{L}

which has both endpoints in
Q_{i}
and no other intersection with
S_{i}
,
such that
\gamma
is positively transverse to
\mathscr{F}_{i}
and meets
L
.
Furthermore, we denote
S_{0}=S_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S_{n}
and
Q_{0}=Q_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup Q_{n}
,
then we obtain similar paths or loops
\alpha_{q}
and
\gamma_{L}
with respect to
\mathscr{F}_{0}
.
By concatenating lifts of the above
\alpha_{q}
and
\gamma_{L}
,
we can form a loop
\delta^{\prime}
in
M^{\prime}
to meet any leaf
L^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
,
such that
\delta^{\prime}
is positively transverse to
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
.
Therefore,
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
on
M^{\prime}
is taut.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

With the notations and assumptions in Lemma
4.1
,
the following Euler class formula holds in
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
:

\kappa_{!}(e(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}))=m_{0}\cdot e(\mathscr{F}_{0})+m_{1}\cdot e(\mathscr{F}_{1})+\cdots+m_{n}\cdot e(\mathscr{F}_{n}).

Here,
\kappa_{!}\colon H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

denotes the umkehr homomorphism of the covering projection
\kappa\colon M^{\prime}\to M
,
namely,
\kappa_{!}=\mathrm{PD}\circ\kappa_{*}\circ\mathrm{PD}
.

Proof.

By fixing a Riemannian metric on an oriented
3
–manifold
N
,
any transversely oriented foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
N

determines an oriented rank–
2
real vector bundle
T\mathscr{F}
over
N
,
which can be identified as a complex line bundle
\mathcal{L}
over
N
.
In particular,
e(\mathscr{F})=c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
holds in
H^{2}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Moreover, if
\kappa\colon N^{\prime}\to N
is any finite covering projection,
any complex line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
over
N^{\prime}

gives rise to a complex line bundle
\kappa_{!}\mathcal{L^{\prime}}
on
N
,
such that the fiber of
\kappa_{!}\mathcal{L^{\prime}}
at
q\in N
is
the complex tensor product of all
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{q^{\prime}}
,
where
q^{\prime}
ranges over the lifts of
q
.

When
N
is closed,
the identity
c_{1}(\kappa_{!}\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\kappa_{!}(c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}))

holds in
H^{2}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
An easy way to see this is as follows.
For any embedded loop
\alpha^{\prime}\colon S^{1}\to N^{\prime}
,
one can construct a map
N^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{P}^{1}
,
such that every point outside a compact tubular neighborhood of
\alpha^{\prime}

is mapped to
\infty
in
{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{P}^{1}\cong{\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}
,
and in the tubular neighborhood, parametrized as
D^{2}\times S^{1}
,
the map factors through the projection
D^{2}\times S^{1}\to D^{2}
,
and descending to a map of
D^{2}
to
{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{P}^{1}\cong{\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}

which maps
\partial D
to
\infty

and maps
\mathrm{int}(D^{2})
orientation-preserving homeomorphically onto
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Composing with the standard inclusion of
{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{P}^{1}
into

{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{P}^{\infty}\simeq K({\mathbb{Z}},2)
,
the resulting map
N^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{P}^{\infty}
represents an element in
H^{2}(N^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})
,
which is exactly the Poincaré dual of
[\alpha]\in H_{1}(N^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})
,
and which is also the pullback of the universal first Chern class.
With this interpretation understood, we can rewrite

c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\mathrm{PD}([\alpha^{\prime}])
for some
\alpha^{\prime}
as above,
and reconstruct
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
(isomorphically)
as a pullback of the universal complex line bundle
via the above map
N^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{P}^{\infty}
.
We may also assume that
D^{2}\times S^{1}

embeds into
N
under
\kappa
,
then our construction implies canonical isomorphisms

(\kappa_{!}\mathcal{L}^{\prime})_{q}\cong{\mathbb{C}}
for any
q\in N\setminus\kappa(D^{2}\times S^{1})
,
and
(\kappa_{!}\mathcal{L}^{\prime})_{q}\cong\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{q^{\prime}}

for any
q\in\kappa(D^{2}\times S^{1})
,
where
q^{\prime}
is the unique inverse point in
D^{2}\times S^{1}
.
So,
\kappa_{!}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
over
N
is canonically isomorphic to
the pullback of the universal complex line bundle
via a classifying map
N\to{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{P}^{\infty}

constructed the same way as above,
with respect to the embedded loop
\kappa\circ\alpha^{\prime}\colon S^{1}\to N

and the parametrized tubular neighborhood
\kappa(D^{2}\times S^{1})
.
This implies

c_{1}(\kappa_{!}\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\mathrm{PD}(\kappa_{*}[\alpha^{\prime}])
,
and
the identity
c_{1}(\kappa_{!}\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\kappa_{!}(c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}))
follows.

To prove Lemma
4.3
,
we obtain a complex line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
over
M^{\prime}
,
associated to
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
,
and similarly,

\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{L}_{n}
over
M
,
associated to
\mathscr{F}_{0},\cdots,\mathscr{F}_{n}
.
By assumption,
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{i}
are identical over
S_{i}
.

To compute
\kappa_{!}(c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}))
,
we compare
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}

with another complex line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{0}
over
M^{\prime}
,
namely, the pullback of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
to
M^{\prime}
.
Note that
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are identical
over any prescribed lift of any
S_{i}
,
and over any prescribed lift of any component of
M\setminus(S_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S_{n})
,
so the complex line bundle

\overline{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime}

is canonically isomorphic to the constant complex line bundle
{\mathbb{C}}
over those prescribed lifts.
Therefore, we obtain a canonical isomorphism
of complex line bundles over
M
:

\kappa_{!}\left(\overline{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\right)\cong\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\left(\overline{\mathcal{L}_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{i}\right)^{\otimes m_{i}}.

This implies

\kappa_{!}\left(c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\right)-\kappa_{!}\left(c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{0})\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\cdot\left(c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{i})-c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{0})\right)

On the other hand, since
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{0}
is the pullback of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
to
M^{\prime}
,
and since
M^{\prime}
is a cyclic cover of degree
m=m_{0}+m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}
,
we obtain

\kappa_{!}\left(c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{0})\right)=(m_{0}+m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n})\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{0}).

Therefore, we obtain the identity in
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
:

\kappa_{!}\left(c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\right)=m_{0}\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{0})+m_{1}\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{1})+\cdots+m_{n}\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{n}),

which is the same as the asserted formula in Lemma
4.3
. ∎

5.
The Alexander polynomial criterion

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold.
Suppose that
F\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)

and
F^{\vee}\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)

are a dual pair of closed faces.
Suppose that
w\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
is a rational point in the interior of
F
.
Suppose that
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a primitive homology class,
such that
\Sigma
lies in the cone over the interior of
F^{\vee}
.

Then, there exist some finite cyclic cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
dual to
\Sigma
,
and some transversely oriented taut foliation
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}
on
\tilde{M}
,
such that the following equality holds in
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
:

\kappa_{!}(e(\tilde{\mathscr{F}}))=[\tilde{M}:M]\cdot w.

Here,
\kappa_{!}\colon H^{2}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
denotes umkehr homomorphism for
the covering projection
\kappa\colon\tilde{M}\to M
.

Proof.

Enumerate the vertices of
F
as

v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\in F.

By the Euler class one theorem for vertices (Corollary
2.3
),
for each
v_{i}
, there exists some transversely orientable taut foliation
\mathscr{F}_{i}
on
M

with real Euler class
e(\mathscr{F}_{i})=v_{i}
.
By assumption,
\langle e(\mathscr{F}_{i}),\Sigma\rangle=\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{Th}}

holds for each
\mathscr{F}_{i}
.
By the fully marked surface theorem (Theorem
2.4
),
possiblying after modifying
\mathscr{F}_{i}

without altering the declared properties,
we may assume that for each
\mathscr{F}_{i}
,
there exists some embedded, oriented, closed subsurface
S_{i}
of
M
representing
\Sigma
,
such that the components of
S_{i}
are all leaves of
\mathscr{F}_{i}
.

For any sufficiently large positive integer
d
,
the complements
M\setminus S_{1},\cdots,M\setminus S_{n}

can be lifted to a
d
–cyclic cover
M^{\prime}
of
M
dual to
\Sigma
,
such that the lifts have mutually disjoint closures in
M^{\prime}
.
Note that each
M\setminus S_{i}
is connected,
because
\Sigma
is primitive and because
\mathscr{F}_{i}
are transversely oriented taut.
Take some
d
and
M^{\prime}
with this property,
and take some mutually disjoint lifts
S^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,S^{\prime}_{n}
for
S_{1},\cdots,S_{n}
.
We can also require
S^{\prime}_{i}

to be the outward frontier of a lift of
M\setminus S_{i}
.
This way, the lifts
S^{\prime}_{i}
are mutually homologous in
M^{\prime}
.
Denote by
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{n}

the pullback foliations of
\mathscr{F}_{1},\cdots,\mathscr{F}_{n}
to
M^{\prime}
.
Denote by
\Sigma^{\prime}
the common homology class
[S^{\prime}_{i}]\in H_{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})
.

By construction,
S^{\prime}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S^{\prime}_{n}
is
an embedded, closed, oriented, norm-minimizing subsurface of
M^{\prime}
,
representing the nontorsion homology class
n\Sigma^{\prime}\in H_{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})
.
By Gabai’s construction of taut foliations
from norm-minimizing surfaces (Theorem
2.2
),
there exists some transversely oriented taut foliation
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{0}
on
M^{\prime}
,
such that the components of
S^{\prime}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S^{\prime}_{n}

are all leaves of
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{0}
.

Observe that the image of the real Euler class
e(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{0})

under the umkehr homomorphism
H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

takes the form
d\cdot v_{0}
,
for some rational point

v_{0}\in F

depending on
(M^{\prime},\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{0})
.
In fact,
as
\Sigma
lies in the cone over the interior of
F^{\vee}
,
the linear function
u\mapsto\langle u,\Sigma\rangle

on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)
maximizes exactly on
F

(with maximum value
\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
).
Hence,
the linear function
u^{\prime}\mapsto\langle u^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime}\rangle

on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)
maximizes exactly on
F^{\prime}

(with the same maximum value),
where
F^{\prime}
denotes the maximal closed face of

\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime})

that contains the pullback of
F
.
By construction, we obtain

\langle e(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{0}),\Sigma^{\prime}\rangle=-\chi(S_{i})=\|\Sigma^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
.
This shows that
e(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{0})
lies in
F^{\prime}
,
so its image under the umkehr homomorphism lies in
d\cdot F
.

With the vertices
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}
of
F
and the rational point
v_{0}\in F
as above,
we can find rational numbers
\mu_{0}>0,\mu_{1}\geq 0,\cdots,\mu_{n}\geq 0

with
\mu_{0}+\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n}=1
, such that
the given rational point
w\in\mathrm{int}(F)
can be written as

w=\mu_{0}\cdot v_{0}+\mu_{1}\cdot v_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n}\cdot v_{n}.

For example, we can choose some rational
\mu_{0}>0
small enough,
making sure that
w-\mu_{0}\cdot v_{0}
still lies in
\mathrm{int}(F)
,
then
w-\mu_{0}\cdot v_{0}
can be written as a convex combination

\mu_{1}\cdot v_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n}\cdot v_{n}
.
Note that this is where we need the assumption
w\in\mathrm{int}(F)

(indeed, the construction would fail for any
w\in\partial F

if
v_{0}
happens to lie in
\mathrm{int}(F)
).
Take some
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n})
as above,
and take any sufficiently divisible positive integer
D
,
such that
m^{\prime}_{0}=\mu_{0}D/d,m^{\prime}_{1}=\mu_{1}D/d,\cdots,m^{\prime}_{n}=\mu_{n}D/d
are all integers.

Finally, we apply Lemma
4.1
to the setting with

M^{\prime}
,
\Sigma^{\prime}
, and
(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{0},\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{n})
,
using the integers
(m^{\prime}_{0},m^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,m^{\prime}_{n})
.
Then, we obtain a
D/d
–cyclic cover
\tilde{M}
over
M^{\prime}
dual to
\Sigma^{\prime}
,
and a transversely oriented foliation
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}
on
\tilde{M}
.
By Lemma
4.2
,

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}
is taut, as
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{0},\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{n}
are all taut.
Note that
\tilde{M}
is also a
D
–cyclic cover of
M
dual to
\Sigma
.
By Lemma
4.3
, we compute in
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
:

\kappa_{!}(e(\tilde{\mathscr{F}}))=D\cdot(\mu_{0}\cdot v_{0}+\mu_{1}\cdot v_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n}\cdot v_{n})=[\tilde{M}:M]\cdot w,

as asserted. ∎

.

With the above preparation, we prove Theorem
1.2
as follows.

Let
M
be an oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold.
Let
F\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)

and
F^{\vee}\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)

be a dual pair of closed faces.
Let
w\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
be a rational point in the interior of
F
.
Let
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
be a primitive cohomology class,
such that
\mathrm{PD}(\psi)
lies in the cone over the interior of
F^{\vee}
.

If the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{M}^{\psi}(t)
does not vanish,
the first Betti number is uniformly bounded
for all finite cyclic covers of
M
dual to
\psi
(Lemma
3.3
).
Suppose the maximum first Betti number is achieved by
a
d
–cyclic cover
M^{\prime}
of
M
dual to
\psi
.
Note that the pullback
\psi^{\prime}\in H^{1}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})
has divisibility
d
,
so
\Sigma^{\prime}=\mathrm{PD}(\psi^{\prime}/d)
is a primitive cohomology class.
Denote by
w^{\prime}\in H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
the pullback of
w
to
M^{\prime}
.
Denote by
F^{\prime}\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime})

the minimal closed face containing the pullback of
F
.
We observe
w^{\prime}
lies in
\mathrm{int}(F^{\prime})
.
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
lies in the cone over the interior of

(F^{\prime})^{\vee}\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M^{\prime})
.
Applying Lemma
5.1

to
M^{\prime}
with respect to
w^{\prime}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
,
we obtain some finite cyclic cover
\tilde{M}
of
M^{\prime}
dual to
\Sigma^{\prime}
,
and some transversely oriented taut foliation
\tilde{F}
on
\tilde{M}
,
such that the image of the real Euler class
e(\tilde{F})

under the umkehr homomorphism
H^{2}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})

is equal to
[\tilde{M}:M]\cdot w^{\prime}
.

Since
M^{\prime}
already maximizes the first Betti number among all finite cyclic covers
of
M
dual to
\psi
,
the umkehr homomorphism
H^{2}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})

is an isomorphism.
It follows that
e(\tilde{F})\in H^{2}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})

is equal to the pullback
\tilde{w}
of
w^{\prime}\in H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
to
\tilde{M}
,
which is also the pullback of
w\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
to
\tilde{M}
.
Therefore,
we have verified that

\tilde{M}
and
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}
are as asserted in Theorem
1.2
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

6.
Totally rationally null-homologous knots

For any oriented
3
–manifold
M
,
an oriented null-homologous knot
K\subset M

refers to a locally flatly embedded, oriented circle
which bounds some embedded, oriented compact subsurface of
M
.
In this case, there is a meridian-longitude pair with respect to
K
,
similar as usual for classical knots in
S^{3}
.
To be precise, take a compact tubular neighborhood of
K
in
M
,
parametrized as
\partial D^{2}\times K
,
then the
meridian
refers to the slope
(that is, the isotopy class of an oriented essential simple closed curve)
on
\partial D^{2}\times K
parallel to the first factor;
require that the parametrization makes the second factor
null-homologous in
M\setminus K
,
then the
longitude
refers to the slope parallel to the second factor.

For any rational number
p/q
,
expressed uniquely with coprime integers
p\geq 0
and
q\neq 0
,
the
p/q
–
surgery
of
M
along
K
follows
the similar standard convention as in classical knot theory.
Namely, the resulting manifold
is obtained from
M\setminus\mathrm{int}(D^{2}\times K)

by Dehn filling with another solid torus,
such that the slope represented by
p
times the meridian plus
q
times the longitude
bounds a disk in the filling solid torus.
We denote the resulting manifold of the
p/q
–surgery of
M
along
K

as
M_{p/q}(K)
.

The core of the filling solid torus generates a
{\mathbb{Z}}
–submodule
of
H_{1}(M_{p/q}(K);{\mathbb{Z}})
, which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
;
the quotient of
H_{1}(M_{p/q}(K);{\mathbb{Z}})
by this submodule
is naturally isomorphic to
H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
generalizing the similar fact for surgery on classical knots in
S^{3}
.

Definition 6.1
.

For any
3
–manifold
M
,
a null-homologous knot
K\subset M
is said to be

totally rationally null-homologous

if there exists some compact neighborhood
R
of
K
in
M
,
such that
K
is null-homologous in
R
,
and such that the inclusion map of
R
into
M

induces a zero homomorphism
H_{1}(R;{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{Q}})
.

If we require a stronger property that
R\to M
is null-homotopic,
we will recover the definition of a
totally null-homotopic
knot,
as appeared in
[
BoiW96
, Section 4]
.
In particular, the following lemma is trivial implication
of
[
BoiW96
, Proposition 4.2]
.
We include a sketch of the construction for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 6.2
.

For any oriented connected closed
3
–manifold
M
,
there exists an oriented, null-homologous,
and totally rationally null-homologous knot
K\subset M
,
such that
M\setminus K
supports a complete hyperbolic structure of finite-volume.
Hence, for all but finitely many rational numbers
p/q
,
the surged manifold
M_{p/q}(K)
supports a hyperbolic structure.

Proof.

Take a wedge of circles
\bigvee^{r}S^{1}
with
r\geq 2
.
Take a map
\bigvee^{r}S^{1}\to M
which induces a zero homomorphism

H_{1}(\bigvee^{r}S^{1};{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{Q}})
.
The map
\bigvee^{r}S^{1}\to M
can always be homotoped to an embedding,
such that a compact regular neighborhood
R
of the embedded image
is homeomorphic to a handlebody of genus
r
,
and such that
M\setminus\mathrm{int}(R)
is simple
(that is, irreducible, boundary-irreducible, atoroidal, and acylindrical).
Take an oriented null-homologous knot
K\subset R

with a compact tubular neighborhood
D^{2}\times K
,
such that
R\setminus\mathrm{int}(D^{2}\times K)
is simple.

By construction,
K
is totally rationally null-homologous in
M
.
Moreover,

M\setminus K
supports a complete hyperbolic structure of finite volume,
by Thurston’s hyperbolization theorem.
Hence,
for all but finitely many rational numbers
p/q
,
the surged manifold
M_{p/q}(K)
supports a hyperbolic structure,
by Thurston’s hyperbolic Dehn filling theorem.

In our sketched construction above,
the homotopic modifications for ensuring simplicity of the resulting manifolds
can be done by (repeatedly) using tricks due to Myers.
See
[
BoiW96
, Proof of Proposition 4.2]

for more detail.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
M
be an oriented connected closed irreducible
3
–manifold.
Suppose that
K\subset M
is
an oriented, null-homologous, and totally rationally null-homologous knot.
For any coprime integers
p\geq 0
and
q\neq 0
,
denote by
M_{p/q}(K)
the
p/q
–surgery of
M
along
K
.

If
p/q\neq 0
,
then there is a canonical isomorphism
H^{1}(M_{p/q}(K);{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
and accordingly,
the following equality of Alexander polynomials holds
for any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.

\Delta_{M_{p/q}(K)}^{\psi}(t)\doteq p\cdot\Delta_{M}^{\psi}(t)

Here, the dotted equal symbol means
an equality in
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
up to a monic factor and a sign.

Proof.

Take an embedded compact tubular neighborhood
D^{2}\times K
of
K
in
M
,
with product parametrization consistent with a meridian-longitude pair.
Denote
X=M\setminus\mathrm{int}(D^{2}\times K)
.
For any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
and for any positive integer
m
,
the
m
–cyclic cover
\tilde{M}^{\psi}_{m}
of
M
dual to
\psi

pulls back to an
m
–cyclic cover
\tilde{X}^{\psi}_{m}
of
X
.

Since
K
is null-homologous, the tubular neighborhood
D^{2}\times K

has exactly
m
lifts to
\tilde{M}^{\psi}_{m}
,
the complement of their interiors is by definition
\tilde{X}^{\psi}_{m}
. Since
K
is totally rationally null-homologous,
we can find some oriented compact surface
F
bounded by
K

in a rationally null-homologous neighborhood
R
of
K
in
M
.
Therefore, the
m
lifts of
K
to
\tilde{M}^{\psi}_{m}

are bounded by
m
lifts of
F
to
\tilde{M}^{\psi}_{m}
.
It follows that
the
m
lifts of the meridian of
K
(on
\partial D^{2}\times K
)
represent
{\mathbb{Z}}
–linearly independent elements in

H_{1}(\tilde{X}^{\psi}_{m};{\mathbb{Z}})
,
as is evident by intersection pairing
with
H_{2}(\tilde{X}^{\psi}_{m},\partial\tilde{X}^{\psi}_{m};{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Viewing
H_{2}(\tilde{X}^{\psi}_{m};{\mathbb{Z}})
as a
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}]
–module
with respect to the deck tranformation group
{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
,
these elements span a free
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}]
–submodule

L_{m}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}]
,
so we obtain a natural isomorphism of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}]
–modules:

H_{1}(M^{\psi}_{m};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{1}(\tilde{X}^{\psi}_{m};{\mathbb{Z}})/L_{m},

by the van Kampen theorem and abelianization of fundamental groups.

For any rational number
p/q
, denote for simplicity

N=M_{p/q}(K).

By simutaneously Dehn filling the lifted
p/q
–slopes on
\partial\tilde{X}^{\psi}_{m}
,
we obtain an
m
–cyclic cover
\tilde{N}^{\psi}_{m}
of
N
,
and a natural isomorphism of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}]
–modules:

H_{1}(N^{\psi}_{m};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{1}(\tilde{X}^{\psi}_{m};{\mathbb{Z}})/p\cdot L_{m}.

Therefore, we obtain a natural short exact sequence of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}]
–modules:

Remark 6.4
.

For any oriented connected closed irreducible
3
–manifold
M

and any oriented null-homologous knot
K\subset M
,
there is a canonical isomorphism

H^{1}(M_{0}(K);{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H^{1}(M\setminus K;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Accordingly,
for any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(M\setminus K;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
one can figure out the following formula

\Delta_{M\setminus K}^{\psi}(t)\cdot\left(t^{\psi(\mu)}-1\right)\doteq\Delta^{\psi}_{M_{0}(K)}(t)\cdot\begin{cases}(t-1)&b_{1}(M\setminus K)\geq 2\\
(t-1)^{2}&b_{1}(M\setminus K)=1\end{cases}

where
\mu\in H_{1}(M\setminus K;{\mathbb{Z}})
is represented by the meridian of
K
in
M
.
See
[
Tur76
, Theorem F]
,
[
FriV11
, Section 3.3]
,
and
[
Sun17
, Proposition 2.3]
for facts to derive the formula.

This formula determines
\Delta^{\psi}_{M_{0}(K)}(t)
unless
\psi(\mu)=0
.
If
\psi(\mu)=0
,
and if
K
is totally rationally null-homologous in
M
,
then
\psi
lies in the image of
the natural homomorphism
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(M\setminus K;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
In this case, the finite cyclic covers of
M_{0}(K)
dual to
\psi

have unbounded first Betti number,
implying
\Delta^{\psi}_{M_{0}(K)}(t)\doteq 0
by Lemma
3.3
.

7.
Examples

In this section,
we prove Theorems
1.4
and
1.5

by exhibiting Examples
7.1
and
7.2
,
respectively.
We also prove a simple observation as mentioned in the introduction,
namely,
any counter example to the vanishing Alexander polynomial conjecture (Conjecture
1.6
)
leads to an example of the virtual Euler class one conjecture (Conjecture
1.1
)
with first Betti number
\geq 4
(Proposition
7.3
).

Example 7.1
.

For any integer
e
,
let
N=N(e)
be an oriented circle bundle over an oriented torus with Euler number
e
.
We observe

b_{1}(N)=\begin{cases}2&e\neq 0\\
3&e=0\end{cases}

Note that any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})
is fibered.
With respect to
\psi
, the dual surface fiber of
N
is an oriented torus.
The forward monodromy is the identity, if
e=0
;
or it is the (right-hand) Dehn twist raised to the power
e
,
along the slope represented by the circle-bundle circle, if
e\neq 0
.
Therefore, for any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
we observe

\Delta_{N}^{\psi}(t)\doteq(t-1)^{2}

as an equality in
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
up to a monomial factor and a sign.

Take some oriented, null-homologous, totally rationally null-homologous, and hyperbolic knot
K\subset N
,
and some hyperbolic surgery of
N
along
K
with rational coefficient
p/q\neq 0
,
as guaranteed to exist by Lemma
6.2
.
Denote the resulting oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold as
M=N_{p/q}(K)
.

By Lemma
6.3
,
we obtain
b_{1}(M)=b_{1}(N)
, equal to either
2
or
3
,
and
\Delta^{\psi}_{M}(t)\doteq p\cdot\Delta^{\psi}_{N}(t)
,
nonvanishing for any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
By Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
,
every rational point
w
on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)

is virtually realizable by a taut foliation.
Indeed, the pullback
\tilde{w}
of
w
to some cyclic finite cover
\tilde{M}
of
M

dual to some primitive cohomology class in
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

is the real Euler class of some transversely oriented taut foliation on
\tilde{M}
.

Example 7.2
.

For any integers
g\geq 2
and
0\leq s\leq g
,
let
N=N(g,s)
be an oriented surface bundle over an oriented circle,
such that the surface fiber
S
is connected closed of genus
g
,
and the forward monodromy
f\colon S\to S

is the product of the Dehn twists
along
s
mutually disjoint simple closed curves
of nonseparate union.
We observe

b_{1}(N)=1+2g-s.

In fact,
H^{1}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})
splits
as the direct sum of fixed
{\mathbb{Z}}
–submodule

\mathrm{Fix}(f^{*})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g-s}

of the induced homomorphism
f^{*}\colon H^{1}(S;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(S;{\mathbb{Z}})

and
{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot\mathrm{PD}([S])\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Moreover, the Thurston norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}}

of
N
vanishes on
\mathrm{PD}(\mathrm{Fix}(f^{*}))

and satisfies
\|[S]\|_{\mathrm{Th}}=2g-2
.
Treating
N
as the mapping torus of
f\colon S\to S
,
the suspension flow through any point on
S
outside the Dehn-twist annuli
yields a
1
–periodic trajectory,
representing a homology class
\Gamma\in H_{1}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
From the above description of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
,
the dual Thurston norm unit ball
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(N)

is contained in the linear subspace
{\mathbb{R}}\cdot\Gamma
of
H_{1}(N;{\mathbb{R}})
,
as the closed interval with endpoints
\pm(2g-2)\cdot\Gamma
.

The Alexander polynomial of
N

with respect to the distinguished fibered class

\phi=\mathrm{PD}([S])
in
H^{1}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})

can be computed as the characteristic polynomial of
f_{*}
,
yielding
\Delta^{\phi}_{N}(t)\doteq(t-1)^{2g}
.
The Newton polytope of
\Delta^{\#}_{N}
has to be a segment
due to the shape of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(N)
,
so we infer
\Delta^{\#}_{N}\doteq(\Gamma-1)^{2g-2}

in
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
up to a unit (see Theorem
3.5
).
For any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})
, we obtain

\Delta^{\psi}_{N}(t)\doteq\left(t^{\langle\psi,\Gamma\rangle}-1\right)^{2g-2}\cdot(t-1)^{2}.

Take
M=N_{p/q}(K)
to be a hyperbolic surgery of
N
with rational coefficient
p/q\neq 0
,
along an oriented, null-homologous, totally rationally null-homologous, and hyperbolic knot
K\subset N

(Lemma
6.2
).
Note that
b_{1}(M)=b_{1}(N)
(Lemma
6.3
)
can be any integer
b=1+2g-s\geq 3
, by suitably setting
g
and
s
.

For any primitive cohomology class
\psi

in
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H^{1}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
we obtain
\Delta^{\psi}_{M}(t)\doteq p\cdot\Delta^{\psi}_{N}(t)
(Lemma
6.3
),
vanishing if and only if
\psi
lies in
\mathrm{Fix}(f^{*})
.
Since
M
is hyperbolic,

\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)

must have some pair of opposite vertices lying outside
the codimension–
1
linear subspace

\mathrm{PD}(\mathrm{Fix}(f^{*}))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
of
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
.
These vertices are dual to an opposite pair of top-dimensional closed faces

\pm F\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
.
Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3

apply to
\pm F
,
since we can use a primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

with
\mathrm{PD}(\psi)
an integral multiple of the above vertices.
Therefore,
every rational point
w\in\pm F

is virtually realizable by a taut foliation.

Proposition 7.3
.

If there exists a counter example to Conjecture
1.6
for some
b\geq 4
,
then there exists some oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold
M
with
b_{1}(M)=b
,
such that every rational point
w\in\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)

is virtually realizable by a taut foliation, and indeed,
by similar means as specified in Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

Suppose that
N
is a counter example to Conjecture
1.6
for some integer
b\geq 4
.
This means that

N
is an oriented connected closed
3
–manifold with
b_{1}(N)=b
,
such that for any primitive cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(N;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
the Alexander polynomial
\Delta^{\phi}_{N}(t)
does not vanish.
Under this hypothesis,

N
can take the place of the synonymous manifold in Example
7.1
,
and the similar argument remains valid.
Namely,
we apply Lemma
6.2

to obtain a hyperbolic surgery of
N
with rational coefficient
p/q\neq 0
,
along an oriented, null-homologous, totally rationally null-homologous, and hyperbolic knot
K\subset N
.
The resulting oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold

M=N_{p/q}(K)
with
b_{1}(M)=b

satisfies the asserted property in Proposition
7.3
,
by Lemma
6.3
, and Theorem
1.2
, and Corollary
1.3
.
∎

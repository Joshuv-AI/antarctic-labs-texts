Lung cancer therapy using a chitosan-based nano drug discovery system of silver nanoparticles and chemotherapeutics
Authors: Ghufran Abdulal Shaheed Kemawy, Javad Mohammadnejad, Asghar Narmani, Mehrab Pourmadadi, Hanieh Jafari, Yusuf Döğüş, Sevinc Jafarova Haqverdi, Gülüzar Özbolat, Amin Daemi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related mortality among various types of cancer. In this way, unfortunately, some conventional modalities in cancer treatment such as surgery, immunotherapy, chemotherapy, etc., have high levels of deficiencies and even they can lead to death. To solve this challenge, this paper aims to develop a novel technology such as drug delivery systems (DDSs) based on nanoparticles (NPs).MethodsTo do so, chitosan (CS)-polycaprolactone (PCL) NPs were fabricated for efficient silver (Ag) NPs and sodium butyrate (NB) delivery to A549 lung cancer cells. Then, the CS-PCL-Ag-NB sample was characterized using FT-IR, DLS, TEM, and TGA devices. The quality of the syntheses verified, the size determined at about 100 nm to 20 nm in diameter, high thermal stability determined, and 30% Ag and 8% NB contents were measured for CS-PCL-Ag-NB.ResultsAccording to the obtained results, the controlled (eight folds slower compared with pure NB) and pH-sensitive (3 folds faster in pH 5.0) NB releases were observed for nanocarrier. Moreover, the cell viability assay demonstrated more than 75% cytotoxicity for CS-PCL-Ag-NB after 24 h treatment with 20 nM concentration. Furthermore, qRT-PCR technique exhibited a 9.6, 5.9, and 7.8 folds increase in the expression levels of Caspase9, Bax, and P53 apoptotic genes after treatment with CS-PCL-Ag-NB.ConclusionHigh biocompatibility obtained for CS and CS-PCL samples. CS-PCL-Ag-NB indicated higher cancer cell inhibition potency compared with pure NB while fabricated nanocarrier had very low toxicity on MSC normal cells. Finally, the obtained results confirmed the ability of CS-PCL-Ag-NB in suppressing cancer cells and inducing apoptosis.Keywords: PCL-modified chitosan, Ag nanoparticles, Sodium butyrate, Drug delivery system, Lung cancer therapy

Introduction

Cancer, a kind of malignant disease, is the second leading cause of death all around the globe [1]. Its incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life year have made an eye-catching economic burden on public health systems during the last decades [2]. Several issues, such as ecological, historical, environmental, genetic variables, etc. are associated with cancer incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life year [2, 3]. Among various types of cancer, lung cancer is the most killer cancer in both males and females [4]. Based on the reports, approximately 12 million people will be suffering from cancer annually by 2030 which is attention-getting statistics [5]. In order to prevent the progression of cancer incidence and mortality, efficient technologies are required. For instance, drug delivery systems (DDS) based on nanoparticles (NPs) can develop for the suppression of cancer rather than inefficient conventional modalities, such as immunotherapy, surgery, and chemotherapy [6–8]. In immunotherapy, some issues related to its safety and efficacy should be taken into consideration. In surgery, removing lung tumors is a high risk since lung cancer is disseminated among the lung tissues [9, 10]. Besides, in chemotherapy, high cost of treatment, immunosuppression effects, low specificity, long-term period of time, severe side effects on healthy tissues of the human body, and so forth are mentioned as drawbacks of chemotherapy [11–13]. As a result, there is an increasing focus on the development of novel approaches in prognosis, diagnosis, and treatment of cancer. One of the most efficient methods is applying NP-based breakthrough technologies, which offer unique advantages in targeted delivery, controlled release, and theranostic applications [14, 15].

Polymeric NPs owing to many potential properties, including ease of manipulation, tailored and engineered structures, high drug entrapment efficiency, controlled drug release, and surface functionalization capabilities, are mentioned as the most promising DDSs for bioactive delivery to cancer cells [16, 17]. Among polymeric materials, nano-size and stable biopolymers, such as chitosan (CS) and polycaprolactone (PCL), have received the attention of researchers and clinicians in cancer therapy [18, 19]. These biopolymers, including CS and PCL, have received the approval of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for various preclinical and clinical applications due to their established safety and biocompatibility profiles [16, 20, 21]. CS NPs (poly [β-(1–4)-linked-2-amino-2-deoxy-D-glucose] amino polysaccharide) extract from chitin. These NPs have a wide variety of applications in DDSs, biosensors, tissue engineering, and regenerative medicine [11, 22]. These bio-polymeric NPs possess some specific aspects, such as bioavailability, cost-effectivity, biodegradability, biocompatibility, solubility and stability in the human body serum, non-immunogenicity, and so forth which made them an efficient platform and nanocarrier in cancer therapy [18, 23, 24]. On the other hand, PCL NPs have regular structure with semi-crystalline properties which can improve their susceptibility to efficient surface modification and preparation [25]. PCL is a biocompatible and biodegradable biomaterial. This NP hydrolyzes by cleavage of ester groups in physiological conditions and intracellular degradation and generates acidic byproducts and serves as nutrients in normal cells [26, 27].

On the other hand, silver (Ag) NPs can entrap in the cavity of NPs and serve as therapeutics. These NPs have good stability, biocompatibility, and solubility, which make them suitable for biomedical applications, including cancer therapy and bioimaging [28, 29]. It is reported that the anticancer performance of Ag NPs is due to their ability in damaging DNA, mediating oxidative stress and inflammation (ROS), interfering mitochondria functionality, denaturing of ribosomes, disrupting cytoplasmic membranes [30, 31]. Additionally, sodium butyrate (Na OOCCH2CH2CH3: NB) as a kind of natural root therapeutics in cancer suppression can apply to the inhibition of cancers [32]. These bioactive can inhibit vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) by suppressing histone deacetylases (HDACs), prevent VEGFR-mediated angiogenesis, and eventually, lead to inhibition of cell-cycle in the G2/M phase mediating protein kinase B (Akt). NB also decreases the level of biomolecules associated with angiogenesis and apoptosis, like Akt, Bcl2, IL6, and IL17, thereby promoting a pro-apoptotic and anti-angiogenic tumor microenvironment [33]. These therapeutics can promote autophagy in cancer cells through LKB1/AMPK signaling pathway [34]. Deinavizadeh, Kiasat [35] proposed how good photothermal efficiency of gold nanorods, high loading capacity for doxorubicin, and biocompatibility of the AuNRs@S-β-CD-DOX nanocomposite. Deinavizadeh, Kiasat [36] proposed a synergistic approach by combining chemo-photothermal treatment using gold nanorods (AuNRs) supported on thiol-functionalized mesoporous silica, offering a promising solution for enhanced lung cancer therapy. Deinavizadeh, Kiasat [37] fabricated β-cyclodextrin-based nanosponges encapsulating PEGylated gold nanorods and doxorubicin (DOX), AuNR-S-PEG.β-CD NS-DOX, comprehensively characterized it using TEM, SEM, FE-SEM, FITR, and DLS. Ramhormozi, Khatami [38] investigated a combinatorial strategy using doxorubicin (DOX)-loaded gold nanorods functionalized with thiolated β-cyclodextrin (AuNRs@S-β-CD-DOX) in conjunction with 808 nm NIR laser irradiation in A549 human lung cancer cells.

Kapoor, Gandhi [39] provided a thorough examination of different polymeric NP platforms, such as chitosan, gelatin, alginate, poly (lactic acid), and polycaprolactone, addressing their mechanisms, formulations, and applications in the treatment of lung cancer. Kim, Kwak [40] described the chemical and physicochemical properties of Polycaprolactone (PCL), Polylactic acid (PLA), and Poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA), their synthetic strategies, and the controlled drug release technology through surface functionalization and stimuli-responsive design. Banerjee and Madhyastha [41] investigated the theragnostic approaches in the fields of tissue remodulation and reparation. Then, the engineering of theragnostic nanomaterials was explored for disease diagnosis, imaging, and therapeutic treatments. Lei, Qin [42] described innovative nanotechnology-based drug delivery systems (NDDSs) strategies to overcome Cisplatin (CDDP) resistance, focusing on pre-target, on-target, off-target, and post-target mechanisms within a precision medicine framework.

Therefore, these bioactive can block cell cycle and differentiation and induce apoptosis and autophagy. While both Ag NPs and NB have individually demonstrated anticancer potential, their combined application within a single, targeted drug delivery system remains largely unexplored. Furthermore, the use of a CS-PCL for the simultaneous delivery of these two distinct therapeutic agents to lung cancer cells represents a significant departure from previous studies, which have typically focused on single-drug delivery systems or have utilized different polymer combinations. The novelty of our work is as follows: (1) the rational design of a CS-PCL nanoplatform engineered to co-encapsulate both a metallic nanoparticle (Ag) and a small-molecule drug (NB), (2) the synergistic anticancer effect achieved by this combinatorial approach, and (3) a comprehensive evaluation of the system’s pH-sensitive release profile and its ability to induce apoptosis at the genetic level. In this research, we prepared CS-PCL for efficient delivery of NB and Ag NPs to lung cancer cells which followed by characterizing samples.

Materials and methods

Materials and apparatus

CS (Mw: 50000 to 190000 Da; Viscosity: 20–300 cp.), PCL (Mw: 14,000 Da), AgNO3 salt, NB powder, sodium tripolyphosphate (TPP), polyvinyl alcohol (PVA), dichloromethane (DCM), di-methyl-sulphoxide (DMSO), dimethyl formamide (DMF), and cellulose dialysis tube were purchased from Sigma Aldrich. Besides, 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) salt, RPMI 1640 medium, and fetal bovine serum (FBS) were bought from Sigma-Aldrich. The A549 lung cancer cell lines and MSC normal cell lines were prepared from the Institute of Pasteur, Iran.

Characterization of fabricated samples was done using NanoDrop 2000c UV-vis spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA), Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectrometer (Perkin-Elmer 843), and thermogravimetric (TGA) STA 1500 (Rheometric Scientific). Dynamic light scattering (DLS) (NanoBrook 90 Plus; Brookhaven, USA) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Philips EM 208 S; Netherlands) were also applied for evaluating the physicochemical properties of samples.

Surface modification of CS with PCL

In this section, PCL was conjugated to CS by esterification reaction using NHS and EDC and the formation of amide bond between the PCL and CS. This reaction was performed via the following procedure. Practically, 25 mg of PCL was solubilized in 8 mL DCM. Then, 6 mL of 0.3 mM EDC solution and 6 mL of 0.1 mM NHS solution were prepared and combined. EDC/NHS can serve as an activator of PCL. Thereafter, NHS/EDC mixture was added to the prepared PCL solution under stirring at 80 rpm for 5 h. Afterward, the activated PCL was dropwise to the CS solution (50 mg CS dissolved in 6 mL of acetic acid 1%) and stirred at 80 rpm for 24 h at room temperature. Eventually, the CS-PCL solution was purified by centrifugation (3 times) to separate unreacted PCL polymers. The whole process of reactions was implemented under a nitrogen atmosphere and light-protected conditions. The prepared precipitate was divided into two parts: one of them was saved for the next part of the preparation of nanocarrier and another one was underwent the following procedure to produce globular DDS. As 1 mL of TPP (1 mg/mL) was added to the mixture of CS-PCL solution (acetic acid 1%) drop-by-drop under ultra-sonication conditions at 60% set amplitude for 90 s on an ice bag. Ultimately, the prepared nanocarrier was recovered for analytical characterization and biomedical tests.

Ag and drug loading into CS-PCL

To prepare the therapeutics-containing nanocarrier, Ag and NB solutions were firstly prepared. Briefly, 8 mg of AgNO3 was dissolved in 5 mL of deionized water. Then, this solution was mixed with the 10 mL of CS-PCL solution (prepared in acetic acid 1%) at 80 rpm for 2 h and room temperature. To prepare the spherical DDS, 1 mL of TPP (1 mg/mL) was dropwise to the mixture of CS-PCL- and Ag-containing solution under ultra-sonication conditions at 60% set amplitude for 90 s on an ice bag. The same scenario was conducted for simultaneous Ag and NB entrapment in the CS-PCL nanocarrier. Practically, 8 mg of AgNO3 and 8 mg of NB were separately prepared in their specific solutions which was followed by combining in 10 mL of CS-PCL-containing solution (prepared in acetic acid 1%) under 80 rpm for 2 h and room temperature. In order to prepare the globular nanocarrier, 1 mL of TPP (1 mg/mL) was added to the mixture of CS-PCL-, NB-, and Ag-containing solution drop-by-drop under ultra-sonication circumstances at 60% set amplitude for 90 s on an ice bag. Finally, the prepared DDS was recovered for analytical characterization and biomedical tests.

Characterization of syntheses

The quality and quantity of fabricated DDS were studied using the FT-IR, DLS, TEM, and TGA analytical devices as the following procedure. The qualitative efficiency of CS, CS-PCL, CS-PCL-Ag, and CS-PCL-Ag-NB samples was studied by means of FT-IR spectroscopy. FT-IR was done in the transmittance mode, using the pellet procedure with the KBr procedure in the wavelength range between 400 and 4000 cm− 1. The hydrodynamic diameter of samples was ascertained through a DLS spectrophotometer. In this test, the freshly prepared specimens were vigorously sonicated by ultrasonication at 37 °C for 15 min and the size assessment was conducted at 37 °C in a quartz cuvette at nM concentrations. The size, shape, and morphology of specimens were investigated by applying TEM. In this analysis, the freshly prepared solution of the therapeutics-loaded nanocarrier was dispersed onto a copper grid and dried at 25 °C. After absorbing the excess liquid with filter paper, the image of sample was taken at an accelerating voltage of 120 kV. Also, the thermal stability and therapeutics content of samples were measured using TGA. Practically, 5 mg of samples were heated in a temperature range of 50 to 600 °C at a 10 °C/min heating rate in a pan under a constant nitrogen flow rate (40 mL/min) [43].

Drug release study

The drug release profile of NB-containing nanocarrier (CS-PCL-Ag-NB) was surveyed under optimal circumstances in PBS (as a release medium) at pH of 7.4, 6.0, and 5.0 to study the capability of DDS in controlling the release of NB. This experiment was carried out using the dialysis bag procedure at 37 °C [44]. Practically, 2 mg of CS-PCL-Ag-NB was dissolved in 5 mL of PBS and transferred to a 12,000 Da prepared dialysis tube. The ends of the tube were fastened with clamps. Next, the tube was immersed in 100 mL PBS and the system was placed in an incubator-shaker at 120 rpm and 37 °C. Thereafter, 100 µL of the PBS was removed at time intervals of 0.5, 1 h, 2 h, 4 h, 8 h, 16 h, 32 h, and 64 h the same amounts of PBS replaced. Eventually, the NB absorption of the collected aliquots was measured using a Nanodrop UV–visible spectrophotometer at a wavelength of 480 nm to determine the absorption values.

Cell culture

The human A549 lung cancer cell lines and human MSC normal cell lines were purchased from the Pasture Institute of Iran. These cell lines were cultured in its medium (RPMI; Gibco, UK) supplemented with 10% FBS, 2 mM glutamine, 100 µgmL− 1 streptomycin, and 100 IU mL− 1 penicillin. Then, the cells were incubated at 37 °C and 5% CO2 to grow up.

Cell viability assay

The cytotoxic effects of the fabricated CS-based nanocarriers, including the CS, CS-PCL, CS-PCL-Ag, CS-PCL-Ag-NB, pure Ag, and pure NB on A549 cancer cell lines were scrutinized after 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h of treatments. This test was carried out by the MTT assay using various concentrations of samples, including 25 nM, 50 nM, 100 nM, and 200 nM. This assay is based on the evaluation of mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity in treated cells. This test was performed as the following procedure. Cancer and normal cells were firstly seeded in each well of 96-well microtiter plates (5000 cells/100 µL) and incubated at 37 °C and 5% CO2 for 24 h to attach to wells. On the other hand, after preparation of various concentrations of fabricated specimens in a serum-supplemented culture medium, a 0.4 μm filter was used for sterilizing samples at pH 7.4. Next, the samples were used for the treatment of normal and cancer cells at 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h of post-treatment as the following procedure. The culture medium of the plates was collected, 150 µL serial dilutions of the prepared samples were transferred to wells, and the cells were incubated at the three mentioned times. After determined times, the medium of each well was replaced by 200 µL of RPMI without serum which followed by adding MTT solution (0.5 mg MTT/mL prepared in PBS at pH 7.4). Thereafter, after incubation of the plates for 4 h, the suspension liquid of wells was removed, the cells were re-suspended in 200 mL of DMSO for 10 min at dark conditions; in the wake of this process, the optical density (OD) of the palates was measured at 570 nm by means of ELISA reader to ascertain viable cells.

Gene expression assay

The quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR) technique was employed to survey the relative expression level of apoptotic genes, including Caspase-9, Bax, and P53 at 24 h of post-treatment with IC50 concentration of CS-PCL-Ag-NB in A549 cells using Q Rotor-Gene (Qiagen, Iran) as the following procedure. In the first section, the total RNA was extracted by mixing A549 cells with RNX-PLUS solution followed by adding 200 µL of chloroform. After centrifuging cells at 13,000 rpm for 4 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was collected, the precipitate mixed with isopropanol, and consequently, centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. Afterward, the supernatant was removed and the precipitate merged in 1 mL of ethanol 75%. Next, cDNA was synthesized using 1000 ng of total RNA by the reverse transcription process: 10 µL of Master mix real-time, 3 µL (10 ng) RNA, and 7 µL DD water were transferred to microtubes for synthesizing cDNA. In what follows, the qRT-PCR technique was conducted with a 14 µL of the reaction mixture, 1 µL of cDNA, 7 µL of low rox Master mix real-time, and 0.5 µL of forward primer (Caspase-9: 5ʹTGGCTCCTGGTACGTTGA3ʹ; Bax: 5ʹTTTCTGACGGCAACTTCAACTG3ʹ; P53: 5ʹ GGAGTATTTGGATGACAGAAAC3ʹ) and 0.5 µL of reverse primer (Caspase-9: 5ʹGAAACAGCATTAGCGACCCT3ʹ; Bax: 5ʹTCCAATGTCCAGCCCATGA3ʹ; P53: 5ʹ GATTACCACTGGAGTCTTC3ʹ). The PCR temperature was optimized by qRT-PCR device as the following: heating at 95 °C for 10 min, 45 cycles at 95 °C for 15 s, annealing at 59 °C for 25 s, and elongation at 72 °C for 30 s. In the end, relative gene expression values were normalized to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate-dehydrogenase (GAPDH) and data was quantified as fold change relative to the control.

Statistical analysis

The one-way ANOVA statistical analysis was done for evaluating differences between the various fabricated groups with considered significance at *p < 0.05. The experiments were triplicated.

Results and discussion

Characterization of nano-complex

FT-IR analysis

The synthesis quality of CS-based nanoparticles, including CS, CS-PCL, CS-PCL-Ag, and CS-PCL-Ag-NB, was studied by using the FT-IR technique. Figure 1 indicates the FT-IR peaks of fabricated samples. As shown in Fig. 1, the characteristic peaks at around 3437 cm− 1 and 3337 cm− 1 were associated with –NH2 and –OH groups stretching vibration, respectively [45]. Besides, a peak of 1151 cm− 1 was an indicator of asymmetric stretching of the − C−O − C− bridge while the C − N bond peak was detected at around 1034 cm− 1 [46]. The absorption peaks at around 1490 cm− 1 (C–H stretching in methyl groups) and 2800 cm− 1 to 3000 cm− 1 (CH, CH2, and CH3 stretching vibrations) were also assigned to CS. For CS-PCL, the sharp peaks of hydroxyl (–OH) and ester (C = O) groups of PCL were registered at around 3444 cm− 1 and 1729 cm− 1, respectively [47]. Additionally, the signals at around 2939 cm− 1 to 2866 cm− 1 were attributed to O−CH3 and O−CH2, respectively [48]. The absorption peaks at 1575 cm− 1, 1368 cm− 1, and 1046 cm− 1 were indicators of C = O, C − N stretching, and C − O stretching vibrations in CS-PCL, respectively [49]. Regarding the CS-PCL-Ag sample, the new characteristic peaks did not reveal compared to the CS-PCL spectrum, except for several substitutions and shifts in the location of absorption peaks which approved the presence and encapsulation of Ag NPs in CS-PCL; for instance, a shift from 1575 cm− 1 to 1577 cm− 1 was due to successful entrapment of Ag NPs [50]. About the CS-PCL-Ag-NB nanocarrier, a peak at around 1728 cm− 1 was correlated with C = O stretching of NB, and the shifts in the location of intense peaks at around 2871 cm− 1 to 2957 cm− 1 could be owing to the C–H stretch of the NB drug.

FT-IR spectra of CS, CS-PCL, CS-PCL-Ag, and CS-PCL-Ag-NB samples showing the characteristic functional groups and confirming the successful formation of the nanocomposite. The peaks at around 3437 cm− 1 and 3337 cm− 1 were related to –NH2 and –OH groups stretching vibrations in the CS spectrum, respectively. Regarding the CS-PCL sample, the sharp peaks of hydroxyl (–OH) and ester (C = O) groups were assigned to PCL at around 3444 cm− 1 and 1729 cm− 1, respectively. The shifts in the location of the peak compared to the CS-PCL sample were indicators of Ag NPs entrapment in the nanocarrier

DLS and TEM analyses

The hydrodynamic size, monodispersity, and morphology of CS-based nanocarriers were determined using DLS and TEM analytical devices. For DLS analysis, the hydrodynamic diameter and polydispersity index (PDI) of CS-PCL, CS-PCL-Ag, and CS-PCL-Ag-NB samples was ascertained using DLS. Figure 2 depicts the results of this analysis. According to the results, the hydrodynamic diameter of the CS-PCL specimen was measured at about 262 nm, and a considerable increase in diameter was measured for CS-PCL-Ag, with about 296 nm. After the entrapment of NB, the diameter was enhanced by 300 nm as can be seen in Fig. 2. The size increase after Ag and NB entrapment could be due to the interaction of this therapeutics with the functional groups on the surface of the nanocarrier. The internalization of micro-/nanocarriers can be done through several pathways, including clathrin/caveolar-mediated endocytosis, macropinocytosis, phagocytosis, and pinocytosis. The clathrin-mediated endocytosis is desirable for the nanocarriers with a size of < 200 nm, while the carriers with a size range between 200 nm and 300 nm could be internalized through the caveolae-mediated endocytosis [51, 52]. Moreover, large-sized micro/nanocarriers (300 to 1000 nm) may internalize into target cells by phagocytosis and non-specific macropinocytosis. The pinocytosis pathway helps in the internalization of carriers with a smaller size compared with phagocytosis. Therefore, the fabricated nanocarrier could possibly internalize into cancer cells by caveolae-mediated endocytosis, phagocytosis, and non-specific micropinocytosis [51, 52]. Non-covalent bonds (such as electrostatic and hydrogen bonds) are the main element in interactions between the bioactive and CS-PCL. On the other hand, the TEM image of the CS-PCL-Ag-NB sample indicated a diameter between 100 nm and 200 nm and semi-globular morphology, which approves the appropriate size of the nanocarrier for DDS application. The PDI of samples was determined to be less than 0.2, which shows the monodispersity of nanocarriers [53, 54]. The spherical micro/nanocarriers have exhibited a faster endocytosis rate in cancer cells than non-spherical and tubular micro/nanocarriers. Non-spherical carriers could interact with macrophages and be detected by these immune system cells, which results in higher cytotoxicity on normal cells, especially immune cells, compared with spherical carriers. The weak endocytosis of non-spherical carriers results in prolonged circulation time in the body, increasing the chance of interaction with normal cells and causing cytotoxicity [55]. According to the reports, the size less than 200 nm is suitable for efficient drug delivery since it can easily penetrate cancer tissues due to the vasculature size of cancer tissue which is between 200 nm and 700 nm [53, 56].

DLS size distribution and TEM images of CS-PCL-Ag-NB nanoparticles illustrating the particle size distribution and morphological structure of the synthesized nanoparticles

TG analysis

TG analysis is an analytical assay for the investigation of the thermal stability, physical status, and drug content value of fabricated DDSs. This analysis can give weight loss of samples by the heating-cooling process [57, 58]. The TG analysis of d CS-PCL, CS-PCL-Ag, and CS-PCL-Ag-NB samples was done (Fig. 3). According to the results of this analysis indicated in Fig. 3, there were two weight loss stages for the CS-PCL sample while it was three stages for CS-PCL-Ag and CS-PCL-Ag-NB. The weight loss values of the CS-PCL sample were detected at 80 °C to 100 °C and 120 °C to 400 °C, with about 5% and 48%, respectively. The weight loss at around 80 °C to 100 °C is due to water evaporation in all TG curves. On the other hand, the main second weight loss of CS-PCL-Ag occurred at around 250 °C to 340 °C (80%) whereas it was registered at 300 °C to 450 °C (82%) for CS-PCL-Ag-NB. These considerable weight losses could be owing to the burning of elements of samples. On the other hand, by comparing the weight loss values of the drug-containing nanocarrier with the nanocarriers without drug, the difference between the weight loss values of these two samples is an indicator of the drug loading value. By considering the difference in total weight losses of CS-PCL and CS-PCL-Ag specimens, the Ag loading of CS-PCL-Ag was determined to be about 30%, and the weight loss difference of CS-PCL-Ag and CS-PCL-Ag-NB samples was an indicator of NB loading, which was about 8%. High level of drug content is crucial for DDSs and indicates the potency of nanocarrier for transporting a huge amount of bioactive to cancer tissues and cells which is desirable for effective cancer therapy [59]. TG analysis is also useful for determining the thermal stability of specimens and it can help for DDS production in down-stream processes.

TGA curves of CS-PCL, CS-PCL-Ag and CS-PCL-Ag-NB samples showing their thermal degradation behavior and weight loss (%) as a function of temperature. Three (for CS-PCL-Ag and CS-PCL-Ag-NB) and two (for CS-PCL) major weight losses were detected which shows the thermal stability of samples. As shown, 30% Ag content and 8% NB content were quantified for CS-PCL-Ag and CS-PCL-Ag-NB samples, respectively

Drug release profile

Figure 4 demonstrates in vitro NB release profile of CS-PCL-Ag-NB formulations and pure NB release in PBS solution at different pHs 7.4, 6.0, and 5.0. This release study was done between 0.5 h and 64 h. As can be seen, a controlled NB release manner was observed for the CS-PCL-Ag-NB sample that approves the slow and constant NB release from DDS over the determined period of time [49]. For instance, 46% of drug release was determined for pure NB after 2 h of incubation at pH 7.4 while it was about 5.6% for CS-PCL-Ag-NB formulations at the same circumstances which shows the impact of DDS in decreasing the NB release rate by 8 folds. Also, 1.6% and 16% releases were observed for pure NB and CS-PCL-Ag-NB samples after 1 h of incubation at pH 7.4. On the other hand, CS-PCL-Ag-NB had a fast release rate at acidic pHs which presents the pH-sensitive NB release of DDS. As the release rate of NB from CS-PCL-Ag-NB was about 39% after 3 h of incubation at pH 5.0 which was 1.5 folds and 3 folds faster than that of pH 6.0 and 7.4, respectively. The reason behind this fast release rate is the protonation of amino groups of CS nanocarrier, which results in opening of branches of CS and facilitates the release rate under acidic conditions. This issue is one of the most prominent factors in cancerous cells since early and late endosomes in cancer cells have acidic pH which can facilitate the fast drug release in cancer cells cytosol and induce cell death and apoptosis [60]. Besides, cancerous tissue due to its high metabolic rate and producing acidic metabolites possesses acidic pH which can raise the release rate of DDS [61, 62].

Cumulative sodium butyrate (NB) release profiles of pure NB and CS-PCL-Ag-NB nanoparticles at different pH conditions (7.4, 6.0, and 5.0). Pure NB was evaluated at pH 7.4, while CS-PCL-Ag-NB was tested at pH 7.4, 6.0, and 5.0. The acidic pH resulted in the protonation of CS-PCL the fast NB release which is desirable for fast drug release in cancer cells’ lysosomes. As can be seen, the controlled drug release manner was observed for the nanocarrier at a pH of 7.4. The pH of release medium for the pure NB was 7.4, physiologic conditions. MTT analysis

The MTT assay was used to study the toxic impact of CS-based DDSs, including CS, CS-PCL, CS-PCL-Ag, CS-PCL-Ag-NB, NB, and Ag, on A549 lung cancer cells and MSC normal cells (as control). Figure 5 depicts the cell viability values of these cells after treatment with various concentrations, including 25 nM, 50 nM, 100 nM, and 200 nM, after 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h of treatment. According to the results, it has been demonstrated that the CS and CS-PCL samples had higher biocompatibility and biodegradability than other samples. These two formulations indicated a concentration-dependent manner for their cell viability in both cancer and normal cells; for example, the cell viability values of cancer cells were enhanced from 83% to 94% after 245 h of treatment with 15 nM and 200 nM concentrations of CS. Also, the total biocompatibility of CS-PCL was higher than that of pure CS which verifies the positive effects of PCL in increasing the positive effect of biopolymers in DDS. Moreover, the biocompatibility of CS and CS-PCL samples in MSC cells was higher than that of A549 cells [16, 63]. On the other hand, CS-PCL-Ag, CS-PCL-Ag-NB, NB, and Ag samples had concentration-dependent cytotoxicity in both cells. As by raising the concentration of these formulations, the cell viability values were declined. Generally, the cytotoxicity of these samples was followed in the following order: CS-PCL-Ag-NB > NB > Ag > CS-PCL-Ag. To put in a more vivid picture, the CS-PCL-Ag-NB sample resulted in 27% and 13% cell viabilities after 24 h and 72 h treatment with 200 nM concentration in A549 cells. The cytotoxic effect of NB on cancer cells was considerably lower than that of the CS-PCL-Ag-NB sample. As CS-PCL-Ag-NB sample had about 2 folds to 3 folds higher cytotoxicity on cancer cells than NB in all three times of treatment at 200 nM concentration which expresses the effect of NPs-based biopolymers in the delivery of therapeutics on cancer cells [25]. Pure NB led to higher toxicity on MSC cells as well; however, the CS-PCL-Ag-NB sample had no remarkable negative effect on normal cells compared with NB. For example, cytotoxicity of NB was about two folds of CS-PCL-Ag-NB in MSC cells at 200 nM concentration. On the other hand, Ag and CS-PCL-Ag samples indicated a low toxic impact on cancer cells which verifies the cancer cell suppression capability of Ag NPs [30]. The IC50 values were obtained for the CS-PCL-Ag-NB sample at 39 nM, 36 nM, and 25 nM after 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h of incubation in A549 cells, respectively, while they were 200 nM, 167 nM, and 96 nM after 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h of treatment for the pure NB. According to the results, it has been demonstrated that the CS and CS-PCL samples had higher biocompatibility and biodegradability than other samples. These two formulations indicated what appears to be a concentration-dependent manner for their cell viability in both cancer and normal cells; for example, the cell viability values of cancer cells ranged from 83% to 94% after 24 h of treatment with 25 nM and 200 nM concentrations of CS. This slight, non-significant increase in viability at higher concentrations of the carrier materials (CS and CS-PCL) is not indicative of toxicity but rather confirms their excellent biocompatibility. It suggests that these polymeric nanoparticles provide a favorable substrate for cell growth and do not interfere with metabolic activity, as measured by the MTT assay. Such an increase is sometimes observed with highly biocompatible materials that may slightly enhance nutrient uptake or cell adhesion. Most importantly, the viability remains high (above 80%) across all concentrations, confirming the safety of the nanocarrier itself, which is a crucial prerequisite for a drug delivery system.

In the MTT assay figures (Fig. 5), the term “control” refers to untreated A549 lung cancer cells (and untreated MSC normal cells) that were cultured under identical conditions but without exposure to any of the fabricated samples (CS, CS-PCL, CS-PCL-Ag, CS-PCL-Ag-NB, NB, or Ag). The viability of these untreated cells was set to 100%, and the viability of all treated groups was calculated relative to this baseline. This is a standard practice in cytotoxicity assays to determine the baseline metabolic activity and proliferation of the cells

The results of this test were in accordance with the findings of research by Jusu et al. [64]. They developed poly lactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA)-PEG (polyethylene glycol)-PTX (paclitaxel)-LHRH (luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone) biopolymers for inhibiting the growth of breast cancer cells. According to their results, about 20% of breast cancer cell inhibition ability was measured after 24 h treatment with 0.5 mg/mL concentration of P-PEG-PTX-LHRH while it was more than 75% for A549 cells after treatment with 200 nM concentration in our research. They also indicated the high potency of biocompatibility biopolymers in the delivery of bioactive to cancer cells. The results of this test were in accordance with the findings of research that utilize similar polymeric platforms. For instance, studies have demonstrated the high potential of chitosan-based nanoparticles for delivering chemotherapeutic agents, showing significant cytotoxicity against various cancer cell lines while maintaining biocompatibility [15]. Furthermore, the enhanced efficacy of our CS-PCL-Ag-NB formulation compared to pure NB aligns with the well-established principle that encapsulating therapeutics in polymeric nanoparticles, including PCL-based carriers, improves their intracellular delivery and therapeutic index [15, 19, 23]. Specifically, the IC50 value of 39 nM for our multifunctional CS-PCL-Ag-NB system demonstrates a potent anticancer effect that is competitive with, and often superior to, other recently developed chitosan-based nanocarriers for lung cancer therapy, which often report IC50 values in the micromolar range for similar treatment durations [20–21]. This comparison underscores the advantage of our combinatorial approach, which leverages both Ag NPs and NB within a single, biocompatible CS-PCL carrier.

Gene expression analysis

The apoptotic gene expression ability of CS-PCL-Ag, CS-PCL-Ag-NB, and NB samples was studied on A549 cancer cells using the qRT-PCR technique. CS-PCL-Ag-NB and NB which indicated IC50 values in cancer cells applying MTT assay might affect the regulation and expression rate of Caspase9, Bax, and P53 genes in the cancer cells. Figure 6 exhibits the results of this test after treatment with 50 nM of CS-PCL-Ag, CS-PCL-Ag-NB, and NB formulations at 24 h of post-treatment. The Bax gene can affect the mitochondrial outer membrane and result in apoptosis cancer cell apoptosis, the Caspase9 gene (a family of cysteine proteases) mediates apoptosis by mitochondria-dependent cytochrome c release pathway, and P53 gene another major element in apoptosis pathways [65, 66]. According to the results, the expression levels of Caspase9, Bax, and P53 genes were increased by 2.1, 9.6, and 4.2 folds after treatment with CS-PCL-Ag, CS-PCL-Ag-NB, and NB, respectively which shows the higher ability of CS-PCL-Ag-NB in inducing apoptosis compared with NB. CS-PCL-Ag-NB nanocarrier had also a high effect on the expression levels of Bax and P53 genes; as 5.9 and 7.8 folds increases were quantified for Bax and P53 genes, respectively whereas it was only 2.1 (Bax) and 3.1 (P53) folds increase for A549 cell treated with NB. CS-PCL presented very low impact in enhancing the expression of these genes which further confirms the biocompatibility of CS-PCL nanocarrier [20, 26]. The results of this test were in line with the outputs of the MTT assay. The correlation between the expression of these three apoptosis genes is mentioned in the following. The P53 activates Bax in the early stages of apoptosis, and consequently, Bax induces the permeabilization of the mitochondrial membrane and the release of cytochrome-c which, in turn, promotes caspase-9 at the late stages [67]. This would be the main reason behind the low expression levels of Caspase-9.

Conclusion

In this work, we developed PCL-modified CS NPs for efficient co-delivery of Ag NPs and NB to A549 lung cancer cells. After synthesizing the CS-PCL-Ag-NB nanocarrier, various analytical devices, including FT-IR, DLS, TEM, and TGA, were used for its characterizing and determining its quality and quantity. The diameter of the sample was 100 nm to 200 nm. Approximately, 30% Ag and 8% NB contents ascertained for CS-PCL-Ag and CS-PCL-Ag-NB samples, respectively which are high values and suitable for the potential delivery of samples. Controlled and pH-sensitive drug releases were observed for CS-PCL-Ag-NB. Thereafter, the cell viability assay and qRT-PCR technique were applied for investigating the biocompatibility, cancer suppression ability, and apoptosis gene expression potency of fabricated samples. High biocompatibility obtained for CS and CS-PCL samples. CS-PCL-Ag-NB indicated higher cancer cell inhibition potency compared with pure NB while fabricated nanocarrier had very low toxicity on MSC normal cells. The expression levels of Caspase9, Bax, and P53 apoptotic genes were increased by several folds compared with control and NB in cancer cells which further approved the ability of CS-PCL-Ag-NB in inducing apoptosis in A549 cells. These data have demonstrated that CS-PCL-Ag-NB can be a potential and efficient DDS against lung cancer cells and even treatment of other cancer cells. While this study provides a strong proof-of-concept for the CS-PCL-Ag-NB nanoplatform in lung cancer therapy, we acknowledge that further comprehensive characterization and evaluation are necessary to fully establish its clinical potential. Future studies will focus on: (1) long-term colloidal stability studies under physiological conditions, (2) detailed hemocompatibility and plasma protein interaction assays to assess its behavior in the bloodstream, (3) investigation of cellular uptake mechanisms and intracellular trafficking using confocal microscopy, (4) evaluation of the formulation’s in vivo biodistribution, pharmacokinetics, and therapeutic efficacy in appropriate murine tumor models, and (5) a comprehensive safety and toxicity profile in animal models. These investigations are currently underway in our laboratory and will be the subject of future reports.

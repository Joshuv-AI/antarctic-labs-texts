Semi-Monotone Goldstein Line Search Strategy with Application in Sparse Recovery
Shima Shabani, Michael Breuß

Abstract
Line search methods are a prominent class of iterative methods to solve unconstrained minimization problems. These methods produce new iterates utilizing a suitable step size after determining proper directions for minimization. In this paper we propose a semi-monotone line search technique based on the Goldstein quotient for dealing with convex non-smooth optimization problems. The method allows to employ large step sizes away from the optimum thus improving the efficacy compared to standard Goldstein approach. For the presented line search method, we prove global convergence to a stationary point and local R-linear convergence rate in strongly convex cases. We report on some experiments in compressed sensing. By comparison with several state-of-the-art algorithms in the field, we demonstrate the competitive performance of the proposed approach and specifically its high efficiency.



1
Introduction

Line search methods are popular methods in unconstrained optimization
[
15
]
.
They typically have the format of an iterative method combining a direction
of the iteration with a suitable step size.
Effective choices of both direction and step size are key to success and robustness
of the line search approach.
Given a suitable direction, monotone line search methods aim to ensure that a descent
in the objective function takes place, while non-monotone line search
methods permit some growth in the function value, see e.g.
[
11
]
.
The non-monotone techniques represent a relaxation of monotone
line searching in a way that beneficial properties related
to monotone methods like global convergence may be preserved,
while they help to improve the chance of
finding a global optimum and in the convergence speed
[
23
]
.

To select a practical step size, it is helpful to consider
appropriate conditions to make sufficient decrease
of the objective function. Popular conditions as discussed e.g. in

[
15
]
are the conditions proposed by
Armijo
[
1
]
, Wolfe
[
21
]
, or Goldstein
[
10
]
.
As it is well-known, the Armijo condition
confirms the sufficient decrease for a given scheme even if the
step size is very small, which may result in the scheme being
inefficient. The Wolfe condition overcomes this problem by
considering additionally the local curvature, but this comes
at the expense of a considerably higher computational cost.
The Goldstein condition prevents too small step sizes
by making a possible lower bound explicit, which may
eventually lead to higher
computational efficiency compared to use of Wolfe condition.

As the setting for line search methods we consider in this paper
the unconstrained optimization problem

\begin{array}[]{ll}\min&~F(x)=f(x)+\mu c(x)\\
\textrm{s.t.}&~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{array}

(1)

where the function
f(x)
is convex and smooth, the regularization

c(x)
is a convex and non-smooth function, and the parameter

\mu\in\mathbb{R^{+}}
realizes a trade-off specification between the
objective terms.
One particular form of (
1
) that is of high interest
in some contexts is the
basis pursuit denoising

problem
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
, denoted as

\begin{array}[]{ll}\min&~F(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+\mu\|x\|_{1}\\
\textrm{s.t.}&~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{array}

(2)

Here
\|\cdot\|
is the standard Euclidean norm,

\|\cdot\|_{1}
stands for the
\ell_{1}
-norm, and

A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

(m\ll n)
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are
often called the measurement matrix and the observation vector, respectively.
The model problem (
2
) gives the sparsest solution of the
underdetermined linear system
Ax=b
when the measurements
are noisy, meaning that it is desired that almost all the coefficients
of the linear combination form are zero.
Compressed sensing
[
6
,
8
]
, a technique for efficiently acquiring
and reconstructing a signal, is an actual field of application for
(
2
).

Our contribution and relation to previous work.

We build upon recent work on
optimization methods relying on the Goldstein
quotient
[
9
]
.
The works
[
13
,
14
]
present an improved version of
the Goldstein technique similarly to our underlying
approach, but limited to smooth optimization problems.
With respect to non-smooth optimization,
the most important work related to this paper
is given by
[
17
]
.
There an efficient method utilizing a monotone
Goldstein technique has been deviced for dealing with the
basis pursuit denoising problem, proving global and
R-linear local convergence in that setting.

In the current paper, we propose a new extension of
technique proposed in
[
17
]

that enables relaxation towards larger step sizes during
minimization.
As the novel technique keeps the lower bound of the monotone
version of Goldstein’s condition at the same time, we denote it
here as semi-monotone Goldstein strategy.
For theoretical validation we prove global and
R-linear local convergence of the resulting line search method
in the setting of a general convex non-smooth regularization
function as in (
1
).
This is a considerably more general setting as in
previous work
[
17
]
, as convergence proofs given
there rely on a monotone Goldstein technique
and are also limited to the shrinkage operation
for regularization as in (
2
).
In addition to our new theoretical results we present
a detailed evaluation of randomized numerical experiments
that show the beneficial properties of our approach
compared to the scheme proposed in
[
17
]

as well as its competitive performance with
several state-of-the-art optimization methods.

2
Review of Important Techniques

We consider a general iterative method that seeks
the minimum of (
1
) and produces
to this end a sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
by the
line search rule.
In the following, the components of
line search iteration and related concepts
are briefly summarized for later use.

The basic line search scheme at an iteration index
k
reads as

x_{k+1}\,=\,x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k}

(3)

and it features the direction
d_{k}
and a positive step size
\alpha_{k}
.
For (
1
), the descent in the objective function in
direction
d_{k}
from current iterate
x_{k}
can be measured by

\Delta_{k}=d_{k}^{T}\nabla f_{k}+\mu\Big(c(x_{k}+d_{k})-c_{k}\Big)<0

(4)

As for the notation in (
4
), for a function like
h(x)
,

h_{k}
is the function value at
x_{k}
or
k
th iterate, and

\nabla h_{k}
is the corresponding gradient value.
The Goldstein condition for a non-smooth optimization strategy
can be summarized, using
0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<1
, as

F_{k}+\theta_{2}\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}\leq F_{k+1}\leq F_{k}+\theta_{1}\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}

(5)

In
[
17
]
an efficient monotone
line search technique for (
1
) is presented
in the sense of
Warth & Werner

[
19
]
.
This means, in each iteration, the step size
\alpha_{k}

is chosen such that the following quotient has a fixed
positive lower bound
\gamma

\Big(F_{k}-F_{k+1}\Big)\frac{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}{\Delta_{k}^{2}}>\gamma>0

(6)

for all
k\geq 0
with descent direction
d_{k}
as

d_{k}=x_{k}^{+}-x_{k},~~~~x_{k}^{+}=\mathscr{P}_{c}(x_{k}-\tau_{k}\nabla f(x_{k}),\mu\tau_{k})

(7)

Here

\mathscr{P}_{c}(x,\vartheta)=\textrm{argmin}_{y}\Big\{\vartheta c(y)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}\Big\}

(8)

is the proximal operator
[
16
]
of the scaled function
c(y)
at
x
with
\vartheta>0
,
and
\tau_{k}
is a dynamic parameter employed as a step size for the implicit gradient descent step such that

0<\tau_{\min}\leq\tau_{k}\leq\tau_{\max}<\infty

(9)

To estimate
\alpha_{k}
in (
3
), the authors
of
[
17
]
consider the non-smooth Goldstein quotient

\nu(\alpha_{k})=\frac{F_{k+1}-F_{k}}{\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}}

(10)

by imposing the following monotone sufficient descent condition

\nu(\alpha_{k})\left|\nu(\alpha_{k})-1\right|\,\geq\,\theta

(11)

for a fixed
\theta>0
.
Based on (
11
),
\nu(\alpha_{k})
is supposed to be
sufficiently positive and far from one to control
the step size from below.

3
Semi-Monotone Goldstein Strategy

As indicated, the idea presented in this paper extends the monotone
line search method in
[
17
]
to a semi-monotone case,
to use a larger board compared with (
5
) for
estimating
\alpha_{k}
and simultaneously controlling it from below.

Let us define
l(k)
as an integer satisfying
k-m(k)\leq l(k)\leq k
, in which
m(0)=0
and
0\leq m(k)\leq\min\{m(k-1)+1,N-1\}
, with
N>0
, to identify after a starting phase the maximum function value of
N
prior successful iterates in step
k
with corresponding index
l(k)
via

F_{l(k)}=\max\limits_{0\leq j\leq m(k)}\{F_{k-j}\}

(12)

We consider the convex combination of
F_{l(k)}
and current
function value
F_{k}
as

R_{k}=\eta_{k}F_{l(k)}+(1-\eta_{k})F_{k}

(13)

where
R_{k}\geq F_{k}
in this way, and we have

\eta_{k}\in[\eta_{\min},\eta_{\max}]
,
\eta_{\min}\in[0,1]
, and
\eta_{\max}\in[\eta_{\min},1]
.

As indicated, our aim is to keep the lower bound in (
5
)
but to relax the upper bound to tackle the non-monotone case.
To realize this we consider the estimate from (
13
) to obtain

F_{k}+\theta_{2}\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}\leq F_{k+1}\leq R_{k}+\theta_{1}\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}

(14)

Now, estimating
\alpha_{k}
, we define the relaxed version
of the non-smooth Goldstein quotient (
10
) as

\lambda(\alpha_{k})=\frac{F_{k+1}-R_{k}}{\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}}

(15)

Motivated by (
14
), we impose the following

semi-monotone sufficient descent
condition

\nu(\alpha_{k})\left|1-\lambda(\alpha_{k})\right|\,\geq\,\theta

(16)

using (
10
) and (
15
) to control the size
of
\alpha_{k}
, especially keeping it away from zero.

We will show that condition (
16
) guarantees a reasonable decrease for the objective function.
Regarding (
13
), by adopting the value for
\eta_{k}
, (
15
) allows to employ a non-monotone technique whenever iterates are far away from the optimum and a nearly monotone whenever iterates are close to the optimal value.

4
Convergence Analysis

In this section we discuss convergence properties of the presented line search method. Investigating convergence analysis, we assume

\nabla f(x)
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L>0
, i.e.,
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|

(17)

or equivalently

f(y)\leq f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{T}(y-x)+\frac{L}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}

(18)

4.1
Bounded Gain in the Target Function

The first theorem implies
efficiency
of the iterative
method that enforces a bounded gain to the target function
as described in (
6
).

Theorem 4.1

If for the descent direction
d_{k}
,
the value
\alpha_{k}
satisfies the sufficient descent condition (
16
) and in addition

F_{k+1}-R_{k}\geq-\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}L\|d_{k}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}

(19)

is satisfied, then for any step size
\alpha_{k}^{\prime}
with

F(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}^{\prime}d_{k})\leq F_{k+1}
we have

\Big(F_{k}-F(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}^{\prime}d_{k})\Big)\frac{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}{\Delta_{k}^{2}}\geq\frac{2\theta}{L}

(20)

Proof

Convexity of
c(x)
in (
1
)
results in

\begin{split}&-\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}L\|d_{k}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}\overset{\eqref{e:ass0}}{\leq}~F_{k+1}-R_{k}~\overset{\eqref{e:nm1}}{\leq}F_{k+1}-F_{k}\\
&\overset{\text{\eqref{e:defprob0}}}{=}f_{k+1}-f_{k}+\mu\big(c_{k+1}-c_{k}\big)\overset{\eqref{e:lip1}}{\leq}\alpha_{k}d_{k}^{T}\nabla f_{k}+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}L\|d_{k}\|^{2}+\mu\big(c_{k+1}-c_{k}\big)\\
&\underset{\text{c(x)}}{\overset{\text{Conv}}{\leq}}\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}L\|d_{k}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}d_{k}^{T}\nabla f_{k}+\mu\alpha_{k}\Big(c(x_{k}+d_{k})-c(x_{k})\Big)\overset{\text{\eqref{e:desdks0}}}{=}\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}L\|d_{k}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}\end{split}

(21)

Therefore, in total we can obtain

\left|F_{k+1}-R_{k}-\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}\right|\leq\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}L\|d_{k}\|^{2}

(22)

Making then use of (
15
) gives

\frac{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}{\left|\Delta_{k}\right|}\geq\frac{2}{\alpha_{k}L}\left|\frac{F_{k+1}-R_{k}-\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}\Delta_{k}}\right|=\frac{2}{\alpha_{k}L}\left|\lambda(\alpha_{k})-1\right|

(23)

We have
\Delta_{k}<0
in (
4
). Therefore by
definition of
\nu(\alpha_{k})
from (
10
)

\frac{F_{k}-F_{k+1}}{\left|\Delta_{k}\right|}=\frac{F_{k+1}-F_{k}}{\Delta_{k}}=\alpha_{k}\nu(\alpha_{k})

(24)

Taking the product of terms from (
23
) and
(
24
) and using (
16
) leads to the result. ∎

4.3
R-Linear Local Convergence

To investigate R-linear convergence, we first present some preliminaries. Notice that one can reformulate (
1
) as a smooth minimization problem over a closed convex set or epigraph of
c(x)

[
18
]
as

\begin{array}[]{ll}\min&~F(x)=f(x)+\mu\zeta\\
\textrm{s.t.}&~(x,\zeta)\in\Omega=\big\{(x,\zeta)\mid c(x)\leq\zeta\big\}\end{array}

(29)

So, the first optimality condition for a stationary point

is

\nabla f(x^{*})^{T}(x-x^{*})+\mu\big(\zeta-c(x^{*})\big)\geq 0,~~~\forall(x,\zeta)\in\Omega

(30)

since,
\zeta^{*}=c(x^{*})
.
We consider the generalized form of Lemma 2.1 in
[
20
]
as

Lemma 1

For the proximal operator (
7
), we have the following properties for all

P1.

\big(\mathscr{P}_{c}(x,\vartheta)-x\big)^{T}\big(y-\mathscr{P}_{c}(x,\vartheta)\big)+\vartheta\big(\zeta-c(\mathscr{P}_{c}(x,\vartheta))\big)\geq 0
,
\forall

(y,\zeta)\in\Omega

P2.

\big(\mathscr{P}_{c}(x,\vartheta)-\mathscr{P}_{c}(y,\vartheta)\big)^{T}\big(x-y\big)\geq\|\mathscr{P}_{c}(x,\vartheta)-\mathscr{P}_{c}(y,\vartheta)\|^{2}

P3.

\|\mathscr{P}_{c}(x,\vartheta)-\mathscr{P}_{c}(y,\vartheta)\|\leq\|x-y\|

Proof

P1.

Considering unconstrained nonsmooth minimization problem in (
8

P2.

Replacing

\big(\mathscr{P}_{c}(x,\vartheta)-x\big)^{T}\big(\mathscr{P}_{c}(y,\vartheta)-\mathscr{P}_{c}(x,\vartheta)\big)+\vartheta\big(c\big(\mathscr{P}_{c}(y,\vartheta)\big)-c\big(\mathscr{P}_{c}(x,\vartheta)\big)\big)\geq 0

We obtain a similar inequality if

P3.

Apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (CSI)

∎

Conclusion on Experiments

Complementing the study
of quality with the results on computational efficiency,
one may interpret them as follows. The
high efficiency and robustness properties
of
ISGA
/
smISGA
may have in some experiments
of medium to high difficulty the price of resulting
in higher relative error compared to other methods.
In turn, while the computational effort of the other tested methods
is higher in general, in some experiments of medium to high difficulty
the effort they take for minimization may grow
substantially, which may deteriorate efficiency.
Comparing
smISGA
with the technically related
ISGA

method that is based on the monotone Goldstein technique,
we achieve with the new
smISGA
method a considerably
higher quality at the price of a slightly higher
computational load in some problems.

Conclusion

We have proposed and investigated a new semi-monotone
Goldstein technique useful for compressed sensing and related problems.
We have validated experimentally that the method is a useful
extension of a previous monotone Goldstein technique.
In future work we strive to investigate the theoretical
complexity of the approach, and we aim to tackle
other important optimization problems as the methodology
introduced here is not limited to
basis pursuit denoising.

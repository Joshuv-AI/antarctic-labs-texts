The Goresky-Hingston Coproduct in Morse Homology with DG Coefficients
Jonathan Clivio

Abstract
. We describe the Goresky-Hingston coproduct on the free loop space with real coefficients via the quasi-isomorphism C ∗ ​ ( Λ ​ M ) ≃ C ∗ ​ ( M , C ∗ ​ ( Ω ​ M ) ) C_{*}(\Lambda M)\simeq C_{*}(M,C_{*}(\Omega M)) . This lets us describe the coproduct on the Leray-Serre spectral sequence as the diagonal on the manifold and the coproduct on the based loop space. With this description, we compute the coproduct with real coefficients for manifolds of the form M = S n / G M=S^{n}/G . The appendix contains results on inverting morphisms of 𝒜 ∞ \mathcal{A}_{\infty} -modules that are required for our computations.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we give a description of the Goresky-Hingston coproduct in real coefficients

\displaystyle\nu_{GH}\colon H_{*}(\Lambda M,M)\to H_{1-n+*}((\Lambda M,M)^{2})

via the framework of Morse homology with differential graded coefficients as defined in
[
barraud2023morse
]
. Here,
\Lambda M
denotes the free loop space of a smooth manifold
M
of dimension
n
. It contains
M
as the constant loops.

The Goresky-Hingston coproduct or string topology coproduct is a coproduct on
H_{*}(\Lambda M,M)
of degree
1-n
, introduced by Goresky and Hingston in
[
goresky2009loop
]
and Sullivan in
[
sullivan2004open
]
.

Morse homology with DG coefficients gives a quasi-isomorphism

\displaystyle C_{*}(M,C_{*}(\Omega M,x_{0}))\simeq C_{*}(\Lambda M,M),

where
C_{*}(M,C_{*}(\Omega M,x_{0}))
denotes Morse homology with DG coefficients in the
C_{*}(\Omega M)
-module
C_{*}(\Omega M,x_{0})
. We use the extension of this formalism by Riegel in
[
riegel2024chain
]
. He proves that
(\eta_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, a morphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-modules over
C_{*}(\Omega M)
of the coefficients, induces
\widetilde{\eta}
, a morphism on Morse homology with DG coefficients.

This allows us to prove the following:

Theorem A

(Theorem
8.4
)
.

Let
(M,x_{0})
be a smooth, pointed manifold of dimension
n
. There exists a morphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-modules over
C_{*}(\Omega M)

\displaystyle(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 1}\colon C_{*}(\Omega M,x_{0};\mathbb{R})\to C_{1-n+*}((\Omega M,x_{0})^{2};\mathbb{R}),

where
\nu_{1}
computes the Goresky-Hingston coproduct on the based loop space.

Moreover for the induced map
\widetilde{\nu}
, it holds that

\displaystyle C_{*}(\Lambda M,M;\mathbb{R})\simeq C_{*}(M,C_{*}(\Omega M,x_{0};\mathbb{R}))

\displaystyle\overset{\widetilde{\nu}}{\to}C_{*}(M,C_{1-n+*}((\Omega M,x_{0})^{2};\mathbb{R}))

\displaystyle\overset{\Delta_{*}}{\to}C_{*}(M\times M,C_{1-n+*}((\Omega M,x_{0})^{2};\mathbb{R}))

\displaystyle\simeq C_{1-n+*}((\Lambda M,M)^{2};\mathbb{R})

computes the Goresky-Hingston coproduct on homology of the free loop space.

An immediate corollary of this theorem is a description of the Goresky-Hingston coproduct in the Leray-Serre.

Theorem B

(Corollary
8.5
)
.

The Goresky-Hingston coproduct is computed on the second page of the Leray-Serre spectral sequence
E_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p}(M,H_{q}(\Omega M,x_{0}))
with real coefficients as the following composition

\displaystyle H_{p}(M,H_{q}(\Omega M,x_{0};\mathbb{R}))

\displaystyle\overset{\nu_{1}}{\to}H_{p}(M,H_{q+1-n}((\Omega M,x_{0})^{2};\mathbb{R}))\overset{\Delta_{*}}{\to}H_{p}(M\times M,H_{q+1-n}((\Omega M,x_{0})^{2};\mathbb{R})).

Dually, the dual Goresky-Hingston cohomology product is computed by first taking the cup product with local coefficients in
H_{q+1-n}((\Omega M,x_{0})^{2})
and then computing the cohomology product on the based loop space.

The coproduct on the
E^{2}
-page lets us describe the full coproduct in certain cases. We study in Subsection
8.2
the case
M=S^{n}/G
for a finite group
G
acting freely on
S^{n}
:

Theorem C

(Theorem
8.8
)
.

Let
G
be a non-trivial, finite group acting freely and orientation-preserving on
S^{n}
for
n>1
. The relative homology of the free loop space of
M=S^{n}/G
with real coefficients is given by

\displaystyle H_{*}(\Lambda M;\mathbb{R})\cong

\displaystyle\ \bigoplus_{[g]\in\mathrm{ccl}(G)}\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{R}x_{[g],k}\oplus\mathbb{R}y_{[g],k}

with
x_{[g],k}
in degree
k(n-1)
and
y_{[g],k}
in degree
k(n-1)+n
. The homology
H_{*}(\Lambda M,M;\mathbb{R})
has the same description except missing the
[g]=[1]
and
k=0
summand.

The Goresky-Hingston coproduct lifts to a map

\displaystyle\widehat{\lor}\colon H_{*}(\Lambda M;\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\to H_{*}(\Lambda M;\mathbb{R})\otimes H_{*}(\Lambda M;\mathbb{R})

computed by

\displaystyle\widehat{\lor}(x_{[g],k})=\sum_{i+j=k-1}\sum_{h\in G}x_{[gh^{-1}],i}\otimes x_{[h],j}

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\lor}(y_{[g],k})=\sum_{i+j=k-1}\sum_{h\in G}(x_{[gh^{-1}],i}\otimes y_{[h],j}+y_{[gh^{-1}],i}\otimes x_{[h],j}).

However in general, the coproduct on the
E^{2}
-page does not capture all information. For example for
\Sigma_{g}
a surface of genus
g\geq 2
, the coproduct on the
E^{2}
-page is trivial but not on
H_{*}(\Lambda\Sigma_{g},\Sigma_{g})
(see Example
8.7
).

Moreover on the
E^{2}
-page of the Leray-Serre spectral sequence, we have

\displaystyle\nu^{N}\circ\Lambda f=(\Lambda f\times\Lambda f)\circ\nu^{M}

for
f
a degree
1
map (see Corollary
8.10
). This is in contrast to the Goresky-Hingston coproduct with integer coefficients which does not commute in general with maps that are not simple homotopies (see
[
naef2021string
,
naef2024simple
,
wahl2019invariance
,
kenigsberg2024obstructions
]
). It is an open question where the loss of information appears: while passing from integer coefficients to real coefficients or while passing to maps of spectral sequences (see Remark
8.11
).

1.1.
The Proof Strategy

The main technical goal is to exhibit the Goresky-Hingston coproduct as a morphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-modules over
C_{*}(\Omega M)
. Morally, we want to prove
\Omega M
-equivariance of the coproduct in a coherent way.

The Goresky-Hingston coproduct on a class
\alpha\in H_{*}(\Omega,x_{0})
is computed as follows:

1.

multiply with the unit interval
I:=[0,1]
and obtain a class
I\times\alpha\in H_{*}(I\times\Omega,I\times\{x_{0}\}\cup\partial I\times\Omega)
;

2.

“intersect”

I\times\alpha
with the subspace of loops with self-intersections:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\Omega M:=\{(t,\gamma)\in I\times\Omega M\mid\gamma(t)=\gamma(0)\};

3.

cut using the map
\mathcal{F}\Omega M\to\Omega M\times\Omega M,(t,\gamma)\mapsto(\gamma|_{[0,t]},\gamma_{[t,1]})
.

While steps (
1
) and (
3
) are straight-forward to make equivariant, step (
2
) needs more work.

We follow the description in
[
hingston2017product
]
. In this setup,
“intersecting”
as in (
2
) is done by a cap product

\displaystyle e_{\Omega}^{*}\tau_{x_{0}}\cap\colon C_{*}(e_{\Omega}^{-1}(U_{x_{0}}),I\times\{x_{0}\}\cup\partial I\times\Omega\cup e_{\Omega}^{-1}(V_{x_{0}}^{c}))\to C_{-n+*}(e^{-1}_{\Omega}(U_{x_{0}}),I\times\{x_{0}\}\cup\partial I\times\Omega M).

Here
x_{0}\in V_{x_{0}}\subsetneq U_{x_{0}}\subseteq M
denotes open balls around
x_{0}
,
e_{\Omega}\colon I\times\Omega\to M
denotes the evaluation at time
t
and
\tau_{x_{0}}\in C^{n}(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c})
is a local orientation at
x_{0}
. We explain this in further detail in Subsection
2.2
.

The guiding question for our article thus is the following:

Question 1.1
.

Does there exist a morphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-modules

(1)

\displaystyle(q_{k})_{k\geq 1}\colon C_{*}(e_{\Omega}^{-1}(U_{x_{0}}),I\times\{x_{0}\}\cup\partial I\times\Omega M\cup e_{\Omega}^{-1}(V_{x_{0}}^{c}))\to C_{-n+*}(e_{\Omega}^{-1}(U_{x_{0}}),I\times\{x_{0}\}\cup\partial I\times\Omega M)

over
C_{*}(\Omega M)
with
q_{1}=e_{\Omega}^{*}\tau_{\Omega}\cap
?

To make sense of this, we need to specify a
\Omega M
-action on all the spaces appearing in (
1
). For this, we construct a
\Omega M
-action on itself that is homotopic to conjugation such that, when we extend it trivially to an action on
I\times\Omega M
, it sends the subspaces
e_{\Omega}^{-1}(U_{x_{0}})
and
e_{\Omega}^{-1}(V_{x_{0}}^{c})
to themselves.

To construct such an action we define the notion of a
transitive diffeomorphism lift
in Section
3
. The idea is that we define for any path
\lambda\colon[0,a]\to M
a diffeomorphism

\displaystyle\Theta(\lambda)\colon M\cong M

that

(i)

sends
\lambda(0)
to
\lambda(a)
;

(ii)

works well with composition of paths;

(iii)

sends
U_{x_{0}}
and
V_{x_{0}}^{c}
to themselves if
\lambda\in\Omega M
.

This defines

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\Omega M\times\Omega M

\displaystyle\to\Omega M,

\displaystyle(\gamma,\lambda)

\displaystyle\mapsto\Theta(\lambda)_{*}(\gamma),

the desired
\Omega M
-action on itself making Question
1.1
well-posed.

Producing a morphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-modules as in Question
1.1
comes down to finding (higher) homotopies between

\displaystyle e_{\Omega}^{*}\tau_{x_{0}}\cap(\Phi(\alpha\times\lambda))

and

\displaystyle\Phi((e_{\Omega}^{*}\tau_{x_{0}}\cap\alpha)\times\lambda).

The transitive diffeomorphism lift
\Theta
defines a right
\Omega M
-action

\displaystyle\rho\colon U_{x_{0}}\times\Omega M

\displaystyle\to U_{x_{0}},

\displaystyle(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle\mapsto\Theta(\lambda)(x).

We show in Lemma
4.3
(
a
) that

\displaystyle e_{\Omega}^{*}\tau_{x_{0}}\cap(\Phi(\alpha\times\lambda))=((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\rho(e_{\Omega}\times\mathrm{id}))\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda).

In Section
7
, we construct a local orientation in
\tau_{x_{0}}\in C^{n}(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c};\mathbb{R})\cong C^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n},B_{1}(0)^{c})
that is invariant under the
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-action. We can homotope
\rho
to an action
\rho^{\prime}
that factors through
\mathrm{SO}(n)
.

In Lemma
4.3
(
b
), we show that for such
\tau_{x_{0}}
and
\rho^{\prime}
it holds

\displaystyle((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\rho^{\prime}(e_{\Omega}\times\mathrm{id}))\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda)=\Phi((e_{\Omega}^{*}\tau_{x_{0}}\cap\alpha)\times\lambda).

Therefore answering Question
1.1
comes down to finding (higher) homotopies between

\displaystyle((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\rho(e_{\Omega}\times\mathrm{id}))\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda)

and

\displaystyle((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\rho^{\prime}(e_{\Omega}\times\mathrm{id}))\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda).

We can do this by constructing explicit (higher) homotopies between
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
.

This in particular gives a positive answer to Question
1.1
and lets us exhibit the Goresky-Hingston coproduct as a morphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-morphism:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nu}\colon C_{*}(\Omega M,x_{0};\mathbb{R})\to C_{1-n+*}((\Omega M,x_{0})^{2};\mathbb{R}).

Using Riegel’s framework, we get the map

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nu}\colon C_{*}(M,C_{*}(\Omega M,x_{0};\mathbb{R}))\to C_{*}(M,C_{1-n+*}((\Omega M,x_{0})^{2};\mathbb{R})).

To prove that this map indeed exhibits the Goresky-Hingston coproduct on the free loop space, we introduce
coherent chain homotopies
. This is the algebraic part isolated from Riegel’s work
[
riegel2024chain
]
that lets us show that a map on the total space of a fibration is exhibited by a map in Morse homology with DG coefficients. We define and study these coherent chain homotopies in Section
5
.

An analogous construction to the morphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-modules
\boldsymbol{\nu}
gives a coherent chain homotopy for the Goresky-Hingston coproduct. This lets us prove our main theorem in Section
8
.

Organisation of the Paper.

In Section
2
, we recall the necessary background on Morse homology with DG coefficients, fix the definition of the Goresky-Hingston coproduct of this article and specify our sign conventions. In Section
3
, we define and construct transitive diffeomorphism lifts. In Section
4
, we exhibit the Goresky-Hingston coproduct as a morphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-modules. In Section
5
, we define and study coherent chain homotopies. In Section
6
, we define orthogonal half-constant embeddings and explain how they play an analogous role to
\mathrm{SO}(n)
for constructing a coherent chain homotopy. In Section
7
, we construct a Thom class which is invariant under certain maps using the Mathai-Quillen construction. In Section
8
, we construct a coherent chain homotopy for the Goresky-Hingston coproduct and using this prove the main theorem. Finally, the appendix contains a section about inverting morphisms of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-modules and a section about homotopies of pointed embeddings of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
relative to
B_{1}(0)^{c}
.

Conventions.

Throughout this article
M
denotes a smooth, closed, oriented manifold of dimension
n
with a Riemannian metric. The Riemannian metric induces the Riemannian distance, a metric on
M
. We denote it by
d_{M}\colon M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
. We fix a basepoint
x_{0}\in M
.

For
A,B
smooth manifolds, we denote
\mathrm{Emb}(A,B)
the space of smooth embeddings.

The free loop space on
M
is defined as

\displaystyle\Lambda:=\Lambda M:=\{\gamma\colon[0,a]\to M\mid a\geq 0,\gamma(0)=\gamma(a),\gamma\text{ is piecewise smooth}\}

and the based loop space is defined as

\displaystyle\Omega:=\Omega M:=\{\gamma\colon[0,a]\to M\mid a\geq 0,\gamma(0)=\gamma(a)=x_{0},\gamma\text{ is piecewise smooth}\}.

We denote
\mu\colon\Omega M\times\Omega M\to\Omega M
for the composition.

The path space on a smooth manifold
X
is denoted by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}X:=\{\gamma\colon[0,a]\to X\mid\gamma\text{ is piecewise smooth}\}

and for subspaces
A,B\subseteq X
, we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{A\to B}X:=\{(\gamma\colon[0,a]\to X)\in\mathcal{P}X\mid\gamma(0)\in A,\gamma(a)\in B\}.

In this article, we work with cubical chains. A cubical chain
\sigma\colon I^{k}\to X
is degenerate if there exists
1\leq i\leq k
such that

\displaystyle\sigma(v_{1},\dots,v_{i},\dots,v_{k})=\sigma(v_{1},\dots,v_{i}^{\prime},\dots,v_{k})

for all
v_{1},\dots,v_{k},v_{i}^{\prime}\in I
.

We denote by
C_{k}(X)
the real cubical chains on a topological space. It is the real vector space generated by continuous maps
\sigma\colon I^{k}\to X
relative to the subspace generated by all degenerate chains.

2.
Background

In this section, we recall the machinery of Morse homology with DG coefficients as introduced in
[
barraud2023morse
]
. Moreover, we recall the definition of the Goresky-Hingston coproduct on the free loop space and based loop space of a manifold as first introduced in
[
goresky2009loop
]
and
[
sullivan2004open
]
. Finally in this section, we fix the sign conventions we use in this article for maps of chain complexes which do not preserve degree.

2.1.
Morse Homology with DG Coefficients

In
[
barraud2023morse
]
, Barraud, Damian, Humilière and Oancea describe the framework of Morse homology with differential graded coefficients. We recall in this subsection the parts we need in this article. In particular for a fibration
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

we construct a complex
C_{*}(M,C_{*}(F))
with a quasi-isomorphism

\displaystyle\Psi\colon C_{*}(M,C_{*}(F))\to C_{*}(E).

2.1.1.
Data and Choices

For this, we choose the following data:

•

a basepoint
x_{0}\in M
;

•

a Morse-Smale pair
(f,\xi)
on
M
;

•

an orientation on the unstable manifolds
W^{u}(x)
of
(f,\xi)
;

•

a tree
T\subseteq M
containing
x_{0}
and the critical points of
f
;

•

a homotopy inverse
\theta
to the projection
p\colon M\to M/T
;

•

a representing chain system
\{s_{x,y}\}
(see Definition
2.1
);

•

a compatible representing system
\{s_{x}\}
(see Definition
2.3
);

•

a transitive lifting function
\Phi\colon E\,{}_{\pi}\!\times_{e_{0}}\mathcal{P}M\to E
(see Definition
2.4
).

For any pair of critical points
x,y\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)
, denote the space of Morse trajectories of
\xi
between
x
and
y
by
\mathcal{L}(x,y)
and define the
moduli space of broken Morse trajectories

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y):={\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\cup\bigcup_{z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)}\mathcal{L}(x,z_{1})\times\mathcal{L}(z_{1},z_{2})\times\dots\times\mathcal{L}(z_{k},y).

The space
\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)
admits the structure of a manifold with corners of dimension
|x|-|y|-1
with interior
\mathcal{L}(x,y)
(see e.g.
[
latour1994existence
, Proposition 2.15]
). Moreover, the orientation on the unstable manifolds induce an orientation
[\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)]\in H_{|x|-|y|-1}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y),\partial\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y))
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
representing chain system
of the moduli space of broken Morse trajectories is a collection
\{s_{x,y}\in C_{|x|-|y|-1}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y))\mid x,y\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)\}
such that

(i)

s_{x,y}
represents a cycle in
C_{|x|-|y|-1}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y),\partial\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y))
and
[s_{x,y}]=[\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)]
in homology;

(ii)

the following equation holds

\displaystyle ds_{x,y}=\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)}(-1)^{|x|-|z|}s_{x,z}\times s_{z,y}.

A representing chain system always exists
[
barraud2023morse
, Proposition 5.2.6]
.

Following
[
barraud2023morse
, Lemma 5.2.10]
we denote
\Gamma\colon\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\to\mathcal{P}_{x\to y}M
the map that sends a trajectory to the path from
x
to
y
parametrised by
f
. We note that this is well-defined as this parametrisation produces smooth paths. We set

\displaystyle m_{x,y}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=p_{*}\circ\Gamma_{*}(s_{x,y})\in C_{|x|-|y|-1}(\Omega(M/T)),

\displaystyle m_{x,y}

\displaystyle:=\theta_{*}(m_{x,y}^{\prime})\in C_{|x|-|y|-1}(\Omega M).

By
[
barraud2023morse
, Definition 5.2.11]
, the collection
\{m_{x,y}\mid x,y\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)\}
is a
twisting cocycle
meaning that they satisfy the equation

\displaystyle d(m_{x,y})=\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)}(-1)^{|x|-|z|}\mu(m_{x,z}\otimes m_{z,y}).

Definition 2.2
.

For
x\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)
, the
Latour cell
is defined as

\displaystyle\overline{W^{u}}(x):=W^{u}(x)\cup\bigcup_{y\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)}\overline{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\times W^{u}(y).

The natural topology on the Latour cell makes it into a compact manifold with corners which is homeomorphic to the disk
D^{|x|}
(see
[
latour1994existence
, Proposition 2.11]
).

Definition 2.3
.

A
compatible representing chain system
is a collection
\{s_{x}\in C_{|x|}(\overline{W^{u}}(x))\mid x\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)\}
such that

(i)

s_{x}
represents a cycle in
C_{|x|}(\overline{W^{u}}(x),\partial\overline{W^{u}}(x))
and
[s_{x}]=[\overline{W^{u}}(x)]
;

(ii)

the following equation holds

\displaystyle ds_{x}=\sum_{x\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)}s_{x}\times s_{x,y}.

A compatible representing chain system always exists
[
barraud2023morse
, Lemma 7.3.2]
. We denote
m_{x}^{\prime}:=p_{*}\circ\Gamma_{*}(s_{x})\in C_{|x|}(\Omega M/T)
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
transitive lifting function
is a map

\displaystyle\Phi\colon E\,{}_{\pi}\!\times_{e_{0}}\mathcal{P}M\to E

such that

(i)

\pi\circ\Phi=e_{1}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{2}
;

(ii)

for all
e\in E
, we have
\Phi(e,c_{\pi(e)})=e
, where
c_{\pi(e)}\in\mathcal{P}M
is the constant path at
\pi(e)\in M
;

(iii)

for all
e\in E
and composable
\gamma,\delta\in\mathcal{P}M
with
\gamma(0)=\pi(e)
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\Phi(e,\gamma),\delta)=\Phi(e,\gamma\cdot\delta).

Any fibration is fibre homotopy equivalent to a fibration that admits a transitive lifting function
[
dyer1969some
, Proposition 5.5]
.

A transitive lifting function in particular defines a right
\Omega M
-action on
F
.

2.1.2.
Construction

We now describe the complex
C_{*}(M,C_{*}(F))
and the map
\Psi\colon C_{*}(M,C_{*}(F))\to C_{*}(E)
: the complex
C_{*}(M,C_{*}(F))
has underlying graded abelian group

\displaystyle C_{*}(F)\otimes\mathbb{Z}\mathrm{Crit}(f)

with differential given by

\displaystyle d_{F}(\alpha\otimes x)

\displaystyle=d\alpha+(-1)^{|\alpha|}\sum_{y\in\mathrm{Crit}(f)}\Phi_{*}(\alpha\otimes m_{x,y})\otimes y.

We denote the degree
-1
map

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{m}\colon\mathbb{Z}\mathrm{Crit}(f)

\displaystyle\to C_{*}(\Omega M)\otimes\mathbb{Z}\mathrm{Crit}(f),

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{y}m_{x,y}\otimes y.

Then the differential can be written as

\displaystyle d_{F}=d\otimes\mathrm{id}+(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\boldsymbol{m})

and the above sign
(-1)^{|\alpha|}
comes from a Koszul sign.

For the map
\Psi\colon C_{*}(M,C_{*}(F))\to C_{*}(E)
, we consider the fibration
\pi^{\prime}\colon E^{\prime}\to M/T
given by the pullback of
\pi\colon E\to M
along
\theta\colon M/T\to M
. We can assume that
\theta
is a homeomorphism. Thus
\Phi
defines also a transitive lifting function on
\pi^{\prime}\colon E^{\prime}\to M/T
. This also induces a construction for
C_{*}(M/T,C_{*}(F))
. The differential on
C_{*}(M/T,C_{*}(F))
is given by the action on
C_{*}(F)
by
\Phi(\alpha\otimes m_{x,y}^{\prime}):=\Phi(\alpha\otimes\theta_{*}(m_{x,y}^{\prime}))=\Phi(\alpha\otimes m_{x,y})
.

The map
\Psi
is given by the composition

\displaystyle C_{*}(M,C_{*}(F))\to C_{*}(M/T,C_{*}(F))\overset{\Psi^{\prime}}{\to}C_{*}(E^{\prime})\to C_{*}(E).

The first map is an isomorphism. The third map above is given the homotopy equivalence
\theta
and thus is a quasi-isomorphism. Thus the only map of interest is the middle map
\Psi^{\prime}
. This is given by

\displaystyle=((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ e_{\Omega})\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta_{*}(\Phi(\alpha\otimes\lambda))

\displaystyle=((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ e_{\Omega}\circ\Phi)\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:Theta0 and Phi commute}}{=}((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\eta\circ(e_{\Omega}\times\Theta_{0}))\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda).

For (
b
), we set
a:=|\alpha|
and
l:=|\lambda|
. We compute:

\displaystyle\Phi\circ(q_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\alpha\otimes\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Phi(((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ e_{\Omega})\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta_{*})\otimes\mathrm{id})(\alpha\otimes\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Phi\left(\left(\sum_{J\sqcup K=\{1,\dots a\}}\mathrm{sgn}(J,K)\tau_{x_{0}}(e_{\Omega}(\alpha|_{I^{J}\times\{0\}^{K}}))\alpha|_{\{1\}^{J}\times I^{K}}\right)\otimes\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\sqcup K=\{1,\dots a+l\}\\
J\subseteq\{1,\dots,a\}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{sgn}(J,K)\tau_{x_{0}}(e_{\Omega}(\mathrm{pr}_{1}((\alpha\times\lambda)|_{I^{J}\times\{0\}^{K}})))(\Phi(\alpha\otimes\lambda))|_{\{1\}^{J}\times I^{K}}.

We now note that

is a degenerate chain if

. We can thus drop the condition

in the sum because the added terms are zero. We therefore find

\displaystyle\Phi\circ(q_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\alpha\otimes\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\sqcup K=\{1,\dots a+l\}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{sgn}(J,K)\tau_{x_{0}}(e_{\Omega}(\mathrm{pr}_{1}((\alpha\times\lambda)|_{I^{J}\times\{0\}^{K}})))(\Phi(\alpha\otimes\lambda))|_{\{1\}^{J}\times I^{K}}

\displaystyle=(\left(\tau_{x_{0}}\circ e_{\Omega}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{1}\right)\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda)

We moreover have

\displaystyle(\left(\tau_{x_{0}}\circ e_{\Omega}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{1}\right)\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\left(\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{1}\circ(e_{\Omega}\times r\Theta_{0})\right)\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda).

By our assumption on

as in (

lands in

, we find

\displaystyle(\left(\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{1}\circ(e_{\Omega}\times r\Theta_{0})\right)\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\left(\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\eta\circ(e_{\Omega}\times r\Theta_{0})\right)\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda)

which is what we wanted to show.
∎

4.2.
Higher Homotopies of Embeddings

We now construct a homotopy
H_{1}
between the identity and the retraction

. As explained in Remark

, this lets us construct

. Finally, composing the homotopy

produces the higher maps

Lemma 4.6
.

There exists a homotopy

\displaystyle H_{1}\colon I\times\mathrm{Emb}_{*}^{+}((U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}),(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}))\to\mathrm{Emb}_{*}^{+}((U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}),(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}))

such that, for all
\varphi,\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathrm{Emb}_{*}^{+}((U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}),(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}))
and
t\in I
, it holds

(i)

H_{1}(0,\varphi)=:r(\varphi)\in\mathrm{SOEmb}(U_{x_{0}},U_{x_{0}})
;

(ii)

H_{1}(1,\varphi)=\varphi
;

(iii)

H_{1}(t,r(\varphi_{1})\circ\varphi_{2})=r(\varphi_{1})\circ H_{1}(t,\varphi_{2})
.

Proof.

We fix some orthonormal coordinates on
U_{x_{0}}
. We may thus identify
U_{x_{0}}\cong B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|x\|=d_{M}(x,x_{0})
. We then define

\displaystyle\alpha\colon(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c})

\displaystyle\cong(\mathbb{R}^{n},B_{1}(0)^{c}),

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{x}{\varepsilon-\|x\|}.

This in particular identifies
\mathrm{Emb}_{*}^{+}((U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}),(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}))
with
\mathrm{Emb}_{*}^{+}((\mathbb{R}^{n},B_{1}(0)^{c}),((\mathbb{R}^{n},B_{1}(0)^{c}))
and their respective subspaces
\mathrm{SOEmb}(U_{x_{0}},U_{x_{0}})
and
\mathrm{SO}(n)
.

In Proposition
, we define a homotopy
H
that exhibits
\mathrm{SO}(n)
as a deformation retract of
\mathrm{Emb}_{*}^{+}((\mathbb{R}^{n},B_{1}(0)^{c}),((\mathbb{R}^{n},B_{1}(0)^{c}))
and commutes with left multiplication of
\mathrm{SO}(n)
. The map

\displaystyle H_{1}(t,\varphi):=\alpha^{-1}\circ H(t,\varphi\circ\alpha)

defines the desired homotopy. The invariance of

under left
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-multiplication moreover shows that

is independent of our choice of
\alpha
, because two different choices of

differ by an element in

.
∎

We denote the corresponding chain homotopy by

\displaystyle h_{1}\colon C_{*}(\mathrm{Emb}_{*}^{+}((U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}),(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c})))

\displaystyle\to C_{1+*}(\mathrm{Emb}_{*}^{+}((U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}),(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}))),

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto H_{1}(I\times\varphi).

We define inductively for

\displaystyle H_{k}\colon I^{k}\times\mathrm{Emb}^{+}_{*}((U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}),(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}))^{\times k}

\displaystyle(t_{1},\dots,t_{k},\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k})

\displaystyle\mapsto H_{1}(t_{1},\mu(\varphi_{1},H_{k-1}(t_{2},\dots,t_{k},\varphi_{2},\dots,\varphi_{k})))

and

\displaystyle h_{k}\colon C_{*}(\mathrm{Emb}^{+}_{*}((U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}),(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}))^{\times k})

\displaystyle\to C_{k+*}(\mathrm{Emb}^{+}_{*}((U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}),(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}))),

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto H_{k}(I^{k}\times\varphi).

The higher homotopies are defined in a coherent way such that they satisfy the following useful formulas:

Lemma 4.7
.

The map

in the following sense

\displaystyle h_{k}\circ(\mathrm{id}^{\times k-1}\times\mu)\circ(\mathrm{id}^{\times k}\times r)

\displaystyle=\mu\circ(h_{k}\times r).

are such that

\displaystyle h_{1}d+dh_{1}=\mathrm{id}-r

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{i=2}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}h_{k-1}\circ(\mathrm{id}^{i-2}\times\mu\times\mathrm{id}^{k-i})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\mu\circ(h_{i-1}\times(r\circ\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}\times h_{k-i})))

\displaystyle-\mu\circ(h_{k-1}\times r)).

Proof.

Equation (

) holds for

4.6
(

\displaystyle h_{k})(\varphi_{1}\times\dots\times\varphi_{k})=(-1)^{k+1}H_{k}(d(I^{k})\times\varphi_{1}\times\dots\times\varphi_{k}).

\displaystyle h_{1}d

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1})+dh_{1}(\varphi_{1})=H_{1}(1,\varphi_{1})-H_{1}(0,\varphi_{1})=\varphi_{1}-r(\varphi_{1}).

, we find:

\displaystyle=H_{i-1}(I^{i-1}\times\varphi_{1}\times\dots\times\varphi_{i-2}\times(H_{k-i}(I^{k-i}\times\varphi_{i+1}\times\dots\times\varphi_{k})\circ\varphi_{i}\circ\varphi_{i-1}))

\displaystyle=H_{k-1}(I^{k-1}\times\varphi_{1}\times\dots\times\varphi_{i-2}\times(\varphi_{i}\circ\varphi_{i-1})\times\varphi_{i+1}\times\dots\times\varphi_{k})

\displaystyle=h_{k-1}\circ(\mathrm{id}^{i-2}\times\mu\times\mathrm{id}^{k-i})(\varphi_{1}\times\dots\times\varphi_{k})

and

\displaystyle H_{k}(I^{i-1}

\displaystyle\times\{0\}\times I^{k-i}\times\varphi_{1}\times\dots\times\varphi_{k})

\displaystyle=H_{i-1}(I^{i-1}\times\varphi_{1}\times\dots\times\varphi_{i-2}\times(r(H_{k-i}(I^{k-i}\times\varphi_{i+1}\times\dots\times\varphi_{k})\circ\varphi_{i})\circ\varphi_{i-1}))

Putting this into (

4.3.

We now define the morphisms

for

We define for

\displaystyle\to e_{\Omega}^{-1}(U_{x_{0}}),

\Phi
defines strictly associative

-module structure on source and target. Moreover, since the map is not degree-preserving we need the differential

-modules reads for

For

For

\displaystyle\ ((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\eta\circ(e_{\Omega}\times\Theta_{0}))\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda_{1})

\displaystyle-((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\eta\circ(e_{\Omega}\times(r\circ\Theta_{0})))\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}(\alpha\times\lambda_{1})

\displaystyle\ q_{1}(\Phi(\alpha\otimes\lambda))-\Phi(q_{1}(\alpha)\otimes\lambda).

This shows the Equation (

) for

For

\displaystyle\circ d+(-1)^{N+1+n}d\circ q_{N+1}

\displaystyle\ (-1)^{N+1}((\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\eta\circ(e_{\Omega}\times(\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}\times h_{N-1})\circ\Theta_{0}^{\times N})))\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{*}\circ\times^{N}

5.

7.

In this section, we construct an explicit Thom class that is invariant under orthogonal half-constant embeddings. To construct an explicit Thom class, we review the construction of the Mathai-Quillen Thom class in Subsection

We denote the map

\displaystyle\eta\colon(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c})\times\mathrm{hcEmb_{*}^{+}}((U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c}),(U_{\Delta},V_{\Delta}^{c}))

\displaystyle\to(U_{\Delta},V_{\Delta}^{c}),

\displaystyle(x,\varphi)

\displaystyle\mapsto\varphi(x).

The goal of this section is to prove the following proposition:

Proposition 7.1
.

There exists a Thom class (with real coefficients)
\tau_{\Delta}\in C^{n}(U_{\Delta},V_{\Delta}^{c})
such that

\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta}\circ\eta\circ\times=\tau_{\Delta}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{1}\circ\times

holds as maps
\bigoplus_{i+j=n}C_{i}(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}})\otimes C_{j}(\mathrm{SOEmb}(U_{x_{0}},U_{\Delta}))\to\mathbb{R}

Restricting along
\iota_{x_{0}}

7.1
implies the following corollary:

Corollary 7.2

There exists a Thom class (with real coefficients)
\tau_{x_{0}}\in C^{n}(U_{x_{0}},V_{x_{0}}^{c})
such that

\displaystyle\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\eta\circ\times=\tau_{x_{0}}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{1}\circ\times

holds as maps

Remark 7.3
.

We use de Rham homology in the construction of such a Thom class. This is the bottleneck that forces us to work with real coefficients. In Lemma
4.1
and Corollary

, we need field coefficients. Otherwise, our proof strategy works for any Thom class satisfying Proposition

over any ground ring.

However if such an invariant Thom class exists, the coefficient ring must contain

as the following proposition shows. Without bigger changes to our proof strategy, one can thus only hope to improve the statement from real coefficient to rational coefficients.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let

be a ring and assume that

After intersecting

with a plane containing the origin, we can reduce to the statement for

In dimension

which cuts out

of the circle (see Figure

for

be the chain obtained by gluing together the

\displaystyle d\widehat{\sigma}=\sigma_{1}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\eta(\sigma_{0}\times\varphi^{i}).

7.1.
Mathai-Quillen Thom Class

We recall the construction of the Mathai-Quillen Thom class. This is a de Rham cocycle
\omega_{\Delta}\in C^{n}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U_{\Delta},V^{c}_{\Delta})
. This class was originally introduced in
[
mathai1986superconnections
]
. We follow the construction as in
[
getzler1991thom
, Section 1.6]
.

We denote
\pi\colon U_{\Delta}\to M
the projection to the first coordinate on
U_{\Delta}\subseteq M\times M
. This is a vector bundle. We denote for
i,j\geq 0

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{i,j}:=\Gamma(U_{\Delta},\Lambda^{i}T^{*}U_{\Delta}\otimes\Lambda^{j}\pi^{*}U_{\Delta}).

The orientation on
M
defines an orientation on
U_{\Delta}
. This thus defines a map

\displaystyle B\colon\Lambda^{n}U_{\Delta}\to M\times\mathbb{R}

called the
Berezin integral
. The Berezin integral can be extended to a function

\displaystyle B\colon\mathcal{A}^{i,j}

\displaystyle\to\Gamma(U_{\Delta},\Lambda^{i}T^{*}U_{\Delta})=C^{i}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U_{\Delta}),

\displaystyle\chi\otimes\xi

\displaystyle\mapsto\chi\cdot B(\pi_{*}\xi).

This map is zero if

.

The Riemannian metric defines a metric on the bundle
\pi\colon U_{\Delta}\to M
. We fix some connection
\nabla\colon\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{i}T^{*}M\otimes U_{\Delta})\to\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{i+1}T^{*}M\otimes U_{\Delta})
that is compatible with the metric. The curvature
R:=\nabla^{2}
can be interpreted as an element in

\displaystyle\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\Lambda^{2}U_{\Delta}).

Therefore
\pi^{*}R
can be interpreted as an element in
A^{2,2}
.

The canonical section

\displaystyle\zeta\colon U_{\Delta}

\displaystyle\to\pi_{*}U_{\Delta},

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto(x,x)

defines an element in
\Gamma(U_{\Delta},\Lambda^{0}T^{*}U_{\Delta}\otimes U_{\Delta})
. We can thus interpret
(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\zeta)\in\Gamma(U_{\Delta},\Lambda^{1}T^{*}M\otimes U_{\Delta})\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{1,1}
. Finally, we interpret
\|\cdot\|\colon U_{\Delta}\to\mathbb{R}
as an element in
\mathcal{A}^{0,0}
.

We define
\omega_{0}:=\pi^{*}R+(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\zeta)+\|\cdot\|\in\mathcal{A}^{2,2}\oplus\mathcal{A}^{1,1}\oplus\mathcal{A}^{0,0}
.

For
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define

\displaystyle f(\omega_{0})

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*},

\displaystyle f(\omega_{0})(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(\|x\|)}{k!}((\pi^{*}\nabla)(\zeta)_{\pi(x)}+\pi^{*}R_{\pi(x)})^{k}.

We fix some

smooth such that
f|_{[\varepsilon,\infty)}\equiv 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{(n)}(\tfrac{\|x\|}{2})dx=1
.

Definition 7.5
.

The
Mathai-Quillen Thom class
is defined as

\displaystyle\omega_{\Delta}:=(-1)^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}B(f(\omega_{0}))\in C^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U_{\Delta},V_{\Delta}^{c}).

Proposition 7.6

(
[
getzler1991thom
, Proposition 1.3]
)
.

The Mathai-Quillen Thom class
\omega_{\Delta}
satisfies

(i)

\omega_{\Delta}\in C^{n}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U_{\Delta},V_{\Delta}^{c})
is a cocycle;

(ii)

for any

, we have
\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\iota_{x}^{*}\omega_{\Delta}=1
.

Remark 7.7
.

On a fibre
U_{x}
, the class
\iota_{x}^{*}\omega_{\Delta}
can be described explicitly. We fix some coordinates on
B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cong B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the Riemannian metric is given by the standard scalar product on

. Then
\iota_{x}^{*}\omega_{\Delta}
is given by

(18)

\displaystyle\iota_{x}^{*}\omega_{\Delta}(v)=f^{(n)}(\|v\|)dv_{1}\cdots dv_{n}

for

.

Indeed the term
\pi^{*}R_{\pi(x)}
is zero on

. Moreover, we have
(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\zeta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\otimes dv_{i}
. We therefore have on
U_{x}
that

\displaystyle\iota_{x}\omega_{0}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(\|v\|)}{k!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\otimes dv_{i}\right)^{k}.

The term

lives in

. Thus the only term that survives after evaluating it under the Berezin integral

is when

\displaystyle\iota_{x}^{*}\omega_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\frac{f^{(n)}(\|v\|)}{n!}B\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}\otimes dv_{i}\right)^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\frac{f^{(n)}(\|v\|)}{n!}B\left((-1)^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}n!v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge w_{n}\otimes dv_{1}\cdots dv_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=f^{(n)}(\|v\|)dv_{1}\cdots dv_{n}.

A consequence of this description is that the Mathai-Quillen Thom class is invariant orthogonal half-constant maps
\varphi=\iota_{x}\circ\psi

preserves the Riemannian metric, it follows that

\displaystyle\iota_{x_{0}}^{*}\omega_{\Delta}=\psi^{*}\iota_{x}^{*}\omega_{\Delta}=\varphi^{*}\omega_{\Delta}.

The problem with using the Mathai-Quillen Thom class is that we can only evaluate

We fix the following terminology:

Definition 7.8

A map

\displaystyle I^{k}\twoheadrightarrow I^{\{1,\dots,k\}\setminus J}\cong I^{\{1,\dots,k\}\setminus J}\times\{0\}^{J}\overset{\sigma}{\to}X

\displaystyle\to I^{n},

\displaystyle(v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})

\displaystyle\mapsto(v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1},v,v_{i},\dots,v_{n}).

We recall the notation

\displaystyle\mu\colon\mathrm{SOEmb}(U_{x_{0}},U_{x_{0}})\times\mathrm{SOEmb}(U_{x_{0}},U_{\Delta})

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{SOEmb}(U_{x_{0}},U_{\Delta}),

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}.

We first smooth the chains on

Lemma 7.9

For all continuous maps

\displaystyle H_{\sigma}\colon I\times I^{k}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{SOEmb}(U_{x_{0}},U_{\Delta})

(iv)

The proof follows the idea of smoothing singular chains on a smooth manifold in a way that commutes with faces. This is a standard result from smoothing theory (see for example

).

We prove this statement by induction over

Assume we have constructed

as above for all
\sigma\colon I^{k^{\prime}}\to X

\displaystyle=H_{\sigma^{\prime}}(t,v_{j+1},\dots,v_{k})

We note that

that commutes with face maps as in

for the manifold
\mathrm{SOEmb}(U_{x_{0}},U_{\Delta})

The following lemma shows that we can exhibit all cuttings of a chain at once:

Lemma 7.11

Let
\sigma\colon I^{k}\to U_{\Delta}

\sigma_{0}\colon I^{i_{1}}\to U_{x_{0}}

Proof.

We prove this by induction on

. The statement is the definition of cutting when

. We can thus assume that we have constructed
\sigma_{0},\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{l-1}
as in the statement for

We thus assume that

We can thus compute

\displaystyle\eta\circ(\sigma_{0}^{\prime}(v_{1},\dots,v_{i_{1}})\times\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(v_{i_{1}+1},\dots,v_{i_{2}},0,\dots,0))=\eta\circ(\sigma_{0}^{\prime\prime}(v_{1},\dots,v_{i_{2}})\times\varphi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0,\dots,0)).

We note that the maps in

have the property that their images agree if and only if their images intersect. This calculation thus shows
{\mathrm{im}}(\varphi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0,\dots,0))={\mathrm{im}}(\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(v_{i_{1}+1},\dots,v_{i_{2}},0,\dots,0)

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}^{\prime\prime}(v_{1},\dots,v_{i_{2}})

\displaystyle=\eta(\sigma_{0}^{\prime}(v_{1},\dots,v_{i_{1}})\times(\varphi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0,\dots,0)^{-1}\circ\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(v_{i_{1}+1},\dots,v_{i_{2}},0,\dots,0))).

In this subsection, we use our main theorem to study the invariance or non-invariance of the Goresky-Hingston coproduct loop space under maps of manifolds.

For this we import the following result that shows that the Goresky-Hingston coproduct commutes with degree

Proposition 8.9

Let

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nu}^{N}\colon C_{*}(\Omega N,y_{0})\to C_{1-n+*}((\Omega N,y_{0})^{2})

for a morphism of

-modules as constructed in (

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nu}^{M}\colon C_{*}(\Omega M,x_{0})\to C_{1-n+*}((\Omega N,x_{0})^{2})

\displaystyle\nu_{1}^{N}\circ\Omega g=(\Omega g\times\Omega g)\circ\nu_{1}^{M}.

Proof.

By
[
hopf1930topologie
]

and
[

In particular, we find that

The diagonal

A convex ear decomposition of the augmented Bergman complex of a matroid Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classifications : 05B35, 05E45
Christos A. Athanasiadis, Luis Ferroni

Abstract
. In recent work of Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot and Wang, a class of simplicial complexes associated to matroids, called augmented Bergman complexes, was introduced. The present article concerns the face enumeration of these complexes. We prove that the augmented Bergman complex of any matroid admits a convex ear decomposition and deduce that augmented Bergman complexes are doubly Cohen–Macaulay and that they have top-heavy h h -vectors. We provide some formulas for computing the h h -polynomials of these complexes and exhibit examples which show that, in general, they are neither log-concave nor unimodal.



1.
Introduction

Two well-studied objects that one may associate to a matroid
{\mathsf{M}}
are the

independence complex
, denoted by
{\mathrm{I}}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
, and the
Bergman
complex
, denoted by
\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
. Recently, these simplicial complexes
have received considerable attention. Despite their simple definitions,
several questions about their face enumeration have turned out to be very
challenging. As a notable example, the recent breakthroughs by Brändén
and Huh
[
BH20
]
and by Anari, Liu, Oveis-Gharan and Vinzant

[
ALOGV24
]
proved a long-standing conjecture by Mason, claiming that
the entries of the
f
-vector of
{\mathrm{I}}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
form an
ultra
log-concave
sequence. Moreover, the
h
-polynomial of the Bergman complex

\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
was conjectured to be real-rooted (therefore, also ultra
log-concave) in recent work of the first named author and
Kalampogia-Evangelinou
[
AKE23
]
. The conjecture was verified there
in interesting special cases, such as partition lattices and their
standard type B analogues (also known as braid matroids of types A and B,
respectively) and subspace lattices.

In the
singular
Hodge theory of matroids, developed by Braden, Huh,
Matherne, Proudfoot and Wang
[
BHM+22
,
BHM+23
]
, an instrumental role is
played by a third simplicial complex, called the
augmented Bergman
complex
. This simplicial complex ‘interpolates’ between
{\mathrm{I}}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
and

\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
and contains both as subcomplexes. The focus of this paper
is its face enumeration.

Throughout this paper
{\mathsf{M}}
will denote a matroid on the ground set
E=[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, with lattice of flats
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathsf{M}})
. We let
U=\{u_{i}:i\in E\}
and
V_{{\mathsf{M}}}=\{v_{F}:F\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathsf{M}}){\smallsetminus}\{E\}\}
be
disjoint sets which are in one-to-one correspondence with
E
and

{\mathcal{L}}({\mathsf{M}}){\smallsetminus}\{E\}
, respectively.

Definition 1.1

The
augmented Bergman complex
of
{\mathsf{M}}
, denoted by
\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}}
,
is the simplicial complex on the ground set
U\cup V_{{\mathsf{M}}}
whose faces
are the sets of the form

(1)

\{u_{i}:i\in S\}\cup\{v_{F_{1}},v_{F_{2}},\dots,v_{F_{k}}\},

where
S\in{\mathrm{I}}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
is an independent set of
{\mathsf{M}}
, the
F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathsf{M}}){\smallsetminus}\{E\}
are proper flats and either

S\subseteq F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots\subset F_{k}
and
k\geq 1
,
or
S\subseteq E
and
k=0
.

Augmented Bergman complexes were introduced in
[
BHM+22
]
.
Their combinatorial and topological properties were studied in

[
BKR+22
,
Jef23
]
, where augmented Bergman complexes were shown to
be shellable and, in fact, vertex decomposable, respectively. In
particular, augmented Bergman complexes are Cohen–Macaulay (over

{\mathbb{Z}}
and any field). As a well known consequence of either
shellability or Cohen–Macaulayness (see Sections II.3 and III.2
of
[
Sta96
]
), their
h
-vectors have nonnegative entries.

The main contribution of this paper is a new list of inequalities
that the
h
-vector of
\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}}
satisfies. The main technical
tool employed is that of a convex ear decomposition (see
Section
2.3
), a property that has been established
for the independence complex
{\mathrm{I}}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
by Chari
[
Cha97
]
and for
the Bergman complex
\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
by Nyman and Swartz
[
NS04
]
.
This property, in addition to yielding inequalities for
h
-vectors,
also establishes the double Cohen–Macaulayness of the complex: a
simplicial complex
\Delta
is said to be

doubly Cohen–Macaulay
if it is Cohen–Macaulay and removing
any vertex results in a subcomplex that
is Cohen–Macaulay of the same dimension as
\Delta
(see

[
Sta96
, Section III.3]
).

Theorem 1.2

The augmented Bergman complex
\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}}
has a
convex ear decomposition for every matroid
{\mathsf{M}}
.
In particular,
\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}}
is doubly Cohen–Macaulay,

(2)

h_{0}(\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}})\leq h_{1}(\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}})\leq\cdots\leq h_{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}(\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}})

and

(3)

h_{i}(\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}})\leq h_{d-i}(\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}})

for
0\leq i\leq d/2
, where
d
is the rank of
{\mathsf{M}}
and

(h_{i}(\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}}))_{0\leq i\leq d}
is the
h
-vector
of
\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}}
.

Inequalities (
2
) and (
3
),
often referred to as the
top-heavy
inequalities for the
h
-vector
of
\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}}
, follow from the existence of a convex ear decomposition

[
Cha97
]
. They have been established for several fundamental
enumerative invariants of matroids. For instance, as already mentioned,
the independence complex (after removing coloops) and the Bergman complex
of a matroid have convex ear decompositions. As a result, they have
top-heavy (or strongly flawless, in other terminology)
h
-vectors and
are doubly Cohen–Macaulay. The
h
-vector of the broken circuit
complex, another simplicial complex of fundamental importance associated
to a matroid, is top-heavy thanks to some deep results in the intersection
theory of Chern–Schwartz–MacPherson cycles of matroids by Ardila, Denham
and Huh
[
ADH23
]
; see also
[
JKL18
]
for an earlier partial result.
Furthermore, the sequence of Whitney numbers of the second kind of a
matroid is top-heavy thanks to an important result by Braden, Huh,
Matherne, Proudfoot and Wang
[
BHM+23
, Theorem 1.1]
.

The content and structure of this paper is as follows.
Theorem
1.2
is proven in Section
3
; the proof
may be viewed as an extension of the proof of the corresponding result
of
[
NS04
]
for the Bergman complex. Section
2

explains relevant definitions and background on simplicial complexes,

lemma (see Lemma
2.3
) which is crucial for the
proof of Theorem
1.2
and provides useful formulas for the

f
-vector and the
h
-vector of the augmented Bergman complex (see
Propositions
2.7
and
2.9
).
Section
4
exhibits explicit counterexamples showing that top-heaviness cannot be upgraded to unimodality (or log-concavity).
Section
5
proves
an augmented analogue of a formula conjectured by Hameister, Rao and
Simpson
[
HRS21
]
for the
h
-polynomial of the Bergman complex
in the special case of uniform matroids.

2.1.
Face enumeration

Let
\Delta
be a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex and

f_{i-1}(\Delta)
be the number of
(i-1)
-dimensional faces of

\Delta
, for
i\geq 0
. The sequence

(f_{-1}(\Delta),\ldots,f_{d-1}(\Delta))
is called the

f
-vector
of
\Delta
. The
f
-polynomial
of

\Delta
is defined as

f(\Delta,x)=\sum_{F\in\Delta}x^{|F|}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i-1}(\Delta)x^{i}.

Here we follow the usual convention that
f_{-1}(\Delta)=1

whenever
\Delta
is nonempty and
f_{-1}(\Delta)=0
otherwise.

Correspondingly, the
h
-vector

(h_{0}(\Delta),h_{1}(\Delta),\dots,h_{d}(\Delta))
and the
h
-polynomial

h(\Delta,x)
of
\Delta
are defined by a linear transformation
of the
f
-vector:

(4)

\displaystyle h(\Delta,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\Delta}x^{|F|}(1-x)^{d-|F|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i-1}(\Delta)\,x^{i}(1-x)^{d-i}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}h_{i}(\Delta)x^{i}.

Equivalently,

(5)

f(\Delta,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}h_{i}(\Delta)\,x^{i}\,(1+x)^{d-i}.

In other words, to obtain
f(\Delta,x)
from
h(\Delta,x)
,
one should first reverse (with respect to
d
) the coefficients of

h(\Delta,x)
, then substitute
x+1
for
x
and finally reverse
the order of the coefficients again.

Remark 2.1

Some sources (for instance,
[
ADH23
,
Bjö92
]
) define the

f
-polynomial and the
h
-polynomial with their coefficients in
reverse order. Although there is no ambiguity in the meaning of

f_{i}(\Delta)
and
h_{i}(\Delta)
, the way in which the coefficients
appear does matter when considering some properties of the
polynomials, such as ultra log-concavity.

and hence

2.2.
Shellability

Let
\Delta
be a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex and
let
{\mathcal{F}}
be the set of facets (maximal with respect to inclusion
faces) of
\Delta
. Assume that
\Delta
is pure, meaning that
every facet has exactly
d
elements. Then,
\Delta
is said to be

shellable
if
{\mathcal{F}}
can be linearly ordered so that the
following condition holds: Given
F,G\in{\mathcal{F}}
with
F
preceding

G
in this order, there exists
F^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{F}}
which precedes
G

such that
F\cap G\subseteq F^{\prime}\cap G
and
|F^{\prime}\cap G|=d-1
.
Such a linear ordering is called a
shelling
of
\Delta
.
Shellability has strong consequences on the combinatorial and
topological properties of
\Delta
. For instance, it implies that

\Delta
has a nonnegative
h
-vector and that it is Cohen–Macaulay
(over
{\mathbb{Z}}
and any field); see
[
Bjö84
, Section 1]

[
Bjö92
, Section 7.2]
and
[
Sta96
, Section III.2]
for more
information about this important concept.

Let
V=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{d}\}
be a
d
-element set. Let
{\rm sd}_{V}

denote the barycentric subdivision of the boundary complex of the
simplex
2^{V}
. The faces of
{\rm sd}_{V}
are all chains of nonempty
proper subsets of
V
. Consider another
d
-element set

U=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{d}\}
which is disjoint from
{\rm sd}_{V}
, and
set
u_{S}:=\{u_{i}:i\in S\}
for
S\subseteq[d]
and

v_{S}:=\{v_{i}:i\in S\}
for
S\subset[d]
.

Consider the simplicial complex
\Omega_{V}
having vertex set

U\cup\{v_{S}:S\subset[d]\}
and faces given by the sets of
the form

u_{S}\cup\{v_{S_{1}},v_{S_{2}},\dots,v_{S_{k}}\},

where
S\subseteq S_{1}\subset S_{2}\subset\cdots\subset S_{k}\subset[d]
and
k\geq 1
, or
S\subseteq[d]
and
k=0
. The
complex
\Omega_{V}
is combinatorially isomorphic to the boundary
complex of a
d
-dimensional polytope known as the simplicial
stellohedron
[
BKR+22
, Example 5]

[
PRW08
, Section 10.4]
.

More generally, given a pure
(d-2)
-dimensional subcomplex

\Gamma
of
{\rm sd}_{V}
, we define
\Omega_{V}(\Gamma)
as the
subcomplex of
\Omega_{V}
whose facets are the facets
F
of

\Omega_{V}
for which
F{\smallsetminus}(U\cup\{v_{\varnothing}\})
is a
nonempty face of
\Gamma
. By definition,
\Omega_{V}(\Gamma)

is a pure
(d-1)
-dimensional subcomplex of
\Omega_{V}{\smallsetminus}\{U\}

and
\Omega_{V}(\Gamma)=\Omega_{V}{\smallsetminus}\{U\}
if and only if

\Gamma=\partial(2^{V})
.

Lemma 2.3

For every
d
-element set
V
and every shellable

(d-2)
-dimensional subcomplex
\Gamma
of
{\rm sd}_{V}
, the
simplicial complex
\Omega_{V}(\Gamma)
is a proper shellable

(d-1)
-dimensional subcomplex of
\Omega_{V}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be the set of facets of
\Omega_{V}(\Gamma)
and for

S\subset[d]
, let
{\mathcal{F}}_{S}
be the set of those facets

F\in{\mathcal{F}}
for which
F\cap U=u_{S}
.

Given
S\subset[d]
, we consider a maximal chain in the
poset of nonempty subsets of
S
, ordered by inclusion, and
let
{\mathcal{C}}_{S}\in{\rm sd}_{V}
be the corresponding chain of proper
subsets of
V
. Then,
{\mathcal{F}}_{S}
is in one-to-one correspondence
with the set of facets of the link
{\rm link}_{\Gamma}({\mathcal{C}}_{S})
, which
is a shellable subcomplex of
\Gamma
of codimension equal to

|S|
. For example,
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varnothing}
is in one-to-one
correspondence with the set of facets of
\Gamma
and

{\mathcal{F}}_{\{u_{i}\}}
is in one-to-one correspondence with the
set of facets of
{\rm link}_{\Gamma}(v_{i})
for every
i\in[d]
.

Let us linearly order the facets of
\Omega_{V}(\Gamma)

subject to the following conditions:

(a)

For all
S,T\subset[d]
, the elements of
{\mathcal{F}}_{S}
appear
before those of
{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
whenever
|S|<|T|
.

(b)

For every
S\subset[d]
, the elements of
{\mathcal{F}}_{S}
appear
relative to each other according to a shelling order of

{\rm link}_{\Gamma}({\mathcal{C}}_{S})
.

We now show that such a linear order of
{\mathcal{F}}
is a shelling
of
\Omega_{V}(\Gamma)
. Given
F,G\in{\mathcal{F}}
with
F
preceding

G
, we need to verify that there exists
F^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{F}}
which
precedes
G
such that
F\cap G\subseteq F^{\prime}\cap G
and

|F^{\prime}\cap G|=d-1
. Indeed, this follows from condition (b)
if
F,G\in{\mathcal{F}}_{S}
for some
S\subset[d]
. Otherwise, we
have
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{S}
and
G\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
for some
S,T\subset[d]
with
|S|\leq|T|
and
S\neq T
. Then, there exists

i\in(T{\smallsetminus}S)
and the set obtained from
G
by removing

u_{i}
and including
v_{T{\smallsetminus}\{i\}}
(which is equal to

v_{\varnothing}
, if
T=\{i\}
) can serve as the desired
facet
F^{\prime}
.
∎

2.3.
Convex ear decompositions

Let
\Delta
be a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex.
A
convex ear decomposition
of
\Delta
is a sequence

(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\dots,\Delta_{m})
of subcomplexes of

\Delta
such that:

(i)

\Delta=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\Delta_{i}
,

(ii)

\Delta_{1}
is isomorphic to the boundary complex of a

d
-dimensional simplicial polytope and, for
2\leq k\leq m
,

\Delta_{k}
is a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial ball which is
isomorphic to a proper subcomplex of the boundary complex of
a
d
-dimensional simplicial polytope; and

(iii)

\Delta_{k}\cap\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}\Delta_{i}\right)=\partial\Delta_{k}
for
2\leq k\leq m
.

Convex ear decompositions were introduced by Chari
[
Cha97
]

as a tool to prove the top-heavy inequalities for
h
-vectors
of simplicial complexes
[
Cha97
, Corollary 2]
. Swartz

[
Swa06
, Theorem 4.1]
showed that every simplicial complex

\Delta
which admits a convex ear decomposition is doubly
Cohen–Macaulay, meaning that
\Delta
is Cohen–Macaulay and
that every subcomplex which is obtained by removing a vertex
from
\Delta
is Cohen–Macaulay, of the same dimension as

\Delta
. We refer to
[
Sta96
, Section III.3]
for more
information about this important class of complexes.

of

facets

,

and
\{b,e\}
and

is the one-dimensional ball with facets

. Alternatively, one can choose

\Delta_{1}
to have all facets of
\Delta
other than

\{b,e\}
and
\Delta_{2}
to have the single facet
\{b,e\}
.

Let
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2},\dots,\Sigma_{m}
be subcomplexes of a

(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta
such that:

(i)

\Delta=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\Sigma_{i}
,

(ii)

is isomorphic to the boundary complex of a

d

and

2.4.
Matroids

Let
{\mathsf{M}}
be a matroid on the ground set
E
, having lattice of
flats
\mathcal{L}({\mathsf{M}})
. The
Bergman complex
of
{\mathsf{M}}
,
denoted by
\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
, is defined as the order complex of
the poset
\mathcal{L}({\mathsf{M}})\smallsetminus\{\widehat{0},\widehat{1}\}
, meaning the simplicial complex
consisting of all chains in that poset. Note that
\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{M}}}=\{\varnothing\}
if
{\mathsf{M}}
has rank zero or one.

The following proposition allows one to compute the
f
-polynomial
of the augmented Bergman complex in terms of the
f
-polynomials of
Bergman complexes of contractions of
{\mathsf{M}}
.

Proposition 2.7

Let
{\mathsf{M}}
be a matroid with set of bases
{B}({\mathsf{M}})
. Then, the

f
-polynomial of the augmented Bergman complex of
{\mathsf{M}}
is given by
the formula

f(\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}},x)=|{B}({\mathsf{M}})|\,x^{d}+(x+1)\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in{\mathrm{I}}_{{\mathsf{M}}}\\
S\notin{B}({\mathsf{M}})\end{subarray}}x^{|S|}\,f(\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{M}}/S},x),

where
d
is the rank of
{\mathsf{M}}
and
{\mathsf{M}}/S
is the contraction of
{\mathsf{M}}

by
S
.

Proof.

By definition, a face of
\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}}
has the form
\{u_{i}:i\in S\}\cup\{v_{F_{1}},\ldots,v_{F_{k}}\}
, where
S
is an independent set and

F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{k}
is a (possibly empty) chain of proper
flats containing
S
. After fixing the independent set
S
, we have to
enumerate chains of flats in the contraction
{\mathsf{M}}/S
, but allowing the
first element in the chain to be equal to the bottom element while
disallowing the last element of the chain to be equal to the top element.
In other words, we are enumerating the faces of a cone over the Bergman
complex
\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{M}}/S}
. There is one special case in which the
operation is not an actual coning: when
\widehat{0}=\widehat{1}
in
the poset
\mathcal{L}({\mathsf{M}}/S)
, which happens if and only if
{\mathsf{M}}/S
has
rank zero or, equivalently, when
S
is a basis of
{\mathsf{M}}
. Summing over
all independent sets yields the formula of the statement.
∎

The next result provides an alternative concrete formula for computing
the

-polynomial of the augmented Bergman complex of a matroid
{\mathsf{M}}
.
Specifically, it is given by a convolution of

-polynomials of
independence complexes of restrictions of
{\mathsf{M}}
and Bergman complexes
of contractions of
{\mathsf{M}}
by flats (as opposed to independent sets).

Proposition 2.9

The
h
-polynomial of the augmented Bergman complex of a matroid
{\mathsf{M}}

on the ground set
E
is given by the formula

(6)

4.
On unimodality and log-concavity

This section shows that
f
- and
h
-vectors of
augmented Bergman complexes are not well behaved
with respect to unimodality and log-concavity.
Let
p(x)=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{d}x^{d}
be a
polynomial of degree at most
d
with nonnegative
coefficients. We recall that
p(x)
is called

\bullet

unimodal
, if
a_{0}\leq a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{k}\geq a_{k+1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{d}
for some
k\in\{0,1,\dots,d\}
,

\bullet

log-concave
, if
a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}

for every
i\in[d-1]
,

\bullet

ultra log-concave
, of order
m\geq d
, if

\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{\binom{m}{i}^{2}}\geq\frac{a_{i-1}}{\binom{m}{i-1}}\cdot\frac{a_{i+1}}{\binom{m}{i+1}}

for every
i\in[d-1]
,

\bullet

real-rooted
, if every root of
p(x)
is real,
or
p(x)\equiv 0
.

This is a hierarchy of increasingly stronger properties,
where the implication
\text{log-concave}\Longrightarrow\text{unimodal}
requires that
p(x)
has no internal zeros,
meaning there are no indices
i<j<k
such that
a_{i}a_{k}\neq 0
and
a_{j}=0
. We refer to surveys by Stanley

[
Sta89
]
and Brändén

[
Brä15
]
for more information about unimodality,
log-concavity and real-rootedness. We also point to the work
of Liggett
[
Lig97
]
and Brändén and Huh
[
BH20
]

for ultra log-concavity and multivariate generalizations.

Let
{\mathsf{M}}
be a matroid on
n
elements. The independence
complex
{\mathrm{I}}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
was shown to have an ultra log-concave

f
-polynomial of order
n
in
[
BH20
,
ALOGV24
]

and a log-concave
h
-polynomial in
[
AHK18
]
. A
stronger property has been conjectured for the

h
-polynomial of the Bergman complex.

Conjecture 4.1

(
[
AKE23
]
)

The
f
-polynomial (equivalently, the
h
-polynomial) of

\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{M}}}
is real-rooted for every matroid
{\mathsf{M}}
.

This conjecture, if true, implies that both
f
- and

h
-polynomials of Bergman complexes are ultra log-concave
of any order, and in particular log-concave. These weaker
properties are also open.

One may suspect that
f
- and
h
-polynomials of augmented
Bergman complexes also satisfy some version of log-concavity.
The following example rules out that possibility.

Example 4.2

Consider the uniform matroid
{\mathsf{U}}_{4,189}
. The
f
-vector
of its augmented Bergman complex can be computed explicitly
via Example
2.8
:

f(\Delta_{{\mathsf{U}}_{4,189}})=(1,1125559,11181114,27774180,69213375).

This
f
-vector is not log-concave, because

27774180^{2}=771405074672400<773882636199750=11181114\cdot 69213375.

This example implies that
h
-vectors of augmented Bergman
complexes are not always log-concave. In fact, the situation
is even worse, as these
h
-vectors can even fail to be
unimodal. For the uniform matroid
{\mathsf{U}}_{5,83}
we have

h(\Delta_{{\mathsf{U}}_{5,83}})=(1,1933066,28121900,60710014,28680319,29034396).

The reader may observe that
60710014>28680319<29034396
.
We have not been able to find a non-unimodal
f
-vector.
∎

Remark 4.3

We have shown that
\Delta_{{\mathsf{M}}}
is doubly Cohen–Macaulay.
The first named author and Tzanaki have posed the question

[
AT21
, Question 7.2]
whether the
h
-vector of any

(d-1)
-dimensional doubly Cohen–Macaulay simplicial
complex
\Delta
satisfies the inequalities

(11)

\frac{h_{0}(\Delta)}{h_{d}(\Delta)}\leq\frac{h_{1}(\Delta)}{h_{d-1}(\Delta)}\leq\frac{h_{2}(\Delta)}{h_{d-2}(\Delta)}\leq\cdots\leq\frac{h_{d}(\Delta)}{h_{0}(\Delta)}.

5.
A connection to augmented Chow polynomials

Hameister, Rao and Simpson conjectured a surprising relation between
the
h
-vectors of Bergman complexes and the Hilbert–Poincaré
series of Chow rings of uniform matroids
[
HRS21
, Conjecture 6.2]
.
Their conjecture was proved recently by Ferroni, Matherne, Stevens
and Vecchi
[
FMSV24
, Corollary 3.17]
. Specifically, they
established the following formula.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
FMSV24
]
)

The
h
-polynomial of the Bergman complex of
{\mathsf{U}}_{d,n}
can be
expressed as

x^{d-1}h(\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{d,n}},x^{-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\,\underline{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{i,n}}(x),

where
\underline{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathsf{M}}}(x)
stands for the Hilbert–Poincaré series of the
Chow ring of
{\mathsf{M}}
.

This section provides an analogue of this theorem for augmented Bergman
complexes. We emphasize that, a priori, there are no reasons to expect
such a concise relation between these two classes of polynomials and it
is surprising that it continues to hold true in the augmented setting.

We assume familiarity with basic notions about Chow rings of
matroids and their Hilbert–Poincaré series; our notation here follows

[
FMSV24
]
closely, and we refer to that paper for any undefined
terminology. One important distinction between the Chow polynomial and
the augmented Chow polynomial is their definition on matroids with loops.
In general, the Chow polynomial is defined to be zero if the matroid has
a loop, whereas the augmented Chow polynomial of a matroid with loops
does not change if one removes the loops.

Theorem 5.2

The
h
-vector of the augmented Bergman complex of
{\mathsf{U}}_{d,n}
can be
expressed as

x^{d}h(\Delta_{{\mathsf{U}}_{d,n}},x^{-1})=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\,\mathrm{H}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{i,n}}(x),

where
\mathrm{H}_{{\mathsf{M}}}(x)
stands for the Hilbert–Poincaré series of the augmented
Chow ring of
{\mathsf{M}}
.

Proof.

We will employ the following formula, established in
[
FMSV24
, Theorem 1.3 (iii)]
:

\mathrm{H}_{{\mathsf{M}}}(x)=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{L}({\mathsf{M}})}x^{{\rm rk}(F)}\,\underline{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathsf{M}}/F}(x).

By applying this identity to uniform matroids, we have the following chain of
equalities:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\mathrm{H}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{i,n}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\left(x^{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\binom{n}{j}x^{j}\,\underline{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{i-j,n-j}}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\,x^{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\binom{n}{j}x^{j}\,\underline{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{i-j,n-j}}.

Changing the order of summation in the second term and reindexing,
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\mathrm{H}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{i,n}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\,x^{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{n}{j}x^{j}\sum_{i=j+1}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\,\underline{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{i-j,n-j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\,x^{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{n}{j}x^{j}\sum_{i=1}^{d-j}\binom{n-j-i-1}{d-j-i}\,\underline{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{i,n-j}}.

We now apply the formula of Theorem
5.1
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\mathrm{H}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{i,n}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\,x^{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{n}{j}x^{j}\,\left(x^{d-j-1}h(\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{d-j,n-j}},x^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n-i-1}{d-i}\,x^{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{n}{j}x^{d-1}h(\underline{\Delta}_{{\mathsf{U}}_{d-j,n-j}},x^{-1}).

Finally, we reverse the order of the coefficients and compare to the
identity of Example
2.10
and the proof follows.
∎

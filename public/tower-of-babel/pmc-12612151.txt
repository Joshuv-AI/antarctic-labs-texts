Dynamic failure evolution law and damage constitutive model of coal rock under impact load
Authors: Zhang Kun, Zhang Pengfei, Ren Jianxi, Zhang Sen, Cai Chiheng, Yun Mengchen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To explore the dynamic characteristics and damage evolution laws of deep coal rock under impact loads, the dynamic load response laws of coal rock at different strain rates under high-frequency dynamic loads were studied using the Split Hopkinson pressure bar test device, and the deformation and failure processes of coal rock were analyzed in combination with a high-speed photography acquisition system. The dynamic impact results of coal rock under different impact loads were calculated using the “Three-wave method”. The test results show that the peak stress and strain rate have an approximately linear increasing relationship, and the peak strain and strain rate have a logarithmic increasing relationship. With the increase of strain rate, the dynamic elastic modulus generally shows a logarithmic increasing trend. Under high-frequency dynamic loads, coal rock mainly show a tensile failure mode. Based on the Weibull distribution function and by introducing the damage variable, the Z-W-T constitutive model was improved and optimized. On this basis, a dynamic damage constitutive model considering the impact tendency index was established. The model is in good agreement with the test results and can well describe the damage evolution laws and dynamic mechanical behaviors of coal rock under impact loads, providing a basic research for the mining of deep coal resources under the influence of impact dynamic loads.

Introduction

Energy has always been the core driving force for social progress and development, among which coal plays an important role as the cornerstone of energy and holds an irreplaceable position. With the increase in the depth and intensity of coal resource exploitation, dynamic disasters such as mine rock burst have been intensifying, seriously threatening the safety of coal mining1–4. Therefore, studying the damage evolution law and constitutive model of coal rock under impact loading is of great significance and value for guiding the safe exploitation of deep coal resources.

In the research of rock damage and deformation law based on SHPB test, many scholars at home and abroad have carried out a series of researches on the nature and damage law of coal rock under impact loading by using SHPB test, and many results have been achieved5–9. SHPB is the most reliable device for dynamic testing of rocks at high strain rates10–12, and numerous scholars have shown that the dynamic mechanical response of rocks is highly correlated with the loading rate, and that the destructive strain, the degree of fracture, and the dynamic strength all increase with the increase of strain rate13–15. Chen et al.16 used SHPB tests to explore the dynamic mechanical response characteristics, energy evolution patterns, and fragment distribution characteristics of impact-resistant sandstone under different cycle thresholds and impact air pressures. Wang et al.17 carried out impact experiments of coal samples under different strain rates, and analyzed and derived the dynamic mechanical properties of coal rock and the change rule of its mechanical parameters with strain rate. Yan et al.18 proposed that an increase in strain rate exacerbates rock fragmentation, decreases the average fragmentation size, and increases the fractal dimension of rock fragments. Zheng et al.19 believe that under the action of impact compression loads, the degree of fragmentation of damaged sandstone and the degree of strain concentration at the center of the specimen under the action of impact splitting tensile loads increase with the increase in the degree of damage to the sandstone. Furthermore, the higher the impact pressure, the smaller the difference in the degree of fragmentation and strain concentration caused by damage. Luo et al.20 analyzed the dynamic mechanical properties and damage mode of sandstone based on SHPB test, and the results showed that the damage mode of the rock gradually changed from local tensile shear damage to axial cleavage damage, and finally to crushing damage. Yuan et al.21 found that the dynamic uniaxial compressive strength of sandstone increased as a power function with the increase of water content. Zhao et al.22; Zhang et al.23 and Ding et al.24 pointed out that the fractured pieces of coal rock after the test had obvious fractal characteristics, but due to the strong discrete nature of coal rock itself, the distribution of the fractured pieces was more differentiated. Wu et al.25 investigated the impact loading with different strain rates and the uniaxial cyclic load with different peak stress levels of uniaxial cyclic loading on the physical and mechanical properties of coal rock, and studied the damage variables of coal rock based on the energy dissipation theory.

In the study of viscoelastic damage constitutive models, the commonly used composite structural models mainly fall into two categories: viscoplastic and viscoelastic. In the viscoelastic constitutive theory, the Ju-Wang-Tang constitutive model proposed by Tang et al. is based on the one-dimensional nonlinear viscoelastic constitutive model established by Green&Rivlin for homogeneous and isotropic simple materials, combined with a large number of impact tests on polymer viscoelastic materials. This model is derived by two Maxwell bodies and a nonlinear spring in parallel, which can effectively reflect the typical dynamic mechanical behavior of polymers26–29 proposed a new dynamic damage constitutive model based on the Z-W-T model to describe the dynamic strength and deformation characteristics of fractured rocks at high strain rates. Xie et al.30 considering the strain rate correlation and damage characteristics of composite coal rock mass in dynamic failure process, a 7-parameter composite coal strata laminated constitutive model is constructed. Yang et al.31 believe that the strength and damage variables of micro-elements follow Weibull distribution and analyze the dynamic viscoelastic damage characteristics of soft rocks from macro and micro perspectives. Using energy theory and statistical damage theory, Qu et al.32 established a dynamic statistical damage constitutive model for weathered granite, which can well reflect the stress-strain relationship of rock under dynamic compression test and the process of rock strength change with strain rate. Yang et al.33 established a dynamic statistical damage constitutive model considering the damage and viscosity characteristics of coal samples based on the classical Z-W-T model, D-P criterion and Weibull distribution. Zheng et al.34 analyzed the deformation and failure characteristics of coal rock under different strain rates, and established a dynamic strength type statistical damage model of coal rock based on Weibull statistical distribution and D-P failure criterion. In addition, Pan3 derived the analytical solution of rock burst in circular roadway according to the instability theory of disturbance response, and obtained that there are critical indexes of disturbance quantity and response quantity in coal-rock deformation system, and the critical indexes are determined by control quantity.

The above studies have revealed the damage deformation patterns of rocks under impact loads from different perspectives and proposed relevant constitutive models. However, due to the complexity and uncertainty of the dynamic instability process of coal rock, existing research still has two shortcomings: most constitutive models have not fully considered the differences in impact susceptibility of coal rock, making it difficult to quantify the potential risks of dynamic disasters; the description of the coupling mechanism between plastic evolution and damage in coal rock under high strain rates is insufficient. Currently, there are few constitutive models that can reflect the viscoelastic and damage characteristics of rock materials, especially those lacking targeted models for failure modes under high-frequency dynamic loads. Therefore, the dynamic characteristics of coal rock under impact loads and the corresponding dynamic damage constitutive models require further in-depth research.

In summary, this study selected coal samples from the Binchang mining area. Combining SHPB tests to systematically investigate the dynamic response characteristics of coal rock under different impact loads and the failure patterns under high-frequency dynamic loads. Through theoretical analysis and based on the improved Z-W-T model and Weibull distribution, a dynamic damage constitutive model was established that considers strain rate effects, damage effects, and introduces an impact tendency parameter. This model achieves the coupling of strain rate effects and damage effects, aiming to provide theoretical support for safe and efficient coal mining production and the prevention and control of dynamic disasters.

Sample preparation and SHPB test scheme design

Sample preparation

This experiment involved sampling at a coal mine working face in the Binchang Mining Area. The coal samples were calibrated for bedding orientation, sealed, and stored, then transported back to the processing laboratory for sample preparation. As natural geological materials, coal rocks contain inherent defects such as microcracks, pores, and mineral impurities, and exhibit significant non-homogeneity in composition and structure. These characteristics can influence experimental results in the following ways: (1) Heterogeneity leads to variability in mechanical parameters among different samples under the same impact conditions; (2) Natural defects alter the propagation path of stress waves, increasing the randomness of crack initiation and propagation, and affecting the consistency of failure modes; (3) The distribution of micro-pores influences the characteristics of the compaction stage of coal rock, resulting in differences in the initial segment morphology of stress-strain curves. To minimize these effects, this study employed an acoustic velocity meter to screen specimens during the specimen preparation stage (Table 2) and strictly adhered to International Society for Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering (ISRM) standards for specimen size and end-face flatness35, thereby minimizing experimental errors caused by heterogeneity. Ultimately, 15 cylindrical coal rock specimens with a length-to-diameter ratio of 1:1 were prepared, each measuring Φ50 mm × 50 mm.

Test scheme

The Φ50mm split Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB) loading system was adopted in this dynamic failure test of coal rock. The entire loading system consists of a power drive device, a segmented pressure bar testing device, an energy absorption damping device, a high-speed photography system, and a data acquisition and computer processing system (Fig. 1). The parameters of the loading system are shown in Table 1. In this test, the pre-triggering method was used to record the whole process of the failure and instability of coal samples. During the test, the impact speed of the launched projectile was controlled by adjusting the nitrogen pressure in the air cylinder. The projectile impacted the incident wave, which propagated in the pressure bar and generated strain signals. These strain signals were then converted into electrical signals by the dynamic strain gauge and amplified according to the gain multiple.

The stress-strain curves of coal rock under different strain rates are shown in Fig. 2, and the nodes of each stage are delineated in detail in Fig. 3. From the analysis of the test results, it can be seen that the response process of dynamic stress-strain of coal rock under impact loading is divided into: Ⅰ. Fracture compaction stage (OA section): the initial fissure will be rapidly compacted and closed under different impact loads, and the stress-strain curve shows an obvious micro-concave shape; Ⅱ. Linear elasticity stage (AB section): in this stage, the coal rock stress shows a linear growth trend with the increase of strain, and the slope of the coal rock in this stage is basically kept constant (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${d^2}\sigma /{d^2}\varepsilon =0$$\end{document}), i.e., the coal rock is in the state of elastic deformation, and its slope can be taken as the dynamic elastic modulus of the coal rock; III. Plastic Evolution Stage (BC Segment): Commences at the endpoint of the linear elastic stage (Point B), i.e., the critical point where the stress-strain curve deviates from linear growth; concludes at the peak stress point (Point C), i.e., the point where stress reaches its maximum value. During this stage, stress growth gradually decelerates with increasing strain as the coal rock’s elastic capacity accumulates further, entering a plastic deformation phase. The stress-strain curve exhibits a convex shape before the peak. Ⅳ. Failure Stage (CD segment): As fractures progressively propagate, the coal rock’s load-bearing capacity is completely lost, ultimately leading to failure. The dynamic peak strength and strain rate of the coal rock begin to decrease rapidly and persist for an extended period37.

As shown in Fig. 2, unlike static loading, the consolidation phase is not pronounced in the dynamic stress-strain curve of coal rock. As the strain rate increases, the peak strength rises and axial deformation increases, indicating enhanced plasticity. Before reaching peak strength, plastic deformation accounts for a significant proportion in the dynamic stress-strain curve. After peak strength, no pronounced brittle drop occurs. This indicates that under high strain rate conditions, a plastic stage involving dynamic equilibrium adjustment occurs, where stress softening takes place in the coal rock. The ratio of strain in the plastic stage to peak strain represents the proportion of the plastic stage before peak strength. The difference between peak strain (strain at point C) and elastic stage strain (strain at point B) constitutes the plastic stage strain. Thus, the proportions of the plastic phase preceding peak strength at different strain rates were 40.2%, 44.9%, 57.5%, 61.2%, and 82.1%, respectively.

This study employs a combined strategy of “tangent stiffness method + quantitative threshold” to reduce the subjectivity and error in determining point B (the endpoint of the linear elastic phase). First, for the stress-strain data within the elastic phase (segment AB), the tangent stiffness K (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K{\text{=}}\Delta \sigma /\Delta \varepsilon$$\end{document}) is calculated point-by-point using a sliding window method (with a window step size of 5 data points) to reflect the continuous variation in the curve’s slope. The average tangent stiffness within the linear elastic segment was defined as K0. When the tangent stiffness at any point first decreased to 0.95K0 (i.e., a 5% stiffness decay), that point was designated as Point B35. Concurrently, the homogeneity of the coal samples (Table 2) was utilized for validation to avoid misinterpretation of accidental stiffness fluctuations caused by local defects in the specimens. Due to influences from natural micro-pores in coal rock, mineral impurities, and other factors, the determination of point B has certain limitations. At high strain rates, the rapid nucleation of microcracks within coal rock results in a smoother transition between the elastic and plastic segments, with the tangent stiffness decay curve showing no distinct “inflection point.” Even when using a 5% stiffness threshold, some error may still exist.

Processes and mechanisms of dynamic destruction of coal rock

Under the action of impact loads, the primary cracks inside coal rock will go through initiation, propagation and coalescence, and eventually lead to instability and failure38. Research shows that the entire process of crack evolution inside coal rock is the key to revealing the mechanism of dynamic failure of coal rock. Therefore, a high-speed camera system is used to monitor the complete evolution process of cracks in coal rock under dynamic loads (Fig. 6). From the failure patterns of coal rock under different impact loads, it can be seen that the strain rate has a significant influence on the degree of fragmentation, showing a clear strain rate correlation.

At a strain rate of 43.19 s− 1, approximately 50 μs, a primary crack forms along the stress wave loading path in the coal rock, accompanied by the detachment of edge fragments. The width of the primary crack gradually increases, leading to cleavage failure along the crack direction, ultimately splitting the coal sample into two halves; At a strain rate of 56.82 s− 1, a through crack forms at approximately 50 μs, which continues to propagate, resulting in cleavage and shear failure at approximately 100 μs; At a strain rate of 68.72 s− 1, multiple axial and circumferential through cracks form, followed by rapid crack propagation and a continuous increase in crack number; At strain rates of 68.72 s− 1, 82.18 s− 1, and 93.47 s− 1, the coal sample develops numerous circumferential cracks, with fine cracks along the edges, resulting in more severe damage and a large amount of coal dust being ejected. It can be seen that under high strain rates, the size of the fragments after failure is smaller, and the strain rate effect is more pronounced.

As the strain rate increases, the size of the fragments decreases significantly, and their number increases markedly, exhibiting a failure pattern characterized by “fracturing-breaking-crushing.” Under dynamic impact loads, coal rock exhibits a failure mode characterized by multiple cracks that gradually propagate, resulting in the formation of numerous microcracks internally. As microcracks develop and propagate, they preferentially form one or several wide and large main cracks. The propagation of the main cracks causes their edge zones to become relaxed, thereby hindering the formation and propagation of smaller cracks in the edge regions, ultimately leading to the suppression of small cracks. Under high strain rate dynamic impact loads, the main cracks propagate slowly, and the absorbed impact energy is absorbed by the microcracks and weak interfaces within the coal rock, resulting in multiple microcracks initiating and propagating in an intersecting manner.

Study on dynamic damage constitutive model of coal rock

The constitutive relationship of coal rock is a theoretical description of the correspondence between the macroscopic mechanical response of coal rock under external loading and the deformation and evolution of its internal microstructure under loading39. Coal rock exhibits obvious strong and embrittlement characteristics at medium and high strain rates, and contains a large number of micro-cracks, micro-pores and other defects. Coal rock exhibit obvious damage and softening characteristics under impact loads40,41. Therefore, when establishing the constitutive model of coal rock materials, both the influence on rock mechanical properties and the failure effect should be taken into account. Based on the above research, it is found that under the impact load, the influences of coal rock with different strain rates on the mechanical properties of materials show obvious differences. Based on this, a dynamic damage constitutive model of coal rock is constructed, which can reflect strain rate effect and damage effect and introduce impact tendency index.

Viscoelastic damage constitutive model of coal rock

The deformation of coal rock under impact load is a comprehensive manifestation of elasticity, plasticity and viscosity coupling, and based on the study of its characteristics, a number of typical models can be obtained by considering it as composed of a variety of ideal units connected in series and in parallel. In the model theory, the three basic characteristics of elasticity, plasticity and viscosity are described by the elastic unit, sliding sheet and viscous pot unit respectively. The stress-strain of the elements has the property of varying with time and there is no transient strain when subjected to external loads. The elements are connected in series and parallel to form different combination patterns, and then through the stress-strain relationship that needs to be satisfied by the elements connected in series and parallel, the intrinsic equation of the model is obtained.

A dynamic damage constitutive model considering strain rate effect and damage effect is established

Coal rock damage is formed by factors such as microcracks and micropores that affect damage development, and these factors will cause coal rock damage softening, so the damage softening effect must be considered in the process of establishing the coal rock ontological model. Based on the simplification of Z-W-T model above, the following three assumptions are made:

First, the coal rock has the impact tendency of continuous distribution, and the weak damaged structures such as micro-cracks and micro-holes in the coal rock are uniformly distributed. Second, the microelements of coal rock have damage and viscoelastic properties. The Z-W-T model can reflect the viscoelastic characteristics of the microscopic elements of the material and the dynamic response of the material to high and low strain rates under impact loads, but it cannot describe the failure characteristics of the material. In order to consider the damage effect of the material, it can be seen from the mechanical properties of the components that the clay pot does not have damage characteristics. In this section, the existing damage type Z-W-T model is improved and optimized, and a damage type viscoelastic constitutive model is established considering that the damage evolution law of coal rock materials is subject to Weibull distribution. Because the viscous element has no damage characteristic, the damage element is used instead of the elastic element, which makes up the defect of fuzzy physical concept when the whole model is treated with damage. The dynamic damage constitutive model with impact tendency index is used to fit the stress-strain curves of coal rock under dynamic load. The theoretical curves are in good agreement with the experimental curves, and the complex dynamic mechanical properties of strain hardening and strain rate strengthening can be comprehensively reflected.

Discussion

As shown in the validation results (Fig. 9), the viscoelastic dynamic damage constitutive model established in this paper exhibits distinct plastic characteristics in coal rock specimens at a strain rate of 82.18 s− 1. However, due to differences between the failure stage in the experiment and the actual strain during impact failure of the coal rock, the failure stage of the experimental curve does not exhibit a clear change pattern with increasing strain rate. The fitting accuracy at high strain rates is relatively poor. At strain rates of 56.82 s− 1 and 68.72 s− 1, the fitted curves of the coal rock deviate significantly from the experimental curves in both the plastic and failure stages, and the theoretical results reach their peak earlier. This indicates that the established model has certain limitations, which are attributed to the complexity of the disaster mechanisms in coal rock dynamic disasters44,45. This experiment did not consider the effects of multiple factors such as structural planes, water content, and confining pressure. Additionally, the strain rate range covered by the model is limited, and its applicability at higher strain rates requires further verification. Therefore, it cannot fully correspond to the changes in the dynamic stress-strain curve of coal rock at each loading stage. In the future, the experimental scope can be expanded to cover a wider strain rate and impact load range, and variables such as confining pressure and moisture content can be introduced. Combining CT scanning and numerical simulation can more accurately reveal the intrinsic connection between the impact tendency index and the microdefects of coal rock.

Conclusion

By carrying out SHPB tests under different impact loads, the mechanical properties and energy evolution characteristics of coal rock with impact inclination under different loading conditions are studied. Based on Z-W-T viscoelastic constitutive model, a dynamic damage constitutive model of coal rock is established, which can consider strain rate effect and damage effect and introduce impact inclination index. The main conclusions are as follows:

The stress-strain dynamic response process of coal rock under impact load can be divided into: fracture compaction stage, linear elastic stage, plastic evolution stage and failure stage; The relationship between the peak stress and strain rate is approximately one time function increasing, and the relationship between the peak strain and strain rate is logarithmic function increasing.

Overview of This Information Collection

    Abstract: Federal explosives licensees/permittees (FEL/Ps) play a 
significant role in preserving public safety, including preventing 
crimes involving explosives, such as domestic or foreign terrorist 
acts. ATF's regulation at 27 CFR 555.121 provides that FEL/Ps must keep 
records pertaining to explosive materials in permanent form (e.g., 
commercial invoices, record books) and in the manner required in 27 CFR 
part 555, subpart G. These records are required under 18 U.S.C. 842 
(f),(g),(j), (k), 843(f), and 847, and are established in the manner 
set forth in 27 CFR 555.121, 555.122, 555.123, 555.124, 555.125, and 
555.127. As part of their record-keeping requirements, FEL/Ps must take 
a physical inventory at least once during each year. FEL/Ps must also 
take special physical inventories when they are: (1) commencing 
business, (2) changing premises, (3) discontinuing business, or, (4) 
requested to do so by the ATF Director, Industry Operations. If one of 
these special inventories occurs, the FEL/P is not required to take an 
additional inventory that same year. The inventory aids FEL/Ps in 
accounting for all their explosives material and reconciling cost, 
sales, and use records with inventory. It also establishes initial 
figures from which an audit trail can be developed by ATF field 
officers during a compliance inspection or criminal investigation. 
Specifically, ATF uses inventory figures to reconcile the physical 
inventory on hand and the book inventory the FEL/P maintains. These 
reconciliations are conducted on an ongoing basis, to determine whether 
any unlawful diversions of explosive materials have occurred, 
especially those arising through theft by persons not affiliated with 
the FEL/P.

Revisions to This Information Collection

    As part of this ICR renewal, ATF is making no revisions to this 
information collection, OMB 1140-0028. However, ATF has revised the 
title of the ICR from ``Inventories: Licensed Explosives Importers, 
Manufacturers, Dealers, and Permittees'' to ``Explosives Licensee/
Permittee Inventories'' for plain writing purposes and to make it 
shorter. In addition, the number of annual respondents has decreased 
since the last renewal--from 9,219 in 2023 to 9,130 in 2026--a drop of 
89 respondents. This resulted in a corresponding decrease in the total 
annual burden hours, from 18,438 to 18,260, a difference of 178 hours.

Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem under max-degree constraints
Xizhi Liu, Sijie Ren, Jian Wang

Abstract
We establish the following strengthening of the celebrated Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem: If G G is an n n -vertex K r + 1 K_{r+1} -free graph whose minimum degree δ ⁡ ( G ) \delta(G) and maximum degree Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) satisfy δ ⁡ ( G ) > min ⁡ { 3 ​ r − 4 3 ​ r − 2 ​ n − Δ ⁡ ( G ) 3 ​ r − 2 , n − Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 1 r − 1 } , \displaystyle\delta(G)>\min\left\{\frac{3r-4}{3r-2}n-\frac{\Delta(G)}{3r-2},~n-\frac{\Delta(G)+1}{r-1}\right\}, then G G is r r -partite. This bound is tight for all feasible values of Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) . We also obtain an analogous tight result for graphs with large odd girth. Our proof does not rely on the Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem itself, and therefore yields an alternative proof of this classical result.

Introduction

Given a family
\mathcal{F}
of graphs, we say a graph
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free if it does not contain any member of
\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph.
Let
K_{r}
and
C_{r}
denote the complete graph and the cycle on
r
vertices, respectively.
Extending the seminal theorem of Turán
[
Tur41
]
, Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós
[
AES74
]
established the following classical result.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
AES74
]
)
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer.
Every
K_{r+1}
-free graph on
n
vertices with minimum degree greater than
\frac{3r-4}{3r-1}n
is
r
-partite.

Let
C_{\leq 2k+1}\coloneqq\{C_{3},C_{5},\cdots,C_{2k+1}\}
denote the collection of all odd cycles of length at most
2k+1
.
The following theorem was also established in
[
AES74
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
AES74
]
)
.

Every
C_{\leq 2k+1}
-free graph on
n
vertices with minimum degree greater than
\frac{2}{2k+3}n
is bipartite.

In this note, we study the affect of a large degree vertex on the two theorems of Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós.
Our motivation partly comes from a classical result of Balister–Bollobás–Riordan–Schelp
[
BBRS03
]
, which, extending the seminal theorems of Mantel
[
Man07
]
and Simonovits
[
Sim68
]
(see also
[
Bol78
, p. 150]
), shows that for large
n
, the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
C_{2k+1}
-free graph with maximum degree
\Delta\in[n/2,~n-k-1]
is achieved by the complete bipartite graph with one part of size
\Delta
.
A similar statement for
K_{r+1}
-free graph follows easily from Erdős’ degree-majorization algorithm
[
Erd70
]
(see also
[
Für15
]
).
More recently, related questions have been investigated in
[
HY22
]
and
[
HHL+24
]
for edge-critical graphs and hypergraphs, with further applications to matching-type problems
[
HLL+23
,
HHL+25
,
DHLY25
]
.

For a graph
G
, let
\delta(G)
and
\Delta(G)
denote the minimum and maximum degree of
G
, respectively.
The main results of this note are as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer.
Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex
K_{r+1}
-free graph satisfying

\displaystyle\delta(G)>\min\left\{\frac{3r-4}{3r-2}n-\frac{\Delta(G)}{3r-2},~n-\frac{\Delta(G)+1}{r-1}\right\}.

Then
G
is
r
-partite.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer.
Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex
C_{\leq 2k+1}
-free graph satisfying

\displaystyle\delta(G)>\min\left\{\frac{n}{k+1}-\frac{\Delta(G)}{2k+2},~\frac{n-1-\Delta(G)}{k}\right\}.

Then
G
is bipartite.

It is straightforward to verify that Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
imply the corresponding theorems of Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós.
We note that our proofs differ from those of Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós and the alternative elegant short proof of Brandt
[
Bra03
]
, and they do not rely on Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. Thus, they provide alternative proofs for both theorems of Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós.
Moreover, the bounds in the above theorems are tight, as demonstrated by the following constructions.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Our proof proceeds by induction on
r
.
We begin with the base case
r=2
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex
K_{3}
-free graph satisfying

\displaystyle\delta(G)>\min\left\{\frac{n}{2}-\frac{\Delta(G)}{4},~n-\Delta(G)-1\right\}.

Then
G
is bipartite.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{3}
-free graph.
For convenience, let
V\coloneqq V(G)
,
\Delta\coloneqq\Delta(G)
, and
\delta\coloneqq\delta(G)
.

Case 1
:
\delta>n-\Delta-1
.

Since both
\delta
and
\Delta
are integers, this assumption is equivalent to
\delta+\Delta\geq n
.
Fix a vertex
u\in V
with
d_{G}(u)=\Delta
, and let
v\in N_{G}(u)
. Since
G
is
K_{3}
-free, we have
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)=\emptyset
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle|N_{G}(u)|+|N_{G}(v)|\geq\Delta+\delta=n.

Thus,
N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(v)=V
, and this union is a partition.
By the
K_{3}
-freeness of
G
, both
N_{G}(u)
and
N_{G}(v)
are independent sets.
Thus,
G
is bipartite with parts
N_{G}(u)
and
N_{G}(v)
.

Case 2
:
\delta>\frac{n}{2}-\frac{\Delta}{4}
.

Similar to the proof of Case 1.
Let
\{u,v\}\in G
be an edge with
d_{G}(u)=\Delta
.
Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be two disjoint independent sets in
G
such that
N_{G}(v)\subseteq V_{1}
and
N_{G}(u)\subseteq V_{2}
, and such that
|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|
is maximized.
Let
T\coloneqq V\setminus(V_{1}\cup V_{2})
.
Since
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)=\emptyset
, we have

\displaystyle|T|\leq n-|N_{G}(u)|-|N_{G}(v)|\leq n-\Delta-\delta.

(1)

We may assume
T\neq\emptyset
, since otherwise,
G
is a bipartite graph with parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
.
Fix a vertex
w\in T
.
By the maximality of
|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|
, the vertex
w
must have neighbors in both
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
(otherwise, we can move
w
to
V_{1}
or
V_{2}
).
Let
N_{1}\coloneqq N_{G}(w)\cap V_{1}
and
N_{2}\coloneqq N_{G}(w)\cap V_{2}
, and choose
v_{1}\in N_{1}
and
v_{2}\in N_{2}
.
Since
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are independent, we have

\displaystyle N_{G}(v_{1})\cap V_{1}=N_{G}(v_{2})\cap V_{2}=\emptyset.

(2)

Since
G
is
K_{3}
-free, we also have
N_{G}(v_{1})\cap N_{G}(w)=N_{G}(v_{2})\cap N_{G}(w)=\emptyset
.
It follows that

\displaystyle|N_{G}(v_{1})\cap T|+|N_{G}(v_{2})\cap T|+|N_{G}(w)\cap T|

\displaystyle\leq 2|T|,

\displaystyle|N_{G}(v_{1})\cap V_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq|V_{2}|-|N_{2}|,

\displaystyle|N_{G}(v_{2})\cap V_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq|V_{1}|-|N_{1}|.

Combining these inequalities with (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle d_{G}(v_{1})+d_{G}(v_{2})+d_{G}(w)\leq|V_{2}|-|N_{2}|+|V_{1}|-|N_{1}|+|N_{1}|+|N_{2}|+2|T|=n+|T|.

Together with (
1
), this yields

\displaystyle 3\delta\leq n+|T|\leq n+n-\Delta-\delta,

which simplifies to
\delta\leq\frac{n}{2}-\frac{\Delta}{4}
, contradicting our assumption.

We are now ready to present the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We prove this theorem by induction on
r
.
The base case
r=2
is established in Lemma
2.1
.
Thus we may assume that
r\geq 3
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{r+1}
-free graph.
For convenience, let
V\coloneqq V(G)
,
\Delta\coloneqq\Delta(G)
, and
\delta\coloneqq\delta(G)
.

Case 1
:
\delta>n-\frac{\Delta+1}{r-1}
.

Since both
\delta
and
\Delta
are integers, the assumption is equivalent to
(r-1)\delta+\Delta\geq(r-1)n
(and thus also to
\delta\geq n-\frac{\Delta}{r-1}
).
Thus, by adding edges to
G
while preserving the condition that
G
remains
K_{r+1}
-free, we may assume that
G
is maximal, that is, the addition of any new edge to
G
creates a copy of
K_{r+1}
.

Let
u\in V
be a vertex of maximum degree in
G
.
Let
X\coloneqq N_{G}(u)
and let
H\coloneqq G[X]
.
Since
G
is
K_{r+1}
-free, the graph
H
is
K_{r}
-free.

Claim 2.2
.

The graph
H
is
(r-1)
-partite.

Proof of Claim
2.2
.

Fix a vertex
v\in X
of minimum degree in
H
.
It follows from the assumption on
\delta
that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(H)}{|X|}\geq\frac{d_{G}(v)-\left(n-|X|\right)}{|X|}\geq\frac{\delta-(n-\Delta)}{\Delta}\geq\frac{1}{\Delta}\left(n-\frac{\Delta}{r-1}-(n-\Delta)\right)=\frac{r-2}{r-1}.

Rearranging yields
(r-1)\delta(H)\geq(r-2)|X|
, and thus

\displaystyle(r-2)\delta(H)+\Delta(H)\geq(r-1)\delta(H)\geq(r-2)|X|.

By the inductive hypothesis,
H
is
(r-1)
-partite.

Let
V_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}=X
be an
(r-1)
-partition such that
H[V_{i}]=\emptyset
for
i\in[2,r]
.
We may assume that there exists a vertex
w\in V\setminus(X\cup\{u\})
, since otherwise
G
is already
r
-partite.
Note that
\{u,w\}\not\in G
.
Thus, by the maximality of
G
, the induced subgraph of
G
on
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(w)\subseteq N_{G}(u)
contains a copy of
K_{r-1}
.
It follows that there exist vertices
v_{i}\in V_{i}
for
i\in[2,r]
such that
\{v_{2},\ldots,v_{r}\}
induces a copy of
K_{r-1}
in
G
.

Let
V_{1}\coloneqq\bigcap_{i=2}^{r}N_{G}(v_{i})\subseteq V\setminus X
, noting that
V_{1}
is independent in
G
.
Using the assumption on
\delta
and the Inclusion-Exclusion Principle, we obtain

\displaystyle|V_{1}|\geq(r-1)\delta-(r-2)n\geq(r-1)\left(n-\frac{\Delta}{r-1}\right)-(r-2)n\geq n-\Delta=n-|X|.

Thus,
V_{1}=V\setminus X
, and hence,
V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}
is a partition of
V
.
Consequently,
G
is
r
-partite with parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
.

Case 2
:
\delta>\frac{3r-4}{3r-2}n-\frac{\Delta}{3r-2}
.

Note that the assumption is equivalent to that
(3r-2)\delta+\Delta>(3r-4)n
. As in Case 1, we may assume that
G
is maximal.

Let
u\in V
be a vertex of maximum degree in
G
.
Let
X\coloneqq N_{G}(u)
and let
H\coloneqq G[X]
.
Since
G
is
K_{r+1}
-free, the graph
H
is
K_{r}
-free.

Claim 2.3
.

The graph
H
is
(r-1)
-partite.

Proof of Claim
2.3
.

Fix a vertex
v\in X
of minimum degree in
H
.
It follows from the assumption on
\delta
that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(H)}{|X|}\geq\frac{d_{G}(v)-\left(n-|X|\right)}{|X|}\geq\frac{\delta-(n-\Delta)}{\Delta}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{\Delta}\left(\frac{3r-4}{3r-2}n-\frac{\Delta}{r-1}-(n-\Delta)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3r-3}{3r-2}-\frac{2n}{(3r-2)\Delta}.

Since
(3r-1)\Delta\geq(3r-2)\delta+\Delta>(3r-4)n
, we have
\Delta>\frac{3r-4}{3r-1}n
.
Substituting this bound into the previous inequality gives

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(H)}{|X|}>\frac{3r-3}{3r-2}-\frac{2}{3r-2}\cdot\frac{3r-1}{3r-4}=\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(3r-5)\delta(H)+\Delta(H)\geq(3r-4)\delta(H)>(3r-7)|X|.

So it follows from the inductive hypothesis that
H
is
(r-1)
-partite.

Let
V_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}=X
be an
(r-1)
-partition such that
H[V_{i}]=\emptyset
for
i\in[2,r]
.
As in Case 1, by the maximality of
G
, there exist vertices
v_{i}\in V_{i}
for
i\in[2,r]
such that
\{v_{2},\ldots,v_{r}\}
induces a copy of
K_{r-1}
in
G
.

Let
V_{1}
be a maximum independent set in
G
subject to the condition that
\bigcap_{i=2}^{r}N_{G}(v_{i})\subseteq V_{1}
.
This is well-defined because
\bigcap_{i=2}^{r}N_{G}(v_{i})
is independent in
G
(by the
K_{r+1}
-freeness of
G
).
Observe also that
V_{1}\subseteq V\setminus X
.
It follows from the assumption on
\delta(G)
and the Inclusion-Exclusion Principle that

\displaystyle|V_{1}|\geq\left|\cap_{i=2}^{r}N_{G}(v_{i})\right|\geq(r-1)\delta-(r-2)n

\displaystyle>(r-1)\left(\frac{3r-4}{3r-2}n-\frac{\Delta}{3r-2}\right)-(r-2)n

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{3r-2}n-\frac{r-1}{3r-2}\Delta.

(3)

Let
Z\coloneqq V\setminus(V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r})
.
Using (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle|Z|=n-\left(|V_{1}|+\cdots+|V_{r}|\right)=n-|V_{1}|-|X|

\displaystyle<n-\left(\frac{r}{3r-2}n-\frac{r-1}{3r-2}\Delta\right)-\Delta

\displaystyle=\frac{2r-2}{3r-2}n-\frac{2r-1}{3r-2}\Delta.

(4)

We may assume that
Z\neq\emptyset
, since otherwise
G
is
r
-partite with parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
.
Fix a vertex
z\in Z
.
It follows from the maximality of
V_{1}
that
N_{G}(z)\cap V_{1}\neq\emptyset
.

Fix
u_{1}\in N_{G}(z)\cap V_{1}
. Since
X=N_{G}(u)
and
z\not\in X
, we have
u_{1}\neq u
.
Let
S\subseteq X
be a largest subset such that
\{z,u_{1}\}\cup S
induces a complete subgraph in
G
, noting from the
K_{r+1}
-freeness of
G
that
|S|\leq r-2
.

Let
j\coloneqq|S|+1\leq r-1
, and assume that
S=\{u_{2},\ldots,u_{j}\}
.
For each
i\in[2,r]
, since
G[V_{i}]=\emptyset
, we have
|S\cap V_{i}|\leq 1
.
By relabeling the sets
V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}
if necessary, we may assume that
u_{i}\in V_{i}
for
i\in[2,j]
.

Claim 2.4
.

We have
j=r-1
.

Proof of Claim
2.4
.

Let
N\coloneqq N_{G}(z)\cap N_{G}(u_{1})\cap\cdots\cap N_{G}(u_{j})
.
Suppose to the contrary that
j\leq r-2
.
Then it follows from the assumption on
\delta(G)
and the Inclusion-Exclusion Principle that

\displaystyle|N|\geq(j+1)\delta-jn\geq(r-2+1)\delta-(r-2)n

\displaystyle>(r-1)\left(\frac{3r-4}{3r-2}n-\frac{\Delta}{3r-2}\right)-(r-2)n

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{3r-2}n-\frac{r-1}{3r-2}\Delta.

It follows from the maximality of
S
that
N\subseteq Z
, and hence,
|N|\leq|Z|
.
Combining this with (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{r}{3r-2}n-\frac{r-1}{3r-2}\Delta<\frac{2r-2}{3r-2}n-\frac{2r-1}{3r-2}\Delta,

which implies that

\displaystyle\Delta<\frac{r-2}{r}n<\frac{3r-4}{3r-1}n,

a contradiction to our assumption.
Thus,
j=r-1
, proving the claim.

Claim
2.4
shows that
\{z,u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-1}\}
induces a copy of
K_{r}
in
G
.
Recall that
X=N_{G}(u)
and
z\not\in X
.
By the maximality of
G
, there exists an
(r-1)
-set
W=\{w_{2},\ldots,w_{r}\}\subseteq N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(z)\subseteq X
such that
W
induces a copy of
K_{r-1}
in
G
.
Since
G[X]
is
(r-1)
-partite,

4
Concluding remarks

In this note, we studied the effect of a large-degree vertex on the classical Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem.
We hope this will motivate further attention to the broader program of understanding how the presence of a large-degree vertex influences extremal problems.
For example, a natural step beyond Theorem
1.3
would be to investigate the results of
[
Jin93
,
CJK97
,
HJ98
,
GL11
]
under an additional maximum-degree constraint.

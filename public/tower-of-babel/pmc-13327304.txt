Methodological considerations in interpreting circRNA profiles in diabetic retinopathy
Authors: Yindan Song, Xujun He
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

We read with great interest the article by An et al. 1 , which profiled serum circular RNAs in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus with and without diabetic retinopathy (DR) and identified hsa_circRNA_407262 as a potentially important biomarker and mechanistic candidate 1 . The study provides valuable exploratory data on circulating circRNAs in DR, an area of increasing clinical relevance. However, we have several methodological concerns that potentially threaten the validity of the conclusions.

First, the microarray discovery strategy may have generated an inflated number of statistically significant circRNAs. The authors reported 376 differentially expressed circRNAs, including 169 upregulated and 207 downregulated circRNAs, using a threshold of fold change ≥1 and P < 0.05. Given that the Arraystar platform screened thousands of circRNAs, reliance on nominal P values without an explicitly reported false‐discovery‐rate correction substantially increases the risk of type I error 2 . This concern is especially important because only 8 circRNAs were ultimately advanced to ceRNA analysis, and 2 of 10 selected circRNAs showed expression trends opposite to the microarray results during qRT‐PCR validation. Applying the Benjamini–Hochberg or comparable FDR correction, reporting adjusted P values, and using a biologically meaningful effect‐size threshold would strengthen confidence that the prioritized circRNAs are not false‐positive signals.

Second, the evidence does not yet adequately support the proposed diagnostic‐biomarker implication. Although the authors concluded that hsa_circRNA_407262 may serve as a candidate biomarker for early diagnosis, the study did not report receiver operating characteristic curves, calibration, sensitivity, specificity, or multivariable models adjusted for established DR determinants such as diabetes duration, HbA1c, lipid levels, blood pressure, and renal function. table 1 shows numerically longer diabetes duration and lower triglycerides in the DR group, but the subsequent circRNA analyses appear unadjusted. Without diagnostic‐performance assessment and covariate adjustment, it remains unclear whether circRNA_407262 adds discriminatory value beyond conventional clinical variables 3 . Future analyses should evaluate incremental predictive value in externally validated models.

In summary, this study offers useful exploratory evidence, but stronger multiplicity control and formal diagnostic modeling are essential for correctly interpreting the circRNA findings and for guiding future biomarker research in DR.

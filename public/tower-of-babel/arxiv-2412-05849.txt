Irregular Hodge numbers of Frenkel–Gross connections
Yichen Qin, Christian Sevenheck, Peter Spacek

Abstract
Frenkel and Gross constructed a family of connections on ℙ 1 \ { 0 , ∞ } \mathbb{P}^{1}\backslash\{0,\infty\} , for almost simple groups G ˇ \check{G} and their representations. In this article, we calculate the irregular Hodge numbers of these Frenkel–Gross connections, and, as an application, we prove a conjecture of Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pantev for mirror Landau-Ginzburg models of minuscule homogeneous spaces.

Introduction

The study of irregular singularities of systems of linear differential equations has gained increasing importance across various mathematical fields, such as Hodge theory or arithmetics. Since the differential equations underlying variations of Hodge structures (VHS) can only have regular singularities, we need a broader framework to discuss the Hodge theoretic properties of irregular connections.

A famous example is the Bessel differential equation
(t\partial_{t})^{n+1}-t
. The associated connection, called the
Bessel connection
(or
Kloosterman connection
), is the connection

\nabla=\mathrm{d}+\begin{pmatrix}0&&&t\\
1&\ddots&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&1&0\end{pmatrix}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

(1.0.1)

on the trivial bundle
\mathcal{O}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Be}_{n+1}
. It has a regular singularity at
0
, and an irregular singularity at
\infty
of slope
\frac{1}{n+1}
. When
n=1
, Deligne showed that
\mathrm{Be}_{2}
cannot underlie a variation of Hodge structures
[
Del07
, §8]
, and hence, there does not exist any Hodge filtration on
\mathrm{Be}_{2}
. Instead, Deligne suggested and studied the concept of irregular Hodge filtrations on irregular connections
[
Del07
]
, which was further developed by Esnault, Kontsevich, Sabbah, and Yu in
[
Sab10
,
Yu14
,
SY15
,
ESY17
]
. For example, the irregular Hodge filtration on
\mathrm{Be}_{n+1}
jumps at
0,1,\dots,n
, and the irregular Hodge numbers are all
1
, see e.g.
[
CnDS21
]
.

Since then, a solid framework of the theory of irregular Hodge structures and irregular Hodge modules has been developed in
[
KS11
,
Sab18a
,
FSY22
,
Moc25
]
. We will use the fundamental results of these papers in an essential way here.
Rather than using the term irregular Hodge modules, we adopt the terminology of rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules, as introduced in
[
Moc25
]
, cf.
Section

3.2
. In particular, the underlying
\mathscr{D}
-module of any rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module comes equipped with an irregular Hodge filtration.

The purpose of this paper is to study irregular Hodge theory for certain rigid irregular connections on principal bundles. More precisely,
given any almost simple complex algebraic group
\check{G}
,
we are concerned with a family of connections (denoted
\nabla_{\check{G}}
) on the trivial
\check{G}
-bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}\backslash\{0,\infty\}

that has been constructed by
Frenkel and Gross in
[
FG09
]
, cf. (
2.1.1
). These connections are regular singular at
0
and irregular singular at
\infty
. They are cohomologically rigid
[
FG09
, Thm. 1]
and expected to be physically rigid. The physical rigidity is not known to hold in general, but has been shown when
\check{G}
is of adjoint type
[
Yi24
, Thm. 1]
or simply connected
[
HJ
, Thm 1.1.1]
.
By considering specific representations of
\check{G}
, one can recover examples such as the Bessel connection and certain hypergeometric connections, cf.
Example

2.1
.

Our main result establishes the existence of a rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module associated to Frenkel–Gross connections and provides an explicit computation of the corresponding irregular Hodge numbers.

In the statement of the following theorem, we denote by
\rho=2\rho/2
the half sum of positive coroots of
\check{G}
, which can also be considered as the half sum of positive roots of
G

.

\check{G}

\nabla_{\check{G}}

Frobenius slopes.

One of Deligne’s motivations for studying irregular Hodge filtrations is to obtain lower bounds for
p
-adic slopes of exponential sums. Let us mention an arithmetic application of
Theorem

1.1
for the case of Frenkel–Gross connections, as mentioned in
[
LT24
, Rem. 13.9]
. In
[
XZ22
, Thm. 5.3.2]
, Xu and Zhu equipped Frenkel–Gross connections with Frobenius structures and studied their
p
-adic slopes—i.e.
p
-adic valuations of eigenvalues of Frobenius on Frenkel–Gross connections. The traces of the Frobenius actions are called
Kloosterman sums (for reductive groups)
, which are sums of Frobenius eigenvalues. They showed that the
p
-adic slopes are determined by
\rho
(except for finitely many points). In combination with this result,
Theorem

1.1
implies that the
p
-adic slopes agree with the irregular Hodge numbers. In particular, when
\check{G}=\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}
, we recover a theorem of Sperber
[
Spe80
]
.

Mirror symmetry.

Another motivation for studying irregular Hodge filtration arises from mirror symmetry. As mirrors of Fano varieties one considers Landau–Ginzburg models, namely, pairs
(Y,w)
consisting of a quasi-projective complex variety and a regular function
w\colon Y\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
. A great deal of work has been carried out over the last decades to establish such mirror correspondences, see, e.g.,
[
Iri09
]
and
[
RS15
,
RS17
]
for the toric case, and, on the other hand,
[
Rie08
]
and
[
LT24
]
for the case of homogeneous spaces.

In
[
KKP17
]
, Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pantev proposed three kinds of (conjecturally identical) Hodge numbers of
(Y,w)
, including the irregular Hodge numbers. (Notice, however, that one of these conjectured equalities is incorrectly stated, as can be seen by looking at the case of the mirror of projective spaces.) In particular, this yields a conjectured relationship comparing the Hodge numbers for Fano varieties and the irregular Hodge numbers for their Landau–Ginzburg models. As an application of
Theorem

1.1
, we confirm this conjecture in
Theorem

4.1
for Fano varieties that are minuscule homogeneous spaces.

Idea of the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

In order to orient the reader, let us sketch informally the strategy to show our main result
Theorem

1.1
.
The first ingredient is the fact that the Frenkel–Gross connections
{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)
are of (exponentially) geometrical origin. In fact, by a theorem of Zhu
[
Zhu17a
]
, they are eigen
\mathscr{D}
-modules of Hecke operators, constructed via the method of Heinloth–Ngô–Yun
[
HNY13
]
. Using this strategy, we equip
in
Section

3.2
the connection
{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)
with the structure of a rescalable integrable pure twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module of weight
0
. Furthermore, we show that there is a unique lifting of
\nabla_{\check{G}}
to a tensor functor
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}
from the category of finite dimensional representations of
\check{G}
to the category of rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
(see the discussion before formula (
3.2.5
)).

Via the functoriality of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}
(see
Section

2.2
), we reduce (Lemmas
3.14
and
3.15
) the calculation of Hodge numbers to that of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)
for simple groups
\check{G}
and
V=\mathrm{Ad}(\check{G})
, the adjoint representation of
\check{G}
.

The next step is to express the Frenkel–Gross connections
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
as the Fourier transforms of some regular singular connections. By the stationary phase principle (cf.
[
Sab08
]
), the Fourier transform of a regular
\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\tau}}
-module is a
\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}}
-module (where
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{t}
is the dual affine line of
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{\tau}
) which is smooth on
\mathbb{G}_{m,t}
, regular singular at
0
, and irregular singular at
\infty
of slope
0
or
1
. Frenkel and Gross showed that
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
has slope
0
or
1/h
where
h
is the Coxeter number of
\check{G}
. Therefore, we rather work with
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V):=[h]^{+}\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
, which has the same irregular Hodge numbers as
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
. Using the geometrical presentation of Frenkel–Gross connections mentioned above, we express
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)
as the Fourier transform of some regular
\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\tau}}
-modules in
Proposition

2.5
.

By a (filtered) stationary phase formula
[
SY19
]
, to calculate the irregular Hodge numbers of
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)
, it suffices to calculate the Hodge numbers of the nearby cycle at infinity of
\mathrm{FT}^{-1}(j_{+}\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V))
, where
j:\mathbb{G}_{m,t}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{t}
. In general, the inverse Fourier transform of Frenkel–Gross connections involves some intersection complexes, which makes the Hodge numbers tricky to handle. However, the nilpotent part of the monodromy of the Frenkel–Gross connections at
0
is quite special, namely principally nilpotent. Through the stationary phase principle mentioned above, we can easily determine the monodromy of the inverse Fourier transform
\mathrm{FT}^{-1}\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)
at
\infty
using representation-theoretic information of
V
. In particular, when the nilpotent part of the monodromy operator can be decomposed into Jordan blocks of different sizes, we calculate in
Corollary

3.17
the formula of Hodge numbers of the nearby cycle of
\mathrm{FT}^{-1}(j_{+}\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V))
at
\infty
. Since this condition holds for adjoint representations, we conclude the proof of our main result.

Simple groups.

For an almost simple reductive group
G
over
\mathds{C}
, we fix a choice of a maximal torus
T
and a Borel subgroup
B
containing
T
. The
root datum
of
G
is the data
(X_{*},\Phi,X^{*},\Phi^{\vee},\cdot^{\vee})
, where
X^{*}=X^{*}(T)
and
X_{*}=X_{*}(T)
denote respectively the
character
and
cocharacter groups
, where
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\vee}
denote the
root system
and
coroot system
, and where
\cdot^{\vee}:\Phi\to\Phi^{\vee}:\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\vee}
is a bijection such that
\langle\alpha,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=2
. For each
\alpha\in\Phi
, we denote by
u_{\alpha}:\mathbb{G}_{a}\simeq U_{\alpha}\subset G
the corresponding
root subgroup
.

With the choice of
T\subset B\subset G
, we have the subset
\Phi^{+}
(resp.
\Phi^{-}
) of positive (resp. negative) roots in
\Phi
, i.e. those
\alpha\in\Phi

(resp.
U_{\alpha}\not\subset B
). Moreover, we have a subset
\Delta\subset\Phi^{+}
of
simple roots
. Every element in
\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{-}
can be expressed as a non-negative integer linear combination of elements in
\Delta
. Moreover, we have the
root space decomposition
(also called the

) of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of

as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathfrak{t}=\mathrm{Lie}(T)
is the Cartan sub-Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is the (one-dimensional)

\alpha\in\Phi
.

There exists a unique almost simple reductive group
\check{G}
over
\mathds{C}
with root datum
(X^{*},\Phi^{\vee},X_{*},\Phi,(\cdot^{\vee})^{-1})

of

Frenkel–Gross connections

2.1
Definition of Frenkel–Gross connections

Let
\check{G}
be an almost simple group over
\mathds{C}

It was shown in

(2.2.3)

2.3
Heinloth–Ngo–Yun’s Kloosterman connections

Heinloth–Ngô–Yun constructed (
\ell
-adic) Kloosterman sheaves for reductive groups in
[
HNY13
]
. Applying their construction to
\mathscr{D}
-modules, we also get some
\check{G}
-connections on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

For each almost simple group
G
, we fix a maximal torus and a Borel subgroup
T\subset B\subset G
. Recall that the
loop group

LG
(resp. the
positive loop group

L^{+}G
) is the fppf sheaf on the category of
k
-algebras, defined by

R\mapsto G(R((t))\quad(\text{resp. }R\mapsto G(R[[t]])).

The
affine Grassmannian

\mathrm{Gr}=\mathrm{Gr}_{G}
is the fppf-quotient
LG/L^{+}G

[
Zhu17b
, Prop. 1.3.6]
, which is represented by an ind-scheme.
The group
L^{+}G
has a natural action on
\mathrm{Gr}
, and the orbits are indexed by dominant cocharacters
\lambda\in\Phi^{\vee+}
. This orbit and its closure are denoted by
\mathrm{Gr}_{\lambda}
and
\mathrm{Gr}_{\leq\lambda}
respectively
[
Zhu17b
, Prop. 2.1.5]
.

\mathrm{Gr}_{\leq\lambda}
is a finite-dimensional algebraic variety,
with
\dim(\mathrm{Gr}_{\leq\lambda})=\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle
, and

\mathrm{Gr}_{\lambda}
is an open dense subvariety of it.
We denote by
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}
the intersection complex on
\mathrm{Gr}_{\leq\lambda}
.
For each irreducible representation
V
of the dual group
\check{G}
of highest weight
\lambda
, we also write
\mathrm{IC}_{V}=\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}
.

The Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmanian,

\pi\colon\mathrm{GR}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
, over
\mathbb{G}_{m}
is a global version of affine Grassmannians, see for example
[
Zhu17b
, Def. 3.11]
. We denote by
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{rot}}
the torus
\mathbb{G}_{m}
that acts on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
by multiplication. The specified action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{rot}}
on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
induces an action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{rot}}
on
\mathrm{GR}
such that
\pi
is
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{rot}}
-equivariant. Using this action, we can trivialize the fibration
\pi
as

\mathrm{GR}\simeq\mathrm{Gr}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{m},

where
\mathrm{Gr}
is the affine Grassmannian for
G
, see
[
HNY13
, (5.11)]
.

Let
\mathrm{Bun}_{G(1,2)}
be the moduli stack of
G
-bundles on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
with some level structures at
0
and
\infty
determined by level groups
I(1)
and
I(2)
, as defined in
[
HNY13
, §1.2]
. Then there is a projection
\mathrm{pr}\colon\mathrm{GR}\to\mathrm{Bun}_{G(1,2)}
, and we have the following diagram in
[
HNY13
, §5.2]
or
[
Yun15
, (2.2)]
as follows:

3
The irregular Hodge numbers of Frenkel–Gross connections

In this section, the aim is to prove
Theorem

1.1
. We first recall necessary properties of exponential mixed Hodge modules (or rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules) in
Section

3.1
. Then we will equip Frenkel–Gross connections with structures of exponential mixed Hodge modules in
Section

3.2
. In
Section

3.3
, we will calculate the Hodge numbers of inverse Fourier transformations of pull-backs of Frenkel–Gross connections, which can be calculated from the unipotent monodromy. In
Section

3.4
, we use the functorial properties of Frenkel–Gross connections and a filtered stationary phase formula due to Sabbah-Yu to conclude the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

3.1
Recollections on rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules

Recall from the introduction that we have for any complex algebraic variety
M
the abelian category
\mathrm{MHM}(M,{\mathds{R}})
of
mixed Hodge modules
on
M
with coefficients in
{\mathds{R}}
, which is the category of
{\mathds{R}}
-polarized mixed Hodge structures if
M
is a point. Recall also that we have a forgetful functor
\mathrm{MHM}(M,{\mathds{R}})\to\mathscr{D}_{M}
(and an induced functor between the respective derived categories) compatible with the six functor formalism on both sides.

There is a related category
\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M,{\mathds{R}})
of
integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}_{M}
-modules
when
M
is smooth, which is built in a parallel way (with major new challenges though) to the theory of mixed Hodge modules but uses
\mathscr{R}_{M}^{\mathrm{int}}=\mathscr{D}_{M}[z]\langle z^{2}\partial_{z}\rangle
-modules instead of
\mathscr{D}_{M}
-modules. The underlying
\mathscr{R}
-modules
\mathscr{M}
of objects in
\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)
give rise to (not necessarily regular) holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{M}
-modules through the functor
\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathscr{M})=\mathscr{M}/(z-1)\mathscr{M}
. The induced functor
\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}\colon D^{b}\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)\to D^{b}_{h}(\mathscr{D}_{M})
is also compatible with the six functor formalism and nearby cycle functors
[
Moc15
, Paragraph after Prop. 14.1.24]
. Furthermore, there is a fully faithful exact functor

v\colon D^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(M))\to D^{b}\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)

(3.1.1)

that is built via the Rees construction and which is compatible with the six-functor formalism, as detailed in
[
Moc15
, Prop. 13.5.5 & Prop. 14.3.29]
.

We are interested in a category of “irregular Hodge modules” that lies in between
\mathrm{MHM}(M,{\mathds{R}})
and
\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)
. It was first defined and studied by Sabbah
[
Sab18a
]
. We will rather work with two related categories (studied by Mochizuki in
[
Moc25
]
): namely, the category of rescalable integrable twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules and the category of exponential mixed Hodge modules.

Definition 3.1
.

The category
\mathrm{EMHM}(M)
of
exponential mixed Hodge modules
on
M
is defined as the full subcategory of
\mathrm{MHM}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\theta}\times M,{\mathds{R}})
whose objects
N^{\mathrm{H}}
satisfy
\pi_{M*}N^{\mathrm{H}}=0
for the projection
\pi_{M}\colon\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\theta}\times M\to M
. When
M
is a point, we recover the category of exponential mixed Hodge structures, as defined in
[
KS11
]
.

The category
\textup{EMHM}(M)
can also be defined as the essential image of a projector
P_{*}\colon\mathrm{MHM}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\theta}\times M,{\mathds{R}})\to\mathrm{MHM}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\theta}\times M,{\mathds{R}})
, which is defined in
[
Moc25
, (155)]
,
see also the discussion right after it.

Now we explain how to embed
\mathrm{EMHM}(M)
as a subcategory of
\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)
and to attach an irregular Hodge filtration to its objects. Mochizuki constructed a functor

u\colon\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\tau}\times M)\to\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)

(3.1.2)

in
[
Moc25
, §11.4.4]
by sending
\mathscr{M}
to an object
u(\mathscr{M})
such that
\iota_{!}u(\mathscr{M})=\psi^{(1)}_{\tau-1}\mathscr{M}
.

Definition 3.2

(
[
Moc25
, Def. 11.38]
)
.

The essential image of
u
is a full subcategory of
\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)
, called
rescalable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-modules
and denoted by
\mathrm{MTM}_{\mathrm{resc}}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)
.

Let
\mathbf{FT}_{M}\colon\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\theta}\times M)\to\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\tau}\times M)
be the partial Fourier transform
[
Moc25
, §10.2]
of integrable twistor modules relative to
M
where
\tau
denotes the coordinate of the affine line dual to
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\theta}
. Mochizuki proved the following theorem:

Theorem 3.3

(
[
Moc25
, Thm. 11.45]
)
.

The functor

B\colon\mathrm{MHM}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\theta}\times M,{\mathds{R}})\xrightarrow{u\circ\mathbf{FT}_{M}\circ v}\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M),

(3.1.3)

induces an equivalence of categories of
B_{*}\colon\mathrm{EMHM}(M)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{MTM}_{\mathrm{resc}}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)
.

Moreover, Mochizuki showed that the functor
B
is compatible with the six-functor formalism, nearby and vanishing cycle functors, and duality in
[
Moc25
, Props. 11.46-11.48]
. In particular, these operators preserve rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules. Let us also notice that the category
\textup{MHM}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\theta}\times M,{\mathds{R}})
exists even if
M
is a possibly singular algebraic variety. Furthermore, the push-forward and the pull-back for maps between possibly singular varieties preserve the subcategory of exponential mixed Hodge modules. Therefore, we have the six-functor formalism in the category of exponential mixed Hodge modules (and consequently in the category of rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules) including push-forward and pull-back for maps between singular spaces.

As explained above, the
de Rham
functor
\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}:\textup{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{resc}}(M)\subset\textup{MTM}(M)\rightarrow\textup{Mod}_{h}(\mathscr{D}_{Y})
sends a rescalable integrable twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module
\mathcal{M}
to the holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-module
\mathcal{M}/(z-1)\mathcal{M}
. For any
\mathcal{T}\in\textup{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{resc}}(M)
one can then equip
\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{T})
with an irregular Hodge filtration
F_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\bullet}
, see
[
Sab18a
, Thm. 0.3]
and
[
Moc25
, Cor. 1.6]
. In particular, we denote by
h^{p}_{\mathrm{irr}}
the irregular Hodge numbers, i.e. the (generic) ranks of the graded pieces of
F_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\bullet}\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{T})
.

To end this section, we collect a unicity result, which will be needed later.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
M
be a smooth quasi-projective variety, and let
N
be a semi-simple holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module. Then there is up to isomorphism at most one object
\mathcal{T}\in\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{resc}}(M)
of weight
0
such that
\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{T})\cong N
.

Proof.

We choose a compactification
j_{M}:M\hookrightarrow\overline{M}
, where
\overline{M}
is smooth and projective. Then the intermediate extension
j_{M,{\dagger}+}N
is a semi-simple holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{\overline{M}}
-module. Now, suppose that we have
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\in\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{resc}}(M)
such that
\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{T}_{1})\cong\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{T}_{2})
. According to
[
Moc15
, Thm. 14.3.16 & Prop. 14.3.17]
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
, we have the objects
j_{M,*}\mathcal{T}_{i}
and
j_{M,!}\mathcal{T}_{i}
in
\textup{MTM}(\overline{M})
and by loc.cit. Proposition 14.3.18 there is a morphism
j_{M,!}\mathcal{T}_{i}\rightarrow j_{M,*}\mathcal{T}_{i}
. Denote by
j_{M,!*}\mathcal{T}_{i}
the respective images in
\textup{MTM}(\overline{M})
. Since taking the de Rham functor
\Xi_{\textup{dR}}
commutes with taking direct images (with and without proper support) of integrable mixed twistor modules resp. of holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules, we see that
\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}(j_{M,!*}\mathcal{T}_{i})=j_{M,{\dagger}+}N
for both
i=1,2
. Now by
[
Moc11
, Thm. 1.4.4]
, there is a unique (wild and pure) analytic twistor module
\overline{\mathcal{T}}
on
\overline{M}
such that
\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{\mathcal{T}})=(j_{M,{\dagger}+}N)^{an}
. Hence, we obtain an isomorphism

\phi:(j_{M,!*}\mathcal{T}_{1})^{an}\longrightarrow(j_{M,!*}\mathcal{T}_{2})^{an},

but since
\overline{M}
is projective, the categories
\textup{MTM}(M)^{an}
and
\textup{MTM}(\overline{M})
are equivalent (this follows, e.g., from
[
Moc15
, Lem. 14.1.2]
). Therefore we obtain an isomorphism

\phi^{\mathrm{alg}}:j_{M,!*}\mathcal{T}_{1}\longrightarrow j_{M,!*}\mathcal{T}_{2},

which restricts to an isomorphism

\phi^{\mathrm{alg}}_{|M}:\mathcal{T}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{2},

of (algebraic) mixed twistor modules on
M
. If we now consider
\mathcal{T}_{i}

as integrable twistor modules on
M
, then by
[
Sab18a
, Rem. 1.39]
,
\phi^{\mathrm{alg}}_{|M}
is also an isomorphism in
\textup{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)
.
Since
\textup{MTM}_{\mathrm{resc}}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)
is a a full subcategory of
\textup{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}(M)
,
\phi_{|M}
is an isomorphism of (algebraic) rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules, as required.
∎

can be obtained by imitating the construction of Hecke-eigensheaves of the Hecke operator
\mathrm{Hk}_{V}
in
[
HNY13
, §2.3]
.

More precisely, we can construct the Hecke operator for rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules, by interpreting
[
HNY13
, page 259, last displayed formula]
within the category rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules. For a representation
V
of
\check{G}
, we denote the corresponding functor from
\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{resc}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G(1,2)})
to
\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{resc}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G(1,2)}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\backslash\{0,\infty\})
by
\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{H}}_{V}
.

Moreover, the rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module
A_{\phi,\chi}^{\mathrm{H}}
can be constructed by replacing the Artin–Schreier sheaf
\mathscr{L}_{\psi(x)}
with
\mathcal{T}^{x/z}
in the definition of
A_{\phi,\chi}
in
[
HNY13
, §2.2]
. By the compatibility of the six-functor formalism and the de Rham functor, we deduce for any representation
V
of
\check{G}
that

\Xi_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{H}}_{V}(A_{\phi,\chi}^{\mathrm{H}}))=\mathrm{Hk}_{V}(A_{\phi,\chi})=A_{\phi,\chi}\boxtimes\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)

in
D^{b}(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Bun}_{G(1,2)}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\backslash\{0,\infty\}})
, where
G
is the dual group of
\check{G}
, and
\mathrm{Bun}_{G(1,2)}
is the moduli stack of
G
-bundles with some level structures.
By
Lemma

3.4
, semi-simple holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules can underlie at most one rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module of weight
0
. Hence, we deduce that

\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{H}}_{V}(A_{\phi,\chi}^{\mathrm{H}})=A^{\mathrm{H}}_{\phi,\chi}\boxtimes\nabla^{\mathrm{H}}_{\check{G}}(V).

As in
[
HNY13
, §2.3]
, we can compose Hecke operators using the geometric Satake correspondence for perverse sheaves (resp. regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules). More precisely, for any two representations of
\check{G}
, the composition
\mathrm{Hk}_{V}\circ\mathrm{Hk}_{W}
equals the functor
\mathrm{Hk}_{V\otimes W}
because the convolution of
\mathrm{IC}_{V}
with
\mathrm{IC}_{W}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{IC}_{V\otimes W}
(in particular, the convolution of
\mathrm{IC}_{V}
with
\mathrm{IC}_{W}
is still semi-simple). Using again
Lemma

3.4
for these intersection complexes, we deduce that the convolution of
\mathrm{IC}^{\mathrm{H}}_{V}
with
\mathrm{IC}^{\mathrm{H}}_{W}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{IC}^{\mathrm{H}}_{V\otimes W}
, which implies that
\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{H}}_{V}\circ\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{H}}_{W}=\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{H}}_{V\otimes W}
. Hence,
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}
yields a tensor functor

\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}\colon\mathrm{Rep}_{\check{G}}\to\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{resc}}(\mathbb{G}_{m,t}).

(3.2.5)

As explained in
Section

3.2
, each object in the category of rescalable mixed twistor modules is equipped with an irregular Hodge filtration in a functorial way. When restricted to smooth objects (that is, mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules whose image under the de Rham functor are smooth
\mathscr{D}
-module, i.e. connections), this gives a tensor functor

\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{sm,resc}}(\mathbb{G}_{m,t})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{IrrFil}}\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathbb{G}_{m,t}}

to the category of vector bundles on
\mathbb{G}_{m,t}
filtered by subbundles. Since the essential image of
\nabla^{\mathrm{H}}_{\check{G}}
are connections, they are smooth objects in
\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{resc}}(\mathbb{G}_{m,t})
. So, we can consider the composition

\mathrm{Rep}_{\check{G}}\xrightarrow{\nabla^{\mathrm{H}}}\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{sm,resc}}(\mathbb{G}_{m,t})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{IrrFil}}\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathbb{G}_{m,t}}.

Similar to
[
Lov17
, §3.2.4]
, we call a functor
F\colon\mathrm{Rep}_{\check{G}}\to\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
an
\eta/k
-filtration
for some dominant cocharacter
\eta
and some integer
k
, if for each representation
V
of
\check{G}
, the decreasing filtration by subbundles
F(V)
is given by

F^{\alpha}\nabla_{\check{G}}(V):=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\otimes_{{\mathds{C}}}\biggl(\bigoplus\nolimits_{i\geq k\cdot\alpha}V_{i}\biggr),

where
V=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathds{Z}}}V_{i}
is the decomposition of
V
induced by the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action
\eta_{V}:\mathbb{G}_{m}\xrightarrow{\eta}\check{G}\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
.

Definition 3.6
.

We say that the irregular Hodge filtrations of
\nabla^{\mathrm{H}}_{\check{G}}
are determined by
\eta/k
for some dominant cocharacter
\eta\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\check{G}
and a positive integer
k
if the composition
\mathrm{IrrFil}\circ\nabla^{\mathrm{H}}
is an
\eta/k
-filtration. We say that the irregular Hodge numbers of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}
are determined by
\eta/k
if for any
V\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\check{G}}
the ranks of
\mathrm{gr}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{IrrFil}\circ\nabla^{\mathrm{H}}(V))
equal the
dimensions of the eigenspaces
V_{k\cdot\alpha}
of the operator
\eta(V)
.

If the irregular Hodge filtration of
\nabla^{\mathrm{H}}_{\check{G}}
is determined by
\eta/k
, then so are its irregular Hodge numbers. The converse is not true in general.
Using these notions, we can summarize the above discussion as follows.

Proposition 3.7
.

The Frenkel–Gross connection
\nabla_{\check{G}}
underlies a unique rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}
of weight
0
, i.e. a tensor functor

\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}\colon\mathrm{Rep}_{\check{G}}\to\mathrm{MTM}_{\mathrm{resc}}^{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbb{G}_{m,t}).

In particular, there is a uniquely defined irregular Hodge filtration on
\nabla_{\check{G}}
, and it is determined by
\eta/k
for some cocharacter
\eta
and some integer
k
.

Proof.

We already constructed rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}
on
\nabla_{\check{G}}
in (
3.2.5
). To show the uniqueness of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}
, we decompose any representation
W
of
\check{G}
as
W=\bigoplus W_{i}\otimes M_{i}
with
W_{i}
irreducible and
M_{i}
the multiplicities. By the functoriality of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}
, we have

\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(W)=\bigoplus\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(W_{i})\otimes M_{i}^{\mathrm{H}},

with
M_{i}^{\mathrm{H}}
being trivial. As in
Lemma

3.4
, there is at most one rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules of weight
0
on an irreducible connection on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. So each
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(W_{i})
is unique. Therefore,
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(W)
is also unique.

Lastly, since each
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
is a connection on
\mathbb{G}_{m,t}
, its associated rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module gives rise to an irregular Hodge filtration (by subbundles) in a functorial way. In other words, we have a tensor functor from
\mathrm{Rep}_{\check{G}}
to (
\frac{1}{k}{\mathds{Z}}
-indexed) filtered vector bundles on
\mathbb{G}_{m,t}
for some integer
k
. By
[
Lov17
, Lem. 3.3.1]
, this functor is an
\eta/k
-filtration for some cocharacter
\eta
.
∎

3.3
Inverse Fourier transforms of
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)

In this section, we study the Hodge properties of the (inverse) Fourier transform of the Frenkel-Gross connections and their variants. We first work in a slightly more general geometric framework to simplify our proof. The situation will be specified in the case of Frenkel–Gross connections at the end of this section.

The setup.

Let
Y
be a quasi-projective variety over
{\mathds{C}}
and
g
a regular function on
Y
. We consider the product
Y\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}
and we denote by
\mathrm{pr}_{t}
resp.
\mathrm{pr}_{Y}
the projection
Y\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}
resp. the projection
Y\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}\rightarrow Y
. We have the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t\cdot g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{pr}_{t}}

We start by collecting a few general facts about Fourier transforms of (complexes of)
\mathscr{D}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{t}}
-modules that we will use later.
Recall from the introduction that the Fourier transform of an object
\mathcal{M}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}_{h}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t})
denoted by
\mathrm{FT}^{\pm 1}(\mathcal{M}^{\bullet})
, is the complex of holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}}
-modules defined by

\mathrm{pr}_{s+}(\mathrm{pr}_{t}^{+}\mathcal{M}^{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\pm t\cdot s}),

where
\mathrm{pr}_{t}
and
\mathrm{pr}_{s}
are the projections from
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
to
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}
and
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
respectively. Similarly, we can define the Fourier transform of
\mathcal{M}^{\bullet}
with compact support by

\mathrm{FT}^{\pm}_{\dagger}(\mathcal{M}^{\bullet}):=\mathrm{pr}_{s\dagger}(\mathrm{pr}_{t}^{+}\mathcal{M}^{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\pm t\cdot s}).

The two kinds of Fourier transforms are interchanged by the duality functor

\mathbb{D}\circ\mathrm{FT}^{-1}=\mathrm{FT}_{\dagger}\circ\mathbb{D},

(3.3.1)

and the forget-support morphism induces an isomorphism
\mathrm{FT}_{\dagger}(\mathcal{M})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{FT}(\mathcal{M})
, see
[
Mal91
, App. 2 Prop. 1.7]
, so that we also have

\mathbb{D}\circ\mathrm{FT}^{-1}=\mathrm{FT}\circ\mathbb{D}.

(3.3.2)

Moreover
\mathrm{FT}^{-1}\circ\mathrm{FT}=\mathrm{id}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any
N\in\textup{Mod}_{h}(\mathscr{D}_{Y})
we have isomorphisms in
D^{b}(\mathscr{D}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{s}})
:

\mathrm{FT}^{-1}(\mathrm{pr}_{t+}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{t\cdot g}))\cong g_{+}N

and

\mathrm{FT}^{-1}(\mathrm{pr}_{t\dagger}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{t\cdot g}))\cong g_{\dagger}N,

here we see
g
as a morphism
g\colon X\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{s}
.

Proof.

It suffices to calculate
\mathrm{FT}(g_{+}N)
and
\mathrm{FT}(g_{\dagger}N)
. Consider the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pr_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(g\times\mathrm{id})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pr_{t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pr_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pr_{t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{FT}\left(g_{+}N\right)

\displaystyle=\pr_{t+}\left(\pr_{s}^{+}g_{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{s\cdot t}\right)

\displaystyle=\pr_{t+}\left(\left(g\times\mathrm{id}_{t}\right)_{+}\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{s\cdot t}\right)

smooth base change

\displaystyle=\pr_{t+}\circ\left(g\times\mathrm{id}_{t}\right)_{+}\left(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\left(g\times\mathrm{id}_{t}\right)^{+}\mathcal{E}^{s\cdot t}\right)

projection formula

\displaystyle=\pr_{t+}\circ\left(g\times\operatorname{id}_{t}\right)_{+}\left(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{g\cdot t}\right)

\displaystyle\text{ definition of }\mathcal{E}^{s\cdot t}

\displaystyle=\pr_{t+}\left(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{g\cdot t}\right).

In the same way, we obtain

\mathrm{FT}^{-1}\left(g_{+}N\right)=\pr_{t+}\left(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{-g\cdot t}\right).

(3.3.3)

Using the usual commutation rules of the duality functor with direct and inverse images, as well as the commutation of duality with Fourier transformation discussed above, we derive from the previous computation the following:

\displaystyle\mathrm{FT}(g_{\dagger}{\mathbb{D}}_{Y}(N))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{FT}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{s}}(g_{+}N))

\displaystyle={\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{t}}(\mathrm{FT}^{-1}(g_{+}N))

use formula (
3.3.2
)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{t}}\pr_{t+}\left(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{-g\cdot t}\right)

use formula (
3.3.3
)

\displaystyle=\pr_{t{\dagger}}{\mathbb{D}}_{Y\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{t}}\left(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{-g\cdot t}\right)

\displaystyle=\pr_{t{\dagger}}\left(\pr_{Y}^{+}{\mathbb{D}}_{Y}N\otimes{\mathbb{D}}_{Y\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{t}}\mathcal{E}^{-g\cdot t}\right)

\displaystyle=\pr_{t{\dagger}}\left(\pr_{Y}^{+}{\mathbb{D}}_{Y}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{g\cdot t}\right)

\mathcal{E}^{-g\cdot t}
is locally free.

Since this holds for any
N
, we obtain
\mathrm{FT}(g_{\dagger}N)=\pr_{t{\dagger}}\left(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{g\cdot t}\right)
, as required.
∎

Let
j\colon\mathbb{G}_{m,t}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}
be the inclusion and let
N
be a
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module. Consider

\mathcal{F}:=j^{+}\pr_{t+}(\mathcal{E}^{t\cdot g}\otimes\pr_{Y}^{+}N),

which is a priori an object in
D^{b}(\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{G}_{m,t}})
.
Recall that for
a\in{\mathds{Q}}
and
\chi=\exp(2\pi ia)
, we write
\mathbb{L}_{\chi}:=(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_{m,t}},\mathrm{d}+a\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t})
and we put
\mathcal{F}_{\chi}:=\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi}
.
We will consider the following geometric situation:

Assumption 3.9
.

We take
a\in{\mathds{Q}}\setminus{\mathds{Z}}
, i.e.
\chi\neq 1
, and subject the above objects to the following four conditions.

1.

\mathcal{F}
is a
\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{G}_{m,t}}
-module concentrated in degree
0
, regular singular at
0
, and irregular singular at
\infty
with slopes
1
. Notice that the same assumption then automatically holds for
\mathcal{F}_{\chi}
.

2.

The forget support morphism

j^{+}\mathrm{pr}_{t\dagger}(\mathcal{E}^{\pm t\cdot g}\otimes\pr_{Y}^{+}N)\to j^{+}\mathrm{pr}_{t+}(\mathcal{E}^{\pm t\cdot g}\otimes\pr_{Y}^{+}N)=\mathcal{F}

(3.3.4)

induces an isomorphism of
\mathscr{D}_{{\mathbb{G}_{m,t}}}
-modules concentrated in degree
0
.

3.

The morphism

j_{\dagger}(\mathcal{F}_{\chi})\to j_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{\chi})

(3.3.5)

is an isomorphism, so in particular both
j_{\dagger}(\mathcal{F}_{\chi})
and
j_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{\chi})
are isomorphic to
j_{\dagger+}(\mathcal{F}_{\chi})
.

4.

N
underlies a pure Hodge module
N^{\mathrm{H}}\in\mathrm{MHM}(Y)
of weight
\dim Y
.

We continue our discussion of various Fourier transforms. We restrict, however, from now on our attention to objects satisfying the above four conditions.

For two
\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
-modules
M
and
N
, the additive convolutions are
\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
-modules defined as

M\star_{+}N:=\mathrm{sum}_{+}(M\boxtimes N)\quad\text{and}\quad M\star_{\dagger}N:=\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{D}(M)\star_{+}\mathbb{D}(N)),

where
\mathrm{sum}\colon\mathbb{A}^{1}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
is the summation map.

Proposition 3.10
.

Under
Assumption

3.9
we have

\mathrm{FT}^{-1}(j_{+}\mathcal{F}_{\chi})=g_{+}N\star_{+}j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi^{-1}}

(3.3.6)

and

\mathrm{FT}^{-1}(j_{\dagger}\mathcal{F}_{\chi})=g_{\dagger}N\star_{\dagger}j_{\dagger}\,\mathbb{L}_{\chi^{-1}}.

(3.3.7)

Here
\star_{+}
and
\star_{\dagger}
are the additive convolutions.

Proof.

Since the Fourier transform interchanges the tensor product and the additive convolution, by
[
DS13
, Equation (1.1.2)]
and the first equation in
Proposition

3.8
, we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{FT}^{-1}&(\mathrm{pr}_{t+}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{t\cdot g})\otimes j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi})\\
&=\mathrm{FT}^{-1}(\mathrm{pr}_{t+}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{t\cdot g}))\star_{+}\mathrm{FT}^{-1}(j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi})\\
&=g_{+}N\star_{+}j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi^{-1}}.\end{split}

Notice that the adjunction morphism
\mathrm{id}\to j_{+}j^{+}
induces a morphism

\mathrm{pr}_{t+}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{t\cdot g})\to j_{+}\mathcal{F}

whose kernel and cokernel are supported at
0
. Hence, we deduce an isomorphism

\mathrm{pr}_{t+}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{t\cdot g})\otimes j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi}\simeq j_{+}\mathcal{F}_{\chi}.

Combining the above identities, we deduce (
3.3.6
).

Similarly, we have

\mathrm{FT}(\mathrm{pr}_{t+}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{-t\cdot g})\otimes j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi^{-1}})=\mathrm{FT}(\mathrm{pr}_{t+}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{-t\cdot g}))\star_{+}\mathrm{FT}(j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi}^{-1})=g_{+}N\star_{+}j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi}.

Notice that

\mathrm{pr}_{t+}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{-t\cdot g})|_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\simeq\mathrm{pr}_{t\dagger}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{-t\cdot g})|_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}=\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}^{\vee},

we argue similarly above that

\mathrm{pr}_{t+}(\pr_{Y}^{+}N\otimes\mathcal{E}^{-t\cdot g})\otimes j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi^{-1}}=j_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{\chi})^{\vee}.

So, we deduce that

\mathrm{FT}(j_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{\chi})^{\vee})=g_{+}N\star_{+}j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi}

Applying the duality functor, we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{FT}^{-1}_{\dagger}(j_{\dagger}\mathcal{F}_{\chi})&=\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\circ\mathrm{FT}(j_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{\chi})^{\vee})=\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(g_{+}N\star_{+}j_{+}\mathbb{L}_{\chi})\\
&=\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(g_{\dagger}N)\star_{+}\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(j_{\dagger}\mathbb{L}_{\chi^{-1}}))=g_{\dagger}N\star_{\dagger}j_{\dagger}\mathbb{L}_{\chi^{-1}},\end{split}

which is (
3.3.7
).
∎

Notice that

where

is the pure Hodge module on

of weight

with underlying
\mathscr{D}
-module

, and

is the pure complex Hodge module on

.

Under

3.9
, the

-module

It follows from (

), and (

is an isomorphism.

By the properties of conservation of weights of mixed Hodge modules (see

), the two mixed Hodge modules

and

) can be lifted to an isomorphism of mixed Hodge modules, we conclude that they are pure Hodge modules of weight

.

Corollary 3.12
.

The inverse Fourier transform
\mathrm{FT}^{-1}\circ j_{+}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})
is a regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
-module. If
\chi
is non-trivial, the inverse Fourier transform can be lifted to a pure Hodge module
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\chi}
on
{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
of weight
w:=\dim(\mathrm{Gr}_{\lambda})+1=\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle+1
.

Proof.

First note that
t\cdot g
can be extended to
\mathrm{Gr}^{\circ}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}
. Recall that
\mathrm{IC}_{V,\mathrm{GR}^{\circ}}=\mathrm{pr}_{Y}^{+}\mathrm{IC}_{V,\mathrm{Gr}^{\circ}}
via the projection
\mathrm{pr}_{Y}\colon\mathrm{GR}^{\circ}=\mathrm{Gr}^{\circ}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{Gr}^{\circ}
.
It then follows from
Proposition

2.5
that

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)={\pi}^{\circ}_{+}(\mathcal{E}^{t\cdot g}\otimes\mathrm{pr}_{Y}^{+}\mathrm{IC}_{V,\mathrm{Gr}^{\circ}}).

It can be verified that the conditions of
Assumption

3.9
, as outlined at the beginning of
Section

3.3
, are satisfied for this choice of
(Y,g,N,\mathcal{F}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})
. Indeed, given our choices, the first and last conditions are straightforward. The second condition is verified by (
2.3.2
), and the third condition holds because
\nabla_{G}
is regular singular and has principal unipotent monodromy at
0
.

It then follows from
Proposition

3.10
and
Lemma

3.11
that
\mathrm{FT}^{-1}\circ j_{+}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})
underlies a pure Hodge module on
{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
of weight
\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle+1
.
∎

3.4
The irregular Hodge numbers of
\nabla_{\check{G}}

Now we prove
Theorem

1.1
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two isogenous almost simple groups. If the irregular Hodge filtrations of
\nabla_{\check{G}_{1}}^{\mathrm{H}}
are determined by
\rho_{G_{1}}
, then the irregular Hodge filtrations of
\nabla_{\check{G}_{2}}^{\mathrm{H}}
are determined by
\rho_{G_{2}}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that we have an isogeny
\pi\colon\check{G}_{1}\to\check{G}_{2}
or
\pi\colon\check{G}_{2}\to\check{G}_{1}
. In the former case, we deduce the irregular Hodge filtrations of
\nabla_{\check{G}_{2}}^{\mathrm{H}}
by the “trivial” functoriality, as discussed in
Example

2.3
.

In the latter case, assume that the irregular Hodge filtration of
\nabla_{\check{G}_{2}}^{\mathrm{H}}
is determined by
\eta/k
. By the “trivial” functoriality again,
\eta/k
will induce
2\rho_{G_{1}}
on
\check{G}_{1}
, i.e.
\pi\circ\eta^{2}=(2\rho_{G_{1}})^{k}
.
Hence, the cocharacter
\pi\circ(\eta^{2}\cdot(2\rho_{G_{2}})^{-k})
is trivial, which forces
\eta^{2}\cdot(2\rho_{G_{2}})^{-k}
to take values in
\ker\pi
. Since there is no nontrivial homomorphism from
\mathbb{G}_{m}
to finite groups, we conclude that
\eta^{2}=(2\rho_{G_{2}})^{k}
, which means that
\eta/k=\rho_{G_{2}}
.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

If the irregular Hodge numbers of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)
are given by
\rho(V)
for one faithful representation
V
of
{\check{G}}
, then the irregular Hodge numbers of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}
are given by
\rho
.

Proof.

As algebraic vector bundles on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
are trivial, we can (non-canonically) identify any basis of
\nabla_{\check{G}}(L)
with a basis of
L
for any representation
L
. In particular, the irregular Hodge filtration on
\nabla_{\check{G}}(L)
determines a flag (indexed by
\frac{1}{k}{\mathds{Z}}
)

\mathscr{F}_{\alpha/k}\subset\ldots\subset\nabla_{\check{G}}(L)

for
\alpha\in{\mathds{Z}}
, such that
h^{\alpha}=\rk\mathscr{F}_{\alpha/k}/\mathscr{F}_{(\alpha-1)/k}
.

By
Proposition

3.7
, the irregular Hodge filtration is determined by
\eta/k
for some cocharacter
\eta
of
\check{G}
and an integer
k
. It follows that
\eta(V)
and
\rho^{k}(V)
determines the same flag of
V
. As
V
is faithful,
\eta
and
\rho^{k}
correspond to the same parabolic subgroup
\check{P}
of
\check{G}
up to conjugacy. It follows that for any representation
W
of
\check{G}
,
\eta(W)
and
\rho^{k}(W)
determine again the same flag on
W
. Hence, the irregular Hodge numbers of
\nabla_{\check{G}}(W)
are determined by
\rho(W)
.
∎

Now, we need to calculate the irregular Hodge numbers of some specific representations
V
. Similar to
[
Qin24
, §5.2.1]
, we consider the case where the local monodromy of
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
at
0
consists of Jordan blocks of different sizes. This assumption ensures that the primitive parts of the Lefschetz decomposition of some limiting Hodge structures are of rank
1
, hence of Hodge–Tate type. If the primitive parts have dimensions bigger than
1
, there are more possibilities. In this case the computation of the irregular Hodge numbers may be more involved. As we will see in
Lemma

3.18
below, this case will not occur in our investigation.

Proposition 3.16
.

Assume that
V
is irreducible and the local monodromy of
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
at
0
consists of Jordan blocks of sizes
r_{1}<r_{2}<\dots<r_{k}
. Then the nearby cycle

\psi_{1/\tau}\widetilde{M}_{\chi}^{\mathrm{H}}
is of Hodge–Tate type, and the Hodge numbers coincide with the irregular Hodge numbers of
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
up to a global shift.

More precisely, we have for
p\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathds{Z}}
that

h^{p}_{\mathrm{irr}}=\#\{(i,a)\mid 2p=r_{i}-2a,~0\leq a\leq r_{i}\}.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)=[h]^{+}\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
,
\mathbb{L}_{\chi}
and
g
be as in the previous section. In order to prove the proposition, it suffices to calculate the irregular Hodge numbers of
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi}
for any
\chi\neq 1
.

Recall that
j\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
denotes the canonical embedding. Notice that
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi}
is regular at
0
, with quasiunipotent monodromy of eigenvalue
\chi
. So we have

j_{\dagger}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})=j_{\dagger+}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})=j_{+}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi}).

Recall also that
\widetilde{M}_{\chi}:=\mathrm{FT}^{-1}j_{\dagger+}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})
is the inverse Fourier transform of
j_{\dagger+}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})
, which can be lifted to a pure Hodge module
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\chi}
by
Corollary

3.12
.

Let
\psi_{t}\,(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})
be the nearby cycle module at
0
of
j_{+}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})
. Notice that the local monodromy of
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)
at
0
is also unipotent with Jordan blocks of sizes
r_{1}<\ldots<r_{k}
. So that of
\psi_{t}\,(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})
is quasiunipotent with eigenvalue
\chi
and with Jordan blocks of sizes
r_{1}<\ldots<r_{k}
.

By construction, we have
\widetilde{M}_{\chi}=\mathrm{FT}^{-1}j_{\dagger+}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})
. Applying the (inverse) stationary phase formula (cf.
[
Mal91
, Prop. VII.2.4]
or
[
Sab06
, Prop. 2.3]
), it follows that

\psi_{t}\,(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})=\phi_{1/\tau}\widetilde{M}_{\chi}=\psi_{1/\tau,-1}\widetilde{M}_{\chi}

(3.4.1)

and the corresponding nilpotent part of the monodromy operator
N
has Jordan blocks of sizes
r_{1}<\dots<r_{k}
respectively. In particular,
\rk\,\widetilde{M}_{\chi}=r
.

By our assumption, the (non-zero) primitive parts of the Lefschetz decomposition of
\psi_{1/\tau}\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\chi}
, denoted by
P_{r_{1}},\ldots,P_{r_{k}}
, are one-dimensional and of Hodge–Tate type. Since
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\chi}
is pure of weight
w=\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle+1
, the primitive parts
P_{r_{1}},\dots,P_{r_{k}}
are pure of weights
r_{1}+w-1,\ldots,r_{k}+w-1
respectively. In other words,

P_{r_{i}}=\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{r_{i}+w-1}P_{r_{i}}

for
1\leq i\leq k
. Then, by the Lefschetz decomposition, for each
\ell\in{\mathds{Z}}
, the graded quotient
\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{\ell}\psi_{1/\tau}\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\chi}
is Hodge–Tate
of dimension

\#\{(i,a)\mid\ell=r_{i}+w-1-2a,0\leq a\leq r_{i}\}.

Moreover, we have

\begin{split}\dim\mathrm{gr}_{F}^{p}\psi_{1/\tau}\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\chi}=\dim\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{2p}\psi_{1/\tau}\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\chi}.\end{split}

Since the eigenvalues of
\widetilde{M}_{\chi}^{\mathrm{H}}
at
\infty
are different from
1
, we conclude using
[
SY19
, (7)]
that

\dim\mathrm{gr}_{F_{irr}}^{p+a}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})=\dim\mathrm{gr}_{F}^{p}\psi_{1/\tau,-1}\mathbf{FT}^{-1}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})

which by
Corollary

3.13
coincide with

\dim\mathrm{gr}^{p}_{F}\psi_{1/\tau,-1}\widetilde{M}_{\chi}^{\mathrm{H}}=\#\{(i,a)\mid 2p=r_{i}+w-1-2a,0\leq a\leq r_{i}\}

up to a global shift, where
a
is a rational number such that
\chi=\exp(2\pi ia)
. Notice that the irregular Hodge numbers of
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi}
agree with those of
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)
(as well as
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
) up to a global shift. Therefore, we have shown the formula for the irregular Hodge numbers of
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
as announced in the statement.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

Assume that
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
is irreducible and its local monodromy at
0
consists of Jordan blocks of different sizes. Then the Hodge numbers of
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
are determined by
\rho(V)
.

Proof.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{d\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathds{Z}}}V_{d}
be the degree decomposition of
V
with respect to
2\rho\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\check{G}
, such that
2\rho(t)
acts on
V_{d}
by multiplication by
t^{2d}
. There is a decreasing filtration induced by
\rho
, i.e. the filtration defined by

F_{\rho}^{p}:=\bigoplus_{p\leq 2d}V_{d}

for
p\in{\mathds{Z}}
. Equivalently,
2\rho
can be seen as an element in
\check{\mathfrak{g}}
, which induces an endomorphism
2\rho(V)
on
V
such that
v\in V_{d}
if and only if
2\rho(V)\cdot v=2dv
.

Notice that one can upgrade the principal nilpotent operator
N
to a
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(x,y,h)
such that
y=N
. Recall that
\rho
can equivalently be written as
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}
, the sum of
fundamental coweights
of
\check{G}
satisfying
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\omega_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
for
\{\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{\vee}\}=\Delta
an ordering of the simple roots of
\check{G}
. Hence, we have

[N,\rho]=\textstyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\bigl[X_{-\alpha_{i}^{\vee}},\omega_{j}]=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle-\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\omega_{j}\rangle\,X_{-\alpha_{i}^{\vee}}=-N.

(3.4.2)

So, we deduce that

h=2\rho.

(3.4.3)

In particular, we have

\rho(V)N(V)v=N(V)\rho(V)v+N(V)v=(d+1)N(V)v

for
v\in V_{d}
. Hence, one has for
d\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathds{Z}}
that

N(V)F_{\rho}^{2d}V\subset F_{\rho}^{2d+2}V.

On the other hand, viewing
V
as the representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
via the composition
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\to\check{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, we have

N(V)^{2d}\colon\mathrm{gr}_{F_{\rho}}^{-2d}V=V_{-d}\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{d}=\mathrm{gr}_{F_{\rho}}^{2d}V

when
d\leq 0
by the representation theory of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

[
Hum72
, Thm. 7.2]
. So
F_{\rho}^{2\bullet}
is the same as the monodromy-weight filtration on
V
with respect to
N(V)
.

Notice that
\psi_{t}\,(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\check{G}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{L}_{\chi})
with its monodromy-weight filtration is abstractly isomorphic to
V
with its monodromy-weight filtration with respect to
N(V)
,
and so is
\psi_{1/\tau,-1}\widetilde{M}_{\chi}^{\mathrm{H}}
by the identification from (
3.4.1
). By
Proposition

3.16
,
\psi_{1/\tau,-1}\widetilde{M}_{\chi}^{\mathrm{H}}
is of Hodge–Tate type and have Hodge numbers equal to the irregular Hodge numbers of
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V)
up to a global shift, which are therefore determined by
\rho(V)
.
∎

To achieve the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, it suffices to show the existence of representations
V
such that the Jordan blocks of
N(V)
have different sizes. In fact, adjoint representations are what we are looking for, as suggested by the following lemma:

Lemma 3.18
.

For a simple group
\check{G}
not of type
D_{2n}
, the nilpotent operator
N
acting on the adjoint representation
V=\mathfrak{g}
has Jordan blocks of different sizes.

Proof.

Let
V
be the adjoint representation
V=\check{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathrm{Lie}(\check{G})
of
\check{G}
and
l
be the rank of
\check{\mathfrak{g}}
. Notice that
N\in\check{\mathfrak{g}}
is principally nilpotent, which can be enhanced into a principal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. By
[
Kon59
, §6.5 & Cor. 8.7]
, we can decompose
V=\check{\mathfrak{g}}
into
\bigoplus_{i}V_{k_{i}}
, where
\dim V_{k_{i}}
are
(2k_{i}+1)
-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-representations, and the numbers
n_{1}\leq\ldots\leq n_{l}
are the
exponents
of
\check{\mathfrak{g}}
.

By
[
Bou02
, Plate I-VII]
, the exponents of
\check{\mathfrak{g}}
are distinct numbers. So the dimensions of irreducible representations (as well as the Jordan blocks of

) are distinct numbers.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

The rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module on
\nabla_{\check{G}}
exists by

3.7
. To calculate its irregular Hodge numbers, it suffices to prove the theorem for one type in each row of diagram (

,

E_{7}

E_{8}
, and

For each type as above, by

By

3.15

4
On a conjecture of Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pantev

As explained in the introduction, we are going to apply our main result
Theorem

1.1
to the study of a conjecture by Katzarkov-Kontsevich-Pantev about irregular Hodge numbers of certain Landau-Ginzburg models for smooth projective Fano varieties.

By a
Landau–Ginzburg model
, we mean a pair
(Y,w)
consisting of a quasi-projective variety
Y
and a regular function
w\colon Y\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
. In
[
KKP17
, Conj. 3.7]
, the authors defined
Landau–Ginzburg Hodge numbers
of
(Y,w)
as

f^{p,q}(Y,w):=\dim_{\mathds{C}}\mathrm{H}^{p}(\mathsf{Z},\Omega^{q}_{\mathsf{Z}}(\log D,w)),

where
Z
is a smooth proper compactification of
Y
such that
D:=Z\backslash Y
is a simple normal crossing divisor, where
w
extends to a morphism
\tilde{w}\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and where
\Omega_{Z}^{q}(\log D,w)
is the sheaf

\ker(\Omega_{Z}^{q}(\log D)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{d}w\wedge}\Omega_{Z}^{q+1}(\log D)).

It is known
[
ESY17
, Theorem 1.3.2]
that we have

f^{p,q}(Y,w)=\dim\gr^{p}_{F_{\textup{Yu}}}\mathrm{H}^{p+q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\Omega_{Y}^{\bullet},d+dw),

where
F_{\textup{Yu}}^{\bullet}
is the Yu filtration on the twisted de Rham cohomologies as defined in
[
Yu14
]
. In particular, as the Yu filtration is independent of the choice of a compactification
Z
, so are the numbers
f^{p,q}(Y,w)
.

The authors of
[
KKP17
]
predicted that when a Landau–Ginzburg model
(Y,w)
is the mirror of a Fano variety
X
, the Hodge numbers of
X
are related to the Landau–Ginzburg Hodge numbers of
(Y,w)
by the formula

f^{p,q}(Y,w)=h^{p,n-q}(X),

(4.0.1)

where
n=\dim X
.

This conjecture has been verified in a number of cases, including the case of convenient and non-degenerate Laurent polynomials (
[
Sab18b
, Thm. 3.6]
, where the variant
[
KKP17
, Conj. 3.6]
is shown).

We prove this conjecture when
X=\check{G}/\check{P}
is a minuscule homogeneous space. The mirror Landau–Ginzburg model is
(Y=G\overset{\circ}{/}P,w)
, where
G
resp.
P
are Langlands dual to
\check{G}
resp.
\check{P}
(subject to the choice of a root datum),
G\overset{\circ}{/}P\subset G/P
is the open projected Richardson variety, and
w
is induced by some decoration function, see
[
LT24
, §1.4]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
X=\check{G}/\check{P}
a minuscule homogeneous space with
\dim(X)=n
, consider the Landau–Ginzburg model
(Y,w)
from above.
Then (
4.0.1
) holds, namely:

f^{p,q}(Y,w)=h^{p,n-q}(X)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $q\neq n-p$,}\\
h_{irr}^{p-\frac{n}{2}},&\text{if $q=n-p$.}\end{cases}

In particular, both numbers are determined by
\rho=2\rho/2
up to a shift.

Before giving the proof, we explain how the Hodge numbers
f^{p,q}(Y,w)
are related to our irregular Hodge numbers
h_{\mathrm{irr}}
of Frenkel–Gross connections.

Lemma 4.2
.

The numbers
f^{p,q}(Y,w)
are zero if
q\neq n-p
and are equal to
h_{\mathrm{irr}}^{p-n/2}
when
q=n-p
, which is determined by
\rho
up to a twist.

Proof.

Following
[
LT24
, §1.3]
, let
X_{(G,P)}
be the
(parabolic) geometric crystal
,
f\colon X\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
the
decoration function
, and
\pi\colon X_{(G,P)}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
the
highest weight function
. The fiber
X_{t}=\pi^{-1}(t)
for
t\in\mathbb{G}_{m}({\mathds{C}})
is called the
geometric crystal with highest weight
t
, and is identified with the open projected Richardson variety
G\overset{\circ}{/}P\subset G/P
. Moreover,
(G\overset{\circ}{/}P,f|_{X_{1}})
is the mirror Landau–Ginzburg model of
\check{G}/\check{P}
as mentioned above.

The
character
\mathscr{D}
-module
of the geometric crystal
X_{(G,P)}
is defined as

\mathrm{Cr}_{G,P}:=\pi_{+}\mathcal{E}^{f}.

(4.0.2)

According to
[
LT24
, Thm. 1.8]
and
Theorem

2.4
, the character
\mathscr{D}
-module
\mathrm{Cr}_{G,P}
is a
\mathscr{D}
-module concentrated in degree
0
and is smooth on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
such that

\mathrm{Cr}_{G,P}\simeq\nabla_{\check{G}}(V_{\lambda_{P}}),

(4.0.3)

where
\lambda_{P}
is the minuscule weight of the dual group
\check{G}
of
G
determined by the parabolic subgroup
P
, and
V_{\lambda_{P}}
is the representation of the highest weight
{\lambda_{P}}
. In particular, the fiber of
\nabla_{\check{G}}(V_{\lambda_{P}})
at
1
is the twisted de Rham cohomology
\mathbb{H}^{n}(\Omega_{Y}^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}w)
associated with the Landau–Ginzburg model
(Y,w)
.

By
Theorem

3.3
, we can view the rescalable integrable mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)
in (
3.2.1
) as an exponential mixed Hodge module. By (
4.0.3
), its de Rham fiber is identified with
\mathrm{Cr}_{G,P}
. The fiber (or the pull-back) of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)
at the smooth point
1
is an exponential mixed Hodge module (or structure)
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)_{1}
of weight
0
, whose de Rham fiber is
(\mathrm{Cr}_{G,P})_{1}\simeq\mathbb{H}^{n}(\Omega_{Y}^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}w)(n/2)
, see also
[
FSY22
, Def. A.18]
.

By
[
Moc25
, Prop. 11.22]
, the irregular Hodge filtration
F_{\mathrm{irr}}
of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)
induces that of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)_{1}
. Moreover, the irregular Hodge filtration on the de Rham fiber of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)_{1}
coincides with the Yu filtration on the twisted de Rham cohomology
[
ESY17
, Prop. 1.7.4]
with a shift by
n/2
. Therefore, we have

h_{\mathrm{irr}}^{p-\frac{n}{2}}=\dim\mathrm{gr}^{p}_{F_{\mathrm{Yu}}}\mathbb{H}^{n}(\Omega_{Y}^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}w)=f^{p,n-p}.

Lastly, since
\mathbb{H}^{p+q}(\Omega_{Y}^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}w)=0
when
p+q\neq n
, we have
f^{p,q}=0
when
q\neq n-p
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

It is well known (see e.g.
[
Che94
]
or
[
CG10
, Chap. 3]
) that the cohomology of a homogeneous space
\check{G}/\check{P}
is of Hodge–Tate type, i.e.
h^{p,n-q}(X)=0
if
q\neq n-p
and
h^{p,p}(X)=b_{2p}(X)
. Hence, we need to identify the Betti numbers of
X
with our

h^{p-\frac{n}{2}}_{irr}
. Since the latter are given by
\rho
,
it is sufficient to show that
\mathrm{H}^{2p}(X)
can be identified with the eigenspace of
2\rho
acting on
V
, which under our assumptions is a minuscule representation of
\check{G}
. This is exactly what is stated in
[
LT24
, Prop. 4.12]
(especially the last sentence of its proof). Lastly, by
Lemma

4.2
, we also have the identity
h^{p,n-q}(X)=f^{p,q}(Y,w)
.
∎

In this subsection, we give examples of representations

such that

have Jordan blocks of different sizes. Following our

, this yields concrete results for the irregular Hodge numbers of Frenkel–Gross connections in these cases.

We take

6
The
\mathcal{R}
-module associated to the Frenkel-Gross connection

We have established in the previous sections using some geometric arguments that the Frenkel–Gross connection
\nabla_{\check{G}}
can be upgraded to a tensor functor

\nabla^{\mathrm{H}}_{\check{G}}\colon\mathrm{Rep}({\check{G}})\to\mathrm{MTM}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{resc}}(\mathbb{G}_{m,t})

(see the discussion around (
3.2.5
)).
Hence, for any
V\in\mathrm{Rep}(\check{G})
, the object
\nabla^{\mathrm{H}}_{\check{G}}(V)
can in particular be considered as an integrable mixed twistor module on
\mathbb{G}_{m,t}
. Therefore, letting
\mathcal{R}:=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathbb{G}_{m,t}}
be the sheaf of rings with global sections equal to
{\mathds{C}}[z,t^{\pm}]\langle z^{2}\partial_{z},z\partial_{t}\rangle
, we have a
\mathcal{R}
-triple
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\prime},C)
, where
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
are coherent
\mathcal{R}
-modules. Our conjecture concerns an explicit expression for
\mathcal{M}
.
It seems difficult to establish this conjecture directly, however, if one could do so, the main result of this paper would follow almost immediately.

For an almost simple group
{\check{G}}
, we associate a tensor functor

\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathcal{R}}\colon\mathrm{Rep}({\check{G}})\to\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{R})

as follows. For each finite-dimensional complex representation
V
of
{\check{G}}
, let
\mathcal{E}(V):=\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{z}\times\mathbb{G}_{m,t}}\otimes_{\mathds{C}}V
be the trivial bundle on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{z}\times\mathds{G}_{m,t}
, the associated connection on
\mathcal{E}(V)
is given by

\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathcal{R}}(V)=\mathrm{d}+\bigl(N(V)+tE(V)\bigr)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{tz}-\bigl(N(V)+tE(V)\bigr)h(\check{G})\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z^{2}}+\rho(V)\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z},

(6.0.1)

where
h({\check{G}})
is the Coxeter number of
{\check{G}}
,
N(V)
and
E(V)
are the endomorphisms of
V
induced by the action of
N,E\in\check{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\rho(V)
is the semisimple (i.e. diagonalizable) matrix induced by the action of
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha^{\vee}\in(\Phi^{\vee})^{+}}\alpha^{\vee}
, half sum of positive coroots of
\check{G}
.

Notice that we have

\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathcal{R}}(V):\mathcal{E}(V)\to\mathcal{E}(V)\otimes\frac{1}{z}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{z}\times\mathbb{G}_{m,t}}\left(\log(\{0\}\times\mathbb{G}_{m,t})\right).

Proposition 6.1
.

The pair
(\mathcal{E}(V),\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathcal{R}}(V))
is integrable, i.e. we have
(\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathcal{R}}(V))^{2}=0
. In particular, the localization
(\mathcal{E}(V)(*(\{0\}\times\mathbb{G}_{m,t})),\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathcal{R}}(V))
is a flat meromorphic connection, and yields a local system on
\mathds{G}_{m,z}\times\mathds{G}_{m,t}
.

Proof.

First, recall that a connection
\nabla
of the form
\nabla=A\mathrm{d}t+B\mathrm{d}z
(for
A,B\in\textup{Mat}(\dim(V)\times\dim(V),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_{m,z}\times\mathbb{G}_{m,t}})
) is integrable if and only if
A
and
B
satisfy
\partial_{z}A-\partial_{t}B=[A,B]
. The connection in (
6.0.1
) is of this form with
A=\frac{1}{tz}(N+tE)
and
B=-\frac{h}{z^{2}}(N+tE)+\frac{1}{z}\rho
. The left side of the condition is easy enough to evaluate:
\partial_{z}A-\partial_{t}B=\frac{1}{tz^{2}}\bigl(-N+t(h-1)E\bigr)
.

For the right side, we work inside the Lie algebra:
[A,B]=\frac{1}{tz^{2}}\bigl[N+tE,\rho\bigr]
. Recall that we have shown in (
3.4.2
) that

[N,\rho]=-N.

On the other hand, it is well-known that
\langle\theta,\rho\rangle=h-1
, so we find that
[E,\rho]=(h-1)E
.

Hence, we conclude that
[A,B]=\frac{1}{tz^{2}}\bigl(-N+t(h-1)E\bigr)=\partial_{z}A-\partial_{t}B
, i.e. that
\nabla
is integrable.
∎

We finish this paper by the following conjecture about an explicit expression of the irregular Hodge module structure defined on the Frenkel-Gross connection.

Conjecture 6.2
.

For any
V\in\mathrm{Rep}({\check{G}})
, if
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\prime},C)
is the
\mathcal{R}
-triple of the rescalable integrable mixed twistor module
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)
, then we have an isomorphism
\mathcal{M}\cong\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathcal{R}}(V)
of integrable
\mathcal{R}
-modules.
Consequently, the irregular Hodge filtration of
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)
is determined by
\rho
in the sense of
Definition

3.6
.

One can verify this conjecture in the examples from
Section

5.1
coming from classical groups. As mentioned above, when assuming the conjecture, our main result
Theorem

1.1
would be a direct consequence using a similar strategy to
[
CnDS21
, Prop. 4.6]
or to
[
CnDRS19
, Thm. 5.9]
(compare the expression (
6.0.1
) to the expression of the connection
\nabla
in
[
CnDS21
, Lem. 4.3]
), where an adapted basis for the irregular Hodge filtration was constructed. It is, however, unclear how to show the above conjecture when the irregular Hodge structure
\nabla_{\check{G}}^{\mathrm{H}}(V)
is defined using the geometric construction from
Section

2.3
.

Remark 6.3
.

Recently, Yi demonstrated in
[
Yi25
]
that the
\check{G}
-Airy connections coincide with those constructed via the geometric Langlands program by Jakob-Kamgarpour-Yi in
[
JKY23
]
. This suggests that a similar conjectural framework as in
Conjecture

6.2
may be applicable to
\check{G}
-Airy connections. In particular, one can attempt to construct, as above,
\mathcal{R}
-modules associated with these connections and conjecture that they form mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules. With explicit descriptions of such
\mathcal{R}
-modules, one may then extract the corresponding irregular Hodge filtrations. It is, however, currently even unclear how to construct an
\mathcal{R}
-module from the
\check{G}
-Airy connections that satisfies the integrability property shown for the Frenkel–Gross case in
Proposition

6.1
.

Moreover, the methods from the previous sections used to compute the irregular Hodge numbers for Frenkel–Gross connections do not extend to the case of
\check{G}
-Airy connections, which are regular at the origin. Consequently, new techniques need to be developed to investigate the irregular Hodge theory in this setting.

which defines an additional set of three state–dependent functions
{\cal X}^{i}
. Our aim is to derive the resulting asymptotic symmetry algebra under affine boundary conditions through a canonical analysis. For this purpose, we systematically investigate all gauge transformations of the form (
14
) that preserve the boundary data encoded in equations (
20
) and (
49
).

At this stage, it is convenient to parameterize the gauge transformation in terms of the
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
Lie algebra basis:

\lambda=b^{-1}\left[\epsilon^{i}{\tt L}_{i}\right]b.

(22)

The gauge parameter
\lambda
thus contains three bosonic functions
\epsilon^{i}
, which are arbitrary functions defined on the boundary. We now focus on those gauge parameters that satisfy the transformation law (
14
). The corresponding infinitesimal variations of the dynamical fields are given by:

\displaystyle 2\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}^{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k}{4}\partial_{t}\epsilon^{0}-\mathcal{L}^{+1}\epsilon^{-1}+\mathcal{L}^{-1}\epsilon^{+1},

(23)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}^{\pm 1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{2}\partial_{t}\epsilon^{\mp 1}+\mathcal{L}^{0}\epsilon^{\mp 1}\mp\mathcal{L}^{\mp 1}\epsilon^{0},

(24)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{X}^{0}

\displaystyle=2\big(\mathcal{X}^{+1}\epsilon^{-1}-\mathcal{X}^{-1}\epsilon^{+1}\big),

(25)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{X}^{\pm 1}

\displaystyle=\mp\mathcal{X}^{0}\epsilon^{\pm 1}\pm\mathcal{X}^{\pm 1}\epsilon^{0}.

(26)

As a final step in this construction, we define the canonical boundary charge
\mathcal{Q}_{a}[\lambda]
, which generates the gauge transformations of the connection field described in Eqs. (
23
)–(
24
). In parallel, we introduce the canonical boundary charge
\mathcal{Q}_{x}[\lambda]
, associated with the transformations of the dilaton field as specified in Eqs. (
25
)–(
26
).

The infinitesimal variation of these charges, which encodes the structure of the asymptotic symmetry algebra, is given by
Banados:1994tn
:

\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}_{a}=\frac{k}{2\pi}\int\mathrm{d}t\;\mathfrak{tr}\left(\lambda\delta a_{t}\right),\quad\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}_{x}=\frac{k}{2\pi}\int\mathrm{d}t\;\mathfrak{tr}\left(\lambda\delta x\right).

(27)

By performing a functional integration of the variations, we obtain the explicit form of the canonical boundary charges:

\mathcal{Q}_{a}[\lambda]=\int\mathrm{d}t\;\left(\mathcal{L}^{i}\epsilon^{-i}\right),\quad\mathcal{Q}_{x}[\lambda]=\int\mathrm{d}t\;\left(\mathcal{X}^{j}\epsilon^{-j}\right).

(28)

Having established both the infinitesimal gauge transformations and the associated canonical boundary charges, we now derive the asymptotic symmetry algebra using the standard procedure outlined in
Blagojevic:2002du
. The algebraic structure is encoded in the following relation:

\delta_{\lambda}\digamma=\{\digamma,\mathcal{Q}_{a,x}[\lambda]\}

(29)

for any generic phase space functional
\digamma
. The asymptotic symmetry algebra is thus generated by the boundary charges
\mathcal{L}^{i}
and
\mathcal{X}^{i}
. Ultimately, the corresponding operator product algebra takes the form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{i}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{L}^{j}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{2}\eta^{ij}}{\tau_{12}^{2}}+\frac{(i-j)}{\tau_{12}}\mathcal{L}^{i+j}

(30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{i}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{X}^{j}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{(2i+j)}{\tau_{12}}\mathcal{X}^{i+j},

(31)

where
\tau_{12}=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}
. The coefficients
\eta^{ij}=\mathfrak{tr}({\tt L}_{i}{\tt L}_{j})
define the components of the invariant bilinear form in the fundamental representation of the
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
Lie algebra. An alternative and more compact representation of the operator product algebra can be written using index-free notation as follows:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{J}}}^{A}(\tau_{1})\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{J}}}^{B}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{2}\eta^{AB}}{\tau_{12}^{2}}+\frac{\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{f}}}^{AB}_{\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ C}\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{J}}}^{C}}{\tau_{12}}.

(33)

Here,
\eta^{AB}
denotes the trace matrix, and
\mathfrak{f}^{AB}_{\penalty\ \penalty\ C}
are the structure constants of the underlying algebra, with indices running over
(A,B=0,\pm 1)
. In particular, one has
\mathfrak{f}^{ij}_{\penalty\ \penalty\ i+j}=(i-j)
. Accordingly, under the most relaxed set of boundary conditions for
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
dilaton gravity, the resulting asymptotic symmetry algebra is identified with a single copy of the affine
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{k}
algebra.

The time–dependent profile of the dilaton field at the affine boundary introduces structural modifications that go beyond the classical asymptotic symmetry algebra of
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
JT dilaton gravity. In the standard BF formulation, the asymptotic symmetry algebra is determined solely by gauge transformations of the
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
–valued connection
a_{t}
. However, once the
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
–valued dilaton field
x
is treated as a dynamical degree of freedom, the set of residual symmetries becomes further constrained, giving rise to an enriched physical structure. In this extended framework, the symmetry algebra must incorporate not only the reparametrization modes but also new dynamical currents induced by the dilaton.

As a consequence, the full symmetry algebra necessitates the inclusion of abelian currents alongside the classical
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{k}
affine modes. This reveals that the affine boundary dynamics of JT gravity cannot be adequately described by the Schwarzian action alone, but instead call for a more general, multi-mode effective action. In this refined picture, the dilaton field plays a dual role: it determines both the geometric background and the extended algebraic structure realized at the boundary.

3.4
Conformal boundary conditions

In this section, our goal is to analyze the asymptotic symmetry algebra associated with Brown–Henneaux-type boundary conditions. This gauge choice is specifically designed to make the asymptotic symmetry structure manifest and to streamline the analysis of boundary dynamics. By decoupling the radial dependence, it effectively isolates the relevant asymptotic degrees of freedom, thereby facilitating the computation of conserved quantities and the identification of corresponding boundary theories.

This gauge plays a central role in maintaining the full asymptotic symmetry group and in determining the effective boundary action, particularly within the context of holographic duality. To proceed, we impose the Drinfeld–Sokolov highest weight gauge condition on the
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
-valued connection defined in Eq. (
20
), which further restricts the allowed functional form of the coefficients. Under this reduction, the fields are constrained as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{0}=0,\quad\mathcal{L}^{-1}=\mathcal{L},\quad\alpha_{+1}\mathcal{L}^{+1}=1.

(34)

where
\alpha_{-1}=\alpha
. It is important to note that these conformal boundary conditions correspond to the well–established Brown–Henneaux boundary conditions originally formulated in
Brown:1986nw
for
AdS_{2}
gravity. Inspired by analogous developments in the context of three–dimensional gravity
Perez:2016vqo
;
Cardenas:2021vwo
, we propose that the gauge connection and the dilaton field adopt the following specific structure:

\displaystyle a_{t}

\displaystyle={\tt L}_{+1}+\alpha\mathcal{L}{\tt L}_{-1},

(35)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle={\cal X}{\tt L}_{1}-{\cal X}^{\prime}{\tt L}_{0}+\bigg(\alpha\mathcal{L}{\cal X}+\frac{{\cal X}^{\prime\prime}}{2}\bigg){\tt L}_{-1}.

(36)

Here,
\alpha
acts as a scaling parameter whose exact value will be fixed at a later stage. We define two key functionals: the boundary charge
\mathcal{L}
and the dilaton field
{\cal X}
. With these constraints in place, we are now prepared to derive the conformal asymptotic symmetry algebra. As dictated by the Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction, the residual gauge parameter
\lambda
depends on four independent functions, with
\epsilon\equiv\epsilon^{+1}
serving as the primary functional degree of freedom. The gauge parameter then takes the following form:

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{-1}\left[\epsilon{\tt L}_{1}-\epsilon^{\prime}{\tt L}_{0}+\bigg(\alpha\mathcal{L}\epsilon+\frac{\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}{2}\bigg){\tt L}_{-1}\right]b.

(37)

The dilaton field
x
is defined in the same form as the gauge parameter
\lambda
in order to maintain consistency with the equations of motion and compatibility with the asymptotic symmetry group. This structural alignment allows the dilaton to behave as a coadjoint element in the gauge theory and to act as a stabilizing agent for the residual symmetries. Moreover, this choice ensures the well-posedness of the variational principle and enables the consistent derivation of the conserved Casimir function
C
. Thus, defining
x
in the same form as
\lambda
is crucial for preserving the asymptotic symmetry group, ensuring the consistency of the theory, and facilitating the formulation of boundary dynamics in a holographic context.

By substituting the gauge parameter
\lambda
into the field transformation laws (
14
), the resulting infinitesimal gauge transformations are found to be:

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\alpha}\epsilon^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}^{\prime}+2\mathcal{L}\epsilon^{\prime},

(38)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}{\cal X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon{\cal X}^{\prime}-\epsilon^{\prime}{\cal X}.

(39)

Under the imposed boundary conditions, the corresponding canonical charges
\mathcal{Q}_{a}[\lambda]
and
\mathcal{Q}_{x}[\lambda]
remain integrable, thereby allowing
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{X}
to be interpreted as conserved quantities. These gauge transformations are instrumental in uncovering the structure of the asymptotic symmetry algebra
Banados:1998pi
.

By analyzing the asymptotic symmetries, one can integrate the variations of the canonical boundary charges,
\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}_{a}
as given in Eq. (
38
) and
\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}_{x}
in Eq. (
39
), to arrive at the following expressions:

\mathcal{Q}_{a}[\lambda]=\int\mathrm{d}\varphi\;\left(\mathcal{L}\epsilon\right),\quad\mathcal{Q}_{x}[\lambda]=\int\mathrm{d}\varphi\;\left(\mathcal{X}\epsilon\right).

(40)

These canonical boundary charges naturally induce an operator product algebra at the asymptotic boundary. In contrast to the standard two–dimensional CFT framework, a one-dimensional CFT description can be obtained by effectively suppressing one of the coordinates. This is achieved by setting
z_{2}=0
and identifying
z_{1}
with the boundary coordinate
\tau
, or more simply by taking
z=\tau
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{L}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{{3k}}{{2\tau_{12}^{4}}}+\frac{2\mathcal{L}}{{\tau_{12}^{2}}}+\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}{\tau_{12}},

(41)

\displaystyle{\cal L}(\tau_{1}){\cal X}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle-\frac{{\cal X}}{{\tau_{12}^{2}}}+\frac{{\cal X}^{\prime}}{\tau_{12}}.

(42)

From these relations, it follows that
{\cal X}
transforms as a vector under boundary diffeomorphisms, while
\mathcal{L}
behaves as a spin-two density. Throughout this analysis, the explicit dependence on the gauge parameter
\lambda
has been omitted, as the boundary conditions no longer require it. In effect, the residual symmetries associated with the connection
a_{t}
and the dilaton
x
are interpreted as diffeomorphisms on the boundary circle, generated by the vector field
\epsilon
. It is thus observed that the asymptotic symmetry algebra of
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
dilaton gravity with conformal boundary conditions is given by a single copy of the Virasoro algebra with central charge
c=3k
.

The conformal boundary profile of the dilaton field induces a structural extension analogous to the affine case, leading to an enriched version of the classical asymptotic symmetry algebra in
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
JT gravity. In the conventional BF formulation, the asymptotic symmetry algebra is generated solely by gauge transformations of the
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
-valued connection
a_{t}
. However, once the dilaton field
x
is promoted to a dynamical quantity, the residual symmetry subalgebra becomes more constrained, resulting in a more intricate physical structure. This generalized framework requires that the full symmetry algebra incorporate both the standard reparametrization modes and additional currents sourced directly by the dynamical behavior of the dilaton.

As a consequence, the boundary symmetry structure must necessarily include abelian extensions beyond the classical Virasoro algebra. This observation suggests that the Schwarzian action alone is insufficient to capture the complete boundary dynamics in the presence of a nontrivial dilaton configuration. Instead, a multi–component extended action is required to account for all dynamical features. In this context, the dilaton field assumes a dual role, encoding not only the background geometry but also the full algebraic structure realized at the boundary.

4
The
\mathcal{N}=1

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
higher–spin dilaton supergravity

Building upon the canonical analysis of the
\mathcal{N}=1

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
case, we now formulate the two–dimensional higher-spin dilaton supergravity theory with negative cosmological constant in the BF framework. This theory can be equivalently described by the following action on a spacetime manifold
{\cal M}
:

S[{\cal X},{\cal A}]=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{{\cal M}}\mathfrak{str}[{\cal X}{\cal F}]+S_{\rm bdy},

(43)

where
\mathcal{A}
is a one–form superconnection with corresponding field strength
{\cal F}=\mathrm{d}{\cal A}+{\cal A}\wedge{\cal A}
, and
k
denotes a coupling constant. The boundary term
S_{\rm bdy}
is included to ensure a well-defined variational principle by enforcing suitable boundary conditions at
\partial\mathcal{M}
, which corresponds to the asymptotic boundary at infinite radius.

The dilaton field
{\cal X}
is an algebra-valued scalar taking values in the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
, just like the superconnection
{\cal A}
. The supertrace operation
\mathfrak{str}
provides the invariant bilinear form used to contract the generators of the superalgebra.

To describe the structure of the dilaton supergravity system, we now introduce the underlying
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
superalgebra. It consists of bosonic generators
L_{n}
with
n=0,\pm 1
and fermionic generators
G_{p}
with
p=\pm\tfrac{1}{2}
. These generators satisfy the following graded (anti-)commutation relations:

\begin{split}\left[L_{m},L_{n}\right]&\,=\,(m-n)L_{m+n}\ ,\quad\left[L_{m},G_{p}\right]=\left(\frac{1}{2}m-p\right)G_{m+p}\ ,\\
\left\{G_{p},G_{q}\right\}&\,=\,-2L_{p+q}\ .\end{split}

(44)

An explicit matrix representation of the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
generators can be written as:

\begin{split}L_{1}\,=\,&\left[\begin{array}[]{c c|c}0&0&0\\
1&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right]\ ,\quad L_{0}\,=\,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|c}\frac{1}{2}&0&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{2}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right]\ ,\quad L_{-1}\,=\,\left[\begin{array}[]{c c|c}0&-1&0\\
0&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right]\ ,\\
G_{\frac{1}{2}}\,=\,&\left[\begin{array}[]{c c|c}0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
\hline\cr-1&0&0\\
\end{array}\right]\ ,\quad G_{-\frac{1}{2}}\,=\,\left[\begin{array}[]{c c|c}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1&0\\
\end{array}\right]\ .\end{split}

(45)

In this basis, the invariant bilinear form is defined via the supertrace, and is given by:

\mathfrak{str}\big(L_{n},L_{m}\big)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-1\\
0&\frac{1}{2}&0\\
-1&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathfrak{str}\left\{G_{p}G_{q}\right\}=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\
-2&0\end{pmatrix}.

(46)

where the supertrace
\mathfrak{str}
of a matrix
{\tt M}
is defined as

\mathfrak{str}{\tt M}\equiv{\tt M}_{11}+{\tt M}_{22}-{\tt M}_{33}.

(47)

4.1
\mathcal{N}=1
affine boundary conditions

The goal of this section is to construct an extended higher–spin
AdS_{2}
framework by formulating it as an
\mathcal{N}=1

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
dilaton supergravity theory defined with affine boundary conditions. Our analysis aims to determine the resulting asymptotic symmetry algebra under the most general boundary conditions consistent with this setup.

Following the discussion in the previous section, we adopt the principal embedding of
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
into
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
as a subalgebra. This embedding provides a natural foundation for implementing affine boundary conditions in asymptotically
AdS_{2}
spacetimes. To proceed with this construction, we introduce the gauge connection in the following form:

\displaystyle a_{t}=\alpha_{i}\mathcal{L}^{i}{\tt L}_{i}+\beta_{i}\mathcal{G}^{i}{\tt G}_{i}

(48)

where the scaling relations among the parameters are given by
\alpha_{0}=-2\alpha_{\pm 1}=\mp 4\beta_{\pm\frac{1}{2}}
. As a consequence, the gauge connection contains five independent dynamical functions, denoted by
\mathcal{L}^{i}
and
\mathcal{G}^{i}
, which are interpreted as the corresponding boundary charges.

The dilaton field
x
is expanded in the same
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
basis and takes the form:

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{i}{\tt L}_{i}+\mathcal{Y}^{i}{\tt G}_{i}

(49)

In this framework, we obtain eight additional state-dependent functions,
{\cal X}^{i}
and
{\cal Y}^{i}
, arising from the dilaton field expansion. Our goal is to derive the asymptotic symmetry algebra associated with these affine boundary conditions via a canonical analysis. To this end, we systematically investigate all gauge transformations that preserve the structure of the boundary conditions defined above.

At this stage, it is convenient to express the gauge parameter in terms of the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
Lie superalgebra basis:

\lambda=b^{-1}\left[\epsilon^{i}{\tt L}_{i}+\eta^{i}{\tt G}_{i}\right]b.

(50)

Here, the gauge parameter consists of five bosonic functions,
\epsilon^{i}
and
\eta^{i}
, which are arbitrary functions defined on the boundary. We now proceed to analyze those gauge parameters that satisfy the transformation law (
14
), and determine the corresponding infinitesimal gauge transformations for both the connection and the dilaton fields.

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}^{\mp 1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k}{2}\partial_{t}\epsilon^{\mp 1}\pm\eta^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mp 2\mathcal{L}^{0}\epsilon^{\mp 1}\mp\mathcal{L}^{\mp 1}\epsilon^{0},

(51)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{4}\partial_{t}\epsilon^{0}-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathcal{L}^{1}\epsilon^{-1}+\mathcal{L}^{-1}\epsilon^{1},

(52)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{G}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\pm k\partial_{t}\eta^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}+2\eta^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{L}^{\mp 1}+2\eta^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{L}^{0}-\mathcal{G}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}\epsilon^{\mp 1}\mp\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\epsilon^{0},

(53)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{X}^{\mp 1}

\displaystyle=-2\eta^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{Y}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mp\mathcal{X}^{\mp 1}\epsilon^{0}\pm\mathcal{X}^{0}\epsilon^{\mp 1},

(54)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{X}^{0}

\displaystyle=-2\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{Y}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-2\eta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{Y}^{\frac{1}{2}}+2\mathcal{X}^{1}\epsilon^{-1}-2\mathcal{X}^{-1}\epsilon^{1},

(55)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{Y}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{X}^{0}\mp\eta^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{X}^{\mp 1}\pm\mathcal{Y}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}\epsilon^{\mp 1}\mp\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{Y}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\epsilon^{0}.

(56)

As a final step in this construction, we define the canonical boundary charge
\mathcal{Q}_{a,x}[\lambda]
, which generates the gauge transformations presented in Eqs. (
51
)–(
56
). The variation of this charge, which encodes the structure of the asymptotic symmetry algebra, is given by
Banados:1994tn
:

\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}_{a}=\frac{k}{2\pi}\int\mathrm{d}t\;\mathfrak{tr}\left(\lambda\delta a_{t}\right),\quad\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}_{x}=\frac{k}{2\pi}\int\mathrm{d}t\;\mathfrak{tr}\left(\lambda\delta x\right).

(57)

Integrating this variation functionally yields the explicit expression for the canonical boundary charges:

\mathcal{Q}_{a}[\lambda]=\int\mathrm{d}t\;\left(\mathcal{L}^{i}\epsilon^{-i}+\mathcal{G}^{i}\eta^{-i}\right),\quad\mathcal{Q}_{x}[\lambda]=\int\mathrm{d}t\;\left(\mathcal{X}^{i}\epsilon^{-i}+\mathcal{Y}^{i}\eta^{-i}\right).

(58)

Having established both the infinitesimal gauge transformations and the canonical boundary charge, we are now in a position to derive the asymptotic symmetry algebra using the standard procedure outlined in
Blagojevic:2002du
. This is accomplished through the fundamental relation:

\delta_{\lambda}\digamma=\{\digamma,\mathcal{Q}_{a,x}[\lambda]\}

(59)

for an arbitrary phase space functional
\digamma
. In this setting, the charges
\mathcal{L}^{i}
and
\mathcal{G}^{i}
, along with their corresponding dilaton partners
\mathcal{X}^{i}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{i}
, serve as the generators of the asymptotic symmetry algebra. Ultimately, the operator product algebra takes the following form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{i}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{L}^{j}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{2}\eta^{ij}}{\tau_{12}^{2}}+\frac{(i-j)}{\tau_{12}}\mathcal{L}^{i+j},

(60)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{i}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{G}^{j}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{(\frac{i}{2}-j)}{\tau_{12}}\mathcal{G}^{i+j},

(61)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{i}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{G}^{j}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{2}\theta^{ij}}{\tau_{12}^{2}}+\frac{2}{\tau_{12}}\mathcal{L}^{i+j},

(62)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{i}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{X}^{j}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{(2i+j)}{\tau_{12}}\mathcal{X}^{i+j},

(63)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{i}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{Y}^{j}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{(\frac{i}{2}+j)}{\tau_{12}}\mathcal{Y}^{i+j},

(64)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{i}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{X}^{j}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\tau_{12}}\mathcal{Y}^{i+j},

(65)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{i}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{Y}^{j}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{(\frac{3i}{2}+\frac{j}{2})}{\tau_{12}}\mathcal{Y}^{i+j}.

(66)

where
\tau_{12}=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}
. In this formulation,
\eta^{ij}=\mathfrak{tr}({\tt L}_{i}{\tt L}_{j})
and
\theta^{ij}=\mathfrak{tr}({\tt G}_{i}{\tt G}_{j})
denote the invariant bilinear forms in the fundamental representation of the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
Lie superalgebra. The operator product algebra may be recast in a more compact, index-free form as follows:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{J}}}^{A}(\tau_{1})\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{J}}}^{B}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{2}\eta^{AB}}{\tau_{12}^{2}}+\frac{\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{f}}}^{AB}_{\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ C}\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{J}}}^{C}}{\tau_{12}}.

(67)

Here,
\eta^{AB}
denotes the invariant trace matrix, while
\mathfrak{f}^{AB}_{\penalty\ \penalty\ C}
represent the structure constants of the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
algebra, with indices
(A,B=0,\pm 1,\pm\tfrac{1}{2})
. These constants satisfy the relation
\mathfrak{f}^{ij}_{\penalty\ \penalty\ i+j}=(i-j)
. Under the most general (least restrictive) boundary conditions, the asymptotic symmetry algebra of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
dilaton supergravity is characterized by a single copy of the affine
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)_{k}
algebra.

In the context of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
JT dilaton gravity with affine boundary conditions, much like in the
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
JT dilaton gravity under analogous assumptions, the asymptotic symmetry algebra is initially given by the infinite–dimensional
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)_{k}
algebra. However, the presence of a time-dependent dilaton field dynamically deforms this structure. As a result, even the expected preservation of the finite
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
subalgebra within
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)_{k}
is partially broken. The dilaton acts as a stabilizing agent for the gauge superconnection and, through its boundary fluctuations, obstructs the full realization of the infinite–dimensional affine symmetry.

This symmetry breaking is attributed to the influence of the dilaton on free boundary excitations. Consequently, the residual symmetries encoded in the fields
a_{t}
and
x
correspond to boundary diffeomorphisms, generated respectively by the bosonic and fermionic components
\epsilon^{i}
and
\eta^{i}
of the gauge parameter.

4.2
\mathcal{N}=1

In this section, we aim to analyze the asymptotic symmetry algebra associated with the Brown–Henneaux boundary conditions in the context of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
-valued connection defined in Eq. (

), which further restricts the coefficient structure. This gauge fixing yields the following field constraints:

\mathcal{L}^{0}=\mathcal{G}^{+\frac{1}{2}}=0,\quad\mathcal{L}^{-1}=\mathcal{L},\quad\mathcal{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{G},\quad\alpha_{+}\mathcal{L}^{+1}=1,

(68)

and introduces the new scaling parameters
\alpha_{-1}=\alpha
and
\beta_{-\frac{1}{2}}=\beta
.

It is worth noting that these conformal boundary conditions are in direct analogy with the well-known Brown–Henneaux boundary conditions initially developed in
Brown:1986nw
for
AdS_{3}
gravity. Our approach draws on boundary conditions previously explored in the three–dimensional setting
Perez:2016vqo
;
Cardenas:2021vwo
. In this spirit, we propose that the gauge connection and the dilaton field take the following form:

\displaystyle a_{t}=

\displaystyle{\tt L}_{+1}+\alpha\mathcal{L}{\tt L}_{-1}+\beta\mathcal{G}{\tt G}_{-\frac{1}{2}},

(69)

\displaystyle x=

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}{\tt L}_{1}+\mathcal{Y}{\tt G}_{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathcal{X}^{\prime}{\tt L}_{0}+\left(\alpha\mathcal{G}\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\right){\tt G}_{-\frac{1}{2}}

(70)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\alpha\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{G}+\beta\mathcal{L}\mathcal{X}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}\right){\tt L}_{-1}

(71)

After implementing the above procedures, we are now positioned to derive the conformal asymptotic symmetry algebra. As a consequence of the Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction, the residual gauge parameter
\lambda
is effectively described by only four independent functions. These are denoted by
\epsilon\equiv\epsilon^{+1}
and
\eta\equiv\eta^{+\frac{1}{2}}

5
Conclusions and Discussion

Two–dimensional gravity models, most notably JT gravity, where the dilaton plays an active, dynamical role, have become central in the study of low–dimensional holography and quantum gravity. In its
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
formulation, JT gravity defines the asymptotic conditions of
\mathrm{AdS}_{2}
spacetime and allows spacetime diffeomorphisms to be enhanced into an infinite–dimensional Virasoro symmetry. However, the presence of a time–dependent dilaton field induces a nontrivial deformation in this structure, dynamically breaking even the expected preservation of the
{\tt S}{\tt L}(2,\mathbb{R})
subgroup within the full Virasoro algebra. This breakdown is directly linked to dilaton fluctuations at the boundary, which govern the emergent dynamics of physical quantities near the asymptotic region. Thus, the dilaton evolves from a mere geometric label into a core dynamical variable that reshapes the boundary metric structure itself.

This foundational mechanism is further developed in the context of an
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
supergravity extension explored in this work, where symmetry breaking and symmetry extension are jointly realized within a unified gauge–theoretic formulation.

As a natural supersymmetric extension of classical
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
JT gravity, the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
JT supergravity framework introduces a structurally richer theory that incorporates both higher–order gauge connections and a more intricate configuration of boundary symmetries. This is not merely a formal enlargement of the algebra via fermionic generators; rather, it entails a nontrivial extension of the dynamical phase space and the gauge sector itself.

Notably, the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
gauge fields exhibit a novel mechanism of symmetry breaking, one that does not ensure the full preservation of even the
\tt{O}\tt{S}p(1|2)
subgroup, resulting in substantial modifications to the boundary dynamics. This extended algebraic framework plays a key role in exploring generalized holographic dualities, wherein extended symmetries affect thermodynamic quantities such as energy and entropy. The resulting theory provides fertile ground for investigating the interplay between supersymmetry, higher–spin fields, and low–dimensional holography.

In contrast to earlier formulations of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
dilaton supergravity, most of which rely on coadjoint orbit techniques or metric–based descriptions, our BF–theoretic approach provides a systematic derivation of the full
\mathcal{N}=1
superconformal algebra. More importantly, it unveils novel dynamical effects induced by the dilaton fields that have no analogue within the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
framework. These effects arise naturally from the gauge–theoretic treatment and highlight the deeper physical implications of supersymmetric extensions, beyond their algebraic structure alone.

The primary distinction between
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
–based JT gravity theories lies in the role of the dilaton field and its impact on the extension of asymptotic symmetry algebras. While the
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
structure is sufficient to encode the isometric features of
\mathrm{AdS}_{2}
geometry, the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
framework offers a richer mathematical landscape to investigate more intricate holographic dualities and the dynamical behavior of boundary physical quantities. In particular,
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
provides a natural starting point for systematically extending the theory to higher–rank super Lie algebras and Chern–Simons–based formulations.

The findings of this study demonstrate that the asymptotic symmetry algebra is governed not only by the structural features of the gauge fields but also by the dynamical configurations of the dilaton field near the boundary. The dilaton is not an external matter sector, it is an intrinsic and structural part of the theory, taking values in the adjoint representation of the gauge algebra. Under suitable boundary conditions, its time–dependent components become dynamical and contribute to the asymptotic symmetry algebra in the form of an abelian ideal composed of mutually commuting modes. This extension is not the result of adding an external
U(1)
symmetry but instead arises from internal degrees of freedom that become dynamically relevant at the boundary. Consequently, the commuting currents induced by the dilaton should be viewed as a natural substructure within the extended asymptotic symmetry algebra, consistent with a semi–direct sum structure. The boundary behavior of the dilaton therefore plays a pivotal role in enriching the algebra while preserving its internal coherence, revealing the significance of internal gauge dynamics in shaping the asymptotic structure of two–dimensional gravitational systems.

In conclusion, the presence of the dilaton field profoundly affects not only the boundary dynamics in both
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
JT gravity frameworks, but also the mechanisms of extension and breaking within their respective asymptotic symmetry algebras. These extended models serve as powerful tools for uncovering new symmetry structures tied to boundary conditions and significantly enrich the theoretical landscape of holographic duality. In particular, they provide a robust foundation for probing deeper into holographic constructions such as the AdS/CFT correspondence and the SYK model. Future investigations into higher–rank Lie group–based generalizations are expected to further advance our theoretical understanding in this domain.

As discussed in the Introduction, several recent studies have examined the connection between generalized JT gravity and higher–spin extensions, particularly in the context of deformed SYK models and enhanced symmetry structures. For instance, in
Momeni:2024ygv
, a deformed SYK model incorporating spin–
3

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)

\mathcal{N}=1

Momeni:2020tyt

Momeni:2020zkx

Momeni:2021jhk

While these efforts share similar motivations, our approach is distinguished by its BF–theoretic formulation, which systematically derives the asymptotic symmetry algebra and elucidates the role of dilaton-induced symmetry breaking. This perspective advances the theoretical understanding of how higher–spin structures can be coherently integrated into two–dimensional holographic frameworks.

In our model, the interplay between the dilaton and higher–spin fields at the boundary induces a breakdown of the asymptotic symmetry structure. The initially present infinite–dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
superconformal algebra becomes dynamically reduced to its
\tt{O}\tt{S}p(1|2)
subgroup under the influence of the dilaton. This mechanism closely resembles Schwarzian–type symmetry breaking. However, beyond this complete reduction, the boundary fluctuations of the dilaton field can also support instances of symmetry extension. As a result, our framework exhibits a novel coexistence of both asymptotic symmetry breaking and symmetry enhancement. In conclusion, JT gravity, formulated as a two–dimensional BF theory, offers complementary theoretical frameworks for probing the geometric and physical origins of asymptotic symmetries and Schwarzian dynamics. The gauge symmetries inherent to BF theory, together with the algebraic structure of the target space, form the foundation of asymptotic symmetry algebras at the boundary. JT gravity, in this context, serves as a specific realization of such structures. In both formulations, the Schwarzian action emerges as an effective reduced model capturing the residual symmetries inherited from bulk gauge transformations. However, the dynamics governed by the Schwarzian action do not encompass the entirety of the gauge freedom, but rather reflect the behavior of a physically significant subset. Therefore, BF theory and JT gravity jointly provide a rich and unified approach to elucidating the full structure of boundary symmetries and their holographic implications.

An open question remains as to whether the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
JT gravity framework developed in this work admits a well–defined quantum mechanical dual, possibly in the spirit of an extended SYK–like model involving spin-
\tfrac{3}{2}

\mathfrak{osp}(\mathcal{N}|2)

\mathcal{N}>1

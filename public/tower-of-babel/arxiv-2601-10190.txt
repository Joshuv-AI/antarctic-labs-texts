Exponential Analysis for Entanglement Distillation
Zhiwen Lin, Ke Li, Kun Fang

Abstract
Historically, the focus in entanglement distillation has predominantly been on the distillable entanglement, and the framework assumes complete knowledge of the initial state. In this paper, we study the reliability function of entanglement distillation, which specifies the optimal exponent of the decay of the distillation error when the distillation rate is below the distillable entanglement. Furthermore, to capture greater operational significance, we extend the framework from the standard setting of known states to a black-box setting, where distillation is performed from a set of possible states. We establish an exact finite blocklength result connecting to composite correlated hypothesis testing without any redundant correction terms. Based on this, the reliability function of entanglement distillation is characterized by the regularized quantum Hoeffding divergence. In the special case of a pure initial state, our result reduces to the error exponent for entanglement concentration derived by Hayashi et al. in 2003. Given full prior knowledge of the state, we construct a concrete optimal distillation protocol. Additionally, we analyze the strong converse exponent of entanglement distillation. While all the above results assume the free operations to be non-entangling, we also investigate other free operation classes, including PPT-preserving, dually non-entangling, and dually PPT-preserving operations.

The

In this paper, we study the reliability function of entanglement distillation to encompass not only mixed states but also the black-box scenario. In natural operational settings, complete knowledge of the initial state is unattainable due to unknown noise or adversarial effects. Within the finite blocklength analysis, we derive an exact relationship between entanglement distillation and composite correlated hypothesis testing. II-B

Quantum Divergences

A functional
{{\mathbb{D}}}:\mathscr{D}\times\mathscr{P}\to{{\mathbb{R}}}
is called a
quantum divergence
if it satisfies the data-processing inequality: for any quantum channel
\Lambda
and any
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathscr{D}\times\mathscr{P}
, it holds that
{{\mathbb{D}}}(\Lambda(\rho)\|\Lambda(\sigma))\leq{{\mathbb{D}}}(\rho\|\sigma)
. In the following, we introduce several quantum divergences that will be used throughout this work. We also define quantum divergences between two sets of quantum states.

Definition 1

For any
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathscr{D}\times\mathscr{P}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
, the Umegaki relative entropy
[
27
]
is defined by

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma):=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)],

the Petz Rényi divergence
[
28
]
is defined by

\displaystyle D_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm P},\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha}\right],

and the sandwiched Rényi divergence
[
29
,
30
]
is defined by

\displaystyle D_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S},\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\operatorname{Tr}\left[\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right]^{\alpha},

if either
\alpha<1
and
\rho\not\perp\sigma
or
\alpha>1
and
{\operatorname{supp}}(\rho)\subseteq{\operatorname{supp}}(\sigma)
. Otherwise, we set the divergences as
+\infty
.

Definition 2
(Quantum divergence between two sets of states)

Let
{{\mathbb{D}}}
be a quantum divergence between two quantum states. Then for any sets
{{\mathscr{A}}},{{\mathscr{B}}}\subseteq\mathscr{D}({\cal H})
, the quantum divergence between these two sets is defined by

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{D}}}({{\mathscr{A}}}\|{{\mathscr{B}}}):=\inf_{{\rho\in{{\mathscr{A}}},\sigma\in{{\mathscr{B}}}}}{{\mathbb{D}}}(\rho\|\sigma).

Let
{{\mathscr{A}}}=\{{{\mathscr{A}}}_{n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}
and
{{\mathscr{B}}}=\{{{\mathscr{B}}}_{n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}
be two sequences of sets of quantum states, where each
{{\mathscr{A}}}_{n},{{\mathscr{B}}}_{n}\subseteq\mathscr{D}({\cal H}^{\otimes n})
. the regularized divergence between these sequences is defined by

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{D}}}^{\infty}({{\mathscr{A}}}\|{{\mathscr{B}}}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}{{\mathbb{D}}}({{\mathscr{A}}}_{n}\|{{\mathscr{B}}}_{n}),

if the limit exists.

In many practical scenarios, the sequences of sets under consideration are not arbitrary but possess a structure that is compatible with tensor products. This property, known as
stability
(or closeness) under tensor product, is formalized as follows.

Definition 3
(Stable sequence)

Let
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
,
\mathscr{B}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
, and
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
. We say that
(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B},\mathscr{C})
is stable under tensor product if, for any

and

, it holds that

. In short, we write
\mathscr{A}\otimes\mathscr{B}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
. A sequence of sets
\{\mathscr{A}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\mathscr{A}_{n}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
is called stable under tensor product if
\mathscr{A}_{n}\otimes\mathscr{A}_{m}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{n+m}

.

II-C

Composite Correlated Hypothesis Testing

We set up a hypothesis testing problem with the composite null hypothesis comprising all separable states
\mathscr{F}_{n}
, against the alternative hypothesis which is the set of initial states
\mathscr{R}_{n}
. Two types of error can occur. The type-I error is the probability that we incorrectly accept the resource state
\mathscr{R}_{n}
while it is actually the free state
\mathscr{F}_{n}
, and the type-II error corresponds to the converse situation. Providing a fixed number
r>0
and constraining the type-I error to be at most
2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor}
, we denote the optimal type-II error by

\displaystyle\beta_{n}(\mathscr{R}_{n},2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq M_{n}\leq\mathds{1}\\
\max_{\sigma_{n}\in\mathscr{F}_{n}}\operatorname{Tr}((\mathds{1}-M_{n})\sigma_{n})\leq 2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor}\end{subarray}}\max_{\rho_{n}\in\mathscr{R}_{n}}\operatorname{Tr}M_{n}\rho_{n}.

Problem Statement and Main Results

Entanglement distillation involves two separated parties, conventionally named Alice and Bob. In previous studies
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
25
]
, two parties share multiple copies of a bipartite entangled state
\rho_{AB}
and aim to convert them into a smaller number of maximally entangled states. However, the initial state is not fully accessible in practical implementations. Therefore, considering the initial resource states as a set

, it becomes necessary to investigate universal distillation protocols in “state-agnostic” scenarios. For this conversion, not all quantum channels are available. We restrict the free operations to non-entangling operations, i.e., those that map separable states to separable states, denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}:=\{\Lambda\in\mathrm{CPTP}:\Lambda(\sigma)\in\mathscr{F},\forall\sigma\in\mathscr{F}\}
.

III-A

The Reliability Function

Let
\mathscr{R}_{n}
be a convex compact set of quantum states on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{AB}^{\otimes n}
. When the distillation rate is
r
, which means that the yield is
\lfloor rn\rfloor
copies of the two-qubit maximally entangled states
\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}
, the error of entanglement distillation in a black-box setting is defined as

\varepsilon(\mathscr{R}_{n}\!\!\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\!\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor})\!\!:=\!\!\min_{\Lambda_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\max_{\rho_{n}\in\mathscr{R}_{n}}\!\!(1\!-\!F(\Lambda_{n}(\rho_{n}),\!\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor})\!).

We establish an exact equivalence between the error of entanglement distillation in the black-box setting and the optimal type-II error in the composite correlated hypothesis testing.

Theorem 4
(Finite blocklength relation)

Let
\mathscr{R}_{n}
be a convex compact set of quantum states on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{AB}^{\otimes n}
. Let
r>0
be a real number, and the distillation protocol is restricted to non-entangling operations
\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}
. Then the error of entanglement distillation in the block box setting equals the optimal type-II error of hypothesis testing

\varepsilon\left(\mathscr{R}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\right)=\beta_{n}\left(\mathscr{R}_{n},2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor}\right).

(1)

Proof:

1). Proof of the upper bound:

Construct a distillation protocol,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(X)\!=\!\operatorname{Tr}(\!(\mathds{1}-M_{n})X)\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor}\!+\!\operatorname{Tr}(M_{n}X)\frac{\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor}}{(2^{\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor})^{2}\!-\!1},

(2)

where
\{\mathds{1}-M_{n},~M_{n}\}
is the measurement that achieves the optimal type-II error
\beta_{n}(\mathscr{R}_{n},2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor})
.

First, prove that
\Lambda_{n}
constructed above belongs to non-entangling channels. For any
\sigma_{n}\in\mathscr{F}_{n}
,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(\sigma_{n})\!=\!\operatorname{Tr}(\!(\mathds{1}-M_{n})\sigma_{n})\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor}\!\!+\!\!\operatorname{Tr}(M_{n}\sigma_{n})\frac{\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor}}{(2^{\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor})^{2}-1}.

It is known that the isotropic state

\displaystyle p\Phi_{2}^{\otimes m}+(1-p)\frac{\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes m}}{(2^{m})^{2}-1}

is separable for
p\in[0,2^{-m}]

[
32
, page. 319]
. Due to
\operatorname{Tr}((\mathds{1}-M_{n})\sigma_{n})\leq 2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor}
, we obtain
\Lambda_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}
.

Then, we verify the figure of merit of distillation. For any
\rho_{n}\in\mathscr{R}_{n},

\displaystyle 1-F(\Lambda_{n}(\rho_{n}),\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda_{n}(\rho_{n})\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(M_{n}\rho_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{\rho_{n}\in\mathscr{R}_{n}}\operatorname{Tr}(M_{n}\rho_{n}),

where the first equality follows that
\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}
is a pure state, and the second equality results from a direct computation upon substituting
\Lambda_{n}(\rho_{n})
according to Eq. (
2
).
Based on the above, due to definition
\varepsilon(\mathscr{R}_{n}\!\!\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\!\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor})
, we get
\varepsilon(\mathscr{R}_{n}\!\!\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\!\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor})\leq\beta_{n}(\mathscr{R}_{n},2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor})
.

2). Proof of the lower bound:
Let
\Lambda_{n}
be the optimal distillation operation for
\varepsilon(\mathscr{R}_{n}\!\!\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\!\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor})
.
Construct a measurement,

\displaystyle\left\{M_{n},\ \mathds{1}-M_{n}\right\}:=\left\{\mathds{1}-\Lambda_{n}^{\dagger}\big(\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\big),\ \Lambda_{n}^{\dagger}\big(\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\big)\right\}.

(3)

On the one hand, for any
\sigma_{n}\in\mathscr{F}_{n}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left((\mathds{1}-M_{n})\sigma_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{n}^{\dagger}(\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor})\sigma_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\Lambda_{n}(\sigma_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left((\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor})^{\Gamma}\Lambda_{n}(\sigma_{n})^{\Gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{1}{2^{\lfloor rn\rfloor}}W\Lambda_{n}(\sigma_{n})^{\Gamma}\right)

(4)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{1}{2^{\lfloor rn\rfloor}}\Lambda_{n}(\sigma_{n})^{\Gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor},

where
\Gamma
is the partial transpose on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{B}^{\otimes n}
, and
W
is the swap operator on the Hilbert space
\mathds{C}_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\otimes\mathds{C}_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}
. The inequality is because
W\leq\mathds{1}
and
\Lambda_{n}(\sigma_{n})^{\Gamma}
is separable since
\Lambda_{n}
is a non-entangling channel, and hence is positive semi-definite. The final equality also follows from the fact that
\Lambda_{n}(\sigma_{n})^{\Gamma}
is a separable state.

On the other hand, for any
\rho_{n}\in\mathscr{R}_{n},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(M_{n}\rho_{n})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}((\mathds{1}-\Lambda_{n}^{\dagger}(\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}))\rho_{n})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}((\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor})\Lambda_{n}(\rho_{n}))

\displaystyle=1-\mathrm{F}(\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor},\Lambda_{n}(\rho_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}\left(1-F(\Lambda_{n}(\rho_{n}),\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor})\right),

Due to definition
\beta_{n}(\mathscr{R}_{n},2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor})
, we get
\varepsilon(\mathscr{R}_{n}\!\!\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\!\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor\!rn\!\rfloor})\geq\beta_{n}(\mathscr{R}_{n},2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor})
.

We now turn to the asymptotic behavior of entanglement distillation. Let
\mathscr{R}=\{\mathscr{R}_{n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}
be a stable sequences of convex compact sets of quantum states, where
\mathscr{R}_{n}\subseteq\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB}^{\otimes n})
. When the distillation rate

is below the distillable entanglement, the error of entanglement distillation converges exponentially to

E^{\operatorname{rf}}(\mathscr{R},\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}},r):=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\varepsilon\left(\mathscr{R}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\right).

(5)

Based on the quantum Hoeffding bound for composite correlated hypotheses established in
[
33
]

Theorem 5
(Reliability function for entanglement distillation)

Let
\mathscr{R}=\{\mathscr{R}_{n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}
be a stable sequences of convex compact sets of quantum states with each
\mathscr{R}_{n}\subseteq\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB}^{\otimes n})

be a real number, and the distillation protocol is restricted to non-entangling operations

\displaystyle E^{\operatorname{rf}}(\mathscr{R},\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}},r)=H_{r}^{\infty}(\mathscr{R}\|\mathscr{F}),

(6)

where the RHS follows Definition

and is induced by the Hoeffding divergence for two states

\displaystyle H_{n,r}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma_{n}):=\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}(nr-D_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm P},\alpha}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma_{n})).

(7)

This result is a direct consequence of Theorem

and

. The proof of the upper bound follows the proof method in Section 5.2 of

\displaystyle=

where

.

Remark 6

Examining the proof of Theorem
4
, we observe that the distillation rate is associated with the Type-I error in hypothesis testing, while the distillation error is linked to the Type-II error. Furthermore, in the context of Hoeffding’s hypothesis testing framework, the Type-I error and the Type-II error are symmetric. Consequently, by restricting the Type-II error to
2^{-rn}
and minimizing the Type-I error, the latter will converge exponentially at rate
H_{r}^{\infty}(\mathscr{F}\|\mathscr{R})
. Analogous to Remark
9
below, let the parameter
r
decay as
O(1/n)
, then it immediately follows that the distillable entanglement in the black-box setting is bounded by the regularized relative entropy of composite entanglement
D^{\infty}(\mathscr{R}\|\mathscr{F})
.
Provided that the regularized Petz Rényi relative entropy
D^{\infty}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm P},\alpha}(\mathscr{R}\|\mathscr{F})
exhibits continuity at
\alpha=1
, the distillation rate is characterized by
D^{\infty}(\mathscr{R}\|\mathscr{F})
.

Given complete prior knowledge of the initial state—that is, when distillation starts from a precisely known quantum state
\rho_{AB}
—setting
\mathscr{R}_{n}=\rho^{\otimes n}
immediately yields a corollary.

Corollary 7

Let
\rho
be an entangled state on Hilbert space

. Let

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}(r-D_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm P},\alpha}(\mathscr{F}_{1}\|\psi))

III-B

The Strong Converse Exponent

Returning to the black-box scenario, let
\mathscr{R}=\{\mathscr{R}_{n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}
be a stable sequences of convex compact sets of quantum states with each
\mathscr{R}_{n}\subseteq\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB}^{\otimes n})
. When the distillation rate
r
exceeds the distillable entanglement, the error of entanglement distillation no longer tends to 0 but instead converges inevitably to 1. To investigate the asymptotic behavior in this regime, we define the fidelity of entanglement distillation in the black-box setting,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}(\mathscr{R}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}):=\max_{\Lambda_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\min_{\rho_{n}\in\mathscr{R}_{n}}F(\Lambda_{n}(\rho_{n}),\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}).

The strong converse exponent for entanglement distillation in black-box setting is defined as the convergence exponent of the fidelity of entanglement distillation,

\displaystyle E^{\operatorname{sc}}(\mathscr{R},\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}},r):=\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathfrak{f}\left(\mathscr{R}_{n}\!\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\!\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\right).

(11)

A direct observation from the definition is that the fidelity and the error of entanglement distillation in the black-box setting satisfy the relation

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}\left(\mathscr{R}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\right)=1-\varepsilon\left(\mathscr{R}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\right).

Similar to the approach used in proving the reliability function, it is useful to establish a connection with hypothesis testing. Therefore, we consider the
detection probability

[
36
]
of
\mathscr{R}_{n}
under the null hypothesis
\mathscr{F}_{n}
at a level
2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor}
,

\displaystyle 1-\beta_{n}(\mathscr{R}_{n},2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq M_{n}\leq\mathds{1}\\
\max_{\sigma_{n}\in\mathscr{F}_{n}}\operatorname{Tr}((\mathds{1}-M_{n})\sigma_{n})\leq 2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor}\end{subarray}}\min_{\rho_{n}\in\mathscr{R}_{n}}(1-\operatorname{Tr}(M_{n}\rho_{n})).

From Theorem
4
, we immediately derive the following corollary.

Corollary 10

Let
\mathscr{R}_{n}
be a convex compact set of quantum states on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{AB}^{\otimes n}
. Let
r>0
be a real number, and the distillation protocol is restricted to non-entangling operations
\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}
. Then the fidelity of entanglement distillation in the black-box setting can be related to the detection probability of hypothesis testing

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}\left(\mathscr{R}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}}\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\right)=1-\beta_{n}\left(\mathscr{R}_{n},2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor}\right).

(12)

Applying the strong converse exponent for composite correlated hypotheses established in
[
33
, Theorem 6.1]
yields the following results for entanglement distillation.

Proposition 11

Let
\mathscr{R}=\{\mathscr{R}_{n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}
be a stable sequences of convex compact sets of quantum states with each
\mathscr{R}_{n}\subseteq\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB}^{\otimes n})
. Let
r>D^{\infty}(\mathscr{R}\|\mathscr{F})
be a real number, and the distillation protocol is restricted to non-entangling operations
\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}}
. Then the strong converse exponent for entanglement distillation in the black-box setting is lower bounded by the regularized quantum Hoeffding anti-divergences

\displaystyle E^{\operatorname{sc}}(\mathscr{R},\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}},r)\geq\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}(r-D_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S},\alpha}^{\infty}(\mathscr{R}\|\mathscr{F})).

(13)

Furthermore, if
\{\mathscr{R}_{n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}=\{\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}
and
D^{\infty}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S},\alpha}(\mathscr{R}\|\mathscr{F})
is differentiable in
\alpha
for
\alpha\geq 1
, then the strong converse exponent for entanglement distillation with complete information about the initial state is characterized by the regularized quantum Hoeffding anti-divergences

\displaystyle E^{\operatorname{sc}}(\rho,\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{NE}},r)=\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}(r-D_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S},\alpha}^{\infty}(\rho\|\mathscr{F})).

(14)

III-C

Error Exponents across Different Free Operations

Within the finite blocklength framework, the proof approach of Theorem
4
can be extended directly to PPT states and PPT-preserving operations upon replacing separable states and non-entangling operations. In addition, PPT states satisfy the conditions required for composite correlated hypothesis testing. Consequently, the reliability function for entanglement distillation under PPT-preserving operations can be derived.

Example 1
(PPT states and PPT-preserving operations)

A state
\sigma
on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{AB}
is called the positive-partial-transpose (PPT) state if it satisfies
\sigma_{AB}^{\Gamma}\geq 0
, where
\Gamma
denotes the partial transpose. The set of PPT states is denoted by
\mathrm{PPT}
. The class of positive-partial-transpose-preserving (PPT-preserving) operations is denined as
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{PPT}}:=\{\Lambda\in\mathrm{CPTP}:\Lambda(X)\in\mathrm{PPT},\forall X\in\mathrm{PPT}\}
.

The validity of the proof of Theorem
4
is maintained under the substitution of PPT states for separable states and PPT-preserving operations
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{PPT}}
for non-entangling operations
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{NE}}
. Then we get the error of entanglement distillation under PPT-preserving operations corresponds precisely to the Type-II error of hypothesis testing with null hypothesis
\mathrm{PPT}
and alternative hypothesis
\mathscr{R}
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\mathscr{R}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{PPT}}}\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor})=\beta_{n}(\mathscr{R}_{n},\mathrm{PPT},2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor}).

(15)

The reliability function of entanglement distillation under PPT-preserving operations is characterized by a regularized quantum Hoeffding divergence

\displaystyle E^{\operatorname{rf}}(\mathscr{R},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{PPT}},r)=H_{r}^{\infty}(\mathscr{R}\|\mathrm{PPT}).

(16)

Remark 12

Consider the distillation rate
r=m/n
for some fixed integer
m
and the resource sequence
\{\mathscr{R}_{n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}=\{\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}
. Applying the approach of
[
25
]
or Theorem 10 of
[
36
]
to the known initial state
\rho_{AB}
in the vanishing rate limit, we obtain that the error exponent is characterized as the single-letter divergence

under PPT-preserving operations. This achieves a result analogous to that in
[
25
]
.

Except for the constraint on state evolution in the Schrödinger picture, specifically non-entangling and PPT-preserving operations, we additionally impose constraints on the evolution of measurement operators in the Heisenberg picture, thereby defining the dually non-entangling and dually PPT-preserving operations.

Example 2
(Separable states and dually non-entangling operations)

A channel is called dually non-entangling if it is non-entangling in the Schrödinger picture, i.e., it maps separable states to separable states, and its adjoint is non-entangling in the Heisenberg picture, i.e., it maps separable measurement operators to separable operators. The class of dually non-entangling operations is denoted as
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{DNE}}:=\{\Lambda\in\mathrm{CPTP}:\Lambda(\sigma)\in\mathscr{F},\forall\sigma\in\mathscr{F},\text{and}~\Lambda^{\dagger}(M)\in\mathrm{cone}(\mathscr{F}),\forall M\in\mathrm{cone}(\mathscr{F})\},

where
\mathrm{cone}(\mathscr{F})
is the cone generated by the set of separable states
\mathscr{F}
.

To address this scenario, we formulate a hypothesis testing framework where only separable measurements
\mathds{SEP}
are allowed. Adapting the proof of Theorem
4
to the present case necessitates two adjustments. First, the construction of the distillation protocol in Eq. (
2
) remains unchanged. Its adjoint map

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}^{\dagger}(Y)=\operatorname{Tr}

\displaystyle(\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}Y)(\mathds{1}-M_{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(2^{\lfloor rn\rfloor})^{2}-1}\operatorname{Tr}((\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor})Y)M_{n}

preserves separability because the measurement operators
\{\mathds{1}-M_{n},M_{n}\}
are separable. Second, the construction of the measurement in Eq. (
3
) is modified to

\displaystyle M_{n}:=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\lfloor rn\rfloor}}{2^{\lfloor rn\rfloor}+1}\Lambda_{n}^{\dagger}(\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}),

\displaystyle\mathds{1}-M_{n}:=

\displaystyle\ \Lambda_{n}^{\dagger}(\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}+\frac{\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}}{2^{\lfloor rn\rfloor}+1}).

Because
\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}
and
\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}+\frac{1}{2^{\lfloor rn\rfloor}+1}(\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor})
are separable and channel
\Lambda_{n}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{DNE}}

When the sequence of initial state sets
\{\mathscr{R}_{n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}
is compatible with separable measurements and the distillation rate approaches zero, by applying Theorem 16 of
[

\displaystyle D^{\mathds{SEP},\infty}(\mathscr{F}\|\rho):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{\mathds{SEP}}(\mathscr{F}_{n}\|\rho^{\otimes n}),

(17)

where

\displaystyle D^{\mathds{SEP}}(\sigma\|\rho):=\sup_{\{M_{x}\}_{x}\in\mathds{SEP}}\sum_{x}\operatorname{Tr}(M_{x}\sigma)\log\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(M_{x}\sigma)}{\operatorname{Tr}(M_{x}\rho)}.

Example 3
(PPT states and dually PPT-preserving operations)

A channel is dually PPT-preserving if it preserves PPT states in the Schrödinger picture and its adjoint preserves PPT measurement operators in the Heisenberg picture. We denote the class of dually PPT-preserving operations by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{DPPT}}:=\{\Lambda\in\mathrm{CPTP}:\Lambda(\sigma)\in\mathrm{PPT},\forall\sigma\in\mathrm{PPT},\text{and}~\Lambda^{\dagger}(M)\in\mathrm{cone}(\mathrm{PPT}),\forall M\in\mathrm{cone}(\mathrm{PPT})\},

where
\mathrm{cone}(\mathrm{PPT})

Analogous to Example

\mathds{PPT}

\mathscr{R}_{n}

\{\mathscr{R}_{n}\}_{n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}
is compatible with PPT measurements, the distillation operations are restricted to be dually PPT-preserving and the distillation rate approaches zero, then the error exponent for entanglement distillation is characterized by

\displaystyle D^{\mathds{PPT},\infty}(\mathrm{PPT}\|\rho):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{\mathds{PPT}}(\mathrm{PPT}_{n}\|\rho^{\otimes n}),

(18)

where

\displaystyle D^{\mathds{PPT}}(\sigma\|\rho):=\sup_{\{M_{x}\}_{x}\in\mathds{PPT}}\sum_{x}\operatorname{Tr}(M_{x}\sigma)\log\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(M_{x}\sigma)}{\operatorname{Tr}(M_{x}\rho)}.

III-D

Optimal Distillation Protocol

In this section, we develop a concrete distillation protocol, based on the information-spectrum method, for the case of complete prior knowledge of the initial entangled state
\rho_{AB}
. This protocol achieves the optimal convergence exponent given in (
8
).

The construction of the explicit distillation protocol relies on the following lemma, which is adapted from Lemma 1 of
[
38
]
.

Lemma 13

In the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A_{n}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B_{n}}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{A_{i}}=\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B_{i}}=\mathcal{H}_{B}
for each
i
, there exists a universal permutation invariant product state
\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}
, such that for any permutation invariant separable state
{\sigma}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}
, we have

{\sigma}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\leq g_{n,|A|,|B|}~\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}

where
g_{n,|A|,|B|}\leq(n+1)^{|A|^{2}+|B|^{2}-2}
. The above separable partition is
A_{1}...A_{n}:B_{1}...B_{n}
, and the permutation acts on the
n
subspaces
A_{1}B_{1}:...:A_{n}B_{n}
.

Proof:

Lemma 1 of
[
38
]
states that for any Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and any
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, there exists a universal permutation invariant state
\omega_{A^{n}}^{(n)}
on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{\otimes n}
, such that for any permutation invariant state
\eta_{A^{n}}
, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{A^{n}}\leq g_{n,|A|}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}},

where
g_{n,|A|}\leq(n+1)^{|A|^{2}-1}
.

Therefore, there exist universal permutation invariant states
\omega_{A^{n}}^{(n)}
and
\omega_{B^{n}}^{(n)}
on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{\otimes n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}^{\otimes n}
respectively, such that for any permutation invariant state
\eta_{A^{n}}\in\mathscr{D}^{inv}(\mathcal{H}_{A}^{\otimes n})
and
\eta_{B^{n}}\in\mathscr{D}^{inv}(\mathcal{H}_{B}^{\otimes n})
, the relation

\displaystyle\eta_{A^{n}}\leq g_{n,|A|}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}},~\eta_{B^{n}}\leq g_{n,|B|}\omega^{(n)}_{B^{n}},

holds,
where
g_{n,|A|}\leq(n+1)^{|A|^{2}-1}
and
g_{n,|B|}\leq(n+1)^{|B|^{2}-1}
.

Let
\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}=\omega_{A^{n}}^{(n)}\otimes\omega_{B^{n}}^{(n)}
and
g_{n,|A|,|B|}=g_{n,|A|}g_{n,|B|}
. We then arrive at the conclusion.

Lemma 14
(
[
40
,
41
]
)

Let
A
and
B
be positive semi-definite operators, then for all
s\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\{A\geq B\}B)+\operatorname{Tr}(\{A<B\}A)\leq\operatorname{Tr}A^{s}B^{1-s}.

(19)

We now construct an entanglement distillation protocol that attains the optimal error exponent.

Theorem 15

Let
\rho
be an entangled state on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{AB}
. The entanglement distillation protocol is constructed by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(X)\!=\!\operatorname{Tr}((\mathds{1}-{M}_{n})X)\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}\!+\!\operatorname{Tr}({M}_{n}X)\frac{\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lfloor rn\rfloor}}{(2^{\lfloor rn\rfloor})^{2}-1},

where
{M}_{n}=\{\rho^{\otimes n}\leq 2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}
, i.e., the projection onto the positive part of the operator
2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}-\rho^{\otimes n}
and

a_{n}=\frac{rn+(s-1)D_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm P},s}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\mathscr{F}_{n})+\log g_{n,|A|,|B|}+1}{s},

for a fixed
s\in(0,1)
. Then the protocol is non-entangling and can achieve the reliability function of entanglement distillation with the initial state
\rho
in Corollary
7
.

Proof:

We first prove that
\Lambda_{n}
is an non-entangling channel. Since the isotropic state
p\Phi_{2}^{\otimes m}+(1-p)\frac{\mathds{1}-\Phi_{2}^{\otimes m}}{(2^{m})^{2}-1}
is separable when
p\in[0,2^{-m}]

[
32
, p. 319]
, in order to have
\Lambda_{n}(\sigma_{n})\in\mathscr{F}
, for any
\sigma_{n}\in\mathscr{F}
, it suffices to ensure that
\operatorname{Tr}(\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}\sigma_{n})\leq 2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor}
.
It is straightforward to observe

\displaystyle\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{U_{\pi_{n}}\rho^{\otimes n}U_{\pi_{n}}^{*}>2^{a_{n}}U_{\pi_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}U_{\pi_{n}}^{*}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{\pi_{n}}\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}U_{\pi_{n}}^{*}

holds for any
\pi_{n}\in S_{n}
, and therefore

\displaystyle\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|S_{n}|}\sum_{\pi_{n}\in S_{n}}U_{\pi_{n}}\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}U_{\pi_{n}}^{*}.

Then for any
\sigma_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}\sigma_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\frac{1}{|S_{n}|}\sum_{\pi_{n}\in S_{n}}U_{\pi_{n}}\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}U_{\pi_{n}}^{*}\sigma_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}\frac{1}{|S_{n}|}\sum_{\pi_{n}\in S_{n}}U_{\pi_{n}}^{*}\sigma_{n}U_{\pi_{n}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}\tilde{\sigma}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}),

(20)

where
\tilde{\sigma}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}
is a separable state invariant under permutations. Furthermore, applying Lemma
13
, the above Eq. (
20
) is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}g_{n,|A|,|B|}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g_{n,|A|,|B|}2^{-a_{n}}\operatorname{Tr}((\rho^{\otimes n})^{s}(2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}})^{1-s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{n,|A|,|B|}2^{-a_{n}s}2^{(s-1)D_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm P},s}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g_{n,|A|,|B|}2^{-a_{n}s}2^{(s-1)D_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm P},s}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{F}_{n})},

where the first inequality is due to Lemma
14
for any fixed
s\in(0,1)
.
In order to achieve
\operatorname{Tr}(\{\rho^{\otimes n}>2^{a_{n}}\omega^{(n)}_{A^{n}:B^{n}}\}\sigma_{n})\leq 2^{-\lfloor rn\rfloor}
, it suffices to set

\displaystyle a_{n}=\frac{rn+(s-1)D_{{\scriptscriptstyle\rm P},s}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{F}_{n})+\log g_{n,|A|,|B|}+1}{s}.

(21)

Subsequently, we analyze the performance of the entanglement distillation protocol.

IV
Conclusion and Discussion

Our black-box extension of the conventional entanglement distillation is a crucial step towards modelling realistic scenario, accounting for both environmental uncertainty and non‑i.i.d. correlations. In particular, we establish an exact equivalence, free of redundant correction terms, between the error of entanglement distillation in the black-box setting and the optimal type-II error in composite correlated hypothesis testing. Therefore, we obtain the precise reliability function for entanglement distillation in the black-box setting under the classes of non-entangling and PPT-preserving operations.
When the initial state is pure, our result reduces to the formula of Hayashi et al.
[
24
]
. We also derive the error exponent for dually non-entangling and dually PPT-preserving operations in the limit of vanishing distillation rate. Given complete knowledge of the initial state, we construct an explicit non-entangling protocol that achieves the reliability function. Additionally, a lower bound for the strong converse exponent of entanglement distillation is provided in the black-box setting.

Investigating the continuity of the regularized Petz Rényi relative entropy at
\alpha=1
is an interesting question, as it relates to recovering the result of Lami et al.
[
25
]
and to the distillable entanglement in the black-box setting. Although
[
33
]
provides counterexamples demonstrating the breakdown of continuity in the general case, no explicit counterexample has been identified for scenarios where the first component constitutes sequences of separable state sets, which is the case of interest in this work. Notably, the antisymmetric Werner state—the universal counterexample in entanglement theory—remains continuous under this configuration
[
33
]
. Moreover, extending the study of the reliability function to the distillation of multipartite entanglement and other resources presents an interesting direction for future work.

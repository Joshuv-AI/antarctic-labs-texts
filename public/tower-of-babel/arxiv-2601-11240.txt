Highly regular vertex-transitive graphs are globally rigid
Angelo El Saliby

Abstract
. A graph is said to be globally rigid in d d -dimensional space if almost all of its embeddings are unique up to isometries. If a graph has enough automorphisms to send any of its vertices into any other, then it is called vertex-transitive. We show that, in any dimension, highly regular vertex-transitive graphs are globally rigid, positively answering a conjecture of Sean Dewar. Furthermore, we construct examples that show that our constant for regularity is best possible.

1.
Introduction

Given a graph and an assignment of a point in
d
-dimensional space for every one of its vertices,
graph rigidity asks whether there are finitely or infinitely many other equivalent realizations up to isometry,
i.e. other assignments of points in
d
-dimensional space that preserve the edge lengths of edges in the graph,
but that cannot be mapped one into another through an isometry of the ambient space.
When the starting realization is
generic
this is a property of the graph itself,
and we say that a graph is rigid if there are finitely many equivalent realizations to any given generic one,
again up to isometry.
If all generic realizations are unique up to isometry, we say that the graph is globally rigid.
Efficient combinatorial characterizations of rigidity and global rigidity exist in dimension
1
and
2
,
while finding combinatorial characterizations in dimension
d\geq 3
is one of the main open problems in rigidity theory.
As such, many results and classifications are known in the plane but not in higher dimensions.
One such example arises in the study of vertex-transitive graphs,
which are graphs whose local structure at each vertex is the same.
Vertex-transitive graphs are regular,
meaning that all the vertices have the same degree,
and it was proved in
[
3
]
that a vertex-transitive graph of degree at least
6
is globally rigid in the plane.
This leads to the following conjecture.

Conjecture

(
[
1
, Conjecture 20]
)
.

There exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
with the following property.
For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, if
G
is a vertex-transitive graph of degree at least
f(d)
, then
G
is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proving that a highly vertex-connected graph is globally rigid
was a decades long open conjecture in graph rigidity theory
until
[
6
]
.
There, the proof builds on recent developments on
weakly globally linked pairs
from
[
4
]
.
Although a connected vertex-transitive graph might have a higher degree than its vertex-connectivity,
it’s vertex-connectivity is at least two thirds of its degree.
As an immediate consequence one can verify Dewar’s conjecture.
In this paper, we improve on this result by providing a tight constant:

Theorem 1.1
.

If
G
is a connected vertex-transitive graph of degree at least
d(d+1)
,
then
G
is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Moreover, there exist connected vertex-transitive graphs of degree
d(d+1)-1
that are not globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

To prove the result for the improved constant,
we employ the same argument as in
[
6
]
with minor adaptations.
To show that the constant we provide is best possible we generalize a result of
[
3
]
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In
Section

2
we briefly introduce the rigidity matroid
and we recall some important definitions and results on weakly globally linked pairs from
[
4
]
.
Furthermore, we discuss ordering-induced subgraphs and some criteria for their independence in the rigidity matroid,
summarizing results from
[
6
]
.
In
Section

3
we prove the main result of this paper.

2.
Weakly globally linked pairs

We briefly summarize the notions and results from
[
6
]
and
[
4
]
that we need.
We start by giving the usual definitions in graph rigidity. More details can be found in
[
2
,
5
]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple, undirected graph on vertex set
V
and edge set
E
.
If
v,w\in V
are the endpoints of an edge in
E
, we write
vw\in E
.
Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d\geq 1
. A function

p\colon V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

is called
realization
of
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Such a realization can also be viewed as a point in
\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigr)^{V}
.
Define the
rigidity map

f_{G}\colon\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigr)^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{E}
by

\bigl(p_{v}\bigr)_{v\in V}\mapsto\bigl(\|p_{v}-p_{w}\|^{2}\bigr)_{vw\in E}.

Let
K_{V}
denote the complete graph on
V
. We say that two realizations
p
and
q
of
G
are
equivalent
if
f_{G}(p)=f_{G}(q)
,
while we say they are
congruent
if
f_{K_{V}}(p)=f_{K_{V}}(q)
.
We say that the graph
G
is
globally rigid
if there exists an open dense subset
U
of
\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigr)^{V}
such that for all
p\in U
we have

(1)

f_{G}^{-1}\bigl(f_{G}(p)\bigr)=f_{K_{V}}^{-1}\bigl(f_{K_{V}}(p)\bigr).

The rigidity matroid on a realization
p
of
K_{V}
is the row matroid
associated to the Jacobian of
f_{K_{V}}
, evaluated at
p
.
It can be shown that there exists an open dense subset of
\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigr)^{V}
such that the corresponding matroids are all isomorphic.
We call the arising matroid the
rigidity matroid
of
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and we call any realization in this open dense subset
generic
.
We say that a graph
G=(V,E)
is
rigid
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
E
has maximal rank in the rigidity matroid of
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2.1.
Weakly globally linked pairs

Most of the results and definitions in this section are summarized from
[
4
]
,
but the exposition and the selection of them is strongly reliant on
[
6
]
.
We refer to these papers for further details.
Weakly globally linked pairs adapt the combinatorial aspects of linked pairs to the global rigidity setting.
They were introduced in
[
4
]
and they are a crucial ingredient in
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and consider two distinct vertices
u,v\in V
.
Let
p
be a realization of
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We call the tuple
(G,p)
a
framework
(in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
).
We say that the pair
u,v
is
globally linked
in
(G,p)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if the set

\Bigl\{\bigl\|q(v)-q(u)\bigr\|\text{ s.t.\ the framework }(G,q)\text{ is equivalent to }(G,p)\Bigr\}

contains only one element.

Hence, a framework is globally rigid if and only if every pair of its vertices is globally linked.
Global linkedness of pairs is not a generic property,
in the sense that a pair can be globally rigid in a generic realization without being so in
all
generic realizations.
This warrants the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.
A pair of vertices
u,v\in G
is
weakly globally linked
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

if there exists a generic
d
-realization
p
of
G
such that
u
and
v
are globally linked in
(G,p)
.
If
u
and
v
are not weakly globally linked, they are called
globally loose
.

Despite the previous remark,
the following theorem shows that global rigidity is equivalent to every pair of vertices being weakly globally linked.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
4
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.
Then
G
is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if
every pair of vertices of
G
is weakly globally linked in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Weak global linkedness of pairs is a combinatorial property
and is hence invariant under automorphisms of the graph,
as we make precise in the following lemma.
The proof is immediate and is hence omitted.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.
Let

W_{d}(G)\coloneq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{u,v\}\,\,s.t.\,\,u,v\in V\text{ and }u,v\text{ are }\\
\text{weakly globally linked in $G$ in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$}\end{array}\right\}.

For any
e\in E(K_{V})
denote by
O_{G}(e)
the orbit of
e
under the automorphisms group of
G
.
Then for any
e\in E(K_{V})
we have that

e\in W_{d}(G)\iff O_{G}(e)\subseteq W_{d}(G).

The following result is contained in the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
6
]
)
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
and let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.
Assume that
G
is vertex-
(d+1)
-connected and that all weakly globally linked pairs in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are adjacent in
G
.
Then for every vertex
v\in V
,
if
V_{1},V_{2}\subseteq N_{G}(v)
are distinct maximal cliques in
G\bigl[N_{G}(v)\bigr]
,
we have that
|V_{1}\cap V_{2}|<d-1
.

2.2.
Ordering-induced subgraphs

Ordering induced subgraphs were introduced in
[
6
]
and
they play a central role in the proof of the main theorems of that paper.
Here, we briefly recall the construction and state the main results from
[
6
]
that we need.
The main result asserts that if in graph all weakly globally linked pairs are adjacent,
then all ordering-induced subgraphs are independent in the rigidity matroid (
Lemma

2.8
).

Definition 2.6
.

Let
V
be a finite set of cardinality
n
.
An
ordering
of
V
is an ordered tuple
\pi=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
such that
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
.
To every ordering
\pi
, we associate an order relation on
V
, denoted
<_{\pi}
.
For every
u,w\in V
, the order relation
<_{\pi}
is defined by

v<_{\pi}w\iff\exists i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\,\,s.t.\,\,v=v_{i},w=v_{j}\text{ and }i<j.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
\pi=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
be an ordering of
V
.
For any
v\in V
, we define the
ordered parents
of
v
in
G
as

N_{G,\pi}(v)\coloneq N_{G}(v)\cap\{w\in V\,\,s.t.\,\,w<_{\pi}v\}.

Furthermore, we define the
ordered degree
of
v
as

\deg_{G,\pi}(v)\coloneq\bigl|N_{G,\pi}(v)\bigr|.

Definition 2.7
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
\pi
be an ordering of
V
.
We define a family of subgraphs of
G
induced by
\pi
.
We call any of these subgraphs a
\pi
-subgraph
of
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
n\coloneq|V|
. A
\pi
-subgraph of
G
is a graph of the form
G_{\pi}^{n}
,
where
G_{\pi}^{n}
is iteratively constructed as follows.
Set
G_{\pi}^{0}\coloneq(\emptyset,\emptyset)
.
For any
i\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
,
to obtain
G_{\pi}^{i+1}
from
G_{\pi}^{i}
, let
H^{i}\coloneq G_{\pi}^{i}+v_{i}
and consider
N_{G,\pi}(v_{i})
and
\deg_{G,\pi}(v_{i})
.
Then:

(1)

if
\deg_{G,\pi}(v_{i})\leq d
, the graph
G_{\pi}^{i+1}
is obtained from
H^{i}
by connecting
v_{i}
to all vertices in
N_{G,\pi}(v_{i})
;

(2)

if
\deg_{G,\pi}(v_{i})>d
and
N_{G,\pi}(v_{i})
induces a clique in
G
,
the graph
G_{\pi}^{i+1}
is obtained from
H^{i}
by connecting
v_{i}
to
d
vertices chosen from
N_{G,\pi}(v_{i})
;

(3)

if
\deg_{G,\pi}(v_{i})>d
and
N_{G,\pi}(v_{i})
does not induces a clique in
G
,
the graph
G_{\pi}^{i+1}
is obtained from
H^{i}
by connecting
v_{i}
to
d+1
distinct vertices chosen in
N_{G,\pi}
,
subject to the constraint that at least two of those
d+1
vertices are not adjacent in
G
.

A graph that is isomorphic to a
\pi
-subgraph of
G
, for some ordering
\pi
of
V
, is called
ordering-induced
subgraph of
G
.

The following two results are at the heart of the proofs of the main theorems of
[
6
]
. In the same way, they will ultimately allow us to reach a contradiction in the proof of
Theorem

3.4
.
The statement of
Lemma

2.9
is slightly different and it is obtained by applying Maxwell’s necessary condition to
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
, let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
\mathcal{R}_{d}
be the rigidity matroid on
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Assume that all weakly globally linked pairs in
G
are also adjacent in
G
,
then any ordering-induced subgraph of
G
is
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-independent.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph such that, for every

, we have:

(1)

\deg_{G}(v)\geq d(d+1)
;

(2)

N_{G}(v)
is not a clique;

(3)

if
H_{1},H_{2}\subseteq N_{G}(v)
are vertex sets of two maximal cliques in

3.
Vertex-transitive graphs

The paper
[
3
]
provides a characterization of vertex-transitive globally rigid graphs in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
and in particular it shows that every vertex-transitive graph of degree at least
6
is globally rigid in the plane.
In this section, we generalize this result and show that a vertex-transitive graph of regularity at least
d(d+1)
is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
Theorem

3.4
).
Moreover, we construct a connected vertex-transitive graph of degree
d(d+1)-1
that is not globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
Corollary

3.11
),
showing that
d(d+1)
is the best constant. Together, these facts imply
Theorem

1.1
.
The papers
[
3
]
and
[
1
]
go further in the analysis in the plane
by characterizing when a vertex-transitive graph of degree less than
6
is globally rigid.
We leave this question to future work.

We start by recalling the definition of this family of graphs.

Definition 3.1
.

A graph
G=(V,E)
is
vertex-transitive
if for every pair of distinct vertices
v,w\in V
there exists an automorphism
\phi
of
G
such that
\phi(v)=w
.

One key result about vertex-transitive graphs is that, if they are connected, then they are highly vertex-connected.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
7
, Theorem 3]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected vertex-transitive graph of degree
\omega
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
be the maximal number such that
G
is vertex-
k
-connected, then

k\geq\frac{2}{3}\omega.

In
[
6
]
, Villányi proved that a vertex-
d(d+1)
-connected graph is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, thus verifying Dewar’s conjecture.

Corollary 3.3
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 1
.
If
G
is a connected vertex-transitive graph of regularity at least
\frac{3}{2}d(d+1)
then
G
is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The main result of this paper improves on this constant.

Theorem 3.4
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and let
G
be a connected vertex-transitive graph of degree at least
d(d+1)
. Then
G
is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The following is an easy observation that is nevertheless essential in the proof of the theorem.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and assume that for every
v\in V
the set
N_{G}(v)
induces a clique in
G
.
Then every connected component of
G
is a complete graph.

Proof of
Theorem

3.4
.

Let
V
be a finite set.
Suppose for a contradiction that there exists a vertex-transitive graphs on
V
of degree at least
d(d+1)
that is not globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Since there are finitely many graphs on
V
, we can take one such graph that has the maximal number of edges. We denote it
G=(V,E)
.
Notice that our assumption on the degree of
G
implies that
Item

1
of
Lemma

2.9
holds for
G
.

First, we show that all weakly globally linked pairs of vertices in
G
are adjacent.
Indeed, if for a contradiction
u,v\in V
are weakly globally linked but not adjacent,
then every edge in the orbit of
\{u,v\}
under the automorphisms group of
G
, that we denote by
O_{G}(e)
, is weakly globally linked (
Lemma

2.4
).

Since
G
is not globally rigid,
by
Theorem

2.3
neither
G+O_{G}(e)
is globally rigid.
However, the graph
G+O_{G}(e)
is again a vertex-transitive graph on
V
of degree at least
d(d+1)
,
but with more edges than
G
.
This contradicts the maximality assumption on the cardinality of
E
, and proves that all weakly globally linked pairs of vertices in
G
are adjacent. By
Lemma

2.8
, we conclude that all ordering induced subgraphs of
G
are independent in the rigidity matroid.

Secondly, for every
v\in V
, the set
N_{G}(v)
does not induce a clique.
Indeed, since
G
is vertex-transitive, if one neighborhood induces a clique, all neighborhoods induce a clique and
G
is a complete graph (
Lemma

3.5
).
Since complete graphs are globally rigid, this would yield a contradiction.
We have proved that
Item

2
of
Lemma

2.9
holds for
G
.
Finally, since all weakly globally linked pairs are adjacent in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we have that all ordering-induced subgraphs of
G
are independent in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
Lemma

2.8
).
The fact that all weakly globally linked pairs are adjacent also implies, by
Lemma

2.5
,
that
Item

3
of
Lemma

2.9
holds for
G
.
Notice that we can apply
Lemma

2.5
because the vertex connectivity of
G
is greater than

\frac{2}{3}d(d+1)\geq d+1,

assuming that
d>1
(
Theorem

3.2
).

On the one hand, we have proved that all ordering-induced subgraphs of
G
are independent in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. On the other hand, we have verified that the conditions of
Lemma

2.9
are satisfied by
G
, which implies in particular that at least one ordering-induced subgraph of
G
is dependent in the rigidity matroid, a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The analogous result of
Theorem

for Cayley graphs can also be proved independently, by considering the orbit of an edge under the action of the corresponding group and proving that adding such an orbit yields yet another Cayley graph.

Remark 3.7

The outline contained in the proof of

3.4
is essentially identical to the outline of the proof of the main theorem of

]
. What we have done is simply observe that the main steps also work when dealing with vertex-transitive graphs.

Remark 3.8
.

The lexicographic product of two graphs is a new graph whose vertex set is the Cartesian product of the vertex sets of the starting graphs. If
G
and
H
are graphs, a pair of vertices
(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in V(G)\times V(H)
is adjacent in the lexicographic product of

and
H
if either
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G)
or
g=g^{\prime}
and
\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(H)
.
By considering the lexicographic product between circuits and complete graphs, it is possible to find a family of vertex-transitive graphs whose global rigidity is implied by
Theorem

3.4
but not by the results in
[
6
]
.

Notice that

This means that

Moreover, the edge sets

\displaystyle r\bigl(E\setminus\{e\}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq ks\left(d+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d(d+1)}{2k}-\frac{1}{ks}\right)

However, since

\displaystyle dks-\binom{d+1}{2}-ks\left(d+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d(d+1)}{2k}-\frac{1}{ks}\right)\geq

\displaystyle\geq ks\left(d-d-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d(d+1)}{2k}+\frac{1}{ks}\right)-\frac{d+1}{2}

\displaystyle\geq ks\left(\frac{d(d+1)-k}{2k}+\frac{1}{ks}\right)-\binom{d+1}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}s-\binom{d+1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}>0,

as soon as
s\geq d(d+1)
, we conclude that the upper bound of
Section

3
is strictly smaller than the required rank for rigidity.

We have shown that
G-e
is not rigid, which implies that
G
is not redundantly rigid. By Hendrickson’s necessary conditions for global rigidity, we conclude that
G
is not globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

For all
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
, there exists a connected
vertex-transitive graph of degree

that is not globally rigid
in

.

Proof.

Fix

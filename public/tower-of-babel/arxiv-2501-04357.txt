Generators of top cohomology
Manoj Kummini, Mohit Upmanyu

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative noetherian ring and f : X ⟶ Spec ⁡ R f:X\longrightarrow\Spec R a proper smooth morphism, of relative dimension n n . From Hartshorne, Residues and Duality , Springer, 1966, one knows that the trace map Tr f : H n ⁡ ( X , ω X / R ) ⟶ R \Tr_{f}:\homology^{n}(X,\omega_{X/R})\longrightarrow R is an isomorphism when f f has geometrically connected fibres. We construct an exact sequence that generates Ext X n ⁡ ( 𝒪 X , ω X / R ) = H n ⁡ ( X , ω X / R ) \Ext_{X}^{n}(\mathscr{O}_{X},\omega_{X/R})=\homology^{n}(X,\omega_{X/R}) as an R R -module in the following cases: (1) when R R is a DVR and f f has a section; (2) when R = ℤ R=\mathbb{Z} and X X is the Grassmannian G 2 , m G_{2,m} for some m ≥ 4 m\geq 4 . This partially answers a question raised by Lipman.



1.
Introduction

J. Lipman posed the following question (in private communication to the
first author).
Let

be a (commutative) noetherian ring and

a proper

Write

By the duality theorem for proper

[
Har66
, Chapter VII, Theorem 3.3]

we get an isomorphism

\mathbf{R}\!f_{*}\mathbf{R}\!\mathcal{H}om_{X}(\omega_{X/R},\omega_{X/R}[n])=\mathbf{R}\!\Hom_{R}(\mathbf{R}\!f_{*}\omega_{X/R},R).

Since

is smooth, we also have a trace

[
Har66
, Chapter VII, Theorem 4.1]

\Tr_{f}:\homology^{n}(X,\omega_{X/R})\longrightarrow R

which is an isomorphism when
f
has geometrically connected
fibres
[
Har66
, Chapter III, Theorem 11.2(g)]
.

Question 1.1

(Lipman)
.

Can

2.
Strategy

Our starting point for finding a generator for
\Ext
is the
the following lemma.
It might be well-known, but we include a proof at the end of this section
for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a Gorenstein local ring and
\mathbf{a}:=a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
a regular
sequence in
A
. Then the augmented Koszul complex

0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow A^{m}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow A^{m}\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow A/\mathbf{a}A\longrightarrow 0

is a generator of the
A/\mathbf{a}A
-module
\Ext_{A}^{m}(A/\mathbf{a}A,A)\simeq A/\mathbf{a}A
.

To use the above lemma in our context, we look for a complete intersection
inside
X
that is of relative dimension zero over
R
. For the strategy to
work, this is not enough; we also need that
X\longrightarrow\Spec R
has a section.

Notation 2.2
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay ring with a dualizing module
\omega_{R}=R

and
X
a smooth projective
R
-scheme, of relative
dimension
n
. Assume that

(1)

the map
X\longrightarrow\Spec R
has a section
Z
;

(2)

There exists a rank-
n
locally free sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
and
\sigma\in\homology^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
such that the zero section
X_{n}
of

\sigma
is flat and of relative dimension
0
over
R
;

(3)

Z
is a connected component of
X_{n}
.

Note that if
R
is not local, the hypothesis on
R
above
might be stronger than requiring
R
to be Gorenstein, i.e.,
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
a Gorenstein local ring for all
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Spec R
.

Notation 2.3
.

We consider only relative dualizing sheaves in this paper. Therefore we
abbreviate
\Omega_{X/R}
as
\Omega_{X}

and
\omega_{X/R}
as
\omega_{X}
.

Given such an
\mathcal{F}
and
\sigma
, we have the Koszul complex

0\longrightarrow\det\mathcal{F}^{*}\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{n-1}\mathcal{F}^{*}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{2}\mathcal{F}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{O}_{X}\longrightarrow 0,

which is a locally free resolution of
\mathscr{O}_{X_{n}}
,

X_{n}
has codimension

X
.
Apply
\mathcal{H}om_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}(-,\omega_{X})

\omega_{X_{n}}=\mathcal{E}xt^{n}_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}(\mathscr{O}_{X_{n}},\omega_{X})

to obtain an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\omega_{X}\longrightarrow\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\omega_{X}\otimes\det\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\omega_{X_{n}}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
X_{n}
is local complete intersection inside
X
and
\omega_{X}
is
a locally free
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module of rank

\omega_{X_{n}}
is a locally free
\mathscr{O}_{X_{n}}
-module of rank
1

\widetilde{K}_{\bullet}

It is labelled in such a way that
\widetilde{K}_{n}=\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{F}

and

\widetilde{K}_{1}=\omega_{X}\otimes\det\mathcal{F}

the ideal of the projective subscheme of
\projective_{R}^{N}
defined by

J
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Note that
Z
is defined by
n
linearly independent linear forms. After a
suitable change of coordinates, we may assume that
Z
is defined by

{\mathfrak{p}}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.

(
1
):
We prove this by induction on

. Let

.
Note that

is a prime ideal of height

.
Hence
{\mathfrak{p}}\nsubseteq\left({\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\cup I_{X}\right)

and the assertion holds for

.

Now assume that
1<k\leq n
and that we have found
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k-1}
with
the desired properties.
Since
X
is non-singular,

\bar{S}/I_{X}

and, therefore,

\bar{S}/(I_{X}+(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k-1}))^{\mathrm{sat}}

are Cohen-Macaulay except possibly at the
irrelevant ideal.
Since
\height(I_{X}+(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k-1}))=N-n+k-1<N
,
we can find an

f_{k}\in{\mathfrak{p}}\smallsetminus{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}
that is a non-zero-divisor in

\bar{S}/(I_{X}+(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k-1}))^{\mathrm{sat}}
.

(
2
):
Write
I_{Z}
for the ideal sheaf of
Z
in
X
.
By Lemma
2.10
,
\homology^{0}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})=R
, and
arguing as in the proof of Lemma

,

\homology^{0}(X,I_{Z})=0
.
Hence, by Serre duality
[

, Theorem III.7.6]
,
(
2.9
) holds; therefore by
(
2.8
), we get the first assertion.
Since

is
R
-flat, we get the second assertion by flat base-change.
∎

(The statement of Serre duality in
[

assumes

used only to see that local rings on projective spaces (over a field) are

5.
Over a DVR

In this section, we show that the strategy described in the previous
section can be carried out over a DVR. We assume that

has a section (with

a DVR) has a section

,
and show that there exists a complete intersection

in

is a connected component.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},\Bbbk)
be a DVR.
Let

be a projective

-subscheme of

\projective^{N}_{R}
. Assume that the structure morphism

is
smooth and of relative dimension
n
,
and that it has a section
\Spec R\longrightarrow Z\subseteq X
.
Write

for the homogeneous coordinate ring of

.
Then:

(1)

There exist homogeneous polynomials
\tilde{f}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{f}_{n}\in S

such that they define a complete intersection

inside

.

(2)

The natural map

(3)

is a direct summand

(4)

The top row of (

Moreover, the base-change of this exact sequence generates

Various parts of the above theorem will be proved as separate lemmas /
propositions, in more generality than stated in the theorem:
(

)
and (
2
) as

; (

)
as Proposition

; finally

We start with an observation.

Proposition 5.2
.

Without loss of generality,
Z
is defined by the ideal

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, the homogeneous coordinate
x_{0}
does not
vanish at the closed point of

. Hence it does not vanish at the generic

lies inside the open

. Write

Write

Therefore

is defined inside
\projective^{N}_{R}
by

.
∎

Write

for the closed point
\Spec\Bbbk\in\Spec R
.
For subschemes

of

for its

.

Proposition 5.3
.

Write
\bar{S}:=\Bbbk[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}]

and
{\mathfrak{p}}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\bar{S}
.
There exist homogeneous

,

is a non-zero-divisor in

\bar{S}/(I_{X_{s}}+(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k-1}))^{\mathrm{sat}}

Proof.

See the proof of Theorem

(

).
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Assume that
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},\Bbbk)
is a local Cohen-Macaulay ring.
For

, write

f_{k}=x_{0}^{\deg f_{k}-1}l_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})+\text{terms of lower degree
in $x_{0}$},

Let
\tilde{l}_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})

to

Consider any lift
\tilde{f}_{k}

Let

(Here

Proof.

(

It is immediate from the definition that

since the reduction of the ideal

.

(

):

.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose that

is a regular local ring.
Then

.

Proof.

X_{n}
is finite and flat over

at

∎

By now, we have proved (

),

.

Conclusion of the proof of

.

The first assertion of

.

To complete the proof of Theorem

First note that

6.
Grassmannian

Let
m\geq 4
and

X
the Grassmannian
G_{2,m}
over
\mathbb{Z}
, i.e., the scheme whose

R
-points (for a ring
R
) classify the locally free (over
R
) quotient
modules of rank
m-2
of
R^{\oplus m}
. It is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}
-scheme of
relative dimension
n:=2(m-2)
.
We show that the morphism
X\longrightarrow\Spec\mathbb{Z}
has a section
Z
and that
there exists a complete intersection
X_{n}
inside
X
of relative dimension

0
over
\mathbb{Z}
satisfying the conditions of Notation
2.2
.
Since
\mathbb{Z}
is a PID, the strategy of Section
2
can
be carried out to give a generator of

\Ext_{X}^{n}(\mathscr{O}_{X},\omega_{X})
as a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module.

Let
N=\binom{m}{2}-1
.
Then
X
can be embedded inside
\projective:=\projective_{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
as a closed
subscheme, defined by the Pluecker relations;
see
[
EH00
, Chapter III, §2.7]
.
Let
p_{i,j},1\leq i<j\leq m
be homogeneous coordinates for

\projective
.
Let
S=\mathbb{Z}[p_{i,j},1\leq i<j\leq m]
.
The main result of this section is:

Theorem 6.1
.

For
3\leq k\leq 2m-1
, write
l_{k}=\sum_{i+j=k}p_{i,j}
.
Write
V\subseteq\projective
for the linear subvariety defined by
l_{3},l_{4},\ldots,\widehat{l_{m+1}},\ldots,l_{2m-1}
.
Then
X\cap V
has zero-dimensional fibres and there is a section

\Spec\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow X\cap V
which defines an irreducible and
reduced component of
X\cap V
.

To prove that
X\cap V
has zero-dimensional fibres, we may do a
base-change along
\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\Bbbk
where
\Bbbk
is an algebraically
closed field, and show that
X_{\Bbbk}\cap V_{\Bbbk}

(the respective base-changes)
is a zero-dimensional scheme. We do this now.
We use
[
Ses07
, Chapter 1]
as the reference on Schubert
varieties and standard monomial theory.
We denote the Schubert varieties in

X_{\Bbbk}
by
Y_{i,j}

1\leq i<j\leq n
.
(This is the Schubert variety in
X_{\Bbbk}
corresponding to a permutation

\sigma\in S_{n}
with
\{\sigma(1),\sigma(2)\}=\{1,2\}
.)

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field.
Then
X_{\Bbbk}\cap V_{\Bbbk}
is a zero-dimensional scheme.

Proof.

We think of
X_{\Bbbk}
as the quotient of the space of rank-two
m\times 2

matrices under the natural action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\Bbbk)
on the right.
The homogeneous coordinate
p_{i,j}
of
\projective_{\Bbbk}
becomes, on

X_{\Bbbk}
, the determinant of the
2\times 2
minor consisting of the
rows
i
and
j
and both the columns.

Note that
V_{\Bbbk}
is defined inside
\projective_{\Bbbk}
by (the images in

S\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Bbbk
) of the
l_{k}
.
For each
m+2\leq k\leq 2m-1
, let
W_{k}
be the linear
subvariety defined by
l_{k},\ldots,l_{2m-1}
.
We claim that

X_{\Bbbk}\cap W_{k}
is set-theoretically the union of all the
Schubert subvarieties of
X_{\Bbbk}
of dimension
k-4
.
Note that
k-4=\dim X_{\Bbbk}-\codim_{\projective_{\Bbbk}}W_{k}
.
Therefore the irreducible components of
X_{\Bbbk}\cap W_{m+2}
are
precisely the Schubert varieties
Y_{k,m+1-k}
for all
1\leq k<\frac{m}{2}
.

For each
3\leq k\leq m
, let
W^{\prime}_{k}
be the
linear subvariety defined by
l_{3},\ldots,l_{k}
.
By symmetry, and assuming the above claim,

X_{\Bbbk}\cap W^{\prime}_{k}
is set-theoretically the union of all the
opposite Schubert subvarieties of
X_{\Bbbk}
of codimension
k-2
.
In particular, the irreducible components of
X_{\Bbbk}\cap W^{\prime}_{m}
are
precisely the opposite Schubert varieties
Y^{k,m+1-k}
for all
1\leq k<\frac{m}{2}
.
Hence the irreducible components of

X_{\Bbbk}\cap V_{\Bbbk}=(X_{\Bbbk}\cap W_{m+2})\cap(X_{\Bbbk}\cap W^{\prime}_{m})

are the Richardson varieties
Y^{k,m+1-k}_{k,m+1-k}
for all
1\leq k<\frac{m}{2}
, all of which are zero-dimensional. (See,
e.g.,
[
Bri05
, § 1.3]
.)

To prove the claim, we proceed by downward induction on
k
. When
k=2m-1
,

X_{\Bbbk}\cap W_{k}
is the Schubert divisor
Y_{m-2,m}
, which is of
dimension
2m-5
. Assume that the claim has been proved up to
k+1
.
Let
Y
be a Schubert variety of dimension
k-3
.
Hence
Y
is an irreducible component of
X_{\Bbbk}\cap W_{k+1}
.
Say
Y=Y_{i,k-i}
. Then, in the terms of
l_{k}
, all but
l_{i,k-i}

vanish along
Y
, since the ideal of
Y_{i,k-i}
in
X_{\Bbbk}
is generated
by
\{p_{j_{1},j_{2}}\mid(j_{1},j_{2})\not\leq(i,k-i)\}
. Moreover,

Y_{i,k-i}\cap\{p_{i,k-i}=0\}
is the union of the codimension-
1

Schubert subvarieties of
Y_{i,k-i}
. Further, every
(k-4)
-dimensional
Schubert variety is a subvariety of a
(k-3)
-dimensional Schubert variety.
Hence

X_{\Bbbk}\cap W_{k}=X_{\Bbbk}\cap W_{k+1}\cap\{l_{k}=0\}

is set-theoretically the union of all the

(k-4)
-dimensional Schubert varieties.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

As we remarked above,
Proposition
6.2

implies that
X\cap V
has zero-dimensional fibres.
We now prove the existence of a section of the structure morphism
X\cap V\longrightarrow\Spec\mathbb{Z}
with the desired properties.
Let
\affine\subseteq\projective
be the affine space obtained by
inverting
p_{1,n}
. More precisely,
write
q_{i,j}=\frac{p_{i,j}}{p_{1,n}}
,

R=\mathbb{Z}[q_{i,j}:1\leq i<j\leq n,(i,j)\neq(1,n)]

and
{\mathfrak{m}}
for the prime ideal of
R
generated by the
q_{i,j}
.
Then
\affine=\Spec R
.
Note that
X
is defined by the
the Pluecker relations, which are of the form

p_{i,j}p_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\pm p_{i,i^{\prime}}p_{j^{\prime},j}\pm p_{i,j^{\prime}}p_{i^{\prime},j}\;\text{where}\;i<i^{\prime},j>j^{\prime}.

See, e.g.,
[
EH00
, Chapter III, §2.7]
.
(For the sake of convenience, we do not specify
which terms on the right appear with positive sign and which with negative
sign.)
A Pluecker relation of the form

p_{1,n}p_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\pm p_{1,k}p_{j^{\prime},n}\pm p_{1,j^{\prime}}p_{k,n},\;\text{with}\;1<i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}<n

gives a generator

q_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\pm q_{1,k}q_{j^{\prime},n}\pm q_{1,j^{\prime}}q_{k,n}

of the ideal of
X\cap\affine
inside
\affine
.
The other generators (i.e., with
i<i^{\prime},j>j^{\prime},(i,j)\neq(1,n)
) give
the polynomials

q_{i,j}q_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\pm q_{i,k}q_{j^{\prime},j}\pm q_{i,j^{\prime}}q_{k,j}

inside the ideal of
X\cap\affine
.
For
3\leq k\leq 2m-1,k\neq n+1
, the polynomial
l_{k}
gives the
polynomial
q_{1,k-1}+q_{2,k-2}+\cdots\in R
.

We need to show that
{\mathfrak{m}}
is a minimal prime over
the
R
-ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
generated by

(1)

q_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\pm q_{1,i^{\prime}}q_{j^{\prime},n}\pm q_{1,j^{\prime}}q_{i^{\prime},n}
,

1<i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}<n
;

(2)

q_{i,j}q_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\pm q_{i,i^{\prime}}q_{j^{\prime},j}\pm q_{i,j^{\prime}}q_{i^{\prime},j}
,

i<i^{\prime},j>j^{\prime},(i,j)\neq(1,n)
;

(3)

(a)

q_{1,k-1}+q_{2,k-2}+\cdots
,

3\leq k\leq n
;

(b)

\cdots+q_{k-n+1,n-1}+q_{k-n,n}
,
n+2\leq k\leq 2m-1

and that
{\mathfrak{a}}R_{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{m}}R_{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Note that
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Therefore if we show that
\Omega_{(S/{\mathfrak{a}})/\mathbb{Z}}
vanishes at
{\mathfrak{m}}
,
it would follow that
S/{\mathfrak{a}}
is unramified over
\mathbb{Z}
at
{\mathfrak{m}}
,
whence it would follow that
{\mathfrak{a}}R_{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{m}}R_{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Note that
\Omega_{(S/{\mathfrak{a}})/\mathbb{Z}}
is the cokernel of the jacobian matrix
of the aforementioned generating set of
{\mathfrak{a}}
with respect to the
variables
q_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
.
Further, for each
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
, there exists a unique generator in
the above list in which
q_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
appears with coefficient
1
, so the
jacobian matrix has full rank in the residue field
\kappa({\mathfrak{m}})
at

{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Therefore
\Omega_{(S/{\mathfrak{a}})/\mathbb{Z}}
vanishes at
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Theorem
6.1

to complete the

.

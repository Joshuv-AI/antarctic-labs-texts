Super Covering Maps
Beat Nairz

Abstract
We define analytic maps between super Riemann surfaces which extend the notion of branched covering maps to a supersymmetric setting. We show that these super covering maps appear naturally both in symmetric product orbifolds of superconformal field theories, as well as in the hybrid formalism for tensionless string theory on AdS 3 × S 3 × 𝕋 4 \text{AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4} . In the former, they can be used to calculate correlators in a manifestly supersymmetric way, while in the latter they solve Ward identities of correlators with space-time supersymmetry.

Covering maps in the

1.2
Overview

Does a similar geometric identification hold in the supersymmetric setting? To answer this question, we need to formulate the theories in a manifestly supersymmetric way. We review the relevant notions for two-dimensional CFTs, such as super Riemann surfaces and superconformal structures, in Section
2
. Super covering maps will then be functions of an even variable
z
, as well as an odd variable
\Theta
. We define these maps and show some examples in Section
3
. We can see that these are the correct objects both for the symmetric orbifold, as well as for string theory. We study the CFT side in Section
4
, where we look at the symmetric product orbifold of a superconformal field theory, defined on superspace. The super covering map directly computes correlators in this theory in a manifestly supersymmetric way. We discuss the super Weyl anomaly and the lifting of fractional modes. In Section
5
, we look at the dual string theory in the hybrid formalism. We define the vertex operators dual to the super vertex operators in the CFT. We show that the Ward identities satisfied by the string theory correlators are naturally solved by the super covering map.
3
3

3

The caveat of this analysis is that we do not have manifest worldsheet supersymmetry, and thus not the full super covering map can be seen. Concretely, the map solving the Ward identities is the super covering map with the odd worldsheet coordinate set to zero. This is explained in Section
5.2.1
.

2
Super Riemann surfaces

We begin by reviewing the notion of a super Riemann surface (SRS) and a superconformal field theory (SCFT) defined thereon. A SRS is a supermanifold with an additional structure linking the even and odd variables
Friedan:1986rx
;
DHoker:1988pdl
;
Witten:2012ga
;
Witten:2019ylx
;
Varadarajan:2004yz
. On such a surface, there is an analogue of the usual complex analysis, allowing for differentiation, integration, and Taylor expansion. The superconformal structure is the natural geometric foundation for a SCFT, where a field and its superpartner are combined into a single superfield.

2.1
Definition

The natural geometric objects in the study of
\mathcal{N}=1
SUSY are super Riemann surfaces (SRS). These are complex supermanifolds
Varadarajan:2004yz
of complex dimension
1|1

\Sigma^{\text{red}}
is a Riemann surface. A SRS is endowed with an additional superconformal structure
Witten:2012ga
. This structure can be abstractly specified as follows. For a SRS
\Sigma
, a
superconformal structure
is a
0|1
-dimensional, maximally non-integrable subbundle
\mathcal{D}
of the tangent bundle
T\Sigma
.
4
4

4

This is similar to the definition of a complex structure for normal manifolds. There, the tangent bundle of a two-dimensional real manifold is split into a holomorphic and an antiholomorphic subbundle
moroianu_2007
.
Here, “maximally non-integrable” means that
[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}]

5

The bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the graded Lie bracket on the tangent bundle.
is everywhere linearly independent of
\mathcal{D}
.

Concretely, this means that around each point there exist local coordinates
(x,\theta)
, such that
\mathcal{D}
is spanned by the
superderivative

D=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\theta\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\ ,

(2.1)

which satisfies
D^{2}=\partial/\partial_{x}

Witten:2012ga
. Coordinates with this property are called

, and a SRS is defined by an atlas of superconformal coordinate systems. Different coordinates

are superconformal if and only if the transition functions satisfy the following important property

\widetilde{\theta}\,D\widetilde{\theta}=D\widetilde{x}\ ,

(2.2)

where

The most general form of a superconformal transformation is

\tilde{x}=f+\theta\,\rho\,\sqrt{\partial f}\ ,\qquad\tilde{\theta}=\rho+\theta\,\sqrt{\partial f+\rho\,\partial\rho}\ ,

(2.3)

There is a unique SRS

(2.5)

Integration and Taylor expansion

There is a natural analogue of complex analysis on SRSs

\displaystyle=x_{1}-x_{2}-\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\ ,

\displaystyle\theta_{12}

\displaystyle=\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\ .

The superanalytic functions on SRSs we consider are

The component-wise expansion of

(2.15)

where

Furthermore, we can define contour integration

d\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=D\widetilde{\theta}\,d\mathbf{x}\ .

2.3
Superconformal field theory

The superconformal structure of a SRS allows for the geometric definition of a superconformal field theory (SCFT)
Friedan:1986rx
.

The basic objects of a SCFT are super primaries. A chiral super primary of dimension
h
is a field
\Phi(\mathbf{x})=\varphi(x)+\theta\,\psi(x)
, which transforms as

(D\widetilde{\theta})^{2h}\,\widetilde{\Phi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})=\Phi(\mathbf{x})

(2.20)

under superconformal transformations, meaning that
\Phi(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}^{2h}
is coordinate invariant. Both components of
\Phi
are usual primaries; specialising to a superconformal coordinate transformation of the form
\widetilde{x}=g(x)
,
\widetilde{\theta}=\theta\,\sqrt{\partial g}

\varphi
is a conformal primary of dimension
h
, while
\psi
has dimension
h+1/2
.

Suppose we are given a CFT with
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry, energy momentum tensor
L
, and supercharge
G
. In the NS sector, for a state
\ket{\phi}
of dimension
h
, there is an associated super vertex operator

\Phi(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{V}(\ket{\phi},\mathbf{x}):=V(\ket{\phi},x)+\theta\,V(G_{-1/2}\ket{\phi},x)\ ,

(2.21)

where
V(\ket{\phi},x)
is the usual vertex operator. If the state
\ket{\phi}
is annihilated by all the positive modes of
L
and
G
, this super vertex operator is a super primary; the components
\varphi,\psi
of

are thus superpartners, combined into a single field.

The global modes

form an
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)

are the generators of supertranslations and superscalings in eqs. (

\displaystyle e^{\lambda\,G_{-1/2}}\,\mathbf{V}(\ket{\phi},x,\theta)\,e^{-\lambda\,G_{-1/2}}

\displaystyle=(1)^{2h}\,\mathbf{V}(\ket{\phi},x-\lambda\,\theta,\theta+\lambda)\ ,

\displaystyle e^{\kappa\,G_{1/2}}\,\mathbf{V}(\ket{\phi},x,\theta)\,e^{-\kappa\,G_{1/2}}

\displaystyle=(1-\kappa\,\theta)^{2h}\,\mathbf{V}(\ket{\phi},x-\kappa\,\theta\,x,\theta+\kappa\,x)\ ,

as one can see by applying both sides to the vacuum

The OPE of two super vertex operators has a nice form in superconformal coordinates. If we write

3
Super covering maps

We now describe super covering maps, which are the main objects of this paper. They are maps between two super Riemann surfaces satisfying superconformality and regularity properties.

We will define these maps and give some simple examples.

3.1
Definition

Consider two SRS
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
, with local coordinates
\mathbf{z}=\big({z,\Theta}\big)
and
\mathbf{x}=\big({x,\theta}\big)
, respectively. We want to describe specific maps

\bm{\Gamma}:\,\Sigma_{1}\longrightarrow\Sigma_{2};\,\,\mathbf{z}\longmapsto\big({x,\theta}\big)=\big({\Gamma_{1}(\mathbf{z}),\Gamma_{2}(\mathbf{z})}\big)\ ,

(3.1)

which behave well in the context of a SCFT.

First, such a map should respect the superconformal structure
\mathcal{D}_{i}\subseteq T\Sigma_{i}
, i.e.

\mathrm{d}\bm{\Gamma}\big({\mathcal{D}_{1}}\big)\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2}\ ,

(3.2)

where
\mathrm{d}\bm{\Gamma}
denotes the differential of
\bm{\Gamma}
.
In local coordinates, this can be written as

D\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}\,D\Gamma_{2}\ ,\qquad D=\frac{\partial}{\partial\Theta}+\Theta\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\ .

(3.3)

We discussed the ramification structure of ordinary covering maps in Section
1.1
. Super covering maps should have a similar structure of ramification- and branch points. The correct behaviour can be motivated as follows. Each superconformal
\bm{\Gamma}
gives rise to an ordinary branched covering between the reduced manifolds,

\Gamma^{\text{red}}:\,\,\Sigma_{1}^{\text{red}}\longrightarrow\Sigma_{2}^{\text{red}}\ .

(3.4)

If there are no odd moduli, we should recover the non-supersymmetric case
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma^{\text{red}}
. If
z_{i}
is a ramification point of
\Gamma^{\text{red}}
, the behaviour of
\Gamma_{1}
near
\mathbf{z}_{i}=(z_{i},0)
is

\Gamma_{1}\big({z,\Theta}\big)-x_{i}=a_{i}\,\big({z-z_{i}}\big)^{w}+\dots\ .

(3.5)

The branch point is
\mathbf{x}_{i}=(x_{i},0)
. The local behaviour for arbitrary ramification points
\mathbf{z}_{i}=\big({z_{i},\Theta_{i}}\big)
and branch points
\mathbf{x}_{i}=\big({x_{i},\theta_{i}}\big)
can be found by supertranslating

(z,\Theta)\mapsto(z-\Theta\,\Theta_{i},\Theta+\Theta_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad(x,\theta)\mapsto(x-\theta\,\theta_{i},\theta+\theta_{i})\ .

(3.6)

The above regularity condition is then transformed to

\Gamma_{1}(\mathbf{z})-x_{i}-\Gamma_{2}(\mathbf{z})\,\theta_{i}=a_{i}\,(z-z_{i}-\Theta\,\Theta_{i})^{w_{i}}+\mathcal{O}\big((z-z_{i}-\Theta\,\Theta_{i})^{w_{i}+1}\big)\ ,\quad\mathbf{z}\to\mathbf{z}_{i}\ .

(3.7)

We should note that this is not the only way one could extend the reduced regularity in eq. (
3.5
), as one can add nilpotent terms with lower power (see the example in eq. (
A.11
)). However, we want the series to start only at
(z-z_{i}-\Theta\,\Theta_{i})^{w_{i}}
, as the map should lift
w_{i}
twisted fields; we will discuss this further below, as well as in Appendix
A
. Thus, near a ramification point
\mathbf{z}_{i}
we impose the regularity condition
8
8

8

We introduced the subscript
0
for the free variable
\mathbf{z}_{0}
to be able to make use of the short-hand
\mathbf{z}_{0i}
.

\Gamma_{1}(\mathbf{z}_{0})-x_{i}-\Gamma_{2}(\mathbf{z}_{0})\,\theta_{i}=a_{i}\,\mathbf{z}_{0i}^{w_{i}}+\mathcal{O}\big(\Theta_{0i}\,\mathbf{z}_{0i}^{w_{i}}\big)\ ,\quad\mathbf{z}_{0}\to\mathbf{z}_{i}\ .

(3.8)

Note that
a_{i}
is not in general the same as in the bosonic case, but can depend on the odd moduli
\theta_{i},\Theta_{i}
. However,

In summary, a

3.2
Properties

We briefly discuss some of the properties of the super covering maps defined above.

Near a ramification point, we can also describe what the second component of

3.5
Ramond punctures

Let us also briefly discuss Ramond punctures. As we saw in eq. (
3.9
), the odd component of
\bm{\Gamma}
looks multivalued around a NS puncture with even ramification
w
. As the odd coordinate
\theta
on the base space is single valued when encircling the branch point, this implies that the odd coordinate
\Theta
on the covering surface must be multi-valued. The reason is clear from the perspective of the symmetric orbifold: if one inserts a twist operator with even cycle length, it is lifted to a spin operator on the covering surface, meaning that the fermions are in the Ramond sector
Lunin:2001pw
. The proper notion of puncture to use in the case of even ramification indices is thus that of a Ramond puncture, which is discussed in
(
Witten:2012ga
, Section 4.1.3)
. There are two descriptions of such a Ramond puncture: either,
\Theta
is multi-valued and ill-defined at the puncture, or one replaces
\Theta
by a single-valued
\tilde{\Theta}
at the cost of changing the superconformal structure. The former description is more suitable for describing vertex operators in a SCFT, while the global structure of the SRS is more apparent in the latter description. Let us explain this a bit further.

In the global description, a Ramond puncture is not a point but rather a
0|1
dimensional submanifold of the SRS
Witten:2012ga
. At a R puncture, the superconformal structure
\mathcal{D}
degenerates, and one can always find superconformal coordinates
\big({z,\tilde{\Theta}}\big)
around the R puncture in which the superderivative becomes

D=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\Theta}}+z\,\tilde{\Theta}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\ .

(3.36)

In these coordinates, the R puncture is the submanifold
\big\{\big({z=0,\tilde{\Theta}}\big)\big\}
.
The conditions for
\bm{\Gamma}
in the presence of R punctures are now formally unchanged, see eqs. (
3.3
), (
3.8
), but the superderivative
D
is modified. Concretely, if the even part of
\bm{\Gamma}
behaves as

\Gamma_{1}\big({z,\tilde{\Theta}}\big)=a\,z^{w}+\dots\ ,\qquad w\in 2\mathbb{N}\ ,

(3.37)

near the R puncture, the superconformality condition fixes

\Gamma_{2}\big({z,\tilde{\Theta}}\big)=\sqrt{wa}\,\tilde{\Theta}\,z^{\frac{w}{2}}+\dots\ .

(3.38)

Thus,
\Gamma_{2}
is single valued in these coordinates, and is globally defined without branch cuts.

To pass to the second description of the R puncture, one can define

\Theta=\sqrt{z}\,\tilde{\Theta}\ .

(3.39)

This coordinate is now multi-valued around
z=0
, and one recovers the behaviour

\Gamma_{2}\big({z,{\Theta}}\big)=\sqrt{wa}\,{\Theta}\,z^{\frac{w-1}{2}}+\dots

(3.40)

for the odd component, as in eq. (
3.9
). An important point is that
\Gamma_{2}
is still single-valued, because the monodromies of
\Theta
and
z^{\frac{w-1}{2}}
around
z=0
cancel.

The upshot of this discussion is the following: if we want to see the global structure super covering map, and in particular its single-valuedness, explicitly, it is better to use the
\big({z,\tilde{\Theta}}\big)
coordinates. On the other hand, if we work with fields in a SCFT, it is more natural to use the multi-valued
\Theta
, as the fermionic fields are also multi-valued in the presence of a spin field. In particular, in the context of the symmetric orbifold,
\Theta
is the correct coordinate for lifting fields to the covering surface. We give an explicit example of a covering map in the presence of R punctures and discuss the relation to the orbifold in Appendix
B
.

4
Coverings in the Orbifold

We now discuss symmetric product orbifolds of superconformal field theories on super Riemann surfaces. We first show that the analytic structure of the super covering map defined in Section
3.1
is the correct one to lift fields in the presence of a twist operator. We then discuss how the analysis of
Lunin:2000yv
extends to the supersymmetric case, expressing the ground state correlator in terms of super covering map data. We also discuss an extended example of three-point functions of primary operators.

4.1
Lifting fields

Here we discuss the way super fields are twisted by twist operators in the symmetric product orbifold of a SCFT. We show how a super covering map naturally resolves this twisting and lifts the fields to a single-valued field on the super covering surface. We focus on the case where the fields are inserted at NS punctures on the covering surface, i.e. odd twist
w\in 2\,\mathbb{N}-1

We sketched how a covering map undoes the twist in Section

k

4.2
Correlators

Correlators in the orbifold of a SCFT can be calculated using the super covering maps. We first discuss the correlators of super twist fields

\bm{\sigma}_{w}(\mathbf{x})=\sigma_{w}(x)+\theta\,G_{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\sigma_{w}(x)\ ,

These correlators are given by a super Weyl anomaly when passing to the covering surface. We then show an example of three-point functions of descendants of twist operators and relate the covering map branch points to Ward identities for the orbifold correlators.

4.2.1
Correlator of twist fields

6
Conclusion and Outlook

In this paper, we have introduced the notion of super covering maps. These are maps between super Riemann surfaces which satisfy a superconformality condition eq. (
3.3
) and have a simple local structure around their ramification points, see eq. (
3.8
). We have shown that these maps play an important role in the
\text{AdS}_{3}/\text{CFT}_{2}
correspondence. Concretely, they appear in the symmetric product orbifold of a SCFT, where they can be used to calculate correlators in a manifestly supersymmetric way. On the string theory side of the duality, super covering maps solve the Ward identities of the hybrid formalism correlators. The symplectic bosons and free fermions thus give components of the super covering map when inserted into correlators.

In string theory, we were unable to see the full super covering map in the correlators because of difficulties in formulating theories with simultaneous worldsheet and space-time supersymmetry. Indeed, in the hybrid formalism analysis of Section
5
, only a part of the super covering map appears, because the worldsheet fields are inserted without a superpartner. As the
\mathcal{N}=4
algebra in the hybrid formalism is twisted, the natural superspace in this case is semi-rigid geometry
Distler:1991mf
. This geometry does not naturally fit together with the space-time SRS. Conversely, in the RNS formalism, it is easy to introduce worldsheet superpartners, but adding a dependence on an odd space-time variable inserts twist fields into the correlators
Giveon:1998ns
, making the Ward identity analysis more difficult.
19
19

19

It is straight-forward to analyse the Ward identities in the RNS formalism if one sets all the odd space-time variables to zero. However, in this case the super covering maps which appear are trivial extensions of the underlying covering map.

It would be interesting to see if there exists a formulation of the tensionless worldsheet theory which could incorporate both supersymmetries manifestly, or at least make it manifest on the level of the correlators. The appearance of super covering maps in such a formulation seems very likely and could serve as guidance for its construction.

\underset{C(z_{1})}{\oint}dz\,\Big(\big({z-z_{1}}\big)^{h+r-1}\,\mathcal{V}(z)+\Theta^{\mathrm{mv}}_{1}\,\big({z-z_{1}}\big)^{h+r-1}\,\mathcal{P}(z)\Big)\,V\big({\ket{h_{R}},z_{1}}\big)\ .

(B.15)

Since
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{P}
have opposite statistics, one of them is multi-valued in the presence of the spin field
V\big({\ket{h_{R}},z_{1}}\big)
, while the other is single-valued. Therefore, the integral above only makes sense for
\mathcal{V}
. To obtain a sensible integrand for
\mathcal{P}
, we need to change back to the single-valued modulus
\Theta_{1}^{\mathrm{sv}}
, which is related to
\Theta^{\mathrm{mv}}_{1}
as in eq. (
B.3
),

\Theta_{1}^{\mathrm{mv}}=\Theta_{1}^{\mathrm{sv}}\,\sqrt{z-z_{1}}+\dots\ ,\qquad z\to z_{1}\ .

(B.16)

When plugging this in, the integral in eq. (
B.15
) evaluates to

V\big({\mathcal{V}_{r}\ket{h_{R}},z_{1}}\big)+\Theta_{1}^{\mathrm{sv}}\,V\big({\mathcal{P}_{r}\ket{h_{R}},z_{1}}\big)=\big({1+\Theta_{1}^{\mathrm{sv}}\,G_{0}}\big)\,V\big({\mathcal{V}_{r}\ket{h_{R}},z_{1}}\big)=\mathbf{V}_{R}\big({\mathcal{V}_{r}\ket{h_{R}},\mathbf{z}_{1}}\big)\ .

(B.17)

When applying further fractional modes, the lifting proceeds iteratively and works in the same way.

B.3
A three-point correlator

We now discuss three-point functions of primaries as an example to see that this lifting procedure gives the correct correlators. This is analogous to the example in Section
4.2.2
. Consider three super primaries
\Phi_{i}=\varphi_{i}+\theta\,\psi_{i}
and the correlator

\mathcal{C}=\left\langle{\mathbf{V}\big({\varphi_{1,-\frac{h_{1}}{w_{1}}}\sigma_{w_{1}};\infty,0}\big)\,\mathbf{V}\big({\varphi_{2,-\frac{h_{2}}{w_{2}}}\sigma_{w_{2}};1,0}\big)\,\mathbf{V}\big({\varphi_{3,-\frac{h_{3}}{w_{3}}}\sigma_{w_{3}};0,\theta_{3}}\big)}\right\rangle\ ,

(B.18)

where
w_{2}
and
w_{3}
are even. We can again compare the calculation using the super covering map and the ordinary (reduced) covering map explicitly. Using the super covering map, the correlator is given by

\mathcal{C}=\text{factors}\times\left\langle{\mathbf{V}\big({\varphi_{1,-h_{1}}\ket{0};\infty,0}\big)\,\mathbf{V}_{R}\big({\varphi_{2,-h_{2}}\ket{h_{R}};1,\Theta_{2}^{\mathrm{sv}}}\big)\,\mathbf{V}_{R}\big({\varphi_{3,-h_{3}}\ket{h_{R}};0,\Theta_{3}^{\mathrm{sv}}}\big)}\right\rangle\ .

(B.19)

The
\theta_{3}
component of this expression is given by

\Theta_{2}^{\mathrm{sv}}\,\left\langle{V_{1}\,\big({G_{0}V_{2}}\big)\,V_{3}}\right\rangle+\Theta_{3}^{\mathrm{sv}}\,\left\langle{V_{1}\,V_{2}\,\big({G_{0}V_{3}}\big)}\right\rangle

(B.20)

with
V_{j}
denoting the bottom component of the
j
’th operator above.

If one uses the reduced covering map
\Gamma^{\text{red}}
instead, the
\theta_{3}
component of the correlator is

\theta_{3}\,\underset{C(0)}{\oint}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{\partial\Gamma^{\text{red}}}(z)}\,\left\langle{V_{1}\,V_{2}\,G(z)\,V_{3}}\right\rangle\ .

(B.21)

To relate the two expressions, we need to calculate the correlator with the
G
insertion,

g(z)=\left\langle{V_{1}\,V_{2}\,G(z)\,V_{3}}\right\rangle\ .

(B.22)

The function
g
can be expressed in terms of OPE data, which can be seen as follows. Since
g
has a branch cut from
0
to
1
, and we can define a function

h(z)=\sqrt{z(z-1)}\,g(z)\ .

(B.23)

Because the square root factor cancels the branch cut,
h
is a meromorphic function. Furthermore, using the fact that the
V_{i}
are super primaries, we see that
h
has simple poles at
0
and
1
and decays as
1/z
for
z\to\infty
. By Liouville’s theorem, it is thus given by the residues at its poles,

\displaystyle h(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{res}_{u=1}h(u)}{z-1}+\frac{\text{res}_{u=0}h(u)}{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle{V_{1}\,\big({G_{0}V_{2}}\big)\,V_{3}}\right\rangle}{z-1}+i\,\frac{\left\langle{V_{1}\,V_{2}\,\big({G_{0}V_{3}}\big)}\right\rangle}{z}\ ,

(B.24)

where we have used the OPE

G(z)V_{j}=z^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,G_{0}\,V_{j}+z^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,G_{-1}\,V_{j}+\dots\ ,\quad j=1,2\ ,

(B.25)

and the factor of
i
comes from the square root in the definition of
h
.

Example.

We now carry out the calculation for the correlator with
w_{1}=w+1
,
w_{2}=w
,
w_{3}=2
. The covering map for this correlator was written down in eq. (
B.1
) and the moduli
\Theta_{i}^{\mathrm{sv}}=\Theta_{i}^{\mathrm{mv}}/\sqrt{z(z-1)}
are given in eqs. (
B.6
) and (
B.7
). The super covering map calculation of the correlator, see eq. (
B.20
), then gives

-i\sqrt{\frac{1}{w(w+1)}}\,\theta_{3}\,\left\langle{V_{1}\,\big({G_{0}V_{2}}\big)\,V_{3}}\right\rangle+(-1)^{\frac{w}{2}}\,\sqrt{\frac{w}{4\,(w+1)}}\,\theta_{3}\,\left\langle{V_{1}\,V_{2}\,\big({G_{0}V_{3}}\big)}\right\rangle\ .

(B.26)

Using the reduced covering map in eq. (
B.2
) instead, one obtains

\displaystyle\theta_{3}\,\underset{C(0)}{\oint}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{\partial\Gamma^{\text{red}}}(z)}\,\frac{h(z)}{\sqrt{z(z-1)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta_{3}}{\sqrt{-w(w+1)}}\,\underset{C(0)}{\oint}dz\,\frac{h(z)}{z\big({z-1}\big)^{\frac{w}{2}}}

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\theta_{3}}{\sqrt{w(w+1)}}\,\left\langle{V_{1}\,\big({G_{0}V_{2}}\big)\,V_{3}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\frac{w}{2}}\,\frac{\theta_{3}}{\sqrt{w(w+1)}}\,\frac{w}{2}\,\left\langle{V_{1}\,V_{2}\,\big({G_{0}V_{3}}\big)}\right\rangle\ .

(B.27)

Appendix C
The super Liouville action

In this appendix we show how the vacuum correlator on the spherical covering SRS can be evaluated in terms of super covering map data. This leads to the formula eq. (
4.16
) for the correlator of super twist fields.

First, we need the supersymmetric analogue of the Weyl anomaly. In the super vielbein formalism
Howe:1978ia
, a super Weyl transformation of some reference vielbein
{E}^{A}
is defined as
22
22

22

Written in the notation of
DHoker:1988pdl
.
M
denotes all superspacetime indices,
A
all internal indices, with
a
the bosonic ones and
\alpha
the spinor indices.

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{M}^{a}

\displaystyle=e^{\omega}\,{E}_{M}^{a}\ ,

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{M}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=e^{\omega/2}\,\left({E}_{M}^{\alpha}+{E}_{M}^{a}\,(\gamma_{a})^{\alpha\beta}{D}_{\beta}\omega\right)\ .

(C.1)

The super Weyl anomaly can then be written as
(
DHoker:1988pdl
, eq. (3.113))

S_{sL}\big(\omega\big)=\frac{c}{96\pi}\,\int d^{2}\mathbf{x}\,\,\mathrm{sdet}\big({E}\big)\left({D}\omega\,{\bar{D}}\omega-i\,{\mathcal{R}}_{+-}\omega\right)\ ,

(C.2)

where
d^{2}\mathbf{x}=d^{2}x\,d\theta\,d\bar{\theta}
and
{\mathcal{R}}
is the curvature.
Flat superspace is also defined in
Howe:1978ia
. For this vielbein,

D=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\theta\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\ ,\quad\bar{D}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\theta}}+\bar{\theta}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{x}}\ ,\quad\mathcal{R}=0\ .

(C.3)

For
\bm{\Gamma}:\,\Sigma_{1}\to\Sigma_{2}
a superconformal map and
{E}^{A}_{1},{E}^{A}_{2}
locally flat vielbeine on
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
, respectively, one can calculate the following. The pull-back of
{E}^{A}_{2}
by
\bm{\Gamma}
to
\Sigma_{1}
is related to
{E}^{A}_{1}
by a super Weyl transformation with

\omega=\frac{1}{2}\,\log\left(\big({D}\Gamma_{2}\big)^{2}\,\big({\bar{D}}\bar{\Gamma}_{2}\big)^{2}\right)\ .

(C.4)

As a sanity check, we can compare this to the bosonic case where
\Gamma_{1}(\mathbf{z})=f(z)
with holomorphic
f
. Then
\Gamma_{2}=\Theta\,\sqrt{\partial f}
and
\omega=\frac{1}{2}\,\log\left(\partial f\,\bar{\partial}\bar{f}\right)
as in
Lunin:2000yv
.

Given these preliminaries, we can now find the super Weyl anomaly for super covering maps, following the derivation of
Lunin:2000yv
. We consider the correlator

\left\langle{\bm{\sigma}_{w_{1}}\big({\mathbf{x}_{1}}\big)\cdots\bm{\sigma}_{w_{n}}\big({\mathbf{x}_{n}}\big)}\right\rangle

(C.5)

with a sphere covering
\bm{\Gamma}:\mathbb{CP}^{1|1}\to\mathbb{CP}^{1|1}
and odd ramifications
w_{i}
. Denote the ramification points on the covering surface by
\mathbf{z}_{i}
, and the poles by
\bm{\zeta}_{j}
. By a Möbius transformation, we can always arrange one of the
\bm{\zeta}_{j}
to be
\big({\infty,0}\big)
. In the base space, we cut holes around
\mathbf{x}_{i}
and
\infty
, and similarly around
\mathbf{z}_{i}
and
\bm{\zeta}_{j}
on the covering surface. As the Weyl factor
\omega
is a sum of a holomorphic and an antiholomorphic function, the super Weyl anomaly eq. (
C.2
) can be partially integrated to give

S_{sL}\big({\omega}\big)=i\frac{c}{96\pi}\underset{\partial\Sigma}{\int}d\mathbf{z}\,\,\omega\,D\omega+\text{c.c.}\ ,

(C.6)

where
\Sigma
denotes the covering surface with holes cut out. Near a ramification point
\mathbf{z}_{i}
, the Weyl factor behaves as

\omega(\mathbf{z}_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\log\big({w_{i}a_{i}\mathbf{z}_{0i}^{w_{i}-1}}\big)+\text{c.c.}+\dots\ ,

(C.7)

where
a_{i}
is the leading coefficient in the Taylor expansion, which can have nilpotent components.
Integrating over the odd variable in the super Liouville action above reduces the contribution from the hole around
\mathbf{z}_{i}
to

i\frac{c}{96\pi}\underset{C(z_{i})}{\int}dz\,\,\phi\,\partial\phi+\text{c.c.}\ ,\qquad\phi(z)=\log\big({w_{i}a_{i}(z-z_{i})^{w_{i}-1}}\big)+\dots\ .

(C.8)

This is exactly the form in
(
Lunin:2000yv
, eq. (3.8))
, and when carrying out the integration one obtains

-\frac{c}{24}\frac{w_{i}-1}{w_{i}}\log\big({a_{i}}\big)+\text{c.c.}+\text{regulators}\ ,

(C.9)

where the regulators drop out in the final expressions. The analysis is similar for the pole coefficients: if the behaviour of
\Gamma_{1}
near a pole
\bm{\zeta_{j}}=\big({\zeta_{j},\varphi_{j}}\big)
is

\Gamma_{1}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{C_{j}}{z-\zeta_{j}-\Theta\,\varphi_{j}}+\dots\ ,

(C.10)

the contribution to the Liouville action is

-\frac{c}{12}\log\big({C_{j}}\big)+\text{c.c.}+\text{regulators}\ .

(C.11)

The pole at
(\infty,0)
does not contribute to the action.
The total contribution of
\bm{\Gamma}
to the correlator of twist fields is then given by

\left\langle{\bm{\sigma}_{w_{1}}\big({\mathbf{x}_{1}}\big)\cdots\bm{\sigma}_{w_{n}}\big({\mathbf{x}_{n}}\big)}\right\rangle_{\bm{\Gamma}}=e^{S_{sL}(\omega)}\ ,

(C.12)

which produces the
a_{i},C_{j}
dependence shown in eq. (
4.16
). The rest of the normalisation can be found by keeping track of the contributions from the holes more carefully, which is completely analogous to
Lunin:2000yv
.

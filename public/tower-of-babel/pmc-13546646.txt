A Prospective, Multi-Center, Randomized Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Two Absorbable Barbed Sutures in Thyroid Surgery
Authors: Xiuhe Zou, Ting Zhang, Wenxin Zhao, Yijun Wu, Wei Wei, Xiubo Lu, Changsheng Teng, Tao Huang, Meiping Shen, Niels-Derrek Schmitz, Bogdan Ilie, Shun Qu, Hao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aimed to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of two types of absorbable barbed sutures, STRATAFIXTM Spiral PDSTM Plus and STRATAFIXTM Spiral MONOCRYLTM Plus, compared with traditional sutures made of the same material in the suturing of thyroid surgery incision.Patients and MethodsThis study was a multicenter, prospective, evaluator-blinded, three-arm, non-inferiority, randomized controlled design study with a planned enrollment of 501 patients undergoing open thyroid surgery. Subjects were randomized to Spiral PDS Plus group, Spiral MONOCRYL Plus group, or the control group, according to the allocation ratio of 1:1:1. The primary endpoint was the success rate of subjects achieving grade A healing of surgical incisions 5–7 days after surgery.ResultsA total of 500 subjects were randomized in this study. The PP analysis was considered the primary analysis, while the FAS analysis was considered supportive. In the PPset, which included 154 subjects in Spiral PDS Plus group, 157 subjects in Spiral MONOCRYL Plus group, and 156 subjects in the control group, the grade A wound healing success rates were 99.4%, 98.7%, and 99.4%, respectively. The differences [Wald 95% CI] in success percentage between the two investigation groups and the control group were −0.01% [−1.79%, 1.77%] and −0.63% [−2.79%, 1.52%], respectively. Both Spiral PDS Plus and Spiral MONOCRYL Plus were found to be non-inferior to control in achieving Grade A healing of surgical incision (0.05 margin, p < 0.0001 for both).ConclusionThis study demonstrated the short-term safety and efficacy outcomes of using STRATAFIXTM Spiral PDSTM Plus and STRATAFIXTM Spiral MONOCRYLTM Plus sutures in the suturing of thyroid surgery incision, with acceptable risk-benefit.

Introduction

With the development of surgical techniques and people’s continuous pursuit of beauty, patients pay more attention to the appearance of incisions after surgery. Surgeons must choose appropriate surgical incision and approach, suture technique and material, minimize damage at different anatomical layers, reduce incision scar, decrease appearance of scar and improve patients’ quality of life, without compromising the quality of the surgery. Knotless and absorbable surgical suture techniques were developed for these purposes and have been demonstrated to be effective for wound stitching (including high tensile tissues) and to reduce surgery (suture) time in various clinical trials.1–5 A decrease in surgery time achieved by using barbed sutures might further reduce wound complications.6 World Health Organization (WHO), American College of Surgeons and US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recommend the application of knotless sutures in surgeries to prevent surgical site infection (SSI) in their guidelines.7–9 In addition, sutures containing triclosan, an antibacterial agent, have also been shown to reduce about 30% of SSIs in clinical studies.10,11

Two new types of surgical suturing devices, combining knotless technique and a triclosan-coating into one device, were used in the study. Knotless function is achieved by forming barbs via cutting the suture body. This study was aimed to demonstrate the clinical efficacy and safety of these two suture devices in wound closure in Chinese patients.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Population

This was a three-arm, prospective, multi-center, randomized, controlled study to compare the clinical efficacy and safety of two barbed suturing devices to that of non-barbed suturing devices in suturing soft tissue. There were two investigational groups, Spiral PDS Plus and Spiral MONOCRYL Plus, using STRATAFIXTM Spiral PDSTM Plus Knotless Tissue Control Device (STRATAFIXTM Spiral PDSTM Plus), and STRATAFIXTM Spiral MONOCRYLTM Plus Knotless Tissue Control Device (STRATAFIXTM Spiral MONOCRYLTM Plus), respectively. The control group used PDSTM Plus or MONOCRYLTM Plus. From April 1, 2019, to December 5, 2019, 523 patients from 9 clinical sites in China provided informed consent to participate in the study. Patients enrolled into the study had open thyroid surgery performed through an anterior cervical curved incision (Kocher’s incision) and their survival time after surgery was not less than 3 months. Patients were excluded if they had: (1) clinical stage IV thyroid cancer before surgery or planned cervical lymph nodes dissection or suspected or confirmed anaplastic thyroid cancer; (2) peripheral vascular disease affecting blood supply of the neck or active collagenosis (eg, scleroderma), or any other disease that would interfere with wound healing, including but not limited to fasting plasma glucose ≥7.7 mmol/L, and history of coagulation diseases; (3) chemotherapy or radiotherapy or immunosuppressant use (eg, steroids) within the last 6 months before enrollment or planned chemotherapy or radiotherapy during the study; (4) history of any thyroid surgery (except thyroid fine-needle aspiration biopsy), or personal or family history of keloid formation or hyperplasia. After screening, eligible patients were randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to one of the three groups before surgery. Wound healing grading was assessed by central imaging evaluators on day 5–7 post-surgery. Patients were followed up until 28–35 days post-surgery. In the study, all the patients, as well as the central imaging evaluators, were kept blind to the type of suture used for wound closure, while the investigators who were responsible for the incision suturing were not blind to the treatment due to the different shapes and usages between the investigation groups and the control group.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki on good clinical practice. The local medical ethics committee at the sites approved the study protocol. Surgical Procedures and Treatment Application

It was required to use Kocher’s incision to make an anterior cervical curved incision, which is a collar-like, curved incision about 1–2 cm above the suprasternal notch along a pre-existing skin crease, and parallel to the skin crease. All patients had their incision closed with sutures according to the treatment group to which they were assigned. The postoperative drainage tubes (if any) were exited through a different incision and not through the surgical wound to avoid the influence of drainage tube on the main incision. For the PIs, chief surgeon with at least 10 years of thyroid surgery experience was required. And for the assist surgeons, attending doctor with at least 5 years of surgical experience was required. Outcome Measures

Efficacy

The primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of subjects achieving Grade A healing of surgical incision (success) in each group, assessed by the blinded central imaging evaluators with pictures taken on day 5–7 post-surgery. Wound healing status was classified into three grades: Grade A healing was defined as good healing with no adverse reaction; Grade B healing was defined as inflammatory reaction(s) at the healing, such as red swelling, induration, hematoma, effusion, etc., but without suppuration; Grade C healing was defined as suppuration of incision, requiring incision, drainage and other treatments.

The secondary efficacy endpoints included: (1) incision suturing time, defined as the time required from the first needle insertion for stitching ribbon muscles to the completion of intradermal suture (min). (2) Modified Hollander Wound Evaluation Scale, assessed by the blinded central imaging evaluators with pictures taken on Day 28–35 post surgery. (3) postoperative incision pain score using Visual Analog Scale (VAS), assessed on Day 5–7 and Day 28–35 days post-surgery. (4) health related quality of life assessment with EQ-5D-5L scale, EQ-5D index and EQ-5D VAS at baseline, Day 5–7 and Day 28–35 post surgery. (5) Intraoperative suture performance index is assessed by the surgeon who completed the 2nd surgery using the two investigational suturing devices with the Intraoperative Suture Performance form in VAS scale.

Safety

Safety endpoints in the study included: (1) the incidence of postoperative SSI, defined according to the standard of US Centers for Disease Prevention and Control;12 (2) ASEPSIS score calculated to define the severity of infection,13,14 only assessed in subjects with confirmed SSI; (3) the incidence of incisional wound separation or wound dehiscence requiring intervention; (4) the incidence of delayed incisional wound healing events; (5) the incidence of other adverse events (AE); (6) product complaints.

Statistical Analysis

This was a non-inferiority trial with two treatment group comparisons, Spiral PDS plus versus control group, and Spiral MONOCRYL Plus versus control group. The overall family-wise error rate was controlled at 0.025 level using the Holm’s multiple comparisons procedure. One hundred fifty evaluable subjects per study arm would achieve 80% power to detect a non-inferiority margin difference in group proportions of −0.05 for each of the two comparisons using a Pearson Chi-square test with a one-sided significance level of 0.0125. The expected success percentage was 98% for all the three groups. To account for a potential 10% dropout rate, approximately 167 subjects per arm and 501 subjects in total were to be enrolled.

Full Analysis Set (FAS) includes subjects who were randomized and for whom the randomized suture materials and methods specified in the protocol were used for incision closure, with at least one data to evaluate the post-baseline effectiveness. Per Protocol (PP) Set contains all subjects in the FAS who had no major protocol deviations affecting the primary endpoint. Safety Set (SS) includes all enrolled subjects who underwent surgery, for whom the study product was used, and who had post-baseline follow-up data. The primary efficacy endpoint was analyzed using the FAS and PP sets. The PP analysis was considered the primary analysis, while the FAS analysis was considered supportive. Descriptive summaries for each secondary efficacy endpoint were provided by treatment group in the FAS. Descriptive summaries were generated by treatment group for safety endpoints using the SS.

All continuous variables were summarized by number of subjects, mean, and standard deviation. All categorical data were summarized by frequencies and associated percentages. For the primary endpoint analysis, the Holm’s step-down procedure was used. For each of the two group comparisons, the p-value associated with the non-inferiority test was calculated using the Normal approximation Z (pooled) statistic. For all secondary efficacy endpoints, descriptive summaries were provided by treatment group. For all safety endpoints, descriptive summaries were provided by treatment group. All analyses were carried out by using the SAS Enterprise Guide® (version 7.1).

Operation Time and Operating Room Time

The mean (±SD) operation time (the time in minutes from skin incision to the skin closure) in Spiral PDS Plus group, Spiral MONOCRYL Plus group and the control group were 72.1 (±25.5) minutes, 76.5 (±31.7) minutes, and 80.0 (±31.0) minutes, respectively. The mean (±SD) total operating room time (time in minutes from anesthesia to subject exiting the operating room) in the three groups was 107.6 (±36.5) minutes, 114.4 (±40.6) minutes, and 117.1 (±41.9) minutes, respectively.

Efficacy

Primary Efficacy Endpoint

In the study, the analysis in PP was considered the primary analysis for the primary efficacy endpoint. The number and percentage of the patients achieving grade A healing of surgical incision (success) on Day 5–7 post-surgery were 153 (99.35%) in Spiral PDS Plus group, 155 (98.73%) in Spiral MONOCRYL Plus group, and 155 (99.36%) in the control group. The difference in the percentage of successes (two-sided 95% CI, Wald method) was −0.01% [−1.79%, 1.77%] between Spiral PDS Plus and control group, and −0.63% [−2.79%, 1.52%] between Spiral MONOCRYL Plus and control group. The P values of the non-inferiority tests (0.05 non-inferiority margin) were both <0.0001 for the comparisons between investigation and control group, indicating that the two new suturing devices were both non-inferior to the control in achieving Grade A healing of surgical incision. The results of the analysis in FAS were consistent with those in PP. Safety

The safety evaluation showed that there were no SSIs, events of incisional wound separation or dehiscence requiring intervention, delayed incisional wound healing events, or complaints to study products observed in the study. Of the 485 patients in SS, 266 (54.8%) experienced AEs. For the analysis on the severity of AE, the maximum level of severity of all AEs occurring in each subject was used for summary. There were 223 subjects (46.0%) with mild AEs (Spiral PDS Plus group vs Spiral MONOCRYL Plus group vs control group: 50.0% vs 42.9% vs 45.1%), 42 subjects (8.7%) with moderate AEs (9.3% vs 8.7% vs 8.0%), and 1 subject (0.2%) in Spiral MONOCRYL Plus group with severe AEs. No subjects in the other two groups experienced severe AEs. One device-related AE was experienced by a subject in Spiral PDS Plus group. It was mild soft tissue swelling occurring at the incision site and healed with no treatment. Ten subjects (2.1%) experienced serious adverse events (SAEs) with no device-related SAEs or deaths.

Discussion

With the rapid development of surgical techniques and the update and iteration of surgical implantable absorbable products, barbed suture, as an indispensable one in surgical procedures, has been widely used in various surgeries including urology,15 orthopedics,16 gastrointestinal,17,18 colorectal,19 gynecology,20 etc. Numerous high-quality prospective randomized controlled studies have been carried out to confirm the safety of barbed suture in various surgeries and explore the clinical value of barbed suture. This study was conducted to address the safety and efficacy of two totally new absorbable barbed suture products in clinical use, which combined cutting barbing process with antibacterial triclosan process. This study showed that among Chinese adult patients, the success percentage of achieving Grade A healing of surgical incision 5–7 days after open thyroid surgery was over 98% when these two barbed, triclosan-coating devices were used according to indications and operation specifications. Both devices were found to be non-inferior to the control group using non-barbed sutures in the success percentage of wound healing. Furthermore, based on the total score of the Modified Hollander Wound Evaluation Scale assessed 28–35 days after surgery, the percentage of subjects with adequate wound closure was similar among the three groups.

In this study, the mean incision suturing time, mean operation time, and mean total operating room time were slightly shorter in the two investigational groups than the control group but without statistical significance. Previous studies showed that using barbed sutures was associated with a significantly faster closing time and improved operative efficiency.4,21–23 Given the fact that three layers were included in the record of the suturing time but for only one layer the investigational device was used, the incision time of all three layers might not reflect the real difference between the barbed devices and the non-barbed devices. Future more targeted studies are needed to prove the clinical value of barbed suture in operative time savings for thyroid surgeries.

The other efficacy endpoints of the study, including postoperative pain score and health related quality of life assessment, demonstrated similar results among the three groups at all observed time points. The safety profiles of the two barbed sutures were good with only one mild device-related AE reported in the study.

Compared with non-barbed sutures, barbed sutures benefit from their abilities of antistress relaxation and tension loading. The biomechanical data showed good or better absolute strength retained after implantation of the two barbed sutures compared to that historically observed with conventional (plain) or surgical chromic catgut. Also, there is good predictability of the degradation and absorption timelines with the use of the two barbed sutures. The sutures are substantially absorbed within 210 days after implantation of STRATAFIXTM Spiral PDSTM Plus and within 91 days for STRATAFIXTM Spiral MONOCRYLTM Plus. According to the result of the intraoperative suture performance index in the study, most of the surgeons had good performance experience using these two devices, making them good options for surgical sutures.

The study has limitations. It could not demonstrate the full advantages of barbed sutures, including scar cosmesis, as the follow-up duration after surgery was relatively short. Therefore, long-term studies in a broader patient population are warranted. Furthermore, whether the drainage tube is placed or not may have some influence on the wound healing. Although in this study we chose not to exit the drainage tube through the main incision to avoid the influence of the drainage tube on wound healing, there was no restriction on whether to use the drainage tube, depending on the specific condition of the subject’s surgical wounds. The value of the use of drainage tube for open thyroid surgery must be further studied.

Conclusion

The study demonstrated the safety and efficacy of STRATAFIXTM Spiral PDSTM Plus and STRATAFIXTM Spiral MONOCRYLTM Plus used for soft tissue approximation in suturing thyroid surgery incision. The short-term clinical benefit-risk profile was acceptable. Further long-term studies are needed to demonstrate the clinical benefit of the Spiral Plus products in terms of SSI prevention, cosmesis improvement, or long-term safety.

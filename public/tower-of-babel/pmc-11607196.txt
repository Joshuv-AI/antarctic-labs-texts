The role of predominant polarity on cognitive dysfunctions in patients with bipolar disorder
Authors: Ekin Atay, Çağatay Ermiş, İrem Nur Gökbayrak Atay, Ömer Aydemir, Erol Özmen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCognitive impairment is frequently observed in bipolar disorder (BD). Previous findings indicated that predominant polarity could have an effect on cognitive deficits. This study aimed to examine the association between predominant polarity and cognitive impairments in BD.Materials and methodsEuthymic BD patients with manic (MPP, n = 31), depressive (DPP, n = 25), undetermined predominant polarity (UPP, n = 28), and healthy controls (HC, n = 27) participated in the study. A battery of neurocognitive and social cognitive tests was implemented. Neurocognitive domains were identified via principal component analysis.ResultsThe MPP group performed worse in the Controlled Oral Word Association Test (COWAT), Reading the Mind in the Eyes Test (RMET), and Hinting Test (HT) compared to the DPP group and reasoning/problem-solving skills compared to the UPP group. Both MPP and UPP groups showed impairments in processing speed compared to HC. Among patient groups, there was no significant difference in working memory, attention, processing speed, verbal, and visual domain scores. The MPP group had poorer scores compared to controls in most of the social cognitive and neurocognitive domains in the study, while the overall cognitive impairment in the DPP group was relatively milder.ConclusionsAlthough our sample size was relatively small, the MPP group yielded more severe cognitive impairment in verbal fluency and social cognition tests compared to DPP. Patients with MPP are particularly vulnerable to cognitive impairment, making them a priority for cognitive enhancement interventions. Future studies should focus on the outcomes of cognitive and pharmacological interventions in these polarity subgroups.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40345-024-00363-6.

Introduction

Bipolar disorder (BD) is a recurrent and chronic psychiatric disorder, associated with impairment in social and occupational functioning (Akers et al. 2019; Grande et al. 2013). BD typically begins at a young age and is characterized by depressive, manic or hypomanic episodes, along with subthreshold symptoms between these mood episodes (Grande et al. 2013, 2016).

The concept of predominant polarity (PP) refers to the tendency for recurrence in one episode type compared to another (Colom and Vieta 2009). Depressive PP (DPP) is slightly more common than manic PP (MPP) (Vieta and Phillips 2007). PP, an illness- characteristic of BD, has shown strong correlations with various clinical variables. (Colom and Vieta 2009; Vieta and Phillips 2007). MPP has been associated with male gender, bipolar I disorder (BD-I) diagnosis, psychotic features, early onset, and manic onset, whereas DPP has been associated with depressive onset, a higher number of mood episodes, and a history of suicide attempts (Bartoli et al. 2024). A study examining the influence of affective temperament on PP found associations between cyclothymic and hyperthymic temperaments and MPP, suggesting that differences in clinical presentation across PP groups may be influenced by variations in temperament characteristics (Azorin et al. 2015).

Findings from prognostic follow-up studies are inconsistent: while some studies associate MPP patients with poorer outcomes (Baldessarini et al. 2012; Belizario et al. 2018; Popovic et al. 2014), others suggest a more favorable prognosis (Pallaskorpi et al. 2019). Factors contributing to poorer outcomes in DPP may include higher rates of suicide attempts, self-harming behaviors, prolonged episode durations, and residual depressive symptoms. Conversely, in MPP, negative outcomes may be linked to a higher frequency of hospitalizations, a greater incidence of psychotic symptoms, and an increased number of manic episodes (Grover et al. 2021).

Undetermined predominant polarity (UPP), characterized by a lack of clear polarity, is considered an intermediate phenotype between MPP and DPP. Studies suggest that individuals without a distinct polarity, and thus without polarity-specific treatment focus, may experience a higher frequency of relapses, mixed episodes, aggressive behaviors, seasonality, and an overall more severe course of illness compared to those with DPP or MPP (Fico et al. 2022).

The concept of PP is significant in determining maintenance treatment, as it relates not only to specific clinical characteristics but also indicates a patient’s tendency toward a particular mood episode polarity. The polarity index (PI), introduced by Popovic et al., is calculated by dividing the number needed to treat (NNT) for the prevention of depressive episodes by the NNT for the prevention of manic episodes (Popovic et al. 2012). Therefore, NNT values of both polarities, along with the number needed to harm (NNH), provide valuable insights for appropriate treatment selection in conjunction with PI (Carvalho et al. 2014).

Cognitive impairment is commonly observed in BD with many patients experiencing poor cognitive performance (Bora et al. 2010, 2016b). Evidence indicates that these impairments in cognition are present not only during manic or depressive episodes but also during euthymic intervals (Bourne et al. 2013). During euthymic phases of BD, there is a generalized, moderate level of impairment across various neurocognitive domains, including executive functions, processing speed, sustained attention, and verbal memory (Bora et al. 2009; Little et al. 2024), with particularly pronounced deficits in verbal learning and memory (Kurtz and Gerraty 2009). Social cognitive deficits, similar to those in the neurocognitive domain, tend to be more pronounced during the acute phases of the illness but are also observed during euthymic periods (Bora et al. 2016a).

Numerous studies have investigated cognitive impairments in BD and associated factors, such as genetic polymorphisms, structural and functional brain correlates, neuroimaging findings (including hippocampal gray matter loss, ventricular enlargement, and reduced volumes in the anterior cingulate and frontal cortices), along with inflammation markers, cytokines, brain-derived neurotrophic factor, and patient characteristics (e.g., medication, smoking, body mass index) (Long et al. 2024; Mario et al. 2024; Van Rheenen et al. 2020). However, the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood (Huang et al. 2023). Genome-wide association studies suggest a potential link between neurodevelopmental genes and BD. Cognitive impairment in BD is associated with several genetic variations, including schizophrenia-risk genes, suggesting underlying abnormalities in neurodevelopmental stages (Kloiber et al. 2020).

Neuroimaging studies investigating the neurobiological basis of PP have highlighted distinct patterns of brain structure changes across different types of PP. Janiri et al. found reduced hippocampal volume in DPP and UPP, but not in MPP, when compared to healthy controls (HC) (Janiri et al. 2020). Ruiz et al. further reported a significant reduction in the thickness of the right fusiform gyrus and left lingual gyrus, as well as a marked decrease in right thalamic volume, in patients with MPP compared to those with DPP or UPP (Ruiz et al. 2022). In another study, DPP showed the most pronounced cortical thickness reduction in several regions, including the right medial frontal cortex and left inferior frontal gyrus, whereas MPP showed the least reduction (Argyropoulos et al. 2023).

Previous studies have examined the relationship between the frequency of manic episodes and impairment in cognitive abilities (Bourne et al. 2013; Cavanagh et al. 2002; López-Jaramillo et al. 2010). However, the association between PP and cognitive functions has been investigated far less. Belizario et al. indicated cognitive deficits in MPP, whereas Bhardwaj et al. suggested that DPP was associated with poorer cognitive performance (Belizario et al. 2017; Bhardwaj et al. 2023).

Given the limited number of studies in the current literature, no study has yet investigated the association between PP and social cognition. Therefore, our study aimed to investigate the relationship between PP and social cognitive as well as neurocognitive impairments. Clarifying the relationship between PP and cognitive deficits could offer valuable insights for clinicians in developing pharmacological and psychotherapeutic strategies tailored to individual patient needs. We hypothesized that MPP would show more severe impairments in neurocognitive abilities than DPP and HC. Additionally, we expected that social cognitive impairment would be more pronounced in the MPP group.

Methods

Participants

The patient group was recruited consecutively from the Manisa Celal Bayar University outpatient clinic between December 2020 and October 2022. The HC group was also recruited from the same epidemiological area via advertisements and word of mouth. The groups consisted of volunteers with similar demographic characteristics. The study was approved by the Health Sciences Ethics Committee of Manisa Celal Bayar University Medical School (Protocol number: 20.478.486), and all participants signed an informed consent form.

The study included 111 participants (84 patients in remission and 27 HC). When identifying the PP groups, the definition by Colom et al. was used (Colom et al. 2006; Tohen et al. 2009). According to this definition, PP is characterized by at least two-thirds of episodes occurring in one polarity throughout a patient’s lifetime (Colom et al. 2006). Patients were divided into three subgroups according to their PP characteristics: manic (MPP, n = 31), depressive (DPP, n = 25), and undetermined PP (UPP, n = 28).

Inclusion criteria for patients were: a diagnosis of BD-I or bipolar-II disorder (BD-II) according to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders- 5 (DSM-5); age between 18 and 65 years; literacy in Turkish; and euthymia for the past two months (i.e. Young Mania Rating Scale [YMRS] < 6 and Hamilton Depression Rating Scale [HDRS] < 8 points).

Exclusion criteria included: (i) prior diagnoses of schizophrenia spectrum, other psychotic disorders, or substance-induced disorders according to the DSM-5 criteria (ii) intellectual disability or neurological illnesses affecting the central nervous system (iii) drug/alcohol use disorder within the past six months (iv) hearing or visual impairment affecting cognitive tests (v) history of electroconvulsive therapy or transcranial magnetic stimulation therapy within the last three months; and (vi) treatment with benzodiazepines or psychostimulants within the last six months.

The HC group was assessed through medical history, psychiatric history, and a Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-5 Disorders-Clinician Version (SCID-5/CV) interview. Matched with the patient groups in terms of age and gender, the HC group comprised healthy volunteers without any history of systemic or brain disorders that could affect cognitive functions. Additionally, they had no sensory impairments that could cause neurocognitive limitations (e.g., hearing or visual impairment), were not using medications with significant anticholinergic, hypnotic, or stimulant effects, and did not currently meet the criteria for any psychiatric disorder according to the DSM-5.

Procedures

The SCID-5/CV was used to confirm all psychiatric diagnoses (Elbir et al. 2019). The Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale-Revised Vocabulary Subtest, HDRS, Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HARS), and YMRS were used to determine premorbid intelligence and current affective status (Akdemir et al. 2001; Karadağ et al. 2002; Torrent et al. 2007; Yazici et al. 1998).

All participants were individually assessed using a neuropsychological battery that included tests of verbal memory/ learning, visual memory/ learning, attention, processing speed, working memory, problem-solving/reasoning, and social cognition.

Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test

The Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test (RAVLT) was used to evaluate verbal learning and memory abilities. It is based on a list-learning method, where participants are presented with 15 nouns and asked to recall as many as possible. Scores from the first to fifth trials were summed to calculate the overall verbal learning score. Late recall and word recognition from the list indicate verbal memory skills (Strauss et al. 2006).

Stroop Test

The Stroop Test (ST) examines the ability to inhibit automatic responses as well as focused attention. In this task, participants are asked to identify ink colors from a word list written in colored ink, ignoring the word itself. ST Çapa Form evaluates total duration, the number of errors made, and spontaneous corrections. The Çapa Form comprises three sections, each containing 60 items (Emek Savaş et al. 2020).

Trail-making test

The Trail Making Test-A Form (TMT-A) is a neuropsychological assessment tool used to evaluate processing speed and attention skills. In this test, participants are asked to connect 25 dots scattered on a page, starting from the number “1” to “25” in sequential order as accurately and swiftly as they can.

The Trail Making Test B Form (TMT-B) also measures cognitive flexibility, visual attention, and task-switching abilities, as TMT-B introduces a mix of both numbers and letters. In this test, participants connect the numbers and letters alternately, in ascending order (e.g., 1-A-2-B-3-C, and so on) as quickly and accurately as possible (Reitan 1958).

Wechsler Memory Scale - Visual Reproduction Test

The Visual Reproduction section of the Wechsler Memory Scale (WMS-R VR) involves four distinct figures that participants are then asked to recall and replicate after a brief presentation. Each figure is presented individually to assess visual learning performance. After a period, participants are requested to draw the figures from memory which assesses delayed recall skills and visual memory (Strauss et al. 2006).

Controlled oral Word Association Test

The Controlled Oral Word Association Test (COWAT) was used to evaluate phonemic fluency, where participants are asked to generate words that begin with a specific letter within a time limit. This test measures the ability to produce words fluently (Strauss et al. 2006).

Auditory consonant trigrams

The Auditory Consonant Trigrams (ACT) was used to measure working memory abilities. Participants were asked to recall three consonant letters followed by a randomly generated number. They were then instructed to count down from the given number while retaining the consonants in mind for intervals of 3, 9, and 18 s. Subsequently, participants were asked to recall the three consonants (Anil et al. 2003; Strauss et al. 2006).

Wisconsin Card sorting test

The Wisconsin Card Sorting Test (WCST) is a cognitive assessment tool that measures cognitive flexibility and problem-solving abilities. Participants attempt to find the correct card based on patterns in the previous responses, without direct instruction. The test also evaluates problem-solving strategies, adaptive skills, and abstract thinking (Karakaş 2004).

Conners continuous performance test

The Conners Continuous Performance Test (CCPT) is a computer-based assessment that measures attention and impulsivity. Participants respond to stimuli on the screen, and their performance reflects sustained attention, selective attention, and impulsivity. CCPT- II version was used in this study (Conners et al., 2000).

Reading the mind in the eyes

The Reading the Mind in the Eyes Test (RMET) assesses the ability to infer emotions and mental states based on eye photographs. Participants choose the emotion or state that best matches what the person in the image may feel or think. RMET was used to measure visual theory of mind (ToM) skills in the context of social cognition (Yıldırım et al. 2011).

The hinting test

The Hinting Test (HT) assesses the ability to understand indirect speech and hidden meanings in social situations. Participants interpret brief scenarios in which characters make indirect statements, demonstrating comprehension of subtle communication cues. This test measures ToM skills and social cognition (Bora et al. 2006).

Statistical analyses

All neurocognitive data were converted into z-scores using the mean and standard deviations of HC. In line with prior literature (Saykin et al. 1994), participants who were unable to complete a test due to cognitive impairment were assigned the lowest available score in the sample. To enable parametric analysis, the results of TMT, ST, WCST, and CCPT scores were normalized using the two-step normalization method previously described in the literature (Templeton 2011).

Principal component analysis (PCA) was implemented to identify neurocognitive domains (Dickinson et al. 2008), as a useful dimension reduction technique to calculate cognitive domain scores. PCA is particularly valuable for assessing performance within specific cognitive domains, as it minimizes the effects of other correlated cognitive domains and is more suitable than summing test scores. In addition, single-factor PCA was applied to assess global cognition scores (Ceylan et al. 2020; Sağlam et al. 2023).

When comparing neurocognitive tests between groups, ANCOVA analysis of variance was used. Age and education were entered into the model as covariates. The Bonferroni method was applied in post-hoc analysis. For all analyses, a two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered significant. Bonferroni correction has been made for p-values on a per-table basis. SPSS 20.0 program was used for data analysis (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY).

Results

Euthymic BD patients with manic (MPP, n = 31, 36.9%), depressive (DPP, n = 25, 29.8%), undetermined PP (UPP, n = 28, 33.3%), and healthy controls (HC, n = 27) participated in the study. The sociodemographic data of the study participants are summarized in Table 1. The mean age of participants was 40.4 ± 12.2 years. There were no significant differences among the four groups concerning age, gender, hand dominance, or family history of psychiatric disorders. The frequency of smokers was higher in the UPP group compared to the HC group. In the MPP group, the rate of unemployment was higher than that in the HC group. Additionally, the MPP group exhibited lower levels of education and vocabulary subtest scores compared to both the DPP and HC groups.

Table 3 presents the overall assessment of the neurocognitive domains and neuropsychological tests used in the study. The patient groups demonstrated impaired ST interference and RAVLT scores compared to controls. Verbal domain and global cognition were also more impaired in all patient groups compared to controls. The HC group performed better than both the MPP and UPP groups in processing speed, specifically in TMT scores (Table 3).

The MPP group performed worse than the HC group in attention and working memory. Additionally, the MPP group had significantly poorer scores in reasoning/problem-solving and the number of completed categories in WCST compared to the UPP group (Table 3; Fig. 1). The DPP group also performed better than the MPP group on the COWAT, RMET, and Hinting Test. Processing speed, TMT A duration, ST interference, verbal learning and memory, RAVLT sum of 1–5, RAVLT trial 7, ACT, reasoning and problem-solving, WCST category, global cognition, RMET, and Hinting Test remained significantly associated even after correction for the false discovery rate.

Discussion

This study aimed to investigate the relationship between PP and neurocognitive and social cognitive impairments. Our findings supported the hypothesis that MPP was overall associated with poorer cognitive performance. The results revealed that MPP patients underperformed in verbal fluency compared to the DPP and HC groups. These findings were aligned with a previous study that highlighted poorer neurocognitive functions in the MPP group (Belizario et al. 2017). Furthermore, our findings extend previous research by demonstrating the effect of MPP on deficits in social cognition and ToM skills.

In our study, UPP exhibited the second most significant cognitive impairments, following MPP, while the DPP group had the most benign profile. These results align with the literature, which shows that more frequent manic episodes are associated with poorer cognitive performance (Bourne et al. 2013; Cavanagh et al. 2002; López-Jaramillo et al. 2010). Thus, it can be argued that the susceptibility for mania recurrence might stem from similar underlying neurobiological etiology that also gives rise to impaired cognition and ToM.

The brain regions/connections involved in auditory and visual processing with ToM have been previously investigated in schizophrenia (Demirlek et al. 2023). Another previous work also suggested that decreased cortical thickness in the fusiform and lingual gyri was related to MPP (Ruiz et al. 2022). Based on these findings, ToM deficits in MPP group could be related to specific neurobiological underpinnings of each polarity group.

UPP, by definition, exhibits a higher frequency of mania than DPP but a lower frequency than MPP. UPP was also moderately associated with negative prognostic factors and cognitive impairment. Beyond being an intermediate phenotype, UPP may represent a distinct patient subgroup associated with aggressiveness and higher episode frequency (Fico et al. 2022), which may contribute to cognitive impairment (Martino et al. 2013). Altogether, clinical features and cognitive impairment are associated with poor outcomes and require tailored treatment strategies.

On the other hand, the verbal learning/memory domain did not differ among the study groups, in contrast to the previous literature (Belizario et al. 2017). All polarity groups demonstrated impairments in verbal learning/memory, with no significant differences among patient groups, suggesting that deficits in the verbal domain may occur in BD independent of polarity type.

In our study, consistent with the previous literature (Bartoli et al. 2024), the MPP group was more likely to have a BD-I diagnosis and psychotic features. A meta-analysis suggested that psychotic features and a full manic episode are linked to cognitive deficits in BD (Bora 2018); however, psychotic features were not very common in the study sample and do not seem to account for the entire difference between groups (Bora 2018; Keramatian et al. 2021).

Targeted pharmacological and psychotherapeutic interventions have been tested to enhance cognitive functions, including erythropoietin, mifepristone, transcranial magnetic stimulation, intranasal insulin as well as medications that affect cholinergic and dopaminergic pathways (Tamura et al. 2021). A recent meta-analysis of 993 participants found that cognitive remediation therapy led to small to moderate improvements in both cognitive abilities and depressive symptoms in mood disorders (Goldberg et al. 2023). Individuals in the MPP group are particularly vulnerable to cognitive impairment; thus, they may need individually tailored cognitive enhancement interventions, especially suitable for cognitive remediation therapy during euthymia.

On the other hand, chronic or recurrent depression is rather common in the context of BD and can lead to severe consequences, including occupational impairment, overall disability, morbidity, and mortality (Baldessarini et al. 2020). However, the DPP group in this study was included during remission and had relatively milder cognitive impairment. Accordingly, adjunctive cognitive enhancement strategies can also be considered after adequate treatment of depressive mood symptoms.

The limitations of the study should be acknowledged when interpreting its findings. First, this study was conducted in a single center, with a modest sample size for each subgroup, and its cross-sectional design only allowed for associations rather than causality. Additionally, participants were using various medications, introducing heterogeneity. Other illness-related confounding factors require further analysis as they may influence the findings. Furthermore, there is a modest correlation between social cognition and neurocognitive domains, varying around 0.2–0.3 (Ventura et al. 2013). Finally, although the definition of PP is generally consistent over time, it is not static, and changes may occur (Pallaskorpi et al. 2019). However, the total duration of illness was 23.2 years in our study, providing a relatively stable basis for this clinical feature’s assignment.

Longitudinal studies are needed where follow-up and treatment can be systematically managed from the initial episodes to observe the potential effects of medications and other illness characteristics (i.e. the number of episodes and the duration of illness) throughout the disease course. Additionally, future research could aim to clarify underlying neurobiological processes that lead to specific phenotypes and their relation to neurocognitive impairment. This approach could shed light on the structural, molecular, and functional underpinnings that contribute to specific endophenotypes. Similarly, the specific effects of polarity-based treatments and cognitive remediation programs in different PP subgroups have yet to be investigated to improve patients’ functioning and quality of life.

In conclusion, the cognitive impairment seen in patients with MPP is more pronounced than in subjects with DPP and UPP, especially in verbal fluency and social cognition skills. These deficits in the MPP group can be specifically targeted in clinical trials. Future studies should focus on the long-term course of subjects with different PP, as well as its moderating effect on pharmacological and cognitive interventions.

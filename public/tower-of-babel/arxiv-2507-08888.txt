A two-parameter deformation of the gamma function, associated functions, and some related inequalities
Anton Asare-Tuah, Emmanuel Djabang, Eyram K. A Schwinger, Benoît Florent Sehba, Ralph A. Twum

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a new two-parameter deformation of the Gamma function that generalizes some existing Gamma-type functions in the literature. We study properties of this function and associated Beta, Psi and Zeta functions. We also prove some inequalities for the corresponding Beta function and Polygamma functions.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to introduce a new two-parameter gamma function, which is another deformation of the usual gamma function and related functions, and to prove some inequalities.

There are some deformations of the usual Gamma function in the literature that have been intensively studied (see
[
5
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
). Our proposed deformation generalizes two existing deformations in the literature. Let us recall these two deformations here:

•

The
k
-Gamma function of
[
5
]
is defined by

\Gamma_{k}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!k^{n}(nk)^{\frac{x}{k}-1}}{(x)_{n,k}},\quad k>0,x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus k\mathbb{Z}^{-}.

•

The
\nu
-analogue of the Gamma function was introduced and studied in
[
9
]
and is defined by

\Gamma^{\nu}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!\nu^{n}\left(\frac{n}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{\nu}-1}}{(x)_{n,\nu}},\quad k>0,x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\nu\mathbb{Z}^{-}.

Here,
(x)_{n,k}=x(x+k)\cdots(x+(n-1)k)
is the so-called Pochhammer
k
-symbol.
Deformations of the classical Gamma function have applications in quantum mechanics and physics, combinatorics, special functions, and orthogonal polynomials (see, for example,
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
and the references therein).

In this note, we introduce the
(k,\nu)
-Gamma function as follows:

(1)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!(k\nu)^{n}\left(\frac{nk}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}}{(x)_{n,k\nu}},\quad k>0,\nu>0,x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus k\nu\mathbb{Z}^{-}.

One clearly has that

\Gamma_{1,\nu}=\Gamma^{\nu}\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma_{k,1}=\Gamma_{k}.

In this work, the
(k,\nu)
Gamma function and the associated Beta function, Psi function, and Zeta function are studied. We provide various properties and study some inequalities.

In the next section, we bring some known results that are useful for our journey. In Section 3, we give an equivalent definition of
\Gamma_{k,\nu}
and several properties. In Section 4, the associated Beta function that we call
(k,\nu)
Beta function is introduced and some of its properties are studied. In particular, we obtain some inequalities for this function and estimates involving the
(k,\nu)
Gamma function. In Section 5, we introduce the
(k,\nu)
Psi function, provide some properties, some associated partial differential equations, and prove some inequalities for the associated polygamma functions. We end this fifth section by introducing the
(k,\nu)
Zeta function. In Section 6, we compare the bounds obtained for the ratios of the Beta functions in Section 4.

The notation
\Gamma
,
B
,
\Psi
(
k=\nu=1
), will always represent the usual Gamma, Beta and Digamma functions, respectively. For more on these classical functions, we refer the reader to
[

]
.

We start with the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x>0
. Then the following holds.

(2)

\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{-x}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{x-1}}{1+t}dt=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi x)}.

Proof.

The first equality follows from a basic substitution, and the second can be proved using the residues theorem.

The following Chebychev integral inequality will be needed (see

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f,g,h:I\subseteq\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be so that
h(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in I
, and the functions
h,hf,hg
and
hfg
are integrable on

f
and

(3)

\left(f(x)-f(y)\right)\left(g(x)-g(y)\right)\geq(\leq)\,0\quad\text{for all}\quad x,y\in I,

then

(4)

\int_{I}h(x)dx\int_{I}h(x)f(x)g(x)dx\geq(\leq)\,\int_{I}h(x)f(x)dx\int_{I}h(x)g(x)dx.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f,g:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth functions on
(a,b)
where
a,b\in[0,\infty)
. Assume that
f
reaches a global minimum at a unique point
c\in(a,b)
and

. Then one has that

\int_{a}^{b}g(x)e^{-\frac{f(x)}{h}}dx=\sqrt{h}e^{-\frac{f(c)}{h}}\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{g(c)}{\sqrt{f^{\prime\prime}(c)}}+O(h).

3.
The
(k,\nu)
Gamma function

We discuss in this section, equivalent characterizations of the
(k,\nu)
Gamma function and some of its properties.

We recall that for
x\in\mathbb{C}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the Pochhammer a-symbol is defined as

(5)

(x)_{n,a}=x(x+a)\cdots(x+(n-1)a).

We also recall our definition of
\Gamma_{k,\nu}
.

Definition 3.1
.

For
k>0
and
\nu>0
, the
(k,\nu)
Gamma function
\Gamma_{k,\nu}
is defined as

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!(k\nu)^{n}\left(\frac{nk}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}}{(x)_{n,k\nu}},\quad x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus k\nu\mathbb{Z}^{-}.

We have the following basic facts.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
k,l,\nu,\mu>0,
. For
x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(k\nu\mathbb{Z}^{-}\cap l\mu\mathbb{Z}^{-})
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following hold.

(a)

(x)_{n,l\mu}=\left(\frac{l\mu}{k\nu}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{k\nu x}{l\mu}\right)_{n,k\nu}
.

(b)

\Gamma_{l,\mu}(x)=\left(\frac{l\nu}{k\mu}\right)^{\frac{x}{l\mu}-1}\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(\frac{k\nu x}{l\mu}\right)
.

Let us prove the following equivalent definition of
\Gamma_{k,\nu}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. For any
x\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(x)>0
, we have

(6)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}dt.

Proof.

We recall that

e^{-t}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1-\frac{t}{n}\right)^{n}.

Using for example the Monotone Convergence Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{n}\left(1-\frac{t}{n}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{n}\left(1-\frac{t}{n}\right)^{n}t^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}dt.

Put

A_{n,n-j}(x):=\int_{0}^{n}\left(1-\frac{t}{n}\right)^{n-j}t^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}dt,j=0,1,\cdots,n.

Then using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{n,n-j}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n-j}{n}\frac{k\nu}{x}\int_{0}^{n}\left(1-\frac{t}{n}\right)^{n-(j+1)}t^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n-j}{n}\frac{k\nu}{x}A_{n,n-(j+1)}(x+k\nu).

We deduce that

(7)

\displaystyle A_{n,n}(x)=\frac{n!(k\nu)^{n}}{n^{n}x(x+k\nu)\cdots\left(x+(n-1)k\nu\right)}A_{n,0}(x+nk\nu).

But

A_{n,0}(x+nk\nu)=\int_{0}^{n}t^{\frac{x}{k\nu}+n-1}dt=\frac{k\nu}{x+nk\nu}n^{\frac{x}{k\nu}+n}.

Hence

A_{n,n}(x)=\frac{n!(k\nu)^{n}n^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}}{(x)_{n,k\nu}\left(1+\frac{x}{nk\nu}\right)}.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n,n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!(k\nu)^{n}n^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}}{(x)_{n,k\nu}\left(1+\frac{x}{nk\nu}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!(k\nu)^{n}n^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}}{(x)_{n,k\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!(k\nu)^{n}\left(\frac{nk}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}}{(x)_{n,k\nu}}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x).

∎

We have the following identities.

Proposition 3.4
.

The following hold.

(1)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+k\nu)=x\nu^{-2}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)
.

(2)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(kx)=k^{\frac{x}{\nu}-1}\Gamma_{1,\nu}(x)
.

(3)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(\nu x)=\nu^{1-\frac{x}{k}}\Gamma_{k,1}(x)
.

(4)

(x)_{n,k\nu}=\nu^{2n}\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+nk\nu)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}
.

(5)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(k\nu-x)=\frac{\nu}{k}\frac{\pi}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{k\nu}\right)}
.

(6)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(2x)=\frac{2^{\frac{2x}{k\nu}-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+\frac{k\nu}{2})
.

Proof.

Assertions (1), (2) and (3) follow from the definition of
\Gamma_{k,\nu}
. Assertion (4) follows from assertion (1). We will deduce assertion (6) from a property of the
(k,\nu)
Beta function later. Let us prove assertion (5).

We have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(k\nu-x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(t+u)}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{-\frac{x}{k\nu}}dtdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-v}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}(v-t)\right)^{-\frac{x}{k\nu}}dtdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{v}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}(v-t)\right)^{-\frac{x}{k\nu}}dt\right)e^{-v}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(1-\lambda\right)^{-\frac{x}{k\nu}}d\lambda\right)e^{-v}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{k}\frac{\pi}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{k\nu}\right)}

where at the last line, we have used Lemma
2.1
. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We obviously have
\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)=\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{k\nu}\right)
. This relation can be combined with the properties of the usual gamma function to derive some properties of
\Gamma_{k,\nu}
. We do not use this approach here as we wish the paper to be essentially self-contained.

We have the following analogue of Stirling’s formula.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Re(x)>0
. Then the following identity holds.

(8)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+k\nu)(x)=\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}\left(\frac{x}{\nu^{2}}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}\left(\frac{x}{k\nu}\right)^{1/2}e^{-\frac{x}{k\nu}}+O(\frac{1}{x}).

Proof.

We note that

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+k\nu)=\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}e^{-t}dt.

Now,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{x}{k\nu}\ln(t)-t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x\left(\frac{t}{x}-\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln(t)\right)}dt.

Put
f(t)=\frac{t}{x}-\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln(t)
and
g(t)=1
. Then

f^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{k\nu t}
and vanishes only at
t=\frac{x}{k\nu}
.

One easily checks that
f
has a global minimum at
c=\frac{x}{k\nu}
. Also, we have that

f^{\prime\prime}(c)=\frac{1}{k\nu t^{2}}>0.

Take
h=\frac{1}{x}
and note that
f(c)=\frac{1}{k\nu}-\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln\left(\frac{x}{k\nu}\right)
and
f^{\prime\prime}(c)=\frac{k\nu}{x^{2}}
. It follows from Proposition
2.3
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x\left(\frac{t}{x}-\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln(t)\right)}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}e^{-x\left(\frac{1}{k\nu}-\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln\left(\frac{x}{k\nu}\right)\right)}\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{x}{\sqrt{k\nu}}+O(\frac{1}{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{x}{\nu^{2}}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}\left(\frac{x}{k\nu}\right)^{1/2}e^{-\frac{x}{k\nu}}+O(\frac{1}{x}).

The proof is complete.
∎

Combining the above with the first identity in Proposition
3.4
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\Re(x)>0
. Then the following identity holds.

(9)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)=\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}\left(\frac{x}{\nu^{2}}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(\frac{x}{k\nu}\right)^{1/2}e^{-\frac{x}{k\nu}}+O(\frac{1}{x}).

We also have the following formula for the reciprocal of the
(k,\nu)
Gamma function.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. We have

(10)

\frac{1}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}=\nu^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}k^{-\frac{x}{k\nu}}\frac{x}{\nu}e^{\frac{\gamma x}{k\nu}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{x}{jk\nu}\right)e^{-\frac{x}{jk\nu}}.

Proof.

We have from the definition of
\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\nu}{nk}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\frac{x(x+k\nu)\cdots\left(x+(n-1)k\nu\right)}{(k\nu)(2k\nu)\cdots\left((n-1)k\nu\right)(nk\nu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\nu}{nk}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\frac{x}{nk\nu}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{x}{jk\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\frac{x}{k\nu}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{x}{jk\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\frac{x}{k\nu}\exp\left({\frac{x}{k\nu}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{j}-\ln(n-1)\right)}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{x}{jk\nu}\right)e^{-\frac{x}{jk\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\frac{x}{k\nu}e^{\frac{\gamma x}{k\nu}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{x}{jk\nu}\right)e^{-\frac{x}{jk\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}k^{-\frac{x}{k\nu}}\frac{x}{\nu}e^{\frac{\gamma x}{k\nu}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{x}{jk\nu}\right)e^{-\frac{x}{jk\nu}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

We note that the function
(k,\nu)
Gamma is logarithmically convex. Indeed, applying Hölder’s inequality, one easily obtains that for
0<p,q<1
with
p+q=1
, and
x,y>0
,

(11)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(px+qy)\leq\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\right)^{p}\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)\right)^{q}.

Let us prove the following extension of Bohr-Mollerup’s theorem (for the the functions
\Gamma(x)
and
\Gamma_{k,1}(x)
, see
[
3
,
5
]
).

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
, and let
k,\nu>0
. Suppose that

(i)

f(k\nu)=1
;

(ii)

f(x+k\nu)=x\nu^{-2}f(x)
;

(iii)

f
is logarithmically convex.

Then
f(x)=\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

We have by the definition of
\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)
that

f(x)=\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(x)_{n,k\nu}f(x)}{n!(k\nu)^{n}\left(\frac{nk}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}}=1.

Fom the hypotheses on
f(x)
and properties of
\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)
, we can suppose that
0<x<k\nu
.

As
f
is logarithmically convex, the following inequalities are satisfied:

\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln\left(\frac{f(nk\nu+k\nu)}{f(nk\nu)}\right)\leq\frac{1}{x}\ln\left(\frac{f(nk\nu+k\nu+x)}{f(nk\nu+k\nu)}\right)\leq\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln\left(\frac{f(nk\nu+2k\nu)}{f(nk\nu+k\nu)}\right).

As
f(x+k\nu)=x\nu^{-2}f(x)
, the above reduces to

\frac{x}{k\nu}\ln\left(\frac{nk}{\nu}\right)\leq\ln\left(\frac{(x+nk\nu)\left(x+(n-1)k\nu\right)\cdots xf(x)}{n!\left(k\nu\right)^{n}}\right)\leq\frac{x}{k\nu}\ln\left(\frac{(n+1)k}{\nu}\right).

That is

0\leq\ln\left(\frac{(x+nk\nu)\left(x+(n-1)k\nu\right)\cdots xf(x)}{n!\left(k\nu\right)^{n}\left(\frac{nk}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}}\right)\leq\ln\left(\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}\right).

As
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\ln\left(\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}\right)=0
, we conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\ln\left(\frac{(x+nk\nu)\left(x+(n-1)k\nu\right)\cdots xf(x)}{n!\left(k\nu\right)^{n}\left(\frac{nk}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}}\right)=0.

Hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(x)_{n,k\nu}f(x)}{n!(k\nu)^{n}\left(\frac{nk}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}}=1.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.
The
(k,\nu)
Beta function and some inequalities

We introduce the
(k,\nu)
Beta function in this section. We present some of its properties and prove some inequalities.

4.1.
Definition and first properties

Definition 4.1
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. The
(k,\nu)
Beta function
B_{k,\nu}
is defined by

(12)

B_{k,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+y)}.

We have the following equivalent definition of the above.

Proposition 4.2
.

The
B_{k,\nu}
function satisfies

(13)

B_{k,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{\nu}{k}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(1-t\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}dt.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(t+u)}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}dtdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{u}^{\infty}e^{-v}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}(v-u)\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{v}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}(v-u)\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}du\right)e^{-v}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\nu}{k}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}(1-\lambda)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}d\lambda\right)e^{-v}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}v\right)^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-1}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+y)\frac{\nu}{k}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}(1-\lambda)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}d\lambda.

The proof is complete.
∎

The following also holds.

Proposition 4.3
.

The
B_{k,\nu}
function satisfies

(14)

B_{k,\nu}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\frac{\nu}{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}dt.

Proof.

An elementary substitution on the right-hand side of (
13
) provides this identity. Let us give a proof that does not use the previous result.

Put

F(z):=\int_{0}^{\frac{\nu}{k}z}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(z-\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}du

and note that

F(1)=\int_{0}^{\frac{\nu}{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}du.

We easily obtain that

F(z)=z^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{\nu}{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}du.

That is

(15)

F(z)=z^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-1}F(1).

Put

I_{k,\nu}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(z)e^{-z}dz.

We are going to compute the above integral in two different ways.

Firstly, using (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle I_{k,\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(1)\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-1}e^{-z}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(1)\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}z\right)^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-1}e^{-z}dz.

That is

(16)

I_{k,\nu}(x,y)=F(1)\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-1}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+y).

Secondly, using
F(z)
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{k,\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z}\int_{0}^{\frac{\nu}{k}z}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(z-\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}dudz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\frac{k}{\nu}u}^{\infty}e^{-z}\left(z-\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}dz\right)\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}dt\right)e^{-\frac{k}{\nu}u}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{k}{\nu}u}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}du.

One easily checks that

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{k}{\nu}u}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}du=\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x).

Thus

(17)

I_{k,\nu}(x,y)=\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y).

From (
16
) and (
17
), we deduce that

F(1)\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-1}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+y)=\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)

that is

F(1)=\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+y)}=B_{k,\nu}(x,y).

The proof is complete.
∎

We have the following identities.

Proposition 4.4
.

The following hold.

(a)

B_{k,\nu}(x+k\nu,y)=\frac{x}{x+y}B_{k,\nu}(x,y)
.

(b)

B_{k,\nu}(x,y+k\nu)=\frac{y}{x+y}B_{k,\nu}(x,y)
.

(c)

B_{k,\nu}(x+k\nu,y)+B_{k,\nu}(x,y+k\nu)=B_{k,\nu}(x,y)
.

(d)

For
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\frac{B_{k,\nu}(x+nk\nu,y+mk\nu)}{B_{k,\nu}(x,y)}=\frac{(x)_{n,k\nu}(y)_{m,k\nu}}{(x+y)_{n+m,k\nu}}.

(e)

B_{k,\nu}(x,y)=\displaystyle\frac{x+y}{xy}\nu^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(jk\nu)(jk\nu+x+y)}{(jk\nu+x)(jk\nu+y)}
.

(f)

B_{k,\nu}\left(\frac{x+k\nu}{2},\frac{k\nu-x}{2}\right)=\frac{\nu}{k}\pi\sec\left(\frac{\pi x}{2k\nu}\right),\,\,0<x<k\nu
.

(g)

B_{k,\nu}(x,x)=2^{1-\frac{2x}{k\nu}}B_{k,\nu}(x,\frac{k\nu}{2})
.

Proof.

The assertions (a), (b) and (d) follow from the definition of the
(k,\nu)
Beta function and assertion (1) in Proposition
3.4
. Assertion (c) follows from assertions (a) and (b). Assertion (e) follows from Proposition
3.8
. Assertion (f) follows from the definition and assertion (5) in Proposition
3.4
. Let us prove assertion (g).

Recall that

B_{k,\nu}(x,x)=\int_{0}^{\frac{\nu}{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}du.

Make the substitution
u=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\nu}{k}+t\right)
. Then

\displaystyle B_{k,\nu}(x,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{1-\frac{2x}{k\nu}}\int_{-\nu/k}^{{\nu}/{k}}\left(1+\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{2-\frac{2x}{k\nu}}\int_{0}^{{\nu}/{k}}\left(1-\frac{k^{2}}{\nu^{2}}t^{2}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{1-\frac{2x}{k\nu}}\frac{\nu}{k}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}s^{-1/2}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{1-\frac{2x}{k\nu}}B_{k,\nu}(x,\frac{k\nu}{2}).

The proof is complete.
∎

We deduce the following Legendre’s duplication-type formula.

Corollary 4.5
.

The following is true.

(18)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(2x)=\frac{2^{\frac{2x}{k\nu}-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{1/2}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right).

Proof.

From assertion (g) in the previous proposition, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}^{2}(x)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(2x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{1-\frac{2x}{k\nu}}\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(\frac{k\nu}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{1-\frac{2x}{k\nu}}\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{k}}\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right)}\sqrt{\pi}.

We have used that

\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(\frac{k\nu}{2}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{k}}\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{k}}\sqrt{\pi}.

Thus

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(2x)=\frac{2^{\frac{2x}{k\nu}-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{1/2}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right).

∎

4.2.
Some inequalities for the
(k,\nu)
Gamma function and the
(k,\nu)
Beta function

We start with the following observation. As
e^{-t}dt
is a probability measure on
(0,\infty)
, and

\int_{0}^{\infty}te^{-t}dt=1,

an application of Jensen’s inequality gives the following.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. Assume that
x>0
. Then the following hold.

(a)

If
0<x<k\nu
or
x\geq 2k\nu
, then

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\geq\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}.

(b)

If
k\nu\leq x\leq 2k\nu
, then

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\leq\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}.

We have the following result.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. Assume that
x>0
and
y>0
are such that

(19)

\left(x-k\nu\right)\left(y-k\nu\right)\geq(\leq)\,0.

Then

(20)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+y)\geq(\leq)\,\,\left(\frac{xy}{k\nu^{3}}\right)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)

or equivalently,

(21)

B_{k,\nu}(x,y)\geq(\leq)\,\,\frac{k\nu^{3}}{xy}.

Proof.

The result follows by applying Lemma
2.2
to the functions
h,f,g:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

h(t)=\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)e^{-t},f(t)=\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\quad\text{and}\quad g(t)=\left(\frac{k}{\nu}t\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}}

and using assertion (1) in Proposition
3.4
.
∎

The following is an easy consequence of the above.

Corollary 4.8
.

Suppose that
k\geq\nu>0
. Then
\ln\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)
is superadditive for
x>k\nu
. i.e.

(22)

\ln\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x,y)\right)\geq\ln\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\right)+\ln\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)\right)\quad\text{for all}\quad x,y\in(k\nu,\infty).

Another easy consequence is the following inequality.

Corollary 4.9
.

Suppose that
k,\nu>0
. Then for any
x>0
and any integer
n\geq 1
,

(23)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(nx)\geq(n-1)!\left(k\nu^{3}\right)^{1-n}\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\right)^{n}.

Let us explain the following other consequence.

Corollary 4.10
.

For any
x>0
, it holds that

(24)

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\leq k^{3/2}\nu^{5/2}\frac{2^{\frac{2x}{k\nu}-1}}{x^{2}\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right).

Proof.

Recall the duplication formula

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(2x)=\frac{2^{\frac{2x}{k\nu}-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{1/2}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right).

But by (
20
), we have that

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(2x)\geq\frac{x^{2}}{k\nu^{3}}\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\right)^{2}.

Putting these two observations together, we obtain

\frac{2^{\frac{2x}{k\nu}-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{1/2}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right)\geq\frac{x^{2}}{k\nu^{3}}\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\right)^{2}

from which the result follows.
∎

We have the following.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. For
x,y>0
, the following hold.

(a)

If
(x,y)\in(0,k\nu]\times[2k\nu,\infty)\cup[2k\nu,\infty)\times(0,k\nu]
, then

(25)

B_{k,\nu}(x,y)\geq\frac{\nu}{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-2}.

(b)

If
(x,y)\in[k\nu,2k\nu]^{2}
, then

(26)

B_{k,\nu}(x,y)\leq\frac{\nu}{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-2}.

Proof.

It is easy to verify that the function
\Phi_{a,b}(t)=t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}
defined on
(0,1)
, is convex if
(a,b)\in(0,1]\times[2,\infty)\cup[2,\infty)\times(0,1]
. It is concave if
(a,b)\in[1,2]\times[1,2]
.

Put
a=\frac{x}{k\nu}
and
b=\frac{y}{k\nu}
. Then
in case (a), we obtain using Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle B_{k,\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{k}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}(1-t)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{k}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{a,b}(t)dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{k}\Phi_{a,b}\left(\int_{0}^{1}tdt\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{k\nu}-2}.

The proof of the second assertion follows in the same way.
∎

Let us prove the following two-sided inequality.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. For
x_{1},x_{2},y>0
with
x_{1}<x_{2}
, the following holds.

(27)

\frac{x_{2}+y}{x_{1}+y}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}+1}<\frac{B_{k,\nu}(x_{2},y)}{B_{k,\nu}(x_{1},y)}<\frac{x_{2}+y}{x_{1}+y}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{x_{1}+y+k\nu}{x_{2}+y+k\nu}\right)^{\frac{y}{k\nu}}.

Proof.

By putting
a=\frac{x}{k\nu}
and
b=\frac{y}{k\nu}
,
we can reduce the matter to the usual Beta function
B(a,b)
. We then only have to prove that for
0<a_{1}<a_{2}
and
b>0
, we have

(28)

\frac{a_{2}+b}{a_{1}+b}\left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\right)^{b+1}<\frac{B(a_{2},b)}{B(a_{1},b)}<\frac{a_{2}+b}{a_{1}+b}\left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{1}+b+1}{a_{2}+b+1}\right)^{b}.

Recall that

B(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}dt.

Put
g(a,b)=B(a+1,b)
. Then

\frac{\partial g}{\partial a}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a}(1-t)^{b-1}\ln(t)dt.

Using that for
0<t<1
,

\frac{t-1}{t}<\ln t<t-1,

we obtain

-\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b}dt<\frac{\partial g}{\partial a}<-\int_{0}^{1}t^{a}(1-t)^{b}dt.

That is

-B(a,b+1)<\frac{\partial g}{\partial a}<-B(a+1,b+1)

or equivalently,

-\frac{b}{a}g(a,b)<\frac{\partial g}{\partial a}<-\frac{b}{a+b+1}g(a,b).

That is

-\frac{b}{a}<\frac{\frac{\partial g}{\partial a}}{g(a,b)}<-\frac{b}{a+b+1}.

Integrating from
a=a_{1}
to
a=a_{2}
, we obtain after simplification, that

\ln\left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\right)^{b}<\ln\left(\frac{B(a_{2}+1,b)}{B(a_{1}+1,b)}\right)<\ln\left(\frac{a_{1}+b+1}{a_{2}+b+1}\right)^{b},

that is

\left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\right)^{b}<\frac{B(a_{2}+1,b)}{B(a_{1}+1,b)}<\left(\frac{a_{1}+b+1}{a_{2}+b+1}\right)^{b}.

Using that
B(x+1,y)=\frac{x}{x+y}B(x,y)
, we obtain that the above is equivalent to (
28
).
The proof is complete.
∎

Let us prove the following inequality.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. Then for
y>x>0
, we have

(32)

B_{k,\nu}(x,y)\leq\sqrt{\pi}2^{1-\frac{2x}{k\nu}}\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{1/2}\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right)}.

Proof.

As the function
t\mapsto\Gamma_{k,\nu}(t)

t\mapsto\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}^{\prime}(t)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(t)}
is increasing. Hence for

\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}^{\prime}(t)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(t)}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}^{\prime}(t+a)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(t+a)}.

Integrating the above from
t=x
to

, we obtain

\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y+a)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+a)}.

In particular, for
a=x
, this reduces to

\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+y)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(2x)}

or equivalently,

\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(y)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x+y)}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(2x)}.

Multiplying both members of the above inequality by
\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)
, we are led to

(33)

B_{k,\nu}(x,y)\leq\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}^{2}(x)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(2x)}.

Recall with Corollary
4.5

\Gamma_{k,\nu}(2x)=\frac{2^{\frac{2x}{k\nu}-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)^{1/2}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right).

Taking this into (
33
), we obtain

B_{k,\nu}(x,y)\leq\sqrt{\pi}2^{1-\frac{2x}{k\nu}}\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)^{1/2}\frac{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right)}.

The proof is complete.

5.
The
(k,\nu)

(k,\nu)

In this section, we look at the
(k,\nu)
Psi function and its derivatives, we also introduce the
(k,\nu)
Zeta function and give its integral representation.

5.1.
The
(k,\nu)
Psi function and some of its properties

Definition 5.1
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. The
(k,\nu)
Psi (Digamma) function denoted
\Psi_{k,\nu}(x)
is defined as the derivative of
\ln\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\right)
.

We have the following.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. For any
x>0
, we have

(37)

\Psi_{k,\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{k\nu}\left(\ln k-\ln\nu-\gamma\right)-\frac{1}{x}-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{x+jk\nu}-\frac{1}{jk\nu}\right);

(38)

\Psi_{k,\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{k\nu}\left(\ln k-\ln\nu-\gamma\right)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-k\nu t}-e^{-xt}}{1-e^{-k\nu t}}dt;

(39)

\Psi_{k,\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{k\nu}\left(\ln k-\ln\nu-\gamma\right)+\frac{1}{k\nu}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-u^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}}{1-u}du.

Proof.

The first identity follows from the definition of
\Psi_{k,\nu}(x)
and the identity

\frac{1}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}=\nu^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}k^{-\frac{x}{k\nu}}\frac{x}{\nu}e^{\frac{\gamma x}{k\nu}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{x}{jk\nu}\right)e^{-\frac{x}{jk\nu}}.

For the second one, use the first one and that, applying the Monotone Convergence Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{x+jk\nu}-\frac{1}{jk\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{x+jk\nu}-\frac{1}{(j+1)k\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-(x+jk\nu)t}-e^{-(j+1)k\nu t}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-jk\nu t}\left(e^{-xt}-e^{-k\nu t}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-xt}-e^{-k\nu t}}{1-e^{-k\nu t}}dt.

The last identity follows from an elementary substitution.
∎

Let us prove the following about the anti-derivative of the
(k,\nu)
Digamma function.

Theorem 5.3
.

The function
\Psi(k,\nu,x)=\ln\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)\right)
is a solution of the following nonlinear partial differential equations

(40)

k^{2}\partial_{k}^{2}\Psi+2k\partial_{k}\Psi-x^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\Psi=-1-\frac{x}{k\nu}

and

(41)

\nu^{2}\partial_{\nu}^{2}\Psi+2\nu\partial_{\nu}\Psi-x^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\Psi=2+\frac{x}{k\nu}.

Proof.

Recall that

\Psi(k,\nu,x)=\frac{x}{k\nu}\left(-\ln\nu+\ln k-\gamma\right)+2\ln\nu-\ln x-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\ln\left(1+\frac{x}{jk\nu}\right)-\frac{x}{jk\nu}\right).

It follows that

\partial_{k}\Psi=\frac{x}{k^{2}}\left(\frac{\ln\nu-\ln k+\gamma+1}{\nu}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{x+jk\nu}-\frac{1}{j\nu}\right)\right);

\partial_{k}^{2}\Psi=-2\frac{x}{k^{3}}\left(\frac{\ln\nu-\ln k+\gamma+1}{\nu}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{x+jk\nu}-\frac{1}{j\nu}\right)\right)+\frac{x}{k^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{k\nu}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x}{(x+jk\nu)^{2}}\right).

Also,

\partial_{x}\Psi=\frac{1}{k\nu}\left(\ln k-\ln\nu-\gamma\right)-\frac{1}{x}-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{x+jk\nu}-\frac{1}{jk\nu}\right)

and

(42)

\partial_{x}^{2}\Psi=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(x+jk\nu)^{2}}.

One easily get that

k^{3}\partial_{k}^{2}\Psi+2k^{2}\partial_{k}\Psi=kx\left(-\frac{1}{k\nu}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x}{(x+jk\nu)^{2}}\right)

and so

k^{3}\partial_{k}^{2}\Psi+2k^{2}\partial_{k}\Psi-kx^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\Psi=-k-\frac{x}{\nu}.

That is
\Psi
satisfies (
40
).

The proof for the second equation follows in the same way.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Equation (
42
) also tells us that
\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)
is logarithmically convex. We even derive from it that

\Gamma_{k,\nu}^{\prime\prime}(x)\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)-\left(\Gamma_{k,\nu}^{\prime}(x)\right)^{2}>0.

From the same (
42
), we deduce that
\Psi_{k,\nu}(x)
is strictly increasing.

Let us now give some basic identities for the
(k,\nu)
Digamma function and its higher-order derivatives.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. The following are satisfied.

(a)

\Psi_{k,\nu}(x)-\Psi_{k,\nu}(k\nu-x)=-\frac{\pi}{k\nu}\cot\left(\frac{\pi x}{k\nu}\right),\quad x\notin k\nu\left(\mathbb{Z}^{-}\cup\{0\}\right)
.

(b)

\Psi_{k,\nu}(2x)=\frac{1}{k\nu}\left(\ln 2+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\nu}{k}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\Psi_{k,\nu}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\Psi_{k,\nu}\left(x+\frac{k\nu}{2}\right)
.

Proof.

The two assertions follow, respectively, from assertions (5) and (6) of Proposition
3.4
.
∎

We have the following.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. Assume that
x>0
.Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

(43)

\Psi_{k,\nu}(x+(n+1)k\nu)-\Psi_{k,\nu}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{x+jk\nu}.

Moreover,

(44)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\Psi_{k,\nu}(x+(n+1)k\nu)-\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln n\right)=\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right).

Proof.

Equation (
43
) follows from assertion (4) in Proposition
3.4
. We can also use (
37
).

From (
37
) and (
43
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Psi_{k,\nu}(x+(n+1)k\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{k,\nu}(x)+\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{x+jk\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k\nu}\left(\ln\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)-\gamma\right)-\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{x+jk\nu}-\frac{1}{jk\nu}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{jk\nu}.

Hence

\Psi_{k,\nu}(x+(n+1)k\nu)-\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln n=\frac{1}{k\nu}\left(\ln\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right)-\gamma\right)-\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{x+jk\nu}-\frac{1}{jk\nu}\right)+\frac{1}{k\nu}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}-\ln n\right).

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, it easily follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\Psi_{k,\nu}(x+(n+1)k\nu)-\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln n\right)=\frac{1}{k\nu}\ln\left(\frac{k}{\nu}\right).

The proof is complete.
∎

5.2.
(k,\nu)
Polygamma function and some inequalities

We start this part with a definition.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
m>0
be an integer. The
(k,\nu)
m-polygamma function
\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(m)}
is the
m^{th}
derivative of the
(k,\nu)
Psi function i.e.

\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(m)}(x)=\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(m)}(x).

From Proposition
5.5
, we obtain the following.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
k,\nu>0
, and let
m>0
be an integer. Then for
x>0
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(m)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{m+1}m!\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(x+nk\nu)^{m+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{m+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}e^{-xt}}{1-e^{-k\nu t}}dt.

From the above, we make the following useful observation.

(45)

\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(m)}\quad\text{is}\,\begin{cases}\text{increasing and concave}&\text{if m is even}\\
\text{decreasing and convex}&\text{if m is odd}\end{cases}

We have the following.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
k,\nu>0
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Put
\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(0)}=\Psi_{k,\nu}
and
\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(-1)}=\ln\Gamma_{k,\nu}
. Then for
0<x<y
, we have the following

(46)

\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(y)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(x)}{y-x}\leq\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)

and

(47)

\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)\leq\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(y)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(x)}{y-x}.

Proof.

To prove (
46
), we use the fact that
\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}
is concave and the Jensen’s inequality as follows

\displaystyle\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y-x}\int_{x}^{y}\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(y)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(x)}{y-x}.

The inequality (
47
) follows with the same type of arguments.
∎

Let us prove the following.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
k,\nu>0
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Put
\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(0)}=\Psi_{k,\nu}
and
\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(-1)}=\ln\Gamma_{k,\nu}
. Then for
0<x<y
, we have the following

(48)

\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(x)+\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(y)}{2}\leq\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(y)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(x)}{y-x}

and

(49)

\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(y)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(x)}{y-x}\leq\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(x)+\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(y)}{2}.

Proof.

We only prove the first inequality because the second one uses the same argument. For this, we consider the function

F(y)=\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(y)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(x)-(y-x)\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(x)+\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(y)}{2}.

Clearly,

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(y)-\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(x)+\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(y)}{2}-(y-x)\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(y)}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{y-x}{2}\left[\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(y)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(x)}{y-x}-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{y-x}{2}\left(\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(c)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(y)\right)

for some
c\in(x,y)
, thanks to the Mean Value Theorem. But by (
45
),
\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}
is decreasing. Hence
F^{\prime}(y)\geq 0
, that is
F
is an increasing function. As
x<y
, we deduce that
F(y)\geq F(x)=0
. That is

\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(y)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(x)-(y-x)\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(x)+\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(y)}{2}\geq 0

or equivalently,

\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(x)+\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(y)}{2}\leq\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(y)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m-1)}(x)}{y-x}.

∎

Let us also prove the following.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
k,\nu>0
,
\theta\geq 0
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then for
x,y>0
, and
r>1
, we have the following

(50)

\frac{\left[\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+x)\right]^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+rx)}\leq\frac{\left[\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+x+y)\right]^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+r(x+y))}

and

(51)

\frac{\left[\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+x+y)\right]^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+r(x+y))}\leq\frac{\left[\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+x)\right]^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+rx)}.

Proof.

We only prove the first inequality as the second one follows in the same way.

Let

F(t)=\ln\left(\frac{\left(\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+t)\right)^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+rt)}\right)=\ln\left(\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+t)\right)^{r}-\ln\left(\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+rt)\right),\quad t>0.

Then

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+t)}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+t)}-r\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+rt)}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+rt)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\frac{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+t)\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+rt)-\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+t)\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+rt)}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+t)\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+rt)}.

By (
45
), we have that

\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+t)\leq\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+rt)

and

\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+rt)\leq\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+t).

Hence
F^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
. That is,
t\mapsto F(t)
is increasing and so
t\mapsto\frac{\left(\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+t)\right)^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+rt)}
is increasing. Thus,

\frac{\left[\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+x)\right]^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+rx)}\leq\frac{\left[\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+x+y)\right]^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+r(x+y))}.

∎

The following is obtained as above.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
k,\nu>0
,
\theta\geq 0
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then for
x,y>0
, and
r<1
, we have the following

(52)

\frac{\left[\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+x+y)\right]^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+r(x+y))}\leq\frac{\left[\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+x)\right]^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m)}(\theta+rx)}

and

(53)

\frac{\left[\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+x)\right]^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+rx)}\leq\frac{\left[\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+x+y)\right]^{r}}{\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(2m+1)}(\theta+r(x+y))}.

5.3.
The
(k,\nu)
Zeta function

We now introduce the
(k,\nu)
Zeta function.

Definition 5.13
.

Let
k,\nu>0
. The
(k,\nu)
Riemann Zeta function, denoted
\zeta_{k,\nu}
is defined by

(54)

\zeta_{k,\nu}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(nk\nu)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}}},\quad x>k\nu.

The
(k,\nu)
Hurwitz zeta function is defined by

(55)

\zeta_{k,\nu}(x,s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(x+nk\nu\right)^{\frac{s}{k\nu}}},\quad x>0,s>k\nu.

We observe that for any integer
m>0
,

(56)

\zeta_{k,\nu}(x,(m+1)k\nu)=\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{m!}\Psi_{k,\nu}^{(m)}(x).

Also, applying Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem, we obtain

(57)

\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\zeta_{k,\nu}(x,s)=\zeta_{k,\nu}(s)\quad\text{for all}\quad s>k\nu.

We have the following equivalent definition.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
x>0
and
s>k\nu
. Then

(58)

\zeta_{k,\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(x)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{x}{k\nu}-1}\frac{1}{e^{(k\nu)u}-1}du

and

(59)

\zeta_{k,\nu}(x,s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{k,\nu}(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{s}{k\nu}-1}\frac{e^{-xu}}{1-e^{-(k\nu)u}}du.

Proof.

We note that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{s}{k\nu}-1}e^{-(x+nk\nu)u}du=\frac{1}{\left(x+nk\nu\right)^{\frac{s}{k\nu}}}\Gamma_{k,\nu}(s),\quad x\geq 0.

It follows using the Monotone Convergence Theorem that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k,\nu}(s)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(x+nk\nu\right)^{\frac{s}{k\nu}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{s}{k\nu}-1}e^{-(x+nk\nu)u}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{s}{k\nu}-1}e^{-xu}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-(nk\nu)u}\right)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{\nu}u\right)^{\frac{s}{k\nu}-1}\frac{e^{-xu}}{1-e^{-(k\nu)u}}du.

The result for
\zeta_{k,\nu}(x)
follows the same lines using this time that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-(nk\nu)u}=\frac{e^{-k\nu u}}{1-e^{-k\nu u}}=\frac{1}{e^{k\nu u}-1}.

∎

6.
Comparison of bounds of ratios of the Beta function

We compare here the bounds obtained for the ratio of the Beta function in Section 3.

6.1.
Comparing upper bounds in (
27
) and (
29
)

We have that the upper bound in (
27
) is better that the one in (
29
).

Proof.

Let us show that

\frac{b+y}{a+y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\left(\frac{a+y+1}{b+y+1}\right)^{y}<\left(\frac{a+y}{b+y}\right)^{y}

or equivalently,

\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\left(\frac{a+y+1}{b+y+1}\right)^{y}<\left(\frac{a+y}{b+y}\right)^{y+1}.

Put
f(y)=\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\left(\frac{a+y+1}{b+y+1}\right)^{y}
and
g(y)=\left(\frac{a+y}{b+y}\right)^{y+1}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\ln\left(f(y)\right)-\ln\left(g(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln a-\ln b+y\left(\ln(a+y+1)-\ln(b+y+1)\right)-(y+1)\left(\ln(b+y)-\ln(a+y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{t}dt-y\int_{a+y+1}^{b+y+1}\frac{1}{t}dt+(y+1)\int_{a+y}^{b+y}\frac{1}{t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left(-\frac{1}{t}+\frac{y+1}{t+y}-\frac{y}{t+y+1}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{a}^{b}\frac{y^{2}+y}{t(t+y)(t+y+1)}dt

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 0.

Hence
f(y)<g(y)
which is enough to conclude that the upper bound in (
27
) is better than the one in (
29
).
∎

6.2.
Comparing the lower bounds in (
27
) and (
30
)

We show that the lower bound in (
30
) is better than the one in (
27
).

Proof.

It is enough to show that for
y>0
,
0<a<b<\infty
,

b^{b-1}a^{1-a}(b+y)^{1-b-y}(a+y)^{a+y-1}>\frac{b+y}{a+y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{y+1}.

Put

f(y)=b^{b-1}a^{1-a}(b+y)^{1-b-y}(a+y)^{a+y-1}

and

g(y)=\frac{b+y}{a+y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{y+1}.

One easily checks that

\displaystyle h(y)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\ln f(y)-\ln g(y)=(a+y)\ln\left(1+\frac{y}{a}\right)-(b+y)\ln\left(1+\frac{y}{b}\right).

As the function
t\mapsto(t+y)\ln\left(1+\frac{y}{t}\right)
is strictly deacrasing, we conclude that
h(y)>0
for any
y>0
. Thus,
f(y)>g(y)
.
∎

Comparing the upper bounds in (
27
) and (

)

We only have to compare the following functions of

Conclusion

In this paper, we have defined a new two-parameter deformation of the Gamma function, associated Beta function, polygamma functions, and the the the the the the the Zeta function. We have provided properties of these functions and proved several inequalities, some of which improve already existing inequalities in the literature.

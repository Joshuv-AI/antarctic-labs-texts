Elucidating the pathogenic mechanism of a pedigree with complex rearrangements on chromosome 4 using optical genome mapping technology: a study on the genetics and functional pathways in a child with developmental delay
Authors: Jiangfeng Qin, Yanfei Zeng, Songqiang Qin, Wendan Wang, Jun Huang, Xiaobao Wei, Rongni Chang, Xiudan Zheng, Yuanxiu Li, Dejian Yuan, Xiaoni Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionChromosomal structural variations (SVs) are important causes of neurodevelopmental disorders in children, but traditional detection techniques often fail to accurately resolve the precise breakpoints and pathogenic genes of complex rearrangements. To apply optical genome mapping (OGM) detects SVs across the whole genome by high-throughput labeling of ultra-long (>150 kb) DNA molecules, high-resolution fluorescence imaging, and alignment algorithms using a reference genome. Its resolution is up to 500 bp and is especially effective in finding exact breakpoints and orientations of complex rearrangements. This provides unprecedented technical support for clinical diagnosis and research. To perform genetic analysis of a family with chromosome 4 abnormalities using optical genome mapping technology, aiming to uncover the underlying pathogenic mechanisms. By integrating functional pathway enrichment analysis, this study explores the genotype-phenotype correlation in the patient and provides a theoretical basis for clinical diagnosis and treatment. MethodsKaryotype analysis, multicolor fluorescence in situ hybridization (M-FISH), and OGM were performed on the proband and family members. Functional enrichment analysis was conducted using Metascape and GeneMANIA.ResultsThe results showed that OGM technology precisely located the breakpoints, revealing that the patient carried a maternally derived derivative chromosome 4 (der(4)), with three copies of the 1q31.3, 1q31.3-q41, and 1q43 segments (totaling 20.5 Mb), involving 319 genes. Metascape analysis indicated that the genes were significantly enriched in multiple biological processes and pathways, especially in immune-related pathways and nervous system development processes, with the complement activation pathway having the highest enrichment degree, with -log10(p) reaching 13.9. Genemania showed that the candidate gene network was significantly enriched in functions related to humoral immune regulation, complement system activation, and muscle structure development, with a co-expression ratio of 98.07%. ConclusionOGM technology can identify complex chromosomal rearrangements that cannot be detected by conventional methods and provides molecular evidence for the familial pattern of disease. Combined with functional pathway enrichment analysis, the study proposes that disruption of the “complement–neurodevelopmental axis” may be the main cause of the proband’s neurodevelopmental disorder. These findings offer family-level evidence supporting the clinical application of OGM.

Introduction

Neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs) represent a group of clinical syndromes characterized by intellectual disability (ID), global developmental delay (GDD), and social-cognitive impairments as core features, whose etiologies are complex and exhibit high genetic heterogeneity [1–3]. Research indicates that genetic factors are closely associated with NDD cases, with structural variations (SVs) accounting for approximately 15% of pathogenic genetic alterations. Notably, non-coding structural variants significantly contribute to NDD pathogenesis by dysregulating gene expression. Integrated multi-omics approaches can enhance diagnostic yield and provide novel pathogenic insights for undiagnosed cases [4, 5]. SVs—including balanced translocations, inversions, insertions, duplications, deletions, and complex rearrangements can directly induce neurodevelopmental abnormalities by disrupting gene dosage, perturbing chromatin 3D architecture, or truncating critical functional genes [6]. However, conventional genetic testing techniques such as G-banding karyotyping are limited by resolution (5–10 Mb), rendering them inadequate for detecting small insertions (< 5 Mb), complex intrachromosomal rearrangements, and balanced structural variants, frequently resulting in misclassification as variants of uncertain significance (VUS). Meanwhile, fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)—due to its probe dependency—fails to systematically screen for de novo rearrangements genome-wide. Furthermore, it lacks orientation information: while capable of confirming breakpoint existence, FISH cannot determine insertion fragment orientation or the topological configuration of complex rearrangements [7–9].

Recent advances in genomics technology have provided novel tools for resolving complex SVs. Although chromosomal microarray analysis (CMA) can detect unbalanced copy number variations (CNVs), it exhibits an inherent detection gap for balanced rearrangements. Consequently, 25%–50% of neurodevelopmental disorder cases remain without molecular diagnoses, thus severely constraining the implementation of precision medicine [10]. Although whole-genome sequencing (WGS) theoretically covers all variant types, its short-read nature inherently constrains the resolving power for repetitive genomic regions or breakpoint junctions in long-range rearrangements [11]. Previous studies have employed next-generation sequencing (NGS) to characterize breakpoints in chromosomal insertion cases, revealing complex fragment junctions and potential replication-based mechanisms [12–14]. These findings provide important insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying insertional rearrangements. However, NGS remains limited in resolving the spatial architecture of large-scale insertions and in characterizing complex repetitive regions. OGM leverages high-throughput labeling of ultra-long DNA molecules (>150 kb), high-resolution fluorescence imaging, and reference genome-based alignment algorithms to detect structural variations across the entire genome at resolutions down to 500 bp. This technology demonstrates particular proficiency in precisely identifying breakpoints and orientation in complex rearrangements, thereby providing unprecedented technical support for clinical diagnosis and mechanistic research [15–17].

This study focuses on a 5-year-old male patient presenting with severe global developmental delay and intellectual disability. Conventional karyotyping revealed an abnormal chromosome 4q (46,XY, der(4) ins(4;?)(q21;?)), though both parents exhibited normal phenotypes with an unclear inheritance pattern. To decipher the molecular basis of this aberrant chromosome and its phenotypic correlation, we employed an integrated approach combining optical genome mapping, multicolor fluorescence in situ hybridization (M-FISH), and functional pathway analysis. This strategy enabled the first elucidation of the pathogenic mechanism underlying the complex chromosomal rearrangement in this pedigree. Further functional enrichment analysis established genotype-phenotype correlations, providing pedigree-level evidence for the clinical translation of OGM.

Methods

Subjects

A 5-year-old male patient presenting with global developmental delay, intellectual disability, and motor developmental delay was recruited from Liuzhou Maternity and Child Healthcare Hospital in China. The proband and his family members served as research subjects for genetic testing and phenotypic characterization. The family pedigree includes phenotypically normal parents and an older sister, with no significant familial medical history documented.

Methods

Cytogenetic analysis

Chromosomal abnormalities in the proband and family members were screened using standard cytogenetic techniques. Peripheral blood lymphocytes were cultured in Dahui lymphocyte medium (37 °C, 5% CO₂) for 72 h to prepare metaphase chromosomes. After G-banding staining (Trypsin-Giemsa method), karyotype analysis was performed microscopically at a resolution of ≥ 400 bands, following ISCN 2024 guidelines.

For the initially detected chromosome 4 abnormality, multicolor fluorescence in situ hybridization (M-FISH) with 24 whole-chromosome painting probes (MetaSystems, Germany) was employed for validation. Probe mixtures were co-hybridized with chromosomal specimens (37 °C, 72 h). Fluorescence signals were captured using the Metafer scanning system, and IKAROS software was utilized to determine the origin and extent of translocations/insertions while excluding false-positive rearrangements.

OGM Detection and data analysis

Ultra-high molecular weight (UHMW) DNA was extracted from peripheral blood using the Bionano Prep kit, including leukocyte lysis, protein digestion, magnetic bead purification, DNA homogenization, and quantification. Genome-wide specific labeling was then performed using the Direct Label and Stain (DLS) method, in which DNA molecules were fluorescently labeled at specific sequence motifs by the DLE-1 enzyme, followed by dialysis to remove unbound fluorophores, backbone staining, and quantification of the labeled DNA. The labeled DNA was loaded onto chips, assembled with Clips, and run on the Bionano Saphyr system to generate single-molecule ultra-long DNA maps. Data analysis was performed using Bionano Access v3.8.1. The de novo assembly pipeline was applied to assemble ultra-long molecule data into consensus genome maps, which were then aligned to the reference genome (GRCh38/hg38) to detect genome-wide structural variants, including insertions, deletions, inversions, and translocations.

Bioinformatics and functional annotation

For copy number variations (CNVs) identified via optical genome mapping, initial annotation involved cross-referencing with the ClinGen Triplosensitive (TS) database (v3.0) to identify dosage-sensitive genes. Subsequent functional enrichment analysis was performed using Metascape, incorporating all genes within triplicated segments. Enriched terms were extracted from Gene Ontology (GO) biological processes, KEGG pathways, and Reactome databases, with statistical significance defined at P < 0.01 following Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple testing. Gene interaction networks were constructed via Genemania to explore functional associations, expression correlations, and potential synergistic mechanisms among these genes.

CNV characteristics

The three copy segment of chromosome 1 in the patient includes: the first segment is the q31.3 segment of chromosome 1 (chr1:196306701 − 198.315746, 2.01 Mb). This segment contains 21 genes including ASPM, CFH, CFHRI, CFHR3, CRBI, etc. Among them, 15 are protein coding genes. The second segment is the q31.3q41 segment of chromosome 1 (chr1:198465639–215100481, 16.63 Mb). This segment contains 152 protein coding genes, including ABHDI7AP3, ACTGIP25, ADIPORI, ADORA1, ANGEL2, etc. The third segment is the 1q43 segment of chromosome 1 (chr1:237.326.713–239195419.713,1.87 Mb). This segment contains 12 genes including KRTI8P32, RYR2, MIPEPP2, MICOIP38, ZP4, etc. Two of them are protein coding genes. The triplicated region did not overlap with ClinGen triplosensitive (TS) regions. Analysis using the DECIPHER database showed that among the genes located within the triplicated segment, 41 genes had pTriplo scores ≥ 0.68, including 12 genes with scores ≥ 0.94, suggesting that these genes may have strong triplosensitivity and represent potential novel candidate genes.Fig. 3Karyotype of the proband’s mother

Functional enrichment and interaction network

Metascape analysis (Fig. 7) revealed significant functional associations among the 319 genes within the triplicated segments. Enriched pathways and GO terms identified from the input gene set indicate potential regulatory roles across multiple biological processes. Heatmap visualization employs -log₁₀(P) as the statistical significance metric, with higher values denoting greater enrichment significance. The heatmap analysis is divided into two parts: one representing the “Selected GO Terms” (Fig. 7A), which highlights specific key pathways and biological processes enriched by the target gene list, and the other representing their corresponding “Parent GO Terms” (Fig. 7B) offering a broader, more generalized overview of the biological context. Together, these two levels of annotation provide both refined and global insights into the functional enrichment landscape. In the specific GO term and pathway enrichment analysis, the target genes were significantly enriched in key biological processes such as complement activation, cytolysis by host of symbiont cells, striated muscle cell development, negative regulation of mRNA catabolic process, and regulation of MAPK cascade. At the broader level, parent GO term enrichment analysis revealed that gene functions were primarily concentrated in the immune system process, with a -log10(P) value of approximately 14.0. Subsequently, enrichments were observed in the regulation of biological processes and in biological processes involved in interspecies interactions, with -log10(P) values ranging from 5.5 to 6.5.Fig. 4M-FISH analysis of the proband’s mother

GeneMANIA network

The GeneMANIA interaction network shows that candidate genes within the copy number variation regions are functionally enriched in the regulation of humoral immune response, complement activation, and complement regulation. Some genes may also be involved in muscle and cellular structural organization. The candidate genes form a strongly co-expressed network (98.07%) (Fig. 8).

Discussion

The case in this study involves a chromosomal abnormality caused by the insertional duplication of the 1q31.3–q43 segment into 4q21, resulting in delayed development and intellectual disability. The symptoms in early childhood are relatively recognizable, and long-term medical and supportive care is often required. Such chromosomal abnormalities can place a significant burden on both the patient’s quality of life and the family’s socioeconomic situation. Currently available diagnostic techniques, such as conventional karyotyping and M-FISH, have limitations in detecting complex rearrangements. Therefore, more advanced and comprehensive diagnostic technologies are needed to provide complete genetic information, ultimately facilitating effective clinical management and intervention [18, 19].Fig. 5Circular schematic diagram of OGM analysis in the proband’s mother

Functional enrichment analysis suggests that abnormal expression of candidate genes in the complement activation pathway may represent a key molecular mechanism underlying the patient’s clinical phenotype. Metascape enrichment analysis revealed that genes within the copy number gain regions were primarily enriched in the complement activation pathway, followed by cytolysis by host of symbiont cells, skeletal muscle development, negative regulation of mRNA catabolic process, and regulation of MAPK cascade.

Traditionally, the complement system—an essential component of the innate immune system—has been regarded as primarily playing a role in host defense against pathogens. However, emerging evidence over recent years has demonstrated that complement proteins are widely involved in the development of the central nervous system (CNS), including synaptic pruning, neuronal remodeling, and glial cell function regulation [21–23]. In particular, complement components play a critical regulatory role in synapse formation and pruning during early development. Aberrant expression of these components may lead to abnormal neural network development, excessive or insufficient synaptic connections, and erroneous wiring, ultimately resulting in neurodevelopmental disorders [24, 25]. Additionally, CD55 and CD59 are complement regulatory proteins, and abnormal expression of their genes can increase neuronal susceptibility to complement-mediated cytotoxicity, ultimately leading to neurodevelopmental abnormalities and neurodegeneration [26]. The study observed that the candidate genes were enriched in pathways related to cerebral cortex development and astrocyte differentiation, and abnormalities in these pathways may be involved in the mechanisms underlying the patient’s cognitive impairment [27]. In addition, the candidate genes were also enriched in pathways related to cell cycle regulation and signal transduction, including the MAPK signaling pathway, spindle formation, and protein monoubiquitination. These pathways are critically important for the division, migration, and differentiation of neural progenitor cells.

The results of the GeneMANIA network analysis suggest that the gene set may be involved in the regulation of humoral immune responses, complement activation and regulation, as well as in muscle and tissue structural development through the participation of certain genes—potentially contributing to motor development. These functional involvements may underlie the observed intellectual disability and developmental abnormalities. The network analysis further revealed that 98.07% of the genes are connected through co-expression relationships, indicating a high level of expression concordance during developmental stages and suggesting that these genes may jointly participate in key biological processes [28]. The clinical significance of this study lies in its potential to guide practice by enabling the early detection of chromosomal abnormalities associated with neurodevelopmental disorders through advanced technologies. This, in turn, can facilitate the development of intervention strategies for affected children, with the goal of improving their quality of life [4, 29]. Clinically, this finding highlights the importance of incorporating genomic analysis into diagnostic practice, with the aim of identifying genetic risks at an early stage and enabling timely intervention. Such an approach can lay the foundation for ongoing monitoring and treatment, provide direction for the clinical management of children with intellectual disabilities, and promote the advancement of precision medicine [30].

Therefore, abnormalities in the “complement–neurodevelopment axis” may represent a key biological pathway underlying the patient’s phenotype, suggesting that immune-related genes—particularly complement regulatory factors—should be included in genetic screening for children with neurodevelopmental disorders.

In summary, this study successfully resolved the inheritance pattern of a complex chromosomal rearrangement within the family using OGM, with its high resolution and precise breakpoint localization providing critical evidence for inferring the composition of the derivative chromosome. Furthermore, integrating functional enrichment analysis with phenotype database comparison can greatly enhance the clinical interpretation of CNVs and provide a theoretical foundation for subsequent functional studies using overexpression models and targeted drug screening.

However, this study also has certain limitations. First, the relatively small sample size may affect the generalizability and applicability of the results. Future studies with larger cohorts are needed to verify the reliability of the present findings, and caution should be exercised when extrapolating these results. Secondly, the study was conducted at a single center, lacking multicenter clinical validation, and did not further perform breakpoint-specific PCR and Sanger sequencing for verification. Conclusion

OGM detected one case of complex chromosomal rearrangement. Combined with functional pathway analysis, it was proposed that the trisomic gene cluster on chromosome 1 mediates the patient’s phenotype through the “complement-neurodevelopmental axis,” and the possible relationship between chromosome 4 variation and intellectual disability was also elucidated. This study provides data on the disease pathogenesis and offers a basis for precision therapy; it also provides clues for early prevention and intervention of intellectual disability. Looking ahead, this work can be expanded and further explored.

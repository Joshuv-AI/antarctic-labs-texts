A bibliometric analysis of the evolution of nanotechnology research in precision theranostics for nasopharyngeal carcinoma
Authors: Tong Fei Qu, Zuo Jie Li, Miao Miao Zhang, Ke Ke Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to analyze the global research landscape, collaborative patterns, and developmental trends of nanotechnology applications in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) using bibliometric analysis and knowledge visualization techniques.MethodsPublications from 1985 to 2024 were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection database. A bibliometric analysis was conducted using the R-package bibliometrix (version 4.5.0) to evaluate publication metrics, while knowledge graph visualization was performed using CiteSpace software (version 6.2.6) to identify research clusters, evolutionary pathways, and emerging topics.ResultsWe identified 321 relevant publications, demonstrating exponential growth (14.85/year, R2=0.913) with a significant increase after 2017. China dominated global research (72.59% of publications), while Western countries demonstrated higher citation impact. The most prolific institutions were Sun Yat-sen University (26 papers), Jinan University (21 papers), and Fujian Normal University (19 papers). The International Journal of Nanomedicine was the leading publication venue (15 papers). Three major research clusters emerged: Nanoscale diagnostic platforms employing SERS technology, targeted therapeutic systems incorporating folic acid modification, and bioinspired nanomaterials with exosomes exhibiting promising growth. The research evolved from basic nanomaterials (2002–2013) through multifunctional platforms (2014–2020) to precision medicine applications (2021–2024), with recent emphasis on overcoming treatment resistance.ConclusionNanotechnology in NPC is rapidly evolving toward integrated theranostic platforms; however, clinical translation requires addressing scale-up manufacturing and long-term safety assessment challenges.

Introduction

Nanotechnology, a leading interdisciplinary field of the 21 st century, is extensively utilized in cancer diagnosis and treatment [1]. The unique physicochemical properties—including size effects, surface effects, and quantum effects—allow nanomaterials to overcome the limitations of conventional diagnostic and therapeutic approaches, providing innovative strategies for precision cancer diagnosis and personalized treatment [2]. In malignant tumors, nanotechnology has substantially improved diagnostic and therapeutic outcomes through various mechanisms, including targeted drug administration, enhanced imaging contrast, and immune response stimulation [3, 4]. Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a malignant tumor with a distinct anatomical location and complex etiology, demonstrating notable regional prevalence in Southern China, Southeast Asia, and North Africa [5, 6]. Global data indicate that in 2020, there were approximately 133, 354 new cases of NPC, with approximately 80, 008 deaths, with over 40% occurring in East Asia [7]. According to the World Health Organization classification, NPC is primarily categorized into three types: keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma, non-keratinizing carcinoma, and basaloid squamous cell carcinoma. Non-keratinizing carcinoma is significantly associated with Epstein-Barr virus infection and is characterized by early metastasis and delayed diagnosis [8]. NPC diagnosis and treatment encounter numerous challenges. Diagnostically, due to its concealed anatomical location and atypical early symptoms, conventional endoscopic examination has notable incidences of missed diagnosis, causing discomfort to patients [9]. A definitive diagnosis frequently depends on histopathological examination of biopsy specimens; however, this method is invasive and difficult to implement for ongoing monitoring [10]. For treating locoregionally advanced NPC, concurrent chemoradiotherapy is standard. Recent trials show that adding gemcitabine-cisplatin induction chemotherapy before this approach increases 3-year recurrence-free survival to 85.3% from 76.5%, mainly by reducing distant metastasis (hazard ratio 0.51; P = 0.001) [11]. Furthermore, existing treatment regimens are frequently accompanied by severe adverse effects, including radiation-induced neural damage, endocrine dysfunction, and secondary malignancies, which severely affect patients’ quality of life [12].

Nanotechnology offers new strategies to tackle these clinical challenges. In diagnostics, nanomaterial-based biosensors and molecular imaging technologies facilitate early non-invasive detection and precise classification of NPC [13]. Researchers have explored SERS-based nanobiosensors with gold nanoparticles for NPC tumor staging via label-free blood plasma tests, achieving 83.5% accuracy for early-stage (T1) and 93.3% for advanced-stage (T2-T4) NPC compared to healthy controls, using principal component analysis and linear discriminant analysis [14]. The SPIO-erastin-PEG nanoplatform combines MRI capability with ferroptosis therapy, achieving 93% tumor inhibition in NPC models and extending erastin’s half-life from under 5 min to 1.25 h, with good biocompatibility. This highlights nanomedicine’s potential to improve NPC diagnosis and treatment, though more research is needed for clinical application (Fig. 1) [15].

Nevertheless, the swift proliferation of literature within this specialized area has resulted in an “information overload” that conventional reviews are insufficient to fully address. Although several narrative syntheses [16, 17] have provided summaries of specific subtopics, these qualitative studies are inherently constrained by subjective selection bias and are unable to objectively capture the broader structural evolution of the field. At present, there is a notable gap in our understanding of how research priorities have shifted on a global scale—particularly the transition from traditional imaging techniques to integrated nanotheranostics, such as UCNP-based platforms [18] and Gd2O3-based PET/MRI [19]—and the identification of critical barriers to global collaboration. To address this gap, this study offers the first comprehensive bibliometric analysis of nanotechnology in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) over the past three decades. In contrast to prior narrative reviews, this study employs quantitative knowledge mapping to achieve the following objectives: (1) to visualize the spatiotemporal distribution of global research and identify key collaborative networks; (2) to trace thematic evolution, thereby elucidating the emergence of frontiers such as ferroptosis and immunotherapy that are supplanting traditional topics; and (3) to forecast future trends, facilitating the transition from preclinical models to clinical application. This research endeavors to furnish a data-driven reference for clinicians and materials scientists, enabling them to discern high-impact directions within this rapidly evolving interdisciplinary domain.

Materials and methods

Literature search and screening

The Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) is globally acknowledged as one of the most important and authoritative databases, commonly regarded as the preferred database for bibliometric analysis [20]. The Science Citation Index Expanded (SCIE) and Social Sciences Citation Index (SSCI) in WoSCC are the primary repositories for literature retrieval. Because nanotechnology applications in NPC diagnosis and treatment involve numerous disciplinary fields, this study searched the SCIE and SSCI citation index databases in the WOS Core Collection as sources. To ensure comprehensive inclusion of high-quality academic publications, we utilized the “Topic” search in the Web of Science Core Collection to gather and archive data using predefined search terms. The search strategy was as follows: TS=(“nasopharyngeal carcinoma” OR “nasopharyngeal cancer” OR “nasopharyngeal neoplasm” OR “nasopharyngeal neoplasms” OR “nasopharynx cancer” OR “nasopharynx carcinoma” OR “nasopharyngeal carcinomas” OR “nasopharyngeal carcinoma” OR “human nasopharyngeal carcinoma” OR “nasal pharyngeal cancer” OR “nasopharyngeal tumor” OR “nasopharyngeal carcinoma tumor” OR “rhinopharyngeal carcinoma” OR “nasopharyngeal squamous carcinoma” OR “human nasopharyngeal cancer” OR “nasal pharynx cancer” OR nasopharyngeal carcinoma) AND TS=(nanodot OR nanoparticle* OR nanomaterial* OR nanotube* OR nanosheet* OR nanofiber* OR nanosphere* OR nanorod* OR nanowire* OR nanocrystal* OR nanocomposite* OR nanodevice* OR nanocluster* OR nanotechn* OR nanocarrier* OR nanowire* OR nanoliposome* OR nanoemulsion OR nanocrystal* OR nanoconjugate* OR nanogels* OR nanodiamond* OR nanoporou* OR nanosilver* OR nanopore* OR nanomicell* OR “nano size*” OR nanomedicine* OR nanofibrou* OR nanoplatform*). On June 3, 2025, we retrieved English articles published between January 1, 1985, and December 31, 2024, categorizing document types as articles and review articles, while excluding conference abstracts, editorial materials, proceedings papers, early access, letters, corrections, and book chapters. The inclusion and exclusion of papers were performed on the WoSCC website. These exclusions were implemented to ensure that the analysis concentrated on high-quality, peer-reviewed original research characterized by comprehensive data. We utilized the “Full Record and Cited References” feature to export literature records and formatted the output as plain text. Literature must include the basic elements of academic papers, including abstracts, author information, keywords, and references. Any literature lacking these elements was excluded. The records were imported into CiteSpace (version 6.2.6) for deduplication by comparing titles, authors, publication years, and DOIs. No duplicates were found, resulting in 321 articles for final analysis. The data utilized in this study came from public databases; hence, ethical approval was not required. Statistical analysis and visualization

This study employed bibliometric analysis and knowledge graph visualization techniques, utilizing the R package (version 4.5.0), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), and CiteSpace (version 6.2.6), to examine the developmental trends in the application of nanotechnology to nasopharyngeal carcinoma. First, the bibliometrix R package (version 4.5.0) was employed for basic bibliometric analysis, including statistics on annual publication volume, citation frequency, major journals, and country/region and institutional outputs, and Normalized Citation Local Score (NCLS) calculated. The document NCLS of a document is calculated by dividing the actual count of local citing items by the expected local citation rate for documents with the same year of publication. The MNCLS of a set of documents, for example the collected works of an individual, or published on a journal, is the average of the NCS values for all the documents in the set. In this study, the term “local citation set” is specifically defined as the assemblage of documents that have been retrieved and analyzed, with the exclusion of any external citations. The 321 standard format literature records (plain text format) obtained from the Web of Science platform were imported into the R environment using UTF-8 encoding for analysis and processing. In addition, VOSviewer was utilized to produce the overlay visualization of keyword co-occurrence.

Knowledge graphs were developed using CiteSpace (version 6.2.6) [21], employing a 1-year time slicing approach to maintain temporal granularity in rapidly evolving fields [22]. Noun-phrase extraction was utilized as the default method for term recognition [21], and burst detection was conducted using Kleinberg’s algorithm with a γ value of 0.5 to identify features exhibiting high intensity over finite time periods [23]. The preprocessing phase involved the removal of stop words and the harmonization of synonyms, facilitated by CiteSpace’s built-in module. The criteria for node selection were established to include the top 50 most frequently occurring items from each temporal segment (Top N = 50). Network simplification was achieved through the application of the Pathfinder and Pruning sliced networks algorithms, which served to streamline the network architecture and emphasize the most significant connections. The Link Retaining Factor (LRF) was configured to 3.0, the Look Back Years (LBY) parameter was set to 5, and the parameter e for Top N was assigned a value of 1.0.Network evaluation was performed based on betweenness centrality, while cluster quality was assessed using modularity Q (values greater than 0.3 indicate well-structured clusters) and silhouette S (values greater than 0.7 indicate high homogeneity), as reported by CiteSpace for each network [22]. The reporting process was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 guidelines to ensure a rigorous literature screening process and methodological transparency [24]. Additionally, PRISMA-S was employed to enhance the reporting quality of our search strategy [25]. To ensure robustness, analyses were repeated with 2-year slices and γ values ranging from 0.4 to 0.6, revealing that major clusters and themes remained consistent across these parameter variations.In the analysis of thematic evolution, the Stability Index (SI) and Weight Index (WI) were utilized to measure the extent of thematic inheritance and transformation across different temporal segments. A WI value of 1 denotes complete inheritance, whereas a WI value of 0 indicates total transformation. Furthermore, to maintain data consistency, synonymous keywords, such as “nasopharyngeal carcinoma” and “nasopharynx cancer,” were consolidated utilizing CiteSpace’s “Alias” function, facilitated by a manually curated thesaurus file.

Results

Scientific output

We retrieved 321 articles related to nanotechnology applications in NPC globally between 1985 and 2024. Since the first relevant publication in 2002, research output has demonstrated a linear growth trend, with an annual growth rate of 14.85% (R2 = 0.913, CI: 0.803–0.962, P < 0.001) (Fig. 3A), notably marked by a significant increase in publications after 2017, reaching an annual peak of 31 papers in 2021 (Fig. 3B). Simultaneously, annual total citations exhibited a gradual increase, cumulatively attaining 1701 times by 2024, with the highest average citations per paper recorded at 69.36 times in 2023 (Fig. 3C). Overall bibliometric indicators indicated that the research period involved 1585 authors, 189 source journals, an international collaboration rate of 14.33%, an average of 6.8 authors per paper, 910 research keywords, 14,434 references, an average publication age and citation count of 7.32 years, and 37.56 times per paper, respectively (Fig. 3D).

Publication output and collaboration network analysis by countries/regions

An analysis of SCIE and SSCI databases reveals that 321 publications in nanotechnology in NPC between 2002 and 2024 came from 54 countries/regions. China dominated the global research landscape with 233 papers (72.59%), leading in total citation frequency (6,083 times) and H-index (42). The United States (19 papers, 5.92%) ranked second; although its output was 8.15% of China’s, its average citation frequency (65.47 times/paper) was higher than China’s (26.11 times/paper), indicating its high-quality research characteristics. Iran (18 papers), India (16 papers), Canada (13 papers), Japan, and Taiwan (China) were among the top 10. Despite publishing fewer articles, Canada achieved an average citation frequency of 119.08 times/paper, far exceeding other countries, indicating its research quality advantage (Table 1). Visualization analysis revealed that China occupied a central hub position in the international collaboration network (centrality 0.60) [26], forming high-frequency collaborative relationships with countries, including Canada (12 times), the United States (7 times), and India (4 times) (Fig. 4A). China-Canadian collaborations excel in clinical resources and technology, with China providing patient samples and Canada offering advanced SERS diagnostics, supported by the China-Canada Health Program. China-U.S. partnerships focus on technology, with China handling samples and execution, while the U.S. contributes advanced tools like quantum dots. These are more varied than the SERS-focused China-Canadian links. Meanwhile, Chian-Indian collaborations, with only four publications, focus on basic nanoscience and drug delivery, lacking the strong clinical-tech integration found in North American partnerships. Temporal analysis revealed that after 2010, global publications exhibited fluctuating growth, with China consistently maintaining a dominant position (Fig. 5A), while the international scientific research collaboration network became increasingly diversified.

Institutional distribution and collaboration patterns

Institutional output analysis revealed that Chinese institutions predominated the field of nanotechnology applications in NPC research between 2002 and 2024. Sun Yat-sen University (26 papers, 8.1%), Jinan University (21 papers, 6.54%), Fujian Normal University (19 papers, 5.92%), and Southern Medical University (18 papers, 5.61%) were the top four, collectively contributing 26.17% of global research output. Regarding academic impact, Fujian Normal University (total citations 1,289, average 67.84 citations/paper, H-index 15) and Sun Yat-sen University (total citations 997, average 38.35 citations/paper, H-index 16) exhibited the most prominent performance. However, Shenzhen University, despite publishing merely 6 papers, achieved an average citation count of 68.17 times/paper, reflecting exceptionally high research quality (Table 2). Network analysis revealed that Jinan University (centrality 0.34) and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (centrality 0.32) held pivotal hub positions in the collaboration network (Fig. 4B), markedly surpassing other institutions, including Sun Yat-sen University (0.037), which had the highest publication count, highlighting their essential roles in facilitating academic exchange and resource integration within the field. The elevated betweenness centrality values suggest that these institutions serve a pivotal bridging function in facilitating the flow of information within the research network. Annual publication analysis revealed that the main research clusters emerged between 2017 and 2021, with several top institutions producing numerous high-impact results during this period, especially peaking in 2020–2021 (Fig. 5B). The three-field Sankey diagram further validated the international collaboration structure focused on China, extending to the Southeast Asia and Middle East regions (Fig. 6).

Journal distribution and academic impact

The distribution of articles highlights key journals, with the International Journal of Nanomedicine (IJN, Q1, Impact Factor:6.5, CiteScore:10.9) being the most frequent venue (15 articles), followed by JOURNAL OF MATERIALS CHEMISTRY B (Q2, Impact Factor:5.8, CiteScore:28.5, 7 articles) and Biosensors and Bioelectronics (Q1, Impact Factor:10.9, CiteScore:20.5, 7 articles) (Fig. 7A). IJN’s focus on nanomedicine, particularly in clinical applications like nanodiagnostics and drug delivery, aligns with NPC-nanotechnology topics. Despite having a lower Journal Impact Factor than the other two journals, IJN’s similar CiteScore reflects its strong impact in applied nanomedicine. This suggests researchers prefer journals that align with their work’s scope, with translational NPC research often choosing IJN.

Characteristics of core authors’ output and collaborative networks

Author productivity was analyzed using Lotka’s Law to understand the distribution of scientific contributions. The data showed a power-law-like pattern (R² = 0.879) in relation to the number of documents authored (Fig. 8A). However, a Chi-square test indicated a significant deviation from Lotka’s theoretical distribution (χ² = 235.165, P < 0.001), suggesting the empirical data does not strictly follow Lotka’s Law. The observed distribution had a sharper initial decline (81.3% vs. 63.8% for single-publication authors) and a slightly heavier tail at higher productivity levels. This suggests a concentration of low-productivity authors and a modest presence of highly productive ones, typical of emerging interdisciplinary fields with developing collaborative networks. Chen R and Feng SY were jointly the most prolific authors, each publishing 15 articles, with identical total citation counts (61 times, averaging 4.07 times/paper), closely followed by Liu T (14 papers) and Shakeri-Zadeh A (11 papers) [28]. Notably, despite Chen GN and Lin JQ having relatively lower publication volumes, their average citations per paper were 4.4 times and 4.78 times, respectively, indicating their research quality advantage; Xie MQ’s 8 papers published as corresponding author received 14 citations, exhibiting outstanding performance based on corresponding author impact. First author contribution analysis revealed that Feng SY published 6 first-authored papers and received 39 citations, holding a leading position in original research (Table 4). Temporal analysis revealed that research activity in this field has significantly increased since 2010, with productive authors, including Chen R, Feng SY, and Shakeri-Zadeh A, remaining continuously active recently (Fig. 8B). Collaboration network analysis revealed that Chen R, Feng SY, and Chen GN occupied central positions and formed a tight collaborative team, while researchers, including Liu T and Shakeri-Zadeh A, played bridging roles, connecting multiple research clusters (Fig. 8C).

Characteristic distribution of subject categories

Knowledge graph analysis revealed a multifaceted disciplinary network with 89 research categories and 55 connections (Fig. 9). “Nanoscience & Nanotechnology” was the primary research category (75 papers), followed by “Pharmacology & Pharmacy” (52 papers), “Materials Science, Multidisciplinary” (46 papers), “Materials Science, Biomaterials” (42 papers), and “Chemistry, Multidisciplinary” (40 papers). Discussion

Development dynamics and regional distribution characteristics of nanotechnology in NPC research

In contrast to the field of general cancer nanomedicine, which is predominantly led by Western countries, our analysis reveals a distinct “China-centric” landscape, with 72.59% of publications originating from China. This trend is largely driven by the epidemiological fact that over 40% of global nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) cases are located in East Asia [7]. Unlike comprehensive bibliometric analyses in oncology, which often highlight the dominance of the United States in basic research, our findings suggest a distinct regional specialization. Although China surpasses other countries in publication volume, research from Western nations, including the United States and Canada, typically achieves a higher average citation rate. This suggests a structural dichotomy in research focus: Asian research is frequently “clinical-problem driven,” emphasizing the application of established nanomaterials to large patient cohorts, whereas Western research is often “methodology driven,” focusing on the development of innovative synthesis techniques, such as quantum dots.

The pattern of fragmentation followed by consolidation reflects the early developmental stages observed in other nanomedicine subfields, wherein an initial diversity of research efforts is succeeded by the formation of predominant research consortia. Prominent institutions in South China serve as the cornerstone of the collaborative network, whereas North American partners often provide a disproportionately significant methodological influence. To address the observed “East-West gap” in impact relative to volume, future initiatives should progress beyond mere authorship co-occurrence and strive for comprehensive functional integration. This entails synthesizing Western foundational innovations with Eastern expertise in clinical translation.

Evolution of nanotechnology research themes

The analysis of keyword evolution revealed a distinct three-phase technological paradigm shift that directly corresponds to the increasing clinical demands in the management of nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC). The early exploration stage (2002–2013) focused on the synthesis and characterization of basic nanomaterials, culminating in the advancement of SERS technology. From a clinical perspective, this focus was directed towards addressing the pressing necessity of reducing the elevated rate of misdiagnosis associated with occult T1 lesions. The functional integration stage (2014–2020) marked a pivotal shift towards the development of multifunctional nanoplatforms. This transition reflects a fundamental change in clinical needs: from merely “detecting disease” to “guiding treatment decisions in real-time.” The folic acid-conjugated magnetic nanocarrier (CDDP-FA-ASA-MNP) developed by Xie M et al. [34] represented a typical achievement of this stage. By incorporating MRI contrast agents with drug delivery systems, these platforms achieved MRI-visible targeted therapy, effectively tackling the challenge of simultaneous diagnosis and treatment.

The precision medicine stage (2021–2024) exhibited a trend toward intelligent and personalized nanomedicine platforms. The emergence of “ferroptosis” and “exosomes” reflects a more profound engagement with tumor biology, particularly in addressing the challenge of radioresistance, which is the principal cause of locoregional failure [12]. Wang et al. [14] summarized research related to multifunctional nanoparticle platforms integrating radiosensitization, photothermal therapy, and diagnostic functions, significantly improving radiotherapy effects. The folate-targeted zinc oxide nanoparticles developed by Guo et al. [35] achieved co-loading of cisplatin and docetaxel. The thematic progression from diagnostic sensitivity to biological modulation mirrors the broader paradigm shift occurring within the field of precision oncology.

Technological breakthroughs and clinical value of nano-diagnostics and molecular imaging

In comparison to invasive tissue biopsies, nanodiagnostic platforms represent a paradigm shift towards non-invasive and repeatable evaluation. Nanodiagnostic technology, especially SERS-based platforms, is enhancing NPC screening and T-stage classification. Lin et al. [15] created a label-free blood plasma SERS platform using gold nanoparticles and principal component analysis, achieving 83.5% accuracy for T1-stage and 93.3% for T2-T4 stages in 120 samples with a 30-minute turnaround. From a clinical perspective, this differentiation is crucial as it determines the therapeutic approach: radiotherapy alone is indicated for T1, whereas concurrent chemoradiotherapy is recommended for more advanced stages (T2-T4). Accordingly, a proposed clinical workflow incorporates Surface-Enhanced Raman Spectroscopy (SERS) at key decision-making junctures: screening high-risk populations (EBV+), elucidating unclear endoscopic findings, and monitoring minimal residual disease (MRD) following treatment.

Theranostic nanoplatforms, which synergistically combine diagnostic and therapeutic functionalities, hold promise for enhancing decision-making in salvage treatments. Targeted systems, such as GE11-modified nanoparticles, synergistically combine therapeutic and imaging capabilities to effectively address the challenge of detecting residual tumors. Proposed clinical applications of these nanoplatforms include: (1) Pre-operative magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to delineate tumor margins; (2) Intraoperative fluorescence-guided evaluation; (3) Treatment monitoring through serial T2-weighted MRI. These applications collectively aim to decrease the positive margin rates in salvage nasopharyngectomy from the current 20–30% to below 10%. Nevertheless, widespread adoption necessitates the completion of Phase I-III clinical trials to confirm that enhanced imaging resolution leads to improved overall survival and cost-effectiveness. Additionally, these trials are essential for the development of standardized protocols for clinical implementation.

Delivery strategy innovation and efficacy enhancement mechanisms of nano-therapeutic systems

The prevalence of research on “nanocarriers” underscores the essential need to address and reduce the dose-limiting toxicities, such as ototoxicity, associated with conventional chemotherapy. Nanocarrier-targeted therapy occupied a dominant position in the co-citation network, with its novelty primarily manifested in two aspects: multiple targeted delivery and microenvironment responsiveness. The lipid-polymer hybrid nanoparticles (FA-GEF-Y90-LPNP) developed by Fu et al. [36] combined passive targeting with active targeting to facilitate the co-delivery of gefitinib and yttrium-90, offering a new strategy for combined radiochemotherapy. The peptide-guided supramolecular nanoparticles developed by Wang et al. [37], enhanced delivery precision and drug loading capacity through π-π stacking stabilization technology.

Intelligent response and multi-modal synergy are key features of new-generation nanosystems [38]. Recent reviews have elucidated those smart materials, which respond to specific physiological stimuli such as pH and enzymes, facilitate precise spatiotemporal control of drug release, thereby meeting the “smart” criteria of contemporary nanomedicine [38]. Zhang et al. [39] developed targeted mesoporous silica nanoparticles that facilitate the synergistic delivery of the hypoxia prodrug TH-302 and afatinib, to selectively target the resistant hypoxic core of tumors, offering a new approach for the combined treatment of nasopharyngeal carcinoma. Moreover, recent research on magnetic drug targeting has revealed considerable potential for increased efficacy. For example, doxorubicin-loaded superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles (SPIONs) have been demonstrated to enhance drug accumulation through magnetic guidance and to exhibit synergistic antitumor effects by inducing oxidative stress and G2/M cell cycle arrest [40, 41], thereby significantly surpassing the performance of free drugs. The isorhamnetin nanoparticles developed by Yang et al. [42], achieved targeted inhibition of NPC cells. These innovative strategies including emerging ferroptosis-inducing nanoengineering [43], improved therapeutic effects and significantly reduced systemic toxicity, thereby opening new avenues for the precision treatment of NPC. Nonetheless, addressing the disparity between preclinical efficacy and clinical application remains a critical priority, necessitating the development of more predictive models to effectively evaluate these complex formulations.

Research progress and clinical prospects of bioinspired nanomaterials and exosome technology

Bioinspired engineering constitutes a cutting-edge advancement in the field of nanomedicine, effectively addressing the significant limitation associated with synthetic carriers, namely the foreign body response. Bioinspired nanomaterial engineering, a significant cluster in the co-citation network, signifies the research frontier, with “exosomes” exhibiting substantial growth potential. Compared to synthetic nanomaterials, natural nanocarriers, including exosomes, possess significant advantages, including enhanced biocompatibility, robust targeting ability, and ease of penetrating biological barriers, making them particularly effective for delivering nucleic acid and protein drugs [44]. Exosome technology has revolutionized both diagnostics and therapy. In diagnostics, exosomes containing specific miRNA and protein profiles, such as miR-24-3p, can serve as liquid biopsy markers for early detection and molecular typing of NPC with high accuracy [45]. For instance, Ye et al. [45] found that miR-24-3p hinders T-cell function by targeting FGF11, aiding immune evasion and offering a prognostic biomarker. In EBV-associated NPC, exosomes from infected cells carry viral components like LMP1, useful for non-invasive disease monitoring. Therapeutically, exosome-mediated systems provide significant benefits over traditional nanocarriers, allowing for efficient and low-immunogenicity delivery of therapeutic agents. Recent research is increasingly concentrating on the engineering of these vesicles to circumvent drug resistance mechanisms that commonly obstruct synthetic carriers.

In addition to exosomes, various bioinspired platforms are facilitating the development of multifunctional therapeutic strategies. Innovative bioinspired nanomaterials, including the hyaluronic acid-modified carbon nanotube (MWCNT-HA) composite material developed by Guo et al. [46], facilitated photothermal therapy through near-infrared laser irradiation, inducing apoptosis in CNE-1 cells, providing a new nanomaterial platform for photothermal therapy of nasopharyngeal carcinoma. Similarly, core-shell structured lipid-polymer hybrid nanoparticles by Yu et al. [47] achieved co-loading of gefitinib and apatinib, indicating excellent antiproliferative activity and cellular uptake efficiency in CNE2 and SUNE1 cells. The exploration of exosomes further encompasses their role in immunotherapy and the mitigation of drug resistance. Regarding immune regulation, tumor-derived exosomes can serve as natural antigen delivery systems to stimulate specific antitumor immune responses, with stronger immunogenicity and lower toxicity than conventional adjuvants [48]. Emerging research focuses on engineering exosomes with siRNA or chemotherapy drugs to bypass drug resistance, utilizing their natural ability to efficiently cross biological barriers and deliver payloads to target cells.

Finally, cutting-edge platforms are progressing beyond basic ablation techniques to incorporate mechanisms for tissue repair. The comprehensive therapeutic platform of gelatin-sodium alginate hydrogel (Gel-SA) loaded with bismuth selenide nanosheets (Bi2Se3 NSs) developed by Ni et al. [49], achieved tumor ablation while protecting adjacent tissues, exemplifying the innovative concept of “treatment-anti-inflammation-tissue repair” integration. Despite significant potential, clinical translation encounters challenges, including standardized preparation, scale-up production, and safety assessment; overcoming these issues will be crucial for advancing clinical applications of bioinspired nanotechnology [50].

Clinical translation challenges and future development trends

The clinical translation of NPC nanomedicine encounters a significant challenge: the disparity between benchtop efficacy and industrial scalability. Our analysis reveals a notable paucity of keywords associated with “Phase III clinical trials” and “good manufacturing practice (GMP),” suggesting that the majority of nanotechnology research in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) predominantly remains at the preclinical stage, with minimal progression towards clinical development. Minor variations in preparation conditions can substantially impact critical quality attributes, making the establishment of reproducible manufacturing processes essential. For instance, although paclitaxel-loaded nano-apoliposomes have been shown to reverse cisplatin resistance in vitro and in vivo [51], their large-scale manufacturing and long-term safety profile require further validation. To address these challenges, two primary strategies are highlighted: (i) the implementation of microfluidic manufacturing techniques to ensure batch-to-batch consistency [52]; and (ii) the development of tiered, standardized toxicological frameworks that encompass evaluations from acute to chronic exposure [4]. Complex multifunctional platforms, such as FA MNP/CDDP/TFPI 2 [53], particularly necessitate such rigorous standardization to facilitate their transition from conceptualization to clinical application.

In the bioinspired sector, challenges related to yield and standardization continue to pose significant obstacles. Cellular nanoporation offers a solution to the yield issue by enabling the large-scale production of functional exosomes, thereby ensuring scalable and consistent product characteristics [44]. Simultaneously, adherence to established characterization criteria is essential for maintaining the identity, potency, and safety of these products throughout their development and testing phases [50]. Looking ahead, the field must transition toward adaptive intelligent nanosystems (e.g., stimuli-responsive carriers that autonomously adjust drug release based on tumor microenvironment pH or hypoxia) and multimodal imaging-guided theranostics. Ultimately, the integration of immune modulation with tumor microenvironment remodeling represents the next frontier for accelerating the safe translation of NPC nanomedicines.

Research limitations and improvement suggestions

This bibliometric analysis, though systematic, is inherently constrained by limitations in data coverage and methodological algorithms. The exclusive dependence on the Web of Science Core Collection and English-language articles introduces a selection bias, which likely results in the exclusion of a substantial corpus of native-language literature from regions with high incidence rates, such as China and Southeast Asia. This exclusion may lead to an underrepresentation of regional clinical insights. Additionally, temporal and algorithmic factors affect the precision of the findings. Specifically, the truncation of the citation window for publications from 2023 to 2024 restricts citation accrual, potentially leading to an underestimation of the prominence of recent research clusters. Despite efforts toward methodological standardization, minor inaccuracies in author disambiguation and keyword normalization (such as the distinction between “EBV” and “Epstein–Barr virus”) may still occur. However, sensitivity analyses have validated the stability of the principal clusters and co-citation patterns presented in this study. To address these limitations, future research should incorporate data from multiple sources, including patents and clinical trial registries, and utilize mixed-method approaches. This comprehensive strategy will enhance the assessment of technological maturity and the trajectory of clinical translation, ultimately advancing precision medicine in the management of nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC).

Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis of 321 publications from 2002 to 2024 elucidates the developmental trajectory of nanotechnology within nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) research, highlighting a distinct evolution through three paradigmatic phases: (1) the initial phase of exploration, which concentrated on SERS-based diagnostics for T-stage classification; (2) the phase of functional integration, which focused on MRI-guided theranostic platforms; and (3) the phase of precision medicine, which targets radioresistance through ferroptosis and exosome-based bioengineering. Although China leads in research output, accounting for 72.59% of publications, Western institutions exhibit a higher citation impact, underscoring a dichotomy between clinical application and methodological innovation.

Our analysis critically highlights a substantial translational gap: while folate-targeted delivery and bioinspired carriers show therapeutic potential, the lack of keywords such as “Phase III trials” or “GMP” suggests that most innovations remain at the preclinical stage. To address this gap, future research should focus on (1) scalable microfluidic manufacturing to ensure batch consistency, (2) standardized long-term toxicological frameworks, and (3) the development of adaptive, stimuli-responsive nanosystems that incorporate immune modulation. By offering evidence-based insights into these technological frontiers and challenges, this study provides a strategic roadmap for expediting the clinical translation of precision nanomedicine in the management of nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC).

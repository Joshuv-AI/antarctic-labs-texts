Evaluating the Efficacy of Ozone Therapy and Low-Level Laser Therapy in Treating Premalignant and Malignant Lesions: A Systematic Review
Authors: E Gracelin Ranjitha, Shruthi Sridhar, Angeline Deepthi, J. John H. Deepak
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Premalignant lesions and conditions are comparably frequent, arising in less than 3% of the general public and a possible objective for cancer prevention. In a workshop conducted by WHO in 2005, the topic of discussion was “Oral lesion with a predisposition to malignant transformation”; thus, they proposed the term “Potentially Malignant Disorders [OPMDs]”. Therefore, patients evaluated with OPMDs may have an inclined vulnerability to develop lesion anywhere in their mouth during their lifetime. There are various treatment modalities in treating OPMDs, among which the looming and imminent treatment modality is “Laser therapy” using “Low level laser therapy [LLLT] and Ozone therapy”.Aims and Objectives:Evaluating the efficaciousness of LLLT and ozone therapy in treating OPMDs.MethodologyThis systematic review was performed using PRISMA guidelines. Systematic search of the literature was done with specific MESH keywords.Results:Based on the preformatted criteria, 12 articles were scrutinized and critically analyzed in the PICO format. Therefore, LLLT and ozone therapy have proven to be beneficial in treating premalignant lesions.Conclusion:Various treatment modalities exist in treating OPMDs; one of the less ventured and yet promising modalities is laser irradiation using LLLT and ozone therapy.

INTRODUCTION

Premalignant lesion entails an extensive range of pathology and combat in a large patient population.[1] OPMD (oral potentially malignant disorders) is recently defined “as a group of oral mucosal lesions with an increased risk of malignant transformation”.[2] The global prevalence of OPMD is 4.57% in the Asian population with increased male predilection.[3] The causative factors of “OPMD” are correlated with tobacco chewing and deficiency of certain nutrients and minerals. Other etiological factors reported are chronic friction, alcohol, tobacco, and so on.[4] Treating OPMDs encompasses a wide range of modalities; amid them, one of the looming and imminent treatment modalities is laser irradiation using “Low level laser therapy” and “Ozone therapy”. Researches on their efficacy are still being done. They could be a light of hope among the current therapeutic approaches in treating oral potentially malignant disorders.

Low-level laser therapy (LLLT) is a nonionizing radiation with nil genetic complications and is completely noninvasive and painless. An additional non-pharmacological-based emerging modality in the field of dentistry is ozone therapy. Its use in oral mucosal lesions is also emerging venture, and researches based on that are still being done to probe more in harnessing its full use.

LLLT and ozone therapy are an undoubted emerging treatment modality in the novel arena of dentistry. Even though there are a diverse number of studies available, a critical evaluation is inevitable to analyze the efficacy and effectiveness of both in the area of research. A systematic review will comprehensively evaluate the results of the study rather than a conventional literature review, and this could be a prompt eye opening insight in the field of treating OPMDs in dentistry.

RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

In our systematic review, “The Preferred Reporting Items for systematic review and meta-analyses (PRISMA) guidelines were used”. This systematic review was framed using “population, intervention, comparison, and outcome (PICO) framework”. The “PICO criteria” were as follows: P, patients with premalignant and malignant lesions; I, LLLT and ozone therapy; C, comparison between LLLT and ozone therapy; and O, effectiveness of pain relief, reduction in size, and a decrease in the recurrence of the lesions.

In September 2023, an invasive systematic search of the literature was conducted from the databases of “Pubmed”, “Pubmed Central”, “Cochrane Library”, and “Medline”, and a concluding random search was also done in “Google Scholar” published from 2010 to 2024 with specific keywords. The keywords “Low Level Laser Therapy, LLLT, Ozone therapy, OPMD, Premalignant lesions” were used. The Boolean logic “AND” was used between “LLLT and Premalignant lesions” and “LLLT AND Ozone Therapy in Premalignant lesions”, which furnished more results than combinations from other keywords in the database.

The inclusion criteria included original articles; clinical trials that evaluated the potency of LLLT and ozone therapy in patients with premalignant and malignant lesions; all clinical studies including case series, case reports, and cohort study only if the LLLT or ozone therapy was used as a treatment modality in treating patients; and free articles and articles in English. The exclusion criteria were unpublished data, newsletters, review articles, letter to the editor, and articles that were in languages other than English. The process of selecting the articles was conducted. The reviewed articles were analyzed to assure that they were pertinent to the aim of this study.

RESULTS

From this systematic search of the database, a total of 756 results were procured and the references of the abovementioned articles were also acquired if it was relevant to the review. A total of 744 articles were excluded, and therefore, 12 articles were attained for further analysis based on preformed inclusion and exclusion criteria. DISCUSSION

Early detection of premalignant lesion is inevitable to prevent the risk of malignant transformation; if left undiagnosed, managing them would be challenging. A broad array of modalities are attainable in the management of OPMDs, including topical and systemic corticosteroids, phytochemicals like retinoids, phenols and flavanoids, and surgical intervention.[2] In recent years, newer treatment modalities like photodynamic therapy, ozone therapy, and LLLT have evolved into presence to provide diagnostical relief to the patients and an expeditious return to normal lifestyle.

Laser is considered a great novelty that serves diverse fields including medicine, surgery, dentistry, and cosmetics. With the prevailing technical advance, many types of laser are introduced. Recently, LLLT has become an area of interest by many researchers because of its many advantageous characteristics. One of these characteristics is its antiinflammatory action and ability of increasing collagen production, reducing the inflammatory exudation and enhancing revascularization and epithelization.[5] Ozone is a blue gas existing sufficiently in the stratosphere with a concentration of less than 20 mg/m. Ozone has a higher oxidative potential than chlorine when used as an antimicrobial agent. The use of ozone in dental and medical fields is owing to its strong oxidant property.[6]

Both ozone and LLLT are novel and looming treatment modalities in the treatment of premalignant and malignant lesions. In order to compare the potential outcomes of both the varied yet promising treatment entities, this systematic review was undertaken.

A study performed by Agha et al. (2012)[7] compared the efficacy of “low level laser (LLLT) and Co2 laser” in subjects with oral lichen planus in 28 patients. There was a significant decline in pain, and the extent of the lesion was higher with patients treated with LLLT in all follow-up stages. A study conducted by Jajarm et al. (2011),[8] compared the efficacy of “Low Intensity laser and Topical Corticosteroids” in the management of erosive–atrophic oral lichen planus with 30 adult patients. Reductions in pain and lesion size were observed in both case and control groups. LLLT was as efficacious as and comparable with topical steroid therapy in the management of OLP. A similar study[9] was conducted in 24 patients with symptomatic lichen planus who were subjected to laser irradiation weekly twice and topical corticosteroids with 0.1% triamcinolone acetonide orabase. Prior to treatment, there was no significant difference among the two groups. Post intervention, the corticosteroids group showed statistically significantly lesser mean TNF-α levels when compared to the laser group.

Another study performed by kazancioglu HO et al. in (2015)[10] compared the effectiveness of “Low Level Laser therapy and Ozone therapy” in the management of oral lichen planus with 120 adult patients. Significant improvement in pain and a reduced lesion size were observed in corticosteroid and ozonated groups. Ozone laser and steroid management were more effective than 808 nm LLLT. A study by Darenskaya MA et al.[11] in 2023 compared the efficacy of collective application of “cryosurgical method and local ozone therapy” in 33 patients with leukoplakia. Significant improvement in pain at the 3rd day of intervention was observed, and the highest rate of epithelization was noted.

CONCLUSION

To prove the effectiveness of “Low Level Laser Therapy” and “Ozone Therapy” in treating premalignant and malignant lesions, a “systematic review” was done and a “meta-analysis” was also planned. The studies related to ozone therapy and premalignant lesions so far available were meager and have heterogenicity like the laser irradiation; different forms of ozone therapy application and the follow-up period were seen while analyzing the articles, and thus, literature data for a stronger meta-analysis were not feasible. “The Low Level Laser Therapy” and “Ozone Therapy” have proven to be beneficial in treating premalignant lesions. They have an outstanding biostimulating antioxidant property with no adverse effects.

With the limitations of the present analysis, we conclude that further studies are warranted, which could compare the novel treatment approaches in larger patient data with a long-term follow-up, which will provide a definitive clarity of entrusting these readily available and innovative treatment options in near future in the management of OPMDs.

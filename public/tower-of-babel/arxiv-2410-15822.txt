Introduction

One of the main questions of computational learning theory is how efficiently we can learn an unknown object that is promised to have some structure. Two of the most studied structured objects are juntas, which are multi-bit or multi-qubit objects where only a few of the bits or qubits are relevant, and constant-depth circuits.

Our learning algorithms in a nutshell

All of our algorithms are refinements of the

of Linial, Mansour, and Nisan
[

\displaystyle f\left\lparen x\right\rparen=\sum_{s\in\left[L\right]}\widehat{f}\lparen S_{s}\rparen\prod_{i\in S_{s}}x_{i}

for some
S_{s}\subseteq[n]
with
\lvert S_{s}\rvert\leq d
. First, we will see how the low-degree algorithm would perform to learn
f
from samples

where

\{-1,1\}^{n}

Low-degree algorithm

Step 1.
For every
\lvert S\rvert\leq d
, obtain
\widehat{f}^{\prime}(S)
that approximates
\widehat{f}\lparen S\rparen
up to error
\sqrt{\varepsilon/n^{d}}
.

Output.
We output
f^{\prime}\lparen x\rparen=\sum_{\lvert S\rvert\leq d}\widehat{f}^{\prime}\lparen S\rparen\prod_{i\in S}x_{i}
.

It is well-known that with
\lparen 1/\alpha^{2}\rparen\cdot\log\lparen M\rparen
samples one can estimate
M
Fourier coefficients of

\lparen n^{d}/\varepsilon^{2}\rparen\cdot\log\lparen n^{d}\rparen

\displaystyle\sum_{\left\lvert S\right\rvert\leq d}\left\lvert\widehat{f}\left\lparen S\right\rparen-\widehat{f}^{\prime}\left\lparen S\right\rparen\right\rvert^{2}\leq\sum_{\lvert S\rvert\leq d}\frac{\varepsilon}{n^{d}}\leq\varepsilon,

where in the first inequality we have used the guarantees of Step 1, and in the second that
\lvert\{\lvert S\rvert\leq d\}\rvert\leq n^{d}

\mbox{\rm Pr}[f\lparen x\rparen\neq\sign\lparen f^{\prime}\lparen x\rparen\rparen]\leq\varepsilon

However, note that the low-degree algorithm does not use the fact that

Step 1.
For every

Note that Step 1 now just requires

\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{and}\quad Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

form an orthonormal basis for this space.
The Pauli expansion of a matrix
M
is given by

M=\sum_{P\in\left\{I,X,Y,Z\right\}^{\otimes n}}\widehat{M}\left\lparen P\right\rparen P,

(3)

where
\widehat{M}\lparen P\rparen=\langle P,M\rangle
are Pauli coefficients of
M
. We will refer to the collection of non-zero Pauli coefficients as the Pauli spectrum of
M
.
As
\{P\}_{P\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}}
is an orthonormal basis, we have Parseval’s identity

\left\langle M,M\right\rangle=\sum_{P\in\left\{I,X,Y,Z\right\}^{\otimes n}}\left\lvert\widehat{M}\left\lparen P\right\rparen\right\rvert^{2}.

Given a matrix
M
, its degree is the minimum
d
such that
\widehat{M}\lparen P\rparen=0
for any
P
that takes more than
d
times a non-identity value. This notion of degree for matrices generalizes the classical notion of Fourier degree. For an extensive introduction to Pauli analysis, see
[
22
]
.

2.3
Concentration inequalities

We state a few concentration inequalities that we often use.

Lemma 7

(Hoeffding bound)
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
be independent-random variables that satisfy
-a_{i}\leq\lvert X_{i}\rvert\leq a_{i}
for some
a_{i}>0
.
Then, for any
\tau>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Pr}\left[\left\lvert\sum_{i\in\left[m\right]}X_{i}-\sum_{i\in\left[m\right]}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right]\right\rvert>\tau\right]\leq 2\exp\left\lparen-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2\left\lparen a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{m}^{2}\right\rparen}\right\rparen.

Lemma 8

(Bernstein inequality)
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
be independent-random variables with
\lvert X_{i}\rvert\leq M
for some
M>0
. Then,

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Pr}\left[\left\lvert\sum_{i\in\left[m\right]}X_{i}-\sum_{i\in\left[m\right]}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right]\right\rvert>\tau\right]\leq 2\exp\left\lparen-\frac{\tau^{2}/2}{\sum_{i\in\left[m\right]}\mathrm{Var}\left[X_{i}\right]+\tau M/3}\right\rparen.

2.4
Very brief introduction to
\mathsf{QAC}^{0}

A
\mathsf{QAC}^{0}
is a circuit composed of single-qubit gates and Toffoli gates, which are the unitaries defined via

\displaystyle\left\lvert x_{1},\dots,x_{l},b\right\rangle\mapsto\left\lvert x_{1},\dots,x_{l},b\cdot\and\left\lparen x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right\rparen\right\rangle,

where here
x_{1},\dots,x_{l},b\in\{-1,1\}

\and(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})=-1
if and only if
x_{1}=\dots=x_{l}=-1

\lparen n+a+1\rparen
-qubit

n

\lparen a+1\rparen
qubits are initialized in a fixed state
\sigma

\mathsf{QAC}^{0}
circuit defines an
n

1

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen=\Tr_{\left[n+a\right]}\left[U\left\lparen\rho\otimes\sigma\right\rparen U^{\dagger}\right],

where
U
is the unitary implemented by the circuit and
\Tr_{[n+a]}
is the trace with respect to the input and auxiliary qubits. The Choi state of a
\mathsf{QAC}^{0}
circuit is the Choi state of its correspondent channel, namely the
\lparen n+1\rparen
-qubit state

\displaystyle\rho_{\Phi_{\sigma}}=\Phi_{\sigma}\otimes I_{n}\left\lparen{\lvert\mathrm{EPR}_{n}\rangle\langle\mathrm{EPR}_{n}\rvert}\right\rparen,

The original motivation when Moore introduced of
\mathsf{QAC}^{0}

[
23
]
, namely to approximate
n
-to-
1

\displaystyle\Phi_{f}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen=\sum_{x\in\{-1.1\}^{n}}\langle x\rvert\rho\lvert x\rangle\lvert f\lparen x\rparen\rangle\langle f\lparen x\rparen\rvert.

It is easy to check that the Choi state of these channels is given by

\rho_{f}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left\lparen I^{\otimes\left\lparen n+1\right\rparen}+\sum_{S\subseteq\left[n\right]}\widehat{f}\left\lparen S\right\rparen Z_{S}\otimes Z\right\rparen,

(4)

where

. Hence, for

2^{n}\left\lvert\rho_{f}-\rho_{g}\right\rvert_{F}^{2}=2^{2n}\sum_{P\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes\left\lparen n+1\right\rparen}}\left\lvert\widehat{\rho}_{f}\left\lparen P\right\rparen-\widehat{\rho}_{g}\left\lparen P\right\rparen\right\rvert^{2}=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[n\right]}\left\lvert\widehat{f}\left\lparen S\right\rparen-\widehat{g}\left\lparen S\right\rparen\right\rvert^{2}=\mbox{\rm Pr}\left[f\left\lparen x\right\rparen\neq g\left\lparen x\right\rparen\right],

(5)

where in the first equality we have used Parseval’s identity, in the second
Equation

4
and the last equality is elementary. From

4
follows that learning the Choi state of a
\mathsf{QAC}^{0}
circuit in the
2^{n}
-Frobenius norm is a pretty natural problem.

7

sets of size at most

(6)

For every

\widehat{p}^{\prime\prime}\left\lparen S\right\rparen=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }|\widehat{p}^{\prime}(S)|\leq\varepsilon/(2\cdot 2^{n}\cdot\sqrt{2^{k}}),\\

\displaystyle\left\lVert p-p^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L_{2}}^{2}

Remark 9
.

The algorithm described in the proof of

k

depends on. We consider

p^{\prime}
to these variables and round all its values to
0
if they are negative.
This step only takes time
O\lparen 2^{k}\rparen
, and it does not harm the approximation because the range of
p
is contained in
\mathopen{[}0,\infty\mathclose{)}
. Let
p^{\prime\prime}|_{K}\colon\{-1,1\}^{k}\to\mathopen{[}0,\infty\mathclose{)}
the resulting function and
p^{\prime\prime}\colon\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathopen{[}0,\infty\mathclose{)}\colon x\to p^{\prime\prime}|_{K}(x_{K})
its extension to
n

We define

. As

\left\lvert 1-C\right\rvert=\left\lvert\sum_{x\in\left\{-1,1\right\}^{n}}p\left\lparen x\right\rparen-\sum_{x\in\left\{-1,1\right\}^{n}}p^{\prime\prime}\left\lparen x\right\rparen\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert p-p^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert_{\ell 1}\leq\varepsilon.

\left\lVert p^{\prime\prime\prime}-p\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}\leq\left\lVert p^{\prime\prime\prime}-p^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}+\left\lVert p^{\prime\prime}-p\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}\leq\left\lparen 1-\frac{1}{C}\right\rparen+\varepsilon\leq O\left\lparen\varepsilon\right\rparen,

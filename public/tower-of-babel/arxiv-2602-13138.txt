Exceptional versus τ \tau -exceptional sequences for the Auslander algebra of K ⁡ [ x ] / ( x t ) K[x]/(x^{t})
Maximilian Kaipel

Abstract
. For 𝒜 t \mathscr{A}_{t} , the Auslander algebra of K ⁡ [ x ] / ( x t ) K[x]/(x^{t}) , it is shown that every complete exceptional sequence of 𝒜 t \mathscr{A}_{t} -modules is a complete τ \tau -exceptional sequence. Moreover, it is established that the mutation of complete τ \tau -exceptional sequences generalises the mutation of complete exceptional sequences in the category of 𝒜 t \mathscr{A}_{t} -modules.



1.
Introduction

Exceptional sequences are classical objects in algebraic geometry and representation theory, which were introduced by Rudakov’s school in the study of vector bundles
[
22
,
23
,
43
]
. These sequences live in the triangulated categories arising naturally, as bounded derived categories, in these mathematical areas. They consist of exceptional objects, that is, objects without self-extensions and with simple endomorphism algebras. An exceptional sequence is an ordering of such objects so that
\Hom
and
\Ext
vanish in one direction. The remarkable power of an exceptional sequence is most apparent when it generates the entire ambient triangulated category, in which case the sequence is called
complete
. Complete exceptional sequences induce semi-orthogonal decompositions of triangulated categories
[
5
]
and can be used to construct stability conditions on triangulated categories
[
36
]
.

For these reasons, the pursuit of triangulated categories admitting complete exceptional sequences has been a long-standing endeavour for researchers since the 1970s
[
4
,
32
,
16
,
33
,
41
,
15
]
. Complete exceptional sequences are also closely connected to the theory of highest weight categories and quasi-hereditary algebras
[
34
]
. One particularly important example of such a quasi-hereditary algebra is the Auslander algebra
\mathscr{A}_{t}
of
K[x]/(x^{t})
for
t\geq 1
and
K
an algebraically closed field. This finite dimensional algebra arises when studying actions of linear groups on flags
[
25
, Sec. 4]
and is related to chains of
(-2)
-curves on smooth projective surfaces
[
28
, Sec. 3.2]
. Tilting modules and the quasi-hereditary structure of the algebra
\mathscr{A}_{t}
were investigated in
[
6
]
and complete exceptional sequences in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
, the category of finite dimensional right
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules, were characterised in
[
27
]
. The algebra
\mathscr{A}_{t}
is also closely related to the preprojective algebra of type
A

[
42
]
.

Inspired by the introduction of cluster algebras
[
19
]
and their associated combinatorics, the theory of tilting modules over finite dimensional algebras was extended with great success using Auslander–Reiten theory
[
1
]
. Within this new framework of
\tau
-tilting theory, many classical concepts from representation theory have successfully been generalised to all finite dimensional algebras. Especially the combinatorial aspects of classical tilting theory behave best for hereditary algebras, but generally break beyond this setting. Here, support
\tau
-tilting modules generalise tilting modules and resolve this issue.

Complete exceptional sequences of modules exist for hereditary algebras
[
16
]
but generally fail to exist for arbitrary finite dimensional algebras. To always have complete sequences, the authors of
[
14
]
introduced
\tau
-exceptional sequences and showed that these two notions coincide for hereditary algebras. However, in contrast to the relationship between tilting and
\tau
-tilting modules, a complete exceptional sequence (if it exists) is not necessarily a complete
\tau
-exceptional sequence, see
Remark

3.8
. In fact, essentially no relationship between the two types of sequences is known beyond hereditary algebras. The only exception are tensor algebras of hereditary path algebras with finite dimensional commutative local
K
-algebras, whose
\tau
-exceptional sequences are in bijection with those of the hereditary path algebra
[
40
]
. The first main theorem of this paper establishes such a relationship for the algebra
\mathscr{A}_{t}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
Theorem

3.6
)
Every complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
is a
\tau
-exceptional sequence. More precisely, there is an explicit bijection

\Phi:\{\text{basic tilting $\mathscr{A}_{t}$-modules}\}\longrightarrow\{\text{complete exceptional sequences in $\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}$}\},

given by the bijection between TF-ordered
\tau
-tilting modules and complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences of
[
37
]
.

With this result as a starting point it is natural to compare the mutation theories of complete exceptional sequences and complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences. Both mutation operations are defined via a mutation operation on two consequence terms in a complete (
\tau
-)exceptional sequence, so called (
\tau
-)exceptional pairs. The mutation of exceptional sequences is classical but various questions, especially regarding its transitivity remained open until very recently
[
15
]
. In general, the mutation of an exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
does not remain a sequence of objects in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
, but rather becomes a sequence of objects in the bounded derived category
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mods\mathscr{A}_{t})
. In contrast, the mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences always remains in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
. Therefore, the comparison in this article restricts itself to the case where the mutation of an exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
remains in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
. The mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences was recently introduced in
[
8
]
and has already inspired substantial work. Its transitivity has been established for a large class of finite dimensional algebras
[
9
]
and investigations of whether the mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences satisfies braid group relations have yielded insightful results
[
31
,
39
]
. The second main theorem establishes that the mutation of complete exceptional sequences which remains in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
is a particular case of the mutation of complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
Theorem

4.15
)
Let
\mathcal{E}=(E_{t},\dots,E_{1})
be a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
and let
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
. If the left mutation of
\mathcal{E}
as an exceptional sequence (at position
i
) lies in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
then it coincides with the left mutation of
\mathcal{E}
as a
\tau
-exceptional sequence (at position
i
).

Tilting modules, which are in bijection with complete exceptional sequences by
Theorem

1.1
, have their own mutation theory, which was generalised by that of support
\tau
-tilting modules. It turns out that the mutation of tilting modules, which replaces one indecomposable summand of a tilting module by another, is the lift of the mutation of complete exceptional sequences under the inverse of
\Phi
. In other words,
\Phi
commutes with the mutations. A similar phenomen was also observed in
[
20
, Sec. 9]
.

Proposition 1.3
.

(
Proposition

4.17
, simplified)
The explicit bijection
\Phi
of
Theorem

1.1
is compatible with the mutation of tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules and the mutation of complete exceptional sequences in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
.

To summarise the results above, let
t\geq 2
and let
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
.

As an Auslander algebra of a representation finite algebra, the algebra
\mathscr{A}_{t}
is of global dimension at most 2
[
3
, Thm. 4.6]
. The category of finite dimensional right
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules is denoted by
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
and given
M\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
, the number of isomorphism classes of indecomposable direct summands of
M
is denoted by
|M|
. There is a sequence of inclusions of indecomposable projective
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules

P(1)\subset P(2)\subset\dots\subset P(t)

which induces a (unique) quasi-hereditary structure on
\mathscr{A}_{t}
. In this paper, various special families of
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules play central roles. This section introduces these and collects their most relevant properties.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
. Then
T
is called
partial tilting
if
\Ext^{1}(T,T)=0
for
i\geq 1
and
\projdim T\leq 1
. It is called
tilting
if additionally
|T|=t
.

For the algebra
\mathscr{A}_{t}
, it turns out that studying its maximal ideals provides an insightful perspective on its tilting modules. Consider the maximal ideal
I_{i}\coloneqq\langle 1-e_{i}\rangle\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{t}
, where
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{t}
is a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents of
\mathscr{A}_{t}
and let
\langle I_{1},\dots,I_{t-1}\rangle
denote the semigroup generated multiplicatively by the maximal ideals
\{I_{i}\}_{i=1}^{t-1}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{t}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
29
, Thm. 1.1(1)]

The set of basic tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules is given by
\langle I_{1},\dots,I_{t-1}\rangle
.

For the algebra
\mathscr{A}_{t}
, many properties of its tilting modules can be deduced from properties of its projective modules as a consequence of the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
6
, Thm. 4]

For any basic tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-module
T
, there is an isomorphism
\End(T)\simeq\mathscr{A}_{t}
.

This result can also be found in
[
29
, Thm. 3.6]
. The following enumeration of basic tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules is a key ingredient in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
6
, Thm. 3]

There is a bijection between the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{t}
and the set of basic tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules. Consequently, there are
t!
basic tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules.

Importantly, there are also
t!
complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}

[
27
]
and these bijections are also discussed in
[
20
, Sec. 9]
. Generalised tilting complexes in the bounded homotopy category of
\mathscr{A}_{t}
are characterised in
[
20
,
44
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
E\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
, then
E
is called
exceptional
if
\Ext^{i}(E,E)=0
for
i\geq 1
and
\End(E)\simeq K
. A sequence
(E_{r},\dots,E_{1})
of exceptional
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules is called an
exceptional sequence
if
\Hom(E_{i},E_{j})=0=\Ext^{\ell}(E_{i},E_{j})
for
1\leq i<j\leq r
. It is called a complete exceptional sequence if
r=t
.

The goal of this paper is to compare these classical notions with modern adaptations in the context of
\tau
-tilting theory
[
1
]
. A red thread commonly seen throughout
\tau
-tilting theory uses the Auslander–Reiten translation
\tau
in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
to replace
\Ext^{1}(!,?)
vanishing conditions by
\Hom(?,\tau(!))
vanishing conditions.

Definition 2.6
.

[
1
]

Let
T\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
, then
T
is called
\tau
-rigid
if
\Hom(T,\tau T)=0
and it is called
\tau
-tilting
if additionally
|T|=t
. A
\tau
-rigid module
T
is called
support
\tau
-tilting
if it is
\tau
-tilting in
\mathscr{A}_{t}/\langle e\rangle
for some idempotent
e\in\mathscr{A}_{t}
. A pair
(T,P)
of
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules is called
\tau
-rigid
if
T
is
\tau
-rigid and
\Hom(P,T)=0
. Sometimes a
\tau
-rigid pair
(T,P)
is denoted by
T\oplus P[1]
.

It is a well-known fact that partial tilting objects are
\tau
-rigid, which makes it immediate that tilting objects are
\tau
-tilting.

Lemma 2.7
.

Every partial tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-module is
\tau
-rigid. Conversely, every
\tau
-rigid module of projective dimension at most 1 is partial tilting.

Proof.

If
\projdim T\leq 1
then
\Ext^{1}(T,T)=0
is equivalent to
\Hom(T,\tau T)=0
by
[
2
, Cor. IV.4.7]
.
∎

By
[
1
, Thm. 0.5]
, associated to every
\tau
-rigid module
T
in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
are a torsion class
\Gen T
and its corresponding torsion-free class
T^{\perp}
, which are full subcategories defined by

\Gen T\coloneqq\{M\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}:\exists T^{r}\twoheadrightarrow M\to 0\}

T^{\perp}\coloneqq\{M\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}:\Hom(T,M)=0\}.

Together, they form a torsion pair
(\Gen T,T^{\perp})
in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
which comes with two associated maps of objects
t_{T}:\mods A\to\mods A
and
f_{T}:\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}\to\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
, see
[
17
]
. These maps are such that for every
M\in\mods A
there is a unique (up to isomorphism) short exact sequence

0\to t_{T}(M)\to M\to f_{T}(M)\to 0

satisfying
t_{T}(M)\in\Gen T
and
f_{T}(M)\in T^{\perp}
. The full subcategory
{}^{\perp}T
is defined dually to
T^{\perp}
. This leads to the
\tau
-perpendicular
subcategory
J(T)\coloneqq T^{\perp}\cap{}^{\perp}\tau T
. If
(T,P)
is
\tau
-rigid, set
J(T,P)\coloneqq T^{\perp}\cap{}^{\perp}\tau T\cap P^{\perp}
. Then
J(T,P)
is an extension-closed full subcategory of
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
and abelian, whence it has an Auslander–Reiten translation and
\tau
-rigid modules which are possibly distinct from those in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{T}
, see
[
30
]
.

Remark 2.8
.

Let
T
be
\tau
-rigid. The map
f_{T}(-)
defines a bijection

\displaystyle\{\text{indecomposable }M\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}:M\oplus T\text{ is $\tau$-rigid in $\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}$ and }M\not\in\Gen T\}

\displaystyle\to\{\text{indecomposable }N\in J(T):N\text{ is $\tau$-rigid in $J(T)$}\}

by
[
14
, Prop. 4.5]
. By a slight abuse of notation, the inverse assignment of this bijection is denoted
f_{T}^{-1}(-)
. This is used heavily in
Section

4
, where this notation is not used in any other context than to refer to this bijection.

The observation that
\tau
-rigid modules in
J(T)
may not necessarily be
\tau
-rigid in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
is fundamental in the following recursive definition.

Definition 2.9
.

[
14
, Def. 1.3]

A sequence
(M_{r},\dots,M_{1})
of indecomposable
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules is called
\tau
-exceptional
if
M_{1}
is
\tau
-rigid in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
and
(M_{r},\dots,M_{2})
is a
\tau
-exceptional sequence in
J(M_{1})
. The sequence is called
complete
if
r=t
.

In particular, the modules
M_{r},\dots,M_{2}
need not be
\tau

-exceptional sequences in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T
be a basic tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-module. Then its quasi-hereditary decomposition is a TF-ordered
\tau
-tilting module.

Proof.

Clearly,
T
is a
\tau
-tilting module by
Lemma

2.7
. Assume for a contradiction that
T_{i}\in\Gen(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{i-1}T_{j})
for some
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
. Then there exists an epimorphism
g:(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{i-1}T_{j})^{r}\to T_{i}
for some
r\geq 1
. However, every component
g_{\ell}
of
g
factors through the monomorphism
h_{i-1}:T_{i-1}\to T_{i}
by
Lemma

3.1
(1). Therefore,
g
cannot be an epimorphism.
∎

To establish a connection between exceptional modules and
\tau
-rigid modules, the following characterisation of exceptional modules is important. Recall that a module
M\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
is called
thin
if its dimension vector only has entries equal to 0 or 1.

Theorem 3.4
.

[
27
, Thm. 2.2(1)]

Let
M\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
. Then
M
is exceptional if and only if
M
is indecomposable, thin and satisfies
\dim\Hom(P(t),M)=1
.

The final condition in the preceding theorem is related to the following.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
T
be a basic tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-module. Then
\dim\Hom(P(t),T_{i})=i
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
.

Proof.

Let
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
. From the description of
T
as an element of the ideal semigroup in
Theorem

2.2
, it follows directly that each
T_{i}
is a submodule of
e_{i}\mathscr{A}_{t}=P(i)
. The indecomposable projective
P(i)
is such that
\dim\Hom(P(t),P(i))=i
for all
1\leq i\leq t
. Therefore
i
is an upper bound for
\dim\Hom(P(t),T_{i})
.

Observe that
e_{t}\in I_{j}
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,t-1\}
. It follows that
T_{t}=P(t)
, this establishes the result for
i=t
. Otherwise, let
X=\langle e_{t}\rangle\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{t}
be the ideal generated by the idempotent at vertex
t
. It follows that
X
is a submodule of
T
and that
e_{i}X
is a submodule of
T_{i}
. It is readily checked that
\dim\Hom(P(t),e_{i}X)=i
, which implies that
i
is also a lower bound for
\dim\Hom(P(t),T_{i})
. The result follows.
∎

In particular, every complete exceptional sequence in

Proof.

, the quasi-hereditary decomposition of
T
is a TF-ordered
\tau
-tilting module and hence lies in the domain of
\Phi
, whose codomain is the set of
\tau

\tau
-exceptional sequence. Once it is established that
\Phi(T)
is a complete exceptional sequence, the fact that there are

2.4

t!

shows that all complete exceptional sequences must arise in this way because
\Phi
is a bijection.

It remains to show that
\Phi(T_{t}\oplus\dots\oplus T_{1})

Lemma

3.2
, the isomorphism

f_{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{i-1}T_{j}}(T_{i})\simeq T_{i}/T_{i-1}

holds for all

. It follows that

\Phi(T)=(T_{t}/T_{t-1},\dots,T_{2}/T_{1},T_{1}).

Moreover,

0\subset T_{1}\subset T_{2}\subset\dots\subset T_{t-1}\subset T_{t}=P(t)

\Phi(T)

(1)

\dim\Hom(P(t),T_{i})=i
;

(2)

T_{1}
is an exceptional module;

(3)

each
T_{i}/T_{i-1}
for
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
is an exceptional module.

Lemma

3.5
. Observe that the indecomposable projective
P(1)
is thin. Thus, its submodule
T_{1}
is also thin, which yields that
T_{1}
is an exceptional modules by
Theorem

3.4
and
Lemma

3.5
. It remains to show (3).

Let
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
. By
Theorem

3.4
it is necessary and sufficient to show that each
T_{i}/T_{i-1}
is indecomposable, thin and such that
\dim\Hom(P(t),T_{i}/T_{i-1})=1
. Consider the short exact sequence

(3.a)

0\to T_{i-1}\xrightarrow{h_{i-1}}T_{i}\to T_{i}/T_{i-1}\to 0,

where
h_{i-1}
is the minimal right
\add(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{i-1}T_{j})
-approximation of
T_{i}
of

3.1
(1). Applying
\Hom(P(t),-)
to

3.a
and using (1), yields

\dim\Hom(P(t),T_{i}/T_{i-1})=\dim\Hom(P(t),T_{i})-\dim\Hom(P(t),T_{i-1})=i-(i-1)=1.

To show that each
T_{i}/T_{i-1}
is indecomposable and thin and thus complete the proof, it is sufficient to show that
T_{i}/T_{i-1}
satisfies
\End(T_{i}/T_{i-1})\simeq K
by
[
27
, Cor. 2.4]
.
Applying
\Hom(T_{i-1},-)
to
Eq.

3.a
yields an exact sequence

\Hom(T_{i-1},T_{i-1})\xrightarrow{\Hom(T_{i-1},h_{i-1})}\Hom(T_{i-1},T_{i})\to\Hom(T_{i-1},T_{i}/T_{i-1})\to\Ext^{1}(T_{i-1},T_{i-1})

which implies that
\Hom(T_{i-1},T_{i}/T_{i-1})=0
because
\Hom(T_{i-1},h_{i-1})
is surjective since
h_{i-1}
is a right approximation and
\Ext^{1}(T_{i-1},T_{i-1})=0
since
T
is tilting. Consequently, applying
\Hom(-,T_{i}/T_{i-1})
to
Eq.

3.a
gives an exact sequence

(3.b)

0\to\End(T_{i}/T_{i-1})\to\Hom(T_{i},T_{i}/T_{i-1})\to\Hom(T_{i-1},T_{i}/T_{i-1})=0.

There is an equivalence of categories
\Hom(T,-):\add T\to\add\mathscr{A}_{t}
because left multiplication defines an isomorphism
\mathscr{A}_{t}\to\End(T)
by
Theorem

2.3
. This implies that

\dim\Hom(P(i),P(i))=i\quad\text{and}\quad\dim\Hom(P(i),P(i-1))=i-1

restrict to
T_{i}
and
T_{i-1}
. Applying
\Hom(T_{i},-)
to

3.a
and using
\Ext^{1}(T_{i},T_{i-1})=0

\dim\Hom(T_{i},T_{i}/T_{i-1})=\dim\End(T_{i},T_{i})-\dim(T_{i},T_{i-1})=i-(i-1)=1.

Combining this with the isomorphism
\End(T_{i}/T_{i-1})\simeq\Hom(T_{i},T_{i}/T_{i-1})
of

\End(T_{i}/T_{i-1})\simeq K

is exceptional and thus the proof of the statement.

Remark 3.7
.

Not every exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
is also a
\tau
-exceptional sequence. This can be immediately observed from the fact that not every exceptional module is
\tau
-rigid. Indeed, consider the case
t=2
in
Example

1.4
. The module
\begin{smallmatrix}&2\\
1&\end{smallmatrix}
, which can equivalently be written as
P(2)/S(1)
, is exceptional but not
\tau
-rigid, as can be easily checked.

Remark 3.8
.

For an arbitrary finite dimensional
K
-algebra, not every complete exceptional sequence is necessarily a
\tau
-exceptional sequence. Consider the algebra

\Gamma=K(1\xrightarrow{a}2\xrightarrow{b}3)/\langle ab\rangle.

It can readily be checked that
(P_{2},P_{1},S_{1})
is a complete exceptional sequence but not a
\tau
-exceptional sequence because
\Hom(P_{2},\tau S_{1})\neq 0
.

4.
Comparing mutations

Now that it is established that each complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
is a complete
\tau
-exceptional sequence, it is natural to compare their mutation theories. Recall that generally the mutation of complete exceptional sequences is an operation in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mods\mathscr{A}_{t})
whereas the mutation of complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences remains in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
. Let
(E,F)
be an exceptional sequence consisting of two terms, also called an exceptional pair. There is a natural map

\Hom(E,F)\otimes_{K}E\to F,\quad\phi\otimes e\mapsto\phi(e)

which, for each integer
\ell
, defines a natural map

\Hom(E[-\ell],F)\otimes_{K}E[-\ell]=\Hom(E,F[\ell])\otimes_{K}E\to F.

Taking direct sums this defines the
canonical map
and a triangle in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mods\mathscr{A}_{t})
given by

(4.a)

\RHom(E,F)\otimes E\to F\to\mathcal{L}_{E}F\to\quad.

The
left mutation
of
(E,F)
, later called
left
\psi
-mutation
to distinguish it from the mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequence, is then defined to be the exceptional pair
(\mathcal{L}_{E}F,E)
, see
[
21
, Sec. 2.2]
. The dual canonical map similarly yields a triangle

\mathcal{R}_{F}E\to E\to\RHom(E,F)^{*}\otimes E\to

in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mods\mathscr{A}_{t})
which defines the
right
\psi
-mutation

(F,\mathcal{R}_{F}E)
of
(E,F)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E_{t},\dots,E_{1})
be a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
and let
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
. Define the
left
\psi
-mutation of
\mathcal{E}
at (position)
i
to be

\psi_{i}(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq(E_{t},\dots,E_{i+1},\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1},E_{i},E_{i-2},\dots,E_{1}).

Similarly, define the
right
\psi
-mutation of
\mathcal{E}
at (position)
i
to be

\psi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq(E_{t},\dots,E_{i+1},E_{i-1},\mathcal{R}_{E_{i-1}}{E}_{i},E_{i-2},\dots,E_{1}).

This notation is justified because these operations are inverse to each other by
[
21
, Sec. 2.3]
. It is important to remember that the left and right
\psi
-mutations of a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
may not be complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
. This property of right
\psi
-mutation is characterised in
[
27
, Lem. 4.7, Cor. 4.8]
. To prove the analogous statement for left
\psi
-mutation and for various other proofs, the following collection of statements about complete exceptional sequences in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
is essential.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(E_{t},\dots,E_{1})
be a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
. The following hold:

(1)

\dim\Hom(E_{i},E_{j})=1=\dim\Ext^{1}(E_{i},E_{j})
for any
i>j
;

(2)

\Ext^{2}(E_{i},E_{j})=0
for any
i>j
;

(3)

Every nonzero morphism
E_{i}\to E_{i-1}
is either injective or surjective for all
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
;

(4)

If
E_{2}\to E_{1}
is injective, then
\projdim E_{2}\leq 1
;

(5)

If
f:E_{i}\to E_{i-1}
is injective for
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
, then
\dim\Ext^{1}(\coker f,E_{i})=1
.

Proof.

(1) and (2) are
[
27
, Cor. 3.7]
. (3) holds for worm diagrams by
[
27
, Lem. 3.2]
, which are equivalent to complete exceptional sequences by
[
27
, Prop. 3.3, Cor. 3.6]
. (4) The exceptional module
E_{1}
is a submodule of
P(1)
, which is uniserial and thin with socle
S(t)
. If
E_{2}\to E_{1}
is injective, then
E_{2}
is also uniserial and thin with socle
S(t)
. Notice that
\topp(E_{2})\not\simeq S(1)
, as otherwise
E_{2}\simeq P(1)
leads to a contradiction. Let
\topp(E_{2})\simeq S(j)
for some
j\in\{2,\dots,t\}
. It is easily checked that its minimal projective resolution is given by

0\to P(j-1)\to P(j)\to E_{2}\to 0.

(5) Applying
\Hom(-,E_{i})
to the short exact sequence defining
\coker f
yields an exact sequence

(4.b)

0=\Hom(E_{i-1},E_{i})\to\Hom(E_{i},E_{i})\to\Ext^{1}(\coker f,E_{i})\to\Ext^{1}(E_{i-1},E_{i})=0

where the outermost terms are zero by the defining property of exceptional sequences. Since
E_{i}
is exceptional, the equality
\dim\Hom(E_{i},E_{i})=1
holds and the result follows from the isomorphism
\Hom(E_{i},E_{i})\simeq\Ext^{1}(\coker f,E_{i})
in
Eq.

4.b
.
∎

The dual of
[
27
, Lem. 4.7, Cor. 4.8]
is then readily proved as follows.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E_{t},\dots,E_{1})
be a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
and let
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

any nonzero morphism
f:E_{i}\to E_{i-1}
is injective;

(2)

\psi_{i}(\mathcal{E})
is a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
.

In this case
\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1}\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
is defined by the short exact sequence

(4.c)

0\to\coker f\to\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1}\to E_{i}\to 0

in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
.

Proof.

This statement is unambiguous because
\dim\Hom(E_{i},E_{i-1})=1
by
Lemma

4.2
(1) and therefore
\coker f_{1}\simeq\coker f_{2}
for any two
f_{1},f_{2}\in\Hom(E_{i},E_{i-1})
.

The triangle defining
\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1}
, given by
Eq.

4.a
, gives rise to a long exact sequence in cohomology

(4.d)

\displaystyle 0=H^{-1}(E_{i-1})\to H^{-1}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1})\to\Hom(E_{i},E_{i-1})\otimes E_{i}\to E_{i-1}\to H^{0}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1})

\displaystyle\to\Ext^{1}(E_{i},E_{i-1})\otimes E_{i}\to H^{1}(E_{i-1})=0

using the fact that
E_{i-1}
is concentrated in degree zero in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mods\mathscr{A}_{t})
to get
H^{\neq 0}(E_{i-1})=0
; the fact that
\Ext^{2}(E_{i},E_{i-1})=0
by
Lemma

4.2
(2) and the fact that
\Ext^{\geq 3}(E_{i},E_{i-1})=0
since
\mathscr{A}_{t}
is of global dimension 2 to conclude that
\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1}
is concentrated degrees -1 and 0.

Moreover, since
\dim\Hom(E_{i},E_{i-1})=1=\Ext^{1}(E_{i},E_{i-1})
by
Lemma

(1), the tensor products in
Eq.

4.d
simplify to yield the exact sequence

(4.e)

0\to H^{-1}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1})\to E_{i}\to E_{i-1}\to H^{0}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1})\to E_{i}\to 0.

Note that
E_{i}\to E_{i-1}
is nonzero in
Eq.

4.e
, so that
Lemma

4.2
(3) implies that it is either surjective or injective. If it is injective it follows that
H^{-1}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1})=0
and
\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1}\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
has the desired description, and implying that
\psi_{i}(\mathcal{E})
is a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
.

Conversely, if
\psi_{i}(\mathcal{E})
is a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
, then in particular
\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1}\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
, implying
H^{-1}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i-1})=0
, consequently
f:E_{i}\to E_{i-1}
is injective. This concludes the proof.
∎

For the algebra

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(E_{t},\dots,E_{1})
be a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
such that

is projective in

\varphi^{-1}(E_{2},E_{1})=(E_{1},?)

Proof.

First, consider the expression

as it appears in the subscript of the definition of

The second case is

[

The left

By
Theorem

3.6
, every complete exceptional sequence is also a complete

Notice that the only projective exceptional
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-module is

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E_{t},\dots,E_{1})
be a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
which can be left
\psi
-mutated at position

. Assume that

\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}

Since

4.5
states that either

\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}

Applying

to the above yields an exact sequence

is a complete
\tau
-exceptional sequence by

[

\mathcal{L}_{E_{2}}E_{1}\to E_{2}
. Hence all nonzero morphisms from
\mathcal{L}_{E_{2}}E_{1}
to
E_{2}
are surjective by
Lemma

4.2
(3).

(4) By (1) the modules
T_{2}\oplus E_{1}
and
T_{2}\oplus E_{2}
are both partial tilting, in particular of projective dimension at most 1. Then
[
2
, Cor. IV.4.7]
implies that

J(T_{2}\oplus E_{i})=\{X\in\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}:\Hom(T_{2}\oplus E_{i},X)=0=\Ext^{1}(T_{2}\oplus E_{i},X)\}

for

. The result follows from the long exact sequence obtained by applying
\Hom(-,X)
to the short exact sequence

0\to E_{1}\to T_{2}\to E_{2}\to 0

induced by
Theorem

3.6
.

(5) This is immediate by
[
8
, Prop. 3.13(a)]
since
f_{E_{1}}^{-1}(E_{2})=T_{2}
.

(6) By (3) the pair
(\mathcal{L}_{E_{2}}E_{1},E_{2})
is an exceptional pair in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
which can be right
\psi
-mutated. The complete exceptional sequence
\psi_{2}(\mathcal{E})
is also a complete
\tau
-exceptional sequence by
Theorem

3.6
. This implies that
(\mathcal{L}_{E_{2}}E_{1},E_{2})
is a
\tau
-exceptional pair.

Assume for a contradiction that
(\mathcal{L}_{E_{2}}E_{1},E_{2})
is a right regular
\tau
-exceptional pair. In this case
Lemma

4.11
gives

\varphi_{2}^{-1}(\psi_{2}(\mathcal{E}))=\psi_{2}^{-1}(\psi_{2}(\mathcal{E}))=\mathcal{E}.

As
(\mathcal{L}_{E_{2}}E_{1},E_{2})

[

, Def.-Prop. 4.5]
implies that
\varphi_{2}^{-1}(\psi_{2}(\mathcal{E}))=\mathcal{E}
is left regular at position
2
, that is,
(E_{2},E_{1})
is a left regular
\tau
-exceptional pair. This is a contradiction.
∎

It is now possible to prove the base case for the inductive proof that the mutations coincide.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E_{t},\dots,E_{1})
be a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
. If
\mathcal{E}
can be left
\psi
-mutated at position
2
, then
\psi_{2}(\mathcal{E})=\varphi_{2}(\mathcal{E})
.

Proof.

This is well-defined because
\mathcal{E}
is also a complete
\tau
-exceptional sequence by
Theorem

3.6
. If
(E_{2},E_{1})
is a left regular
\tau
-exceptional pair, the result follows from
Lemma

4.10
.

If
(E_{2},E_{1})
is a left irregular
\tau
-exceptional pair consider
\psi(E_{2},E_{1})=(\mathcal{L}_{E_{2}}E_{1},E_{2})
. By
Proposition

4.12
(6) this is a right irregular
\tau
-exceptional pair. Combining
[
8
, Prop. 3.14(e)]
with
Proposition

4.12
(2) yields

\tau(T_{2}\oplus E_{2})=\tau\mathrm{P}_{\textnormal{ns}}(\mathrm{T}(J(T_{2}\oplus E_{2}))).

Observe that
T_{2}
and
E_{2}
are not projective in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
by
Proposition

4.12
(5). Using
Proposition

4.12
(4) and applying
\tau^{-1}
yields

T_{2}\oplus E_{2}=\mathrm{P}_{\textnormal{ns}}(\mathrm{T}(J(T_{2}\oplus E_{1}))).

Since
E_{2}\in\Gen T_{2}
it is clear that
T_{2}=\mathrm{P}_{\textnormal{s}}(\Gen\mathrm{P}_{\textnormal{ns}}(\mathrm{T}(J(T_{2}\oplus E_{1}))))
and thus
\varphi(E_{2},E_{1})=(f_{E_{2}}(T_{2}),E_{2})
by the definition of left irregular
\varphi
-mutation. It follows from
Proposition

4.12
(2) that
f_{E_{2}}(T_{2})\simeq\mathcal{L}_{E_{2}}E_{2}
, whence

(4.f)

\varphi_{2}(\mathcal{E})=(E_{t},\dots,E_{3},\mathcal{L}_{E_{2}}E_{1},E_{2})=\psi_{2}(\mathcal{E})

holds.
∎

This shows that
\psi
-mutation and
\varphi
-mutation at the right-most position of a complete exceptional sequence coincide. To show the same for mutation at other positions, the following reduction lemma is useful.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E_{t},\dots,E_{1})
be a complete exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
. Then
J(E_{i},\dots,E_{1})
is equivalent to
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t-i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,{t-1}\}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show this for
i=1
because of the recursive definition of
J(E_{i},\dots,E_{1})
, see
[
13
, Thm. 1.4]
and
[
12
, Thm. 6.4]
, and the observation that
(E_{t},\dots,E_{2})
is a complete exceptional sequence in
J(E_{1})
because the subcategory is full and extension-closed. Indeed, if
J(E_{i},\dots,E_{1})\simeq\mods\mathscr{A}_{t-i}
and the case
i=1
hold, then the proof follows inductively from

J(E_{i+1},E_{i},\dots,E_{1})=J^{J(E_{i},\dots,E_{1})}(E_{i+1})\simeq J^{\mods\mathscr{A}_{t-i}}(E_{i+1})=\mods\mathscr{A}_{t-i-1}

as required. Let
T
be the tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-module such that
\Phi(T)=\mathcal{E}
via
Theorem

3.6
, which is such that
E_{1}=T_{1}
. Consider the
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-module
T^{\prime}=\mathrm{P}(^{\perp}\tau T_{1})
, which is tilting since
T_{1}
is partial tilting by
[
35
, Thm. A]
. It is clear that
e_{1}(T^{\prime})=e_{1}T
, giving
(T^{\prime})_{1}=T_{1}
in the quasi-hereditary decomposition of
T^{\prime}
.

By
Theorem

2.3
the endomorphism algebra of
T^{\prime}
satisfies
\End(T^{\prime})\simeq\mathscr{A}_{t}
and by
Lemma

3.1
(1), the module
T_{1}
has a monomorphism to every other direct summand of
T^{\prime}
. This means that
\Hom(T^{\prime},T_{1})
is the indecomposable projective
\End(T^{\prime})
-module which is a submodule of every other indecomposable projective. In other words,
\Hom(T^{\prime},T_{1})
is isomorphic to the projective at vertex 1 in the presentation of
\mathscr{A}_{t}
given at the start of
Section

2
. From this presentation it is clear that
\mathscr{A}_{t}/\langle e_{1}\rangle\simeq\mathscr{A}_{t-1}
. It follows that

J(E_{1})\simeq\mods(\End(T^{\prime})/[\Hom(T^{\prime},T_{1})])\simeq\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}/\langle e_{1}\rangle\simeq\mods\mathscr{A}_{t-1},

where the first isomorphism is
[
30
, Thm. 3.8]
. This concludes the proof.
∎

The preceding lemma enables the inductive step in the following proof.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E_{t},\dots,E_{1})
be an exceptional sequence in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
and let
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
. If
\mathcal{E}
can be left
\psi
-mutated at position

, then
\psi_{i}(\mathcal{E})=\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{E})
.

Proof.

This is well-defined since
\mathcal{E}
is also a complete
\tau
-exceptional sequence by
Theorem

3.6
. If
i=2
, then the result is
Proposition

4.13
. If
i\in\{3,\dots,t\}
, then

by

4.14
. It follows that

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=(E_{t},\dots,E_{i+1},\varphi^{J(E_{i-2},\dots,E_{1})}(E_{i},E_{i-1}),E_{i-2},\dots,E_{1})

\displaystyle=(E_{t},\dots,E_{i+1},\psi^{J(E_{i-2},\dots,E_{1})}(E_{i},E_{i-1}),E_{i-2},\dots,E_{1})

\displaystyle=(E_{t},\dots,E_{i+1},\psi(E_{i},E_{i-1}),E_{i-2},\dots,E_{1})

\displaystyle=\psi_{i}(\mathcal{E}).

where the first and final equalities hold by definition, the second equality is
Proposition

4.13
in the subcategory

is a wide subcategory of

by

Eq.

4.c
lies in this subcategory. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Relationship with the mutation of (
\tau
-)tilting modules

The final result of this article establishes a close relationship between the mutation of tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules and the mutation of (
\tau
-)exceptional sequences in
\mods\mathscr{A}_{t}
. First, it is necessary to establish the following result, again setting up the inductive step of the proof.

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
T
be a basic tilting
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-module. Then
T_{t}/T_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus T_{2}/T_{1}

Proof.

First of all

T_{1}
to
P(i)
is a monomorphism for all
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}

It then follows from a proof entirely analogous to that of
Lemma

3.2
that for every summand
T_{i}\in\add T

\alpha_{i}:T_{1}\to T_{i}
is a minimal right
\Gen(T_{1})
-approximation. It follows that
f_{T_{1}}(T_{i})\simeq T_{i}/T_{1}
for all
i=\{2,\dots,t\}
.

By
[
18
, Lem. 7.1]
, the class of modules filtered by the standard modules of (the quasi-hereditary algebra)
\mathscr{A}_{t}
is closed under submodules and contains all projective
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules. By
[
6
, Lem. 1]
, this class of modules is equivalent to the class of
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-modules of projective dimension at most 1. In particular, every submodule of a projective
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-module has projective dimension at most 1.

From the chain of inclusions
T_{1}\subset T_{i}\subset T_{t}=P(t)
it follows that
f_{T_{1}}(T_{i})=T_{i}/T_{1}
is a submodule of
f_{T_{1}}(T_{t})=T_{t}/T_{1}
for all
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
. By
[
14
, Prop. 4.5]
, the modules
T_{i}/T_{1}
and
T_{t}/T_{1}
are
\tau
-rigid in
J(T_{1})
. Moreover, sine
T_{t}=P(t)
is projective, it is projective in
{}^{\perp}\tau T_{1}
, whence
T_{t}/T_{1}
is projective in
J(T_{1})
by
[
30
, Prop. 3.14]
, see also
[
14
, Lem. 4.9]
. By the above,
T_{i}/T_{1}
is therefore a subobject of a projective object in
J(T_{1})
. Since
J(T_{1})\simeq\mods\mathscr{A}_{t-1}
by
Lemma

4.14
, it follows that
T_{i}/T_{1}
has projective dimension at most 1 in
J(T_{1})
for all
i\in\{2,\dots,t\}
. Therefore, it is partial tilting in
J(T_{1})
and the result follows.
∎

as both

-)tilting modules. This is a contradiction. For the general case, iteratively applying

, allows the use of the same argument in the subcategory

, which is isomorphic to

by

4.14
. This completes the proof.

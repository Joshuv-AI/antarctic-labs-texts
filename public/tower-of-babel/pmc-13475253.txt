Assessing quality gaps in hypertensive disorders of pregnancy care in China: an unannounced standardised patient study protocol
Authors: Yang Shen, Huifeng Shi, Yanan Zhao, Fangyi Zeng, Lulu Sun, Qing Guo, Yangyu Zhao, Ping He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractBackground Improving the quality of outpatient obstetric care is essential for achieving better maternal and perinatal outcomes, especially in resource-constrained settings. Despite guideline recommendations, substantial gaps remain in the delivery of evidence-based services and patient-centred care during routine antenatal encounters. Unannounced standardised patient (USP) methodology provides a robust and reproducible approach to objectively measure clinical performance. However, its large-scale application and value for multidimensional service quality assessment in obstetric outpatient settings remain underexplored, especially in low-income and middle-income countries (LMICs).Methods and analysisWe present a protocol that uses USPs to evaluate the quality and efficiency of outpatient obstetric care delivery. The study will be conducted across six cities in China’s Hebei, Shanxi and Yunnan provinces. Trained USPs will conduct a total of 108 clinical visits from October to December 2025, with a projected study end date of December 2026. Primary outcomes will encompass two core dimensions: (1) technical capabilities, assessed through four metrics—(a) completeness of key history-taking items (continuous, 0–1 scale), (b) adherence to essential testing protocols (continuous, 0–1 scale), (c) diagnostic accuracy (ordinal: 0=incorrect, 1=partially correct, 2=correct) and (d) therapeutic appropriateness (ordinal: 0=incorrect, 1=partially correct, 2=correct); and (2) patient-centred care, quantified using the validated Chinese version of the Revised Patient Perception of Patient-Centeredness scale (PPPC-R; continuous, range 1–4, lower scores indicating more patient-centred care). USPs will receive comprehensive training to guarantee strict protocol adherence and consistent case presentation across all study sites. We will use OLS regression for continuous outcomes and logistic regression for binary outcomes to assess differences in case management across provider levels and USP-scenario interactions, controlling for city indicators. Logistic regression will also examine links between provider characteristics and quality outcomes, adjusting for city and USP dummies. Introduction

Global progress in maternal health remains insufficient to meet Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) targets, which call for reducing maternal mortality to below 70 per 100 000 live births and neonatal mortality to below 12 per 1000 live births by 2030.1 2 Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), complicating 5–10% of pregnancies globally,3 4 remain a leading cause of maternal deaths,5 particularly in low-income and middle-income countries (LMICs). In these settings, suboptimal adherence to antihypertensive guidelines significantly contributes to the persistently high rates of HDP-related mortality.6 Efforts to optimise care pathways for HDP are further hampered by wide variation in the capacity of health facilities to deliver appropriate care. Many institutions are strained by high volumes of routine pregnancy cases, which can divert resources from critical interventions and undermine the consistent, timely delivery of guideline-concordant care for time-sensitive conditions such as HDP. This variation in the capacity to manage HDP emergencies contributes to preventable mortality and underscores the urgent need for objective, standardised methods to assess and improve the quality of obstetric triage and management.

Traditional quality assessment methods, such as retrospective medical record reviews and patient self-reports, often fail to capture the complexity of clinical care and patient-provider interactions.7 These methods are limited by their reliance on historical data, recall bias and the inability to assess real-time clinical decision-making.7 8 Moreover, current research paradigms predominantly focus on service utilisation metrics,9 often overlooking the multidimensional aspects of care quality, such as adherence to clinical guidelines, effective communication and timely intervention. This methodological gap becomes particularly consequential when evaluating hierarchical health systems, where the interplay between facility tiers strongly shapes population-level outcomes. Together, these limitations highlight the need for more reliable and objective approaches to assess the quality of care, especially in complex and time-sensitive clinical settings such as obstetrics. Unannounced standardised patients (USPs) have emerged as a robust alternative to traditional assessment methods in several medical fields, including ophthalmology,10 tuberculosis diagnosis11 and primary care.12 USPs enable controlled, reproducible simulations of real-world clinical interactions, offering a distinct advantage for evaluating care quality in complex scenarios.7 13

The use of USPs in obstetrics remains underexplored, primarily due to the unique challenges in simulating pregnancy-specific clinical dynamics. Although USPs cannot reproduce certain physiological signs of pregnancy, such as oedema and proteinuria, these limitations can be partially addressed through structured longitudinal training programmes incorporating evidence-based simulation protocols. A more fundamental challenge lies in obstetrics’ inherent longitudinal care requirements: managing a chronic condition such as gestational hypertension requires repeated, sequential visits, whereas conventional USP validation typically focuses on single, isolated encounters. To address this, we will embed prescripted medical records in the simulation scenarios to realistically reproduce continuity of care, enabling USPs to portray the progression of a clinical course over time.

This study aims to quantify systemic gaps in care quality using a framework that compares facilities across tiers of the health system. Its primary objective is to establish a USP methodology for assessing obstetric outpatient care quality, an approach currently lacking in the obstetric literature. Using this novel framework, we will evaluate care across critical operational dimensions, specifically focusing on the timeliness of acute deterioration recognition, adherence rates to evidence-based protocols and the appropriateness of diagnostic and treatment decisions. Within this framework, a key secondary objective is to compare performance between secondary and tertiary institutions, thereby decoding potential structural determinants of HDP outcome disparities. In addition, the methodology will allow us to map breakdown along the care pathway, from triage inaccuracies at secondary facilities to protocol deviations in tertiary referral management. By addressing the evidence gap in obstetric USP research, our work will help inform future interventions to improve maternal health outcomes and advance progress towards the SDG targets in LMICs.

Methods

Study setting

USP research can be conducted at either the clinician or the facility level. We adopted a facility level, which is particularly appropriate when research objectives do not focus on performance of individual provider performance, as is the case for interventions randomised across healthcare facilities.7 14

We selected Hebei, Shanxi and Yunnan provinces to represent China’s eastern, central and western geographical regions, respectively. This selection reflects the nation’s socioeconomic diversity, encompassing areas with varying levels of economic development and distinct healthcare system characteristics. Within each province, two cities will be chosen for data collection, with a specific focus on obstetric care services in secondary and tertiary healthcare institutions. This stratified sampling strategy ensures comprehensive representation of China’s heterogeneous healthcare landscape while enabling systematic comparisons across regions with differential healthcare infrastructure and socioeconomic conditions.

The study will target secondary and tertiary medical institutions in each selected city, where obstetric care is provided by a range of healthcare professionals, including licensed physicians, assistant physicians and registered nurses. Data collection for HDP is scheduled to conclude in December 2025, with overall study completion, including data analysis, anticipated by December 2026.

Participants and recruitment

Recruitment of USPs

Recruitment of USPs will occur through multiple channels, including the Peking University online forum and local social media platforms. Inclusion criteria for USPs are (1) age 22–50 years; (2) high school diploma or higher educational attainment and (3) good physical and mental health, including strong memory and attention to detail. Preference will be given to local residents, particularly those with obstetric knowledge or prior pregnancy experience, and to women with strong comprehension skills and the ability to accurately role-play a patient. Individuals will be excluded if (1) they have serious health conditions that may affect their ability to simulate symptoms or (2) if they cannot accurately simulate gestational hypertensive disorders. All selected USPs will undergo a screening process, including interviews, and only those meeting all inclusion and no exclusion criteria will be included in the study.

Recruitment of obstetricians

The participating hospitals will be informed 3 months before the formal USP visits. All obstetricians at these target hospitals will be invited to provide written informed consent. Obstetricians interacting with the USPs will not be informed beforehand that the patient they are seeing is a standardised patient. Eligible obstetricians are those who (1) are formally employed by the hospital, (2) hold a valid Chinese Medical Practitioner License and (3) make regular outpatient visits.

Sample size

This cross-sectional study will use USPs to assess the quality of obstetric services across secondary-level and tertiary-level healthcare facilities in six geographically diverse cities in China. The sampling process will begin with a target sample size calculation based on predefined statistical parameters. Given an assumed quality service qualification rate of 75% for secondary-level institutions15 and 95% for tertiary-level institutions,16 with a significance level (α) of 0.05 and 80% statistical power, a minimum of 49 tertiary and 49 secondary medical institutions will be required. To achieve this, a multistage sampling strategy will be implemented across three provinces—Hebei, Shanxi and Yunnan—deliberately selected to reflect regional economic diversity. Within each province, two cities will be strategically chosen to represent varying economic development levels (eg, one economically advanced city and one less developed city), resulting in six cities in total.

In the second stage, probability proportional to size (PPS) sampling will be employed within each selected city to identify specific institutions. This method will assign selection probabilities to hospitals proportionate to their annual obstetric delivery volume, ensuring that larger institutions have a higher likelihood of inclusion. From every city, exactly nine tertiary and nine secondary medical institutions will be systematically sampled using this approach. Consequently, the aggregate sample will reach 54 institutions per tier (6 cities×9 institutions; Figure 1), exceeding the minimum required sample size of 49 per tier. One USP visit will be conducted at each sampled institution, yielding 108 clinical visits in total (54 secondary and 54 tertiary).

Facility selection process.

USP training

USPs will undergo comprehensive training, including instruction on obstetric knowledge, common symptoms and potential complications of gestational hypertension (such as preeclampsia), and routine diagnostic and treatment methods. This training will emphasise simulating real clinical scenarios to ensure natural interaction with physicians and consistent case presentation. To achieve this, the training will incorporate case script development, memory and recall exercises, and role-playing practice. Trainers and collaborating physicians from the research team will provide feedback during simulated interviews, helping USPs internalise their roles and accurately recall case details. USPs will be rigorously evaluated on their familiarity with the script (see online supplemental file S1), understanding of doctor–patient communication, performance in simulated interviews, comprehension of exit surveys, attitude and cooperation. Regular rehearsals and assessments will reinforce training content and ensure natural, convincing performances in the actual clinical encounters.

Outcome measurement

The outcome measurement in this study will be the quality of care for suspected gestational hypertension assessed by USP visits (see table 1). The quality assessment methodology aligns with established frameworks. Building on the Institute of Medicine (IOM) dimensions,17 18 we operationalise clinical quality through a rigorously validated quality checklist assessing guideline adherence (effectiveness/safety), complemented by blinded evaluations of diagnostic-therapeutic decisions using ordinal scales (effectiveness/timeliness), while further employing the standardised PPPC-R instrument to measure patient-centred communication dynamics (patient-centredness/equity). The Quality Checklist includes explicit criteria related to history taking, physical examination (as relevant to an in-person encounter), ordering of relevant investigations (eg, urine protein), diagnosis and treatment planning for gestational hypertension. The checklist’s development followed an evidence-based and expert opinion protocol, focusing on clinician actions easily evaluated by a USP within the context of a standardised encounter.

Primary and secondary outcomes

RMB, Renminbi (Chinese Yuan).

Primary outcomes will be evaluated across two core dimensions: technical capabilities and patient-centred care. Technical quality will be measured through four distinct metrics: (a) completeness of key history-taking items (continuous variable, 0–1 scale; Measurement Pathway 1.1), (b) adherence to essential testing protocols (continuous variable, 0–1 scale; Measurement Pathway 1.2), (c) diagnostic accuracy (ordinal variable: 0=incorrect, 1=partially correct, 2=correct; Measurement Pathway 1.3) and (d) therapeutic appropriateness (ordinal variable: 0=incorrect, 1=partially correct, 2=correct; Measurement Pathway 1.3). Patient-centred quality will be quantified using the validated Chinese version of the Revised Patient Perception of Patient-Centeredness (PPPC-R) scale (continuous variable, range 1–4, lower scores indicating more patient-centred care; Measurement Pathway 2). All technical metrics will be derived from standardised checklists aligned with Chinese expert consensus on blood pressure management during pregnancy.19 Continuous variables (0–1) represent proportional adherence rates, while ordinal scales (0–2) evaluate clinical decision quality. The PPPC-R instrument will be used to measure communication efficacy and shared decision-making processes.

Following each USP encounter, the USP will complete the Chinese version of the PPPC-R scale.20 The Chinese version of the PPPC-R is a validated 21-item instrument covering three dimensions of patient-centred care: exploring the disease and illness experience, understanding the whole person and finding common ground. Each item is rated on a 4-point Likert scale (from ‘completely’ to ‘not at all’), with lower raw scores indicating more patient-centred care. Following the standard PPPC-R scoring approach, the overall score will be calculated as the mean of all item scores, and dimension scores as the mean of the items within each dimension, each ranging from 1 to 4, with lower scores indicating more patient-centred care. The instrument assesses aspects such as how well the clinician listened, explained information, considered the patient’s perspective, involved the patient in decisions, and demonstrated empathy and respect (see online supplemental table S4).

Secondary outcomes include five efficiency indicators: (a) waiting time (continuous, minutes), (b) consultation duration (continuous, minutes), (c) diagnostic test costs (continuous, RMB, Renminbi (Chinese Yuan)), (d) therapeutic regimen costs (continuous, RMB) and (e) total expenditure (continuous, RMB). These will be extracted directly from medical records and billing systems. To ensure measurement fidelity, USPs will receive standardised training, with a target protocol adherence of >95%. Cost analysis will exclude indirect expenditures (eg, transportation). All USP cases are specifically designed to enable assessment of diagnosis and treatment planning within the context of an in-person outpatient consultation (see later sections on the USP measurement tool in online supplemental file S2).

Other relevant variables

Supplementing our primary assessment of HDP care quality outcomes, we will document supplementary institutional and clinician variables (see online supplemental files). Institutional attributes will encompass ownership type (public/private), management model (non-profit/for-profit), facility classification, geographic province (Hebei, Shanxi, Yunnan) and whether the institution is located in the provincial capital (yes/no). Clinician characteristics will include gender, age, academic qualifications, professional titles, administrative position status, years of clinical experience, institutional tier classification, diagnostic approach regarding proactive disclosure without patient prompting (yes/no) and USP identification accuracy (yes/no).

Statistical analysis

We will calculate the proportion or mean and 95% CI for correct diagnosis and treatment, stratified by hospital level (secondary hospital vs tertiary hospital), along with PPPC-R scores. For the PPPC-R outcome, lower scores indicate more patient-centred care; accordingly, a negative coefficient for the tertiary-institution indicator would suggest more patient-centred care at tertiary institutions. To assess differences in case management across provider levels and interactions between USP and scenarios, we will employ ordinary least squares (OLS) regression for continuous dependent variables and logistic regression for binary dependent variables, incorporating indicator variables for each city as additional controls. Additionally, we will use logistic regression to examine the association between characteristics of secondary and tertiary institution providers and quality assessment outcomes, controlling for city and USP dummy variables. Results will be reported as ORs with corresponding 95% CIs. All analyses will be performed using Stata 18 (Stata Corporation, College Station, Texas, USA).

Discussion

As the pioneering USP study in obstetric care, this protocol addresses inherent limitations of conventional quality assessment methods by introducing an innovative tool for detecting service quality disparities across healthcare facilities. Nevertheless, several methodological constraints warrant consideration. First, while the single-visit simulation design reflects budgetary constraints, it incorporates integrated baseline assessments and clinical deterioration scenarios to partially capture continuity of care. Second, the non-pregnant status of the USPs may compromise diagnostic authenticity in procedures such as blood pressure measurement. In addition, although the USP approach is feasible for evaluating the diagnosis and management of maternal disease, assessing fetal conditions remains challenging without more advanced simulation devices capable of replicating parameters such as fetal heart rate. To mitigate these measurement deviations, we will implement standardised training protocols coupled with simulated physiological parameters. Third, despite comprehensive sampling across economically diverse regions (eastern, central and western China), the generalisability of findings to other LMICs necessitates contextual adaptations accounting for structural variations in referral systems. Fourth, and importantly, the simulation-based design does not capture real-world clinical outcomes. Outcomes such as maternal mortality, length of hospital stay, adverse obstetric events (eg, HELLP syndrome, myocardial infarction, stroke, pulmonary oedema), adverse fetal outcomes, post-discharge readmission rates and blood pressure control at 6 weeks post partum cannot be measured through the USP methodology. This is an inherent limitation of the standardised patient approach and represents a critical gap between process quality—which this study is designed to measure—and ultimate patient outcomes. Future studies should link USP-measured care quality to routinely collected clinical outcome data to establish the translational validity of this framework in obstetric settings.

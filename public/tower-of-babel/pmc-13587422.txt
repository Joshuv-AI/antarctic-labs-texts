E7389-LF as a first-line chemotherapy for patients with metastatic/advanced HER2-negative breast cancer: results from the phase 1 dose-expansion part of study 114
Authors: Hiroshi Ishiguro, Yasuo Miyoshi, Hiroyuki Yasojima, Junichiro Watanabe, Toshimi Takano, Akihiko Shimomura, Eriko Tokunaga, Toru Mukohara, Yasuto Naoi, Daiko Matsuoka, Shiori Okumura, Yohei Otake, Takuya Suzuki, Takao Takase, Taro Semba, Ziming Zhao, Kan Yonemori
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThere is an ongoing need for first-line (1L) chemotherapies for metastatic HER2-negative breast cancer. This study evaluated the efficacy and safety profile of the dose-expansion part 6 of Study 114 in patients receiving E7389-LF as a 1L chemotherapy for metastatic/advanced HER2-negative breast cancer.MethodsJapanese patients (≥ 20 years of age) with confirmed HER2-negative breast cancer (HER2-0 or HER2-low) were enrolled; subgroup analyses included luminal-like and triple-negative breast cancer tumors. Eligible patients had ≥ 1 measurable lesion per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumor version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1) and had not received prior chemotherapy for advanced/metastatic disease. Objectives included safety, efficacy, and biomarker changes. Tumors were assessed by investigators per RECIST v1.1.ResultsTwenty-six patients received E7389-LF. Overall, 24 patients (92.3%) had discontinued treatment by the data cutoff date (October 18, 2023). Most common treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) of any grade were alopecia (88.5%), neutropenia (88.5%; grade ≥ 3, 76.9%), and thrombocytopenia (84.6%; grade ≥ 3, 34.6%). Three patients discontinued due to TRAEs. No patient showed a complete response, and 8 (30.8%) had confirmed partial responses, for an objective response rate of 30.8%. Median progression-free survival (PFS) was 8.1 months overall (patients with luminal breast cancer [n = 22]: 8.1 months; triple-negative breast cancer [n = 4]: 9.8 months). Median overall survival was not reached at 22.7 months of follow-up. Median PFS on next-line therapy was 20.6 months. Changes in vasculature- and IFNγ-related biomarkers were observed in all patients who received E7389-LF.ConclusionsThese results highlight the antitumor activity and manageable safety profile of E7389-LF, indicating the potential of E7389-LF as a 1L treatment for patients with HER2-negative breast cancer, warranting further study.Clinical trials registrationNCT03207672. Registered 5 July 2017.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13058-026-02295-8.

Introduction

Breast cancer is the leading cause of cancer death in women worldwide accounting for 11.6% of all new cancer diagnoses in women globally [1]. In 2018, there were > 400,000 cases of metastatic breast cancer in the European Union [2], and it was estimated that 169,347 patients will be living with metastatic breast cancer in the United States as of January 2025 [3]. In Japan, breast cancer accounts for approximately one-fifth of cancers in women, and has an age-standardized mortality of 9.7 per 100,000 [4].

Breast cancer is a biologically heterogeneous disease and is classified by the American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists based on the expression levels of different biomarkers such as hormone receptors (HRs), including estrogen and progesterone receptors, and the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) [5, 6]. HER2-negative breast cancers account for about 80% of breast cancers and can be further categorized into HER2-0 or HER2-low breast cancers [7, 8]. HER2-negative disease can also be categorized as luminal-like (positive for estrogen and/or progesterone receptors) or triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC; negative for estrogen and progesterone receptors) [9]. Approximately 80% of HER2-low breast cancers are luminal-like and 15–20% are TNBC [10].

Metastatic breast cancer remains incurable despite advances in understanding and classifying the disease [2]. Currently, systemic therapy is recommended [2]; however, prolonged treatment with taxane causes significant toxicities [11]. Bevacizumab combined with paclitaxel significantly prolonged median progression-free survival (PFS) compared with paclitaxel alone (11.8 months vs. 5.9 months; P < 0.001) [12]. Although bevacizumab plus paclitaxel received accelerated approval by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) as a first-line (1L) treatment for patients with HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer, the approval was removed after widespread clinical uptake showed increased toxicity and no benefit to patient survival [13, 14]. Recently, the HER2-directed trastuzumab deruxtecan showed prolonged overall survival (OS) and PFS in previously treated metastatic HER2-low breast cancer and in untreated metastatic luminal HER2-low and ultralow (HER2-0 with membrane staining) breast cancer compared with the physician’s choice of chemotherapy [15, 16]. However, trastuzumab deruxtecan—a HER2-targeted antibody-drug conjugate (ADC)—was not studied in patients with HER2-0 without membrane staining or HR-negative breast cancer in the 1L setting, which highlights the ongoing need for therapies that can be broadly used in the 1L treatment of HER2-negative breast cancer.

Eribulin mesylate (known as eribulin) is a nontaxane microtubule dynamics inhibitor [17] reported to induce a phenotypic reversal from epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) to mesenchymal–epithelial transition (MET) in breast cancer cells [18]. Eribulin mesylate has established efficacy in heavily pretreated patients with HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer. In the EMBRACE study, eribulin significantly increased the median OS in patients with locally recurrent or metastatic breast cancer who received 2–5 prior lines of chemotherapy compared with treatment of physician’s choice, (median OS 13.1 vs. 10.6 months, respectively, P = 0.041, log-rank) [19]. Eribulin further demonstrated prolonged median OS (15.9 months) in patients with metastatic breast cancer who received ≤ 3 prior chemotherapy treatments, including an anthracycline and a taxane in Study 301 [20]. Notably, in a pooled analysis of EMBRACE and Study 301, wherein 11% of patients did not receive prior chemotherapy for metastatic breast cancer, patients treated with eribulin had favorable OS results compared with the control arms [21].

Recently, E7389-LF was developed as a liposomal formulation of eribulin mesylate and is hypothesized to improve the accumulation of eribulin in tumor tissue. E7389-LF demonstrated improved antitumor activity compared with eribulin in a preclinical model [22] and was well tolerated in patients with solid tumors [23]. In the open-label phase 1 Study 114 of E7389-LF in Japanese patients with advanced solid tumors, a recommended regimen of 2.0 mg/m2 as free eribulin once every 3 weeks (Q3W) was determined based on the dose-escalation part [24]. In the dose-expansion part 1 of Study 114 that examined Japanese patients with HER2-negative breast cancer who received prior chemotherapy of an anthracycline and a taxane and had ≤ 3 prior chemotherapy regimens for advanced/metastatic breast cancer, the overall objective response rate (ORR) was 35.7% (95% confidence interval [CI] 18.6–55.9). Notably, of the 7 patients without prior chemotherapy for advanced/metastatic breast cancer, 5 presented with a partial response (PR; ORR 71.4%) [25]. In addition to antitumor activity, previous reports of E7389-LF have shown increases in vascular- and immune-related biomarkers, suggesting that treatment contributes to remodeling of the tumor microenvironment [24, 25].

Here, we present the antitumor activity and safety data from the dose-expansion part 6 of Study 114 in Japanese patients with confirmed metastatic HER2-negative breast cancer who had not received prior chemotherapy for locally advanced/metastatic disease across molecular subtypes.

Methods

Study design

Study 114 (NCT03207672) is an open-label phase 1 trial of E7389-LF, a liposomal formulation of eribulin, in patients with solid tumors. Study 114 consists of a previously reported dose-escalation part [24] and 6 dose-expansion cohorts conducted in patients with various solid tumors. Here, we focus on the dose-expansion part 6 of Study 114. This part included patients with breast cancer who received the recommended dose of E7389-LF free base intravenously at 2.0 mg/m2 on day (D) 1 of a 21-day cycle (C), Q3W, until disease progression, development of unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent.

Patients

Japanese patients (≥ 20 years of age) were required to have a confirmed diagnosis of HER2-negative breast cancer as determined by an immunohistochemistry (IHC) score of 0 (HER2-0), or an IHC score of 1 + /2 + and a negative fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH; HER2-low). IHC tests were performed using FDA-approved HER2 testing kits as follows: Ventana Ultraview Pathway HER2 in 12 patients (46.2%), Ventana I-VIEW Pathway HER2 in 3 patients (11.5%), and the Bond polymer system HER2 test (CB11) in 1 patient (3.8%). Patients with TNBC were eligible if they were diagnosed as programmed cell-death ligand 1 (PD-L1)-negative per local testing or were clinically judged as not eligible for atezolizumab or another immuno-checkpoint inhibitor-containing regimen by their site investigator. Patients had to have at least 1 measurable lesion based on Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1); lesions previously treated with radiotherapy or loco-regional therapies must have shown evidence of progressive disease to be deemed measurable. Patients could not have received prior chemotherapy regimens for advanced or metastatic disease.

In addition, eligible patients must have had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1 and adequate organ function. Patients were excluded if they had a Child–Pugh score B or C indicating liver dysfunction, an active or acute oral infection requiring dental treatment, or a confirmed radiologic disease progression within 12 months after completing a taxane regimen in the neo- and/or adjuvant setting. Prior hormonal therapy including combination therapy (eg, containing a cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 [CDK4/6] inhibitor or everolimus) and bone-modifying agents (eg, bisphosphonates, denosumab) were not counted as prior therapies.

Study objectives and assessments

The primary objective of Study 114 was to determine the maximum tolerated dose of E7389-LF (previously reported to be 2.0 mg/m2 Q3W) [24]. Secondary objectives assessed safety, pharmacokinetic parameters, ORR, and PFS. Exploratory endpoints included PFS on next-line therapy (PFS2) defined from the start of study treatment until disease progression on next-line therapy, OS, disease control rate (DCR), clinical benefit rate (CBR), and potential biomarkers of E7389-LF. DCR was defined as the proportion of patients who had a best overall response (BOR) of complete response (CR), PR, or stable disease (SD) (≥ 5 weeks after C1D1). CBR is the proportion of patients with a BOR of CR, PR, or durable SD (≥ 23 weeks of SD).

Exploratory subanalyses of tumor response endpoints were conducted by HER2 status (HER2-0 or HER2-low), disease type (luminal breast cancer or TNBC), and presence of visceral crisis (lung or liver) or nonvisceral metastases. Additionally, PFS2 was assessed according to second-line treatment (chemotherapy or endocrine therapy or ADC).

Tumors were assessed by investigators based on RECIST v1.1 at screening, every 6 weeks from C1D1, at discontinuation, and when clinically indicated. After week 24, tumor assessments were conducted every 12 weeks. CR and PR were to be confirmed by a subsequent evaluation at least 28 days later. During survival follow-up, survival status and subsequent anticancer therapy were collected every 3 months (± 2 weeks) or per request from the sponsor after discontinuation from study treatment; information on the reasons for discontinuing subsequent therapy was collected for PFS2 assessment. Safety assessments consisted of monitoring and recording all adverse events, including all grades according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events of the National Cancer Institute version 4.03 (for both increasing and decreasing severity), and serious adverse events. Of particular interest was febrile neutropenia, which was monitored and was considered for subanalyses; at the investigators’ discretion, pegfilgrastim agents could be administered for the prevention of febrile neutropenia. Premedication with antihistamines was instituted on C1D1, other prophylactic medications were further administered at the investigator’s discretion on C1D1.

Blood (serum) biomarker samples were collected from patients predose on D1 of each cycle (C1–C6) and on D8 of C1. Plasma concentrations of eribulin and biomarkers were measured by a validated liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry method. A total of 78 markers were planned for analysis to assess the tumor microenvironment, including tumor immunity and vascularization.

Statistical analysis

To test the null hypothesis that the true response rate is 21.2% based on the ORR of paclitaxel [12] against a 1-sided alternative, at least 22 patients are required to achieve a type I error rate of 5% and a power of 85% when the true response rate is 50.0%. Assuming that approximately 2 patients may not be appropriately assessed for tumors as scheduled and withdrawn from the study, the sample size needed was determined to be 24 patients. All safety and tumor response analyses were performed on the safety analysis set defined as all patients who received at least 1 dose of the study drug.

BOR was summarized and ORR, DCR, and CBR were provided with 95% and 90% (ORR in overall population only) CIs calculated with the Clopper-Pearson method. PFS, OS, and PFS2 were estimated and plotted over time using the Kaplan–Meier method. 95% CIs were calculated based on Greenwood formula and a generalized Brookmeyer and Crowley method with log–log transformation. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS software (version 9.2 or later, SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

Serum protein biomarkers were analyzed for biomarkers in which ≥ 70% of the samples demonstrated levels above the lower limit of quantification. Percent changes from C1D1 were assessed using the 1-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The cutoff value (33rd percentile) for serum biomarker percentage changes classified patients into a “low” group (with percentage change in serum biomarkers < cutoff value) and a “high” group (with percentage change in serum biomarkers ≥ cutoff value). Dichotomized analyses of each serum biomarker were performed at C1D8 and C5D1, and the hazard ratios of PFS were calculated using univariate Cox regression and a log-rank test to investigate possible prognostic biomarkers for PFS.

Results

Patients

A total of 26 patients with breast cancer were included in the dose-expansion cohort 6 (Table 1). The median age was 59.0 years (range 35, 77). Of patients included in this analysis, 22 (84.6%) had luminal breast cancer and 4 (15.4%) had TNBC; 6 patients (23.1%) had de novo stage IV disease, and 19 (73.1%) had recurrent disease. Visceral (lung or liver) metastatic sites were observed in 20 patients (76.9%); 6 patients (23.1%) had nonvisceral metastatic sites. Fourteen patients (53.8%) received prior taxane or anthracycline regimens for adjuvant or neoadjuvant treatment of breast cancer, and 15 (57.7%) received a prior CDK4/6 inhibitor for metastatic disease (Table 1). Safety

A summary of treatment-related treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) is shown in Table 2. Treatment-related TEAEs led to study drug discontinuation in 3 (11.5%) patients, reduction in 15 (57.7%) patients, and interruption in 3 (11.5%) patients; the 3 leading to drug discontinuation of E7389-LF were drug hypersensitivity, peripheral motor neuropathy, and interstitial lung disease (n = 1 each). Treatment-related TEAEs of any grade and grade ≥ 3 severity occurred in 100% and 96.2% of the 26 enrolled patients, respectively (Table 2). All 26 patients were given prophylactic medication for hypersensitivity management during C1: 23 received an antihistamine alone and 3 received an antihistamine plus a steroid. During C1, 7 of 23 patients (30.4%) given prophylactic antihistamine and one (33.3%) of the 3 patients given both a prophylactic antihistamine and a steroid developed a drug hypersensitivity reaction, all hypersensitivity events were grade 1. Additionally, 7 of 26 patients received prophylactic pegylated granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (peg-GCSF) in C1, none of whom developed febrile neutropenia of any grade in C1. Febrile neutropenia was observed in 2 of 19 (10.5%) patients who did not receive prophylactic pegylated granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (peg-GCSF) in C1.

Antitumor activity

Among all 26 patients in the expansion cohort, no patient had a CR, and 8 (30.8%) had a confirmed PR, for an ORR of 30.8% (90% CI 16.3–48.7, 95% CI 14.3–51.8) (Table 3). Discussion

Here, we report results of the dose-expansion part 6 of Study 114 in Japanese patients with confirmed metastatic HER2-negative breast cancer (including HER2-0 and HER2-low breast cancer) who had not received prior chemotherapy for locally advanced/metastatic disease.

E7389-LF (2.0 mg/m2 Q3W as a free base) was tolerated and had a manageable safety profile in patients with metastatic HER2-negative breast cancer and who did not receive prior metastatic chemotherapy regimens. E7389-LF showed antitumor activity in the 1L setting, similar to a previous report of E7389-LF that required prior treatment with an anthracycline and a taxane [25].

Adverse events developed in all patients and 3 patients discontinued treatment due to treatment-related TEAEs (drug hypersensitivity, peripheral motor neuropathy, and interstitial lung disease). The safety profile was consistent with the previous study of E7389-LF in cohort 1 of the dose-expansion part of Study 114 [25]. Febrile neutropenia in C1 was only observed in patients who did not receive peg-GCSF (n = 2). The use of prophylactic peg-GCSF might be considered to prevent febrile neutropenia in high-risk subpopulations including elderly patients or patients with a clinical history of febrile neutropenia developed on previous chemotherapies in perioperative settings.

Regarding hypersensitivity reactions, the importance of antihistamine premedication was suggested in a prior cohort of this study enrolling 34 patients with gastric cancer [26]. In that study, no infusion-related reactions were encountered in those who received a steroid and antihistamine (n = 3) or only an antihistamine (n = 10), while 6 (28.6%) had an infusion-related reaction among 21 patients who received no premedication with steroid or antihistamine [26]. Here, although hypersensitivity reactions during C1 were relatively common (30.4%) in patients who received prophylactic antihistamines, all events during C1 were grade 1 and most hypersensitivity events throughout the study were of grade 1–2. Thus, antihistamines may have some merit in preventing severe reactions, and should be investigated further. It remains unclear whether the addition of steroids provides any clinical benefit, as only 3 patients in this study received both antihistamines and steroids.

E7389-LF showed promising antitumor activity as a 1L chemotherapy treatment in patients with HER2-negative breast cancer; ORR was 30.8% and the median PFS was 8.1 months in the overall population. Median OS was not reached; thus, additional follow-up will be needed. Acknowledging the limitations of cross-study comparisons, E7389-LF showed a median PFS comparable to that of the historical data of paclitaxel administered in the 1L setting (median PFS 5.9–11.8 months) [12]. Moreover, in this study, E7389-LF showed favorable PFS compared with a historical analysis of eribulin in patients who had no prior chemotherapy for advanced/metastatic disease (8.1 months vs. 4.2 months, respectively) [27]. Considering the differences between E7389-LF and eribulin mesylate, liposomal encapsulation of eribulin may have contributed to an enhanced antitumor effect, resulting in prolonged PFS.

Recently, the ADC trastuzumab deruxtecan showed promising median PFS (13.2 months) in patients with HR-positive and HER2-low or HER2-ultralow metastatic breast cancer, but this treatment was not examined for patients with HR-positive HER2-0 or HR-negative HER2-low and HER2-0 metastatic breast cancer [15], thus cross-study comparisons between E7389-LF and trastuzumab deruxtecan are limited.

Given the known differences among subtypes of HER2-negative breast cancer, we explored antitumor activity outcomes across molecular subtype (luminal vs. TNBC), HER2-negative status (HER2-0 vs. HER2-low), and other disease characteristics. Median PFS was comparable between patients with luminal breast cancer (8.1 months) and TNBC (9.8 months). Similarities in PFS and OS among luminal breast cancer and TNBC subtypes observed in our metastatic patient population are consistent with those observed in a meta-analysis of patients with metastatic breast cancer treated with chemotherapy [28]. ORR appeared favorable in the HER2-0 group versus the HER2-low group (66.7% vs. 20.0%), however median PFS was similar between groups (8.4 months vs. 8.1 months, respectively).

Changes in vasculature- and IFNγ-related serum markers were observed in all patients who received E7389-LF, suggesting that they are pharmacodynamic. Per a previous study, E7389-LF may induce vascular remodeling and increase T-cell infiltration in the tumor tissue, either directly via cytotoxic effects on tubulin inhibition or indirectly by changing the tumor microenvironment [29]. Notably, our study suggests that changes in vascular and IFNγ-related markers appear to be associated with improved PFS. We also found the median PFS2 for patients who received subsequent chemotherapy (16.2 months) to be notable; thus, it is possible that changes observed in serum IFNγ and vascular markers indicate a change in the tumor microenvironment that persists after treatment discontinuation. Further studies are needed to explore the potential link between changes in serum biomarkers and the observed antitumor effect in the clinical setting.

This study has several limitations, such as the small sample size overall (particularly in patients with TNBC) and inclusion of Japanese patients only. Subgroup analyses of OS with longer follow-up are further needed. Biomarker analyses were performed in serum samples rather than tumor-specific samples and could indicate systemic changes rather than changes in the tumor microenvironment. Moreover, ADCs were not established as major therapeutic options in the first-line treatment of breast cancer at the time this study was initiated. However, later advances in the field have significantly changed the treatment landscape. In TNBC, sacituzumab govitecan [30] and datopotamab deruxtecan [31] have demonstrated superior efficacy compared with standard chemotherapy in the first-line setting, emerging as promising new standard-of-care candidates. Moreover, in HR-positive breast cancer, the introduction of novel HER2 classifications (such as HER2-low and HER2-ultralow) has facilitated the adoption of the ADC trastuzumab deruxtecan as treatment for patients with these HER2 profiles [32], further transforming current therapeutic strategies.

Despite these shifts in clinical practice, the present study demonstrates that E7389‑LF, which operates through mechanisms distinct from those of ADCs, exhibits meaningful clinical activity in patients with HER2‑negative breast cancer receiving first-line therapy. These findings suggest that E7389‑LF may continue to represent a potential therapeutic option within the modern and increasingly diversified treatment environment, especially for patients without target molecule expression corresponding to approved ADCs.

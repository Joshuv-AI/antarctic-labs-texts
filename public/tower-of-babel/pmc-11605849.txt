Interplay between personality and attitude towards emotions with creative self concept among young adults
Authors: Ananya Gupta, Swati Pathak, Neeraj Panwar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCreative self-concept, intimately intertwined with the personality traits and plays a pivotal role in shaping individuals’ behavioral tendencies. Personality traits are largely responsible to influence how people perceive and navigate their creative abilities and self-expression. Moreover, attitudes towards emotions are another key facet of one’s psychological landscape, impacting their inclination to perceive, process, and manage emotional experiences. Keeping this view, the present research attempts to explore the interconnectedness of creative self-concept, personality traits, and attitudes towards emotions among young adults, as well as focuses on exploring the predictors of creative self concept. For this purpose participants consisted of 200 young adults with a mean age of 21.20 years. Statistical outcomes revealed that creative self concept is a significant positive correlate of openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, attitude towards sadness, and attitude towards fear. Additionally, stepwise multiple regression analysis confirmed that openness (R2 = 27%), neuroticism (R2 = 2%) and attitude towards sadness (R2 = 2%) emerged as the significant predictors of creative self concept. Findings from the current research concludes that for young adults to have self-perception in the realm of creativity, personality traits and attitude towards emotions are significant contributing factors. By recognizing and employing these connections, individuals, educators, counselors, and practitioners can contribute to the cultivation of creativity and personal development.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40359-024-02128-3.Keywords: Creative self concept, Personality, Attitude towards emotions, Creativity

Background

The transition from adolescence to young adulthood is a pivotal phase in one’s life, marked by profound personal and social changes with the central aspect of evolving attitude towards varied emotions (joy, sadness, anger, disgust, fear). The knowledge about emotions plays a crucial role in shaping how young adults navigate their relationships, decisions, and overall well-being. Creative self-concept is understood as “the language that lives in the heart and the spirit.” This depiction encapsulates the profound depth of its connection to inner truth, as the creative expression serves to be a transformative force, guiding individuals toward a profound understanding of themselves. It is through the act of creative expression that individuals embark on a journey to connect with their authentic selves. This journey is nothing short of enchanting, an odyssey of self-discovery that transcends mere creation; it is a pathway to self-enlightenment and the embodiment of personal authenticity [1].

Extensive research has delved into the intricate relationship between personality, creativity, and the creative self-concept which often celebrates the uniqueness of creative individuals (10; 11 & 12). Furthermore, research also revealed that openness to experience, conscientiousness, and neuroticism are predictors of creative self-efficacy in both the genders. On the one hand, for female participants agreeableness had an adverse relationship with creative self-efficacy, whereas it was found to be a favorable association with extraversion in women but not in their male counterparts. Notably, male participants found high on conscientiousness reported better creative self-efficacy. Additionally, creative self-efficacy was consistently predicted by extraversion, neuroticism, and openness, irrespective of gender [13].

Openness to experience, as reported in the body of literature also indicates that, most consistent and strongly associated with creativity (14 & 15). In a comprehensive analysis of the impact of the personality traits (openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism) on individuals creativity, research delves into the moderating role of extrinsic motivation, it was concluded that extraversion and openness to experience had positive significant effects on creative performance [16]. However, there remains an underexplored dimension in the study of personality variability among individuals. This perspective acknowledges the uniqueness inherent to each individual [17].

The creative self-concept takes center stage in the exploration that revolves around the convictions individuals hold regarding their own abilities and plays a pivotal role in shaping task performance [6]. Beyond task performance, it extends to encompass personal growth and achievement. The benefits of creative expression are not confined by age, exemplifying its enduring potential to enrich lives [7]. However, the effects of emotion on creativity have yielded conflicting results. Further statistical findings indicated that the effects of emotions on creativity depend on numerous conditions. In general, a positive mood tends to enhance creative performance [8].

Moreover, a Chinese study over a 14-day period shed light on the role of creative self concept in happiness and confirmed the mediating role of creative self-assessment in the relationship between creative self-concept and positive affect, as well as the chain-mediating role of creative behavior and creative self-assessment [9]. Creative self-efficacy signifies a person’s belief in their capacity to carry out creative tasks successfully. It explores how people judge their capacity to come up with original ideas, find creative solutions, and engage in creative activities [2]. Also, there is a significant role of a person’s earlier creative successes by pointing out that these experiences impact their sense of the difficulties that come with creative endeavors [3]. Furthermore, creative personal identity evaluates how much a person regards creativity as a key component of their self-concept [4]. In the similar lines, findings suggest that assessment frequently entails looking at how individuals incorporate creativity into their self-identity [5].

Researchers assert that individual differences in primary emotions, often regarded as the foundational elements of human personality, play a significant role in shaping the very essence of human personality [18]. Additionally, there exists a concrete connection between primary emotions and personality traits [19]. These emotions, rooted in the deepest and most ancient regions of the brain, interact in harmony with the top-down regulatory mechanisms of the cortex. It is through this interplay that an individual’s unique personality takes shape, emerging from the strength of emotional bursts originating in subcortical brain regions and their capacity to exercise conscious control over these emotional processes through cortical regulation.

In a parallel vein, attitudes toward emotions are structured within a framework that acknowledges discrete emotions as distinct entities, moving beyond the conventional categorization based on valence (positive and negative) and arousal (intensity) dimensions [30]. This perspective posits that individuals hold distinct attitudes toward specific emotions, thereby further molding their emotional experiences and responses. Similarly, Eysenck’s biological theory of personality states that neuroticism dimension also affects how individuals reciprocate to an emotional arousal (cited from [31]) and Extraversion appears to be especially inclined towards experiencing high arousal states [32]. Additionally, the relationship between emotional valence and arousal on creative task performance describes how emotions influence creativity [33].

In light of these multifaceted considerations, it becomes evident that the creative self-concept, intricately woven with the personality traits of openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism, assumes a central role in shaping the behavioral inclinations of individuals. These personality dimensions influence how individuals perceive, harness, and express their creative capacities in all areas. Simultaneously, attitudes toward emotions form a significant facet of an individual’s psychological landscape, wielding influence over their approach to perceiving, processing, and managing emotional experiences. Thus, this intricate interplay between the creative self-concept, the Big Five Personality traits, and attitudes toward emotions forms a complex and integral framework that shapes an individual’s approach to both creativity and emotional well-being.

Rationale of the study

This research aims to explore the relationship among creative self concept, personality and attitude towards emotions among young adults. There is a small body of literature that explains how attitude towards emotions has a significant impact on emotional regulation, overall well-being, and mental health outcomes. People who have positive views towards emotions and see them as enablers of development and wellbeing are more likely to adopt adaptive emotion regulation techniques, which improve their emotional well-being [21]. Conversely, individuals who maintain negative attitudes toward emotions, viewing them as obstacles or threats to be avoided, are more inclined to employ maladaptive emotion regulation techniques, often leading to diminished emotional well-being [22].

In this regard, personality traits have been overlooked and generated to bridge the gap by assessing personality traits along with attitude toward emotions and creative self-concept. Understanding this relationship can empower individuals to harness their creative self concept in various life domains beyond just artistic skills. In a concise form, this study aims to inform education, clinical approaches, and personal development by unraveling the connections between personality, creativity, and attitude towards emotions in young adults.

Method

Research design

The primary objective in the present study was directed towards finding out the relationship between creative self concept, personality and attitude towards emotions among young adults using a correlation design and the above variables were measured through standardized scales namely Short Scale for Creative Self (SSCS), Big Five Inventory (BFI) and Attitude towards Emotions Scale (ATE).

Participants

The participants consisted of 200 college students aged 18 to 25 with the mean age of 21.20 years (SD = 1.90). There were 31.40% males, 67.20% females and 1.50% belonged to the category of others respectively. Majority of the participants were students (81.90%), only 13.70% participants were employed and 3.90% were unemployed. The participants were contacted through snowball sampling.

Measures

The Short scale of Creative Self (SSCS) developed by Karwowski was used to measure the creative self concept of the respondents. The scale measures trait-like creative self-efficacy (6 items) and creative personal identity (5 items). It consists of 11 statements such as “I think I am a creative person”; “I know I can efficiently solve even complicated problems” etc. It is a 5 point likert scale ranging from 1 - Definitely not, 2 - somewhat not, 3- neither yes or no, 4 - somewhat yes, and 5 - definitely yes. Reliability for creative self efficacy is 0.92 & for creative personal identity is 0.91 [23].

The Big Five Inventory (BFI) developed by John and Srivastava was used to measure the personality of the respondents. It consists of 44 items such as “I see Myself as someone who, tends to find fault with others”; “I see Myself as someone who, can be somewhat careless” etc. and measures personality amongst the subscales of openness (10 items), conscientiousness (9 items), extraversion (8 items), agreeableness (9 items), and neuroticism (8 items) on a 5 point likert scale ranging from 1 - Disagree strongly, 2- Disagree a little, 3 - Neither agree nor disagree, 4 - Agree a little, and 5 - Agree strongly. Test-Retest reliability was found significant and Convergent discriminant validity showed adequate coefficients [24].

Attitude Towards Emotions (ATE Scale) developed by Harmon-Jones consists of 29 items such as “ I like the feeling of power I get from expressing my anger”; “ I prefer to hang around with people who make me happy” etc. and measures attitude towards emotions amongst five dimensions of attitude towards anger (5 items), attitude towards joy (5 items), attitude towards sadness (6 items), attitude towards disgust (6 items) and attitude towards fear (6 items) on a 5 point likert scale ranging from 1 - rarely/never 2 - occasionally 3 - sometimes 4 - often 5 - almost always/always. Adequate reliability and validity was to be found for the scale [20].

Procedure

The online questionnaire in the form of Google forms, was provided to the participants with the objective to find the role of personality and attitude towards emotions in relation to creative self concept. The participants were provided with an informed consent form before proceeding. After receiving the consent from all the participants, the data collected was coded and analyzed with an appropriate statistical analysis. Ethical consideration and data privacy were taken into consideration throughout the research process and it has been approved by The Research Conduct and Ethics Committee (RCEC) at CHRIST University. Lastly, the findings of the study shed light on the role of personality and attitude towards emotions in relation to creative self concept.

Data analysis

The present study used IBM Statistical Program Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) software version 21.0 to analyze the coded data. Firstly, the descriptive statistics such as Mean, Standard deviation provide a comprehensive overview of the dataset’s central tendencies and variability. Internal consistency of the items were also calculated. Furthermore, Pearson’s product moment correlation was used to analyze the relationship between the variables and a stepwise multiple regression was used to examine the predictive relationship between the dataset.

The statistical findings from Table 1 reveals that individuals who believe in themselves and their abilities while performing a task tend to be imaginative i.e., openness (r = .52; p < .001), determined to pursue their goals i.e. conscientiousness (r = .30; p < .001), sociable and enthusiastic i.e., extroversion (r = .27; p < .001), and trustworthy i.e., agreeableness (r = .21; p < .01).

Furthermore, out of five only two of the dimensions of attitude of emotions found to be correlated with creative self concept, including attitude towards sadness (r = .15; p < .05) which implies that these individuals have an ability to regulate and express emotions drawing from personal experiences and attitude towards fear (r = .16; p < .05) which play a role in shaping creative self-concept by influencing individuals’ approach to risk-taking, resilience, and personal growth (see Table 2).

Summary of correlation matrix (see Table 3) reveals that participants found to be high on receptiveness to new experience and ideas hold more positive attitudes towards joy (r = .22; p < .01). Further, findings indicate that participants who are more engaged in moral based behavior reported higher levels of attitude towards sadness (r = .15; p < .05 ) and fear (r = r = .19; p < .01 ). Participants who exhibit an extroverted trait reported a more positive attitude towards anger (r = .22; p < .01 ), sadness (r = .21; p < .01) and fear (r = .32; p < .001). On the other hand, participants those who show more of agreeableness traits found to be reporting positive attitude towards joy (r = .22; p < .01) whereas, neuroticism traits are more associated with attitude towards joy (r = .28; p < .001), sadness (r = .21; p < .01) and fear (r = .19; p < .01).

Final objective of the study is to determine the predictors of the creative self concept by using the stepwise regression analysis and the statistical outcomes are provided through Table 4. The findings revealed that two of the personality traits including openness and neuroticism along with one dimension of attitude towards emotions i.e., attitude towards sadness emerged as the significant predictors of the creative self concept.

Further, stepwise multiple regression analysis confirmed that openness emerged as the primary and strongest positive predictor and contributed 27% of the total variance towards the create self concept, followed by neuroticism and attitude towards sadness, both emerged as the negative predictor and each contributes an additional 2% of the variance (over and above contributed by openness) towards creative self concept. In total, all three predictors contribute a variance of 31% towards creative self concept.

Discussion

This study aimed to clarify the relationship between creative self-concept, attitudes toward emotions, and personality factors. The results indicate that people with greater levels of extraversion, conscientiousness, openness, and agreeableness also believe they are more creative. Conversely, people who exhibit higher levels of neuroticism could find it difficult to maintain mental health and might be less confident in their creative abilities. Previous studies have discovered a negative correlation between neuroticism and both of these variables [25, 26]. To further understand the link between neuroticism and extraversion in this setting, more research is necessary.

Furthermore, the results of this study cast doubt on the widespread notion that bad feelings are invariably a hindrance to creativity. It implies that some people may really find that being more prone to negative emotions and being in situations that elicit significant negative emotional reactions increases their creative creativity. The aforementioned results align with previous studies [27, 28]. Consequently, comprehending the intricate connection between emotions and creativity can offer significant perspectives on augmenting artistic expression and inspiration.

The current study also showed that those who are more open also have a tendency to view melancholy in a more positive light, indicating that accepting unpleasant emotions could support a more creative self-concept. These results shed insight on the diverse nature of creativity by highlighting the intricate interactions among personality traits, emotions, and creative self-perception [13, 29].

The research findings may be explained within cultural and social context, even though they are not specifically contextualized there. The positive relationship shown between conscientiousness, agreeableness, openness to new experiences, and creative self-concept may be affected by the values that permeate diverse and culturally rich society norms. Cultural differences in how people express their emotions might also be important; for example, suppressed feelings of grief and terror may find creative outlets. The favorable correlation between openness and creative self-concept may be further explained by the education system’s rising emphasis on creativity and innovation, as well as the rich legacy of diverse art forms [34].

Conclusion

To conclude, the present study has given insights into the intricate dynamics between creative self-concept, personality traits, and attitudes towards emotions among young adults. The findings have confirmed a significant and positive correlation between the big five personality traits and creative self-concept, aligning with prior research. The present research also partially accepted the hypothesis regarding the relationships between personality traits and attitudes towards emotions. Additionally, traits like openness and neuroticism emerged as significant predictors of creative self-concept. These results hold implications for educational interventions, emotional well-being strategies, and future research, ultimately contributing to a deeper understanding of creative self concept, personality and attitude towards emotions in young adults.

Faster and Simpler Online Computation of String Net Frequency
Shunsuke Inenaga

Abstract
An occurrence of a repeated substring u u in a string S S is called a net occurrence if extending the occurrence to the left or to the right decreases the number of occurrences to 1. The net frequency ( NF ) of a repeated substring u u in a string S S is the number of net occurrences of u u in S S . Very recently, Guo et al. [SPIRE 2024] proposed an online O ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ σ ) O(n\log\sigma) -time algorithm that maintains a data structure of O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) space which answers Single-NF queries in O ⁡ ( m ​ log ⁡ σ + σ 2 ) O(m\log\sigma+\sigma^{2}) time and reports all answers of the All-NF problem in O ⁡ ( n ​ σ 2 ) O(n\sigma^{2}) time. Here, n n is the length of the input string S S , m m is the query pattern length, and σ \sigma is the alphabet size. The σ 2 \sigma^{2} term is a major drawback of their method since computing string net frequencies is originally motivated for Chinese language text processing where σ \sigma can be thousands large. This paper presents an improved online O ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ σ ) O(n\log\sigma) -time algorithm, which answers Single-NF queries in O ⁡ ( m ​ log ⁡ σ ) O(m\log\sigma) time and reports all answers to the All-NF problem in output-optimal O ​ ( | 𝖭𝖥 + ​ ( S ) | ) O(|\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)|) time, where 𝖭𝖥 + ​ ( S ) \mathsf{NF^{+}}(S) is the set of substrings of S S paired with their positive NF values. We note that | 𝖭𝖥 + ​ ( S ) | = O ​ ( n ) |\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)|=O(n) always holds. In contrast to Guo et al.’s algorithm that is based on Ukkonen’s suffix tree construction, our algorithm is based on Weiner’s suffix tree construction.

Strings

.
For any string

Let

.
Let
\Sigma^{+}=\Sigma\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
.
If
S=XYZ
, then
X
,
Y
, and
Z
are called
a
prefix
, a
substring
, and a
suffix
of
S
, respectively.
For any
1\leq i\leq j\leq|S|
,
let
S[i..j]
denote the substring of
S
that begins at position
i

and ends at position
j
in
S
.
For convenience, let
S[i..j]=\varepsilon
if
i>j
.
For any
1\leq i\leq|S|
, let
S[i]
denote the
i
th character of
S
.
For any string
T
, let

\mathsf{Substr}(S)
and

denote the sets of substrings and suffixes of

,

For any string

,

.

2.2
String Net Frequency and Maximal Repeats

For a string
w
and a string
S
,
let
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(w)
denote the number of occurrences of
w
in
S
,
i.e.

\mathsf{occ}_{S}(w)=|\{i\mid S[i..i+|w|-1]=w\}|.

A string
w
with
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(w)\geq 2
is called a
repeat
in
S
.
For a repeat
w\in\mathsf{Substr}(S)
of a string
S
,
let

\Phi_{S}(w)=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\Sigma\times\Sigma\mid\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w\beta)=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w)=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(w\beta)=1\}.

(1)

The
net frequency
(
NF
) of
w
in
S
,
denoted
\phi_{S}(w),
is the number of
ordered pairs
(\alpha,\beta)\in\Sigma\times\Sigma
of characters such that

\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w\beta)=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w)=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(w\beta)=1
, i.e.,

\phi_{S}(w)=|\Phi_{S}(w)|.

(2)

A repeat
w
in a string
S
is called
a
left-maximal repeat
in
S

if
w
is a prefix of
S
,
or there are at least two distinct characters
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}

that immediately precede
w
in
S

(i.e.
\alpha w,\alpha^{\prime}w\in\mathsf{Substr}(S)
).
Similarly,
a repeat
w
in
S
is called a
right-maximal repeat
in
S

if
w
is a suffix of
S
,
or there are at least two distinct characters
\beta,\beta^{\prime}

that immediately follow
w
in
S

(i.e.
w\beta,w\beta^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Substr}(S)
).
Let
\mathsf{LM}(S)
and
\mathsf{RM}(S)
denote the sets of
left-maximal and right-maximal repeats in
S
, respectively.

For a left-maximal repeat
u
in
S
,
a character
\alpha\in\Sigma
such that
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w)\geq 1

is called a
left-extension
of
w
in
S
.
Similarly, for a right-maximal repeat
w
in
S
,
a character
\beta\in\Sigma
such that
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(w\beta)\geq 1

is called a
right-extension
of
w
in
S
.

A repeat
u
is called a
maximal repeat
in
S

if
w\in\mathsf{LM}(S)\cap\mathsf{RM}(S)
.
Let
\mathsf{M}(S)=\mathsf{LM}(S)\cap\mathsf{RM}(S)
denote the set of maximal repeats in
S
.

Guo et al.
[
8
]
showed that
if
\phi_{S}(w)\geq 1
, then
w\in\mathsf{RM}(S)
.
Below we present a stronger statement.

Lemma 1
.

If
\phi_{S}(w)\geq 1
, then
w\in\mathsf{M}(S)
.

Proof.

Since
\phi_{S}(w)\geq 1
,
there exists a pair
(\alpha,\beta)
of characters
such that
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w)=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(w\beta)=1
.
Since
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(w)\geq 2
,
there is an occurrence
w=S[i..i+|w|-1]
of
w

such that
S[i-1]\neq\alpha
or
i=1
.
Similarly, there is an occurrence
w=S[j-|w|+1..j]
of
w

such that
S[j+1]\neq\beta
or
j=|S|
.
Thus
w\in\mathsf{LM}(S)\cap\mathsf{RM}(S)=\mathsf{M}(S)
.
∎

Let

\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)=\{(w,\phi_{S}(w))\mid w\in\mathsf{M}(S),\phi_{S}(w)\geq 1\}

denote the set of
pairs of string
w
and its NF that is positive in
S
.
It follows from Lemma
1
that if
(w,\phi_{S}(w))\in\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)
,
then
w\in\mathsf{M}(S)
.
Since
|\mathsf{M}(S)|\leq n-1
for any string of length
n
(c.f.
[
3
]
),
it is immediate that
|\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)|\leq n-1
.

2.3
Online Computation of String Net Frequency

We will design an online algorithm for computing
substrings that have positive values of net frequencies,
in which new characters are
prepended
to the input string.
Namely, our algorithm works in a right-to-left online manner.
Note that
\phi_{S}(w)
is closed under reversal, i.e.,

\phi_{S}(w)=\phi_{S^{R}}(w^{R})
.
Hence, by reversing the input string,
our algorithm can be used in the left-to-right online setting as well.

We consider the following problem of maintaining
the list
\mathcal{L}
that stores
\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)
for an online string
S
:

Problem 1

(Right-to-Left Online NF Computation)
.

Let
S
be a string of length
n

for which
\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)
has been computed.
Given a character
a\in\Sigma
,
update
\mathcal{L}
by computing

\mathsf{NF^{+}}(aS)\Delta\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)=\left(\mathsf{NF^{+}}(aS)\setminus\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)\right)\cup\left(\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)\setminus\mathsf{NF^{+}}(aS)\right)
.

Problem
1

requires one to consider the following cases
each time a new character
a\in\Sigma
is prepended to the current string
S
:

Case (i)

Increase the NFs of some existing maximal repeats: For each
w\in\mathsf{M}(aS)\cap\mathsf{M}(S)

such that
(w,\phi_{S}(w))\in\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)
and
\phi_{aS}(w)>\phi_{S}(w)\geq 1
,
replace
(w,\phi_{S}(w))
with
(w,\phi_{aS}(w))
in the list
\mathcal{L}
.
Also, for each
w\in\mathsf{M}(aS)\cap\mathsf{M}(S)

such that
(w,\phi_{S}(w))\notin\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)
and
\phi_{aS}(w)>\phi_{S}(w)=0
,
add
(w,\phi_{aS}(w))
in the list
\mathcal{L}
.

Case (ii)

Add new maximal repeats whose NFs become positive: For each
w\in\mathsf{M}(aS)\setminus\mathsf{M}(S)
such that
(w,\phi_{S}(w))\notin\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)
and
\phi_{aS}(w)>\phi_{S}(w)=0
,
add
(w,\phi_{aS}(w))
to the list
\mathcal{L}
.

Case (iii)

Decrease the NFs of some existing maximal repeats: For each
w\in\mathsf{M}(aS)\cap\mathsf{M}(S)

such that
(w,\phi_{S}(w))\in\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)
and
\phi_{aS}(w)<\phi_{S}(w)
,
replace
(w,\phi_{S}(w))
with
(w,\phi_{aS}(w))
in the list
\mathcal{L}

if
\phi_{aS}(w)\geq 1
,
and remove
(w,\phi_{S}(w))
from
\mathcal{L}

if
\phi_{aS}(w)=0
.

Example 1
.

Consider string
S=\mathtt{ababbababcababbb\$}

of which the set of maximal repeats is

\mathsf{M}(S)=\{\mathtt{b},\mathtt{ab},\mathtt{bb},\mathtt{bab},\mathtt{abab},\mathtt{ababb}\}

We have

•

\Phi_{S}(\mathtt{bb})=\{(\underline{\mathtt{b}},\underline{\mathtt{\$}})\}

\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{bb})=3

•

\Phi_{S}(\mathtt{bab})=\{(\mathtt{\underline{b}},\mathtt{\underline{a}})\}
and thus
\phi_{S}(\mathtt{bab})=1
, because
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{bab})=4
and
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{\underline{b}bab})=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{bab\underline{a}})=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{\underline{b}bab\underline{a}})=1

•

\Phi_{S}(\mathtt{abab})=\{(\mathtt{\underline{b}},\mathtt{\underline{c}})\}
and thus
\phi_{S}(\mathtt{abab})=1
, because
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{abab})=3
and
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{\underline{b}abab})=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{abab\underline{c}})=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{\underline{b}abab\underline{c}})=1

•

\Phi_{S}(\mathtt{ababb})=\{(\mathtt{\underline{c}},\mathtt{\underline{b}})\}
and thus

, because
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{ababb})=2
and
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{\underline{c}ababb})=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{ababb\underline{b}})=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\mathtt{\underline{c}ababb\underline{b}})=1
.

Since the other maximal repeats in
S
do not have positive net frequencies,
the list
\mathcal{L}
stores
\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)=\{(\mathtt{bb},1),(\mathtt{bab},1),(\mathtt{abab},1),(\mathtt{ababb},1)\}
.

Consider string
\mathtt{b}S=\mathtt{bababbababcababbb\$}
of which the set of maximal repeats is

\mathsf{M}(\mathtt{b}S)=\{\mathtt{b},\mathtt{ab},\mathtt{bb},\mathtt{bab},\mathtt{abab},\mathtt{ababb},\mathtt{babab}\}

For the new maximal repeat
\mathtt{babab}\in\mathsf{M}(\mathtt{b}S)\setminus\mathsf{M}(S)

\Phi_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{babab})=\{(\mathtt{\underline{b},\mathtt{\underline{c}}})\}
and thus
\phi_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{babab})=1
, because
\mathsf{occ}_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{babab})=2
and
\mathsf{occ}_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{\underline{b}babab})=\mathsf{occ}_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{babab\underline{c}})=\mathsf{occ}_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{\underline{b}babab\underline{c}})=1

For the existing maximal repeats in

, we have

•

\Phi_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{ababb})=\{(\mathtt{\underline{b}},\mathtt{\underline{a}}),(\mathtt{c},\mathtt{b})\}
and thus
\phi_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{abab})=2
, because
\mathsf{occ}_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{ababb})=2
,
\mathsf{occ}_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{\underline{b}ababb})=\mathsf{occ}_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{ababb\underline{a}})=\mathsf{occ}_{\mathtt{b}S}(\mathtt{\underline{b}ababb\underline{a}})=1

•

2.4
Suffix Trees and Weiner Links

For convenience,
we assume that each string
S
terminates with a unique character
\$
that does not appear elsewhere in
S
.

A compacted trie is a rooted tree such that

•

each edge is labeled by a non-empty string,

•

each internal node is branching, and

•

the string labels of the out-going edges of each node begin with mutually distinct characters.

The
suffix tree

[
18
]

for a string
S
, denoted
\mathsf{STree}(S)
,
is a compacted trie which represents
\mathsf{Suffix}(S)
.
We sometimes identify node
v
with the substring it represents.

For any string
S
of length
n
that terminates with
\$
,

\mathsf{STree}(S)
has exactly
n
leaves,
and has at most
n-1
internal nodes (including the root)
since every non-leaf node is branching.
By representing each edge label
x
with an ordered pair
\langle i,j\rangle

of integers such that
x=S[i..j]
,

\mathsf{STree}(S)
can be stored in
O(n)
space.

We define the
suffix link
of each non-root node
av

of
\mathsf{STree}(S)
with
a\in\Sigma
and
v\in\Sigma^{*}
,
by
\mathsf{slink}(av)=v
.
For each node
v
and
a\in\Sigma
,
we also define the reversed suffix link (a.k.a.
Weiner link
)
by
{\mathsf{W\_link}}_{a}({v})=avy
, where
y\in\Sigma^{*}
is the shortest string
such that
avy
is a node of
\mathsf{STree}(S)
.

{\mathsf{W\_link}}_{a}({v})
is undefined if
av\notin\mathsf{Substr}(S)
.
A Weiner link
{\mathsf{W\_link}}_{a}({v})=avy
is said to be
hard

if
y=\varepsilon
, and
soft
if
y\in\Sigma^{+}
.

It is clear that every internal node in
\mathsf{STree}(S)

represents a right-maximal repeat.
Hence, the first characters in the out-edges of each node
u

are right-extensions of the corresponding right-maximal repeat
u
in
S
.
Also, for each internal node
u
that is also a left-maximal repeat (i.e.,
u\in\mathsf{M}(S)
),
the characters
a\in\Sigma
, for which
{\mathsf{W\_link}}_{a}({u})
are defined, are the left-extensions of
u
in
S
.

Lemma 2

(
[
2
]
)
.

For any string
S
of length
n>2
,
the total number of hard and soft Weiner links in
\mathsf{STree}(S)
is at most
3n-4
.

Throughout the description of our algorithm in Section
3
,
we will assume that the input string
S
terminates with a unique end-marker
\$
.
We note that this assumption only gives little impact in our NF computation.
By our key lemma (Lemma
3
) from the next section,
we have that there can exist at most one character
\alpha

such that
(\alpha,\$)\in\Phi_{S}(w)
for a repeat
w
in
S
.
Moreover, there can exist at most one repeat
w

that can have
\$
in the second element
of the pair
(\alpha,\$)\in\Phi_{S}(w)
:
Let
w
be the longest repeated suffix of
S[1..n-1]
where
n=|S|
,
and let
\alpha w
be the shortest non-repeated suffix of
S[1..n-1]

where
\alpha\in\Sigma
.
Since
S[n]=\$
,
we have
\mathsf{occ}_{S[1..n-1]}(w)=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(w)>1
and

\mathsf{occ}_{S[1..n-1]}(\alpha w)=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w)=1
.
Since
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(w\$)=\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w\$)=1
,

(\alpha,\$)\in\Phi_{S}(w)
holds.
Note that no other suffixes of

can satisfy
these properties.

3
Algorithm

Let
S
be the input string.
As in the previous work
[
9
]
,
a node
w
in
\mathsf{STree}(S)
stores the NF value
\phi_{S}(w)
iff
\phi_{S}(w)\geq 1
.

3.1
Key Lemma

Our algorithm is based on the following lemma:

Lemma 3

(Unique Character Pairs)
.

Let
w
be a repeat in a string
S
.
Let
(\alpha,\beta)\in\Phi_{S}(w)
.
Then, for any character
\beta^{\prime}\neq\beta
,

(\alpha,\beta^{\prime})\notin\Phi_{S}(w)
.
Similarly, for any character
\alpha^{\prime}\neq\alpha
,

(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)\notin\Phi_{S}(w)
.

Proof.

For a contradiction assume that
(\alpha,\beta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{S}(w)
.
Then,
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w\beta)=1
and
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w\beta^{\prime})=1
.
Since
\beta\neq\beta^{\prime}
,
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w)\geq 2
.
However, since
(\alpha,\beta)\in\Phi_{S}(w)
, we have
\mathsf{occ}_{S}(\alpha w)=1
,
a contradiction.
The case for
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)
is symmetric.
∎

Lemma
3
implies that for any character
\alpha\in\Sigma

that is a left-extension of a (maximal) repeat
w
in
S
,
there is at most one character
\beta\in\Sigma
that contributes to
the positive NF of
w
in
S
.
Thus the following corollary is immediate:

Corollary 1
.

For any repeat
w
in a string
S
,

\phi_{S}(w)\leq\min\{|\Sigma^{L}_{w}|,|\Sigma^{R}_{w}|\}
,
where
\Sigma^{L}_{w}
and
\Sigma^{R}_{w}
are the sets of left-extensions
and right-extensions of
u
in
S
, respectively.

Since the total number of right-extensions of the maximal repeats
is most
2n-2
for any string of length
n\geq 2
(c.f.
[
3
]
),
the next lemma immediately follows from Lemma
1
and Corollary
1
.

Lemma 4
.

For any string
S
of length
n
,

\sum_{w\in\mathsf{Substr}(S)}\phi_{S}(u)\leq 2n-2
.

This bound is weaker than
the bound
\sum_{w\in\mathsf{Substr}(S)}\phi_{S}(w)\leq n
given by
Guo et al.
[
8
]
,
but it is sufficient for our purpose.
Namely, if needed, one can explicitly store the character pairs in
\Phi_{S}(w)

in
O(n)
total space instead of the counts
\phi_{S}(w)
.
Our algorithm to follow can easily be modified to store
the character pairs in
\Phi_{S}(w)
as well, in the same complexity.

3.2

Suppose that all the Weiner links have also been computed for

and find the lowest ancestor

There are two cases:

We continue climbing up the path until finding the lowest ancestor

(2) If

Lemma 5

(
[

2
]

The number of nodes visited during the update from

3.8
Complexities

Theorem 1
.

For a string
S
of length
n
and a character
a\in\Sigma
,
Weiner’s algorithm solves Problem
1

of online NF computation in
O(\log\sigma)
amortized time per character
using
O(n)
space.

Proof.

It is known that Weiner’s algorithm finds
the nodes
u
,
v
, and
z
in
O(1)
amortized time for
a new character
a
that is prepended to
S
(Lemma
5
).
It follows from our previous discussions that
given the nodes
u
,
v
, and
z
,
we can compute
\mathsf{NF^{+}}(aS)\triangle\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)

in
O(\log\sigma)
amortized time,
where the
\log\sigma
factor is the cost to access the Weiner links
from nodes
v
and
z
.

By Lemma
2
the number of soft and hard Weiner links is
O(n)
.
Since the suffix tree takes
O(n)
space,
the total space requirement is
O(n)
.
∎

Since all positive values of NFs are stored and maintained
in the respective nodes of the online suffix tree,
we obtain the following:

Corollary 2
.

There exists an online data structure that can be maintained in

O(n\log\sigma)
total time with
O(n)
space, and can answer

Single-NF
queries in
O(m\log\sigma)
time,
where
m
is the query pattern length.

By maintaining
\mathcal{L}
as a doubly-linked list
such that each element is associated to the corresponding node in the suffix tree, we obtain the following:

Corollary 3
.

There exists an online data structure that can be maintained in

O(n\log\sigma)
total time with
O(n)
space,
and can report all answers to the
All-NF
problem in output optimal

O(|\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)|)
time.

Guo et al.
[
8
]
also considered a version of the
All-NF
problem, named

,
that requires us to compute the product
|u|\cdot\phi_{S}(u)
for all pairs
(u,\phi_{S}(u))\in\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)

Guo et al.
[
8
]
proposed an offline algorithm that works in

time for

for integer alphabets,
where
\delta
denotes the
substring complexity

[
10
]

Our online algorithm can readily be extended to the
All-NF-Extract
problem:

Corollary 4
.

There exists an online data structure that can be maintained in

O(n\log\sigma)
total time with
O(n)
space,
and can report all answers to the
All-NF-Extract
problem in
output optimal
O(|\mathsf{NF^{+}}(S)|)

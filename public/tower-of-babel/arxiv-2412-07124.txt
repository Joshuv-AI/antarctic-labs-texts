Abstract
. We study a weakly asymmetric exclusion process with long jumps and with infinitely many extended reservoirs. We prove that the stationary fluctuations of the process are governed by the generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process or the stochastic Burgers equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions depending on the strength of the asymmetry of the dynamics.



1.
Introduction

The Kardar-Parisi-Zhang equation was introduced in
[
15
]
to describe the universal fluctuations of growing interfaces. Due to the non-linearity of the equation, it is ill-posed and has attracted a lot of attention in the mathematical society. Different methods have been proposed to solve the equation: the rough path theory developed by
[
13
]
, derivation of the Cole-hopf solution from the weakly asymmetric simple exclusion process (WASEP) by Bertini and Giacomin
[
5
]
, and derivation of the stationary energy solution from the WASEP by Gonçalves and Jara
[
10
]
. Recently, Matetski, Quastel and Remenik
[
17
]
constructed a Markov process, the KPZ fixed point, via the limit of rescaled height function for TASEP. This process is believed to be the universal limit for models in KPZ universality class.
It was shown in
[
20
]
that finite range asymmetric exclusion processes converge to the KPZ fixed point. A natural extension is to consider the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang equation with boundary conditions. In
[
8
]
, Corwin and Shen derived the KPZ equation on the unit interval with Neumann boundary conditions by considering the height functions of the asymmetric exclusion process on an interval. Gonçalves
et al.

[
12
]
derived the stochastic Burgers equation, which can be roughly viewed as the gradient of the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang equation, with Dirichlet boundary conditions from the boundary driven WASEP. See
[
6
,
7
,
19
]
and references therein for an excellent review on this topic.

The aim of this article is to derive the stochastic Burgers equation from other interacting particle systems. Precisely, we study the boundary driven WASEP with long jumps. Recently, particle systems with long jumps and infinitely extended reservoirs have been extensively investigated. Hydrodynamic limits for such models are closely related to the fractional Laplacian with various boundary conditions, see
[
4
,
2
,
3
]
and references therein. For fluctuations, Bernardin
et al.

[
1
]
studied a symmetric exclusion process with long jumps and infinitely extended reservoirs in the diffusive regime, where they derived generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes with various boundary conditions.

The dynamics we considered is composed of two parts: the first one is the same as in
[
1
]
, where particles jump on the segment
\Lambda_{n}=\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
according to some symmetric transition rate with long jumps subject to the exclusion rule, and the boundary of
\Lambda_{n}
acts as infinitely extended particle reservoirs. Precisely, the symmetric jump rate is defined as
s_{\alpha}(x)=|x|^{-1-\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
. On top of the symmetric dynamics, we add an asymmetric dynamics with jump rate given by
p_{\gamma}(x)=|x|^{-1-\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{\{x>0\}}
for
\gamma>0
. We assume the particle densities of the reservoirs are
1/2
. See Figure
2.1
for dynamics of the model. We mainly focus on the case
\alpha>2
. Let the symmetric dynamics accelerate by
n^{2}
, and the asymmetric dynamics by
n^{\theta}
for some
\theta>0
. Under Assumption
2.1
below, we prove that if
\theta<3/2
, then the limit of the density fluctuation fields is given by the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with Dirichlet boundary conditions, while if
\theta=3/2
, then the limit is given by the stochastic Burgers equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

The main novelties of the article are twofold. First, inspired by the work in
[
1
]
, we proposed a different definition to the solution of the stochastic Burgers equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions (see Definition
2.4
), which is equivalent to the one in
[
12
]
(see Definition
2.3
). Moreover, Definition
2.4
is quite natural from the model we considered. Second, the critical step of the proof is to prove the second-order Boltzmann-Gibbs principle, see Proposition
3.3
, which was first introduced in
[
10
]
and has been shown to hold for a large class of interacting particle systems, see
[
21
,
11
,
9
]
for example. Compared with previous works,
the boundary terms are more involved in this model due to the long jumps, and we need to take advantage of the test functions.

We finally remark that it is challenging for us to consider the case
\alpha<2
, from which we expect to derive fractional stochastic Burgers equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions. One of the problems for us is that we cannot find a proper space to define the solution to the limiting SPDE.

The article is organized as follows. We state the model and results in Section
2
. The proof is outlined in Section
3
. Section
4
is devoted to the proof of the second order Boltzmann-Gibbs principle,
i.e.
, Proposition
3.3
, which is the main step of the proof. Finally, we prove the tightness of the fluctuation field and the uniqueness to the solution of the limiting SPDE in Sections
5
and
6
respectively.

2.
Model and results

The state space of the exclusion process is
{\mathcal{X}}_{n}=\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{n}}
, where
\Lambda_{n}=\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
. Fix parameters
\theta,\gamma,\alpha>0
. The generator of the process is

L_{n}=n^{2}L_{s}+n^{\theta}L_{a}.

Above, for
f:{\mathcal{X}}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle L_{s}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}s_{\alpha}(x-y)[f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta)]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\notin\Lambda_{n}}s_{\alpha}(x-y)[f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)],

\displaystyle L_{a}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{y})[f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta)]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\leq 0}p_{\gamma}(x-y)(1-\eta_{x})[f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\geq n}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\eta_{x}[f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)],

where
\eta^{x,y}
is the configuration obtained from
\eta
after swapping the values of
\eta_{x}
and
\eta_{y}
, and
\eta^{x}
is the one after flipping the value of
\eta_{x}
,

\eta^{x,y}_{z}=\begin{cases}\eta_{x},\quad&\text{if }z=y,\\
\eta_{y},\quad&\text{if }z=x,\\
\eta_{z},\quad&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\quad\eta^{x}_{z}=\begin{cases}1-\eta_{x},\quad&\text{if }z=x,\\
\eta_{z},\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The jump rates are given by

s_{\alpha}(x)=|x|^{-1-\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{\{x\neq 0\}},\quad p_{\gamma}(x)=|x|^{-1-\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{\{x>0\}}.

The factor
1/2
in the definition of the generators corresponds to the particle density of the reservoirs. See Figure
2.1
for dynamics of the model.

Remark 2.1
.

The reservoir density
1/2
is important since under the Bernoulli product measure with constant density
1/2
, the center of the mass travels at speed zero. Otherwise, we are not aware of how to choose the proper space of test functions in Subsection
2.1
.

We make the following assumptions on the above parameters throughout the article.

Assumption 2.1
.

\alpha>2
,
\theta<\gamma\wedge 2
.

Remark 2.2
.

For
0<\alpha<2
or
\theta=\gamma
, the fractional Laplacian would appear in the limiting SPDE. In this case, we cannot find a proper space to define the solution to the fractional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process or the fractional stochastic Burgers equation with boundary conditions. That is the reason why we made the above assumptions on the parameters.

For
\rho\in(0,1)
, let
\nu_{\rho}
be the product measure on the state space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
with constant particle density
\rho
,

\nu_{\rho}(\eta_{x}=1)=\rho,\quad x\in\Lambda_{n}.

One could check directly that
\nu_{1/2}
is reversible for the generator
L_{s}
, and is invariant for
L_{a}
.

Denote by
\eta(t)\equiv\eta^{n}(t)
the Markov process with generator
L_{n}
starting from the measure
\nu_{1/2}
. Let
\mathbb{P}\equiv\mathbb{P}^{n}
be the probability measure on the path space
\mathcal{D}([0,\infty);\mathcal{X}_{n})
induced by the process
\eta(t)
, and by
\mathbb{E}\equiv\mathbb{E}^{n}
the corresponding expectation.

2.1.
The limiting SPDE

In this subsection, we rigorously define the solution to the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process and the stochastic Burgers equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Before that, we first introduce the space of test functions. Denote
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\left\{H\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1]):H^{(i)}(0)=H^{(i)}(1)=0,\;\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\rm Dir}

\displaystyle=\left\{H\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1]):H^{(2i)}(0)=H^{(2i)}(1)=0,\;\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\rm Neu}

\displaystyle=\left\{H\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1]):H^{(2i+1)}(0)=H^{(2i+1)}(1)=0,\;\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

We equip the above spaces with the family of seminorms
\{\|H^{(i)}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])},\;i\in\mathbb{N}\}
and denote respectively by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{S}_{\rm Dir}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{S}_{\rm Neu}^{\prime}
their topological duals. For
H,G\in L^{2}([0,1])
, denote

\big\langle H,G\big\rangle:=\int_{0}^{1}H(u)G(u)du.

2.1.1.
The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with Dirichlet boundary conditions

We first define the solution of the following Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with Dirichlet boundary conditions:

(2.1)

\partial_{t}\mathcal{Y}_{t}=A\Delta\mathcal{Y}_{t}+\sqrt{D}\nabla\dot{\mathcal{W}_{t}},

where
A,D>0
are two parameters and
\dot{\mathcal{W}_{t}}
is a standard
\mathcal{S}_{\rm Neu}^{\prime}
-valued white noise.

Definition 2.1

(The first definition of the solution to (
2.1
))
.

We say
\big\{\mathcal{Y}_{t},t\in[0,T]\big\}\in\mathcal{C}\big([0,T];\mathcal{S}_{\rm Dir}^{\prime}\big)
is a stationary solution of (
2.1
) if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

for any
t\in[0,T]
, for any
H,G\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm Dir}
,

(2.2)

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Y}_{t}(H)\mathcal{Y}_{t}(G)]=\frac{D}{2A}\big\langle H,G\big\rangle.

(2)

for any
H\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm Dir}
,

\mathcal{M}_{t}(H):=\mathcal{Y}_{t}(H)-\mathcal{Y}_{0}(H)-A\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}_{s}(\Delta H)ds

is a continuous martingale with quadratic variation

\big\langle\mathcal{M}(H)\big\rangle_{t}=tD\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2}.

Remark 2.3
.

We refer the readers to
[
1
, Theorem 2.13]
or
[
12
, Proposition 3.1]
for the uniqueness of the solution to (
2.1
).

In the following, we shall use an alternative definition of the solution to (
2.1
). The equivalence of the two definitions are proved in
[
1
, Theorem 2.13]
. For
\varepsilon>0
and
u\in[0,1]
, define
\iota_{\varepsilon,u}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\iota_{\varepsilon,u}(v)=\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf{1}\{u\leq v<u+\varepsilon\}\quad&\text{if }\;0\leq u<1-\varepsilon,\\
\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf{1}\{u-\varepsilon\leq v<u\}\quad&\text{if }\;1-\varepsilon\leq u\leq 1.\end{cases}

Definition 2.2

(The second definition of the solution to (
2.1
))
.

We say
\big\{\mathcal{Y}_{t},t\in[0,T]\big\}\in\mathcal{C}\big([0,T];\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\big)
is a stationary solution of (
2.1
) if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

the process
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
is stationary in the sense of (
2.2
).

(2)

for any
H\in\mathcal{S}
,

\mathcal{M}_{t}(H):=\mathcal{Y}_{t}(H)-\mathcal{Y}_{0}(H)-A\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}_{s}(\Delta H)ds

is a continuous martingale with quadratic variation

\big\langle\mathcal{M}(H)\big\rangle_{t}=tD\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2}.

(3)

for any
u\in\{0,1\}
,

(2.3)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}_{s}(\iota_{\varepsilon,u})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that the space of test functions in Definitions
2.1
and
2.2
are different. In Definition
2.1
, the information of the boundary conditions is contained in the space of test functions, while in Definition
2.2
, it is contained in Eqn. (
2.3
).

2.1.2.
The stochastic Burgers equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions

Next, we define the solution of the following stochastic Burgers equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions:

(2.4)

\partial_{t}\mathcal{Y}_{t}=A\Delta\mathcal{Y}_{t}+B\nabla\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{2}+\sqrt{D}\nabla\dot{\mathcal{W}_{t}},

where
A,B,D>0
and
\dot{\mathcal{W}_{t}}
is a standard
\mathcal{S}_{\rm Neu}^{\prime}
-valued white noise.

Definition 2.3

(The first definition of the solution to (
2.4
))
.

We say the process
\big\{\mathcal{Y}_{t},t\in[0,T]\big\}
in
\mathcal{C}\big([0,T];\mathcal{S}_{\rm Dir}^{\prime}\big)
is a stationary energy solution of (
2.4
) if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

the process
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
is stationary in the sense of (
2.2
).

(2)

the process
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
satisfies the following energy estimate: there exists some finite constant
C
such that for any
H\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm Dir}
, for any
\delta<\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and any
0\leq s<t\leq T
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\mathcal{A}_{s,t}^{\varepsilon}(H)-\mathcal{A}_{s,t}^{\delta}(H)\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq C(t-s)\varepsilon\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2},

where

(2.5)

\mathcal{A}_{s,t}^{\varepsilon}(H):=-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\mathcal{Y}_{r}\big(\iota_{\varepsilon,u}\big)\Big)^{2}\nabla H(u)du\,dr.

(3)

for any
H\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm Dir}
,

\mathcal{M}_{t}(H):=\mathcal{Y}_{t}(H)-\mathcal{Y}_{0}(H)-A\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}_{s}(\Delta H)ds-B\mathcal{A}_{t}(H)

is a continuous martingale with quadratic variation

\big\langle\mathcal{M}(H)\big\rangle_{t}=tD\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2},

where
\mathcal{A}_{t}(H)
is the
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
-limit of
\mathcal{A}_{0,t}^{\varepsilon}(H)
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

(4)

the reversed process
\{\mathcal{Y}_{T-t},0\leq t\leq T\}
satisfies condition (3) with
B
replaced by
-B
.

Remark 2.5
.

We refer the readers to
[
12
, Theorem 3.3]
for the uniqueness of the stationary energy solution to (
2.4
).

Similar to Definition
2.2
, below we give an alternative definition of the stationary energy solution to (
2.4
).

Definition 2.4

(The second definition of the solution to (
2.4
))
.

We say the process
\big\{\mathcal{Y}_{t},t\in[0,T]\big\}
in
\mathcal{C}\big([0,T];\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\big)
is a stationary energy solution of (
2.4
) if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

the process
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
is stationary in the sense of (
2.2
).

(2)

the process
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
satisfies the following energy estimate: there exists some finite constant
C
such that for any
H\in\mathcal{S}
, for any
\delta<\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and any
0\leq s<t\leq T
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\mathcal{A}_{s,t}^{\varepsilon}(H)-\mathcal{A}_{s,t}^{\delta}(H)\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq C(t-s)\varepsilon\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2},

where

(2.6)

\mathcal{A}_{s,t}^{\varepsilon}(H):=-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\mathcal{Y}_{r}\big(\iota_{\varepsilon,u}\big)\Big)^{2}\nabla H(u)du\,dr.

(3)

for any
H\in\mathcal{S}
,

\mathcal{M}_{t}(H):=\mathcal{Y}_{t}(H)-\mathcal{Y}_{0}(H)-A\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}_{s}(\Delta H)ds-B\mathcal{A}_{t}(H)

is a continuous martingale with quadratic variation

\big\langle\mathcal{M}(H)\big\rangle_{t}=tD\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2},

where
\mathcal{A}_{t}(H)
is the
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
-limit of
\mathcal{A}_{0,t}^{\varepsilon}(H)
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

(4)

for any
u\in\{0,1\}
,

(2.7)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}_{s}(\iota_{\varepsilon,u})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

(5)

the reversed process
\{\mathcal{Y}_{T-t},0\leq t\leq T\}
satisfies condition (3) with

replaced by
-B
.

The following result shows that Definitions
2.3
and
2.4
coincide, whose proof is presented in Section

.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\big\{\mathcal{Y}_{t},t\in[0,T]\big\}
be given as in Definition

. Then, it can be extended to
\mathcal{C}\big([0,T];\mathcal{S}_{\rm Dir}^{\prime}\big)

2.2.
Density fluctuation fields

We are interested in the limit of the density fluctuation field of the process, which acts on functions
H\in\mathcal{S}
as

{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{t}(H)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(t)H(x/n),

where
\bar{\eta}_{x}=\eta_{x}-1/2
. The following is the main result of this article.

Theorem 2.2
.

Fix
T>0
. Under Assumption
2.1
, the sequence of processes
\{{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}_{n\geq 1}
converges in distribution in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
towards

(1)

if
\theta<3/2
, the solution to the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with Dirichlet boundary conditions (
2.1
) with
A=2\sum_{y=1}^{\infty}y^{1-\alpha},D=\sum_{y=1}^{\infty}y^{1-\alpha}
;

(2)

if
\theta=3/2
, the solution to the stochastic Burgers equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions (
2.4
) with
A=2\sum_{y=1}^{\infty}y^{1-\alpha},B=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\infty}y^{-\gamma},D=\sum_{y=1}^{\infty}y^{1-\alpha}
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

3.1.
Associated martingales

For any
H\in\mathcal{S}
, by Dynkin’s martingale,

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t}(H)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{t}(H)-{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{0}(H)-\int_{0}^{t}L_{n}{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)ds

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t}(H)^{2}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\big\{L_{n}{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)^{2}-2{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)L_{n}{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)\big\}ds

are both martingales.

We first calculate the integral term in (
3.1
). Below, we write
\eta_{x}(s)
simply as
\eta_{x}
when there are no confusions. For the symmetric part,

\displaystyle n^{2}L_{s}{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}s_{\alpha}(x-y)\big[H(\tfrac{x}{n})-H(\tfrac{y}{n})\big][\eta_{y}-\eta_{x}]

\displaystyle+\frac{n^{2}}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\notin\Lambda_{n}}s_{\alpha}(x-y)H(\tfrac{x}{n})(1-2\eta_{x}).

Denote

(3.3)

r_{\alpha}(x):=\sum_{y\notin\Lambda_{n}}s_{\alpha}(x-y),\quad x\in\Lambda_{n}.

Note that
1-2\eta_{x}=-2\bar{\eta}_{x}
. Then,
n^{2}L_{s}{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)=A^{n,1}_{s}(H)+A^{n,2}_{s}(H)
, where

(3.4)

\displaystyle A^{n,1}_{s}(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}s_{\alpha}(x-y)\big[H(\tfrac{x}{n})-H(\tfrac{y}{n})\big][\bar{\eta}_{y}-\bar{\eta}_{x}],

(3.5)

\displaystyle A^{n,2}_{s}(H)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}r_{\alpha}(x)H(\tfrac{x}{n})\bar{\eta}_{x}.

For the asymmetric part,

\displaystyle n^{\theta}L_{a}{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{y})\big[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{n^{\theta}}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\leq 0}p_{\gamma}(x-y)(1-\eta_{x})H(\tfrac{x}{n})

(3.6)

\displaystyle-\frac{n^{\theta}}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\geq n}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\eta_{x}H(\tfrac{x}{n}).

Define
s_{\gamma}
and
a_{\gamma}
as the symmetric and anti-symmetric parts of
p_{\gamma}
respectively,

s_{\gamma}(x)=\frac{p_{\gamma}(x)+p_{\gamma}(-x)}{2},\quad a_{\gamma}(x)=\frac{p_{\gamma}(x)-p_{\gamma}(-x)}{2}.

Then, the first term on the righthand side of (
3.6
) equals

\displaystyle A^{n,3}_{s}(H)+\frac{n^{\theta}}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{\gamma}(y-x)[\eta_{x}-\eta_{y}]^{2}\big[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big],

where

A^{n,3}_{s}(H)=\frac{n^{\theta}}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}s_{\gamma}(y-x)[\bar{\eta}_{x}-\bar{\eta}_{y}]\big[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big].

Since

[\eta_{x}-\eta_{y}]^{2}=(\bar{\eta}_{x})^{2}+(\bar{\eta}_{y})^{2}-2\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{y}=\frac{1}{2}-2\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{y},

the first term on the righthand side of (
3.6
) equals

\displaystyle A^{n,3}_{s}(H)+A^{n,4}_{s}(H)+\frac{n^{\theta}}{4\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{\gamma}(y-x)\big[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]

(3.7)

\displaystyle=A^{n,3}_{s}(H)+A^{n,4}_{s}(H)+\frac{n^{\theta}}{4\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}[a_{\gamma}(x-y)-a_{\gamma}(y-x)]H(\tfrac{x}{n}),

where

A^{n,4}_{s}(H)=-\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{\gamma}({y-x})\big[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{y}.

The sum of the second and the third terms on the righthand side of (
3.6
) equals

(3.8)

A^{n,5}_{s}(H)+\frac{n^{\theta}}{4\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\leq 0}p_{\gamma}(x-y)H(\tfrac{x}{n})-\frac{n^{\theta}}{4\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\geq n}p_{\gamma}(y-x)H(\tfrac{x}{n}),

where

A^{n,5}_{s}(H)=-\frac{n^{\theta}}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}r_{\gamma}(x)H(\tfrac{x}{n})\bar{\eta}_{x},\quad r_{\gamma}(x)=\sum_{y\leq 0}p_{\gamma}(x-y)+\sum_{y\geq n}p_{\gamma}(y-x).

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{n}}[a_{\gamma}(x-y)-a_{\gamma}(y-x)]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{y\notin\Lambda_{n}}[a_{\gamma}(x-y)-a_{\gamma}(y-x)]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{y\notin\Lambda_{n}}[p_{\gamma}(x-y)-p_{\gamma}(y-x)]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{y\leq 0}p_{\gamma}(x-y)+\sum_{y\geq n}p_{\gamma}(y-x),

the constant terms in (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) cancel out each other. Thus,

(3.9)

L_{n}{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)=\sum_{j=1}^{5}A^{n,j}_{s}(H).

For the integral term in (

), one can easily show that

\displaystyle L_{n}{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)^{2}-2{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)L_{n}{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{s}(H)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}[ns_{\alpha}(x-y)+n^{\theta-1}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{y})](\eta_{x}-\eta_{y})^{2}\big[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\Big[\frac{n}{2}r_{\alpha}(x)+\frac{n^{\theta-1}}{2}\sum_{y\leq 0}p_{\gamma}(x-y)(1-\eta_{x})+\frac{n^{\theta-1}}{2}\sum_{y\geq n}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\eta_{x}\Big]H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}.

Next, we deal with the above terms respectively.

The following result shows that we can replace the term
A^{n,1}_{s}
by a constant multiple of
\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{s}(\Delta H)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
H\in\mathcal{S}
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\big\{A^{n,1}_{s}(H)-C_{\alpha}\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{s}(\Delta H)\big\}ds\Big)^{2}\Big]=0,

where
C_{\alpha}:=2\sum_{y=1}^{\infty}y^{1-\alpha}
.

Proof.

Recall (
3.4
), since
s_{\alpha}
is symmetric,

A^{n,1}_{s}(H)=\frac{2n^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}s_{\alpha}(x-y)\big[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n}))\big]\bar{\eta}_{x}.

For any
H\in\mathcal{S}
, let
\tilde{H}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be the extension of
H
from
[0,1]
to
\mathbb{R}
such that
\tilde{H}(u)=0
for
u\notin[0,1]
. Then,

\displaystyle A^{n,1}_{s}(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}\cdot n^{2}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}|x-y|^{-1-\alpha}\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}\cdot n^{2}\sum\limits_{y\notin\Lambda_{n}}|x-y|^{-1-\alpha}\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big].

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the lemma follows directly from the following estimates:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}\cdot n^{2}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}|y-x|^{-1-\alpha}[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})]-C_{\alpha}\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{s}(\Delta H)\Big)^{2}\Big]=0,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}\cdot n^{2}\sum\limits_{y\notin\Lambda_{n}}|x-y|^{-1-\alpha}\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

We first prove (
3.10
). We first write

n^{2}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}|y-x|^{-1-\alpha}\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]\\
=n^{2}\sum\limits_{|y-x|\leq n}|y-x|^{-1-\alpha}\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]+n^{2}\sum\limits_{|y-x|>n}|y-x|^{-1-\alpha}\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big].

The second term on the right hand side has order
n^{2-\alpha}
, which converges to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
since
\alpha>2
. For the first term on the right hand side, by Taylor’s expansion, it equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{|y|\leq n}|y|^{1-\alpha}\cdot\tilde{H}^{(2)}(\tfrac{x}{n})+\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\alpha},

where

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\alpha}|\leq C(H)n^{-2}\sum_{|y|\leq n}|y|^{3-\alpha}.

Note that the error term above has order
O(n^{-2})
if
\alpha>4
,
O(n^{2-\alpha}\log n)
if
\alpha=4
,
O(n^{2-\alpha})
if
\alpha<4
, which converges to 0 as
n\to\infty
. Thus,

(3.12)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\Big|n^{2}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}|y-x|^{-1-\alpha}\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]-\frac{C_{\alpha}}{2}\tilde{H}^{(2)}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big|=0.

This proves (
3.10
) by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

It remains to prove (
3.11
). Since
\tilde{H}(y)=0
if
y\notin[0,1]
, the term in the square can be rewritten as

(3.13)

\frac{2n^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\sum\limits_{y\notin\Lambda_{n}}|x-y|^{-1-\alpha}.

Since

\sum\limits_{y\notin\Lambda_{n}}|x-y|^{-1-\alpha}\leq C(\alpha)(x^{-\alpha}+(n-x)^{-\alpha}),

we have

\displaystyle E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\frac{2n^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\sum\limits_{y\notin\Lambda_{n}}|x-y|^{-1-\alpha}\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\alpha)n^{3}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}(x^{-2\alpha}+(n-x)^{-2\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\alpha)n^{4-2\alpha}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{1-\frac{1}{n}}\tilde{H}(u)^{2}(u^{-2\alpha}+(1-u)^{-2\alpha})\,du.

Since
H\in\mathcal{S}
,
\tilde{H}(u)^{2}\leq C(H)[u^{2\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{\{u\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]\}}+(1-u)^{2\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{\{u\in(\frac{1}{2},1]\}}]
. In particular, the last integral is finite. This concludes the proof since
\alpha>2
.
∎

The next result shows that
A^{n,i}_{s}
vanishes as

for

Lemma 3.2
.

For
i=2,3,5
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}A^{n,i}_{s}(H)ds\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

Proof.

Observe that the term
A^{n,2}_{s}
is the same as (
3.13
). The term
A^{n,3}_{s}
can be dealt with similarly to the term
A^{n,1}_{s}
. Indeed, we first write

\displaystyle A^{n,3}(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}}s_{\gamma}(y-x)\bar{\eta}_{x}\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]

\displaystyle-\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big(\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\notin\Lambda_{n}}+\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{n},x\notin\Lambda_{n}}\Big)s_{\gamma}(y-x)\bar{\eta}_{x}\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big].

For
0<\gamma<2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(n^{\gamma}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}s_{\gamma}(y-x)\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}du\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\tilde{H}(u)-\tilde{H}(v)}{|u-v|^{1+\gamma}}dv\Big)^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}du\int_{\mathbb{R}}dv\frac{[\tilde{H}(u)-\tilde{H}(v)]^{2}}{|u-v|^{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{|u-v|>1}\frac{2[\tilde{H}(u)^{2}+\tilde{H}(v)^{2}]}{|u-v|^{1+\gamma}}dudv+C\iint_{|u-v|\leq 1}\|\tilde{H}^{\prime}\|_{1,\infty}(v)^{2}|u-v|^{1-\gamma}dudv

\displaystyle\leq C(H),

where
\|H\|_{1,\infty}(v)=\sup_{|u-v|\leq 1}|H(u)|
. For
\gamma\geq 2
, similar to the proof of (

), we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(n^{2}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}s_{\gamma}(y-x)\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]\Big)^{2}\leq\begin{cases}C(H)\quad&\text{if}\quad\gamma>2,\\
C(H)(\log n)^{2}\quad&\text{if}\quad\gamma=2.\end{cases}

Thus, we bound

E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}}s_{\gamma}(y-x)\bar{\eta}_{x}\big[\tilde{H}(\tfrac{y}{n})-\tilde{H}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq C(H)\begin{cases}n^{2\theta-2\gamma}\quad&\text{if}\quad 0<\gamma<2,\\
n^{2\theta-4}(\log n)^{2}\quad&\text{if}\quad\gamma=2,\\
n^{2\theta-4}\quad&\text{if}\quad\gamma>2,\\
\end{cases}

which converges to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
by Assumption
2.1
.
For the sum
\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\notin\Lambda_{n}}
, we first rewrite it as

\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}H(\tfrac{x}{n})\sum_{y\notin\Lambda_{n}}s_{\gamma}(y-x),

and then bound

\displaystyle E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}H(\tfrac{x}{n})\sum_{y\notin\Lambda_{n}}s_{\gamma}(y-x)\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma)n^{2\theta-1}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}\big(x^{-2\gamma}+(n-x)^{-2\gamma}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma)n^{2\theta-2\gamma}\int_{\tfrac{1}{n}}^{1-\tfrac{1}{n}}H(u)^{2}\big(u^{-2\gamma}+(1-u)^{-2\gamma}\big)du

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma,H)n^{2\theta-2\gamma}.

The sum

can the treated in the same way. This concludes the estimates for the term
A_{s}^{n,3}
. Similarly, for

\displaystyle T^{2}E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(A^{n,5}(H)\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{2\theta-1}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}r_{\gamma}(x)^{2}H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{2\theta-2\gamma}\int_{\tfrac{1}{n}}^{1-\tfrac{1}{n}}H(u)^{2}[u^{-2\gamma}+(1-u)^{-2\gamma}]du

\displaystyle\leq C(H)n^{2\theta-2\gamma},

thus concluding the proof.
∎

3.2.1.
The case

In the last subsection, we have shown that, for any

\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)=\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{t}(H)-\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{0}(H)-C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{s}(\Delta H)ds-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=2}^{5}A^{n,i}_{s}(H)ds-\mathcal{E}_{t}^{n,1}(H),

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=2}^{5}A^{n,i}_{s}(H)ds+\mathcal{E}_{t}^{n,1}(H)\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

Letting

, the limiting point satisfies that

\mathcal{M}_{t}(H)=\mathcal{Y}_{t}(H)-\mathcal{Y}_{0}(H)-C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}_{s}(\Delta H)ds

3.4

\mathcal{M}_{t}(H)^{2}-\frac{tC_{\alpha}}{2}\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2}

is also a martingale. This verifies condition
(2)
in Definition
2.2
. The first condition in Definition
2.2
follows directly from the fact that the process
\eta(t)
is stationary. At last, for condition
(3)
, it suffices to prove the following result.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
u\in\{0,1\}
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{s}(\iota_{\varepsilon,u})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

Proof.

We only consider the case
u=0
, and the other case can be proved in the same way. Note that

\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{s}(\iota_{\varepsilon,0})=\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x=1}^{\varepsilon n}\bar{\eta}_{x}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it suffices to show that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x=1}^{\varepsilon n}(\bar{\eta}_{x}-\bar{\eta}_{1})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]=0,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{n}\bar{\eta}_{1}ds\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

We first prove (
3.14
). By Kipnis-Varadhan inequality
[
16
, Proposition 6.1, Appendix 1]
, the expectation in (
3.14
) is bounded by

20T\sup_{f}\Big\{\int\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x=1}^{\varepsilon n}(\bar{\eta}_{x}-\bar{\eta}_{1})f(\eta)d\nu_{1/2}-D_{n}(f)\Big\},

where the supremum is taken over all densities with respect to the measure
\nu_{1/2}
, and
D_{n}(f)
is the Dirichlet form of
f
for the generator
L_{n}
under
\nu_{1/2}
,

\displaystyle D_{n}(f):=\langle f,-L_{n}f\rangle_{\nu_{1/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}s_{\alpha}(x-y)E_{\nu_{1/2}}[(f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta))^{2}]

\displaystyle+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\notin\Lambda_{n}}s_{\alpha}(x-y)E_{\nu_{1/2}}[(f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta))^{2}]

\displaystyle+\frac{n^{\theta}}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}p_{\gamma}(y-x)E_{\nu_{1/2}}[\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{y})(f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta))^{2}]

\displaystyle+\frac{n^{\theta}}{4}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\leq 0}p_{\gamma}(x-y)E_{\nu_{1/2}}[(1-\eta_{x})(f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta))^{2}]

\displaystyle+\frac{n^{\theta}}{4}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n},y\geq n}p_{\gamma}(y-x)E_{\nu_{1/2}}[\eta_{x}(f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta))^{2}].

We rewrite

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x=1}^{\varepsilon n}(\bar{\eta}_{x}-\bar{\eta}_{1})f(\eta)d\nu_{1/2}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x=1}^{\varepsilon n}\sum_{y=1}^{x-1}(\bar{\eta}_{y+1}-\bar{\eta}_{y})f(\eta)d\nu_{1/2}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{2\varepsilon\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x=1}^{\varepsilon n}\sum_{y=1}^{x-1}(\bar{\eta}_{y+1}-\bar{\eta}_{y})\big(f(\eta)-f(\eta^{y,y+1})\big)d\nu_{1/2},

where we made the transformation
\eta\mapsto\eta^{y,y+1}
in the last identity. By Young’s inequality, the last expression is bounded by

\displaystyle\int\frac{n}{2\varepsilon}\sum_{x=1}^{\varepsilon n}\sum_{y=1}^{x-1}\big(f(\eta)-f(\eta^{y,y+1})\big)^{2}d\nu_{1/2}+\int\frac{1}{8\varepsilon n^{2}}\sum_{x=1}^{\varepsilon n}\sum_{y=1}^{x-1}(\bar{\eta}_{y+1}-\bar{\eta}_{y})^{2}d\nu_{1/2}.

Since the first term in the last expression is bounded by
D_{n}(f)
, and the second one is bounded by
C\varepsilon
, the proof of (

For (

20T\sup_{f}\Big\{\int\sqrt{n}\bar{\eta}_{1}f(\eta)d\nu_{1/2}-D_{n}(f)\Big\}.

Making the change of variable

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}\int\bar{\eta}_{1}\big(f(\eta)-f(\eta^{1})\big)d\nu_{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4}\int\big(f(\eta)-f(\eta^{1})\big)^{2}d\nu_{1/2}+\frac{1}{4n}\int(\bar{\eta}_{1})^{2}d\nu_{1/2}

thus concluding the proof.
∎

4.1.

By first using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and then developing the square, we bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}A^{n,4}_{s}(H)ds\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq T^{2}E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{\gamma}(x-y)\big[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{y}\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T^{2}n^{2\theta}}{n}\sum_{x_{i},y_{i}\in\Lambda_{n},\atop i=1,2}a_{\gamma}(x_{1}-y_{1})a_{\gamma}(x_{2}-y_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\times[H(\tfrac{y_{1}}{n})-H(\tfrac{x_{1}}{n})][H(\tfrac{y_{2}}{n})-H(\tfrac{x_{2}}{n})]E_{\nu_{1/2}}[\bar{\eta}_{x_{1}}\bar{\eta}_{x_{2}}\bar{\eta}_{y_{1}}\bar{\eta}_{y_{2}}].

Notice that the above expectation is zero unless
x_{1}=x_{2},y_{1}=y_{2}
or
x_{1}=y_{2},x_{2}=y_{1}
. Thus, the last line is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{CT^{2}n^{2\theta}}{n}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{\gamma}(x-y)^{2}[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{2}n^{2\theta-2\gamma-1}\iint_{|u-v|>n^{-1},|u|\leq 1,|v|\leq 1}|u-v|^{-2\gamma-2}[H(u)-H(v)]^{2}du\,dv.

Since
[H(u)-H(v)]^{2}\leq C(H)|u-v|^{2}
, the last line is bounded by

C(H)T^{2}\times\begin{cases}n^{2\theta-2\gamma-1}\quad&\text{if }\;\gamma<1/2,\\
n^{2\theta-2}\log n\quad&\text{if }\;\gamma=1/2,\\
n^{2\theta-2}\quad&\text{if }\;\gamma>1/2.\end{cases}

Since
\theta<\gamma\wedge 1
, the proof is concluded.

4.2.
The case
1\leq\theta<3/2

We shall use the multi-scale analysis introduced in
[
11
]
, where the authors studied the density fluctuations for the exclusion process on
\mathbb{Z}
with long jumps. Here, we need to deal with the boundary terms carefully since the model we considered is defined on the line segment. Below, let
K=K(n)
be determined later. The following lemma allows us to restrict the sum
\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}
in
A_{s}^{n,4}(H)
to
\sum_{|x-y|\leq K}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under assumption
2.1
, if
K\gg n^{\frac{2\theta-2}{2\gamma-1}}
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x-y|>K\\
x,y\in\Lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}a_{\gamma}(x-y)[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})]\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{y}ds\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the case
\theta<1
. Thus, we only sketch it. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and developing the square, the expectation in the lemma is bounded by

\displaystyle CT^{2}n^{2\theta-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x-y|>K\\
x,y\in\Lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}a_{\gamma}(y-x)^{2}[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT^{2}n^{2\theta-2\gamma-1}\iint_{|u-v|\geq\tfrac{K}{n},|u|\leq 1,|v|\leq 1}|u-v|^{-2\gamma-2}[H(u)-H(v)]^{2}dudv.

Under assumption
2.1
,
\gamma>\theta\geq 1
. Since
(H(u)-H(v))^{2}\leq C(H)(u-v)^{2}
, the last line is bounded by

C(H)T^{2}\frac{n^{2\theta-2}}{K^{2\gamma-1}},

thus concluding the proof.
∎

It remains to deal with the term

-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq|x-y|<K\\
x,y\in\Lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}a_{\gamma}(y-x)[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})]\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{y}ds.

By Taylor’s expansion,

H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})=H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})\frac{y-x}{n}+\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2n^{2}}H^{\prime\prime}(u^{n}_{x,y})

for some
u^{n}_{x,y}
between
x/n
and
y/n
. The following lemma shows that the second term on the right-hand side of the last identity is negligible.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under assumption
2.1
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{n^{\theta}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq|x-y|\leq K\\
x,y\in\Lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}a_{\gamma}(x-y)\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{y}\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{n^{2}}H^{\prime\prime}(u^{n}_{x,y})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and developing the square, the expectation in the lemma is bounded by

\displaystyle CT^{2}n^{2\theta-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq|x-y|\leq K\\
x,y\in\Lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}a_{\gamma}(y-x)^{2}\frac{(y-x)^{4}}{n^{4}}H^{\prime\prime}(u^{n}_{x,y})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C(H)T^{2}n^{2\theta-2\gamma-1}\iint_{\tfrac{1}{n}\leq|u-v|\leq\frac{K}{n},|u|\leq 1,|v|\leq 1}|u-v|^{2-2\gamma}dudv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C(H)T^{2}\times\begin{cases}n^{2\theta-2\gamma-1}\quad&\text{if }\;\gamma\in(1,\frac{3}{2}),\\
n^{2\theta-4}\log n\quad&\text{if }\;\gamma=\frac{3}{2},\\
n^{2\theta-4}\quad&\text{if }\;\gamma\in(\frac{3}{2},\infty).\end{cases}

We conclude the proof by noting that
\theta<2\wedge\gamma
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Lemma 4.7
.

(4.8)

\displaystyle\quad+2\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}n^{\theta-\frac{3}{2}}\sum\limits_{x=1}^{n-K-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\sum\limits_{y=K_{i-1}+1}^{K_{i}}ya_{\gamma}(y)\{\psi_{x}^{K_{i}}(\eta(s))-\psi_{x}^{K}(\eta(s))\}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big].

(4.9)

where
K_{x}=n-1-x

Lemma 4.9
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, there exists some constant
C=C(\lambda,H)
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}n^{\theta-\frac{3}{2}}\sum\limits_{x=n-K}^{n-2}\sum\limits_{y=1}^{n-1-x}ya_{\gamma}(y)\{\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+y}-\psi_{x}^{K_{x}}(\eta(s))\}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq CT\frac{K^{\lambda+1}}{n^{2-2\theta+\lambda}}.

Proof.

Define

F_{x}(\eta)=\sum\limits_{y=1}^{n-1-x}ya_{\gamma}(y)\{\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+y}-\psi_{x}^{K_{x}}(\eta)\}.

Then, the expectation can be bounded as

Kn^{2\theta-3}\sum\limits_{x=n-K}^{n-2}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}F_{x}(\eta(s))ds\Big)^{2}\Big].

Using Proposition
4.5
to
f=F_{x}
, the last line is bounded by

\displaystyle CTKn^{2\theta-3}\sum\limits_{x=n-K}^{n-2}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}\frac{K_{x}^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum\limits_{y=1}^{n-1-x}y^{2}a_{\gamma}(y)^{2}.

Since
\sum_{y=1}^{n-1-x}y^{2}a_{\gamma}(y)^{2}
is uniformly bounded for
x=n-K,...,n-2
, the last line is bounded by

CTKn^{2\theta-2}\int_{1-\frac{K}{n}}^{1-\frac{2}{n}}H^{\prime}(u)^{2}(1-u)^{2}du.

Since
H\in\mathcal{S}
, for any
\frac{\lambda-3}{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
H^{\prime}(u)\leq C(\lambda,H)(1-u)^{\frac{\lambda-3}{2}}
. So the last line can further be bounded by

C(\lambda,H)TKn^{2\theta-2}\int_{1-\frac{K}{n}}^{1-\frac{2}{n}}(1-u)^{\lambda-1}du\leq C(\lambda,H)T\frac{K^{\lambda+1}}{n^{2-2\theta+\lambda}},

thus concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, there exists some constant
C=C(\lambda,H)
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}n^{\theta-\frac{3}{2}}\sum\limits_{x=n-K}^{n-2}\sum\limits_{y=1}^{n-1-x}ya_{\gamma}(y)\psi_{x}^{K_{x}}(\eta(s))H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq CT^{2}\frac{K^{\lambda+1}}{n^{4-2\theta+\lambda}}.

Proof.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
4.3
), the expectation can be bounded by

\displaystyle T^{2}n^{2\theta-3}E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\sum\limits_{x=n-K}^{n-2}\sum\limits_{y=1}^{n-1-x}ya_{\gamma}(y)\psi_{x}^{K_{x}}(\eta)H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT^{2}Kn^{2\theta-3}\sum\limits_{x=n-K}^{n-2}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\psi_{x}^{K_{x}}(\eta)\Big)^{2}\Big]\Big(\sum\limits_{y=1}^{n-1-x}ya_{\gamma}(y)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT^{2}Kn^{2\theta-3}\sum\limits_{x=n-K}^{n-2}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}\frac{1}{K_{x}^{2}}.

Using the similar argument in the proof of last lemma, the last line is bounded by

C(\lambda,H)T^{2}Kn^{2\theta-4}\int_{1-\frac{K}{n}}^{1-\frac{2}{n}}(1-u)^{\lambda-1}du\leq CT^{2}\frac{K^{\lambda+1}}{n^{4-2\theta+\lambda}},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Adding up the above estimates, we have shown that, if
K\gg n^{\tfrac{2\theta-2}{2\gamma-1}}
, then the expectation in Proposition
3.3
is bounded by, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,

(4.14)

C\Big\{T^{2}\Big(\frac{n^{2\theta-2}}{K}+\frac{K^{\lambda+1}}{n^{4-2\theta+\lambda}}\Big)+T\Big(\frac{K^{4-2\gamma}}{n^{4-2\theta}}+\frac{K}{n^{4-2\theta}}+\frac{K^{\lambda+1}}{n^{2-2\theta+\lambda}}\Big)\Big\}+o_{n}(1).

Since
\theta<3/2
and
\gamma>\theta\geq 1
,

\frac{2\theta-2}{2\gamma-1}\leq 2\theta-2<1.

Take
K=n^{1-\delta}
with
0<\delta<3-2\theta
, and take
\lambda>(2-\delta-1)/\delta
. Then, one can check that

2\theta-2<1-\delta,\quad(1-\delta)(\lambda+1)<2-2\theta+\lambda.

Thus, all the terms in (
4.14
) have order
o_{n}(1)
, which concludes the proof in the case
\theta<3/2
.

4.3.
The case
\theta=\frac{3}{2}

In the last section, if we take
\delta
and
\lambda
satisfying

1>1-\delta>\frac{1}{2\gamma-1},\quad(1-\delta)(\lambda+1)<2-2\theta+\lambda,

then we have shown that, for
K=n^{1-\delta}
,

(4.15)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\big\{A_{s}^{n,4}(H)-2\sum_{x=1}^{n-K-1}\sum\limits_{y=1}^{K}ya_{\gamma}(y)\psi_{x}^{K}(\eta(s))H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big\}ds\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

The factor
2
comes from the symmetry between the two regions
\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{II}
. Note that

2\sum_{y=1}^{\infty}ya_{\gamma}(y)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}yp_{\gamma}(y)=m.

Thus,

(4.16)

\displaystyle m\sum_{x=1}^{n-1}\psi_{x}^{\varepsilon n}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})-2\sum_{x=1}^{n-K-1}\sum\limits_{y=1}^{K}ya_{\gamma}(y)\psi_{x}^{K}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})

\displaystyle=m\sum_{x=1}^{n-\varepsilon n}(\psi_{x}^{\varepsilon n}-\psi_{x}^{K})H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})+m\sum_{x=n-\varepsilon n+1}^{n-1}\psi_{x}^{\varepsilon n}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})-m\sum_{x=n-\varepsilon n+1}^{n-K-1}\psi_{x}^{K}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})

\displaystyle+2\sum\limits_{y=K}^{\infty}ya_{\gamma}(y)\sum_{x=1}^{n-K-1}\psi_{x}^{K}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})

Note that for
x=n-\varepsilon n,\ldots,n-1
,

\psi_{x}^{\varepsilon n}=E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x-1}\Big|\sum^{y=x}_{x-\varepsilon n+1}\eta_{y}\Big]=:\psi_{x,L}^{\varepsilon n}.

Similarly, we denote the average over the right box by
\psi_{x,R}^{\varepsilon n}
. Then, the second and the third terms on (
4.16
) equals

(4.17)

\displaystyle m\sum_{x=n-\varepsilon n+1}^{n-1}\psi_{x,L}^{\varepsilon n}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})-m\sum_{x=n-\varepsilon n+1}^{n-K-1}\psi_{x,R}^{K}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})=m\sum_{x=n-\varepsilon n+1}^{n-K-1}\big(\psi_{x,L}^{\varepsilon n}-\psi_{x,L}^{K}\big)H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})

\displaystyle+m\sum_{x=n-K}^{n-1}\psi_{x,L}^{\varepsilon n}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})-m\sum_{x=n-\varepsilon n+1}^{n-K-1}\big(\psi_{x,R}^{K}-\psi_{x,L}^{K}\big)H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})

The first term on the right-hand side of (
4.17
) can be dealt with in the same way as in Lemma
4.11
below. For the second term,

\displaystyle E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\sum\limits_{x=n-K}^{n-1}\psi_{x,L}^{\varepsilon n}(\eta)H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq K\sum\limits_{x=n-K}^{n-1}E_{\nu_{1/2}}[\psi_{x,L}^{\varepsilon n}(\eta)^{2}]H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CK\sum\limits_{x=n-K}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(\varepsilon n)^{2}}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}\leq\frac{C(H)}{\varepsilon^{2}n^{\delta}}.

For the last term on the right-hand side of (
4.17
), by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Proposition
4.5
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x=n-\varepsilon n+1}^{n-K-1}\big(\psi_{x,R}^{K}-\psi_{x,L}^{K}\big)H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq CT\varepsilon n\sum_{x=n-\varepsilon n+1}^{n-K-1}\frac{K^{2}}{n^{2}}E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\psi_{x,R}^{K}-\psi_{x,L}^{K}\Big)^{2}\Big]H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(H)T\varepsilon^{2}.

For the last term on the right-hand side of (
4.16
), by Lemma
4.6
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{y=K}^{\infty}ya_{\gamma}(y)\sum_{x=1}^{n-K-1}\psi_{x}^{K}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq\frac{CT^{2}n}{K^{2\gamma-1}},

which converges to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
since
(1-\delta)(2\gamma-1)>1
.

It remains to bound the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.16
).

Lemma 4.11
.

There exists some constant
C(H)=C\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2}
for some
C>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{x=1}^{n-\varepsilon n}(\psi_{x}^{K}(\eta(s))-\psi_{x}^{\varepsilon n}(\eta(s)))H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq C(H)T\varepsilon.

Proof.

Using Proposition
4.5
and (
4.3
), for
M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{x=1}^{n-\varepsilon n}(\psi_{x}^{M}(\eta(s))-\psi_{x}^{2M}(\eta(s)))H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2M}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{H}_{j}}(\psi_{x}^{M}(\eta(s))-\psi_{x}^{2M}(\eta(s)))H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CTM\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2M}\frac{M^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{H}_{j}}E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big((\psi_{x}^{M}(\eta(s))-\psi_{x}^{2M}(\eta(s)))\Big)^{2}\Big]H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT\frac{M^{3}}{n^{2}}\sum\limits_{x=1}^{n-\varepsilon n}\frac{1}{M^{2}}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}\leq C(H)T\frac{M}{n},

where
\mathcal{H}_{j}
is the same with (
4.4
) except that
K
is replaced with
2M
.

Without loss of generality, let us assume
\varepsilon n=2^{l}K
for some integer
l
. Set
K_{0}=K
and
K_{i}=2^{i}K
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, using Minkowski’s inequality and the estimate above, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{x=1}^{n-\varepsilon n}(\psi_{x}^{K}(\eta(s))-\psi_{x}^{\varepsilon n}(\eta(s)))H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{x=1}^{n-\varepsilon n}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{l-1}(\psi_{x}^{K_{i}}(\eta(s))-\psi_{x}^{K_{i+1}}(\eta(s)))H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{l-1}\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{x=1}^{n-\varepsilon n}(\psi_{x}^{K_{i}}(\eta(s))-\psi_{x}^{K_{i+1}}(\eta(s)))H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big)^{2}\Big]\Big)^{1/2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{l-1}\sqrt{C(H)T\frac{K_{i}}{n}}\bigg)^{2}\leq C(H)T\varepsilon.

This ends the proof.
∎

5.
Tightness

In this section, we first prove that the sequence
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{n},t\in[0,T]\}_{n\geq 1}
is tight in the space
\mathcal{D}([0,T];\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
equipped with the
J_{1}
-Skorokhod topology, then we prove that any limiting point concentrates on the continuous trajectory.

We first recall Mitoma’s criterion
[
18
]
.

Proposition 5.1

(Mitoma’s criterion)
.

A sequence
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{n},t\in[0,T]\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of stochastic processes in
\mathcal{D}([0,T];\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
is tight with respect to the
J_{1}
-Skorokhod topology if and only if the sequence of real-valued processes
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{n}(H),t\in[0,T]\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight with respect to the
J_{1}
-Skorokhod topology of
\mathcal{D}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
for any
H\in\mathcal{S}
.

By (
3.1
), (
3.9
) and Proposition
5.1
, we only need to prove the tightness of the following processes

\left\{\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{n}(H)\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\quad\left\{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{n}(H),t\in[0,T]\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\quad\left\{\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}^{n,j}(H)ds,t\in[0,T]\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\;j=1,...,5

for any
H\in\mathcal{S}
, respectively.

5.1.
Convergence of the initial fluctuation field

In this subsection, we show that the sequence
\{\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{n}(H)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in distribution to a Gaussian random variable
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(H)
of mean zero and variance
\frac{1}{4}\|H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2}
. In particular,
\{\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{n}(H)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight.

Indeed, by Taylor’s expansion, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\log\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\{i\lambda\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{n}(H)\}\Big]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\log\int\exp\left\{\frac{i\lambda}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\left(\eta_{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)H(\tfrac{x}{n})\right\}d\nu_{1/2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\log\int\exp\left\{\frac{i\lambda}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\eta_{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)H(\tfrac{x}{n})\right\}d\nu_{1/2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\log\Big(1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8n}H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}+\mathcal{O}_{H}(n^{-3/2})\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8}\|H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2}.

This ends the proof.

5.2.
Convergence of the martingale

In this section, we prove Proposition
3.4
, from which the tightness of the martingale follows directly. By
[
22
]
, we only need to check the following conditions:

(i)

for any
n>1
, the quadratic variation process
\langle\mathcal{M}^{n}(H)\rangle_{t}
has continous trajectories, a.s.;

(ii)

the following limit holds

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{0\leq s\leq T}|\mathcal{M}_{s}^{n}(H)-\mathcal{M}_{s-}^{n}(H)|\Big]=0;

(iii)

for any
t\in[0,T]
, the sequence
\{\langle\mathcal{M}^{n}(H)\rangle_{t}\}_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\frac{tC_{\alpha}}{2}\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}{[0,1]}}^{2}
in probability.

By the calculations in Subsection
3.1
, the quadratic variation of
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{n}(H)
is given by

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{M}^{n}(H)\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}[ns_{\alpha}(x-y)+n^{\theta-1}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{y})](\eta_{x}-\eta_{y})^{2}\big[H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big]^{2}ds

(5.1)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\Big[\frac{n}{2}r_{\alpha}(x)+\frac{n^{\theta-1}}{2}\sum_{y\leq 0}p_{\gamma}(x-y)(1-\eta_{x})+\frac{n^{\theta-1}}{2}\sum_{y\geq n}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\eta_{x}\Big]H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}ds.

Then condition (i) immediately holds.

By (
3.1
),

\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{n}(H)-\mathcal{M}_{t-}^{n}(H)|=\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{n}(H)-\mathcal{Y}_{t-}^{n}(H)|\leq\frac{C(H)}{\sqrt{n}}.

So condition (ii) holds.

It remains to check condition (iii). We first prove the convergence of the expectation for
\langle\mathcal{M}^{n}(H)\rangle_{t}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
H\in\mathcal{S}
,

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\langle\mathcal{M}^{n}(H)\rangle_{t}]=t\frac{C_{\alpha}}{2}\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2}

with
C_{\alpha}=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}|y|^{1-\alpha}
.

Proof.

By Fubini’s theorem, we can pass the expectation inside the integral in (
5.1
). Consider the first integral in the right-hand side in (
5.1
). We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}ns_{\alpha}(y-x)(\eta_{x}-\eta_{y})^{2}\big(H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big)^{2}\Big]ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{tn}{2}\sum\limits_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}s_{\alpha}(y-x)\big(H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big)^{2}.

By Taylor’s expansion, the last line can be written as

\frac{t}{2n}\sum\limits_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n},\atop y\neq x}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{\alpha-1}}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}

plus an error term which is bounded by

C(H)\frac{t}{n^{3}}\sum\limits_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n},\atop y\neq x}|y-x|^{3-\alpha}.

The latter term vanishes as
n\to\infty
since
\alpha>2
, and the former term converges to
\frac{C_{\alpha}t}{2}\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2}
.
Furthermore, we can bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum\limits_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}n^{\theta-1}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{y})\big(H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big)^{2}\Big]ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{tn^{\theta-1}}{4}\sum\limits_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}s_{\gamma}(y-x)\big(H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ctn^{\theta-\gamma}\int\int_{0\leq u,v\leq 1,|u-v|\geq\frac{1}{n}}\frac{(H(u)-H(v))^{2}}{|u-v|^{1+\gamma}}dudv.

The last line has order
n^{\theta-\gamma}
if
\gamma<2
,
n^{\theta-2}\log n
if
\gamma=2
,
n^{\theta-2}
if
\gamma>2
, which vanishes in the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
by Assumption
2.1
.

Then we consider the second integral in the right-hand side of (
5.1
). By (
3.3
), the first part can be bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{nt}{2}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}\Big(\sum\limits_{y\leq 0}s_{\alpha}(x-y)+\sum\limits_{y\geq n}s_{\alpha}(x-y)\Big)\leq Cnt\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}[x^{-\alpha}+(n-x)^{-\alpha}].

Since
H\in\mathcal{S}
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is some
C(k,H)>0
such that
|H(\frac{x}{n})|\leq C(k,H)|\frac{x}{n}|^{k}
for
x\in\Lambda_{n}
. Thus, choosing
k
large enough such that
2k-\alpha>0
, the first term in the last expression is bounded by

(5.2)

C(k,H)tn^{1-2k}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}x^{2k-\alpha}\leq C(k,H)tn^{2-\alpha},

which vanishes as
n\to\infty
since
\alpha>2
.
The same arguments hold for the other term. By using the same argument as above, the expectation of the remaining part for the second integral in the right-hand side of (
5.1
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\Big(\frac{n^{\theta-1}}{2}\sum_{y\leq 0}p_{\gamma}(x-y)(1-\eta_{x})+\frac{n^{\theta-1}}{2}\sum_{y\geq n}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\eta_{x}\Big)H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}ds\Big]

\displaystyle\leq Ctn^{\theta-1}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}[x^{-\gamma}+(n-x)^{-\gamma}]\leq Ctn^{\theta-\gamma},

which vanishes as
n\to\infty
since
\theta<\gamma
. This ends the proof.
∎

Finally, we can verify condition (iii) by proving the following result.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
H\in\mathcal{S}
,

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\langle\mathcal{M}^{n}(H)\rangle_{t}-\mathbb{E}[\langle\mathcal{M}^{n}(H)\rangle_{t}]\Big)^{2}\Big]=0,

where
\langle\mathcal{M}^{n}(H)\rangle_{t}
is defined in (
5.1
).

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the expectation in the lemma is bounded by some constant times the sum of the following three terms

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}ns_{\alpha}(x-y)M_{x,y}(\eta(s))\left(H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\right)^{2}ds\Big)^{2}\Big],

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}n^{\theta-1}p_{\gamma}(y-x)N_{x,y}(\eta(s))\left(H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\right)^{2}ds\Big)^{2}\Big],

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\Big\{\frac{n^{\theta-1}}{2}\sum\limits_{y\leq 0}p_{\gamma}(x-y)-\frac{n^{\theta-1}}{2}\sum\limits_{y\geq n}p_{\gamma}(y-x)\Big\}Z_{x}(\eta(s))H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}ds\Big)^{2}\Big],

where
M_{x,y}(\eta)=(\eta_{y}-\eta_{x})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}
,
N_{x,y}(\eta)=\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{y})-\frac{1}{4}
and
Z_{x}(\eta)=\frac{1}{2}-\eta_{x}
.

We first prove that (
5.3
) vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Notice that,
\nu_{1/2}
is a product measure on
\Lambda_{n}
and
E_{\nu_{1/2}}[M_{x,y}(\eta)]

=0,\,\forall x,y\in\Lambda_{n}
. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
5.3
) is bounded by

\displaystyle Ct^{2}n^{2}\sum\limits_{x\neq y\in\Lambda_{n}}\sum\limits_{z\in\Lambda_{n}\setminus\{x\}}s_{\alpha}(x-y)s_{\alpha}(x-z)\left(H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\right)^{2}\left(H(\tfrac{z}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\right)^{2}E_{\nu_{1/2}}[M_{x,y}(\eta)M_{x,z}(\eta)]

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{2}n^{2}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{y\in\Lambda_{n}\setminus\{x\}}\left(H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\right)^{2}s_{\alpha}(x-y)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{2}n^{3-2\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\int_{|v-u|\geq n^{-1},0\leq v\leq 1}\frac{(H(u)-H(v))^{2}}{|u-v|^{1+\alpha}}dv\Big)^{2}du.

Since
[H(u)-H(v)]^{2}\leq C(H)(u-v)^{2}
and
\alpha>2
, the last term has order
n^{-1}
and thus vanishes as
n\to\infty
.

Similarly, (
5.4
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle Ct^{2}n^{2\theta-2}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{y\in\Lambda_{n}\setminus\{x\}}\left(H(\tfrac{y}{n})-H(\tfrac{x}{n})\right)^{2}p_{\gamma}(x-y)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{2}n^{2\theta-1-2\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\int_{|v-u|\geq n^{-1},0\leq v\leq 1}\frac{(H(u)-H(v))^{2}}{|u-v|^{1+\gamma}}dv\Big)^{2}du.

Note that the last line has order
n^{2\theta-1-2\gamma}
if
\gamma<2
,
n^{2\theta-5}(\log n)^{2}
if
\gamma=2
,
n^{2\theta-5}
if
\gamma>2
, which converges to zero by Assumption
2.1
.

For (
5.5
), we bound it by

\displaystyle Ct^{2}n^{2\theta-2}\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}H(\tfrac{x}{n})^{4}[x^{-2\gamma}+(n-x)^{-2\gamma}]\leq Ct^{2}n^{2\theta-2\gamma-1},

which vanishes as
n\to\infty
. In the last inequality, we used the same argument as in (
5.2
). This concludes the proof.
∎

5.3.
Tightness of the integral terms

In this subsection, we prove the tightness of the sequences
\{\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}^{n,j}(H)ds,t\in[0,T]\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},

j=1,...,5
. For this purpose, we first recall Kolmogorov-Centsov’s tightness criterion, see
[
14
, page 64]
for example.

Proposition 5.4
.

A sequence of continuous processes
\{X_{t}^{n},t\in[0,T]\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight with respect to the uniform topology of
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, if there exist constants
K,a,b>0
such that

E\left[|X_{t}^{n}-X_{s}^{n}|^{a}\right]\leq K|t-s|^{1+b}

for any
s,t\in[0,T]
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

For
j=1
, following the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{s}^{t}A_{r}^{n,1}(H)dr\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq C(H)(t-s)^{2},

so this term is tight. For
j=2,3,5
, in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we actually showed that

(5.6)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{s}^{t}A_{r}^{n,j}(H)dr\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq o_{n,H}(1)(t-s)^{2},

where
o_{n,H}(1)
is some constant depending on
H
and vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
, which verifies the tightness of the integral terms for
j=2,3,5
.

Finally, we prove the tightness of

\Big\{\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}^{n,4}(H)ds,t\in[0,T]\Big\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.

5.3.1.
The case
\theta<1

For
\theta<1
, we actually proved in section 4.1 that (
5.6
) also holds for
j=4
, which proves the tightness.

5.3.2.
The case
1\leq\theta\leq\frac{3}{2}

By Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, we only need to show the tightness of the following process

(5.7)

\int_{0}^{t}n^{\theta-3/2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x-y|\leq K\\
x,y\in\Lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}(x-y)a_{\gamma}(x-y)H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{y}(s)ds,

where
K\gg n^{\frac{2\theta-2}{2\gamma-1}}
. On one hand, by Lemmas
4.6
,
4.7
and
4.8
, for any positive integer
\lambda
,

(5.8)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{s}^{t}n^{\theta-3/2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x-y|\leq K\\
x,y\in\Lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}(x-y)a_{\gamma}(x-y)H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})\bar{\eta}_{x}(r)\bar{\eta}_{y}(r)dr\Big)^{2}\Big]\\
\leq C(t-s)^{2}\Big(\frac{n^{2\theta-2}}{K}+\frac{K^{\lambda+1}}{n^{4-2\theta+\lambda}}\Big)+C(t-s)\left(\frac{K^{4-2\gamma}}{n^{4-2\theta}}+\frac{K}{n^{4-2\theta}}+\frac{K^{\lambda+1}}{n^{2-2\theta+\lambda}}\right).

Let
K=n^{1-\delta}
and let
\lambda
be large enough such that

(5.9)

2\theta-2<1-\delta,\quad(\lambda+1)(1-\delta)<2-2\theta+\lambda,

then the above bound is of order
o_{n,H}(1)(t-s)^{2}+C(t-s)/n^{a}
for some
a>0
. On the other hand, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{s}^{t}n^{\theta-3/2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x-y|\leq K\\
x,y\in\Lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}(x-y)a_{\gamma}(x-y)H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})\bar{\eta}_{x}(r)\bar{\eta}_{y}(r)dr\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(t-s)^{2}n^{2\theta-3}E_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x-y|\leq K\\
x,y\in\Lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}(x-y)a_{\gamma}(x-y)H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{y}\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t-s)^{2}n^{2\theta-3}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x-y|\leq K\\
x,y\in\Lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}(x-y)^{2}a_{\gamma}(x-y)^{2}H^{\prime}(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}\leq C(t-s)^{2}n^{2\theta-2}.

Then, the tightness of the sequence
\{\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}^{n,4}(H)ds,t\in[0,T]\}
follows from the above two estimates, the following lemma and the trivial fact that
(t-s)^{2}\leq C(T)(t-s)^{1+\frac{a}{a+b}}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
a,b>0
,

\min\Big\{\frac{t}{n^{a}},t^{2}n^{b}\Big\}\leq t^{1+\frac{a}{a+b}}.

Proof.

If
t\leq n^{-(a+b)}
, then

t^{2}n^{b}=t^{1+\frac{a}{a+b}}(tn^{a+b})^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\leq t^{1+\frac{a}{a+b}}.

If
t>n^{-(a+b)}
, then
n^{-a}<t^{\frac{a}{a+b}}
, and thus

tn^{-a}\leq t^{1+\frac{a}{a+b}},

concluding the proof.
∎

In this case, we take

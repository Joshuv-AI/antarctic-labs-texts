A Generalization of Diophantine Tuples
Zijie Gu

Abstract
This paper investigates a generalized version of Diophantine tuples in finite fields. Applying Shparlinski’s method, we obtain power-saving results on the number of such tuples.

Introduction

Classically, a Diophantine
m

R

\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}\subset R

a_{i}a_{j}+1

\mathbb{Q}
. However, finite fields

This paper considers a generalization of Diophantine tuples in finite fields.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a polynomial which is non-square and non-constant, q be an odd prime power. For integers
d,m

such that for any
d

from the set, the element

f(a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{d}})

In simple terms, this includes the classical case where

Our main result is the following Theorem:

The number

The Weil estimate
[

Let us recall one form of the Weil estimate in its simplest form.

Definition 2.1
.

A
multiplicative character
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a map

\chi:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}

such that

•

\chi(0)=0
,

•

\chi(a)\neq 0
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
,

•

\chi(ab)=\chi(a)\chi(b)
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
.

The
order
of
\chi
is the smallest positive integer
e
such that
\chi^{e}(a)=1
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
.

For example, if
q=p
is a prime number, then the Legendre symbol

a\mapsto\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }a=0,\\
1&\text{if }a\neq 0\text{ is a quadratic residue modulo }p,\\
-1&\text{if }a\neq 0\text{ is not a quadratic residue modulo }p.\end{cases}

(2)

is a multiplicative character on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of order 2.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
4
, Theorem 11.23]

Let
\chi
be a multiplicative character of order
e
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a polynomial such that there does not exist
g\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
with
f=g^{e}
. Then

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(f(x))=O\left(q^{1/2}\right).

More precisely, if
\deg f=d

\left|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(f(x))\right|\leq(d-1)q^{1/2}.

Conversion from Diophantine Tuples to Character Sums

In this section, we transform the problem of counting the number of Diophantine tuples into characters’ sum.

Let
\mathscr{A}=\binom{\left[m\right]}{d}
be the set family with all the subsets of
\left[m\right]
which have cardinality
d
. Let us consider the system of polynomial equations over the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
:

f\left(\prod_{i\in A}a_{i}\right)=b_{A}^{2},\quad\forall A\in\mathscr{A}

(3)

Let
D
be the set of all solutions to the system above in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the map

\pi:D\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m},\quad\pi(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots a_{m},b_{A_{1}},b_{A_{2}},\ldots,b_{A_{|\mathscr{A}|}})=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots a_{m})

is “
2^{\binom{m}{d}}
-to-1”. Let
\mathrm{Conf}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
be the set of all
m
-tuples
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
satisfying

a_{i}\neq a_{j},\quad\forall 1\leq i<j\leq m.

Let
D^{*}=\pi^{-1}(\mathrm{Conf}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))

N_{f}^{\binom{[m]}{d}}(q)=\frac{\mathrm{Card}(D^{*})}{m!2^{\binom{m}{d}}}

Since
\mathrm{Card}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}\setminus\mathrm{Conf}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))=O(q^{m-1})

1

Case 1: When
\deg(f)=1

\mathrm{Card}(D)=q^{m}+O(q^{m-1}).

(4)

Case 2: When

\mathrm{Card}(D)=q^{m}+O(q^{m-\frac{1}{2}}).

(5)

, the unique multiplicative character of order 2. For any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the equation

3

A\in\mathscr{A}
of terms
\chi\left(f\left(\prod\limits_{i\in A}a_{i}\right)\right)+1
.

For each
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots a_{m})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}

(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots a_{m},b_{A_{1}},b_{A_{2}},\ldots,b_{A_{|\mathscr{A}|}})

\displaystyle\mathrm{Card}(D)

\displaystyle=q^{m}+\sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\binom{m}{d}},\epsilon\neq 0}R(\epsilon)

Theorem 4.1

Let

Corollary 4.1.1
.

For a polynomial
f(x)
over
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
depending on
m-1
parameters
a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}
, let

A=\{(a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})\in k^{m-1}\mid f(x)\ \text{is a square polynomial over}\ k\}.

If
\overline{A}\neq\varnothing

\mathrm{Card}\bigl(A\bigr)=O(q^{m-2})

Proof.

Suppose that
\deg f=d
, and write

f(x)=c_{d}x^{d}+c_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\ldots+c_{0},

where each coefficient
c_{i}
is a polynomial function
f_{i}(a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})
of the parameters. As
(a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})
vary, the tuples
(c_{0},\ldots,c_{d})
form a constructible subset of
k^{d+1}
. Let
Z\subset k^{d+1}
denote its Zariski closure; then
Z
is an algebraic variety with
\dim Z\leq m-1
.

Consider the set of monic polynomials of degree at most
d
that are squares. Any such polynomial can be written as

g(x)^{2}=f^{*}(x)=c_{d}^{*}x^{d}+\ldots+c_{0}^{*},

where
\deg g\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor\triangleq d^{\prime}
and

g(x)=b_{d^{\prime}}x^{d^{\prime}}+\ldots+b_{0}

with
b_{i}\in k
. Each coefficient
c_{i}^{*}
is a polynomial function of the
b_{j}
, so the tuples
(c_{0}^{*},\ldots,c_{d}^{*})
form a constructible subset of
k^{d+1}
. Let
V\subset k^{d+1}
denote its Zariski closure; then
V
is an algebraic variety.

Since
V\subset k^{d+1}
, it follows that
V\cap Z\subset Z
. Both
V
and
Z
are varieties in
k^{d+1}
, so
V\cap Z
is also a variety in
k^{d+1}
. If
\overline{A}\neq\varnothing
, then

. By Theorem

, we have
\dim(V\cap Z)<\dim(Z)

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
|\epsilon|>1
, i.e., more than one component
\epsilon_{A}=1
. Then

R(\epsilon)=\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\chi\left(f\left(\prod_{i\in A_{j}}a_{i}\right)\right)=O(q^{m-1}),

where
\chi
is the quadratic character on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
f
is a fixed polynomial.

The following proof uses a method initially proposed by Igor E. Shparlinski
[
7
]
.

Proof.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
(
k\geq 2
) be the subsets corresponding to the nonzero entries of
\epsilon
. There exists an element
a
in the union
A_{1}\cup\dots\cup A_{k}
that does not belong to every
A_{j}
. Define

\mathscr{B}=\{j\mid a\in A_{j}\},\qquad\mathscr{C}=\{j\mid a\notin A_{j}\}.

Both
\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{C}
are nonempty.

Then we can write

R(\epsilon)=\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\prod_{j\in\mathscr{B}}\chi\left(f\left(\prod_{i\in A_{j}}a_{i}\right)\right)\prod_{j\in\mathscr{C}}\chi\left(f\left(\prod_{i\in A_{j}}a_{i}\right)\right).

Note that
\forall b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, the map (without loss of generality, we assume
a=a_{1}
)

(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\mapsto\left(\frac{a_{1}}{b^{d-1}},a_{2}b,\dots,a_{m}b\right),

is a permutation on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
. Hence,

R(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\prod_{j\in\mathscr{B}}\chi\Big(f\big(\prod_{i\in A_{j}}a_{i}\big)\Big)\prod_{j\in\mathscr{C}}\chi\Big(f\big(b^{d}\prod_{i\in A_{j}}a_{i}\big)\Big).

Rearranging the sums over
a_{1}
and
b
, we define

F(a)=\prod_{j\in\mathscr{B}}f\Big(\prod_{i\in A_{j}}a_{i}\Big),\qquad G(b)=\prod_{j\in\mathscr{C}}f\Big(b^{d}\prod_{i\in A_{j}}a_{i}\Big),

so that

R(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{a_{2},\dots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\sum_{a_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(F(a))\right)\left(\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(G(b))\right).

Let

\mathscr{D}=\left\{(a_{2},\dots,a_{m})\mid F(a)\text{ is a square polynomial over }\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\right\},

and

\mathscr{E}=\left\{(a_{2},\dots,a_{m})\mid G(b)\text{ is a square polynomial over }\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\right\}.

Note that when choosing a set
A_{F}\in\mathscr{B}
, let
a_{i}=1
for all
i\in A_{F}\setminus\{1\}
, and
a_{i}=0
for all
i\notin A_{F}
, we have

F(a)=f(a)\cdot f(0)^{|\mathscr{B}|-1},

which is not a square polynomial over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.

Similarly, when choosing a set
A_{G}\in\mathscr{C}
, let
a_{i}=1
for all
i\in A_{G}
and
a_{i}=0
for all
i\notin A_{G}
, we have

G(b)=f(b^{d})\cdot f(0)^{|\mathscr{C}|-1},

which is not a square polynomial over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.

By Corollary
4.1.1
, we have
\mathrm{Card}(\mathscr{D})=O(q^{m-2})
and
\mathrm{Card}(\mathscr{E})=O(q^{m-2})
.
Hence,

\displaystyle R(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{(a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathscr{D}\cup\mathscr{E}}\left(\sum_{a_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(F(a))\right)\left(\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(G(b))\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{(a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})\notin\mathscr{D}\cup\mathscr{E}}\left(\sum_{a_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(F(a))\right)\left(\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(G(b))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\times O(q^{m-2})\times O(q)\times O(q)+\frac{1}{q-1}\times O(q^{m-1})\times O(q^{1/2})\times O(q^{1/2})

\displaystyle=O(q^{m-1}).

∎

Comments and Future Directions

6.1
Remark

However, when we attempted to extend this approach to more general symmetric polynomial conditions of the form
f(a_{i_{1}},a_{i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{d}})
being a square, Shparlinski’s technique fails, as the necessary separation of variables is no longer possible.

This limitation means that using the product form
\prod\limits_{k=1}^{d}a_{i_{k}}
is not just helpful, but actually necessary for this method to work. On the other hand, the conditions studied by Kim, Yip, and Yoo
[
5
]
focusing on more general symmetric polynomials cannot apply Shparlinski’s method in the same way.

6.2
Future Research Directions

1.

Whether Shparlinski’s method can be improved to be adapted to more general symmetric polynomials remains an open question.

2.

The implied constant in the error term is not explicit. It may be possible to compute it more precisely, as in
[
1
]
.

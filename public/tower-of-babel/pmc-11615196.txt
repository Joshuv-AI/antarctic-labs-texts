Association of systemic immunity-inflammation index with type 2 diabetes and insulin resistance in NHANES 2005–2018
Authors: Qinying Zhao, Xuan Liu, Jialu Xu, Xiaojuan Rao, Ming Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough the interplay between inflammation and diabetes is increasingly recognized, it is unclear whether the systemic immunity-inflammation index (SII), as a biomarker of systemic inflammatory response, is associated with type 2 diabetes (T2D) and insulin resistance (IR). This cross-sectional study was performed in the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) from 2005 to 2018, and finally enrolled 17,017 participants. To explore the relationship between SII and T2D and IR, a series of statistical analyses were conducted including weighted multivariate linear regression, logistic regression, and subgroup analysis. The fully adjusted multivariate linear regression revealed a positive correlation between SII and fasting plasma glucose (β = 0.13, 95% CI: 0.01, 0.24), fasting serum insulin (β = 12.90, 95% CI: 6.77, 19.04), and homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (β = 0.68, 95% CI: 0.25, 1.10). A per-SD increase in SII was found to be associated with a 4% increase in the odds of T2D, and a 5% increase in the odds of IR. The trend was significant across all SII quartile groups. Subgroup analysis revealed a stronger positive association existed between SII and T2D and IR in female, younger, and obese populations. SII was positively associated with the risk of T2D and IR, indicating that reducing SII levels may help prevent these conditions in the general population.Keywords: Systemic immunity-inflammation index, Type 2 diabetes, Insulin resistance, NHANESSubject terms: Endocrinology, Medical research

Introduction

Approximately 530 million adults worldwide have diabetes1, with type 2 diabetes (T2D) accounting for 98%2. T2D is characterized by hyperglycaemia, insulin resistance (IR), and relative impairment in insulin secretion. Related complications contribute to a range of morbidity, mortality, and enormous healthcare spending. Therefore, early identification and treatment are critical for T2D.

In recent years, epidemiological studies have shown that Inflammation plays a role in the pathogenesis of T2D3. T2D patients show a range of pathological changes in their islets, including immune cell infiltration, adipose expansion, cell death, and fibrosis4–7. Numerous studies have demonstrated that a positive association between inflammation biomarkers, including tumour necrosis factor-alpha, ESR, and pro-inflammatory cytokines, and the incidence and progression of IR and T2D3,8. Moreover, recent evidence confirmed that the interfering with IL-1 pathway could alleviate the restoration of β-cell dysfunction in T2D and its complications9–11.

The systemic immunity-inflammation index (SII) is a novel biomarker that serves as an indicator of both the local immune response and the systemic inflammatory response throughout the body12. The SII was initially proposed as a means of evaluating the prognosis of patients undergoing hepatocellular carcinoma resection, and was subsequently adopted for use in a wide range of clinical research studies12. Several current studies have shown that SII is related to various diseases, including rheumatoid arthritis13, hepatic steatosis14, cerebral vasospasm15, colorectal cancer16, and others. Additionally, SII has been found to be positively associated with a range of diabetic complications, including diabetic kidney disease17, diabetic macular edema18, coronary heart disease19, and others. However, the relationship between SII and T2D and IR remains to be fully elucidated. Therefore, we conducted a cross-sectional study based on the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) database with the objective of exploring the aforementioned relationship in the general population of the United States and identifying an indicator for the evaluation of T2D and IR.

Methods

Study population

Our study involved participants from the NHANES, which represents a non-institutionalized civilian population residing in 50 states of the United States20. The NHANES protocols were approved by the Health Statistics Research Ethics Review Board, and informed consent was obtained from all participants during the initial assessment. Detailed information about NHANES is available on the official website (www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes). This study utilized data from the NHANES 2005–2018, encompassing a total of 85,751 participants. Individuals lacking SII data or with missing fasting plasma glucose (FPG), glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c), fasting serum insulin (FSI), or homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) data were excluded. We also excluded individuals below 20 and those not examined at the Mobile Examination Center (MEC). Finally, 17,017 participants with complete data were enrolled in this cross-sectional study (see Fig. 1).

Flow chart of the study population.

Exposure variable and outcome variables

The exposure variable was the SII, which was calculated by multiplying the platelet count (103/counts) by the neutrophil count (103/counts) and dividing by the lymphocyte count (103/counts). To account for the skewed distribution of SII, a logarithmic transformation was performed prior to the following analysis. The participants were divided into four groups based on their SII quartile: Q1 [0.183, 2.506), Q2 [2.506, 2.655), Q3 [2.655, 2.81), and Q4 [2.81, 4.453). The outcome variables comprised risk markers of T2D, including FPG, HbA1c, FSI, and HOMA-IR, as well as the prevalence of T2D and IR. The HOMA-IR was calculated using the following formula: [FPG×FSI]/22.521. T2D is defined as one or more of the following, except T1DM: HbA1C 6.5%+, FPG 7 mmol/L+; a random plasma glucose level of 11.1 mmol/L or greater, a two-hour oral glucose tolerance test blood glucose level of 11.1 mmol/L or greater; the use of diabetes medication, or a doctor’s diagnosis of T2D22. In the United States, a HOMA-IR value of 2.6 or greater indicates IR23.

Covariates

Our study considered demographic data and variables that affect both SII and diabetes in clinical practice as covariates. The demographic data included age, sex, race (Mexican American, Non-Hispanic Black, Non-Hispanic White, other Hispanic, and other race), education level (less than high school, high school, and more than high school), smoking status (former smoker, non-smoker, and current smoker), and drinking status (former drinker, non-drinker, and current drinker). The dietary condition of the participants was evaluated using the Healthy Eating Index (HEI) 2015, as proposed by the American Dietary Guidelines24. The physical activity condition was assessed using the Metabolic Equivalent (MET/h) per week metric25. Anthropometric measurements included systolic blood pressure (SBP, mmHg), diastolic blood pressure (DBP, mmHg), and body mass index (BMI, kg/m2). Laboratory indicators included FPG (mmol/L), HbA1c (%), FSI (pmol/L), total cholesterol (TC, mmol/L), serum creatinine (SCr, umol/L), serum uric acid (SUA, umol/L), hemoglobin (Hb, g/ dL), lymphocyte count (103/uL), neutrophil count (103/uL), and platelet count (103/uL). All blood specimens were collected after an overnight and stored under refrigerated conditions (2–8℃) until they were shipped to the University of Missouri for testing. Cardiovascular disease (CVD) encompasses a range of conditions, including coronary heart disease, congestive heart failure, heart attack, stroke, and angina. Hypertension is defined as a SBP of 140mmHg or greater and/or a DBP of 90mmHg or greater, or self-reported hypertension and the use of anti-hypertensive drugs. Obesity is defined as a BMI of 30 or greater kg/m2. Besides, trained technicians collected data on medication, including those for diabetes, dyslipidaemia and hypertension.

Statistical analysis

In this cross-sectional study, the MEC exam weights (wtmec2 year) were used to account for the complex sample design of the NHANES26. Participants were categorized into four groups based on the quartile of log(SII). Continuous variables were presented as weighted means (standard error), while categorical variables were presented as weighted proportions. Baseline characteristics were compared between different groups using a weighted linear regression for continuous variables and a weighted chi-square test for categorical variables.

To evaluate the relationship between SII and diabetes, multiple weighted regression analyses were conducted in our study. Three models were employed: a crude model, which was an unadjusted regression; a model 1, which was adjusted for age, sex, race, and education level; and a model 2, which was additionally adjusted for smoking status, drinking status, the quartile of HEI, the quartile of physical activity, CVD, hypertension, SCr, SUA, TC, Hb, BMI, use of diabetic medicine, lipid-lowering agents, and anti-hypertensive drugs. Furthermore, the relationship between SII and risk markers of T2D (FPG, HbA1c, FINS, and HOMA-IR) was analyzed using weighted multivariate linear regression models and the calculated β (95% CIs). Additionally, in order to ascertain whether the correlation between SII and T2D and IR persisted under different conditions, weighted multivariate logistic regression models were conducted for subgroups analyses based on sex, age (≥ 60 years), and BMI (≥ 30 kg/m2). The aforementioned associations were deemed reliable when the interaction p-value was not significant. Conversely, there may be a special population otherwise. Statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.3.1 (http://www.r-project.org/). A p-value of less than 0.05 was deemed to be statistically significant.

Results

Clinical characteristics of the participants

The clinical characteristics of the participants who were enrolled in our study were summarized in the Table 1. Participants in the Q4 group were older, less male, more Non-Hispanic White, had a lower education level, were former or current smokers and former drinkers, had CVD, T2D, hypertension, obesity, and had higher levels of SBP, BMI, FPG, HbA1c, FSI, HOMA-IR, SCr, neutrophil count, and platelet count in comparison to those in the lower SII group. Significant differences were observed between the various groups in the terms of Hb levels, lymphocyte count, HEI, and physical activity. In addition, participants with elevated SII levels exhibited a higher prevalence of diabetic medication, lipid-lowering agent, and antihypertensive drug utilization (all P < 0.05).

Association between SII and risk markers of T2D

The association between SII and risk markers of T2D was shown in Table 2. After fully adjusting for confounding factors in the multivariate linear regression models, significant positive associations were observed between SII and FPG (β = 0.13, 95% CI: 0.01, 0.24), FSI (β = 12.90, 95% CI: 6.77, 19.04), and HOMA-IR (β = 0.68, 95% CI: 0.25, 1.10). Meanwhile, in the fully adjusted multivariate logistic regression models, the odds ratios (ORs) for FPG were 0.06 (95% CI: 0, 0.12) for Q2, 0.04 (95% CI: -0.04, 0.11) for Q3, and 0.08 (95% CI: 0.02, 0.15) for Q4 compared to Q1; the ORs for FSI were 0.42 (95% CI: -3.04, 3.89) for Q2, 2.57 (95% CI: -0.59, 5.73) for Q3, and 7.07 (95% CI: 2.57, 11.57) for Q4 compared to Q1. The ORs for HOMA-IR were − 0.01 (95% CI: -0.23, 0.22) for Q2, 0.04 (95% CI: -0.18, 0.25) for Q3, and 0.30 (95% CI: 0.01, 0.58) for Q4 compared to Q1. However, no statistically significant correlation was observed between SII and HbA1c. Furthermore, no statistically significant linear trend was identified between SII and FPG, FSI, and HOMA-IR.

Association between SII with T2D

As shown in Table 3, after adjusting for confounding variables (model 2), a per-SD increase in SII was associated with a 4% increase in the odds of T2D. Additionally, the OR for Q4 was 1.09 (95% CI: 1.07, 1.11), 1.07 (95% CI: 1.05, 1.09), and 1.02 (95% CI: 1.01, 1.03), respectively, in the crude model (no adjustment), model 1 (adjusted for age, sex, race, and education level), and model 2 (adjusted for age, sex, race, education level, smoking status, drinking status, the quartile of HEI, the quartile of physical activity, CVD, hypertension, SCr, SUA, TC, Hb, BMI, use of diabetic medicine, lipid-lowering agents, and anti-hypertensive drugs). The linear trend remained statistically significant among the SII quartile groups (all p-values for trend were less than 0.05). To examine the robustness of the above results in different groups, a subgroup analysis was conducted (Fig. 2A). A stronger positive association between SII and T2D was observed in female individuals (OR = 1.34, 95% CI: 1.12, 1.56), those aged under 60 years (OR = 2.30, 95% CI: 1.17, 3.42), and obese individuals (OR = 1.21, 95% CI: 1.09, 1.33). Additionally, sex was considered as the most significant factor influencing the relationship between SII and T2D (P for interaction < 0.001).

Association between SII with IR

A per-SD increase in SII was found to be associated with an elevated risk of IR in multivariate logistic regression models (crude model: OR = 1.18, 95% CI: 1.14, 1.23; model 1: OR = 1.20, 95% CI: 1.15, 1.24; model 2: OR = 1.05, 95% CI: 1.01, 1.09). Moreover, this trend remained statistically significant when SII was considered as a categorical variable in quartiles. Table 4 showed a progressively elevated risk of IR in the quartile with the highest SII compared to the lowest quartile in the multivariate logistic regression models (P for trend < 0.05). In the subgroup analysis (Fig. 2B), sex (P for interaction = 0.023) was considered as the most prominent interactive factor influencing the correlation between SII and IR. The study found that an increase in SII was associated with an elevated risk of IR in females (OR = 1.99, 95% CI: 1.37, 2.60) compared to males. Additionally, individuals with obesity exhibited an elevated risk of IR (OR = 1.63, 95% CI: 1.07, 1.99) compared to those without obesity. Furthermore, the association between SII and the elevated risk of IR was more pronounced in participants younger than 60 years old (OR = 1.63, 95% CI: 1.15, 2.11).

Discussion

In this cross-sectional study, 17,017 adults met our criteria and were consequently included in our analysis. After adjusting for confounding factors, a positive association were observed between SII and risk markers of T2D, including FPG, FSI, and HOMA-IR). In the fully adjusted multivariate logistic regression models, higher SII levels were found an independent risk factor for T2D and IR. The subgroup analysis indicated that a stronger association between SII and T2D and IR was observed in females, aged under 60 years, and obese individuals. Besides, the interaction test showed that sex may be a prominent factor influencing the association between SII and T2D and IR. The above results suggested that SII may serve as a potential biomarker for T2D and IR.

Recently, the interplay between inflammation and T2D has been increasingly recognized. Inflammation is an important pathogenesis of T2D, leading to β-cell dysfunction and IR. Cruz Nathalia G, et al. have proposed that inflammation may establish a causal relationship with T2D, which further contributes to the incidence of IR and diabetic complications27. In the islets of patients with T2D, a series of pathological changes associated with inflammation have been observed, including immune cell infiltration, adipose expansion, cell death, and fibrosis4–7. Studies have shown that the inflammation biomarkers, including tumor necrosis factor-alpha, ESR and pro-inflammatory cytokines, are positively associated with the incidence and progression of T2D3,8. Besides, previous studies have demonstrated that the inhibition of the IL-1 pathway can alleviate the progression of T2D and restore β-cell function in clinical practice9–11. Therefore, identifying and stopping inflammation early may help prevent and manage T2D.

SII has been validated as a reliable biomarker for assessing the local and system inflammatory response throughout the body12. Several studies have shown a correlation between SII and various diseases. In a cohort study of 8,697 US adults, Chan Yang, et al. found that higher SII levels were significantly and non-linearly associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality (HR 1.74, 95% CI: 1.15, 2.63) and a 70% higher risk of cardiovascular mortality (HR 1.70, 95% CI: 1.18, 3.30)28. Guo W, et al. Conducted a study involving 3,937 T2D patients, which revealed that higher SII levels were associated with an increased likelihood of diabetic kidney disease (OR 1.42, 95% CI: 1.10, 1.83)17. Similarly, another study revealed a correlation between elevated SII levels and augmented urinary albumin excretion in a cohort of 36,463 participants29. A cross-sectional study found that SII levels were markedly elevated in serous macular detachment secondary to diabetic macular oedema, particularly in advanced cases18. Song Y, et al. found a positive correlation between a high SII level and the risk of developing hepatic steatosis (OR = 1.30, 95% CI: 1.10, 1.52) in a cross-sectional investigation30. Ma J, et al. also found that high SII values were associated with a higher incidence of coronary heart disease19. Previous studies have reported on the relationships between SII and diabetic complications, however, the association of SII with T2D and IR remains unclear. In light of these findings, we postulated that SII may serve as a reliable marker for T2D and IR in our study.

Considering the skewed distribution of SII, we employed the logarithmic transformation of SII in conducting the subsequent analysis. Diabetes is affected by various factors, including age, sex, race, diet, smoking and drinking status, physical activity, BMI, CVD, hypertension, medication, and others. So our study took into account a number of potential confounding variables, including age, sex, race, education level, smoking status, drinking status, HEI, physical activity, CVD, hypertension, SCr, SUA, TC, Hb, BMI, use of diabetic medicine, lipid-lowering agents, and anti-hypertensive drug. And we further investigated the association of SII with T2D and IR in US adults. Our findings indicated that subjects with elevated SII levels exhibited elevated FPG, FSI, and HOMA-IR values. After adjusting for the confounding factors, higher SII levels remained independently associated with an increased risk of T2D and IR. This finding is consistent with that of a previous study conducted in the United States31. Additionally, a more pronounced association between SII and T2D and IR was discerned in the female cohort, comprising individuals below the age of 60 years and with a BMI of 30 kg/m2of above. This phenomenon may be related to gender differences, age effects, and obesity status. Previous studies have reported that variations in sex chromosomes and alterations in estrogen levels may contribute to disturbances in glucose and lipid metabolism, which in turn may elevate the risk of developing T2D32. Obesity is a significant predictor of T2D and is associated with increased inflammatory processes in T2D by elevating the levels of inflammatory markers33. Researches has proposed that the association between inflammation and T2D appears to be stronger in the older population. Conversely, our study demonstrated that the association of SII with T2D and IR was more significant in younger individuals (aged < 60 years). It is postulated that this result may be attributable to the elevated lymphocyte count, which could result in a reduction in SII levels.

Our study has several strengths. Firstly, we proposed novel insights into the association of SII with T2D and IR in the general population. Additionally, the large sample size and precise characterization of patient attributes facilitated a more comprehensive illustration of this association. However, as a cross-sectional and observational study, our research is subject to a number of limitations. One limitation of this study is that it only established an observational association and did not establish a causal relationship between SII and T2D or IR in US adults. Further prospective and cohort studies are needed to elucidate the potential association of SII with T2D and IR. Besides, despite the adjustment for several covariates, there is a possibility that unconsidered potential confounders may have influenced the results, such as dietary patterns, the course of diabetes, and other metabolic diseases. Therefore, further research is required to investigate the influence of additional confounding factors on SII in the progression of T2D and IR.

In conclusion, our study indicated that higher SII levels significantly correlated to the elevated levels of FPG, FSI, and HOMA-IR. Moreover, a positive association between SII and T2D and IR was observed in the general population. Further prospective and experimental studies are required to confirm the causal relationship and underlying mechanism.

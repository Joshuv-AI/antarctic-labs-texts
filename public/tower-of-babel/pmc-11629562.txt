Assessing the feasibility, acceptability, and preliminary efficacy of the ‘TraumaRelief’ app for PTSD symptom management in Turkey: Study protocol for a pilot randomized controlled trial
Authors: Hasan Arslan, A Esin Yilmaz
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rate of exposure to traumatic events is high globally, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is one of the most serious consequences. It was reported that approximately 14 million people were affected by earthquakes in Turkey in February 2023. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is a therapeutic approach to treating PTSD and has a proven efficacy. However, access to face-to-face therapies such as CBT is limited and there is a high dropout rate. Technology-based interventions can offer new solutions to make CBT more accessible and integrated into daily life. In this study, we will develop an application called TraumaRelief, which aims to relieve PTSD symptoms. We will test the feasibility, acceptability, and efficacy of this application through a pilot randomized controlled study conducted in Turkey, with 105 participants aged between 18 and 65 years who have experienced a traumatic event. Participants will be randomly assigned to the Application Plus Online Therapist Support Group (AT + OT Group), Application Only Group (Group A), and Waitlist Control Group (WLC Group). This study aims to evaluate symptoms of PTSD, depression, and anxiety, as well as their effects on quality of life. In addition, the feasibility and acceptability of the study; the attrition, consent, recruitment, and adherence rate to the application; fidelity of intervention delivery; system usability score; preferred and least preferred module components; app utilization frequency; and likelihood to recommend the application will be examined. Application efficacy will be monitored with follow-ups at one and three months. The results could provide important information on the integration of technology-based treatments with psychotherapy. In addition, it could allow the evaluation of potential mobile applications in the field of psychotherapy and represents an important step in the development and evaluation of a customized mobile application for a society with unique cultural and social dynamics, such as Turkey. It could also be an important resource for increasing the capacity to cope with the effects of traumatic events that have occurred in Turkey or future events.Clinical Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov Identifier NCT06288594. Introduction

The rate of exposure to traumatic events is high globally. In a study involving 68,894 participants from 24 countries, 70 % stated that they had experienced at least one traumatic event in their lifetime. Additionally, 30.5 % had experienced four or more traumatic events (Benjet et al., 2016). Recently, the Turkish Republic's Strategy and Budget Presidency (Strateji and Başkanlığı, 2023) reported that approximately 14 million people in Turkey experienced the earthquakes that occurred in February 2023.

PTSD is one of the most common outcomes associated with exposure to traumatic events (Kessler, 2000). Some individuals may show severe PTSD symptoms after experiencing a traumatic event, even if they are not diagnosed with PTSD (Gagnon-Sanschagrin et al., 2022).

One of the most important evidence-based approaches to PTSD treatment is CBT. Many studies have proven that CBT can eliminate or alleviate PTSD symptoms (Öst et al., 2023; Watkins et al., 2018). However, there are many problems associated with participating in or continuing treatment, including stigma and reluctance to seek psychotherapy (Corrigan et al., 2014), biases against psychotherapy (Gary, 2005), reluctance to share emotional problems, negative beliefs about psychotherapy (Vogt, 2011), and transportation difficulties and associated costs (Cochran et al., 2022; Paris and Professor, 2020). Additionally, high dropout rates have been reported for CBT (Bhattacharya et al., 2023; Fernandez et al., 2015; Swift and Greenberg, 2014).

These challenges have inspired the creation of new solutions to make CBT more accessible and adaptable to daily life. In particular, in post-earthquake Turkey, the lack of professionals providing psychosocial support (officially, only 7810 psychosocial support personnel are available) (Strateji and Başkanlığı, 2023) and insufficient resources make it necessary to develop technology-based interventions.

CBT is an important intervention in PTSD therapy; therefore, technological aids such as online platforms and mobile applications can amplify the reach of CBT, making it more accessible and affordable. Additionally, problems such as stigmatization and reluctance to express emotions can be overcome. Phone applications have recently been suggested as a solution to make CBT accessible to a wider general population. According to the “We Are Social” (Kemp, 2023) report, 95 % of the Turkish population is a mobile phone user, and 97 % use the internet for an average of seven hours a day, indicating a significant reliance on mobile devices for daily activities. Based on such high rates of smartphone use, phone applications offer a great opportunity to increase access to CBT-based therapy and integrate it into daily life.

Many CBT-based phone applications are available to relieve PTSD symptom severity (Bröcker et al., 2022; Dworkin et al., 2023). While many of these phone applications include portions of CBT components, there is a lack of evidence-based support (Bröcker et al., 2023; Goreis et al., 2020). Additionally, the lack of therapist-supported interventions is a major problem in experimental studies conducted using phone applications aimed at alleviating or eliminating PTSD symptom severity. Thus, there were no significant differences in PTSD severity between the experimental and control groups (Ellis et al., 2022; Goreis et al., 2020). It is worth noting that despite the needs arising from the recent earthquake and the lack of expert resources in the field of psychology, no application targeting PTSD and its related symptoms has been developed in Turkey.

In this context, this study reflects an attempt to develop an evidence-based phone application called TraumaRelief to meet the many CBT-based needs of individuals exposed to traumatic events. This application aims to eliminate existing deficiencies in this field by using online therapist support, eight separate modules, dozens of animated videos, tens of thousands of words of text support, and more than 30 CBT-based exercises.

TraumaRelief provides tools needed to combat PTSD and related symptoms, develops coping skills, and aims to use the advantages of mHealth (using mobile technology to enhance health outcomes) at the highest level. TraumaRelief has the potential to enable users to actively participate in their treatment and to integrate what they learn into their daily lives. It also offers interactive tools that provide 24/7 accessible resources.

This is a pilot randomized controlled study that aims to explain the effect of TraumaRelief, not only on PTSD but also on depressive symptom severity and quality of life. The study also aims to evaluate the acceptability and feasibility of TraumaRelief.

Participants were randomly assigned to three groups: The first group will use the application and receive therapist support via an online consultation module (Application Plus Online Therapist Support Group [AT + OT Group]). The second group will use the application but will not receive online therapy support (Application Only Group [A Group]). The third group is the waitlist control group (Waitlist Control Group [WLC Group]).

The study's feasibility and acceptability is examined with these questions: 1) What will be the enrollment, consent, and retention rates of the participants? 2) How consistently do participants adhere to the application? (3) How accurate are the interventions provided by online therapists in achieving the intended outcomes? 4) How will acceptability be determined, including the system usability scale score, preferences for different modules and components, frequency and duration of application use, and overall recommendation level for the application by the participants?

Method

Study design

This pilot randomized controlled trial is designed to evaluate the feasibility and acceptability of TraumaRelief for PTSD symptoms and to collect pilot data on the application's preliminary efficacy. The research was divided into three groups: AT + OT Group, A Group, and WLC Group which were randomly assigned (1:1:1).

Participants will comprise individuals 18 and 65 aged who have experienced trauma and have exceeded the PTSD symptom severity threshold (as measured by the PCL-5 scale). To determine eligibility, a preliminary assessment will be performed using self-report scales and telephone interviews.

The intervention duration of the study will be five weeks. The AT + OT Group will have standardized sessions lasting 20–30 min once a week. The A Group will also be able to use the application for five weeks but will not receive therapist support.

The assessment timeframes for the study are as follows: First, participants will complete screening assessment forms. After the eligibility baseline assessment will do (T0). Then the allocation of participants to groups will be determined (T1). Following this, a five-week intervention period will begin (T2). The intervention will involve the use of the app and/or online therapist support. However, no intervention will be performed on the WLC Group. After the five-week intervention period, there will be an assessment to determine the effects of the intervention on the participants (T3). One month after the intervention, participants will receive a follow-up evaluation (T4). Finally, the follow-up evaluation will be repeated three months after the intervention (T5).

For this research, we are following several key guidelines: CONSORT (Eldridge et al., 2016), SPIRIT (Chan et al., 2013) guidelines, and the m-health evidence reporting and assessment checklist (mERA) (Agarwal et al., 2016) (see Appendix A). Fig. 1 illustrates the consort flow chart.

The online research will be conducted in Turkey. The implementation and evaluation team will comprise psychologists each with at least a master's degree in psychology.

Sample size

In this study, a Cohen's d value of 0.025, alpha error level of 0.05, and power of 0.80 will be taken as effect sizes. For the analysis involving three groups and four measurements, the correlation between measurements will be taken as 0.5. Based on these parameters, the G*Power 3.1 analysis (Faul et al., 2009) suggests a sample size of 102 participants per group. In addition, according to Teare et al. (2014), the sample size will be calculated to preserve the statistical power of the study, considering potential dropout and missing data. Therefore, the study is planned with a total of 105 participants, with 35 participants in each group, ensuring the sustainability of the study.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria includes: (1) adults aged between 18 and 65 years; (2) experience of at least one traumatic event; (3) score of 10 or higher on the PCL-5 scale; (4) having a smartphone; (5) constant access to the Internet; and (6) being at least a primary school graduate; (7) required to have proficient Turkish reading and comprehension skills (the app is in Turkish). Exclusion criteria includes: (1) no access to a smartphone or the Internet, (2) currently receiving any therapy, (3) risk of suicide, (4) refusal to provide informed consent, or (5) history of neurological disorders.

Randomization and blinding

The recruitment process will begin with internet and social media announcements calling for research participants. Individuals who respond to the announcement will complete an informed consent form that includes the study purpose, duration, and nature of the intervention. Those who accept and complete the informed consent form will complete the screening assessments. Participants who complete this first stage will be contacted for confirmation via phone. Participants will be determined by considering the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Participants will be randomized using random.org. In this study, the randomization process assigned participants equally to the three intervention groups: AT + OT Group, A Group, and WLC Group.

After randomization, the group to which the participants are assigned and what is expected from them in their group will be explained via e-mail. Due to the nature of our research design, it is not possible to implement complete blindness between participants and researchers conducting the intervention. That is, each participant will know which group they are in.

Ethical considerations and informed consent

This study will be carried out within the framework of the approval provided by the Dokuz Eylül University Ethics Committee (E-87347630-659-877,270). Each individual will be informed that their participation is voluntary and that they can leave the study at any time. Particularly, participants who stated on the sociodemographic information form that they had thoughts or plans to harm themselves will be referred to professional health services. In addition, it is stated that the research process and the privacy of the participants will be protected, and the information will not be shared with third parties. The information will be used for scientific study without disclosing personal information. What can be done about possible risks and problems that may arise in the study is explained.

Development and preparation process of the application

The application was prepared under the supervision of a doctoral student and professor at Izmir Dokuz Eylül University. At the preparation stage, the content was evaluated by two experts with a doctorate in clinical psychology, ensuring that the application meets quality standards. Revisions were made in accordance with the evaluator's judgment. In the next stage, three people that have experienced traumatic events will be accessed for deficiencies and suggestions regarding the application. The application will be finalized based on the suggestions and feedback requested. This last step will make the application user-friendly and effective to the extent that final adjustments can be made.

Introduction of TraumaRelief

TraumaRelief is designed for individuals who have experienced a traumatic event and comprises eight modules. The contents, aims, usage conditions, and who can access the modules are displayed in Appendix B.

Technical specifications of the application

TraumaRelief is designed for both iOS and Android operating systems so that everyone who uses a smartphone can operate it. A constant Internet connection is required to download the application and use it effectively and ensures that the app stays up-to-date and users have constant access to the intervention content.

The application tracks users' progress in each module (how much time they spend in each module and the number of times they log in to the application). In addition, after 80 % of the users' psychoeducation module (1) is completed, the symptom coping module (2) is made available. After 80 % of the symptom coping module is completed, the imaginary exposure module (4) can be accessed. This is designed to ensure that learning progresses step-by-step and there is full understanding of the content of each module.

Additionally, the written text in the application is in summary form. Participants are advised to refer to www.traumarelieftr.com for detailed information of the modules. If participants visit this site, they can access more detailed texts on the subject they want.

Intervention

A Group will use only the mobile application, AT + OT Group will be provided with weekly therapist support in addition to application use, and the WLC Group will not use the application during the study but will gain access after the study is complete.

AT + OT Group

Participants will meet online with therapists once a week for five weeks, with each session lasting 20–30 min. These interviews will be implemented according to a pre-prepared therapist manual to ensure that the participants better understand the application content and use the application effectively. The manual covers topics such as information about the week's application module, sample solutions, homework checks, and assistance with application use. This manual was prepared and reviewed by a professor in clinical psychology and two individuals who completed their doctorates in clinical psychology.

A Group

Participants will have access to most of the modules in the application. This group will undergo a comprehensive intervention that includes various modules, such as psychoeducation, symptom coping modules, daily tasks, and imaginal exposure. However, this group will not be able to use the additional online module. They will use the application entirely by themselves, enabling us to evaluate the efficacy of independent application use.

WLC Group

Participants will complete a baseline assessment and will not receive any intervention during the five-week waiting period. At the end of the process, participants will complete the posttest and participate in follow-up assessments at one and three months. After the follow-up assessments, the WLC Group will also have access to the application.

Measures

Sociodemographic information form and trauma event details

The sociodemographic information form includes questions such as age, current city of residence, marital status, economic level, educational status, self-harming thoughts or plans, use of psychiatric medication, and whether they have received psychotherapy.

Details of traumatic events are assessed using the 17-item Life Events Checklist for DSM-5 (LEC-5; Weathers et al., 2013). A Turkish adaptation of this scale was conducted by Boysan et al. (2017). It measures exposure to 17 potentially traumatic events (such as sexual assault, earthquake, fire) and six levels of exposure (according to the type of trauma experience given in DSM-5). It also includes detailed information such as the number of times a traumatic event occurred.

Primary outcome measure

PTSD symptom severity (PCL-5; Weathers et al., 2013) is measured using a 20-item self-report scale to assesses PTSD symptoms, including reexperiencing (five items), avoidance (two items), negative changes in cognition and mood (seven items), and arousal (six items). A high score indicates that an individual's PTSD symptoms are severe. In an adaptation study in Turkey (Boysan et al., 2017), the Cronbach's alpha reliability coefficient was 0.94. The threshold value is reported as 31 points.

Secondary outcome measures

The Depression, Anxiety and Stress Symptom Severity scale (DASS-21; Lovibond and Lovibond, 1995) comprises 21 items such as depression (seven items), anxiety (seven items), and stress (seven items). A high DASS-21 score indicates significant levels of depression, anxiety, and stress. The Turkish adaptation was made by Bilgel and Bayram (2010), and Cronbach's alpha internal consistency coefficients were calculated as 0.92 for the depression subscale, 0.86 for the anxiety subscale, and 0.88 for the stress subscale, respectively.

The Improvement in Quality of Life scale (Burckhardt and Anderson, 2003) comprises 16 items. A higher score on this scale indicates a better perceived quality of life in various areas such as physical and psychological well-being, independence, social relationships, environment, and spirituality. The reliability and validity of the Turkish adaptation (Erci, 2005) were tested, and Cronbach's alpha was 0.92.

Feasibility and acceptability measures

The Feasibility and Acceptability Feedback Form measures participants' frequency of application use, modules found useful, whether the application meets their needs, and their intentions to use the application in the future. In addition, feedback will be requested on the integration of the application into daily life, and suggestions for improvement.

Feasibility measures

The Attrition Rate is the percentage of participants who leave the study during the study period. It is measured by assessing how well participants comply with the study rules and how committed they are to the study. Recruitment Rate measures the proportion of applicants who qualify and are accepted in the study. It calculates how many of the applicants are successfully included based on the study's criteria. Retention Rate refers to the percentage of participants who remain engaged throughout the study process from start to finish and indicates the study's ability to maintain participant interest and motivation. Adherence Rate to the Mobile Application includes interaction metrics such as the number of participants logged into TraumaRelief and the time they spend in the modules are analyzed to show how frequently and consistently they comply with the recommended treatment program. By determining how often and for how long the participants use the application, their adherence to the treatment is observed. Consent Rate includes general interest in the research and willingness to participate which is measured. The attractiveness and motivation for the study will be evaluated. Fidelity of Intervention Delivery: 40 % of online sessions will be evaluated by two clinical psychologists with completed doctoral degrees. With this method, we will attempt to guarantee compliance with the protocol and the quality of the intervention. In this section, it is determined whether therapists follow the steps specified in the Fidelity Checklist which comprises specific tasks that must be performed during the intervention, ensuring that it is delivered consistently to all participants.

Acceptability measures

The System Usability Scale (SUS) Score for Acceptability (Brooke, 1996) comprises 10 items. A scoring system between 0 and 100 is used to determine whether the systems are user-friendly. A higher score indicates that the system is more useful. A Turkish adaptation was carried out by Demirkol and Şeneler (2018). In a study involving 324 university students, the scale was found to be highly reliable.

Participants indicate the components they find most and least useful in the application through feasibility and acceptability feedback forms. Thus, predictions can be made regarding application acceptability. It can also provide ideas about edits to be made to the module content to improve user experience.

The average daily number of times participants interact with the app is examined. A high number of log-ins indicates the integration and acceptability of the app into daily routines.

The probability of participants recommending the app to someone they care about is measured. A high probability of recommendation indicates the acceptability of the intervention and possible approval by users.

Process evaluation

The AT + OT Group will be assessed by clinical psychologists using a standardized manual. Online interviews will be recorded of which 40 % will be randomly selected and examined to ensure that the intervention complies with protocol. These videos will be examined using the Fidelity Checklist by two evaluators with doctoral degrees in clinical psychology. This checklist was designed to determine whether the steps specified in the manual are followed. A reminder e-mail will be sent to participants a day before the online interviews.

Users can access their e-mail and code to view their data on the app. Data such as module time use and the number of applications logged into are accessible to our research team. All data collected from the participants (personal information and psychological measurements) will be transferred to Google Forms for secure storage. Access to these data is possible with a password that is available only to the project manager. Thus, maximum data security is guaranteed. Statistical analysis

Analysis strategy

Data will be analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics software (version 24.0). The alpha level for statistical significance will be set at 0.05. Chi-square tests will be used to assess baseline group differences in categorical variables, and t-tests and analysis of variance (ANOVA) for independent groups will be used for continuous variables.

Sociodemographic information form and trauma events details analysis

Demographic information and trauma exposure levels will be analyzed using descriptive statistics. Chi-square tests will be used for categorical variables with baseline differences between groups, and independent sample t-tests or ANOVA will be used for continuous variables. Group and individual differences in trauma will be assessed using the LEC-5.

Primary and secondary outcomes analysis

The primary outcome measure is PTSD symptom severity. The secondary outcome measures are improvements in depression, anxiety, stress levels, and quality of life. Changes will be examined at four time points: baseline, end of the intervention, and 1- and 3-month follow-up periods. The change in the intervention effect over time and the differences between groups will be analyzed using repeated-measures ANOVA. Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) will be used to control for baseline characteristics at the beginning of the study to determine the pure effect of the intervention. This method allows us to evaluate the effect sizes and significant differences between the groups over time with a 95 % confidence interval. Thus, an extensive analysis of the intervention effects will be done.

Feasibility and acceptability analysis

Feasibility and acceptability variables, such as participant feedback, application usage frequencies; most preferred modules; participation, recruitment, retention, adherence, and consent rates; and fidelity of intervention delivery will be analyzed using descriptive statistics. At the end of the intervention, the SUS will be used to evaluate application acceptability. The score average obtained and the usability level will be analyzed.

Missing data management and statistical significance

Missing data will be analyzed based on the assumption that the data are completely missing at random. Multiple imputation methods will not be used in the analyses. Statistical significance will be set at p < .05.

Discussion

This study reports the protocol of a pilot randomized controlled trial researching the feasibility and efficacy of TraumaRelief on PTSD symptoms. This study aims to investigate how the application affects PTSD, depression, anxiety, and quality of life. The study was designed according to the CONSORT, SPIRIT, and mERA guidelines and will improve the quality of reports and make the methodology of the study more transparent. This study will help us understand how to integrate technology-based treatments with psychotherapy and the results will provide important information regarding the use of applications for treating PTSD symptoms.

This research also provides an opportunity to evaluate the power of applications in psychotherapy. However, to understand the study strengths and weaknesses, we need to monitor and evaluate factors such as the participants' level of use of the app, the impact of the app on participants, and the long-term sustainability of the app. This app may cause accessibility problems for users who do not have constant Internet access. This can reduce the potential user-base of the app. In addition, the app may not fully consider each person's unique needs, making it difficult to offer tailored help. Use and interest in the application may decrease over time. This can negatively affect the long-term efficacy and sustainability of the application. Whether these problems will occur or not will become apparent with this pilot study.

In studies on reducing PTSD symptoms, high acceptability and feasibility have been reported (Bröcker et al., 2022; Cernvall et al., 2018). However, most of these studies were conducted in high-income countries, and there are some limitations to the efficacy of mobile applications (Bröcker et al., 2023). The fact that a customized mobile application has not been developed for a population with unique cultural and social dynamics, such as Turkey, even for the traumatic events experienced by millions of people there, highlights a significant gap in the existing literature and underscores the necessity and importance of a Turkey-specific mobile application. This application can be a vital resource for helping individuals cope with PTSD symptoms. Thus, the development and evaluation of TraumaRelief is an important step in improving the capacity to cope with the effect of traumatic events that have occurred or may occur in Turkey.

II.1
The modified GI model

Based on the GI model, which was proposed by Godfrey and Isgur in 1985 to describe relativistic meson spectra with great success, especially for low-lying mesons

\tilde{H}=\sum_{i}{({m_{i}^{2}+\mathbf{p}_{i}^{2}})}^{1/2}+\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{eff}},

(2.1)

where
m_{i}
denotes the mass of the quark (or antiquark). The mass of the strange quark (or antiquark)
m_{s}=0.377

\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{eff}}
includes the following items:

\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{eff}}=\tilde{G}_{12}+\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{cont}}+\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{tens}}+\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{so(v)}}+\tilde{S}_{12}(r)+\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{so(s)}},

(2.2)

where the individual terms are interpreted as follows:

•

\tilde{G}_{12}
is referred to as the Coulomb term, which is understood to arise from one-gluon exchange.

•

\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{cont}}
is referred to as the contact term, which is introduced to account for the short-range interaction between quarks.

•

\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{tens}}
is denoted as the tensor term, which is generated by the spin–spin interaction from one-gluon exchange.

•

\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{so(v)}}

•

\tilde{S}_{12}(r)
is denoted as the screened confinement term, which is introduced to reflect the color-screening effect.

•

\tilde{V}^{\mathrm{so(s)}}
is referred to as the scalar spin–orbit term.

\tilde{G}(r)=-\sum_{k}\frac{4\alpha_{k}}{3r}\left[\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\gamma_{k}r}e^{-x^{2}}dx\right],

We use the simple harmonic oscillator (SHO) basis to solve the spectrum of the light mesons. The SHO wave functions are given by

\begin{split}\psi_{nLM_{L}}^{SHO}(\mathbf{r})=R_{nL}^{SHO}(r,\beta)Y_{LM_{L}}(\Omega_{r}),\\
\psi_{nLM_{L}}^{SHO}(\mathbf{p})=R_{nL}^{SHO}(p,\beta)Y_{LM_{L}}(\Omega_{p}),\end{split}

(2.15)

with

\displaystyle R_{nL}^{SHO}(r,\beta)=N_{nL}\beta^{3/2}(\beta r)^{L}e^{\frac{-r^{2}\beta^{2}}{2}}L_{n-1}^{L+1/2}(\beta^{2}r^{2}),

(2.16)

\displaystyle R_{nL}^{SHO}(p,\beta)=\frac{(-1)^{(n-1)}(-i)^{L}}{\beta^{3/2}}N_{nL}e^{-\frac{p^{2}}{2\beta^{2}}}{(\frac{p}{\beta})}^{L}\times L_{n-1}^{L+1/2}(\frac{p^{2}}{\beta^{2}}),

(2.17)

and normalization factor

\displaystyle N_{nL}=\sqrt{\frac{2{(n-1)}!}{\Gamma(n+L+1/2)}},

(2.18)

where

denotes the spherical harmonic function,

the associated Laguerre polynomial and

And

\displaystyle R_{nL}(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{n_{\rm max}}C_{n}{R}_{nL}^{\rm SHO}(r,\beta),

(2.19)

\displaystyle R_{nL}(p)=\sum_{n=1}^{n_{\rm max}}C_{n}{R}_{nL}^{\rm SHO}(p,\beta)

(2.20)

are the spatial wave functions of the mesons, where

are the expansion coefficients,

2.1
).

Since then, a considerable amount of experimental data on

III.2
Study of the new state
a_{4}(2610)

Recently, COMPASS observed a broad shoulder at a mass above that of the
a_{4}(1970)
in the
KK

a_{4}

609\pm 22^{+35}_{-311}

For this newly observed

being the candidate for
a_{4}(4F)
or
a_{4}(2H)

According to the analysis of the mass spectrum (Table
3
) by the MGI model, the theoretical mass of the
a_{4}(2H)
state,
2589~\text{MeV}
, is in closer agreement with the experimental value of
2608\pm 9^{+5}_{-38}~\text{MeV}
than the predicted mass of the
a_{4}(4F)
state,
2640~\text{MeV}
.

Subsequently, we investigate the two-body strong decay behaviors based on that the
a_{4}(2610)
is the candidate for
a_{4}(4F)
or
a_{4}(2H)

5
.

,
\pi b_{1}(2240)
,
\rho a_{2}
,
\pi b_{1}(1960)

\pi\rho_{3}(1990)

,
\pi b_{1}
,
\rho\omega_{3}

\pi\rho_{3}
and

When we assign
a_{4}(2610)

\pi\rho_{2}(1D)

IV
CONCLUSION

In this work, we have systematically studied the mass spectra and OZI-allowed two-body strong decay behaviors of the
a_{4}
family, especially the newly observed
a_{4}(2610)
state.

Our study indicates that for the already established ground state of the
a_{4}
meson family, namely the
a_{4}(1970)
, the mass and decay properties predicted by the model we used are in very good agreement with experimental data.
Likewise, the
a_{4}(2255)
can be well interpreted as the first radial
excitation of the
a_{4}(1970)
, with the theoretical predictions for both its mass and width being consistent with measurements.

For the newly observed
a_{4}(2610)
, we have examined its possible assignments as the
a_{4}(4F)
and
a_{4}(2H)
states. Both the mass spectrum and the total width
support that
a_{4}(2610)
is more likely to be the
a_{4}(2H)
state, while
a_{4}(4F)
may correspond to a relatively narrow state with a width of 170 MeV.

In addition, we predict the masses and widths of the
a_{4}(3F)
and
a_{4}(1H)
states to be

\begin{cases}M(a_{4}(3F))=2466~\text{MeV}\\
\Gamma(a_{4}(3F))=250~\text{MeV}\end{cases}\begin{cases}M(a_{4}(1H))=2405~\text{MeV}\\
\Gamma(a_{4}(1H))=685~\text{MeV}\end{cases}.

We also provide their dominant decay modes. These results may serve as valuable guidance for the experimental identification and future searches of the
a_{4}(3F)
,
a_{4}(4F)
, and
a_{4}(1H)
states.

We look forward to upcoming experimental studies, which will be crucial for clarifying the nature of the newly observed light meson family members with
I^{G}J^{PC}=1^{-}4^{++}
, as well as in validating or exploring these theoretical predictions presented here.

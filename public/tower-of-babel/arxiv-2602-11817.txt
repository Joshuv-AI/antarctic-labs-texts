Third-Order Dynamical Systems for Generalized Inverse Mixed Variational Inequality Problems
Tran Van Nam Email: namtv@hcmute.edu.vn

Abstract
In this paper, we propose and analyze a third-order dynamical system for solving a generalized inverse mixed variational inequality problem in a Hilbert space H H . We establish the existence and uniqueness of the trajectories generated by the system under suitable continuity assumptions, and prove their exponential convergence to the unique solution under strong monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity conditions. Furthermore, we derive an explicit discretization of the proposed dynamical system, leading to a forward–backward algorithm with double inertial effects. We then establish the linear convergence of the generated iterates to the unique solution.



Keywords
Monotonicity, Dynamical system, Generalized inverse mixed variational inequality, Exponential convergence, Linear convergence.

47J20, 49J40, 90C30, 90C52.

1
Introduction

1.1
Background on Inverse Variational Inequalities and Dynamical Systems

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space equipped with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the associated norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let
\Omega\subset H
be a nonempty closed convex set. Given a continuous mapping
\psi:H\to H
, the classical
variational inequality problem (VIP)
consists in determining
w^{*}\in\Omega
such that

\langle\psi(w^{*}),w-w^{*}\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall w\in\Omega.

Variational inequalities provide a unifying mathematical framework for a wide spectrum of models arising in nonlinear analysis, economics, and engineering sciences
[
25
,
33
]
. Their theoretical and algorithmic developments have attracted considerable attention, particularly through projection-based iterative schemes
[
22
,
42
,
34
]
.

In various practical applications, including transportation networks and market equilibrium models, the operator
\psi
may not be directly accessible. Instead, the model is naturally formulated via an inverse mapping, leading to the
inverse variational inequality problem (IVIP)
. Let
F:H\to H
be a continuous single-valued operator. The IVIP associated with
F
and
\Omega
aims to find
w^{*}\in H
such that

F(w^{*})\in\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad\langle v-F(w^{*}),w^{*}\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in\Omega.

Recent years have witnessed growing interest in inverse variational models
[
27
,
30
,
29
,
11
]
. Nevertheless, many contemporary applications demand additional flexibility to incorporate nonsmooth terms or regularization effects. This requirement motivates the study of mixed-type formulations.

In this work, we consider a more general model, referred to as the
generalized inverse mixed variational inequality (GIMVI)
. The problem consists in finding
w^{*}\in H
such that
F(w^{*})\in\Omega
and

\langle g(w^{*}),\,v-F(w^{*})\rangle+h(v)-h\big(F(w^{*})\big)\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in\Omega,

(1)

where
F,g:H\to H
are continuous operators. This formulation strictly generalizes several existing inverse and mixed variational inequality models and offers a flexible framework for complex equilibrium problems
[
23
,
44
,
36
]
.

Several well-known models are recovered as particular instances of (
1
). When
g
is the identity operator, the problem reduces to an inverse mixed variational inequality. If additionally
h\equiv 0
, one recovers the classical inverse variational inequality problem. Conversely, if
F
is the identity operator, (
1
) becomes a mixed variational inequality, and further reduces to the classical VIP when
h\equiv 0
.

Parallel to these developments, an active line of research has explored the interplay between optimization theory and dynamical systems. Continuous-time models based on ordinary differential equations (ODEs) have proven to be powerful tools for analyzing and designing numerical algorithms for optimization
[
2
,
3
,
10
,
12
]
, variational inequalities
[
18
,
26
,
35
,
47
]
, monotone inclusions
[
1
,
5
,
6
]
, fixed point problems
[
15
,
16
]
, equilibrium problems
[
21
,
38
,
46
,
49
]
, and inverse quasi-variational inequalities
[
45
,
40
]
. The ODE perspective not only clarifies the mechanism behind acceleration schemes such as Nesterov’s method, but also guides the construction of new algorithms with improved convergence behavior (see, e.g.,
[
10
,
9
,
18
,
43
]
).

1.2
Some Historical Aspects

Among inertial-type methods, the Heavy Ball with Friction system introduced by Polyak
[
41
]
plays a fundamental role. It is described by

w^{(2)}(t)+\gamma w^{(1)}(t)+\nabla f(w(t))=0,

(2)

and was designed to accelerate gradient-based minimization of a convex differentiable function
f
. When
\nabla f
is Lipschitz continuous, it is co-coercive (see Section
2
). Extensions of (
2
) to constrained settings and co-coercive operators were developed in
[
4
]
.

Boţ and Csetnek
[
15
]
later investigated second-order systems with time-dependent damping,

w^{(2)}(t)+\gamma(t)w^{(1)}(t)+\lambda(t)B(w(t))=0,

for locating zeros of a co-coercive operator
B
, and applied their results to forward-backward dynamics of the form

w^{(2)}(t)+\gamma(t)w^{(1)}(t)+\lambda(t)\left[w(t)-\mathcal{J}_{A}(w(t)-\gamma B(w(t)))\right]=0,

(3)

where
A
is maximal monotone and
\mathcal{J}_{A}=(I+A)^{-1}
denotes its resolvent. For strongly monotone operators, exponential convergence of (
3
) was established in
[
17
]
, with rate
O(e^{-t})
under suitable parameter conditions. Further developments for merely monotone operators were proposed in
[
19
]
.

Continuous-time approaches have also been applied to GIMVI-type problems
[
48
,
24
]
. Most existing contributions focus on first-order systems such as

w^{(1)}(t)=\rho\big[P_{K}^{\gamma h}\big(F(w(t))-\gamma g(w(t))\big)-F(w(t))\big].

While first-order dynamics are well understood, their convergence speed may be limited due to the absence of inertial effects. Motivated by acceleration principles
[
13
,
14
]
, second-order systems incorporating
\ddot{w}(t)
have been proposed to enhance convergence and stability
[
31
,
20
]
. For the GIMVI problem, a second-order time-varying system was studied in
[
37
]
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} w^{(2)}(t)&+\kappa(t)w^{(1)}(t)+\rho(t)\big[F(w(t))-P_{K}^{\gamma h}\big(F(w(t))-\gamma g(w(t))\big)\big]=0,\\
w(0)&=w_{0},\quad\dot{w}(0)=v_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Higher-order dynamics have recently emerged as a promising direction. Attouch, Chbani and Riahi
[
7
,
8
]
introduced third-order systems for convex minimization, including

w^{(3)}(t)+\frac{a}{t}w^{(2)}(t)+\frac{2a-6}{t^{2}}w^{(1)}(t)+\nabla f\left(w(t)+tw^{(1)}(t)\right)=0.

Using temporal scaling and Lyapunov techniques, they obtained convergence rates of order
\frac{1}{t^{3}}
for function values, and exponential convergence under strong convexity. An improved variant (TOGES-V) in
[
8
]
achieved
\mathcal{O}(1/t^{3})
for
f(x(t))-\inf_{H}f
.

For generalized monotone inclusion problems, third-order dynamics were investigated in
[
39
]
:

w^{(3)}(t)+\alpha_{2}w^{(2)}(t)+\alpha_{1}w^{(1)}(t)+\alpha_{0}\left[w(t)-\mathcal{J}_{A}(w(t)-\gamma B(w(t)))\right]=0,

where existence, uniqueness, and exponential convergence were established.

1.3
Our Contributions

Motivated by the above developments, we introduce for the first time a third-order dynamical system tailored to the generalized inverse mixed variational inequality problem (
1
). The analysis is carried out in both continuous and discrete frameworks.

Our results show that, under appropriate assumptions, the trajectories converge exponentially with rate
O\left(e^{-\varepsilon t}\right)
for some
\varepsilon>1
(in particular
\varepsilon=2
), which improves upon the classical second-order rate obtained in
[
17
]
. The explicit time discretization leads to a forward-backward algorithm incorporating double inertial effects and allowing a broader stepsize range.

The convergence analysis relies entirely on Lyapunov energy methods, in contrast to the temporal scaling techniques used in
[
7
,
8
]
. The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:

•

We propose a third-order dynamical system for the generalized inverse mixed variational inequality problem.

•

We prove existence and uniqueness of its trajectories.

•

We establish exponential convergence of the continuous-time trajectories to the unique solution.

•

We derive an explicit discretization and prove linear convergence of the associated forward-backward algorithm with double inertial effects.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
collects preliminary notions and auxiliary results. Section
3
introduces the proposed third-order dynamical system and proves its exponential convergence to the solution of (
1
). In Section
4
, we study its explicit discretization and analyze the corresponding forward-backward algorithm.

Definitions and Preliminaries

2.1
Background on Monotone Operators and Generalized Projection Operators

In this section, we present several definitions and auxiliary results that will be needed in the subsequent analysis.

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and associated norm
\|\cdot\|
.
We also consider the Cartesian product space
H^{3}:=H\times H\times H
, endowed with the inner product and norm introduced in
[
39
]
. For any
(x,y,z),(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*})\in H^{3}
, define

\langle(x,y,z),(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*})\rangle:=\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle y,y^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle,

and

\|(x,y,z)\|:=\sqrt{\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}+\|z\|^{2}}.

We next recall several standard concepts concerning operator properties.

Definition 2.1
.

[
32
]

Let
\Omega
be a nonempty subset of
H
. An operator
F:H\to H
is said to be

(i)

monotone
on
\Omega
if

\langle F(w)-F(v),w-v\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall\,w,v\in\Omega;

(ii)

strongly monotone
on
\Omega
with modulus
\lambda>0
if

\langle F(w)-F(v),w-v\rangle\geq\lambda\|w-v\|^{2},\quad\forall\,w,v\in\Omega.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a nonempty closed convex subset of
H
, and let
F,g:H\to H
be two operators.
The pair
(F,g)
is called
\zeta
-strongly coupled monotone
on
\Omega
if there exists
\zeta>0
such that

\langle F(w)-F(v),g(w)-g(v)\rangle\geq\zeta\|w-v\|^{2},\quad\forall\,w,v\in\Omega.

It is clear that when
g
coincides with the identity operator, this notion reduces to the classical definition of strong monotonicity.

Definition 2.3
.

[
32
]

An operator
F:H\to H
is said to be
\eta
-Lipschitz continuous
on
\Omega
if there exists
\eta\geq 0
such that

\|F(w)-F(v)\|\leq\eta\|w-v\|,\quad\forall\,w,v\in\Omega.

Assume that
\Omega
contains at least two distinct points. Suppose that
F
and
g
are Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
with constants
\eta
and
\beta
, respectively, and that
(F,g)
is
\zeta
-strongly coupled monotone on
\Omega
. Then one necessarily has

\zeta\leq\eta\,\beta.

Similarly, if
F
is
\eta
-Lipschitz continuous on
H
and
\lambda
-strongly monotone, then

\lambda\leq\eta.

We will also make use of the generalized
h
-projection operator (see
[
50
]
), defined by

P_{\Omega}^{\gamma h}(w):=\arg\min_{v\in\Omega}\left\{\gamma h(v)+\frac{1}{2}\|w-v\|^{2}\right\},

where
\Omega
is a nonempty closed convex subset of
H
,

h:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous function, and
\gamma>0
. In convex analysis, this operator is commonly referred to as the
proximal operator
.

In the particular case where
h
is the indicator function of
\Omega
, namely

h(w)=\begin{cases}0,&w\in\Omega,\\
+\infty,&w\notin\Omega,\end{cases}

the operator
P_{\Omega}^{\gamma h}
coincides with the standard metric projection onto
\Omega
.

The following lemma collects several fundamental properties of the generalized
h
-projection.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
33
]

Let
\Omega
be a nonempty closed convex subset of
H
. Then:

(i)

\langle w-P_{\Omega}^{\gamma h}(w),P_{\Omega}^{\gamma h}(w)-v\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall\,w\in H,\ v\in\Omega;

(ii)

P_{\Omega}^{\gamma h}
is nonexpansive, that is,

\|P_{\Omega}^{\gamma h}(w)-P_{\Omega}^{\gamma h}(v)\|\leq\|w-v\|,\quad\forall\,w,v\in H;

(iii)

For
w\in H
,
w^{*}=P_{\Omega}^{\gamma h}(w)
if and only if

\langle w^{*}-w,v-w^{*}\rangle+\gamma h(v)-\gamma h(w^{*})\geq 0,\quad\forall\,v\in\Omega.

Throughout the paper, we repeatedly use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:
for all
w,v\in H
,

\langle w,v\rangle\leq\|w\|\,\|v\|.

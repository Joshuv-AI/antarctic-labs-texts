Scalar waves from unbound orbits in a TS spacetime: PN reconstruction of the field and radiation losses in a self-force approach
Giorgio Di Russo 1 , Massimo Bianchi 2 , Donato Bini 3

Abstract
We analyze scalar wave emission from unbound orbits in a Topological Star spacetime. Our study uses a self-force approach and leads to a Post-Newtonian reconstruction of the field along the orbit, both in the time domain and in the frequency domain. We also compute leading-order radiation losses, namely energy and angular momentum.

Writing the solution to the radial equation according to these schemes can be anyway performed in PN sense, i.e., limiting the solution at some, specified PN order.
Other complications arise when trying to reconstruct the perturbing field, because one has to subtract the contribution of the singular field (associated with the particle’s world line where its mass-energy distribution is concentrated) before summing up over the harmonics. Furthermore, to reach a high-PN result for the reconstructed perturbing field or for the evaluation of any associated gauge-invariant quantity one has to combine these methods, e.g., PN and MST, whereas the analytic reconstruction of the singular field can be eventually accomplished by using the JWKB approach.

IV.1
A warming up exercise: spatial straight line motion

To explore a simplified situation we limit our considerations to spatial straight line motion (but spacetime curved motion, i.e., accelerated motion).
The latter choice has advantages (it is simple) and disadvantages (it is clearly non-geodetic, and approaches the geodetic behavior only asymptotically). Actually, the problem of radiation emission in this context has been considered long ago by Ruffini and Wheeler

V
Scalar self force

In
D=4
the definition of scalar self force along the source’s world line reads

F_{\alpha}(\tau)=qP(u)_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\psi\bigg|_{x^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha}(\tau)}\,,

(193)

where
P(u)_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}+u_{\alpha}u^{\beta}

projects orthogonally to
u^{\alpha}
, the four velocity of the source.
In general, we will distinguish left/right force components,
F_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\tau)
, because the field is continuous across the source world line but not its derivatives.

The field at a generic spacetime point has been given in Eq. (
80
). Thanks to spherical symmetry, both geodetic and non-geodetic motion can be taken to be on the equatorial plane
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
so that
F_{\theta}=0
. Moreover, due to the orthogonality condition
F_{\alpha}u^{\alpha}=0
, only
F_{t}
and
F_{\phi}
should be computed
since

\displaystyle F_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{u^{t}}{u^{r}}\left(F_{t}+\frac{u^{\phi}}{u^{t}}F_{\phi}\right)

(194)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{dr_{p}}{dt}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{t}+\frac{d\phi_{p}}{dt}F_{\phi}\right)\,,

with

\displaystyle F_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(\partial_{t}\psi+u_{t}u^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\psi)\,,

\displaystyle F_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(\partial_{\phi}\psi+u_{\phi}u^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\psi)\,,

(195)

where now we have to consider left and right parts of the field separately (since derivatives may be discontinuous along the world line)

\psi^{\pm}(t,r,\theta,\phi)=\sum_{lm}Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}R_{l{}\omega}^{\pm}(r)\,,

(196)

with

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi^{\pm}(t,r,\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{lm}Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}(-i\omega)R_{l{}\omega}^{\pm}(r)\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{\phi}\psi^{\pm}(t,r,\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{lm}(im)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}R_{l{}\omega}^{\pm}(r)\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\psi^{\pm}(t,r,\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{lm}Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}\partial_{r}R_{l{}\omega}^{\pm}(r)\,,

(197)

and then define a corresponding left and right force,
F_{\alpha}^{\pm}
, evaluated along the orbit, using

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi^{\pm}(t,r,\theta,\phi)\big|_{\rm orb}\,,\quad\partial_{\phi}\psi^{\pm}(t,r,\theta,\phi)\big|_{\rm orb}\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\psi^{\pm}(t,r,\theta,\phi)\big|_{\rm orb}\,.

(198)

21
), that is

\displaystyle R_{lm\omega}^{\pm}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi qY^{*}_{lm}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\int dt^{\prime}G_{l{}\omega}^{\pm}(r,r_{p}(t^{\prime}))\times

(199)

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{p}(t^{\prime}))}e^{i(\omega t^{\prime}-m\phi_{p}(t^{\prime}))}}{u^{t}(t^{\prime})}\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R_{\rm in}^{l{}\omega}(r^{\prime})R_{\rm up}^{l{}\omega}(r)}{W_{l{}\omega}}\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R_{\rm in}^{l{}\omega}(r)R_{\rm up}^{l{}\omega}(r^{\prime})}{W_{l{}\omega}}\,.

(200)

We find it convenient to integrate over
\omega
first and then over

. Therefore, for example

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi q\sum_{lm}|Y_{lm}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)|^{2}\int dt^{\prime}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}(-i\omega)G_{l{}\omega}^{\pm}(r_{p}(t),r_{p}(t^{\prime}))\frac{\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{p}(t^{\prime}))}e^{im(\phi_{p}(t)-\phi_{p}(t^{\prime}))}}{u^{t}(t^{\prime})}

(202)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{\phi}\psi^{\pm}(t,r,\theta,\phi)\bigg|_{\rm orb}

\displaystyle-4\pi q\sum_{lm}|Y_{lm}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)|^{2}(im)\int dt^{\prime}\,\frac{\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{p}(t^{\prime}))}e^{im(\phi_{p}(t)-\phi_{p}(t^{\prime}))}}{u^{t}(t^{\prime})}\,{\rm FT}[G_{l{}\omega}^{\pm}(r_{p}(t),r_{p}(t^{\prime}))]\,,

\displaystyle-4\pi q\sum_{lm}|Y_{lm}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)|^{2}\int dt^{\prime}\,\frac{\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{p}(t^{\prime}))}e^{im(\phi_{p}(t)-\phi_{p}(t^{\prime}))}}{u^{t}(t^{\prime})}\,{\rm FT}[\partial_{r}G_{l{}\omega}^{\pm}(r,r_{p}(t^{\prime}))|_{r=r_{p}(t)}]\,.

Hadamard regularization (also called Hadamard finite part or Hadamard’s partie finie) is a method of regularizing divergent integrals by dropping some divergent terms and keeping the finite part. The definition is the following

\displaystyle{\rm Pf}_{T}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{dv}{v}g(v)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}\frac{dv}{v}(g(v)-g(0))

(215)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}\frac{dv}{v}g(v)+g(0)\ln\frac{a}{T}\,,\qquad

where
T
is a “scale” and
a
is an arbitrary constant value.
Practically, the two divergent integrals

g(0)\int_{0}^{a}\frac{dv}{v}\,,\qquad g(0)\int_{T}^{\infty}\frac{dv}{v}\,,

(216)

(the first diverging in
v\to 0
and the second in
v\to\infty
)
are excluded.

The definition implies that the Pf integral does not depend on
a
, in the sense that

\frac{d}{da}\left[{\rm Pf}_{T}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{dv}{v}g(v)\right]=0\,.

(217)

Integral equations containing Hadamard finite part integrals (with the integrand unknown) are termed
hypersingular integral equations
.
As an example consider the integral (see e.g., Eq. 5.8 of Ref.
[
50
]
)

(218)

\omega

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx}{x}[\cos(x)-1]}_{-\gamma+{\rm Ci}(1)}

(219)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(x)}{x}dx}_{-{\rm Ci}(1)}-\ln(|\omega|T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma-\ln(|\omega|T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\ln(e^{\gamma}|\omega|T)\,.

]

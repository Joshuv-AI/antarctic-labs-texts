On Dual Algebras of Hopf Algebroids
Jingbang Guo

Abstract
We study the dual algebras of (discrete) Hopf algebroids. In particular, we understand comodules over a Hopf algebroid as (discrete) modules over its dual algebra.



1

Introduction

This elementary article attempts to understand comodules over a Hopf algebroid as modules over the corresponding dual algebra.

Remark 1.1
.

A Hopf algebroid is a pair of commutative rings
(A,\Gamma)
, together with ring homomorphisms
\eta_{L},\eta_{R}:A\rightarrow\Gamma
,
\epsilon:\Gamma\rightarrow A
,
\Delta:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma
, and
c:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma
, so that the pair of functors
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CAlg}}(A,-),\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CAlg}}(\Gamma,-):\mathrm{CAlg}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sets}
together form a functor
\mathrm{CAlg}\rightarrow\textrm{Groupoids}
, such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CAlg}}(A,-)
is the functor of objects, and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CAlg}}(\Gamma,-)
is the functor of morphisms, and
\eta_{L},\eta_{R}
correspond to the source and target,
\epsilon
corresponds to the identity,
\Delta
corresponds to the composition of morphisms, and finally,
c
corresponds to the inversion of morphisms. For more details on Hopf algebroids, see
[
15
, Appendix A1]
.

Remark 1.2
.

To each Hopf algebroid there is the associated stack: heuristically, a pair of rings
(A,\Gamma)
together with the structure morphisms give rise to a geometric object
\mathrm{Coeq}(\textbf{Spec}(\Gamma)\rightrightarrows\textbf{Spec}(A))
, where the two arrows are induced by
\eta_{L}
and
\eta_{R}
. Conversely, for a suitable stack
\mathfrak{X}
with a suitable affine cover
\mathrm{Spec}(A)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
(see
[
14
, Definition 6]
), there would be a pullback square of the form

\textstyle{\textbf{Spec}(\Gamma)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{Spec}(\eta_{R})}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{Spec}(\eta_{L})}
\textstyle{\textbf{Spec}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{e}
\textstyle{\textbf{Spec}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{e}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{X},}

making
(A,\Gamma)
a Hopf algebroid. In fact, the
2
-category of flat Hopf algebroids and the
2
-category of rigidified algebraic stacks are equivalent to each other, see
[
14
, Theorem 8]
.

Remark 1.3
.

Let
(A,\Gamma)
be a flat Hopf algebroid (in the sense that
\eta_{L}:A\rightarrow\Gamma
is flat), and
\mathrm{Spec}(A)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
the associated rigidified algebraic stack. A quasi-coherent sheaf on

can then be interpreted as an

-module with descent data, that is to say, a quasi-coherent sheaf on
\mathfrak{X}
can be interpreted as a (right)
\Gamma
-comodule: a
\Gamma
-comodule is an
A
-module
M
equipped with a comodule structure map
\psi_{M}:M\rightarrow M\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma
, which is
A
-linear with respect to the
\eta_{R}
-structure of
\Gamma
, and satisfies co-associativity and co-unitarity (See
[
15
, Definition A1.1.2]
). In fact, there is an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{X})\simeq\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma},

through which quasi-coherent sheaf cohomology corresponds to comodule cohomology. See
[

,
\lx@sectionsign
3.4]
.

Let
(A,\Gamma)
be a Hopf algebroid, and regard
\Gamma
as an
A
-module through
\eta_{L}:A\rightarrow\Gamma
. The dual algebra of
(A,\Gamma)
is
\Gamma^{\vee}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{A,\eta_{L}}(\Gamma,A)
.

Remark 1.7
.

The algebra structure of
\Gamma^{\vee}
is determined by the co-multiplication
\Delta:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma
: given
D_{1},D_{2}\in\Gamma^{\vee}
, that is, given
A,\eta_{L}
-linear map
D_{1},D_{2}:\Gamma\rightarrow A
,
D_{2}\circ D_{1}
is then the following composite

D_{2}\circ D_{1}:\Gamma\xrightarrow{\Delta}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{1}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{D_{2}}A.

Also, the multiplicative unit
1\in\Gamma^{\vee}
is determined as the co-unit
\epsilon:\Gamma\rightarrow A
, which induces a ring homomorphism
\epsilon^{\vee}:A\rightarrow\Gamma^{\vee}
. Note that in general the algebra
\Gamma^{\vee}
is not commutative.

Example 1.8
.

Consider the Hopf algebroid
(A,\Gamma)=(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]],\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q_{0}-1,q_{1}-1]]\{\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{[p]_{q_{0}}}\}_{\delta})
, together with the following ring homomorphism

m_{q}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q_{0}-1,q_{1}-1]]\{\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{[p]_{q_{0}}}\}_{\delta}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]],\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988ptq_{0}\mapsto q,q_{1}\mapsto q^{1+p},

which is well-defined by the universal property of prismatic envelopes (see
[
1
, Proposition 3.13]
). In particular,
m_{q}\in\Gamma^{\vee}
. Moreover, it is not difficult to check that, for any
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q_{0}-1,q_{1}-1]]\{\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{[p]_{q_{0}}}\}
,
m_{q}(f)-f\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]]
is divisible by
q-1
, and there is an
A,\eta_{L}
-linear map
\nabla_{q}:=\frac{1}{q(q-1)}\circ(m_{q}-1):\Gamma\rightarrow A
, or an element
\nabla_{q}\in\Gamma^{\vee}
. It turns out that, up to topology,
\Gamma^{\vee}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]]
-algebra (through
\epsilon^{\vee}:A\rightarrow\Gamma^{\vee}
) generated by
\nabla_{q}
, with the relation that
\nabla_{q}\circ q=q^{1+p}\circ\nabla_{q}+[p]_{q}
, or

\nabla_{q}\circ f(q)=f(q^{1+p})\circ\nabla_{q}+\frac{f(q^{1+p})-f(q)}{q(q-1)},\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988ptf(q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]].

Remark 1.9
.

Through
\eta_{L}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]]\xrightarrow{q\mapsto q_{0}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q_{0}-1,q_{1}-1]]\{\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{[p]_{q_{0}}}\}_{\delta}

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q_{0}-1,q_{1}-1]]\{\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{[p]_{q_{0}}}\}_{\delta}
is a topologically free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]]
-module with a basis
\{\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}(\delta^{i}(\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{[p]_{q_{0}}}))^{n_{i}}\}
, where
0\leq n_{i}<p
and all but finitely many

\partial_{q}\in\Gamma^{\vee}
be the dual basis of
\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{[p]_{q_{0}}}\in\Gamma
, then
\Gamma^{\vee}
can also be generated by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]]
and
\partial_{q}
; however, it is far from immediate to determine the multiplicative relations between a general

and
\partial_{q}
.

Remark 1.10
.

According to the canonical isomorphism (see
[

, Definition A1.2.1]
)

\Gamma^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{A,\eta_{L}}(\Gamma,A)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{LCoMod}_{\Gamma}}(\Gamma,\Gamma),

the algebra structure of

corresponds to the endomorphism algebra structure of

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}

, then

\Gamma^{\vee}\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{LCoMod}_{\Gamma}}(\Gamma,\Gamma)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{X})}(e_{*}e^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}},e_{*}e^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}).

Example 1.11
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring, and consider the Hopf algebroid
(A,A\otimes{Z}A)
with the canonical structure morphisms. By definition, it dual algebra is

\mathrm{Hom}_{A,\eta_{L}}(A\otimes{Z}A,A)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}{Z}(A,A),

and it is routine to check that, the algebra structure is exactly that of the endomorphism algebra.

Remark 1.12
.

While the Hopf algebroid
(A,\Gamma)
actually expresses the co-monadicity of the affine cover
e:\mathrm{Spec}(A)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
, the dual algebra
\Gamma^{\vee}
should express the monadicity. Heuristically, suppose that the functors
e_{*},e^{*},e_{!},e^{!}
are all provided, then a quasi-coherent sheaf on
\mathfrak{X}
can be understood as a
e^{*}e_{*}
-comodule in
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
, or a
e^{!}e_{!}
-module in
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
.

Example 1.13
.

Let
n
be an integer, and consider the product algebra
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, together with the very trivial morphism
e:\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
. The identification
\mathrm{Shv}(\{1,2,\ldots,n\};\mathrm{Mod}{Z})\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
suggests that, we should have functors
e^{*}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\otimes{Z}-:\mathrm{Mod}{Z}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
,
e_{*}=e_{!}:\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}{Z}
which is the functor of underlying abelian groups, and
e^{!}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},-):\mathrm{Mod}{Z}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
. In this case, we have
e^{!}(e_{!}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}))=\mathrm{Hom}{Z}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n})=M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, which is also the dual algebra of the Hopf algebroid
(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{Z}^{n})
; the Morita equivalence says that
\mathrm{Mod}{Z}\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})}
.

Let
M
be a (right)
\Gamma
-comodule, that is,
M
is an
A
-module equipped with a comodule structure map
\psi_{M}:M\rightarrow M\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma

\Gamma^{\vee}
acts on

,

M\xrightarrow{\psi_{M}}M\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{M}\otimes D}M.

Remark 1.14
.

The canonical map
\Gamma\rightarrow(\Gamma^{\vee})^{\vee}
induces the following composite

M\xrightarrow{\psi_{M}}M\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\rightarrow M\otimes_{A}(\Gamma^{\vee})^{\vee},

and by adjunction it further induces a morphism
\Gamma^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}{Z}(M,M)
, which is in fact a ring homomorphism.

Remark 1.15
.

The module
A
is naturally equipped with a right comodule structure through
\eta_{R}:A\rightarrow\Gamma\simeq A\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma
, and therefore there is a canonial ring homomorphism
\Gamma^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}{Z}(A,A)
.

Remark 1.17
.

It is expected that, by regarding a
\Gamma
-comodule as a
\Gamma^{\vee}
-module, no essential information would be lost. However, in order to recover comodule structures from module structures, the dual algebra
\Gamma^{\vee}
, even though
A
and
\Gamma
are discrete rings, must be treated as a topological algebra:
\Gamma^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{A,\eta_{L}}(\Gamma,A)
should be equipped with the compact-open topology. A first observation is that, if
\Gamma
is a free
A
-module through
\eta_{L}:A\rightarrow\Gamma
, then the canonical map
\Gamma\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Gamma^{\vee},A)
is an isomorphism, provided that
\Gamma^{\vee}
is equipped with the compact-open topology and
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Gamma^{\vee},A)
, which is also equipped with the compact-open topology, is the group of continuous homomorphisms.

Theorem 1.18

(Theorem
2.22
)
.

Assume that, through the left unit map
\eta_{L}:A\rightarrow\Gamma
,
\Gamma
is a free
A
-module with a countable basis. Then, by regarding comodules as modules, there is an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Mod}^{d}_{\Gamma^{\vee}},

where
\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma}
is the category of right
\Gamma
-comodules and
\mathrm{Mod}^{d}_{\Gamma^{\vee}}
is the category of discrete
\Gamma^{\vee}
-modules (with continuous
\Gamma^{\vee}
-actions).

Remark 1.20
.

The category
\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma}
is symmetric monoidal, and under the equivalence of categories
\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma}\simeq\mathrm{Mod}^{d}_{\Gamma^{\vee}}
, this symmetric monoidal structure should be reflected to certain co-multiplcation of the algebra
\Gamma^{\vee}
. But such a co-multiplication would require a notion of tensor product of general topological abelian groups, for example, the solid tensor of solid abelian groups.

Remark 1.21
.

Let
f=(f_{o},f_{m}):(A,\Gamma)\rightarrow(B,\Sigma)
be a morphism of Hopf algebroids. There is the functor
f^{*}:\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Sigma}
, which, in view of Remark
1.3
, corresponds to the pullback of quasi-coherent sheaves on the associated stacks. However, this functoriality is not immediate from the perspective of dual algebras: there is no immediate functoriality for the passage from Hopf algebroids to their dual algebras. Yet the morphism
f
factors as

(A,\Gamma)\rightarrow(B,B\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A}B)\rightarrow(B,\Gamma),

and sometimes (see
[
14
, Theorem 13]
) the morphism
(A,\Gamma)\rightarrow(B,B\otimes_{f_{o},A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f_{o}}B)
would induce an equivalence of the categories of comodules, and for
(B,B\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A}B)\rightarrow(B,\Gamma)
there can be an induced morphism of dual algebras.

Example 1.22
.

Resume Example
1.8
. The Hopf algebroid
(A,\Gamma)=(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]],\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q_{0}-1,q_{1}-1]]\{\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{[p]_{q_{0}}}\}_{\delta})
admits a Frobenius endomorphism

\varphi:(A,\Gamma)\rightarrow(A,\Gamma),

where
\varphi(q)=q^{p}
,
\varphi(q_{0})=q_{o}^{p}
, and
\varphi(q_{1})=q_{1}^{p}

\Gamma_{\varphi}:=A\otimes_{\varphi,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,\varphi}A
, then

and up to

It is expected that, under the equivalence of categories

to a category of
\Gamma^{\vee}

\Gamma^{\vee}
-modules.

Remark 1.24

Suppose that, in the Hopf algebroid

Remark 1.25
.

For a profinite ring
\Lambda

\Lambda

Remark 1.26
.

Since the dual algebra
\Gamma^{\vee}
, in general, is not commutative, the notion of left

-modules and that of right
\Gamma^{\vee}
-modules are different. However, in some cases the left modules and right modules are related. For example, if

\mathrm{Mod}_{\Gamma^{\vee}}
and

should be related with the dualizing object
\mathrm{RHom}_{\Gamma^{\vee}}(A,\Gamma^{\vee})
, which again should make sense provided the theory of solid abelian groups.

It would be helpful to end this introduction by listing the basic structure of the algebra
\Gamma^{\vee}
.

•

The multiplicative unit of
\Gamma^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Gamma,A)
is given by the co-unit
\epsilon:\Gamma\rightarrow A
of the Hopf algebroid
(A,\Gamma)
.

•

Also, the co-unit
\epsilon
induces a morphism of algebras
\epsilon^{\vee}:A\rightarrow\Gamma^{\vee}
, and
\Gamma^{\vee}
is thus an
A
-algebra. However, in general
\epsilon^{\vee}(A)
is not in the center of
\Gamma^{\vee}
:
\epsilon^{\vee}(A)
is in the center of
\Gamma^{\vee}
if and only if
\eta_{L}=\eta_{R}
.

•

The
A
-module structure on
\Gamma^{\vee}
from
\eta_{L}
is the left action of
A
on
\Gamma^{\vee}
through
\epsilon^{\vee}
, and the
A
-module structure on
\Gamma^{\vee}
from
\eta_{R}
is the right action of
A
on
\Gamma^{\vee}
through
\epsilon^{\vee}
.

•

The left unit
\eta_{L}:A\rightarrow\Gamma
induces a morphism of
A
-modules
\eta_{L}^{\vee}:\Gamma^{\vee}\rightarrow A
. This morphism is generally not
A
-linear with respect to the right
A
-module structure on
\Gamma^{\vee}
.

•

There is a canonical continuous morphism of
A
-algebras
\Gamma^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A,A)
. In particular,
A
is a left
\Gamma^{\vee}
-module. Moreover,
\eta_{L}^{\vee}:\Gamma^{\vee}\rightarrow A
is a morphism of left
\Gamma^{\vee}
-modules.

We might call
\Gamma^{\vee}
(or the Hopf algebroid
(A,\Gamma)
) restrictive if the canonical morphism
\Gamma^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}{Z}(A,A)
is a monomorphism, and in this case
\Gamma^{\vee}
is a suitable algebra between
A=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(A,A)
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A,A)
.

The Dual Algebras of Hopf algebroids

In this section we elaborate the ideas suggested in Section
1
.

Convention 2.1
.

In this section, let
(A,\Gamma)
be a Hopf algebroid where
A
is a commutative ring and
\Gamma
, through the left unit map
\eta_{L}:A\rightarrow\Gamma
, is a free
A
-module with a countable basis, and we will refer to such a Hopf algebroid as a free Hopf algebroid. Let
\eta_{R}:A\rightarrow\Gamma
denote the right unit map,
\Delta:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma
the co-multiplication, and
\epsilon:\Gamma\rightarrow A
the co-unit, and
c:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma
the conjugation.

By a
\Gamma
-comodule it means a right
\Gamma
-comodule, that is, an
A
-module
M
equipped with a map

\psi_{M}:M\rightarrow M\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma,

which is
A
-linear with respect to the
\eta_{R}
-linear structure of
\Gamma
, and is co-associative and co-unitary; let
\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma}
denote the category of right
\Gamma
-comodules.

Finally, almost all rings and modules considered are equipped with a topology, although sometimes the discrete topology; and
\mathrm{Hom}(-,-)
is taken to be the group of continuous homomorphisms, and it is further equipped with the compact-open topology unless otherwise specified. For the group of not necessarily continuous homomorphisms, we use the notation
\mathrm{Hom}^{un}(-,-)
.

We would like to study the linear dual
\mathrm{Hom}_{A,\eta_{L}}(\Gamma,A)
, which turns out to be a topological ring and encodes almost all of the essential information associated to the Hopf algebroid
(A,\Gamma)
.

Definition 2.2
.

The dual algebra of the Hopf algebroid
(A,\Gamma)
is defined to be

\Gamma^{\vee}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{A,\eta_{L}}(\Gamma,A),

where the algebra structure is determined as follows: for
D_{1},D_{2}\in\Gamma^{\vee}
,
D_{2}\circ D_{1}\in\Gamma^{\vee}
is the composite

D_{2}\circ D_{1}:\Gamma\xrightarrow{\Delta}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A\eta_{L}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{1}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{D_{2}}A,

and the unit of
\Gamma^{\vee}
is the co-unit of the Hopf algebroid
\epsilon:\Gamma\rightarrow A
.

Moreover,
\Gamma^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{A,\eta_{L}}(\Gamma,A)
is equipped with the compact-open topology where
A
is regarded as a discrete topological ring, and
\Gamma,A
are discrete topological
A
-modules.

The dual algebra

, defined in Definition

, is indeed an algebra.

Lemma 2.4
.

The dual algebra
\Gamma^{\vee}
, defined in Definition
2.2
, is indeed a topological ring.

Proof

By definition, since both
\Gamma
and
A
is equipped with the discrete topology,
the compact-open topology of
\Gamma^{\vee}
is generated by subsets of the form
B(K,U)=\{D\in\Gamma^{\vee}|D(K)\subset U\}
, where
K
is a finite subset of
\Gamma
, and
U
is a subset of
A
. Therefore, it suffices to consider finite intersections of sets of the form
B(\gamma,a)=\{D\in\Gamma^{\vee}|D(\gamma)=\{a\}\}
, where
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
a\in A
.

First we check that the addition of
\Gamma^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{A,\eta_{L}}(\Gamma,A)
is continuous. Given
D_{1},D_{2}\in\Gamma^{\vee}
with
D_{1}+D_{2}\in B(\gamma,a)
(strictly speaking we shall consider a finite intersection of sets of the form
B(\gamma,a)
, but the argument is essentially the same, up to a possibly finite intersection), where
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
a\in A
, in particular
a=D_{1}(\gamma)+D_{2}(\gamma)
. Then
B(\gamma,D_{1}(\gamma))
is a neighborhood of
D_{1}
, and
B(\gamma,D_{2}(\gamma))
is a neighborhood of
D_{2}
, and
B(\gamma,D_{1}(\gamma))+B(\gamma,D_{2}(\Gamma))\subset B(\gamma,a)
.

Then we check that the multiplication of
\Gamma^{\vee}
is continuous. Given
D_{1},D_{2}\in\Gamma^{\vee}
with
D_{2}\circ D_{1}\in B(\gamma,a)
, where
\gamma\subset\Gamma
and
a\in A
. Recall that
D_{2}\circ D_{1}
is the following composite

\Gamma\xrightarrow{\Delta}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{1}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{D_{2}}A;

write
\Delta(\gamma)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\gamma_{i}^{\prime}\otimes\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma
, then

(\mathrm{id}_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{1})(\Delta(\Gamma))=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\gamma_{i}^{\prime}\eta_{R}(D_{1}(\gamma_{i})):=\widetilde{\gamma},

and
D_{2}(\widetilde{\gamma})=a
. Thus
\cap_{1\leq i\leq k}B(\gamma_{i},D_{1}(\gamma_{i}))
is a neighborhood of
D_{1}
, and
B(\widetilde{\gamma},a)
is a neighborhood of
D_{2}
, and
B(\widetilde{\gamma},a)\circ(\cap_{1\leq i\leq k}B(\gamma_{i},D_{1}(\gamma_{i})))\subset B(\gamma,a)
.

\square

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
M
be a free
A
-module, and choose a basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
M
as a free
A
-module. Then the compact-open topology of
M^{\vee}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M,A)
coincides with the product topology of
M^{\vee}\simeq\prod_{i\in I}Ae_{i}^{\vee}
, where each
Ae_{i}^{\vee}
is equipped with the discrete topology.

Proof

Since
M
and
A
are discrete, the compact-open topology of
M^{\vee}
is generated by subsets of the form
B(m,a)=\{f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M,A)|f(m)=a\}
, where
m\in M
,
a\in A
. Similarly, the product topology of
M^{\vee}\simeq\prod_{i\in I}Ae_{i}^{\vee}
is generated by subsets of the form
\{a_{i}e_{i}^{\vee}\}\times\prod_{j\neq i}Ae_{j}^{\vee}
. It is immediate to see that,
\{a_{i}e_{i}^{\vee}\}\times\prod_{j\neq i}Ae_{j}^{\vee}=B(e_{i},a)
; also, if
m=\sum_{i}m_{i}e_{i}
, then
B(m,a)
is a union of subsets of the form
\cap_{i,m_{i}\neq 0}B(e_{i},a_{i})
where
\sum_{i}m_{i}a_{i}=a
. Therefore the compact-open topology of
M^{\vee}
coincides with the product topology of
M^{\vee}\simeq\prod_{i\in I}Ae_{i}^{\vee}
.

\square

Example 2.6
.

Consider the Hopf algebra (
\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle t\rangle
), where the underlying ring of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle t\rangle
is the divided power polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
generated by the indeterminate

, and the underlying co-multiplication is given by the ring homomorphism
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle t\rangle\xrightarrow{t\mapsto t\otimes 1+1\otimes t}\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle t\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle t\rangle

Then

which is the dual basis of

, and it is also equipped with the co-multiplication
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t^{\vee}]]\xrightarrow{t^{\vee}\mapsto t^{\vee}\otimes 1+1\otimes t^{\vee}}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t^{\vee}]]\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t^{\vee}]]

The underlying ring of

By taking the

.

.

The right unit map

Lemma 2.14
.

Let

be a free

an

The Functoriality

In this section we discuss the functoriality for the construction of dual algebras of Hopf algebroids.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
f=(f_{o},f_{m}):(A,\Gamma)\rightarrow(B,\Sigma)
be a morphism of Hopf algebroids. There is the functor
f^{*}:\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Sigma}
, which sends a
\Gamma
-comodule
M
, with the comodule structure
M\xrightarrow{\psi_{M}}M\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma
, to a
\Sigma
-comodule
M\otimes_{A}B
, with the comodule structure map
M\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow(M\otimes_{A}B)\otimes_{B,\eta_{R}}\Sigma
induced from the following composite:

M\xrightarrow{\psi_{M}}M\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{M}\otimes g}M\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Sigma\simeq(M\otimes_{A}B)\otimes_{B,\eta_{R}}\Sigma.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
f=(f_{o},f_{m}):(A,\Gamma)\rightarrow(B,\Sigma)
be a morphism of Hopf algebroids. In general this morphism does not immediately induce a morphism
\Sigma^{\vee}\rightarrow\Gamma^{\vee}
, although, with the perspective of Proposition
2.22
, there should always be a functor
\mathrm{Mod}^{d}_{\Gamma^{\vee}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}^{d}_{\Sigma^{\vee}}
corresponding to
\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Sigma}
. However, it is still possible to understand this functoriality through a morphism of dual algebras, by choosing a suitable Hopf algebroid “equivalent to
(A,\Gamma)
”.

Consider the following morphisms

\alpha:A\xrightarrow{\eta_{L}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{\Gamma}\otimes f}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B,

\beta:B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B\xrightarrow{\eta_{L}\otimes g\otimes\eta_{R}}\Sigma;

in particular, there is the natural factorization

f:(A,\Gamma)\rightarrow(B,B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B)\rightarrow(B,\Sigma).

Theorem 3.3

(
[
14
, Theorem 13]
)
.

Let
f=(f_{o},f_{m}):(A,\Gamma)\rightarrow(B,\Sigma)
be a morphism of flat Hopf algebroids (in the sense that the left and right unit maps are flat), with associated morphisms
\alpha
and
\beta
determined in Remark
3.2
. Then the following statements are equivalence:

(i)

f
induces an isomorphism of the associated stacks.

(ii)

f^{*}:\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Sigma}
is an equivalence.

(iii)

\alpha
is faithfully flat and
\beta
is an isomorphism.

Proof

Refer to
[
14
, Theorem 13]
or
[
6
, Theorem 6.2]
.

\square

Construction 3.4
.

Let
(A,\Gamma)
be a Hopf algebroid, and
f:A\rightarrow B
a ring homomorphism. As it is mentioned in Remark
3.2
, the pair of rings
(B,B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B)
can naturally be equipped with the structure of a Hopf algebroid. More precisely, the left and right units
\eta_{L},\eta_{R}:B\rightarrow B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B
are given as the inclusions of the first and the third factors; the co-multiplication is determined as the following composite

\displaystyle B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}}B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}(\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma)\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B

\displaystyle\rightarrow B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}(\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A}B\otimes_{A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma)\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}(B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B)\otimes_{B}(B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B);

the co-unit is determined as the following composite

B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}}B\otimes_{A}B\xrightarrow{\text{multiplication}}B;

finally, the conjugation
c:B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B\rightarrow B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B
is induced by the conjugation of
\Gamma
together with interchanging the first and the third factors. It is routine to demonstrate that the axioms for Hopf algebroids are satisfied; write
\Gamma_{f}:=B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B
, and there is the natural morphism of Hopf algebroids

(f,\iota):(A,\Gamma)\rightarrow(B,\Gamma_{f}).

.

Let
(A,\Gamma)
be a flat Hopf algebroid, and let

be a faithfully flat ring homomorphism. Then the morphism
(f,\iota):(A,\Gamma)\rightarrow(B,\Gamma_{f}=B\otimes_{f,A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B)
of Construction

induces an equivalence of categories

(f,\iota)^{*}:\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathrm{RCoMod}_{\Gamma_{f}}.

Proof

According to Theorem
3.3
, it suffices to check that, the morphism

\alpha:A\xrightarrow{\eta_{L}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{\Gamma}\otimes f}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B

Note that, since

is a flat Hopf algebroid, the left (or right) unit

is in fact faithfully flat:
\eta_{L}
is flat by assumption, and the axioms of Hopf algebroids implies that, for any

, the following composite

is the identity of

if and only if

.

It is also clear that, since

is faithfully flat,

is also faithfully flat. Therefore
\alpha:A\xrightarrow{\eta_{L}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{\Gamma}\otimes f}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,f}B
is faithfully flat.

\square

Profinite Rings and Dual Algebras

In order to illustrate the possible study of comodule cohomology through the perspective of dual algebras, in this section we review the classical results of profinite rings, especially the corresponding cohomological algebra, for which the main reference is
[
16
]
. A comparison of the classical approach to cohomological algebra with the cohomological algebra in solid modules can be found in
[
20
]
.

Definition 5.1

(
[
16
, Proposition 5.1.2]
)
.

A profinite ring is a topological ring
\Lambda
which satisfies the following equivalent conditions:

(1)

\Lambda
is isomorphic to a cofiltered limit of finite discrete rings.

(2)

\Lambda
is compact and Hausdorff.

(3)

\Lambda
is compact, Hausdorff, and totally disconnected.

(4)

The zero element of
\Lambda
has a fundamental neighborhood
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of open ideals, and
A\xrightarrow{\sim}\varprojlim_{i\in I}A/T_{i}
.

(5)

There is a cofiltered diagram
\{\Lambda_{i},\varphi_{ij}\}
where each
\varphi_{ij}
is an epimorphism, so that there is an isomorphism
\Lambda\simeq\varprojlim_{i\in I}\Lambda_{i}
.

In particular, every finite discrete ring is a profinite ring.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a profinite ring. By a topological
\Lambda
-module it means a Hausdorff topological abelian group
M
equipped with a continuous
\Lambda
-action
\Lambda\times M\rightarrow M
which is bi-additive and associate.

Let
M,N
be two topological
\Lambda
-modules. The group of continuous
\Lambda
-homomorphisms
\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(M,N)
can be equipped with the compact-open topology, and is therefore a topological abelian group.

Let
\Lambda
be a profinite ring, and let
\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(-,-)
denote the group of continuous
\Lambda
-homomorphisms between topological
\Lambda
-modules. In order to develop a theory of cohomological algebra, we specify the following two types of topological
\Lambda
-modules.

Definition 5.3
.

A discrete
\Lambda
-module is a topological
\Lambda
-module
N
whose underlying topological space is discrete, that is,
N
has the following property (
[
16
, Lemma 5.1.1]
):

•

N
is the union of its finite
\Lambda
-submodules.

Let
\mathrm{Mod}^{d}_{\Lambda}
denote the category of discrete
\Lambda
-modules. It is routine to check that
\mathrm{Mod}^{d}_{\Lambda}
is an abelian category.

Definition 5.4
.

A profinite
\Lambda
-module is a topological
\Lambda
-module
M
whose underlying topological abelian group is profinite, that is,
M
has the following property (
[
16
, Lemma 5.1.1]
):

•

M
is the inverse limit of its finite quotient
\Lambda
-modules; equivalently, the submodules of
M
of finite index form a fundamental system of neighborhoods of
0
.

Let
\mathrm{Mod}^{pf}_{\Lambda}
denote the category of profinite
\Lambda
-modules. It is routine to check that
\mathrm{Mod}^{pf}_{\Lambda}
is an abelian category.

Remark 5.5
.

Let
\mathrm{Mod}^{pf}_{\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}}
denote the category of right profinite
\Lambda
-modules. There is the profinite tensor product

\widehat{\otimes}_{\Lambda}:\mathrm{Mod}^{pf}_{\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}}\times\mathrm{Mod}^{pf}_{\Lambda}\rightarrow\mathrm{PfAb},

where
\mathrm{PfAb}
is the category of profinite abelian groups. This profinite tensor product can be characterized by the standard universal property, or more concretely, given a right profinite
\Lambda
-module
M
which is expressed as a cofiltered limit of finite right
\Lambda
-modules
M\simeq\varprojlim_{i\in I}M_{i}
, and a left profinite
\Lambda
-module
N
which is expressed as a cofiltered limit of finite left
\Lambda
-modules
N\simeq\varprojlim_{j\in J}N_{j}
, then

M\widehat{\otimes}_{\Lambda}N\simeq\varprojlim_{i\in I,j\in J}M_{i}\otimes_{\Lambda}N_{j}.

Remark 5.6
.

Let
M
be a profinite
\Lambda
-module, which is written as a cofiltered limit of its finite quotient
\Lambda
-modules:
M\simeq\varprojlim_{i\in I}M_{i}
; and let
N
be a discrete
\Lambda
-module. Then the canonical morphism

\varinjlim_{i\in I^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(M_{i},N)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\varprojlim_{i\in I}M_{i},N)

is an isomorphism (
[
16
, Lemma 5.1.4]
). In particular, the compact-open topology for
\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(M,N)
is discrete.

Proposition 5.7

(Pontryagin Duality)
.

The assignment
M\mapsto\mathrm{Hom}{Z}(M,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
induces an equivalence of categories
(\mathrm{Mod}^{pf}_{\Lambda})^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Mod}^{d}_{\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}}
.

Proof

This is an application of the Pontryagin duality between discrete torsion groups and profinite groups, see
[
16
, Theorem 2.9.6]
and
[
16
,
\lx@sectionsign
5.1]
.

\square

Remark 5.8

(
[
16
, Propositions 5.4.2, 5.4.4]
)
.

The category
\mathrm{Mod}^{pf}_{\Lambda}
has enough projectives, and a profinite
\Lambda
-module is projective if and only if it is a direct summand of a free profinite
\Lambda
-module.

Dually, the category
\mathrm{Mod}^{d}_{\Lambda}
has enough injectives, and a discrete
\Lambda
-module is injective if and only if it is a direct factor of a cofree discrete
\Lambda
-module.

Lemma 5.9

(
[
16
, Exercise 5.4.7]
)
.

Let
P
be a projective profinite
\Lambda
-module, then the functor

\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P,-):\mathrm{Mod}^{d}_{\Lambda}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ab}

is exact.

Similarly, let
I
be an injective discrete
\Lambda
-module, then the functor

\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(-,I):(\mathrm{Mod}^{pf}_{\Lambda})^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ab}

is exact.

Proof

Since the two statements are dual to each other by Pontryagin duality,
it suffices to check the first statement, that is, for any epimorphism
r:N\rightarrow N^{\prime}
of discrete
\Lambda
-modules, the corresponding morphism
\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P,N)\xrightarrow{r\circ-}\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P,N^{\prime})
should be an epimorphism. Given any continuous
\Lambda
-homomorphism
f:P\rightarrow N^{\prime}
, since
P
is compact and
N^{\prime}
discrete,
f(P)
is a finite
\Lambda
-submodule of
N^{\prime}
. Note that,
r^{-1}(f(P))
, being a discrete
\Lambda
-submodule of
N
, is a union of its finite
\Lambda
-submodules, and therefore there is a finite
\Lambda
-submodule
\widetilde{N}\subseteq r^{-1}(f(P))\subseteq N
such that
r|_{\widetilde{N}}:\widetilde{N}\rightarrow f(P)
is an epimorphism. Therefore, it reduces to the case where
N,N^{\prime}
are finite
\Lambda
-modules, which are certainly profinite
\Lambda
-modules, and the statement follows.

\square

Remark 5.10
.

Although currently we have not yet set up a suitable category of

•

Further Development

In this final section we include some ideas which has not yet been developed in this article but are necessarily demanded by both theory and applications. Still, let
(A,\Gamma)
be a Hopf algebroid as in Convention
2.1
.

Remark 6.1
.

The Hopf algebroids in consideration might be graded or filtered. In that case, there are potentially two approaches to dual algebras: either we consider the graded or filtered
\mathrm{Hom}
and obtain dual algebras as (topological) graded or filtered algebras; or, recall that, graded abelian groups are comodules over the Hopf algebroid
(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}])
, and filtered abelian groups are comodules over the Hopf algebroid
(\mathbb{Z}[\tau],\mathbb{Z}[\tau][t,t^{-1}])
(see for example
[
11
]
), thus a graded Hopf algebroid might be treated as an ordinary Hopf algebroid of the form
(A,\Gamma[t,t^{-1}])
, and a filtered Hopf algebroid might be treated as an Hopf algebroid
(A,\Gamma[t,t^{-1}])
which is further equipped with an endomorphism
\tau
.

Remark 6.2
.

As it constantly reappears throughout this article, a complete treatment of the dual algebra
\Gamma^{\vee}
should be carried out in the context of solid abelian groups. In particular, we should have the dualizing object
\omega:=\mathrm{RHom}_{\Gamma^{\vee}}(A,\Gamma^{\vee})
as an object in the derived category of right
\Gamma^{\vee}
-modules.

Example 6.3
.

Resume Example
1.8
, and also recall from Example
1.23
that, there should be following resolution of left

-modules:

suggesting that

is computed by the complex

which is quasi-isomorphic to

Note also that, regarding

There might be another possible computation of the dualizing object for

Resume Example

(A,\Gamma)=(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]],\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q_{0}-1,q_{1}-1]]\{\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{[p]_{q_{0}}}\}_{\delta})
, together with the ring homomorphism

m_{q}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q_{0}-1,q_{1}-1]]\{\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{[p]_{q_{0}}}\}_{\delta}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[q-1]],\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988ptq_{0}\mapsto q,q_{1}\mapsto q^{1+p},

and the operator
\nabla_{q}=\frac{1}{q(q-1)}\circ(m_{q}-1)\in\Gamma^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{A,\eta_{L}}(\Gamma,A)
. If
\Gamma
is regarded as a right
\Gamma
-comodule through
\Delta:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma
, then
\nabla_{q}
acts on
\Gamma
through the composite (and we abusively denote this composite still by
\nabla_{q}
)

\nabla_{q}:\Gamma\xrightarrow{\Delta}\Gamma\otimes_{\eta_{R},A,\eta_{L}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{\Gamma}\otimes\nabla_{q}}\Gamma,

which is not a morphism of right
\Gamma
-comodule (not even a morphism of
A,\eta_{R}
-modules), yet is a morphism of left
\Gamma
-comodules; moreover, there is the following (
(p,[p]_{q})
-completely) exact sequence

0\rightarrow A\xrightarrow{\eta_{L}}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\nabla_{q}}\Gamma\rightarrow 0,

suggesting that
\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{LCoMod}_{\Gamma}}(\Gamma,A)
is computed by the complex

(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{LCoMod}_{\Gamma}}(\Gamma,\Gamma)\xrightarrow{\nabla_{q}\circ-}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{LCoMod}_{\Gamma}}(\Gamma,\Gamma))\simeq(\Gamma^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\nabla_{q}\circ-}\Gamma^{\vee}).

Remark 6.6
.

The object
\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{LCoMod}_{\Gamma}}(\Gamma,A)
, or
\mathrm{RHom}_{\Gamma^{\vee}}(A,\Gamma^{\vee})
, by definition, depends on the Hopf algebroid
(A,\Gamma)
, or the presentation
\mathrm{Spec}(A)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
where
\mathfrak{X}
is the stack associated to
(A,\Gamma)
. However, the object
\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{LCoMod}_{\Gamma}}(\Gamma,A)
or
\mathrm{RHom}_{\Gamma^{\vee}}(A,\Gamma^{\vee})
might sometimes be identified as canonical (at least for
\mathrm{Spec}(A)
): for example, consider the Hopf algebroid
(\mathbb{C}[x],\mathbb{C}[x,y]^{\wedge}_{(x-y)})=(\mathbb{C}[x],\mathbb{C}[x][[dx]])
; note that in this case we can not immediately apply Theorem
2.22
since for
(\mathbb{C}[x],\mathbb{C}[x][[dx]])
we should consider co-continuous comodules; yet following similar spirit we have the dual algebra
\mathbb{C}[x][\frac{d}{dx}]
, with the relations that
\frac{d}{dx}\circ f=f\circ\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{d}{dx}(f)
, and in this case we have

\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathbb{C}[x][\frac{d}{dx}]}(\mathbb{C}[x],\mathbb{C}[x][\frac{d}{dx}])\simeq\mathbb{C}[x][\frac{d}{dx}]/(\frac{d}{dx}\circ\mathbb{C}[x][\frac{d}{dx}])[-1],

and there is an isomorphism of right
\mathbb{C}[x][\frac{d}{dx}]
-modules

C[x][\frac{d}{dx}]/(\frac{d}{dx}\circ\mathbb{C}[x][\frac{d}{dx}])\simeq\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{C}[x]/\mathbb{C}},

where
f\frac{d}{dx}
acts on
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{C}[x]/\mathbb{C}}
through
-\mathcal{L}_{f\frac{d}{dx}}
.

Still, it is expected that, the object
\mathrm{RHom}_{\Gamma^{\vee}}(A,\Gamma^{\vee})\otimes^{L}_{\Gamma^{\vee}}A\in D(\mathbb{Z})
depends only on the stack
\mathfrak{X}
associated to
(A,\Gamma)
.

Neoadjuvant pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in germline BRCA-mutated early triple-negative breast cancer: a multicenter real-world cohort
Authors: Monique Celeste Tavares, Flávia C Balint, Romualdo Barroso-Sousa, Laura Testa, Pablo Mandó, Natália C Nunes, Sergio Alejandro Mazzotta, Micaela Rigesti, Isadora M Sousa, Matheus O Andrade, Mariana Gouveia, Fernanda Madasi, José Bines, Rafael D P Ferreira, Daniela D Rosa, Candice L Santos, Mariana R Monteiro, Zenaide S Souza, Daniele Assad-Suzuki, Carlos H dos Anjos, Débora M Gagliato, Ana Maria U Gomes, Bruna M Zucchetti, Anezka Ferrari, Mayana L Brito, Maria Marcela F Monteiro, Gilmara Resende, Noele J B Gomes, Maria Victoria Costanzo, Solange Moraes Sanches, Vladmir C Lima, Jose Claudio Casali Rocha, Elizabeth Santana dos Santos, Fabiana B Makdissi, Paulo M Hoff, Maria del Pilar Estevez-Diz, Renata Colombo Bonadio
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNeoadjuvant pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy improves outcomes in early triple-negative breast cancer, but real-world evidence in patients with pathogenic BRCA1/2 variants remains limited. We analyzed 726 consecutively treated patients from the multicenter Neo-Real/GBECAM-0123 cohort who received at least one cycle of neoadjuvant pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy and underwent surgery. Patients with pathogenic BRCA1/2 variants (mBRCA) were identified in 105 of 600 tested patients (17.5%), corresponding to 14.5% of the overall cohort. Among mBRCA carriers, 82 (78.1%) had BRCA1 variants, 22 (21.0%) had BRCA2 variants, and in 1 patient (0.1%) the BRCA subtype was not specified. The comparator group comprised 621 patients with wild-type or unknown BRCA status (wt/unknown BRCA). Pathologic complete response was higher in the mBRCA group than in the wt/unknown BRCA group (74.0% vs 61.7%). With a median follow-up of 22 months, event-free and overall survival were favorable in both groups, with a non-significant trend toward improved event-free survival in the mBRCA group, particularly among patients with residual disease.Subject terms: Cancer, Oncology

Introduction

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is characterized by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) expression and by lack of HER2 overexpression or gene amplification. This receptor profile limits established targeted treatment options and is generally associated with more aggressive tumor biology, higher early recurrence risk, and poorer prognosis compared with hormone receptor–positive disease. TNBC accounts for approximately 10–15% of all breast cancers, is diagnosed more frequently in younger patients, and is overrepresented in some populations (e.g., African American and Hispanic women), contributing to substantial psychosocial and economic burden. Biologically, TNBC is heterogeneous and comprises distinct molecular subtypes with potential therapeutic relevance; however, broadly effective, universally applicable targeted strategies remain limited1,2.

KEYNOTE-522 established pembrolizumab combined with neoadjuvant chemotherapy (paclitaxel–carboplatin followed by anthracycline/cyclophosphamide) and continued adjuvant pembrolizumab as a standard for high-risk early-stage TNBC, improving pCR, event-free survival, and overall survival versus chemotherapy alone3,4. In parallel, 10–15% of TNBC tumors harbor germline BRCA1/2 pathogenic variants and frequently exhibit homologous recombination deficiency (HRD), identifiable by genomic scars and mutational signature2–7. HRD is associated with enhanced sensitivity to DNA-damaging agents (including platinum) and to PARP inhibitors in metastatic disease (OlympiAD, EMBRACA) and in the adjuvant setting (OlympiA)8–13. Mechanistically, HRD may also modulate immunogenicity and the tumor microenvironment, potentially influencing response to immune checkpoint blockade2–14. Recent exploratory biomarker analyses from KEYNOTE-522 presented at SABCS 2024 further support this biological framework. In this analysis, ~21% of patients in the biomarker-evaluable population harbored BRCA alterations (including ~17% germline), and HRD positivity was associated with higher pathologic complete response rates. Because pCR correlates with long-term outcomes at the trial level, pCR and residual disease metrics can serve as clinically meaningful intermediate endpoints15–17.

Real-world evidence (RWE) complements clinical trials by characterizing outcomes, treatment patterns, and resource use in routine practice, thereby informing clinical decision-making and implementation. Given the limited data evaluating the KEYNOTE-522 regimen in patients with mBRCA, we aimed to assess clinical characteristics, pathologic response, survival outcomes, and safety in a multicenter real-world cohort of patients with TNBC and mBRCA treated according to the KEYNOTE-522 regimen1–18.

Results

Germline BRCA testing

Overall, 600 (82.64%) underwent germline BRCA1/BRCA2 mutation testing and had the testing results available. A hundred twenty-six patients (17.4%) were not tested or had the testing status unavailable. Among those tested, pathogenic or likely pathogenic germline BRCA1/2 variants (mBRCA) were identified in 105/600 patients (17.5%), corresponding to 105/726 (14.5%) of the overall cohort. BRCA1 variants were observed in 82 patients (78.1%) and BRCA2 variants in 22 (21%). The control group comprised 621 patients (wt/unknown BRCA) (Table 1).

Patients characteristics

mBRCA patients were younger than patients with wt/unknown BRCA status (median 40.2 vs 45.3 years; p < 0.001) and were more frequently premenopausal; BMI did not differ between groups. Initial clinical stage distribution was similar (stage II 72.3% and stage III 27.7% in both groups; p = 0.96). Histologic grade 3 was numerically more frequent in mBRCA, although not statistically significant (76.2% vs 69.1%; p = 0.183). Ki-67 was higher in mBRCA (median 80% vs 70%; p = 0.010). Invasive carcinoma of no special type predominated in both groups (95.1% vs 91.2%) (Table 2).

Pathologic complete response rates

Patients with mBRCA had higher pCR rates than those with wt/unknown BRCA status (74.0% vs 61.7%; OR 1.78, 95% CI 1.10–2.87; P = 0.0244). Progression during neoadjuvant therapy occurred in 1.2% of patients with mBRCA versus 4.4% of those with wt/unknown BRCA status (p = 0.23) (Fig. 1).

In the multivariable logistic regression model including BRCA status, disease stage, tumor grade, Ki-67 index, and number of neoadjuvant pembrolizumab cycles, mBRCA remained significantly associated with pCR (OR 1.70, 95% CI 1.01–2.89; P = 0.048). Post-neoadjuvant treatment patterns varied according to both BRCA status and pathologic response. Among patients who achieved pCR, continuation of adjuvant pembrolizumab was frequent in both groups, whereas capecitabine and olaparib were rarely used. Among patients with residual disease, however, olaparib-based strategies were predominantly used in the mBRCA group, while capecitabine-based approaches were substantially more common in the wt/unknown BRCA group (Table 5B).

Given the clinical relevance of post-neoadjuvant PARP inhibition in this setting, we performed an exploratory EFS analysis among patients with mBRCA and residual disease according to olaparib use. Among 26 patients in this subgroup, 18 received adjuvant olaparib, 7 did not, and 1 had missing data and was excluded from this exploratory comparison. A numerical EFS advantage was observed in the olaparib group, with estimated 24-month EFS rates of 92.9% versus 68.6% in patients who did not receive olaparib; however, this difference was not statistically significant (HR 0.17, 95% CI 0.02–1.88; log-rank p = 0.10) and should be interpreted as exploratory only.

Survival outcomes

With a median follow-up of 22 months, patients with mBRCA showed a trend toward improved EFS compared with wt/unknown BRCA counterparts, with 2-year EFS rates of 92.3% and 86.2%, respectively (HR: 0.51, 95% CI 0.23–1.11, P = 0.091). Although not statistically significant, this pattern suggests a clinically meaningful benefit (Fig. 2). Median EFS was not reached in either cohort.

For patients whose tumors achieved a pathologic complete response, favorable EFS was observed regardless of the BRCA status, with 2-year EFS rates greater than 94% in both groups (HR 1.10, 95% CI 0.31–3.88, P = 0.875). Safety

Use of G-CSF tended to be higher in mBRCA (74% vs 65.5%; p = 0.122). Notably, G-CSF is routinely administered as part of primary prophylaxis for patients receiving dose-dense AC in routine practice; therefore, in this real-world dataset, G-CSF use likely reflects both protocol-driven prophylaxis and clinical indications, and we did not systematically distinguish primary from secondary prophylaxis. Overall, the safety patterns were similar in patients with mBRCA and wt/unknown BRCA. Rates of febrile neutropenia were similar (10.2% vs 8.7%; p = 0.85). Dose reductions (12.8% vs 15.5%; p = 0.65), treatment delays (25.3% vs 25.7%; p = 1.00), and discontinuations (24.4% vs 22.9%; p = 0.88) were comparable between the two groups (Fig. 5).

Adverse events according to germline BRCA status.

Discussion

In this real-world Latin American cohort (n = 726) treated according to the KEYNOTE-522 protocol, we observed that patients with mBRCA had higher pCR rates and a trend toward improved long-term outcomes compared with those with wt/unknown BRCA. In the pivotal trial, BRCA status was not reported3,4. In our cohort, approximately one in seven patients carried a mBRCA, with a predominance of BRCA1. These patients were younger and showed features consistent with a more proliferative phenotype, including higher Ki-67, whereas the proportion of histologic grade 3 tumors was numerically higher but did not significantly differ between groups; they also showed a trend toward higher TILs. The addition of pembrolizumab to taxane–carboplatin followed by anthracycline/cyclophosphamide resulted in substantially higher pCR in mBRCA compared with wt/unknown BRCA (74.0% vs. 61.7%), translating into excellent EFS and OS.

Biologically, pathogenic BRCA1/2 variants are a hallmark of homologous recombination deficiency (HRD), which is common in TNBC and can be characterized by genomic scars, including loss of heterozygosity (LOH), telomeric allelic imbalance (TAI), large-scale state transitions (LST), and mutational signatures5–7. This biology underpins sensitivity to platinum-based chemotherapy (e.g., TNT; GeparSixto) and synthetic lethality with PARP inhibition, with demonstrated clinical benefit in metastatic disease (OlympiAD, EMBRACA) and in high-risk early-stage disease (OlympiA)8–13. HRD may also increase tumor immunogenicity through DNA-sensing pathways, contributing to higher TILs in BRCA1-associated tumors in some cohorts, although results are heterogeneous2,14. Notably, an exploratory biomarker analysis of KEYNOTE-522 presented at SABCS 2024 further supports the biologic relevance of HRD in early TNBC. In that analysis, BRCA/HRD status was positively associated with higher pCR rates, reinforcing the concept that HRD-related tumors may be particularly sensitive to neoadjuvant treatment. These data are consistent with our findings and support the interpretation that the higher pCR observed in mBRCA may reflect, at least in part, an underlying HRD-driven phenotype rather than a purely BRCA-specific immunotherapy effect17.

Our findings are broadly consistent with smaller real-world series that specifically evaluated mBRCA TNBC treated with anthracycline–taxane–platinum regimens plus pembrolizumab25–28. The prevalence of mBRCA1/2 in our cohort (17.5% among tested patients; 14.5% overall) falls within the range reported in other real-world early TNBC series and appears particularly similar to the frequency reported by Park et al. (14.6%), while lower than in more selected cohorts such as those reported by Fedele et al. and Bae/Kim et al. In a multicenter Italian cohort of 184 patients, Fedele et al. reported pCR rates of 80.0% in mBRCA1, 75.0% in mBRCA2 and 61.1% in wild-type tumors; pooled mBRCA1/2 had a higher probability of pCR than wild-type patients (78.4% vs 61.1%; OR 2.17; 95% CI 1.01–4.97), survival data remain immature25. A Korean series of 199 patients treated with a KEYNOTE-522–like regimen with or without pembrolizumab similarly found numerically higher pCR in mBRCA (73.5% vs 59.3%; p = 0.075) and comparable absolute pCR gains with pembrolizumab in mutated and non-mutated groups, suggesting that mBRCA behaves primarily as a chemosensitivity/HRD marker rather than a specific predictor of incremental immunotherapy benefit26. Etan et al. reported analogous findings in an Israeli cohort of 100 stage II–III TNBC patients treated with anthracycline, taxane and carboplatin (ACTC) or a KEYNOTE-522-based chemo-immunotherapy regimen: one-third were mBRCA and attained higher pCR irrespective of regimen, with pCR reaching 100% among mBRCA treated with KN522. At 50 months, KN522 was associated with improved OS/EFS versus ACTC, and pCR, rather than mBRCA status, was the main driver of long-term outcomes27. A larger Korean cohort of 316 patients uniformly treated with KN522-based regimens also showed significantly higher pCR in mBRCA vs wild-type tumors (73.9% vs 59.3%; p = 0.040) but no independent effect of mBRCA on DFS after adjusting for stage, PD-L1, adjuvant therapy, and pCR28 (Table 6).

Taken together, these real-world data, including the present Neo-Real cohort, consistently show that mBRCA TNBC derives higher pCR rates, typically in the 70–80% range, representing an absolute gain of 10–15 percentage points compared with wt/unknown BRCA tumors under KEYNOTE-522–like regimens. However, a distinct survival advantage attributable to BRCA status alone has not been robustly demonstrated; in multivariable models, mBRCA per se has not emerged as an independent prognostic factor for DFS/OS once pCR and standard clinicopathologic variables are accounted for24–27. Current evidence therefore supports mBRCA1/2 as a marker of enhanced platinum-based chemosensitivity/HRD, while long-term benefit from KEYNOTE-522, like chemo-immunotherapy seems to be driven predominantly by achieving pCR. At the trial level, pCR and residual disease burden are associated with long-term outcomes, supporting their use as intermediate endpoints while recognizing imperfect surrogacy for EFS/OS15–19.

From a post-neoadjuvant perspective, RCB provides granularity beyond binary pCR and has been validated as a prognostic tool across breast cancer subtypes; higher RCB is associated with worse long-term outcomes and can guide escalation strategies19–23. In TNBC with residual invasive disease after standard neoadjuvant chemotherapy, adjuvant capecitabine improved disease-free and overall survival (CREATE-X) and remains a relevant option alongside immunotherapy-based regimens28. For mBRCA with high-risk early disease, adjuvant olaparib improved invasive and distant disease-free survival and overall survival in OlympiA, supporting consideration of PARP inhibition in eligible patients after completion of local therapy and standard systemic treatment12,13.

In our cohort, separation of the EFS curves was more evident among patients with residual disease, a setting in which adjuvant treatment is typically risk-adapted. While higher pCR rates may partly explain cohort-level differences in EFS, the non-significant EFS trend observed in the residual disease subgroup may also reflect differential use of post-neoadjuvant escalation strategies. In particular, olaparib-based approaches were used predominantly in eligible patients with mBRCA, whereas capecitabine-based strategies were more frequently used in the wt/unknown BRCA group, with pembrolizumab continuation also varying according to response and BRCA status. This point is especially relevant because all patients in our cohort received carboplatin as part of the neoadjuvant regimen, and the role of adjuvant olaparib after prior platinum exposure remains a clinically important question. In an exploratory analysis of patients with mBRCA and residual disease, olaparib use was associated with a numerically more favorable EFS, although this comparison was based on a very small number of patients and events and did not reach statistical significance. Therefore, differences in post-neoadjuvant treatment should be considered a potential confounding factor in the interpretation of EFS outcomes according to BRCA status. Given the observational design and the heterogeneity and incomplete capture of post-neoadjuvant therapy delivery, these findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating. The higher mastectomy rate observed in mBRCA patients in our cohort (88.3% vs. 34.7%) likely reflects a combination of therapeutic indication and risk-reducing intent, including contralateral risk-reducing mastectomy, which is known to be associated with improved survival outcomes in young BRCA carriers with breast cancer32. However, our dataset did not systematically distinguish between therapeutic and risk-reducing procedures, which limits interpretation of this finding.

TILs are a recognized prognostic biomarker in early TNBC. However, in our cohort, TILs data were available for only a limited subset of patients and were not included in the multivariable analysis because of substantial missingness. Therefore, TIL-related observations in this study should be considered exploratory and interpreted with caution29–31.

Emerging evidence suggests that mBRCA1/2 alterations may modulate the tumor microenvironment and influence response to immune checkpoint blockade14. Moreover, breast cancers arising in mBRCA1 versus mBRCA2 carriers may represent distinct biological and clinical entities with potentially divergent therapeutic opportunities2.

This real-world study suggests that TNBC patients with mBRCA may have distinct clinical characteristics and treatment response patterns, including higher pCR rates and a trend toward improved EFS with a KEYNOTE-522–based regimen. The observed 12.3% absolute increase in pCR was accompanied by favorable EFS trajectories. Collectively, these findings support further investigation of risk-adapted strategies and more individualized treatment approaches for patients with mBRCA.

This retrospective multicenter study has inherent limitations, including missing data (TILs, RCB), inter-center heterogeneity, variability in AE reporting, and the absence of standardized auditing. Approximately 17% of patients did not undergo BRCA testing. These individuals were pragmatically grouped with the wild-type cohort for analysis; however, we acknowledge that this approach may dilute observed differences. The exact timing of germline BRCA result availability was not systematically captured. In routine practice, testing was generally requested soon after biopsy-confirmed TNBC, with the goal of obtaining results before surgery; however, delays could occur, particularly due to insurance authorization, which may have affected surgical planning and access to adjuvant olaparib. Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, this represents the largest cohort evaluating the KEYNOTE-522 regimen in mBRCA patients and provides valuable insights to guide future research. Importantly, it also contributes data from a Latin American population, which remains underrepresented in prospective clinical trials.

Methods

Study design and setting

We performed a multicenter, observational cohort study of patients with early-stage TNBC across 14 cancer centers in Brazil and 16 centers in Argentina between July 2020 and July 2025. All patients received neoadjuvant pembrolizumab in combination with a standardized chemotherapy regimen consistent with the KEYNOTE-522 protocol. The chemotherapy backbone consisted of a taxane (paclitaxel) plus carboplatin, followed by an anthracycline (doxorubicin or epirubicin) and cyclophosphamide. Pembrolizumab was administered concurrently with chemotherapy during the neoadjuvant phase. The use of carboplatin and all chemotherapy components was mandated by protocol, with no deviations in regimen selection allowed. Data were collected from electronic medical records using a standardized case report form and independently verified.

Participants

Eligible patients had histologically confirmed TNBC (any histologic subtype) and received ≥1 cycle of neoadjuvant pembrolizumab with chemotherapy. Baseline disease stage mirrored the KEYNOTE-522 population (cT2N0 and/or cT1 with node-positive disease; M0). Patients who underwent definitive breast surgery after the neoadjuvant therapy were included in the primary analyses of pCR.

All consecutively treated patients were included, regardless of the availability of germline testing. Germline BRCA1/2 testing was universally recommended for patients with early-stage TNBC across participating centers; however, test uptake was contingent on local availability and insurance/health system authorization, which varied over time and across sites. When performed, BRCA1/2 testing used one or more of the following platforms, with validated clinical assays: multigene next-generation sequencing (NGS) panels; targeted NGS for BRCA1/2; multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA) for large genomic rearrangements; and/or targeted qPCR or digital PCR for specific variants. According to the results, patients were categorized as:

mBRCA: carriers of pathogenic or likely pathogenic germline BRCA1/2 variants; variants of uncertain significance (VUS) were not classified as mBRCA; or

mBRCA: carriers of pathogenic or likely pathogenic germline BRCA1/2 variants; variants of uncertain significance (VUS) were not classified as mBRCA; or

wt/unknown BRCA or wt/unkBRCA: germline BRCA1/2 wild-type/negative results or unknown/unavailable status (including untested patients), analyzed together.

wt/unknown BRCA or wt/unkBRCA: germline BRCA1/2 wild-type/negative results or unknown/unavailable status (including untested patients), analyzed together.

This multicenter observational study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was approved under protocol CAAE 67950623.0.2013.5432 by the Ethics Committee of A.C.Camargo Cancer Center (São Paulo, Brazil) and under protocol CAAE 67950623.0.1001.0068 by the Ethics Committee of Rede D’Or/Instituto D’Or de Pesquisa e Ensino (IDOR) (Brazil), corresponding to the coordinating institutions of the study. Additional ethical approvals were obtained from the institutional review boards of all participating centers, including: Grupo Brasileiro de Estudos em Câncer de Mama (GBECAM) Research Committee (São Paulo, Brazil); Hospital Brasília – Américas Oncologia Ethics Committee (Brasília, Brazil); Instituto D’Or de Pesquisa e Ensino (IDOR) Research Ethics Committees (São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, and Recife, Brazil); Instituto do Câncer do Estado de São Paulo, Faculdade de Medicina da Universidade de São Paulo Research Ethics Committee (São Paulo, Brazil); SUMA (Grupo Cooperativa Argentino para el estudio y la investigación del Cáncer de Mama) Ethics Committee (Buenos Aires, Argentina); Instituto Américas Ethics Committee (Rio de Janeiro, Brazil); Hospital 9 de Julho (Américas Oncologia) Ethics Committee (São Paulo, Brazil); Hospital Moinhos de Vento Ethics Committee (Porto Alegre, Brazil); Hospital Samaritano (Américas Oncologia) Ethics Committee (São Paulo, Brazil); Hospital Sírio-Libanês Ethics Committees (Brasília and São Paulo, Brazil); Hospital Beneficência Portuguesa Ethics Committee (São Paulo, Brazil); Hospital Santa Paula (Américas Oncologia) Ethics Committee (São Paulo, Brazil); Clínica AMO Ethics Committee (Salvador, Brazil); Instituto do Câncer do Ceará Ethics Committee (Fortaleza, Brazil); Centro Integrado de Pesquisa da Amazônia (CINPAM) Ethics Committee (Manaus, Brazil); and Hospital São Domingos Ethics Committee (São Luís, Brazil). Due to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of de-identified data, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the ethics committees in accordance with local regulations and national regulations (Brazil and Argentina).

Statistical analysis

The primary outcomes were pathologic complete response (pCR), defined as ypT0/Tis ypN0 at surgery. The wt/unknown BRCA category was analyzed as a control group per the prespecified plan. Univariate and multivariable logistic regression were used to evaluate factors associated with pCR. Variables were selected for the multivariable regression based on their clinical relevance and the association with pCR in the univariate analysis. Residual disease was categorized using the Residual Cancer Burden (RCB) index when available18–22.

Secondary outcomes included event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS). EFS was defined as the time from the first neoadjuvant dose to any of the following events: disease progression precluding surgery, local, regional, or distant recurrence, development of a second invasive cancer, or death from any cause. OS was calculated from the date of first neoadjuvant dose until the date of death from any cause. OS was analyzed descriptively given the low number of events. Patients without events were censored at the date of last contact. Median follow-up was estimated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. Additional secondary outcomes comprised progression during neoadjuvant therapy and safety, assessed by the incidence of grade ≥3 adverse events (AEs).

Continuous variables are reported as median (IQR) and were compared with the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Categorical variables are summarized as counts (percentages) and compared using χ² or Fisher’s exact tests. Time-to-event endpoints were estimated with the Kaplan–Meier method and compared by the log-rank test; hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were derived from Cox proportional-hazards models. Two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Missing data were handled by complete-case analysis for each endpoint.

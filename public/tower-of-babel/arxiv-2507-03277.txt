Magnetic, charge and orbital properties of pure and Sr-doped La 2 NiO 4 and its pressure evolutions Preprint: APS/123-QED
Han-Yu Wang, Shu-Hong Tang, Xiao-Teng Huang, Ya-Min Quan, Xian-Long Wang, Yan-Ling Li, Da-Yong Liu, H.-Q. Lin, Zhi Zeng, Liang-Jian Zou

Abstract
Recent discovery of unconventional superconductivity on multilayer nickelates under high pressure have stimulated great interest. The magnetic and charge configurations in the normal phase of multilayer nickelates which closely relate to the spin-charge fluctuations remain under strongly debated. In this work, we focus the normal-state magnetic, charge and orbital configurations and its evolutions with hydrostatic pressure and Sr-doping concentration of monolayer nickelate La 2 NiO 4 . We reveal that in the ambient pressure tetragonal La 2 NiO 4 displays Neel-type antiferromagnetic order in planer Ni spins with negligible interlayer magnetic coupling; with the increase of pressure, La 2 NiO 4 evolves from insulate into metallic state with the critical pressure about P = 50 GPa; with the substitution of La by Sr, the magnetic ground state evolves from G-type, A-type, to double spin striped antiferromagnetic states, as well as weak charge and orbital orders, at x=1; These results can greatly promote our understanding of the magnetic properties of Ruddlesden-Popper nickelates, and shed the light of the pairing mechanism of unconventional nickelate superconductors.

Pressure Evolution of Electronic Structures

We also investigated the magnetic ground states at various pressures, these results reveal that the magnetic ground states consistently exhibit an intralayer antiferromagnetic phase with negligible interlayer spin interactions. Both crystallographically distinct Ni atoms within the unit cell demonstrate identical magnetic moments of 1.6
\mu_{B}
at ambient pressure, which gradually decrease under applied pressure to approximately 1.4
\mu_{B}
at 75 GPa. This spin density wave phase devoid of magnetic moment split suggests that La
2
NiO
4
maintains a pure spin-ordered system across all pressure regimes up to 100 GPa, exhibiting neither charge ordering nor orbital ordering.

Figure
3
(a-d) presents the pressure-dependent electronic structures of La
2
NiO
4
in its magnetic ground state. At pressures ranging from 0 to 50 GPa, the band structure consistently reveals an insulating character, characterized by a finite energy gap near the Fermi level. The magnitude of this gap exhibits systematic pressure dependence: initially measuring approximately 1 eV under ambient conditions, it undergoes progressive closure with increasing pressure until to P = 50 GPa, and ultimately transits to a fully metallic state when P = 50-100 GPa. This gradual gap rclosing correlates with a continuous insulator-metal transition, where the material evolves from a gapped insulating state to a metallic regime through intermediate semi-metallic phases.
Notably, the pressure-driven metallization occurs without abrupt structural phase transitions, suggesting a smooth evolution of electronic structure under hydrostatic compression. These findings align with current experimental observations
Ji et al. 2024

Our DFT calculations corroborate this result through partial density of states (PDOS) results. As demonstrated in Fig

The DOS spectra across all pressure regimes consistently exhibit comparable electronic configuration characteristics, with no significant pressure-induced modifications to orbital occupation configurations. The spatial uniformity in both charge distribution and orbital configuration is thereby confirmed through this equivalent electronic occupation pattern at both nickel sites.
The two Ni atoms exhibit identical electronic configurations of (

This behavior arises from the strong on-site Coulomb repulsion, which suppresses higher-energy double occupancy by favoring electron localization. The preserved orbital symmetry under crystal field distortion highlights the dominant role of electronic correlations in governing the orbital occupation hierarchy, as manifested by the absence of orbital polarization between these split energy levels. Such characteristics further corroborate the significant influence of strong correlation effects in stabilizing the observed electronic structure.

Bioconvection driven flow of magnetized micropolar hybrid nanofluid with Ohmic heating and variable thermal conductivity
Authors: Muzher Saleem, Fazal Haq, Hassan Ali Ghazwani, Mounirah Areshi, Jihad Younis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Bioconvection in hybrid nanofluid(HNF) flow arises from the collective, upward swimming of motile microorganisms (bacteria, algae, and fungi) suspended in a base fluid that also contains diverse types of nanoparticles. This phenomenon generates spontaneous, gravity-driven flow patterns such as plumes, cells, or vortices that significantly modify the fluid’s thermal and mass transport characteristics. Understanding this process is vital for advancing applications in biomedical engineering, environmental science, and energy systems. In this study, the dynamics of bioconvective engine oil-based HNF flow by a porous stretched sheet under the impact of magnetic force is investigated. The hybrid nanofluid is formulated by suspending rigid nanoparticles of alumina (Al2O3) and gold (Au) in the base fluid. The micropolar fluid model is chosen for analysis. Influences of heat source effects, magnetic field, dissipation, and thermal radiation are accounted in the formulation. Using the local non-similarity approach, the governing dimensional system is transformed into a set of dimensionless, nonlinear, coupled differential equations. The NDSolve function of Mathematica is used to investigate the influence of diverse variables on HNF thermal, velocity, microrotation, concentration, and motile density profiles. The numerical results reveal that the hybrid nanofluid enhances the surface skin-friction coefficient by up to 10%, the heat transfer rate by approximately 19%, and the motile microorganism transfer rate by nearly 6% compared with the corresponding mono nanofluid. These findings demonstrate the superior thermal and transport performance of engine oil-based hybrid nanofluids, highlighting their potential for advanced heat transfer systems and bio-inspired engineering applications.

Introduction

Micropolar HNFs represent an advanced class of complex fluids in which two dissimilar nanosized solid particles are suspended within a micropolar fluid that accounts for the microstructure and microrotation of fluid elements. Unlike conventional Newtonian/non Newtonian fluids, micropolar fluids incorporate the effects of particle spin, micro-rotation inertia, and couple stresses. These features making them particularly suitable for modeling fluids with internal microstructures such as polymeric solutions, liquid crystals, blood, and certain lubricants. The inclusion of dissimilar nanoparticles significantly improves thermal conductivity, heat transfer rate, and mass transport, while micropolar characteristics provide a more realistic description of shear thickening /thinning behavior and rotational interactions at the microscale. Owing to these collective features, engine oil based micropolar nanofluids have found important applications in thermal management of electronic devices, microelectromechanical systems, biomedical engineering (drug delivery and biofluid modeling), lubrication technology, energy systems, and advanced manufacturing processes [1–5]. The concept of micropolar fluids was first introduced by Eringen [6] to describe fluids with internal microstructure and microrotation effects. Ali et al. [7] have investigated the impact of the magnetic properties of micropolar particles based on a magnetic dipole. Considering joule heating, Gangadhar et al. [8] investigated the impact of nanoparticle shape on a three-dimensional MHD micropolar hybrid nanofluid comprising silver and magnesium oxide in blood. Khader and Sharma [9] conducted a comprehensive investigation of the flow and heat transfer behavior of an MHD micropolar fluid over a stretching sheet, highlighting the effects of heat generation and absorption. Aarathi and Reddy [10] explored the physical aspects of entropy generation caused by a micropolar ternary HNF flow by a vertical stretching cylinder under quadratic thermal radiation and heat source/sink. Recently, many researchers have investigated various aspects of micropolar fluids [11–15].

Hybrid nanofluids represent a new class of engineered heat transfer fluids obtained by suspending two dissimilar rigid nanoparticles in a conventional base fluid, such as water, ethylene glycol, or oil. The synergistic effects arising from the combined nanoparticles markedly improve the thermophysical and transport properties of the suspension, resulting in enhanced thermal performance compared with traditional mono nanofluids. By combining nanoparticles with distinct physical and chemical characteristics such as metal oxides, metals, carbides, or carbon-based constituents, the HNFs synergistically boost thermal conductivity, heat transfer rate, and energy transport efficiency while maintaining acceptable flow characteristics. These fluids also offer improved stability and tunable properties, making them particularly appropriate for high efficiency thermal management devices. As a result, HNFs have gained noteworthy attention in applications comprising cooling of electronic devices, heat exchangers, renewable energy systems, vehicle radiators, and advanced manufacturing processes, where efficient heat management is critical [16–20]. Sachhin et al. [21–23] examined the thermal and flow characteristics of non-Newtonian fluids containing multiple rigid nanoparticles, with particular emphasis on entropy generation (irreversibility), Brownian motion, convective (Biot) boundary conditions, thermal radiation, and viscous dissipation. Their studies demonstrated the significant influence of these physical mechanisms on heat transfer enhancement and overall transport behavior. Mahabaleshwar et al. [24, 25] examined the transport features of heat and mass in ternary HNF flow with Brinkman and porosity effects under the influence of Ohmic heating. Taneja et al. [26] implemented numerical methods and cubic multiple regression to examine the entropy generation aspects in THNF flow confined between a pair of revolving disks. Devi and Kumar [27, 28] explored the significance of Darcy-Forchheimer and couple stress in ternary hybrid nanofluid flow in an asymmetric channel and a stretchable cylinder. Akbar et al. [29–31] investigated kerosene–alumina nanofluids as advanced coolants for semi-cryogenic rocket engines, examining the thermal performance of both non-aggregated and aggregated Al₂O₃ nanoparticles. Iqbal and Abbasi [32] presented a comprehensive numerical investigation of magnetohydrodynamic Reiner–Philippoff nanofluid flow using non-similar transformation technique. Li et al. [33, 34] developed a dynamic magneto-fluid coupling framework for ferrofluid micropumps by integrating Maxwell-based magnetic field computation, nonconformal mesh interpolation, and a time-sequence control strategy to simulate traveling magnetic field-driven pumping. Entropy and thermal optimization in non-Newtonian nanofluid flow containing rigid nanoparticles, under Ohmic and Hall current assumptions, were scrutinized by Iqbal et al. [35, 36]. Agrawal and Kaswan [37, 38] scrutinized the entropy generation and heat transfer improvement in hybrid nanofluid fluid flow by a vertical cylinder and between parallel disks. Refs. [39–43] covers diverse aspects and applications of HNFs in the fields of science and engineering.

Bioconvection in HNF flows arises from the collective motion of motile microorganisms suspended in a base fluid. The microorganisms, typically gyrotactic or oxytactic, swim preferentially in certain directions, leading to density gradients that trigger convective instabilities and self-organized flow patterns. When combined with hybrid nanofluids, bioconvection significantly alters the transport characteristics by enhancing fluid mixing, thermal conductivity, and mass diffusion. The interaction between nanoparticle induced thermal enhancement and microorganism driven convection results in improved heat and mass transfer rates, greater flow stability, and reduced particle agglomeration. Consequently, bioconvective HNF flows have attracted considerable attention for advanced engineering and biomedical applications, including bio-microsystems, energy transport devices, and cooling technologies, where efficient thermal management and stable suspension behavior are essential [44–48]. In fluid flows, Kuznetsov [49, 50] introduced the concept of bioconvection caused by oxytactic and gyrotactic microorganisms and concluded that the bioconvection Rayleigh number decreases the system’s stability. Entropy generation and the impact of Joule dissipation in bioconvective HNF flow by a permeable medium are reported by Tanveer et al. [51]. Khan et al. [52] explored the consequences of radiative thermal conduction and reactions of type homogenous/heterogeneous in bioconvective Maxwell fluid flow. Kaswan et al. [53] presented an intelligent computational analysis of bioconvective magneto-cross nanofluid flow with activation energy and gyrotactic microorganisms via artificial neural networks. References [54–58] examined various aspects of bioconvective HNF flows.

Although significant progress has been made in the analysis of HNF flows, several important challenges remain unresolved. Most existing investigations are confined to similarity solutions and neglect the combined influence of micropolarity, bioconvection, and multiple transport mechanisms. Furthermore, studies involving engine oil-based hybrid nanofluids containing Al₂O₃ and Au nanoparticles under the simultaneous effects of magnetic field, thermal radiation, heat generation, viscous dissipation, and porous stretching surfaces are extremely limited, particularly within a non-similar framework. To address these research gaps, the present work develops a comprehensive mathematical model for the MHD bioconvective flow of a micropolar engine oil-based HNF over a porous stretching sheet. The governing nonlinear equations are transformed using the local non-similarity approach proposed by Minkowycz and Sparrow [59] and then solved numerically using the NDSolve algorithm in Mathematica. The findings provide new physical insight into the coupled momentum, thermal, nanoparticle concentration, microrotation, and microorganism transport mechanisms, thereby contributing to the design and optimization of advanced thermal management systems employing biologically active hybrid nanofluids. Problem formulation

Hybrid nanofluid formation is carried out with the suspension of two distinct types of nanoparticles, alumina and gold. The micropolar fluid model is considered to account the microrotation and spin gradient effects in HNF. Physical aspects of temperature dependent non uniform thermal conductivity, viscous dissipation, thermal radiation, and chemical reaction are accounted. At the stretching surface, the first boundary condition represents the prescribed stretching velocity, while the second indicates the absence of suction or injection through the surface. The third boundary condition specifies the microrotation velocity of the fluid particles, whereas the fourth, fifth, and sixth conditions prescribe the wall temperature, nanoparticle concentration, and motile microorganism density, respectively. At the far-field (ambient) boundary, the first and second conditions indicate that both the fluid velocity and microrotation approach their quiescent ambient values. The remaining conditions specify that the temperature, nanoparticle concentration, and motile microorganism density asymptotically attain their corresponding ambient values. Results and discussion

The transformed dimensionless non-similar system (17–21) with boundary restrictions (22) is solved in the Mathematica package using NDSolve. The NDSolve function in Mathematica is a robust numerical tool for solving strongly nonlinear and coupled differential equations. This parameter characterizes the relative strength of the microrotation (spin) effects of suspended microelements compared with the viscous forces in the fluid. The surface porosity (or permeability) parameter quantifies the ability of the surface to allow fluid suction or injection through it. An increase in surface porosity generally enhances the interaction between the solid boundary and the nanofluid, thereby modifying the momentum transport within the boundary layer. Physically, stronger suction withdraws fluid from the boundary layer, suppressing momentum diffusion and thinning the velocity boundary layer. As a result, the axial velocity decreases, and the flow becomes more stable near the surface. Figure 6 illustrates how the Eckert number affects the temperature profile of mono and hybrid nanofluids. In nanofluid flows, the Eckert number plays a crucial role in shaping the thermal field due to the enhanced viscous effects arising from nanoparticle and fluid interactions. An increase in Ec signifies stronger conversion of kinetic energy into internal energy via viscous dissipation, which acts as a volumetric heat source in the energy equation. Consequently, higher Ec values lead to an increase in the fluid temperature within the boundary layer. The behavior of the thermal conductivity parameter on thermal profile is captured in Fig. 7. This figure shows that the temperature of the mano and hybrid nanofluids rises as its thermal conductivity accelerates. Physically, the \varepsilon represents the temperature dependent heat conduction behavior of nanofluids. An increase in \varepsilon enhances thermal diffusion, thickens the thermal boundary layer, and raises the fluid temperature within the boundary layer. Figure 8 shows that the temperature profile for both mono nanofluid and HNF increases through rising magnetic variable. Physically, the application of an external magnetic field induces Lorentz force that opposes the fluid motion, thereby suppressing the velocity field. This resistive force converts part of the kinetic energy into thermal energy through ohmic dissipation, which directly enhances the thermal field within the boundary layer. Consequently, an increase in the magnetic parameter leads to a thickening of the thermal boundary layer and an upsurge in the fluid temperature for both HNF and mono nanofluid. The effects of Prandtl number on the temperature profile of mono nanofluid and HNF are explored in Fig. 9. The Prandtl number is a key dimensionless parameter in nanofluid/HNF heat transfer, defined as the ratio of momentum diffusivity to thermal diffusivity. A higher Prandtl number corresponds to lower thermal diffusivity, which causes the thermal boundary layer to become thinner. As a result, heat diffusion is weaker compared to momentum diffusion, leading to steeper temperature gradients near the surface and a reduction in fluid temperature away from the wall. Figure 10 illustrates the effect of increasing the heat generation variable on fluid temperature. We observe here that an increase in heat generation accelerates the thermal field of mono nanofluid and HNF. Since an increase in the heat generation parameter intensifies internal thermal energy, leading to a rise in the nanofluid temperature and a thickening of the thermal boundary layer. The relationship between the thermal field of mono nanofluid/HNF and thermal radiation is shown in Fig. 11. As thermal radiation levels are increased, the temperature profile improves. Since higher radiation variable improves the surface heat flux by reducing the mean absorption coefficient. Concluding remarks

This study investigates the steady, incompressible mono nanofluid and hybrid nanofluid flow over a stretched sheet. We utilized Mathematica software to obtain numerical solutions through a local non-similar approach. The results are presented in tables and graphs, which highlight the dynamic characteristics of the boundary layer and detail the complex features of micropolar-based magnetized nanofluid flow near the stretching sheets. The findings significantly enhance our understanding regarding the micropolar-based magnetic nanofluids, particularly in the context of materials science and engineering applications. The main findings of the present study are as follows:The velocity profile for both mono and hybrid nanofluid velocity fields diminished via surface porosity and magnetic variable.The thermal fields of mono and hybrid nanofluid upsurge against Eckert number, heat source, variable thermal conductivity, thermal radiation, and magnetic variable, while they decrease with increasing inputs of Prandtl number.The fluid concentration reduced for both mono and hybrid nanofluids as the chemical reaction and Schmidt number are increased.HNF exhibits a surface roughness force that is up to 10 percent greater than that of mono nanofluids.The influence of HNF on heat transfer is up to 19 percent greater than that of mono nanofluid.The density transfer rate of HNF is up to 6 percent higher than that of mono nanofluid.

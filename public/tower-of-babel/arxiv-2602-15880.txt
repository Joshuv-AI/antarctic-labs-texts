Abstract




Abstract.

Nonnegative sparse signal recovery has been extensively studied due to its broad applications. Recent work has integrated rectified linear unit (ReLU) techniques to enhance existing recovery algorithms. We merge Newton-type thresholding with ReLU-based approaches to propose two algorithms: Newton-Direction-Based ReLU-Thresholding (NDRT) and its enhanced variant, Newton-Direction-Based ReLU-Thresholding Pursuit (NDRTP). Theoretical analysis iindicates that both algorithms can guarantee exact recovery of nonnegative sparse signals when the measurement matrix satisfies a certain condition.. Numerical experiments demonstrate NDRTP achieves competitive performance compared to several existing methods in both noisy and noiseless scenarios.

Key words:
Nonnegative sparse signal recovery, (ReLU), Newton-type method,
Thresholding method, Singular values.

Introduction

A fundamental task of compressive sensing is to recover (or reconstruct) the sparse signal from limited measurements.
In many situations, the signals are not only sparse but also nonnegative. In such cases, one need to handle the nonnegative sparse signal recovery, which has been widely studied in various areas such as geoscience and remote sensing
[
13
,
5
]
,
medical imaging
[
21
]
and face recognition
[
14
,
27
,
11
,
1
]
.

Given a measurement matrix
{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
m\ll n
, the inaccurate measurements with noise
{\bm{e}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
of a nonnegative sparse signal

{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are obtained as

{\bm{y}}={\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}+{\bm{e}}.

(1)

The nonnegative sparse-signal-recovery problem is concerning about the recovery of
{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
from the acquired measurements
{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
which are significantly smaller than the signal length. This problem can be formulated as the following sparse optimization problem with a nonnegative constraint:

\min_{{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|{{\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}^{2}\ :\ \left\|{{\bm{x}}}\right\|_{0}\leq k,{\bm{x}}\geq\bf{0}\right\},

(2)

where
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{0}
is the ’
\ell_{0}
-norm’ counting the number of nonzero entries.
It has been shown in
[
7
,
32
]
that the exact recovery of nonnegative sparse signals can be achieved through solving an
\ell_{1}
-regularized nonnegative least squares (NNLS) problem. The classical active-set algorithm proposed by Lawson and Hanson
[
17
]
is one of the efficient methods for such optimization problems. Their algorithm for NNLS has become a fundamental tool in nonnegative sparse recovery due to its guaranteed convergence to an optimal solution to the problem.
Various algorithms based on NNLS have been introduced to solve (
1
), such as hard thresholding NNLS
[
24
,
25
]
, sparse NNLS
[
23
]
, and nonnegative orthogonal least squares
[
19
]
.
Nonnegative orthogonal matching pursuit (NNOMP)
[
3
]
refines the selected coefficients by solving NNLS subproblems. Later, a fast implementation of NNOMP called fast NNOMP
[
28
]
was proposed.
Several nonnegative variants of the standard greedy sparse-signal-recovery algorithms have been introduced in
[
15
]
, including compressive sampling matching pursuit, subspace pursuit, and hard thresholding pursuit.
For convenience of discussion in this paper, we refer to the nonnegative variant of subspace pursuit as Nonnegative Subspace Pursuit (NNSP).
It has been shown that nonnegative sparse signals can be exactly recovered via
\ell_{1}
-minimization if the null space of the measurement matrix satisfies a certain condition
[
16
,
29
]
. In addition, Zhao
[
32
,
31
]
introduced the Range Space Property of transposed matrix to characterize the uniform recovery of nonnegative sparse signals, offering a complementary perspective to traditional null space analysis.

The rectified linear unit (ReLU) function was first introduced by Fukushima’s neocognitron model
[
9
]
, inspired by biological neurons’ activation thresholds. It gained prominence in 2010s with deep learning breakthroughs, replacing Sigmoid/Tanh due to its sparsity induction and vanishing gradient mitigation
[
20
]
.
ReLU enforces non-negativity and sparsity by zeroing negative inputs, making it ideal for recovering non-negative sparse signals. Its computational efficiency and gradient stability accelerate training and improve convergence in deep networks for signal reconstruction.
Recently, some algorithms combining the ReLU function with hard thresholding have been proposed for nonnegative signal recovery, such as the ReLU-based hard thresholding (RHT)
[
10
]
, momentum-boosted adaptive thresholding
[
12
]
and ReLU-based hard thresholding pursuit (RHTP)
[
22
]
.

The Newton-type method is a powerful numerical method for optimization problems, which exhibits local quadratic convergence and often achieves high-accuracy solutions using far fewer iterations compared to the first-order methods. Recently, it has been extended to solve the sparse-signal-recovery problem (see, e.g.,
[
18
]
). The basic idea to extend the classic Newton method to sparse signal recovery is to apply a certain thresholding such as the hard thresholding to an iterate generated by the Newton search direction. Specifically, they take the following iterative scheme:

{\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}=\mathcal{H}_{k}\left({\bm{x}}^{(p)}+\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}({\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}^{(p)})\right),

(3)

where
{\bm{x}}^{(p)}
is the current iterate,
{\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}
is the next iterate,
\lambda>0
is the stepsize,
\varepsilon>0
is a regularization parameter introduced to avoid singularity of the matrix, and
\mathcal{H}_{k}(\cdot)
is the hard thresholding operator which keeps the
k
largest-magnitude entries of a vector and zeros its remaining entries.
Following this scheme,
[
18
]
presents two novel algorithms: Newton-Step-Based Iterative Hard Thresholding (NSIHT) and Newton-Step-Based Hard Thresholding Pursuit (NSHTP), which are empirically demonstrated to be effective and stable.
However, their convergence analysis requires
\varepsilon>\sigma_{1}^{2}
, where
\sigma_{1}
is the largest singular value of the measurement matrix
{\bm{A}}
. When
\sigma_{1}
is large, this constraint obliges
\varepsilon
to be large as well, so
{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}}
can not be regarded as a small perturbation of the true Hessian
{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}
of the objective function in (
2
).
This may compromise the advantages of Newton’s method by preventing it from fully leveraging the Hessian information.

For nonnegative sparse signal recovery, we propose the so-called Newton-Direction-Based ReLU-Thresholding (NDRT) algorithm, which is a modification of the framework in (
3
) through merging the ReLU operation, Newton step and hard thresholding. By further incorporating a nonnegative projection step, we develop the so-called Newton-Direction-Based ReLU-Thresholding Pursuit (NDRTP). Furthermore, our convergence analysis eliminates the restrictive condition
\varepsilon>\sigma_{1}^{2}
used in
[
18
]
, and hence our method allows
\varepsilon
to be an arbitrarily small positive number. The main contribution of the paper includes:

•

By integrating the Newton direction, ReLU thresholding and hard thresholding, we propose NDRT and its enhanced variant NDRTP for the nonnegative sparse signal recovery problem (
2
); see Section 2 for details.

•

Under certain assumptions on the measurement matrix, we develop sufficient conditions to ensure the guranteed performance of the proposed algorithms in nonnegative sparse signal recovery. The main theoretical guarantees are presented in Theorems
3.2
and
3.5
.
Our spectral-decomposition-based analysis allows the algorithmic parameter
\varepsilon
to be small, so that
{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}}
a truly slight perturbation of the Hessian
{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}
.

•

Leveraging the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions, we establish a fundamental error bound associated with nonnegative projection problem, summarized in Theorem
3.4
, which plays a crucial role in establishing the guaranteed performance of NDRTP.

•

Through simulations, we compare the performance of the proposed algorithms with several existing algorithms in nonnegative-sparse-signal recovery. Moreover, we propose a very helpful empirical formula for the iterative stepsize
\lambda
in NDRTP.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces some fundamental inequalities and outlines our algorithms. Section 3 performs a theoretical analysis of the algorithms. Section 4 presents some numerical results, and Section 5 concludes the paper.

2.2
Algorithms

The Hessian matrix of the objective function in (
2
) is
{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}
,
which is singular because
{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
m\ll n
.
To address this issue, the Newton-step-based hard-thresholding algorithms proposed in
[
18
]
employ a modified Hessian
{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}}

\varepsilon>0

However, the iterates

2

k
-sparse vectors. This is achieved by retaining the largest
k
nonnegative entries and setting all other entries to zero, thereby yielding a
k
-sparse nonnegative vector at every step.
Thus we obtain the following iterative scheme:

{\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}=\mathcal{H}_{k}\left(\Psi\left({\bm{x}}^{(p)}+\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}({\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}^{(p)})\right)\right),

(8)

where
\Psi(\cdot)
is the ReLU function.
A direct implementation of this scheme leads to the NDRT algorithm,
which is summarized in
Algorithm
1

Algorithm
2

3
Analysis of Algorithms

In this section, we perform a theoretical analysis to establish the guaranteed performance results for the proposed algorithms under certain conditions. We first improve upon a fundamental lemma from
[
18
]
, originally derived under the restrictive condition
\varepsilon>\sigma_{1}^{2}=\|{{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}}\|_{2}
, which ensures convergence of the matrix power series:

\left({\bm{I}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}\right)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}})^{j}.

This requirement on
\varepsilon
is limiting both theoretically and practically. The present work removes this restriction entirely through an alternative analysis based on the following spectral property: for any real symmetric matrix
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n},
and any rational function
q
for which
q(\mathbf{M})
is well-defined, the eigenvalues of
q(\mathbf{M})
are precisely
q(\lambda_{1}),\dots,q(\lambda_{n}).

We now state the improved version of Lemma 3.2 in
[
18
]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
be a measurement matrix with
m\ll n
and
\varepsilon>0
be a given parameter.
Denote the largest and smallest singular values of
{\bm{A}}
by
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{m}
, respectively.
Given an index set
\Omega\subseteq[n]
and vectors
{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

let

be a positive parameter such that
\lambda\leq\sigma_{m}^{2}+\varepsilon

Then

|\langle{\bm{u}},({\bm{I}}-\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}){\bm{v}}\rangle|\leq\left(\delta_{k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon}\right)\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2}\|{{\bm{v}}}\|_{2}

if

,

Proof.

Let us start by defining the function

q(t):=\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{t+\varepsilon}\right)t,\quad t\geq 0.

Since
{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}}

the matrix

\begin{split}|\langle{{\bm{u}}},{({\bm{I}}-\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}){\bm{v}}}\rangle|&=|\langle{{\bm{u}}},{({\bm{I}}-{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+q({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}})){\bm{v}}}\rangle|\\

&\leq\,\delta_{k}\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2}\|{{\bm{v}}}\|_{2}+\|{q({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}})}\|_{2}\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2}\|{{\bm{v}}}\|_{2},\end{split}

(12)

where the first term of the final inequality follows from (
6

|\operatorname{supp}({\bm{u}})\cup\operatorname{supp}({\bm{v}})|\leq k

We now examine the norm of

Let
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{m}

{\bm{A}}

Since

As a result, the eigenvalues of the matrix

It follows from (

\displaystyle\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2}^{2}

Dividing both sides by

{\bm{u}}\neq\bm{0}
, otherwise the inequality (
11
) holds trivially), we obtain

\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2}\leq\left(\delta_{s}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon}\right)\|{{\bm{v}}}\|_{2}.

This is exactly the inequality (
11
), as desired.
∎

We now establish the main performance result for NDRT in noisy settings.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
{\bm{y}}={\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}+{\bm{e}}
, where
{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a measurement matrix with
m\ll n
,

{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a nonnegative
k
-sparse signal and
{\bm{e}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a noise vector.
Let
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{m}
be the largest and smallest singular values of
{\bm{A}}
, respectively.
Suppose that
{\bm{A}}
satisfies that

\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\sigma_{m}^{2}<\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}\approx 0.618.

(13)

For any given
\varepsilon>0
, let the stepsize
\lambda
be taken such that

\sigma_{m}^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\varepsilon\leq\lambda\leq\sigma_{m}^{2}+\varepsilon.

(14)

Then the sequence
\{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}\}
generated by
NDRT
satisfies

\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\leq\alpha^{p}\|{{\bm{x}}^{(0)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\frac{\gamma}{1-\alpha}\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2},

(15)

where

\alpha:=\phi\left(\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon}\right)<1,\qquad\gamma:=\frac{\phi\lambda\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}+\varepsilon},

(16)

with
\phi
being the golden ratio.
In particular, if
{\bm{e}}=\bf{0}
, then
\{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}\}
converges to
{\bm{x}}
.

Proof.

For notational simplicity, we denote
{\bm{u}}^{(p+1)}
in NDRT by
{\bm{u}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle={\bm{x}}^{(p)}+\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}({\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}^{(p)})

\displaystyle={\bm{x}}^{(p)}+\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}({\bm{A}}({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{(p)})+{\bm{e}})

\displaystyle=({\bm{I}}-\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}})({\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}})+{\bm{x}}+\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}}.

Let

{\bm{g}}:=\left({\bm{I}}-\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}\right)({\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}}),\quad{\bm{h}}:=\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}}.

Then
{\bm{u}}={\bm{g}}+{\bm{x}}+{\bm{h}}
.
For any given
\Omega\subseteq[n]
, we have

\displaystyle\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{({\bm{u}}^{+}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}+({\bm{u}}^{-})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{({\bm{u}}^{+}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{({\bm{u}}^{-})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}^{2}+2\left\langle{({\bm{u}}^{+}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}},{({\bm{u}}^{-})_{\Omega}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\|{({\bm{u}}^{+}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}^{2},

(17)

where the inequality is obtained from

\left\langle{({\bm{u}}^{+}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}},{({\bm{u}}^{-})_{\Omega}}\right\rangle=\left\langle{({\bm{u}}^{+})_{\Omega}},{({\bm{u}}^{-})_{\Omega}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{{\bm{x}}_{\Omega}},{({\bm{u}}^{-})_{\Omega}}\right\rangle\geq 0,

which follows from the nonnegativity of
{\bm{x}}
. Now, let

\Omega:=\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}}^{(p+1)})\cup\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}})=\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}_{k}({\bm{u}}^{+}))\cup\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}}).

Then, we have

\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}=\|{\mathcal{H}_{k}({\bm{u}}^{+})-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\leq\phi\|{({\bm{u}}^{+}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}\leq\phi\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}=\phi\|{\left({\bm{g}}+{\bm{h}}\right)_{\Omega}}\|_{2},

(18)

where the first and second inequalities follow from (
7
) and (
3
), respectively.
Note that
\lambda\leq\sigma_{m}^{2}+\varepsilon
and

|\Omega\cup\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}})|\leq|\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}}^{(p+1)})\cup\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}})\cup\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}}^{(p)})|\leq 3k.

By (
11
), we have

\|{{\bm{g}}_{{}_{\Omega}}}\|_{2}=\left\|\left(\left({\bm{I}}-\lambda({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}\right)({\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}})\right)_{\Omega}\right\|_{2}\leq\left(\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon}\right)\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}.

(19)

Next we determine the upper bound of
\|{{\bm{h}}_{\Omega}}\|_{2}
. To this end, we first examine the norm of
({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}
.
Suppose
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{m}
are all singular values of
{\bm{A}}
. Then
{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}
has eigenvalues

\sigma_{1}^{2},\cdots,\sigma_{m}^{2}
along with additional
(n-m)
zeros. Define a function

Q(t):=\frac{t}{(t+\varepsilon)^{2}},\quad t\geq 0.

Then the matrix
(({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}){(({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!})}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}=Q({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}})
has eigenvalues

Q(\sigma_{i}^{2})=\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{(\sigma_{i}^{2}+\varepsilon)^{2}},\quad i=1,\cdots,m,

with additional
(n-m)
zeros. Thus, we obtain

\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}}\|_{2}=\max_{i\in[m]}\sqrt{Q(\sigma_{i}^{2})}=\max_{i\in[m]}\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}+\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}+\varepsilon},

where the final inequality is ensured by
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{m}
. It follows that

\begin{split}\|{{\bm{h}}_{{}_{\Omega}}}\|_{2}&\leq\left\|{\bm{h}}\right\|_{2}=\lambda\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}\\
&\leq\lambda\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}}\|_{2}\,\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}\\
&\leq\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}+\varepsilon}\,\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

(20)

Merging (
18
), (
19
) and (
20
) together, we obtain

\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\leq\phi\left(\|{{\bm{g}}_{{}_{\Omega}}}\|_{2}+\|{{\bm{h}}_{{}_{\Omega}}}\|_{2}\right)\\
\leq\alpha\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\gamma\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2},

(21)

where
\alpha
and
\gamma
are specified in (
16
).

Under the conditions of the theorem, we now show that
0\leq\alpha<1
, i.e.,

0\leq\phi\left(\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\varepsilon+\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)<1.

The nonnegativity
\alpha\geq 0
follows directly from
\lambda\leq\sigma_{m}^{2}+\varepsilon
and
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{m}
.
To ensure
\alpha<1
, it suffices to require

\lambda>\frac{\left(\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}.

This is guaranteed by conditions (
14
) and (
13
):

\lambda\geq\sigma_{m}^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\varepsilon=\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}>\frac{\left(\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}.

Having established that
0\leq\alpha<1
, we may recursively apply the inequality (
21
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p-1)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\gamma\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{p}\|{{\bm{x}}^{(0)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\frac{1-\alpha^{p}}{1-\alpha}\gamma\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}

(22)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{p}\|{{\bm{x}}^{(0)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\frac{\gamma}{1-\alpha}\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}.

Clearly,
\{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}\}
converges to
{\bm{x}}
if
{\bm{e}}=\bm{0}
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Our analysis and main result established above is significantly different from those in
[
18
]
. The convergence result of NSIHT in
[
18
]
(Theorem 3.4 therein) requires that

{\bm{A}}
satisfy
\delta_{3k}<1/\sqrt{3}
and the parameter
\varepsilon
satisfy

\varepsilon>\max\left\{\sigma_{1}^{2},\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}-\sigma_{m}^{2}}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}-\delta_{3k}}-1\right)\sigma_{1}^{2}\right\}.

(23)

However, as shown in Theorem
3.2
, the original restriction on
\varepsilon
such as (
23
) can be entirely removed under the RIP condition (
13
). This means the parameter
\varepsilon
can set to be any small positive number. In this case, the term
{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}}
can be interpreted as a perturbation of the Hessian
{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}
, and hence
({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}}+\varepsilon{\bm{I}})^{-1}{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}({\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}})
can be seen as a truly
modified Newton direction. Similarly, our analysis here can also be used to improve the convergence result of NSHTP in
[
18
]
, i.e., Theorem 4.3 therein.

Next, we investigate the guaranteed performance of NDRTP in noisy settings.
Before stating the main results, we first establish a fundamental property of nonnegative projection, which plays a critical role in the subsequent analysis.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
{\bm{y}}={\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}+{\bm{e}}
be noisy measurements of a nonnegative
k
-sparse signal
{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
where the measurement matrix
{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
satisfies the RIP of order
2k
, and
{\bm{e}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
a noise vector.
For any index set
\Lambda\subseteq[n]
with
|\Lambda|\leq k
, denote

{\bm{z}}^{*}:=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{{\bm{z}}}\left\{\|{{\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{z}}}\|_{2}:\operatorname{supp}({\bm{z}})\subseteq\Lambda,{\bm{z}}\geq\bm{0}\right\}.

Then

\|{{\bm{z}}^{*}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}_{2k}}}\|{({\bm{z}}^{*}-{\bm{x}})_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta_{k}}}{1-\delta_{2k}}\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}.

Proof.

Note that the constraint of
{\bm{z}}^{*}
can be written equivalently as

z_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in\Lambda
and
z_{j}=0
for all
j\in\overline{\Lambda}
.
So,
{\bm{z}}^{*}
is the optimal solution to the constrained minimization problem:

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle\quad f({\bm{z}}):=\frac{1}{2}\|{{\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{z}}}\|_{2}^{2}

s.t.

\displaystyle\quad z_{i}\geq 0~\text{for all}~i\in\Lambda,

\displaystyle\quad z_{j}=0~\text{for all}~j\in\overline{\Lambda}.

The corresponding Lagrangian function is given by

\mathcal{L}({\bm{z}},\bm{\mu}):=f({\bm{z}})-\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\mu_{i}z_{i}-\sum_{j\in\overline{\Lambda}}\mu_{j}z_{j},

where
\bm{\mu}={(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the vector of Lagrange multipliers.
According to the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker optimality conditions, there exists a vector

\bm{\mu}^{*}={(\mu^{*}_{1},\cdots,\mu^{*}_{n})}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\nabla f({\bm{z}}^{*}))_{i}-\mu^{*}_{i}=0,&&i\in\Lambda,\\
&(\nabla f({\bm{z}}^{*}))_{j}-\mu^{*}_{j}=0,&&j\in\overline{\Lambda},\\
&z^{*}_{i}\geq 0,&&i\in\Lambda,\\
&z^{*}_{j}=0,&&j\in\overline{\Lambda},\\
&\mu^{*}_{i}\geq 0,\ \mu^{*}_{i}z_{i}^{*}=0,&&i\in{\Lambda}.\end{aligned}\right.

(24)

Let
\Lambda_{1}:=\operatorname{supp}({\bm{z}}^{*})\subseteq\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{2}:=\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{1}
.
By the complementary slackness condition in (
24
), we have
\mu_{i}^{*}=0
for all
i\in\Lambda_{1}
, i.e.,

(\bm{\mu}^{*})_{\Lambda_{1}}=\bm{0}
.
Combining the stationarity condition and dual feasibility in (
24
) leads to

(\nabla f({\bm{z}}^{*}))_{\Lambda}=(\bm{\mu}^{*})_{\Lambda}\geq\bm{0}
. Therefore, we have

(\nabla(f({\bm{z}}^{*})))_{\Lambda_{1}}=(\bm{\mu}^{*})_{\Lambda_{1}}=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{and}\quad(\nabla(f({\bm{z}}^{*})))_{\Lambda_{2}}=(\bm{\mu}^{*})_{\Lambda_{2}}\geq\mathbf{0}.

It follows that

\|{({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*})_{\Lambda_{1}}}\|_{2}=\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}+\nabla f({\bm{z}}^{*})\right)_{\Lambda_{1}}}\|_{2},

\|{({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*})_{\Lambda_{2}}}\|_{2}\leq\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}+\nabla f({\bm{z}}^{*})\right)_{\Lambda_{2}}}\|_{2},

where the inequality holds due to
({\bm{z}}^{*})_{\Lambda_{2}}=\bm{0}
and

{\bm{x}}\geq\bm{0}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\Lambda_{1}}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\Lambda_{2}}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}+\nabla f({\bm{z}}^{*})\right)_{\Lambda_{1}}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}+\nabla f({\bm{z}}^{*})\right)_{\Lambda_{2}}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}+\nabla f({\bm{z}}^{*})\right)_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}.

As
\nabla f({\bm{z}}^{*})={{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}({\bm{A}}{\bm{z}}^{*}-{\bm{y}})
, where
{\bm{y}}={\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}+{\bm{e}}
, the inequality above becomes

\displaystyle\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}+{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}({\bm{A}}{\bm{z}}^{*}-{\bm{y}}))_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{(({\bm{I}}-{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}})({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*})-{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{(({\bm{I}}-{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}})({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}))_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}-2\left\langle{(({\bm{I}}-{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}})({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}))_{\Lambda}},{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|{(({\bm{I}}-{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}})({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}))_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|{(({\bm{I}}-{{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{A}})({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}))_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}\,\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{2k}^{2}\|{{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}+2\delta_{2k}\|{{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}}\|_{2}\,\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2},

where the final inequality is obtained from (
5
) with
|\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*})\cup\Lambda|\leq 2k
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|{{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{2k}^{2}\|{{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}+2\delta_{2k}\|{{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}}\|_{2}\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}+\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\|_{2}^{2},

which implies that

(1-\delta_{2k}^{2})\|{{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}}\|_{2}^{2}-2\delta_{2k}\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}\|{{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}}\|_{2}-\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}-\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 0.

The above inequality amounts to
P(\|{{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}}\|_{2})\leq 0
, where
P(t)
is the quadratic function in variable
t
defined by

P(t):=(1-\delta_{2k}^{2})t^{2}-2\delta_{2k}\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}t-\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}-\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\|_{2}^{2}.

This implies that
\|{{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}}\|_{2}
is less than the largest root of
P(t)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|{{\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta_{2k}\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}+\sqrt{\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}^{2}+(1-\delta_{2k}^{2})\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\|_{2}^{2}}}{1-\delta_{2k}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\delta_{2k})\|{({{\bm{A}}}^{\hskip-1.63885pt\top\!}{\bm{e}})_{\Lambda}}\|_{2}+\sqrt{1-\delta_{2k}^{2}}\|{\left({\bm{x}}-{\bm{z}}^{*}\right)_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\|_{2}}{1-\delta_{2k}^{2}}.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}_{2k}}}\|{({\bm{z}}^{*}-{\bm{x}})_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta_{k}}}{1-\delta_{2k}}\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2},

where the final inequality follows from (
4
) with
|\Lambda|\leq k
, we thereby complete the proof.
∎

Based on Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
, we now further analyze the guaranteed performance of NDRTP. The proof of the main result here is inspired by the approach in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
{\bm{y}}={\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}+{\bm{e}}
, where
{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is the measurement matrix,

{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a nonnegative
k
-sparse signal and
{\bm{e}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a noise vector.
Let
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{m}
be the largest and smallest singular values of the matrix
{\bm{A}}
.
Suppose
{\bm{A}}
satisfies that

\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\sigma_{m}^{2}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\approx 0.577.

(25)

Let
\varepsilon
be a positive number and let the stepsize
\lambda
be given by (
14
), i.e.,

\sigma_{m}^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\varepsilon\leq\lambda\leq\sigma_{m}^{2}+\varepsilon.

Then the sequence
\left\{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}\right\}
generated by
NDRTP
satisfies

\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\leq\rho^{p}\|{{\bm{x}}^{(0)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\frac{\tau}{1-\rho}\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2},

(26)

where

\rho=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-\delta_{2k}^{2}}}\left(\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon}\right)<1

and

\tau=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-\delta_{2k}^{2}}}\,\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta_{k}}}{1-\delta_{2k}}.

In particular, if
{\bm{e}}=\bf{0}
, then the sequence
\{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}\}
converges to
{\bm{x}}
.

Proof.

Let
{\bm{u}}
denote the positive part of
{\bm{u}}^{(p+1)}
in NDRTP, i.e.,
{\bm{u}}:=\Psi({\bm{u}}^{(p+1)})=({\bm{u}}^{(p+1)})^{+}
.
Then
S^{(p+1)}=\mathcal{L}_{k}({\bm{u}})
. Thus,

\begin{split}\|{{\bm{u}}_{S\setminus S^{(p+1)}}}\|_{2}&=\|{{\bm{u}}_{S\setminus(S\cap S^{(p+1)})}}\|_{2}\leq\|{{\bm{u}}_{S^{(p+1)}\setminus(S\cap S^{(p+1)})}}\|_{2}\\
&=\|{{\bm{u}}_{S^{(p+1)}\setminus S}}\|_{2}=\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{S^{(p+1)}\setminus S}}\|_{2},\end{split}

(27)

where
S=\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}})
.
Since
\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}}^{(p+1)})\subseteq S^{(p+1)}
in NDRTP, we obtain

\|{({\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}-{\bm{x}})_{\overline{S^{(p+1)}}}}\|_{2}=\|{{\bm{x}}_{S\setminus S^{(p+1)}}}\|_{2}\leq\|{{\bm{u}}_{S\setminus S^{(p+1)}}}\|_{2}+\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{S\setminus S^{(p+1)}}}\|_{2}.

(28)

Combining (
27
) with (
28
), we have

\begin{split}\|{({\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}-{\bm{x}})_{\overline{S^{(p+1)}}}}\|_{2}&\leq\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{S^{(p+1)}\setminus S}}\|_{2}+\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{S\setminus S^{(p+1)}}}\|_{2}\\
&\leq\sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{S^{(p+1)}\setminus S}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{S\setminus S^{(p+1)}}}\|_{2}^{2}}\\
&=\sqrt{2}\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2},\end{split}

(29)

where
\Omega:=(S\setminus S^{(p+1)})\cup(S^{(p+1)}\setminus S)
.
Evidently, the inequality (
3
) remains valid under our current definitions of

{\bm{u}}
,
{\bm{x}}
and
\Omega
; that is,

\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}=\|{(({\bm{u}}^{(p+1)})^{+}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}\leq\|{({\bm{u}}^{(p+1)}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}.

With
{\bm{g}}
and
{\bm{h}}
defined in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we have

{\bm{u}}^{(p+1)}={\bm{g}}+{\bm{x}}+{\bm{h}}
and

\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}\leq\|{{\bm{g}}_{\Omega}}\|_{2}+\|{{\bm{h}}_{\Omega}}\|_{2}\leq\left(\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\varepsilon+\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}}{\varepsilon+\sigma_{m}^{2}}\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2},

(30)

where the final inequality follows from (
19
) and (
20
).
By substituting
{\bm{z}}^{*}={\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}
and
\Lambda=S^{(p+1)}
into Theorem
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{2k}^{2}}}\|{({\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}-{\bm{x}})_{\overline{S^{(p+1)}}}}\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta_{k}}}{1-\delta_{2k}}\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}.

Combining the above result with
(
29
) and (
30
) yields

\begin{split}\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p+1)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}&\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-\delta_{2k}^{2}}}\|{({\bm{u}}-{\bm{x}})_{\Omega}}\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta_{k}}}{1-\delta_{2k}}\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}\\
&\leq\rho\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\tau\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2},\end{split}

(31)

where
\rho
and
\tau
are given exactly as in Theorem
3.5
.
The condition
\lambda\leq\sigma_{m}^{2}+\varepsilon\leq\sigma_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon
implies
\rho\geq 0
.
Applying an analysis similar to that in (
3
), we obtain

\|{{\bm{x}}^{(p)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\leq\rho^{p}\|{{\bm{x}}^{(0)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\frac{1-\rho^{p}}{1-\rho}\tau\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}\leq\rho^{p}\|{{\bm{x}}^{(0)}-{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\frac{\tau}{1-\rho}\|{{\bm{e}}}\|_{2}.

We now prove that

\rho=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-\delta_{2k}^{2}}}\left(\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\varepsilon+\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)<1.

By (
25
) and
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{m}
, we have
\delta_{3k}<1/\sqrt{3}
. This together with the monotonicity property
\delta_{2k}\leq\delta_{3k}
implies that

\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-\delta_{2k}^{2}}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-\delta_{3k}^{2}}}<\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-(1/\sqrt{3})^{2}}}=\sqrt{3}.

To ensure
\rho<1
, it is sufficient to show that

\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{\lambda\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\varepsilon+\sigma_{1}^{2}}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}},

which amounts to

\lambda>\frac{(\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})(\varepsilon+\sigma_{1}^{2})}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}.

This is guaranteed by (
25
) together with the lower bound of
\lambda
in (
14
). In fact,

\lambda\geq\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\varepsilon+\sigma_{m}^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}(\varepsilon+\sigma_{1}^{2})}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}>\frac{\left(\delta_{3k}+\sigma_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)(\varepsilon+\sigma_{1}^{2})}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Parameter configurations of NDRT and NDRTP

NDRT is observed to be highly sensitive to the stepsize
\lambda

\varepsilon

Experiment results show that
\varepsilon=0.1

Thus, we adopt

According to Theorem

, determining the stepsize

requires computing the largest and smallest singular values of the measurement matrix

, which increases the computational burden. Based on this theorem, setting the stepsize to the square of the largest singular value is a reasonable choice. However, according to random matrix theory

\lambda=\left\lceil\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\right)^{2}\right\rceil.

(33)

Numerical experiments demonstrate that such an empirical formula for stepsize indeed yields satisfactory performance of our algorithms.

\lambda

\lambda

and
n=2000
into the empirical formula (
33

\lambda=8
, which matches the observed optimum, validating the effectiveness of this empirical formula.

5
Conclusion

Two novel algorithms integrating ReLU activation with Newton-type thresholding for nonnegative sparse signal recovery were proposed. The convergence of the proposed algorithms was established under joint conditions on the restricted isometry constant, measurement matrix singular values, and stepsize selection. Simulations demonstrate NDRTP achieves superior recovery performance compared to several existing methods, despite higher per-iteration computational cost. At higher sparsity levels, NDRTP also exhibits faster runtime than NDRT, NNOMP, and NNSP, and all algorithms except NNOMP remain relatively stable when the measurements are slightly inaccurate.

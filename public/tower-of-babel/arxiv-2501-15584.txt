Abstract
. In this paper we give an explicit and calculable algebraic model for the block of rational G G -spectra on full subgroups when G G has identity component a 2-torus 𝕋 \mathbb{T} , and component group of order 2 acting non-trivially on H 1 ​ ( 𝕋 ) H_{1}(\mathbb{T}) . The example of particular interest is the normalizer of the maximal torus in U ⁡ ( 2 ) U(2) , which constitutes one of the most complicated blocks in the analysis of S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) . This builds on the determination of subgroups up to conjugacy in [ 6 ] .

1.A.
Context

For a compact Lie group
G

[
3
]
that the category of
rational
G

objects of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}(G)
. In this paper we make

\mathcal{A}(G)
explicit for some 2-dimensional groups
G

have identity component a torus.

Toral groups

1\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}\longrightarrow G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}W\longrightarrow 1

are classified by the
\mathbb{Z}W
-module
\Lambda_{0}=H_{1}(\mathbb{T})
and an extension
class. The rational representation
\Lambda_{0}^{\mathbb{Q}}=H_{1}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{Q})
describes the
general shape. We are concerned with the special case that
\mathbb{T}
is a
2-torus and
W
is of order 2, so there are three possibilities:

\mathbb{Q}^{2},\mathbb{Q}\oplus\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}

extensions of a torus, and the last case has no fixed circles; these
are more straightforward and covered elsewhere
[
11
,
12
]
.

The present paper deals with the case

\Lambda_{0}^{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}
. In this case we have either

\Lambda_{0}=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}
or
\Lambda_{0}=\mathbb{Z}W
. Since
H^{2}(W;\mathbb{Z}\oplus\tilde{\mathbb{Z}})
is of order 2, there are two groups of the first type (namely

G\cong O(2)\times T
or
G\cong Pin(2)\times T
), and since
H^{2}(W;\mathbb{Z}W)=0

the second case is automatically split, with
G\cong T^{2}\rtimes C_{2}
and
C_{2}
exchanging the factors.
The present paper proves the conjecture in these three mixed cases of rank 2.

1.B.
The shape of the model

In general the abelian category
\mathcal{A}(G)

the space

of

G

[
5

the h-topology (the quotient of the Hausdorff metric topology on
\mathrm{Sub}(G)

closed sets in the Zariski topology are the sets that are h-closed and
closed under passage to cotoral subgroups
1

K

H

H

(K)

representative subgroups.

We note that every subgroup
H

identity component
\mathbb{T}

\pi H=W

\mathfrak{X}_{G}=\mathcal{V}^{G}_{1}\amalg\mathcal{V}^{G}_{W}

\mathcal{V}^{G}_{1}=\mathrm{Sub}(\mathbb{T})/W\mbox{ and }\mathcal{V}^{G}=\{(H)\;|\;H\mbox{ is full

This means

\mathcal{A}(G)=\mathcal{A}(G|\mathfrak{X}_{G})=\mathcal{A}(G|\mathcal{V}^{G}_{1})\times\mathcal{A}(G|\mathcal{V}^{G}_{W}).

Since the toral part

2

The spaces

their general structures are the same. In all three cases the dimension comes half from limits of subgroups
with finite Weyl groups and half from a cotoral inclusion.
In all three cases

We are dealing with three isomorphism classes of groups: two with toral

2.C.
The Thomason filtration

We are considering the space of full subgroups of
G
.
The pattern is visible from the pictures we have drawn.
The group
G
is the unique point of height 2,
there are three lines of height 1, two vertical cotoral lines on the
right and horizontal one at the top that his purely topological.
The height 0 points can be arranged in lines below
the points with finite Weyl groups, and form a 2 dimensional panel.

Lemma 2.1
.

The Thomason height of a subgroup
H
is equal to the dimension of
H
.
The subgroups with finite
Weyl groups are precisely those containing
Z
.

The space subgroups of Thomason height 1 is partitioned into

\mathrm{Th}_{1}^{+}:=\mathsf{V}Z\setminus\mathsf{V}G=\{H\;|\;Z\subseteq H\neq G\}\mbox{
and }\mathrm{Th}_{1}^{-}=\mathsf{V}\tilde{T}\setminus\mathsf{V}G:=\{H\;|\;\tilde{T}\subseteq H\neq G\}

Remark 2.2
.

By
[
1
, 11.5]
, the fact that
Thomason height is equal to the dimension holds for any toral group
for which all simple summands of the rational toral lattice are one
dimensional.

Proof :
This may all be observed directly from the calculations in Sections
9 and 11 of
[
6
]
as summarized above.

The statement about finite Weyl groups is clear since every subgroup

H
is cotoral in
HZ
.

Finite subgroups are obviously cotorally minimal and they are h-isolated (even) amongst all
subgroups of
T^{2}
, so are of Thomason height 0.

The
W
-invariant subgroups containing a circle contain

Z
or
\tilde{T}
, and are therefore not isolated, so the groups of
Thomason height 0 are precisely the finite subgroups.

The one dimensional subgroups are obviously cotorally minimal amongst
the remainder, and h-isolated by the classification. Since
G
is not
h-isolated, we see that the one dimensional groups are of Thomason
height 1 and partitioned as stated.
∎

3.

The structure of the argument is the same as that for tori in

[

rings which are isotropically concentrated and formal in a strong

The core of the proof is the fact that the sphere
S^{0}
is a homotopy pullback
of a punctured cube of isotropically simpler ring spectra.

This allows us to outline the proof: we give a symbolic description
and then discuss the steps.

\mbox{$G$-{\bf spectra}}|\mathcal{V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\simeq}}S^{0}\mbox{-mod}-\mbox{$G$-{\bf spectra}}|\mathcal{V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\simeq}}\mathrm{Cell}((S^{0})^{\lrcorner}\mbox{-mod}-\mbox{$G$-{\bf spectra}})\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\simeq}}\mathrm{Cell}(((\widetilde{S^{0}})^{\lrcorner})^{G}\mbox{-mod}-\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{spectra})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 3}}{{\simeq}}\mathrm{Cell}(C^{G}_{*}((\widetilde{S^{0}})^{\lrcorner})\mbox{-mod}-\mathbb{Q}[\mathcal{W}]\mbox{-mod})\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 4}}{{\simeq}}\mathrm{Cell}(\pi^{G}_{*}((\widetilde{S^{0}})^{\lrcorner})\mbox{-mod}-\mathbb{Q}[\mathcal{W}]\mbox{-mod})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 5}}{{\simeq}}DG-\mathcal{A}(G|\mathcal{V})

Equivalence 0 simply uses the fact that
G
-spectra are
modules over the sphere spectrum. Equivalence 1 uses the fact

[

that the category of modules over a homotopy pullback ring is equivalent to the
cellularization of the category of generalized diagrams over the
individual modules, together with the fact that the localized sphere
is the pullback.

From Equivalence 2 onwards, we introduce variants on the terms of the initial cube
so as to keep track of the finite Weyl groups. The cospan

(\widetilde{S^{0}})^{\lrcorner}
of
G
-spectra replaces terms of

(S^{0})^{\lrcorner}
by coinductions which vary by subgroup.
The new objects have the property that their

products of spectra with homology

and the category of modules take values in the corresponding product of
categories with

-action. Equivalence 2 is based on the fact
[
13

that taking fixed points under a connected group gives an equivalence.

Equivalence 3 follows from Shipley’s Theorem
[

]
, and is
easily adapted to the type of diagram we have. Equivalence 4 is a formality
statment, again requiring proof in each case. Finally, Equivalence 5
folows from the Cellular Skeleton Theorem, which will
identify the cellularization of the algebraic category of modules with
the derived category of an abelian category. Again this needs
discussion in each case.

5.C.
The coinduced diagram

Finally, we need to identify the coinduced diagram (for extended
discussion of the coinduction process, see
[
7
]
).
We need to describe the diagram

\textstyle{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathsf{V}G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathsf{V}Z)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathsf{V}\tilde{T})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathsf{V}1)}

This is designed to account for groups not being normal and for the
Weyl group not being connected. To deal with the component structure
where the discrete quotient
W_{G}^{d}(K)
of
W_{G}(K)
acts, we let
N_{G}^{f}(K)
denote the
inverse image of
W_{G}^{e}(K)=N_{G}(K)/K
in
N_{G}(K)
, so that

N_{G}(K)/N_{G}^{f}(K)=W_{G}^{d}(K)
. Thus, at a subgroup
K
, the ring
\mathcal{R}(K)
gets
replaced by the ring
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(K)=F_{N^{f}_{G}(K)}(G_{+},\mathcal{R}(K))
so that

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(K)^{G}=\mathcal{R}(K)^{N^{f}_{G}(K)}.

For full normal
subroups
N_{G}^{f}(G)=G
, and hence
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(K)=\mathcal{R}(K)
, and this applies
to all subgroups in
\mathsf{V}\tilde{T}
, so that

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathsf{V}G)=\mathcal{R}(\mathsf{V}G)=\overline{e}_{G}S^{\infty V(Z)}\mbox{ and }\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathsf{V}\tilde{T})=\mathcal{R}(\mathsf{V}\tilde{T})=\overline{e}_{G}D(S(\infty V(Z))_{+}).

On the other hand, for any finite subgroup
F=H^{\lambda}(m,n)
has
normalizer
N_{G}(H^{\lambda}(m,n))=H(\infty,2n)
with

N_{G}^{f}(H^{\lambda}(m,n))=H(\infty,n)
so that

\mathcal{R}(\mathsf{V}1)=\prod DE\langle F\rangle\mbox{ and }\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathsf{V}1)=\prod F_{N^{f}_{G}(F)}(G_{+},DE\langle F\rangle).

Similarly, any subgroup containing
Z
is of the form
H(\infty,n)

with
N_{G}(K)=N_{G}^{f}(K)=H(\infty,2n)
and

\mathcal{R}(\mathsf{V}Z)=S^{\infty V(Z)}\wedge DE\mathcal{F}/Z_{+}=S^{\infty V(Z)}\wedge\prod DE\langle K\rangle\mbox{ and }\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathsf{V}Z)=S^{\infty V(Z)}\wedge\prod F_{K}(G_{+},DE\langle K\rangle).

Lemma 5.4
.

For
K=1,Z,\tilde{T},G
we have
\pi^{G}_{*}(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathsf{V}K))=\pi^{G}_{*}(\mathcal{R}(\mathsf{V}K))
.

Proof :
For definiteness, we take
K=1
, and calculate one of the terms. The
other arguments are similar. Writing
N=N_{G}(F),N^{f}=N_{G}(F),W^{d}=W_{G}^{d}(F)
, we have

\pi^{G}_{*}(DE\langle F\rangle)=[E\langle F\rangle,S^{0}]^{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}[E\langle F\rangle,S^{0}]^{N}=([E\langle F\rangle,S^{0}]^{N^{f}})^{W^{d}},

but the action of
W_{G}^{d}
is trivial in each case.
∎

-module

-spectra

\sigma_{K}=G_{+}\wedge_{K}e_{K}S^{0}

1
Introduction

1.1
Background

The most well-known measure of the connectedness of a graph
G
is its vertex connectivity
\kappa(G)
, which is
defined as the minimum size of a vertex subset whose removal disconnects
G
(unless
G
is isomorphic to a complete graph, in which case
\kappa(G)=|V(G)|-1)
. Menger’s theorem shows that
\kappa(G)
is equal to the maximum number of internally disjoint paths that can always be found between any pair of vertices in
G
. For distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, we let
\kappa_{G}(u,v)
denote the maximum number of internally disjoint
u
-
v
paths in
G
,
so Menger’s theorem can be formulated as

\kappa(G)=\min_{u,v\in V(G)}{\kappa_{G}(u,v)}

Then, for distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, we have
\kappa(G)\leq\kappa_{G}(u,v)\leq\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(v)\}
. If
\kappa_{G}(u,v)=\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(v)\}
for every pair of vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, we say that
G
is
ideally connected
.

Ideal connectedness has arisen independently, and under different names, in work on various properties and quantities reflecting a graph’s connectivity. Beineke, Oellermann, and Pippert
[
2
]
introduced the average connectivity parameter, which for a graph
G
is defined as the average (as opposed to the minimum) of
\kappa_{G}(u,v)
among all pairs of vertices in
G
. Whereas the standard vertex connectivity measures the “worst case” scenario of
\kappa_{G}(u,v)
among all pairs of vertices, the average connectivity can be a more global and more refined measure of a graph’s overall connectedness in applications, particularly in network modeling
[
1
]
. Ideal connectedness is the “best case” outcome for the average connectivity of a graph, given its degree sequence. It turns out that ideally connected graphs frequently arise as the extremal examples for average connectivity under certain further conditions. For instance, Beineke, Oellermann, and Pippert
[
2
]
showed that among all graphs with a fixed number of vertices and edges, the ones of largest average connectivity are ideally connected; for other examples, see

[
3
,
6
,
17
]
. Hence, it is of interest to study the structure of ideally connected graphs themselves, since in some sense this amounts to studying the structure of graphs with largest average connectivity.

Hellwig and Volkmann
[
12
]
studied ideally connected graphs under the name of “maximally locally connected graphs” as a generalization of maximally connected graphs, where a graph is called
maximally connected
if its vertex connectivity equals its minimum degree. Among other results, Hellwig and Volkmann give a minimum degree condition that is sufficient to ensure that a graph is ideally connected. Finally, in theoretical computer science, various researchers have studied ideally connected graphs under the name of “strongly Menger-connected graphs” in the context of parallel routing in computer networks. In fact, researchers have investigated the more general problem of finding internally disjoint paths between two vertices in networks with faults. A graph
G
is called
strongly
m
-Menger connected
if for any vertex set
F\subseteq V(G)
of size at most
m
, the subgraph of
G
induced by the vertices in
V(G)\setminus F
is ideally connected. Then, a graph
G
is ideally connected if and only if it is strongly
0
-Menger connected. Past work has focused on showing strong Menger connectivity in particular graph classes, particularly for various hypercubes networks
[
4
,
10
,
19
]
and permutation star graphs
[
18
]
.

1.2
Overview of Results

In this paper we study ideal connectedness in two well-known graph classes: cographs and chordal graphs. A graph
G
is called a
cograph
if
G
does not contain an induced path on four vertices. A graph
G
is called
chordal
if every cycle in
G
has a chord. Cographs and chordal graphs are both well-studied graph classes that have widespread connections across graph theory and computer science, such as in the study of perfect graphs and efficient algorithms; see
[
9
]
for further background.

For a fixed collection
\mathcal{H}
of finite graphs, we say that
G
is
\mathcal{H}
-free
if there is no induced subgraph of
G
that is isomorphic to a graph in
\mathcal{H}
. If
\mathcal{H}
consists of a single element
H
, we say that a graph
G
is
H
-free if it is
\mathcal{H}
-free where
\mathcal{H}=\{H\}
. For instance, cographs are exactly the
P_{4}
-free graphs and chordal graphs are exactly the
\{C_{k}:k\geq 4\}
-free graphs.

In Section
2
we characterize the ideally connected cographs as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a cograph. Then
G
is ideally connected if and only if
G
is
2K_{2}
-free.

We then study ideal connectedness in chordal graphs. One special subclass of chordal graphs is the class of
split graphs
, the graphs whose vertices can be partitioned into a clique and an independent set. If the vertices
V(G)
of a graph
G
can be partitioned into a clique
K
and independent set
I
, we say that
G
is
(K,I)
-split
. If
U\subseteq V(G)
is a set of vertices of
G
and the vertices in
U
can be listed as
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{|U|}
so that for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,|U|-1\}
we have
N_{G}(v_{i})\subseteq N_{G}(v_{i+1})
, then we say that
U
has the
nested neighborhood property
in
G
.
If
G
is a
(K,I)
-split graph and
I
has the nested neighborhood property in
G
, then
G
is called a
threshold graph
. Threshold graphs are also sometimes referred to as
nested split graphs
. Figure
1
shows an example of a threshold graph on sixteen vertices.

Figure 1:
A threshold graph with sixteen vertices.

The class of threshold graphs is a subclass of both cographs and chordal graphs that was introduced by Chvátal and Hammer
[
5
]
, and independently by Henderson and Zalcstein
[
13
]
, in 1977. There are several known characterizations of threshold graphs. For instance, threshold graphs are exactly the graphs that can be obtained from the single-vertex graph by repeatedly adding either an isolated vertex or a dominating vertex. Threshold graphs are also exactly the
\{C_{4},P_{4},2K_{2}\}
-free graphs. For a comprehensive resource on threshold graphs, we refer the reader to the book by Mahadev and Peled
[
15
]
.

In Section
3
we prove a structure theorem for any ideally connected graph with a minimum vertex cutset that forms a
clique
, a set of pairwise adjacent vertices. In Section
4
, by using the property that every minimum vertex cutset of a chordal graph is a clique, we prove that the ideally connected chordal graphs are precisely the threshold graphs.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph. Then
G
is ideally connected if and only if
G
is a threshold graph.

Theorem
1.2
gives a new way of describing threshold graphs as a subclass of chordal graphs.

One of the most common ways to represent the structure of a chordal graph
G
is through a
clique tree
, a tree that has maximal cliques in
G
as its nodes and where for any
v\in V(G)
, the subgraph of the tree induced by the nodes containing
v
is connected. To further describe the subclass of chordal graphs formed by the threshold graphs, in Section
5
we characterize the clique trees of threshold graphs. We show that for any threshold graph
G
with set
\mathcal{M}_{G}
of maximal cliques, any tree on
|\mathcal{M}_{G}|
nodes is a clique tree of
G
. This universality property is interesting because it does not hold for general chordal graphs or even for general split graphs.

2
Ideally Connected Cographs

In this section we characterize the ideally connected cographs.
If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are vertex-disjoint cographs, one can show that
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
and
G_{1}\nabla G_{2}
are also cographs. In fact, Lerchs
[
14
]
showed that cographs can be characterized as the graphs that can be constructed from isolated vertices by a finite number of graph union and graph join operations. Hence, to understand whether a cograph is ideally connected, we first study whether ideal connectedness is preserved when taking graph joins.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be vertex-disjoint cographs. Then
G_{1}\nabla G_{2}
is ideally connected if and only if both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are ideally connected.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be vertex-disjoint cographs and let
G
be the graph join
G_{1}\nabla G_{2}
.

First suppose
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are both ideally connected. We will show that
G
is ideally connected too.
For distinct vertices
u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{1})
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\Deg_{G}(u)\leq\Deg_{G}(u^{\prime})
. Clearly,
\Deg_{G}(u)=|N_{G_{1}}(u)|+|V(G_{2})|
and
\Deg_{G_{1}}(u)\leq\Deg_{G_{1}}(u^{\prime})
. Since
G_{1}
is ideally connected, there is a collection
\mathcal{C}_{1}
of
\Deg_{G_{1}}(u)
internally disjoint
u
-
u^{\prime}
paths in
G_{1}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{2}
denote the collection of the
|V(G_{2})|
internally disjoint
u
-
u^{\prime}
paths of the form
\{uvu^{\prime}:v\in V(G_{2})\}
. Each path in
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is internally disjoint from
V(G_{1})
, so the paths in
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}
show that
\kappa_{G}(u,u^{\prime})=\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(u^{\prime})\}
. Similarly, for distinct vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in V(G_{2})
we have
\kappa_{G}(v,v^{\prime})=\min\{\Deg_{G}(v),\Deg_{G}(v^{\prime})\}
.

For vertices
u\in V(G_{1})
and
v\in V(G_{2})
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\Deg_{G}(u)\leq\Deg_{G}(v)
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
denote the collection of paths consisting of the length-one path
uv
, the
|N_{G_{1}}(u)|
paths of the form
\{uyv:y\in N_{G_{1}}(u)\}
, and the
\Deg_{G_{2}}(v)
paths of the form
\{uwv:w\in N_{G_{2}}(v)\}
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{1}
consists of
\Deg_{G_{1}}(u)+|N_{G_{2}}[v]|
internally disjoint
u
-
v
paths.
Since
\Deg_{G}(u)\leq\Deg_{G}(v)
, we have
\Deg_{G_{1}}(u)+|V(G_{2})|\leq\Deg_{G_{2}}(v)+|V(G_{1})|
, which implies that
|V(G_{1})|-\Deg_{G_{1}}(u)\geq|V(G_{2})|-\Deg_{G_{2}}(v)
, so the number of nonneighbors of
u
in
G_{1}
is at least as large as the number of nonneighbors of
v
in
G_{2}
. Hence, in
G
there is a collection
\mathcal{C}_{2}
of
|V(G_{2})\setminus N_{G_{2}}[v]|
internally disjoint
u
-
v
paths of the form
\{uxyv:x\in V(G_{2})\setminus N_{G_{2}}[v],y\in V(G_{1})\setminus N_{G_{1}}[u]\}
. Then, the paths in
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}
show that
\kappa_{G}(u,v)=\Deg_{G_{1}}(u)+|V(G_{2})|=\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(v)\}
. Hence, if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are ideally connected cographs, then so is
G
.

Conversely, suppose that
G
is ideally connected.
We will show that
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are ideally connected. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that this is not the case, so without loss of generality we may assume that
G_{1}
is not ideally connected. Then there are distinct vertices
u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{1})
with
\Deg_{G_{1}}(u)\leq\Deg_{G_{1}}(u^{\prime})
and
\kappa_{G_{1}}(u,u^{\prime})<\Deg_{G_{1}}(u)
. Clearly we have
\Deg_{G}(u)=\Deg_{G_{1}}(u)+|V(G_{2})|<\Deg_{G}(u^{\prime})
. By the assumption that
G
is ideally connected, there are
\Deg_{G}(u)
internally disjoint
u
-
u^{\prime}
paths, saturating each neighbor of
u
in
G
. Hence, since
N_{G}(u)
contains
V(G_{2})
, there are
\Deg_{G}(u)-|V(G_{2})|=\Deg_{G_{1}}(u)
internally disjoint
u
-
u^{\prime}
paths in
G_{1}
, a contradiction. Hence, if
G
is ideally connected, then
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
must be ideally connected too.
∎

We can now prove Theorem
1.1
, which states that a cograph is ideally connected if and only if it is
2K_{2}
-free.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
G
be a cograph. We will prove the theorem by induction on
|V(G)|
. The theorem clearly holds for
|V(G)|\leq 2
and clearly holds if
G
is an edgeless graph, so we may assume that
|V(G)|\geq 3
and
G
has at least one edge.

First suppose that
G
is ideally connected. Since
G
is a cograph, there are vertex-disjoint cographs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
so that either
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
or
G=G_{1}\nabla G_{2}
. If
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
, then since
G
is ideally connected, both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are ideally connected and at least one of them is edgeless. We assume without loss of generality that
G_{2}
is edgeless. Since
G_{1}
is ideally connected, the inductive hypothesis implies that
G_{1}
is
2K_{2}
-free. Clearly, the graph union of a
2K_{2}
-free graph with an edgeless graph is
2K_{2}
-free, so in this case
G
is
2K_{2}
-free. On the other hand, if
G=G_{1}\nabla G_{2}
, then by Lemma
2.1
, both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are ideally connected. By the inductive hypothesis,
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are
2K_{2}
-free. It is easy to see that the graph join of two vertex-disjoint
2K_{2}
-free graphs is also
2K_{2}
-free, so in this case
G
is also
2K_{2}
-free. Hence, if
G
is an ideally connected cograph, then
G
is
2K_{2}
-free.

Now suppose
G
is a
2K_{2}
-free cograph. Then there are vertex-disjoint
2K_{2}
-free cographs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
so that either
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
or
G=G_{1}\nabla G_{2}
. By the inductive hypothesis, both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are ideally connected. If
G=G_{1}\nabla G_{2}
, then
G
is ideally connected by Lemma
2.1
. If
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
then since
G
is
2K_{2}
-free, at least one of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is edgeless. In this case, since the graph union of an ideally connected graph with an edgeless graph is also ideally connected, then
G
is ideally connected. Hence, any
2K_{2}
-free cograph is ideally connected.
∎

For vertex-disjoint cographs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, say that the graph union operation
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
is
safe
if either
G_{1}
or
G_{2}
is edgeless. It follows from Theorem
1.1
that the ideally connected cographs are exactly the graphs that can be constructed from isolated vertices by a finite number of graph join and safe graph union operations.

Theorem
1.1
shows that the ideally connected cographs are exactly the
\{P_{4},2K_{2}\}
-free graphs.
The complement of the graph
P_{4}
is isomorphic to
P_{4}
and the complement of the graph
2K_{2}
is the cycle
C_{4}
. The
\{P_{4},C_{4}\}
-free graphs are known as
trivially perfect graphs
, so Theorem
1.1
shows that the ideally connected cographs are exactly the complements of trivially perfect graphs.
Since the threshold graphs are the
\{P_{4},C_{4},2K_{2}\}
-free graphs, the threshold graphs are exactly the ideally connected cographs whose complements are also ideally connected.
In particular, Theorem
1.1
shows that threshold graphs are ideally connected. This fact will be used in later sections.

Corollary 2.1
.

If
G
is a threshold graph, then
G
is ideally connected. ∎

An interesting direction for future work would be to determine the ideally connected graphs in graph classes that are superclasses of cographs. In particular, an approach extending the one taken in this section seems promising for graph classes defined by split decompositions (edge cuts that induce complete bipartite graphs), such as distance-hereditary graphs, parity graphs, or circle graphs.

Ideally Connected Graphs with
\kappa
-Clique Cuts

In this section we analyze the structure of ideally connected graphs with
\kappa
-clique cuts, which we will apply to chordal graphs in Section
4
. We begin with a few simple lemmas about the degrees of pairs of vertices in an ideally connected graph.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph with a
\kappa
-clique cut
S
. Let
t=|S|
. If
G
is ideally connected, then at most one component of
G-S
has a vertex of degree greater than
t
in
G
.

Proof.

Suppose that
C_{i}
and
C_{j}
are distinct components of
G-S
and there is a vertex
v\in V(C_{i})
with
\Deg_{G}(v)>t
. For any vertex
v^{\prime}
in
V(C_{j})
, we will show that
\Deg_{G}(v^{\prime})\leq t
.

Any
v
-
v^{\prime}
path intersects
S
, so
\kappa_{G}(v,v^{\prime})\leq|S|=t
. Since
G
is ideally connected, we have
\min\{\Deg_{G}(v),\Deg_{G}(v^{\prime})\}=\kappa_{G}(v,v^{\prime})\leq t
. Then, since
\Deg_{G}(v)>t
, we must have
\Deg_{G}(v^{\prime})\leq t
.
∎

The lemma shows that in an ideally connected graph
G
with a
\kappa
-clique cut
S
, for any pair of vertices
u,v\in V(G)\setminus S
in distinct
S
-subgraphs of
G
, we have
\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(v)\}=|S|
.

The next lemma considers the quantity
\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(s)\}
for vertices
u\in V(G)\setminus S
and
s\in S
in an ideally connected graph
G
with a
\kappa
-clique cut
S
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a graph with a
\kappa
-clique cut
S
, and suppose
u\in V(H)\setminus S
for an
S
-subgraph

of

. If

is ideally connected, then for every

, we have
\Deg_{H}(u)=\Deg_{G}(u)\leq\Deg_{H}(s)<\Deg_{G}(s)
and
\kappa_{H}(u,s)=\kappa_{G}(u,s)=\Deg_{H}(u)
.

Proof.

The identity
\Deg_{H}(u)=\Deg_{G}(u)
is obvious. By definition of a
\kappa
-clique cut,

has at least two components and so the vertex

has a neighbor

in a component of

different from

. Then,

. Hence, it remains to show that
\Deg_{G}(u)\leq\Deg_{G}(s)
.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
\Deg_{G}(u)>\Deg_{G}(s)
. Since
G
is ideally connected, there’s a collection
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{\Deg_{G}(s)}\}
of
\Deg_{G}(s)
internally disjoint
u
-
s
paths in
G
saturating
s
, so that every neighbor of
s
appears in some path in
\mathcal{P}
. Let

denote the path in this collection that contains the vertex
z
, so
P_{i}
is a

-
u
path that includes the vertex
z
but no vertex in

. However, in order to reach vertex

from

, the path

must intersect
S\setminus\{s\}
, a contradiction. This contradiction establishes that
\Deg_{G}(u)\leq\Deg_{G}(s)
, and so since

is ideally connected, we have
\kappa_{G}(u,s)=\Deg_{H}(u)=\kappa_{H}(u,s)
.
∎

.

If

. Moreover, we have

\Delta(G)=\max\{\operatorname{deg}_{G}(v):v\in S\}\geq|S|+1

Proof.

The second property follows immediately from Lemma
3.2
. To prove the first property, suppose for the sake of contradiction that
S^{\prime}
is another
\kappa
-clique cut in
G
that is different from
S
. Then, since both
S
and
S^{\prime}
have size
\kappa(G)
, there are vertices
s\in S\setminus S^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}\setminus S
. Let
H
denote the
S
-subgraph of
G
containing
s^{\prime}
and let
H^{\prime}
denote the
S^{\prime}
-subgraph of
G
containing
s
. By applying Lemma
3.2
to the
S^{\prime}
-subgraph

, we have
\Deg_{G}(s)=\Deg_{H^{\prime}}(s)\leq\Deg_{H^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})<\Deg_{G}(s^{\prime})
. However, by applying the Lemma
3.2
to the
S
-subgraph
H
we obtain
\Deg_{G}(s^{\prime})=\Deg_{H}(s^{\prime})\leq\Deg_{H}(s)<\Deg_{G}(s)
, which contradicts that
\Deg_{G}(s)<\Deg_{G}(s^{\prime})
. Hence,
S
must be the unique
\kappa
-clique cut in
G
.
∎

Finally, we compare the degrees of vertices
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
in an ideally connected graph
G
with a
\kappa
-clique cut
S
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a graph with a
\kappa
-clique cut
S
, and let

be distinct vertices in

where
\Deg_{G}(s_{1})\leq\Deg_{G}(s_{2})
. Then for any
S
-subgraph

of

, we have
\min\{\Deg_{H}(s_{1}),\Deg_{H}(s_{2})\}=\Deg_{H}(s_{1})
and
\kappa_{H}(u,v)=\Deg_{H}(s_{1})
.

Proof.

Let
c=\Deg_{G}(s_{1})
and let
t=|S|
. Since
G
is ideally connected, there’s a collection
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{c}\}
of
c
internally disjoint
s_{1}
-
s_{2}
paths in

saturating
s_{1}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
P_{1}
is the length-one path
s_{1}s_{2}
and that
\{P_{2},\ldots,P_{|S|-1}\}
are the distinct length-two paths of the form
s_{1}s^{\prime}s_{2}
where
s^{\prime}\in S\setminus\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
. Hence, there are
c-(t-1)>0

s_{1}
-
s_{2}
paths, each of length greater than one, in
G
that are internally disjoint from one another and internally disjoint from
S
. Any such path has its interior entirely in a single component of
G-S
, so since the paths in
\mathcal{P}
saturate
s_{1}
, the vertex
s_{2}
must have at least at many neighbors as
s_{1}
in any component of
G-S
. Hence, in any
S
-subgraph
H
of
G
, we have
\min\{\Deg_{H}(s_{1}),\Deg_{H}(s_{2})\}=\Deg_{H}(s_{1})
and
\kappa_{H}(s_{1},s_{2})=\Deg_{H}(s_{1})
.
∎

If
G
is an ideally connected graph, it is natural to ask for sufficient conditions for a subgraph of

to also be ideally connected. When

has a
\kappa
-clique cut

, we now show that each
S
-subgraph of

is also ideally connected, which gives one sufficient condition for ideal connectedness to be preserved when taking subgraphs.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
S
be a
\kappa
-clique cut of an ideally connected graph

, and let
t=|S|
. Then for any
S
-subgraph
H
of
G
,

is ideally connected and
\kappa(H)\geq t
.

Proof.

Let
H
be an
S
-subgraph of
G
. We first show that

is ideally connected. Let
u,v\in V(H)
be distinct vertices of

and let
c=\min\{\Deg_{H}(u),\Deg_{H}(v)\}
. We will show that
\kappa_{H}(u,v)=c
.

If
u\in V(H)\setminus S
and
v\in S
then
\kappa_{H}(u,v)=\kappa_{G}(u,v)=\Deg_{G}(u)=c
by Lemma
3.2
. If
u,v\in S
are distinct vertices in
S
, then
\kappa_{H}(u,v)=\min\{\Deg_{H}(u),\Deg_{H}(v)\}=c
by Lemma
3.3
. Hence, it remains to show that for distinct vertices
u,v\in V(H)\setminus S
, we have
\kappa_{H}(u,v)=\min\{\Deg_{H}(u),\Deg_{H}(v)\}
. Since
\Deg_{H}(u)=\Deg_{G}(u)
and
\Deg_{H}(v)=\Deg_{G}(v)
for
u,v\in V(H)\setminus S
, we must show that
\kappa_{H}(u,v)=\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(v)\}
.

For
u,v\in V(H)\setminus S
, let
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
denote the set of all
c

(P_{1},\ldots,P_{c})
where
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{c}\}
is a set of

is ideally connected,
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
is nonempty. Among all tuples in
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
, choose
\mathcal{P}=(P^{*}_{1},\ldots,P^{*}_{c})\in\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
so that the corresponding

(|P^{*}_{1}|,\ldots,|P^{*}_{c}|)
has minimum lexicographical order among all the tuples of lengths from elements of
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
. The minimality of

implies that in particular,
|P^{*}_{i}|\leq|P^{*}_{j}|
for all

, and
P^{*}_{1}
is a path of length one if and only if
uv\in E(G)
.

We claim that for each
i\in[c]
, the path
P^{*}_{i}
is a subgraph of

. For the sake of contradiction, suppose that this is not the case, meaning that for some
i\in[c]
, the path
P^{*}_{i}
contains a vertex

. Then since
S
is a vertex cutset, there is a subpath

of
P^{*}_{i}
and distinct vertices
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
such that
P^{\prime}_{i}=s_{1}P^{*}_{i}s_{2}
and
P^{\prime}_{i}
contains the vertex

. Then replacing

with the edge

on

, we obtain a new
u

v

of length strictly less than
|P^{*}_{i}|
. The path

remains internally disjoint from each path in

because

. Also,

has length greater than one because it still includes

and
s_{2}
as vertices, so

. Since

has length greater than one, it is distinct from the path

. Then
(P^{*}_{1},\ldots,P^{*}_{i-1},Q^{*}_{i},P^{*}_{i+1},\ldots,P^{*}_{c})\in\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
, contradicting the choice of
\mathcal{P}
. This contradiction establishes that for each
i\in[c]
, the path

is a subgraph of

.

Therefore, the collection
\{P^{*}_{1},\ldots,P^{*}_{c}\}
is a set of
c=\min\{\Deg_{H}(u),\Deg_{H}(v)\}
internally disjoint
u
-
v
paths in

, so
\kappa_{H}(u,v)=c
and

is ideally connected.

Now we show
\kappa(H)\geq t
. Since

is ideally connected it suffices to show that minimum degree
\delta(H)
of

is at least

. Since
\kappa(G)=t
, we have

, so for any vertex

, we have
\Deg_{H}(v)=\Deg_{G}(v)\geq t
. For a vertex

, we have

because

has

neighbors in

and by the minimality of the cut

,

has at least one neighbor in

. Hence,
\kappa(H)\geq t
.
∎

The conclusion of Lemma
3.4
does not hold without the assumption that

has a minimum cutset that is a clique. Indeed, if
S
is a minimum vertex cut of an ideally connected graph

and

is a component of

, there may be vertices

with a path between them passing through another component of

. Crucially, if

is not a clique, there may be

-

paths leaving

that cannot be rerouted through

, so the subgraph

might not be ideally connected. It is for this reason that ideal connectedness is harder to analyze in non-chordal graphs, since as we will see in Section

, such graphs may not have a

-clique cut.

We can now give a structure theorem for ideally connected graphs with
\kappa
-clique cuts. The theorem shows that if

is an ideally connected graph with

-clique cut

, then each

-subgraph of

is ideally connected and any two

-subgraphs intersect precisely at

. Moreover,

is the union of its

-subgraphs and satisfies certain degree restrictions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose

is a graph with a
\kappa
-clique cut

. Let

, let

denote the

-subgraphs of

Then

is ideally connected if and only if each of the following conditions hold:

(3.1.1)

Every

(3.1.2)

Among each of the subgraphs

, at most one has a vertex of degree greater than

, exists, then

for every

and

.

(3.1.3)

For distinct vertices
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
where

, we have
\Deg_{H_{i}}(s_{1})\leq\Deg_{H_{i}}(s_{2})
for every
i\in[m]
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

If

is ideally connected, then Conditions

,
3.1.2
, and
3.1.3
follow from Lemmas

,
3.2
,
3.3
, and

Conversely, suppose

is a graph with a
\kappa
-clique cut

satisfying Conditions

,
3.1.2
, and
3.1.3
in the theorem statement. We will show that

is ideally connected. Without loss of generality, we may assume

is the only component of

that might have a vertex of degree larger than

For any pair of vertices

. Then, since

Next, for vertices

. By Condition

, we have

, as needed.

For distinct vertices

, we may assume without loss of generality that
\Deg_{G}(s_{1})\leq\Deg_{G}(s_{2})
. By Condition

, each

is ideally connected, and by Condition

, we have
\Deg_{H_{i}}(s_{1})\leq\Deg_{H_{i}}(s_{2})
for all

. Then, for each
i\in[m]
, there is a collection of
\Deg_{H_{i}}(s_{1})
internally disjoint
s_{1}
-
s_{2}
paths saturating
s_{1}
. We may assume that among these
\Deg_{H_{i}}(s_{1})

and exactly

are the length-two paths of the form

for

, and so the others are internally disjoint from each other and internally disjoint from

. Thus, for each

, there are

. Then,

\kappa_{G}(u,v)\geq(t-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\Deg_{H_{i}}(s_{1})-(t-1))=\Deg_{G}(s_{1})

as needed.

Finally, for distinct vertices

\Deg_{H_{1}}(u)=\Deg_{G}(u)
and
\Deg_{H_{1}}(v)=\Deg_{G}(v)
. Since

\kappa_{G}(u,v)=\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(v)\}
.

Therefore, we have
\kappa_{G}(u,v)=\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(v)\}
for any distinct vertices

∎

4
Ideally Connected Chordal Graphs

In this section we apply Theorem
3.1
to characterize the ideally connected chordal graphs. Recall that a graph is called
chordal
if it has no induced cycle of length larger than three. There are several known characterizations of chordal graphs, one of which is the following.

Theorem 4.1

(Dirac
[
7
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph not isomorphic to a complete graph. Then
G
is chordal if and only if every minimum vertex cutset in
G
is a
\kappa
-clique cut. Moreover, if
G
is chordal and
S
is a
\kappa
-clique cut in
G
, then every
S
-subgraph of
G
is chordal.

Recall that a vertex
v
in a chordal graph
G
is called
simplicial
if its neighborhood
N_{G}(v)
is a clique in
G
. By applying Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph not isomorphic to a complete graph, and let
S
be a
\kappa
-clique cut in
G
with
t=|S|
. Then
G
is ideally connected if and only if the
S
-subgraphs of
G
can be listed as
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
such that the following properties hold:

(4.1.1)

H_{1}
is an ideally connected,
t
-connected chordal graph.

(4.1.2)

For each
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,m\}
,
H_{i}
consists of the complete graph
G[S]
along with a single vertex
v_{i}
that is simplicial to
S
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a chordal graph not isomorphic to a complete graph and with a
\kappa
-clique cut

. First suppose

is ideally connected. Then by Theorem
3.1
, we can list the
S
-subgraphs of
G
as
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
such that the following conditions hold:

1.

H_{i}
is ideally connected and
\kappa(H_{i})\geq t
for each
i
.

2.

For each
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,m\}
, we have
\kappa(H_{i})=t
and every vertex in
V(H_{i})\setminus S
has degree exactly
t
in
G
. For any vertices
u\in V(H_{1})\setminus S
and
s\in S
, we have
\Deg_{H_{1}}(u)\leq\Deg_{H_{1}}(s)<\Deg_{G}(s)
.

3.

For distinct vertices
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
where
\Deg_{G}(s_{1})\leq\Deg_{G}(s_{2})
, we have
\Deg_{H_{i}}(s_{1})\leq\Deg_{H_{i}}(s_{2})
for every
i\in[m]
.

By Condition
1
and Theorem
4.1
,
H_{1}
is ideally connected,

4.1.1
.
Now fix
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,c\}
. We claim that
H_{i}
is isomorphic to the complete graph

. By Theorem
4.1
,

is chordal and so either

is isomorphic to a complete graph or

has a
\kappa
-clique cut. In the former case, Condition
2
implies that

must be isomorphic to
K_{t+1}
.

We claim that the latter case cannot hold. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that for some
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,m\}
, the subgraph
H_{i}
is not isomorphic to a complete graph. Then
H_{i}
has a
\kappa
-clique cut

of size
t
, so

has at least two components. If

contains a vertex

, then

has

neighbors in

and has neighbors to each component of

, so

has degree at least

in

, contradicting Property

. Hence, we must have

and so since

, we must have

. But since

is connected, the subgraph

is also connected, which is a contradiction to the fact that

has at least two components.

Therefore, for each
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,m\}
, the subgraph
H_{i}
is isomorphic to the complete graph

, so
H_{i}
consists of
G[S]
along with a single vertex

that is simplicial to

. This proves Property
4.1.2
.

Conversely, if
G
is a chordal graph not isomorphic to a complete graph and

is a
\kappa
-clique cut in

, and moreover

satisfies Property

and Property

, then it is easy to see that

satisfies the conditions from Theorem

that guarantee that

is ideally connected.
∎

We can now prove Theorem
1.2
, that the ideally connected chordal graphs are precisely the threshold graphs.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph. If

is a threshold graph, then by Corollary

,

is ideally connected.

Hence, it only remains to show that any ideally connected chordal graph is a threshold graph. Suppose that
G
is ideally connected.
We will prove that

|V(G)|
. Clearly, the theorem holds if
|V(G)|\leq 4
or if

|V(G)|\geq 5
and that

has a
\kappa
-clique cut

.

Let

. By Lemma
4.1
, the
S
-subgraphs of

H_{1},\ldots H_{m}
such that the following properties hold:

1.

H_{1}
is an ideally connected,
t

2.

For each
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,m\}
,
H_{i}
consists of
G[S]
along with a single vertex
v_{i}
that is simplicial to
S
.

If
H_{1}
is isomorphic to a complete graph, then letting
K
be the vertices in
V(H_{1})
and

, we see that

is
(K,I)
-split and all vertices in

Thus, we may assume that

,

has a
\kappa

.

We claim that

contains

. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that this is not the case, so since

Since

. Since

is not isomorphic to a complete graph, we can choose

I^{\prime}
so that for any vertex
v^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}
, the neighborhood
N_{H_{1}}(v^{\prime})
of
v^{\prime}
in
H_{1}
is a proper subset of
K^{\prime}
. In particular, we may choose
K^{\prime}
and
I^{\prime}
so that
S\subseteq S^{\prime}\subseteq K^{\prime}
. Hence, since
S^{\prime}
is a
\kappa
-clique cut of
H_{1}
, for every vertex
v^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}
we have
S\subseteq S^{\prime}\subseteq N_{H_{1}}(v^{\prime})
. Then, taking
K=K^{\prime}
and adding the vertices
v_{2},\ldots,v_{m}
to
I^{\prime}
to produce the independent set
I
, we see that
G
is
(K,I)
-split and the vertices of
I
have the nested neighborhood property in
G
. Hence,
G
is a threshold graph.

∎

Let
u,v\in V(G)
be distinct vertices of an ideally connected chordal graph
G
where
\Deg_{G}(u)\leq\Deg_{G}(v)
. From Theorem
1.2
, we see that if
\Deg_{G}(u)<\Deg_{G}(v)
then
N_{G}(u)\subseteq N_{G}[v]
, and if
\Deg_{G}(u)=\Deg_{G}(v)
then either
N_{G}(u)=N_{G}(v)
or
N_{G}[u]=N_{G}[v]
.
As a result, for any distinct vertices
u,v
in an ideally connected chordal graph
G
, we can not only conclude that
\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(v)\}
internally disjoint
u
-
v
paths exist in
G
, but we can also guarantee that each of these paths have length at most two, and therefore that these paths can be found very efficiently. Indeed, for any two vertices
u,v
in a threshold graph
G
, the collection of length-two paths
\{uzv:z\in N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)\}
, along with the length-one path
uv
if it exists, gives a collection
\min\{\Deg_{G}(u),\Deg_{G}(v)\}
internally disjoint
u
-
v
paths in
G
. This is related to the fact that recognizing if a graph is threshold can be done in linear time
[
11
]
.

5
Clique Trees of Threshold Graphs

In this section, we further describe threshold graphs within the class of chordal graphs by characterizing the clique trees of threshold graphs.

We first state some terminology related to intersection graphs and clique trees.
For a family
\mathcal{C}=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}\}
of
m
sets, the
intersection graph
of
\mathcal{C}
is the graph
G
with vertices
V(G)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
and edges
E(G)=\{v_{i}v_{j}:i\neq j\text{ and }S_{i}\cap S_{j}\neq\emptyset\}
. Gavril
[
8
]
proved that a graph is chordal if and only if it is the intersection graph of a family of subtrees of a tree.
For a chordal graph
G
and a tree
T
, let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{G}
if
G
can be represented as the intersection graph of a family of subtrees of
T
. Since
\mathcal{T}_{G}
is infinite, it is useful to only consider the trees in
\mathcal{T}_{G}
of minimum size, in which case the nodes of the trees correspond to maximal cliques of
G
. This reasoning motivates the concept a
clique tree
, a tree that has maximal cliques in
G
as its nodes and where for any
v\in V(G)
, the subgraph of the tree induced by the nodes containing
v
is connected. More precisely, for a chordal graph
G
we let
\mathcal{M}_{G}
denote the set maximal cliques of
G
, and we say that the pair
(T,\sigma)
is a
clique tree pair
of graph
G
if
T
is a tree and
\sigma:V(T)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G}
is a bijection such that for any
v\in V(G)
, the subgraph of
T
induced by the nodes in
\{u\in V(T):v\in\sigma(u)\}
is a subtree of
T
. Clearly, a graph
G
has tree
T
as a clique tree if any only if it has
(T,\sigma)
as a clique tree pair for some bijection
\sigma:V(T)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G}
.

We can now state Gavril’s characterization of chordal graphs in terms of intersection graphs and clique trees.

Theorem 5.1

(Gavril
[
8
]
)
.

For a graph
G
, the following are equivalent:

•

G
is chordal.

•

G
has a clique tree.

•

G
is an intersection graph of a family of subtrees of a tree.

Since the threshold graphs form a subclass of chordal graphs, it is natural to ask what the possible clique trees of threshold graphs are. For the class of split graphs, which is a superclass of threshold graphs, McMorris and Shier
[
16
]
showed that a chordal graph is a split graph if and only if it has a star graph (the bipartite graph
K_{1,n}
for some positive integer
n
) as a clique tree. Therefore, any threshold graph has a star graph as a clique tree.

In order to state the characterization of the clique trees of threshold graphs, we need one more piece of notation.
For a threshold graph
G
with set
\mathcal{M}_{G}
of maximal cliques, for positive integer
j
we let
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{j}=\{M\in\mathcal{M}_{G}:|M|\geq j\}
be the set of maximal cliques in
G
of size at least
j
.
For instance, in the threshold graph
G
in Figure
3
,
\mathcal{M}_{G}
consists of five cliques: the clique consisting of the left twelve vertices, and
N_{G}[v_{i}]
for
i\in[4]
. We have
|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{j}|=0
for
j>12
,
|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{j}|=1
for
j\in\{7,8,\ldots,12\}
,
|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{6}|=2
,
|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{5}|=3
, and
|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{j}|=5
for
j\leq 4
.

We can now characterize the clique tree pairs of threshold graphs. Unsurprisingly, the characterization is based on a nested subtree condition, analogous to the nested neighborhood condition characterizing threshold graphs.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G
be a threshold graph with clique number
\omega(G)
, let
T
be a tree, and let
\sigma:V(T)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G}
be a bijection.
For positive integer
j\in[\omega(G)]
, let
T_{j}
denote the subgraph of
T
induced by the nodes in
\{\sigma^{-1}(M):M\in\mathcal{K}_{G}^{j}\}
.

Then
(T,\sigma)
is a clique tree pair of
G
if and only if for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,\omega(G)\}
,
T_{j}
is a subtree of
T
and moreover for
j\in[\omega(G)-1]
,
T_{j+1}
is a subtree of
T_{j}
.

Proof.

If
G
is isomorphic to a complete graph, then
|\mathcal{M}_{G}|=1
and so the theorem statement trivially holds.
Now assume that
G
is a threshold graph that is not isomorphic to a complete graph. Let
T
be a tree and let
\sigma:V(T)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G}
be a bijection.
Letting
K
denote a clique in
G
of size
\omega(G)
and with
I=V(G)\setminus K
, which is nonempty since
G
is not isomorphic to a complete graph, we have that
G
is
(K,I)
-split and the vertices of
I
can be listed as
v_{1},\ldots,v_{|I|}
so that for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,|I|-1\}
we have
N_{G}(v_{i})\subseteq N_{G}(v_{i+1})\subsetneq K
.
Hence,
\mathcal{M}_{G}
consists of the clique
K
and the closed neighborhoods
N_{G}[v_{i}]
for each
i\in[|I|]
.

Let
m
denote the number of distinct elements of the collection
\{N_{G}(v_{1}),\ldots,N_{G}(v_{|I|})\}
, so
m\leq|I|
. Let
(N_{1},\ldots,N_{m})
be the
m
-tuple consisting of the
m
distinct elements of
\{N_{G}(v_{1}),\ldots,N_{G}(v_{|I|})\}
, arranged in increasing order of size. Thus, we have
N_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq N_{m}\subsetneq K
.

Let
z_{1}
be any vertex in
N_{1}
and for each
i\in\{2,\ldots,m\}
, let
z_{i}\in K
be any vertex in
N_{i}\setminus N_{i-1}
. Then for any
i\in[m]
, we have
|N_{i}|<\omega(G)
and a maximal clique
M\in\mathcal{M}_{G}
contains
z_{i}
if and only if
M\in\mathcal{K}_{G}^{|N_{i}|+1}
.

First suppose that
(T,\sigma)
is a clique tree pair of
G
. Then each
T_{z_{i}}
is a subtree of
T
, so for each
j\in[\omega(G)-1]
, we have that
T_{j}
is a subtree of
T
. Moreover
T_{\omega(G)}
is a subtree of
T
because every maximum clique in
G
intersects
K
. Hence, for any
j\in[\omega(G)]
,
T_{j}
is a subtree of
T
.
Moreover, for
j\in[\omega(G)-1]
we have
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{j}=\mathcal{K}^{j+1}_{G}\cup\{M\in\mathcal{M}_{G}:|M|=j\}
, so
V(\mathcal{K}^{j+1}_{G})\subseteq V(\mathcal{K}^{j}_{G})
. Since
T_{j+1}
and
T_{j}
are both subtrees of
T
, it follows that
T_{j+1}
is a subtree of
T_{j}
.

Conversely, suppose that
T
is a tree,
\sigma:V(T)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G}
is a bijection,
T_{j}
is a subtree of
T
for any
j\in[\omega(G)]
, and for
j\in[\omega(G)-1]
we have that
T_{j+1}
is a subtree of
T_{j}
. Fix
v\in V(G)
and let
H_{v}
denote the subgraph of
T
induced by the nodes in
\{\sigma^{-1}(M):v\in M\}
.
We will show that
H_{v}
is a subtree of
T
. If
v\notin K
, there is exactly one maximal clique in
G
containing
v
, namely
N_{G}[v]
. Similarly, if
v\in K\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{|I|}N_{G}(v_{i})
, the vertex
v
is in only one maximal clique in
G
, namely
K
. In these two cases,
|V(H_{v})|=1
so
H_{v}
is a subtree of
T
. Finally, if
v\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{|I|}N_{G}(v_{i})
, let
\ell\in[m]
be the smallest index where
v\in N_{\ell}
, so for any
M\in\mathcal{M}_{G}
, we have
v\in M
if and only if
M\in\mathcal{K}_{G}^{|N_{\ell}|+1}
. In other words, we have
H_{v}=T_{j}
where
j=|N_{\ell}|+1
. By the assumption that
T_{j}
is a subtree of
T
, it follows that
H_{v}
is a subtree of
T
. Hence,
(T,\sigma)
is a clique tree pair of
G
.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
5.2
, we can prove that threshold graphs are “universal” in the sense that any threshold graph
G
has any tree on
|\mathcal{M}_{G}|
nodes as a clique tree. We say that a chordal graph
G
is
clique tree universal
if any tree on
|\mathcal{M}_{G}|
nodes is a clique tree for
G
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Every threshold graph is clique tree universal.

Proof.

Suppose
G
is a threshold graph whose vertices can be partitioned into a clique
K
and independent set
I
so that
I
has the nested neighborhood property in
G
.
Given a tree
T
on
|\mathcal{M}_{G}|
nodes, let
r
be an arbitrary node of
T
. We will construct a bijection
\sigma:V(T)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G}
. First, set
\sigma(r)=K
. Then, perform a breadth-first search from
r
, and upon encountering a new node
u
of
T
, set
\sigma(u)=M
where
M\in\mathcal{M}_{G}
is any element of
\mathcal{M}_{G}
of maximum size among the elements of
\mathcal{M}_{G}
that have not already been assigned to a node of
T
under
\sigma
. It is straightforward to verify that this process produces a bijection
\sigma:V(T)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G}
satisfying the conditions of Theorem
5.2
, so
T
is a clique tree of
G
.
∎

Corollary
5.1
extends the corollary of McMorris and Shier’s result that any threshold graph has a star graph as a clique tree. The clique tree universality property of threshold graphs is interesting because it does not hold for general chordal graphs, or even for general split graphs. For instance, the split graph

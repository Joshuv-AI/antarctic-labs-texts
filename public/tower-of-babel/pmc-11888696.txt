Exploring the Role of β-Sitosterol, Genistein, and Emodin in Periodontal Regeneration
Authors: Prabhu M. Natarajan, Mohamed A. Jaber, Vidhyarekha Umapathy, Bhuminathan Swamikannu, Manickam S Nandhini, Vijay B. Desai, Mohammad K. S. Khot
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe incidence rate of periodontal diseases is high, which affects the quality of life of patients, and is one of the main causes of adult dentition defects. Periodontal diseases cause the periodontal intraosseous defect. If not treated in time, it can lead to alveolar bone resorption and defect, periodontal attachment loss, teeth loosening, and complete tooth loss. Bone grafting is to repair periodontal defects by transplanting materials to restore the anatomical morphology of the alveolar bone and the function of periodontal tissue. This study evaluated the osteogenic activity of 3 phytochemicals - β-sitosterol, Genistein, and Emodin when compared to hydroxyapatite alone and tested the in vitro osteogenic activity of the prepared graft materials. The procedure for adhesion, viability, proliferation, and alkaline phosphatase activity was reported. The addition of phytochemicals to hydroxyapatite enhances its bioactivity. This is based on adhesion, viability, proliferation, and ALP activity assessed on the seventh and ninth days respectively in the prepared samples of the phytochemical. Based on the successful results of this study an efficient and cost-effective osteogenic bone graft with effective clinical application can be proposed. The phytochemicals β-Sitosterol, Genistein, and Emodin can be mixed in the optimum quantities to the hydroxyapatite grafts to be used as periodontal regenerative materials. In addition, such optimized concentrations can be further studied for their application to other alloplastic resorbable materials to be used for the grafting of larger defects.

INTRODUCTION

Periodontal diseases, encompassing gingivitis and periodontitis, are prevalent oral health conditions characterized by inflammation and damage to the tissues supporting teeth. Traditional treatment approaches focus on managing symptoms and preventing disease progression. However, emerging research suggests that natural compounds like β-sitosterol, genistein, and emodin hold promise in promoting periodontal regeneration. This article delves into the mechanisms and potential applications of these bioactive molecules in restoring periodontal health. Periodontal regeneration involves the restoration of lost or damaged periodontal tissues, including the alveolar bone, cementum, and periodontal ligament (PDL). The ideal regenerative therapy aims to recreate the complex architecture and functionality of these tissues, ultimately promoting tooth stability and function.[1]

CHALLENGES IN PERIODONTAL REGENERATION

Traditional approaches to periodontal therapy often fall short of achieving complete tissue regeneration. Current techniques such as guided tissue regeneration (GTR) and bone grafting have limitations, including donor site morbidity, limited tissue integration, and variable clinical outcomes. Moreover, the high cost and invasiveness of these procedures underscore the need for alternative regenerative strategies.[2]

EXPLORING NATURAL COMPOUNDS FOR PERIODONTAL REGENERATION

Nature has long been a source of inspiration for novel therapeutic agents, and natural compounds have gained attention for their potential role in periodontal regeneration. Among these, β-sitosterol, genistein, and emodin have emerged as promising candidates due to their diverse biological activities and minimal adverse effects.[3]

β-Sitosterol

β-Sitosterol—a plant sterol found abundantly in various fruits, vegetables, and nuts—has garnered interest for its cholesterol-lowering properties. Beyond its lipid-lowering effects, β-sitosterol exhibits anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and osteogenic activities, making it an attractive candidate for periodontal regeneration.[3]

Studies have shown that β-sitosterol enhances osteoblastic differentiation and mineralization, crucial processes for bone formation in periodontal defects. Additionally, its anti-inflammatory properties help mitigate periodontal inflammation, creating a favorable microenvironment for tissue regeneration. By modulating key signaling pathways involved in bone metabolism, β-sitosterol holds promise as a therapeutic agent for enhancing periodontal regeneration.[3]

Genistein

Genistein, a bioactive isoflavone found predominantly in soybeans, has gained attention for its diverse pharmacological effects, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and estrogenic activities. In the context of periodontal regeneration, genistein demonstrates remarkable osteogenic potential and modulates various cellular processes critical for tissue repair.[5]

Preclinical studies have highlighted genistein’s ability to stimulate osteoblastic differentiation and inhibit osteoclastic activity, thereby promoting bone formation and preventing alveolar bone resorption.[6] Moreover, its anti-inflammatory properties attenuate periodontal inflammation, fostering an environment conducive to tissue regeneration. These findings underscore genistein’s therapeutic potential for enhancing periodontal regeneration and warrant further investigation in clinical settings. In the context of periodontal regeneration, genistein exhibits several beneficial effects[6]:

Promotion of osteogenesis: The alveolar bone, similar to the bones in other body parts, is repeatedly resorbed by osteoclasts and renewed by osteogenic cells. Emodin

Emodin—a natural anthraquinone derivative abundant in medicinal plants like rhubarb—exhibits diverse pharmacological activities, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antimicrobial properties.[7] In the context of periodontal regeneration, emodin holds promise as a multifaceted therapeutic agent targeting various aspects of tissue repair and regeneration.

Studies have shown that emodin promotes osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) and enhances extracellular matrix (ECM) synthesis—crucial processes for periodontal tissue regeneration. Furthermore, its anti-inflammatory effects alleviate periodontal inflammation and mitigate tissue destruction, paving the way for enhanced healing outcomes. Emodin’s broad-spectrum antibacterial activity also contributes to periodontal health by inhibiting the growth of periodontopathogens, thus reducing the risk of disease recurrence. In the context of periodontal regeneration, emodin exerts the following effects[7]:

Inhibition of periodontal pathogens: Emodin exhibits antimicrobial activity against periodontal pathogens such as Porphyromonas gingivalis and Prevotella intermedia, which contribute to periodontal inflammation and tissue destruction.Reduction of the inflammatory response: Emodin suppresses the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and mediators, attenuating the inflammatory cascade in the periodontium and promoting tissue healing.Promotion of osteogenesis: Emodin has been shown to enhance osteoblast proliferation and differentiation, leading to increased bone formation and regeneration in periodontal defects.Antioxidant properties: Emodin scavenges free radicals and inhibits oxidative stress, protecting periodontal tissues from damage and supporting their regeneration.[7]

METHODOLOGY

Alamar blue viability assay

At specified periods, 100 mL of Alamar Blue dye (HIMEdia Labs, Mumbai, India) was added to each well and incubated for 4 h. Then, the medium (containing dye) was retrieved for analysis, and the wells were replaced with fresh medium. The absorbance of the retrieved medium was analyzed at 570 and 600 nm. The percentage reduction of each well was calculated using the standard formula for Alamar Blue. The experiment was run in triplicate. Viability was taken as follows: Viability = %Rt/%Rc multiplied by 100 where %Rt and %Rc are percentage reductions of Alamar Blue in test wells and control wells, respectively.[8]

Alamar blue proliferation assay

To assess the proliferation rate, the values of percentage reduction of respective wells on 96 h were compared to that on 48 h, using the following formula Rate of proliferation, P = R96 − R48/R48 multiplied by 100 where R96 and R48 are percentage reductions of viability tests conducted at 96 and 48 h, respectively.[8]

Alkaline phosphatase activity

At specified periods, the scaffolds were thoroughly washed with calcium- and phosphate-free phosphate buffer saline (PBS). Cells were lysed from the scaffolds using ice-cold lysis buffer (20 mM Tris-HCl – pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 1% v/v Triton X-100), followed by centrifugation at 3300g, at 4 C for 5 min. The supernatant was carefully retrieved and stored at 4 C until further use. The working solution of the alkaline phosphatase (ALP) kit was prepared as per the manufacturer’s guidelines. Lysis buffer was used as a reference for absorbance. Then, absorbance was measured at 405 nm for 10 min. The enzyme activity was calculated according to the manufacturer’s formula as follows: Enzyme activity, U = OD/t multiplied by 2750 where OD is change in absorbance, and t is time in minutes. The experiment was done in triplicate.[89]

DISCUSSION

Alamar blue viability analysis

The viability of cells on plain scaffolds after 48 h was about 93%, demonstrating the biocompatibility of the selected scaffold material. The viability levels were the same even after 96 h. The incorporation of β-Sitosterol, Genistein, and Emodin in hydroxyapatite did not bring about any significant change in the viability in the periods of 48 and 96 h. The results indicate that the hydroxyapatite–phytochemical system is highly biocompatible.[910]

Proliferation assay

The cells on hydroxyapatite had maximum proliferation (37%), followed by those on β-Sitosterol, Genistein, and Emodin in hydroxyapatite (22%). These values were significantly higher than those exhibited on hydroxyapatite (17%), suggesting that β-Sitosterol, Genistein, and Emodin in hydroxyapatite extract had better proliferative action on the cells. Nevertheless, the proliferation of β-Sitosterol, Genistein, and Emodin in hydroxyapatite was significantly higher than that of the hydroxyapatite group. Earlier studies on β-Sitosterol, Genistein, and Emodin in hydroxyapatite in the literature report their osteogenic activity; the significant improvement in proliferation can be attributed to the phytochemicals released from the respective herbal extracts.[10]

ALP activity

Analysis of ALP activity showed that cells seeded on all scaffolds showed similar activity on the fourth day. However, on the eighth day, cells on all scaffolds showed a remarkable increase in activity. This conforms to the usual pattern of ALP expression by bone-like cells. Cells on β-Sitosterol, Genistein, and Emodin in hydroxyapatite showed maximum activity, followed by the hydroxyapatite group. ALP is an early marker of differentiation, which reaches the peak of expression on the ninth day of seeding. The phytochemicals stimulate the receptors on the cells to initiate various pathways. Β-Sitosterol, Genistein, and Emodin in hydroxyapatite probably stimulated both proliferation and osteogenesis, but hydroxyapatite could only promote osteogenesis and not much proliferation. This requires an in-depth study of the phytochemical aspect in the future. Nevertheless, results suggest that the presence of the phytochemicals accelerates bone differentiation and growth. In a clinical condition, we can assume that the phytochemicals will be released at a slower rate than those in the cell culture wells. In addition, cells tend to grow towards the direction of stimulus of growth called chemokinesis. Since the signals for growth are received from the hydroxyapatite, it is more likely that the cells can gradually migrate. This kind of in-growth of cells can produce a strong mechanical bonding of scaffold to the bone in the future.[911]

CONCLUSION

The quest for effective regenerative therapies in periodontology has led researchers to explore the therapeutic potential of natural compounds such as β-sitosterol, genistein, and emodin.[12] These bioactive molecules exhibit diverse pharmacological activities that hold promise for promoting periodontal tissue regeneration while mitigating inflammation and microbial colonization. However, further research is warranted to elucidate their precise mechanisms of action and optimize their therapeutic applications in clinical settings.[13] By harnessing the therapeutic potential of nature’s bounty, we may pave the way for innovative regenerative strategies that revolutionize periodontal care and improve patient outcomes. β-Sitosterol, genistein, and emodin represent promising natural compounds with potential applications in periodontal regeneration.[14] Through their diverse pharmacological effects, including the promotion of osteogenesis, modulation of inflammation, and antioxidant properties, these compounds offer exciting prospects for enhancing the outcomes of periodontal therapy. Further research is needed to fully elucidate their mechanisms of action and optimize their clinical utility in the treatment of periodontal diseases. Nevertheless, harnessing the regenerative potential of these natural compounds holds great promise for improving the management and long-term outcomes of periodontal regeneration.[1516]

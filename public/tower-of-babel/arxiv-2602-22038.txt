Quantitative entropy estimates for 2D stochastic vortex model on the whole space under moderate interactions
Alexandre B. de Souza

Abstract
We derive quantitative estimates for the stochastic 2D vortex model on the whole Euclidean space from stochastic particle systems driven by individual and environmental noises, obtaining pathwise quantitative bounds in the terms of relative entropy. The main novelties lie in the application of the Donsker–Varadhan inequality within the framework of moderately interacting particle systems to handle the nonlinear terms, as well as in the introduction of localization techniques combined with a probabilistic data setting, in order to derive estimates for the quadratic variation terms. Moreover, we prove the existence of a suitable solution to the aforementioned model.

Introduction

In this paper, we establish a quantitative derivation of the stochastic 2D vortex model in the whole Euclidean space, which is a particular case of the following stochastic Fokker–Planck equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}=\Delta\rho_{t}\,dt+\frac{1}{2}D^{2}\rho_{t}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})_{t}\,dt-\nabla\cdot(\rho_{t}(K\ast\rho_{t}))\,dt-\nabla\rho_{t}\cdot\sigma_{t}\,dB_{t},

(1)

from a stochastic moderately interacting particle system given by

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{i,N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(K\ast V^{N}\right)\left(X_{t}^{i,N}-X_{t}^{k,N}\right)\,dt+\sqrt{2}\,dW_{t}^{i,N}+\sigma_{t}\,dB_{t},\quad

(2)

In the particle system (

2.5

We now remove the stopping time
\tau^{N}

and

First, since

[
25
]

3.2
Inequalities

In this section, we present several inequalities used throughout the text. The first one is the classical Csiszár-Kullback-Pinsker inequality, which is a fundamental result in information theory. It quantifies how control of the relative entropy implies control of the total variation distance.

Lemma 3

(Csiszár-Kullback-Pinsker inequality)
.

It holds for
f,g\in\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,

\displaystyle\|f-g\|_{1}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{H}(f|g).

(81)

Now, we recall the Donsker–Varadhan inequality (see
[
23
]
) which follows from the variational representation of the relative entropy. It provides a tool to estimate the expectation of a test function under a non-factorized probability density and, in particular, allows for a change of measure between two probability measures.

Lemma 4

(Donsker-Varadhan Inequality)
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,
\eta>0
and a function
\Phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
g\exp{\left(\Phi/\eta\right)}\in L^{1}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\Phi\,dx\leq\eta\mathcal{H}\left(f|g\right)+\eta\ln{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\exp{\left(\Phi/\eta\right)}\,dx\right)}.

(82)

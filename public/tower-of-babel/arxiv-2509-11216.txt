Wrapped D4-branes from maximal 6D gauged supergravity


Abstract
We study a large class of domain wall solutions with M ​ k ​ w 3 × Σ 2 Mkw_{3}\times\Sigma^{2} and M ​ k ​ w 2 × Σ 3 Mkw_{2}\times\Sigma^{3} slices from maximal gauged supergravity in six dimensions. Σ 2 \Sigma^{2} and Σ 3 \Sigma^{3} are given by a Riemann surface and a 3 3 -manifold with constant curvature while M ​ k ​ w 3 , 2 Mkw_{3,2} denotes three/two-dimensional Minkowski space. We consider the maximal gauged supergravity with C ​ S ​ O ​ ( p , q , 5 − p − q ) CSO(p,q,5-p-q) and C ​ S ​ O ​ ( p , q , 4 − p − q ) ⋉ ℝ 4 CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4} gauge groups arising from an S 1 S^{1} reduction of seven-dimensional maximal gauged supergravity with C ​ S ​ O ​ ( p , q , 5 − p − q ) CSO(p,q,5-p-q) and C ​ S ​ O ​ ( p , q , 4 − p − q ) CSO(p,q,4-p-q) gauge groups. The two types of gauge group can be embedded in type IIA theory via consistent truncations on H p , q × ℝ 5 − p − q H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{5-p-q} and H p , q × ℝ 4 − p − q × S 1 H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{4-p-q}\times S^{1} , respectively. By performing topological twists on Σ 2 \Sigma^{2} and Σ 3 \Sigma^{3} , we find a number of solutions interpolating between locally flat domain walls in the UV and curved domain walls with M ​ k ​ w 3 × Σ 2 Mkw_{3}\times\Sigma^{2} and M ​ k ​ w 2 × Σ 3 Mkw_{2}\times\Sigma^{3} slices in the IR. Many solutions admit physical IR singularities and can be interpreted as holographic RG flows across dimensions from five-dimensional field theories to three- and two-dimensional non-conformal field theories in the IR. Upon uplifted to type IIA theory, we expect the solutions to describe brane configurations involving D4-branes wrapped on Σ 2 \Sigma^{2} and Σ 3 \Sigma^{3} .

N=(2,2)
gauged supergravity constructed in
[
44
]
by using the embedding tensor formalism. We mainly consider
CSO(p,q,5-p-q)
and

The bosonic field content of the theory consists of the vielbein
e^{\hat{\mu}}_{\mu}
, two-form potentials
B_{\mu\nu,M}=(B_{\mu\nu,m},{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m})
, three-forms
C_{\mu\nu\rho,A}
, vector fields
{A_{\mu}}^{A}
, and scalars
{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
that parametrize the coset space
SO(5,5)/\left(SO(5)\times SO(5)\right)
. We will use the convention that curved and flat space-time indices are denoted by
\mu
,
\nu
and
\hat{\mu}
,
\hat{\nu}
, respectively. Lower and upper
m,n,\ldots=1,\ldots,5
indices respectively label fundamental and anti-fundamental representations of
GL(5)\subset SO(5,5)
. The index
A=1,2,\ldots,16
describes Majorana-Weyl spinors of the
SO(5,5)
duality symmetry, and the indices
\alpha=1,2,3,4
and
\dot{\alpha}=\dot{1},\dot{2},\dot{3},\dot{4}
are respectively two sets of
SO(5)
spinor indices in the
SO(5)\times SO(5)
local symmetry. For spinor fields, there are gravitini
\psi_{+\mu\alpha}
and
\psi_{-\mu\dot{\alpha}}
, and the spin-
\frac{1}{2}
fields
\chi_{+a\dot{\alpha}}
and
\chi_{-\dot{a}\alpha}
where
a,=1,\ldots,5
and
\dot{a}=\dot{1},\ldots,\dot{5}
are respectively two sets of
SO(5)
vector indices in
SO(5)\times SO(5)
. Indices
M,N,\ldots=1,2,\ldots,10
denote
SO(5,5)
vector representation. We use
\pm
to indicate space-time chiralities of the spinors.

We also note that in ungauged supergravity, only the electric two-forms
B_{\mu\nu,m}
appear in the Lagrangian while the magnetic duals
{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m}
are introduced on-shell. Therefore, only the
GL(5)
subgroup of the full global
SO(5,5)
symmetry is a manifest off-shell symmetry. When the gaugings involve a symmetry that is not a subgroup of the off-shell
GL(5)
symmetry, the magnetic two-forms are introduced in the gauged Lagrangian via the topological term. The electric and magnetic two-forms are combined into a vector representation of the global symmetry group
SO(5,5)
denoted by
B_{\mu\nu,M}=(B_{\mu\nu,m},{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m})
.

2.1
The maximal gauged supergravity in six dimensions

The most general gaugings can be described by the embedding tensor
{\Theta_{A}}^{MN}
which implements the minimal coupling of various fields via the covariant derivative

D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-g{A_{\mu}}^{A}\ {\Theta_{A}}^{MN}\boldsymbol{t}_{MN}\,.

(2.1)

It should also be pointed out that the gauge coupling constant
g
can also be absorbed in the definition of the embedding tensor. The gauge generators
X_{A}={\Theta_{A}}^{MN}\boldsymbol{t}_{MN}
of a gauge group
G_{0}\subset SO(5,5)
are given by particular linear combinations of the
SO(5,5)
generators
\boldsymbol{t}_{MN}
.

Supersymmetry requires the embedding tensor to transform as
\mathbf{144}_{c}
representation of
SO(5,5)
. Accordingly,
{\Theta_{A}}^{MN}
can be written in terms of a vector-spinor representation
\theta^{AM}
of
SO(5,5)
as

{\Theta_{A}}^{MN}\ =\ -\theta^{B[M}(\Gamma^{N]})_{BA}\ \equiv\ \left(\Gamma^{[M}\theta^{N]}\right)_{A}\,.

(2.2)

\theta^{AM}
is subject to the constraint

(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}\,\theta^{BM}\ =\ 0

(2.3)

where
(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}
are symmetric
SO(5,5)
gamma matrices.

With the
SO(5,5)
generators in vector and spinor representations given by

{(\boldsymbol{t}_{MN})_{P}}^{Q}=4\eta_{P[M}\delta^{Q}_{N]}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{(\boldsymbol{t}_{MN})_{A}}^{B}\ =\ {(\Gamma_{MN})_{A}}^{B},

(2.4)

the corresponding gauge generators take the forms

\displaystyle{(X_{A})_{M}}^{N}=2\left(\Gamma_{M}\theta^{N}\right)_{A}+2\left(\Gamma^{N}\theta_{M}\right)_{A}

and

\displaystyle{(X_{A})_{B}}^{C}=\left(\Gamma^{M}\theta^{N}\right)_{A}{(\Gamma_{MN})_{B}}^{C}\,.

(2.5)

The
SO(5,5)
invariant tensor is given by

\eta_{MN}\ =\ \eta^{MN}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&\mathds{1}_{5}\\
\mathds{1}_{5}&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.6)

Consistency requires the generators
X_{A}
to form a closed subalgebra of
SO(5,5)

\left[X_{A},X_{B}\right]\ =\ -{(X_{A})_{B}}^{C}\,X_{C}\,.

(2.7)

Therefore, the embedding tensor needs to satisfy the quadratic constraints of the form

\theta^{AM}\theta^{BN}\eta_{MN}\ =\ 0,\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\theta^{AM}\theta^{B[N}(\Gamma^{P]})_{AB}=0\,.

(2.8)

By decomposing
\theta^{AM}=(\theta^{Am},{\theta^{A}}_{m})
under
GL(5)\subset SO(5,5)
, gaugings triggered by
\theta^{Am}
are electric in the sense that only electric two-forms participate in the gauged theory. On the other hand, in magnetic gaugings triggered by
{\theta^{A}}_{m}
, it is necessary to introduce magnetic two-forms together with additional three-form tensor fields. This leads to a hierarchy of non-abelian vectors and tensor fields of various ranks.

For all solutions considered in this paper, all of the two-form and three-form fields can be consistently truncated out. Accordingly, we will set all these fields to zero from now on for brevity. In addition, the ansatze for the gauge fields relevant for performing topological twists lead to identically vanishing topological terms. With all these, the bosonic Lagrangian of the six-dimensional
N=(2,2)
gauged supergravity can be written as

e^{-1}\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{4}R-\frac{1}{16}{P_{\mu}}^{a\dot{a}}{P^{\mu}}_{a\dot{a}}-\mathbf{V}-\frac{1}{4}M_{AB}{\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}}^{A}\mathcal{H}^{\mu\nu,B}\,.

(2.9)

With vanishing two-form fields, the covariant gauge field strength tensors are simply given by the usual non-abelian gauge field strengths

{\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}}^{A}=2\partial_{[\mu}{A_{\nu]}}^{A}+g{X_{[BC]}}^{A}{A_{\mu}}^{B}{A_{\nu}}^{C}\,.

(2.10)

The scalar matrix
M_{AB}
is defined by

M_{AB}=\Omega_{\alpha\beta}\Omega_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}{V_{B}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}

(2.11)

with
\Omega_{\alpha\beta}
and
\Omega_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}
being the
USp(4)\sim SO(5)
symplectic forms. Together with the complex counjugate
\Omega^{\alpha\beta}=(\Omega_{\alpha\beta})^{*}
and
\Omega^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}=(\Omega_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})^{*}
, these symplectic forms can be used to raise and lower
\alpha
and
\dot{\alpha}
indices.

The scalar fields are described by the
16\times 16
coset representative
{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
in chiral spinor representation of
SO(5,5)
. The inverse of the coset representative will be denoted by
{V^{A}}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
. The kinetic term for scalar fields is given in terms of the scalar vielbein

{P_{\mu}}^{a\dot{a}}=\frac{1}{4}{(\gamma^{a})}_{\alpha\beta}{(\gamma^{\dot{a}})}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}V^{A\alpha\dot{\alpha}}D_{\mu}{V_{A}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}

(2.12)

with
{(\gamma^{a})}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{4}\Omega_{\beta\gamma}{(\gamma^{a})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\gamma\dot{\alpha}}
,
{(\gamma^{\dot{a}})}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}=\frac{1}{4}\Omega_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}{(\gamma^{\dot{a}})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}
and
V^{A\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\Omega^{\alpha\beta}\Omega^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}{V^{A}}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}
.

The scalar potential can be written in terms of the embedding tensor and the coset representative as

\mathbf{V}=\frac{g^{2}}{2}\theta^{AM}\theta^{BN}{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{a}{\mathcal{V}_{N}}^{b}\left[{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}{(\gamma_{a})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}{(\gamma_{b})_{\beta}}^{\gamma}V_{B\gamma\dot{\alpha}}\right].

(2.13)

The coset representative in vector representation
{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{\underline{A}}=({\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{a},{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{\dot{a}})
is related to
{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
by the following relations

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}V^{A\alpha\dot{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}{(\gamma^{a})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}{V^{B}}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\textrm{and}\qquad{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{\dot{a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16}V^{A\alpha\dot{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}{(\gamma^{\dot{a}})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}{V^{B}}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\,.

(2.15)

In these relations,
{(\Gamma_{\underline{A}})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}=({(\gamma_{a})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}},{(\gamma_{\dot{a}})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}})
are
SO(5,5)
gamma matrices in a basis with diagonal
\eta_{\underline{A}\underline{B}}
.

We finally note the fermionic supersymmetry transformations of fermionic fields

\displaystyle\delta\psi_{+\mu\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\epsilon_{+\alpha}+\frac{g}{4}\hat{\gamma}_{\mu}{T_{\alpha}}^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}}+\frac{1}{8}({\hat{\gamma}_{\mu}}^{\ \nu\rho}-6\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}\hat{\gamma}^{\rho}){\mathcal{H}_{\nu\rho}}^{A}\Omega_{\alpha\beta}{V_{A}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}},

(2.16)

\displaystyle\delta\psi_{-\mu\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}-\frac{g}{4}\hat{\gamma}_{\mu}{T^{\beta}}_{\dot{\alpha}}\epsilon_{+\beta}+\frac{1}{8}({\hat{\gamma}_{\mu}}^{\ \nu\rho}-6\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}\hat{\gamma}^{\rho}){\mathcal{H}_{\nu\rho}}^{A}\Omega_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}{V_{A}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{+\beta},

(2.17)

\displaystyle\delta\chi_{+a\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}{P^{\mu}}_{a\dot{a}}\hat{\gamma}_{\mu}{(\gamma^{\dot{a}})_{\dot{\alpha}}}^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}}+2g{(T_{a})^{\beta}}_{\dot{\alpha}}\epsilon_{+\beta}-\frac{g}{2}{T^{\alpha}}_{\dot{\alpha}}{(\gamma_{a})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\epsilon_{+\beta}

(2.18)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}}^{A}{(\gamma_{a})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\hat{\gamma}^{\mu\nu}\Omega_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}{V_{A}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{+\beta},

\displaystyle\delta\chi_{-\dot{a}\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}{P^{\mu}}_{a\dot{a}}\hat{\gamma}_{\mu}{(\gamma^{a})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\epsilon_{+\beta}+2g{(T_{\dot{a}})_{\alpha}}^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}}+\frac{g}{2}{T_{\alpha}}^{\dot{\alpha}}{(\gamma_{\dot{a}})_{\dot{\alpha}}}^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}}

(2.19)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}}^{A}{(\gamma_{\dot{a}})_{\dot{\alpha}}}^{\dot{\beta}}\hat{\gamma}^{\mu\nu}\Omega_{\alpha\beta}{V_{A}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}}\,.

The covariant derivatives of supersymmetry parameters,
\epsilon_{+\alpha}
and
\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}
, are defined by

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\epsilon_{+\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\epsilon_{+\alpha}+\frac{1}{4}{\omega_{\mu}}^{\nu\rho}\hat{\gamma}_{\nu\rho}\epsilon_{+\alpha}+\frac{1}{4}{Q_{\mu}}^{ab}{(\gamma_{ab})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\epsilon_{+\beta},

(2.20)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{4}{\omega_{\mu}}^{\nu\rho}\hat{\gamma}_{\nu\rho}\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{4}{Q_{\mu}}^{\dot{a}\dot{b}}{(\gamma_{\dot{a}\dot{b}})_{\dot{\alpha}}}^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}}

(2.21)

with space-time gamma matrices denoted by
\hat{\gamma}_{\mu}=e_{\mu}^{\hat{\mu}}\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{\mu}}
. The
SO(5)\times SO(5)
composite connections,
Q_{\mu}^{ab}
and
Q_{\mu}^{\dot{a}\dot{b}}
, are given by

\displaystyle{Q_{\mu}}^{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}{(\gamma^{ab})}_{\alpha\beta}\Omega_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}V^{A\alpha\dot{\alpha}}D_{\mu}{V_{A}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}},

(2.22)

\displaystyle{Q_{\mu}}^{\dot{a}\dot{b}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\Omega_{\alpha\beta}{(\gamma^{\dot{a}\dot{b}})}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}V^{A\alpha\dot{\alpha}}D_{\mu}{V_{A}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}.

(2.23)

The T-tensors appearing in the fermionic supersymmetry transformations are defined as

(T^{a})^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}={\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{a}\theta^{AM}{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad(T^{\dot{a}})^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=-{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{\dot{a}}\theta^{AM}{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}

(2.25)

In terms of these T-tensors, the scalar potential can alternatively be written as

\mathbf{V}=g^{2}\left[(T^{a})^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}(T_{a})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{2}T^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}T_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\right].

D_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}-gA_{\mu,m}X^{m}-g{A_{\mu}}^{mn}X_{mn}-gA_{\mu}^{\ast}X_{\ast}

\mathbf{16}_{c}

Matrices
\mathbb{T}^{Am}
,
\mathbb{T}^{A}_{mn}
, and

,
\mathbb{T}_{A}^{mn}
, and
\mathbb{T}_{A\ast}

By the decomposition of

\mathbf{10}\ \rightarrow\ \mathbf{5}^{+2}\,\oplus\,\overline{\mathbf{5}}^{-2}
, we can write the two components

and

of the embedding tensor as

\displaystyle\theta^{Am}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\qquad\overline{\mathbf{5}}^{+3}\,\oplus\,\mathbf{10}^{-1}\,\oplus\,\mathbf{24}^{-5}\,\oplus\,\overline{\mathbf{40}}^{-1},

(2.31)

\displaystyle{\theta^{A}}_{m}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\qquad\overline{\mathbf{5}}^{+3}\,\oplus\,\mathbf{5}^{+7}\,\oplus\,\mathbf{10}^{-1}\,\oplus\,\mathbf{15}^{-1}\,\oplus\,\overline{\mathbf{45}}^{+3}\,.

(2.32)

The gaugings obtained by these components have been extensively studied in
[

]
and
[
46
]
. In this paper, we will only consider gaugings arising from

and

The gaugings from the component

component of the uplifted metric to ten or eleven dimensions. As in

3.2
D4-branes wrapped on a Riemannian three-manifold

We now carry out a similar analysis for supersymmetric solutions describing D4-branes wrapped on a Riemannian three-manifold
\Sigma^{3}_{k}
. These solutions would lead to two-dimensional field theories arising from twisted compactifications of the maximal SYM theory in five dimensions. As in the previous case, we will consider the case in which
\Sigma^{3}_{k}
has constant curvature characterized by a parameter
k=1,0,-1
corresponding to a three-dimensional sphere
S^{3}
, a flat space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, or a hyperbolic space
H^{3}
, respectively.

The ansatz for the metric takes the form of

ds_{6}^{2}=e^{2U(r)}\eta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}dx^{\bar{\mu}}dx^{\bar{\nu}}+dr^{2}+e^{2V(r)}ds^{2}_{\Sigma^{3}_{k}}

(3.67)

where
\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu}=0,1
are spacetime indices of a two-dimensional Minkowski space
Mkw_{2}
. The metric on
\Sigma^{3}_{k}
is given by

ds^{2}_{\Sigma^{3}_{k}}=d\psi^{2}+f_{k}(\psi)^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}{\theta}d\zeta^{2})

(3.68)

with the function
f_{k}(\psi)
defined as in (
3.11
). Using the vielbein

\displaystyle e^{\hat{\bar{\mu}}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pte^{U}dx^{\bar{\mu}},\qquad e^{\hat{r}}=dr,\qquad e^{\hat{\psi}}=e^{V}d\psi,

\displaystyle e^{\hat{\theta}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pte^{V}f_{k}(\psi)d\theta,\qquad e^{\hat{\zeta}}=e^{V}f_{k}(\psi)\sin{\theta}d\zeta,

(3.69)

we find non-vanishing components of the spin connection as follows

\displaystyle{\omega_{\hat{\bar{\mu}}}}^{\hat{\bar{\nu}}\hat{r}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046ptU^{\prime}\delta_{\hat{\bar{\mu}}}^{\hat{\bar{\nu}}},\qquad{\omega_{\hat{i}}}^{\hat{j}\hat{r}}=V^{\prime}\delta_{\hat{i}}^{\hat{j}},\qquad{\omega_{\hat{\theta}}}^{\hat{\theta}\hat{\psi}}=e^{-V}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\psi)}{f_{k}(\psi)},

\displaystyle{\omega_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\psi}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pte^{-V}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\psi)}{f_{k}(\psi)},\qquad{\omega_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\theta}}=e^{-V}\frac{\cot\theta}{f_{k}(\psi)}

(3.70)

where
\hat{i}=\hat{\psi},\hat{\theta},\hat{\zeta}
is a flat index on
\Sigma^{3}_{k}
. We will perform two types of topological twists by turning on
SO(3)
and
SU(2)
gauge fields in order to eliminate the following components of the spin connection
{\omega_{\hat{\theta}}}^{\hat{\theta}\hat{\psi}}
,
{\omega_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\psi}}
, and
{\omega_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\theta}}
.

We first consider the case of

twist with

embedded in

as

subgroup. All of these gauge groups are characterized by the embedding tensor of the form

These gauge groups are given by

(
\kappa=\lambda=1
),

(
\kappa=-\lambda=1
),
SO(3,2)
(
\kappa=\lambda=-1
),
CSO(4,0,1)
(
\kappa=1
,

),
CSO(3,1,1)
(
\kappa=-1
,
\lambda=0
), and
CSO(3,0,2)
(
\kappa=\lambda=0
).

We then turn on the
SO(3)
gauge fields corresponding to
X_{12}
,
X_{13}
, and
X_{23}
generators by the following ansatz

{A_{\hat{\theta}}}^{12}=e^{-V}\frac{p}{4k}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\psi)}{f_{k}(\psi)},\ \ \ {A_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{13}=e^{-V}\frac{p}{4k}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\psi)}{f_{k}(\psi)},\ \ \ {A_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{23}=e^{-V}\frac{p}{4k}\frac{\cot\theta}{f_{k}(\psi)}\,.\

(3.72)

In addition to the dilaton, there are four

singlet scalars corresponding to the following non-compact generators

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{1\dot{1}}+2\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{2\dot{2}}+2\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{3}}-3\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}}-3\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{5\dot{5}},\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}\,=\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{5}},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}}-\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{5\dot{5}},\qquad\quad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{4}\,=\,\boldsymbol{s}_{45}\,.

With the coset representative

(3.74)

the scalar potential takes the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g^{2}e^{2(\varphi-8\phi_{1})}}{4}\left[3+6e^{20\phi_{1}}\left((\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa-\lambda)\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)\phantom{\frac{1}{2}}\right.

(3.75)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{e^{40\phi_{1}}}{4}\left(\kappa^{2}+10\kappa\lambda+\lambda^{2}-(3\kappa^{2}-2\kappa\lambda+3\lambda^{2})\cosh{8\phi_{3}}\phantom{\frac{1}{2}}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\phantom{\frac{1}{2}}-2(\kappa+\lambda)^{2}\cosh{4\phi_{2}}\cosh^{2}{4\phi_{3}}+4(\kappa^{2}-\lambda^{2})\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\sinh{8\phi_{3}}\right)\right].

composite connections are given as follows

\displaystyle{Q_{r}}^{45}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varsigma^{\prime}-4\varsigma(\varphi^{\prime}-3\phi^{\prime}_{1})-\phi^{\prime}_{2}\sinh 4\phi_{3},

(3.76)

\displaystyle{Q_{r}}^{\dot{4}\dot{5}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varsigma^{\prime}+4\varsigma(\varphi^{\prime}-3\phi^{\prime}_{1})-\phi^{\prime}_{2}\sinh 4\phi_{3},

(3.77)

\displaystyle{Q_{\hat{\theta}}}^{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{Q_{\hat{\theta}}}^{\dot{1}\dot{2}}\ =\ -e^{-V}\frac{gp}{k}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\psi)}{f_{k}(\psi)},

(3.78)

\displaystyle{Q_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{13}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{Q_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\dot{1}\dot{3}}\ =\ -e^{-V}\frac{gp}{k}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\psi)}{f_{k}(\psi)},

(3.79)

\displaystyle{Q_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{23}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{Q_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\dot{2}\dot{3}}\ =\ -e^{-V}\frac{gp}{k}\frac{\cot\theta}{f_{k}(\psi)}\,.

(3.80)

The relevant topological twist can be achieved by imposing the projectors

\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{\theta}\hat{\psi}}\epsilon_{+\alpha}={(\gamma_{12})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\epsilon_{+\beta}\quad\text{ and }\quad\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\theta}}\epsilon_{+\alpha}={(\gamma_{23})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\epsilon_{+\beta}

(3.81)

as well as

\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{\theta}\hat{\psi}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}={(\gamma_{\dot{1}\dot{2}})_{\dot{\alpha}}}^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\theta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}={(\gamma_{\dot{2}\dot{3}})_{\dot{\alpha}}}^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}}

(3.82)

together with a twist condition

gp=k\,.

(3.83)

In order to consistently truncate out all the two- and three-form fields, we need to set
\varsigma=0
for
p\neq 0
. This condition leads to a consistent set of BPS equations that are compatible with all the second-ordered field equations.

With
\varsigma=0
and the projector
\hat{\gamma}_{r}\epsilon_{\pm}=\epsilon_{\mp}
, we find the following set of BPS equations

\displaystyle U^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ge^{\varphi-8\phi_{1}}}{4\sqrt{2}}\left[3+e^{20\phi_{1}}\left((\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa-\lambda)\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)\right]

(3.84)

\displaystyle-\frac{3p}{8\sqrt{2}}e^{-2V-\varphi+8\phi_{1}},

\displaystyle V^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ge^{\varphi-8\phi_{1}}}{4\sqrt{2}}\left[3+e^{20\phi_{1}}\left((\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa-\lambda)\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)\right]

(3.85)

\displaystyle+\frac{5p}{8\sqrt{2}}e^{-2V-\varphi+8\phi_{1}},

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ge^{\varphi-8\phi_{1}}}{20\sqrt{2}}\left[3+e^{20\phi_{1}}\left((\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa-\lambda)\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)\right]

(3.86)

\displaystyle+\frac{3p}{40\sqrt{2}}e^{-2V-\varphi+8\phi_{1}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ge^{\varphi-8\phi_{1}}}{10\sqrt{2}}\left[2-e^{20\phi_{1}}\left((\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa-\lambda)\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)\right]

(3.87)

\displaystyle-\frac{p}{10\sqrt{2}}e^{-2V-\varphi+8\phi_{1}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ge^{\varphi+12\phi_{1}}}{\sqrt{2}}(\kappa+\lambda)\sinh{2\phi_{2}}\,\text{sech }{4\phi_{3}},

(3.88)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ge^{\varphi+12\phi_{1}}}{2\sqrt{2}}\left((\kappa-\lambda)\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)

(3.89)

with the Killing spinors given by

\epsilon_{+}(r)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\left[U(r)+W(r)\gamma_{45}\right]}\epsilon_{+}^{0}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\epsilon_{-}(r)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\left[U(r)-W(r)\gamma_{\dot{4}\dot{5}}\right]}\epsilon_{-}^{0}\,.

fixed points in these equations. Before giving the solutions, we note that for the

16

We begin with the simplest case of
CSO(3,0,2)

\kappa=\lambda=0

There is another singularity as
\rho\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\sim

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{8}\ln\rho,\qquad\qquad V\sim\frac{5}{8}\ln\rho,

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\sim

\displaystyle\frac{3}{40}\ln\rho,\qquad\phi_{1}\sim-\frac{1}{10}\ln\rho\,.

[

For

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\ln\rho,\qquad\qquad V\sim\frac{7}{4}\ln\rho,

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\sim

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{20}\ln\rho^{2},\qquad\phi_{1}\sim\frac{1}{5}\ln\rho\,.

two-dimensional field theory arising from D4-branes wrapped on

\lambda=0
, we can analytically solve these equations. The resulting solution is given by

\displaystyle U=-5\varphi,\qquad V=\ln\left[\frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{2}g}\right]-5\varphi,

\displaystyle\phi=-\varphi,\qquad\varphi=C-\frac{k\rho^{2}}{20}+\frac{3}{40}\ln\rho

(3.111)

with
C
being an integration constant. The new radial coordinate
\rho
is defined by
\frac{d\rho}{dr}=e^{-V}
. At large
\rho
, we find that the solution becomes a locally flat domain wall with the following asymptotic behavior

U\sim V\sim\frac{k\rho^{2}}{4},\qquad\varphi\sim-\frac{k\rho^{2}}{20},\qquad\phi\sim\frac{k\rho^{2}}{20}\,.

(3.112)

On the other hand, as
\rho\rightarrow 0
, the above solution reduces to

U\sim-\frac{3}{8}\ln\rho,\qquad V\sim\ln\left[\frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{2}g}\right]-\frac{3}{8}\ln\rho,\qquad\varphi\sim-\phi\sim\frac{3}{40}\ln\rho.

(3.113)

However, near this singularity, both the effective potential and the type IIA metric component
\hat{g}_{00}
diverge to
+\infty
leading to solutions with unphysical IR singularities.

In
SO(5)
and
SO(4,1)
gauge groups with
\lambda\neq 0
, the BPS equations can only be solved numerically. We begin with solutions that, as
r\rightarrow+\infty
, are asymptotic to the locally flat domain wall with
SO(5)
symmetry of the form

U\sim V\sim 5\ln gr,\qquad\varphi\sim-\ln gr,\qquad\phi\sim\frac{1}{g^{16}r^{16}}\,.

(3.114)

Examples of these solutions are given in figures

and
35
for different values of
g
. From these solutions, we find that all solutions can be uplifted to solutions of type IIA theory with physical IR singularities for any values of
g
.

Another class of solutions has an asymptotic geometry in the form of a locally flat domain wall with

4
Wrapped D4-branes from
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
gauged supergravity

We repeat the same analysis for gaugings from
\overline{\mathbf{40}}^{-1}
representation associated with the embedding tensor

\theta^{Am}=\mathbb{T}^{A}_{np}U^{np,m}\quad\text{and}\quad{\theta^{A}}_{m}\ =\ 0\,.

(4.1)

The tensor
U^{mn,p}
satisfies the conditions
U^{mn,p}=U^{[mn],p}
with
U^{[mn,p]}=0
, and the quadratic constraint determines
U^{mn,p}
to take the form

U^{mn,p}\ =\ v^{[m}w^{n]p}

(4.2)

in which
v^{m}
is a
GL(5)
vector, and
w^{mn}
is a symmetric tensor,
w^{mn}=w^{(mn)}
.

Following
[
48
]
, we can use the
SL(5)
symmetry to fix the vector
v^{m}=\delta^{m}_{5}
and split the
SL(5)
index
m=(i,5)
,
i=1,..,4
. For simplicity, we will also set
w^{i5}=w^{55}=0
. The remaining
SL(4)\subset SL(5)
residual symmetry can be used to diagonalize the
4\times 4
block corresponding to
w^{ij}
as

w^{ij}\ =\ \text{diag}(\underbrace{1,..,1}_{p},\underbrace{-1,..,-1}_{q},\underbrace{0,..,0}_{r})

(4.3)

with
p+q+r=4
. From the decomposition in (
2.28
), the only non-vanishing gauge generators are given by

X_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varepsilon_{ijkk^{\prime}}w^{kl}{\boldsymbol{t}^{k^{\prime}}}_{l}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad X^{i}=w^{ij}\boldsymbol{s}_{5j}\,.

(4.4)

These gauge generators satisfy the following commutation relations

[X^{i},X^{j}]=0,\qquad[X_{ij},X^{k}]={(X_{ij})_{l}}^{k}X^{l},\qquad[X_{ij},X_{kl}]={(X_{ij})_{kl}}^{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}X_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}

(4.5)

where
{(X_{ij})_{kl}}^{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}=2{(X_{ij})_{[k}}^{[k^{\prime}}\delta_{l]}^{l^{\prime}]}
. The corresponding gauge group takes the form

G_{0}=CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}=SO(p,q)\ltimes\left(\mathbb{R}^{(p+q)(4-p-q)}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right).

(4.6)

The
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\subset SL(4)
factor and the four-dimensional translation group from the shift symmetries
\mathbb{R}^{4}
are respectively generated by
X_{ij}
and
X^{i}
. We recall that gaugings in
\overline{\mathbf{40}}^{-1}
representation are purely electric, so the three-form
C_{\mu\nu\rho,A}
and magnetic two-form
{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m}
fields do not appear. Furthermore, we will set the four gauge fields
A_{\mu,i}
corresponding to the shift symmetry generators
X^{i}
to zero. It should also be pointed out that this six-dimensional gauged supergravity can be obtained from a dimensional reduction of the maximal gauged supergravity in seven dimensions with
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)
gauge group on a circle
S^{1}
.

For scalar fields, we split the
SO(5)\times SO(5)
vector indices as
a=(i,5)
and
\dot{a}=(\dot{i},\dot{5})
resulting in the decomposition of
SL(5)
non-compact generators as

\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{a\dot{b}}\rightarrow\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{i\dot{j}},\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{i\dot{5}},\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{5\dot{5}}\right).

(4.7)

Since the
SL(5)
generators
\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{a\dot{b}}
are traceless, the generator
\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{5\dot{5}}
is related to the trace part of
\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{i\dot{j}}
according to the relation
\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{1\dot{1}}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{2\dot{2}}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{3\dot{3}}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{4\dot{4}}=-\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{5\dot{5}}
. It is then convenient to define new non-compact generators
\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}_{i\dot{j}}
as

\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}_{i\dot{j}}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{i\dot{j}}+\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{5\dot{5}}\delta_{i\dot{j}}

(4.8)

which are symmetric and traceless. The nine scalar fields corresponding to these generators then parametrize an
SL(4)/SO(4)
coset. The other four scalars associated with
\tilde{\boldsymbol{t}}_{i\dot{5}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{i\dot{5}}
are nilpotent scalars and will be denoted by
b_{i}
. The remaining scalar in
SL(5)/SO(5)
coset is another dilatonic scalar corresponding to the
SO(1,1)
factor in
SL(4)\times SO(1,1)\subset SL(5)
. In addition, there are additional ten axionic scalars corresponding to the antisymmetric shift generators as in the previous section.

As previously mentioned, the six-dimensional gauged supergravity with
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
gauge group can be embedded in type IIA theory via a consistent truncation on
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{4-p-q}\times S^{1}
. The truncation of type IIA theory on
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{4-p-q}
leading to the maximal gauged supergravity in seven dimensions with
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)
gauge group has been studied in
[
51
]
. In particular, the
(00)
-component of the ten-dimensional metric is given, in the present notation, by

\hat{g}_{00}=e^{-3\phi_{0}}\Delta^{\frac{1}{4}}g^{(7)}_{00}\,.

(4.9)

\phi_{0}
is the dilatonic scalar corresponding to
SO(1,1)
factor in the decomposition
SL(5)\rightarrow SL(4)\times SO(1,1)
as mentioned above. The warp factor
\Delta
is defined by

\Delta=m^{ij}\eta_{ik}\eta_{jl}\mu^{k}\mu^{l},\qquad i,j,\ldots=1,2,3,4,

(4.10)

in which
m^{ij}
is the inverse of the
SL(4)
matrix
m_{ij}=(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{T})_{ij}
with
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
being
SL(4)/SO(4)
coset representative. The coordinates
\mu^{i}
satisfy
\mu^{i}\mu^{j}\eta_{ij}=1
with
\eta_{ij}
being the
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)
invariant tensor.

By another dimensional reduction on
S^{1}
, we can uplift the six-dimensional metric of the domain wall solutions to type IIA theory. This results in

\hat{g}_{00}=e^{-3\phi_{0}}\Delta^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{2U+2\varphi}

(4.11)

with
\varphi
being the dilaton corresponding to the
SO(1,1)
factor in
GL(5)\sim SL(5)\times SO(1,1)
as in the previous section.

4.1
D4-branes wrapped on a Riemann surface with
SO(2)\times SO(2)
twist

We now look for supersymmetric solutions describing D4-branes wrapped on a Riemann surface
\Sigma^{2}
with
SO(2)\times SO(2)
twists. The ansatz for the six-dimensional metric is still given by (
3.9
). The
SO(2)\times SO(2)
corresponds to
X_{12}
and
X_{34}
gauge generators under which there are five singlet scalars described by the coset representative

V=e^{\varphi\boldsymbol{d}+\phi_{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}+\phi\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}+\varsigma_{1}\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}+\varsigma_{2}\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{3}}\,.

(4.12)

The first scalar
\varphi(r)
is the dilaton corresponding to the
SO(1,1)
generator
\boldsymbol{d}
while the second one
\phi_{0}
is the dilaton corresponding to the
SO(1,1)
factor in
SL(4)\times SO(1,1)
. The explicit form of this non-compact generator is given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{1\dot{1}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{2\dot{2}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{3}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}}-4\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{5\dot{5}}\,.

(4.13)

The remaining three scalars
\phi(r)
,
\varsigma_{1}(r)
, and
\varsigma_{2}(r)
correspond to the following
SO(5,5)
non-compact generators

\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{1\dot{1}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{2\dot{2}}-\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{3}}-\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}},\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}=\boldsymbol{s}_{12},\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{3}=\boldsymbol{s}_{34}\,.

(4.14)

There are two gauge groups containing
SO(2)\times SO(2)
as a subgroup. These can be characterized by the embedding tensor of the form

w^{ij}=\text{diag}(1,1,\kappa,\kappa)

(4.15)

where
\kappa=1,-1
respectively correspond to
SO(4)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
and
SO(2,2)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
gauge groups.

With the coset representative (
4.12
), the scalar potential is given by

\mathbf{V}=-g^{2}\kappa e^{2\varphi-8\phi_{0}}.

(4.16)

We will perform a topological twist by turning on the following gauge fields

{A_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{12}=-e^{-V}\frac{p_{1}}{2\sqrt{2}k}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\theta)}{f_{k}(\theta)}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad{A_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{34}=-e^{-V}\frac{p_{2}}{2\sqrt{2}k}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\theta)}{f_{k}(\theta)}\,.

(4.17)

The twist can be achieved by imposing the projection conditions given in (
3.25
) and (
3.26
) as well as

\hat{\gamma}_{r}\epsilon_{\pm}=\gamma^{5}\epsilon_{\mp}

(4.18)

on the Killing spinors (
3.40
) together with the twist condition (
3.24
).

As in the case of gaugings in
\mathbf{15}^{-1}
representation, the solutions preserve four supercharges corresponding to
N=2
supersymmetry in three dimensions. In addition, consistency of the BPS equations requires either all the shift scalars
\varsigma_{1}
and
\varsigma_{2}
vanish or the condition
p_{2}=p_{1}/\kappa
must be imposed. We again consider these two possibilities separately.

4.2
D4-branes wrapped on a Riemann surface with
SO(2)

We now move to another type of topological twists on
\Sigma^{2}
achieved by turning on an
SO(2)
gauge field. There are many gauge groups admitting an
SO(2)
subgroup. They are collectively characterized by the following component of the embedding tensor

w^{ij}=\text{diag}(1,1,\kappa,\lambda)

(4.29)

with
\kappa=0,\pm 1
and
\lambda=0,\pm 1
.

Apart from the two dilatons, there are additional nine
SO(2)
singlet scalars. Three of them are in the
SL(4)/SO(4)
coset corresponding to non-compact generators

\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{1\dot{1}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{2\dot{2}}-\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{3}}-\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}},\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{4}},\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{3}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{3}}-\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}}\,.

(4.30)

The remaining ones consist of two nilpotent scalars associated with

\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{4}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{5}},\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{5}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{5}}

(4.31)

and four shift scalars corresponding to

\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{6}=\boldsymbol{s}_{12},\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{7}=\boldsymbol{s}_{35},\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{8}=\boldsymbol{s}_{45},\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{9}=\boldsymbol{s}_{34}\,.

(4.32)

The analysis turns out to be highly complicated, so we will perform a subtruncation by setting the shift scalar corresponding to
\boldsymbol{s}_{12}
and the two nilpotent scalars corresponding to

5
Conclusions and discussions

We have found a large number of supersymmetric solutions interpolating between locally flat domain walls and
Mkw_{3}\times\Sigma^{2}
/
Mkw_{2}\times\Sigma^{3}
-sliced domain walls from maximal gauged supergravity in six dimensions with
CSO(p,q,5-p-q)
and
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
gauged groups. Both of these gauge groups can be embedded in string/M-theory although the complete truncation ansatze have not been worked out to date. However, with some partial results from exceptional field theory (EFT), it is possible to study the behavior of the metric components
\hat{g}_{00}
near the IR singularities. We have found that upon uplifted to type IIA theory, many solutions found in this paper admit physically acceptable singularities. Therefore, these solutions describe holographic RG flows across dimensions from five-dimensional field theories to non-conformal field theories in three/two dimensions and are expected to arise from D4-branes wrapped on
\Sigma^{2}
and
\Sigma^{3}
.

It is interesting to construct the complete truncation ansatze of type IIA theory on both
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{5-p-q}
and
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{4-p-q}\times S^{1}
. This would allow to uplift the solutions found here to explicit solutions of type IIA theory in which the interpretation in terms of various brane configurations involving D4-branes wrapped on
\Sigma^{2}
and
\Sigma^{3}
is possible. Identifying the dual field theories in both five and three/two dimensions participating in these RG flows across dimensions could also be of particular interest. With some of the solutions given analytically, we hope the present results could provide a very useful tool to further study along these and related directions. Extending the present study to the case of D4-branes wrapped on spindles and topological disks as in the recent results
[
42
,
43
]
is also worth considering.

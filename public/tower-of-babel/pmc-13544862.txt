Relation between Time from Onset to Randomization and Benefit Magnitude in Recent Clinical Trials of Thrombectomy for Patients with Large Ischemic Cores
Authors: Byron J Gajewski, Katherine R Gajewski, Pooja Khatri, Jeffrey L Saver, Karen C Johnston, Jonathan Beall, Jordan J Elm
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Six randomized trials have demonstrated the benefit of endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) for patients with large ischemic core stroke, but the extent to which treatment effect diminishes with increasing onset-to-treatment time remains unclear. We evaluated whether trial-level median time from symptom onset to randomization modifies the absolute EVT effect compared with medical management (MM). Using study-level data from six trials (n=1,881), we modeled differences in 90-day utility-weighted modified Rankin Scale (uw-mRS) outcomes between EVT and MM as a function of median onset-to-randomization time via Bayesian regression, selecting models based on deviance information criteria. Increasing time was strongly associated with declining EVT benefit (posterior probability =0.9925). The absolute treatment effect fell below 0.10 at 10 hours (95% CrI, 7–15) and below 0.03 at 18 hours (95% CrI, 12–33). These findings suggest that treatment delays attenuate EVT benefit and may inform hypothesis generation and future trial design in acute stroke.

Introduction

Six recent clinical trials demonstrated the benefit of endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) relative to medical management with large core ischemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion.[12345678] Prior intravenous thrombolysis and EVT trials have robustly shown earlier treatment leads to better clinical outcomes.[9] To guide future clinical trials and future treatments, we wanted to know specifically if the absolute EVT effect was diminished as median time of onset to randomization was increased in patients presenting with large ischemic core.

Methods

While study-level data limit the strength of the conclusions compared with an individual participant data analysis, aggregate results for each of six randomized clinical trials testing endovascular stroke thrombectomy for patients with large ischemic cores were reviewed.[128] Patients in each trial were randomized to EVT or medical management (MM) and assessed at 90-day follow-up for global functional outcome assessed by the modified Rankin Score (mRS). For participants randomized to EVT, treatment included stent retriever, aspiration, or some combination. For those randomized to MM, participants were treated using local medical protocol including thrombolysis.

We used median time from onset (last known well) to randomization by study and treatment arm [Table 1]. We assessed the average study difference in disability-related quality of life at 90 days as measured with the utility-weighted modified Rankin Scale (UW-mRS, range 0-1.0, higher scores indicate higher quality of life) in the EVT relative to MM groups. The relation of treatment benefit to median time from onset to randomization was assessed using Bayesian regression. We explored multiple models with various complexities, including a measurement error model. We selected the model with the lowest deviance information criteria (DIC), the best model, for final inference. All analyses were conducted using OpenBUGS software version 3.2.3 rev 1012. We calculated the posterior probability of a slope <0 (pp) as well as the posterior distribution of time in which EVT effect is less than a system centered minimally important clincal difference (MCID) effect size (0.1) and patient centered MCID effect size (0.03) in UW-mRS.[10]

Results

Among the six studies with 1,881 participants, mean [SD] age was 66.9 [13.2] years, n [%] men was 1039 [55.1%], and mean [SD] National institutes of health stroke scale (NIHSS) was 19.1 [2.3]. The median Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score (ASPECTS) value, a validated estimet of severity of injury, varied from 2-4 and for the reported four out of six studies the median Infarct volume varied from 61-137 ml. The median times from onset to randomization ranged widely, from 2.1-11.6 hours. The number who received thrombolysis nor the criteria used is not known from the references. As the median time from onset to randomization increased the absolute benefit of the difference of EVT from MM decreased pp = 0.9925 [Figure 1 shows the fitted relationship]. The time in hours in which absolute EVT effect drops below 0.10 is 9.8 hours; (95% CI, 7.0-14.9 hours); and below 0.03 is 18.2 hours; (95% CI, 12.2-33.2).

Discussion

In this study, the model indicated that at 10 hours, the EVT effect is about 0.09 and at 2 hours is about 0.17, therefore a 8-hour increase in median time from onset to randomization was associated with a relative 47% decrease in in the magnitude of health utility benefit of EVT versus MM. The magnitude of benefit dropped below the system-change MCID at 9.8 hours and below the patient-centered MCID at 18.2 hours. The timing of the patient-centered MCID should be interpreted with caution since this is extrapolating outside the data window. These findings may inform clinical research and accelerate treatment discoveries, including predicting treatment effects in future clinical trials.

This study was limited to study-level data in patients with large ischemic cores and high NIHSS. It is emphasized that a stronger conclusion would be made if the study was done with individual patient data analysis. In addition, the small number of trials limited adjustment analysis for age, patient transfer time, time to reperfusion, and baseline NIHSS. Addionally, the variation in patient characteristics, imaging criteria, and medical management may confound the observed time-effect relationship, so this limited any other variables to be used other than onset to randomization. Further, this study focused on onset to randomization rather than onset to reperfusion, which is clinically more relevant. However, onset to randomization is is precisely captured as randomization time is always captured. Also, time of onset to randomization is heavily influenced by time of onset to arrival. We likely couldn’t use reperfusion time because it is not always precisely captured. Because treatment delays after randomization may vary across trials, this choice could introduce bias into the results.

Conclusions

The absolute effect of EVT relative to MM decreases as time from onset to randomization increases.

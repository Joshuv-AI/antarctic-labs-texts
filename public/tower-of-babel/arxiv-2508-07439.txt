Electroconvection in a Magnetic Field Thanks: MSC Classification: 35Q35, 35R11, 78A25.
Elie Abdo, Peter Constantin, Mihaela Ignatova, Quyuan Lin

Abstract
. Electroconvection in a porous medium under a strong transversal magnetic field is described by an active scalar equation for the charge density. The equation has global weak solutions with L ∞ L^{\infty} data. We show that for strong enough magnetic fields, L ∞ L^{\infty} -small solutions are smooth globally in time and they obey surface quasigeostrophic equations in the limit of infinite magnetic field strength.



1.
Introduction

Electroconvection, the flow of charges in fluids, is characterized by the fact that the charge density and the fluid’s velocity and pressure are directly coupled. The charges in the solvent exert forces on the fluid. The fluid responds to these forces while convecting the charges. This results in a nonlinearly coupled system of
equations for the charge density and the fluid’s velocity and pressure. The subject belongs to a large class of electrohydrodynamic problems of broad scientific interest.
Experimental and theoretical studies
[
34
,
21
,
19
,
20
]
on smectic films revealed complex dynamical behavior in rotating, two-dimensional conductive fluids subjected to three-dimensional electrostatic forces. In experimental studies, applied transversal magnetic fields significantly impact the dynamics of the electroconvection
[
24
,
4
,
29
,
27
,
28
]
. In particular, when a magnetic field is applied, the critical voltage required for instability increases, leading to enhanced system stability
[
4
]
.
Magnetic fields applied to ordered electrically sensitive fluid, such as liquid crystals in porous media, have been documented to produce modifications in phase transitions, changes in orientational order, alterations in the elastic properties and in the behavior of the director field
[
18
,
38
,
25
]
.

In this paper we are concerned with mathematical properties of electroconvection in a magnetic field.
We show that in the limit of strong transversal magnetic field, the solutions of equations of electroconvection in porous media converge to solutions of the surface quasigeostrophic equation (SQG).

The charge density
q(x,t)
of electroconvection in porous media is an active scalar. Active scalars
[
8
,
35
]
are transported by incompressible velocities they create by means of a time-independent equation. This widely studied class of equations includes the two-dimensional incompressible vorticity equation, the surface quasigeostrophic equation SQG and generalized g-SQG equations interpolating between them.
The SQG active scalar arose in geophysics
[
26
]
as a model of the large scale mid-latitude surface temperature evolution in quasigeostrophic flow. Electroconvection of charges in porous media under strong magnetic fields and atmosphere-ocean thermal dynamics are such disparate physical systems, and yet, mathematically, they turn out to be related.
The inviscid SQG equation is studied in the context of singularity formation in fluids
[
8
,
15
,
11
]
. Global regularity from arbitrary smooth data is not known. The equation is ill posed in spaces of low regularity
[
17
,
16
]
. Global weak solutions were obtained in
[
36
]
(see also
[
12
]
for bounded domains). Well-posedness and loss of regularity results have been obtained for g-SQG in the case the initial data is a patch (that is a step function) with smooth boundary in a half-space
[
32
,
31
,
39
,
23
]
and fronts
[
5
,
22
]
. Solutions that exist for all time have been constructed. They include steady radial solutions, time-periodic rotating solutions
[
7
]
and quasiperiodic solutions (see the monograph
[
37
]
and references therein). The critical dissipative SQG equation has global smooth solutions from arbitrary data
[
30
,
6
]
. Critical dissipative SQG is
L^{\infty}
critical. Small
L^{\infty}
data lead to stability and global existence
[
9
]
; for large data, an initial data dependent modulus of continuity
[
30
]
or a
C^{\alpha}
norm
[
13
]
are non-increasing in time. These quantities depend on the size of the initial data in
L^{\infty}
and are highly sensitive to the structure of the equation: finding them relies on the linear nonlocal equation of state of the velocity in terms of the scalar. This is a source of difficulty in analyzing nonlinear nonlocal top-order perturbations of the critical SQG, such as we encounter in the present work. Once these quantities are finite, higher regularity follows
[
14
]
.

We describe now the electroconvection setting we are discussing in this paper. We consider an incompressible fluid occupying a very thin region in space, represented as the two dimensional plane
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{2}=\{(x,y,z)\left|\right.\,z=0\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. We denote by
q
a charge density in
\Omega
, denote by
\tilde{E}=(\tilde{E}_{1},\tilde{E}_{2},\tilde{E}_{3})
and
\tilde{B}=(\tilde{B}_{1},\tilde{B}_{2},\tilde{B}_{3})
three-dimensional electric and magnetic fields, and by
\rho
the total charge density
\rho=2q\delta_{\Omega}
as a distribution in the whole space. We also denote by
E=(E_{1},E_{2})
the restriction of
(\tilde{E}_{1},\tilde{E}_{2})
to
\Omega
. The three-dimensional electric field
\tilde{E}
obeys Gauss’ law

(1.1)

\nabla_{3}\cdot\tilde{E}=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}\rho=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}2q\delta_{\Omega},

where
\epsilon_{0}
is the vacuum permittivity and we denote by
\nabla_{3}
the three dimensional gradient. The electric field is given by an electrostatic potential
\Phi
,

(1.2)

\tilde{E}=-\nabla_{3}\Phi.

Gauss’ law gives the Poisson equation obeyed by
\Phi
,

(1.3)

-\Delta_{3}\Phi=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}2q\delta_{\Omega}.

We discuss the case when
q
and
\Phi
are
2\pi
periodic functions of
x
and
y
and
\Phi
vanishes as
|z|\to\infty
. The potential
\Phi
is then given by

(1.4)

\Phi(x,y,z)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}e^{-z\Lambda}\Lambda^{-1}q,\hskip 28.45274ptz>0,\\
\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}e^{z\Lambda}\Lambda^{-1}q,\hskip 28.45274ptz<0,\end{cases}

where
\Lambda=\sqrt{-\Delta}
is the square root of the two dimensional Laplacian with
2\pi
periodic boundary conditions. Therefore,
E:=(\tilde{E}_{1},\tilde{E}_{2})|_{z=0}
can be expressed as

(1.5)

E=-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}\nabla\Lambda^{-1}q

where
\nabla
is the two dimensional gradient. We denote by
R
the Riesz transforms

(1.6)

R=\nabla\Lambda^{-1}

and thus the electric field restricted to the domain occupied by fluid is

(1.7)

E=-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}Rq.

The magnetic field generated by the electric field is negligible. We consider an imposed external magnetic field
\tilde{B}=(0,0,B)
where
B\geq 0
is a constant independent of both time and space. Notice that this external magnetic field
\tilde{B}
satisfies the divergence-free condition, has zero curl, and is time-independent.

The total current density is given by the sum of the advective current
(u_{1},u_{2},0)q
and the Ohmic conduction current
\sigma\tilde{E}^{\prime}
, where
\sigma>0
is the conductivity of the medium and
\tilde{E}^{\prime}
is the electric field experienced by the fluid element in its rest frame
[
33
]
. When the fluid is moving with
respect to the external magnetic field at the velocity
u
, using the Lorentz transformation we have

\tilde{E}^{\prime}=\tilde{E}+(u_{1},u_{2},0)\times\tilde{B}.

Therefore,

(1.8)

\tilde{j}=(u_{1},u_{2},0)q+\sigma(\tilde{E}+(u_{1},u_{2},0)\times\tilde{B}).

The restriction of
\tilde{j}
to the surface is denoted by
j=(\tilde{j}_{1},\tilde{j}_{2})|_{z=0}
and is therefore

(1.9)

j=uq+\sigma(E-Bu^{\perp}),

where
u^{\perp}=(-u_{2},u_{1})
. The surface charge density
q
obeys the conservation

(1.10)

\partial_{t}q+\nabla\cdot\,j=0

which is, in view of (
1.9
),

(1.11)

\partial_{t}q+u\cdot\nabla q+\sigma(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}\Lambda q-B\nabla\cdot u^{\perp})=0.

The fluid’s divergence-free velocity
u
obeys an equation forced by the electrostatic force
qE
plus the Lorentz force

(\tilde{j}\times\tilde{B})
in the plane, which is computed as

(1.12)

(\tilde{j}\times\tilde{B})_{\left|\right.{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_{0}}BR^{\perp}q-Bqu^{\perp}-\sigma B^{2}u.

To ease the notation we take
\sigma=\epsilon_{0}=1
. The total force exerted on the fluid is given by

(1.13)

F=BR^{\perp}q-qRq-Bqu^{\perp}-B^{2}u.

There are two important velocity contributions in
F
due to the imposed external magnetic field.
The first one,
-Bqu^{\perp}
, yields a rotation effect similar to a Coriolis force with frequency
Bq
. The
second one
-B^{2}u
, is a strong damping or friction effect.
Electroconvection equations couple (
1.11
) to a momentum equation driven by
F
. For instance, the Navier-Stokes equations forced by
F
are

(1.14)

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\nu\Delta u+B^{2}u+Bqu^{\perp}+\nabla p=BR^{\perp}q-qRq,\quad\nabla\cdot u=0,

where
\nu>0
is the kinematic viscosity. In the absence of an applied transversal magnetic field, the Navier-Stokes electroconvection system, i.e the system (
1.11
), (
1.14
) with
B=0
, was shown to have global solutions in
[
10
]
. The long time dynamics were described in
[
1
]
in
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
and in
[
3
]
in bounded domains, both with, and without time independent body forces in the fluid.

If we replace viscous friction by Darcy’s law and neglect inertial time dependence, we arrive at the equations

(1.15)

\mu\,u+\nabla p=F,\quad\nabla\cdot u=0,

where
\mu>0
is porosity. This is the fluid equation we consider in this paper.
Taking
\mu=1
and retaining
B
as the only (large) parameter, (
1.15
) becomes the time independent law

(1.16)

(1+B^{2})u+Bqu^{\perp}+\nabla p=BR^{\perp}q-qRq,\quad\nabla\cdot u=0.

Together with the charge density equation (
1.11
), this gives rise to the following system,

(1.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q+u\cdot\nabla q+\Lambda q-B\nabla\cdot u^{\perp}=0,

(1.18)

\displaystyle(1+B^{2})u+Bqu^{\perp}+\nabla p=BR^{\perp}q-qRq,

(1.19)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u=0.

The unknowns are
q
,
u
,
p
. Using

-B\nabla\cdot u^{\perp}=B\omega
, taking the two dimensional curl of (
1.18
), and replacing in (
1.17
), the latter becomes

(1.20)

\partial_{t}q+\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}(R^{\perp}q\cdot\nabla q)+\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}u\cdot\nabla q+\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}\Lambda q=0.

In the absence of an external magnetic field, i.e. when
B=0
, the system (
1.17
)-(
1.19
) describes electroconvection in a porous medium. The global existence for large data of smooth solutions of this system is a challenging open problem. Global regularity for small initial data in Besov spaces smaller than
L^{\infty}
was obtained in
[
2
]
.

We consider three time scales,
t=t_{lab}
the laboratory time scale,

t_{1}
, the magnetic gyration time scale or first magnetic time scale, and
t_{2}
, the magnetic friction time scale or second magnetic time scale. They are related by

(1.21)

t_{1}=\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}t\quad\text{and}\quad t_{2}=\frac{1}{B}t_{1}=\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}t.

In laboratory time scale, the equation for the unknown
q(x,t)
is (
1.20
), that is

(1.22)

\partial_{t}q+\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}(R^{\perp}q\cdot\nabla q)+\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}\left(u[q]\cdot\nabla+\Lambda\right)q=0,

with
u[q]=u
the unique solution of

(1.23)

(1+B^{2})\left(u+\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}\mathbb{P}(qu^{\perp})\right)=BR^{\perp}q-\mathbb{P}(qRq)

where
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{I}+\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot=\mathbb{I}+R\otimes R
is the Leray projector on divergence free vector fields.
The time independent equation of state
u[q]
and its properties are described in detail below in Section
2
.

In the first (gyration) magnetic time scale, the equation for
q_{1}(x,t_{1})=q(x,\frac{1+B^{2}}{B}t_{1})
where
q
solves (
1.22
) becomes

(1.24)

\partial_{t_{1}}q_{1}+R^{\perp}q_{1}\cdot\nabla q_{1}+\frac{1}{B}\left(u[q_{1}]\cdot\nabla+\Lambda\right)q_{1}=0.

The initial data are the same,
q(x,0)=q_{1}(x,0)
, and there is no rescaling of space variable
x
.

In the second magnetic time scale, the equation for the boosted field

(1.25)

Q(x,t_{2})=Bq_{1}(x,Bt_{2})=Bq(x,(1+B^{2})t_{2})

becomes

(1.26)

\partial_{t_{2}}Q+R^{\perp}Q\cdot\nabla Q+\left(u\left[\frac{Q}{B}\right]\cdot\nabla+\Lambda\right)Q=0.

The initial data is
Q(x,0)=Bq(x,0)
and there is no rescaling of space variable. Note that
Q=Bq
is the frequency of rotation produced by the force
F
.

For fixed
B
, the three
equations (
1.22
), (
1.24
), (
1.26
) are versions of the same equation, and they are entirely equivalent. We refer to these equations as Darcy law electroconvection in a magnetic field, DECM equations.

The main results of this work are as follows. We prove first that weak solutions of (
1.20
) with initial data in
L^{\infty}
exist globally (Theorem
3.1
).
As
B\to\infty
, in laboratory time scale, the equation converges to
\partial_{t}q=0
. It is only in the first magnetic time scale that nontrivial dynamics arise. The solutions
q_{1}
of (
1.24
) converge to solutions of inviscid SQG. We prove (Theorem
4.1
)

Theorem 1.1
.

Any family of weak solutions
q^{B}
of (
1.20
)
has a subsequence such that
q^{B}(x,\frac{1+B^{2}}{B}t)
converges weakly as
B\to\infty
to a weak solution
q(x,t)
of the inviscid SQG

\partial_{t}q+(R^{\perp}q)\cdot\nabla q=0,

in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})
.

The dissipative nature of the DECM equation emerges in longer time scales, and the boosted field
Q
converges in the second magnetic time scale to solutions of critical dissipative SQG. We prove global regularity of the boosted field
Q
(Theorem
5.1
) for small
L^{\infty}
initial data and large enough
B
.
In laboratory time scale, the regularity result is (Remark
5.1
)

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist
c>0
and
C>0
such that, if
q_{0}
obeys
B\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c
and
\|q_{0}\|_{H^{3}}\leq C(1+B^{2})
, then the solution of (
1.20
) with initial data
q_{0}
exists globally, is unique, and satisfies

\|q(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{3}}\leq\|q_{0}\|_{H^{3}}e^{-\frac{t}{4(1+B^{2})}}

for all
t\geq 0
.

The regularity in
H^{3}
implies by parabolic estimates
C^{\infty}
regularity, a fact that follows by well known methods and is not pursued in this paper.

We prove convergence to dissipative critical SQG without assuming the condition of small
initial
L^{\infty}
norm of
q
, but we do need to assume enough regularity to allow for absolute continuity of the
L^{2}
norm of the DECM solution. Of course, this regularity is guaranteed for short time or for arbitrary time if the initial
L^{\infty}
norm is small. The result we prove is (Theorem
5.2
and Remark
5.2
)

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
q
be a strong solution of (
1.20
) on
[0,(1+B^{2})T]
with initial data
q_{0}=\frac{1}{B}Q_{0}
, with fixed
Q_{0}\in H^{3}
. There exists a constant
C
depending only on
\|Q_{0}\|_{H^{3}}
and

such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Bq(\cdot,(1+B^{2})t)-\overline{Q}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{1+B^{2}}

where
\overline{Q}
is the global smooth solution of critical SQG

\partial_{t}Q+(R^{\perp}Q)\cdot\nabla Q+\Lambda Q=0

with initial data
Q_{0}
.

The constant
C
above is in fact finite as soon as
Q_{0}\in C^{\alpha}
, for any
\alpha>0
. The result
holds for fixed
Q_{0}
while

varies, and not for fixed
q_{0}
. If we fix
q_{0}
and consider the family of initial data
Bq_{0}
for the critical SQG, then the constant
C
depends badly on
B
.

2.
Equation of State for the Velocity in Terms of the Charge Density

Taking the curl of (
1.23
) we obtain the time-independent equation for the vorticity,

(2.1)

\omega=\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}\left(R^{\perp}q\cdot\nabla q\right)-\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}\left(u\cdot\nabla q+\Lambda q\right),

where

(2.2)

\omega=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u=\Delta\psi,\quad\nabla\cdot u=0.

Writing
\omega=-\Lambda v
where
v=\Lambda\psi
with
\psi
the stream function,
we have from (
2.1
) after applying
-\Lambda^{-1}
to both sides

(2.3)

v-\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}R\cdot(qR^{\perp}v)=\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}\left(q-\frac{1}{B}R\cdot(qR^{\perp}q)\right),

and

(2.4)

u=R^{\perp}v.

We denote by
L_{q}
the operator

(2.5)

L_{q}(f)=R\cdot(qR^{\perp}f)

Both
q
and
f
are scalar. Because
R\cdot R^{\perp}=0
, this is a commutator,

(2.6)

L_{q}(f)=[R,q]\cdot R^{\perp}f.

Now (
2.3
) can be written as

(2.7)

v-\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}L_{q}v=\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}\left(q-\frac{1}{B}L_{q}q\right).

The operator
\mathbb{I}-\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}L_{q}
is bounded in
L^{2}
(if
q\in L^{\infty}
) and unconditionally invertible. Because
q
is real, the operator
L_{q}
is bounded anti-selfadjoint in
L^{2}
so its spectrum lies in a segment of
i\mathbb{R}
. The inverse of
\mathbb{I}-\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}L_{q}
can be defined by familiar functional calculus, and has norm less than 1, no matter how large is the norm of
\|q\|_{L^{\infty}}
. We recall that under DECM equations this norm is not growing in time.

Introducing

(2.8)

w=v-\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}q

the equation (
2.7
) becomes

(2.9)

w-\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}L_{q}w=-\frac{1}{(1+B^{2})^{2}}L_{q}q,

that is,

(2.10)

w[q]=-\frac{1}{(1+B^{2})^{2}}\left(\mathbb{I}-\frac{BL_{q}}{1+B^{2}}\right)^{-1}(L_{q}q)

and (
2.4
) becomes

(2.11)

u[q]=R^{\perp}\left(w[q]+\frac{Bq}{1+B^{2}}\right).

The left hand side of (
1.23
) defines an operator
T_{q}
in
H
, the space of periodic divergence-free vector fields in
L^{2}
:

(2.12)

T_{q}(u)=(1+B^{2})\left(u+\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}\mathbb{P}(qu^{\perp})\right).

Using (
2.4
) we express in terms of
v
,

(2.13)

T_{q}(u)=(1+B^{2})\left(R^{\perp}v-\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}\mathbb{P}(qRv)\right).

Now, in view of

(2.14)

(\mathbb{P}f)_{i}=\left(\delta_{ij}+R_{i}R_{j}\right)f_{j}

(2.15)

L_{q}(v)=-R^{\perp}\cdot(qRv),

and

(2.16)

R_{i}R_{i}=-\mathbb{I},

by applying from the left
R^{\perp}_{i}=\epsilon_{ji}R_{j}
to (
2.13
) (where
\epsilon_{ji}=0
if
j=i
, and it is the signature of the permutation
(1,2)\mapsto(j,i)
if
j\neq i
), we obtain

(2.17)

R^{\perp}\cdot T_{q}(u)=-(1+B^{2})\left(\mathbb{I}-\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}L_{q}\right)(v).

This shows that
u
determined by inverting
T_{q}
is the same as
u[q]
.
Indeed, using (
2.7
) to solve for
v
, we deduce

(2.18)

R^{\perp}\cdot T_{q}(u)=-(1+B^{2})\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}\left(q-\frac{1}{B}L_{q}q\right)=-Bq+L_{q}q.

Now, because
T_{q}(u)
is divergence-free, we have

(2.19)

T_{q}(u)=-R^{\perp}(R^{\perp}\cdot T_{q}(u)),

and using (
2.18
) we deduce

(2.20)

T_{q}(u)=BR^{\perp}q-R^{\perp}(L_{q}q).

Because
\mathbb{P}(qRq)
is divergence free we have that

(2.21)

\mathbb{P}(qRq)=-R^{\perp}\left(R^{\perp}\cdot\mathbb{P}(qRq)\right),

and using (
2.15
) we see that

(2.22)

\mathbb{P}(qRq)=R^{\perp}(L_{q}q)

and thus, from (
2.20
)
that

(2.23)

T_{q}(u)=BR^{\perp}q-\mathbb{P}(qRq).

This shows that
u=R^{\perp}v
solves (
2.23
). The solution is unique, and this establishes the converse implication as well. We have thus,

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
q\in L^{\infty}
. The unique solution in
H
of (
1.23
),

(2.24)

u[q]=T_{q}^{-1}\left(BR^{\perp}q-\mathbb{P}(qRq)\right)

is given by (
2.11
) with
w[q]
solving (
2.9
) given by (
2.10
).

Note that
L_{q}(f)
is bilinear, in particular
BL_{q}=L_{Bq}
. In view of (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) we have

(2.25)

u[q]=\frac{B}{1+B^{2}}R^{\perp}\left(q-\frac{1}{B^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{BL_{q}}{1+B^{2}}\right)^{n+1}q\right).

For
Q=Bq
we have

(2.26)

U[Q]=R^{\perp}\left(\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}Q+W[Q]\right)

with

(2.27)

W[Q]=-\frac{1}{B^{2}(1+B^{2})}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{L_{Q}}{(1+B^{2})}\right)^{n+1}Q.

The scalar
w
and velocity
u

(2.28)

W[Q]=w\left[\frac{Q}{B}\right],

(2.29)

U[Q]=u\left[\frac{Q}{B}\right].

Let us consider a Banach algebra
\mathcal{B}
, smaller than
L^{\infty}
and where the Riesz transforms are bounded. Two canonical examples are

C^{\alpha}
and
H^{s}
,
s>1
. Then, if
Q\in\mathcal{B}
we have that
L_{Q}
is bounded in
\mathcal{B}
with

(2.30)

\|L_{Q}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B})}\leq\gamma\|Q\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

The expressions (
2.25
), (
2.27
) are explicit analytic expansions in
\mathcal{B}
, and we have

(2.31)

\|W[Q]\|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\frac{\gamma}{B^{2}(1+B^{2})^{2}}\frac{\|Q\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}}}{\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{1+B^{2}}\|Q\|_{\mathcal{B}}\right)}.

If

(2.32)

\|Q\|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\frac{1+B^{2}}{2\gamma}

then

(2.33)

\|W(Q)\|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\frac{2\gamma}{B^{2}(1+B^{2})^{2}}\|Q\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}}.

These considerations can be greatly improved by removing the quantitative condition (
2.32
) in

We prove now (1)–(4).

(4)

Proposition 3.2

) as

(4.6)

q^{B_{n}}(t)-q_{0}=-\int_{0}^{t}R^{\perp}q^{B_{n}}\cdot\nabla q^{B_{n}}ds-\frac{1}{B_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}u^{B_{n}}\cdot\nabla q^{B_{n}}dx-\frac{1}{B_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda q^{B_{n}}ds.

As
\left\{\Lambda^{-1}q^{B_{n}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges strongly in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}})
, it has a subsequence, still denoted
\left\{\Lambda^{-1}q^{B_{n}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, which converges strongly in
L^{2}
to
\Lambda^{-1}q
for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, and hence,

(4.7)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\phi(x)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi dx\rightarrow 0

for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
and for all
\phi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}
. In view of the uniform bound
\|q^{B_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
that holds for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{n}}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda q^{B_{n}}\phi dxds\right|\leq\frac{1}{B_{n}}\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda\phi\|_{L^{2}}T\to 0,

and thus,

(4.9)

\frac{1}{B_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\Lambda q^{B_{n}}\phi dxds\rightarrow 0

for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\phi\in H^{1}
. By making use of the uniform velocity bound
\|u^{B_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})
, we estimate

(4.10)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}u^{B_{n}}\cdot\nabla q^{B_{n}}\phi dxds\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{B_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}\|q^{B_{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}(\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+1)\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}T,

and hence,

(4.11)

\frac{1}{B_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}u^{B_{n}}\cdot\nabla q^{B_{n}}\phi dxds\rightarrow 0

for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\phi\in H^{1}
. Finally, we consider the difference

(4.12)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}R^{\perp}q^{B_{n}}\cdot\nabla q^{B_{n}}\phi\,dxds-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}R^{\perp}q\cdot\nabla q\phi\,dxds,

and we decompose it into the sum of two terms
\mathcal{I}_{n}
and
\mathcal{O}_{n}
where

(4.13)

\mathcal{I}_{n}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\left(R^{\perp}q\cdot\nabla(q^{B_{n}}-q)+R^{\perp}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\cdot\nabla q\right)\phi dxds

and

(4.14)

\mathcal{O}_{n}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}R^{\perp}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\cdot\nabla(q^{B_{n}}-q)\phi dxds.

Since
q\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty})
, it follows that both quantities
R^{\perp}q\cdot\nabla\phi
and
R^{\perp}\cdot(q\nabla\phi)
lie in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})
for any
\phi\in H^{2}
, and thus
\mathcal{I}_{n}\rightarrow 0
by the weak convergence of
q^{B_{n}}
to
q
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})
. As for the term
\mathcal{O}_{n}
, we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{n}=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}R^{\perp}(q^{B_{n}}-q)(q^{B_{n}}-q)\cdot\nabla\phi dxds

via integration by parts, and thus

(4.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda^{-1}(q^{B_{n}}-q)(q^{B_{n}}-q)\cdot\nabla\phi dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\Lambda^{-1}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\nabla^{\perp}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\cdot\nabla\phi dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\Lambda^{-1}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\Lambda\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda^{-1}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\cdot\nabla\phi dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}R^{\perp}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\cdot\Lambda\left(\Lambda^{-1}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\nabla\phi\right)dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}R^{\perp}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\cdot\left[\Lambda(\Lambda^{-1}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\nabla\phi)-\nabla\phi\Lambda(\Lambda^{-1}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\right]dxds-\mathcal{O}_{n}.

The latter yields the identity

(4.17)

\mathcal{O}_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}R^{\perp}(q^{B_{n}}-q)[\Lambda,\nabla\phi]\Lambda^{-1}(q^{B_{n}}-q)dxds.

An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the spatial variable gives rise to

(4.18)

|\mathcal{O}_{n}|\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|q^{B_{n}}-q\|_{L^{2}}\|[\Lambda,\nabla\phi]\Lambda^{-1}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\|_{L^{2}}\right)ds.

Using the periodic commutator estimate

(4.19)

\|[\Lambda,f]g\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{4}}\|g\|_{L^{4}}

that holds for any
f\in W^{1,4}
and
g\in L^{4}
, and standard continuous Sobolev embeddings, we bound

(4.20)

\|[\Lambda,\nabla\phi]\Lambda^{-1}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{3}}\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\|_{L^{2}},

and consequently, we obtain

(4.21)

|\mathcal{O}_{n}|\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{3}}\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(q^{B_{n}}-q)\|_{L^{2}}ds\rightarrow 0

due to the strong convergence of
\Lambda^{-1}q^{B_{n}}
to
\Lambda^{-1}q
in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}})
. Therefore,
q
is a weak solution of the inviscid SQG equation.
∎

We consider here the DECM equation for the boosted field

. As mentioned in the introduction, if

is a solution of (

Q(x,t)=Bq(x,(1+B^{2})t)

(5.2)

\partial_{t}Q+R^{\perp}\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}\right)Q+w\right)\cdot\nabla Q+\Lambda Q=0

with

(5.3)

w=W[Q],

which is the unique solution of (
2.34
) discussed in Proposition

. We recall that Proposition
2.2
shows that
w
is small relative to
\|Q\|^{2}
in

when
B
is large. When
B
is of order 1, this term is difficult to handle and global existence for large data of solutions of the equation is not known, just as in the case of electroconvection in porous media. We prove here

Theorem 5.1
.

There exist constants
c>0
,
C>0
such that if
Q_{0}\in H^{3}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
obeys

(5.4)

\|Q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c

and

(5.5)

\|Q_{0}\|_{H^{3}}\leq CB(1+B^{2}),

then the solution of (
5.2
), (
5.3
) with initial data
Q_{0}
is unique, exists for all time and obeys

(5.6)

\|Q(t)\|_{H^{3}}\leq\|Q_{0}\|_{H^{3}}e^{-\frac{t}{4}}

for all
t\geq 0
.

Remark 5.1
.

In laboratory time scale, the result says that if
q_{0}
obeys
B\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c
and
\|q_{0}\|_{H^{3}}\leq C(1+B^{2})
, then

(5.7)

\|q(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{3}}\leq\|q_{0}\|_{H^{3}}e^{-\frac{t}{4(1+B^{2})}}.

Proof.

We start by observing that, without loss of generality we may assume that

(5.8)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}Q(x,t)dx=0,

because this average is time independent. Secondly, we note that the
L^{\infty}
norm of
Q
is nonincreasing in time. We denote by
M

(5.9)

\|Q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}=M

and we have

(5.10)

\|Q(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M

a priori. Next, we use the fact that

(5.11)

\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\sim\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\partial^{\alpha}Q(x,t)|^{2}dx

because
Q
has mean zero. Above
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
is a multi-index, with
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
. We recall the
notation
\alpha!=\alpha_{1}!\alpha_{2}!
and
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
.
We compute the evolution

(5.12)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\partial^{\alpha}Q(x,t)|^{2}dx+D^{2}=R_{1}+R_{2},

where

(5.13)

D^{2}=\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\|\partial^{\alpha}Q\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2},

and

(5.14)

R_{1}=(1+\frac{1}{1+B^{2}})\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,|\beta|>0}\frac{\alpha!}{\beta!\gamma!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\left(\partial^{\beta}(R^{\perp}Q)\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}Q\right)\partial^{\alpha}Qdx,

and

(5.15)

R_{2}=\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,|\beta|>0}\frac{\alpha!}{\beta!\gamma!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\left(\partial^{\beta}(R^{\perp}w)\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}Q\right)\partial^{\alpha}Qdx.

The terms with
\beta=0
vanish because
R^{\perp}Q
and
R^{\perp}w
are divergence-free. Therefore
|\gamma|\leq 2
. We estimate the two contributions
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
differently. The first one, like in critical SQG
[
9
]
, is going to be absorbed in
D^{2}
if
M
is small,

(5.16)

|R_{1}|\leq CMD^{2}.

The second contribution is small compared to
\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{4}
,

(5.17)

|R_{2}|\leq C\frac{1}{B^{2}(1+B^{2})^{2}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{4}.

We note that

(5.18)

D^{2}\geq C{\|Q\|^{2}_{H^{3.5}}}

because
Q
has mean zero.

For
R_{1}
we use
L^{3}
bounds.

(5.19)

\|\partial^{\beta}Q\|_{L^{3}}\leq CM^{a}D^{1-a}

where
a=\frac{19-6|\beta|}{15}
,

(5.20)

\|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}Q\|_{L^{3}}\leq CM^{b}D^{1-b}

where
b=\frac{19-6(|\gamma|+1)}{15}

and

(5.21)

\|\partial^{\alpha}Q\|_{L^{3}}\leq CM^{c}D^{1-c}

where

c={\frac{19-18}{15}}
. These are all the same Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

(5.22)

\|\partial^{s}Q\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{19-6|s|}{15}}\|Q\|_{H^{3.5}}^{\frac{6|s|-4}{15}}

for
1\leq|s|\leq 3
.
Noting that
a+b+c=1
because
|\beta|+|\gamma|=3
, we proved (
5.16
).

For the term
R_{2}
, if
|\beta|=1
then we estimate
L^{\infty}-L^{2}-L^{2}
.

(5.23)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\partial^{\beta}R^{\perp}w\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}Q)\partial^{\alpha}Qdx\right|\leq C\|\nabla R^{\perp}w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{3}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{2}.

If
|\beta|=2
then we estimate
L^{4}-L^{4}-L^{2}
.
We use the inequality

(5.24)

\|\partial^{\beta}R^{\perp}w\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}\|w\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{5}{6}}

(5.25)

\|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}Q\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{5}{6}}.

These are two instances of the inequality

(5.26)

\|\partial^{s}Q\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{5}{6}}

for
|s|=2
, so we have for
|\beta|=2
,

(5.27)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\partial^{\beta}R^{\perp}w\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}Q)\partial^{\alpha}Qdx\right|\leq C\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}\|w\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{5}{6}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{3}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{2}.

Finally, for
|\beta|=3

to obtain

(5.28)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\partial^{\beta}R^{\perp}w\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}Q)\partial^{\alpha}Qdx\right|\leq C\|w\|_{H^{3}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{3}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{2}.

Putting these together we have

(5.29)

|R_{2}|\leq C\|w\|_{H^{3}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{2}.

Now we use the bound (
2.36
) of Proposition
2.2
. Assuming without loss of generality that

we obtain from (
2.36
)

(5.30)

\|w\|_{H^{3}}\leq C\frac{M}{B^{2}(1+B^{2})^{2}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}(1+\|Q\|_{H^{3}})

and using it in (

) we obtain (
5.17
).

Considering now

(5.31)

y(t)=\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\partial^{\alpha}Q(x,t)|^{2}dx

and taking

to absorb the term

in

in view of (

),
we have

\frac{dy}{dt}+y\leq C\frac{1}{B^{2}(1+B^{2})^{2}}y^{2}

with some constant

, where we used a Poincaré inequality

. Thus, if

then, as long as

we have that

\frac{dy}{dt}+\frac{1}{2}y\leq 0,

which implies

But, because

and because of (
5.33
), the condition (

)
is never violated, so (
5.36
) holds for all time. The condition (
5.5
) is the square root of (
5.33
) (with another name for the constant). The bound (
5.6
) is obtained by taking square roots of both sides of (
5.36
).

∎

Theorem 5.2

In laboratory time scale, this result says that strong solutions

Hence, we have

(A.13)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Delta w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\Delta w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{(1+B^{2})^{2}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\|\Delta w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{B^{4}(1+B^{2})^{4}}\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{2},

and so

(A.14)

\|\nabla\Delta w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C(\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}^{5}+\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3}+\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})}{B^{4}(1+B^{2})^{4}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{4}+\frac{C\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}{B^{4}(1+B^{2})^{4}}\|Q\|_{H^{3}}^{2}.

due to (
A.10
).

Appendix B
Proof of Proposition
3.1

Proof.

For each integer
n\geq 1
, we consider the Galerkin approximants

(B.1)

\mathbb{P}_{n}\theta=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}(\theta,\omega_{j})_{L^{2}}\omega_{j}

where
\omega_{j}
are the eigenfunctions of the negative Laplacian operator with periodic boundary conditions. For fixed
\epsilon>0
and
n\geq 1
, we consider the Galerkin approximate system

(B.2)

\partial_{t}q_{n}^{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{n}(u_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon})+\frac{B}{(1+B^{2})}\mathbb{P}_{n}(J_{\epsilon}R^{\perp}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon})+\frac{1}{(1+B^{2})}\Lambda q_{n}^{\epsilon}-\epsilon\Delta q_{n}^{\epsilon}=0,

(B.3)

u_{n}^{\epsilon}=\mathbb{P}_{n}\left(J_{\epsilon}T_{J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}}^{-1}\left[-\mathbb{P}\left(J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}RJ_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\right)+BR^{\perp}J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\right]\right),

with initial data
q_{n}^{\epsilon}(0)=\mathbb{P}_{n}q^{\epsilon}(0)
and periodic boundary conditions. The latter is a finite-dimensional system of autonomous ODEs and has a unique smooth solution on a maximal time interval. Next, we derive
a priori
bounds.

Step 1.
L^{2}
bounds.
We take the
L^{2}
inner product of the equation (
B.2
) obeyed by
q_{n}^{\epsilon}
with
q_{n}^{\epsilon}
. Using the self-adjointness of the Galerkin projectors
\mathbb{P}_{n}
, the identity
\mathbb{P}_{n}q_{n}^{\epsilon}=q_{n}^{\epsilon}
, and the divergence-free condition obeyed by both
u_{n}^{\epsilon}
and
R^{\perp}q_{n}^{\epsilon}
, the nonlinear terms vanish, namely

(B.4)

(\mathbb{P}_{n}(u_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon}),q_{n}^{\epsilon})_{L^{2}}=(\mathbb{P}_{n}(J_{\epsilon}R^{\perp}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon}),q_{n}^{\epsilon})_{L^{2}}=0.

This gives rise to the following energy balance

(B.5)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(1+B^{2})}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

Integrating in time from
0

to
t
and using the uniform boundedness of
\mathbb{P}_{n}
on
L^{2}
, we infer that

(B.6)

\|q_{n}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2}{1+B^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}q_{n}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+2\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds=\|q_{n}^{\epsilon}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|q^{\epsilon}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

for all
t\geq 0
.

Step 2.
H^{m}
bounds for
m\geq 1
.
We multiply the equation (
B.2
) obeyed by
q_{n}^{\epsilon}
by
\Lambda^{2m}q_{n}^{\epsilon}
and we integrate over
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. We obtain the evolution equation

(B.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{m}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(1+B^{2})}\|\Lambda^{m+\frac{1}{2}}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|\Lambda^{m+1}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\Lambda^{m-1}(u_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon})\Lambda^{m+1}q_{n}^{\epsilon}dx-\frac{B}{(1+B^{2})}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\Lambda^{m-1}(J_{\epsilon}R^{\perp}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon})\Lambda^{m+1}q_{n}^{\epsilon}dx.

Applications of the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities give rise to the differential inequality

(B.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{m}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1+B^{2})}\|\Lambda^{m+\frac{1}{2}}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|\Lambda^{m+1}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1+B^{2})^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}(u_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{B^{2}}{(1+B^{2})^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}(J_{\epsilon}R^{\perp}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By making use of periodic fractional product estimates and standard continuous Sobolev embeddings, we have

(B.9)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{m-1}(u_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Lambda^{m-1}u_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+C\|u_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{m+1}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

provided that
m\geq 2
.
We point out that the latter estimate trivially holds when
m=1
by the Hölder and Sobolev inequalities.
By making use of the explicit relation (
B.3
), the boundedness of
\mathbb{P}_{n}
on Sobolev spaces, and the boundedness of the mollifier
J_{\epsilon}
from
L^{2}
to
H^{m+1}
, and the boundedness of
T_{J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}}^{-1}
on
L^{2}
, we estimate

(B.10)

\displaystyle\|u_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{m+1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-2m-2}\left\|T_{J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}}^{-1}\left[-\mathbb{P}\left(J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}RJ_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\right)+BR^{\perp}J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\right]\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-2m-2}(1+B^{2})^{-2}\left\|-\mathbb{P}\left(J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}RJ_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\right)+BR^{\perp}J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-2m-2}(1+B^{2})^{-2}\left(\|J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|RJ_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+B^{2}\|R^{\perp}J_{\epsilon}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

As
J_{\epsilon}
and
R
are bounded operators on
L^{4}
and
L^{2}
, and due to the Ladyzhenskaya interpolation inequality, the latter yields

(B.11)

\displaystyle\|u_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{m+1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-2m-2}(1+B^{2})^{-2}\left(\|q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+B^{2}\|q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-2m-2}(1+B^{2})^{-2}\left(\|q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+B^{2}\|q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Consequently, we obtain the bound

(B.12)

\|\Lambda^{m-1}(u_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\epsilon^{-2m-2}(1+B^{2})^{-2}\left(\|\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+B^{2}\right)\|q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Similarly, we have

(B.13)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{m-1}(J_{\epsilon}R^{\perp}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|J_{\epsilon}R^{\perp}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{m+1}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-2m-2}\|q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Putting all these estimates together, the energy inequality (
B.8
) boils down to

(B.14)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{m}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{(1+B^{2})}\|\Lambda^{m+\frac{1}{2}}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|\Lambda^{m+1}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\epsilon^{2m+2}(1+B^{2})^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{(1+B^{2})^{2}}\left(\|\nabla q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+B^{2}\right)+B^{2}\right]\|q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}q_{n}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Finally, we integrate in time from
0
to
t
, use the uniform bounds (
B.6
) derived in Step 1, and deduce that for any
T>0
,

(B.15)

q_{n}^{\epsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{m+1}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})).

Step 3. Convergence.
The convergence follows from the uniform-in-
n
boundedness of solutions in the Lebesgue spaces
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
and
L^{2}(0,T;H^{m+1}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and the Aubin-Lions lemma. We point out that the
u_{n}^{\epsilon}
converges to
u^{\epsilon}
due to the uniform boundedness of mollifiers on Sobolev spaces and the Lipschitz estimates (
2.39

Step 4. Uniqueness.
Suppose there are two smooth solutions
q_{1}^{\epsilon}
and
q_{2}^{\epsilon}
with same initial data and with
u_{1}^{\epsilon}
and
u_{2}^{\epsilon}
determined by
q_{1}^{\epsilon}
and
q_{2}^{\epsilon}
respectively. Denoting the differences by
q^{\epsilon}=q_{1}^{\epsilon}-q_{2}^{\epsilon}
and
u^{\epsilon}=u_{1}^{\epsilon}-u_{2}^{\epsilon}
, we have

(B.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q^{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}(u_{1}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q^{\epsilon}+u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{2}^{\epsilon})+\frac{B}{(1+B^{2})}(J_{\epsilon}R^{\perp}q_{1}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q^{\epsilon}+J_{\epsilon}R^{\perp}q^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{2}^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1+B^{2})}\Lambda q^{\epsilon}-\epsilon\Delta q^{\epsilon}=0.

Multiplying the latter by
q^{\epsilon}
and integrating over
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
give

(B.17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(1+B^{2})}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|\nabla q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+B^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{2}^{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{B}{(1+B^{2})}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}J_{\epsilon}R^{\perp}q^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{2}^{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}dx=0.

Using the boundedness of the operator
T_{J_{\epsilon}q_{2}^{\epsilon}}^{-1}
on
L^{2}
, the Lipschitz estimate (
2.39
), and continuous Sobolev embeddings, we estimate
\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
as follows,

(B.18)

\displaystyle\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{1}^{\epsilon}-u_{2}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|J_{\epsilon}(T_{J_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}}^{-1}-T_{J_{\epsilon}q_{2}^{\epsilon}}^{-1})\left[-\mathbb{P}\left(J_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}RJ_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}\right)+BR^{\perp}J_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}\right]\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\|J_{\epsilon}T_{J_{\epsilon}q_{2}^{\epsilon}}^{-1}\left[-\mathbb{P}\left(J_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}RJ_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}+J_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}RJ_{\epsilon}q_{2}^{\epsilon}\right)+BR^{\perp}J_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}\right]\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|J_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|-\mathbb{P}\left(J_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}RJ_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}\right)+BR^{\perp}J_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\|J_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}RJ_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}+J_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}RJ_{\epsilon}q_{2}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\epsilon^{2}}\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\left\|-\mathbb{P}\left(J_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}RJ_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}\right)+BR^{\perp}J_{\epsilon}q_{1}^{\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C(\|q_{1}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}}+\|q_{2}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}}+1)\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\epsilon^{2}}(\|q_{1}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|q_{1}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}})\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}+C(\|q_{1}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}}+\|q_{2}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}}+1)\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain the bound

(B.19)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{2}^{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}dx\leq C_{\epsilon}(1+\|q_{1}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}}^{4}+\|q_{2}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}}^{2})\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By making use of the Hölder and Sobolev inequalities, we have

(B.20)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}J_{\epsilon}R^{\perp}q^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q_{2}^{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}dx\leq C\|q_{2}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}}\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining the above estimates gives rise to the energy inequality

(B.21)

\frac{d}{dt}\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\epsilon}(1+\|q_{1}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}}^{4}+\|q_{2}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}}^{2})\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Consequently, the uniqueness of solutions follows from the Gronwall inequality.

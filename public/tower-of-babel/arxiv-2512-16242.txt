Self-testing GHZ state via a Hardy-type paradox
Smritikana Patra, Soumyajit Pal, Ranendu Adhikary

Abstract
Self-testing is a correlation-based framework that enables the certification of both the underlying quantum state and the implemented measurements without imposing any assumptions on the internal structure of the devices. In this work, we introduce a self-testing protocol for the Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger (GHZ) state based on a natural generalization of Hardy’s nonlocality argument. Within this framework, we prove that the correlation achieving the maximal Hardy success probability constitutes an extremal point of the quantum correlation set and, moreover, that this point is exposed . To address experimentally relevant imperfections, we further develop a robust self-testing analysis tailored to the Hardy construction. Additionally, we show that, in this scenario, the quantum correlation that attains the maximal violation of the Hardy-type paradox coincides with the correlation that yields the maximal violation of the Mermin inequality. This establishes a unified perspective in which the same multipartite correlation admits both a logical-paradox interpretation and a Bell-inequality-based characterization. Collectively, our results pave the way for investigating whether the correlations that maximally violate the generalized N N -party Hardy paradox remain exposed in higher-party regimes.

V
Characterization of Triparty Hardy scenario

We now examine the extremal quantum correlation
\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{H}
associated with the tripartite Hardy paradox (
3
). Given that an extremal point of the quantum correlation set may be either
exposed
or
non-exposed
, our aim is to determine the nature of extremality of this particular correlation. Recall that an extremal point
\mathcal{P}
of a convex set
\mathcal{C}
is called
exposed
if there exists a supporting hyperplane whose intersection with
\mathcal{C}
is exactly
\{\mathcal{P}\}
. If no such hyperplane singles out the point uniquely, then the extremal point is termed
non-exposed
.
Exposed points correspond to unique optimizers of linear functionals over the set, whereas non-exposed points arise as limits of exposed points but are not themselves uniquely determined by any linear functional.

In the tripartite scenario with two dichotomic measurements per party and binary outcomes, the set of no-signaling correlations forms a polytope of dimension
26
. A convenient way to represent any point of this polytope is through the full list of single-body, two-body, and three-body correlators. Accordingly, any correlation
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{26}
can be expressed using the coordinate array

\begin{array}[]{c||c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c}&\langle B_{0}\rangle&\langle B_{1}\rangle&\langle C_{0}\rangle&\langle C_{1}\rangle&\langle B_{0}C_{0}\rangle&\langle B_{0}C_{1}\rangle&\langle B_{1}C_{0}\rangle&\langle B_{1}C_{1}\rangle\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr\langle A_{0}\rangle&\langle A_{0}B_{0}\rangle&\langle A_{0}B_{1}\rangle&\langle A_{0}C_{0}\rangle&\langle A_{0}C_{1}\rangle&\langle A_{0}B_{0}C_{0}\rangle&\langle A_{0}B_{0}C_{1}\rangle&\langle A_{0}B_{1}C_{0}\rangle&\langle A_{0}B_{1}C_{1}\rangle\\
\langle A_{1}\rangle&\langle A_{1}B_{0}\rangle&\langle A_{1}B_{1}\rangle&\langle A_{1}C_{0}\rangle&\langle A_{1}C_{1}\rangle&\langle A_{1}B_{0}C_{0}\rangle&\langle A_{1}B_{0}C_{1}\rangle&\langle A_{1}B_{1}C_{0}\rangle&\langle A_{1}B_{1}C_{1}\rangle\\
\end{array}

(24)

Then
\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{H}
has the following form

\begin{array}[]{c||c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c}&\langle B_{0}\rangle=0&\langle B_{1}\rangle=0&\langle C_{0}\rangle=0&\langle C_{1}\rangle=0&\langle B_{0}C_{0}\rangle=0&\langle B_{0}C_{1}\rangle=0&\langle B_{1}C_{0}\rangle=0&\langle B_{1}C_{1}\rangle=0\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr\langle A_{0}\rangle=0&\langle A_{0}B_{0}\rangle=0&\langle A_{0}B_{1}\rangle=0&\langle A_{0}C_{0}\rangle=0&\langle A_{0}C_{1}\rangle=0&\langle A_{0}B_{0}C_{0}\rangle=0&\langle A_{0}B_{0}C_{1}\rangle=-1&\langle A_{0}B_{1}C_{0}\rangle=-1&\langle A_{0}B_{1}C_{1}\rangle=0\\

\vec{\mathcal{B}}

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we present a self-testing scheme for tripartite Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger (GHZ) state based on a generalized version of Hardy’s nonlocality argument
17
. Using this framework, we derive a device-independent upper bound on the success probability of the generalized tripartite Hardy test and show that its maximal value is
\frac{1}{8}
. In addition, taking into account that experimental implementations are unavoidably affected by noise, we perform a robust self-testing analysis for the tripartite Hardy scenario. We establish that the protocol remains valid for noise levels up to
10^{-4}
in the zero-condition probabilities, thereby underscoring the practical applicability of Hardy-type self-testing methods in realistic device-independent settings.

Beyond their direct operational implications, our results provide additional structural insight into the geometry of the quantum correlation set. In particular, we investigate the quantum correlation that achieves the maximal violation of the tripartite Hardy paradox and establish that this correlation corresponds not only to an extremal point of the quantum set, but in fact to an exposed extremal point. This geometric feature is especially noteworthy given that the quantum correlation set is known to be non-closed
68
, which makes the explicit identification and characterization of exposed points both technically challenging and conceptually informative.

Moreover, recent work
31
has demonstrated that no single quantitative measure of nonlocality is sufficient to fully capture nonlocal behavior even in the simplest bipartite scenarios, underscoring the need to analyze different manifestations of nonlocality as fundamentally distinct resources. Our findings contribute to this program by showing that Hardy-type nonlocality constitutes a substantive complement to approaches based on Bell inequalities. Specifically, in the tripartite setting, we prove that the correlation that maximizes the Hardy-type paradox coincides exactly with the correlation that achieves the maximal quantum violation of the Mermin inequality. This establishes a unified framework in which the same multipartite nonlocal correlation admits both a logical-paradox characterization and a Bell-inequality-based characterization.

This work suggests several promising avenues for further investigation. A central open problem is to determine whether the correlations that achieve the maximal violation of the generalized
N
-party Hardy paradox for
N>3
continue to correspond to exposed extremal points, or whether novel forms of non-exposed extremality arise in higher multipartite configurations. Addressing this question would refine the geometric characterization of the quantum boundary and elucidate the structure of extremal correlations in higher dimensional multipartite scenarios. Furthermore, it is of considerable interest to explore whether other logical paradoxes—beyond the Hardy family—admit analogous multipartite self-testing frameworks, and whether such constructions reveal additional structural relationships among paradox-based, inequality-based, and geometric formulations of quantum nonlocality.

Non degeneracy of blow-up solutions of non-quantized singular Liouville-type equations and the convexity of the mean field entropy of the Onsager vortex model with singular sources
Daniele Bartolucci, Wen Yang, Lei Zhang

Abstract
. We establish the non-degeneracy of bubbling solutions for singular mean field equations when the blow-up points are either regular or involve non-quantized singular sources. This extends the results from Bartolucci-Jevnikar-Lee-Yang [ 7 ] , which focused on regular blow-up points. As a consequence, we establish the strict convexity of the Entropy in the large energy limit for a specific class of two-dimensional domains in the Onsager mean field vortex model with singular sources.

-sink in the large energy limit. Consequently, we need to extend first the results from
[

and
[

regarding the existence of negative temperature thermodynamic equilibrium states and the equivalence of statistical ensembles for a specific class of domains (domains of the first kind) see Definition

and sections

.
This extension relies on adaptations of known (
[

) dual variational methods, a sharp singular version (
[

]
) of the Moser-Trudinger inequality

, recent findings in
[

and

]
and
[

]
, which

However, as in
[

]
and
[

]

) is much more subtle as it requires, among other things, the understanding of the existence/non existence of solutions of the mean field equation (which is (

and a full description of the set of solutions in a small enough supercritical region behind

-sources) case, see

]
for partial results concerning this point and the very interesting counterexamples recently found in

]

[

]
,
[

[

]

At last, interestingly enough, as an application of these refined analysis, we come up with the exact counting of the number of solutions for the mean field equation (

Throughout the article,
B_{\tau}=B_{\tau}(0)
will always represent a ball centered at the origin in some local isothermal coordinates

This paper is organized as follows: Section 2 presents preliminary results essential for proving non-degeneracy, including a precise estimate for the difference between the parameter

Let
{\rm w}_{k}

and assume that

blows up at

(2.1)

Then we can rewrite the equation for
{\rm w}_{k}

(2.2)

\Delta_{g}{\rm w}_{k}+\rho_{k}(He^{{\rm w}_{k}}-1)=0\quad{\rm in}\ \;M.

From well known results about Liouville-type equations (

{\rm w}_{k}-\overline{{\rm w}_{k}}\ \to\sum_{j=1}^{m}8\pi(1+\alpha_{j})G(x,p_{j})\quad{\rm in}\quad{\rm C}_{\rm loc}^{2}(M\backslash\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}\}),

where

{\rm w}_{k}

\overline{{\rm w}_{k}}=\int_{M}{\rm w}_{k}{\rm d}\mu

M=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\overline{M}_{j};\quad M_{j}\cap M_{l}=\varnothing,\ {\rm if}\ j\neq l;\quad B(p_{j},3r_{0})\subset M_{j},\quad j=1,\cdots,m.

According to this definition
M_{1}=M
, if

.

The notation about local maximum is of particular relevance in this context. If
p_{j}
is a regular blow-up point (i.e.
\alpha_{j}=0
) we define
p_{k,j}
and
\lambda_{k,j}
as follows,

\lambda_{k,j}=u_{k}(p_{k,j})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max_{B(p_{j},r_{0})}u_{k},

while if
p_{j}
is a singular blow-up point (i.e.
\alpha_{j}\neq 0
), then we define,

p_{k,j}:=p_{j}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lambda_{k,j}:=u_{k}(p_{k,j}).

Next, let us define the so called ”standard bubble”
U_{k,j}
to be the solution of

\Delta U_{k,j}+\rho_{k}h_{j}(p_{k,j})|x-p_{k,j}|^{2\alpha_{j}}e^{U_{k,j}}=0\quad{\rm in}\ \;\mathbb{R}^{2}

which takes the form (

,

,

U_{k,j}(x)=\lambda_{k,j}-2\log\Big(1+\frac{\rho_{k}h_{j}(p_{k,j})}{8(1+\alpha_{j})^{2}}e^{\lambda_{k,j}}|x-p_{k,j}|^{2(1+\alpha_{j})}\Big).

It is well-known (
[

,
18
,
54

) that

can be approximated by the standard bubbles

near

\big|u_{k}(x)-U_{k,j}(x)\big|\leq C,\quad x\in B_{r}(p_{j},r_{0}).

As a consequence, in particular we have,

|\lambda_{k,i}-\lambda_{k,j}|\leq C,\quad 1\leq i,j\leq m,

for some
C
independent of
k
.

In case
m_{1}<m
, it has been shown in
[

(2.3)

\nabla(\log h+G_{j}^{*})(p_{k,j})=O(\lambda_{k,j}e^{-\lambda_{k,j}}),\quad m_{1}+1\leq j\leq m,

which, in view of the non-degeneracy condition

readily implies that,

|p_{k,j}-p_{j}|=O(\lambda_{k,j}e^{-\lambda_{k,j}}),\quad m_{1}+1\leq j\leq m.

It has been proved in

\rho_{k}-\rho_{*}=L(\mathbf{p})e^{-\frac{\lambda_{1}^{k}}{1+\alpha_{M}}}+O(e^{-\frac{1+\epsilon_{0}}{1+\alpha_{M}}\lambda_{k,j}})

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
\alpha_{l}\in(-1,0)
for

,
\alpha_{l}=0
for

\displaystyle\rho_{k}-\rho_{*}=~

\displaystyle\frac{16\pi e^{-\lambda_{m}^{k}}}{\rho_{*}h_{m}^{2}(0)e^{G_{m}^{*}(p_{m})}}L(\mathbf{p})[\lambda_{m}^{k}+\log(h_{m}^{2}(0)e^{G_{m}^{*}(p_{m})}r^{2}\rho_{*})-2]

\displaystyle+\frac{64e^{-\lambda_{m}^{k}}}{\rho_{*}h_{m}^{2}(0)e^{G_{m}^{*}(p_{m})}}(D_{R}+O(r^{\epsilon_{0}}))

\displaystyle+e^{-\lambda_{1}^{k}}\rho_{*}e^{-G_{1}^{*}(p_{1})}\left(\frac{8(1+\alpha_{1})^{2}}{\rho_{*}h_{1}(0)}\right)^{2}(D_{s}+O(r^{\epsilon_{0}}))+O(e^{-(1+\epsilon_{0})\lambda_{1}^{k}}),

\epsilon_{0}

and
\lambda_{m}^{k}
satisfy,

-\lambda_{m}^{k}=-\lambda_{1}^{k}+2\log\frac{h_{m}(0)(1+\alpha_{1})^{2}}{h_{1}(0)}+G_{m}^{*}(p_{m})-G_{1}^{*}(p_{1})+O(e^{-\epsilon_{0}\lambda_{1}^{k}}).

Remark 2.1
.

The sum of the last two terms in the expansion of

can be written either as follows,

e^{-\lambda_{1}^{k}}\rho_{*}e^{-G_{1}^{*}(p_{1})}\left(\frac{8(1+\alpha_{1})^{2}}{\rho_{*}h_{1}(0)}\right)^{2}(D(\mathbf{p})+O(r^{\epsilon_{0}})),

whenever there exists at least one singular source or as follows,

\frac{64e^{-\lambda_{m}^{k}}}{\rho_{*}h_{m}^{2}(0)e^{G_{m}^{*}(p_{m})}}(D(\mathbf{p})+O(r^{\epsilon_{0}})),

if at least one regular blow-up point exists.

Proof of Theorem

.

Here for convenience we define

(2.7)

\Phi(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}8\pi(1+\alpha_{j})G(x,p_{j}),

and recall the equation for
{\rm w}_{k}
in (

2.1

p_{l}

\Delta_{g}f_{k}=\rho_{k}~\mbox{in}\,\,B(p_{l},\tau),\quad f_{k}(p_{l})=0,\quad f_{k}=\mbox{constant on }\partial B(p_{l},\tau).

Then we set

u_{k}={\rm w}_{k}-f_{k}

and have

\Delta_{g}u_{k}+\rho_{k}He^{f_{k}}e^{u_{k}}=0,\quad\mbox{in}\quad B(p_{l},{\tau}).

Using the conformal factor function

To avoid cumbersome notations, for the time being we use the same notation for
p_{l}
in local and global coordinates. Recall the definition (

(2.12)

h_{l}(x)=h(p_{l}+x)\exp\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}(-4\pi\alpha_{j}G(x,p_{j})\bigg),\quad m_{1}<l\leq m,

and

h_{l}(0)=h(p_{l})\exp\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}(-4\pi\alpha_{j}G(p_{l},p_{j}))\bigg),\quad m_{1}<l\leq m.

Here we also note that around
p_{l}
for

\Delta(\log H+\chi+f_{k})(0)=\Delta\log h(p_{l})+\rho_{k}-N^{*}-2K(p_{l}).

2.1.

In this subsection we estimate an integral in the following Dirichlet problem:

\int_{\Omega(q,\tau)}\rho_{k}H(x)e^{{\rm w}_{k}(x)}\xi_{k}=\int_{B_{\tau/\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}}}\bar{h}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k}+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}y)e^{U_{k}}\xi_{k}(\bar{q}^{k}+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}y)+O(\varepsilon_{k}^{2+\epsilon_{0}}).

Remark that

)-(

) we see that
all the terms including

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tau/\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}}}\bar{h}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k}+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}y)e^{U_{k}}\xi_{k}(\bar{q}^{k}+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}y)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\tau/\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}}}\bar{h}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k})e^{U_{k}}(\bar{w}^{k}_{0,\xi}+\bar{w}_{k})+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}\int_{B_{\tau/\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}}}(\nabla\log\bar{h}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k})\cdot y)\frac{e^{U_{k}}(\bar{b}_{k}\cdot y)}{1+\frac{\bar{h}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k})}{8}|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}\int_{B_{\tau/\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}}}(\nabla\log\bar{h}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k})\cdot y)e^{U_{k}}\bar{w}_{k}+O(\varepsilon_{k}^{2+\epsilon_{0}})

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\tau/\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}}}\bar{h}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k})e^{U_{k}}\bar{w}_{k}+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}\int_{B_{\tau/\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}}}(\nabla\log\bar{h}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k})\cdot y)\frac{e^{U_{k}}(\bar{b}_{k}\cdot y)}{1+\frac{\bar{h}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k})}{8}|y|^{2}}+O(\varepsilon_{k}^{2+\epsilon_{0}})

\displaystyle=O(\varepsilon_{k}^{2+\epsilon_{0}}),

where
\bar{b}_{k}=(\bar{b}^{k}_{1},\bar{b}^{k}_{2})
and we used the same argument as in (

) to show that

\int_{B_{\tau/\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}}}\bar{h}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k})e^{U_{k}}\bar{w}^{k}_{0,\xi}

O(\varepsilon_{k}^{2+\epsilon_{0}})
.
Therefore we eventually deduce that,

(4.43)

\int_{\Omega(q,\tau)}\rho_{k}Hc_{k}(x)\xi_{k}=O(\varepsilon_{k}^{2+\epsilon_{0}}).

In view of (
4.40
), (
4.41
), (
4.43
), we come up with
a contradiction as follows,

\displaystyle 0=\int_{M}\rho_{k}Hc_{k}\xi_{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega(p_{1},\tau)}Hc_{k}\xi_{k}+\int_{\Omega(p_{2},\tau)}Hc_{k}\xi_{k}+\int_{\Omega(q,\tau)}Hc_{k}\xi_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{M\setminus\{\Omega(p_{1},\tau)\cup\Omega(p_{2},\tau)\cup\Omega(q,\tau)\}}Hc_{k}\xi_{k}

\displaystyle=Cb_{0}\Delta\log h_{k}(p_{1})\varepsilon_{k}^{2}+o(\varepsilon_{k}^{2}),

for some constant
C\neq 0
, since in particular the integrals on
\Omega(p_{2},\tau)
,
\Omega(q,\tau)

and
M\setminus\{\Omega(p_{1},\tau)\cup\Omega(p_{2},\tau)\cup\Omega(q,\tau)\}

o(\varepsilon_{k}^{2})
.
Since for

large
L(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
is the same as
\Delta\log h_{k}(p_{1})\neq 0
,
we obtain a contradiction as far as
b_{0}\neq 0
.

In this subsection, we shall prove that

We shall divide our argument into several steps as below, first, we would like to get some estimation on
\bar{v}_{k}:

Step one: Intermediate estimates for

We work in local coordinates centered at

,
0=x(q)
, with

. As before, by a slightly abuse of notation we set

in

and

(4.45)

\bar{\xi}_{k}(y)=\xi_{k}(\bar{q}^{k}+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}y),\quad y\in\Omega_{k}.

Obviously,
\bar{v}_{k}

\partial\Omega_{k}
. By Theorem
3.3
we have

(4.46)

\bar{v}_{k}(y)=U_{k}+c_{0,k}+c_{2,k}+O(\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2+\delta})(1+|y|)^{\delta},

\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2}\rho_{k}H\bar{c}_{k}(\bar{q}^{k}+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}y)\xi_{k}=\bar{h}_{k,0}(\bar{q}^{k}+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}y)e^{\bar{v}_{k}}\bar{\xi}_{k},

where
\bar{h}_{k,0}=\bar{h}_{k}e^{\psi_{k}}
. According to our setting,
\bar{\lambda}^{k}=u_{k}(\bar{q}^{k})
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}=e^{-\bar{\lambda}^{k}/2}
. Now we set

\bar{w}^{k}(y)=\bar{v}_{k}(y)-c_{0,k}(y)-c_{2,k}(y).

where
c_{i,k}

3.3
. Different from the expansion of
v_{k}
in the neighborhood of

, the term

can be regarded as error term, this is due to that
\nabla\bar{h}_{k,0}(\bar{q}^{k})=O(\bar{\lambda}^{k}e^{-\bar{\lambda}^{k}})
.
After direct calculation one can find that
\bar{w}^{k}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta\bar{w}^{k}+\bar{h}_{k,0}(\bar{q}^{k})e^{\bar{v}_{k}}\bar{w}^{k}=O(\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}^{3-\epsilon_{0}})(1+|y|)^{-1},\\

\bar{w}^{k}(0)=|\nabla\bar{w}^{k}(0)|=0,\end{cases}

and the oscillation of

|\bar{w}^{k}(y)|\leq C\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2+\epsilon_{0}}(1+|y|)^{2\epsilon_{0}},

which implies

|\bar{v}_{k}-U_{k}-c_{0,k}-c_{2,k}|\leq C\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2+\epsilon_{0}}(1+|y|)^{2\epsilon_{0}},\quad y\in\Omega_{k}.

As a consequence,

(4.48)

\nabla\bar{v}_{k}=\nabla U_{k}+\nabla c_{0,k}+\nabla c_{2,k}+O(\tau)\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}^{3},\quad y\in\Omega_{k},\quad|y|\sim\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}^{-1}.

Based on (
4.46
), we also have

(4.49)

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{k}(y)+\log\frac{\bar{h}_{k,0}(\bar{q}^{k}+\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}y)}{\bar{h}_{k,0}(\bar{q}^{k})}=

\displaystyle U_{k}+c_{0,k}+\tilde{c}_{2,k}+\frac{\Delta(\log\bar{h}_{k,0})(\bar{q}^{k})\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2}r^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle+O(\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}^{3})r^{3}+O(\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2+\delta})(1+r)^{\delta}.

Step two: Improved estimate on the oscillation of
\xi_{k}
away from blow-up points and vanishing rates of

,

From Lemma

In the following lemma, we shall prove that the oscillation of

Proposition 4.1
.

For any

Remark 4.3

Proof.

around

In local coordinates around
p_{1}

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists
C>0
independent of
k
such that

|b_{0}^{k}|\leq Ce^{-\frac{\lambda^{k}}{2}+\frac{\lambda^{k}}{1+\alpha}}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\alpha>1.

w_{2,\xi}^{k}
was defined in (

Then by standard potential estimates, we get

|\bar{\xi}_{k}(y)-\bar{w}_{0,\xi}^{k}|\leq C(\delta)\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}(1+|y|)^{\delta}.

As before, we set
\bar{\psi}_{\xi}^{k}
is set to be a harmonic function which encodes the bounded oscillation of
\bar{\xi}_{k}(y)-\bar{w}_{0,\xi}^{k}

B_{\tau/\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}}

Let

Mean field theory of the vortex model with singular sources

Let

by definition the energy of a fixed

To simplify the exposition, with an abuse of terminology (see Remark

According to standard conventions

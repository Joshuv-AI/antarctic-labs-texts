Survival benefit added by adjuvant chemotherapy in adenoid cystic carcinoma of salivary gland
Authors: Ning An, Yanan Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe aim of this study was to investigate the potential survival advantages associated with chemoradiotherapy (CRT) compared to radiotherapy (RT) as standalone modalities in the treatment of adenoid cystic carcinoma (ACC) of the salivary glands. Patients diagnosed with resected salivary gland ACC were retrospectively enrolled and categorized into two groups based on the type of adjuvant therapy received. The overall survival outcomes between the CRT and RT cohorts were evaluated using a multivariable Cox model. Post propensity score-matching, a total of 114 patients (57 in each treatment group) were included in the analysis. In the general patient population, CRT did not confer an additional survival benefit compared to RT alone. High-grade tumors, positive surgical margins, and the presence of five or more positive lymph nodes were identified as independent prognostic factors associated with poorer overall survival. Specifically, for patients with positive surgical margins, CRT was significantly associated with improved overall survival relative to RT, displaying a hazard ratio of 0.93 (95% CI: 0.81–0.99). Furthermore, in patients with more than four metastatic lymph nodes, CRT significantly reduced the risk of mortality by 6% (95% CI: 1-24%) when compared to RT alone. Conversely, in patients with high-grade tumors, the addition of adjuvant chemotherapy to RT did not yield significant alterations in survival outcomes compared to RT alone (p = 0.437, HR: 0.95, 95% CI: 0.75–2.07). CRT may offer an overall survival benefit for patients with salivary gland ACC, particularly those characterized by positive margin or the presence of five or more metastatic lymph nodes.Keywords: Salivary gland cancer, Adenoid cystic carcinoma, Chemoradiotherapy, Radiotherapy, PrognosisSubject terms: Head and neck cancer, Oral cancer, Cancer, Oral diseases

Introduction

Adenoid cystic carcinoma (ACC) ranks among the prevailing malignancies affecting the salivary glands, distinguished by its proclivity for perineural invasion and distant metastasis1,2. Current consensus across multiple guidelines underscores the significance of complete excision complemented by adjuvant radiotherapy (RT) as the cornerstone of standard care for salivary gland ACC, with RT demonstrating notable efficacy in enhancing locoregional control3–5. However, the incorporation of adjuvant chemotherapy into treatment regimens remains a subject of debate. While the NCCN guideline advocates for considering adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with ACC harboring high-risk features3, conflicting perspectives emerge from the American Society of Clinical Oncology guidelines, which caution against the routine use of concurrent chemotherapy alongside RT as a conventional approach for salivary gland cancer patients, unless within the realm of clinical trials4. Echoing this sentiment, the guidelines furnished by the German Society of Radiation Oncology discourage the routine addition of concurrent chemotherapy for salivary gland cancer patients, highlighting the ongoing necessity for prospective randomized trials to ascertain its therapeutic efficacy5.

The therapeutic conundrum concerning the optimal postoperative management of salivary gland carcinomas is largely compounded by the dearth of robust evidence substantiating the survival benefits conferred by chemoradiotherapy (CRT) following surgical intervention. Clinicians grappling with the decision to implement adjuvant CRT in salivary gland cancer cases often navigate a landscape characterized by a paucity of high-quality data. Their rationale for considering adjuvant CRT frequently draws from insights gleaned from randomized clinical trials and subsequent pooled analyses, predominantly in the realm of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, which have consistently showcased improved outcomes with postoperative CRT in comparison to RT alone6–8. Notably, while these seminal trials have unequivocally excluded malignant salivary gland neoplasms, the presence of extranodal extension (ENE) and positive surgical margins has been identified as determinant factors prompting the adjunctive use of chemotherapy alongside RT in head and neck malignancies.

Significantly few studies have directly compared the efficacy of CRT versus RT in the postoperative care of ACC within the salivary glands. Previous investigations have yielded equivocal findings, failing to establish a discernible correlation between enhanced prognosis and the application of CRT9. However, a recent study has surfaced, positing that CRT may represent a more efficacious therapeutic modality than RT alone in mitigating locoregional recurrences in this context10.

Thus, our primary objective centered on elucidating the potential survival advantages associated with CRT relative to RT as standalone modalities in the management of salivary gland ACC, with a specific emphasis on discerning whether such benefits exhibit variance based on the presence of distinct high-risk variables.

Patients and methods

Ethical approval

This study was approved by Henan Cancer Hospital Institutional Research Committee, and written informed consent for medical research was obtained from all patients before starting the treatment. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Study design

To fulfill our research objective, a retrospective review encompassing patients who underwent surgical intervention for salivary gland ACC from January 2000 to December 2022 was undertaken. Inclusion criteria comprised the following prerequisites: a primary diagnosis of the disease, receipt of either RT or CRT, conduction of elective or therapeutic neck dissection, and the availability of follow-up data spanning a minimum of two years. Exclusion criteria entailed individuals with a history of antecedent malignancies. Pertinent data pertaining to patient demographics, pathological characteristics, treatment modalities, and subsequent follow-up outcomes were meticulously extracted for comprehensive analysis.

Variable definition

All pathologic sections had been reviewed by at least two pathologists to confirm the correct diagnosis. Tumor and neck stages were determined in accordance with the 8th AJCC system. The pathological grade was stratified into low and high categories based on the extent of solid areas, utilizing a defined cutoff threshold of 30%9. Lymphovascular invasion (LVI) was deemed positive if cancer cells were present within the lymphatics, while perineural invasion (PNI) was considered positive if cancer cells had invaded a nerve, ENE was defined as the presence of cancer cells outside the lymph node capsule11. The severity and grade of adverse reactions were assessed in accordance with the CTCAE 5.0 system12.

Primary outcome variables were 5-year overall survival (OS), time of OS was calculated from the date of surgery to the date of death or the last follow-up.

Treatment principle

In our cancer center, all patients underwent preoperative ultrasound, CT/MRI and/or PET-CT examination for salivary gland ACC. Frozen section of primary tumor and margin was routinely conducted, and neck dissection was performed if there were clinically positive lymph nodes (LNs) or T3/4 stage or other adverse features, the dissected range included level I-III/IV at least. RT or CRT was likely to be suggested if there was presence of PNI, LVI, high grade, T3/4, LN metastasis, ENE or positive margin. The target volume included the tumor bed and neck area and prescribed 60–66 Gy. Chemotherapy typically comprised 4 to 6 cycles, predominantly utilizing cisplatin at a dosage of 70–100 mg/m² administered every three weeks.

Statistic analysis

Missing data patterns among tumor stage, pathologic grade, PNI, LVI, and ENE were deemed not missing completely at random13. Missing rates among the variables were 15.2% for tumor stage, 10.1% for pathologic grade, 9.4% for PNI, 8.2% for LVI, and 7.5% for ENE. Missing data were imputed using multiple imputation using Fully Conditional Specifications implemented by the multiple imputation by chained equations algorithm14.

The patients were divided into CRT and RT groups, the difference of clinicopathologic variables between the two groups was compared using the Chi-square test. A propensity score-matching (PSM) method was employed to minimize any differences in clinicopathologic factors between the two groups. The matching was done in a 1:1 ratio using the nearest neighbor method.

For primary outcome variable, actuarial survival rates were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and survival curves were compared using logrank test. Univariate Cox analyses were performed to estimate hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) for each covariate. A multivariable cox regression model was used to adjust for effects of other prognostic factors.

All statistic analyses were performed using R 3.4.4, and a p < 0.05 was considered to be significant.

Results

Baseline data

A total of 181 patients were encompassed in the study, exhibiting a mean age of 50 ± 10 years. Of these individuals, 84 (46.4%) were male, while 97 (53.6%) were female. The primary tumor site was localized within a major salivary gland in 162 (89.5%) cases, with the remaining 19 (10.5%) originating in minor salivary glands. Pathological tumor staging delineated 35 (19.3%) patients as T1/2, and 146 (80.7%) as T3/4. PNI was evident in 124 (68.5%) patients, while LVI was identified in 34 (18.8%) cases. Notably, high-grade pathology was observed in 95 (52.5%) tumors, with the remaining 86 (47.5%) cases exhibiting low-grade features. Evaluation of pathologic neck staging revealed N0/1 classification in 100 (55.2%) patients, contrasted by N2/3 designation in 81 (44.8%) patients. Among these individuals, ENE manifested in 25 (13.8%) cases, while 59 (32.6%) patients exhibited more than four positive lymph nodes. Metastasis at level IV/V was detected in 23 (12.7%) patients. Furthermore, a negative surgical margin was achieved in 124 (68.5%) patients.

CRT was administered to 57 patients, demonstrating a significantly higher prevalence of advanced neck stages (p = 0.016) and lymph node metastasis burden (p = 0.028) compared to individuals subjected solely to RT. No other notable distinctions were observed between the two treatment cohorts (Table 1). Consequently, these two factors were integrated into the propensity score calculation, leading to the inclusion of a total of 114 patients (57 in each treatment arm) for subsequent survival analysis. Remarkably, both groups displayed homogeneous distribution across all clinicopathologic variables (all p > 0.05, as illustrated in Table 2). Throughout the follow-up period, spanning a median duration of 5 years, 46 patients succumbed to their respective conditions.

Univariate analysis

Patients at T3/4 stage (p < 0.001), displaying high-grade pathology (p < 0.001), classified as N2/3 stage (p < 0.001), exhibiting positive surgical margins (p < 0.001), or presenting with five or more metastatic LNs (p < 0.001) exhibited a notably unfavorable prognosis in terms of OS. These factors were further scrutinized through multivariable Cox analysis. Notably, patients subjected to CRT demonstrated a comparable impact on OS when juxtaposed with individuals receiving RT alone (p = 0.449, HR: 0.95, 95% CI: 0.66–1.99). Moreover, no significant associations with OS were identified for the remaining factors (all p > 0.05, as outlined in Table 3). The 5-year OS rates were 68% (95% CI: 60-76%) for the CRT cohort and 60% (95% CI: 55-65%) for the RT cohort, with the disparity proving statistically nonsignificant (p = 0.407, see Fig. 1).

Multivariable analysis

Comparatively, high-grade tumors exhibited a significantly augmented HR of 4.87 (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 2.18–13.20) in contrast to low-grade neoplasms. The presence of positive surgical margins conferred an approximately 2.5-fold higher risk of mortality (p < 0.001, HR: 3.45, 95% CI: 1.34–8.90). Patients with five or more positive LNs experienced a 2.11 times increased likelihood of mortality (95% CI: 1.27–6.58) as opposed to those with no more than four metastatic LNs. However, neither tumor stage nor neck stage exhibited a discernible impact on OS (refer to Table 4).

Interaction analysis

In a bid to further elucidate the potential influence of CRT versus RT, the impact of different adjuvant therapies on OS was compared while stratified by the three independent prognostic factors. As depicted in Table 5, for patients with positive surgical margins, CRT was significantly linked to a superior OS compared to RT, with a HR of 0.93 (95% CI: 0.81–0.99). Furthermore, in patients harboring more than four metastatic LNs, CRT significantly diminished the risk of mortality by 6% (95% CI: 1-24%) relative to RT alone. Conversely, in patients with high-grade tumors, the introduction of adjuvant chemotherapy alongside RT did not alter survival outcomes compared to RT alone (p = 0.437, HR: 0.95, 95% CI: 0.75–2.07).

Acute toxicity

CRT demonstrated a higher incidence of hematologic-related events, particularly grade 3–4 reactions (p = 0.007). Both arms exhibited comparable distributions of nausea/vomiting, mucositis, xerostomia, and skin-related events (all p > 0.05). In both groups, the most prevalent grade 1–2 adverse event was xerostomia, followed closely by mucositis. Regarding grade 3–4 adverse events, hematologic reactions were the most common in the CRT group, whereas mucositis emerged as the predominant issue within the RT arm. There were no fatalities attributable to the treatment (Table 6).

Discussion

Our paramount discovery revealed that, in the comprehensive context of resected salivary gland ACC, the inclusion of CRT did not yield a superior OS outcome compared to RT as a singular intervention. Nonetheless, a notable observation emerged in patients presenting with positive surgical margins or more than four metastatic LNs, where the adjunctive application of adjuvant chemotherapy to RT exhibited prolonged survival benefits beyond that of RT in isolation. ENE neither functioned as an independent prognostic indicator nor reliably dictated the necessity of adjuvant chemotherapy. This investigation has thus cast a luminous beam on the optimal candidates for CRT, thereby influencing pivotal clinical deliberations pertaining to the strategic management of salivary gland ACC.

Chemotherapy assumes a pivotal role as an adjunctive component in the therapeutic armamentarium against head and neck cancer, effectively diminishing the likelihood of both locoregional and distant metastases while bolstering the sensitivity of radiation therapy. The current therapeutic paradigms dictating the utilization of CRT predominantly stem from the seminal discoveries encapsulated within two landmark clinical trials and their subsequent amalgamated analyses6–8. The EORTC 22,931 trial6intimately embedded 167 individuals afflicted by resected stage III/IV head and neck cancer into a rigorous randomization of treatment, dividing them into either RT or CRT cohorts. The ensuing appraisal of survival outcomes illuminated a stark revelation: the adjunctive administration of chemotherapy alongside RT engendered markedly enhanced 5-year rates of progression-free survival, locoregional control, and OS, with a parallel observation of analogous mucosal toxicities in both treatment arms. Similarly, the RTOG trial7meticulously allocated 459 patients into analogous treatment factions, delineating a notable 39% reduction in the jeopardy of locoregional relapse upon the augmentation of chemotherapy within the CRT group, accompanied by substantial ameliorations in disease-free survival rates. A comprehensive analysis orchestrated by Bernier et al8. further buttressed the pivotal roles ascribed to ENE and positive surgical margins as pivotal prognosticators heralding unfavorable clinical outcomes. While the ambit of these investigations did not explicitly probe the intricacies of salivary gland carcinomas, ENE and positive margins have subsequently crystalized as renowned cornerstones precipitating the incorporation of CRT into the therapeutic schema governing head and neck malignancies. Nonetheless, a backdrop of ongoing discourses lingers, foregrounded by the intrinsic chemoresistance frequently characterizing salivary gland cancers, typified by objective response rates oscillating within the broad spectrum spanning from 0 to 44%15.

The investigation into the prognostic implications of CRT versus RT in the realm of salivary gland carcinomas has been meticulously probed in various scholarly endeavors. Amini et al16. meticulously scrutinized a cohort of 2210 salivary gland cancer patients extracted from the NCDB, with 1842 individuals undergoing RT and the remaining subjects receiving CRT. Intriguingly, through a multifaceted multivariable analysis, a striking revelation emerged - a discernible compromise in OS was unraveled with the adjunctive application of CRT (HR: 1.22, 95% CI: 1.03–1.44) in contrast to RT in isolation, a trend that persisted even following meticulous propensity score matching (HR: 1.20, 95% CI: 0.98–1.47). In a seminal work by Cheraghlou et al17., a cohort encompassing 8580 salivary gland cancer patients from the identical NCDB repository was stratified into four distinct categories predicated on cancer staging (early vs. late) and the presence of adverse clinicopathological features. Amongst individuals grappling with early-stage disease amid the backdrop of adverse features, a therapeutic regimen entailing surgery concomitant with adjuvant therapy heralded enhanced OS metrics relative to surgery as a solitary modality. However, attributable to constrained sample sizes, the investigators refrained from a direct juxtaposition of the outcomes stemming from RT versus CRT. In poignant juxtaposition, challenging cases of late-stage disease devoid of adverse features juxtaposed a therapeutic milieu involving adjuvant therapy that did not register substantive enhancements in OS outcomes. Primarily elucidated by the meager prevalence of RT or CRT administration within this subgroup, hovering at a mere 41.5% of patients, notably lower than the anticipated norms. Diametrically opposing this narrative, the subset faced with late-stage disease cloaked in the garb of adverse features witnessed a paradigmatical shift towards a therapeutic trajectory encompassing surgery concomitant with adjuvant therapy, notably underscoring the pivotal role of RT in conferring a remarkable survival edge over surgery monotherapy. Gordon et al18., meticulously scrutinizing a vast cohort of 12,052 salivary gland cancer patients, uncovered a waning trend in the utilization of CRT across the broader patient populace, including those emblematic of high-risk attributes. Moreover, the adjunctive integration of chemotherapy into the RT gamut failed to delineate a tangible correlation with protracted survival, as evidenced by the cogent insights gleaned from both propensity score matching and multifactorial analyses. Echoing resonances akin to these sentiments, Aro et al19. expounded analogous findings, accentuating that while these inquiries were predicated on expansive NCDB collectives, palpable disparities in delineating high-risk delineators were conspicuous, potentially culminating in the inadvertent oversight of pivotal prognosticators such as the depth of infiltration, PNI, and LVI.

Nevertheless, the collective body of research has often grouped ACC with other pathological types, obscuring its distinctive features of infiltrative growth and propensity for distant metastasis. ACC, known for its infiltrative nature and metastatic tendencies, could potentially serve as an exemplary candidate for adjuvant chemotherapy. Surprisingly, scant studies have ventured into the realm of elucidating this precise conundrum. Qiu et al10.. endeavored to dissect the comparative efficacy of CRT versus RT in addressing head and neck ACC. The study scrutinized 206 patients, with 147 individuals subjected to RT and the remaining cohort embracing CRT. While the CRT cohort exhibited higher occurrences of upper gastrointestinal and hematologic toxicities, patients navigated through CRT showcased a significantly reduced incidence of locoregional failure when juxtaposed against RT monotherapy. Regrettably, lacrimal ACC was also roped into this study, albeit the mandate for adjuvant chemotherapy was fervently embraced to preserve organ integrity. Turning to the work of Hsieh et al20., which delved into a subset of 91 salivary gland ACC cases, a narrative unfurled wherein patients undergoing CRT demonstrated heightened rates of locoregional control vis-à-vis the cohort managed solely with RT, with comparable outcomes in terms of disease-free survival and OS both pre and post the deft hand of propensity score matching. Interestingly, our scrutinization did not buttress the preceding findings; a superior OS echelon was discerned within the CRT realm, a variance potentially underpinned by disparate inclusion criteria. The entirety of patients underwent neck dissection, thus rendering a higher burden of LN metastases, thereby affording them increased probabilities of reaping survival benefits from CRT.

The profound impact of positive margins on the prognostic tapestry of salivary gland ACC has been a fertile ground for exploration21. Antecedent inquiries have espoused the revelation that enhanced 5- and 8-year locoregional control prognoses were heralded by CRT, particularly in cohorts featuring stage III/IV disease, positive margins, or PNI20. Nevertheless, the debate surrounding whether the burden of LN metastases wields influence over the efficacy of adjuvant chemotherapy remained ensconced in uncertainty. LN metastasis, a relative rarity in salivary gland ACC, conferred a unique characteristic wherein the presence of a solitary metastatic LN failed to significantly alter mortality risks in comparison to the non-LN metastasis group22. Moreover, the emergence of 5 or more positive LNs underscored a high burden of LN metastases. Current research delineated that the survival dividends reaped from additional chemotherapy manifested most conspicuously in instances dominated by a high burden of LN metastases, as elucidated in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma23. Our own inquiry mirrored these findings in salivary gland ACC, unraveling a compelling nexus and unveiling a novel yardstick for the gauging of adjuvant chemotherapy efficacy.

Limitation in current study must be acknowledge, first, this was a retrospective study, there was inherent selection bias; second, our sample size was not large enough, it might decrease our statistic power; third, only the ACC was analyzed, thus, it remains uncertain whether our findings can be generalized to other histological types, consequently, further validation is essential prior to clinical application; fourth, due to the extended duration of our study, we were unable to make a comparative analysis of quality of life between RT and CRT groups.

In conclusion, the utilization of CRT did not demonstrate a markedly superior outcome in OS compared to RT in salivary gland ACC overall. Nevertheless, among patients diagnosed with positive margin or 5 or more positive LNs, the inclusion of chemotherapy alongside RT was associated with improved OS.

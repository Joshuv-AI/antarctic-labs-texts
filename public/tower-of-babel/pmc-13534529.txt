Coupled fractional-order, rotational, and hall current effects on deformation in a magneto-micropolar thermoviscoelastic half-space
Authors: Emad K Jaradat, Saurav Sharma, S M Abo-Dahab, Rajneesh Kumar, Sharif Abu Alrub, Montaser Fekry
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study develops a fractional-order magneto-micropolar thermoviscoelastic model to examine the coupled influence of viscosity, rotation, and Hall current in a rotating half-space. The governing equations, formulated using the fractional-order heat conduction law, are solved through the normal mode analysis method. The results demonstrate that viscosity acts as a stabilizing factor by dissipating mechanical energy and reducing displacement and temperature amplitudes. The fractional-order parameter introduces memory-dependent and nonlocal behavior, amplifying deformation at lower orders, while the rotation parameter enhances oscillatory motion through Coriolis coupling and delays attenuation. The findings reveal a distinct coupling mechanism between fractional-order and rotational effects, which governs the propagation and dissipation of thermoelastic and micropolar fields. This framework provides new physical insight into wave behavior in rotating conductive materials and offers potential applications in the design of smart composites, magneto-viscoelastic structures, and high-speed aerospace components.Keywords: Micropolar thermoviscoelasticity, Fractional-order derivative, Hall current, Rotation, Viscosity, Ramp-type normal loading, Normal mode analysisSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Physics

Introduction

The classical elasticity theory proves inadequate for characterizing materials that exhibit intrinsic microstructural behavior, such as polycrystalline substances and those with coarse-grained or fibrous internal configurations. While this theory effectively predicts the behavior of conventional materials like aluminum and steel–where stress–strain relations remain linear–experimental findings have revealed discrepancies that emphasize the significance of microstructural effects on mechanical response. To overcome these limitations, Voigt1 introduced an additional couple vector to describe the interactions among neighboring particles, initiating the concept of couple stresses. Cosserat and Cosserat2 further developed an asymmetric elasticity theory, later refined by Eringen3 and Eringen and Suhubi4, into the well-established micropolar elasticity theory, which incorporates microstructural effects and grants materials six degrees of freedom—three translational and three rotational.

Thermomechanical interactions in solids are of broad scientific and technological importance in areas such as aerospace, nuclear systems, and high-energy technologies. Eringen5 and Nowacki6 extended micropolar elasticity to include thermal effects, while Tauchert et al.7 developed corresponding constitutive relations. Boschi and Ieşan8 incorporated second sound phenomena, and Ciarletta9 demonstrated finite-speed thermal wave propagation in micropolar media. Sherief and co-workers later established governing equations for linear micropolar thermoelasticity. Magnetic field interactions have significantly expanded the scope of thermoelasticity with applications in geophysics, plasma dynamics, optics, and acoustics. In such contexts, mechanical and electromagnetic couplings arise through Lorentz forces and electromotive effects represented by modified Ohm’s law. Kaliski and Nowacki10 analyzed magneto-micropolar thermoelastic waves, while Othman et al.11 examined rotational and microtemperature effects in micropolar thermoelastic materials. Many practical materials exhibit viscous characteristics under dynamic or time-dependent loading, necessitating viscoelastic models. Eringen12 formulated a linear micropolar viscoelastic model, later expanded by McCarthy and Eringen13 to define wave propagation conditions. Cicco and Nappa14 provided decay estimates in micropolar viscoelastic dynamics, and Kumar and Sharma15 studied wave motion with interfacial imperfections. Subsequent works by Sharma16,17 and Sharma et al.18–21 explored viscosity and thermal effects in viscoelastic and two-temperature micropolar systems, while Sharma and Sharma22 addressed internal heat generation in biological media. Marin et al.23 and Fekry and Othman24,25 further refined these frameworks for thermoelastic diffusion and viscous micropolar systems.

In recent years, fractional calculus has emerged as a powerful tool for modeling nonlocal and memory-dependent processes in heat transfer, diffusion, viscoelasticity, and continuum mechanics. Fractional operators account for hereditary behavior more effectively than classical derivatives. The foundational work of Abel26, Caputo and Mainardi27, and Bagley and Torvik28, supported by comprehensive texts29–31, established fractional modeling as an essential framework. Rossikhin and Shitikova32 reviewed fractional approaches in wave propagation, while Povstenko33 generalized Fourier’s law using space–time fractional derivatives. Sherief et al.34 introduced a fractional-order thermoelastic model demonstrating uniqueness for 0<α<1, and subsequent works by Ezzat35, Youssef36, and Shaw and Mukhopadhyay37 extended fractional formulations to magneto-thermoelastic and micropolar systems.

The Hall effect plays a vital role in analyzing electromagnetic responses in conducting materials and forms the basis of modern sensors and detectors. Cowling38 demonstrated that strong magnetic fields necessitate a modified Ohm’s law to account for Hall currents. Zakaria39 studied Hall current and rotation effects in magneto-micropolar media, while Kumar et al.40 and Othman and Abd-Elaziz41 incorporated fractional-order and porosity effects, respectively. Deshwal et al.42 investigated fractional-order micropolar thermoviscoelasticity under ramp-type loads, and related studies43–47 examined renewable energy systems, phase-lag effects, and nanoscale structures. Marin et al.48, Sharma et al.49, and Kumar et al.50 extended such analyses to nonlocal, temperature-dependent, and piezoelectric systems.

Recent advancements in nonlocal and fractional thermoelasticity have significantly deepened the understanding of coupled thermo-mechanical behavior in composite and micropolar continua. Guo et al.51. investigated nonlocal thermo-viscoelastic responses of layered nanocompos.ites under transient thermal shock, while Li52 analyzed transient thermo-mechanical deformation in multilayered viscoelastic laminates. Nonlinear thermoelastic diffusion incorporating Soret and Dufour effects was developed by Guo et al.53, and fractional-order thermoelastic diffusion frameworks were established by Li et al.54,55. Further, Fekry, M56. proposed a method to reduce residual stresses in a growing thermo-viscoelastic cylinder, and Li et al.57 examined size-dependent thermo-electromechanical behavior in piezoelectric nanoplates. Some new contributions on thermos-viscoelasticity have been discussed in58–61.

Nonlocal thermoelastic theories are widely used to model size-dependent behavior in micro- and nano-scale structures. Peng et al.62 analyzed the nonlocal thermoelastic response of functionally graded microbeams, showing a strong influence of nonlocal parameters on thermal stresses and vibrations. Subsequently, Peng and Pan63 studied thermoelastic damping in functionally graded sandwich microbeam resonators using a nonlocal dual-phase-lag model with modified couple stress theory, highlighting the role of thermal relaxation and microstructural effects. More recently, Peng et al.64 demonstrated that surface and double nonlocal effects significantly affect damping and resonant behavior in graphene-reinforced microbeams.

Recently, Wenguang et al.65 presented an enhanced cavitation model that includes thermodynamic effects and multiple cavitation regimes. Peng et al.66 examined the nonlocal thermoelastic analysis of a microbeam made from functionally graded materials. Peng et al.67 explored the damping analysis of three-phase-lag thermoelastic behavior in graphene-reinforced laminated composite microplate resonators using a modified strain gradient theory.

Given their ability to dissipate energy, viscoelastic materials are widely used as vibration isolators and dampers. The present investigation develops a fractional-order magneto-micropolar thermoviscoelastic model for a rotating half-space subjected to Hall current and ramp-type thermal loading. Unlike prior studies, this work establishes the coupled mechanism between fractional-order effects and rotational dynamics, revealing how fractional parameters influence energy dissipation, stress propagation, and field localization under electromagnetic and viscous effects. Using the normal mode analysis method, closed-form solutions for displacement, stress, couple stress, and temperature are derived and examined numerically.

Motivated by these studies, the present work extends nonlocal thermoelastic modeling to a generalized viscoelastic continuum with rotation. The main contribution of this study lies in presenting a unified framework that couples fractional-order heat conduction, viscoelastic damping, rotation, and Hall current within a micropolar medium. This novel formulation captures memory-dependent and nonlocal effects absent in conventional theories and provides a foundation for analyzing smart composites, rotating machinery, and aerospace materials exposed to complex thermo-magnetic environments.

Effect of viscosity and fraction order parameter

To avoid ambiguity between the effects of the fractional-order parameter and those of viscoelasticity, it is important to emphasize that the fractional-order parameter θ and the viscoelastic relaxation parameters represent distinct physical mechanisms. The fractional order θ arises solely in the fractional heat-conduction law and therefore governs thermal memory and nonlocal heat diffusion, influencing the phase lag and dispersion of thermoelastic waves without introducing mechanical dissipation. Lower values of θ enhance hereditary thermal effects, resulting in larger temperature amplitudes and stronger thermoelastic coupling. In contrast, the relaxation parameters appear in the rate-dependent constitutive relations for stresses and couple stresses, where they characterize mechanical memory associated with viscous stress relaxation. These parameters produce genuine mechanical damping, reducing displacement and stress amplitudes and accelerating spatial decay through energy dissipation within the solid. Thus, θ modifies thermal dispersion and nonlocality, whereas control viscous attenuation and mechanical energy loss, and the two sets of parameters influence wave propagation through fundamentally different physical pathways.

Discussion of three dimensional figures

The three-dimensional field distributions provide a broader understanding of how viscosity and rotation reshape wave propagation and thermomechanical behavior within the half-space. Viscosity acts primarily as a dissipative mechanism, converting mechanical and microrotational energy into heat through internal friction. As a result, the 3D plots show smoother wave fronts, reduced displacement peaks, and diminished stress concentrations, especially in regions close to the boundary. This smoothing effect is particularly evident in the shear stress, couple stress, and temperature surfaces, where viscosity suppresses high-frequency oscillations and produces more gradual spatial transitions. Conversely, when viscosity is absent, the fields become sharper and more oscillatory, reflecting a more elastic, energy-preserving response.

Rotation, on the other hand, introduces distinct inertial contributions through Coriolis and centrifugal terms. These effects redistribute mechanical energy and modify the coupling between horizontal and vertical displacements, leading to pronounced asymmetries in the 3D field patterns. Higher rotational speeds amplify oscillations, deepen stress valleys, and delay spatial decay, which is clearly visible in the 3D surfaces for ​, ​, ​, and ​. Rotation increases the effective stiffness in some directions while promoting additional lateral motion in others, thereby generating richer spatial structures and extended influence zones. Overall, viscosity controls damping and attenuation, while rotation controls inertial amplification and directional coupling, and the 3D plots vividly illustrate the interplay between these mechanisms.

Conclusion and physical interpretation of results

The present study investigates the combined influence of fractional-order effects, viscosity, and rotation on a magneto-micropolar thermoviscoelastic half-space subjected to Hall current. Numerical and graphical analyses reveal that viscosity acts as a stabilizing mechanism, dissipating mechanical and rotational energy, thereby reducing displacement amplitudes, stress concentrations, and temperature gradients. The fractional-order parameter (θ₁), on the other hand, governs the memory-dependent and nonlocal responses, amplifying displacements and stresses at lower values, and highlighting the inherent complexity of fractional viscoelastic behaviour. The rotation parameter (Ω₀) significantly affects both mechanical and thermal responses, amplifying oscillations and delaying attenuation through enhanced Coriolis and inertial coupling. Collectively, these findings demonstrate the intricate coupling among viscosity, fractional order, and rotation in controlling the propagation, attenuation, and localization of thermoelastic and micropolar fields.

Physical interpretation of results

The observed variations arise from the interplay of viscosity, fractional order, and rotation. Viscosity dissipates mechanical energy through internal friction, reducing displacement and stress amplitudes and smoothing temperature gradients. Fractional-order effects (θ < 1) introduce memory and nonlocality, amplifying and prolonging deformation and stress responses due to hereditary behavior. Rotation enhances inertial and Coriolis coupling, intensifying oscillations and delaying attenuation. Collectively, viscosity governs damping, fractional order controls energy retention, and rotation augments dynamic coupling, defining the complex thermo-magneto-mechanical behavior of the system.

The developed model provides a theoretical framework with practical implications across multiple domains as geophysics and earthquake engineering: smart and functionally graded materials: biomedical engineering.

Future scope

Future research directions may include: (i) extending the present model to anisotropic and functionally graded materials to capture more realistic material behaviours. (ii) incorporating temperature-dependent viscosity and magnetic diffusion effects to better simulate high-temperature or electromagnetic environments.

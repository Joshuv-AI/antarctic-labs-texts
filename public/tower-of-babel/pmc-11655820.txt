The roles of LncRNA CARMN in cancers: biomarker potential, therapeutic targeting, and immune response
Authors: Huafeng Liu, Xuewen Liu, Yanjun Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs) are crucial regulators of gene expression and cellular processes, with significant implications for cancer research. This review focuses on the role of LncRNA CARMN (Cardiac Arrest and Regulated Myocyte Nuclear Protein) in various cancers. CARMN, originally identified for its function in cardiac tissues, has shown dysregulated expression in several tumor types, including cervical, breast, colorectal, and esophageal cancers. Its altered expression often correlates with tumor progression, metastasis, and patient prognosis, suggesting its potential as both a biomarker and therapeutic target. In cervical cancer, CARMN's role as a tumor suppressor is highlighted by its ability to inhibit cell proliferation, migration, and invasion through interaction with the miR-92a-3p/BTG2 axis and modulation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. In breast cancer, CARMN acts as an enhancer RNA, affecting epithelial-mesenchymal transition and metastasis by regulating MMP2 via DHX9. The downregulation of CARMN in triple-negative breast cancer is associated with enhanced sensitivity to chemotherapy. In colorectal cancer, CARMN’s expression is regulated by m6A methylation and mutant p53, influencing tumor growth through miR-5683 and FGF2. Lastly, in esophageal cancer, genetic variations in CARMN affect cancer susceptibility, with certain SNPs and haplotypes associated with either increased or decreased risk. Additionally, the relationship between CARMN and immune cell dynamics highlights its potential role in cancer immune surveillance and therapy. Finally, we found that CARMN may regulate immune cell exhaustion in the tumor microenvironment by influencing the recruitment and activation of NK cells and T cells, as well as modulating macrophage polarization. This review emphasizes the diverse roles of CARMN across different cancers and its potential as a diagnostic and therapeutic tool. Future research should address the mechanistic details of CARMN’s involvement in cancer, validate its clinical utility, and explore its therapeutic potential in combination with existing treatments.

Introduction

Cancer is a disease characterized by the abnormal proliferation of cells that have the ability to invade and damage surrounding tissues [1]. Globally, cancer is a major health issue and the second leading cause of death, following cardiovascular diseases [2, 3]. Each year, approximately 10 million people are diagnosed with cancer, and around 6 million die from it. The incidence of cancer increases with age and varies significantly among different regions and populations [4, 5]. For example, lung cancer is most common in men, while breast cancer is the most common cancer in women. The impact of cancer on society is multifaceted. Health-wise, cancer poses a severe threat to patients' health, affecting their quality of life and leading to pain, functional impairment, and complications [6, 7]. Economically, the costs of diagnosis and treatment are high, placing a substantial burden on families and healthcare systems. Socially, cancer affects patients' family and social relationships, leading to psychological stress and social isolation. Current cancer treatments include surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, and hormone therapy [5, 8]. Surgery is used to remove tumor tissue, radiotherapy employs high-energy radiation to kill cancer cells, and chemotherapy uses drugs to systematically kill or inhibit cancer cell proliferation [9, 10]. Targeted therapy focuses on specific molecular targets on cancer cells, immunotherapy harnesses the immune system to attack cancer cells, and hormone therapy interferes with hormone levels in the body [11, 12]. Advances in personalized medicine allow for treatment plans to be customized based on genetic and molecular characteristics of both the patient and the tumor, enhancing effectiveness and reducing side effects. In terms of prognosis, early detection and diagnosis are generally associated with better outcomes, as early-stage tumors are often localized and more amenable to treatment [13–15]. Tumor staging and grading are key factors affecting prognosis, with higher stages and grades usually indicating poorer outcomes. Additionally, the effectiveness of treatment, patient response, healthy lifestyle choices, and effective health management also influence prognosis. With ongoing advancements in medical technology and a deeper understanding of cancer biology, treatment and prognosis are expected to continue improving.

Non-coding RNA (ncRNA) refers to RNA molecules that do not encode proteins. Although they are not involved in protein synthesis, they play crucial roles in gene regulation, cellular functions, and organism development [16, 17]. Based on their length and function, non-coding RNAs can be classified into several types, including long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), small RNAs (such as miRNA and siRNA), and circular RNA (circRNA), among others [18–20]. Long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs) are RNA molecules longer than 200 nucleotides that do not encode proteins but play crucial roles in regulating gene expression and cellular functions [21, 22]. They exert their effects through various mechanisms, including modulating transcription, influencing chromatin modifications, forming nuclear structures, affecting RNA splicing, and regulating cell functions such as cell cycle, apoptosis, migration, and invasion [23, 24]. In cancer research, lncRNAs are of significant importance due to their potential as tumor biomarkers, as they often exhibit specific and stable expression patterns in different types of tumors [25, 26]. This makes them valuable for early diagnosis, staging, prognosis assessment, and predicting treatment responses [27, 28]. Additionally, many LncRNAs are involved in tumor initiation and progression by regulating cancer-related signaling pathways, promoting cell proliferation, inhibiting apoptosis, or enhancing invasiveness. LncRNAs also present promising therapeutic targets, with potential strategies including RNA interference and anti-LncRNA drugs [29–31]. They play roles in shaping the tumor microenvironment by influencing immune cells, angiogenesis, and matrix remodeling, which impacts tumor growth and metastasis. Furthermore, LncRNA expression levels are closely associated with clinical outcomes, helping to personalize treatment strategies [32–34]. Overall, LncRNAs offer unique insights into cancer biology and hold significant potential for advancing diagnostic, therapeutic, and prognostic approaches in oncology.

CARMN (Cardiac Arrest and Regulated Myocyte Nuclear Protein) is a long non-coding RNA (LncRNA) primarily expressed in cardiac tissue, though it is also found in various other tissues [35]. It is encoded by a gene located on human chromosome 19 and has a transcript length exceeding 200 nucleotides. CARMN's expression is regulated by various transcription factors and can be influenced by cellular states and environmental factors. Functionally, CARMN plays a crucial role in heart muscle cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival, thereby impacting normal cardiac function and structure [36, 37]. Beyond cardiac tissues, CARMN is involved in regulating cell fate in other tissues by influencing genes associated with cell growth and apoptosis [38–40]. In cancer research, CARMN has emerged as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target due to its altered expression levels in various tumors. Its expression is often elevated in certain cancers, potentially correlating with tumor aggressiveness and metastasis. CARMN also regulates gene expression by interacting with other RNA or protein molecules, affecting transcription and translation processes [41–46]. Overall, CARMN's unique expression patterns and functional roles make it an important subject for studying cellular biology and cancer mechanisms.

This review provides a comprehensive evaluation of long non-coding RNA (LncRNA) CARMN in cancer, focusing on its roles, functional characteristics, and clinical potential. It summarizes CARMN’s molecular features, regulatory mechanisms, and its impact on tumor growth, metastasis, and drug resistance. The review also assesses CARMN's potential as a biomarker for cancer diagnosis, prognosis, and personalized therapy. By addressing current research gaps and proposing future directions, this review aims to advance the understanding and application of CARMN in oncology. Molecular mechanisms of lncRNA CARMN in tumors

Molecular characteristics of LncRNA CARMN

Gene structure and transcriptional characteristics of CARMN

CARMN located on human chromosome 19. It produces a relatively long RNA transcript, typically exceeding 200 nucleotides, and is encoded by a gene with multiple exons and introns. The transcription of CARMN is regulated by its promoter region, which interacts with various transcription factors and is subject to epigenetic modifications such as DNA methylation and histone changes [44, 48]. CARMN exhibits tissue-specific expression, being highly expressed in cardiac tissues but also detectable in skeletal muscle and certain cancers. Its expression is influenced by physiological conditions and cellular states, and it may undergo alternative splicing to produce different isoforms with diverse functions [35, 39, 49, 50]. Predominantly localized in the nucleus, CARMN interacts with chromatin and nuclear components, playing a role in regulating gene expression and cellular functions.

The expression of lncRNA CARMN in cancers

RNA-Seq (transcriptome sequencing) is a high-throughput sequencing technology used to comprehensively analyze the transcriptome of cells or tissues, including both mRNA and non-coding RNAs [51, 52]. It involves extracting total RNA from samples, constructing a sequencing library from cDNA, and performing high-throughput sequencing to generate short sequence reads. These reads are then aligned to a reference genome for gene expression quantification, transcript assembly, and functional annotation [53, 54]. RNA-Seq provides insights into gene expression levels, transcript structures, and gene fusion events, and is widely used in gene expression studies, functional annotation, disease mechanisms, and drug screening. The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) is a large public database containing genomic, transcriptomic, and epigenetic data from various cancer types [55–57]. It includes data on gene sequences, RNA-Seq, DNA methylation, histone modifications, and clinical information from primary tumors, metastatic tumors, and corresponding normal tissues. Accessible through the Genomic Data Commons (GDC) platform, TCGA provides extensive datasets for researchers to download and analyze, supporting studies on cancer molecular mechanisms, biomarker discovery, novel therapeutic strategies, and cancer classification and prognosis. Together, RNA-Seq technology and TCGA data are essential tools for advancing cancer research and precision medicine. We analyzed TCGA datasets using Xiantao tool (https://www.xiantao.love/) and found that the expression of lncRNA CARMN was distinctly dysregulated in several types of tumors (Fig. 1A). Its upregulation was observed in CHOL, HNSC, KIRC, LIHC and THCA, and its downregulation was observed in BLCA, BRCA, CESC, COAD, KICH, KIRP, LUAD, LUSC, PRAD, READ, UCEC. In recent years, several studies have reported the different expression of lncRNA CARMN in cancers in different cancers based on RT-PCR. The distinct downregulation of lncRNA CARMN was observed in cervical cancer, breast cancer, colorectal cancer. However, whether the expression of lncRNA CARMN is upregulated in cancer has not been investigated. The observed dysregulation of lncRNA CARMN in various cancers, as identified through TCGA datasets and recent RT-PCR studies, suggests its significant role in tumor biology. The upregulation of CARMN in cancers like CHOL, HNSC, KIRC, LIHC, and THCA, contrasted with its downregulation in BLCA, BRCA, CESC, and other cancers, indicates that CARMN may have diverse and context-dependent functions in cancer progression. These distinct expression patterns highlight CARMN's potential as a biomarker for diagnosis, prognosis, and therapy. However, the precise mechanisms by which CARMN influences tumor development remain unclear. Future research should focus on functional studies to determine CARMN's exact role in cancer, validate its clinical utility, explore the reasons behind its upregulation in specific cancers, and consider its potential as a therapeutic target. The clinical significance of lncRNA CARMN in cancers

LncRNAs can serve as tumor prognostic factors due to their ability to regulate gene expression through various mechanisms, such as influencing transcription factor activity and altering chromatin states [58, 59]. Many lncRNAs play crucial roles in key tumor biological processes, including cell proliferation, apoptosis, migration, and invasion, thereby impacting tumor growth and metastasis [60, 61]. Their specific expression patterns in different tumor types or stages make them valuable biomarkers for assessing tumor type, stage, recurrence risk, and treatment response. Additionally, lncRNAs exhibit high stability in body fluids, facilitating non-invasive monitoring. Their interactions with microRNAs and proteins, as well as their roles as competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs), further contribute to their significance in cancer prognosis [62–64]. Overall, the diverse functions and specific expression profiles of lncRNAs make them promising candidates for tumor prognosis and personalized treatment strategies. We observed that lncRNA CARMN was associated with the clinical prognosis of several tumor patients, including BLCA, COAD, KIRC, KIRP, MESO and PAAD (Fig. 1B). Previous studies also provided evidences that low expression of lncRNA CARMN was associated with poor prognosis of cervical cancer patients, breast cancer patients, COAD patients, HCC patients [43, 47, 65]. This connection indicates that CARMN may influence tumor biology by affecting key processes such as tumor growth and metastasis. As a result, CARMN could be valuable in improving diagnostic accuracy, personalizing treatment strategies, and assessing disease progression. These findings underscore the need for further research to elucidate the mechanisms through which CARMN impacts cancer prognosis and to explore its potential as a therapeutic target.

Role and mechanisms of CARMN in cancers

Cervical cancer

Wang et al. explores the role of the long noncoding RNA (lncRNA) CARMN in cervical cancer and its underlying mechanisms [47]. The researchers found that CARMN and B-cell translocation gene 2 (BTG2) were downregulated, while miR-92a-3p was upregulated in CC tissues and cells. They showed that overexpression of CARMN reduced CC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, and increased apoptosis. Mechanistically, CARMN inhibits miR-92a-3p, leading to enhanced BTG2 transcription, and this effect was reversed by either miR-92a-3p overexpression or BTG2 downregulation. Additionally, CARMN blocked the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway via the miR-92a-3p/BTG2 axis. These findings highlight CARMN's potential as a tumor-suppressor in CC and its ability to regulate key molecular pathways. A recent study explores the post-transcriptional regulation of lncRNA CARMN in cervical cancer (CC), focusing on m6A modification and miRNA interactions. It reveals that CARMN, which is significantly modified by m6A in CC tissues, undergoes increased degradation mediated by the m6A reader YTHDF2. Additionally, miR-21-5p was identified as a key regulator, negatively affecting CARMN levels by binding directly to it and promoting its degradation. In vivo experiments corroborated that higher CARMN expression correlates with lower miR-21-5p levels. These findings highlight the critical roles of m6A modification and miR-21-5p in regulating CARMN and provide new insights into potential therapeutic targets for CC, suggesting novel strategies for improving diagnosis and treatment. Future research on lncRNA CARMN in cervical cancer (CC) should focus on several key areas. Mechanistically, it is crucial to further elucidate how CARMN regulates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway and its broader implications for tumor progression, including its interaction with miR-92a-3p and BTG2. Additionally, exploring how m6A modification influences CARMN degradation and evaluating whether targeting m6A readers or writers could stabilize CARMN and enhance its tumor-suppressive effects is essential. From a therapeutic perspective, strategies to inhibit miR-21-5p should be developed to prevent CARMN degradation, potentially through specific inhibitors or antagonists. Gene therapy or small molecules to restore CARMN expression in cases where it is downregulated could also be promising. Clinically, validating CARMN, miR-92a-3p, and miR-21-5p as biomarkers for CC diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment response is necessary, along with developing assays to measure these molecules in patient samples. Investigating the efficacy of combining CARMN-targeted therapies with existing treatments, such as chemotherapy, may improve overall treatment outcomes for CC patients. Unresolved questions include understanding how CARMN’s role changes across different stages of CC, identifying other non-coding RNAs that interact with CARMN, and assessing the systemic effects and safety of modulating CARMN levels in patients (Fig. 2). Breast cancer

Han et al. highlights the role of lncRNA CARMN as a tumor suppressor in early-stage breast cancer (BC) and reveals its mechanisms of action [43]. CARMN was identified as a key player in BC, with higher expression linked to better prognosis. It inhibits migration and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) by suppressing MMP2, a crucial enzyme in these processes. Mechanistically, CARMN acts as an enhancer RNA (eRNA) by binding to DHX9, which otherwise activates MMP2 transcription. By competing with DHX9 for binding to the MMP2 promoter, CARMN blocks MMP2 activation, thereby inhibiting BC cell metastasis and EMT. These findings underscore CARMN’s potential as both a therapeutic target and a biomarker for early-stage BC, offering new insights into managing BC progression and enhancing our understanding of lncRNA functions in cancer. Sheng et al. explores the role of lncRNA CARMN in triple negative breast cancer (TNBC), revealing its potential as both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic target. The research found that CARMN is downregulated in TNBC and its overexpression correlates with improved prognosis and enhanced sensitivity to cisplatin-based chemotherapy. Mechanistically, CARMN inhibits DNA replication by acting as a host gene for miR143-3p, which downregulates MCM5, a key factor in DNA replication. This interaction helps explain CARMN’s role in suppressing tumorigenesis and enhancing chemosensitivity. These findings highlight CARMN's potential to serve as a predictive biomarker for treatment response and underscore its value in developing more effective therapeutic strategies for TNBC (Fig. 2). Overall, these findings underscore CARMN's dual role in different breast cancer subtypes. It highlights its potential to serve as both a prognostic biomarker and a target for therapeutic interventions, offering new avenues for personalized treatment and a deeper understanding of lncRNA functions in cancer progression and treatment response.

Colorectal cancer

CARMN is significantly downregulated in CRC patients harboring mutant p53, primarily due to extensive m6A methylation, which promotes cancer cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis [42]. The demethylase ALKBH5, which normally maintains CARMN expression by targeting 477 methylation sites, is hindered by mutant p53 interacting with its promoter, leading to increased m6A methylation and subsequent degradation of CARMN by YTHDF2/YTHDF3. Overexpression of CARMN was found to suppress CRC progression in vitro and in vivo, with CARMN regulating miR-5683, which downregulates FGF2, a key factor in tumor growth. These findings provide insights into the regulatory mechanisms involving m6A modifications and highlight potential therapeutic strategies, such as targeting m6A methylation or enhancing ALKBH5 activity, as well as focusing on the miR-5683/FGF2 axis, offering novel approaches for CRC treatment and diagnosis. Future research on CARMN in colorectal cancer (CRC) should delve into several key areas. Mechanistic studies are needed to explore how m6A modifications specifically impact CARMN’s function in CRC, including the effects of mutant p53 on m6A methylation patterns. Additionally, further elucidation of the interaction between mutant p53 and ALKBH5 is crucial to understand how this interaction impairs ALKBH5’s function and subsequently affects CARMN regulation. Therapeutic strategies should focus on developing small molecules or genetic tools to target m6A methylation and restore CARMN levels, as well as exploring methods to enhance ALKBH5 activity to counteract the effects of mutant p53. Targeting the miR-5683/FGF2 axis could also offer potential treatments to suppress CRC growth. Clinical implications include validating CARMN, miR-5683, and FGF2 as biomarkers for CRC diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment response, and investigating the efficacy of combining these strategies with existing CRC treatments. Unresolved questions remain regarding how CARMN’s role and its regulation through m6A and miRNA change across different CRC stages, the systemic effects and safety of therapies targeting CARMN or m6A modifications, and interactions with other non-coding RNAs. Addressing these areas will provide valuable insights into CARMN’s role in CRC and pave the way for novel therapeutic strategies (Fig. 2).

Esophageal cancer

A previous study explored the impact of genetic variations in the long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) CARMN on esophageal cancer risk in northwest China [49]. The key findings include a significant association between the TT genotype of SNP rs353299 and a reduced risk of esophageal cancer (OR = 0.42, 95% CI 0.18–0.97, p = 0.042). Additionally, the influence of three CARMN polymorphisms (rs11168100, rs353300, and rs353299) on cancer risk varied based on factors such as age, gender, smoking, drinking, and lymph node metastasis status. Haplotype analysis identified that the haplotype Trs11168100Ars353303Trs353300Crs353299 acted as a protective factor (OR = 0.71, 95% CI 0.52–0.98, p = 0.038), while haplotypes Ars11168100Grs353303Crs353300 and Ars11168100Ars353303 were associated with increased risk (OR = 1.49, 95% CI 1.02–2.19, p = 0.041 and OR = 1.57, 95% CI 1.05–2.35, p = 0.027, respectively). These findings suggest that CARMN genetic variations could influence susceptibility to esophageal cancer, offering potential for genetic risk assessment and personalized prevention strategies. Future research should focus on several key areas to build on the findings related to CARMN genetic variations and esophageal cancer risk. Mechanistic studies should delve into how specific SNPs and haplotypes in CARMN influence its function and expression, and how these genetic variations affect cancer-related cellular processes. Additionally, exploring gene-environment interactions, such as the impact of smoking and alcohol consumption, could provide insights into how environmental factors modify esophageal cancer risk in the context of CARMN variations. Broader population studies are needed to validate these findings across diverse ethnic and geographical groups, identifying any population-specific genetic risk factors. Clinical applications should aim to develop genetic screening tools that incorporate CARMN SNPs and haplotypes for assessing esophageal cancer risk and guiding personalized prevention strategies. Furthermore, targeted prevention and treatment approaches could be informed by CARMN genetic profiles, particularly for high-risk individuals. Functional validation through in vivo and in vitro studies will be crucial to confirm how specific CARMN SNPs impact tumor biology and cancer progression. Unresolved questions include understanding the biological mechanisms through which CARMN genetic variations influence cancer risk, exploring gene–gene interactions, and assessing the long-term implications of these variations on cancer prognosis and survival (Fig. 2). Addressing these questions will enhance our understanding of CARMN's role in esophageal cancer and inform the development of personalized medicine approaches for prevention and treatment.

The role of long non-coding RNAs in tumor immune microenvironment: mechanisms and therapeutic implications

The immune microenvironment is a complex and crucial component of the tumor microenvironment, comprising various types of immune cells, cytokines, chemokines, and other non-cellular elements [66, 67]. The immune microenvironment not only has a profound impact on tumor initiation, progression, and metastasis but also determines the tumor’s response to immunotherapy. Immune infiltration refers to the process by which immune cells migrate from peripheral blood into the tumor tissue and accumulate within the microenvironment [68–70]. The nature and extent of this infiltration directly influence tumor biology and patient prognosis. For instance, a high level of T cell infiltration is often associated with a better prognosis, while the infiltration of immunosuppressive cells such as regulatory T cells (Tregs) or myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) is typically linked to tumor progression and poorer outcomes [71, 72]. In this context, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), a class of RNA molecules that do not encode proteins but have diverse functions, have recently been found to play a significant role in regulating the tumor immune microenvironment and immune infiltration [32, 73, 74]. First, lncRNAs can directly regulate the immune microenvironment by influencing the differentiation, activation, and function of immune cells [75, 76]. For example, certain lncRNAs can modulate T cell activation or inhibit macrophage polarization, thereby altering the intensity and nature of the immune response within the tumor. Second, lncRNAs play a crucial role in tumor immune evasion. Tumor cells can upregulate specific lncRNAs to suppress the host immune response, such as by regulating the expression of immunosuppressive molecules like PD-L1, which weakens the anti-tumor activity of T cells and helps tumor cells escape immune surveillance. Moreover, lncRNAs can influence the pattern of immune cell infiltration within tumors. Certain lncRNAs regulate the expression of chemokines or cytokines, attracting or repelling specific types of immune cells into the tumor microenvironment, thereby altering the level and nature of immune infiltration [77–79]. This regulation can significantly impact tumor progression and patient response to treatment. Lastly, lncRNAs interact with inflammation-related signaling pathways, modulating the inflammatory response within the tumor microenvironment and further influencing the strength of the immune response. For instance, lncRNAs can alter the expression profiles of inflammatory cytokines by modulating pathways like NF-κB or STAT3, thereby shaping the tumor’s immune microenvironment. In summary, lncRNAs have diverse and complex mechanisms of action within the tumor immune microenvironment. They not only influence tumor progression by regulating immune cell behavior but also support tumor growth by promoting immune evasion and altering immune infiltration patterns. The discovery of these mechanisms not only provides new insights into tumor biology but also opens up new avenues for future immunotherapy strategies. Investigating the role of lncRNAs in the tumor immune microenvironment could provide valuable clues for developing new therapeutic targets and enhancing the effectiveness of existing immunotherapies.

NK cells can recognize and destroy tumor cells without prior sensitization, while T cells, especially cytotoxic T cells, can target and eliminate tumor cells following antigen presentation. The positive association between CARMN expression and these immune cells indicates that CARMN may be involved in enhancing the recruitment or activation of these cells within the tumor microenvironment, potentially boosting the body’s natural anti-tumor response. Macrophages within the tumor microenvironment can adopt different phenotypes, such as the M1 (pro-inflammatory and anti-tumor) or M2 (anti-inflammatory and pro-tumor) polarization states. The positive correlation between CARMN and macrophage levels could suggest that CARMN influences macrophage recruitment or polarization [82, 83]. If CARMN promotes M1 macrophage polarization, it could enhance anti-tumor immunity. Conversely, if it is associated with M2 polarization, it might support tumor growth and metastasis. Further research would be needed to clarify this relationship. The association of CARMN with key immune cells suggests that it could serve as a biomarker for predicting the response to immune-related therapies, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors. Tumors with high CARMN expression might have a more active immune microenvironment, making them more susceptible to therapies that enhance immune cell function. If CARMN is found to actively modulate the presence or function of these immune cells, it could be explored as a therapeutic target. Modulating CARMN expression could potentially enhance the recruitment of immune cells to the tumor microenvironment, improving the efficacy of immunotherapies or reducing tumor immune evasion. The fact that this association is observed across multiple cancer types highlights the broad relevance of CARMN in tumor immunology. This pan-cancer association suggests that CARMN could be a universal modulator of immune cell dynamics in tumors, making it a potentially valuable target for a wide range of cancers. The potential role of CARMN in regulating immune cell exhaustion within the tumor microenvironment

Immune cell exhaustion refers to the state in which immune cells, particularly T cells, gradually lose their function due to prolonged exposure to antigens, such as those present in tumors [84]. This is characterized by reduced proliferative capacity, decreased cytokine production, and diminished cytotoxicity, ultimately leading to a reduced ability to combat tumors [85]. NK cells may also experience similar exhaustion, manifesting as decreased cytotoxic activity and abnormal cytokine production [86, 87]. The positive correlation between CARMN expression and these immune cells suggests that CARMN may influence immune cell dynamics in several ways. First, CARMN might enhance the recruitment or activation of NK cells and T cells within the tumor microenvironment, helping to maintain their functional state, prevent or delay exhaustion, and thus sustain an effective anti-tumor response. Second, the polarization of macrophages into M1 or M2 types may also play a role in immune cell exhaustion. M1 macrophages support anti-tumor immunity by producing pro-inflammatory cytokines and may help reverse or prevent T cell and NK cell exhaustion [88, 89]. In contrast, M2 macrophages may create an immunosuppressive environment, promoting exhaustion. If CARMN influences macrophage polarization, it could indirectly affect T cell and NK cell exhaustion. Understanding the mechanisms by which CARMN affects these immune cells, whether by preventing or promoting exhaustion, is crucial for guiding future therapeutic strategies. If CARMN helps prevent immune cell exhaustion, it could become a key target for enhancing the efficacy of immunotherapies; conversely, if CARMN promotes exhaustion, targeting CARMN might help reverse immunosuppression and reactivate exhausted immune cells. Therefore, the association between CARMN and NK cells, T cells, and macrophages suggests that CARMN may be an important regulator of the tumor immune microenvironment, warranting further research to clarify its specific role in immune cell exhaustion.

Future perspectives and clinical application potential

Future research should further explore the multidimensional regulatory role of CARMN in the tumor immune microenvironment, particularly its potential to influence anti-tumor immune responses by regulating immune cell recruitment, activation, and polarization. Based on the findings of this study, which show a positive correlation between CARMN and NK cells, T cells, and macrophages, experimental validation across different cancer types will be crucial. This will help assess the potential of CARMN as a broadly applicable tumor immune-modulating molecule, especially in cancer types with complex and heterogeneous immune microenvironments, such as lung cancer and melanoma. Such research will provide a comprehensive understanding of CARMN’s role in various tumor microenvironments, both in terms of consistency and specificity. Moreover, future studies should delve deeper into CARMN’s impact on immune cell function, particularly whether it can prevent or delay the exhaustion of T cells and NK cells [90, 91]. Immune cell exhaustion is a common feature in prolonged antigen exposure, characterized by reduced proliferation, cytokine production, and cytotoxicity, which diminishes the cells’ ability to fight tumors. If CARMN is found to play a positive role in immune cell infiltration and the maintenance of immune cell function, it could become a promising target to enhance the effectiveness of existing immunotherapies. Targeting CARMN to prevent immune cell exhaustion may help restore immune function, particularly for patients who have poor responses to current immune treatments, thus enhancing personalized therapeutic strategies. Further research should also focus on the interactions between CARMN and other components of the tumor microenvironment, such as its relationship with inflammatory factors and cytokine expression. CARMN's potential role in regulating inflammation-related signaling pathways, like NF-κB or STAT3, should be explored [92, 93]. If CARMN is involved in modulating inflammatory responses, it could help shape the tumor immune environment, either promoting or suppressing immune evasion. Should CARMN be found to exert a dual function in both inflammation modulation and immune escape, it may offer a new target for combined immunotherapy and anti-inflammatory treatment approaches, aimed at reversing tumor-induced immune suppression. Molecular mechanism studies could further investigate CARMN’s role in tumor progression and therapy resistance, such as its interaction with tumor stem cell characteristics and drug resistance pathways. This will provide the theoretical foundation for developing CARMN-targeted therapeutic strategies. In summary, CARMN, as a potential tumor immune modulator, not only provides new insights into tumor biology but also opens new possibilities for the development of cancer immunotherapies. As our understanding of CARMN’s molecular mechanisms deepens, it could become a widely applicable biomarker and therapeutic target in clinical settings.

Conclusion

Long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs), including CARMN (Cardiac Arrest and Regulated Myocyte Nuclear Protein), have emerged as critical regulators of cancer biology, offering valuable insights into tumor development, progression, and treatment response. CARMN's role in various cancers, including cervical, breast, colorectal, and esophageal cancers, underscores its potential as both a biomarker and a therapeutic target. In cervical cancer, CARMN acts as a tumor suppressor by regulating key signaling pathways, such as the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, and modulating interactions with microRNAs. Its role in influencing cell proliferation, migration, and apoptosis highlights its potential for targeted therapies and diagnostic applications. Similarly, in breast cancer, CARMN's function as a tumor suppressor is evident through its regulation of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and its interaction with transcription factors and miRNAs.

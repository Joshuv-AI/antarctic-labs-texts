Open cell property in weakly o-minimal structures
Tomohiro Kawakami, Hiroshi Tanaka

Abstract
. In an o-minimal structure, Wilkie proved that every bounded definable open set is a finite union of definable open cells. We generalze it to weakly o-minimal structures.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 03C64

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,\ldots)
be an expansion of a dense linear order without endpoints.
A subset
A
of
M
is said to be convex
if
a,b\in A
and
c\in M
with
a<c<b
then
c\in A
.
Moreover if

A=\emptyset
or
\inf A,{\rm sup}A\in M\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}
,
then
A
is called an interval in
M
.
We say that
\mathcal{M}
is o-minimal (weakly o-minimal)
if every definable subset of
M
is
a finite union of intervals (convex sets), respectively.

In o-minimal structures, Wilkie (
[
9
]
) proved every bounded definable open set is a finite union of open cells.
Andrews (
[
1
]
) proved that if an o-minimal structure admits
CE-cell decomposition then any definable open set is expressed as
a finite union of definable open cells.
Edmundo et al (
[
3
]
) proved in semi-bounded o-minimal expansion of an ordered group,
any definable open set is covered a finite union of open cells.
The relation between strong cell decomposition in weakly o-minimal structures and open cell property in o-minimal structures is proved by
[
4
]
.
We consider a generalization of the above results in weakly o-minimal structures.

2.
Weakly o-minimal expansions of real closed fields

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,\ldots)
be an weakly o-minimal structure.
For any subsets
C,D
of
M
,
we write
C<D
if
c<d
whenever
c\in C
and
d\in D
.
A pair
\langle C,D\rangle
of non-empty subsets of
M
is called
a cut in
\mathcal{M}

if
C<D,C\cup D=M
and
D
has no lowest element.
A cut
\langle C,D\rangle
is said to be definable in
\mathcal{M}

if the sets
C,D
are definable in
\mathcal{M}
.
The set of all cuts definable in
\mathcal{M}
will be denoted by
\overline{M}
.
Note that we have
M=\overline{M}
if
\mathcal{M}
is o-minimal.
We define a linear ordering on
\overline{M}
by

\langle C_{1},D_{1}\rangle<\langle C_{2},D_{2}\rangle

if and only if
C_{1}\subsetneq C_{2}
.
Then we may treat
(M,<)
as a substructure of
(\overline{M},<)

by identifying an element
a\in M
with
the definable cut
\langle(-\infty,a],(a,+\infty)\rangle
.
Morever, if
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,+,\ldots)
is an expansion of an ordered group
(M,<,+)
,
then the cut
\langle C,D\rangle
is said to be valuational
if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
x\in C
and
y\in D
, we have
y-x>\varepsilon
.
Otherwise the cut
\langle C,D\rangle
is said to be non-valuational.
The structure
\mathcal{M}
is said to be non-valuational if
all definable cuts in
\mathcal{M}
are non-valuational.

We equip
M
(
\overline{M}
) with the interval topology
(the open intervals form a base),
and each product
M^{n}
(
(\overline{M})^{n}
) with
the corresponding product topology, respectively.

Recall the notion of definable functions from
[
8
]
.
Let
n
be a positive integer and
A\subseteq M^{n}
definable.
A function
f:A\to\overline{M}
is said to be definable
if the set

\{\langle x,y\rangle\in M^{n+1}:x\in A,y<f(x)\}
is definable.
A function
f:A\to\overline{M}\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}

is said to be definable
if
f
is a definable function from
A
to
\overline{M}
,

f(x)=-\infty
for all
x\in A
, or
f(x)=+\infty

for all
x\in A
.
A definable function
f:A\to\overline{M}\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}

is said to be regular
if it takes all its values in one of the sets
M
,
\overline{M}-M
,
\{-\infty\}
, or
\{+\infty\}
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

For every
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
we define, by induction, strong cells
C\subseteq M^{m}
and their completions
\overline{C}\subseteq\overline{M}^{m}
.

(1)

Any singleton of
M
is a
0
-strong cell in
M
and is equal to its completion.

(2)

Any non-empty open convex definable subset of
M
is a 1-strong cell in
M
.
If
C\subseteq M
is a 1-strong cell,
then we define its completion by
\overline{C}:=\{x\in\overline{M}:(\exists a,b\in C)(a<x<b)\}
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
k\leq m
and suppose that we have already defined
k
-strong cells in
M^{m}
and
their completions in
\overline{M}^{m}
.

(3)

If
C\subseteq M^{m}
is a
k
-strong cell and
f:C\to M
is a continuous definable function
which has a unique continuous extension
\overline{f}:\overline{C}\to\overline{M}
,
then the graph of
f
which is denoted by
\Gamma(f)_{C}
, is a
k
-strong cell in
M^{m+1}
and
its completion in
\overline{M}^{m+1}
is defined as
\Gamma(\overline{f})_{\overline{C}}
.

(4)

If
C\subseteq M^{m}
is a
k
-strong cell and
f,g:C\to M\cup\{\pm\infty\}
is continuous definable functions
such that
f,g
have continuous extensions
\overline{f},\overline{g}:\overline{C}\to\overline{M}
, where
\overline{f}(\overline{a})<\overline{g}(\overline{a})
for any
\overline{a}\in\overline{C}
,
then the set
(f,g)_{C}:=\{\langle\overline{a},b\rangle\in C\times M:f(\overline{a})<b<g(\overline{a})\}
is called a
(k+1)
-strong cell in
M^{m+1}
.
The completion of
(f,g)_{C}
in
\overline{M}^{m+1}
is defined as
(\overline{f},\overline{g})_{\overline{C}}:=\{\langle\overline{a},b\rangle\in\overline{C}\times\overline{M}:\overline{f}(\overline{a})<b<\overline{g}(\overline{a})\}
.

A strong cell
C\subseteq M^{m}
,
m\geq 2
, is called a refined strong cell if
each of the boundary functions appearing in its definition is regular.
Refined strong cells in
M
coincide with strong cells in
M
.

Let
C\subseteq M^{m}
be a strong cell and
f:C\to\overline{M}
definable.
The function
f
is said to be strongly continuous
if it has a unique continuous extension
\overline{f}:\overline{C}\to\overline{M}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Any finite partition of
M
into singletons and convex open sets definable in
\mathcal{M}
is called a strong cell decomposition of
M
.
A finite partition
\mathcal{C}
of
M^{m+1}
into strong cells is said to be a strong cell decomposition of
M^{m+1}

if
\pi[\mathcal{C}]:=\{\pi[C]:C\in\mathcal{C}\}
is a strong cell decomposition of
M^{m}
.
Here
\pi:M^{m+1}\to M^{m}
denotes the projection dropping the last coordinate.
A strong cell decomposition
\mathcal{C}
of
M^{m}
partitions
X\subseteq M^{m}

if for any
C\in\mathcal{C}
, we have
C\subseteq X
or
C\cap X=\emptyset
.
The structure
\mathcal{M}
has the strong cell decomposition property
if for any positive integers
m
,
k
and any definable sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\subseteq M^{m}
,
there exists a decomposition of
M^{m}
into finitely many strong cells partitioning each of the sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
.
Similarly, we can define the refined strong cell decomposition property.

The following result follows from Lemma 2.6 and Corollary 2.16 of
[
7
]
.

Fact 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,+,\ldots)
be a weakly o-minimal expansion of an ordered group.
Then the structure
\mathcal{M}
is a non-valuational if and only if it has a strong cell decomposition property.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are refined strong cell decompositions of
M^{m}
.
We say that
\mathcal{D}
refines
\mathcal{C}
(denoted by
\mathcal{C}\prec\mathcal{D}
)
if every cell in
\mathcal{C}
is a union of some cells in
\mathcal{D}

The following statement is Fact 2.1 of
[
8
]
.

Fact 2.4
.

Let
k,m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
If
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\subseteq M^{m}
are definable sets,
then there exists a smallest refined strong cell decomposition
\mathcal{D}
of
M^{m}
partitioning each of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and any refined strong cell
C\subseteq M^{n}
,
we denote by
R_{C}
an
n
-ary relational symbol.
We interpret
R_{C}
in
\overline{M}^{n}
as
\overline{C}
, the completion of
C
.
According to Section 2 of
[
8
]
, the structure
\overline{\mathcal{M}}:=(\overline{M},<,(R_{C}:C\text{ is a refined strong cell}))
is o-minimal
and is called the canonical o-minimal extension of
\mathcal{M}
.
If
X\subseteq\overline{M}^{m}
is a set definable in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
, then
X\cap M^{m}
is definable in
\mathcal{M}
.
If additionally
Y\subseteq M^{m}
is definable in
\mathcal{M}
, then
X\cap Y
is definable in
\mathcal{M}
.

The following definition is Definition 3.1 of
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
Suppose that
X
is a defianble subset of
M^{m}
and
\mathcal{C}
is the smallest refined strong cell decomposition
of
M^{m}
partitioning
X
.
We define the completion of
X
in
\overline{M}^{m}
as
\overline{X}:=\bigcup\{\overline{C}:C\in\mathcal{C}\wedge C\subseteq X\}
.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that there exist sets
C,D
such that
C\subseteq D
but
\overline{C}\not\subseteq\overline{D}
.
Let
R_{alg}
denote the ordered field of real algebraic numbers
and let the unary predicate symbol
P
be interpreted by the convex set
S=(-\pi,\pi)\cap R_{alg}
.
Then, by Proposition 2.1 from
[
5
]
,
the structure
\mathfrak{R}=(R_{alg},<,+,\cdot,P)
is non-valuational weakly o-minimal.
So the structure
\mathfrak{R}
has the refined strong cell decomposition property.
Consider the functions
f,g:I=(0,4)\to\mathfrak{R}
defined by
f(x)=x
and
g(x)=x+1
,
and the function
h:J=(0,\pi)\to\mathfrak{R}
defined by
h(x)=x+2
.
Let
C=\Gamma(f)_{I}\cup\Gamma(g)_{I}
and
D=\Gamma(f)_{I}\cup\Gamma(g)_{I}\cup\Gamma(h)_{J}
.
Then, we have
C\subseteq D
but
\overline{C}\not\subseteq\overline{D}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
Suppose that
X
is a defianble subset of
M^{m}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the smallest refined strong cell decomposition of
M^{m}
partitioning
X

such that
X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}
and
\overline{X}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\overline{C_{i}}
with
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\in\mathcal{C}
.
If
X
is open in

, then
\overline{X}
is open in
\overline{M}
.

Proposition 2.8

(Weak definable choice)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,+,\ldots)
is a weakly o-minimal expansion of an ordered group
(M,<,+)
.
Let
S\subseteq M^{m+n}
be definable and

\pi:M^{m+n}\to M^{m}
the projection of the first
m
coordinates.
Then, there exists a definable map
f:\pi(S)\to\overline{M}^{n}
such that

\Gamma(f)\subseteq\overline{S}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the smallest refined strong cell decomposition of
M^{m+n}
partitioning
S

such that
S=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}
and
\overline{S}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\overline{C_{i}}
with
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\in\mathcal{C}
.
By the o-minimality of
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
and Proposition 6.1.2 of
[
2
]
, for any
C\in\mathcal{C}
with
C\subseteq S
,
there exists a map
\overline{f}_{C}:\overline{\pi(C)}\to\overline{M}^{n}
such that

\Gamma(\overline{f}_{C})\subseteq\overline{C}
and
\overline{f}_{C}
is definable in
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
.
By Propisition 2.3 from
[
8
]
,
\overline{f}_{C}|_{\overline{\pi(C)}\cap M^{m}}:\overline{\pi(C)}\cap R^{m}\to\overline{M}^{n}
is definable in
{\mathcal{M}}
.
By
S=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}
and
\overline{S}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\overline{C_{i}}
, this completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,+,\ldots)
is a weakly o-minimal expansion of an ordered group
(M,<,+)
.
Let
S\subseteq M^{m+n}
be definable and

\overline{\pi}:\overline{M}^{m+n}\to\overline{M}^{m}
the projection of the first
m
coordinates.
Then, there exists a map
\overline{g}:\overline{\pi}(\overline{S})\to\overline{S}

such that
\overline{\pi}\circ\overline{g}=\mathrm{id}_{\overline{\pi}(\overline{S})}
and

\overline{g}
is definable in
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:=\overline{\pi}|_{M^{m+n}}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is the smallest refined strong cell decomposition of
M^{m+n}
partitioning
S

such that
S=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}
and
\overline{S}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\overline{C_{i}}
with
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\in\mathcal{C}
.
By the o-minimality of
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
and Proposition 6.1.2 of
[
2
]
, for any
C\in\mathcal{C}
with
C\subseteq S
,
there exists a map
\overline{f}_{C}:\overline{\pi(C)}\to\overline{M}^{n}
such that

\Gamma(\overline{f}_{C})\subseteq\overline{C}
and
\overline{f}_{C}
is definable in
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
.
Let
\overline{f}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\overline{f}_{C_{i}}
.
Then we have
\overline{f}:\overline{\pi}(\overline{S})\to\overline{M}^{n}
and
\Gamma(\overline{f})\subseteq\overline{S}
.
We define the
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
-definable map
\overline{g}:\overline{\pi}(\overline{S})\to\overline{S}
by

\overline{g}(x)=(x,\overline{f}(x))
.
Then it follows
\overline{\pi}\circ\overline{g}=\mathrm{id}_{\overline{\pi}(\overline{S})}
.
∎

Example 2.10
.

Let
R_{alg}
denote the ordered field of real algebraic numbers
and let the unary predicate symbol
P
be interpreted by the convex set
S=(-\pi,\pi)\cap R_{alg}
.
Then, by Proposition 2.1 from
[
5
]
,
the structure
\mathfrak{R}=(R_{alg},<,+,\cdot,P)
is non-valuational weakly o-minimal.
So the structure
\mathfrak{R}
has the refined strong cell decomposition property.
Consider the functions
f,g:I=(-1,1)\to\overline{\mathfrak{R}}
defined by
f(x)=-4
and
g(x)=\pi x
.
Then, the set
(f,g)_{I}
is an open strong cell but not an open refined strong cell.
Moreover,
(f,g)_{I}
cannot be expressed as a finite union of open refined strong cells.

Lemma 2.11

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a weakly o-minimal expansion of an ordered field.
Then
\mathcal{M}
is a real closed field.

Fact 2.12

(
[
9
]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,+,\cdot,\ldots)
is an o-minimal expansion of a real closed field.
Let
U
be a definable bounded open subset of
M^{n}
.
Then, there exists a finite collection of open cells in
M^{n}
whose union is
U
.

Theorem 2.13
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,+,\cdot,\ldots)
is a non-valuational weakly o-minimal expansion of a real closed field.
Let
U
be a definable bounded open subset of
M^{n}
.
Then, there exists a finite collection of open strong cells in
M^{n}
whose union is
U
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the smallest refined strong cell decomposition of
M^{n}
partitioning
U

such that
U=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}
and
\overline{U}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\overline{C_{i}}
with
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\in\mathcal{C}
.
By Remark
2.7
, the set
\overline{U}
is a definable bounded open subset of
\overline{M}^{n}
.
Then, by Fact
2.12
, there exist open cells
D_{1},\dots,D_{l}
such that

\overline{U}=D_{1}\cup\dots\cup D_{l}
.
Since each
D_{i}\cap M^{n}
is an open strong cell and
{U}=(D_{1}\cap M^{n})\cup\dots\cup(D_{l}\cap M^{n})
,
this completes the proof.
∎

3.
Weakly o-minimal expansions of ordered groups

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a non-vaulational weakly o-minimal expansion
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,+,0,\cdots)
of an ordered group.
We say that
\mathcal{M}
is
semi
-
bounded
if
there exists no definable bijection between a bounded interval and a unbounded interval.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semi-bounded o-minimal expansion
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,+,0,\cdots)
of an ordered group.
Then every non-empty definable open set is a finite union of open cells.

Theorem 3.2
.

Consider a semi-bounded non-valuational weakly o-minimal expansion
\mathcal{M}=(M,<,+,\dots)
of an ordered group.
Let
U
be a definable open subset of
M^{n}
.
Then there exist finitely many strong open cells whose union is
U
.

Proof.

Take the smallest refined strong cell decomposition
\mathcal{C}
of
M^{n}
partitioning
U

such that
U=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}
and
\overline{U}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\overline{C_{i}}
with
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\in\mathcal{C}
.
By Remark
2.7
, the set
\overline{U}
is a definable open subset of
\overline{M}^{n}
.
Then, by Theorem 3.1, there exist open cells
D_{1},\dots,D_{l}
such that

\overline{U}=D_{1}\cup\dots\cup D_{l}
.
Since each
D_{i}\cap M^{n}
is an open strong cell and
{U}=(D_{1}\cap M^{n})\cup\dots\cup(D_{l}\cap M^{n})
,
this completes the proof.
∎

TP53 mutations and baseline lymphocytes indicate response to glofitamab in relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Jing Ruan, Yan Zhang, Daobin Zhou, Yan Zhang, Danqing Zhao, Chong Wei, Wei Zhang, Jian Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough CAR-T cell therapy has emerged as a promising therapeutic option for relapsed/refractory (R/R) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), long manufacturing time, high cost and toxicities limit its clinical use. T-cell engagers (TCEs) including glofitamab may address these unmet needs, but real-world data remain scarce. This real-world study (NCT06481826) enrolled 30 Chinese patients. The median prior treatment lines were 2 (2–5) and 90% had no prior CAR-T therapy. After a median follow-up of 15 months, the overall response rate (ORR) was 66.7%. The 1-year progression-free survival (PFS) was 62.5% with a median PFS of 15 months. The 1-year overall survival (OS) was 70%. Univariate analysis identified more than 1 extranodal involvement site, TP53 mutation and low baseline lymphocytes as poor prognostic factors. Safety profile was manageable. 43.3% patients developed cytokine release syndrome (CRS, mostly grade 1–3) and 3.3% had grade 1 immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS). The hematologic toxicity was mostly mild. COVID-19 infection occurred in 20% of patients. This study demonstrates that glofitamab is effective and well-tolerated in Chinese R/R DLBCL patients. Earlier lines of glofitamab may be more beneficial, while expanded cohorts are warranted to explore its efficacy among patients with multiple extranodal lesions, TP53 mutations and low baseline lymphocytes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-026-07068-z.Keywords: Glofitamab, Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, TP53 mutation

Background

Relapsed or refractory (R/R) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) has poor prognosis with limited treatment options. Although CAR-T cell therapy has emerged as a promising therapeutic option for these patients [1], a proportion of them will not receive CAR-T therapy due to rapidly progressive disease, comorbidities and importantly, substantial economic burden in China.

T-cell engagers (TCEs) can redirect autologous T cells against tumor cells. Glofitamab is a TCE bispecific CD20×CD3 antibody with a novel 2:1 structure, harboring bivalency for CD20 and monovalency for CD3 [2]. Since 2023, it has been approved by the National Medical Products Administration of China (NMPA). In the pivotal cohorts of the global NP30179 phase I/II study [3], glofitamab monotherapy demonstrated manageable safety and relatively high response rates in patients with R/R DLBCL who had received ≥ 2 prior therapies. However, only a small number of Asian patients were enrolled in this global study, and real-world data on frail patients, those without heavily treated including previous CAR-T therapy remain scarce. Furthermore, the patient population that can benefit most from glofitamab treatment remains to be defined.

In this real-world cohort, we summarized the efficacy and safety of glofitamab in Chinese R/R DLBCL patients. The factors affecting glofitamab efficacy and prognosis were also explored. (NCT06481826)

Methods

Patient population

This study prospectively enrolled R/R DLBCL patients with measurable lesions in Peking Union Medical College Hospital and Beijing Longfu Hospital between 2024 and 2025. Patients had received at least two prior lines of therapy. Written informed consent was signed by all the patients. The study was approved by the ethics committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Treatment and follow-up

Patients received standard treatment of glofitamab as follows: Cycle 1: D1 obinutuzumab 1000 mg, D8 glofitamab 2.5 mg, D15 glofitamab 10 mg. From cycle 2, glofitamab was given in a fixed dose of 30 mg every 3 weeks for up to 12 cycles. Other combined therapies were allowed in this real-world status. The treatment response was determined by enhanced computed tomography (CT) or positron emission tomography (PET), according to the Lugano criteria.

Adverse reactions at different time points were monitored and analyzed. CRS and ICANS were documented by primary care physicians based on consensus guidelines from the American Society of Transplantation and Cellular Therapy for Cytokine Release Syndrome and Neurologic Toxicity Associated with Immune Effector Cells [4]. Other adverse events were graded using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events Version 5.0.

NGS data analysis

Baseline diagnostic pathological formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tumor tissues (FFPE) were used for next-generation sequencing (NGS). The NGS panel was designed based on previous NGS studies in lymphoma and included 273 genes (Supplementary Material). Genomic DNA was extracted from FFPE tumor tissues of patients with DLBCL using the QIAamp DNA FFPE Tissue Kit (Qiagen). DNA samples were fragmented via enzymatic digestion to generate fragments with an average size of 200–300 bp. Library preparation and sequencing were performed in our hematology laboratory on a MGISEQ-2000 platform using 150 bp paired-end reads. The mean sequencing depth of each sample was 2000×.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive variables were displayed as percents of all patients with available data while continuous variables were calculated as mean ± SEM. Quantitative data were compared by unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test assuming equal variance while Welch’s t-test was adopted if variance was heterogeneous. Non-parametric tests (Mann-Whitney U test for two groups, Kruskal-Wallis H test for multiple groups) were used when normality failed or in small sample sizes (n < 30). Categorical data were compared using the exact Chi-square test. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from receiving glofitamab to disease progression. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from receiving glofitamab to death by any cause. PFS and OS were analyzed using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. A two-sided p value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

From 2024 to 2025, 30 patients received ≥ 1 dose of glofitamab via commercial use in two centers in our study. Basic characteristics were shown in Table 1. The median age was 59.5 years old (26–85 years) and 9 (30%) of them were males. 10 (33.3%) patients were transformed from follicular lymphoma. 16 (53.3%) were GCB type, 11 (36.7%) were double expressor and 8 (26.7%) had TP53 mutations. 24 (80%) patients were stage IV at diagnosis according to Ann Arbor stages. The Revised International Prognostic Index (R-IPI) score was 1–2 in 7 (23.3%) patients and was 3–5 in 23 (76.7%) patients. The median number of previous therapies was 2 [2–5] and 17 (56.7%) were refractory to first-line therapy. 2 (6.7%) patients had undergone previous autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) and 3 (10%) had received CD19 CAR-T therapy.

Clinical characteristics at the initiation of glofitamab and the best response of treatment

Treatment and clinical outcome

24 (80%) patients were treated with glofitamab as monotherapy. 6 patients received combined treatment, 2 with polatuzumab vedotin, 2 with zanubrutinib, 1 with lenalidomide, and 1 with venetoclax and selinexor.

Till submission, the median follow-up time was 15 (8–20) months. Outcome were shown in Fig. 1. As for best response, 16/30 (53.3%) patients achieved CR, 4/30 (13.3%) achieved partial response (PR), 4/30 (13.3%) achieved stable disease (SD) and 6/30 (20%) achieved progressive disease (PD), with an overall response rate (ORR) of 66.7%. 12/16 (75%) patients remained CR during follow-up. 3/16 (18.7%) patients had PD within 3 to 5 months after the completion of 12 cycles of glofitamab. 1/16 (6.3%) died of COVID-19 pneumonia after cycle 5 while lymphoma was stable. Among patients with a best response of PR, 1/4 (25%) remained PR after 12 cycles and 3/4 (75%) eventually developed PD after 5–8 cycles of treatment. Of the 4 patients with SD as best response, 3 (75%) progressed at cycles 3–4, and 1 (25%) died of gastrointestinal bleeding and sepsis after 1 cycle. The 1-year PFS was 62.5%, with a median PFS of 15 months. The 1-year OS was 70%, and median OS had not yet been reached.

Clinical outcome displayed by the swimmer plot

In our cohort, 3/30 (10%) patients had previous CD19 CAR-T. 2/3 (66.7%) achieved CR and 1/3 (33.3%) had PD. Of the two CR patients, one developed PD 1 month after infusion of the investigational CD19 CAR-T product (Chineo, China) and then received glofitamab; the other had PD 9 months after relma-cel infusion and underwent glofitamab. The PD patient had an underlying TP53 mutation, progressed 4 months after axi-cel infusion, and then received glofitamab. Of the 15/30 (50%) patients who experienced PD during glofitamab treatment, 4/15 (27%) patients underwent CAR-T therapy: 2 received CD19 CAR-T and 2 received CD19/CD20 dual-target CAR-T. All of them remained CR by the time of submission.

Univariate analysis was performed to explore the associations of clinical characteristics with glofitamab treatment response, PFS and OS (Table 2). The number of extranodal involvement sites was significantly associated with treatment response, PFS and OS. The overall response rates (ORRs) were 85% and 30%, respectively, in patients with ≤ 1 and >1 extranodal involvement sites. The 1-year PFS was 75% in patients with ≤ 1 extranodal involvement sites while it was 24% in patients with > 1 sites (p = 0.024) (Fig. 2). Additionally, the 1-year OS was significantly lower in patients with >1 extranodal involvement (30% vs. 90%, p < 0.001). Notably, 2 patients with bone marrow involvement and 3 patients with liver involvement progressed within 1–2 months. Two patients with central nervous system (CNS) involvement had best response of PR and progressed in 3–6 months. TP53 mutation was also found to be associated with poor prognosis in patients treated with glofitamab. The ORR was 77% in patients without TP53 mutation versus 37% in those with TP53 mutation (p = 0.042). The 1-year PFS was 25% in patients with TP53 mutation, significantly lower than that in patients without TP53 mutation (72%, p = 0.009). The ORR was numerically higher in DLBCL transformed from follicular lymphoma (FL) than novo DLBCL (90% vs. 55%, p = 0.058). Other factors including age, gender, Ann-arbor stage, R-IPI score, COO subtype, double expression, primary refractory disease, lines of treatment and CRS were not related to the ORR, PFS and OS in our cohort.

The changes of peripheral lymphocytes in the first three cycles were recorded during treatment (Supplementary Fig. 1). Lymphocyte counts decreased significantly on the second day after glofitamab administration but mostly recovered by the start of the next treatment cycle. The number of lymphocytes before treatment (C1D0) was significantly related to the PFS and OS (Fig. 3). Using a cutoff value of 0.6 × 109/L, the 1-year PFS was 60.9% and 37.5% respectively (p = 0.023). Patients with a baseline lymphocyte count greater than 0.6 × 10⁹/L also had better 1-year OS (93.8% vs. 60%, p = 0.028), although the number of events was limited.

The number of lymphocytes before treatment (C1D0) was significantly related to the PFS and OS. With the cut-off value to be 0.6 × 109/L, the 1-year PFS was 0.609 and 0.375 (p = 0.023), and the 1-year OS was 0.938 and 0.6 (p = 0.028), respectively

Adverse effects

CRS was observed in 13/30 (43.3%) patients, of whom 7/30 (23.3%) had grade 1 CRS, 1/30 (3.3%) had grade 2 CRS, and 5/30 (16.7%) had grade 3 CRS. Tocilizumab was administered to patients with grade 2–3 CRS and corticosteroids were used for grade 3 CRS. Only 1/30 (3.3%) patient developed grade 1 ICANS without CRS in our cohort on C1D15 and he recovered following treatment with dexamethasone. All CRS and ICANS were observed in cycle 1. 3/30 (10%) patients had neutropenia, 2 were grade 3 and 1 was grade 4, all received G-CSF. 1/30 (3.3%) had grade 2 thrombocytopenia and recovered without transfusion. 1 patient with gastrointestinal tract involvement died of gastrointestinal bleeding and sepsis during cycle 1. 10/30 (33.3%) had infections, among whom 5/30 (16.7%) were grade 3, 1/30 (3.3%) was grade 4 and 1/30 (3.3%) was grade 5. Notably, 6/30 (20%) patients developed COVID-19 infection during treatment while 1 patient died of COVID-19 pneumonia.

Discussion

Although CAR-T therapy has been recommended for R/R DLBCL in clinical guidelines based on various clinical trials, the high cost, prolonged manufacturing time, and severe adverse effects have limited its clinical application. Bispecific antibodies, including glofitamab, are considered promising treatment options for these patients.

In the global phase I/II study [3], fixed duration of glofitamab monotherapy was prescribed and significantly lower rates of CRS and ICANS were observed compared with CAR-T therapy. At a median follow-up of 12.6 months among the 155 patients who received the phase 2 dose of glofitamab, the ORR was 52% (CR 39%), and the 1-year PFS was 37%. Data in real-world setting, especially in Chinese patients remain limited. In the largest real-world cohort published in 2025 [5], 156 patients received epcoritamab and 89 received glofitamab in 21 US institutions; the ORR was 51% for epcoritamab and 53% for glofitamab, respectively, with a median PFS of 2.6 months and a 6-month PFS rate of 36%. Notably, 147 of them had received prior CAR-T therapy. Björn Chapuy et al. reported another multicenter real-world study in Germany, Austria and Switzerland [6]. The ORR was 46% and median PFS was 3.6 months in 70 patients whose median prior treatment lines were 4. Another real-world cohort in Israel [7], in which 91% patients had previous CAR-T therapy, showed an ORR of 34% and a median PFS of 2 months. Burhan Ferhanoglu et al. [8] reported the real-world data of glofitamab monotherapy in 46 patients in Turkey, the ORR was 37% and the median PFS was 3.3 months. These patients were heavily treated: the median number of prior treatment lines was 4 (range: 3–6), and 20 patients (47.6%) had undergone prior ASCT. As for Asian patients, Zhu et al. [9] had firstly reported the results from a multicenter phase I study in 2023, in which Chinese patients with R/R DLBCL after ≥ 2 prior lines of treatment were included. The ORR was 66.7% (CR 52%), the median PFS was 8.6 months, and the median OS was not reached after a median follow-up of 15 months. Only one real-world study was published in Asian population, which was performed in multi-centers in Taiwan [10]. The ORR was 56% and the 1-year PFS was 33%, while these 34 patients had a median prior treatment line of 5. In our cohort, the ORR was 66.7%, the 1-year PFS was 62.5% and the median PFS was 15 months, better than previously reported data. Compared with other studies, 33% of our patients were DLBCL transformed from FL, the median line of previous therapy was 2 and most (90%) had not received prior CAR-T. These data suggest that the incorporation of glofitamab in earlier lines of therapy might be more beneficial.

To further explore the factors affecting glofitamab efficacy, univariate analysis was performed. We found that patients with >1 extranodal involvement sites before glofitamab had significantly poorer response and long term survival which was not valued in previous studies. In fact, the number of extranodal involvement sites is a well-established poor prognostic factor in DLBCL [11], mainly because this indicator is closely linked to tumor aggressiveness, disease burden, and immune escape ability. Patients with bone marrow and liver involvement also seemed to have poor response to glofitamab in our study although the number of these patients was relatively low and needs further investigation. In the context of TCE therapy, these extranodal sites may have distinct tumor immune microenvironment and limit the ability of T cells to infiltrate and eliminate tumor cells [12]. Another important finding in our study was that TP53 mutation was also proved to be associated with glofitamab nonresponse and shorter PFS. Bröske et al. performed gene expression analysis of the pretreatment tumor biopsy samples in study NP30179 and revealed that TP53 signaling was associated with resistance to glofitamab [2]. They found that non-CR patients had lower expression of TP53 target genes and there was a trend that TP53 mutant group had more non-CRs (p = 0.09). Recent studies have highlighted the impact of TP53 mutations on immune escape [13–15]. Preclinical studies show that TP53-mutant tumor cells are less susceptible to T-cell mediated cytotoxicity, as these cells can evade apoptosis and upregulate immune checkpoint molecules [16]. Notably, the negative impact of TP53 mutation on glofitamab efficacy has not been fully characterized in global trials and further studies including in vivo and in vitro are needed. Since TP53 mutations are known to be acquired after chemotherapy, we speculate that patients with more prior lines of treatment in other real-world cohorts may have a higher proportion of acquired TP53 mutations. This finding could potentially explain the relatively poorer prognosis in those cohorts, which is also worthy of further exploration. Interestingly, in our study we also found that low baseline absolute lymphocytes were associated with poorer PFS and OS in patients treated with glofitamab. This is reasonable, since the pool of circulating and tumor‑infiltrating T cells is important for TCE-mediated activation and tumor killing, and we observed sustained or even further decreases in lymphocytes after 3 cycles of glofitamab in patients with low baseline lymphocyte counts. Furthermore, low baseline lymphocyte may indicate innate immune exhaustion status in lymphoma patients. Overall, our findings suggest that multiple extranodal sites, TP53 mutations and low baseline lymphocytes may indicate poorer response to glofitamab while expanded cohorts are warranted to confirm the conclusions. Combined agents that modulate the tumor microenvironment may help enhance response rates and durability in selected patient populations.

We also observed a trend toward higher ORR in DLBCL transformed from FL while this difference did not reach statistical significance. Björn Chapuy et al. also reported the same conclusion in their cohort [6]. It is consistent with observations that DLBCL transformed from FL often retains a more indolent immune phenotype and may be a particularly suitable population for TCE therapy. Notably, traditional prognostic factors such as age, gender, Ann Arbor stage, R-IPI score, COO subtype, double expression (MYC/BCL2), primary refractoriness, and even number of prior lines of therapy were not associated with ORR, PFS, or OS in our cohort. This contrasts with some global studies which may be due to limited sample size and ethnic differences. Further studies with larger, ethnically diverse cohorts are needed to clarify these discrepancies.

Adverse events in our cohort included CRS in 43% patients and ICANS in 3.3% patients, similar to the previous studies and all cases were effectively managed. Bone marrow suppression was also less frequent compared with conventional chemotherapy or CAR-T. All these data confirmed that the standard dosing regimen is safe and effective for Chinese patients, especially for the elderly or frail patients. A unique observation in our cohort was the high rate of COVID-19 infection (20%) during treatment and 1 patient died of pneumonia while the lymphoma was stable. This likely reflects the timing of the study (2024–2025), when COVID-19 remained prevalent in China and deserves more attention in clinical practice. Furthermore, whether lymphocyte depletion or exhaustion after glofitamab treatment would influence long-term infectious events requires further observation.

This study has several limitations. The sample size was relatively small, and multivariate analysis was not performed owing to the limited number of outcome events. This may restrict the statistical power to identify associations between potential prognostic factors. In Addition, only two medical centers were involved and may not fully represent the broader Chinese population. The follow-up duration was also relatively short, particularly for assessing long-term efficacy durability and late-onset AEs.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this real-world study demonstrates that glofitamab is highly effective and well tolerated in Chinese patients with R/R DLBCL. Involvement of more than one extranodal site, TP53 mutations and low baseline lymphocytes may indicate poorer responses to glofitamab and worse prognosis. Administering glofitamab in earlier lines of therapy might be more beneficial, although the underlying mechanisms require further investigation.

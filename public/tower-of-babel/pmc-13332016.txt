Physics-informed Koopman learning approach for fixed-time synchronization of stochastic neural dynamics
Authors: Mubasil P, Prakash Mani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study proposes a physics-informed Koopman operator framework for the fixed-time (FXT) synchronization of Hindmarsh-Rose (HR) neurons subject to mixed Gaussian and Poisson jump noise. This paper introduces the Koopman operator to lift the nonlinear neuronal dynamics into a higher dimensional space where the evolution becomes globally linear. Distinct to the data-driven methods which rely on genric basis functions, this paper design a physics-informed lifting dictionary explicitly derived from the governing algebraic nonlinearities of the HR dynamics. Based on the proposed lifted framework, this study designs a non-singular FXT controller which guarantees the convergence within a pre-determined settling time, independent of the initial error states. Further, the communication bandwidth limitations are handled through a dynamic event-triggering mechanism (DETM) ensuring the control actions are executed only when the event-triggering condition is satisfied. The numerical simulations are performed to validate the performance of the proposed theoretical frameworks.Keywords: HR neuron model, Koopman operator, Physics informed lifting function, Fixed-Time synchronizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

In the year 1952, authors Hodgkin and Huxley developed the first differential model to understand the neuronal activities that bridges the mathematics and biology1. The proposed model explains the movement of sodium ( and potassium ( ions across the membrane. Later on, the simplified model comprises of two-dimensional model developed by FitzHugh-Nagumo (FHN) in the year 1961 which can capture the basic neuronal behaviors like periodic spiking, excitability, and refractory period2. The main limitation of FHN model is considerably capturing the bursting behavioral characteristics. Hindmarsh and Rose added one more variable in the study, which is slow adaptation variable, relaxes the limitation of the FHN model3. The common factor among all the models is nonlinear characteristics and mathematically analyzing the nonlinear models are considerably challenging. The conventional methods for analyzing those characteristics are briefly discussed by the authors in4,5. The dynamical investigation is performed for the HR by transforming the model into an equivalent linear representation6–8. Although the eigen value based method is simple and computationally efficient, it’s approximated locally around a small neighborhood of equilibrium point of the system, which limit the accuracy, failing to capture the global nonlinear dynamics. In contrast, fuzzy based method approximates to larger range by using multiple local models, but this technique results in higher computational cost. A promising alternative viewpoint is offered by the Koopman operator theory of dynamical systems, where linear representation of the system can be achievable even for severely nonlinear dynamics through the infinite-dimensional. The theory is named after the mathematician Bernard Koopman, who introduced the operator in 1931, that explains how measurements of Hamiltonian systems change over time9. In 1932, the theory was extended by Bernard Koopman and Von Neumann to encompass systems having a continuous eigenvalue spectrum10. In the recent decades the studies shows tremendous advancements due to the combination of contemporary Koopman theory and a straightforward and efficient numerical realization11–13. The modern Koopman operator theory for dynamical systems discussed by the studies offers a fundamentally different perspective by transforming nonlinear dynamics into an infinite-dimensional linear representation14 and15. This method states that observable functions of nonlinear systems can evolve linearly under the operation of a linear but infinite-dimensional operator, even when state trajectories of those systems evolve nonlinearly. The authors shows the effectiveness of Koopman operator-based control critically depends on the selection of proper lifting functions form the observable dictionary16. While generic polynomial expansions or radial basis functions can be employed, physics-informed lifting functions that exploit domain knowledge about the underlying system structure often yield superior performance with lower-dimensional representations17,18.

Neuronal models may operate in noisy environments where stochastic fluctuations, including Gaussian and Poisson jump noises arise from multiple sources across different temporal and spatial scales. In such environments, continuous control methods consume more communication energy. Under ideal scenarios traditional control methods can achieve stability, however those methods will struggle in the presence of stochastic disturbances which eventually leads the system to slow convergence, persistent oscillations, or even loss of synchronization19,20. To mitigate these limitations, various control strategies have been developed21–23. Among those, FXT control strategy has the advantage of achieving convergence within a predetermined time bound, irrespective of initial conditions. Despite the aforementioned benefits, FXT control requires high communication and computational costs due to frequent updates. Event-triggered control resolves this issue by reducing the unnecessary control updates, it will update only when the prescribed triggering conditions are satisfied. By integrating FXT with event-triggered mechanisms, FXT event-triggered control achieves uniform convergence guaranties while significantly reducing communication and control effort22,24. By ensuring the convergence of the error system, synchronization can be achieved. To theoretically guarantee the convergence without obtaining an explicit analytical solution, Lyapunov stability theory can be employed25. Using Lyapunov stability theory, the FXT event-triggered control framework provides theoretical assurance and practical implementability to achieve rapid synchronization.

A key gap exists in developing the physics informed Koopman lifting method to analyze the HR neuron model by obtaining exact linear representation, without relying on data-driven learning. Conclusion

This study establishes an effective control framework for synchronization of stochastic HR neuron model by integrating physics informed Koopman method with fixed time and DETM. By constructing a lifting function using dictionary of observables directly from the non linearities in the system, the method achieves linearization which overcomes the limitations of local approximations and mitigate the computational cost. The proposed FXT control design achieves synchronization convergence of error model within a pre-determined time bound independent of initial conditions. The DETM integrated with FXT control, which helps to minimize the control updates while maintaining the synchronization error convergence. This study shows higher robustness and and less control energy than classical method under Gaussian and Poisson jump noises. In future this work can be further extended to pre-defined synchronization, also other neuronal models and time delays can also be considered.

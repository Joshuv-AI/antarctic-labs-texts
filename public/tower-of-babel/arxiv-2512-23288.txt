Abstract
. Under nondegeneracy assumptions on the diffusion coefficients, we establish the derivative formulae of Bismut-Elworthy-Li’s type for forward-backward stochastic differential equations with respect to Poisson random measure using the lent particle method created by Bouleau and Denis, which is not given before. Applying this formula, the existence and uniqueness of a solution of nonlocal quasi-linear integral partial differential equations, which are differentiable with respect to the space variable, are obtained, even if the initial datum and coefficients of this equation are not.

60H10, 60H07

1.
Introduction

In the present paper, we are concerned with the problem of derivative formulae of Bismut-Elworthy-Li’s type for the following forward-backward stochastic differential equations (SDEs in short) with respect to Poisson random measure:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X(\tau,t,x)=x+\int_{t}^{\tau}b(s,X(s,t,x))ds\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\int_{t}^{\tau}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}\sigma(s,X(s-,t,x))u\widetilde{N}(ds,du),\tau\in[t,T],\\
X(\tau,t,x)=x,\tau\in[0,t],\end{cases}

(1)

where
b:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\sigma:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
,

{\mathcal{O}}=\{u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l};|u|\leqslant 1\}\setminus\{0\}
,
\widetilde{N}
is the compensated process of some Poisson point process
N
on
({\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{O}}))
with intensity
\nu
, and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&Y(\tau,t,x)+\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}Z(s-,t,x,u)\widetilde{N}(ds,du)\\
=&-\int_{\tau}^{T}\psi(s,X(s,t,x),Y(s,t,x),\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}Z(s,t,x,u)l(u)\nu(du))ds+\phi(X(T,t,x)),\end{split}

(2)

where
\tau\in[0,T]
,
\psi:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
are given functions, and
l:{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a Borel function with growth condition
|l(u)|\leqslant C(1\wedge|u|)
,
u\in{\mathcal{O}}
.

A derivative formula for Wiener functionals was first established by Bismut in
[
1
]
using Malliavin calculus on Wiener space. This result was subsequently extended by Elworthy and Li in their foundational work
[
10
]
, where they systematically derived formulae for the derivatives of heat semigroups—this contribution, together with Bismut’s earlier result, laid the core theoretical basis for what is now known as the Bismut-Elworthy-Li formulae. Unlike the Wiener space, there are several versions of Malliavin calculus on Poisson space which are not equivalent. In the recent years, quite a few papers appeared concerning the Bismut-Elworthy-Li formulae for SDEs with jumps, using various approaches, e.g., SDEs driven by general Poisson jumps processes by using stochastic diffeomorphism flows and Girsanov’s transformation (
[
19
]
), SDEs driven by subordinated Brownian motion by using conditional Malliavin calculus (
[
22
,
21
]
), SDEs driven by
\alpha
-stable-like noise by using Bismut’s approach(
[
20
]
).

In a series of works, Bouleau and Denis (see
[
4
]
and references therein) presented systematically a new method called the lent particle one which may be considered as a differential calculus on Poisson space completely comparable to the Malliavin differential calculus on the Wiener space. The main feature of this method is its simplicity and convenience when applied to Poisson functionals, especially to solutions of stochastic differential equations driven by Lévy processes. Using this method, the Bismut-Elworthy-Li formulae for SDEs with jumps is also established in our recent paper
[
17
]
which generalizes and improves some previous works in this respect.

Although some authors have studied the Bismut-Elworthy-Li formulae for SDEs with jumps, no one has yet discussed the same problem for forward-backward SDEs with jumps. For the cases of forward-backward SDEs without jumps, under nondegeneracy assumptions on the diffusion coefficient (that may be nonconstant), Fuhrman and Tessitore
[
11
]
have proved an analogue of the Bismut-Elworthy-Li formulae. After that, Masiero
[
16
]
has also obtained a Bismut-Elworthy-Li formula for forward-backward SDEs in a Markovian framework when the generator has quadratic growth with respect to
Z
. In the present paper, using the lent particle method, we establish a derivative formula of Bismut-Elworthy-Li’s type for forward-backward SDEs with respect to Poisson random measure under the nondegeneracy assumption on the diffusion coefficients, which has not been given so far to our knowledge, and we apply this formula to the study of nonlocal quasi-linear integral partial differential equations (PDEs in short).

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we recall the lent particle method which is the basic tool in our article. In Section 3, some notations and preliminary results on forward-backward SDEs are introduced. In Section 4, we obtain a derivative formula of Bismut-Elworthy-Li’s type for forward-backward SDEs with jumps. Building upon this formula, in Section 5, we establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions to nonlocal quasi-linear integral-PDEs that are differentiable with respect to the space variable, regardless of the potential non-differentiability of the initial conditions or coefficients of the equation.

In the present paper, let
C_{p}
be a positive constant only depending on some parameter
p
, whose value may change from line to line. When we don’t want to emphasize this dependence we just use
C
instead.

Forward-Backward System

4.
Bismut-Elworthy-Li Formulae

We will make use of the following assumptions.

(L)

(a)

The Lévy measure
\nu
satisfies
\nu({\mathcal{O}})=+\infty
.

(b)

k\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{1}({\mathcal{O}};{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\setminus\{0\})
, and there exists a positive constant
C_{B}
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla\log k(u)|\leqslant C_{B}|u|^{-1},\quad u\in{\mathcal{O}}.

(c)

there exists
\beta\in(0,2)
such that

(i)

For any
p\geqslant 2
, there exists a positive constant
C_{O}
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\{|u|\leqslant\varepsilon\}}|u|^{p}\nu(du)\leqslant C_{O}\varepsilon^{p-\beta};

(ii)

For any
p\geqslant 1
, there exists a positive constant
C_{I}
such that for any
\tau\in[t,T]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[(\int_{t}^{\tau}\int_{\{|u|\leqslant\varepsilon\}}|u|^{3}N(ds,du))^{-p}]\leqslant C_{I}(((\tau-t)\varepsilon^{3-\beta})^{-p}+(\tau-t)^{-\frac{3p}{\beta}}).

Let us give some comments on theses assumptions.
In
[
19
,
20
]
, the following growth condition near the origin on the intensity measure is used:

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{\beta-2}\int_{\{|u|<\varepsilon\}}|u|^{2}\nu(du)>0,~\beta\in(0,2).

(4)

It can be seen from
[
20
, Lemma 2.5, Lemma 2.6]
that the assumption (
L
-c) is satisfied under this growth condition. It is easy to see that if
\nu(du)/du=k(u)=a(u)|u|^{-d-\beta}
with

\displaystyle a(u)=a(-u),\quad 0<a_{0}\leqslant a(u)\leqslant a_{1},\quad|\nabla a(u)|\leqslant a_{2},

then the growth condition (
4
) and the assumption (
L
-a,b) are satisfied. The Lévy processes corresponding to this
\nu
is called
\beta
-stable-like process as in
[
6
]
.

However, Proposition 4.11 in
[
17
]
reveals that the growth condition (
4
) is stronger than the following one: there exists
\beta\in(0,2)
such that

\displaystyle(0,\infty)\ni\kappa=\limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{\beta/3}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}(1-\exp\{-\lambda|u|^{3}\})\nu(du).

The following proposition, whose detailed proof is included in the Appendix B, shows that the assumption (
L
-c) still holds under this relatively weak condition.

Proposition 4.1
.

If there exists
\beta\in(0,2)
such that

\displaystyle(0,\infty)\ni\kappa=\limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{\beta/3}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}(1-\exp\{-\lambda|u|^{3}\})\nu(du),

(5)

then

(1)

for any
p\geqslant 2
, there exists a positive constant
C_{O}
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\{|u|\leqslant\varepsilon\}}|u|^{p}\nu(du)\leqslant C_{O}\varepsilon^{p-\beta};

(2)

for any
p\geqslant 1
, there exists a positive constant
C_{I}
such that for any
\tau\in[t,T]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[(\int_{t}^{\tau}\int_{\{|u|\leqslant\varepsilon\}}|u|^{3}N(ds,du))^{-p}]\leqslant C_{I}(((\tau-t)\varepsilon^{3-\beta})^{-p}+(\tau-t)^{-\frac{3p}{\beta}}).

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and let
\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)
be a smooth real function with

\displaystyle\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)=\begin{cases}|u|^{3},&|u|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{3},\\
0,&|u|>\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle|\nabla\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)|\leqslant C|u|^{2},

where
C
is independence of
\varepsilon
. Denote by
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathcal{O}})
the set of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
-functions defined on
{\mathcal{O}}
and with compact support and by
H
the subspace consisting of
f\in L^{2}(\nu)\cap L^{1}(\nu)
such that
f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathcal{O}})
. Then the bilinear form

\displaystyle e_{\varepsilon}(\phi,\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\partial_{i}\phi(u)\partial_{i}\psi(u)k(u)du,\quad\forall\phi,\psi\in H

is closable and its closure, which will be denoted by
(\mathbf{d}_{\varepsilon},e_{\varepsilon})
, is a local symmetric Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(kdu)
which admits a carré du champ operator
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
given by

\displaystyle\quad\gamma_{\varepsilon}[\phi,\psi]=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{i}\psi,\quad\forall\phi,\psi\in\mathbf{d}_{\varepsilon}.

Since

\displaystyle\gamma_{\varepsilon}[\phi](u)=\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\sum_{i=1}^{l}(\partial_{i}\phi(u))^{2},\quad\forall\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathcal{O}}),

and the identity map
j_{\varepsilon}
belongs to
\mathbf{d}_{\varepsilon}
, we have
\gamma_{\varepsilon}[j_{\varepsilon},j_{\varepsilon}^{*}](u)=\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)I
. Therefore,

\displaystyle j_{\varepsilon}^{\flat}(u,r)=\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)\xi(r).

Here

\displaystyle\xi=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\xi_{1}(r)\\
\xi_{2}(r)\\
\vdots\\
\xi_{l}(r)\end{array}\right),

where
\xi_{i}\in L^{2}(\rho)
,
i=1,2,\cdots,l
, satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{R}\xi_{i}(r)\rho(dr)=0

and

\displaystyle\int_{R}\xi_{i}(r)\xi_{j}(r)\rho(dr)=\delta_{ij}

for all
i,j=1,\cdots,l
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\phi^{\flat}(u,r)=\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\partial_{i}\phi(u)\xi_{i}(r).

Here and in the sequel, the operators
\flat
,
\delta_{\flat}
,
\sharp
and
\delta_{\sharp}
corresponding to

depend of course on
\varepsilon
, but for notational simplicity, we will omit this
\varepsilon
as a subscript. For a

which is
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
in the first variable,

\displaystyle[\delta_{\flat}U](u)=-\int_{R}[\theta_{\varepsilon}(u,r)U(u,r)+\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\partial_{i}U(u,r)\xi_{i}(r)]\rho(dr),

(7)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\theta_{\varepsilon}(u,r)&=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\partial_{i}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)k(u))}{k(u)}\xi_{i}(r)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{l}(\partial_{i}\log k(u)\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)+\frac{1}{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}(u)\partial_{i}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u))\xi_{i}(r).\end{split}

(8)

In fact, according to the integration by parts formula and Fubini theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}\int_{R}U(u,r)\phi^{\flat}(u,r)\rho(dr)\nu(du)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{R}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}U(u,r)\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\partial_{i}\phi(u)\xi_{i}(r)k(u)du\rho(dr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{R}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\xi_{i}(r)(\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}\partial_{i}\phi(u)U(u,r)\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)k(u)du)\rho(dr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{R}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\xi_{i}(r)(\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}\phi(u)\partial_{i}(U(u,r)\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)k(u))du)\rho(dr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{R}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\xi_{i}(r)(\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}\phi(u)\partial_{i}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)k(u))U(u,r)du)\rho(dr)

\displaystyle+\int_{R}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\xi_{i}(r)(\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}\phi(u)\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)k(u)\partial_{i}U(u,r)du)\rho(dr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\int_{R}(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\partial_{i}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)k(u))}{k(u)}\xi_{i}(r)

\displaystyle+\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(u)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\partial_{i}U(u,r)\xi_{i}(r))\rho(dr))\phi(u)\nu(du),

then the desired result can be obtained through the definition of
[\delta_{\flat}U](u)
.

5.
Nonlocal Quasi-Linear Integral-PDEs

5.1.
Notations and Notions

We introduce the semigroup
\{P_{t,\tau};0\leqslant t\leqslant\tau\leqslant T\}
, acting on the space of Borel functions
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
having polynomial growth, defined by:

\displaystyle P_{t,\tau}[\phi](x):={\mathbb{E}}[\phi(X(\tau,t,x))],\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

By
{\mathcal{L}}_{t}
we denote the formal generator of
P_{t,\tau}
, namely:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{t}[\phi](x):=b(t,x)\cdot\nabla\phi(x)+p.v.\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\phi(x+\sigma(t,x)u)-\phi(x))\nu(du),

where p.v. stands for the Cauchy principle value.

We consider the following nonlocal quasi-linear integral-PDEs: for any
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial v(t,x)}{\partial t}+{\mathcal{L}}_{t}[v(t,\cdot)](x)\\
\quad\quad\quad=\psi(t,x,v(t,x),\int_{\mathcal{O}}(v(t,x+\sigma(t,x)u)-v(t,x))l(u)\nu(du)),\\
v(T,x)=\phi(x).\end{cases}

(17)

Now we give the definition of the mild solution to Eq. (
17
).

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a continuous function
v:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a mild solution of Eq. (
17
) if the following conditions hold:

(1)

v\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0,1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}})
;

(2)

for all
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle|v(t,x)|\leqslant C(1+|x|)^{C},\quad|\nabla_{x}v(t,x)|\leqslant Cf(t)(1+|x|)^{C},

for some constant
C>0
, and some real function
f
satisfying
\int_{0}^{T}f(t)dt<\infty
;

(3)

for any
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle v(t,x)=-\int_{t}^{T}P_{t,\tau}[\psi(\tau,\cdot,v(\tau,\cdot),\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}(v(\tau,\cdot+\sigma(\tau,\cdot)u)-v(\tau,\cdot))l(u)du)](x)d\tau+P_{t,T}[\phi](x).

5.2.
Joint Quadratic Variation

Since we are considering mild solutions here, in order to prove the uniqueness of the solution to Eq. (
17

[
18
]
, and it is shown in
[

that the standard definition of joint quadratic variation coincides with the generalized joint quadratic variation defined below.

Definition 5.2

Given a couple of
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued càdlàg processes

,
t\geqslant 0
, we define their joint quadratic variation on

\displaystyle{\langle}X(t),Y(t){\rangle}_{[0,T]}:={\mathbb{P}}-\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}(X((t+\epsilon)\wedge T)-X(t))(Y((t+\epsilon)\wedge T)-Y(t))dt,

where

denotes the limit to be taken in probability.

The following lemma can be found in
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
v:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is locally Lipschitz with respect to the second variable and with at most polynomial growth, i.e., there exist
C>0
and

and

\displaystyle|v(t,x)-v(t,y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C|x-y|(1+|x|+|y|)^{m},

\displaystyle|v(t,0,0)|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C.

and

\displaystyle\{v(s,X(s,t,x));s\in[t,T]\}

\displaystyle J(s)=\int_{t}^{s}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}u\widetilde{N}(ds,du)

\displaystyle{\langle}v(\cdot,X(\cdot,t,x)),J(\cdot){\rangle}_{[t,T]}

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}(v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)+\sigma(\tau,X(\tau,t,x))u)-v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)))uN(d\tau,du).

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}U(\tau,u)uN(d\tau,du).

\displaystyle(v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)+\sigma(\tau,X(\tau,t,x))u)-v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)))=U(\tau,u).

\displaystyle v(s,X(s,t,x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}\psi(\tau,X(\tau,t,x),v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)),

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}(v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)+\sigma(\tau,X(\tau,t,x))u)-v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)))l(u)\nu(du))d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}(v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)+\sigma(\tau,X(\tau,t,x))u)-v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)))\widetilde{N}(d\tau,du)+v(t,x).

\displaystyle v(s,X(s,t,x))+\int_{s}^{T}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}(v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)+\sigma(\tau,X(\tau,t,x))u)-v(\tau,X(\tau,t,x)))\widetilde{N}(d\tau,du)

Comparing with Eq. (

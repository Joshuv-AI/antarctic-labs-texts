A Study of Fine Selmer Groups Over Function Fields via Greenberg Neighbourhoods
Sohan Ghosh

Abstract
. In [ Gre73 ] , Greenberg examined the local behavior of Iwasawa invariants as functions on the set ℰ ⁡ ( F ) \mathcal{E}(F) , the set of all ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of a number field F F . Kleine [ Kle21 ] later extended these ideas to explore the variation of Iwasawa invariants in the context of Selmer groups of elliptic curves across different ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of F F . Let K K be a global function field of characteristic p p . In this article, we investigate the relation between Iwasawa invariants of fine Selmer groups of an elliptic curve over K K across various ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of K K , utilizing Kleine’s techniques. Furthermore, we connect this analysis to an analogue of Conjecture (Conjecture A) . by Coates and Sujatha [ CS05 ] for different ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of the function field K K .

1.
Introduction

Let us fix a prime
p
throughout. Iwasawa theory originated from the work of K. Iwasawa, who studied the growth of the
p
-primary parts of class groups in towers of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions. He established the following celebrated theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(Iwasawa)
.

[
Iwa59
]

Let
F
be a number field and
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
. For each
n\geqslant 0
, let
p^{e_{n}}
denote the exact power of
p
that divides
\#Cl(F_{n})
. Then there exist invariants
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
, depending on
p
but independent of
n
, such that
e_{n}=\lambda n+\mu p^{n}+\nu
for
n\gg 0
.

For the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
of
F
, Iwasawa further conjectured that
\mu=0
. Ferrero and Washington
[
FW79
]
later proved this conjecture for abelian number fields, but it remains open for general number fields.

In 2005, Coates and Sujatha
[
CS05
]
found a surprising connection between Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
conjecture and the structure of fine Selmer groups of elliptic curves. More precisely, they proved the following important theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

[
CS05
, Theorem 3.4]
Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve, and let
p
be an odd prime such that
F(E_{p^{\infty}})/F
is a pro-
p
extension. Then
R(E/F_{\text{cyc}})^{\vee}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module if and only if Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
conjecture holds for
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
.

Motivated by this result, Coates and Sujatha formulated the following conjecture:

Conjecture

(Conjecture A)
.

For all elliptic curves
E
over
F
,
R(E/F_{\text{cyc}})^{\vee}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module.

Following
Conjecture

(Conjecture A)
.
, the fine Selmer groups over cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of a number field have been studied by various authors including
[
LKM16
,
JS11
,
She18
,
Wut07
]
etc.

A natural question is how the Iwasawa invariants vary across different
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of
F
. Greenberg addressed this by introducing a topology on
\mathcal{E}(F)
, the set of all
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of
F
, allowing for a comparative analysis of Iwasawa invariants across distinct
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions. Using Fukuda modules, Kleine
[
Kle17
]
examined the local behavior of Iwasawa invariants across
\mathcal{E}(F)
. He later generalized these ideas to Selmer groups associated with elliptic curves having good-ordinary reduction at primes above
p

[
Kle21
]
.

In
[
GJS
]
, the authors initiated the study of fine Selmer groups over function fields of characteristic
p
. These fine Selmer groups are subgroup of the classical Selmer groups. In this article, we investigate the variation of Iwasawa invariants of fine Selmer groups over certain neighborhoods of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of a function field
K
, which is defined over a finite field of characteristic
p
. Our main results are Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
3.6
. We are also working on generalizing these ideas to Selmer groups in both equal and unequal characteristic case over function fields as a part of our future project.

The structure of the article is as follows: In §
2
, we review key definitions and preliminaries necessary for the remainder of the article. In §
3
, we present the proofs of our main results. Finally, in §
4
, we conclude with a discussion of an example.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
C_{K}
be a curve over the finite field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p
, with
K
as its function field. Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve. Consider
U
to be a dense open subset of
C_{K}
such that
E/K
has good reduction at every place in
U
. Denote by
\Sigma_{K}
the set of all places of
K
, and let
S
represent the set of places of
K
outside
U
.

Let
L
be a finite extension of
K
inside
K_{S}
, the maximal algebraic extension of
K
unramified outside
S
. Let
v
be any prime of
K
and
w
denote a prime of
L
. Define

(1)

\displaystyle J_{v}^{1}(E/L):=\underset{w\mid v}{\bigoplus}\frac{H^{1}_{\operatorname{fl}}(L_{w},E_{p^{\infty}})}{im(\kappa_{w})}\ \text{and }K_{v}^{1}(E/L):=\underset{w\mid v}{\bigoplus}H^{1}_{\operatorname{fl}}(L_{w},E_{p^{\infty}}).

Here
H^{i}_{\operatorname{fl}}(-,-)
denote the flat cohomology
[
Mil86
, Chapters II, III]
and
\kappa_{w}:E(L_{w})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hookrightarrow H^{1}_{\operatorname{fl}}(L_{w},E_{p^{\infty}})
is induced by the Kummer map. The following definition of the Selmer group is recalled from
[
KT03
]
:

Definition 2.1
.

[
KT03
, Prop. 2.4]

With
\Sigma_{K},S\text{ and }K\subset L\subset K_{S}
as above, define

(2)

S(E/L):=\ker\big(H^{1}_{\operatorname{fl}}(L,{E}_{p^{\infty}})\longrightarrow\underset{v\in\Sigma_{K}}{\bigoplus}J_{v}^{1}(E/L)\big).

We define the
S
-fine Selmer group as:

(3)

\displaystyle R^{S}(E/L):={}\ker\left(H^{1}_{\operatorname{fl}}(L,E_{p^{\infty}})\longrightarrow\underset{v\in S}{\bigoplus}K_{v}^{1}(E/L)\underset{v\in\Sigma_{K}\setminus S}{\bigoplus}J_{v}^{1}(E/L)\right)

\displaystyle\cong\ker\left(S(E/L)\longrightarrow\underset{w\mid v,v\in S}{\bigoplus}E(L_{w})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right).

For an infinite algebraic extension
\mathcal{L}
of
K
, these definitions extend as usual by taking the inductive limit over finite subextensions of
\mathcal{L}
over
K
.

2.1.
Examples of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of function field

Let
\mathbb{F}^{(p)}
be the unique subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}
, the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}
, such that
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}^{(p)}/\mathbb{F})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Set
K_{\infty}:=K\mathbb{F}^{(p)}
. Note that
K_{\infty}/K
is unramified everywhere. This
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}
is referred to as in the literature as the "arithmetic"
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.

Unlike the number fields, in case of function fields, we can construct infinitely many
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-extensions of
K
for any
d\geqslant 1
.

We will now explore a few examples of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-extensions constructed from Carlitz modules. These
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-extensions are constructed in the following manner:

Let
K=\mathbb{F}(t)
and
P(t)=a_{n}t^{n}+\cdots a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}[t]
. We define the Carlitz polynomial
[P(t)](X)
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}[t]
recursively as follows:

[1](X)=X
,

[t](X)=X^{p}+tX
,

[t^{n}](X)=[t]([t^{n-1}](X))
and

[a_{n}t^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}t+a_{0}](X)=a_{n}[t^{n}](X)+\cdots+a_{1}[t](X)+a_{0}(X).

Consider a field extension
F
of
K
. Then
F
can be thought of as a
\mathbb{F}[t]
-module, where the action of
\mathbb{F}[t]
is given by the Carlitz polynomials.
Choose a prime
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathbb{F}[t]
. For
n>0
, let

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{P}^{n}}:=\{\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{F}(t)}|[\mathfrak{P}^{n}](\lambda)=0\}.

Here
K(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{P}^{n}})/K
is Galois with
\mathrm{Gal}(K(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{P}^{n}})/K)\cong(\mathbb{F}[t]/\mathfrak{P}^{n})^{\times}
. Put
\widetilde{K}:=\underset{n\geqslant 1}{\bigcup}K(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{P}^{n}})
, then
\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/K)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathbb{N}}\times(\mathbb{F}[t]/\mathfrak{P})^{\times}
.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-extensions obtained from
\widetilde{K}
, for
d\geqslant 1
are ramified only at the prime
\mathfrak{P}
and they are totally ramified at that prime
[
Ros02
, Proposition 12.7]
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions, thus obtained, are examples of “geometric"
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions.

2.2.
Greenberg topology

For a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}/K
, we define
\mathcal{P}(K_{\infty})
as the set of primes in
K
, ramified in
K_{\infty}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}(K)
be the set of all
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of
K
, which are finitely ramified. Define

\mathcal{U}(K_{\infty},n):=\{K_{\infty}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}(K)|[K_{\infty}\cap K_{\infty}^{\prime}:K]\geqslant p^{n},\mathcal{P}(K_{\infty}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{P}(K_{\infty})\},

for some
n\geqslant 0
and
K_{\infty}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}(K)
. We define a topology on
\mathcal{E}(K)
generated by these sets.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
K
. Define the set

S_{1}:=\{v\in\Sigma_{K}\ |\ v\textrm{ ramifies in }K_{\infty}\}\bigcup\{v\in\Sigma_{K}\ |\ E\textrm{ has bad reduction at }v\}.

Note that
S_{1}
is a finite set. Let
S_{2}
be another finite set of primes of
K
, where
S_{1}\subseteq S_{2}
, and for any prime
v\in S_{2}\setminus S_{1}
, the prime
v
splits completely in
K_{\infty}
.

We consider a refinement of the above topology: for
{K}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}(K)
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{V}(E,K_{\infty},m)\subset\mathcal{U}(K_{\infty},m)
denote the subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions
\widetilde{K}_{\infty}
of
K
in
\mathcal{U}(K_{\infty},m)
such that each prime of
S_{2}\setminus S_{1}
(that is totally
split in
K_{\infty}
) splits completely in
\widetilde{K}_{\infty}
. We consider the topology on
\mathcal{E}(K)
, generated by the sets
\mathcal{V}(E,K_{\infty},m)
, i.e, the smallest topology on
\mathcal{E}(K)
generated by these sets. This is a finer topology on
\mathcal{E}(K)
than the topology generated by the sets

\{\mathcal{U}(K_{\infty},m),m\geqslant 0\}
. Indeed it is easy to see that if
K_{\infty}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}(K_{\infty},m)
, then
L_{\infty}\in\mathcal{V}(E,K_{\infty}^{\prime},m+1)\subset\mathcal{U}(K_{\infty},m)
. Recall, that a similar type of refined neighbourhoods were also considered in
[
Kle21
]
.

2.3.
Fukuda Modules

In
[
Kle17
]
, Kleine introduced a special family of Iwasawa modules
X=\varprojlim X_{n}
. These modules were designed to derive information about the projective limit by utilizing data from a sufficiently large number of layers
X_{n}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
1\leq d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
R
be the ring of formal power series over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in
d
variables with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(p,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
. Let
X=\varprojlim X_{n}
denote the projective limit of
R
-modules
X_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, each assumed to be an abelian pro-
p
group. Further assume that
X
is compact as an
R
-module with respect to the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic topology. Let
Y_{n}\subset X
denote the kernel of the projection map
X\to X_{n}
for each
n
. Let
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
be
p
-powers. Then
X
is called a Fukuda
R
-module with parameters
(C_{1},C_{2},C_{3})
if there exists a family of compact
R
-submodules
(Z_{n})_{n}
of
X
such that

|\operatorname{coker}(\operatorname{pr}_{n})|\leq C_{1},Z_{n+1}\subset\mathfrak{m}\cdot Z_{n},[Y_{n}:(Y_{n}\cap Z_{n})]\leq C_{2},

and

[Z_{n}:(Y_{n}\cap Z_{n})]\leq C_{3}

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Consider a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-extension
K_{\infty}/K
, where
d\geqslant 1
. Write
K_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n}K_{n}
with
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{n}/K)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{d}
. Let
X=\varprojlim X_{n}
, where
X_{n}
is the
p
-primary subgroup of the ideal class group of
K_{n}
. Then, under appropriate assumptions on the ramification of primes in
K_{\infty}/K
, one can show that
X
is a Fukuda-
R
-module with parameters
(1,1,1)
(see
[
Kle21
, §3]
), where
R\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)]]
.

Before moving further, we recall the following result from
[
BL09
]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

[
BL09
, Lemma 4.1]

Let
\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
and
B
a finite
p
-primary
\Gamma
-module. Then

|H^{1}(\Gamma,B)|\leqslant|B|^{d}\quad\text{and}\quad|H^{2}(\Gamma,B)|\leqslant|B|^{\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}.

3.
Main Results

Let
K
be a function field over the finite field of characteristic

K_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of

\Gamma:=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)

Recall the definition of
S_{1}
as a finite set of primes of
K

K_{\infty}
and those where

has bad reduction. Similarly, recall that
S_{2}
is another finite set of primes of
K

S_{1}\subseteq S_{2}

.

We first prove a control theorem from

, where the set

or

.

Let

be a non-isotrivial elliptic curve (i.e;

) defined over
K
. Let
S
be either
S_{1}
or

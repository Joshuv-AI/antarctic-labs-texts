Tate-valued Characteristic Classes
Shachar Carmeli, Kiran Luecke

Abstract
We define a projective variant of classical complex orientation theory. Using this, we construct a map of spectra which lifts the total Chern class, providing an alternative answer to an old question of Segal [ 17 ] , previously answered by Lawson et al [ 8 ] . We also lift and generalize the “sharp” construction of Ando-French-Ganter [ 3 ] to an operation on arbitrary 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -complex orientations, thereby providing a rich source of new 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -orientations for commutative ring spectra. In particular we give an 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -lift of the Jacobi orientation, a generalization of the much-studied two variable elliptic genus. Finally, we construct some new complex orientations of periodic ring spectra as requested in [ 12 ] .

Introduction

1.1
Background and main results

Let
\omega:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R
be a complex oriented ring spectrum. A common method for studying this complex orientation is as follows. First, one finds a ring map
f:R\rightarrow R^{\prime}
, together with a simpler orientation
\omega^{\prime}:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R^{\prime}
. Then
R^{\prime}
has two complex orientations,
f_{*}\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
, and by comparing these one gains information about
\omega
. Perhaps the most commonly deployed instance of this produces the
Hirzebruch series
: one takes
f
to be the rationalization map
f:R\rightarrow R\otimes\mathbb{Q}
and
\omega^{\prime}
the orientation
\omega_{\text{add}}:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow R\otimes\mathbb{Q}
whose formal group law is the additive one. Each of these orientations of
R\otimes\mathbb{Q}
produces an element in
R_{*}[[x]]\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, and their ratio is precisely the classical Hirzebruch series of
\omega
. By construction this series is an element in
(R_{*}[[x]]\otimes\mathbb{Q})^{\times}
, so via the splitting principle it produces a stable exponential characteristic class, i.e. a map of
H
-spaces
\mathrm{BU}\rightarrow GL_{1}(R\otimes\mathbb{Q})
, which we will call the
Hirzebruch characteristic class
.

In this paper we will carry out a similar program, but replacing the map
R\rightarrow R\otimes\mathbb{Q}
with the much less destructive Tate construction
R\rightarrow R^{t\mathbb{T}}
, where
\mathbb{T}\subset\mathbb{C}
is the topological group of unit complex numbers. In particular, we will define a variant of complex orientation theory (cf.
Definition

3.10
) in which
R^{t\mathbb{T}}
has a canonical orientation
\omega_{\mathrm{eul}}
which plays the role of
\omega_{\text{add}}
in the method described above. We will call the resulting series (resp. exponential characteristic class) the
Euler-Tate
series (resp. class). Crucially, our construction will be carried out at the level of symmetric monoidal categories, and thus automatically produces
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-
lifts
of the characteristic classes in question, as opposed to mere maps of
H
-spaces. Our first main result is the following.

Theorem A
.

(
4.4
,
4.5
)
Let
\omega:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R
be an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring map, with associated formal group law
F
, and orientation parameter
t\in R^{2}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
. Note that
\pi_{*}R^{t\mathbb{T}}\simeq R_{*}((t))
. Then there is an associated “Euler-Tate” characteristic class

c^{\mathfrak{et}}:\mathrm{bu}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{1}(R^{t\mathbb{T}})

whose characteristic series is
t^{-1}(x+_{F}t)
. In particular, when
R=\mathbb{Z}
the underlying map of spaces is the total Chern class with respect to
t^{-1}
.

This answers a question of Graeme Segal from 1975
[
17
]
, which asks if such an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-lift of the total Chern class exists. The question was answered in the affirmative by Lawson et al
[
8
]
, using their theory of ‘algebraic cycles’ to construct infinite loop spaces out of geometric objects.

Recall that

is defined to be the Thom spectrum of the
j

n
-th connective cover of
\mathrm{ku}
. For example,

,
\mathrm{MU}\langle 2\rangle=\mathrm{MU}
, and
\mathrm{MU}\langle 4\rangle=\mathrm{MSU}
. The question of when an
\mathrm{MU}\langle n\rangle
orientation, considered as a homotopy ring map

, admits an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-lift is a difficult problem of great interest (
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
12
]
,
[

). The number of known examples of

orientations is quite small. Our next result gives a big supply of them: a

of the Ando-French-Ganter construction of
[

. Their construction turns homotopy
\mathrm{MU}\langle 2n\rangle
-orientations of an even periodic ring spectrum

\mathrm{MU}\langle 2n-2\rangle
-orientations of

, when

. Our construction produces the following; we refer the reader to
Section

Theorem B
.

(

Let
\omega:\mathrm{MU}\langle 2n\rangle\rightarrow R

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}

Our third main result is an immediate corollary, gotten by specializing to the case

Recollection of complex orientations and characteristic classes

In this section we recall the basics of complex orientation theory, mainly to fix notation and conventions.

2.1
Complex orientations

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a homotopy ring spectrum. A
complex orientation
of
R
is one of the following equivalent pieces of data:

(1)

a homotopy ring map
\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R

(2)

a factorization of the unit
\mathbb{S}\rightarrow R
through the bottom cell
\mathbb{S}\rightarrow\Sigma^{\infty-2}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}

(3)

a class
t\in R^{2}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
which restricts to
\Sigma^{2}1\in R^{2}S^{2}
along
S^{2}\simeq\mathbb{CP}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
and induces an isomorphism
\pi_{-*}R[[t]]\rightarrow R^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
.

(4)

a functorial and additive
1
1

1

That is,
\theta_{V}\theta_{W}=\theta_{V\oplus W}
.
assignment of “Thom” classes
\theta_{V}\in R^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{V})
for every complex vector bundle
V\rightarrow X
such that multiplication by
\theta_{V}
induces an isomorphism
R^{*}X\simeq R^{*+n}(\mathbb{S}^{V})
, where
\mathbb{S}^{V}
denotes the Thom space of
V
.

We shall refer to the class
t
from Item 3 as the
parameter
of the complex orientation, and to the isomorphisms of Item 4 as the
Thom isomorphism
associated with the orientation.
Note that by Item 3 and the Künneth formula,
R^{*}(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{CP}^{\infty})\simeq\pi_{-*}R[[x,y]]
where
x
and
y
are the pullbacks of
t
along the two projections. Let
\mu:\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
be the multiplication map, which classifies the tensor product of line bundles. The
formal group law

F_{R}(x,y)
is defined to be the power series associated to the element
\mu^{*}t
. When
R
is clear from the context, we denote it by
F(x,y)
.

Remark 2.2
.

The majority of this paper is concerned with the much more structured notion of an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
complex orientation. By definition these are maps
\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R
of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring spectra. This is of course only defined when
R
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring spectrum. Given item 1, there is an evident forgetful map from
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
complex orientations to complex orientations.

Let
R
be a complex oriented homotopy ring spectrum, and let
V\rightarrow X
be a complex vector bundle. Consider the associated projective bundle
\mathbb{P}(V)
, together with the map
\phi\colon\mathbb{P}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
classifying the tautological bundle on it. More precisely, if
L\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
is the tautological line bundle, then
\phi
is the map such that
\phi^{*}L
is the canonical one-dimensional sub-bundle of
p^{*}V
, where
p\colon\mathbb{P}(V)\rightarrow X
is the projection.

The class
t\in R^{2}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
in Item 3 of
Definition

2.1
pulls back to a class
x=\phi^{*}t\in R^{2}\mathbb{P}(V)
which produces (via e.g. the Leray-Hirsch theorem) an isomorphism

of
R^{*}X
-modules

R^{*}\mathbb{P}(V)\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}R^{*}(X)\cdot x^{k}.

However,
x^{n}
need not be zero, and its failure to be so is measured by characteristic classes.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
R
be a complex oriented homotopy ring spectrum, let
V\rightarrow X
a vector bundle of dimension
n
, and let
x\in R^{2}\mathbb{P}(V)
be defined as above. Define the
Chern classes

c_{i}(V)\in R^{2i}(X)

by declaring
c_{0}(V)=1
, and then for
i\geq 1
, defining
c_{i}(V)
to be the unique elements such that the equation

0=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}c_{i}(V)x^{n-i}

holds in
R^{*}\mathbb{P}(V)\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}R^{*}(X)\cdot x^{k}.

For a parameter
z
of cohomological degree -2, define the
total Chern class with respect to
z
,
c(V,z)\in R^{*}(X)[[z]]
to be the power series

c(V,z):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}(V)z^{i}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
R
be a complex oriented homotopy ring spectrum and
V\rightarrow X
a vector bundle of rank
n
. Define the
Euler class

e(V)\in R^{n}(X)

to be the pullback of
\theta_{V}\in R^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{V})
(Item 3
Definition

2.1
) along the zero section
a_{V}\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{V}
. It is a standard fact that this agrees with the
n
-th Chern class
c_{n}(V)
of
Definition

2.3
.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that when
V\rightarrow X
is a line bundle, classified by a map
\xi_{V}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
, then

e(V)=c_{1}(V)=\xi_{V}^{*}t\in R^{2}(X),

where
t
is as in
Definition

2.1
.

2.2
Characteristic series and characteristic classes

Using the theory of Chern classes, one can classify all “exponential” characteristic classes valued in a complex oriented ring spectrum.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
R
be a homotopy ring spectrum. An
R
-valued
exponential characteristic class
is a map of
H
-spaces

c\colon\mathrm{BU}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{1}(R).

If
R
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring spectrum, an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
exponential characteristic class
is a map of spectra

c\colon\mathrm{bu}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{1}(R).

Thus, an exponential characteristic class assigns to every (rank zero, virtual) vector bundle
V\rightarrow X
a class
c(V)\in R^{0}(X)^{\times}
, natural in maps of spaces and satisfying
c(V\oplus U)=c(V)c(U)
. We extend it to virtual vector bundles of non-zero rank by the convention that
c(\mathbb{C}^{n})=1
in
R^{0}(\text{pt})
.

Assume that
R
is complex oriented, with corresponding parameter
t\in R^{2}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
.
Given a characteristic class
c\colon\mathrm{BU}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{1}(R)
, we can evaluate it on the tautological line bundle
L\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
to get a class

f(t):=c(L)\in R^{0}(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty})\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1em]{\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}}}\pi_{0}(R[[t]]).

Since
c(\mathbb{C})=1
and
L|_{\text{pt}}\simeq\mathbb{C}
, we see that
f(0)=1
.

Proposition 2.7

(Splitting Principle)
.

Let
R
be a complex oriented ring spectrum.
Then, the association

c\mapstochar\rightarrow f(t):=c(L)

induces an isomorphism between the abelian group of isomorphism classes of
R
-valued exponential characteristic classes and the abelian group of power series
f(x)=1+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}+\dots
with
a_{k}\in\pi_{2k}R
, endowed with the multiplicative group structure.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
R
be a complex oriented ring spectrum with associated parameter
t\in R^{2}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
.
We denote the inverse of the above association by

f\mapstochar\rightarrow c^{f},

taking a power series
f(x)=1+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}+\dots
to the unique
R
-valued exponential characteristic class
c^{f}
satisfying
c^{f}(L)=f(t)\in R^{0}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
.

Remark 2.9
.

One can explicitly recover
c^{f}
from
f
. Namely, let
V\rightarrow X
be a vector bundle of rank
n
over a space
X
. We can expand the multivariate power series
\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})
uniquely as a power series in the elementary symmetric polynomials

\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})=G(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n})

where

\sigma_{k}=\sum_{I\subseteq[n],|I|=k}\prod_{i\in I}x_{i}.

Then, using the splitting principle one easily shows that

c^{f}(V)=G(c_{1}(V),\dots,c_{n}(V)).

Remark 2.10
.

Note that
c^{f}
depends on the complex orientation of
R
through the choice of parameter
t\in R^{2}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
and not only on the abstract power series
f(x)
.

Example 2.11
.

Let
R
be a complex oriented ring spectrum, and consider the
R
-algebra
R[z]
with
|z|=2
. Then, the
\pi_{*}R[z]
-valued power series
f(x)=1+zx
defines a characteristic class

c^{f}\colon\mathrm{BU}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{1}(R[z]).

In this case, the corresponding multivariate power series is

G(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})=1+\sigma_{1}u+\dots+\sigma_{n}z^{n}

and hence

c^{f}(V)=\sum_{k}c_{k}(V)z^{k}=c(V;z)

is the total Chern class from
Definition

2.3
.

Example 2.12
.

(cf.
Section

5
)
Consider now
R((z^{-1}))
with
|z|=2
, let
F(x,y)
be the formal group law associated with the complex orientation of
R
and let

f(x)=z(x+_{F}z^{-1})=1+xz+\dots.

We obtain a canonical characteristic class

c^{f}\colon\mathrm{BU}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{1}(R((z^{-1}))).

The characteristic class in the
Example

2.12
is the main object of study in this paper. Observe that when

is the additive formal group law, it agrees with the extension of scalars of the total Chern class along the canonical map of homotopy ring spectra
R[z]\rightarrow R((z^{-1}))

The Euler orientation

3.1
Orientations of categories

Let
\mathcal{J}

3.4
The Euler map

Endow the
\infty

\Fun(\mathcal{J},\Perf(\mathbb{S})^{B\mathbb{T}})

\mathcal{J}
is initial, the constant functor
\underline{\mathbb{S}}\colon\mathcal{J}\rightarrow\Perf(\mathbb{S})^{B\mathbb{T}}
which takes all the objects of
\mathcal{J}
to the sphere spectrum with constant

-action is the unit of
\Fun(\mathcal{J},\Perf(\mathbb{S})^{B\mathbb{T}})
.

Definition 3.11
.

Since
\underline{\mathbb{S}}
is the unit of
\Fun(\mathcal{J},\Perf(\mathbb{S})^{B\mathbb{T}})

of symmetric monoidal functors.

For

, the

is obtained by applying

Next, we discuss a categorical variant of the Tate fixed points functor for
\mathbb{T}

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\in\cat^{\mathrm{perf}}
.
There is a fully faithful embedding

\mathrm{Nm}_{\mathbb{T}}\colon\mathscr{C}[B\mathbb{T}]\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{B\mathbb{T}}

from the constant

-shaped colimit to the constant

-shaped limit in
\cat^{\mathrm{perf}}
, with essential image the thick subcategory of
\mathscr{C}^{B\mathbb{T}}
generated by the induced local systems, i.e., the objects of the form
X\otimes\mathbb{T}
for

(and with the action coming from the translation action of
\mathbb{T}
on itself). If
\mathscr{C}
is stably symmetric monoidal then
\mathrm{Nm}_{\mathbb{T}}
is the inclusion of a thick tensor ideal, so that the quotient

\mathscr{C}^{t\mathbb{T}}:=\mathscr{C}^{B\mathbb{T}}/\mathscr{C}[B\mathbb{T}]

admits a canonical commutative
\mathscr{C}^{B\mathbb{T}}
-algebra structure in
\cat^{\mathrm{perf}}
. In this case,
\End(\mathds{1}_{\mathscr{C}^{t\mathbb{T}}})\simeq\End(\mathds{1}_{\mathscr{C}})^{t\mathbb{T}}
, where the right hand side is the classical Tate fixed points construction on
\End(\mathds{1}_{\mathscr{C}})
.

Proof.

We have an embedding

\mathscr{C}[B\mathbb{T}]\hookrightarrow\Ind(\mathscr{C}[B\mathbb{T}])\simeq\Ind(\mathscr{C})^{B\mathbb{T}}.

To identify its essential image, note that
\mathscr{C}[B\mathbb{T}]
is generated from the image of the push-forward functor
\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}[B\mathbb{T}]
along the inclusion
\text{pt}\hookrightarrow B\mathbb{T}
, whose image under
\mathrm{Nm}_{\mathbb{T}}
is exactly the induced objects
X\otimes\mathbb{T}
in
\Ind(\mathscr{C})^{B\mathbb{T}}
. In particular, since
\mathbb{T}
is a finite space, the essential image lies in
\mathscr{C}^{B\mathbb{T}}\subseteq\Ind(\mathscr{C})^{B\mathbb{T}}
.

When
\mathscr{C}
is symmetric monoidal, we have that
Y\otimes(X\otimes\mathbb{T})\simeq(Y\otimes X)\otimes\mathbb{T}
for
Y\in\mathscr{C}^{B\mathbb{T}}
and

(regarding

as an object of
\mathscr{C}
), showing that
\mathscr{C}[B\mathbb{T}]
is a thick tensor ideal. Finally, the determination of the endomorphism object of the unit in
\mathscr{C}^{t\mathbb{T}}
follows from the same consideration as in the proof of
[
16

.
∎

We denote the quotient functor from the proposition above by

is symmetric monoidal so is

∎

Clinical Outcomes, Treatment Patterns, and Molecular Heterogeneity of TFE3‐Rearranged Renal Cell Carcinoma: A Multicenter Real‐World Study
Authors: Shuxuan Zhu, Siqi Zhou, Xi Tian, Jun Wang, Ze Wang, Jun Jiang, Qiuli Liu, Xin Gou, Xiang Zhou, Jun Gu, Jianfeng Yang, Jianhui Chen, Shushang Chen, Wenfeng Wang, Aihetaimujiang Anwaier, Kun Chang, Yuanyuan Qu, James Brugarolas, Wenhao Xu, Hailiang Zhang, Dingwei Ye

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundTFE3‐rearranged renal cell carcinoma (TFE3‐rRCC) is uncommon and encompasses biologically diverse tumors, while evidence to guide systemic treatment remains sparse. We examined clinical outcomes, treatment patterns, and molecular features in a multicenter real‐world cohort.MethodsWe reviewed 151 patients with TFE3‐rRCC treated at eight centers in China. Treatment analyses included the 59 patients who had metastatic or relapsed disease and sufficiently detailed treatment records. Response was assessed by RECIST version 1.1. Kaplan–Meier methods were used for time‐to‐event outcomes, and potential confounding was explored with Cox regression.ResultsMedian first‐line progression‐free survival (PFS) was 6.1 months among the 59 treated patients. PFS was longer with first‐line ICI/TKI therapy than with TKI monotherapy (log‐rank p = 0.034). In an exploratory multivariable model, distant lymph‐node metastasis was associated with shorter overall survival (HR, 5.22; 95% CI, 1.17–23.40; p = 0.031). In a small subgroup comparison, NONO‐TFE3 tumors had a relatively immune‐depleted transcriptomic profile and lower disease control with ICI/TKI therapy.Conclusions TFE3‐rRCC is a heterogeneous disease with poor survival and a variable therapeutic response. ICI/TKI combinations had modest activity; newer approaches are needed.Keywords: fusion partner, real‐world study, systemic therapy, TFE3‐rearranged renal cell carcinoma, translocation renal cell carcinoma

Introduction

Also known as Xp11.2 translocation renal cell carcinoma (RCC), TFE3‐rearranged RCC (TFE3‐rRCC) is one of the RCC subtypes with a low incidence but high aggressiveness [1, 2]. It accounts for 1%–4% of adult kidney cancers, but can reach 50%–75% among juveniles [3, 4]. TFE3‐rRCC is characterized by a TFE3 translocation leading to a fusion protein [5]. Common fusion partner genes include ASPSCR1, NONO, PRCC, etc. [6]. The fusion often involves 5′ sequences from the partner gene (such as exons 1–11 of NONO) and 3′ sequences from TFE3 [3]. Fusion proteins typically retain the DNA binding domain of TFE3, which may be implicated in gene transactivation [7, 8]. Recently, expression of a human ASPSCR1‐TFE3 fusion was shown to suffice for tRCC development in mice [9]. While several studies have attempted to explore the molecular characteristics of TFE3‐rRCC (including RNA‐seq, proteomics, etc.) [10, 11], our understanding remains limited, particularly considering the heterogeneity induced by the fusion type.

Many patients with TFE3‐rRCC exhibit distant metastasis at the time of diagnosis. Even in cases without metastatic disease at presentation, approximately 50% of TFE3‐rRCC patients develop metastatic relapse within 2 years post‐surgery [12]. Available treatment‐related data are limited [3]. In the IMmotion151 trial, six TFE3‐rRCC patients received sunitinib monotherapy; however, the median PFS was only 3.5 months, suggesting that TKI therapy may have limited efficacy [13]. Studies have also suggested that immune checkpoint inhibitors may only be effective against certain fusion types [10]. Overall, there is currently a lack of effective treatment strategies for metastatic TFE3‐rRCC.

In this study, we retrospectively reviewed data from several Chinese medical centers, creating a large cohort of metastatic TFE3‐rRCC with detailed follow‐up. We systematically analyzed patients' treatments and outcomes. Additionally, we performed multi‐omics analysis.

Materials and Methods

Chinese Multicenter TFE3‐rRCC Cohort

Patients were retrospectively identified at eight medical centers in China, and previously published multi‐omics data from our institution were incorporated [11]. The cases spanned many years, during which diagnostic practice differed by center and changed over time. TFE3 rearrangement was determined locally with break‐apart fluorescence in situ hybridization probes, RNA sequencing, or both, according to the available workflow. A central pathological or molecular review could not be undertaken because archival material was unavailable for many cases. Analyses by fusion partner were consequently limited to tumors in which the partner had been identified. The study followed the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the institutional review board of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (approval no. 2404294‐2).

Clinical Data and Systemic Treatment Analysis

The clinical variables collected were age at diagnosis, sex, laterality and size of the primary tumor, TNM stage, timing and sites of metastasis, fusion partner, systemic regimen, line of therapy, best response, and treatment duration. Response was evaluated according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1. Patient‐level details are listed in Tables S1 and S2. Treatment was classified as TKI monotherapy, mTOR inhibitor (mTORi) monotherapy, TKI/mTORi combination therapy, immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) monotherapy, ICI/TKI combination therapy, or dual‐ICI therapy. Drug availability, treatment period, and prescribing practice varied among centers; comparisons between treatment groups were therefore used to describe associations rather than causal effects.

Transcriptomic, Proteomic, and Tumor Microenvironment Analyses

Gene‐set variation analysis (GSVA) [14] was applied to RNA‐sequencing data to score pathways related to TKI targets, including c‐MET, EGF, ERK, FGF, MAPK, PDGF, TRK, and VEGF signaling. Gene sets were obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database [15]. Tumor microenvironment (TME) features, including angiogenesis, fibroblast, and immune‐infiltration signatures, were scored with the framework reported by Bagaev et al. [16]. Fusion types were then mapped descriptively to immune‐enriched fibrotic, immune‐enriched non‐fibrotic, fibrotic, or immune‐depleted patterns. The omics datasets did not consistently overlap with the 59‐patient treatment cohort. Accordingly, these results were used to generate biological hypotheses and were not interpreted as patient‐level mechanisms of treatment response.

Multiplex Immunofluorescence

Archival sections from one ASPL‐TFE3 tumor and one NONO‐TFE3 tumor were examined as an exploratory visual comparison. The six‐marker multiplex immunofluorescence panel comprised CD68, PD‐1, CD206, PD‐L1, TFE3, and CD8, with DAPI as the nuclear counterstain. Antigen retrieval in AR9 buffer lasted 20 min, followed by 45‐min incubations with each primary antibody. Fluorophores were assigned as follows: Opal 480, CD68; Opal 520, PD‐L1; Opal 570, PD‐1; Opal 620, TFE3; Opal 690, CD206; and Opal 780, CD8. Three representative regions of tumor were imaged at 4×, 10×, and 20× magnification.

Composition and Overlap of Multi‐Omics Datasets

The combined dataset included institutional and public cohorts (Table S3). Within the FUSCC series, DNA‐sequencing data were available for 66 tumors, RNA‐sequencing data for 26, and proteomic data for 65. Twenty tumors had paired RNA and proteomic profiles, and 14 were represented in all three modalities. The identifier crosswalk added to Table S1 matched 54 of the 151 patients in the multicenter clinical cohort to FUSCC multi‐omics records: 47 had DNA‐sequencing data, 20 had RNA‐sequencing data, and 46 had proteomic data. The public datasets contributed 5 DNA‐sequenced tumors from Durinck et al., 16 from MSK, 11 DNA‐ and 13 RNA‐sequenced tumors from TCGA, and 48 DNA‐ and 63 RNA‐sequenced tumors from West China Hospital. Somatic mutations from 146 patients were displayed with ComplexHeatmap [17]. The crosswalk documents partial overlap between the clinical and institutional omics cohorts, but molecular profiles and treatment outcomes were not jointly available for every patient. Molecular‐response findings were thus treated as exploratory rather than as uniformly paired mechanistic analyses. Prior RCC genomic studies were used to place the TCGA comparisons in context [18, 19].

Statistical Analysis

Analyses were based on available cases, without imputation. Continuous variables were screened for implausible entries and are reported as median (interquartile range [IQR]); categorical variables are given as n/N (%). Clinical variables were neither transformed nor excluded as outliers. Response proportions were compared with two‐sided Fisher exact tests, and exact binomial 95% CIs were calculated for objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR). First‐line TKI and ICI/TKI groups were compared at baseline with the Wilcoxon rank‐sum or Fisher exact test, as appropriate; standardized mean differences were also reported to show imbalance. Overall survival (OS) and PFS were estimated by Kaplan–Meier analysis and compared with two‐sided log‐rank tests. Cox proportional‐hazards models used Efron's method for ties, and proportionality was examined with Schoenfeld residuals. Age, Stage IV disease, and number of metastatic sites were included in the exploratory first‐line PFS model. The separate OS model examined liver and distant lymph‐node metastases with adjustment for age, metastatic burden, and first‐line ICI/TKI use. NRF2 scores in Figure S1H were compared with a two‐sided nonparametric Wilcoxon rank‐sum test. Given the small number of events, the models were kept parsimonious and no multiplicity correction was applied. A two‐sided p < 0.05 defined statistical significance, although subgroup findings were considered exploratory. R version 4.3.1 and Python 3.11 were used.

Results

Cohort Construction and Clinical Characteristics of the Chinese Metastatic TFE3‐rRCC Patients

Across the eight participating centers, 160 patients were initially identified and nine were lost to follow‐up, leaving 151 evaluable patients with TFE3‐rRCC (Figure 1A; Table S1). Metastatic or relapsed disease occurred in 92 patients, of whom 59 had detailed systemic‐treatment records. Sixty‐two FISH‐positive tumors did not have an identified fusion partner. Among characterized fusions, ASPSCR1/ASPL was the most frequent (n = 22), followed by PRCC (n = 16) and SFPQ (n = 16) (Figure 1B). Median disease‐free survival after surgery was 23.8 months (Figure 1D). The 59‐patient treatment cohort had a median age of 30 years (IQR, 22–41); 39 patients (66.1%) were female, 22 (37.3%) had Stage IV disease at diagnosis, 32 (54.2%) presented with synchronous metastases, and 27 (45.8%) later developed metachronous metastases (Table 1).

After first‐line systemic therapy began, observed OS ranged from 4.2 to 116.7 months; median PFS was 6.1 months (Figure 1E,F). Retroperitoneal lymph nodes were the most frequent metastatic site (32/59, 54.2%), followed by lung (20/59, 33.9%), liver (15/59, 25.4%), and bone (14/59, 23.7%) (Figure S1G). Liver and distant lymph‐node metastases were each associated with shorter OS in unadjusted analyses (Figures 1G and S1A). In the exploratory multivariable model, the association persisted for distant lymph‐node metastasis (HR, 5.22; 95% CI, 1.17–23.40; p = 0.031). The liver‐metastasis estimate was imprecise (HR, 2.73; 95% CI, 0.76–9.80; p = 0.123). With only 19 deaths, both estimates warrant cautious interpretation.

Systemic Treatment Patterns and Outcomes

Systemic therapy was administered to all 59 patients (Table 2; Figure 2). When courses from all treatment lines were considered, 51 evaluable TKI‐monotherapy courses yielded an ORR of 11.8% and a DCR of 66.7%. The corresponding rates among 29 ICI/TKI courses were 24.1% and 82.8%. Progressive disease occurred in all four courses of everolimus monotherapy, although this small series cannot determine lack of efficacy. In the first‐line setting, patients receiving TKI alone (n = 36) were older and had more metastatic sites than those receiving ICI/TKI (n = 16) (Table S5). Median PFS was 5.6 months with TKI and 13.7 months with ICI/TKI (unadjusted log‐rank p = 0.034). After adjustment for age, Stage IV disease, and number of metastatic sites, the estimated association weakened (ICI/TKI vs. TKI HR, 0.48; 95% CI, 0.20–1.11; p = 0.087). The proportional‐hazards assumption was not violated (global p = 0.79). These results describe an association and should not be read as evidence of treatment superiority (Table 3; Figure 3).

Fusion‐Subtype Heterogeneity: Exploratory Molecular and Clinical Associations

Pathway scores differed across fusion subtypes (Figure 4A). Several TKI‐related pathways scored higher in ASPL‐TFE3 tumors, whereas many of the evaluated pathways showed lower activation in RBM10‐TFE3 and PRCC‐TFE3 tumors. Because the transcriptomic data were unpaired, this pattern raises—but does not test—the possibility that fusion partner influences TKI sensitivity. The clinical subgroups were small and received heterogeneous regimens. In the prespecified combined ASPL/PRCC/RBM10 group, DCR with TKI monotherapy was 7/16, compared with 12/15 in the other displayed fusion groups (two‐sided Fisher exact p = 0.066). On the present evidence, fusion partner cannot be used as a treatment‐selection biomarker.

TME signatures were examined by fusion subtype with the Bagaev framework (Figure 4D,E). Multiplex immunohistochemistry (mIHC) was performed on the available NONO‐TFE3 (n = 2) and ASPL‐TFE3 cases. Representative images from three selected fields are presented in Figure S2. The NONO‐TFE3 tumors consistently exhibited minimal immune cell infiltration across all fields, consistent with an immune desert phenotype. Due to the extreme rarity of NONO‐TFE3 fusion‐positive tumors, only two samples were obtainable for staining; therefore, quantitative statistical comparisons could not be conducted. Nevertheless, these descriptive findings provide supportive evidence for a lack of immune activation in this tumor subtype. Disease control was observed in 2/5 evaluable NONO‐TFE3 ICI/TKI courses (40.0%; exact 95% CI, 5.3%–85.3%) and in 14/14 courses from the other displayed fusion groups (100%; pooled exact 95% CI, 76.8%–100%; two‐sided Fisher exact p = 0.039). This comparison is limited by wide CIs, regimen heterogeneity, and incomplete patient‐level overlap between the omics and treatment datasets, and it is not sufficient to guide treatment.

There was no statistically significant OS difference between the Chinese and external TFE3‐rRCC cohorts (Figure S1F), allowing the cohorts to be combined for descriptive purposes. Within the integrated cohort, unadjusted OS was shorter for ASPL‐TFE3 and NONO‐TFE3 cases than for the remaining fusion groups (Figure 4H). Stage, treatment, and follow‐up were not harmonized across the contributing cohorts, so this finding requires confirmation in a comparably characterized population.

Integrative Somatic Mutation Landscape and Prognosis of TFE3‐rRCC

In this study, we integrated DNA sequencing data from public data sets (146 TFE3‐rRCC samples, as described in the method section) and constructed the somatic mutation landscape (Figure 5A, the integrated mutation data is provided as Table S4). Genes with relatively high mutation frequencies included PCLO (5%), BCDIN3D (4%), NDRG1 (4%), and ZNF668 (4%). We also explored common gene mutations in other types of renal cancer. While mutations in MTOR, ARID1A, and KMT2D were observed in some TFE3‐rRCC samples, the mutation frequencies of these genes were all below 3%.

Published findings on prognosis are not uniform: one study reported worse outcomes for TFE3‐rRCC than for other RCC subtypes [20], whereas another found outcomes comparable to ccRCC [21]. In our unadjusted analyses, OS and PFS were shorter in TFE3‐rRCC than in a Chinese ccRCC cohort (Figure 5B,C); comparisons with TCGA subtypes varied by endpoint (Figure 5D,E). Age, stage, IMDC risk, treatment, and follow‐up were not matched, and the patient‐level covariates needed for robust adjustment were not available. The cross‐cohort curves are therefore descriptive and do not show that TFE3‐rRCC uniformly carries a poorer prognosis than every non‐TFE3 RCC subtype.

Discussion

Recurrent somatic mutations were uncommon in the integrated TFE3‐rRCC genomic data. PCLO was mutated in 5% of tumors, although the functional significance of this finding is uncertain. The low mutation frequencies direct attention to fusion‐driven biology but do not rule out relevant low‐frequency mutations, copy‐number changes, or epigenomic drivers. Previous molecular and clinical series have also described marked genomic heterogeneity and variable treatment outcomes in tRCC [22, 23, 24].

Progressive disease was recorded in each of the four evaluable everolimus‐monotherapy courses. The number is too small, and treatment line too confounded, to conclude that mTOR inhibition lacks activity. Enrichment of mTOR‐ and autophagy‐related processes in the proteomic data suggests a possible biological explanation, not a proven resistance mechanism. The interplay between autophagy initiation and NRF2 is complex [25, 26]. Autophagy has been studied as a therapeutic target in cancer [27] and has been linked to resistance to chemotherapy [28] and radiotherapy [29]. Early‐phase studies in RCC have evaluated combined autophagy and mTOR inhibition [30, 31], while rapalog resistance appears to involve several mechanisms [32]. Whether autophagy modulation changes mTORi sensitivity in TFE3‐rRCC will require prospective functional testing.

Variation in pathway and TME scores across fusion groups provides leads for future mechanistic work, but it does not establish fusion partner as a predictive biomarker. MET signaling can be activated by TFE3 fusions [33], and retrospective activity of cabozantinib has been described in MiT‐family tRCC [34]. Our cohort, however, included only three cabozantinib‐monotherapy courses. The immune‐depleted signature seen in NONO‐TFE3 tumors and the lower observed disease control with ICI/TKI therapy likewise need assessment in datasets with paired molecular and clinical information [35]. Work on autophagy, immune‐metabolic competition, emerging immune checkpoints, and organoid models offers several routes for investigating these observations [36, 37, 38, 39].

Treatment of metastatic tRCC is largely adapted from non‐clear‐cell RCC and commonly uses VEGF/MET‐directed TKIs or ICI‐based combinations; there is no established fusion‐specific standard. Retrospective studies have reported activity with cabozantinib and ICI combinations [22, 23, 24], but prospective evidence focused on this subtype remains limited. Trials of nivolumab with or without axitinib are ongoing. Patient‐derived organoids and models that incorporate immune components could support functional testing of fusion‐related questions, although both standardization and clinical validation are still needed. In this cohort, differences in treatment era, drug access, and baseline characteristics restrict comparisons between regimens.

Several features of the study constrain interpretation. Its retrospective, multicenter design is susceptible to selection and information bias. Detailed treatment records were missing for 33 of 92 patients with metastatic or relapsed disease, and a fusion partner could not be assigned in many FISH‐positive cases. The cohort accrued over a long period; local testing used break‐apart probes, RNA sequencing, or both, and central re‐review was not possible for cases without remaining archival material. Treatment also varied by drug, line, and calendar period. IMDC risk, treatment‐era variables, and patient‐level covariates from the external comparator cohorts were unavailable, precluding adjusted or matched cross‐cohort analyses. Although the identifier crosswalk connected 54 clinical cases to institutional omics records, molecular modalities and treatment outcomes were only partly paired. Finally, subgroup sizes were small, multiple comparisons were made without multiplicity correction, and prognostic comparisons across cohorts were unmatched. These considerations limit the findings to hypothesis generation.

Conclusion

In this multicenter series, TFE3‐rRCC showed substantial variation in clinical outcomes and molecular features. First‐line ICI/TKI therapy was associated with longer PFS than TKI monotherapy before adjustment, although the estimate weakened in the adjusted model. Differences in pathway scores, TME features, and treatment response across fusion groups remained uncertain because the relevant subgroups were small. Distant lymph‐node metastasis was associated with shorter OS in a parsimonious multivariable model. Fusion partners should not inform treatment selection until these observations are tested in prospective cohorts with uniform characterization and paired molecular and clinical data.

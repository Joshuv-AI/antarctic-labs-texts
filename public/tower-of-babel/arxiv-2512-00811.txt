1.
introduction

Quasi-polynomials are ubiquitous in math. Let
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be a quasi-polynomial of degree
r
. Say
f(n)=a_{r}(n)n^{r}+\text{lower order terms}
. A natural question is when

a_{i}(-)
are constant for
i>\delta
. Following Erhart, the smallest such
\delta
is called the
grade
of
f
.
To put this question in the right historical framework let us consider the following:
Let
P
be a
d
-dimensional rational convex polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
E(P,k)=\sharp\ kP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be the Ehrhart
function (here
\sharp V
denotes cardinality of a set
V
). It is well-known that
E(P,k)
is of quasi-polynomial type cf.
[
1
, 6.3.11]
, say
E(P,n)=b_{s}(n)n^{s}+\text{lower terms}
.
We state a
simplified version of a conjecture due to Ehrhart:
If the affine span of every
d-1
dimensional face of
P
contains a point with integer co-ordinates then

b_{s}(-)
is a constant.

A more general version of this was proved independently by McMullen (see
[
4
]
) and Stanley
(
[
6
]
, Theorem 2.8). For a recent proof see
[
2
, 5]
.

The Hilbert function of a ring of invariants
R=k[V]^{G}
provide a rich class of quasi-polynomials. In this paper we study Erhart’s question in this case.

is a quasi-polynomial and
a_{d-1}(-)\neq 0

2.
A Lemma

The following Lemma is a crucial ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.1
). Let
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{t}\}
be a linearly independent subset of
(V^{*})^{G}
. Then

(1)

\dim R/(z_{1},\ldots,z_{t})\leq d-t
.

(2)

If
P
is a minimal prime over
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{t})
then
0ptP=t
.

(3)

There exists a homogeneous system of parameters
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{t},y_{1},\ldots,y_{d-t}\}
of
R
.

(4)

The Hilbert series of
R
is of the form

\frac{Q(u)}{(1-u)^{t}\prod_{j=1}^{d-t}(1-u^{\deg y_{j}})}\quad\text{where}\ Q(u)\in\mathbb{Z}[u]\ \text{and}\ Q(1)\neq 0.

Proof.

(1) We prove the result by induction on
t
. When
t=1
we have nothing to prove. Assume the result for
t-1\geq 1
and we prove the result for
t
. The action of
G
on
V^{*}/kz_{1}
fixes
z_{2},\ldots,z_{t}
. We have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow S(-1)\xrightarrow{z_{1}}S\rightarrow T\rightarrow 0
. Taking invariants we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow R(-1)\xrightarrow{z_{1}}R\xrightarrow{u}T^{G}\rightarrow H^{1}(G,S)(-1)\xrightarrow{z_{1}}H^{1}(G,S),

where
H^{1}(G,S)
is the first group cohomology of
S
with respect to
G
. Set
A=\operatorname{image}u
and
K=\ker(H^{1}(G,S)(-1)\xrightarrow{z_{1}}H^{1}(G,S))
.
So we have an exact sequence
0\rightarrow A\rightarrow T^{G}\rightarrow K\rightarrow 0
. We take Koszul homology with respect to
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}=z_{2},\ldots,z_{t}
. So we have an exact sequence

H_{1}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime},K)\rightarrow A/\mathbf{z}^{\prime}A\rightarrow T^{G}/\mathbf{z}^{\prime}T^{G}\rightarrow K/\mathbf{z}^{\prime}K\rightarrow 0.

By induction hypothesis we have
\dim T^{G}/\mathbf{z}^{\prime}T^{G}\leq d-1-(t-1)=d-t
. It follows that
\dim K/\mathbf{z}^{\prime}K\leq d-t
. We note that
H_{1}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime},K)
is annhilated by
\operatorname{ann}K
and
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}
.
So
\dim H_{1}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime},K)\leq d-t
. It follows that
\dim A/\mathbf{z}^{\prime}A\leq d-t
. As
A=R/z_{1}R
we get that
\dim R/\mathbf{z}R\leq d-t
; here
\mathbf{z}=z_{1},\ldots,z_{t}
.
The result follows.

(2) Let
P
be a minimal prime of
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{t})
. Then
P
is a homogeneous prime. Then
\dim R/P\leq d-t
by (1). We note that
R
is equi-dimensional (as it is a domain) and catenary. So
\dim R/P=d-0ptP
, see

[
3
, Lemma 2, Section 31]
for the local case, the same proof works for the *-local case. So
0ptP\geq t
. But
P
is minimal over
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{t})
. So
0ptP\leq t
. Thus
0ptP=i
.

(3) By (2) it follows that
0pt(z_{1},\ldots,z_{t})=t
. We can extend
\mathbf{z}
to get a homogeneous system of parameters of
R
.

(4) This follows from (3).
∎

3.
A result regarding quasi-polynomials

In this section we prove a result regarding quasi-polynomials that we need.

3.1
.

Recall a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is said to be of quasi-polynomial type if there exists a quasi-polynomial
P(X)
such that
f(n)=P(n)
for all
n\gg 0
. If

P(n)=a_{r}(n)n^{r}+\cdots+a_{0}(n)
with
a_{i}(-)
periodic functions and
a_{r}(-)\neq 0
then we say
\deg(f)=r
.

3.2
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be of quasi-polynomial type of degree
r-1
. Then

\sum_{n\geq 0}f(n)z^{n}=\frac{Q(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(1-z^{d_{i}})}\quad\text{where}\ Q(z)\in\mathbb{Z}[z]\ \text{and}\ Q(1)\neq 0\ \text{and}\ d_{i}\geq 1.

The following result is crucial in our proof of Theorem
1.2

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be of quasi-polynomial type of degree
d-1
. Assume

\sum_{n\geq 0}f(n)z^{n}=\frac{Q(z)}{(1-z)^{r}\prod_{i=1}^{d-r}(1-z^{d_{i}})}\quad\text{where}\ Q(z)\in\mathbb{Z}[z]\ \text{and}\ Q(1)\neq 0\ \text{and}\ d_{i}\geq 1.

If for
n\gg 0
we have
f(n)=a_{d-1}(n)n^{d-1}+\cdots a_{1}(n)n+a_{0}(n)
where
a_{i}(-)
are periodic functions, then
a_{d-1}(-),\ldots,a_{d-r}(-)
are constants.

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on
r
. We first consider the case when
r=1
.
We have

(1-z)\sum_{n\geq 0}f(n)z^{n}=\frac{Q(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}(1-z^{d_{i}})}=\sum_{n\geq 0}g(n).

Then
g
is of polynomial type of degree
d-2
. Say
g(n)=b_{d-2}n^{d-2}+\cdots
.
We note that
f(n)-f(n-1)=g(n)
. It follows that
a_{d-1}(-)
is a constant as otherwise
\deg g
will be
d-1
. The result follows when
r=1
.

Now assume that result holds for
r-1
and we prove it for
r
(here
r\geq 2
).
By induction hypothesis
a_{d-1}(-),\ldots,a_{d-r+1}(-)
are constants.
We have

(1-z)\sum_{n\geq 0}f(n)z^{n}=\frac{Q(z)}{(1-z)^{r-1}\prod_{i=r}^{d-1}(1-z^{d_{i}})}=\sum_{n\geq 0}g(n).

Then
g
is of polynomial type of degree
d-2
. Say
g(n)=b_{d-2}n^{d-2}+\cdots
. Then again by induction hypotheses
b_{d-2}(-),\ldots,b_{d-r}(-)
are constants. We note that
f(n)-f(n-1)=g(n)
. We get that
a_{d-r+1}+a_{d-r}(n)-a_{d-r}(n-1)=b_{d-r}
which is a constant. As
a_{d-r}(-)
is periodic it follows that
a_{d-r}(-)
is a constant.
The result holds by induction.
∎

5.
An example

We give an example which shows that our result is sharp.

Example 5.1
.

Let
G=<\sigma>\subseteq GL_{2}(k)
where

\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

Then
k[V]^{G}=k[x+y,xy]
. We note that the dimension of fixed points of
V^{*}
is one. The Hilbert series of this ring of invariants is

H_{R}(n)=n/2+a_{0}(n)
for
n\gg 0
. Here
a_{0}(n)=1
if
n
is even and
1/2
when
n
is odd. So
a_{0}(-)
is
not
a constant.

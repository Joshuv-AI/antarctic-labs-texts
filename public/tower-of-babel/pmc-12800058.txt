A MAGDM approach based on dual hesitant Q-rung orthopair fuzzy Dombi norm with Hamy mean operators and its application
Authors: Xiuqin Ma, Mengyao Wang, Hongwu Qin, Qi Wei, Tao Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs complex real-world decision-making intensifies, handling inherent fuzziness and uncertainty becomes crucial. Dual hesitant q-rung orthopair fuzzy (DHq-ROF) sets offer a solution for addressing ambiguity and hesitancy for multi-attribute group decision-making (MAGDM). However, the existing MAGDM methods fail to simultaneously consider attribute correlation and flexibility, and involve lower variation rate of the scored values. Additionally, these techniques concern distance measurement with normalization, which leads to information redundancy. To address these above issues, we propose a new MAGDM approach based on DHq-ROF Dombi norm with Hamy Mean (HM) Operators. First, the HM operator considering attribute correlation and the Dombi t-norms and t-conorms (Dt-N&t-CNs) which offer enhanced flexibility are combined within the DHq-ROF environment, proposing DHq-ROF weighted Dombi HM and DHq-ROF weighted Dombi dual HM aggregation operators to obtain comprehensive evaluative information. Second, a new distance measurement method without normalization is put forward to avoid information redundancy, and subsequently, the related entropy measures for objectively deriving the undetermined weights of attributes are presented. Third, this study proposes a novel DHq-ROF-based approach for MAGDM. Finally, a case study on traditional Chinese medicine-based sleep disorder diagnosis confirms the method’s effectiveness, achieving a higher variation rate of the scored values while simultaneously considering flexibility and attribute correlation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-30997-0.Keywords: Dual hesitant q-rung orthopair fuzzy set, Dombi t-norms and t-conorms, Hamy mean operators, Multi-attribute group decision-makingSubject terms: Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Research background of TCM diagnosis for SD

Sleep Disorders (SD) refers to abnormal amount of sleep as well as the manifestation of abnormal behaviors during sleep, and also the manifestation of disturbances in the normal rhythmic alternation between sleep and wakefulness. SD has become a public health issue of world concern. Statistics from the “2024 White Paper on Chinese Residents’ Sleep Health” released in March 2024 show that the overall sleep quality among the population is poor, and sleep problems are prominent, with the average length of sleep of Chinese people at 6.75 h, and as many as 59% of the population suffering from insomnia, while only 19% of the population having no SD at all1. The causes of SD are mainly biological, such as changes in biological rhythms, physical illnesses, genetic factors, changes in body temperature, etc. Secondly, it is psychological causes, sleep expectation, bad sleep habits and wrong cognition. Thirdly, it is related to stress and changes in the environment, encountering things that are not too favorable in life. SD is very unfavorable to our physical and mental health. Chronic sleep deficiency is associated with elevated risks of developing various health complications, including cardiovascular disorders, metabolic dysfunctions, and mental health conditions. SD has become one of the most prevalent and pressing medical problems facing modern society2–5. TCM is one of the best way to diagnose SD. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) diagnoses SDs in various ways, and different methods can be chosen for treatment according to the specific conditions of patients and different causes of SDs.

TCM constitutes a holistic medical system encompassing the study of human physiological and pathological mechanisms, while emphasizing disease prevention, clinical diagnosis, therapeutic intervention, and health maintenance6. TCM treatments include medication, acupuncture, massage and tuina, qigong, dietary therapy, and other modalities, which aim to adjust the yin-yang equilibrium in the body, clear the meridians and channels, and harmonize qi and blood, so as to achieve the goal of curing diseases. The 72nd World Health Assembly approved the ICD-11 revision, marking a historic inclusion of Chinese traditional medicine practices in WHO’s global classification system7. Additionally, China also actively participates in the work of the International Organization of TCM, the World Federation of Acupuncture and Moxibustion Societies, and other international TCM organizations, demonstrating the significant influence of TCM in the international arena is enormous8. TCM is a unique system of medicine, characterized by its safety, effectiveness, and wide generalization. It has a significant standing in the world’s medical arena due to its special theoretical system and diagnostic and treatment means, and has provided substantial contributions to the health of humanity. TCM collects information about a patient’s condition through four diagnostic means of looking, smelling, questioning and cutting, and then conducts diagnostic analysis according to the patient’s specific situation to determine the cause, nature, and location of the disease. So as to formulate a personalized and comprehensive treatment plan, the determination of TCM diagnostic plan is also subject to the influence of subjectivity and uncertainty. One of the major features of TCM is that it is different from person to person and dialectical treatment. Individuals are very different from each other and many of them have some other symptoms at the same time, and due to the differences in patients’ individuality. Even for the same disease, patients with different constitution may show different conditions, which leads to potential differences in the treatment results. Therefore, fuzzy MAGDM has an important application in the field of TCM9,10.

Development of DHq-ROFSs

A significant problem in real-world applications involving MAGDM is managing ambiguous and fuzzy data11. Originally introduced by Zadeh12, fuzzy set (FS) theory emerged as a mathematical framework for handling real-world ambiguity and uncertainty phenomena. It involves the degree of membership (MD), in which elements are part of a particular set with a specific degree of membership. Subsequently, Atanassov and Atanassov13 introduced intuitionistic FSs, which incorporate both MD and non-membership degree (NMD) to represent uncertain information. This approach offers more options for describing phenomena while adhering to the constraint that MD + NMD ≤ 1.

In recent years, Yager14,15 expanded upon the classical FS and intuitionistic FS and then proposed Pythagorean fuzzy sets (PFSs) which can represent vagueness and uncertainty across a broader range. However, PFSs also have limitations regarding universality. Consequently, Yager16 introduced q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets (q-ROFSs) by incorporating q-rung relations. However, due to the more complex problems encountered in the real world, decision-makers (DMs) often hesitate when evaluating alternatives among several possible fuzzy numbers that cannot be represented by a single exact value. In response to this challenge, Torra17 introduced the idea of the hesitant fuzzy set (HFS), a recent expansion of FSs that accommodates multiple values for the degree of MD of an element. Subsequently, recognizing that HFS is limited by its focus solely on MD, Zhu et al.18 introduced the dual hesitant fuzzy set (DHFS). This innovative framework combines the characteristics of IFS and HFS, allowing each element to possess multiple possible values for both MD and NMD, with the requirement that the largest MD plus the largest NMD is no more than 1. With the increasing prevalence of hesitancy and uncertainty in the decision-making process, Xu et al.19 introduced the dual hesitant q-rung orthopair fuzzy set (DHq-ROFS) by extending DHFS based on q-ROFS, which allows each element to possess multiple possible MDs and NMDs, and the sum of the q of the largest MD and the q of the largest NMD is not more than one. When q = 1, DHq-ROFS simplifies to DHFS, and when q = 2, it simplifies to the dual hesitant Pythagorean fuzzy set (DHPFS). The DHq-ROFS model can express the DM’s hesitancy and uncertainty more comprehensively and accurately, offering significant advantages in terms of universality. In practical applications, it assists DMs in addressing complex problems more holistically, thereby greatly enhancing the quality and efficiency of decision-making. It serves as an effective tool for tackling intricate decision-making challenges. The properties of FS, IFS, PFS, q-ROFS, HFS, DHFS, DHPFS, and DHq-ROFS are compared in Table 1. This comparison clearly demonstrates that DHq-ROFS possesses superior properties for managing complex decision-making problems.

A brief review of MAGDM

One of the most successful applications of DHq-ROFS, involves MAGDM, which effectively addresses complex problems20–23. MAGDM is a decision issue of identifying the most desirable option or ordering candidate solutions by simultaneously considering the opinions of different DMs simultaneously with the consideration of multiple attributes, which is an important decision-making method in decision science. As evidenced by prior research, MAGDM has been extensively utilized in domains. For example, Fu and Xiao24achieved optimal selection of projects such as venture capital by building a MAGDM in an interval-valued intuitionistic fuzzy context. Arya and Kumar25 MAGDM to handle the supplier selection issue in a q-rung orthopair fuzzy setting. He et al.26 employed the MAGDM approach to tackle the problem of financial management performance evaluation for the purpose of identifying investment opportunities. Tian et al.27 presented a MAGDM approach relying on flexible linguistic representation and used it for the assessment of urban flood risks. By analyzing specific cases, we can gain insights into the effects and challenges of MAGDM methods in practical applications. The importance of MAGDM is that it enables DMs to systematically handle complex problems, thus improving the efficiency and precision of decision-making.

A brief description of Dt-N&t-CNs and HM aggregation AOs

The Aggregation Operator (AO) function, which combines and integrates multiple data items to produce a single output value during the data fusion process, plays a crucial role in MAGDM.

An excellent AO known as Dt-N&t-CNs was introduced by Dombi28. First, the Dombi parameter can be adjusted according to the needs of DM, which enhances the flexibility of the information aggregation process. Secondly, Dt-N&t-CNs efficiently handles complex and uncertain data, providing a robust and reliable solution for data fusion. Numerous researchers have conducted in-depth studies on Dt-N&t-CNs across various fuzzy domains. Akram et al.29 proposed the Pythagorean Dombi fuzzy weighted arithmetic averaging operator, the Pythagorean Dombi fuzzy weighted geometric averaging operator, and four additional operators, addressing the Multi-Criteria Decision-Making problem under PFS. Jana et al.30 described the q-ROF Dombi-weighted averaging, order-weighted averaging, and hybrid weighted averaging, along with their geometric variants in the q-ROF context. Subsequently, Jana et al.31 introduced Pythagorean fuzzy power Dombi weighted averaging, order-weighted averaging, and hybrid weighted averaging operators, as well as their geometric variants. Seikh and Mandal have contributed to fuzzy decision-making by developing Dombi operations for intuitionistic and Fermatean sets32,33, Frank operators for q-ROF sets34, and by applying these within methods like EDAS to solve selection problems35–38.

In actual MAGDM problems, most of the attributes being fused are strongly correlated with each other. In 1890, Hamy39 proposed the Hamy symmetric function, which was later extended to the r-order HM; in 1998, Hara et al.40 improved the inequality of arithmetic mean and geometric mean, proving that HM and its dual value are monotonically between arithmetic mean and geometric mean. The parameters of the HM operator can be adjusted according to the specific needs of decision-making, which enhances its flexibility and yields results that are more aligned with the actual situation. Compared to the Bonferroni Mean and the Heronian Mean, the HM offers distinct advantages. While the Bonferroni Mean and the Heronian Mean primarily focus on the interrelationships between pairs of attributes, HM takes into account the interrelationships among multiple attributes. This characteristic makes HM highly flexible and generalized, rendering it more suitable for complex decision-making problems in practical applications. As a result, it has garnered significant attention from scholars. Xing et al.41 combined the HM operator with interaction operational laws to construct a q-ROF operator, which are applied to solve MAGDM problems. Garg et al.42 developed a series of complex Pythagorean fuzzy aggregation operators, encompassing both basic and dual forms with weighted variants under the HM framework. Hussain et al.43 presented a series of IF Aczel-Alsina HM operators by integrating the HM operator with the Aczel-Alsina norm in an IF environment. Recent works by Ali et al. have advanced the development of aggregation operators, introducing Hamy mean, copula-based power, and partitioned Maclaurin symmetric mean operators for various q-rung orthopair and other fuzzy set extensions44–48.

In the context of MAGDM, each alternative may possess multiple attributes, each exerting varying degrees of influence on the final decision outcome. Consequently, the proposed methodology yields more reliable and flexible outcomes compared to existing approaches.

Third, the method minimizes information loss by introducing a distance metric that does not require normalization. Building on this, the entropy weight method is proposed for objectively calculating attribute weights within the context of DHq-ROFSs, thereby enhancing the reliability of diagnostic results for patients with SD. (Zhou et al.; Hussain et al.; Wang et al.; Wang et al.; Kavitha et al.)54–58 all focused on the decision-making of a single expert. In contrast, the proposed MAGDM approach accommodates multiple experts, effectively overcoming the limitations of individual decision-making and proving to be more applicable to complex real-world decisions.

Conclusions and future research directions

In this research, we first propose the DHq-ROFWDHM and DHq-ROFWDDHM operators by integrating HM and Dt-N&t-CNs within the DHq-ROF framework. This integration enhances information aggregation flexibility through adjustable Dombi parameters, and the HM operator effectively captures interrelationships among multiple correlated attributes—this is a key advantage over methods that only handle independent attributes. Second, we introduce a new non-normalized distance measure and an entropy metric for determining unknown attribute weights. The non-normalized distance measure avoids information loss caused by normalization, and the entropy metric eliminates decision-maker subjectivity, which is a critical improvement over subjective weight-assignment methods. Subsequently, we develop a new MAGDM method based on these innovations, and validate its efficacy through a tangible case study on TCM-based sleep disorder diagnosis. Experimental results confirm that our approach, which simultaneously considers flexibility and attribute correlation, achieves a larger variation rate of score values. This strengthens alternative differentiation compared to existing methods, and the approach provides a reliable tool for TCM sleep disorder diagnosis while enriching fuzzy decision-making theory.

However, this study has two main limitations. First, while the HM operator captures interrelationships between attributes and addresses the flaw of ignoring attribute dependence, it increases the complexity of attribute fusion. Its aggregation process requires calculating attribute combinations, such as combinations for the -order HM, which consumes more computational resources and reduces efficiency in large-scale tasks. Second, the proposed MAGDM method has not integrated advanced decision-making approaches like TOPSIS, VIKOR, and TODIM. This limits its adaptability to diverse practical scenarios. These limitations may restrict the method’s application efficiency and scope.

Future research will focus on addressing current limitations while exploring prospective research directions with targeted innovations. First, adopt advanced fuzzy decision-making methods to streamline HM-based attribute combination calculations, cutting computational costs while preserving attribute correlation capture for large-scale tasks. Second, develop hybrid frameworks by fusing the MAGDM method with advanced approaches—including regret theory and prospect theory, as well as combining it with TODIM to reflect TCM practitioners’ risk preferences or integrating it with VIKOR to balance conflicting attributes like efficacy and safety. Third, extend the DHq-ROF framework to probabilistic linguistic or interval-valued fuzzy environments to handle complex TCM data uncertainty, and verify the optimized method’s reliability and applicability using multi-source to enhance its practical value in clinical decision-making.

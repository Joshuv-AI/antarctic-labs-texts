Effects of electronic patient-reported outcome monitoring on symptom burden and health-related quality of life in patients with metastatic breast cancer: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol
Authors: Huanhuan Xiong, Qijing Luan, Wei Li, Pingping Tian, Jie Ning, Zhihao Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionMetastatic breast cancer is increasingly managed as a long-term condition, yet symptom burden and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) remain major patient-centred concerns. Electronic patient-reported outcome (ePRO) monitoring has shown promise in improving symptom surveillance and supporting more responsive care in oncology; however, evidence specifically relating to metastatic breast cancer remains fragmented and has not yet been systematically synthesised. Introduction

Metastatic breast cancer is increasingly managed as a long-term condition because advances in systemic therapy have extended survival for many patients.1–3 However, longer survival does not necessarily translate into better symptom control, functioning or health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and maintaining patient-centred outcomes has become an important priority alongside disease control.4 Recent reviews have shown that women with metastatic breast cancer continue to experience compromised HRQoL and substantial supportive care needs, indicating that quality of survival remains a major concern in this population.4–6

Patients with metastatic breast cancer often live with a persistent and multifaceted symptom burden throughout the disease trajectory and during ongoing systemic treatment.5 This burden commonly includes physical symptoms such as fatigue, pain, nausea, peripheral neuropathy and sleep disturbance, together with psychological distress associated with uncertainty, disease progression and the demands of ongoing treatment.7–9 These symptoms rarely occur in isolation; instead, they may accumulate or interact over time, increasing the overall burden experienced by patients.10 As symptom burden increases, patients may experience poorer physical functioning, greater limitations in daily activities and reduced participation in social and everyday roles.11 HRQoL, as a broader patient-centred construct, reflects the combined impact of these physical, psychological and social consequences.12 Accordingly, symptom burden and HRQoL should be regarded as two closely linked and clinically important outcomes in metastatic breast cancer, because worsening symptoms are likely to compromise broader perceptions of well-being and daily functioning.13

Electronic patient-reported outcome (ePRO) monitoring offers a structured way to capture symptoms and functional concerns directly from patients in real time and outside routine clinic visits.14–16 Compared with conventional follow-up alone, this approach may facilitate earlier recognition of deteriorating symptoms, support timely clinical responses, and promote more individualised symptom management.17 Evidence from broader oncology populations suggests that ePRO-based interventions can improve symptom burden and quality of life, particularly when monitoring is combined with tailored advice or clinician alert systems.18–20

Emerging evidence suggests that ePRO monitoring may offer clinical value for patients with metastatic breast cancer. Recent interventional and feasibility studies have indicated that this approach may support earlier symptom identification, improve selected dimensions of HRQoL and facilitate more responsive symptom management during systemic treatment.21–25 However, the currently available evidence remains fragmented, with substantial variation in patient populations, treatment settings, monitoring platforms, follow-up duration and outcome measurement. In addition, existing reviews have mainly focused on mixed cancer populations, broader ePRO applications or non-metastatic breast cancer, whereas evidence specifically addressing symptom burden and HRQoL in metastatic breast cancer has not yet been systematically synthesised.20 26 27 Therefore, a dedicated systematic review and meta-analysis is warranted to evaluate the effects of ePRO monitoring on symptom burden and HRQoL in patients with metastatic breast cancer.

Materials and methods

Study registration

This protocol has been registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) under registration number: CRD420261383449. Any important amendments to the protocol will be updated in the PROSPERO record and reported in the final systematic review and meta-analysis. The review protocol was prepared with reference to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) 2015 statement. The completed PRISMA-P checklist is provided in online supplemental S1 file.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval is not required for this systematic review and meta-analysis because it will synthesise data from previously published studies and will not involve the collection of primary data from human participants. No individual patient data will be obtained, and there will be no direct contact with patients or the public. The findings of this review will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presentation at relevant academic conferences. The results are expected to provide evidence to inform the design and implementation of ePRO monitoring for patients with metastatic breast cancer and to guide future research and clinical practice.

Eligibility and exclusion criteria

Population

This review will include studies involving adults (aged 18 years or older) diagnosed with metastatic breast cancer. Eligible participants may have either de novo metastatic breast cancer or recurrent metastatic breast cancer. Studies including mixed populations will be eligible only if data for patients with metastatic breast cancer can be extracted separately. Studies involving patients with non-metastatic breast cancer only, including localised or locally advanced non-metastatic disease, will be excluded. Studies including children or adolescents, non-human subjects, caregivers or healthcare professionals only will also be excluded.

Intervention

The intervention of interest in this review will be ePRO monitoring. Eligible studies must involve a digital system or platform through which patients with metastatic breast cancer repeatedly report patient-centred outcomes during treatment or follow-up, including symptoms, treatment-related adverse effects, functioning or HRQoL, using electronic devices such as smartphones, tablets, computers, web-based platforms or mobile applications. To be eligible, the intervention should represent an ongoing or longitudinal monitoring process rather than a one-time electronic assessment. ePRO monitoring may be delivered as a stand-alone intervention or as part of a broader care programme, provided that the ePRO component can be clearly identified. Because ePRO monitoring programmes may be complex interventions, additional components will be extracted and classified where reported, including automated reminders, threshold-triggered alerts, clinician review or feedback, healthcare professional follow-up, symptom triage, symptom management support and tailored self-management advice. Studies using paper-based patient-reported outcome measures only, one-off electronic surveys without subsequent monitoring or digital interventions without a clearly defined ePRO monitoring component will be excluded.

Comparison

The comparator of interest will be usual care or non-ePRO-based follow-up. Eligible comparator conditions may include routine oncology care, standard follow-up without ePRO monitoring, paper-based symptom or quality-of-life assessment, delayed or non-automated review of patient-reported data or other supportive care approaches that do not involve a clearly defined ePRO monitoring component. Studies comparing different modes or intensities of ePRO monitoring will also be considered, provided that the comparison allows evaluation of the relative effects of ePRO-based monitoring strategies on symptom burden or HRQoL. Studies without a clearly defined comparator will be excluded.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes of this review will be symptom burden and HRQoL. Symptom burden will be defined as patients’ self-reported severity, frequency or interference of disease-related or treatment-related symptoms, and will preferably be assessed using validated instruments, such as the Patient-Reported Outcomes version of the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events or other validated symptom measures.28–30 HRQoL will be defined as patients’ perceived overall well-being and functioning in relation to health, illness and treatment and will preferably be assessed using validated instruments, such as the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire Core 30 (EORTC QLQ-C30),31 the Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy-Breast (FACT-B),32 or other validated HRQoL measures.33 When multiple outcome measures are reported for the same construct, overall symptom burden scores and global HRQoL scores will be prioritised where available. For symptom burden, validated multisymptom overall or summary scores will be prioritised over individual symptom scores. For HRQoL, global HRQoL scores or total scores from validated cancer-specific or generic HRQoL instruments will be prioritised over domain-specific scores. If overall or global scores are unavailable, relevant domain-specific or symptom-specific outcomes will be extracted and reported separately. If more than one eligible measure remains after applying these criteria, the measure designated as primary in the original study will be selected; if no primary measure is specified, the measure listed first in the Methods section will be used. Secondary outcomes will include physical, role and social functioning, individual symptom domains, healthcare utilisation, treatment adherence or interruption, patient satisfaction and feasibility indicators.

Study design

This review will include comparative quantitative primary studies evaluating the effects of ePRO monitoring in patients with metastatic breast cancer. Eligible study designs will include randomised controlled trials and non-randomised controlled studies, provided that they compare ePRO monitoring with usual care, non-ePRO-based follow-up or another eligible comparator and report data on symptom burden, HRQoL or other relevant predefined outcomes. Randomised controlled trials will be considered the primary source of evidence for estimating intervention effects, whereas non-randomised controlled studies will be interpreted as complementary evidence, with attention to potential confounding, selection bias and other design-related limitations. Single-arm pilot or feasibility studies, uncontrolled before-and-after studies, cohort studies, analytical cross-sectional studies, case–control studies, qualitative studies, case reports, case series, conference abstracts without sufficient data, study protocols, reviews, editorials, commentaries and letters will be excluded.

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search will be conducted in PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, CINAHL and the Cochrane Library from database inception to the date of the final search. The planned literature search will be conducted from July 2026 to December 2026, and the actual final search date will be documented in the completed review. This manuscript reports the protocol for the planned systematic review and meta-analysis; the formal literature search, study selection and evidence synthesis have not yet been completed. To identify ongoing or unpublished studies, ClinicalTrials.gov and the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform will also be searched. The search strategy will combine controlled vocabulary terms and free text terms related to metastatic breast cancer, ePRO monitoring, symptom burden and HRQoL. The search strategies will be adapted for each database according to its specific indexing system and search interface. No restrictions will be imposed on publication year. Non-English records will be screened using translated titles and abstracts where available, and potentially eligible full texts will be translated or summarised using language-proficient reviewers or validated translation tools, with key eligibility and extraction decisions checked by two reviewers. In addition, the reference lists of all included studies and relevant reviews will be screened manually, and forward citation tracking will be undertaken where appropriate. The complete database-specific search strategies are provided in online supplemental S2 file.

Study selection

All records identified through the database search will be imported into reference management software (EndNote X9) for reference management and automatic duplicate removal. After automated deduplication, two reviewers will manually check the remaining records to identify and remove any additional duplicates before title and abstract screening. Two reviewers (HX and QL) will independently screen the titles and abstracts of all retrieved records according to the predefined eligibility and exclusion criteria. The full texts of eligible studies will then be obtained and assessed independently by the same two reviewers. Any disagreements arising during the screening process will be resolved through discussion, and, if necessary, by consultation with a third reviewer (HZ). Reasons for exclusion at the full-text screening stage will be recorded. The selection process will be presented in a PRISMA flow diagram (online supplemental figure S1).

Data extraction

Data from the included studies will be extracted independently by two reviewers (HX and WL) using a standardised data extraction form. Prior to formal extraction, the form will be pilot-tested on a small number of eligible studies and refined where necessary to ensure consistency and completeness. The following information will be extracted, where available: first author, year of publication, country, study design, study setting, sample size, participant characteristics, details of the intervention and comparator, outcome measures, follow-up duration, main findings relevant to symptom burden and HRQoL and information on funding sources or conflicts of interest. When a study reports multiple instruments, subscales or repeated assessments for the same outcome, all eligible measures and time points will be extracted. Any discrepancies between the two reviewers will be resolved through discussion, and, if necessary, by consultation with a third reviewer (HZ). If multiple reports relate to the same study, data will be collated and treated as a single study to avoid double counting.

Dealing with missing data

Where outcome data or other relevant information are missing, incomplete or unclear, attempts will be made to contact the corresponding authors of the included studies for clarification or additional data. If missing data cannot be obtained, the available data will be analysed as reported in the original studies, and the potential impact of missing information on the review findings will be considered in the interpretation of results. Studies will not be excluded solely because of missing data, unless the missing information precludes judgement of eligibility or extraction of key outcome data. Where appropriate, the extent and handling of missing data in the included studies will be recorded during data extraction and taken into account in the assessment of risk of bias and certainty of evidence.

Assessment of risk of bias

The risk of bias of the included studies will be assessed independently by two reviewers (HX and PT). For randomised controlled trials, the revised Cochrane Risk of Bias tool for randomised trials (RoB 2) will be used.34 For non-randomised controlled studies evaluating the effects of the intervention, the Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies of Interventions (ROBINS-I) tool will be applied.35 Any disagreements between the two reviewers will be resolved through discussion and, if necessary, by consultation with a third reviewer (HZ). The results of the risk-of-bias assessment will be presented in tables and figures.

Certainty of evidence assessment

The certainty of evidence for the primary outcomes and, where appropriate, selected secondary outcomes will be assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach.36 Two reviewers (HX and JN) will independently rate the certainty of the body of evidence for each outcome as high, moderate, low or very low. The assessment will consider the domains of risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. For non-randomised controlled studies, the certainty of evidence may also be rated up where appropriate, in accordance with GRADE guidance. Any disagreements between reviewers will be resolved through discussion and, if necessary, consultation with a third reviewer (HZ). Summary of findings tables will be prepared, where appropriate, to present the main findings and certainty ratings for the key outcomes.

Data synthesis

Data synthesis will be conducted in accordance with the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.37 Meta-analysis will be undertaken only when at least two studies are sufficiently comparable in terms of participants, ePRO intervention characteristics, comparator conditions, outcome definitions, measurement instruments, follow-up time points and study design. Randomised controlled trials and non-randomised controlled studies will be synthesised separately and will not be combined in the same meta-analysis. Randomised controlled trials will be considered the primary source of evidence for estimating intervention effects, whereas non-randomised controlled studies will be interpreted as complementary evidence, with attention to potential confounding, selection bias, baseline imbalance and other design-related limitations. For continuous outcomes, mean differences (MDs) with 95% CIs will be calculated when the same measurement instrument is used across studies, and standardised MDs (SMDs) with 95% CIs will be used when similar constructs are measured using different instruments. For dichotomous outcomes, risk ratios with 95% CIs will be calculated. If time-to-event outcomes are reported, HRs with 95% CIs will be used where available. For non-randomised controlled studies, adjusted effect estimates will be pooled only when they are sufficiently comparable in estimand, effect measure and adjustment for key confounders. Adjusted and unadjusted estimates will not be combined in the same meta-analysis; unadjusted estimates will be synthesised separately or narratively. For the primary outcomes, outcome measures will be selected according to the prespecified hierarchy described in the Outcomes section. Where both change-from-baseline and postintervention values are available, change-from-baseline data will be prioritised. Follow-up time will be calculated from initiation of the ePRO intervention; if this is not reported, baseline will be used. Follow-up will be classified as short-term (≤3 months), medium-term (>3 to 6 months) and longer term (>6 months). When multiple assessments are reported within the same category, the time point closest to 3, 6 and 12 months will be selected for the short-term, medium-term and long-term analyses, respectively; if two time points are equally close, the later one will be selected. Each study will contribute one effect estimate per outcome and time-point category. If multiple eligible arms represent the same intervention category, they will be combined using standard formulae. If distinct eligible comparisons share a comparator, the shared comparator will be divided appropriately across comparisons to avoid double counting. For continuous outcomes reported as medians with IQRs or ranges, or with SEs, CIs or P values, means and/or SD will be estimated or derived where appropriate. If conversion is not feasible or data remain insufficient after contacting the study authors, the findings will be synthesised narratively.

Given the anticipated clinical and methodological heterogeneity across studies, random-effects meta-analyses will use the restricted maximum likelihood estimator to estimate between-study variance, and 95% CIs for pooled effects will be calculated using the Hartung-Knapp method. Results based on a small number of studies will be interpreted cautiously. Statistical heterogeneity will be assessed using the χ2 test and the I2 statistic. Where substantial heterogeneity is identified, subgroup analyses will be conducted only when sufficient studies are available within each subgroup and when the subgroup variable is clinically and methodologically meaningful. Potential subgroup analyses may be considered according to study design, ePRO intervention components, comparator type, outcome measurement instrument and follow-up duration and will be interpreted cautiously, particularly when the number of studies in each subgroup is limited. Sensitivity analyses will be performed, where possible, by excluding studies at high risk of bias, studies with substantial methodological limitations, and non-randomised controlled studies to examine whether the findings are robust when restricted to studies with stronger methodological designs or lower risk of bias. Meta-regression will be considered only when substantial heterogeneity remains after planned subgroup analyses, at least 10 studies are available in a meta-analysis for the covariate of interest, and sufficient between-study variation exists in that covariate. If these conditions are not met, meta-regression will not be conducted. If meta-analysis is not appropriate because of considerable clinical, methodological or statistical heterogeneity, insufficient comparable data or serious concerns about risk of bias, the findings will be synthesised narratively in a structured manner. The narrative synthesis will consider study design, intervention and comparator characteristics, outcome measurement, follow-up time points, direction and consistency of effects and risk of bias when interpreting the findings. Publication bias will be explored using funnel plots and, where appropriate, statistical tests for funnel plot asymmetry when at least 10 studies are included in a meta-analysis. Meta-analysis will be conducted using RevMan V.5.4.1 or other appropriate statistical software. Summary of findings tables will be prepared for the primary outcomes.

Discussion

In metastatic breast cancer, symptom burden and HRQoL remain central concerns despite continuing advances in systemic treatment. Although therapeutic progress has extended survival for many patients, recent evidence indicates that women living with metastatic breast cancer continue to experience compromised HRQoL, persistent physical and psychological symptom burden and substantial unmet supportive care needs.4 5 In this context, ePRO monitoring is of particular interest because it may offer a more patient-centred approach to longitudinal care by enabling structured and ongoing assessment of symptoms and functioning during treatment and follow-up.38–40 Accordingly, synthesising the available evidence on ePRO monitoring in this population is both clinically relevant and timely.

This review is needed because the current evidence base remains fragmented and only partially applicable to metastatic breast cancer. Existing systematic reviews have mainly focused on mixed cancer populations, broader ePRO applications or non-metastatic breast cancer, rather than specifically examining the effects of ePRO monitoring on symptom burden and HRQoL in metastatic breast cancer.19 20 26 27 Previous reviews have also tended to emphasise overall implementation, feasibility or general effectiveness across diverse oncology settings, which may not fully capture the specific clinical and supportive care needs of patients living with metastatic disease.41–43 By focusing on a clearly defined population, intervention and patient-centred outcomes, the present review may provide a more targeted and clinically meaningful synthesis, help clarify the consistency of current findings and identify whether particular intervention features are associated with more favourable outcomes in this setting.

The present review may contribute to the field in several important ways. The use of a predefined protocol, a comprehensive search strategy, design-specific risk-of-bias assessment, structured synthesis methods and certainty-of-evidence evaluation should strengthen the transparency and methodological rigour of the review process. Importantly, this review may help clarify the current state of evidence on ePRO monitoring in metastatic breast cancer, identify key gaps in intervention design, follow-up duration and outcome measurement and support the development of more consistent and clinically relevant research in this field.

Limitation

Several limitations of this review should be acknowledged. First, differences across included studies in intervention characteristics, monitoring processes, treatment settings, follow-up time points and outcome assessment approaches may affect the comparability of findings and should be considered when interpreting the results. Second, although this review will include only studies with a comparator, non-randomised controlled studies may still be affected by confounding, selection bias and baseline imbalance, and causal inferences from these studies should therefore be made cautiously. Third, reporting quality in the original studies, including incomplete outcome reporting or insufficient methodological detail, may affect the assessment of risk of bias and certainty of evidence. Fourth, differences in ePRO intervention components, such as alerts, clinician feedback, healthcare professional follow-up, symptom management support and self-management advice, may influence the observed effects and contribute to heterogeneity across studies. Therefore, the findings of this review will need to be interpreted in light of the characteristics and reporting quality of the available evidence.

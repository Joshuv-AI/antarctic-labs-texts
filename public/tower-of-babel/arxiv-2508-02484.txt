Simply connectedness of spaces of tight frames
Augustin-Liviu Mare

Abstract
. Complex tight frames can be canonically viewed as elements of a complex Stiefel manifold. We present a class of spaces of such frames which are simply connected relative to the subspace topology. To this class belongs the space of finite unit-norm tight frames.



1.
Statement of the main result

Let
0\leq k\leq n
be integers. A
normalized tight frame
is a matrix
F\in{\rm Mat}_{k\times n}(\mathbb{C})
whose product with its conjugate transpose
F^{*}
is

(1)

FF^{*}={\rm I}_{k}.

For more insight on this notion, see the monograph
[
16
]
.
Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
be column vectors, such that
F=[\ f_{1}\ |\ \ldots\ |\ f_{n}\ ]
.
Given
d=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose components are all non-negative, we denote by
{\mathcal{F}}_{n,k}^{d}
the collection of all normalized tight frames
F
with the property that

\|f_{j}\|^{2}=d_{j},\ {\rm for\ all\ }1\leq j\leq n,

the norm being the standard one.
For this space to be non-empty, it is necessary and sufficient that

\displaystyle 0\leq d_{j}\leq 1,\ {\rm for\ all\ }1\leq j\leq n,

\displaystyle d_{1}+\cdots+d_{n}=k,

see Rem.
2.1
below.
Denote by
\Delta_{n,k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the polytope described by the equations above.
It was proved in
[
14
]
that for any
d\in\Delta_{n,k}
, the space
{\mathcal{F}}_{n,k}^{d}
is path connected
relative to the topology of subspace of
{\rm Mat}_{k\times n}(\mathbb{C})
. The main result of this note is:

Theorem 1.1
.

If
d=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})\in\Delta_{n,k}
such that

(2)

d_{i_{1}}+\cdots+d_{i_{n-k}}\geq 1\ {\it for\ any\ pairwise\ distinct}\ i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-k}\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

then
{\mathcal{F}}_{n,k}^{d}

is a simply connected topological subspace of
{\rm Mat}_{k\times n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Remark 1.2
.

If assumption
(
2
) is satisfied, then
2\leq k\leq n-2
, as shown in
[
12
, Rem. 1.3]
. Also see Ex.
5.1
below.

Example 1.3
.

Given
n
and
k
such that
2\leq k\leq n-2
, there exists
d\in\Delta_{n,k}
which satisfies
assumption (
2
), namely

d=\left(\frac{k}{n},\ldots,\frac{k}{n}\right).

The corresponding frames have been previously investigated by several authors, although in a slightly different presentation,
being called
unit-norm
or
spherical
tight frames, see for instance
[
5
]
and
[
3
]
.

The proof of Thm.
1.1
which is presented below uses a certain relationship between the space
{\mathcal{F}}_{n,k}^{d}

and the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional complex vector subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The latter is coming equipped with a canonical symplectic form and a Hamiltonian torus action, the image of the moment map being just the polytope
\Delta_{n,k}
above.
To achieve our goal, we first show that the pre-image under the moment map of any
d\in\Delta_{n,k}
which satisfies assumption (
2
) is a simply connected subspace of the Grassmannian, see Thm.
3.1
below. It remains to deduce that
{\mathcal{F}}_{n,k}^{d}
is simply connected as well, which can be done due to its canonical structure of a
{\rm U}(k)
-principal bundle over the pre-image above.
Results from symplectic geometry are fundamental ingredients here. Connections between this branch of mathematics and frame theory were for the first time pointed out in the aforementioned work
[
14
]
of Needham and Shonkwiler. Recently, in
[
4
]
, further progress in studying the topology of
{\mathcal{F}}_{n,k}^{d}

has been made by the same two authors, this time together with Caine, by identifying points
d\in\Delta_{n,k}
for which certain higher order homotopy groups of this space vanish. More precisely, this is the content of
[
4
, Thm. 4.5]
, and from this, simply connectedness criteria can immediately be deduced. However, they will be different from the one given by Thm.
1.1
above.
Similarities between
[
4
, Sect. 4]
and the present note can also be noticed concerning the methods used, see also Rem.
4.4
below.

Before starting the proof, we would like to mention that the manifolds in this note are all going to be smooth and submanifolds will always be embedded.
As a first step, we will set the stage by introducing the Stiefel and the Grassmann manifolds and making some considerations which will turn out to be useful later on.

2.
Frames via the Stiefel and the Grassmann manifolds

We start by endowing
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with the usual Hermitean inner product, that is,
\langle u,v\rangle=uv^{*}
, for all
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Recall that the Stiefel manifold

{\rm V}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
consists of all sequences of
k
orthonormal vectors in

\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Any element of this space can be identified with the
k\times n
matrix
F
whose rows are exactly the
k
vectors.
In this way,
{\rm V}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is exactly the space of all normalized tight frames, that is, of matrices
F\in{\rm Mat}_{k\times n}(\mathbb{C})

which satisfy condition (
1
).

To any
k
-tuple of orthonormal vectors one attaches their span, which is a
k
-dimensional complex vector subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
and obtains in this way a map from
{\rm V}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
to the Grassmannian
{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
of all such subspaces.
This map is a principal
{\rm U}(k)
-bundle, where the unitary group
{\rm U}(k)
acts by matrix multiplication from the left on the Stiefel manifold.
Let us now identify any vector space
V
in
{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
with the linear endomorphism
\pi_{V}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
which represents the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
onto
V
with respect to the inner product
mentioned above. The matrix of this endomorphism relative to the canonical basis is Hermitean and, if

{\rm Herm}_{n}
denotes the space of all such
n\times n
complex matrices, one obtains

(3)

{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\{P\in{\rm Herm}_{n}\mid P^{2}=P,\ {\rm tr}(P)=k\}.

Like in Sect.
1
, let
d=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a vector with all
d_{j}\geq 0
. Then
{\mathcal{F}}_{n,k}^{d}
is a subspace of

{\rm V}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, clearly
{\rm U}(k)
-invariant. We wish to describe its image under the principal bundle map
mentioned above. To this end, we attach to any matrix
P\in{\rm Herm}_{n}
the vector
\mu(P)
, which consists of the diagonal entries of
P
, and obtain in this way a map
\mu:{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The principal bundle map
{\rm V}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
can be described as
F\mapsto F^{*}F
, for all
F\in{\rm V}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
and consequently it transforms
{\mathcal{F}}_{n,k}^{d}
into the pre-image
\mu^{-1}(d)
.
By also taking into account the topologies inherited from
{\rm Mat}_{k\times n}(\mathbb{C})
and
{\rm Herm}_{n}
, one obtains a
homeomorphism

(4)

{\mathcal{F}}_{n,k}^{d}/{\rm U}(k)\simeq\mu^{-1}(d).

Remark 2.1
.

As a special case of the Schur-Horn theorem, see
[
15
]
and
[
9
]
, the image of
\mu:{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is just the polytope

\Delta_{n,k}
defined in Sect.
1
(see also
[
2
, Sect. 2.2]
). This explains why
{\mathcal{F}}_{n,k}^{d}
is non-empty exactly if
d
lies in this polytope.

In order to prove Thm.
1.1
, we will now use the homeomorphism (
4
), combined with the knowledge of
the first two homotopy groups of
\mu^{-1}(d)
. These will be computed in the next two sections.

3.
Homotopy groups of
\mu^{-1}(d)

In this section we will make the first steps towards proving the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
d=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})\in\Delta_{n,k}

2

then the first two homotopy groups of

\mu^{-1}(d)

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(\mu^{-1}(d))=\{0\}

Due to its rich structure, the Grassmannian mentioned in the previous section will be for us a useful tool. It admits a presentation as an adjoint

{\rm U}(n)

Let us now consider

(5)

\langle X,Y\rangle={\rm tr}(XY),

X,Y\in{\rm Herm}_{n},

which is clearly invariant under the adjoint action of
{\rm U}(n)

Like any adjoint orbit of a compact Lie group,
{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
can be equipped with a Kähler structure.
The space of all diagonal matrices in
{\rm U}(n)
is an
n
-torus, call it
T

Hamiltonian, and the corresponding moment map is just
\mu:{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to{\mathfrak{t}}

(
{\mathfrak{t}}
denotes the Lie algebra of
T
, which is naturally identified with
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
).
Strictly speaking, the moment map takes values in the dual space,
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}

{\mathfrak{t}}
by means of the

5

We will investigate the pre-image
\mu^{-1}(d)
by using results obtained by Kirwan in
[
10

.
The main tool is the “norm squared of the moment map”, that is the function

f:{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}

f(V)=\|\mu(V)-d\|^{2},\ V\in{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),

where the norm is the one arising from the standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
More concretely, we will use results from
[
10

One can see there that a prominent role is played by real-valued functions on the symplectic manifold which are obtained by pairing the moment map
\mu(\cdot)-d

{\mathfrak{t}}

3

4.
Proof of Theorems

and

We need some preparation before being able to actually start proving the theorems.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume

satisfies assumption (
2

and

Proof.

By Thm.
3.6
(i),
\Sigma_{a,u}
and

have the same codimension.
By Rem.
3.3

This can be proved by contradiction, using the same method as in
[

]

\{V\in{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\mid\dim V\cap E=c\}

[

Recall now that, by definition, a continuous map
f:(X,x_{0})\to(Y,y_{0})
between two pointed topological spaces is

f_{*}:\pi_{k}(X,x_{0})\to\pi_{k}(Y,y_{0})
is an isomorphism for

and is surjective for

Lemma 4.2
.

(B. Williams)
Let

Z\subset M

. Then the inclusion map

M\setminus Z\hookrightarrow M
is
(d-1)
-connected.

Note that

can be a finite union of connected closed submanifolds,

being the minimal codimension of a component.

This will be used in proving the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

If

\mu^{-1}(d)\hookrightarrow{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

Proof.

The map mentioned in the proposition can be decomposed as

\mu^{-1}(d)\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{(0,\ldots,0),u}\hookrightarrow{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),

where

is an arbitrary subset with

By Thm.
3.6

We will now analyze the other inclusion map, which is
\Sigma_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, see again
Thm.
3.6
(iii). We clearly have

(17)

\Sigma_{\beta_{0}}={\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\setminus\bigcup_{\beta\neq\beta_{0}}\Sigma_{\beta}.

Let
\beta^{1}_{1},\ldots,\beta^{1}_{m_{1}}
be the minimal elements of the indexing set
B
. By eq. (
15
), the corresponding

\Sigma

{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Prop.

and Lemma

imply that the map

{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m_{1}}\Sigma_{\beta_{i}^{1}}\hookrightarrow{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

is 3-connected. Let now
\beta^{2}_{1},\ldots,\beta^{2}_{m_{2}}
be the minimal elements of

\Sigma_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow{\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

is a composition of several 3-connected maps and thus is itself 3-connected.
∎

We are now ready to achieve the two main goals of this section:

Proof of Theorems
3.1
and
1.1
.

\displaystyle\pi_{2}({\rm Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))\simeq\mathbb{Z},

Remark 4.4
.

As noticed in the introduction, there are similarities between this work and
[
4
]
, also with regard to the methods used in proving the main results. More precisely, for both
[
4
, Thm. 4.5]
and Thm. 3.1 above, homotopy groups of a subspace of a well-understood manifold are computed by means of a stratification of the manifold, to which belongs a unique open stratum, where the latter contains the subspace one is interested in as a deformation retract, the crucial ingredient being that all the non-open strata have sufficiently high codimensions. This general idea goes back to Kirwan
[
10
]
, and was already used in the context of frames in
[
12
]
.

5.
Examples

Example 5.1
.

The case when
n=2
and
k=1
can be easily understood. First,
{\rm Gr}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})=\mathbb{C}P^{1}
is a 2-sphere and

\mu
can be viewed as the orthogonal projection of the sphere on one of its diameters. Its levels are almost all non-degenerate circles and hence not simply connected. The Stiefel manifold

{\rm V}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is just the unit sphere

S^{3}
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and

\Delta_{2,1}=\{(d_{1},d_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid d_{1},d_{2}\geq 0,d_{1}+d_{2}=1\}.

Consequently,
{\mathcal{F}}_{2,1}^{d}
are products of two circles and consequently not simply connected.

Example 5.2
.

For
n=4
and
k=2
we consider
d=(1,1,0,0)
. Let
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
be, as before, the standard Hermitean inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
. Then
\mu^{-1}(d)
consists of all complex
2-planes
V
in
\mathbb{C}^{4}
such that

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{V}(e_{1}),e_{1}\rangle=1

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{V}(e_{2}),e_{2}\rangle=1

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{V}(e_{3}),e_{3}\rangle=0

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{V}(e_{4}),e_{4}\rangle=0.

The first two conditions show that
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are in
V
, in other words, that
V
is the span of
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
.
So in this case
\mu^{-1}(d)
is just a point in
{\rm Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{4})
.
Since
{\mathcal{F}}_{4,2}^{(1,1,0,0)}
is acted freely by
{\rm U}(2)
, the orbit being just a point, it must be homeomorphic to

{\rm U}(2)
.
Thus
{\mathcal{F}}_{4,2}^{(1,1,0,0)}
is not simply connected. As expected,
d=(1,1,0,0)
is not satisfying assumption (
2
).

Example 5.3
.

Again for
n=4
and
k=2
, we now take
d=(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},1)
. This time the 2-planes
V
in
\mu^{-1}(d)
are determined by:

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{V}(e_{1}),e_{1}\rangle=\frac{1}{3}

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{V}(e_{2}),e_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{3}

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{V}(e_{3}),e_{3}\rangle=\frac{1}{3}

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{V}(e_{4}),e_{4}\rangle=1.

From the last condition,
e_{4}\in V
. So
V
is uniquely determined by its quotient
V/\mathbb{C}e_{4}
,
which is a line, say
\ell
, in
\mathbb{C}^{4}/\mathbb{C}e_{4}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{3}
such that

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{\ell}(e_{1}),e_{1}\rangle=\frac{1}{3}

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{\ell}(e_{2}),e_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{3}

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{\ell}(e_{3}),e_{3}\rangle=\frac{1}{3}.

Now
\ell
is in
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
and we write it as
\ell=\mathbb{C}v
, where
v=(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
,
|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}+|c|^{2}=1
.
Then
\pi_{\ell}(e_{1})=\overline{a}v
,
\pi_{\ell}(e_{2})=\overline{b}v
, and
\pi_{\ell}(e_{3})=\overline{c}v
,
thus the three equations above are equivalent to

|a|=|b|=|c|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}.

In conclusion,
\mu^{-1}(d)
is the quotient of
S^{1}\times S^{1}\times S^{1}
by the diagonal action of
S^{1}
, thus a 2-torus, which is not simply connected.
Let us now consider the following piece of the long exact homotopy sequence of the
{\rm U}(2)
-principal bundle

{\mathcal{F}}_{4,2}^{(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},1)}\to\mu^{-1}(d)
:

\pi_{1}({\mathcal{F}}_{4,2}^{(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},1)})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(\mu^{-1}(d))\longrightarrow\pi_{0}({\rm U}(2))=\{0\}.

Since
\pi_{1}(\mu^{-1}(d))\neq\{0\}
, it follows that
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{F}}_{4,2}^{(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},1)})
is non-zero as well.

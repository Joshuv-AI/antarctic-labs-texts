Controllability of induced bilinear systems on the sphere
Marco A. Colque-Choquecallata 1 , Efrain Cruz-Mullisaca 1 , Victor H. Patty-Yujra 1

Abstract




1
Instituto de investigación Matemática, Universidad Mayor de San Andrés, Bolivia

Abstract

In this paper, we investigate the controllability of bilinear control systems of the form
\dot{s}=As+uBs
, where
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
A,B\in gl(3,\mathbb{R})
are skew-symmetric matrices. First, we prove that the algebraic condition
[A,B]\neq 0
ensures that the Lie algebra rank condition is satisfied for these systems. Next, we show that this same condition implies the controllability of the system. Finally, in an explicit and descriptive manner, we demonstrate controllability by exhibiting trajectories that transfer a given initial state to another.

Keywords:

Controllability; Bilinear systems; Lie algebra rank condition.

1.
Introduction

Consider a bilinear control system in
\mathbb{R}^{3},
with control
u\in\mathbb{R},
given by

(1)

\Sigma:\hskip 21.68121pt\dot{x}=Ax+uBx,\hskip 21.68121ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\smallsetminus\{0\},

where
A,B\in gl(3,\mathbb{R}),
are
3\times 3
matrices with real coefficients. The system
\Sigma
can be projected radially onto the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}:
for
x(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\smallsetminus\{0\},
solution of (
1
), writing
s(t):=x(t)/|x(t)|\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
we get the
induced bilinear system
on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
given by

(2)

\dot{s}=h_{A}(s)+uh_{B}(s),\hskip 14.45377pts\in\mathbb{S}^{2},u\in\mathbb{R},

where, for instance,
h_{A}(s)=As-\left\langle As,s\right\rangle s,
for all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.
If
A
and
B
are skew-symmetric, we have
h_{A}(s)=As
and
h_{B}(s)=Bs,
for all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2},
thus, the induced bilinear system (
2
) remains as

(3)

\mathbb{P}\Sigma:\hskip 21.68121pt\dot{s}=As+uBs,\hskip 14.45377pts\in\mathbb{S}^{2},u\in\mathbb{R}.

The main goal of this paper is to study the controllability problem of the induced bilinear system
\mathbb{P}\Sigma
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}.
Using techniques from Lie Theory, it is shown that the control system (
3
) is controllable on
\mathbb{S}^{2},
see
[
2
,
7
]
; however, our approach in this paper is descriptive, that is, we will solve the controllability problem by making explicit the trajectories of the system that transfer a certain initial state to another.

We quote the following related papers: in
[
3
, Corollary 12.2.6]
it is shown that the controllability of (
2
) is a necessary condition for the controllability of (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\smallsetminus\{0\},
this motivates the present paper. The controllability problem of bilinear systems in the plane was studied by the second author
et al
in
[
1
]
, however, in this work the study of the Lie algebra rank condition of the system was not contemplated; the second and third author in
[
6
]
obtain an algebraic condition that ensures the range condition and with elementary techniques recover the main result of
[
1
]
.

The outline of the paper is as follows: in Section
2
we will study the Lie algebra rank condition for the induced bilinear system (
2
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
(Theorem
2.1
); in particular, in Theorem
2.2
we give an algebraic condition that ensures the range condition for the system
\mathbb{P}\Sigma.
In Section
3
we study the controllability problem for the induced bilinear system
\mathbb{P}\Sigma:
we will prove in Theorem
3.1
that the hypothesis that ensures the range condition also implies the controllability of
\mathbb{P}\Sigma.
Finally, we give an illustrative example of the technique used in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

2.
Lie algebra rank condition for induced bilinear systems

It is well known that a necessary condition for the controllability of the induced bilinear system (
2
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is given by the
Lie algebra rank condition
(LARC): if we denote by
\chi(\mathbb{S}^{2})
the Lie algebra of differentiable vector fields on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
then, the Lie algebra generated by the system, given by

\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}:=\langle h_{A}+uh_{B}\mid u\in\mathbb{R}\rangle\subset\chi(\mathbb{S}^{2}),

is such that for all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2},
the vector space
\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}(s)\subset T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2}
satisfies

(4)

\dim\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}(s)=\dim\langle h_{A}(s)+uh_{B}(s)\mid u\in\mathbb{R}\rangle=2;

see for example
[
3
,
5
]
.

In this section, we will study the Lie algebra rank condition for the induced bilinear system (
2
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2};
in particular, the rank condition for the system (
3
). We need the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

The zeros of the induced vector field
h_{A}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2},
with
A\in gl(3,\mathbb{R}),
follows the direction of the eigenvectors of the matrix
A.

Proof.

Using the Lagrange identity we have
|h_{A}(s)|=|As\times s|,
for all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2},
where
\times
denotes the usual cross product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}.
Thus,
h_{A}(s)=0
if and only if
As=\lambda s,
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

∎

We consider the usual Lie bracket of matrices
[A,B]=AB-BA,
for
A
and
B
in
gl(3,\mathbb{R}).
We have the following interesting relation.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
A
and
B
in
gl(3,\mathbb{R})
we have

h_{[A,B]}=[h_{A},h_{B}],

where
[h_{A},h_{B}]=dh_{A}(h_{B})-dh_{B}(h_{A})
denotes the Lie bracket on
\chi(\mathbb{S}^{2}).

Proof.

By a straightforward computation, for all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
v\in T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2},
we have

d(h_{A})_{s}(v)=Av-\langle Av,s\rangle s-\langle As,v\rangle s-\langle As,s\rangle v;

therefore
d(h_{A})_{s}(h_{B}(s))-d(h_{B})_{s}(h_{A}(s))=(AB-BA)s-\langle(AB-BA)s,s\rangle s=h_{[A,B]}

like we wanted.
∎

Proposition 2.1
.

The map
\Phi:gl(3,\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow\chi(\mathbb{S}^{2}),
given by
\Phi(A)=h_{A}

is a homomorphism of Lie algebras. In particular, if
Id\in gl(3,\mathbb{R})
denotes the identity matrix, we have
h_{Id}=0.

Proof.

For all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
we have

h_{A+uB}(s)=h_{A}(s)+uh_{B}(s)
and,
using Lemma
2.2
we get

\Phi[A,B]=h_{[A,B]}=[h_{A},h_{B}]=[\Phi(A),\Phi(B)].

∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be in
gl(3,\mathbb{R}),

x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\smallsetminus\{0\}
and write
s:=x/|x|.
Then,
\{x,Ax,Bx\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is linearly independent if and only if
\left\{h_{A}\left(s\right),h_{B}\left(s\right)\right\}\subset T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2}
is linearly independent.

Proof.

We suppose that
\{x,Ax,Bx\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is linearly independent, without loss of generality, there exist
\alpha,\beta
such that
Bx=(\alpha Id+\beta A)x,
thus

h_{B}(s)=\alpha h_{Id}(s)+\beta h_{A}(s)=\beta h_{A}(s),

i.e.,

h_{A}(s)
and
h_{B}(s)
are linearly dependent in
T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2}.
Reciprocally, if
\left\{h_{A}\left(s\right),h_{B}\left(s\right)\right\}\subset T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2}
is linearly dependent,
i.e.,
if the exists
\lambda
such that
h_{A}(s)=\lambda h_{B}(s),
we get
h_{A-\lambda B}(s)=0;
using Lemma
2.1
, there exists
\gamma
with
(A-\lambda B)s=\gamma s,
therefore
As=\gamma s+\lambda Bs,

i.e.,

s,As
and
Bs
are linearly dependent.
∎

Observation 2.1
.

If
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\smallsetminus\{0\}
is a eigenvector of
A
or
B,
the set
\{x_{0},Ax_{0},Bx_{0}\}
is linearly dependent; according to Proposition
2.2
, the set
\{h_{A}(s),h_{B}(s)\}\subset T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2}
is linearly dependent in
s=x_{0}/|x_{0}|\in\mathbb{S}^{2};
in this case, we note that
h_{A}(s)=0
or
h_{B}(s)=0,
thus, it is necessary to consider an additional direction in the vector space
T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2}.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2},
the vector space
\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}(s)\subset T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2}
it is generated by the set

\mathcal{H}:=\left\{h_{A}(s),\ h_{B}(s),\ h_{[A,B]}(s),\ h_{[A,[A,B]]}(s),\ h_{[B,[A,B]]}(s),\ldots\right\}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}(s)\subset T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2}
is generated by the vectors
Z_{u}(s)=h_{A}(s)+uh_{B}(s)\in T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2},
with
u\in\mathbb{R},
taking
u=0,
we have the direction
h_{A}(s);
moreover, for all
u\neq v
in
\mathbb{R},
we have the direction
Z_{u}(s)-Z_{v}(s)=(u-v)h_{B}(s).
On the other hand, using Proposition
2.1
, the Lie bracket of
Z_{u}=h_{A}+uh_{B}
and
Z_{v}=h_{A}+vh_{B},
in
\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma},
satisfies

\displaystyle[Z_{u},Z_{v}]=[h_{A}+uh_{B},h_{A}+vh_{B}]=(v-u)[h_{A},h_{B}]=(v-u)h_{[A,B]};

thus, the Lie bracket of
Z_{u}
and
Z_{v},
in
s,
follows the direction of
h_{[A,B]}(s).
Therefore, all linear combinations of
Z_{u},
with
u\in\mathbb{R},
and the Lie brackets of them, are generated by
\mathcal{H}.

∎

According to Lemma
2.3
, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

The induced bilinear system (
2
) satisfies the Lie algebra rank condition (LARC) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
if

\mbox{rank}\begin{pmatrix}h_{A}(s)&h_{B}(s)&h_{[A,B]}(s)\end{pmatrix}=2,

for all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.
In particular, if
[A,B]=0,
the bilinear system (
2
) does not satisfy LARC.

Proof.

The condition ensures the linear independence of two vectors in
T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2},
for all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.
If
[A,B]=0,
we have
h_{[A,B]}=h_{[A,[A,B]]}=h_{[B,[A,B]]}=\cdots=0;
by Observation
2.1
, the system (
2
) does not satisfy LARC.
∎

Example 2.1
.

We consider the bilinear control system
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\smallsetminus\{0\}
given by

\dot{x}=Ax+uBx=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
-1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}x+u\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&-1&0\end{pmatrix}x.

Writing
s=\begin{pmatrix}s_{1}&s_{2}&s_{3}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\in\mathbb{S}^{2},
by a straightforward computation, the induced bilinear system is given by

\dot{s}=h_{A}(s)+uh_{B}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}s_{2}\\
-s_{1}\\
0\end{pmatrix}+u\begin{pmatrix}0\\
s_{3}\\
-s_{2}\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ s\in\mathbb{S}^{2},\ \ u\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
[A,B]=AB-BA=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
-1&0&0\end{pmatrix},
we have

h_{[A,B]}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}s_{3}\\
0\\
-s_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

Finally, since
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2},
without loss of generality, we can assume that
s_{2}\neq 0,
thus

\displaystyle\mbox{rank}\begin{pmatrix}h_{A}(s)&h_{B}(s)&h_{[A,B]}(s)\end{pmatrix}=\mbox{rank}\begin{pmatrix}s_{2}&0&s_{3}\\
-s_{1}&s_{3}&0\\
0&-s_{2}&-s_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\mbox{rank}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\frac{s_{3}}{s_{2}}\\
0&1&\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}=2;

according to Theorem
2.1
, the induced bilinear system satisfies the rank condition on
\mathbb{S}^{2}.

Example 2.2
.

We consider the bilinear control system
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\smallsetminus\{0\}
given by

\dot{x}=Ax+uBx=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}x+u\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}x.

Writing as before
s=\begin{pmatrix}s_{1}&s_{2}&s_{3}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\in\mathbb{S}^{2},
the induced bilinear system remains as

\dot{s}=h_{A}(s)+uh_{B}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}s_{2}-s_{1}^{2}s_{2}\\
-s_{1}s_{2}^{2}\\
-s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\end{pmatrix}+u\begin{pmatrix}s_{3}-s_{1}^{2}s_{3}\\
-s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\\
-s_{1}s_{3}^{2}\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ s\in\mathbb{S}^{2},\ \ u\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
[A,B]=0,
the induced system does not satisfy LARC: we have
h_{A}(s)=h_{B}(s)=0
in
s=(1,0,0)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

We will prove that if the matrices
A
and
B
are skew-symmetric, the induced bilinear system
\mathbb{P}\Sigma
given in (
3
) satisfies LARC on
\mathbb{S}^{2};
we need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
A\in gl(3,\mathbb{R})
is skew-symmetric, there exist
a>0
and
P\in gl(3,\mathbb{R})
orthogonal such that

\mathcal{J}(A):=P^{-1}AP=\begin{pmatrix}0&a&0\\
-a&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

We suppose that
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{1}&a_{2}\\
-a_{1}&0&a_{3}\\
-a_{2}&-a_{3}&0\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.
If
a_{2}=0
and
a_{3}=0,
taking
P=Id
we get the result; we can suppose that
\alpha:=a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}\neq 0.
Writing
a:=\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}},
the characteristic polynomial of
A
is given by

p(\lambda)=\det(\lambda I-A)=\lambda(\lambda^{2}+a^{2}),
thus,
\lambda_{1,2}=ia
and
\lambda_{3}=0
are its eigenvalues.
The associated eigenvectors are given by

v_{1}=\dfrac{1}{a\sqrt{\alpha}}\begin{pmatrix}-a_{1}a_{3}\\
a_{1}a_{2}\\
\alpha\end{pmatrix},\quad v_{2}=\dfrac{-1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}\\
a_{3}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad v_{3}=\dfrac{1}{a}\begin{pmatrix}a_{3}\\
-a_{2}\\
a_{1}\end{pmatrix},

which form an orthonormal set. The matrix
P:=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}&v_{2}&v_{3}\end{pmatrix}
is orthogonal and satisfies the equality described above
∎

Observation 2.2
.

We consider the bilinear control system
\Sigma,
given in (
1
), with
A
and
B
skew-symmetric, and the matrix
P
given in Lemma
2.4
. Writing
y=P^{-1}x,
we get

\dot{y}=P^{-1}\dot{x}=P^{-1}(A+uB)x=P^{-1}(A+uB)Py=P^{-1}APy+uP^{-1}BPy;

the matrix
\tilde{B}:=P^{-1}BP
is skew-symmetric, thus, we have the equivalent bilinear control system

\dot{y}=\mathcal{J}(A)y+u\tilde{B}y.

Moreover, since

[\mathcal{J}(A),\tilde{B}]=[P^{-1}AP,P^{-1}BP]=P^{-1}[A,B]P,

we have
[\mathcal{J}(A),\tilde{B}]\neq 0
if and only if
[A,B]\neq 0.
Therefore, it is sufficient to study the Lie algebra rank condition of induced bilinear systems of the form

(5)

\mathbb{P}\Sigma:\hskip 21.68121pt\dot{s}=As+uBs=\begin{pmatrix}0&a&0\\
-a&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}s+u\begin{pmatrix}0&b_{1}&b_{2}\\
-b_{1}&0&b_{3}\\
-b_{2}&-b_{3}&0\end{pmatrix}s,\hskip 21.68121pta>0,\ s\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

Finally, in this case note that:
[A,B]\neq 0
if and only if
b_{2}^{2}+b_{3}^{2}\neq 0.

Theorem 2.2
.

The induced bilinear system
\mathbb{P}\Sigma:

\dot{s}=As+uBs,
given by (
5
), satisfies the Lie algebra rank condition on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
if
[A,B]\neq 0.

Proof.

By a straightforward computation, we have

[A,B]=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&ab_{3}\\
0&0&-ab_{2}\\
-ab_{3}&ab_{2}&0\end{pmatrix};

thus, for all
s=(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
we get

h_{A}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}as_{2}\\
-as_{1}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\hskip 21.68121pth_{B}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}s_{2}+b_{2}s_{3}\\
-b_{1}s_{1}+b_{3}s_{3}\\
-b_{2}s_{1}-b_{3}s_{2}\end{pmatrix}\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.45377pth_{[A,B]}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}ab_{3}s_{3}\\
-ab_{2}s_{3}\\
-ab_{3}s_{1}+ab_{2}s_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

We will show that the matrix

C=\begin{pmatrix}h_{A}(s)&h_{B}(s)&h_{[A,B]}(s)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}as_{2}&b_{1}s_{2}+b_{2}s_{3}&ab_{3}s_{3}\\
-as_{1}&-b_{1}s_{1}+b_{3}s_{3}&-ab_{2}s_{3}\\
0&-b_{2}s_{1}-b_{3}s_{2}&-ab_{3}s_{1}+ab_{2}s_{2}\end{pmatrix}

has rank 2 at all points of
\mathbb{S}^{2}.
Let us consider the following cases:

Case 1.
If
s_{2}\neq 0
and
s_{3}\neq 0,
we easily obtain

C\sim\begin{pmatrix}as_{2}&b_{1}s_{2}+b_{2}s_{3}&ab_{3}s_{3}\\
0&b_{2}\dfrac{s_{1}s_{3}}{s_{2}}+b_{3}s_{3}&-ab_{2}s_{3}+ab_{3}\dfrac{s_{1}s_{3}}{s_{2}}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix};

thus,
C
has rank 1 if and only if
b_{2}s_{1}s_{3}+b_{3}s_{2}s_{3}=0
and
-ab_{2}s_{2}s_{3}+ab_{3}s_{1}s_{3}=0
, this happens if and only if
b_{2}=b_{3}=0,
therefore,
\mbox{rank}(C)=2
if and only if
b_{2}^{2}+b_{3}^{2}\neq 0.

Case 2.
If
s_{2}=0,
we get

C\sim\begin{pmatrix}-as_{1}&-b_{1}s_{1}+b_{3}s_{3}&-ab_{2}s_{3}\\
0&b_{2}s_{3}&ab_{3}s_{3}\\
0&-b_{2}s_{1}&-ab_{3}s_{1}\end{pmatrix};

consider the following cases:

2.1.

If
s_{1}\neq 0
and
s_{3}\neq 0,
we have that
\mbox{rank}(C)=2
if and only if
b_{2}^{2}+b_{3}^{2}\neq 0.

2.2.

If
s_{1}=0,
we have
s_{3}=1,
therefore

C=\begin{pmatrix}0&b_{2}&ab_{3}\\
0&b_{3}&-ab_{2}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix};

thus, we have that
\mbox{rank}(C)=2
if and only if
b_{2}^{2}+b_{3}^{2}\neq 0.

2.3.

If
s_{3}=0,
we have
s_{1}=1,
thus

C=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
-a&-b_{1}&0\\
0&-b_{2}&-ab_{3}\end{pmatrix};

as before,
\mbox{rank}(C)=2
if and only if
b_{2}^{2}+b_{3}^{2}\neq 0.

Case 3.
If
s_{3}=0
we can assume that
s_{2}\neq 0;
in fact, if
s_{2}=0
we return to Case 2.3. We have thus

C\sim\begin{pmatrix}as_{2}&b_{1}s_{2}&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&-b_{2}s_{1}-b_{3}s_{2}&-ab_{3}s_{1}+ab_{2}s_{2}\end{pmatrix};

therefore,

\mbox{rank}(C)=1
if and only if
-b_{2}s_{1}-b_{3}s_{2}=0
and
ab_{2}s_{2}-ab_{3}s_{1}=0,
this happens if and only if
b_{2}=b_{3}=0.
Finally,
\mbox{rank}(C)=2
if and only if
b_{2}^{2}+b_{3}^{2}\neq 0.

∎

3.
Controllability of induced bilinear systems

According to Observation
2.2
, in this section we will study the controllability problem of induced bilinear systems of the form

(6)

\mathbb{P}\Sigma:\hskip 21.68121pt\dot{s}=As+uBs=\begin{pmatrix}0&a&0\\
-a&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}s+u\begin{pmatrix}0&b_{1}&b_{2}\\
-b_{1}&0&b_{3}\\
-b_{2}&-b_{3}&0\end{pmatrix}s,\hskip 21.68121pta>0,\ s\in\mathbb{S}^{2};

we will explicitly determine the trajectories and controls that solve the controllability problem. Writing

\beta:=\sqrt{(a+ub_{1})^{2}+(ub_{2}^{2})+(ub_{3})^{2}},

the characteristic polynomial of the matrix

C=A+uB=\begin{pmatrix}0&a+ub_{1}&ub_{2}\\
-(a+ub_{1})&0&ub_{3}\\
-ub_{2}&-ub_{3}&0\end{pmatrix}

is given by
p(\lambda)=\lambda\left[\lambda^{2}+\beta^{2}\right];

thus, the eigenvalues of
C
are given by
\lambda_{1}=0
y
\lambda_{2,3}=\pm i\beta.
The eigenvectors associated are determined considering the following cases:

Case 1.
If
u=0:

•

For
\lambda_{1}=0
we have

\lambda_{1}I-C=\begin{pmatrix}0&-a&0\\
a&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\sim\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

thus,
v_{1}:=(0,0,1)
is the eigenvector associated with
\lambda_{1}.

•

For
\lambda_{2}=i\beta=ia,
we have

\lambda_{2}I-C=\begin{pmatrix}ia&-a&0\\
a&ia&0\\
0&0&ia\end{pmatrix}\sim\begin{pmatrix}ia&-a&0\\
0&0&ia\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}

thus,
v=:v_{2}+iv_{3}=(-i,1,0)
is the eigenvector associated with
\lambda_{2,3}.
For the conjugate eigenvalue
\lambda_{3}=-i\beta
a similar result is obtained.

Thus, the trajectories of the system
\mathbb{P}\Sigma
, for control
u=0,
are given by

s(t)=c_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}t}v_{1}^{T}+c_{2}\left[\cos(at)v_{2}^{T}-\sin(at)v_{3}^{T}\right]+c_{3}\left[\cos(at)v_{3}^{T}+\sin(at)v_{2}^{T}\right],

or more precisely given by

s(t)=\begin{pmatrix}c_{2}\sin(at)-c_{3}\cos(at)\\
c_{2}\cos(at)+c_{3}\sin(at)\\
c_{1}\end{pmatrix},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Observation 3.1
.

Given
s_{0}=(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})\in\mathbb{S}^{2};
the trajectory of
\mathbb{P}\Sigma
, with control
u=0
and initial condition
s_{0},
is given by

s(t;s_{0},0)=\begin{pmatrix}b_{0}\sin(at)+a_{0}\cos(at)\\
b_{0}\cos(at)-a_{0}\sin(at)\\
c_{0}\end{pmatrix},

which are circles on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with constant height
z=c_{0}.

Case 2.
If
u\neq 0:

•

For
\lambda_{1}=0,
we have

\lambda_{1}I-C\sim\begin{pmatrix}ub_{2}&ub_{3}&0\\
0&a+ub_{1}&ub_{2}\\
a+ub_{1}&0&-ub_{3}\end{pmatrix}\\
\sim\begin{pmatrix}ub_{2}&ub_{3}&0\\
0&a+ub_{1}&ub_{2}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}

thus, the eigenvectors are of the form

\left(\dfrac{ub_{3}}{a+ub_{1}},-\dfrac{ub_{2}}{a+ub_{1}},1\right)t,\hskip 14.45377ptt\in\mathbb{R}

when
a+ub_{1}\neq 0;
taking
t=a+ub_{1}
we have
v_{1}=(ub_{3},-ub_{2},a+ub_{1}).
In the case
a+ub_{1}=0,
a similar result is obtained.

•

For
\lambda_{2}=i\beta
we have

\lambda_{2}I-C\sim\begin{pmatrix}i\beta&-(a+ub_{1})&-ub_{2}\\
0&i\dfrac{u^{2}\alpha}{\beta}&-ub_{3}-i\dfrac{(a+ub_{1})ub_{2}}{\beta}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

where
\alpha:=b_{2}^{2}+b_{3}^{2};
thus, if
\alpha\neq 0,
the associated eigenvector to
\lambda_{2}
is given by

v=v_{2}+iv_{3}=\left(-\dfrac{b_{3}(a+ub_{1})}{u\alpha}-i\dfrac{b_{2}\beta}{u\alpha},\dfrac{b_{2}(a+ub_{1})}{u\alpha}-i\dfrac{b_{3}\beta}{u\alpha},1\right).

For the conjugate eigenvalue
\lambda_{3}=-i\beta
a similar result is obtained.

Thus, the trajectories of the system
\mathbb{P}\Sigma
, for control
u\neq 0,
are given by

s(t)=c_{1}v_{1}^{T}+c_{2}(\cos(\beta t)v_{2}^{T}-\sin(\beta t)v_{3}^{T})+c_{3}(\cos(\beta t)v_{3}^{T}+\sin(\beta t)v_{2}^{T}),

or more precisely given by

s(t)=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}b_{3}u-\left[\dfrac{b_{3}c_{2}(a+ub_{1})+b_{2}c_{3}\beta}{u\alpha}\right]\cos(\beta t)+\left[\dfrac{b_{2}c_{2}\beta-b_{3}c_{3}(a+ub_{1})}{u\alpha}\right]\sin(\beta t)\\
-c_{1}b_{2}u+\left[\dfrac{b_{2}c_{2}(a+ub_{1})-b_{3}c_{3}\beta}{u\alpha}\right]\cos(\beta t)+\left[\dfrac{b_{3}c_{2}\beta+b_{2}c_{3}(a+ub_{1})}{u\alpha}\right]\sin(\beta t)\\
c_{1}(a+ub_{1})+c_{2}\cos(\beta t)+c_{3}\sin(\beta t)\end{pmatrix},

with
t\in\mathbb{R}.

Observation 3.2
.

For controls
u\neq 0,
it is possible to determine values for
c_{1},c_{2}
and
c_{3}
such that
s(0)=s_{0}=(a_{0},b_{0},0)\in\mathbb{S}^{2},
for certain
a_{0}
and
b_{0}.
In fact, writing

s(0)=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}b_{3}u-\left[\dfrac{b_{3}c_{2}(a+ub_{1})+b_{2}c_{3}\beta}{u\alpha}\right]\\
-c_{1}b_{2}u+\left[\dfrac{b_{2}c_{2}(a+ub_{1})-b_{3}c_{3}\beta}{u\alpha}\right]\\
c_{1}(a+ub_{1})+c_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}\\
b_{0}\\
0\end{pmatrix},

we can take
a_{0}:=b_{3}/\sqrt{\alpha}
and
b_{0}:=-b_{2}/\sqrt{\alpha},
these satisfy
a_{0}b_{2}+b_{0}b_{3}=0
and
a_{0}^{2}+b_{0}^{2}=1.
Taking components we have

b_{2}c_{1}b_{3}u-\dfrac{b_{2}b_{3}c_{2}(a+ub_{1})}{u\alpha}-\dfrac{b_{2}^{2}c_{3}\beta}{u\alpha}=a_{0}b_{2}\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.45377pt-b_{3}c_{1}b_{2}u+\dfrac{b_{3}b_{2}c_{2}(a+ub_{1})}{u\alpha}-\dfrac{b_{3}^{2}c_{3}\beta}{u\alpha}=b_{0}b_{3},

therefore

-\dfrac{b_{2}^{2}c_{3}\beta}{u\alpha}-\dfrac{b_{3}^{2}c_{3}\beta}{u\alpha}=-\dfrac{c_{3}\beta}{u}=0,

thus
c_{3}=0.
On the other hand, replacing
c_{2}=-c_{1}(a+ub_{1})
in

c_{1}b_{3}u-(b_{3}c_{2}(a+ub_{1}))/(u\alpha)=a_{0},

and using the definition of
\beta,
we get

c_{1}b_{3}\beta^{2}/u\alpha=a_{0};

therefore

c_{1}=\dfrac{a_{0}u\alpha}{b_{3}\beta^{2}}\hskip 21.68121pt\mbox{and}\hskip 21.68121ptc_{2}=-\dfrac{a_{0}u\alpha(a+ub_{1})}{b_{3}\beta^{2}}.

Given
s_{0}=(a_{0},b_{0},0)\in\mathbb{S}^{2};
the trajectory of
\mathbb{P}\Sigma
, with control
u\neq 0
and initial condition
s_{0},
is given by

s(t,s_{0},u)=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{a_{0}u^{2}\alpha}{\beta^{2}}+\dfrac{a_{0}(a+ub_{1})^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\cos(\beta t)-\dfrac{a_{0}b_{2}(a+ub_{1})}{b_{3}\beta}\sin(\beta t)\\
-\dfrac{a_{0}b_{2}u^{2}\alpha}{b_{3}\beta^{2}}-\dfrac{a_{0}b_{2}(a+ub_{1})^{2}}{b_{3}\beta^{2}}\cos(\beta t)-\dfrac{a_{0}(a+ub_{1})}{\beta}\sin(\beta t)\\
\dfrac{a_{0}u\alpha(a+ub_{1})}{b_{3}\beta^{2}}-\dfrac{a_{0}u\alpha(a+ub_{1})}{b_{3}\beta^{2}}\cos(\beta t)\end{pmatrix}.

Finally, writing
P_{0}^{T}=\left(\dfrac{a_{0}u^{2}\alpha}{\beta^{2}},-\dfrac{a_{0}b_{2}u\alpha}{b_{3}\beta^{2}},\dfrac{a_{0}u\alpha(a+ub_{1})}{b_{3}\beta^{2}}\right),
we have

\|s(t;s_{0},u)-P_{0}\|=\left|\dfrac{a_{0}(a+ub_{1})}{b_{3}\beta}\right|,

i.e.,
the trajectory
s(t;s_{0},u),
with initial condition
s_{0}=(a_{0},b_{0},0)
is a circle on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with center
P_{0}.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

The induced bilinear system
\mathbb{P}\Sigma:\dot{s}=As+uBs,\ s\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

\mathbb{S}^{2}

.

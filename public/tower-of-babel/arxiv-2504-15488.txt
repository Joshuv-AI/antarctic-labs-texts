Floating bodies for ball-convex bodies
Carsten Schütt Elisabeth M. Werner Diliya Yalikun

Abstract
We define floating bodies in the class of n n -dimensional ball-convex bodies. A right derivative of volume of these floating bodies leads to a surface area measure for ball-convex bodies which we call relative affine surface area. We show that this quantity is a rigid motion invariant, upper semi continuous valuation.

A convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a compact, convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonempty
interior. For a given convex body
C
, we consider the class of
C
-ball convex bodies

(1)

This class was introduced and investigated in
[
31
]
.
In this paper we will only consider

-ball convex bodies

that are non-empty and proper, i.e.,
K\neq\emptyset
is a proper subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In particular, when

is the

-dimensional Euclidean unit ball centered at

with radius

R
-ball convex and

Due to its important properties, which make it an effective and powerful tool, it

Results

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a point
x
on the boundary
\partial K
of
K
we denote by
N_{K}(x)
the outer unit normal vector of
K
at
x
.
By a theorem of Rademacher, e.g.,
[
19
]
,
N_{K}(x)
is unique a.e. on
\partial K
. The map
N:\partial K\to S^{n-1}
is called the spherical image map or Gauss map.
Its differential resp. generalized differential is called the Weingarten map. By a theorem of Alexandrov
[
2
]
and Busemann and Feller
[
20
]

these generalized derivatives exist a.e. on
\partial K
.
The eigenvalues of the Weingarten map are the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}(K,x)
of
K
at
x
.
The (generalized) Gauss curvature is
\kappa(K,x)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\kappa_{i}(K,x)
.
For more information and the details we refer to e.g.,
[
25
,
43
]
.

We recall that for a convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
\delta\geq 0
, the (classical) convex floating body is defined as

K_{\delta}=\bigcap_{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(H^{-}\cap K)\leq\delta}H^{+}.

(3)

We now introduce an analog to the classical floating body
[
7
,
45
]
, the
C
-ball floating body.

Definition 1
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
. Let
K
be a
C
-ball convex body.
We define the
C
-ball floating body
K_{\delta}^{C}
by

K_{\delta}^{C}=\bigcap_{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\setminus C+x)\leq\delta}C+x.

(4)

In particular, when
C=R\,B^{n}_{2}
, we
write in short
K_{\delta}^{R\,B^{n}_{2}}=K_{\delta}^{R}
and call it the
R
-ball floating body of
K
.

Obviously,
K_{\delta}^{C}\subset K
and
K_{\delta}^{C}
is an
C
-ball convex body.
In this paper we will mostly treat the case
C=R\,B^{n}_{2}
. Despite a slight abuse of notation, there should be
no issue if we
write in short
K_{\delta}^{R\,B^{n}_{2}}=K_{\delta}^{R}
.

In the definition of the
C
-convex floating body
K_{\delta}^{C}
the convex body
C
replaces the hyperplanes of the definition of the convex floating body (
3
). When
C=R\,B^{n}_{2}
and
R\to\infty
, then
R\,B^{n}_{2}\to H^{+}
and we recover the usual convex floating body

[
7
,
45
]
.

Let
g_{K}(x)=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(x+K))
be the
covariogram operator
.
We want to note that the
C
-ball floating body can be defined using an extension of the covariogram operator, the
cross variogram
operator

g_{K,C}(x)=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(x+C)),

and then

K_{\delta}^{C}=\bigcap\left\{x+C:g_{K,C}(x)\geq\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)-\delta\right\}.

(5)

The covarigram is important in the recent development of
higher order convex bodies
, e.g.,
[
27
,
30
]
. It is also used to define the convolution bodies

[
42
]
, see also
[
55
]
,

C(\delta,K)=\frac{1}{2}\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(x+K))\geq\delta\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:g_{K}(x)\geq\delta\right\}.

I. Molchanov
[
37
]
proposed an extension of the convolution bodies using the cross covariogram.
It was this suggestion by I. Molchanov that inspired definition (
5
).
In dimension
2
, these are the
r
-spindle floating bodies
[
23
]
.

Note also that we have for
all affine maps
T
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with determinant
\mbox{det}(T)\neq 0

that
K
is
R
-ball convex if and only if
T(K)
is
T(R\,B^{n}_{2})
-ball convex
and that for all
\delta\geq 0

(T(K))^{T(L)}_{\delta}=T\left(K^{L}_{\frac{\delta}{|det(T)|}}\right).

(6)

The following theorem is the main result of the paper. We denote by

\mathcal{K}_{R}^{+}
the set of all
R
-convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that for all
x\in\partial K
,
\kappa_{i}(K,x)>\frac{1}{R}
, for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{R}^{+}
and let
K_{\delta}^{R}
be its
R
-ball floating body. Then

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)-\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{\delta}^{R})}{\delta^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}=c_{n}\int_{\partial K}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x),

where
c_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n+1}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(B^{n-1}_{2})}\right)^{\frac{2}{n+1}}
.

Note that if
R\to\infty
, we recover the classical affine surface area (
2
).
This leads naturally to calling the expression of the above theorem
relative affine surface area
.

Definition 2
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{R}
. Then the relative affine surface area of
K
is

as^{R}(K)=\int_{\partial K}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x)

More generally, when
K
is an
L
-convex body, we define

as^{L}(K)=\int_{\partial K}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-\kappa_{i}(L,N_{L}^{-1}(N_{K}(x))\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x).

When
K
is sufficiently smooth,
as^{R}
can be written as

as^{R}(K)=\int_{S^{n-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{r_{i}(K,u)}-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}f_{K}(u)d\sigma(u),

(7)

and, more generally,

as^{L}(K)=\int_{S^{n-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{r_{i}(K,u)}-\frac{1}{r_{i}(L,u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}f_{K}(u)d\sigma(u),

where, for
u\in S^{n-1}
such that
N_{K}(x)=u
,
r_{i}(K,u)=(\kappa_{i}(K,N_{K}^{-1}(u))^{-1}
, and
f_{K}=\kappa^{-1}
is the curvature function.

It cannot happen for an
R
-ball convex body that one of the
\kappa_{i}=0
.
In fact, for an
R
-convex body
\kappa_{i}\geq\frac{1}{R}
for all
i
.
It can happen for an
R
-ball convex body that one or all of the
\kappa_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{R}
.
If one or all of the
\kappa_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{R}
almost everywhere on
\partial K
, then
as^{R}(K)=0
.
For instance this is the case for
R
-ball polyhedra
, i.e. the intersection of
finitely many
R
-balls. In particular
as^{R}(RB^{n}_{2})=0
.

A crucial ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1
is the following proposition which may be known but we could not find a proof.
It may also be useful in other contexts.

Proposition 2
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
c_{i}>0
. Then

\int_{S^{n-1}}\frac{d\sigma(\xi)}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]^{-1}=\sigma(S^{n-1})\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]^{-1}.

We require in Proposition
2
that all
c_{i}>0
,
1\leq i\leq n
. If only one of the
c_{i}

equals
0
the integral equals
\infty
. Indeed, suppose that
c_{n}=0
, while the others are strictly greater than
0
. Then on a set of volume
d(n)\,\varepsilon^{n-1}
the integrand
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}
is greater than
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}
. Thus the integral is unbounded.

It is obvious that relative affine surface area is invariant under rigid motions. The next proposition states some more properties. Further properties will be in a forthcoming paper.
We will need the definition of the Hausdorff distance
d
of convex sets
K
and
L
,

d(K,L)=\inf\{\lambda>0:K\subset L+\lambda B^{n}_{2},L\subset K+\lambda B^{n}_{2}\}.

Proposition 3
.

Let
K
be an
R
-ball convex body.

(i) Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
a>0
. Then
as^{R}
is homogeneous of degree
n\frac{n-1}{n+1}
,

as^{aR}(aK)=a^{n\frac{n-1}{n+1}}\,as^{R}(K).

(ii) The relative affine surface area
as^{R}
is a valuation, i.e. for
R
-ball convex bodies
K
and
L
such that
K\cup L
is again
R
-ball convex,

as^{R}(K\cup L)+as^{R}(K\cap L)=as^{R}(K)+as^{R}(L).

(iii) We have

as^{R}(K)\leq n\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B^{n}_{2})^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}},

with equality if and only if
R=\infty
and
K
is an ellipsoid.

(iv) The relative affine surface area
as^{R}
is upper semi continuous: If
K_{l}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
, is a sequence of
R
-ball convex
bodies that converge in the Hausdorff distance to the
R
-ball convex body
K
, then

\lim\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\,as^{R}(K_{l})\leq as^{R}(K).

Relative affine surface area is not continuous: Every
R
-ball convex body with strictly positive relative affine surface area
can be approximated by a sequence of
R
-ball polyhedra.

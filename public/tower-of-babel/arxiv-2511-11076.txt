Explosion and implosion of birth-and-death continuous-time random walks
Andrey Pilipenko, Vadym Tkachenko

Abstract
We provide necessary and sufficient conditions for explosion and implosion of birth-and-death (non-Markov) continuous-time random walks. In other words, we obtain conditions for ∞ \infty to be accessible and for it to be an entrance point. We derive the analytical regularity criteria in terms of the appropriate scale function and the speed measure, which involve transition probabilities and the Laplace transform of the waiting times. We show that these criteria closely resemble classical ones for diffusions and Markov birth-and-death processes. We then calculate explicit conditions of regularity for semi-Markov processes with waiting times that have (a) finite first moments; (b) regularly varying tails (in particular, α \alpha -stable distribution). Keywords: continuous-time random walk, semi-Markov process, explosion, implosion, exit boundary, entrance boundary MSC 2020: 60J74, 60K15, 60K50



1. Introduction

Semi-Markov processes, commonly known as continuous-time random walks, were introduced by Lévy
[
15
]
and Smith
[
18
]
as a generalization of Markov processes on a countable state space. Markov property of the latter dictates the distribution of waiting times to be exponential, which is not the case for many observed processes (population processes, queueing networks, microbiological processes).

Questions about regularity arise naturally when modeling physical or natural phenomena. A core issue is whether a process, while evolving continuously in time, can travel through infinitely many states in a finite duration. This phenomenon, known as explosion, must be ruled out for processes that model clearly non-explosive systems. Another classical question is that of the entrance boundary, or implosion: can a process be initiated from its boundary that is a not part of the state space. We provide all needed definitions and some preliminaries in the Section
2
. We proceed with a short historical overview of these problems.

Boundary classification of one dimensional diffusion processes begins with Feller’s 1952 paper
[
4
]
. In Section 11 of this classical work, he introduces four types of boundaries depending on the behavior of the diffusion: regular, exit, entry, and natural. His approach lies in the semigroup method. To express the boundary conditions, Feller uses what is later to be called the scale function. In the follow-up 1957 paper
[
5
]
he introduces the concept of the speed measure and establishes a canonical infinitesimal generator in terms of it and the scale function. These notions play a central role in the current paper. Boundary classification for diffusions was advanced and completed using the sample-path approach in the book by Itô and McKean
[
13
]
. For more details, we refer to a comprehensive overview by Peskir
[
17
]
.

Moving to Markov processes on a countable state space, Feller discusses birth-and-death processes in a 1959 paper
[
7
]
, using probabilistic approach to boundary problems. Dynkin and Yushkevich
[
3
]
provide analytical conditions for explosion of such processes in terms of transition probabilities and the expectation of waiting times. They construct the appropriate scale function and speed measure such that the regularity condition becomes as in the case of diffusion. Their approach is of a great importance to the current paper.

Boundary behavior for general Markov processes is challenging. For time-homogeneous Markov chains, the problem is resolved in
[
2
,
6
]
. Results also exist for continuous-state branching processes with competition
[
10
]
.

For semi-Markov processes on a countable state space, Feller
[
8
]
derives an analogue of Kolmogorov’s backward equations and uses the Laplace transform to obtain a minimal solution. He shows that the boundary can be reached in finite time if and only if a certain problem admits a minimal substochastic solution. However, this condition is difficult to verify in practice. Gerrard
[
11
]
uses a similar approach to obtain explosion conditions, but they remain implicit.

We take a different approach and focus specifically on birth-and-death processes. This allows us to derive natural and explicit analytical conditions in terms of the appropriate scale function and speed measure.

3. Main results

In this section, we formulate two main theorems of this paper and compare them to known results for diffusion processes. Let
X
be a continuous-time random walk
from Definition
2.1
. Introduce the
scale

\delta_{k}=\frac{q_{1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{1}\dots p_{k}},\quad k\geq 0,

(1)

and the
speed measure

\nu_{i}=(1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}})\frac{p_{1}\dots p_{i-1}}{q_{1}\dots q_{i}},\quad i\geq 0,

(2)

where
\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}}=p_{i}\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}^{+}}+q_{i}\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}^{-}}
, and the product over an empty set is equal to
1

\delta_{0}=1,\quad\nu_{0}=1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{0}^{+}},\quad\nu_{1}=(1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{1}})\frac{1}{q_{1}}.

Theorem 3.1

(Explosion condition)
.

For every initial distribution of
X
, the following alternative holds:

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{X\text{ explodes to }\infty\}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\nu_{i}\right)\delta_{k}<\infty,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{X\text{ explodes to }\infty\}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\nu_{i}\right)\delta_{k}=\infty.

(4)

Theorem 3.2

(Implosion condition)
.

For every initial distribution of
X
, the following alternative holds:

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{X\text{ implodes from }\infty\}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\nu_{i}\right)\delta_{k}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}=\infty,

(5)

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{X\text{ implodes from }\infty\}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\nu_{i}\right)\delta_{k}=\infty\quad\text{ or }\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\infty.

(6)

Proofs can be found in Section
5
.

Remark 3.3
.

If we replace the Laplace transform
\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}}
with
\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau_{i}}
for some
\lambda>0
, the results will stay the same.

3.1. Comparison with known results for diffusions

Let us compare the conditions for explosion and implosion with known results for one-dimensional diffusions (see

\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}M[x_{0},x]\,\mathrm{d}S(x)<\infty,

where

The boundary

for a strong Markov process

\lim_{t\to 0+}X(t)=+\infty
. In terms of the scale function and the speed measure of a diffusion, this happens if and only if

\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}M[x,\infty]\,\mathrm{d}S(x)<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad S(\infty)=\infty.

(8)

Define the scale function and the speed measure using

from (
1

2

S(n)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\delta_{k},\quad M(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nu_{i},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then (

4. Examples

In this section, we apply Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
to specific types of processes. Section

The following will be useful throughout this section. Denote
\Phi_{i}=1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}}
, and rewrite the series from the explosion condition changing the order of summation:

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\nu_{i}\right)\delta_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\nu_{i}\delta_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\Phi_{i}\frac{p_{1}\dots p_{i-1}}{q_{1}\dots q_{i}}\frac{q_{1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{1}\dots p_{k}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{i}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\frac{q_{i+1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{i}\dots p_{k}}.

(9)

Analogously, rewrite the series from the implosion condition:

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\nu_{i}\right)\delta_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\nu_{i}\delta_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\Phi_{i}\frac{p_{1}\dots p_{i-1}}{q_{1}\dots q_{i}}\frac{q_{1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{1}\dots p_{k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Phi_{i}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\frac{p_{k+1}\dots p_{i-1}}{q_{k+1}\dots q_{i}}.

(10)

In these formulas, we agree that
q_{i+1}\dots q_{k}=1
if
i=k
, and
p_{k+1}\dots p_{i-1}=1
if
k=i-1
. Note that convergence of either series implies

\lim_{i\to\infty}\Phi_{i}=0.

(11)

4.1. Markov case

Suppose
X
is a birth-and-death Markov processes. In this case, the waiting times
\tau_{i}^{+}
and
\tau_{i}^{-}
are equally exponentially distributed with some rate parameters
a_{i}>0
. The Laplace transform is
\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau_{i}}=\frac{a_{i}}{\lambda+a_{i}}
, and hence
\Phi_{i}=1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}}=\frac{1}{1+a_{i}}
.

Consider the explosion condition (
3
). Then using (
9
), (
11
), and the fact that
\frac{1}{1+a_{i}}\sim\frac{1}{a_{i}}
as
a_{i}\to\infty
, we arrive at

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\frac{q_{i+1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{i}\dots p_{k}}<\infty.

(12)

This is a well-known explosion condition for Markov birth-and-death processes (
[
3
, IV, § 5]
).

Now consider a homogeneous process with
p_{i}\equiv p
and
q_{i}\equiv q=1-p
. If
p>q
, the series in (
12
) simplifies as follows:

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\frac{q_{i+1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{i}\dots p_{k}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}p}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{l}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}p}\frac{1}{1-\frac{q}{p}}=\frac{1}{p-q}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}}.

Thus, the explosion condition for a homogeneous birth-and-death Markov process is

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad p>q.

(13)

It coincides with the explosion condition for the Yule process of pure birth (see
[
9
, XIV, §8]
).
The condition makes intuitive sense, because the average number of visits of each state by the Markov chain
(X_{k})
equals
1/(p-q)
.

Consider implosion condition (
5
). Analogously to explosion, using (
10
) and (
11
), we get the implosion condition for a birth-and-death Markov process:

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\frac{p_{k+1}\dots p_{i-1}}{q_{k+1}\dots q_{i}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}=\infty.

(14)

Now consider a homogeneous process
with
p_{i}\equiv p
and
q_{i}\equiv q=1-p
. If
p<q
, we get

\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\frac{p_{k+1}\dots p_{i-1}}{q_{k+1}\dots q_{i}}=\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{i-k}\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\Big(\frac{p}{q}\Big)^{i}}{q-p}.

For a symmetric walk with
p=q=1/2
, we get

\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\frac{p_{k+1}\dots p_{i-1}}{q_{k+1}\dots q_{i}}=2i.

Therefore, the condition (
14
) holds if and only if one of the following is true:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad p<q,

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{i}{a_{i}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad p=q.

(16)

4.2. Waiting times with finite first moments

Suppose
\tau_{i}^{\pm}\overset{d}{=}\tau^{\pm}/a_{i}^{\pm}
, where
\tau^{\pm}
are positive random variables, and
a_{i}^{\pm}
are positive numbers with
\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}^{\pm}=\infty
. Assume that
\mathsf{E}\tau^{\pm}<\infty
. It is well known that the asymptotics of the Laplace transform in the neighborhood of zero is
1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau}\sim\lambda\mathsf{E}\tau
as
\lambda\to 0+
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}}

\displaystyle=1-p_{i}\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}^{+}}-q_{i}\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}^{-}}=1-p_{i}\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau^{+}/a_{i}^{+}}-q_{i}\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau^{-}/a_{i}^{-}}

\displaystyle=p_{i}\left(1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau^{+}/a_{i}^{+}}\right)+q_{i}\left(1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau^{-}/a_{i}^{-}}\right)\sim\frac{p_{i}}{a_{i}^{+}}\mathsf{E}\tau^{+}+\frac{q_{i}}{a_{i}^{-}}\mathsf{E}\tau^{-},\quad i\to\infty.

In particular, if
\mathsf{E}\tau^{+}=\mathsf{E}\tau^{-}
and
a_{i}^{+}=a_{i}^{-}
, then the conditions for explosion and implosion in this case are identical to conditions (
12
)–(
16
) in the Markov case from Section
4.1
.

4.3. Waiting times with regularly varying tails

Suppose
\tau_{i}^{\pm}\overset{d}{=}\tau^{\pm}/a_{i}^{\pm}
, where
\tau^{\pm}
are positive random variables with distribution functions
F^{\pm}
, and
a_{i}^{\pm}
are positive numbers with
\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}^{\pm}=\infty
. Suppose that
1-F^{\pm}
varies regularly at
\infty
with exponent
-\alpha^{\pm}
, where
0<\alpha^{\pm}<1
. That is,

1-F^{\pm}(x)=x^{-\alpha^{\pm}}l^{\pm}(x),\quad x\to+\infty,

where
l^{\pm}
are slowly varying functions at
\infty
(see
[
1
, I, §4]
). Then from a Tauberian-Abelian Theorem (see
[
9
, XIII, §5, Thm. 1]
), we get the following asymptotics of the Laplace transform:

1-\varphi^{\pm}(\lambda)\sim\Gamma(1-\alpha^{\pm})(1-F^{\pm}(1/\lambda)),\quad\lambda\to{0+}.

The explosion condition (
3
) becomes

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(1-p_{i}F^{+}(a_{i}^{+})-q_{i}F^{-}(a_{i}^{-})\right)\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\frac{q_{i+1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{i}\dots p_{k}}<\infty.

(17)

The implosion condition (
5
) becomes

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-p_{i}F^{+}(a_{i}^{+})-q_{i}F^{-}(a_{i}^{-})\right)\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\frac{p_{k+1}\dots p_{i-1}}{q_{k+1}\dots q_{i}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}=\infty.

(18)

4.4. Stable waiting times

An interesting special case of Section
4.3
is the case where
\tau^{+}
and
\tau^{-}
are equally distributed positive random variables with
\alpha
-stable distribution,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
a_{i}^{+}=a_{i}^{-}=a_{i}
. Then the Laplace transform is

\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}}=\exp\{-ca_{i}^{-\alpha}\},\quad i\geq 0,

for some constant

(see
[
9
, XIII, §6]
). Therefore, the following equivalence holds:

1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}}=1-\exp\{-ca_{i}^{-\alpha}\}\sim ca_{i}^{-\alpha},\quad a_{i}\to\infty.

The explosion condition (
3
) becomes

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}^{\alpha}}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\frac{q_{i+1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{i}\dots p_{k}}<\infty.

(19)

The implosion condition (
5
) becomes

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}^{\alpha}}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\frac{p_{k+1}\dots p_{i-1}}{q_{k+1}\dots q_{i}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}=\infty.

(20)

If
p_{i}\equiv p
and
q_{i}\equiv q
, condition (
19
) becomes

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}^{\alpha}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad p>q.

(21)

Condition (
20
) becomes one of the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{i}^{\alpha}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad p<q,

(22)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{i}{a_{i}^{\alpha}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad p=q.

(23)

Remark 4.1
.

Conditions (
19
)–(
23
) coincide with conditions (
12
)–(
16
) from the Markov case if we formally set
\alpha=1

is called
transient
if, starting from any state, there is a positive probability of never returning to it. The Markov chain is called
recurrent
if it returns to each initial state with probability one. It is straightforward that explosion of the process
X
may occur only if its embedded Markov chain
(X_{k})
is transient, and implosion may occur only if it is recurrent. To formulate analytical conditions of transience and recurrence, let us introduce the
canonical scale
(see
[
3
, p. 157]
). Consider a set
\{u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},\dots\}
such that
u_{0}=0
,
u_{1}=1
,

u_{i}=1+\delta_{1}+\dots+\delta_{i-1}=1+\frac{q_{1}}{p_{1}}+\dots+\frac{q_{1}\dots q_{i-1}}{p_{1}\dots p_{i-1}},\quad i\geq 2.

(25)

One useful consequence is that for any states
l<i<r
the probabilities of leaving the interval
(l,r)
through the right-hand side or through the left-hand side are

\mathsf{P}_{i}\{\sigma_{l}>\sigma_{r}\}=\frac{u_{i}-u_{l}}{u_{r}-u_{l}},\quad\mathsf{P}_{i}\{\sigma_{l}<\sigma_{r}\}=\frac{u_{r}-u_{i}}{u_{r}-u_{l}},

(26)

respectively. Next, set by definition

r=\lim_{i\to\infty}u_{i}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{q_{1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{1}\dots p_{k}}.

The Markov chain
(X_{k})
is transient if
r<\infty
and recurrent if
r=\infty
(see
[
3
]
, IV, §4).

5.3. Preliminaries

Before we begin the proof of theorems, we need to do some preliminary work. First, introduce the conditional Laplace transform (at the point
1
) of the moment of explosion

f_{i}=\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{\infty}}=\mathsf{E}[\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{\infty}}\mid X(0)=i],\quad i\geq 0,

and of the hitting times of states
n\geq 0

f_{i}^{n}=\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{n}}=\mathsf{E}[\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{n}}\mid X(0)=i],\quad 0\leq i\leq n.

Lemma 5.5
.

The following properties hold:

(1)

For a fixed initial state
i\geq 0
, the sequence
\{f_{i}^{n}\}_{n\geq i}
is monotone decreasing;

(2)

For a fixed end state
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the values
f_{0}^{n}
,
f_{1}^{n}
,…,
f_{n}^{n}
are increasing;

(3)

For all
i\geq 0
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{i}^{n}=f_{i}
.

Proof.

We immediately get properties (1) and (2) as a consequence of the alternative construction of
X
(see Remark
5.4
). Property (3) follows from the Theorem of monotone convergence.
∎

Let us establish a connection between the Laplace transform and the moment of explosion.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
P_{0}
be the initial distribution of
X
. The following alternative holds:

\displaystyle(a)\lim_{i\to\infty}f_{i}=1

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow f_{i}>0\text{ for some }i

\displaystyle\quad(b)\lim_{i\to\infty}f_{i}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow f_{i}=0\text{ for some }i

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow f_{i}>0\text{ for all }i

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow f_{i}=0\text{ for all }i

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\sigma_{\infty}<\infty\ P_{0}\text{-a.s.}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\sigma_{\infty}=\infty\ P_{0}\text{-a.s.}

Proof.

From the alternative construction of
X
(see Remark
5.4
), we get

f_{i}=\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{\infty}}=\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{k=i+1}^{\infty}\eta_{k}},

where
\eta_{k}
are independent random variables. Therefore, the statement follows from Kolmogorov’s zero-one law.
∎

Now let us establish conditions that provide
\lim_{i\to\infty}f_{i}=1
. To do this, we examine the Laplace transform
f_{i}^{n}
of hitting times more closely. Suppose
n\in\mathbb{N}
is fixed. Recall that
\{\tau_{i}^{\pm}\}
are the waiting times of the process
X
from Definition
2.1
, and denote
\varphi_{i}^{\pm}=\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}^{\pm}}
for
i\geq 0
. Conditioned on
X(0)=n
,
\sigma_{n}=0
almost surely, hence
f_{n}^{n}=1
. For
X(0)=i\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
, we can rewrite
\sigma_{n}=\tau+\tilde{\sigma}_{n}
, where
\tau
is the first moment of jump, and
\tilde{\sigma}_{n}
is the remaining time before the process hits
n
. By construction of
X
, random variables
\tau
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{n}
are independent. We arrive at the system of equations

\displaystyle f_{0}^{n}=\varphi_{0}^{+}f_{1}^{n},

(27)

\displaystyle f_{i}^{n}=p_{i}\varphi_{i}^{+}f_{i+1}^{n}+q_{i}\varphi_{i}^{-}f_{i-1}^{n},\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1,

\displaystyle f_{n}^{n}=1.

This system is difficult to study because of the multiplication by
\varphi_{i}^{\pm}
. The idea then is to make it additive in some sense. Denote
F_{i}^{n}=1-f_{i}^{n}
and
\Phi_{i}^{\pm}=1-\varphi_{i}^{\pm}
. In this notation, the above system transforms as follows:

\displaystyle F_{0}^{n}=\Phi_{0}^{+}(1-F_{1}^{n})+F_{1}^{n},

(28)

\displaystyle F_{i}^{n}=p_{i}\Phi_{i}^{+}(1-F_{i+1}^{n})+q_{i}\Phi_{i}^{-}(1-F_{i-1}^{n})+p_{i}F_{i+1}^{n}+q_{i}F_{i-1}^{n},\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1,

\displaystyle F_{n}^{n}=0.

Recall a few results about linear recurrent equations and their probabilistic interpretations from the theory of Markov processes on a countable state space. Consider a system

\displaystyle y_{0}=A_{0}+y_{1},

(29)

\displaystyle y_{i}=A_{i}+p_{i}y_{i+1}+q_{i}y_{i-1},\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1,

\displaystyle y_{n}=0,

where
A_{i}
are some non-negative numbers. Note that (
28
) is a particular case of (
29
) with
A_{i}=p_{i}\Phi_{i}^{+}(1-F_{i+1}^{n})+q_{i}\Phi_{i}^{-}(1-F_{i-1}^{n})
. The above system has a simple probabilistic interpretation: we travel in the state space
\{0,1,\dots,n\}
with transition probabilities
p_{i}
,
q_{i}
and get a profit of
A_{i}
at each state
i
until we hit
n
. Then
y_{i}
is the total expected profit when starting form
i
upon reaching
n
(recall that
p_{01}=1
). From this interpretation, a universal lemma for handling such systems follows. Recall that
(X_{k})
is the embedded Markov chain of the process
X
and denote
\varkappa_{n}=\inf\{k\geq 0\mid X_{k}=n\}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

System (
29
) has a unique solution given by the formula

y_{i}=\mathsf{E}_{i}\sum_{k=0}^{\varkappa_{n}-1}A_{X_{k}},\quad 0\leq i\leq n.

We get the following comparison lemma as a corollary.

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose
\tilde{y}_{i}
satisfy the system (
29
) with
\widetilde{A}_{i}
instead of
A_{i}
and
\widetilde{A}_{i}\leq A_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. Then
\tilde{y}_{i}\leq y_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

The following lemma from the theory of Markov processes provides a useful explicit formula for the solution of systems of equations we are interested in.

Lemma 5.9

(see
[
3
]
, IV, §5)
.

The solution of system (
29
) with coefficients
A=\{A_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
is given by
y_{j}=S_{n}(A)-S_{j}(A)
, where

S_{j}(A)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{j-1}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}A_{i}\frac{q_{i+1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{i}\dots p_{k}},\quad 0\leq j\leq n.

5.4. Proof of the explosion condition (Theorem
3.1
)

We first assume the right-hand side of (
3
) holds, that is,

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\nu_{i}\right)\delta_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}})\frac{q_{i+1}\dots q_{k}}{p_{i}\dots p_{k}}<\infty.

From this we prove that
\mathsf{P}\{X\text{ explodes to }\infty\}=1
. Put by definition

\Phi_{i}\coloneqq 1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}}=1-p_{i}\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}^{+}}-q_{i}\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{i}^{-}}=p_{i}\Phi_{i}^{+}+q_{i}\Phi_{i}^{-}.

Then, following Lemma
5.9
, we can denote the above series by
S_{\infty}(\Phi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{n}(\Phi)
, and hence our assumption becomes
S_{\infty}(\Phi)<\infty
.

Recall that
F_{i}^{n}=1-f_{i}^{n}=1-\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{n}}
satisfy system (
28
). We want to compare
F_{i}^{n}
with a solution to a simpler system. Note that, for all indices,

p_{i}\Phi_{i}^{+}(1-F_{i+1}^{n})+q_{i}\Phi_{i}^{-}(1-F_{i-1}^{n})\leq p_{i}\Phi_{i}^{+}+q_{i}\Phi_{i}^{-}=\Phi_{i}.

(30)

Let
G_{i}^{n}
,
0\leq i\leq n
, satisfy the system

\displaystyle G_{0}^{n}=\Phi_{0}+G_{1}^{n},

(31)

\displaystyle G_{i}^{n}=\Phi_{i}+p_{i}G_{i+1}^{n}+q_{i}G_{i-1}^{n},\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1,

\displaystyle G_{n}^{n}=0.

By Lemma
5.7
,
G_{i}^{n}
are determined uniquely. Moreover,
F_{i}^{n}\leq G_{i}^{n}
for
0\leq i\leq n
due to estimate (
30
) and comparison Lemma
5.8
. Combining this with Lemma
5.9
, we get

F_{i}^{n}\leq G_{i}^{n}=S_{n}(\Phi)-S_{i}(\Phi),\quad 0\leq i\leq n.

By Lemma
5.5
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{i}^{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(1-f_{i}^{n})=1-f_{i}
, hence we can pass to the limit

1-f_{i}=\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{i}^{n}\leq S_{\infty}(\Phi)-S_{i}(\Phi).

Since
S_{\infty}(\Phi)<\infty
and
1-f_{i}\geq 0
, it follows that

0\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}(1-f_{i})\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}(S_{\infty}(\Phi)-S_{i}(\Phi))=0.

Hence
\lim_{i\to\infty}f_{i}=1
, and Lemma
5.6
implies that
\mathsf{P}\{X\text{ explodes to }\infty\}=1
.

Let us show that
\mathsf{P}\{X\text{ explodes to }\infty\}=1
implies
S_{\infty}(\Phi)<\infty
. The idea is to estimate
F_{i}^{n}=1-\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{n}}
from below, using the fact that it satisfies the system (
28
). Lemma
5.5
implies that, for all
0\leq i\leq n
,

1-F_{i}^{n}=f_{i}^{n}=\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{n}}\geq\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{\infty}}\geq\mathsf{E}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{\infty}}=f_{0}>0.

Denoting
c=f_{0}
, we have

p_{i}\Phi_{i}^{+}(1-F_{i+1}^{n})+q_{i}\Phi_{i}^{-}(1-F_{i-1}^{n})\geq p_{i}\Phi_{i}^{+}c+q_{i}\Phi_{i}^{-}c=\Phi_{i}c.

(32)

Now consider a system

\displaystyle H_{0}^{n}=\Phi_{0}c+H_{1}^{n},

(33)

\displaystyle H_{i}^{n}=\Phi_{i}c+p_{i}H_{i+1}^{n}+q_{i}H_{i-1}^{n},\quad

\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq n-1,

\displaystyle H_{n}^{n}=0.

Then from (
32
) and Lemmas
5.7
,
5.8
, it follows that
H_{i}^{n}\leq F_{i}^{n}
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. On the other hand, from Lemma
5.9
, we get

H_{i}^{n}=cG_{i}^{n}=c(S_{n}(\Phi)-S_{i}(\Phi)).

This leads us to the estimate

S_{n}(\Phi)-S_{i}(\Phi)=\frac{1}{c}H_{i}^{n}\leq\frac{1}{c}F_{i}^{n}=\frac{1}{c}(1-\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{n}})\leq\frac{1}{c}

for all
0\leq i\leq n
, which implies the convergence of
S_{n}(\Phi)
to a finite limit.

The statement (
4
) for non-explosion follows from (
3
) and Lemma
5.6
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

5.5. Proof of the implosion condition (Theorem
3.2
)

Assume the right-hand side of (
5
) holds, that is,

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\nu_{i}\right)\delta_{k}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}=\infty.

From this we prove that
\mathsf{P}\{X\text{ implodes from }\infty\}=1
, i.e.,
X
comes down from
\infty
to any state
l
in a finite time. We first examine the hitting time
\sigma_{l}=\inf\{t\geq 0\mid X(t)=l\}
when starting from some finite state
i\geq l
. Add a point
r
on the right of
i
, and consider the minimum of hitting times
\sigma_{l}
and
\sigma_{r}
, denoted by
\sigma_{l}\wedge\sigma_{r}
. We define

F_{i}^{l,r}=1-\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{l}\wedge\sigma_{r}},\quad l\leq i\leq r,

and, analogously to derivation of (
27
) and (
28
), arrive at the system

\displaystyle F_{l}^{l,r}=F_{r}^{l,r}=0,

(34)

\displaystyle F_{i}^{l,r}=p_{i}\Phi_{i}^{+}(1-F_{i+1}^{l,r})+q_{i}\Phi_{i}^{-}(1-F_{i-1}^{l,r})+p_{i}F_{i+1}^{l,r}+q_{i}F_{i-1}^{l,r},\quad l<i<r.

We want to compare the solution of (
34
) with a solution of a simpler system

\displaystyle G_{l}^{l,r}=G_{r}^{l,r}=0,

(35)

\displaystyle G_{i}^{l,r}=\Phi_{i}+p_{i}G_{i+1}^{l,r}+q_{i}G_{i-1}^{l,r},\quad l<i<r.

It has the same probabilistic interpretation as (
29
), but now
G_{i}^{l,r}
is the total expected profit in interval
(l,r)
when starting from
i
up to hitting
l
or
r
. Recall that
(X_{k})
is the embedded Markov chain of the process
X
, and
\varkappa_{n}=\inf\{k\geq 0\mid X_{k}=n\}
. We arrive at the formula

G_{i}^{l,r}=\mathsf{E}_{i}\sum_{k=0}^{\varkappa_{l}\wedge\varkappa_{r}-1}\Phi_{X_{k}},\quad l\leq i\leq r.

Using a suitable version of comparison Lemma
5.8
, we get that
F_{i}^{l,r}\leq G_{i}^{l,r}
for all
l\leq i\leq r
.

Since
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}=\infty
, the Markov chain
(X_{k})
is recurrent due to Section
5.2
. Recall that in the recurrent case
\sigma_{\infty}=\infty
almost surely, implying
\lim_{r\to\infty}\sigma_{l}\wedge\sigma_{r}=\sigma_{l}
almost surely. Hence by Theorem of monotone convergence there exist limits

F_{i}^{l}\coloneqq\lim_{r\to\infty}F_{i}^{l,r}=1-\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{l}},\quad G_{i}^{l}\coloneqq\lim_{r\to\infty}G_{i}^{l,r}=\mathsf{E}_{i}\sum_{k=0}^{\varkappa_{l}-1}\Phi_{X_{k}}

with
F_{i}^{l}\leq G_{i}^{l}
for all
i\geq l
. Let us now rewrite the total profit as a sum over all states

G_{i}^{l}=\mathsf{E}_{i}\sum_{k=0}^{\varkappa_{l}-1}\Phi_{X_{k}}=\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}\mathsf{E}_{i}\sum_{k=0}^{\varkappa_{l}-1}\Phi_{j}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{k}=j\}}\eqqcolon\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}y_{i}(j),

(36)

where the value
y_{i}(j)
resembles the average profit at the state
j>l
when starting from
i\geq l
up to hitting
l
. Let us reduce it to the case of
j=i
. If the states
i
and
j
are different, the process gets to
j
earlier than to
l
with probability
\mathsf{P}_{i}\{\sigma_{j}<\sigma_{l}\}
, and starts afresh from
j
. Hence we have

y_{i}(j)=y_{j}(j)\mathsf{P}_{i}\{\sigma_{j}<\sigma_{l}\}.

Let us denote
y_{j}=y_{j}(j)
and examine it more closely. Starting from a state
j
, we first get a profit of
\Phi_{j}
. Then we either go right with probability
p_{j}
and return to
j
with probability one due to recurrence, or go left with probability
q_{j}
and return to
j
with probability
\mathsf{P}_{j-1}\{\sigma_{j}<\sigma_{l}\}
. This leads to the relation

y_{j}=\Phi_{j}+p_{j}y_{j}+q_{j}y_{j}\mathsf{P}_{j-1}\{\sigma_{j}<\sigma_{l}\},

from which we get the explicit formula

y_{j}=\frac{\Phi_{j}}{1-p_{j}-q_{j}\mathsf{P}_{j-1}\{\sigma_{j}<\sigma_{l}\}}=\frac{\Phi_{j}}{q_{j}\mathsf{P}_{j-1}\{\sigma_{j}>\sigma_{l}\}}.

(37)

The latter probability can be calculated as follows (see Section
5.2
):

\mathsf{P}_{j-1}\{\sigma_{j}>\sigma_{l}\}=\frac{u_{j}-u_{j-1}}{u_{j}-u_{l}}.

Recall that
\nu_{j}=\Phi_{j}\frac{p_{1}\dots p_{j-1}}{q_{1}\dots q_{j}}
. Hence (
37
) becomes

y_{j}=\frac{\Phi_{j}(u_{j}-u_{l})}{q_{j}(u_{j}-u_{j-1})}=\frac{\Phi_{j}(u_{j}-u_{l})}{q_{j}\delta_{j-1}}=\nu_{j}(u_{j}-u_{l})=\nu_{j}\sum_{k=l}^{j-1}\delta_{k}.

Let us return to the total profit
G_{i}^{l}
in the interval
(l,\infty)
up to hitting
l
. We can expand (
36
):

\displaystyle G_{i}^{l}=\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}y_{j}\mathsf{P}_{i}\{\sigma_{j}<\sigma_{l}\}\leq\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}y_{j}=\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}\nu_{j}\sum_{k=l}^{j-1}\delta_{k}=\sum_{k=l}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\nu_{j}\right)\delta_{k}<\infty.

Moreover, since
\mathsf{P}_{i}\{\sigma_{j}<\sigma_{l}\}
is increasing in
i
, the monotone convergence theorem implies

G^{l}\coloneqq\lim_{i\to\infty}G_{i}^{l}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}y_{j}\mathsf{P}_{i}\{\sigma_{j}<\sigma_{l}\}=\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}y_{j}=\sum_{k=l}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\nu_{j}\right)\delta_{k}.

(38)

Recall the alternative construction of
X
(Section
5.1
), and the alternative definition of implosion (Definition
5.1
), in which
\rho_{\infty}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\theta_{k}
represents the first time of hitting
0
when starting at
\infty
. Observe that
F_{i}^{l}=1-\mathsf{E}_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma_{l}}=1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{k=l+1}^{i}\theta_{k}}
. Then there exists a limit
F^{l}=\lim_{i\to\infty}F_{i}^{l}
, and since
F_{i}^{l}\leq G_{i}^{l}
for all
i\geq l
, we have

1-\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{k=l+1}^{\infty}\theta_{k}}=F^{l}\leq G^{l}=\sum_{k=l}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\nu_{j}\right)\delta_{k}.

(39)

The right hand side of (
39
) goes to
0
as
l\to\infty
, because the series is convergent. This implies that
\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{k=l+1}^{\infty}\theta_{k}}>0
for large enough
l
, and hence for all
l
. Thus,
\rho_{\infty}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\theta_{k}<\infty
with probability one due to Kolmogorov’s zero-one law. This finishes the first part of the proof.

Suppose
\rho_{\infty}<\infty
almost surely. Then
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}=\infty
due to Section
5.2
. Let us show that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\nu_{i}\right)\delta_{k}<\infty.

From the assumption, it is clear that for all
l\geq 0

f^{l}\coloneqq\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{k=l+1}^{\infty}\theta_{k}}\geq\mathsf{E}\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\theta_{k}}=f^{0}>0.

Analogously to explosion condition proof, using (
38
), we obtain the estimate

\sum_{k=l}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\nu_{i}\right)\delta_{k}=G^{l}\leq\frac{1}{f^{0}}F^{l}\leq\frac{1}{f^{0}},

which implies the convergence of the series and finishes the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

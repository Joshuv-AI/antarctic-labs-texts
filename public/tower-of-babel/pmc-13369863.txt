Environmental fee-to-tax reform policy driving corporate green governance performance: the sustainable path to reducing carbon emission intensity
Authors: Yajie He, Tiantian Zhang, Jia You
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Against the backdrop of growing resource constraints and ecological degradation, green development has emerged as the core pathway for advancing sustainable economic transformation. Using data from Chinese A-share listed companies on the Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchanges between 2010 and 2023, this study examines the impact and underlying mechanisms of the environmental fee-to-tax reform (EPFT) on corporate green governance performance (GGP). We find that EPFT significantly improves corporate GGP and this positive effect exhibits clear heterogeneity. It is stronger in non-resource-based cities while insignificant in resource-based cities, where stronger local environmental regulation can offset the policy’s ineffectiveness. In terms of industry characteristics, the effect is significant in non-heavy-polluting and capital-intensive industries but insignificant in heavy-polluting and non-capital-intensive industries. For managerial characteristics, the effect is concentrated in firms led by executives with environmental education or work experience, particularly CEOs, while it is insignificant for those without such backgrounds. Mechanistically, EPFT enhances GGP by alleviating financing constraints, attracting green investors, and promoting green innovation. Furthermore, EPFT strengthens the spillover effects of GGP on corporate ESG performance and CSR fulfillment, and ultimately reduces carbon emission intensity by elevating GGP levels. These findings provide empirical support and actionable insights for optimizing the environmental tax system and advancing regional sustainable development.

Introduction

In recent years, global environmental issues have grown increasingly acute. Key challenges such as climate change, biodiversity loss, and air and water pollution have continued to escalate, which have become core obstacles to the sustainable development of countries worldwide1. According to a report by the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP), global greenhouse gas emissions keep rising, thereby exacerbating the vulnerability of ecosystems2. In this context, governments and international organizations have been advocating for accelerated green transformation, and corporate behavior has emerged as a key focus. As primary actors in resource consumption and pollution emissions, companies produce substantial volumes of carbon emissions, industrial wastewater, and solid waste in the course of their production activities, with their environmental externalities being unignorable3. Therefore, enhancing corporate green governance performance has become a critical lever for attaining environmental governance goals.

Corporate green governance performance refers to the comprehensive capabilities and outcomes reflected in enterprises’ environmental management and governance practices4,5, covering dimensions such as resource utilization efficiency, pollution control level, green technology application, environmental information disclosure, and compliance behavior. Enhancing green governance performance can effectively reduce pollution source intensity, drive industrial green transformation, and contribute to the achievement of carbon peaking and carbon neutrality goals6. Although market mechanisms, social pressure, and internal corporate ethical development can, to some extent, steer corporate green transformation, these non-policy measures often yield limited effectiveness due to factors such as weak enforceability, inconsistent standards, or insufficient incentives7. In contrast, institutionalized environmental policies, particularly legally binding economic policy tools, are more effective in internalizing enterprises’ external environmental costs, incentivizing them to optimize their governance structures, and ultimately enhancing their green governance performance.

As the world’s largest developing country and carbon emitter, China’s environmental policies and green governance performance play a crucial role in the global climate framework8. The rapid economic growth in China has led to significant resource and environmental pressures. Long-standing issues such as an unbalanced industrial structure and lagging environmental regulation have resulted in persistently high levels of pollutant emissions and unsatisfactory ecological environmental quality9. According to data released by the International Energy Agency (IEA) in 2023, China’s total carbon emissions have continued to rise for many years10,11, as shown in Fig. 1. To address the institutional bottlenecks in environmental governance, China formally implemented the Environmental Protection Tax Law in 2018, completing the transition from the “pollution fee system” to the “environmental protection tax system,” known as the “Environmental Fee-to-Tax” (EPFT) reform12. This reform marks a new stage in China’s environmental economic policy, shifting towards rule of law and standardization. Compared to the previous pollution fee system, the environmental tax is more legally binding and can effectively raise the cost of pollution for businesses. It guides companies to increase environmental investments, adopt clean production technologies, and optimize governance mechanisms, thereby improving their green governance performance. Therefore, EPFT is a key institutional tool for driving corporate transformation and achieving ecological civilization goals.Fig. 1Trends in China’s carbon emissions. In current environmental governance research, scholars primarily focus on macro-level issues such as green innovation13, clean technology development14, and energy structure transformation15. These studies provide solid theoretical support for advancing ecological construction and achieving the dual carbon goals. However, discussions regarding the internal green governance performance of enterprises remain insufficient. As key micro-level entities in the implementation of environmental policies and the enhancement of resource utilization efficiency, the green governance level of enterprises directly affects the actual outcomes of carbon emission reductions16,17. This paper delves into the impact paths and mechanisms of EPFT policy on corporate green governance performance, filling the gap in existing research that overlooks the governance dimension of enterprises. Through theoretical construction and empirical testing, it offers a new analytical framework for understanding the interaction between environmental policies and corporate behavior. In the context of increasingly stringent environmental regulation and the growing emphasis on corporate environmental responsibility, clarifying the mechanisms through which EPFT affects corporate green governance performance helps assess the effectiveness of policy implementation, guides precise policy optimization, and provides actionable recommendations for enterprises to achieve green transformation.

The contributions of this study are mainly reflected in the following aspects: First, from the environmental tax policy perspective, this study systematically examines the impact and internal mechanism of the EPFT on GGP, clarifying how the tax system guides corporate green transformation through constraint and incentive effects. Unlike prior studies focusing on macro emission reduction or a single dimension of corporate green innovation, this study links environmental tax policies to micro-level corporate green governance, expanding the theoretical boundaries of green governance research and filling the gap in exploring the specific impact mechanism of environmental taxes on corporate green governance.

Second, this study identifies the heterogeneous effects of EPFT on GGP and explores the boundary conditions of policy effectiveness. Empirically, EPFT’s positive effect is more significant in non-resource-based cities, non-heavy-polluting industries, and capital-intensive enterprises, but insignificant in resource-based cities, heavy-polluting industries, and non-capital-intensive enterprises. Notably, stronger local environmental regulation offsets EPFT’s ineffectiveness in resource-based cities. In terms of managerial characteristics, the effect concentrates on firms led by executives with environmental backgrounds, with a particular focus on CEOs, and is insignificant otherwise. These findings reveal that GGP is jointly shaped by policy, regional endowments, industrial traits, and managerial human capital, providing empirical support for differentiated environmental governance strategies and targeted policy design.

Third, via a mediation effect model, this study identifies three key transmission pathways through which EPFT enhances GGP: alleviating financing constraints, attracting green investors, and promoting green innovation. It overcomes the limitation of prior single-pathway studies, confirming that corporate green transformation depends on the synergy of capital support, green investment, and technological innovation, and provides practical references for optimizing corporate green governance pathways.

Finally, this study explores the spillover effects of EPFT-driven GGP improvement, finding that enhanced GGP significantly boosts corporate ESG performance, CSR fulfillment, and ultimately reduces carbon emission intensity. This extends the research perspective on environmental tax policy value, linking EPFT to corporate sustainable development and regional green transformation, and offers practical guidance for optimizing the environmental tax system and promoting green transformation.

Literature review

Existing studies on EPFT have primarily focused on its environmental effects and fiscal and financial impacts. Regarding environmental effects, Kong et al.18 argue that EPFT has effectively promoted pollutant emission reduction and improved environmental quality by increasing pollution discharge costs and strengthening legal enforceability, thereby enhancing the standardization and efficiency of environmental governance. At the fiscal and financial level, EPFT is regarded as an important means to optimize fiscal revenue structures19, strengthening local governments’ fiscal capacity for environmental governance and, to a certain extent, facilitating the flow and allocation of green financial resources12. Additionally, in response to tax pressure, firms are more inclined to adopt environmental protection technologies and improve green management practices to mitigate institutional cost burdens4,5. Thus, as a key institutional arrangement in the environmental governance system, EPFT embodies the legalization of the “polluter pays” principle, driving green initiatives among both firms and governments across multiple dimensions20, and has become a critical policy tool in green transformation pathways.

Notably, existing studies mostly comb EPFT and corporate green governance research separately, lacking critical and systematic analysis of their interactive relationship, and failing to clarify why the EPFT-green governance action mechanism is ignored and the limitations of single-path analysis. Internationally, European and American scholars have verified the role of environmental tax reform in driving corporate green governance from the perspective of policy rigidity10,11,21 and market incentives22,23, while studies on emerging economies focus on the synergy between environmental tax and local institutional quality24,25, which are rarely integrated into existing research. To fill this gap, this study systematically explores the interactive relationship between EPFT and corporate green governance, critically points out the deficiencies of existing single-path analysis, integrates international classic and latest literatures, clarifies its multi-channel transmission mechanism, defines the theoretical contribution and research positioning of this paper, and establishes a logical connection with subsequent research hypotheses.

Research on the influencing factors of corporate green governance has focused on three main dimensions: internal governance structure, external institutional environment, and market pressure. Regarding internal governance structure, Yi et al.26 suggest that endogenous factors such as corporate executive characteristics, organizational culture, and board composition exert significant influences on green governance. Executives’ environmental awareness and educational background can shape firms’ adoption of green strategies. An organizational culture with pro-environmental orientations can foster green preferences in decision-making processes27, while board diversity and independence are regarded as key factors driving the effectiveness of green governance. In terms of the external institutional environment, government environmental regulations, policy guidance, and regulatory oversight intensity are important drivers of green governance. Tools such as administrative law enforcement, environmental standards, and tax incentives can establish positive incentive and constraint mechanisms for firms28, thereby optimizing resource allocation and production processes. Market pressure stems from changes in consumer preferences, investor preferences, and public opinion. Against the backdrop of the increasing sophistication of green finance and ESG rating mechanisms, firms face higher requirements in terms of reputation, financing, and market competition29.

At the level of the impacts of corporate green governance, existing studies mainly examine its effects on economic, environmental, and social performance. In terms of economic performance, green governance can enhance firms’ resource utilization efficiency30, strengthen technological innovation capabilities, and reduce environmental risks31. By implementing clean production, energy conservation, emission reduction32, and green procurement33, firms can reduce long-term operational costs34, which also contributes to enhancing brand value and market competitiveness6. In terms of environmental performance, as an important manifestation of corporate environmental responsibility, green governance shapes pollutant emission levels, energy structure optimization, and ecological footprint management15, thereby achieving green transformation at the firm level. Furthermore, green governance is regarded as an important means to enhance social performance35,36, the environmental responsibility demonstrated by firms in the process of green governance facilitates the building of good public relations, enhances social recognition16,17, and bolsters employees sense of belonging and satisfaction, thereby forming a positive cycle of governance and responsibility.

In summary, existing research has notable deficiencies in the critical analysis of the interaction between EPFT and GGP (as shown in Fig. 2). The core limitation is the persistence of a unidirectional analytical logic, which neglects the inherently bidirectional and dynamic nature of their interrelationship. Prior studies neither explore the mechanisms of mutual empowerment and co-evolution between EPFT and GGP nor critically assess how a single analytical perspective impairs the objectivity and comprehensiveness of research conclusions, thus hindering the revelation of their complex interaction patterns. Overall, a distinct theoretical gap exists in this field, as no systematic analytical framework for examining their interaction has yet been developed. This study addresses this gap by critically evaluating the limitations of extant research, systematically synthesizing relevant findings, and clarifying the underlying logic of the EPFT-GGP interaction. It not only refines the research framework for the interplay between environmental regulation and corporate green governance to fill the theoretical void but also offers practical insights for the global optimization of EPFT policies, the advancement of corporate green governance practices, and the promotion of industrial green transformation.Fig. 2Literature review.

Policy background and research hypotheses

Policy background

EPFT is a landmark institutional adjustment in the field of environmental regulation, which is rooted in the long-term practice of the pollutant discharge fee system and aims to solve the inherent defects of the original system through statutory institutional design. The pollutant discharge fee system was formally established in the Trial Version of Environmental Protection Law of the People’s Republic of China in 1979, and was fully implemented nationwide with the issuance of the Regulations on the Administration of Collection and Use of Pollutant Discharge Fees in 2003. As the core environmental regulation tool for a long time, the pollutant discharge fee system has played a positive role in restraining enterprise pollution discharge and promoting environmental governance, but it has also exposed inherent defects in the long-term implementation process, including insufficient law enforcement rigidity, inconsistent regional collection standards, weak collection and management constraints, and local protectionism, which limited the long-term effectiveness of environmental regulation.

To address these defects and promote the modernization of the environmental governance system, the Environmental Protection Tax Law of the People’s Republic of China was officially adopted at the 25th Session of the Standing Committee of the 12th National People’s Congress on December 25, 2016, and was formally implemented nationwide on January 1, 2018. The implementation of this law marks the official completion of the EPFT reform, and the pollutant discharge fee system that had been implemented for nearly 40 years was abolished simultaneously. This law is the first single-line tax law in China that specifically reflects the “green tax system” and promotes the construction of ecological civilization, and it is also the first tax law formulated after the reform task of “implementing the statutory principle of taxation” was put forward, which has a solid legal and institutional basis.

The institutional changes of the reform that constitute exogenous shocks to micro enterprises are reflected in three key dimensions. First, the reform has realized the statutory transformation of pollution discharge levy, which raises the legal level of environmental regulation, clarifies the legal liability of non-compliance, and fundamentally enhances the rigidity of environmental regulation enforcement. Second, the reform has reconstructed the collection and management system, changing the main body of collection and management from the environmental protection department to a collaborative model led by tax authorities and supported by environmental protection departments, which effectively reduces the interference of local protectionism and improves the standardization and enforcement of collection and management. Third, the reform has established a more scientific levy and incentive mechanism, taking the original pollutant discharge fee standard as the lower limit of the environmental tax amount, allowing provincial governments to adjust the tax amount within a specified range, and setting a clear tax reduction incentive for enterprises with pollutant discharge concentration lower than the national and local standards, which takes into account the unification of the regulatory framework and the flexibility of regional adjustment, and strengthens the endogenous incentive for enterprises to carry out long-term environmental governance.

The EPFT is implemented uniformly nationwide at a fixed time point, and the institutional adjustment is formulated and promoted at the national level, which is completely independent of the decision-making of individual micro enterprises. This feature makes the reform an ideal quasi-natural experiment scenario, which can effectively identify the causal effect of environmental regulation on corporate green governance behavior, and provides a solid institutional foundation for the empirical design of this study.

Theoretical analysis and research hypotheses

The impact of EPFT on corporate green governance performance

GGP in this paper refers to systematic, institutionalized and strategy-level environmental governance practices led by the board of directors, including the establishment of special environmental governance committees37, the formulation of long-term green development strategies, the construction of full-process environmental risk oversight mechanisms, the integration of environmental objectives into executive performance assessment, and the improvement of standardized green information disclosure systems38. The acquisition and maintenance of ISO14000 environmental management system certification and green audit awards of enterprises are based on the above board-led green governance institutional arrangements, while the occurrence of environmental violations and sudden environmental accidents is essentially the result of the absence or ineffective implementation of corporate green governance top-level design. This core connotation is fundamentally different from passive end-of-pipe compliance behaviors such as simply purchasing pollution control equipment, and also differs from single-dimensional green technological innovation activities39. GGP focuses on the top-level governance structure and internal control system that support the long-term stable improvement of corporate environmental performance, rather than fragmented, temporary environmental response behaviors.

EPFT reform promotes significant improvement in corporate GGP by enhancing the legal enforceability, rigidity and uniformity of environmental regulation. The statutory nature of environmental taxation raises the marginal cost of non-compliance, unifies emission levy standards across regions and industries, and strengthens the full-process supervision of pollutant discharge, which makes temporary end-of-pipe treatment face increasing marginal costs and uncontrollable compliance risks. Simply relying on one-off pollution control equipment investment can no longer meet the continuous, standardized regulatory requirements brought by EPFT, and will even lead to higher long-term operating costs and legal risks. This institutional pressure will be transmitted from the operational level to the top decision-making level of the enterprise, forcing the board of directors to take environmental management as a core part of corporate strategy, rather than a subsidiary operational issue. To systematically reduce environmental costs, control compliance risks and adapt to long-term regulatory trends, the board of directors must adjust the internal governance structure, improve the environmental oversight system, and establish a long-term green governance mechanism, which directly drives the comprehensive improvement of corporate GGP.

Based on the above analysis, this study proposes Hypothesis 1:

H1

EPFT can significantly promote the improvement of corporate GGP.

Mechanism of financing constraint alleviation

EPFT alleviates corporate financing constraints by strengthening environmental compliance incentives, improving information transparency and optimizing market expectations, and the alleviation of financing constraints further drives the improvement of corporate GGP. EPFT raises the marginal cost of pollutant discharge, driving enterprises to incorporate environmental governance into business strategies, increase targeted environmental investment and improve substantive environmental performance40, which sends positive signals of standardized operation and sustainable development to the capital market. According to signaling theory, enterprises with sound environmental practices convey information of high operational quality and low environmental risk to financial institutions and investors, enhancing their reputation and credibility in the capital market41. The implementation of EPFT also improves the standardization of environmental information disclosure and supervision, reduces information asymmetry between enterprises and investors, alleviates adverse selection in the credit market, and enables enterprises with excellent environmental performance to obtain more low-cost financing support.

The alleviation of financing constraints can effectively act on the core links of corporate green governance, rather than just increasing one-off environmental investment. Corporate green governance system construction is a long-term work with continuous capital demand, including the introduction of professional environmental governance talents, the establishment of special environmental governance committees, the construction of full-process environmental risk internal control systems, and the formulation and implementation of long-term green development strategies. The improvement of capital availability brought by financing constraint alleviation provides stable financial support for these top-level governance system construction, and fills the resource gap that cannot be covered by temporary end-of-pipe investment. The reduction of financing pressure also alleviates the short-term profit pressure of the management, enables the board of directors to focus on long-term sustainable development, and avoids myopic decisions that only pursue short-term financial returns through temporary compliance. Meanwhile, sufficient financial resources enhance the risk-bearing capacity of enterprises, making the board of directors more willing to incorporate environmental objectives into the top-level strategic design, and promote the transformation of environmental management from passive compliance to active strategic governance, which comprehensively improves the level of corporate GGP.

Based on the above analysis, this study proposes Hypothesis 2:

H2

EPFT can improve corporate GGP by alleviating corporate financing constraints.

Mechanism of green investor attraction

EPFT reform helps enterprises attract green investors with clear environmental preferences and long-term value investment philosophy, and the participation of green investors further promotes the improvement of corporate GGP. EPFT serves as a credible policy certification for enterprises’ green transformation practices, unifies the measurement standard of corporate environmental performance, reduces the information screening cost of green investors, and matches the investment preference of green investors that focus on corporate long-term environmental governance capabilities. The statutory and uniform environmental tax standard also makes the environmental performance of enterprises more comparable across the industry, enabling green investors to more accurately identify enterprises with substantive green governance practices, and promoting more green investors to hold shares and participate in corporate governance.

Different from the pure capital supplement effect of financing constraint alleviation, the core role of green investors lies in their targeted governance intervention, which directly acts on the top-level governance structure of enterprises. Green investors as professional institutional investors with clear environmental goals have stronger motivation and professional capacity to supervise corporate environmental behavior, and will actively intervene in the decision-making of the board of directors through shareholder voting, proposal submission and in-depth negotiation. They will urge the board of directors to establish a special environmental governance committee, clarify the environmental responsibilities of directors and executives, and integrate environmental performance into the executive assessment system, to improve the institutionalization level of green governance from the top-level design. Most green investors are long-term value investors, whose investment returns are closely tied to the long-term sustainable development of enterprises, which can effectively alleviate the short-sighted agency problem of the management, and incentivize the board of directors to formulate and implement long-term green development strategies, rather than relying on temporary compliance measures. In addition, the entry of well-known green investors will further enhance the market’s attention to corporate environmental governance, push the board of directors to improve the quality of green information disclosure, and establish a full-process environmental risk oversight mechanism, forming a benign promotion effect on the continuous improvement of corporate GGP.

Based on the above analysis, this study proposes Hypothesis 3:

H3

EPFT can improve corporate GGP by attracting green investors.

Mechanism of green technological innovation

EPFT reform significantly promotes corporate green technological innovation, and the continuous improvement of green technological innovation capabilities further drives the comprehensive improvement of corporate GGP. According to the Porter Hypothesis, reasonable environmental regulation can stimulate enterprises to carry out innovative activities to offset compliance costs. EPFT internalizes the environmental costs of enterprises, raises the marginal cost of pollutant discharge, creates a pressure-driven mechanism that compels enterprises to increase R&D investment in green technologies, and promotes enterprises to shift from end-of-pipe treatment to source clean production technology innovation. Meanwhile, EPFT sends a clear long-term green development signal to the market, prompting enterprises to prioritize green technology R&D in their strategic planning, and form core competitive advantages through green technological innovation.

Green technological innovation is not only a technical activity, but also a systematic project that requires the matching support of the top-level governance system, which in turn forces the continuous improvement of corporate green governance. The implementation of source clean production technology and full-process green process innovation requires the overall adjustment of the enterprise’s production process, supply chain management and internal control system, which must be led by the board of directors and supported by a sound green governance system. To ensure the smooth implementation and transformation of green technological innovation achievements, the board of directors must establish a corresponding green R&D management system, set up a special environmental technology supervision department, and integrate green innovation objectives into the corporate strategic assessment system. Meanwhile, the core competitive advantages formed by green technological innovation enable enterprises to realize the synergy of environmental benefits and economic benefits, making green governance shift from passive compliance requirements to active value creation behaviors. This will further incentivize the board of directors to improve the long-term green governance mechanism, optimize the environmental oversight system, and continuously improve the level of corporate GGP.

Based on the above analysis, this study proposes Hypothesis 4:

H4

EPFT can improve corporate GGP by promoting corporate green technological innovation.

Research design

Data

This study uses A-share listed companies on China’s Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchanges from 2010 to 2023 as the initial research sample. The sample screening follows the steps below. First, we exclude financial listed companies with special accounting standards and business models. Second, we remove ST and *ST listed companies with sustained losses. Third, we eliminate observations with missing core variable data or significantly abnormal values. Finally, we apply 1% winsorization to all continuous variables at the upper and lower tails to mitigate the interference of extreme values on regression results.

Corporate financial and governance data are obtained from CSMAR database. The raw data for Corporate Green Governance Performance (GGP) are collected from listed companies’ annual reports, social responsibility reports and environmental reports, and supplemented by the CSMAR database and CNRDS database. The heavy-polluting industry classification is based on the official catalogue issued by the Ministry of Ecology and Environment of the People’s Republic of China, and the full catalogue with corresponding industry codes is attached as Appendix A of the manuscript.

Variable definitions

Dependent variable

The dependent variable of this study is GGP, measured by the J-F coefficient method based on multi-dimensional positive and negative governance indicators. We choose this method for three core theoretical and practical reasons. First, the J-F coefficient method uses a piecewise function design to effectively distinguish the positive incentive of compliant green governance practices and the negative penalty of environmental violations, which highly fits the dual attributes of green governance. Second, this method is an objective evaluation method based on the actual performance of sample enterprises, which avoids subjective weight bias from expert scoring or hierarchical analysis, and ensures the neutrality and horizontal comparability of evaluation results. Third, compared with other comprehensive evaluation methods, the J-F coefficient method has more stable anti-interference ability for binary discrete indicator systems, which is highly consistent with the characteristics of our GGP indicator system. This method has also been widely used in corporate governance performance measurement in authoritative domestic and international literature, with sufficient academic support.

We construct the GGP indicator system with 3 positive governance indicators and 4 negative governance indicators, covering the core dimensions of corporate environmental compliance, governance practice and risk management. These indicators are not simply environmental compliance or performance indicators, but the direct external reflection of the implementation effect of enterprise green governance institutional arrangements. The positive indicators reflect the effective operation of the board-led green governance system, and the negative indicators reflect the failure of green governance mechanisms, which can effectively map the actual level of corporate green governance performance. The positive indicators (p) include whether the enterprise has obtained ISO 14000 series environmental management system certification, whether the enterprise has obtained environmental protection honors or green audit awards, and whether the enterprise has fully complied with pollutant discharge standards in the current year. All positive indicators are assigned 1 for “yes” and 0 for “no”, and the total positive score p is the sum of the three indicators, with a value range of 0–3. The negative indicators (q) include whether the enterprise has received environmental administrative penalties, whether the enterprise has had sudden environmental accidents, whether the enterprise has been identified as having environmental violations, and whether the enterprise has received major environmental complaint cases in the current year. Explanatory variable

This reform represents a fundamental institutional adjustment in China’s environmental regulation system, with heavy polluting industries as its statutory core supervision and levy objects. The grouping of treat is thus set in line with the policy orientation. The explanatory variable of this study is the EPFT, a nationwide quasi natural experiment formally implemented in the People’s Republic of China on January 1, 2018. This reform represents a fundamental institutional adjustment in China’s environmental regulation system, officially abolishing the nearly 40 year old pollutant discharge fee system established in 1979 and replacing it with a statutory environmental tax regime. Its core objectives are to enhance the rigidity, standardization, and enforceability of environmental regulation, address inherent flaws in the original fee system such as insufficient law enforcement, inconsistent regional standards, and local protectionism, and promote the green transformation of enterprises.

To accurately identify the causal effect of the EPFT reform on GGP, this study employs a difference in differences method, constructing two key dummy variables and their interaction term as follows. First, the grouping dummy variable treat: enterprises in heavy polluting industries are assigned a value of 1 as the experimental group, while those in non heavy polluting industries are assigned a value of 0 as the control group. The classification of heavy polluting industries strictly adheres to the Catalogue of Heavy Polluting Industries for Environmental Statistics issued by the Ministry of Ecology and Environment of the People’s Republic of China, formerly the Ministry of Environmental Protection, the classification standards of authoritative existing literature, and the National Economic Industry Classification (GB/T 4754 2017). Specifically, heavy polluting industries cover 11 major categories under three industrial sectors, namely Mining, Manufacturing, and Textile Industry, with clear corresponding sub industries and national economic industry classification codes, ensuring the accuracy, operability, and rigor of the grouping. These industries are characterized by high pollutant emissions, high energy consumption, heavy environmental pressure, and strong sensitivity to environmental regulation, making them the key supervision targets of the EPFT reform and subject to more significant policy shocks.

Second, the time dummy variable post: taking 2018, the official implementation year of the EPFT reform, as the critical time node, the period before 2018 from 2010 to 2017 is assigned a value of 0, and the period from 2018 to 2023 is assigned a value of 1. This definition is fully consistent with the time span of the research sample and the actual implementation cycle of the reform. The interaction term treat × post is the core coefficient to be estimated in this study, which specifically measures the average net policy effect of the EPFT reform on the GGP of heavy polluting enterprises in the experimental group relative to non heavy polluting enterprises in the control group. This design effectively isolates the impact of other time varying factors such as macroeconomic fluctuations and other environmental policies and achieves reliable causal identification of the policy effect on the statutory core regulatory objects.

Parallel trend test

The DID estimator relies on the counterfactual framework and requires that the treatment and control groups exhibit parallel trends in the absence of treatment. To test this assumption and examine the dynamic effects of the EPFT reform, we employ an event study approach with the period immediately before policy implementation (t − 1) as the baseline. The results, presented in Fig. 4, show that the estimated coefficients for all pre-policy periods (pre_3 to pre_1) are statistically insignificant, confirming the parallel trend assumption. In the policy implementation year (current), the coefficient remains insignificant, indicating no immediate effect. From the second year after implementation (post_2) onward, the coefficients become positive and statistically significant at least at the 5% level, while post_1 and post_3 are positive but marginally significant (p < 0.10). These findings suggest that the EPFT reform has a lagged positive impact on corporate green governance performance, with the effect strengthening over time as evidenced by the increasing magnitude in later periods (e.g., post_4). Robustness test

The Heckman two-stage model seeks to address potential sample selection bias. In this study, whether a firm is a heavy-polluting firm is used as the dependent variable to specify a first-stage Probit model, and the Inverse Mills Ratio (IMR) is computed. In the second stage, IMR is included as a control variable, and the regression is re-estimated. Results are presented in Column (3) of Table 6. The coefficient of the EPFT variable is 0.013, statistically significant at the 10% level, indicating that after controlling for selection bias, EPFT still exerts a significantly positive impact on GGP. Meanwhile, the statistically significant negative coefficient of IMR confirms the presence of sample selection bias.

Lagged dependent variables help control for inertial factors in GGP, enhancing the model’s explanatory power. Additionally, this method can mitigate potential endogeneity issues: introducing lagged terms helps identify the true direction and persistence of policy effects. This study regresses the dependent variable on its one- and two-period lags. The EPFT coefficient remains positive and statistically significant in Columns (4) and (5) of Table 6, indicating that EPFT has a significant positive effect on GGP, confirming the robustness of the baseline results.

To isolate the impact of other policies implemented during the sample period, this study introduces dummy variables for the Environmental Protection Law (HBF), central environmental protection inspections (ZYHB), regional carbon emission trading pilots (TJY), and green financial reform (GFR) into Model (2) to identify and exclude potential confounding effects, thereby more accurately estimating the marginal impact of EPFT. Regression results are presented in Column (6) of Table 6. The EPFT coefficient remains positive and statistically significant at the 10% level, indicating that after controlling for these concurrent policies, EPFT still has a significant positive effect on GGP, strengthening the validity and reliability of the findings.

To isolate unobserved time-varying heterogeneity at the provincial and industrial levels and rule out confounding effects arising from regional policy adjustments, industry cyclical fluctuations and sector-wide technological changes, we augment our baseline regression with additional joint fixed effects. Specifically, Column (7) of Table 6 adds industry by year joint fixed effects (Ind × Year) on top of the baseline industry and year fixed effects, while Column (8) adds province by year joint fixed effects (Prov × Year). These high-dimensional specifications effectively absorb time-varying external shocks at the industrial and provincial levels respectively. The regression results show that the coefficient of EPFT remains significantly positive at the 10% level in both columns, which is fully consistent with our baseline finding. This verifies that the positive effect of the EPFT reform on corporate GGP is not biased by unobserved time-varying external interference, thus confirming the robustness of our core conclusion.

To address potential concerns about the measurement of GGP using the J-F coefficient, we conduct a robustness test by replacing the dependent variable with the environmental pillar score of ESG ratings (data sourced from Wind ESG Database). This alternative measure reflects firms’ environmental performance from a multi-dimensional perspective (including environmental management, emission reduction, and green investment) and is widely recognized in academic research for its granularity and objectivity. The regression results (Table 6 Column 9) show that the coefficient of EPFT remains positive and statistically significant at the 1% level when explaining the ESG environmental pillar score, consistent with the baseline findings that EPFT significantly improves corporate green governance performance. This confirms that our core conclusions are not driven by the choice of GGP measurement methods and are robust to alternative indicators.

To address the concern about nationwide policy shocks and verify the reliability of baseline results, we conduct a continuous DID test using provincial variance in EPFT tax rate hikes as the core explanatory variable. We first calculate the tax rate increase ratio for each pollutant in each province, defined as the percentage change in environmental tax rates relative to the original pollution discharge fee standards (a continuous variable). We then construct a triple interaction term tax_rate_increase × treat × post (DDD) to capture the heterogeneous policy effects of varying tax hike intensities. Regression results (Table 6 Column 10) show the coefficient of this triple interaction term is significantly positive at the 1% level, indicating that a larger provincial tax rate increase corresponds to a stronger positive effect of EPFT on heavy-polluting firms’ GGP. This result confirms the robustness of our baseline DID conclusion and further validates the policy intensity mechanism underlying the EPFT’s effect on corporate green governance.

Urban type heterogeneity

Resource-based and non-resource-based cities differ significantly in resource endowments, industrial structure, environmental governance foundations, and policy enforcement capacity. Resource-based cities are mostly reliant on traditional resource-intensive industries (e.g., coal), with a relatively singular economic structure and substantial pressure for industrial transformation. Firms in these cities tend to prioritize short-term economic gains, which constrains their enthusiasm and capacity for green governance43. Meanwhile, local governments may exhibit flexibility in enforcing environmental policies to safeguard economic growth and employment. In contrast, non-resource-based cities have stronger capacities for economic diversification and higher awareness of green development, firms here are also more likely to obtain financial and technical support for environmental investment, thus possessing greater capacity and willingness to respond to environmental policies.

To test the impact of urban type heterogeneity on policy effects, this study divides sample firms into resource-based and non-resource-based city groups based on the State Council’s list of resource-based cities and conducts subgroup regression analyses. Heterogeneity tests.

Industry type heterogeneity

Based on the baseline did design that takes heavy-polluting enterprises as the treatment group to identify the policy’s net impact on statutory core supervision objects, this section further explores the heterogeneous response of different industries to the same EPFT policy shock. The essential reason for the differentiated policy effects between heavy-polluting and non-heavy-polluting industries lies in the significant differences in their production characteristics, transformation costs and policy response capabilities, rather than the absence of policy shocks on heavy-polluting industries. Heavy-polluting and non-heavy-polluting industries differ significantly in production processes, pollutant emissions, environmental pressure, and institutional responsiveness. Heavy-polluting industries are characterized by high asset specificity, strong technical path dependence, and rigid environmental investment allocation; their production systems are deeply locked in traditional high-emission models, and short-term green transformation faces an obvious technical ceiling, making it difficult to achieve substantive improvement in green governance performance through incremental policy incentives. More importantly, the current environmental tax rate of China’s EPFT reform is set with the original pollution fee standard as the lower limit, and the regional floating range is limited; the marginal tax cost borne by heavy-polluting enterprises is still lower than the total cost of green technology transformation and full-process pollution control, leading to a “pay to pollute” rational choice for such enterprises. In contrast, non-heavy-polluting industries have low asset specificity and flexible production adjustment space, with no obvious technical bottleneck in green transformation; the tax-induced marginal cost increase is sufficient to trigger proactive environmental governance behaviors, and their environmental investment is more incremental and flexible, affording greater room for adjustment in green governance. Such fundamental differences in industrial characteristics lead to significant heterogeneity in the policy implementation effects of EPFT.

To test the transmission mechanism of policy incentives at the firm level, this study divides sample firms into heavy-polluting and non-heavy-polluting industry groups based on the classification by the Ministry of Ecology and Environment of the People’s Republic of China and conducts subgroup regressions. Table 7 Column (3) shows that in non-heavy-polluting industries, the impact coefficient of EPFT is statistically significant at the 5% level, indicating that EPFT can significantly enhance GGP in non-heavy-polluting industries. In Column (4), the coefficient for heavy-polluting industries is positive but statistically insignificant, meaning heavy-polluting industries did not significantly improve GGP after policy implementation. This counter-intuitive result essentially reflects the dual constraints of technical transformation barriers and insufficient marginal tax incentives for heavy-polluting industries, rather than the failure of the EPFT policy itself; it also reveals that the current EPFT system needs to be combined with differentiated tax rates and technical support policies to form an effective incentive for heavy-polluting industries. Thus, following EPFT implementation, non-heavy-polluting firms are more likely to proactively respond to tax-induced cost changes through green technology upgrading and process optimization, thereby enhancing their green governance performance.

Heterogeneity in capital-intensive industries

Differences in firms’ capital and resource allocation capacities may lead to significant variations in their response pathways and adjustment flexibility to environmental policies. Capital-intensive industries have stronger financial strength and technical foundations, enabling them to respond promptly to policy-induced cost changes by introducing green technologies, upgrading environmental protection equipment, and optimizing production processes, thus improving green governance levels. Non-capital-intensive industries, mostly composed of small and medium-sized enterprises, are constrained by capital, technology, and management capacities, resulting in a weaker ability to adapt to tax system changes and greater obstacles to improving green governance. Using industry capital intensity as a grouping metric helps reveal the mechanism through which EPFT operates at the micro-firm level. Referring to the Industrial Classification for National Economic Activities and using the ratio of firms’ fixed assets to total assets (those above the median are classified as capital-intensive), this study divides sample firms into capital-intensive and non-capital-intensive industry groups for subgroup regressions. In Column (6), the EPFT coefficient for capital-intensive industries statistically significant at the 10% level, indicating that EPFT significantly promotes GGP in capital-intensive industries; in Column (5), the coefficient for non-capital-intensive industries is statistically insignificant. This result highlights the key role of capital factor endowments in shaping EPFT’s effectiveness.

Managerial characteristic heterogeneity

Executives’ environmental awareness and professional backgrounds may affect firms’ cognition, response speed, and governance capacity regarding green policies. Executives with professional knowledge in environmental science and sustainable development, among others, are better able to identify long-term strategic benefits of environmental investment. Executives with environmental backgrounds are more likely to implement green governance and optimize resource allocation proactively. In contrast, executives without environmental backgrounds may perceive EPFT as a singular cost constraint, resulting in a relatively weak willingness to invest in green governance and lower execution efficiency. This study conducts a heterogeneity test on managerial characteristics, dividing firms into those with executives possessing environmental backgrounds and those without, to examine how managerial traits influence policy effects. Based on executive resumes from the CSMAR database of listed companies, this study employs text analysis to identify keywords related to environmental governance and sustainable development, classifies executives’ environmental backgrounds, and conducts subgroup regressions. Column (7) shows that for firms with executives possessing environmental backgrounds, the coefficient is 0.022, statistically significant at the 5% level, indicating that EPFT can significantly improve GGP; in Column (8), for firms with executives lacking environmental backgrounds, the EPFT coefficient is statistically insignificant. Thus, executives’ environmental cognition and professional competence can strengthen the incentive effect of policies.

(2) Managerial environmental background as a transmission channel

Columns (3) and (4) of Table 8 present subgroup regressions by executive environmental background. For firms whose executives have environmental education or work experience (Column 3), EPFT exerts a significant positive effect on GGP. In contrast, for executives without such backgrounds (Column 4), the coefficient is statistically insignificant. These results confirm that environmental literacy serves as a critical transmission belt: executives with relevant expertise can better interpret policy requirements and implement green strategies, while those without lack the capacity to do so.

To further unpack this mechanism, we refine the classification of environmental backgrounds into two distinct types: educational background (holding a degree in environmental science, environmental engineering, or related fields) and work experience (prior employment in environmental protection agencies, green technology firms, or sustainability-related positions). As shown in Columns (5) and (6) of Table 8, both subgroups exhibit significant positive coefficients, yet the magnitude is larger for executives with work experience than for those with educational background only. This suggests that hands-on experience in environmental fields may confer more actionable knowledge and stronger implementation capabilities than formal education alone.

Additionally, we examine heterogeneity across executive positions. Columns (7) and (8) report results for CEO and board chair subgroups, respectively. The EPFT coefficient is significant for CEOs but insignificant for board chairs. This finding aligns with the organizational hierarchy perspective: CEOs, who are directly responsible for firm operations and resource allocation, possess greater authority to translate regulatory pressure into concrete green governance actions, whereas board chairs focus more on oversight than on operational execution.

Together, these results underscore that the effectiveness of EPFT in stimulating green governance depends not only on whether executives possess environmental literacy, but also on the type of literacy and the positional authority they hold within the firm.

Mechanism test

It is important to clarify that the three mediating variables are not independent, parallel pathways. Instead, financing constraints, green investors, and green technological innovation are interconnected and mutually reinforcing. Their potential interactions may introduce endogenous correlations; for example, greater green innovation can attract additional green investors, which in turn helps ease financing constraints. While this study verifies each mediating path separately for analytical clarity, we explicitly acknowledge these interconnections throughout the analysis.

Mediating test of financing constraints

The institutional transformation from a fee-based system to a tax-based regime enhances the rigidity of environmental policy while improving the standardization and transparency of corporate environmental practices. This shift consequently strengthens firms’ external image and credit ratings. Such changes help enterprises gain greater recognition in financing channels such as bank loans, green credit, and government subsidies, thus alleviating financing difficulties. Therefore, this paper introduces financing constraints (FC) as a mediator to reveal the internal path through which EPFT improves GGP by alleviating financing constraints. The FC index is used as a proxy variable for the degree of financing constraints, where a larger FC value indicates more severe financing constraints. Column (1) shows that EPFT has a significantly negative impact on corporate financing constraints at the 1% level, indicating that the implementation of EPFT effectively alleviates the financing pressure of enterprises and provides financial support for the improvement of green governance performance. Column (2) shows that after controlling for EPFT, financing constraints still have a significantly negative impact on GGP at the 1% level. Mediating test of green investors

EPFT promotes high-quality environmental information disclosure and green behaviors, making enterprises more likely to attract green investors. Such capital inflows help increase funding sources for enterprises’ green projects and indirectly enhance their willingness and capacity for environmental governance. This paper selects “green investors” as a mediating variable to reflect the key role of capital markets in driving enterprises’ green transformation. The shareholding ratio of green institutional investors is used as a proxy indicator for green investors (Ginst). Columns (3) and (4) of Table 9 verify the mediating role of green institutional investors. Column (3) shows that EPFT has a significantly positive impact on the shareholding ratio of green investors at the 1% level (β = 0.014, t = 4.862), which indicates that the environmental regulatory pressure brought by EPFT makes enterprises more attractive to green institutional investors who focus on environmental performance and sustainable development. Column (4) shows that green investors have a significantly positive impact on GGP at the 1% level after controlling for EPFT (β = 0.042, t = 3.518). The Bootstrap test result shows that the indirect effect of EPFT on GGP through green investors is 0.0006 and the 95% confidence interval is [0.0002, 0.0010], which excludes zero and proves the 5% level significant partial mediating effect of green investors. It can be seen that EPFT attracts the entry of green institutional investors, and green investors play a role in supervision and resource provision, prompting enterprises to optimize green governance practices and improve GGP, verifying Research Hypothesis H3. In practice, green investors’ participation can also further ease enterprises’ financing constraints and promote green innovation, linking this path with the other two mediating channels.

Mediating test of green technological innovation

EPFT strengthens the pressure of environmental externalities on enterprises by increasing pollution costs, prompting them to seek green transformation solutions in production and management. The Porter hypothesis suggests that moderate environmental regulations can incentivize enterprises to conduct technological innovation. To test the micro-implementation path of EPFT’s policy incentive effects, green technological innovation is included in the mediating effect analysis, with the number of green patent applications used to measure green innovation level (Inno). Columns (5) and (6) of Table 9 examine the mediating effect of green innovation. Column (5) shows that EPFT has a significantly positive impact on corporate green innovation at the 1% level (β = 0.003, t = 47.259), indicating that the rigid environmental regulation of EPFT forces enterprises to increase R&D investment in green technologies and improve green innovation capabilities. Column (6) shows that green innovation has a significantly positive impact on GGP at the 1% level after controlling for EPFT (β = 3.926, t = 4.107). The Bootstrap test result shows that the indirect effect of EPFT on GGP through green innovation is 0.0118, and the 95% confidence interval is [0.0045, 0.0191], which excludes zero and verifies the 1% level significant partial mediating effect of green innovation. This suggests that EPFT drives enterprises to carry out green innovation activities, and the improvement of green innovation level further promotes the institutionalization and standardization of corporate green governance, thus effectively raising GGP, verifying Research Hypothesis H4. Importantly, improved green innovation can enhance enterprises’ attractiveness to green investors and reduce financing constraints, reflecting the interconnectedness of the three mediating paths.

Economic consequence test

To further explore the external patterns associated with EPFT‑induced green governance, we examine whether the policy is associated with stronger spillover effects of GGP on corporate ESG performance and CSR practices. This analysis complements our core findings on the direct impact of EPFT on GGP and carbon emission intensity by suggesting how internal green governance, under institutional pressure, may translate into broader social and governance outcomes.

By raising the stringency of environmental regulation, EPFT is associated with firms’ adoption of more comprehensive green governance systems that extend beyond basic environmental compliance. In the environmental dimension, this involves stricter emission control, cleaner production processes and targeted green technology adoption, which are positively associated with firms’ environmental pillar performance in ESG assessments. Socially, firms under EPFT exposure tend to integrate green procurement and low carbon supply chain management into daily operations, while strengthening stakeholder engagement with employees, local communities and environmental groups. At the governance level, EPFT is accompanied by the establishment of formal environmental responsibility mechanisms, systematic environmental risk management frameworks and transparent environmental information disclosure structures, which are in turn associated with higher overall quality of corporate governance. Together, these systematic improvements in green governance provide a supportive institutional background for firms to elevate their comprehensive ESG performance and fulfill substantive CSR commitments. Meanwhile, when subject to the regulatory pressure of EPFT, firms appear to adjust their governance behaviors to secure organizational legitimacy. EPFT exposure is associated with a tendency for firms to institutionalize green governance to meet regulatory requirements, which naturally aligns with the environmental compliance and responsibility fulfillment dimensions of CSR. In addition, firms under the policy may send outward signals of environmental responsibility through standardized green governance, which helps secure recognition from key stakeholders including governments, institutional investors and consumers. This recognition may further support their market competitiveness and brand value, and could be related to more active voluntary CSR practices.

Discussion

First, this study finds that EPFT can significantly enhance GGP. This result aligns with the research of4,5, which suggests that environmental regulations facilitate corporate green technological innovation. Jin et al.35 also notes that environmental policies prompt firms to enhance their governance structures by increasing environmental cost pressures. However, existing literature has primarily focused on the impact of environmental regulations on corporate pollution emissions or green technological innovation, with limited attention to systemic changes in firms’ internal governance performance. This study centers on green governance performance, broadening the research scope regarding the impact pathways of tax policies. Heterogeneity tests reveal that the policy effect is more pronounced in non-resource-based cities, non-heavily polluting industries, and capital-intensive firms, potentially linked to resource allocation efficiency and the sensitivity of policy response mechanisms; notably, weak environmental regulation enforcement leads to insignificant EPFT effect in resource-based cities, while strengthening regulation can restore its incentive role. This highlights the role of regional resource allocation and corporate characteristics in policy transmission mechanisms. Additionally, this paper examines how differences in executives’ environmental backgrounds influence policy effects, uncovering that the positive effect is stronger for CEOs than for board chairs and highlighting the key role of corporate human capital and institutional arrangements, thereby enriching the theoretical landscape of environmental economics concerning institution-driven corporate green transformation.

Second, mechanism analysis reveals that EPFT can significantly enhance GGP by mitigating firms’ financing constraints, attracting green investors, and fostering green innovation, and these three paths are mutually correlated and mutually reinforcing, forming a positive feedback loop. Identifying this mechanism offers a new perspective for understanding the internal logic of how environmental policies drive corporate green transformation. Existing studies have primarily explored the impact of environmental regulations from the lens of macro policy orientation or micro cost constraints. Peng and Kong36 proposes that environmental regulations may stimulate corporate innovation and Song et al.44 notes that green financial instruments play a key role in alleviating financing bottlenecks for green projects. However, these studies have focused on single mechanisms or external incentives without systematically elucidating how EPFT drives the greening of corporate governance structures through multi-dimensional mechanisms. Using micro-level corporate data and combining multi-channel mechanism identification methods, this paper is the first to systematically unpack the multiple pathways through which EPFT enhances GGP, deepening the theoretical underpinnings of policy performance evaluation and providing a practical foundation for governments to formulate more targeted green policies.

Third, further analysis indicates that EPFT can significantly strengthen the positive effect of GGP on corporate ESG performance and CSR fulfillment. This finding extends the spillover scope of environmental policies, revealing the transmission pathway through which GGP influences corporate sustainable development in the context of environmental regulations. Existing research in ESG and CSR has primarily focused on the driving role of corporate responsibility awareness, external reputation mechanisms, and capital market pressures in sustainable development. Yadav and Asongu45 points out that firms with high ESG performance exhibit stronger long-term financial stability, Jadiyappa et al.46 emphasizes that CSR fulfillment helps strengthen stakeholder relationships and enhance corporate competitiveness. However, limited existing research has examined how green governance performance affects ESG and CSR under institutional support from a policy and institutional perspective. This paper uncovers the catalytic role of EPFT in strengthening the relationship between GGP and ESG/CSR, reflecting the positive interaction between policy pressure, corporate green governance, and corporate responsibility behaviors. This finding enriches the theoretical framework of green policy evaluation and provides pathway guidance for firms to develop sustainable development strategies.

Fourth, this study confirms that the EPFT can effectively reduce carbon emission intensity by enhancing GGP. Specifically, the EPFT incentivizes enterprises to curtail pollutant emissions and augment investments in environmental protection. While most existing literature focuses on the “direct inhibitory” effect of tax mechanisms on pollutant emissions, this research incorporates GGP into the mediating variable framework to examine the actual emission reduction effect arising from the EPFT’s promotion of GGP. This approach underscores the pivotal role of incentivizing corporate green governance in attaining emission reduction targets, realizing the synergistic effect of environmental regulation on corporate green governance and carbon emission reduction. Notably, this finding furnishes a theoretical basis for designing incentive policies that reconcile firms’ economic efficiency with environmental benefits.

Conclusions and recommendations

Conclusions

First, as an exogenous quasi-natural experiment, the EPFT significantly enhances GGP, and this promoting effect becomes more pronounced with the increase of provincial EPFT tax rates. Second, the policy effect exhibits obvious heterogeneity: it is more pronounced in non-resource-based cities, non-heavily polluting industries, capital-intensive firms, and firms with executives possessing environmental backgrounds (especially CEOs rather than board chairs); notably, weak environmental regulation enforcement leads to insignificant EPFT effect in resource-based cities, while strengthening regulation can restore its incentive role. Third, EPFT promotes GGP through three mutually correlated and mutually reinforcing mediating paths: mitigating financing constraints, attracting green investors, and fostering green technological innovation, which form a positive feedback loop. Fourth, EPFT helps strengthen the facilitating effect of GGP on corporate ESG performance and CSR fulfillment. Finally, the EPFT’s promotion of GGP can effectively reduce enterprises’ carbon emission intensity, realizing the synergistic effect of environmental regulation on corporate green governance and carbon emission reduction.

Policy Recommendations

Based on the research findings, the following policy recommendations are proposed:

This paper systematically examines the impact of China’s EPFT on corporate green governance performance, verifies three core transmission mechanisms, and identifies significant heterogeneous effects in resource-based cities and enterprises with different executive background characteristics. Based on these empirical findings, this section puts forward targeted, operable and hierarchical policy suggestions for local governments, enterprises and capital market regulators, to maximize the policy effect of EPFT reform and promote the continuous improvement of corporate green governance.

For local governments, especially city managers in coal-heavy provinces and other resource-based regions, the core of policy optimization is to formulate differentiated supporting measures based on local industrial characteristics, and give full play to the structural adjustment effect of EPFT reform. The first core action is to optimize the supporting rules for environmental tax collection and management within the statutory framework. City managers in coal-dominated provinces can set stepped collection and management standards for leading local heavy-polluting industries including coal mining, washing, coking and non-ferrous metal smelting, within the tax range specified by the national Environmental Protection Tax Law. They can also clarify the strict identification rules for tax reduction incentives linked to pollutant emission reduction, to guide enterprises to improve the systematic green governance capacity rather than temporary end-of-pipe treatment. Meanwhile, they should allocate more than 60% of the local retained portion of environmental tax revenue to targeted subsidies for the construction of green governance system of local enterprises, including the establishment of special environmental governance committees, the introduction of environmental protection professionals and the R&D of green technology, to form a closed loop of tax collection, targeted investment and governance improvement.

The second core action for city managers in coal-heavy provinces is to build a targeted supporting mechanism for the green governance of local key enterprises, based on the heterogeneous effect of executive background characteristics verified in this paper. They should establish a green performance tracking system for executives of local key pollution discharging enterprises, link the long-term green governance performance of enterprises with the industry access, personal credit rating and political honor evaluation of executives, to strengthen the incentive for executives to pay attention to long-term green governance. They should also guide local state-owned enterprises which account for a dominant proportion in the coal and related industrial chains to optimize the executive selection and employment mechanism, give priority to the selection of candidates with environmental protection work experience, green technology R&D background or sustainable strategic management experience, and set up special environmental governance responsibilities in the job description of senior executives, to give full play to the positive role of executive characteristics in improving corporate green governance.

The third core action for city managers in coal-heavy provinces is to amplify the signal effect of EPFT reform through institutional design, and give full play to the governance role of green investors. They should integrate the standardized green governance performance information of local enterprises into the regional credit information sharing platform, and regularly release the green governance list of local enterprises to reduce the information screening cost of green institutional investors. They can also hold regular docking activities between local resource-based enterprises and national green institutional investors, green industry funds and policy banks, to guide more long-term green capital to flow into local enterprises with sound green governance performance, and form a positive cycle of policy guidance, capital inflow and governance improvement.

For enterprises, the core of policy optimization is to take the EPFT reform as an opportunity to improve the top-level design of green governance, rather than passive compliance. Enterprises should optimize the executive employment and incentive mechanism, incorporate green governance objectives into the annual performance assessment and long-term incentive plan of senior executives, and give full play to the subjective initiative of the management in green governance. They should also improve the internal governance structure, set up a special environmental governance committee led by the board of directors, establish a full-process environmental risk internal control system, and improve the quality of green information disclosure, to form a long-term and stable green governance mechanism.

For capital market regulators, the core of policy optimization is to improve the supporting system to amplify the transmission effect of EPFT reform. Regulators should further improve the mandatory green information disclosure system of listed companies, clarify the disclosure standards of corporate green governance structure, environmental governance investment and long-term green development strategy, to reduce the information asymmetry between enterprises and investors. They should also guide institutional investors to pay more attention to the long-term green governance performance of enterprises, and give full play to the supervision and governance role of green investors in improving corporate environmental performance.

Research limitations and future prospects

Research limitations

This study has several limitations that warrant careful consideration. First, the research sample is restricted to Chinese A-share listed firms, which constitute only a small fraction of all market entities in China. These firms differ substantially from non-listed enterprises in terms of governance structure, information disclosure standards, and financing capacity. Consequently, the external validity of our findings may be limited, and caution should be exercised when generalizing the conclusions to unlisted or small and medium sized firms. Second, although we treat the EPFT reform as a one time exogenous policy shock in a quasi natural experiment framework, this design does not fully capture the dynamic policy effects arising from provincial level adjustments in environmental tax rates. After the reform, local governments adjusted specific tax rates multiple times within the statutory ranges according to regional conditions. The heterogeneous impact of varying tax intensities on corporate GGP has not been thoroughly examined in this study, which limits our understanding of the policy’s long term dynamic effects and potential threshold patterns. Third, our analysis may be subject to confounding from other concurrent environmental policies implemented during the sample period. Major initiatives such as the national carbon emission trading market pilots, the revised Environmental Protection Law, and central environmental protection inspections were rolled out alongside the EPFT reform. While we have controlled for several policy dummy variables, we cannot completely rule out the possibility that these overlapping policies interfere with the accurate identification of EPFT’s net effect. Future research employing more refined identification strategies is needed to isolate the marginal contribution of EPFT from the broader policy mix. Fourth, the three mediating paths examined in this study, namely the alleviation of financing constraints, the attraction of green investors, and the promotion of green technological innovation, are analyzed as separate parallel channels. However, these mechanisms are likely interconnected and mutually reinforcing in practice. For example, green technological innovation can attract green investors, whose participation may in turn ease financing constraints, creating a positive feedback loop. Our parallel mediation approach does not fully account for these endogenous interactions, nor does it disentangle the potential causal ordering among the mediators. This limitation suggests that the estimated indirect effects should be interpreted as indicative rather than definitive, and future research could employ chain mediation or simultaneous equation models to better capture the systemic nature of the transmission process.

Future research prospects

This study provides empirical evidence for the impact of EPFT reform on corporate GGP, and leaves space for further in-depth expansion in future research. First, future research can explore the dynamic nonlinear effect of EPFT tax intensity on corporate GGP by collecting provincial panel data of annual environmental tax rate adjustments and constructing a panel threshold model, so as to identify the optimal tax intensity range for the policy to exert its effect and clarify the dynamic change of policy effect with tax rate adjustments. Second, it can analyze the synergy or substitution effect between EPFT and other environmental policies by introducing interaction terms between EPFT reform and policy dummy variables such as carbon emission trading pilot and central environmental protection inspection, and building a multi-period difference-in-differences model, providing empirical support for the optimal design of environmental policy portfolios. Third, the research sample can be expanded to non-listed industrial enterprises above designated size by using micro databases such as the China Industrial Enterprise Database, and the benchmark conclusions can be re-verified with the multi-period difference-in-differences method, so as to improve the external validity of the research conclusions and explore the heterogeneous policy effects among enterprises with different characteristics. Fourth, future research can adopt a multi-level linear model to integrate urban-level government-enterprise relation characteristics and firm-level micro data, exploring the cross-level regulatory effect of regional government-enterprise relations on the impact of EPFT reform on corporate GGP, which helps open the black box of the institutional environment in policy implementation. Fifth, it can focus on the inherent connection and interactive mechanism among the three intermediary paths, constructing a chain mediation model to test their chain transmission relationship and adopting a simultaneous equation model to address endogeneity issues, so as to more accurately identify their independent and synergistic effects and enrich the theoretical system of the policy’s mediation mechanism.

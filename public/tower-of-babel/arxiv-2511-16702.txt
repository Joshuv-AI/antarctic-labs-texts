Introduction

The pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives play an important role in geometric function theory, especially using pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives embedding models to characterize Teichmmüller spaces. They are also important tools for studying the inner radius of univalency for planer domains and quasiconformal extensions, see
[
26
,
27
]
. The research of pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives has a long history and is closely related to other disciplines. The concept of Schwarzian derivative originated from the works of Kummer as early as
1836
.
Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives are closely related to conformal mappings and have found significant applications in complex analysis. Pre-Schwarzian derivatives, as a precursor or generalization, also play a crucial role in understanding the properties of functions and their transformations.
In the broader context of function theory, Schwarzian derivatives are essential for understanding the properties of analytic and meromorphic functions. Several sufficient conditions for univalent analytic functions are obtained by using the notions of pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives.

1.1.
Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of analytic functions.

For a locally univalent analytic function

respectively. The pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms are

(1.2)

\displaystyle||Pf||_{\Omega}=\sup_{z\in{\Omega}}|Pf|\eta^{-1}_{\Omega}\;\mbox{and}\;||Sf||_{\Omega}=\sup_{z\in{\Omega}}|Sf|\eta^{-2}_{\Omega}

respectively, where

2.
Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norm Estimates for Robertson class

A domain
\Omega
containing the origin is called
\alpha
-spirallike if for each point
\omega_{0}
in
\Omega
the arc of the
\alpha
-spiral from the origin to the point
\omega_{0}
entirely lies in
\Omega
. A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be an
\alpha
-spirallike if

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(e^{i\alpha}\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)>0\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D},

where
|\alpha|<\pi/2
. In
1933
,
\check{S}
pa
\check{c}
ek (see
[
41
]
) introduces and studied the class of
\alpha
-spirallike functions and this class is denoted by
\mathcal{SP}(\alpha)
. Later on, Robertson
[
38
]
introduced a new class of functions, denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
, in connection with
\alpha
-spirallike functions. A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is in the class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
if, and only if,

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(e^{i\alpha}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\right)>0\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

We note that
f\in\mathcal{A}
is in the class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
if and only if
zf^{\prime}(z)\in\mathcal{SP}(\alpha)
.

Before we state our main result, let us recall another important and useful tool known as the differential subordination technique. Many problems in geometric function theory can be solved in a simple and sharp manner with the help of differential subordination. A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be subordinate to another function
g\in\mathcal{A}
if there exists an analytic function
\omega:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
\omega(0)=0
such that
f(z)=g(\omega(z))
and it is denoted by
f\prec g
. Moreover, when
g
is univalent, then
f\prec g
if, and only if,
f(0)=g(0)
and
f(\mathbb{D}\subset g(\mathbb{D})
.

In terms of the subordination, for a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\Longleftrightarrow e^{i\alpha}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\prec\frac{e^{i\alpha}+e^{-i\alpha}z}{1-z}.

In particular, when
\alpha=0
, the class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
reduces to the classical class
\mathcal{C}
of convex functions. For general values of
\alpha
, Roberson
[
38
]
proved that functions in the class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
need not be univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. By using Nehari’s test, Robertson
[
38
]
also proved that functions in the class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
are univalent in
\mathbb{D}
if
\alpha
satisfies the inequality
0<\cos\alpha\leq x_{0}\approx 0.2034...
, where
x_{0}
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle 16x^{3}+16x^{2}+x-1=0.

Later, Libera and Zeigler
[
28
]
improved the range of
\alpha
to
0<\cos\alpha\leq x_{0}\approx 0.2564...
in 1972. In 1975, Chichra
[
13
]
further improved the range to
0<\cos\alpha\leq x_{0}\approx 0.2588...
. Interestingly, in the same year, Pfaltzgraff
[
33
]
proved that the functions in the class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
are univalent if
0<\cos\alpha\leq x_{0}\approx 1/2
. Currently, this remains the best improvement for
\alpha
for which functions in
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
are univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. In 1977, Sing and Chichra
[
40
]
proved that if
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
with
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, then the function

is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
for all values of
\alpha
, where
|\alpha|<\pi/2
.

In
[
15
]
, Chuaqui
et. al.
proved a result by applying the Schwarz-Pick lemma and the fact that the expression
1+z(f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime})(z)
is subordinate to the half-plan mapping
\ell(z)=(1+z)/(1-z)
, which is

(2.2)

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\ell(w(z))=\frac{1+w(z)}{1-w(z)}

for some function

holomorphic and such that
w(0)=0
.

The expression which is defined in (
2.2

(2.3)

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{C}\;\mbox{if, and only if,}\;{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2},

and

(2.4)

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{C}\;\mbox{if, and only if,}\;\bigg|\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-2\bar{z}\bigg|\leq 2,

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

The Schur class
\mathbb{S}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}=\{f:\mathbb{D}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}:f\;\mbox{is analytic}\}.

By the Maximum Modulus Principle, we know that
f(z)\in\mathbb{T}:=\partial\mathbb{D}
for some
z\in\mathbb{D}
if, and only if,
f
is a constant function with
|f(z)|=1
. Thus, the class
\mathbb{S}
consists of non-constant analytic functions
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
along with the constant functions with values in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. A fundamental cornerstone of complex analysis is the Schwarz lemma. Its deceptive simplicity belies its many profound consequences
[
16
]
. Although Schwarz first proved this lemma for injective functions, Carathéodory later provided the proof for its general version.

Lemma A
.

(Schwarz Lemma)
[
39
]

If
f\in\mathbb{S}
with
f(0)=0
, then

(i)

|f(z)|\leq|z|
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and

(ii)

|f^{\prime}(0)|\leq 1
.

Moreover,
|f(w)|=|w|
for some
w\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
, or if
|f^{\prime}(0)|=1
, then there is a
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
so that
f(z)=\zeta z
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Let
\mathbb{B}
denote the set of all functions
\omega
that are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and satisfy
|\omega(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. We also consider the subfamily
\mathbb{B}_{0}:=\{\omega\in\mathbb{B}:\omega(0)=0\}

Theorem 2.1
.

For
-\pi/2<\alpha<\pi/2
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{S_{\alpha}}
;

(ii)

(2.5)

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(1+e^{i\alpha}\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq 1-\cos\alpha+\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{4\cos\alpha}\right)\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2};

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{\left(1-z\zeta\right)^{2e^{-i\alpha}\cos\alpha}}

\displaystyle\bigg|(1-|z|^{2})\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)-(2\cos\alpha)\bar{z}\bigg|\leq 2\cos\alpha.

Since

Since

which implies that

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(e^{i\alpha}\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq-\cos\alpha+\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{4\cos\alpha}\right)\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}.

When

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(1+e^{i\alpha}\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq 1-\cos\alpha+\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{4\cos\alpha}\right)\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}.

If the equality in (

and hence,

for some

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{2\zeta e^{-i\alpha}\cos\alpha}{1-z\zeta}\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D},

which by integration shows that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{\left(1-z\zeta\right)^{2e^{-i\alpha}\cos\alpha}}

for some
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}

Next, we will prove that (ii) is equivalent to (iii).

Multiplying (
2.11
) by
(1-|z|^{2})
both side, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}\leq 4\cos^{2}\alpha(1-|z|^{2})+4\cos\alpha(1-|z|^{2}){\rm Re}\left(e^{i\alpha}\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)

This completes the proofs.

A simple calculation shows that

(2.15)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{4})\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}\leq 4\cos^{2}\alpha\;|z|^{2}+4\cos\alpha\;|z|^{2}\;{\rm Re}\left(e^{i\alpha}\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)

Multiplying both sides of (
2.15
) by
(1-|z|^{4})
, we obtain

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{4})^{2}

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}-\cos\alpha\;|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{4}){\rm Re}\left(e^{i\alpha}\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4\cos^{2}\alpha\;|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{4}).

By adding
\left(2\cos\alpha|z|^{3}\right)^{2}
to both sides of the above inequality, we obtain

(2.16)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{4})^{2}

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}-\cos\alpha\;|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{4}){\rm Re}\left(e^{i\alpha}\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+\left(2\cos\alpha|z|^{2}\bar{|z|}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\cos^{2}\alpha\;|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{4})+\left(2\cos\alpha|z|^{2}\bar{|z|}\right)^{2}.

Multiplying both sides by
|z|
, a simple calculation yields

(2.17)

\displaystyle\bigg|(1-|z|^{4})\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-(2\cos\alpha)|z|^{4}\bigg|\leq(2\cos\alpha)|z|^{2}

which implies

(2.18)

\displaystyle\frac{-(2\cos\alpha)|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}\leq{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\leq\frac{(2\cos\alpha)|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

Substituting
z=re^{i\theta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{-(2\cos\alpha)r}{1+r^{2}}\leq\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\log|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|\right)\leq\frac{(2\cos\alpha)r}{1-r^{2}}.

Case A.
When
\alpha=0
, if we integrate with respect to
r
, we obtain

(2.19)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{2})}\leq|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})}

Case B.
When
\alpha\neq 0
, if we integrate respect to
r
, we obtain

(2.20)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{2})^{\cos\alpha}}\leq|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\cos\alpha}}.

Thus the distortion theorem is established.

Next, for the growth part of the theorem, from the upper bound it follows that

(2.21)

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|=\bigg|\int_{0}^{r}f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})e^{i\theta}dt\bigg|\leq\int_{0}^{r}|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|dt\leq\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{(1-t^{2})^{\cos\alpha}}dt

which implies

(2.22)

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leq\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{1}{(1-\xi^{2})^{\cos\alpha}}d|\xi|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. It is well-known that if
f(z_{0})
is a point of minimum modulus on the image of the circle
|z|=r
and
\gamma=f^{-1}(\Gamma)
, where
\Gamma
is the line segment from
0
to
f(z_{0})
, then we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle|f(z)|\geq|f(z_{0})|\geq\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{(1+\xi^{2})^{\cos\alpha}}d|\xi|.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we find the sharp bounds of the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms for the class
\mathcal{S_{\alpha}}
with respect to
f^{\prime\prime}(0)
, assuming
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. The following lemma is key to proving this result.

Lemma A
.

[
12
]

If
\phi(z):\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
is an analytic function, then

(2.24)

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{(\phi(0)+|z|)^{2}}{(1-|\phi(0)|)^{2}(1-|z|^{2})|)}

In the next result, we present the sharp bound of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions in the class
\mathcal{S_{\alpha}}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S_{\alpha}}
, then for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
-\pi/2<\alpha<\pi/2
, then

(2.25)

\displaystyle||Pf||\leq 2\cos\alpha.

The inequality (
2.25
) is sharp.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Since
\phi(z)=z\xi(z)
, with
|\xi(z)|<1
, then in (
2.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\frac{|2e^{-i\alpha}\cos\alpha\;z\xi(z)|}{1-|z|^{2}|\xi(z)|}

\displaystyle\leq(2\cos\alpha)\sup_{0\leq r\leq 1}\frac{r(1-r^{2})}{(1-r^{2})}

\displaystyle=2\cos\alpha.

Thus, the desired inequality (
2.25
) is obtained.

The next part of the proof is to show that the inequality (
2.25
) is sharp. To this end, we consider the extremal function given by

(2.26)

\displaystyle f^{*}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-\xi^{2})^{\cos\alpha}}d\xi.

It can be easily shown that
||Pf^{*}||=2\cos\alpha.
∎

We have the following immediate result from Theorem
2.3
.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S_{\alpha}}
, then for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\alpha=0
, we have

(2.27)

\displaystyle||Pf||\leq 2.

The inequality (
2.27
) is sharp.

Sharpness of Corolary 4.3 :

For
\alpha=0
, it follows from that

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{0}}{f_{0}^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{2z}{1-z^{2}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;Pf_{0}=\frac{2}{1-z^{2}}.

A simple computation thus yields that

\displaystyle||Pf_{0}||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-z^{2}\right)|Pf_{0}|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\frac{2}{1-|z|^{2}}=2

and we see the constant
2
is sharp.

Our next result provides a sharp bound for the Schwarzian norm when
f\in\mathcal{S_{\alpha}}
. The method adopted for the proof is a direct application of the Schwarz Lemma.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S_{\alpha}}
, for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
-\pi/2<\alpha<\pi/2
, then the Schwarzian norm

(2.28)

\displaystyle\|Sf\|=\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq 2\cos\alpha\left(2-\cos\alpha\right).

The inequality (
2.28
) is sharp.

For the classical class
\mathcal{S}_{0}\subset\mathcal{C}
of convex functions, we have the following result from Theorem
2.4
.

Corollary 2.4
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}\subset\mathcal{C}
, then for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

(2.29)

\displaystyle\|Sf\|\leq 2.

The inequality (
2.29
) is sharp.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

From (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{(2e^{-i\alpha}\cos\alpha)\phi(z)}{(1-z\phi(z))}.

A straightforward computation shows that

(2.30)

\displaystyle Sf(z)=(2e^{-i\alpha}\cos\alpha)\left[\frac{\phi^{\prime}(z)+\left(1-e^{-i\alpha}\cos\alpha\right)\phi^{2}(z)}{(1-z\phi(z))^{2}}\right].

By using triangle inequality and Schwarz pick lemma, we obtain

(2.31)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf|

\displaystyle\leq(2\cos\alpha)\bigg|\phi^{\prime}(z)+\left(1-e^{-i\alpha}\cos\alpha\right)\phi^{2}(z)\bigg|\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}

(2.32)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\cos\alpha)(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}\left(\frac{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}+\left(1-|e^{i\alpha}\cos\alpha|\right)|\phi(z)|^{2}\right).

We define the function
\Psi(z):\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
such that

(2.33)

\displaystyle\Psi(z):=\frac{\bar{z}-\phi(z)}{1-z\phi(z)}.

Since
\phi(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
then
(1-|z|^{2})(1-|z\phi(z)|^{2})>0
, it follows that

(2.34)

\displaystyle|\bar{z}-\phi(z)|^{2}<|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}.

Thus, we conclude that
|\Psi(z)|^{2}<1
. Therefore,

(2.35)

\displaystyle 1-|\Psi(z)|^{2}=\frac{(1-|\phi(z)|^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}

and

(2.36)

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}=\frac{(1-|\Psi(z)|^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}{(1-|\phi(z)|^{2})}.

If we substitute the expression (
2.36
) and (
2.35
) we have

(2.37)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|\leq(2\cos\alpha)(1-|\Psi_{1}(z)|^{2})\left(1+\left(1-|e^{i\alpha}\cos\alpha|\right)\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})}{(1-|\phi(z)|^{2})}\right).

Since
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
implies that
\phi(0)=0
, using Lemma A, we obtain

(2.38)

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

Using (
2.38
) in (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|\leq(2\cos\alpha)(1-|\Psi(z)|^{2})\left(1+\left(1-\cos\alpha\right)|z|^{2}\right).

Again, since
1-|\Psi(z)|^{2}\leq 1
, we see that

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq(2\cos\alpha)\left(1+(1-\cos\alpha)\right)

\displaystyle=2\cos\alpha\left(2-\cos\alpha\right).

Thus, the inequality is established. The sharpness follows from the Example
2.1
.
∎

Example 2.1
.

The family of parameterized functions defined as:

(2.39)

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-\xi^{2})^{\cos\alpha}}d\xi,\;\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;-\pi/2<\alpha<\pi/2

maximizes the Schwarzian norm defined as:

\displaystyle||S_{f}||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf|

and from this, the sharpness of the inequality holds for
-\pi/2<\alpha<\pi/2
. Note that

(2.40)

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}}{f_{\alpha}^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{(2\cos\alpha)z}{1-z^{2}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;Sf_{\alpha}=\frac{(2\cos\alpha)}{(1-z^{2})^{2}}\left[1+\left(1-\cos\alpha\right)z^{2}\right]

which implies that

(2.41)

\displaystyle||S_{f_{\alpha}}||

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf_{\alpha}|

(2.42)

\displaystyle=2\cos\alpha\left(2-\cos\alpha\right).

In general, the integral formula for
f_{\alpha}
given in above does not give primitives in terms of elementary functions, however when
\alpha=0

\displaystyle f_{0}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-\xi^{2})}d\xi=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right),

where
||Sf_{0}||=2
.

For functions
f
in the class
\mathcal{S_{\alpha}}
, we obtain the following result which finding an upper bound of
(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|
.

Theorem 2.5
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S_{\alpha}}
, for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
-\pi/2<\alpha<\pi/2
and

(2.43)

\displaystyle\gamma=|\phi(0)|=\frac{|f^{\prime\prime}(0)|}{2\cos\alpha},

then

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|\leq(2\cos\alpha)\left(1+(1-\cos\alpha)\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right).

Corollary 2.5
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}\subset\mathcal{C}
, for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
with

(2.44)

\displaystyle\gamma=|\phi(0)|=\frac{|f^{\prime\prime}(0)|}{2\cos\alpha},

then

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|\leq(2\cos\alpha)\left(1+(1-\cos\alpha)\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right).

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
\gamma=|\phi(0)|
. Applying the Lemma A, we calculate

(2.45)

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{(\gamma+|z|)^{2}}{(1-\gamma^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}.

If we substitute in (2.30), we get

(2.46)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|\leq(2\cos\alpha)(1-|\Phi_{1}(z)|^{2})\left(1+(1-\cos\alpha)\frac{(\gamma+|z|)^{2}}{(1-\gamma^{2})}\right).

From the fact that
|z|<1
and
1-|\Phi_{1}(z)|^{2}\leq 1
, we can easily calculate

(2.47)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|\leq(2\cos\alpha)\left(1+(1-\cos\alpha)\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

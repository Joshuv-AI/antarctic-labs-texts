A generalization of Frenkel’s formula
Shmuel Friedland

Abstract
. We generalize Frenkel’s integral formula for traces of operators to operators. The resulting formula holds for bounded self-adjoint positive operators and p p -Schatten class of compact positive operators.

Introduction

Denote:
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\supset\mathrm{H}_{n}\supset\mathrm{H}_{n,+}\supset\mathrm{H}_{n,++}

For

(1)

T_{+}=T\{T>0\},\quad T_{-}=T\{-T>0\},\quad T=T_{+}-T_{-}.

The von Neumann entropy of

[

as
[
4

:

(2)

D(A\|B):=\tr\big(A(\log A-\log B))-A+B\big).

Furthermore, for
B>0
let

(3)

D\log[B](A):=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\log(B+tA)-\log B}{t}.

For numerical computations we have the following well known observations: the von Neumann entropy of
A
can be computed by finding the eigenvalues of
A
;
D(A\|B)
can be found by using the spectral decompositons of
A
and
B
. A less known observation is that

can be found by using the spectral decomposition of
B
as follows: Find a unitary matrix
U
such that

and let

be Hermitian matrices of order

. Then
[
2

or
[
7

state:

(4)

D\log[B](A)=U([c_{ij}\frac{\log b_{i}-\log b_{j}}{b_{i}-b_{j}}]U^{*},\quad\frac{\log b-\log a=b}{b-b}=\frac{1}{b}\textrm{ for }b>0.

Observe that

(5)

\tr BD\log[B](A)=\tr A,\quad D\log[B](B)=I_{n},

where
I_{n}\in\mathrm{H}_{n}
is the identity matrix.
In many theoretical applications physicists prefer integral formulas for the above and similar functions. A new notable formula that attracted recent activity in quantum information theory (QIT) is Frenkel’s integral formula for
D(A\|B)

[
5
]
:

(6)

D(A\|B)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{|t|(t-1)^{2}}\tr\big((1-t)A+tB\big)_{-}

See for example
[
3
,
4
,
11
,
13
]

The aim of this note is to give the following generalization of (
6
) to operators:

(7)

A(\log A-\log B)-BD\log[B](A)+B=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{|t|(t-1)^{2}}\big((1-t)A+tB\big)_{-},\quad A\geqslant 0,B>0.

We call the left hand side of this formula the divergence operator:

(8)

\Delta(A\|B):=A(\log A-\log B)-BD\log[B](A)+B.

Let

(9)

O_{\gamma}(A\|B)=(A-\gamma B)_{+}.

We show the following equivalent formula analogous to
[

,
[

, Proposition 4.2]
and
[
4

:

(10)

\Delta(A\|B)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\big(\gamma^{-1}O_{\gamma}(A\|B)+\gamma^{-2}O_{\gamma}(B\|A)\big)d\gamma,\quad A\geqslant 0,B>0.

It is easy to verify these equalities if
AB=BA
, by assuming that both
A
and
B
are diagonal matrices. The surprising fact that these equalites hold for any
A,B>0
.
We suspect that there are other integral formulas in QIT where the trace can be replaced by the corresponding operators.

A.4.
Integral representation of two operators

In this subsection we give two convergent formulas for the operators appearing in

\Delta(A\|B)
:

Lemma 4
.

Assume that
A\in\mathcal{S},B\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
, and
A^{2}\leqslant\alpha^{2}B^{2}
for some
\alpha>0
.

(1)

The following integral formula for
BD[B](A)
converges, and its norm is bounded from above:

(46)

\displaystyle BD[B](A)=\int_{0}^{\infty}B(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}A(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}dx,

\displaystyle\|BD[B](A)\|\leqslant\alpha\|B\|.

(2)

Suppose that
A\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
. Then the following integral formula converges

(47)

A(\log A-\log B)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(A(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}-A(A+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\big)dx

if one of the following conditions hold:

(a)

The inequality
B^{2}\leqslant\beta^{2}A^{2}
holds for some
\beta\geqslant\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Then

(48)

\|A(\log A-\log B)\|\leqslant\alpha(1+\beta)\|A\|\|B\|\frac{\log\|B\|-\log\|A\|}{\|B\|-\|A\|}.

(b)

The inequality
(A-B)^{2}\leqslant\beta^{2}A^{2}
holds. Then

(49)

\|A(\log A-\log B)\|\leqslant\alpha\beta\|A\|\|B\|\frac{\log\|B\|-\log\|A\|}{\|B\|-\|A\|}.

Proof.

Since
A^{2}\leqslant\alpha^{2}B^{2}
, we deduce that
\ker B\subseteq\ker A
.
Hence, without loss of generality we can assume that
B\in\mathcal{S}_{++}
.
For
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
let
A_{\varepsilon}=A+\varepsilon\mathbf{1},B_{\varepsilon}=A+\varepsilon\mathbf{1}
.

(1)

Fix
x>0
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.
Clearly,
A^{2}\leqslant\alpha^{2}B^{2}_{\varepsilon}
.
The equality (
31
) yields:

BD[B_{\varepsilon}](A)=\int_{0}^{\infty}B(B+(x+\varepsilon)\mathbf{1})^{-1}A(B+(x+\varepsilon)\mathbf{1})^{-1}dx.

Observe that for
x>0

\displaystyle\|B(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}A(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant\|B(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\|A(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant

\displaystyle\|B(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\|(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}A^{2}(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|^{1/2},

\displaystyle(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}A^{2}(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\leqslant(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\alpha^{2}B^{2}(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\|(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}A^{2}(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant\alpha^{2}\|(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}B^{2}(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|=\alpha^{2}\|B(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\|B(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant\max_{t\in[0,\|B\|]}\frac{t}{t+x}=\frac{\|B\|}{\|B\|+x}.

(Observe that this inequality is equality.)
Hence,

\displaystyle\|B(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}A(B+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant\frac{\alpha\|B\|^{2}}{(\|B\|+x)^{2}}\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\|BD[B_{\varepsilon}](A)\|\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}\|B(B+(x+\varepsilon)\mathbf{1})^{-1}A(B+(x+\varepsilon)\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|dx\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha\|B\|^{2}}{(\|B\|+x+\varepsilon))^{2}}dx=\alpha\frac{\|B\|^{2}}{\|B\|+\varepsilon}<\alpha\|B\|.

These inequalities shows that the integral in (
46
) converges. The Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem yields that for
\varepsilon_{n}=1/n,n\in\mathbb{N}
the integrals for
BD[B_{1/n}](A)
converge in the norm
\|\cdot\|
to the integral in (
46
).
(See the proof of Theorem
3
.) This proves (
46
).

(2)
Use (
30
) to deduce

\displaystyle A(\log A_{\varepsilon}-\log B_{\varepsilon})=\int_{0}^{\infty}A\big((B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}-(A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\big)dx=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}A\left(B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\big((A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})-(B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})(A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\right)dx=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}A(B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}(A-B)(A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}dx=F(\varepsilon)-G(\varepsilon),

\displaystyle F(\varepsilon)=\int_{0}^{\infty}A(B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}A(A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}dx,\quad G(\varepsilon)=\int_{0}^{\infty}A(B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}B(A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}dx.

We claim that

(50)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|F(1/n)-F(0)\|=0,\quad lim_{n\to\infty}\|G(1/n)-G(0)\|,

if the condition (a) holds, and
lim_{n\to\infty}\|\big(F(1/n)+G(1/n)\big)-\big(F(0)+G(0)\big)\|=0
if the condition (b) holds.
Assume first the condition (a).
Use the arguments of part
(1)
to deduce

\displaystyle\|A(B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}A(A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant\|A(B+(x+\varepsilon)\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\|A(A+x+\varepsilon\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha\|B\|}{\|B\|+x+\varepsilon}\right)\left(\frac{\|A\|}{\|A\|+x+\varepsilon}\right)

Hence,

\displaystyle\|F(\varepsilon)\|\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}\|A(B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}A(A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|dx\leqslant

\displaystyle\alpha\|A\|\|B\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(\|B\|+x+\varepsilon)(\|A\|+x+\varepsilon)}dx=

\displaystyle\alpha\|A\|\|B\|\frac{\log(\|B\|+\varepsilon)-\log(\|A\|+\varepsilon)}{\|B\|-\|A\|}\leqslant\alpha\|A\|\|B\|\frac{\log(\|B\|+1)-\log(\|A\|+1)}{\|B\|-\|A\|}

The arguments in part
(1)
yield that
F_{\varepsilon}
is well defined for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. To estimate
G(\varepsilon)
we use the inequality
B^{2}\leqslant\beta^{2}A^{2}
to show

\displaystyle\|A(B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}B(A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant\|A(B+(x+\varepsilon)\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\|B(A+x+\varepsilon\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha\|B\|}{\|B\|+x+\varepsilon}\right)\left(\frac{\beta\|A\|}{\|A\|+x+\varepsilon}\right)\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\|G(\varepsilon)\|\leqslant\alpha\beta\|A\|\|B\|\frac{\log(\|B\|+\varepsilon)-\log(\|A\|+\varepsilon)}{\|B\|-\|A\|}\leqslant\alpha\beta\|A\|\|B\|\frac{\log(\|B\|+1)-\log(\|A\|+1)}{\|B\|-\|A\|}

The equalities (
50
) follow from Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem.
This proves (2a).

Assume now the condition (b)
(A-B)^{2}\leqslant\beta^{2}A^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\|A(B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}(A-B)(A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant\|A(B+(x+\varepsilon)\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\|(A-B)(A+(x+\varepsilon)\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha\|B\|}{\|B\|+x+\varepsilon}\right)\|(A+(x+\varepsilon)\mathbf{1})^{-1}(A-B)^{2}(A+(+\varepsilon)x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|^{1/2}\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha\|B\|}{\|B\|+x+\varepsilon}\right)\left(\frac{\beta\|A\|}{\|A\|+x+\varepsilon}\right)

Hence,

\displaystyle\|F(\varepsilon)-G(\varepsilon)\|\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}\|A(B_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}(A-B)(A_{\varepsilon}+x\mathbf{1})^{-1}\|dx\leqslant

\displaystyle\alpha\beta\|A\|\|B\|\frac{\log(\|B\|+\varepsilon)-\log(\|A\|+\varepsilon)}{\|B\|-\|A\|}\leqslant\alpha\beta\|A\|\|B\|\frac{\log(\|B\|+1)-\log(\|A\|+1)}{\|B\|-\|A\|}.

The arguments of the proof of the case (2a) yield (2b).
∎

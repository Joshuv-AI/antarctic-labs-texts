Abstract
A divisibility relation on ultrafilters on the set ℕ \mathbb{N} of natural numbers is defined as follows: ℱ ​ ∣ ~ ​ 𝒢 {\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G} if and only if every set in ℱ \cal F upward closed for divisibility also belongs to 𝒢 \cal G . Previously we isolated basic classes: powers of prime ultrafilters, and described the pattern of an ultrafilter, measuring the quantity of members of each basic class dividing a given ultrafilter. In this paper we define a topology on the set of basic classes which will allow us to calculate the pattern of the limit of a ∣ ~ \mathaccent 869{\mid} -increasing chain of ultrafilters. Using this we characterize which patterns can actually appear as patterns of an ultrafilter. Defining the = ∼ =_{\sim} -divisibility classes by identifying mutually divisible ultrafilters, in the respective quotient order ( β ℕ / = ∼ , ∣ ~ ) (\beta\mathbb{N}/=_{\sim},\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt) we identify singleton classes and consider their patterns. Finally, we give a sufficient condition for a = ∼ =_{\sim} -divisibility class to have an immediate predecessor.



Keywords: divisibility, Stone-Čech compactification, ultrafilter, pattern

MSC2020 classification: 03H15, 11U10, 54D35, 54D80

Ultrafilters are often used in place of choice principles in various constructions. For this purpose it is sometimes useful to have an ultrafilter with special properties. A typical example is the ultrafilter proof of Hindman’s theorem, which uses an idempotent ultrafilter (see
[
5
]
for this and many other examples). Hence, for constructions involving number-theoretic concepts it may be useful to have ultrafilters on the set
\mathbb{N}
of natural numbers with some properties derived from divisibility. This was the motivation for defining a divisibility relation on ultrafilters in
[
7
]
.

In this paper we continue the structural analysis of ultrafilters from the point of view of their place within the divisibility quasiorder, initiated in
[
12
]
. This is accomplished via so-called patterns: they reflect the structure of nonstandard generators of an ultrafilter.

The first of the two main results of the paper is Theorem
3.7
, calculating the pattern of the limit of a sequence of ultrafilters. It provides a way of controlling the pattern within recursive constructions. An example of such a construction leads to the other main result, Corollary
4.7
, which characterizes the patterns which can be realized by an ultrafilters. This, in turn, provides information on the structure of nonstandard generators of an ultrafilter. Actually, what we obtain is a general result on the number of powers of primes generating a given basic class that can be exact factors of a nonstandard number (Corollary
4.9
).

Introduction: divisibility and patterns

Whenever we write “ultrafilter” it will be understood, unless otherwise stated, that it is an ultrafilter on the set of natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
. Let
\beta\mathbb{N}
be the set of all ultrafilters on
\mathbb{N}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the principal ultrafilter
\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:n\in A\}
is identified with
n
. Considering the topology with base sets
\overline{A}=\{{\cal F}\in\beta\mathbb{N}:A\in{\cal F}\}
,
\beta\mathbb{N}
is an extension of the discrete space
\mathbb{N}
, called the Stone-Čech compactification of
\mathbb{N}
.

Every function
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely extended to a continuous
\mathaccent 869{f}:\beta\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\beta\mathbb{N}
. For
{\cal F}\in\beta\mathbb{N}
,
\mathaccent 869{f}({\cal F})
is the ultrafilter generated by
\{f[A]:A\in{\cal F}\}
. In
[
7
]
a similar way to extend the divisibility relation
\mid
to
\beta\mathbb{N}
was proposed: if

A\hskip-2.84526pt\uparrow=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:(\exists a\in A)a\mid n\}

for
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}

{\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G}
if for every
A\in{\cal F}
holds
A\hskip-2.84526pt\uparrow\in{\cal G}

{\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G}\Leftrightarrow{\cal F}\cap{\cal U}\subseteq{\cal G},

where

{\cal U}=\{A\in P({\mathbb{N}})\setminus\{\emptyset\}:A=A\hskip-2.84526pt\uparrow\}

is the family of nonempty upwards-closed sets. The relation
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
is a quasiorder, and defining

{\cal F}=_{\sim}{\cal G}\Leftrightarrow{\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G}\land{\cal G}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal F},

\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
can be thought of as a partial order on equivalence classes from
\beta\mathbb{N}/\hskip-1.42262pt=_{\sim}
, which we denote simply by
[{\cal F}]
. Various aspects of
(\beta\mathbb{N}/\hskip-2.84526pt=_{\sim},\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt)
were considered in papers
[
7
]
–
[
13
]
.

Parallel to
\beta\mathbb{N}
we can observe a nonstandard extension of
\mathbb{N}
, denoted by
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}

The connection between nonstandard and ultrafilter extensions of

The element

(

If
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}

{{c}}^{+}
-saturated, the following conditions are equivalent for every two ultrafilters

(i)
{\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G}

(ii) there are generators

x\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526pty

-saturated and satisfies Henson’s Isomorphism property.

We denote
\mathbb{N}_{\infty}=\omega\cup\{\infty\}
. Topologically,

is the one-point compactification of the discrete space on
\omega

We recall some more notation and previous results. By
\mathbb{P}
we denote the set of prime numbers. Ultrafilters containing
\mathbb{P}
are called prime; they are divisible only by 1 and themselves and their generators are exactly the prime numbers from
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{P}}

A,B\subseteq\mathbb{N}
denote
A^{c}=\mathbb{N}\setminus A
and
A\cdot B=\{ab:a\in A\land b\in B\land gcd(a,b)=1\}
. For

.

Now denote
\mathbb{P}^{exp}:=\{p^{x}:p\in\mathbb{P}\land x\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Elements of
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{P}^{exp}}
generate some ultrafilters; when we identify those of them that are
=_{\sim}
-equivalent, we get so-called basic classes, denoted by
{\cal P}^{u}
. They serve as powers of prime ultrafilters
{\cal P}\in\mathbb{P}
.

Definition 1.2

Let
{\cal P}\in\overline{\mathbb{P}}
. The relation
\approx_{\cal P}
on
\{(p,a):p\models{\cal P}\land a\in{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}\}
is defined as follows:

(p,a)\approx_{\cal P}(q,b)\mbox{ if and only if }tp(p^{a}/\mathbb{N})=_{\sim}tp(q^{b}/\mathbb{N}).

Then
\approx_{\cal P}
is an equivalence relation; let

{\cal E}_{\cal P}=\{[(p,a)]_{\approx_{\cal P}}:p\models{\cal P}\land a\in{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}\}

be the set of its equivalence classes.

On
{\cal E}_{\cal P}
we define the relation:

\displaystyle u\prec_{\cal P}v

if and only if

\displaystyle u\neq v\mbox{ and for some }p\models{\cal P}\mbox{ and some }a,b\in{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\mbox{ holds }(p,a)\in u,(p,b)\in v\mbox{ and }a<b.

We write
u\preceq_{\cal P}v
if
u\prec_{\cal P}v
or
u=v
.

Families of ultrafilters of the form
{\cal P}^{u}:=\{tp(p^{a}/\mathbb{N}):(p,a)\in u\}
for some
{\cal P}\in\overline{\mathbb{P}}
and

are called basic classes. By
{\cal B}
we denote the set of all basic classes.

Of course,
({\cal E}_{\cal P},\prec_{\cal P})
is a linear order. For
p\in\mathbb{P}
there is only one “infinite” element of
{\cal E}_{p}
, and the corresponding basic class is denoted by
p^{\omega}
. When we write
A\in{\cal P}^{u}
for some
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, it means that
A\in{\cal F}
for all
{\cal F}\in{\cal P}^{u}
. Generators

of some
{\cal F}\in{\cal P}^{u}
are also called generators of
{\cal P}^{u}
and we write
p^{a}\models{\cal P}^{u}
.

If
{\cal P}\in\overline{\mathbb{P}}\setminus\mathbb{P}
we distinguish
{\cal P}^{u}\in{\cal B}
of the first kind (such that, for every
p\models{\cal P}
, the set

is a singleton) and those of the second kind (such that, for every
p\models{\cal P}
,
u_{p}
is a union of consecutive galaxies - blocks of order type
(\mathbb{Z},<)
in
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}
). The generators of basic classes of the first kind are of the form
p^{{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptf}(p)}
for some

, so such classes have immediate predecessors and immediate successors (generated by
p^{{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptf}(p)-1}
and

respectively). Basic classes
{\cal P}^{u}
of the second kind have neither, and we can represent

as a supremum of an increasing sequence, or an infimum of a decreasing sequence in
({\cal E}_{\cal P},\prec_{\cal P})
. For more information on

see
[
12
]
.

Let
\langle p_{n}:n\in{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}\rangle
be the increasing enumeration of
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{P}}
. In any nonstandard extension a generalization of the Fundamental theorem of arithmetic holds, saying that every
x\in{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}
can be written uniquely as
\prod_{n\leq z}p_{n}^{h(n)}
for some
z\in{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}
and some internal function

so that

. Also,
p^{a}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptx
means that

where
p\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathchar 13613\relax}\hskip 2.84526ptx^{\prime}

is an exact divisor of

Definition 1.3

Let
{\cal A}
be the set of all functions
\alpha:{\cal B}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{\infty}
such that
\sum_{k\in\omega+1}\alpha(p^{k})\leq 1

For any

Thus,

We sometimes write

distinct from all

Finally, for

Limits of
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
-chains

When we need an ultrafilter
\cal F
with some prescribed divisibility-related properties, one natural way to construct it would be to approach it “from below”. In other words, we can try to approximate
\cal F
by elements of a
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
-increasing chain. We now formalize this idea.

If
\langle{\cal F}_{i}:i\in I\rangle
is a sequence of ultrafilters and
\cal H
is an ultrafilter on
I
, the limit of the sequence is the ultrafilter defined by:
A\in\lim_{i\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{F}_{i}
if and only if
\{i\in I:A\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\}\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proposition 2.1
(
[
10
]
, Lemma 4.1.)

Let
\langle[\mathcal{F}_{i}]:i\in I\rangle
be a chain in the order
(\beta\mathbb{N}/\hskip-2.84526pt=_{\sim},\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt)
.

(a) It has the least upper bound
[\mathcal{G}_{U}]
, obtained as
\mathcal{G}_{U}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{F}_{i}
for any ultrafilter
\mathcal{H}
containing all final segments of
I
.

(b) It has the greatest lower bound
[\mathcal{G}_{L}]
, obtained as
\mathcal{G}_{L}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{F}_{i}
for any ultrafilter
\mathcal{H}
containing all initial segments of
I
.

(c)
\mathcal{G}_{U}\cap\mathcal{U}=\bigcup_{i\in I}(\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{U})
and
\mathcal{G}_{L}\cap\mathcal{U}=\bigcap_{i\in I}(\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{U})
.

So in both cases the
=_{\sim}
-equivalence class of the limit does not depend on the ultrafilter
{\cal H}
we take the limit over (as long as it contains all initial/final segments of
I
). Hence, if
\langle[\mathcal{F}_{\xi}]:\xi\in\gamma\rangle
is a well-ordered
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
-increasing chain, we can write
[\mathcal{G}_{U}]:=\lim_{\xi\rightarrow\gamma}[\mathcal{F}_{\xi}]
, meaning that we take
\mathcal{G}_{U}
to be any ultrafilter from the
=_{\sim}
-equivalence class containing
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow{\cal H}}\mathcal{F}_{\xi}
for all
\cal H
containing all final segments of
\gamma
.

Applying the proposition above to the linear order of basic classes yields the following corollary.

Proposition 2.2
(
[
12
]
, Lemma 2.7)

For every
{\cal P}\in\overline{\mathbb{P}}
, every increasing sequence in
({\cal E}_{\cal P},\prec_{\cal P})
has a supremum and every decreasing sequence has an infimum.

In particular there are the greatest element and the smallest infinite element in
{\cal E}_{\cal P}
; the corresponding basic classes are denoted by
{\cal P}^{max}
and
{\cal P}^{\omega}
.

Lemma 4.4 from
[

\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
-chain
\langle{\cal F}_{\xi}:\xi<\gamma\rangle

: there is a
\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
-chain of generators

. We adjust its proof to show the following.

Let

,

Patterns of limits

Definition 3.1

A set
A
is convex if, for all
a,b\in A
,
a\mid c
and
c\mid b
imply
c\in A
.

Let
\cal C
denote the family of all nonempty convex subsets of
\mathbb{P}^{exp}
.

Define
\overline{A}:=\{{\cal P}^{u}\in{\cal B}:A\in{\cal P}^{u}\}
for
A\in{\cal C}
.

This duplicate notation (
\overline{A}
as a set of basic classes and
\overline{A}=\{{\cal F}\in\beta\mathbb{N}:A\in{\cal F}\}
as a set of ultrafilters) should not cause a confusion since, for
A\in{\cal C}
and
{\cal P}^{u}\in{\cal B}
,

\displaystyle{\cal P}^{u}\in\overline{A}

if and only if

\displaystyle{\cal F}\in\overline{A}\mbox{ for every }{\cal F}\in{\cal P}^{u}

if and only if

\displaystyle{\cal F}\in\overline{A}\mbox{ for some }{\cal F}\in{\cal P}^{u}.

Of course, if
A\in{\cal C}
, then
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptA}\subseteq{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{P}}^{exp}
.

Lemma 3.2

(a) The sets
\overline{A}
for

constitute a basis for some topology

on
\cal B

(b) The space

is Hausdorff.

(c) The space

Proof.
(a) Obvious, since the intersection of convex sets is also convex.

(b) Let
{\cal P}^{u},{\cal Q}^{v}\in{\cal B}
be distinct. If
{\cal P}\neq{\cal Q}
, there is
A\subseteq\mathbb{P}
such that
A\in{\cal P}\setminus{\cal Q}
, so

and

. Otherwise, if for example

, there is some

, so

and

.

(c) The set
\overline{\mathbb{P}^{exp}}
is closed in
\beta\mathbb{N}
and thus compact. The map
\varphi:\overline{\mathbb{P}^{exp}}\rightarrow{\cal B}
, defined by
\varphi({\cal F})={\cal P}^{u}
if

, is continuous and onto so

is compact too.
\Box

In

a pattern
\alpha
was called
\cal U
-closed if, whenever
{\cal P}^{u}\in{\cal B}
and

,
\sum_{{\cal Q}^{v}\in\overline{A}}\alpha({\cal Q}^{v})\geq n
for every

implies
\sum_{w\succeq_{\cal P}u}\alpha({\cal P}^{w})\geq n
. It was proven that the pattern

of every ultrafilter is
\cal U
-closed. Now we define a strengthening of

-closedness.

Definition 3.3

We will say that
\alpha\in{\cal A}
is
\cal C
-closed if:

(C1) for every
A\in{\cal C}
, the value
\sum_{{\cal Q}^{v}\in\overline{A}}\alpha({\cal Q}^{v})
belongs to the set
\mathbb{N}_{\infty}
;

(C2) whenever
{\cal P}^{u}\in{\cal B}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sum_{{\cal Q}^{v}\in\overline{A}}\alpha({\cal Q}^{v})\geq n
for every
A\in{\cal P}^{u}\cap{\cal C}
implies
\alpha({\cal P}^{u})\geq n
.

Clearly, for
\alpha\in{\cal A}
satisfying (C1), the condition (C2) is equivalent to any of the following two:

(C2’) for all
{\cal P}^{u}\in{\cal B}
,

\alpha({\cal P}^{u})=\min_{A\in{\cal P}^{u}\cap{\cal C}}\sum_{{\cal Q}^{v}\in\overline{A}}\alpha({\cal Q}^{v});

(C2”) whenever
\alpha({\cal P}^{u})<\infty
, there is some
A\in{\cal P}^{u}\cap{\cal C}
such that
\alpha({\cal Q}^{v})=0
for all
{\cal Q}^{v}\in\overline{A}\setminus\{{\cal P}^{u}\}
.

We do not prove now that
\cal C
-closedness implies
\cal U
-closedness, since we will deduce this directly from further results as Corollary
4.8
.

Note also that (C2) does not affect basic classes
p^{k}
for
p\in\mathbb{P}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Namely, such basic classes are singletons and they have a singleton neighborhood
\{p^{k}\}
.

Lemma 3.4

For every
{\cal F}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}

\alpha_{\cal F}
is
\cal C
-closed.

Proof.
Let

be arbitrary, so

{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptA}

To show (C2), assume that

Lemma 4.2

2.2

5
Singleton classes and immediate predecessors

In this section we turn to two problems that will show some limitations in applying the pattern method. First we consider the question: which
=_{\sim}
-equivalence classes are singletons? It is known (
[
8
]
, Corollary 5.10) that all classes on the first
\omega
-many levels of the
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
-quasiorder are singletons. Basic classes of the first kind are clearly also examples of singletons.

Theorem 5.1

For every
{\cal F}\in\beta\mathbb{N}
the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)
[{\cal F}]
is a singleton;

(ii) there are no distinct generators
x
and
y
of
\cal F
such that
x\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526pty
;

(iii) there is a
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
-antichain
A\in{\cal F}
.

Proof.
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Assume the opposite, that there are distinct generators
x
and
y
of
\cal F
such that
x\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526pty
. Let
p\in{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{P}}
be such that
p^{m}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptx
and
p^{n}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526pty
for some

. Then
px
can not be a generator of
\cal F
, since one of
x
and
px
belongs to
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\{}\prod_{i\leq k}q_{i}^{a_{i}}:(\forall i\leq k)q_{i}\mbox{ is prime }\land 2\mid\sum_{i\leq k}a_{i}\}

{\cal G}=tp(px/\mathbb{N})
, then by Proposition
1.1

{\cal G}=_{\sim}{\cal F}
, a contradiction.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Assume that there is some
{\cal G}\neq{\cal F}
such that
{\cal G}=_{\sim}{\cal F}
. Using Proposition
1.1
, take any generator
x
of
\cal F
, any generator
z
of
\cal G
such that
x\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526ptz
and any generator
y
of
\cal F
such that
z\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526pty
. Then
x
and
y
contradict (ii).

(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) By Lemma 2.2.12 from
[
6
]
the condition
(\exists A\in{\cal F})(\forall a,b\in A)(a\mathchar 13613\relax b\land b\mathchar 13613\relax a)
is equivalent to: for all generators

and

of
{\cal F}
,

\Box

Define

{\rm supp}\alpha:=\{{\cal P}^{u}\in{\cal B}:\alpha({\cal P}^{u})>0\}.

Lemma 5.2

If
\alpha\in{\cal A}
is
\cal C
-closed and
\alpha({\cal P}^{u})<\infty
for all basic classes
{\cal P}^{u}
, then
{\rm supp}\alpha
is finite.

Proof.
The set
{\rm supp}\alpha
is closed in the topology introduced in Lemma
3.2
: if some
{\cal Q}^{v}\notin{\rm supp}\alpha
, then by the
\cal C
-closedness of
\alpha
there is a neighborhood
\overline{A}
of
{\cal Q}^{v}
disjoint from
{\rm supp}\alpha
. By Lemma
3.2
(c),
{\rm supp}\alpha
is therefore compact. But it is also discrete: for every
{\cal P}^{u}
,
\alpha({\cal P}^{u})<\infty

\overline{A}
of
{\cal P}^{u}

\Box

Theorem 5.3

For every equivalence class
[{\cal F}]\in\beta\mathbb{N}/=_{\sim}
which is not a singleton at least one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(1) there is
{\cal P}^{u}\in{\cal B}
such that
\alpha_{\cal F}({\cal P}^{u})=\infty
; or

(2) there is
{\cal P}^{u}\in{\cal B}
of the second kind such that
\alpha_{\cal F}({\cal P}^{u})>0

Proof.
Assume the opposite, that both (1) and (2) are false. By Lemma
5.2

{\rm supp}\alpha_{\cal F}
is finite, and it contains only basic classes of the first kind. Let
\alpha_{\cal F}=\{({\cal P}_{i}^{u_{i}},m_{i}):i<n\}
and
m=\sum_{i<n}m_{i}
. Assume that
{\cal G}\neq{\cal F}
is such that
{\cal G}=_{\sim}{\cal F}
. Since
\cal F
contains

B:=\left\{\prod_{i<m}q_{i}^{a_{i}}:q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{m}\in\mathbb{P}\mbox{ are distinct}\right\},

it follows that generators of
\cal G
must also have exactly
m

We prove that actually
\alpha_{\cal F}=\alpha_{\cal G}
. For every
{\cal P}^{u}\in{\rm supp}\alpha_{\cal F}
there is some

not contained in any other

\cal F
, and thus also to
\cal G
, which means that
\alpha_{\cal G}({\cal P}^{u})=k
as well.

Now, let
x=p_{1}^{a_{1}}p_{2}^{a_{2}}\dots p_{m}^{a_{m}}\models{\cal F}
and

be such that

, but also

for at least one

are of the first kind, so such

and

can not be generators of the same basic class. Thus,
\cal F
and
\cal G
can not have the same patterns, a contradiction.
\Box

Example 5.4

For each of the conditions (1) and (2) from Theorem

{\cal P}\in\overline{\mathbb{P}}

On the other hand, let

\cal F
containing the set

, since any two generators of
\cal F
are
\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt

Example 5.5

The converse of the implication in Theorem

such that
p^{a}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptx
,
x=py
and
y\models{\cal G}
. By
\cal U
-closedness of
\alpha_{x}
, there is a set
A\in{\cal P}^{u+1}\cap{\cal U}
such that
A\notin{\cal Q}^{v}
for all
{\cal Q}^{v}\in{\rm supp}\alpha_{x}\setminus\{{\cal P}^{w}:w\succeq_{\cal P}u+1\}
. Then
A^{(k)}\uparrow\in{\cal F}\cap{\cal U}\setminus{\cal G}
. Hence
[{\cal F}]\neq[{\cal G}]
, and it remains to show that
[{\cal G}]
is an immediate predecessor of
[{\cal F}]
.

Assume the opposite, that there is some
{\cal H}\in\beta\mathbb{N}
such that
[{\cal F}]\neq[{\cal H}]\neq[{\cal G}]
and
{\cal G}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal H}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal F}
. By Proposition
1.1
there are some
z_{1}\models{\cal H}
and
z_{2}\models{\cal H}
such that
z_{1}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526ptx
and
y\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{2}
. Obviously,
p^{a-1}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{2}
:
p^{a-1}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{2}
follows from
y\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{2}
, and
p^{a}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{2}
would mean that
x\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{2}
. In a similar way we deduce that
p^{a}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{1}
. For
\alpha\in{\cal A}
and
{\cal P}^{u}\in{\cal B}
, denote
\alpha\mathchar 13334\relax{\cal P}^{u}:=\langle\alpha({\cal P}^{w}):w\succeq_{\cal P}u\rangle
. The condition
{\cal G}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal H}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal F}
implies that
\alpha_{\cal F}\mathchar 13334\relax{\cal P}^{u}
dominates
\alpha_{\cal H}\mathchar 13334\relax{\cal P}^{u}
, which in turn dominates
\alpha_{\cal G}\mathchar 13334\relax{\cal P}^{u}
. But the only difference between
\alpha_{\cal F}\mathchar 13334\relax{\cal P}^{u}
and
\alpha_{\cal G}\mathchar 13334\relax{\cal P}^{u}
is that
\alpha_{\cal F}({\cal P}^{u+1})=\alpha_{\cal G}({\cal P}^{u+1})+1
and
\alpha_{\cal F}({\cal P}^{u})=\alpha_{\cal G}({\cal P}^{u})-1
, so there is no sequence between them (in sense of domination). As above, there is some
B\in{\cal P}^{u}\cap{\cal U}
such that
B\notin{\cal Q}^{v}
for all
{\cal Q}^{v}\in{\rm supp}\alpha_{x}\setminus\{{\cal P}^{w}:w\succeq_{\cal P}u\}
. We consider two cases.

Case 1.
\alpha_{\cal H}\mathchar 13334\relax{\cal P}^{u}=\alpha_{\cal G}\mathchar 13334\relax{\cal P}^{u}
. This means that there is some
q^{b}\models{\cal P}^{u+1}
such that
q^{b}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptx
, but
q^{b-1}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{1}
. Since
q^{b}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526pty
, we must also have
q^{b}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{2}
.

Case 2.
\alpha_{\cal H}\mathchar 13334\relax{\cal P}^{u}=\alpha_{\cal F}\mathchar 13334\relax{\cal P}^{u}
. This time there is some
q^{b}\models{\cal P}^{u}
such that
q^{b}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptx
,
q^{b+1}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{2}
,
q^{b}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526pty
and
q^{b}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\parallel}\hskip 2.84526ptz_{1}
.

In both cases we have the same
\sum_{w\succeq_{\cal P}u}\alpha_{\cal F}({\cal P}^{w})
(hence finitely many) primes such that the exact factors from
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptB}
of
x,y,z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are some powers of those primes. Suppose that
p
is the
i
-th smallest of those primes. We can define a function

as follows: if

is the
i
-th smallest prime such that

which is some power of
r

be such that

. Furthermore, since

is of the first kind, there is some

such that
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptg}(p)=a
. Now, if we define

\Box

However, the patterns alone can not give us an equivalent condition for the problem of immediate predecessors, as the next example shows.

Example 5.10

Let
{\cal P},{\cal Q}\in\overline{\mathbb{P}}\setminus\mathbb{P}
and
\beta=\{({\cal P}^{max},1),({\cal Q}^{\omega},1)\}
. Let
p\models{\cal P}
and
q\models{\cal Q}
be such that
(p,q)
is a tensor pair, in other words that
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptf}(q)>p
for all
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptf}(q)\notin\mathbb{N}
. If
{\cal F}:=tp(p^{q}q^{p}/\mathbb{N})
, then
\alpha_{\cal F}=\beta
by
[
12
]
, Example 2.8. The
=_{\sim}
-equivalence class
[{\cal F}]
is a singleton by Theorem
5.1
, since the antichain
\{a^{b}b^{a}:a,b\in\mathbb{P}\land a\neq b\}
belongs to
\cal F
. Hence
p^{q}q^{p-1}
generates an ultrafilter strictly below
\cal F
, and it is clearly an immediate predecessor of
\cal F
.

Now let
s,t\in{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}\setminus\mathbb{N}
be such that
p^{s}\models{\cal P}^{max}
,
q^{t}\models{\cal P}^{\omega}
, but
s\neq{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptf}(p,q,t)
for all
f:\mathbb{N}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
. If
{\cal G}:=tp(p^{s}q^{t}/\mathbb{N})
, again we have
\alpha_{\cal G}=\beta
. Assume that
[{\cal H}]
is an immediate predecessor of
[{\cal G}]
. Then there is some
A\in{\cal G}\cap{\cal U}\setminus{\cal H}
. Let
B
be the set of
\mid
-minimal elements of
A
. There are some
x,y\in{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathbb{N}}
such that
p^{x}q^{y}\in B
and
p^{x}q^{y}\hskip 2.84526pt{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid}\hskip 2.84526ptp^{s}q^{t}
. If we define a function
f
(on an appropriate subset of
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{N}
) so that
f(a,b,k)
is the unique
l
such that
a^{l}b^{k}\in A
, then clearly
x={{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptf}(p,q,y)
. This means that
p^{x}q^{y}\neq p^{s}q^{t}
. Thus,
tp(p^{x}q^{y}/\mathbb{N})
is between
{\cal H}
and
{\cal G}
: it is strictly above
{\cal H}
because it contains
A
, and strictly below
{\cal G}
because it contains
\{n\in\mathbb{N}:(\exists z\in A)n\mid z\}
, whose complement is in
\cal U
.

What causes the limited efficiency of the pattern method for these problems? It is the fact that, for
x=\prod_{i\leq z}p_{i}^{h(i)}
with more than one prime divisor, the exponents
h(i)
may be functions of prime factors other than
p_{i}
and/or of each other. For example, the difference between
p^{q}q^{p}
and some other
p^{s}q^{t}
in the last example can not be registered by the pattern of the ultrafilter, but it affects the properties of an ultrafilter having a singleton
=_{\sim}
-class or an immediate predecessor.

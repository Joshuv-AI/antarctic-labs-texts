Stability of parabolic systems of Hodge bundles over punctured ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}
Xingyu Cheng

Abstract
We consider the problem of existence of semistable systems of Hodge bundles with parabolic structure over a finite set S ⊂ ℙ 1 S\subset\mathbb{P}^{1} of type ( 1 , n ) (1,n) . That is, we consider parabolic Higgs bundles ( ℰ , θ ) (\mathcal{E},\theta) , where ℰ = ℒ ⊕ 𝒱 \mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V} and θ ⁡ ( ℒ ) ⊂ 𝒱 ⊗ Ω ℙ 1 1 ​ ( log ⁡ S ) \theta(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log S) , where rank ⁡ ℒ = 1 \rank\mathcal{L}=1 and rank ⁡ 𝒱 = n \rank\mathcal{V}=n . Such systems of Hodge bundles are ℂ × \mathbb{C}^{\times} -fixed points in the space of all such (parabolic) Higgs bundles and these correspond to local systems coming from complex variations of Hodge structure under Simpson’s correspondence. In the spirit of Agnihotri-Woodward and Belkale, we use enumerative geometry to give numerical criteria for the existence of such semistable parabolic systems of Hodge bundles with semisimple local monodromy.

1.2
The unitary existence problem

Suppose now that each matrix
A_{i}
is unitary with eigenvalues

e^{2\pi i\alpha_{1}(p_{i})},\ldots e^{2\pi i\alpha_{n}(p_{i})}

with
0\leq\alpha_{1}(p_{i})\leq\alpha_{2}(p_{i})\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{n}(p_{i})<1
. Mehta-Seshadri
[
14
]
showed that irreducible local systems with (special) unitary local monodromy matrices and prescribed eigenvalues as in above are in correspondence with stable parabolic vector bundles of parabolic degree 0, which are vector bundles
\mathcal{V}
such that for each
p\in S
,
\mathcal{V}_{p}
is equipped with a (complete) flag and to each subspace of the flag is attached parabolic weights given by the
\alpha_{j}(p)
’s (see Definition
2
). In this case, the problem of existence of unitary matrices in prescribed conjugacy classes multiplying to one is equivalent to the existence of (semi)stable parabolic bundles of degree 0.

1.2.1
Preliminary notation

We will denote by
Gr(r,W)
the Grassmannian of dimension
r
subspaces of a vector space
W
. We will also denote
Gr(r,\mathbb{C}^{n})=Gr(r,n)
.

Definition
.

Let
F_{\bullet}:F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\ldots\subset F_{n}=W
be a complete flag of subspaces of an
n
-dimensional vector space
W
. For a subset
I=\{i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{r}\}\subset[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
of cardinality
r
, the
Schubert variety

\Omega_{I}(F_{\bullet})\subset Gr(r,W)
is

\Omega_{I}(F_{\bullet})=\{U\in Gr(r,W)\mid\dim(U\cap F_{i_{k}})\geq j,j=1,\ldots,r\}.

We will denote by
\sigma_{I}\in A^{*}Gr(r,W)
the cycle class of
\Omega_{I}(F_{\bullet})
. We will also write

\codim\sigma_{I}=\codim\Omega_{I}(F_{\bullet})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}(n-r+j-i_{j}).

Definition
.

Let
I^{p_{1}},\ldots,I^{p_{s}}\subset[n]
be subsets each of cardinality
r
and
d\geq 0
an integer. The
Gromov-Witten
number
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{d}
is the number of stable maps (counted as zero if infinite)
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to Gr(r,n)
of degree

such that
f(p_{i})\in\Omega_{I^{p_{i}}}(F_{\bullet}(p_{i}))
for complete flags
F_{\bullet}(p_{1}),\ldots,F_{\bullet}(p_{s})
of
W
in general position.

Such Gromov-Witten numbers can be computed using the quantum cohomology of Grassmannians
[
6
]
.

1.2.2
Existence of semistable parabolic bundles

Because (semi)stability is an open condition, it suffices to look at generic parabolic bundles of parabolic degree 0. We can then apply the shifting operation described in Section
4
to get the sum of weights

\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(p)=0.

Doing this forces the parabolic bundle to have degree 0. Generic bundles of rank
n
and degree
0
are isomorphic to the trivial bundle
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}
. This then reduces the problem to checking the semistability of the trivial bundle
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}
with generic flags and prescribed parabolic weights.

For such a parabolic bundle to be semistable, we need every subbundle
\mathcal{V}
, with parabolic structure induced by how it intersects the data of flags, to satisfy the parabolic slope inequality
\parmu\mathcal{V}\leq\parmu\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}=0
(see Definition
2
), that is, we must not have a subbundle that contradicts this inequality. A rank
r
subbundle
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}
have a degree,
-d
, and induced weights determined by how
\mathcal{V}
intersects the parabolic flags of
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}
. How
\mathcal{V}
intersects the flag at
p
can be recorded by which Schubert cell
\Omega_{I}(F_{\bullet}(p))\subset Gr(r,n)=Gr(r,\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}_{p})
contains
\mathcal{V}_{p}
. The parabolic slope of
\mathcal{V}
is completely determined by this data of degree and weights, and so the question of which inequalities to write becomes one of existence of subbundles of
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}
with prescribed degree such that
\mathcal{V}_{p}
lies in some Schubert variety in
Gr(r,n)=Gr(r,\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}_{p})
for
p\in S
.

The question of which inequalities to write is then an enumerative problem. Degree
-d
subbundles of
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}
are exactly in correspondence with degree
d
maps
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to Gr(r,n)
and to ask for
\mathcal{V}_{p}
to lie in a Schubert variety
\Omega_{I^{p}}(F_{\bullet}(p))
is exactly the same as as having
f(p)\in\Omega_{I^{p}}(F_{\bullet}(p))
. The Gromov-Witten invariant
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{d}
records exactly this information.

Agnihotri-Woodward
[
1
]
and Belkale
[
3
]
were then able to give numerical conditions for the existence of such semistable parabolic bundles in terms of nonvanishing Gromov-Witten numbers and semistability inequalities.

Theorem

(The unitary existence theorem)
.

A semistable parabolic bundle of parabolic degree zero and weights
\alpha_{1}(p)\leq\alpha_{2}(p)\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{n}(p)<\alpha_{1}(p)+1
for each
p\in S
exists if and only if the following condition holds:

•

For any integers
r,d
with
0<r<n
, and subsets
I^{p}=\{i^{p}_{1}<i^{p}_{2}<\ldots<i^{p}_{r}\}
such that
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{d}\neq 0
, the semistability inequality

-d+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\alpha_{n-i+1}(p)\leq 0

must hold.

1.3
The existence problem for variation of Hodge structures

On the other hand, Simpson (
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
) showed that
GL(n)
solutions of such matrix problems can be associated with certain parabolic Higgs bundles (see Section
2.2
). In particular, there is a correspondence between filtered local systems on one side and parabolic Higgs bundles on the other. In the space of such (parabolic) Higgs bundles, there are certain special
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
fixed points called systems of Hodge bundles (see
[
16
]
), which are in association with complex variations of Hodge structures. Such local systems are interesting, for example, because these are the local systems that come from geometry and all rigid local systems over a multiply punctured
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in the sense of
[
10
]
are of this type.

We can set up a general algebro-geometric problem. Let
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
be a parabolic system of Hodge bundles. Then by Definition
5
, we must have that

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{E}_{N}

and
\theta
must satisfy
\theta|_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}:\mathcal{E}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\log S)
. In this parabolic system of Hodge bundles case, we can assume that the parabolic flags are compatible with this decomposition of
\mathcal{E}

[
17
]
, i.e. the subspaces making up the flag of
\mathcal{E}_{p}
can be broken up into a direct sum of subspaces of
\mathcal{E}_{i,p}
. We can then give each
\mathcal{E}_{i}
the data of flags with weights at each point
p\in S
. Set
\alpha^{i}_{1}(p)\leq\alpha^{i}_{2}(p)\leq\ldots\leq\alpha^{i}_{r_{i}}(p)
be the parabolic weights of
\mathcal{E}_{i,p}
, and denote the flags of
\mathcal{E}_{i,p}
as
F_{1}^{i}(p)\subset F_{2}^{i}(p)\subset\ldots\subset F_{r_{i}}^{i}(p)
, where the subspace
F^{i}_{j}(p)
will be associated with weight
\alpha^{i}_{n-i+1}(p)
.

Question
.

If we fix the data of
\rank\mathcal{E}_{i}
,
\deg\mathcal{E}_{i}
,
\rank\theta|_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}
, and weights
\{\alpha^{i}_{j}(p)\mid 1\leq j\leq r_{i},i\in[N],p\in S\}
, then does there exist a semistable parabolic system of Hodge bundles
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
, with nonzero
\theta
, where
\mathcal{E}_{i}
has flags with weights
\alpha^{i}_{j}(p)
for each
j
and
p
as in the above?

The problem may look very different for different numerical data, and so this question actually consists of many different problems, each perhaps requiring separate techniques. Another question to ask is to ask exactly what numerical data is needed to isolate an irreducible component of the moduli space of all such objects, and therefore make the question more tractable for enumerative techniques. We explain more about the general problem in Section
3
and give some examples of some situations that give rise to irreducible components.

The difference between our situation of systems of Hodge bundles and the unitary case is that the existence of our Higgs field
\theta
imposes certain restraints. In the unitary case, we can assume our parabolic bundle are as general as possible and that our flags are as general as possible. However the condition that
\theta:\mathcal{E}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
along with the numerical data of
\rank\theta\mid_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}
imposes restrictions on the bundles
\mathcal{E}_{i}
. Furthermore,
\Res_{p}\theta
(see Definition
5
) is required to preserve the flags of the parabolic systems of Hodge bundles, i.e.
\Res_{p}\theta(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha(p)})\subset\mathcal{E}_{\alpha(p)}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)_{p}
, where
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha(p)}
denotes the (largest) part of the flag of
\mathcal{E}_{p}
with weight
\alpha(p)
. This then imposes additional restraints on the parabolic flags and hence we cannot assume the flags to be generic in this case.

1.4
The
(1,n)
problem

We will not attempt to solve the question for every possible combination of numerical data. We restrict our attention to solving the case where where our bundle is of type
(1,n)
(explained below) with distinct parabolic weights, along with the
(1,2)
case when we ask only for semisimple local monodromies instead of distinct weights. This is the main goal of this present work.

Restricting to the
(1,n)
case means
\mathcal{E}
only breaks up into two bundles
\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}
where
\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle and
\mathcal{E}_{2}=\mathcal{V}
is rank
n
. For
p\in S
, we give
\mathcal{L}
weights
\alpha(p)
and
\mathcal{V}
weights
\beta_{1}(p)<\beta_{2}(p)<\ldots<\beta_{n}(p)
. We further ask that
\alpha(p)\neq\beta_{i}(p)
for all
i
as well, so the all the weights are distinct or generic. Furthermore, through parabolic shifting (see Section
4
), we can further assume that
\alpha(p)<\beta_{1}(p)
for all
p
.

Semistability of such
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
are determined by
\theta
-stable subbundles of
\mathcal{E}
, and so in this
(1,n)
situation, there are two types of subbundles to consider:

(I)

All subbundles
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{V}
.

(II)

Subbundles of the form
\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{S}
where
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{V}
is a subbundle such that
\mathcal{S}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
contains the image
\theta(\mathcal{L})
.

We must handle these two situations separately and they give rise to two different classes of slope inequalities.

We must also consider two different types of bundles as well. Let
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. Set
w=\deg\mathcal{W}
and
d=\deg\mathcal{L}
. If
d
is too large, then we cannot assume
\mathcal{W}
and hence
\mathcal{V}
to be generic, and so this case must be dealt with separately.

(A)

If
d\leq\lceil w/n\rceil
, then in this case we can deform
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}
so that
\mathcal{W}
is a generic degree
w
bundle containing
\mathcal{L}
.

(B)

If
d>\lceil w/n\rceil
, say
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d)
, then the most generic
\mathcal{W}
containing
\mathcal{L}
we can deform to is
\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
, where
\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
denotes a generic degree
-d
rank
n-1
bundle. A generic rank
n
bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is one of the form
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}(e_{i})
where
|e_{i}-e_{j}|\leq 1
for all
i,j
.

From hereon, we will always assume
\mathcal{V}
, equivalently
\mathcal{W}
, to be deformed to be as generic as possible as described above.

1.4.1
Generalized Gromov-Witten numbers

A main ingredient in the
(1,n)
case will the generalized Gromov-Witten numbers, which will allow us to count subbundles beyond just the trivial bundle. We can replace
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}
by a generic degree
-D
vector bundle and play the same game with counting subbundles. A generic rank
n
vector bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
takes the form
\mathcal{W}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}(e_{i})
, where
|e_{i}-e_{j}|\leq 1
for all
i,j
. In this situation, we will also call generic vector bundles
evenly split
. Evenly split bundles are unique in the sense that for any fixed degree
-D
and rank
n
, there is a collection of integers
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
such that any evenly split bundle of this degree and rank is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}(e_{i})
. This leads to the notion of generalized Gromov-Witten invariants (see, for example, Section 2.4 of
[
4
]
and Section 4.3 of
[
5
]
).

Definition 1
.

Let
I^{1},\ldots,I^{s}
be subsets of
[n]
with cardinality
r
each. Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{W}
be a generic vector bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
-D
, and suppose we have general (increasing) flags
F_{\bullet}(p)\in\operatorname{Fl}(\mathcal{W}_{p})
for all
p\in S
.

The generalized Gromov-Witten number
\langle\sigma_{I^{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{s}}\rangle_{d,D}
is defined the be number of degree
-d
rank
r
subbundles (counted as 0 if infinite)
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{W}
such that for
p=p_{j}\in S
,
\mathcal{V}_{p}\in\Omega_{I^{j}}(F_{\bullet}(p))\subset Gr(r,\mathcal{W}_{p})
.

One can reduce the calculation of these generalized Gromov-Witten numbers to the regular Gromov-Witten numbers via the parabolic shifting operation described in Section
4
. If we pick a point
p=p_{j}\in S
to apply the parabolic shift operation at, we get that

\langle\sigma_{I^{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{s}}\rangle_{d,D}=\langle\sigma_{K^{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{K^{s}}\rangle_{d^{\prime},D-1}

where

•

d^{\prime}=d
if
1\notin I^{j}
and
d^{\prime}=d-1
if
1\in I^{j}
;

•

K^{k}=I^{k}
for
k\neq j
;

•

K^{j}=\{i^{j}_{1}-1,i^{j}_{2}-1,\ldots,i^{j}_{r}-1\}
if
1\notin I^{j}
and
K^{j}=\{i^{j}_{2}-1,\ldots,i^{j}_{r}-1,n\}
if
1\in I^{j}
.

1.4.2
The main
(1,n)
theorem

Recall that we have made the assumption that
\alpha(p)<\beta_{1}(p)<\beta_{2}(p)<\ldots<\beta_{n}(p)
. The data that we need to fix for this problem is the data of
\deg\mathcal{L}
,
\deg\mathcal{V}
, and the data of parabolic weights. If we have fixed all such data, then by definition

\pardeg\mathcal{E}=\deg\mathcal{L}+\deg\mathcal{V}+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\alpha(p)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}(p)\right)

is a fixed number. In the statement of the theorem, we also have a corrective factor
r(2-s)
. This corrective factor comes from us looking at subbundles of
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
, and so to get a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}
, we need to twist by
\left(\Omega^{1}(\log S)\right)^{\vee}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-2+s)^{\vee}\simeq\mathcal{O}(2-s)
. Thus for a rank
r
subbundle
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
, to get a subbundle
\mathcal{V}
, we need
\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{O}(2-s)
, which has degree
\deg\mathcal{S}+r(2-s)
.

Theorem 1

(Existence of semistable
(1,n)
parabolic system of Hodge bundles with distinct weights)
.

A semistable system of Hodge bundles of type
(1,n)
with weights
\alpha(p)
and
\beta_{1}(p)<\beta_{2}(p)<\ldots<\beta_{n}(p)
as above with
\deg\mathcal{L}
and
\deg\mathcal{V}
fixed exist if and only if the following conditions in the different cases of bundles are satisfied.

(A)

If
\deg\mathcal{L}\leq\lceil w/n\rceil
, then the following two types of numerical conditions must be met.

(I)

For every
I^{p_{1}},\ldots,I^{p_{s}}\subset[n]
of size
r
such that the usual Gromov-Witten number

\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta}\neq 0,

the inequality

\frac{-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\beta_{n-i+1}(p)}{r}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

holds.

(II)

For every
J^{p_{1}},\ldots,J^{p_{s}}\subset[n-1]
of size
r-1
such that the generalized Gromov-Witten number
\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d}\neq 0
, the inequality

\frac{2d-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{j\in J^{p}}\beta_{n-j+1}(p)\right)}{r+1}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

holds.

(B)

If
\deg\mathcal{L}>\lceil w/n\rceil
, then the following two types of numerical conditions must be met.

(I)

For every
I^{p_{1}},\ldots,I^{p_{s}}\subset[n]
of size
r
such that
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,-w}\neq 0
, the inequality

\frac{-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\beta_{n-i+1}(p)}{r}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

is satisfied.

(II)

For every
J^{p_{1}},\ldots,J^{p_{s}}\subset[n-1]
of size
r-1
such that
\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d-w}\neq 0
, then the inequality

\frac{2d-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{j\in J^{p}}\beta_{n-j+1}(p)\right)}{r+1}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

is satisfied.

If
n=2
, we could in fact strengthen the conditions on the weights. Here we can allow the weights
\alpha(p),\beta_{1}(p),\beta_{2}(p)
to be equal. We can then apply the parabolic shifting operation at every point of
S
to get rid of the parabolic structure and get similar inequality conditions for existence of semistable
(1,2)
bundles. We explore this in Section
7
and in particular Theorem
4
is another one of our main results.

We also analyze in Section
8
parabolic systems of Hodge bundles of type
(1,1)
. Such local systems correspond to
U(1,1)
representations of
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus S)
and so must not be
U(2)
local systems. We give a proof that stable
(1,1)
parabolic systems of Hodge bundles cannot satisfy the criterion given in
[
8
]
for the existence of
U(2)
local systems. The
(1,1,\ldots,1)
case was also analyzed with particular focus on
(1,1,1)
systems of Hodge bundles in Section

.

2
Basic notions

2.1
Parabolic vector bundles

Definition 2
.

A
parabolic vector bundle
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with parabolic structure at
S
is a vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
such that at each
p\in S
,
\mathcal{V}_{p}
comes equipped with a complete flag, which we will denote as

F_{1}(p)\subset F_{2}(p)\subset\ldots F_{n}(p)=\mathcal{V}_{p}.

To each subspace
F_{i}(p)
we associate a
parabolic weight

\alpha_{n-i+1}(p)
, which must satisfy

\alpha_{1}(p)\leq\alpha_{2}(p)\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{n}(p)<\alpha_{1}(p)+1.

Given a bundle with parabolic structure, we may also denote by
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha(p)}
to be the largest subspace in the flag of
\mathcal{V}_{p}
associated with weight
\alpha(p)
.

The
parabolic degree
of
\mathcal{V}
is defined as

\pardeg\mathcal{V}=\deg\mathcal{V}+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(p).

The
parabolic slope
of
\mathcal{V}
is defined as

\parmu\mathcal{V}=\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{V}}{\rank\mathcal{V}}.

Given a subbundle
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}
of rank
r
, we can give
\mathcal{W}
induced flags by intersecting
\mathcal{W}_{p}
with
F_{i}(p)
. This subspace, call it
U\subset\mathcal{W}_{p}
is then given the largest weight possible, that is, if
U=\mathcal{W}_{p}\cap F_{i}(p)=\mathcal{W}_{p}\cap F_{j}(p)
, then we give
U
the weight
\max\{\alpha_{n-i+1}(p),\alpha_{n-j+1}(p)\}
. This procedure may not necessarily produce a complete flag. For example, we could have
\dim\mathcal{W}_{p}\cap F_{i}(p)=m+\dim\mathcal{W}_{p}\cap F_{i+1}(p)
. In this case, we can complete the flag by adding in generic subspaces which must get the largest weight possible. In our example, we add in, say,
U_{1}\subset\ldots\subset U_{m-1}
between
\mathcal{W}_{p}\cap F_{i}(p)
and
\mathcal{W}_{p}\cap F_{i+1}(p)
where each
U_{j}
gets weight
\alpha_{n-i}(p)
.

Denote the the induced weights of
\mathcal{W}
by
\beta_{i}(p)
,
i=1,\ldots,r
. Then

\pardeg\mathcal{W}=\deg\mathcal{W}+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\beta_{i}(p).

Definition 3
.

A parabolic bundle
\mathcal{V}
is called
(semi)stable
if for all subbundles
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}
, we have that

\parmu\mathcal{W}=\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{W}}{\rank\mathcal{W}}<\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{V}}{\rank\mathcal{V}}=\parmu\mathcal{V}\quad(\leq).

2.2
Parabolic Higgs bundles and Simpson’s correspondence

Definition 4
.

A
parabolic Higgs bundle
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
consists of a pair
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
, where
\mathcal{E}
is a parabolic bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with parabolic structure at
S
as above, and a morphism

\theta:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\log S).

Locally, over a point
p\in S
, given a local parameter
t
, we have

\theta_{p}=A\frac{dt}{t}+A_{0}+A_{1}t+\ldots.

We will call the
A
in the above expansion the residue, written
\Res_{p}\theta:=A
. These residues will also be part of the data of a parabolic Higgs bundle. The residue is required to preserve the parabolic flags of
\mathcal{E}_{p}
, that is
\Res_{p}\theta(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha_{i}(p)})\subset\mathcal{E}_{\alpha_{i}(p)}.

A parabolic Higgs bundle is called
(semi)stable
if for all subbundles
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{E}
, which are preserved by
\theta
, we have that

\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{W}}{\rank\mathcal{W}}<\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{\rank\mathcal{E}}\quad(\leq).

To state Simpson’s correspondence, we need the notion of a filtered local system. Given a local system
\mathcal{L}
on
U
with base point
b
, we give
\mathcal{L}_{b}
a filtration for each
p\in S

\mathcal{L}_{b}=\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}\supset\mathcal{L}_{2}^{p}\supset\ldots\supset\mathcal{L}_{n}^{p}

such that each
\mathcal{L}_{i}^{p}
is associated with a number
\beta_{i}(p)
with
\beta_{1}(p)\leq\beta_{2}(p)\leq\ldots\leq\beta_{n}(p)
. The local monodromy matrix
A_{i}
(the image of
\gamma_{i}
under the representation map) acts on the associated graded of
\mathcal{L}_{b}
with the filtration labeled by
\beta_{\bullet}(p_{i})
; call that matrix
\Res(A_{i})
. Now
\Res A_{i}
acts on the associated graded part
\mathcal{L}_{j}^{p_{i}}/\mathcal{L}_{j+1}^{p_{+}i}
of
\mathcal{L}_{b}
. Putting
\Res A_{i}
into Jordan canonical form gives us a partition
P_{i}^{\beta_{j}(p_{i}),\lambda}
which denotes the sizes of the Jordan blocks associated to eigenvalue
\lambda
.

On the Higgs bundle side, since
\Res_{p}\theta
preserves the filtration at each
p\in S
, this also acts on the associated graded. Call that induced map
Gr\Res_{p}\theta
. We can take
\overline{A}(p_{i})=Gr\Res_{p_{i}}\theta
at each
p_{i}\in S
, which acts on
\mathcal{E}_{i,p}
, and from this we can similarly get partitions
P_{i}^{\alpha_{j}(p_{i}),\tau}
which denotes the sizes of the Jordan blocks of
\overline{A}(p_{i})
associated to its eigenvalue
\tau
. Under Simpson’s correspondence, the partitions we get from
\Res A_{i}
are the same as the partitions we get from
\overline{A}(p_{i})
after doing the following change of labels:

\displaystyle(\alpha,\tau=a+b\sqrt{-1})

\displaystyle\mapsto(\beta=-2a,\lambda=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\alpha-4\pi b}),

\displaystyle(\beta,\lambda)

\displaystyle\mapsto(\alpha=\operatorname{arg}(\lambda),\tau=-\beta/2-\sqrt{-1}\log|\lambda|/4\pi).

2.3
Variation of Hodge Structures

Over any curve
C
, the moduli space of Higgs bundles carries with it a natural
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
action by
t\cdot(\mathcal{E},\theta)=(\mathcal{E},t\theta)
. If
C
is compact, then the limits
t\to 0
exist and give fixed points of the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
action. Fixed points of this
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
action gives rise to local systems coming from complex variation of Hodge structures. Over a noncomplete curve, we lose the ability to take limits of the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
action. However
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
fixed points in this moduli of Higgs over the noncompact curve are still in correspondence with local systems coming from complex VHS as shown in
[
16
]
. Such local systems are interesting, for instance, because they are local systems coming from geometry and all rigid local systems over
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus S
in the sense of
[
10
]
are of this class.

Parabolic Higgs bundles that come from such complex VHS break up in a special way. They correspond to systems of Hodge bundles defined below.

Definition 5
.

A
parabolic system of Hodge bundles
consists of a parabolic Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
with parabolic structure at
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
\mathcal{E}
decomposes as

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{E}_{N},

with
\theta|_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}:\mathcal{E}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. We will further say that a system of Hodge bundles has type
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{N})
if
\rank\mathcal{E}_{i}=r_{i}
for each
i
.

Such parabolic systems of Hodge bundles are called
(semi)stable
if they are (semi)stable as parabolic Higgs bundles.

To check stability of such systems of Hodge bundles one only need to check subbundles compatible for the Hodge decomposition
[
15
]
. That is, we only need to check stability for bundles
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{W}_{N}
with each
\mathcal{W}_{i}\subset\mathcal{E}_{i}
and
\theta\mid_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{W}_{i})\subset\mathcal{W}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. We will call such subbundles
subsystems of Hodge bundles
. Furthermore, we can suppose that the flags of
\mathcal{E}_{p}
respect the filtration as well, that is the subspaces of the flags of
\mathcal{E}_{p}
breaks up into a direct sum of subspaces of
\mathcal{E}_{i,p}
. Thus, we can give each
\mathcal{E}_{i}
the data of its own flags and weights. Now, since
\Res_{p}\theta
preserves the filtration, this means that
\Res_{p}\theta(\mathcal{E}_{1,\alpha})\subset\mathcal{E}_{2,\beta}
, where
\beta\geq\alpha
.

In this situation of local systems coming from
\mathbb{C}
-VHS, since we have that
\Res_{p}\theta(\mathcal{E}_{i,p})\subset\mathcal{E}_{i+1,p}
, after composing
\Res_{p}\theta
with itself
N
times we must have that
(\Res_{p}\theta)^{N}=0
. Hence all the eigenvalues of
\Res_{p}\theta
are zero. Looking at the change of labels are described by the Simpson correspondence above,
(\alpha,\tau)\mapsto(\beta,\lambda)
, we must have that every
\beta=0
because, all eigenvalues the of
\Res_{p}\theta
,
\tau=0
. This means that there is no filtration on the level of local systems. Furthermore, because
\tau=0
, we must also have that
\lambda
must also lie on the unit circle. Thus, when we restrict ourselves to looking at local systems coming from
\mathbb{C}
-VHS, the correspondence is between stable parabolic systems of Hodge bundles of parabolic degree zero and local systems with local monodromies with eigenvalues of unit length.

2.4
Strongly parabolic Higgs bundles

In
[
7
]
, a notion of
strongly
parabolic Higgs bundles was defined. Adapted to our case and notation, it is defined in the following way.

Definition 6
.

A
strongly parabolic Higgs bundle
will mean a parabolic Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
as we defined such that the Higgs field
\theta
will be such that

\Res_{p}\theta(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha(p)})\subset\mathcal{E}_{\widehat{\alpha}(p)}

where
\widehat{\alpha}(p)
will denote the next highest weight after
\alpha(p)
, i.e.
\alpha(p)<\widehat{\alpha}(p)
with no other weights in between. In terms of flags, this means that
\Res_{p}\theta
takes each subspace to the next smallest subspace. In this case, we will also call
\theta
as a
strongly parabolic Higgs field
.

Remark
.

We note that in
[
7
]
they call this notion as just a parabolic Higgs bundle. However, we will reserve this notion for Higgs bundles with the structure of parabolic vector bundles.

In terms of the local monodromies on the local system side, we note that asking for the Higgs field to be strongly parabolic will mean that once we take associated graded,
Gr\Res_{p}\theta=0
. So then going back to Simpson’s correspondence to local systems, we will have that each local monodromy must be semi-simple, since the only partitions we will have are blocks of size one each. We will restrict ourselves to only looking at local systems with semisimple monodromies from now on, and hence we only look at strongly parabolic systems of Hodge bundles.

2.5
Stability and the Harder-Narasimhan filtration

Any rank
n
parabolic Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
comes equipped with a unique Harder-Narasimhan filtration by subbundles

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\supsetneq\mathcal{E}_{2}\supsetneq\ldots\supsetneq\mathcal{E}_{h}

such that each
\mathcal{E}_{i}
is preserved by
\theta
(i.e. is a Higgs subbundle) with
\parmu(\mathcal{E}_{1})<\parmu(\mathcal{E}_{2})<\ldots<\parmu(\mathcal{E}_{h})
(see e.g. Section 3 of
[
18
]
). Consequently, if
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
is not a semistable parabolic Higgs bundle, then
\mathcal{E}_{h}
will be the unique subbundle with maximal slope that violates the semistability inequalities. This
\mathcal{E}_{h}
is unique, therefore when we do enumerative Gromov-Witten calculations, it suffices to check for only the cases where the Gromov-Witten number is one.

3
The general algebro-geometric problem

We now explain more about the general algebro-geometric/enumerative problem associated with the existence of stable parabolic systems of Hodge bundles. The big main question we context with is the following.

Question 1
.

Given the data of
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{N})
,
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{N})
,
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{N-1})
and weights
\{\alpha^{i}_{j}(p)\mid 1\leq j\leq r_{i},i\in[N],p\in S\}
, does there exist a semistable parabolic system of Hodge bundles
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
with

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{E}_{N}

with
\rank\mathcal{E}_{i}=r_{i}
,
\deg\mathcal{E}_{i}=d_{i}
,
\rank\theta|_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}=e_{i}
, and such that
\mathcal{E}_{i}
has flags at
p\in S
with weights
\alpha^{i}_{j}(p)
for
1\leq j\leq r_{i}
?

This question can be tackled using enumerative geometry. Asking for such a stable bundle is the same as asking for all
\theta
-stable subbundles compatible with the Hodge decomposition to have parabolic slopes less than that of
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
. In other words, suppose we have a subbundle
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{W}_{N}
where each
\mathcal{W}_{i}\subset\mathcal{E}_{i}
is a subbundle of rank, say,
s_{i}
, and
\theta\mid_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{W}_{i})\subset\mathcal{W}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. Give each
\mathcal{W}_{i}
the induced filtration from
\mathcal{E}_{i}
as described in a similar process to usual parabolic bundles, and call these induced weights
\beta^{i}_{j}(p)
for
1\leq j\leq s_{i}
and
i=1,\ldots,N
and
p\in S
. To ask for
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
to be semistable is to ask for all such subbundles
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{E}
the inequality

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\deg\mathcal{W}_{i}+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{j=1}^{s_{i}}\beta^{i}_{j}(p)\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}s_{i}}<\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\deg\mathcal{E}_{i}+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}\alpha^{i}_{j}(p)\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}}

holds.

In order to write down the inequalities above, we need to know when we have subsystems of Hodge bundles of type
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N})
that meets the flags of
\mathcal{E}
in such a way as to give rise to parabolic weights
\{\beta^{i}_{j}(p)\mid i\in[N],j\in[s_{i}],p\in S\}
. This leads us to our enumerative problem.

Question 2
.

Given a parabolic system of Hodge bundles
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
with parabolic weights
\alpha^{i}_{j}(p)
and flags as denoted above, when is there a
\theta
-stable Hodge subbundles
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{W}_{N}
that meets the flags of
\mathcal{E}
in such a way as to give
\mathcal{W}
weights
\beta^{i}_{j}(p)
as above.

To tackle such an enumerative question, one should ask for irreducible components of the moduli space of all such Higgs bundles. Because stability is an open condition, to do the enumerative problem is to ask for general points of each component to be stable. So then we are lead to asking what data do we need to give ourselves an irreducible component?

•

One easy example of an irreducible component is to ask for only one component in the Hodge decomposition and fixing any degree, i.e. ask for
\theta=0
and fixing the degree of
\mathcal{E}
. So here we are looking at the moduli space of all bundles with fixed rank and degree, which is irreducible. In this case, we are really left with solving the unitary problem because the data we have is exactly the data of a regular parabolic vector bundle.

•

We can next ask for bundles of type
(1,n)
, so here
\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V}
,
\theta:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. We ask for
\theta\neq 0
and so is injective, then this leaves us with the case of a bundle lying as a subsheaf inside another bundle. If we fix the numerical data of the
\mathcal{E}_{i}
’s (i.e. their ranks and degrees), this would then correspond to a quot scheme, which over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is irreducible.

•

Another irreducible component we can find is the case where
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
is such that
\theta_{i}:\mathcal{E}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
makes each
\mathcal{E}_{i}
into a subbundle of the next. So here we have flags

\mathcal{V}_{1}\subset\mathcal{V}_{2}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{V}_{N},

where each
\mathcal{V}_{i}
is an appropriated twisted
\mathcal{E}_{i}
. To then get irreducibility, we need to fix the numerical data of the degree and rank of each
\mathcal{E}_{i}
.

•

We may also consider the dual version of the situation above. That is, consider a system of Hodge bundles
\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{E}_{N}
where each
\theta_{i}:=\theta\mid_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}:\mathcal{E}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
is a surjection. So, for example when
N=2
, we are left with
\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{2}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
is a surjection, in which case this is exactly the situation parametrized by certain quot schemes after fixing ranks and degrees. In the general situation, we have chains of surjections

\mathcal{V}_{1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{V}_{2}\twoheadrightarrow\ldots\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{V}_{N}

and the space of which is also irreducible again after fixing degrees and ranks of each
\mathcal{V}_{i}
.

In general, we may need to fix not just the data of the type of the system of Hodge bundles and degrees of the bundles, but also the data of the ranks of each
\theta_{i}:\mathcal{E}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. An interesting question would be to determine exactly what numerical conditions we need to fix in order to fix an irreducible component of the moduli space.

4
Parabolic shifting operations

For a parabolic bundle, there is a notion of a parabolic shifting operation. This operation does a cyclic shift on the weights and flags of the parabolic bundle and will be one of our main technical tools.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a rank
n
bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with parabolic structure at point
p\in S
, with weights
\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{n}<\alpha_{1}+1
. Let
F_{n}\supset F_{n-1}\supset\ldots\supset F_{1}
be the flag of
\mathcal{V}_{p}
coming from the parabolic structure with each
F_{n-i+1}
associated with weight
\alpha_{i}
. Fix a local parameter
t
at
p
. Then the shifted parabolic bundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
consists of sections
s\in\mathcal{V}(p)
such that
ts|_{p}\in F_{1}
.
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
coincides with
\mathcal{V}
outside of
p
, so nothing happens away from
p
, therefore it suffices to only look at what happens when applying the shifting operation at the single point
p
. At
p
, we have that
\mathcal{V}_{p}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{p}
has kernel
F_{1}
. We can define
\widetilde{F}_{i}
to be the image of
F_{i+1}
of this map for
i<n
and equal to
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{p}
for
i=n
. The shifted weights of
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
at
p
are called
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}
, and similar with the flags
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}=\alpha_{i-1}
for
i>1
and
\widetilde{\alpha}_{1}=\alpha_{n}-1
. For our shifted bundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
, at point
p
we have the flag

\widetilde{F}_{n}\supset\widetilde{F}_{n-1}\supset\ldots\supset\widetilde{F}_{1}

associated to weights

\widetilde{\alpha}_{1}=\alpha_{n}-1\leq\widetilde{\alpha}_{2}=\alpha_{1}\leq\ldots\widetilde{\alpha}_{n}=\alpha_{n-1}.

Explicitly, if
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
is a basis of
\mathcal{V}_{p}
compatible with the filtration, that is,
F_{1}=\langle e_{1}\rangle\subset F_{2}=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle\subset\ldots\subset F_{n}=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle
, then a basis of
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{p}
would be
e_{2},\ldots,e_{n},e_{1}/t
and we have

\widetilde{F}_{1}=\langle e_{2}\rangle\subset\widetilde{F}_{2}=\langle e_{2},e_{3}\rangle\subset\ldots\widetilde{F}_{n-1}=\langle e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle\subset\widetilde{F}_{n}=\langle e_{2},\ldots,e_{n},e_{1}/t\rangle.

We can apply this operation to parabolic systems of Hodge bundles, and it turns out that, after applying this operation, we are still left with a parabolic system of Hodge bundles. The main thing to verify when applying the parabolic shifting operations in this setting is what happens with the Higgs field
\theta:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. Locally at a point
p
in
S
with a chosen local parameter
t
,

\theta_{p}=A\frac{dt}{t}+B+\ldots,

and the matrices
A,B
must be linear in
t
as well. Given our local basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
of
\mathcal{E}_{p}
compatible with the filtration of
\mathcal{E}_{p}
as above, once we shift, we are left with basis
e_{2},\ldots,e_{n},e_{1}/t
. Then
\widetilde{\theta}
is defined by extending
\theta
to
e_{1}/t
by factoring out the
1/t
linearly. We will be much more explicit about how the shifting changes the Higgs field in various situations in the proof of Lemma
1
.

One subtlety around defining the shift operation for systems of Hodge bundles comes when we allow weights to be the same. Suppose we have a system of Hodge bundles given by
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}
, where
\rank\mathcal{E}_{1}=r
and
\rank\mathcal{E}_{2}=s
, and
\theta:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{2}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\log S)
. Suppose at
p
,
\mathcal{E}_{1}
has weights
\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{r}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
has weights
\beta_{1}\leq\beta_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\beta_{s}
. When we shift the whole bundle
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
, we need to shift only the highest weight and hence move the lowest subspace of the flag at
p
. Therefore, we need to resolve the ambiguity whenever
\alpha_{r}=\beta_{s}
. In this case, we will always take the subspace associated with
\beta_{s}
to be the subspace we shift along, i.e. the smallest subspace of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
.

This shifting operation is also invertible. Going back to the original situation of rank
n

\mathcal{V}
with weights
\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{n}
, then if we shift
n
times, we get that
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{V}(p)
with weights

\alpha_{1}-1\leq\alpha_{2}-1\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{n}-1.

We can then recover the original bundle
\mathcal{V}
by twisting by
\mathcal{O}(-p)
and the original weights by adding each weight of
\mathcal{V}
by 1. Alternatively, we can simply reverse all the operations we’ve done for the shifting operation backwards.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
be a parabolic system of Hodge bundles over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with parabolic structure at
S
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
be the image of
\mathcal{E}
under the shift operator, and
\widetilde{\theta}
be the induced map. Then
(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}},\widetilde{\theta})
is also a parabolic system of Hodge bundles.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this for systems of Hodge bundles of type
(r,s)
. Suppose
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}
,
\theta:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{2}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
,
\rank\mathcal{E}_{1}=r,
and
\rank\mathcal{E}_{2}=s
. Fix
p\in S
as needed. Denote the parabolic weights of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
at
p
as

\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{r}

and the weights of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
at
p
as

\beta_{1}\leq\beta_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\beta_{s}.

Denote the flag of
\mathcal{E}_{1,p}
as
F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\ldots\subset F_{r}
and the flag of
\mathcal{E}_{2,p}
as
G_{1}\subset\ldots\subset G_{s}
, where
F_{i}
gets weight
\alpha_{r-i+1}
and
G_{j}
gets weight
\beta_{s-j+1}
.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}
be a (local) basis for
\mathcal{E}_{1,p}
compatible with its filtration, i.e.

F_{1}=\langle e_{1}\rangle\subset F_{2}=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle\subset\ldots\subset F_{r}=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\rangle

Similarly, we pick a compatible basis
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}
of
\mathcal{E}_{2,p}
such that

G_{1}=\langle f_{1}\rangle\subset G_{2}=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle\subset\ldots G_{s}=\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\rangle.

There are two possible cases for the shift operation: either
\alpha_{r}
or
\beta_{s}
is the highest weight, that is either
\alpha_{r}>\beta_{s}
or
\alpha_{r}\leq\beta_{s}
.

Case

\alpha_{r}>\beta_{s}
:

We first tackle when
\alpha_{r}
is the highest weight. After shifting
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}_{1}}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}
.
\mathcal{E}_{2}
remains unchanged after the shift operator with all the flags and weights remaining the same. The weights of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
become now

\alpha_{r}-1\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{r-1},

and the compatible basis for
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{1,p}
becomes
e_{2},\ldots,e_{r},e_{1}/t
and the subspaces in the flag becomes

\widetilde{F}_{1}=\langle e_{2}\rangle\subset\widetilde{F}_{2}=\langle e_{2},e_{3}\rangle\subset\ldots\widetilde{F}_{r-1}=\langle e_{2},\ldots,e_{r}\rangle\subset\widetilde{F}_{r}=\langle e_{2},\ldots,e_{r},e_{1}/t\rangle.

Here
\widetilde{F}_{r}
gets weight
\alpha_{r}-1
and
\widetilde{F}_{i}
gets weight
\alpha_{n-i}
for
i<r
. We need to check that
\Res_{p}\widetilde{\theta}
still preserve this filtration. Note that the only thing we need to check in this situation is what happens to
e_{1}/t
.

Write
\theta_{p}
as

A\frac{dt}{t}+B\,dt+\ldots,

where we can write
A=(a_{ij})
and
B=(b_{ij})
. Note that in the original unshifted bundle,
\alpha_{r}
was the largest weight. This means that
\Res_{p}\theta(e_{1})=0
, so
a_{i1}=0
. Further applying
B
to
e_{1}/t
will now leave a pole. Hence we must have that
\Res_{p}\widetilde{\theta}_{p}(e_{1}/t)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i1}e_{1}
. Now, since
\alpha_{r}-1<\beta_{1}
, this means that actually
e_{1}/t
is allowed to go to anything in
\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\rangle=\mathcal{E}_{2,p}
, so
\widetilde{\theta}
still sends
e_{1}/t
to the correct part of the flag in
\mathcal{E}_{2,p}
. Thus,
\Res_{p}\widetilde{\theta}
preserves the filtration in this case.

Case

\alpha_{r}\leq\beta_{s}
:

Next we need to check this result for when
\beta_{s}
is the largest weight. In that case
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}\simeq{\mathcal{E}_{1}}\oplus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}_{2}}
, where now
\mathcal{E}_{1}
remains unchanged. The new weights of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
at
p
are

\beta_{s}-1\leq\beta_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\beta_{s-1},

the new basis is
f_{2},\ldots,f_{s},f_{1}/t
, and the flag becomes

\widetilde{G}_{1}=\langle f_{2}\rangle\subset\widetilde{G}_{2}=\langle f_{2},f_{3}\rangle\subset\ldots\widetilde{G}_{s-1}=\langle f_{2},\ldots,f_{s}\rangle\subset\widetilde{G}_{s}=\langle f_{2},\ldots,f_{s},f_{1}/t\rangle,

where similar to above,
\widetilde{G}_{s}
gets weight
\beta_{s}-1
while
\widetilde{G}_{i}
gets weight
\beta_{s-i}
for
i<s
. Now we need to make sure that
\Res_{p}\widetilde{\theta}
still preserve the filtration. Suppose
\Res_{p}\widetilde{\theta}(\mathcal{E}_{1,\alpha})\subset\mathcal{E}_{2,\beta}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{1,\alpha}=F_{i}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2,\beta}=G_{j}
, so we have that
\Res_{p}\theta(F_{i})\subset G_{j}
, i.e. we have that
\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{i}\rangle\to\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{j}\rangle,
and when we shift, we must have that
\Res_{p}\widetilde{\theta}(\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{i}\rangle)\subset\langle f_{2},\ldots,f_{j}\rangle
for
j<s
and if
j=s,
we must have
\Res_{p}\widetilde{\theta}(\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{i}\rangle)\subset\langle f_{2},\ldots,f_{s},f_{1}/t\rangle
. The only case we need to worry about is when
j<s
.

If
j<s
, then we encounter a problem only if any image of
\Res_{p}\theta|_{F_{i}}
has a component in
f_{1}=t(f_{1}/t)
. But note that we can decompose
\Res_{p}\theta
by

\Res_{p}\theta(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}A_{i}(v)f_{i}+A_{s}(v)f_{s},

where each
A_{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{1,p}^{\vee}
. So then under the shift operation,
\widetilde{\theta}
becomes

\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}A_{i}f_{i}+A_{s}t\left(\frac{f_{s}}{t}\right)+\ldots,

which means then that the
f_{s}
part is no-longer part of the residue, and hence
\Res_{p}\widetilde{\theta}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}A_{i}(v)f_{i}
, and this map preserves the filtration because the original
\Res_{p}\theta
preserved the filtration, and hence every basis vector goes to the correct part of the filtration that it needs to go to.
∎

Corollary 1
.

The parabolic shift operations preserve stability of systems of Hodge bundles.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
be a parabolic system of Hodge bundles of rank
n
and let
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{E}
be a subsystem of Hodge bundles or rank
r
, that is,
\mathcal{W}
is a
\theta
-stable subbundle of
\mathcal{E}
. This in particular implies that
(\mathcal{W},\theta|_{\mathcal{W}})
is a system of Hodge bundles in its own right and it has the parabolic structure inherited as a parabolic subbundle. Fix a point
p
where
\mathcal{E}
has parabolic structure and suppose that the weights of
\mathcal{E}
at
p
are given by

\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{n},

while
\mathcal{W}
has induced weights
w_{1}\leq\ldots\leq w_{r}.
Applying the parabolic shifting operation at
p
, we have that
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
becomes
(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}},\widetilde{\theta})
, which is a parabolic system of Hodge bundles where
\deg\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}=\deg{\mathcal{E}}+1
with parabolic weights at
p
given now by

\alpha_{n}-1<\alpha_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{n-1},

so we have then that
\pardeg\mathcal{E}=\pardeg\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
because the
-1
in
\alpha_{n}-1
cancels out with the
+1
in
\deg\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}=\deg\mathcal{E}+1
.

Now let
F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\ldots\subset F_{n}
be the parabolic flag at
\mathcal{E}_{p}
. One of two things may happen with
(\mathcal{W},\theta|_{\mathcal{W}})
after shifting along
F_{1}
. First, denote the resulting bundle by
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\widetilde{\theta}|_{\mathcal{W}})
. If
F_{1}\subset\mathcal{W}_{p}
, then this means that after shifting, we have a new bundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
with
\deg\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\deg\mathcal{W}+1
and
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\widetilde{\theta})
has parabolic weights at
p
given by

w_{r}-1<w_{1}\leq\ldots\leq w_{r-1}.

Again by the same argument for
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
we have that
\pardeg\mathcal{W}=\pardeg\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
. If
F_{1}\not\subset\mathcal{W}_{p}
, then
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\widetilde{\theta})=(\mathcal{W},\theta)
, so we still have
\pardeg\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\pardeg\mathcal{W}
.

Now with a similar argument with the above lemma, one can show that the inverse shifting operation also preserves the property of being a system of Hodge bundles. This means that we have also a one-to-one correspondence between
\theta
-stable subbundles of
\mathcal{E}
and
\widetilde{\theta}
-stable subbundles of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
via the shifting operation. We just saw that this shifting operation preserves parabolic degrees, and hence stability of
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
is preserved by the shifting operation.
∎

We will analyze this shifting operation again, more explicitly, when we analyze the
(1,2)
case.

5
The setup of the general
(1,n)
problem

Here our system of Hodge bundles decompose as
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V}
as above where
\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle and
\rank\mathcal{V}=n
with
\theta(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. The main difficulty in the
(1,n)
case is to determine when rank
r
subbundles
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{V}
passes through prescribed Schubert cells in
Gr(r,\mathcal{V}_{p})
. Since (semi)stability is an open condition, we are allowed to deform to the most generic case possible. In the unitary case, we are able to deform so that our bundle is an evenly split bundle, however, in this
(1,n)
case, we have
\theta:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
, which means that
\mathcal{V}
must contain a certain line subbundle (or subsheaf). This becomes an obstruction to deformation, and hence we cannot assume that
\mathcal{V}
is a generic rank
n
bundle. Furthermore, the condition that
\Res_{p}\theta
preserves the filtration means that in certain configuration of weights, we need the flags of
\mathcal{V}_{p}
to contain
\mathcal{L}_{p}
in some special way, which means that we may not assume our flags to be generic in general. These obstructions means that we cannot apply older techniques of Gromov-Witten theory from the unitary case to this new situation as is.

Since
\theta:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
and we need to consider only those bundles preserved by
\theta
for stability of
(\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V},\theta)
, there are two types of bundles to consider.

(
I
)

Every subbundle
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{V}
gets mapped to
0
by
\theta
and are in particular
\theta
-stable. Hence, we need to check every subbundle of
\mathcal{V}
.

(
II
)

The other type of bundle to consider are those bundles containing the
\mathcal{L}
factor in
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V}
. Thus, we need to consider subbundles of
\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
that contains the image of
\mathcal{L}
.

To enumerate bundles of type
(I)
, we will need to do enumerative geometry involving the nongeneric bundle
\mathcal{V}
equipped with nongeneric (quasi)parabolic structure, i.e. nongeneric flags at points
p\in S
. To enumerate bundles of type
(II)
, we can look at suitable quotient bundles.

Bundles of type
(II)
must be of form
\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V}
, where
\mathcal{L}
is the first factor of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{S}
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}
. Note that because of our conditions on weights, we may have
\theta:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
may not make
\mathcal{L}
into a subbundle. For example, if
\alpha(p)
are the weights of
\mathcal{L}
and
\beta_{1}(p)\leq\beta_{2}(p)\leq\ldots\beta_{n}(p)
are the weights of
V
, and we have that
\beta_{n}(p)<\alpha(p)
for some
p\in S
, then we must have that
\theta
has a zero at
p
, and hence would fail to make
\mathcal{L}
into a subbundle. We can insist on
\theta
generic enough so that the only zeroes are those where
\beta_{n}(p)<\alpha(p)
. In this case, suppose that
p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}
are the points where
\beta_{n}(p)<\alpha(p)
and
p_{l+1},\ldots,p_{s}
are the points where
\beta_{n}(p)\geq\alpha(p)
. Suppose
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-d)
and let
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
denote the saturation of
\mathcal{L}
, then we have that
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-d+l)
. If
\mathcal{S}
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}
such that
\mathcal{S}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
contains
\mathcal{L}
, then because
\mathcal{S}
is a subbundle, it must contain the saturation
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
as well.

Now
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-d+l)
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
and, by arguments in
[
4
]
, we can assume that
\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)/\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
is an evenly split bundle. In this case, suppose
\mathcal{V}
has degree
v
, then
\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)/\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
is an evenly split bundle of degree
v+n(s-2)+d-l
. The strategy now is to control
\mathcal{S}
by enumerating subbundles of
\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)/\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
and then taking preimages, since such preimages are exactly those subbundles that contain
\mathcal{L}
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a vector bundle and
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}
is a subbundle. Suppose
\mathcal{W}
has flags at point
p
given by
F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\ldots\subset F_{n}
. Denote by
\overline{\mathcal{W}}=\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{L}
and
\overline{F}_{1}\subset\overline{F}_{2}\subset\ldots\subset\overline{F}_{n}
the induced flag. Suppose
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
is a subbundle that contains
\mathcal{L}
, and denote
\overline{\mathcal{S}}=\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{L}
, the image in the quotient
\overline{\mathcal{W}}
. Then

\dim\left(\mathcal{S}_{p}\cap F_{i}\right)=\dim\left(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\cap\overline{F}_{i}\right)+\dim(F_{i}\cap\mathcal{L}_{p}).

Proof.

Consider the intersection
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\cap\overline{F}_{i}
. Writing this out, we get

\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\cap\overline{F}_{i}=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{p}}{\mathcal{L}_{p}}\cap\frac{F_{i}+\mathcal{L}_{p}}{\mathcal{L}_{p}}=\frac{\left(\mathcal{S}_{p}\cap F_{i}+\mathcal{L}_{p}\right)}{\mathcal{L}_{p}}=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{p}\cap F_{i}}{F_{i}\cap\mathcal{L}_{p}}.

Hence we find that
\dim\left(\mathcal{S}_{p}\cap F_{i}\right)=\dim\left(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\cap\overline{F}_{i}\right)-\dim\left(F_{i}\cap\mathcal{L}_{p}\right)
.
∎

6
The
(1,n)
case with generic weights

In our attack for the
(1,n)
case, we need to make some extra assumptions on the weights of our bundle. Give
\mathcal{L}
weights
\alpha(p)
for all
p\in S
and
\mathcal{V}
weights
\beta_{1}(p)<\beta_{2}(p)<\ldots<\beta_{n}(p)
, and fix the degrees of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{V}
. Furthermore, we also ask for the
\alpha
’s to be distinct from the
\beta
’s, that is, we ask for all weights distinct. Suppose that the induced flags on
\mathcal{V}
are called
F_{1}(p)\subset F_{2}(p)\subset\ldots\subset F_{n}(p)=\mathcal{V}_{p}
, where each
F_{i}(p)
is associated to weight
\beta_{n-i+1}(p)
(remember the weights and flags have reverse ordering).

Because we assumed that all the weights of
\mathcal{E}
are distinct, the shifting operation works in the exact same way as in the unitary case. And hence we still have a correspondence between subbundles of
\mathcal{E}
and subbundles of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
. In this case, there is no trouble if we just shift our weights to be such that

\alpha(p)<\beta_{1}(p)<\ldots<\beta_{n}(p)

for all
p\in S
. In this case, we have that
\theta(\mathcal{L})_{p}\subset F_{n}(p)
for all
p
, and hence we can give
\mathcal{V}
generic flags. Furthermore, since we have that
\alpha(p)<\beta_{1}(p)<\beta_{n}(p)
for all
p
, we necessarily have that
\theta
has poles at every
p\in S
, and hence we will have no subtleties in terms of
\theta
not making
\mathcal{L}
into a subbundle in this case. Thus, we assume further that
\theta
makes
\mathcal{L}
into a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
.

Set
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. For simplicity, suppose that
\deg\mathcal{W}=\deg\left(\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)\right)=0
, since the other cases will not be substantially different; the only difference would be, if
\deg\mathcal{W}=w
, we would compare
\deg\mathcal{L}
with
\lceil w/n\rceil
instead of 0. There are now two situations to consider:

(
A
)

\deg\mathcal{L}\leq 0
, then in this case case, we can assume
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-d)
for
d\geq 0
and, in this case, we can deform
\mathcal{W}
along with
\mathcal{L}
to be an evenly split bundle, so we can assume
\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}
. So in this case we have
\mathcal{O}(-d)\subset\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}
.

(
B
)

If
\deg\mathcal{L}>0
, so say
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d)
for
d>0
. In this case the most generic
\mathcal{W}
(along with
\mathcal{L}
) we can deform to is
\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
(see Lemma
3
), where
\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
denotes the evenly split degree
-d
rank
n-1
bundle, so we can assume
\mathcal{W}
is of this form already.

From here on, we assume that
\mathcal{E}
has been deformed to the most generic case possible as described in the two cases above.

The case when
\deg\mathcal{L}\leq 0
has already been solved before. In this case, we are left with counting subbundles of an evenly split bundle
\mathcal{W}
meeting generic flags with prescribed incidence conditions, which has been solved using quantum cohomology of Grassmannians. So the interesting case is exactly the case when we have a large subbundle.

The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1
. Recall the statement of the main theorem. Recall that
\pardeg\mathcal{L}+\pardeg\mathcal{V}=\pardeg\mathcal{E}
, and so fixing
\pardeg\mathcal{L}
and
\pardeg\mathcal{V}
automatically fixes
\pardeg\mathcal{E}
. Recall that we also made the simplifying assumption that
\deg\mathcal{W}=0
.

Theorem
.

A semistable system of Hodge bundles of type
(1,n)
with weights
\alpha(p)
and
\beta_{1}(p)<\beta_{2}(p)<\ldots<\beta_{n}(p)
as above with fixed
\deg\mathcal{L}
and
\deg\mathcal{V}
exist if and only if the following conditions in the different cases of bundles are satisfied.

(A)

If
\deg\mathcal{L}\leq 0
, then the following two types of numerical conditions must be met.

(I)

For every
I^{p_{1}},\ldots,I^{p_{s}}\subset[n]
of size
r
such that the usual Gromov-Witten number

\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta}\neq 0,

the inequality

\frac{-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\beta_{n-i+1}(p)}{r}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

holds.

(II)

For every
J^{p_{1}},\ldots,J^{p_{s}}\subset[n-1]
of size
r-1
such that the generalized Gromov-Witten number
\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d}\neq 0
, the inequality

\frac{2d-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{j\in J^{p}}\beta_{n-j+1}(p)\right)}{r+1}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

holds.

(B)

If
\deg\mathcal{L}>0
, then the following two types of numerical conditions must be met.

(I)

For every
I^{p_{1}},\ldots,I^{p_{s}}\subset[n]
of size
r
such that
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta}\neq 0
, the inequality

\frac{-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\beta_{n-i+1}(p)}{r}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

is satisfied.

(II)

For every
J^{p_{1}},\ldots,J^{p_{s}}\subset[n-1]
of size
r-1
such that
\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d-w}\neq 0
, then the inequality

\frac{2d-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{j\in J^{p}}\beta_{n-j+1}(p)\right)}{r+1}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

is satisfied.

We will prove this theorem by proving Theorem
2
which covers Case
(A)
and Theorem
3
which covers Case
(B)
, from which the result follows as an immediate corollary.

6.1
Case where
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}
deforms to a generic bundle

We will now tackle the Case
(A)
situation. Recall that to check stability for
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
,
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V}
, we need to check for two types of bundles: bundles that contain
\mathcal{L}
and bundles that are contained purely in
\mathcal{V}
. To control bundles that contain
\mathcal{L}
, note that such bundles take the form

\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V}

where
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{V}
is a subbundle where
\theta(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{S}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. To enumerate such bundles, we can use the strategy outlined above by looking at subbundles
\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
quotiented out by
\mathcal{L}
, which is given by
\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
.

Let
F_{\bullet}(p)
denote the flags of
\mathcal{W}
at the point
p\in S
. Since we asked for
\alpha(p)<\beta_{1}(p)
for all
p
, we always have
\theta(\mathcal{L})_{p}\subset F_{n}(p)
. And so the induced flag on
\overline{\mathcal{W}}:=\mathcal{W}/\theta(\mathcal{L})\simeq\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
for each
p\in S
is given by
\overline{F}_{1}(p)\subset\overline{F}_{2}(p)\subset\ldots\subset\overline{F}_{n-1}(p)
with associated weights
\beta_{n}(p)>\beta_{n-1}(p)>\ldots>\beta_{2}(p)
respectively. For subset
J^{p}=\{j^{p}_{1}<\ldots<j^{p}_{r-1}\}\subset[n-1]
, we know there will be a subbundle
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
of
\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
of degree
-\delta
and rank
r-1
meeting
\overline{F}_{\bullet}(p)
such that
\dim\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\cap\overline{F}_{j^{p}_{a}}(p)\geq a
if the generalized Gromov-Witten number (see
[
5
]
Definition 4.3.1)
\langle\sigma_{J^{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{s}}\rangle_{\delta,-d}\neq 0.
Because of our arguments in Section
2.5
, it suffices to just check the cases where these Gromov-Witten numbers are nonzero.

Proposition 1
.

With the assumptions above, if
J^{p_{1}},\ldots,J^{p_{s}}\subset[n-1]
are cardinality
r-1
subsets such that
\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d}\neq 0
, then the inequality

\frac{-2d-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{j\in J^{p}}\beta_{n-j+1}(p)\right)}{r+1}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

is a necessary inequality for the semistability of the parabolic system of Hodge bundles
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
.

Proof.

As above, we let
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
be the subbundle of
\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}\simeq\overline{\mathcal{W}}
guaranteed by the nonvanishing of the generalized Gromov-Witten number
\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d}
. This lifts to a subbundle of
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
containing
\theta(\mathcal{L})
of rank
r
; call this subbundle
\mathcal{S}
. Then
\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{S}(2-s)\subset\mathcal{E}
will be a
\theta
-stable subbundle of rank
r+1
, and hence we need to check
\parmu\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{S}(2-s)
against
\parmu\mathcal{E}
.

Firstly, note that
\deg\overline{\mathcal{S}}=-\delta
and
\overline{\mathcal{S}}\simeq\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{O}(-d)
, this gives us that
\deg\mathcal{S}=-\delta-d
, and hence
\deg\mathcal{S}(2-s)=-d-\delta+r(2-s)
. Hence we get that

\deg(\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{S}(2-s))=-2d-\delta+r(2-s).

Note that we can always take
\theta(\mathcal{L})
to be contained in
F_{n}(p)
for all
p\in S
, this means that we can give
\mathcal{S}(2-s)
the weights
\beta_{1}(p)
at all times, and the other weights must come exactly by how
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
meets
\overline{F}_{1}(p)\subset\ldots\subset\overline{F}_{n-1}(p)
at each
p\in S
, i.e. the weights of
\mathcal{S}(2-s)
are exactly given by
\beta_{1}(p)
and
\beta_{n-j+1}(p)
for
j\in J^{p}
for each
p\in S
.
∎

Next we need to enumerate the subbundles purely of
\mathcal{V}
, equivalently those purely of
\mathcal{W}
. In such a case, we can simply use regular Gromov-Witten numbers to count such subbundles.

Proposition 2
.

Let
I^{p}=\{I^{p}_{1}<I^{p}_{2}<\ldots<I^{p}_{r}\}\subset[n]
be size
r
subsets for
p\in S
. If
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta}\neq 0
, then the inequality

\frac{-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\beta_{n-i+1}(p)}{r}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

is a necessarily inequality for the semistability of
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
is the trivial rank
n
bundle, subbundles of this are exactly enumerated by Gromov-Witten invariants, i.e.
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta}
counts the number of degree
-\delta
subbundles of
\mathcal{W}
. If this is nonzero, then we have a subbundle
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
such that
\mathcal{S}_{p}
meets the flag
F_{\bullet}(p)
of
\mathcal{W}_{p}
at
j^{p}_{a}
for
a=1,\ldots,r
, which would give
\mathcal{S}
weights
\beta_{n-j+1}(p)
for
j=j^{p}_{1},j^{p}_{2},\ldots,j^{p}_{r}
. Now
\mathcal{S}
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{W}
if and only if
\mathcal{S}(2-s)
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}
, so then writing down the semistability condition for
\mathcal{S}(2-s)
gives us the inequality we desire.
∎

Combining the two propositions above together, we get the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

A semistable
(1,n)
parabolic system of Hodge bundles
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
, with parabolic weights
\{\alpha(p),\beta_{i}(p)\}_{i\in[n],p\in S}
given as above and of Case
(A)
exists if and only if the following conditions hold:

(I)

For every
I^{p_{1}},\ldots,I^{p_{s}}\subset[n]
of size
r
such that the usual Gromov-Witten number

\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta}\neq 0,

the inequality

\frac{-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\beta_{n-i+1}(p)}{r}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

holds.

(II)

For every
J^{p_{1}},\ldots,J^{p_{s}}\subset[n-1]
of size
r-1
such that the Generalized Gromov-Witten number
\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d}\neq 0
, the inequality

\frac{-2d-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{j\in J^{p}}\beta_{n-j+1}(p)\right)}{r+1}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

holds.

6.2
Case when the degree of
\mathcal{L}
is large

In Case
(B)
, when
\deg\mathcal{L}>0
, this requires more care. Here, we can deform
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}
so that
\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
by the following argument. In the below argument we do not make any assumptions on
\deg\mathcal{W}=0
, which again will not make a difference.

Lemma 3
.

In Case
(B)
, we can deform
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}
so that
\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{G}
, where
\mathcal{G}
is an evenly split rank
n-1
bundle.

Proof.

After sufficiently twisting
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}
by
\mathcal{O}(-m)
for
m>\!\!>0
, we can say that
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{O}^{\oplus N}
for some
N
. Thus, we can view
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{O}^{\oplus N}
as some element of a flag quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}
. Kim
[
11
]
showed that such quot schemes are irreducible, so if we can show the locus where
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{O}^{\oplus N}
is such that
W\simeq L\oplus\mathcal{G}
where
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}
sits inside
\mathcal{W}
as the first factor is open in this quot scheme, then we are done because semistability is an open condition and in an irreducible space, the intersection of two nonempty open sets is still nonempty open.

Consider the bundle
\mathcal{U}
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathcal{Q}
defined such that if
q
represents the point
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}
,

\mathcal{U}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times q}=\mathcal{U}|_{q}\simeq\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{L}.

It is a standard fact then that the locus in
\mathcal{Q}
where
\mathcal{U}|_{q}
splits as an evenly split bundle is open in
\mathcal{Q}
, say
\mathcal{U}|_{q}\simeq\mathcal{G}
. So we can restrict to looking at
0\to\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{G}\to 0.

If we can show that
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{G}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})=0
, then every extension
0\to\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{G}\to 0
is trivial, thus split, and hence we must have
\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{G}
.

Say that
\deg\mathcal{L}=d
and
\deg\mathcal{W}=w
,
\rank\mathcal{W}=n
. We know that
\mathcal{G}
must have degree
w-d
and rank
n-1
, so then
\mathcal{G}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{O}(e_{i})
where each
|e_{i}-e_{j}|\leq 1
. This forces
e_{i}\leq\left\lceil\frac{w-d}{n-1}\right\rceil
.
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=0
if each

\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}(e_{i}),\mathcal{L})=\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}(e_{i}),\mathcal{O}(d))\simeq H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(d-e_{i}))=0,

so if we can show that
d-e_{i}\geq 0
, then we are done. We know that

d-e_{i}\geq d-\left\lceil\frac{w-d}{n-1}\right\rceil,

so it suffices to show
d>\left\lceil\frac{w-d}{n-1}\right\rceil
. We will show that
w/n>(w-d)/(n-1)
, and hence

\left\lceil\frac{w}{n}\right\rceil\geq\left\lceil\frac{w-d}{n-1}\right\rceil.

Since
d>\lceil w/n\rceil\geq w/n
, we must have that
-d<-w/n
, and so

\frac{w(n-1)}{n}-w=\frac{wn-w}{n}-w>-d.

Rearranging, we then get that

\frac{w}{n}>\frac{w-d}{n-1}.

But by case
(B)
assumptions, we must have that
d>\lceil w/n\rceil
, and thus
d>\lceil(w-d)/(n-1)\rceil
.
∎

Now we want to understand subbundles
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
. We will proceed along techniques similar to Section 9 of
[
2
]
. Here, we are looking for subbundles

\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}.

Here there are two cases to consider:

(a)

When
\mathcal{O}(d)\subset\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
and

(b)

\mathcal{O}(d)
is not in
\mathcal{S}
.

The strategy is as follows: If
\mathcal{O}(d)\subset\mathcal{S}
, then since the inclusion
\mathcal{O}(d)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
is canonical (up to scalars),
\mathcal{S}
is then fully determined by
\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{O}(d)\subset\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}/\mathcal{O}(d)\simeq\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
. If
\mathcal{S}
does not contain a
\mathcal{O}(d)
, then we will look at
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}/\mathcal{S})
which is the obstruction space controlling the deformations of
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
; we will show that in this case we have no obstructions and thus we can view this case as a deformation of a subbundle inside a generic bundle.

Lemma 4
.

If
\mathcal{O}(d)
is not in
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
, then
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{S})=0
.

Proof.

Consider
0\to\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{S}\to 0
. Because we are over a curve, all
\Ext^{2}
’s vanish, and thus we have a surjection
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W})\to\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{S}).
Thus, it suffices to show that
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W})=0
.

Suppose
\mathcal{S}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}(a_{i})
. We have that

\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W})=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{W}),

so it suffices to show that

\displaystyle h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-a_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W})

\displaystyle=h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-a_{i})\otimes(\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}))

\displaystyle=h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(d-a_{i}))+h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-a_{i})\otimes\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1})=0.

Since we have that
\mathcal{O}(d)\not\subset\mathcal{S}
, this means in particular that each
a_{i}<d
, and hence
h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-a_{i})\otimes\mathcal{O}(d))=h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(d-a_{i}))=0
because
d-a_{i}\geq 0
. Thus, we are left with looking at
h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-a_{i})\otimes\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1})
.

Suppose that all the factors of
\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
are either
\mathcal{O}(e)
or
\mathcal{O}(e+1)
, then

\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}(a_{i}),\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1})

is a direct sum of vector spaces of the form

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-a_{i})\otimes\mathcal{O}(e))=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(e-a_{i}))

and

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-a_{i})\otimes\mathcal{O}(e+1))=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(e+1-a_{i})).

Because we asked for
\mathcal{O}(d)\not\subset\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}
is a subbundle, it must be then that the map
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
gives a nonzero map
\mathcal{O}(a_{i})\to\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
, which then implies that
a_{i}\leq e+1
. This implies that
e-a_{i}\geq-1
and
e+1-a_{i}\geq 0
, which then implies that

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(e-a_{i}))=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(e+1-a_{i}))=0,

and hence
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}(a_{i}),\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1})=0
. This gives us
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W})=0
.
∎

This result shows that shows that we have no obstructions to deforming both
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{S}
. This means that by standard deformation of quot schemes (see e.g. Chapter 7 of
[
9
]
or Chapter I of
[
12
]
), there exists a scheme
B
, a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-family
T\to B
, and bundles
\mathscr{W}
and
\mathscr{S}\subset\mathscr{W}
over
T
such that over a point
b\in B
we have that
\mathscr{S}|_{b}=\mathcal{S}\subset\mathscr{W}|_{b}=\mathcal{W}.
Generically,
\mathscr{W}
will be an evenly split bundle, and our goal will then be to count subbundles along the generic fiber instead.

Lemma 5

(Case
(B)
type
(I)
inequalities)
.

For each
p\in S
, let
I^{p}=\{i^{p}_{1}<i^{p}_{2}<\ldots<i^{p}_{r}\}\subset[n]
. If
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta}\neq 0
, then the inequality

\frac{-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\beta_{n-i+1}(p)}{r}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

is necessary for the semistability of
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
.

Proof.

If we have a subbundle
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
where
\mathcal{O}(d)\not\subset\mathcal{S}
, then by Lemma
4
we have that
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{S})=0
, so
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
deforms in a family
\mathscr{S}\subset\mathscr{W}
over base
B
as above. Let
\mathcal{Q}\to B
denote the quot scheme of subsheaves of
\mathscr{W}
and let
\mathcal{Q}_{b}
be the fiber of
\mathcal{Q}
over
b
. Let
U\subset\mathcal{Q}
be the locus of subbundles inside
\mathscr{W}
, and let
U_{b}
be the fiber over
b
. We have a smooth map
U_{b}\to\prod_{p\in S}Gr(r,\mathcal{W}_{p})
by sending the subbundle to its fiber over each
p\in S
. Now if
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is in some
\Omega_{I^{p}}(F_{\bullet}(p))
, then we can pull back to get an intersection of cycles on
U
, and by Kleiman transversality, we can assume that these cycles intersect transversely. Transversality is an open condition, and so we can look at the corresponding intersection over a generic fiber, which can be counted using Gromov-Witten invariants.

Let
\xi\in B
be a general point of
B
such that
\mathscr{W}|_{\xi}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus n}
. We want to know if there exists a subbundle
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
such that
\mathcal{S}_{p}\in\Omega_{I^{p}}(F_{\bullet}(p))
for general flags
F_{\bullet}(p_{1}),\ldots,F_{\bullet}(p_{s})
. By the arguments of the previous paragraph, we know such
\mathcal{S}
exists if there is some subbundle of the same rank and degree of
\mathscr{W}|_{\xi}
that lives in Schubert varieties of the given classes
\sigma_{I^{p}}
for
p\in S
. These are exactly given by the numbers
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta}
, which if nonzero means there is a subbundle of the generic bundle of rank
r
and degree
-\delta
that hits generic flags at the
i
-th component for
i\in I^{p}
for each
p\in S
, which then tells us such a rank
r
degree
-\delta

\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
exists.

Since
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
, we need to look at
\mathcal{S}(2-s)\subset\mathcal{V}
, which has degree

\deg\mathcal{S}(2-s)=-\delta+r(2-s).

At each
p\in S
,
\mathcal{S}
gets weights
\beta_{n-i+1}(p)

for each
i\in I^{p}
, hence we get

\pardeg\mathcal{S}(2-s)=-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\beta_{n-i+1}(p),

from which the semistability inequality follows.
∎

Remark
.

In the above argument, if we did not assume that
w=\deg\mathcal{W}=0
, then instead of the usual Gromov-Witten number
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta}
, we would need to consider instead the generalized Gromov-Witten number
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,-w}
.

Next, we need to handle the subbundles where
\mathcal{O}(d)\subset\mathcal{S}
. In this case, we would need to look at subbundles
\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{O}(d)\subset\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{O}(d)\simeq\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
. Here,
\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
comes equipped with the induced flags
\overline{F}_{1}(p)\subset\overline{F}_{2}(p)\subset\ldots\overline{F}_{n-1}(p)
because
\mathcal{O}(d)_{p}=\theta(\mathcal{L})_{p}
lives (strictly) in
F_{n}(p)
. In this case counting subbundles of
\mathcal{W}
that contain
\mathcal{O}(d)
is also the same as counting bundles of type
(II)
.

Lemma 6

(Case
(B)
type
(II)
inequalities)
.

For each
p\in S
, let
J^{p}=\{j^{p}_{1}<\ldots<j^{p}_{r-1}\}\subset[n-1]
. If the generalized Gromov-Witten number

\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d}\neq 0,

then the inequality

\frac{2d-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{j\in J^{p}}\beta_{n-j+1}(p)\right)}{r+1}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

is necessary for the semistability of
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
.

Proof.

If the generalized Gromov-Witten invariant
\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d}\neq 0
, then there exists a subbundle

\overline{\mathcal{S}}\subset\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}

of degree
-\delta
and rank
r-1
such that
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
meets the induced flags
\overline{F}_{\bullet}(p)
at each each
j\in J^{p}
for all
p\in S
. Then looking at the quotient
\mathcal{W}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{O}(d)\simeq\mathcal{G}_{-d,n-1}
, there is a unique (up-to-scalar) lift of
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
to a subbundle
\mathcal{S}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}
containing
\mathcal{O}(d)
of rank
r
.
\mathcal{S}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\overline{\mathcal{S}}
, so it has degree
d-\delta
. This bundle meets
F_{\bullet}(p)
at
F_{j}(p)
for each
j\in J^{p}
and at
F_{n}(p)
for each
p\in S
, which means that
\mathcal{S}
gets weights
\beta_{n-j+1}(p)
and
\beta_{1}(p)
.

Now, if
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{W}
, then we shift to
\mathcal{S}(2-s)
to get a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}
. Thus, for such
\mathcal{S}
containing
\theta(\mathcal{L})
, we get the inequality

\frac{d-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{j\in J^{p}}\beta_{n-j+1}(p)\right)}{r}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}.

Since this is an inequality for a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}
, it was already covered in the previous proposition. Now the subbundles of
\mathcal{E}
of type
(II)
are also exactly of the form
\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{S}(2-s)
for such
\mathcal{S}
, which gives us the inequality

\frac{2d-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{j\in J^{p}}\beta_{n-j+1}(p)\right)}{r+1}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}.

∎

Remark
.

As in the previous lemma, when
w=\deg\mathcal{W}\neq 0
, the generalized Gromov-Witten number we need to look at instead would be
\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d-w}
.

Once again, combining the two lemmas above, we get the following criteria for semistability of
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
.

Theorem 3
.

A
(1,n)
semistable parabolic system of Hodge bundles
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
with parabolic weights
\{\alpha(p),\beta_{i}(p)\}_{i\in[n],p\in S}
as above and of Case
(B)
exists if the following criteria are met.

(I)

For every subsets
I^{p_{1}},\ldots,I^{p_{s}}\subset[n]
of size
r
such that
\langle\sigma_{I^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{I^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta}\neq 0
, the inequality

\frac{-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\beta_{n-i+1}(p)}{r}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

is satisfied.

(II)

For every subsets
J^{p_{1}},\ldots,J^{p_{s}}\subset[n-1]
of size
r-1
such that
\langle\sigma_{J^{p_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{J^{p_{s}}}\rangle_{\delta,d}\neq 0
, the inequality

\frac{2d-\delta+r(2-s)+\sum_{p\in S}\left(\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{i\in J^{p}}\beta_{n-j+1}(p)\right)}{r+1}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{n+1}

is satisfied.

Combining Theorems
2
and
3
, we get Theorem
1
immediately.

Example 1
.

We now do a basic example using
S=\{0,1,\infty\}
.

Suppose we are given the data of weights

\displaystyle\alpha(0)<\beta_{1}(0)<\beta_{2}(0)<\beta_{3}(0)<\beta_{4}(0),

\displaystyle\alpha(1)<\beta_{1}(1)<\beta_{2}(1)<\beta_{3}(1)<\beta_{4}(1),

\displaystyle\alpha(\infty)<\beta_{1}(\infty)<\beta_{2}(\infty)<\beta_{3}(\infty)<\beta_{4}(\infty),

where
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V}
gets the weights in the same notation as above. Consider
\deg\mathcal{L}=1
and
\deg\mathcal{V}=0
. Because
\alpha(p)<\beta_{1}(p)<\beta_{\infty}(p)
for all
p
, we can assume that
\theta
takes
\mathcal{L}
to a subbundle of
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}(1)
. In this case, we can assume that
\mathcal{V}
is evenly split. We assume also that
\pardeg\mathcal{E}=0
, in which case we need that the sum of weights

\sum_{p\in\{0,1,\infty\}}\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\beta_{2}(p)+\beta_{3}(p)=0.

In this scenario, we have that
\theta
takes
\mathcal{O}(1)
to
\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 4}
. Here, taking
\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{L}
we get
\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 3}
. Since both bundles are twists of the trivial bundle, subbundles of
\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{L}
are then completely determined by quantum cohomology of
Gr(r,3)
and subbundles of
\mathcal{V}
by
Gr(r,4)
.

For subbundles of
\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{L}
, we can consider subsets
J^{0},J^{1},J^{\infty}
of
[4-1]
of size
r-1
. Here we can simply look at

\langle\sigma_{J^{0}},\sigma_{J^{1}},\sigma_{J^{\infty}}\rangle_{\delta},

which would count rank
r-1
and degree
1-\delta
subbundles of
\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 3}
in this instance. For each such nonzero
\langle\sigma_{J^{0}},\sigma_{J^{1}},\sigma_{J^{\infty}}\rangle_{\delta}
, we would get the semistability inequality

1-(\delta+r)+\sum_{p\in\{0,1,\infty\}}\left(\alpha(p)+\beta_{1}(p)+\sum_{i\in J^{p}}\beta_{4-i}(p)\right)\leq 0.

For subbundles of
\mathcal{V}
, we would need to consider subsets
I^{0},I^{1},I^{\infty}
of
[4]
and from here look at
\langle\sigma_{I^{0}},\sigma_{I^{1}},\sigma_{I^{\infty}}\rangle_{\delta}\neq 0
. Again, this would count all degree
-\delta
subbundles of
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{O}^{\oplus 4}
. This leads to the semistability inequality

\delta+\sum_{p\in\{0,1,\infty\}}\sum_{i\in I^{p}}\beta_{4-i+1}(p)\leq 0.

Hence now we need to consider when curve counts in
Gr(r,3)
and
Gr(r,4)
are nonzero.

For subbundles of
\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{L}
both
Gr(2,3)
and
Gr(1,3)
are the same as quantum cohomology of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, so for an easy illustration, we can simply look at the
r=1
case, i.e. we are looking for rank two subbundles of
\mathcal{W}
that contains
\mathcal{L}
. In this case, the subsets are
\{1\},\{2\}
and
\{3\}
. To do quantum cohomology computations, we convert these subsets to Young diagram data, which for
\mathbb{P}^{2}
just means a single integer
0\leq\lambda\leq 2
. The conversion is given as follows:

\displaystyle\{1\}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow 2,

\displaystyle\{2\}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow 1,

\displaystyle\{3\}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow 0.

We abuse notation and also denote
\sigma_{\lambda}
for the Schubert class associated with the Young diagram
\lambda
. Using this notation, we can then list out the quantum multiplication table for
Gr(1,3)
:

\sigma_{0}

\sigma_{1}

\sigma_{2}

\sigma_{0}

\sigma_{0}

\sigma_{1}

\sigma_{2}

\sigma_{1}

\sigma_{1}

\sigma_{2}

\sigma_{0}q

\sigma_{2}

\sigma_{2}

\sigma_{0}q

\sigma_{1}q

Reading off some select values, we see that
\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle_{1}\neq 0
and
\langle\sigma_{2},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{1}\rangle_{1}\neq 0.
Converting to subsets, we get
\langle\sigma_{\{2\}},\sigma_{\{2\}},\sigma_{\{1\}}\rangle_{1}\neq 0
and
\langle\sigma_{\{1\}},\sigma_{\{1\}},\sigma_{\{2\}}\rangle_{1}\neq 0
. These would correspond to

-1+\alpha(0)+\beta_{1}(0)+\beta_{2}(0)+\alpha(1)+\beta_{1}(1)+\beta_{2}(1)+\alpha(\infty)+\beta_{1}(\infty)+\beta_{3}(\infty)\leq 0

and

-1+\alpha(0)+\beta_{1}(0)+\beta_{3}(0)+\alpha(1)+\beta_{1}(1)+\beta_{3}(1)+\alpha(\infty)+\beta_{1}(\infty)+\beta_{2}(\infty)\leq 0

respectively.

For an inequality involving subbundles of
\mathcal{V}
, we would need to look at
Gr(r,4)
. In this case, the only Grassmannian with interesting quantum cohomology is when
r=2
, i.e.
Gr(2,4)
. One nonzero Gromov-Witten invariant we can get in this case would be
\langle\sigma_{(2,2)},\sigma_{(2,2)},\sigma_{(2,2)}\rangle_{2}=1
, which after translating to the language of subsets becomes

\langle\sigma_{\{1,2\}},\sigma_{\{1,2\}},\sigma_{\{1,2\}}\rangle_{2}=1.

Translating this into a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}
, we get a degree
-2
subbundle of
\mathcal{V}\simeq\mathcal{O}^{\oplus 4}
that passes through the first and second parts of the flags of
\mathcal{V}
at each
0,1,\infty
. This corresponds to the semistability inequality

-2+\sum_{p\in\{0,1,\infty\}}\beta_{4}(p)+\beta_{3}(p)\leq 0.

7
The
(1,2)
case using parabolic shifting

We can make less assumptions about the weights of our bundle in the
(1,2)
system of Hodge bundles case. Instead of asking for all the weights to be distinct, we will make no assumptions on the weight data, and instead ask just for the local monodromies of the local systems to be semisimple.

In this setting, our system of Hodge bundles decomposes again as
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V},
with
\theta:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. Now, we have
\rank\mathcal{L}=1
and
\rank\mathcal{V}=2
. We also fix
\deg\mathcal{L}
and
\deg\mathcal{V}
to be some chosen integers. Let
\alpha(p)
for
p\in S
be the single weight for the line bundle
\mathcal{L}
and
\beta_{1}(p)\leq\beta_{2}(p)
be the weights for the rank 2 bundle
\mathcal{V}
. Suppose there are
m
points
p\in S
such that

\beta_{1}(p)\leq\alpha(p)<\beta_{2}(p).

This means then that we have
m
points where the flag of
\mathcal{V}
at that point comes from
\mathcal{L}
. That is, if
F_{p}\subset\mathcal{V}_{p}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)_{p}
is the rank 1 subspace coming from the parabolic structure on
\mathcal{V}
, then we necessarily have that
F_{p}=\theta(\mathcal{L}_{p})\simeq\mathcal{L}_{p}.

To simplify the situation further, suppose that
p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}
are the points in
S
such that
\alpha(p)<\beta_{2}(p).
That is, these are exactly the points where
\theta
has a pole. So then
p_{l+1},\ldots,p_{s}
are the points where
\theta
has no pole. This means that we can simplify our situation to be such that

\theta:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(p_{1}+\ldots+p_{l})\subset\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S).

Simplifying this numerically, we have
\theta:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{V}(l-2)=\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}(l-2)
. Now, since stability is an open condition, and the degrees of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{V}
are fixed, we can suppose then that
\theta
is generic enough to make
\mathcal{L}
into a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}(l-2)
. For simplicity of notation, set
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}(l-2).

We will always deform to the most generic bundle
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V}
possible. There are two separate cases of vector bundles we can deform to. Suppose
\deg\mathcal{W}=\deg\mathcal{V}(l-2)=w
, then we have either that
\deg\mathcal{L}>\lceil w/2\rceil
or
\deg\mathcal{L}\leq\lceil w/2\rceil
. If
d=\deg\mathcal{L}>\lceil w/2\rceil
, then we necessarily have that the most generic version of
\mathcal{W}
we can deform to is
\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d)\oplus\mathcal{O}(w-d)
(see Lemma
3
). And in the case that
\deg\mathcal{L}\leq\lceil w/2\rceil
, then
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}
can be allowed to be deformed so that
\mathcal{W}
is an evenly split vector bundle of degree
w
. These two cases are essentially numerically the same in this situation as we will see by our arguments in the rest of this section, so we restrict ourselves to the case where
\mathcal{W}
is generic. Further, any differences differ only by numerical conditions, and does not fundamentally change the problem, so for ease of notation, we restrict ourselves to
\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}
. In summary, we simplify to the case of analyzing the situation of
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{O}^{\oplus 2}
, with
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d)
,
d\leq 0
.

Once again, there are two types of subbundles to consider, each giving a type of inequality.

(I)

Subbundles of
\mathcal{W}
.

(II)

\mathcal{L}\oplus\theta(\mathcal{L})\simeq\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{L}
.

The main challenge here is to study subbundles of
\mathcal{W}
, since we already know how
\mathcal{L}\oplus\theta(\mathcal{L})
behaves using the fact that the residues of
\theta
must preserve the filtration.

The strategy to handle the
(1,2)
case is to do the parabolic shifting operation at every point
p\in S
. Denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
the bundles after applying the parabolic shifting operation at every point of
S
. By doing this, we essentially get rid of the parabolic structure in the following sense: Asking for numerical conditions means asking which stability inequalities we need to write. Such inequalities are given by subbundles of
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{V}
. So if we shift along every point, we know then that the inequality coming from
\mathcal{S}
is valid if and only if
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
, the result of
\mathcal{S}
after applying the shift operation, is still a valid subbundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
. We can control this by directly reading the numerical data of
\pardeg\mathcal{S}
, i.e.
\deg\mathcal{S}
and when
\mathcal{S}_{p}
hit the special line subspace of
\mathcal{W}_{p}
for each
p\in S
, which are controlled by which weights
\mathcal{S}
has at
p
.

There is one small subtlety with regards to the parabolic shifting here: if we have that
\beta_{1}(p)\leq\alpha(p)<\beta_{2}(p)
(i.e.
\Res_{p}\theta
takes
\mathcal{L}_{p}
to the special subspace of
\mathcal{W}_{p}
), then we will actually apply the shift operation on
\mathcal{E}
twice so that we shift both
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{V}
in this instance; otherwise, we only shift once. This is mainly a bookkeeping device to preserve the number of poles of the Higgs field and will not affect anything else because the second shift will only affect
\mathcal{L}
, leaving
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
and hence
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
intact.

For this
(1,2)
case, we have the following theorem. Recall that fixing
\deg\mathcal{L}
and
\deg\mathcal{V}
and the weight data is enough to fix
\pardeg\mathcal{E}
.

Theorem 4

(Existence of semistable type
(1,2)
system of Hodge bundles)
.

Assume
\mathcal{V}
is generic in the locus where
\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}(p_{1}+\ldots+p_{l})
contains
\mathcal{L}
as a subbundle. Suppose also that
\deg\mathcal{L}
and
\deg\mathcal{V}
are some fixed numbers.

A stable system of Hodge bundles of type
(1,2)
where
\mathcal{L}
takes weights
\alpha(p)
and
\mathcal{V}
takes parabolic weights
\beta_{1}(p)\leq\beta_{2}(p)
and
\mathcal{E}
has the degree data as specified above exists if and only if the following conditions hold:

(I)

Let
I=(i(p))_{p\in S}
where each
i(p)\in\{1,2\}
. Set
N=|\{p\in S\mid i(p)=2\}|
. Then for all
I
and
\delta
such that
\mathcal{O}(\delta+N)
is a valid subbundle of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
, we have

\delta-(l-2)+\sum_{p\in S}\beta_{i(p)}(p)\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{3}.

(II)

The inequality

\frac{2\deg\mathcal{L}-(l-2)+\sum_{p\in S}\alpha(p)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\beta_{1}(p_{i})+\sum_{i=m+1}^{s}\beta_{2}(p_{i})}{2}\leq\frac{\pardeg\mathcal{E}}{3}

must hold.

7.1
Shifting operations in the (1,2) case

We will examine what the shifting operation does more explicitly in this
(1,2)
scenario.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a rank 2 bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Over
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, give
\mathcal{W}_{p}
a flag
F_{1}\subset F_{2}=\mathcal{W}_{p}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
. To complete the parabolic structure of
\mathcal{W}
, suppose we give
\mathcal{W}
weights
\beta_{1}\leq\beta_{2}
at
p
and for simplicity, we ignore all other points and say
\mathcal{W}
only has parabolic structure at
p
. Then in this case
F_{1}
has weight
\beta_{2}
and
F_{2}
has weight
\beta_{1}
.

Let
t
be a local parameter of
p
and give
\mathcal{W}_{p}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
a basis compatible with the filtration
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
such that

F_{1}=\mathbb{C}e_{1},\quad F_{2}=\mathbb{C}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{2}.

Locally around
p
, we have that
\mathcal{W}
is trivialized as
\mathcal{O}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}e_{2}
. When we apply the shift operation around
p
, we get a new bundle of degree 1 higher,
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
with flag
\widetilde{F}_{1}=\mathbb{C}e_{2}\subset\widetilde{F}_{2}=\mathbb{C}e_{2}\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{1}/t
and local trivialization
\mathcal{O}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}e_{1}/t
. After shifting, we have that the parabolic weights of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
are
\widetilde{\beta}_{1}=\beta_{2}-1\leq\widetilde{\beta}_{2}=\beta_{1}
, with
\widetilde{F}_{1}
taking weight
\widetilde{\beta}_{2}=\beta_{1}
and
\widetilde{F}_{2}
has weight
\widetilde{\beta}_{1}=\beta_{2}-1
.

7.1.1
Shifting and subbundles

Suppose
\mathcal{S}
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{W}
of degree
d
. Then locally around
p
,
\mathcal{S}
is given by a section
ae_{1}+be_{2}
. When we shift, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
denote the subbundle
\mathcal{S}
as viewed as a
subsheaf
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
. Locally,
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
is given by the section

at\frac{e_{1}}{t}+be_{2}.

The goal now is to understand the correspondence between subbundles of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
and subbundles of
\mathcal{W}
and also how the parabolic structure is affected by this correspondence.

Suppose
\mathcal{S}
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{W}
that hits
F_{1}
. That means
\mathcal{S}_{p}=\mathbb{C}e_{1}
, and so locally
\mathcal{S}
can be given by the section
e_{1}+ate_{2}.
In this case, once we shift, we get that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
is given by

te_{1}/t+ate_{2}

which means that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
now fails to be a subbundle of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
. We can then replace
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
by its saturation. Doing this, we get that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
is given locally by

e_{1}/t+ae_{2}

which is a subbundle that does not hit
\widetilde{F}_{1}=\mathbb{C}e_{2}
, and hence
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
gets the weight coming from
\widetilde{F}_{2}
, i.e.
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
gets weight
\widetilde{\beta}_{1}=\beta_{2}-1
. Since
\mathcal{S}
hits
F_{1}
, this means that
S
gets weight
\beta_{2}
. Since locally
\widetilde{S}
is given by
e_{1}/t+ate_{2}
, it means that we now have a pole of
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
at
p
, and hence
\deg\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}=d+1
. So then looking at the parabolic degrees of
\mathcal{S}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
, we get that

\pardeg\mathcal{S}=d+\beta_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\pardeg\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}=d+1+\widetilde{\beta}_{1}=(d+1)+(\beta_{2}-1)=d+\beta_{2}.

So in fact, we have that
\pardeg{\mathcal{S}}=\pardeg\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
in this case.

Suppose now
\mathcal{S}
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{W}
that does not hit
F_{1}
. So then locally
\mathcal{S}
is given by

ae_{1}+e_{2},

and
\pardeg\mathcal{S}=d+\beta_{1}.
Then applying the parabolic shift operation, we get that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
is given locally by section

at(e_{1}/t)+e_{2}.

Note that we do not have a pole at
p
, so in fact
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
is still a subbundle of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
. Note also that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
must hit
\widetilde{F}_{1}=\mathbb{C}e_{2}
at
p
, so we have that
\pardeg\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}=d+\widetilde{\beta}_{2}=d+\beta_{1}
. So then, we have that once again
\pardeg S=\pardeg\widetilde{S}
. This means that shifting in this way does not affect the parabolic degree.

Next, we want to look at subbundles of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
. Suppose
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
is a subbundle of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
of degree
d+1
. Suppose first that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
hits
\widetilde{F}_{1}=\mathbb{C}e_{2}
at
p
. Then we can rewrite the local section giving
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
as

e_{2}+ate_{1}/t.

Now note that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
now does not have a pole at
p
and so in fact is also a subbundle of
\mathcal{W}
. As above, to distinguish between
\widetilde{S}
as a subbundle of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
and as a subbundle of
\mathcal{W}
, we again denote by
\mathcal{S}
the subbundle of
\mathcal{W}
given by
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
. As a subbundle of
\mathcal{W}
,
\mathcal{S}
is given by local section
e_{2}+ae_{1}
, which in particular means that
\mathcal{S}
must not
F_{1}=\mathbb{C}e_{1}
at
p
. In terms of parabolic structure, we can say that

\pardeg\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}=d+1+\widetilde{\beta}_{2}=d+1+\beta_{1}

and

\pardeg{\mathcal{S}}=d+1+\beta_{1}.

So in particular,
\pardeg S=\pardeg\widetilde{S}
once again in this situation.

Now suppose
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
is a subbundle of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
that does not hit
\widetilde{F}_{1}=\mathbb{C}e_{2}
. This means that locally
\widetilde{S}
is given by

ae_{2}+e_{1}/t.

Consider a subbundle
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{W}
given locally by

ate_{2}+e_{1}.

This is a subbundle that hits
F_{1}=\mathbb{C}e_{1}
, so then from the above arguments, we see that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
arise as a result of taking the saturation of
\mathcal{S}
when viewed as a subsheaf of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
. Then, same argument as above tells us that
\deg\mathcal{S}=\deg\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}-1=d
. Under these circumstances, we see that

\pardeg\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}=d+1+\widetilde{\beta}_{1}=d+1+\beta_{2}-1=d+\beta_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\pardeg{\mathcal{S}}=d+\beta_{2}.

Note in this case that
\pardeg\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}=\pardeg\mathcal{S}
as well.

So in summary, what we have is that writing the stability inequalities for
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
implies every stability inequality for
\mathcal{W}
, as the subbundles of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
corresponds exactly to the subbundles of
\mathcal{W}
and as we checked, they all have the same parabolic degrees as well.

7.2
Parabolic shifts on the splitting type

Now we look at
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}
, where
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-d)
and
\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}
. Suppose that we have
m
points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
such that
\theta(\mathcal{L})_{p}
is the special subspace
F_{p}\subset\mathcal{W}_{p}
. Numerically speaking,
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
are the points where
\beta_{1}(p)\leq\alpha(p)<\beta_{2}(p)
. After doing parabolic shifts along the
m
points, we shift the bundle to

\mathcal{O}(m-d)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}.

The question is then what happens to the splitting type of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
? As a first step, we look at when
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
is guaranteed to fail to be evenly split.

Lemma 7
.

If
m>2d+1
, then
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
fails to be evenly split.

Proof.

Shifting along the
m
points, we have

\mathcal{O}(m-d)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
has degree
m
. Suppose that
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\simeq\mathcal{O}(a)\oplus\mathcal{O}(b)
and that
a\geq b
. In order for
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
to be evenly split, we would need
a\leq\lceil m/2\rceil
. For convenience, we can simply recast this inequality as

a\leq m/2+1/2.

For
\mathcal{O}(m-d)
to even inject into
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
, we would need

m-d\leq a\leq m/2+1/2.

Thus we have
m-d\leq m/2+1/2
which implies that
m\leq 2d+1
. So if
m>2d+1

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
cannot be generic.
∎

Example 2
.

For a simple example of the situation, consider
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}(-1)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}
. This corresponds to a degree 1 map
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which we know can send 3 points to any 3 points. That means for 3 points, we can guarantee that the subspaces coming from
\mathcal{L}
are generic. We can recover this 3 from Lemma
7
because, here
d=1
, so
2d+1=3
.

Suppose we have
m>2d+1
points coming from
\mathcal{L}
. That is, we have
m
points, say
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
in
S
such that
\theta(\mathcal{L})_{p}=F_{p}\subset\mathcal{W}_{p}
for each
p=p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
. We want to apply the parabolic shifting operation along these
m
points. We can actually see very explicitly what happens to the splitting type.

We do the shifts one at a time. We can suppose that
\theta(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{W}
generically enough such that at any three points,
\theta(\mathcal{L})_{p}
sits as a generic subspace inside
\mathcal{W}_{p}
. Suppose we shift first along
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
.

No shifts, i.e. the original situation:
\mathcal{O}(-1)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}

Shift at
p_{1}
:
\mathcal{O}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}

Shift at
p_{2}
:
\mathcal{O}(1)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)

Shift at
p_{3}
:
\mathcal{O}(2)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)

Now by the above lemma, we see that afterwards from here, we only get non-evenly split bundles. If we shift along a fourth point
p_{4}
, we would get

\mathcal{O}(3)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(3)\oplus\mathcal{O}(1).

Now this must be the case because in order for
\mathcal{O}(3)
must inject into
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
, one of the summands of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
must be
\mathcal{O}(a)
for
a\geq 3
. Further, we have that
\mathcal{W}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\subset\mathcal{W}(p_{4})
, which numerically translates to

\mathcal{O}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\subset\mathcal{O}(3)\oplus\mathcal{O}(2).

Note that
\deg\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\deg\mathcal{W}+1
. So then in order for
\mathcal{O}(3)
to inject into
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
, the only possible choice for
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
is
\mathcal{O}(3)\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)
.

It is then easy to see that continuing further, we get

\mathcal{O}(m-1)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(m-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)

for the general case after shifting along
m
points lying on the
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}(-1)
sitting inside the
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}
.

We can suppose that
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\theta(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{W}
are generic enough such that if we shift along less than
2d+1
points, then the resulting shifted bundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
is still generic.

Lemma 8
.

For
m>2d+1
, if we shift
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}(-d)\subset\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}
along the
m
points, the splitting type of the resulting bundles must be

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{O}(m-d)\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\mathcal{O}(m-d)\oplus\mathcal{O}(d).

Proof.

We can choose
2d+1
among the
m
points generic enough so that after shifting along them we get

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}(2d+1-d)=\mathcal{O}(d+1)\subset\mathcal{W}^{\prime}

where
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
will denote
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{W}
after shifting along the
2d+1
points. The degree of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is
2d+1
and it is generic, thus

\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d+1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(d).

We can assume WLOG that
m=2d+2
. So then shifting one more time, say along
p
, we then have

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{O}(2d+2-d)=\mathcal{O}(d+2)

and
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
has degree
2d+2
with
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\subset\mathcal{W}(p)
. We have that

\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{O}(d+1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(d)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{W}(p)\simeq\mathcal{O}(d+2)\oplus\mathcal{O}(d+1).

Then in order for
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{O}(d+2)
to inject into
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
, the only option available is

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\mathcal{O}(d+2)\oplus\mathcal{O}(d).

Continuing this way for arbitrary
m>2d+1
, we would get

\mathcal{O}(m-d)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(m-d)\oplus\mathcal{O}(d).

∎

Now
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
are the only points that impose special conditions on the flags of
\mathcal{V}
, and hence at all the other points, we may choose the flags to be as generic as we like. Then, shifting along all the other points will make our bundle less special. If we shift along
p_{m+1},\ldots,p_{l}
, we would get then that splitting type of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
would be
\mathcal{O}(m-d)\oplus\mathcal{O}(d+l-m)
if
d+l-m\leq m-d
and otherwise
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
would be a generic rank 2 degree
l
bundle. However, we must now be careful about shifting at all the other points.

For
p=p_{l+1},\ldots,p_{s}
, we have that
\beta_{2}(p)\leq\alpha(p)
, which means that
\theta
does not admit poles at these points, but then after shifting, we have that
\widetilde{\alpha}(p)<\widetilde{\beta}_{2}(p)
, which means then that we suddenly get a new pole. This means that extra book keeping is required here. Fortunately, what happens is that we get an extra pole of
\widetilde{\theta}_{p}
, and so we will consider instead the shifted bundle but now twisted with
\mathcal{O}(p)
. For example, when we have
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\simeq\mathcal{O}(m-d)\oplus\mathcal{O}(d+l-m)
, i.e. we have shifted at all the
l
points where
\theta
already has a pole, if we shift one more time and twist by
\mathcal{O}(p_{l+1})
, we now only consider
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}(p_{l+1})\simeq\left(\mathcal{O}(m-d)\oplus\mathcal{O}(d+l-m)\right)\otimes\mathcal{O}(1).
We will abuse notation to denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
what happens to
\mathcal{W}
after shifting at all points
p\in S
as described in the above procedures, including this extra bookkeeping of adding in extra poles.

7.3
The semistability inequalities we need

Now we want to write down the inequalities guaranteeing semistability of our bundle
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{V}
. Recall that we defined
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}(l-2)
. Denote the correction term by
Q=l-2
for simplicity of notation. Recall that to check stability of
\mathcal{E}
requires us to check for two types of subbundles:

(I)

Subbundles purely of
\mathcal{W}
(which are the same as subbundles of
\mathcal{V}
after an appropriate twist).

(II)

The subbundle
\mathcal{L}\oplus\left(\theta(\mathcal{L})(-Q)\right)
.

The first case gives us an easy inequality to write, since by our assumptions,
\theta(\mathcal{L})
hits the special subspace of
\mathcal{V}
only when
\beta_{1}(p)\leq\alpha(p)<\beta_{2}(p)
, which by assumption only happens when
p=p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in S
. This means that the inequality we need to write here is

Possible connection to tropical geometry

The problem of enumerating stability conditions for
(1,2)
systems Hodge bundles with non-generic rank 2 component is essentially the same as the problem of enumerating subbundles of the non-generic rank 2. In such a case, suppose for simplicity that we are left with the situation that
\theta:\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a)
. Consider then the Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a))
, and consider a section
C\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a)))
. By universal property characterizing projective bundles, we must have that the section
C\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
corresponds to a line subbundle of
\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a)
. The degree of this subbundle is then determined by the divisor class of
C
inside
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a))
. Suppose we set
F
to be the fiber class and
C_{0}
the 0-section class, then all sections of
\pi:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a))\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
must have divisor class of the form
C=C_{0}+bF
. Then to understand the stability conditions for
\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a)
, we are left with understanding the curves corresponding to certain section classes living inside the Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a))
.

The
(1,2)
system of Hodge bundles situation prescribes that we have certain flags, some coming from
\theta:\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a)
, which would correspond to points lying on the infinity section of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a))
, and other flags coming without conditions, which would become generic points on the Hirzebruch surface. The question is then to compute the (logarithmic) Gromov-Witten invariants of curves passing through certain prescribed generic points along with adding certain incidence conditions with respect to the infinity section. Such counts have been computed using techniques from tropical geometry as investigated in

The above analysis of the generic bundle case and three points give good indication for what happens in the general case. Again let
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
be our bundle. Suppose the weights of
\mathcal{L}
are
\alpha(p)
and the weights of
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are
\alpha^{\prime}(p)
for
p\in S

S

.

A stable parabolic system of Hodge bundles over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with parabolic structure over
S=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
of type
(1,1)
with semisimple local monodromies exists if and only if there is a system of weights
(\alpha(p),\alpha^{\prime}(p))_{p\in S}
such that

•

If
\sum_{p\in S}(\alpha(p)+\alpha^{\prime}(p))=2N+1
, then

\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in S}\ell(p)<N+1-\sum_{p\in S}\alpha^{\prime}(p);

•

\sum_{p\in S}(\alpha(p)+\alpha^{\prime}(p))=2N

1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in S}\ell(p)<N-\sum_{p\in S}\alpha^{\prime}(p).

Proof.

We tackle first the case
\sum_{p\in S}(\alpha(x)+\alpha^{\prime}(x))=2N
. Then for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, our bundle must be
\mathcal{O}(-N-k)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-N+k),
where now we can assume
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-N-k)
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-N+k)

\ell(p)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\alpha^{\prime}(p)>\alpha(p)$,}\\

\theta:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
becomes

\theta:\mathcal{O}(-N-k)\to\mathcal{O}(-N+k)\otimes\mathcal{O}\left(-2+\sum_{p\in S}\ell(p)\right),

which tells us that
-N-k\leq-N+k-2+\sum_{p\in S}\ell(p)
, i.e. the

k\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in S}\ell(p).

And then from the stability condition we get that

\deg\mathcal{L}^{\prime}+\sum_{p\in S}\alpha^{\prime}(p)<0.

Then since
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}(-N+k)
, this condition is exactly

-N+k+\sum_{p\in S}\alpha^{\prime}(p)<0.

So then we can bound

to be

1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in S}\ell(p)\leq k<N-\sum_{p\in S}\alpha^{\prime}(p).

. With
\ell(p)
defined the same as before,

-N-k\leq-N-1+k-2+\sum_{p\in S}\ell(p),

so we have

k\geq\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in S}\ell(p).

The stability condition finally becomes

-N-1+k+\sum_{p\in S}\alpha^{\prime}(p)<0.

So we get

\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in S}\ell(p)\leq k<N+1-\sum_{p\in S}\alpha^{\prime}(p).

Of course, any data that satisfies the conditions specified can be a valid parabolic system of Hodge bundles.
∎

8.3
Comparison with regular parabolic stability

Note that for regular parabolic stability, we need that every subbundle with the induced filtration must have parabolic degree less than zero. We will see that this is explicitly not the case in our Higgs bundle setting.

Proposition 5
.

Stable parabolic system of Hodge bundles of type
(1,1)
are not stable as regular parabolic bundles.

Proof.

Suppose after applying shift operations we have that
\sum_{p\in S}\alpha(p)+\alpha^{\prime}(p)=0
. This means that
N=0
in Proposition
4
, so our bundle must look like

\mathcal{O}(-k)\oplus\mathcal{O}(k),

where
\theta:\mathcal{O}(-k)\to\mathcal{O}(k)\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S).
The parabolic degree 0 condition becomes

\left(-k+\sum_{p\in S}\alpha(p)\right)+\left(k+\sum_{p\in S}\alpha^{\prime}(p)\right)=0,

and the parabolic stability condition tells us that

k+\sum_{p\in S}\alpha^{\prime}(p)<0.

But then this implies that

-k+\sum_{p\in S}\alpha(p)>0,

which contradicts stability, since this is the parabolic degree of the
\mathcal{O}(-k)
subbundle.
∎

8.4
Comparison with Biswas’ criterion

Biswas provided a criterion for the existence of rank 2 stable parabolic bundles of degree zero with prescribed weights. Suppose that after applying our shift operations on our bundles, we have that the parabolic weights sum to zero. Then in this setting, Biswas’ criterion becomes the following theorem.

Theorem

(
[
8
]
)
.

There is a parabolic bundle of degree zero and with parabolic weights
\{\alpha(p)\geq\beta(p)\}
at
p\in S
if and only if for any subset
A\subset S
of cardinality
2j+1
, with
j
a non-negative integer, the following inequality holds:

-j+\sum_{p\in A}\alpha(p)+\sum_{p\in S\setminus A}\beta(p)<0.

Now in the case of
n=|S|=3
, we have that our local system is necessarily rigid in the sense of
[
10
]
. So if
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is our parabolic Higgs bundle, then any other local system with local monodromies conjugate to the ones given by the Higgs bundle must give an isomorphic local system. But now, Biswas’ criterion gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a
U(2)
local system, whereas our conditions guarantee the existence of
U(1,1)
local system. In particular, because we require our local systems to be irreducible, this necessarily means that we cannot satisfy both Biswas’ criterion and the criterion of Proposition
4
. We will show this directly:

Proposition 6
.

Suppose
|S|=3
and
\{\alpha(p),\alpha^{\prime}(p)\}_{p\in S}
is a system of weights that satisfy Biswas’ criterion. Suppose further that
\alpha(p)+\alpha^{\prime}(p)=0
for all
p\in S
, i.e. we have local monodromies of determinant one. Then this system of weights cannot satisfy the criterion given in Proposition
4
.

Proof.

To fix notation, suppose
S=\{0,1,\infty\}
and set
\ell=\sum_{p\in S}\ell(p)
for ease of notation.
\ell
could be
0,1,2,
or
3
. We will deal with ease of these cases separately. Now from Proposition
4
, we see that

1-\frac{1}{2}\ell\leq k<-\sum_{p}\alpha^{\prime}(p)=\sum_{p}\alpha(p),

and recall that we are considering the stability of the bundle
\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
with Higgs field
\theta
sending
\mathcal{L}
to
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. We have in this case that
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-k)
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\simeq\mathcal{O}(k)
. We further have that
\max(\alpha(p),\alpha^{\prime}(p))\leq 1/2
because
\alpha(p)+\alpha^{\prime}(p)=0
, and
|\alpha(p)-\alpha^{\prime}(p)|\leq 1
because the shift operation preserves their distances.

Now we consider each case of
\ell
.

\ell=0
: In this case we have that

1\leq k<\sum_{p}\alpha(p)\leq 3/2,

so the only possible
k
is
1.
In this case, set
j=1
, so then
A\subset S
has cardinality
2+1=3
, so
A=S
. Now since
\ell=0
, we have that each
\alpha^{\prime}(p)\leq\alpha(p)
, so then Biswas’ inequality becomes

-1+\alpha(0)+\alpha(1)+\alpha(\infty)<0.

But, this implies that
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the stability condition, i.e. we have that
\pardeg\mathcal{L}<0=\pardeg\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, but this necessarily implies that
\pardeg\mathcal{L}^{\prime}>0
as
\pardeg\mathcal{L}+\pardeg\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=0
, which contradicts the stability condition for
\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
to be a stable parabolic system of Hodge bundles.

\ell=1
: In this case, suppose WLOG that
\alpha^{\prime}(0)>\alpha(0)
. This then implies that
\alpha(0)<0
, which then implies that

\frac{1}{2}\leq k<\sum\alpha(p)\leq 1.

So in this case, no such
k
is allowed.

\ell=2
: Suppose WLOG that
\alpha^{\prime}(0)>\alpha(0)
and
\alpha^{\prime}(1)>\alpha(1)
, so we have that
\alpha(0),\alpha(1)<0
. So then we have that

0\leq k<1/2.

So the only possible
k
is 0. Now use the Biswas criterion for
j=0
. So in this case
|A|=1
. In this case, let
A=\{\infty\}
, so then Biswas’ inequality becomes

\alpha(\infty)+\alpha(0)+\alpha(1)<0.

So again, this contradicts parabolic Higgs stability.

\ell=3
: This is the case where each
\alpha(p)\leq\alpha^{\prime}(p)
. But then this implies that

-\frac{1}{2}\leq k<0,

and no such
k
can satisfy this condition.

∎

9
The
(1,1,\ldots,1)
case

We can further examining the case of
(1,1,\ldots,1)
systems of Hodge bundles. In this situation, there are no particular subtleties involved with subbundles and flags, and so stability in this case is a purely numerical phenomenon.

In this setting, we suppose our bundle
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{E}_{N}
with each
\mathcal{E}_{i}
being a line bundle. Our Higgs field is
\theta:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
with
\theta_{i}:=\theta\mid_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}:\mathcal{E}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. Denote the weights of
\mathcal{E}_{i}
at
p\in S
by
\alpha_{i}(p)
. Suppose again that we have semisimple monodromies on the level of local systems, then this implies that we have a strongly parabolic bundle and in particular we have that

\Res_{p}\theta(\mathcal{E}_{i,\alpha})\subset\mathcal{E}_{i-1,\widehat{\alpha}},

where
\widehat{\alpha}
is the next highest weight after
\alpha
.

If we have that
\alpha_{i}(p)\leq\alpha_{i-1}(p)
, then since

\Res_{p}\theta(\mathcal{E}_{i-1,\alpha_{i}(p)})\subset\mathcal{E}_{i,\widehat{\alpha_{i}(p)}}=0,

this means that
\theta_{i}:\mathcal{E}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
, has no pole at
p
, and so we can regard this map actually as a morphism
\mathcal{E}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega(\log(S\setminus\{p\}))
. We can use this information to restrict the possible degrees of
\mathcal{E}_{i}
.

Similar to the
(1,1)
case, define

\ell_{i}(p)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\alpha_{i}(p)>\alpha_{i-1}(p)$},\\
0&\text{if $\alpha_{i}(p)\leq\alpha_{i-1}(p)$.}\end{cases}

So then from here, we see that
\ell_{i}:=\sum_{p\in S}\ell_{i}(p)
counts the number of poles of
\theta_{i}
. And since
\theta_{i}
is a nonzero map

\theta_{i}:\mathcal{E}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}\left(\sum_{p\in S}\ell_{i}(p)\right),

we must have then that
d_{i}:=\deg\mathcal{E}_{i}\leq d_{i+1}+\left(-2+\sum_{p\in S}\ell_{i}(p)\right)
.

In the
(1,1,\ldots,1)
case, the only
\theta
-stable subbundles compatible with the Hodge decomposition is
\mathcal{E}_{m}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{E}_{N}
, so the semistability condition tells us that

\sum_{i=m}^{N}d_{i}+\sum_{i=m}^{N}\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{i}(p)\leq 0,

with
d_{i}\leq d_{i+1}-2+\sum_{p\in S}\ell_{i}(p)
.

In the case where our bundle is of type
(1,1,1)
, we can combine all the inequalities into one single long string of inequalities. In this case, we will denote our parabolic system of Hodge bundles as
\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{3}
with
\theta(\mathcal{L}_{i})\subset\mathcal{L}_{i+1}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
. Furthermore, we can also repeatedly apply the parabolic shifting operation described in Section
4
so that the sum of weights is equal to 0, i.e.

\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\alpha_{i}(p)=0.

Proposition 7
.

Let
(\alpha_{1}(p),\alpha_{2}(p),\alpha_{3}(p))_{p\in S}
be a system of numbers such that

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{i}(p)=0

and let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.

There exists a semistable type
(1,1,1)
parabolic system of Hodge bundles
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
of parabolic degree 0 with
\mathcal{L}_{i}
having weights
\alpha_{i}(p)
and
\deg\mathcal{L}_{i}=d_{i}
if and only if the following numerical conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\bullet\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{2}(p)+\alpha_{3}(p)\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}-2+\ell_{1}\leq d_{3}-4+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq-\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{3}(p)-4+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2},

\displaystyle\bullet\quad

\displaystyle d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}=0.

Proof.

We assumed that
\pardeg\mathcal{E}=0
, so we have that

\pardeg\mathcal{E}=d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{i}(p)=0.

But we have that
\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{i}(p)=0
, which implies that
d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}=0
.

The only
\theta
-stable subsheaves to check in this situation are
\mathcal{L}_{3}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{3}
. The semistability inequality on
\mathcal{L}_{3}
gives us

d_{3}+\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{3}(p)\leq 0,

which gives us that

d_{3}\leq-\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{3}(p).

The semistability condition on
\mathcal{L}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{3}
gives

d_{2}+d_{3}+\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{2}(p)+\alpha_{3}(p)\leq 0.

Since
d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}=0
, we have that
d_{1}=-d_{2}-d_{3}
, and hence we get

\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{2}(p)+\alpha_{3}(p)\leq d_{1},

which is the first part of our inequalities. Now since
\mathcal{L}_{1}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}\otimes\Omega^{1}(\log S)
is a nonzero map, we have that

d_{1}\leq d_{2}-2+\sum_{p\in S}\ell_{1}(p),

and similarly since
\mathcal{L}_{2}\to\mathcal{L}_{3}\otimes\Omega(\log S)
, we get that
d_{2}\leq d_{3}-2+\sum_{p}\ell_{2}(p)
. Putting everything together, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{2}(p)+\alpha_{3}(p)

\displaystyle\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}-2+\sum_{p\in S}\ell_{1}(p)\leq d_{3}-4+\sum_{p\in S}\ell_{1}(p)+\ell_{2}(p)\leq

\displaystyle-\sum_{p\in S}\alpha_{3}(p)-4+\sum_{p\in S}\ell_{1}(p)+\ell_{2}(p).

Replacing
\sum_{p\in S}\ell_{i}(p)
by
\ell_{i}
gives us the system of inequalities that we desire.

Conversely given numerical data that satisfies inequalities given, it is easy to construct a semistable parabolic system of Hodge bundles of type
(1,1,1)
with these numerical conditions.
∎

Remark
.

Since the conditions for the
(1,1,\ldots,1)
case are purely numerical and require no subbundle enumeration, we can easily change the inequalities above to strict inequalities and get necessary stability inequalities.

An improved hypergraph Mantel’s Theorem
Daniel Iľkovič, Jun Yan

Abstract
In a recent paper [ 1 ] , Chao and Yu used an entropy method to show that the Turán density of a certain family ℱ \mathcal{F} of ⌊ r / 2 ⌋ \left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor triangle-like r r -uniform hypergraphs is r ! / r r r!/r^{r} . Later, Liu [ 8 ] determined for large n n the exact Turán number ex ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) \ex(n,\mathcal{F}) of this family, and showed that the unique extremal graph is the balanced complete r r -partite r r -uniform hypergraph. These two results together can be viewed as a hypergraph version of Mantel’s Theorem. In this paper, building on their methods, we improve both of these results by showing that they still hold with a subfamily ℱ ′ ⊂ ℱ \mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F} of size ⌈ r / e ⌉ \left\lceil r/e\right\rceil in place of ℱ \mathcal{F} .

Introduction

Given a family of
r
-uniform hypergraphs
\mathcal{F}
, an
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
is said to be
\mathcal{F}
-free
if
H
does not contain any
F\in\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph. The famous Turán problem studies the following two quantities.

•

The
Turán number

\ex(n,\mathcal{F})
, which is the maximum number of edges in an
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices.

•

The
Turán density

\pi(\mathcal{F})
, which is defined to be
\lim_{n\to\infty}\ex(n,\mathcal{F})/\binom{n}{r}
.

One can show using an averaging argument that
\ex(n,\mathcal{F})/\binom{n}{r}
is decreasing in
n
, which implies that the limit above always exists, so
\pi(\mathcal{F})
is well-defined.

In the case when
r=2
, or equivalently on graphs, the Turán problem is well-understood. For
\chi\geq 2
, define the
Turán graph

T(n,\chi)
to be the balanced complete
\chi
-partite graph on
n
vertices. The earliest and most basic result in this area is Mantel’s Theorem, which states that for the triangle graph
K_{3}
,
\pi(K_{3})=1/2
and
\ex(n,K_{3})=\left\lfloor n^{2}/4\right\rfloor
, with
T(n,2)
being the unique extremal graph. Turán’s Theorem generalises this to all cliques, asserting that
\pi(K_{\chi+1})=1-1/\chi
and
\ex(n,K_{\chi+1})=|E(T(n,\chi))|
, with
T(n,\chi)
being the unique extremal graph. For non-bipartite graphs, the Turán problem is solved asymptotically by the Erdős-Stone Theorem, which states that if
\chi(F)=\chi+1\geq 3
, then
\pi(F)=1-1/\chi
and
\ex(n,F)=\left(1-1/\chi+o(1)\right)\binom{n}{2}
, with
T(n,\chi)
being an asymptotic extremal graph. For a general bipartite graph
F
, the Erdős-Stone theorem shows that
\pi(F)=0
and
\ex(n,F)=o(n^{2})
, but the exact asymptotic behaviour of
\ex(n,F)
remains an interesting open problem.

In contrast, when
r\geq 3
, the hypergraph Turán problem is notoriously difficult with very few known results. As an example, the Turán density is still unknown even for
K_{4}^{(3)}
, the complete
3
-uniform hypergraph on 4 vertices. For a comprehensive overview on known results in hypergraph Turán problems, we refer the readers to Keevash’s survey
[
7
]
.

For every

and every family
\mathcal{F}
of

is
r
-partite, and

otherwise. Noting that

, by finding a family

of hypergraph analogues of the triangle that satisfies

. This is further motivated by a major open conjecture of Erdős that states that for every

, there exists

such that there is no family

of
r
-uniform hypergraphs with

4

that this conjecture is true if and only if there exists a finite family

of
r
-uniform hypergraphs with

but the blowup density of every

is larger than

. As such, a big obstacle towards Erdős’ conjecture is potentially the lack of characterisations, or even examples, of families satisfying

One attempt at generalising Mantel’s Theorem to hypergraphs uses the

2
The entropy method

In this section, we give an overview of the entropy method introduced by Chao and Yu in
[
1
]
to prove Theorem
1.1
. For more details and proofs, we refer the readers to
[
1
]
. We begin by defining (Shannon) entropy.

Definition 2.1

(Entropy)
.

Let
X,Y,X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be any discrete random variables.

•

The
support
of
X
is the set of values
x
satisfying
\mathbb{P}(X=x)>0
, and is denoted by
\supp(X)
.

•

The
(Shannon) entropy
of
X
is defined to be

\mathbb{H}(X)=-\sum_{x\in\supp(X)}\mathbb{P}(X=x)\log_{2}\mathbb{P}(X=x).

•

The entropy of the random tuple
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
is denoted by
\mathbb{H}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
.

•

The
conditional entropy
of
X
given
Y
is defined to be

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}(X\mid Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\supp(Y)}\mathbb{P}(Y=y)\mathbb{H}(X\mid Y=y)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{(x,y)\in\supp(X,Y)}\mathbb{P}(X=x,Y=y)\log_{2}\left(\frac{\mathbb{P}(X=x,Y=y)}{\mathbb{P}(Y=y)}\right).

The following proposition collects several useful properties of entropy.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X,Y,X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be any discrete random variables.

•

\mathbb{H}(X\mid Y)=\mathbb{H}(X,Y)-\mathbb{H}(Y)
.

•

(Chain rule)
\mathbb{H}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\mathbb{H}(X_{1})+\mathbb{H}(X_{2}\mid X_{1})+\dots+\mathbb{H}(X_{n}\mid X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1})
.

•

(Uniform bound)
\mathbb{H}(X)\leq\log_{2}\left\lvert\supp(X)\right\rvert
, with equality if and only if
X
is uniform.

•

(Subadditivity)
\mathbb{H}(X,Y)\leq\mathbb{H}(X)+\mathbb{H}(Y)
.

•

(Dropping condition)
\mathbb{H}(X\mid Y)\leq\mathbb{H}(X)
.

2.1
Mixture and the mixture bound

In this subsection, we define and study the
mixture
of random variables. We are particularly interested in mixtures of random variables whose supports do not overlap too much.

Definition 2.3

(Mixture)
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be discrete random variables. Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and let
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\geq 0
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=1
.

•

X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
have
(a+1)
-wise disjoint supports
if for any element
x\in\cup_{i=1}^{n}\supp(X_{i})
, there are at most
a
indices
i\in[n]
such that
x\in\supp(X_{i})
.

•

The
mixture
of
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
with
weight

w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
is the random variable
Z
obtained by first picking an independent random index
\mathbf{i}
with probability
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{i}=i)=w_{i}
for every
i\in[n]
, then setting
Z=X_{\mathbf{i}}
.

The following lemma from
[
1
]
shows that for given discrete random variables with
(a+1)
-wise disjoint supports, one of their mixtures has large entropy.

Lemma 2.4

(Mixture bound,
[
1
, Lemma 3.9]
)
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be discrete random variables with
(a+1)
-wise disjoint supports. Then there exists a mixture of
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
, say
Z
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}2^{\mathbb{H}(X_{i})}\leq a2^{\mathbb{H}(Z)}.

2.2
Blowup density and entropic density

In this subsection we define two related notions of density. We begin with the following well-known notions of homomorphism and blowup density, and two useful results in the study of hypergraph Turán problems.

Definition 2.5

(Homomorphism)
.

Let
G,H
be
r
-uniform hypergraphs and let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
r
-uniform hypergraphs.

•

A
homomorphism
from
G
to
H
is a map
f:V(G)\to V(H)
such that for every edge
e
in
G
, the image
f(e)
is also an edge in
H
.

•

H
is
\mathcal{F}
-hom-free
if there is no homomorphism from any
F\in\mathcal{F}
to
H
.

Definition 2.6

(Blowup density)
.

Let
H=(V,E)
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph.

•

The
Lagrangian
of
H
, denoted by
L(H)
, is defined by

L(H)=\max\left\{\sum_{e\in E}\prod_{v\in e}x_{v}\hskip 4.2679pt\middle|\hskip 4.2679ptx_{v}\geq 0\text{ for every }v\in V\text{ and }\sum_{v\in V}x_{v}=1\right\}.

•

The
blowup density
of
H
, denoted by
b(H)
, is defined to be
r!L(H)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
r
-uniform hypergraphs, then
\pi(\mathcal{F})=\sup\{b(H)\mid H\text{ is }\mathcal{F}\text{-hom-free}\}
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be two families of
r
-uniform hypergraphs. If for every
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, there exists
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that there exists a homomorphism from
F
to
F^{\prime}
, then
\pi(\mathcal{F})\leq\pi(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
.

To study the blowup density using entropy, Chao and Yu defined the following concept of entropic density in
[
1
]
. Here, a tuple
(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})
of random variables is
symmetric
if the distributions of
(X_{\sigma(1)},\dots,X_{\sigma(r)})
are the same for all permutations
\sigma
of
[r]
. In particular, the distributions of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
are all the same.

Definition 2.9

(Entropic density, ratio sequence)
.

Let
H
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph.

•

A tuple of random vertices
(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})\in V(H)^{r}
is a
random edge with uniform ordering
on
H
if
(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})
is symmetric and
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{r}\}
is always an edge of
H
.

•

The
entropic density
of
H
, denoted by
b_{\textup{entropy}}(H)
, is the maximum value of
2^{\mathbb{H}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})-r\mathbb{H}(X_{1})}
over all random edges
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
with uniform ordering on
H
.

•

The
ratio sequence
of a random edge
(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})
with uniform ordering is the sequence
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
given by
x_{i}=2^{\mathbb{H}(X_{i}\mid X_{i+1},\dots,X_{n})-\mathbb{H}(X_{i})}
for each
i\in[r]
.

Note that the maximum in the definition of
b_{\textup{entropy}}(H)
always exists, as the space of random edges with uniform ordering on
H
is compact. Also, it follows from Proposition
2.2
that the ratio sequence satisfies
0<x_{1}\leq\dots\leq x_{r}=1
and
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}=2^{\mathbb{H}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})-r\mathbb{H}(X_{1})}
.

In
[
1
]
, Chao and Yu proved the following results, which establishes the equivalences between the aforementioned densities.

Proposition 2.10

(
[
1

)
.

For every
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
,
b_{\textup{entropy}}(H)=b(H)
.

Corollary 2.11

(
[
1
, Corollary 5.6]
)
.

For every family
\mathcal{F}
of
r
-uniform hypergraphs,
\pi(\mathcal{F})
is the supremum of
2^{\mathbb{H}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})-r\mathbb{H}(X_{1})}

2.3
Partial hypergraphs

In this subsection, we recall Chao and Yu’s

Definition 2.12

(Partial hypergraphs)
.

•

A hypergraph

•

A

•

A

from a partial

such that for any edge

is injective on

•

The

-hypergarph

, denoted by
\widetilde{F}

-uniform hypergraph obtained as follows. Let

be the set of maximal edges in

to an edge of size

As an example, for
1\leq i\leq\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor

\Delta_{(r-i,i)}^{p}
to be the partial
r
-hypergraph with vertex set

whose set of maximal edges is (see Figure

\{\{1,2,\ldots,r\},\{1,2,\ldots,i,r+1\},\{i+1,i+2,\ldots,r+1\}\}.

Then, it is easy to verify that the extension of

is

.

The following result shows that for any partial

-hypergraph

-uniform hypergraph

, a homomorphism from

to

Proposition 2.13

(
[
1

)
.

Let
F
be a partial

-hypergraph, and let

be an

-uniform hypergraph. Then there is a homomorphism from

to

Motivated by a method of Szegedy
[

]
, Chao and Yu showed that one can sample random homomorphisms from some tree-like structures with high entropy. The relevant definitions and their result is as follows.

Definition 2.14

(Partial forest and forest sequence)
.

Let
F
be a partial
r
-hypergraph and let
<
be a linear order on
V(F)
.

•

For any vertex
v\in V(F)

be the set of edges in

is

.

•

F
is a

with respect to

if for every

, there is exactly one edge in

that is maximal in

. This edge is denoted by

.

•

The

of a partial forest
F
with respect to

is the sequence
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
where for each

Lemma 2.15

(
[
1
, Lemma 6.5]
)
.

Let
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
be a random edge with uniform ordering on an
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
, and let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
be its ratio sequence. For any partial forest
F
with respect to a linear order
<
, if
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
is its forest sequence, then one can sample a random homomorphism

from

with entropy equal to

|V(F)|\mathbb{H}(X_{1})+\log_{2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{f_{r+1-i}}\right).

Moreover, the random homomorphism can be sampled such that for any edge

, the distribution of

is the same as
(X_{i})_{r-\left\lvert e\right\rvert+1\leq i\leq r}
.

2.4
Reduction to an optimisation problem

In this subsection, we put everything together and adapt Chao and Yu’s entropy method to reduce the problem of determining
\pi(\mathcal{F}_{r,k})
to an optimisation problem using the following key lemma.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
1\leq k\leq\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor
, let
H
be an
\mathcal{F}_{r,k}
-hom-free
r
-uniform hypergraph, and let
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
be a random edge with uniform ordering on
H
whose ratio sequence is
0<x_{1}\leq\cdots\leq x_{r}=1
. Then,
x_{i}+x_{j}\leq x_{i+j}
for all
i\leq k
and
i+j\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r},w\}
and equip it with the linear order
v_{1}<\cdots<v_{r}<w
. Fixed any
i\leq k
and
i+j\leq r
. Let
F^{(1)}
be the partial
r
-hypergraph containing exactly two maximal edges
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}
and
\{v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{r},w\}
. Let
F^{(2)}
be the partial
r
-hypergraph exactly two maximal edges
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}
and
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r-j},w\}
. It is easy to check from definition that both
F^{(1)}
and
F^{(2)}
are partial forests with respect to
<
. Moreover, if
(f^{(1)}_{1},\ldots,f^{(1)}_{r})
is the forest sequence of
F^{(1)}
, then

contributes 1 to
f^{(1)}_{\ell}
for every

, and

contributes 1 to

. Thus,
f^{(1)}_{\ell}=1
for every
\ell\in[r]
except
f^{(1)}_{r+1-i}=2
. Similarly, if
(f^{(2)}_{1},\ldots,f^{(2)}_{r})
is the forest sequence of
F^{(2)}
, then
f^{(2)}_{\ell}=1
for every
\ell\in[r]
except
f^{(2)}_{r+1-j}=2
.

Let
(Y^{(1)}_{v})_{v\in V}
and
(Y^{(2)}_{v})_{v\in V}
be the random homomorphisms from
F^{(1)}
and
F^{(2)}
to
H
given by Lemma
2.15
, respectively.
Note that if some tuple
(y_{v})_{v\in V}
of vertices in
V(H)
is in the supports of both
(Y^{(1)}_{v})_{v\in V}
and
(Y^{(2)}_{v})_{v\in V}
, then
(y_{v})_{v\in V}
corresponds to a homomorphism from
F^{(1)}\cup F^{(2)}
to
H
.
However,
\Delta^{p}_{(r-i,i)}
is a subgraph of
F^{(1)}\cup F^{(2)}
, so there is a homomorphism from
\Delta^{p}_{(r-i,i)}
to
H
as well. Hence, by Proposition
2.13
, there is a homomorphism from
\Delta_{(r-i,i)}
to
H
, a contradiction. Therefore, the two random homomorphisms
(Y^{(1)}_{v})_{v\in V}
and
(Y^{(2)}_{v})_{v\in V}
have disjoint support.

This allows us to apply Lemma
2.4
with
a=1
to obtain a mixture
(Z_{v})_{v\in V}
of
(Y^{(1)}_{v})_{v\in V}
and
(Y^{(2)}_{v})_{v\in V}
such that, along with Lemma
2.15
, it satisfies

2^{\mathbb{H}((Z_{v})_{v\in V})}\geq 2^{\mathbb{H}(Y^{(1)}_{v})_{v\in V}}+2^{\mathbb{H}(Y^{(2)}_{v})_{v\in V}}\geq 2^{(r+1)\mathbb{H}(X_{1})}(x_{i}+x_{j})\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}x_{\ell}.

Moreover, observe that both
F^{(1)}
and
F^{(2)}
contain the edges
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}
and
\{v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{r-j},w\}
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.15
,
(Y^{(1)}_{v_{1}},\ldots,Y^{(1)}_{v_{r}})
and
(Y^{(2)}_{v_{1}},\ldots,Y^{(2)}_{v_{r}})
both have the same distributions as
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
, so
(Z_{v_{1}},\ldots,Z_{v_{r}})
has the same distribution as
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
as well by the definition of mixture. Similarly,
(Z_{w},Z_{v_{i+1}},\ldots,Z_{v_{r-j}})
has the same distribution as
(X_{i+j},\ldots,X_{r})
.
As a consequence, using Proposition
2.2
and the definition of ratio sequence, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}\left((Z_{v})_{v\in V}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{H}(Z_{v_{1}},\ldots,Z_{v_{r}})+\mathbb{H}(Z_{w}\mid Z_{v_{i+1}},\ldots,Z_{v_{r-j}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{H}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})+\mathbb{H}(X_{i+j}\mid X_{i+j+1},\ldots,X_{r})

\displaystyle=(r+1)\mathbb{H}(X_{1})+\log_{2}\left(x_{i+j}\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}x_{\ell}\right).

Combining the two inequalities above gives

2^{(r+1)\mathbb{H}(X_{1})}(x_{i}+x_{j})\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}x_{\ell}\leq 2^{(r+1)\mathbb{H}(X_{1})}x_{i+j}\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}x_{\ell},

from which it follows that
x_{i}+x_{j}\leq x_{i+j}
.
∎

With Lemma
2.16
in mind, for every
1\leq k\leq\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor
, define
\mathcal{X}_{r,k}\subset[0,1]^{r}
to be

\mathcal{X}_{r,k}=\left\{0<x_{1}\leq\cdots\leq x_{r}=1\mid x_{i}+x_{j}\leq x_{i+j}\text{ for every }i\in[k]\text{ and }i\leq j\leq r-i\right\}.

We can now reduce the Turán density problem to an optimisation problem over
\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
1\leq k\leq\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor
, and let
H
be an
\mathcal{F}_{r,k}
-hom-free
r
-uniform hypergraph, then

b(H)=b_{\textup{entropy}}(H)\leq\max\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}\hskip 4.2679pt\middle|\hskip 4.2679pt(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
be any random edge with uniform ordering on
H
and let
0<x_{1}\leq\cdots\leq x_{n}=1
be its ratio sequence. Recall that
b(H)=b_{\textup{entropy}}(H)
by Proposition
2.10
and
\mathbb{H}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})-r\mathbb{H}(X_{1})=\log_{2}(\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i})
by Proposition
2.2
. The statement then follows from the definition of entropic density as
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
by Lemma
2.16
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
by solving the optimisation problem obtained at the end of Section
2
. Specifically, we prove the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every
r\geq 4
, let

\max\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}\hskip 4.2679pt\middle|\hskip 4.2679pt(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}\right\}=\frac{r!}{r^{r}},

We first show that Theorem
3.1
implies Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

First, we have
\pi(\mathcal{F}_{r,k})\geq r!/r^{r}
by Lemma
2.7
as the
r
-uniform hypergraph consisting of just a single edge does not contain the homomorphic image of any

, and has blowup density
r!/r^{r}
. For the reverse inequality, if
H
is
\mathcal{F}_{r,k}
-hom-free, then by Proposition
2.17
and Theorem
3.1
, we have
b(H)=b_{\textup{entropy}}(H)\leq r!/r^{r}
.
Thus, by Lemma

,
\pi(\mathcal{F}_{n,k})=\sup\{b(H)\mid H\text{ is }\mathcal{F}_{r,k}\text{-hom-free}\}\leq r!/r^{r}

∎

The rest of the section is devoted to proving Theorem
3.1
. Observe that the maximum of
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
over
\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
can always be attained as
\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
is compact. Note that if
k=\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor
, then Theorem
3.1
follows from its counterpart Theorem 7.1 in
[
1
]
, so for the rest of this section we will assume
k=\left\lceil r/e\right\rceil\leq\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor-1
, unless stated otherwise. We begin with some preliminary results and definitions.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
maximizes
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
, then
x_{j}+x_{n-j}=1
for all
j\in[k]
.

Proof.

We use induction on
j
. For the base case
j=1
, let
x_{r-1}^{\prime}=1-x_{1}\geq x_{r-1}
and
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}
for all other
i\in[r]
. The only inequality we need to check to verify
(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{r}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
is
x_{1}^{\prime}+x_{r-1}^{\prime}\leq x_{r}^{\prime}=1
, but this holds from definition. Hence,
x_{r-1}^{\prime}=x_{r-1}=1-x_{1}
, as otherwise
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}<\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{\prime}
, a contradiction.

Now let
1<j\leq k
and assume
x_{i}+x_{r-i}=1
for all
i\in[j-1]
. Let
x_{r-j}^{\prime}=1-x_{j}\geq x_{r-j}
and
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}
for all other
i\in[r]
. The only inequalities we need to check to verify
(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{r}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
are
x_{i}^{\prime}+x_{r-j}^{\prime}\leq x_{r-j+i}^{\prime}
for all
i\in[j]
.
x_{j}^{\prime}+x_{r-j}^{\prime}\leq x_{r}^{\prime}=1
from definition. For
i\in[j-1]
,
j-i\in[j-1]
, so by induction hypothesis we have
x_{r-j+i}^{\prime}=x_{r-j+i}=1-x_{j-i}
, and thus
x_{i}^{\prime}+x_{r-j}^{\prime}=x_{i}+1-x_{j}\leq 1-x_{j-i}=x_{r-j+i}^{\prime}
, as required. Thus,
x_{r-j}^{\prime}=x_{r-j}=1-x_{j}
, as otherwise
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
is not maximized.
∎

For all integers
L\leq R
, call the set
\{L,L+1,\cdots,R\}
an
interval
, and denote it by
[L,R]
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
. Recall that
x_{r}=1
, let
x_{0}=0
. Let
0\leq L\leq R\leq r
.

•

[L,R]
is a
uniform interval
if
x_{i}=x_{L}+(i-L)x_{1}
for all
L\leq i\leq R
.

•

[L,R]
is a
segment
if it is a maximal uniform interval. In other words, if it is a uniform interval that is not properly contained in any other uniform interval.

•

The
length
of an interval
[L,R]
is
R-L+1
, the number of integers it contains.

•

The unique segment of the form
[0,I]
is called the
initial segment
.

•

A segment is
central
if it is contained in
[k+1,r-k-1]
. A segment is
left-crossing
if it contains
[k,k+1]
, is
right-crossing
if it contains
[r-k-1,r-k]
, and is
crossing
if it is left and/or right-crossing.

•

A segment is
super
if it has length
I+1
.

The following lemma shows that if
I\leq k-1
, then super segments are exactly the segments with maximum possible length.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
I\leq k-1
, then every segment has length at most
I+1
.

Proof.

Let
[L,R]
be a segment. If
[L,R]=[0,I]
this is trivial, so assume
L\geq I+1
. Suppose for a contradiction that
[L,R]
has length at least
I+2
, then
L+I+1\leq R
. Since
[0,I]
and
[L,R]
are both segments, we have
x_{I+1}>(I+1)x_{1}
and
x_{L+I+1}=x_{L}+(I+1)x_{1}
. But as
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
and
I+1\leq k
, we have
x_{L+I+1}\geq x_{L}+x_{I+1}>x_{L}+(I+1)x_{1}
, a contradiction.
∎

In the following, we collect several lemmas about segments.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
[L_{1},R_{1}],[L_{2},R_{2}]
be two segments with
L_{1}<L_{2}
. If
i\in[L_{1},R_{1}]
and
i+j\in[L_{2},R_{2}]
satisfy
L_{2}-L_{1}>j
and
\min\{i,j\}\leq k
, then
x_{i}+x_{j}<x_{i+j}
.

Proof.

From the definition of segments, we have
x_{i+j}=x_{L_{2}}+(i+j-L_{2})x_{1}
and
x_{i+j}>x_{L_{2}-1}+(i+j-L_{2}+1)x_{1}
. Similarly, using
i-(i+j-L_{2}+1)=L_{2}-j-1\geq L_{1}
, we have
x_{i}=x_{L_{2}-j-1}+(i+j-L_{2}+1)x_{1}
. Thus,
x_{i+j}-x_{i}>x_{L_{2}-1}-x_{L_{2}-j-1}\geq x_{(L_{2}-1)-(L_{2}-j-1)}=x_{j}
, as required. Note that in the last inequality we used that either
j\leq k
, or
L_{2}-j-1\leq i+j-j-1=i-1\leq k
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
[L_{1},R_{1}],[L_{2},R_{2}]
be two segments with
L_{1}<L_{2}
. If
i\in[L_{1},R_{1}]
and
i+j\in[L_{2},R_{2}]
satisfy
R_{2}-R_{1}>j
and
\min\{R_{1}+1,j\}\leq k
, then
x_{i}+x_{j}<x_{i+j}
.

Proof.

From the definition of segments, we have
x_{R_{1}}=x_{i}+(R_{1}-i)x_{1}
and
x_{R_{1}+1}>x_{i}+(R_{1}-i+1)x_{1}
. Similarly, using
R_{2}\geq(i+j)+(R_{1}-i+1)=j+R_{1}+1
, we have
x_{j+R_{1}+1}=x_{i+j}+(R_{1}-i+1)x_{1}
. Thus,
x_{i+j}-x_{i}>x_{j+R_{1}+1}-x_{R_{1}+1}\geq x_{j}
, as required. Note that in the last inequality we used that either
j\leq k
, or
R_{1}+1\leq k
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
I\leq k-1
. Let
0\leq L\leq R\leq r
.

•

If
R\leq k-1
, then
[L,R]
is a segment if and only if
[r-R,r-L]
is. If
R\leq k
, then
[L,R]
is a super segment if and only if
[r-R,r-L]
is.

•

If
L\leq k
and
R\geq r-k
, or in other words if
[L,R]
is both a left and a right-crossing segment, then
L=r-R
.

•

If
[L,R]
is left but not right-crossing, then
r-L
is the right endpoint of a segment that contains
r-k
. If
[L,R]
is right but not left-crossing, then
r-R
is the left endpoint of a segment that contains
k
.

Proof.

Trivially,
x_{0}=1-x_{r}
. By Lemma
3.2
,
x_{i}=1-x_{r-i}
for all
i\in[k]
. We will refer to these properties as symmetry.

If
R\leq k
, then by symmetry,
[L,R]
is uniform if and only if
[r-R,r-L]
is uniform. Moreover, if
R\leq k-1
, then
[L,R]
is maximally uniform if and only if
[r-R,r-L]
is, so
[L,R]
is a (super) segment if and only if
[r-R,r-L]
is. If
R=k
and
[L,R]
is a super segment, then
[r-R,r-L]
is uniform and has the same length as
[L,R]
, so it is also a super segment. The reverse implication is similar.

Now suppose
[L,R]
is both a left and a right-crossing segment. If
L<r-R
, then in particular
[L,k]
is uniform, so
[r-k,r-L]
is also uniform by symmetry. But as
r-L>R\geq r-k
, this means that
[L,r-L]
is a longer uniform interval than
[L,R]
, a contradiction. Similarly, we reach a contradiction if
L>r-R
, so
L=r-R
.

If
[L,R]
is left but not right-crossing, then
L\leq k
and
[L,k]
is uniform, so
[r-k,r-L]
is also uniform by symmetry. If
L=0
, then
r-L=r
is trivially the right endpoint of the segment containing
[r-k,r-L]
. If
L>0
, then maximality of
[L,R]
implies that
x_{L-1}<x_{L}-x_{1}
, so symmetry implies that
x_{r-L+1}>x_{r-L}+x_{1}
, and thus
r-L
is the right endpoint of the segment containing
[r-k,r-L]
. The case when
[L,R]
is right but not left-crossing is similar.
∎

We now prove a key technical lemma that shows that the initial segment of any maximiser must have length at least
k+1
. This is done by proving that if a maximiser has a shorter initial segment, then there is a small perturbation of it that remains inside
\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
but has a larger product.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
maximizes
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
, then
I\geq k
.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
I\leq k-1
. By Lemma
3.2
,
x_{i}=1-x_{r-i}
for all
i\in[k]
. Set
x_{0}=0=1-x_{r}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant to be chosen later. We define a new sequence
(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{r}^{\prime})
by modifying each
x_{i}
by at most
\varepsilon
as follows. Recall that all super segments have length
I+1\geq 2
.

•

x^{\prime}_{I}=x_{I}+\varepsilon
and
x^{\prime}_{r-I}=x^{\prime}_{r-I}-\varepsilon
.

•

For every super segment
[L,R]\not=[0,I],[r-I,r]
that is not central, set
x^{\prime}_{L}=x_{L}-\varepsilon
and
x^{\prime}_{R}=x_{R}+\varepsilon
.

•

If
[L,R]
is a left but not right-crossing super segment, additionally set
x^{\prime}_{r-L}=x_{r-L}+\varepsilon
.

•

If
[L,R]
is a right but not left-crossing super segment, additionally set
x^{\prime}_{r-R}=x_{r-R}-\varepsilon
.

•

For every central super segment
[L,R]
, set
x^{\prime}_{R}=x_{R}+\varepsilon
.

•

For all other variables
x_{i}
, set
x^{\prime}_{i}=x_{i}
.

Note that from Lemma
3.7
and the definition above, if
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}+\varepsilon
, then
i
is the right endpoint of a segment, and if
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}-\varepsilon
, then
i
is the left endpoint of a segment. We now show that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,
(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{r}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
and
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{\prime}>\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
, which contradicts the maximality of
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
and finishes the proof.

We first show that
(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{r}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
. Observe from Lemma
3.7
and the definition above that
x_{i}^{\prime}+x_{r-i}^{\prime}=1\leq x_{r}^{\prime}
for all
i\in[k]
. For every
i\in[k]
and
i\leq j\leq r-1-i
, since
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
, we have
x_{i}+x_{j}\leq x_{i+j}
. Call such a triple of indices
(i,j,i+j)

tight
if
x_{i}+x_{j}=x_{i+j}
. Note that if
x_{i},x_{j},x_{i+j}
is not tight, then for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
we have
x_{i}^{\prime}+x_{j}^{\prime}\leq x_{i+j}^{\prime}
as well. Therefore, we only need to check the desired inequalities hold on triples that are tight. In the case analysis that follows, we assume
i\in[k]
,
i\leq j\leq r-1-i
and
(i,j,i+j)
is tight. For every
*\in[r]
, we denote the left and right endpoints of the unique segment containing
*
by
L_{*}
and
R_{*}
.

Case 1.

x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}+\varepsilon
.

From definition, since
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}+\varepsilon
and
i\leq k
,
[L_{i},i]
must be a super segment. Then,
(i+j)-L_{i+j}\leq i-L_{i}
, so
L_{i+j}-L_{i}\geq j
. If
L_{i+j}-L_{i}>j
, then the triple
(i,j,i+j)
is not tight by Lemma
3.5
, a contradiction. If
L_{i+j}-L_{i}=j
, then
[L_{i+j},i+j]
is also a super segment because it has the same length as
[L_{i},i]
, so
x^{\prime}_{i+j}=x_{i+j}+\varepsilon
.

If
x_{j}^{\prime}\leq x_{j}
, then
x_{i}^{\prime}+x_{j}^{\prime}\leq x_{i+j}^{\prime}
as required. Otherwise,
x_{j}^{\prime}=x_{j}+\varepsilon
, so
j
is the right endpoint of a segment. Since every super segment has length at least 2, it follows that
x_{i+j}\geq x_{j+1}+x_{i-1}>(x_{j}+x_{1})+(x_{i}-x_{1})=x_{i}+x_{j}
, so
(i,j,i+j)
is not tight, a contradiction. This finishes Case 1.

Case 2.

x_{i}^{\prime}\leq x_{i}
and
x_{j}^{\prime}=x_{j}+\varepsilon
.

If
x_{i+j}^{\prime}=x_{i+j}+\varepsilon
, then
x_{i+j}^{\prime}\geq x_{i}^{\prime}+x_{j}^{\prime}
and we are done, so assume otherwise. From definition, there are two ways we can have
x_{j}^{\prime}=x_{j}+\varepsilon
. If
[L_{j},j]
is a super segment, we proceed as in Case 1 above to reach a contradiction.

The additional possibility is that
r-j
is the left endpoint of a super segment
[r-j,R]
that is left but not right-crossing. Note that this is not possible in Case 1 as
i\leq k
. Then,
j
is the right endpoint of a segment by Lemma
3.7
, so
L_{i+j}\geq j+1
. Since
x_{i+j}^{\prime}\leq x_{i+j}
,
i+j
is not the right endpoint of a super segment. Let
d=i+j-L_{i+j}<R-r+j
, so
x_{r+i-L_{i+j}+1}=x_{r-j+(d+1)}=x_{r-j}+(d+1)x_{1}
and
x_{L_{i+j}-1}<x_{i+j}-(d+1)x_{1}
. Note that
i+j>L_{i+j}-1\geq j\geq r-k
, so it follows that
x_{r-L_{i+j}+1}=1-x_{L_{i+j}-1}>1-x_{i+j}+(d+1)x_{1}=x_{r-i-j}+(d+1)x_{1}
. Therefore,

x_{i}+x_{r-i-j}<x_{i}+x_{r-L_{i+j}+1}-(d+1)x_{1}=x_{i}+x_{r-L_{i+j}+1}+x_{r-j}-x_{r+i-L_{i+j}+1}\leq x_{r-j}
.
It follows that
x_{i}+x_{j}<x_{i+j}
, so
(i,j,i+j)
is not tight, a contradiction. This finishes Case 2.

Case 3.

x^{\prime}_{i+j}=x_{i+j}-\varepsilon
,
x_{i}^{\prime}\leq x_{i}
and
x_{j}^{\prime}\leq x_{j}
.

If either
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}-\varepsilon
or
x_{j}^{\prime}=x_{j}-\varepsilon
, then
x_{i}^{\prime}+x_{j}^{\prime}\leq x_{i+j}^{\prime}
, as required, so we assume both
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}
and
x_{j}^{\prime}=x_{j}
. Recall that
x_{i+j}^{\prime}=x_{i+j}-\varepsilon
implies that
i+j
is the left endpoint of a segment, so
x_{i+j-1}<x_{i+j}-x_{1}
. If
L_{i}<i
, then
x_{i-1}=x_{i}-x_{1}
, so
x_{i}+x_{j}=x_{i-1}+x_{1}+x_{j}\leq x_{i+j-1}+x_{1}<x_{i+j}
, and thus
(i,j,i+j)
is not tight, a contradiction. Similarly,
L_{j}<j
implies
(i,j,i+j)
is not tight. Therefore, we may assume
L_{i}=i
and
L_{j}=j
from now on. From definition, we can have
x^{\prime}_{i+j}=x_{i+j}-\varepsilon
in the following four ways.

If
i+j
is the left endpoint of a super segment and
i+j\leq k
, then we have
R_{i+j}-i-j\geq R_{i}-i
. If
R_{i+j}-i-j>R_{i}-i
, then
R_{i+j}-R_{i}>j
, so by Lemma
3.6
and using
R_{i}<i+j\leq k
,
(i,j,i+j)
is not tight, a contradiction. If
R_{i+j}-i-j=R_{i}-i
, then
[i,R_{i}]
is also a super segment. Since
i\in[k]
,
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}-\varepsilon
, a contradiction.

If
i+j
is the left endpoint of a super segment with
i+j\geq r-k
, then by Lemma
3.7
,
[r-R_{i+j},r-i-j]
is a super segment, and
r-i
is the right endpoint of the segment
[L_{r-i},r-i]
. Since
[i+j,R_{i+j}]
is a super segment,
R_{i+j}-i-j\geq r-i-L_{r-i}
. If
R_{i+j}-i-j=r-i-L_{r-i}
, then
[L_{r-i},r-i]
is a super segment with
r-i\geq r-k
, so
x_{i}^{\prime}=1-x_{r-i}^{\prime}=1-(x_{r-i}+\varepsilon)=x_{i}-\varepsilon
from definition, a contradiction. If
R_{i+j}-i-j>r-i-L_{r-i}
, then
L_{r-i}-(r-R_{i+j})>j
, so we can apply Lemma
3.5
to conclude that
(r-i-j,j,r-i)
is not tight. Hence,
(i,j,i+j)
is not tight, a contradiction.

If
i+j
is the left endpoint of a super segment that is right but not left-crossing, then we have
R_{i+j}-i-j\geq R_{i}-i
and
R_{i+j}\geq r-k
. If
R_{i}\geq k
, then
i=r-R_{i+j}
by Lemma
3.7
, so
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}-\varepsilon
from definition, a contradiction. As such, we can assume
R_{i}\leq k-1
. If
R_{i+j}-i-j>R_{i}-i
, then we can apply Lemma
3.6
to conclude that
(i,j,i+j)
is not tight, a contradiction. If
R_{i+j}-i-j=R_{i}-i
, then
[i,R_{i}]
is also a super segment, so
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}-\varepsilon
as
i\in[k]
, a contradiction.

The last possibility is
i+j=r-R
, where
[L,R]
is a super segment that is right but not left-crossing. Then
R_{i}-i\leq R-L
, and by Lemma
3.7
,
i+j
is the left endpoint of a segment, so
R_{i}<i+j\leq k
. If
R_{i}-i=R-L
, then
[i,R_{i}]
is a super segment, so
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}-\varepsilon
, a contradiction. Otherwise, let
d=R_{i}-i<R-L
, then
x_{R-d-1}=x_{R}-(d+1)x_{1}
and
x_{R_{i}+1}=x_{i+d+1}>x_{i}+(d+1)x_{1}
. It follows that
x_{r-R_{i}-1}<x_{r-i}-(d+1)x_{1}
as
R_{i}+1\leq k
, and
x_{j}+x_{R-d-1}\leq x_{R-d+j-1}=x_{r-R_{i}-1}
as
j\leq k
. Thus,
x_{r-i-j}=x_{R}=x_{R-d-1}+(d+1)x_{1}\leq x_{r-R_{i}-1}-x_{j}+(d+1)x_{1}<x_{r-i}-x_{j}
. Therefore,
x_{i}+x_{j}<x_{i+j}
and
(i,j,i+j)
is not tight, a contradiction. This finishes Case 3.

If none of Case 1, Case 2 and Case 3 holds, then
x_{i}^{\prime}\leq x_{i}
,
x_{j}^{\prime}\leq x_{j}
and
x_{i+j}^{\prime}\geq x_{i+j}
, so
x_{i}^{\prime}+x_{j}^{\prime}\leq x_{i+j}^{\prime}
. This proves
(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{r}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
.

Finally, we show that
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{\prime}>\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
. Note that
x_{i}=1-x_{r-i}
and
x_{i}^{\prime}=1-x_{r-i}^{\prime}
for all
i\in[k]
, and
x_{i}^{\prime}\geq x_{i}
for all
k+1\leq i\leq r-k-1
. Let
J=\{i\in[k]\mid x_{i}^{\prime}\not=x_{i}\}
. Note that if we arrange indices
i\in J
in increasing order, they alternate between
x_{i}^{\prime}>x_{i}
and
x_{i}^{\prime}<x_{i}
, starting with
x_{I}^{\prime}>x_{I}
. Pair up the consecutive indices in
J
starting with
I
, with possibly
I^{\prime}=\max J
being left over if
|J|
is odd, and let
P
be the set of pairs obtained this way. By Lemma
3.9
below, if
P\not=\emptyset
, then

\prod_{(i,j)\in P}(x_{i}+\varepsilon)(x_{r-i}-\varepsilon)(x_{j}-\varepsilon)(x_{r-j}+\varepsilon)>\prod_{(i,j)\in P}x_{i}x_{r-i}x_{j}x_{r-j}

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. Similarly, if
|J|
is odd, then
(x_{I^{\prime}}+\varepsilon)(1-x_{I^{\prime}}-\varepsilon)>x_{I^{\prime}}(1-x_{I^{\prime}})
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. Since
P=\emptyset
implies that
|J|=1
is odd, it follows that we always have
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{\prime}>\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
, finishing the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

For every
0<a<b<1/2
and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,

(a+\varepsilon)(b-\varepsilon)(1-a-\varepsilon)(1-b+\varepsilon)>ab(1-a)(1-b).

Proof.

Let
f(t)
be the polynomial
(a+t)(b-t)(1-a-t)(1-b+t)-ab(1-a)(1-b)
. Then
f(0)=0
, and the linear term of
f(t)
, which is equal to
f^{\prime}(0)
, is

b(1-a)(1-b)-a(1-a)(1-b)-ab(1-b)+ab(1-a)=(b-a)((1-a)(1-b)+ab)>0.

Therefore, for every sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,
f(\varepsilon)>0
, as required.
∎

We can now prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Recall that we may assume
k=\left\lceil r/e\right\rceil\leq\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor-1
, as the case when
k=\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor
is proved by Theorem 7.1 in
[
1
]
. It is easy to check that if
x_{i}=i/r
for every
i\in[r]
, then
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
and
\prod_{i=1}^{r}=r!/r^{r}
, so the maximum is at least
r!/r^{r}
.

Now suppose that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
maximises
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
, by Lemma
3.8
, we may assume that
I\geq k
. Since
x_{1}+x_{i}\leq x_{i+1}
for all
i\in[r-1]
, we have
rx_{1}\leq x_{r}=1
and thus
x_{1}\leq 1/r
. Let
x_{1}=(1-\varepsilon)/r
for some
\varepsilon\geq 0
. It follows from
I\geq k
that
[1,k]
is uniform, so
x_{i}=ix_{1}=i(1-\varepsilon)/r
for all
i\in[k]
. Moreover, for every
k+1\leq i\leq r
, we have
(r-i)x_{1}+x_{i}\leq x_{r}=1
, so
x_{i}\leq 1-(r-i)x_{1}=(i+(r-i)\varepsilon)/r
.

To prove
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}\leq r!/r^{r}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{i}{r}
, it suffices to show

\log\left(\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{i}{r}}\right)\leq\log\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varepsilon)\prod_{i=k+1}^{r}\left(1+\frac{(r-i)\varepsilon}{i}\right)\right)\leq 0.

Indeed, using
\log(1+t)\leq t
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\log\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varepsilon)\prod_{i=k+1}^{r}\left(1+\frac{(r-i)\varepsilon}{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log(1-\varepsilon)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{r}\log\left(1+\frac{(r-i)\varepsilon}{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-k\varepsilon+\sum_{i=k+1}^{r}\frac{(r-i)\varepsilon}{i}\leq\varepsilon\left(r\int_{\frac{k}{r}}^{1}\frac{1-t}{t}\text{d}t-k\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\left(r\left(-1-\log\left(\frac{k}{r}\right)+\frac{k}{r}\right)-k\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon r(\log r-\log k-1)\leq 0,

where the last inequality follows from
r\leq ke
. This proves the upper bound and shows the maximum is exactly
r!/r^{r}
. In fact, we have
r<ke
since
e
is irrational, so the last inequality is above is strict unless
\varepsilon=0
. In that case, from the proof above we have
x_{i}\leq i/r
for all
i\in[r]
, so the maximum value
r!/r^{r}
is achieved if and only if
x_{i}=i/r
for all
i\in[r]
.
∎

Finally, we show that the choice of
\left\lceil r/e\right\rceil
in Theorem
3.1
is essentially best possible, so the choice of
\left\lceil r/e\right\rceil
in Theorem
1.3
cannot be improved using this entropy method.

Lemma 3.10
.

For every
r\geq 2
, if
1\leq k<\left\lfloor r/e\right\rfloor
, then
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}>r!/r^{r}
for some
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be sufficiently small. Define
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
by

x_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{i}{r}-\frac{i\varepsilon}{r},&\text{if }i\in[k],\\
\frac{i}{r}+\frac{(r-i)\varepsilon}{r},&\text{if }k+1\leq i\leq r.\end{cases}

It is clear that for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, we have
0<x_{1}\leq\cdots\leq x_{r}=1
. For all
1\leq i<j\leq k
,
x_{i}+x_{j}=(1-\varepsilon)(i+j)/r\leq x_{i+j}
. For all
1\leq i\leq k<j\leq r
that satisfy
i+j\leq r
, we have
x_{i}+x_{j}=(i+j+(r-i-j)\varepsilon)/r=x_{i+j}
. Hence,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
.

To prove
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}>r!/r^{r}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{i}{r}
, it suffices to show

\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{i}{r}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varepsilon)\prod_{i=k+1}^{r}\left(1+\frac{(r-i)\varepsilon}{i}\right)>1.

Define

f(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-t)\prod_{i=k+1}^{r}\left(1+\frac{(r-i)t}{i}\right).

Then
f(t)
is a polynomial in
t
with
f(0)=1
. The linear term of
f(t)
, which is equal to
f^{\prime}(0)
, is

\displaystyle-k+\sum_{i=k+1}^{r}\frac{(r-i)}{i}

\displaystyle=-k+r\sum_{i=k+1}^{r-1}\frac{1-\frac{i}{r}}{\frac{i}{r}}\cdot\frac{1}{r}\geq-k+r\int_{\frac{k+1}{r}}^{1}\frac{1-t}{t}\text{d}t

\displaystyle=-k+r\left(-1-\log\left(\frac{k+1}{r}\right)+\frac{k+1}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=1+r(\log r-\log(k+1)-1)>0,

as
r\geq(k+1)e
. Hence, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,
f(\varepsilon)>f(0)=1
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
. This is done by combining Theorem
1.3
and the machinery developed in
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
. We proceed similarly as in
[
8
]
where Liu obtained Theorem
1.2
from Theorem
1.1
. We begin by introducing the necessary concepts.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
H
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
r
-uniform hypergraphs. Let
\mathfrak{H}
be a family of
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraphs.

•

H
is
2-covered
if every pair of vertices is contained in at least one edge of
H
.

•

H
is
symmetrised
if
H
is the blowup of a 2-covered
r
-uniform hypergraph.

•

\mathcal{F}
is
blowup-invariant
if every blowup of an
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph
F
is also
\mathcal{F}
-free.

•

\mathcal{F}
is
hereditary
if for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
, every subgraph
F^{\prime}
of
F
is in
\mathcal{F}
as well.

•

\mathcal{F}
is
symmetrised-stable
with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
if every symmetrised
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph is contained in
\mathfrak{H}
.

Now assume additionally that
\pi(\mathcal{F})>0
.

•

\mathcal{F}
is
edge-stable
with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
if for every
\delta>0
there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
N
such that every
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
on
n\geq N
vertices with
|E(H)|\geq\left(\pi(\mathcal{F})/r!-\varepsilon\right)n^{r}
can be turned into a member in
\mathfrak{H}
by removing at most
\delta n^{r}
edges.

•

\mathcal{F}
is
degree-stable
with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
if there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
N
such that every
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
on
n\geq N
vertices with
\delta(H)\geq\left(\pi(\mathcal{F})/(r-1)!-\varepsilon\right)n^{r-1}
is in
\mathfrak{H}
.

•

\mathcal{F}
is
vertex-extendable
with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
if there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
N
such that for every
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
on
n\geq N
vertices with
\delta(H)\geq\left(\pi(\mathcal{F})/(r-1)!-\varepsilon\right)n^{r-1}
, if
H-v
is in
\mathfrak{H}
, then
H
is in
\mathfrak{H}
as well.

These notions were introduced in
[
9
]
to provide a unified framework for proving the degree-stability of certain classes of hypergraph families. This method was later refined in
[
6
]
. Their main stability results are the following.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.7]
and
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a family of
r
-uniform hypergraphs with
\pi(\mathcal{F})>0
, and
\mathfrak{H}
is a hereditary family of
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraphs.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
\mathcal{F}
is blowup-invariant, and both symmetrised-stable and vertex-extendable with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
, then
\mathcal{F}
is degree-stable with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{F}
is both edge-stable and vertex-extendable with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
, then
\mathcal{F}
is degree-stable with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
.

Since
\mathcal{F}_{r,k}
is not blowup-invariant, for part of the proof we need to consider the following broader family of triangle-like
r
-uniform hypergraphs. Denote by
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
the collection of
r
-uniform hypergraphs consisting of three edges
A,B,C
such that

\displaystyle A\subset B\cup C,\quad\text{and}\quad(B\cap C)\setminus A\neq\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad|A\cap B|\geq r-k.

Note that indeed
\mathcal{F}_{r,k}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
, so
\pi(\mathcal{T}_{r,k})\leq\pi(\mathcal{F}_{r,k})=r!/r^{r}
by Theorem
1.3
. But
\pi(\mathcal{T}_{r,k})\geq r!/r^{r}
because it is at least the blowup density of a single edge, so
\pi(\mathcal{T}_{r,k})=r!/r^{r}
. Moreover, one can verify that
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
is blowup-invariant. To prove Theorem
1.4
we need following three propositions.

Proposition 4.3
.

For every
r\geq 4
, let
k=\left\lceil r/e\right\rceil
, then there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
N
such that the following holds for all
n\geq N
. Suppose that
H
is the subgraph of a symmetrised
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices with
\delta(H)\geq n^{r-1}/r^{r-1}-\varepsilon n^{r-1}
. Then
H
is
r
-partite.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, if
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}_{r,k}
maximizes
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
, then
x_{i}=i/r
for
1\leq i\leq r
. We can then prove an analogous version of Proposition 3.1 in
[
8
]
, with
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
replacing the
\mathcal{T}_{r}
there and using the same proof. Then we proceed as in Proposition 5.1 in
[
8
]
to prove an analogous version again with
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
replacing
\mathcal{T}_{r}
, which gives exactly Proposition
4.3
.
∎

Proposition 4.4

(
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

For every
r\geq 2
,
\Delta_{(r-1,1)}
is vertex-extendable with respect to
\mathfrak{K}_{r}^{r}
, the family of all
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraphs.

Proposition 4.5
.

For every
r\geq 4
, let
k=\left\lceil r/e\right\rceil
, then
\mathcal{F}_{r,k}
is degree-stable with respect to
\mathfrak{K}_{r}^{r}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{S}
denote the collection of all
2
-covered
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraphs. Let
\mathfrak{H}
be the collection of every
r
-uniform hypergraph

that is the subgraph of a blowup of some
G\in\mathfrak{S}
. In particular,
\mathfrak{H}
contains every blowup of every
G\in\mathfrak{S}
, as well as the collection
\mathfrak{K}_{r}^{r}
of all
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraphs.

We first prove that
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
is degree-stable with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
. It follows from the definition that
\mathfrak{H}
is hereditary and
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
is symmetrised-stable with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
. Also,
\pi(\mathcal{T}_{r,k})=r!/r^{r}>0
and
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
is blowup-invariant, so by Theorem
4.2

(i)
, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
is vertex-extendable with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a sufficiently small constant and let

be a sufficiently large integer. Let
H
be a
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices with

. Suppose that

is a vertex such that

. Then, as

we have that

. By Proposition

\Delta_{(r-1,1)}\in\mathcal{T}_{r,k}

, so

is as well. Therefore, by definition,

, which proves that

is vertex-extendable with respect to

, and thus degree-stable with respect to

by Theorem

(i)
.

By Proposition
4.3
again,
\mathcal{T}_{r,k}
being degree-stable with respect to

implies that it is also degree-stable with respect to

. This then implies that

). Since for every

is edge-stable with respect to

and therefore

.

Now we can bring everything together and prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For simplicity, let
|E(T^{r}(n))|=e_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. By Proposition
4.5
, there exists
\varepsilon,N
such that every
\mathcal{F}_{r,k}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
on
n\geq N
vertices with
\delta(H)\geq n^{r-1}/r^{r-1}-\varepsilon n^{r-1}
is
r
-partite.

Now let
N_{0}\gg N
satisfy
\ex(n,\mathcal{F}_{r,k})\leq(1+\varepsilon)n^{r}/r^{r}
for all

, and let
H
be an
\mathcal{F}_{r,k}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph on
n\geq 2N_{0}
vertices with
|E(H)|\geq|E(T^{r}(n))|
. Define a sequence of subgraphs

with
|V(H_{i})|=n_{i}=n-i
for all

by repeatedly deleting a single vertex (as long as it exists) from

with degree at most

to obtain

. This process must terminate after at most

steps as otherwise for

,

and

|E(H_{i})|\geq e_{n_{i}}+i\varepsilon n_{i}^{r-1}/2\geq n_{i}^{r}/r^{r}+\varepsilon n_{i}^{r}/20>(1+\varepsilon)n_{i}^{r}/r^{r},

\ex(n_{i},\mathcal{F}_{r,k})\leq(1+\varepsilon)n_{i}^{r}/r^{r}
. Suppose now that this process terminated after

, then
H_{t}
satisfies
|E(H_{t})|\geq e_{n_{t}}+t\varepsilon n_{t}^{r-1}/2
,
n_{t}\geq N
and

\delta(H_{t})\geq e_{n_{t}}-e_{n_{t}-1}-\varepsilon n_{t}^{r-1}/2\geq\left\lfloor n_{t}/r\right\rfloor^{r-1}-\varepsilon n_{t}^{r-1}/2\geq n_{t}^{r-1}/r^{r-1}-\varepsilon n_{t}^{r-1}.

Thus,
H_{t}
is
r
-partite from degree-stability, so
|E(H_{t})|\leq|E(T^{r}(n_{t}))|=e_{n_{t}}
, which gives a contradiction unless
t=0
. In that case
H_{0}=H
is
r
-partite and so
|E(H)|\leq|E(T^{r}(n))|
, with equality if and only if
H=T^{r}(n)
.
∎

5
Concluding Remarks

We begin by posing two follow up questions. By Lemma
3.10
, it is not possible to use this entropy method to show
\pi(\mathcal{F}_{r,k})=r!/r^{r}
for
k<\left\lfloor r/e\right\rfloor
. For
k=\left\lfloor r/e\right\rfloor
, careful examination of our proof shows that whether this method works depends on how close
r
is to an integer multiple of
e
. The natural question then is whether
\pi(\mathcal{F}_{r,k})>r!/r^{r}
if
k<\left\lfloor r/e\right\rfloor
, so that our result obtained from the entropy method is essentially tight.

Question 5.1
.

Is
\pi(\mathcal{F}_{r,k})>r!/r^{r}
if
k<\left\lfloor r/e\right\rfloor
? If not, what is the largest
k
such that this holds?

Also, in this paper we considered only subfamilies of
\mathcal{F}_{r,\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor}
of the form
\mathcal{F}_{r,k}
, but one could consider more general subfamilies of
\mathcal{F}_{r,\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor}
. Therefore, it would interesting to know the following.

Question 5.2
.

What are the subfamilies
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{F}_{r,\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor}
that satisfy
\pi(\mathcal{F})=r!/r^{r}
?

Finally, we mention two related problems, for which our results have some implications.

5.1
Generalised tents

More generally, for any partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\vdash r
with
r>\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{m}
, the
\lambda
-tent

\Delta_{\lambda}
is defined to be the unique
r
-uniform hypergraph up to isomorphism with
m+1
edges
e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
, such that

•

|e_{0}\cap e_{i}|=\lambda_{i}
for every
i\in[m]
,

•

e_{0}\cap e_{1},\ldots,e_{0}\cap e_{m}
partition
e_{0}
,

•

there is a vertex
v
such that
e_{i}\cap e_{j}=\{v\}
for every
1\leq i<j\leq m
.

Note that when
m=2
, this coincides with our earlier definition of the
(r-i,i)
-tent
\Delta_{(r-i,i)}
.

In
[
10
]
, Mubayi and Pikhurko showed that
\pi(\Delta_{(1,1,\ldots,1)})=r!/r^{r}
, and for large
n
,
\ex(n,\Delta_{(1,1,\ldots,1)})=|E(T^{r}(n))|
, with the
T^{r}(n)
being the unique extremal graph. Since there is a homomorphism from
\Delta_{(1,1,\ldots,1)}
to
F
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}_{r,\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor}
, we have
\pi(\Delta_{(1,1,\ldots,1)})\leq\pi(\mathcal{F}_{r,\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor})
by Corollary
2.8
, so Theorem
1.1
is stronger than Mubayi and Pikhurko’s result. Moreover, as there is a homomorphism from
\Delta_{(\left\lceil r/2\right\rceil,1,\ldots,1)}
to
F
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}_{r,\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor}
, Theorem
1.1
also implies that
\pi(\Delta_{(\left\lceil r/2\right\rceil,1,\ldots,1)})=r!/r^{r}
. Using the same argument, we obtain the following improvement as a corollary to Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 5.3
.

For every
r\geq 4
, let
k=\left\lceil r/e\right\rceil
, then
\pi(\Delta_{(r-k,1,\ldots,1)})=r!/r^{r}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
2.8
, using the observation that there is a homomorphism from
\Delta_{(r-k,1,\ldots,1)}
to
F
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}_{r,k}
.
∎

5.2
L
-intersecting hypergraphs

Let
L\subset\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}
. An
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
is
L
-intersecting
if for any distinct
e,e^{\prime}\in E(H)
,
|e\cap e^{\prime}|\in L
. Determining the maximum blowup density of an
L
-intersecting
r
-uniform hypergraph is an interesting open problem, and is related to Turán number of family of tents because of the following simple lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of tents satisfying that
\lambda\vdash r
and
r>\lambda_{1}\geq k
for all
\Delta_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{F}
. Let
L\subset\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}
. Then for every
L
-intersecting
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
,
|E(H)|\leq\ex(|H|,\mathcal{F})
and
b(H)\leq\pi(\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

Since
H
is
L
-intersecting and every
\Delta_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{F}
contains two edges whose intersection has size at least
k
,
H
is
\mathcal{F}
-free, so
|E(H)|\leq\ex(|H|,\mathcal{F})
.

Now let
\Delta_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{F}
be a tent with edges
e_{0},\ldots,e_{m}
and
\cap_{i=1}^{m}e_{i}=\{v\}
as in the definition, and suppose there is a homomorphism
f:\Delta_{\lambda}\to H
. Then,
|e_{0}\cap e_{1}|=\lambda_{1}\geq k
and
H
is
L
-intersecting implies that
f(e_{0})=f(e_{1})
, so in particular,
f(v)=f(u)
for some
u\in e_{0}
. But both
u
and
v
are in some edge
e_{i}
for some
i\in[m]
, so
f
is not injective on
e_{i}
, a contradiction. Hence,
H
is
\mathcal{F}
-hom-free, and the result follows from Lemma
2.7
.
∎

Using this argument, Liu
[
8
]
observed that Theorem
1.1
implies that if
L\subset\{0,1,\ldots,\left\lceil r/2\right\rceil-1\}
and
H
is
r
-uniform and
L
-intersecting, then
b(H)\leq r!/r^{r}
. We can improve this as follows.

Corollary 5.5
.

For every
r\geq 4
, let
k=\left\lceil r/e\right\rceil
, let
L\subset\{0,1,\ldots,r-k-1\}
. If
H
is an
L
-intersecting
r
-uniform hypergraph, then
b(H)\leq r!/r^{r}
.

Proof.

This is the immediate consequence of Theorem
1.3
and Lemma
5.4
.
∎

In the other direction, Lemma
5.4
also provides a possible approach to Question
5.1
. For a certain
\alpha\approx 0.89
,
L=\{0,1,\ldots,\left\lfloor\alpha r\right\rfloor\}
and
r
large, Chao and Yu constructed in
[
1
]
an
L
-intersecting
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
on
2r
vertices with
b(H)>r!/r^{r}
. By Lemma
5.4
, this implies that for all sufficiently large
r
and any family
\mathcal{F}
of tents satisfying
\lambda\vdash r
and
r>\lambda_{1}>\alpha r
for all
\Delta_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
\pi(\mathcal{F})\geq b(H)>r!/r^{r}
. In particular, the answer to the second part of Question
5.1
is asymptotically at least
(1-\alpha)r
. It is an open question to determine the smallest
\alpha
for which a construction like Chao and Yu’s exists. Note that Liu’s observation above implies that this is at least 1/2, and Corollary
5.5
improves this lower bound to
1-1/e
.

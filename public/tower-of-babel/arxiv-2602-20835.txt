The formal spectrum of a tensor-triangulated category
Drew Heard, Marius Nielsen

Abstract
. To any essentially small tensor-triangulated category 𝒦 \mathscr{K} and Thomason subset Y ⊆ Spc ⁡ ( 𝒦 ) Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K}) we associate a ringed space ( Spf ⁡ ( 𝒦 , Y ) , 𝒪 Spf ⁡ ( 𝒦 , Y ) ) (\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y),\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)}) , called the formal spectrum of ( 𝒦 , Y ) (\mathscr{K},Y) . We establish basic properties of this construction and compute it in several examples from algebraic geometry, chromatic homotopy theory, equivariant homotopy theory, and modular representation theory.



1.
Introduction

Tensor–triangular geometry packages a tensor–triangulated category into a geometric
object via the Balmer spectrum
[
10
]
. Concretely, if
\mathscr{K}
is an essentially
small tt-category (or a
2
-ring
in our terminology), then its Balmer spectrum

\Spc(\mathscr{K})
is a spectral space. Its topology has a basis of closed sets given by supports of objects in

\mathscr{K}
, and it carries a natural structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\Spc(\mathscr{K})}
valued in graded rings.
When
\mathscr{K}=\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c}
for a quasi-compact and quasi-separated (qcqs) scheme
X
, one
recovers
X
as a locally ringed space from
(\Spc(\mathscr{K}),\mathcal{O}_{\Spc(\mathscr{K})})
;
see
3.20
. This is Balmer’s reconstruction theorem
[
5
]
,
relying crucially on Thomason’s classification of thick tensor ideals in
\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c}

[
42
]
.

In classical algebraic geometry, passing from a scheme
X
to its formal completion

\widehat{X}_{Y}
along a closed subset
Y
isolates the infinitesimal neighborhood of

Y
and remembers all nilpotent thickenings at once. This is preferable whenever a
problem is intrinsically local around
Y
, and it is natural to ask for an analogue in tensor–triangular geometry. For example, the
K(n)
-local stable homotopy category is obtained from
the
E(n)
-local category by a process resembling adic completion (see the formulas in

[
31
, Proposition 7.10]
), and it morally behaves like a “residue field”.
One might therefore expect its spectrum to be a single point. However, in tt-geometry
one must work with essentially small subcategories, and the compact objects in the

K(n)
-local category do not contain the unit (hence do not form a tt-category), while
the spectrum of dualizable objects is, perhaps, unexpectedly large
[
13
]
.

The goal of this paper is therefore to develop a formal analogue of the Balmer spectrum.
Given an essentially small tt-category

In the classical case, it is straightforward to check that the completion map

An analogue holds in the tt-world, although the proof is more delicate. Since Bousfield
localization is most naturally formulated in the presentable setting, it is convenient
to pass from an essentially small
\mathscr{K}
to the associated stable homotopy theory
\mathscr{C}\coloneqq\Ind(\mathscr{K})

\mathscr{C}

Unlike categories usually considered in tensor–triangular geometry,
\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}

is typically not rigidly-compactly generated. Our first main result identifies the formal
spectrum computed from the dualizable objects of
\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}
with the original
definition in terms of
(\mathscr{K},Y)

Theorem A

(
5.14
)
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a
2

Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
be a Thomason subset. There

(\varphi,\varphi^{\#})\colon\Spf(\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{\dual}_{Y},\varphi^{-1}(Y))\longrightarrow\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y),

where

is the map on Balmer spectra induced by completion.

This result is fundamental for us: the left-hand side is often the conceptual object of
interest, while the right-hand side is typically easier to compute. For the remainder
of this introduction, we abbreviate the left-hand side to
\Spf(\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{\dual}_{Y})

suppressing the implicit Thomason subset

A basic computation in tt-geometry is the Hopkins–Neeman theorem: for any commutative
noetherian ring
R
one has a canonical identification
\Spc(\Der(R)^{c})\cong\Spec(R)
, under
which tt-support agrees with ordinary (homological) support. Since formal completion in
algebraic geometry is taken along a closed subset, we fix an ideal
I\subseteq R
and consider
the closed subset
V(I)\subseteq\Spec(R)\cong\Spc(\Der(R)^{c})
. Our formal spectrum recovers the
usual affine formal scheme:

Theorem B

(
6.3
)
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring and
I\subseteq R
an ideal. There is a natural
isomorphism of formal schemes

\rho\colon\Spf\bigl(\Der(R)^{c},V(I)\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spf(R,I).

Consequently, the completion of
\Der(R)
at
V(I)
satisfies

\Spf\bigl(\widehat{\Der(R)}^{d}_{V(I)}\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spf(R^{\wedge}_{I}).

The completed category
\widehat{\Der(R)}_{V(I)}
here is the usual complete derived category of a noetherian ring at an ideal; see
4.6
.

We next extend this affine computation to noetherian schemes. The key input is a locality
statement for the formal spectra with respect to restriction to quasi-compact opens. If

U\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
is quasi-compact with closed complement
Z
, recall that

\mathscr{K}(U)\coloneqq(\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{K}_{Z})^{\natural}
denotes the idempotent-completed Verdier
quotient (cf.
3.10
). Then we prove:

Theorem C

(
5.16
)
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a
2
-ring and
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
a Thomason subset. Let

U\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
be a quasi-compact open subset and let
q_{U}\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}(U)

be the canonical functor. Set
Y^{\prime}\coloneqq Y\cap U
, viewed as a Thomason subset of

\Spc(\mathscr{K}(U))\cong U
. Then restriction along
Y^{\prime}\hookrightarrow Y
induces an isomorphism
of ringed spaces

\Spf(\mathscr{K}(U),Y^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\cong}\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)\big|_{Y^{\prime}}.

With
C
in hand, the affine computation globalizes by
covering a noetherian scheme by finitely many affine opens and checking that the resulting
local identifications glue on overlaps. This yields a formal version of Thomason’s theorem.

Theorem D

(
7.7
)
.

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme, and let
Z\subseteq X
be
a closed subset. Then there is a natural isomorphism of formal schemes

\Phi\colon\Spf\bigl(\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c},Z\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{X}_{Z},

where
\widehat{X}_{Z}
denotes the formal completion of
X
along
Z
.

Our second main family of examples comes from chromatic homotopy theory. Fix a prime
p
and a
height

, and write
{\mathscr{S}p}_{n}
for the

-local stable homotopy category with compact

{\mathscr{S}p}_{n}^{c}

\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{n}^{c})
is the
finite chain of primes

\mathscr{P}_{0}\subsetneq\mathscr{P}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathscr{P}_{n}

indexed by chromatic height, and the structure sheaf on the basic opens can be described

L_{k}S^{0}

. Completion at

{\mathscr{S}p}_{K(h+1)\vee\cdots\vee K(n)}

Theorem E

(
9.8
)
.

Fix an integer
0\leq h\leq n-1
, then the formal spectrum

\Spf\bigl({\mathscr{S}p}_{K(h+1)\vee\cdots\vee K(n)}^{\dual}\bigr)

has underlying space
Y_{h+1}
, and its structure sheaf on basic opens is given by

\mathcal{O}(\overline{U}_{k})\cong\pi_{*}L_{K(h+1)\vee\cdots\vee K(k)}S^{0}\qquad(k\geq h+1),

for the opens
\overline{U}_{k}=U(L_{n}F(k+1))\cap Y_{h+1}
described in

These chromatic examples illustrate one of the motivations for introducing

completion can drastically change the geometry detected by dualizable objects, and the formal

2.
Formal schemes and formal completions

We begin by recalling the definition of the formal spectrum of a commutative noetherian ring.
We do this to fix our conventions, which may differ slightly from the standard literature.

2.1 Definition
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring and let
I\subseteq R
be an ideal.
The
formal spectrum
of
R
(with respect to
I
), denoted
\Spf(R,I)
, is the locally ringed space

(|\Spf(R,I)|,\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(R,I)})

where:

(a)

The underlying topological space
|\Spf(R,I)|
is the closed subset of the Zariski spectrum given by the vanishing locus of
I
, i.e.,

|\Spf(R,I)|\coloneqq V(I)=\big\{\,\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\,\big|\,I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\,\big\}.

It has a basis of open subsets of the form

\overline{D}(s)\coloneqq D(s)\cap V(I)=\big\{\,\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\,\big|\,I\subseteq\mathfrak{p},\,s\notin\mathfrak{p}\,\big\},

where
s\in R
and

D(s)=\big\{\,\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\,\big|\,s\notin\mathfrak{p}\,\big\}.

(b)

The structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(R,I)}
is defined on the basic open sets
\overline{D}(s)
as the
I
-adic completion of the localization
R[1/s]
.

2.2 Example
.

Let
R
be a noetherian ring, and let
I
be a nilpotent ideal (for example,
I=(0)
).
Then it is immediate from the definitions that

\Spf(R,I)\cong\Spec(R),

the usual Zariski spectrum.

2.3 Example
.

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}
and
I=(p)
for a prime
p
.
Then
\Spf(\mathbb{Z},(p))
is a one-point locally ringed space with ring of global sections
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

2.4 Remark
.

There is an obvious extension to
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative rings, where we work with
homogeneous
prime ideals.
We denote this by a superscript
h
, i.e.,
\Spec^{h}(R_{*})
denotes the homogeneous Zariski spectrum of a graded ring
R_{*}
, and
\Spf^{h}(R,I)
denotes the formal homogeneous spectrum with respect to a homogeneous ideal
I
.

2.5 Remark
.

We do not assume that
R
is complete with respect to the
I
-adic topology.
However, replacing
R
by its completion
R^{\wedge}_{I}
does not change the formal spectrum; there is an isomorphism of locally ringed spaces

\Spf(R^{\wedge}_{I},IR^{\wedge}_{I})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spf(R,I)

induced by the completion map
R\to R^{\wedge}_{I}
.
In the former case, since the ideal for the formal spectrum is clear, we may simply write
\Spf(R^{\wedge}_{I})
.

2.6 Remark
.

Although the definition of the formal spectrum of a ring
R
does not strictly require
R
to be noetherian, we fix this assumption due to the poorly behaved nature of
I
-adic completion in the non-noetherian case.

2.7 Remark
.

There is a useful way to think about the topological space
|\Spf(R,I)|
.
Recall that the support of a (finitely generated)
R
-module
M
is

\Supp_{R}(M)=\big\{\,\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\,\big|\,M_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\,\big\}.

Since

S^{-1}(R/I)=0\iff S\cap I\neq\varnothing,

we see that

\Supp_{R}(R/I)=\big\{\,\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\,\big|\,I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\,\big\}.

In other words,

|\Spf(R,I)|=\Supp_{R}(R/I).

In the same way that the spectrum of a ring globalizes to define schemes, the formal spectrum globalizes to define formal schemes.

2.8 Definition
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
be a locally ringed space.
We say that
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is a
noetherian formal scheme
if
|X|
is quasi-compact and there exists a covering of
|X|
by open subsets
U_{\alpha}
such that each ringed space

(U_{\alpha},\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{\alpha}})
is isomorphic to

(|\Spf(R_{\alpha},I_{\alpha})|,\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(R_{\alpha},I_{\alpha})})

for some noetherian ring
R_{\alpha}
and ideal
I_{\alpha}\subseteq R_{\alpha}
.

2.9 Remark
.

It is clear from the definition that the condition of being a noetherian formal scheme is local: if there exists a covering
\{U_{\alpha}\}
of
X
such that each
(U_{\alpha},\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{\alpha}})
is a noetherian formal scheme, then so is
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

2.10 Remark
.

We form the category of noetherian formal schemes as the full subcategory of the category of locally ringed spaces whose objects are noetherian formal schemes. In contrast to some other sources, we do not require the structure sheaf to carry a topological structure (i.e., we work with locally ringed spaces rather than topologically ringed spaces).

3.
The Balmer spectrum of a tt-category

We assume the reader has some familiarity with ordinary tt-geometry, for example, the foundational paper
[
10
]
. For the benefit of the reader, we briefly recall the basic definitions. We begin by introducing the ‘small’ and ‘big’ variants of triangulated categories that will be used; see also
[
12
, Section 5]
for a more thorough discussion.

3.1 Definition
.

Let

\mathrm{2}\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}\coloneqq\CAlg(\Cat^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty})_{\mathrm{rig}}

be the
\infty
-category of commutative algebra objects in
\Cat^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
, the
\infty
-category of small, stable, idempotent-complete
\infty
-categories and exact functors, equipped with the symmetric monoidal structure described in
[
17
, Section 3.1]
.
The subscript
\mathrm{rig}
denotes the full subcategory spanned by those
\mathscr{K}

for which every object of the underlying symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category is dualizable.
A
2-ring
is then an object
\mathscr{K}\in\mathrm{2}\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}
.

We will also need a ‘big’ variant of this notion.

3.2 Definition
.

A
stable homotopy theory
is an object of
\CAlg(\Pr^{L}_{\mathrm{st}})
, i.e., a presentable, symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-category
(\mathscr{C},\otimes,\mathbb{1})
whose tensor product preserves all colimits separately in each variable.
The full subcategory
\mathscr{K}\coloneqq\mathscr{C}^{\dual}
of dualizable objects is then a
\mathrm{2}\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}
in the sense of
3.1
(see
[
37
, Lemma 2.5]
).
A stable homotopy theory is
rigidly-compactly generated
if it is compactly generated and its subcategory of dualizable objects coincides with its subcategory of compact objects.

3.3 Remark
.

The homotopy category of a given
\mathscr{K}\in\mathrm{2}\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}
is a tensor-triangulated category in the
usual sense of
[
10
]
. Likewise, if
\mathscr{C}
is a stable homotopy theory, then its
homotopy category is a tensor-triangulated category with small coproducts.

For the definition of the Balmer spectrum
\Spc(\mathscr{K})
it suffices to work at the level of
these underlying triangulated categories. By contrast, our construction of the formal spectrum
as a
ringed space
is most naturally formulated after passing to “big” categories, as we will soon describe. We therefore work throughout in the setting of stable

\infty
-categories: among other advantages, Ind-completion is well behaved in this setting,
whereas on the level of triangulated categories the Ind-completion need not remain triangulated;
see
[
32
, Remark 5.9]
for an example.

3.4 Remark
.

By taking Ind-categories, any 2-Ring can be turned into a rigidly-compactly generated stable homotopy theory. Indeed, set
\mathscr{C}\coloneqq\Ind(\mathscr{K})
. As noted in
[
37
]
, this is presentable, stable, and symmetric monoidal by combining
[
34
, Theorem 5.5.1.1]
and
[
35
, Corollary 4.8.1.14]
, and it is rigidly-compactly generated by construction. Moreover, the (restricted) Yoneda embedding
j\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{C}
is symmetric monoidal. In fact, if
\Pr^{L,\omega}_{\mathrm{st}}
denotes the (non-full) symmetric monoidal
subcategory of
\Pr^{L}_{\mathrm{st}}
spanned by compactly generated stable
\infty
-categories
and colimit-preserving functors that preserve compact objects (equivalently, whose right adjoint
preserves colimits), then
\Ind
-completion defines a symmetric monoidal equivalence

\Ind\colon\Cat^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pr\nolimits^{L,\omega}_{\mathrm{st}},

with inverse given by sending
\mathscr{D}
to
\mathscr{D}^{\omega}
.
See
[
17
, Section 3.1]
and
[
15
, Proposition 2.3]
. We will use this equivalence implicitly whenever needed.

Having fixed terminology for both the small and big settings, we now give a standard example that will serve as a running model in what follows.

3.5 Example
.

The prototypical example of a
\mathrm{2}\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}
is the category
\Mod^{c}_{R}
of compact
R
-modules, where
R
is a ring spectrum. The associated stable homotopy theory is the category
\Mod_{R}
of all
R
-modules. Note that if
R=HA
is an Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum, these correspond to the derived category of perfect complexes of
A
-modules and the unbounded derived category of
A
-modules, respectively.

3.6 Definition

(Balmer
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{K}\in\mathrm{2}\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}
. The
spectrum
of
\mathscr{K}
is the set

\Spc(\mathscr{K})\coloneqq\{\,\mathscr{P}\mid\mathscr{P}\text{ is a prime thick }\otimes\text{-ideal of }\mathscr{K}\,\}.

We topologize this set by declaring a basis of open sets
\{\,U(c)\,\}_{c\in\mathscr{K}}
defined by

U(c)\coloneqq\big\{\,\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{K})\,\big|\,c\in\mathscr{P}\,\big\}.

3.7 Definition
.

The
support
of an object
a\in\mathscr{K}
is

\supp(a)\coloneqq\big\{\,\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{K})\,\big|\,a\notin\mathscr{P}\,\big\}=U(a)^{\complement}.

The sets
\{\supp(a)\}_{a\in\mathscr{K}}
form a basis of closed subsets for the topology on
\Spc(\mathscr{K})
.

3.8 Remark
.

The spectrum is functorial
[
10
, Proposition 3.6]
: given a symmetric monoidal exact functor
F\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{L}
, there is an induced continuous map

\varphi\coloneqq\Spc(F)\colon\Spc(\mathscr{L})\longrightarrow\Spc(\mathscr{K}),\qquad\mathscr{P}\longmapsto F^{-1}(\mathscr{P}).

Moreover,
\varphi^{-1}(\supp_{\mathscr{K}}(a))=\supp_{\mathscr{L}}(F(a))
and
\varphi^{-1}(U(c))=U(F(c))
.

One major reason to study the spectrum is its classification of thick tensor ideals.

3.9 Theorem

(Balmer,
[
10
, Theorem 4.10]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a 2-ring. Then support induces an inclusion-preserving bijection:

\displaystyle\Bigl\{\text{thick $\otimes$-ideals }\mathscr{J}\subseteq\mathscr{K}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\leftrightarrows\Bigl\{\text{Thomason subsets }Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\supp(\mathscr{J})\coloneqq\bigcup_{x\in\mathscr{J}}\supp(x)

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}_{Y}\coloneqq\{\,x\in\mathscr{K}\mid\supp(x)\subseteq Y\,\}

\displaystyle\longmapsfrom Y.

3.10 Remark
.

We now describe Balmer’s structure sheaf on
\Spc(\mathscr{K})
.
Given a thick
\otimes
-ideal
\mathscr{J}\subseteq\mathscr{K}
as above, we can form the Verdier quotient
\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{J}

If

is a quasi-compact open subset with closed complement
Z

\mathscr{K}_{Z}\coloneqq\big\{\,a\in\mathscr{K}\,\big|\,\supp(a)\subseteq Z\,\big\}

and define

\mathscr{K}(U)\coloneqq\big(\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{K}_{Z}\big)^{\natural},

where
(-)^{\natural}
denotes idempotent completion. This is also known as the Karoubi quotient.
There is a natural functor
q_{U}\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}(U)

\mathbb{1}_{U}
for the image of the unit

under

Let

\mathscr{K}(U)\coloneqq(\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{K}_{Z})^{\natural}\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathscr{C}(U)^{c})^{\natural}\simeq\mathscr{C}(U)^{c}.

3.14 Remark
.

If
\mathbb{1}_{U}
denotes the tensor unit of the localized category

(3.15)

U\longmapsto\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1}_{U}),

where in the right-hand side,
\mathbb{1}_{U}

\mathscr{C}

Although (

\mathscr{C}
will be crucial for the construction of the formal Balmer spectrum, where we will encounter localizations that do not preserve compact objects, necessitating the use of the large category
\mathscr{C}

3.16 Remark
.

In
[

2
-Ring
\mathscr{K}
with associated stable homotopy theory

\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{K}}\colon\Open(\Spc(\mathscr{K}))^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\CAlg(\Pr\nolimits^{L}_{\mathrm{st}}).

3.17 Remark
.

It follows from
[
35

and

\Alg({\mathscr{S}p})\longrightarrow\Pr\nolimits_{\mathrm{st}}^{L},

\End(\mathbb{1}_{(-)})\colon\CAlg(\Pr\nolimits_{\mathrm{st}}^{L})\longrightarrow\CAlg({\mathscr{S}p}),

sending a stable homotopy theory

If we compose

In particular, Balmer’s structure sheaf of graded rings

3.19 Remark

(Functoriality)
.

The functoriality of

For details, see
[

3.20 Example
.

The most fundamental example comes from work of Hopkins and Neeman in the affine case, as extended to schemes by Thomason

,
38
,
42
]

f\colon X\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spc(\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c})

given by

f(x)=\big\{\,a\in\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c}\,\big|\,a_{x}\simeq 0\text{ in }\Der_{qc}(\mathcal{O}_{X,x})^{c}\,\big\}\quad\text{ for all }x\in X.

Moreover, under this equivalence, the homological support
4

4

That is, the support of the total homology of
a
.

\supph(a)\subseteq X

.

For the translation to tt-geometry in this form, see

3.21 Remark
.

When

3.22 Theorem

(Balmer)

Let
\mathscr{K}\in\mathrm{2}\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}

\rho\colon\Spc(\mathscr{K})\to\Spec^{h}(\pi_{*}\End_{\mathscr{K}}(\mathbb{1}))

It is not always the case that

4.
Completions of tt-categories

The tensor-triangular analog of
I
-adic completion is completion with respect to a closed subset of the Balmer spectrum. In this short section we recall the notion, and review the most basic example of the derived category of a commutative ring. Nothing in this section is new; the ideas date back to at least Greenlees
[
25
]
, and we follow the recent exposition in
[
9
]
.

4.1 Definition
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a rigidly compactly generated stable homotopy theory, and let
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{C}^{d})
be a Thomason subset.
We set

\mathscr{C}^{d}_{Y}\coloneqq\big\{\,x\in\mathscr{C}^{d}\,\big|\,\supp(x)\subseteq Y\,\big\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathscr{C}_{Y}\coloneqq\Loc\langle\mathscr{C}^{d}_{Y}\rangle.

4.2 Remark
.

The inclusion
\mathscr{C}_{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}
admits a right adjoint given by tensoring with the Balmer–Favi idempotent
e_{Y}

[
6
]
. Our primary interest, however, lies in the double right orthogonal to
\mathscr{C}_{Y}
. Recall that if
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
is any class of objects, we write

\mathscr{E}^{\perp}\coloneqq\big\{\,c\in\mathscr{C}\,\big|\,\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(s,c)=0\text{ for all }s\in\mathscr{E}\,\big\}.

Note that this is closed under suspension in
\mathscr{C}
.

4.3 Definition
.

The
completion
of
\mathscr{C}
at
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{C}^{d})
is the full subcategory

\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}\coloneqq(\mathscr{C}_{Y})^{\perp}{}^{\perp}.

Objects of
\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}
are called
Y
-complete
.

4.4 Remark
.

The inclusion
\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}
has a symmetric monoidal left adjoint, given by
\widehat{(-)}_{Y}\coloneqq\mathsf{hom}(e_{Y},-)
. The category
\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}
is a tensor-triangulated category under the localized tensor product. It is not, however, rigidly-compactly generated, except in trivial cases (see
[
8
, Remark 2.12]
). Note that via the inclusion we can also consider
\widehat{(-)}_{Y}
as an endofunctor on
\mathscr{C}
.

4.5 Remark
.

We are often interested in the case where
Y=\supp(A)
for some
A\in\mathscr{C}^{d}
. In this case, the completion functor
\widehat{(-)}_{\supp(A)}
coincides with Bousfield localization with respect to
A
(see, for example,
[
14
, Proposition 2.34]
).

4.6 Example
.

The most basic example is as follows. Let
R
be a noetherian ring,
I\subseteq R
an ideal, and consider the closed subset
Y=V(I)\subseteq\Spec(R)\cong\Spc(\Der(R)^{c})
(see
3.20
). Then the functor

\widehat{(-)}_{I}\colon\Der(R)\ \longrightarrow\ \widehat{\Der(R)}_{I}\coloneqq\widehat{\Der(R)}_{V(I)}

is the derived
I
-adic completion functor; see, for instance,
[
23
,
21
]
. Note that even if
M
is a discrete
R
-module, the derived completion
M^{\wedge}_{I}
need not agree with the classical
I
-adic completion. For example, with
R=\mathbb{Z}
,
I=(p)
, and
M=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
, the two notions differ
[
36
, Example 10.1.16]
.

5.
The formal spectrum of a tt-category

We now turn to the definition of the formal spectrum in the tensor-triangular setting.

5.1 Definition
.

Let
\mathscr{K}\in\mathrm{2}\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}
be a 2-ring and let
\mathscr{J}=\mathscr{K}_{Y}
be the thick
\otimes
-ideal in
\mathscr{K}

corresponding to a Thomason subset
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
(note that every thick
\otimes
-ideal is of this form by
3.9
).
Write
\mathscr{C}
for the stable homotopy theory associated to
\mathscr{K}
(
3.4
).
The
formal spectrum
of
\mathscr{K}
(with respect to
Y
), denoted
\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)
, is the ringed space

(|\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)|,\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)})

defined as follows:

(a)

The underlying topological space is

|\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)|\coloneqq Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})

equipped with the subspace topology. A basis of open sets is given by

\overline{U}(c)\coloneqq U(c)\cap Y=\big\{\,\mathscr{P}\in Y\,\big|\,c\in\mathscr{P}\,\big\}.

(b)

The structure sheaf is given on basic opens
\overline{U}(c)
by the sheafification (in
\mathrm{gr}\!\CAlg^{\heartsuit}
) of the presheaf

\overline{U}(c)\longmapsto\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}\Bigl(\mathbb{1},\widehat{(\mathbb{1}[c^{-1}])}_{Y}\Bigr),

where
\mathbb{1}[c^{-1}]
denotes the tensor unit of the localization
\mathscr{C}[c^{-1}]=\mathscr{C}(U(c))
, viewed as an object of
\mathscr{C}
via the right adjoint to the localization functor, and
\widehat{(-)}_{Y}
denotes
Y
-completion as introduced in
Section

4
.

5.2 Question
.

Is
\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)
always a
locally
ringed space?

5.3 Remark
.

Recall that
\widehat{(-)}_{Y}\simeq\mathsf{hom}(e_{Y},-)
. Therefore, the presheaf can equivalently be written as

\overline{U}(c)\longmapsto\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}\bigl(e_{Y},\mathbb{1}[c^{-1}]\bigr).

5.4 Remark
.

Recall from
3.14
there is a sheaf

\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{K}}^{\mathbb{1}}\colon\Open(\Spc(\mathscr{K}))^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\CAlg({\mathscr{S}p})

of commutative algebras in spectra. On a quasi-compact open
U
the value of the sheaf is given by
\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1}_{U})
. If
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
is a Thomason subset and
e_{Y}
is the associated Balmer-Favi idempotent, then the association sending a quasi-compact open
U\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
to
\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},\widehat{(\mathbb{1}_{U})}_{Y})
defines a functor

\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{K}_{Y}}\colon\Open(\Spc(\mathscr{K}))^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\CAlg({\mathscr{S}p}).

We claim that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{K}_{Y}}
is a sheaf. By combining
[
35
, Theorem 4.8.5.11]
,
[
35
, Theorem 4.8.5.16]
and
[
3
, Corollary 5.41]
we see that the functor

\Open(\Spc(\mathscr{K}))^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\CAlg(\mathscr{C})

sending a quasi-compact open
U\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
to
\mathbb{1}_{U}
is a sheaf. In particular, it suffices to see that the functor sending
U
to
\widehat{(\mathbb{1}_{U})}_{Y}
is a sheaf. This following from
[
34
, Theorem 7.3.5.2]
, since
\widehat{(-)}_{Y}\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
preserves limits and
\Spc(\mathscr{K})
is spectral.

We claim this sheaf is extended from its restriction to
|\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)|=Y
. It suffices to see that for any quasi-compact open
U\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
such that
U\cap Y=\varnothing
the commutative algebra in spectra of sections
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{K}_{Y}}(U)\simeq 0
is trivial. This follows from the calculus of idempotents coming from Thomason subsets of
\Spc(\mathscr{K})
presented in
[
6
, Theorem 5.18]
. Indeed, let
Z
be the closed complement of
U
, so
\mathbb{1}_{U}\simeq f_{Z}
.
If
U\cap Y=\varnothing
then
Y\subseteq Z
, hence
e_{Y}\otimes e_{Z}\simeq e_{Y}
.
Tensoring the triangle
e_{Z}\to\mathbb{1}\to f_{Z}
with
e_{Y}
shows
e_{Y}\otimes f_{Z}\simeq 0
,
so
\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},f_{Z})\simeq\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y}\otimes f_{Z},f_{Z})\simeq 0
, i.e.
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{K}_{Y}}(U)\simeq 0
.

We deduce, like in
3.17
, that the structure sheaf of graded commutative rings defined in
5.1
is induced from
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{K}_{Y}}|_{Y}
via
\pi_{*}\colon\CAlg({\mathscr{S}p})\to\mathrm{gr}\!\CAlg^{\heartsuit}
.

5.5 Remark
.

Unlike in
3.16
, we are currently unable to construct a sheaf valued in
\CAlg(\Pr\nolimits^{L}_{\mathrm{st}})
. We hope to pursue this further in future work.

5.6 Remark
.

Taking
c=0
(which lies in every prime), the value on
\overline{U}(0)=Y
is

Y\longmapsto\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}\bigl(\mathbb{1},\widehat{\mathbb{1}}_{Y}\bigr)\cong\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}\bigl(e_{Y},\mathbb{1}\bigr).

5.7 Example
.

Taking
\mathscr{J}=\thick_{\otimes}\langle\mathbb{1}\rangle
(equivalently
Y=\Spc(\mathscr{K})
), the construction reduces to the usual Balmer spectrum:

\Spf(\mathscr{K},\Spc(\mathscr{K}))\cong\Spc(\mathscr{K})

as ringed spaces (compare with (
3.15
)).

5.8 Remark
.

There is a subtle difference from the algebraic situation: for rings, one typically forms formal spectra only along
closed
subsets of
\Spec(R)
, whereas here we allow arbitrary Thomason subsets
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
.

The formal spectrum is functorial in the obvious way:

5.9 Lemma
.

Let
\mathscr{K},\mathscr{L}
be 2-rings and let
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
and
Y^{\prime}\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{L})
be
Thomason subsets. Suppose
F\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{L}
is an exact symmetric monoidal functor and write

\varphi\coloneqq\Spc(F)\colon\Spc(\mathscr{L})\to\Spc(\mathscr{K})
for the induced map.
If
\varphi(Y^{\prime})\subseteq Y
, then there is a morphism of ringed spaces

(\varphi,\varphi^{\#})\colon\Spf(\mathscr{L},Y^{\prime})\longrightarrow\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y).

Proof.

On underlying spaces this is the restriction
\varphi|_{Y^{\prime}}\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
.
For the sheaf map, we set
\mathscr{C}\coloneqq\Ind(\mathscr{K})
and
\mathscr{D}\coloneqq\Ind(\mathscr{L})
; the functor
F
extends to a colimit-preserving symmetric monoidal functor
F\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
.

Let
\overline{U}(c)\coloneqq U(c)\cap Y
be a basic open of
\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)
, with
c\in\mathscr{K}
compact. Using
5.3
we have

\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)}(\overline{U}(c))\cong\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},\mathbb{1}[c^{-1}]).

Define on the basis the composite

\begin{split}\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},\mathbb{1}[c^{-1}])&\longrightarrow\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{D}}(F(e_{Y}),F(\mathbb{1}[c^{-1}]))\\
&\xlongrightarrow{\sim}\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{D}}(e_{\varphi^{-1}(Y)},\mathbb{1}[F(c)^{-1}])\\
&\xlongrightarrow{\mathrm{res}}\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{D}}(e_{Y^{\prime}},\mathbb{1}[F(c)^{-1}]),\end{split}

where
F(e_{Y})\simeq e_{\varphi^{-1}(Y)}
and
F(\mathbb{1}[c^{-1}])\simeq\mathbb{1}[F(c)^{-1}]
by the functoriality of idempotents (see
[
7
, Proposition 5.11]
), and the last map is induced by the morphism of idempotents
e_{Y^{\prime}}\to e_{\varphi^{-1}(Y)}
coming from
Y^{\prime}\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(Y)
. Note that the target is precisely
\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathscr{L},Y^{\prime})}(\overline{U}(F(c)))
, where
\overline{U}(F(c))\coloneqq U(F(c))\cap Y^{\prime}
and
\overline{U}(F(c))=\varphi^{-1}(\overline{U}(c))
as open sets of
Y^{\prime}
. The constructions are all natural in
c
and compatible with restriction maps, and hence define a morphism

\varphi^{\#}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)}\longrightarrow\varphi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathscr{L},Y^{\prime})}.

Therefore,
(\varphi,\varphi^{\#})
is a morphism of ringed spaces
\Spf(\mathscr{L},Y^{\prime})\to\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)
.
∎

5.10 Example
.

As a special case of
5.9
, take
F=\id
and
Y=\Spc(\mathscr{K})
.
Together with
5.7
, this shows that for any Thomason subset

Y^{\prime}\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
, there is a canonical morphism of ringed spaces

\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y^{\prime})\longrightarrow\Spc(\mathscr{K}),

which realizes the inclusion
Y^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\Spc(\mathscr{K})
on underlying spaces. Here the morphism of sheaves is induced by the map
e_{Y^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{1}
.

5.11 Notation
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a 2-ring, and write
\mathscr{C}
for the associated stable homotopy theory. For a Thomason subset
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
, consider the functor

\widehat{(-)}_{Y}\colon\mathscr{C}\to\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}.

This is symmetric monoidal, and so restricts to a functor of 2-rings

(5.12)

\widehat{(-)}_{Y}\colon\mathscr{C}^{\dual}=\mathscr{K}\to\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{\dual}_{Y}.

By
5.9
we therefore get a morphism of ringed spaces

(5.13)

(\varphi,\varphi^{\#})\colon\Spf(\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{\dual}_{Y},\varphi^{-1}(Y))\longrightarrow\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y).

The crucial ingredient for the following result is due to Balmer and Sanders
[
9
]
. Note that this is the analog of
2.5
.

5.14 Theorem
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a 2-ring and let
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
be a Thomason subset.
Then the canonical morphism

(\varphi,\varphi^{\#})\colon\Spf(\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{\dual}_{Y},\varphi^{-1}(Y))\longrightarrow\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)

is an isomorphism of ringed spaces.

Proof.

On underlying topological spaces,
\varphi
is a homeomorphism by
[
9
, Theorem 4.6]
.
It remains to show that
\varphi^{\#}
is an isomorphism of sheaves.
Since both structure sheaves are defined as sheafifications of presheaves on the basis
\{\overline{U}(c)\}
, it suffices to check that
\varphi^{\#}
is an isomorphism on each basic open set.

Fix a basic open
\overline{U}(c)
and set
X\coloneqq\mathbb{1}[c^{-1}]\in\mathscr{C}
.
Using
5.3
, the sections of
\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)}
over
\overline{U}(c)
is

\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},X)
.
On the other hand, in
\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}
we have
L=\widehat{(-)}_{Y}\simeq\mathsf{hom}(e_{Y},-)
, and hence the corresponding ring of sections is
\pi_{*}\Hom_{\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}}(L(e_{Y}),L(X))
.
The map

\varphi^{\#}_{\overline{U}(c)}\colon\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},X)\longrightarrow\pi_{*}\Hom_{\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}}(L(e_{Y}),L(X))

is induced by the symmetric monoidal functor
L
.

We claim that the underlying map of mapping spectra

\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},X)\longrightarrow\Hom_{\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}}(L(e_{Y}),L(X))

is an equivalence. Indeed, by the adjunction
L\dashv\iota
and the identification
\iota L(-)\simeq\mathsf{hom}(e_{Y},-)
, there are natural equivalences

\displaystyle\Hom_{\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}}(L(e_{Y}),L(X))

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},\iota L(X))

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},\mathsf{hom}(e_{Y},X))

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y}\otimes e_{Y},X)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},X),

where the third equivalence is the tensor–hom adjunction in
\mathscr{C}
and the last uses the idempotence
e_{Y}\otimes e_{Y}\simeq e_{Y}
.
Under these identifications, the map induced by
L
corresponds to the composite above, hence is an equivalence.
Applying
\pi_{*}
shows that
\varphi^{\#}_{\overline{U}(c)}
is an isomorphism for every basic open
\overline{U}(c)
, and therefore
\varphi^{\#}
is an isomorphism of sheaves.
∎

5.15 Remark
.

Because of this theorem, we will usually write
\Spf(\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{\dual}_{Y})
instead of the more cumbersome
\Spf(\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{\dual}_{Y},\varphi^{-1}(Y))
.

We finish this section with the following locality theorem.

5.16 Theorem
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a 2-ring and
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
a Thomason subset.
Let
U\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
be a quasi-compact open subset, and let

q_{U}\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}(U)
be the functor from
3.10
.
Set
Y^{\prime}\coloneqq Y\cap U\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K}(U))
. Then the restriction of

\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)
to
Y^{\prime}
identifies with
\Spf(\mathscr{K}(U),Y^{\prime})
as
a ringed space:

\Spf(\mathscr{K}(U),Y^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\cong}\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)\big|_{Y^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Since
U
is quasi-compact, there exists
d\in\mathscr{K}
such that
U=U(d)

[
10
, Proposition 2.14]
. Consequently, the localization
\mathscr{K}(U)
is identified with the idempotent completion of the Verdier quotient,
(\mathscr{K}[d^{-1}])^{\natural}
.

By functoriality (
5.9
) applied to
q_{U}
, we obtain a morphism

(\varphi,\varphi^{\#})\colon\Spf(\mathscr{K}(U),Y^{\prime})\longrightarrow\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y).

On underlying topological spaces,
\varphi
is the inclusion
Y^{\prime}\hookrightarrow Y
. Since the image of
\varphi
lies in the open set
Y^{\prime}
, this morphism factors through the restriction
\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)|_{Y^{\prime}}
. To prove that this factorization is an isomorphism, it remains to show that the induced map on structure sheaves is an isomorphism. It suffices to verify this on a basis of the open sets of
Y^{\prime}
.

Let
\overline{V}\subseteq Y^{\prime}
be a basic open set. We can write
\overline{V}=U(c)\cap Y^{\prime}
for some
c\in\mathscr{K}
. By replacing
c
with
c\oplus d
(which satisfies
U(c\oplus d)=U(c)\cap U(d)
), we may assume without loss of generality that
U(c)\subseteq U(d)=U
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\coloneqq\Ind(\mathscr{K})
and let
L\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}(U)
be the localization functor, with fully faithful right adjoint
\iota
. We compare the sections over
\overline{V}
:

(a)

On
\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y)
: Since
U(c)\cap Y=\overline{V}
(as
U(c)\subseteq U
), the section is

\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{C}}[c^{-1}]).

(b)

On
\Spf(\mathscr{K}(U),Y^{\prime})
: The section is

\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}(U)}(e_{Y^{\prime}},\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{C}(U)}[c^{-1}]).

Since
U(c)\subseteq U(d)
, the object
\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{C}}[c^{-1}]
is
U
-local. Thus, the canonical map
\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{C}}[c^{-1}]\to\iota(\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{C}(U)}[c^{-1}])
is an equivalence. Using the adjunction
L\dashv\iota
and the fact that
L(e_{Y})\simeq e_{Y\cap U}=e_{Y^{\prime}}
(see
[
7
, Proposition 5.11]
), we obtain natural isomorphisms:

\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y},\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{C}}[c^{-1}])\cong\Hom_{\mathscr{C}(U)}(L(e_{Y}),\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{C}(U)}[c^{-1}])\cong\Hom_{\mathscr{C}(U)}(e_{Y^{\prime}},\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{C}(U)}[c^{-1}]).

This shows the map of sheaves is an isomorphism on basic open sets, completing the proof.
∎

6.
The formal Hopkins–Neeman theorem

Let
R
be a noetherian ring and let
I\subseteq R
be an ideal. In
4.6
, we introduced the derived category of
I
-complete
R
-modules,
\widehat{\Der(R)}_{I}
, as the completion of
\Der(R)
along the closed subset
V(I)\subseteq\Spc(\Der(R)^{c})
. In this section, we compute the formal spectrum
\Spf(\Der(R)^{c},V(I))
and identify it with the classical affine formal scheme
\Spf(R^{\wedge}_{I})
.

6.1 Remark
.

We first recall some properties of the derived
I
-completion functor
\widehat{(-)}_{I}\colon\Der(R)\to\widehat{\Der(R)}_{I}
from
4.6
.
For a complex
M\in\Der(R)
, the object
\widehat{M}_{I}
is the
derived

I
-adic completion of
M
. Even if
M
is a discrete module,
\widehat{M}_{I}
may differ from the classical
I
-adic completion. In general,
[
23
, Proposition 1.1]
provides a short exact sequence for any discrete module
M
:

0\to{\varprojlim_{k}}^{1}\Tor^{R}_{i+1}(R/I^{k},M)\to\pi_{i}\widehat{M}_{I}\to\varprojlim_{k}\Tor^{R}_{i}(R/I^{k},M)\to 0.

This implies the following useful lemma:

6.2 Lemma
.

If
M
is a flat discrete
R
-module, then the natural map
\widehat{M}_{I}\to M^{\wedge}_{I}
is an isomorphism, where
M^{\wedge}_{I}
denotes the ordinary
I
-adic completion.

Our version of the Hopkins–Neeman theorem is the following:

6.3 Theorem
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring and let
I\subseteq R
be an ideal. Then there is an isomorphism of locally ringed spaces (and hence, formal schemes)

\rho\colon\Spf\bigl(\Der(R)^{c},V(I)\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spf(R^{\wedge}_{I})

induced by the comparison map of
3.22
. Consequently, we have an isomorphism

\Spf\bigl(\widehat{\Der(R)}_{I}^{\dual}\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spf(R^{\wedge}_{I}).

Proof.

By
2.5
and
5.14
, it suffices to establish the isomorphism

\rho\colon\Spf\bigl(\Der(R)^{c},V(I)\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spf(R,I).

In this context, the stable homotopy theory associated to
\Der(R)^{c}
is
\Der(R)
.

On the level of topological spaces, the result follows from
3.20
: the isomorphism
\Spc(\Perf(R))\cong\Spec(R)
identifies the tensor-triangular support with the classical support of
R
-modules, mapping
V(I)
homeomorphically to itself.

It remains to identify the structure sheaves. We check this on the basis of open sets
U=\overline{D}(s)=D(s)\cap V(I)
.
Recall from
[
11
, Theorem 5.3]
that under
\rho
, the preimage of
D(s)
is the open set
U(\cone(s))
. Thus,

\rho^{-1}\bigl(\overline{D}(s)\bigr)=U(\cone(s))\cap V(I)=\overline{U}(\cone(s)).

The ring of sections of the structure sheaf of
\Spf(R,I)
over this open set is simply

\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(R,I)}\bigl(\overline{D}(s)\bigr)=R[1/s]^{\wedge}_{I}.

On the tensor-triangular side, since
R
is the tensor unit of
\Der(R)
, the ring of sections is given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\Der(R)^{c},V(I))}\bigl(\overline{U}(\cone(s))\bigr)&=\pi_{*}\Hom_{\Der(R)}\bigl(R,\widehat{(\mathbb{1}[\cone(s)^{-1}])}_{I}\bigr)\\
&\cong\pi_{*}\bigl(\widehat{(\mathbb{1}[\cone(s)^{-1}])}_{I}\bigr).\end{split}

Here,
\mathbb{1}[\cone(s)^{-1}]
denotes the tensor unit in the localization of
\Der(R)
at the open set
U(\cone(s))
. This localization corresponds to the Verdier quotient by the subcategory generated by
\cone(s)
. As shown in
[
30
, Theorem 3.3.7]
, this quotient identifies with
\Der(R[1/s])
as tensor-triangulated categories. Under this equivalence, the localized unit
\mathbb{1}[\cone(s)^{-1}]
corresponds to the ring
R[1/s]
.
Therefore, we have an equivalence of completions

\widehat{(\mathbb{1}[\cone(s)^{-1}])}_{I}\simeq\widehat{R[1/s]}_{I}.

Since
R[1/s]
is a flat
R
-module, its derived completion coincides with its classical completion by
6.2
. Combining these results yields a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\Der(R)^{c},V(I))}\bigl(\overline{U}(\cone(s))\bigr)\cong R[1/s]^{\wedge}_{I}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(R,I)}\bigl(\overline{D}(s)\bigr).

These local isomorphisms are compatible with restriction, so they glue to an isomorphism of structure sheaves. Thus,
\rho
is an isomorphism of ringed spaces. Finally, since
\Spf(R,I)
is a locally ringed space, the isomorphism means that
\Spf(\Der(R)^{c},V(I))
is as well, and by definition, this means it is an isomorphism of formal schemes.
∎

6.4 Remark
.

This result will also follow from results in
Section

8
, in particular
8.6
. However we believe it is still instructive to do this special case.

7.
Globalizing to schemes

We can now globalize the formal Hopkins–Neeman theorem (
6.3
) to arbitrary noetherian schemes. We begin by recalling the notion of completion in the setting of schemes.

7.1 Definition
.

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme, and let
Y\subseteq|X|
be a closed subset. An
ideal of definition
for
Y
is a quasi-coherent ideal sheaf
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}
of finite type such that
|V(\mathcal{J})|=Y
.

7.2 Definition
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact quasi-separated noetherian scheme, and
Y\subseteq|X|
a closed subset with ideal of definition
\mathcal{J}
. For
n\geq 0
, set

X_{n}\coloneqq(V(\mathcal{J}^{n+1}),\ \mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{J}^{n+1}).

The colimit in the category of locally ringed spaces

\widehat{X}_{Y}\coloneqq\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}

is called the
formal completion
of
X
along
Y
.

7.3 Remark
.

Explicitly,
\widehat{X}_{Y}
is the locally ringed space whose underlying topological space is
Y
, equipped with the structure sheaf

\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}_{Y}}\coloneqq\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{J}^{n+1}.

By
[
26
, Proposition 10.8.5]
,
\widehat{X}_{Y}
is a formal scheme.

7.4 Example
.

Let
X=\Spec(R)
and let
Y\subseteq|\Spec(R)|
be a closed subset. Then there exists an ideal
I\subseteq R
such that
V(I)=Y
, and one has natural isomorphisms

\widehat{X}_{Y}\cong\Spf(R,I)\cong\Spf(R^{\wedge}_{I}).

7.5 Definition
.

A formal scheme is
algebraizable
if it is of the form
\widehat{X}_{Y}
for some noetherian scheme
X
and a closed subset
Y\subseteq|X|
.

7.6 Remark
.

In the rest of this section, we will restrict our attention on algebraizable formal schemes. Even in the noetherian case, there exist non-algebraizable formal schemes, although they are difficult to construct; see
[
28
, Section 5]
.

7.7 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme, and let
Z\subseteq X
be a closed subset.
Let
\widehat{X}_{Z}
denote the formal completion of
X
along
Z
.
Then there is an isomorphism of locally ringed spaces (and hence formal schemes)

\Phi\colon\Spf\bigl(\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c},Z\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{X}_{Z}.

Proof.

Let
\{U_{\alpha}\}
be a finite affine open cover of
X
, where
U_{\alpha}=\Spec(R_{\alpha})
.
Since
U_{\alpha}
is affine, it is a quasi-compact open subset of
X
.
Let
Z_{\alpha}\coloneqq Z\cap U_{\alpha}
be the restriction of the closed subset to the affine patch; note that
Z_{\alpha}=V(I_{\alpha})
for some ideal
I_{\alpha}\subseteq R_{\alpha}
.

By the definition of the formal completion of a scheme,
\widehat{X}_{Z}
is obtained by gluing the affine formal schemes

(\widehat{X}_{Z})|_{U_{\alpha}}\cong\Spf(R_{\alpha},I_{\alpha}).

On the tensor-triangular side, we identify
\Spc(\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c})\cong X
as topological spaces (
3.20
).
Since
U_{\alpha}
is a quasi-compact open set, we may apply the locality theorem (
5.16
):

\Spf\bigl(\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c},Z\bigr)\big|_{Z_{\alpha}}\cong\Spf\bigl(\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c}(U_{\alpha}),Z_{\alpha}\bigr).

where
Z_{\alpha}\coloneqq Z\cap U_{\alpha}
. Recall from
3.10
that
\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c}(U_{\alpha})
denotes the Karoubi quotient. By the Thomason–Trobaugh localization theorem
[
41
, Theorem 5.2.2]
(see also
[
5
, Theorem 2.13]
), there is an equivalence

\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c}(U_{\alpha})\simeq\Der_{\qc}(U_{\alpha})^{c}\simeq\Der(R_{\alpha})^{c}.

Thus, we have an isomorphism of formal schemes

\Spf\bigl(\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c},Z\bigr)\big|_{Z_{\alpha}}\cong\Spf\bigl(\Der(R_{\alpha})^{c},V(I_{\alpha})\bigr).

Now we apply the affine formal Hopkins–Neeman theorem (
6.3
). This provides an isomorphism of ringed spaces

\rho_{\alpha}\colon\Spf\bigl(\Der(R_{\alpha})^{c},V(I_{\alpha})\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spf(R_{\alpha},I_{\alpha}).

Combining these identifications, we obtain local isomorphisms

\phi_{\alpha}\colon\Spf\bigl(\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c},Z\bigr)\big|_{Z_{\alpha}}\xrightarrow{\sim}(\widehat{X}_{Z})|_{U_{\alpha}}.

To verify that these glue, consider an intersection
U_{\alpha\beta}=U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
. Since
X
is qcqs, this intersection is quasi-compact. The comparison map
\rho
in
6.3
is natural with respect to localization, and the locality theorem is functorial with respect to restriction. Consequently, the isomorphisms
\phi_{\alpha}
and
\phi_{\beta}
satisfy the cocycle condition on the overlaps
U_{\alpha\beta}
. Therefore, they glue to a global isomorphism of ringed spaces. The argument is then completed the same way as in
6.3
.
∎

7.8 Remark
.

One can ask for extensions beyond schemes (e.g., to certain algebraic stacks or other
locally affine tensor-triangular situations). Conceptually, the argument above only uses
(i) an affine identification (a formal Hopkins–Neeman statement),
(ii) a locality statement for
\Spf
under restriction to quasi-compact opens, via
5.16

and (iii) a Thomason–Trobaugh type localization theorem identifying the relevant Verdier
quotients with the corresponding “affine” categories on opens.
Any setting in which analogues of (i)–(iii) hold admits the same gluing argument.

7.9 Definition
.

Let
\widehat{X}_{Z}
denote the formal completion of
X
along
Z
as above. We set

\Der^{\wedge}_{\qc}(\widehat{X}_{Z})\coloneqq\widehat{\Der_{\qc}(X)}_{Z},

the completion of
\Der_{\qc}(X)
at the Thomason subset

Z\subseteq\Spc(\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c})\cong X
in the sense of
4.3
.

7.10 Corollary
.

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme and let
Z\subseteq X
be a closed subset. Let

\widehat{X}_{Z}
denote the formal completion of
X
along
Z
. Then there is an
isomorphism of ringed spaces (and hence formal schemes)

\Phi\colon\Spf\bigl(\Der^{\wedge}_{\qc}(\widehat{X}_{Z})^{\dual}\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{X}_{Z}.

7.11 Remark
.

Recall from
4.4
that completion at a Thomason subset

Z\subseteq\Spc(\Der_{\qc}(X)^{c})\cong X
is given by the endofunctor

\widehat{(-)}_{Z}\simeq\mathsf{hom}(e_{Z},-),

where
e_{Z}
is the Balmer–Favi idempotent associated to
Z
.

When
X
is separated, one can identify this idempotent with a familiar geometric object:
there is an equivalence

e_{Z}\simeq\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{Z}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

in
\Der_{\qc}(X)
, where
\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{Z}
denotes the right derived functor of
\Gamma_{Z}
, the subsheaf with supports in
Z
, see
[
40
, Lemma 5.4]
. In particular, in this
case the abstract completion functor
\mathsf{hom}(e_{Z},-)
agrees with the derived completion (local
homology) functor of Alonso Tarrío–Jeremías López–Lipman: by
[
1
, Theorem (0.3)]
,
the right adjoint to
\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{Z}
is naturally isomorphic to
\mathbb{L}\Lambda_{Z}
, and
under the identification
e_{Z}\simeq\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{Z}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
this yields a natural
equivalence of endofunctors on
\Der_{\qc}(X)

\widehat{(-)}_{Z}\simeq\mathsf{hom}(\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{Z}(\mathcal{O}_{X}),-)\simeq\mathbb{L}\Lambda_{Z}(-).

7.12 Remark
.

We briefly indicate how
7.9
compares with the triangulated category
denoted
\Der^{\wedge}_{\qc}(\widehat{X}_{Z})
in
[
2
, §6.3]
, at least when

X
is separated. This comparison is not used elsewhere in the paper.

Write
\widetilde{\Der}^{\wedge}_{\qc}(\widehat{X}_{Z})
for the category constructed in

[
2
, Remark 6.3.1]
. Alonso–Jeremías–Lipman show that on the formal scheme

\widehat{X}_{Z}
the “complete” and “torsion” variants agree, and identify the torsion
category on
\widehat{X}_{Z}
with the
Z
-supported subcategory on
X
:

\widetilde{\Der}^{\wedge}_{\qc}(\widehat{X}_{Z})\simeq\Der_{\qct}(\widehat{X}_{Z})\simeq\Der_{\qc Z}(X),

where
\Der_{\qc Z}(X)
denotes the essential image of
\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{Z}
on
\Der_{\qc}(X)
.
(For precise statements and hypotheses, see
[
2
, §6.3]
and

[
2
, Proposition 5.2.4]
.)

On the other hand,
7.11
identifies our completion functor

\widehat{(-)}_{Z}
with
\mathbb{L}\Lambda_{Z}
(when
X
is separated). A form of
Greenlees–May duality identifies the torsion and complete pieces as equivalent
subcategories of
\Der_{\qc}(X)
, so the essential image of
\mathbb{L}\Lambda_{Z}
agrees with the completion
subcategory
\widehat{\Der_{\qc}(X)}_{Z}
of
7.9
. Under these identifications,
one obtains an equivalence

\widetilde{\Der}^{\wedge}_{\qc}(\widehat{X}_{Z})\simeq\widehat{\Der_{\qc}(X)}_{Z}.

In fact, the formulas in
[
2
, Proposition 5.24]
implies that this equivalence is given by
\mathbb{L}\Lambda_{Z}\circ k_{*}
where
k\colon\widehat{X}_{Z}\to X
is the completion map.

8.
A comparison map

One of the most important tools in computing the Balmer spectrum of a tensor-triangulated
category is Balmer’s comparison map (
3.22
). A general strategy for
computing
\Spc(\mathscr{K})
is to first determine
\Spec^{h}(\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1}))

and then compute the fibers of the comparison map; see
[
7
]
and

[
4
]
for examples. It is therefore natural to seek an analogue of the comparison
map for the formal spectrum introduced in this paper. Throughout this section we assume
R_{*}\coloneqq\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1})
is noetherian, which implies that
\rho
is surjective
[
11
, Theorem 7.3]
. In this section we will construct a comparison map for ‘algebraic’ completions, i.e., those coming from an ideal in
R_{*}
.

8.1 Definition
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a
\mathrm{2}\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}
and let
I\subseteq R_{*}
be a homogeneous ideal. Write

Y(I)\coloneqq\rho^{-1}(V(I))
for the preimage of the closed subset

V(I)\subseteq\Spec^{h}(R_{*})
under the comparison map of
3.22
.
Define

\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{I}\coloneqq\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y(I)}

to be the completion of
\mathscr{C}
at the closed subset
Y(I)\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
in
the sense of
4.3
.

8.2 Example
.

If
\mathscr{K}=\Perf(R)
for a commutative noetherian ring
R
, then
\rho
is a
homeomorphism, and we are exactly in the situation of
4.6
.

8.3 Remark
.

The spectrum
R\coloneqq\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1})
is a commutative algebra in spectra
and, for any
X\in\mathscr{C}
,
\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},X)
is an
R
-module. By Morita theory
there is a symmetric monoidal adjunction

(8.4)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F\colon\Mod_{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{C}\penalty\mspace{6muplus1mu}{:}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu\mathpunct{}\mspace{2mu}G}}

with
F(M)=M\otimes_{R}\mathbb{1}
and
G(X)=\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},X)
. Since

\mathscr{C}=\Ind(\mathscr{K})
is rigidly-compactly generated and
\mathbb{1}
is compact,

F
is fully faithful and
G
exhibits
\Mod_{R}
as a colocalization of
\mathscr{C}
.

Let
\rho_{\mathscr{K}}\colon\Spc(\mathscr{K})\to\Spec^{h}(R_{*})
and

\rho_{R}\colon\Spc(\Mod_{R}^{c})\to\Spec^{h}(R_{*})
be the respective comparison maps.
Functoriality of the comparison map
[
11
, Theorem 5.3]
yields a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Spc(\mathscr{K})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Spc(\Mod_{R}^{c})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Spec^{h}(R_{*})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Spec^{h}(R_{*})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{\mathscr{K}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spc(F)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{R}}

and hence, for any homogeneous ideal
I\subseteq R_{*}
, the associated Thomason subsets

Y(I)\coloneqq\rho_{\mathscr{K}}^{-1}\bigl(V(I)\bigr)\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K}),\qquad Y_{R}(I)\coloneqq\rho_{R}^{-1}\bigl(V(I)\bigr)\subseteq\Spc(\Mod_{R}^{c})

satisfy

Y(I)=\Spc(F)^{-1}\!\bigl(Y_{R}(I)\bigr).

Let
e_{Y(I)}\in\mathscr{C}
and
e_{Y_{R}(I)}\in\Mod_{R}
be the associated tensor idempotents.
By functoriality
[
7
, Proposition 5.11]
we have
F(e_{Y_{R}(I)})\cong e_{Y(I)}
,
and since
F
is fully faithful,
G(e_{Y(I)})\cong e_{Y_{R}(I)}
in
\Mod_{R}
.

8.5 Proposition
.

For every
X\in\mathscr{C}
there is a natural isomorphism of
R
-modules

\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y(I)},X)\cong\Hom_{\Mod_{R}}\!\bigl(e_{Y_{R}(I)},\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},X)\bigr).

Proof.

Using
F(e_{Y_{R}(I)})\cong e_{Y(I)}
and the adjunction (
8.4
),

\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(e_{Y(I)},X)\cong\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}\bigl(F(e_{Y_{R}(I)}),X\bigr)\cong\Hom_{\Mod_{R}}\bigl(e_{Y_{R}(I)},\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},X)\bigr)

as required.
∎

8.6 Proposition
.

Let
\mathscr{K}\in\mathrm{2}\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}
with
R_{*}
noetherian, and let
I\subseteq R_{*}
be a homogeneous ideal.
Set
Y(I)\coloneqq\rho^{-1}(V(I))\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
, where

\rho\colon\Spc(\mathscr{K})\to\Spec^{h}(R_{*})
is Balmer’s comparison map
of
3.22
. Then there is a natural morphism of locally ringed spaces

\overline{\rho}\colon\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y(I))\longrightarrow\Spf^{h}(\pi_{*}R,I),

whose underlying map of spaces is the restriction

\rho|_{Y(I)}\colon Y(I)\to V(I)
. In particular, if
\rho
is an isomorphism, then so
is
\overline{\rho}
.

Proof.

On spaces,
\rho(Y(I))=\rho(\rho^{-1}(V(I)))=V(I)
. For the sheaves, it suffices to
construct compatible maps on a basis of opens of
\Spf^{h}(R_{*},I)
, namely

\overline{D}(s)=D(s)\cap V(I)
with
s\in R_{*}
. By
[
11
, Theorem 5.3(b)]
,

\rho^{-1}(D(s))=U(\cone(s))
, hence

\overline{\rho}^{-1}\bigl(\overline{D}(s)\bigr)=Y(I)\cap\rho^{-1}(D(s))=\overline{U}(\cone(s)).

By definition,

\mathcal{O}_{\Spf^{h}(R_{*},I)}(\overline{D}(s))=R_{*}[1/s]^{\wedge}_{I}
.
On the other hand, by
5.3
and
8.5
,

\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y(I))}\!\bigl(\overline{U}(\cone(s))\bigr)\cong\pi_{*}\Hom_{\Mod_{R}}\!\bigl(e_{Y_{R}(I)},\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1}[1/s])\bigr).

The right-hand side is the derived
I
-adic completion of the
R
-module

\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1}[1/s])
, computed by the Greenlees–May spectral sequence

[
24
, Eq. (3.3), Theorem 4.2]
with

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{I}_{-p,-q}\bigl(\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1}[1/s])\bigr)

Note that
H^{I}_{-p,*}(-)
denote the local homology groups of
[
23
]
. Since
\pi_{*}\Hom_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1}[1/s])\cong R_{*}[1/s]
is
R_{*}
-flat,
[
23
, Theorem 2.5 and 4.1]
imply that these terms vanish for
p\neq 0
, so that the spectral sequence collapses and

\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y(I))}\!\bigl(\overline{U}(\cone(s))\bigr)\cong R_{*}[1/s]^{\wedge}_{I}=\mathcal{O}_{\Spf^{h}(R_{*},I)}(\overline{D}(s)).

These identifications are natural in
s
and compatible with restriction, hence glue to

\overline{\rho}^{\#}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\Spf^{h}(R_{*},I)}\!\to\overline{\rho}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathscr{K},Y(I))}
. If
\rho
is an isomorphism, the same
local identifications show
\overline{\rho}
is an isomorphism of ringed spaces.
∎

8.7 Corollary
.

Under the hypotheses above, there is a natural morphism of ringed spaces

\widehat{\rho}\colon\Spf\bigl(\widehat{\mathscr{C}}_{Y}^{\dual}\bigr)\longrightarrow\Spf^{h}\bigl((R_{*})^{\wedge}_{I}\bigr).

Proof.

Combine
5.14
with
2.5
and

8.6
.
∎

8.8 Example
.

Let
A
be an even periodic commutative ring spectrum with
\pi_{0}(A)
a complete
regular local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. By

[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
the comparison map

\rho\colon\Spc(\Perf(A))\longrightarrow\Spec^{h}(\pi_{*}A)\cong\Spec(\pi_{0}A)

is a homeomorphism. In fact, by
[
11
, Proposition 6.11]
, this is even an isomorphism of ringed spaces. Let
\kappa(A)\coloneqq A/\mathfrak{m}
(so that

\pi_{0}\kappa(A)\cong\pi_{0}(A)/\mathfrak{m}
). This is an
A
-module with

\supp(\kappa(A))=V(\mathfrak{m})
. Applying
8.6
and

8.7
with
I=\mathfrak{m}
we obtain a homeomorphism

\Spf\bigl((L_{\kappa(A)}\Mod_{A})^{\dual}\bigr)\xrightarrow{\cong}\Spf(\pi_{0}A).

Here we use
4.5
to identify the completion functor as a
Bousfield localization in this case.

9.
Chromatic homotopy

We now turn to examples coming from chromatic homotopy theory. As usual, we fix a prime number
p
throughout.

9.1 Remark
.

Recall that the Brown–Peterson spectrum
BP
has coefficient ring

BP_{*}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[v_{1},v_{2},\ldots].

Johnson–Wilson theory
E(n)
is obtained from
BP
by first quotienting by the ideal
(v_{n+1},v_{n+2},\ldots)
and then inverting
v_{n}
(using, for example, highly structured ring spectra
[
22
]
). Its coefficients are

E(n)_{*}\cong v_{n}^{-1}BP_{*}/(v_{n+1},v_{n+2},\ldots).

More generally, for an invariant regular sequence
J_{k}=(p^{i_{0}},v_{1}^{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{k-1}^{i_{k-1}})
, we can form a quotient
BP/J_{k}
with
\pi_{*}(BP/J_{k})\cong BP_{*}/J_{k}
, and for
0\leq n\leq k
define a spectrum

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L_{K(h+1)\vee\cdots\vee K(n)}L_{k}S^{0}}}

In this short section, we consider two further examples, from equivariant homotopy theory and modular representation theory.

10.1 Notation
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
p
a prime. We write
{\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}^{\cofree}
for the Bousfield
localization of
{\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}
at
G_{+}
, or equivalently, the completion of
{\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}
at the
Thomason subset

Y_{G}\coloneqq\supp(G_{+})\subseteq\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}^{c}).

As a set, the spectrum
\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}^{c})
and the subset
Y_{G}
are computed in

[
7
]
. Moreover,
[
7
, Corollary 4.13]
shows that
restriction induces a homeomorphism from
\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{(p)}^{c})
onto

.

10.2 Proposition
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
p
a prime. Restriction along the inclusion
e\leq G
defines an exact
symmetric monoidal functor

\res^{G}_{e}\colon{\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}\longrightarrow{\mathscr{S}p}_{(p)}.

Let
Y_{G}\coloneqq\supp(G_{+})\subseteq\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}^{c})
. Then
\res^{G}_{e}
induces a morphism
of ringed spaces

(\varphi,\varphi^{\#})\colon\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{(p)}^{c})\longrightarrow\Spf({\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}^{\cofree,\dual}).

Moreover,
\varphi
is a homeomorphism of the underlying topological spaces. However, for
G=C_{2}
and

p=2
, this morphism is not an isomorphism of ringed spaces.

Proof.

Apply
5.9
to the restriction functor between compact objects:

\res^{G}_{e}\colon{\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}^{c}\longrightarrow{\mathscr{S}p}_{(p)}^{c}.

Let
Y\coloneqq\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{(p)}^{c})
. Then
\Spc(\res^{G}_{e})
is a continuous map

\Spc(\res^{G}_{e})\colon\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{(p)}^{c})\longrightarrow\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}^{c}),

and we set
Y^{\prime}\coloneqq\Spc(\res^{G}_{e})(Y)\subseteq\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}^{c})
.
By
[
7
, Corollary 4.13]
, this image is precisely

Y^{\prime}=\supp(G_{+})=Y_{G}.

Therefore,
5.9
yields a morphism of ringed spaces

(\varphi,\varphi^{\#})\colon\Spf({\mathscr{S}p}_{(p)}^{c},Y)\longrightarrow\Spf({\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}^{c},Y_{G}).

By
5.7
, the domain is
\Spf({\mathscr{S}p}_{(p)}^{c},Y)\cong\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{(p)}^{c})
, and by

5.14
, the target identifies with
\Spf({\mathscr{S}p}_{G,(p)}^{\cofree,\dual})
,
yielding the stated map. The fact that
\varphi
is a homeomorphism on underlying spaces is exactly

[
7
, Corollary 4.13]
.

Finally, we show this is not an isomorphism for
G=C_{2}
and
p=2
by evaluating global sections.
On the nonequivariant side (the source), we have

\mathcal{O}_{\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{(2)}^{c})}\bigl(\Spc({\mathscr{S}p}_{(2)}^{c})\bigr)\cong\pi_{*}\End_{{\mathscr{S}p}_{(2)}}(\mathbb{S}_{(2)})\cong\pi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{(2)}.

In degree
0
, this is the
2
-local integers
\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}
.

On the cofree side (the target), using
5.6
(and the identification of
the whole space with
Y_{G}
), we have

\mathcal{O}_{\Spf({\mathscr{S}p}_{C_{2},(2)}^{\cofree})}(Y_{G})\cong\pi_{*}\Hom_{{\mathscr{S}p}_{C_{2},(2)}}(e_{Y_{G}},\mathbb{S}_{(2)}).

The Segal conjecture for
C_{2}
(specifically Lin’s theorem
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
) identifies the degree
0

I
-adic completion of the Burnside ring

, where

is the augmentation ideal. Since the two rings are not isomorphic (for example, the latter contains the
2
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
as a subring, and so is uncountable), the induced map on degree-
0

global sections is not an isomorphism. Therefore,
(\varphi,\varphi^{\#})
is not an isomorphism of ringed spaces.
∎

10.3 Remark
.

Our final example comes from modular representation theory. Fix a finite group
G
and a field

k
of characteristic
p
dividing

. Let
\mathscr{T}\coloneqq K(\Inj kG)

be the homotopy category of complexes of injective

\infty

\mathscr{T}\simeq\Mod_{{\mathscr{S}p}_{G}}(\underline{k}),

where

denotes the Borel-equivariant

-spectrum associated to

\mathscr{T}^{\dual}\simeq\Der^{b}(\Mod kG).

By
[

and

\rho\colon\Spc\bigl(\Der^{b}(\Mod kG)\bigr)\longrightarrow\Spec^{h}\bigl(H^{\bullet}(G,k)\bigr)

has a unique closed

\mathfrak{m}\coloneqq H^{>0}(G,k).

Let
Y_{\mathfrak{m}}\subseteq\Spc(\Der^{b}(\Mod kG))
denote the corresponding singleton.

The tt-completion of
\mathscr{T}
at

identifies with the unbounded derived category

generated. Using

we deduce the following:

10.4 Theorem
.

With notation as above, the formal spectrum

\Spf\bigl(\Der^{b}(\Mod kG)\bigr)

is a one-point ringed space, and its ring of global sections is
H^{\bullet}(G,k)^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}}.

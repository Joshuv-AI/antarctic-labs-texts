Introduction

This paper is a contribution to the understanding of the precise singularity formation
for solutions of superlinear parabolic problems involving
non scale invariant nonlinearities
,
in continuation to the works
[

Consider the following semilinear heat equation

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=f(u),\quad x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
u=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\ t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\end{cases},

For the model cases

\hbox{$f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u$ \quad($p>1$) \quad and \quad$f(u)=e^{u}$},

the blowup behavior has been the subject of extensive research
and is rather well understood, especially in the Sobolev subcritical power case

where a complete classification of blow-up profiles is available.
For

we refer to

,

However the analysis of sharp blow-up asymptotics in these works heavily depends on the exact form of the nonlinearities and their scale invariance properties,
namely the invariance of the equations under the transformations

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{2/(p-1)}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t)\quad(\lambda>0),

and

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t)+\log\lambda\quad(\lambda>0)

respectively (or at least on an asymptotic homogeneity of the nonlinearities as the variable
u\to\infty
).

Let us give a brief review of known results on the blowup behavior of solutions of problem (
1.1
)
for non scale-invariant nonlinearities.
In the early work
[
14
]
,
blow-up rate estimates were obtained for time increasing positive solutions of (
1.1

For suitable classes of non scale-invariant nonlinearities,
results on blow-up sets were obtained in
[

In
[

for problem (
1.1

f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u\log(2+u^{2})
,
blowup rate estimates were obtained (without sign or monotonicity assumption),
and special solutions with a prescribed blowup profile were constructed
(see also
[
21
,
34
]
for blowup rate estimates for the related nonlinear wave equations).
In
[
7
,
8
,
33
,
37
]
, positive solutions were studied for problem (
1.1
) with general regularly varying nonlinearities,
of the form
f(u)=u^{p}L(u)
with

of slow variation (see (
2.4
) for precise definition).
In
[
33
,
37
]
,
parabolic Liouville type theorems were obtained, along with applications to universal bounds and blowup rate estimates.
An analogue of the classical Giga-Kohn result was obtained in
[

using i.a. the type I blowup estimate from
[
37
]
.
Moreover, building on the result in
[

, the final and space-time blow-up profile of positive radially decreasing solutions
was obtained in
[

, revealing
a

of the blow-up profile, robust with respect to the factor

.

In this paper, we are interested in non-scale invariant nonlinearities with exponential growth at infinity.
We note that, concerning blowup rate estimates, the results in
[

]
and in the recent work
[

cover such nonlinearities (under suitable assumptions).
Our main goal here is to provide a very precise description of the blowup asymptotics,
including space-time and final blowup profiles,
for radially decreasing solutions of problem (

for a large class of exponentially growing nonlinearities without scale invariance.
Our analysis follows in part that in our previous works

2
Main results

Assume

f\in C^{1}([0,\infty)),\quad f(0)\geq 0,\quad\hbox{$f$ is $C^{2}$ and positive for large $s$.}

(2.1)

By standard theory,
for
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
u_{0}\geq 0
, problem (
1.1
) has a unique, nonnegative classical solution, of maximal existence time
T=T(u_{0})\in(0,\infty],
that we will denote by
u
throughout this paper.
If
1/f
is integrable at infinity and
u_{0}
is suitably large, then
u
blows up in finite time, i.e
T<\infty
and

\lim_{t\to T}\|u(t)\|_{\infty}=\infty.

In this case,
T
is called the blowup time of
u
. Given
a\in\overline{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say that
a
is a blowup point of
u
if there exists
(a_{j},t_{j})\to(a,T)
such that
u(a_{j},t_{j})\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
.

We consider nonlinearities of the form

f(s)=e^{s}L(e^{s}),\quad s\geq 0.

(2.2)

The function
L
will be assumed to satisfy

\frac{sL^{\prime}(s)}{L(s)}=o(\log^{-\alpha}(s))\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Bigl(\frac{sL^{\prime}(s)}{L(s)}\Bigl)^{\prime}=o\Bigl(\frac{1}{s\log s}\Bigr)\quad\hbox{ as $s\to\infty$, \quad for some $\alpha>\frac{1}{2}$.}

(2.3)

Such
L

include logarithms and their powers and iterates, as well as some strongly oscillating functions
(see Remark
3.2
below for typical examples). Note that (
2.3
) is a subclass of the functions with
slow variation
, i.e

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{L(\lambda s)}{L(\lambda)}=1\quad\text{for each }\ s>0

(2.4)

and that, for
L\in C^{1}
near infinity, a sufficient condition for (
2.4
) is
\lim_{s\to\infty}s\frac{L^{\prime}(s)}{L(s)}=0
.

To state our results we introduce the following function

G(X):=\int_{X}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{f(s)}.

(2.5)

Note that, under assumptions (
2.1
)-(
2.3
),
G
is well defined and there exists a large
A>0
such that
G:[A,\infty)\to(0,G(A)]
is decreasing. Moreover,

\psi(t):=G^{-1}(T-t)

(2.6)

is the positive solution of the corresponding ODE, i.e
y^{\prime}(t)=f(y)
, which blows up at
t=T
.
Concerning
u
we will focus on positive solutions that are radial decreasing in space and increasing in time.
To this end, we will assume

\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\Omega=B_{R}
,
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
u_{0}\geq 0
,

(2.7)

u_{0}
radially symmetric, nonincreasing in
r=|x|
, with
u_{0}
nonconstant if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(2.8)

This guarantees that
u(x,t)=u(r,t)\geq 0
with
r=|x|
and
u_{r}\leq 0
.
As for the property
u_{t}\geq 0
, we recall that it is satisfied for instance when
u_{0}\in BC(\bar{\Omega})\cap H^{2}_{loc}(\Omega)
is such that
\Delta u_{0}+f(u_{0})\geq 0
and
u_{0}=0
on
\partial\Omega
(see, e.g.,
[
32
, Proposition 52.19]
).

Our first main result is the following global, refined blowup estimate, valid in the scale of the original variables
(x,t)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume (
2.1
)-(
2.3
), (
2.7
), (
2.8
),
T=T(u_{0})<\infty

and
u_{t}\geq 0
.
Then

u(x,t)=G^{-1}\bigg(T-t+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4|\log|x|^{2}|}\bigg)+o(1)\quad\text{as }(x,t)\to(0,T).

(2.9)

Combining (
2.9
) with the asymptotic formula of
G^{-1}
(see Lemma
4.1
(ii) below),
we obtain the explicit blow-up asymptotics of
u
, which takes the form

u(x,t)=-\log\bigg(T-t+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4|\log|x|^{2}|}\bigg)-\log\Bigg\{L\bigg(\frac{1}{T-t+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4|\log|x|^{2}|}}\bigg)\Bigg\}+o(1)\quad\text{as }(x,t)\to(0,T).

The latter displays the precise influence of the factor
L
on the profile.
For instance for the typical case

f(u)=u^{q}e^{u},\quad q\geq 1,

corresponding to
L(s)=(\log s)^{q}
, we obtain

u(x,t)=-\log\bigg(T-t+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4|\log|x|^{2}|}\bigg)-q\log\Bigg\{-\log\bigg(T-t+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4|\log|x|^{2}|}\bigg)\Bigg\}+o(1)\quad\text{as }(x,t)\to(0,T).

See Remark
3.2
below for more examples.

On the other hand, we note from (
2.9
) that
u
blows up only at
x=0
hence, by standard parabolic estimates, the limit
u(x,T):=\lim_{t\to T}u(x,t)
exists
for all
x\neq 0
. As direct consequence of Theorem
2.1
, we obtain the final blow-up profile and the corresponding refined space-time profile of the solution.

Corollary 2.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, the following holds:

(i)

(Final profile estimate)

u(x,T)=G^{-1}\Bigg(\frac{|x|^{2}}{4|\log|x|^{2}|}\Bigg)\quad\text{as }x\to 0.

(2.10)

(ii)

(Refined space-time profile estimate)

u(\xi\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|},t)=G^{-1}\bigg[(T-t)\bigg(1+\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4}\bigg)\bigg]+o(1)\quad\text{as }\ t\to T

(2.11)

uniformly for
\xi
bounded.

In the special case of the pure exponential, we have
G^{-1}(Y)=-\log Y
for
Y>0
and Corollary
2.1
recovers the known final and refined profiles (cf.
[
2
,
12
,
23
,
39
]
).
As for the global profile in Theorem
2.1
, it seems new even for the pure exponential
and completes the upper estimate that was obtained in
[
36
]
.

In the course of the proof of Theorem
2.1
,
we establish the following expansion of
u(y\sqrt{T-t},t)-\psi(t)
with second key order term for bounded
y
.
This is of independent interest
since it gives more precise expansion of the solution than (
2.9
), but only in the more restricted range

|x|=O(\sqrt{T-t})
.
In the special case
f(u)=e^{u}
this result was proved in
[
2
,
12
,
23
]
.
Here and in the rest of this paper, we denote

L^{2}_{\rho}:=\Bigl\{v\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n});\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{2}(y)\rho(y)dy<\infty\Bigr\},\quad H^{1}_{\rho}:=\bigl\{f\in L^{2}_{\rho};\ \nabla f\in L^{2}_{\rho}\bigr\},\quad\rho(y):=e^{-|y|^{2}/4},

(2.12)

and
u
is defined to be
0
outside
\Omega
in case
\Omega=B_{R}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Under assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, we have

u(y\sqrt{T-t},t)-\psi(t)=\frac{2n-|y|^{2}+o(1)}{4|\log(T-t)|},\quad\text{as}\ t\to T,

(2.13)

with convergence in
H^{1}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and uniformly for bounded
y
, where
\psi
is as in (
2.6
).

3
Discussion and outline of proofs

3.1
Discussion

Remark 3.1
.

(Structure of the profile)

The global blow-up profile (
2.9
)
possesses a universal structure, given by the “resolvent”
G^{-1}
of the ODE composed with the
universal time-space building block

T-t+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4|\log|x|^{2}|},

(3.1)

and the latter is not affected by the slowly varying factor
L
of the nonlinearity.
At principal order, the effect of the latter on the profile is reflected only in the application
of the resolvent
G^{-1}
to this building block.

A similar observation was made in
[
8
, Remark
2.1
]
in the case of nonlinearities of the form
f(u)=u^{p}L(u)
.
In turn, we remark that the time-space building block (
3.1
) is the limit of that in
[
8
, formula

(2.10)
]
(in the power case) as
p\to\infty
.

The above also applies for the final and refined space-time profiles and
for the sharp behavior in backward space-time parabolas
(cf. Corollary
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
).

Remark 3.2
.

(Examples)

Typical examples of nonlinearities
f(s)
to which Theorems
2.1
-
2.2
apply are given by:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hbox{\ $\bullet$\ $(s+K)^{q}e^{s}$ for $K>0$ and $q\in\mathbb{R}$, or $K=0$ and $q\geq 1$,}\\
\vskip 2.84526pt\cr&\hbox{\ $\bullet$\ $[\log^{q}(s+K)]e^{s}$ for $K>1$ and $q\in\mathbb{R}$, or $K=1$ and $q\geq 1$}\\
\vskip 2.84526pt\cr&\hbox{\ $\bullet$\ $\exp(s\pm(s+1)^{\nu})$ with $\nu\in(0,1/2)$,}\\
\vskip 2.84526pt\cr&\hbox{\ $\bullet$\ the strongly oscillating functions}\\
&\hbox{\hskip 20.44434pt$(s+1)^{\sin[\log(s+1)]}e^{s}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\exp\bigl[s+(s+1)^{\nu}\cos((s+1)^{\gamma})\bigr],\ \ \nu,\gamma>0,\ \nu+\gamma<1/2$,}\\
\vskip 2.84526pt\cr&\hbox{\ $\bullet$\ $\bigl[1+a\sin\bigl((s+1)^{\nu}\bigr)\bigr]e^{s}$ with $\nu\in(0,1/2)$ and $|a|<1$.}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(3.2)

Indeed, by tedious but elementary computations, one can check that the corresponding functions
L

satisfy condition (
2.3
).
Our results thus cover a large class of exponentially growing, non scale invariant nonlinearities.
However, it so far remains an open problem what is the largest possible class of
f

for which the conclusions of Theorems
2.1
-
2.2
hold.

Remark 3.3
.

(Alternative type I assumption)

For
n\leq 2
, Theorems
2.1
-
2.2
remain valid
(see Remark
7.1
below)
if we replace the time increasing assumption

by

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\leq G^{-1}(T-t)+M,\quad T-\delta<t<T,

(3.3)

for some

.

In any dimension, property (

) is in particular guaranteed if

(see Lemma

, which follows from arguments in
[

, Section 4]
).
However, in higher dimensions, the conclusions of Theorems
2.1
-
2.2

are not true in general under the weaker assumption (

) (see Remark
3.4
(i)).

In the special case

with

satisfying (
2.7
), (
2.8
),
property (
3.3
) is also known to be true when

(see
[
23
, p.133]
) or

(see
[

On the other hand, for

Lemma 4.4

Step 4.

Weighted energy functional and its properties.

The Liapunov functional for equation (

) will be given by the weighted energy defined as follows:

\mathcal{E}[w](s):=E[w](s)+C_{1}s^{-\gamma}

(4.29)

where
\gamma=\alpha-\frac{1}{2}
,

and

Lemma 4.5
.

For all
s>s_{0}
, there exists
M_{0}>0
(independent of
s,y
) such that

\frac{d}{ds}\mathcal{E}[w](s)\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{D(s)}w_{s}^{2}\rho\leq 0

(4.30)

and

\mathcal{E}[w](s)\geq-M_{0}.

(4.31)

Proof.

Fix any

. By parabolic regularity (see, e.g.,
[
7

3.2.
]
for details), we have

D^{2}w,\ (1+|y|)^{-1}w_{s},\ (1+|y|)^{-1}\nabla w_{s}\in BC(\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{s_{1}}),

(4.32)

where
\mathcal{W}_{s_{1}}=\mathcal{W}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(s_{0},s_{1}))
and
BC
denotes the set of bounded continuous functions. Using the exponential decay of
\rho

various integrals and justifies the integrations by parts in the rest of the proof.

First, we compute the variation of the first part

of the energy. We have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\int_{D(s)}w\rho dy

\displaystyle=\int_{D(s)}\rho w_{s}dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial D(s)}w\rho(y\cdot\nu)d\sigma,

(4.33)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\int_{D(s)}e^{w}\rho dy

\displaystyle=\int_{D(s)}\rho w_{s}e^{w}dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial D(s)}e^{w}\rho(y\cdot\nu)d\sigma,

where

denotes the surface measure on
\partial D(s)

and
\nu
the exterior unit normal on
\partial D(s)
.

Now, we control the variation of the term involving
\nabla w
by using integration by parts as follows

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\int_{D(s)}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho

\displaystyle=2\int_{D(s)}\nabla w_{s}\cdot(\rho\nabla w)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial D(s)}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho(y\cdot\nu)d\sigma

\displaystyle=-2\int_{D(s)}w_{s}\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla w)+2\int_{\partial D(s)}\rho w_{s}(\nabla w\cdot\nu)d\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial D(s)}\rho|\nabla w|^{2}(y\cdot\nu)d\sigma,

and then use

w_{s}=-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla w-h(s)
(owing to (
4.15
)) and
\nabla w=(\nabla w\cdot\nu)\nu

on
\partial D(s)

w_{s}(\nabla w\cdot\nu)=-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w|^{2}(y\cdot\nu)-h(s)\nabla w\cdot\nu
, which yields

\frac{d}{ds}\int_{D(s)}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho=-2\int_{D(s)}w_{s}\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla w)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial D(s)}\rho|\nabla w|^{2}(y\cdot\nu)d\sigma-2h(s)\int_{\partial D(s)}\rho\nabla w\cdot\nu d\sigma.

(4.34)

Also, we have
\partial D(s)=e^{s/2}\partial\Omega
hence
\int_{\partial D(s)}d\sigma\leq C(\Omega)e^{s/2}
and
|y|=Re^{s/2}
on
\partial D(s)
.
Consequently, using (

), we get (here and in the rest of the proof,

denotes a generic positive constant independent of

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D(s)}\rho|\nabla w|^{2}|y\cdot\nu|d\sigma\leq

\displaystyle CM_{1}^{2}e^{s/2}\exp\bigl[-(Re^{s/2})^{2}/4\bigr]\leq C\exp\bigl(-ce^{s}\bigr),

(4.35)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D(s)}\rho|\nabla w\cdot\nu|d\sigma\leq

\displaystyle CM_{1}e^{s/2}\exp\bigl[-(Re^{s/2})^{2}/4\bigr]\leq C\exp\bigl(-ce^{s}\bigr),

and using

on

\Big|\int_{\partial D(s)}w\rho(y\cdot\nu)d\sigma-\int_{\partial D(s)}e^{w}\rho(y\cdot\nu)d\sigma\Big|\leq C\psi_{1}(s)e^{s/2}\exp\bigl[-(Re^{s/2})^{2}/4\bigr]\leq C\exp\bigl(-ce^{s}\bigr).

(4.36)

Combining (
4.22
) and (

)-(

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}E[w]

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{D(s)}w_{s}^{2}\rho+\int_{D(s)}w_{s}H(s,y)\rho+C\exp\bigl(-ce^{s}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{D(s)}w_{s}^{2}\rho+\frac{1}{2}\int_{D(s)}H^{2}(s,y)\rho+C\exp\bigl(-ce^{s}\bigr)

(4.38)

It then follows from (

We first recall the proof of Lemma
4.2

[

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

By assumption (
3.8
), there exists
s_{0}>1
such that

\frac{|L^{\prime}(s)|}{L(s)}\leq\frac{1}{s\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s}\quad\hbox{ for all $s\geq s_{0}$.}

(8.1)

Also, since
s\exp(-\frac{1}{8}\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s)\to\infty

as
s\to\infty
, there exists
s_{1}>s_{0}
such that
s\exp(-\frac{1}{8}\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s)>s_{0}
for all
s\geq s_{1}
.
It suffices to prove that, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there holds

\Bigl|\frac{L(\lambda s)}{L(s)}-1\Bigl|\ \leq\varepsilon+4\frac{|\log\lambda|}{\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s}\quad\hbox{ for all $\lambda\in I_{s}$.}

(8.2)

Fix
s\geq s_{1}
and assume for contradiction that there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that (
8.2
) fails.
Since the inequality in (
8.2
) is true for
\lambda
close to
1
by continuity of
L
,
there must exist
\lambda_{0}\in I_{s}\setminus\{1\}
such that

\Bigl|\frac{L(\lambda s)}{L(s)}-1\Bigl|\ \leq\varepsilon+4\frac{|\log\lambda|}{\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s}\ \hbox{ for all $\lambda\in(a,b)$\quad and }\quad\Bigl|\frac{L(\lambda_{0}s)}{L(s)}-1\Bigl|\ =\varepsilon+4\frac{|\log\lambda_{0}|}{\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s},

(8.3)

where either
a=\lambda_{0}<b=1
or
a=1<b=\lambda_{0}
.
On the other hand, using the elementary inequality
|(1+h)^{1-\alpha}-1|\leq 2(1-\alpha)|h|
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
h\in(-1,1)
, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{as}^{bs}\frac{dz}{z\log^{\alpha}z}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha)^{-1}\bigl|\log^{1-\alpha}(bs)-\log^{1-\alpha}(as)\bigr|=(1-\alpha)^{-1}\bigl|\bigl(\log s\pm|\log\lambda_{0}|\bigr)^{1-\alpha}-\log^{1-\alpha}s\bigr|

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha)^{-1}\log^{1-\alpha}s\Bigl|\Bigl(1\pm\frac{|\log\lambda_{0}|}{\log s}\Bigr)^{1-\alpha}-1\Bigr|\leq 2\frac{|\log\lambda_{0}|}{\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s}.

It then follows from (
8.1
), (
8.3
)
and
\lambda_{0}\in I_{s}\setminus\{1\}
that

\displaystyle 4\frac{|\log\lambda_{0}|}{\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s}

\displaystyle<\Bigl|\frac{L(\lambda_{0}s)}{L(s)}-1\Bigl|\ =\frac{|L(\lambda_{0}s)-L(s)|}{L(s)}\leq\int_{as}^{bs}\frac{|L^{\prime}(z)|}{L(s)}dz\leq\int_{as}^{bs}\frac{L(z)}{L(s)}\frac{dz}{z\log^{\alpha}z}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(1+\varepsilon+4\frac{|\log\lambda_{0}|}{\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s}\Bigl)\int_{as}^{bs}\frac{dz}{z\log^{\alpha}z}<2\Bigl(\frac{3}{2}+4\frac{|\log\lambda_{0}|}{\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s}\Bigl)\frac{|\log\lambda_{0}|}{\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s}\leq 4\frac{|\log\lambda_{0}|}{\log^{\alpha}{\hskip-2.5pt}s}:

a contradiction.
∎

We next give a proof of Lemma
4.6
, that we used in the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
We note that our proof is significantly simpler than that of
[
3
, Lemma 4.2(a)]
,
owing to the introduction of the auxiliary function
Z

Proof of Lemma
4.6
.

Assume for contradiction that
\phi\not\equiv 0
, hence
\phi(0)>0
by local uniqueness for problem (
4.41
).
By direct calculation, we have

g^{\prime\prime}+c(r)g^{\prime}+F(\phi)g=0,\quad g(0)>0,\ g^{\prime}(0)=0.

(8.4)

Since
g\geq 0
by assumption and
g\not\equiv 0
,
local uniqueness for (
8.4
) yields that actually
g>0
on
[0,\infty)
.
Set

Z:=e^{2\phi}(ge^{-\phi})^{\prime}=e^{\phi}(g^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}g).

Using (
4.41
) and (
8.4
), we compute, for
r>0
:

\displaystyle e^{-\phi}Z^{\prime}=\phi^{\prime}(g^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}g)+(g^{\prime\prime}-\phi^{\prime\prime}g-\phi^{\prime}g^{\prime})=-{\phi^{\prime}}^{2}g-cg^{\prime}-F(\phi)g+g(c\phi^{\prime}+F(\phi))=-{\phi^{\prime}}^{2}g-c(g^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}g).

Letting
\mu(r)=r^{n-1}e^{-r^{2}/4}
, we deduce

(\mu Z)^{\prime}=\mu(Z^{\prime}+cZ)=-\mu{\phi^{\prime}}^{2}ge^{\phi}\leq 0,\quad r>0.

(8.5)

Note that
\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)=-n^{-1}F(\phi(0))<0
, hence
\phi^{\prime}<0
for
r>0
small.
Since
Z(0)=0
, integrating (
8.5
) and using also
g>0
and
e^{\phi}
nonincreasing,
we obtain, for some constant
C>0
,

Z(r)=-\mu^{-1}(r)\int_{0}^{r}\bigl[\mu{\phi^{\prime}}^{2}ge^{\phi}\bigr](s)ds\leq-Cr^{1-n}e^{r^{2}/4}e^{\phi(r)},\quad r\geq 1,

hence

(ge^{-\phi})^{\prime}(r)=[e^{-2\phi}Z](r)\leq-Cr^{1-n}e^{r^{2}/4}e^{-\phi(r)}\leq-Cr^{1-n}e^{-\phi(0)}e^{r^{2}/4},\quad r\geq 1.

But this implies
(ge^{-\phi})(r)\to-\infty
as
r\to\infty
, contradicting
g\geq 0
.
∎

We end this section by proving Lemma
6.2
used in the proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

Integrating by parts, for
X
large enough, we have

Q(X)=\int_{X}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s^{2}L(s)}=\frac{X^{-1}}{L(X)}-\int_{X}^{\infty}\frac{s^{-1}L^{\prime}(s)}{L^{2}(s)}ds.

By the first part of (
6.17
), we deduce
Q(X)-\frac{1}{XL(X)}=o(Q(X))
, hence

Q(X)\sim\frac{1}{XL(X)}\qquad\hbox{as }X\to\infty.

(8.6)

Integrating by parts a second time yields

\displaystyle Q(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{X^{-1}}{L(X)}+\int_{X}^{\infty}Q^{\prime}(s)\frac{sL^{\prime}(s)}{L(s)}ds=\frac{X^{-1}}{L(X)}-\frac{XL^{\prime}(X)}{L(X)}Q(X)-\int_{X}^{\infty}Q(s)\Bigl(\frac{sL^{\prime}(s)}{L(s)}\Bigl)^{\prime}ds,

hence

\bigl(XL(X)+X^{2}L^{\prime}(X)\bigr)Q(X)-1=\mathcal{J}(X):=-XL(X)\int_{X}^{\infty}Q(s)\Bigl(\frac{sL^{\prime}(s)}{L(s)}\Bigl)^{\prime}ds.

By the second part of (
6.17
) and (
8.6
), we have

\mathcal{J}(X)=o\bigl(XL(X)\mathcal{\tilde{J}}(X)\bigr)\ \ \hbox{as $X\to\infty$,\quad where }\mathcal{\tilde{J}}(X):=\int_{X}^{\infty}\frac{s^{-2}}{L(s)\log s}ds.

To estimate
\mathcal{\tilde{J}}(X)
, for all
Y>X
, integrating by parts, we write

\int_{X}^{Y}\frac{s^{-2}}{L(s)\log s}ds=\Bigl[\frac{-s^{-1}}{L(s)\log s}\Bigr]_{X}^{Y}-\int_{X}^{Y}s^{-2}\frac{s(L(s)\log s)^{\prime}}{(L(s)\log s)^{2}}ds.

Next using
\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{s(L(s)\log s)^{\prime}}{L(s)\log s}=\lim_{s\to\infty}\bigl(\frac{sL^{\prime}(s)}{L(s)}+\frac{1}{\log s}\bigr)=0
, we get

\left|\int_{X}^{Y}\frac{s^{-2}}{L(s)\log s}ds+\Bigl[\frac{s^{-1}}{L(s)\log s}\Bigr]_{X}^{Y}\right|\leq\varepsilon(X)\int_{X}^{Y}\frac{s^{-2}}{L(s)\log s}ds.

Since the first part of (
6.17
) implies

\frac{1}{L(s)}=o(s^{\eta})\ \ \hbox{as $s\to\infty$,\ for any $\eta>0$,}

we may let
Y\to\infty
to deduce

\mathcal{\tilde{J}}(X)\sim\frac{X^{-1}}{L(X)\log X}
,
hence
\mathcal{J}(X)=o(1/\log X)
, as
X\to\infty
. This proves the lemma.
∎

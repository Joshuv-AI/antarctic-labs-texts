Comparison of the effect of self-care education with two methods, teach-back and smartphone application, on the adherence to treatment in patients with inflammatory bowel disease
Authors: Zahra Sadat Manzari, Mohammad Sajjad Ghaderi, Hassan Vossoughinia, Hossein Rafiei, Mohamad Hossein Mafi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Self-care is one of the basic principles in the management of chronic diseases, which influences follow-up and adherence to treatment. Therefore, the current study was conducted with the aim of comparing the effect of teach-back (TB) and a smartphone application on adherence to treatment in patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD).Methods:The current clinical trial was conducted among 80 patients with IBD in Mashhad, Iran, in 2021–2022. Self-care education (diet, personal and social relationships, medications, sleep, physical activity, sexual relationships, etc.) was provided through TB method in one group and by using a smartphone application in another group. The control group only received the routine education. A checklist for demographic information and the adherence questionnaire in patients with chronic diseases were used for data collection.Results:Patients’ mean age was 38.73 ± 10.32 years. The majority of patients had ulcerative colitis (81%) and were married (67%). Mean and standard deviation score of adherence to treatment were the same in all three groups before the intervention (P = 0.668). The mean post-test scores of adherence to treatment in the TB, application, and control groups were 170.04 ± 14.19, 167.99 ± 11.59, and 159.60 ± 10.94, respectively. The difference was statistically significant (P = 0.003). A significant difference was observed in regards to the mean post-test scores of adherence to treatment between TB and control groups (P = 0.004) and app and control groups (P = 0.048). However, the difference between TB and app groups was not significant (P = 0.989).Conclusions:TB method and smartphone application have the same effect on adherence to treatment in patients with IBD. Due to the usability and cost-effectiveness of smartphone applications, this method can be used by health-care providers to educate this group of patients.

INTRODUCTION

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic disease that is expanding throughout the world, especially in Asia, in the 21st century. Affected patients are often young and require long-term treatments.[12] The prevalence of IBD in the world increased by 47% from 1990 to 2019. The number of cases of IBD increased from 3.32 million to 4.90 million.[3] According to statistics, the prevalence of IBD in the Middle East and Iran is expected to increase by 2.3 and 2.5 times, respectively, in 2035 compared to 2020.[4] The annual incidence and prevalence of IBD is also reported to be increasing in Iran. The prevalence of this disease in northeast Iran has increased 7–10 times in a period of almost 20 years.[5] Patients with IBD may experience different clinical manifestations, including bloody diarrhea with or without mucus, overt or hidden rectal bleeding, abdominal pain, fecal incontinence, fever, and weight loss.[6] Its frequent recurrence of symptoms as well as complications double the decline in patients’ quality of life. The symptoms, in turn, can lead to functional impairment in patients, which usually affects their daily activities, school performance, work ability, and social life.[7] In the long term, other organs of the body are exposed to extraintestinal complications, including arthritis, kidney disease, uveitis, erythema nodosum, ankylosing spondyloarthropathy, stomatitis aphthous, and gallstones.[8] Timely treatment and follow-up, in addition to reducing these recurrence intervals, are highly recommended to prevent the complications.[1] The main key to prevent such complications in IBD patients is adherence to treatment.[9]

Adherence to treatment, in addition to accurately taking medications, refers to attending scheduled appointments with a physician, following up the treatments and undergoing tests, and executing appropriate behaviors or a healthy lifestyle.[10] Poor management of the disease and nonadherence to treatment can be associated with increased risk of recurrence and colorectal cancer, decreased quality of life, and ultimately mortality. In addition, nonadherence to treatment can impose costs on patients and health care systems, which is an additional economic burden on societies.[19] Previous studies reported nonadherence to treatment in IBD patients to be 40%–72%, which causes exacerbation and recurrence of the disease.[911] Like in other chronic diseases, adherence to treatment is not always followed correctly by IBD patients. Hence, self-care education is considered as an important treatment program in this regard.[12] Educating patients to take care of themselves has a positive effect on taking medications, following up the medical treatments, and executing a healthy lifestyle, which are related to adherence to treatment.[13] Therefore, self-care education with the aim of promoting self-care behaviors in patients can probably improve their adherence to treatment and management of their diseases.[14]

Regarding self-care education, individuals may be educated via different methods. One of these methods, which has aroused interest and motivation in individuals to learn and increase their adherence to treatment, is the teach-back (TB) interactive educational method.[15] TB is an efficient method to improve patients’ learning and immediate understanding of the educational content during discharge. Through this method, patients can easily obtain the required information about their diseases.[16] The effectiveness of TB among a wide range of populations and on ensuring that patients completely learn the information are among the advantages of this method. Despite the limitations such as the need for a large number of workers, physical space, and a lot of time for training, TB has been able to play an effective role in the management of chronic diseases and has shown positive results in patients.[1718] Lack of continuity of education and need for updating educational concepts can also be considered as other limitations of this method.[19]

Another method which has improved some of the limitations of traditional methods is the use of a smartphone application (app). This method is associated with positive results for health-care systems as well as patients.[20] For patients with IBD, adoption of mobile health strategies can promote improved quality of care delivery and clinical outcomes.[21] Apps are effective tools in improving patients’ knowledge and skills. They can be introduced as an effective method in patient education on account of their low expense, high versatility, online or offline application at any time and in any place, easy learning, etc.[22] Moreover, creating a proper partnership between physician and patient is one of the other capabilities of the apps that can be achieved remotely and without physical presence. This can facilitate the decision-making process regarding the treatment of the disease.[23] Despite these well-known benefits of apps for the management of IBD, development of such apps by medical teams, based on evidence-based guidelines, is still rare and their use is not common among IBD patients.[232425]

Considering that patient education plays a key role in improving patients’ adherence to treatment, it is necessary to adopt the best educational method for them. A comparison done between the best traditional educational methods (TB) and new methods (app) can help health-care systems in allocating human and financial resources to develop an effective educational method. Despite the importance of adherence to treatment in IBD patients, no similar study has been carried out in Iran. In addition, despite the fact that TB and apps have advantages and disadvantages, a comparison of the effectiveness of these two methods has not been done. Therefore, the current study was conducted with the aim of comparing the effect of TB and an app on adherence to treatment in IBD patients.

PATIENTS AND METHODS

Participants

The current three-arm randomized clinical trial was conducted among 80 IBD patients in 2021-2022. The study setting was a gastroenterologist’s office in Mashhad, Iran. The inclusion criteria of the study patients were: age ≥18 years, diagnosed with Crohn’s disease or ulcerative colitis by a specialist or based on medical records, able to read and write to complete the questionnaire, and had access to a smartphone (in app group). Those patients who did not complete the questionnaire (>5% of the items), had other chronic diseases, or had a mental illness diagnosed by a specialist were excluded.

Sample size

The mean differences formula was used to determine the sample size. For this purpose, a 95% confidence level and an 80% test power were considered. Given the lack of a similar study, a pilot sample was also used. Ten patients were recruited in each group. Based on the mean and standard deviation score of the main outcome and a 10% dropout rate, the sample size was calculated to be 28 in each group (84 in total).

Study design

The educational content was first prepared by widely reviewing the scientific texts, evidence-based websites, scientific databases, and reputable scientific associations, as well as consulting with specialists and experts. It was then validated by experts and adopted to the cultural and social conditions of Iranian patients. The content was categorized according to the self-care areas of the disease (information about diet, personal and social relationships, medications, sleep, physical activity, vaccination, smoking, sexual relationships, follow-ups, and periodic monitoring). In the app group, the content was presented in a multimedia form (video, photo, text, and voice) to be loaded in an app. The android app was designed as a hybrid app. It comprised several features such as self-care recommendations, medication reminders, medical monitoring (tests, visits, etc.), a section to contact the physician, etc. Before using the app, it was validated through the black box and white box tests. Its quality was also evaluated from the users’ point of view by the mobile application rating scale. Based on random allocation, educational content was then provided to each group of app and TB in a specific way. It should be noted that the educational content was the same in both groups and only the teaching method was different.

TB group: Two individual teaching sessions on two different days were held for patients in this group. The maximum length of each session was 60 min. The time interval between the two sessions was 3–5 days. Due to the spread of the coronavirus disease and patients’ unwillingness for in-person sessions, the teaching sessions were held virtually through video calls. In each session, the steps of the TB method were followed. The content was asked at the end of each session, and if the patient did not understand a section, that section was taught again. At the end of each session, the text was provided to the patients by email.

App group: The app was installed for patients in this group and they were taught on how to use its different sections. This took 20 min. An educational package on the features of the app, embedded as a video, was also sent to the patients by email/social networks. They used the app for the next 30 days, and could answer their questions about the disease through the app. The researchers also contacted the patients every week to remind them to use the app and answer their questions.

Control group: No education was provided to patients in this group at the time of intervention by the research team, and they only received the routine education (face-to-face education, pamphlets, etc.) provided at the time of diagnosis or visits by health-care providers.

Randomization

This parallel, randomized clinical trial was conducted in three groups. Patients were allocated to the groups using a simple blocked randomization. For this purpose, blocks of six subjects (two from each group) were prepared. The series were like AABBCC, ABABCC, ABCABD, etc., There were two subjects from each group in all blocks. The sequence of allocations was determined by randomly choosing one of the series (sequences of six blocks). Sealed envelopes were used to hide the allocations. In this study, only the statistical analyst was blinded.

Outcomes measurement

For data collection, a checklist for demographic information and the adherence questionnaire in patients with chronic diseases were used. All patients completed the questionnaire once before the intervention and once 1 month after installing the app in the app group, 1 month after the last teaching session in the TB group, and 1 month after the pre-test in the control group.

The demographic checklist included questions on patients’ age, disease duration, sex, marital status, education, family history, surgical history, etc.

In this study, the adherence questionnaire in patients with chronic diseases was used to assess adherence to treatment. This scale consists of 40 items and contains the following seven factors: making effort for treatment (nine items), intention to take the treatment (seven items), adaptability (seven items), integrating illness into life (five items), stick to the treatment (four items), commitment to treatment (five items), and indecisiveness for applying treatment (three items). Answers were based on a 6-item Likert scale from completely (5 points) to never (1 point). Items 33, 34, 35, 37, 38, 39, and 40 are scored inversely. Hence, the higher the total score or the score of each factor, the higher the adherence to treatment.[26] In Seyed Fatemi et al.’s[27] study, the content validity index of this questionnaire was 0.91 and Cronbach’s alpha coefficient in a sample of 120 adults with chronic diseases was 0.92. In the current study, the validity of the questionnaire was confirmed by 10 professors of Mashhad University of Medical Sciences. For reliability, Cronbach’s alpha coefficient was reported to be 0.89.

Statistical analysis

Frequency (percentage) and mean (standard deviation) were used to describe data. Kolmogorov–Smirnov and Shapiro–Wilk tests were used to assess the normal distribution of data. Chi-square, Chi-square exact, and Kruskal–Wallis tests were used to determine the differences between the three groups with regard to demographic data. Kruskal–Wallis and Dunn’s tests were used to determine the differences between the three groups with regard to the mean scores of adherence to treatment. Paired t-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test were used to determine the differences between the mean pre- and post-test scores of adherence to treatment in each group. Data were analyzed using International Business Machine Statistical Package for the Social Sciences Statistics for Windows, version 25. P <0.05 was considered significant in all tests.

Ethical considerations

The current study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Mashhad University of Medical Sciences with the code of ethics of IR.MUMS.REC.1399.451. In this study, the objectives of the study and the method of implementation were explained to the participants before taking any action. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. Participants were assured of voluntary participation in the study and confidentiality of the obtained information. All participants had the right to withdraw from the study at any time.

Patients’ mean age and duration of disease were 38.73 ± 10.32 and 8.93 ± 3.68 years, respectively. The majority of patients had ulcerative colitis (81%) and were married (67%). DISCUSSION

Adherence to treatment is one of the most important concepts in the control and management of IBD. The results of the current study showed that both self-care education methods (TB and app) were almost equally effective in enhancing adherence to treatment.

In the current study, TB was able to enhance adherence to treatment among IBD patients. This finding is in line with the results of Azizi et al.[28] In their study, it was reported that TB can enhance adherence to treatment among acute coronary syndrome patients. In addition, in a systematic review, it was reported that this method has positive effects on adherence to treatment in patients with chronic diseases and can enhance the adherence.[15] Therefore, it was consistent with the current study. Number of training sessions and almost similar interventions in the communities of chronic patients can be considered as one of the causes of alignment. TB is a suitable method to ensure whether patients learn the educational content correctly or not. This method is also introduced in nursing texts and fundamentals as it is an effective method for patient education.[29] In the current study, this method showed significant improvements in adherence to treatment among IBD patients. The application of this effective teaching method is suggested for nurses in the hospitals for patient education during hospitalization and discharge.

In the current study, the app was also able to enhance adherence to treatment among IBD patients. In a systematic review, Xu and Long[30] reported that a smartphone intervention leads to a reduction in blood pressure and an increase in medication adherence for patients with hypertension. In Li et al.’s[31] study in patients with chronic diseases, an app was able to enhance adherence to treatment. It also enhanced disease management even for patients with multiple chronic diseases. These results are in line with the findings of the current study. This is attributed to the methodological similarities and the same educational method used. Since IBD is a chronic disease and affected patients are required to execute self-care behaviors and consider adherence to treatment throughout their lives, this method can be very helpful. Nevertheless, it is mentioned in the above studies and in other studies[3233] that further studies are needed to provide high-quality evidence. In addition, it is better to investigate the effectiveness of apps on adherence to treatment over a longer period. This leads to determining the effectiveness of apps on the persistence of health behaviors. In the current study, the mean pre-test score of adherence to treatment was high. Hence, the application of this method in new patients who do not have much information about their disease seems to be associated with better results.

On comparison of the two teaching methods (TB and app), the results showed that both the methods were almost equally effective in enhancing adherence to treatment. This finding is in line with the results of Ghorbani et al.[34] In their study, the effects of app and TB on adherence to a therapeutic regimen were examined in patients after coronary artery bypass graft surgery. The results showed that there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups. In both studies, the post-test was taken 30 days after the intervention. It seems that since the updated educational content is continuously available to patients at any time and in any place, the advantages of the app are focused on its lasting effect. Hence, following up adherence to treatment in future studies in longer periods of time can provide a better opportunity to judge the effectiveness of these two teaching methods. In a similar study, Negarandeh et al.[35] compared the impact of TB and pictorial image educational strategies on knowledge, adherence to medication, and diet among patients with type 2 diabetes. In line with the findings of the current study, both educational strategies showed significant improvements with regard to knowledge as well as adherence to medications and diet compared to the control group. In Zabolypour et al.’s[36] study, the effects of TB and motivational interviewing on adherence to medical regimen in patients with hypertension were compared. In line with the findings of the current study, no significant difference was found with regard to the mean scores of adherence to the medical regimen between the two groups. Although one of the intervention groups in the aforementioned study was different from the current study, no similar study showing that TB and app are different from each other with regard to adherence to treatment was found, and previous studies reported that both the methods are almost equally effective in the short-term follow-ups.

It seems that in the near future, the use of apps in patient education will face a lot of changes. Combining artificial intelligence with the app provides more possibilities for education.[37] Categorizing information and analyzing and presenting the results of the analysis have been one of the important actions of artificial intelligence in the field of disease detection and management. In recent years, efforts have been made to use artificial intelligence to diagnose the condition and manage symptoms in IBD.[38] Integrating artificial intelligence with new training can provide personalized training to each person. which facilitates remote patient education and acts as a permanent caregiver for the patient.[39] The development of artificial intelligence in the educational app for patients with IBD should be considered in other studies.

Implications for practice

Based on the results, both methods are recommended for clinical staff to educate patients. Perhaps one method cannot be statistically superior to another. According to their facilities and resources, managers can choose a method as the policy of their medical organization for educating the patient. However, due to the lack of resources and health-care personnel in most countries, the app method seems to be more cost-effective. In addition, due to the chronic nature of the disease and involvement with the disease until the end of life, updated training should be provided to the patient continuously. Therefore, using the app method in training during discharge is also important, and by using the features of the app, it is possible to provide patient follow-up. Of course, the medical staff, while performing therapeutic interventions in the hospital, are faced with questions from the patient that require immediate training for the patient. In this situation, it is better to provide education with the TB method to ensure correct understanding by the patient.

Limitations

The outbreak of the coronavirus disease made conducting in-person classes difficult, and these sessions were held through video calls. In addition, because some patients did not prefer going to hospital clinics or some clinics were closed during the pandemic, sampling was done only from one physician’s office. Due to such limitations, the post-test was taken 30 days after the intervention. It is recommended that future studies consider a longer follow-up period, so that the lasting effects of the interventions can also be examined.

In conclusion, the results of this study showed the effectiveness of TB and app in enhancing adherence to treatment among IBD patients. In addition, no significant difference was found between the two methods in this regard. Based on these findings as well as the facilities available in the health-care systems, it is recommended that one of these methods be used to improve adherence to treatment among patients with chronic diseases, especially among IBD patients. However, it seems that benefits such as distance education, interesting and multimedia content, saving manpower, the possibility of updating the educational content, availability at any time and in any place, and lower expenses make apps more prominent than TB.

Symbolic Reduction of Multi-loop Feynman Integrals via Generating Functions
Bo Feng a,b,c,d, Xiang Li e, Yuanche Liu g, Yan-Qing Ma e,f, Yang Zhang g,d,f

Abstract
We introduce a novel, systematic method for the complete symbolic reduction of multi-loop Feynman integrals, leveraging the power of generating functions. The differential equations governing these generating functions naturally yield symbolic recurrence relations. We develop an efficient algorithm that utilizes these recurrences to reduce integrals to a minimal set of master integrals. This approach circumvents the exponential growth of traditional Integration-by-Parts relations, enabling the reduction of high-rank, multi-loop integrals critical for state-of-the-art calculations in perturbative quantum field theory.

Introduction

Loop-level Feynman diagrams play a central role in perturbative quantum field theories (QFTs). In the era of precision physics, The current era of precision physics presents an urgent demand for the calculation of multi-loop amplitudes, which are crucial for collider phenomenology, gravitational wave predictions from black hole scattering, as well as formal aspects of QFT. In multi-loop computations, a key step is to reduce all Feynman integrals to a finite, linearly independent set consisting of master integrals. In this work, we present a novel and highly efficient method, based on generating functions

;
Guan et al. 2023

By expressing Feynman integrals in terms of generating functions, integration-by-parts (IBP) relations are translated into differential equations for these generating functions. The reduction of Feynman integrals is then transformed into the task of reducing a differential operator with respect to these differential equations. We introduce a new approach for differential operator reduction, based on operator grading and integrability conditions, which allows us to efficiently tobtain symbolic reduction rules for arbitrary Feynman integrals.

This paper is organized as follows. We first review the definition of generating functions for Feynman integral reduction. We then focus on our new systematic method for obtaining symbolic reduction rules. As illustrations, we apply this method to three multi-loop reduction examples: the sunset diagram, and planar and non-planar double-box diagrams.

During the preparation of this manuscript, we became aware of the recent paper
Smith and Zeng 2025

II
Generating Functions for Feynman integrals

For
L
-loop integrals with
E
independent external momenta, a family of scalar FIs can be represented as

\displaystyle I(\vec{\nu})=\int\prod_{i=1}^{L}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{D}\ell_{i}}{\mathrm{i}\pi^{D/2}}\frac{\mathcal{D}_{K+1}^{-\nu_{K+1}}\cdots\mathcal{D}_{N}^{-\nu_{N}}}{\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\nu_{1}}\cdots\mathcal{D}_{K}^{\nu_{K}}},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

where

\ell_{i}

\mathcal{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{K}
are the inverse propagators,
\mathcal{D}_{K+1},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{N}

In (
1

\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{K}
can be arbitrary integers (i.e, positive, negative or zero), while
\nu_{K+1},\ldots,\nu_{N}

\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{K}>0

To organize all integrals within a sector, we introduce a generating function defined as:

\displaystyle G_{\vec{\mu}}(\eta)=\int\prod_{i=1}^{L}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{D}\ell_{i}}{\mathrm{i}\pi^{D/2}}{e^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1-\mu_{j})\eta_{j}s_{0}^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{j}}\over\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\mathcal{D}_{i}-s_{0}\eta_{i})^{\mu_{i}}},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

(2)

\vec{\mu}=(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{N})

\mu_{i}=0,1

s_{0}
is an auxiliary scale that make
\eta_{i}

\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{K}=1
and
\mu_{K+1},\cdots,\mu_{N}=0

can be expanded as:

\displaystyle G_{\vec{\mu}}(\vec{\eta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{n}\geq 0}\vec{\eta}^{\vec{n}}F_{\vec{n}},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

(3)

\displaystyle F_{\vec{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=K+1}^{N}{1\over n_{j}!}\right)s_{0}^{\sum_{i=1}^{K}n_{i}-\sum_{j=K+1}^{N}n_{j}}

(4)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle I(...,n_{K}+1,-n_{K+1},...,-n_{N})\penalty\ .

The formalism (

\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}

Since the generating function encodes all Feynman integrals in the family, it is sufficient to construct IBP relations for the generating function alone, which we call as the fundamental IBP relations. These relations yield DEs of the form:

\displaystyle\sum_{t}c_{t}\hat{O}_{t}G_{\vec{\mu}}(\vec{\eta})=B\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \hat{O}_{t}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\eta_{i}^{(\vec{a}_{t})_{i}}{\partial^{(\vec{b}_{t})_{i}}\over\partial\eta_{i}^{(\vec{b}_{t})_{i}}\penalty\ },\penalty\

(5)

where
B
is the function of generating functions of subsectors and

are coefficients. We define the
degree of an operator
as
\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\vec{o})_{i}
, where the index vector is
\vec{o}\equiv\vec{b}-\vec{a}
. By substituting the expansion (
3
) to equation (
5

\vec{\eta}^{\vec{n}}

\displaystyle\sum_{t}c_{t}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{(\vec{b}_{t})_{j}}(n_{j}-(\vec{a})_{j}+p)\right)F_{\vec{n}+\vec{b}_{t}-\vec{a}_{t}}=\widetilde{B},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

(6)

where for simplicity, we have made the convention that
F_{\vec{n}}=0
if any component
(\vec{n})_{i}<0

We call equation (
6

recurrence relation
derived from the DE (
5

Within this framework, the goal of symbolic reduction is transformed into an algebraic problem: expressing a higher-degree operator as a linear combination of lower-degree operators, using the DEs derived from the fundamental IBP relations. The primary objective of this work is to develop a systematic algorithm to achieve this task.

III
Symbolic Reduction Rules

The DEs derived from fundamental IBP identities can be classified based on the highest-degree operators. This classification directly informs how symbolic reduction rules are extracted:

I:

The equation contains a single, unique highest degree operator, which can be directly solved to yield an immediate reduction rule.

II:

The equation contains multiple highest degree operators that share the same operator index. When translated into the recurrence form (
6
), they effectively leave a single highest-degree term, yielding a reduction rule.

III:

The equation contains multiple highest degree operators with different operator indices. In this case, one operator is chosen as the target to solve, according to the aim of reduction.

Given a reduction rule, i.e, expressing a higher operator index
\vec{o}
by others, we can determine which points can be reduced or can not. They are given by two sets, the reduced set

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\widehat{O}}=\{\vec{n}\in Z^{N}|\vec{n}\geq 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \&\penalty\ \penalty\ \vec{n}-\vec{o}\geq 0\}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

(7)

and unreduced set

\displaystyle{\cal U}_{\widehat{O}}=\{\vec{n}\in Z^{N}|\vec{n}\geq 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \&\penalty\ \penalty\ \exists i,(\vec{n}-\vec{o})_{i}<0\}\penalty\ .\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

(8)

A crucial component of our methodology is the generation of "descendant" rules by acting on an existing DE with differential operators. The complete algorithm is structured as an iterative loop that expands the set of known reduction rules until a complete basis is found.

We now outline the core algorithm for deriving a complete set of symbolic reduction rules:

(A)

Initialization:

Generate an initial set of DEs from the fundamental IBP relations. Extract immediate reduction rules and define the initial reduced set
\mathcal{S}
and unreduced set
\mathcal{U}
. Initialize the maximum degree of differential operators to
M=2
.

(B)

Generation of New Equations:

Create a new set of DEs by applying differential operators
\prod_{i}(\partial/\partial\eta_{i})^{k_{i}}
, up to the current maximum degree
M
, to the existing DEs. Apply all known reduction rules (as well as their descendant reduction rules) to new DEs to eliminate as many as possible highest-degree operators in favor of lower-degree ones, which typically refers to integrability conditions of differential operators. In this way, we get the DE for new unsolved highest operators.

(C)

Finding new reduction rules:

Using the obtained DE to solve new highest operators based on the current unreduced set
\mathcal{U}
. Simplifying new reduction rules
by setting terms in DE’s to zero when applying these reduction rules to the set
\mathcal{U}
.

(D)

Update and Completeness Check:

Based on new found reduction rules, update the reduced set
\mathcal{S}
and unreduced set
\mathcal{U}
. Check if the size of the updated
\mathcal{U}
matches the number of master integrals. If so, the reduction is complete and the algorithm terminates successfully. Else, return to step
(B)
.

(E)

Expand the maximum degree of differential operators:

If steps above failed to produce any new reduction rules, the system has stagnated at the current order
M
. Increment the maximum degree
M\to M+1
, and return to step
(B)
to generate more complex new equations and find new rules.

Our framework offers distinct advantages over existing methods. Compared to methods based on gröbner basis
Tarasov 1998
;
Gerdt 2004
;
Smirnov and Smirnov 2006a
;
Smirnov and Smirnov 2006b
;
Smirnov 2006
, our approach circumvents complex non-commutative algebra. Instead, it systematically reduces the derivation of reduction rules to a more tractable linear algebra problem. Compared to heuristic algorithms
Lee 2012
;
Lee 2014
, our approach provides a general and reusable solution with a clear termination condition. This contrasts sharply with the ad-hoc nature of heuristic strategies, which lack guarantees of completeness and often fail when faced with exceptionally complex topologies.

Furthermore, our method streamlines previous generating function formalisms
Guan et al. 2023
. By employing a single generating function per sector, we avoid the expensive cost of constructing closed differential equations for a large number of master generating functions, which is achieved by Laporta’s algorithm in Ref.
Guan et al. 2023
. And by introducing a novel simplification technique on the unreduced set, we significantly accelerate the generation of recurrence rules and descendant equations, making the entire reduction process more direct and computationally tractable.

Following this general framework, we will present several examples to demonstrate the algorithm of finding complete symbolic reduction rules in the following section. Our analysis will focus exclusively on top-sector integrals, because any integral in sub-sector can be treated as a top-sector integral within a simpler sub-family, defined by the propagators with non-positive powers.

VII
Conclusion and Outlook

In this work, we introduced a novel systematic approach to derive complete symbolic reduction rules for multi-loop Feynman integrals with generating functions. By the DEs for these generating functions, we obtain recurrence relations that enable the efficient reduction without the exponential proliferation of IBP identities in traditional methods. Through explicity examples, including the sunset diagrams (both massive and massless), two-loop double-box, and two-loop non-planar integrals, we demonstrate the algorithm’s effectiveness in complex cases with high-degree numerators and high-power propagators. This method addresses key bottlenecks in perturbative QFT computations.

With the proof of concept in the note, the most important thing is to automate the aforementioned algorithm. In a longer companion paper, more details of the algorithm will be presented. The combination of the idea in the paper and other reduction methods is also worth pursuing.

From Classical to Quantum: Explicit Classical Distributions Achieving Maximal Quantum f f -Divergence
Dimitri Lanier, Julien Béguinot, and Olivier Rioul

Abstract
THIS PAPER IS ELIGIBLE FOR THE STUDENT PAPER AWARD. Explicit classical states achieving maximal f f -divergence are given, allowing for a simple proof of Matsumoto’s Theorem, and the systematic extension of any inequality between classical f f -divergences to quantum f f -divergences. Our methodology is particularly simple as it does not require any elaborate matrix analysis machinery but only basic linear algebra. It is also effective, as illustrated by two examples improving existing bounds: (i) an improved quantum Pinsker inequality is derived between χ 2 \chi^{2} and trace norm, and leveraged to improve a bound in decoherence theory; (ii) a new reverse quantum Pinsker inequality is derived for any quantum f f -divergence, and compared to previous (Audenaert-Eisert and Hirche-Tomamichel) bounds.



I
Introduction

Let
f(x)
be a convex, real-valued function defined for
x\geq 0
and satisfying
f(1)=0
.
We consider the following definition.

Definition 1
(Classical
f
-Divergence
[
1
]
)

Let
p,q
be classical distributions
p=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n})
and
q=(q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{n})
. The
f
-divergence between
p
and
q
is

D_{f}(p\|q)=\sum_{i}f\bigl(\frac{p_{i}}{q_{i}}\bigr)\,q_{i}

(1)

where by convention, summation is over all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
.

As is well known,
f
-divergence is nonnegative and satisfies the data processing inequality

D_{f}(W(p)\|W(q))\leq D_{f}(p\|q)

(2)

for any classical channel
W
.
Classical
f
-divergences have been extensively studied in information theory, and many inequalities between various
f
-divergences have been established.

In quantum information theory, classical probability distributions are replaced by (mixed) quantum states represented by density matrices, and several generalizations of
f
-divergences have been proposed
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
We adopt the following general definition.

Definition 2
(Quantum
f
-Divergence)

Let
\rho,\sigma
denote (mixed) quantum states in a Hilbert space of dimension
n
.
A quantum
f
-divergence
D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)
is any nonnegative measure of
\rho,\sigma
such that

(i)

it coincides with the classical
f
-divergence
D_{f}(p\|q)
when
\rho
and
\sigma
commute (
\rho\sigma=\sigma\rho
) so that they reduce to classical states with eigenvalues
p=\lambda(\rho)
and
q=\lambda(\sigma)
;

(ii)

it satisfies the quantum data processing inequality

D_{f}(\mathcal{W}(\rho)\|\mathcal{W}(\sigma))\leq D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)

(3)

for any quantum (completely positive and trace preserving) channel
\mathcal{W}
.

The
maximal
f
-divergence
, first proposed in
[
5
]
, further studied in
[
6
]
and popularized by Matsumoto
[
7
]
stands out as a particularly important example of quantum
f
-divergence, due to its property of being the only quantum generalization that is maximal among all those satisfying condition (i) above.
The maximal
f
-divergence has many interesting properties, including monotonicity under quantum channels (ii) when
f
is operator convex
[
5
]
, coincidence with quantum
\chi^{2}
divergence for
f(x)=x^{2}-1
, as well as coincidence with trace norm for
f(x)=|x-1|
on a significant set of states
[
7
]
.

In the remainder of this paper, we revisit Matsumoto’s maximal divergence theorem
[
7
]
by providing explicit
classical
distributions achieving maximal
f
-divergence.
In particular, this gives a simple proof of Matsumoto’s Theorem, including the quantum data processing inequality for maximal
f
-divergence, and the possibility of systematically extending any inequality between classical
f
-divergences to quantum
f
-divergences. Our methodology, detailed in Section
II
is particularly simple, and also effective as shown by two applications in Section
III
.

II
Explicit Maximal Divergence Theorem

II-A

Derivation

The main result of the paper revisits Matsumoto’s maximal divergence theorem
[
7
]

by providing explicit classical distributions achieving the maximal
f
-divergence, which allows a simple proof.

Theorem 1

D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\triangleq\Tr\bigl(\sigma^{1/2}f(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2})\sigma^{1/2}\bigr)

(4)

is maximal over all quantum
f
-divergences
D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)
:

D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)

(5)

for any states
\rho,\sigma
(where state
\sigma
is invertible).
Furthermore, there exist classical distributions
r,s
depending only on
\rho,\sigma
(explicit expressions (
8
) given below in the proof) such that

D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)=D_{f}(r\|s).

(6)

Proof:

Let

\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}

(7)

be the spectral decomposition of
\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
and
orthonormal eigenvectors
{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}}
, and define

\displaystyle s_{i}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathinner{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{\sigma}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}}>0

(8)

\displaystyle r_{i}

\displaystyle\triangleq\lambda_{i}s_{i}\geq 0.

Observe that

r=(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})
and
s=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n})
are classical distributions, since

\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}s_{i}=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}\Tr(\sigma{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}})=\Tr(\sigma\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}})=\Tr(\sigma)=1

(9)

and

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}r_{i}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}\lambda_{i}\Tr(\sigma{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}})=\Tr(\sigma\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}\lambda_{i}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}})

(10)

\displaystyle=\Tr(\sigma\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2})=\Tr(\rho)=1.

Also define

A_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{i}}}\sigma^{1/2}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}

(11)

for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
.
The following operator sum representation

\mathcal{V}(\tau)=\sum_{i}A_{i}\tau A_{i}^{\dagger}

(12)

defines a quantum channel
\mathcal{V}
, which is trace preserving since

\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}A_{i}^{\dagger}A_{i}=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{s_{i}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}\sigma{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}=\sum_{i}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}=I.

(13)

Now since the
{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}}
are orthonormal, for any
k\neq i
,
A_{k}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}}=0
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}(\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}s_{i}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}})=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}s_{i}A_{i}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}A_{i}^{\dagger}

(14)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}\sigma^{1/2}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}\sigma^{1/2}=\sigma^{1/2}\sigma^{1/2}=\sigma

and similarly,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(r)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}r_{i}A_{i}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}A_{i}^{\dagger}=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}\lambda_{i}\sigma^{1/2}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}\sigma^{1/2}

(15)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{1/2}(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2})\sigma^{1/2}=\rho.

Therefore, by the quantum data processing inequality (ii) satisfied by
D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)
, one has

D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)=D_{f}(\mathcal{V}(r)\|\mathcal{V}(s))\leq D_{f}(r\|s)

(16)

where

\displaystyle D_{f}(r\|s)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}f\bigl(\frac{r_{i}}{s_{i}}\bigr)s_{i}

(17)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}f\bigl(\lambda_{i}\bigr)\Tr({\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}\sigma)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}f\bigl(\lambda_{i}\bigr){\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}\sigma\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(f(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2})\sigma\bigr),

which proves (
5
) and the Theorem.
∎

II-B

Alternative Expressions and a Few Examples

Notice that from Lemma
1
in the Appendix,

\displaystyle D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(\sigma^{1/2}f(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2})\sigma^{1/2}\bigr)

(18)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(\sigma f(\sigma^{-1}\rho)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(f(\rho\sigma^{-1})\sigma\bigr).

Additionally if
f(x)=x\,g(x)
, using (
59
),

\displaystyle D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(\rho g(\sigma^{-1}\rho)\bigr)

(19)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(\rho g(\rho\sigma^{-1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(\rho^{1/2}g(\rho^{1/2}\sigma^{-1}\rho^{1/2})\rho^{1/2}\bigr).

The symmetrical expressions such as (
4
) are often preferred because the are explicitly written in terms of positive operators
\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\geq 0
,

\rho^{1/2}\sigma^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\geq 0
, etc. We give three examples.

II-B
1
f(x)=x\log(x)

The well-known quantum extension of the Kullback-Leibler divergence
D(p\|q)=\sum_{i}\log(\frac{p_{i}}{q_{i}})\,p_{i}
is the quantum relative entropy

D(\rho\|\sigma)\triangleq\Tr\bigl(\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)\bigr)

(20)

also noted
S(\rho\|\sigma)
. The corresponding maximal
f
-divergence is
D^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)=\Tr\bigl(\rho\log(\rho\sigma^{-1})\bigr)
, or in symmetric form

D^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)=\Tr\bigl(\rho^{1/2}\log(\rho^{1/2}\sigma^{-1}\rho^{1/2})\rho^{1/2}\bigr).

(21)

It was studied in
[
8
]
and plays a role in quantum statistical estimation
[
9
]
.

This maximal divergence is not to be confused with the quantum
max-relative entropy
or
max-divergence

[
10
,
11
]
:

D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\triangleq\log\lambda_{\max}(\rho\sigma^{-1})=\log\lambda_{\max}(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}})

(22)

(where
\lambda_{\max}
denotes the largest eigenvalue),
which is not a maximal
f
-divergence but rather corresponds to the Rényi divergence of order
\infty
.

II-B
2
f(x)=x^{2}-1
(or
(x-1)^{2}
)

The chi-squared divergence

\chi^{2}(p||q)=\sum_{i}\frac{(p_{i}-q_{i})^{2}}{q_{i}}=\sum_{i}\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{q_{i}}-1

(23)

has the natural quantum extension

\chi^{2}(\rho\|\sigma)=\Tr\bigl((\rho-\sigma)^{2}\sigma^{-1}\bigr)=\Tr\bigl(\rho^{2}\sigma^{-1}\bigr)-1.

(24)

It turns out that it also equals the corresponding maximal
f
-divergence, since

\displaystyle(\chi^{2})^{\max}(\rho||\sigma)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl((\rho\sigma^{-1})^{2}\sigma-\sigma\bigr)

(25)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(\rho\sigma^{-1}\rho\bigr)-\Tr(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(\rho^{2}\sigma^{-1}\bigr)-1

\displaystyle=\chi^{2}(\rho\|\sigma).

II-B
3
f(x)=|x-1|

The natural quantum extension of the total variation distance
\|p-q\|_{1}=\sum_{i}|p_{i}-q_{i}|
is the trace distance

\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}=\Tr(|\rho-\sigma|),

(26)

while the corresponding maximal
f
-divergence is

\displaystyle\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}^{\max}

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(|\rho\sigma^{-1}-1|\sigma\bigr)

(27)

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigl(\sigma^{1/2}|\sigma^{-1/2}(\rho-\sigma)\sigma^{-1/2}|\sigma^{1/2}\bigr).

II-C

Data Processing Inequality for Maximal
f
-Divergence

In this Subsection we assume that
f
is
operator convex
, that is, for any positive operators
X_{i}\geq 0
, and real numbers
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
such that
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1
, one has

f\bigl(\smash[b]{\sum_{i}}\lambda_{i}X_{i}\bigr)\leq\smash[b]{\sum_{i}}\lambda_{i}f(X_{i})

(28)

where
\leq
denotes the Loewner order. By Lemma
2
we also have a variant of Jensen’s inequality the form

f\bigl(\smash[b]{\sum_{i}}\Lambda_{i}x_{i}\bigr)\leq\smash[b]{\sum_{i}}\Lambda_{i}f(x_{i})

(29)

for any real numbers
x_{i}\geq 0
and positive operators
\Lambda_{i}\geq 0
such that
\sum_{i}\Lambda_{i}=I
.

We have the following extension of Theorem
1
:

Theorem 2
(Data Processing Inequalities)

Let
\rho,\sigma
,
r,s
be as in Theorem
1
and consider any quantum channel
\mathcal{W}
. Then

D_{f}^{\max}(\mathcal{W}(r)\|\mathcal{W}(s))\leq D_{f}^{\max}(r\|s).

(30)

In other words, letting
\tilde{\rho}=\mathcal{W}(r)
and
\tilde{\sigma}=\mathcal{W}(s)
we have

D_{f}^{\max}(\tilde{\rho}\|\tilde{\sigma})\leq D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma).

(31)

By Theorem
1
, equality holds in (
30
) or (
31
) if
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}
is defined by (
12
).

In addition, by composition of channels
\mathcal{W}\circ\mathcal{V}
, we may consider
\tilde{\rho}=\mathcal{W}(\rho)=\mathcal{W}\bigl(\mathcal{V}(r)\bigr)
and
\tilde{\sigma}=\mathcal{W}(\sigma)=\mathcal{W}\bigl(\mathcal{V}(s)\bigr)
. Then (
31
) gives the
quantum data processing inequality
(ii) for maximal
f
-divergence for any quantum channel
\mathcal{W}
:

D_{f}^{\max}(\mathcal{W}(\rho)\|\mathcal{W}(\sigma))\leq D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)

(32)

which generalizes (
30
) for any mixed states
\rho,\sigma
.

Proof:

Proceed to prove
D_{f}^{\max}(\tilde{\rho}\|\tilde{\sigma})\leq D_{f}^{\max}(r\|s)
where
\tilde{\sigma}=\mathcal{W}(s)
and

\tilde{\rho}=\mathcal{W}(r)=\mathcal{W}(\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}r_{i}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}})=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}r_{i}\mathcal{W}({\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}}).

(33)

We have

\tilde{\sigma}^{-1/2}\tilde{\rho}\,\tilde{\sigma}^{-1/2}=\smash[b]{\sum_{i}}\frac{r_{i}}{s_{i}}\cdot\tau_{i}

(34)

where
\tau_{i}=s_{i}\cdot\tilde{\sigma}^{-1/2}\mathcal{W}({\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}})\tilde{\sigma}^{-1/2}\geq 0
are positive operators such that

\sum_{i}\tau_{i}=\tilde{\sigma}^{-1/2}\mathcal{W}(s)\tilde{\sigma}^{-1/2}=\tilde{\sigma}^{-1/2}\tilde{\sigma}\tilde{\sigma}^{-1/2}=I.

(35)

Therefore, by Lemma
2
,

f(\tilde{\sigma}^{-1/2}\tilde{\rho}\,\tilde{\sigma}^{-1/2})\leq\sum_{i}f(\frac{r_{i}}{s_{i}})\tau_{i}.

(36)

Multiplying by
\tilde{\sigma}
and taking the trace, we obtain

D_{f}^{\max}(\tilde{\rho}\|\tilde{\sigma})\leq\sum_{i}f(\frac{r_{i}}{s_{i}})\Tr(\tilde{\sigma}^{1/2}\tau_{i}\tilde{\sigma}^{1/2})

(37)

where

\Tr(\tilde{\sigma}^{1/2}\tau_{i}\tilde{\sigma}^{1/2})=s_{i}\cdot\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{W}({\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}})\bigr)=s_{i}\cdot\Tr({\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\rangle$}}\mkern-5.65mu\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{78643}{45875}{$\langle$}}{u_{i}}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{45875}{$|$}}})=s_{i}.

(38)

This proves (
30
).
∎

II-D

Application: From Classical to Quantum

The main application studied in this paper of our derivation of Theorem
1
is to extend classical inequalities to the quantum setting, thanks to the following

Corollary 1
(Classical to Quantum)

If for some function
\phi
the inequality between classical
f
-divergences

D_{f}(p\|q)\leq\phi(D_{g}(p\|q))

(39)

holds for any classical distributions
p,q
,
then any quantum
f
-divergence
D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)
satisfies the inequality

D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\phi(D_{g}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma))

(40)

for any
\rho,~\sigma
with
D_{g}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\!=\!\Tr\bigl(\sigma^{1/2}g(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2})\sigma^{1/2}\bigr)
.

Proof:

By Theorem
1
, there exist classical distributions
r,s
depending only on
\rho,\sigma
such that

D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)=D_{f}(r\|s)
and

D_{g}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)=D_{g}(r\|s)
.
Since by hypothesis we have

D_{f}(r\|s)\leq\phi(D_{g}(r\|s))

it follows that

D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\phi(D_{g}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma))
.
∎In the sequel, we use Corollary
1
to generalize both classical Pinsker and reverse Pinsker inequalities to the quantum case.

III
Applications to (Reverse) Pinkser Inequalities

III-A

Improved Quantum Pinsker Inequality for
\chi^{2}
-divergence

Pinsker’s inequality
[
12
]
, first established by Schützenberger
[
13
]
—see
[
14
]
for an extensive historical review—is a fundamental result in information theory that provides a lower bound on the Kullback-Leibler divergence between two probability distributions in terms of their total variation distance (or trace distance in the quantum case):

\displaystyle D(p\|q)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|p-q\|_{1}^{2}

(41)

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}^{2}

where divergences are expressed in nats (with logs to base
e
).

Pinsker’s inequality has been generalized to
f
-divergences, particularly to the chi-squared divergence
defined by (
23
), (
24
).
A well-known Pinsker inequality for
\chi^{2}
-divergence is
[
15
]

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(p||q)

\displaystyle\geq\|p-q\|_{1}^{2}

(42)

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(\rho||\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}^{2}.

Based on the following classical improvement of Pinsker’s inequality
[
16
, Eq. (138)]
.

\chi^{2}(p||q)\geq\begin{cases}\|p-q\|_{1}^{2},&\text{if }\|p-q\|_{1}\in[0,1],\\
\frac{\|p-q\|_{1}}{2-\|p-q\|_{1}},&\text{if }\|p-q\|_{1}\in(1,2].\end{cases}

(43)

we easily obtain the following

Theorem 3
(Improved Quantum Pinsker Inequality for
\chi^{2}
-Divergence)

The quantum chi-squared divergence
\chi^{2}(\rho||\sigma)
satisfies the following inequality

\chi^{2}(\rho||\sigma)\geq\begin{cases}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}^{2},&\text{if }\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}\in[0,1],\\
\frac{\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}}{2-\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}},&\text{if }\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}\in(1,2].\end{cases}

(44)

for any quantum states
\rho
,
\sigma
.

Proof:

Observe that the quantum
\chi^{2}
-divergence is the maximal
f
-divergence for
f(x)=x^{2}-1
,
by (
25
).
The result is then immediate from (
43
) by Corollary
1
applied to
f(x)=|x-1|
and
g(x)=x^{2}-1
.
∎

Application

Using Theorem
3
we can improve Temme
et al.
bound in decoherence theory
[
15
]
. The authors consider a quantum system time evolution given by a Liouvillian of unique equilibrium state
\sigma
.
Letting
\rho_{t}
the state of the quantum system at time
t
, the trace distance
\|\rho_{t}-\sigma\|_{1}
eventually vanishes as time goes by.
It is important when designing quantum technology to evaluate the rate of convergence to
0
of this quantity is order to estimate how long can one keep significant quantum information.
In
[
15
, Eq. (43) for
k=0
]
the authors show that

\|\rho_{t}-\sigma\|_{1}^{2}\leq\chi^{2}(\rho_{t}\|\sigma)\leq e^{-\lambda t}\chi^{2}(\rho_{0}\|\sigma)

(45)

where
\lambda>0
is the second largest eigenvalue of the Liouvillian defined in their article.
Now, improving the first inequality
\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}^{2}\leq\chi^{2}(\rho\|\sigma)
using Theorem
3
, one readily obtains the following improved bound for short times:

\|\rho_{t}-\sigma\|_{1}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{2e^{-\lambda t}\chi^{2}(\rho_{0}||\sigma)}{1+e^{-\lambda t}\chi^{2}(\rho_{0}||\sigma)},&\text{if }e^{\lambda t}<\chi^{2}(\rho_{0}||\sigma)\\
e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}t}\chi(\rho_{0}||\sigma),&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(46)

where we have noted
\chi=\sqrt{\chi^{2}}
.

Figure
1
illustrates this improvement, which is naturally all the more important as
\chi^{2}(\rho_{0}||\sigma)
is large, i.e., the starting state is far away from the stationary one.

Fig. 1:
Comparison of the previous Temme
et al.
bound and the improved bound of this paper on the trace norm distance
||\rho_{t}-\sigma||_{1}
as a function of time, when
\lambda=0.1
. Different curves correspond to different initial values of the chi-squared divergence
\chi^{2}(\rho_{0}||\sigma)
. The improved bound provides a tighter estimate at earlier times, particularly for larger initial
\chi^{2}(\rho_{0}||\sigma)
.

III-B

Reverse Pinsker Inequalities for
f
-Divergences

While Pinsker’s inequality gives a lower bound on the
f
-divergence in terms of the total variation distance,
a
reverse Pinsker inequality
gives an upper bound on the
f
-divergence, under some conditions on the distributions. This again can be extended in the quantum setting in the form of a upper bound in terms of the trace distance between states:

D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\phi(\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}).

(47)

In this section, we use Corollary
1
to derive a reverse Pinsker inequality for any
f
-divergence, based on Binette’s sharp (classical) reverse Pinsker inequality
[
17
]
:

D_{f}(p\|q)\leq\frac{\|p-q\|_{1}}{2}\Bigl(\frac{f(m)}{1-m}+\frac{f(M)}{M-1}\Bigr)

(48)

where
m=\min_{i}\frac{p_{i}}{q_{i}}
and
M=\max_{i}\frac{p_{i}}{q_{i}}
.

Theorem 4

For any mixed states
\rho,\sigma
satisfying the condition

|\rho-\sigma|\leq\rho+\sigma,

(49)

we have

D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\frac{\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}}{2}\biggl(\frac{f(m)}{1-m}+\frac{f(M)}{M-1}\biggr).

(50)

where
m=\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}})

and
M=\lambda_{\max}(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}})
are the smallest and largest eigenvalues of
\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, respectively.
Furthermore, the inequality is sharp: equality holds for some
\rho,\sigma
satisfying (
49
).

Proof:

According to
[
7
, Eq. (64)]
, (
49
) is a (necessary and) sufficient condition for the maximal
g(x)=|x-1|
-divergence to coincide with the trace distance:

D_{g}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)=\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}
.
Also observe that from the classical distribution representation of maximal
f
-divergence of Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\triangleq\min_{i}\frac{r_{i}}{s_{i}}=\min_{i}\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}})

(51)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\triangleq\max_{i}\frac{r_{i}}{s_{i}}=\max_{i}\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{\max}(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}})

which depend only on
\rho,\sigma
.
Therefore, we may apply Corollary
1
to
\phi(x)=\frac{x}{2}(\frac{f(m)}{1-m}+\frac{f(M)}{M-1})
,
D_{g}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)=\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}
.
This gives (
50
). Equality is achieved for commuting states
\rho,\sigma
, which necessarily satisfy (
49
) and correspond to the ternary classical distributions given in
[
17
, Eq. (16)]
achieving equality in (
48
).
∎

III-C

Comparison to Hirche-Tomamichel’s Bound

First notice comparing (
51
) to (
22
) that in terms of the max-relative entropy,

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\exp\bigl(D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\bigr)

(52)

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\exp\bigl(-D_{\max}(\sigma\|\rho)\bigr)

where the latter equality comes from

m=\lambda_{\min}\bigl(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{-1}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)=\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}})\bigr)^{-1}
.
Hirche and Tomamichel’s bound
[
4
, Prop. 5.2]
is

D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\frac{\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}}{2}\zeta_{1}(\rho,\sigma)

(53)

where
\zeta_{1}(\rho,\sigma)
is defined by a complicated integral relation
[
4
, Eq. (5.12)]

which from (
52
) reduces to

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}(\rho,\sigma)\!

\displaystyle\triangleq\!\int_{1}^{M}\!\tfrac{M-\gamma}{M-1}f^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)d\gamma\!+\!\int_{1}^{\frac{1}{m}}\tfrac{\frac{1}{m}-\gamma}{\frac{1}{m}-1}\gamma^{-3}f^{\prime\prime}(\gamma^{-1})d\gamma

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{M}\!\tfrac{M-\gamma}{M-1}f^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)d\gamma+\int_{m}^{1}\!\tfrac{\gamma-m}{1-m}f^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)d\gamma

(54)

\displaystyle=\frac{f(M)}{M-1}-f^{\prime}(1)+\frac{f(m)}{1-m}+f^{\prime}(1)

(55)

where we made the change of variable
\gamma\to\gamma^{-1}
in the second integral in (
54
),
and integrated by parts both integrals in (
55
).
Thus as it turns out, we find that Hirche-Tomamichel’s bound
matches exactly
the bound of Theorem
4
, which is the natural extension of Binette’s bound to the quantum case.

There are some differences, however:

•

Hirche-Tomamichel’s bound was derived only for their specific
f
-divergence defined as an integral over hockey stick divergences. Our bound holds in general for any
f
-divergence, in particular for the maximal
f
-divergence.

•

Hirche and Tomamichel assume that
f
is twice differentiable, which is not needed in this paper.

•

on the downside, we assume condition (
49
), which is not needed by Hirche and Tomamichel. Our simulations show, however, that randomly drawn states in dimension 4 satisfy (
49
) more than 80% of the time. We conjecture that (
49
) is in fact not necessary for (
50
) to hold.

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we have revisited Matsumoto’s maximal divergence theorem by providing explicit classical distributions achieving maximal
f
-divergence.
Leveraging these distributions, we showed that classical
f
-divergence inequalities
can be easily extended to their quantum counterparts. This approach simplifies the derivation of quantum inequalities, as it avoids complex matrix analysis and relies instead on simple linear algebra.
We applied our methodology to find both quantum Pinsker and quantum reverse Pinsker inequalities.
In particular, the reverse Pinsker inequality of Hirche and Tomamichel was found to match the quantum generalization of the classical reverse Pinsker inequality of Binette.
Our quantum Pinsker inequality for
\chi^{2}
divergence was also applied to improve a bound on the evolution of quantum states under dynamical equations, which illustrates the operational relevance of our approach.

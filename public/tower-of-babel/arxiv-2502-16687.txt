Abstract
. We construct new families of Artinian Gorenstein graded K K -algebras of arbitrary codimension having binomial Macaulay dual generators and satisfying the weak or the strong Lefschetz property. This is a companion paper to [ ADF+25 ] , which studies codimension three algebras having binomial Macaulay dual generators in great depth, establishing in particular that they enjoy the strong Lefschetz property.



1.
Introduction

The main objective of this paper is to advance the understanding of a challenging question in commutative algebra: under what conditions does a graded Artinian Gorenstein (AG) algebra over a field
K
satisfy the weak Lefschetz property (WLP) or the strong Lefschetz property (SLP)? These properties play a crucial role in the study of Artinian algebras, influencing their algebraic structure and leading to geometric and combinatorial applications; see
[
HMM+13
,
MN13
,
JMR24
]
for overviews of the topic and
[
Sta80
,
HMNW03
]
for crucial advances.

Via Macaulay-Matlis duality, an AG graded
K
-algebra corresponds to a homogeneous polynomial, called the Macaulay dual generator. When this generator is a monomial, the AG algebra is a quotient of the polynomial ring by a monomial complete intersection. In this case, previous studies by Stanley
[
Sta80
]
and Watanabe
[
Wat87
]
have shown that if
K
has characteristic zero the resulting AG algebra satisfies the WLP and even the SLP. Along with this, AG algebras having monomial Macaulay dual generator satisfy several other desirable properties which we list below:

(1)

The defining ideal is a monomial complete intersection;

(2)

The minimal generators of the monomial complete intersection form a Gröbner basis;

(3)

The minimal free resolutions for (monomial) complete intersections are given by the Koszul complex;

(4)

(Monomial) complete intersections can be obtained by a construction termed doubling from 0-dimensional schemes in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.

The present paper and the companion paper
[
ADF+25
]
investigate to what extent similar results hold for AG algebras having binomial Macaulay dual generator. In particular, given the list of properties enumerated above we ask the following questions motivating our investigations:

Open Problems
.

(1)

Characterization of complete intersections:
Under what conditions can an AG algebra with a binomial Macaulay dual generator be classified as a complete intersection? This classification could help simplify the study of these algebras and provide a clearer structural picture. It is a step towards the larger goal of characterizing Artinian complete intersections based on their Macaulay dual generators.

(2)

Strong and weak Lefschetz properties:
While codimension-three AG algebras with binomial Macaulay dual generators are shown in
[
ADF+25
]
to satisfy both WLP and SLP, determining broader conditions that guarantee these properties in higher codimensions remains an open problem.

(3)

Bounds on the Sperner number and flat h-vectors:
The Sperner number, which reflects the maximal value of the Hilbert function of an AG algebra, and the flatness of its h-vector (i.e., the number of equal entries corresponding to the Sperner number) are critical aspects that influence the structure of the algebra and allow to transfer the Lefschetz properties from an AG algebra to some of its quotients.

(4)

Doubling construction:
What criteria allow these algebras to be viewed as doublings, i.e., constructed as extensions of certain one-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay algebras? Understanding the doubling properties may provide a pathway to building higher-dimensional AG algebras having specific properties of interest. It further relates the properties of AG algebras to those of non-reduced point schemes in projective space.

(5)

Minimal Generators and Free Resolutions:
Determining bounds on the minimal number of generators required to define these algebras and describing their minimal free resolutions are fundamental to understanding the presentation and homological properties of AG algebras with binomial Macaulay dual generators. Understanding which algebras arise as doublings may provide a pathway to determining their minimal free resolutions.

It is shown in
[
ADF+25
]
that codimension three AG algebras having binomial Macaulay dual generators retain most of the properties of monomial complete intersections, satisfying in particular both WLP and SLP. However, extending these results to higher codimensions has proven to be a complex task, as some binomially generated AG algebras in codimension four or greater fail to meet the WLP under similar conditions; see
[
ADF+25
, Example 3.5]
. In the current paper, we focus on identifying new families of AG algebras in higher codimensions that do satisfy the WLP or SLP, as well as investigating their structural characteristics.

Our main results can be summarized as follows:

Main Theorem
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero and let
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},m
be non-negative integers. Let
A_{F}
denote the AG algebra having Macaulay dual generator
F
.

(1)

If
F=X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}(X_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{b_{r}}-X_{r+1}^{b_{r+1}}\cdots X_{n}^{b_{n}})
for some
1\leq r\leq n
is homogeneous, i. e.
b_{1}+\cdots+b_{r}=b_{r+1}+\cdots+b_{n}
, and the inequality
a_{1}>a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}+b_{r+1}+\cdots+b_{n}
holds, then
A_{F}
satisfies the WLP.

(2)

If
F=X_{i}^{a_{i}}(X_{1}^{b_{1}}-X_{2}^{b_{2}}X_{3}^{b_{3}}\cdots X_{n}^{b_{n}})
for some
1\leq i\leq n
with
\sum_{i=2}^{n}b_{i}=b_{1}
, then
A_{F}
has the WLP.

(3)

If
F=X_{1}^{c_{1}}X_{2}^{c_{2}}X_{3}^{c_{3}}\cdots X_{n}^{c_{n}}(X_{1}^{m}-X_{2}^{m})
, then
A_{F}
has the SLP.

(4)

If
F=X_{1}^{c_{1}}X_{2}^{c_{2}}X_{3}^{c_{3}}\cdots X_{n}^{c_{n}}(m_{1}-m_{2})
where
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are monomials of the same degree in 3 variables
X_{i},X_{j},X_{r}
, then
A_{F}
has the SLP.

In
Section

2
, we discuss the tools necessary for our analysis, which include higher Hessian matrices as introduced by Watanabe
[
Wat00
]
and Gondim–Zappalà
[
GZ19
]
and a criterion for WLP from
[
ADF+25
]
originating in work concerning Lefschetz properties of AG algebras that are connected sums by McDaniel–Iarrobino–Seceleanu
[
IMS22
]
. Making use of these tools, we prove our main results in
Section

3
. To conclude, we re-emphasize the open problems proposed above in
Section

4
. They reflect challenges and potential directions for future research applicable to all AG algebras, which we are optimistic can be settled in the setting proposed in this paper by leveraging the binomial nature of the Macaulay dual generator.

Although many of these questions are resolved in the codimension three case, they remain largely open in higher codimensions, with the only partial results available to date presented in this paper. Our work lays the groundwork for advancing the themes proposed above and opens pathways for further investigation into the structural nuances of AG algebras with binomial Macaulay dual generators. Addressing these questions will deepen our understanding of AG algebras at large.

2.
Background

Throughout this paper
K
will be a field of characteristic zero.
Given a standard graded Artinian
K
-algebra
A=R/I
where
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
I
is a homogeneous ideal of
R
,
we denote by
\mathrm{HF}_{A}\colon\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
with
\mathrm{HF}_{A}(j)=\dim_{K}[A]_{j}=\dim_{K}[R/I]_{j}

the Hilbert function of
A
and
H_{A}(t)=\sum_{i}\dim_{K}[A]_{i}t^{i}
its Hilbert series. Since
A
is Artinian, its Hilbert function is
encoded in its
h
-vector

h=(h_{0},\dots,h_{d})
where
h_{i}=\mathrm{HF}_{A}(i)>0
and
d
is the largest index with this property. The integer
d
is called the
socle degree of

A
or the
regularity
of
A
and denoted
\reg(A)
.

2.1.
Gorenstein algebras and Macaulay duality

A standard graded Artinian
K
-algebra
A=R/I
with socle degree
d
is said to be
Gorenstein
if its
socle
(0:m_{A})
is a one dimensional
K
-vector space.
We sometimes refer to the number of variables of
R
as the
codimension
of
A
.

We recall the construction of the standard graded Artinian Gorenstein algebra
A_{F}
with
Macaulay dual generator
a given form
F\in S=K[X_{1},\dots,X_{N}]
. We denote by
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]
the ring of differential operators acting on the polynomial ring
S
, i. e.
x_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}
. Therefore
R
acts on
S
by differentiation. Given polynomials
p\in R
and
G\in S
we will denote by
{p\circ G}
the differential operator
p\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{n}}\right)
applied to
G
. For a homogeneous polynomial
F\in S
, we define

\Ann_{R}(F):=\{p\in R\mid p\circ F=0\}\subset R,\quad\text{ and }\quad A_{F}=R/\Ann_{R}(F).

The ring
A_{F}
is a standard graded Artinian Gorenstein
K
-algebra (AG algebra, for short) and
F
is called its
Macaulay dual generator
. It is worthwhile to point out that every standard graded Artinian Gorenstein
K
-algebra is of the form
A_{F}
for some homogeneous polynomial
F
, in view of Macaulay’s “double annihilator theorem”, see for instance
[
IK99
, Lemma 2.12]
. Moreover the degree of the Macaulay dual generator
F
is equal to the regularity of
A_{F}
.

Next, we single out the largest value of the Hilbert function and the degree range where it is attained. If this degree range is relatively wide, the Hilbert function has a flat.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be an AG algebra. We define the
Sperner number

S_{A}
of
A
as the largest value of its Hilbert function.

Let
h=(h_{0},\dots,h_{d})
be the h-vector of
A
. We set
NS_{A}=|\{h_{i}\mid h_{i}=S_{A}\}|

and we will say that the h-vector of
A
has a
flat if
NS_{A}\geq 3
.

2.2.
Lefschetz properties and Hessian matrices

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A=R/I
be a graded Artinian
K
-algebra. We say that
A
has the
weak Lefschetz property
(WLP, for short)
if there is a linear form
\ell\in[A]_{1}
such that, for all
integers
i\geq 0
, the multiplication map

\times\ell:[A]_{i}\longrightarrow[A]_{i+1}

has maximal rank.
In this case, the linear form
\ell
is called a weak Lefschetz
element of
A
. If for the general form
\ell\in[A]_{1}
and for an integer
j
the
map
\times\ell:[A]_{j-1}\longrightarrow[A]_{j}
does not have maximal rank, we will say that the ideal
I
fails the WLP in
degree
j
.

A
has the
strong Lefschetz property
(SLP, for short) if there is a linear form
\ell\in[A]_{1}
such that, for all
integers
i\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
, the multiplication map

\times\ell^{k}:[A]_{i}\longrightarrow[A]_{i+k}

has maximal rank. Such an element
\ell
is called a strong Lefschetz element for
A
.

To determine whether an Artinian standard graded
K
-algebra
A
has the WLP or SLP seems a simple problem of linear algebra, but it has proven to be extremely elusive and much more work on this topic remains to be done, see
[
JMR24
]
.

We end this preliminary subsection with results due to Watanabe
[
Wat00
]
and Gondim-Zappalà
[
GZ19
]
which establish a useful connection between the failure of Lefschetz properties and the vanishing of higher order Hessians.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
F\in S=K[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
be a homogeneous polynomial and let
A_{F}=R/\Ann_{R}(F)
be the AG algebra with Macaulay dual generator
F
. Fix ordered

K
-basis

\mathcal{B}=\{w_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq h_{t}:=\dim_{K}A_{t}\}\subset A_{t}\text{ and}

\mathcal{B^{\prime}}=\{u_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq h_{s}:=\dim_{K}A_{s}\}\subset A_{s}.

The
(t,s)
-th mixed (relative)
Hessian matrix
of
F
with respect to
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B^{\prime}}
is the
h_{t}\times h_{s}
matrix:

\Hess_{F}^{(s,t)}=((w_{i}u_{j})\circ F)_{i,j}.

The
(t,s)
-th mixed
Hessian of

F

with respect to

\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B^{\prime}}
is

\hess_{F}^{(s,t)}=\det(\Hess_{F}^{(s,t)}).

Ween
s=t
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B^{\prime}}
the notions above become
the
t
-th (relative)
Hessian matrix
of
F
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
, namely the
h_{t}\times h_{t}
matrix:

\Hess_{F}^{t}=((w_{i}w_{j})\circ F)_{i,j}

and the
t
-th
Hessian of

F

with respect to

\mathcal{B}
, respectively,

\hess_{F}^{t}=\det(\Hess_{F}^{t}).

The 0-th Hessian is the polynomial
F
itself and, in the case
\dim_{K}A_{1}=n
, the 1-st Hessian, with respect to the standard basis given by the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
, is the classical Hessian
\Hess_{F}^{1}=(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial X_{i}\partial X_{j}})_{i,j}
. The definition of
Hessians of order
t
depends on the choice of a basis of
A_{t}
but the vanishing of the
t
-th Hessian is independent of this choice.
We denote the
t
-th Hessian evaluated at the point
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
by
\hess_{F}^{t}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
Wat00
, Theorem 4]
,
[
MW09
, Theorem 3.1]
,
[
GZ19
, Theorem 2.4]
)
.

Let
F\in S=K[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
and let
A_{F}=R/\Ann_{R}(F)
be the
associated AG algebra. Consider a linear form
\ell=a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}\in A_{1}
.

(1)

The multiplication map
\times\ell^{d-2t}:A_{t}\to A_{d-t}
has full rank if and only if the
t
-th Hessian
\hess_{F}^{t}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
. In particular,
\ell
is a strong Lefschetz element of
A
if and only if
\hess_{F}^{t}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
for
t=1,\dots,\lfloor d/2\rfloor
.

(2)

The multiplication map
\times\ell^{d-s-t}:A_{s}\to A_{d-t}
has full rank if and only if the
(s,t)
-th mixed Hessian
\hess_{F}^{(s,t)}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
.

The theorem above can be used to establish the WLP as well as the SLP. Due to their duality properties, the WLP for AG algebras is determined by a single map.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
MMRN11
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Let
A
be an AG algebra with
\reg(A)=d
. Then the following are equivalent

(i)

\ell\in A_{1}
is a Weak Lefschetz element on
A

(ii)

the map
\times\ell:A_{\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor}\to A_{\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor+1}
has maximum rank (is injective)

(iii)

the map
\times\ell:A_{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}\to A_{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1}
has maximum rank (is surjective).

As a consequence of
Theorem

2.4
and
Proposition

2.5
, the WLP is determined by the non-vanishing of a single mixed Hessian determinant.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
F
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
and let
A_{F}=R/\Ann_{R}(F)
be the associated AG algebra. Set
t=\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
. A linear form
\ell=a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}\in A_{1}
is a Weak Lefschetz element on
A
if and only if
the (mixed) Hessian
\hess_{F}^{(d-t-1,t)}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
.

3.
Algebras with binomial Macaulay dual generator that satisfy the WLP

Throughout this section, we fix a binomial
F=m_{1}-m_{2}\in K[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
with
m_{1},m_{2}
monomials. After reordering the variables, if necessary, any binomial can be factored as

F=X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}(X_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{b_{r}}-X_{r+1}^{b_{r+1}}\cdots X_{n}^{b_{n}}),\quad\text{ where }1\leq r\leq n-1\text{ and}

\displaystyle m_{1}

\displaystyle=X_{1}^{a_{1}+b_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{a_{r}+b_{r}}X_{r+1}^{a_{r+1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}},

\displaystyle m_{2}

\displaystyle=X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{a_{r}}X_{r+1}^{a_{r+1}+b_{r+1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}+b_{n}},

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}

(3.1)

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}b_{i}.

Our goal will be to describe the main features of the Artinian Gorenstein algebra
A_{F}
with binomial Macaulay dual generator
F
in terms of
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
.

3.1.
A criterion for WLP

In
[
ADF+25
]
, the authors prove that codimension 3 AG algebras
A_{F}
with binomial Macaulay dual generator
F
enjoys the SLP, but even the WLP can fail for AG algebras of higher codimension with binomial Macaulay dual generator. The same paper gives a useful general result which we employ in our present work.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
ADF+25
, Theorem 3.4]
)
.

If
F=m_{1}-m_{2}
is a homogeneous binomial such that
g=\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})
satisfies
\deg(g)<\left\lfloor\frac{\deg(F)-1}{2}\right\rfloor
, then
A_{F}
has the WLP.

The result above is the best possible in the sense that there are examples of Artinian Gorenstein algebras
A_{F}
with
\deg(g)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{\deg(F)-1}{2}\right\rfloor
failing the WLP
[
ADF+25
, Example 3.5]
. Our aim is to describe new families of binomials which in spite of verifying the inequality

\deg(g)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{\deg(F)-1}{2}\right\rfloor

do satisfy the WLP.

The following lemma will be useful in describing some of these families. A more general result is presented in
[
KU92
]
. We include a short proof of this special case for completeness.

Lemma 3.2
.

A surjective degree-preserving homomorphism
\pi:A\to B
between AG algebras of the same socle degree is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
f\in\ker(\pi)
and assume that
f\neq 0
. Then since
A
is AG, there exists
g\in A
so that
fg\in\Soc(A)
and
fg\neq 0
. Suppose
\reg(A)=\reg(B)=d
. Since
\pi
is degree-preserving and
A,B
are AG,
\pi
induces an isomorphism
\pi:A_{d}\xrightarrow{\cong}B_{d}
. Therefore, since
0\neq fg\in A_{d}
we conclude
0\neq\pi(fg)=\pi(f)\pi(g)
. This is a contradiction, since
\pi(f)=0
. Thus,
\ker(\pi)=0
and so
\pi
is an isomorphism.
∎

3.2.
New families of algebras satisfying the WLP

We are now ready to analyze the WLP for various families of AG algebras with binomial Macaulay dual generator. In terms of the notation introduced in (
3.1
) our families will have one of the following properties

(1)

There is a variable in the support of
g
that dominates, that is, its exponent exceeds the exponent of all the other variables combined. We analyze this case in
Theorem

3.3
, where we note that in addition to satisfying WLP the algebras in this family also have a flat.

(2)

The gcd
g
of the two monomials in
F
is a pure power of a single variable. We analyze this case in
Proposition

3.7
and
Proposition

3.6
.

(3)

The support of the binomial
F/g
contains at most three variables. We analyze this case in
Proposition

3.8
and
Proposition

3.9
.

Our first family concerns binomials
F
for which the degree of a variable in the support of
g
exceeds half of the degree of
F
.

Theorem 3.3
.

We consider the binomial

F=X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}(X_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{b_{r}}-X_{r+1}^{b_{r+1}}\cdots X_{n}^{b_{n}}),\quad 1\leq r\leq n-1,

where
\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}=\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}b_{i}
.

(i)

Assume that
a_{1}-(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n})>b_{r+1}+\cdots+b_{n}.
Then,
A_{F}
has the WLP.

(ii)

Assume that
a_{1}-(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n})\geq b_{r+1}+\cdots+b_{n}
and
b_{1}>0
. Then,

NS_{A_{F}}\geq 3+a_{1}-(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n})-(b_{r+1}+\cdots+b_{n}).

Proof.

(i) Denote
F=F_{1}-F_{2}
, where
F_{i}
are the monomials

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{1}^{a_{1}+b_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{a_{r}+b_{r}}X_{r+1}^{a_{r+1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{a_{r}}X_{r+1}^{a_{r+1}+b_{r+1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}+b_{n}}.

We set
d=\deg(F)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}b_{i}
and

. By assumption there inequality

holds.

We first consider the case

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{i}=\{x_{1}^{t-i}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\mid\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=i\hskip 5.69054pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69054pt

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}\leq a_{2},\dots,\alpha_{r}\leq a_{r},

\displaystyle\alpha_{r+1}\leq a_{r+1}+b_{r+1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\leq a_{n}+b_{n}\}.

Then

Order

Denote by

the submatrix of
\Hess^{t}_{F}
corresponding to rows and columns in
\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}
. We claim that
M
is triangular with
X_{1}
as anti-diagonal entries. We denote by
M_{(\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{i},\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{j})}
the block of
M
with entries
uv\circ F
such that
u\in\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{i},v\in\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{j}
.
Note that for every
u=x_{1}^{t-i}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\in\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{i}
and
v=x_{1}^{t-j}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{j}
we have

uv=x_{1}^{2t-i-j}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}\text{ such that }{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}+\cdots+{\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}=i+j.

If
j>d^{\prime}-i
or equivalently
i+j>d^{\prime}
we get
uv\circ F=0
. Thus for every
j>d^{\prime}-i
the block
M_{(\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{i},\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{j})}
is a zero block which means that all the entries of
M
bellow the anti-diagonal blocks are zero.

Now we show that that for all
0\leq i\leq d^{\prime}
, the anti-diagonal blocks of
M
, i.e.
M_{(\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{i},\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{d^{\prime}-i})}
, are square blocks for which the anti-diagonal entries are all equal to
X_{1}
and entries bellow the anti-diagonal entries are all zero.
For
u\in\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{i},v\in\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{d^{\prime}-i}
, we have

uv=x_{1}^{2t-d^{\prime}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}\quad\text{ where }\quad{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}+\cdots+{\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}=d^{\prime}.

Since
\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}
is ordered in reverse lexicographic order the anti-diagonal entries of
M_{(\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{i},\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{d^{\prime}-i})}
are equal to

x_{1}^{2t-d^{\prime}}x_{2}^{a_{2}+b_{2}}\cdots x_{r}^{a_{r}+b_{r}}x_{r+1}^{a_{r+1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}\circ F=X_{1}.

The displayed equality holds because we have
2t-d^{\prime}=a_{1}-1
. On the other hand, the entries bellow the anti-diagonal entries of
M_{(\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{i},\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{d^{\prime}-i})}
are equal to

x_{1}^{2t-d^{\prime}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}+\alpha^{\prime}_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}+\alpha^{\prime}_{n}}\circ F=0

since
\alpha_{n}+\alpha^{\prime}_{n}>a_{n}+b_{n}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}
be the monomial basis for the complete intersection algebra
A_{F_{1}}
. The same proof as above shows that the submatrix
N
of
\Hess^{t}_{F}
with respect to a basis of
[A_{F}]_{t}
restricted to rows and columns in
\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}
is a triangular matrix having
X_{1}
as antidiagonal entries.

Now we claim that the submatrix of
N
with rows and columns in
\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}
has a similar shape. To show this we show that for
m_{1},m_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}
such that
m_{1}m_{1}^{\prime}\circ F_{1}=X_{1}
, if
m_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}
then
m_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}
. Suppose not. So either
m_{1}^{\prime}\circ F_{2}\neq 0
or
m_{1}^{\prime}\circ F_{1}=m_{2}^{\prime}\circ F_{2}
for some
m_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}
.

First assume that
m_{1}^{\prime}\circ F_{2}\neq 0
then
m_{1}^{\prime}\circ\gcd(F_{1},F_{2})\neq 0
. If we set

m_{1}=x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}x_{2}^{\beta_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}\quad\text{ and }\quad m_{1}^{\prime}=x_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}x_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{\gamma_{n}},

then for every
2\leq i\leq r
we must have
\gamma_{i}\leq a_{i}
and since
m_{1}^{\prime}
has degree
t
and
a_{1}>t
for
i=1
we have
\gamma_{1}<a_{1}
. On the other hand,
m_{1}m_{1}^{\prime}\circ F_{1}=X_{1}
forces
\beta_{i}+\gamma_{i}=a_{i}+b_{i}
for every
2\leq i\leq r
. This implies
\beta_{i}\geq b_{i}
for every
2\leq i\leq r
. From these inequalities we obtain that for monomial

m_{2}=x_{1}^{a_{1}-\gamma_{1}-1}x_{2}^{\beta_{2}-b_{2}}\cdots x_{r}^{\beta_{r}-b_{r}}x_{r+1}^{\beta_{r+1}+b_{r+1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}+b_{n}}

we have
m_{1}\circ F=m_{2}\circ F
which contradicts the assumption that
m_{1}\notin\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{(1,t)}
. Next if we assume
m_{1}^{\prime}\circ F_{1}=m_{2}^{\prime}\circ F_{2}\neq 0
then by acting by
m_{1}
we obtain
m_{1}m_{1}^{\prime}\circ F_{1}=m_{1}m_{2}^{\prime}\circ F_{2}\neq 0
and this means
m_{1}\circ F_{2}\neq 0
which contradicts the assumption that
m_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}.

We observe that the entries of the columns corresponding to
\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}
and corresponding to row
\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}
is either zero or linear in
X_{2},\dots,X_{n}
. By construction only
F_{1}
contributes in columns corresponding to
\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}
and since
\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}
are disjoint sets there does not exist
m_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}
and
m_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}
such that
m_{1}m_{2}\circ F=m_{1}m_{2}\circ F_{1}=X_{1}
.

We conclude that
\Hess_{F}^{t}
has the block form
\begin{pmatrix}N&C\\
D&M\\
\end{pmatrix}
where
N
and
M
are a triangular square matrices of size
|\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}|
and
|\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}|
respectively with
X_{1}
as anti-diagonal entries. Moreover, the entries of

are in terms of
X_{2},\dots,X_{n}
. This implies that
\Hess_{F}^{t}
evaluated at
(1,0,\dots,0)
has non-zero determinant which allows us to use
Corollary

2.6
and conclude that
x_{1}
is a Lefschetz element for
A_{F}
when
\deg(F)=2t+1
.

Now assuming
\deg(F)=2t
we get
a_{1}>t
and
d^{\prime}\leq t-1
. We construct
\mathcal{B}_{t-1}=\mathcal{A}_{(1,t-1)}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{A}_{(2,t-1)}
a spanning set for
[A_{F}]_{t-1}
similar as above. We show that
\mathcal{B}_{t}
and
\mathcal{B}_{t-1}
have the same number of elements. First observe that since
d^{\prime}\leq t-1
all elements in
\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}
are divisible by
x_{1}
and moreover
since we define
\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}^{i}
and
\mathcal{A}_{(2,t-1)}^{i}
for
0\leq i\leq d^{\prime}\leq t-1
multiplication by
x_{1}
defines a bijection from
\mathcal{A}_{(2,t-1)}
to
\mathcal{A}_{(2,t)}
. Now note that if
m_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{(1,t-1)}^{\prime}
satisfies
m_{1}\circ F_{1}\neq 0
,
m_{1}\circ F_{2}=0
and
m_{1}\circ F=m_{2}\circ F
for some
m_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{(2,t-1)}
then there is a relation
x_{1}m_{1}\circ F=x_{1}m_{2}\circ F
of degree
t
implying
x_{1}m_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}
. Since every monomial in the basis of
[A_{F}]_{t}
is divisible by
x_{1}
then dividing every monomial in
\mathcal{A}_{(1,t)}^{\prime}
gives a monomial in
\mathcal{A}_{(1,t-1)}^{\prime}
. This finishes the proof that multiplication by
x_{1}
is a bijection from
\mathcal{B}_{t-1}
to
\mathcal{B}_{t}
. Thus we have shown that
x_{1}
is the Weak Lefschetz element for
A_{F}
when
\deg(F)=2t
.

(ii) We proceed by induction on
t=a_{1}-(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n})-(b_{r+1}+\cdots+b_{n})
. Assume
t=0
and consider the binomials
F
and
G=X_{1}F
.
We first observe that the socle degree of
A_{F}
is even and equal to
d=2a_{1}=2(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}+b_{r+1}+\cdots+b_{n})
while the socle degree of
A_{G}
is odd and equal to
d+1=2a_{1}+1
.
We define the K-vector spaces:

E_{s}=\langle x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}}\circ F\mid i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=s\rangle

and

D_{s}=\langle x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}}\circ G\mid i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=s\rangle.

A straightforward computation gives us

(3.2)

\dim E_{s}=\dim D_{s}\text{ for all }s\leq a_{1}+1=\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}+\sum_{j=r+1}^{n}b_{j}+1.

Therefore, we have

(3.3)

\mathrm{HF}_{A_{G}}(d+1-s)=\mathrm{HF}_{A_{F}}(d-s)\text{ for all }s\leq a_{1}+1=\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}+\sum_{j=r+1}^{n}b_{j}+1.

Since the socle degree of
A_{G}
is odd and equal to
2a_{1}+1
we have
\mathrm{HF}_{A_{G}}(a_{1})=\mathrm{HF}_{A_{G}}(a_{1}+1)
. Using the equalities (
3.3
) we get
\mathrm{HF}_{A_{F}}(a_{1}-1)=\mathrm{HF}_{A_{F}}(a_{1})
and being the socle degree of
A_{F}
even and equal to
2a_{1}
we conclude that
HF_{A_{F}}(a_{1}-1)=\mathrm{HF}_{A_{F}}(a_{1})=\mathrm{HF}_{A_{F}}(a_{1}+1)
; i.e.
NS_{A_{F}}\geq 3
which also implies that
NS_{A_{G}}\geq 4
.

We now assume that the result is true for
t=\rho
and we will prove for
t=\rho+1
. We start with

F=X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}(X_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{b_{r}}-X_{r+1}^{b_{r+1}}\cdots X_{n}^{b_{n}}),\quad 1\leq r\leq n-1,

where
\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}=\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}b_{i}
,
b_{1}>0
and
t=a_{1}-(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n})-(b_{r+1}+\cdots+b_{n})=\rho
. We define
G=X_{1}F
and we denote by
d
(resp.
d+1
) the socle degree of
A_{F}
(resp.
A_{G}
). Arguing as above we get

(3.4)

\dim\langle x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}}\circ F\mid i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=s\rangle=\dim\langle x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}}\circ G\mid i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=s\rangle\text{ for all }s\leq a_{1}+1.

and, hence,

(3.5)

\mathrm{HF}_{A_{G}}(d+1-s)=\mathrm{HF}_{A_{F}}(d-s)\text{ for all }s\leq a_{1}+1.

which allow us to conclude that

NS_{A_{G}}=NS_{A_{F}}+1\geq(a_{1}+1)-(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n})-(b_{r+1}+\cdots+b_{n})=\rho+1,

which proves what we want.
∎

and that
A_{G_{1}}

and
A_{G_{2}}
have the SLP. Note that
A_{G_{1}}
is a monomial complete intersection and, thus, it has the SLP in characteristic zero by
[
Sta78
]
, and
A_{G_{2}}
is an AG algebra of codimension two, and hence satisfies SLP in view of the fact that all codimension two Artinian algebras satisfy this property in characteristic zero; see
[
HW07
, Proposition 3.25]
.
∎

Using the main result of our work
[
ADF+25
]
, we can generalize the last result and prove:

Proposition 3.9
.

If
K
is a field of characteristic zero, the algebra
A_{F}
having the binomial Macaulay dual generator

F=X_{1}^{c_{1}}X_{2}^{c_{2}}X_{3}^{c_{3}}\cdots X_{n}^{c_{n}}(m_{1}-m_{2})

where
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are monomials of the same degree in the variables
X_{i_{1}}
,
X_{i_{2}}
and
X_{i_{3}}
, has the SLP.

Proof.

Without lose of generality, we can assume that
(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})=(1,2,3)
.
For

G_{1}=X_{4}^{c_{4}}\cdots X_{n}^{c_{n}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad G_{2}=X_{1}^{c_{1}}X_{2}^{c_{2}}X_{3}^{c_{3}}(m_{1}-m_{2})

consider the AG algebras

A_{G_{1}}=K[x_{4},\dots,x_{n}]/\Ann(G_{1})\quad\mbox{and}\quad A_{G_{2}}=K[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]/\Ann(G_{2}).

We have
F=G_{1}G_{2}
and therefore
A_{F}=A_{G_{1}}\otimes_{K}A_{G_{2}}
using the fact that
G_{1}
and
G_{2}

are polynomials in disjoint sets of variables and
[
HMM+13
, Proposition 3.77]
.
Now the SLP of
A_{F}
follows from
[
HW07
, Theorem 3.10]
and that
A_{G_{1}}

and
A_{G_{2}}
have the SLP. Note that
A_{G_{1}}
is a monomial complete intersection and, thus, it has the SLP in characteristic zero by
[
Sta78
]
while
A_{G_{2}}
is an AG algebra of codimension 3 with Macaulay dual generator a binomial, and hence by
[
ADF+25
, Theorem 3.45]
it satisfies SLP.
∎

4.
Open problems

In this last section we would like to formulate the open problems appearing in the introduction in the concrete case of AG algebras having binomial Macaulay dual generator.

Open Problems
.

Fix an integer
n\geq 3
and consider a binomial

F=X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}(X_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{b_{r}}-X_{r+1}^{b_{r+1}}\cdots X_{n}^{b_{n}}),\quad 1\leq r\leq n-1.

We ask:

(1)

To determine in terms of
a_{i}
and
b_{j}
when
A_{F}
is a complete intersection,

(2)

To determine in terms of
a_{i}
and
b_{j}
when
A_{F}
has WLP/SLP,

(3)

To determine bounds for the Sperner number
S_{A_{F}}
of
A_{F}
and for
NS_{A_{F}}
,

(4)

To determine an upper bound for the minimal number of generators of
A_{F}
,

(5)

To determine in terms of
a_{i}
and
b_{j}
when
A_{F}
is a doubling,

(6)

To determine a minimal free resolution of
A_{F}
.

In
[
ADF+25
]
the authors solved all the above problems in the codimension 3 case (
n=3
), while for arbitrary codimenson the problems are largely open, although partial results to some of them are given in previous sections of this paper.

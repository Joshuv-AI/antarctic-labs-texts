A computational approach to the study of finite-complement submonids of an affine cone
J. C. Rosales, R. Tapia-Ramos, A. Vigneron-Tenorio

Abstract
Let 𝒞 ⊆ ℕ p \mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p} be an integer cone. A 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroup S ⊆ 𝒞 S\subseteq\mathcal{C} is an affine semigroup such that the set 𝒞 ∖ S \mathcal{C}\setminus S is finite. Such 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroups are central to our study. We develop new algorithms for computing 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroups with specified invariants, including genus, Frobenius element, and their combinations, among other invariants. To achieve this, we introduce a new class of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroups, termed ℬ \mathcal{B} -semigroups. By fixing the degree lexicographic order, we also research the embedding dimension for both ordinary and mult-embedded ℕ 2 \mathbb{N}^{2} -semigroups. These results are applied to test some generalizations of Wilf’s conjecture.



Key words:
affine semigroup,
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, embedding dimension, Frobenius element, generalized numerical semigroup, genus, rooted tree, Wilf’s conjecture.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers. We consider an affine semigroup
S
to be a finitely generated commutative additive submonoid of
\mathbb{N}^{p}
(for a positive integer
p
) such that the zero element belongs to
S
. For convenience, we use
0
instead of
(0,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
whenever it is unambiguous. It is well known that any affine semigroup
S
admits a unique minimal system of generators, denoted by
\text{msg}(S)
(see
[
22
]
), and the cardinality of the minimal generating set, called the embedding dimension, is represented by
\text{e}(S)
. Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p}
be an affine (integer) cone. A submonoid
S\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup if the set
\mathcal{C}\setminus S
is finite; this structure was introduced in
[
17
]
. When
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{N}^{p}
,
S
is referred to as a generalized numerical semigroup, first defined in
[
14
]
. In the special case where
p=1
, the
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
is known as a numerical semigroup. Note that the structures of
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups and generalized numerical semigroups naturally extend the notion of numerical semigroups to higher dimensions.

Most of the invariants analysed in the study of numerical semigroups can be generalized to
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups. In addition to the embedding dimension, the set
\mathcal{H}(S)=\mathcal{C}\setminus S
is called the set of gaps of
S
, and the genus,
\text{g}(S)
, is the cardinality of
\mathcal{H}(S)
. Following the notation given in
[
19
]
, let
\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{t}\}
be the set of extremal rays of
\mathcal{C}
. For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, the i-multiplicity of
S
, denoted by
\text{mult}_{i}(S)
, is the minimum element in
\tau_{i}\cap S
under the componentwise partial order in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
. To extend certain invariants to
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups, it is necessary to define a total order on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
, which is an order relation
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
that is compatible with addition and satisfies
0\preceq x
for any
x\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
(see
[
10
]
). Once a total order
\preceq
is fixed on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
, for instance, the Frobenius element of
S
,
\mathrm{Fb}(S)
, is defined as
\max_{\preceq}(\mathcal{C}\setminus S)
. By convention, if
S=\mathcal{C}
, then
\mathrm{Fb}(S)=(-1,-1,\ldots,-1)
. The conductor of
S
, denoted by
\text{c}(S)
, is the minimum element
x\in S
such that
\mathrm{Fb}(S)\prec x
. An element
s
of
S
is said to be a small element if
s\prec\mathrm{Fb}(S)
. The set of all small elements is denoted by
\text{N}(S)
, and its cardinality by
\text{n}(S)
. Additionally, the smallest non-zero element of
S
with respect to the total order
\preceq
is called the multiplicity of
S
, denoted by
m(S)
. For any element
f
of
\mathcal{C}
, let
\mathcal{N}(f)
the cardinality of the set
\{x\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}\mid x\preceq f\}
. In particular, when
f
is the Frobenius element of
S
,
\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{Fb}(S))
is referred to as the Frobenius number of
S
. In the case of numerical semigroups, note that
\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{Fb}(S))=\mathrm{Fb}(S)
.

Although the study of
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups and generalized numerical semigroup is relatively recent, much research has focused on examining these structures through their invariants. For instance,
[
7
]
and
[
14
]
include algorithms for computing all possible
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-semigroups with a fixed genus. A recent study on the unbounded behaviour of certain invariants, such as the conductor, in
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups can be found in
[
1
]
. Moreover,
[
16
]
provides a method to compute the set of all
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups with a fixed Frobenius element, defined as
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)=\{S\text{ is a }\mathcal{C}\text{-semigroup}\mid\mathrm{Fb}(S)=f\}
, we provide an alternative procedure for computing it.

In this work, we contribute to this ongoing research by developing and describing several algorithms to compute all possible
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups with specified invariants, including the small elements and the genus, the Frobenius element, the genus, and the combination thereof, i.e., we focus on
\mathfrak{C}(\text{gen}=g,\text{se}=n)
which corresponds to
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups with a fixed genus and a number of small elements,
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f,\text{gen}=g)
for a fixed Frobenius element and genus, and
\mathfrak{C}(\text{gen}=g)
denotes the set of all
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups with genus
g
. Additionally, we develop a new class of
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups based on their invariants, which we call
\mathcal{B}
-semigroups. We provide a graphical classification of these semigroups and show how the study of
\mathcal{B}
-semigroups is a tool for computing the set
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)
.

In 1978, Wilf conjectured that for any numerical semigroup
S
, the inequality
\text{e}(S)\text{n}(S)\geq\mathrm{Fb}(S)+1

holds (see
[
25
]
). Although the general case of this conjecture remains unresolved, specific cases have been addressed (see, for instance,
[
12
,
13
]
and
[
15
]
). A detailed discussion of this topic can be found in
[
11
]
and the references therein. The suggestion to extend Wilf’s conjecture to higher dimensional structures was proposed in
[
14
]
, leading to several contributions such as the Generalize Wilf Conjecture (see
[
6
]
) and the Extended Wilf Conjecture (see
[
17
]
).

Following the analysis of invariants, another objective of this work is to discuss the embedding dimension and the minimal generated set of two specific classes of
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroups: ordinary
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroups and mult-embedded
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroups. Given a total order
\preceq
, an ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup is defined as a semigroup
S_{\preceq,c}=\{0\}\cup\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid x\succeq c\}
for some
c\in\mathcal{C}
. Note that our definition of an ordinary semigroup differs from the one given in
[
5
]
.
In the context of numerical semigroups, it is also known as
half-lines
(see
[
23
]
). In contrast, a mult-embedded
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup is defined as
S=\{m,2m,\ldots,(k-1)m\}\sqcup S_{\preceq,km}
, where
m
is a non-zero element of
\mathcal{C}
.
For
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{N}^{2}
, studying the embedding dimension of these classes of semigroups ordered by the graded lexicographic order allows us to test the Generalized and the Extended Wilf Conjecture for
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroups.

The content of this work is organized as follows: In Section
1
, we study the sets
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f,gen=g)
and
\mathfrak{C}(gen=g)
. We show some bounds for computing the mentioned invariants. Section
2
is devoted to study
\mathcal{B}
-semigroups. Besides, some procedures are given to compute the sets of all
\mathcal{B}
-semigroups with a fixed genus, a fixed Frobenius element, and both fixed genus and Frobenius element. In Section
3
, we provide an algorithm to compute the set
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)
by using
\mathcal{B}
-semigroups. In the last sections (Section
4
and
5
), we prove that ordinary and mult-embedded
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroups ordered by the graded lexicographic order satisfy the Generalized and the Extended Wilf Conjecture. The results introduced throughout are not only theoretical but also provide computational methods, which are illustrated through examples. To this aim, we have used third-party software (
Normaliz

[
3
]
) and some libraries developed by the authors in
Mathematica

[
26
]
.

1
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups with fixed Frobenius element and genus, and fixed genus

Given
A
a non-empty subset of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{p}
, with
\mathbb{R}_{\geq}
denoting the set of non-negative real numbers, the real cone determined by
A
is

L(A)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{h}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\mid h\in\mathbb{N},\,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{h}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq},\,a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}\in A\right\},

and the integer cone determined by
A
is the set
L(A)\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}
. In general, a non-degenerated real (or integer) cone is the set of real (or integer) points belonging to the convex hull of finitely many half lines in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{p}
emanating from the origin.

An integer cone
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p}
is affine, that is, it is finitely generated, if there is a finite subset
A
of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{C}=L(A)\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}
. In
[
2
, Chapter 2]
is proved that a cone
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p}
is affine if and only if it has a rational point in each of its extremal rays. Moreover, any subsemigroup of
\mathcal{C}
is finitely generated if and only if there exists an element in the subsemigroup in each extremal ray of
\mathcal{C}
. We assume that any integer cone considered in this work is affine.

Fix an integer cone
\mathcal{C}
, and a total order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
. Recall that

\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f,gen=g)=\{S\text{ is a }\mathcal{C}\text{-semigroup }\mid\mathrm{Fb}(S)=f,\text{g}(S)=g\}.

In this section, we have two main goals. The objectives are to describe an algorithm for the computation of
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f,gen=g)
and to provide a procedure to compute those
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups with a fixed genus. We consider some bounds to the genus and the Frobenius number of the semigroups to achieve these.

Using the terminology from
[
18
]
, a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup is called irreducible if it cannot be expressed as the intersection of two
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups that properly contain it. For any
x,y\in L\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p}
, consider the partial order
x\leq_{L}y
if
y-x\in L
. The following proposition establishes an irreducible
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup from an existing
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, we provide the most general case applying any total order.

Proposition 1
.

[
16
, Lemma 12]

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup and let
f\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, the set

\Delta(S,f)=\bigg(\mathcal{C}\setminus\{f\}\bigg)\setminus\bigg\{x\in S\setminus\{0\}\mid x\leq_{\mathcal{C}}f,\text{ and }x\preceq\frac{f}{2}\bigg\}

is an irreducible
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, with Frobenius element
f
.

The next proposition combines Corollaries 8 and 9 in
[
16
]
. We denote by
\mathcal{B}(f)
the set
\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid x\leq_{\mathcal{C}}f\}
with
f\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
. From now on,
\lceil\cdot\rceil
denotes the ceiling function, which rounds up to the nearest integer, and for any set
A
, the symbol
\sharp
denotes the cardinality of the set
A
. Besides, using the notation from
[
20
]
, for any two natural numbers
a
and
b
, with

a\leq b
, the set
\llbracket a,b\rrbracket=\{r\in\mathbb{N}\mid a\leq r\leq b\}
. If
a=0
, instead of
\llbracket 0,b\rrbracket
, we use
\llbracket b\rrbracket
for short.

Proposition 2
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup with Frobenius element
f
. Then,
S
is irreducible if and only if
\text{g}(S)=\left\lceil\dfrac{\sharp\mathcal{B}(f)}{2}\right\rceil
.

In the specific context of
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-semigroups, the value of the cardinality of
\mathcal{B}(f)
can be determined explicitly as
\sharp\mathcal{B}(f)=\prod_{i\in\llbracket p\rrbracket}(f_{i}+1)
for
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{p})\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

Corollary 3
.

[
8
, Theorem 5.6 and Theorem 5.7]

Let
S
be a
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-semigroup with Frobenius element
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{p})
. Then,
S
is irreducible if and only if
\text{g}(S)=\left\lceil\frac{\prod_{i\in\llbracket p\rrbracket}(f_{i}+1)}{2}\right\rceil
.

We gather the previous results to obtain the announcement result. For any element
f\in\mathcal{C}
, recall that
\mathcal{N}(f)
is the cardinality of the set
\{x\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}\mid x\preceq f\}
. For this section, we need a total order
\preceq
such that
\mathcal{N}(f)
is finite. So, we assume that the fixed total order
\preceq
satisfies that property. For example, a graded order can be used (see
[
10
]
).

Theorem 4
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
and any positive integer
g
, the set
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f,gen=g)
is non-empty if and only if

\left\lceil\frac{\sharp\mathcal{B}(f)}{2}\right\rceil\leq g\leq\mathcal{N}(f).

Proof.

Assume that
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f,gen=g)
is non-empty, and let
S\in\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f,gen=g)
. Trivially,
\text{g}(S)\leq\mathcal{N}(f)
. To the other inequality, consider
X=\mathcal{B}(f)\cap S
and
Y=\mathcal{B}(f)\setminus S
. Define the injective map
\varphi:X\longrightarrow Y
via
\varphi(x)=f-x
. Note that
\varphi
is well-defined, since
f-x\notin S
, otherwise
f\in S
, which it is not possible. Therefore,
\sharp X\leq\sharp Y
, and since
\mathcal{B}(f)
equals the disjoint union of
X
and
Y

\text{g}(S)\geq\sharp Y\geq\big\lceil\frac{\sharp\mathcal{B}(f)}{2}\big\rceil
.

Conversely, by combining Propositions
1
and
2

\Delta(\mathcal{C},f)=(\mathcal{C}\setminus\{f\})\setminus\{x\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}\mid x\leq_{\mathcal{C}}f,\text{ and }x\preceq\frac{f}{2}\}
belongs to
\mathfrak{C}\left(Fb=f,gen=\big\lceil\frac{\sharp\mathcal{B}(f)}{2}\big\rceil\right)

T_{0}=\Delta(\mathcal{C},f)
, and
T_{i+1}=T_{i}\setminus\{\text{m}(T_{i})\}

\text{m}(T_{i})\prec f
, otherwise
T_{i+1}=T_{i}
, for every positive integer
i

i_{0}
such that
T_{i_{0}}=T_{i_{0}+1}
, and
\cup_{i=0}^{i_{0}}\text{g}(T_{i})=\llbracket\big\lceil\frac{\sharp\mathcal{B}(f)}{2}\big\rceil,\mathcal{N}(f)\rrbracket
.
∎

Since
\sharp\mathcal{B}(f)

\prod_{i\in\llbracket p\rrbracket}(f_{i}+1)

\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{N}^{p}

\mathbb{N}^{p}

Corollary 5

For any
f\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\setminus\{0\}

\left\lceil\frac{\prod_{i\in\llbracket p\rrbracket}(f_{i}+1)}{2}\right\rceil\leq g\leq\mathcal{N}(f).

Motivated by their relationship between the genus and the number of Frobenius, precisely,
\text{n}(S)-1+\text{g}(S)=\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{Fb}(S))

\mathcal{C}
-semigroups with a fixed genus and a fixed number of small elements.

Proposition 7
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup,

, and let

be a positive integer such that
\big\lceil\frac{\sharp\mathcal{B}(f)}{2}\big\rceil\leq g\leq\mathcal{N}(f)

\text{n}(S)=\mathcal{N}(f)-1-g

\text{g}(S)=g

To achieve the announcement method, for any two natural numbers

\mathfrak{C}(\text{gen}=g,\text{se}=n)

\mathcal{C}
-semigroups with genus

-semigroups depend on the choice of total order, the set
\mathfrak{C}(\text{gen}=g,\text{se}=n)
does as well. We deduce the following result as a consequence of Proposition

Corollary 9
.

If
g
and

are two positive integers, then

\mathfrak{C}(gen=g,se=n)=\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f,gen=g),

Based on Corollary

such that

, and the second one is to apply Algorithm

Recall that
\mathfrak{C}(gen=g)
is the set formed by all the
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups with genus equals

\mathfrak{C}(gen=g)
. Note that this set is non-empty since the ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S,

\displaystyle S_{i+1}

Let us start by introducing some notations. Again, an integer cone
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p}
and a total order
\preceq

\mathbb{N}^{p}
are fixed.
Given
f\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
S,T\in\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)
, we define an equivalence relation
\sim
such that
S\sim T
if and only if
\mathcal{O}(S)=\mathcal{O}(T)
, recall that
\mathcal{O}(S)=S\cup(\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{B}(f))
. Equivalently,
S\sim T
if and only if
S\cap\mathcal{B}(f)=T\cap\mathcal{B}(f)
. For
S\in\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)
, the equivalence class of
S
modulo
\sim
, called its
\sim
-class, is defined as
[S]=\{T\in\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)\mid S\sim T\}
.
The collection of all
\sim
-classes forms a partition of
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)
, denoted by
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)/_{\sim}
.

This section aims to compute
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)
. To this end, we study the partition
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)/_{\sim}
as a tool for the desired computation. We show how the set
[S]
can be arranged in a tree for any
S\in\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)
.

The following proposition establishes the relationship between the partition
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)/_{\sim}
and the set
\mathfrak{B}(Fb=f)
, proving that the set
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)/_{\sim}
can be determined from the
\mathcal{B}
-semigroups with Frobenius element

Proposition 18
.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
, then
\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)/_{\sim}=\{[R]\mid R\in\mathfrak{B}(Fb=f)\}
. Moreover, if
R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}(Fb=f)
such that
R_{1}\neq R_{2}
, then
[R_{1}]\cap[R_{2}]=\emptyset
.

Proof.

To prove the first statement, consider
S\in\mathfrak{C}(Fb=f)
, by definition,
[S]=[R]
, where
R=\mathcal{O}(S)\in\mathfrak{B}(Fb=f)
. For the second statement, assume
R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}(Fb=f)
such that
R_{1}\neq R_{2}
, this implies that there is at least one gap
x
of
R_{i}
such that
x\in R_{j}
with
i,j\in\{1,2\}
and
i\neq j
. Thus
R_{1}\not\sim R_{2}
, hence
[R_{1}]\cap[R_{2}]=\emptyset
.
∎

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup with Frobenius element
\mathrm{Fb}(S)=f
, we define
\lambda(S)
as the maximum element in
\left(\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{B}(f)\right)\cap\mathcal{H}(S)
with respect to the fixed total order on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
. If

\lambda(S)=0
. We deduce the following result from the maximality of
\lambda(S)
. Let
R\in\mathfrak{B}(Fb=f)

Lemma 19
.

If
S\in[R]
, then
S\cup\{\lambda(S)\}\in[R]
.

We define the directed graph
G([R])
whose vertex set is the
\sim
-class
[R]
, and
(S,T)\in[R]^{2}
is directed edge if and only if
T=S\cup\{\lambda(S)\}
. In particular, as mentioned before, if
(S,T)
is a directed edge,
S
is usually known as a child of
T
.

Theorem 20
.

The graph
G([R])
is a tree with root
\mathcal{O}(R)
. Furthermore, the set of children of any
T\in[R]

\{T\setminus\{x\}\mid x\in msg(T)\text{ and }0\neq\lambda(T)\prec x\prec f\}.

Proof.

Let
S\in[R]
such that
S\neq\mathcal{O}(R)
. We construct the sequence
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq[R]
defined by

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle S_{i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}S_{i}\cup\{\lambda(S_{i})\}&\text{ if }S_{i}\neq\mathcal{O}(R),\\
S_{i}&\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Since
\lambda(S_{i})\in\mathcal{H}(S_{i})

\lambda(S_{i})

S_{i}

4
Ordinary
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroups

Fixed a total order
\preceq
, we say that a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
is ordinary if
S=\{0\}\cup\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid x\succeq c\}
for some
c\in\mathcal{C}
, and it is denoted by
S_{c}
which depends on the choice of the order. Equivalently,
S
is ordinary if the conductor of
S
equals the multiplicity of
S
. Note that an ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup containing all the
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups determined by the given total order and the multiplicity.

Our goal in this section is to prove that any ordinary
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroup ordered by the graded lexicographic order satisfies the Generalized Wilf Conjecture and the Extended Wilf Conjecture. We study the minimal generating set of any ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup to achieve this. First, we provide a lower bound for the embedding dimension of any ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup.

Proposition 23
.

Let
S
be an ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. Then
\text{g}(S)<\text{e}(S)
.

Proof.

We fix a total order
\preceq
and assume that
S
is an ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. Trivially, if
S=\mathcal{C}
, then
\text{e}(S)>0
. We proceed by induction on
\text{g}(S)
. Suppose that such that
\text{g}(S)<\text{e}(S)
, and we show that
\text{g}(S\setminus\{m\})<\text{e}(S\setminus\{m\})
where
m=\text{m}(S)
. Note that
\text{g}(S\setminus\{m\})=\text{g}(S)+1
. Consider
r=\min_{\preceq}(S\setminus\{0,m\}),
and
t=\min_{\preceq}(S\setminus\{0,m,r\})
the second and third minimum elements of
S\setminus\{0\}
, respectively. Let
z\in\{2m,m+r,m+t\}
, if
z
is not a minimal generator of
S\setminus\{m\}
, then
z=a+b
, where
a
and
b
are two non-zero elements of
S\setminus\{m\}
. Without loss of generality, we deduce
a\prec m
, which leads to a contradiction, since
m
is the multiplicity of
S
. So,
2m,m+r,m+t
are minimal generators of
S\setminus\{m\}
. From
[
17
, Lemma 3]
follows
\text{msg}(S)\setminus\{m\}\subset\text{msg}(S\setminus\{m\})
, hence
\text{e}(S\setminus\{m\})\geq\text{e}(S)-1+3
. By applying the induction hypothesis,
\text{g}(S\setminus\{m\})=\text{g}(S)+1<\text{e}(S)+1<\text{e}(S\setminus\{m\})
, which completes the proof.
∎

To emphasize the relevance of the ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups, we introduce two families of
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups: arf semigroups and saturated semigroups, and we prove that any ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup belongs to both families. Recall that for any positive integer
b
,
we denote the set
\llbracket b\rrbracket=\{0,1,2,\ldots,b\}
.

We say that an affine semigroup
S
is an arf (affine) semigroup if, for any
x,y,z\in S
with
x\geq_{\mathcal{C}}y\geq_{\mathcal{C}}z
, then
x+y-z\in S
. We say that
S
is a saturated (affine) semigroup if
s,s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in S
are such that
s_{i}\leq_{\mathcal{C}}s
for all
i\in\llbracket r\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
are such that
z_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+z_{r}s_{r}\in\mathcal{C}
then,
s+z_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+z_{r}s_{r}\in S
. In the case of numerical semigroups, both classes of semigroups have been studied in the literature (see, for example,
[
4
]
and
[
24
]
).

The following lemma generalizes Proposition 3.31 in
[
23
]
from numerical semigroups to affine semigroups.

Lemma 24
.

Every saturated semigroup is an arf semigroup.

Proof.

Let
S
be a saturated affine semigroup. Take
x,y,z\in S
such that
x\geq_{\mathcal{C}}y\geq_{\mathcal{C}}z
, implying
y-z
belongs to
\mathcal{C}
. By definition of being saturated, we have that
x+y-z\in S
.
∎

Proposition 25
.

Every ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup is a saturated semigroup.

Proof.

Let
S_{c}
an ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. Suppose
s,s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in S_{c}
such that
s_{i}\leq_{\mathcal{C}}s
for all
i\in\llbracket r\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
t=z_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+z_{r}s_{r}\in\mathcal{C}
. By hypothesis

, for some
\lambda\in\mathcal{C}
. Since

and

is an ordinary, then

.
∎

Corollary 26
.

Every ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup is an arf semigroup.

The following definitions will be needed throughout the remainder of the work. Let
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{p})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{p}
be a vector, and consider the map
\pi_{w}:\mathbb{N}^{p}\to\mathbb{N}
defined via
\pi_{w}(x)=w\cdot x
, where
\cdot
denotes the inner product. For any
x,y\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we define
x\preceq_{w}y
if and only if
\pi_{w}(x)\leq\pi_{w}(y)
. We refer to
\preceq_{w}
as the weight order determined by
w
.

To determine a weight order in general, we choose a primary weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{p}
. A secondary weight vector
u\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{p}
is employed to break ties. If ties persist (i.e., when
\pi_{w}(x)=\pi_{w}(y)
and
\pi_{u}(x)=\pi_{u}(y)
), a third weight vector is introduced, and so on. Thus, every monomial order
\preceq
can be obtained through this finite process of applying weight vectors. From now on, we are interested in the first weight vector. Hence, we use
\pi_{\preceq}
instead of
\pi_{w}
. Graphically, it can be interpreted as the existence of a hyperplane that separates the space into two regions, one containing
x
and the other containing
y
. For a detailed treatment of monomial orders and their relation to weight orders, consult
[
10
]
and
[
21
]
. We assume that the vector
w
defining the fixed order has non-zero entries.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup with
t
external rays, and minimally generated by
\text{msg}(S)=E\sqcup A
with
E=\cup_{i\in\llbracket t\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}}\text{mult}_{i}(S)=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}\}
, and
A=\{m_{t+1},\ldots,m_{r}\}
. The next result states that the minimal system of generators of an ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup is bounded.

Lemma 27
.

Let
S_{c}
be an ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup and
x\in\text{msg}(S_{c})
. Then,
\pi_{\preceq}(x)\leq\pi_{\preceq}(m)
, where
m=\sum_{i\in\llbracket t\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}}\text{mult}_{i}(S_{c})
.

Proof.

Let
x\in S_{c}
such that
\pi_{\preceq}(x)>\pi_{\preceq}(m)
we have to show that
x\notin\text{msg}(S_{c})
. We assume that
x-m_{i}\notin S_{c}
for any
i\in\llbracket t\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}
, otherwise it is easily checked that
x
is not a minimal generator. We distinguish the following cases:

•

If there exists
j\in\llbracket t\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}
such that
x-m_{j}\in\mathcal{C}
, then
x=m_{j}+h
for some
h\in\mathcal{H}(S_{c})
. Given that
\pi_{\preceq}
is a linear map, we have
\pi_{\preceq}(x)=\pi_{\preceq}(m_{j})+\pi_{\preceq}(h)>\sum_{i\in\llbracket t\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}}\pi_{\preceq}(m_{i})
. This implies that
\pi_{\preceq}(h)>\sum_{i\in\llbracket t\rrbracket\setminus\{j\}}\pi_{\preceq}(m_{i})>\pi_{\preceq}(c)
. Since
\pi_{\preceq}
is an increasing map,
h\succ c
, and thus,
h\in S_{c}
, which it is not possible.

•

If
x-m_{i}\notin\mathcal{C}
for any
i\in\llbracket t\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}
, since
x
belongs to
S_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, there exist some rational numbers
0\leq\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t}<1
such that
x=\sum_{i\in\llbracket t\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}}\lambda_{i}m_{i}
. So,
\pi_{\preceq}(x)=\sum_{i\in\llbracket t\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}}\lambda_{i}\pi_{\preceq}(m_{i})<\sum_{i\in\llbracket t\rrbracket}\pi_{\preceq}(m_{i})=\pi_{\preceq}(m)
, in contradiction with the hypothesis.

∎

Building on this, the following lemma proves a more robust result about the relationship between some elements in
\mathcal{C}
and the minimal system of generators of
S_{c}
. From now on, we assume that the conductor

is non-zero.

Lemma 28
.

Let
S_{c}
be an ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup and
x\in S_{c}
such that
\pi_{\preceq}(x)<2\pi_{\preceq}(c)
, then
x\in\text{msg}(S_{c})
.

Proof.

Let
x\in S_{c}
such that
\pi_{\preceq}(x)<2\pi_{\preceq}(c)
, and suppose that
x=s_{1}+s_{2}
for some
s_{1},s_{2}\in S_{c}
. Since
S_{c}
is an ordinary
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup,
\pi_{\preceq}(s_{1}),\pi_{\preceq}(s_{2})\geq\pi_{\preceq}(c)
. Consequently
\pi_{\preceq}(x)=\pi_{\preceq}(s_{1})+\pi_{\preceq}(s_{2})\geq 2\pi_{\preceq}(c)
, contradicting the initial assumption.
∎

In particular, if
S_{c}
is an ordinary
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroup, and
\preceq_{glex}
corresponds to the graded lexicographic order, the set
\text{msg}(S_{c})
can be explicitly determined.
Note that in this context, the map
\pi_{\preceq_{glex}}:\mathbb{N}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is defined via
\pi_{\preceq_{glex}}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}+x_{2}
. From now on, we use the symbol
\pi
instead of
\pi_{\preceq_{glex}}
for short.

Proposition 29
.

Let
S_{c}
be an ordinary
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroup with conductor
c=(0,c_{2})
, ordered by
\preceq_{glex}
. Then,
S_{c}
is minimally generated by

\left\{x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid\pi(c)\leq\pi(x)\leq 2\pi(c)-1\right\}.

Therefore,
\text{e}(S_{c})=\dfrac{c_{2}(3c_{2}+1\big)}{2}
.

Proof.

Since
\pi(\text{mult}_{i}(S_{c}))=\pi(c)
for each
i=1,2
, and by applying Lemmas
27
and
28
, it suffices to show that any element
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
satisfying
\pi(x)\geq 2\pi(c)
cannot be a generator of
S_{c}
. If
x_{1},x_{2}<c_{2}
, then
\pi(x)<2\pi(c)
which it is impossible by hypothesis. Thus, without loss of generality, we assume
x_{1}\geq c_{2}
. It follows that
x-(c_{2},0)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, and since
\pi(x-(c_{2},0))\geq\pi(c)
, it can be deduced that
x-(c_{2},0)\in S_{c}
. Therefore,
x\notin\text{msg}(S_{c})
.

The embedding dimension of
S_{c}
is determined by the number of solutions
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
of
c_{2}\leq x_{1}+x_{2}\leq 2c_{2}-1
. Equivalently, it is the number of natural solutions of the equation of
x_{1}+x_{2}=k
, where
k\in\{c_{2},c_{2}+1,\ldots,2c_{2}-1\}
. Note that the above expression involves
2c_{2}
equations. Fixed
k
, it is straightforward to deduce that
x_{1}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
and consequently,
x_{2}=k-x_{1}
then, there exist
k+1
solutions. Thus,

\displaystyle\text{e}(S_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=c_{2}}^{2c_{2}-1}(k+1)=\sum_{i=1}^{c_{2}}(c_{2}+i)=\dfrac{c_{2}(3c_{2}+1\big)}{2}.

∎

The above result corresponds to a particular case discussed within the framework of T-stripe generalized numerical semigroups (see
[
9
, Proposition 3.3]
). In cases where
c_{1}\neq 0
, the determination of
\text{msg}(S_{c})
has not been addressed in the literature, as such semigroups do not fall under the classification of T-stripe
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroups. Therefore, we propose the following proposition. For any total order
\preceq
, let
a\preceq b
be two elements in
\mathcal{C}
. We denote the intervals of elements in
\mathcal{C}
between
a
and
b
under the order
\preceq
as follows:
[a,b]_{\preceq},[a,b)_{\preceq},(a,b]_{\preceq}
and

for the closed interval, left-open interval, right-open interval and open interval, respectively.

Proposition 30
.

Let
S_{c}
be an ordinary
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroup with conductor
c=(c_{1},c_{2})
such that
c_{1}\neq 0
, ordered by
\preceq_{glex}
. Then,
S_{c}
is minimally generated by

\Big[c,\;2c\Big)_{\preceq_{glex}}\sqcup\Big[\big(0,2\pi(c)+1\big),\;c+\big(0,\pi(c)+1\big)\Big)_{\preceq_{glex}}

(4)

and
\text{e}(S_{c})=\dfrac{3\pi^{2}(c)+\pi(c)+4c_{1}}{2}
.

Proof.

From direct application of Lemma
27
, the set (
4
) is a system of generators of
S_{c}
, since
\text{mult}_{1}(S_{c})+\text{mult}_{2}(S_{c})=2\pi(c)+1
. Furthermore, by Lemma

it suffices to analyse those

such that
2\pi(c)\leq\pi(x)\leq 2\pi(c)+1
. We distinguish two cases:
x\not>c
and
x>c
.

If
x\not>c
, where

x_{2}<c_{2}
or
x_{1}<c_{1}
. Suppose that
x_{2}<c_{2}

2c_{1}+2c_{2}\leq x_{1}+x_{2}<x_{1}+c_{2}

, and suppose that
x=s+t
for some

. Since
x_{1}<c_{1}
then

. By the linearity of
\pi

2\pi(c)\leq\pi(x)\leq 2\pi(c)+1
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
\pi(s)=\pi(c)
; otherwise,
\pi(t)=\pi(c)
and the argument is analogous. By definition of
S_{c}
, we have that

, which is a contradiction. Therefore, we conclude that the set (

\text{msg}(S_{c})
.

If

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{N}

and
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\neq 0

\lambda_{1}>c_{1}

then

. Assuming

. By definition of

, it follows that

. Which finishes the first part of the proof.

\displaystyle msg(S_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid\pi(x)=\pi(c)\text{ and }x_{1}\geq c_{1}\}

Remark 31
.

From previous results, we observe that the minimal generating set of an ordinary

Conjecture 32
.

[

Let

\nu(S)e(S)\geq p\,\gamma(S),

[

Let

\text{n}(S)\text{e}(S)\geq\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{Fb}(S))+1.

[

As a consequence of Propositions

Lemma 37

satisfies
\pi(x)
is smaller than or equal to
2k\pi(m)
or
2k\pi(m)+1
, according to the value of
m
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, we consider that its coordinates are
x_{1}
, and
x_{2}
.

Theorem 39
.

Let
k
be a positive integer,
m=(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, and
S
be the mult-embedded
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroup ordered by
\preceq_{glex}
with multiplicity
m
and conductor
km
. Then,
S
is minimally generated by

\begin{split}A=&\{m\}\sqcup\Big(km,\;(k+1)m\Big)_{\preceq_{glex}}\\
&\sqcup\Big\{x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid(k+1)\pi(m)\leq\pi(x)\leq 2k\pi(m)-1\text{ and }x_{2}<m_{2}\Big\}\\
&\sqcup\Big\{x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid(k+1)\pi(m)+1\leq\pi(x)\leq 2k\pi(m)+1\text{ and }x_{1}<m_{1}\Big\},\end{split}

(11)

and
\text{e}(S)=\dfrac{(4k-1)\pi^{2}(m)+\pi(m)+4m_{1}}{2}
.

Proof.

Trivially,
m\in\text{msg}(S)
. Denote
B=\Big(km,\;(k+1)m\Big)_{\preceq_{glex}}
. First, let us prove that
B\subset\text{msg}(S)
. Suppose
x\in B
such that
x=s+t
for some
s,t\in S
. We distinguish the following two cases. Without loss of generality, if we consider
\pi(s)\geq k\pi(m)
, then
\pi(t)<k\pi(m)
, and since,
S
is a mult-embedded
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroup, it follows that
t=qm
, for some positive integer
q
. Then,
\pi(x)=\pi(s)+\pi(qm)\geq k\pi(m)+q\pi(m)\geq(k+1)\pi(m)
, which contradicts the initial hypothesis.
So, we assume
\pi(s),\pi(t)<k\pi(m)
, and then we have that
s
and
t
are multiples of
m
. Consequently,
x
is a multiple of
m
. So,
x\notin B
and it leads to a false statement.

Now, let
x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
such that
(k+1)\pi(m)\leq\pi(x)\leq 2k\pi(m)-1
, and
x_{2}<m_{2}
. If
x=s+t
, for some
s=(s_{1},s_{2})
and
t=(t_{1},t_{2})
belonging to
S
, then
s_{2},t_{2}<m_{2}
. It implies that
s
and
t
are not multiples of
m
, and so
\pi(s),\pi(t)\geq k\pi(m)
. Consequently,
\pi(x)\geq 2k\pi(m)
. Again, we get a contradiction. Therefore,
x\in\text{msg}(S)
.

Assuming that
x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
such that
(k+1)\pi(m)+1\leq\pi(x)\leq 2k\pi(m)+1
, and
x_{1}<m_{1}
, and using a similar structure as above, if
x=s+t
, for some
s,t\in S
, then
s_{1},t_{1}<m_{1}
. So,
\pi(s),\pi(t)\geq k\pi(m)+1
, and thus,
\pi(x)\geq 2k\pi(m)+2
, which it is not possible. Hence,
x\in\text{msg}(S)
. Summarizing, we have just proved
that
A
is a subset of
\text{msg}(S)
.

From Remark
38
,
A
is the minimal generating set of
S
if and only if no minimal generators belong to the set
\{x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid\pi(x)\leq 2k\pi(m)\}\setminus A
, when
m_{1}=0
, or
\{x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid\pi(x)\leq 2k\pi(m)+1\}\setminus A
, when
m_{1}\neq 0
.

Consider
x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
such that
x\geq m
and
x\succeq_{glex}(k+1)m
. Trivially,
(k+1)m\notin\text{msg}(S)
. So, we assume that
x\succ_{glex}(k+1)m
. Since
x\geq m
,
x=m+\lambda
, for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
. And thus
\pi(m)+\pi(\lambda)\geq(k+1)\pi(m)
, which implies that
\pi(\lambda)\geq k\pi(m)
. We distinguish two cases depending on the value of
\pi(\lambda)
. If
\pi(\lambda)>k\pi(m)
, then
\lambda\in S
. So, we conclude that
x\notin msg(S)
. If
\pi(\lambda)=k\pi(m)
, and as
x\succ_{glex}(k+1)m
, then
x=((k+1)m_{1}+i,(k+1)m_{2}-i)
for some
i\in\llbracket km_{2}\rrbracket\setminus\{0\}
. Hence,
x-m=(km_{1}+i,km_{2}-i)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, and
\pi(x-m)=k\pi(m)
. We deduce that
x-m\in S
, so
x\notin msg(S)
.

Finally, suppose
x\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, with
x_{2}<m_{2}
such that
\pi(x)=2k\pi(m)+i
where
i\in\{0,1\}
.
Note that
x_{1}+m_{2}>x_{1}+x_{2}=\pi(x)\geq 2km_{1}+2km_{2}
, which implies that
x_{1}>2k\pi(m)-m_{2}
. Take
s=(k\pi(m),0)\in S
. Since
x-s=(x_{1}-k\pi(m),x_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, and
\pi(x-s)=k\pi(m)+i\geq k\pi(m)
, we obtain that
x-s
belongs to
S
. Whence,
x
is not a minimal generator.

By definition,
\text{e}(S_{c})=\sharp A
. Similarly to the proof of Lemma
30
,

\displaystyle\sharp A

\displaystyle=1+km_{2}+\sum_{l=k\pi(m)+1}^{(k+1)\pi(m)-1}(l+1)+(k+1)m_{1}

\displaystyle+m_{2}(k-1)\pi(m)+m_{1}\big((k-1)\pi(m)+1\big)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(4k-1)\pi^{2}(m)+\pi(m)+4m_{1}}{2}.

∎

The following corollary is a direct consequence of the Theorem
39
.

Corollary 40
.

Every mult-embedded
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroup, ordered by
\preceq_{glex}
, satisfies the Generalized Wilf Conjecture and Extended Wilf Conjecture.

Proof.

Let
S
be a mult-embedded
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-semigroup with conductor
km=k(m_{1},m_{2})
, ordered by
\preceq_{glex}
. Given Proposition
34
, it is enough to show that the inequality (
9
) holds. Trivially,
\nu(S)=k
, and we assume that
k>1
, otherwise, it has already been proved (Corollary
35
). If
c_{1}=0
then
\gamma(S)=km_{2}
. Thus,

ke(S)=k\frac{(4k-1)m_{2}^{2}+m_{2}}{2}\geq k\frac{((4k-1)m_{2}+1)m_{2}}{2}\geq 2\gamma(S),

which it is true, since
(4k-1)m_{2}+1\geq 4
for all
k>1
, and natural number
m_{2}
.
If
c_{1}\neq 0
, then
\gamma(S)=km_{1}(km_{2}+2)
. According to Theorem
39
,

\displaystyle ke(S)

\displaystyle=k\frac{(4k-1)\pi^{2}(m)+\pi(m)+4m_{1}}{2}\geq k\frac{3k\pi^{2}(m)+5m_{1}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4k^{2}m_{1}m_{2}+3k^{2}m_{1}^{2}+5km_{1}}{2}\geq\frac{4k^{2}m_{1}m_{2}+8km_{1}}{2}=2\gamma(S),

which proves the result.
∎

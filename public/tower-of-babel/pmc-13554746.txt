Reply
Authors: Juri Iwata, Kentaro Hayashida
Published: 2026

We sincerely thank Dr Useini and colleagues for their thoughtful comments regarding our recent study1 and for emphasizing the growing importance of long-term outcomes and sex-specific investigations in transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR).

We fully agree that women have historically been under-represented in cardiovascular research and that dedicated studies are essential as TAVR expands to younger patients with longer life expectancy. In this context, individualized treatment strategies that integrate patient anatomy, clinical characteristics, procedural risk, and anticipated lifetime management are becoming increasingly important.

As highlighted in our study, self-expanding valves demonstrated consistently superior hemodynamic performance throughout 7 years of follow-up. However, these favorable echocardiographic findings did not translate into an independent prognostic advantage after multivariable adjustment. Instead, established clinical factors—including age, frailty, paravalvular leakage, and severe prosthesis-patient mismatch—were more strongly associated with long-term outcomes than transcatheter heart valve type itself. These findings underscore that valve selection should not be based solely on hemodynamic performance but rather on a comprehensive assessment of patient-specific anatomical and clinical characteristics.

We also share the authors' perspective that the discussion should extend beyond a simple comparison between transcatheter heart valve platforms or between TAVR and surgical aortic valve replacement. As contemporary transcatheter heart valves continue to evolve, the objective should be to optimize individualized valve therapy through multidisciplinary heart team decision-making while considering long-term durability and lifetime management.

We appreciate the authors for placing our findings within the broader context of personalized cardiovascular care and believe that continued investigation using contemporary devices with longer follow-up will further refine optimal valve selection for women undergoing TAVR.

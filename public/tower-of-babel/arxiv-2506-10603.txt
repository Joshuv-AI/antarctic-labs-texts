Abstract
. Graph products of monoids provide a common framework for free products and direct products. Trace monoids are graph products of finitely generated free monoids. We investigate the interaction of certain finitary conditions with graph products. Specifically, we examine the conditions of being weakly left noetherian (that is, every left ideal is finitely generated) and weakly left coherent (that is, every finitely generated left ideal has a finite presentation) and the related conditions of the ascending chain condition on principal left ideals, being left ideal Howson, and being finitely left equated. All of these conditions, and others, are preserved under retract; as a consequence, if a graph product has such a property, then so do all the constituent monoids. We show that the converse is also true for all the conditions listed except that of being weakly left noetherian. In the latter case we precisely determine the graph products of monoids which are weakly left noetherian.

1.
Introduction

A finitary condition for a class of algebras is one possessed by all its finite members: this paper examines finitary conditions in the context of graph products of monoids. Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a simple graph and let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{\alpha}:\alpha\in V\}
be a set of monoids, referred to as vertex monoids; we do not assume that
\Gamma
is finite. The graph product of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to
\Gamma
is the freest monoid generated by
\bigcup_{\alpha\in V}M_{\alpha}
such that each
M_{\alpha}
is a submonoid, and elements in monoids

M_{\alpha}
and
M_{\beta}
commute if
(\alpha,\beta)\in E
. A formal definition in terms of presentations is given in Section
2
. Free products and restricted direct products (that is, the submonoid of elements of direct products with finite support) are instances of graph products at opposite extremes. In the former,
E=\emptyset
so that non-identity elements in distinct vertex monoids do not commute. In the latter,
E=V\times V
so that all elements in distinct vertex monoids commute. There are many sources and motivations for graph products; in the form given here the concept was introduced by Green
[
15
]
for groups and by da Costa
[
6
]
for monoids. Trace monoids and right angled Artin groups, where
\Gamma
is finite, and the vertex monoids are
finitely generated free monoids or free groups, respectively, provide important instances. The former were introduced in the study of combinatorial problems for rearrangement of words; they have been extensively studied by mathematicians and computer scientists (see
[
7
,
8
]
).

It is a natural question whether certain monoid properties pass between a graph product and its vertex monoids. Algorithmic properties have been extensively considered
[
6
,
15
,
16
]
as have algebraic properties
[
1
,
10
,
13
]
. Recently, the authors, together with Cho and Ruškuc, show that a graph product of monoids is residually finite if and only if so is each vertex monoid
[
4
]
.
In this article we again address finitary conditions, in this case those related to noetherianity and coherency.

Recall that a ring is left noetherian if every left ideal is finitely generated and is left coherent if every finitely generated left ideal has a finite presentation. For monoids there are two natural choices for replacements for each of these notions. Those depending on left congruences are known as left noetherianity and left coherency and those depending on left ideals as weak left noetherianity and weak left coherency. In terms of understanding their behaviour with respect to direct products, let alone graph products, left coherency and left noetherianity are mysterious. The direct product of two left coherent monoids need not be left coherent, although the direct product of a left coherent monoid with a finite monoid is
[
14
]
. It is not known whether an arbitrary direct product of left noetherian monoids is left noetherian, although again it is known that the direct product of a left noetherian monoid with a finite monoid is again left noetherian
[
25
]
.

We are therefore led to examine weak left noetherianity and weak left coherency. The former property again does not pass up perfectly to a graph product from the vertex monoids; essentially, the non-group vertex monoids must all be adjacent with the exception of at most one pair, precisely stated in Theorem
4.9
. Via a standard argument, a monoid is left noetherian if and only if it has the ascending chain condition on left ideas. If we consider the ascending chain condition on
principal
left ideas (ACCPL), then we do have a completely positive result - Theorem
3.13
states that a graph product has ACCPL if and only if so does each vertex monoid. The situation for weak left coherency is equally pleasing. Our main result, Theorem
8.1
, gives that a graph product of monoids is weakly left coherent if and only if so is each vertex monoid. This we do by examining two finitary conditions of importance in their own right: that of being left ideal Howson and that of being
finitely left equated (FLE). The former is also called being finitely (left) aligned
[
9
]
and the latter is equivalent to monogenic left monoid acts having finite presentations
[
5
]
; their conjunction is equivalent to being weakly left coherent
[
11
]
. In Theorems
6.1
and
7.1
respectively we show that a graph product of monoids is left ideal Howson (respectively, FLE) if and only if so is each vertex monoid. Consequently, we deduce Theorem
8.1
, which says that a graph product of monoids is weakly left coherent if and only if so is each vertex monoid.

2.1.
Monoid presentations

Let
X
be a set. The

on

consists of all words over

with operation of juxtaposition.
We denote a non-empty word by

where

for

is the

of the word. We also

for juxtaposition of words. The empty word is denoted by

. Throughout, our convention is that if we say

for all

, unless we explicitly say otherwise. A

of

is defined as a word with a form

where

; we do not assume that

is a factor of

in

.

A

\langle X\mid R\rangle
, where

is a set and

, determines the monoid
X^{*}/R^{\sharp}
, where

is the congruence on
X^{*}
generated by
R
. In the usual way, we identify

with the formal equality

in a presentation
\langle X\mid R\rangle
. We denote elements of the quotient by
[w]
, where
w\in X^{*}
.

Definition 2.1

The monoids

Remark 2.2
.

Let

\bullet

If

\bullet

1\leq j\leq h
, then
k=h
and
[w_{i}]=[w_{i}^{\prime}]

for
1\leq i\leq k
.

The next result is folklore, but follows easily from uniqueness in Theorem
2.14
.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
w=x_{1}\circ\cdots\circ x_{n}\in X^{*}
have left Foata normal form
w_{1}\circ w_{2}\circ\dots\circ w_{k}
,
with blocks
w_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. Then
w_{1}
consists of all the letters
x_{i}
such that
(\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(x_{i}),\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(x_{j}))\in E
for all
1\leq j<i
.

It can be useful to refer to the letters in
w_{1}
above as ‘the letters that shuffle to the front of

’, particularly if we do not require
the full labelling of left Foata normal form.

3.
The ascending chain condition on principal left ideals

Recall that a monoid
S
satisfies
ascending chain condition on principal left ideals
, abbreviated by ACCPL, if every chain

Sa_{1}\subseteq Sa_{2}\subseteq\cdots

stabilises, that is, there is an
n\geq 1
such that

Sa_{n}=Sa_{n+k}
for all
k\geq 1
. Stopar
[
29
]
showed that the class of monoids with ACCPL is closed under finite direct products. Miller reproved this and also showed the corresponding result for free products
[
24
]
. The aim of this section is to prove that a graph product
\mathscr{GP}
has ACCPL if and only if each vertex monoid has ACCPL.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T
be a retract of a monoid
S
. Then for any
a,b\in T
we have

Ta\subseteq Tb\mbox{ if and only if }Sa\subseteq Sb.

Proof.

Let
S
be a monoid with retract
T
, that is,
T
is a submonoid of
S
and there is an onto morphism
\theta:S\rightarrow T
that
restricts to the identity on
T
. For any monoid
M
and
u,v\in M
we have
Ma\subseteq Mb
if and only if
a=sb
for some
s\in M
.
Clearly then if
a,b\in T
and
Ta\subseteq Tb
, then
Sa\subseteq Sb
. On the other hand, if
Sa\subseteq Sb
, then choosing
t\in S
with

a=tb
and applying the retraction
\theta
, we have
a=(t\theta)b
, so that
Ta\subseteq Tb
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

The class of monoids satisfying ACCPL is closed under retract.

We have observed in Remark
2.2
that each vertex monoid
M_{\alpha}
naturally embeds into the graph product
\mathscr{GP}
, and moreover
there is a retraction from
\mathscr{GP}
to
M_{\alpha}
. These considerations immediately yield the following.

Corollary 3.3
.

If
\mathscr{GP}
has ACCPL, then so does each vertex monoid
M_{\alpha}
.

We aim to establish the converse to Corollary
3.3
. To do so, we characterise the inclusion relation between principal left ideals in
\mathscr{GP}
. We begin by investigating the inclusion
\mathscr{GP}[u]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[v]
for
u,v\in X^{*}
under the additional constraint that
v
contains no left invertible letters that can be shuffled to the front, or equivalently, the first block of a left Foata normal form of
u
contains no left invertible letters.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
u=u_{1}\circ\cdots\circ u_{m}\in X^{*}
is a left Foata normal form with block length
m
and blocks
u_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq m
, such that
u_{1}
contains no left invertible letters, and let
a\in X^{*}
be a reduced word. Let
z
be a reduced form of
a\circ u_{1}
. Then
z\circ u_{2}\circ\cdots\circ u_{m}
is a reduced form of
a\circ u
. Further,
|z|\geq|u_{1}|
(and hence
|z\circ u_{2}\circ\cdots\circ u_{m}|\geq|u|
), and
|z|=|u_{1}|
(and hence
|z\circ u_{2}\circ\cdots\circ u_{m}|=|u|
) if and only if
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(a)\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(u_{1})
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
13
, Lemma 4.4]
that
z\circ u_{2}\circ\cdots\circ u_{m}
is a reduced form of
a\circ u
. Since
u_{1}
contains no left invertible letters, the support of the reduced form
z
of
a\circ u_{1}
must contain
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(u_{1})
, implying
|z|\geq|u_{1}|
.
It is clear from repeated applications of Lemma
2.11
that
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(a)\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(u_{1})
if and only if
|z|=|u_{1}|
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
a,b\in X\setminus I
be such that
b
is not left invertible. Then
\mathscr{GP}[a]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[b]
if and only if
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(a)=\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(b)
and
M_{\mathbf{s}(a)}a\subseteq M_{\mathbf{s}(b)}b
.

Consequently, if also
a
is not left invertible, then
\mathscr{GP}[a]=\mathscr{GP}[b]
if and only if
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(a)=\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(b)
and
M_{\mathbf{s}(a)}a=M_{\mathbf{s}(b)}b
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathscr{GP}[a]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[b]
. Then there exists a reduced word
c\in X^{*}
such that
[a]=[c][b]
. By Lemma
3.4
, we have
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(c)=\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(b)
, and consequently,
[a]=[cb]
. Applying Remark
2.2
, we deduce that
a=cb
holds in
M_{\mathbf{s}(a)}
, and thus

M_{\mathbf{s}(a)}a\subseteq M_{\mathbf{s}(b)}b.

The proof of the converse is direct.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
a=a_{1}\circ\cdots\circ a_{n}\in X^{*}
be a complete block containing no left invertible letters. Then

\mathscr{GP}[a]=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathscr{GP}[a_{i}].

Proof.

Since
a=a_{1}\circ\cdots\circ a_{n}
is complete, we can easily deduce
\mathscr{GP}[a]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[a_{i}]
for each
1\leq i\leq n
, and thus

\mathscr{GP}[a]\subseteq\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathscr{GP}[a_{i}].

Conversely, let
[z]\in\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathscr{GP}[a_{i}]
. Then there exist reduced words
w_{1},\cdots,w_{n}\in X^{*}
such that

[z]=[w_{1}][a_{1}]=\cdots=[w_{n}][a_{n}].

For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
w_{i}=x^{i}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ x^{i}_{m_{i}}
. Then, by Lemma
2.11
, either
x^{i}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ x^{i}_{m_{i}}\circ a_{i}
is reduced or it reduces to

x^{i}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ x^{i}_{j-1}\circ x^{i}_{j+1}\circ\cdots\circ x^{i}_{m_{i}}\circ x_{j}^{i}a_{i}

for some
1\leq j\leq m_{i}
. This implies either
a_{i}
or
x^{i}_{j}a_{i}
is in the last block of a right Foata normal form of

, and hence of

. Therefore,

[z]=[u][p][a_{1}\circ\cdots\circ a_{n}]

where
p
is the product (in any order) of the
x^{i}_{j}
(where they exist). This gives that

[z]\in\mathscr{GP}[a_{1}\circ\cdots\circ a_{n}]=\mathscr{GP}[a].

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
a=a_{1}\circ\cdots\circ a_{m}
and
b=b_{1}\circ\cdots\circ b_{n}\in X^{*}
be complete blocks such that neither
a
nor
b
contain any left invertible letters. Then
\mathscr{GP}[a]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[b]
if and only if

\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(b)\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(a)

and with suitable relabelling of the letters of
a
we have

\mathscr{GP}[a_{i}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[b_{i}]

for each
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathscr{GP}[a]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[b]
. Then there exists a reduced word
c\in X^{*}
such that
[a]=[c][b]
. Since
b
contains no left invertible letters, it follows from Lemma
2.11
that
c\circ b
reduces to a word

w=q\circ p_{1}b_{1}\circ\cdots\circ p_{n}b_{n}

where
c=q\circ p_{1}\circ\cdots\circ p_{n}
(as a product in
X^{*}
) and
p_{i}
is either some letter from
c
or
\epsilon
, for each
1\leq i\leq n
. Consequently,

[a]=[w]\mbox{ and }\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(w)=\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(a)\supseteq\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(b).

Relabelling the elements of
a

so that
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(a_{i})=\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(b_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
we have that
a_{i}=p_{i}b_{i}
and thus
\mathscr{GP}[a_{i}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[b_{i}]
for each

1\leq i\leq n
.

Conversely, if
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(b)\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(a)
and
\mathscr{GP}[a_{i}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[b_{i}]
, then, by Lemma
3.6
, we have

\mathscr{GP}[a_{1}\circ\cdots\circ a_{n}]=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathscr{GP}[a_{i}]=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathscr{GP}[b_{i}]=\mathscr{GP}[b_{1}\circ\cdots\circ b_{n}]=\mathscr{GP}[b].

Furthermore, since
a
is a complete block, it follows that

\mathscr{GP}[a]=\mathscr{GP}[a_{n+1}\circ\cdots a_{m}\circ a_{1}\circ\cdots\circ a_{n}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[a_{1}\circ\cdots\circ a_{n}],

so that
\mathscr{GP}[a]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[b]
, as required.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
a=a_{1}\circ\cdots\circ a_{m}
and
b=b_{1}\circ\cdots\circ b_{n}\in X^{*}
be complete blocks such that both
a
and
b
contain no left invertible letters. Then
\mathscr{GP}[a]=\mathscr{GP}[b]
if and only if

\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(a)=\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(b)

(so that

) and with suitable relabelling of the letters of
a
we have

\mathscr{GP}[a_{i}]=\mathscr{GP}[b_{i}]

for each
1\leq i\leq n
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose each vertex monoid satisfies ACCPL. Let
\{w_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a set of complete blocks with the same length
n
, containing no left invertible letters. Then the following chain

\mathscr{GP}[w_{1}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[w_{2}]\subseteq\cdots

of principal left ideals terminates.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.7
, we have
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(w_{i})=\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(w_{j})
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
w_{i}=x^{i}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ x^{i}_{n}\in X^{*}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider
w_{1}=x^{1}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ x^{1}_{n}
and let
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(x^{1}_{k})=\alpha_{k}
for each
1\leq k\leq n
. By Lemma
3.7
and with suitable relabelling of the letters of
w_{i}
for all
i\leq 2
, we have the following
n
chains

\mathscr{GP}[x^{1}_{i}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[x^{2}_{i}]\subseteq\cdots

where
1\leq i\leq n
. Furthermore, it follows from Lemma
3.5
that for
1\leq i\leq n
we have

M_{\alpha_{i}}x^{1}_{i}\subseteq M_{\alpha_{i}}x^{2}_{i}\subseteq\cdots

Since each vertex monoid satisfies the ascending chain condition on principal left ideals, there exist
t_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, such that

M_{\alpha_{i}}x^{t_{i}}_{i}=M_{\alpha_{i}}x^{t_{i}+1}_{i}=\cdots.

Let

be the maximum of
t_{i},1\leq i\leq n
. Then

M_{\alpha_{i}}x^{d}_{i}=M_{\alpha_{i}}x^{d+1}_{i}=\cdots

for

. It follows from Lemma

that

\mathscr{GP}[x^{d}_{i}]=\mathscr{GP}[x^{d+1}_{i}]=\cdots

again for

. Consequently,
by Corollary
3.8
, we have

\mathscr{GP}[w_{d}]=\mathscr{GP}[w_{d+1}]=\cdots

implying the chain

\mathscr{GP}[w_{1}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[w_{2}]\subseteq\cdots

∎

Lemma 3.10
.

For each word
w\in X^{*}
, there exists a reduced word

such that
\mathscr{GP}[w]=\mathscr{GP}[u]
, where

has no left invertible elements that can be shuffled to the front.

Proof.

It follows from
[

that
[w]=[a][u]
where

is reduced,

is left invertible and

has no left invertible elements that can be shuffled to the front. This implies
\mathscr{GP}[w]=\mathscr{GP}[u]
.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
u=u_{1}\circ\cdots\circ u_{m},v=v_{1}\circ\cdots\circ v_{n}\in X^{*}
be left Foata normal forms with blocks
u_{i},v_{j}
,
1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n
. Suppose

contains no left invertible letters and
\mathscr{GP}[u]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[v]
. Then one of the following holds

(i)

|u|>|v|
.

(ii)

|u|=|v|
,
m=n
,
\mathscr{GP}[u_{1}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[v_{1}]
and
[u_{i}]=[v_{i}]
for all
2\leq i\leq m
.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{GP}[u]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[v]
, there exists a reduced word
c\in X^{*}
such that
[u]=[c][v]
. It follows from Lemma
3.4
that
|u|\geq|v|
. Suppose
|u|=|v|
. Again, by Lemma

,
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(c)\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(v_{1})
. Let
z
be a reduced form of

. Then

is a complete block and
\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(z)=\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(v_{1})
. It follows that

is a left Foata normal form of

. By Lemma

this implies that

,
[u_{1}]=[z]=[c][v_{1}]
, and so
\mathscr{GP}[u_{1}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[v_{1}]

[u_{i}]=[v_{i}]
for all

.

It is clear that if
\mathscr{GP}[u]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[v]
then
\mathscr{GP}[u][w]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[v][w]
for any

.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
u=u_{1}\circ\cdots\circ u_{m},v=v_{1}\circ\cdots\circ v_{n}\in X^{*}
be left Foata normal forms with blocks

,

if and only if

|u|=|v|,\quad m=n,\quad\mathscr{GP}[u_{1}]=\mathscr{GP}[v_{1}],\quad[u_{i}]=[v_{i}]

for each

Theorem 3.13
.

A graph product
\mathscr{GP}=\mathscr{GP}(\Gamma,\mathcal{M})
satisfies the ascending chain condition on principal left ideals if and only if each so does every vertex monoid.

Proof.

One direction is Corollary
3.3
.

For the converse, suppose that each vertex monoid satisfies the ascending chain condition on principal left ideals. Let
\{w_{i}\in X^{*}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a set of reduced words and

\mathscr{GP}[w_{1}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[w_{2}]\subseteq\cdots

a chain of principal left ideals of
\mathscr{GP}
. By Lemma
3.10
, we may assume each

w_{i}=p^{i}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ p^{i}_{\lambda(i)}

is in left Foata normal form with block length

and blocks

,

, and the first block contains

contains no left invertible letters.

By Lemma
3.11
we have that

|p_{1}^{1}|\geq|p_{1}^{2}|\geq\cdots.

Let

be such that
|p_{1}^{n}|=|p_{1}^{n+k}|
for all

. Again by Lemma

we have that

\lambda(n)=\lambda(n+k)
,
[p^{n}_{j}]=[p^{n+k}_{j}]
for all

and

,
and further,

\mathscr{GP}[p_{1}^{n}]\subseteq\mathscr{GP}[p_{1}^{n+1}]\subseteq\cdots.

From Lemma

this ascending chain terminates. Putting

and noting that for

it follows that

and hence our original ascending chain, terminates.

Corollary 3.14
.

Cf.
[

A free product or restricted direct product of monoids

satisfies the ascending chain condition on principal left ideals if and only if so does each

.

4.
Weakly left noetherian

A monoid is
weakly left noetherian
if it has the ascending chain condition on left ideals; equivalently, every left ideal is finitely generated (as a left ideal). This is a strictly stronger property than having the ascending chain condition on principal left ideals, as may easily be seen by considering
an infinite null semigroup with an identity adjoined. On the other hand, it is not as strong as that of being
left noetherian
, which states that the ascending chain condition on the lattice of left congruences holds (equivalently, every left congruence is finitely generated). We remark that in the context of rings, the analogous properties of being weakly left noetherian and left noetherian coincide and imply
left coherency
. Weakly left noetherian monoids have been extensively investigated, see, for example,
[
17
,
23
,
27
]
.

In this section we consider the property of being weakly left noetherian in the context of graph products. As presented in Theorem
4.9
, we will see that it is somewhat less amenable in this regard than that of the ascending chain condition on principal left ideals.

In the positive direction,
[
23
, Lemma 4.1]
gives that the class of weakly left noetherian monoids is closed under morphic image, and so certainly under retract. Note that the class of ACCPL monoids is not closed under morphic image, which is clear from considering the fact that any free monoid has ACCPL.

Corollary 4.1
.

If
\mathscr{GP}
is weakly left noetherian, then so is each vertex monoid.

Unlike the case for the ascending chain condition on principal left ideals, the converse to Corollary
4.1
does not hold in general. This follows from the known result for free products of monoids.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
23
]

Let
M
and
N
be non-trivial monoids.

(i)

The free product of
M
and
N
is weakly left noetherian if and only if either
|M|=|N|=2
or both
M
and
N
are groups.

(ii)

The direct product of
M
and
N
is weakly left noetherian if and only if both
M
and
N
are weakly left noetherian.

In our work there is no assumption that the graph
\Gamma
is finite. We therefore need an infinitary version of Lemma
4.2
(ii). It is helpful to recall that a monoid in which every element is left invertible is a group.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}:i\in I\}
be a collection of monoids and let

P
be the restricted direct product of the monoids in
\mathcal{M}
. Then
P
is weakly left noetherian if and only if each
M_{i}
is weakly left noetherian, and

J:=\{i\in I:M_{i}\mbox{ is not a group}\}
is finite.

Proof.

If
P
is weakly left noetherian, then so is each
M_{i}
, by Corollary
4.1
. Suppose that

J
is infinite. For each
j\in J
we pick an element
a_{j}\in M_{j}
such that
a_{j}
is not left invertible and define

f_{j}\in P
by
jf_{j}=a_{j}
and
if_{j}=I_{M_{i}}
for all
i\neq j
. Clearly
F=\{f_{j}:j\in J\}\subseteq P
.
Let
I
be the left ideal of
P
generated by
F
. Since
P
is weakly left noetherian,
I
is generated by a finite subset
F^{\prime}
of
F
.
Pick
f_{k}\notin F^{\prime}
; by assumption,
f_{k}=gf_{j}
for some
g\in P
and
f_{j}\in F^{\prime}
, and so in particular,
k\neq j
. Then

I_{M_{j}}=jf_{k}=j(gf_{j})=(jg)(jf_{j})=(jg)a_{j},

contradicting the fact that
a_{j}
is not left invertible. Thus
J
is finite.

For the converse, suppose that each
M_{i}
is weakly left noetherian, and

J:=\{i\in I:M_{i}\mbox{ is not a group}\}
is finite. Then
P\cong P_{J}\times P_{G}
, where
P_{J}
is the direct product of the monoids in
\mathcal{M}_{J}:=\{M_{j}:j\in J\}
and

P_{G}
is the restricted direct product of the groups in
\mathcal{M}_{G}:=\{M_{j}:j\in I\setminus J\}
. Certainly
P_{2}
is a group, and so is weakly left noetherian. The result then follows from Lemma
4.2
. ∎

To identify which graph products are weakly left noetherian we introduce the notion of a graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
being relatively complete with respect to a set of vertex monoids
\mathcal{M}
, as follows.

Definition 4.4
.

A graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
is
relatively complete with respect to
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{\alpha}:\alpha\in V\}
if for any pair
(\alpha,\beta)\notin E
with
\alpha\neq\beta
, one of the following holds:

(i)

|M_{\alpha}|=|M_{\beta}|=2
, either
M_{\alpha}
or
M_{\beta}
is not a group, and
(\alpha,\gamma),(\beta,\gamma)\in E
for all
\gamma\in V\setminus\{\alpha,\beta\}
;

(ii)

both
M_{\alpha}
and
M_{\beta}
are groups.

Moreover, there exists at most one pair
(\alpha,\beta)\notin E
satisfying condition (i).

Before proceding with our arguments, we make two straightforward observations.

Remark 4.5
.

(i)

Suppose that
\Gamma^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is an induced subgraph of
\Gamma=(V,E)
, and let
\mathscr{GP}^{\prime}=\mathscr{GP}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})
be the graph product of
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\{M_{\alpha}:\alpha\in V^{\prime}\}
with respect to
\Gamma^{\prime}
. We denote an
element of
\mathscr{GP}^{\prime}
using the notation
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
. Using Remark
2.2
, for any words
x,y,z\in X^{*}
consisting of letters only from
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, we have that
\lfloor x\rfloor=\lfloor y\rfloor
in
\mathscr{GP}^{\prime}
if and only if
[x]=[y]
in
\mathscr{GP}
; furthermore,
\lfloor x\rfloor\lfloor y\rfloor=\lfloor z\rfloor
in
\mathscr{GP}^{\prime}
if and only if
[x][y]=[z]
in
\mathscr{GP}
.

(ii)

Suppose that
x,y\in X^{*}
are shuffle equivalent. Let
x^{\prime}
be a subword of
x
which shuffles (within
x
) to the subword
y^{\prime}
of
y
. Then
[x^{\prime}]=[y^{\prime}]
. Consequently, if

x^{*}
and
y^{*}
are the subwords of
x
and
y
obtained by deleting
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
respectively, then also
[x^{*}]=[y^{*}]
.

Lemma
4.6
will be crucial for our discussion. It enables us to decompose
\mathscr{GP}=\mathscr{GP}(\Gamma,\mathcal{M})
, where
\Gamma
is relatively complete with respect to
\mathcal{M}
, into a direct product of two or three monoids, each of which is weakly right noetherian, as in Proposition
4.7
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose
V
is a disjoint union of

6.
Left ideal Howson

Recall that a monoid
M
is said to be
left ideal Howson
if the intersection of any two finitely generated left ideals, or, equivalently, any two principal left ideals, is again finitely generated. This term was introduced in honour of the author of
[
19
]
,
who showed that the intersection of finitely generated subgroups of free groups is finitely generated.
By very definition, a weakly left noetherian monoid is left ideal Howson. The converse is not true, as may be seen by considering
the natural numbers under the operation of maximum, with an identity adjoined. The left ideal Howson property was considered extensively for other classes of semigroups, (see
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
28
]
). Further, it was showed in
[
3
]
that the class of left ideal Howson monoids is closed under taking both free products and direct products.
The aim of this section is to show that the class of left ideal Howson monoids is closed under taking graph products.

Theorem 6.1
.

A graph product of monoids is left ideal Howson if and only if each vertex monoid is left ideal Howson.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{GP}=\mathscr{GP}(\Gamma,\mathcal{M})
.
Suppose that each vertex monoid is left ideal Howson. As remarked, to show that
\mathscr{GP}
is left ideal Howson, it is enough to show that the intersection of two principal left ideals is finitely generated. To this end, consider
\mathscr{GP}[a]
and
\mathscr{GP}[b]

where

and
b=b_{1}\circ\cdots\circ b_{n}\in X^{*}
are reduced. By Lemma
3.10
, we can assume that the left Foata normal forms of

and
b
are standard, that is, that there are no left invertible letters in
a
(respectively
b
) that can be shuffled to the front of
a
(respectively
b
).

We show that
\mathscr{I}:=\mathscr{GP}[a]\cap\mathscr{GP}[b]
is finitely generated. By convention, this is true if

\mathscr{I}=\emptyset
. Suppose therefore that
\mathscr{I}\neq\emptyset
and let

[z]\in\mathscr{I}
. Let
{s}
and

be reduced words such that
[z]=[{s}][a]=[{t}][b].

By
Corollary
5.3
and the subsequent comment, either
{s}\circ a
is reduced or reduces in

, giving rise to a (re)labelling of
the words

and

such that:

(1)

\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(s_{i})=\mbox{$\mathbf{s}$}(a_{i})
for

(2)

shuffles to

(3)

(4)

Similarly, we have a word

such that:

7.
Left annihilators of elements

A
left congruence
on a monoid
M
is an equivalence relation closed under multiplication by elements of
M
on the left. We will denote the smallest left congruence containing a subset
X
of
M\times M
by
\lambda_{X}
. For any
a\in M
it is easily seen that
the left annihilator

\mathbf{l}(a):=\{(s,t)\in M\times M:sa=ta\}

is a left congruence. We say that
M
is
finitely left equated
if

\mathbf{l}(a)
is finitely generated for all
a\in M
. Notice that this property is much weaker than being left noetherian. For instance, it was proved in
[
5
, Corollary 4.8]
that every regular monoid is finitely left equated. Regular monoids need not even be weakly left noetherian, as the example of the natural numbers under maximum, with an identity adjoined, demonstrates. The aim of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 7.1
.

A graph product of monoids
\mathscr{GP}=\mathscr{GP}(\Gamma,\mathcal{M})
is finitely left equated if and only if so is each vertex monoid.

Proof.

The forward direction follows from

[
5
]
, which tells us the fact that the class of finitely left equated monoids is closed under retract.

We prove the reverse direction by examining the left annihilators of elements of
\mathscr{GP}
.
For the remainder of this section we consider
\mathscr{GP}=\mathscr{GP}(\Gamma,\mathcal{M})
, where each vertex monoid is finitely left equated. We choose and fix

[a]\in\mathscr{GP}
where
a=a_{1}\circ\dots\circ a_{m}
is reduced. Our aim is to find a finite set of generators for
\mathbf{l}([a])
. Unlike the situation in Section
6
, we cannot assume that
a
has no left invertible elements in its first block in left Foata normal form, which adds to the complications we encounter.

For each left invertible letter
a_{i}
of
a
, we choose and fix a left inverse
a_{i}^{\prime}
so that
a_{i}^{\prime}a_{i}=1_{\mathbf{s}(a_{i})}
in
M_{\mathbf{s}(a_{i})}
and

[a_{i}^{\prime}][a_{i}]=[a_{i}^{\prime}\circ a_{i}]=[\epsilon]=1
in
\mathscr{GP}
. Note that we may not need to call upon all such elements. For each
1\leq i\leq m
we can pick a set of generators
X_{i}
of the left annihilator
\mathbf{l}(a_{i})
in the vertex monoid
M_{\mathbf{s}(a_{i})}
, that is,
\lambda_{X_{i}}=\mathbf{l}(a_{i})
. Again we note that we may not need to call upon all sets
X_{i}
.

Let
\theta=\theta(h,k,I,J)
be a reduction function for

with labelling
I=\{i_{\ell}:1\leq\ell\leq h\}
(where

labels the glueing moves and

) and

(where

).
We let

Y^{\theta}=\{([p\circ a_{j_{l-1}\theta}^{\prime}\circ\ \dots\circ a_{j_{1}\theta}^{\prime}],[q\circ a_{j_{l-1}\theta}^{\prime}\circ\ \dots\circ a_{j_{1}\theta}^{\prime}]):(p,q)\in X_{j_{l}\theta},1\leq l\leq k\}.

Lemma 7.2
.

With notation as above we have
Y^{\theta}\subseteq\mathbf{l}([a])
. Further,
for any left inverses
s_{j_{l}}
of
a_{j_{l}}
,
1\leq l\leq k
, we have

[s_{j_{k}\theta}\circ\dots\circ s_{j_{1}\theta}]\,\,\lambda_{Y^{\theta}}\,\,[a^{\prime}_{j_{k}\theta}\circ\dots\circ a^{\prime}_{j_{1}\theta}].

Proof.

Since
\theta
is a reduction function for
a
, we have

[a]=[a_{j_{1}\theta}\circ\cdots\circ a_{j_{k}\theta}\circ a_{i_{h}\theta}\circ\cdots\circ a_{i_{1}\theta}\circ a^{M}].

Let

([p\circ a_{j_{l-1}\theta}^{\prime}\circ\ \dots\circ a_{j_{1}\theta}^{\prime}],[q\circ a_{j_{l-1}\theta}^{\prime}\circ\ \dots\circ a_{j_{1}\theta}^{\prime}])\in Y^{\theta}.

Then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}p\circ a_{j_{l-1}\theta}^{\prime}\circ\cdots\circ a_{j_{1}\theta}^{\prime}\circ a&\equiv&(pa_{j_{l}\theta})\circ a_{j_{l+1}\theta}\circ\cdots\circ a_{j_{k}\theta}\circ a_{i_{h}\theta}\circ\cdots\circ a_{i_{1}\theta}\circ a^{M}\\

.

We show by finite induction on

Again, for any reduction function

The proof of the following is similar to that of Lemma

Lemma 7.3
.

With notation as above, we have

\{([p\circ a^{\prime}_{j_{k}\theta}\circ\dots\circ a^{\prime}_{j_{1}\theta}],[q\circ a^{\prime}_{j_{k}\theta}\circ\dots\circ a^{\prime}_{j_{1}\theta}]):(p,q)\in\mathbf{l}(a_{i_{\ell\theta}}),1\leq l\leq h\}\subseteq\mathbf{l}([a]).

. Further, if

[s_{i_{l}\theta}\circ a^{\prime}_{j_{k}\theta}\circ\dots\circ a^{\prime}_{j_{1}\theta}]\,\lambda_{T^{\theta}}\,[s^{\prime}_{i_{l}\theta}\circ a^{\prime}_{j_{k}\theta}\circ\dots\circ a^{\prime}_{j_{1}\theta}].

Let

of

are reduced.

From the first and last equivalences above, we obtain that

([s],[t])=([w\circ\bar{s}],[w\circ\bar{t}])=([w][\bar{s}],[w][\bar{t}]),

We have concentrated in this article on finitary conditions arising from and related to left noetherianity, the
ascending
chain condition on the lattice of left congruences of a monoid. There are, of course, conditions that arise from the
descending
chain condition on the lattice of left congruences or, indeed, chain conditions on the lattice of (two-sided) congruences. We suggest that an investigation of these conditions in the context of direct products, and then potentially that of graph and free products, would be worthwhile. It is known that the direct product of two Artinian groups (that is, groups with no infinite strictly descending chain of subgroups) is Artinian
[
26
]
. The corresponding result is not known for monoids, in the case of left ideals or of left congruences. It is clear, however, that a free product of two non-trivial monoids has an infinite descending chain of principal left ideals.

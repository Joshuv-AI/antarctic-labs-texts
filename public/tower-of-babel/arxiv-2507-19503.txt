Fibonacci-harmonic sums


Abstract
We offer several new summation identities involving harmonic numbers, odd harmonic numbers, and Fibonacci numbers. Our results are derived using three different approaches: partial summation, polynomial identities and binomial transformation.

Introduction

The Fibonacci numbers
F_{j}
and the Lucas numbers
L_{j}
are defined, for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, through the recurrence relations

\displaystyle F_{j}=F_{j-1}+F_{j-2},

\displaystyle\quad j\geq 2,\quad F_{0}=0,\,F_{1}=1,

\displaystyle L_{j}=L_{j-1}+L_{j-2},

\displaystyle\quad j\geq 2,\quad L_{0}=2,\,L_{1}=1,

with
F_{-j}=(-1)^{j-1}F_{j}
and
L_{-j}=(-1)^{j}L_{j}
. The Binet formulas for these sequences are

F_{j}=\frac{\alpha^{j}-\beta^{j}}{\alpha-\beta},\qquad L_{j}=\alpha^{j}+\beta^{j},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z},

(1)

with
\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
being the golden ratio and
\beta=-\frac{1}{\alpha}
.
They are indexed as sequences A000045 and A000032 in the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences
[
11
]
.
Koshy
[
10
]
and Vajda
[
12
]
have written excellent books on these sequences. The gibonacci sequence

(G_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a slight generalization of
F_{j}
and
L_{j}
. It has the same recurrence relation as the Fibonacci sequence
but starts with arbitrary initial values, i.e.,

G_{j}=G_{j-1}+G_{j-2},\quad(j\geq 2),

with
G_{0}
and
G_{1}
arbitrary numbers (usually integers) not both zero.

Harmonic numbers
H_{z}
and odd harmonic numbers
O_{z}
are defined
for
0\neq z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
by the recurrence relations

H_{z}=H_{z-1}+\frac{1}{z}\qquad\text{and}\qquad O_{z}=O_{z-1}+\frac{1}{2z-1},

with
H_{0}=0
and
O_{0}=0
. Harmonic numbers are connected to the digamma function
\psi(z)=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
through the fundamental relation

H_{z}=\psi(z+1)+\gamma.

(2)

Generalized harmonic numbers
H_{z}^{(m)}
and odd harmonic numbers
O_{z}^{(m)}
of order
m\in\mathbb{C}
are defined by

H_{z}^{(m)}=H_{z-1}^{(m)}+\frac{1}{z^{m}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad O_{z}^{(m)}=O_{z-1}^{(m)}+\frac{1}{(2z-1)^{m}},

with
H_{0}^{(m)}=0
and
O_{0}^{(m)}=0
so that
H_{z}=H_{z}^{(1)}
and
O_{z}=O_{z}^{(1)}
. The recurrence relations imply
that if
z=n
is a non-negative integer, then

H_{n}^{(m)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^{m}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad O_{n}^{(m)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2j-1)^{m}}.

Exploring identities that combine Fibonacci numbers and harmonic numbers offers a unique intersection between combinatorics and analysis. While each sequence has been extensively studied on its own, their combination reveals surprising and elegant mathematical structures. Investigating such identities deepens our understanding of number theory and enriches the study.

There is a growing body of work dedicated to identities involving Fibonacci and harmonic numbers.
In
[
1
]
, Batır and Sofo presented many Fibonacci-harmonic sums and we state two beautiful sums from their paper.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}F_{3k}=2^{n}\left(H_{n}F_{2n}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{2n-2k}}{k2^{k}}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}F_{3k}=(-1)^{n}2^{n}\left(H_{n}F_{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}F_{n-k}}{k^{2}2^{k}}\right).

Also, in
[
4
]
Frontczak presented the following;

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}kF_{k}H_{k}=nF_{2n-1}H_{n}-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(kF_{2k}+F_{2k+1}\right)H_{n-1-k},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}kL_{k}H_{k}=nL_{2n-1}H_{n}-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(kL_{2k}+L_{2k+1}\right)H_{n-1-k}.

Although significant progress has been made in evaluating series that involve Fibonacci and harmonic numbers, many avenues remain largely unexplored, especially those concerning generalized harmonic numbers, alternating series, and variations of the Fibonacci sequence. This paper contributes to the ongoing research by establishing new identities and summation formulas that interweave these classical sequences in fresh and insightful ways. By employing three different techniques, we reveal deeper connections that underscore the elegance and depth inherent in Fibonacci-harmonic sums. A sample of our new results without binomial coefficients is given below:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{F_{k}F_{k+1}}H_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{k}}{F_{k+1}}\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{F_{n}}{F_{n+1}}H_{n+1},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{4k+2}}{F_{2k}^{2}F_{2k+2}^{2}}H_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{F_{2k+2}^{2}}\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{H_{n+1}}{F_{2n+2}^{2}}+1,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{k}F_{n-k}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{{F_{n-k}F_{n-k-1}}}{{k+1}},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{k}G_{k+1}^{2}=H_{n+1}G_{n+1}G_{n+2}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{G_{k+1}G_{k+2}}{k+1}.

Required identities

Lemma 1
.

For
u,v\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{u}\left({1-x}\right)^{v}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\binom{{u+v+1}}{{u+1}}\left({u+1}\right)}},

(3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{u}\left({1-x}\right)^{v}\ln x\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{{H_{u+v+1}-H_{u}}}{{\binom{{u+v+1}}{{u+1}}(u+1)}},

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{u}\left({1-x}\right)^{v}\ln^{2}x\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{{\left({H_{u+v-1}-H_{u}}\right)^{2}+H_{u+v+1}^{(2)}-H_{u}^{(2)}}}{{\binom{{u+v+1}}{{u+1}}(u+1)}},

(5)

and, in particular,

\int_{0}^{1}x^{u-1}\ln(1-x)dx=-\frac{{H_{u}}}{u},\qquad 0\neq u\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}.

(6)

Proof.

The identity (
3
) is the well-known Beta function integral while (
4
) is obtained by differentiating with respect
to
u
. Identity (
5
) follows from differentiating (
4
) with respect to
u
. These identities are also entries of Gradshteyn and Ryzhik’s book
[
8
]
and other tables.
∎

Lemma 2
.

If
n
is an integer, then

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}

\displaystyle=2O_{n}-2\ln 2

(7)

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{-1/2}

\displaystyle=2O_{n},

(8)

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{1/2}

\displaystyle=2\left({O_{n}-1}\right),

(9)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{-1/2}

\displaystyle=2O_{n+1},

(10)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{1/2}

\displaystyle=2\left({O_{n+1}-1}\right),

(11)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{n-1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{{2n+1}},

(12)

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{-3/2}

\displaystyle=2\left({O_{n}-1}\right),

(13)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{-3/2}

\displaystyle=2\left({O_{n+1}-1}\right).

(14)

Proof.

Use (
2
) as the definition of the harmonic numbers for all complex
n
(excluding zero and the negative integers)
and use the known result for the digamma function at half-integer arguments, namely,

\psi(n+1/2)=-\gamma-2\ln 2+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2k-1}.

∎

Lemma 3
.

We have

\displaystyle\binom{{r+1/2}}{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\frac{{2r+1}}{{2s}}\binom{{2s}}{s}}}{{\binom{{r}}{s}2^{2s}}},

(15)

\displaystyle\binom{{1/2}}{r}

\displaystyle=\left({-1}\right)^{r+1}\frac{{\binom{{2r}}{r}}}{{2^{2r}\left({2r-1}\right)}},

(16)

\displaystyle\binom{{r-1/2}}{s}

\displaystyle=\binom{{2r}}{r}\binom{{r}}{s}\frac{1}{{\binom{{2\left({r-s}\right)}}{{r-s}}2^{2s}}},

(17)

\displaystyle\binom{{-1/2}}{r}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}\binom{{2r}}{r}2^{-2r}.

(18)

Proof.

These consequences of the generalized binomial coefficients, are easy to derive using the Gamma function. They can also be found in
Gould’s book
[
6
]
. We will impose the various restrictions later.
∎

3
Method I: Identities based on Abel’s partial summation formula

The approach here is, in each case, to use either a basic Fibonacci identity or a combinatorial identity in conjunction with Abel’s partial summation formula.

Lemma 4
.

Let
{(a_{j})}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
{(b_{j})}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be sequences. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}(a_{k+1}-a_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k+1}(b_{k}-b_{k+1})+a_{n+1}b_{n+1}-a_{1}b_{1},

(19)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}(a_{k+1}+a_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k+1}(b_{k}+b_{k+1})-a_{n+1}b_{n+1}+a_{1}b_{1}.

(20)

Proof.

The proof of these very useful identities is surprisingly simple. One merely needs to observe that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k+1}b_{k+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}b_{k}+a_{n+1}b_{n+1}-a_{1}b_{1}.

∎

3.1
Identities derived from basic Fibonacci identities

Most of the identities used in this section come from Vajda’s classic
[
12
]
.
We begin with two theorems that follow essentially from the definition of Fibonacci numbers.

Theorem 1
.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s,t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s+t\notin\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{k+r+2}}{\binom{t+k+s}{t+1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{k+r+1}}{\binom{t+k+s}{t+1}}-\frac{F_{n+r+1}}{\binom{t+n+s+1}{t+1}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{k+r+1}}{\binom{t+k+s+1}{t+1}}+\frac{F_{r+1}}{\binom{s+t+1}{t+1}}

(21)

Thus, for
n
a non-negative integer, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{k+r+2}}{k+s}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(2k+3)F_{k+r+1}}{(k+s)(k+s+1)}+\frac{F_{r+1}}{s+1}-\frac{F_{n+r+1}}{n+s+1}.

(22)

Proof.

Since we know that
F_{k+1}-F_{k}=F_{k-1}
, we set
a_{k}=F_{k+r}
and
b_{k}=(1-x)^{k}
in (
20
), to obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1-x)^{k}F_{k+r+2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1-x)^{k}F_{k+r+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1-x)^{k+1}F_{k+r+1}-(1-x)^{n+1}F_{n+r+1}+(1-x)F_{r+1}.

Multiply through by
x^{t}(1-x)^{s-1}
and then by term-wise integration, we obtain (
21
) directly on account
of Lemma
1
. Setting
(s,t)=(0,0)
in (
21
) gives rise to (
22
).
∎

Theorem 2
.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s,t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s+t\notin\mathbb{Z}^{-}
,

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{H_{t}-H_{k+t+s-1}}{\binom{t+k+s-1}{t+1}}F_{k+r-1}&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{H_{t}-H_{k+t+s-1}}{\binom{t+k+s-1}{t+1}}F_{k+r+1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{H_{t}-H_{k+t+s}}{\binom{t+k+s}{t+1}}F_{k+r+1}\\
&\qquad+\frac{H_{t}-H_{n+t+s}}{\binom{n+t+s}{t+1}}F_{n+r+1}-\frac{H_{t}-H_{s+t}}{\binom{t+s}{t+1}}F_{r+1}.\end{split}

(23)

Proof.

In (
19
), set
a_{k}=F_{k+r}
and
b_{k}=(1-x)^{k}
, multiply through by
x^{t}(1-x)^{s-2}\ln x
and integrate from 0 to 1. The result is
(
23
) using (
4
).
∎

Theorem 3
.

For
n
a positive integer, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{H_{k}}}{k}F_{k-2}F_{k+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{{H_{k+1}-1}}{k(k+1)}F_{k}^{2}+\frac{{H_{n}}}{n}F_{n}^{2},

(24)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{2k}}{k}\frac{O_{k}}{\binom{{2k}}{k}}F_{k-2}F_{k+1}=2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{2^{2k}}{k(k+1)}\frac{{\left({O_{k+1}-1}\right)}}{{\binom{{2\left({k+1}\right)}}{{k+1}}}}F_{k}^{2}+\frac{{2^{2n}}}{n}\frac{{O_{n}}}{{\binom{{2n}}{n}}}F_{n}^{2},

(25)

and more generally, for
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s,t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s+t\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
,

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{{H_{k+s+t}-H_{t}}}{{\binom{{k+s+t}}{{t+1}}}}F_{k+r-1}F_{k+r+2}}&=\frac{{t+1}}{{t+2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{{H_{k+s+t}-H_{t+1}}}{{\binom{{k+s+t}}{{t+2}}}}F_{k+r}^{2}}-\frac{{H_{s+t}-H_{t}}}{{\binom{{s+t}}{{t+1}}}}F_{r+1}^{2}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{{H_{n+s+t}-H_{t}}}{{\binom{{n+s+t}}{{t+1}}}}F_{n+r+1}^{2}.\end{split}

(26)

Proof.

Here, the basic Fibonacci identity is

F_{k-1}F_{k+2}=F_{k+1}^{2}-F_{k}^{2}.

In (
19
), set
a_{k}=F_{k+r}^{2}
and
b_{k}=(1-x)^{k}
to obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1-x)^{k+1}F_{k+r-1}F_{k+r+2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1-x)^{k}xF_{k+r}^{2}-(1-x)F_{r+1}^{2}+(1-x)^{n+1}F_{n+r+1}^{2},

which upon multiplying through by
x^{t}(1-x)^{s-2}
gives

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{x^{t}\left({1-x}\right)^{k+s-1}F_{k+r-1}F_{k+r+2}}&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{x^{t+1}\left({1-x}\right)^{k+s-2}F_{k+r}^{2}}-x^{t}\left({1-x}\right)^{s-1}F_{r+1}^{2}\\
&\qquad+x^{t}\left({1-x}\right)^{s+n-1}F_{n+r+1}^{2};\end{split}

from which term-wise integration produces (
26
) on account of Lemma
1
. Identities (
24
) and (
25
) are special cases of (
26
), being evaluations respectively at
(s=0=t)
and
(s=0,t=-1/2)
. Note the use of Lemmata
2
and
3
.

In deriving (
24
) and (
25
) we used the following limits:

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{H_{s+1}-1}{s}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-1,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{O_{r+1}-1}{r}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}-2.

which are easily derived by converting to the digamma function and using L’Hospital’s rule.
∎

Theorem 4
.

For all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s,t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s+t\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{4(k+r)+2}}{\binom{k+t+s-1}{t+1}}=\frac{t+1}{t+2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{2(k+r)+2}^{2}}{\binom{k+t+s}{t+2}}+\frac{F_{2(n+r)+2}^{2}}{\binom{n+t+s}{t+1}}-\frac{F_{2(r+1)}^{2}}{\binom{t+s}{t+1}}.

(27)

In particular, we have for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s\geq 1

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{4(k+r)+2}}{k+s-1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{2(k+r)+2}^{2}}{(k+s)(k+s-1)}+\frac{F_{2(n+r)+2}^{2}}{n+s}-\frac{F_{2(r+1)}^{2}}{s}.

(28)

Proof.

The basic Fibonacci identity is

F_{2k+2}^{2}-F_{2k}^{2}=F_{4k+2},

which is a special case of an identity of Howard
[
9
]

F_{a+b}^{2}-F_{a-b}^{2}=F_{2a}F_{2b}.

(29)

Hence, we choose in (
19
)
a_{k}=F_{2(k+r)}^{2}
and
b_{k}=(1-x)^{k}
. Then, after multiplying through by
x^{t}(1-x)^{s-2}
we get

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{n}x^{t}(1-x)^{k+s-2}F_{4(k+r)+2}&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}F_{2(k+r)+2}^{2}x^{t+1}(1-x)^{k+s-2}+F_{2(n+r)+2}^{2}x^{t}(1-x)^{k+s-1}\\
&\qquad-F_{2(r+1)}^{2}x^{t}(1-x)^{s-1}.\end{split}

The result is now a consequence of (
3
).
∎

A Fibonacci-harmonic identity is obtained by exchanging (
3
) by (
4
).

Theorem 5
.

For all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s,t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s+t\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{H_{t}-H_{k+t+s-1}}{\binom{k+t+s-1}{t+1}}F_{4(k+r)+2}&=\frac{t+1}{t+2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{H_{t+1}-H_{k+t+s}}{\binom{k+t+s}{t+2}}F_{2(k+r)+2}^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{H_{t}-H_{n+t+s}}{\binom{n+t+s}{t+1}}F_{2(n+r)+2}^{2}-\frac{H_{t}-H_{t+s}}{\binom{t+s}{t+1}}F_{2(r+1)}^{2}.\end{split}

(30)

In particular, we have for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s\geq 1

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{H_{k+s-1}}{k+s-1}F_{4(k+r)+2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{H_{k+s}-1}{(k+s)(k+s-1)}F_{2(k+r)+2}^{2}+\frac{H_{n+s}}{n+s}F_{2(n+r)+2}^{2}-\frac{H_{s}}{s}F_{2(r+1)}^{2}.

(31)

Remark
.

The Lucas counterparts of Theorems
4
and
5
are obtained straightforwardly using another
identity of Howard
[
9
]
, namely,

L_{a+b}^{2}-L_{a-b}^{2}=5F_{2a}F_{2b}.

We continue with some sums involving reciprocals of products of Fibonacci numbers.

Theorem 6
.

For all
r\geq 1
and
s\geq 0
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{rk}}{F_{rk}F_{r(k+1)}}H_{k+s-1}=\frac{1}{F_{r}^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{r(k+2)}}{F_{r(k+1)}}\frac{1}{k+s}-\frac{F_{r(n+2)}}{F_{r(n+1)}}H_{n+s}+L_{r}H_{s}\right).

(32)

In particular,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{F_{k}F_{k+1}}H_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{k}}{F_{k+1}}\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{F_{n}}{F_{n+1}}H_{n+1}.

(33)

Proof.

The Fibonacci identity

(-1)^{t}F_{a}F_{b}=F_{t+a}F_{t+b}-F_{t}F_{t+a+b}

produces after making the replacements
t\mapsto rk
and
a=b=r
the identity

\frac{F_{r(k+2)}}{F_{r(k+1)}}-\frac{F_{r(k+1)}}{F_{rk}}=(-1)^{rk+1}\frac{F_{r}^{2}}{F_{rk}F_{r(k+1)}}.

This shows that we can choose
a_{k}=F_{r(k+1)}/F_{rk}
and
b_{k}=H_{k+s-1}
, and the result follows from (
19
).
∎

There also exists an expression for the left hand side of (
32
) involving Lucas numbers, which we state in the next theorem.

Theorem 7
.

For all
r\geq 1
and
s\geq 0

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{rk}}{F_{rk}F_{r(k+1)}}H_{k+s-1}=\frac{1}{2F_{r}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{L_{r(k+1)}}{F_{r(k+1)}}\frac{1}{k+s}-\frac{L_{r(n+1)}}{F_{r(n+1)}}H_{n+s}+\frac{L_{r}}{F_{r}}H_{s}\right).

(34)

Proof.

Here, the relevant Fibonacci identity is

F_{a}L_{b}-L_{a}F_{b}=2(-1)^{b}F_{a-b}.

After making the replacements
a\mapsto rk
and
b\mapsto r(k+1)
, and keeping in mind that
F_{-n}=(-1)^{n+1}F_{n}
we get the identity

\frac{L_{r(k+1)}}{F_{r(k+1)}}-\frac{L_{rk}}{F_{rk}}=(-1)^{rk+1}\frac{2F_{r}}{F_{rk}F_{r(k+1)}}.

This shows that we can choose
a_{k}=L_{rk}/F_{rk}
and
b_{k}=H_{k+s-1}
, and the result follows from (
19
).
∎

Corollary 8
.

For all
r\geq 1
and
s\geq 0
we have the relation

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{L_{r(k+1)}}{F_{r(k+1)}}\frac{1}{k+s}=\frac{2}{F_{r}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{r(k+2)}}{F_{r(k+1)}}\frac{1}{k+s}+\left(L_{r(n+1)}-\frac{2}{F_{r}}F_{r(n+2)}\right)\frac{H_{n+s}}{F_{r(n+1)}}+\frac{L_{r}}{F_{r}}H_{s}.

(35)

Remark
.

The Lucas counterpart of Theorem
6
can be derived using the identity

5(-1)^{t+1}F_{a}F_{b}=L_{t+a}L_{t+b}-L_{t}L_{t+a+b}.

Theorem 9
.

For all odd
r\geq 1
and all
s\geq 0
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{r(2k+1)}}{F_{2rk}F_{2r(k+1)}}H_{k+s-1}=\frac{1}{L_{r}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{F_{2r(k+1)}}\frac{1}{k+s}-\frac{H_{n+s}}{F_{2r(n+1)}}+\frac{H_{s}}{F_{2r}}\right).

(36)

In particular,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{F_{2k+1}}{F_{2k}F_{2k+2}}H_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{F_{2k+2}}\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{H_{n+1}}{F_{2n+2}}+1.

(37)

Proof.

The basic Fibonacci identity that we need is

L_{a}F_{b}=F_{a+b}+(-1)^{a}F_{b-a}.

Now, with
a=r
odd and
b=r(2k+1)
gives

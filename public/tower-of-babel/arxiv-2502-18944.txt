Automorphisms and quotients of 2-colored quasi best match graphs
Annachiara. Korchmaros

Abstract
2-colored quasi best match graphs (2-qBMGs) are directed graphs that arose in phylogenetics. Investigations of 2-qBMGs have mostly focused on computational issues. However, 2-qBMGs also have relevant properties for structural graph theory; in particular, their undirected underlying graph is free from induced paths and cycles of size at least 6 6 . In this paper, results on the structure of the automorphism groups of 2-qBMGs are obtained, which shows how to construct 2-qBMGs with large automorphism groups.

Introduction

Automorphisms (also called symmetries) of geometric structures are elaborated, widely used technical tools, especially in topology and algebraic geometry. In the last three decades, they also appeared in numerous investigations of combinatorial structures, such as finite geometries and graphs. Motivation for the study of graph automorphisms came from group theory since many important groups were known to enjoy a very useful permutation representation as an automorphism group of a particular graph whose specific features provide a complete description of the structure of the group in terms of actions on vertices, edges, and certain subgraphs.

This was the starting point for ongoing investigations aimed at characterizing families of groups with prescribed action on some directed or undirected graphs, involving primitivity on vertices, transitivity on edges and arcs, or flag-transitivity.
Conversely, one can ask to describe the possibilities for the structures and actions of the automorphism groups of graphs belonging to a given family of directed or undirected graphs. A considerable amount of investigations have been done on this problem, particularly on Cayley graphs, cubic graphs, and tournaments, following the pioneering work in the 1980s by Alspach, Marušič, Moon, and others. For the rich literature on this topic, the reader is referred to the recent monograph
Dobson and Malnič, Aleksander and Marušič, Dragan (2022)
.

In this paper, we focus on the family of 2-qBMGs (two-color quasi-best match graphs), which arose from evolution theory
Geiß et al. (2019a)
;
Schaller et al. (2021a)
;
Geiß et al. (2019b)
;

;
Schaller et al. (2021c)
;

2.1
Graph theory

In this paper,
\overrightarrow{G}=(V,E)
always denotes a digraph (directed graph) without loops and multiple edges, but digraphs without any edge are admitted. With the usual notation,
V=V(\overrightarrow{G})
is its vertex-set,
E=E(\overrightarrow{G})
is its edge-set where for
u,v\in V
, the edge with tail
u
and head
v
is denoted by
uv
or
[u,v]
. For any vertex
v\in V
,
N^{+}(v)
and
N^{-}(v)
stand for the set of the out-neighbors and in-neighbors of
v
, respectively. A
symmetric edge
is a pair of edges such that both
uv,vu\in E(\overrightarrow{G})
hold. For a vertex
v
of
\overrightarrow{G}
,
v
is a
sink
if
N^{+}(v)=\emptyset
, and a
source
if
N^{-}(v)=\emptyset
. In this paper,
G
denotes an undirected graph, and
uv
with
u,v\in V(G)
is an undirected edge of
G
.

A digraph is
oriented
if it does not have a symmetric edge. An oriented digraph has a
topological vertex ordering
if its vertices can be labeled
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}
such that for any edge
u_{i}u_{j}
we have
i<j
. A vertex
u
is
minimal
for a topological vertex ordering of
\overrightarrow{G}
if
N^{-}(u)=\emptyset
; maximality for vertices is defined analogously. An
orientation

\overrightarrow{O}
of
\overrightarrow{G}
is an oriented digraph obtained from
\overrightarrow{G}
by keeping the same vertex set but retaining exactly one edge from each symmetric edge. From the definition,
E(\overrightarrow{O})\subseteq E(\overrightarrow{G})
whereas
V(\overrightarrow{G})=V(\overrightarrow{O})
.

A
bipartite digraph

\overrightarrow{G}=\overrightarrow{G}(V,E)
is a digraph whose vertices are partitioned into two subsets (partition sets) such that the endpoints of every edge fall in different subsets. These two subsets
U
and
W
may be viewed as the color classes of a proper vertex coloring of two colors in the sense that no edge has its endpoints colored of the same color. A bipartite graph is
balanced
if
|U|=|W|
.

Let
\overrightarrow{G}=\overrightarrow{G}(V,E)
be any digraph. Take a vertex partition
V_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}V_{k}
of
V
. The associated
quotient graph
is the digraph
\overrightarrow{G}^{*}
whose vertices are the parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
and
V_{i}V_{j}
is an edge of
\overrightarrow{G}^{*}
if there exist
v_{i}\in V_{i}
and
v_{j}\in V_{j}
such that
v_{i}v_{j}\in E
.
For two digraphs
\overrightarrow{G}
and
\overrightarrow{G}^{*}
, a
homomorphism
is a surjective map
\varphi
from
V(\overrightarrow{G})
to
V(\overrightarrow{G}^{*})
that preserves the edges. In this case,
\overrightarrow{G}^{*}
is the
quotient graph
of
\overrightarrow{G}
w.r.t. the map
\varphi
.

The equivalence relation
\dot{\sim}
in a digraph
\overrightarrow{G}
is introduced by defining
x{\dot{\sim}}y
whenever the vertices
x
and
y
have the same out-neighbors and in-neighbors. This gives rise to a particular quotient graph
\partial{\overrightarrow{G}}
, the
classical quotient digraph
of
\overrightarrow{G}
, whose vertices are the equivalence classes w.r.t. the relation
\dot{\sim}
, and edges are as follows: Let
\bar{u}
and
\bar{v}
be two vertices of
\partial\overrightarrow{G}=\partial\overrightarrow{G}(\bar{V},\bar{E})
, then
\bar{u}\bar{v}\in\bar{E}
whenever
uv\in E
for some (and hence for all)

and
v\in\bar{v}
. If
\overrightarrow{G}
contains isolated vertices, then they form a unique equivalence class

, and hence
\partial\overrightarrow{G}
has a unique isolated vertex. If
\overrightarrow{G}=\overrightarrow{G}(V,E)
is bipartite with color classes
U
and
W

is contained in either
U
or
W
. Therefore,
\partial\overrightarrow{G}
is also bipartite. An important property of bipartite digraphs

is that
\partial{\overrightarrow{G}}
is thin, i.e. it contains no two distinct equivalent vertices, in other words
\partial({\partial{\overrightarrow{G}}})=\partial{\overrightarrow{G}}
.

The underlying undirected graph
G
of
\overrightarrow{G}
has the same vertex set of
\overrightarrow{G}
whereas
xy\in E(G)
if and only if either
xy\in E(\overrightarrow{G})
or
yx\in E(\overrightarrow{G})
for any two vertices
x,y\in V(G)
.

2.2
Graphs and Groups

An

of a digraph
\overrightarrow{G}=\overrightarrow{G}(V,E)
is a permutation

V
such that

implies
\pi(x)\pi(y)\in E
for any two vertices

. The automorphisms of
\overrightarrow{G}
form a subgroup of the symmetric group

on

. This group is the

of
\overrightarrow{G}
and is denoted by
\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
. Each subgroup of

is called an automorphism group of
\overrightarrow{G}
. For an undirected graph,

, automorphisms and automorphism groups are defined analogously.

If
\overrightarrow{G}
is a non-connected digraph and
\overrightarrow{G}_{1},\ldots,\overrightarrow{G}_{k}
are its connected components, then
\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
has a subgroup isomorphic to
\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G}_{1})\times\cdots\times\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G}_{k})
.

If
\overrightarrow{G}
is connected and vertex-colored with two colors, then an automorphism
h
of
\overrightarrow{G}
is either
color-preserving
, or
color-switching
according as
h
maps every vertex to another of the same, or opposite color, respectively; see
(
Mirafzal, 2020
, Lemma 3.1)
. The color-preserving automorphisms of any bipartite graph
\overrightarrow{G}
form a subgroup
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
of
\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
. If
\overrightarrow{G}
is connected then either
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})=\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
or
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
has index
2
in
\overrightarrow{G}
. The hypothesis of being connected in the latter claim is necessary.

For any automorphism group
H
of
\overrightarrow{G}
, the
H
-orbit

of a vertex
x\in V(\overrightarrow{G})
consists of all vertices
y\in V(\overrightarrow{G})
such that there exists an automorphism
h\in H
with
y=h(x)
. Since
y\in x^{H}
implies
x\in y^{H}
, the
H

. If two vertices

are in the same

|N^{+}(x)|=|N^{+}(y)|
and
|N^{-}(x)|=|N^{-}(y)|
, but the converse does not hold in general.

Let
\overrightarrow{G}
be a bipartite digraph with color classes
U
and
W
. Take a partition for each color class, say
U=U_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}U_{r}
and
W=W_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}W_{s}
, respectively. Let
\mathcal{C}(\overrightarrow{G})
be the arising quotient graph. For a subgroup
\Gamma
of
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
, if all
U_{i}
and
W_{j}
are
\Gamma
-orbits then

is denoted by
\overrightarrow{G}/\Gamma
, and in this case
\overrightarrow{G}
is a
\Gamma
-cover
of
\overrightarrow{G}/\Gamma
and
\overrightarrow{G}/\Gamma
is the
\Gamma
-quotient
of
\overrightarrow{G}
. If
\Gamma
is a normal subgroup of
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
then
\overrightarrow{G}/\Gamma
has an automorphism group isomorphic to the factor group
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})/\Gamma
. If this is the case, then
\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})/\Gamma
is the
inherited
automorphism group of
\overrightarrow{G}/\Gamma

For an intermediate subgroup
\Sigma
between
\Gamma
and
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
, let
\overrightarrow{G}/\Sigma
be the associated
\Sigma

\overrightarrow{G}/\Sigma
is also a
\Sigma

\overrightarrow{G}/\Gamma

Every automorphism of
\overrightarrow{G}
is also an automorphism of its underlying undirected graph

and equality does not generally hold.

2.3
Two-quasi-best match graphs

It may happen that one or more of the defining axioms trivially hold in a 2-qBMG. If this is the case, then
\overrightarrow{G}
is (Ni)-
trivial
for
1\leq i\leq 3
if the hypotheses in axiom (Ni) are satisfied trivially. More precisely,
\overrightarrow{G}
is (N1)-trivial if there exists no quadruple
(u,v,w,t)
of vertices with

\overrightarrow{G}
is (N2)-trivial if there exists no quadruple of vertices with

\overrightarrow{G}
is (N3)-trivial if there exist no two distinct vertices with a common out-neighbor. If
\overrightarrow{G}
is (N3)-trivial, then it is also
(N3*)-
trivial
. If
\overrightarrow{G}
is non-trivial for (N2), then it is not trivial for (N1) and (N3), as well, and
\overrightarrow{G}
is a
proper
2-qBMG.
In the extreme case, i.e. if all three defining properties hold trivially,
\overrightarrow{G}
is (N)-
trivial
.

For four vertices

of
\overrightarrow{G}
, we say that

is an (N1)-
configuration
if

implies either

or

; in other words when condition (N1) holds for

and

Orientations of a 2-qBMG which is either thin, or enjoys property

4
Automorphism groups of digraphs containing equivalent vertices

We show a useful result on automorphism groups of bipartite digraphs that contain equivalent vertices. The first is a straightforward consequence of the definition of equivalency
\dot{\sim}
and the notion of an automorphism.

Lemma 4.1
.

For a vertex
x
of a bipartite digraph
\overrightarrow{G}
on
n
vertices, let
X
denote the set of all vertices of
\overrightarrow{G}
which are equivalent to
x
. Let
H
be the subgroup of
{\rm{Sym}}_{n}
such that

(i)

H
leaves
X
invariant;

(ii)

H
acts on
X
as the symmetric group on
|X|
labels;

(iii)

H
fixes every vertex off
X
.

Then
H
is an automorphism group of
\overrightarrow{G}
.

Proof.

Let
U
and
W
be the color classes of
\overrightarrow{G}
where
x\in U
. Since
x
is not an isolated vertex, any vertex in
X

is in
U
. Take a vertex
w\in W
in
N^{+}(x)
. Since
w\not\in X
, every automorphism
h\in H
fixes
w
. Therefore the edge
xw
of
\overrightarrow{G}
is mapped by
h
to
h(x)w
. Since
x\dot{\sim}h(x)
, we have that
h(x)w
is also an edge of
\overrightarrow{G}
. The same argument shows that if
w\in N^{-}(x)
, then
wh(x)
is an edge of
\overrightarrow{G}
. Since
h
fixes each vertex off
X
, the claim follows.
∎

For a bipartite digraph
\overrightarrow{G}=\overrightarrow{G}(V,E)
, let
V=X_{1}\cup\ldots\cup X_{k}
be the partition of
V
whose members are the equivalency classes w.r.t. the relation
\dot{\sim}
. For each
X_{i}
let
H_{i}
be the automorphism group of
\overrightarrow{G}
as defined in Lemma
4.1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The subgroup
\Gamma=\langle H_{1},\ldots,H_{K}\rangle
of
\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
which is generated by
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}
is the direct product
H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{k}
and
\Gamma
is a normal subgroup of
\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
.

Proof.

From the definition,
h_{i}h_{j}=h_{j}h_{i}
for
h_{i}\in H_{i},\,h_{j}\in H_{j}
whence
H_{i}H_{j}=H_{j}H_{i}
. Furthermore,
H_{i}\cap H_{j}=\{1\}
for
1\leq i,j\leq k
. Therefore
\langle H_{1},\ldots,H_{K}\rangle=H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{k}
. Take
g\in\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
and
H_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. We have to show that
g\circ H_{i}\circ g^{-1}\in\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}\}
for any
1\leq j\leq k
. Since
g
takes equivalent vertices to equivalent vertices, if there exists
\xi_{j}\in H_{j}
such that
g(\xi_{j})\in H_{i}
then for every
x_{j}\in X_{j}
we have
g(x_{j})\in X_{i}
. From this
|H_{j}|=|H_{i}|
, as
g
is a bijection on
V
.
Moreover, for
h_{i}\in H_{i}
, since
x^{\prime}_{i}\in X_{i}
,

g\circ h_{i}\circ g^{-1}(x_{j})=(g\circ h_{i})(x_{i})=g(h_{i}(x_{i}))=g(x^{\prime}_{i})\in X_{j}.

This shows that
g\circ H_{i}\circ g^{-1}
leaves
X_{j}
invariant. Furthermore, for
x_{m}\in X_{m}
with
m\neq j
, we have
x_{t}=g^{-1}(x_{m})\notin X_{i}
; in particular
h_{i}(x_{t})=x_{t}
.
Therefore,
g\circ h_{i}\circ g^{-1}(x_{m})=(g\circ h_{i})(x_{t})=g(h_{i}(x_{t}))=g(x_{t})=x_{m}
. This shows that
g\circ H_{i}\circ g^{-1}
fixes any vertex outside
X_{j}.
Then, by definition,
g\circ H_{i}\circ g^{-1}\leq H_{j}.
Since
|H_{i}|=|H_{j}|
, this yields
g\circ H_{i}\circ g^{-1}=H_{j}
. Finally, let
g\in\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
such that
g
preserves
X_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
. Then
g
induces a permutation on
X_{i}
, and hence there exists
h_{i}\in H_{i}
such that
g(x_{i})=h_{i}(x_{i})
for every
x_{i}\in X_{i}
. With these
h_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, we have
g=h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{k}
.
∎

The following result is obtained since the orbits of
\Gamma
are exactly the equivalence classes w.r.t. the relation
\dot{\sim}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
\overrightarrow{G}
is a bipartite digraph, then its quotient digraph
\partial\overrightarrow{G}
is the
\Gamma
-quotient of
\overrightarrow{G}
with
\Gamma=H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{k}
.

The converse of Proposition
4.2
also holds.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\overrightarrow{G}
be a bipartite digraph with color classes
U,W
and partitions
U=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{r}
,

W=X_{r+1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{k}
. For
1\leq i\leq k
suppose that
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
has a subgroup
H_{i}\cong{\rm{Sym}}_{X_{i}}
that satisfies (i),(ii), (iii) of Lemma
4.1
for
X=X_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq k
. Then each
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
consists of pairwise equivalent vertices and
\Gamma=\langle H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}\rangle=H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{k}
.

Proof.

For
1\leq i\leq k
, take
x,y\in X_{i}
. Choose an automorphism
h_{i}\in H_{i}
that takes
x
to
y
. Let
v\in V
such that
v\in N^{+}(x)
. Then
v\not\in X_{i}
. Therefore,
h_{i}
takes the ordered pair
(x,v)
to
(h(x),h(v))=(y,v)
. Since
xv\in E(\overrightarrow{G})
and
h
preserves the edges, this yields that
yv\in E(\overrightarrow{G})
. Replacing
N^{+}(x)
with
N^{-}(x)
in the above argument shows that
vx\in E(\overrightarrow{G})
implies
vy\in E(\overrightarrow{G})
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

In Proposition

on

, then its proof still works.

Given any bipartite digraph, Proposition

gives an idea of how to obtain from it some bipartite digraphs with large automorphism groups.
Start with a bipartite digraph

, then

.

5
Automorphism groups of thin 2-qBMGs

Proposition
4.2
shows that the structure of the automorphism group of a digraph is determined by that of its quotient digraph. This motivates the study of automorphism groups of thin digraphs, particularly in the context of the present paper, the study of the automorphism groups of thin 2-qBMGs.

In this section,
\overrightarrow{G}=\overrightarrow{G}(V,E)
always denotes a 2-qBMG. Therefore,
\overrightarrow{G}
has properties (N1),(N2),(N3) and (N3*). In particular,
\overrightarrow{G}
is a bipartite graph with color classes
U
and
W
where
V=U\cup W
.
A key property is stated in the following claim.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
x,y
are from the same
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
-orbit and have a common out-neighbor, then they are equivalent.

Proof.

Take
x,y\in V
be with the following property. There exist
h\in\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
such that
y=h(x)
and
w\in V
such that
w\in N^{+}(x)\cap N^{+}(y)
. We have to show that
x\dot{\sim}y
. From the hypothesis,
x
and
y
are in the same color class, say
U
. Three cases are investigated separately.

Case (I).
Some out-neighbor of
x
is an in-neighbor of
y
.
In this case, there exists
w\in W
for which
w\in N^{+}(x)
and
w\in N^{-}(y)
so that
xw,wy\in E
. We show that
N^{-}(x)\subseteq N^{-}(y)
. Take
z\in W
with
z\in N^{-}(x)
. Then
zx\in E
, and (N2) applies to
zxwy
whence
zy\in E
. Therefore,
z\in N^{-}(y)
. Thus
N^{-}(x)\subseteq N^{-}(y)
. Furthermore,
|N^{-}(x)|=N^{-}(y)|
by
y=h(x)
whence
N^{-}(x)=N^{-}(y)
follows. To show
N^{+}(x)=N^{(}y)
it is enough to observe that (N3) applies to the pair
\{x,y\}
. Since
|N^{+}(x)|=N^{+}(y)|
by
y=h(x)
, neither
N^{+}(x)\subsetneq N^{+}(y)
nor
N^{+}(y)\subsetneq N^{+}(x)
are possible. Therefore,
N^{+}(x)=N^{+}(y)
. Thus
x\dot{\sim}y
, a contradiction.

Case (II).
Some out-neighbor of
y
is an in-neighbor of
x
.
The proof of Case (I) holds true prior to swapping
x
and
y
.

Case (III).
The hypotheses in (N3*) are satisfied.
In this case, (N3*) implies that
N^{-}(x)=N^{-}(y)
and that either
N^{+}(x)\subseteq N^{+}(y)
or
N^{+}(y)\subseteq N^{+}(x)
. Since
|N^{+}(x)|=|N^{+}(y)|
, the latter case only occurs when
N^{+}(x)=N^{+}(y)
. Therefore
x\dot{\sim}y
.
∎

5.1
Orbits of
\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
n vertices

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
\overrightarrow{G}
is a thin 2-qBMG. If an automorphism
g
of
\overrightarrow{G}
fixes a vertex
v\in V
, then
g
fixes each vertex in the in-neighborhood of
v
.

Proof.

Take
g\in\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
and
v\in V
such that
g(v)=v
. On the contrary, assume that
x\in N^{-}(v)
exists, such as
g(x)\neq x
. Let
y=g(x)
. Then
y
belongs to the orbit
x^{H}
where
H
is the subgroup generated by
g
. Furthermore,
v
is an out-neighbor of
x
and
y
. Therefore, Proposition
5.1
applies whence
x\dot{\sim}y
follows, a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that
\overrightarrow{G}
is a thin 2-qBMG. For any two subsets
U_{1}\subseteq U
and
W_{1}\subseteq W
which are
\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
-orbits, let
\overrightarrow{G}_{1}=\overrightarrow{G}_{1}(V_{1},E_{1})
be the directed subgraph of
\overrightarrow{G}
induced on
V_{1}=U_{1}\cup W_{1}
. If there exist
x_{1}\in U_{1},y_{1}\in W_{1}
such that
x_{1}y_{1}\in E
then one of the following cases occurs:

(i)

\overrightarrow{G}_{1}
is the union of pairwise disjoint stars where
U_{1}
comprises sources while
W_{1}
does sinks, and
|N^{+}(x_{1})||U_{1}|=|W_{1}|
; in particular if
|U_{1}|=|W_{1}|
then
E_{1}
is the union of pairwise disjoint edges.

(ii)

E_{1}
is the union of pairwise disjoint symmetric edges.

Proof.

Take

such that

. Assume that there exist

and
y_{2}\in W_{1}
such that

. Since

is a
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
-orbit, we find some
\pi\in\rm{Aut}(\overrightarrow{G})
such that
\pi(y_{2})=y_{1}
. Let

. Then
x_{1}y_{1},y_{1}x_{3}\in E
. Also,

as

is an
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
-orbit. Two cases occur according as

or

In the former case, the arguments in Case (I) of the proof of Proposition
5.1
can be used for

to prove that
x_{1}{\dot{\sim}}x_{3}
. Since
\overrightarrow{G}
is thin, this is a contradiction. Thus, as
U_{1}
and
W_{1}
are
\overrightarrow{G}
-orbits, each vertex in
U_{1}
is a source whereas each vertex in
W_{1}
is a sink of
\overrightarrow{G}_{1}
. We show the second claim in (i). From Proposition

,
N^{+}(x_{1})\cap N^{+}(u_{1})=\emptyset
for any two distinct

. Since

is a
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
-orbit,

holds for any

. Therefore,
|N^{+}(x_{1})||U_{1}|=|W_{1}|
for

. If
|U_{1}|=|W_{1}|
then
|N^{+}(u_{1})|=|N^{+}(x_{1})|=1
for every

. Since
|N^{-}(w)|=1

\overrightarrow{G}_{1}
is regularly
N
-trivial.

In the latter case,
x_{1}=x_{3}
, and hence
x_{1}y_{1}
is a symmetric edge in

. Since
U_{1}
is an
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
-orbit, each vertex in
U_{1}
is an endpoint of a symmetric edge. Since the same is valid for

, the first claim in (ii) holds. From Proposition

, there exists no

in

other than

such that

holds. Similarly, no

in

other than

satisfies

. Therefore, the second claim in (ii) also holds.
∎

A corollary of Proposition

is stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.4
.

Assume that
\overrightarrow{G}=\overrightarrow{G}(U\cup W,E)

\overrightarrow{G}

and

of

.

6
Orientations and automorphisms of 2-qBMGs

We state two properties of orientations of 2-qBMGs.

Theorem 6.1
.

Every orientation of a 2-qBMG satisfying (
*
) is a 2-qBMG.

Proof.

Let
\overrightarrow{G}=\overrightarrow{G}(V,E)
be a 2-qBMG satisfying (
*
). Take an orientation
\overrightarrow{O}=\overrightarrow{O}(V,F)
of
\overrightarrow{G}
with
F\subseteq E
. We prove that
\overrightarrow{O}
also satisfies (N1),(N2) and (N3*).

Take an (N1)-configuration [u,t,w,v] in
\overrightarrow{O}
. This is also an (N1)-configuration in
\overrightarrow{G}
, therefore
uv
are dependent in
E
and also in
F
. Hence, (N1) holds for
\overrightarrow{O}
.

To show (N2) take
u,v,w,t\in V
such that
uv,vw,wt\in F
. As
F\subseteq E
, and (N2) holds in
\overrightarrow{G}
, this yields
ut\in E
. If
ut
is not a symmetric edge of
\overrightarrow{G}
, then
ut\in F
also holds. If
ut
is a symmetric edge of
\overrightarrow{G}
then
tu,uv,vw\in E
and hence
tw\in E
from (N2). But then
t
is an endpoint of two distinct symmetric edges, namely
tu
and
tw
, a contradiction with (
*
).

To show that (N3) holds take
u,v\in V
such that
w\in N(u)\cap N(v)
in
\overrightarrow{O}
. Then (N3) applies to
u
and
v
, and
N^{+}(u)\subseteq N^{+}(v)
or
N^{+}(v)\subseteq N^{+}(u)
in
\overrightarrow{G}
. Suppose this does not hold for
\overrightarrow{O}
. Then there are
v^{*}\in N^{+}(u)\setminus N^{+}(v)
and
u^{*}\in N^{+}(v)\setminus N^{+}(u)
in
\overrightarrow{O}
. Hence,
uu^{*}
and
vv^{*}
must be symmetric edges in
\overrightarrow{G}
. From (
*
)
wv\notin E
and
wu\notin E
, and (N3*) applies to
u
and
v
in
\overrightarrow{G}
. Hence
N^{-}(u)=N^{-}(v)
in
\overrightarrow{G}
, and
u^{*}\in N^{-}(v)
, violating
(*)
for
v
.
∎

If
\overrightarrow{O}
is an orientation and
g\in\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
is an automorphism of
\overrightarrow{G}
, then
g
is not an automorphism of
\overrightarrow{O}
in general but some important exceptions occur. Such an exception is the
UW
-
orientation,
which is the special orientation of
\overrightarrow{G}
obtained by retaining that edge from each symmetric edge whose tail and head are in the color classes
U
and
W
, respectively. This claim is a consequence of the following theorem.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\overrightarrow{G}
be a 2-qBMG with color classes
U
and
W
. Let
\overrightarrow{O}
be a
UW
-orientation of
\overrightarrow{G}
.
Then
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})=\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{O})
.

Proof.

Take a symmetric edge with endpoints
u
and
w
. Then
uw,wu\in E
.

If
g\in\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
then
u\in U
implies
g(u)\in U
, and hence
g(w)\in W
. Therefore,
g
takes the edge
uw
of
\overrightarrow{O}
to the edge
g(u)g(w)
, which is also an edge in
\overrightarrow{O}
. Thus
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})\leq\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{O})
.

Conversely, let
g\in\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{O})
, and extend the action of
g
to
E
by defining the image of
wu\in E
by
g(wu)=g(w)g(u)
. Then

implies that
g(u)g(w)\in E
is a symmetric edge. Therefore,
g(w)g(u)\in E
. Thus
\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{O})\leq\rm{Aut}_{I}(\overrightarrow{G})
.
∎

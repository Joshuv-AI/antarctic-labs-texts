Introduction

In this paper, we will investigate the existence and multiplicity of normalized solutions to the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\lambda u+f(u)&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,\zeta,\gamma}u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx=\mu,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 1
) is a smooth bounded domain,
\mu>0
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is a part of the unknown which appears as a Lagrange multiplier,
f,g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous functions satisfying some technical conditions. The boundary operator
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,\zeta,\gamma}
is defined by

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,\zeta,\gamma}u=\alpha u+\zeta\frac{\partial u}{\partial\eta}-\gamma g(u),

where
\alpha,\zeta,\gamma\in\{0,1\}
and
\eta
denotes the outward unit normal on
\partial\Omega
.

If
\alpha=1
and
\zeta=\gamma=0
, problem
(P)^{\mu}_{\alpha,\zeta,\gamma}
becomes the following one

[formula]

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=\lambda u+f(u)\textrm{ \ in \ }\Omega,\\
&u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\\
&\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dx=\mu,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 1)
can be a smooth bounded domain or the whole
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
is strongly related with the following time-dependent, nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\dfrac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}(t,x)=\Delta_{x}\Psi(t,x)+h(|\Psi(t,x)|)\Psi(t,x),&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}|\Psi(t,x)|^{2}\,dx=\mu&\end{array}\right.

where
f(u)=h(|u|)u
, the prescribed mass
\sqrt{\mu}
appears in nonlinear optics and the theory of Bose-Einstein condensates (see
[
24
,
45
,
47
]
). Solutions
u
to
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
correspond to standing waves
\Psi(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)
of the foregoing time-dependent equation. The prescribed mass represents the power supply in nonlinear optics or the total number of particles in Bose-Einstein condensates.

If
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there is a vast literature concerning the existence, multiplicity and stability of normalized solutions for problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
. In the seminar paper
[
17
]
, for the case of
L^{2}
-subcritical, Cazenave and Lions proved the existence of normalized ground states by the minimization method and presented a general method which enables us to prove the orbital stability of some standing waves for problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
. However, the
L^{2}
-supercritical problem is
totally different. In
[
28
]
, Jeanjean introduced the fibering map

\tilde{I}(u,s)=\frac{e^{2s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{e^{sN}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(e^{\frac{sN}{2}}u(x))\,dx,

where
F(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,ds
. Using the scaling technique and the Pohožaev identity, the author proved that
I(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)\,dx
on
S(\mu):=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,dx=\mu\}
and
\tilde{I}
on
S(\mu)\times\mathbb{R}
satisfy the mountain pass geometry with equal mountain pass levels, then the author constructed a bounded Palais-Smale sequence on
S(\mu)
and obtained a radial solution to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
. It is worth noting that the scaling technique plays a crucial role in the study of normalized solutions to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For further results related to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, see

If
\Omega
is bounded, unlike the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we are no longer faced with the lack of compactness, and some problems become simpler. For instance, in the
L^{2}
-subcritical case, the compact embeddings imply the energy functional is weakly lower semi-continuous, and then, since the energy functional is bounded from below and coercive on
S(\mu):=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx=\mu\}
, one can easily prove the existence of normalized ground states by the minimization method. However, in the
L^{2}
-supercritical case, some new difficulties arise. For example, the scaling technique—commonly used in the analysis on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
—is no longer applicable, and the Pohožaev identity for bounded domains has an additional term involving the boundary of the set
\Omega
, which creates further complications when attempting to prove the boundedness of minimizing sequences. To address these challenges, several works have focused on the
L^{2}
-supercritical case. In
[
40
]
, when
f(u)=\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u
and
2+\frac{4}{N}\leq p<2^{*}
, using blow-up analysis and Morse theory, Pierotti and Verzini proved that, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
admits solutions having Morse index bounded above by
k
only if
\mu
is sufficiently small and then, they provide existence results for certain ranges of
\mu
.
Their assumptions on
f
are different from ours. Since we are dealing with general
f\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, the arguments based on Morse theory and blow-up analysis do not apply in our case.

In
[
10

normalized solutions
(u_{r,\mu},\lambda_{r,\mu})\in H_{0}^{1}(r\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}
to the following problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+V(x)u=\lambda u+\lvert u\rvert^{q-2}u+\beta\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u&\text{in }r\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial r\Omega,\\
\int_{r\Omega}u^{2}\,dx=\mu,\end{array}\right.

and showed that

in

,
\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to\infty}\max_{x\in r\Omega}u_{r,\mu}<C_{\mu}
for some positive constant

and
\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to\infty}\lambda_{r,\mu}<0
, where
\Omega
is a bounded smooth star-shaped domain or

,

,

,

, and

is large. The inequality

\partial\Omega

denotes the outward unit normal vector at

. However, for a general smooth bounded domain
\Omega

\mu>0
, there exists

such that, for all

, problem (

) admits a solution. Compared to this existence result for the case
\beta=0
, we will establish an existence result for the general

-supercritical case under the condition that the domain has a sufficiently small first eigenvalue. In particular, this implies that for any smooth bounded domain

is given by
\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}{r^{2}}
. In
[
44

, for the Brézis-Nirenberg problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\lambda u+\lvert u\rvert^{2^{*}-2}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\

where

is a smooth bounded domain, Song and Zou constructed a new type of linking within an open set of a Hilbert-Riemannian manifold below a certain energy, and proved the existence of multiple sign-changing normalized solutions. In addition, the authors also studied the Morse index of the solutions and the bifurcation points of the above problem. In particular, the authors extended their results to the cases

In contrast, for general nonlinearities

More importantly, the approach developed in this paper can be applied to a large class of elliptic equations when studying the existence and multiplicity of normalized solutions. If
\alpha=0
,
\zeta=1
and
\gamma=0
, problem
(P)^{\mu}_{\alpha,\zeta,\gamma}
becomes the following one

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\lambda u+f(u)&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\eta}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx=\mu,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 1)
is a smooth bounded domain. A key motivation for studying problem
(P)^{\mu}_{0,1,0}
is the ergodic Mean Field Games (MFG) system. MFG were independently introduced in seminal works by Huang, Caines, and Malhamé
[
27
]
and by Lasry and Lions
[
35
]
. The primary objective of MFG is to establish a framework for characterizing Nash equilibria in differential games involving an infinite number of agents that are indistinguishable from one another. For more details, we refer reader to
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
41
]
and the references therein. Such equilibria can be characterized by a system satisfying normalization in
L^{1}(\Omega)
as follows

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+H(\nabla v)+\lambda=h(m(x))&\text{in }\Omega,\\
-\Delta m-\text{div}(m\nabla H(\nabla v))=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial\eta}=0,\quad\dfrac{\partial m}{\partial\eta}+m\nabla H(\nabla v)\cdot v=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}mdx=1,\quad\int_{\Omega}vdx=0,\end{cases}

From the point of view of PDE, problem (
1.2
) is an elliptic system that combines a Kolmogorov equation for
m
and a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation for
v
. The Neumann boundary conditions are based on the assumption that agents’ trajectories are restricted to
\Omega
by bouncing off the boundary in a normal direction. For the quadratic Hamilton case
H(\nabla v)=|\nabla v|^{2}
, by using a Hopf-Cole transformation
\phi=e^{-\nu}/\int e^{-\nu}=\sqrt{m}
, (
1.2
) is reduced to

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\phi=\lambda\phi+h(\phi^{2})\phi&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\eta}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}\,dx=1.\end{array}\right.

Problem (
1.3
) is equivalent to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{0,1,0}
by taking a simple transformation
\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}u
and
f(u)=h(\frac{u^{2}}{c})u
.

In
[
39
]
, Pellacci, Pistoia, Vaira and Verzini investigated normalized solutions of problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
and

with

as

, where

is either a non-degenerate critical point of the mean curvature

of the boundary
\partial\Omega
, or the maximum point of the distance function from
\partial\Omega
. In
[
19
]
, using the monotonicity trick, Morse theory and blow-up analysis, Chang, Rǎdulescu and Zhang proved the existence of normalized solutions to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{0,1,0}
for
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
with
\mu_{0}>0
. They assumed that

(

) is a smooth bounded domain and that

satisfies

there exist constants

and

such that

and there exist constants

and

such that

It is worth noting that their assumptions on

differ substantially from ours. Since we dealing with general nonlinearities

without assuming differentiability, the techniques employed in

2.
The problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}

In this section, we endow
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
with the standard norm

\|u\|:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which induces the scalar product

(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v\,dx.

For
L^{p}(\Omega)
spaces, we use the usual norm denoted by
\lVert u\rVert_{p}
. The scalar product induced by
L^{2}(\Omega)
-norm is denoted by

(u,v)_{2}:=\int_{\Omega}uv\,dx.

We consider the energy functional
E:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
associated with problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
, defined by

E(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}-\Psi(u),

where

\Psi(u)=\int_{\Omega}F(u)\,dx.

2.1.
The Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities

Since
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\subset H(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(see
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
), for any
p\in[2,2^{*})
, there exists a constant
C_{p,N}>0
, depending only on
p
and
N
, such that

(2.1)

\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq C_{p,N}\|u\|_{2}^{(1-\beta_{p})p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\beta_{p}p}\quad\forall u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

where
\beta_{p}=N\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)
.

2.2.
The perturbation functional

We define the following perturbation functional

E_{r,\mu}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}-\Psi(u)-H_{r,\mu}(u),\;u\in U_{\mu},

where
U_{\mu}:=\{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}<\mu\}
, and
H_{r,\mu}(u)
is a penalization term defined by

H_{r,\mu}(u):=f_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)\text{ with }f_{r}(s):=\frac{s^{r}}{1-s}\;\quad\forall s\in[0,1),

and
r>1
is a parameter that will be chosen large enough. A straightforward computation gives

(2.2)

f_{r}^{\prime}(s)=\frac{rs^{r-1}}{1-s}+\frac{s^{r}}{(1-s)^{2}}>\frac{r}{s}f_{r}(s)>0,\;\quad\forall s\in(0,1)

and

(2.3)

f^{\prime\prime}_{r}(s)=\frac{r(r-1)s^{r-2}}{1-s}+\frac{rs^{r-1}}{(1-s)^{2}}+\frac{rs^{r-1}}{(1-s)^{2}}+\frac{2s^{r}}{(1-s)^{3}}>0,\;\quad\forall s\in(0,1),

from where it follows that
f^{\prime}_{r}
is increasing for
s\in[0,1)
. Fixing
h_{r}(s)=f^{\prime}_{r}(s)s-f_{r}(s)
for
s\in[0,1)
, the information above yields

(2.4)

h^{\prime}_{r}(s)=f^{\prime\prime}_{r}(s)s>0,\;\quad\forall s\in(0,1),

showing that
h_{r}
is an increasing function for
s\in[0,1)
.

It is standard to prove that
E_{r,\mu}\in C^{1}(U_{\mu},\mathbb{R})
and for any
u\in U_{\mu}
and
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, one has

\langle E_{r,\mu}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=(u,v)-\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\,dx-\frac{2}{\mu}f^{\prime}_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)(u,v)_{2}.

Let
\beta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
be such that

\begin{cases}\beta\equiv-1&\text{ on }(-\infty,-1),\\
\beta(t)=t,&\forall t\geq 0,\\
\beta(t)\leq 0,&\forall t\leq 0.\end{cases}

We define a new functional
J_{r,\mu}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.5)

J_{r,\mu}(u)=\begin{cases}\beta(E_{r,\mu}(u))&\text{ if }\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}<\mu,\\
-1&otherwise.\end{cases}

By Lemma
7.1
,
J_{r,\mu}\in C^{1}(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, if
u
is a critical point of
J_{r,\mu}
with
J_{r,\mu}(u)\geq 0
, then
u
is also a critical point of
E_{r,\mu}
at the same energy level. The same conclusion holds for Cerami sequences. Therefore, instead of
E_{r,\mu}
, we can look for positive min-max levels of
J_{r,\mu}
, which is more convenient, since
J_{r,\mu}
is defined on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and satisfies
J_{r,\mu}=-1
on
\partial U_{\mu}
.

2.3.
Compactness of Cerami sequences

In this subsection, we always assume that (
f_{1}
)-(
f_{3}
) hold or (
f_{1}
) and (
f_{4}
) hold. Fix
\mu>0
. Assume that for any
r>1
sufficiently large, there exists a sequence
\{u_{n,r}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\{u_{n,r}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a Cerami sequence of
E_{r,\mu}
at level
c_{r}>0
, i.e.,
\{u_{n,r}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies

(2.6)

E_{r,\mu}(u_{n,r})\rightarrow c_{r}\text{ and }(1+\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert)\lVert E_{r,\mu}^{\prime}(u_{n,r})\rVert\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

Moreover, we assume that
r\mapsto c_{r}
is non-decreasing.
Define

\lambda_{n,r}:=\frac{2}{\mu}f^{\prime}_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right).

Lemma 2.1
.

For
r>1
sufficiently large,
\{u_{n,r}\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We first assume that (
f_{1}
)-(
f_{3}
) hold. Let
t_{n}\in[0,1]
be such that
E_{r,\mu}(t_{n}u_{n,r})=\max_{t\in[0,1]}E_{r,\mu}(tu_{n,r})
. We claim that
\{E_{r,\mu}(t_{n}u_{n,r})\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded from above. In fact, if either
t_{n}=0
or
t_{n}=1
, we are done. Thereby, we can assume
t_{n}\in(0,1)
, and so,
E_{r,\mu}^{\prime}(t_{n}u_{n,r})u_{n,r}=0
. From this,

\displaystyle 2E_{r,\mu}(t_{n}u_{n,r})=

\displaystyle 2E_{r,\mu}(t_{n}u_{n,r})-E_{r,\mu}^{\prime}(t_{n}u_{n,r})t_{n}u_{n,r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[f(t_{n}u_{n,r})t_{n}u_{n,r}-2F(t_{n}u_{n,r})\right]\,dx

\displaystyle+2f^{\prime}_{r}\left(\frac{t_{n}^{2}\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)\frac{t_{n}^{2}\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert^{2}_{2}}{\mu}-2f_{r}\left(\frac{t_{n}^{2}\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right).

The condition (
f_{2}
) gives that
s\mapsto f(s)s-2F(s)
is non-decreasing on
(0,+\infty)
and non-increasing on
(-\infty,0)
. By (
2.4
), we know that
s\mapsto h_{r}(s)=f_{r}^{\prime}(s)s-f(s)
is increasing on
[0,1)
, and thus,

\displaystyle 2E_{r,\mu}(t_{n}u_{n,r})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[f(u_{n,r})u_{n,r}-2F(u_{n,r})\right]\,dx+2f^{\prime}_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert^{2}_{2}}{\mu}-2f_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=2E_{r,\mu}(u_{n,r})-E_{r,\mu}^{\prime}(u_{n,r})u_{n,r}=2E_{r,\mu}(u_{n,r})+o_{n}(1).

Since
\{E_{r,\mu}(u_{n,r})\}_{n\geq 1}
converges, it follows that
\{E_{r,\mu}(t_{n}u_{n,r})\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded from above.

To prove the boundedness of
\{u_{n,r}\}_{n\geq 1}
, we argue by contradiction. Suppose, on the contrary, that
\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
. Let
v_{n}=u_{n,r}/\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert
, then
\lVert v_{n}\rVert=1
. Then, up to a subsequence, there exists
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v
. Next, we will show that
v=0
. Otherwise, for
x\in\Omega_{*}=\{y\in\Omega:v(y)\neq 0\}
, we have
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}|u_{n,r}(x)|=\infty
. Hence, it follows from (
f_{2}
)-(
f_{3}
) and Fatou’s lemma that

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{c_{r}+o_{n}(1)}{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{E_{r,\mu}(u_{n,r})}{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{2}-\int_{\Omega_{*}}\frac{F(u_{n,r})}{(u_{n,r})^{2}}v_{n}^{2}\,dx\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{*}}\frac{F(u_{n,r})}{(u_{n,r})^{2}}v_{n}^{2}\,dx\leqslant\frac{1}{2}-\int_{\Omega_{*}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{F(u_{n,r})}{(u_{n,r})^{2}}v_{n}^{2}\,dx=-\infty.

This contradiction shows that
v=0
.

Notice that for each
B>0
, one has
\frac{B}{\|u_{n,r}\|}\in[0,1]
and
\frac{B^{2}\lVert v_{n}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}=\frac{B^{2}}{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert^{2}}\in[0,1)
for
n
sufficiently large. Thus,

E_{r,\mu}(t_{n}u_{n,r})\geq E_{r,\mu}\left(\frac{B}{\|u_{n,r}\|}u_{n,r}\right)=E_{r,\mu}(Bv_{n})=\frac{B^{2}}{2}-\int_{\Omega}F(Bv_{n})-f_{r}\left(\frac{B^{2}\lVert v_{n}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right).

Since the embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
is compact for all
2\leq p<2^{*}
, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}E_{r,\mu}(t_{n}u_{n,r})\geq\frac{B^{2}}{2}\quad\text{for every }B>0,

which is a contradiction showing that
\{u_{n,r}\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, once that
\{E_{r,\mu}(t_{n}u_{n,r})\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded from above.

Now, we assume that (
f_{1}
) and (
f_{4}
) hold. Then, by choosing
r>\frac{q}{2}
, (
f_{4}
) combined with (
2.2
) leads to

\displaystyle\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2qE(u_{n,r})-2\langle E^{\prime}(u_{n,r}),u_{n,r}\rangle}{q-2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2qE(u_{n,r})-2\langle E^{\prime}(u_{n,r}),u_{n,r}\rangle+4f^{\prime}_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert^{2}_{2}}{\mu}-2qf_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)}{q-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2qE_{r,\mu}(u_{n,r})-2\langle E_{r,\mu}^{\prime}(u_{n,r}),u_{n,r}\rangle}{q-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2qc_{r}}{q-2}+o_{n}(1).

This complete the proof of Lemma
2.1
.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If
c_{\infty}:=\lim\limits_{r\to+\infty}c_{r}<+\infty
, then

\lambda_{r}:=\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda_{n,r}<\infty

and

\limsup_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda_{r}\leq\frac{2c_{\infty}}{\mu}.

Proof.

First, by assumptions
r\mapsto c_{r}
is non-decreasing and
c_{r_{0}}>0
for some
r_{0}>1
sufficiently large, we have
c_{\infty}\geq c_{r_{0}}>0
. Since
f_{r}^{\prime}(0)=f_{r}(0)=0
and
h_{r}(s)=f_{r}^{\prime}(s)s-f_{r}(s)\to+\infty
as
s\to 1^{-}
, by continuity of
s\mapsto f_{r}^{\prime}(s)s-f_{r}(s)
, there exists
\xi_{r}\in(0,1)
such that

c_{\infty}=f_{r}^{\prime}(\xi_{r})\xi_{r}-f_{r}(\xi_{r}).

We now claim that
\xi_{r}\to 1^{-}
as
r\to+\infty
. Argue by contradiction, suppose that there exists a subsequence
\{r_{n}\}
such that
r_{n}\nearrow+\infty
(monotone sequences) and
\displaystyle\xi_{r_{n}}\to\xi
as
n\to+\infty
with
\xi\in[0,1)
. Then, by the definition of
f_{r}
,

c_{\infty}=f_{r_{n}}^{\prime}(\xi_{r_{n}})\xi_{r_{n}}-f_{r_{n}}(\xi_{r_{n}})=\frac{(r_{n}-1)\xi_{r_{n}}^{r_{n}}}{1-\xi_{r_{n}}}+\frac{\xi_{r_{n}}^{r_{n}+1}}{(1-\xi_{r_{n}})^{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty,

which is a contradiction. Then,
\xi_{r}\to 1^{-}
. Thus, by (
2.2
),

(2.7)

f_{r}(\xi_{r})\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad f_{r}^{\prime}(\xi_{r})\to c_{\infty}\quad\text{as }r\to+\infty.

Thanks to (
2.6
), one has

\displaystyle 2E_{r,\mu}(u_{n,r})+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=2E_{r,\mu}(u_{n,r})-\langle E_{r,\mu}^{\prime}(u_{n,r}),u_{n,r}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(u_{n,r})u_{n,r}\,dx-2\int_{\Omega}F(u_{n,r})\,dx+2f^{\prime}_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert^{2}_{2}}{\mu}-2f_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right).

As
f(t)t-2F(t)\geq 0
for all

, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\left[f^{\prime}_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert^{2}_{2}}{\mu}-f_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}E_{r,\mu}(u_{n,r})

\displaystyle=c_{r}\leq c_{\infty}=f_{r}^{\prime}(\xi_{r})\xi_{r}-f_{r}(\xi_{r}).

Employing the fact that
s\mapsto f^{\prime}_{r}(s)
and
s\mapsto f_{r}^{\prime}(s)s-f_{r}(s)
are strictly increasing on
[0,1)
, we deduce that

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\lambda_{n,r}=\frac{2}{\mu}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}f^{\prime}_{r}\left(\frac{\lVert u_{n,r}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\mu}\right)\leq\frac{2}{\mu}f_{r}^{\prime}\left(\xi_{r}\right),

then by (

), one has

\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\lambda_{r}\leq\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{2}{\mu}f_{r}^{\prime}\left(\xi_{r}\right)=\frac{2c_{\infty}}{\mu}.

This completes the proof.

as

.
Repeating the argument used in the proof of Theorem
1.4
and applying Lemma
3.1
with

, we conclude that, for any

, the functional

admits at least

4.
The problem
(P)^{\mu}_{0,1,0}

In this section, we consider the problem below

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\lambda u+f(u)&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\eta}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx=\mu\end{array}\right.

which is equivalent to solve the problem below

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+u=\lambda u+f(u)&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\eta}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx=\mu.\end{array}\right.

We define the norm on
H^{1}(\Omega)
by

\|u\|:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
E:H^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be the energy functional associated with problem
(P)^{\mu}_{0,1,0}
defined by

E(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}-\Psi(u),

where

\Psi(u)=\int_{\Omega}F(u)\,dx.

By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, for any
p\in[2,2^{*})
,

(4.1)

\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq C_{p,\Omega}^{\prime\prime}\|u\|_{2}^{(1-\beta_{p})p}\|u\|^{\beta_{p}p}\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where
\beta_{p}=N\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)
and
C_{p,\Omega}^{\prime\prime}>0
depends only on
p
and
\Omega
. Moreover,
H^{1}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
2\leq p<2^{*}
. Have this in mind, all computations performed in the previous section can be adapted to the present setting with only minor modifications. Assume that (
f_{1}^{\prime\prime}
), (
f_{2}
) and (
f_{3}
) hold or (
f_{1}^{\prime\prime}
) and (
f_{4}
) hold. Then, we obtain a solution
(u_{pe},\lambda_{pe})\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times[-1,-1+\lambda_{0,1,0}]
to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
. However, it remains unknown whether the solution
u_{pe}
is one of the constant functions
\pm\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}}
, because
\left(\pm\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}},-\frac{f\left(\pm\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}}\right)}{\pm\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}}}\right)
are also solutions to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
. An open and interesting question is whether one can distinguish
u_{pe}
from the constant functions. Let
f(u)=\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u
with
2+\frac{4}{N}<p<2^{*}
and
\mu>0
small. Then, the constant function
\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}}
is a local minimizer on
S_{0,1,0}(\mu):=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx=\mu\}
, see
[
19

. Thus, the constant function
\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}}
is a solution to equation

-\Delta u=-\left(\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}u+\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u

with the Morse index
m\left(\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}}\right)=1
. In
[

, Remark 3.6]
, the authors proves problem

has a solution
(u_{mp},\lambda_{mp})\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}
and

is a critical point of mountain pass type constrained on

. Then,

cannot be a constant function. However, this argument fails when we apply the perturbation method. In fact, repeating the argument of Lemma
2.3
and

, we have
\lVert u_{r_{n}}\rVert^{2}_{2}<\mu=\left\|\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}}\right\|_{2}^{2}
and

remains unknown. Moreover, by a standard argument, see

15

, we will conclude that

is a solution to equation

with the Morse index
m(u_{pe})=1=m\left(\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}}\right)
, hence, it is hard to distinguish
u_{pe}
from the constant functions with a Morse index argument.

Now suppose further that
f(t)=-f(-t)
. Then, following the argument in the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we conclude that, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, there exists
\mu_{m}^{***}>0
, depending on

and

, such that, for any

admits at least

nontrivial solutions
(u_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{1}),(u_{2},\bar{\lambda}_{2}),\cdots,(u_{m},\bar{\lambda}_{m})\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times[0,+\infty)
, and these solutions have distinct energy levels, i.e.,

, for any

5.
Existence of ground states to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}

We first characterize the energy of

is a solution to problem

Define

and

(5.1)

\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mu):=\{u\in S_{1,0,0}(\mu):\langle E^{\prime}(u),u\rangle\geq 0\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that either (
f_{1}
)-(
f_{3}
) hold or (
f_{1}
), (
f_{2}
) and (
f_{4}
) hold. Let
(u,\lambda_{u})
denote the solution of problem

obtained in Theorems
1.1
or

u
satisfies

(5.2)

E(u)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(\mu)}E(u)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mu)}E(u).

Proof.

Let
(u,\lambda_{u})
be a solution to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
obtained in Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
. It is clear that

and

, hence,

(5.3)

E(u)\geq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(\mu)}E(u)\geq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mu)}E(u).

We now prove

(5.4)

E(u)\leq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mu)}E(u).

For any
v\in\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mu)
, define

g(t):=E(tv),\quad t\geq 0.

Then, by (f
2
), for all
0\leq t\leq 1
, we have

g^{\prime}(tv)=t\lVert v\rVert^{2}-\int_{\Omega}f(tv)v\,dx\geq t\left(\lVert v\rVert^{2}-\int_{\Omega}f(v)v\,dx\right)=t\langle E^{\prime}(v),v\rangle\geq 0,

which implies

is non-decreasing on

.
By (
2.8
), we conclude that

E(u)=c_{\infty}\leq\sup_{0\leq t<1}E(tv)=\sup_{0\leq t<1}g(t)=\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}g(t)=g(1)=E(v).

This proves (
5.4
). Thus, by (
5.3
), we obtain

c_{\infty}=E(u)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(\mu)}E(u)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mu)}E(u).

∎

We note that (
5.2
) implies that
u
is a minimizer of
E
constrained on
\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mu)
, and that
(u,\lambda_{u})
is a solution to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
such that its energy

is minimal among all the solutions
(v,\lambda_{v})
of problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
with

. Moreover, this result can be easily extended to all other cases mentioned in this paper.

Define

\mathcal{S}(\mu):=\{u\in S_{1,0,0}(\mu):\langle E^{\prime}(u),\cdot\rangle=\lambda_{u}(u,\cdot)_{2}\text{ for some }\lambda_{u}\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Definition 5.2
.

We say that
(u,\lambda_{u})
is a normalized ground state solution to problem

, if

such that

Next, we present our main result of this section, which concerns the existence of ground states to problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 3)
be a smooth bounded domain, and let
\Omega
be star-shaped with respect to the origin. Suppose that (
f_{1}
), (
f_{2}
) and (
f_{4}
) hold with
q>2+\frac{4}{N}
.
If
0<\mu\leq\mu^{*}_{s}
, where
\mu^{*}_{s}
is defined in (
5.7

(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
has a ground state solution
(u,\lambda_{u})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times\left[\frac{2\lambda_{1,0,0}(q-2^{*})}{2^{*}(q-2-\frac{4}{N})},\lambda_{1,0,0}\right]
.

The following lemma plays a key role in proving the existence of ground states for problem
(P)^{\mu}_{1,0,0}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that (
f_{1}
), (
f_{2}
) and (
f_{4}
) hold with
q>2+\frac{4}{N}
. For all
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mu)
, when
E(u)\leq\frac{\mu\lambda_{1,0,0}}{2}
, we have

\frac{N\lambda_{1,0,0}(q-2^{*})}{2^{*}(q-2)}\leq\lambda_{u}\leq\lambda_{1,0,0}.

Proof.

Since
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mu)
, we have

(5.5)

\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}_{2}=\lambda_{u}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{2}+\int_{\Omega}f(u)u\,dx=\mu\lambda_{u}+\int_{\Omega}f(u)u\,dx.

By
E(u)\leq\frac{\mu\lambda_{1,0,0}}{2}
and (
f_{4}
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mu\lambda_{1,0,0}}{2}\geq E(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}_{2}-\int_{\Omega}F(u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu\lambda_{u}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}f(u)u\,dx-\int_{\Omega}F(u)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mu\lambda_{u}}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})\int_{\Omega}f(u)u\,dx,

hence,
\lambda_{u}\leq\lambda_{1}
and

(5.6)

\int_{\Omega}f(u)u\,dx\leq\frac{q\mu(\lambda_{1,0,0}-\lambda_{u})}{q-2}.

By the Pohozǎev identity, see, e.g.,
[
46
, Theorem B.1]
, we have

\frac{N-2}{2N}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\partial\Omega}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}(x\cdot\bm{n})\,d\sigma=\frac{\lambda_{u}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}F(u)\,dx,

where
\bm{n}(x)
denotes the outward unit normal vector at
x\in\partial\Omega
. Since
\Omega
be star-shaped with respect to the origin, we have
x\cdot\bm{n}(x)>0
. Thus,

\frac{N-2}{2N}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{\lambda_{u}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}F(u)\,dx=\frac{\mu\lambda_{u}}{2}+\int_{\Omega}F(u)\,dx.

By (
5.5
), we obtain

\frac{\mu\lambda_{u}}{2}+\int_{\Omega}F(u)\,dx\geq\frac{(N-2)\mu\lambda_{u}}{2N}+\frac{N-2}{2N}\int_{\Omega}f(u)u\,dx.

Therefore, by (
5.6
) and (
f_{4}
),

\lambda_{u}\geq\frac{N}{\mu}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2^{*}}f(u)u-F(u)\right)\,dx\geq\frac{N}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2^{*}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\int_{\Omega}f(u)u\,dx\geq\frac{N(\lambda_{1,0,0}-\lambda_{u})(q-2^{*})}{2^{*}(q-2)},

hence, since
q>2+\frac{4}{N}
, we conclude that

\lambda_{u}\geq\frac{2\lambda_{1,0,0}(q-2^{*})}{2^{*}(q-2-\frac{4}{N})}.

∎

It is the position to provide the proof of Theorem
5.3
.

.

6.2.

We also investigate the existence and multiplicity of normalized solutions to the following nonlinear Schrödinger equations with magnetic fields

We suppose that the nonlinearity

6.3.
Bi-harmonic equations

Another interesting class of problem that our approach can be applied is the bi-harmonic equation of the form

(6.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}u-\beta\Delta u=\lambda u+f(u),&\text{in}\,\,\Omega,\\
u=\Delta u=0\,\,(\mbox{or}\,\,u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\eta}=0),&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx=\mu,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 1
) is a smooth bounded domain and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Under suitable assumptions, this problem admits a solution for
\mu>0
small enough. For example, we may assume
f\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
and satisfies:

(f_{6})

There exists
p\in(2,2_{*})

\limsup_{|s|\to+\infty}\frac{|f(s)|}{|s|^{p-1}}<+\infty,

where
2_{*}=\frac{2N}{N-4}
if

and

if

(f_{7})

There exists

where
F(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,ds
.

It is well known that the space
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
(for the Dirichlet boundary condition
u=\Delta u=0

(for the Navier boundary condition

\lVert\Delta u\rVert^{2}_{2}=\int_{\Omega}\lvert\Delta u\rvert^{2}\,dx.

By
[
14
]
, we have

(6.4)

\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq C_{B}\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{2}\lVert u\rVert_{2}.

If
\beta\geq 0
, then all computations in the previous sections apply here with minor modifications.
Now, suppose that
\beta<0
. Applying Young’s inequality together with (
6.4
), we have

\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{2}^{2}-\beta\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{2}^{2}-\beta C_{B}\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{2}\lVert u\rVert_{2}\geq\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{2}^{2}-2\beta^{2}C_{B}^{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{2}.

Let
s:=1+2\beta^{2}C_{B}^{2}
, it follows that

\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{2}^{2}-\beta\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{2}^{2}+s\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{2}^{2}+\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{2}.

Then, with slight modifications, all the computation in the previous section can be adapted to the following equivalent formulation of problem (
6.3
).

(6.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}u-\beta\Delta u+su=\lambda u+f(u),&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=\Delta u=0\,\,(\mbox{or}\,\,u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\eta}=0),&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx=\mu.\end{array}\right.

More precisely, we can obtain the following existence and multiplicity of normalized solutions for
\mu>0
is small enough.

(or

such that, for all

(or

7.
appendix

In this appendix, we present a mountain pass type theorem for
C^{1}
functional defined on open subset, due to
[
15
,
22
]
that was used in Section
2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, and let
U
be a open subset of
X
satisfying
\partial U\neq\emptyset
. For
u\in X
and
R>0
, define
B_{R}(u):=\{v\in X:\lVert v-u\rVert_{X}<R\}
. Let
I\in C^{1}(U,\mathbb{R})
and assume that for each
u\in\partial U
, there exists
\varepsilon:=\varepsilon(u)>0
such that
I(v)\leq-1
for all
v\in U\cap B_{\varepsilon}(u)
.

Let
\beta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
be such that

\begin{cases}\beta\equiv-1&\text{ on }(-\infty,-1),\\
\beta(t)=t,&\forall t\geq 0,\\
\beta(t)\leq 0,&\forall t\leq 0.\end{cases}

Define a new functional
J:X\to\mathbb{R}
by

J(u)=\begin{cases}\beta(I(u))&\text{ if }u\in U,\\
-1&otherwise.\end{cases}

Lemma 7.1
.

J\in C^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})
and

J^{\prime}(u)=\begin{cases}\beta^{\prime}(I(u))I^{\prime}(u)&\text{ if }u\in U,\\
0&\text{ if }u\in U^{c}.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
J(u)>0
, then
u\in U
and
J^{\prime}(u)=\beta^{\prime}(I(u))I^{\prime}(u)=I^{\prime}(u)
.

Proof.

It is clear that
J\in C^{1}(U,\mathbb{R})\cup C^{1}(\bar{U}^{c},\mathbb{R})
with

J^{\prime}(u)=\begin{cases}\beta^{\prime}(I(u))I^{\prime}(u)&\text{ if }u\in U,\\
0&\text{ if }u\in\bar{U}^{c}.\end{cases}

and, if
J(u)>0
, then
u\in U
and
J^{\prime}(u)=\beta^{\prime}(I(u))I^{\prime}(u)=I^{\prime}(u)
.
Hence, to prove Lemma
7.1
, it suffices to show that for any
u\in\partial U
,
J^{\prime}(u)
exits in a sense of Fréchet derivative. Here, we are going to show that
J^{\prime}(u)=0
when
u\in\partial U
, from where it follows
J^{\prime}
is continuous in
X
. By the definition of
I
and
J
, we conclude that, for any
v\in U\cap B_{\varepsilon}(u)
, we have
J(v)=\beta(I(v))=-1=J(u)
and
J^{\prime}(v)=\beta^{\prime}(I(v))I^{\prime}(v)=0
. Moreover, since, for any
v\in U^{c}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(u)
,
J(v)=-1=J(u)
, we obtain
J^{\prime}(u)=0
. Since
u\in\partial U
is arbitrary, for any
v\in\partial U
, we conclude
J^{\prime}(v)=0
, and thus, for any
v\in U\cap B_{\varepsilon}(u)
, we have
J^{\prime}(v)=0
, which completes the proof of Lemma
7.1
.
∎

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
X
,
U
,
I
and
J
be defined as above and further assume that
0\in U
and
I(0)=0
. If

c:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(\gamma(t))>0,

where

\Gamma:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],U):\gamma(0)=0,\,I(\gamma(1))<0\}.

Then, there exists a Cerami sequence of
I
at level
c
, i.e., a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset U
satisfying

I(u_{n})\rightarrow c\text{ and }(1+\lVert u_{n}\rVert)\lVert I^{\prime}(u_{n})\rVert\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

Proof.

Define

c^{\prime}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}J(\gamma(t)),

and

\Gamma^{\prime}:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],X):\gamma(0)=0,\,J(\gamma(1))<0\}.

Firstly, we show that
c=c^{\prime}
. It follows from
J(u)=\beta(I(u))=u
for all
u\in U
with
I(u)>0
that, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have
\max_{t\in[0,1]}J(\gamma(t))=\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(\gamma(t))\geq c>0
. Since
\Gamma\subset\Gamma^{\prime}
, we conclude that
c\geq c^{\prime}
. On the other hand, by the definition of
c^{\prime}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{\prime}
such that
c^{\prime}\leq\max_{t\in[0,1]}J(\gamma(t))<c^{\prime}+\varepsilon
. It is clear that, if
\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}([0,1])\cap U^{c}=\emptyset
, then
\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\in\Gamma
, and thus,
c<c^{\prime}+\varepsilon
. As a result, we may assume that
\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}([0,1])\cap U^{c}\neq\emptyset
. Let

t_{0}:=\min\{t\in[0,1]:\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\in U^{c}\}.

Since
B_{\rho}(0)\subset U
for
\rho>0
small enough, we know that
t_{0}>0
. Then, by the definition of
t_{0}
, we know that, for all
t\in[0,t_{0})
,
\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\in U
. Thus,
\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t_{0})\in\partial U
. By the definition of
I
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for all
t\in(t_{0}-\delta,t_{0})
,
I(\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t))\leq-1<0
. Let
t_{1}\in(t_{0}-\delta,t_{0})
, and define

\displaystyle\gamma_{\varepsilon}:[0,1]

\displaystyle\to U

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\left(\frac{t}{t_{1}}\right).

Then,
\gamma_{\varepsilon}\in\Gamma
, and

c\leq\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(\gamma_{\varepsilon}(t))=\max_{t\in[0,1]}J(\gamma_{\varepsilon}(t))=\max_{t\in[0,t_{1}]}J(\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t))\leq\max_{t\in[0,1]}J(\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t))<c^{\prime}+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that
c=c^{\prime}
.

In a view of
[
46
, Theorem 2.9 and 4.2]
and
[
11
,
18
]
, since
c^{\prime}=c>0
, we obtain a Cerami sequence of
J
at level
c
, i.e., a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset X
satisfying

J(u_{n})\rightarrow c\text{ and }(1+\lVert u_{n}\rVert)\lVert J^{\prime}(u_{n})\rVert\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

By Lemma
7.1
, we conclude that
u_{n}\in U
for
n
sufficiently large, and, up to a subsequence, we have

I(u_{n})\rightarrow c\text{ and }(1+\lVert u_{n}\rVert)\lVert I^{\prime}(u_{n})\rVert\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

∎

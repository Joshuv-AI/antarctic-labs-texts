Exponential mixing of frame flows for three dimensional manifolds of quarter-pinched negative curvature
Mark Pollicott and Daofei Zhang

Abstract
. For a compact three-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold of strictly 1 4 \frac{1}{4} -pinched negative sectional curvature, we establish exponential mixing of the frame flow with respect to the normalized volume. More generally this result extends to a class of torus extensions of Anosov flows, subject to assumptions on the Brin transitivity group and the smoothness of the stable subbundle. Our approach is based on a simplified dynamical model for studying the extension flow, constructed via a Young tower of the underlying Anosov flow. Exponential mixing is then obtained through a strengthened Dolgopyat type estimate on the corresponding transfer operators.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 37A25, 37C30; Secondary: 37D30, 37D40.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statement of main results

Geodesic flows for compact negatively curved manifolds are one of the most important examples of ergodic systems. They have been extensively studied using techniques from the theory of Anosov flows for over sixty years
[
2
]
, but the study of the dynamical properties of geodesic flows can be traced back even further to work of Hadamard in 1898
[
18
]
. Within this setting, exponential mixing (or exponential decay of
correlations) has particular importance. Roughly speaking, a smooth
flow
\phi_{t}
on a compact manifold is said to be exponentially mixing with respect to an invariant probability measure
\mu
if for any Hölder continuous functions
E
and
F
, the following correlation function

\rho_{E,F}(t):=\int E\circ\phi_{t}.Fd\mu-\int Ed\mu\int Fd\mu

decays exponentially to zero as
t\to+\infty
. This dynamical property provides both a precise measure of the efficiency of the mixing of the system and an important foundation for establishing other quantitative statistical properties. Exponential mixing of geodesic flows with respect to the Liouville measure
\mu
for compact surfaces follows from the ground breaking work of Dolgopyat in
[
14
]
(cf. also the work of Chernov in
[
13
]
). Subsequently, the result was generalized to higher dimensions in
[
21
]
by showing that contact Anosov flows are exponentially mixing. In particular, this applied to all geodesic flows on compact manifolds of negative curvature.

Frame flows for compact negatively curved
n
-manifolds (for
n\geq 3
) represent another important class of flows in ergodic theory naturally related to geodesic flows. The frame flow acts on orthonormal frames by parallel translating every frame along the geodesic, and thus forming a
SO
(n-1)
-extension of the geodesic flow
[
8
]
. These flows are no longer Anosov and provide one of the simplest and most important examples of partially hyperbolic systems. The natural invariant probability measure for the frame flow is the normalized volume which is the local product of the Liouville measure and the Haar measure on SO
(n-1)
. As a statistical property, exponential mixing of frame flows is not only important in its own right but can also be used to help solve problems in other fields, such as the surface subgroup conjecture of Waldhausen proved in
[
19
]
. Unlike the now well established exponential mixing for all geodesic flows of negatively curved manifolds, the study of the behaviour of frame flows presents
substantial additional difficulties. Notably, some examples of frame flows of compact negatively curved manifolds may fail to be mixing or even ergodic
[
8
]
. While exponential mixing is known for frame flows in constant negative curvature
[
22
]
, the variable curvature case remains extremely challenging. Under certain restrictive conditions, weaker rapid mixing (i.e. superpolynomial mixing) has been established in
[
12
,
28
,
33
]
.

The aim of this paper is to establish exponential mixing in the concrete case
of frame flows for three-dimensional manifolds of variable negative sectional curvature. As usual, a compact Riemannian manifold
M
is said to be
a
-pinched if there exists
C>0
such that the sectional curvatures of
M
lie in the interval
[-C,-aC]
. Our main result can be formulated as follows.

Theorem 1
.

For a compact smooth three-dimensional Riemannian manifold of strictly
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched negative curvature, the frame flow is exponentially mixing with respect to the normalized volume.

Without the above pinching condition, it had been previously established by Brin
[
7
]
(see also
[
9
]
) that such flows were ergodic and mixing, and the present authors in
[
28
]
recently showed that generically such flows had the intermediate property of superpolynomial decay of correlations with respect to the local-product measures of Gibbs measures and the Haar measure on SO
(2)
. Independently, M. Cekić and T. Lefeuvre obtained the same result of superpolynomial decay of correlations for all such flows but it only holds true for the normalized volume
[
12
]
.

Since frame flows for compact three-dimensional manifolds are
\mathbb{T}^{1}
-extensions of the geodesic flows, it is also natural to consider extensions of Theorem
1
to more general
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extensions of Anosov flows. Fix an integer
d\geq 1
. Let
M
be a compact smooth Riemannian manifold manifold, and let
g_{t}:M\to M
be a
C^{2}
jointly non-integrable Anosov flow. Let
\mu_{SRB}
be the unique SRB measure of the flow
g_{t}
. Let
\mathbb{T}^{d}
be the
d
-torus, and let
\widehat{M}
be a smooth
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-bundle over
M
. We denote the bundle projection by
\varrho:\widehat{M}\to M
, and then for all
x\in M
the fibre
\varrho^{-1}(x)
is isometric to
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. The dynamical system of interest is a
C^{2}

\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extension, denoted as
f_{t}:\widehat{M}\to\widehat{M}
, of
g_{t}:M\to M
. The extension flow
f_{t}
preserves the probability measure
\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}
which is the local product of
\mu_{SRB}
and the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Our more general result is as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f_{t}
be a
C^{2}

\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extension of a jointly non-integrable Anosov flow
g_{t}
. If the stable subbundle of
g_{t}
is of class
C^{1+\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
and the Brin transitivity group of
f_{t}
is not contained in any
\mathbb{T}^{d-1}
subgroup, then
f_{t}
is exponentially mixing with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}
: for any
\beta\in(0,1)
there exist
c>0
and
C>0
such that

\bigg|\int_{\widehat{M}}E\circ f_{t}.Fd\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}-\int_{\widehat{M}}Ed\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}\int_{\widehat{M}}Fd\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}\bigg|\leq C||E||_{C^{\beta}}||F||_{C^{\beta}}e^{-ct},

for all
t>0
and
E,F\in C^{\beta}(\widehat{M})
.

The above result immediately implies Theorem
1
. Recall that geodesic flows of negatively curved manifolds are jointly non-integrable Anosov flows
[
14
]
. Under the strictly
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched curvature condition, it is well-known that the horospheric foliations are
C^{1+\alpha}
regular for some
\alpha>0
. Consequently, both stable and unstable subbundles maintain
C^{1+\alpha}
regularity. In the three-dimensional case, the frame flow is a
\mathbb{T}^{1}
-extension of the geodesic flow. Applying Theorem
2
, we need only verify that the Brin transitivity group is not contained in any
\mathbb{T}^{0}
subgroup (equivalently, that it is nontrivial). This condition holds automatically, as Brin’s fundamental work
[
6
,
8
]
established that the transitivity group is in fact dense in
\mathbb{T}^{1}
.

Exponential mixing of isometric extension of Anosov flows has been considered by other authors. In particular, Siddiqi
[
31
]
has a related statement. However, there is a step in his argument which appears to be incomplete. More precisely, he uses Markov Partitions and attempts to apply the so-called Dolgopyat argument. Unfortunately, to do this it has come to light that some additional technical properties of the Markov Partition are implicitly needed which are not known to hold in higher dimensions (see
[
4
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
23
]
). Since we bypass the use of Markov Partitions we are able to give a different and complete proof of Theorems
1
and
2
.

1.2.
Outline of the paper

Let us explain the difficulties of the problem and highlight the novelty of our proof. The exponential mixing of frame flows in constant negative curvature was established in the works of
[
22
,
27
]
. In their proofs, representation group theory plays a crucial role, but this approach cannot be adapted to variable negative curvature cases or general Anosov flows. The above result was reproved recently in dimension 3 by Guillarmou–Küster
[
17
]
using semiclassical analysis, but the mechanism relies heavily on the symplectic structure of the manifold which fails in variable negative curvature.
Unlike the aforementioned approaches, which are ineffective in variable negative curvature, we develop a more powerful and more broadly applicable method, at least for abelian extensions. Our argument builds on several key techniques: the uniform non-integrability used by Dolgopyat to prove exponential mixing of geodesic flows in
[
14
]
, the non-coboundary technique employed for the exponential mixing of the Teichmüller flow in
[
4
]
, the infinitely many
C^{1+\alpha}
inverse branches technique applied to the Lorenz attractor in
[
3
]
, the Young towers of Anosov flows in
[
23
]
, and our recent work on rapid mixing for frame flows in
[
28
]
. By synthesizing these techniques with some additional new ideas, we prove Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
.

While our previous work
[
28
]
relied on Markov partitions, the tower construction employed in this paper helps us to avoid many of the complications that would otherwise arise from Markov partitions (and in particular their boundaries) for Anosov flows on manifolds of dimension greater than or equal to four, as discussed briefly in
[
23
]
and in our Section
3.1
. However, as a consequence of using this technique
we are restricted to considering the case that
\mu
is the normalized volume, which is the most natural case.

The
\frac{1}{4}
-pinching hypothesis is important to give the
C^{1+\alpha}
regularity to the stable manifolds used in the Dolgopyat method. The restriction to toral extensions in Theorem
2

(and thus three dimensional manifolds in Theorem
1
) makes the analysis of the spectrum of the underlying transfer operators viable.

We conclude this section by outlining the structure of the paper.
Section
2
provides the necessary preliminaries, including the definitions and properties of Anosov flows, their
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extensions, and the Brin transitivity group. Section
3
introduces the dynamical models used in later analysis. These consist of suspensions of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extensions over expanding maps, and suspensions of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extensions over hyperbolic skew products. Section
4
establishes a connection between the extension flow and a suspension flow of a
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extension over a hyperbolic skew product (as detailed in Subsection
4.2
). We then further reduce this to the corresponding suspension semiflow of the
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extension over expanding maps (treated in Subsection
4.4
). Section
5
presents the proof of exponential mixing for the suspension semiflow. Finally, in Section
6
we make some comments and questions.

Notation.
Throughout this paper, we denote
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. If
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we use
xy
to denote their inner product, i.e.,
xy:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}y_{i}
, and we use
|x|
to denote its supremum norm, i.e.,
|x|:=\sup_{1\leq i\leq d}|x_{i}|
. We will also use notation
\partial
to represent various derivatives.

2.
Preliminaries

This section establishes some of the foundational concepts. In Subsection
2.1
, we recall Anosov flows and their joint non-integrability properties. Subsection
2.2
then introduces
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extensions of Anosov flows and the Brin transitivity group.

2.1.
Anosov flows

We begin with the formal definition and key properties of Anosov flows. Standard references include
[
16
]
and
[
24
]
. Let
M
be a smooth compact Riemannian manifold with tangent bundle
TM
, and let
g_{t}:M\to M
be a
C^{1}
flow.

Definition 2.1.1
.

We say
g_{t}:M\to M
is an
Anosov flow
if there is a
\partial g_{t}
-invariant splitting
TM=E^{s}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{u}
over
M
, and exist constant
C>0
and
0<\lambda<1
such that
E^{c}
is the
one dimensional bundle tangent to
g_{t}
, and
||\partial g_{t}|_{E^{s}}||,\ ||\partial g_{-t}|_{E^{u}}||\leq C\lambda^{t}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Usually, we assume that
g_{t}
is
topologically mixing
which means for any two non-empty open sets
U,V
of
M
and large enough
t>0
we have
U\cap g_{t}V\not=\emptyset
. It is then well-known that for any Hölder continuous real-valued function
\Phi
on
M
, there exists a unique equilibrium state
\mu_{\Phi}
on
M

(maximizing
h(\mu)+\int\Phi d\mu
where
\mu
is an invariant probability measure)
which is called the
Gibbs measure
of
\Phi

[
5
]
. In this paper, we always consider
C^{2}
topologically mixing Anosov flows. In this case, we could consider the geometric potential
\Phi:=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}\log|\det\partial g_{t}|_{E^{u}}|
which is Hölder continuous since
g_{t}
is
C^{2}
. The associated equilibrium state of the geometric potential is called the
Sinai-Ruelle-Bowen
(SRB) measure, denoted by
\mu_{SRB}
. This measure is the most relevant from the physical point of view.

For two test functions
E,F\in C^{0}(M)
, their correlation function with respect to
\mu_{SRB}
is defined by

\rho_{E,F}(t):=\int_{M}E\circ g_{t}.Fd\mu_{SRB}-\int_{M}Ed\mu_{SRB}\int_{M}Fd\mu_{SRB}.

It is well-known that
g_{t}
is always mixing with respect to
\mu_{SRB}
, namely
\rho_{E,F}(t)\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
for any
E,F\in C^{0}(M)

[
5
]
. A central question in the theory of dynamical systems is to characterize the rate of mixing of
g_{t}
with respect to
\mu_{SRB}
, which is also called
the decay of correlations
.

Definition 2.1.2
.

We say
g_{t}
is
exponentially mixing
with respect to
\mu_{SRB}
if for any
\beta\in(0,1)
there exist
C>0
and
0<\lambda<1
such that for any
t>0
and any
E,F\in C^{\beta}(M)
, we have
|\rho_{E,F}(t)|\leq C||E||_{C^{\beta}}||F||_{C^{\beta}}\lambda^{t}
.

The subbundles
E^{s}
and
E^{u}
are called the
stable and unstable subbundles
, respectively. These subbundles are always integrable, and their corresponding integral manifolds form the
stable and unstable foliations
, denoted by
W^{s}
and
W^{u}
. These foliations are
g_{t}
-invariant and can be characterized dynamically as follows:

W^{s}(x)=\{y\in M:d(g_{t}(y),g_{t}(x))\to 0\ as\ t\to\infty\},

and

W^{u}(x)=\{y\in M:d(g_{-t}(y),g_{-t}(x))\to 0\ as\ t\to\infty\}.

For practical applications, it is often useful to consider local versions of these manifolds. For sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, we define:

W^{s}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{y\in W^{s}(x):\sup_{t\geq 0}d(g_{t}(y),g_{t}(x))\leq\varepsilon\},

and

W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{y\in W^{u}(x):\sup_{t\geq 0}d(g_{-t}(y),g_{-t}(x))\leq\varepsilon\}.

When the specific size
\varepsilon
is not crucial, we simply denote these local manifolds by
W^{s}_{loc}(x)
and
W^{u}_{loc}(x)
. Under the flow action, points in these local manifolds exhibit exponential convergence:
W^{s}_{loc}(x)
contracts exponentially under
g_{t}
as
t\to+\infty
, while
W^{u}_{loc}(x)
contracts exponentially under
g_{-t}
as
t\to+\infty
.

We will use the following concept of u-s paths to define the Brin transitivity group in the next subsection.

Definition 2.1.3
.

A
u-s path
of
g_{t}
is a set of finitely many points
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p}\subset M
so that
x_{i+1}\in W_{loc}^{s}(x_{i})
or
W_{loc}^{u}(x_{i})
for each
1\leq i\leq p-1
. A
closed chain

W
at
x
is a u-s path
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p}
so that
x_{1}=x_{p}=x
.

It is worth mentioning that we can also use u-s paths to describe the non-integrability of an Anosov flow. In general, the codimension one subbundle
E^{s}\oplus E^{u}
is not integrable, and we say that
g_{t}
is
jointly non-integrable
if this is the case. It is convenient to characterize joint non-integrability using u-s paths of
g_{t}
as shown in
[
16
,
25
]
. For a point
x\in M
, consider the collection
\mathcal{W}_{\delta}(x)
of all u-s paths
W=\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{4}
with
x_{0}=x
,

x_{1}\in W^{u}_{\delta}(x_{0}),\quad x_{2}\in W^{s}_{\delta}(x_{1}),\quad x_{3}\in W^{u}_{\delta}(x_{2}),\quad x_{4}\in W^{s}_{\delta}(x_{3})\quad\text{and}\quad x_{4}\in g_{t_{W}}(x_{0})

for some
t_{W}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,
g_{t}
is jointly non-integrable if and only if there exist
x\in M
and small
\delta>0
such that
t_{W}\not=0
for any u-s path
W\in\mathcal{W}_{\delta}(x)
. Joint non-integrability always implies topological mixing, and they are equivalent if
g_{t}
is codimension one
[
25
, Theorem 3.7]
.

2.2.
Torus extensions

Having introduced the basic properties of Anosov flows, we now turn to the central focus of this paper,
that of their
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extensions. While these extensions lose the Anosov property, they constitute one of the most fundamental classes of partially hyperbolic flows.

Let
M
be a compact manifold equipped with an Anosov flow
g_{t}:M\to M
. Consider a smooth
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-bundle
\widehat{M}
over
M
with projection map
\varrho:\widehat{M}\to M
. We study a
C^{2}
flow
f_{t}
on
\widehat{M}
. The
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-action on
\widehat{M}
is denoted by
R_{\theta}:\widehat{M}\to\widehat{M}
for
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, representing
a rotation by
\theta
in the fiber direction.

Definition 2.2.1
.

We say
f_{t}:\widehat{M}\to\widehat{M}
is a
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extension of
g_{t}
if

(1)

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\varrho\circ f_{t}=g_{t}\circ\varrho
;

(2)

for any
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
f_{t}\circ R_{\theta}=R_{\theta}\circ f_{t}
.

The SRB measure
\mu_{SRB}
on
M
canonically lifts to a probability measure
\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}
on
\widehat{M}
defined as the local product of
\mu_{SRB}
with the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Formally, we express this as:

\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}=\int_{M}\text{Leb}_{x}d\mu_{SRB}(x),

where
\text{Leb}_{x}
is the push-forward of the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
to the fibre
\varrho^{-1}(x)
. This measure
\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}
is immediately seen to be
f_{t}
-invariant due to the fiberwise isometric nature of the flow. Our primary concern is the mixing rate of
f_{t}
with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}
. By analogy with the case of Anosov flows, for
E,F\in C^{0}(\widehat{M})
, their correlation function with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}
is defined as

\rho_{E,F}(t):=\int_{\widehat{M}}E\circ f_{t}.Fd\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}-\int_{\widehat{M}}Ed\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}\int_{\widehat{M}}Fd\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}

for
t>0
. Then the definition
of
f_{t}
being
exponentially mixing
with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}
is
analogous to that in Definition
2.1.2
.

It is known
[
8
]
that
f_{t}
possesses two invariant foliations called the
stable foliation
and
unstable foliation
of
f_{t}
, denoted by
W^{s}_{f_{t}}
and
W^{u}_{f_{t}}
respectively,
with leaves

\displaystyle W^{s}_{f_{t}}(\widehat{x})

\displaystyle=\left\{\widehat{y}\in\widehat{M}\hbox{ : }d\left(f_{t}\widehat{y},f_{t}\widehat{x}\right)\to 0\hbox{ as }t\to+\infty\right\}\hbox{ and }

\displaystyle W^{u}_{f_{t}}(\widehat{x})

\displaystyle=\left\{\widehat{y}\in\widehat{M}\hbox{ : }d\left(f_{-t}\widehat{y},f_{-t}\widehat{x}\right)\to 0\hbox{ as }t\to+\infty\right\}

for
\widehat{x}\in\widehat{M}
.
These project under
\rho
precisely to the stable foliation
W^{s}
and unstable foliation
W^{u}
of the underlying flow
g_{t}
. Both foliations remain invariant under
f_{t}
and demonstrate exponential convergence under the flow’s action. For any point
\widehat{x}\in\varrho^{-1}(x)
, the following relationships hold:

\varrho(W^{j}_{f_{t}}(\widehat{x}))=W^{j}(x)
and
W^{j}_{f_{t}}(R_{\theta}(\widehat{x}))=R_{\theta}W^{j}_{f_{t}}(\widehat{x})
for
j=s,u
. This shows that
W^{j}_{f_{t}}(\widehat{x})
effectively represents a graph mapping from
W^{j}(x)
to its fiber. When considering local trivializations of fibers
\varrho^{-1}(x)
, we recall these are isometries mapping
\varrho^{-1}(U)
to
U\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
for some neighborhood
U
of
x
. Using such trivializations, for convenience we may naturally identify
\varrho^{-1}(U)

with
U\times\mathbb{T}^{d}

for small
U\subset M
.

Definition 2.2.2
.

For
x,y\in U
with
y\in W^{s}(x)
, let
\varphi_{x}
and
\varphi_{y}
be the trivializations of
\varrho^{-1}(x)
and
\varrho^{-1}(y)
respectively. Consider the point
(x,0)
in the fibre
\varrho^{-1}(x)
. Then the point in the graph
W^{s}_{f_{t}}(x,0)
that passes through the fibre
\varrho^{-1}(y)
is equal to
(y,\mathcal{T}^{s}(x,y))
, say. We call
\mathcal{T}^{s}(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}=\rho^{-1}(y)
the
stable twist
of
x
and
y
.
The
unstable twist

\mathcal{T}^{u}(x,y)
of
y\in W^{u}(x)
can be defined in an analogous manner.

Obviously
\mathcal{T}^{s}(x,y)
and
\mathcal{T}^{u}(x,y)
depend on the trivializations at
x
and
y
. Since
W^{s}_{f_{t}}(R_{\theta}(\widehat{x}))=R_{\theta}W^{s}_{f_{t}}(\widehat{x})
, for any point
(x,\theta)
in
\varrho^{-1}(x)
and
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
, the point in the graph
W^{s}_{f_{t}}(x,\theta)
that passes through the fibre
\varrho^{-1}(y)
is equal to
(y,\mathcal{T}^{s}(x,y)+\theta)
in these coordinates. We will express the stable twist
\mathcal{T}^{s}
and unstable twist
\mathcal{T}^{u}
precisely in the proof of Lemma
4.3.2
using the dynamic model constructed in Subsection
4.2
.

Given a closed chain
W=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p}
of
g_{t}
at
x\in\Lambda
and trivializations
\varphi_{x_{i}}
at
x_{i}
, the twist induced by
W
is defined as
\theta_{W}:=\sum_{i=2}^{p}\mathcal{T}^{j_{x_{i-1},x_{i}}}(x_{i-1},x_{i})
where
j_{x,y}=s
if
y\in W^{s}(x)
and
j_{x,y}=u
if
y\in W^{u}(x)
. Although
\mathcal{T}^{s}
and
\mathcal{T}^{u}
depend on the trivializations, this is not the case for
\theta_{W}
.

Lemma 2.2.3
.

For any closed chain
W=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p}
of
g_{t}
at
x
, we have
\theta_{W}
is independent of the choice of the trivialization
\varphi_{x_{i}}
at
x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq p-1
.

Proof.

This result was essentially established in
[
28
, Lemma 4.3.2]
.
∎

It is straightforward to verify that all twists
\theta_{W}
induced by closed chains
W
at
x
form a subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. In Brin’s fundamental work (e.g.,
[
6
,
8
]
), this subgroup plays an important
role in establishing ergodicity for frame flows and more general partially hyperbolic systems. Therefore, it has become standard to call this the Brin transitivity group.

Definition 2.2.4
.

For a point
x\in M
, the
Brin transitivity group

H(x)\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
is defined as the collection of all twists
\{\theta_{W}\}
arising from closed chains
W
at
x
.

The following lemma establishes that
H(x)
is actually independent of the base point
x\in M
.

Lemma 2.2.5
.

For any
x,x^{\prime}\in M
, we have
H(x)=H(x^{\prime})

\sum_{y\in\mathcal{H}}e^{g\circ y(x)}e^{|a||r\circ y|_{\infty}}\leq C_{2}
for all
|a|\leq\delta_{1}
and all
x\in X
;

(4)

\int_{X}e^{\delta_{1}r}d\mu_{g}\leq C_{2}
.

Proof.

The first part follows from the fact that
R
is constant on each
X_{i}
and thus
|\partial(r\circ y)|_{\infty}=|\partial(r_{0}\circ y)|_{\infty}\leq|\partial r_{0}|_{\infty}|\partial y|_{\infty}\leq C_{1}
for some uniform constant
C_{1}>0
.

The second part follows from the exponential tail estimate of
R
which we stated below: it is proved in
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
that there exists
\lambda_{1}\in(0,1)
such that Leb
(R\geq n)\leq C_{1}\lambda_{1}^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Therefore, we
can bound
Leb
(r\geq t)\leq\text{Leb}(R\geq t-r_{0})\leq\text{Leb}(R\geq\lfloor t\rfloor-1)\leq C_{1}\lambda_{1}^{\lfloor t\rfloor-1}\leq C_{1}\lambda_{1}^{t-2}
. Finally, note that by Lemma
3.1.2
the density
h_{g}=d\mu_{g}/dx
is bounded and thus we deduce that
\mu_{g}(r\geq t)\leq C_{1}|h_{g}|_{\infty}\lambda_{1}^{t-2}\leq C_{2}\lambda_{1}^{t}
for all
t>0
, where
C_{2}\geq C_{1}|h_{g}|_{\infty}\lambda_{1}^{-2}
.

To prove the third part, using the exponential tail estimate in the proof of the second part we can show that
\int_{X}e^{aR}dx\leq C_{1}\lambda_{1}^{-1/2}(1-\lambda_{1}^{1/2})^{-1}
for all
|a|\leq\delta_{1}
with
\delta_{1}\leq-\log\lambda_{1}/2
. Note that
R
is constant on each
y(X)
, thus

\sum_{y\in\mathcal{H}}\text{Leb}(y(X))e^{aR_{y}}=\int_{X}e^{aR}dx\leq C_{1}\lambda_{1}^{-1/2}(1-\lambda_{1}^{1/2})^{-1},

where
R_{y}:=R|_{y(X)}\in\mathbb{R}
. Recalling
g=-\log|\det\partial T|
, and so
\text{Leb}(y(X))=e^{g\circ y(x_{y})}
for some
x_{y}\in y(X)
. Since
e^{g\circ y(x_{y})-g\circ y(x)}\leq e^{|g\circ y|_{C^{\alpha}}d(x,x_{y})^{\alpha}}\leq e^{C_{1}}
for all
x\in X
, we obtain that

\sum_{y\in\mathcal{H}}e^{g\circ y(x)}e^{aR_{y}}\leq e^{C_{1}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{H}}e^{g\circ y(x_{y})}e^{aR_{y}}=e^{C_{1}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{H}}\text{Leb}(y(X))e^{aR_{y}}\leq C_{2}/2,

for all
x\in X
provided
C_{2}\geq 2e^{C_{1}}C_{1}\lambda_{1}^{-1/2}(1-\lambda_{1}^{1/2})^{-1}
. Now, since
r=R+r_{0}
with
r_{0}\in(-\delta_{0},\delta_{0})\subset(-1,1)
, we deduce that
\sum_{y\in\mathcal{H}}e^{g\circ y(x)}e^{|a||r\circ y|_{\infty}}\leq C_{2}e^{\delta_{1}}/2\leq C_{2}
where
e^{\delta_{1}}\leq 2
.

The last part follows from the second part. Indeed, we can write

\int_{X}e^{\delta_{1}r}d\mu_{g}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{n-1\leq r\leq n}e^{\delta_{1}r}d\mu_{g}\leq C_{1}|h_{g}|_{\infty}\lambda_{1}^{-3}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{(\delta_{1}+\log\lambda_{1})n}\leq C_{2},

provided
C_{2}\geq e^{C_{1}}C_{1}\lambda_{1}^{-5/2}(1-\lambda_{1}^{1/2})^{-1}
.
∎

By construction, the return time on the section
\sum
is given by

which is constant on each stable leaf

of

We have

for all

and all

.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
4.1.1
and the equation (

Let

.

.

Lemma 4.2.4
.

We have
\widehat{\Pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\emph{Leb})=\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}

Proof.

We need to demonstrate the equality:

\int_{\widehat{M}}Fd\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}=\int_{\widehat{M}}Fd\widehat{\Pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb}),\quad\text{for any }F\in C^{0}(\widehat{M}).

Leveraging Lemma
4.1.2
, we start by expressing the left-hand side of the equation,

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{M}}Fd\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\int_{\varrho^{-1}(w)}F(w,y)d\text{Leb}_{w}(y)d\mu_{SRB}(w)

(4.2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S^{r}}\int_{\varrho^{-1}(\Pi(x,z,u))}F(\Pi(x,z,u),y)d\text{Leb}_{\Pi(x,z,u)}(y)d(\mu_{S}\times\text{Leb})(x,z,u)

where Leb

represent the push-forward of
d\theta
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
to the fibre
\varrho^{-1}(w)

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{M}}Fd\widehat{\Pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb})=

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{S}^{r}}F\circ\widehat{\Pi}d(\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb})

(4.2.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{r(x)}F(\widehat{\Pi}(x,z,\theta,u))dud\theta d\mu_{S}(x,z)

4.3.
The Brin transitivity group and linear independence

In the last subsection, we have seen that the extension flow
f_{t}:\widehat{M}\to\widehat{M}
is semi-conjugated to the suspension flow
\widehat{P}_{t}:\widehat{S}^{r}\to\widehat{S}^{r}
. Thus, we can deduce exponentially mixing of
f_{t}
from that of
\widehat{P}_{t}
. To show
\widehat{P}_{t}
is exponentially mixing with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times
Leb, we require the roof function
r
and the skewing function
\Theta
to satisfy a nonlinear condition which we called linear independence (Definition
4.3.1
). In this subsection, we show that this independence of
r
and
\Theta
can be confirmed via the Brin transitivity group assumption in Theorem
2
.

We recall that the roof function
r:S\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is constant on stable leaves where
S=X\times Z
, and thus can be
reduced
to a function on
X
. Since the stable subbundle of
g_{t}
is of class
C^{1}
, we further obtain that
r
is
C^{1}
on each connected component
X_{i}
of
X
. The same conclusion holds for the skewing function
\Theta
as well, and we deduce that
\Theta:X\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and is
C^{1}
on each connected component
X_{i}
.

Definition 4.3.1
.

We say
r
and
\Theta
are
linearly independent
if for any
l\in\mathbb{R}
and any
L\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
(l,L)\not=(0,0)

there is no
h\in C^{1}(X)
such that
lr+L\Theta-h\circ T+h
is constant on each connected component
X_{i}
.

It is not difficult to to see that if this technical property fails then there is little prospect of exponential mixing. Fortunately, there is a more straightforward and geometric hypothesis which helps to establish this property.

Lemma 4.3.2
.

If the Brin transitivity group
H
of
f_{t}
is not contained in any
\mathbb{T}^{d-1}
subgroup, then
r
and
\Theta
are linearly independent.

Proof.

For
w,w^{\prime}\in M
, assume
w
has coordinate
(x,z,u)
and
w^{\prime}
has coordinate
(x^{\prime},z^{\prime},u^{\prime})
in the system
\widehat{X}^{r}
. If
w^{\prime}\in W^{s}(w)
, then we can write
u^{\prime}=u+\Delta^{s}(x,z,x^{\prime},z^{\prime})
for some
\Delta^{s}
which depends on
(x,z,x^{\prime},z^{\prime})
. By construction, and since
r
is constant on stable leaves, we must have
x^{\prime}=x
and
\Delta^{s}(x,z,x^{\prime},z^{\prime})=0
which means
u^{\prime}=u
.

We now turn to the analysis of the case of unstable manifolds. Assume
w^{\prime}\in W^{u}(w)
, then we can write
u^{\prime}=u+\Delta^{u}(x,z,x^{\prime},z^{\prime})
for some
\Delta^{u}
which depends on
(x,z,x^{\prime},z^{\prime})
. We firstly study the backward iterations of the point
(x,z,0)
. Since
\{P(X_{i}\times Z)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
forms a partition of
S=X\times Z
, there exists an inverse branch
y_{1}
of
T
and a point
z_{1}\in Z
such that
P(y_{1}(x),z_{1},0)=(x,z,0)
. Inductively, we can find a sequence of inverse branches of
T
which denote by
\{y_{n}\in\mathcal{H}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and a sequence of points
\{z_{n}\in Z\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
P(y_{n}(x),z_{n},0)=(x_{n-1},z_{n-1},0)
. Since
w
has coordinate
(x,z,u)
and
w^{\prime}
has coordinate
(x^{\prime},z^{\prime},u^{\prime})
, we know that
\Pi((x^{\prime},z^{\prime},u^{\prime}-u))\in W^{u}(\Pi(x,z,0))
. Hence, the backward iterations of the point
(x^{\prime},z^{\prime},0)
have the form
\{(y_{n}(x^{\prime}),z_{n}^{\prime},0)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
for a sequence of points
\{z_{n}^{\prime}\in Z\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Since
g_{t}
and
P_{t}
are semi-conjugated, we deduce that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}d((x,z,u-t),(x^{\prime},z^{\prime},u^{\prime}-t))=0.

(4.3.1)

Now, by the above analysis of back-iterations of points
(x,z,0)
and
(x^{\prime},z^{\prime},0)
, we have

(x,z,u-t)=(y^{(n)}(x),z_{n},u-t+r_{n}(y^{(n)}(x)))

(4.3.2)

and

(x^{\prime},z^{\prime},u^{\prime}-t)=(y^{(n)}(x^{\prime}),z_{n}^{\prime},u^{\prime}-t+r_{n}(y^{(n)}(x^{\prime}))),

(4.3.3)

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
where
y^{(n)}:=y_{n}\circ\cdots\circ y_{1}
.
Substituting (
4.3.2
) and (
4.3.3
) into (
4.3.1
) gives us

u^{\prime}=u+\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}(y^{(n)}(x))-r_{n}(y^{(n)}(x^{\prime}))=u+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r\circ y^{(n)}(x)-r\circ y^{(n)}(x^{\prime}),

where the sum is well-defined due to the contraction of
y^{(n)}
. Hence,
\Delta^{u}(x,z,x^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r\circ y^{(n)}(x)-r\circ y^{(n)}(x^{\prime})
.

Now, to show
r
and
\Theta
are linearly independent, by definition, it suffices to prove the following two conclusions:

(1)

there is no
h\in C^{1}(X)
such that
r-h\circ T+h
is constant on each connected component
X_{i}
;

(2)

for any
0\not=L\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
l\in\mathbb{R}
, there is no
h\in C^{1}(X)
such that
lr+L\Theta-h\circ T+h
is constant on each connected component
X_{i}
.

The conclusion (1) is well-known due to
g_{t}

being
jointly non-integrable and is proved in
[
23
]
(see also
[
4
]
). We now prove (2). For any closed chain
W=(w_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
at
w
with
w_{1}=w_{n}=w
, assume they have local coordinates
(x_{i},z_{i},u_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
. Then, we have
u_{i+1}=u_{i}+\Delta^{\delta_{i}}(x_{i},z_{i},x_{i+1},z_{i+1})
and thus
u_{n}=u_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\Delta^{\delta_{i}}(x_{i},z_{i},x_{i+1},z_{i+1})
, where
\delta_{i}=s
if
w_{i+1}\in W^{s}(w_{i})
and
\delta_{i}=u
if
w_{i+1}\in W^{u}(w_{i})
. Note that
u_{n}=u_{1}
since
w_{n}=w_{1}
, and therefore

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\Delta^{\delta_{i}}(x_{i},z_{i},x_{i+1},z_{i+1})=0.

(4.3.4)

Recalling the twist of
W
induced by
f_{t}
is given by
\theta_{W}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{T}^{\delta_{i}}(w_{i},w_{i+1})
. The Brin transitivity group is given by
H=\{\theta_{W}:W\text{ is a closed chain}\}
. We can similarly express
\mathcal{T}^{\delta_{i}}(w_{i},w_{i+1})
in term of
features of
the system
\widehat{T}_{t}:\widehat{X}^{r}\to\widehat{X}^{r}
. Firstly, since
\Theta
is constant on stable leaves, we deduce that
\mathcal{T}^{s}(w_{i},w_{i+1})=0
if
w_{i+1}\in W^{s}(w_{i})
. Secondly, using a similar argument
to
(
4.3.1
) and (
4.3.2
), if
w_{i+1}\in W^{u}(w_{i})
, we can deduce that
\mathcal{T}^{u}(w_{i},w_{i+1})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Theta\circ y^{(n)}(x_{i})-\Theta\circ y^{(n)}(x_{i+1})
.

We now prove the result by contradiction. Assume for some
0\not=L\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
l\in\mathbb{R}
, there is
h\in C^{1}(X)
such that
lr+L\Theta-h\circ T+h
is constant on each connected component
X_{i}
. Then, by the expressions of
\Delta^{\delta_{i}}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\delta_{i}}
, we have
l\Delta^{\delta_{i}}(x_{i},z_{i},x_{i+1},z_{i+1})+L\mathcal{T}^{\delta_{i}}(w_{i},w_{i+1})=h(x_{i})-h(x_{i+1})
. Thus, summing over
i
, we obtain that

l\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\Delta^{\delta_{i}}(x_{i},z_{i},x_{i+1},z_{i+1})+L\theta_{W}=0.

Substituting (
4.3.4
) into the above equality, we have
L\theta_{W}=0
for all closed chains
W
. Since
L\not=0
, we deduce that the Brin transitivity group
H
is contained in some
\mathbb{T}^{d-1}
subgroup which contradicts the assumption of Theorem
2
and thus completes the proof.
∎

4.4.
Integration along stable leaves

As established in Subsection
4.2
, the systems
(\widehat{P}_{t},\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb})
and
(f_{t},\widehat{\mu}_{SRB})
are semi-conjugate. This allows us to derive the exponential mixing of
f_{t}
with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{SRB}
from the corresponding property of
\widehat{P}_{t}
with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb}
. In this subsection, we complete this chain of reasoning by establishing the exponential mixing of
\widehat{P}_{t}
with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb}
from that of
\widehat{T}_{t}
with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{g}\times\text{Leb}
.

Let
\widehat{T}:\widehat{X}\to\widehat{X}
be the
\mathbb{T}^{d}
-extension of
T:X\to X
with the skewing function
\Theta:X\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The extension
\widehat{T}
preserves the natural probability product measure
\widehat{\mu}_{g}:=\mu_{g}\times d\theta
. Let
\widehat{T}_{t}:\widehat{X}^{r}\to\widehat{X}^{r}
be the suspension semiflow of
\widehat{T}
under
r
, with the
\widehat{T}_{t}
-invariant probability measure
\widehat{\mu}_{g}\times
Leb, introduced in Subsection
3.3
. As a consequence of Lemma
3.2.2
, we have the following.

Lemma 4.4.1
.

For any
\beta\in(0,1)
there exists
C>0
such that for any
E\in\mathcal{F}^{\beta}(\widehat{S}^{r})
the function
(x,\theta,u)\mapsto\widehat{E}(x,\theta,u):=\eta_{x}(E(x,\cdot,\theta,u))
lies in
\mathcal{F}^{\beta}(\widehat{X}^{r})
and
||\widehat{E}||_{\beta}\leq C||E||_{\beta}
.

Proof.

By definition, we have
||\widehat{E}||_{\beta}=\sup_{i}||\widehat{E}|_{\widehat{X}_{i}^{r}}||_{C^{\beta}}
. For each set
\widehat{X}^{r}_{i}
and any
(x,\theta,u),(x^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in\widehat{X}^{r}_{i}
, using Lemma
3.2.2
we can rewrite

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{Z}E(x,z,\theta,u)d\eta_{x}(z)-\int_{Z}E(x^{\prime},z,\theta^{\prime},u^{\prime})d\eta_{x^{\prime}}(z)\bigg|

(4.4.1)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\int_{Z}E(x,z,\theta,u)d\eta_{x}(z)-\int_{Z}E(x^{\prime},z,\theta,u)d\eta_{x^{\prime}}(z)\bigg|

\displaystyle+\int_{Z}|E(x^{\prime},z,\theta,u)-E(x^{\prime},z,\theta^{\prime},u^{\prime})|d\eta_{x^{\prime}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq C||E||_{\beta}d(x,x^{\prime})+||E||_{\beta}d((\theta,u),(\theta^{\prime},u^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq C||E||_{\beta}d((x,\theta,u),(x^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},u^{\prime})).

The above estimate
shows that
|\widehat{E}|_{X^{r}_{i}}|_{C^{\beta}}\leq C||E||_{\beta}
. On the other hand, it is easy to see that
|\widehat{E}|_{X^{r}_{i}}|_{\infty}\leq||E||_{\beta}
. Therefore, we deduce that
||\widehat{E}||_{X^{r}_{i}}|_{C^{\beta}}\leq C||E||_{\beta}
which completes the proof.

Lemma 4.4.2
.

If
\widehat{T}_{t}
is exponentially mixing with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{g}\times\emph{Leb}
, then
\widehat{P}_{t}
is exponentially mixing with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\emph{Leb}

Proof.

For any

, we need to show the quantity

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\widehat{S}^{r}}E\circ\widehat{P}_{t}.Fd\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb}-\int_{\widehat{S}^{r}}Ed\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb}\int_{\widehat{S}^{r}}Fd\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb}\bigg|

3.2.2
we can rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{S}^{r}}Ed\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb}=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{Z}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{r(x)}E(x,z,\theta,u)dud\theta d\eta_{x}(z)d\mu_{g}(x)

(4.4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{r(x)}\int_{Z}E(x,z,\theta,u)d\eta_{x}(z)dud\theta d\mu_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{r(x)}\widehat{E}(x,\theta,u)dud\theta d\mu_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{X}^{r}}\widehat{E}d\widehat{\mu}_{g}\times\text{Leb}.

\int_{\widehat{S}^{r}}Fd\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb}=\int_{\widehat{S}^{r}}Fd\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb}

It remains to deal with the first item in (

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{S}^{r}}E\circ\widehat{P}_{t}.Fd\widehat{\mu}_{S}\times\text{Leb}

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{Z}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{r(x)}E(x,z,\theta,u+t)F(x,z,\theta,u)dud\theta d\eta_{x}(z)d\mu_{g}(x)

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{r(x)}\int_{Z}E(x,z,\theta,u+t)F(x,z,\theta,u)d\eta_{x}(z)dud\theta d\mu_{g}(x)

\displaystyle\int_{Z}|E(x,z,\theta,u+t)-E(x,z^{\prime},\theta,u+t)|d\eta_{x}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}|E(P^{n(x,u+t)}(x,z),\theta+\Theta_{n(x,u+t)}(x),u+t-r_{n(x,u+t)}(x))

where

\displaystyle C||E||_{\beta}||F||_{\beta}\int_{X^{r}}\lambda^{n(x,u+t)}d\mu_{g}\times\text{Leb}+C||\widehat{E}||_{\beta}||\widehat{F}||_{\beta}e^{-ct}.

\leq C||E||_{\beta}||F||_{\beta}e^{-ct}+C||E||_{\beta}||F||_{\beta}e^{-ct},

(4.4.6)

which completes the proof. ∎

5.1.
A Dolgopyat type estimate and proof of Theorem
2

To prove Theorem
2
, by Lemmas
4.2.5
and
4.4.2
, we need to show that the suspension semiflow
\widehat{T}_{t}
is exponentially mixing with respect to
\widehat{\mu}_{g}\times\text{Leb}
. The standard strategy involves investigating the Laplace transform of the correlation function. By showing that the Laplace transform admits an analytic extension to a suitable region in the left half-plane, together with a polynomial bound, we obtain the decay rate of the correlation function depending on the shape of this region. The Laplace transform can be written
in terms of
transfer operators, and the analytic extension follows from a Dolgopyat type estimate of these transfer operators as stated in Proposition
5.1.5
. This subsection is devoted to achieving this goal.

Recall that the correlation function for
E,F\in\mathcal{F}^{2d}(\widehat{X}^{r})

with

\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we could express
E
and
F

E(x,\theta,u)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e_{m}(x,u)e^{im\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad F(x,\theta,u)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{m}(x,u)e^{im\theta},

where
e_{m}(x,u)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}E(x,\theta,u)e^{-im\theta}d\theta
and
f_{m}(x,u)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(x,\theta,u)e^{-im\theta}d\theta

E_{m}(x,\theta,u):=e_{m}(x,u)e^{im\theta}
and
F_{m}(x,\theta,u):=f_{m}(x,u)e^{im\theta}

1

|m|
denote its supremum norm for a number

|m|:=\sup_{1\leq i\leq d}|m_{i}|

Lemma 5.1.1

For any

By the Fourier expansions of

Lemma 5.1.2

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{r(x)}E_{m}(x,\theta,u+t)F_{m^{\prime}}(x,\theta,u)dud\theta d\mu_{g}(x)

6.
Final comments and questions

(1)

The strictly
\frac{1}{4}
-pinching hypothesis on the negative sectional curvatures in Theorem
1
is essential to the proof of the result. This is in order that we can work with transfer operators of
C^{1+\alpha}
expanding maps acting on smooth functions, which in turn, is central to the cancellation argument that is used in a crucial way to bound their spectra. It would be a reasonable expectation that the same result would be true without any pinching condition, i.e., smooth three-dimensional frame flows are exponentially mixing.

An alternative approach to try to avoid the pinching assumption might come from
transfer operators defined on suitable anisotropic spaces of distributions. However, a major obstacle would be to identify a suitable Banach space and operator and to develop a new theory associated to these.

(2)

We would conjecture that an analogue of Theorem
1
would be true on compact odd dimensional manifolds of negative sectional curvatures or compact even dimensional manifolds of strictly
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched negative sectional curvatures. However, in addition to the complications discussed in (1) above and the difficulty in establishing the necessary properties of the Brin transitivity group there would be significant difficulties in formulating and proving results for transfer operators associated to non-abelian groups SO
(n)
, for
n\geq 3
.

(3)

It is natural to expect that the exponential decay of correlations in Theorems
1
and
2

would also hold when the measure
\mu_{SRB}
is replaced by a Gibbs measure for a Hölder continuous potential.
However, it is the use of towers in our proofs which restricts our results to the measure
\mu_{SRB}
.

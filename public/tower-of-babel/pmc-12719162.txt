Isolated right hepatic duct dilation – Type VI of Mirizzi syndrome?
Authors: Longchang Chen, Yan Sun, Quanda Liu
Published: 2025

Mirizzi syndrome (MS) is defined as biliary obstruction caused by extrinsic compression of the common hepatic duct (CHD) or common bile duct (CBD) from impacted gallstones in the cystic duct or Hartmann's pouch [1]. The compression may lead to different degrees of complex biliary obstruction, erosion, fibrosis, or fistula. According to the mostly accepted Csendes-Beltran's classification [1], there are 5 types of MS categorized by the extent of the CHD compression, erosion, with or without cholecystobiliary or cholecystoenteric fistula. The clinical manifestations of MS include fever, right upper abdominal pain, obstructive jaundice, and gallstone ileus. Different types of MS have relevant management algorithms [2].

A 37-year-old woman incidentally exhibited an isolated right hepatic duct (RHD) dilation and abnormalities in serum transaminases, and no remarkable clinical manifestations presented. Laboratory studies showed serum alanine aminotransferase 240 U/L, aspartate aminotransferase 158 U/L, alkaline phosphatase 579 U/L, γ-glutamyl transpeptidase 897 U/L, carbohydrate antigen 19–9 63.3 U/mL, and normal bilirubin. The abdominal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed RHD dilation and a mass in the dilated RHD, and the contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) demonstrated local wall thickness and enhancement of RHD (Figs. 1). A diagnosis of Bismuth Type IIIa hilar cholangiocarcinoma was suspected, whereas it should be differentiated from hepatolithiasis. An initial laparoscopic exploration found that the right hemiliver appeared ischemic, the gallbladder was atrophic, a solid mass (size of 2 cm × 2 cm) was arising from RHD with upstreaming dilation of the biliary trees (Fig. 2), and the common bile duct was normal. Therefore, due to a high suspicion of hilar cholangiocarcinoma and a dread of potential risk of tumor cell dissemination [3], a decision was made to convert to an open procedure. After meticulous anatomical dissection, it revealed that the cystic duct and Hartmann's pouch were merged with the RHD above the hepatic duct confluence, in which the solid mass was formed. The lesioned RHD and gallbladder were resected en bloc, followed by an end-to-side anastomosis of the proximal RHD to the left hepatic duct and insertion of a T-tube with limbs positioned into both the right and left hepatic ducts. Surgical exploration confirmed the RHD obstruction attributable to an impacted gallstone in the cystic duct, where a cholecystobiliary fistula was formed – a finding consistent with MS. The patient recovered uneventfully. The T-tube was removed 2 months later as the cholangiography was normal. In this case, this manifestation was easily misdiagnosed as hilar cholangiocarcinoma, because a cholecystobiliary fistula occurring at RHD is an extremely rare condition, which is very difficult to be recognized. We propose adding a novel type VI to the Csendes-Beltran classification of Mirizzi syndrome, which constitutes a valuable extension to the current classification system (see Table 1).

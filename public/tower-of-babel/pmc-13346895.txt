Big data information security encryption scheme based on complex chaotic systems in the Internet of Things
Authors: Lili Liang, Lanqing Lou, Mengjing Wang, Yuanling Xu, Chunyang Liu, Chunlin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the increasing demand for image information security in the Internet of Things, traditional encryption methods still have shortcomings in key space, scrambling diffusion coordination, and anti-attack capabilities. A multi-level image encryption model is proposed, which integrates a two-dimensional lagged complex Logistic chaotic system, a complex Chen-Lorenz framework, deoxyribonucleotide bidirectional operation, and wavelet domain decomposition. The model enhances key sensitivity through multi-source chaotic sequences and perturbations from the dynamic perturbation function, achieves nonlinear diffusion in the deoxyribonucleotide domain, and combines wavelet decomposition to improve randomness and robustness. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model achieves average encryption and decryption times of approximately 510 ms and 500 ms, respectively. Ablation experiments validate the effectiveness of each submodule from the perspectives of internal state stability and modular synergy. With the integration of composite chaos and dual-domain processing, the root mean square error of intermediate states decreases from 0.12 to 0.06, indicating enhanced numerical stability after multi-module integration. In security testing, the memory consumption for encryption and decryption remains between 250 kb and 500 kb, with the highest value not exceeding 650 kb. Under six typical IoT tampering attacks, the bit error rate of the model is consistently maintained below 0.2, demonstrating superior performance compared to most competing methods. This highlights the ciphertext’s excellent statistical indistinguishability. The overall results have verified the comprehensive improvement of the research method in terms of encryption and decryption efficiency, security, and robustness, and have good application and promotion value in Internet of Things scenarios.

Background

With the speedy advancement of the Internet of Things (IoT), cloud computing, and big data technologies, various sensing terminals are continuously deployed in application scenarios such as smart cities, intelligent transportation, industrial monitoring, and remote healthcare, driving significant improvements in data collection, transmission, and analysis capabilities. In this context, image data, as an important component of big data, plays a core role in fields such as video surveillance, autonomous driving, remote sensing surveying, and medical imaging diagnosis1. However, compared with traditional text or structured data, image data often has characteristics such as large data volume, high information dimension, and strong content sensitivity. Once it is subjected to theft, tampering, or replay attacks during transmission or storage, it will directly threaten public safety, personal privacy, and commercial secrets2. Therefore, achieving efficient, secure, and controllable image information encryption and protection in the big data environment has become a key issue that urgently needs to be addressed in the area of information security. To deal with this issue, Huang X et al. designed a novel compressed sensing meaningful image encryption (IE) algorithm by combining discrete wavelet transform, chaotic system, and Hadamard matrix. The findings denoted that the algorithm had high efficiency and effectiveness in encrypting images with large amounts of data3. Ma X et al. designed a digital IE scheme based on taboo search algorithm and Chen Chao chaotic system. Simulation experiments showed that compared with other encryption schemes, the proposed scheme had good effectiveness and reliability4. Li Q et al. designed an IE algorithm based on 6D high-dimensional chaotic system and DeoxyriboNucleic Acid (DNA) encoding technology. The findings denoted that compared with the baseline method, this algorithm had certain merits in image entropy, pixel correlation, and image complexity5. Mansoor et al. designed a unique, adaptive, hybrid IE scheme. The findings revealed that the designed scheme exhibited excellent encryption performance and could withstand various attacks6. Kumar et al. designed a new method combining chaotic mapping, elliptic curve cryptography and a genetic algorithm to enhance IE. The findings suggested that the proposed approach was highly effective and efficient in safeguarding sensitive image data from unauthorized access and potential attacks7.

Chaos theory has been broadly employed in the area of IE due to its sensitivity to initial values, pseudo randomness, and ergodicity. However, the phase space structure of a single chaotic system is relatively simple, and it may leak some information patterns when facing differential attacks or statistical analysis. Therefore, in recent years, research has gradually shifted towards complex chaotic systems. The term “composite chaotic system” does not simply denote a combination of multiple chaotic mappings, but rather refers to a joint dynamic system constructed by coupling two or more chaotic systems with distinct dynamics or phase-space structures through parameter coupling, state mapping, or sequence fusion. The output sequences of such systems exhibit significantly higher performance in phase-space dimensions, Lyapunov exponent distribution, and ergodicity compared to single chaotic systems. This definition aligns with existing research on composite or coupled chaotic systems and can be regarded as an engineering implementation of the joint dynamic behavior of multi-chaotic systems8. Zheng J et al. proposed a fast unified encryption and decryption algorithm based on composite chaotic systems. By combining logistic mapping and sine mapping, a new logistic sine mapping was obtained. Simulation experiments showed that the algorithm significantly improved encryption and decryption speed9. A. A. Abdul-Kareem et al. proposed a composite sine function and applied it to chaotic systems. The findings denoted that this method exhibited excellent encryption efficiency and high security10. Wang et al. proposed an IE algorithm using multi-basis diffusion and a new 4D chaotic system. Experimental findings such as histograms, entropy analyses and anti-differential attack analyses have proven that the designed algorithm is highly secure11. Wang et al. suggested an image encryption algorithm based on DNA and a composite chaotic system for colour images. Their findings revealed that the algorithm exhibited excellent encryption performance, strong security and high processing speed12. Gilmolk et al. developed a new lightweight composite chaotic encryption method with fuzzy access control for encrypting optical images in the field of the IoT while maintaining image quality. The simulation findings denoted that compared with the methods in other articles, its uniformity, energy, and contrast were significantly improved13.

In summary, although current IE technology has made some progress in chaotic systems, DNA operations, and transform domain processing, there are still problems such as slow encryption speed, limited key space, and insufficient resistance to differential attacks, which make it hard to satisfy the real-time security requirements in large-scale IoT environments. Therefore, a multi-stage intelligent IE model that balances efficiency and security has been developed to strengthen the robustness and applicability of the algorithm in complex scenarios. It should be emphasized that the multi-component encryption framework proposed by the research institute is not a simple aggregation of various technical approaches, but rather a systematic design based on functional complementarity and security requirements stratification. Specifically, the Two-Dimensional Lag Complex Logistic Map (2D-LCLM) serves as the foundational key stream generation module, primarily providing high initial value sensitivity and strong nonlinear characteristics. The introduction of the Complex Chen System (CCS) and Complex Lorenz System (CLS) aims to mitigate potential local chaos attenuation issues that may occur in single chaotic systems during long sequence iterations, enhancing overall key stream complexity and ergodicity through expanded phase space dimensions. The dynamic perturbation mechanism further introduces time-varying uncertainties at the parameter level, effectively reducing the risk of fixed parameters being inferred or reconstructed. Building on this foundation, DNA encoding and nucleotide operations achieve nonlinear diffusion at the pixel level, making the encryption process difficult to characterize through traditional algebraic analysis or statistical modeling. Wavelet decomposition introduces multi-scale perturbations from the frequency domain perspective, enhancing local randomness and noise resistance while preserving global structure. Dynamic S-boxes are employed to strengthen nonlinear substitution and avalanche effects in the round function. These modules operate across distinct security layers in the time, spatial, and frequency domains, forming a hierarchical, functionally complementary multi-domain collaborative encryption architecture rather than redundant combinations with overlapping functionalities.

Methodology

To address the issues of insufficient complexity of key streams and limited resistance to attacks in IoT IE, this study first constructs an improved Two-Dimensional Lag Complex Logistic Map (2D-LCLM) system combined with dynamic parameter tuning based on the perturbation of the dynamic perturbation function. Subsequently, the Complex Chen System (CCS) and Complex Lorenz System (CLS) are introduced to form a composite chaotic framework, and DNA encoding and nucleotide operations are combined to achieve dual key driven nonlinear diffusion in the encoding domain. Finally, the chaotic sequence is subjected to multi-scale decomposition and reconstruction using Wavelet Functions (WF), resulting in a ciphertext image with significantly improved security and randomness.

Design of IoT image encryption algorithm based on improved 2D-LCLM

In IoT IE, the chaotic encryption process usually includes four core steps: chaotic sequence generation, pixel scrambling, pixel diffusion, and ciphertext generation. Firstly, a highly random and initial value sensitive key stream is generated through a chaotic system. Then, the spatial position of image pixels is scrambled using this sequence to break the original spatial correlation. Subsequently, global perturbation of pixel grayscale values is combined with diffusion operations to ultimately form a highly secure encrypted image14–16. In this study, the generation of chaotic sequences relies on 2D-LCLM, which introduces complex domain operations and time lag terms on the basis of traditional logistic mapping, and can generate more complex and evenly distributed chaotic trajectories, suitable for application in high security IE scenarios17–19. To enhance the reproducibility of the algorithm, key implementation details and parameter specifications are further elaborated. For the two-dimensional delayed complex Logistic chaotic system (2D-LCLM), the control parameters α and β are set within the ranges α∈[3.6,4.0] and β∈[0.2,0.9], respectively, while the phase perturbation parameter λ ranges from [0.6,0.8]. The parameter configurations for the complex Chen system (CCS) and complex Lorenz system (CLS) adhere to their standard chaotic state ranges, with consistent parameter combinations maintained throughout experiments. All chaotic systems’ initial conditions are generated via SHA-256 hash mapping of a 256-bit master key, with precision maintained at 10− 14 to ensure initial value sensitivity. To eliminate transient effects, the three-channel chaotic system undergoes 1,000 iterations of Ttrans prior to formal encryption participation, with outputs discarded during this phase. Subsequent valid iterations match the image pixel count. These settings remain consistent across all experiments. The encryption rules are shown in Fig. 1.

As shown in Fig. 1, this scheme employs symbol matrix perturbations to define multi-directional logical operation paths (vertical, horizontal, cross, etc.) for logically scrambling image data. The results from each path are then XOR-diffused with the chaotic key stream generated by 2D-LCLM, achieving pixel-level perturbation. Subsequently, the results of each path operation are XORed diffused with the chaotic key stream generated by the improved 2D-LCLM to achieve pixel level perturbation. Finally, the diffusion results of multiple paths are fused again to form a globally encrypted ciphertext image26,27. Construction of image encryption strategy for composite chaotic IoT based on DNA transformation

After constructing an improved 2D-LCLM-based IE algorithm for the IoT, considering that a single chaotic system may experience local chaos attenuation in long sequence generation, the study introduces a composite chaotic system by coupling and fusing multiple chaotic systems with different dynamic characteristics and phase space structures to achieve a higher dimensional and more complex key stream generation mechanism. Based on the comprehensive dynamic characteristics and safety requirements, the fusion of CCS and CLS is selected for research. On the one hand, CCS has strong nonlinearity and higher Lyapunov exponent, which can generate highly complex trajectories in the complex domain, making it suitable for improving the unpredictability of key streams28. On the other hand, CLS exhibits stable global chaos and excellent traversal under multi-parameter conditions, which can effectively compensate for the shortcomings of CCS in local phase space distribution29,30. The operating mechanism of CCS and CLS in the encryption process is shown in Fig. 5.

Fig. 5The hybrid encryption principle of CCS and CLS.

The hybrid encryption principle of CCS and CLS.

In Fig. 5, the encryption begins with an externally input key and initial value, which serve as the initial conditions for CCS and CLS, respectively. After multiple iterations, two sets of high-dimensional chaotic sequences are generated. Meanwhile, the original image is processed into encrypted data blocks through grayscale and pixel grouping. Subsequently, chaotic sequences from CCS and CLS are injected into the scrambling and diffusion modules, respectively. During the scrambling phase, the chaotic sequences are used to rearrange the spatial positions of pixels; In the diffusion stage, the chaotic sequence nonlinearly modulates the pixel grayscale values. Finally, the scrambled and diffused data blocks are recombined and output as ciphertext images. On this basis, the study further introduces DNA transformation mechanism to improve the 2D-LCLM-CCS-CLS composite chaotic system. By mapping chaotic sequences to image pixel values as DNA encoding sequences composed of four types of bases A, C, G, and T, and utilizing nucleotide operation rules, the biological computation of scrambling and diffusion processes is achieved31. Results and analysis

The study established a comprehensive experimental platform. First, model configurations were optimized through hyperparameter testing. Subsequently, module-level ablation experiments were conducted, and four advanced IE methods were compared to evaluate their advantages in terms of encryption and decryption efficiency, throughput, and resource utilization. Further testing was conducted under multiple types of attacks to test the performance and practicality of the model in terms of security, robustness, and adaptability.

Performance testing of a new IoT image encryption strategy model

To enhance the mapping between experimental results and real-world IoT application environments, this study implemented constraint settings on algorithm operation modes on a high-performance hardware platform. All experiments adopted single-thread CPU scheduling and single-process GPU invocation, without enabling multi-GPU parallelism, mixed-precision acceleration, or batch concurrency optimization. Image encryption and decryption processes were independently executed on a per-image basis (Batch Size = 1) to simulate real-time processing scenarios at IoT edge nodes or gateway devices. On the GPU side, only basic CUDA core computing functions were utilized, with no Tensor Cores or deep learning acceleration modules invoked, thereby avoiding bias in algorithm performance evaluation caused by dedicated computing power from high-end hardware. Regarding IoT application-level mapping, the experimental setup primarily corresponds to edge computing or gateway layer scenarios in IoT systems. Specifically, the image resolutions processed (256 × 256,512 × 512,1024 × 1024) and single-channel processing modes can be mapped to typical IoT application scenarios such as intelligent camera front-end preprocessing, edge server aggregation processing, and high-resolution surveillance data encryption. The encryption/decryption time and memory usage recorded during experiments reflect computational load characteristics of single-task execution under constrained concurrency conditions, serving as reference data for performance migration and resource allocation among IoT nodes with varying computing power levels. Although this study has implemented operational constraints on high-performance hardware platforms—including single-thread CPU scheduling, single-process GPU invocation, and batch size limitations of 1—to approximate the single-task processing behavior of IoT edge nodes, current experiments are primarily conducted in desktop-level high-performance computing environments. Real-world deployment testing on resource-constrained lightweight IoT terminals such as Raspberry Pi and Jetson Nano has not yet been performed. Consequently, the performance results presented here are more suitable for benchmark evaluation of algorithms in edge gateway or small-scale edge server scenarios. Practical operational metrics including real-time latency, peak memory consumption, and sustained operational stability on ultra-low-power terminal devices require further validation in subsequent research.

To comprehensively validate the independent contributions of different module combinations in terms of security and performance, the study further expanded the experimental design. Systematic comparative tests were conducted on four schemes: 2D-LCLM, 2D-LCLM-CCS, 2D-LCLM-CCS-CLS, and 2D-LCLM-CCS-CLS-WF. Beyond average entropy (AE) and processing time (Tproc), the experiments introduced additional metrics: key space size (KSS), computational complexity for brute-force attacks, differential attack indicators (NPCR, UACI), and resistance analysis against chosen plaintext attacks (CPA) and chosen ciphertext attacks (CCA). To assess whether the differences in security and performance metrics among various encryption schemes are statistically significant, this study employed one-way ANOVA and two-tailed t-tests with a significance level of 0.05. The ANOVA analysis yielded degrees of freedom (df₁ = 3, df₂ = 36) and a F-statistic of 9.27 (p < 0.01), indicating significant differences in comprehensive performance metrics between the models. Subsequent two-tailed t-tests further confirmed that the proposed model demonstrated statistically significant differences from the comparison methods at the 0.05 level, as detailed in Table 3.

Furthermore, this paper introduces statistical significance testing in the NPCR and UACI metrics, not as a criterion for cryptographic security, but to assist in analyzing statistical differences in differential diffusion performance among various encryption schemes. The security effectiveness of NPCR and UACI is first determined by whether they meet theoretical critical thresholds. On this basis, statistical testing is solely used to compare the significance of mean differences between metrics across different algorithms. Specifically, statistical testing employs multi-group plaintext-ciphertext pairs as sample sources. For each algorithm, NNN groups of plaintext image pairs are randomly selected under fixed key conditions (N = 50 in this study), with corresponding NPCR and UACI values calculated to form the sample set. The null hypothesis is set as: no significant difference exists between the means of different algorithms in NPCR or UACI metrics; the alternative hypothesis is: the proposed method exhibits significant differences from the comparison algorithm in the corresponding metrics. Considering the large sample size and the approximate continuous distribution of metrics, a two-tailed t-test based on normal approximation is adopted during the testing process, with a significance level set at 0.05.

The resistance to chosen plaintext attacks (CPA) can be quantitatively evaluated using differential diffusion metrics. The basic 2D-LCLM model achieved NPCR (97.82%) and UACI (31.84%) below standard security thresholds (NPCR ≈ 99.6%, UACI ≈ 33.3%), indicating exploitable differential structures under CPA conditions. It should be emphasized that the introduction of wavelet functions (WFs) serves not merely to increase computational complexity, but makes a distinct and measurable contribution to system security. As shown in Table 2, while the 2D-LCLM-CCS-CLS scheme without WFs demonstrates significant improvements in key space and chaotic complexity, it still lags behind the full model in differential attack metrics (NPCR, UACI) and resistance to chosen plaintext/chosen ciphertext attacks. The implementation of WFs enables multi-scale perturbation in the frequency domain, redistributing pixel energy between low-frequency and high-frequency subbands. This substantially weakens attackers ‘ability to analyze spatial domain statistical features or local differential patterns. Compared to the WF-free scheme, the full model’s enhanced performance in NPCR, UACI, and CPA/CCA resistance demonstrates that WFs play an independent role in security enhancement beyond chaotic scrambling and DNA diffusion, rather than being a simple additive effect.

Although the proposed model incorporates multiple functional modules, its design adheres to the principle of functional orthogonality rather than empirical stacking. Specifically, 2D-LCLM handles initial value sensitivity and nonlinear generation of the base key stream, while CCS and CLS extend phase space complexity to prevent dynamic degradation of a single chaotic system. The dynamic perturbation mechanism introduces time-varying parameter uncertainties, DNA computation achieves pixel-level nonlinear diffusion, wavelet decomposition introduces multi-scale perturbations in the frequency domain, and the dynamic S-box enhances nonlinear wheel functions. Each module operates independently across the parameter domain, state domain, pixel domain, and frequency domain, with non-redundant functionalities that cannot be substituted. From a complexity perspective, the algorithm’s total computational load grows linearly with pixel scale, while computational overheads from each module are controllably additive. Correspondingly, multi-chaos and multi-domain diffusion significantly expand the key space scale and enhance resistance to differential and statistical attacks. Ablation results demonstrate a clear and interpretable trade-off between increased computational complexity and enhanced security, rather than empirical performance stacking.

Conclusion

Aiming at the problems of insufficient key space, weak coordination of scrambling diffusion, and poor adaptability to complex attacks in traditional IE models, a multi-level IE model integrating 2D-LCLM chaotic system, composite perturbation mechanism, and DNA-WF dual-domain processing was studied and constructed. The comprehensive experimental results indicate that when the mapping parameter is set to 0.8 and the weighting factor to 0.7, the model exhibits optimal performance in terms of iterative convergence speed and stability, with the loss value decreasing rapidly and maintaining a steady state. On the SNID and IoT-SID datasets, the model’s average encryption/decryption time was controlled within approximately 300 ms. Its overall performance surpassed that of several comparative algorithms in the literature (e.g., CIE, CSIE, and FrFT-IE), with resource utilization reduced by about 15% and throughput increased by approximately 20%. The experimental results of the ablation demonstrate that the MSE decreases from 0.12 to 0.06 with the introduction of WF domain multi-scale decomposition and dynamic S-box module, indicating a significant improvement in the numerical stability of the system’s internal state after multi-module integration. The security advantages are further validated by information entropy, NPCR, UACI, and attack test results. Security analysis indicates the model exhibits excellent performance in pixel distribution uniformity and information entropy, with entropy values approaching theoretical maximums. Under six attack scenarios, the BER remains stable below 0.2, demonstrating strong resistance to noise and tampering. It is noteworthy that the model architecture incorporating multiple modules results in slightly higher computational complexity compared to traditional algorithms, which may still present efficiency bottlenecks in large-scale data processing or high-frame-rate scenarios. Although this study simulated single-channel image processing patterns at IoT edge devices using constrained concurrency methods, deployment validation has not been conducted on real-world resource-limited IoT devices such as Raspberry Pi. Therefore, current conclusions are more applicable to image secure transmission scenarios under edge gateway or small-scale edge server configurations. Future research should integrate typical embedded platforms like Raspberry Pi and Jetson Nano to conduct practical testing focusing on runtime latency, energy consumption, memory footprint, and continuous task stability. Algorithm engineering applicability in real IoT terminals can be enhanced through lightweight module optimization, parallel processing techniques, and cross-domain collaborative acceleration mechanisms. Future research may further incorporate lightweight parallel structures and cross-domain attention mechanisms to balance encryption strength and real-time performance, thereby enhancing the model’s scalability and engineering applicability in IoT and multi-source image transmission environments.

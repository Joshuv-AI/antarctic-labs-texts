Asymptotic Equivalence and Decay Characterization in Second-Grade Fluid Equations
Felipe W. Cruz, César J. Niche, Cilon F. Perusato

Abstract
We establish new asymptotic results for the solutions of the second-grade fluids equations and characterize their decay rate in terms of the behavior of the initial data. Moreover, assuming more regularity for the initial data, we study the large-time behavior of these solutions by comparing them to the solutions of the linearized equations. As a consequence, we obtain lower bounds for the solutions. Other auxiliary results of interest are discussed.

Introduction

Second-grade fluids are non-Newtonian fluids whose stress tensor is

\sigma=-p\,Id+2\nu\,A_{1}+\alpha_{1}A_{2}+\alpha_{2}A_{1}^{2},

(1)

where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are the Rivlin - Ericksen
[
39
]
tensors given by

A_{1}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla u+\nabla u^{t}\right),\quad A_{2}(u)=\partial_{t}A_{1}+(u\cdot\nabla)A_{1}+(\nabla u)^{t}A_{1}+A_{1}(\nabla u).

(2)

Here,
\mathbf{u}
and
p
denote the velocity of the fluid and its pressure, respectively, defined on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Some properties of solutions to systems of equations arising from (
1
) depend on the relation between the coefficients
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
in (
2
), see Dunn and Fosdick
[
16
]
, Fosdick and Ragajopal
[
18
,
19
]
. In particular, the choice
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=0,\nu>0
leads to the thermodynamically consistent equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})-\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}+\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}+\nabla p={\boldsymbol{0}},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\end{cases}

(3)

where
\alpha=\alpha_{1}
and
\mu>0
is the kinematic viscosity, see Dunn and Fosdick
[
16
]
. Due to its importance for modeling different non-Newtonian fluids and its relation to other well known models, different aspects concerning solutions to (
3
) have been studied, see Bresch and Lemoine
[
6
]
, Busuioc
[

,

, Busuioc and Ratiu
[

]
, Cioranescu and El Hacène
[

, Cioranescu, Girault and Ragajopal
[

, Cioranescu and Girault
[

, Clark, Friz and Rojas-Medar

, Coscia and Galdi

, Friz, Guillén-González and Rojas-Medar

, Girault and Scott

The existence of the regularizing term
-\alpha\>\!\partial_{t}\Delta\mathbf{u}
in (
3
) implies (formally) the energy identity

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\bigl\|\mathbf{u}(t)\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}}^{2}+\alpha\>\!\bigl\|\nabla\>\!\mathbf{u}(t)\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}}^{2}\right)=-2\mu\>\!\bigl\|\nabla\>\!\mathbf{u}(t)\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}}^{2},

(4)

provided
\mathbf{u}
is a sufficiently regular solution. This naturally leads to the question of what is the decay rate for the natural
H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
norm

\bigl\|\mathbf{u}(t)\bigr\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}}^{2}:=\bigl\|\mathbf{u}(t)\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}}^{2}+\alpha\>\!\bigl\|\nabla\>\!\mathbf{u}(t)\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}}^{2},

(5)

which is equivalent to the usual
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
norm.

Our main goal in this article is to prove decay estimates for this norm for solutions to (
3
) in terms of the initial data
u_{0}
. To accomplish this, we associate to each
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
a decay character
r^{\ast}=r^{\ast}(u_{0})
, which quantifies the “order” of
\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)
near the origin in the frequency domain, i.e.
r^{\ast}=r^{\ast}(u_{0})
if
|\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)|\approx|\xi|^{r^{\ast}}
near
\xi=0
. This idea has been developed by Bjorland and Schonbek
[
4
]
, Brandolese
[
5
]
and Niche and Schonbek
[
34
]
. We adopt the definitions and conventions from Brandolese
[
5
]
, who introduced generalized decay characters
r^{\ast}_{+}
and
r^{\ast}_{-}
when aiming for upper and lower decay bounds respectively, and showed that when
r^{\ast}_{+}=r^{\ast}_{-}
, they coincide with the original
r^{\ast}
, for full details see Section
2.2
.

We state now our first main result. For the definition of
V_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}\subset H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
, see Section
2.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in V_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
and suppose that
r^{\ast}_{+}(\mathbf{u}_{0})=r^{\ast}_{+}
. Additionally, assume that
\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{V_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}}<\epsilon
for a sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. Then, for any weak solution
\mathbf{u}
to (
3
), the following estimate holds:

\bigl\|\mathbf{u}(t)\bigr\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}}^{2}\leq C\,(t+1)^{-\min\left\{\frac{3}{2}+r^{\ast}_{+},\frac{5}{2}\right\}},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0,

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{V_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}},\alpha,r^{\ast}_{+}
, and
\mu
.

There are few results concerning decay of solutions to (
3
). Assuming small data in
H^{3}
, Galdi and Passerini
[
24
]
proved that
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\,\left(\ln(t)\right)^{-1}
, for large enough
t
. Nečasová and Penel
[
32
]
showed that, for initial data
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{3}\cap L^{1}
with
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}_{0}=0
and no restrictions on size, solutions decay as

\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

In both of these results, the authors assume the existence of weak solutions. In Theorem
1.1
we provide a decay with improved rates for the natural norm (
5
) for a much larger family of initial data, see Remark
2.2
and note that
r^{\ast}(\mathbf{u}_{0})=0
for
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}\cap L^{1}
. Moreover, as the linear part of the equation decays as
t^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{\ast}_{+}\right)}
, see Theorem
2.4
, we are able to separate the contribution of the linear and nonlinear parts of the solution to the decay in terms of initial data. For the sake of completeness, in Section
2.1
we provide a proof of the existence of weak solutions to (
3
). The choice of subspace of
H^{3}
and the hypotheses on small data in Theorem
1.1
are due to them being essential in the proof of this existence result. For similar decay estimates for solutions to models related to (
3

[

, Bjorland
[

, Bjorland and Schonbek
[

]
, Busuioc
[
8
]
, Gao, Lyu and Yao
[

]
, Zhao
[

]
.

We now study some aspects of the asymptotic behavior of solutions to (
3
), in particular we study decay of
\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{\bar{u}}(t)
, where
\mathbf{\bar{u}}
is a solution to the pseudo-parabolic linear part

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}-\alpha\Delta\overline{\mathbf{u}})-\mu\Delta\overline{\mathbf{u}}={\boldsymbol{0}},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\operatorname{div}\overline{\mathbf{u}}=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\overline{\mathbf{u}}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}.\end{cases}

(6)

Our second main result in this article is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}_{0}=0
, and suppose that
r^{\ast}_{+}(\mathbf{u}_{0})=r^{\ast}_{+}
and that
\mathbf{u}_{0}
is small in
V_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, as in Theorem
1.1
. Let
\mathbf{u}
be a weak solution to (
3
), and let
\overline{\mathbf{u}}
be the solution to the linear part (
6
) with the same initial data
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, any weak solution

\mathbf{u}(t)
satisfies the estimate

\bigl\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\bigr\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}}^{2}\leq C\,(t+1)^{-\min\left\{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{\ast}_{+},\frac{5}{2}\right\}},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0,

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}},\alpha,r^{\ast}_{+}
, and
\mu
.

Then, the solution
\mathbf{u}
of (
3
) is asymptotically equivalent to the solution
\overline{\mathbf{u}}
of the pseudo-parabolic equation (
11
) with the same data. Other aspects of asymptotic behavior of (
3
), in particular of the vorticity
\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf{u}
, have been studied by Jaffal-Mourtada
[
30
]
. We need to increase the regularity of initial data to
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
in order to use the estimates for
\overline{\mathbf{u}}
in Theorem
4.1
, see Remark
4.1
for a detailed explanation of this fact.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
, lower bounds for decay of
\mathbf{u}
can be obtained for a restricted range of
r^{\ast}
, providing sharp estimates for this family of initial data.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{0}
be as in Theorem
1.2
. If
r^{*}_{+}=r^{*}_{+}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\in(-3/2,1)
, then the weak solution
\mathbf{u}
to (
3
) which such initial data satisfies

\bigl\|\mathbf{u}(t)\bigr\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\geq C\,(t+1)^{-\min\left\{\frac{3}{2}+r^{*},\frac{5}{2}\right\}},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0,

where
C>0
depends only on
\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\alpha,r^{*},\mu
.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall definitions, results and estimates needed later on in our proofs. In Section
3
we prove Theorem
1.1
, while in Section

1.2

Preliminaries

2.1
Existence of weak solutions

We provide a sketch of a proof of the existence of weak solutions for equations (
3
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. As we follow closely the arguments in Cioranescu and Girault
[
13
]
for the existence of a solution in bounded domains, and in Bjorland and Schonbek
[
3
]
for using such kind of results to obtain solutions in the whole space for the Camassa-Holm equations, we only indicate the main points in our proof and do not provide details for the remaining, standard arguments.

We first recall the definitions and results we need from Cioranescu and Girault
[
13
]
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an open bounded domain with
\partial\Omega\in C^{3,1}
. We then consider the following functions spaces

\begin{split}H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega):v=0\,\,\text{in}\,\,\partial\Omega\},\\
V=\{\mathbf{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}:\operatorname{div}\,\mathbf{v}=0\,\,\text{in}\,\,\partial\Omega\},\\
V_{2}=\{\mathbf{v}\in V:\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{v}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{v})\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\}.\end{split}

From Lemma 2.1 in
[
13
]
, we have that the
H^{3}
norm and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{2}}
induced by the inner product

(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})_{V_{2}}=(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})+\alpha(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v})+(\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u}),\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{v}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{v}))

(7)

are equivalent in
V_{2}
, where
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the usual
L^{2}
inner product.

We recall now the trilinear form

b(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}=\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\frac{\partial v_{i}}{x_{j}}w_{i}\,dx.

The fact that

\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}\,dx=b(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})-\alpha b(\mathbf{u},\Delta\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})+\alpha b(\mathbf{v},\Delta\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})

leads to the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in V_{2}
. We say that
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;V_{2})
such that
\mathbf{u^{\prime}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;V)
is a weak solution to (
3
) if for a.e.
t
in
(0,T)
and every
\mathbf{v}\in V

(\mathbf{u^{\prime}},\mathbf{v})+\alpha\,(\nabla\mathbf{u^{\prime}},\nabla\mathbf{v})+\mu\,(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v})+b(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})-\alpha\,b(\mathbf{u},\Delta\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})+\alpha\,b(\mathbf{v},\Delta\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})=0.

The following is the key result which we will use as main ingredient in our proof.

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem 4.7,
[
13
]
)
.

For any small enough
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in V_{2}
there exists a unique weak solution to (
3
) for all
t\geq 0
, in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

We now proceed to sketch the proof of the existence of a weak solution to (
3
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Take
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in V_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})
, where

V_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})=\{\mathbf{u}:\mathbf{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3},\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{v}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{v})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}\},

endowed with the norm induced by (
7
). Consider the ball
B^{R_{k}}
, centered at the origin with radius
R_{k}
. Localize
\mathbf{u}_{0}
by taking
\mathbf{u}_{0}^{k}=\mathbf{u}_{0}\,\chi_{B^{R_{k}-\epsilon}}
, for some small
\epsilon>0
, where
\chi_{B^{R_{k}-\epsilon}}
is a smooth cutoff function which is identically one in
B^{R_{k}-\epsilon}
and equal to zero on
\partial B^{R_{k}}
. As
\mathbf{u}_{0}^{k}\in V_{2}=V_{2}(B^{R_{k}})
, from Theorem
2.1
we have a unique weak solution
\mathbf{u}^{k}
in
B^{R_{k}}
. From the statement of Theorem 2.1 and the proof of Proposition 4.6 in
[
13
]
it can be seen that
\mathbf{u}^{k}
and
\mathbf{u^{\prime}}^{\,k}
are bounded, in

and

. In fact, these constants depend only on

and the continuity constant in the Stokes operator, see pages 312 and 320, (2.13) and page 330 in
[
13
]
. Moreover, from (4.18) in the statement of Theorem 4.6, smallness of initial data in Theorem 2.1 can also be expressed in terms of these constants only, i.e. independently of
k
.

Now take
\mathbf{u}^{k}
from the previous paragraph and extend it to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by making it identically zero outside of
B^{R_{k}}
. By abuse of notation, call this extension
\mathbf{u}^{k}
as well. It follows from the previous paragraph that these global-in-space
\mathbf{u}^{k}
are uniformly bounded in
k
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{k}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbf{u}\,\,\text{in}\,\,L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};V_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})),

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u^{\prime}}^{k}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbf{u^{\prime}}\,\,\text{in}\,\,L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};V({\mathbb{R}^{3}})).

(9)

We now provide the definition of weak solution for (
3
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in V_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})
. We say that
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;V_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}}))
such that
\mathbf{u^{\prime}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;V({\mathbb{R}^{3}}))
is a weak solution to (
3
) if for a.e.
t
in
(0,T)
and every compactly supported
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})

(\mathbf{u^{\prime}},\phi)+\alpha\,(\nabla\mathbf{u^{\prime}},\nabla\phi)+\mu\,(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\phi)+b(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u},\phi)-\alpha\,b(\mathbf{u},\Delta\mathbf{u},\phi)+\alpha\,b(\phi,\Delta\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})=0.

Now take an arbitrary
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})
and
k
such that
\phi\subset B^{R_{k}}
. Extend
\phi
to
B^{R_{k}}
by defining it as zero in the complement of its support. Then
\phi\in V(B^{R_{k}})
and we can apply Theorem
2.1
to obtain

\displaystyle(\mathbf{u^{\prime}}^{\,k},\phi)

\displaystyle+\alpha\,(\nabla\mathbf{u^{\prime}}^{\,k},\nabla\mathbf{v})+\mu\,(\nabla\mathbf{u^{k}},\nabla\phi)+b(\mathbf{u}^{k},\mathbf{u}^{k},\phi)

\displaystyle-\alpha\,b(\mathbf{u}^{k},\Delta\mathbf{u}^{k},\phi)+\alpha\,b(\phi,\Delta\mathbf{u}^{k},\mathbf{u}^{k})=0.

(10)

Convergence of
\mathbf{u}^{k}
and
\mathbf{u^{\prime}}^{k}
in (
8
) and (

) and standard arguments for treating each term in (

) lead us to conclude the following.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any small enough
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in V_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

.

2.2
Decay character theory

In this Section we provide definitions and results concerning the decay character and its relation to decay of solutions to linear dissipative equations. Throughout this article, we denote the Fourier transform either by
\mathcal{F}
or
\widehat{}
,

\mathcal{F}\{\varphi\}(\xi)\equiv\widehat{\varphi}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\!e^{-i\,\xi\cdot x}\varphi(x)\,dx,\qquad i^{2}=-1.

In particular, by Plancherel’s theorem,
we have
\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
,
for every
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 2.3

(Bjorland and Schonbek
[
4
]
, Niche and Schonbek
[
34
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
B(\rho):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,;\,|\xi|\leq\rho\}
. For
r\in\left(-\frac{n}{2},\infty\right)
, we define the decay indicator of
\mathbf{v}_{0}
as

P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0}):=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0^{+}}\rho^{-2r-n}\int_{B(\rho)}|\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi,

provided this limit exists.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the decay indicator is always zero when
r\leq-n/2
. Moreover, observe that the decay indicator compares
\left|\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{0}}(\xi)\right|^{2}
to
|\xi|^{2r}
near
\xi=0
.

Definition 2.4

(Bjorland and Schonbek
[
4
]
, Niche and Schonbek
[
34
]
)
.

The decay character of
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, denoted by
r^{*}=r^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{0})
, is the unique
r\in\left(-\frac{n}{2},\infty\right)
such that
0<P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})<\infty
, provided this number exists.

Theorem 2.3

(Niche and Schonbek
[
34
]
)
.

Suppose
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
s>0
. Let
r_{s}^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{0})=r^{*}(\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{v}_{0})
, where
\Lambda=\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then
-\frac{n}{2}+s<r_{s}^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{0})<\infty
and
r_{s}^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{0})=s+r^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{0})
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that the decay character can be explicitly computed in some important instances, for example
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),1\leq p<2
, see Ferreira, Niche and Planas
[
17
]
. In these cases, we have
r^{\ast}(\mathbf{v}_{0})=-n\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)
.

In Definitions
2.3
and
2.4
, assumptions are made so as to ensure a positive
P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})
. However, these do not hold universally for all
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. As demonstrated by Brandolese
[
5
]
, it is possible to construct initial data with strong oscillations near the origin such that the limit in Definition
2.3
fails to exist for certain values of
r
. Consequently, the decay character is not defined in such cases. He proposed a slightly more general definition of decay character than those in Definitions
2.3
and
2.4
, which we adopt in order to state Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
and Corollary
1.1
.

Definition 2.5

(Brandolese
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
B_{\rho}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|\xi|\leq\rho\}
.
The generalized lower and upper decay indicators of
v_{0}
are

P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})_{-}=\liminf_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\rho^{-2r-n}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\qquad

and

P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})_{+}=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\rho^{-2r-n}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi,

respectively. When
P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})_{-}=P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})_{+}
, then we can define the decay indicator of
\mathbf{v}_{0}
as

P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})=P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})_{-}=P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})_{+}
.

Definition 2.6

(Brandolese
[
5
]
)
.

The generalized upper and lower decay characters of
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are respectively defined by

\displaystyle r^{\ast}_{+}(\mathbf{v}_{0})

\displaystyle=\sup\{r\in\mathbb{R}\colon P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})_{+}<\infty\},

\displaystyle r^{\ast}_{-}(\mathbf{v}_{0})

\displaystyle=\inf\{r\in\mathbb{R}\colon P_{r}(\mathbf{v}_{0})_{-}>0\}.

The decay character, as defined in Definition
2.4
, exists if and only if
r^{\ast}_{+}(\mathbf{v}_{0})=r^{\ast}_{-}(\mathbf{v}_{0})
in Definition
2.6
. Alternatively, Brandolese
[
5
]
proved that the decay character
r^{\ast}(\mathbf{v}_{0})
exists if and only if
\mathbf{v}_{0}
lies within a specific subset of a homogeneous Besov space, namely
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in\dot{\mathcal{A}}^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{\ast}\right)}_{2,\infty}\subset\dot{B}^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{\ast}\right)}_{2,\infty}
.

2.3
The associated linear problem

We shall consider here the following pseudo-parabolic equation in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty)
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}-\alpha\Delta\overline{\mathbf{u}})-\mu\Delta\overline{\mathbf{u}}={\boldsymbol{0}},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\operatorname{div}\overline{\mathbf{u}}=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\overline{\mathbf{u}}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).\end{cases}

(11)

A straightforward calculation gives the energy identity

\frac{d}{dt}\bigl\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\bigr\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=-2\mu\>\!\bigl\|\nabla\,\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

(12)

Since
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we have, in particular, that
\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Hence, the transform
\widehat{\overline{{\mathbf{u}}}}(t)
is well defined. Taking the Fourier transform of (
11
), we get

\begin{cases}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})\widehat{\overline{{\mathbf{u}}}}_{t}+\mu|\xi|^{2}\widehat{\overline{{\mathbf{u}}}}={\boldsymbol{0}},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr i\xi\cdot\widehat{\overline{{\mathbf{u}}}}(\xi,t)=0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad t>0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\widehat{\overline{{\mathbf{u}}}}(0)=\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}.\end{cases}

So,

\widehat{\overline{{\mathbf{u}}}}(\xi,t)=e^{-\frac{\mu|\xi|^{2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}t}\>\!\widehat{\overline{{\mathbf{u}}}}(\xi,0)\equiv e^{tM_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)}\>\!\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi),

where

M_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi):=-\frac{\mu|\xi|^{2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}.

(13)

In particular:

\bigl|\widehat{\overline{{\mathbf{u}}}}(\xi,t)\bigr|\leq\bigl|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)\bigr|,\quad\,\,\forall\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\quad\text{and}\quad t\geq 0.

(14)

Next, we characterize the decay of solutions to (
11
) in terms of the decay character
r^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{0})=r^{*}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H_{\alpha}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
r^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{0})=r^{*}
, that is,

r^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{0})=\max\{r\in\mathbb{R}\colon P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0})_{+}<\infty\}=\min\{r\in\mathbb{R}\colon P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0})_{-}>0\}.

If
-\frac{n}{2}<r^{*}<\infty
, then for the solution
\overline{{\mathbf{u}}}(t)
of (
11
) with initial data
\mathbf{u}_{0}
we have that there exist constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{3}>0
such that

C_{1}(t+1)^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{*}\right)}\leq\bigl\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\bigr\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq C_{2}(t+C_{3})^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{*}\right)},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0.

Remark 2.3
.

The proof of this result follows the approach of C. Niche in
[
33
]
. Moreover, with minor adjustments to our argument (following the ideas of L. Brandolese in
[
5
]
), it is possible to establish the following result:

-

If
P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0})_{-}>0
then there is a constant
C_{1}>0
such that, for all
t>0
,

C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}(r+n/2)}\leq\|\mathbf{\bar{u}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}.\phantom{\leq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}(r+n/2)}}.

-

If
P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0})_{+}<\infty
, then there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that, for all
t>0
,

\phantom{C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}(r+n/2)}\leq}\|\mathbf{\bar{u}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}(r+n/2)}.

Proof.

Initially, note, by Theorem
2.3
, that
r^{*}(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0})=1+r^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{0})
, with
r^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{0})=r^{*}\in\left(\frac{n}{2},\infty\right)
. Since
P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0})>0
, it follows from the definition of the limit that there exist sufficiently small
\rho_{0}>0
and
c_{1}>0
such that for
0<\rho<\rho_{0}
,

c_{1}<\rho^{-2r-n}\int_{B(\rho)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi,\quad\text{ and }\quad c_{1}<\rho^{-2(r+1)-n}\int_{B(\rho)}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi.

Note that
\rho_{0}
and
c_{1}
depend on
P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0})
. From now on, we will denote
B(t):=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,;\,|\xi|\leq\rho(t)\right\}
, for some continuous, positive and decreasing
\rho(t)
to be determined later. Thus:

\displaystyle\displaystyle(2\pi)^{n}\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\geq\int_{B(t)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})e^{2tM_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(t)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})e^{-2\mu|\xi|^{2}t}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq e^{-2\mu t\rho(t)^{2}}\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi+\alpha e^{-2\mu t\rho(t)^{2}}\int_{B(t)}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\rho(t)^{2r+n}\>\!e^{-2\mu t\rho(t)^{2}}+\alpha\>\!c_{1}\rho(t)^{2(r+1)+n}\>\!e^{-2\mu t\rho(t)^{2}}.

Choosing
\rho(t):=\rho_{0}(t+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, we obtain

(2\pi)^{n}\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\geq c_{1}e^{-2\mu\rho_{0}^{2}}[\rho_{0}^{2r+n}(t+1)^{-(r+\frac{n}{2})}+\alpha\rho_{0}^{2(r+1)+n}(t+1)^{-(r+1+\frac{n}{2})}],

which shows that

\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\geq C_{1}(t+1)^{-(r+\frac{n}{2})},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0.

Here,
C_{1}=C_{1}(\mu,\alpha,r,n,P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0}))\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Now, to derive the upper estimate, we use the Schonbek’s method, known in the literature as “Fourier splitting” technique (see
[
41
,
42
]
). For this, we define the closed ball centered at the origin with radius
\rho(t)
:

B(t):=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,;\,|\xi|\leq\rho(t)\equiv\left(\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{2\mu g(t)-\alpha g^{\prime}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}\!,

where
g
is a differentiable function on
(0,\infty)
satisfying

g(0)=1,\quad g(t)\geq 1,\quad g^{\prime}(t)>0,\quad\text{and}\quad 2\mu g(t)>\alpha g^{\prime}(t),\quad\,\,\forall\;t>0.

Using Plancherel’s theorem, it follows from the energy identity (
12
) that

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[\>\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}\right)\!|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\right]=-2\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi.

Multiplying the above identity by
g(t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[g(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}|^{2}\,d\xi\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle-\,2\mu g(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}|^{2}\,d\xi.

From the definition of
B(t)
, we have

\frac{2\mu g(t)|\xi|^{2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}>g^{\prime}(t),\quad\,\,\forall\;\xi\in B(t)^{c}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\backslash\,B(t).

Thus, by (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[g(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\right]

\displaystyle\leq g^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle-2\mu g(t)\int_{B(t)^{c}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq g^{\prime}(t)\int_{B(t)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq g^{\prime}(t)\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi+g^{\prime}(t)\>\!\alpha\int_{B(t)}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi.

Since
0<P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0})<\infty
and
r^{*}(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0})=1+r^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{0})
, it follows from the limit definition that there exist
\rho_{0}>0
sufficiently small and
c_{2}>0
such that for
0<\rho<\rho_{0}
,

\rho^{-2r-n}\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq c_{2},\quad\text{ and }\quad\rho^{-2(r+1)-n}\int_{B(t)}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq c_{2}.

Here,
\rho_{0}
and
c_{2}
depend on
P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0})
. Thus:

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[g(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}\right)|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\right]\leq c_{2}\>\!g^{\prime}(t)\rho(t)^{2r+n}+\alpha\>\!c_{2}\>\!g^{\prime}(t)\rho(t)^{2(r+1)+n}.

Taking
g(t)=b^{-k}(t+b)^{k}
, where
k>1+r+\frac{n}{2}
and
b>\frac{k\alpha}{2\mu}
, we have
g(0)=1
,
g(t)\geq 1
,
g^{\prime}(t)>0
,
2\mu g(t)>\alpha g^{\prime}(t)
, and
\rho(t)=(\frac{k}{2\mu})^{\frac{1}{2}}(t+a)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, where
a=b-\frac{k\alpha}{2\mu}
(
0<a<b
). Therefore:

\displaystyle g(t)\cdot(2\pi)^{n}\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g(0)\cdot(2\pi)^{n}\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(s)\rho(s)^{2r+n}\,ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\alpha\>\!c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(s)\rho(s)^{2(r+1)+n}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+C(t+b)^{k-(r+\frac{n}{2})}+C(t+b)^{k-(r+1+\frac{n}{2})},

where
C=C(\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\alpha,c_{2},\mu,a,b,k,r,n)>0
, and hence

\displaystyle\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+b)^{-k}+C(t+b)^{-(r+\frac{n}{2})}+C(t+b)^{-(r+1+\frac{n}{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+b)^{-(r+\frac{n}{2})},

for all
t\geq 0
. So,

\bigl\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\bigr\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq C_{2}(t+C_{3})^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{*}\right)},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0.

Therefore, we conclude that if
-\frac{n}{2}<r^{*}<\infty
, then there exist constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{3}>0
such that

C_{1}(t+1)^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{*}\right)}\leq\bigl\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\bigr\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq C_{2}(t+C_{3})^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{*}\right)},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0,

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0}),\alpha,\mu,r^{*},n)
,
C_{2}=C_{2}(P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0}),\alpha,\mu,r^{*},n,\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})
, and
C_{3}=C_{3}(\alpha,\mu,r^{*},n)
. This proves the theorem.
∎

3
Decay characterization of solutions

We start with the proof of a particularly useful lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be a weak solution to (
3
). Then for all
t\geq 0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
we have

\left|\mathcal{F}\left\{\operatorname{rot}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}\right\}\!(\xi,t)\right|\leq C\!\left(|\xi|+|\xi|^{2}\right)\!\bigl\|\mathbf{u}(t)\bigr\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
. In particular,

\left|\mathcal{F}\left\{\operatorname{rot}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}\right\}\!(\xi,t)\right|\leq C(|\xi|+|\xi|^{2}),

where
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
and
\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
.

Proof.

Multiplying (
3
) by
\mathbf{u}
in
L^{2}
and using the incompressibility condition, we obtain the energy identity

\frac{d}{dt}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=-2\mu\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

(15)

As a consequence,

\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0.

(16)

Note that, from (
16
), we have
\mathbf{u}(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for all
t\geq 0
, hence
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(t)
is well-defined. Using standard vector identities we obtain

\operatorname{rot}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}=(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}-\nabla\!\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{2}\right)-\alpha\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}.

Using that
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}=0
and integrating by parts, we find

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}\left\{(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\right\}(\xi,t)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathcal{F}\left\{\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})\right\}(\xi,t)\right|=\left|i\xi\cdot\mathcal{F}\{\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{1}}|\xi|\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}|\xi|,

(17)

and

\left|\mathcal{F}\left\{\nabla\!\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{2}\right)\right\}(\xi,t)\right|=\left|i\mathcal{F}\left\{\frac{|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{2}\right\}\xi\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\||\mathbf{u}|^{2}\|_{L^{1}}|\xi|=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}|\xi|.

(18)

Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle[\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}]_{j}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\Delta\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}\cdot D_{j}\Delta\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}(D_{l}^{2}D_{k}u_{j})u_{k}-\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}(D_{l}^{2}D_{j}u_{k})u_{k}.

(19)

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left\{[\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}]_{j}\right\}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}(D_{l}^{2}D_{j}u_{k})u_{k}\,dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\,\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}(D_{l}^{2}D_{j}u_{k})u_{k}\,dx=:\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}.

It is easy to deduce through integration by parts that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\xi_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}u_{k}D_{l}^{2}u_{j}\,dx=-\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\xi_{k}\xi_{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}u_{k}D_{l}u_{j}\,dx

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\xi_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}D_{l}u_{k}D_{l}u_{j}\,dx,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\xi_{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}u_{k}D_{l}D_{j}u_{k}\,dx+\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}D_{l}u_{k}D_{l}D_{j}u_{k}\,dx

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\xi_{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}u_{k}D_{l}D_{j}u_{k}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}D_{j}[(D_{l}u_{k})^{2}]\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\xi_{l}\xi_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}u_{k}D_{l}u_{k}\,dx+i\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\xi_{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}D_{j}u_{k}D_{l}u_{k}\,dx\}

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\xi_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}(D_{l}u_{k})^{2}\,dx.

Then we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle((\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u})_{j})|(\xi,t)\leq\left|\mathcal{I}_{1}\right|+\left|\mathcal{I}_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}|\xi_{k}||\xi_{l}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{k}||D_{l}u_{j}|\,dx+\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}|\xi_{k}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D_{l}u_{k}||D_{l}u_{j}|\,dx

\displaystyle+\,\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}|\xi_{l}||\xi_{j}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{k}||D_{l}u_{k}|\,dx+\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}|\xi_{l}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D_{j}u_{k}||D_{l}u_{k}|\,dx

\displaystyle+\,\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}|\xi_{j}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D_{l}u_{k}|^{2}\,dx,

and thus

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}\left\{\alpha\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}\right\}\!(\xi,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\alpha|\xi|^{2}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{5\alpha}{2}|\xi|\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha|\xi|^{2}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\!\left(\frac{5}{2}|\xi|+|\xi|^{2}\right)\!\|\nabla\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

In view of (
17
), (
18
), and (
20
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

We have now the following estimate in frequency space.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be a weak solution to (
3
). Then

|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\leq 2e^{2tM_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}+C(|\xi|^{2}+|\xi|^{4})\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{2}\!,

for all
t\geq 0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
M_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)
is given by (
13
). Here,
C>0
is a constant that depends only on
\alpha
.

Proof.

Taking the Fourier transform of (
3
), we obtain

\begin{cases}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{t}+\alpha|\xi|^{2}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{t}+\mu|\xi|^{2}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}+i\xi\widehat{p}=-\mathscr{F}\!\left\{\operatorname{rot}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}\right\}\!(\xi,t)=:G(\xi,t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr i\xi\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)=0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad t>0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,0)=\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

From this it follows that

(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})(\partial_{t}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}|^{2})+2\mu|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\leq 2|G(\xi,t)||\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|,

and thus

\displaystyle|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{tM_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)M_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)}\>\!\frac{|G(\xi,s)|}{\sqrt{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}\,}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{tM_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)M_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)}\>\!|G(\xi,s)|\,ds,

where
M_{\alpha,\mu}
is given by (
13
). By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\bigl|G(\xi,t)\bigr|\leq C\!\left(|\xi|+|\xi|^{2}\right)\!\bigl\|\mathbf{u}(t)\bigr\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

for all
t\geq 0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
. This completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

We present now the proof of the first main result of this paper.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We will again use the Fourier splitting method. We define the closed ball centered at the origin with radius
\rho(t)

B(t):=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,;\,|\xi|\leq\rho(t)\right\}\!,

where

\rho(t):=\left(\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{2\mu g(t)-\alpha g^{\prime}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!,

and
g
is a differentiable function on
(0,\infty)
satisfying

g(0)=1,\quad g(t)\geq 1,\quad g^{\prime}(t)>0,\quad\text{and}\quad 2\mu g(t)>\alpha g^{\prime}(t),\quad\,\,\forall\;t>0.

Using Plancherel’s Theorem, it follows from the energy identity (
15
) that

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\widehat{\nabla\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=-2\mu\|\widehat{\nabla\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

i.e.,

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\,d\xi\right)=-2\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\,d\xi.

Multiplying the above identity by
g(t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(g(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\,d\xi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\,2\mu g(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\,d\xi.

From the definition of
B(t)
, we have

\frac{2\mu g(t)|\xi|^{2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}>g^{\prime}(t),\quad\,\,\forall\;\xi\in B(t)^{c}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{3}\,\backslash\,B(t).

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\!\left(g(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\right)\leq g^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle-2\mu g(t)\int_{B(t)^{c}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq g^{\prime}(t)\int_{B(t)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi,

that is,

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(g(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\right)\leq g^{\prime}(t)\int_{B(t)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi.

Integrating the above inequality with respect to time, we obtain

g(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq I_{1}+I_{2}(t),

(21)

where

I_{1}:=g(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi,

and

I_{2}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\left(g^{\prime}(s)\int_{B(s)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,s)|^{2}\,d\xi\right)\!ds.

Note that, by Plancherel’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{\nabla\mathbf{u}}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\widehat{\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\!\left(\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=(2\pi)^{3}\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

Therefore,

I_{1}=g(0)\cdot(2\pi)^{3}\>\!\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=(2\pi)^{3}\>\!\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},

(22)

since
g(0)=1
. Next, we will estimate
I_{2}(t)
. By the Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(s)\!\left(\int_{B(s)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})e^{2sM_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\right)\!ds

\displaystyle+\,C\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(s)\!\left(\int_{B(s)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})(|\xi|^{2}+|\xi|^{4})\!\left(\int_{0}^{s}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{2}d\xi\right)\!ds

\displaystyle=:I_{2,1}(t)+I_{2,2}(t).

Initially, we observe that, by Plancherel

\displaystyle\int_{B(s)}

\displaystyle(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})e^{2sM_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=\int_{B(s)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,s)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,s)|^{2}\,d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,s)|^{2}\,d\xi+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,s)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,s)|^{2}\,d\xi+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,s)|^{2}\,d\xi=\|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\widehat{\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\left(\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=(2\pi)^{3}\>\!\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

Therefore, by Theorem
2.4
, we have

I_{2,1}(t)\leq 2(2\pi)^{3}\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(s)\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\leq 16\pi^{3}\>\!C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(s)\!\left(s+C_{3}\right)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right)}\,ds.

(23)

On the other hand, using polar coordinates, it follows that

I_{2,2}(t)\leq C\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(s)(\rho(s)^{5}+\rho(s)^{7}+\rho(s)^{9})ds\right)\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{2}.

(24)

For a fixed
r^{*}\in(-\frac{3}{2},\infty)
, we choose

g(t)=\widetilde{b}^{-m}(t+\widetilde{b})^{m},\quad\text{where}\quad m>\max\left\{\frac{5}{2},\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right\}\!,\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{b}\geq 1+\frac{\alpha\>\!m}{2\mu}.

This way,

\rho(t)=\left(\frac{m}{2\mu}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\left(t+\widetilde{a}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\text{where}\quad\widetilde{a}:=\widetilde{b}-\frac{\alpha\>\!m}{2\mu}\geq 1.

(25)

From this, it follows from (
23
) and (
24
), respectively, that

\displaystyle I_{2,1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 16\pi^{3}\>\!C_{2}\widetilde{b}^{-m}\>\!m\int_{0}^{t}(s+\widetilde{b})^{m-1}(s+C_{3})^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right)}\,ds

(26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\int_{0}^{t}(s+C_{5})^{m-1-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right)}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}(t+C_{5})^{m-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right)},

and

\displaystyle I_{2,2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{6}\int_{0}^{t}(s+\widetilde{b})^{m-1}\!\left((s+\widetilde{a})^{-\frac{5}{2}}+(s+\widetilde{a})^{-\frac{7}{2}}+(s+\widetilde{a})^{-\frac{9}{2}}\right)\!ds

(27)

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{7}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(s+C_{8})^{m-1-\frac{5}{2}}ds\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{7}(t+C_{8})^{m-\frac{5}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{2}\!.

Hence, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi=(2\pi)^{3}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},

it follows from (
21
), (
22
), (
26
) and (
27
) that

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}g(t)\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{3}\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+C_{4}(t+C_{5})^{m-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}

\displaystyle+C_{7}(t+C_{8})^{m-\frac{5}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{2}.

(28)

Initially, using (
16
), we obtain

g(t)\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+C_{4}(t+C_{5})^{m-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}+\,C_{7}(t+C_{8})^{m-\frac{5}{2}+2},

and consequently

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-m}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(29)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1}(t+\widetilde{C}_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{3}{2}+r^{*},\frac{1}{2}\right\}},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0,

since
g(t)=\widetilde{b}^{-m}(t+\widetilde{b})^{m}
. This estimate preliminary can be further improved, as we’ll show below. Suppose that
\min\!\left\{\frac{3}{2}+r^{*},\frac{1}{2}\right\}=\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}
, i.e.,
-\frac{3}{2}<r^{*}\leq-1
. In this case, we have from (
29
) that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1}^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(s+\widetilde{C}_{2})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}\,ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1}^{2}\left((t+\widetilde{C}_{2})^{-(\frac{1}{2}+r^{*})}\right)^{2}=\widetilde{C}_{1}^{2}(t+\widetilde{C}_{2})^{-(1+2r^{*})}.

Therefore, remembering the choice of
g
, we have by (
28
), that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-m}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{5}{2}+1+2r^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{7}{2}+2r^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}.

On the other hand, if
\min\!\left\{\frac{3}{2}+r^{*},\frac{1}{2}\right\}=\frac{1}{2}
, i.e.,
r^{*}\geq-1
, it follows by (
29
) that

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{2}\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(s+\widetilde{C}_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\right)^{2}\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}^{2}(t+\widetilde{C}_{2}).

Again from (
28
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-m}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right)}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{5}{2}+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right)}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right),},\text{ if }-1\leq r^{*}\leq 0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{3}{2}},\text{ if }r^{*}\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, from the above considerations, we have

\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq K_{1}(t+K_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{3}{2}+r^{*},\frac{3}{2}\right\}},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0.

(31)

It is important to note that
K_{2}\geq 1
(see (
25
)). We will apply the “bootstrap” argument once more. Suppose that
\min\!\left\{\frac{3}{2}+r^{*},\frac{3}{2}\right\}=\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}
, i.e.,
r^{*}\leq 0
.
With (
31
) in mind, we will divide the analysis into three cases:

(i) If
-\frac{3}{2}<r^{*}<-\frac{1}{2}
, we then have

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{2}\leq K_{1}^{2}(t+K_{2})^{-(1+2r^{*})}.

(ii) If
r^{*}=-\frac{1}{2}
, we then have (since
K_{2}\geq 1
)

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{2}\leq K_{1}^{2}\ln^{2}(t+K_{2}).

(iii) If
-\frac{1}{2}<r^{*}\leq 0
, we then have

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{2}\leq C.

So, by (
28
) and by the choice of
g
:

(i) If
-\frac{3}{2}<r^{*}<-\frac{1}{2}
, then

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-m}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{5}{2}-1-2r^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{7}{2}+2r^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}.

(ii) If
r^{*}=-\frac{1}{2}
, then

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-m}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{5}{2}}\ln^{2}(t+\widetilde{C})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-1}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{5}{2}}\ln^{2}(t+\widetilde{C})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\tilde{C})^{-1}=C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}.

(iii) If
-\frac{1}{2}<r^{*}\leq 0
, then

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-m}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{5}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}.

On the other hand, if
\min\!\left\{\frac{3}{2}+r^{*},\frac{3}{2}\right\}=\frac{3}{2}
, i.e.,
r^{*}\geq 0
, then, by (
31
):

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{2}\leq C,

and, again by (
28
), we find

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-m}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{5}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*})}+C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{5}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right),},\text{ if }0\leq r^{*}\leq 1,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr C(t+\widetilde{C})^{-\frac{5}{2}},\text{ if }r^{*}\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

This ends the proof of the theorem.
∎

4
Asymptotic equivalence

The estimate from Theorem
1.1
enables us to determine the decay rate for the difference between a solution
\mathbf{u}
of (
3
) and the solution
\overline{\mathbf{u}}
to the linear part (
11
) with the same initial data. However, due to the presence of the regularizing term
-\alpha\>\!\partial_{t}\Delta\mathbf{u}
in (
3
), we need more regularity for the initial data.

Before proving Theorem
1.2
, we need the following auxiliary result for the derivatives of the solutions of the linear problem.

Proposition 4.1

(Decay of the derivatives of linear part)
.

Let
\overline{\mathbf{u}}
be the solution to the linear part (
11
), with
D^{m}\>\!\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}_{0}=0
. Then,

\bigl\|D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\bigr\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{2}(t+\widetilde{C}_{3})^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}+m\right)},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0.

Proof.

Applying the derivation operator
D^{m}
to (
11
), we obtain

\partial_{t}(D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}-\alpha\Delta D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}})-\mu\Delta D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}=0.

Here, again we will use the Fourier splitting method. Multiplying (dot product) the equation above by
D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}
and integrating in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we find

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\|D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|D^{m+1}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\mu\|D^{m+1}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

Using Plancherel, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\widehat{D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\mu\|\widehat{D^{m+1}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{2m+2}\>\!|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi.

Multiplying the above inequality by
g(t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[g(t)\|\widehat{D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\right]=g^{\prime}(t)\|\widehat{D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}-2\mu g(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{2m+2}|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq g^{\prime}(t)\|\widehat{D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}-2\mu g(t)\int_{B(t)^{c}}|\xi|^{2m+2}|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi,

and consequently

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[g(t)\|\widehat{D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\xi|^{2m}|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\,2\mu g(t)\int_{B(t)^{c}}\frac{|\xi|^{2m+2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi.

For
\xi\in B(t)^{c}
, we have

\frac{2\mu g(t)|\xi|^{2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}>g^{\prime}(t).

Thus, considering (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[g(t)\|\widehat{D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)\int_{B(t)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\xi|^{2m}|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)\int_{B(t)}|\xi|^{2m}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\alpha\>\!g^{\prime}(t)\int_{B(t)}|\xi|^{2(m+1)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi.

Since

0<P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0})<\infty,\quad r^{*}(D^{m}\mathbf{u}_{0})=m+r^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{0}),\quad\text{and}\quad r^{*}(D^{m+1}\mathbf{u}_{0})=m+1+r^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{0}),

it follows from the definition of the limit that there exist
\widetilde{\rho}_{0}>0
sufficiently small and
\widetilde{c}>0
such that for
0<\rho<\widetilde{\rho}_{0}
:

\rho^{-2(r+m)-3}\int_{B(t)}|\xi|^{2m}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\widetilde{c},

and

\rho^{-2(r+m+1)-3}\int_{B(t)}|\xi|^{2(m+1)}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\widetilde{c}.

Here,
\widetilde{\rho}_{0}
and
\widetilde{c}
depend on
P_{r}(\mathbf{u}_{0})
. Thus:

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[g(t)\|\widehat{D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\right]\leq\widetilde{c}\>\!g^{\prime}(t)\rho(t)^{2(r+m)+3}+\alpha\>\!\widetilde{c}\>\!g^{\prime}(t)\rho(t)^{2(r+m+1)+3}.

By Plancherel’s theorem, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[g(t)\|D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-3}\>\!\widetilde{c}\>\!g^{\prime}(t)\rho(t)^{2(r+m)+3}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-3}\>\!\alpha\>\!\widetilde{c}\>\!g^{\prime}(t)\rho(t)^{2(r+m+1)+3}.

Now, taking
g(t)=b^{-k}(t+b)^{k}
, where
k>1+r+m+\frac{3}{2}
and
b>\frac{k\>\!\alpha}{2\mu}
, we have
g(0)=1
,
g(t)\geq 1
,
g^{\prime}(t)>0
,
2\mu g(t)>\alpha g^{\prime}(t)
, and

\rho(t)=\left(\frac{k}{2\mu}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(t+a)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where
a=b-\displaystyle\frac{k\>\!\alpha}{2\mu}
(
0<a<b
). Therefore, integrating the last differential inequality above over
(0,t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle g(t)\|D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g(0)\|D^{m}\>\!\mathbf{u}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+(2\pi)^{-3}\>\!\widetilde{c}\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(s)\rho(s)^{2(r+m)+3}\,ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-3}\>\!\alpha\>\!\widetilde{c}\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(s)\rho(s)^{2(r+m+1)+3}\,ds.

Since
g(0)=1
and
D^{m}\>\!\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it follows that

g(t)\|D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C+C(t+b)^{k-(r+m+\frac{3}{2})}+C(t+b)^{k-(r+m+1+\frac{3}{2})},

and hence

\displaystyle\|D^{m}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+b)^{-k}+C(t+b)^{-(r+m+\frac{3}{2})}+C(t+b)^{-(r+m+\frac{5}{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+b)^{-(r+m+\frac{3}{2})},\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0.

This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

As we will see in the proof of the next theorem, in addition to using Theorem
1.1
, we will use Proposition
4.1
with
m=1
and
m=3
. In other words, we need
\mathbf{u}_{0}
,
\nabla\>\!\mathbf{u}_{0}
,
D^{3}\>\!\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. This means that we must have
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. That is why we increase the regularity of the initial data in the Theorem
1.2
.

Now, we are in position to establish the proof of the asymptotic equivalence result.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\mathbf{w}:=\mathbf{u}-\overline{\mathbf{u}}
, where
\overline{\mathbf{u}}
is the solution to the linear part (
11
) with the same initial data
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty)
, we have

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}(\mathbf{w}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{w})-\mu\Delta\mathbf{w}+\text{rot}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}+\nabla p={\boldsymbol{0}},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\text{div}\,\mathbf{w}=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\mathbf{w}(0)={\boldsymbol{0}}.\end{cases}

(33)

Applying the Fourier transform, we find

\begin{cases}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{t}+\mu|\xi|^{2}\widehat{\mathbf{w}}+i\xi\widehat{p}=-\mathscr{F}\!\left\{\operatorname{rot}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}\right\}\!(\xi,t)=:G(\xi,t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr i\xi\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(\xi,t)=0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad t>0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(0)={\boldsymbol{0}}.\end{cases}

Since
\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(0)={\boldsymbol{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle|\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(\xi,t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)M_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi)}\>\!\frac{|G(\xi,s)|}{\sqrt{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}\,}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|G(\xi,s)|\,ds,

where

M_{\alpha,\mu}(\xi):=-\frac{\mu|\xi|^{2}}{1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}.

Thus, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that

\bigl|\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(\xi,t)\bigr|\leq C\!\left(|\xi|+|\xi|^{2}\right)\!\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\|\mathbf{u}(s)\bigr\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\,ds,\quad\,\,\forall\;t\geq 0\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(34)

where
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
. On the other hand, it follows from (
33
) that:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\|\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\right)+\mu\|\nabla\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\langle\operatorname{rot}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u},\overline{\mathbf{u}}\>\!\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Since

\operatorname{rot}(\mathbf{u}-\alpha\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u}=(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}-\nabla\!\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{2}\right)-\alpha\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u},

we have, due to the incompressibility conditions
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}=\operatorname{div}\overline{\mathbf{u}}=0
, that

\displaystyle\langle(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u},\overline{\mathbf{u}}\>\!\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\langle(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\overline{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{u}\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|^{\frac{1}{4}}_{L^{2}}\|D^{3}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|^{\frac{3}{4}}_{L^{2}}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|^{\frac{1}{4}}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|D^{3}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|^{\frac{3}{4}}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},

also that

-\left\langle\nabla\!\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{2}\right),\overline{\mathbf{u}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\frac{|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{2},\text{div}\,\overline{\mathbf{u}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,

and

\displaystyle-\alpha\langle\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u},\overline{\mathbf{u}}\>\!\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\langle\mathbf{u}\times\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u}),\overline{\mathbf{u}}\>\!\rangle_{L^{2}}=\alpha\langle\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{u}\times\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u})\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\langle\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\times\mathbf{u},\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u})\rangle_{L^{2}}=\alpha\langle\operatorname{rot}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}\times\mathbf{u}),\Delta\mathbf{u}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\langle(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\overline{\mathbf{u}}-(\overline{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u},\Delta\mathbf{u}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\left(2\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|D^{2}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H_{\alpha}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\sqrt{\alpha\>\!}\>\!\|D^{2}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H_{\alpha}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

Since

\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{3}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}},

and

\|D^{2}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|D^{2}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{4}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}},

it follows that

\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{3}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3}{4}}

and

\sqrt{\alpha\>\!}\>\!\|D^{2}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{3}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3}{4}}.

From these estimates, it follows that

-\alpha\langle\operatorname{rot}(\Delta\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{u},\overline{\mathbf{u}}\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq 3\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{3}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3}{4}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},

and therefore

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\|\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\right)+2\mu\|\nabla\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 8\|\nabla\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{3}\>\!\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3}{4}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

Now, notice that, due to Main Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
4.1
, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\|\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\right)\leq-2\mu\|\nabla\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+H(t),

where

H(t):=C_{1}(t+C_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{7}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{9}{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{*}\right\}}.

Multiplying the above inequality by
g(t)
and working as before, we get

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[g(t)\|\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\right]\leq(2\pi)^{-3}g^{\prime}(t)\int_{B(t)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi+g(t)H(t).

From (
34
), we infer

\int_{B(t)}(1+\alpha|\xi|^{2})|\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq C\!\left[\rho(t)^{5}+\rho(t)^{7}+\rho(t)^{9}\right]\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{2}\!.

Taking
g(t)=C_{2}^{-m}(t+C_{2})^{m}
, with

C_{2}\geq 1+\frac{\alpha m}{2\mu},\quad\text{ and }\quad m>\max\!\left\{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{7}{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{*},\frac{5}{2}\right\}\!,

it follows that

\rho(t)=\left(\frac{m}{2\mu}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!(t+\widetilde{C}_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where
\widetilde{C}_{2}=C_{2}-\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\>\!m}{2\mu}\geq 1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[g(t)\|\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{m-\frac{7}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\,C(t+C_{2})^{m-\min\left\{\frac{7}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{9}{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{*}\right\}}.

Integrating with respect to time, we find

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(t+C_{2})^{-\frac{5}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+C(t+C_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{7}{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{*}\right\}}.

Next, keeping Theorem
1.1
in mind, we will divide the study into three cases:

(i) If
-\frac{3}{2}<r^{*}<-\frac{1}{2}
, then

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{2}\leq C(t+C_{2})^{-(1+2r^{*})}.

(ii) If
r^{*}=-\frac{1}{2}
, then

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{2}\leq C_{1}^{2}\ln^{2}(t+C_{2}),\quad\text{ since }\quad C_{2}\geq 1.

(iii) If
r^{*}>-\frac{1}{2}
, then

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{2}\leq C.

Therefore:

(i) If
-\frac{3}{2}<r^{*}<-\frac{1}{2}
, we then have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\frac{5}{2}}(t+C_{2})^{-(1+2r^{*})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{7}{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{*}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\left(\frac{7}{2}+2r^{*}\right)}+C(t+C_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{7}{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{*}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{7}{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{*}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\left(\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*}\right)}.

(ii) If
r^{*}=-\frac{1}{2}
, we then have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\frac{5}{2}}\ln^{2}(t+C_{2})+C(t+C_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{7}{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{*}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\frac{5}{2}}\ln^{2}(t+C_{2})+C(t+C_{2})^{-\frac{7}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\frac{7}{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\left(\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*}\right)}.

(iii) If
r^{*}>-\frac{1}{2}
, we then have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\frac{5}{2}}+C(t+C_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{7}{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{*}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{7}{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{*},\frac{5}{2}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t+C_{2})^{-\min\left\{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{3}{2}r^{*},\frac{5}{2}\right\}}.

Combining all these cases, we obtain the desired result.
∎

To finish, let us prove Corollary
1.1
.

Proof of Corollary

.

We just need to observe that, by using the triangular inequality, we get

\|\mathbf{{u}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\|\mathbf{\bar{u}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}-\|\mathbf{{u}}(t)-\mathbf{\bar{u}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

Now, we can conclude by observing that by Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
lead to the desired lower bound only when the decay of linear part is slower than that of the difference.

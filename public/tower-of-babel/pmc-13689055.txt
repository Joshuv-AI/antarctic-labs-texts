Genome assembly and annotation of two clinical Naegleria fowleri isolates from Costa Rica
Authors: Nina Allouch, Lissette Retana Moreira, Nalhyte Aurago, Elizabeth Abrahams Sandí, Isabel Marcelino
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHere, we report the genome assembly and annotation of two clinical isolates of Naegleria fowleri from primary amebic meningoencephalitis cases in Costa Rica in 2020 using Illumina sequencing. Each assembly comprised 37 reference-guided scaffolds, with genome sizes of approximately 28 Mbp and a GC content of 36.87%.

ANNOUNCEMENT

The free-living amoeba Naegleria fowleri (supergroup Excavata; phylum Percolozoa; class Heterolobosea) is distributed worldwide in freshwater and soil environments and causes primary amebic meningoencephalitis (PAM), which is highly fatal (>97%) (1). Although a genus-level Naegleria pangenome has provided important structural and functional insights into the diversity and adaptability of these free-living amoebae (2), the pathogenicity and biology of N. fowleri remain incompletely understood. Additional close-to-complete assemblies from clinical PAM cases, particularly from Central America, are needed to better represent pathogenic lineages. Here, we assembled and annotated two clinical isolates obtained in 2020 from PAM cases in the Guanacaste and Limón provinces of Costa Rica (3, 4).

N. fowleri Guanacaste (MT090627; NF_Guanacaste) and N. fowleri Limón (MT210902; NF_Limón) were isolated from cerebrospinal fluid and maintained axenically in 2% casein hydrolysate at 37°C (3, 4). Genomic DNA was extracted from approximately 1 × 10⁷ trophozoites using the QIAamp DNA Mini Kit with RNase A treatment, quantified with NanoDrop, and sequenced (2 × 150 bp) on a NextSeq 500 system with Nextera XT libraries at the Biomics Core Facility (Institut Pasteur, France). Reads were assessed with FastQC v0.12.1 (5), trimmed with Cutadapt v5.0 (6), and filtered with Trimmomatic v0.39 (7) (LEADING:5, TRAILING:5, SLIDINGWINDOW:4:20, and MINLEN:36). Default parameters were used except where otherwise noted.

De novo genome assembly was performed with SPAdes v4.0.0 (8) and evaluated with QUAST v5.3.0 (9) and BUSCO v5.7.1, using eukaryota_odb10 (255 markers) in genome mode (10). Contigs were scaffolded with RagTag v2.1.0 using the scaffold command against the N. fowleri TY reference genome (NF_TY) (11). Final coverage was estimated by mapping Illumina reads to each reference-guided assembly with BWA-MEM v0.7.18 and calculating mean depth with samtools. Repeats were modeled with RepeatModeler v2.0.5 and masked with RepeatMasker v4.1.6 using the Dfam database before MAKER v2.31.11 gene prediction, which followed previously described procedures (2). Protein-coding genes were predicted using evidence-based alignments, the ab initio predictors SNAP and AUGUSTUS, genome-guided transcript assemblies from public N. fowleri RNA-seq data (ENA project PRJNA642022; NF1_LV and NF45_HV strains from Guadeloupe) (12), and Swiss-Prot proteins as evidence.

Annotation completeness was assessed using BUSCO in transcriptome mode on MAKER-predicted transcripts and using Annotation Edit Distance (AED). AED scores were calculated using the AED_cdf_generator.pl script (13); gene models with AED ≤0.5 represented 98.0% and 97.8% of NF_Guanacaste and NF_Limón, respectively. Shared and strain-specific genes were identified using MAKER-predicted protein sequences with the MMseqs2 easy-rbh workflow with default parameters (14, 15).

Final reference-guided draft assemblies comprised 37 scaffolds, with genome sizes of 27.95 and 27.97 Mbp, guanine-cytosine (GC) contents of 36.87%, and mean sequencing depths of 341× and 270× for NF_Guanacaste and NF_Limón, respectively. Scaffold ends were not determined to be complete. Gene prediction identified 9,082 and 8,920 protein-coding genes in NF_Guanacaste and NF_Limón, respectively. BUSCO completeness was nearly 88% in genome mode and nearly 80% in annotation transcriptome mode (Table 1). Comparative analysis identified 8,306 shared genes, with 714 genes unique to NF_Guanacaste and 542 unique to NF_Limón.

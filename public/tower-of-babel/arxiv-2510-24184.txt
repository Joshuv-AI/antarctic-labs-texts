Spectral-Geometric Deformations of Function Algebras on Manifolds
Amandip Sangha

Abstract
We propose a novel method for deforming the algebra of smooth functions on a compact Riemannian manifold based on spectral data rather than group actions or Poisson structures. The deformation is defined through spectral coefficients of the Laplacian and a weight function satisfying a fusion-type cocycle condition, producing a noncommutative product that depends intrinsically on Laplacian spectrum of the manifold. We develop the analytic and algebraic foundations of this construction, establishing associativity, continuity, and the existence of a group structure on admissible weights. We show that the construction is functorial with respect to spectrum-preserving maps of manifolds. This spectral–geometric deformation quantization approach provides a fully intrinsic analytic model of noncommutative geometry based solely on spectral data of the manifold.



1
Introduction

The deformation of commutative function algebras
C^{\infty}(M)
has long been a central theme in noncommutative geometry and mathematical physics. Its origins lie in the search for a systematic passage from classical to quantum mechanics. This quest for an elegant route from classical to quantum mechanics reflects one of the central aims of twentieth-century physics: to understand how the laws governing macroscopic systems formulated by Newton and Hamilton, give way to the fundamentally probabilistic world of atomic and subatomic phenomena. From the early formulations of quantization by Planck and Bohr to the canonical structures introduced by Heisenberg and Schrödinger, physicists sought a coherent mathematical bridge between the deterministic geometry of classical mechanics and the operator-based formalism of quantum theory. Establishing this correspondence is crucial not only for conceptual continuity but also for developing quantization schemes that faithfully reproduce classical limits.
Over the past five decades, several distinct paradigms of deformation quantization have emerged, each emphasizing different structural data—symmetry, Poisson geometry, or operator theory. The present paper introduces a new
spectral–geometric
deformation, in which the intrinsic spectral data of the Laplace–Beltrami operator serve as the organizing principle. Before describing our construction, we review the historical development of deformation quantization.

1.1
Historical background

The notion of deformation quantization arose as a systematic attempt to pass from classical to quantum mechanics by deforming the commutative algebra of observables rather than replacing functions by operators. Early ideas go back to Weyl and to the phase-space formulations of Groenewold and Moyal
[
1
,
2
]
, but a coherent mathematical framework was laid out in the 1970s by Bayen–Flato–Fronsdal–Lichnerowicz–Sternheimer
[
3
,
4
]
. In their formulation, quantization appears as a continuous deformation of the pointwise product on a Poisson (in particular, symplectic) manifold:

f\star g\;=\;fg\;+\;\frac{i\hbar}{2}\{f,g\}\;+\;O(\hbar^{2}),

so that the Poisson bracket controls the first quantum correction and the classical limit is recovered as
\hbar\to 0
.

Fedosov
[
5
]
then provided a global geometric construction of star products on any symplectic manifold via a flat connection on the Weyl bundle, while Kontsevich
[
6
]
proved that every Poisson manifold admits a formal deformation, unique up to gauge equivalence, given by a universal graph-weighted formula (the formality theorem). In parallel with these formal approaches, Rieffel
[
7
]
developed an analytic (“strict”) deformation theory for
C^{*}
-algebras using strongly continuous actions of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and oscillatory integrals. In the flat case this recovers the Moyal product on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,

(f\star_{\Theta}g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}e^{\,2\pi i\,\xi\cdot(x-y)}\,f\!\left(x-\tfrac{1}{2}\Theta\xi\right)\,g(x+y)\,d\xi\,dy,

for a constant skew-symmetric matrix
\Theta
, and more generally his deformation for an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-action
\alpha
may be written abstractly as

a\times_{J}b\;=\;\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\alpha_{x}(a)\,\alpha_{y}(b)\,e^{2\pi i\,x\cdot Jy}\,dx\,dy,

where
J
is a fixed skew form. The
C^{*}
-completions yield standard examples such as the noncommutative torus and the Moyal plane. Connes and Landi
[
8
]
further showed that, in the presence of an isometric torus action, one can construct
isospectral
deformations of spectral triples that preserve the Dirac spectrum.

A third analytic viewpoint arises from
Drinfeld twists

[
9
]
, where deformations are induced by unitary
2
-cocycles on Hopf algebras or quantum groups. Here the deformation acts at the level of tensor categories of representations (leading to quasi-Hopf and braided structures) rather than just on a single algebra, and encompasses
q
-deformations of compact Lie groups and their homogeneous spaces.

In the setting of operator algebras, Kasprzak
[
10
]
reformulated Rieffel’s
[
7
,
11
]
construction in the
Landstad
picture of crossed products by introducing a measurable 2–cocycle
\Omega
on the dual group
\widehat{G}
of an abelian group
G
.
The deformed algebra
A_{\Omega}
appears as the Landstad algebra of a
C^{*}
-crossed product
C^{*}(G)\rtimes_{\Omega}A
. This operator-algebraic framework was subsequently generalized to nonabelian and locally compact quantum groups
[
12
]
. Building on Kasprzak’s approach, Neshveyev-Tuset
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
12
]
developed a unified
2–cocycle deformation
theory for C
∗
– and von Neumann algebras acted upon by locally compact quantum groups. Their construction extends Rieffel’s deformation beyond the abelian setting, using measurable unitary 2–cocycles on the quantum dual and showing that the resulting deformations preserve key analytic and K–theoretic properties. This method covers a wide range of examples including Drinfeld twists, Rieffel-type deformations, and deformations of spectral triples.

Together, these developments established deformation quantization as a unifying bridge between geometry, algebra, and physics, linking the classical and quantum descriptions of observables through a common mathematical framework.

Other notable frameworks include:

•

the
Etingof–Kazhdan quantization
of Lie bialgebras via associators;

•

the
Berezin–Toeplitz
quantization of Kähler manifolds, where deformations arise from Toeplitz operators on holomorphic sections;

•

warped convolution
[
15
]
deformations in algebraic quantum field theory, where operator products are twisted by phases determined by the joint spectrum of translation generators; and

•

more recent
covariant
or
modular
deformations within locally compact quantum groups and their duals, which unify both cocycle and Rieffel-type constructions.

Taken together, these diverse perspectives reveal a common pattern: each deformation scheme relies on some auxiliary geometric structure—a Poisson tensor, a symmetry group, or a quantum group action—to introduce noncommutativity into
C^{\infty}(M)
or its operator-algebraic completions.

1.2
From symmetry to spectrum

Despite their success, existing methods presuppose extrinsic structure—such as a Poisson tensor, a symmetry group or quantum group action to introduce noncommutativity. In contrast, our construction is
intrinsically spectral
: it requires only the Laplace spectral data together with a choice of
weight

\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
satisfying a
3
–cocycle identity, and no global group action or Poisson bracket. Thus, the additional data in our approach are encoded by
\omega
, which is built from (or constrained by) the manifold’s spectral geometry rather than imposed from external symmetry. When
\omega
is chosen to restrict as a bicharacter in symmetry–rich settings such as tori or homogeneous spaces, our deformation specializes to the classical Rieffel, Connes-Landi or Kasprzak constructions, showing that the spectral framework recovers known extrinsic deformations as special instances when considering the smooth function algebra of a compact Riemannian manifold. Hence, our objective is to formulate a deformation intrinsic to the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
itself, depending only on its spectral geometry.
The Laplace–Beltrami operator encodes the full metric information through its eigenvalues and eigenfunctions; its spectrum determines the manifold up to isometry in many cases.
We construct a deformation of
C^{\infty}(M)
by twisting the
spectral fusion coefficients
—the structure constants expressing products of Laplace eigenfunctions—by a bounded weight function
\omega
satisfying an associativity (3–cocycle) identity. This analytic construction depends only on the intrinsic spectral data and yields a strict deformation even in the absence of global symmetries.

Outline of the paper

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 sets up the spectral–geometric framework and defines the deformed product via Laplace eigenbases. Section 3 establishes the main analytic and algebraic results, including associativity, continuity, and the group law on admissible weights. Section 4 describes the construction in terms of category theory. Section 5 analyzes behaviour under Riemannian isometries. Section 6 studies dependence on metric perturbations and differentiability with respect to the metric. 2
Spectral–Geometric Setup

Let (M, g) be a compact Riemannian manifold with Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
defined
by

\Delta f=\mathrm{div}_{g}(\nabla f)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\partial_{i}\!\Big(\sqrt{|g|}\,g^{ij}\partial_{j}f\Big),\quad f\in C^{\infty}(M),

where
(g^{ij})
denotes the inverse of the metric tensor
(g_{ij})
and
|g|=\det(g_{ij})
. The operator

\Delta
is elliptic, essentially self-adjoint on
C^{\infty}(M)
, and extends to a self-adjoint, non-negative
operator on
L^{2}(M)
. By the spectral theorem for self-adjoint elliptic operators on compact
manifolds,
\Delta
has a discrete spectrum
0\leq\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\to\infty
, where each eigenvalue has
finite multiplicity, and an orthonormal eigenbasis
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}\subset C^{\infty}(M)
satisfying
\Delta\phi_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}
.
For
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
, write
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}f(\alpha)\,\phi_{\alpha}
, with spectral coefficients
f(\alpha)=\langle f,\phi_{\alpha}\rangle
.
The pointwise product on the eigenbasis is given by

\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma}C_{\alpha\beta}^{\ \gamma}\phi_{\gamma},

where the
fusion coefficients
(or structure constants)
C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}
are defined by

C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}:=\langle\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta},\,\phi_{\gamma}\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}=\int_{M}\phi_{\alpha}(x)\,\phi_{\beta}(x)\,\overline{\phi}_{\gamma}(x)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}(x).

(1)

They satisfy the Hermitian symmetry

\overline{C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}}=C^{\gamma}_{\beta\alpha}.

(2)

If the eigenbasis
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}
is chosen real, which is possible for all compact
Riemannian manifolds with real Laplace–Beltrami operator, the coefficients are real
and symmetric:
C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=C^{\gamma}_{\beta\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
. As these coefficients, in eigenspaces of multiplicity greater than one, depend on the choice of eigenbasis, our deformation procedure likewise inherits this dependence. To avoid complications, we shall always assume a fixed choice of a real orthonormal eigenbasis in any setting we speak about our deformed product. In particular,

C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=\int_{M}\phi_{\alpha}\,\phi_{\beta}\,\phi_{\gamma}\,d\mathrm{vol}\in\mathbb{R},\qquad C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=C^{\gamma}_{\beta\alpha}.

Throughout,
C^{\infty}(M)
denotes
complex-valued smooth functions unless stated otherwise. We define
P:=\mathrm{span}\{\phi_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
to be the finite linear
span of eigenfunctions.

Definition 2.1

(Spectral–Geometric Deformation)
.

Let
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
be the Laplace eigenbasis introduced above, indexed by the discrete set
I
.
Let
\omega:I\times I\times I\to\mathbb{T}
be a function defined on triples

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in I^{3}
with
C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\neq 0
, satisfying

|\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)|=1,\qquad\overline{\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}=\omega(\beta,\alpha,\gamma),

(3)

for all
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
with
C_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\neq 0
.
Define a new product
\star_{\omega}
on
P\subset C^{\infty}(M)
by

(f\star_{\omega}g)(x):=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta)\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,\phi_{\gamma}(x),

(4)

for
x\in M
.

When we calculate with coefficients, we shall write

(f\star_{\omega}g)_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta)\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}.

Remark 2.2
.

For analytic control, we will assume a mild regularity condition on
\omega
, see Definition
3.1
.

Remark 2.3
.

The assumption that
M
be compact ensures that the Laplace– Beltrami operator
\Delta
has discrete
spectrum and an orthonormal basis of smooth eigenfunctions. This allows all spectral expansions to be
expressed as countable sums and makes the deformation formulas analytically
tractable. For noncompact manifolds,
\Delta
typically has continuous or mixed spectrum, and the sums in
(
4
) would have to be replaced by integrals over the spectral measure, requiring additional analytic
control (weighted Sobolev spaces, scattering theory, etc.). For these reasons, compactness is a standard and
technically convenient assumption in the first stage of the theory.

Remark 2.4
.

We require
\overline{\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}=\omega(\beta,\alpha,\gamma)
so that the deformed product will be compatible with involution, and the resulting deformed algebra to be defined later will become a
\ast
-algebra.

3
Analytic and Algebraic Foundations

Sobolev spaces and notation

Let
(M,g)
be compact and
\Delta
a selfadjoint, nonnegative, second–order elliptic operator
(e.g. the Laplace–Beltrami operator on functions). For
s\in\mathbb{R}
we define the Sobolev space

H^{s}(M)\ :=\ \mathrm{Dom}\bigl((1+\Delta)^{s/2}\bigr),

equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}}\ :=\ \|(1+\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

Equivalently, if
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}
is an orthonormal eigenbasis of
\Delta
with

\Delta\phi_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}
and
\widehat{f}(\alpha)=\langle f,\phi_{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}}
,
then

\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\ =\ \sum_{\alpha}(1+\lambda_{\alpha})^{s}\,|\widehat{f}(\alpha)|^{2},\qquad H^{s}(M)\ =\ \Bigl\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M):\sum_{\alpha}(1+\lambda_{\alpha})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\alpha)|^{2}<\infty\Bigr\}.

For
s<0
,
H^{s}(M)
is defined by duality as
\bigl(H^{-s}(M)\bigr)^{\ast}
with respect to the
L^{2}
pairing.
These norms are equivalent to the standard local Sobolev norms defined via charts and partitions of unity.
When
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
H^{s}(M)
coincides with the completion of
C^{\infty}(M)
in the norm

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\|\nabla^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}}
, and
C^{\infty}(M)=\bigcap_{s}H^{s}(M)
.
Throughout, we write
A\lesssim_{N}B
(or
A\tilde{\leq}_{N}B
)
to mean that there exists a constant
C_{N}>0
, depending only on the parameter
N
,
such that
A\leq C_{N}B
.
Similarly,
A\lesssim B
means
A\leq CB
for some absolute constant
C>0
.

This section establishes the rigorous analytic and algebraic foundations
of the spectral–geometric deformation, ensuring that all spectral expansions,
sums, and associativity statements are well defined and converge
in the smooth topology.

3.1
Analytic extension to smooth functions

We now extend the
\star_{\omega}
–product, originally defined on the finite linear span

P=\mathrm{span}\{\phi_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
, to the full algebra
C^{\infty}(M)
.
The goal is to show that the product remains well defined and continuous in the Fréchet
topology induced by the Sobolev norms.
To get the Sobolev norm bounds we need in order to extend the deformed product from the finite linear span to the closure, we pose a mild analytic requirement on the weight function.

Definition 3.1

(Log–Lipschitz spectral control on
\omega
)
.

Let
I
be the spectral index set for the Laplace eigenbasis
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{\alpha}\geq 0
.
The weight function
\omega:I^{3}\to\mathbb{T}
satisfies the
log–Lipschitz spectral control
if there exists
L\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\big|\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)-\omega(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\big|\ \leq\ L\,\Big(

\displaystyle\big|\log(1+\lambda_{\alpha})-\log(1+\lambda_{\alpha^{\prime}})\big|

(5)

\displaystyle+\big|\log(1+\lambda_{\beta})-\log(1+\lambda_{\beta^{\prime}})\big|

\displaystyle+\big|\log(1+\lambda_{\gamma})-\log(1+\lambda_{\gamma^{\prime}})\big|\Big)

for all distinct triples
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma),(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\in I^{3}
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\omega
admits partial derivatives in the spectral variables, (
5
) follows from the bound

\sup_{\lambda\in[0,\infty)^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}(1+\lambda_{i})\,|\partial_{\lambda_{i}}\omega(\lambda)|\ \leq\ L,

obtained via the mean–value theorem in the variables
t_{i}=\log(1+\lambda_{i})
.

Lemma 3.3

(Sobolev boundedness of the twisted product)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact
n
–manifold and
s>\tfrac{n}{2}
.
Assume
\omega:I^{3}\to\mathbb{T}
is unimodular, satisfies the algebraic cocycle/adjoint axioms of the paper, and obeys the log–Lipschitz control (
5
) with constant
L
.
Then the twisted product
\star_{\omega}
extends uniquely to a bounded bilinear map

H^{s}(M)\times H^{s}(M)\ \longrightarrow\ H^{s}(M),\qquad\|f\star_{\omega}g\|_{H^{s}}\ \leq\ C_{s}(1+L)\,\|f\|_{H^{s}}\,\|g\|_{H^{s}},

with
C_{s}
depending only on
s
and
(M,g)
.

Proof.

The argument follows the bilinear Littlewood–Paley estimates of
Coifman–Meyer
[
16
]
, and their manifold analogues based on spectral projection bounds as in Sogge
[
17
, Theorem 4.3.1]
, and
Grafakos–Torres
[
18
, Theorem 1]
.
For each integer
j
, define

\mathcal{A}_{j}:=\big\{\alpha\in I:\;2^{\,j-1}\leq 1+\lambda_{\alpha}\leq 2^{\,j+1}\big\},

so that
\log(1+\lambda_{\alpha})\in[j-1,j+1]
. These
\mathcal{A}_{j}
form a dyadic decomposition of the spectrum.

Choose smooth functions
\psi_{j}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,\infty))
supported in the interval
[2^{j-1},2^{j+1}]
and satisfying

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{j}(\lambda)=1
for all
\lambda>0
.
Define

P_{j}f:=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\psi_{j}(1+\lambda_{\alpha})\,\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\phi_{\alpha},

so
P_{j}
projects
f
onto the frequencies in
\mathcal{A}_{j}
. The family
\{P_{j}\}
is smooth and has finite overlap in
j
. Such a dyadic spectral partition and the equivalence of Sobolev norms

\|f\|_{H^{s}}\simeq\big(\sum_{j}2^{2js}\|P_{j}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)^{1/2}

are standard; see
Sogge
[
17
, Thm. 4.3.1]
.

For each nontrivial block
(j,k,\ell)
with overlapping supports, pick fixed representative indices

\alpha^{\ast}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
,
\beta^{\ast}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
,
\gamma^{\ast}\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
.
For
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
in this block define

\delta_{jkl}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):=\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)-\omega(\alpha^{\ast},\beta^{\ast},\gamma^{\ast}).

By the log–Lipschitz control, the localized symbol

\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
varies at most logarithmically across each
dyadic block, so the difference estimates satisfy the bilinear
Coifman–Meyer conditions.
Hence each localized bilinear form is uniformly bounded on
H^{s}
, by the
Coifman–Meyer theorem
[
16
,
19
]
and its refinement in
Grafakos–Torres
[
18
, Thm. 1]
; see also
[
20
]
.

Because
\log(1+\lambda_{\alpha})
,
\log(1+\lambda_{\beta})
, and
\log(1+\lambda_{\gamma})
vary by at most a universal
C_{0}
inside each annulus, (
5
) yields

\varepsilon:=|\delta_{jkl}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)|\leq 3C_{0}L.

By the definition of
\star_{\omega}
,

(f\star_{\omega}g)(x)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}(\alpha)\widehat{g}(\beta)\,C_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\,\phi_{\gamma}(x),

where
C_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=\langle\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta},\phi_{\gamma}\rangle
.
Inserting the above splitting
\omega=\omega(\alpha^{\ast},\beta^{\ast},\gamma^{\ast})+\delta_{jkl}
for indices in block
(j,k,\ell)
gives the exact algebraic identity

\displaystyle P_{\ell}\!\big((P_{j}f)\star_{\omega}(P_{k}g)\big)=

\displaystyle\,\omega(\alpha^{\ast},\beta^{\ast},\gamma^{\ast})\,P_{\ell}((P_{j}f)(P_{k}g))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,P_{\ell}\!\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\delta_{jkl}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{P_{j}f}(\alpha)\widehat{P_{k}g}(\beta)C_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\phi_{\gamma}.

Taking
H^{s}
–norms and using
|\delta_{jkl}|\leq\varepsilon
and unimodularity,

\|P_{\ell}((P_{j}f)\star_{\omega}(P_{k}g))\|_{H^{s}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,K_{s}\,\|P_{j}f\|_{H^{s}}\|P_{k}g\|_{H^{s}},

where
K_{s}
is the standard constant for the untwisted
H^{s}
algebra inequality:

\|uv\|_{H^{s}}\leq K_{s}\|u\|_{H^{s}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}

when
s>\tfrac{n}{2}
. Summing over
(j,k,\ell)
, and using the finite overlap of
\{P_{j}\}
, gives

\|f\star_{\omega}g\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\,K_{s}(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|g\|_{H^{s}}.

Since
\varepsilon\leq 3C_{0}L
, absorb constants into
C_{s}
to obtain

\|f\star_{\omega}g\|_{H^{s}}\leq C_{s}(1+L)\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|g\|_{H^{s}}
.
∎

Corollary 3.4

(Extension to smooth functions)
.

The bilinear map
\star_{\omega}:P\times P\to P
extends uniquely by continuity to a
jointly continuous bilinear map

\star_{\omega}:C^{\infty}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(M),

which preserves the
*
–structure:

(f\star_{\omega}g)^{*}=g^{*}\star_{\omega}f^{*}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
,
\star_{\omega}
is bounded

H^{s}(M)\times H^{s}(M)\to H^{s}(M)
for
s>\frac{n}{2}
.
Since
C^{\infty}(M)=\bigcap_{s\in\mathbb{R}}H^{s}(M)
with the projective limit topology,
the bilinear map extends continuously to
C^{\infty}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M)
.
The Hermitian symmetry

\overline{\omega}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\omega(\beta,\alpha,\gamma)

ensures that the extension respects the involution. Indeed, writing out the coefficients explicitly,

\displaystyle(f\star_{\omega}g)_{\gamma}^{*}

\displaystyle=\overline{(f\star_{\omega}g)}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in I}\overline{\omega}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\overline{C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}}\,\overline{\widehat{f}(\alpha)}\,\overline{\widehat{g}(\beta)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in I}\omega(\beta,\alpha,\gamma)\,C^{\gamma}_{\beta\alpha}\,\widehat{g}^{*}(\beta)\,\widehat{f}^{*}(\alpha)=(g^{*}\star_{\omega}f^{*})_{\gamma}.

∎

3.2
Associativity and continuity theorems

Theorem 3.5
.

The deformed product
\star_{\omega}
extends to
C^{\infty}(M)
and
is associative if and only if
\omega
satisfies the fusion
3
–cocycle identity

\sum_{\delta\in I}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\,\omega(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)\,C^{\delta}_{\alpha\beta}\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\delta\gamma}=\sum_{\delta\in I}\omega(\beta,\gamma,\delta)\,\omega(\alpha,\delta,\varepsilon)\,C^{\delta}_{\beta\gamma}\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\delta},

(6)

for all
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\varepsilon)\in I^{4}
such that the involved
fusion coefficients are nonzero.

Proof.

For
f,g,h\in P
we have

((f\star_{\omega}g)\star_{\omega}h)_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in I}\Bigg(\sum_{\delta\in I}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\,\omega(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)\,C^{\delta}_{\alpha\beta}\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\delta\gamma}\Bigg)\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta)\,\widehat{h}(\gamma).

Similarly,

(f\star_{\omega}(g\star_{\omega}h))_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in I}\Bigg(\sum_{\delta\in I}\omega(\beta,\gamma,\delta)\,\omega(\alpha,\delta,\varepsilon)\,C^{\delta}_{\beta\gamma}\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\delta}\Bigg)\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta)\,\widehat{h}(\gamma).

Hence associativity on
P
is equivalent to the identity
(
6
).
By Lemma
3.3
,
\star_{\omega}
extends continuously to

C^{\infty}(M)
, and since
P
is dense, the associativity relation extends
to all smooth functions.
∎

Hence,
(C^{\infty}(M),\star_{\omega},{}^{*})
is a Fréchet
*
–algebra
whose topology is equivalent to that of
C^{\infty}(M)
.

Theorem 3.6

(Continuity of the spectral deformation)
.

Let
\{\omega_{t}\}_{t\in I}
be a
C^{1}
family of weights with
\omega_{0}\equiv 1
, and let
\star_{\omega_{t}}
denote the corresponding deformed products. Then for every
s>\tfrac{n}{2}
:

1.

The maps
\star_{\omega_{t}}:H^{s}(M)\times H^{s}(M)\to H^{s}(M)
are uniformly bounded for
t
in compact subintervals of
I
; in particular,

\|f\star_{\omega_{t}}g\|_{H^{s}}\ \leq\ C_{s}\,\|f\|_{H^{s}}\,\|g\|_{H^{s}}\qquad\text{with }C_{s}\ \text{independent of }t\ \text{on compacts.}

2.

For all
f,g\in H^{s}(M)
,

\|\,f\star_{\omega_{t}}g-fg\,\|_{H^{s}}\ \longrightarrow\ 0\qquad\text{as }t\to 0.

Proof.

(1) This is immediate from Lemma
3.3
applied at each
t
: the hypotheses
|\omega_{t}|=1
give the same
H^{s}
-algebra bound with a constant depending only on
s
(hence uniform on compact
t
-sets).

(2) First assume
f
and
g
are finite spectral sums (equivalently, smooth with only finitely many nonzero eigenfunction coefficients). In that case

f\star_{\omega_{t}}g-fg

is a
finite
linear combination of eigenfunctions whose coefficients depend continuously on
t
and vanish at
t=0
because
\omega_{0}\equiv 1
. Hence
\|f\star_{\omega_{t}}g-fg\|_{H^{s}}\to 0
as
t\to 0
for such
f,g
.

For general
f,g\in H^{s}
choose finite spectral truncations
f_{N},g_{N}\to f,g
in
H^{s}
. Using the uniform bound from (1),

\displaystyle\|f\star_{\omega_{t}}g-fg\|_{H^{s}}\ \leq\

\displaystyle\|(f-f_{N})\star_{\omega_{t}}g\|_{H^{s}}+\|f_{N}\star_{\omega_{t}}(g-g_{N})\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|f_{N}\star_{\omega_{t}}g_{N}-f_{N}g_{N}\|_{H^{s}}+\|f_{N}g_{N}-fg\|_{H^{s}}.

The first two terms are bounded by
C_{s}\|f-f_{N}\|_{H^{s}}\|g\|_{H^{s}}
and
C_{s}\|f_{N}\|_{H^{s}}\|g-g_{N}\|_{H^{s}}
, respectively, uniformly in
t
. The third term tends to
0
as
t\to 0
by the finite-sum case, and the last term tends to
0
as
N\to\infty
by continuity of pointwise multiplication on
H^{s}
for
s>\tfrac{n}{2}
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, choose
N
so that the
t
-uniform terms are
<\varepsilon/2
, then choose
t
small so that the third term is
<\varepsilon/2
. This proves
\|f\star_{\omega_{t}}g-fg\|_{H^{s}}\to 0
.
∎

3.3
Group structure on admissible 3–cocycles

We conclude this section by observing that the set of admissible spectral weights
satisfying the
3
–cocycle identity forms an abelian group under pointwise multiplication.

Definition 3.7

(Square 3–cocycles)
.

The spectral weight
\omega:I^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
is a
square 3–cocycle
if

|\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)|=1,\qquad\overline{\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}=\omega(\beta,\alpha,\gamma),

and for every admissible square

(\alpha,\beta,\delta),\ (\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon),\ (\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime}),\ (\alpha,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon)

(i.e.
C^{\delta}_{\alpha\beta}C^{\varepsilon}_{\delta\gamma}\neq 0
and
C^{\delta^{\prime}}_{\beta\gamma}C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\delta^{\prime}}\neq 0
) one has

\omega(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\,\omega(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)=\omega(\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime})\,\omega(\alpha,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon).

(7)

We write
Z^{3}_{\mathrm{sq}}
for the set of such
\omega
.

Remark 3.8
.

If
\omega\in Z^{3}_{\mathrm{sq}}
, then
\omega
satisfies the summed associativity identity (5) (Theorem 3.4): multiply (
7
) by the corresponding fusion coefficients and sum over admissible channels to obtain the equality of the two weighted sums. The converse can fail in the presence of multiplicities: equality of the weighted sums need not force channelwise equality.

Proposition 3.9

(Group law on square 3–cocycles)
.

Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in Z^{3}_{\mathrm{sq}}
. Then their pointwise product

(\omega_{1}\omega_{2})(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):=\omega_{1}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\omega_{2}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)

(8)

is again in
Z^{3}_{\mathrm{sq}}
. The identity element is
\omega_{0}\equiv 1
, and inverses are given by complex conjugation
\omega^{-1}=\overline{\omega}
. Thus
Z^{3}_{\mathrm{sq}}
is an abelian group under pointwise multiplication.

Proof.

We work with the fusion–square form of the
3
–cocycle identity: for every
admissible square

(\alpha,\beta,\delta),\quad(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon),\quad(\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime}),\quad(\alpha,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon),

one has

\omega(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\,\omega(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)=\omega(\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime})\,\omega(\alpha,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon).

(9)

(i) Closure under pointwise product.

Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
be admissible
3
–cocycles, i.e. each satisfies
(
7
) on every admissible square and obeys the unit–modulus and
Hermitian symmetry conditions. For a fixed square as above, write the two identities:

\omega_{1}(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\,\omega_{1}(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)=\omega_{1}(\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime})\,\omega_{1}(\alpha,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon),

\omega_{2}(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\,\omega_{2}(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)=\omega_{2}(\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime})\,\omega_{2}(\alpha,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon).

Multiplying these equalities termwise gives

\big(\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\big)(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\,\big(\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\big)(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)=\big(\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\big)(\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime})\,\big(\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\big)(\alpha,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon),

which is precisely (
7
) for
\omega_{1}\omega_{2}
. Thus

\omega_{1}\omega_{2}
satisfies the
3
–cocycle identity.
Moreover,
\big|\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\big|=|\omega_{1}|\,|\omega_{2}|=1
,

\overline{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\overline{\omega_{1}}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\overline{\omega_{2}}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\omega_{1}(\beta,\alpha,\gamma)\,\omega_{2}(\beta,\alpha,\gamma)=\big(\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\big)(\beta,\alpha,\gamma),

so unit modulus and Hermitian symmetry are preserved.

(ii) Identity and inverses.

The constant cocycle
\omega_{0}\equiv 1
clearly satisfies
(
7
) and the admissibility conditions, hence is the identity.

For inverses, let
\omega
be admissible and set

\omega^{-1}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):=\overline{\omega}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\omega(\beta,\alpha,\gamma).

Then
|\omega^{-1}|=1
and

\overline{\omega^{-1}}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\omega^{-1}(\beta,\alpha,\gamma)
.
For (
7
), compute on any admissible square:

\omega^{-1}(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\,\omega^{-1}(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)=\overline{\omega(\alpha,\beta,\delta)}\;\overline{\omega(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)}=\overline{\,\omega(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\,\omega(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)\,}.

By (
7
) for
\omega
, this equals

\overline{\,\omega(\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime})\,\omega(\alpha,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon)\,}=\omega^{-1}(\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime})\,\omega^{-1}(\alpha,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon),

which is (
7
) for
\omega^{-1}
. Finally,

(\omega\cdot\omega^{-1})(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\overline{\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}=1
,
so
\omega^{-1}
is indeed the inverse under pointwise multiplication.

Therefore the admissible
3
–cocycles form an abelian group under pointwise
multiplication, with identity
\omega_{0}\equiv 1
and inverse
given by complex conjugation.
∎

3.4
Equivariance under Group Actions

Let
G
act on the compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
by isometries.
The induced unitary representation on
L^{2}(M)
is

(U_{h}f)(x):=f(h^{-1}\!\cdot x),\qquad h\in G.

Because isometries preserve the Laplace–Beltrami operator, each
U_{h}
commutes with
\Delta
and hence preserves every eigenspace

E_{\lambda}:=\{\,f\in C^{\infty}(M)\mid\Delta f=\lambda f\,\}.

Let
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I_{\lambda}}
be an orthonormal eigenbasis of
E_{\lambda}
.
Then
U_{h}
acts within
E_{\lambda}
by a finite-dimensional unitary matrix

U_{h}\phi_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in I_{\lambda}}\rho_{h}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\,\phi_{\alpha^{\prime}},

where

\rho_{h}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}):=\langle U_{h}\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha^{\prime}}\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}

are the matrix coefficients of the unitary representation

\rho:G\to\prod_{\lambda}U(E_{\lambda})
.

Intuitively,
h\!\cdot\!\alpha
shall denote the transformed spectral label under this action; when each eigenspace is one-dimensional this corresponds to a permutation of indices, while in general it expresses a unitary mixing within
E_{\lambda}
. We define the transformed spectral label
h\!\cdot\!\alpha
as the formal image of
\alpha
under
this representation, i.e.

h\!\cdot\!\alpha:=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in I_{\lambda_{\alpha}}}\rho_{h}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\,\alpha^{\prime},

(10)

so that

\phi_{h\!\cdot\!\alpha}:=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in I_{\lambda_{\alpha}}}\rho_{h}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\,\phi_{\alpha^{\prime}}=U_{h}\phi_{\alpha}.

Equivariance of the fusion coefficients.

Since

\phi_{\alpha}\,\phi_{\beta}=\sum_{\varepsilon\in I}C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}\,\phi_{\varepsilon},

comparison with the transformed product

U_{h}(\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta})=(U_{h}\phi_{\alpha})(U_{h}\phi_{\beta})=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\rho_{h}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\,\rho_{h}(\beta,\beta^{\prime})\,\phi_{\alpha^{\prime}}\phi_{\beta^{\prime}}

gives the covariance law

C^{h\!\cdot\!\alpha,\,h\!\cdot\!\beta}_{\,h\!\cdot\!\varepsilon}=C^{\alpha\beta}_{\varepsilon}.

Equivalently, in tensor form,

C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,\rho_{h}(\gamma,\varepsilon)=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\rho_{h}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\,\rho_{h}(\beta,\beta^{\prime})\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}.

Thus the fusion coefficients transform covariantly with respect to the
G
-action.

Equivariance of the deformation.

Let
\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
be an admissible
3
-cocycle determining the deformed product

(f\star_{\omega}g)(x)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon\in I}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}\,\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta)\,\phi_{\varepsilon}(x).

We say the deformation is
G
-equivariant
if for every
h\in G

U_{h}(f\star_{\omega}g)=(U_{h}f)\star_{\omega}(U_{h}g),\qquad f,g\in C^{\infty}(M).

Proposition 3.10

(Equivariance criterion for the
3
-cocycle)
.

The deformed product
\star_{\omega}
is
G
-equivariant if and only if

\omega(h\!\cdot\!\alpha,\,h\!\cdot\!\beta,\,h\!\cdot\!\gamma)=\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\qquad\text{for all admissible triples $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ and all }h\in G.

(11)

Proof.

Fix
h\in G
. We compare the
\gamma
–th spectral coefficient on both sides.

Left-hand side.
Using unitarity of
U_{h}
,

U_{h}(f\star_{\omega}g)(\gamma)=\big\langle U_{h}(f\star_{\omega}g),\phi_{\gamma}\big\rangle=\big\langle f\star_{\omega}g,\,U_{h^{-1}}\phi_{\gamma}\big\rangle=\sum_{\varepsilon\in I}\rho_{h^{-1}}(\gamma,\varepsilon)\,(f\star_{\omega}g)(\varepsilon).

By the spectral formula for the deformed product,

(f\star_{\omega}g)(\varepsilon)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in I}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}\,\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta).

Hence

U_{h}(f\star_{\omega}g)(\gamma)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon\in I}\rho_{h^{-1}}(\gamma,\varepsilon)\,\omega(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}\,\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta).

(12)

Right-hand side.
First observe

U_{h}f(\alpha^{\prime})=\big\langle U_{h}f,\phi_{\alpha^{\prime}}\big\rangle=\big\langle f,U_{h^{-1}}\phi_{\alpha^{\prime}}\big\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\rho_{h^{-1}}(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha)\,\widehat{f}(\alpha),

and similarly
U_{h}g(\beta^{\prime})=\sum_{\beta}\rho_{h^{-1}}(\beta^{\prime},\beta)\widehat{g}(\beta)
.
Therefore

(U_{h}f)\star_{\omega}(U_{h}g)(\gamma)=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in I}\omega(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma)\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\,\widehat{U_{h}f}(\alpha^{\prime})\,\widehat{U_{h}g}(\beta^{\prime})

=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\alpha,\beta\in I}\omega(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma)\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\,\rho_{h^{-1}}(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha)\,\rho_{h^{-1}}(\beta^{\prime},\beta)\,\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta).

Using the covariance of the fusion coefficients under the isometric action (equivalently,
the intertwining relation)

\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in I}C^{\gamma}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\,\rho_{h^{-1}}(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha)\,\rho_{h^{-1}}(\beta^{\prime},\beta)=\sum_{\varepsilon\in I}\rho_{h^{-1}}(\gamma,\varepsilon)\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta},

we obtain

\displaystyle\,(U_{h}f)\star_{\omega}(U_{h}g)(\gamma)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon\in I}\Big(\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in I}\omega(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma)\,

(13)

\displaystyle\cdot\rho_{h^{-1}}(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha)\,\rho_{h^{-1}}(\beta^{\prime},\beta)\Big)\,\rho_{h^{-1}}(\gamma,\varepsilon)\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}\,\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta).

(14)

Equivalence.

Assume first that

\omega(h\!\cdot\!\alpha,\,h\!\cdot\!\beta,\,h\!\cdot\!\gamma)=\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\quad\text{for all }(\alpha,\beta,\gamma).

(15)

Then
\omega(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma)=\omega(h^{-1}\!\cdot\!\alpha^{\prime},\,h^{-1}\!\cdot\!\beta^{\prime},\,h^{-1}\!\cdot\!\gamma)
,
and, since
\rho_{h^{-1}}
implements the change of indices

\alpha^{\prime}=h\!\cdot\!\alpha
,
\beta^{\prime}=h\!\cdot\!\beta
, we have

\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in I}\omega(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma)\,\rho_{h^{-1}}(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha)\,\rho_{h^{-1}}(\beta^{\prime},\beta)=\omega(\alpha,\beta,h^{-1}\!\cdot\!\gamma).

Substituting this into (
13
) gives

(U_{h}f)\star_{\omega}(U_{h}g)(\gamma)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon\in I}\rho_{h^{-1}}(\gamma,\varepsilon)\,\omega(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}\,\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta),

which coincides with (
12
). Hence
U_{h}(f\star_{\omega}g)=(U_{h}f)\star_{\omega}(U_{h}g)
.

Conversely, suppose
U_{h}(f\star_{\omega}g)=(U_{h}f)\star_{\omega}(U_{h}g)
holds for all

f,g
. Comparing (
12
) and (
13
) and using the fact that the
coefficients
\widehat{f}(\alpha)\widehat{g}(\beta)
are arbitrary, we obtain for all

\alpha,\beta,\gamma
:

\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\omega(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma)\,\rho_{h^{-1}}(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha)\,\rho_{h^{-1}}(\beta^{\prime},\beta)=\omega(\alpha,\beta,h^{-1}\!\cdot\!\gamma).

Replacing
h
by
h^{-1}
yields exactly (
15
).
∎

Compatibility with the internal automorphisms.

When a group element
h\in G
acts trivially on the spectrum, the induced automorphism

U_{h}
commutes with the internal phase automorphisms
\gamma_{\chi}
of
Section 3.5, since both act diagonally on the spectral side:

U_{h}\,\gamma_{\chi}=\gamma_{\chi}\,U_{h}.

Thus the combined symmetry group of
(C^{\infty}(M),\star_{\omega})

is generated jointly by the geometric isometries of
M

and the internal spectral phase transformations arising from admissible
1
-cocycles.

3.5
Internal Actions on the Deformed Algebra

Beyond geometric isometries,
(C^{\infty}(M),\star_{\omega})
admits natural
automorphisms built from spectral data.

For any function
\chi:I\to\mathbb{T}
, define the automorphism

\widehat{(\gamma_{\chi}f)}(\alpha):=\chi(\alpha)\,\widehat{f}(\alpha),\qquad f\in C^{\infty}(M),\;\alpha\in I.

(16)

That is,
\gamma_{\chi}
acts by multiplying each spectral coefficient by the phase
\chi(\alpha)
.
The map
\gamma_{\chi}:(C^{\infty}(M),\star_{\omega})\to(C^{\infty}(M),\star_{\omega})

is a
\ast
-automorphism if and only if
\chi
satisfies the
fusion
1
-cocycle condition
:

\chi(\alpha)\,\chi(\beta)=\chi(\gamma)\quad\text{whenever }C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\neq 0.

(17)

For a real-valued function
\theta:I\to\mathbb{R}
, set

\chi_{t}(\alpha):=e^{it\theta(\alpha)},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Then
\{\gamma_{\chi_{t}}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a strongly continuous one-parameter group of unitary automorphisms
[
21
, VIII.3-4]
.
Its
infinitesimal generator

\delta_{\theta}
is defined by

\delta_{\theta}(f):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\gamma_{\chi_{t}}(f),\qquad\widehat{\delta_{\theta}(f)}(\alpha)=i\,\theta(\alpha)\,\widehat{f}(\alpha).

(18)

By Stone’s theorem
[
22
]
,
\delta_{\theta}
is a densely defined, skew-adjoint derivation on
L^{2}(M)
, and

\gamma_{\chi_{t}}=e^{t\delta_{\theta}}
.

3.6
Fourier–Side Description as Twisted Convolution

Let

\mathcal{F}:C^{\infty}(M)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{I},\qquad(\mathcal{F}f)_{\alpha}:=\widehat{f}_{\alpha},

denote the spectral transform sending a smooth function to its coefficients in the Laplace eigenbasis.
Write
\mathcal{S}(I)\subset\mathbb{C}^{I}
for the space of rapidly decaying coefficient functions (the image of
C^{\infty}(M)
under
\mathcal{F}
).
Assume throughout that
\omega:I\times I\times I\to\mathbb{T}
is an admissible unit–modulus fusion weight with the Hermitian symmetry

\overline{\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}=\omega(\beta,\alpha,\gamma),

defined for triples with
C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\neq 0
, and satisfying the fusion
3
–cocycle identity
(6)
.
As we work with a fixed
real
eigenbasis
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}
, define

\mathcal{F}^{*}(\alpha):=\overline{\mathcal{F}(\alpha)}.

Definition.

For
F,G\in\mathcal{S}(I)
, define the
fusion twisted convolution
by

(F*_{\omega}G)_{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in I}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)\,F_{\alpha}\,G_{\beta}\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}.

(19)

Lemma 3.8 (Spectral transform as a Fréchet isomorphism).

The spectral transform
\mathcal{F}
implements a Fréchet
*
–algebra isomorphism

\mathcal{F}:\bigl(C^{\infty}(M),\,\star_{\omega}\bigr)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\bigl(\mathcal{S}(I),\,*_{\omega}\bigr),

in the sense that for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
one has

\mathcal{F}(f\star_{\omega}g)\;=\;(\mathcal{F}f)*_{\omega}(\mathcal{F}g),\qquad\mathcal{F}(f^{*})=(\mathcal{F}f)^{*}.

In particular,
*_{\omega}
is associative on
\mathcal{S}(I)
whenever
\omega
satisfies the fusion
3
–cocycle identity
(6)
, and the Fréchet topology agrees with that of
C^{\infty}(M)
under
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
. By the definition of the deformed product on the physical side,

(f\star_{\omega}g)(x)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in I}\,\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{g}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,\phi_{\gamma}(x).

Taking the
\varepsilon
–th Fourier coefficient gives

\bigl(\mathcal{F}(f\star_{\omega}g)\bigr)_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in I}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{g}_{\beta}\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}\;=\;\bigl((\mathcal{F}f)*_{\omega}(\mathcal{F}g)\bigr)_{\varepsilon},

which is
(14)
with
F=\mathcal{F}f
and
G=\mathcal{F}g
.
The involution compatibility follows from the Hermitian symmetry of
\omega
and the symmetry of the fusion constants, yielding

\bigl((f\star_{\omega}g)^{*}\bigr)_{\gamma}=\overline{(f\star_{\omega}g)_{\gamma}}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\omega(\beta,\alpha,\gamma)\,\overline{\widehat{g}_{\beta}}\,\overline{\widehat{f}_{\alpha}}\,C^{\gamma}_{\beta\alpha}=\bigl(g^{*}\star_{\omega}f^{*}\bigr)_{\gamma}.

Associativity of
*_{\omega}
is obtained by comparing the
\varepsilon
–mode of

\bigl((F*_{\omega}G)*_{\omega}H\bigr)_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\,\omega(\delta,\gamma,\varepsilon)\,F_{\alpha}G_{\beta}H_{\gamma}\,C^{\delta}_{\alpha\beta}C^{\varepsilon}_{\delta\gamma},

with that of

\bigl(F*_{\omega}(G*_{\omega}H)\bigr)_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime}}\omega(\beta,\gamma,\delta^{\prime})\,\omega(\alpha,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon)\,F_{\alpha}G_{\beta}H_{\gamma}\,C^{\delta^{\prime}}_{\beta\gamma}C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\delta^{\prime}},

and using the fusion
3
–cocycle identity
(6)
on admissible fusion squares
(where the involved
C
’s are nonzero); this matches the proof of associativity for

\star_{\omega}
and shows
*_{\omega}
is associative as well.
Finally, the continuity estimates for
\star_{\omega}

transfer via
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathcal{S}(I)
, yielding a Fréchet
*
–algebra
isomorphism with the same topology.
∎

4
Spectral–Geometric Deformation as a Functor

In this section we formulate the spectral–geometric deformation procedure in categorical terms.
The construction is shown to be functorial from a category of spectral manifolds, whose morphisms
intertwine Laplacians and their associated 3–cocycle weights, to a category of deformed
Fréchet
*
–algebras.

4.1
The category of spectral manifolds

Definition 4.1

(Category
SpecMan
)
.

Objects of
\mathbf{SpecMan}
are triples
(M,g;\omega)
, where
(M,g)
is a compact Riemannian
manifold and
\omega:I^{3}\to\mathbb{T}
is an admissible spectral weight satisfying the fusion
3
–cocycle identity.
A morphism

\Phi:(M_{1},g_{1};\omega_{1})\longrightarrow(M_{2},g_{2};\omega_{2})

is a smooth map
\Phi:M_{1}\to M_{2}
satisfying the following compatibility conditions:

[(a)]

1.

Spectral intertwining:

\Phi^{*}\circ\Delta_{(M_{2},g_{2})}=\Delta_{(M_{1},g_{1})}\circ\Phi^{*}\quad\text{on }C^{\infty}(M_{2}).

Hence
\Phi^{*}
sends Laplace eigenfunctions on
M_{2}
to eigenfunctions on
M_{1}

with the same eigenvalues.

2.

Cocycle compatibility:

\Phi^{*}\omega_{2}=\omega_{1},

meaning that the pullback on spectral labels induced by
\Phi
preserves the
3–cocycle structure on admissible triples. In particular, if
\phi^{(2)}_{\alpha}
is
an eigenfunction of
\Delta_{(M_{2},g_{2})}
with label
\alpha
, then

\phi^{(1)}_{\alpha}:=\Phi^{*}\phi^{(2)}_{\alpha}
defines a fusion system on
M_{1}

with structure constants and weight
\omega_{1}
satisfying the same 3–cocycle relations.

Composition of morphisms is the usual composition of smooth maps, and identities are given
by the identity maps on manifolds.

4.2
The category of deformed algebras and the deformation functor

Let
\mathbf{Alg}^{*}
denote the category of complex Fréchet
*
–algebras with continuous

*
–homomorphisms.

Definition 4.2

(Spectral–geometric deformation functor)
.

Define

\mathcal{D}:\mathbf{SpecMan}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Alg}^{*},

by setting, on objects,

\mathcal{D}(M,g;\omega):=\bigl(C^{\infty}(M),\star_{\omega}\bigr),

where
\star_{\omega}
is the spectral 3–cocycle deformation product introduced in
Section 3.

For a morphism
\Phi:(M_{1},g_{1};\omega_{1})\to(M_{2},g_{2};\omega_{2})
in
\mathbf{SpecMan}
,
define

\mathcal{D}(\Phi):=\Phi^{*}:\bigl(C^{\infty}(M_{2}),\star_{\omega_{2}}\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl(C^{\infty}(M_{1}),\star_{\omega_{1}}\bigr).

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\Phi
satisfies the conditions of Definition 4.1, then

\Phi^{*}
is a continuous
*
–homomorphism with respect to the deformed products:

\Phi^{*}(f\star_{\omega_{2}}g)=(\Phi^{*}f)\star_{\omega_{1}}(\Phi^{*}g),\qquad(\Phi^{*}f)^{*}=\Phi^{*}(f^{*}).

Hence
\mathcal{D}
is a contravariant functor.

Proof.

By spectral intertwining (a), the pullback
\Phi^{*}
maps each eigenfunction

\phi^{(2)}_{\alpha}
of
\Delta_{(M_{2},g_{2})}
to an eigenfunction of

\Delta_{(M_{1},g_{1})}
with the same eigenvalue, inducing an identification of the
spectral index sets. For
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M_{2})
, we have

f\star_{\omega_{2}}g=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\omega_{2}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{g}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma,(2)}_{\alpha\beta}\,\phi^{(2)}_{\gamma},

and applying
\Phi^{*}
gives

\Phi^{*}(f\star_{\omega_{2}}g)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\omega_{2}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{g}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma,(2)}_{\alpha\beta}\,\Phi^{*}\phi^{(2)}_{\gamma}.

By (a)–(b), the pullback eigenbasis
\Phi^{*}\phi^{(2)}_{\gamma}=\phi^{(1)}_{\gamma}
has
fusion constants
C^{\gamma,(1)}_{\alpha\beta}=C^{\gamma,(2)}_{\alpha\beta}
and

\omega_{1}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\omega_{2}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
.
Therefore

\Phi^{*}(f\star_{\omega_{2}}g)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\omega_{1}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{(\Phi^{*}f)}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{(\Phi^{*}g)}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma,(1)}_{\alpha\beta}\,\phi^{(1)}_{\gamma}=(\Phi^{*}f)\star_{\omega_{1}}(\Phi^{*}g),

and similarly for the involution. Continuity follows from the Sobolev bounds
of Section 3, which are preserved under
\Phi^{*}
since it intertwines Laplacians.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

We denote by

\mathbf{DefAlg}:=\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{D})\subset\mathbf{Alg}^{*}

the full subcategory whose objects are the spectrally deformed Fréchet
*
–algebras and
whose morphisms are the
*
–homomorphisms induced by morphisms in
\mathbf{SpecMan}
.
Thus
\mathcal{D}:\mathbf{SpecMan}\to\mathbf{DefAlg}
is a well–defined
contravariant functor.

Remark 4.5
.

Functoriality of
\mathcal{D}
expresses a form of geometric invariance of the
spectral–geometric deformation: spectral structure-preserving maps of manifolds induce

*
–isomorphisms of the corresponding deformed algebras.

5
Behaviour under Riemannian Isometries

5.1
Isometries between Riemannian manifolds

Let
(M_{1},g_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2})
be compact Riemannian manifolds, and let
\Phi:M_{1}\to M_{2}
be a smooth isometry. Then
\Phi^{*}g_{2}=g_{1}
and the Laplace–Beltrami operators are intertwined:

\Delta_{g_{1}}(f\circ\Phi)=(\Delta_{g_{2}}f)\circ\Phi,

so pullback carries eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{g_{2}}
to eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{g_{1}}
with the same eigenvalue.

Fix orthonormal eigenbases
\{\phi^{(i)}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I_{i}}
of
\Delta_{g_{i}}
. Since
\Phi
is an isometry, the pullback on each eigenspace is unitary: there exist unitary matrices
U^{(\lambda)}=(U_{\mu\alpha})
(block-diagonal over eigenvalue blocks) so that

\phi^{(2)}_{\alpha}\circ\Phi\;=\;\sum_{\mu:\,\lambda_{\mu}=\lambda_{\alpha}}U_{\mu\alpha}\,\phi^{(1)}_{\mu},

(20)

i.e. the eigenbasis mixes unitarily within each eigenspace. Consequently, the fusion (structure) constants transform covariantly:

C^{(1)\,\gamma}_{\mu\nu}\;=\;\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon}U_{\mu\alpha}\,U_{\nu\beta}\,\overline{U_{\gamma\varepsilon}}\;C^{(2)\,\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta},

(21)

which is the tensor form of the relation recorded in Section 3.4 (equivariance of fusion coefficients).

Let
\omega_{g_{i}}:I_{i}^{3}\to\mathbb{T}
be admissible spectral
3
-cocycles. We say that
\omega_{g_{1}}
and
\omega_{g_{2}}
are
spectrally compatible under
\Phi
if, blockwise with respect to the eigenspace unitaries
U
, one has

\sum_{\mu,\nu,\gamma}\overline{U_{\mu\alpha}}\;\overline{U_{\nu\beta}}\;U_{\gamma\varepsilon}\;\omega_{g_{1}}(\mu,\nu,\gamma)\;=\;\omega_{g_{2}}(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)

(22)

for all admissible triples
(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)
(i.e. where the relevant
C
’s do not vanish). When eigenspaces are simple, (
22
) just reads

\;\omega_{g_{1}}(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)=\omega_{g_{2}}(\Phi^{*}\alpha,\Phi^{*}\beta,\Phi^{*}\varepsilon)
. Writing
U^{(\lambda)}
for the unitary on each eigenspace block and
viewing
\omega_{g_{i}}
as a tensor supported on admissible triples,

\omega_{g_{2}}\;=\;(U^{(\lambda)}\!\otimes U^{(\lambda)}\!\otimes U^{(\lambda)})\,\omega_{g_{1}},

(23)

i.e.

\sum_{\mu,\nu,\gamma}U_{\mu\alpha}\,U_{\nu\beta}\,U_{\gamma\varepsilon}\,\omega_{g_{1}}(\mu,\nu,\gamma)=\omega_{g_{2}}(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)

componentwise on each block.

Theorem 5.1

(Intertwining pullbacks induce
*
–isomorphisms)
.

If
\Phi:(M_{1},g_{1})\to(M_{2},g_{2})
is an isometry and the
3
-cocycles satisfy the spectral compatibility (
22
), then the pullback

\Phi^{*}:\bigl(C^{\infty}(M_{2}),\star_{\omega_{g_{2}}}\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl(C^{\infty}(M_{1}),\star_{\omega_{g_{1}}}\bigr)

is a continuous
*
–isomorphism. In particular, if
\omega_{g_{i}}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\Theta(\lambda_{\alpha},\lambda_{\beta},\lambda_{\gamma})
depends only on eigenvalues, then (
22
) holds automatically for every isometry
\Phi
.

Proof.

Let
(M_{i},g_{i})
,
i=1,2
, have Laplace eigenbases
\{\phi^{(i)}_{\alpha}\}
with fusion constants

\phi^{(i)}_{\alpha}\,\phi^{(i)}_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma}C^{(i)\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,\phi^{(i)}_{\gamma},\qquad i=1,2,

and let
\omega_{g_{i}}
be admissible 3–cocycles. Denote by

F_{i}(f)=(\widehat{f}^{(i)}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
the spectral transform, so that

(F_{i}f\ast_{\omega_{g_{i}}}F_{i}g)_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\omega_{g_{i}}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}^{(i)}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{g}^{(i)}_{\beta}\,C^{(i)\gamma}_{\alpha\beta},

and
F_{i}(f\star_{\omega_{g_{i}}}g)=F_{i}f\ast_{\omega_{g_{i}}}F_{i}g
.

Let
\Phi:(M_{1},g_{1})\to(M_{2},g_{2})
be an isometry. On each eigenspace the pullback
acts by a unitary block matrix
U
, so that

F_{1}(\Phi^{*}f)=U\,F_{2}(f),\qquad C^{(1)}_{\gamma\mu\nu}=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime}}U_{\mu\alpha^{\prime}}\,U_{\nu\beta^{\prime}}\,\overline{U_{\gamma\varepsilon^{\prime}}}\,C^{(2)\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}.

Assume spectral compatibility of the cocycles under
\Phi
:

\omega_{g_{2}}=(U\otimes U\otimes U)\,\omega_{g_{1}},\quad\text{i.e.}\quad\sum_{\mu,\nu,\gamma}U_{\mu\alpha}\,U_{\nu\beta}\,U_{\gamma\varepsilon}\,\omega_{g_{1}}(\mu,\nu,\gamma)=\omega_{g_{2}}(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon).

Equivalently,
\omega_{g_{1}}=(U^{*}\!\otimes U^{*}\!\otimes U^{*})\,\omega_{g_{2}}
.
We show that

\Phi^{*}(f\star_{\omega_{g_{2}}}g)=(\Phi^{*}f)\star_{\omega_{g_{1}}}(\Phi^{*}g)
.

Coefficient–side computation.

Using
F_{1}(\Phi^{*}h)=UF_{2}(h)
and the convolution form of
\star
,

\displaystyle F_{1}\big(\Phi^{*}(f\star_{\omega_{g_{2}}}g)\big)

\displaystyle=U\,F_{2}(f\star_{\omega_{g_{2}}}g)=U\big(F_{2}f\ast_{\omega_{g_{2}}}F_{2}g\big),

\displaystyle F_{1}\big((\Phi^{*}f)\star_{\omega_{g_{1}}}(\Phi^{*}g)\big)

\displaystyle=(UF_{2}f)\ast_{\omega_{g_{1}}}(UF_{2}g).

Expanding the
\gamma
–component of the rightmost expression gives

\sum_{\mu,\nu}\omega_{g_{1}}(\mu,\nu,\gamma)\,(UF_{2}f)_{\mu}\,(UF_{2}g)_{\nu}\,C^{(1)\gamma}_{\mu\nu}.

Insert
(UF_{2}f)_{\mu}=\sum_{\alpha}U_{\mu\alpha}\,\widehat{f}^{(2)}_{\alpha}
,
expand
C^{(1)}
and use the compatibility.
Rearranging the sums, each
U
is paired with a conjugate

\overline{U}
in the same eigenspace index, so unitarity

\sum_{\mu}U_{\mu\alpha}\,\overline{U_{\mu\alpha^{\prime}}}=\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}

contracts all blocks, yielding

\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon}U_{\gamma\varepsilon}\,\omega_{g_{2}}(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)\,\widehat{f}^{(2)}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{g}^{(2)}_{\beta}\,C^{(2)\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}=\big[U\,(F_{2}f\ast_{\omega_{g_{2}}}F_{2}g)\big]_{\gamma}.

Thus

F_{1}\big((\Phi^{*}f)\star_{\omega_{g_{1}}}(\Phi^{*}g)\big)=F_{1}\big(\Phi^{*}(f\star_{\omega_{g_{2}}}g)\big)
,
and applying
F_{1}^{-1}
gives the claimed multiplicativity.

Involution and continuity.

Because
\Phi
is an isometry,
\Phi^{*}
is unitary on
L^{2}
, hence

(\Phi^{*}f)^{*}=\Phi^{*}(f^{*})
.
Since
|\omega|=1
and
\omega
has Hermitian symmetry, the

\star_{\omega_{g_{i}}}
products are
\ast
–compatible.
Continuity in the Fréchet topology follows from the usual Sobolev
bounds for
\star_{\omega}
.

Bijectivity.

As
\Phi
is a diffeomorphism,

\Phi^{*}
is a topological
\ast
–isomorphism with inverse
(\Phi^{-1})^{*}
.
∎

6
Metric Perturbations

Let
\{g_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a smooth one–parameter family of Riemannian metrics on a compact manifold
M
, with corresponding Laplace–Beltrami operators
\Delta_{t}
and (fixed) real orthonormal eigenbases

\Delta_{t}\phi^{(t)}_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}(t)\phi^{(t)}_{\alpha},\qquad\langle\phi^{(t)}_{\alpha},\phi^{(t)}_{\beta}\rangle_{L^{2}(M,g_{t})}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

Let the spectral fusion coefficients be

C^{(t)\,\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}:=\int_{M}\phi^{(t)}_{\alpha}\phi^{(t)}_{\beta}\overline{\phi^{(t)}_{\gamma}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{t}}.

Assume we have a
C^{k}
family of admissible unit-modulus Hermitian fusion
3
-cocycles

\omega_{t}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
satisfying the cocycle identity (6) for all
t
.
For
f,h\in C^{\infty}(M)
we consider as above the deformed product

(f\star_{\omega_{t}}h)(x)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\omega_{t}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}^{(t)}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}^{(t)}_{\beta}\,C^{(t)\,\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,\phi^{(t)}_{\gamma}(x),

with
\widehat{f}^{(t)}_{\alpha}=\langle f,\phi^{(t)}_{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}(M,g_{t})}
and
\widehat{h}^{(t)}_{\alpha}=\langle h,\phi^{(t)}_{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}(M,g_{t})}
.

Metric derivative of the deformation

For
f,h\in C^{\infty}(M)
we study

\partial_{t}\!\big|_{t=0}(f\star_{\omega_{t}}h):=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f\star_{\omega_{t}}h-f\star_{\omega_{0}}h}{t},

whenever the limit exists in every Sobolev norm
H^{s}
.
We denote
\dot{(\,)}=\partial_{t}|_{t=0}
.

Theorem 6.1

(Smooth dependence on the metric)
.

Assume
t\mapsto g_{t}
and
t\mapsto\omega_{t}
are
C^{k}
in
t
with uniform symbol bounds of order
m>0
. Then

(t,f,h)\longmapsto f\star_{\omega_{t}}h

is
C^{k}
as a map
[0,1]\times C^{\infty}(M)^{2}\to C^{\infty}(M)
. Moreover, the derivative at
t=0
exists and has the form

\partial_{t}\!\big|_{t=0}(f\star_{\omega_{t}}h)=B[\dot{g}](f,h),

where
B[\dot{g}]
is a continuous bilinear map for all
s>\frac{n}{2}
.

Proof.

Differentiating the expansion termwise gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\!\big|_{t=0}(f\star_{\omega_{t}}h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\Big[\dot{\omega}_{t}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}+\omega_{0}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\big(\dot{\widehat{f}}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}_{\beta}+\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\dot{\widehat{h}}_{\beta}\big)C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\omega_{0}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,\dot{C}^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}+\omega_{0}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,\dot{\phi}_{\gamma}\Big].

(24)

Here
\omega_{0}=\omega_{t=0}
, and
\dot{\omega}_{t}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=:\eta(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
plays the role of the infinitesimal 3-cocycle.

The standard metric variation identities (
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
) read:

\displaystyle\dot{d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{t}}}\!\big|_{0}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\operatorname{tr}_{g}\dot{g})\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g},

\displaystyle\dot{\widehat{f}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}f\big(\dot{\phi}_{\alpha}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\operatorname{tr}_{g}\dot{g})\phi_{\alpha}\big)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g},

\displaystyle\dot{C}^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\!\big(\dot{\phi}_{\alpha}\,\phi_{\beta}\,\overline{\phi_{\gamma}}+\phi_{\alpha}\,\dot{\phi}_{\beta}\,\overline{\phi_{\gamma}}+\phi_{\alpha}\,\phi_{\beta}\,\overline{\dot{\phi}_{\gamma}}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\operatorname{tr}_{g}\dot{g})\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}\overline{\phi_{\gamma}}\big)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g},

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}[f]

\displaystyle=-\langle\dot{g},\nabla^{2}f\rangle+\langle\operatorname{div}\dot{g}-\tfrac{1}{2}d(\operatorname{tr}_{g}\dot{g}),\nabla f\rangle,

\displaystyle(\Delta-\lambda_{\gamma})\dot{\phi}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=-\big(\dot{\Delta}[\!\dot{g}]-\dot{\lambda}_{\gamma}\big)\phi_{\gamma},\quad\dot{\lambda}_{\gamma}=\langle\dot{\Delta}[\!\dot{g}]\phi_{\gamma},\phi_{\gamma}\rangle_{L^{2}(M,g)}.

For general multiplicity, one works in a
C^{k}
orthonormal moving frame using the Riesz projector;
the formulas below hold for each frame vector.

Expanding
\dot{\phi}_{\gamma}=\sum_{\varepsilon\neq\gamma}A_{\varepsilon\gamma}\phi_{\varepsilon}
with

A_{\varepsilon\gamma}=\frac{\langle-(\dot{\Delta}[\!\dot{g}]-\dot{\lambda}_{\gamma})\phi_{\gamma},\phi_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}(M,g)}}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}-\lambda_{\gamma}},\qquad A_{\gamma\gamma}=0,

and writing
(\cdot)_{\varepsilon}=\langle\cdot,\phi_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}(M,g)}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle B[\dot{g}](f,h)_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\eta(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}+\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\omega_{0}(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)\big(\dot{\widehat{f}}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}_{\beta}+\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\dot{\widehat{h}}_{\beta}\big)C^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\omega_{0}(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,\dot{C}^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}+\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\omega_{0}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,A_{\varepsilon\gamma}.

(25)

The first line of (
25
) corresponds to the infinitesimal 3-cocycle variation
B_{\eta}
;
the next two lines collect the variations of Fourier coefficients and fusion constants, and the last
sum accounts for the re-expansion of the output basis
\dot{\phi}_{\gamma}
.

Using elliptic regularity for
(\Delta-\lambda_{\gamma})^{-1}
on the orthogonal complement, each

involves at most two derivatives of the background coefficients (through

\dot{\Delta}[\!\dot{g}]

(\lambda_{\varepsilon}-\lambda_{\gamma})^{-1}

Because
\omega_{0}
is an admissible 3-cocycle of order

define a continuous bilinear map

B[\dot{g}]:H^{s}(M)\times H^{s}(M)\to H^{s-m}(M),

with coefficients linear in

and norm bounded by

\|\,\dot{g}\,\|_{C^{r}}
for some fixed

(cf. Lemma 3.1). Thus

is a bilinear
bidifferential operator of order

Uniform symbol bounds for

imply

is bounded

H^{s}\times H^{s}\to H^{s-m}
uniformly in

The
C^{k}
dependence of eigenpairs and spectral projectors gives
C^{k}
dependence of

\widehat{f}^{(t)}
,
\widehat{h}^{(t)}
,

Differentiating under the absolutely convergent sums in (

Hence

is

, and

\partial_{t}\!\big|_{t=0}(f\star_{\omega_{t}}h)=B[\dot{g}](f,h),

with

given explicitly above.
∎

7
Old Examples

We illustrate how the spectral–geometric deformation by a closed fusion
3
–cocycle

\omega
reproduces several well–known deformation quantizations within the discrete
spectral framework of Section 3. All manipulations remain purely algebraic at the level of
spectral coefficients; no oscillatory integrals are introduced.

7.1
Rieffel deformation

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold equipped with a smooth, isometric action of
the abelian Lie group
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Diff}(M),\qquad\alpha_{v}(x):=v\cdot x,

so that each
\alpha_{v}
is an isometry of
(M,g)
and

\alpha_{v+w}=\alpha_{v}\circ\alpha_{w}
. The induced action on functions is
\alpha_{v}(f)=f\circ\alpha_{-v}
,
and the infinitesimal generators

X_{j}f=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\alpha_{te_{j}}(f),\qquad j=1,\dots,d,

are commuting, self-adjoint, first-order differential operators on

C^{\infty}(M)
, denoted
X_{1},\dots,X_{d}
. Assume that

L\;:=\;-\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}^{2}

is of Laplace–type, so that
L
has discrete spectrum

\{\lambda_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in V}
and an orthonormal eigenbasis

\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in V}
satisfying the fusion expansion

\phi_{\alpha}\,\phi_{\beta}\;=\;\sum_{\gamma\in V}C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,\phi_{\gamma},\qquad C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=0\text{ unless }\gamma=\alpha+\beta.

Indeed, for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by
G
–equivariance of the action and invariance of
d\mathrm{vol}_{g}
,

C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=\!\int_{M}\!\phi_{\alpha}(x)\phi_{\beta}(x)\overline{\phi_{\gamma}(x)}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}(x)=\!\int_{M}\!\alpha_{v}(\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}\overline{\phi_{\gamma}})(x)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}(x)=e^{i\,v\cdot(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}.

Hence
(e^{i\,v\cdot(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)}-1)C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=0
for all
v
, which forces

C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=0
unless
\gamma=\alpha+\beta
.

Fix a real skew–symmetric matrix
J\in\mathfrak{so}(d)
and define

\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\exp\!\Big(\pi i[\alpha\!\cdot\!J\beta+\beta\!\cdot\!J\gamma+\gamma\!\cdot\!J\alpha]\Big),\qquad\gamma=\alpha+\beta.

(26)

For
\gamma=\alpha+\beta
this gives

\omega(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)=e^{-\pi i\,\alpha\cdot J\beta}=\sigma(\alpha,\beta),

where
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)=e^{-\pi i\,\alpha\cdot J\beta}
is Rieffel’s bicharacter.
Thus our identification corresponds to the deformation with parameter
J
,
so
(C^{\infty}(M),\star_{\omega})
coincides with
(C^{\infty}(M),\times_{J})
.

The associated
2
–face restriction

\sigma(\alpha,\beta):=\omega(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)=e^{-\pi i\,\alpha\!\cdot\!J\beta}

(27)

is the classical Rieffel–type bicharacter.

Under the Laplace–type assumption, every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
admits a discrete expansion

f=\sum_{\alpha\in V}\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\phi_{\alpha}
, and for
f,h\in C^{\infty}(M)
we have as usual

(f\star_{\omega}h)_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in V}\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}.

(28)

For additive fusion,
C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=0
unless
\gamma=\alpha+\beta
, so that

(f\star_{\omega}h)_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}e^{-\pi i\,\alpha\!\cdot\!J\beta}\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}.

Rieffel’s oscillatory product and isomorphism.

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{d}\curvearrowright C^{\infty}(M)
be the isometric action with
smooth vectors
C^{\infty}(M)^{\infty}
(for
\alpha
). For a fixed skew matrix
J\in\mathfrak{so}(d)
,
Rieffel’s product on
C^{\infty}(M)^{\infty}
is defined by the oscillatory integral

f\times_{J}h:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\pi i\,u\!\cdot\!Jv}\;\alpha_{u}(f)\,\alpha_{v}(h)\,du\,dv,

understood in the standard sense on smooth vectors.

Proposition 7.1

(Coefficient identity)
.

Assume
L=-\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}^{2}
is of Laplace–type, with orthonormal eigenbasis

\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in V}
and structure constants

\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma}C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\gamma}
.
Let
\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
be the fusion
3
–cocycle from (
26
), and

\sigma(\alpha,\beta):=\omega(\alpha,\beta,0)=e^{-\pi i\,\alpha\!\cdot\!J\beta}
.
Then for all
f,h\in C^{\infty}(M)^{\infty}
and all
\gamma\in V
,

(f\times_{J}h)_{\gamma}\;=\;\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in V}\sigma(\alpha,\beta)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\;=\;(f\star_{\omega}h)_{\gamma}.

Proof.

Write
f=\sum_{\alpha}\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}
,
h=\sum_{\beta}\widehat{h}_{\beta}\phi_{\beta}
.
Since
\alpha_{u}(\phi_{\alpha})=e^{i\,u\cdot\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}
and

\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma}C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\gamma}
, we have

\alpha_{u}(f)\,\alpha_{v}(h)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}e^{i\,u\cdot\alpha}\,e^{i\,v\cdot\beta}\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,\phi_{\gamma}.

Taking the
\gamma
–coefficient after applying the oscillatory kernel gives

(f\times_{J}h)_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\Big(\int e^{-\pi i\,u\cdot Jv}\,e^{i\,u\cdot\alpha}\,e^{i\,v\cdot\beta}\,du\,dv\Big)\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}.

The
u,v
–integral equals the bicharacter value
e^{-\pi i\,\alpha\cdot J\beta}
times the
fusion selection
\gamma=\alpha+\beta
(Kronecker/Dirac orthogonality for the abelian action),
hence contributes
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)
precisely on admissible channels and zero otherwise.
Therefore

(f\times_{J}h)_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}e^{-\pi i\,\alpha\cdot J\beta}\,\widehat{f}_{\alpha}\,\widehat{h}_{\beta}\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta},

which matches
(f\star_{\omega}h)_{\gamma}
because

\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\omega(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)=\sigma(\alpha,\beta)
.
∎

As a consequence, the identity map on
C^{\infty}(M)^{\infty}
implements a
*
–isomorphism

(C^{\infty}(M)^{\infty},\star_{\omega})\;\cong\;(C^{\infty}(M)^{\infty},\times_{J}).

7.2
Connes–Landi
\theta
–deformation

We now specialize our construction to the flat
d
–torus

M=\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}

equipped with its standard flat Riemannian metric

g=\sum_{j=1}^{d}dx_{j}\otimes dx_{j},

and the corresponding Laplace operator

\Delta=-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}.

The algebra
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of smooth complex functions on
\mathbb{T}^{d}

has the generators

U_{n}(x):=e^{2\pi i\,n\cdot x},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

which consists of smooth unitary functions generating
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

as a Fréchet
*
–algebra.
Each
U_{n}
is an eigenfunction of
\Delta
with eigenvalue
4\pi^{2}|n|^{2}
, and
the basis satisfies the additive fusion law

U_{n}\,U_{m}=U_{n+m},\qquad C^{k}_{nm}=\delta_{k,n+m}.

Fusion
3
–cocycle and
2
–face restriction.

Fix a real skew–symmetric matrix
\Theta\in\mathfrak{so}(d)
and define
the fusion
3
–cocycle by

\omega(n,m,k):=\exp\!\big[\pi i\,\big(n\!\cdot\!\Theta m+m\!\cdot\!\Theta k+k\!\cdot\!\Theta n\big)\big],\qquad(n,m,k)\in E.

The
2
–face restriction is

\sigma(n,m):=\omega(n,m,n+m)=e^{\pi i\,n\!\cdot\!\Theta m},

which is the usual bicharacter appearing in the Connes–Landi deformation.

Coefficient formula and generator relations.

For
f,h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with expansions

f=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}_{n}\,U_{n}
and

h=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{h}_{m}\,U_{m}
,
we have

(f\star_{\omega}h)_{k}=\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\omega(n,m,k)\,\widehat{f}_{n}\,\widehat{h}_{m}\,C^{k}_{nm}.

Using the additive fusion rule
C^{k}_{nm}=\delta_{k,n+m}
and the
skew–symmetry of
\Theta
, this becomes

(f\star_{\omega}h)_{k}=\sum_{n+m=k}e^{\pi i\,n\cdot\Theta m}\,\widehat{f}_{n}\,\widehat{h}_{m}.

In particular, for the canonical unitaries
U_{j}:=U_{e_{j}}
associated to
the standard basis vectors
e_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

U_{j}\star_{\omega}U_{k}=e^{2\pi i\,\Theta_{jk}}\,U_{k}\star_{\omega}U_{j},

which are precisely the Connes–Landi relations defining the smooth
noncommutative torus.

Identification with the smooth noncommutative torus.

Let
A_{\Theta}^{\infty}
denote the smooth noncommutative torus algebra
generated by unitaries
V_{1},\dots,V_{d}
satisfying

V_{j}V_{k}=e^{2\pi i\,\Theta_{jk}}\,V_{k}V_{j},\qquad\Theta_{jk}=-\Theta_{kj}.

Define the algebra map

\Phi_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\colon(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),\star_{\omega})\longrightarrow A_{\Theta}^{\infty},\qquad\Phi_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(U_{n})=V_{1}^{\,n_{1}}\cdots V_{d}^{\,n_{d}},

for each
n=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Since both products coincide on the generators,
\Phi_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
extends
uniquely to a
*
–isomorphism of Fréchet algebras. Thus the Connes–Landi
isospectral
\theta
–deformation is obtained exactly as the specialization
of our fusion
3
–cocycle deformation on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, with the restriction

\omega(n,m,k)\mapsto\sigma(n,m)=e^{\pi in\cdot\Theta m}
on
2
–faces
recovering the classical bicharacter twist.

7.3
Kasprzak deformation as twisted crossed products

Kasprzak
[
10
]
reformulates Rieffel’s deformation in the framework of

twisted crossed products
of
C^{*}
–dynamical systems.
Here the deformation is governed by a Borel measurable unitary
2
–cocycle
on a locally compact abelian group
G
, rather than by a skew matrix.
This picture subsumes Rieffel’s
\mathbb{R}^{d}
deformation and extends it to general
measurable cocycles on abelian groups.
Although our spectral–geometric deformation is purely intrinsic and requires no
G
–action
on the manifold, it recovers Kasprzak’s deformation at the level of Fourier coefficients,
as we now explain.

Kasprzak deformation

Let
G
be a second–countable locally compact abelian group with Haar measure
du
,
and let
(A,G,\alpha)
be a
C^{*}
–dynamical system with a strongly continuous action

\alpha:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(A)
.
A Borel measurable map
\psi:G\times G\to\mathbb{T}
is called a

unitary
2
–cocycle
if

\psi(u,v)\,\psi(u+v,w)=\psi(v,w)\,\psi(u,v+w),\qquad u,v,w\in G.

(29)

Given such
\psi
, the
Kasprzak–twisted crossed product

A\rtimes_{\alpha,\psi}G
is generated by integrable
A
–valued functions

f\in L^{1}(G,A)
with multiplication

(f*_{\psi}g)(x)=\int_{G}f(u)\,\alpha_{u}\!\big(g(u^{-1}x)\big)\,\psi(u,u^{-1}x)\,du,

(30)

and involution

f^{*}(x)=\psi(x,x^{-1})^{-1}\,\alpha_{x}(f(x^{-1})^{*}).

The completion under the universal
C^{*}
–norm yields
A\rtimes_{\alpha,\psi}G
.
Kasprzak’s deformed algebra
A_{\psi}
is the Landstad (fixed–point) subalgebra of

A\rtimes_{\alpha,\psi}G
for the
\psi
–twisted dual action.
For
G=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\psi(u,v)=e^{2\pi i\,\langle u,Jv\rangle}
,
this recovers Rieffel’s oscillatory–integral deformation.

From
\psi
to the spectral
3
–cocycle
\omega
.

Let
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
be the Laplace eigenbasis of
L^{2}(M)
with
structure constants
C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}
defined by

\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma\in I}C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,\phi_{\gamma},\qquad f=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\phi_{\alpha}.

For the comparison with Kasprzak’s twisted convolution, we associate to each
basis element
\phi_{\alpha}
a symbol
U_{\alpha}
in the deformed algebra
A_{\psi}
,
defined by

U_{\alpha}:=\Phi_{\psi}(\phi_{\alpha}),

where
\Phi_{\psi}
is the coefficient identification map introduced below.
Finite linear combinations of
U_{\alpha}
’s form the algebraic (trigonometric–polynomial)
core of
A_{\psi}
, dense by Landstad duality and Kasprzak’s construction
(see also
[
14
]
).

Multiplication in this core has the form

U_{\alpha}\,U_{\beta}\;=\;\sum_{\gamma\in I}m_{\psi}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma)\,U_{\gamma},

(31)

for certain structure constants
m_{\psi}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma)\in\mathbb{C}

determined by the cocycle
\psi
.
We then define the spectral
3
–cocycle by

\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\;:=\;\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{m_{\psi}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma)}{C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}},&\text{if }C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\neq 0,\\[5.38193pt]
1,&\text{if }C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=0.\end{cases}

(32)

Using the cocycle identity (
29
) and associativity of
(
30
), a direct computation yields the

spectral
3
–cocycle identity
on admissible quadruples.

Coefficient identification.

Define the linear map

\Phi_{\psi}:C^{\infty}(M)\longrightarrow A_{\psi},\qquad\Phi_{\psi}\!\left(\sum_{\alpha\in I}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\phi_{\alpha}\right):=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,U_{\alpha}.

(33)

By (
31
), for
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
we have

\displaystyle\big(\Phi_{\psi}(f)*_{\psi}\Phi_{\psi}(g)\big)_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta)\,m_{\psi}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma),

(34)

\displaystyle(f\star_{\omega}g)_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\,\widehat{g}(\beta)\,C^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\,\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma).

(35)

By the definition (
32
) of
\omega
, the coefficients
in (
34
) and (
35
) coincide for every
\gamma
; hence

\Phi_{\psi}(f\star_{\omega}g)\;=\;\Phi_{\psi}(f)\;*_{\psi}\;\Phi_{\psi}(g),

(36)

where
*_{\psi}
is the Kasprzak product (
30
).
Thus
\Phi_{\psi}
implements a
*
–isomorphism between the spectral deformation

(C^{\infty}(M),\star_{\omega})
and the Landstad algebra
A_{\psi}
inside the twisted
crossed product
A\rtimes_{\alpha,\psi}G
.

8
New Examples

Unlike Rieffel, Connes–Landi, or Kasprzak deformations, none of the examples below
require a group action or global symmetry on
M
.
They therefore constitute genuinely new associative noncommutative geometries.

8.1
Deformation on compact hyperbolic surfaces

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
be a discrete, torsion–free, cocompact Fuchsian group, and let

M=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2}

be the corresponding compact hyperbolic surface equipped with its standard metric
g_{\Gamma}
of constant curvature
-1
.
Define the spectral weight

\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):=\exp\!\big(i(\lambda_{\alpha}+\lambda_{\beta}-\lambda_{\gamma})\big),

which is additive in the spectral indices and therefore satisfies the
fusion
3
–cocycle identity on all admissible triples.

Relation to classical deformation schemes.

Compact hyperbolic surfaces admit no nontrivial continuous isometry group:
every Killing vector field on
(M,g_{\Gamma})
vanishes, so

\mathrm{Isom}(M,g_{\Gamma})
is finite.
Consequently,
M
carries no smooth
\mathbb{T}^{k}
–action by isometries and no nontrivial

\mathbb{R}^{d}
–action at all.
This excludes the standard frameworks of Rieffel, Connes–Landi, and Kasprzak,
all of which rely on such continuous actions to generate a
2
–cocycle twist
of the function algebra.
In particular, Rieffel’s method requires a smooth
\mathbb{R}^{d}
–action, which on a surface of
genus
g\!\geq\!2
must be trivial; Connes–Landi deformations require an isometric torus action;
and Kasprzak’s dual cocycle approach presupposes a locally compact group action.
The present spectral
3
–cocycle deformation therefore operates in a genuinely new setting:
it produces an associative, analytic deformation of
C^{\infty}(M)

even when
M
possesses no continuous symmetries whatsoever.

8.2
The two–sphere
\boldsymbol{S^{2}}

The round two–sphere
(S^{2},g_{\mathrm{rd}})
admits no effective isometric action of a torus of rank
\geq 2
(its isometry group is
SO(3)
, whose maximal abelian subgroup is a circle), so it lies outside all Rieffel– or Connes–Landi–type deformation schemes. Nevertheless, its Laplace spectrum provides a natural setting for an intrinsically spectral
deformation.

Let
\{\phi_{\ell m}\}_{\ell\geq 0,\,-\ell\leq m\leq\ell}
denote the spherical harmonics

\phi_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi):=Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\varphi)=\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\,\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}\,P_{\ell}^{m}(\cos\theta)\,e^{im\varphi},

P_{\ell}^{m}(x):=(-1)^{m}(1-x^{2})^{m/2}\,\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}P_{\ell}(x),

P_{\ell}(x):=\frac{1}{2^{\ell}\ell!}\,\frac{d^{\ell}}{dx^{\ell}}\!\big[(x^{2}-1)^{\ell}\big].

The
\{\phi_{\ell m}\}_{\ell\geq 0,\,-\ell\leq m\leq\ell}
form an orthonormal eigenbasis of the Laplace–Beltrami operator,

\Delta\,\phi_{\ell m}=\lambda_{\ell}\,\phi_{\ell m},\qquad\lambda_{\ell}=\ell(\ell+1).

The classical product of spherical harmonics expands via the Gaunt coefficients
[
26
]
,

\phi_{\ell_{1}m_{1}}\,\phi_{\ell_{2}m_{2}}=\sum_{\ell_{3},m_{3}}C^{\;\ell_{3}m_{3}}_{\ell_{1}m_{1},\;\ell_{2}m_{2}}\,\phi_{\ell_{3}m_{3}},

which satisfy the standard Clebsch–Gordan selection rules
[
27
]
.

Spectral deformation.

Define the spectral weight

\omega(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}):=\exp\!\big(i\,F(\lambda_{\ell_{1}},\lambda_{\ell_{2}},\lambda_{\ell_{3}})\big),\qquad F(a,b,c)=a+b-c.

(37)

The deformed product of two smooth functions
f,g\in C^{\infty}(S^{2})
is defined on
spectral coefficients by

(f\star_{\omega}g)_{\ell_{3}m_{3}}=\sum_{\ell_{1},m_{1}}\sum_{\ell_{2},m_{2}}\omega(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})\,C^{\;\ell_{3}m_{3}}_{\ell_{1}m_{1},\;\ell_{2}m_{2}}\,\widehat{f}(\ell_{1}m_{1})\,\widehat{g}(\ell_{2}m_{2}),

(38)

where
\widehat{f}(\ell m)=\langle f,\phi_{\ell m}\rangle_{L^{2}}
.

Because
\omega
depends only on the Laplace eigenvalues, the deformation is

\mathrm{SO}(3)
–equivariant:
each isometry
R\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
acts by

U_{R}(f\star g)=(U_{R}f)\star(U_{R}g)
.
The
\star
–product preserves the
\ell
–isotypic decomposition of
L^{2}(S^{2})
,
and the constant mode
\phi_{00}
remains central.
The deformation is driven purely by the spectral geometry of the round metric, with
noncommutativity introduced through the Casimir–linear phase in (
37
).
Hence this example provides one of the simplest and most geometric instances of the spectral–geometric deformation framework, independent of any group–action paradigm.

9.3. Deformation on the three–sphere
\boldsymbol{S^{3}}
via
\boldsymbol{SU(2)}
matrix elements

The group manifold
S^{3}\simeq SU(2)
carries the bi–invariant Laplacian

\Delta=-\sum_{i=1}^{3}X_{i}^{2},\qquad[X_{i},X_{j}]=2\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\,X_{k},

whose eigenfunctions are the Wigner matrix elements
t^{\ell}_{mn}
of the irreducible representations of
SU(2)
.
They satisfy

\Delta\,t^{\ell}_{mn}=\lambda_{\ell}\,t^{\ell}_{mn},\qquad\lambda_{\ell}=4\,\ell(\ell+1),

with half–integer
\ell\geq 0
and integers
m,n
such that
-\ell\leq m,n\leq\ell
.

Spectral 3–cocycle deformation.

For three spectral labels
(\ell_{i},m_{i},n_{i})
,
i=1,2,3
, we define the phase

\displaystyle\omega\!\left((\ell_{1},m_{1},n_{1}),(\ell_{2},m_{2},n_{2}),(\ell_{3},m_{3},n_{3})\right)

(39)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\exp\!\Big[-\pi i\,\big(m_{1}(n_{2}-n_{3})+m_{2}(n_{3}-n_{1})+m_{3}(n_{1}-n_{2})\big)\Big],

This expression is antisymmetric under permutation of the three triples and satisfies the fusion
3–cocycle identity. The deformed product of matrix elements is then

t^{\ell_{1}}_{m_{1}n_{1}}\star_{\omega}t^{\ell_{2}}_{m_{2}n_{2}}=\!\!\sum_{\ell,m,n}\!\!\omega\!\left((\ell_{1},m_{1},n_{1}),(\ell_{2},m_{2},n_{2}),(\ell,m,n)\right)\,C^{\ell m}_{\ell_{1}m_{1},\,\ell_{2}m_{2}}\,C^{\ell n}_{\ell_{1}n_{1},\,\ell_{2}n_{2}}\,t^{\ell}_{mn},

(40)

where
C^{\ell m}_{\ell_{1}m_{1},\,\ell_{2}m_{2}}
are the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients of
SU(2)
.

This construction yields a non–abelian, bi–covariant spectral deformation of the function
algebra on
S^{3}
, driven entirely by the Laplace spectrum of
SU(2)
and the Clebsch–Gordan
fusion tensor.
It is genuinely distinct from the usual quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{su}(2))
:
here the twist is a spectral 3–cocycle acting on eigenfunction labels, not a
Drinfel’d 2–cocycle twisting of a Hopf algebra structure.
No coproduct, antipode, or algebraic
R
–matrix is deformed;
instead, the noncommutativity arises directly from spectral geometry.
Consequently,
(C^{\infty}(S^{3}),\star_{\omega})
provides a new
class of smooth noncommutative three–spheres,
spectrally induced yet fully covariant under the classical
SU(2)\times SU(2)
action.

Remark.

More general choices of the phase function in (
39
),
such as
\exp[i\,F(\lambda_{\ell_{1}},\lambda_{\ell_{2}},\lambda_{\ell_{3}})]

where
F
is an additive function of the Casimir (Laplace) eigenvalues, yield continuous families of such spectral 3–cocycles.
The choice above gives a very simple closed expression while preserving
left–right covariance of the
SU(2)
action.

9
Discussion

The results presented here establish that associative noncommutative structures can be generated directly from the intrinsic spectral geometry of a Riemannian manifold, without invoking any external group action, symmetry, or Hopf algebra. The deformation of
C^{\infty}(M)
emerges entirely from the Laplace spectrum and the fusion tensor of eigenfunctions, mediated by a unit–modulus spectral weight
\omega(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
satisfying the fusion 3–cocycle identity. This single analytic requirement proves both necessary and sufficient for associativity of the
\star_{\omega}
–product, revealing a new cohomological layer inherent to spectral analysis.

This 3–cocycle formulation extends and unifies earlier 2–cocycle and bicharacter deformations in operator–algebraic frameworks. By lifting the twisting mechanism from the representation or group level to the spectral level, the deformation becomes intrinsic to the manifold itself. The analytic continuity results and functoriality under Cartesian products confirm that the construction behaves naturally within differential–geometric categories, while examples on
S^{2}
,
S^{3}
, and hyperbolic surfaces illustrate that noncommutativity can arise even in the complete absence of continuous symmetries.

Conceptually, the framework exposes deformation as a spectral phenomenon governed by higher cocycles, providing an analytic bridge between noncommutative geometry, harmonic analysis, and cohomological deformation theory. It demonstrates that the familiar group–action paradigm is a special case of a more fundamental spectral–cohomological principle. The resulting algebras are smooth, associative, and geometrically contravariant, offering a new class of noncommutative manifolds beyond the reach of quantum–group or toric techniques.

The principal novelty of the present framework lies in its ability to produce

analytic
, associative deformations of
C^{\infty}(M)
without relying on any
underlying group action, Poisson/symplectic tensor, or global symmetry.
Whereas most deformation schemes—from Rieffel’s and Connes–Landi’s to
Drinfel’d or Fedosov–Kontsevich constructions—depend on external symmetry data,
the spectral–geometric deformation acts directly on the intrinsic Laplace
spectrum and fusion coefficients of
(M,g)
.
This independence from symmetry establishes that noncommutativity can emerge
solely from the manifold’s internal analytic structure, encoded in its eigenbasis.
Consequently, the deformation is valid even on manifolds with trivial or
nonexistent isometry groups, thereby extending quantization beyond the
homogeneous or toric settings of earlier approaches.
Moreover, the spectral
3
–cocycle condition replaces algebraic associators or
bicharacters with an intrinsically geometric cohomological constraint, revealing
a deeper layer of analytic control over the emergence of noncommutative geometry.
The resulting theory thus offers a unified spectral origin for deformation,
integrating harmonic analysis, cohomology, and differential geometry into a
single analytic mechanism.

In summary, the spectral–geometric deformation framework presented here establishes a new paradigm: noncommutativity can be generated from geometry alone. The combination of analytic rigor, geometric universality, and categorical coherence places this approach as a candidate foundation for a fully spectral theory of deformation in noncommutative geometry.

10
Outlook

The spectral–geometric deformation introduced in this paper provides an intrinsically
analytic and functorial mechanism for producing noncommutative function algebras on compact
Riemannian manifolds. Several natural extensions arise from this framework, both analytic and
categorical, which we outline below.

1. Cohomological classification

We believe the spectral-geometric deformation can be naturally classified using a cohomology theory for our weight functions
\omega
.

2. Fedosov/Kontsevich deformation.

The relationship between the spectral-geometric deformation constructed here and the formal deformation quantization frameworks of Fedosov and Kontsevich needs to be investigated. In particular, one could look for an equivalence between the spectral-geometric deformation of function algebras and the Fedosov–Kontsevich formal star product on symplectic manifolds. In any case, this comparison is expected to clarify the conceptual and structural correspondence between strict and formal approaches to noncommutative geometry.

3. Deformation of Hermitian bundles.

An interesting direction is to extend the present construction from scalar functions to sections of Hermitian vector bundles
E\to M
. We would like to study a deformed module structure on
\Gamma(E)
and obtain new examples of projective finitely generated modules over
(C^{\infty}(M),\star_{\omega})
.

4. C
∗
–algebraic realizations.

The smooth deformations constructed here admit
C^{*}
–completions forming continuous fields of
C^{*}
–algebras. Future work will develop explicit examples where the resulting algebras are of
AF type, continuous–trace, or have nontrivial Dixmier–Douady invariants. These cases would
connect the present spectral framework with classical classification theory of
C^{*}
–algebras.

5. Foliated manifolds and homogeneous spaces.

It is natural to extend the construction to Riemannian foliations and to homogeneous spaces

G/H
of compact Lie groups. The foliation setting may lead to new spectral models of
noncommutative leaf spaces, while the homogeneous case might link to quantum group
deformations and to the analytic approach of Neshveyev and Tuset
[
28
]
.

6. K–theory and Hochschild or cyclic cohomology.

Since the deformation is implemented spectrally, the K–theory, cyclic and Hochschild
(co)homology of
(C^{\infty}(M),\star_{\omega})
can be studied explicitly via spectral
perturbation techniques. We expect the K–theory to remain isomorphic to that of

C^{\infty}(M)
, while the Hochschild or cyclic cohomology may encode curvature corrections of the
underlying geometry.

7. Monoidal structures and tensor categories.

The fusion–cocycle viewpoint lends a categorical interpretation: admissible weights
\omega

form a group under composition, and the deformed algebras constitute objects in a monoidal
category where morphisms are spectrum–preserving maps. Developing this tensor‐categorical
structure would connect the spectral–geometric deformation to braided and quasi–Hopf
categories arising in quantum topology.

8. Noncompact extensions.

Although the present work assumes compactness to ensure discrete Laplace spectrum,
the formalism extends to noncompact manifolds by replacing spectral sums with integrals
against the spectral measure. This requires weighted Sobolev analysis and scattering
theory, leading to a continuous version of the deformation analogous to warped convolution
and modular deformations in AQFT.

9. Spin geometry and spectral triples.

Replacing the Laplacian by the Dirac operator
D
on a spin manifold should give rise to a
spectral deformation at the level of spectral triples
(A,H,D)
. The resulting

(A_{\omega},H,D)
would be an isospectral and KK–equivalent deformation in the sense of
Connes
[
29
]
, potentially yielding new analytic examples of noncommutative spin geometries.

10. Relation to locally compact quantum groups.

Another direction is to compare the intrinsic spectral deformation with the cocycle and
crossed–product deformations of locally compact quantum groups developed by
Neshveyev and Tuset
[
12
]
. The aim is to determine when the two coincide and whether the spectral
version is able to produce genuinely new deformations beyond the quantum–group setting.

11. Representation theory and physical models.

The representation theory of the deformed algebras, especially their spectral and unitary
representations, remains largely open. Understanding these may reveal new quantum‐mechanical
or field‐theoretic models whose observables are governed by spectral–geometric rather than
Poisson or symmetry data.

Overall, the spectral–geometric framework opens a new landscape
for future research, unifying classical geometric quantization, operator algebraic
deformation, and noncommutative geometry through a single spectral paradigm.

Abstract
. Modular units are functions on modular curves whose divisors are supported on the cusps. They form a free abelian group of rank at most one less than the number of cusps. In this paper we study the group of modular units on X 1 ​ ( p ) X_{1}(p) , with prime level p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 . We give an explicit basis for this group and study certain rational subgroups of it. We use the basis to numerically investigate the structure of the cuspidal group of X 1 ​ ( p ) X_{1}(p) and its rational subgroup. In the later stages of this paper we use our basis to determine a specific large quotient of the cuspidal group.

1.1.
Set up

In this paper we study the group of modular units on
X_{1}(p)
, where
p\geq 5
is prime.

Definition 1.1
.

A
modular unit
for a congruence subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is a meromorphic function on the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
, with no zeros or poles in
\mathbb{H}
and which is invariant under the action of
\Gamma
.

It follows that any modular unit for
\Gamma
is a function on the associated complete modular curve
X_{\Gamma}
, whose divisor is supported on cusps only. It is for this reasons that we refer to a modular unit for
\Gamma
as a
modular unit on

X_{\Gamma}
, and vice versa. Throughout this paper we use these two notions interchangeably.

The modular units that we construct are products of
Siegel functions
, see Section
3
for details. Associated to any
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{2}
there is a meromorphic function
g_{\mathbf{a}}:\mathbb{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
which has no zeros or poles in
\mathbb{H}
. The work of Kubert and Lang (see Theorem
8
) completely describes the modular units of
\Gamma(p)
in terms of Siegel functions. Our aim is to understand when such functions are invariant under the action of
\Gamma_{1}(p)\supseteq\Gamma(p)
. This leads us to the following natural construction, as detailed in Section
4
.

For the rest of this paper we fix a prime number
p\geq 5
and an integer

whose reduction modulo

has order

. All constructions in this paper depend on this choice of

Let

and for

E_{i}(\tau)=g_{\left(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},0\right)}(\tau)\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{p-2}g_{\left(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},\frac{\alpha^{j}}{p}\right)}(\tau)\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ F_{i}(\tau)=g_{\left(0,\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p}\right)}(\tau).

In Section

we give necessary and sufficient conditions for products of such functions to be modular units on

to parametrise the cusps of

fall into a natural partition:

As detailed in Section

\{x\}\in\left[0,1\right)
denotes the fractional part of

.

3.2.
Modular Units on
X(p)

Kubert and Lang
[
9
, Chapter 4, Theorem 1.3]
proved that if

is coprime to

, the modular units on

are products of Siegel functions.

Theorem 8
.

(Kubert, Lang)
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\text{gcd}\left(N,6\right)=1
and let

(3.1)

f(\tau)=\displaystyle\prod_{\mathbf{a}}g_{\mathbf{a}}(\tau)^{e_{\mathbf{a}}}

where the product is taken over all
\mathbf{a}\in\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and the exponents are integers satisfying the following congruence relations:

•

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}}e_{\mathbf{a}}\left(a_{1}N\right)^{2}\equiv 0\mod{N};

•

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}}e_{\mathbf{a}}\left(a_{2}N\right)^{2}\equiv 0\mod{N};

•

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}}e_{\mathbf{a}}\left(a_{1}N\right)\left(a_{2}N\right)\equiv 0\mod{N};

•

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}}e_{\mathbf{a}}\equiv 0\ \text{mod}\ 12
.

Then

is a modular unit on
X\left(N\right)
.
Furthermore, all modular units on
X\left(N\right)
are, up to scaling, of the above form.

In particular, this results fully describes the modular units on
X(p)
, for prime level

. As
\Gamma(p)\subset\Gamma_{1}(p)
, modular units on
X_{1}(p)
are necessarily modular units on
X(p)
, and hence of the above form. In Section

we determine when such products of Siegel functions are additionally invariant under
\Gamma_{1}(p)/\Gamma(p)
.

4.
Constructing Modular Units on
X_{1}(p)

The main result of this section gives a necessary and sufficient criteria for certain products of Siegel functions to be modular units on the curve
X_{1}(p)
. In the proceeding section, we prove that all modular units on
X_{1}\left(p\right)
are of this form.

For the rest of this paper we fix an integer
\alpha
whose reduction modulo
p
has multiplicative order
p-1
in
\left(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)^{*}
. All of our constructions use this fixed
\alpha
. We set
n=\frac{p-1}{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and for
0\leq i\leq n-1
define:

•

E_{i}(\tau)=g_{\left(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},0\right)}(\tau)\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{p-2}g_{\left(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},\frac{\alpha^{j}}{p}\right)}(\tau);

•

F_{i}(\tau)=g_{\left(0,\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p}\right)}(\tau)
.

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 9
.

Let
e_{i},f_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
and set

(4.1)

f(\tau)=\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}E_{i}(\tau)^{e_{i}}F_{i}(\tau)^{f_{i}}.

Then
f
is a modular unit for
\Gamma_{1}(p)
if and only if the exponents satisfy the following congruence conditions:

•

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_{i}\alpha^{2i}\equiv 0\mod{p};

•

\ \displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f_{i}\alpha^{2i}\equiv 0\mod{p};

•

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}pe_{i}+f_{i}\equiv\ 0\mod{12}.

We first note that in the notation of Theorem
8
, for any
f
as (
4.1
):

•

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}}e_{\mathbf{a}}\left(a_{1}N\right)^{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}pe_{i}\alpha^{2i}\ \equiv\ 0\ \text{mod}\ p,

•

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}}e_{\mathbf{a}}\left(a_{2}N\right)^{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_{i}\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}\alpha^{2j}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f_{i}\alpha^{2i}\equiv\ \displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f_{i}\alpha^{2i}\ \equiv\ 0\ \text{mod}\ p,

•

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}}e_{\mathbf{a}}\left(a_{1}N\right)\left(a_{2}N\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_{i}\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}\alpha^{i+j}\ \equiv\ 0\ \text{mod}\ p,

•

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}}e_{\mathbf{a}}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}pe_{i}+f_{i}\ \equiv\ 0\ \text{mod}\ 12,

and hence
f
is a modular unit for
\Gamma(p)
. Therefore, to prove that such an

is a modular unit for
\Gamma_{1}(p)
it suffices to show

f(\gamma\tau)=f(\tau)

where
\gamma
runs through any set of coset representatives of
\Gamma_{1}(p)/\Gamma(p)
. Our argument uses the following preliminary results; the first of which fixes a good choice of representatives with our notation, and the second proves transformation formulae for
E_{i}
and
F_{j}
under these representatives.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\gamma_{k}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\alpha^{k}\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for
k=0,\ldots p-2
. Then
\{\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{p-2},I_{2\times 2}\}
is a complete set of coset representaives for
\Gamma_{1}(p)/\Gamma(p)

Proof.

For
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{1}(p)
with

coprime to

,

for some

, and one shows that
\gamma\gamma_{k}^{-1}\in\Gamma(p)
.
∎

Proposition 11
.

Let
\gamma=\gamma_{k}
for some
k\in\{0,\ldots,p-2\}
be as in Lemma
10
. For any
i\in\{0,\ldots n-1\}

•

E_{i}(\gamma\tau)=\zeta\left(\gamma\right)^{p}(-1)^{\alpha^{i+k}}\exp\left(\frac{\pi i\alpha^{2i+k}}{p}\right)\ E_{i}(\tau),

•

F_{i}(\gamma\tau)=\zeta\left(\gamma\right)F_{i}(\tau),

where
\zeta\left(\gamma\right)
is a
12
th root of unity, which depends on
\gamma
only (c.f Proposition
6
).

Proof.

The result is immediate for
F_{i}
by Proposition
6
. From the definition of

and Proposition

we obtain

\displaystyle E_{i}(\gamma\tau)

\displaystyle=\zeta\left(\gamma\right)^{p}g_{(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},0)\gamma}(\tau)\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{p-2}g_{(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},\frac{\alpha^{j}}{p})\gamma}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\zeta\left(\gamma\right)^{p}g_{(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},\frac{\alpha^{i+k}}{p})}(\tau)\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{p-2}g_{(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},\frac{\alpha^{i+k}+\alpha^{j}}{p})}(\tau)

where

is as before. We set

and note that for all

we have

for some unique

\displaystyle g_{(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},\frac{\alpha^{i+k}+\alpha^{j}}{p}))}(\tau)

\displaystyle=g_{(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},\frac{\alpha^{m_{j}}}{p})+(0,n_{j})}(\tau)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n_{j}}\exp\left(\pi in_{j}\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p}\right)g_{(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},\frac{\alpha^{m_{j}}}{p})}(\tau).

Hence substituting this into the above expression, we obtain

E_{i}\left(\gamma\tau\right)=\zeta\left(\gamma\right)^{p}\left(-1\right)^{\sum_{j}n_{j}}\exp\left(\pi i\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p}\sum_{j}n_{j}\right)\displaystyle\prod_{j}g_{(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},\frac{\alpha^{m_{j}}}{p})}(\tau)

Note that the function

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}=\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}\frac{1}{p}\left(\alpha^{i+k}+\alpha^{j}-\alpha^{m_{j}}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\alpha^{i+k}}{p}=\alpha^{i+k}

∎

Theorem 14

The cuspidal class number

Proof.

In
[

Fix

All of the functions above are modular units for

12p^{2}
. To compute the determinant of
B
we consider the
(p-1)\times(p-1)
block matrix:

J=\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}-\frac{1}{12}&\dots&a_{n-1}-\frac{1}{12}&0&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{n-1}-\frac{1}{12}&\dots&a_{n-2}-\frac{1}{12}&0&\dots&0\\
0&\dots&0&a_{0}-\frac{1}{12}&\dots&a_{n-1}-\frac{1}{12}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\dots&0&a_{n-1}-\frac{1}{12}&\dots&a_{n-2}-\frac{1}{12}\\
\end{bmatrix}

and we perform the following sequence of row and column operations on
J
:

1.

add all rows to the
(n+1)
th row;

2.

add all columns to the last one;

3.

factor out
-2n
from the
n+1
th row;

4.

add the
n+1
th row to all other rows.

Applying Lemma
15
, we the resulting matrix is:

-2n\cdot\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}&\dots&a_{n-1}&\frac{1}{12}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{n-1}&\dots&a_{n-2}&\frac{1}{12}&\dots&0\\
\frac{1}{12}&\dots&\frac{1}{12}&\frac{1}{12}&\dots&\frac{2n}{12}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{1}{12}&\dots&\frac{1}{12}&a_{n-1}&\dots&0\\
\end{bmatrix}
.

Expanding by the last column, the determinant of
J
is:

\text{det}\left(J\right)=-2n\left(\frac{2n}{12}\right)\text{det}\left(B\right)
.

Since
J
is a block matrix, we find

\text{det}\left(J\right)=\text{det}\left(N\right)^{2}

where
N
is the matrix defined in Lemma
16
. Thus

\text{det}\left(B\right)=\frac{-1}{12}\displaystyle\prod_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\frac{1}{4}B_{2,\chi}
,

and hence

|\text{det}\left(M\right)|=|\text{det}\left(A\right)||\text{det}\left(B\right)|=p^{2}|\displaystyle\prod_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}B_{2,\chi}|^{2}

and so our result follows from Theorem
14
.
∎

We remarked that although the function
H_{0}(\tau)
was defined at the beginning of this section, it does not appear in our basis. We give the explicit presentation of this function in terms of our basis and conclude with a corollary which determines different bases of
\mathcal{F}(p)
containing
H_{0}
.

Consider the function

\displaystyle F(\tau)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}G_{i}(\tau)H_{i}(\tau)=f(\tau)E_{n-1}(\tau)^{C-1}F_{n-1}(\tau)^{D-1}

where
f(\tau)=\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}E_{i}(\tau)F_{i}(\tau)
and the integer constants
C,D
are defined as

•

C=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}-\alpha^{2i+2}=\frac{\alpha^{2n}-\alpha^{2}}{1-\alpha^{2}};

•

D=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}p\left(\alpha^{2i+2}-1\right)-\alpha^{2i+2}+p\beta\left(\alpha^{2i+2}-1\right)-p\beta\left(n-1\right)
.

The function
f(\tau)
is a modular unit for
\Gamma_{1}(p)
by Proposition
9
and since the order of vanishing at any cusp is
0
, it is necessarily a constant function. Let
C_{1}
be this constant value. We further observe that
C-1
is divisible by
p
and let
C_{2}=\frac{C-1}{p}
. Then

F(\tau)=C_{1}G_{n-1}(\tau)^{C_{2}}F_{n-1}(\tau)^{\beta\left(C-1\right)+D-1}
.

By our choice of
\beta
,
\beta(C-1)+D-1\ \equiv\ 0\mod{12p}
, and thus

F(\tau)=C_{1}G_{n-1}(\tau)^{C_{2}}H_{n-1}(\tau)^{C_{3}}

where
C_{3}=\frac{1}{12p}\left(\beta(C-1)+D-1\right)\in\mathbb{Z}
. We deduce the following.

Corollary 17
.

Any subset of
\{G_{0}(\tau),\ldots,G_{n-1}(\tau),H_{0}(\tau),\ldots,H_{n-1}(\tau)\}
consisting of
p-2
functions and containing
G_{n-1}
and
H_{n-1}
is a basis for the group of modular units for
\Gamma_{1}(p)
modulo constants.

6.
Subgroups of Modular Units

There are two natural subgroups of modular units which one is lead to consider when studying the rational cuspidal group
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q})
. In this section we write

\Div_{c}^{\infty}(p)
for the group of degree zero divisors supported on
P_{0},\ldots,P_{n-1}
and
\Div_{c}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
for the group of degree
0
divisors supported on cusps and fixed by the action of Galois. Furthermore, we denote by
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(p)
and
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
the subgroups of
\mathcal{F}(p)
consisting of functions with divisor in
\Div_{c}^{\infty}(p)
and

\Div_{c}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
respectively. The arising quotients:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\infty}(p)=\Div_{c}^{\infty}(p)/\langle\divv(f):f\in\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(p)\rangle

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p):=\Div_{c}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)/\langle\divv(f):f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)\rangle

are natural subgroups of the
rational cuspidal subgroup

\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q})
, that is the subgroup consisting of elements of
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)
fixed by the action of Galois, and we have the clear inclusions:

\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\infty}(p)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q}).

In this this section we construct explicit bases for
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(p)
and
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
modulo constants and prove the the above inequalities are in fact equalities.

6.1.
Proof of Theorem
2

In
[
26
, Theorem 1]
Yang finds a basis
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}
for
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(p)
, constructed using
n-1
functions
\tilde{E}_{i}(\tau)
, which are defined as products of scaled Siegel functions. We construct
n-1
functions
I_{1},\ldots I_{n-1}
and prove that their orders of vanishing at each cusps coincide with those of the
f_{i}
. More precisely, let
\gamma=\alpha^{p-2}
and define

\displaystyle I_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{i-1}(\tau)E_{i+1}(\tau)^{\gamma^{2}}}{E_{i}(\tau)^{\gamma^{2}+1}}

for
1\leq i\leq n-2
and
I_{n-1}(\tau)=\left(\frac{E_{n-2}(\tau)}{E_{n-1}(\tau)}\right)^{p}
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

The basis constructed in
[
26
, Theoren 1]
is as follows: for
1\leq i\leq n-2

\displaystyle f_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{E}_{i-1}(\tau)\tilde{E}_{i+1}(\tau)^{\gamma^{2}}}{\tilde{E}_{i}(\tau)^{\gamma^{2}+1}}

and

\displaystyle f_{n-1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\tilde{E}_{n-2}(\tau)}{\tilde{E}_{n-1}(\tau)}\right)^{p}.

where
\tilde{E}_{i}(\tau)=E_{\alpha^{i}}^{(p)}(\tau)=-g_{(\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p},0)}(p\tau)
, as defined in

From
[

we deduce that for
1\leq i\leq n-2
and

\displaystyle\text{ord}_{P_{k}}\left(f_{i}\right)=\frac{p}{2}B_{2}\left(\left\{\frac{\alpha^{i-1+k}}{p}\right\}\right)+\frac{p\gamma^{2}}{2}B_{2}\left(\left\{\frac{\alpha^{i+1+k}}{p}\right\}\right)-\frac{p(\gamma^{2}+1)}{2}B_{2}\left(\left\{\frac{\alpha^{i+k}}{2}\right\}\right),

\displaystyle\text{ord}_{Q_{k}}\left(f_{i}\right)=0,

and similarly, for all

\displaystyle\text{ord}_{P_{k}}\left(f_{n-1}\right)=\frac{p^{2}}{2}\left(B_{2}\left(\left\{\frac{\alpha^{n-2+k}}{p}\right\}\right)-B_{2}\left(\left\{\frac{\alpha^{n-1+k}}{p}\right\}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\text{ord}_{Q_{k}}\left(f_{n-1}\right)=0.

Using Proposition
13
, we verify that the orders of vanishing of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{n-1}
at each cusp coincide with the above.
∎

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
3

Notably,
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(p)
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
, and we extend the basis
I_{1},\ldots,I_{n-1}
constructed in the previous section to basis of
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
as follows.
In our choice of model the cusps
P_{i}
are rational and the
Q_{j}
form a single orbit under the Galois action. For
f\in\mathcal{F}(p)
, its divisor is fixed by the action of Galois if and only if it is of the form

(6.1)

\divv f=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d_{i}P_{i}+d\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}Q_{i}

for integers
d_{0},\ldots,d_{n-1},d
.

When
p\equiv 1\mod{4}
, define:

(6.2)

I_{n}\left(\tau\right)=\left(E_{0}\left(\tau\right)E_{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\tau\right)\right)^{6}.

When
p\ \equiv\ 3\mod{4}
, let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that

(6.3)

\alpha^{2m}\equiv\begin{cases}-2\mod{p}&\text{if}\ \ p\equiv 3\mod{8}\\
2\mod{p}&\text{if}\ \ p\equiv 7\mod{8}.\end{cases}

We assume, without loss of generality, that
m
is the unique integer with the above property and
0\leq m<n
. Define

(6.4)

I_{n}\left(\tau\right)=\begin{cases}E_{0}\left(\tau\right)^{8}E_{m}\left(\tau\right)^{4}\ &\text{if}\ p\equiv 3\mod{8}\\
E_{0}\left(\tau\right)^{24}E_{m}\left(\tau\right)^{-12}\ &\text{if}\ p\equiv 7\mod{8}.\end{cases}

It follows from Proposition
9
that in all cases
I_{n}
is a function on

and using Proposition
13
we deduce that its order of vanishing at each
Q_{i}
is
1
. We prove that
I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}
is a basis for
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
modulo constants.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

The divisor of any
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
is necessarily of the form

(6.5)

\divv(f)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d_{i}P_{i}+d\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}Q_{i}

for an integer

. As the order of vanishing of
I_{n}
at each

is

, we have
f/I_{n}^{d}\in\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(p)
; and hence, up to scaling,

is in the span of

.
∎

6.3.
Rational Cuspidal Subgroup

As previously discussed, the cuspidal group
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)
has three interesting rational subgroups, with the following clear inclusions:

\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\infty}(p)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)\left(\mathbb{Q}\right).

Below we prove that the above inclusions are in fact equalities, and thus in this case we have a well defined
rational cuspidal group
and an efficient method of computing it using the basis constructed in Theorem
2
.

The first equality is a simple corollary of Theorem
3
.

Corollary 18
.

\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\infty}\left(p\right)=\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left(p\right).

Proof.

Take any
\left[D\right]\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left(p\right)\setminus\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\infty}\left(p\right)
, thus

D=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d_{i}P_{i}+d\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}Q_{i}

for integers
d_{0},\ldots,d_{n-1},d
. Then
D\sim D-\divv(I_{n}^{d})=D^{\prime}
, and
\left[D^{\prime}\right]\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\infty}\left(p\right)
.
∎

The second equality is not as clear, but can still be proved using elementary methods.

Proposition 19
.

\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}}(p)=\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)\left(\mathbb{Q}\right).

Proof.

By definition, there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\divv(\mathcal{F}(p))\longrightarrow\Div_{c}(p)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)\longrightarrow 0

We observe that the above is in fact an exact sequence of
\Gal\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}\right)
-modules, and we take Galois cohomology:

\Div_{c}(p)\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{1}\left(p\right)\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)\longrightarrow H^{1}\left(\text{Gal}\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}\right),\divv\left(\mathcal{F}\left(p\right)\right)\right)

and our claim follows if we show
H^{1}\left(\text{Gal}\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}\right),\text{div}\left(\mathcal{F}_{1}\left(p\right)\right)\right)=0
.
This follows from the fact that the absolute Galois group acts through
\text{Gal}\left(\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{p}\right)^{+}/\mathbb{Q}\right)
, which is a finite cyclic subgroup. Let
\sigma
be a generator of the Galois group, and
N=1+\sigma+\ldots+\sigma^{n-1}
. As shown in
[
8
, Chapter 10, page 189]

our problem reduces to proving

\{D\in\divv\left(\mathcal{F}(p)\right)\ :ND=0\}=\left(1-\sigma\right)\divv\left(\mathcal{F}(p)\right)

and this is clear since the Galois action fixes the
P_{i}
and permutes the
Q_{i}
cyclically.
∎

Computing the subgroup
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\infty}(p)=\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)
is of particular interest since it’s closely related to the rational torsion subgroup of
J_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q})_{{\operatorname{tors}}}
. More specifically, Ohta
[
18
]
proved that the two groups are equal up to
2-
torsion.

7.2.
Explicit Bases of Divisors for
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)
and
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q})

We our bases of modular units to explicitly investigate the structure of the cuspidal group and its rational subgroup. More specifically, for certain
p
in the previously stated interval, we find independent cuspidal divisors
D_{i}
and
D_{j}^{\prime}
such that

\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)=\langle[D_{1}]\rangle\oplus\ldots\oplus\langle[D_{l}]\rangle
and
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q})=\langle[D^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}]\rangle\oplus\ldots\oplus\langle[D^{{}^{\prime}}_{k}]\rangle
.

In what follows, we write
D=P_{0}-Q_{0}
and
D^{\prime}=P_{0}-P_{n-1}
. We found that for all

,
[D]
and
[D^{\prime}]
generate the largest factor in the abstract decomposition of the cuspidal and rational cuspidal group respectively. We study the orders of these elements in detail in the next section.

\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)
and
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q})
have no small factors, linear combinations of

and

are sufficient to generate our groups. Analysing our calculations, we note that there are two prominent structures. This is extremely likely to be a small prime phenomena, and we expect the general structure of the cuspidal group to be significantly more complicated as the level increases, however, we hope that by studying these simpler structures, we can gain a useful insight into the general structure of the cuspidal group.
Firstly, suppose that

and

. More precisely, if

then

is cyclic and

. We explicitly found

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(17)=\langle[D^{\prime}+2D]\rangle\oplus\langle[D]\rangle,\ \ \ \mathcal{C}_{1}(17)(\mathbb{Q})=\langle[D^{\prime}]\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(19)=\langle[D^{\prime}+3D\rangle\oplus\langle[D]\rangle,\ \ \ \mathcal{C}_{1}(19)(\mathbb{Q})=\langle[D^{\prime}]\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(37)=\langle[D^{\prime}+3D]\rangle\oplus\langle[D]\rangle,\ \ \ \mathcal{C}_{1}(11)(\mathbb{Q})=\langle[D^{\prime}]\rangle.

For all other named primes in our interval we found that

\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)=\langle[nD^{\prime}+n^{2}D]\rangle\oplus\langle[D]\rangle
and

,

where

There are

8.
Orders of Cuspidal Divisors

In this section we give explicit formulae for the orders of the elements
D=P_{0}-Q_{0}
and
D^{\prime}=P_{0}-P_{n-1}
in
J_{1}(p)
. Computing these involves working with certain
circulant matrices
whose entries correspond to the orders of vanishing of the functions
E_{i}
and
F_{j}
at cusps. In the first subsection we give some general results on circulant matrices. In the final two subsections we apply these results to compute the orders of
D
and
D^{\prime}
.

8.1.
Circulant Matrices

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k})\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
. The
circulant matrix
defined by
\mathbf{c}
is the
k\times k
matrix

\Circ(\mathbf{c}):=\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}&c_{2}&\ldots&c_{k-1}&c_{k}\\
c_{2}&c_{3}&\ldots&c_{k}&c_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
c_{k}&c_{1}&\ldots&c_{k-2}&c_{k-1}\end{bmatrix}

and the
alternative circulant matrix
defined by
\mathbf{c}
is the
k\times k
matrix:

\Circa(\mathbf{c}):=\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}&c_{2}&\ldots&c_{k-1}&c_{k}\\
c_{k}&c_{1}&\ldots&c_{k-2}&c_{k-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
c_{2}&c_{3}&\ldots&c_{k}&c_{1}\end{bmatrix}.

The two matrices are related by
\Circ(\mathbf{c})=J\Circa(\mathbf{c})
, where
J
is defined as

J=\Circ((1,0\ldots,0))=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&0&\ldots&0&1\\
0&0&\ldots&1&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&1&\ldots&0&0\end{bmatrix}
.

The following result is proved by an elementary calculation. The author believes it to be well-known, but could not find a suitable reference and thus gives a short proof the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 20
.

Fix a primitive
k
th root of unity
\omega
. For
1\leq i\leq k
, let
e_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\omega^{i-1})^{j-1}c_{j}
and
\mathbf{e}_{i}=(1,\omega^{i-1},\ldots,(\omega^{i-1})^{k-1})^{T}
. Then
e_{i}
is an eigenvalue for
\Circa(\mathbf{c})
and
\mathbf{e}_{i}
is an associated eigenvector.

Proof.

The
jth
entry of
\Circa(\mathbf{c})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{j-1}c_{k-j+1+s}(\omega^{i-1})^{s-1}+\displaystyle\sum_{s=j}^{k}c_{s+1-j}(\omega^{i-1})^{s-1}=\displaystyle\sum_{t=k+2-j}^{k}c_{t}(\omega^{i-1})^{t-k-2+j}+\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k+1-j}c_{t}(\omega^{i-1})^{j-1}=e_{i}(\omega^{i-1})^{j-1}
.

∎

From now on we write
J_{k,a}
for the
k\times k
matrix whose entries are all
a
. If
M
is any
k\times k
matrix, we write
\Bl(M,a)
for the
2k\times 2k
block matrix

\Bl(M,a)=\begin{bmatrix}M&J_{k,a}\\
J_{k,a}&M\end{bmatrix}
.

The following is an elementary calculation, whose proof is very similar to that of the previous result.

Proposition 21
.

Let
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k})\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
,
a\in\mathbb{C}
and fix
\omega
a primitive
k
th root of unity.
The pairs
(e_{i},\mathbf{e}_{i})
stated below are a complete eigensystem for the matrix
\Bl(\Circa(\mathbf{c}),a)
,

•

e=\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}c_{t}+ka
,
\mathbf{e}=(1,\ldots,1)^{T}
;

•

e=\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}c_{t}-ka
,
\mathbf{e}=(1,\ldots,1,-1,\ldots,-1)^{T}
;

•

for
2\leq i\leq k
,
e=\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}c_{t}(\omega^{i-1})^{t-1}
,
\mathbf{e}=(1,\omega^{i-1},\ldots,(\omega^{i-1})^{k-1},1,\omega^{i-1},\ldots,(\omega^{i-1})^{k-1})^{T}
;

•

for
2\leq i\leq k
,
e=\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}c_{t}(\omega^{i-1})^{t-1}
,
\mathbf{e}=(1,\omega^{i-1},\ldots,(\omega^{i-1})^{k-1},0,0,\ldots,0)^{T}
;

8.2.
Orders of Rational Cuspidal Divisors

The arguments appearing in this section are heavily inspired by those from
[
28
, Section 3]
and
[
10
, Section 2]
.

Recall that any element in
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q})
is represented by a divisor supported on
P_{0},\ldots,P_{n-1}
. Define the following sets:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}E_{i}(\tau)^{e_{i}}\ |\ e_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_{i}=0\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}=\{\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d{i}P_{i}(\tau)^{e_{i}}\ |\ d_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d_{i}=0\}.

Let
\varphi
be a homomorphism on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, defined by

\varphi(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}E_{i}(\tau)^{e_{i}})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}k_{i}P_{i}

where
k_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}e_{j}a_{[i+j]}
, with
[l+k]:=l+k\mod{n}
and
a_{i}=\frac{p}{2}B_{2}(\{\frac{\alpha^{i}}{p}\}
as before. First, we note that the image of
\varphi
lies in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, since

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}k_{i}=\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n=1}e_{j}\right)\times\left(\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n=1}a_{l}\right)=0
.

Moreover, this is in fact a bijection; and we argue this below. We begin by identifying both
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
with the following set

\displaystyle S=\{(k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1})^{T}|\ k_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}k_{i}=0\}.

We simply view
\varphi
as the function
S\rightarrow S
given by the circulant matrix
M=\Circ(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1})
, acting on the vector corresponding to the exponents. To prove that
\varphi
is a bijection, we show that
M
is invertible and explicitly compute its inverse.

For the rest of this section we fix
\omega
, a primitive
n
th root of unity. For
0\leq i\leq n-1
we write
\chi_{i}
for the even Dirichlet character modulo
p
satisfying
\chi_{i}(\alpha)=\omega^{i}
and
B_{2,i}=B_{2,\chi_{i}}=p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\chi_{i}(a)B_{2}(\frac{a}{p})
for the corresponding generalised Bernoulli number. Moreover, we write

b_{j}=\frac{4}{n}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\omega^{ji}\cdot B_{2,i})^{-1}

for
0\leq j\leq n-1
.

Proposition 22
.

The matrix
M
is invertible, with inverse

M^{-1}=\Circ(b_{0},b_{n-1},\ldots,b_{1})
.

Proof.

We write
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1})^{T}
. Recall that
\Circ(\mathbf{a})=J\Circa(\mathbf{a})
as in the previous subsection, and it follows from Proposition
20
that
\Circa(\mathbf{a})=P^{-1}\Diag(y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1})P
where:

P=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\dots&1&1\\
1&\omega&\dots&\omega^{n-2}&\omega^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&\omega^{n-1}&\dots&(\omega^{n-1})^{n-2}&(\omega^{n-1})^{n-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}
,

P^{-1}=\frac{1}{n}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\dots&1&1\\
1&\omega^{-1}&\dots&\omega^{-n+2}&\omega^{1-n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&\omega^{-n}&\dots&(\omega^{1-n})^{n-2}&(\omega^{1-n})^{n-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}
.

and
y_{i}
are the eigenvalues,

y_{i}:=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(\omega^{i})^{j}a_{j}=\displaystyle\frac{p}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\chi_{i}(\alpha^{j})B_{2}(\{\frac{\alpha^{j}}{p}\})=\frac{1}{4}B_{2,i}
.

Note that
y_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
, since they are all factors of the cuspidal class number. The result then follows after a long, but elementary matrix multiplication.
∎

To compute the order of element in
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q})
we use the following result.

Proposition 23
.

Let
e_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
f(\tau)=\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}E_{i}(\tau)^{e_{i}}
. Then
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(p)
if and only if the exponents satisfy the following congruence conditions:

(i)

e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
i
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_{i}\alpha^{2i}\equiv 0\mod{p};

(iii)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_{i}=0
.

Proof.

The reverse implication follows from Proposition
9
. If such an
f
is a modular unit and the exponents are integers, the second congruence holds by Proposition
9
. The last condition holds since the divisor of such a function has order of vanishing
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{12}e_{i}
at all cusps
Q_{j}
, and this is assumed to be zero. Thus, it remains to show that the exponents are necessarily integers. For this we argue as in
[
17
, Section 4]
. More precisely,
f=(\tilde{f})^{1/n}
where
n\geq 1
and
\tilde{f}
is a product of the
E_{i}
with integer exponents. Then
\tilde{f}
is a power series in
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
with rational coefficients, with bounded denominators, and we conclude using Ogg’s lemma from the same reference.
∎

Let
D=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d_{i}P_{i}\in\Divv^{\infty}(p)
, then by the Manin-Drinfeld theorem there exists a minimal
N\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, the order of
[D]
, such that

\divv(f)=N\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d_{i}P_{i}
,

and since
N\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d_{i}=0
and
\varphi
is invertible, up to scaling,
f\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, with integer exponents satisfying the congruences of Proposition
23
. Note that this implication also follows from the explicit basis computed in Theorem
2
. Therefore, we have the following.

Proposition 24
.

For
D=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d_{i}P_{i}\in\Divv^{\infty}(p)
, the order of
[D]\in J_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q})_{{\operatorname{tors}}}
is the smallest positive integer
N
such that

M^{-1}\cdot(Nd_{0},\ldots,Nd_{n-1})^{T}

satisfy the necessary and sufficient conditions of Proposition
23
.

We apply this proposition to
D^{\prime}=P_{0}-P_{n-1}
.

Proposition 25
.

With notation as above, define

•

c_{0}=b_{0}-b_{1}
;

•

c_{1}=b_{n-1}-b_{0}
;

•

for
2\leq i\leq n-1
,
c_{i}=b_{n-i}-b_{n+1-i}
.

Then
c_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
0\leq j\leq n-1
. Let
L
be the lowest common multiple of the denominators of
c_{j}
and
T:=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}Lc_{i}\alpha^{2i}
. The order of
[P_{0}-P_{n-1}]\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)(\mathbb{Q})
is
N=\frac{p}{\gcd(p,T)}L
.

Proof.

Note that
(c_{0},\ldots c_{n-1})^{T}=M^{-1}\cdot(1,0,\ldots,-1)^{T}
. The fact that
c_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}
follows from the fact that
b_{s}\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
s
(since the eigenvalues
y_{i}
and
y_{n-i}
are complex conjugates; and so are
(\omega^{j})^{i}
and
(\omega^{j})^{n-i}
). The rest follows from the previous proposition.
∎

8.3.
The Order of
D

The orders of arbitrary elements of
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)
is not as straightforward to compute. The main stumbling block is the fact that the analogous matrix to
M
is
\Bl(\Circ(\mathbf{a}),\frac{1}{12})
, where
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1})^{T}
, which is singular since the sums of all rows (or columns) is the zero vector (cf Lemma
15
). Instead, we use Proposition
21
and the structure of the basis of
\mathcal{F}(p)
to compute the order of
D=P_{0}-Q_{0}
in
J_{1}(p)
.

We begin with the following simple lemma, which results from the construction of our basis.

Lemma 26
.

Let
f(\tau)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}E_{i}(\tau)^{e_{i}}\prod_{j-1}^{n-1}F_{j}(\tau)^{f_{j}}
, where
e_{i},f_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy the congruence conditions of Proposition
9
. Then

f(\tau)=\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}G_{i}(\tau)^{e_{i}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-2}H_{j}(\tau)^{f_{j}}\right)G_{n-1}(\tau)^{C_{1}}H_{n-1}(\tau)^{C_{2}}

where
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
are defined as

•

C_{1}=\frac{1}{p}\left(e_{n-1}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\alpha^{2i+2}e_{i}\right);

•

C_{2}=\frac{1}{12p}\left(f_{n-1}+C_{1}\beta p-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}f_{i}(-\alpha^{2i+2}+p\beta(\alpha^{2i+2}-1))-\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}pe_{i}(\alpha^{2i+2}-1)\right).

Proof.

The fact that
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
follows from the congruence conditions of Proposition
9
and the rest follows from the definitions of the
G_{i}
and
H_{j}
.
∎

This gives us a strategy to compute the order of a cuspidal divisor in
J_{1}(p)
.

Corollary 27
.

For any degree
0
divisor
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(x_{1}P_{i}+y_{i}Q_{i})
supported on cusps, its order in
\mathcal{C}_{1}(p)
is the smallest positive integer
N
such that

(i)

\Bl(\Circ(\mathbf{a}),\frac{1}{12})\cdot(e_{0},\ldots,e_{n-1},0,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1})^{T}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1},y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1})^{T}
;

(ii)

Ne_{i},Nf_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all i,j;

(iii)

Ne_{i},Nf_{j}
satisfy the congruence conditions stated in Proposition
9
.

Proof.

For any such divisor
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(x_{i}P_{i}+y_{i}Q_{i})
, its order in the Jacobian is the smallest positive integer
N
for which there exist integers
g_{i},h_{j}
with

\divv\left(\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}G_{i}(\tau)^{g_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}H_{j}(\tau)^{h_{j}}\right)=N\cdot\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(x_{i}P_{i}+y_{i}Q_{i}).

Thus, if one finds
\mathbf{x}=(e_{0},\ldots,e_{n-1},0,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1})^{T}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2n}
with

\Bl(\Circ(\mathbf{a}),\frac{1}{12})\cdot\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1},y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1})^{T}

then the order is simply the smallest positive integer
N
such that
N\cdot\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
and it satisfies the congruence conditions of Proposition
9
.
∎

We apply this to the divisor
P_{0}-Q_{0}
. In what follows, we assume as before that
\omega
is a primitive
n
th root of unity. We want to find a solution
\mathbf{x}=(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1},f_{0},\ldots,f_{n-1})^{T}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2n}
with
f_{0}=0
and such that

\Bl(\Circ(\mathbf{a}),\frac{1}{12})\cdot\mathbf{x}=(1,0,\ldots,0,-1,0,\ldots 0)^{T}.

For
1\leq i\leq n-1
define

\beta_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(\omega^{i})^{j}a_{j}=\frac{1}{4}B_{2,i}
.

We diagonalise
\Bl(\Circa(\mathbf{a}),\frac{1}{12})
using Proposition
21
. More precisely,
\Bl(\Circa(\mathbf{a}),\frac{1}{12})=P^{-1}DP
where

P=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\dots&1&1&1&1&\dots&1&1\\
1&\omega&\dots&\omega^{n-2}&\omega^{n-1}&1&\omega&\dots&\omega^{n-2}&\omega^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&\omega^{n-1}&\dots&(\omega^{n-1})^{n-2}&(\omega^{n-1})^{n-1}&1&\omega^{n-1}&\dots&(\omega^{n-1})^{n-2}&(\omega^{n-1})^{n-1}\\
1&1&\dots&1&1&-1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
1&\omega&\dots&\omega^{n-2}&\omega^{n-1}&-1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&\omega^{n-1}&\dots&(\omega^{n-1})^{n-2}&(\omega^{n-1})^{n-1}&-1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
\end{bmatrix}
,

and
D=\Diag(0,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n-1},\frac{-n}{6},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n-1})
. Thus
\mathbf{x}
defines the following system of equations:

•

\beta_{j}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\omega^{j})^{i}e_{i}=1
for
1\leq j\leq n-1;

•

\frac{-n}{6}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_{i}-f_{0}\right)=1;

•

\beta_{j}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\omega^{j})^{i}(e_{i}+f_{j})=1-\omega^{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n-1
.

Setting
f_{0}=0
, we find the unique solution to the above system:

•

e_{i}:=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{-6}{n}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\beta_{j}}(\omega^{-i})^{j}\right)=\frac{-6}{n^{2}}+b_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq n-1
;

•

f_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{\omega^{j}}{\beta_{j}}(1-(\omega^{j})^{n-i})\right)=b_{n-1}-b_{i-1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
;.

where the
b_{i}
are as in the previous subsection.
Let
L
be the lowest common multiple of the denominators of
e_{i}
and
f_{j}
. Define

•

D_{1}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}Le_{i}\alpha^{2i}
;

•

D_{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}Lf_{i}\alpha^{2i}
;

•

D_{3}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}Lpe_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}Lf_{i}
;

and
n_{1}=\gcd(D_{1},p)
,
n_{2}=\gcd(D_{2},p)
and
n_{3}=\gcd(D_{3},12)
. We have proved the following.

Proposition 28
.

The order of
P_{0}-Q_{0}
in
J_{1}(p)
is

N=\frac{p}{\gcd(n_{1},n_{2})}\cdot\frac{12}{\gcd(n_{3},12)}\cdot L.

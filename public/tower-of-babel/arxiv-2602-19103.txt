Near-perfect Noisy Quantum State Teleportation
Md Manirul Ali, Sovik Roy, Dipankar Home

Abstract
Achieving high fidelity of quantum teleportation (QT) in a noisy environment is an essential requirement for its real-world applications. To this end, we devise a distinctive protocol for ensuring teleportation fidelity close to unity , hinging essentially on the timing of Alice’s Bell-basis measurement (BM) dependent on the choice of Bob’s local noise parameters, but is independent of Alice’s local noise. Our scheme is enabled by Alice communicating to Bob only two of the BM outcomes corresponding to the states that are decoherence-free under common dephasing at Alice’s wing. On the other hand, Bob is asked to discard the states of his qubit for the other two BM outcomes in order to maximize fidelity of the teleported state. This ensures the teleportation fidelity’s independence of noise parameters in Alice’s wing. We formulate the protocol in terms of a generic two-level quantum system, subjected to non-Markovian dephasing noise, applicable for any pure maximally/non-maximally entangled state as well as a Werner-type mixed state as resource. Notably, we show that high fidelity is achievable even using resource states with small values of the entanglement measure. Remarkably, even within the local regime of Werner states, where Bell-CHSH inequalities are not violated, the teleportation fidelity remains significantly high. Finally, we discuss the empirical feasibility of our scheme using photonic qubits.

Now, let us highlight the following salient features of our protocol whose resource can be any pure (maximal or non-maximal) entangled state or mixed entangled state, such as the Werner type state:

3.1
The time evolution in Alice’s wing

The microscopic Hamiltonian of two qubits in a common dephasing environment at Alice’s wing can be described by

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!H\!=H_{S}+H_{E}+H_{SE},

(43)

where,
H_{S}=\frac{\hbar}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\!\omega_{0}\sigma_{z}^{i}
is the system Hamiltonian representing the two qubits at Alice’s side,
\hbar
denoting the Planck’s constant and
\omega_{0}
is the qubit transition frequency between the two energy levels of each qubit. Also
\sigma_{z}^{i}
are the Pauli spin operators for
i^{th}
qubit (
i=A_{1},A_{2})
at Alice’s wing. The environment Hamiltonian
H_{E}=\hbar\!\sum_{k}\!\omega_{k}a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{k}
is the common dephasing environment modeled as a collection of Bosonic field modes with frequencies
\omega_{k}
where
a_{k}^{\dagger}
and
a_{k}
are respectively the creation and annihilation operators of the
k^{th}
mode of the environment. The two qubits (
A_{1},\>A_{2}
) at Alice’s wing interact with the common environment where the system-environment interaction is described by the Hamiltonian
H_{SE}=\hbar S_{z}\sum_{k}(g_{k}a_{k}^{\dagger}+g_{k}^{*}a_{k})
, i.e
H_{SE}
is composed of two terms viz.
S_{z}
(the observable of the two-qubit system) and
\sum_{k}(g_{k}a_{k}^{\dagger}+g_{k}^{*}a_{k})
(the operator corresponding to the dephasing environment). The operator
S_{z}=\sum_{i}\>\sigma_{z}^{i}
is the collective spin operator of the two-qubit system at Alice’s wing. The coupling strength between the system and
k^{th}
mode of the environment is described by the parameter
g_{k}
. The dephasing model (
43
) exhibits decoherence in its purest form, where off-diagonal elements representing quantum coherence are absent without any decay in the diagonal elements of the density matrix. However, in the context of open quantum system a dephasing model refers to a specific type of interaction between the quantum system and its environment that leads to the loss of quantum coherence without energy exchange. The environment is initially considered in thermal equilibrium at temperature
T
and the system was decoupled from the environment initially at time
t=0
.

The Liouville-von Neumann master equation for the two-qubits under this common dephasing noise is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\varrho_{A_{1}A_{2}}(t)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\Big[H_{S},\varrho_{A_{1}A_{2}}(t)\Big]+{\mathcal{L}(t)}\varrho_{A_{1}A_{2}}(t),

where the dissipative term causing the decoherence is given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}(t)}\varrho_{A_{1}A_{2}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(A(t)~S_{z}\varrho_{A1A2}(t)S_{z}-2\alpha(t)~S_{z}S_{z}\varrho_{A_{1}A_{2}}(t)

\displaystyle-2\alpha^{\ast}(t)~\varrho_{A1A2}(t)S_{z}S_{z}\Big).

(45)

The time dependent functions
A(t)
and
\alpha(t)
are given by

\displaystyle A(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\int_{0}^{\infty}J(\omega)\coth\Big(\frac{\hbar\omega}{2k_{B}T}\Big)\frac{\sin(\omega t)}{\omega}d\omega,

(46)

and

\displaystyle\alpha(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A(t)}{4}-i\int_{0}^{\infty}J(\omega)\frac{1-\cos(\omega t)}{\omega}.

(48)

Here
J(\omega)=\sum_{k}|\>g_{k}\>|^{2}\delta(\omega-\omega_{k})
is the spectral density of the environment. If the reservoir spectrum is continuous,
g_{k}\rightarrow g(\omega)
, and consequently we have
J(\omega)=P(\omega)|g(\omega)|^{2}
, where
P(\omega)
is the density of states of the reservoir.

Under this quantum master equation (
3.1
), a two-qubit density matrix at Alice’s wing evolves as

\displaystyle\varrho_{A_{1}A_{2}}(\tau)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\begin{pmatrix}\varrho_{11}(0)&f_{\tau}\varrho_{12}(0)&f_{\tau}\varrho_{13}(0)&a_{\tau}\varrho_{14}(0)\\
f^{\ast}_{\tau}\varrho_{21}(0)&\varrho_{22}(0)&\varrho_{23}(0)&g^{\ast}_{\tau}\varrho_{24}(0)\\

where

and

are determined by the functions

\alpha(t)

is determined
by the functions
A(t)

and

4
Conclusion

Quantum teleportation in realistic environments is fundamentally limited by decoherence acting on both the sender’s and receiver’s subsystems. In this work, we have introduced a noise-avoiding teleportation protocol that exploits a decoherence-free subspace arising from collective dephasing at Alice’s wing, combined with a timing-based optimization strategy. A central outcome of our analysis is that the average
teleportation fidelity can be made entirely independent of the noise acting on Alice’s subsystems.
By selectively retaining only those Bell-measurement outcomes immune to collective dephasing, and discarding the remaining outcomes that accumulate noise from both wings, the protocol achieves near-unity fidelity through joint optimization of Alice’s measurement timing and Bob’s local environmental parameters. Using a general non-Markovian spin-boson framework, we have demonstrated that high teleportation fidelity is attainable not only with maximally entangled channels but also with non-maximally entangled pure states and Werner-type mixed states.

Importantly, our scheme is experimentally feasible within existing quantum optics platforms. In photonic systems, controlled dephasing noise with an Ohmic spectral profile can be engineered by coupling polarization and frequency degrees of freedom using birefringent quartz plates. The common dephasing environment at Alice’s wing and the local environment at Bob’s wing can both be simulated through polarization-frequency interactions, with the dephasing channel effectively activated as photons propagate through the birefringent media. By appropriately tailoring the photon-reservoir interaction times and synchronizing Alice’s Bell-state measurement with the engineered noise parameters, the near-perfect fidelities predicted by our analysis can be realized. This establishes a clear route toward experimentally implementing high-fidelity teleportation under realistic noisy conditions using well-established photonic architectures. Overall, our results introduce a paradigm in which measurement timing and environmental tuning function as operational control parameters in open-system quantum communication. Beyond teleportation, our framework provides a general strategy for
engineering noise-resilient quantum networking protocols in structured, non-Markovian environments.

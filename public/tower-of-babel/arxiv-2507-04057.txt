Three bound states with prescribed angular momentum to the cubic-quintic NLS equations in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}
Shuai Yao, Juntao Sun

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate bound states with prescribed angular momentum and mass for the nonlinear Schrödinger equations (NLS) with the cubic-quintic nonlinearity in dimensions three. We demonstrate that there exist three solutions for the double constrained problem: a local minimizer, a mountain pass type solution, and a global minimizer. Moreover, by means of the minimax method, we construct a new mountain pass path and further obtain the geometric link among the three solutions as well as a comparison of their energy levels. This seems to be the first paper concerning three solutions, with the method also being applicable to the single constraint problem.



Keywords:
Prescribed angular momentum; Multiple solutions; Orbital
stability; Variational methods.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
35A15; 35Q41; 35B35.

Introduction

Consider the following the cubic-quintic NLS equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}\psi=H\psi-|\psi|^{2}\psi+\mu|\psi|^{4}\psi,&\text{
in }\mathbb{R\times R}^{3},\\
\psi|_{t=0}=\psi_{0},&\end{array}\right.

(1)

where the constants
\mu>0
characterize the interaction strengths, and

H=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+V(x).

Here, the potential
V(x)
is supposed to be trapping, a standard example
being the polyharmonic potential given by
V(x)=\omega|x|^{k}
with
k>2
,
where
\omega>0
is the trapping frequency. The cubic-quintic Schrödinger equation has many interpretations in physics. In the context of a
Boson gas, it describes two-body attractive and three-body repulsive
interactions. The cubic-quintic nonlinearity was introduced in
[
16
]

and is employed in numerous physical models
[
14
]
. For three dimensional,
it models the evolution of a monochromatic wave complex envelope in a medium
with weakly saturating nonlinearity.

Our working space
\Sigma
is defined as

\Sigma:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{k}|u|^{2}dx<+\infty\right\},

which is a Hilbert space with the inner product and norm

(u,v)_{\Sigma}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\frac{1}{2}\nabla u\nabla v+\omega|x|^{k}uv+uv)dx\quad\text{and }\|u\|_{\Sigma}^{2}:=\|u\|_{\dot{\Sigma}}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2},

where
\|u\|_{\dot{\Sigma}}^{2}:=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\omega\|x^{k/2}u\|_{2}^{2}
.

When the local well-posedness holds, then there exist
T_{\max}>0

and a unique solution
\psi\in C((-T_{\max},T_{\max}),\Sigma)
to Eq. (
1
) conserving the mass

M(\psi):=\|\psi\|_{2}^{2}

and the total energy

E(\psi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\omega|x|^{k}|\psi|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\psi|^{4}+\frac{\mu}{3}|\psi|^{6}\right)dx.

Indeed, besides the mass and total energy, another important physical
quantity exists, namely the mean angular momentum of
\psi
around a
specified rotation axis in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. To elaborate further and
without loss of generality, we denote the coordinates of a point in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by
(x_{1},x_{2},z)
and assume that the rotation axis is the
z
-axis. Consequently, the mean angular momentum of
\psi
is expressed as

L(u):=\left\langle\psi,L_{z}\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where

L_{z}u=-i(x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}\psi-x_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}\psi).

In quantum mechanics
[
3
]
, the angular momentum of a particle
corresponds to the operator
\mathbb{L}:=-ix\wedge\nabla
, so
L_{z}
is the
third component of the quantum mechanical angular momentum operator
\mathbb{L}
. A straightforward calculation (see
[
1
]
) demonstrates that the
time-evolution of
L(u)
under the flow of(
1
) satisfies:

L(\psi(t))+\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\psi(\tau)|^{2}L_{z}Vdxd\tau=L\left(\psi_{0}\right).

Since
V(x)
is radially symmetric, it holds that
L_{z}V=0
. Thus the
dynamics of (
1
) also satisfies the angular momentum conservation
law

L(\psi(t))=L(\psi_{0}).

Let us now consider a given angular velocity
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}
and
recall that
\Omega L_{z}
is the generator of time-dependent rotations
about the
z
-axis. Specifically, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the
action of
L_{z}
is characterized by

e^{\mathrm{i}t\Omega L_{z}}f(x)=f\left(e^{t\Theta}x\right),

where
\Theta
is the skew symmetric matrix given by

\Theta:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\Omega&0\\
-\Omega&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right).

It is evident that
\left(e^{t\Omega L_{z}}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

constitutes a family of unitary operators

e^{t\Omega L_{z}}:\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma.

Define

v(t,x):=e^{t\Omega L_{z}}\psi(t,x)=\psi\left(t,e^{t\Theta}x\right).

By a straightforward calculation, then
v
satisfies the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}v=Hv-|v|^{2}v+\mu|v|^{4}v-\Omega L_{z}v,&\\
v|_{t=0}=v_{0}.&\end{array}\right.

(2)

In particular, it holds that
L(v(t))=L(\psi(t))=L(\psi_{0})
. The equation (
2
) appears in the mean-field description of rotating Bose-Einstein
condensates.

A standing wave of Eq. (
2
) with a prescribed angular momentum
l>0

and mass
m>0
is a solution having the form
v(x,t)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)

such that
u
weakly solves

\begin{array}[]{ll}Hu-\lambda u-\Omega L_{z}u=|u|^{2}u-\mu|u|^{4}u,&\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{array}

(3)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}dx=m,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}L_{z}u\bar{u}dx=l.

(4)

In this respect, solutions to problem (
3
)-(
4
) can be
obtained by searching critical points of the energy functional

E(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\omega|x|^{k}|u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|u|^{4}+\frac{\mu}{3}|u|^{6}\right]dx

on the constraint

S(m,l):=\left\{u\in\Sigma:M(u)=m,L(u)=l\right\}.

Here
\lambda,\Omega\in\mathbb{R}
are unknown, which appear as Lagrange
multipliers associated to the double constraints.

In recent years, for a single mass constraint case, the existence
and multiplicity of normalized solutions have been studied by many scholars,
see
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
13
]
. However, there has been relatively
little research on the doubly constraint problem (
3
)-(
4
).
As far as we know, it seems that this type of problem was first studied in

[
15
]
. For the single power term, i.e.
|u|^{p-2}u
with
2<p<2+\frac{4}{N}
, the energy functional
E
restricted on
S(m,l)
is bounded from below.
In
[
15
]
, they obtained solutions to problem (
3
)-(
4
)
by searching the global minimizer, and orbital stability of solutions was
established. As a complement of the investigation conducted in
[
15
]
,
Gou and Shen
[
11
]
considered the mass supercritical case, and they
proved that there exist two non-radial symmetric solutions, one of which is
local minimizer and the other is mountain pass type solution. It is natural
to ask a question: in the case of combining nonlinear terms, will it have an
impact on the number of solutions? This is also the research motivation and
purpose of this paper. Therefore, here we consider the case of combining
nonlinear terms with the focusing cubic and defocusing quintic, which have
important physical significance.

Now, we present the main results. Set

\alpha_{m,l}:=\inf_{u\in S(m,l)}E(u).

For any given
\rho>0
, define

\beta_{m,l}^{\rho}:=\inf_{u\in S(m,l)\cap B_{\rho}}E(u),

where

B_{\rho}=\left\{u\in\Sigma:\|u\|_{\dot{\Sigma}}^{2}\leq\rho\right\}.

(5)

Theorem 1.1

Let
\omega>0
. Then there exist
m^{\ast}>0
and
\mu_{\ast}>0
such that for
m<m^{\ast}
and
\mu<\mu_{\ast}
, there admit two
solutions
u^{(1)}
and
u^{(2)}
for problem (
3
)-(
4
),
where
u^{(1)}
is a local minimizer satisfying
E(u^{(1)})=\beta_{m,l}^{\rho}
, and
u^{(2)}
is a global minimizer satisfying
E(u^{(2)})=\alpha_{m,l}
. Moreover, if the local well-posedness holds, then the set of global minimizer

\mathcal{A}_{m,l}:=\left\{u\in S(m,l):E(u)=\alpha_{m,l}\right\}

and the set of local minimizer

\mathcal{B}_{m,l}^{\rho}:=\left\{u\in S(m,l):E(u)=\beta_{m,l}^{\rho}\right\}

are both orbitally stable under the flow of (
1
).

Remark 1.2

The operator
H
defined on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is essentially
self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, however, we remain uncertain as to whether Eq. (
1
) is locally well-posed. In
[
19
]
, for single power term
|u|^{p-2}u
, when
2<p<4+\frac{4}{k}
, the local well-posedness can be obtained by applying
Strichartz estimates.

Theorem 1.3

Let
\mu<\mu_{\ast}
. Then there exist
m_{\ast}\leq m^{\ast}

and
\tilde{\omega}>0
such that for
m<m_{\ast}
and
\omega<\tilde{\omega}
, there admits a mountain pass type solution
u^{(3)}
for problem (
3
)-(
4
).

Remark 1.4

It is noted that in Theorem
1.3
, we need to control the
frequency
\omega
. This condition is intended to construct a special
mountain pass geometric structure, so as to obtain the precise geometric
positions of the three solutions (see Corollary
1.5
below). If only the
mountain pass solution is obtained, this condition can be omitted, and the
proof is similar to that in
[
11
]
, in which the first local minimum is
selected as the starting point of the mountain pass path.

Corollary 1.5

Let
\omega<\tilde{\omega}
,
m<m_{\ast}
and
\mu<\mu_{\ast}
,
then there exist three solutions
u^{(i)}
for
i=1,2,3
, and

E(u^{(2)})<0<E(u^{(1)})<E(u^{(3)}).

Moreover, there holds

\|u^{(1)}\|_{\dot{\Sigma}}^{2}<\|u^{(3)}\|_{\dot{\Sigma}}^{2}<\|u^{(2)}\|_{\dot{\Sigma}}^{2}.

Let us present the main difficulties and research methods of this paper.
First, we restrict the energy functional
E
to the ball
S(m,l)\cap B_{\rho}
and consider the local minimization problem
\beta_{m,r}^{\rho}
.
Then, we show that the compactness of any minimizing sequence holds and
local minimizer is attained. Here, it is worth mentioning that the
verification of compactness is complicated due to the constraint of angular
momentum conservation and the presence of critical nonlinear term. In
addition, to ensure that the local minimizer is attained in the interior of
the ball
S(m,l)\cap B_{\rho}
, we need to rule out the case where it lies
on the boundary. However, the presence of critical term makes it impossible
to achieve this merely by controlling the mass, which is different from
[
11
]
. With the help of local minimizer, we can find global minimizer with
negative energy level through the method of asymptotic analysis. Finally, we
construct a mountain pass solution by establishing the mountain pass
structure of the functional
E
. This method of constructing mountain pass
solution has two main differences from that in
[
11
]
.
(i)
To obtain
more information about the solutions, we construct a new mountain pass path,
with the global minimizer serving as the endpoint of this path, that is

\gamma(m,l):=\inf_{g\in\Gamma(m,l)}\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}E(g(t)),

where

\Gamma(m,l):=\left\{g\in C([0,1],S(m,l)):g(0)=(u^{(2)})_{l_{1}},g(1)=u^{(2)}\right\}.

Here, we can find
l_{1}>0
such that
(u^{(2)})_{l_{1}}\in S(m,l)\cap B_{\rho}
. In this way, both the energy level and the geometric link can be clearly demonstrated.
(ii)
we do not need the
Pohozaev identity to ensure the boundedness of the sequence, so the method
of constructing the sequence is simpler.

Remark 1.6

In this paper, we restrict the potential function to polyharmonic
functions. In fact, the conditions on the potential can be more general, see
below assumption in
[
15
,
11
]
.

(V1)
The potential
V\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})
is
assumed to be radially symmetric and confining, i.e.
V(x)\rightarrow+\infty
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, there exists
k>2
and
R>0
, such that for
|x|>R
:

c_{\alpha}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{k-|\alpha|}\leq|\partial^{\alpha}V(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{k-|\alpha|},\,\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3},

where
\left\langle x\right\rangle=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
and
c_{\alpha},C_{\alpha}
are positive constants. Furthermore, the Pohozaev identity is
not required to obtain the mountain pass type solution, which allows the
conditions
\left\langle\nabla V(x),x\right\rangle>0
in
[
11
]
to be
eliminated.

In what follows, we introduce the well-known Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

Lemma 2.1
(Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality)

Let

. Then there exists a sharp constant
\mathcal{S}_{q}>0
such that

\|u\|_{q}\leq\mathcal{S}_{q}^{1/q}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{3(q-2)}{2q}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{6-q}{2q}},

where

ground state solution of the following equation

-\Delta U+\frac{6-q}{3(q-2)}U=\frac{4}{3(q-2)}|U|^{q-2}U.

Lemma 2.2
(Sobolev inequality)

There exists an optimal constant
S>0
depending only on
dimension such that

\left\|u\right\|_{6}^{6}\leq S\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{6},\quad\forall u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

(7)

where

denotes the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with respect to the norm
\left\|u\right\|_{D^{1,2}}:=\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}
. It is well known
[
17
]

that the optimal constant is achieved by

U_{\varepsilon,y}\left(x\right)=3^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}+\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{for }\varepsilon>0,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

which are the only positive classical solutions to the critical Lane-Emden
equation

-\triangle\omega=\omega^{5},\text{ }\omega>0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
20
, Lemma 3.1]
) For
q\in\left[2,6\right)
, the embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})

Lemma 2.4

(
[
13

) If

), then

The Pohozaev identity

and

respectively, and then integrating

The proof is complete.

Assume that the functional

Lemma 3.1

Lemma 3.2

\displaystyle\left|\int_{|x|>R}\overline{u_{n}}x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}u_{n}dx\right|

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}\left(\int_{|x|>R}|x|^{2}|u_{n}|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R^{\frac{k-2}{2}}}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}\||x|^{k/2}u_{n}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R^{\frac{k-2}{2}}}\|u_{n}\|_{\Sigma}^{2}.

Let

For the estimates of

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\overline{u_{n}}x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}u_{n}dx

\displaystyle=

Lemma 3.3

Let

Proof.
It follows from Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{\Sigma}}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{6}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{4}dx

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{\Sigma}}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{3}m^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{4}dx\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{4}dx

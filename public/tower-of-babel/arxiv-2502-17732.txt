Abstract
. We consider vanishing viscosity approximations to solutions of the stochastic incompressible Euler equations in two space dimensions with additive noise. We identify sufficient and necessary conditions under which martingale solutions of the stochastic Euler equations satisfy an exact energy balance in mean. We find that the tightness of the laws of the approximating sequence of solutions of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations in L 2 ​ ( [ 0 , T ] × D ) L^{2}([0,T]\times D) is equivalent to the limiting martingale solution satisfying an energy balance in mean. Numerical simulations illustrate the theoretical findings.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we consider the stochastic incompressible Euler equations on
D=\mathbb{T}^{2}
, and a time interval
t\in[0,T]
for some
T>0
,

(1.1)

\begin{split}du+u\cdot\nabla u\,dt&=-\nabla p\,dt+f\,dt+\sigma\cdot dW,\\
\operatorname{div}u&=0,\\
u(0,x)&=\bar{u}(x),\end{split}

describing the motion of an inviscid, incompressible fluid on a periodic domain. Here
u:[0,T]\times D\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the velocity field and
p:[0,T]\times D\to\mathbb{R}
is the pressure. The
stochastic forcing is assumed to be a mean zero, white-in-time and colored-in-space Gaussian
process, that can be represented by

\sigma\cdot W=\sigma(x)\cdot W(t,\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}e_{j}(x)\beta_{j}(t,\omega)

where
\beta_{j}
are independent standard 1D Brownian motions defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
with a filtration
(\mathscr{G}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
. We assume that the
\sigma
-algebras
\mathscr{G}_{t}
are completed with respect to
(\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
, that is,
(\mathscr{G}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
contains all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets
A\in\mathscr{F}
.

\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a basis of Stokes eigenfunctions on
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
, where
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
is the space of
L^{2}(D)
-valued functions that are divergence free almost everywhere.
We assume furthermore the bounded trace conditions

(1.2)

\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}:=\bar{\sigma}<\infty,\quad\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\left\|\operatorname{curl}e_{j}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}:=\bar{\rho}<\infty.

f
represents a deterministic forcing term satisfying
\operatorname{div}f=0
a.e. in
[0,T]\times D
. The stochastic term is added to account for uncertainties and random fluctuations that are inherent in real-world fluid flows. These could be environmental factors such as temperature variations, wind patterns, random external forces such as vibrations, etc. Furthermore, taking a probabilistic view is common in turbulence modeling and goes back to the famous work of Kolmogorov in 1941
[
23
,
22
,
24
]
.
The Euler equations describe the motion of an idealized fluid in the absence of friction and other diffusive forcing and are the formal vanishing viscosity limit of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations. Formally, with a simple application of Itô’s formula, one can derive the energy balance in mean

(1.3)

\mathbb{E}\int_{D}|u(t)|^{2}dx=\mathbb{E}\int_{D}|\bar{u}|^{2}dx+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}f(s)\cdot u(s)dxds+\bar{\sigma}t.

If
u
is sufficiently smooth in space, this identity can be rigorously justified. However, when martingale solutions, which are the stochastic equivalent of weak solutions in the deterministic setting, are considered, (
1.3
) may only hold as an inequality. Moreover, energy dissipation is an integral part of Kolmogorov’s K41 theory for 3D incompressible turbulence. Lars Onsager conjectured in 1949
[
29
]
that deterministic three-dimensional turbulent flows conserve the energy law if the Hölder exponent
\theta
of the solution is greater than 1/3, whereas anomalous dissipation of energy occurs when the Hölder exponent is less than 1/3. For the 3D deterministic Euler equations, the
\theta>1/3
case has been settled a long time ago in the works by Eyink
[
14
]
and Constantin, E and Titi
[
9
]
. The
\theta<1/3
part of the conjecture was finally proved in the recent work by Isett
[
19
]
building upon earlier work by Sheffer
[
31
]
, Shnirelman
[
32
]
, and De Lellis and Székelyhidi
[
11
]
, and others. In two space dimensions, the results of
[
9
]
are readily applied to obtain energy conservation for
\theta>1/3
in the force-free setting. One might hope that in two space dimensions, energy conservation might be possible under weaker conditions; however, this is not the case: Recenlty, Giri and Radu
[
16
]
constructed for any
\theta<1/3
nontrivial weak solutions of the unforced Euler equations that satisfy
u\in C^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
and do not conserve the energy.

In mathematical turbulence theory, solutions of the Euler equations are characterized as the zero viscosity limit of solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations. In fact, most numerical methods for approximating the Euler equations are built on the same paradigm: As the mesh size decreases, the numerical viscosity in the scheme vanishes. Therefore, it is interesting to determine conditions on the approximating sequence which guarantee that the limiting solution of the Euler equations satisfies an energy balance. In the 2D deterministic setting, such conditions were first investigated in the work
[
7
]
in the unforced deterministic case. This work was extended to provide a complete characterization of energy-conservative solutions of the incompressible Euler equations that can be obtained as the zero viscosity of Navier-Stokes solutions in
[
26
]
. In fact, it is shown that the zero viscosity limit that satisfies energy conservation is equivalent to the strong convergence of the sequence of solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations in
L^{2}([0,T]\times D)
(where
[0,T]
is an arbitrary time interval). The strong convergence in
L^{2}([0,T]\times D)
again is equivalent to the Navier-Stokes solutions’ time-averaged second-order structure functions having a uniform modulus of continuity. Structure functions quantify how differences in a flow’s velocity behave over a given distance or time separation, typically in terms of the difference in values at two points; for the precise definition used here, see Definition
2.8
. Later, these results were extended to the deterministically forced case in
[
28
,
20
]
. In a recent work by De Rosa and Park
[
12
]
, it is furthermore shown that when the initial vorticity is a measure with positive singular part, any sequence of vanishing viscosity Leray-Hopf solutions to the periodic two-dimensional unforced incompressible Navier-Stokes equations does not display anomalous dissipation, thus further characterizing the dissipation mechanism in the case of weak convergence.
While these results hold in the two-dimensional periodic case, Ciampa
[
8
]
derived sufficient conditions for energy conservation in the case of the two dimensional plane.

As mentioned earlier, stochastic versions of the Navier-Stokes and Euler equations may be more natural to answer questions of turbulence than the corresponding deterministic models. However, the question of whether an energy balance in mean such as (
1.3
) holds for suitable notions of weak solutions of the stochastic Euler equations (
1.1
) that are obtained as vanishing viscosity limits from the Navier-Stokes equations does not appear to have been investigated yet. We therefore aim to start addressing this issue in this work and derive sufficient and necessary conditions under which an energy balance holds.
We find that if an averaged (in probability space) version of the structure function condition in
[
26
]
holds, the laws of the Navier-Stokes solutions in the vanishing viscosity limit are tight in
L^{2}(0,T\times D)
. Thus, using Skorokhod’s theorem, random fields can be identified such that the limit is a martingale solution of (
1.1
) and satisfies an energy balance in mean as in (
1.3
) with respect to the probability space coming from the application of Skorokhod’s theorem. Conversely, we prove that if
u
is a solution of the stochastic Euler equations that satisfies the energy balance (
1.3
) and is obtained as the vanishing viscosity limit of a sequence of solutions of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations, then the laws of this approximating sequence are tight on
L^{2}([0,T]\times D)
. This is roughly a stochastic version of the equivalence of strong convergence and energy balance for the zero viscosity limit that was established in
[
26
]
. We are able to adapt many of the technical tools of
[
26
,
20
]
to the stochastic setting, however, the probabilistic setting added additional difficulty such as possibly unbounded trajectories. We resolved this by using a stopping time argument that allows us to deal with a.s.
L^{2}
-bounded realizations in which we can pass the viscosity to zero before finally sending the stopping time to infinity.

Although this work is restricted to additive smooth-in-space white noise, we hope that many of the techniques will also apply to other forms of noise, such as multiplicative or transport noise. We plan to study this in future work.

We conclude with numerical experiments with flat vortex sheet and fractional Brownian bridge initial data illustrating that the energy balance is close to being achieved in practice.

The rest of this article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary notation and definitions of solutions. In Section
3
, we establish conditions for the energy balance in mean for solutions of (
1.1
) obtained as limits of solutions of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations. Finally, in the last Section
4
, we present numerical examples that confirm that the energy balance is maintained for rather rough initial conditions.

H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)=\left\{u\in H^{1}(D):{\textrm{div}}u=0\right\}.

Sometimes, we will abbreviate
L^{2}=L^{2}(D)
and

.
We will denote the
L^{2}(D)
and
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
-inner product for functions or vector fields
f,g\in L^{2}(D)

(f,g)=\int_{L^{2}(D)}f(x)\cdot g(x)dx.

For a Banach space
X
, we denote by

is continuous for any
\psi\in X^{*}
.
Next, we define the space of smooth cylindrical functions
\text{Cyl}(X)
for a Hilbert space
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

We denote by
\Pi_{d}(X)
the space of all maps
\pi:X\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the form

\pi(x)=(\langle x,e_{1}\rangle,\langle x,e_{2}\rangle,\dots,\langle x,e_{d}\rangle),\quad x\in X,

where
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d}\}
is any orthonormal family of vectors in

.
We denote by
\text{Cyl}(X)
the functions
\varphi=\psi\circ\pi
with
\pi\in\Pi_{d}(X)

.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
\varphi=\psi\circ\pi\in\text{Cyl}(X)
is a Lipschitz function and continuous with respect to the weak topology on
X
. By
[
1
, Lemma 5.1.12]
, we have that a sequence if

\mathscr{K}:=\{\mu_{n}\}\subset\mathscr{P}(X)
is weakly tight in the sense that

\forall\varepsilon>0\quad\exists R_{\varepsilon}>0,\quad\text{such that }\quad\mu(X\backslash\overline{B_{R_{\varepsilon}}})\leqslant\varepsilon,\quad\forall\mu\in\mathscr{K}

it converges weakly to
\mu
in
\mathscr{P}(X_{{w}})
if and only if

(2.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}\varphi(x)d\mu_{n}(x)=\int_{X}\varphi(x)d\mu(x),\quad\forall\varphi\in\text{Cyl}(X).

Next, we define what we mean by solutions of the Euler equations (
1.1
) and of the Navier-Stokes equations which can be seen as the viscous approximation of the Euler equations. We start with the Navier-Stokes equations.

Definition 2.3

(Solutions of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations
[
25
]
)
.

Let
T>0
. Let
f_{\nu}\in L^{2}([0,T);L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
and
\bar{u}_{\nu}\in L^{2}(\Omega,L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
. Let
\{\beta_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be Brownian motions defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
with a filtration
\mathscr{G}_{t}

t\geqslant 0
which is complete with respect to
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
, and assume that
\bar{u}_{\nu}
is measurable with respect to
\mathscr{G}_{0}
. Then an
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
-valued process
u_{\nu}(t)
,
t\geqslant 0
, is called a
probabilistically strong solution
of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations

(2.2)

\begin{split}du_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\cdot\nabla u_{\nu}\,dt&=-\nabla p_{\nu}\,dt+\nu\Delta u_{\nu}\,dt+f_{\nu}\,dt+\sigma\cdot dW\\
\operatorname{div}u_{\nu}&=0,\\
u_{\nu}(0,x)&=\bar{u}_{\nu},\end{split}

for
t\in[0,T]
if

(a)

The process
u_{\nu}(t)
is adapted to the filtration
\mathscr{G}_{t}
, and almost every of its trajectories belongs to the space

C([0,T],L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))\cap L^{2}([0,T],H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)).

(b)

For all
t\in[0,T]
, and all divergence free
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(D)
, the following identity holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{D}u_{\nu}(t)\cdot\varphi dx+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\nabla u_{\nu}(s):\nabla\varphi dxds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\left[(u_{\nu}(s)\cdot\nabla)u_{\nu}(s)\right]\cdot\varphi dxds\\
&\qquad=\int_{D}\bar{u}_{\nu}\cdot\varphi dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}f_{\nu}\cdot\varphi dxds+\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}b_{k}e_{k}\cdot\varphi dxd\beta_{k}(s).\end{split}

(c)

The initial condition
u_{\nu}(0)=\bar{u}_{\nu}
is satisfied almost surely.

Existence and uniqueness of such solutions of the 2D-Navier-Stokes equations was shown in
[
25
, Proposition 2.4.6]
. In
[
25
, Propositon 2.4.8]
, it was also shown that such solutions satisfy the energy balance

(2.4)

\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t_{2})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t_{1})\right\|_{L^{2}}+\nu\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left\|\nabla u_{\nu}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds=\frac{1}{2}\bar{\sigma}(t_{2}-t_{1})+\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(f_{\nu},u_{\nu})ds.

for every
t_{2}>t_{1}
,
t_{1}=0
and almost every
t_{1}\in(0,\infty)
,
as long as the initial data
\bar{u}_{\nu}
has finite second moment.

Remark 2.4
.

The last term in the energy balance (
2.4
) can be bounded using Young’s inequality:

\left|\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(u_{\nu},f_{\nu})ds\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds.

Then using Grönwall inequality, it follows that
\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t_{2})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is bounded by
\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t_{1})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
times a constant depending on time,
f_{\nu}
and
\bar{\sigma}
.

Finally, we require the definition of the second-order structure function and the modulus of continuity.

Definition 2.8

(Structure functions)
.

For

define the second-order

S_{2}(v;r)=\left(\int_{D}\int_{B_{r}(0)}|v(x+h)-v(x)|^{2}\,dh\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

S_{2}^{T}(v;r)=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}\int_{B_{r}(0)}|v(t,x+h)-v(t,x)|^{2}\,dh\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/2}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}S_{2}(v(t),r)^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}.

Definition 2.9

(Modulus of continuity)
.

A modulus of continuity is a function
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
to measure the continuity of functions quantitatively.
\phi
is continuous and satisfies
\phi(0)=0
. We say a function
f:(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
, where
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
are two metric spaces, admits a modulus of continuity
\phi
if

d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))\leqslant\phi(d_{X}(x,y)),

for any
x,y\in X
.

We will assume in the following that any modulus of continuity
\phi
is monotone increasing, which will not be a restriction, since if
f
admits any given modulus of continuity
\phi
, we can find a continuous
\widetilde{\phi}
that satisfies
\widetilde{\phi}(0)=0
and
\widetilde{\phi}\geqslant\phi
and is monotone increasing and hence a modulus of continuity for the same given function.
It turns out that if the structure functions
S_{2}(v_{n},r)
of a bounded sequence of functions
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(D)
have a common modulus of continuity, the sequence is precompact in
L^{2}(D)
:

Theorem 2.10

(Fréchet-Kolmogorov theorem)
.

Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence of functions in
L^{2}(D)
where
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is bounded,
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

S_{2}(v_{n},r)\leqslant\phi(r),\quad\forall\,r>0,

for some modulus of continuity
\phi
. Then
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence that is convergent in
L^{2}(D)
.

For a proof, see e.g.
[
18
, Theorem A.8]
. If the structure functions
S_{2}^{T}(v_{n},r)
of a bounded sequence of time-dependent functions
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(D))
, where
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded domain, have a uniform modulus of continuity and in addition some time regularity, then the sequence is precompact in
L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(D))
:

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence of functions in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(D))
, where
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is bounded,
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

S_{2}^{T}(v_{n},r)\leqslant\phi(r),\quad\forall\,r>0,

for some modulus of continuity
\phi
. Assume in addition that

\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{\beta_{1},p_{1}}(0,T;H^{-L}(D))+\dots+W^{\beta_{m},p_{m}}(0,T;H^{-L}(D)),

uniformly for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
where

\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}\in(0,1],\quad p_{1},\dots,p_{m}>1

satisfy
\beta_{i}p_{i}>1
for all

. Then
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\alpha}(0,T;H^{-L}(D))
uniformly in
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\alpha=\min_{i=1,\dots,m}(\beta_{i}-1/p_{i})
and
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence that is strongly convergent in

.

Proof.

We first note that by the fractional version of Morrey’s inequality, see e.g.
[

]
, we have that
W^{\beta_{1},p_{1}}(0,T;H^{-L}(D))+\dots+W^{\beta_{m},p_{m}}(0,T;H^{-L}(D))
embeds into

for

since each of the

do. Then the proof follows as in, e.g.
[
26
, Proposition 2.10]
.
∎

Equivalently, in Proposition

,

3.
Energy balance

3.1.
A sufficient condition for an energy balance in mean

Our goal is now to show that if the sequence of Navier-Stokes solutions
\{u_{\nu}\}_{\nu}
satisfy a uniform condition on the modulus of continuity of the structure functions in mean,

(3.1)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0<r\leqslant r_{0}}\frac{S_{2}^{T}(u_{\nu},r)^{2}}{\phi(r)^{2}}\leqslant C,

for some
C>0
,
r_{0}>0
,
uniformly in
\nu>0
for some modulus of continuity
\phi
, then the sequence of laws of
u_{\nu}
,
\mu_{\nu}=\mathbb{P}\circ u_{\nu}^{-1}
is tight and with Skorokhod’s theorem, a random variable and a probability space can be identified which satisfies an energy balance in mean with respect to that probability measure. Conversely, if
u_{\nu}
is a physical realization obtained through a sequence of solutions of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations, and its limit
u
satisfies an energy balance in mean, then the sequence of laws
\mu_{\nu}
is tight on
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
. Since the condition (
3.1
) combined with the uniform weak continuity in time of the
u_{\nu}
yield tightness in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
, this is a stochastic version of the reverse direction.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
.
Let
\{u_{\nu}\}_{\nu>0}
be a sequence of stochastically strong solutions of the 2D stochastic Navier-Stokes equations (
2.2
) with initial data
\bar{u}_{\nu}\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
and forcing term
f_{\nu}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
.
Assume that as
\nu\to 0
,
\bar{u}_{\nu}\to\bar{u}
in
L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
and that
f_{\nu}\rightharpoonup f
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
.
Assume that the initial data satisfies uniformly in
\nu>0
for some
p>2
,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\leqslant C<\infty,

and assume that
\bar{u}_{\nu}
are measurable with respect to
\mathscr{G}_{0}
.
Furthermore, assume that the
u_{\nu}
satisfy, uniformly in
\nu
,

(3.2)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0<r\leqslant r_{0}}\frac{S_{2}^{T}(u_{\nu}(t),r)^{2}}{\phi(r)^{2}}\leqslant C,

for some monotonically increasing modulus of continuity
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
an some
r_{0}>0
. Then a subsequence of the laws
\mu_{\nu}=\mathbb{P}\circ u_{\nu}^{-1}
of
\{u_{\nu}\}_{\nu>0}
converges weakly on
L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
and there exists a probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathscr{F}},\{\widetilde{\mathscr{G}}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
and random variables
\widetilde{u}_{\nu}
that have the same laws as
u_{\nu}
and converge
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. to
\widetilde{u}
which is a martingale solution of the stochastic Euler equations as in Definition
2.6
that satisfies for almost every
t\in[0,T]
, the energy balance

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\widetilde{\bar{u}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}(\widetilde{u},f)ds+\bar{\sigma}t.

To prove this, we need a couple of auxiliary lemmas whose deterministic versions were proved in
[
26
]
. We show here how to modify them to hold in the stochastic case.
We start with the following enhanced Poincaré lemma which was proved in
[
26
]
:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
v
be a random variable on
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},(\mathscr{G}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
taking values in
H^{2}(D)\cap L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
almost surely and denote
\eta:=\operatorname{curl}v
. Assume that

(3.3)

\left(\mathbb{E}S_{2}^{T}(v;r)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leqslant C\phi(r),\quad\forall\,0\leqslant r\leqslant r_{0}

for some monotonically increasing modulus of continuity and a suitable
r_{0}>0
. Furthermore, let
\tau
be a stopping time that is bounded almost surely, i.e.,
\tau\leqslant C
a.s. for some
C>0
. Then for any
\delta>0

(3.4)

\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt\right)^{2}\gamma\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt\right)\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt,

where
\gamma:\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
is a continuous monotonically increasing function with the property that
\gamma(y)\geqslant\gamma_{0}>0
for all
y\geqslant 0
and
\gamma(y)\to\infty
as
y\to\infty
.

Proof.

The key idea of the proof is Lemma 2.6 in
[
26
]
, i.e., we have to show that for a function
v\in H^{2}(D)\cap L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
with
\operatorname{curl}v=\eta
we have

(3.5)

\left\|\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leqslant 2S_{2}(v;r)^{2}+Cr^{2}\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

To show this inequality, we assume that
v
is smooth, then the result follows by using the density of smooth functions in
H^{2}(D)\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
. By Taylor’s theorem, we have for
h\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

h\cdot\nabla v(x)=v(x+h)-v(x)-\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)(h\otimes h):\nabla^{2}v(x+th)dt.

By triangle inequality, we have

|h\cdot\nabla v(x)|^{2}\leqslant 2|v(x+h)-v(x)|^{2}+2\left|\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)h\otimes h:\nabla^{2}v(x+th)dt\right|^{2}.

Now we integrate over
B_{r}(0)\times D
and divide by
|B_{r}(0)|
:

\displaystyle\int_{D}\fint_{B_{r}(0)}|h\cdot\nabla v(x)|^{2}dhdx

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{D}\fint_{B_{r}(0)}|v(x+h)-v(x)|^{2}dhdx+2\int_{D}\fint_{B_{r}(0)}\left|\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)h\otimes h:\nabla^{2}v(x+th)dt\right|^{2}dhdx

\displaystyle\leqslant 2S_{2}(v;r)^{2}+2\int_{D}\fint_{B_{r}(0)}|h|^{4}|\nabla^{2}v(x)|^{2}dhdx

\displaystyle\leqslant 2S_{2}(v;r)^{2}+Cr^{4}\left\|\nabla^{2}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We note that due to the periodic boundary conditions and the fact that
v
is divergence free, we have
\left\|\nabla^{2}v\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|\Delta v\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|\operatorname{curl}\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}
. Furthermore, we can rewrite the integrand on the left hand side as follows using spherical coordinates:

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{r}(0)}|h\cdot\nabla v(x)|^{2}dh

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi r^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{2\pi}s\left[(s\sin\theta\partial_{1}v^{1}+s\cos\theta\partial_{2}v^{1})^{2}+(s\sin\theta\partial_{1}v^{2}+s\cos\theta\partial_{2}v^{2})^{2}\right]d\theta ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi r^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}s^{3}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Bigg[\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\cos(2\theta)}{2}\right)\left((\partial_{1}v^{1})^{2}+(\partial_{1}v^{2})^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\cos(2\theta)}{2}\right)\left((\partial_{2}v^{1})^{2}+(\partial_{2}v^{2})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sin(2\theta)(\partial_{1}v^{1}\partial_{2}v^{1}+\partial_{1}v^{2}\partial_{2}v^{2})\Bigg]d\theta ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\nabla v(x)|^{2}\int_{0}^{r}s^{3}ds=\frac{r^{2}}{4}|\nabla v(x)|^{2}.

Thus we obtain

\frac{r^{2}}{4}\left\|\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{r^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leqslant 2S_{2}(v;r)^{2}+Cr^{4}\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which proves (
3.5
). Note that this identity is independent of time and realization of the random event
\omega
. Thus after dividing by
r^{2}/4
, we integrate the above identity in time on
[\delta\wedge\tau,t\wedge\tau]
and then on
[\delta\wedge\tau,T\wedge\tau]
and take expectations to obtain

(3.6)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt&\leqslant\frac{8}{r^{2}}\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}S_{2}(v;r)^{2}dsdt+4Cr^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt\\
&\leqslant\frac{C\phi(r)^{2}}{r^{2}}+Cr^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt.\end{split}

We choose
r
to balance terms, c.f.
[
26
, p. 1094]
,

r=\frac{\phi(\bar{r})^{1/2}}{\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt\right)^{1/4}},\quad\text{where }\quad\bar{r}:=\frac{\bar{\phi}^{1/2}}{\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt\right)^{1/4}},

and
\bar{\phi}>0
provides an upper bound
\phi(r)\leqslant\bar{\phi}
. Then (
3.6
) can be bounded as (recall that
\phi
is monotonically increasing)

(3.7)

\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt\leqslant C\phi(\bar{r})\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt\right)^{1/2}.

For simplicity, we denote
\widetilde{\phi}(r)=(C\phi(\bar{\phi}^{1/2}r))^{2}
which is still a monotonically increasing function of
r
and
\widetilde{\phi}(0)=0
, thus it is a modulus of continuity. Using this on the right hand side of (
3.7
) yields

(3.8)

\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt\right)^{2}\leqslant\widetilde{\phi}\left(\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt\right)^{-1/4}\right)\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt.

We define
f(z)=\widetilde{\phi}(z^{-1/4})z
. Clearly, we have
f(z)=o(z)
as
z\to\infty
, because
\widetilde{\phi}
is a modulus of continuity, and goes to zero as
z\to\infty
. From
[
26
, Lemma C.1]
, we obtain that there is a dominating function
F(z)\geqslant f(z)
still satisfying
F(z)=o(z)
, which is continuous and stricty monotonically increasing and thus has an inverse
F^{-1}
which grows superlinearly at infinity and can be represented in the form
F^{-1}(y)=\gamma(\sqrt{y})y
where
\gamma:\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
is a continuous monotonically increasing function with the property that there exists
\gamma_{0}>0
such that
\gamma(\sqrt{y})\geqslant\gamma_{0}>0
for all
y\geqslant 0
and
\gamma(\sqrt{y})\to\infty
as
y\to\infty
. Thus, using this result, we obtain from (
3.8
),

\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt\right)^{2}\gamma\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt\right)\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdt.

which proves the claim.
∎

Next, we prove a bound on the vorticity that is independent of the initial vorticity
\bar{\eta}_{\nu}=\operatorname{curl}\bar{u}_{\nu}
(which might blow with
\nu
.) The proof is an adaption of
[
20
, Lemma 3.7]
to the stochastic setting.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u_{\nu}
be a solution of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations (
2.2
) with forcing
f_{\nu}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
and initial data
\bar{u}_{\nu}\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
as in Definition
2.3
. Let
\tau
be a stopping time that is almost surely bounded and positive, i.e.
0<\tau\leqslant c
a.s., and satisfies
\mathbb{E}t\wedge\tau>0
for any
t>0
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))}
,
\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
and
\bar{\rho}
and
T
such that the vorticity
\eta_{\nu}=\operatorname{curl}u_{\nu}
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left(T\wedge\tau\left\|\eta_{\nu}(t\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\nu t}C(f,T,\bar{\sigma},\bar{\rho}).

Proof.

Since
u_{\nu}
is a solution of (
2.2
), we obtain from
[
25
, Proposition 2.4.8]
,
that it satisfies an energy balance in mean, which we rewrite for any
t\in[0,T]
as

(3.9)

\begin{split}\nu\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt&=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}(f_{\nu},u_{\nu})dt+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\sigma}\mathbb{E}t\wedge\tau\\
&\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(D))}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\sigma}t\\
&\leqslant C(\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(D))},\bar{\sigma},T).\end{split}

Next, we consider the energy balance for
\eta_{\nu}
: Since a priori, we can only expect
\eta_{\nu}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
from the energy balance for
u_{\nu}
, (
3.9
), we consider a Galerkin approximation in which we will pass to the limit at the end. So let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
the previous basis of smooth Stokes eigenfunctions of
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
used to define
\sigma\cdot W
and define as in
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
or
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
the Galerkin projection

(3.10)

P_{m}v=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(v,e_{i})e_{i}.

Then, let us consider a Galerkin approximation of
u_{\nu}
(
\eta_{\nu}
respectively) denoted by
u^{m}_{\nu}
(
\eta_{\nu}^{m}
respectively) where
m\in\mathbb{N}
is the number of basis functions, i.e.,

(du_{\nu}^{m},e_{j})+\int_{D}\left((u^{m}_{\nu}\cdot\nabla)u^{m}_{\nu}\right)\cdot e_{j}dxdt+\nu\int_{D}\nabla u_{\nu}^{m}:\nabla e_{j}dxdt=(f_{\nu},e_{j})dxdt+b_{j}d\beta_{j}(t),

which translates to

(3.11)

(d\eta_{\nu}^{m},\operatorname{curl}e_{j})+\int_{D}\left((u^{m}_{\nu}\cdot\nabla)\eta^{m}_{\nu}\right)\cdot\operatorname{curl}e_{j}dxdt+\nu\int_{D}\nabla\eta_{\nu}^{m}:\nabla\operatorname{curl}e_{j}dxdt\\
=(\operatorname{curl}f_{\nu},\operatorname{curl}e_{j})dt+b_{j}\left\|\operatorname{curl}e_{j}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\beta_{j}(t),

for
\eta_{\nu}^{m}
. We denote
\eta_{\nu}^{m}(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\eta_{i}^{m}(t)\operatorname{curl}e_{i}(x)
and
f_{\nu}^{m}:=P_{m}f_{\nu}
as well as
\sigma^{m}dW:=P_{m}\sigma dW
.
Next, we use Itô’s formula for
F(\eta^{m}_{\nu})=\left\|\eta^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
(c.f.
[
10
, Lemma 4.24, Theorem 4.32]
)

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\eta_{\nu}^{m}(t\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=\left\|\eta^{m}_{\nu}(r\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\nu\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+2\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}(\operatorname{curl}f^{m}_{\nu},\eta_{\nu}^{m})ds\\
&\quad+\left\|\operatorname{curl}\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(t\wedge\tau-r\wedge\tau)+2\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\eta_{\nu}^{m}\operatorname{curl}\sigma^{m}dW(s)\\
&\leqslant\left\|\eta^{m}_{\nu}(r\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\nu\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta_{\nu}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+\frac{1}{\nu}\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|f^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\\
&\quad+\left\|\operatorname{curl}\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}t\wedge\tau+2\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\eta_{\nu}^{m}\operatorname{curl}\sigma^{m}dW(s).\end{split}

We integrate this over
r\in[0,t\wedge\tau]
and take expectations,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left(t\wedge\tau\left\|\eta_{\nu}^{m}(t\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)&\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta_{\nu}^{m}(r\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dr-\nu\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\nabla\eta_{\nu}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdr+\frac{1}{\nu}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|f^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dsdr\\
&\quad+\left\|\operatorname{curl}\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathbb{E}(t\wedge\tau)^{2}+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\eta_{\nu}^{m}\operatorname{curl}\sigma^{m}dW(s)dr\\
&\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta_{\nu}^{m}(r)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dr+\frac{t}{\nu}\left\|f^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))}^{2}+\bar{\rho}t^{2}\\
&\quad+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\!\!\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\eta_{\nu}^{m}\operatorname{curl}\sigma^{m}dW(s)dr.\end{split}

We need to estimate the last term, the stochastic integral. We can rewrite and bound it as follows, using first the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and then Itô isometry
[
10
, Equation (4.30)]
:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\!\!\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\eta_{\nu}^{m}\operatorname{curl}\sigma^{m}dW(s)dr\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t\wedge\tau]}(r)\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\eta_{\nu}^{m}\operatorname{curl}\sigma^{m}dW(s)dr\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t\wedge\tau]}(r)\right)^{1/2}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{[r\wedge\tau,t\wedge\tau]}(s)\eta_{\nu}^{m}(s)\operatorname{curl}\sigma^{m}dW(s)\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}dr

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{[r\wedge\tau,t\wedge\tau]}(s)\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}^{2}|(\eta_{\nu}^{m},\operatorname{curl}e_{k})|^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}dr

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{r\wedge\tau}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta_{\nu}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}dr\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}\left\|\operatorname{curl}e_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\bar{\rho}^{1/2}t\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|\eta_{\nu}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\bar{\rho}^{1/2}t}{\nu}C(\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(D))},\bar{\sigma},T),

by (
3.9
) (which can be repeated for the Galerkin approximation and yields the same, uniform in
m
bound). Thus, we can estimate (
3.13
) as follows:

\mathbb{E}\left(t\wedge\tau\left\|\eta_{\nu}^{m}(t\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\nu}C(f,T,\bar{\sigma},\bar{\rho}).

Next, since

\frac{t\wedge\tau}{T\wedge\tau}\geqslant\frac{t}{T},\quad\text{a.s.},

since
\tau>0
a.s. and
0<t\leqslant T
, we can lower bound this by

\frac{t}{T}\mathbb{E}\left(T\wedge\tau\left\|\eta_{\nu}^{m}(t\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left(t\wedge\tau\left\|\eta_{\nu}^{m}(t\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\nu}C(f,T,\bar{\sigma},\bar{\rho}).

Thus, we obtain after multiplying this inequality by
\frac{T}{t}

\mathbb{E}\left(T\wedge\tau\left\|\eta_{\nu}^{m}(t\wedge\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\nu t}C(f,T,\bar{\sigma},\bar{\rho}).

Note that this bound is uniform in
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we can pass
m\to\infty
and obtain the result for the limit
\eta_{\nu}
. That
u^{m}_{\nu}\to u_{\nu}
and
\eta^{m}_{\nu}\to\eta_{\nu}
in a unique manner follows as in
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
.

∎

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

From (
2.4
), we know that solution to the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations satisfy an energy balance

(3.14)

\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\nu\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(u_{\nu},f_{\nu})ds+\bar{\sigma}t,

see e.g.
[
25
, Proposition 2.4.8]
. As in
[
15
]
one can also show that

(3.15)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\leqslant C(p).

(To make clear that this bound is independent of
\nu
, we state the proof in the appendix, c.f. Lemma
A.1
.)

In order to show tightness of the laws of
u_{\nu}
, we proceed as in
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
, we write
u_{\nu}
as

(3.16)

u_{\nu}(t)=\underbrace{\bar{u}_{\nu}}_{\text{I}}-\underbrace{\nu\int_{0}^{t}Au_{\nu}(s)ds}_{\text{II}}-\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}B(u_{\nu}(s),u_{\nu}(s))ds}_{\text{III}}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}f_{\nu}(s)ds}_{\text{IV}}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW(s)}_{\text{V}},

where
A=-P\Delta:D(A)\to L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
is the Stokes operator (
P
the Leray projector) and
B:H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)\times H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)\to(H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))^{*}
is the nonlinear operator defined through the trilinear form:

b(u,v,w)=\int_{D}(u\cdot\nabla)v\cdot wdx,\quad\langle B(u,v),w\rangle=b(u,v,w).

B
can be extended to a continuous operator
B:L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)\times L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)\to\mathscr{D}(A^{-\kappa})
for certain
\kappa>1
. The goal is to get a bound on the laws of
u_{\nu}
in
W^{\beta,p}(0,T;(H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)\cap H^{k}(D))^{*})
for certain
\beta,p,k
. Thus we estimate

\mathbb{E}\left\|\text{I}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\leqslant C,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\text{II}\right\|_{H^{1}(0,T;(H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))^{*})}^{2}\leqslant C,

by the energy balance (
3.14
). For the fourth term, we have

\mathbb{E}\left\|\text{IV}\right\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(D))}^{2}\leqslant C,

due to the assumption that
f\in L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
. For the fifth term, Lemma 2.1 in
[
15
]
yields for all
\beta\in(0,1/2)
and
p\geqslant 2

(3.17)

\mathbb{E}\left\|\text{V}\right\|_{W^{\beta,p}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))}^{p}\leqslant C(\beta).

Finally, for the third term, we use the skew-symmetry of the trilinear form
b
:

\left|b(u,u,v)\right|=\left|b(u,v,u)\right|\leqslant\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{3}\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)},

therefore

\left\|B(u_{\nu}(s),u_{\nu}(s))\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;(H^{3}\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))^{*})}^{2}\leqslant C\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|^{4}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))},

hence

\mathbb{E}\left\|\text{III}\right\|_{H^{1}(0,T;(H^{3}\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))^{*})}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))}^{2}\leqslant C,

using (
3.15
). Pick
\beta
and
p\geqslant 2
in (
3.17
) such that
\beta p>1
and denote
Z:=H^{1}(0,T;(H^{3}\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))^{*})+W^{\beta,p}(0,T;(H^{3}\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))^{*})
. Then we have, collecting all the terms,

\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{Z}\leqslant C.

This implies together with (
3.2
) and (
3.15
)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\sup_{0<r<r_{0}}\frac{S_{2}^{T}(u_{\nu},r)^{2}}{\phi(r)^{2}}+\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{Z}\leqslant C.

By Chebyshev inequality, we have for any
M>0
,

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0<r<r_{0}}\frac{S_{2}^{T}(u_{\nu},r)^{2}}{\phi(r)^{2}}\geqslant M\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{0<r<r_{0}}\frac{S_{2}^{T}(u_{\nu},r)^{2}}{\phi(r)^{2}}>M\right].

Thus, we obtain for any
M>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\sup_{r>0}\frac{S_{2}^{T}(u_{\nu},r)^{2}}{\phi(r)^{2}}+\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{Z}>M\right]\\
\leqslant\frac{1}{M}\left(\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\sup_{0<r<r_{0}}\frac{S_{2}^{T}(u_{\nu},r)^{2}}{\phi(r)^{2}}+\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{Z}\right)\leqslant\frac{C(\beta,p)}{M},

thus for a given
\varepsilon>0
, choosing
M_{\varepsilon}
such that
\frac{C(\beta,p)}{M_{\varepsilon}}<\varepsilon
, we get

(3.18)

\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\sup_{r>0}\frac{S_{2}^{T}(u_{\nu},r)^{2}}{\phi(r)^{2}}+\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{Z}\leqslant M_{\varepsilon}\right]\geqslant 1-\varepsilon.

The set

K_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{u_{\nu}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))\,:\,\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\sup_{r>0}\frac{S_{2}^{T}(u_{\nu},r)^{2}}{\phi(r)^{2}}+\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{Z}\leqslant M_{\varepsilon}\right\},

is a compact set in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
by Proposition
2.11
, thus by Prokohorov’s theorem, the sequence of measures
\mu_{\nu}=\mathbb{P}\circ u_{\nu}^{-1}
is tight in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
.
Furthermore, since we obtain from Proposition
2.11
that
Z\subset C^{\alpha}(0,T;(H^{3}\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))^{*})
for
\alpha=\min\{1/2,\beta-1/p\}
and
C^{\alpha}([0,T];(H^{3}\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))^{*})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))\subset C([0,T];(L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))_{w})
by
[
6
, Lemma II.5.9]
, the
\mu_{\nu}
are bounded on
C([0,T];L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)_{w})
and thus tight in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))\cap C([0,T];L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)_{w})
by
[
1
, Lemma 5.1.12 (c)]
, c.f. Remark
2.2
.
We pick a weakly convergent subsequence denoted by
\nu
for ease of notation.
Next, by the Skorokhod embedding theorem, there exists a stochastic basis
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathscr{F}},\{\widetilde{\mathscr{G}}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
and on this basis
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))\cap C([0,T];(L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))_{w}
-valued random variables
\widetilde{u}
,
\widetilde{u}_{\nu}
,
\nu>0
such that
\widetilde{u}_{\nu}
has the same law as
u_{\nu}
on
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))\cap C([0,T];(L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))_{w}
and
\widetilde{u}_{\nu}\to\widetilde{u}
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))\cap C([0,T];L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)_{w})

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-almost surely. Hence, we can pass to the limit in the weak formulation of all terms in (

) to find that the limit satisfies for all

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.

(\widetilde{u}(t),v)=(\widetilde{{u}}(0),v)+\int_{0}^{t}b(\widetilde{u},v,\widetilde{u})ds+\int_{0}^{t}(f(s),v)ds+\int_{0}^{t}(\widetilde{\sigma},v)d\widetilde{W}(s).

Since
\widetilde{u}\in C([0,T];L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)_{w})
, it is continuous at zero and we have almost surely

since the limit of

is unique.
Moreover, since

is a Brownian motion on the probability space

,

is a martingale solution.

It remains to show the energy balance. To do so, we introduce the following stopping time for

,

\tau^{\nu,M}=\inf\{t>0\,:\,\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\geqslant M\}\wedge T.

This is a stopping time since it is adapted to the filtration

and the stochastic process

has a continuous modification by Kolmogorov’s extension theorem. Indeed, we have for

, and
\alpha>0

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}|X^{\nu}(t)-X^{\nu}(s)|^{\alpha}=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left|\int_{s}^{t}\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(r)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dr\right|^{\alpha}\leqslant|t-s|^{\alpha}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(r)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2\alpha}\leqslant C(2\alpha)|t-s|^{\alpha},

for any

by (

). Thus picking

(which is possible since w assume that

), we obtain that

has a

-Hölder continuous modification by the Kolmogorov extension theorem, for any
\nu\geqslant 0
. Since
M>0
and
\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds=0
for

\tau^{\nu,M}
is a.s. continuous, we have that
\tau^{\nu,M}>0
almost surely. We also note that this stopping time is bounded,
\tau^{\nu,M}\leqslant T
, by definition, and since
\widetilde{u}_{\nu}\to\widetilde{u}
in

, we have

(3.20)

\tau^{M}:=\inf\{t>0\,:\,\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\widetilde{u}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\geqslant M\}\wedge T\leqslant\liminf_{\nu\to 0}\tau^{\nu,M},\quad\text{a.s.}

as for example in
[
17
, Section 3]
. Now since we have from the previously established a.s.

-convergence of
\widetilde{u}_{\nu}
, for any continuous

, and almost every

,

and therefore also for almost every

for almost every

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(t)-\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(t\wedge\tau^{M})-\widetilde{u}(t\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

and therefore also

(3.22)

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt.

From (
3.21

,

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(t\wedge\tau^{M})-\widetilde{u}(t\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Next, we observe that the limit
\widetilde{u}(t)

t\mapsto\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}(\widetilde{u}(t),v),\quad\text{is continuous. }

Then, by Fatou’s lemma,

(3.25)

{\mathbb{E}}\left\|{\bar{u}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\widetilde{\bar{u}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leqslant\liminf_{t\to 0}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\widetilde{u}(t\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(t\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(\widetilde{u}_{\nu},f_{\nu})ds+\bar{\sigma}\mathbb{E}t\wedge\tau^{M}.

We can use the strong convergence of the initial data

in

, the weak convergence of

in

Now using again (

Combining this with (

\lim_{t\to 0}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\widetilde{u}(t\wedge\tau^{M})-\widetilde{\bar{u}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0,

For term II, since we are picking

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\left\|\widetilde{u}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\quad-\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\left(\left\|\widetilde{u}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\right)dt}_{\text{IIa}}.

We bound the second term as follows:

\displaystyle|\text{IIa}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}|\theta(t)|\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\left|\left\|\widetilde{u}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\right|dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left|\left\|\widetilde{u}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\right|T\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])},

which goes to zero by (

).
Thus, we have

\text{II}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\left\|\widetilde{u}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt

Now, from (
3.29
), we obtain

\left|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\left\|\widetilde{u}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt-\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\left\|\widetilde{\bar{u}}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}T,

thus

\lim_{\nu\to 0}\left|\text{II}-\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{\bar{u}}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt\right|\leqslant C\varepsilon.

Next, we rewrite term III as follows:

III

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f,\widetilde{u})dsdt

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f_{\nu}-f,\widetilde{u})dsdt}_{\text{IIIa}}+\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f_{\nu},\widetilde{u}_{\nu}-\widetilde{u})dsdt}_{\text{IIIb}}.

For the second term on the right hand side we use the weak convergence of
f_{\nu}
in

to obtain

\text{IIIa}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

For the third term on the right hand side, we use the uniform boundedness of the
f_{\nu}
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
combined with (
3.21
) to obtain

|\text{IIIb}|\leqslant\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}T\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(t)-\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Thus we have

\text{III}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f,\widetilde{u})dsdt.

Now since

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\!\theta(t)\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f,u)dsdt-\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\!\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f,u)dsdt\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\left|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\!\theta(t)\int_{0}^{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}(f,u)dsdt\right|+\left|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\!\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f,u)dsdt\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}T\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\left\|f(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\delta\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\left\|f(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\varepsilon\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}T\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

by (
3.30
), we have

\lim_{\nu\to 0}\left|\text{III}-\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\!\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f,u)dsdt\right|\leqslant C\varepsilon.

Term IV does not depend on
\nu
, we can estimate it in terms of
\delta
and
\varepsilon
as follows:

\left|\text{IV}-\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\bar{\sigma}t\wedge\tau^{M}dt\right|\leqslant C\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\bar{\sigma}T\delta\leqslant C\varepsilon

We proceed to term V: We write it as

\begin{split}\text{V}&=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt\\
&+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},t\wedge\tau^{M}]}(s)(\widetilde{u}_{\nu}-\widetilde{u})\cdot\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt}_{\text{Va}}.\end{split}

We use the Itô isometry for
\Phi^{\nu}(s):=\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},t\wedge\tau^{M}]}(s)(\widetilde{u}_{\nu}-\widetilde{u})(s)
, c.f.
[
10
, Equation (4.30)]
to show that term
Va
goes to zero. We estimate, first using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and then the Itô isometry:

\displaystyle|\text{Va}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}|\theta(t)|\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}(t)\right)^{1/2}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},t\wedge\tau^{M}]}(s)(\widetilde{u}_{\nu}-\widetilde{u})(s)\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}|\theta(t)|\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},t\wedge\tau^{M}]}(s)(\widetilde{u}_{\nu}-\widetilde{u})(s)\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}|\theta(t)|\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\nu}(s)\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}|\theta(t)|\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}\left|(\Phi^{\nu}(s),e_{k})\right|^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}|\theta(t)|\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},t\wedge\tau^{M}]}(s)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}\left|((\widetilde{u}_{\nu}-\widetilde{u})(s),e_{k})\right|^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}|\theta(t)|\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}\left|((\widetilde{u}_{\nu}-\widetilde{u})(s),e_{k})\right|^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}|\theta(t)|\bar{\sigma}^{1/2}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\widetilde{u}(s)-\widetilde{u}_{\nu}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}dt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

by (
3.21
).
Hence,
\text{Va}\to 0
as
\nu\to 0
and

\text{V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt.

We have

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt-\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[0,T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\int_{0}^{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt\right|+\left|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt\right|

We use the Itô isometry once more to show that the two terms on the right hand side are of order
\varepsilon
:

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\int_{0}^{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}|\theta(t)|\sqrt{\bar{\sigma}}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant T\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\sqrt{\bar{\sigma}}\delta^{1/2}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\left\|\widetilde{u}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\varepsilon,

by the assumptions on
\delta
and (
3.15
). Similarly, the second term above can be show to be of order
\mathscr{O}(\varepsilon)
. Thus we have that

\lim_{\nu\to 0}\left|\text{V}-\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt\right|\leqslant C\varepsilon.

Next, we show that term
\text{VI}\to 0
. First, we note that we can upper bound this term as follows:

\displaystyle|\text{VI}|

\displaystyle=\left|\nu\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\mathbf{1}_{[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dsdt\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\nu\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dsdt.

Hence it is sufficient to show that

\text{VIa}:=\nu\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dsdt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Without loss of generality, let us assume that the
\widetilde{u}_{\nu}
are sufficiently smooth in space for the following calculations. If it is not the case, we can consider a Galerkin approximation as in Lemma
3.3
, c.f., (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), and pass to the limit after establishing a uniform bound. We consider the energy balance for the vorticity
\eta_{\nu}:=\widetilde{u}_{\nu}
, c.f., (
3.12
),
using
[
25
, Theorem 7.7.5]
for
F(\eta_{\nu})=\left\|\eta_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
for any
\delta>0
,

\left\|\eta_{\nu}(t\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\left\|{\eta}_{\nu}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-2\nu\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\nabla\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}ds\\
-2\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f_{\nu},\operatorname{curl}\eta_{\nu})\,ds+2\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(\operatorname{curl}(\sigma),\eta_{\nu})d\widetilde{W}(s)+\bar{\rho}(t\wedge\tau^{M}-\delta\wedge\tau^{M}).

With Young’s inequality, we estimate

-2\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f_{\nu},\operatorname{curl}\eta_{\nu})\,ds\leq\nu\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\nabla\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,ds+\nu^{-1}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,ds.

Integrating this identity on
[\delta\wedge\tau^{M},T\wedge\tau^{M}]
and taking expectations, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\eta_{\nu}(t\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}(T\wedge\tau^{M}-\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\left\|{\eta}_{\nu}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\nu\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\nabla\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt

\displaystyle\quad+\nu^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt+\bar{\rho}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}(T\wedge\tau^{M}-\delta\wedge\tau^{M})^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left(T\wedge\tau^{M}\left\|{\eta}_{\nu}(\delta\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right)-\nu\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\nabla\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt

\displaystyle\quad+\nu^{-1}T\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))}+\bar{\rho}T^{2}.

Using Lemma
3.3
with
t=\delta
, we can upper bound the first term on the right hand side so that we obtain

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\eta_{\nu}(t\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt\leq\frac{C}{\delta\nu}-\nu\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\nabla\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt+\nu^{-1}T\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))}+\bar{\rho}T^{2}.

Now, we use the enhanced Poincaré Lemma
3.2
, to bound this by

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\eta_{\nu}(t\wedge\tau^{M})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt\leq\frac{C}{\delta\nu}\\
-\nu\gamma\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt\right)\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt\right)^{2}+\nu^{-1}T\left\|f_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))}+\bar{\rho}T^{2}.

We multiply this inequality by
\nu
and use that the left hand side is nonnegative, thus after rearranging

\gamma\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt\right)\left(\nu\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt\right)^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\delta}.

To show that

\limsup_{\nu\to 0}\nu\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt=0,

we follow the argument in
[
20
, Lemma 3.11]
. We denote

\zeta_{\nu}=\nu\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\!\!\int_{\delta\wedge\tau^{M}}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left\|\eta_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,dsdt,

and assume by contradiction that
\limsup_{\nu\to 0}\zeta_{\nu}\geqslant 2\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Then we can find a subsequence
\{\nu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\zeta_{\nu_{k}}\geqslant\varepsilon_{0}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we must have that

\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\gamma_{0}\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\gamma\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\nu_{k}}\right)\leqslant\zeta_{\nu_{k}}^{2}\gamma\left(\frac{\zeta_{\nu_{k}}}{\nu_{k}}\right)\leqslant\frac{C}{\delta}.

As
\nu_{k}\to 0
, we must have
{\varepsilon_{0}}/{\nu_{k}}\to\infty
, thus by the properties of
\gamma
, we obtain
\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma(\zeta_{\nu_{k}}/\nu_{k})=\infty
. Hence the left hand side goes to infinity as
k\to\infty
while the right hand side is uniformly bounded by
M/\delta<\infty
.

This is a contradiction and hence we must have
\limsup_{\nu\to 0}\zeta_{\nu}=0
. This proves that VIa and hence the dissipation term VI vanishes.
Thus passing to the limit
\nu\to 0
in all the terms in (
3.32
), we obtain

(3.33)

\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt}_{\text{(i)}}=\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{\bar{u}}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt}_{\text{(ii)}}\\
+2\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(f,\widetilde{u})dsdt}_{\text{(iii)}}+\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\bar{\sigma}tdt}_{\text{(iv)}}\\
+2\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt}_{\text{(v)}}+\mathscr{O}(\varepsilon).

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this holds as an equality.
Next, we want to send
M\to\infty
. We will show first that

(3.34)

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}[\lim_{M\to\infty}\tau^{M}=T]=1,

thus
\tau^{M}\to T
as
M\to\infty
almost surely.
We note that by Chebyshev inequality, for any
M>0
, we have

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}[\tau^{M}<T]=\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\widetilde{u}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\geqslant M\right]\leqslant\frac{1}{M}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\widetilde{u}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leqslant\frac{C}{M},

where the last inequality follows (for example) from (
3.15
). Sending
M\to\infty
, we obtain in this inequality

\lim_{M\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}[\tau^{M}<T]=0.

Since
\tau^{M}
is monotone increasing in
M
, we conclude that

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}[\lim_{M\to\infty}\tau^{M}<T]=\lim_{M\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}[\tau^{M}<T]=0,

thus (
3.34
) holds. Now we can send
M\to\infty
in all the terms in (
3.33
). We assume for the moment that
\theta
is nonnegative. For the first term, (i), since
\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt
is increasing in
M
and converges to
\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt
almost surely, we can use the monotone convergence theorem and pass to the limit
M\to\infty
and obtain

\text{(i)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

In the same way, we establish convergence of terms (ii) and (iv):

\text{(ii)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{\bar{u}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt,\quad\text{(iv)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\bar{\sigma}tdt.

For terms (iii) and (v), we first note that we can replace
t\wedge\tau^{M}
in the inner integral by
t
.
Then, we bound the integrands uniformly in
\tau^{M}
. For (iii), we have

\left|\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t}(f,\widetilde{u})dsdt\right|\leqslant T\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))}\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))},

which is integrable with respect to
d\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
by (
3.15
), thus we can use the dominated convergence theorem to pass to the limit in (iii). For the integrand in (v), we have by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt\right|&\leqslant\left(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}|\theta(t)|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)\right)^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leqslant\sqrt{T}\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)\right)^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\end{split}

which is integrable in
d\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
thanks to the Itô isometry
[
10
, Equation (4.30)]
and the uniform bound (
3.15
). Thus, we can also send
M\to\infty
in the term (v) using the dominated convergence theorem once more. Combining all of this, we obtain in the limit
M\to\infty
for (
3.33
),

(3.35)

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\left\|\widetilde{\bar{u}}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}dt\\
+2\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t}(f,\widetilde{u})dsdt+2\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\bar{\sigma}tdt+2\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}(s)dt.

For nonpositive
\theta
, we can multiply identity (
3.33
) by
-1
and repeat the argument above and general
\theta\in C([0,T])
can be decomposed into their nonnegative and nonpositive part via
\theta=\theta^{+}-\theta^{-}
where
\theta^{+}(t)=\max(0,\theta(t))
and
\theta^{-}(t)=\min(0,\theta(t))
. Note that the last term in (
3.35
) is zero since
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{u}\sigma d\widetilde{W}=0
.
Now finally
\theta
gets its big moment.
Since
\theta\in C([0,T])
can be chosen arbitrary, it follows from (
3.35
) that for almost all
t\in[0,T]
it holds that

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\widetilde{u}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\widetilde{\bar{u}}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}+2\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}(f,\widetilde{u})ds+\bar{\sigma}t,

which is what we wanted to prove.

∎

Remark 3.4

(Algebraic decay of structure functions)
.

If the modulus of continuity is of the form
\phi(r)=r^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
, one can replace condition (
3.1
) by

(3.36)

\mathbb{E}S^{T}_{2}(u_{\nu},r)^{2}\leqslant C\phi(r)^{2},\quad\forall\,0\leqslant r\leqslant r_{0}.

In fact, condition (

) is used in its precise form only to derive tightness of the measures
\mu_{\nu}=\mathbb{P}\circ u_{\nu}^{-1}
on

as in (
3.18
). Condition (
3.36
) implies that
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|u_{\nu}|_{H^{s}}^{2}dt<\infty
uniformly in
\nu>0
for
0<s<\alpha
as we will show in the upcoming Lemma
3.5
. Since
H^{s}(D)
is a compact subset of
L^{2}(D)
for
s>0
, one can derive

\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\int_{0}^{T}|u_{\nu}|_{H^{s}}^{2}dt+\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{Z}\leqslant M_{\varepsilon}\right]\geqslant 1-\varepsilon.

for any
\varepsilon>0
instead of (
3.18
), which then implies tightness of the measures
\mu_{\nu}
on
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
using a variant of Proposition
2.11
(c.f. also
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
).

Lemma 3.5
.

The following relation between structure function and Sobolev spaces holds:

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|v(t)|_{W^{\alpha,p}}^{p}dt<\infty\implies\forall\;r>0:\mathbb{E}S_{p}^{T}(v(t);r)^{p}\leq Cr^{p\alpha}\implies\forall\;s<\alpha:\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|v(t)|_{W^{s,p}}^{p}dt<\infty.

Proof.

For the first implication observe that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{S_{p}(v;r)}{r^{\alpha}}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=C\int_{D}\int_{B_{r}(0)}\frac{|v(x+h)-v(x)|^{p}}{r^{d+p\alpha}}\,dh\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{D}\int_{D}\frac{|v(x+h)-v(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{d+p\alpha}}\,dh\,dx

\displaystyle=C|v|_{W^{\alpha,p}}^{p},

where we used the Sobolev–Slobodeckij seminorm in the last line. Integrating over time and taking expectations yields the first implication.

For the second implication let
r_{0}=\diam(D)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
and
r_{i}=2^{-i}r_{0}
for
i>0
, and denote by
A_{r_{i}}
the annulus around
0
with radii
r_{i}
and
r_{i+1}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|v|_{W^{s,p}}^{p}dt

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}\int_{D}\frac{|v(x+h)-v(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{d+sp}}\,dh\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{A_{r_{i}}}\frac{|v(x+h)-v(x)|^{p}}{r_{i+1}^{d+sp}}\,dh\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r_{i+1}^{-sp}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}\fint_{B_{r_{i}}}|v(x+h)-v(x)|^{p}dt\,dh\,dx

\displaystyle=C\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r_{i+1}^{-sp}\,\mathbb{E}\,S_{p}^{T}(v;r_{i})^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r_{i+1}^{(\alpha-s)p}

\displaystyle<\infty,

for any
s<\alpha
.

∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\{u_{\nu}\}_{\nu>0}
be a sequence of solutions of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations as in Definition
2.3
.
Assume that
f_{\nu}\to f
in
L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
with
f,f_{\nu}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
and that
\bar{u}_{\nu}\to\bar{u}
in
L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
.
Assume that the laws
\mu_{\nu}:=\mathbb{P}\circ u_{\nu}^{-1}
are weakly tight on
\mathscr{P}(C([0,T];L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)_{w})\cap L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))_{w})
and converge weakly in
\mathscr{P}(C([0,T];L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)_{w})\cap L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))_{w})
to
\mu:=\mathbb{P}\circ u^{-1}
that satisfies

(3.37)

\mathbb{E}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(u,f)ds+\bar{\sigma}t.

Then
\mu_{\nu}
are a tight sequence on
\mathscr{P}(L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)))
, i.e., with respect to the strong topology on
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
.

Proof.

We pick a subsequence, still denoted by
\nu
such that
\mu_{\nu}\to\mu
weakly on
\mathscr{P}(C(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)_{w})\cap L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))_{w})
and
f_{\nu}\to f
in
L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
. Denote
X:=C(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)_{w})\cap L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
.
We subtract (
3.37
) from (
2.4
) to obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\nu\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla u_{\nu}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(u_{\nu},f_{\nu})ds-\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(u,f)ds\\
&\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(u_{\nu},f_{\nu})ds-\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(u,f)ds.\end{split}

Using the assumption on the convergence of the initial data, we have

\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Since
f_{\nu}\to f
in
L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(D))
, we have

\left|\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(u_{\nu},f-f_{\nu})ds\right|\leqslant\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f-f_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}ds\to 0,\quad\text{as }\,\nu\to 0,

using (
3.15
).
Moreover,

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(u-u_{\nu},f)ds=\int_{X}\int_{0}^{t}(u,f)dsd\mu(u)-\int_{X}\int_{0}^{t}(u,f)dsd\mu_{\nu}(u)\to 0,\quad\text{as }\,\nu\to 0,

since
\mu_{\nu}
converges to
\mu
in
\mathscr{P}(X_{w})
. This follows from
[
1
, Lemma 5.1.12 (d)]
, since

\psi(u)=\int_{0}^{t}(f,u)ds

is weakly continuous on bounded sets of
X
and
|\psi(u)|
is uniformly integrable on
X
.
Thus

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(f_{\nu},u_{\nu})ds-\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(f,u)ds\to 0,\quad\text{as }\,\nu\to 0.

We obtain that

(3.38)

\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{X}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\mu_{\nu}(u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{X}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\mu(u)=\mathbb{E}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Ths is true for any
t\in[0,T]
. Since
\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
are uniformly bounded by the energy balance, we can use the dominated convergence theorem to derive

\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt=\int_{X}\left\|u\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}d\mu_{\nu}(u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{X}\left\|u\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}d\mu(u)=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

Now we can apply
[
1
, Theorem 5.1.13]
with
X=L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
and
j(x)=x^{2}
to obtain that
\mu_{\nu}\to\mu
on
\mathscr{P}(X)
, in particular, with respect to the strong topology on
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
.

∎

Remark 3.7
.

We note that in this case
t\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is continuous thanks to the assumption of the energy balance (
3.37
) and the fact that the right hand side of (
3.37
) is continuous with respect to
t
.
Moreover, we have that
t\mapsto\mathbb{E}(u(t),v)
is continuous for any
v\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
that is measurable with respect to
\mathscr{F}
since
u
is weakly continuous with values in
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
, c.f. the argument for (
3.24
). Thus

\lim_{s\to t}\mathbb{E}\left\|u(t)-u(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lim_{s\to t}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|u(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\mathbb{E}(u(t),u(s))\right)=0,

which implies that
\left\|u(t)-u(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to 0
a.s. as
s\to t
. Thus
u\in C(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))
almost surely.

4.
Numerical Experiments

We simulate the Navier-Stokes equations (
2.2
) in the domain
[0,1]^{2}
with periodic boundary conditions using the highly efficient GPU based solver azeban
[
30
]
.

Fix a mesh width
\delta=\frac{1}{N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We consider the following spectral discretization of the Navier-Stokes equations in the Fourier domain

(4.1)

\begin{cases}du_{\nu}^{\delta}+\mathscr{P}_{N}(u_{\nu}^{\delta}\cdot\nabla u_{\nu}^{\delta})+\nabla p_{\nu}^{\delta}&=\nu\Delta u_{\nu}^{\delta}\,dt+f\,dt+\sigma\cdot dW\\
\operatorname{div}u_{\nu}^{\delta}&=0\\
u_{\nu}^{\delta}|_{t=0}&=\mathscr{P}_{N}\bar{u}\end{cases}

where
\mathscr{P}_{N}
is the spatial Fourier projection operator mapping a function
g(x,t)
to its first
N
Fourier modes:
\mathscr{P}_{N}=\sum_{|k|_{\infty}\leqslant N}\hat{g}_{k}(t)e^{ik\cdot x}
.

Note that this scheme merely conserves the divergence of
u_{\nu}^{\delta}
instead of setting it to zero. For this reason, we implicitly project all initial conditions onto divergence-free vector fields using the Leray projection.

For the sake of the experiments, we will choose the basis for the stochastic forcing term as

(4.2)

e_{i,j}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{2}{\sqrt{i^{2}+j^{2}}}\begin{bmatrix}j\cos(2\pi ix_{1})\cos(2\pi jx_{2})\\
i\sin(2\pi ix_{1})\sin(2\pi jx_{2})\end{bmatrix}

indexed by
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
and set all
b_{i,j}
corresponding to a basis function with one of the indices
i,j>N_{b}\in\mathbb{N}
to zero. All other
b_{i,j}
are chosen to be equal to
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We further set the deterministic forcing term
f=0
. We therefore expect the energy of the system to evolve according to

(4.3)

\mathbb{E}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\nu\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds=\mathbb{E}\|\bar{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\bar{\sigma}t,\quad t\geq 0,

where
\bar{\sigma}=\sum_{i,j}b_{i,j}^{2}=N_{b}^{2}\sigma^{2}
and
\nu
the artificial viscosity of our numerical solver. For the experiments performed here, we will set
N_{b}=9
leaving us with a total of
9^{2}=81
non-zero basis functions each having a coefficient
\sigma
. The energy is therefore expected to rise linearly in time with slope
\bar{\sigma}=81\sigma^{2}
. To experimentally verify this theoretical value of
\bar{\sigma}
, we compute solutions to the initial conditions given below and compute the value of

(4.4)

\mathbb{E}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\nu\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds-\mathbb{E}\|\bar{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

The integral over the gradient is hereby approximated by a simple Riemann sum over a total of 10000 rectangles (40000 for the flat vortex sheet experiment with
\sigma=0.1
). The norm of the gradient itself is computed in Fourier space making use of Parseval’s identity.

Furthermore, all simulations are performed with the three different viscosities
\nu=\frac{0.05}{N}
,
\nu=\frac{0.1}{N}
, and
\nu=\frac{0.2}{N}
where
N
is the resolution of the computational grid (
N=256
unless otherwise noted). We use 32 different realizations of each experiment.

As a first experiment, we use the Flat Vortex Sheet initial condition from
[

. The initial data is given by

The random variables
\alpha_{k}
and

and

This experiment is taken from

3.1

1.3

(4.7)

\text{sc}_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}\sin(x)&\mbox{ for }i=0\\
\cos(x)&\mbox{ for }i=1\end{cases}

and the
\alpha_{k}^{(mn)}\sim\mathscr{U}_{[-1,1]}
.

The forcing term is kept the same as before. In the following figures, first columns, we observe numerically that the solutions have a modulus of continuity in mean. We thus expect the evolution of the energy to be the same as before, independent of the initial condition. We observe almost perfect correspondence between the theoretical evolution of the energy and the energy of the simulated system. It is probable that part of the deviation stems from the discretization of the integral computing the loss of energy through dissipation due to viscosity. Increasing the number of samples generally improved the accuracy of the fit (especially for the shear layer), but simultaneously increased memory requirements. Discretizing using 10000 samples was found to be a good compromise. We note that as the viscosity
\nu
is decreased,
\mathbb{E}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
more closely follows
\bar{\sigma}t

Appendix A
Auxiliary results

Lemma A.1
.

Under the assumption that
\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{u}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}<\infty
for some
p\geqslant 2
,
solutions
u_{\nu}
of the Navier-Stokes equations (
2.2
) satisfy uniformly in
\nu

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{p}\leqslant C(p).

Proof.

We consider a Galerkin approximation
u^{m}_{\nu}
of
u_{\nu}
in order to be able to do any formal manipulations (c.f. the proof of Lemma
3.3
, equation (
3.10
) and ff.)).
By Itô’s formula, we have for
p\geqslant 2
,

d\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\leqslant p\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}(u_{\nu}^{m},du_{\nu}^{m})+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

Thus,

d\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\nu p\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leqslant p\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}(f^{m}_{\nu},u^{m}_{\nu})dt+p\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}(u^{m}_{\nu},\sigma^{m})dW\\
+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

since
b(u_{\nu}^{m},u_{\nu}^{m},u_{\nu}^{m})=0
. Using Young’s inequality, we obtain

d\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\nu p\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leqslant\frac{p}{2}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p}_{L^{2}}dt+\frac{p-2}{2}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\left\|f^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt+\left\|f^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\\
+p\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}(u^{m}_{\nu},\sigma^{m})dW+\left(\frac{1}{2}(p-2)(p-1)\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p}_{L^{2}}+p-1\right)\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

We integrate in time and take the supremum over time:

\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\nu p\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\\
\leqslant\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}(0)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\frac{p}{2}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\int_{0}^{s}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p}_{L^{2}}d\tau+\frac{p-2}{2}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\int_{0}^{s}\left\|f^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\\
+p\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\int_{0}^{s}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}(u^{m}_{\nu},\sigma^{m})dW(\tau)+\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\int_{0}^{s}\left(\frac{1}{2}(p-2)(p-1)\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p}_{L^{2}}+p-1\right)\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau.

We take expectations and then use the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality for the term involving the Brownian motion:

\begin{split}p\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}(u^{m}_{\nu},\sigma^{m})dW(\tau)\right|&\leqslant Cp\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2p-2}\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+Cp^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p-2}\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\\
&\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+Cp^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+1\right)\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\\
&\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+Cp^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau+Cp\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau.\end{split}

Thus, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\nu p\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p-2}_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\\
\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}(0)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant\tau}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}(s)\right\|^{p}_{L^{2}}d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau+\frac{p-2}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant\tau}\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}d\tau\\
+Cp^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant\tau}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\left\|\sigma^{m}(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau+Cp^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}(p-2)(p-1)\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant\tau}\left\|u_{\nu}^{m}\right\|^{p}_{L^{2}}+p-1\right)\left\|\sigma^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau.

Then we obtain by using Grönwall’s inequality

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\left\|u^{m}_{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\leqslant C.

The estimate is uniform in
m
and thus we can send
m\to\infty
and obtain that it holds in the limit also. As
u_{\nu}
is unique, we have
u_{\nu}^{m}\to u_{\nu}
almost surely. We further note that this estimate is uniform in
\nu
also.
∎

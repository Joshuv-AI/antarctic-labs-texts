Well-Posedness and Regularity of the Heat Equation with Robin Boundary Conditions in the Two-Dimensional Wedge
Marco Bravin, Manuel V. Gnann, Hans Knüpfer, Nader Masmoudi, Floris B. Roodenburg †, Jonas Sauer

Abstract
Well-posedness and higher regularity of the heat equation with Robin boundary conditions in an unbounded two-dimensional wedge is established in an L 2 L^{2} -setting of monomially weighted spaces. A mathematical framework is developed which allows to obtain arbitrarily high regularity without a smallness assumption on the opening angle of the wedge. The challenging aspect is that the resolvent problem exhibits two breakings of the scaling invariance, one in the equation and one in the boundary condition.

For a bounded domain, the terms with highest scaling are of leading order, while terms of lower lower scaling can be treated by perturbative methods.

To get both existence of weak solutions as well as higher regularity, we work in intersection spaces where both weighted and unweighted norms are controlled.
This approach necessitates a careful analysis, since the transition from weak solutions to classical solutions in this setting is surprisingly non–trivial.
This is related to the fact that the spaces of test functions associated with the intersection type spaces are naturally sum-type spaces.

Lemma 2.12

\displaystyle[\hskip-1.29167pt[v]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{\ell,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j+m=\ell}\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda={\mathfrak{s}}_{\ell+\alpha}^{\Omega}}\int_{0}^{\theta}|\lambda^{j}\partial_{\varphi}^{m}\widehat{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\ (\ell+1)\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda={\mathfrak{s}}_{\ell+\alpha}^{\Omega}}\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{|f(\lambda\varphi)|^{2}}{|g(\lambda\theta)|^{2}}\ \,\mathrm{d}\varphi\ |\lambda^{\ell}\widehat{\psi}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda\hskip 0.0pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{int-coscos}}}{{\leq}}\ C\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda={\mathfrak{s}}_{\ell+\alpha}^{\Omega}}|\lambda^{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{\psi}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda=[\psi]_{\ell-\frac{1}{2},\alpha}.

This yields the assertion.
∎

3
Variational Solution

In this section we will establish for sufficiently smooth data a variational solution to the resolvent equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \mu u-\Delta u\ &=\ f&&\text{in $\Omega$,}\\
u+\partial_{\nu}u\ &=g&&\text{on $\partial\Omega$},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

where
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
is the complex variable related to a Laplace transform of (
1.1
) in time.
The idea is to use a Lax-Milgram argument in an (unweighted) space
H
(see (
3.6
)) of sufficient regularity which ensures that (
3.1
) is fulfilled not only in a weak sense, but pointwise almost everywhere.
In order to find a suitable sesquilinear form, we test (
3.1
) with a certain linear combination of derivatives of
v\in H
which ensures the right amount of smoothness of the solution, see Definition
3.3
.
However, in order to use the fundamental lemma of calculus of variations to identify the Lax-Milgram solution with a distributional solution to (
3.1
), we show that the class of these linear combinations of derivatives of
v
contains
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
as
v
runs through
H
, and a similar argument is given for functions on the boundary.
The outline of this section is therefore as follows:
In Section
3.1
we show that the class of test functions is rich enough in the above sense.
In Section
3.2
we use this richness of the test functions to obtain via a Lax-Milgram scheme a variational solution
u\in H
which at the same time is a distributional solution.
Finally, in Section
3.3
we update the unweighted information on
u
to a weighted estimate.

3.1
Test Function Problem

In this section, we will provide certain surjectivity results in the space of test functions.
In Section
3.2
we will define a sesquilinear form in terms of the function
v
, which itself is defined by a smooth and compactly supported function
w
via the test function problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Av&=w\qquad\qquad&&\text{in $\Omega$,}\\
v&=0\qquad&&\text{on $\partial\Omega$,}\end{aligned}\right.

(3.2)

where
A:=c_{2}r^{2}\kappa+c_{0}-c_{1}(r\partial_{r})^{2}-\partial_{\varphi}^{2}
and
\kappa,c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}\in(0,\infty)
are suitable constants.
The advantage of a sesquilinear form in terms of such a test function is that a Lax-Milgram argument immediately yields a solution with sufficient regularity such that all terms in (
3.1
) are defined pointwise almost everywhere.
In this section we argue that the image of the operator
A
(which is acting on the dual side of the problem) is large enough to ensure uniqueness for the primal objects.

Note that problem (
3.2
) still has a (single) non-scaling invariance which does not allow for a pure Mellin approach.
We therefore want to employ a Lax-Milgram type argument in the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, defined as the closure of
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\lVert v\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\kappa\int_{\Omega}(|v|^{2}+|r\nabla v|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(|r^{-1}v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla r\nabla v|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proposition 3.1

(Test function problem)
.

Let
\kappa,c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}\in(0,\infty)
.
For
w\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
there is a unique solution
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
of (
3.2
), and it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(\kappa|v|^{2}+|\kappa rv|^{2}+\kappa|r\nabla v|^{2}+|r^{-1}v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla r\nabla v|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\ \lesssim\ \int_{\Omega}|r^{-1}w|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.\

(3.3)

Proof.

We introduce another Hilbert space
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
, defined as the closure of
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\lVert v\rVert_{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\lVert v\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\kappa\int_{\Omega}|(r\nabla)^{2}v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For a parameter
\delta>0
(specified below) we define
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\times\tilde{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(v,\psi):=\int_{\Omega}r^{-2}Av\,\big(1-\delta(r\partial_{r})^{2}-\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\big)\overline{\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Observe that for
v,\psi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
we obtain through integration by parts that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}r^{-2}Av\,\overline{\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=c_{2}\kappa\int_{\Omega}v\,\overline{\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{0}\int_{\Omega}r^{-2}v\,\overline{\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{1}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{r}v)\,(\partial_{r}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\int_{\Omega}(r^{-1}\partial_{\varphi}v)\,(r^{-1}\partial_{\varphi}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}r^{-2}Av\,((r\partial_{r})^{2}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=c_{2}\kappa\int_{\Omega}(r\partial_{r}v)\,(r\partial_{r}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x+2c_{2}\kappa\int_{\Omega}v\,(r\partial_{r}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{0}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{r}v)\,(\partial_{r}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+c_{1}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{r}r\partial_{r}v)\,(\partial_{r}r\partial_{r}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{r}\partial_{\varphi}v)\,(\partial_{r}\partial_{\varphi}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}r^{-2}Av\,(\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=c_{2}\kappa\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{\varphi}v)\,(\partial_{\varphi}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{0}\int_{\Omega}(r^{-1}\partial_{\varphi}v)\,(r^{-1}\partial_{\varphi}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+c_{1}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{r}\partial_{\varphi}v)\,(\partial_{r}\partial_{\varphi}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(r^{-1}\partial_{\varphi}^{2}v)\,(r^{-1}\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\overline{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x.

It follows that there is
C>0
such that
|\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(v,\psi)|\leq C\lVert v\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}}}
for all
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
\psi\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
.
Consequently,
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
has a unique extension to a bounded sesquilinear form
{\mathcal{B}}:{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{C}
.
Moreover, for
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
it holds

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{\mathcal{B}}(v,v)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa\,c_{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\kappa c_{2}\delta(1-2\delta)\int_{\Omega}|r\partial_{r}v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\kappa\,c_{2}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{\varphi}v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+c_{0}\int_{\Omega}(|r^{-1}v|^{2}+\delta|\partial_{r}v|^{2}+|r^{-1}\partial_{\varphi}v|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{1}\int_{\Omega}(|\partial_{r}v|^{2}+\delta|\partial_{r}r\partial_{r}v|^{2}+|\partial_{r}\partial_{\varphi}v|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\int_{\Omega}(|r^{-1}\partial_{\varphi}v|^{2}+\delta|\partial_{r}\partial_{\varphi}v|^{2}+|r^{-1}\partial_{\varphi}^{2}v|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x.

Hence, choosing
\delta:=\frac{1}{4}
we obtain a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{\mathcal{B}}(v,v)\geq c\Big(\kappa\int_{\Omega}(|v|^{2}+|r\nabla v|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(|r^{-1}v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla r\nabla v|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\geq c\lVert v\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}.

In conclusion,
{\mathcal{B}}:{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded and coercive sesquilinear form.

Define
{\mathcal{F}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{C}
for
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}
through

{\mathcal{F}}(\psi):=\int_{\Omega}r^{-2}w\,(1-\delta(r\partial_{r})^{2}-\partial_{\varphi}^{2})\overline{\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Then we can estimate

|{\mathcal{F}}(\psi)|\leq\Big(\int_{\Omega}|r^{-1}w|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|r^{-1}(1-\delta(r\partial_{r})^{2}-\partial_{\varphi}^{2})\psi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{w}\lVert\psi\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}}}

for a
C_{w}<\infty
, that is,
{\mathcal{F}}
is a bounded anti-linear functional. The Lax-Milgram theorem entails existence of a unique
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that

{\mathcal{B}}(v,\psi)={\mathcal{F}}(\psi)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(\kappa|v|^{2}+\kappa|r\nabla v|^{2}+|r^{-1}v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla r\nabla v|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\ \lesssim\ \int_{\Omega}|r^{-1}w|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.\

(3.4)

Since
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
, due to the definition of
{\mathcal{H}}
and by taking traces, we conclude that the boundary condition in (
3.2
) is satisfied.
For
\psi\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
we obtain
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(v,\psi)={\mathcal{B}}(v,\psi)={\mathcal{F}}(\psi)
, so that

\int_{\Omega}r^{-2}(Av-w)\,(1-\delta(r\partial_{r})^{2}-\partial_{\varphi}^{2})\overline{\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\psi\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}.

In order to conclude that the first line in (
3.2
) is satisfied, we thus need to show that for each
\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
there is
\psi\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
with

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} (1-\delta(r\partial_{r})^{2}-\partial_{\varphi}^{2})\psi&=\Phi&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\psi&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

We use the Mellin transform in
r
and expand in a sine Fourier series in the angle
\varphi\in(0,\theta)
, that is,

\widehat{\psi}(\lambda,\varphi)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\psi}_{k}(\lambda)a_{k}(\varphi),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\widehat{\psi}_{k}(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\theta}\widehat{\psi}(\lambda,\varphi)\,a_{k}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}\varphi\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pta_{k}(\varphi):=\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\theta}}\sin\big(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta}\varphi\big),

satisfying (
3.5
) on taking

\widehat{\psi}_{k}(\lambda):=\frac{\widehat{\Phi}_{k}(\lambda)}{1-\delta\lambda^{2}-(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\widehat{\Phi}_{k}(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\theta}\widehat{\Phi}(\lambda,\varphi)\,a_{k}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}\varphi.

Using the Plancherel identity for the Mellin transform and Parseval’s identity for the sine Fourier series, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\psi\rVert_{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{\theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\kappa r^{2}+1)(|\psi|^{2}+|r\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{\varphi}\psi|^{2}+|(r\partial_{r})^{2}\psi|^{2}+|r\partial_{r}\partial_{\varphi}\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\psi|^{2})\,\tfrac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi

\displaystyle=\kappa\int_{0}^{\theta}\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda=-1}\big((1+|\lambda|^{2}+|\lambda|^{4})\,|\widehat{\psi}(\lambda,\varphi)|^{2}+(1+|\lambda|^{2})\,|\partial_{\varphi}\widehat{\psi}(\lambda,\varphi)|^{2}+|\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\widehat{\psi}(\lambda,\varphi)|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\varphi

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\int_{0}^{\theta}\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda=0}\big((1+|\lambda|^{2}+|\lambda|^{4})\,|\widehat{\psi}(\lambda,\varphi)|^{2}+(1+|\lambda|^{2})\,|\partial_{\varphi}\widehat{\psi}(\lambda,\varphi)|^{2}+|\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\widehat{\psi}(\lambda,\varphi)|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\varphi

\displaystyle=\kappa\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda=-1}\big(1+|\lambda|^{2}+|\lambda|^{4}+(1+|\lambda|^{2})\,(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{2}+(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{4}\big)\,|\widehat{\psi}_{k}(\lambda)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda=0}\big(1+|\lambda|^{2}+|\lambda|^{4}+(1+|\lambda|^{2})\,(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{2}+(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{4}\big)|\widehat{\psi}_{k}(\lambda)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda

\displaystyle=\kappa\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{3+s^{2}+s^{4}+(2+s^{2})(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{2}+(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{4}}{(1-\delta-(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{2}+\delta s^{2}-2i\delta s)^{2}}\,|\widehat{\Phi}_{k}(-1+is)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1+s^{2}+s^{4}+(1+s^{2})\,(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{2}+(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{4}}{(1-(\tfrac{k\pi}{\theta})^{2}+\delta s^{2})^{2}}\,|\widehat{\Phi}_{k}(is)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\theta}\kappa\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\Phi}_{k}(-1+is)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\Phi}_{k}(is)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=\kappa\int_{\Omega}|\Phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}r^{-2}|\Phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Hence,
\psi\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
and therefore
v
fulfills (
3.2
).
In particular

\displaystyle|\kappa rv|^{2}=c_{2}^{-2}|rw-rAv|^{2}\lesssim|rw|^{2}+|r^{-1}v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla r\nabla v|^{2},

so that (
3.4
) updates to (
3.3
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\kappa,c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
.
Then for each
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
there is
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
\big(c_{2}\kappa+r^{-2}(c_{0}-c_{1}(r\partial_{r})^{2})\big)\rho=\eta
and for each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
with
2c_{1}\ell^{2}<c_{0}
and each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
it holds

\displaystyle\kappa\int_{0}^{\infty}|r^{\ell+1}(r\partial_{r})^{j}\rho|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}+\int_{0}^{\infty}|r^{\ell}(r\partial_{r})^{j}\rho|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}<\infty.

Proof.

Introduce the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{K}}
as the closure of
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with respect to the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{{\mathcal{K}}}
, where

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{{\mathcal{K}}}^{2}\

\displaystyle=\ \kappa\int_{0}^{\infty}|r^{\ell+1}\rho|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}+\int_{0}^{\infty}(|r^{\ell}\rho|^{2}+|r\partial_{r}r^{\ell}\rho|^{2})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}

\displaystyle\cong_{\ell}\kappa\int_{0}^{\infty}|r^{\ell+1}\rho|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}+\int_{0}^{\infty}(|r^{\ell}\rho|^{2}+|r^{\ell}(r\partial_{r})\rho|^{2})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}.

We define
{\mathcal{C}}\colon{\mathcal{K}}\times{\mathcal{K}}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}(\rho,\psi)

\displaystyle:=c_{2}\kappa\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2\ell+2}\rho\,\overline{\psi}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}+(c_{0}-c_{1}\ell^{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2\ell}\rho\,\overline{\psi}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-2\ell c_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\ell}\rho\,(r\partial_{r}r^{\ell}\overline{\psi})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}+c_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(r\partial_{r}r^{\ell}\rho)\,(r\partial_{r}r^{\ell}\overline{\psi})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}.

Clearly
{\mathcal{C}}(\rho,\psi)\lesssim\|\rho\|_{\mathcal{K}}\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{K}}
, and by Young’s inequality we obtain for all
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}(\rho,\rho)

\displaystyle:=c_{2}\kappa\int_{0}^{\infty}|r^{\ell+1}\rho|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}+(c_{0}-(1+\varepsilon^{-2})c_{1}\ell^{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}|r^{\ell}\rho|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}+c_{1}(1-\varepsilon^{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}|r\partial_{r}r^{\ell}\rho|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}.

Choosing
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
sufficiently close to
1
such that
c_{0}-(1+\varepsilon^{-2})c_{1}\ell^{2}>0
(recall that
2c_{1}\ell^{2}<c_{0}
by assumption), we may employ the Lax-Milgram theorem and obtain a unique
\rho\in{\mathcal{K}}
such that
{\mathcal{C}}(v,\psi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2\ell+2}\eta\overline{\psi}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}r}{r}
for all
\psi\in{\mathcal{K}}
, in particular for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
Integrating by parts, we thus learn that

\displaystyle\big(c_{2}\kappa+r^{-2}(c_{0}-\ell^{2}-2\ell c_{1}(r\partial_{r})-c_{1}(r\partial_{r})^{2})\big)r^{\ell}\rho=r^{\ell}\eta

in the sense of distributions, that is
\big(c_{2}\kappa+r^{-2}(c_{0}-c_{1}(r\partial_{r})^{2})\big)\rho=\eta
.
Observe that quantitatively, only the information
\int_{0}^{\infty}|r^{\ell+1}\eta|^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}r}{r}<\infty
was used.
Hence, since
\rho_{j}:=(r\partial_{r})^{j}\rho
solves

\displaystyle\big(c_{2}\kappa+r^{-2}(c_{0}-c_{1}(r\partial_{r})^{2})\big)\rho_{j}=(r\partial_{r})^{j}\eta+c_{1}\sum_{m=0}^{j-1}\binom{j}{m}2^{j-m}(r\partial_{r})^{m}\rho

for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, the same argument yields iteratively the estimate for the higher derivatives.
∎

3.2
Unweighted Variational Solutions with Higher Regularity

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi)
and
\mu\in\Sigma_{\pi-\varepsilon}
.
For
\kappa:=|\mu|>0
consider the space

\displaystyle H\ =\ \overline{C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\})}^{\|\cdot\|_{H}},

(3.6)

where the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}
is given by

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H}^{2}\

\displaystyle=\ \kappa\int_{\Omega}\Big(|u|^{2}+\kappa|ru|^{2}+|r\nabla u|^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla r\nabla u|^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(|u|^{2}+\kappa|ru|^{2}+|r\partial_{r}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\Big).

Here we write
\int_{\partial\Omega}f\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r,0)+f(r,\theta)\,\mathrm{d}r
.
We note that the space
H
does not depend on
\kappa>0
.
Note that all terms in the norm have the same scaling if we use parabolic scaling in the sense that
\kappa
scales like the square of the inverse length.

Definition 3.3

(Sesquilinear form)
.

For
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

B:H\times C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\})\to\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle B(u,v)\

\displaystyle:=\ \int_{\Omega}(\mu u-\Delta u)(c_{0}-c_{1}(r\partial_{r})^{2}+c_{2}|\mu|r^{2}-\partial_{\varphi}^{2})\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int_{\partial\Omega}(\gamma u+\partial_{\nu}u)(c_{0}-c_{1}(r\partial_{r})^{2}+c_{2}|\mu|r^{2})\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Since
\mu u-\Delta u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\})
and
\gamma u+\partial_{\nu}u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\partial\Omega\setminus\{0\})
for
u\in H
, the sesquilinear form is well–defined.
Using integration by parts we can show that the sesquilinear form has a unique continuous extension which is coercive on
H\times H
.

Lemma 3.4

(Continuity and Coercivity)
.

Let
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
B
be as in Definition
3.3
.

(i)

There is a unique continuous extension
B:H\times H\to\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

For
u,v\in H
with
v{{}_{|\partial\Omega}}=0
it holds
B(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}(\mu u-\Delta u)(c_{0}-c_{1}(r\partial_{r})^{2}+c_{2}|\mu|r^{2}-\partial_{\varphi}^{2})\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}x
.

(iii)

For
c_{0}\gg c_{1}\gg c_{2}\gg 1
, we have
|B(u,u)|\ \gtrsim_{\varepsilon}\ \|u\|_{H}^{2}
for all
u\in H
.

Proof.

The proof rests on the identity

\displaystyle\begin{split}B(u,v)\ =\ \tilde{B}(u,v)&+c_{1}\Big(2\int_{\Omega}\mu ur\partial_{r}\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}ur\partial_{r}\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)\\
&+2c_{2}|\mu|\int_{\Omega}r\partial_{r}u\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}(\mu u-\partial_{r}^{2}u)\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(3.7)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{B}(u,v)\

\displaystyle=\ c_{0}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\mu u\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}u\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\ c_{1}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\mu(r\partial_{r}u)(r\partial_{r}\overline{v})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla r\partial_{r}u\cdot\nabla r\partial_{r}\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}(r\partial_{r}u)(r\partial_{r}\overline{v})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\ c_{2}|\mu|\Big(\int_{\Omega}\mu r^{2}u\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(r\nabla u)\cdot(r\nabla\overline{v})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}r^{2}u\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\ \int_{\Omega}r^{-2}\partial_{\varphi}^{2}u\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\overline{v}\,\mathrm{d}x,

which we will establish for
u,v\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\})
below.

Assuming that (
3.7
) holds, assertions (
i
) and (
ii
) follow immediately by density of
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\})
in
H
.
Furthermore, we note that
\tilde{B}(u,u)
has the form
\mu a^{2}+b^{2}
with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
(where
a,b
depend on
|\mu|
).
We thus can use Lemma
C.2
to estimate
|\mu a^{2}+b^{2}|

\gtrsim_{\varepsilon}

|\mu|a^{2}+b^{2}
and get by an application of Young’s inequality

\displaystyle|B(u,u)|\

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{\varepsilon}\ c_{0}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\mu||u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+c_{1}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\mu||r\partial_{r}u|^{2}-2|\mu||u|^{2}+|\nabla r\partial_{r}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}|r\partial_{r}u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)

By Plancherel’s identity for the Laplace transform (Lemma
A.4
(
iii
)) we obtain

\displaystyle\|U\|_{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\|U\|_{H_{\beta,0}^{\frac{j}{2}}(H^{2-j}_{\alpha}(\Omega))}+\|U\|_{H_{\beta,0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(H^{0}_{\alpha}(\partial^{\prime}\Omega))}\lesssim\ \sum_{j=0}^{2}\||\cdot|^{\frac{j}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(S_{\beta},H^{2-j}_{\alpha}(\Omega))}+\||\cdot|^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(S_{\beta},H^{0}_{\alpha}(\partial^{\prime}\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\theta}\|f\|_{{L^{2}(S_{\beta},H^{0}_{\alpha})}}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(S_{\beta},H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\alpha}(\partial^{\prime}\Omega))}+\||\cdot|^{\frac{1}{4}}g\|_{L^{2}(S_{\beta},H^{0}_{\alpha}(\partial^{\prime}\Omega))}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\||\cdot|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f\|_{{L^{2}(S_{\beta},L^{2}(\Omega))}}+\||\cdot|^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}g\|_{L^{2}(S_{\beta},L^{2}(\partial\Omega))}\ \lesssim\ \|F\|_{\mathbb{F}}+\|G\|_{\mathbb{G}}.

This proves the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The proof is analogous as the one before,
but we replace the application of Theorem
4.1
by the application of Theorem
4.6
, so that we have for
u:=\Phi[f,g]
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\ell+2}|\mu|^{\frac{j}{2}}\|u\|_{\ell-j+2,\alpha}\lesssim_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\theta,\ell}\ \sum_{j=0}^{\ell}|\mu|^{\frac{j}{2}}\Big(\|f\|_{\ell-j,\alpha}+|g|_{\ell-j+\frac{1}{2},\alpha}\Big)+|\mu|^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\big(|\mu|^{\frac{1}{4}}|g|_{0,\alpha}+|\mu|^{-\frac{1}{4}}|g|_{1,\alpha}\big).

We define

.
As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
,

satisfies the equation, and we have

\displaystyle\|U\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell+2}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell+2}\|U\|_{H_{\beta,0}^{\frac{j}{2}}(H^{\ell-j+2}_{\alpha}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\ell+2}\||\cdot|^{\frac{j}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(S_{\beta},H^{\ell-j+2}_{\alpha}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\theta,\ell}\ \sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\Big(\||\cdot|^{\frac{j}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(S_{\beta},H^{\ell-j}_{\alpha}(\Omega))}+\||\cdot|^{\frac{j}{2}}g\|_{L^{2}(S_{\beta},H^{\ell-j+\frac{1}{2}}_{\alpha})}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt+\||\cdot|^{\frac{1}{4}}g\|_{L^{2}(S_{\beta},H^{0}_{\alpha})}+\||\cdot|^{-\frac{1}{4}}g\|_{L^{2}(S_{\beta},H^{1}_{\alpha})}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\Big(\|F\|_{H_{\beta,0}^{\frac{j}{2}}(H^{\ell-2}_{\alpha}(\Omega))}+\|G\|_{H_{\beta,0}^{\frac{j}{2}}(H^{\ell-j+\frac{1}{2}}_{\alpha}({\partial^{\prime}\Omega}))}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt+\|G\|_{H_{\beta,0}^{\frac{1}{2}(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}(H^{0}_{\alpha}(\partial^{\prime}\Omega))}+\|G\|_{H_{\beta,0}^{\frac{1}{2}(\ell-\frac{1}{2})}(H^{1}_{\alpha}(\partial^{\prime}\Omega))}

\displaystyle=\|F\|_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}+\|G\|_{\mathbb{G}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}},

which is the desired bound.
∎

Appendix A
Integral Transforms

In this section we provide known properties of the Mellin transform and Laplace transform, which constitute an important tool in our analysis.
It will be convenient to have these transforms defined for Hilbert space valued functions.
We recall that given
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
S_{\beta}
for the line
\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\ \colon\ \mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda=\beta\right\}
, and more generally
S_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}
for the strip
\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\ \colon\ \mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda\in(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\right\}
if
\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}
.
We recall Bochner spaces of vector-valued integrable functions, and the concept of vector-valued analytic functions, see e.g.
[
1
, Chapter 1.1 and Appendix A]
.

Definition A.1

(Mellin transform)
.

Let
\mathsf{H}
be a Hilbert space.

(i)

For
\psi\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathsf{H})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
we define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}\psi(\lambda)\ :=\ \widehat{\psi}(\lambda)\ :=\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\lambda}\psi(r)\ \frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}.

Whenever this integral converges, we call it the Mellin transform of
\psi
at
\lambda
.

(ii)

For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varphi\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(S_{\beta},\mathsf{H})
and
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}^{-1}\varphi(r)\ :=\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda=\beta}r^{\lambda}\varphi(\lambda)\ \mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda.

(A.1)

Whenever this integral converges, we call it the inverse Mellin transform of
\varphi
at
r
(along
S_{\beta}
).

For general functions
\psi\in L_{\textrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathsf{H})
, the Mellin transform
might fail to converge for certain
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. However, if it is well defined
for some
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda_{1}=\beta_{1}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda_{2}=\beta_{2}
,
then convergence is also guaranteed on the so called strip of convergence

S_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}\subset\mathbb{C}
. For functions on the wedge
\Omega
we apply the
Mellin transform in the radial direction and consider the angular variable as a
parameter,
i.e.
, for
\Phi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\backslash\{0\},\mathsf{H})
we
write

\displaystyle\widehat{}\Phi(\lambda,\varphi)\ :=\ {\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\lambda}\Phi(r,\varphi)\ \frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for $(\lambda,\varphi)\in\mathbb{C}\times[0,\theta]$.}

The Mellin transform has several
useful properties which are listed below.

Lemma A.2

(Properties of Mellin transform)
.

For
\psi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathsf{H})
the Mellin transform
\widehat{\psi}
is an entire function.
Furthermore, we have

(i)

\widehat{r^{\beta}\psi}(\lambda)\ =\ \widehat{\psi}\left(\lambda-\beta\right)
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

\widehat{r\partial_{r}\psi}(\lambda)\ =\ \lambda\widehat{\psi}\left(\lambda\right)
.

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2\beta}\overline{\psi_{1}(r)}\psi_{2}(r)\ \frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\ =\ \int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda=\beta}\overline{\widehat{\psi}_{1}(\lambda+\gamma)}\widehat{\psi}_{2}(\lambda-\gamma)\ \mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda
for all
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathsf{H})
and
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

In particular, for
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
the Mellin transform can be continuously extended to a linear
operator

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}:\big\{\psi\ :\ r^{-\beta-\frac{1}{2}}\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathsf{H})\big\}\ \to\ L^{2}(S_{\beta},\mathsf{H}).

(A.2)

(iv)

For every
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
the map (
A.2
) is an isometric isomorphism.
Whenever the integral in (
A.1
) converges, it yields the inverse of the map (
A.2
).

(v)

If
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}
, and
r^{\beta_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}\psi,r^{\beta_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathsf{H})
, then
\widehat{\psi}
is analytic on the strip
S_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}
.

Proof.

We refer to e.g.
[
16
, Chapter 6]
in the scalar case.
The arguments carry over verbatim to the Hilbert space case.
Parseval’s identity in the generalized form can be found e.g. in
[
27
, Theorem 73]
.
∎

For time–dependent functions we use the Laplace transform.
Since all functions have an extension to negative times by zero, it will be possible to use the two-sided Laplace transform.
In our convention, it is given as follows:

Definition A.3

(Laplace transform)
.

Let
\mathsf{H}
be a Hilbert space.

(i)

For
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{H})
and
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}f(\mu)\ :=\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\mu t}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Whenever this integral converges, we call it the Laplace transform of
f
at
\mu
.

(ii)

For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
g\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(S_{\beta},\mathsf{H})
and
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\beta}^{-1}g(t)\ :=\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\mu=\beta}e^{\mu t}g(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\mu.

(A.3)

Whenever this integral converges, we call it the inverse Laplace transform of
g
at
t
(along
S_{\beta}
).

We note that the Mellin transform is given by the composition of the
Laplace transform and the change of variables
t=\ln r
. In particular, we get
the corresponding properties as for the Mellin transform also for the Laplace
transform if we replace the factor
r^{\beta}
by
e^{\beta t}
and
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}r}{r}
by
\,\mathrm{d}t
.

Lemma A.4

(Properties of Laplace transform)
.

Let
\mathsf{H}
be a Hilbert space.
For
\psi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{H})
the Laplace transform
\widehat{\psi}
is an entire function.
Furthermore, we have

(i)

\mathcal{L}(e^{\beta t}\psi)(\mu)\ =\ \mathcal{L}\psi\left(\mu-\beta\right)
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{L}{\partial_{t}\psi}(\mu)\ =\ \mu\mathcal{L}\psi\left(\mu\right)
.

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\beta t}\overline{\psi_{1}(t)}\psi_{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\mu=\beta}\overline{\mathcal{L}\psi_{1}(\mu+\gamma)}\mathcal{L}\psi_{2}(\mu-\gamma)\,\mathrm{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\mu
for all
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{H})
and
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

In particular, for
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
the Laplace transform can be continuously extended to a linear
operator

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta}:\big\{\psi\ :\ e^{-\beta t}\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{H})\big\}\ \to\ L^{2}(S_{\beta},\mathsf{H}).

(A.4)

(iv)

For every
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
the map (
A.4
) is an isometric isomorphism.
Whenever the integral in (
A.3
) converges, it yields the inverse of the map (
A.4
).

(v)

If
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}
, and
e^{-\beta_{1}t}\psi,e^{-\beta_{2}t}\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{H})
, then
\mathcal{L}\psi
is analytic on the strip
S_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}
.

Appendix B
Neumann Problem on the Wedge

In this section, we consider the elliptic boundary problem with Neumann conditions, i.e.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta v\ &=\ f&&\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}v\ &=\ g&&\qquad\text{on}\ \partial^{\prime}\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(B.1)

Observe that the direction of the outer normal vector yields
\partial_{\nu}=-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\varphi}
on
\partial_{0}\Omega
and
\partial_{\nu}=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\varphi}
on
\partial_{1}\Omega
.
We note that elliptic problems of the form (
B.1
) have been studied in the literature, see e.g.
[
2
,
16
,
18
]
.
Since we treat higher regularity beyond the regime of the first resonance, we include details here for the convenience of the reader.
We first give a solution of the corresponding system to (
B.1
) in Mellin variables.

Lemma B.1

(Neumann problem in Mellin variables)
.

Let
\theta\in(0,2\pi)
,
\mathfrak{f}\in C^{\infty}([0,\theta])
, and

\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with

\lambda\notin\frac{\pi}{\theta}\mathbb{Z}
, there is a unique classical solution

\mathfrak{v}(\lambda,\cdot)
to the boundary-value problem

\displaystyle(\lambda^{2}+\partial_{\varphi}^{2})\mathfrak{v}(\lambda,\varphi)\

\displaystyle=\ \mathfrak{f}(\varphi)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for }\varphi\in(0,\theta),

(B.2a)

\displaystyle-\partial_{\varphi}\mathfrak{v}(\lambda,0)\

\displaystyle=\ \mathfrak{g}_{1},

(B.2b)

\displaystyle\partial_{\varphi}\mathfrak{v}(\lambda,\theta)\

\displaystyle=\ \mathfrak{g}_{2}.

(B.2c)

The function
\mathfrak{v}(\cdot,\varphi):\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
is meromorphic for each

\varphi\in[0,\theta]
with all poles contained in
\frac{\pi}{\theta}\mathbb{Z}
.
The pole at
\lambda=0
is at most of order
2
and all other poles are simple.
The solution
\mathfrak{v}
can be represented as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{v}(\lambda,\varphi)\ =\ -G(\lambda,\varphi,0)\mathfrak{g}_{1}+G(\lambda,\varphi,\theta)\mathfrak{g}_{2}+\int_{0}^{\theta}G(\lambda,\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})\mathfrak{f}(\varphi^{\prime})\ \mathrm{d}\varphi^{\prime},

(B.3a)

where the meromorphic Green’s function
G(\cdot,\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
to (
B.2
)
is given by

\displaystyle G(\lambda,\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\lambda\sin(\lambda\theta)}\left\{\begin{array}[]{LL}\cos(\lambda(\theta-\varphi^{\prime}))\cos(\lambda\varphi)&\text{for $\varphi\in[0,{\varphi^{\prime}}]$},\\
\cos(\lambda\varphi^{\prime})\cos(\lambda(\theta-\varphi))&\text{for $\varphi\in({\varphi^{\prime}},\theta]$}.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, if
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}>0
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then whenever
\theta\left\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda\right\rvert\leq\alpha_{0}
and
\dist(\theta\lambda,\pi\mathbb{Z})\geq\alpha_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-25.83325pt\sum_{j+m=\ell+2}\int_{0}^{\theta}|\lambda|^{2j}\left\lvert\partial_{\varphi}^{m}\mathfrak{v}(\lambda,\varphi)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi\lesssim_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ell}\ \sum_{j+m=\ell}\int_{0}^{\theta}|\lambda|^{2j}|\partial_{\varphi}^{m}\mathfrak{f}(\varphi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi+|\lambda|^{2\ell+1}(|\mathfrak{g}_{1}|^{2}+|\mathfrak{g}_{2}|^{2}).

(B.4)

Proof.

The claim about the pole set follows directly from the formula for
G
.
For
\lambda\theta\notin\pi\mathbb{Z}
it follows from standard ODE arguments that
\mathfrak{v}(\lambda,\cdot)
is the unique solution to (
B.2
).
If
\mathfrak{f}=0
, (
B.4
) follows from the representation
of
\mathfrak{v}
and Lemma
C.1
. In the following, we hence assume

\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}=0
. For
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}=0
, we test (
B.2a
) with

\overline{\mathfrak{v}}
to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\mathfrak{f}\ \mathrm{d}\varphi\

\displaystyle=\ \int_{0}^{\theta}\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\mathfrak{v}\ \mathrm{d}\varphi+\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\theta}\left\lvert\mathfrak{v}\right\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\varphi\ =\ -\int_{0}^{\theta}\left\lvert\partial_{\varphi}\mathfrak{v}\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi+\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\theta}\left\lvert\mathfrak{v}\right\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\varphi.

We take the real part and absorb the term on the left hand side using Young’s
inequality. If
\lambda
has a large imaginary part

\theta\left\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda\right\rvert\geq 2\alpha_{0}
, we have
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\lambda^{2})\sim-\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert^{2}
,
which implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}\left(\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\mathfrak{v}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert\partial_{\varphi}\mathfrak{v}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\varphi\ \lesssim\ \left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert^{-2}\int_{0}^{\theta}\left\lvert\mathfrak{f}\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi.

(B.5)

If
\theta\left\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda\right\rvert\leq 2\alpha_{0}
(and hence
\theta\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert\leq 3\alpha_{0}\lesssim 1
), we have
\left\lvert\lambda G(\lambda,\cdot,\varphi^{\prime})\right\rvert
,
\left\lvert\partial_{\varphi}G(\lambda,\cdot,\varphi^{\prime})\right\rvert\lesssim 1
since the sine and cosine are bounded on
B_{(\theta-\varphi^{\prime})\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert},B_{\varphi^{\prime}\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}\subset B_{3\alpha_{0}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and since by the assumptions on
\lambda

\displaystyle|\sin(\theta\lambda)|^{2}\ =\ \sin^{2}(\theta\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda)+\sinh^{2}(\theta\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda)\geq\sin^{2}(\theta\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda)\gtrsim_{\alpha_{1}}\ 1.

With Jensen’s inequality we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}\left(\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\mathfrak{v}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert\partial_{\varphi}\mathfrak{v}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\varphi\ \hskip-3.44444pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem-v-repr-3}}}{{\lesssim}}\ \theta\Big(\int_{0}^{\theta}\left\lvert\mathfrak{f}\right\rvert\mathrm{d}\varphi\Big)^{2}\ \leq\ \theta^{2}\int_{0}^{\theta}\left\lvert\mathfrak{f}\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi\lesssim\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert^{-2}\int_{0}^{\theta}\left\lvert\mathfrak{f}\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi,

(B.6)

where we have used
\theta\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert\lesssim 1
in the last step.
By multiplying (
B.5
) and (
B.6
) with
\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert^{2}
and using equation (
B.2a
) once more, we obtain the corresponding bound on
\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\mathfrak{v}
, thus proving (
B.4
) for
\ell=0
.

Now assume that the assertion holds for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Multiplying (
B.2
) by
\lambda
, we obtain from (
B.4
)

\displaystyle\sum_{j+m=\ell+2}\int_{0}^{\theta}\left\lvert\lambda^{j+1}\partial_{\varphi}^{m}\mathfrak{v}(\lambda,\varphi)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi\ \lesssim\ \sum_{j+m=\ell}\int_{0}^{\theta}|\lambda^{j+1}\partial_{\varphi}^{m}\mathfrak{f}(\varphi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi+|\lambda^{\ell+1+\frac{1}{2}}\mathfrak{g}_{1}|^{2}+|\lambda^{\ell+1+\frac{1}{2}}\mathfrak{g}_{2}|^{2}.

Using

∎

\displaystyle\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\Omega}_{\ell+2,\beta}}\lesssim_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ell,\theta}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\big([\hskip-1.29167pt[f]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{\ell_{j},\beta_{j}}+\left[g\right]_{\ell_{j}+\frac{1}{2},\beta_{j}}\big).

Proof.

(i):
For fixed

(B.8)

) with data

,

.
Observe that for all

,

(B.9)

Indeed, for all

and

\widehat{(rg)}(\cdot,0)

as claimed.
By Plancherel’s theorem in form of Lemma

(

on

.

is smooth.
Moreover,
by (
A.1
) in Lemma
A.2
it holds
\widehat{v}(\lambda,\varphi)=\mathfrak{v}(\lambda,\varphi)
for all
\varphi\in[0,\theta]
and all
\lambda\in S_{\ell+\alpha+1}
.

Next we verify that

does indeed solve (

).
Note that by Lemma

, we have for all
\lambda\in S_{\ell+\alpha+1}

\displaystyle\left(\lambda^{2}+\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\right)\widehat{v}(\lambda,\varphi)\ =\ \widehat{(r^{2}f)}(\lambda,\varphi)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi\in(0,\theta),

as well as

and

Since the Mellin transform induces an isomorphism between

and

, cf. Lemma

(

), we conclude that
v
solves problem (

) by Lemma
A.2
(

).
This proves that

is a classical solution to (
B.1
).

For
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda={\mathfrak{s}}_{\ell+\alpha+2}^{\Omega}={\mathfrak{s}}_{\ell+\alpha+\frac{3}{2}}=\ell+\alpha+1
, the
assumptions on
\lambda
in Lemma

are fulfilled, so that from

) and the Mellin representation of the norms we get for all

with

and

, and for all

that

\displaystyle[\hskip-1.29167pt[v]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{\ell-j+2,\alpha+j}\

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ell}\ [\hskip-1.29167pt[r^{2}f]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{\ell-j,\alpha+j+2}+\left[rg\right]_{\ell-j+\frac{1}{2},\alpha+j+1}<\infty.

(B.10)

In particular

by Lemma
2.2
.

(ii):
By classical methods two classical solutions

and

with at most polynomial growth differ only by elements in

,

,

]
. Suppose first that

. Since

[\hskip-1.29167pt[v]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{\ell_{1}+2,\beta_{1}}=\infty
for all

and

[\hskip-1.29167pt[v]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{\ell_{1}+2,\beta_{1}}=0

(the set

Suppose now

By the result for

We can hence assume that

Without loss of generality we assume

Since

,

\displaystyle v_{1}(r,\varphi)-v_{2}(r,\varphi)=\sum_{\pi_{k}\in(\frac{\pi}{\theta}\mathbb{Z})\cap(\sigma_{2},\sigma_{1})}\text{Res}_{\psi}(\pi_{k}).

(B.11)

Since all poles at

\displaystyle\cos(\pi_{k}(\theta-\varphi))=(-1)^{k}\cos(\pi_{k}\varphi),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{Res}_{1/\sin(\lambda\theta)}(\pi_{k})=\frac{1}{\theta\cos(\pi_{k}\theta)}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\theta}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.61108pt\text{Res}_{\psi}(\pi_{k})=r^{\pi_{k}}\text{Res}_{\mathfrak{v}(\cdot,\varphi)}(\pi_{k})=\frac{r^{\pi_{k}}\cos(\pi_{k}\varphi)}{\pi_{k}\theta}\Big[-\mathfrak{g}_{1}(\pi_{k})+(-1)^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{2}(\pi_{k})+\int_{0}^{\theta}\cos(\pi_{k}\varphi^{\prime})\mathfrak{f}(\pi_{k},\varphi^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\varphi^{\prime}\Big].

if

is holomorphic in

, we have

\displaystyle\text{Res}_{\psi}(0)\

\displaystyle=\ (\lambda^{2}G(\lambda,\varphi,0)\partial_{\lambda})|_{\lambda=0}(-r^{\lambda}\mathfrak{g}_{1}(\lambda))+(\lambda^{2}G(\lambda,\varphi,\theta)\partial_{\lambda})|_{\lambda=0}(r^{\lambda}\mathfrak{g}_{2}(\lambda))

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int_{0}^{\theta}(\lambda^{2}G(\lambda,\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})\partial_{\lambda})|_{\lambda=0}(r^{\lambda}\mathfrak{f}(\lambda,\varphi^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\varphi^{\prime})

Thus (

) gives

Lemma B.4
.

Let
\beta_{1}<\beta<\beta_{2}
. Then there
is
C_{\beta}<\infty
, such that for any
v\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\|c\|_{L^{2}({(0,\theta)})}\

\displaystyle\lesssim\ C_{\beta}[\hskip-1.29167pt[v]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{0,\beta_{1}}^{\frac{\beta_{2}-\beta}{\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}}}[\hskip-1.29167pt[v-cr^{\beta}]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{0,\beta_{2}}^{\frac{\beta-\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}}},

(B.12)

as long as both factors on the right hand side are finite.

Proof.

We may assume
[\hskip-1.29167pt[v]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{0,\beta_{1}},[\hskip-1.29167pt[v-cr^{\beta}]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{0,\beta_{2}}\in(0,\infty)
.

and

\displaystyle\|c\|_{L^{2}({(0,\theta)})}^{2}\

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\ R^{-2\beta}\int_{\Omega_{R}}|cr^{\beta}|^{2}\ \frac{dr}{r}d\varphi\ \lesssim_{\beta}\ R^{-2\beta}\int_{\Omega_{R}}|v|^{2}\ \frac{dr}{r}d\varphi+C_{\beta}R^{-2\beta}\int_{\Omega_{R}}|v-cr^{\beta}|^{2}\ \frac{dr}{r}d\varphi

(B.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\ R^{2(\beta_{1}-\beta)}[\hskip-1.29167pt[v]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{0,\beta_{1}}^{2}+C_{\beta}R^{2(\beta_{2}-\beta)}[\hskip-1.29167pt[v-cr^{\beta}]\hskip-1.29167pt]_{0,\beta_{2}}^{2}.

Appendix C
Auxiliary Estimates

Lemma C.1

(Auxiliary estimate)
.

Let
\alpha_{0}>0
,
\alpha_{1}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Then there is
c>0
such that for all
\theta>0
, for all
f,g\in\{\sin,\cos\}
, and for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\theta\left\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda\right\rvert\leq\alpha_{0}
and

\displaystyle\dist(\theta|\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda|,g^{-1}(\{0\}))\geq\alpha_{1}

we have

\displaystyle 0<\sin(\alpha_{1})\leq\left\lvert g(\lambda\theta)\right\rvert\leq\cosh(\theta\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda)

(C.1)

and

\displaystyle|\lambda|\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{|f(\lambda\varphi)|^{2}}{|g(\lambda\theta)|^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}\varphi\ \leq\ \max\left\{\frac{2\alpha_{0}\cosh^{2}(\alpha_{0})}{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})},\frac{\alpha_{0}+\sinh(\alpha_{0})\cosh(\alpha_{0})}{\sinh^{2}{\alpha_{0}}}\right\}.

(C.2)

Proof.

By a straightforward calculation we have the elementary formula

\displaystyle\left\lvert f(z)\right\rvert^{2}=f(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}z)^{2}+\sinh^{2}(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for $f\in\{\sin,\cos\}$}.

(C.3)

By the symmetry properties of
\sin
and
\cos
and the condition

\alpha_{1}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
this gives both

\displaystyle|f(z)|^{2}\

\displaystyle\leq\ 1+\sinh^{2}(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z)=\cosh^{2}(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z),

\displaystyle|g(\lambda\theta)|^{2}\

\displaystyle\geq\ g(\theta\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda)^{2}\geq\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})>0,

which together proves (
C.1
).

For the proof of (
C.2
) we consider two cases: We first assume
that
\theta\left\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda\right\rvert\leq\alpha_{0}
holds. In particular

\left\lvert\lambda\varphi\right\rvert\leq\theta|\lambda|\leq 2\alpha_{0}
for
\varphi\in(0,\theta)
and
hence
\left\lvert f(\lambda\varphi)\right\rvert^{2}\leq\cosh^{2}(\varphi\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda)\leq\cosh^{2}(\alpha_{0})
by
(
C.1
) and the symmetry and monotonicity of
\cosh
. Using
(
C.1
) we get

\displaystyle|\lambda|\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{|f(\lambda\varphi)|^{2}}{|g(\lambda\theta)|^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}\varphi\ \leq\ \theta|\lambda|\frac{\cosh^{2}(\alpha_{0})}{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})}\ \leq\ \frac{2\alpha_{0}\cosh^{2}(\alpha_{0})}{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})}.

It remains to consider the case when

\theta\left\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda\right\rvert\geq\alpha_{0}\geq\theta\left\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda\right\rvert
. Then

|\lambda|\leq 2\left\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda\right\rvert
and by (
C.3
) we get

|g(\lambda\theta)|^{2}\geq\sinh^{2}(\theta\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda)
. Since

h(t):=(t+\sinh(t)\cosh(t))/\sinh^{2}(t)
is monotonically decreasing for

t>0
, we arrive at

|\lambda|\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{\left\lvert f(\lambda\varphi)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert g(\lambda\theta)\right\rvert^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}\varphi\leq\frac{|\lambda|}{\sinh^{2}(\theta\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda)}\int_{0}^{\theta}\cosh^{2}(\varphi\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda)\ \mathrm{d}\varphi=\frac{|\lambda|}{2\left\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda\right\rvert}h(\theta\left\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\lambda\right\rvert)\leq h(\alpha_{0}).

∎

For the proof of the coercivity estimate we note the following simple fact:

Lemma C.2
.

Let
\omega\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Then for all
z,w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
with

|\arg z-\arg w|\leq 2\omega
there holds

\displaystyle\frac{|z+w|}{|z|+|w|}\geq\cos\omega.

Proof.

After rotation we may assume that
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}z,\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}w\geq 0
with
\tfrac{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}z}{|z|}=\tfrac{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}w}{|w|}=\cos\psi
for some
\psi\in[0,\omega]
.
Therefore we have

\displaystyle(|z|+|w|)\cos\omega

\displaystyle\leq(|z|+|w|)\cos\psi=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}z+\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}w=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(z+w)\leq|z+w|.\qed

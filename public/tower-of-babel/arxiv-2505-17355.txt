Examining dark neutron decay using f-mode gravitational waves
Wasif Husain

Abstract
In this study, the impact of neutron decay into dark matter and various dark matter self-interaction strengths on neutron star properties have been explored. Using the quark-meson coupling (QMC) model for nucleon-only equations of state (EoSs), the effects of different matter compositions have been compared, including strange matter and self-interacting dark matter. The results demonstrate that increasing DM-DM self-repulsion stiffens the EoS, influencing the mass-radius relationship and stability of neutron stars. Furthermore, fundamental mode (f-mode) oscillations have been analyzed, which serve as a diagnostic tool for probing neutron star interiors. Both first- and second-order f-mode frequencies follow universal relations, reinforcing their applicability for constraining dense matter properties. It has been shown that neutron stars composed of nucleons-only and self-interacting dark matter exhibit a universal behavior in damping time and angular frequency, whereas strange matter and non-self-interacting dark matter deviate from this trend. These findings highlight the significance of gravitational wave detections in investigating neutron star structure, particularly with next-generation observatories such as the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer. This study provides a pathway for testing dark matter models through gravitational wave asteroseismology, bridging astrophysical observations and fundamental particle physics.

9
Conclusion

In conclusion, this study highlights the impact of different equations of state (EoSs) on neutron star properties, including mass, radius, damping time, and oscillation frequencies. The findings confirm that nucleon-only EoSs produce the stiffest models, while increasing dark matter (DM) self-repulsion strengthens the equation of state. Notably, neutron decay into dark matter aligns more closely with nucleon-only EoSs as self-interaction increases.

The study further reinforces the existence of universal relations in neutron star oscillations. Both the first- and second-order damping time equations demonstrate consistency across selected EoSs, with strange matter and non-self-interacting dark matter exhibiting deviations. Similarly, mass-scaled angular frequency correlates with compactness, suggesting that more compact neutron stars may emit stronger gravitational waves, making them promising candidates for detection.

Additionally, the f-mode frequency results indicate that all selected models follow a universal trend, remaining within the detection range of next-generation observatories such as the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer. This consistency supports the viability of probing neutron star interiors through gravitational wave observations, emphasizing the importance of understanding the role of dark matter interactions in dense astrophysical objects.

The results of this study enhance our understanding of neutron star behavior and provide valuable insights for future gravitational wave detections, opening avenues for further investigations into exotic matter compositions and their astrophysical implications.

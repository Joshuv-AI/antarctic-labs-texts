SGL-TX-MR—study protocol acute effects of SGLT2 inhibitor on kidney allograft oxygen tension, a randomized, double-blind, placebo controlled crossover trial
Authors: Lotte Borg Lange, Claus Bistrup
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract {1b}BackgroundThe acute effect of sodium-glucose co-transporter type 2 inhibitors, SGLT2i, has never been investigated in non-diabetic kidney transplant recipients.MethodsSGL-TX-MR will investigate the acute effects of SGLT2 inhibitor on kidney allograft oxygen tension in a randomized, double-blind, placebo controlled crossover trial. Eight non-diabetic-KTR, adults, more than 6 months post-transplant with a stable estimated glomerular filtration rate > 20 ml/min, recruited from Odense University Hospital, Kidney Transplant Outpatient Clinic, from 2025 to 2026, will be randomized to a double blind crossover intervention with a single dose 50 mg Jardiance, SGLT2i, or placebo in random order, separated by a 2-week wash-out period. Primary outcome: kidney transplant cortical and medullary oxygen tension, estimated by blood oxygen level dependent–magnetic resonance imaging-based renal T2* relaxation rate. Secondary endpoint: Renal cortical and medullary perfusion, renal artery blood flow, blood glucose, blood pressure, and heart rate.DiscussionWe will investigate if a single dose of SGLT2i improves renal cortical oxygenation within 3 and 6 h from admission in non-diabetic kidney transplant recipients. This finding will improve the understanding of the mechanisms behind the impressive renoprotective effects recently seen with SGLT2i in major clinical trials with non-transplant chronic kidney disease patients and provide new horizons for therapeutic treatments targeting hypoxia in renal allograft.Trial registration {4}ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06933355. Registered on April 18, 2025.

Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Kidney transplant is first choice treatment of end-stage renal disease (ESRD), if the patients are suitable for transplantation. More than 3000 patients in Denmark have a transplanted kidney [1], and annually around 250 new patients are transplanted. With a current allograft survival of average 15 years [2], most kidney transplant patients outlive the allograft kidney. Currently, around 500 [2] are on waiting list for a new donor kidney. The side effects for immunosuppressive medicine are serious and include among other post-transplant diabetes mellitus (PTDM) [3], increased risk of cancer [4], atherosclerosis, and calcineurin inhibitor (CNI)-induced nephropathy.

Thus, by investigating sodium-glucose co-transporter type 2 inhibitor (SGLT2i) effect on the kidney allograft, we may discover mechanisms that might prevent transplant nephropathy. If we can prolong the kidney allograft survival, this will benefit patient’s life quality and decrease the socio-economic burden for society.

Post-transplant diabetes mellitus

The incidence of PTDM is 10–20% during the first year post-transplant [3, 5]. PTDM has the same harmful health care consequences as type 2 diabetes, including cardiovascular diseases and nephropathy [6]. Prophylactic post-transplant insulin therapy has proven to decrease the risk of PTDM [7, 8], suggesting an association with high blood glucose, leading to the hypothesis that SGLT2i may contribute to lowering the incidence of PTDM.

Pooled analyses of two SGLT2i trials have demonstrated 33% decreased incidence of new-onset type 2 diabetes in non-transplanted, non-diabetic chronic kidney disease (CKD) patients [9]. A randomized study in kidney transplant recipient (KTR) with PTDM displayed improved glycemic control, and did not affect estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) after 24 weeks intervention [10].

Sodium-glucose co-transporter type 2 inhibitor

The kidney allograft is continuously filtering blood glucose; 80–90% of the filtered glucose load is reabsorbed in the proximal tubules by Sodium-Glucose co transporter type 2 (SGLT2) [11, 12]. Physiological effects of inhibition such as glycosuria, plasma volume reduction, natriuresis, lowering of blood pressure, ketogenesis, and increased lipid oxidation is described [13].

The potential mechanisms leading to renal protection includes lower intraglomerular pressure due to tubuloglomerular feedback effects, loop diuretic sparing effect–preservation of intravascular volume and reduced volume overload, lowering albuminuria, reducing possible direct toxic effects on renal tubules, increasing oxygenation of tubular cells based on a reduced demand for solute transport and higher hematocrit leading to increased oxygen delivery, decreased ambient proinflammatory/profibrotic pathways (experimental evidence only), improved metabolic parameters (lower HbA1c and weight), lowering blood pressure, diminishing endothelial dysfunction, reduce arterial stiffness, and the potential direct cardiovascular effects improved cardiac function with maintenance of renal perfusion [14].

The SGLT2i has with convincing data shown in type 2 diabetics to protect kidney function in the DECLARE-trial [15] and reducing cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in the DAPA-trial [16, 17], EMPA-REG-trial [18, 19], and CREDENCE-trial [20], and slow the progression of albuminuria in the CANVAS-trial [21].

The DAPA-CKD trial significantly described slower annual decline in kidney function in non-diabetic patients treated with SGLT2i [16], and confirm that the kidney protective effects of SGLT2 inhibition extend to the broader population of persons with chronic kidney disease but without type 2 diabetes, for whom angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEi) previously was the only pharmacologic treatment that with significantly evidence, had shown to prevent kidney failure [22, 23] and lowering U-ACR.

Decreased glucose and sodium reabsorption in the proximal tubules seem to be the primary protective mechanism for SGLT2i. The proximal tubules’ metabolic stress lowers and has downstream effects that appear to protect the kidney. However, no study has yet described renal physiological effects of SGLT2 inhibition in non-diabetic kidney transplant recipients.

The initial fall in glomerular filtration rate (GFR) following SGLT2i is reversible and demonstrated within the primary four weeks of therapy. Annually, decrease in GFR is lessened by 50% when treated with SGLT2i compared to non-SGLT2i therapy. After 1 year of SGLT2i therapy, the initial decline in GFR has been surpassed by fall in GFR in the placebo group; 3 years follow up showed that SGLT2i had significantly reduced risk of ESRD, compared to placebo, and this effect has been observed in non-diabetic patients [17, 24].

Calcineurin inhibitor

To avoid rejection of the kidney allograft, lifelong immunosuppressive medication is vital. Tacrolimus is preferred standard immunosuppressive. Unfortunately, one side effect is vasoconstriction of the afferent glomerular arteriole, leading to ischemia/hypoxia, compatible with the characteristic striped fibrosis along radial arterioles of the kidney cortex [25], causing declined intraglomerular pressure and reduced GFR referred to as CNI nephropathy. Short-term studies in rats have demonstrated preventive effect of SGLT2i in the evolution of CNI nephropathy [26, 27]. Further investigations on whether CNI in combination with SGLT2i in KTR may induce decrease in intraglomerular pressure, build upon the thesis that SGLT2i causes afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction and efferent arteriolar vasodilation [28] and thus further decline in GFR when used in combination with CNI, or if in fact the opposite occurs, SGLT2i will reduce hypoxia, as shown in diabetes type 1 patients [29], are needed.

Renal hypoxia

The SGLT2 is highly energy (oxygen) consuming and cortical hypoxia correlates with the rate of decline in kidney function in CKD and diabetic-CKD patients [30, 31]. In rats and humans with type 1 diabetes, SGLT2i increases renal oxygenation [29, 32] by lowering oxygen consumption due to decreased metabolic demand for sodium transport [11].

Hypoxia is strongly associated with developing chronic diabetic kidney disease [33, 34].

Investigating the SGLT2i’s effect on renal allograft might show difference in occurrence of cortical hypoxia.

Blood-oxygen-level-dependent magnetic resonance imaging

Blood oxygen level-dependent–magnetic resonance imaging (BOLD-MRI) is an imaging method that is sensitive to changes in intrarenal oxygenation using the paramagnetic properties of deoxyhemoglobin [35]. The acquired BOLD-MRI images is expressed by the transverse relaxation time (T2*) with units of s-1. Note that changes in renal T2* values depends on either changes in oxygen delivery (renal artery flow) or changes in intrarenal oxygen consumption. Renal artery flow is measured using a phase-contrast MRI sequence with image slices perpendicular to the renal vessels.

This method has not previously been used in KTR, but we expect it to be just as useful in the kidney transplant as in native kidneys to determine the transverse relaxation time, because changes in intrarenal oxygenation is the same within the transplanted kidney.

Objectives {7}

Hypothesis

We hypothesize a single doses SGLT2i in Kidney transplant recipients will improve kidney allograft cortex oxygenation by reducing renal metabolism by blocking sodium-glucose transport compared to placebo.

Primary objective

To test the acute effect of single dose SGLT2i on kidney allograft oxygen tension in cortex and medulla compared with placebo, by using BOLD-MRI.

Secondary objective

To test the acute effect of single dose SGLT2i on; renal cortical and medullary perfusion with arterial spin labelling, renal artery blood flow with phase contrast imaging, blood glucose with blood sample, systolic and diastolic blood pressure with blood pressure device and heart rate, all compared to placebo treatment.

Trial design {8}

Randomized, double blinded, placebo controlled, cross-over intervention study.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

The intervention will be SGLT2i compared with placebo.

SGLT2i is approved for treatment of non-diabetic kidney disease for adults (≥ 18 years) with proteinuria U-ACR 200–5000 mg/g, with beneficial cardioprotective and renoprotective effects shown in previous conducted long-term studies [18, 19].

SGLT2i is not yet recommended for KTR, due to lack of RCT in KTR supporting the renoprotective mechanism in the renal allograft.

A single dose of orally administrated SGLT2i (Jardiance tablet 50 mg) has been chosen to achieve optimal efficacy and to compare with previous studies investigating SGLT2i effect on renal oxygenation. Laursen et al. [31] used 50 mg SGLT2i to show better kidney cortex oxygenation in patients with type 1 diabetic; they had no adverse drug reactions.

The SmPC for Jardiance states under Sect. 4.9 Overdose: Symptoms: In controlled clinical studies single doses of up to 800 mg empagliflozin in healthy volunteers and multiple daily doses of up to 100 mg empagliflozin in patients with type 2 diabetes did not show any toxicity.

Side effects in single dose usage on 50 mg as we planned in this intervention study, has not been observed.

A single dose of 50 mg Jardiance is therefor considered safe and within the limits of recommendations in the SmPC.

Intervention description {11a}

The intervention will be SGLT2i compared with placebo.

The trial include a total of 8 non-diabetic kidney transplant recipients.

Four patients will randomly be assigned to start in the active arm, and treated with a single dose SGLT2i at visit 2 (V2) and a single dose placebo at visit 3 (V3).

Four patients will randomly be assigned to start in the non-active arm, each participant will be treated with a single dose placebo at V2 and a single dose SGLT2i at V3 (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Intervention description

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

We improve adherence to intervention protocols by observing the participants as they take the study medication and by maintaining an individual medication log for all study days.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

All medication that the patient normally takes is permitted during this trial.

It will not be allowed to participate in this study in case of hospitalization due to illness that needs treatment, in the time period of this clinical trial—in that case the patient can participate in the trial when discharged.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

Following the termination of the study, there are no predefined post-trial care. Participants will resume their routine appointments with their attending physician at the kidney transplant outpatient clinic as usual.

In accordance with the relevant national regulations, subjects enrolled in the study are under The Danish Patient Insurance that covers all study subjects as guaranteed by The Danish Act on the Right to Complain and Receive Compensation within the Health Service administered by The Patient Assurance Association.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

The allocation sequence consists of computer-generated random numbers, prepared by an independent data manager from OPEN.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

The computer-generated allocation sequence is stored on a secure, access-logged drive, ensuring that the list remains concealed and blinded from the investigator until the trial is completed. The allocation sequence is integrated into the REDCap database, allowing patients to be randomized directly within the system.

The investigational medicinal product (IMP) will be manufactured, packaged, labeled, and stored in accordance with good manufacturing practice (GMP) regulations by the hospital Pharmacy FYN. Pharmacy FYN manufactures the investigational medicinal product in accordance with the predefined allocation sequence. Each study participant will be randomized to receive either SGLT2i or placebo on study visit V2. Following a 2-week washout period, participants will cross over to the alternate treatment arm and receive the opposite intervention on study visit V3.This ensures that allocation remains concealed and blinded from both the participant and the investigator.

Implementation {16c}

The allocation sequence will be generated by an independent data manager at OPEN.

The principal investigator, a specialist in nephrology, will be responsible for enrolling participants and conducting the randomization. Participants will be assigned to interventions in a double-blind manner.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

The investigator will remain blinded to the allocation sequence until completion of the final participant’s last study visit. Once all data have been collected, the dataset will be locked to prevent further modification.

For the purpose of statistical analysis, the dataset will remain blinded, with treatment groups labelled as “Group A” and “Group B.” The principal investigator and statistician will perform the predefined analyses without knowledge of the actual allocation. Unblinding will only occur after the primary analysis has been completed and verified, at which point the treatment identities corresponding to “A” and “B” will be revealed.

All other study personnel, including laboratory technicians, outcome assessors, study nurses, and participants, will remain blinded throughout the study and data analysis period.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

In the event that unblinding of a study participant becomes necessary due to a medical emergency, the investigator holds sole responsibility for determining whether emergency unblinding is warranted and will carry out the procedure if deemed appropriate. If the investigator is unavailable, a designated individual at the Department of Nephrology, Odense University Hospital (OUH), who is not otherwise involved in the study, will perform the unblinding. Alternatively, the sponsor may conduct the unblinding procedure.

In the event of a breach of the randomization code or unblinding, the affected participant will be withdrawn from the study, and the Good Clinical Practice (GCP) unit (monitor) will be notified immediately.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

Baseline characteristics of participants will be extracted from the electronic patient record (EPJ), including medical notes, laboratory data, and the Shared Medication Record (FMK). Collected baseline variables will include demographic information, current medication (including immunosuppressive agents, antihypertensives, diuretics, lipid-lowering drugs, and anticoagulants), weight, height, body mass index (BMI), blood pressure (BP), routine blood biochemistry, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (U-ACR). If existing blood or urine biochemistry data are older than two months at the time of inclusion, new samples will be collected and used as baseline values.

Blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) will be performed at the Department of Radiology, OUH. Data will be acquired using a multi-element cardiac receive coil. Participants will undergo BOLD-MRI T2*-weighted sequence to assess the transplant kidney renal tissue oxygenation in cortex and medulla (T2*), renal cortical and medullary perfusion (ml/100 g/min), and renal artery blood flow (ml/min). Measurements will be conducted at baseline and approximately 3 and 6 h post intervention.

Blood samples will be collected at study visits 2 and 3, both prior to the intervention and following completion of the final MRI scan. A total of 10.7 ml of blood will be drawn per sampling time point. The Department of Clinical Biochemistry will perform analyses including: plasma creatinine, from which kidney function will be estimated using the CKD-EPI equation, plasma urea, plasma glucose, glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), plasma potassium, plasma sodium, plasma albumin, hemoglobin, leukocyte count, platelet count, plasma alanine aminotransferase (ALAT), plasma bilirubin, plasma international normalized ratio [29], and a lipid panel (total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, and triglycerides).

If the participant consents to donate blood for future unspecified research, an additional 11 ml of blood will be collected once in the morning of visit 2 for biobanking purposes.

Urine samples will be collected at study visits 2 and 3, both prior to the intervention and following the final MRI scan. A 3 ml aliquot of urine will be sent to the Department of Clinical Biochemistry at Odense University Hospital (OUH) for analysis of the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (U-ACR). In addition, a urine dipstick test will be performed by the principal investigator to assess for the presence of glucose.

If the participant provides consent for biobanking, an additional 100 ml urine sample will be collected once, in the morning of visit 2, for future unspecified research purposes.

Drug accountability will be conducted on both intervention days.

Adverse events will be recorded from the first intervention day until the conclusion of the study, which includes a follow-up telephone call 4 days after the final intervention day.

Protocol deviations will be documented throughout the study period if they occur.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

This study is conducted according to a per-protocol design. Participants who do not complete the study protocol in full will be withdrawn from the study and replaced by new participants. Missing data will not be accepted in this trial; therefore, only participants with complete data sets will be included in the final analysis.

Participant adherence to the intervention days will be ensured in part by the presence of the principal investigator, who will accompany the participant throughout the day and perform all data collection procedures, including urine and blood sampling.

Confidentiality {27}

Data protection and confidentiality

All information pertaining to study participants will be handled in strict compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the Danish Act on Processing of Personal Data, and the Danish Health Act. If deviations from the original statistical plan are necessary, these will be thoroughly justified, described, and approved through CTIS. All statistical analyses will be conducted or reviewed by an independent statistician, who will be blinded to treatment allocation.

Baseline characteristics both clinical and demographic will be presented in a table. With means and standard deviation, SD or medians with interquartile range (IQR) for continuous outcomes. Percentages for categorical variables.

Renal cortex oxygenation, renal perfusion, and renal artery blood flow will be analyzed with linear mixed-effects models to evaluate the effect of SGLT2i versus placebo on the outcomes: renal T2*, renal perfusion, and renal artery blood flow measured at baseline, 3 h, and 6 h. The model will include fixed effects for time (baseline, 3 h, 6 h), and the interaction between treatment and time to assess whether the treatment effect varies across time points.

A random intercept for each subject will account for the correlation of repeated measurements within individuals. No period effect will be included due to the washout period, which minimizes potential carryover or period-related effects. Differences between changes will be presented as estimates with a 95% confidence interval and corresponding to p value.

To meet the assumption of normal distribution of residuals, outcomes will be log-transformed where necessary.

Statistical significance will be defined as a two-tailed p value < 0.05.

Physiological parameter outcomes includes blood glucoses, blood pressure, and heart rate.

Changes from baseline in physiological parameter outcomes will be analyzed using the same linear mixed-effects models described above. Fixed effects will include treatment (SGLT2i vs. placebo), time (baseline, 3 h, 6 h), and the interaction between treatment and time to assess whether the treatment effect varies across time points. A random intercept for each subject will account for the correlation of repeated measurements within individuals.

Differences in changes from baseline between groups will be presented as estimates with 95% confidence intervals and corresponding p values.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

In addition to the primary and secondary outcome analyses, predefined subgroup analyses will be performed to explore the consistency of the intervention effect across clinically relevant strata, including age groups (< 60 vs. ≥ 60 years), baseline kidney function (eGFR), and U-ACR (< 30, 30–300, > 300).

Adjusted analyses will be conducted using multivariable linear or logistic regression models, as appropriate, to account for potential confounding variables such as age, sex, body mass index (BMI), and baseline outcome values.

All additional analyses will be exploratory in nature unless otherwise specified and will be clearly identified as such. Statistical significance will be interpreted cautiously in light of multiple comparisons.

All analyses will be performed using statistical software STATA version 18.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

This study will follow a per-protocol analysis approach. Only participants who fully adhere to the trial protocol—including receiving the assigned intervention and completing all required assessments—will be included in the final analysis population. Participants who deviate from the protocol or fail to complete the study will be excluded and replaced, as missing data will not be accepted.

The rationale for using a per-protocol analysis is to maximize internal validity and to ensure that the treatment effect is assessed under optimal conditions, reflecting true physiological responses to the interventions when delivered as intended. This approach is particularly important in mechanistic studies or trials focused on assessing biological effects, where protocol adherence is critical to the validity of the outcomes.

As complete datasets are required for all participants, no statistical methods for handling missing data (e.g., multiple imputation) will be applied.

No separate trial steering committee or endpoint adjudication committee is planned, as the study is relatively small and tightly monitored within a single research unit.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

Justification for not establishing a Data Monitoring Committee

A Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) has not been established for this trial, as it is a single-dose intervention study with a short intervention period limited to two separate study days. The trial is considered low-risk, with minimal anticipated harm or burden to the participants.

Given the limited duration and low-risk profile of the intervention, the oversight typically provided by a DMC is not deemed necessary. Instead, trial monitoring will be conducted by the GCP Unit at OUH in accordance with GCP standards. Furthermore, the study protocol will be reviewed and approved by both the GCP Unit and via the CTIS prior to study initiation, ensuring regulatory and ethical oversight.

This approach is consistent with current guidelines, which permit the omission of a DMC in low-risk trials with short duration and limited exposure.

Adverse event

An AE is an undesirable medical occurrence or condition that has arisen after treatment with a drug, regardless of whether the event is considered related to the drug or not. That is, an AE does not necessarily have to be related to the treatment with the investigational drug. AE also includes a worsening of an already existing medical condition. AE covers symptoms, diagnoses, changes in laboratory values that demands treatment, etc. The collection, recording, and assessment of AE are noted in the eCRF, and the investigator assesses whether it is likely that the event is related to the drug.

If it is assessed that there is a probable connection between the AE and the investigational drug, it is then considered an adverse reaction (AR).

Discussion

Operational and practical considerations

This study is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial including 8 kidney transplant recipients. Each participant will receive a single dose of either 50 mg SGLT2 inhibitor or placebo, followed by a crossover to the alternate intervention after a 2-week washout period.

Given the crossover design and requirement for repeated imaging, careful logistical coordination is essential. Participants will undergo baseline BOLD-MRI followed by post-intervention scans at 3 and 6 h after drug administration. The procedure is repeated identically at the second study visit, following the crossover. The total time commitment per visit 8 h and the physical and logistical demands of repeated MRI scans may affect recruitment and retention; however, as only two intervention days are required per participant, overall burden is limited.

Participants will be required to fast before each visit and remain at the hospital for the duration of imaging, food and drink will be provided after the first MR scan. Clear communication, structured scheduling, and close collaboration with the Department of Radiology are necessary to ensure the feasibility of the protocol. Coordination with clinical nephrology staff is also crucial to avoid overlap with routine clinical care and to ensure participant safety.

No major technical or methodological challenges are anticipated, but motion artefacts and patient tolerance during long MRI sessions approximately 30 min pr. MRI, may impact data quality. Measures such as patient comfort management and clear instructions before imaging will be employed to minimize these risks.

All imaging will be conducted using the same scanner and protocol parameters to reduce variability. The relatively small sample size reflects the exploratory nature of the study, which is designed to assess the acute renal effects of a single dose of SGLT2 inhibition as measured by cortical T2* on BOLD-MRI.

A theory of locally impenetrable elastic tubes
Krishnan Suryanarayanan, Harmeet Singh

Abstract
We present a reduced order theory of locally impenetrable elastic tubes. The constraint of local impenetrability — an inequality constraint on the determinant of the 3D deformation gradient — is transferred to the Frenet curvature of the centerline of the tube via reduced kinematics. The constraint is incorporated into a variational scheme, and a complete set of governing equations, jump conditions, and boundary conditions are derived. It is shown that with the local impenetrability actively enforced, configurations of an elastic tube comprise segments of solutions of the Kirchhoff rod theory appropriately connected to segments of constant Frenet curvature. The theory is illustrated by way of three examples: a fully flexible tube hanging under self-weight, an elastic tube hanging under self-weight, and a highly twisted elastic tube.



I
Introduction

A fundamental assumption in continuum modeling of material bodies is that matter cannot penetrate itself
[
1
]
.
In other words, no two distinct material points on a body may occupy a common point in the ambient space.
While impenetrability is a global restriction on deformation of material bodies, it is locally implied by the inequality

\displaystyle\det{\bf F}>0\,,

(1)

where
{\bf F}
is the associated deformation gradient.
This condition is necessary and sufficient to ensure that no two distinct, and infinitesimally close, material points overlap upon deformation.
Although all physically admissible solutions must satisfy (
1
), it is typically not actively enforced in continuum theories.
It only serves as a criteria to discard physically inadmissible solutions.

Our objective in this article is to construct a reduced order theory of slender elastic tubes of uniform circular cross-section, where the constraint of local impenetrability is actively enforced.
We will refer to such a tube as a
locally impenetrable elastic tube
.

Our theory is built upon the
Kirchhoff rod theory
(KRT)
[
1
;
33
]
, which is a widely used model for slender bodies.
KRT entails reducing a slender body to an inextensible material curve, called the base curve, endowed with an orthonormal frame of directors.
The base curve may be interpreted as a curve connecting the centroids of the cross sections of the slender body.
The director frame is connected to the base curve by the constraints of inextensibility and unshearability, and essentially tracks the rotations of the cross-sections of the rod across its length.
The material surrounding the base curve manifests in the Kirchhoff rod theory through certain bending moduli that relate the internal moments to the bending strains in a linear fashion.

In the present theory of locally impenetrable elastic tubes, the material comprising the tube plays an important role beyond imparting bending moduli to the base curve.
It is shown that, under reduced kinematics, local impenetrability of the material of the tube places an upper cap on the Frenet curvature of the base curve.
If the base curve is extensible, this upper cap becomes a pointwise function of its stretch and the radius of the cross-section of the tube.
For inextensible base curves, as is the case in KRT, the upper cap on the Frenet curvature is simply determined by the radius of the cross section, and is thus uniform across the length of the tube.

Our motivation for this work stems from a broad set of problems concerning slender bodies in which local impenetrability plays a pivotal role.
The counterintuitive dynamics of chains and strings is one such class of problems.
The tip of a chain falling vertically on a table accelerates faster than the gravitational acceleration
[
21
;
20
]
.
When one end of a hanging folded chain, whose both ends are initially attached to the same datum, is released, it accelerates faster than the gravitational acceleration
[
7
;
53
]
.
The counterintuitive siphoning out of a long chain placed in a jar upon pulling of one of its ends over the rim (popularly known as the “chain fountain”) is another such example
[
30
;
49
;
5
;
28
]
.
It has been reported and argued in the literature that all these “chain paradoxes”
[
50
]
arise only in chains of a certain kind.
These are chains which, by virtue of their microstructure, cannot be bent beyond a certain curvature due to the local impenetrability of its constituents.
One such example is the bead/ball, chain, where small spherical beads are connected by short lengths of wire.
The maximum curvature that a bead chain may acquire is essentially determined by the size of its constituent beads
[
21
;
28
;
5
]
.
For vertically falling bead chains, the experiments reported in
[
11
]
show that the tip acceleration exceeding gravity is not attained until the chain attains its maximum curvature at the point of contact with the table.

Local impenetrability in slender bodies is also of significance in the computation of
ideal shape
of knots
[
15
]
and clasps
[
43
;
8
]
.
Loosely speaking, the ideal shape of a knotted curve of a given topology is the shape it acquires when thickened into the thickest possible smooth tube, subject to the constraint of self avoidance.
Here, by self avoidance we mean both local and global impenetrability
[
16
]
.
The ideal shape of a clasp is defined as the shape acquired by two linked filaments of finite thickness that are pulled against one another
[
18
;
43
]
.
The constraint of global impenetrability is actively incorporated in the computations of the ideal shapes of knots and clasps
[
24
;
18
]
.
However, the cap on the Frenet curvature imposed by the local impenetrability constraint may still be exceeded in such models (
[
18
]
, Figure S16), rendering the resulting configurations non-smooth and unphysical.

Another set of problems where local impenetrability is of importance concerns optimal packing of filaments in a confined volume.
Local impenetrability greatly affects the optimal packing of flexible filaments, as it imposes a hard constraint on the extent to which each tube may bend
[
34
;
19
]
.
Questions of optimal packing of filaments also arise in the context of biomolecules, such as the DNA, and have been widely studied under the purview of rod models
[
4
;
3
;
27
;
40
;
41
;
45
;
52
;
17
;
25
;
35
;
37
;
48
]
.
While a substantial part of the literature on the subject
[
13
;
9
;
10
;
44
;
42
]
, partly inspired by its relevance to biological structural shapes, accounts for global impenetrability, the constraint of local impenetrability has received far less attention outside the realm of pure mathematics
[
32
;
23
]
.

In the present work, we derive a theory of locally impenetrable elastic rods via a variational framework.
The upper cap on the Frenet curvature of the base curve, implied by the impenetrability constraint (
1
), is converted to an equality constraint by way of a slack function.
The concerned functional is then augmented with the equality constraint using a Lagrange multiplier.
Subsequent derivation of the associated Euler-Lagrange equations show that a deformed configuration of an elastic tube comprises solutions of the standard KRT connected by regions of constant Frenet curvature (known as Salkowski curves
[
29
]
).
We refer to the former regions as
inactive
, and the latter as
active
.
An active region nucleates at a point where the curvature of the rod reaches its upper cap, and begins to spread as the rod is further deformed.
The active and the inactive regions are connected by appropriate jump conditions obtained from the variational framework.
The location and the extent of the active region are unknowns of the problem, and are determined as part of the solution.
The presence of the local impenetrability constraint also modifies the constitutive relations within the active regions.
It is demonstrated that, whereas the inactive regions are characterized by a linear constitutive relation between internal moment and bending strain, the active regions manifest a distinctly nonlinear relationship between these quantities.

We illustrate our theory with three representative examples.
We first compute planar configurations of a locally impenetrable fully flexible tube of a given thickness, hanging under self-weight, with its terminal ends separated by a horizontal distance.
When the two ends of the flexible tube are brought sufficiently close, an active region nucleates and spreads at the lowest point of the tube as the two ends are brought closer.
It is shown that local impenetrability in the active region produces internal moments and shear forces, in contrast to the inactive regions.
In the second example, we add bending elasticity to the tube from the previous example and treat it as an elastic tube.
We identify a scaling parameter that determines whether or not the impenetrability constraint is activated when the two terminal ends are brought closer to the maximum possible extent.
This scaling parameter is a measure of the strength of gravity relative to the bending stiffness of the elastic tube.
It is shown that, above a critical value of the scaling parameter, the local impenetrability constraint is activated when the two terminal ends of the elastic tube are brought sufficiently close.
For smaller values of the scaling parameter, the bending elasticity ensures that the deformation never violates local impenetrability.

Finally, we compute highly twisted 3D solutions of an elastic tube using our theory.
In the absence of local impenetrability, the bending energy of the tube tends to concentrate away from its terminal ends.
With the local impenetrability incorporated, a small active region nucleates at the mid length of the rod in the form of a helix, and eventually starts spreading across the length of the rod as the twist is gradually increased.

The rest of the manuscript is organized as follows.
We begin in Sec.

by reducing constraint (
1
) along the base curve of a tubular volume, and show that it places an upper cap on the Frenet curvature of the base curve.
In Sec.

The force and moment balance equations, as well as the effective relation between the internal moments and bending strains in the active and inactive regions are derived.
Various conservation laws in the present theory are discussed and compared with the corresponding conservation laws in the standard Kirchhoff rod theory.
It is shown that the presence of local impenetrability constraint does not affect the status of any conservation law of the underlying Kirchhoff rod theory.
In Sec.
IV
, configurations of a locally impenetrable fully flexible tube hanging under self-weight are computed using the theory developed.
Sec.
V
contains solutions to a locally impenetrable elastic tube hanging under self-weight.
3D solutions to a highly twisted locally impenetrable elastic tube are presented in Sec.
VI
.
We conclude in section
VII

The details of the variational procedure employed in Sec.

are set forth in Appendix
A

\displaystyle\kappa\leq\frac{1}{t}\,,

Constitutive relations

The constitutive relations for a locally impenetrable Kirchhoff rod can be written as a piecewise function using (

\displaystyle{\bm{m}}=\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial u_{i}}{\bm{d}}_{i}\quad&\text{for}\quad s\in(0,s_{0})\\

To reiterate,
(0,s_{0})
is the inactive region whereas

The constitutive relation in the inactive region is the well known constitutive relation for a Kirchhoff elastic rod

In the active region, the constitutive relation contains an additional term which warrants further discussion.

It can be shown that the vector

The following properties of this vector will prove to be useful later

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{d}}_{3}=0\,,\qquad\mathbb{P}{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{d}}^{\prime}_{3}=0\,,\qquad\mathbb{P}{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{u}}^{\prime}=0\,,

In the Kirchhoff rod theory, the energy density function takes the following form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{2}K_{i}u_{i}^{2}\,,

where

Since we are only concerned with elastic tubes of circular cross-sections, we have

The following relations are arrived at from (

VII
Conclusion and future work

We derived a theory of elastic tubes with the constraint of local impenetrability actively enforced.
We started with the local impenetrability constraint (
1
) on the full 3D deformation, and reduced it to an inequality constraint on the Frenet curvature of the base curve (or centerline) of the tube.

Equations governing the equilibria of a locally impenetrable elatic tube were derived from the extremization of a functional augmented with the local impenetrability constraint.
The resulting equations revealed that under general loading, the impenetrability constraint activates at a point where the Frenet curvature reaches the upper cap, and spreads into a finite region of constant Frenet curvature (the active region), as the loading progresses.
The extent and location of the active region is unknown apriori, and is computed as part of the solution.
The active region exhibits a constitutive behavior that is qualitatively different from the inactive regions.
Therefore, a deformed configuration of a locally impenetrable elastic tube can also be thought of as comprising two “phases”, each with its own constitutive relation.
The jump conditions connecting the two phases are also delivered by the variational principle.

Our analysis further shows that the standard conserved quantities from the Kirchhoff rod theory persist along the full length of the tube, even though the inactive and active regions display qualitatively distinct constitutive behavior.

We illustrated our theory using three examples where standard Kirchhoff theory results in solutions with extremely high curvatures, leading to local penetration of the material of the tube.
The first was a fully flexible tube hanging under self-weight, where local impenetrability constraint delivered considerably different shapes when compared to standard catenary solutions.
More interestingly, the active regions in this case leads to the generation of bending moment and shear resistance which are otherwise absent in the inactive regions.
Next we show using an example of an extremely soft elastic tube hanging under self-weight that even though bending elasticity may lower the peak curvatures, thus avoiding local penetration, the local impenetrability constraint may become active in certain regimes.

Finally we considered a highly twisted Kirchhoff rod.
Solutions from the standard KRT result in the concentration of curvature in a small region of the rod.
Solutions generated with the present theory on the other hand show the nucleation and propagation of a helix across the length of the tube as the twist was increased.
These solutions are markedly different, both quantitatively and qualitatively, from the ones delivered by the standard Kirchhoff rod theory.

There are several different avenues along which the present work could be developed further, such as the following.
Local impenetrability imposes an upper cap on the Frenet curvature, but does not affect the geometric torsion of the tube’s centerline.
We anticipate that for certain loading conditions, the profile of the geometric torsion of the centerline could be governed by considerations of global contact, where two points distant in the reference configuration come close enough in space to establish contact.
Successful incorporation of the constraint of global impenetrability in the current theory could lead to a complete reduced theory of self-avoiding tubes.
Such a theory could have a significant impact on understanding optimal packing of elastic tubes, and by extension, on the optimal structural arrangements of several biomolecules
[
27
]
.

1.
Introduction

Let
S=\{1^{2},2^{2}\dots,\}
be the sequence of square numbers. A classical result of Landau states that
|(S+S)\cap\{1,2,\dots,n\}|\gg\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
.

One can ask what we can say on
|Q+Q|
where
Q
is an arbitrary finite subset of
S
. Clearly
|Q+Q|\leq|Q|^{2}
and the bound is strict as we get
Q=\{4^{k}\}_{1\leq k\leq n}
.

A nice and challenging conjecture of I.Z. Ruzsa states that there is a positive real number
c>0
, such that for every
finite subset
Q
of
S
,
|Q+Q|\geq|Q|^{1+c}
(see e.g. [3]). We are very far to prove this conjecture and the author did not find any quantitative lower bound for the cardinality of this sumset (see further remark in the last section).

Nevertheless we prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any non-empty finite set
Q
of squares we have

|Q+Q|\geq C^{\prime}|Q|(\log|Q|)^{1/3+o(1)}

(Here
o(1)
is negative, i.e. the power of
\log|Q|
is less than
1/3
.)

2.
Proof

The proof is simple and is supported by two deep results. So let
Q\subset S
be any finite subset of squares, and write
|Q+Q|/|Q|=K(|Q|):=K
.

The first ingredient is the Chang version of Freiman theorem (see [2]):

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
is a finite set satisfying
|A+A|\leq K|A|
. Then
A\subset P
, where
P
is a proper

d-
dimensional arithmetic progression with

d\leq\lfloor K-1\rfloor;\quad|P|\leq|A|\exp\{CK^{2}\log^{3}K)\}

The proper
d-
dimensional arithmetic progression is a set in the form

P(x_{0},x_{1},\dots x_{d}):=\{x_{0}+t_{1}x_{1}+t_{2}x_{2}+\dots+t_{d}x_{d}:0\leq t_{i}\leq l_{i};\ i=1,2,\dots,d\}

and proper means that
|P|=\prod_{1\leq i\leq d}l_{i}
.

By this lemma we get that
Q
can be covered by a proper
d-
dimensional arithmetic progression
P
with
|P|\leq|Q|\exp\{CK^{2}\log^{3}K)\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
P
be a proper
K-
dimensional arithmetic progression. The number of squares
|Q|
is

|Q|\leq|P|^{1-\frac{2}{5K}}.

Proof of lemma
2.2
.

Write
P=P(x_{0},x_{1},\dots x_{K}):=\{x_{0}+t_{1}x_{1}+t_{2}x_{2}+\dots+t_{K}x_{K}:0\leq t_{i}\leq l_{i};\ i=1,2,\dots,K\}
. We can write
P
as
P=x_{0}+P(0,x_{1})+P(0,x_{2})+\cdots+P(0,x_{K})
.

Write
P^{\prime}=x_{0}+P(0,x_{1})+P(0,x_{2})+\cdots+P(0,x_{K-1})
. Then
P=\cup_{u\in P^{\prime}}(u+P(0,x_{K}))
. Hence
|P|\leq|P^{\prime}||P(0,x_{K})|
.

Now, one of Bombieri-Zannier’s deep results is that for any
1\leq i\leq K
, the number of squares in
P(0,x_{i})
is at most
x_{i}^{3/5+o(1)}
(see in [1]). Thus

(1)

|Q|\leq\prod_{1\leq j\leq K;\ i\neq j}x_{j}x_{i}^{3/5+o(1)}

Multiply equation
(1)
for every
1\leq i\leq K
, we obtain

|Q|^{K}\leq\prod_{1\leq i\leq K}\prod_{1\leq j\leq K;\ i\neq j}x_{j}x_{i}^{3/5+o(1)}=|P|^{K-2/5+o(1)}

from which we get
|Q|\leq|P|^{1-\frac{2}{5K}+o(1/K)}
as we wanted.

Finally by Lemma
2.1
we get

|Q|\leq|P|^{1-\frac{2}{5K}+o(1/K)}\leq(|Q|\exp\{CK^{2}\log^{3}K)\})^{1-\frac{2}{5K}+o(1)}

and thus

|Q|^{\frac{2}{5K}+o(1/K)}\leq(\exp\{CK^{2}\log^{3}K)\})

which implies

C^{\prime}(\log|Q|)^{1/3+o(1)}\leq K

Hence

|Q+Q|\geq K|Q|\geq C^{\prime}|Q|(\log|Q|)^{1/3+o(1)}

which is the stated bound.
∎

3.
Concluding remarks

1. It has not escaped my attention that in
(1)
we gave a broad estimate of
P^{\prime}
. In fact, if
K=2
, i.e. if
P=x_{0}+P(0,x_{1})+P(0,x_{2})
, then we gave a bound on
P(0,x_{2})
based on the Bombier-Zannier result. For
P(0,x_{1})
– knowing that the set of square numbers does not contain a
4-
term arithmetic progression – we can give a bound
r_{4}(x_{2})
, (the maximum size of
4-
term free set up to
x_{2}
). Notice that
r_{4}(n)=O(\frac{n}{(\log n)^{c}})
, and this bound does not give better estimation.

2. Ruzsa (and maybe many others) conclude that without the Bombieri-Zannier’s result the bound
|Q+Q|\geq|Q|^{1+c}
implies that the number of squares in an arithmetic progression with length
N
is at most
\ll N^{\frac{1}{1+c}}
(indeed if
Q\subset\{x_{0}+dt:\ 1\leq t\leq N\}
then
Q+Q\subset\{2x_{0}+dt:\ 2\leq t\leq 2N\}
). Hence
c>2/3
would beat the known best result.

3. In 1960 Rudin conjectured, that in any
N-
term arithmetic progression the number of square numbers is
\ll\sqrt{N}
(see in [4]). The proof of this conjecture still seems hopeless today.

4. Several conditional bounds are known for
|Q+Q|
. By the Szemerédi-Trotter incidence theorem one can obtain a bound

\max\{|A+A|,|A^{2}+A^{2}|\}\gg|A|^{5/4},

where
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and
A^{2}=\{a^{2}:a\in A\}
.

5. A bound similar to the one in the theorem holds for any set
X\subseteq\mathbb{N}
satisfying the condition that for all
x_{0},N\in\mathbb{N}
,
|X\cap\{x_{0}+dt:\ 1\leq t\leq N\}|<N^{\alpha}
,
\alpha<1
, i.e. for every
\emptyset\neq X^{\prime}\subseteq X

|X^{\prime}+X^{\prime}|\geq K|X^{\prime}|\gg|X^{\prime}|(\log|X^{\prime}|)^{1/3+o(1)}.

holds.

Floquet Recurrences in the Double Kicked Top
Avadhut V. Purohit, Udaysinh T. Bhosale

Abstract
We study exact quantum recurrences in the double kicked top (DKT), a driven spin model that extends the quantum kicked top (QKT) by introducing an additional time-reversal symmetry-breaking kick. Reformulating its dynamics in terms of effective parameters k r k_{r} and k θ k_{\theta} , we analytically show exact periodicity of the Floquet operator for k r = j ​ π / 2 k_{r}=j\pi/2 and k r = j ​ π / 4 k_{r}=j\pi/4 with distinct periods for integer and half-odd integer j j . These exact recurrences were found to be independent of k θ k_{\theta} . The long-time-averaged entanglement and fidelity rate function show dynamical quantum phase transition (DQPT) for k r = j ​ π / 2 k_{r}=j\pi/2 at time-reversal symmetric cases k θ = ± k r k_{\theta}=\pm k_{r} . In the other time-reversal symmetric case k θ = 0 k_{\theta}=0 , the DQPT exists only for a half-odd integer j j . Using level statistics, a smooth transition is observed from integrable to non-integrable nature as k r k_{r} is changed away from j ​ π / 2 j\pi/2 . Our work demonstrates that regular and chaotic regimes can be controlled for any system size by tuning k r k_{r} and k θ k_{\theta} , making the DKT a useful platform for quantum control and information processing applications.



I
Introduction

Classical recurrence is the phenomenon in which the phase space point returns arbitrarily close to the initial point for any finite-volume, conservative system
(
Barreira 2006
;
Anishchenko and Astakhov 2013
;
Saussol 2009
)
. Its quantum analogue arises naturally from the unitary evolution
(
Bocchieri and Loinger 1957
;
Peres 1982
;
Schulman 1978
;
Fishman et al. 1982
)
. These recurrences play an important role in the study of quantum chaos. While classical chaos is hypersensitive to initial conditions, quantum dynamics remain linear and unitary. Thus, it is important to study the spectral properties
(
Wigner 1951
;
Dyson 1962
;
Berry and Tabor 1977
;
Bohigas et al. 1984
;
Korenblit et al. 2012
)
of the unitary operator of the corresponding dynamical system and recurrence patterns vital
(
Peres 1984a
;
Peres 1984b
;
Feingold et al. 1984
)
.

The QKT is a simple yet rich model that exhibits a smooth transition from regular to chaotic dynamics
(
Haake et al. 1987
)
. The existence of the classical limit allows us to not only study classical-quantum correspondence. But also serves as a testbed for studying quantum information theory due to its connection with the quantum many-body dynamics. Its experimental realizations involve cold atoms
(
Chaudhury et al. 2009
)
, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) platforms
(
Krithika et al. 2023
;
Krithika et al. 2019
)
, and superconducting qubits
(
Neill et al. 2016
)
. They offer pathways to faster computation
(
Monroe et al. 2021
)
, enhanced metrological precision
(
Pezze et al. 2018
)
, and efficient quantum thermal machines
(
Zhang et al. 2007
)
. Its generalizations to systems with long-range interactions involve statistical mechanics
(
Campa et al. 2009
;
Kastner 2011
)
, quantum many-body physics
(
Fey and Schmidt 2016
;
Britton et al. 2012
)
, cosmology
(
Peebles 1987
;
Bouchet et al. 2010
)
, atomic and nuclear physics
(
Bohr and Mottelson 1998
;
Brink 1993
)
, and plasma physics
(
Nicholson and Nicholson 1983
)
.

Although early studies discuss the arbitrary closeness of the phase space point to its initial position, the recurrence time remains arbitrarily large. Recent studies find periods for exact recurrences by analyzing Floquet spectrum
(
Sankaranarayanan and Lakshminarayan 2003
;
Anand et al. 2024
;
Anand et al. 2025
;
Sharma and Bhosale 2024a
;
Sharma and Bhosale 2024b
)
. It was shown in the QKT system
(
Anand et al. 2024
)
that state-independent exact recurrences generally occur at large chaoticity and do not have a classical analog. These studies investigated the relationship between the chaoticity parameter proportional to a rational multiple of
j\pi
and the existence of quantum recurrences. In addition, the relationship between QKT periodicities and the quantum resonances in the kicked rotor has also been established
(
Anand et al. 2024
)
.

To study the effects of broken time-reversal symmetry on quantum chaos, the DKT was considered by introducing a second instantaneous kick per driving period
(
Haake et al. 1987
)
. A recent study
(
Purohit and Bhosale 2025
)
has revealed that the DKT exhibits two distinct features of chaos, modified phase-space structures, and entanglement dynamics being highly sensitive to the relative strengths of the two kicks. This introduction of a second kick (
k^{\prime}\neq 0
) allows much finer control over entanglement evolution for small system size—crucial for quantum information processing. The study
(
Purohit and Bhosale 2025
)
leads us to ask whether the DKT exhibits exact quantum recurrences, and if so, how these recurrences depend on the time-reversal symmetry-breaking parameter
k_{\theta}
. It remains unclear whether broken time-reversal symmetry brings out local dynamical changes or global structural changes.

In this work, we analytically and numerically investigate the periodicity of the DKT Floquet operator across a range of kicking strengths. We show that the operator becomes exactly periodic at special values of the effective kicking parameter
k_{r}=j\pi/2
, regardless of
k_{\theta}
. By distinguishing between integer and half-odd-integer spin
j
, we classify the corresponding periodic regimes in terms of the powers of the Floquet operator. We further explore these behaviors using Husimi distributions and von Neumann entropy.

Although the quantum recurrences occur across
k_{\theta}
, the dynamics remain distinct. To see this, we used fidelity decay as it gives the probability that the time-evolved state returns to its initial state
(
Gorin et al. 2006
;
Jalabert and Pastawski 2001
;
Andraschko and Sirker 2014
)
. The rate function associated with fidelity is similar to the free energy in thermodynamics. Thus, the fidelity together with its rate function
(
Prosen et al. 2003
;
Gorin et al. 2006
;
Jalabert and Pastawski 2001
;
Andraschko and Sirker 2014
;
Anand et al. 2025
)
allows us to study effects of broken time-reversal symmetry on the stability and DQPTs
(
Yang et al. 2019
;
Berdanier et al. 2017
;
Naji et al. 2022
;
Jafari et al. 2022
)
.

In Sec.
II
, we introduce the DKT and its dynamics in terms of effective kicking parameters. In Sec.
III
, we examine the Floquet operator’s periodicity for
k_{r}=j\pi/2
with integer and for half-odd-integer spin
j
. In Sec.
IV
, we extend the analysis to
k_{r}=j\pi/4
. The role of time-reversal symmetry and its breaking is discussed in Sec.
V
, focusing on entanglement dynamics and fidelity rate function. In Sec.
VI
, we study the entanglement dynamics by perturbing the DKT near exact recurrences. Finally, in Sec.
VII
, we summarize the results and discuss their implications.

II
Background

The DKT introduces an extra kick of strength
k^{\prime}
immediately after the first kick of strength
k
to break the time-reversal symmetry
(
Haake et al. 1987
)
. It is governed by the following Floquet operator:

\mathcal{U}=\exp\left(-i\frac{k^{\prime}}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{k}{2j}J_{z}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{y}\right).

(1)

It was shown that if we transform kick strengths
(k,k^{\prime})
to the following kick parameters
(
Purohit and Bhosale 2025
)
:

k_{r}=\frac{k+k^{\prime}}{2}\;\;\text{ and}\quad k_{\theta}=\frac{k-k^{\prime}}{2}.

(2)

Then, the effective kick strength
k_{r}
gives QKT equivalent dynamics, and
k_{\theta}
is responsible for breaking time-reversal symmetry. The case
k_{\theta}=\pm k_{r}
recovers the standard QKT, and the other time-reversal symmetric case
k_{\theta}=0
is unique to the DKT.

It was demonstrated that the diagonalization of the Floquet operator
\mathcal{U}
for systems with 2 to 4 qubits allows complete characterization of entanglement dynamics
(
Purohit and Bhosale 2025
)
. These small systems have revealed periodic behavior of the entanglement dynamics at
k_{r}=j\pi/2
, regardless of
k_{\theta}
. The exact recurrences in small systems motivate us to investigate whether such periodicities persist in larger systems. This, in turn, offers greater control over entanglement dynamics for the system of any size.

III
Transformed kick strength
k_{r}=j\pi/2

In this section, we analytically demonstrate that the Floquet operator is periodic for both integer and half-odd integer values of
j
. Additionally, we show that the period remains independent of
k_{\theta}
. For
k_{\theta}=k_{r}
, the DKT reduces to the standard kicked top with
k=2k_{r}
. Therefore, for this case, we recover the results of the standard QKT with
k=j\pi

(
Anand et al. 2024
)
.

III.1
\mathbf{Integer}j

For integer values of
j
, we express the Floquet operator using Pauli matrices for
a
-th qubit denoted as
\sigma_{x}^{(a)}
,
\sigma_{z}^{(a)}
, and
\sigma_{y}^{(a)}

(
Anand et al. 2024
)
. In this representation, the non-linear and precession terms can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\exp\left[-i\left(\frac{k_{r}-k_{\theta}}{2j}\right)J_{x}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-i\left(\frac{k_{r}-k_{\theta}}{8j}\right)\left(\sum_{a=1}^{2j}\sigma_{x}^{(a)}\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\exp\left[-i\left(\frac{k_{r}+k_{\theta}}{2j}\right)J_{z}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-i\left(\frac{k_{r}+k_{\theta}}{8j}\right)\left(\sum_{a=1}^{2j}\sigma_{z}^{(a)}\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\text{and }\;\exp\left[-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{y}\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sum_{a=1}^{2j}\sigma_{y}^{(a)}\right].

(3)

For even-
2j
, utilizing the properties of Pauli matrices and setting
k_{r}=j\pi/2
, we can show that
\mathcal{U}^{2}
can be written as follows (see supplementary material
(
sup
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{2}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}2j}}{4}\left[C_{1}+C_{2}\cos\left(\frac{k_{\theta}}{2j}\right)+C_{3}\sin\left(\frac{k_{\theta}}{2j}\right)\right]

(4)

\displaystyle\times\left[C_{1}+C_{2}\cos\left(\frac{k_{\theta}}{2j}\right)-C_{3}\sin\left(\frac{k_{\theta}}{2j}\right)\right]\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)^{2},

where,

\displaystyle C_{1}=

\displaystyle\left[\mathds{I}^{\otimes 2j}+(i\sigma_{y})^{\otimes 2j}\right]\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+i\left[(i\sigma_{z})^{\otimes 2j}+(i\sigma_{x})^{\otimes 2j}\right]\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right),

\displaystyle C_{2}=

\displaystyle\;\mathds{I}^{\otimes 2j}-(i\sigma_{y})^{\otimes 2j},\quad C_{3}=i\left[(i\sigma_{z})^{\otimes 2j}-(i\sigma_{x})^{\otimes 2j}\right]\text{ and}

\displaystyle\gamma=

\displaystyle\;e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{y}}.

(5)

Using identities
C_{1}C_{2}+C_{2}C_{1}=0
,
C_{3}C_{1}-C_{1}C_{3}=0
,
C_{3}C_{2}-C_{2}C_{3}=0
and

as follows (see supplementary material
(
sup
)
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{2}=\;\frac{e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}2j}}{4}\left(C_{1}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}\right)\cdot\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)^{2}.

(6)

It can be seen that

. This makes all even powers of

identical with the corresponding Floquet operator of QKT

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{4}=-e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}2j}{(i\sigma_{y})}^{\otimes 2j}\implies\mathcal{U}^{8}=\mathds{I}.

\displaystyle=-\text{Tr}[\rho_{1}(n)\log_{2}\rho_{1}(n)],

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{2}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-i\frac{k^{\prime}}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{k_{r}}{j}J_{z}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{k}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-i\frac{k^{\prime}}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{z}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{k}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)^{2}.

(12)

Unlike the case of even-
2j
, here, we do not get the simplified form obtained in Eq. (
6
). Hence, continuing along the similar lines of Sec.
III.1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{3}=e^{-i\frac{k^{\prime}}{2j}J_{x}^{2}}e^{-i\frac{k_{r}}{j}J_{z}^{2}}e^{-i\frac{k_{r}}{j}J_{x}^{2}}e^{-i\frac{k}{2j}J_{z}^{2}}\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)^{3}.

(13)

Proceeding further by setting

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{3}=e^{-i\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{k_{\theta}}{2j}\right)J_{x}^{2}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{z}^{2}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{x}^{2}}e^{-i\left(\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{k_{\theta}}{2j}\right)J_{z}^{2}}\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)^{3}.

We provide detailed derivations in the supplementary material
(

\displaystyle\exp\left(i\frac{k_{\theta}}{4j}\sigma_{x}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{z}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{k_{\theta}}{4j}\sigma_{y}\right),

\displaystyle\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{x}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{k_{\theta}}{4j}\sigma_{z}\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left(i\frac{k_{\theta}}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{z}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{z}^{2}\right)\exp\left(i\frac{k_{\theta}}{2j}J_{y}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{x}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{k_{\theta}}{2j}J_{z}^{2}\right)

Using these relations, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{3}=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{x}^{2}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{z}^{2}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{x}^{2}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{z}^{2}}\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)^{3}.

(17)

k_{\theta}
. Continuing further, we obtain the operator
\mathcal{U}^{6}

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{6}=

\displaystyle\;\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{x}^{2}\right){\left[\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{z}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{2}J_{x}^{2}\right)\right]}^{3}

IV
Transformed kick strength
k_{r}=j\pi/4

We examine the periodicity of
\mathcal{U}
for the case
k_{r}=j\pi/4
. In the case of integer
j
, we show that the period of the Floquet operator is independent of
k_{\theta}
. However, for a half-odd integer
j
, we do not observe periodic behavior. As mentioned earlier, for
k_{\theta}=k_{r}
, the DKT reduces to the standard QKT with
k=j\pi/2
. Although this case has been studied numerically for the standard QKT
(
Anand et al. 2024
)
, the analytical proof for integer
j
was not given, and the reason for the absence of periodicity for half-odd integer
j
was also not provided. In this section, we provide analytical calculations for these cases of the DKT. As a consequence, we recover the results for the standard QKT.

IV.1
\textbf{Integer }j

First, we show that the operator
\mathcal{U}^{12}
is independent of
k_{\theta}
and then calculate the period of this case. Following a similar analysis as in Sec.
III
, we obtain the following expression for the third power of the Floquet operator (see supplementary material
(
sup
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{3}=

\displaystyle\;\exp\left(-i\frac{k^{\prime}}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\left[\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{z}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{x}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left(-i\frac{k}{2j}J_{z}^{2}\right){\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)}^{3}.

(21)

By repetitive multiplications of this operator and simplifying by swapping
{\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)}^{3}
to the right by transforming
J_{z}^{2}\to J_{x}^{2}
,
J_{x}^{2}\to J_{z}^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{12}=\;\exp\left(-i\frac{k^{\prime}}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\mathcal{O}\exp\left(-i\frac{k}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\exp\left(i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{x}^{2}\right){\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)}^{12},

(22)

where,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}={\left[\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{z}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{x}^{2}\right)\right]}^{6}.

(23)

Since the operator
\mathcal{O}
commutes with
J_{x}^{2}
operator (see supplementary material
(
sup
)
), the above Floquet operator simplifies as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{12}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{O}\cdot{\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)}^{12}.

(24)

For even-
2j
, the operator
\mathcal{O}
can be simplified using properties of the Pauli matrices and the Caley-Hamilton theorem (see supplementary material
(
sup
)
) as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=\frac{e^{-i\frac{3\pi}{4}2j}}{2}\left[\mathds{I}^{\otimes 2j}+{(-1)}^{j}{\sigma_{y}}^{\otimes 2j}+i{\sigma_{z}}^{\otimes 2j}+i{\sigma_{x}}^{\otimes 2j}\right].

(25)

On squaring further, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{24}=-\sigma_{y}^{\otimes 2j}\implies\mathcal{U}^{48}=\mathds{I}^{\otimes 2j}.

(26)

The Floquet operator
\mathcal{U}
, therefore, has period 48.

IV.2
\textbf{Half-odd integer }j

In contrast to the integer case, numerical results reported in
(
Anand et al. 2024
)
show that the Floquet operator does not exhibit periodicity for half-odd integer values of
j
. In this subsection, we provide an analytical explanation for this observation with the DKT extension. The 12th power of the Floquet operator for this case is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{12}=

\displaystyle\;\exp\left(-i\frac{k^{\prime}}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\mathcal{O}\exp\left(-i\frac{k}{2j}J_{x}^{2}\right)\exp\left(i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{x}^{2}\right){\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2j}\right)}^{12}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=

\displaystyle\;{\left[\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{z}^{2}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{x}^{2}\right)\right]}^{6}.

(27)

Now, we construct a state
|\psi\rangle_{2j}
for which

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{m}|\psi\rangle_{2j}\neq|\psi\rangle_{2j}\quad\text{for any }m\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}.

(28)

To do so, we split the odd-
2j
system into
(j-3/2)
-pairs of qubits and unpaired 3-qubits. Let
(a,b)=(1,2),(3,4),\cdots
represent the paired qubits and
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}
represent the unpaired qubits. For each pair
(a,b)
, the two-spin singlet state is defined as follows:

\displaystyle|\psi_{ab}^{-}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|01\rangle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|10\rangle.

(29)

It satisfies the property given by

\displaystyle\left(\sigma_{i}^{(a)}+\sigma_{i}^{(b)}\right)|\psi_{ab}^{-}\rangle=0

\displaystyle\text{for }i=x,y,z.

(30)

Using this two-qubit singlet state, the full odd-
2j
state can be constructed as follow

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle_{2j}=\bigotimes_{(a,b)}^{j-3/2}|\psi_{ab}^{-}\rangle\otimes|\chi_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}\rangle.

(31)

By splitting the full angular momentum operator given by

\displaystyle J_{i}=\sum_{(a,b)}J_{i}^{(a,b)}\otimes\mathds{I}^{\otimes 3}+\mathds{I}^{\otimes(2j-3)}\otimes J_{i}^{(3)},

(32)

the action of
J_{i}
on the singlet subspace reduces to the action of
J_{i}
on the unpaired three qubits:

\displaystyle J_{i}|\psi\rangle_{2j}=\mathds{I}^{\otimes(2j-3)}\otimes J_{i}^{(3)}|\psi\rangle_{2j}\quad\text{for }i=x,y,z.

(33)

Here, the angular momentum
J_{i}^{(3)}=\left(\sigma_{i}^{t_{1}}+\sigma_{i}^{t_{2}}+\sigma_{i}^{t_{3}}\right)/2
acts only on the state
|\chi_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}\rangle
and the angular momentum
J_{i}^{(a,b)}
of the pair
(a,b)
acts trivially on the state
|\psi_{ab}^{-}\rangle
. It follows that the action of a polynomial
P_{n}\left(J_{i}\right)
on the state
|\psi\rangle_{2j}
reduces to the action of its corresponding unpaired three-qubit version on the state
|\chi_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}\rangle
:

\displaystyle P_{n}\left(J_{i}\right)|\psi\rangle_{2j}=\mathds{I}^{\otimes(2j-3)}\otimes P_{n}(J_{i}^{(3)})|\psi\rangle_{2j}.

(34)

Therefore, the action of an operator
\mathcal{O}
on the state
|\psi\rangle_{2j}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}|\psi\rangle_{2j}=\mathds{I}^{\otimes(2j-3)}\otimes{\left(\mathcal{A}^{(3)}\right)}^{6}|\psi\rangle_{2j},

(35)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(3)}=\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{z}^{(3)}\cdot J_{z}^{(3)}\right)\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}J_{x}^{(3)}\cdot{J_{x}^{(3)}}\right).

(36)

The eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}^{(3)}
are
e^{i\pi/4}
with multiplicity 4 and
e^{i\pi/4}(\pm\sqrt{7}-3i)/4
with multiplicity 2 for each. The last four eigenvalues cannot be written in the form
(-1)^{r}
for any
r\in\mathds{Q}
. This shows that the action of the operator
\mathcal{O}
is not periodic on the state
|\psi\rangle_{2j}
.

If there was some integer
t
satisfying
\mathcal{O}^{t}=\mathds{I}
then, it would have satisfied for the state
|\psi\rangle_{2j}
as well. But this is a contradiction. Hence,
\mathcal{O}^{t}\neq\mathds{I}
for all positive integer values of
t
. As a result, the Floquet operator
\mathcal{U}
does not have periodicity.

V
Broken time-reversal symmetry

We examine the role of time-reversal symmetry in exact recurrences and its breaking through the fidelity rate function. In earlier sections, we have shown that the long-time-averaged von Neumann entropy landscapes for even-
2j
and odd-
2j
exhibit distinct behaviors in both time-reversal-symmetric cases,
k_{\theta}=k_{r}
and
k_{\theta}=0
(see Figs.
3
and
7
). Additionally, the states
|\theta_{0}=0,\phi_{0}=0\rangle
and
|\theta_{0}=\pi/2,\phi_{0}=-\pi/2\rangle
are of particular interest. The state
|\theta_{0}=0,\phi_{0}=0\rangle
exhibits sharp transition in entanglement only for
k_{\theta}=k_{r}
(see Figs.
4
(a) and
8
(a)). The state
|\theta_{0}=\pi/2,\phi_{0}=-\pi/2\rangle
, on the other hand, shows formation of vortices just before
k_{\theta}=k_{r}
(see Figs.
4
(b) and
8
(b)). However, at
k_{\theta}=k_{r}
, there are no noteworthy changes. This suggests that the mere breaking of time-reversal symmetry does not necessarily lead to abrupt changes in entanglement dynamics. These qualitatively distinct features motivate us to further investigate using the fidelity rate function, as it gives insights into the DQPTs.

The rate function
R(n)
, derived from the fidelity
\mathcal{Z}(n)
, is defined
(
Heyl et al. 2013
)
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(n)=

\displaystyle\;\left|\langle\theta_{0},\Phi_{0}|{\mathcal{U}(j,k_{r},k_{\theta})}^{n}|\theta_{0},\Phi_{0}\rangle\right|^{2}\;\;\mbox{and}

(37)

\displaystyle R(n)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2j+1}\ln\mathcal{Z}(n).

(38)

For the exactly periodic case, the fidelity
\mathcal{Z}(m)=1
and the associated rate function
R(m)=0
, where
m
is the period. In a dynamical sense, the fidelity measures the probability that the time-evolved state is found in the initial state. The rate function
R(n)
, on the other hand, plays a role similar to the free energy density in thermodynamics. Cusps in the rate function are interpreted as DQPTs. Our computations of the long-time-averaged rate function given by

\displaystyle\langle R\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}R(t),

(39)

shows a pronounced peak at
k_{\theta}=k_{r}
. However, for the case
k_{\theta}=0
, sharp changes in the rate function are observed only for half-odd integer values of
j
.

P^{\tilde{k}}(r,\beta)

is slightly increased, the SRF evolves into an intermediate statistics of Poisson and Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE) (see Fig.

13

j
. Nonetheless, for finite

, we can expect a mixed regime due to the presence of intermediate statistics for certain values of
k_{r}
near

.

VII
Results and Conclusion

In this work, we have investigated exact recurrences of the DKT using analytical and numerical techniques. Our key result is the identification of the exact periodicity of the Floquet operator for
k_{r}=j\pi/2,j\pi/4
, and its independence from the symmetry-breaking parameter
k_{\theta}
. At the exact quantum recurrence points, our computations of the Husimi distribution show that the quantum wave packets return to their initial position and become localized after a predicted time, regardless of the value of
k_{\theta}
.

For integer
j
, we find that the Floquet operator exhibits periodicity with period 8 when
k_{r}=j\pi/2
, and period 48 when
k_{r}=j\pi/4
. In contrast, for half-odd integer values of
j
, the periodicity turns out to be 12 for
k_{r}=j\pi/2
, but absence thereof for
k_{r}=j\pi/4
. In all cases, the periodicity is shown to be independent of
k_{\theta}
, despite its role in breaking temporal symmetry. With this, we have generalized the recent work on quantum recurrences
(
Anand et al. 2024
)
by explicit analytical derivations.

To probe entanglement dynamics, we have studied the long-time-averaged von Neumann entropy of the single-qubit reduced density matrix. Breaking time-reversal symmetry by perturbing
k_{\theta}
does not show any noteworthy change in entanglement for most initial states near
k_{\theta}=0
. However, near
k_{\theta}=\pm k_{r}
, such perturbations create vortices. Motivated by these findings, we further examine the dynamical response of the initial state
|\theta_{0}=0,\phi_{0}=0\rangle
using the fidelity rate function as a probe for DQPTs. Pronounced signatures are observed at
k_{\theta}=\pm k_{r}
. However, for
k_{\theta}=0
, these features depend on whether
2j
is even or odd. These results suggest a qualitative distinction between the two time-reversal symmetric cases
k_{\theta}=0
and
k_{\theta}=\pm k_{r}
. It is an open question whether the distinction between these two cases arises from local or global quantum dynamics.

In the vicinity of the periodic point
k_{r}=j\pi/2
, we observe signatures of integrable behavior in both entropy and level spacing ratio statistics. As
k_{r}
departs from
j\pi/2
, the system transitions from integrable to non-integrable, and the states corresponding to the trivial fixed points exhibit bifurcation in the long-time-averaged von Neumann entropy. This bifurcation is unique as it does not have a classical analogue.

The exact recurrences identified in this work are useful from a quantum control perspective. Even though the transformed kick strengths
(k_{r},k_{\theta})
are relevant for the dynamics, the physical kicks are
(k,k^{\prime})
. This allows us to tune the system of any size to get a desirable output in terms of entanglement dynamics.

The DKT can be realized by extending established QKT platforms. In NMR systems
(
Krithika et al. 2023
;
Krithika et al. 2019
)
, the DKT extension can be achieved by adding a second nonlinear twist immediately after the first. In cold-atom setups
(
Chaudhury et al. 2009
)
and Floquet synthetic lattice systems
(
Meier et al. 2019
)
, a second Stark-shift kick can be applied in a rotated basis. The same effect can be obtained using an additional quadratic-phase gate in the superconducting qubits
(
Neill et al. 2016
)
.

In summary, our study reveals that exact quantum recurrences in the DKT are robust to the time-reversal symmetry-breaking parameter
k_{\theta}
. The DQPT is observed at
k_{\theta}=\pm k_{r}
, but in the case of
k_{\theta}=0
, it depends on whether
2j
is even or odd, indicating a qualitative distinction between these two time-reversal symmetric cases. The transition from integrable to chaotic behavior at
k_{r}=j\pi/2
shows how quantum effects can suppress the underlying chaotic dynamics. These findings establish the DKT as a versatile platform for exploring quantum recurrences, effects of time-reversal symmetry, and controlling driven Floquet systems.

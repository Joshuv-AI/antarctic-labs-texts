From Diamond Gaugings to Dualisations
Dimitrios Chatzis, John M. Marley and Daniel C. Thompson

Abstract
We revisit the proposal that coupling two six-dimensional holomorphic Chern–Simons theories generates gaugings throughout the twistor-space diamond relating 6d hCS, 4d self-dual Yang-Mills, 4d Chern–Simons, and 2d integrable models. In previous work this mechanism was demonstrated only in a special case, leaving its general status unclear. By reformulating the construction in the language of Cartan geometry, we expose the underlying gauge structure and show that the argument extends to generic choices of meromorphic data. We then apply this to the pole structure that yields the well-studied λ \lambda -deformations of the WZW model. The coupled 6d system indeed induces gaugings of the associated λ \lambda -models, but necessarily introduces Lagrange multipliers enforcing flatness of the gauged connection. The resulting two-dimensional theories are therefore non-Abelian dualisations rather than ordinary gauged λ \lambda -models.

4
Gauged holomorphic Chern-Simons theory on twistor space

There is a wide variety of integrable models in two dimensions that can be thought of as having gauge symmetry (not only e.g. principal chiral theories on geometric cosets, but also gauged WZW, Pohlmeyer reduced theories, lambda deformations). One should anticipate that they can also be seeded within the six-dimensional perspective.

In
9
this was approached from a bottom up perspective, starting with a gauged WZW model in two dimensions. These enjoy a remarkable property; they can be expressed as a difference of two ungauged WZW terms. This is captured via the Polyakov-Wiegmann identity
22

S_{\text{g}\mathrm{WZW}_{2}}[g,B]=S_{\mathrm{WZW}_{2}}[\tilde{g}]-S_{\mathrm{WZW}_{2}}[\tilde{h}]\,,

where the various fields take values in:
g\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma,G)
,
B\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{h}
,
\tilde{g}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma,G)
,
\tilde{h}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma,H)
, and the WZW action is

S_{\text{WZW}_{2}}[g]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\text{tr}\,\left(g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g\wedge\star g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g\right)+\frac{1}{3}\int_{{\cal M}_{3}}\text{tr}\left(\,\hat{g}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\hat{g}\wedge\hat{g}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\hat{g}\wedge\hat{g}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\hat{g}\right)\,.

with
\partial{\cal M}_{3}=\Sigma
and
\hat{g}
an appropriate extension.
The same concept is generalized, although some details enter the picture, to four-dimensional gauged WZW models
21
, where the WZW action takes the form:

S_{\text{WZW}_{4}}[g]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\text{tr}\,\left(g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g\wedge\star g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g\right)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}\times[0,1]}\omega\wedge\text{tr}\,(\hat{g}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\hat{g}\wedge\hat{g}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\hat{g}\wedge\hat{g}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\hat{g})\,,

with
\omega
being a Kähler form on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. In this case the four-dimensional analogue of the Polyakov-Wiegmann identity holds only when the gauge connection is flat.

This
difference of theories
structure motivated the approach of
9
wherein the difference of two holomorphic Chern-Simons terms was proposed as the avatar of such integrable models. The fundamental fields of the theory are now the connections

{\cal A}\in\Omega^{(0,1)}(\mathbb{PT})\otimes\mathfrak{g}\,,\quad{\cal B}\in\Omega^{(0,1)}(\mathbb{PT})\otimes\mathfrak{h}\,,

which are combined in the action

S_{\text{ghCS}}[{\cal A},{\cal B}]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\Omega\wedge\Big({\cal L}_{\text{CS}}[{\cal A}]-{\cal L}_{\text{CS}}[{\cal B}]\Big)+S_{\text{int}}[{\cal A},{\cal B}]\,,

where we have the
ad hoc
introduction of the interaction term

\begin{split}&S_{\text{int}}[{\cal A},{\cal B}]=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\bar{\partial}\Omega\wedge\text{tr}\left({\cal A}\wedge{\cal B}\right)\,.\end{split}

We now follow broadly the procedure laid out in the ungauged model. The first step is to note the boundary terms coming from variation of the action

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\bar{\partial}\Omega\wedge\mathrm{tr}\left[\left(\delta\mathcal{A}+\delta\mathcal{B}\right)\wedge\left(\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{B}\right)\right]\,.\end{split}

Here one encounters a puzzle. Whilst it is clear that the WZW-type boundary condition of the ungauged theory can be readily lifted to this term, it appears at first sight rather more challenging to apply the more general cases of boundary conditions that relate different poles. However it was seen in
9
that for the case of two double poles that we can solve the boundary condition in terms of edge modes of
{\cal A}
, but can’t determine
{\cal B}
; indeed a careful study of the gauge symmetries shows that the
{\cal B}
sources a four-dimensional gauged field that covariantises derivatives of the edge mode with respect to the
H
-local symmetry. All steps involved required significant amount of algebra especially since symmetries are not made manifest during the calculation.

Instead of this rather cumbersome procedure we will utilise the background field variables of section
2
, namely we shall work with
{\cal B}
and the combination

{\cal C}={\cal A}-{\cal B}\,.

This provides an immediate justification for the inclusion of the interaction term
S_{\text{int}}
since it eliminates the production of boundary terms in when changing variables such that

S_{\text{ghCS}}[{\cal A},{\cal B}]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\Omega\wedge{\cal L}_{\text{CS}}^{\nabla}[{\cal C},{\cal B}]+\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\Omega\wedge\text{tr}\,\left({\cal C}\wedge{\cal F}[{\cal B}]\right)\,,

where we invoke the gauged Chern-Simons three-form defined in (
6
).
This presentation makes manifest local
H
-gauge transformations parametrised by elements
\hat{h}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PT},H)
which act as

\hat{h}:\quad{\cal B}\mapsto\hat{h}^{-1}\bar{\partial}\hat{h}+\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat{h}}^{-1}{\cal B},\quad{\cal C}\mapsto\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat{h}}^{-1}{\cal C},

and under which the anti-holomorphic covariant derivative,
\bar{\nabla}=\bar{\partial}+{\cal B}
, of
{\cal C}
transforms adjointly,
\bar{\nabla}{\cal C}\mapsto\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat{h}}^{-1}(\bar{\nabla}{\cal C})
.
Since this transformation holds is exact and does not generate boundary terms, it does not produce edge modes and there is no need from the outset to perform any Stueckelberg type analysis on the
{\cal B}
gauge field.

In contrast however under the
G
-transformations parametrised by
\hat{f}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PT},G)

\hat{f}:\quad{\cal C}\to\hat{f}^{-1}\bar{\nabla}\hat{f}+\text{Ad}_{\hat{f}}^{-1}{\cal C}\,,

do not lead the action strictly invariant but instead produce the contributions

\displaystyle\delta_{\hat{f}}S_{\text{ghCS}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\bar{\partial}\Omega\wedge\mathrm{tr}\left({\cal C}\wedge\bar{\nabla}\hat{f}\hat{f}^{-1}\right)-\Omega\wedge{\cal L}^{(3)}_{\text{gWZ}}[\hat{f},{\cal B}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\bar{\partial}\Omega\wedge\mathrm{tr}\left({\cal C}\wedge\bar{\nabla}\hat{f}\hat{f}^{-1}-\hat{f}^{-1}\bar{\partial}\hat{f}\wedge{\cal B}-\bar{\partial}\hat{f}\hat{f}^{-1}\wedge{\cal B}-{\cal B}\wedge\hat{f}{\cal B}\hat{f}^{-1}\right)-\Omega\wedge{\cal L}_{\text{WZ}}[\hat{f}]\,.

We see that the
{\cal B}
enters here to simply
H
-covariantise the anomalous terms that were already present in the ungauged theory. So our consideration of the
{\cal C}
field will proceed,
mutatis mutandis
, in the same fashion as the treatment of
{\cal A}
in the ungauged theory with the following steps

1.

Stueckelberg fields
To restore exact invariance under
G-transformations we will introduce a set of Stueckelberg modes
\hat{g}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PT},G)
and parametrise

{\cal C}=\hat{g}^{-1}\bar{\nabla}\hat{g}+\hat{g}^{-1}{\cal C}^{\prime}\hat{g}\,.

Acting on the field
{\cal C^{\prime}},{\cal B}
and
\hat{g}
we inherit both the original gauge symmetries and new internal symmetries refelcting the rundancy of the parameterisation. These act as:

\displaystyle\hat{f}:

\displaystyle{\cal B}\mapsto{\cal B}\,,\quad\,

\displaystyle{\cal C}^{\prime}\mapsto{\cal C}^{\prime},\,\quad

\displaystyle\hat{g}\mapsto\hat{g}\hat{f}\,,

\displaystyle\check{f}:

\displaystyle{\cal B}\mapsto{\cal B}\,,\quad\,

\displaystyle{\cal C}^{\prime}\mapsto\check{f}^{-1}\bar{\nabla}\check{f}+\check{f}^{-1}{\cal C}^{\prime}\check{f},\,\quad

\displaystyle\hat{g}\mapsto\check{f}^{-1}\hat{g}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{h}:

\displaystyle{\cal B}\mapsto\hat{h}^{-1}\bar{\partial}\hat{h}+\hat{h}^{-1}{\cal B}\hat{h}\,,\quad\,

\displaystyle{\cal C}^{\prime}\mapsto\hat{h}^{-1}{\cal C}^{\prime}\hat{h},\,\quad

\displaystyle\hat{g}\mapsto\hat{h}^{-1}\hat{g}\hat{h}\,.

2.

Set boundary conditions
We impose, by choice, a set of boundary conditions on
{\cal C}
that ensure the vanishing of

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\bar{\partial}\Omega\wedge\text{tr}\,{\cal C}\wedge\delta{\cal C}\,.

Structurally this has the same form as in the ungauged theory, so whatever boundary conditions were employed on
{\cal A}
in that case could equally be applied for
{\cal C}
in this gauged version.
Our gauge redundancies are reduced to those that preserve the chosen set of boundary conditions

3.

Partial Fix
We use the
\hat{h}
symmetry and the internal
\check{f}
symmetry to make the fixing that
{\cal C}^{\prime}_{0}=0
and
{\cal B}_{0}=0
, i.e. that neither field has
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
legs. As in the ungauged case, this gauge choice is consistent with the constraints. In bulk, away from the poles, this fixing requires the equations for the remaining components are pure constraint and that

{\cal C}^{\prime}_{\dot{a}}=\pi^{a}C_{a\dot{a}}\,,\quad{\cal B}_{\dot{a}}=\pi^{a}B_{a\dot{a}}\,.

Note that the bulk terms then vanish upon these constraints so that the theory reduces simply to

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\bar{\partial}\Omega\wedge\mathrm{tr}\left({\cal C}^{\prime}\wedge\bar{\nabla}\hat{g}\hat{g}^{-1}-\hat{g}^{-1}\bar{\partial}\hat{g}\wedge{\cal B}-\bar{\partial}\hat{g}\hat{g}^{-1}\wedge{\cal B}-{\cal B}\wedge\hat{g}{\cal B}\hat{g}^{-1}\right)-\Omega\wedge{\cal L}_{\text{WZ}}[\hat{g}]\,.

(8)

In addition we may further choose to fix some properties on the edge mode
\hat{g}
, the precise details of which would be dependent on the choice of theory, but again we emphasise that the analysis in the ungauged model would be equally valid in this.

4.

Determine
C_{a\dot{a}}
We now solve the boundary conditions to determine
C_{a\dot{a}}
in terms of
\hat{g}
(or rather its evaluation at the poles) and
B_{a\dot{a}}
. Indeed, if this analysis has been performed already in the ungauged model, it is now manifest that the result will remain the same up to the modification that partial derivatives become covariantised with respect to the, now 4d, gauge field
B_{a\dot{a}}
.

5.

Localise
We are now in a position to complete the localisation, by inserting into the edge mode action the solution for
{\cal C^{\prime}}
in term of
\hat{g}
and
B
, evaluating
{\bar{\partial}}\Omega
to produce delta functions and their derivatives at poles allowing the integration over the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
directions to take place. Thus, modulo the straightforward covariantisation by

{\cal B}
, the gauged construction inherits the localisation structure of the ungauged model, paving the way to concrete examples such as the
(1,1,2) pole structure relevant to the
\lambda
-deformation of gauged WZW models that we now consider.

5
Example
\lambda
-model

In this section we will utilize the above formulation of holomorphic Chern-Simons theory on
\mathbb{PT}
with the meromorphic
(3,0)
-form relevant for the
\lambda
-model, as per equation (
7
). As this is a technical section let us direct the reader to the main results: the presentation of the 4d integrable model, eq. (
15
); the establishment of its equations of motion as

(anti-)self-duality of a connection
A
; the reduction to a gauged two-field 2d integrable model, eq. (
18
) and its Lax formulation, eq. (
21
); and the identification of a parametric point of enhanced symmetry resulting in the simpler 2d integrable model of eq. (
25
).

According to our general discussion we are going to employ the boundary conditions used in the ungauged model
10
. These conditions involve cancellations between contributions at the poles
\pi=\alpha
and
\pi=\tilde{\alpha}
and are expressed in terms of a free scale parameter
\sigma
, and a unit norm spinor
\mu
:

\begin{split}&\left.\frac{[{\cal C}\hat{\mu}]}{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle}\right|_{\alpha}=\sigma^{-1}\left.\frac{[{\cal C}\hat{\mu}]}{\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle}\right|_{\tilde{\alpha}},\quad\left.\frac{[{\cal C}\mu]}{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle}\right|_{\alpha}=\sigma\left.\frac{[{\cal C}\mu]}{\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle}\right|_{\tilde{\alpha}},\quad{\cal C}\big|_{\beta}=0\,.\end{split}

(9)

To elucidate these conditions we work with adapted coordinates

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle}\tilde{\alpha}_{a}x^{a\dot{a}}\hat{\mu}_{\dot{a}}~,\qquad

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle}\tilde{\alpha}_{a}\mu_{\dot{a}}x^{a\dot{a}}~,

(10)

\displaystyle\mathsf{z}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle}\alpha_{a}\hat{\mu}_{\dot{a}}x^{a\dot{a}}~,\qquad

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{z}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle}\alpha_{a}\mu_{\dot{a}}x^{a\dot{a}}~,

such that

\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}\wedge\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{w}}\wedge\mathrm{d}\mathsf{z}\wedge\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{z}}=\frac{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle^{2}\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle^{2}}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle^{2}}\mathrm{vol}_{4}\,.

Recall that as a hyperkähler manifold,
\mathbb{R}^{4}
is equipped with a
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
’s worth of complex structures. The coordinates
\mathsf{z}
and
\hat{\mathsf{z}}
are holomorphic with respect to the complex structure defined by the point
\pi=\alpha
and
\mathsf{w}
and
\hat{\mathsf{w}}
by the one at
\pi=\tilde{\alpha}
. Given a complex structure defined by
\pi\in\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, the self-dual two-form of type
(2,0)
is defined by
\Sigma_{\pi}=\pi_{a}\pi_{b}\epsilon_{\dot{a}\dot{b}}\mathrm{d}x^{a\dot{a}}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{b\dot{b}}
and in particular we have

\Sigma_{\beta}=2\mathrm{d}\mathsf{u}\wedge\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{u}}\,,\quad\Sigma_{\alpha}=2\frac{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle^{2}}{\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle^{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{z}\wedge\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{z}}\,,\quad\Sigma_{\tilde{\alpha}}=2\frac{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle^{2}}{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle^{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}\wedge\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{w}}\,,

in which
\mathsf{u}=\mathsf{z}+\mathsf{w}
,
\hat{\mathsf{u}}=\hat{\mathsf{z}}+\hat{\mathsf{w}}
(we will also make use occasionally of
\mathsf{v}=\mathsf{w}-\mathsf{z}
and
\hat{\mathsf{v}}=\hat{\mathsf{w}}-\hat{\mathsf{z}}
).

Expressing
{\cal C}={\cal C}_{\dot{a}}\bar{e}^{\dot{a}}
in terms of these coordinates and using the localisation methods that we shall detail momentarily, we find that the boundary term in the variation of the action includes contributions at the single poles of the form

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\textrm{vol}_{4}\,\left(\iota_{\mathsf{w}}{\cal C}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}\delta{\cal C}-\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}{\cal C}\iota_{\mathsf{w}}\delta{\cal C}\right)|_{\alpha}-\left(\iota_{\mathsf{z}}{\cal C}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}\delta{\cal C}-\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}{\cal C}\iota_{\mathsf{z}}\delta{\cal C}\right)|_{\tilde{\alpha}}\,,

in which we denote, for example,
\iota_{\mathsf{w}}\equiv\iota_{\partial_{\mathsf{w}}}
the contraction with respect to
\partial_{\mathsf{w}}
. Thus we see that the boundary conditions at the single poles, eq. (
9
), which read in these coordinates

\iota_{\mathsf{w}}{\cal C}|_{\alpha}=\sigma\iota_{\mathsf{z}}{\cal C}|_{\tilde{\alpha}}\ ,\quad\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}{\cal C}|_{\alpha}=\sigma^{-1}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}{\cal C}|_{\tilde{\alpha}}\,,

do indeed set to zero the contribution above. Of particular note is that freedom to include the constant parameter
\sigma
such that this defines an entire family of possible models.

We now express these boundary conditions in terms of the Stueckelberg parameterisation

{\cal C}=\hat{g}^{-1}\bar{\nabla}\hat{g}+\hat{g}^{-1}{\cal C}^{\prime}\hat{g}\,,

under the gauge fixing that
{\cal C}^{\prime}_{0}=0
subject to the fixing and definitions

\hat{g}|_{\alpha}=g\,,\quad\hat{g}|_{\tilde{\alpha}}=\tilde{g}\,,\quad\hat{g}|_{\beta}=\text{id}_{G}\,.

Given the bulk requirement that
{\cal C}^{\prime}_{\dot{a}}=\pi^{a}C_{a\dot{a}}
we can address the boundary condition at the double pole in terms of the 4d gauge field
C=C_{a\dot{a}}\mathrm{d}x^{a\dot{a}}
:

{\cal C}|_{\beta}=0\Leftrightarrow{\cal C}^{\prime}|_{\beta}=0\Leftrightarrow\beta^{a}C_{a\dot{a}}=0\Leftrightarrow C_{a\dot{a}}\mathrm{d}x^{a\dot{a}}=C_{\mathsf{u}}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{u}+C_{\hat{\mathsf{u}}}\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{u}}\,.

The boundary conditions at the first order poles then become

g^{-1}\nabla_{\mathsf{w}}g+g^{-1}C_{\mathsf{u}}g=\sigma\left(\tilde{g}^{-1}\nabla_{\mathsf{z}}\tilde{g}+\tilde{g}^{-1}C_{\mathsf{u}}\tilde{g}\right),\quad g^{-1}\nabla_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}g+g^{-1}C_{\hat{\mathsf{u}}}g=\sigma^{-1}\left(\tilde{g}^{-1}\nabla_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}\tilde{g}+\tilde{g}^{-1}C_{\hat{\mathsf{u}}}\tilde{g}\right)\,.

(11)

Using the short hand notation
R^{\nabla}=\nabla gg^{-1}
and
\tilde{R}^{\nabla}=\nabla\tilde{g}\tilde{g}^{-1}

we can hence determine

C_{\mathsf{u}}=-U_{+}R^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{w}}-U_{-}^{T}\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{z}}\,,\quad C_{\hat{\mathsf{u}}}=-U_{-}R^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}-U_{+}^{T}\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}\,,

(12)

where the operators
U_{\pm}
are defined as

U_{\pm}=(\textrm{id}-\sigma^{\pm 1}\text{Ad}_{g}\circ\text{Ad}_{\tilde{g}}^{-1})^{-1}\,.

5.1
Localisation

We now turn to the final step of the procedure described above, namely performing the integral over the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. In general we are faced with integrals of the form

I=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\bar{\partial}\Omega\wedge Q\,,

(13)

where in general
Q\in\Omega^{(0,2)}(\mathbb{PT})
however since the legs in the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
directions are saturated by the integration measure
\bar{\partial}\Omega
we can restrict to consider
Q=Q_{\dot{a}\dot{b}}\bar{e}^{\dot{a}}\wedge\bar{e}^{\dot{b}}
.
To understand these it is expedient to move to inhomogeneous coordinates on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
setting
\pi=(1,\zeta)

such that we can employ the identities

\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\zeta-\alpha}\bigg)=-2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\delta^{2}(\zeta-\alpha)\ ,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\zeta\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\zeta}\,\delta^{2}(\zeta-\alpha)\,f(\zeta)=f(\alpha)\ .

Having done this one can localise
I
to a four-dimensional integral, and revert back to homogeneous spinor coordinates.
For the case at hand of
\Omega
given in eq. (
7
), we have the general result

\displaystyle I=\frac{K}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle\langle\alpha\beta\rangle^{2}}\Sigma_{\alpha}\wedge Q|_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle^{2}}\Sigma_{\tilde{\alpha}}\wedge Q|_{\tilde{\alpha}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(\langle\alpha\beta\rangle\langle\tilde{\alpha}\hat{\beta}\rangle+\langle\alpha\hat{\beta}\rangle\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle\right)}{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle^{2}\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle^{2}\langle\beta\hat{\beta}\rangle}\Sigma_{\beta}\wedge Q|_{\beta}-\frac{1}{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle}\Sigma_{\beta}\wedge\partial_{0}Q|_{\beta}\,.

When
Q=\pi^{a}\pi^{b}q_{a\dot{a}b\dot{b}}\bar{e}^{\dot{a}}\wedge\bar{e}^{\dot{b}}
we have the further simplifications

\Sigma_{\pi}\wedge Q=\Sigma_{\pi}\wedge q\,,\quad\Sigma_{\pi}\wedge\partial_{0}Q=\Sigma_{\pi}\wedge\partial_{0}q+\frac{2}{\langle\pi\hat{\pi}\rangle}\xi_{\pi}\wedge q\,,

in which
q=q_{a\dot{a}b\dot{b}}\mathrm{d}x^{a\dot{a}}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{b\dot{b}}
and
\xi_{\pi}=\pi_{a}\hat{\pi}_{b}\epsilon_{\dot{a}\dot{b}}\mathrm{d}x^{a\dot{a}}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{b\dot{b}}
.

We begin with the contribution to (
8
) in which

Q_{1}=\text{tr}\,{\cal C}^{\prime}\wedge\bar{\nabla}\hat{g}\hat{g}^{-1}\,\quad\textrm{such that}\quad q_{1}=\text{tr}\,C\wedge(\mathrm{d}\hat{g}\hat{g}^{-1}+B-\hat{g}B\hat{g}^{-1})\,.

Since the boundary condition ensures
{\cal C}^{\prime}|_{\beta}=\partial_{0}{\cal C}^{\prime}|_{\beta}=0
we find, after some moderate algebra using the solution for the components of
C_{a\dot{a}}
eq. (
12
), the localised contribution yields

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\bar{\partial}\Omega\wedge Q_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{K}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\mathrm{vol}_{4}\,\text{tr}\Big[\frac{1}{2}R^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{w}}(U_{+}^{T}-U_{-})R^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{z}}(U_{+}^{T}-U_{-})\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}-R^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{w}}U_{+}^{T}\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}+\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{z}}U_{-}R^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}\Big]\,.

This contribution matches the ungauged model with the replacement of derivatives to derivatives covariantised with the connection
B
.
The next contribution comes from the WZ terms, and here we explicitly introduce an extension parametrised by coordinate
\rho
,

Q_{2}=-\int_{I}\mathrm{d}\rho\;\text{tr}\left(\hat{g}^{-1}\partial_{\rho}\hat{g}\,\hat{g}^{-1}\bar{\partial}\hat{g}\wedge\hat{g}^{-1}\bar{\partial}\hat{g}\right)\,,

which gives

I_{2}=-\frac{K}{2\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}\times I}\textrm{vol}_{4}\wedge\mathrm{d}\rho\,\text{tr}\left(g^{-1}\partial_{\rho}g[g^{-1}\partial_{\mathsf{w}}g,g^{-1}\partial_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}g]-\tilde{g}^{-1}\partial_{\rho}\tilde{g}[\tilde{g}^{-1}\partial_{\mathsf{z}}\tilde{g},\tilde{g}^{-1}\partial_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}\tilde{g}]\right)\,.

The final term to consider is the gauge completion of the WZ contribution given by

Q_{3}=-\text{tr}\left(\hat{g}^{-1}\bar{\partial}\hat{g}\wedge{\cal B}+\bar{\partial}\hat{g}\hat{g}^{-1}\wedge{\cal B}+{\cal B}\wedge\hat{g}{\cal B}\hat{g}^{-1}\right)\,.

This term is more delicate as one needs to carefully consider contributions from all poles, including the double pole at
\beta
. At the single poles we have

q_{3}|_{\alpha}=-\text{tr}\left(g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g\wedge B+\mathrm{d}gg^{-1}\wedge B+B\wedge gBg^{-1}\right)\,,

and similarly a contribution for
\tilde{g}
arises at
\tilde{\alpha}
.
These first order pole contributions combine with the WZ terms produced in
I_{2}
above to yield the gauged WZ Lagrangian.

At the double we need to consider the Lie algebra valued field,
u\propto\hat{g}^{-1}\partial_{0}\hat{g}|_{\beta}
(where we choose a normalisation of
u
of convenience to absorb factors)

q_{3}|_{\beta}=0\,,\quad\partial_{0}q_{3}|_{\beta}=\text{tr}(uF[B]+\mathrm{d}(uB))\,.

Hence the combined contribution from
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
can be expressed as (omitting the total derivative
\mathrm{d}(uB)
assuming appropriate fall-off of the fields on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
)

\begin{split}I_{2}+I_{3}=&-\frac{K}{2\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle}\int\mathrm{vol}_{4}(\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}\iota_{\mathsf{w}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{gWZ }}[g,B]-\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}\iota_{\mathsf{z}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{gWZ }}[\tilde{g},B]))\\
&-\frac{K}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{u}\wedge\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{u}}\wedge\text{Tr}(uF[B])\,,\end{split}

where we recall the gauged WZW Lagrangian two-form is

\mathcal{L}_{\text{gWZ}}[g,B]=\text{tr}\left(g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g\wedge B+\mathrm{d}gg^{-1}\wedge B+B\wedge gBg^{-1}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\int_{I}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\text{tr}\,g^{-1}\partial_{\rho}g\,g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g\wedge g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g\,.

(14)

Combining all the terms produces we recover the action,

\begin{split}S_{\text{IFT}_{4}}&=-\frac{K}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\mathrm{vol}_{4}\,\text{tr}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}R^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{w}}(U_{+}^{T}-U_{-})R^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{z}}(U_{+}^{T}-U_{-})\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}-R^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{w}}U^{T}_{+}\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}+\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{z}}U_{-}R^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}\\
&\hskip 102.43008pt+\frac{1}{2}\left(\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}\iota_{\mathsf{w}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{gWZ }}[g,B]-\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}\iota_{\mathsf{z}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{gWZ }}[\tilde{g},B]\right)\Big\}\\
&\hskip 17.07182pt-\frac{K}{\langle\alpha\tilde{\alpha}\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{u}\wedge\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{u}}\wedge\text{tr}(uF[B])\,.\end{split}

(15)

5.2
Equations of motion

As detailed in appendix
A
, the equations of motion of the action eq. (
15
) are given by

\displaystyle\delta g:

\displaystyle\quad 0=F^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{w}\hat{\mathsf{w}}}[C]+F_{\mathsf{w}\hat{\mathsf{w}}}[B]\,,

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{g}:

\displaystyle\quad 0=F^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{z}\hat{\mathsf{z}}}[C]+F_{\mathsf{z}\hat{\mathsf{z}}}[B]\,,

\displaystyle\delta u:

\displaystyle\quad 0=\mathrm{d}\mathsf{u}\wedge\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{u}}\wedge F[B]\,,

in which we use

F^{\nabla}[C]=\nabla C+C\wedge C\,,\quad F[B]=\mathrm{d}B+B\wedge B\,,

with
C
is evaluated in terms of the currents in eq. (
12
). Recalling that
C=C_{\hat{\mathsf{u}}}\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{u}}+C_{\mathsf{u}}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{u}
we also have identically
\mathrm{d}\mathsf{u}\wedge\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathsf{u}}\wedge F^{\nabla}[C]=0
, and thus these equations combine to the anti-self-duality of
F^{\nabla}[C]+F[B]
. Indeed, if we let
C=A-B
(now working in terms of the four-dimensional connections) these equations correspond exactly to the anti-self-duality of
A
.

Turning to the equations of motion that follow from variation with respect to
B
we obtain

\displaystyle\delta B_{\mathsf{w}}:

\displaystyle\quad 0=Q_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}-\nabla_{\hat{\mathsf{v}}}u\,,\quad\delta B_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}:\quad 0=Q_{\mathsf{w}}-\nabla_{\mathsf{v}}u\,,

\displaystyle\delta B_{\mathsf{z}}:

\displaystyle\quad 0=\widetilde{Q}_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}-\nabla_{\hat{\mathsf{v}}}u\,,\quad\delta B_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}:\quad 0=\widetilde{Q}_{\mathsf{z}}-\nabla_{\mathsf{v}}u\,.

in which we define

Q=C-C^{g}|_{\mathfrak{h}}=A-A^{g}|_{\mathfrak{h}}\,,\quad\widetilde{Q}=C-C^{\tilde{g}}|_{\mathfrak{h}}=A-A^{\tilde{g}}|_{\mathfrak{h}}\,,

with
A^{g}=g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g+g^{-1}Ag
and
C^{g}=g^{-1}\nabla g+g^{-1}Cg
.
Combining these yields a condition

Q_{\mathsf{w}}-\widetilde{Q}_{\mathsf{z}}=0\,,\quad Q_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}-\widetilde{Q}_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}=0\,.

Note that the construction of
C
that arose in eq. (
11
) ensures that

C_{\mathsf{w}}=C_{\mathsf{z}}\,,\quad C^{g}_{\mathsf{w}}=\sigma C^{\tilde{g}}_{\mathsf{z}}\,,\quad C_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}=C_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}\,,\quad C^{g}_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}=\sigma^{-1}C^{\tilde{g}}_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}\,.

(16)

and hence we obtain the condition

(1-\sigma)C_{\mathsf{z}}^{\tilde{g}}|_{\mathfrak{h}}=0\,,\quad(1-\sigma^{-1})C_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}^{\tilde{g}}|_{\mathfrak{h}}=0\,,

or explicitly

(1-\sigma^{-1})\text{Ad}_{\tilde{g}}^{-1}U_{+}(\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{z}}-R^{\nabla}_{\mathsf{w}})|_{\mathfrak{h}}=0\,,\quad(1-\sigma)\text{Ad}_{\tilde{g}}^{-1}U_{-}(\tilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}-R^{\nabla}_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}})|_{\mathfrak{h}}=0\,.

Since our construction assumes
\sigma\neq 1
, (else the matrices
U_{\pm}
are ill defined) these conditions invoke the vanishing of the subgroup components of the currents, and this determine implicitly two of four components of
B
in terms of the other fields in the model.

Given that
F_{\mathsf{v}\hat{\mathsf{v}}}[B]=0
by virtue of the
u
equation of motion, we obtain an integrability condition that

\dagger\equiv\nabla_{\mathsf{v}}Q_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}-\nabla_{\hat{\mathsf{v}}}Q_{\mathsf{w}}=F_{\mathsf{v}\hat{\mathsf{v}}}[B]\cdot u=0\,,

which might, in principle, be a cause for concern as it could impose conditions on
g
and
\tilde{g}
on top of the anti-self-duality of
A
. However with some work we can show that this integrability condition is automatically satisfied on the remaining equations of motion. First we use the
B
equation of motion
Q_{\mathsf{w}}=\widetilde{Q}_{\mathsf{z}}
to recast as

\dagger=\iota_{\mathsf{w}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}\nabla(C-C^{g})|_{\mathfrak{h}}-\iota_{\mathsf{z}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}\nabla(C-C^{\tilde{g}})|_{\mathfrak{h}}\,.

Now if
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
is a reductive decomposition, which we will assume, then
[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{m}]\subset\mathfrak{m}
and we can take the projection after the covariant derivative i.e.

\dagger=\iota_{\mathsf{w}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}(\nabla C-\nabla C^{g})|_{\mathfrak{h}}-\iota_{\mathsf{z}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}(\nabla C-\nabla C^{\tilde{g}})|_{\mathfrak{h}}\,.

To complete these derivatives of
C
into the combination
F^{\nabla}[C]
we are required to incorporate contributions of the type
C\wedge C
. Fortunately the conditions in eq. (
16
) ensure

\iota_{\mathsf{w}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}C\wedge C=\iota_{\mathsf{z}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}C\wedge C\,,\quad\iota_{\mathsf{w}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}C^{g}\wedge C^{g}=\iota_{\mathsf{z}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}C^{\tilde{g}}\wedge C^{\tilde{g}}\,.

and thus we can recast the integrability condition as

\dagger=\iota_{\mathsf{w}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}(F^{\nabla}[C]-F^{\nabla}[C^{g}])|_{\mathfrak{h}}-\iota_{\mathsf{z}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}(F^{\nabla}[C]-F^{\nabla}[C^{\tilde{g}}])|_{\mathfrak{h}}\,.

Now we can use
F^{\nabla}[C^{g}]=\text{Ad}_{g}^{-1}F^{\nabla}[C]+\text{Ad}_{g}^{-1}F[B]-F[B]
, which follows directly from the definition, to obtain

\dagger=(1-\text{Ad}_{g}^{-1})\iota_{\mathsf{w}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{w}}}(F^{\nabla}[C]+F[B])|_{\mathfrak{h}}-(1-\text{Ad}_{\tilde{g}}^{-1})\iota_{\mathsf{z}}\iota_{\hat{\mathsf{z}}}(F^{\nabla}[C]+F[B])|_{\mathfrak{h}}\,,

which immediately now vanishes upon the
g,\tilde{g}
equation of motion.

5.3
Reduction

We now perform a reduction to two dimensions. The data that defines the reduction consists of a set of unit norm spinors
\gamma_{a}
and

We will in this work make the choice

The details about the coordinate transformation and full form of the action (

We proceed by using a reduction ansatz of the form

5.3.1
Case of Enhanced symmetry

The structure of the operators
U_{\pm}
suggests that simplifications may be obtained by defining

such that

\displaystyle J_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}=\left(\frac{1}{a-b}-\frac{\sigma^{-1}}{\tilde{a}-b}\right)U_{-}R^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}+\frac{\sigma^{-1}}{\tilde{a}-b}U_{-}\bar{R}^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}},\,

\displaystyle J_{\mathtt{w}}=\left(\frac{a}{a-b}-\frac{\tilde{a}\sigma}{\tilde{a}-b}\right)U_{+}R^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}+\frac{\tilde{a}\sigma}{\tilde{a}-b}U_{+}\bar{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}\,.

(22)

\begin{split}S_{\text{IFT}_{2}}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathtt{w}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\mathtt{w}}&\,\text{tr}\,\Big[c_{1}R^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}U_{+}^{T}R^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-c_{2}R^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}U_{-}R^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}+c_{3}\bar{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}U_{+}^{T}R^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-c_{4}R^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}U_{-}\bar{R}^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}\\

6
Example and Comparison to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

In the above we saw that as a result of the enhancement we obtain a gauging by a vectorial action of a subgroup

Evidently this truncated theory describes a deformation of the

WZW model, and so it is natural to ask what is the relationship of this theory to the known integrable
\lambda

G/H

15

The integrable
\lambda
-deformation of the
G/H
WZW model exists when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
grading with respect to the grade zero subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}
and is given by a deformation of a

H

A\in\mathfrak{g}
be a connection, and
P^{(i)}A=A^{(i)}
its projection into
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
and identify

\displaystyle S_{\lambda-G/H}

\displaystyle=kS_{G/G~\text{gWZW}}[\bar{g},A]+k(1-\lambda^{-1})\int\mathrm{d}\mathtt{w}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\mathtt{w}}\,\text{tr}A^{(1)}_{\mathtt{w}}A^{(1)}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}\,.

The components of
A

B
but eliminate out the

\displaystyle S_{\lambda-G/H}

\displaystyle=kS_{G/H~\text{gWZW}}[\bar{g},B]+k\int\mathrm{d}\mathtt{w}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\mathtt{w}}\,\text{tr}\left(A^{(1)}_{\mathtt{w}}\bar{R}^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-A^{(1)}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}\bar{L}^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}-\lambda A^{(1)}_{\mathtt{w}}U_{+}^{-1}A^{(1)}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}\right)

H

7
Conclusions

The aim of this work has been to clarify the higher dimensional origin of gauged integrable models. The key concept presented here is a refined framework in which one can formulate gauged holomorphic Chern-Simons theories on twistor space, based on the diamond construction of
5
;
10
;
9
in a conceptually clear way. It is from these theories that we can then extract new four and two-dimensional integrable models, as many works have recently demonstrated in the literature. The theories in six dimensions consist of two Lie algebra valued connections,
{\cal A}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{PT})\otimes\mathfrak{g}
and
{\cal B}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{PT})\otimes\mathfrak{h}
, as well as an auxiliary meromorphic three-form
\Omega\in\Omega^{(3,0)}(\mathbb{PT})
whose holomorphic data, together with the reduction data, completely determines the lower dimensional theories. By choosing to describe the theories in terms of the fields
{\cal C}={\cal A}-{\cal B}
and
{\cal B}
instead, and by using the language of Cartan connections, we acquire a manifestly covariant formulation which motivates the need for a boundary term in the action, previously included in an
ad hoc
fashion. As a demonstration we use the holomorphic data corresponding to the
\lambda
-model in the ungauged case, i.e. a (1,1,2) pole structure for
\Omega
. Localizing the theory on the poles in the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
fibre we obtain new families of four-dimensional integrable field theories described by the action (
15
), whose equations of motion correspond to the anti-self duality of a four-dimensional connection
A
. Upon dimensional reduction, we find new two-dimensional integrable models given by (
18
) containing covariant gauged currents from the edge modes of each single pole, with interactions between them. Common to the un-gauged discussion of
10
there is a parametric point for which the theories gain additional local symmetries such that only one out of the two edge mode fields contributes to the dynamics.

In this way we obtain a two-dimensional model that corresponds to the gauging of the
\lambda
-deformed
\mathrm{WZW}_{2}
by means of a flat connection. This can be understood as implementing an non-Abelian T-dualisation of the
\lambda
-deformed
\mathrm{WZW}_{2}
. Indeed the emergence of a two-dimensional Lagrange multiplier term can be directly traced to a coupling
{\cal C}\wedge{\cal F}[{\cal B}]
that is required for gauge invariance in six-dimensions.

In the undeformed
\lambda\rightarrow 0
considered in
9
it is possible to dispense of the Lagrange multiplier term all-together and obtain the
G/H
coset CFT realised as gauged WZW. Here however, once the deformation is active,
\lambda\neq 0
, dropping the requirement that the gauging is flat is no longer
in general
something that can be done in a way that preserves integrability and we contrast this truncation with the known integrable
\lambda
-deformation of the
G/H
CFT.

As an exception however to this general picture we study the case where
G/H=\mathrm{SU}(2)/\mathrm{U}(1)
. In this specific case, one
can
achieve matching with the
\lambda
-model after truncating the theory at the level of the action by discarding the term enforcing the constraint. Nevertheless, the truncated Lax connection still encodes information from the constraint and thus needs further modification to exactly match the one for the
\lambda
-deformed
\mathrm{WZW}_{2}
.

7.1
Future Directions

The six-dimensional gauging of the diamond has thus provided a concrete realization of dualisation in integrable models, opening several avenues for further study:

•

Poisson–Lie Dualisation
The current construction produces non-Abelian dualisations. Given the close connection between integrable models and Poisson–Lie duality
18
, it is natural to ask whether a six-dimensional origin for general Poisson–Lie dualisations can be formulated, especially since these do not admit a straightforward Buscher-type
7
implementation.

•

Fermionisation/Bosonisation
Could other dualities, such as fermionisation or bosonisation, be understood from a higher-dimensional perspective? These are known to admit gauging procedures in two dimensions
6
, suggesting a potential 6d origin.

•

Quantum Aspects of Non-Abelian Dualisation
In the Abelian case, ungauged and gauged models are quantum mechanically equivalent. For non-Abelian dualisations, the quantum status is less clear. Extending the current framework to address arbitrary genus 2d worldsheets and embedding them into the diamond remains an important challenge, with recent work
16
in this direction. At the 6d level even in the case of Abelian gaugings it is not clear that gauged models and ungauged models are equivalent and elucidating this both classically and quantum mechanically may be profitable.

•

Boundary Conditions and Defects
A general expectation is that some dualisations could correspond to different choices of boundary conditions. This is motivated by the way that pairs of dual sigma models correspond to complementary lagrangian subalgebras of a Drinfeld double. It remains somewhat open to address the full classification of boundary conditions in 6d, and to identify the cases for which the 2d theories are a classical/quantum dual pair. Here we obtained a dualisation through our 6d gauging construction; one might consider if it is possible to interpret gauged 6d models as ungauged models with alternative boundary conditions.

•

Connections to Lattice Models
Recent advances
2
;
1
;
3
suggest that
\lambda
-models and more generally 4d Chern–Simons theory setups can provide a pathway to light-cone lattice quantisation and associated spin-chain descriptions. At the same time there have been exciting advances in framing dualisation in lattice systems
20
;
27
, and it would be valuable to relate the continuum dualisation mechanisms developed here to these lattice constructions.

Finally, it is worth noting that one original motivation of this study was to identify a six-dimensional origin of the integrable
\lambda
-deformation of the
G/H
gauged WZW model. Our results show that this is
not
directly realized within the current framework. Achieving this goal likely requires explicitly incorporating the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(or
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
) grading of the undeformed integrable structure, potentially via a modified gauging procedure, alternative boundary conditions, or a yet-to-be-determined construction (see
11
,
4
for cases in which such grading is crucial and also the more general branch cut defects of
14
).

Beyond the specific gauging mechanism analysed here, there remain several structural questions about the holomorphic Chern–Simons origin of integrable models. A natural next step is a systematic study of admissible boundary conditions for the six-dimensional fields on twistor space and the corresponding web of dual two-dimensional theories they generate. More generally, understanding how different meromorphic structures for

\Omega
organise the full landscape of integrable deformations, how quantum considerations enter this picture, and whether any of these constructions admit a string-theoretic or gravitational interpretation, will be key arenas for development. These questions sit somewhat beyond the scope of the present work, but they form a broader context in which the gauging of the diamond developed here can be viewed as one concrete piece.

In this section we note a number of calculation details that are used in the derivation of the four-dimensional action and its equations of motion. Although algebraic in nature, these details are sufficiently tedious to reproduce that it warrants recording them for posterity.

For the gauge transformation

\nabla R^{\nabla}\equiv\mathrm{d}R^{\nabla}+B\wedge R^{\nabla}+R^{\nabla}\wedge B=R^{\nabla}\wedge R^{\nabla}+F[B]-gF[B]g^{-1}\,.

Useful identities to handle the variation of these covariant Maurer-Cartan forms acting on some object

\displaystyle\text{tr}\left[(\delta_{g}R_{\mu}^{\nabla})X\right]=\text{tr}[\delta gg^{-1}\left(-\nabla_{\mu}X+[R^{\nabla}_{\mu},X]\right)]+\text{total derivative}\,,

\displaystyle\text{tr}\left[\left(\delta_{B}R^{\nabla}_{\mu}\right)X\right]=\text{tr}\left[(1-\mathrm{Ad}^{-1}_{g})X\,\delta B_{\mu}\right]\,.

Under arbitrary variations of
g

B

\displaystyle\delta_{B}{\cal L}_{\text{gWZ}}[g,B]=-\text{tr}\left[\delta B\wedge(R^{\nabla}+L^{\nabla})\right]\,.

and the gauged WZ terms

\begin{split}{\cal Y}_{4}^{\text{gWZ}}&=-\frac{1}{2\langle\alpha\beta\rangle^{2}}\Big[\langle\alpha\hat{\gamma}\rangle^{2}\iota_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}\iota_{\bar{\mathtt{z}}}{\cal L}_{\text{gWZ}}[g,B]+\langle\alpha\gamma\rangle^{2}\iota_{\mathtt{w}}\iota_{\mathtt{z}}{\cal L}_{\text{gWZ}}[g,B]\\

&+\frac{1}{2\langle\tilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle^{2}}\Big[\langle\tilde{\alpha}\hat{\gamma}\rangle^{2}\iota_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}\iota_{\bar{\mathtt{z}}}{\cal L}_{\text{gWZ}}[\tilde{g},B]+\langle\tilde{\alpha}\gamma\rangle^{2}\iota_{\mathtt{w}}\iota_{\mathtt{z}}{\cal L}_{\text{gWZ}}[\tilde{g},B]\\

\displaystyle\textrm{term}_{1}

\displaystyle\textrm{term}_{2}

Appendix D
More general reductions

In the treatment of section
5.3
we made two key assumptions in the reduction to an
\text{IFT}_{2}
each of which can be relaxed. In both cases we do not find a NLSM interpretation of the result but are of sufficient interest to warrant a brief comment.

First, we aligned
\kappa_{\dot{a}}\sim\mu_{\dot{a}}
, i.e. that the dotted spinor setting reduction to directions matches that involved in setting the boundary conditions. If this choice is not made a rather elaborate action is obtained, and does not yield a two-dimensionally Lorentz invariant theory in the sense that the Kinetic operator contains not only
\partial_{\mathtt{w}}\partial_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}
but also
\partial_{\mathtt{w}}^{2}
and
\partial_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}^{2}
terms and so the interpretation as a NLSM is limited. However, there remains some underlying structure since in this more general scenario the Lax operators can be expressed in terms of currents

\displaystyle J_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}=\left[\frac{1}{a-b}U_{-}R^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{a}-b}U^{T}_{+}\widetilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}\right]\,,\quad J_{\mathtt{w}}=\left[\frac{a}{a-b}U_{+}R^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}+\frac{\tilde{a}}{\tilde{a}-b}U_{-}^{T}\widetilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}\right]\,,

\displaystyle K_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}=\left[\frac{1}{a-b}U_{+}R^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{a}-b}U^{T}_{-}\widetilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}\right]\,,\quad K_{\mathtt{w}}=\left[\frac{a}{a-b}U_{-}R^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}+\frac{\tilde{a}}{\tilde{a}-b}U_{+}^{T}\widetilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}\right]\,,

as (n.b. the mixed
\mathtt{w}
and
\bar{\mathtt{w}}
index structure)

\displaystyle L^{\kappa}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}+(b-\zeta)\left([\kappa\mu][\hat{\kappa}\hat{\mu}]K_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-[\kappa\hat{\mu}][\hat{\kappa}\mu]J_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}+[\hat{\kappa}\mu][\hat{\kappa}\hat{\mu}](K_{\mathtt{w}}-J_{\mathtt{w}})\right)\,,

\displaystyle L^{\kappa}_{\mathtt{w}}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mathtt{w}}+\frac{(b-\zeta)}{\zeta}\left([\kappa\mu][\hat{\kappa}\hat{\mu}]K_{\mathtt{w}}-[\kappa\hat{\mu}][\hat{\kappa}\mu]J_{\mathtt{w}}+[\kappa\mu][\kappa\hat{\mu}](K_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-J_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}})\right)\,.

In this ‘non-aligned’ scenario we also note that at the point of enhancement eq. (
22
) under the redefinition
\bar{g}=g\tilde{g}^{-1}
there is no-longer an elimination of the
g
degree’s of freedom; they are retained in the
K
currents. This ultimately due to the fact that the enhanced gauge symmetry in two-dimensions is a result of the residual symmetries preserved by the boundary conditions in four-dimensions. It is only when the reduction is aligned to boundary conditions that the four-dimensional semi-local symmetries give rise to fully localised gauge freedom in two-dimensions.

A second consideration in in the reduction ansatz. In a previous work,
9
, it was shown that it was possible to relax the ansatz that
B_{\mathtt{z}}=B_{\bar{\mathtt{z}}}=0
. There choosing
B_{\mathtt{z}}
and
B_{\bar{\mathtt{z}}}
to be constant and in the centre of
\mathfrak{h}
(but not necessarily central in
\mathfrak{g}
) was shown to modify the resultant two-dimensional gauged WZW by integrable potentials (examples of which include the complex Sine-Gordon model). Restricting for simplicity again to the aligned
\mu\sim\kappa
case, we extract the comments
L_{\mathtt{w}}
and
L_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}
without imposing any reduction ansatz as

L_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}=\nabla_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-\zeta\nabla_{\mathtt{z}}+(b-\zeta)J^{\prime}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}\,,\quad J^{\prime}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}=\left[\frac{1}{a-b}(U_{-}R^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-aU_{-}R^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{z}})+\frac{1}{\tilde{a}-b}(U^{T}_{+}\widetilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-\tilde{a}U^{T}_{+}\widetilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{z}})\right]\,,

L_{\mathtt{w}}=\nabla_{\mathtt{w}}+\frac{1}{\zeta}\nabla_{\bar{\mathtt{z}}}+\zeta^{-1}(b-\zeta)J^{\prime}_{\mathtt{w}}\,,\quad J^{\prime}_{\mathtt{w}}=\left[\frac{1}{a-b}(aU_{+}R^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}+U_{+}R_{\bar{\mathtt{z}}})\frac{1}{\tilde{a}-b}(\tilde{a}U_{-}^{T}\widetilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}+U_{-}^{T}\widetilde{R}^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{z}}})\right]\,.

If we choose
\partial_{\mathtt{z}}=\partial_{\bar{\mathtt{z}}}=0
and also put
B_{\mathtt{z}}=\phi
,
B_{\bar{\mathtt{z}}}=\bar{\phi}
as constant and central in
\mathfrak{h}
we anticipate that the reduced theory will remain integrable and consistent. In that case we have

J^{\prime}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}=J_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-\bar{{\cal N}}\cdot\phi\,,\quad\bar{{\cal N}}=\frac{a}{a-b}U_{-}(1-\text{Ad}_{g})+\frac{\tilde{a}}{\tilde{a}-b}U_{+}^{T}(1-\text{Ad}_{\tilde{g}})\,,

J^{\prime}_{\mathtt{w}}=J_{\mathtt{w}}+{\cal N}\cdot\bar{\phi}\,,\quad{\cal N}=\frac{1}{a-b}U_{+}(1-\text{Ad}_{g})+\frac{1}{\tilde{a}-b}U_{-}^{T}(1-\text{Ad}_{\tilde{g}})\,.

Focusing our attention at the point of enhancement, eq. (
22
), and letting
\phi=a^{-1}\Phi
and
\bar{\phi}=a\bar{\Phi}
we obtain

L_{\mathtt{w}}=\nabla_{\mathtt{w}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{z}}\bar{\Phi}-\frac{\mathfrak{z}^{-1}+\sigma}{1+\sigma}U_{+}\left(\bar{R}^{\nabla}_{\mathtt{w}}+\sigma^{2}(1-\text{Ad}_{\bar{g}})\bar{\Phi}+(1-\sigma^{2})(1-\text{Ad}_{g})\bar{\Phi}\right)\,,

L_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}=\nabla_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-\zeta\Phi-\frac{1+\zeta\sigma}{1+\sigma}U_{-}\left(\bar{R}^{\nabla}_{\bar{\mathtt{w}}}-\sigma^{-2}(1-\text{Ad}_{\bar{g}})\Phi-(1-\sigma^{-2})(1-\text{Ad}_{g})\Phi\right)\,.

This is an intriguing situation in which one finds that whilst
g
no-longer decouples, it appears in the Lax only through the adjoint action on
\Phi
and
\bar{\Phi}
. If we set
\Phi\propto\bar{\Phi}
then we see that
g
enters in the Lax only defined upto the right action of the stabiliser of
\Phi
, which by definition is at least
H
, but could in principle be larger.

Adaptive Crouzeix-Raviart finite elements for the first eigenpair of p p -Laplacian
Guanglian Li, Yueqi Wang, Yifeng Xu

Abstract
In this paper, we propose and analyze an adaptive Crouzeix-Raviart finite element method for computing the first Dirichlet eigenpair of the p p -Laplacian problem. We prove that the sequence of error estimators produced by the adaptive algorithm has a vanishing limit and that, starting from a fine initial mesh, the relevant sequence of approximate eigenvalues converges to the first eigenvalue and the distance in a mesh-dependent broken norm between discrete eigenfunctions and the set composed of relevant continuous eigenfunctions also tends to zero. The analysis hinges on establishing a compactness property for Crouzeix-Raviart finite elements over a sequence of adaptively generated meshes, which represents key theoretical challenges and novelties. We present numerical results to illustrate the advantage of the proposed algorithm.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded polyhedral connected domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
), and fix
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Consider the following Dirichlet eigenvalue problem of the
p
-Laplacian operator:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\mathrm{div}(|\bm{\nabla}u|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}u)&=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u&&\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u&=0&&\text{on}~\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

In the engineering literature, the
p
-Laplacian operator is often used to describe non-Newtonian fluids, turbulent flow, flow through porous media and power-law materials etc.

The weak formulation of (
1.1
) is to find
(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times V:=W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}u|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}u\cdot\bm{\nabla}v\mathrm{d}x=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}uv\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall v\in V.

(1.2)

The existing theory
[
36
,
42
,
44
]
states that there is a nondecreasing sequence of positive eigenvalues

\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
tending to
\infty
. Moreover, the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}

is simple and isolated
[
44
]
, and can be characterized as the minimum of the Rayleigh quotient:

\lambda_{1}=\inf_{v\in V\setminus\{0\}}\mathcal{J}(v):=\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}v|^{p}\mathrm{d}x/\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

(1.3)

By the direct method in calculus of variation
[
5
,
44
]
, there exists a minimizer to problem (
1.3
) among all
L^{p}
-normalized functions. This implies an
L^{p}
-normalized
eigenfunction set with respect to
\lambda_{1}
:

E_{\lambda_{1}}:=\left\{u\in V~\Big|~\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}u|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}u\cdot\bm{\nabla}v\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}uv\mathrm{d}x,~\forall v\in V,~\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1\right\}.

The numerical treatment of problem (
1.1
) is challenging, due to the degeneracy of the differential operator and the possible presence of geometric singularities of the domain
\Omega
, e.g., reentrant corners. One effective strategy to resolve the challenges is to employ a suitable adaptive technique, e.g., adaptive finite element method (AFEM). The AFEM can achieve the desired accuracy with minimal degrees of freedom, making it a popular tool in scientific and engineering computing. The standard AFEM typically consists of the following four modules in every loop:

\textrm{SOLVE}\to\textrm{ESTIMATE}\to\textrm{MARK}\to\textrm{REFINE}.

(1.4)

Since its first introduction in
[
4
]
, there has been significant progress in mathematical analysis of AFEM. The use of a posteriori estimation, a key component in the ESTIMATE module, is well-documented
[
2
,
57
]
. Over the past three decades, there has been substantial progress on the convergence and complexity of AFEM; see the reviews
[
9
,
17
,
48
]
and the references therein.

In this work, we develop a novel adaptive nonconforming linear FEM, using the Crouzeix-Raviart (C-R) FEM
[
26
]
for computing the first eigenpair of (
1.2
) (cf. Algorithm

), and establish the convergence of the sequence of discrete eigenpairs
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
(cf. Theorems

that measures the discontinuity of the discrete eigenfunction

up to a subsequence (see Lemma

) if the computable quantity tends to zero as

. Moreover, the

[

(see also

Moreover, we prove that a residual functional associated with the discrete eigenpair

vanishes in the limit as a computable quantity related to (
2.4
) tends to zero (see Lemma

). This result relies on an interpolation operator from

to the C-R FE space

,

in Theorem

Hence, the convergence analysis motivates the two estimators in the ESTIMATE module of Algorithm

2
Crouzeix-Raviart FEM

Now we describe the C-R FE space for approximating problem (
1.3
). Let
\mathcal{T}
be a regular conforming
triangulation of
\overline{\Omega}
into a set of closed simplices with a discretization parameter
h_{T}:=|T|^{1/d}
, equivalent to
\mathrm{diam}(T)
, for each
T\in\mathcal{T}
. The set
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}
(respectively

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\Omega)
) consists of all faces (respectively all interior faces) in

\mathcal{T}
. The local mesh size of each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}
is given by
h_{F}:=|F|^{1/(d-1)}
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
denote the set of vertices of
\mathcal{T}
, and let
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}(\Omega)
(resp.
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}({\partial\Omega})
) denote the interior vertices (resp. the boundary vertices). For
T\in\mathcal{T}
,

\bm{n}_{T}
denotes the unit normal of
\partial T
pointing towards the outside of
T
. We assign a fixed unit normal vector
\bm{n}_{F}
to each

F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}
and when
F\subset\partial\Omega
,
\bm{n}_{F}
coincides with the unit outward normal
\bm{n}
to
\partial\Omega
. For
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\Omega)
shared by two elements
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
and
w_{j}=w|_{T_{j}}
with
j=1,2
, we define the jump of
w
on
F
by
[w]:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_{i}(\bm{n}_{T_{i}}|_{F},\bm{n}_{F})
as the jump across an interior face
F
with
(\cdot,\cdot)
the inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For a boundary face
F\subset\partial\Omega
,
[w]:=w
.
Then C-R FE space over
\mathcal{T}
is given by
[
26
]
:

V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}:=\Big\{v\in L^{p}(\Omega):~v|_{T}\in P_{1}(T),\forall T\in\mathcal{T},~\int_{F}[v]\mathrm{d}s=0,\forall F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}\Big\}.

(2.1)

A function in

V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}
is completely determined by its nodal values at the centers of all
interelement faces and it takes zero value at the centers of boundary faces. Over

V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}
, we define a mesh-dependent broken norm:

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{1,p,\mathcal{T}}:=\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{T}|\bm{\nabla}\cdot|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/p}.

It reduces to the usual norm
\|\bm{\nabla}\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
over the
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, by the Poincaré inequality. Moreover, there holds the discrete Poincaré inequality
[
12
, Section 10.6]

[
15
, Corollary 4.3]
over
V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}
:

\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{dp}}\|v\|_{1,p,\mathcal{T}},\quad\forall v\in V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}},

(2.2)

with the positive constant
C_{\mathrm{dp}}
only depending on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}
.

Next, we define the energy functional over
V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{T}}(v):=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{T}|\bm{\nabla}v|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\Big/\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

Then the nonconforming FE approximation of (
1.3
) reads: find
u_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}
such that

\mu_{\mathcal{T}}:=\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{T}}(u_{\mathcal{T}})=\inf_{v\in V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}\setminus\{0\}}\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{T}}(v).

(2.3)

Theorem 1
.

The infimum in (
2.3
) is positive and attained by some nonnegative
u_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

We follow the argument in
[
5
]
for the continuous problem (
1.3
). The discrete Poincaré inequality (
2.2
) implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{T}}(v_{\mathcal{T}})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{T}|\bm{\nabla}v_{\mathcal{T}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\Big/\int_{\Omega}|v_{\mathcal{T}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\geq C_{\mathrm{dp}}^{-p}>0,\quad\forall v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}\setminus\{0\}.

Therefore,
\mu_{\mathcal{T}}
is positive. Let
\{v_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}\subset V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}\setminus\{0\}
be a minimizing sequence. Since
\{|v_{m}|\}_{m\geq 0}
is also a
minimizing sequence, we may assume that
v_{m}\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
for
all
m
. Moreover, the homogeneity of
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{T}}
allows the normalization

\int_{\Omega}|v_{m}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=1
. Then
\{\|v_{m}\|_{1,p,\mathcal{T}}\}_{m\geq 1}
is bounded in the finite dimensional space

V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}
. There exist a subsequence, still denoted by
\{v_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
, and some
u_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}
such that

\|v_{m}-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{1,p,\mathcal{T}}+\|v_{m}-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\to 0,\quad v_{m}\to u_{\mathcal{T}}~\mbox{a.e. in}~\Omega.

This implies
u_{\mathcal{T}}\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
,
\int_{\Omega}|u_{\mathcal{T}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=1
and

\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{T}}(u_{\mathcal{T}})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{T}}(v_{m})=\mu_{\mathcal{T}}
.

The proof shows that the minimizer
u_{\mathcal{T}}
to problem (
2.3
) may be normalized as
\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
in the analysis and the adaptive algorithm.
The differential calculus for
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{T}}
indicates that the solution pair

(\mu_{\mathcal{T}},u_{\mathcal{T}})
given by problem (
2.3
) satisfies the discrete
formulation of problem (
1.2
):

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{T}|\bm{\nabla}u_{\mathcal{T}}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}u_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}v\mathrm{d}x=\mu_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\Omega}|u_{\mathcal{T}}|^{p-2}u_{\mathcal{T}}v\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall v\in V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}.

(2.4)

3
Sufficient condition for convergence

In this section, we derive computable quantities that guarantee the convergence of nonconforming approximations generated by problem (
2.3
). More precisely, we prove that if these quantities vanish asymptotically and the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
is sufficiently refined, the resulting sequence of discrete solutions converges to the solution of (
1.3
).

Let
\mathbb{T}
be the set of all possible conforming triangulations of

\overline{\Omega}
generated from an initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
through successive bisection refinement
[
41
,
48
,
53
]
. This refinement process guarantees uniform shape regularity in
\mathbb{T}
(i.e., the shape regularity of any
\mathcal{T}\in\mathbb{T}
is uniformly bounded by a constant depending on the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
)
[
48
,
55
]
. Thus all subsequent constants depend exclusively on
\mathcal{T}_{0}
and problem data. We call
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
a refinement of

\mathcal{T}
for any
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}
if
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is
produced from
\mathcal{T}
by a finite number of bisections. For
T\in\mathcal{T}
, we denote by
D_{T}
the patch of elements sharing at least one vertex with
T
, i.e.,
D_{T}:=\bigcup\{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}:T^{\prime}\cap T\neq\emptyset\}
. The nonconforming nature of

V_{\mathcal{T}}^{\operatorname{CR}}
results in potential discontinuity across
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\Omega)
, and therefore weak derivatives may not be square integrable. To address this issue, we employ the notation
\bm{\nabla}_{\mathcal{T}}
to represent the piecewise gradient operator over
\mathcal{T}
. Then the broken Sobolev norm can be expressed as
\|\cdot\|^{p}_{1,p,\mathcal{T}}=\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot|^{p}\mathrm{d}x
. The dependence on
\mathcal{T}_{k}
is indicated by the subscript
k
, e.g.,
V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}:=V_{\mathcal{T}_{k}}^{\operatorname{CR}}
.

Let
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
be a sequence of discrete solutions to problem (
2.3
) associated with a sequence of meshes
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{T}
with
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}
being a refinement of

\mathcal{T}_{k}
, that is, the sequence
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{T}
is nested. The discrete pair
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}
satisfies

\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{k}v\mathrm{d}x=\mu_{k}\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}v\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall v\in V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}.

(3.1)

Next, we show that the sequence of eigenvalues
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is bounded from above. Over each
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, we define the standard
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
-conforming linear FE space
V_{k}
by

\displaystyle V_{k}:=\{v\in V|~v|_{T}\in P_{1}(T),~\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}\},

and consider the following minimization problem: find

\widetilde{u}_{k}\in V_{k}
such that

\mathcal{J}_{k}(\widetilde{u}_{k}):=\inf_{v\in V_{k}\setminus\{0\}}\mathcal{J}_{k}(v).

(3.2)

By the argument for Theorem
1
, problem (
3.2
) is well-posed and one can take
\|\widetilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
. Since
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is nested,
\{\mathcal{J}_{k}(\widetilde{u}_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
is a
monotonically decreasing sequence of positive numbers. This and the fact that
\mu_{k}=\mathcal{J}_{k}(u_{k})=\|u_{k}\|_{1,p,k}^{p}\leq\mathcal{J}_{k}(\widetilde{u}_{k})=|\widetilde{u}_{k}|_{1,p}^{p}
(due to
V_{k}\subsetneq V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}
) imply

\mu_{k}^{1/p}=\|u_{k}\|_{1,p,k}\leq|\widetilde{u}_{k}|_{1,p}\leq|\widetilde{u}_{0}|_{1,p}=C,\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(3.3)

Lemma 2
.

Let the sequence
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{T}
be nested, and let
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
be its corresponding sequence of discrete solutions to problem (
2.3
). Suppose that

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}h_{F}\|[u_{k}]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as~}k\to\infty.

(3.4)

Then there exists a subsequence
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
and a limit
w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle u_{k_{j}}\rightarrow

\displaystyle w\qquad\mbox{weakly in}~L^{p}(\Omega),

(3.5)

\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\rightarrow

\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}w\quad\mbox{weakly in}~L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}.

(3.6)

Proof.

The uniform boundedness of
\{u_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
in (
3.3
) and the normalization
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
imply

\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|u_{k}\|_{1,p,k}\leq C
.
Hence, we can extract a subsequence, denoted by
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
, as well as some functions
w\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\bm{\sigma}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
such that

u_{k_{j}}\rightarrow w~\mbox{weakly in}~L^{p}(\Omega),\quad\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\rightarrow\bm{\sigma}~\mbox{weakly in}~L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}.

(3.7)

It suffices to prove
w\in V
with
\bm{\nabla}w=\bm{\sigma}
. By elementwise integration by parts, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}u_{k_{j}}\textrm{div}\bm{\varphi}\mathrm{d}x=-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k_{j}}}\int_{T}\bm{\nabla}u_{k_{j}}\cdot\bm{\varphi}\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}}\int_{F}[u_{k_{j}}]\bm{\varphi}\cdot\bm{n}_{F}\mathrm{d}s,\quad\forall\bm{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}.

(3.8)

Let
\overline{\bm{\varphi}}_{F}:=\int_{F}\bm{\varphi}\mathrm{d}s/|F|
(i.e., the integral mean of
\bm{\varphi}
over
F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}
). Since
\int_{F}[u_{k_{j}}]\mathrm{d}s=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}}\int_{F}[u_{k_{j}}]\bm{\varphi}\cdot\bm{n}_{F}\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}}\int_{F}[u_{k_{j}}](\bm{\varphi}\cdot\bm{n}_{F}-\overline{\bm{\varphi}}_{F}\cdot\bm{n}_{F})\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}}\int_{F}|[u_{k_{j}}]|\mathrm{d}s\|\bm{\varphi}-\overline{\bm{\varphi}}_{F}\|_{L^{\infty}(F)}.

By the interpolation error estimate and Hölder’s inequality, we derive

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}}\int_{F}[u_{k_{j}}]\bm{\varphi}\cdot\bm{n}_{F}\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}}h_{F}\int_{F}|[u_{k_{j}}]|\mathrm{d}s\right)\|\bm{\nabla}\bm{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}}\int_{F}h_{F}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/q}\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}}\int_{F}h_{F}|[u_{k_{j}}]|^{p}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/p}\|\bm{\nabla}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C|\Omega|^{1/q}\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}}h_{F}\|[u_{k_{j}}]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\|\bm{\nabla}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Then inserting (
3.7
) and (
3.4
) into (
3.8
) leads to

\int_{\Omega}w\textrm{div}\bm{\varphi}\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\varphi}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\bm{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}.

This and the definition of distributional gradient yields
w\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
with
\bm{\sigma}=\bm{\nabla}w
in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
. Since
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}
is dense in the space

\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega):=\{\bm{v}\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{d},\mathrm{div}\bm{v}\in L^{q}(\Omega)\}
(see Theorem
12
), we further derive

\int_{\Omega}w\textrm{div}\bm{\varphi}\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\bm{\nabla}w\cdot\bm{\varphi}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\bm{\varphi}\in\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega).

Note that the normal-component trace space of
\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
is

W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
due to Theorems
13
and
14
in the appendix, then an application of the Green’s formula
(
A.3
) implies duality pairing
\langle\bm{\varphi}\cdot\bm{n},w\rangle_{W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega),W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)}=0
for any
\bm{\varphi}\in\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
, i.e.,
w=0
on
\partial\Omega
.

Since
\mu_{k}=\|u_{k}\|_{1,p,{k}}^{p}\leq C
for any
k\geq 0
by (
3.3
), there exists a subsequence, denoted by
\{\mu_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
, and some
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mu_{k_{j}}\to\mu
. In view of Lemma
2
, we shall prove that
(\mu,w)
is an eigenpair of problem (
1.2
). Since
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, it suffices to upgrade the weak convergence in (
3.5
) to a strong one. Using the
W_{0}^{1,p}
-conforming linear FE space
V_{k}
, we define a connection operator
[
11
,
29
]

E_{k}:~V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}\rightarrow V_{k}
by

E_{k}v(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{\#{N}(z)}\displaystyle{\sum_{T\in{N}(z)}v|_{T}(z)}&\quad z\in\mathcal{N}_{k}({\Omega}),\\
0&\quad z\in\mathcal{N}_{k}({\partial\Omega}),\end{array}\right.

(3.9)

where
{N}(z):=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}|\;z\in\partial T\}
is the set of elements sharing the vertex
z
and
\#{N}(z)
is the cardinality of
{N}(z)
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
E_{k}:~V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}\rightarrow V_{k}
be defined in (
3.9
). Then there holds

\displaystyle\|v-E_{k}v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq C\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}h_{F}\|[v]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p},\quad\forall v\in V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}},

(3.10)

where the constant
C
depends only on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
.

Proof.

For any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, let
z\in\mathcal{N}_{k}({\Omega})\cap T
be an interior vertex. Then for any
T^{\prime}\in{N}(z)
, there exists a
sequence of elements
T_{1}=T,\ldots,T_{m}=T^{\prime}
in
{N}(z)
with each consecutive pair

T_{i}
and
T_{i+1}
sharing a common face, where
\#{N}(z)
and
m
are both bounded by a
constant depending on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
. Then by the inverse estimate,

\displaystyle|v|_{T}(z)-v|_{T^{\prime}}(z)|^{p}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left|v|_{T_{i}}(z)-v|_{T_{i}+1}(z)\right|^{p}\leq C\sum_{F\in\mathcal{V}(z)}h^{1-d}_{F}\|[v]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p},

where
\mathcal{V}(z)
is the set of faces
F
with
z
as a common vertex on
\partial F
. Then definition (
3.9
) of
E_{k}
implies

\displaystyle|v|_{T}(z)-E_{k}v(z)|^{p}

\displaystyle=\big|v|_{T}(z)-\frac{1}{\#{N}(z)}\sum_{T^{\prime}\in{N}(z)}v|_{T^{\prime}}(z)\big|^{p}\leq C\sum_{F\in\mathcal{V}(z)}h^{1-d}_{F}\|[v]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}.

(3.11)

Similarly, for any boundary vertex
z\in\mathcal{N}_{k}{(\partial\Omega)}\cap T
, with
z\in F\subset T\cap\partial\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle|v|_{T}(z)-E_{k}v(z)|^{p}

\displaystyle=|v|_{T}(z)|^{p}\leq Ch^{1-d}_{F}\|[v]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}.

(3.12)

On any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, the norm equivalence and the local quasi-uniformity of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
imply

\displaystyle\|v-E_{k}v\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}\leq Ch_{T}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{k}\cap T}|v(z)-E_{k}v(z)|^{p},

which, together with (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), yields

\displaystyle\|v-E_{k}v\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{T}^{d}\Big(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(\Omega)\cap T}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{V}(z)}h_{F}^{1-d}\|[v]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(\partial\Omega)\cap T}\sum_{F\subset\partial T}h_{F}^{1-d}\|[v]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(\Omega)\cap T}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{V}(z)}h_{F}\|[v]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(\partial\Omega)\cap T}\sum_{F\subset\partial T}h_{F}\|[v]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}\Big).

(3.13)

Then by summing up (
3.13
) over all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, we obtain the desired estimate.

Remark 3.1
.

The proof of Lemma
3
is similar to that in
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]
,
[
47
, Lemma 3.2]
with
p=2
and
[
12
, Lemma 10.6.6]
(for piecewise constant functions). By adapting the argument, we can also derive

\displaystyle\|v-E_{k}v\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}\leq Ch_{T}^{p}\|\bm{\nabla}_{k}v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(D_{T})},\quad\forall v\in V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}.

This, the inverse estimate and the finite overlapping property of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
yield the stability of
E_{k}
:

|E_{k}v|_{1,p}\leq C\|v\|_{1,p,k},\quad\forall v\in V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}},

(3.14)

with the positive constant
C
depending only on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let condition (
3.4
) hold. Then the
L^{p}
strong convergence holds for the weakly convergent subsequence
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
in Lemma
2
, i.e.

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|u_{k_{j}}-w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0.

(3.15)

Proof.

The proof relies on the connection operator
E_{k}
defined in (
3.9
). The Poincaré inequality, (
3.14
) and (
3.3
) imply that
\{\|E_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\}_{j\geq 0}
is uniformly bounded. By Sobolev compact embedding theorem, there exist a Cauchy subsequence, still denoted by
\{E_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
, and some
\widehat{w}\in V
, satisfying

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|E_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}-\widehat{w}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0.

(3.16)

Hence, Lemma
3
and condition (
3.4
) lead to

\|u_{k_{j}}-E_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{j}}}h_{F}\|[u_{k_{j}}]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\to 0\quad\text{as}~j\to\infty.

(3.17)

Finally, by combining the estimates (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), we derive

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\|u_{k_{j}}-\widehat{w}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0.

The uniqueness of the weak limit in Lemma
2
implies
\widehat{w}=w
, and thus the assertion (
3.15
) follows.

In Lemmas
2
-
4
, the computable
quantity
\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}h_{F}\|[u_{k}]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}
measures the nonconformity of
discrete solutions since
V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}\nsubseteqq V
, and plays a very important role.
The discrete compactness of C-R eigenfunctions
\{u_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
over locally refined meshes
is recovered in Lemma
4
under the condition that
\{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}h_{F}\|[u_{k}]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}\}_{k\geq 0}
is a null sequence. The compactness property of nonconforming FE spaces on uniformly refined meshes was
first established in
[
54
]
. Our result can be viewed as a version of the result for the adaptive mesh refinement strategy with
\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}h_{F}\|[u_{k}]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}\to 0
replacing the vanishing mesh size.

Next we relate the limit pair
(\mu,w)
to an eigenpair of the continuous problem (
1.2
). This involves an error indicator associated with problem (
3.1
). First, we define a linear functional associated with
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{R}(\mu_{k},u_{k}),v\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}\cdot\bm{\nabla}v\mathrm{d}x-\mu_{k}\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}v\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall v\in V.

We define an interpolation operator
{I}_{k}:V\rightarrow V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}

[
26
,
49
]
, which satisfies

\int_{F}{I}_{k}v\mathrm{d}s=\int_{F}v\mathrm{d}s,\quad\forall F\in\mathcal{F}_{k},~\forall v\in V.

(3.18)

This operator satisfies the following stability estimate and the first-order approximation property
[
49
, Lemma 2]
:
for any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
and any
v\in V
, there hold

\|\bm{\nabla}{I}_{k}v\|_{L^{p}(T)}\leq\|\bm{\nabla}v\|_{L^{p}(T)},\quad\|v-{I}_{k}v\|_{L^{p}(T)}\leq(1+2/d)\mathrm{diam}(T)\|\bm{\nabla}v\|_{L^{p}(T)}.

(3.19)

Then we get a lower bound on the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of problem (
1.2
) in terms of
\mu_{k}
.

Theorem 5
.

Let the sequence
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{T}
be nested, and let
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
be its associated sequence of discrete solutions
to problem (
2.3
). If
\displaystyle(1+d/2)\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\mathrm{diam}(T)\lambda_{1}^{1/p}<1
, then there holds

\frac{\mu_{k}^{1/p}}{\displaystyle\left(1+(1+d/2)\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\mathrm{diam}(T)\mu_{k}^{1/p}\right)}\leq\lambda_{1}^{1/p}.

(3.20)

Proof.

For any
u\in E_{\lambda_{1}}
, by taking
v_{k}={I}_{k}u
in (
2.3
) over
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, using the stability
estimate (
3.19
) and assuming
\displaystyle(1+d/2)\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\mathrm{diam}(T)\lambda_{1}^{1/p}<1
, we get

\mu_{k}=\|\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq\frac{\|\bm{\nabla}_{k}{I}_{k}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|{I}_{k}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}\leq\frac{\|\bm{\nabla}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|{I}_{k}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{(1-C_{\mathrm{apx}}\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\mathrm{diam}(T)\lambda_{1}^{1/p})^{p}},

with
C_{\mathrm{apx}}=1+2/d
, from which, the desired lower bound follows.

Lemma 6
.

Let the sequence
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{T}
be nested, and let
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
be its associated sequence of discrete solutions to problem (
2.3
). Suppose that

\mu_{k}^{q}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}h_{T}^{q}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}\to 0~\quad\mbox{as}~k\to\infty.

(3.21)

Then for the whole sequence
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
, there holds

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{R}(\mu_{k},u_{k}),v\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall v\in V.

(3.22)

Proof.

For any
v\in V
, we test (
3.1
) with
{I}_{k}v\in V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}
and perform elementwise integration by parts, together with the identity
\mathrm{div}(|\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k})=0
on each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, then we derive

\displaystyle\quad\left|\langle\mathcal{R}(\mu_{k},u_{k}),v\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{k}(v-{I}_{k}v)\mathrm{d}x-\mu_{k}\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}(v-{I}_{k}v)\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}-\mu_{k}\int_{T}|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}(v-{I}_{k}v)\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\sum_{F\subset\partial T}\int_{F}|\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}\cdot\bm{n}_{T}(v-{I}_{k}v)\mathrm{d}s\right|.

Next, since
|\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k}{u_{k}}
is a constant vector on
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, the definition (
3.18
) directly implies

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\sum_{F\subset\partial T}\int_{F}|\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}\cdot\bm{n}_{T}(v-{I}_{k}v)\mathrm{d}s=0.

Note that
\mathrm{diam}(T)\leq Ch_{T}
due to the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, then a combination of the identity
q=p/(p-1)
, Hölder’s inequality and the approximation property in (
3.19
), leads to

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathcal{R}(\mu_{k},u_{k}),v\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\mu_{k}\left\||u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}\right\|_{L^{q}(T)}\|v-{I}_{k}v\|_{L^{p}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}h_{T}^{q}\left\|\mu_{k}|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}\right\|_{L^{q}(T)}^{q}\Big)^{1/{q}}\|\bm{\nabla}v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Finally, condition (
3.21
) implies the desired assertion (
3.22
).

We define two nonlinear mappings
\mathcal{F}(\bm{\tau}):=|\bm{\tau}|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}
:
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}

\to

L^{q}(\Omega)^{d}
and
\mathcal{G}(v):=|v|^{p-2}v
:
L^{p}(\Omega)

\to

L^{q}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 7
.

The mappings
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are both continuous with respect to the
L^{p}
-norm.

Proof.

We take any sequence
\{\bm{\tau}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
such that
\bm{\tau}_{n}\to\bm{\tau}
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
as
n\to\infty
. Then by Fischer-Riesz Theorem (cf.
[
5
, Theorem 1.2.7]
,
[
13
, Theorem 4.9]
), there exist a subsequence
\{\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
and
w_{1}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying

\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}\to\bm{\tau}
a.e. in
\Omega
and
|\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}|^{p}\leq w_{1}
a.e. in
\Omega
for all
j
. This implies

\displaystyle\left|\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}\right|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}

\displaystyle\to|\bm{\tau}|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}\quad\mbox{a.e. in}~\Omega\quad\text{as}~j\to\infty,

\displaystyle\left||\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}-|\bm{\tau}|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq(|\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}|^{p-1}+|\bm{\tau}|^{p-1})^{q}\leq C(|\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}|^{p}+|\bm{\tau}|^{p})\leq C(w_{1}+|\bm{\tau}|^{p})\in L^{1}(\Omega).

Then by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}||\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}-|\bm{\tau}|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\to 0\quad\text{as}~j\to\infty.

Since for every sequence
\bm{\tau}_{n}\to\bm{\tau}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
as
n\to\infty
, there exists a subsequence
\{\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
such that
|\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}_{n_{j}}\to|\bm{\tau}|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}
in
L^{q}(\Omega)^{d}
as
j\to\infty
, the standard subsequence contradiction argument implies that the whole sequence also satisfies
|\bm{\tau}_{n}|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}_{n}\to|\bm{\tau}|^{p-2}\bm{\tau}

in
L^{q}(\Omega)^{d}
as
n\to\infty
. This argument applies also to the strong continuity of
\mathcal{G}
from
L^{p}(\Omega)
to
L^{q}(\Omega)
.

Now we can state the first main result of this section.

Theorem 8
.

Let the sequence
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{T}
be nested, and let
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
be the associated sequence of discrete solutions to problem (
2.3
). Then under the conditions (
3.4
) and (
3.21
), i.e.,

\mu_{k}^{q}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}h_{T}^{q}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}h_{F}\|[u_{k}]\|^{p}_{L^{p}(F)}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}~k\to\infty,

(3.23)

there exist a subsequence
\{(\mu_{k_{j}},u_{k_{j}})\}_{j\geq 0}
and a pair
(\mu,w)\in\mathbb{R}\times V
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mu_{k_{j}}=\mu,\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}\|u_{k_{j}}-w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0,\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}\|u_{k_{j}}-w\|_{1,p,k_{j}}=0.

(3.24)

Proof.

First, the inequality (
3.3
) implies boundedness of the discrete eigenvalues:

\displaystyle\mu_{k}=\||\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}=\|u_{k}\|^{p}_{1,p,k}\leq C.

Then there exist two subsequences
\{\mu_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
and
\{|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
with limits
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\bm{\xi}\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{d}
such that

\mu_{k_{j}}\to\mu\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\rightarrow\bm{\xi}\quad\mbox{weakly in}~L^{q}(\Omega)^{d}.

(3.25)

Thus, the first convergence in (
3.24
) follows.
Next, by Lemmas
2
and
4
, there exist a subsequence, still denoted by
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
, and
w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

u_{k_{j}}\rightarrow w\quad\mbox{strongly in}~L^{p}(\Omega),\quad\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\rightarrow\bm{\nabla}w\quad\mbox{weakly in}~L^{p}(\Omega)^{d},

(3.26)

i.e., the second convergence in (
3.24
) holds.
Now (
3.25
), the
L^{p}
strong convergence in (
3.26
) and Lemma
7
lead to

\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle\mathcal{R}(u_{k_{j}},\mu_{k_{j}}),v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{\nabla}v\mathrm{d}x-\mu\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p-2}wv\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall v\in V.

Condition (
3.23
) and Lemma
6
imply
\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle\mathcal{R}(u_{k_{j}},\mu_{k_{j}}),v\rangle=0
. Consequently,

\int_{\Omega}\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{\nabla}v\mathrm{d}x=\mu\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p-2}wv\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall v\in V.

(3.27)

Since
\|u_{k_{j}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, together with the strong convergence in (
3.26
), there holds
\|w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
. Taking
v=w
in (
3.27
) gives

\int_{\Omega}\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{\nabla}w\mathrm{d}x=\mu.

(3.28)

Note that
|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{d}
due to
w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, by the weak convergence in (
3.26
), we derive

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}w|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}w\cdot(\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}-\bm{\nabla}w)\mathrm{d}x=0.

Moreover, by testing (
3.1
) with
u_{k_{j}}\in V_{k_{j}}^{\operatorname{CR}}
, the normalization
\|u_{k_{j}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
and (
3.25
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mu_{k_{j}}=\mu.

Combining with the weak convergence in (
3.25
) and (
3.28
), we derive

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}w\mathrm{d}x=\mu-\int_{\Omega}\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{\nabla}w\mathrm{d}x=0.

Now by Hölder’s inequality, we derive

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}-|\bm{\nabla}w|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}w)\cdot(\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}-\bm{\nabla}w)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p}+|\bm{\nabla}w|^{p}-|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}w-|\bm{\nabla}w|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}w\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}w|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\left(\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}w|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}w|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}_{k_{j}}u_{k_{j}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\|u_{k_{j}}\|_{1,p,k_{j}}^{p-1}-\|\bm{\nabla}w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\right)\left(\|u_{k_{j}}\|_{1,p,k_{j}}-\|\bm{\nabla}w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)\geq 0.

This and the two vanishing limits yield

\|u_{k_{j}}\|_{1,p,k_{j}}\to\|\bm{\nabla}w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
as
j\to\infty
.
Together with the weak convergence in (
3.26
) and the uniform convexity of
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
, this gives the third result in (
3.24
).

Remark 3.2
.

Theorem 9
.

Let the sequence
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{T}
be nested, and let
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
be the associated sequence of discrete solutions to problem (
2.3
).
Suppose that the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
is sufficiently fine and condition (
3.23
) is satisfied. Then there holds

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}=\lambda_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\inf_{v\in E_{\lambda_{1}}}\|u_{k}-v\|_{1,p,k}=0.

(3.29)

Proof.

The proof is divided into four steps.

Step 1.
Let
(\mu,w)\in\mathbb{R}\times V
be defined in (
3.24
). Then Lemmas
6
and
7
and Theorem
8
imply

\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}w|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}w\cdot\bm{\nabla}v\mathrm{d}x=\mu\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p-2}wv\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall~v\in V,

(3.30)

i.e.
(\mu,w)
is an eigenpair of problem (
1.2
) with
\|w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
.

Step 2.
Let
E:=\{v\in V|~v~\mbox{satisfies \eqref{vp_eigen} with}~\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1~\text{for some}~\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}
, which consists of all eigenfunctions of problem (
1.2
). Since problem (
1.2
) has a nondecreasing sequence of positive eigenvalues
[
36
,
42
,
44
]
, there holds
E_{\lambda_{1}}\subsetneq E
. Since any
u\in E_{\lambda_{1}}
is also a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}
over
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
\lambda_{1}=\mathcal{J}(u)\leq\inf_{v\in E\setminus E_{\lambda_{1}}}\mathcal{J}(v)
. Suppose that the equality is attained. Let

\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\subset E\setminus E_{\lambda_{1}}
be a minimizing sequence such that

\mathcal{J}(w_{n})\to\inf_{v\in E\setminus E_{\lambda_{1}}}\mathcal{J}(v)=\lambda_{1}
. Then
\mathcal{J}(w_{n})
is also a sequence of eigenvalues other than
\lambda_{1}
. This contradicts the fact that
\lambda_{1}
is isolated
[
44
]
. Hence
\lambda_{1}=\mathcal{J}(u)<\inf_{v\in E\setminus E_{\lambda_{1}}}\mathcal{J}(v)
for any
u\in E_{\lambda_{1}}
.

Step 3.
Recall that
V_{k}
is the
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
-conforming linear FE space over
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, and
that
\widetilde{u}_{k}\in V_{k}
is the minimizer defined in (
3.2
). Let
V_{\infty}:=\overline{\bigcup_{k\geq 0}V_{k}}

in the
W^{1,p}
-norm. If
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is a sequence of uniformly refined meshes, then for any

u\in E_{\lambda_{1}}
there exists a sequence
\{v_{l}\}_{l\geq 0}\subset V_{\infty}
such that
v_{l}\to u
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Note that
\mathcal{J}
is continuous over

W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{J}(u)<\inf_{v\in E\setminus E_{\lambda_{1}}}\mathcal{J}(v)
as proved in Step 2, we derive
\mathcal{J}(v_{l})<\inf_{v\in E\setminus E_{\lambda_{1}}}\mathcal{J}(v)
for sufficiently large
l
or a sufficiently small mesh size of
\mathcal{T}_{l}
. With (
3.2
) and the inclusive property
V_{k}\subsetneq V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}
invoked, a fine enough initial
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, in the sequence of
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
, guarantees that
\mu_{k}=\mathcal{J}_{k}(u_{k})\leq\mathcal{J}_{k}(\widetilde{u}_{k})\leq\mathcal{J}_{0}(\widetilde{u}_{0})<\inf_{v\in E\setminus E_{\lambda_{1}}}\mathcal{J}(v)
. Taking
v=w
in (
3.30
) and noting
\mu_{{k}_{j}}\to\mu
in (
3.24
) as well as
\|w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, we get
\mathcal{J}(w)=\mu<\inf_{v\in E\setminus E_{\lambda_{1}}}\mathcal{J}(v)
, which, along with (
3.30
) and
\|w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
again, implies
w\in E_{\lambda_{1}}
and
\mu=\lambda_{1}
. Moreover, this argument shows that
\widetilde{\mu}=\lambda_{1}
for any convergent subsequence of
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
with a limit pair
(\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{w})
. So the limit
\lambda_{1}
is independent of a particular subsequence. This leads to the first result in (
3.29
).

Step 4.
To prove the second result in (
3.29
), we also argue by contradiction. If the result is false, then there exist a number
\varepsilon>0
and a subsequence
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
of
\{u_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
such that
\inf_{v\in E_{\lambda_{1}}}\|u_{k_{j}}-v\|_{1,p,k_{j}}\geq\varepsilon
for all
k_{j}
. By Remark
3.2
, we can extract a subsequence of discrete eigenfunctions
\{u_{k_{j_{m}}}\}_{m\geq 0}
converging to some
\widetilde{w}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and a subsequence of corresponding eigenvalues
\{\mu_{k_{j_{m}}}\}_{m\geq 0}
to some
\widetilde{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
. By repeating the argument in Steps 1-3, we deduce that
(\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{w})
satisfies (
3.30
) with
\widetilde{\mu}=\lambda_{1}
and
\|\widetilde{w}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, i.e.,
\widetilde{w}\in E_{\lambda_{1}}
. This contradicts the assumption that
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}
does not converge to any eigenfunction in
E_{\lambda_{1}}
.

4
Adaptive algorithm

Theorem
9
implies that condition (
3.23
) on the computable quantities (and a sufficiently fine initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
) ensures the convergence of relevant C-R FE approximations. Motivated by this observation, we use
the computable quantity to design an adaptive algorithm to approximate problem (
1.3
) even if it is not a reliable bound of the error. To this end, we first introduce some notation. For any
(\nu,v)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times V_{k}^{\operatorname{CR}}
and any

\eta_{k,1}(\nu,v,T):=\nu^{q}h_{T}^{q}\|v\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p},\quad\eta_{k,2}(v,T):=\sum_{F\subset\partial T\cap\Omega}\frac{1}{2}h_{F}\|[v]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}+\sum_{F\subset\partial T\cap\partial\Omega}h_{F}\|[v]\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p},

and two global error estimators

\displaystyle\eta_{k,1}(\nu,v,\mathcal{M}):=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{M}}\eta_{k,1}(\nu,v,T)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta_{k,2}(v,\mathcal{M}):=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{M}}\eta_{k,2}(v,T),

for any
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{k}
. If
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{T}_{k}
, then it is dropped.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\{(\mu_{k},u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence of discrete eigenpairs and
\{\mathcal{M}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
the sequence of marked sets generated by Algorithm
1
. Then the sequence of estimators
\{\eta_{k,1}(\mu_{k},u_{k})+\eta_{k,2}(u_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
generated by Algorithm
1

\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{k,1}(\mu_{k},u_{k})+\eta_{k,2}(u_{k})=0.

(4.7)

Hence, condition (
3.23
) holds.

Proof.

Let
\eta_{k}(\mu_{k},u_{k}):=\eta_{k,1}(\mu_{k},u_{k})+\eta_{k,2}(u_{k})
. Then it can be split into

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(\mu_{k},u_{k})=\eta_{k}(\mu_{k},u_{k},\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+})+\eta_{k}(\mu_{k},u_{k},\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+})\quad\text{with }k>l.

(4.8)

Similar to the proof of Lemma
10
, the upper bound (
3.3
) and the normalization
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
yield

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(\mu_{k},u_{k},\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+})\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+}}\left(h_{T}^{q}\mu_{k}^{q}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}+\sum_{F\subset\partial T}h_{F}\|[u_{k}]\|^{p}_{L^{p}(F)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+}}h_{T}^{q}\mu_{k}^{q}\|u_{k}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(T)}+C\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+}}h_{T}^{p}\int_{D_{T}}|\bm{\nabla}_{k}u_{k}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+}}h_{T}^{q}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+}}h_{T}^{p}\|u_{k}\|^{p}_{1,p,k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+}}h_{T}^{\min(p,q)}.

Note that
\displaystyle{\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+}}}h_{T}\leq\|h_{l}\chi_{l}^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
when
k>l
. Then the property (
4.2
) implies

\eta_{k}(\mu_{k},u_{k},\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+})<\varepsilon/2
for any given positive
\varepsilon
when
l
is sufficiently large. Note also that
\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+}\subset\mathcal{T}_{k}^{+}\subset\mathcal{T}_{k}
and
\mathcal{M}_{k}\cap\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+}=\emptyset
for
k>l
. Then the marking requirement (
4.1
) implies

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(\mu_{k},u_{k},\mathcal{T}_{l}^{+})

\displaystyle\leq\#\mathcal{T}^{+}_{l}\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}^{+}_{l}}\eta_{k}(\mu_{k},u_{k},T)\leq\#\mathcal{T}^{+}_{l}\max_{T\in\mathcal{M}_{k}}\eta_{k}(\mu_{k},u_{k},T)

\displaystyle\leq\#\mathcal{T}^{+}_{l}\left(\max_{T\in\mathcal{M}_{k}}\eta_{k,1}(\mu_{k},u_{k},T)+\max_{T\in\mathcal{M}_{k}}\eta_{k,2}(u_{k},T)\right).

By Lemma
10
, we can choose some
K>l

6
Conclusion

We propose an adaptive finite element method based on Crouzeix-Raviart elements to compute the first Dirichlet eigenpair of the
p
-Laplacian. The method relies on two error indicators that quantitatively characterize the residual and the nonconformity associated with the discrete eigenpair. We prove the strong convergence of the sequence of discrete eigenpairs generated by the adaptive algorithm. The numerical tests confirm the theoretical findings. The analysis crucially relies on the discrete compactness of Crouzeix-Raviart finite element eigenfunctions over a sequence of adaptively generated meshes. This interesting property may be useful in the study of adaptive nonconforming finite element methods for other nonlinear eigenvalue problems, e.g., the ground state of Bose-Einstein condensates.

In the appendix, we extend several results on the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space
\bm{H}(\mathrm{div};\Omega):=\{\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d},\mathrm{div}\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}
in
[
38
]
(cf. Theorem 2.4, Theorem 2.5 and Corollary 2.8 on pages 27-28) to the
L^{q}
-case (
1<q<\infty
). Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open bounded domain with a Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. The
L^{q}
-version of
\bm{H}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
is defined by

\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega):=\{\bm{v}\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{d},\mathrm{div}\bm{v}\in L^{q}(\Omega)\}

equipped with the graph norm
\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div})}:=(\|\bm{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\|\mathrm{div}\bm{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q})^{1/q}
. For the space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
p=q/(q-1)
, we also need its trace space
W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)
and dual space
W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
.

Theorem 12
.

The space
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}
is dense in
\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
\ell\in\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)^{\prime}
, the dual space of
\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
. By the Riesz representation theorem, there exists an
\bm{\ell}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
and an
\ell_{d+1}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
(
p=q/(q-1)
) such that

\ell(\bm{v})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\ell_{i}v_{i}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\ell_{d+1}\mathrm{div}\bm{v}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega).

(A.1)

Now we assume that
\ell(\bm{v})=0
for any
\bm{v}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}
and let
\tilde{\ell}_{i}
denote the extension of
\ell_{i}
by zero outside
\Omega
. Then (
A.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tilde{\ell}_{i}v_{i}+\tilde{\ell}_{d+1}\mathrm{div}\bm{v}\right)\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad\forall\bm{v}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d},

which implies that in the sense of distributions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\tilde{\bm{\ell}}=\bm{\nabla}\tilde{\ell}_{d+1}
,
with
\tilde{\bm{\ell}}=(\tilde{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\ell}_{d})^{T}
. Hence
\tilde{\ell}_{d+1}\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
since
\tilde{\bm{\ell}}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}
. As
\tilde{\ell}_{d+1}
is also the zero extension of
\ell_{d+1}
, it follows from
[
38
, Theorem 1.2, Chapter I]
(also cf.
[
1
]
) that
\ell_{d+1}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. The density of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
admits that there exists a sequence
\{\psi_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
converging to
\ell_{d+1}
in
W^{1,p}
-norm, which, along with the distributional definition of
\mathrm{div}
, yields

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(\bm{\nabla}\psi_{m}\cdot\bm{v}+\psi_{m}\mathrm{div}\bm{v}\right)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\left(\bm{\nabla}\ell_{d+1}\cdot\bm{v}+\ell_{d+1}\mathrm{div}\bm{v}\right)\mathrm{d}x=\ell(\bm{v}),\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega).

Therefore
\ell
also vanishes on
\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
. The desired density follows.

Theorem 13
.

The normal-component trace operator
\gamma_{n}:\bm{v}\mapsto\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}
is continuous from
\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
to
W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

Note that the following integration by parts formula holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\psi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\bm{v}\psi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial\Omega}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\psi\mathrm{d}s,\quad\forall\bm{v}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{d},\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}).

Since
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is dense in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)

(p=q/(q-1))
, the identity defines a linear functional on
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
satisfying

\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\psi\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div})}\|\psi\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)},\quad\forall\bm{v}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{d},\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

Note that the trace mapping
\gamma:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)
is bounded and surjective. Then the open mapping theorem implies

\inf_{v\in\mathrm{ker}\gamma}\|\psi+v\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\gamma\psi\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)},\quad\forall\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

(A.2)

Since
\mathrm{ker}\gamma=W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a closed subspace of
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, by the uniform convexity of
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
(
1<p<\infty
), we deduce that the infimum in (
A.2
) is attained at a unique
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Thus for any
g\in W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)
, there exists a
\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
\psi=g
on
\partial\Omega
and

\left|\langle\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n},g\rangle_{W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega),W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)}\right|\leq\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div})}\|\psi\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div})}\|g\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)},

for any
\bm{v}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}
. Thus

\|\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\|_{W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)}
, and the linear mapping
\gamma_{n}:\bm{v}\mapsto\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}
is continuous from
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}
to
W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
. Since
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}
is dense in
\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
(cf. Theorem
12
), we may extend
\gamma_{n}
continuously, still denoted by
\gamma_{n}
, from
\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
to
W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
.

Remark A.1
.

Theorems
12
and
13
imply the following Green’s formula: for any
\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
,
\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
1/p+1/q=1
,

\int_{\Omega}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\psi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\bm{v}\psi\mathrm{d}x=\langle\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n},\psi\rangle_{W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega),W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)}.

(A.3)

Theorem 14
.

The normal-component trace operator
\gamma_{n}:\bm{v}\mapsto\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}
is
surjective from
\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
to
W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

Consider the following
p
-Laplacian problem: given
g\in W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
, find
\phi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\mathrm{div}(|\bm{\nabla}\phi|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}\phi)+|\phi|^{p-2}\phi&=0&&\mbox{in}~\Omega,\\
|\bm{\nabla}\phi|^{p-2}\partial_{n}\phi&=g&&\mbox{on}~\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(A.4)

Let
\mathcal{I}(\psi)=\int_{\Omega}|\bm{\nabla}\psi|^{p}\mathrm{d}x/p+\int_{\Omega}|\psi|^{p}\mathrm{d}x/p-\langle g,\psi\rangle_{W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega),W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)}
. Then problem (
A.4
) is equivalent to a minimization problem: find
\phi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\mathcal{I}(\phi)=\inf_{\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\mathcal{I}(\psi).

(A.5)

The trace theorem implies that on
W^{1,p}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\psi)

\displaystyle\geq(\|\psi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|\bm{\nabla}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p})/p-\|g\|_{W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq(\|\psi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|\bm{\nabla}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p})/p-C\|g\|_{W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Since
p>1
,
\mathcal{I}
is coercive. Now assume that
\psi_{n}\to\psi
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. The weak convergence also holds in
W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)
due to the continuity of the trace operator from
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
to
W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)
. So we have

\mathcal{I}(\psi)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{I}(\psi_{n}),

i.e.,
\mathcal{I}
is weakly lower semi-continuous in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Clearly,
\mathcal{I}
is strictly convex. By the direct method in calculus of variations
[
27
]
, problem (
A.5
) has a unique minimizer. This gives a unique weak solution of problem (
A.4
).
By setting
\bm{v}=|\bm{\nabla}\phi|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}\phi\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{d}
, we get from (
A.4
) that
\mathrm{div}\bm{v}=|\phi|^{p-2}\phi\in L^{q}(\Omega)
and
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=g
. That is, for any
g\in W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
there exists a
\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
such that
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=g
on
\partial\Omega
.

Remark A.2
.

The variational formulation of problem (
A.4
) further implies

\|\phi\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\langle g,\phi\rangle_{W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega),W^{1/q,p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)}\|\phi\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Since
\bm{v}=|\bm{\nabla}\phi|^{p-2}\bm{\nabla}\phi
,
\mathrm{div}\bm{v}=|\phi|^{p-2}\phi
and
q=p/(p-1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div})}\leq C\|g\|_{W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)}=C\|\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\|_{W^{-1/q,q}(\Omega)},

which, together with Theorems
13
and
14
, further implies that
\gamma_{n}:\bm{W}^{q}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)\to W^{-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
is an isomorphism with a bounded inverse.

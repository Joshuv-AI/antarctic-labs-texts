Out ​ ( F r ) \textup{Out}(F_{r}) train track automata I: Proper full fold decompositions
Catherine Eva Pfaff

Abstract
. We describe train track automata for large classes of fully irreducible elements of Out ​ ( F r ) \textup{Out}(F_{r}) , and their associated geodesics in Culler-Vogtmann Outer Space.

2.
Background

Assume throughout this section that
\Gamma
is a finite oriented graph where each vertex has valence
\geq 3
and
F_{r}
is a free group of rank
r\geq 3
. This section will contain explanations of background given in previous works, included here for convenience. Expository overlap may occur.

2.1.
Edge Maps on Graphs

Suppose
\Gamma
has positively oriented edges
\{e_{1},e_{2}\dots,e_{n}\}
and vertices
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{m}\}
. We use the notation
E\Gamma:=\{e_{1},e_{2}\dots,e_{n}\}
for the positively oriented edge set, and
V\Gamma:=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{m}\}
for the vertex set, and
E^{\pm}\Gamma=\{e_{1},\overline{e_{1}},\dots,e_{n},\overline{e_{n}}\}
for the full directed edge set, with an overline indicating a reversal of orientation.

2.1.1.
Directions, turns, & edge paths

Given
v\in V\Gamma
, a
direction at
v
is an element of
E^{\pm}\Gamma
with initial vertex
v
. For each
v\in V\Gamma
, let
\mathcal{D}(v)
denote the set of directions at
v
and
\mathcal{D}\Gamma:=\cup_{V\Gamma}\mathcal{D}(v)
. A
turn at
v
is an unordered pair
\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
of directions at
v
and is
degenerate
if
d_{1}=d_{2}
.

\parpic

[r]

An
edge path
(or just
path
)
\rho
in
\Gamma
is a finite sequence
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{\ell})\in(E^{\pm}\Gamma)^{\ell}
such that there
exists a sequence
(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{\ell-1})\in(V\Gamma)^{\ell-1}
satisfying that the turn
\{\overline{a_{j}},a_{j+1}\}
is a turn at
v_{j}
for each
j\in\{1,2,\dots,\ell-1\}
. For such a path
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m})
we write
\gamma=a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{m}
and say
\gamma

contains
the oriented edges
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m}
and
takes
the turns
\{\overline{a_{1}},a_{2}\}
,
\{\overline{a_{2}},a_{3}\}
,
\dots
,
\{\overline{a_{n-1}},a_{n}\}
. Note that
\overline{\gamma}=\overline{a_{1}}~\overline{a_{2}}\dots\overline{a_{m}}
take the same set of turns. We call
\gamma

tight
if it takes no degenerate turns, colloquially described as there being no “backtracking.” Fixing endpoints, there is a unique tight path in the homotopy class of each path
\gamma
. We say this path is obtained from
\gamma
by
tightening

\gamma
.

2.1.2.
Graph maps

An
edge (or graph) map

g:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
is

•

a map
\mathcal{V}:V\Gamma\to V\Gamma^{\prime}
, where we write
g(v)
for
\mathcal{V}(v)
, together with

•

for each
e\in E^{\pm}\Gamma
, an assignment of a path
g(e)
in
\Gamma^{\prime}
such that

(1)

if the initial vertex of
e
is
v
, then the initial vertex of
g(e)
is
g(v)
, and

(2)

if
g(e)
is the edge path
g(e)=a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{m}
, then
g(\overline{e})
is the edge path
g(\overline{e})=\overline{a_{m}}\dots\overline{a_{2}}~\overline{a_{1}}
.

Viewing
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
as topological spaces,
g
is a continuous map sending vertices to vertices.

g_{\#}(e)
is obtained from
g(e)
by tightening, as in §
2.1.1
.

2.1.3.
Taken turns & tight maps

Given a graph map
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
, we define

\tau(g):=\{\{d_{1},d_{2}\}\mid\{d_{1},d_{2}\}\text{ is taken by }g(e)\text{ for some }e\in E\Gamma\}

and say each turn
T\in\tau(g)
is
g
-taken
. Let

\tau_{\infty}(g)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\tau(g^{k})

and say each turn
T\in\tau_{\infty}(g)
is
g_{\infty}
-taken
.

We then call
g

tight
if the image of each edge is a tight path. In particular, no degenerate turns are
g
-taken. If
\gamma=a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{n}
is a path in
\Gamma
for some
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\in E^{\pm}\Gamma
, then
g(\gamma)
will mean the concatenation of edge paths
g(\gamma)=g(a_{1})g(a_{2})\dots g(a_{n})
. Note that
g(\gamma)
is tight if and only if
\gamma
is tight and
g
is locally injective on
\gamma
.

2.1.4.
Directions maps & gate structures

To
g
we associate a
direction map

Dg:\mathcal{D}\Gamma\to\mathcal{D}\Gamma^{\prime}
such that if
g(e)=a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{m}
, for some
m\geq 1
and
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m}\in E^{\pm}\Gamma
, then
Dg(e)=a_{1}
.
We call a direction
e

periodic
if
Dg^{k}(e)=e
for some
k>0
, and
fixed
if
k=1
. When
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
is a self-map, the turn
\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
is an
illegal turn
for
g
if
\{Dg^{k}(d_{1}),Dg^{k}(d_{2})\}
is degenerate for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Defining an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{D}\Gamma
by
d_{1}\sim d_{2}
when
\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
is an illegal turn, the equivalence classes are
gates
and the partitioning of the directions at each vertex into gates is the
induced gate structure
.
Note that each gate at a periodic vertex contains a unique periodic direction.

2.1.5.
Markings & representatives

Viewing
g
as a continuous map of graphs, we say
g

represents

\varphi
when
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
has been identified with
F_{r}
(i.e.
\Gamma
is
marked
) and
\varphi
is the induced map of fundamental groups. When a marking is not explicitly given, we mean “there exists a marking such that.”

2.2.
Train track (tt) maps and fully irreducible outer automorphisms

2.2.1.
Train track (tt) maps

Suppose
g:\Gamma\to\Gamma
is an edge map. We call
g
a
train track (tt) map
if
g^{k}
is tight for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. We assume throughout that tt maps are surjective, as they will automatically be for homotopy equivalences, due to our vertex valence restriction at the start of the section. We call the train track map
g

expanding
if for each edge
e\in E\Gamma
we have
|g^{n}(e)|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, where for a path
\gamma
we use
|\gamma|
to denote the number of edges
\gamma
traverses (with multiplicity). Note that, apart from our not requiring a “marking,” these definitions coincide with those in
[
BH92
]
when
g
is in fact a homotopy equivalence of graphs (viewed topologically).

2.2.2.
Transition matrices

The
transition matrix

M(g)
of a tt map
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
is the square
|E\Gamma|\times|E\Gamma|
matrix
[a_{ij}]
such that
a_{ij}
, for each
i
and
j
, is the number of times
g(e_{i})
contains either
e_{j}
or
\overline{e_{j}}
. Note that each transition matrix is a nonnegative integer matrix.

A nonnegative integer matrix
A=[a_{ij}]
is
irreducible
if for each
(i,j)
, there is a
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
so that the
{ij}^{th}
entry of
A^{k}
is positive, and so in particular is at least
1
.
Furthermore,
A
is
Perron–Frobenius (PF)
if there exists an
N
such that, for each
k\geq N
, we have that
A^{k}
is strictly positive.

2.2.3.
(Fully) irreducible outer automorphisms

A tt map is
irreducible
if its transition matrix is irreducible. Not every element of
\textup{Out}(F_{r})
is represented by a tt map, and even fewer by irreducible tt maps. An outer automorphism
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
is
fully irreducible
if no positive power preserves the conjugacy class of a proper free factor of
F_{r}
. Bestvina and Handel
[
BH92
]
proved that each fully irreducible outer automorphism has expanding irreducible tt representatives, with Perron–Frobenius transition matrix.

If
g
is a tt representative of a fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
, then
\tau_{\infty}(g)
is the set of turns taken by the
[
BFH97
]
“stable lamination”
\Lambda_{\varphi}
.

2.3.
(Periodic) Nielsen paths

Let
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
be an expanding irreducible tt map. Bestvina and Handel
[
BH92
]
define a nontrivial immersed path
\rho
in
\Gamma
to be a
periodic Nielsen path (PNP)
if, for some power
R\geq 1
, we have
g^{R}(\rho)\cong\rho
rel endpoints (and just a
Nielsen path (NP)
if
R=1
). An NP
\rho
is
indivisible
(hence is an “iNP”) if it cannot be written as
\rho=\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}
, where
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are themselves NPs. Bestvina and Handel describe in
[
BH92
, Lemma 3.4]
the structure of iNPs:

Lemma 2.1

(
[
BH92
]
)
.

Let
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
be an expanding irreducible train track map and
\rho
an iNP for
g
. Then
\rho=\bar{\rho}_{1}\rho_{2}
, where
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are nontrivial legal paths originating at a common vertex
v
and such that the turn at
v
between
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
is a nondegenerate illegal turn for
g
.

2.3.1.
Rotationless powers

By
[
FH11
, Corollary 4.43]
, for each
r\geq 2
, there exists a
rotationless
power
R(r)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that for each expanding irreducible tt representative
g
of a fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
, among other properties, each periodic vertex, direction, and PNP is fixed by
g^{R(r)}
.

2.4.
PNP detection

2.4.1.
(Dangerous) long turns

The notion of a dangerous long turn is first introduced in
[
CL15b
]
. Suppose
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
on
\Gamma
is a tt map. By a
long turn
at a vertex
v\in V\Gamma
we mean a pair of legal paths
\{\alpha,\beta\}
emanating from
v
. We call
\{\alpha,\beta\}

legal
or
illegal
when
\{D(\alpha),D(\beta)\}
is, respectively, legal or illegal. A long turn
\{\alpha,\beta\}
is
dangerous
if we have that

•

g(\alpha)
is not an initial subpath of
g(\beta)
, and

•

g(\beta)
is not an initial subpath of
g(\alpha)
, and

•

g_{\#}(\overline{\alpha}\beta)
is an illegal path (i.e. cancellation of
g_{\#}(\alpha)
and
g_{\#}(\beta)
ends with an illegal turn).

2.4.2.
Identifying PNPs

The following is
[
AKKP19
, Lemma 4.2]
and gives a means for identifying PNPs via dangerous long turns.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
AKKP19
]
)
.

Let
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
be an expanding irreducible tt map and
\rho
an iNP for
g
. Then
\rho=\bar{\rho}_{1}\rho_{2}
, where
\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\}
is a dangerous long turn for each positive power
g^{k}
of
g
. More generally, if
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
has a PNP, then
\Gamma
contains dangerous long turns for each positive power
g^{k}
of
g
. Thus, an expanding irreducible train track map with no dangerous long turns has no PNPs.

We now prove a lemma that arises in various forms in other papers but is needed in this fuller generality here.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
is an expanding irreducible tt map and
g^{\prime}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime\prime}\colon\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Gamma
are surjective tight graph maps such that
g=g^{\prime\prime}\circ g^{\prime}
. Let
f=g^{\prime}\circ g^{\prime\prime}\colon\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Gamma^{\prime}
and suppose
\rho=\bar{\rho}_{1}\rho_{2}
is an iNP for
g
. Then each of the following holds.

(1)

f
is an expanding irreducible tt map with PF transition matrix
M(f)
.

(2)

If
\rho_{1}^{\prime}\subset\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}^{\prime}\subset\rho_{2}
are subpaths such that
g^{\prime}_{\#}(\rho)=\overline{\rho_{1}^{\prime}}\rho_{2}^{\prime}
, then
\rho^{\prime}
is an iNP for
f
and
\{\rho_{1}^{\prime},\rho_{2}^{\prime}\}
is a dangerous turn for
f
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
f^{k}
is not injective on the interior of some
e\in E\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since
g^{\prime}
is surjective
g^{\prime}(e^{\prime})
contains
e
for some
e^{\prime}\in E\Gamma^{\pm}
. Then there would be cancellation on the interior of
g^{k+1}(e^{\prime})=g^{\prime\prime}(f^{k}(g^{\prime}(e^{\prime})))\supseteq g^{\prime\prime}(f^{k}(e))
, contradicting that
g
is a tt map.

Since
g
is expanding, there exists an edge
e\in E\Gamma
such that
|g^{n}(e)|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Since
g^{\prime\prime}
is surjective, there exists an edge
e^{\prime}\in E\Gamma^{\prime}
so that
g^{\prime\prime}(e^{\prime})
contains
e^{\prime}
and thus
|f^{n}(e)|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

To show
M(f)
is PF, it suffices to show that there exists a power
n
of
f
so that
f^{n}(e)
, for each
e\in E\Gamma^{\prime}
, contains either
e^{\prime}
or
\overline{e^{\prime}}
for each
e^{\prime}\in E\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since
M(g)
is PF, there exists a power
k
so that each edge of
E\Gamma
contains an orientation of each edge of
E\Gamma
in its
g^{k}
-image. Then, for each
e\in E\Gamma^{\prime}
, we know
g^{k}(g^{\prime\prime}(e))
contains an orientation of each edge of
E\Gamma
in its image. And, since
g^{\prime}
is surjective,
g^{\prime}(g^{k}(g^{\prime\prime}(e)))=f^{k+1}(e)
contains an orientation of each edge of
E\Gamma^{\prime}
in its image.

(b) Let
\rho^{\prime}:=g^{\prime}_{\#}(\rho)
. By the definitions and the fact that
\rho
is an iNP for
g
,

f(\rho^{\prime})\simeq f(g^{\prime}(\rho))\simeq(f\circ g^{\prime})(\rho)\simeq(g^{\prime}\circ g^{\prime\prime}\circ g^{\prime})(\rho)\simeq(g^{\prime}\circ g)(\rho))\simeq g^{\prime}(g(\rho))\simeq g^{\prime}(\rho)\simeq\rho^{\prime}.

So
\rho^{\prime}
is by definition an NP for
f
. By symmetric argumentation, a decomposition of
\rho^{\prime}
into Nielsen paths would yield a decomposition of
\rho
into Nielsen paths, contradiction that
\rho
is an iNP. Thus,
\rho^{\prime}
is in fact an iNP. Finally,
\{\rho_{1}^{\prime},\rho_{2}^{\prime}\}
is a dangerous turn for
f
.
∎

2.5.
Whitehead graphs & lamination train track (ltt) structures

Local Whitehead graphs, stable Whitehead graphs, and ideal Whitehead graphs were first introduced by Handel and Mosher in
[
HM11
]
. We stray from their definitions by assuming throughout that
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
is a tt map with no PNPs. However, the presence of PNPs only impacts the ideal Whitehead graph definition.

2.5.1.
Local & stable Whitehead graph

For each
v\in V\Gamma
the
local Whitehead graph

\LW(g,v)
has vertices for the directions of
\mathcal{D}(v)
and edges connecting the directions of each turn in
\tau_{\infty}(g)
. Given a
g
-periodic
v\in V\Gamma
, the
stable Whitehead graph

\SW(g,v)
is the restriction of
\LW(g,v)
to the periodic direction vertices and edges betwixt them. In terms of gates,
\SW(g,v)
has a vertex for each gate at
v
.

2.5.2.
Ideal Whitehead graph

In the absence of PNPs, if
g
represents a fully irreducible outer automorphism
\varphi
, then the
ideal Whitehead graph

\IW(\varphi)
for
\varphi
is defined as

\IW(\varphi)=\bigsqcup_{v\in V\Gamma}\SW(g,v),

but with components containing only two vertices left out.

The ideal Whitehead graph is an invariant of the conjugacy class of the outer automorphism represented by
g
and
\IW(\varphi^{k})=\IW(\varphi)
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

[
HM11
,
Pfa12
]
.

2.5.3.
Lamination train track (ltt) structure
\mathcal{G}(g)

The
lamination train track (ltt) structure

\mathcal{G}(g)
is obtained from its
underlying graph

\Gamma
by replacing each vertex
v\in V\Gamma
with the local Whitehead graph
\LW(g,v)
as follows. Replace
v
with a vertex for each direction at
v
labeled with that direction and then identify each of these new vertices with the vertex of
\LW(g,v)
of the same label. Vertices and edges of each
\SW(g,v)
are colored purple and the remaining vertices and (open) edges of each
\LW(g,v)
are colored red. Alternatively, one could start with
\bigsqcup_{v\in V\Gamma}\LW(g,v)
, color the
\LW(g,v)
as just described, and then include a directed edge
[e,\overline{e}]
for each
e\in E\Gamma
. See §
9.2
for examples.

2.6.
Folds & Stallings fold decompositions

Suppose
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are graphs viewed topologically and
e_{0},e_{1}\in E^{\pm}\Gamma
are distinct directed edges emanating from a common vertex. Then
\Gamma^{\prime}
is obtained from
\Gamma
by a
proper full fold of
e_{1}
over
e_{0}
when there exist orientation-preserving homeomorphisms
\sigma_{0}\colon[0,1]\to e_{0}
and
\sigma_{1}\colon[0,2]\to e_{1}
so that

\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma\backslash\sim
is the topological quotient of
\Gamma
with respect to the equivalence relation
\sim
defined by
\sigma_{0}(t)=\sigma_{1}(t)
for each
t\in[0,1]
. Further,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is obtained from
\Gamma
by a
complete fold of
e_{0}
and
e_{1}
if instead
\sigma_{1}\colon[0,1]\to e_{1}
and a
partial fold of
e_{0}
and
e_{1}
if instead
\sigma_{0}\colon[0,2]\to e_{0}
. Proper full folds, complete folds, and partial folds are together called
folds
.

\parpic

[r]

Suppose there are edges
e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\in E\Gamma
such that a partial fold of
\{e,e^{\prime}\}
and a partial fold of
\{\bar{e},e^{\prime\prime}\}
terminate at a common point
p
in
e
. Suppose further that we can write
e=e_{1}e_{2}
, and
e^{\prime}=e_{1}^{\prime}e_{2}^{\prime}
, and
e^{\prime\prime}=e_{1}^{\prime\prime}e_{2}^{\prime\prime}e_{3}^{\prime\prime}
so that
f(e_{1})=f(e_{1}^{\prime})
, and
f(\overline{e_{2}})=f(e_{1}^{\prime\prime})
, and
f(e_{2}^{\prime})=f(e_{2}^{\prime\prime})
. Define a
tripod proper full fold
by first folding
\{e,e^{\prime}\}
and
\{\bar{e},e^{\prime\prime}\}
, then afterward folding
\{e_{2}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime\prime}\}
. A tripod fold could be seen as the composition of three folds, namely two partial folds and a proper full fold.

Note that a proper full fold does not change the number of edges, a complete fold decreases the number of edges, and a partial fold or tripod proper full fold increases the number of edges.

2.6.1.
Proper full fold notational conventions

In the automata defined here, all folds are proper
\parpic

[l]

full folds. We thus establish here notational conventions for proper full folds. Suppose
f\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
is a single proper full fold of an edge
e_{j}
over an edge

2.6.2.
Stallings fold decompositions

We follow the
[
AHLP24
]
description and language of Stallings fold decompositions. In general, each fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
has multiple tt representatives, each of which can have several distinct Stallings fold decompositions. An example of the multiple Stallings fold decompositions arising from a tt map with 2 illegal turns is given in Example

Since a Stallings fold decomposition can end with a homeomorphism changing edge labels, we define an
edge-permutation graph isomorphism
as a graph isomorphism that possibly permutes edge labels and possibly reverses the orientations on some subset of the edges.

3.
Indices, the index inequality, & the index defecit

3.1.
Index sum & list

Let
\varphi\in Out(F_{r})
be fully irreducible. For each component
C_{i}
of
IW(\varphi)
, let
k_{i}
denote the number of vertices of
C_{i}
. Then the
index sum
(also called the
rotationless index
) is defined as

i(\varphi):=\sum 1-\frac{k_{i}}{2}
. Since the index sum can be computed as such from an ideal Whitehead graph, one can define an index sum for an ideal Whitehead graph, or in fact for any finite graph. For a graph
\mathcal{G}
, we denote the index sum by
i(\mathcal{G})
. Note that the index sum is always in
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
.

Writing the terms
1-\frac{k_{i}}{2}
as a list, we obtain the
index list
for
\varphi
. By
[
GJLL98
]
, we know that each fully irreducible
\varphi\in Out(F_{r})
satisfies the “index inequality”:

(3)

0>i(\varphi)\geq 1-r,\quad\text{ or equivalently, }\quad\frac{1}{2}\geq i(\varphi)\geq 1-r.

It is constructively shown in
[
CL15a
]
that, for each rank
r\geq 4
and list of negative
\frac{1}{2}
-integers (hence sum) satisfying Equation
3
, there is a fully irreducible outer automorphism in
\textup{Out}(F_{r})
having that list as its index list. The case of
r=3
was proved in
[
Pfa15b
]
.

3.2.
Ageometric fully irreducible outer automorphisms

An ageometric fully irreducible
\varphi\in Out(F_{r})
can be characterized by satisfying
0>i(\varphi)>1-r
, a phenomena proved generic in
[
KMPT22
]
. The index list, index sum, and ideal Whitehead graph are all invariant under taking powers of the outer automorphism, hence the same holds for being ageometric.

By
[
BH92
]
, all fully irreducible outer automorphisms have “stable” tt representatives and stable tt representatives of ageometric outer automorphisms have no NPs
[
BF94
, Theorem 3.2]
. They in fact have tt representatives for which all powers are stable. As such, we see no loss in assuming throughout that all tt representatives of ageometric outer automorphisms have no PNPs, thus also satisfy that their ideal Whitehead graph is the disjoint union of their stable Whitehead graphs.

Fully irreducible outer automorphisms that are not ageometric can be geometric, i.e. induced by surface homeomorphisms, or parageometric. We avoid both these circumstances, as they are rare.

3.2.1.
Ageometric full irreducibility criterion

We use the criterion of Proposition
3.1
to prove that certain tt maps represent ageometric fully irreducible outer automorphisms. Proposition
3.1
is
[
AKKP19
, Proposition 3.35]
, which is the elevation of
[
Pfa13
, Proposition 4.1]
to include the observation that a fully irreducible with a PNP-free tt representative is in fact ageometric.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
Pfa13
]
,
[
AKKP19
]
)
.

(
The Ageometric Full Irreducibility Criterion (FIC)
)
Let
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
be a PNP-free, irreducible tt representative of
\varphi\in Out(F_{r})
. Suppose that the transition matrix for
g
is Perron-Frobenius and that the local Whitehead graph at each vertex of
\Gamma
is connected. Then
\varphi
is an ageometric fully irreducible outer automorphism.

3.3.
Index deficit

In light of Equation
3
, and following language proposed by Lee Mosher in conversations, we call

(4)

ID(\varphi)=i(\varphi)+r-1

the

of
\varphi
. For any tt representative

of
\varphi
, the Euler characteristic
\chi(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
satisfies
\chi(\Gamma)=1-r
. So, in fact,

(5)

ID(\varphi)=i(\varphi)-\chi(\Gamma).

As an ageometric fully irreducible
\varphi\in Out(F_{r})
is characterized by satisfying
0>i(\varphi)>1-r
, it could also be characterized by having positive index deficit. The follow lemma says that

ranges from 0 (for geometric and parageometric
\varphi\in Out(F_{r})
) to

:

Lemma 3.2

(Index deficit values)
.

For each integer

, each
\frac{1}{2}
-integer value

is realized as the index deficit

for a fully irreducible

.

Proof.

Suppose that

and

is a
\frac{1}{2}
-integer value satisfying

. Then
R-r+1
is a
\frac{1}{2}
-integer value satisfying
\frac{1}{2}\geq R-r+1\geq 1-r
.
Thus, by
[

]
, there exists a fully irreducible

so that

4.
Outer space
\textup{CV}_{r}
, fully irreducible axes, & lone axis outer automorphisms

4.1.
Outer space
\textup{CV}_{r}

Outer space
\textup{CV}_{r}
was first defined in
[
CV86
]
. We do not use in this manuscript all details of the definitions, so only hit on a few highlights here and then refer the reader to
[
FM11
,
Bes14
,
Vog15
]
for further reading on the topic.

Points in
\textup{CV}_{r}
are triples
(\Gamma,m,\ell)
, called
marked metric graphs
, where

\bullet

\Gamma
is a finite graph such that valence
(v)\geq 3
for each
v\in V\Gamma
, and

\bullet

m\colon F_{r}\to\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
is an isomorphism, called a
marking
, and

\bullet

\ell\colon E\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is an assignment of
lengths
to edges satisfying that
\sum_{e\in E\Gamma}\ell(e)=1
.

Two triples are equivalent that differ by an isometric change of marking.

Outer space can be endowed with what is known as the
Lipschitz metric

\mathcal{L}
, which is in fact not a metric, as it is asymmetric.
\textup{Out}(F_{r})
acts on
\textup{CV}_{r}
isometrically by changing the marking.

4.2.
Fold line geodesics in
\textup{CV}_{r}

In
[
Sko89
]
, Skora interpreted a Stallings fold decomposition for a graph map homotopy equivalence
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
as a sequence of folds performed continuously. In
[
AKP15
, Proposition 3.17]
it is proved that any fold sequence similarly determines a geodesic in
\textup{CV}_{r}
provided that there is some conjugacy class in
F_{r}
whose realization in the graphs of the sequence is never folded. We give here a version specialized for our purposes:

Proposition 4.1
.

[
AKP15
, Proposition 3.17]

Let
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\colon x_{i}\to x_{i+1}\}_{i=0}^{k}
be a sequence of folds in
\textup{CV}_{r}
and suppose there is a conjugacy class
\alpha
in
F_{r}
satisfying that, for each
i
, the realization
\alpha_{x_{i}}
of
\alpha
in
x_{i}
is legal with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, ie. is not folded by
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. Then the corresponding fold path
\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})=\{x_{t}\}_{t\in[0,k]}
is an unparametrized geodesic, i.e. for each
r\leq s\leq t
in
[0,k]
, we have
d(x_{r},x_{t})=d(x_{r},x_{s})+d(x_{s},x_{t})
.

4.3.1.
Fold-conjugate decompositions

Since an axis for a fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
has a

\parpic
[r]
periodic structure, one can view its Stallings fold decompositions cyclically. With careful attention paid to the marking, one can see that starting at a different fold in a decomposition now yields a tt map representing the same outer automorphism. Further, tt representatives may start “in the middle of a fold.” These notions of cyclically permuting a Stallings fold decomposition or, equivalently, shifting along an axis are formalized in
[
AHLP24
]
via the language of
fold-conjugate
and
partial-fold conjugate
decompositions. We use the definitions presented there, just including here that a
subdivided fold
is a fold written as a composition of two folds, as depicted to the right. Since fold-conjugate tt maps represent the same outer automorphisms, they share all outer automorphism invariants, such as ideal Whitehead graphs, indices, and whether or not a map is fully irreducible.

4.4.

A main focus of this manuscript is a class of ageometric fully irreducible outer automorphisms proved in

6.
Abstract lamination train track (ltt) structures

In preparation to discuss tt automata, we need an abstract notion of an ltt structure. Suppose
\Gamma
is a directed finite graph of first betti number
r
and such that the valence of each vertex is
\geq 3
. An
abstract lamination train track (ltt) structure

\mathcal{G}
with underlying graph
\Gamma
and “index”
\mathcal{I}
is a partially colored partially directed labeled graph with:

(ltt-i)

a vertex for each direction in
\mathcal{G}
, colored either red or purple, and

(ltt-ii)

2(\mathcal{I}-\chi(\Gamma))
, i.e.
2(\mathcal{I}+r-1)
, of the vertices are colored red, and

(ltt-iii)

at least one red vertex contained in precisely one red edge, and

(ltt-iv)

for each edge
e\in E\Gamma
, a directed black edge labeled with
e
directed from the vertex labeled
e
to the vertex labeled
\bar{e}
, and

(ltt-v)

undirected purple edges connecting some portion of the pairs of purple vertices forming turns in
\Gamma
(i.e. that represent distinct directions at a common vertex in
\Gamma
) and so that each vertex is contained in at least one colored (purple or red) edge and there are never 2 colored edges connecting the same pair of vertices, and

(ltt-vi)

for each
v\in V\Gamma
, the subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
that is the union of the colored (purple or red) edges representing turns at
v
, which we denote by
LW(\mathcal{G},v)
, is connected.

We denote the edge connecting the turn
\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
by
[d_{1},d_{2}]
, recognizing that
[d_{1},d_{2}]=[d_{2},d_{1}]
.

The following language and notation will be used both for abstract ltt structures and those defined by tt maps.

As mentioned in (ltt-vi),
LW(\mathcal{G},v)
, called the
local Whitehead graph at
v
, denotes the subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
that is the union of the colored edges representing turns at
v
. We let
SW(\mathcal{G},v)
denote the purple subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
and call it the
stable Whitehead graph at
v
. The
ideal Whitehead graph of
\mathcal{G}
is
IW(\mathcal{G}):=\bigsqcup SW(\mathcal{G},v)
, as
v
varies over
V\Gamma
.
Note that, if
g
is a tt representative of a fully irreducible
\varphi
satisfying that
g
has PNPs and
ltt(g)=\mathcal{G}
, then
IW(\varphi)\neq IW(\mathcal{G})
.

We consider a path in an ltt structure
\mathcal{G}

smooth
that alternates between black and colored edges. An ltt structure is
birecurrent
that contains a bi-infinite smooth path
\gamma
so that
\gamma~\backslash~\sigma
contains each edge of
\mathcal{G}
for each finite subpath
\sigma
of
\gamma
.

7.
Fully singular outer automorphisms & train track representatives

Suppose that
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
is a train track representative of an ageometric fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
.
As in
[
HM11
]
and
[
MP16
]
, we call a periodic point in
\Gamma
(
g
-)
principal
if it is either the endpoint of a PNP or has
\geq 3
periodic directions. Thus, if
g
has no PNPs, the principal vertices are precisely those periodic vertices with
\geq 3
periodic directions. For a set
g
, let
PV\Gamma
denote the set of
g
-principal vertices of
\Gamma
and
nPV\Gamma
the set of
g
-nonprincipal vertices of
\Gamma
.

7.1.
Fully singular & fully preprincipal tt representatives

We call a tt representative
g
of an ageometric fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})

fully singular
if it is PNP-free and each vertex is principal, i.e. if
PV\Gamma=V\Gamma
. Note that in such a case, the vertices of
\Gamma
are in 1-to-1 correspondence with the components of the ideal Whitehead graph.

We call a tt representative
g
of a fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})

fully preprincipal
if each vertex has
\geq 3
gates, additionally requiring that
g
is PNP-free if
\varphi
is ageometric. Thus, each fully singular representative is fully preprincipal, but the converse does not hold (even for PNP-free tt representatives).

It is unclear at this point whether each ageometric fully irreducible outer automorphism has a fully singular tt representative. However, in the case where
i(\varphi)=\frac{3}{2}-r
, such as for lone axis outer automorphisms,
[
MP16
, Corollary 3.8]
implies the existence of fully singular tt representatives.

We now prove the existence of fully preprincipal tt representatives.

Proposition 7.1

(Fully preprincipal tt representatives)
.

Each fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
has a fully preprincipal tt representative.

Proof.

Suppose
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
is fully irreducible. We show
\varphi
has a (PNP-free if
\varphi
is ageometric) tt representative for which each vertex has at least three gates. Since
\varphi
is fully irreducible, it has a tt representative. In the case where
\varphi
is ageometric, the representative can be chosen to additionally be PNP-free. Let
h\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
be one such representative. We cannot a priori assume
h
is fully preprincipal, so we suppose some
v\in V\Gamma
has less than three
h
-gates. If
v
had only one gate, then any edge passing through
v
would have backtracking, contradicting that
h
is a tt representative of a fully irreducible outer automorphism (an invariant graph could be inserted at
v
if no edge passed over
v
). If
v
had 2 gates, we could perform folds, similar to those in
[
BH92
, pg. 16-17]
, to obtain a new (PNP-free if
\varphi
was ageometric) tt representative of
\varphi
with fewer vertices with less than three gates:

One of the gates must have multiple directions in it, or
v
would have valence two. We fold that gate
\lambda
. Suppose that
k
is the power of
h
that identifies all of the directions
E_{1},\dots,E_{n}
of
\lambda
. Maximally fold
h^{k-1}(E_{1}),\dots,h^{k-1}(E_{n})
. We are then able to fold
h^{k-2}(E_{1}),\dots,h^{k-2}(E_{n})
and do so maximally. Continuing as such, we are eventually able to fold
E_{1},\dots,E_{n}
. Since each fold was maximal, it either ended at a vertex or at a point that (now) has at least three gates. Thus, the number of vertices with less than three gates has decreased.

So it is possible via repeating the folding procedure to obtain a representative in which all vertices have at least three gates. Since the folds cannot increase the number of PNPs, the representative is PNP-free if
h
was.
∎

7.2.
The directional surplus & index deficit for fully preprincipal tt representatives

Recall from §
3
the index deficit
ID(\varphi)=i(\varphi)+r-1
for a fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
. The index deficit will relate to the “directional surplus” of its tt representatives:

Given a tt map
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
, we define the
directional surplus
of
g
by

(11)

DS(g):=\sum_{v\in V\Gamma}\left(\sum_{G~\in~\text{Gates}(v)}(|G|-1)\right),

where for each
v\in V\Gamma
, we denote by
\text{Gates}(v)
the set of gates at
v
.

Since a Stallings fold can only fold directions in the same gate, the directional surplus, roughly speaking, determines how many distinct fold choices one has. As in Example
9.1
, these distinct folds generally lead to a multitude of related Stallings fold decompositions. But, unless some gate has
\geq 3
directions, the directional surplus bounds the number of choices of subsequent folds at each stage of forming a Stallings fold decomposition. The directional surplus gives a more complicated bound on possible folds in the case of gates with
\geq 3
directions.

Proposition 7.2

(Index deficit for fully preprincipal tt representatives)
.

Suppose that
r\geq 3
and
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
is a fully preprincipal tt representative of an ageometric fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
. Then each of the following holds.

(1)

ID(\varphi)~\geq~\frac{1}{2}~|nPV\Gamma|~+~\frac{1}{2}~DS(g)~\geq~\frac{1}{2}~|nPV\Gamma|~+~\frac{1}{2}.

(2)

If
g
is fully singular, then
ID(\varphi)~=~\frac{1}{2}~DS(g)
. In particular, the directional surplus is the same for any two fully singular tt representatives of the same ageometric fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
.

Proof.

Suppose
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
is a fully preprincipal tt representative of
\varphi
. Note that

(12)

|V\Gamma|~=~|PV\Gamma|~+~|nPV\Gamma|

and

(13)

|E\Gamma|~=~\frac{1}{2}~|\mathcal{D}\Gamma|~=~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~V\Gamma}\left(\sum_{G~\in~\text{Gates}(v)}|G|\right)

~=~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~PV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~+~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~nPV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~+~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g).

We first prove (a). Since
g
is fully preprincipal, for each
v\in nPV\Gamma
we have
|\text{Gates}(v)|\geq 3
. So

(14)

\sum_{v~\in~nPV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~\geq~3~|nPV\Gamma|.

Combining (
13
) and (
14
), we obtain

(15)

|E\Gamma|~\geq~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~PV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~+~\frac{3}{2}~|nPV\Gamma|~+~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g).

Since
\chi(G)~=~|V\Gamma|~-~|E\Gamma|
, we have

\chi(G)~\leq~|PV\Gamma|~+~|nPV\Gamma|~-~\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~PV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~+~\frac{3}{2}~|nPV\Gamma|~+~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g)\right)

~=~|PV\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~PV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~-~\frac{1}{2}~|nPV\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g).

Since
\chi(G)~=~1-r
, this gives

(16)

1-r~\leq~|PV\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~PV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~-~\frac{1}{2}~|nPV\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g).

Now,

(17)

i(\varphi)~=~\sum_{v~\in~PV\Gamma}\left(1-\frac{|\text{Gates}(v)|}{2}\right)~=~|PV\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~PV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|.

And so

(18)

ID(\varphi)~=~i(\varphi)~-~(1-r)~\geq

|PV\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~PV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~-~\left(|PV\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~PV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~-~\frac{1}{2}~|nPV\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g)\right).

And so
ID(\varphi)~\geq~\frac{1}{2}~|nPV\Gamma|~+~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g)
.

Since
g
is not a homeomorphism, some
g
-gate must have
\geq 2
directions. Thus
DS(g)\geq 1
and so, in fact

ID(\varphi)~\geq~\frac{1}{2}~|nPV\Gamma|~+~\frac{1}{2},

proving (a).

We now prove (b). Now
g
is fully singular and so

\sum_{v~\in~nPV\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~=~0

and thus

|E\Gamma|~=~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~V\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~+~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g).

So now

\chi(G)~=~|V\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~V\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~-~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~i(\varphi)~=~|V\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~V\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|.

So

ID(\varphi)~=~i(\varphi)~-~\chi(G)~=~\left(|V\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~V\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|\right)~-~\left(|V\Gamma|~-~\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v~\in~V\Gamma}|\text{Gates}(v)|~-~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g)\right).

And so
ID(\varphi)~=~\frac{1}{2}~\text{DS}(g)
, as desired.

∎

Remark 1
.

From the proof one can see that
ID(\varphi)
is impacted by the number of nonprincipal vertices with
\geq 3
gates and the direction surplus
\text{DS}(g)
, i.e. the number of directions in gates containing multiple directions. As
\text{DS}(g)
also relates to the number of choices of folds in a Stallings fold decomposition, one can conjecture that
ID(\varphi)
would give bounds on the dimension of the
[
MP16
]
stable axis bundle.

7.3.
Pff decompositions of fully singular train track representatives

Proposition 7.3

(Pff decompositions of fully singular train track representatives)
.

Suppose
g
is a fully singular tt representative of an ageometric fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
. Then
g
has either a pff decomposition or a Stallings fold decomposition with at least one tripod proper full fold.

If a fully preprincipal tt representative of an ageometric fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
has a pff decomposition then it is fully singular.

Proof.

Suppose
g
is a fully singular tt representative of an ageometric fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
and perform as many proper full folds as possible to decompose
g
as
g=g^{\prime\prime}\circ g^{\prime}
, where
g^{\prime}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
decomposes entirely into proper full folds and no Stallings fold decomposition of
g^{\prime\prime}\colon\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Gamma
can start with a proper full fold. If
g^{\prime\prime}
is a homeomorphism, then the proper full fold decomposition of
g^{\prime}
is the desired pff decomposition. Now suppose that
g^{\prime}
is not a homeomorphism.

Note that
\Gamma^{\prime}
has the same number of vertices as
\Gamma
, i.e. the minimal number of vertices possible for a train track representative of
\varphi
. Thus, simultaneously folding maximally each gate of
g^{\prime\prime}
cannot lead to a complete fold of two edges. Since the decomposition of
g^{\prime\prime}
cannot start with a proper full fold, this leaves that each of the folds is a partial fold.

It is possible for the partial folds to pass over each other. However, unless two of the partial folds end at a common point (as in a tripod fold), no further folding of
g
can occur. Thus, since the partial folds would have increased the number of vertices and
g^{\prime}
preserved the number of vertices, and
g^{\prime\prime}
was not a homeomorphism, there exists an edge
e
such that a partial fold of
\{e,e^{\prime}\}
and a partial fold of
\{\bar{e},e^{\prime\prime}\}
terminate at a common point
p
in
e
. Write
e=e_{1}e_{2}
, and
e^{\prime}=e_{1}^{\prime}e_{2}^{\prime}
, and
e^{\prime\prime}=e_{1}^{\prime\prime}e_{2}^{\prime\prime}
so that
g^{\prime\prime}(e_{1})=g^{\prime\prime}(e_{1}^{\prime})
and
g^{\prime\prime}(\overline{e_{2}})=g^{\prime\prime}(e_{1}^{\prime\prime})
. If either
\{e_{2}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime\prime}\}
can only partially be folded or not folded at all, then we again have a contradiction with the increased number of vertices. If
e_{2}^{\prime}
and
e_{2}^{\prime\prime}
are completely folded, then
g^{\prime\prime}
would identify the terminal vertices of
e_{2}^{\prime}
and
e_{2}^{\prime\prime}
. Note that these vertices could not be equal or the fold would change the homotopy type of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. So completely folding
e_{2}^{\prime}
and
e_{2}^{\prime\prime}
would contradict that
g
and
g^{\prime}
are bijective on vertices, forcing
g^{\prime\prime}
to be bijective on vertices. Thus, the fold is a tripod proper full fold.

We now prove the final sentence. No proper full fold can identify two vertices. Thus, any tt map
g
with a pff decomposition is bijective on vertices and thus each vertex is periodic. Since each vertex of a preprincipal tt representative has
\geq 3
gates, we have that each vertex is in fact principal and
g
is fully singular, as desired.
∎

8.1.

The following two lemmas will help us to understand the ltt structures of fully singular tt representatives.

Lemma 8.1
.

Suppose that

Proof.

We prove that each property holds, following the definition of §
6
, and noting that (ltt-i) and (ltt-iv) are just part of the definition, so require no proof.

(ltt-ii): Since the representative is fully singular, the number
R
of nonperiodic directions is
DS(g)

7.2
,
ID(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}DS(g)

(ltt-iii) This follows from Lemma
5.1
: By (a
′

Dg_{n}(e_{j_{n-1}})\notin Image(Dg)

(ltt-v) Most of (ltt-v) follows by the definition. Each vertex must be contained in a colored edge as follows. Suppose the vertex represents a direction
e
. Then
[e,e^{\prime}]
is an edge in
\mathcal{G}(g)
if and only if some

contains either
\overline{e^{\prime}}e
or
\overline{e}e^{\prime}
. As
\varphi
is fully irreducible,

must be expanding with PF transition matrix so that, for an adequately high power

, the image of each edge maps over each other edge and contains multiple edges in its image. That is, for in fact each

is not the first edge in

to be the edge directly preceding

.

(ltt-vi) This follows from the fact that local Whitehead graphs of fully irreducible outer automorphisms must be connected.

Finally, even though the definitions and situation are slightly different, the proof that
\mathcal{G}(g)
is birecurrent follows that of
[
Pfa15a

, replacing the single local Whitehead graph in that situation with the union of the local Whitehead graphs of
\mathcal{G}(g)
.

∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Suppose
g\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
is a fully singular tt representative of an ageometric fully irreducible
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
, with a pff decomposition, and
\mathcal{G}(g)
its associated ltt structure. Then

(1)

the disjoint union of the purple graphs is the ideal Whitehead graph, and

(2)

the number of
g
-nonperiodic directions in
\Gamma

2(i(\varphi)+|E\Gamma|-|V\Gamma|)

We prove the statements one at a time.

a) Since
g
is a fully singular tt representative of an ageometric

\IW(\varphi)\cong\bigsqcup_{v\in V\Gamma}\SW(g;v).

Since the purple graphs are by definition the

b) The first line follows from Lemma

.

8.2.3.
Special ltt structure maps: tt-friendly symmetry maps & proper full folds

Even abstract ltt structures have underlying graphs, so that we can talk about maps of these graphs, and the maps of ltt structures they induce. We will be particularly interested in two types of such maps that will compose to give tt maps.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is a lone axis ltt structure with underlying graph
\Gamma
and the black edges of
\mathcal{G}
are labeled with

.

(\mathcal{G},f\cdot\mathcal{G})

satisfying that

(tt-pff-i)

and

label vertices in the same component of the colored subgraph of
\mathcal{G}

(tt-pff-ii)

either

or

labels a red vertex, and

(tt-pff-iii)

\mathcal{G}
and
\overline{E^{\prime}}\neq E

(tt-pff-iv)

over

in

.

In the case of lone axis ltt structures, which will be describe in §

, (ii) and (iii) can be rewritten as

9.
False singularities & Pff decomposition among multiple Stallings fold decompositions

9.1.
Merging outer automorphisms & false singularities

Recall from Proposition
7.3
that a fully singular tt representative or an ageometric fully irreducible either has a pff decomposition or a representative with a tripod fold.
Since a tripod fold increases the number of vertices, a Stallings fold decomposition of a fully singular tt representative that contains a tripod fold will necessarily also contain a complete fold of 2 edges (identifying 2 vertices). Further, all turns at the vertex
v
created by a tripod fold are taken, ensuring they are not folded by further folds in the decomposition. However,
v
is not in the image of a vertex. We thus call these vertices created by tripod folds in Stallings fold decompositions of fully singular tt representatives
false singularities
. We more generally call a vertex created in a Stallings fold decomposition, not in the image of a vertex,
vanishing
. We do not discuss such vertices and decompositions beyond this section, but find them intriguing enough to compel our naming them upon their discovery.

We call an ageometric fully irreducible outer automorphism in which no fully singular tt representative has a pff decomposition
merging
. We conjecture that merging outer automorphisms are rare, and particularly that most fully singular tt representative have a pff decomposition. With this in mind, we focus here on pff decompositions of fully singular tt representatives.

9.2.

We conclude this section with an example of how a tt representative of an ageometric fully irreducible outer automorphism can have multiple Stallings fold decompositions, some of which have vanishing vertices and one of which does not. 10.
Lone axis train track automata

Examples of lone axis train track automata are introduced in
[

]
,
[
GP23
]
, and
[

]
. We provide a general description of them here before generalizing further to train track automata encoding more general pff decompositions. A unique aspect of the lone axis situation is that one has that all Stallings fold decompositions of all tt representatives of all lone axis fully irreducible outer automorphisms are fold-conjugate to tt representative Stallings fold decompositions realized as loops in the automata. For this we need the following lemma.

Lemma 10.1

(Lone Axis LTT Structures)
.

Suppose
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
is a lone axis fully irreducible outer automorphism, then each tt representative of
\varphi
is partial-fold conjugate to a tt map

satisfying:

(1)

the disjoint union of the purple graphs in

is the ideal Whitehead graph, and

(2)

all but one direction in
\Gamma
is
g
-periodic and this direction is at a vertex of
\Gamma
with valence
>3
, and

(3)

the nonperiodic direction is contained in precisely 1 turn taken by
g

Proof.

Following the first three paragraphs of the proof of
[
AHLP24
, Lemma 3.2]
, using
[
MP16
, Corollary 3.8]
and via a partial fold, we obtain a tt representative
g
of a rotationless power
\varphi^{R}
for which all vertices and periodic directions are fixed and have
\geq 3
fixed directions. Again, since
\varphi
and
\varphi^{R}
are lone axis fully irreducible outer automorphisms, this fold is within the shared axis
\mathcal{A}
of all tt representative of
\varphi^{R}
. Thus, all tt representatives of
\varphi^{R}
are fold-conjugate to
g
.

Since
\varphi\in\textup{Out}(F_{r})
is a lone axis fully irreducible, its ideal Whitehead graph

, hence also

for each

, has no cut vertices. Thus, by

, no tt representative

of any

has a PNP. Thus,

is the disjoint union of the

having
\geq 3
vertices. Since each vertex of

has
\geq 3

has
\geq 3
periodic directions. And so the disjoint union of the purple graphs is the ideal Whitehead graph, proving (a).

We now prove (b). By
[

, Lemma 3.6]
,
g
, and each

, has precisely one illegal turn and this illegal turn contains the unique nonperiodic direction. Since each vertex is principal, each vertex has
\geq 3
periodic directions. Since one direction in the illegal turn is the nonperiodic direction, the vertex with the illegal turn must then have
>3
vertices.

(c) follows from Lemma

.
∎

Lemma 10.5
.

Suppose

is a tt representative of a lone axis fully irreducible

11.4.
Paths in fully singular pff ltt automata yield geodesics in
\textup{CV}_{r}

Theorem 11.5
.

Suppose
\dots\mathcal{G}_{-1}\xrightarrow{g_{-1}}\mathcal{G}_{0}\xrightarrow{g_{1}}\mathcal{G}_{1}\xrightarrow{g_{2}}\dots
is a bi-infinite path in
\mathcal{A}(G)
for some fully singular rank-
r
ideal Whitehead graph
G
such that each
g_{k}
is a proper full fold. Then the sequence of folds
\dots g_{-2},g_{-1},g_{0},g_{1},g_{2}\dots
defines a geodesic in
\textup{CV}_{r}
.

Proof.

We will want to apply Proposition
4.1
, but Proposition
4.1
only applies to fold rays.

The complication to defining a metric on the graphs only arises in the folding direction because one might worry that one is trying to fold a shorter edge over a longer edge. This aspect of determining a path in
\textup{CV}_{r}
is resolved by first assigning edge-lengths for the fold sequence
g_{0},g_{1},g_{2}\dots
and then adding (and renormalizing) lengths in the reverse direction.

Now suppose that there is some integer
k
so that
g_{k},g_{k+1},g_{k+2}\dots
does not define a geodesic. We reach a contradiction by finding a conjugacy class
\alpha
in
F_{r}
so that, for each
i\geq k
, the realization
\alpha_{i}
of
\alpha
in
\Gamma_{i-1}
is not folded by
g_{i}
. We take a smooth loop
\ell
in
\mathcal{G}_{k-1}
that contains every colored edge in
\mathcal{G}_{k-1}
. This loop
\ell
determines a loop in
\Gamma_{k-1}
and
g_{k}(\ell)
determines a loop in
\mathcal{G}_{i-1}
, so cannot be folded by
g_{i}
because colored turns are never folded in
\mathcal{A}(G)
. The loop
\ell
defines
\alpha
.

∎

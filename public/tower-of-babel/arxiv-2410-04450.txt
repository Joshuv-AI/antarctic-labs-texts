Triangulated spheres with holes in triangulated surfaces
Katie Clinch, Sean Dewar, Niloufar Fuladi, Maximilian Gorsky, Tony Huynh, Eleftherios Kastis, Atsuhiro Nakamoto, Anthony Nixon, Brigitte Servatius

Abstract
Let 𝕊 h \mathbb{S}_{h} denote a sphere with h h holes. Given a triangulation G G of a surface 𝕄 \mathbb{M} , we consider the question of when G G contains a spanning subgraph H H such that H H is a triangulated 𝕊 h \mathbb{S}_{h} . We give a new short proof of a theorem of Nevo and Tarabykin [ 15 ] that every triangulation G G of the torus contains a spanning subgraph which is a triangulated cylinder. For arbitrary surfaces, we prove that every high facewidth triangulation of a surface with h h handles contains a spanning subgraph which is a triangulated 𝕊 2 ​ h \mathbb{S}_{2h} . We also prove that for every 0 ≤ g ′ < g 0\leq g^{\prime}<g and w ∈ ℕ w\in\mathbb{N} , there exists a triangulation of facewidth at least w w of a surface of Euler genus g g that does not have a spanning subgraph which is a triangulated 𝕊 g ′ \mathbb{S}_{g^{\prime}} . Our results are motivated by, and have applications for, rigidity questions in the plane.

1.1
Motivation

The initial motivation for our results comes from rigidity theory, although we believe they are of independent interest. Our proofs do not use any rigidity theory.

In rigidity theory, a
framework

(G,p)
is an ordered pair consisting of a graph
G
and a map
p:V(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
assigning positions in Euclidean
d
-space to the vertices of
G
. The framework is
d
-rigid
if every edge-length-preserving continuous motion of the vertices arises from an isometry of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Considering
p
as a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d|V(G)|}
we say that
(G,p)
is
generic
if the coordinates of
p
form an algebraically independent set over
\mathbb{Q}
. It is well known
[
2
]
that
d
-rigidity is a generic property; that is either every generic framework of
G
is
d
-rigid or every generic framework of
G
is not
d
-rigid. Hence we call a graph
G

d
-rigid
if there exists some generic framework
(G,p)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is
d
-rigid. Moreover, we say that
G
is
minimally
d
-rigid
if
G
is
d
-rigid but
G-e
is not
d
-rigid for every
e\in E(G)
.

A closely related notion is infinitesimal rigidity.
An
infinitesimal motion
of
(G,p)
is a map
\dot{p}:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying the system of linear equations:

(p(v)-p(w))\cdot(\dot{p}(v)-\dot{p}(w))=0\qquad\mbox{ for all $vw\in E(G)$}.

The framework
(G,p)
is
infinitesimally
d
-rigid
if the only infinitesimal motions arise from isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Infinitesimal
d
-rigidity is a sufficient condition for
d
-rigidity that is easier to work with, and in the generic case the two notions coincide
[
2
]
.

When
d=1
it is easy to prove that
G
is
1
-rigid if and only if it is connected. When
d=2
, Pollaczek-Geiringer proved that
G
is minimally 2-rigid if and only if
G
is a Laman graph
[
11
,
16
]
. Here
G
is a
Laman graph
if
|E(G)|=2|V(G)|-3
and every subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
with at least one edge satisfies
|E(G^{\prime})|\leq 2|V(G^{\prime})|-3
. When
d>2
such a characterisation is a central open problem but the case of triangulations of surfaces is well known, see
[
7
]
.

The following natural question was first posed by Adiprasito and Nevo
[
1
]
, see also a very similar question in earlier work of Fekete and Jordán
[
6
]
. For a
d
-rigid graph
G
, how small can a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be, so that there is an infinitesimally rigid framework
(G,p)
with
p(v)\in S
for every vertex
v\in V(G)
?
We define
c_{d}(G)
to be the smallest possible cardinality of such a set
S
,
and for a family of graphs
\mathcal{G}
, we set
c_{d}(\mathcal{G}):=\sup\{c_{d}(G):G\in\mathcal{G}\}
.

It follows from observations of Fekete and Jordán that, if
\mathcal{G}_{d}
is the family of
d
-rigid graphs, then
c_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{1})=2
and
c_{d}(\mathcal{G}_{d})=\infty
for
d\geq 2
; see
[
6
, Section 4]
, since in this case there are
d
-rigid graphs without any proper
d
-rigid subgraphs.
In contrast to this,
Király
[
10
]
proved that,
if
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\mathcal{N}_{h}
are the family of triangulations of the surfaces
\mathbb{T}_{h}
and
\mathbb{N}_{h}
respectively, then
c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})=O(\sqrt{h})
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{N}_{h})=O(\sqrt{h})
,
and if
\mathcal{P}
is the family of planar Laman graphs, then
c_{2}(\mathcal{P})\leq 26
.
Using Király’s latter result in conjunction with the existence of triangulated disks and other such 2-rigid planar graphs,
Nevo and Tarabykin, in
[
15
]
, proved that
c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{1})\leq 26
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{N}_{h})\leq 26
for
h\leq 2
. Kaszanitzky
[
9
]
has recently extended these results by showing that
c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{2})\leq 26
.

Since a triangulated
\mathbb{S}_{g}
is a 2-rigid planar graph
[
9
, Lemma 6]
,

Theorem

1.4
implies the following analogous result for triangulations of arbitrary orientable surfaces with sufficiently large facewidth.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\gamma(h)
be as in (
1
).
Then

for every triangulation

of

of facewidth at least

\mathbb{M}:=\mathbb{T}_{h,q}\setminus\Sigma(D_{a}^{\prime},D_{b}^{\prime})
, and
G^{\prime}:=\psi^{-1}(\psi(G)\cap\mathbb{M})
. Since
\psi(D_{a}^{\prime}\cup D_{b}^{\prime})
is disjoint from
\bd(\mathbb{T}_{h,q})
, we conclude that
\mathbb{M}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{T}_{h-1,q+2}
.
Moreover, every noncontractible circle
C
on
\overline{\mathbb{M}}
is either noncontractible on
\overline{\mathbb{T}_{h,q}}
,
or it crosses one of the faces bounded by
\psi(D_{a}^{\prime})
or
\psi(D_{b}^{\prime})
.
If it crosses the face bounded by
\psi(D_{a}^{\prime})
, then since
\psi(D_{i}^{\prime})
is homotopic to
\psi(D_{a}^{\prime})
for all
i\in[a]
,
it follows that
C
intersects
\psi(D_{i}^{\prime})
at least twice for each
i\in[a]
. Similarly, if
C
crosses the face bounded by
\psi(D_{b}^{\prime})
then
C
intersects
\psi(D_{i}^{\prime})
at least twice for all
i\in\{b,b+1,\dots,\ell\}
.
We conclude that the facewidth of
(G^{\prime},\psi|_{G^{\prime}})
on
\overline{\mathbb{M}}
is at least
\min\{\phi(h,q),2a,2(\ell-b+1)\}\geq\phi(h-1,q+2)
.

By induction, the embedded graph
(G^{\prime},\psi|_{G^{\prime}})
contains vertex-disjoint surface nonseparating cycles
C_{1},C_{1}^{\prime},\dots,C_{h-1},C_{h-1}^{\prime}
such that
C_{i}
is homotopic to
C_{i}^{\prime}
(on
\mathbb{M}
) for all
i\in[h-1]
, and
C_{i}
is not homotopic to
C_{j}
(on
\mathbb{M}
) for all distinct
i,j\in[h-1]
.
Setting
C_{h}=D_{a+1}^{\prime}
and
C_{h}^{\prime}=D_{a+2}^{\prime}
we have that
C_{1},C_{1}^{\prime},\dots,C_{h},C_{h}^{\prime}
are the required cycles in
(G,\psi)
.
∎

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

1.4
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

For all
h\geq 1
, define
\gamma(h)=\phi(h,0)
, where
\phi
is the function from
Lemma

3.2
. The closed form of
\gamma(h)
is the function described in (
1
).
By
Lemma

3.2
,
G
contains vertex-disjoint surface nonseparating cycles
C_{1},C_{1}^{\prime},\dots,C_{h},C_{h}^{\prime}
such that
C_{i}
is homotopic to
C_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i\in[h]
, and
C_{i}
is not homotopic to
C_{j}
for all distinct
i,j\in[h]
. For each
i\in[h]
, let
\Sigma(C_{i},C_{i}^{\prime})
be an (open) cylinder on
\mathbb{M}
bounded by
\psi(C_{i}\cup C_{i}^{\prime})
. Since
C_{i}
and
C_{j}
are vertex-disjoint and
C_{i}
is not homotopic to
C_{j}
for all distinct
i,j\in[h]
,
we note that
\Sigma(C_{i},C_{i}^{\prime})
and
\Sigma(C_{j},C_{j}^{\prime})
are disjoint for all distinct
i,j\in[h]
.
We choose a collection of cycles in
G
such that
\bigcup_{i\in[h]}\Sigma(C_{i},C_{i}^{\prime})
is inclusionwise minimal. We claim that no
\Sigma(C_{i},C_{i}^{\prime})
contains a vertex of
G
. Suppose for contradiction that this is not true. Then since
(G,\psi)
is a triangulation, there must be some
i\in[h]
and a triangle
T=xyz
of
G
such that
\psi(T)
is the boundary of a face of
(G,\psi)
, with
xy\in E(C_{i})
and
\psi(z)\in\Sigma(C_{i},C_{i}^{\prime})
. Thus, replacing
C_{i}
by
(C_{i}\cup\{xz,yz\})\setminus\{xy\}
contradicts the choice of
C_{1},C_{1}^{\prime},\dots,C_{h},C_{h}^{\prime}
.

To complete the proof we let
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}:=\mathbb{M}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in[h]}\Sigma(C_{i},C^{\prime}_{i})
and note that
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
is a sphere with
2h
holes.
Thus,
H:=\psi^{-1}(\psi(G)\cap\mathbb{M^{\prime}})
is a spanning subgraph of
G
such that
(H,\psi|_{H})
is a triangulated
\mathbb{S}_{2h}
, as required.
∎

We remark that
Lemma

3.2
(with a noncomputable facewidth bound) is a special case of a much more general result by Robertson and Seymour
[
17
]
;
who proved that for every fixed graph
H
embedded on a surface
\mathbb{M}
of Euler genus at least 1, there exists a constant
c(H)
such that every graph
G
embedded on
\mathbb{M}
with facewidth at least
c(H)
contains
H
as a ‘surface minor’. Roughly speaking, the surface minor relation is a refinement of the usual minor relation on graphs, where edge contractions and deletions are performed on
\mathbb{M}
.
In contrast, our proof gives an explicit bound, and can be considered as part of an extensive line of work to obtain computable bounds for Robertson and Seymour’s graph minors project (see for example
[
8
]
and
[
21
]
).

Another related theorem of Thomassen
[
20
, Theorem 3.3 with
d=1
]
implies that for triangulations
G
of
\mathbb{T}_{h}
with facewidth at least

2^{h+4}-16
, there is a collection of
h
pairwise disjoint and pairwise nonhomotopic cycles in
G
.

4
Triangulations without spanning spheres with few holes

In this section we prove
Theorem

1.5
. Given a triangulation
T
, the
face subdivision
of
T
is obtained by adding a vertex into each face of
T
and joining this vertex to each of the three vertices on the boundary of its corresponding face.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{M}
be a closed surface with Euler genus
g\geq 1
,
T
be a triangulation of
\mathbb{M}
, and
(G,\psi)
be the face subdivision of
T
. Then for every
g^{\prime}\leq g-1
,
(G,\psi)
does not contain a spanning subgraph
(H,\psi|_{H})
which is a triangulation of
\mathbb{S}_{g^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

We colour the vertices of
T
black and colour the vertices in
V(G)\setminus V(T)
white. Let
B
and
W
be the set of black and white vertices respectively.
By an application of Euler’s formula to
T
, we have
|W|=2|B|+2g-4
, since
|W|
is equal to the number of faces of
T
.

Towards a contradiction, suppose that
G
has a spanning subgraph
H
such that
(H,\psi|_{H})
is a triangulated
\mathbb{S}_{g^{\prime}}
for some
g^{\prime}\leq g-1
. We choose
H
to first be maximal under subgraph inclusion, and subject to that, then minimal with respect to
g^{\prime}
.
Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{g^{\prime}}
be the cycles in
H
which are the boundaries of the holes of
\mathbb{S}_{g^{\prime}}
. The minimality of
g^{\prime}
implies that
|V(C_{i})|\geq 4
for all
i\in[g^{\prime}]
. Let
X:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{g^{\prime}}V(C_{i})
,
W_{1}:=W\setminus X
and
W_{2}:=W\cap X
. Since every vertex contained in
W_{1}
is an interior vertex for the triangulation,
\deg_{H}(w)=3
for all
w\in W_{1}
.

Since
(H,\psi)
is a triangulation, we have that
\deg_{H}(v)\geq 2
for all
v\in V(H)
.
We claim that
\deg_{H}(w)=2
for all
w\in W_{2}
. Towards a contradiction suppose
w\in V(C_{j})
for
1\leq j\leq g^{\prime}
is a white vertex and
\deg_{H}(w)=3
. Let
x,y,z
be the neighbors of
w
. Since
w\in X
and
\deg_{H}(w)=3
, exactly two of the three faces incident to
w
are contained in
\mathbb{S}_{g^{\prime}}
. By symmetry, we may assume that the faces bounded by
wxz
and
wyz
are contained in
\mathbb{S}_{g^{\prime}}
, but the face bounded by
wxy
is not contained in
\mathbb{S}_{g^{\prime}}
. Thus,
x
and
y
are the neighbors of
w
on
V(C_{j})
. However, since
|V(C_{j})|\geq 4
,
H\cup\{xy\}
contradicts the maximality of
H
. Therefore, all white vertices in
W_{2}
have degree 2 in
H
, as claimed.

We now consider the induced subgraph
H^{\prime}=H[B\cup W_{2}]
.
Observe that
(H^{\prime},\psi|_{H^{\prime}})
is formed from
(H,\psi|_{H})
by deleting each vertex
w\in W_{1}
, and merging its 3 incident triangular faces into a single triangular face
F_{w}
. Since every face of
(H,\psi|_{H})
is incident to exactly one white vertex, these operations are disjoint and leave the faces incident to vertices of
W_{2}
untouched. Thus
(H^{\prime},\psi|_{H^{\prime}})
is a triangulation of
\mathbb{S}_{g^{\prime}}
with exactly
|W_{1}|+|W_{2}|=|W|
faces.

Let
D_{1},\dots,D_{g^{\prime}}
be the cycles of
H^{\prime}
which are the boundaries of the holes of
\mathbb{S}_{g^{\prime}}
, and let
\ell:=\sum_{i\in[g^{\prime}]}|V(D_{i})|
. Consider
(H^{\prime},\psi|_{H^{\prime}})
as embedded on the sphere
\overline{\mathbb{S}_{g^{\prime}}}
with
|W|+g^{\prime}
faces. Since each of the
|W|
faces of
(H^{\prime},\psi|_{H^{\prime}})
is triangular, we have
2|E(H^{\prime})|=3|W|+\ell
. Applying Euler’s formula for planar embeddings gives
|W|=2|V(H^{\prime})|+2g^{\prime}-4-\ell
.
Since the white vertices are a stable set of
G
, we conclude that
W_{2}
is also a stable set of
\bigcup_{i\in[g^{\prime}]}D_{i}
. Thus,
|W_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in[g^{\prime}]}|V(D_{i})|=\frac{\ell}{2}
. Combining everything yields,

|W|=2|V(H^{\prime})|+2g^{\prime}-4-\ell\leq 2|B|+2g^{\prime}-4<2|B|+2g-4=|W|,

which is a contradiction. ∎

Note that
Theorem

4.1
implies
Theorem

1.5
by taking
T
to be a high facewidth triangulation in
Theorem

4.1
.

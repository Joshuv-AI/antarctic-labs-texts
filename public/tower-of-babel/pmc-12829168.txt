Cost-effectiveness analysis of different tracers (Patent Blue, Technetium-99 m, and Indocyanine Green) for Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy procedure in breast cancer
Authors: Dorion Denardi, Idam de Oliveira-Junior, Júlia Lopes Ferigatto, Luisa Aguirre Buexm, Antonio Carlos Pereira, Fabiana de Lima Vazquez
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Sentinel Lymph Node (SLN) mapping is a highly accurate method for assessing axillary status in breast cancer, using various tracers such as Patent Blue, Technetium-99 m, Indocyanine Green, and Superparamagnetic Iron Oxide Nanoparticles. Despite its efficacy, there is limited literature on the costs of these procedures, prompting this study. The objective was to conduct a micro-costing and cost-effectiveness analysis of Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (SLNB) using Patent Blue (BD), Technetium-99 m (99mTc), and Indocyanine Green (ICG), estimating costs for a nonprofit institution over time. An economic evaluation using microcosting, cost-effectiveness, and financial impact analysis calculated direct medical and non-medical costs for approximately 100 procedures. The sensitivity analysis varied costs by ± 20%. Results showed the cost per procedure as US$ 67.71 for BD, US$ 88.31 for 99mTc, and US$ 134.98 for ICG, leading to annual costs of US$ 6,771.50, US$ 8,831.09, and US$ 13,498.38, respectively. For institutions with a Nuclear Center, 99mTc is more cost-effective than ICG, which incurs an additional US$ 51.48 per extra unit of effect with similar migration rates (96.5% vs. 97.3%). For institutions without a Nuclear Center, ICG is recommended over BD, despite an additional US$ 58.34 per unit of effect, due to a higher tracer migration rate (97.3% vs. 87.5%). Technetium-99 m is the preferred tracer for institutions with a Nuclear Medicine center due to its cost-effectiveness, offering higher accuracy and lower costs compared to Indocyanine Green, while Patent Blue, though cheaper, may lead to additional treatment costs due to its lower accuracy.

Introduction

The primary route of breast cancer dissemination is through the lymphatic system, with the axilla being the primary site of drainage in 97% of patients. Metastatic involvement of axillary lymph nodes represents an important prognostic factor, impacting therapeutic decisions [1]. Until the 1990s, axillary surgical approach was systematically performed through axillary lymphadenectomy, which is associated with various morbidities [2, 3]. In this context, the need for accurate axillary staging coupled with reduced morbidity led to the emergence of a new technique aimed at identifying the first lymph node (sentinel lymph node) through specific tracers injected into the breast, within the lymphatic drainage of the primary breast tumor, called as sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) [4].

For the implementation of SLNB, costs vary depending on the technique/tracer used by the surgeon [5–7] and the equipment and team involved. Depending on the chosen tracer, the procedure will be subject to variations in effective migration rates (86.8% for Patent Blue, 96.5% for Technetium-99 m, 97.9% for Indocyanine Green, and 97.4% for Superparamagnetic Iron Oxide Nanoparticles), which are directly associated with the rate of sentinel lymph node (SLN) identification and the effectiveness of the technique [8].

The cost of the technique does not clearly indicate the total cost of the procedure; it leads to a decision based on availability rather than efficiency. Therefore, information on direct medical and non-medical costs over a time horizon allows healthcare managers to make decisions based on more robust evidence. Given the variable migration rates and costs not presented in the literature, the aim of this study is to demonstrate only the costs involved in performing the individual procedure using Patent Blue (BD) or Technetium-99m (99mTc) and evaluate whether the inclusion of a new technology, Indocyanine Green (ICG), is feasible, as well as to demonstrate the costs per procedure within a one-year horizon for each tracer.

Materials and methods

Type of study

Partial economic evaluation study of the “Budget Impact Analysis” type from the perspective of an Oncological Hospital that serves patients from the Public Health System and uses Patent Blue and Technetium-99 m as tracers for SLN identification.

Ethics and consent to participate

This study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Barretos Cancer Hospital, Pio XII Foundation, under protocol number 1759/2019 and approval number 3.402.611. All participants gave their informed consent prior to inclusion in the study, in accordance with institutional and national research committee standards.

Cost analysis

For this evaluation, micro-costing was calculated using the bottom-up technique, which involves real-time data collection of the analyzed items’ costs. The guidelines for the development of “Economic Evaluation of the Brazilian Network for Health Technology Assessment (REBRATS [9–11]) were followed for methodological purposes. The perspective of the Hospital do Amor de Barretos and a one-year time horizon were adopted.

Direct non-medical costs (tracer disposal cost and operating room minute cost) were calculated; however, indirect costs (loss of productivity and patient or caregiver disability) were not included in this study.

Direct medical costs (human resources, tracer, and equipment) were calculated through primary cost data collection. For the primary cost data collection, a convenience sample was used (N = 15), consisting of five biopsies for each tracer. Female patients over 18 years old diagnosed with breast cancer (T1 or T2) with clinically or radiologically negative axilla (N0) [12, 13] were included after signing the Informed Consent Form. The inclusion was empirical to guide cost parameters. Patients who did not agree to participate in the research and procedures where tracer migration did not occur were excluded.

Importantly, in this type of budget impact analysis, we cannot speak of representativeness in the results, but rather of cost estimates. At the end of the study, an estimate of monthly and annual costs (outcomes) is presented according to the perspective of 100 SLN procedures annually.

Data collection standardization

For this study, data collection was standardized regarding tracer handling time, equipment cost per minute, team cost, and operating room hour, as well as the amount applied and discarded tracer. The BD and ICG techniques were performed in the Operating Room, while the 99mTc was administered in two stages: first in the Nuclear Medicine department and then in the Operating Room.

Regarding the amount applied, 1.5 ml of Patent Blue, 20 UI of Technetium-99m, and 1.5 ml of Indocyanine Green 1 mg/ml were standardized. The equipment used in this study is standard for this institution, including the GE OPTIMA™ NM/CT 640 SPECT/Computed Tomography (CT) Gamma Camera for tracer migration validation. Skin marking was not performed, and the GAMA PROBE device from EUROPROBE 3 was used for mapping and detection of the traced lymph node during surgery with the 99mTc technique. The Hyper-Eye from Medical System was used for the ICG technique.

The time analysis of the techniques estimated the average of the five procedures recorded in “Tracer handling and application time,” “Equipment setup/dismantling time,” “Surgical procedure time,” and “Final procedure time.”

For this study, the exchange rate used was the dollar rate on November 20, 2020 (1 USD = 5.35 BRL) - https://www.bcb.gov.br/conversao.

Direct non-medical costs such as transportation, meals, accompanying person costs, and indirect costs such as loss of productivity and patient or accompanying person disability were not included in this study.

Data analysis

All direct medical and non-medical expenses were calculated and quantified according to their usage per tracer. The average costs for each technique were totaled and then multiplied by an estimate of 100 Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsies (SLNB) performed in one year. The value of each total cost was then subtracted from the highest to the lowest, thus calculating the budgetary impact.

A sensitivity analysis was conducted using two scenarios, one more optimistic (20% less expenditure) and one more pessimistic (20% more expenditure).

Outcome measures

This study was considered a complete economic analysis, with the primary objective being the effective migration of the tracer and consequently the identification of the Sentinel Lymph Node, making it a diagnostic investigation study.

Procedures where effective migration did not occur were excluded and replaced by a new procedure until a total of 5 per tracer was reached. This study presented 4 procedures where migration did not occur, with 2 for Patent Blue, 1 for Technetium-99 m, and 1 for Indocyanine Green.

Since this study had a sample size that made it impossible to estimate the migration rate, an average of migration rates for Patent Blue, Technetium-99 m, and Indocyanine Green (87.5%, 96.5%, and 97.3%) was estimated based on the meta-analysis studies “Network meta-analysis of novel and conventional sentinel lymph node biopsy techniques in breast cancer” (86.8%, 96.5%, and 97.9%) [8] and “Indocyanine Green Fluorescence Versus Blue Dye or Radioisotope Regarding Detection Rate of Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy and Nodes Removed in Breast Cancer: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis” (86.8%, 96.5%, and 97.9%) [14] to determine the outcomes of the “Cost and Effectiveness Analysis”.

These meta-analyses were chosen because they are recent studies from 2019 to 2020, they used an adequate number of scientific articles to determine the migration rate (35 and 30 articles), and they presented the same study approach, analyzing each tracer individually.

Reporting standards

Adherence to reporting standards

This economic evaluation was conducted following the Brazilian Network for Health Technology Assessment (REBRATS) guidelines for economic evaluation studies. To enhance transparency and reporting quality, this study adheres to the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards (CHEERS) 2022 checklist, which provides standardized guidance for reporting health economic evaluations. The CHEERS 2022 framework, endorsed by ISPOR and the EQUATOR Network, includes 28 items covering study design, methods, results, and interpretation of economic evaluations.​.

Key elements reported according to CHEERS 2022 include: (1) clear identification of the study as a cost-effectiveness analysis in the title and abstract; (2) specification of the target population—female patients over 18 years diagnosed with early-stage breast cancer (T1 or T2) with clinically or radiologically negative axilla; (3) description of the setting and location—Barretos Cancer Hospital, São Paulo, Brazil; (4) explicit statement of comparators—Patent Blue, Technetium-99 m, and Indocyanine Green tracers; (5) definition of the perspective—institutional (hospital) perspective; (6) specification of the time horizon—one year; (7) description of outcome measures—tracer migration rate and sentinel lymph node identification; (8) detailed measurement and valuation of resources and costs using micro-costing methodology; (9) specification of currency, price date (2020), and conversion rate (1 USD = 5.35 BRL as of November 20, 2020); and (10) presentation of sensitivity analysis with optimistic and pessimistic scenarios (± 20% cost variation).

Total cost per procedure

Table 4 demonstrates the costs per analyzed item and per tracer. The micro-costing process was based on direct medical costs (human resources, tracer, and equipment) in addition to direct non-medical costs (tracer disposal cost and operating room minute cost). The cost of operating room time prevailed in all three techniques, Patent Blue (R$ 244.48–67.5% of the total), Technetium-99 m (R$ 232.62–49% of the total), and Indocyanine Green (R$ 386.37–53.5% of the total), with human resources being the second highest cost (26,7%, 33,9% e 21,2% of the total, respectively). However, the equipment cost per procedure was not significant for the final costs (0%, 2.7%, 0.4%). The cost per procedure was estimated at R$ 362.28 for Patent Blue, R$ 472.46 for Technetium-99 m, and R$ 722.16 for Indocyanine Green.

Discussion

The Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (SLNB) has established itself as a highly accurate method for determining axillary status in patients with early-stage breast cancer without lymph node involvement. However, a recent publication shows that the method is also feasible for advanced tumors, as long as there is no axillary involvement. Therefore, over time, the initial contraindications of the technique have become relative, especially regarding its use after neoadjuvant chemotherapy [15, 16].

When analyzing the costs involved in breast cancer treatment, we must consider the epidemiological profile of the disease, which corresponds to the most common type of cancer in the female population, excluding non-melanoma skin cancer. In this way, small fluctuations in costs determine a large final economic amount, a fact that motivates studies and discussions in the field [17].

The diversity of tracers available favors the widespread use of SLN mapping, which is associated with a reduction in surgical morbidity, particularly related to upper limb limitations that impact the economic, functional, and quality of life aspects of the patient [18]. Despite the extensive use of the method, the literature is scarce regarding the costs involved in its implementation. It is known that the financial toxicity involved in breast cancer treatment is significant and affects patients, healthcare services, and governmental economic systems, especially in countries like Brazil, where a large portion of the population depends on the Public Health System [19–21].

As demonstrated in Table 3, an institution will be exposed to variations in cost scenarios, sometimes more optimistic or pessimistic. These cost variations are linked to the country’s economic cycle, affected by macroeconomic factors (inflation, GDP, interest rates, unemployment rate) and microeconomic factors (productive factors, supply, wages, among others). When considering possibilities of cost variations, a manager will need to seek economic evaluations that will assist in decision-making and contribute to efficient resource allocation for future economic challenges of an institution [22–26].

In the evaluation of individual costs, the technique using Patent Blue tracer showed the lowest procedure cost compared to Technetium-99m and Indocyanine Green, with values of R$ 362.28; R$ 472.46 and R$ 722.16, respectively. Thus, when only considering economic representativeness, Patent Blue is the most viable technique. However, every decision-making process should take into account other parameters of the techniques. It is known that when using a single tracer, Patent Blue presents the lowest migration rate (86.8%). In contrast, 99mTc (96.5%) and ICG (97.9%) respectively. This important data must be taken into consideration in decision-making, as the non-detection of the SLN may require adequate axillary staging through axillary lymph node dissection, which can lead to various morbidities, including lymphedema, a chronic condition that limits and may disable women and add direct and indirect costs to an institution and the patient herself [27–30].

The current literature shows that the combination of 99mTc and BD achieves the highest identification rates (95–97%) [5, 31]. Therefore, we can infer that an institution combining the techniques of Patent Blue + Technetium-99m, may have a cost per procedure of R$ 834.74 or US$ 156.03, and if standardizing this combination, the cost for 100 procedures would be R$ 83,473.84 or US$ 15,602.59.

The Technetium-99m tracer is sold in the form of a “Technetium Generator,” and its resale cost in 2020 was R$ 6,835.59 (with a concentration of 2,000 mCi - Millicurie). The generator undergoes a decay process until it transforms into the 99mTc tracer, which is represented by an estimated cost per unit (Ui) of R$ 0.96. For the technique, 20 Ui are used, resulting in a price of R$ 19.18. The generator maintains a steady production of 99mTc, and the more it generates, the more it produces, and consequently, the more it is utilized [32], leading to a reduction in acquisition costs. In the studied institution, the generator is also used for other diagnostic procedures in the Nuclear Medicine center. Despite the high cost of the generator, this technique proves to be more cost-effective than the technique using Indocyanine Green, as it has a lower cost per procedure, albeit with similar effectiveness.

The cost-effectiveness analysis of this study presents the necessary scenarios to achieve a better migration rate per migration percentage. For an institution with a Nuclear Center that uses Technetium-99 m tracer, it is not feasible to perform the procedure with Indocyanine Green, as it would incur an additional cost of R$ 312.13 per unit of effect, and the migration rates are practically equivalent (96.5% x 97.3%), with a difference of 0.8%. For an institution without a nuclear center, the use of the Indocyanine Green technique is viable. It incurs a cost of R$ 36.72 per unit of effect compared to Patent Blue, which costs R$ 12.24. However, there will be a 9.8% increase in the tracer migration rate for sentinel lymph node identification (97.3% x 87.5%).

When analyzing the average time for procedure completion, we observe that using ICG resulted in an increase of 8 min and 20 s (average time of 21 min and 44 s), compared to the BD and 99mTc techniques, which are nearly equivalent (13 min and 42 s and 13 min and 5 s, respectively). One hypothesis for this is that ICG is not a standard technique in the institution studied and requires a learning curve for the surgeon.

The only limitation identified in this study is that the institution does not have a specific cost per hour for the surgical center, relying on references from other institutions to define outcome estimates and infrastructure costs, in addition to general costs associated with surgical center routines that were not included.

Limitations

Limitations related to CHEERS 2022

Given the scope and timeframe of this master’s thesis project, certain CHEERS 2022 items were not fully addressed. A formal health economic analysis plan was not prospectively registered, as this methodology was not yet widely adopted in Brazilian institutions at the time of study design (2019–2020). Decision-analytic modeling (e.g., decision trees or Markov models) was not employed, as the study focused on direct procedural costs within a limited time horizon rather than long-term clinical outcomes across multiple care pathways. The analysis excluded broader societal costs (patient transportation, productivity losses) and downstream costs (axillary dissection, complications), maintaining focus on the specific SLNB procedure from the hospital perspective. These limitations are acknowledged and should be considered when interpreting the study findings and their generalizability.

Temporal limitations and cost updating

A limitation of this study is that all cost data reflect 2020 values, corresponding to the period when this master’s thesis research was conducted. Since 2020, Brazil has experienced cumulative inflation (IPCA) of approximately 39.73% through October 2025, with healthcare-specific inflation substantially higher—medical-hospital costs have increased at rates 2–3 times above general inflation. To adjust reported costs to 2025 values, multiply by the IPCA factor of 1.3973; however, healthcare-specific indices would provide more accurate estimates. For example, the 2020 Patent Blue procedure cost (R$ 362.28 or US$ 67.71 - exchange rate of 1 US$ = R$ 5.35 on 11/20/2020) corresponds to approximately R$ 506.25 or US$93.75 (exchange rate of 1 US$ = R$ 5.40 on 11/24/2025) in 2025 IPCA-adjusted values, though actual healthcare costs likely exceed this estimate. Future cost-effectiveness analyses should incorporate current pricing and healthcare-specific inflation rates for enhanced decision-making relevance. Conclusions

According to the data presented, it is understood that for an institution with a Nuclear Medicine center, it is ideal to perform the technique with Technetium-99 m, as it offers a better cost-effectiveness ratio. Despite an increase of R$ 110.19 or US$ 20.60, this tracer presents higher accuracy compared to Methylene Blue (87.5% x 96.5%) and lower cost compared to Indocyanine Green (R$ 472.46 x R$ 722.16 or US$ 88.31 x US$ 134.98). Ultimately, Patent Blue presents the lowest cost-effectiveness ratio (R$ 362.28 or US$ 67.71), but it also entails uncertainties that could lead to additional costs in the patient’s final treatment.

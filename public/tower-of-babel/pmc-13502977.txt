Study of Subclinical Hypothyroidism and its Association with Lipid Profile Among the Rural Population of Chamarajanagara District—A Hospital-Based Study
Authors: B. V Sumanth, K. J Thoyaj, N Shashank
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Subclinical hypothyroidism (SCH) patients are at an increased risk of coronary artery disease and cerebrovascular disease complications. The association between SCH and dyslipidemia is still controversial.Objectives:To study the prevalence of SCH in Chamarajanagara district and to find its association with lipid profile.Materials and Methods:A prospective cross-sectional study was carried out at Chamarajanagara Institute of Medical Sciences hospital, Chamarajanagar. Age group of more than 18 years and less than 75 years were included in the study. All patients serum samples were processed for thyroid function tests and lipid profile.Results:A total of 496 rural subjects were enrolled. Among them, six had T4/T3 values above the normal levels and were excluded. Out of 490 subjects, 16.3% had thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) values more than 5 μIU/ml and were considered as the SCH group. The remaining 410 subjects, grouped as the euthyroid group (EUT). Median values of total cholesterol (TC) and triglycerides (TG) were increased and statistically significant in the SCH group compared with EUT group. There was no difference between the groups with respect to high-density lipoprotein and low-density lipoprotein. Dyslipidemia was common in the SCH group compared to the EUT group (P = 0.0308). In the SCH group, TG levels were elevated in 68.7% vs 29.7% in the EUT group (P < 0.0001) and TC levels were increased in 26.2% of subjects in the SCH group compared to 13.6% in the EUT group (P = 0.0046).Conclusions:SCH is an important endocrinological health problem in India. Dyslipidemia is more common in the SCH group and the consequences can be detrimental, leading to complications. Therefore, routine serum TSH estimation to detect SCH in clinical practice as a screening test is recommended.

INTRODUCTION

Thyroid hormones are important for the metabolism of carbohydrate, protein, and lipids. Hypothyroidism is characterized by a wide spectrum of clinical features ranging from an overt state of myxedema to an asymptomatic/subclinical condition. In India, hypothyroidism is the most common of thyroid disorders affecting one in 10 adults.[1] In India, it has been estimated that around 42 million people suffer from thyroid diseases.[1] In India, the prevalence of hypothyroidism is 11%, when compared to only 2% in the UK and 46% in the USA and is more in inland cities when compared to coastal cities.[1] Subclinical hypothyroidism (SCH) is defined as a condition with elevated thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) levels accompanied by normal thyroxine (T4) without clinical signs and symptoms of hypothyroidism.[2] The importance of studying SCH lies in the fact that it is much more common than overt hypothyroidism and SCH patients are at risk of progression to overt hypothyroidism in 2% to 6% per year and the risk is more if thyroperoxidase (TPO) antibodies are present.[2] Various epidemiological studies in India have shown a prevalence rate of 9.4%–24.4% for SCH among the general population.[3456] According to hospital-based cross-sectional study from India, SCH prevalence was 24.7% among females who are more than 50 years old.[7]

Thyroid hormone involved in the regulation of lipid synthesis, absorption, and metabolism. It is an established fact that dyslipidemia occurs in individuals with overt hypothyroidism and are reversible with levothyroxine therapy.[8] Therefore, evaluation for hypothyroidism in hyperlipidemia patients is recommended as part of the initial workup. Approximately around 1 to 11% of all dyslipidemia patients have SCH.[8] Even though dyslipidemia is well established in overt hypothyroidism individuals, the association between SCH and dyslipidemia is still controversial and the studies have also been inconsistent.[8] The importance of SCH being the association with an increased risk of coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, and heart failure.[2] Dyslipidemia is one of many cardiovascular risk factors that has been associated with SCH. Other risk factors in SCH include diastolic hypertension, impaired endothelial function, increased arterial stiffness, and altered coagulation parameters.[8] Overall, SCH may be associated with modestly increased cardiovascular risk, especially among younger individuals.[8] According to Rotterdam study, SCH emerged as an independent risk factor for aortic atherosclerosis and myocardial infarction in elderly women.[9] Study from south India observed that SCH with a TSH ≥10.0 mIU/L identified as a potentially modifiable risk factor for heart failure in older adults.[10] In women, SCH often causes dysfunctional uterine bleeding, increased incidence of preeclampsia, and poor pregnancy outcomes.[611]

Studies have been published highlighting the importance of SCH in dyslipidemic patients.[8] There are only a few studies regarding the association between SCH and dyslipidemia among the rural population of India.[1213] As of today, there are no standard guidelines to screen SCH in the general population. Therefore, there is a need for estimating the prevalence of SCH in the rural population along with its association with lipid profile, which may aid in framing policies for the effective management and follow-up of SCH to prevent unnecessary complications. As Chamarajanagara Institute of Medical Sciences (CIMS) caters to a diverse patient base, primarily serving the rural and vulnerable populations of the Chamarajanagar district in Karnataka, this study was undertaken to shed light on this subject.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and setting:- A prospective observation study was carried out over a period of 18 months from August 2024 to July 2025 in the department of medicine, CIMS hospital, Chamarajanagar.

Study participants

Chamarajanagar district is one of the backward districts in Karnataka with 80% rural population (https://censusofindia.net/karnataka/chamarajanagar/578). Patients attending Outpatient Department/admitted in the medicine department, CIMS hospital, Chamarajanagar. Patients belonging to the age group of more than 18 years and less than 75 years were included after taking written informed consent to participate in the study. Patients with thyroid disease/those who have taken medications affecting thyroid function (such as glucocorticoids, amiodarone in the past 3 months)/antithyroid drugs/neck radiotherapy/neck surgery/patients with known dyslipidemia and using lipid-lowering drugs/pregnancy and postpartum women/malignancy, renal and hepatic failure cases/chronic illnesses, such as coronary heart disease, obesity, diabetes mellitus, were excluded from the study.

Sample size and sampling method:-The sample size was calculated based on the prevalence of SCH in the Indian population to be 14.7%[14] with 5% absolute allowable error and the alpha of 5% with a confidence interval of 95%, the sample size was 193. This was a consecutive sampling. To compensate for the precision inaccuracy when applied to the study, a design effect of 2 was considered, and we needed a total of 400 (rounded off) samples.

The patients fulfilling the inclusion criteria were enrolled in the study.

Study tool

All patients’ serum samples were processed for thyroid function tests [T3, T4, and TSH] and lipid profile. Lipid estimations were carried out using an automated analyzer (Hitachi 902 fully automated biochemistry analyzer; Roche, Mannheim, Germany) and commercial kits (Roche, Mannheim, Germany). The normal range for total cholesterol (TC) (150–200 mg/dl), triglycerides (TG) (0–150 mg/dl), high-density lipoprotein (HDL) (35.3–79.5 mg/dl for males, 42–82 mg/dl for females), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) (0–130 mg/dl) as provided by manufacturers of kits. Thyroid function tests were performed by chemiluminescence immunoassay analyzer using Maglumi 800. The normal range for TSH (0.3 to 5 mIU/L), T4 (5 to 11 mcg/dl), and T3 (70 to 204 ng/dl) is provided by manufacturers of kits.

Data collection

The detailed history of all such patients, including any comorbidities, was taken; physical and relevant clinical examination was performed. Data were collected and entered in the proforma. CIMS Institutional Ethical Committee clearance [IEC Ref. No. CIMS/IEC-05/08/24] and necessary permission from the CIMS authority were taken. Written informed consent was taken from the patients/attendants.

Statistical analysis

Data collection was entered in MS Excel and analyzed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 21. Descriptive statistical measures, such as percentage, mean, median, and standard deviation, were applied. Inferential statistical tests, like the Chi-square test, Kruskal–Wallis test, and Shapiro–wilk test, were applied. Data were interpreted as statistically significant at a P-value less than 0.05.

RESULTS

A total of 496 rural subjects having a Below Poverty Line card were enrolled for the study. Among them, T4/T3 values were above the normal levels in six subjects and were excluded from the final data analysis. Most of the subjects are between 41 and 50 years of age (23.8%). The median age of the subjects was 46 years, with an interquartile range from 36 to 60 years. Out of 490 subjects, 281 (57.3%) were men and 209 (42.7%) were women. Baseline parameters of thyroid hormones and lipid profile of 490 subjects are shown in Table 1. Out of 490 subjects, 16.3% had TSH values more than 5 μIU/ml and were considered as the SCH group. The remaining 410 were grouped as the euthyroid (EUT) group. There is no gender difference between the two groups (P = 0.477). There is no difference of T3 and T4 median values between SCH and EUT groups [Table 2]. Median values of TC and TG were increased and statistically significant in the SCH group of subjects when compared with the EUT group. However, there was no significant difference of the median values between the groups with respect to HDL and LDL parameters [Table 2]. Dyslipidemia was more common in the SCH group when compared to the EUT group and statistically significant (P = 0.0308) [Graph 1]. Gender-wise dyslipidemia between males and females was not significant (P = 0.705). Similarly, TC and TG levels were elevated above the cutoff values, which is statistically significant in the SCH group when compared with the EUT group. In the SCH group, TG levels were elevated in 68.7% vs 29.7% in the EUT group (P < 0.0001) and TC levels were increased in 26.2% of subjects in the SCH group when compared to 13.6% in the EUT group (P = 0.0046) [Table 3]. However, there was no difference between the groups with respect to HDL and LDL parameters [Table 3]. TSH levels positively correlated with TG, TC, and LDL parameters [Graph 2].

DISCUSSION

The importance of studying SCH lies in the fact that they have a high rate of progression to clinically overt hypothyroidism.[2] In the current study, out of 490 subjects enrolled, 80 (16.3%) of them were found to have SCH. Among subjects with SCH, 97.6% had TSH ≤10 μIU/ml. In India, various epidemiological studies have shown a prevalence rate of 9.4%–24.4% for SCH among the general population.[3456] In a study from north India, SCH was detected in 10.25% of the 800 adult subjects and from south India in 9.4% of the 986 subjects.[34] Goyal et al.[6] observed SCH in 18% of perimenopausal women. A study from Jammu identified SCH in 24.4% of cases, which included 18% of females and 6.4% of males in a study group of 500 patients.[5] In a Colorado study of 25,862 subjects, 10% found to have SCH, and among them, 74% had TSH <10 μIU/ml.[15] A study from China found SCH in 13.03% of women.[16] Rohil et al.[17] in Nepal was detected SCH in 20.42% of their subjects. In Riyadh, the prevalence of SCH in adults visiting primary health care was 10.3%.[18] Ojha et al.[19] reported the pooled prevalence of hypothyroidism in Indian women with infertility as 28% in their systematic review. In a meta-analysis by Yadav et al.,[20] the pooled prevalence of hypothyroidism in Indian pregnant women is 11.07% and SCH is 9.51%. The prevalence of SCH depends on many factors such as age, gender, population, and family history.[18] Therefore, there is a wide variation of SCH among the various countries.

The association between SCH and dyslipidemia is still controversial and remains ambiguous in view of conflicting reports across the world.[81421] Also, there is a wide variation in association among the various parameters of lipid profile with SCH. In the current study, dyslipidemia was more common in the SCH group; TC and TG levels were elevated above the cutoff values significantly in the SCH group when compared with the EUT group. However, there was no difference between the groups with respect to HDL and LDL levels. Median values of TC and TG were increased significantly in the SCH group when compared with the EUT group. However, there was no significant difference with respect to HDL and LDL parameters. Similar observations of the current study were noticed by Sabharwal et al.[22] from Jammu.

A study from Western India observed that TC and LDL are higher in SCH patients as compared to EUT patients.[12] Comparable findings to this study were made by Asranna et al.[23] In a Colorado study, among 25862 participants, TC, TG, and LDL levels were significantly greater in patients with SCH than those of euthyroid subjects.[15] Lai et al.[24] observed significantly higher TG and lower HDL in patients with SCH than in euthyroid individuals in their study on 1534 Chinese adults. A study from Bangladesh also observed that dyslipidemia was more prevalent in SCH patients compared to the control group.[25]

However, lipid profile parameters were not different in SCH and EUT groups according to a few studies across the countries in the world.[1426272829] Vierhapper et al.[30] also showed that lipid profiles were similar in SCH and EUT subjects. Out of 1423 women, Bell et al.[29] observed that there were no significant differences between SCH and controls for serum lipids. Marwaha et al.[14] from India, observed that in subjects with SCH-1 (TSH = 5 to <10 μIU/ml), did not show any significant difference in the prevalence/lipid-level abnormalities compared to controls. However, TC and LDL levels became significantly elevated in subjects with SCH (TSH >10 μIU/ml) compared with controls and SCH-1 subjects. However, serum HDL was significantly lower in subjects with SCH-2 compared with controls. Even though Hueston and Pearson observed the changes in lipid parameters between SCH and EUTs, but the differences were statistically insignificant after adjusting for age and sex.[21]

In the present study, TSH levels positively correlated with TC, TG, and LDL lipid profile parameters. A study from Syria observed a positive weak correlation of TSH with LDL, TG, and TC, without any correlation with HDL.[27] Some authors have shown a positive correlation of TSH values with lipid profile parameters in SCH.[612141625] Houfa Geng et al.[31] reported that even mildly elevated TSH is associated with an atherogenic lipid profile in SCH postmenopausal women. Iqbal et al.[32] showed a positive association between serum TSH levels and TC and LDL levels. These lipid levels are reduced with thyroxine treatment in subjects with SCH. The Rotterdam study concluded that SCH is strongly and independently associated with aortic atherosclerosis and myocardial infarction in elderly women.[9] A study from north India revealed an increased incidence of dyslipidemia in SCH patients with positive anti-TPO antibodies.[13]

The important question is whether to start levothyroxine therapy or not in SCH. The scenarios for the initiation of therapy include: TSH is >10 μIU/ml, positive TPO antibody, and women of reproductive age.[2]

Limitation

Anti-TPO antibody and iodine status could not be performed in the present study.

Hospital-based recruitment of the subjects, no longitudinal follow-up, and the inability to infer causality from a cross-sectional design.

CONCLUSIONS

It can be reiterated that SCH is an important endocrinological health problem in India. As the consequences of SCH can be detrimental, clinician should have the good art of taking history related to hypothyroidism and examination of thyroid disease.

Half-plane Green‘s function for anti-plane elastic 1D hexagonal QCs
Tsviatko V. Rangelov, George D. Manolis, Petia S. Dineva

Abstract
This paper presents an analytical derivation of a frequency-dependent fundamental solution plus a Green’s function for the uni-dimensional, hexagonal quasicrystal sheet subjected to elastic waves under anti-plane strain conditions. Furthermore, closed-form solutions for the free-fields developing in this sheet for propagating shear waves are also obtained. The analysis presented here provides a foundation for understanding quasicrystal dynamic behavior and for advancing relevant computational methods.

1
Introduction

Quasicrystals (QC) were first discovered in 1982 by
Shechtman et al. 1984
and represent a distinct class of materials with quasiperiodic atomic arrangements that are neither periodic like conventional crystals nor disordered like amorphous solids. This unique structure imparts exceptional physical and mechanical properties, including low friction and adhesion, high hardness (
Tsai et al. 1992
), enhanced wear resistance (
Chen et al. 2016
), improved corrosion resistance, and low electrical and thermal conductivities, making them attractive for advanced applications in the aerospace, automotive, energy and protective coating industries (
Kenzari et al. 2012
). Structurally, QC are intermetallic solids with long-range quasiperiodic order and no conventional crystal periodicity, classified as one-, two-, and three-dimensional (
Jaric and Nelson 1990
). In one-dimensional (1D) QC, atomic arrangements are quasiperiodic along one direction and periodic in the perpendicular plane (
Wang et al. 1997
). This work focuses exclusively on homogeneous, elastic 1D hexagonal QC, which are of particular scientific interest, especially in light of the seminal work of
Merlin et al. 1985
that investigated their quasi-periodicity.

Despite the growing interest of the engineering society on mechanical modelling of QCs, the research field on the fundamental solutions and Green‘s functions for quasicrystal elastodynamics remain scarce. The present work addresses this issue by deriving frequency-dependent solutions for homogeneous, elastic 1D QC under anti-plane wave motion for both a finite and an infinitely-extending sheet. These solutions underpin the development of boundary integral equation methods (
Manolis et al. 2017
) that would have to rely on the availability of families of fundamental solutions and/or Green’s functions for quasicrystals. These methods offer certain advantages, including reduced dimensionality, efficient handling of complex geometries, solution evaluation at selected points without full-domain discretization, semi-analytical accuracy, natural treatment of infinite boundaries, and precise computation of stress concentrations near defects. Leveraging these benefits requires rigorous theoretical development combined with careful numerical implementation. To this end, the paper is organized as follows: Sect.
2
introduces the problem statement, while in Sect.
3
we derive the frequency-dependent elastodynamic fundamental solution and the half-plane Green‘s function. Time-harmonic SH plane wave solutions for both a finite and an infinitely-extending QC sheet are also derived and discussed in Sect.
4
. Finally, some concluding remarks are given in Sect.
5
.

2
Problem Statement

In the Cartesian coordinate system
\displaystyle Ox_{1}x_{2}x_{3}
we considered anti-plane strain wave motion in an infinitely-extending 1D hexagonal QC sheet by assuming qasiperiodicity along the axis
\displaystyle Ox_{3}
. Thus, the field variables are independent of the
\displaystyle x_{3}
coordinate and we have an anti-plane strain, phonon-phason coupling elasticity problem in the frequency domain. We now define

(i)

a phonon displacement field
\displaystyle u_{3}=u_{3}(\mathbf{x},\omega)
along with the corresponding stresses
\displaystyle\sigma_{3i}(\mathbf{x},\omega)
and strain
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{3i}(\mathbf{x},\omega)=0.5u_{3,i}
fields, where
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
\displaystyle\omega
is the frequency of vibration, and

(ii)

a phason displacement field
\displaystyle w_{3}=w_{3}(\mathbf{x},\omega)
, with the corresponding stress
\displaystyle H_{3i}(\mathbf{x},\omega)
and strain
\displaystyle\omega_{3i}(\mathbf{x},\omega)=w_{3,i}(\mathbf{x},\omega)
fields. Note that commas indicate spatial derivatives with respect to indices
\displaystyle i=1,2
.

The constitutive QC law is, see
Wang and Pan 2008
:

\sigma_{3i}=2c_{44}\varepsilon_{3i}+R_{3}\omega_{3i},\quad H_{3i}=2R_{3}\varepsilon_{3i}+K_{2}\omega_{3i}.

(2.1)

In the above
\displaystyle c_{44}
is the elastic constant of the phonon field,
\displaystyle R_{3}
is the coupling coefficient of the phonon-phason filds,
\displaystyle K_{2}
is the elastic constant of the phason field.
These material constants satisfy the following conditions for a well posed problem:

c_{44}>0,\quad K_{2}>0,\quad R_{3}>0,\quad c_{44}K_{2}-R_{3}^{2}>0.

(2.2)

The equations of dynamic equilibrium in time domain are

\sigma_{3j,j}=\rho\frac{\partial u_{3}}{\partial t^{2}},\quad H_{3j,j}=\rho\frac{\partial w_{3}}{\partial t^{2}},

(2.3)

where
\displaystyle\rho
is the material density and
\displaystyle t
is the time variable. For time time-harmonic excitations and by inserting the constitutive law for the stresses, the equations of motion attain the following form:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}c_{44}\Delta u_{3}+R_{3}\Delta w_{3}+\rho\omega^{2}u_{3}=0,\\
R_{3}\Delta u_{3}+K_{2}\Delta w_{3}+\rho\omega^{2}w_{3}=0,\end{array}\right.

(2.4)

where
\displaystyle\Delta=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}
is the 2D Laplace operator. Finally, on the surface defined by a normal vector
\displaystyle\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},n_{2})
, the phonon and phason tractions are defined as follows
\displaystyle t_{3}=\sigma_{3i}n_{i}
,
\displaystyle G_{3}=H_{3i}n_{i}
.

The derivation below of fundamental solution, Green‘s function and free-field waves in QCs is influenced by the anti-plane isotropic case. The only new tool is the decoupling of the QCs system of equations (
2.4
) and (
3.1
) with a help of matrix
\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}
of Eq. (
3.6
).

3
Fundamental Solution and Green‘s Function for Quasicristals

We derive here both a fundamental solution and a Green‘ function for dynamic loads in the form of elastic wave sweeps, which are specific to a QC sheet of infinite and of semi-infinite extent, respectively.

3.1
Fundamental solution derivation

The fundamental solution of Eqs. (
3.2
) is a matrix valued function
\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\ast}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{\ast}_{31}&u^{\ast}_{32}\\
w^{\ast}_{31}&w^{\ast}_{32}\end{array}\right)
, that satisfies the following system of equations:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}c_{44}\Delta u^{\ast}_{3j}+R_{3}\Delta w^{\ast}_{3j}+\rho\omega^{2}u^{\ast}_{3j}=-\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{\xi})\delta_{j1},\\[1.0pt]
\\
R_{3}\Delta u^{\ast}_{3j}+K_{2}\Delta w^{\ast}_{3j}+\rho\omega^{2}w^{\ast}_{3j}=-\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{\xi})\delta_{j2}.\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

Both field
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}
and source
\displaystyle\mathbf{\xi}
points lie on the
\displaystyle Ox_{1}x_{2}
plane, all indices range as
\displaystyle i,j=1,2
, while
\displaystyle\delta(\textbf{x}-\mathbb{\xi})
is Dirac‘s delta function and
\displaystyle\delta_{ji}
is the Kroneker delta.
The stresses corresponding to the fundamental solution are denoted as
\displaystyle\sigma_{3ij}
for the phonon field and
\displaystyle H_{3ij}
for the phason field:

\sigma_{3ij}=2c_{44}\varepsilon_{3i,j}+R_{3}w_{3i,j},\quad H_{3ij}=2R_{3}\varepsilon_{3i,j}+K_{2}w_{3i,j},

(3.2)

with
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{3ij}=\frac{1}{2}u^{\ast}_{3i,j}
and
\displaystyle\omega_{3ij}=w^{\ast}_{3i,j}
the corresponding strains.
Equations (
3.1
) can be written in matrix form as

[\textbf{C}\Delta+\mathbf{\Gamma}]\textbf{v}^{\ast}(\textbf{x},\mathbf{\xi},\omega)=-\delta(\textbf{x}-\mathbf{\xi})\textbf{I}_{2},

(3.3)

where
\displaystyle\mathbf{C}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}c_{44}&R_{3}\\
R_{3}&K_{2}\end{array}\right)
is symmetric and positive definite,
\displaystyle\mathbf{\Gamma}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho\omega^{2}&0\\
0&\rho\omega^{2}\end{array}\right)=\rho\omega^{2}I_{2}
, and
\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2}
is the unit
\displaystyle 2\times 2
matrix.

In order to solve Eq. (
3.3
), we find its
canonical form by diagonalizing matrix
\displaystyle\mathbf{C}
, see
Gel’fand 1961
.
We start with the eigenvalues
\displaystyle a_{1,2}
of matrix
\displaystyle\mathbf{C}
, which are positive due to constraint Eq. (
2.2
)

a_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}[\hbox{Tr}\mathbf{C}\pm\sqrt{(\hbox{Tr}\mathbf{C})^{2}-4\det\mathbf{C}}].

(3.4)

We have that
\displaystyle\hbox{Tr}\mathbf{C}=c_{44}+K_{2}
is the trace and
\displaystyle\det\mathbf{C}=c_{44}K_{2}-R_{3}^{2}
is the determinant of matrix

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}
. The two corresponding
normalized eigenvectors
\displaystyle|\mathbf{q}_{i}|=1
of
\displaystyle\mathbf{C}
are
\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{1}=(q_{1}^{1},q_{1}^{2})
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{2}=(q_{2}^{1},q_{2}^{2})

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{q}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(c_{44}-a_{1})^{2}+R^{2}_{3}}}\left(R_{3},c_{44}-a_{1}\right),\\
\mathbf{q}_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(K_{2}-a_{2})^{2}+R^{2}_{3}}}\left(K_{2}-a_{2},R_{3}\right)\end{array}\right..

(3.5)

A compact form emerges by introducing angle
\displaystyle\psi=\arccos\frac{R_{3}}{\sqrt{(c_{44}-a_{1})^{2}+R_{3}^{2}}}
, so that
\displaystyle q_{1}^{1}=q_{2}^{2}=\cos\psi,q_{1}^{2}=-q_{2}^{1}=\sin\psi

Then the orthogonal matrix
\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}
can be express as

\mathbf{Q}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}q^{1}_{1}&q^{1}_{2}\\
q^{2}_{1}&q^{2}_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos\psi&-\sin\psi\\
\sin\psi&\cos\psi\end{array}\right),

(3.6)

and transforms the basis to canonical form as

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Q}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{1}&0\\
0&a_{2}\end{array}\right).

The next step is to define a
\displaystyle 2\times 2
matrix function
\displaystyle\mathbf{h}
such that
\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\ast}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{h}
, with
\displaystyle\mathbf{h}
satisfies the equation

[\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Q}\Delta+\mathbf{\Gamma}\mathbf{Q}]\mathbf{h}=-\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{\xi})\mathbf{I}_{2}.

(3.7)

Since
\displaystyle\mathbf{\Gamma}=\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}\mathbf{Q}
, a left-hand side multiplication of Eq. (
3.7
) by
\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos\psi&\sin\psi\\
-\sin\psi&\cos\psi\end{array}\right)
yelds

[\mathbf{A}\Delta+\mathbf{\Gamma}]\mathbf{h}=-\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{\xi}),

(3.8)

where
\displaystyle A=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{1}&0\\
0&a_{2}\end{array}\right)
.

Equation (
3.8
) consists of four partial differential
equations of Helmholtz type

[a\Delta+\rho\omega^{2}]p=-\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{\xi})q,

(3.9)

with solution (
Vladimirov 1971
) is

\displaystyle p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\xi},\omega)=\frac{q}{2\pi a}K_{0}(-ikr)=\frac{iq}{4a}H^{(1)}_{0}(kr)
,
where the radial distance between source and receiver is
\displaystyle r=\sqrt{(x_{1}-\xi_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-\xi_{2})^{2}}
,
\displaystyle k=\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{a}}\omega
is the wave number
and
\displaystyle K_{0},H^{(1)}_{0}
are the zero order Kelvin and first kind, zero order Hankel functions, respectively.

Finally the fundamental solution
\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\ast}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{\ast}_{31}&u^{\ast}_{32}\\
w^{\ast}_{31}&w^{\ast}_{32}\end{array}\right)
has the form

\begin{array}[]{l}v^{\ast}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{h}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos\psi&-\sin\psi\\
\sin\psi&\cos\psi\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}h^{u}_{31}&h^{u}_{32}\\
h^{w}_{31}&h^{w}_{32}\end{array}\right)\\
=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos\psi h^{u}_{31}-\sin\psi h^{w}_{31}&\cos\psi h^{u}_{32}-\sin\psi h^{w}_{32}\\
\sin\psi h^{u}_{31}+\cos\psi h^{w}_{31}&\sin\psi h^{u}_{32}+\cos\psi h^{w}_{32}\end{array}\right)\end{array}

(3.10)

with

\begin{array}[]{ll}&h^{u}_{31}=\frac{\cos\psi}{2\pi a_{1}}K_{0}(-ik_{1}r),\quad h^{u}_{32}=\frac{\sin\psi}{2\pi a_{1}}K_{0}(-ik_{1}r),\\
&h^{w}_{31}=\frac{-\sin\psi}{2\pi a_{2}}K_{0}(-ik_{2}r),\quad h^{w}_{32}=\frac{\cos\psi}{2\pi a_{2}}K_{0}(-ik_{2}r).\end{array}

(3.11)

The corresponding stresses and tractions are obtained from Eq. (
3.2
) as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}t_{3i}=\sigma_{3ij}n_{j},\quad\sigma_{3ij}=c_{44}u^{\ast}_{3i,j}+R_{3}w^{\ast}_{3i,j},\\
G_{3i}=H_{3ij}n_{j}(x),\quad H_{3ij}=R_{3}u^{\ast}_{3i,j}+K_{2}w^{\ast}_{3i,j}.\end{array}\right.

(3.12)

The strains
\displaystyle u^{\ast}_{3i,j}
and
\displaystyle w^{\ast}_{3i,j}
are computed below taking into account that the derivative
\displaystyle K^{\prime}_{0}(z)=-K_{1}(z)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{v}^{\ast}_{,j}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{h}_{,j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos\psi&-\sin\psi\\
\sin\psi&\cos\psi\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}h^{u}_{31,j}&h^{u}_{32,j}\\
h^{w}_{31,j}&h^{w}_{32,j}\end{array}\right)\\
=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos\psi h^{u}_{31,j}-\sin\psi h^{w}_{31,j}&\cos\psi h^{u}_{32,j}-\sin\psi h^{w}_{32,j}\\
\sin\psi h^{u}_{31,j}+\cos\psi h^{w}_{31,j}&\sin\psi h^{u}_{32,j}+\cos\psi h^{w}_{32,j}\end{array}\right)\end{array}

(3.13)

with

\begin{array}[]{ll}&h^{u}_{31,j}=-ik_{1}\frac{\cos\psi}{2\pi a_{1}}\frac{r_{j}}{r}K_{1}(-ik_{1}r),\quad h^{u}_{32,j}=-ik_{1}\frac{\sin\psi}{2\pi a_{1}}\frac{r_{j}}{r}K_{1}(-ik_{1}r),\\
&h^{w}_{31,j}=ik_{2}\frac{\sin\psi}{2\pi a_{2}}\frac{r_{j}}{r}K_{1}(-ik_{2}r),\quad h^{w}_{32,j}=-ik_{2}\frac{\cos\psi}{2\pi a_{2}}\frac{r_{j}}{r}K_{1}(-ik_{2}r).\end{array}

(3.14)

Thus, the fundamental solution and its corresponding stresses and tractions for the 1D hexagonal QC depend on its material properties, frequency
\displaystyle\omega
of the applied load and radial distance
\displaystyle r=|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{\xi}|
between source and receiver points.

3.2
Green‘s function derivation

The mathematical definition of a ‘fundamental solution’ is the solution of a differential equation with a singular Dirac‘s function at the right-hand side and just a radiation boundary condition, while the term ‘Green‘s function’ is understood as a fundamental solution that satisfies specific boundary conditions. A Green‘s function, if it exists, is unique, while a fundamental solution is not - if we add any solution of a homogeneous differential equation we obtain again a fundamental solution.

We define a half-plane as
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}_{-}=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2}),x_{2}<0\}
and seek a solution
\displaystyle g^{\ast}
to Eq. (
3.1
) which satisfies traction-free boundary condition on
\displaystyle x_{2}=0
,

t^{g^{\ast}}_{3j}|_{x_{2}=0}=c_{44}g^{\ast u}_{3j,2}+R_{3}g^{\ast w}_{3j,2}=0,\quad H^{g^{\ast}}_{3j}|_{x_{2}=0}=R_{3}g^{\ast u}_{3j,2}+K_{2}g^{\ast w}_{3j,2}=0.

(3.15)

Thus, a correction term will be added to the fundamental solution of (
3.1
) in the form

\mathbf{y}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{31}&u_{32}\\
w_{31}&w_{32}\end{array}\right),

(3.16)

This correction term is the solution of Eq. (
2.4
) and the tractions corresponding to the Green‘s function
\displaystyle\mathbf{g}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\xi},\omega)=\mathbf{v}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\xi},\omega)+\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\xi},\omega)
now satisfy Eq. (
3.1
). We use the notation
\displaystyle t^{g^{\ast}}
and
\displaystyle G^{g^{\ast}}
for the tractions of the phonon and phason fields corresponding to the half-plane Green‘s function. Viewed alternatively, we are modifying the fundamental solution
\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\xi},\omega)
so that its tractions
\displaystyle t^{g^{\ast}}_{3j}|_{x_{2}=0}
and
\displaystyle G^{g^{\ast}}_{3j}|_{x_{2}=0}
are zero along the half-plane boundary.
Since the fundamental solution
\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\ast}
is a
\displaystyle 2\times 2
matrix valued function, see Eqs. (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) that depends only on
\displaystyle r=\sqrt{(x_{1}-\xi_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-\xi_{2})^{2}}
and
\displaystyle\omega
, for
\displaystyle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{-}
we can denote it as
\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\ast}(r,\omega)
. We can find the Green‘s function using the method of images as in the isotropic anti-plane case. For this, denote
\displaystyle\tilde{r}=\sqrt{(x_{1}-\xi_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}+\xi_{2})^{2}}
and let us define the correction term as
\displaystyle\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{v}^{\ast}(\tilde{r},\omega)
. It is clear that
\displaystyle 2\times 2
matrix function
\displaystyle\mathbf{y}
in Eq.(
3.16
) satisfy the Eq. (
2.4
).

Since
\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{r}_{2}}{\tilde{r}}=-\frac{r_{2}}{r}
we have that
\displaystyle v^{\ast}_{,2}|_{x_{2}=0}=-y_{,2}|_{x_{2}=0}
and correspondingly Eq. (
3.15
) holds.

In sum, both the fundamental solution and the half-plane Green‘s function depend on the material properties
\displaystyle c_{44},R_{3},K_{2},\rho
of the 1D hexagonal QC, on the frequency
\displaystyle\omega
of the applied load and the distance
\displaystyle r
between source and receiver points, and distance
\displaystyle\tilde{r}
between source and image of the receiver point.

Remark 3.1
.

Consider the case
\displaystyle R_{3}=0
, then the system of Eq. (
2.4
) decouples and we have two anti-plane isotropic equations for
\displaystyle u_{3}
and for
\displaystyle w_{3}
. The eigenvalues in Eq. (
3.4
) are
\displaystyle a_{1}=c_{44}
,
\displaystyle a_{2}=K_{2}
and wave numbers are:
\displaystyle k_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{c_{44}}}
,
\displaystyle k_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{K_{2}}}
. Since now
\displaystyle\psi=0
, then in Eq. (
3.6
) matrix
\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{I}_{2}
, then the fundamental solution
\displaystyle v^{\ast}
in Eq. (
3.10
), using Eq. (
3.11
) has the form

\mathbf{v}^{\ast}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{\ast}_{31}&u^{\ast}_{32}\\
w^{\ast}_{31}&w^{\ast}_{32}\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{c_{44}}K_{0}(-ik_{1}r)&0\\
0&\frac{1}{K_{2}}K_{0}(-ik_{2}r)\end{array}\right)

(3.17)

These solutions
\displaystyle u^{\ast}_{31}
and
\displaystyle w^{\ast}_{32}
coincide with solutions for the isotropic anti-plane case with constants
\displaystyle c_{44}
( for the phonon field) and
\displaystyle K_{2}
(for the phason field) respectively derived in many works, see for example
Vladimirov 1971
,
Kobayashi 1985
,
Dominguez 1993
.

Concerning the Green‘s function
\displaystyle\mathbf{g}^{\ast}(x,\xi,\omega)
for the case
\displaystyle R_{3}=0
we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{g}^{\ast}(x,\xi,\omega)=\mathbf{v}^{\ast}(r,\omega)+\mathbf{v}^{\ast}(\tilde{r},\omega)\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{c_{44}}(K_{0}(-ik_{1}r)+K_{0}(-ik_{1}\tilde{r}))&0\\
0&\frac{1}{K_{2}}(K_{0}(-ik_{2}r)+K_{0}(-ik_{2}\tilde{r}))\end{array}\right)\end{array}

(3.18)

These Green‘s functions coincide with Green‘s function for the isotropic anti-plane case with constants
\displaystyle c_{44}
(for the phonon field) and
\displaystyle K_{2}
(for the phason field) respectively shown, for example in
Kobayashi 1985
.

4
Free-field Waves in Quasicrystals

We study here the passage of incident waves in QC of both infinite and finite extent. Note that this is scalar wave propagation, albeit coupled, with the closest equivalent being transverse waves in a string. In this classical case, there is a forward moving wave, and upon meeting a boundary generates a reflected backward moving wave.

4.1
Full-plane wave solutions

We have a freely propagating harmonic shear (S) wave in the QC and are asking for a solution in the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u^{in}_{3}\\
w^{in}_{3}\end{array}\right)=A\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\zeta_{1}\\
\zeta_{2}\end{array}\right)e^{ik(x_{1}\cos\varphi+x_{2}\sin\varphi)}

(4.1)

where
\displaystyle\varphi\in(0,\pi/2)
is the incident angle in respect to
\displaystyle Ox_{2}
axis. We have
\displaystyle A
the amplitude of this incident wave,
\displaystyle(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
the direction of the wave propagation and
\displaystyle k
is the wave number all to be determined. The vector function Eq. (
4.1
) satisfies equation of motion (
2.4
), i.e.,

\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}c_{44}\Delta&R_{3}\Delta\\
R_{3}\Delta&K_{2}\Delta\end{array}\right)+\rho\omega^{2}\mathbf{I}_{2}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u^{in}_{3}\\
w^{in}_{3}\end{array}\right)=0.

(4.2)

After reducing the term
\displaystyle Ae^{ik(x_{1}\cos\varphi+x_{2}\sin\varphi)}
, we obtain

\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}-c_{44}k^{2}+\rho\omega^{2}&-R_{3}k^{2}\\
-R_{3}k^{2}&-K_{2}k^{2}+\rho\omega^{2}\end{array}\right)\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\zeta_{1}\\
\zeta_{2}\end{array}\right)=0.

(4.3)

For a nontrivial solution of Eqs. (
4.3
) to exists, the determinant of the system must be set to zero, i.e.,
\displaystyle\det[-\mathbf{C}k^{2}+\rho\omega^{2}\mathbf{I}_{2}]=0
. Now define vector
\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\tau_{1}\\
\tau_{2}\end{array}\right)
such that
\displaystyle Q\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\tau_{1}\\
\tau_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\zeta_{1}\\
\zeta_{2}\end{array}\right)
, where matrix
\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}
is given in Eq. (
3.6
). Left-hand side multiplication of Eq. (
4.3
) with
\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{-1}
yelds

\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}-a_{1}&0\\
0&-a_{2}\end{array}\right)k^{2}+\rho\omega^{2}\mathbf{I}_{2}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\tau_{1}\\
\tau_{2}\end{array}\right)=0.

(4.4)

In the above
\displaystyle a_{1}>a_{2}
are the two positive eigenvalues of matrix
\displaystyle\mathbf{C}
, see Eq. (
3.4
) and
\displaystyle a_{1}
,
\displaystyle a_{2}
are defined as the effective shear moduli of the 2 phases. Then

\det\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}-a_{1}&0\\
0&-a_{2}\end{array}\right)k^{2}+\rho\omega^{2}\mathbf{I}_{2}\right]=(-a_{1}k^{2}+\rho\omega^{2})(-a_{2}k^{2}+\rho\omega^{2})=0.

(4.5)

Finally, we find two orthogonal vectors as solutions of of Eq. (
4.4
), for
\displaystyle k_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{\rho\omega^{2}}{a_{1}}}
,
\displaystyle k_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{\rho\omega^{2}}{a_{2}}}
respectively:

\tau^{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\tau^{1}_{1}\\
\tau^{1}_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}1\\
0\end{array}\right),\hbox{ and }\tau^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\tau^{2}_{1}\\
\tau^{2}_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}0\\
1\end{array}\right),

(4.6)

then

\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\zeta^{1}_{1}&\zeta_{1}^{2}\\
\zeta_{2}^{1}&\zeta_{2}^{2}\end{array}\right)=Q\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos\psi&-\sin\psi\\
\sin\psi&\cos\psi\end{array}\right).

(4.7)

Since in a waveguide defined by a scalar displacement will support a single propagating wave, either in the positive or negative directions, wavenumbers
\displaystyle k_{1}
and
\displaystyle k_{2}
must correspond to the photon/phason cases or a combination thereof. These propagate in the infinite, 1D hexagonal QC plane with a fixed incident angle
\displaystyle\varphi\in(0,\pi/2)
with respect to
\displaystyle Ox_{1}
axis. Using the definition of
\displaystyle\cos\psi
and
\displaystyle\sin\psi
in Eq. (
3.6
) it is easy to check that the incident wave solutions have the form:

\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u^{in,S_{1}}_{3}\\
w^{in,S_{1}}_{3}\end{array}\right)=A\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\cos\psi\\
\sin\psi\end{array}\right)e^{ik_{1}(x_{1}\cos\varphi+x_{2}\sin\varphi)}\\
\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u^{in,S_{2}}_{3}\\
w^{in,S_{2}}_{3}\end{array}\right)=A\left(\begin{array}[]{l}-\sin\psi\\
\cos\psi\end{array}\right)e^{ik_{2}(x_{1}\cos\varphi+x_{2}\sin\varphi)}\end{array}

(4.8)

Note that in contrast with an elastic, homogeneous and isotropic material, the direction vector is a constant and does not depend on the incident angle
\displaystyle\varphi
. In Eq. (
4.8
), we have two modes for the incident S wave, which are orthogonal linear combinations of the phonon (
\displaystyle u_{3}
) and phason (
\displaystyle w_{3}
) displacements. Since
\displaystyle a_{1}
and
\displaystyle a_{2}
are the effective shear moduli of the two phases, the corresponding wave speeds are
\displaystyle c_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{a_{1}}{\rho}}
and
\displaystyle c_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{a_{2}}{\rho}}
where
\displaystyle c_{1}>c_{2}
. Thus, we have the propagation of both a fast (
\displaystyle S_{1}
) and a slow (
\displaystyle S_{2}
) shear wave due to the phonon-phason coupling effect.

4.2
Half-plane wave solutions

In deriving the half-plane solutions, we follow
Achenbach 1973
for the isotropic anti-plane case. The free-field solutions are sum of incident and reflected waves such that their traction on
\displaystyle Ox_{2}
is zero.

Using the Eq. (
4.8
) there are two kind of free-field solutions.

With incident
\displaystyle S_{1}
wave:

\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u^{ff,S_{1}}_{3}\\
w^{ff,S_{1}}_{3}\end{array}\right)=A_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\cos\psi\\
\sin\psi\end{array}\right)\left(e^{ik_{1}(x_{1}\cos\varphi+x_{2}\sin\varphi)}+e^{ik_{1}(x_{1}\cos\varphi-x_{2}\sin\varphi)}\right).

(4.9)

With incident
\displaystyle S_{2}
wave:

\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u^{ff,S_{2}}_{3}\\
w^{ff,S_{2}}_{3}\end{array}\right)=A_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}-\sin\psi\\
\cos\psi\end{array}\right)\left(e^{ik_{2}(x_{1}\cos\varphi+x_{2}\sin\varphi)}+e^{ik_{2}(x_{1}\cos\varphi-x_{2}\sin\varphi)}\right).

(4.10)

Here,
\displaystyle A_{1}
and
\displaystyle A_{2}
are the amplitudes of the incident wave.
In closing, the displacement field in a homogeneous 1D elastic hexagonal QC half-plane subjected to anti-plane wave motion is presented by incident time-harmonic fast
\displaystyle S_{1}
and slow
\displaystyle S_{2}
waves which generate only reflected waves of the same type. They depend on the wave frequency, wave propagation direction, phonon-phason coupling and also on material properties of the QCs.

5
Conclusions

The aim of this work was to derive frequency-dependent fundamental solutions for the homogeneous elastic 1D QC, plus Green‘s functions in the presence of a free surface, all under anti-plane wave motion. These analytical results provide tools for understanding the dynamic behavior of QC and form a foundation for subsequent development of advanced computational methods. In particular, these solutions enable the development of mechanical models based on the boundary integral equation method, an efficient and accurate mesh-reduction technique whose advantages are well documented in the literature. Even on their own, these analytical solutions allow for the treatment of a number of problems involving QC with simple geometries.

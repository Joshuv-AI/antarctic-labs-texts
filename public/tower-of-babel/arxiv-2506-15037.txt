2BSDE with uncertain horizon and application to stochastic control in erratic environments
Alberto GENNARO, Thibaut MASTROLIA

Abstract
We investigate the existence and uniqueness of non-Markovian second-order backward stochastic differential equations with an uncertain terminal horizon and establish comparison principles under the assumption that the driver is Lipschitz continuous. The terminal time is both random and exogenous, and it may not be adapted to the Brownian filtration, leading to a singular jump in the 2BSDE decomposition. We also provide a connection between this new class of 2BSDE and a fully nonlinear PDE in a Markovian setting. Our theoretical results are applied to non-Markovian stochastic control problems in two settings: (1) when an agent seeks to maximize utility from a payoff received at an uncertain terminal time by controlling both the drift and volatility of a diffusion process; and (2) when the agent contends with volatility uncertainty stemming from an external source, referred to as Nature, and optimizes the drift in a worst-case scenario for the ambiguous volatility. We term this class of problems erratic stochastic control, reflecting the dual uncertainty in both model parameters and the timing of the terminal horizon.

1.2
Second order BSDE and volatility-controlled or ambiguous optimization

Controlling the volatility becomes more challenging in the weak formulation setting, as it involves changing the probability measure associated with a diffusion process and its volatility. This requires constructing a family of non-equivalent probability measures, denoted by
\mathbb{P}^{\alpha,\beta}
through a weak formulation and weak solution of the controlled SDE considered. The optimal control problem then consists of maximizing an objective function over a set of probability measures parameterized by the control variables
\alpha

\beta

The optimal measure corresponds to the one under which a martingale optimality principle is satisfied. As an extension of the BSDE introduced earlier, we then expect the problem to be reduced to a second-order BSDE (2BSDE for short), with terminal condition
\xi

1.4
Contribution: 2BSDE with uncertain horizon and erratic stochastic control

Motivation with crypto trading and cyber risk.

We define
erratic stochastic control
as a class of control problems in which the decision-making process must respond to highly irregular, unpredictable stochastic environments and potentially abrupt termination events. The cryptocurrency market illustrates erratic stochastic behaviors. In this domain, asset prices are influenced by extremely volatile and unpredictable factors, including random market noise, sudden regulatory interventions, and unforeseen news events. These dynamics result in behavior that is difficult to capture or forecast using traditional modeling techniques. Panic-driven behaviors have partially led to the cascading collapse of Terra (LUNA) and its associated stablecoin UST in May 2022, which triggered a rapid market-wide reaction
[
17
]
. Cryptocurrency markets frequently undergo sudden regime shifts— such as speculative bull runs, dramatic crashes, or fraudulent events as rug pulls. Market participants often operate under conditions of Knightian uncertainty, where the underlying probability distributions are unknown, ill-defined, or even unknowable, see
[
4
]
. Tracking 146 Proof-of-Work-based cryptocurrencies that started trading prior to
the end of 2014 until December 2018,
[
35
]
shows that about 60% of those
cryptocurrencies were eventually in default. In cyber-risk, Knightian uncertainty plays a crucial role, as cyber-attacks introduce greater volatility and unpredictable conditions into the system. For example, see
[
24
,
65
]
.

Contribution and structure.

In this work, we address the problem of stochastic control in erratic environments by incorporating both control and ambiguity in the volatility of a controlled diffusion, as well as high uncertainty in the termination of the optimization problem. We begin by proposing mathematical models in Section
2
, where we extend the so-called Density Hypothesis in the theory of enlargement of filtrations to a
\mathcal{P}
-density assumption that holds for the default time under a set of probability measures. We then investigate the existence, uniqueness, and comparison results for solutions in Section
3
to a second-order backward stochastic differential equation (2BSDE) with an erratic horizon of the form:

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle=\xi-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}F_{s}^{\mathbb{P}}(Y_{s},\hat{a}_{s}^{1/2}Z_{s},U_{s}),ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}Z_{s}\cdot dX_{s}^{c,\mathbb{P}}+\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}dK_{s}-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}U_{s},dH_{s}-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}dM_{s}^{\mathbb{P}},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }0\leq t\leq T,\;\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s., for every }\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}.

This section extends existing results on BSDEs with random horizons and 2BSDEs with finite or random termination to the setting of 2BSDEs with erratic termination. Our formulation also allows for the aggregation of the
U
process, in contrast to existing theories on 2BSDEs with jumps; see Remark
3.7
. We conclude this theoretical section with a connection to fully nonlinear PDE in section
3.4
. Finally, we investigate erratic stochastic control in Section
4
and explore its connection to 2BSDEs with erratic terminal times. We first present a comprehensive and general structure for the erratic control problem in Section
4.1
, unifying the volatility control and ambiguity models within our framework. We then define the 2BSDE associated with both problems in Section
4.2
. The volatility control problem is studied in Section
4.3
, followed by an analysis of the Knightian uncertainty problem in Section
4.4
.

4.4
The volatility ambiguity case: from adversarial nature to 2BSDE

We now turn to the case where the agent is facing an adversarial player, the Nature, regarding the volatility control. Similarly to the previous section, we aim at connecting the robust optimization (
13
) with the 2BSDE (
15
). Note that this is a worst-case scenario framework and its link to 2BSDE has been previously studied in
[
37
,
65
]
. We extend this result to the uncertain default horizon
T\wedge\tau
. Unlike the previous case in which the boundedness was enough to guarantee well-posedness for the optimization problem, we are facing the extra complexity of a potentially unbounded sup inf problem, where the second sub-problem becomes
-\infty
once the worst strategy of the Nature is considered. To overcome this technical difficulty, we will add another assumption ensuring the existence of an optimizer, known as Isaacs condition, from the seminal work of
[
41
]
. This assumption has been also made in the literature regarding zero-sum games in weak formulation, see for example
[
79
,
37
]
. We assume that the following condition is satisfied.

Assumption 4
.

For any
(t,x,y,z,\Sigma)\in[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathcal{S}^{+}_{d}

\inf_{b\in V_{t}(x,\Sigma)}\sup_{a\in A}f(t,x,y,z,u,a,b,\lambda)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in V_{t}(x,\Sigma)}f(t,x,y,z,u,a,b,\lambda).

We refer to
[
37
, Section 4]
for explicit examples satisfying this condition.

Theorem 6
.

Let Assumption
4
be true. Let
(\underline{Y},\underline{Z},\underline{U},\underline{K})
be the unique solution to (
15
). Then, the value function of the agent (
13
) is given by

\underline{V_{0}}(\xi)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\inf_{(\mathbb{P},\beta)\in\mathcal{N}^{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[\underline{Y}_{0}].

Moreover,
(\alpha^{\star},\mathbb{P}^{\star},\beta^{\star})
are optimal if and only if
(\alpha^{\star},\mathbb{P}^{\star},\beta^{\star})\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{N}^{\alpha^{\star}}
such that:

(i)

(\alpha^{\star},\beta^{\star})
attains the saddle point sup inf in the definition of
\underline{F}(\cdot,X,\underline{Y},\underline{Z},\underline{U},\lambda,\hat{\sigma}^{2})
,
dt\otimes\mathbb{P}^{\star}
–a.e.,

(ii)

\underline{K}_{T\wedge\tau}=0
,
\mathbb{P}^{\star}
–a.s.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.7
together with theorem
1
and Theorem
2
we deduce that there exists a unique solution
(\underline{Y},\underline{Z},\underline{U},\underline{K})
to (
15
). We define for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
the following 2BSDE with uncertain horizon

\displaystyle Y^{\alpha}_{t}=\Phi(\xi)

\displaystyle+\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\inf_{\beta\in V(x,\hat{\sigma}^{2})}f(s,X_{s},Y^{\alpha}_{s},Z^{\alpha}_{s},U_{s}^{\alpha},\alpha_{s},\beta_{s},\lambda_{s})ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}Z^{\alpha}_{s}\cdot dX_{s}

\displaystyle-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}dK^{\alpha}_{s}-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}U^{\alpha}_{s}dH_{s},\;\mathbb{P}-a.s.,\;\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(t,x).

(16)

Similarly, we consider the 2BSDE when neither
\alpha
nor
\beta
are controlled:

\displaystyle Y^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}=\Phi(\xi)

\displaystyle+\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}f(s,X_{s},Y^{\alpha,\beta}_{s},Z^{\alpha,\beta}_{s},U_{s}^{\alpha,\beta},\alpha_{s},\beta_{s},\lambda_{s})ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}Z^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}\cdot dX_{s}

\displaystyle-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}dK^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}U^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}dH_{s},\;\mathbb{P}-a.s.,\;\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(t,x).

(17)

We finally denotes by
(\mathcal{Y}^{\alpha,\beta},\mathcal{Z}^{\alpha,\beta},\mathcal{U}^{\alpha,\beta})
the unique solutions to the Lipschitz BSDE under some probability
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{\mathbb{P}^{\prime};\alpha,\beta}_{t}

\displaystyle=\Phi(\xi)+\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}f(s,X_{s},\mathcal{Y}^{\mathbb{P}^{\prime};\alpha,\beta}_{s},\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbb{P}^{\prime};\alpha,\beta}_{s},U_{s}^{\mathbb{P}^{\prime};\alpha,\beta},\alpha_{s},\beta_{s},\lambda_{s})ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbb{P}^{\prime};\alpha,\beta}_{s}\cdot dX_{s}-\int_{t\wedge\tau}^{T\wedge\tau}\mathcal{U}^{\mathbb{P}^{\prime};\alpha,\beta}_{s}dH_{s}.

(18)

From the dynamic programming principle Proposition
3.4
together with Remark
3.6
, we get

Y^{\alpha}_{0}=\text{ess inf}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(0,\mathbb{P},\mathbb{G})}\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathbb{P}^{\prime},\alpha},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. for all }\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}.

and

Y^{\alpha,\beta}_{0}=\text{ess inf}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(0,\mathbb{P},\mathbb{G})}\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathbb{P}^{\prime},\alpha,\beta},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. for all }\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}.

From the comparison theorem
3
, we then deduce that for any
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}

\displaystyle\underline{Y}_{0}

\displaystyle=\text{ess sup}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}Y_{0}^{\alpha}=\text{ess sup}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\text{ess inf}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\beta\in{V(x,\hat{\sigma}^{2}})}Y_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\text{ess sup}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\text{ess inf}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\beta\in{V(x,\hat{\sigma}^{2}})}\text{ess inf}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(0,\mathbb{P},\mathbb{G})}^{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathbb{P^{\prime}},\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\text{ess sup}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\text{ess inf}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\beta\in{V(x,\hat{\sigma}^{2}})}\text{ess inf}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(0,\mathbb{P},\mathbb{G})}^{\mathbb{P}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}_{\alpha}}[\mathcal{K}_{T\wedge\tau}^{\alpha,\beta}\Phi(\xi)-\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau}\mathcal{K}_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}c_{t}(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})dt],

where we have used a linearization technique for the last equality with
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}_{\alpha}
defined by

\frac{d\mathbb{P}^{\prime}_{\alpha}}{d\mathbb{P}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{E}\left(\int_{.}^{T}\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}(s,X,\beta_{s})\mu(s,X,\alpha_{s},\nu_{s})\cdot dW_{s}^{\mathbb{P}}+\int_{.}^{T}U_{s}dH_{s}\right).

\underline{V_{0}}(\xi)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\inf_{(\mathbb{P},\beta)\in\mathcal{N}^{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[\underline{Y}_{0}].

Finally, going back to the previous steps,
(\alpha^{\star},\mathbb{P}^{\star},\beta^{\star})

(\alpha^{\star},\beta^{\star})
is a saddle point and satisfies Isaacs condition, and

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}^{\star}}[\underline{Y}_{0}]=\mathcal{Y}^{\mathbb{P}^{\star},\alpha^{\star},\beta^{\star}}_{0},

\underline{K}_{T\wedge\tau}=0,\,\mathbb{P}^{\star}-a.s.

Normed modules, integral sequences, and integrals with variable upper limits Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author. Thanks: MSC2020: 16G10; 46B99; 46M40 Thanks: Keywords: Finite-dimensional algebras; normed modules; categorification; integrations with variable upper limits
Miantao Liu, Shengda Liu, Yu-Zhe Liu ∗

Abstract
. This paper provides a new categorification of the Lebesgue integral with variable upper limits by using normed modules over finite-dimensional 𝕜 \mathds{k} -algebras Λ \mathit{\Lambda} and the category 𝒜 Λ p \mathscr{A}^{p}_{\mathit{\Lambda}} associated with Λ \mathit{\Lambda} . The integration process is redefined through the introduction of an integral partially ordered set and an abstract integral with variable upper limits. Finally, we present two important applications: (1) the categorification of basic elementary functions, including (anti-)trigonometric and logarithmic functions, and (2) a new approach for characterizing the global dimensions of gentle algebras.

2.1.
Normed modules and Banach modules

We fix a homomorphism
\tau:\mathit{\Lambda}\to\mathds{k}
between two finite-dimensional
\mathds{k}
-algebras, where
\mathds{k}
is a field equipped with a norm
|\cdot|:\mathds{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
.

Let
V
be a vector space over
\mathds{k}
, then a norm on
V
is a map
\|\cdot\|:V\to\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
satisfying the following four axioms:

(1)

Non-negativity: For every
x\in V
,
\|x\|\geq 0
.

(2)

Positive definiteness: For every
x\in V
,
\|x\|=0
if and only if
x=0.

(3)

Absolute homogeneity: For every
\lambda\in\mathds{k}
and
x\in V
,

\|\lambda x\|=|\lambda|\|x\|.

(4)

Triangle inequality: For every
x\in V
,
y\in V
,

\|x+y\|\leq\|x\|+\|y\|.

The pair
(V,\|\cdot\|)
is called a normed vector space on
\mathds{k}
.

Remark 2.2
.

For the normed vector space
(V,\|\cdot\|)
, the norm
\|\cdot\|
naturally induces a metric and, consequently, induces a topology on
V
.
This metric between two vectors
x
and
y
is given by

d(x,y)=\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in V.

Using this metric, one can define a topology on
V
, known as the norm topology or metric topology. The topological basis of this topology consists of all open balls:

B_{r}(x)=\{y\in V\mid\|y-x\|<r\},\quad\forall x\in V,\ r>0.

This topology determines the open sets in
V
and makes
\|\cdot\|
continuous and which is compatible with the linear structure of
V
in the following ways:

(1)

The addition
+:V\times V\to V
is jointly continuous with respect to this topology. This follows directly from the triangle inequality.

(2)

The scalar multiplication
\cdot:\mathds{k}\times V\to V
, where
\mathds{k}
is the underlying scalar field of
V
, is jointly continuous. This follows from the triangle inequality and homogeneity of the norm.

Definition 2.3
.

We define a
normed
\mathit{\Lambda}
-module
as a triple
(V,h,\|\cdot\|)
consisting of a left
\mathit{\Lambda}
-module
(V,h)
and a norm
\|\cdot\|:V\to\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
on
V
, and for any

and

\|am\|_{\tau}=|\tau(a)|\|m\|.

Notice that
\|\cdot\|
is written as

in our paper since the definition of

depends on
\tau

Each normed
\mathit{\Lambda}
-module
(V,h,\|\cdot\|_{\tau})
has the canonical induced metric defined by

for all vectors

in
V
.

(1)

5.4.1.
Gentle algebras and their Koszul duals

A finite-dimensional algebra
\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
is called a gentle algebra if its bound quiver
(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{I})
is a
gentle pair
, that is,
(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{I})
satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

For any vertex
v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, there are at most two arrows ending with
v
and there are at most two arrows starting with
v
.

(2)

If there are two arrows
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
and there is an arrow
\beta
such that
\mathfrak{t}(\alpha_{1})=\mathfrak{t}(\alpha_{2})=\mathfrak{s}(\beta)
, then at least one of
\alpha_{1}\beta
and
\alpha_{2}\beta
lies in
\mathcal{I}
, and the other one does not lie in
\mathcal{I}
.

(3)

If there is an arrow
\alpha_{1}
and there are two arrows
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
such that
\mathfrak{t}(\alpha)=\mathfrak{s}(\beta_{1})=\mathfrak{s}(\beta_{2})
, then one of
\alpha\beta_{1}\in\mathcal{I}
and
\alpha\beta_{1}\in\mathcal{I}
lies in
\mathcal{I}
, and the other one does not lie in
\mathcal{I}
.

(4)

Each generator of
\mathcal{I}
is a path of length two.

A finite-dimensional algebra
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
is called a
gentle algebra
if its bound quiver
(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{I})
is a gentle pair.
Gentle algebra has become one of the most popular topics in algebra in recent years; see, for example,

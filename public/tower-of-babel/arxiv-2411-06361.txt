Adding the algebraic Ryu-Takayanagi formula to the algebraic reconstruction theorem
Mingshuai Xu, Haocheng Zhong

Abstract
A huge progress in studying holographic theories is that holography can be interpreted via the quantum error correction, which makes equal the entanglement wedge reconstruction, the Jafferis-Lewkowycz-Maldacena-Suh formula, the radial commutativity and the Ryu-Takayanagi formula. We call the equivalence the reconstruction theorem, whose infinite-dimensional generalization via algebraic language was believed to exclude the algebraic version of the Ryu-Takayanagi formula. However, recent developments regarding gravitational algebras have shown that the inclusion of the algebraic Ryu-Takayanagi formula is plausible. In this letter, we prove that such inclusion holds for the cases of type I/II factors, which are expected to describe holographic theories.

Furthermore, two casually independent subregions possess the algebras of localized observables being commutants of each other, as required by causality.

3
Algebraic entropies

In this section, we discuss the algebraic version of relative entropy and von Neumann entropy. Before introducing these algebraic entropies, we relate several seemingly different definitions of “state” which frequently occur in literatures from different fields.

3.1
Definitions of “state”

There are three definitions of “state”:

1.

A state is a vector
|\psi\rangle
normalized as
\langle\psi\vphantom{\psi}|\psi\vphantom{\psi}\rangle=1
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.

2.

A state is a density operator (or density matrix)
\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying

\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}=\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger};\quad\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\geq 0;\quad\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}=1.

(20)

3.

A state is a linear functional
\omega_{\psi}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\omega_{\psi}\geq 0;\quad\omega_{\psi}(\mathbb{I})=1.

(21)

The first two definitions are commonly used in quantum physics, with the second one being more frequent in quantum information. The third definition is less common and mathematical, which readers may encounter in issues with algebraic quantum field theory. These three definitions are correlated to each other via the expectation value:

\left\langle\psi\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\psi}\right|\mathcal{O}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{O}\right)=\omega_{\psi}(\mathcal{O}),\quad\forall\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{A}.

(22)

Hereafter we will denote a state
\psi
and not distinguish the above definitions. Note that the second definition based on density operators is not well-defined in factors of type III as briefly mentioned in the last of subsection
2.1
.

3.2
Araki’s relative entropy

The quantum relative entropy is defined by

S_{\text{rel}}(\rho_{\psi}|\rho_{\phi})\equiv\text{Tr}\left[\rho_{\psi}\left(\log\rho_{\psi}-\log\rho_{\phi}\right)\right]

(23)

which measures how much the state
\psi
differs from another state
\phi
. Its algebraic generalization is defined due to Araki
[
48
,
49
]
:

S_{\text{rel}}(\psi|\phi;\mathcal{A})\equiv\left\langle\psi\vphantom{h_{\phi|\psi}\psi}\right|h_{\phi|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi h_{\phi|\psi}}\right\rangle

(24)

with
|\psi\rangle
being cyclic and separating with respect to
\mathcal{A}
.

To see that Araki’s relative entropy (
24
) is indeed a generalization of the quantum relative entropy (
23
), we now give a finite-dimensional example (factor of type I)
[
43
]
where the two relative entropies coincide with each other. Consider a finite-dimensional bipartite system
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
with
\dim A=\dim B=n
, we define a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{A}
to be of form

\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{O}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}|\forall\mathcal{O}_{A}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}

(25)

then the commutant is given by

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\{\mathbb{I}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}|\forall\mathcal{O}_{B}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\}=\mathbb{I}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})

(26)

A lessen here is that identifying the algebras of localized observables
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is equivalent to identifying a “decomposition” of the total system just like
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
, while the former is well-defined in factors of any type. In this sense, von Neumann algebras are associated with subregions of the system. Symbolically, we write

\mathcal{A}\sim A,\quad\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\sim B,\quad\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\sim\text{Tr}_{A},\quad\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\sim\text{Tr}_{B}.

\mathcal{O}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}
must annihilates all

, which further implies that
\mathcal{O}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}=0
, i.e.
|\psi\rangle
is separating with respect to
\mathcal{A}
. Likewise, we can argue that
|\psi\rangle
is separating with respect to
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}

|\psi\rangle
is separating with respect to

is cyclic with respect to
\mathcal{A}

is cyclic and separating with respect to
\mathcal{A}
. In this case,

To compute Araki’s relative entropy

\Rightarrow\quad\Delta_{\phi|\psi}=\rho_{\phi;A}\otimes\rho_{\psi;B}^{-1}

3.3
Algebraic von Neumann entropy

The von Neumann entropy is defined by

S(\rho_{\psi})\equiv-\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{\psi}\log\rho_{\psi}\right)

(46)

whose algebraic generalization in factors of type I/II is given by

,

S(\psi;\mathcal{A})\equiv-S_{\text{rel}}(\psi|\tau;\mathcal{A})=-\left\langle\psi\vphantom{h_{\tau|\psi}\psi}\right|h_{\tau|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi h_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle

(47)

where
|\psi\rangle
is cyclic and separating with respect to
\mathcal{A}
and
\tau
is a tracial state. A tracial state is a state
|\tau\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying

\left\langle\tau\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}\tau}\right|\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}\left|\tau\vphantom{\tau\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\tau\vphantom{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{O}\tau}\right|\mathcal{P}\mathcal{O}\left|\tau\vphantom{\tau\mathcal{P}\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle,\quad\forall\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{A}

(48)

and each tracial state defines a trace function on
\mathcal{A}
:

\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\tau}(\mathcal{O})\equiv\left\langle\tau\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\tau}\right|\mathcal{O}\left|\tau\vphantom{\tau\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle

(49)

The non-existence of algebraic von Neumann entropy in factors of type III is due to the non-existence of tracial state or trace function. Hereafter we abuse the notation to use
\tau
to denote the tracial states on both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
(which are in general not the same state) for simplicity, and one should distinguish them from the relevant context.

Recall that (
22
) relates the vector representation of a state and its operator representation via
\left\langle\tau\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\tau}\right|\mathcal{O}\left|\tau\vphantom{\tau\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\tau}(\rho_{\tau;\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{O})
, we have
\rho_{\tau;\mathcal{A}}=\mathbb{I}
which implies that a tracial state is a maximally mixed state. One may notice that the notation
\rho_{\tau;\mathcal{A}}=\mathbb{I}
does not coincide with the usual finite-dimensional density matrix
\rho_{\text{max}}=\frac{1}{n}I_{n}
, which is due to that the trace we define on
\mathcal{A}
is not same as the usual trace on the matrix. They only differ by a rescaling, and the only normalization condition for density operator is
\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\rho_{\mathcal{A}}=1
. The advantage of
\rho_{\tau;\mathcal{A}}=\mathbb{I}
is that it is well-defined in infinite-dimension, while
\frac{1}{n}I_{n}
becomes ill-defined when
n\rightarrow\infty
. In fact, there is a theorem ensuring that every factor (not of type III) admits a unique faithful trace up to a rescaling
[
45
,
53
]
6
6

6

If we rescale the trace by a constant

\overline{\text{Tr}}_{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{C}\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\bar{\rho}_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}=C\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}

(50)

which is to ensure that
\overline{\text{Tr}}_{\mathcal{A}}\bar{\rho}_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}=1
. Such rescaling shifts the von Neumann entropy:

\displaystyle\bar{S}(\bar{\rho}_{\psi;\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle\equiv-\overline{\text{Tr}}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\bar{\rho}_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\log\bar{\rho}_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\right)

(51)

\displaystyle=-\underbrace{\left(\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\right)}_{=1}\log C-\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\log\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\right)

\displaystyle=S(\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}})-\log C

It seems like only the difference of von Neumann entropy is physically sensible, which we will come back to in the discussion.
. In this sense, we drop the superscript of the trace function defined in (
49

To compare the von Neumann entropy with the algebraic von Neumann entropy, we need to compute
h_{\tau|\psi}
. We start with

\Delta_{\tau|\psi}=S^{\dagger}_{\tau|\psi}S_{\tau|\psi},\quad S_{\tau|\psi}|\psi\rangle=|\tau\rangle,

(52)

then we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi\vphantom{\Delta_{\tau|\psi}\psi}\right|\Delta_{\tau|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi\Delta_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\psi\vphantom{S^{\dagger}_{\tau|\psi}S_{\tau|\psi}\psi}\right|S^{\dagger}_{\tau|\psi}S_{\tau|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi S^{\dagger}_{\tau|\psi}S_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\psi\vphantom{S^{\dagger}_{\tau|\psi}\tau}\right|S^{\dagger}_{\tau|\psi}\left|\tau\vphantom{\psi S^{\dagger}_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\tau\vphantom{S_{\tau|\psi}\psi}\right|S_{\tau|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\tau S_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle=\langle\tau\vphantom{\tau}|\tau\vphantom{\tau}\rangle

(53)

on the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi\vphantom{\Delta_{\tau|\psi}\psi}\right|\Delta_{\tau|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi\Delta_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\Delta_{\tau|\psi}\right)=\left\langle\tau\vphantom{\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\Delta_{\tau|\psi}\tau}\right|\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\Delta_{\tau|\psi}\left|\tau\vphantom{\tau\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\Delta_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle

(54)

where we use
\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{O}\right)=\left\langle\psi\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\psi}\right|\mathcal{O}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle,~\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{O})=\left\langle\tau\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\tau}\right|\mathcal{O}\left|\tau\vphantom{\tau\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle
successively. We therefore have

\langle\tau\vphantom{\tau}|\tau\vphantom{\tau}\rangle=\left\langle\tau\vphantom{\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\Delta_{\tau|\psi}\tau}\right|\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\Delta_{\tau|\psi}\left|\tau\vphantom{\tau\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\Delta_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle

(55)

which implies

\mathbb{I}=\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\Delta_{\tau|\psi}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\Delta_{\tau|\psi}=\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}^{-1}\quad\Rightarrow\quad h_{\tau|\psi}=\log\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}

(56)

Finally,

\displaystyle S_{\text{rel}}(\psi|\tau;\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\psi\vphantom{h_{\tau|\psi}\psi}\right|h_{\tau|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi h_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\psi\vphantom{\log\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\psi}\right|\log\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi\log\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}}\right\rangle

(57)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\log\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\right)\equiv-S(\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}})

\Rightarrow\quad S(\psi;\mathcal{A})\equiv-S_{\text{rel}}(\psi|\tau;\mathcal{A})=S(\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}})

(58)

i.e. the algebraic von Neumann entropy in
\mathcal{A}
coincides with the von Neumann entropy of reduced density operator restricted in a subregion associated with the algebra
\mathcal{A}
of localized observables.

Now we give a comment about the coincidence between the entropies and their algebraic generalization. Recall the splittable condition of modular Hamiltonian (
41

\Delta_{\phi|\psi}=\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}\otimes\rho^{-1}_{\psi;\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}=\Delta^{-1}_{\tau|\phi}\otimes\Delta_{\tau|\psi}^{\prime}~\Rightarrow~h_{\phi|\psi}=-\log\Delta_{\phi|\psi}=h_{\tau|\psi}^{\prime}-h_{\tau|\phi},

(59)

which implies

S_{\text{rel}}(\psi|\phi;\mathcal{A})=-S(\psi;\mathcal{A}^{\prime})-\left\langle\psi\vphantom{h_{\tau|\phi}\psi}\right|h_{\tau|\phi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi h_{\tau|\phi}}\right\rangle,

(60)

which is in fact an algebraic generalization of a relation between quantum relative entropy and von Neumann entropy:

S_{\text{rel}}(\rho_{\psi;A}|\rho_{\phi;A})=-S(\rho_{\psi;A})+\left\langle\psi\vphantom{H_{\phi;A}\psi}\right|H_{\phi;A}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi H_{\phi;A}}\right\rangle=-S(\rho_{\psi;B})+\left\langle\psi\vphantom{H_{\phi;A}\psi}\right|H_{\phi;A}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi H_{\phi;A}}\right\rangle,

(61)

where
H_{\phi;A}\equiv-\log\rho_{\phi;A}(=\log\Delta_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}=-h_{\tau|\phi})
.

3.4
Extended definitions of “state”

In the beginning of the section, we introduce three definitions of “state” which are related to each other via the expectation value (
22
). Note however that, these three definitions are not completely equivalent. In the vector representation, one can always compute the expectation value of an operator, even in the case that the operator is not an element of the corresponding algebra, i.e. one can compute the expectation
\left\langle\psi\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\psi}\right|\mathcal{O}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle,~\mathcal{O}\notin\mathcal{A}
which makes no sense for the other two definitions. To be specific, the expectation value
\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{O}\right),~\mathcal{O}\notin\mathcal{A}
is ill-defined since
\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{O}
may not belongs to
\mathcal{A}
then it is not in the domain of the trace function of
\mathcal{A}
. As for
\omega_{\psi}(\mathcal{O}),~\mathcal{O}\notin\mathcal{A}
, it is ill-defined since
\omega_{\psi}
is only a linear functional on
\mathcal{A}
.

Nevertheless, we can extend the last two definitions via (
22
) such that the expectation values of the last two definitions are well-defined for a larger class of operators. In the functional representation, we can simply define that

\omega_{\psi}(\mathcal{O})\equiv\left\langle\psi\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\psi}\right|\mathcal{O}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle,~\mathcal{O}\notin\mathcal{A}.

(62)

As for the density operator representation, we only need to extend the domain of the trace function via

\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{O}\right)\equiv\left\langle\psi\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\psi}\right|\mathcal{O}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle,~\mathcal{O}\notin\mathcal{A}.

(63)

Previously, we know that neither the (relative) Tomita operator nor the (relative) modular operator (Hamiltonian) are elements of the algebra. The former is due to the anti-linearity, and the latter is due to the unboundedness. Therefore, we could not regard the Araki’s relative entropy and the algebraic von Neumann entropy as the expectation values of some operators in the sense of state functional. Now we resolve this issue via the above extension such that

S_{\text{rel}}(\psi|\phi;\mathcal{A})=\left\langle\psi\vphantom{h_{\phi|\psi}\psi}\right|h_{\phi|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi h_{\phi|\psi}}\right\rangle=\omega_{\psi}(h_{\phi|\psi}),

(64)

and

S(\psi;\mathcal{A})=-\left\langle\psi\vphantom{h_{\tau|\psi}\psi}\right|h_{\tau|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi h_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle=-\omega_{\psi}(h_{\tau|\psi}).

(65)

3.5
Algebraic entropy difference

Before our discussions about the algebraic reconstruction theorem, we give a comment about the algebraic version of the von Neumann entropy difference with respect to the variation of state:

S(\rho+\delta\rho)-S(\rho)\approx-\text{Tr}(\delta\rho\log\rho).

Algebraically, we introduce a perturbed state
\psi

\phi

\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}=\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon(\rho_{\theta;\mathcal{A}}-\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}).

\log(A+\varepsilon B)=\log A+\varepsilon A^{-1}B+O(\varepsilon^{2})

56

We next define the algebraic entropy difference as

\Delta S(\psi,\phi;\mathcal{A})\equiv S(\psi;\mathcal{A})-S(\phi;\mathcal{A}).

(73)

Using (
65
), (
67
) and (
72
), we find that

\displaystyle S(\psi;\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=-\omega_{\psi}(h_{\tau|\psi})

(74)

\displaystyle\approx-\left[\omega_{\phi}+\varepsilon(\omega_{\theta}-\omega_{\phi})\right]\left[h_{\tau|\phi}+\varepsilon\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}^{-1}(\rho_{\theta;\mathcal{A}}-\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}})\right]

\displaystyle\approx-\omega_{\phi}(h_{\tau|\phi})+\varepsilon(\omega_{\theta}-\omega_{\phi})(h_{\tau|\phi})

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\omega_{\phi}\left[\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}^{-1}(\rho_{\theta;\mathcal{A}}-\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}})\right]

where we omit
O(\varepsilon^{2})
terms. Note the first term is exactly
S(\phi;\mathcal{A})
and the third term vanishes because

\displaystyle\omega_{\phi}\left[\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}^{-1}(\rho_{\theta;\mathcal{A}}-\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}})\right]

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}\cdot\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}^{-1}(\rho_{\theta;\mathcal{A}}-\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}})\right]

(75)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}(\rho_{\theta;\mathcal{A}}-\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}(\rho_{\theta;\mathcal{A}})-\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}(\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle=1-1=0

where the last line is due to that all density operators have unital trace. We therefore have

\Delta S(\psi,\phi;\mathcal{A})=\varepsilon(\omega_{\theta}-\omega_{\phi})(h_{\tau|\phi}).

(76)

to first order. To see that it generalizes (

\rho_{\psi;\mathcal{A}}=\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}+\delta\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}},\quad\delta\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}\equiv\varepsilon(\rho_{\theta;\mathcal{A}}-\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}})

(77)

such that

\displaystyle\Delta S(\psi,\phi;\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\omega_{\theta}-\omega_{\phi})(h_{\tau|\phi})

(78)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\varepsilon(\rho_{\theta;\mathcal{A}}-\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}})h_{\tau|\phi}\right]

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\delta\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}h_{\tau|\phi}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\delta\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}\log\rho_{\phi;\mathcal{A}}\right).

where the last equality is due to (

\mathcal{A}_{phys}
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta S(\Psi,\Phi;\mathcal{A}_{phys})

\displaystyle\equiv S(\Psi;\mathcal{A}_{phys})-S(\Phi;\mathcal{A}_{phys})

(117)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\omega_{\Theta}-\omega_{\Phi})(h_{\bar{\tau}|\Phi})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon~\omega_{\Theta}(h_{\bar{\tau}|\Phi})+\varepsilon S(\Phi;\mathcal{A}_{phys}).

Now combining the statement 4 (

\varepsilon~\omega_{\widetilde{\Theta}}(h_{\widetilde{\tau}|\widetilde{\Phi}})=\varepsilon~\omega_{\Theta}(h_{\bar{\tau}|\Phi})=\varepsilon~\omega_{\widetilde{\Theta}}(V^{\dagger}h_{\bar{\tau}|\Phi}V)

Since

Discussion

As mentioned in footnote

differs the trace associated with
|\tau\rangle
by a rescaling, an extra constant appears and shifts the algebraic von Neumann entropy on
\mathcal{A}_{phys}
. Naively, it seems like the extra constant coincides with the area term in the RT formula,

S_{bdy}=\mathcal{L}+S_{bulk}

(121)

which however is not the case. Unlike the shifting constant can be manually chosen to be as small as we want, the area term diverges. Furthermore, it diverges in the same way as the boundary von Neumann entropy does. If we rewrite the RT formula as

S_{bdy}-\mathcal{L}=S_{bulk}

(122)

then the leading divergence on the LHS cancels. Since recent investigations have argued for a algebraic way to regulate

, which implies that

S_{bulk}=S(\tilde{\Psi};\mathcal{A}_{code}),\quad S_{bdy}-\mathcal{L}=S(\Psi;\mathcal{A}_{phys}).

(123)

One future direction is to investigate how the area term
\mathcal{L}
emerges in the algebraic regulation of
S_{bdy}
.

Another future direction that is worth pursuing is that whether a modified version of the algebraic RT formula is plausible in a general type factor. In
[
37
]
, the authors have argued that it is possible to rigorously define the difference of von Neumann entropy in a general factor, in the sense that the divergence of the algebraic von Neumann entropy cancels after subtraction in type III factors. In this case, it seems that we may promote the statement of algebraic RT formula in the algebraic reconstruction to a new statement regarding the generalized version of the difference of algebraic von Neumann entropy.

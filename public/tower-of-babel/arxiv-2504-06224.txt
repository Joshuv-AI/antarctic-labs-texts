Nonlinear Tails of Gravitational Waves in Schwarzschild Black Hole Ringdown
Alex Kehagias, Antonio Riotto

Abstract
Schwarzschild black holes evolve toward their static configuration by emitting gravitational waves, which decay over time following a power law at fixed spatial positions. We derive this power law analytically for the second-order even gravitational perturbations, demonstrating that it is determined by the fact that the second-order source decays as the inverse square of the distance. Quadratic gravitational modes with multipole ℓ \ell decay according to a law ∼ t − 2 ​ ℓ − 1 \sim t^{-2\ell-1} , in contrast to the linear Price law scaling ∼ t − 2 ​ ℓ − 3 \sim t^{-2\ell-3} . Consequently, nonlinear tails may persist longer than their linear counterparts.

Spectroscopy for a Schwarzschild BH –

Our starting point is a Schwarzschild BH with mass
M
whose spacetime is described by the metric (setting
G_{N}=1
)

{\rm d}s^{2}=-f(r){\rm d}t^{2}+\frac{{\rm d}r^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}{\rm d}\Omega_{2}^{2},\,\,\,f(r)=1-2M/r,

(1)

Adopting the Regge-Wheeler-Zerilli formalism
[
69
,
70
]
for the first-order metric perturbations in the Schwarzschild
spacetime and separating angular variables with tensor harmonics
of indices
(\ell,m)
, the equations are divided into the even and odd parity parts. In this paper we limit ourselves to the the even parity mode, corresponding to the Zerilli equation at first-order. Even though there are seven equations for the even parity part of the metric perturbation, at first-order one can reduce the dynamics to a single Zerilli equation for a function
\psi^{(1)}_{\ell m}(r,t)
in the so-called Regge-Wheeler (RW) gauge

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r_{*}^{2}}-V(r)\right]\psi_{\ell m}^{(1)}(r,t)=0,

\displaystyle V(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(r)\frac{2\lambda^{2}(\lambda+1)r^{3}+6\lambda^{2}Mr^{2}+18\lambda M^{2}r+18M^{3}}{r^{3}(\lambda r+3M)^{2}},

where

\displaystyle r_{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r+2M\ln(r/2M-1),

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\ell-1)(\ell+2).

(3)

The evolution is determined by a retarded Green’s function solving the equation

\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r_{*}^{2}}-V(r)\right]G(t-t^{\prime};r,r^{\prime})=\delta(t-t^{\prime})\delta(r-r^{\prime}),

(4)

under the boundary condition
G(t-t^{\prime};r,r^{\prime})=0
for
t<t^{\prime}
. The retarded Green function receives three contributions,
G=(G_{F}+G_{Q}+G_{B})
.
At linear order and
for
t^{\prime}\geq t-|r-r^{\prime}|
, the Green’s
function
G\simeq G_{F}
behaves as in flat background: a wave
arrives at
(t,r)
without passing through the potential barrier, and thus without being reflected or deformed. For
t^{\prime}\leq t-|r-r^{\prime}|
, the Green’s function
G\simeq G_{Q}
oscillates with QNM frequencies: a wave generated in this region
pass through the potential and is deformed into
a first-order QNM. Finally, the branch-cut part
G_{B}
describes the
backscattering of waves by the long-range part of the potential barrier and is responsible for the Price’s tail. At the linear order the contribution of
G_{B}
is usually small
compared to the other contributions
[
15
]
, and
one may neglect it.
In the Supplementary Material we offer a self-similar and quantum mechanical derivation of the linear Price’s tail which is alternative to the usual Green function branch-cut argument.

At second-order, perturbation theory becomes much more involved, but there are general considerations which we may propose to arrive at some universal results. Consider for instance the multipole
\ell=4
which can be constructed out of the linear modes
(2,\pm 2)
. Following closely Ref.
[
39
]
, one can write the second-order equation for
\psi_{4\pm 4}^{(2)}(t,r)
as

\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r_{*}^{2}}-V(r)\right]\psi^{(2)}_{4\pm 4}(r,t)=S_{4\pm 4}(r,t),

(5)

where
S_{4\pm 4}(r,t)
is the regularized quadratic source built up with the linear modes
(2,\pm 2)
. The key point is that it behaves as
\sim 1/r^{2}
at large radii (and as
(r-2M)
close to the horizon)

S_{4\pm 4}(r\gg 2M,t)\sim\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left[\psi^{(2)}_{2\pm 2}(r,t)\right]^{2}.

(6)

The behaviour is in fact valid
for any multipole, and in the following it will suffice to use the following expression

S_{\ell\pm\ell}(r\gg 2M,t)\sim\frac{e^{-2i\omega_{\ell}(t-r_{*})}}{r^{2}},

(7)

where
\omega_{\ell}
is the QNM of the multipole (
\ell,\pm\ell)
. It represents the starting point for our considerations on the nonlinear tails.

Nonlinear tails –

As we have already mentioned, at first-order in perturbation theory, the QNMs arise from the QNM component
G_{Q}
of the Green’s function, while the Price’s power-law tails come from the branch-cut component
G_{B}
. This indicates that both are generated in a region in which the potential
V(r)
is important: the first-order QNMs are excited near the peak of the potential barrier, and the first-order tails emerge from the long-range decay of the potential or, equiva-
lently, the fact that perturbations in a non-flat space-time travel not
only on the light cone, but also inside it.

In contrast, the second-order power-law tail originates from the flat part
G_{F}
of the Green’s function, meaning they are produced in asymptotically “flat regions”, and from the
edge of the source distribution at
(t^{\prime}-r_{*}^{\prime})\simeq u_{0}
with some finite support.
It might be surprising that a non-oscillating tail can be produced by oscillating QNMs, but it can be understood as follows
[
64
]
: an observer at position
(t,r_{*})
sees the edge
(t^{\prime}+r_{*}^{\prime})
extending from
(t-r_{*})
to
(t+r_{*})
and cannot detect the edge for
(t^{\prime}+r_{*}^{\prime})<t-r_{*}
, as waves generated in this region are partially scattered by the potential barrier before reaching the observer. The first-order perturbations
\psi^{(1)}_{\ell m}(t^{\prime},r_{*}^{\prime})\sim e^{-i\omega(t^{\prime}-r_{*}^{\prime})}
are constant along the edge
(t^{\prime}-r_{*}^{\prime})\simeq u_{0}
and, therefore, they do not exhibit oscillations, thus leading to long-lasting tails
[
64
]
.

Now we arrive at a critical observation. Since the source at large values of
r
decays at
\sim 1/r^{2}
, and since

V(r\gg 2M)\simeq\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}},

(8)

it is clear that the flat Green function
G_{F}
may not be evaluated neglecting such a leading term as it scales with the same power of the source. In other terms, the
flat part
G_{F}
of the Green function must be calculated taking the centrifugal term in the potential.

Imagine that QNMs
turn on at a given time
t_{0}
at a given point
r_{0}
(typically the photon ring
r_{0}\simeq 3M
for
\ell\gg 1
) and
moves outward at light speed, with a finite support in term of the retarded time
u^{\prime}\equiv(t^{\prime}-r^{\prime}_{*})\simeq u_{0}
. On the other hand the advanced time
v^{\prime}\equiv(t^{\prime}+r_{*}^{\prime})
ranges from
(t-r_{*})
to
(t+r_{*})
. We further assume that the observer is located at points farther apart in the time direction than in the radial
direction, that is
t\gg r_{*}
. Under these circumstances the source
acquires the form

S_{\ell\pm\ell}(u,v)\sim\frac{e^{-2i\omega_{\ell}u_{0}}}{r^{2}(u_{0},v)},

(9)

up to a multiplicative constant.
The nonlinear tail
is therefore dictated by the integral

{\cal I}(t,r)=\int_{t-r_{*}(r)}^{t+r_{*}(r)}\frac{{\rm d}v^{\prime}}{r^{2}(u_{0},v^{\prime})}\,G_{F}(t,r;u_{0},v^{\prime}),

(10)

where one has also to account for the fact that at large radii
r_{*}
is not exactly equal to
r
and subleading terms are relevant to catch the nonlinear tails.
The closed form of
G_{F}
is obtained in the Supplementary Material. For instance, for
\ell=2
, it reads

\displaystyle G_{F}(t,r;t^{\prime},r^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{1}{16r^{2}{r^{\prime}}^{2}}\left[(t-t^{\prime})^{4}+3r^{4}+2r^{2}{r^{\prime}}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.3{r^{\prime}}^{4}-6(t-t^{\prime})^{2}(r^{2}+{r^{\prime}}^{2})\right],

leading to

{\cal I}(t\gg r)\simeq\frac{32}{15}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{r^{3}}{t^{6}}.

(11)

For
\ell=4
, using the Green function given in the Supplementary Material, we find

{\cal I}(t\gg r)\simeq\frac{512}{315}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{r^{5}}{t^{10}},

(12)

which reproduces the scaling found numerically in Ref.
[
66
]
.
Furthermore, by using the general form of the Green function in Eq. (
S39
), our general finding is

{\cal I}(t\gg r)\simeq\frac{(-1)^{\ell}2^{2\ell+1}\ell!(\ell-1)!}{(2\ell+1)(2\ell-1)!}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\frac{r^{\ell+1}}{t^{2\ell+2}}.

(13)

Let us notice here that a similar result holds for the Reissner-Nördstrom BH with metric of the form (
1
) with
f=1-2M/r+Q^{2}/r^{2}
. In this case, using the corresponding relation
r_{*}(r)
in the integral (
10
), we get

{\cal I}_{\rm RN}(t\gg r)\simeq C_{\ell}\,\frac{(-1)^{\ell}2^{2\ell+1}\ell!(\ell-1)!}{(2\ell+1)(2\ell-1)!}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\frac{r^{\ell+1}}{t^{2\ell+2}},

(14)

where

C_{\ell}=\sum_{k=0}^{\left[\frac{\ell}{2}\right]}\frac{(l-k)!}{(\ell-2k)!k!}(2M)^{\ell-2k}Q^{4k}.

(15)

There is one more passage to perform though to arrive at the final result.

From the RW to the asymptotic flat gauge –

The last necessary step we have to take in order to
obtain the correct power-law tail of the gravitational waveform is to go
to an asymptotic flat (AF) gauge from the RW gauge. This is because the Regge-Wheeler-Zerilli formalism that we
have employed is under the RW gauge and this gauge is not asymptotically flat.
Following Ref.
[
39
]
, we can write the fundamental modes at infinity in the asymptotic flat gauge at first- and second-order for the modes
(2,\pm 2)
and
(4,\pm 4)
as

\displaystyle h^{\text{(1) \hskip-2.84544pt{\rm AF} }}_{2,\pm 2}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\psi^{(1)}_{2,\pm 2},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h^{\text{(2) \hskip-5.69046pt AF}}_{4,\pm 4}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\psi^{(2)}_{4,\pm 4}+\frac{\sqrt{70}}{1512\sqrt{\pi}r}\left[27\psi^{(1)}_{2,\pm 2}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi^{(1)}_{2,\pm 2}}{\partial t^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.24\left(\frac{\partial\psi^{(1)}_{2,\pm 2}}{\partial t}\right)^{2}+4M\left(\frac{\partial\psi^{(1)}_{2,\pm 2}}{\partial t}\right)\frac{\partial^{2}\psi^{(1)}_{2,\pm 2}}{\partial t^{2}}\right].

From this expression, we deduce that the leading power-law tail for the gravitational waveform for the even modes is

h^{\text{(2) \hskip-2.84544pt{\rm AF} }}_{\ell,\pm m}\sim\int^{t}{\rm d}t^{\prime}\,\frac{1}{r}\psi^{(2)}_{\ell m}\sim t^{-2\ell-1}.

(17)

This is our final result for the nonlinear tail
of the even gravitational degrees of freedom.

Conclusions –

It is well-established that, when a BH reaches its static configuration, linearized fluctuations at fixed spatial positions decay over time according to the Price’s power law. In this Letter, we have provided an analytical explanation for the power-law tail that governs the decay of nonlinear perturbations at second order in perturbation theory. The exponent of this power law is determined by the fact that the second-order source decays as the inverse of the square of the distance, which may result in a significantly longer decay time compared to the linear case. Specifically, the quadratic even modes decay as
\sim t^{-2\ell-1}
, in contrast to the linear Price’s scaling
\sim t^{-2\ell-3}
. In other words, the linear perturbation theory might be inadequate for predicting the late-time evolution of the ringdown, necessitating the inclusion of higher-order corrections.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}G_{F}}{\partial t^{\prime 2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}G_{F}}{\partial r^{\prime 2}}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{\prime 2}}G_{F}=\delta(t-t^{\prime})\delta(r-r^{\prime}).

\widetilde{G}(\omega,r,r^{\prime})
turns out to be

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{F}(\omega,r,r^{\prime})=\omega rr^{\prime}\bigg(j_{\ell}(\omega r)n_{\ell}(\omega r^{\prime})\,\theta(r-r^{\prime})+j_{\ell}(\omega r^{\prime})n_{\ell}(\omega r)\theta(r^{\prime}-r)\,\bigg),

(S34)

so that the Green function in the time domain for
r>r^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle G_{F}(t,r;t^{\prime},r^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\omega e^{i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}\omega rr^{\prime}j_{\ell}(\omega r)n_{\ell}(\omega r^{\prime}),\qquad r>r^{\prime}.

(S35)

By using the following representation of the spherical Bessel functions

\displaystyle j_{\ell}(x)=(-x)^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{x}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\right)^{\ell}\frac{\sin x}{x},\qquad n_{\ell}(x)=-(-x)^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{x}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\right)^{\ell}\frac{\cos x}{x},

(S36)

we find that

\displaystyle G_{F}(t,r;t^{\prime},r^{\prime})=r^{\ell+1}{r^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}\left(\frac{1}{r}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\frac{1}{rr^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\omega e^{i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}\frac{\cos(\omega r)\sin(\omega r^{\prime})}{\omega^{2\ell+1}}.

(S37)

The integral in Eq. (
S37
) can be performed in the complex
\omega
-plane using the fact that it posseses a pole of order
(2\ell+1)
at
\omega=0
. The result for
0<|r-r^{\prime}|<t-t^{\prime}<r+r^{\prime}
is

\displaystyle I(t,t^{\prime},r,r^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\omega\,e^{i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}\,\frac{\cos(\omega r)\sin(\omega r^{\prime})}{\omega^{2\ell+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{4\cdot 2\ell!}\bigg[-((t-t^{\prime})+r-r^{\prime})^{2\ell}+((t-t^{\prime})-r+r^{\prime})^{2\ell}+((t-t^{\prime})-r-r^{\prime})^{2\ell}+((t-t^{\prime})+r+r^{\prime})^{2\ell}\bigg].

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle G_{F}(t,r;t^{\prime},r^{\prime})=r^{\ell+1}{r^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}\left(\frac{1}{r}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\frac{I(t,t^{\prime},r,r^{\prime})}{rr^{\prime}}.

(S39)

One can further proceed and evaluate the derivatives above, but the resulting expressions are not particularly illuminating. Instead, (
S39
) can be used to find easily the Green function for any
\ell
. For example, for
\ell=2
we get

\displaystyle G_{F}(t,r;t^{\prime},r^{\prime})=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{3(t-t^{\prime})^{4}+3r^{4}+2r^{2}{r^{\prime}}^{2}+3{r^{\prime}}^{4}-6(t-t^{\prime})^{2}(r^{2}+{r^{\prime}}^{2})}{16r^{2}{r^{\prime}}^{2}}

(S40)

which agrees with the result in Ref.
[
71
]
.
For
\ell=4
, we get

\displaystyle G_{F}(t,r;t^{\prime},r^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{1}{256\,r^{4}{r^{\prime}}^{4}}\left[35r^{8}+20r^{6}(r^{\prime 2}-7(t-t^{\prime})^{2})+20r^{2}(r^{\prime 2}-7(t-t^{\prime})^{2})(r^{\prime 2}-(t-t^{\prime})^{2})+35(r^{\prime 2}-(t-t^{\prime})^{2})^{4}\right.

(S41)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.6r^{4}(3r^{\prime 4}-30r^{\prime 2}(t-t^{\prime})^{2}+35(t-t^{\prime})^{4})\right].

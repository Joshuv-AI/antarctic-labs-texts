Fidelity of entanglement and quantum entropies: unveiling their relationship in quantum states and channels
Komal Kumar, Bivas Mallick, Tapaswini Patro, Nirman Ganguly

Abstract
Entanglement serves as a fundamental resource for various quantum information processing tasks. Fidelity of entanglement (which measures the proximity to a maximally entangled state) and various quantum entropies are key indicators for certifying entanglement in a quantum state. Quantum states with high fidelity are particularly useful for numerous information-theoretic applications. Similarly, states possessing negative conditional entropy provide significant advantages in several quantum information processing protocols. In this work, we examine the relationship between these two indicators of entanglement, both in state and channel regimes. First, we present a comprehensive analysis and characterization of channels that reduce fidelity of entanglement beyond a threshold limit of bipartite composite systems. In this context, we introduce the notion of fidelity annihilating channel and discuss its topological characterization, along with various information-theoretic properties. We then provide a comparison between channels that diminish the fidelity of entanglement and negative conditional entropies. Extending our analysis from channels to the state level, we further examine the relationship between the fidelity of entanglement and various quantum entropies for general two-qubit states. We derive the upper bound on Rényi 2 2 -entropy, conditional Rényi 2 2 -entropy, Tsallis 2 2 -entropy, and conditional Tsallis 2 2 -entropy, in terms of the fidelity of entanglement. Finally, we explore the relationship between relative entropy and the fidelity of entanglement of a two qudit quantum state.

Quantum Channels, Fidelity of entanglement, Quantum entropy, Relative entropy

Motivated by the above connection between channels diminishing fidelity of entanglement and negative conditional entropies, we then aim to explore the relationship between these two fundamental quantifiers of quantum correlations at the state level. II.1
Bloch-Fano Decomposition of density matrices

In bipartite composite quantum systems, the quantum state is described by density matrices, which can be represented as

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}}[I_{A}\otimes I_{B}+\sum_{i=1}^{d_{A}^{2}-1}a_{i}g_{i}^{A}\otimes I_{B}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{B}^{2}-1}b_{j}I_{A}\otimes g_{j}^{B}+\sum_{i=1}^{d_{A}^{2}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{B}^{2}-1}t_{ij}g_{i}^{A}\otimes g_{j}^{B}],

(1)

where

and

. The hermitian operators

•

II.4
Fidelity and negative conditional entropy breaking channel

Fidelity breaking channel:
In quantum information theory, certain quantum channels operate on one subsystem of a bipartite composite system
AB
in a way that reduces its potential to serve as a resource. Specifically, some channels reduce the fidelity of entanglement of a bipartite quantum state when they are applied to one of its subsystems. These channels are referred to as fidelity breaking channels

. Mathematically, a quantum channel denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{FB}

\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})
, the resulting state
\sigma_{AB}

to one subsystem has a fidelity of entanglement that does not exceed
1/d
, i.e.,

\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{FB}(\rho_{AB})=\sigma_{AB},\quad\text{where}\quad F(\sigma_{AB})\leq\frac{1}{d},

(12)

where
F(\sigma_{AB})
indicates the fidelity of entanglement of the state
\sigma_{AB}
. Throughout the manuscript, we use
\mathcal{N}_{FB}
to describe the fidelity breaking channel.

Negative conditional entropy breaking channel:
There exists a specific kind of quantum channel that operates on one subsystem of a bipartite system, ensuring that the conditional entropy between the two subsystems remains non-negative, these channels are known as negative conditional entropy breaking channels
Muhuri et al. 2023
;
Srinidhi et al. 2024
. This indicates that the channel effectively destroys any quantum correlations that could result in a negative value for conditional entropy. Formally, a quantum channel denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{NB}

\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})
, the conditional entropy
S(A|B)
of the resulting output state
\sigma_{AB}
is non-negative i.e.,

S(A|B)\geq 0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \hskip 1.42271pt{\text{where output sate }}\hskip 1.42271pt\sigma_{AB}=\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{NB}(\rho_{AB}).

(13)

Since negative conditional entropy is a signature of strong quantum correlations such as quantum entanglement and quantum steering, a negative conditional entropy breaking channel fundamentally limits these correlations. Throughout the manuscript, we use
\mathcal{N}_{NB}
to describe the negative conditional entropy breaking channel.

[\mathcal{I}\otimes(\mathcal{N}_{FB}\circ\mathcal{T})](\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB}))\penalty\ =\penalty\ (\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{FB})[\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})]\subset\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{FBC}}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})

III.2
Fidelity annihilating channels

In this subsection, we introduce fidelity annihilating channels and then explore their characteristics and fundamental properties. We denote the set of fidelity annihilating channels by
\mathbb{FAC}
. Fidelity breaking channels are those that diminish the fidelity of entanglement across the partition
A|B
, whereas fidelity annihilating channels reduce the fidelity of entanglement within the bipartite subsystem
B
. That is, we consider that
B
can be partitioned into
B_{1}|B_{2}
, where dimensions of both
B_{1},B_{2}
is
d`
. The set of fidelity annihilating channels can be categorized into two types: local and non-local. We begin by defining non-local and local fidelity annihilating channels.

Definition 1
.

Consider that subsystem
B
can be partitioned into
B_{1}|B_{2}
, where dimensions of both
B_{1},B_{2}
is
d`

Then, a channel denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{FA}
acting on bipartite subsystem
B
is said to be a fidelity annihilating channel if
F(\sigma_{B})\leq 1/d`
where
\sigma_{B}=\mathcal{N}_{FA}(\rho_{B})
, for all
\rho_{B}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
.

Definition 2
.

A fidelity annihilating channel
\mathcal{N}_{FA}
is
2
-local if it can be expressed as
\mathcal{N}_{FA}=\mathcal{N}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{2}
. Otherwise, it is termed as a non-local fidelity annihilating channel and the corresponding set is denoted by non-local
-\mathbb{FAC}
.

III.2.1
Characterization of non-local fidelity annihilating channels

Here, we investigate the topological characterization of non-local fidelity annihilating channels
(\mathbb{FAC})
.

Theorem 1
.

\text{The set non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
is convex.

Proof
.

Since,
\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1},\penalty\ \mathcal{N}_{FA}^{2}\in\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
, then for any
\rho_{B}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
we have

F(\sigma_{B}^{1})\leq 1/d`\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and }\penalty\ \penalty\ F(\sigma_{B}^{2})\leq 1/d`\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{where}\penalty\ \penalty\ \sigma_{B}^{1}=\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}(\rho_{B}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \sigma_{B}^{2}=\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}(\rho_{B})

(27)

We need to show that

\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{N}_{FA}=\lambda\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}+(1-\lambda)\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{2}\in\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}.

Applying
\mathcal{N}_{FA}
on any arbitrary density operator
\rho_{B}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{B}=\mathcal{N}_{FA}(\rho_{B})=(\lambda\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}+(1-\lambda)\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{2})(\rho_{B})=\lambda\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}(\rho_{B})+(1-\lambda)\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{2}(\rho_{B})=\lambda\sigma_{B}^{1}+(1-\lambda)\sigma_{B}^{2}

(28)

Using Eq.(
27
), Eq.(
28
), and result from
Ganguly et al. 2011
, we obtain

F(\sigma_{B})=F(\lambda\sigma_{B}^{1}+(1-\lambda)\sigma_{B}^{2})=\lambda F(\sigma_{B}^{1})+(1-\lambda)F(\sigma_{B}^{2})\leq 1/d`

(29)

Therefore,
\mathcal{N}_{FA}\in\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}.
Hence,
\text{the set non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
is convex.

∎

Theorem 2
.

\text{The set non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
is compact.

Proof
.

Firstly, to prove that the set
\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
is closed, it suffices to show that it contains all of its limit points. Since the set
\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
is convex, it necessarily possesses at least one limit point. Let
\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0}
be an arbitrary limit point of
\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
. Consider an open ball
B_{\frac{1}{r}}(\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0})
of radius
\frac{1}{r}
centered on
\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0}
, then by the definition of a limit point, we have

\{B_{\frac{1}{r}}(\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0})-\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0}\}\penalty\ \cap\penalty\ \text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}\neq\emptyset

(30)

Since, every neighborhood of
\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0}
contains infinitely many points of
\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
, where
\emptyset
is the null set.
Next, we construct a sequence
\{\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{r}\}
of distinct fidelity annihilating channels that converges to
\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}\in B_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0})\penalty\ \cap\penalty\ \text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC},\hskip 4.26773pt\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}\neq\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0}

(31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{2}\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0})\penalty\ \cap\penalty\ \text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC},\hskip 4.26773pt\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{2}\neq\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0},\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}

\displaystyle........\in........

\displaystyle........\in........

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{r}\in B_{\frac{1}{r}}(\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0})\penalty\ \cap\penalty\ \text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC},\hskip 4.26773pt\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{r}\neq\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0},...,\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{r-1}

Now, consider another sequence
\{\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{r}({\rho}_{B})={\chi^{r}_{B}}\}
for arbitrary density operator
{\rho}_{B}\in\mathcal{D}({\mathcal{H}_{B}})
. Since,
\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{r}\in

\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
, it follows that

F(\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{r}({\rho}_{B}))=F(\chi^{r}_{B})\leq\frac{1}{d`}

(32)

It is known that the set of states
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{FAC}}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
, consisting of states whose fidelity of entanglement is at most
\frac{1}{d`}
forms a closed set
Ganguly et al. 2011
, hence for
\chi^{r}_{B}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{FAC}}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
, all limit points of
\chi^{r}_{B}
must also belong to
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{FAC}}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
. Now,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{r}({\rho}_{B})\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0}({\rho}_{B})

(33)

\displaystyle\implies\lim_{r\to\infty}\chi^{r}_{B}=\chi^{0}_{B}\hskip 28.45274pt[\text{where,}\hskip 5.69046pt\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0}({\rho}_{B})={\chi^{0}_{B}}]

(34)

i.e.,
\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0}\in

\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
. Since
\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{0}
was chosen as an arbitrary limit point of
\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
, it follows that
\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
contains all of its limit points. Therefore, the set
\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
is closed.

Furthermore, it is well known that the completely bounded trace norm of quantum channels is equal to 1

. Thus, all channels in the set
\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
are bounded maps. Hence, the set
\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
is both closed and bounded, i.e., compact.
∎

III.2.2
Properties of non-local fidelity annihilating channels

Below, we examine some structural properties of non-local fidelity annihilating channels.

Property 3
.

If
\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}

\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{2}\in\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}

.

Now, consider the series composition,

(37)

Using Eq.(
36
) and Eq.(
37
), we obtain

\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}\circ\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{2}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))\penalty\ =\penalty\ \mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}[\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{2}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))]\subset\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{FAC}}(\mathcal{H}_{B})

(38)

Hence,
\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{1}\circ\mathcal{N}_{FA}^{2}\in\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}.

∎

Property 4
.

If
\mathcal{N}_{FA}\in\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}
and
\mathcal{T}
are any arbitrary channels, then
\mathcal{N}_{FA}\circ\mathcal{T}\in\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}

.

Since,
\mathcal{N}_{FA}\in\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}

Using Eq.(

∎

Property 5
.

If
\mathcal{N}_{FA}\in\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}

Proof
.

Since

\rho_{B}

Now,

acting on input state

\sigma_{B}=\mathcal{N}_{FA}(\rho_{B})=\mathcal{N}_{FA}(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i})=\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathcal{N}_{FA}(\rho_{i})

Using Eq.(

(46)

Hence, we can say

annihilate the fidelity of entanglement of all mixed states.

∎

III.2.3
Necessary and sufficient condition for the detection of non-local fidelity of entanglement
annihilating channels

Here, we provide the necessary and sufficient condition for the identification of non-local fidelity annihilating channels.

.

\mathcal{N}_{FA}

\mathcal{W}

{\rho}_{B}\in\mathcal{D}({\mathcal{H}_{B}})

\rho_{B}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
and for all teleportation witness
\mathcal{W}
. Now, for arbitrary
\rho_{B}\in\mathcal{D}({\mathcal{H}_{B}})

\text{Tr}(\mathcal{W}\mathcal{N}_{FA}(\rho_{B}))=\text{Tr}(\mathcal{W}\sigma_{B})\hskip 5.69046pt\text{where},\hskip 5.69046pt\mathcal{N}_{FA}({\rho}_{B})={\sigma}_{B}

(47)

Since,

\text{non-local}-\mathbb{FAC}

\mathcal{W}

. Thus, we have the following observation for the two-qubit Weyl state

Observation 3
.

The fidelity of entanglement of the two-qubit Weyl state
\rho_{AB}
is greater than half if and only if
S_{\infty}(AB)<1.

Proof
.

Let
F(\rho_{AB})>1/2

\Longleftrightarrow\lambda_{\text{max}}>\frac{1}{2}

\Longleftrightarrow\log_{2}(\lambda_{\text{max}})>\log_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=-1

\Longleftrightarrow-\log_{2}(\lambda_{\text{max}})<1

\Longleftrightarrow-\log_{2}||\rho_{AB}||_{O}<1

\Longleftrightarrow

S_{\infty}(AB)<1.

∎

Conditional min-Rényi entropy of two-qubit Weyl state

is given by

Proof
.

∎

V
Relation between relative Entropy and fidelity of entanglement in
d\otimes d
systems

In this section, we explore the relation between relative entropy and fidelity of entanglement. The fidelity of entanglement of state
\rho_{AB}
is given as

\displaystyle F(\rho_{AB})=\max\limits_{U}\langle\phi|U^{\dagger}\otimes I\rho_{AB}U\otimes I|\phi\rangle

(87)

where
|\phi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d-1}|ii\rangle
, is maximally entangled state.
Further simplifying Eq.(
87
), we get

\displaystyle F(\rho_{AB})=\max\limits_{U}\text{Tr}[\rho_{AB}(U\otimes I)\rho_{\phi}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I)]

(88)

where
\rho_{\phi}=|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|.

Letting
(U\otimes I)\rho_{\phi}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I)=\sigma_{AB}
, we consider the relative entropy
Ruskai 2002
;
Vedral et al. 1997
;
Wilde 2013
;
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
denoted by
D(\sigma_{AB}||\rho_{AB})
, given as

\displaystyle D(\sigma_{AB}||\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}[\sigma_{AB}(\log\sigma_{AB}-\log\rho_{AB})]

\displaystyle=-\text{Tr}[\sigma_{AB}\log\rho_{AB}]\penalty\ ;\penalty\ (\text{Since, $\sigma_{AB}$ is pure}\Rightarrow\penalty\ \text{Tr}[\sigma_{AB}\log\sigma_{AB}]=0)

\displaystyle=-\text{Tr}[(U\otimes I)\rho_{\phi}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I)\log\rho_{AB}]

\displaystyle=-\text{Tr}[\log\rho_{AB}(U\otimes I)\rho_{\phi}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I)])

Now, we define a quantity
R(\rho_{AB})
as

\displaystyle R(\rho_{AB})=\max\limits_{U}\{-\text{Tr}[\log\rho_{AB}(U\otimes I)\rho_{\phi}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I)]\}

(89)

From Eq.(
88
), we have

\displaystyle-F(\rho_{AB})=\max\limits_{U}\{-\text{Tr}[\rho_{AB}(U\otimes I)\rho_{\phi}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I)]\}

(90)

Theorem 13
.

For density matrix
\rho_{AB}
,
R(\rho_{AB})
and fidelity of entanglement
F(\rho_{AB})
satisfy
R(\rho_{AB})\geq-F(\rho_{AB})
.

Proof
.

Consider a function
f(x)=-\log(x)
that is a convex function for all
x>0
&
X
is a random variable then, by Jensen’s inequality
Durrett 2019
, we have

\displaystyle f[E(X)]\leq E[f(X)]

(91)

In quantum scenarios Eq.(
91
) becomes

\displaystyle f[\text{Tr}(\mathbb{AB})]\leq\text{Tr}[f(\mathbb{A})\mathbb{B}]

(92)

where
\mathbb{A}
is density matrix and
\mathbb{B}
is positive semidefinite operator. Further simplifying Eq.(
92
), and putting the
f(x)=-\log(x)
and due to its operator convexity, we obtain

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}[\log(\mathbb{A})\mathbb{B}]\geq-\log[\text{Tr}(\mathbb{AB})]

(93)

Now, we will check the relation between
-\log[\text{Tr}(\mathbb{AB})]
and
-\text{Tr}(\mathbb{AB})
.
Since,
-\log(x)\geq-x+1
for all
0<x\leq 1
. The
\text{Tr}(\mathbb{AB})
is expectation value such that
0<\text{Tr}(\mathbb{AB})\leq 1
where
B\geq 0
and
B
is normalized. So, we have

\displaystyle-\log[\text{Tr}(\mathbb{AB})]

\displaystyle\geq-\text{Tr}(\mathbb{AB})+1

\displaystyle\geq-\text{Tr}(\mathbb{AB})

(94)

Using Eq.(
93
) and Eq.(
V
), we have

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}[\log(\mathbb{A})\mathbb{B}]\geq-\text{Tr}(\mathbb{AB})

(95)

Consider density matrices
\rho_{AB}
and
(U\otimes I)\rho_{\phi}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I)
such that
\mathbb{A}=\rho_{AB}
and
\mathbb{B}=(U\otimes I)\rho_{\phi}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I).

Maximizing over unitary operator
U
on the both side of Eq.(
95
), we obtain

\displaystyle\max\limits_{U}\{-\text{Tr}[\log\rho_{AB}(U\otimes I)\rho_{\phi}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I)]\}\geq\max\limits_{U}\{-\text{Tr}[\rho_{AB}(U\otimes I)\rho_{\phi}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I)]\}

(96)

Hence,
R(\rho_{AB})\geq-F(\rho_{AB})
.

∎

VI
Conclusion

Certifying entanglement is a fundamental area of study, as entangled states serve as a crucial resource for various quantum information processing tasks
Ekert 1991
;
Branciard et al. 2012
;
Pati 2000
;
Hillery et al. 1999
;
Cleve et al. 1999
;
Bandyopadhyay 2000
;
Bennett et al. 1993
. Fidelity of entanglement and different measures of quantum entropy play a pivotal role in assessing and certifying the presence of entanglement in a given quantum state. In this work, we aim to examine the interplay between these two indicators of entanglement, both in state and channel regimes. First, we present a comprehensive analysis and characterization of channels that break the fidelity of entanglement of bipartite composite systems. We then introduce the concept of fidelity annihilating channels, which act on a bipartite system
B
and reduce the fidelity of entanglement within the system
B
. We investigate the topological characterization and several fundamental properties of fidelity annihilating channels. Additionally, we provide examples of these channels, with a specific focus on analyzing the depolarizing channel. we then explore the relationship between
\mathbb{FBC}
and
2
-locally
\mathbb{FAC}
with other resource breaking quantum channels, specifically with entanglement annihilating channels (
\mathbb{EA}
), negative conditional entropy breaking channels (
\mathbb{NCEBC}
) and negative conditional entropy annihilating channels (
\mathbb{NCEAC}
).

Transitioning from the channel level to the state level, we establish a connection between quantum entropies and the fidelity of entanglement for a general two-qubit state. We derive the upper bound for the Rényi
2
-entropy and the conditional Rényi
2
-entropy, providing a necessary and sufficient condition for a general two-qubit state to exhibit a fidelity of entanglement greater than
\frac{1}{2}
of a general two-qubit state. We also observe that the min-Rényi entropy and conditional min-Rényi entropy are both upper-bounded when the state is useful for teleportation. Additionally, we present upper bounds for the Tsallis
2
-entropy and conditional Tsallis
2
-entropy of general two-qubit states, which ensure that the fidelity of entanglement is greater than
\frac{1}{2}
and vice versa. Finally, we extend our study to explore the relationship between relative entropy and the fidelity of entanglement.

Our study paves the way for several promising research directions. One potential avenue is to investigate the relationship between the fidelity of entanglement and various quantum entropies in higher-dimensional quantum states. Additionally, while we have provided a topological characterization of the fidelity annihilating channels, an important future direction could involve determining their Choi-Kraus representation, offering a deeper structural understanding of these channels.

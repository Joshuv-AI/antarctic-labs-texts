The quality and reliability of short videos about choledocholithiasis on TikTok and Bilibili in China
Authors: Xingyao Lu, Yutao Fang, Dongya Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCholedocholithiasis, a disease with a rising incidence and potential for severe complications, has prompted many to seek health information online. TikTok and Bilibili have emerged as key platforms for disseminating such information. This study evaluates the quality and reliability of short videos on choledocholithiasis on these platforms.MethodsThis study analyzed the top 100 choledocholithiasis-related videos from TikTok and Bilibili. The Global Quality Score (GQS), modified DISCERN (mDISCERN) tool, and the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) criteria were employed to assess video quality. Cohen’s Kappa coefficient is used to assess inter-rater agreement.Group comparisons were conducted using Mann-Whitney U and Kruskal-Wallis H tests, while Spearman’s correlation was utilized for correlation analysis.ResultsA total of 170 videos were included, predominantly uploaded by hepatobiliary surgeons. The content of these videos mainly focuses on treatment (81.76%), with limited coverage of etiology and diagnosis. The overall video quality was mediocre, with median scores of 3 for GQS (IQR: 3.00–4.00), 2 for mDISCERN (IQR: 2.00–2.00), and 2 for JAMA (IQR: 2.00–2.00). Videos from hepatobiliary surgeons generally exhibited superior quality. Notably, video quality showed no correlation with engagement metrics.ConclusionsThe content of choledocholithiasis-related videos is structurally deficient. The videos’ quality, reliability, and transparency are relatively poor, though those uploaded by hepatobiliary surgeons stand out for their superior quality and reliability. Importantly, video quality is independent of engagement metrics.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-026-04960-w.

Introduction

Choledocholithiasis, characterized by the presence of stones in the common bile duct, has seen a rising incidence, particularly in urban regions [1]. While some patients remain asymptomatic and are only diagnosed incidentally during imaging tests [2], others may present with abdominal pain, fever, or jaundice. This condition can lead to serious complications such as cholangitis [3], acute obstructive suppurative cholangitis (AOSC) [4], acute pancreatitis [5], and is even linked to cholangiocarcinoma development. A decade-long study tracking 20,808 choledocholithiasis patients revealed that 548 cases developed cholangiocarcinoma [6], underscoring the importance of public education on its clinical manifestations and treatment.

In recent years, social media platforms have become important sources of health information, offering patients easy access to disease-related knowledge [7–9]. Its strengths lie in accessibility, interactivity, and powerful dissemination capabilities [10]. Among these platforms, TikTok and Bilibili have gained widespread popularity in China [11]. TikTok and Bilibili both have over one hundred million monthly active users [12]. They are not only entertainment platforms but also increasingly include medical information [13]. However, the information quality on these platforms is highly variable. Previous studies on short videos concerning diseases like hypertension [14], gonorrhea [15], and inflammatory bowel disease [16] have indicated that, despite high interaction levels, the overall content quality remains mediocre.

Currently, no research has assessed the quality of videos specifically related to choledocholithiasis. Thus, this study aims to evaluate the quality of choledocholithiasis-related videos on these platforms from various angles, encompassing platform characteristics, uploader profiles, and content specifics. Our objective is to devise effective strategies for enhancing the quality of health information on digital media. The ultimate goal is to ensure the public has access to more reliable and precise information on choledocholithiasis, thereby facilitating better disease management.

Methods

Search strategy and data collection

On October 30, 2025, this study conducted searches for choledocholithiasis-related videos on TikTok and Bilibili. To minimize the influence of the algorithm-driven recommendation systems of Bilibili and TikTok, all searches were conducted using a newly registered account, with the browsing history and cache cleared. The collection of all videos was completed within one day to ensure that the results remained unaffected by fluctuations in platform algorithms or prior browsing activity. Using the Chinese term “胆总管结石” (choledocholithiasis), we retrieved the top 100 videos from each platform, ranked by default comprehensive order. Videos were included if they focused on choledocholithiasis and provided sufficient information on the topic. Exclusion criteria encompassed videos that: (1) were not in Chinese; (2) were duplicates; (3) were irrelevant to choledocholithiasis; (4) served commercial or advertising purposes; (5) were uploaded within the past week. For each video, we extracted key variables, including the uploader’s identity, number of likes, shares, comments, video duration (in seconds), and content. As all data were sourced from publicly accessible TikTok and Bilibili platforms, ethical approval was not required.

Uploader classification

After reviewing the identities of all video uploaders, we categorized all videos into three groups based on the uploaders’ identity: hepatobiliary surgeon, non-hepatobiliary surgeon (surgeons and physicians outside the hepatobiliary specialty), and individuals.

Quality assessment of videos

To evaluate video quality, we employed three validated scoring systems from previous studies. The GQS [8], a widely used 5-point scale, assessed overall video quality (Table 1). The mDISCERN tool [11, 17], also a 5-point scale, focused on evaluating video reliability (Table 2). The JAMA [18] evaluated video reliability and professionalism across four dimensions: authorship, attribution, currency, and disclosure (Table 3). Two experienced physicians independently assessed each video to ensure objectivity. In cases of discrepancy, a third rater assigned the final score.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables with non-normal distributions were expressed as median and interquartile range (IQR), while categorical variables were presented as frequency (percentage). For variables with a non-normal distribution, the Mann–Whitney U test was used for comparisons between two groups, and the Kruskal–Wallis H test was used for comparisons among three groups.To assess inter-rater reliability, we calculated Cohen’s Kappa coefficient.We explored the factors influencing video quality through logistic regression analysis.Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was used for correlation analysis, with statistical significance set at P < 0.05. All statistical analyses and plots were generated using R version 4.3.3.

Results

Features of choledocholithiasis videos

A total of 170 videos were selected for analysis (Fig. 1), with 99 videos from TikTok and 71 from Bilibili. The overall video quality was relatively poor (Table 4). Bilibili videos were longer (median: 145.80s, IQR: 70.20-414.90) than TikTok videos. However, TikTok videos outperformed Bilibili in terms of likes (median: 359.00, IQR: 163.00-757.00), collections (median: 109.00, IQR: 38.00-325.50), comments (median: 46.00, IQR: 16.00-154.50), and shares (median: 61.00, IQR: 18.00-278.50) (P < 0.05) (Table 5). Videos uploaded by individuals were the longest (median: 375.60s, IQR: 240.00-1562.10), while those by hepatobiliary surgeons garnered the highest likes (median: 268.00, IQR: 87.00-699.00) and shares (median: 46.00, IQR: 9.00-218.00) (Table 6).

Uploader and content characteristics

Medical professionals predominantly contributed to choledocholithiasis-related videos, with hepatobiliary surgeons accounting for 59.41%, non-hepatobiliary surgeons for 31.76%, and individuals for 8.82% (Fig. 2A). Uploader distribution varied significantly across platforms. On TikTok, hepatobiliary surgeons uploaded 85.86% of videos, whereas Bilibili had a higher proportion of videos from non-hepatobiliary surgeons (57.75%) and individuals (19.71%) (Fig. 2B and C).

Video quality and reliability assessment

The overall video quality was relatively poor, with a median GQS score of 3 (IQR: 3.00–4.00), while both the median mDISCERN and JAMA scores were 2 (IQR: 2.00–2.00 for mDISCERN and JAMA) (Table 4). No statistically significant difference was observed in the GQS scores between the two platforms (P = 0.153) (Table 5). The median JAMA and mDISCERN scores for TikTok and Bilibili are both 2, but there is a statistically significant difference between the two (P < 0.05) (Table 5), which may be due to differences in distribution rather than just a simple difference in medians.The kappa values for JAMA (κ = 0.897), GQS (κ = 0.918), and mDISCERN (κ = 0.900) indicate strong inter-observer agreement.

There were also differences in scores across different uploaders (Table 6). Individual-uploaded videos scored lowest on GQS, mDISCERN, and JAMA (Fig. 4A-C). Further analysis revealed that hepatobiliary surgeons (median: 3, IQR: 3.00–3.00)and non-hepatobiliary surgeons (median: 3, IQR: 3.00–4.00) scored higher on GQS than individuals (Fig. 5A). However, no statistically significant difference was found between the scores of hepatobiliary surgeons and non-hepatobiliary surgeons (P > 0.05). For the mDISCERN score, hepatobiliary surgeons (median:2, IQR: 2.00–2.00) scored the highest, followed by non-hepatobiliary surgeons, and individuals scored the lowest (Fig. 5B). In the JAMA scores, hepatobiliary surgeons also scored the highest (median: 2, IQR: 2.00–2.00), followed by non-hepatobiliary surgeons (median: 2, IQR: 1.00–2.00) (Fig. 5C).

Through regression analysis (Table 7), we further explored the factors influencing video quality. The results indicated that the identity of the uploader is a key factor affecting video quality. Videos uploaded by healthcare professionals, including hepatobiliary surgeons (GQS: β = 1.698, P = 0.002; mDISCERN: β = 5.033, P < 0.001; JAMA: β = 5.288, P < 0.001) and non-hepatobiliary surgeons (GQS: β = 1.895, P = 0.001; mDISCERN: β = 1.888, P = 0.003; JAMA: β = 2.251, P = 0.002), had significantly higher quality than those from the individual group. The influence of video content on quality was not consistent across different scoring metrics. The analysis revealed that videos containing etiology (β = 1.449, P = 0.003) and clinical manifestations (β = 0.843, P = 0.010) were associated with higher GQS scores, but did not show significant effects on the mDISCERN and JAMA scores (P > 0.05). This finding highlights the complexity of video quality assessment and the differential sensitivity of various scoring systems to content factors.

Correlation analysis of video features with quality and video content

Spearman correlation analysis examined relationships between GQS, mDISCERN, JAMA, video length, and interactivity metrics (Fig. 6). Strong correlations (r range from 0.81 to 0.91) were found among interactivity metrics, and moderate to strong correlations (r range from 0.38 to 0.59) were observed between the different scores. However, no correlation was found between video length, interactivity metrics, and video quality scores.

Discussion

This study evaluated the quality and reliability of choledocholithiasis-related videos on TikTok and Bilibili, revealing unsatisfactory results. The overall video quality was relatively poor, with a predominant focus on treatment and relatively little emphasis on etiology. Videos uploaded by hepatobiliary surgeons were of superior quality. These findings offer preliminary insights into the influence of platform and uploader type on health education regarding choledocholithiasis and suggest avenues for further optimization.

General characteristics of the videos

This study analyzed a total of 170 choledocholithiasis-related videos, which collectively garnered 111,814 likes, 52,563 collections, and 34,341 shares, underscoring the significant role of digital health in disseminating health information. TikTok outperformed Bilibili in terms of user interactions such as likes, collections, comments, and shares, aligning with previous research [11]. TikTok is known for its short-form videos [19, 20], while Bilibili focuses more on longer, more detailed content [8]. A study has shown that, regardless of video length, the median engagement time is approximately 6 min [21].TikTok, as a platform for relatively short videos, may be more effective in capturing viewers’ attention and promoting interactive behaviors. In future video production, it is essential to strike a balance between brevity and quality, ensuring that more comprehensive and accurate knowledge is integrated into these shorter videos.

Video content analysis

The content analysis revealed that treatment-related information dominated, followed by clinical symptoms, which helps the public understand choledocholithiasis and seek timely treatment. Currently, various surgical methods are available for treating choledocholithiasis, including endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) [22], laparoscopic common bile duct exploration [23], and open common bile duct exploration [24]. While several studies have compared the advantages and disadvantages of these treatments, no consensus has been reached [25–27]. Providing the public with information on the processes, advantages, and disadvantages of these different surgical options can aid them in selecting the most suitable treatment. However, there is relatively little content on etiology (11.18%) and diagnosis (19.41%), and few videos discuss dietary management or the severity of choledocholithiasis complications. Untreated choledocholithiasis can lead to AOSC [4] and biliary pancreatitis [5]. AOSC can progress to sepsis, multiple organ dysfunction syndrome, and even death [28], while biliary diseases are a major trigger for pancreatitis [29]. Therefore, in addition to objectively explaining the advantages and disadvantages of surgical options, it is crucial to educate the public on dietary precautions [30].

Quality differences analysis

The study found that the quality of videos related to choledocholithiasis is relatively poor. There were no differences in GQS scores between the two platforms, but TikTok scored higher on JAMA and mDISCERN compared to Bilibili. This may be attributed to the fact that most TikTok uploaders are hepatobiliary surgeons [31]. Analyzing different uploaders revealed that videos posted by hepatobiliary and non-hepatobiliary surgeons scored higher in GQS than those by individuals, indicating that medical professionals can ensure overall video quality. This finding is consistent with previous research on liver cancer [32] and gonorrhea [15]. In terms of mDISCERN and JAMA scores, hepatobiliary surgeons scored the highest, while individuals scored the lowest. However, the median mDISCERN and JAMA scores for videos uploaded by hepatobiliary surgeons were both 2, which is not satisfactory. There is a widespread issue across videos related to choledocholithiasis regarding the lack of standardized citation sources and comprehensive content. In the future, video uploaders, especially healthcare professionals, should cite clinical guidelines or reliable literature in these health education videos to enhance the reliability of the content. Additionally, more detailed explanations of disease complications, treatment options, and etiologies should be provided. Currently, most videos on choledocholithiasis are uploaded by hepatobiliary surgeons. Considering that endoscopic treatment based on ERCP is a routine procedure in the management of choledocholithiasis, gastroenterologists and endoscopists often participate in the clinical practice. Therefore, in the future, physicians from these specialties should also be encouraged to engage in public education on choledocholithiasis.

Correlation analysis

This study uncovered a strong correlation among video interaction metrics, suggesting that likes, comments, collections, and shares can mutually reinforce one another. However, no correlation was found between video length, engagement, and quality, aligning with previous research on ovarian failure [33] and thyroid eye disease [34]. This “high engagement - low quality” trend may facilitate the rapid dissemination of low-quality, false, or misleading health information. Therefore, viewers should approach highly engaging videos with a critical eye and avoid blind trust. When developing recommendation algorithms, platforms should consider not only interaction metrics but also video quality.

Implications of the study

Previous research has demonstrated that online video education can enhance treatment outcomes [35, 36]. Thus, improving video quality is paramount. Several measures can effectively enhance the quality of choledocholithiasis-related videos. First, it is advisable to use authoritative references and simple, accessible language when explaining medical knowledge. When explaining treatment plans for diseases, it is essential to ensure the objectivity of the information. Second, the doctor’s affiliation should be clearly stated, with the department to which the doctor belongs indicated on the video page. Lastly, it is essential to incorporate more content on diagnosis and etiology, while also raising public awareness about the serious complications of choledocholithiasis to encourage early treatment-seeking.

Limitation

This study has several limitations. First, it focuses solely on Chinese-language videos, and different languages and cultural backgrounds may influence disease perception and management approaches. Second, while we conducted a preliminary assessment of video quality using widely adopted tools, this approach presents certain limitations in terms of innovation. Future research could further explore longitudinal and dynamic factors influencing changes in video quality by utilizing other scoring tools, as well as conduct interventional studies to evaluate their impact on actual viewer behavior. Third, the cross-sectional study design captures only a snapshot at a specific point in time. Content quality may vary over time, which poses a threat to the stability of our results and their reproducibility. Additionally, the number of videos uploaded by individuals is only 15, which somewhat limits the reliability of the comparison in quality between individuals and medical professionals.Lastly, there is inevitably a certain degree of subjectivity in the video evaluation process.

Conclusion

This study explores the potential benefits and challenges associated with utilizing short video platforms for communicating health information about choledocholithiasis, based on 170 videos. We found the overall quality of the videos is relatively poor, with insufficient attention to daily care and disease complications. Videos produced by hepatobiliary surgeons demonstrate higher quality than those created by non-hepatobiliary surgeons and individuals.Our analysis did not identify a significant correlation between user engagement metrics and video quality.In the future, medical professionals should optimize video content, while platforms should strengthen video regulation and improve dissemination strategies to enhance content quality and credibility.Larger sample sizes in future studies could further explore the factors affecting video quality.

A Two-Layer Framework for Joint Online Configuration Selection and Admission Control
Owen Shen, Haoran Xu, Peter Glynn, Yinyu Ye, Patrick Jaillet

Abstract
We study online configuration selection with admission control problem, which arises in LLM serving, GPU scheduling, and revenue management. In a planning horizon with T T periods, we consider a two-layer framework for the decisions made within each time period. In the first layer, the decision maker selects one of the K K configurations (ex. quantization, parallelism, fare class) which induces distribution over the reward-resource pair of the incoming request. In the second layer, the decision maker observes the request and then decides whether to accept it or not. Benchmarking this framework requires care. We introduce a switching-aware fluid oracle that accounts for the value of mixing configurations over time, provably upper-bounding any online policy. We derive a max-min formulation for evaluating the benchmark, and we characterize saddle points of the max-min problem via primal-dual optimality conditions linking equilibrium, feasibility, and complementarity. This guides the design of SP-UCB–OLP algorithm, which solves an optimistic saddle point problem and achieves O ~ ​ ( K ​ T ) \tilde{O}(\sqrt{KT}) regret.

Many resource-constrained systems require
two sequential decisions
at each time step. First, the decision maker selects a
system configuration
that determines the type of incoming request. Second, after observing the request’s characteristics, the decision maker makes an
admission decision
—whether to accept or reject the request, subject to a cumulative resource budget. Neither decision alone captures the full problem: the configuration shapes what the decision maker will see, and the admission control determines what resources are consumed. It is the
coupling
of both decisions that creates the central challenge.

This two-layer structure arises naturally in several domains. In LLM serving, the system first selects a serving configuration (quantization level, batching strategy)
(
23
;
32
)
, then observes the incoming request’s prompt length and estimated compute cost, and finally decides whether to admit the job under its current resource budget. In cluster scheduling, the system first chooses a parallelism configuration
(
19
;
27
;
29
)
, then observes the job’s resource footprint, and decides whether to schedule it. In revenue management, the firm first selects a fare class
(
31
;
18
)
, then observes a customer’s willingness-to-pay, and decides whether to accept the booking. In all cases, the first decision (configuration) is made
before
the request is revealed, while the second decision (admission) is made
after
observing the request—a
select–observe–admit
protocol.

We formalize this protocol as follows. There are
K

configurations
, each inducing a distribution over reward–resource pairs
(r,\mathbf{a})
. At each period, the decision maker (1)
selects
a configuration
\theta_{t}
, (2)
observes
the reward–resource pair
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})\sim\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{t}}
, and (3)
admits or rejects
the request subject to a cumulative budget
\mathbf{B}
. All decisions are irrevocable. Two existing frameworks each capture one layer of this problem but not both. Bandits with knapsacks (BwK)
(
9
)
models configuration selection under budget constraints, but uses
sample-and-commit
: pulling an arm immediately consumes resources with no opportunity to reject after observing the outcome. Online linear programming (OLP)
(
5
;
24
)
models observation-based admission—the decision maker sees
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})
before committing resources—but assumes a
fixed
request distribution with no configuration selection. Our framework unifies both: configuration selection from BwK with observation-based admission from OLP.

Main Contributions.

1.

Switching-aware fluid oracle.
Designing an appropriate oracle is essential in solving an online decision making problem. An oracle solves a so-called offline optimization problem whose optimal value serves as a benchmark for evaluating the performance of feasible online policies. A natural oracle of our problem is the fixed configuration oracle, i.e., fix a single best configuration and solve the offline resource allocation problem of the selected configuration. The offline resource allocation problem is given in
24
, and its optimal value depends on the reward-resource distribution. The best configuration in this natural oracle refers to the one whose reward-resource distribution gives the highest optimal value of the offline resource allocation problem. However, this oracle which is based on the naive “best-response” interpretation of our problem fails to upper bound the optimal online policy when there are heterogeneous resources. In those situations, a feasible online policy that switches among configurations may have higher total reward than the fixed configuration oracle by exploiting complementary budgets (Example
1
).

In this paper, we propose a
switching-aware
oracle which provides a valid upper bound on all feasible online policies (Theorem
8
). This eliminates the “beating the oracle” pathology of the fixed-configuration oracle.

2.

Primal-dual characterization of the oracle.
We derive an equivalent max-min formulation of the offline optimization problem in the switching-aware oracle by duality. We build the connection between the saddle points of the max-min problem and the primal optimal solutions of the offline optimization problem via threshold rules, feasibility, and complementarity conditions (Theorems
6
–
7
), which also corrects the naive “best-response” interpretation.

3.

Sublinear regret algorithm.
We design SP-UCB–OLP Algorithm by repeatedly solving the saddle points of an optimistic version of the max-min formulation of the offline optimization problem in the switching-aware oracle. We prove the regret of SP-UCB-OLP Algorithm is
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{KT})
(Theorem
9
) where
T
is the number of periods in the planning horizon and
K
is the number of configurations. We also conduct numerical experiments to validate the regret upper bound.

Related Work

Bandits with knapsacks (BwK).

BwK
(
9
)
is a framework that deals with multi-armed bandit problems under budget constraints. Many variants of BwK problems including adversarial input
(
20
)
, contextual features
(
3
;
1
)
, non-linear reward and constraint
(
4
)
and non-stationary environments
(

)
have been studied in the literature. Although BwK has the components of both configuration selection and resource allocation, BwK uses
sample-and-commit
: pulling an arm immediately consumes resources without the option to reject after observing the outcome. This makes our framework and BwK significantly different because our framework leverages
observe-then-decide
admission, where the agent sees
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})
before committing resources.

Online linear programming (OLP) and threshold-based decision rule.

OLP
(
5
)
is a framework of online resource allocation problems that uses
observe-then-decide
. Dual-based algorithms
(
13
;
15
;
2
;
10
;
24
;
12
)
are the most common way to solve OLP problems. These methods learn dual prices and apply threshold rule to admission control. However, OLP assumes a
fixed
reward-resource distribution of the requests and does not explore over configurations. In this paper, our proposed SP-UCB-OLP algorithm learns both a probability distribution over the configurations and a dual price. The distribution guides the configuration selection decision, and the dual price gives a threshold-based admission rule.

Our threshold-based admission rule also relates to prophet inequalities
(
22
;
30
)
, which compare online policies to omniscient prophets. Modern extensions handle matroid constraints
(
21
)
and pricing mechanisms
(
16
)
. Unlike prophet settings with adversarial inputs, reward and resource consumption of the requests in our model are stochastic but configuration-dependent. Dynamic pricing problems in revenue management
(
18
;
31
;
11
)
. are also closely related to the threshold-based admission rule.

Saddle-point of max-min problem.

The offline optimization problem solved by our switching-aware oracle can be expressed as a max-min optimization problem, and our SP-UCB-OLP algorithm solves the joint online configuration selection and admission control problem by learning the saddle point of this max-min optimization problem. Thus our work also aligns with saddle-point learning and multiplicative weights
(
17
;
8
)
.

Our positioning.

We unify configuration exploration (from BwK) with observe-then-decide threshold admission (from OLP) through a switching-aware oracle. This switching-aware oracle provides a valid upper bound for the total reward earned by any feasible online polices, and learning the saddle point of the max-min optimization problem corresponding to this oracle leads us to the SP-UCB-OLP algorithm that achieves sublinear regret.

3
Model

Two-layer decision model.

The planning horizon has
T\in\mathbb{N}
time periods, and one request arrives at each of the time periods. Let
d
be the number of resource types, and
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
be the total budget vector. Denote
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
as the reward-resource pair of request arriving in time period
t
. There are
K
configurations
\Theta=\{1,\ldots,K\}
. Each configuration
\theta
induces a reward–resource distribution
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
. At each time
t=1,\cdots,T
, the decision maker (1) selects a configuration
\theta_{t}\in\Theta
, (2) observes
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})\sim\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{t}}
of the request arriving in time period
t
, (3) chooses
x_{t}\in\{0,1\}
after observing
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})
. If
x_{t}=1
, the decision maker collects reward
r_{t}
and consumes resources
\mathbf{a}_{t}
.
\{x_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
must satisfy the
pathwise budget constraint
:

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{a}_{t}x_{t}\leq\mathbf{B}\quad\text{(componentwise, almost surely)}.

In addition, the decisions made at each time period is irrevocable. The goal of the decision maker is to maximize the expected total reward
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{T}r_{t}x_{t}]
.

3.1
Assumptions

Assumption 1

(Stationarity and Independence)
.

If an algorithm select configuration
\theta
at time period
t
,
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})
is drawn independently from
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
. In addition, the distribution
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
is time-invariant for all
\theta\in\Theta
.

Assumption 2

(Boundedness)
.

There exist constants
R_{\max},A_{\max}>0
such that for all
\theta
and
(r,\mathbf{a})\sim\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
:

0\leq r\leq R_{\max},\quad\|\mathbf{a}\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\max}\quad\text{a.s.}

Assumption 3

(Budget Scaling)
.

There exists
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
such that
B=T\cdot\mathbf{b}
.

In the analysis of this paper, we fix the vector
\mathbf{b}
and discuss the dependence of the performance of algorithms as the number of periods
T
goes to infinity. Under this asymptotic regime, Assumption
3
indicates that we consider the “large resource budget” scenario in which the total budget is proportional to the total number of time periods.

Assumption 4

(Continuous Valuation)
.

For any
\theta\in\Theta
, if
(r,\mathbf{a})\sim\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
, then the conditional distribution of
r
given
\mathbf{a}=\alpha
is continuous for any
\alpha
in the support of
\mathbf{a}
.

We will later show that we do admission control through a price-based threshold method, in which we have a price
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and accept a request with reward-resource pair
(r,\mathbf{a})
if
r>\mathbf{a}^{T}\mathbf{p}
. Assumption
4
serves as a “No-Tie” assumption, i.e., for any
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{P}(r=\mathbf{a}^{T}\mathbf{p})=0
.

4
Switching-Aware Fluid Oracle

We define an oracle and the corresponding offline optimization problem, which provides a benchmark that we can define the regret of an online algorithm with respect to. Recall
\mathbf{b}:=\mathbf{B}/T
is the per-period budget given in Assumption
3
.

4.1
Fixed configuration oracle leads to negative regret

Let
(r_{t,\theta},a_{t,\theta})
be the reward-resource pair observed if the decision maker selects configuration
\theta
. Define

\begin{split}V^{off}_{\theta}(\mathbf{b}):=\mathbb{E}\Big[\max\Big\{&\sum_{t=1}^{T}r_{t,\theta}x_{t,\theta}\Big|\;\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{t,\theta}x_{t,\theta}\leq T\cdot\mathbf{b},\\
&0\leq x_{t,\theta}\leq 1\;\forall t\Big\}\Big].\end{split}

The optimization problem inside the expectation defining
V^{off}_{\theta}(\mathbf{b})
is the so-called offline LP in
24
.
V^{off}_{\theta}(\mathbf{b})
represents the maximal expected total reward earned if the decision maker always selects configuration
\theta
and knows the reward-resource pairs of all the incoming requests in advance. Let
\{x_{t,\theta}^{*}\}_{t=1}^{T}
be the optimal solution of the maximization problem inside the expectation. Then, a natural oracle of our problem is to always select
\theta^{*}=\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta}V^{off}_{\theta}(\mathbf{b})
and accepting requests according to
\{x_{t,\theta}^{*}\}_{t=1}^{T}
. Then, the expected total reward earned by this fixed configuration oracle is
V^{*}(\mathbf{b}):=V^{off}_{\theta^{*}}(\mathbf{b})
. However, the following example indicates that it is possible for an online policy having higher expected total reward than
V^{*}(\mathbf{b})
.

Example 1

(Complementary Resources)
.

Consider
T=100
periods,
d=2
resources with
\mathbf{b}=[0.5,0.5]
, and two configurations with deterministic resource consumption:
(r\sim Uniform(0,2),\mathbf{a}=[1,0])
and
(r=Uniform(0,2),\mathbf{a}=[0,1])
, each consuming only one type of resource. The fixed configuration oracle achieves
V^{*}(\mathbf{b})=\frac{7550}{101}\approx 74.76
(one budget wasted), but the optimal online policy is to select configuration
1
when
t\leq 50
, select configuration
2
when
t>50
and accept all the requests, which earns
100
as the expected total reward.

This example motivates the following derivation of the switching-aware oracle.

4.2
Primal Mixed Fluid Relaxation

Let
\Delta_{K}=\{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}_{+}:\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}=1\}
be the probability simplex. For each
\theta\in\Theta
, let
x_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\to[0,1]
be a measurable acceptance rule. We define the
mixed fluid value
as:

\begin{split}V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}):=\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}\max_{\{x_{\theta}\}}\Big\{&\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}w_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[r\,x_{\theta}(r,\mathbf{a})]\\
&\Big|\;\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}w_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\mathbf{a}\,x_{\theta}(r,\mathbf{a})]\leq\mathbf{b}\Big\}.\end{split}

(1)

The max-max form represents the two-layer framework in our problem. The outer maximization represents the configuration selection, and the inner maximization represents the admission control after a configuration is selected.
V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})
also shows how OLP and BwK are connected with our problem. When
K=1
, our problem is reduced to an OLP problem, and
V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})
is also equivalent to the fluid relaxation of OLP given by
(
14
)
in this case. When the acceptance rule is predetermined and not part of the decision, i.e.,
\{x_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
is fixed, our problem is reduced to a BwK problem, and
V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})
is also equivalent to the LP relaxation of BwK given by
(
9
)
in this case.

The switching-aware fluid oracle knows the optimal solution
(\mathbf{w}^{*},\{x^{*}(\theta)\})
. At each time period
t
, the oracle randomly select a configuration
\theta_{t}
following the probability distribution given by the
mixture

\mathbf{w}^{*}
. After observing
(r_{t},a_{t})
, the oracle applies the acceptance rule
x_{\theta_{t}}
and earns
r_{t}x_{\theta_{t}}(r_{t},a_{t})
. The switching-aware fluid oracle ignores the budget constraint. Thus, the total expected reward earned by the switching-aware fluid oracle is
T\cdot V^{mix}(\mathbf{b})
. In the following subsections, we characterize
V^{mix}(\mathbf{b})
and show
T\cdot V^{mix}(\mathbf{b})
is a valid benchmark.

4.3
Dual Form and Envelope Structure

Envelope and threshold consumption.

For
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define the
surplus
and
threshold consumption
:

g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}):=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}],\;h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}):=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\mathbf{a}\,\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}].

For a mixture
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}
, define

L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}):=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}).

Theorem 5

(Primal–Dual Form of the Mixed Fluid Oracle)
.

Under Assumptions
2
and
3
, let
b_{min}
be the smallest component of
\mathbf{b}
(in particular,
b_{\min}>0
) and
P_{max}
be
2R_{max}/b_{min}
. With price domain
\mathcal{P}=[0,P_{\max}]^{d}
,

\begin{split}V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})&=\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\right\}\\
&=\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\max_{\theta\in\Theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\right\}.\end{split}

(2)

Define the induced consumption
H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}):=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})
.
Let
A(\mathbf{p}):=\arg\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})
be the
active (envelope) set
, and
\mathrm{supp}(\mathbf{w})
be the indices of positive components of
\mathbf{w}
. The following theorem establishes the relationship between the primal form and the max-min form of
V^{mix}(\mathbf{b})
.

Theorem 6

(Primal–dual optimality (saddle/KKT conditions))
.

Assume Assumptions
1
–
4
.
A pair
(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\in\Delta_{K}\times\mathcal{P}
is a saddle point of
\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})

(and hence attains
V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})
) if and only if:

(i)

(Envelope support)

\mathrm{supp}(\mathbf{w}^{\star})\subseteq A(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
.

(ii)

(Feasibility)

H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\leq\mathbf{b}
(componentwise).

(iii)

(Complementarity)

\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\rangle=0
.

Moreover, the mixture
\mathbf{w}^{\star}
together with threshold admission
x_{\theta}(r,\mathbf{a})=\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}
for all
\theta\in\Theta
,
is primal-optimal for (
1
) and

V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})=L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})
.

Theorem
6
indicates that, at equilibrium,
\mathbf{w}^{\star}
randomizes only among envelope-optimal configurations at
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
,
and the induced threshold consumption matches the budget on priced resources (complementarity). The following theorem further characterize the saddle points of the max-min form of
V^{mix}(\mathbf{b})
.

Theorem 7

(Characterization of all saddle points)
.

Assume Assumptions
1
–
4
. Define the envelope objective

f(\mathbf{p}):=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\max_{\theta\in\Theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}),\qquad\mathcal{P}^{\star}:=\argmin_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}f(\mathbf{p}).

Then the set of saddle points of
L
is exactly

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\Big\{(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle\in\Delta_{K}\times\mathcal{P}:\ \mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{\star},\ \mathrm{supp}(\mathbf{w})\subseteq A(\mathbf{p}),

\displaystyle H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})\leq\mathbf{b},\ \langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})\rangle=0\Big\}.

4.4
Oracle Justification

Denote
R_{T}^{\pi}
as the total reward earned by an online policy
\pi
.

Theorem 8

(Switching-Aware Oracle Upper Bound)
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
2
, for any online policy
\pi
satisfying the pathwise budget constraint,

\mathbb{E}[R_{T}^{\pi}]\leq T\cdot V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}).

Proof sketch.

Fix
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}
,
r_{t}x_{t}\leq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle x_{t}+(r_{t}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle)_{+}
almost surely.
By
\sum_{t}\mathbf{a}_{t}x_{t}\leq\mathbf{B}
yields
R_{T}^{\pi}\leq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{B}\rangle+\sum_{t=1}^{T}(r_{t}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle)_{+}
almost surely.
Taking expectation and upper bounding the average surplus by
\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})
gives
\mathbb{E}[R_{T}^{\pi}]\leq T(\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}))
. Minimizing over
\mathbf{p}
completes the proof. Full proof is in Appendix
C
.

.

5
Algorithm: SP-UCB–OLP

The switching-aware oracle motivates the following algorithm. At each time period
t
, we use the observed data to learn the surplus functions
\{g_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
. Using the sample averages
\{\hat{g}_{\theta,t}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
with respect to the observed data is the most straightforward way to learn surplus functions; however, to balance exploration and exploitation, we apply the idea of UCB and add confidence radii
\{\beta_{\alpha,\theta}(t)\}
to
\{\hat{g}_{\theta,t}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
to get our final estimates of the surplus functions. We replace the surplus functions in (
2
) with the estimated surplus function to get an optimistic saddle point problem and solve this problem to get
(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})
. We then randomly select a configuration
\theta_{t}
following the probability distribution given by
\mathbf{w}_{t}
and accept the request if
r_{t}>{\mathbf{a}_{t}}^{T}\mathbf{p}_{t}
and we have enough resources. We introduce the formulation of confidence radii
\beta_{\alpha,\theta}(t)
and the details of algorithm in the following sections.

5.1
Confidence Radii and Optimistic Saddle Point Problem

We store every observed
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})
regardless of whether it is admitted, so that surplus estimates are unbiased.
For each configuration
\theta
, maintain a dataset
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}
of all observed samples (accepted or rejected) with count
N_{\theta}=|\mathcal{S}_{\theta}|
. Define the empirical surplus:

\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p}):=\frac{1}{N_{\theta}\vee 1}\sum_{(r,\mathbf{a})\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}}(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}.

By Lemma
20
and its anytime version (Corollary
21
) in the Appendix,
for any
\alpha\geq 1
and any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
,
the following holds simultaneously for all
\theta\in\Theta
and all rounds
t\leq T
:

\sup_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\big|g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})-\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p})\big|\;\leq\;\beta_{\alpha,\theta}(t),

where the confidence radius is

\beta_{\alpha,\theta}(t):=\alpha\,c_{g}R_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{d\log\!\Big(\frac{c_{0}\,d\,P_{\max}\,A_{\max}\,T}{R_{\max}}\Big)+\log(KT/\delta)}{N_{\theta}\vee 1}},

and
c_{g},c_{0}>0
are absolute constants from the concentration lemma (Appendix
D
).
Then, at each round, we solve the following
optimistic saddle point problem
:

\displaystyle(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})

\displaystyle\in\argmax_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}\argmin_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}L_{t}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})

(3)

\displaystyle=\argmax_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}\argmin_{\mathbf{p}\geq 0}L_{t}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}),

where
L_{t}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}):=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}(\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p})+\beta_{\alpha,\theta}(t))
, and
\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}:=(1-\varepsilon)\mathbf{b}
with

\varepsilon=\sqrt{\log T/T}
. The conservative per-period budget
\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}
is used to do feasibility control, which is consistent with literature practice such as in
5
. The equality that replace
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}
with
\mathbf{p}\geq 0
is by Assumption
2
. For the proof of this equality, please see Appendix
F
.

7
Conclusion

We studied a two-layer sequential decision problem combining configuration exploration and admission control under budget constraints. We construct a
switching-aware fluid oracle
which provides a benchmark that upper bounds the expected total reward of any online policy. This
switching-aware fluid oracle
also inspires the design of
SP-UCB–OLP
algorithm, which solves an optimistic saddle point problem at each round and uses the computed saddle point to select configurations and do admission control. We show that the regret of
SP-UCB–OLP
algorithm against the benchmark given by the switching-aware oracle is
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{KT})
. We also run numerical experiments to study the empirical performance of
SP-UCB–OLP
algorithm.

Limitations and future work.

Our analysis assumes i.i.d. and bounded reward-resource pair of requests within each configuration. Extensions to non-stationary arrivals, contextual settings (where configurations depend on observed context), and delayed feedback are natural future research directions.

C.1
Bounded Dual Prices

We first justify that restricting dual prices to a compact box
\mathcal{P}=[0,P_{\max}]^{d}
is without loss of generality.

Lemma 10

(Bounded Dual Prices are W.L.O.G. (Safe Budget))
.

Assume
0\leq r\leq R_{\max}
a.s. and
\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
satisfies
b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{i}\geq b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{\min}>0
for all
i
. Then every minimizer of

\min_{\mathbf{p}\geq 0}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\rangle+\max_{\theta\in\Theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\right\}

lies in the box
\|\mathbf{p}\|_{\infty}\leq R_{\max}/b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{\min}
. Hence for any
P_{\max}\geq R_{\max}/b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{\min}
, restricting to
\mathcal{P}=[0,P_{\max}]^{d}
does not change
V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})
.

Proof.

Let
i
be any coordinate and suppose
p_{i}>R_{\max}/b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{i}
. Then
\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\rangle\geq p_{i}b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{i}>R_{\max}
. Since
(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}\geq 0
, the objective at
\mathbf{p}
exceeds
R_{\max}
.

At
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}
, the objective equals
\max_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[(r)_{+}]=\max_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[r]\leq R_{\max}
. Therefore any point with
p_{i}>R_{\max}/b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{i}
cannot be optimal. Applying this coordinatewise yields
\|\mathbf{p}^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq R_{\max}/b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{\min}
.
∎

Importance.
This lemma ensures:

•

The minimax theorem (Sion) applies with compact domain
\mathcal{P}
.

•

The upper boundary constraint
p_{i}\leq P_{\max}
is non-binding at optimality.

•

KKT conditions for the inner minimization involve only the lower boundary
\mathbf{p}\geq 0
.

Note:
Whenever the analysis claims “the upper box constraint
p_{i}\leq P_{\max}
is non-binding,” the correct condition is
P_{\max}>R_{\max}/b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{\min}
.

Corollary 11

(Bounded Dual Prices for Empirical Objectives)
.

Fix any mixture
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}
and any time
t
. Consider the empirical objective

\min_{\mathbf{p}\geq 0}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p})\right\},\qquad\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{N_{\theta}(t)}\sum_{(r,\mathbf{a})\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(t)}(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}.

Then every minimizer satisfies
\|\mathbf{p}\|_{\infty}\leq R_{\max}/b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{\min}
.
Hence if
P_{\max}>R_{\max}/b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{\min}
, the upper box constraint
p_{i}\leq P_{\max}

is non-binding at the empirical minimizer(s) as well.

Proof.

The argument is identical to Lemma
10
. If
p_{i}>R_{\max}/b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{i}
, then

\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\rangle\geq p_{i}b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{i}>R_{\max}
while
\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p})\geq 0
,
so the objective exceeds
R_{\max}
. At
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}
, the objective equals

\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{0})=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\frac{1}{N_{\theta}}\sum r\leq R_{\max}
.
Thus such
\mathbf{p}
cannot be optimal. Apply coordinatewise.
∎

C.2
Nash Equilibrium Characterization

We prove that saddle points of zero-sum games are equivalent to Nash equilibria.

Theorem 12

(Saddle Points
\Leftrightarrow
Nash Equilibria)
.

For the zero-sum game with payoff function
L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})
, a pair
(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})
is a saddle point of
L
if and only if it is a Nash equilibrium.

Proof of Theorem
12
.

Consider the zero-sum game with payoff
L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})
where Player 1 receives
L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})
and Player 2 receives
-L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})
.

Definition (Saddle Point).

(w^{*},p^{*})
is a saddle point if:

L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{*})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p})\quad\forall\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K},\forall\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}.

Definition (Nash Equilibrium).

(w^{*},p^{*})
is a Nash equilibrium if no player can improve by unilateral deviation:

1.

L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})\geq L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{*})
for all
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}
(Player 1 best-responds)

2.

-L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})\geq-L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p})
for all
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}
(Player 2 best-responds)

(
\Rightarrow
) Saddle Point implies Nash Equilibrium.

Assume
(w^{*},p^{*})
is a saddle point.

Player 1’s condition:
The left inequality gives
L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{*})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})
for all
\mathbf{w}
, so
w^{*}
maximizes
L(\cdot,\mathbf{p}^{*})
. Player 1 has no profitable deviation.

Player 2’s condition:
The right inequality gives
L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p})
for all
\mathbf{p}
, equivalently
-L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p})\leq-L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})
. Thus
p^{*}
minimizes
L(w^{*},\cdot)
. Player 2 has no profitable deviation.

(
\Leftarrow
) Nash Equilibrium implies Saddle Point.

Assume
(w^{*},p^{*})
is a Nash equilibrium.

From Player 1’s condition:
L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})\geq L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{*})
for all
\mathbf{w}
, yielding the left saddle inequality.

From Player 2’s condition:
-L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})\geq-L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p})
for all
\mathbf{p}
, hence
L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p})
, yielding the right saddle inequality.

Combining:
L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{*})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{p})
for all
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}
.
∎

Remark 4

(Existence and Uniqueness)
.

By Sion’s minimax theorem, a saddle point (and hence Nash equilibrium) exists when
\Delta_{K}
and
\mathcal{P}
are compact convex and
L
is convex in
\mathbf{p}
and linear (hence concave) in
\mathbf{w}
. The optimal mixture
\mathbf{w}^{*}
may be unique (when budget constraints pin down resource utilization), while
\mathbf{p}^{*}
may be non-unique (any price on the “tie surface” where configurations have equal surplus is optimal).

C.3
Pointwise Surplus Inequality

Lemma 13

(Pointwise Surplus Inequality)
.

For any
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
, any
(r,\mathbf{a})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
, and any
x\in[0,1]
:

r\cdot x\leq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\cdot x+(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}.

Proof.

Let
z:=r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle
. Then:

\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\cdot x+(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}=(r-z)\cdot x+z_{+}=r\cdot x+(z_{+}-z\cdot x).

We show
z_{+}-z\cdot x\geq 0
:

•

If
z\geq 0
:
z_{+}-z\cdot x=z(1-x)\geq 0
since
x\leq 1
.

•

If
z<0
:
z_{+}=0
and
z_{+}-z\cdot x=-z\cdot x\geq 0
since
z<0
and
x\geq 0
.

∎

C.4
Optimal Acceptance Under Fixed Price

Lemma 14

(Threshold Optimality)
.

Fix
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}
and
\theta
. The optimization:

\sup_{x_{\theta}:0\leq x_{\theta}\leq 1}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)\cdot x_{\theta}(r,\mathbf{a})\right]

equals
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}]=g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})
, achieved by the threshold rule
x_{\theta}(r,\mathbf{a})=\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}
(ties arbitrary).

Proof.

For any fixed realization
(r,\mathbf{a})
, the quantity
(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)\cdot x
is maximized over
x\in[0,1]
by:

•

x=1
if
r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle>0
,

•

x=0
if
r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle<0
,

•

Any
x\in[0,1]
if
r=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle
(ties).

Thus the pointwise supremum equals
(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}
, achieved by the threshold rule. Taking expectation yields
g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})
.
∎

C.5
Proof of Theorem
5
(Primal–Dual Form)

Proof of Theorem
5
.

We prove:

V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})=\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\right\}=\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\right\}.

Step 1: Lagrangian dual for fixed
\mathbf{w}
.

Fix any
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}
. The inner problem in the primal definition is:

V(\mathbf{w}):=\max_{\{x_{\theta}\}}\left\{\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[r\cdot x_{\theta}]\;\Big|\;\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\mathbf{a}\cdot x_{\theta}]\leq\mathbf{b}\right\}.

This is an infinite-dimensional linear program. Let
\mathcal{Z}:=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
denote the outcome space (reward-resource pairs). Each
x_{\theta}:\mathcal{Z}\to[0,1]
is a Borel-measurable acceptance function in
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{D}_{\theta})
. The objective and constraints are linear in
x_{\theta}
, and the Slater constraint qualification is satisfied since
x_{\theta}\equiv 0
is strictly feasible:
\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\mathbf{a}\cdot 0]=0\prec\mathbf{b}
(componentwise strict inequality holds since
b_{\min}>0
by Assumption
3
). The key observation is that while the primal space
L^{\infty}
is infinite-dimensional, the constraint map
\{x_{\theta}\}\mapsto\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\mathbf{a}\cdot x_{\theta}]
takes values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where the positive cone
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
has non-empty interior. Under this structure, Slater’s condition implies strong duality (see
26
, Chapter 8, or
7
):

V(\mathbf{w})=\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\sup_{\{x_{\theta}\}}\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)\cdot x_{\theta}]\right\}.

By Lemma
14
, the inner supremum equals
\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})
. Hence:

V(\mathbf{w})=\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\right\}.

Step 2: Maximize over
\mathbf{w}
.

By definition,
V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})=\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}V(\mathbf{w})
, so:

V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})=\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\right\}.

Step 3: Minimax swap via Sion’s theorem.

Define
\Phi(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}):=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})
. We verify the conditions for Sion’s minimax theorem:

•

\Delta_{K}
is compact and convex.

•

\mathcal{P}=[0,P_{\max}]^{d}
is compact and convex.

•

For fixed
\mathbf{p}
,
\Phi(\cdot,\mathbf{p})
is linear (hence concave) in
\mathbf{w}
.

•

For fixed
\mathbf{w}
,
\Phi(\mathbf{w},\cdot)
is convex in
\mathbf{p}
because each
g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})=\mathbb{E}[(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}]
is convex (expectation of a convex function in
\mathbf{p}
).

•

\Phi
is continuous and bounded by the boundedness assumptions.

By Sion’s minimax theorem:

\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\Phi(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})=\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}\Phi(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}).

Step 4: Simplify max over simplex.

For fixed
\mathbf{p}
:

\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})=\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}),

since a linear function over the simplex is maximized at an extreme point. Therefore:

V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})=\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\right\}.

∎

C.6
Proof of Theorem
8
(Oracle Upper Bound)

Proof of Theorem
8
.

Let
\pi
be any causal online policy satisfying
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{a}_{t}x_{t}\leq\mathbf{B}
almost surely.

Fix any
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}
. Apply Lemma
13
at each time
t
with
x=x_{t}
:

r_{t}x_{t}\leq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle x_{t}+(r_{t}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle)_{+}.

Summing over
t=1,\ldots,T
:

R_{T}^{\pi}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}r_{t}x_{t}\leq\left\langle\mathbf{p},\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{a}_{t}x_{t}\right\rangle+\sum_{t=1}^{T}(r_{t}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle)_{+}.

By pathwise feasibility:

R_{T}^{\pi}\leq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{B}\rangle+\sum_{t=1}^{T}(r_{t}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle)_{+}.

Taking expectation:

\mathbb{E}[R_{T}^{\pi}]\leq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{B}\rangle+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[(r_{t}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle)_{+}\right].

Condition on
\theta_{t}
. Since
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})\mid(\theta_{t}=\theta)\sim\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
:

\mathbb{E}\left[(r_{t}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle)_{+}\mid\theta_{t}=\theta\right]=g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}).

Model assumption used:
The equality above uses Assumption
1
, which ensures that conditional on
\theta_{t}=\theta
, the pair
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})
is distributed according to
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
.

Thus:

\mathbb{E}\left[(r_{t}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle)_{+}\right]=\sum_{\theta}\Pr(\theta_{t}=\theta)\cdot g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}).

Define the empirical mixture
\bar{w}_{\theta}:=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Pr(\theta_{t}=\theta)
. Then
\bar{\mathbf{w}}\in\Delta_{K}
and:

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[(r_{t}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle)_{+}\right]=\sum_{\theta}\bar{w}_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\leq\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}).

Therefore:

\mathbb{E}[R_{T}^{\pi}]\leq T\left(\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\right).

Minimizing over
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}
and applying Theorem 1:

\mathbb{E}[R_{T}^{\pi}]\leq T\cdot V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}).

∎

C.7
No-Tie Condition and Subgradients

For the regret analysis, we require a technical assumption on online arrivals.

Assumption 15

(No-Tie Condition)
.

For all
\theta\in\Theta
and all
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}
,

\Pr_{(r,\mathbf{a})\sim\mathcal{D}_{\theta}}\big(r=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\big)=0.

See Remark
3
for why we use strict thresholds in the analysis.

Lemma 16

(Subgradients and Tie-Weighted Consumption)
.

Fix any sample
(r,\mathbf{a})
and define
\phi_{\mathbf{p}}(r,\mathbf{a}):=(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}
.
Then
\phi_{\mathbf{p}}
is convex in
\mathbf{p}
and its subdifferential is

\partial_{\mathbf{p}}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}(r,\mathbf{a})=\begin{cases}\{-\mathbf{a}\},&r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle,\\
\{-\lambda\mathbf{a}:\lambda\in[0,1]\},&r=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle,\\
\{\mathbf{0}\},&r<\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle.\end{cases}

Equivalently, for any
\lambda\in[0,1]
, the vector

-\mathbf{a}\Big(\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}+\lambda\mathbf{1}\{r=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}\Big)

is a valid subgradient of
\phi_{\mathbf{p}}(r,\mathbf{a})
.

Now fix
\theta
and time
t
with samples
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(t)=\{(r_{j},\mathbf{a}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N_{\theta}(t)}
and define

\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p}):=\frac{1}{N_{\theta}(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\theta}(t)}(r_{j}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{j}\rangle)_{+}.

For any
\mathbf{p}
and any tie-weight vector
\lambda\in[0,1]^{N_{\theta}(t)}
, define the tie-weighted empirical
consumption

\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda}(\mathbf{p}):=\frac{1}{N_{\theta}(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\theta}(t)}\mathbf{a}_{j}\Big(\mathbf{1}\{r_{j}>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{j}\rangle\}+\lambda_{j}\mathbf{1}\{r_{j}=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{j}\rangle\}\Big).

Then

\partial\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p})=\left\{-\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda}(\mathbf{p}):\lambda\in[0,1]^{N_{\theta}(t)}\right\}.

Moreover, componentwise for every
\mathbf{p}
and
\lambda
,

\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{>,}(\mathbf{p})\;\leq\;\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})\;\leq\;\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\geq,}(\mathbf{p}),

where
\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{>,}(\mathbf{p})
uses the strict indicator and
\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\geq,}(\mathbf{p})
uses
the weak indicator.

Proof.

The pointwise subdifferential formula is standard for the hinge composed with an affine map.
For the empirical average
\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}
, the subdifferential is the average of the pointwise
subdifferentials (finite sum of convex functions), hence it equals the set of averages of admissible
pointwise subgradients; this is exactly the tie-weighted form above. The sandwich inequality holds because
at a tie the contribution is
\lambda_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}
with
\lambda_{j}\in[0,1]
.
∎

C.8
Auxiliary Lemmas for Population Primal–Dual Analysis

Lemma 17

(Upper Box Constraint is Inactive at Minimizers)
.

Assume
0\leq r\leq R_{\max}
a.s. and
b_{\min}>0
. Fix any
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}
and consider the convex function

F_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{p}):=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}),\qquad\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}.

Then every minimizer of
\min_{\mathbf{p}\geq 0}F_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{p})
satisfies
\|\mathbf{p}\|_{\infty}\leq R_{\max}/b_{\min}
.
In particular, if
P_{\max}>R_{\max}/b_{\min}
, then every minimizer of
\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}F_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{p})
lies in the strict interior of the upper box constraints, i.e.,
p_{i}<P_{\max}
for all
i
.

Proof.

Fix coordinate
i
and suppose
p_{i}>R_{\max}/b_{i}
. Then

F_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{p})\geq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle\geq p_{i}b_{i}>R_{\max}.

On the other hand, at
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}
,

F_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{0})=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[r]\leq R_{\max}.

Hence no point with
p_{i}>R_{\max}/b_{i}
can be optimal. Applying this argument to all
i
gives
\|\mathbf{p}\|_{\infty}\leq R_{\max}/b_{\min}
. If
P_{\max}>R_{\max}/b_{\min}
, then
\|\mathbf{p}\|_{\infty}\leq R_{\max}/b_{\min}
implies
\mathbf{p}
cannot satisfy
p_{i}=P_{\max}
for any
i
.
∎

Lemma 18

(Differentiability and Gradient Formula for
g_{\theta}
)
.

Assume Assumption
2
and Assumption
15
(no ties).
Then for each
\theta
,
g_{\theta}
is convex and continuously differentiable on
\mathcal{P}
, and

\nabla g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})=-h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})=-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[\mathbf{a}\,\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}\right].

Consequently, for any
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}
,

\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}).

Proof.

Fix
\theta
and define
\phi(\mathbf{p};r,\mathbf{a}):=(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}
, so
g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\phi(\mathbf{p};r,\mathbf{a})]
.

Step 1 (convexity).

For each fixed
(r,\mathbf{a})
,
\mathbf{p}\mapsto r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle
is affine and
x\mapsto x_{+}
is convex, hence
\mathbf{p}\mapsto\phi(\mathbf{p};r,\mathbf{a})
is convex. Expectation preserves convexity, so
g_{\theta}
is convex.

Step 2 (pointwise derivative).

Fix coordinate
i
and consider the one-sided difference quotient:

D_{t}^{(i)}(r,\mathbf{a}):=\frac{\phi(\mathbf{p}+te_{i};r,\mathbf{a})-\phi(\mathbf{p};r,\mathbf{a})}{t},\qquad t\neq 0.

Because
x\mapsto x_{+}
is 1-Lipschitz and the argument changes by
ta^{(i)}
, we have

|D_{t}^{(i)}(r,\mathbf{a})|\leq a^{(i)}\leq A_{\max}\qquad\text{a.s.}

Moreover, for any
(r,\mathbf{a})
with
r\neq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle
(which holds a.s. by Assumption
15
),
\phi(\cdot;r,\mathbf{a})
is differentiable at
\mathbf{p}
and

\lim_{t\to 0}D_{t}^{(i)}(r,\mathbf{a})=\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\phi(\mathbf{p};r,\mathbf{a})=\begin{cases}-a^{(i)},&r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle,\\
0,&r<\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle.\end{cases}

Equivalently,

\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\phi(\mathbf{p};r,\mathbf{a})=-a^{(i)}\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}\qquad\text{a.s.}

Step 3 (interchange derivative and expectation).

By the uniform bound
|D_{t}^{(i)}(r,\mathbf{a})|\leq A_{\max}
and dominated convergence,

\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})=\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\phi(\mathbf{p};r,\mathbf{a})]=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\phi(\mathbf{p};r,\mathbf{a})\right]=-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[a^{(i)}\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}\right].

Stacking coordinates yields
\nabla g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})=-h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})
.

Step 4 (continuity of the gradient).

Let
\mathbf{p}_{n}\to\mathbf{p}
. Then
\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p}_{n},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}\to\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}
pointwise for all
(r,\mathbf{a})
such that
r\neq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle
; by Assumption
15
this holds a.s. Bounded convergence with
0\leq a^{(i)}\leq A_{\max}
implies
\mathbb{E}[a^{(i)}\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p}_{n},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}]\to\mathbb{E}[a^{(i)}\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}]
, so
\nabla g_{\theta}
is continuous on
\mathcal{P}
.

Finally,
\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{b}+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\nabla g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})
.
∎

Lemma 19

(Nonnegative Dot Product Complementarity is Coordinatewise)
.

If
u,v\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
and
\langle u,v\rangle=0
, then
u_{i}v_{i}=0
for every
i\in[d]
.

Proof.

Each term
u_{i}v_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}u_{i}v_{i}=0
, hence each term must be zero.
∎

C.9
Proof of Theorem
6
(Saddle/KKT Optimality)

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Assume Assumptions
2
–
15
, and assume
P_{\max}>R_{\max}/b_{\min}
so that by Lemma
17
the upper box constraints are inactive at any minimizer of
\mathbf{p}\mapsto L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})
for any fixed
\mathbf{w}
.

We prove the equivalence between:

(S)

(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})
is a saddle point of
L
, i.e.,

L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p})\quad\forall\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K},\forall\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P};

(KKT)

conditions (i)–(iii) in the theorem statement.

(S)
\Rightarrow
(KKT).

Step 1 (Envelope support).

Fix
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
. The map
\mathbf{w}\mapsto L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star})=\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
is linear in
\mathbf{w}
over the simplex. Therefore, any maximizer
\mathbf{w}^{\star}\in\arg\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}}L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star})
must place all its mass on indices attaining the maximum coefficient
g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
:

\mathrm{supp}(\mathbf{w}^{\star})\subseteq A(\mathbf{p}^{\star}):=\arg\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}).

This is condition (i).

Step 2 (First-order optimality for the
\mathbf{p}
-minimization).

Because
(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})
is a saddle,
\mathbf{p}^{\star}\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p})
.
By Lemma
17
,
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
lies in the interior of the upper box constraints, so the effective constraint set is only
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
.

By Lemma
18
,
L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p})
is convex and differentiable in
\mathbf{p}
with
\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p})
. The KKT condition for minimizing a convex differentiable function over
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
is

\mathbf{0}\in\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})+N_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}),

where
N_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
is the normal cone to
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
at
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
.
Equivalently, there exists
v\in N_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
such that

\mathbf{0}=\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})+v.

The normal cone satisfies:
v_{i}=0
if
p_{i}^{\star}>0
, and
v_{i}\leq 0
if
p_{i}^{\star}=0
.
Thus:

p_{i}^{\star}>0\Rightarrow b_{i}-H_{i}(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})=0,\qquad p_{i}^{\star}=0\Rightarrow b_{i}-H_{i}(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\geq 0.

Hence
H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\leq\mathbf{b}
componentwise, which is condition (ii). Also,
b_{i}-H_{i}(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\geq 0
and
p_{i}^{\star}\geq 0
imply
\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\rangle\geq 0
. Moreover, because the coordinatewise implications above include
p_{i}^{\star}(b_{i}-H_{i}(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star}))=0
for each
i
, we obtain

\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\rangle=0,

which is condition (iii).

(KKT)
\Rightarrow
(S).

Assume (i)–(iii).

Step 1 (
\mathbf{w}^{\star}
is a best response to
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
).

For fixed
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
,
\mathbf{w}\mapsto L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star})
is linear over
\Delta_{K}
, and its maximum value equals
\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
.
Condition (i) implies
\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}^{\star}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})=\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
, hence for all
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}
,

L(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star}).

Step 2 (
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
is a best response to
\mathbf{w}^{\star}
).

By Lemma
18
,
L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p})
is convex differentiable in
\mathbf{p}
.
Since (ii) gives
\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
and (iii) gives
\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\rangle=0
, Lemma
19
implies
p_{i}^{\star}(b_{i}-H_{i}(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star}))=0
for each
i
.
Equivalently:

p_{i}^{\star}>0\Rightarrow b_{i}-H_{i}(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})=0,\qquad p_{i}^{\star}=0\Rightarrow b_{i}-H_{i}(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\geq 0.

This is exactly the KKT condition
\mathbf{0}\in\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})+N_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
for minimizing
L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\cdot)
over
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
(upper box inactive by our choice of
P_{\max}
).
Therefore
\mathbf{p}^{\star}\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p})
, and for all
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}
,

L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}).

Combining Steps 1 and 2 yields the saddle inequalities, so
(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})
is a saddle point.

Primal optimality of threshold admission and value identity.

Let
x(r,\mathbf{a}):=\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}
and define the achieved (fluid) reward

U(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star}):=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}^{\star}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[r\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}\right].

Using the identity
r\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}=\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{a}\rangle\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}+(r-\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}
, we obtain

U(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})=\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}^{\star}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}).

Hence

L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})=\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}^{\star}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})=U(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})+\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\mathbf{b}-H(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\rangle.

By condition (iii), the last inner product is zero, so
U(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})=L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})
.
Condition (ii) implies the threshold rule is feasible for the primal constraint.
Finally, since
(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})
is a saddle point,
L(\mathbf{w}^{\star},\mathbf{p}^{\star})
equals the minimax value
V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})
by Theorem
5
. Therefore the mixture
\mathbf{w}^{\star}
together with threshold admission attains
V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})
and is primal-optimal for (
1
).
∎

D.1
Concentration for Surplus Function

Lemma 20

(Uniform Concentration for
g_{\theta}
(Fixed
n
))
.

Fix
\theta
and
n\geq 1
. Let
\{(r_{i},\mathbf{a}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
be i.i.d. samples from
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
and define

\widehat{g}_{\theta,n}(\mathbf{p}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r_{i}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{i}\rangle)_{+},\qquad g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}):=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}].

There exist absolute constants
c_{g},c_{0}>0
such that for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least

1-\delta
,

\sup_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\big|\widehat{g}_{\theta,n}(\mathbf{p})-g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\big|\;\leq\;c_{g}R_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{d\log\!\Big(\frac{c_{0}dP_{\max}A_{\max}n}{R_{\max}}\Big)+\log(2/\delta)}{n}}.

Proof.

Step 1 (Pointwise Hoeffding).

For fixed
\mathbf{p}
, the random variable
(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}
lies in
[0,R_{\max}]
.
Thus by Hoeffding,

\Pr\!\left(\big|\widehat{g}_{\theta,n}(\mathbf{p})-g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\big|>\epsilon\right)\leq 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{2n\epsilon^{2}}{R_{\max}^{2}}\right).

Step 2 (Lipschitzness in
\mathbf{p}
).

For any
\mathbf{p},\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathcal{P}
and any
(r,\mathbf{a})
,

\big|(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}-(r-\langle\boldsymbol{q},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}\big|\leq|\langle\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{q},\mathbf{a}\rangle|\leq A_{\max}\|\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{q}\|_{1},

using
\|\mathbf{a}\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\max}
. Hence both
\widehat{g}_{\theta,n}
and
g_{\theta}
are

A_{\max}
-Lipschitz in
\ell_{1}
.

Step 3 (
\ell_{1}
-net + union bound).

Let
\eta>0
and let
\mathcal{N}
be an
\ell_{1}
-net of
\mathcal{P}=[0,P_{\max}]^{d}
with radius
\eta
.
A standard volume argument gives
|\mathcal{N}|\leq\big(\frac{c_{0}dP_{\max}}{\eta}\big)^{d}

for an absolute constant
c_{0}>0
.

For any
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}
, pick
\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathcal{N}
with
\|\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{q}\|_{1}\leq\eta
. By Lipschitzness,

\big|\widehat{g}_{\theta,n}(\mathbf{p})-g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\big|\leq\big|\widehat{g}_{\theta,n}(\boldsymbol{q})-g_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{q})\big|+2A_{\max}\eta.

Set
\eta=\epsilon/(4A_{\max})
. Then

\Pr\!\left(\sup_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\big|\widehat{g}_{\theta,n}(\mathbf{p})-g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\big|>\epsilon\right)\leq\Pr\!\left(\max_{\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathcal{N}}\big|\widehat{g}_{\theta,n}(\boldsymbol{q})-g_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{q})\big|>\epsilon/2\right)

\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathcal{N}}\Pr\!\left(\big|\widehat{g}_{\theta,n}(\boldsymbol{q})-g_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{q})\big|>\epsilon/2\right)\leq 2|\mathcal{N}|\exp\!\left(-\frac{2n(\epsilon/2)^{2}}{R_{\max}^{2}}\right)=2|\mathcal{N}|\exp\!\left(-\frac{n\epsilon^{2}}{2R_{\max}^{2}}\right).

Substitute
|\mathcal{N}|\leq\big(\frac{4c_{0}dP_{\max}A_{\max}}{\epsilon}\big)^{d}
and solve for
\epsilon

so that the RHS is at most
\delta
. This yields the stated bound.

\epsilon=R_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{d\log\left(\left(\frac{4dP_{max}A_{max}}{R_{max}}\right)^{2}n\right)+\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\right)}{n}}

∎

D.2
Concentration for Threshold Consumption

The threshold consumption function involves weighted indicators
a^{(i)}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}
, which is not a pure indicator class. We use pseudo-dimension to obtain uniform concentration.

Lemma 22

(Pseudo-Dimension of Weighted Threshold Consumption)
.

Fix a coordinate
i\in[d]
and define the function class

\mathcal{F}_{i}:=\left\{f_{\mathbf{p}}(r,\mathbf{a}):=a^{(i)}\,\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}:\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}\right\}.

Then
\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\leq d+2
.

Proof.

Recall the definition of pseudo-dimension:
\mathcal{F}_{i}
pseudo-shatters
n
points
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
if there exist thresholds
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
such that for every labeling
S\subseteq[n]
there exists
f\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
with
f(z_{j})>s_{j}
iff
j\in S
.

Take any candidate set
\{z_{j}=(r_{j},\mathbf{a}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}
and thresholds
\{s_{j}\}
.

If
s_{j}<0
, then
f_{\mathbf{p}}(z_{j})\geq 0>s_{j}
for all
\mathbf{p}
, so the label of
j
cannot vary across
S
. If
s_{j}\geq a_{j}^{(i)}
, then
f_{\mathbf{p}}(z_{j})\leq a_{j}^{(i)}\leq s_{j}
for all
\mathbf{p}
, so again the label cannot vary. Therefore, for a point to be label-flexible under pseudo-shattering, it must satisfy
0\leq s_{j}<a_{j}^{(i)}
.

For such
j
, we have:

f_{\mathbf{p}}(z_{j})>s_{j}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_{j}^{(i)}\,\mathbf{1}\{r_{j}>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{j}\rangle\}>s_{j}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbf{1}\{r_{j}>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}_{j}\rangle\}=1.

Thus, on the subset of flexible points, pseudo-shattering by
\mathcal{F}_{i}
reduces exactly to shattering by the halfspace indicator class

\mathcal{H}:=\left\{(r,\mathbf{a})\mapsto\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}:\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}\right\},

which is a class of halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and satisfies
\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{H})\leq d+2
. Therefore no more than
d+2
flexible points can be shattered, and hence
\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\leq d+2
.
∎

Theorem 23

(Uniform Deviation Bound for Pseudo-Dimension Classes)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a class of functions mapping into
[0,1]
with pseudo-dimension
v
. Let

be i.i.d. samples from any distribution. Then for any

for a universal constant
c>0
.

This is a standard result in statistical learning theory (see, e.g.,
28
).

Appendix E
Regret Proof for Theorem 3

We prove the main theorem following a five-step decomposition.

E.1
Setup

Let
\varepsilon=\sqrt{\log T/T}
and
\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}=(1-\varepsilon)\mathbf{b}

Define the

\widehat{V}_{t}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{w}):=\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\big(\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p})+\beta_{g,\theta}(t)\big)\right\}.

Fix
\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}:=T^{-2}
. Let
\beta_{g,\theta}(t)
be any sequence satisfying

on

For concreteness, by Corollary

with any fixed

Similarly, by Corollary

one may take

\beta_{h,\theta}(t):=c_{h}^{\prime}A_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{(d+2)\log(eT)+\log\!\Big(\frac{4KTd}{\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}}\Big)}{N_{\theta}(t)\vee 1}}.

E.2
Step 1: Good Event

On the good event
\mathcal{E}
(probability
\geq 1-O(1/T)

•

\sup_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}|\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p})-g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})|\leq\beta_{g,\theta}(t)

Lemma 26

(Mixture-Weight Bridge)
.

Since
\theta_{t}\sim\mathbf{w}_{t}
and
\beta_{\theta}(t)
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable,

\mathbb{E}[\beta_{\theta_{t}}(t)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{\theta}(t).

Therefore, for any realization,

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\beta_{\theta_{t}}(t)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{\theta}(t)\right],

and standard concentration bounds apply to the realized arm sequence
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}

Proof.

Fix any round
t

\mathbf{w}_{t}=(w_{t,1},\ldots,w_{t,K})
is determined by the history up to round

(i.e.,
\mathbf{w}_{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
) and
\theta_{t}
is drawn from the categorical distribution with weights
\mathbf{w}_{t}
, we have by definition:

\mathbb{E}[\beta_{\theta_{t}}(t)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\sum_{\theta=1}^{K}\Pr(\theta_{t}=\theta\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})\cdot\beta_{\theta}(t)=\sum_{\theta=1}^{K}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{\theta}(t).

Summing over
t
and taking total expectation via the tower property yields the result. This lemma bridges the gap between our algorithm’s mixture sampling and classical chosen-arm concentration lemmas, justifying why bounds on
\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{\theta}(t)
translate to bounds on
\sum_{t}\beta_{\theta_{t}}(t)
.
∎

and taking
\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}
gives the result.
∎

Let
\mathbf{w}^{\star}\in\argmax_{\mathbf{w}}V(\mathbf{w})
so that
V(\mathbf{w}^{\star})=V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})
.

Lemma 28

(Per-Round Mixture-Value Gap)
.

On
\mathcal{E}
, for all
t\leq T
:

V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})-V(\mathbf{w}_{t})\leq 2\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{g,\theta}(t).

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{w}_{t}
maximizes
\widehat{V}_{t}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{w})
over
\Delta_{K}
:

V(\mathbf{w}^{\star})\leq\widehat{V}_{t}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{w}^{\star})\leq\widehat{V}_{t}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t}),

where the first inequality uses Lemma
27
. By the upper bound in Lemma
27
:

\widehat{V}_{t}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\leq V(\mathbf{w}_{t})+2\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{g,\theta}(t).

Combining gives the result.
∎

E.4
Step 3: Admission-Price Error and KKT Feasibility

Define the unconstrained threshold acceptance
\tilde{x}_{t}:=\mathbf{1}\{r_{t}>\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle\}
and reward
\tilde{R}_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}r_{t}\tilde{x}_{t}
.

For any mixture
\mathbf{w}
and price
\mathbf{p}
, define (using strict threshold
>
per our convention):

\displaystyle U(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[r\cdot\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}],

\displaystyle H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}),

\displaystyle\widehat{H}_{t}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p}).

Lemma 29

(Primal–Dual Inequality for Threshold Rules)
.

For any mixture
\mathbf{w}
and price
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
:

V(\mathbf{w})-U(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})\leq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}-H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})\rangle.

Proof.

Fix any mixture
\mathbf{w}
and price
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
.

Step 1 (dual upper bound on
V(\mathbf{w})
).

By the dual representation of
V(\mathbf{w})
(Theorem 1 in the main paper / Theorem
5
),
for any fixed
\mathbf{p}
we have

V(\mathbf{w})=\min_{\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\boldsymbol{q},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{q})\right\}\leq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}).

Step 2 (identity for the threshold value).

For strict-threshold admission at price
\mathbf{p}
,

r\,\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\,\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}+(r-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle)_{+}.

Taking expectation under
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
and summing with weights
w_{\theta}
yields

U(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[r\,\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}\right]=\langle\mathbf{p},H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}),

where
H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})
is the strict-threshold expected consumption.

Step 3 (combine).

Subtract the expression for
U(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})
from the dual upper bound on
V(\mathbf{w})
:

V(\mathbf{w})-U(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})\leq\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})-\left(\langle\mathbf{p},H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\right)=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}-H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})\rangle.

∎

The following lemma establishes drift control via KKT conditions using tie-weighted consumption.

Lemma 30

(KKT Implies Tie-Weighted Empirical Complementarity)
.

Fix a mixture
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{K}
and time
t
with
N_{\theta}(t)\geq 1
for all
\theta
.
Consider the convex problem

\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\rangle+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p})\right\}.

Assume
P_{\max}>R_{\max}/b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{\min}
so that the upper box constraint is non-binding at minimizers
(Corollary
11
).
Let
\mathbf{p}^{\star}(\mathbf{w})
be any minimizer.
Then there exist tie-weight vectors
\lambda_{\theta}\in[0,1]^{N_{\theta}(t)}
(one per configuration) such that,
with the tie-weighted empirical consumptions
\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda_{\theta}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
from
Lemma
16
, the mixture tie-weighted empirical consumption

\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star}):=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\,\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda_{\theta}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})

satisfies

\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\leq\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\quad\text{(componentwise)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle\mathbf{p}^{\star},\,\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}-\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star})\rangle=0.

Proof.

By optimality of
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
for the constrained convex problem with effective domain
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}

(upper box non-binding), the KKT condition is

\mathbf{0}\in\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\,\partial\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})+N_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}).

Thus there exist subgradients
s_{\theta}\in\partial\widehat{g}_{\theta,t}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
and a normal-cone vector

v\in N_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
such that

\mathbf{0}=\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}+\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}s_{\theta}+v.

By Lemma
16
, each
s_{\theta}
can be written as

s_{\theta}=-\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda_{\theta}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
for some tie-weight vector
\lambda_{\theta}
.
Define
\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star}):=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda_{\theta}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
.
Then the KKT condition becomes

\mathbf{0}=\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}-\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star})+v,\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad v=\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}^{\star})-\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}.

The normal cone
N_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
satisfies:

v_{i}=0
if
p_{i}^{\star}>0
and
v_{i}\leq 0
if
p_{i}^{\star}=0
.
Hence if
p_{i}^{\star}>0
then
\widehat{H}_{t,i}^{\mathrm{kkt}}=b_{i}^{\mathrm{safe}}
, and if
p_{i}^{\star}=0
then

\widehat{H}_{t,i}^{\mathrm{kkt}}\leq b_{i}^{\mathrm{safe}}
. This yields the componentwise feasibility.
The complementarity identity follows because for every coordinate
i
,
either
p_{i}^{\star}=0
or
b_{i}^{\mathrm{safe}}-\widehat{H}_{t,i}^{\mathrm{kkt}}=0
, hence

\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\star}(b_{i}^{\mathrm{safe}}-\widehat{H}_{t,i}^{\mathrm{kkt}})=0
.
∎

Lemma 31

(Consumption Drift Bound)
.

On the good event
\mathcal{E}
(defined in Section
D
with Lemma
24

holding for both strict and weak thresholds), for all
t
,

H(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\leq\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}+\Big(\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t)\Big)\mathbf{1}_{d},

where
H(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}):=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})
is the population consumption under the strict threshold
(which equals the weak threshold by Assumption
15
).

Proof.

Fix
t
and apply Lemma
30
with
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{w}_{t}
and
\mathbf{p}^{\star}=\mathbf{p}_{t}
to obtain

\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\leq\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}
componentwise.
For each
\theta
, the tie-weighted empirical consumption
\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda_{\theta}}(\mathbf{p}_{t})

satisfies

\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{>,}(\mathbf{p}_{t})\leq\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda_{\theta}}(\mathbf{p}_{t})\leq\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\geq,}(\mathbf{p}_{t})\quad\text{(componentwise)}

by Lemma
16
. On
\mathcal{E}
, Lemma
24
implies both

\|\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{>,}(\mathbf{p}_{t})-h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}_{t})\|_{\infty}\leq\beta_{h,\theta}(t)
and

\|\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\geq,}(\mathbf{p}_{t})-h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}_{t})\|_{\infty}\leq\beta_{h,\theta}(t)
.
Since
\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda_{\theta}}(\mathbf{p}_{t})
lies between the strict and weak empirical quantities
coordinatewise and
h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}_{t})
equals the strict/weak population expectation, it follows that

\|\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda_{\theta}}(\mathbf{p}_{t})-h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}_{t})\|_{\infty}\leq\beta_{h,\theta}(t)
as well.
Therefore,

H(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})=\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}h_{\theta}(\mathbf{p}_{t})\leq\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\Big(\widehat{h}_{\theta,t}^{\lambda_{\theta}}(\mathbf{p}_{t})+\beta_{h,\theta}(t)\mathbf{1}_{d}\Big)=\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})+\Big(\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t)\Big)\mathbf{1}_{d}\leq\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}+\Big(\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t)\Big)\mathbf{1}_{d}.

∎

Lemma 32

(Price-Error Bound)
.

On the good event
\mathcal{E}
, for all
t\leq T
,

V(\mathbf{w}_{t})-U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\leq dP_{\max}\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t),

where
U(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}):=\sum_{\theta}w_{\theta}\,\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[r\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}]
.

Proof.

By Lemma
29
,

V(\mathbf{w}_{t})-U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\leq\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}-H(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\rangle.

Apply Lemma
30
(with
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{w}_{t}
) to obtain a tie-weighted empirical consumption

\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})
satisfying

\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}-\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\rangle=0
.
Hence

\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}-H(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\rangle=\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}-\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\rangle+\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})-H(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\rangle=\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})-H(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\rangle.

By Hölder and
\|\mathbf{p}_{t}\|_{1}\leq dP_{\max}
,

\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})-H(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\rangle\leq\|\mathbf{p}_{t}\|_{1}\cdot\|\widehat{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{kkt}}(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})-H(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\|_{\infty}\leq dP_{\max}\cdot\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t),

where the last inequality uses the same sandwich argument as in Lemma
31
.
∎

E.5
Step 4: Budget-Feasibility Loss

The real algorithm uses budget checks, producing reward
R_{T}\leq\tilde{R}_{T}
. We bound
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T}]
using a clean martingale-maximum approach.

Setup.
Let
\tilde{x}_{t}:=\mathbf{1}\{r_{t}>\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle\}
be the unconstrained threshold decision (strict inequality by Assumption
4
), and let
\tilde{R}_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}r_{t}\tilde{x}_{t}
. For each resource coordinate
i
, define
\tilde{C}_{t}^{(i)}:=\sum_{s=1}^{t}a_{s}^{(i)}\tilde{x}_{s}
and the violation time
\tau_{i}:=\inf\{t\geq 1:\tilde{C}_{t}^{(i)}>B_{i}\}
, with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
. Let
\tau:=\min_{i\in[d]}\tau_{i}
.

Lemma 33

(Budget Loss Controlled by Violation Times)
.

Almost surely,

\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T}\leq R_{\max}(T-\tau+1)_{+}\leq R_{\max}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(T-\tau_{i}+1)_{+}.

Proof.

If no coordinate violates, then the budget check never rejects an unconstrained acceptance, so
\tilde{R}_{T}=R_{T}
. Otherwise, after the first violation time
\tau
, the real algorithm may lose at most
R_{\max}
reward per remaining period, giving the first inequality. The second inequality uses
\max_{i}z_{i}\leq\sum_{i}z_{i}
with
z_{i}=(T-\tau_{i}+1)_{+}
.
∎

Martingale structure.
For coordinate
i
, let
Y_{t,i}:=a_{t}^{(i)}\tilde{x}_{t}\in[0,A_{\max}]
. Since
\theta_{t}\sim\mathbf{w}_{t}
and
(r_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})\sim\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{t}}
:

\mathbb{E}[Y_{t,i}|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[a^{(i)}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}\right]=H^{(i)}(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t}).

Define the martingale
M_{t,i}:=\sum_{s=1}^{t}(Y_{s,i}-\mathbb{E}[Y_{s,i}|\mathcal{F}_{s-1}])
with bounded increments
|M_{t,i}-M_{t-1,i}|\leq A_{\max}
.

Lemma 34

(Violation Time Bounded by Martingale Maximum and Drift)
.

Assume the drift bound holds:
\mathbb{E}[Y_{t,i}|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{i}+\Delta_{t}
for all
t
, where
\Delta_{t}\geq 0
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable. Let
\Delta_{1:T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Delta_{t}
. Then for the violation time
\tau_{i}
,

(T-\tau_{i}+1)_{+}\leq 1+\frac{\sup_{t\leq T}(M_{t,i})_{+}+\Delta_{1:T}}{b_{i}}.

Proof.

If
\tau_{i}=\infty
the left side is 0 and the inequality is trivial. Otherwise, by definition
\tilde{C}_{\tau_{i}}^{(i)}>B_{i}=Tb_{i}
. By the martingale decomposition,

\tilde{C}_{\tau_{i}}^{(i)}=\sum_{s=1}^{\tau_{i}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{s,i}|\mathcal{F}_{s-1}]+M_{\tau_{i},i}\leq\tau_{i}b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{i}+\sum_{s=1}^{\tau_{i}}\Delta_{s}+M_{\tau_{i},i}\leq\tau_{i}(1-\varepsilon)b_{i}+\Delta_{1:T}+M_{\tau_{i},i}.

Thus

M_{\tau_{i},i}>Tb_{i}-\tau_{i}(1-\varepsilon)b_{i}-\Delta_{1:T}=(T-\tau_{i})b_{i}+\varepsilon\tau_{i}b_{i}-\Delta_{1:T}\geq(T-\tau_{i})b_{i}-\Delta_{1:T}.

Rearranging gives

T-\tau_{i}<\frac{(M_{\tau_{i},i})_{+}+\Delta_{1:T}}{b_{i}}\leq\frac{\sup_{t\leq T}(M_{t,i})_{+}+\Delta_{1:T}}{b_{i}}.

Adding 1 yields the claim.
∎

Lemma 35

(Expected Maximum of Bounded-Increment Martingale)
.

Let
(M_{t})_{t=0}^{T}
be a martingale with
M_{0}=0
and increments bounded as
|M_{t}-M_{t-1}|\leq A_{\max}
almost surely. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}(M_{t})_{+}\right]\leq A_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{\pi T}{2}}.

Proof.

By Azuma–Hoeffding,

\Pr\left(\sup_{t\leq T}M_{t}\geq u\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{2TA_{\max}^{2}}\right)\quad\text{for all }u\geq 0.

(One may prove this by applying Azuma to the stopped process at the first hitting time of level
u
.)

Integrate the tail bound:

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}(M_{t})_{+}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Pr\left(\sup_{t\leq T}M_{t}\geq u\right)du\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{2TA_{\max}^{2}}\right)du=A_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{\pi T}{2}}.

∎

Corollary 36

(Expected Feasibility Loss Bound)
.

Under the drift condition in Lemma
34
,

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T}]\leq R_{\max}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}[(T-\tau_{i}+1)_{+}]\leq R_{\max}\left(d+\frac{d}{b_{\min}}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{1:T}]+\frac{dA_{\max}}{b_{\min}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi T}{2}}\right).

Proof.

Combine Lemma
33
and Lemma
34
, take expectations, apply Lemma
35
, and use
b_{i}\geq b_{\min}
.
∎

Drift bound verification.
By Lemma
31
, on the good event
\mathcal{E}
:

H(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\leq\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}+\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{d},

so
\mathbb{E}[Y_{t,i}|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq b^{\mathrm{safe}}_{i}+\Delta_{t}
with
\Delta_{t}:=\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t)
. By Corollary
36
:

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T}|\mathcal{E}]\leq R_{\max}\left(d+\frac{d}{b_{\min}}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{1:T}|\mathcal{E}]+\frac{dA_{\max}}{b_{\min}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi T}{2}}\right).

E.6
Step 5: Summing Confidence Radii and Slack Loss

Lemma 37

(Summation of
1/\sqrt{N}
)
.

Let
\theta_{t}
be the arm pulled at time
t
. Then pathwise:

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\theta_{t}}(t)\vee 1}}\leq 2\sum_{\theta=1}^{K}\sqrt{N_{\theta}(T)\vee 1}\leq 2\sqrt{K(T+K)}.

Proof.

For each
\theta
, the
j
-th pull contributes
1/\sqrt{j}
;
\sum_{j=1}^{n}1/\sqrt{j}\leq 1+\int_{1}^{n}s^{-1/2}ds\leq 2\sqrt{n}
. Then apply Cauchy–Schwarz:
\sum_{\theta}\sqrt{N_{\theta}}\leq\sqrt{K\sum_{\theta}N_{\theta}}=\sqrt{KT}
.
∎

Since
\beta_{g}(n),\beta_{h}(n)=O(\sqrt{d\log T/n})
, Lemma
37
implies:

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{g,\theta}(t)=O\left(\alpha\,c_{g}R_{\max}\sqrt{KT\cdot d\log T}\right),

and similarly:

\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{1:T}]=O\left(A_{\max}\sqrt{KT\cdot d\log T}\right).

Lemma 38

(Lipschitzness and Monotonicity in the Budget)
.

For any
\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
,

\big|V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})-V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\prime})\big|\leq P_{\max}\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\|_{1}.

Moreover,
V^{\mathrm{mix}}
is monotone: if
\mathbf{b}\geq\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
componentwise, then

V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})\geq V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\prime})
.
In particular, with
\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}=(1-\varepsilon)\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{b}
,

0\leq T\big(V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})-V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})\big)\leq P_{\max}\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\|_{1}T=P_{\max}\|\mathbf{b}\|_{1}\,\varepsilon T.

Proof.

Using the dual form,

V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})=\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\}.

For any fixed
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}
,

\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})-\big(\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\rangle+\max_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\mathbf{p})\big)=\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\rangle,

hence

V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})-V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\prime})\leq\sup_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\rangle\leq P_{\max}\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\|_{1}
.
Swap
\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
to get the absolute-value bound.
Monotonicity follows since
\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{b}\rangle
is monotone in
\mathbf{b}
and the minimum over
\mathbf{p}

preserves monotonicity.
∎

E.7
Combining All Pieces (Unconditional Expectation; No Conditioning on
\mathcal{E}
)

We now combine the previous steps in a way that is fully rigorous: we
never
condition on the global
good event
\mathcal{E}
(which depends on the entire trajectory), and instead split expectations using
indicators
1_{\mathcal{E}}
and
1_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}
.

Warm-start definition.

For warm-start rounds
t\leq K
, the algorithm sets
x_{t}=0
(pure observation).
We define
\tilde{x}_{t}:=0
and
U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t}):=0
for
t\leq K
, as saddle-point
outputs
(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})
are only computed for
t\geq K+1
. Accordingly, all sums
involving
U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})
or
\tilde{x}_{t}
below are taken over
t=K+1,\dots,T
.

Warm start loss.

During rounds
t\leq K
, the algorithm forces
x_{t}=0
, hence it can lose at most
R_{\max}
reward per round
relative to any benchmark. Therefore the warm start contributes at most
KR_{\max}
to regret.

Step A: relate
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{R}_{T}]
to
U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})
.

For
t>K
, recall the unconstrained strict-threshold decision
\tilde{x}_{t}:=\mathbf{1}\{r_{t}>\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t}\rangle\}
;
for
t\leq K
, we set
\tilde{x}_{t}:=0
by convention. Define
\tilde{R}_{T}:=\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}r_{t}\tilde{x}_{t}
.
Because
\mathbf{w}_{t}
and
\mathbf{p}_{t}
are
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable and
\theta_{t}\sim\mathbf{w}_{t}
, for
t>K
we have

\mathbb{E}[r_{t}\tilde{x}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\,\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[r\,\mathbf{1}\{r>\langle\mathbf{p}_{t},\mathbf{a}\rangle\}\right]=U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t}).

Taking total expectation and summing over
t=K+1,\ldots,T
(tower property) gives

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{R}_{T}]=\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\right].

Hence

TV^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})-\mathbb{E}[\tilde{R}_{T}]=KV^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})+\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})-U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\right].

Step B: per-round expected gap on
\mathcal{E}
.

On the good event
\mathcal{E}
, Lemmas
28
and
32
imply for every round
t>K
:

V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})-U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})=\big(V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})-V(\mathbf{w}_{t})\big)+\big(V(\mathbf{w}_{t})-U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\big)\leq 2\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{g,\theta}(t)+dP_{\max}\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t).

Multiplying by
1_{\mathcal{E}}
and using nonnegativity of the RHS yields the unconditional bound

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\big(V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})-U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\big)1_{\mathcal{E}}\right]\leq 2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{g,\theta}(t)\right]+dP_{\max}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t)\right].

On
\mathcal{E}^{c}
, we use the crude bound
0\leq U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\leq R_{\max}
and

0\leq V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})\leq R_{\max}
, hence

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\big(V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})-U(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})\big)1_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}\right]\leq R_{\max}\Pr(\mathcal{E}^{c}).

Summing over
t
and adding the warm start loss gives

\displaystyle TV^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})-\mathbb{E}[\tilde{R}_{T}]

\displaystyle\leq KR_{\max}+2\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{g,\theta}(t)\right]+dP_{\max}\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t)\right]+TR_{\max}\Pr(\mathcal{E}^{c}).

Step C: feasibility loss
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T}]
.

We split

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T}]=\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T})1_{\mathcal{E}}]+\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T})1_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}].

The bad-event term satisfies
\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T})1_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}]\leq TR_{\max}\Pr(\mathcal{E}^{c})
.

On
\mathcal{E}
, the drift condition in Lemma
34
holds with

\Delta_{t}:=\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t)
by Lemma
31
, so Lemmas

33
–
34
imply (for each coordinate
i
)

(T-\tau_{i}+1)_{+}\,1_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\left(1+\frac{\sup_{t\leq T}(M_{t,i})_{+}+\Delta_{1:T}}{b_{i}}\right)1_{\mathcal{E}}.

Taking expectations and using
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t\leq T}(M_{t,i})_{+}\,1_{\mathcal{E}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t\leq T}(M_{t,i})_{+}]

together with Lemma
35
gives

\mathbb{E}[(T-\tau_{i}+1)_{+}\,1_{\mathcal{E}}]\leq 1+\frac{A_{\max}\sqrt{\pi T/2}}{b_{i}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{1:T}]}{b_{i}}.

Combining with Lemma
33
and
b_{i}\geq b_{\min}
yields

\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T})1_{\mathcal{E}}]\leq R_{\max}\left(d+\frac{dA_{\max}}{b_{\min}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi T}{2}}+\frac{d}{b_{\min}}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{1:T}]\right).

Therefore,

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{R}_{T}-R_{T}]\leq R_{\max}\left(d+\frac{dA_{\max}}{b_{\min}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi T}{2}}+\frac{d}{b_{\min}}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{1:T}]\right)+TR_{\max}\Pr(\mathcal{E}^{c}).

Step D: slack loss.

By Lemma
38
,

0\leq T\big(V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})-V^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}})\big)\leq P_{\max}\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{safe}}\|_{1}T=P_{\max}\|\mathbf{b}\|_{1}\,\sqrt{T\log T}.

Step E: bound the confidence sums.

Using Lemma
26
and Lemma
37
, and the definitions of
\beta_{g,\theta}(t)

and
\beta_{h,\theta}(t)
(both scaling as
O(1/\sqrt{N_{\theta}(t)})
), we obtain

\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{g,\theta}(t)\right]=\tilde{O}\!\left(\sqrt{KT\cdot d}\right),\qquad\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\theta}w_{t,\theta}\beta_{h,\theta}(t)\right]=\tilde{O}\!\left(\sqrt{KT\cdot d}\right),

and similarly
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{1:T}]=\tilde{O}(\sqrt{KT\cdot d})
.

Step F: bad event probability.

By construction of
\mathcal{E}
we have
\Pr(\mathcal{E}^{c})\leq\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}=T^{-2}
, hence the total
bad-event contribution is at most
O(R_{\max}/T)
.

Conclusion.

Combining Steps B–F and recalling
R_{T}\leq\tilde{R}_{T}
yields

\mathrm{Reg}^{\mathrm{mix}}(T)=TV^{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbf{b})-\mathbb{E}[R_{T}]\leq C_{1}\,\alpha\,\sqrt{KT\cdot d\log T}+C_{2}\sqrt{T\log T}+KR_{\max},

for constants
C_{1},C_{2}
depending only on
(d,R_{\max},A_{\max},P_{\max},b_{\min},\|\mathbf{b}\|_{1})
.
This completes the proof of Theorem 3. ∎

F.2
Epoch/Doubling Schedule

For computational efficiency, one can update
(\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})
only when some
N_{\theta}
doubles (i.e., at times
\tau\in\{1,2,4,8,\ldots\}
in
N_{\theta}
). This reduces the number of saddle problem solves from
T
to
O(K\log T)
while preserving the regret bound up to constants.

Rationale
: The confidence radius
\beta_{\theta}(t)\propto 1/\sqrt{N_{\theta}}
changes by at most a factor of
\sqrt{2}
between doubling updates, so the regret analysis remains valid with adjusted constants.

The exploration parameter

scales the confidence radius:

\alpha=0

\alpha=0.1
is the standard UCB scaling.

9
in the main paper) hold for
\alpha\geq 1
; values
\alpha<1
are heuristics that often improve empirical performance but are not covered by the high-probability analysis.

Appendix H
Broader Impact

Our framework for budgeted admission control has both beneficial applications and potential risks.

Positive impacts.

•

Energy efficiency
: Optimizing resource allocation under carbon budgets can reduce infrastructure emissions.

•

Cost reduction
: Improved utilization reduces costs, democratizing compute access.

•

Transparency
: Dual prices provide interpretable rejection signals, unlike cyan-box heuristics.

Risks and limitations.

•

Fairness
: Reward-maximizing admission may disadvantage low-priority users; fairness constraints should supplement efficiency objectives.

•

Strategic behavior
: Observable rejection patterns may incentivize priority inflation.

•

Information leakage
: Publishing dual prices reveals system scarcity.

Recommendations for deployment.

1.

Incorporate fairness metrics alongside efficiency.

2.

Provide clear explanations for rejections.

3.

Monitor for strategic behavior.

4.

Combine with absolute resource limits.

Local-in-time strong solvability of Navier–Stokes type variational inequalities by Rothe’s method Thanks: This work was supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research(C), Grant Number 24K06860.
Takahito Kashiwabara

Abstract
. We consider parabolic variational inequalities in a Hilbert space V V , which have a non-monotone nonlinearity of Navier–Stokes type represented by a bilinear operator B : V × V → V ′ B:V\times V\to V^{\prime} and a monotone type nonlinearity described by a convex, proper, and lower-semicontinuous functional φ : V → ( − ∞ , + ∞ ] \varphi:V\to(-\infty,+\infty] . Existence and uniqueness of a local-in-time strong solution in a maximal- L 2 L^{2} -regularity class and in a Kiselev–Ladyzhenskaya class are proved by discretization in time (also known as Rothe’s method), provided that a corresponding stationary Stokes problem admits a regularity structure better than V V (which is typically H 2 H^{2} -regularity in case of the Navier–Stokes equations). Since we do not assume the cancelation property ⟨ B ⁡ ( u , v ) , v ⟩ = 0 \left<B(u,v),v\right>=0 , in applications we may allow for broader boundary conditions than those treated by the existing literature.

1.
Introduction

Let
V\subset H=H^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}
be a Gelfand triple of Hilbert spaces, with their norms
\|\cdot\|_{H}
and
\|\cdot\|_{V}
, in which the embeddings are continuous and dense.
We assume that the first embedding
V\hookrightarrow H
is compact, and denote the inner product of
H
and the duality pairing between
V^{\prime}
and
V
by
(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
, respectively.

Let
a:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded and coercive bilinear form, i.e.,
a(u,u)\geq\alpha\|u\|_{V}^{2}
for all
u\in V
and some
\alpha>0
.
Since the norm of
V
is equivalent to
\sqrt{a(u,u)}
, we redefine it as
\|u\|_{V}:=\sqrt{a(u,u)}
in what follows.
It can be identified with a bounded linear operator
A:V\to V^{\prime};\;u\mapsto(v\mapsto a(u,v))
.

We introduce a “Navier–Stokes type nonlinearity” as a bounded bilinear operator
B:V\times V\to V^{\prime}
, which satisfies

(1.1)

|\left<B(u,v),w\right>|\leq C_{B}\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}\|w\|_{V}\qquad\forall u,v,w\in V

for some constant
C_{B}>0
.
We further introduce a “monotone type nonlinearity” as a convex, proper, and lower-semicontinuous functional
\varphi:V\to(-\infty,+\infty]
.
By the Hahn–Banach theorem, there exists a constant
C_{\varphi 1}>0
such that (cf.
[
4
, p. 161]
)

(1.2)

\varphi(v)\geq-C_{\varphi 1}(\|v\|_{V}+1)\qquad\forall v\in V.

In this setting, we consider the following parabolic variational inequality: given an external force
f:[0,T]\to H
and initial datum
u^{0}\in V
, find
u:[0,T]\to V
such that
u(0)=u^{0}
, and, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

(1.3)

(\partial_{t}u(t),v-u(t))+a(u(t),v-u(t))+\left<B(u(t),u(t)),v-u(t)\right>+\varphi(v)-\varphi(u(t))\geq(f(t),v-u(t))\qquad\forall v\in V.

Such a parabolic variational inequality of the Navier–Stokes type was already examined in the framework of nonlinear semigroups by
[
4
]
.
However, it assumed the two-dimensional situation.
In fact, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\,(d=2,3)
be a bounded smooth domain and set, as an example,
H:=L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
,
V:=H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
(cf.
[
2
, p. 250]
),
a(u,v):=(\nabla u,\nabla v)
, and
B(u,v):=(u\cdot\nabla)v
to treat the standard incompressible Navier–Stokes equations with the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition on
\partial\Omega
.
Then assumption (3) of
[
4
, p. 159]
reads

(\underbrace{u\cdot\nabla u-v\cdot\nabla v}_{=(u-v)\cdot\nabla u+v\cdot\nabla(u-v)},u-v)\geq-C\|u-v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

We can expect it to be valid only when both of the 2D Ladyzhenskaya inequality
\|u-v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u-v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
and the cancelation property
(v\cdot\nabla(u-v),u-v)=0
hold.
We remark that, for the latter equality to hold, we need—in addition to
\operatorname{div}v=0
—the impermeability condition
v\cdot\nu=0
on the boundary, where
\nu
is the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.

A similar variational inequality was also considered by
[
16
]
, which proved existence of a weak solution for
d=3
and unique existence of a strong solution for
d=2
(assumption (2.19) of
[
16
, p. 881]
holds only for
d=2
by a similar reason as above) by the Galerkin method.
Analysis of related problems is also found in
[
6
,
18
,
21
]
,
We emphasize that the cancelation property
\left<B(u,v),v\right>=0
played an essential role in these results.

Remark 1.1
.

The impermeability condition was not imposed in the problems considered by
[
6
,
16
,
18
,
21
]
.
Instead,
u_{\tau}:=u-(u\cdot\nu)\nu=0
and a unilateral condition on the total pressure
P:=p+\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}
(
p
is the usual pressure) are supplemented.
If we employ
a(u,v):=(\operatorname{rot}u,\operatorname{rot}v)
and
B(u,v):=\operatorname{rot}u\times v
, then we see that
-\Delta u+\nabla p+u\cdot\nabla u=\operatorname{rot}\operatorname{rot}u+\nabla P+B(u,u)
if
\operatorname{div}u=0
,
that
(\operatorname{rot}\operatorname{rot}u,v)=a(u,v)
if
v_{\tau}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and that
\left<B(u,v),v\right>=0
.
However, in case one would like to adopt a boundary condition that does not prescribe
P
, the impermeability condition would be needed to ensure the cancelation property
(u\cdot\nabla v,v)=0
.
In more precise, a slightly weaker condition
(u\cdot\nabla v,v)\geq 0
is sufficient to make an energy estimate available, which holds if the outflow condition
u\cdot\nu\geq 0
is imposed on the boundary (see
[
1
, p. 139]
and
[
23
]
).

Strong solvability of a more general evolution equation, in which both of the monotone part and the non-monotone perturbation part can depend on time and can be multi-valued, was established by
[
19
]
.
As a concrete application, existence and uniqueness of a local-in-time strong solution such that
u\in L^{2}(0,T_{*};H^{2}(\Omega))
for some
T_{*}
are shown for the 3D Navier–Stokes equations (
\Omega
can even depend on
t
) using the cancelation property (see
[
19
, p. 295]
).

An example of boundary conditions in which the cancelation property of
B
does not necessarily hold is the leak boundary condition of friction type (LBCF) introduced by
[
8
]
(nevertheless,
[
8
]
considered only the stationary case; see also
[
9
,
10
]
for the non-stationary Stokes case).
In
[
14
]
, existence and uniqueness of a local-in-time strong solution such that
u\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T_{*};L^{2}(\Omega))
were shown, but only under the assumption that the initial permeability
u^{0}\cdot\nu
is sufficiently small on the boundary.
This additional assumption arose in order to compensate the lack of the cancelation property.
Another boundary condition, in which the outflow condition
u\cdot\nu\geq 0
is imposed in a way similar to the Signorini condition in elasticity problems, was analyzed in
[
1
, Section III.3]
and independently in
[
23
]
.
We refer to
[
15
, Chapter 6]
for a treatment of various mixed boundary conditions including these subdifferential boundary conditions.
We remark that
H^{2}(\Omega)
-regularity was not obtained in these results, cf.
[
14
, p. 777]
.

Remark 1.2
.

For the linear leak boundary condition
u_{\tau}=0
,
\sigma(u,p)\nu\cdot\nu=0
, or the so-called do-nothing boundary condition
\sigma(u,p)\nu=0
, where
\sigma(u,p)
denotes the stress tensor, one can prove strong solvability such that
u\in L^{2}(0,T_{*};H^{2}(\Omega))
, utilizing the eigenfunctions of a Stokes operator in the Galerkin method (see
[
13
, Theorem 6]
).
This strategy, however, is not applicable to e.g. LBCF because the associated Stokes operator is no longer linear.

In view of the situation mentioned above, the goal of the present work is to establish local-in-time strong solvability of the Navier–Stokes type variational inequality (
1.3
)

1)

with
d=3
included;

2)

under a weaker condition on
B
than the cancelation property to allow for boundary conditions like LBCF;

3)

with higher regularity than
V
if a corresponding stationary Stokes problem admits a suitable regularity structure.

Since it is non-trivial to achieve 1)–3) above by the Galerkin method, which somewhat corresponds to spatial discretization, we will make use of temporal discretization (also known as Rothe’s method).
It was already exploited in
[
11
]
to prove
H^{2}
-regularity for a non-stationary Bingham problem, in which a fully-implicit backward Euler scheme was combined with the truncation technique of
B
(cf.
[
2
, Section 5.7]
).
To the contrary, in this paper we propose a semi-implicit scheme where the convection velocity is taken from the previous time step, not truncating
B
.
Then, at the stage of deriving
a priori
estimates for discrete solutions, we already need to restrict to some
T_{*}<T
.
To the best of our knowledge, such a local-in-time argument in Rothe’s method seems to be new and is of independent interest.

The organization of this paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we explain the detailed assumptions on
H,V,B
, and
\varphi
and state the main results.
Examples of boundary value problems that fit the main results are described in Section
3
.
In Section
4
, we discuss solvability of a stationary Oseen type problem, which is necessary to solve our semi-implicit scheme in Section
5
.
We also show
a priori
estimates for the discrete-in-time solutions that are uniform in the time increment
\Delta t
.
Passage to the limit
\Delta t\to 0
is discussed in Section
6
and we construct a strong solution
u
of (
1.3
) such that
u\in H^{1}(0,T;H)
(which is similar to a maximal
L^{2}
-regularity class).
In Section
7
, under additional assumptions on
u^{0}
and
f
, we prove that
u\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;H)
(which is similar to a Kiselev–Ladyzhenskaya class).
The argument of Section
7
requires some facts of evolution equations involving maximal monotone operators, proofs of which are provided in the appendix for the sake of completeness.

2.
Main results

Let us clarify the detailed hypotheses on the Navier–Stokes type nonlinearity
B
and the monotone type nonlinearity
\varphi
.
For each assumption, we will explain how it is satisfied for the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations (abbreviated as NS below) in a bounded smooth domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\,(d=3)
, in which case
H
and
V
are chosen to be closed subspaces of
\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\mid\operatorname{div}v=0\text{ in }\Omega\}
and
\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}\mid\operatorname{div}v=0\text{ in }\Omega\}
, respectively (their actual choices depend on boundary conditions).

First, we relax the cancelation property
\left<B(u,v),v\right>=0
as follows.

(H1)
There exist
C>0
and
\beta\in(0,1]
such that, for arbitrary
u\in V
and
v\in V
,

|\left<B(u,v),v\right>|\leq C\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}\|v\|_{H}^{\beta}\|v\|_{V}^{1-\beta}.

By Young’s inequality for real numbers, this yields

\displaystyle|\left<B(u,v),v\right>|\leq C\big(\|u\|_{V}^{2/\beta}\|v\|_{H}^{2}\big)^{\beta/2}\cdot\big(\|v\|_{V}^{2}\big)^{1-\beta/2}\leq C_{\theta_{1},\epsilon}\|u\|_{V}^{\theta_{1}}\|v\|_{H}^{2}+\epsilon\|v\|_{V}^{2}\qquad\forall\epsilon>0,

where we have rephrased
\theta_{1}:=2/\beta\geq 2
.
In NS case, this holds with
\theta_{1}=4\,(\beta=1/2)
; in fact we have

|(u\cdot\nabla v,v)|\leq\|u\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\quad\forall u,v\in V,

as a result of the Sobolev and Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities.

Next we assume a kind of continuity of
B
with respect to weak convergence in
V
.

(H2)
For each fixed
u\in V
, if
v_{j}\rightharpoonup v
weakly in
V
and
w_{j}\rightharpoonup w
weakly in
V
as
j\to\infty
, then
\left<B(u,v_{j}),w_{j}\right>\to\left<B(u,v),w\right>
.
In particular, when
v_{j}\rightharpoonup v
weakly in
V
, we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left<B(u,v_{j}),v_{j}-w\right>=\left<B(u,v),v-w\right>\quad\forall w\in V,

so that
B(u,\cdot):V\to V^{\prime}
is pseudo-monotone.
In NS case, (H2) follows from

|(u\cdot\nabla v_{j},v_{j}-w)-(u\cdot\nabla v,v-w)|\leq|(u\cdot\nabla v_{j},v_{j}-v)|+|(u\cdot\nabla(v_{j}-v),v-w)|\to 0\quad(j\to\infty),

where we have used the Hölder inequality with
\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}=1
and the compact embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
.

Remark 2.1
.

Recalling the precise definition of the pseudo-monotonicity (see
[
17
, Définition II.2.1]
), in addition to the weak convergence
v_{j}\rightharpoonup v
one could have assumed that
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\left<B(u,v_{j}),v_{j}-v\right>\leq 0
, which was actually unused in the above argument.
In this sense, (H2) is a stronger concept than the pseudo-monotonicity.

In the following we presume existence of a Hilbert space
W\subset V
whose embedding is dense and compact.
In NS case,
W
is taken to be a closed subspace of
\{v\in H^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\mid\operatorname{div}v=0\text{ in }\Omega\}
depending on boundary conditions.
Further assumptions on
W
in connection with
B
are described as follows.

(H3)
There exists
C>0
such that, if
u\in W
and
v\in V
then
B(u,v)\in H
and

\|B(u,v)\|_{H}\leq C\|u\|_{W}\|v\|_{V}.

In NS case, this follows from
\|u\cdot\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and the embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

(H4)
There exist
C>0
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
such that, if
u\in V
and
v\in W
then
B(u,v)\in H
and

\|B(u,v)\|_{H}\leq C\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}^{\gamma}\|v\|_{W}^{1-\gamma}\qquad(\text{in particular, }\|B(u,v)\|_{H}\leq C\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{W}).

By Young’s inequality, this yields

\|B(u,v)\|_{H}\leq C_{\theta_{2},\epsilon}\|u\|_{V}^{\theta_{2}}\|v\|_{V}+\epsilon\|v\|_{W}\qquad\forall\epsilon>0,

where we have rephrased
\theta_{2}:=1/\gamma\geq 1
.
In NS case, (H4) holds with
\theta_{2}=2\,(\gamma=1/2)
because
\|u\cdot\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}
.

Finally, let us make an essential hypothesis on
W
in connection with
\varphi
, which describes an elliptic regularity structure for a stationary Stokes version of (
1.3
).
To state it, we recall the definition of the effective domain
D(\varphi):=\{v\in V\mid\varphi(v)<\infty\}
and that of the subdifferential of
\varphi
.
We define a multi-valued operator
\partial\varphi:V\to V^{\prime}
, which can be identified with either a map
\partial\varphi:V\to 2^{V^{\prime}}
or a subset
\partial\varphi\subset V\times V^{\prime}
, by
\partial\varphi(u)=\{\xi\in V^{\prime}\mid\left<\xi,v-u\right>\leq\varphi(v)-\varphi(u)\;\forall v\in V\}
together with
D(\partial\varphi):=\{u\in V\mid\partial\varphi(u)\neq\emptyset\}
.

(H5)
Let
u\in D(\varphi)
and
f\in H
satisfy the variational inequality

a(u,v-u)+\varphi(v)-\varphi(u)\geq\left<f,v-u\right>\qquad\forall v\in V,

that is,
f\in(A+\partial\varphi)(u)
.
Then we have
u\in W\cap D(\partial\varphi)
and

\|u\|_{W}\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\|f\|_{H}+C_{\varphi 2}

for some positive constants
C_{\mathrm{reg}},C_{\varphi 2}
independent of
u
and
f
.
In other words,
(A+\partial\varphi)^{-1}(H)\subset W
holds.
Examples of variational inequalities that satisfy (H5) will be mentioned in Section
3
.

Remark 2.2
.

A subdifferential of
\varphi
, whose domain and image are enlarged to
H
and restricted to
H
respectively, is defined by
\partial_{*}\varphi:H\to 2^{H}
;
u\mapsto\{\xi\in H\mid(\xi,v-u)\leq\varphi(v)-\varphi(u)\;\forall v\in H\}
with
D(\partial_{*}\varphi):=\{u\in H\mid\partial_{*}\varphi(u)\neq\emptyset\}
, where we note that
\varphi|_{H\setminus V}
is set to be
+\infty
.
We see that
\partial_{*}\varphi(u)=\partial\varphi(u)\cap H
for all
u\in V
.

We are ready to state our main results.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under the hypotheses of (H1)–(H5) above, let
f\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
and
u^{0}\in D(\varphi)\subset V
be arbitrary.
Then there exist some
T_{*}\in(0,T]
and a unique
u\in H^{1}(0,T_{*};H)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T_{*};V)\cap L^{2}(0,T_{*};W)
that solves (
1.3
) for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Under the hypotheses of (H1)–(H5) above, let
f\in W^{1,1}(0,T;H)
and
u^{0}\in W\cap D(\partial\varphi)
with
(A+\partial\varphi)(u^{0})\cap H\neq\emptyset
.
Then there exist some
T_{*}\in(0,T]
and a unique solution
u\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T_{*};H)\cap H^{1}(0,T_{*};V)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T_{*};W)
of (
1.3
).
Moreover, (
1.3
) holds for all
t\in[0,T_{*})
, with
\partial_{t}u(t)
replaced by the right-derivative
\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t):=\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\frac{u(t+h)-u(t)}{h}
.

Remark 2.3
.

(i) Slightly modifying the proofs of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, we see that the same conclusions hold for the problem in which (
1.3
) is replaced by

(\partial_{t}u(t),v-u(t))+a(u(t),v-u(t))+\left<B(b,u(t)),v-u(t)\right>+\varphi(v)-\varphi(u(t))\geq(f(t),v-u(t))\qquad\forall v\in V,

where
b\in W
is given.
Such a situation appears, for example, in convection-diffusion equations with a prescribed velocity field (see Section
3
below).

(ii) Under additional assumptions on
B
and
\varphi
one can obtain global-in-time existence of a strong solution, which corresponds either to the 2D Navier–Stokes equations with the cancelation property or to the 3D Navier–Stokes equations with small data.
We will briefly mention these results at Remarks
5.2
and
6.1
below.

3.
Examples

We discuss applications of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
to concrete problems, checking the validity of (H5).

3.1.
Obstacle problem for convection-diffusion equations

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\,(d=1,2,3)
be a bounded domain with the sufficiently smooth boundary,
H:=L^{2}(\Omega)
,
V:=H^{1}(\Omega)
,
W:=H^{2}(\Omega)
,
a(u,v):=(\nabla u,\nabla v)
,
B(u,v):=b(u)\cdot\nabla v
, where
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a linear function, and

\varphi(v):=\begin{cases}0&\quad\text{if}\quad v\in K:=\{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\mid v\geq 0\;\text{a.e.\ in}\;\Omega\},\\
+\infty&\quad\text{if}\quad v\in V\setminus K.\end{cases}

We know from
[
2
, Proposition 2.11]
and
[
3
, Corollary 14]
that hypothesis (H5) is satisfied.
Therefore, by Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, there exists a unique local-in-time strong solution
u
, which has
H^{2}(\Omega)
-spatial regularity, such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+b(u)\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle=f\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T_{*})\mid u(x,t)>0\},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+b(u)\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle\geq f,\quad u\geq 0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T_{*}),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,T_{*}),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=u^{0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\Omega\times\{0\},

depending on regularity assumptions of
u^{0}
and
f
.
We remark that, although this was already obtained in
[
2
, Corollary 5.2]
for
b\equiv 0
, extension to
b\neq 0
was non-trivial—even if
b(u)\equiv b
(given smooth velocity field; recall Remark
2.3
(i))—because the monotonicity
\left<B(\cdot,u-v),u-v\right>\geq 0
does not necessarily hold (note that neither
\operatorname{div}b=0
in
\Omega
nor
b\cdot\nu\geq 0
on
\partial\Omega
is valid for a general vector field
b
).

3.2.
Nonlinear Neumann problems for convection-diffusion equations

We adopt the same setting as in Subsection
3.1
except that we replace
\varphi:V\to(-\infty,+\infty]
by the following:

\varphi(v):=\begin{cases}\int_{\partial\Omega}j(v)\,d\gamma&\quad\text{if}\quad v\in H^{1}(\Omega),\;j(v)\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega),\\
+\infty&\quad\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
j:\mathbb{R}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is a convex, proper, and lower-semicontinuous function, and
d\gamma
denotes the surface measure of
\partial\Omega
.
We know from
[
2
, Proposition 2.9]
) and
[
3
, Theorem 12]
that hypothesis (H5) is satisfied.
Therefore, by Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, under suitable assumptions on
u^{0}
and
f
, we obtain a local-in-time strong solution
u
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+b(u)\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle=f\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T_{*}),

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}

\displaystyle\in\beta(u)\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,T_{*}),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=u^{0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\Omega\times\{0\},

where
\beta:=\partial j
is a maximal monotone graph of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
and recall that
\nu
is the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
In particular, the choices
j(r)=+\infty\,(r<0)
,
j(r)=0\,(r\geq 0)
and
j(r)=|r|
correspond to simplified Signorini and friction problems, respectively (cf.
[
12
, Sections II.4 and II.5]
).

3.3.
Navier–Stokes equations with boundary conditions of friction type

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\,(d=2,3)
be a bounded smooth domain, in which
\partial\Omega
has two components
\Gamma_{D}
and
\Gamma
(i.e.,
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{D}\cap\Gamma=\emptyset
).
Furthermore, we set
H:=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\mid\operatorname{div}v=0\text{ in }\Omega,\;v\cdot\nu=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}\}
,
V:=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}\cap H\mid v=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}\}
,
W:=H^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\cap V
,
a(u,v):=2(\mathbb{E}(u),\mathbb{E}(v))
, where
\mathbb{E}(u)=\frac{\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\top}}{2}
denotes the rate-of-strain tensor of
u
, and
B(u,v):=u\cdot\nabla v
.
Finally, we consider
\varphi_{\tau}:V\to(-\infty,+\infty]
(resp.
\varphi_{\nu}:V\to(-\infty,+\infty]
) defined by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau}(v):=\begin{cases}\int_{\Gamma}g|v_{\tau}|\,d\gamma&\quad\text{if}\quad v\in V_{\tau}:=\{v\in V\mid v\cdot\nu=0\;\text{on}\;\Gamma\},\\
+\infty&\quad\text{if}\quad v\in V\setminus V_{\tau},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\bigg(\text{resp.}\quad

\displaystyle\varphi_{\nu}(v):=\begin{cases}\int_{\Gamma}g|v\cdot\nu|\,d\gamma&\quad\text{if}\quad v\in V_{\nu}:=\{v\in V\mid v_{\tau}=0\;\text{on}\;\Gamma\},\\
+\infty&\quad\text{if}\quad v\in V\setminus V_{\nu},\end{cases}\bigg)

where
g\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
,
g\geq 0
is a given function and we recall
v_{\tau}=v-(v\cdot\nu)\nu
.
We know from
[
20
]
that hypothesis (H5) is satisfied in the above setting.
Therefore, by Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, under suitable assumptions on
u^{0}
and
f
, we obtain a local-in-time strong solution
u
admitting
H^{2}(\Omega)
-spatial regularity of (the pressure is represented by
p
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p

\displaystyle=f,\quad\operatorname{div}u=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T_{*}),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\Gamma_{D}\times(0,T_{*}),

\displaystyle u\cdot\nu=0,\quad-\sigma_{\tau}(u)

\displaystyle\in g\partial|u_{\tau}|\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\Gamma\times(0,T_{*}),

\displaystyle\big(\text{resp.}\quad u\cdot\tau=0,\quad-\sigma_{\nu}(u,p)

\displaystyle\in g\partial|u\cdot\nu|\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\Gamma\times(0,T_{*}),\big)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=u^{0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\Omega\times\{0\},

where we see that the slip (resp. leak) boundary condition of friction type is imposed on
\Gamma
.
Here,
\sigma_{\tau}(u)
and
\sigma_{\nu}(u,p)
are the tangential and normal components of the traction vector
\sigma(u,p)\nu=-p\nu+2\mathbb{E}(u)\nu
, respectively.
We remark that time-dependent
g
’s are treated by
[
14
]
; however,
H^{2}(\Omega)
-regularity cannot be shown even for time-independent
g
’s by the method of
[
14
]
(see p. 777 there).
We also note that
(u\cdot\nabla v,v)=0
holds for the slip case, thus admitting a global-in-time strong solution if
d=2
.

3.4.
Bingham problem with perfect slip boundary condition

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\,(d=2,3)
be a bounded smooth domain,
H:=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\mid\operatorname{div}v=0\text{ in }\Omega,\;v\cdot\nu=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}
,
V:=H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}\cap H
,
W:=H^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\cap H
,
a(u,v):=(\nabla u,\nabla v)
,
B(u,v):=u\cdot\nabla v
, and
\varphi(v):=\int_{\Omega}g|\nabla v|\,dx
, where
g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
g\geq 0
is a given function.
We know from
[
11
, Corollary 3.1]
that hypothesis (H5) is satisfied.
Therefore, by Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
we obtain a local-in-time strong solution of

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\operatorname{div}(T^{D}-pI)

\displaystyle=f,\quad\operatorname{div}u=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T_{*}),

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\begin{cases}T^{D}=\nabla u+g\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\quad&\text{if}\quad\nabla u\neq 0\\
|T^{D}|\leq g\quad&\text{if}\quad\nabla u=0\end{cases}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T_{*}),

\displaystyle u\cdot\nu=0,\quad(\nabla u\,\nu)_{\tau}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,T_{*}),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=u^{0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\Omega\times\{0\},

where
T^{D}
and
p
constitute the deviatoric and pressure parts of of the stress tensor, respectively.
We remark that strong solvability with
H^{2}(\Omega)
-regularity for time-dependent
g
’s is shown by
[
11
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.2]
; hence the above result, in which
g
is assumed not to depend on
t
, is weaker than
[
11
]
.

4.
Solvability of stationary problems

Sections
4
–
6
will be devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
Let us first recall a well-known result of elliptic variational inequalities (see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem I.4.1]
and
[
2
, Corollary 2.9]
): for all
\lambda\geq 0
and
f\in V^{\prime}
, there exists a unique solution
u\in V\cap D(\partial\varphi)
such that

(4.1)

\lambda(u,v-u)+a(u,v-u)+\varphi(v)-\varphi(u)\geq\left<f,v-u\right>\qquad\forall v\in V.

Therefore,
\lambda I+A+\partial\varphi:V\to V^{\prime}
is bijective.
Since
A+\partial\varphi
is monotone by the coercivity of
a
and
[
2
, Theorem 2.8]
, we find from
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
that
A+\partial\varphi:V\to V^{\prime}
is maximal monotone.
Its restriction to
H
, i.e.,
(A+\partial\varphi)_{H}:H\to 2^{H};\;u\mapsto(A+\partial\varphi)(u)\cap H
, with
D((A+\partial\varphi)_{H}):=\{u\in H\mid Au\in H,\,\partial\varphi(u)\cap H\neq\emptyset\}
, is also a maximal monotone operator.
From now on, we will not distinguish
(A+\partial\varphi)_{H}
from
A+\partial\varphi
.

In the next lemma, we construct a solution of an Oseen version of (
4.1
) for sufficiently large
\lambda>0
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
w\in V
and
f\in H
.
For all
\lambda\geq C_{\theta_{1},1/4}\|w\|_{V}^{\theta_{1}}
, there exists some
u\in V
such that

(4.2)

\lambda(u,v-u)+a(u,v-u)+\left<B(w,u),v-u\right>+\varphi(v)-\varphi(u)\geq(f,v-u)\qquad\forall v\in V.

In particular, we have
u\in D(\partial\varphi)
.

Proof.

Let
L=L_{\lambda,w}:V\to V^{\prime}
be defined by
L(u)=\lambda u+Au+B(w,u)
, which is clearly a bounded (linear) operator.
We observe that
L
is pseudo-monotone; in fact, for a sequence
u_{j}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
V
as
j\to\infty
, one has

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left<L(u_{j}),u_{j}-v\right>

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\left<\lambda u_{j}+B(w,u_{j}),u_{j}-v\right>+\liminf_{j\to\infty}a(u_{j},u_{j}-v)

\displaystyle\geq\left<\lambda u+B(w,u),u-v\right>+a(u,u-v)=\left<L(u),u-v\right>,

where we have used compactness of
V\hookrightarrow H
, hypothesis (H2), and lower semi-continuity of the norm
\sqrt{a(\cdot,\cdot)}=\|\cdot\|_{V}
.
Moreover,
L
is coercive, that is, for some
v_{0}\in D(\varphi)

\frac{\left<L(v),v-v_{0}\right>+\varphi(v)}{\|v\|_{V}}\to+\infty\quad\text{as}\quad\|v\|_{V}\to\infty.

In fact, for any
\|v\|_{V}\geq 1
and
v_{0}\in D(\varphi)\neq\emptyset
we have (recall
\|v\|_{V}^{2}=a(v,v)
)

\displaystyle\left<L(v),v-v_{0}\right>+\varphi(v)

\displaystyle=\lambda\|v\|_{H}^{2}+\|v\|_{V}^{2}+\left<B(w,v),v\right>+\varphi(v)-\left<\lambda v+Av+B(w,v),v_{0}\right>

\displaystyle\geq(\lambda-C_{\theta_{1},1/4}\|w\|_{V}^{\theta_{1}})\|v\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\|v\|_{V}^{2}-C_{\varphi 1}(\|v\|_{V}+1)-(\lambda+1+C_{B}\|w\|_{V})\|v\|_{V}\|v_{0}\|_{V}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{4}\|v\|_{V}^{2}-\big(2C_{\varphi 1}+(\lambda+1+C_{B}\|w\|_{V})\|v_{0}\|_{V}\big)\|v\|_{V},

where we have used hypothesis (H1), (
1.2
), and (
1.1
).
Then an abstract surjectivity result for pseudo-monotone operators (see
[
17
, Théorèm II.8.5]
) asserts the existence of a solution of (
4.2
).
∎

The solution constructed in Lemma
4.1
indeed admits more regularity
u\in W
under hypothesis (H5).

Lemma 4.2
.

In the setting of Lemma
4.1
, if
\lambda\geq C_{\theta_{1},1/4}(2\|w\|_{V})^{\theta_{1}}+1/2
, any solution
u
of (
4.2
) belongs to
W
.

Proof.

If
w\in W
, we immediately have
u\in W
from (H5) by arranging terms of (
4.2
) as follows (note that
B(w,u)\in H
by (H3)):

a(u,v-u)+\varphi(v)-\varphi(u)\geq(f-\lambda u-B(w,u),v-u)\qquad\forall v\in V.

Now consider a general
w\in V
.
Since
W\hookrightarrow V
is dense, there exist
w_{j}\in W\,(j=1,2,\dots)
such that
w_{j}\to w
strongly in
V
as
j\to\infty
.
Without loss of generality we may assume
\|w_{j}\|_{V}\leq 2\|w\|_{V}
.
By the above argument, one can find
u_{j}\in W
such that

(4.3)

\lambda(u_{j},v-u_{j})+a(u_{j},v-u_{j})+(B(w_{j},u_{j}),v-u_{j})+\varphi(v)-\varphi(u_{j})\geq(f,v-u_{j})\qquad\forall v\in V.

We take
v
to be any
v_{0}\in D(\varphi)
and obtain

\displaystyle\lambda\|u_{j}\|_{H}^{2}+\|u_{j}\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(f-B(w_{j},u_{j}),u_{j})-\varphi(u_{j})+\left<\lambda u_{j}+Au_{j}-B(w_{j},u_{j})-f,v_{0}\right>+\varphi(v_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{j}\|_{H}^{2}+C_{\theta_{1},1/4}\|w_{j}\|_{V}^{\theta_{1}}\|u_{j}\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|u_{j}\|_{V}^{2}+C_{\varphi 1}(\|u_{j}\|_{V}+1)+C_{\lambda,C_{B},\|w\|_{V}}\|v_{0}\|_{V}^{2}+\varphi(v_{0}),

where
C_{\lambda,C_{B},\|w\|_{V}}>0
is a constant independent of
j
.
Therefore,

\frac{1}{2}\|u_{j}\|_{V}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{H}^{2}+C_{\varphi 1}^{2}+C_{\varphi 1}+C_{\lambda,A,B,w}\|v_{0}\|_{V}^{2}+\varphi(v_{0}),

which gives a uniform bound of
u_{j}
in
V
(and
H
as well).
We further find from (
4.3
), (H5), and (H4) that

\displaystyle\|u_{j}\|_{W}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(\|f\|_{H}+\lambda\|u_{j}\|_{H}+\|B(w_{j},u_{j})\|_{H})+C_{\varphi 2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(\|f\|_{H}+\lambda\|u_{j}\|_{H})+C_{\mathrm{reg}}C_{\theta_{2},\frac{1}{2C_{\mathrm{reg}}}}\|w_{j}\|_{V}^{\theta_{2}}\|u_{j}\|_{V}+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{j}\|_{W}+C_{\varphi 2},

which shows that
\{u_{j}\}
is uniformly bounded in
W
.
Therefore, there exists a subsequence
\{u_{j^{\prime}}\}
and
u_{*}\in W
such that
u_{j^{\prime}}\rightharpoonup u_{*}
weakly in
W
.
Since
W\hookrightarrow V
is compact,
u_{j^{\prime}}\to u_{*}
strongly in
V
.

Finally we claim that
u=u_{*}
, which proves the lemma.
For this it suffices to show
u_{j}\to u
strongly in
V
as
j\to\infty
.
We substitute
v=u
and
v=u_{j}
in (
4.3
) and (
4.2
) respectively, and add the resulting inequalities to obtain

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lambda\|u-u_{j}\|_{H}^{2}+\|u-u_{j}\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left<B(w_{j},u_{j})-B(w,u),u-u_{j}\right>=\left<B(w_{j},u_{j}-u),u-u_{j}\right>+\left<B(w_{j}-w,u),u-u_{j}\right>

\displaystyle\leq C_{\theta_{1},1/4}\|w_{j}\|_{V}^{\theta_{1}}\|u-u_{j}\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|u-u_{j}\|_{V}^{2}+C_{B}\|w_{j}-w\|_{V}\|u\|_{V}\|u-u_{j}\|_{V},

where we have used (H1).
Absorbing the first and second terms on the right-hand side yields

\frac{3}{4}\|u-u_{j}\|_{V}\leq C_{B}\|w_{j}-w\|_{V}\|u\|_{V}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad j\to\infty,

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

A similar calculation to (
4.4
) shows that the solution of (
4.2
) is unique.

5.
Discrete-in-time approximation and a priori estimates

We propose the following discrete-in-time approximation to (
1.3
), which is semi-implicit with respect to the Navier–Stokes type nonlinearity
B
(and fully implicit with respect to the monotone type nonlinearity
\varphi
).
Given
u^{0}\in D(\varphi)\subset V
,
f\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
, and
\Delta t>0
, we try to find
\{u^{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}\subset D(\varphi)
such that, for
n=1,\dots,N
,

(5.1)

\Big(\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t},v-u^{n}\Big)+a(u^{n},v-u^{n})+\left<B(u^{n-1},u^{n}),v-u^{n}\right>+\varphi(v)-\varphi(u^{n})\geq(f^{n},v-u^{n})\qquad\forall v\in V,

where
f^{n}:=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{(n-1)\Delta t}^{n\Delta t}f(t)\,dt
.
According to Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, the above variational inequalities indeed admit (unique) solutions
u^{n}\in W\cap D(\partial\varphi)\,(j=1,\dots,N)
, provided that
N
satisfies

\frac{1}{\Delta t}\geq C_{\theta_{1},1/4}(2\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|u^{n-1}\|_{V})^{\theta_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}.

We need to ensure that such
N
, which depends on
\Delta t
, can be taken large enough for
N\Delta t
to admit a lower bound
T_{*}>0
that is independent of
\Delta t
.
For this purpose, we establish an
a priori
estimate as follows.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that
\{u^{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}\subset W
solve (
5.1
).
Then, for all
n=1,\dots,N
we have

(5.2)

\Big\|\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}-\|u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2}+\|u^{n}-u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2}}{\Delta t}+\frac{2(\varphi(u^{n})-\varphi(u^{n-1}))}{\Delta t}\leq M(\|u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2\theta_{2}}\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}+\|f^{n}\|_{H}^{2}+1),

where
M>0
is a constant dependent only on
\theta_{2}
,
C_{\mathrm{reg}}
, and
C_{\varphi 2}
.

Proof.

Application of (H5) to (
5.1
), combined with (H4), leads to

\displaystyle\|u^{n}\|_{W}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(\|f^{n}\|_{H}+\Big\|\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\|_{H}+\|B(u^{n-1},u^{n})\|_{H})+C_{\varphi 2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(\|f^{n}\|_{H}+\Big\|\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\|_{H}+C_{\theta_{2},\frac{1}{2C_{\mathrm{reg}}}}\|u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{\theta_{2}}\|u^{n}\|_{V})+\frac{1}{2}\|u^{n}\|_{W}+C_{\varphi 2},

whence, by
(a+b+c+d)^{2}\leq 4(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2})
,

(5.3)

\|u^{n}\|_{W}^{2}\leq 16C_{\mathrm{reg}}^{2}(\|f^{n}\|_{H}^{2}+\Big\|\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\|_{H}^{2}+C_{\theta_{2},\frac{1}{2C_{\mathrm{reg}}}}^{2}\|u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2\theta_{2}}\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2})+16C_{\varphi 2}^{2}.

Next we take
v=u^{n-1}
in (
5.1
) and divide by
\Delta t
to obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}-\|u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2}+\|u^{n}-u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2}}{2\Delta t}+\frac{\varphi(u^{n})-\varphi(u^{n-1})}{\Delta t}\leq\Big(f^{n}-B(u^{n-1},u^{n}),\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big),

where we recall
a(v,v)=\|v\|_{V}^{2}
.
Since the right-hand side is bounded by

\Big\|f^{n}-B(u^{n-1},u^{n})\Big\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\Big\|\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\|_{H}^{2}\leq 2\|f^{n}\|_{H}^{2}+2\|B(u^{n-1},u^{n})\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\Big\|\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\|_{H}^{2},

it follows from (H5) that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\Big\|\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}-\|u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2}+\|u^{n}-u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2}}{2\Delta t}+\frac{\varphi(u^{n})-\varphi(u^{n-1})}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|f^{n}\|_{H}^{2}+4(C_{\theta_{2},\epsilon}^{2}\|u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2\theta_{2}}\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\|u^{n}\|_{W}^{2}).

Taking
\epsilon
such that
4\epsilon^{2}\cdot 16C_{\mathrm{reg}}^{2}=1/4
(thus
\epsilon=1/(16C_{\mathrm{reg}})
) and substituting (
5.4
) into (
5.3
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big\|\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}-\|u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2}+\|u^{n}-u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2}}{2\Delta t}+\frac{\varphi(u^{n})-\varphi(u^{n-1})}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle\frac{9}{4}\|f^{n}\|_{H}^{2}+\big(4C_{\theta_{2},\frac{1}{16C_{\mathrm{reg}}}}^{2}+C_{\theta_{2},\frac{1}{2C_{\mathrm{reg}}}}^{2}/4\big)\|u^{n-1}\|_{V}^{2\theta_{2}}\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}+\frac{C_{\varphi 2}^{2}}{4C_{\mathrm{reg}}^{2}},

which is the desired inequality.
∎

Setting
b_{n}:=\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}+2\varphi(u^{n})
, we have

\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}\leq b_{n}+2C_{\varphi 1}(\|u^{n}\|_{V}+1)\leq b_{n}+2C_{\varphi 1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}+2C_{\varphi 1},

so that

\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}\leq 2b_{n}+\underbrace{4C_{\varphi 1}(C_{\varphi 1}+1)}_{=:C_{\varphi 3}}\Longleftrightarrow b_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u^{n}\|_{V}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}C_{\varphi 3}.

Substitution of this into (
5.2
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n}-b_{n-1}}{\Delta t}\leq M(2b_{n-1}+C_{\varphi 3})^{\theta_{2}}(2b_{n}+C_{\varphi 3})+M(\|f^{n}\|_{H}^{2}+1)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\frac{c_{n}-c_{n-1}}{\Delta t}\leq 2^{\theta_{2}+1}Mc_{n-1}^{\theta_{2}}c_{n}+M(\|f^{n}\|_{H}^{2}+1),

where
c_{n}:=b_{n}+C_{\varphi 3}/2
is non-negative.
We further introduce
d_{n}:=c_{n}+\beta
, in which
\beta>0
is a constant to be fixed later (see (
5.7
) below).
Then, setting
M^{\prime}:=2^{\theta_{2}+1}M
, we see that

(5.5)

\frac{d_{n}-d_{n-1}}{\Delta t}\leq M^{\prime}(d_{n-1}^{\theta_{2}}d_{n}+\|f^{n}\|_{H}^{2}+1).

Let us solve this difference inequality as follows.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}
be sequences of positive numbers such that
x_{n}\geq\beta>0\,(n=0,1,\dots)
.
Assume that they satisfy

\frac{x_{n}-x_{n-1}}{\Delta t}\leq M(x_{n-1}^{\theta}x_{n}+y_{n})\quad n=1,2,\dots,

where
\Delta t>0
,
M>0
, and
\theta\geq 1
are constants.
Then we have
x_{n}\leq 2^{1/\theta}x_{0}
for all
n
that satisfies

(5.6)

4Mx_{0}^{\theta}(n\Delta t)\leq\theta^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad 4Mx_{0}^{\theta}\sum_{m=1}^{n}y_{m}\Delta t\leq\beta^{\theta+1}.

Proof.

Suppose
x_{n}\geq x_{n-1}
.
Then, since
1-(\frac{x_{n-1}}{x_{n}})^{\theta}\leq\theta(1-\frac{x_{n-1}}{x_{n}})\Leftrightarrow x_{n}^{\theta}-x_{n-1}^{\theta}\leq\theta x_{n}^{\theta-1}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})
, it follows that

x_{n-1}^{-\theta}-x_{n}^{-\theta}=\frac{x_{n}^{\theta}-x_{n-1}^{\theta}}{x_{n-1}^{\theta}x_{n}^{\theta}}\leq\frac{\theta(x_{n}-x_{n-1})}{x_{n-1}^{\theta}x_{n}}\leq M\Delta t\Big(\theta+\frac{y_{n}}{\beta^{\theta+1}}\Big).

This obviously holds for the other case
x_{n}\leq x_{n-1}
as well.
Consequently, by summation we obtain

x_{0}^{-\theta}-x_{n}^{-\theta}\leq M\theta t_{n}+M\beta^{-\theta-1}\sum_{m=1}^{n}y_{m}\Delta t\Longleftrightarrow x_{n}\leq\Big(x_{0}^{-\theta}-M\theta t_{n}-M\beta^{-\theta-1}\sum_{m=1}^{n}y_{m}\Delta t)\Big)^{-1/\theta},

where
t_{n}:=n\Delta t
.
We conclude
x_{n}\leq 2^{1/\theta}x_{0}
in the range of
n
where (
5.6
) holds.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

The “fully-implicit” version of the difference inequality above, i.e.,

\frac{x_{n}-x_{n-1}}{\Delta t}\leq M(x_{n}^{\theta+1}+y_{n})

cannot be solved easily.
For this reason, the adoption of the semi-implicit scheme (
5.1
) is essential for our argument.

Remark 5.2
.

Consider a special case where the cancelation property
\left<B(u,v),v\right>=0
is valid and an estimate

\|B(u,v)\|_{H}\leq C\|u\|_{H}^{1/2}\|u\|_{V}^{1/2}\|v\|_{V}^{1/2}\|v\|_{W}^{1/2}\qquad(u\in V,\,v\in W),

which corresponds to the 2D Navier–Stokes equations, is available.
Then we can solve (
5.1
) without any restrictions on
\Delta t
and
N
.
Moreover, taking
v=0
in (
5.1
), we have a uniform bound for
\|u^{n}\|_{H}^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}\|u^{m}\|_{V}^{2}\Delta t
, provided that
0\in D(\varphi)
.
We arrive at a difference inequality similar to (
5.5
) where
d_{n-1}^{\theta_{2}}d_{n}
is replaced by
\|u^{n-1}\|_{H}^{2}d_{n-1}d_{n}
, to which a discrete Gronwall inequality—instead of Lemma
5.1
—is applicable.

Appendix A

Let

We consider the following differential inclusion:

[
2

The following lemma and two propositions are essentially taken from
[

Lemma A.1

) for a.e.

, then for all

Because

A.2
) is
\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)-x\|_{H}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|u(t_{0})-x\|_{H}^{2}
.
On the other hand, multiplying
\partial_{t}u(t)+\xi(t)=f(t)
by
u(t)-x
, where
\xi(t)\in Au(t)
, we have

(A.4)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)-x\|_{H}^{2}+\underbrace{(\xi(t)-y,u(t)-x)}_{\geq 0}=(f(t)-y,u(t)-x)\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in(0,T).

Integration of this for
t_{0}\leq s\leq t
leads to (
A.2
).
In case
u(t_{0})\in D(A)
, we set
x=u(t_{0})
in (
A.4
) and divide by
\|u(t)-u(t_{0})\|_{H}
to obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)-u(t_{0})\|_{H}\leq\|f(t)-f(t_{0})+f(t_{0})-y\|_{H}\qquad\forall y\in A(u(t_{0})),\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in(0,T).

Choosing
y
in such a way that
f(t_{0})-y=(f(t_{0})-Au(t_{0}))^{0}
and integrating with respect to
t
yield (
A.3
).
∎

Proposition A.1
.

Assume that
f\in C([0,T];H)
and that
u\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;H)
satisfies (
A.1
) for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
.
Then, for all
t\in[0,T)
we have
u(t)\in D(A)
.
Moreover,
u:[0,T)\to H
is right-differentiable, that is,
\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t_{0}):=\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\frac{u(t_{0}+h)-u(t_{0})}{h}
exists in
H
for all
0\leq t_{0}<T
; actually,
\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t_{0})=(f(t_{0})-Au(t_{0}))^{0}
holds.

Proof.

Since
u\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;H)
implies
\|\frac{u(t_{0}+h)-u(t_{0})}{h}\|_{H}\leq C
for some constant
C
independent of
t_{0}\in[0,T)
and of
h\in(0,T-t_{0}]
, there exist a subsequence
h^{\prime}\to 0
and some
\xi\in H
such that
\frac{u(t_{0}+h^{\prime})-u(t_{0})}{h^{\prime}}\rightharpoonup\xi
weakly in
H
.
By (
A.2
) we have

\Big(\frac{u(t_{0}+h^{\prime})-u(t_{0})}{h^{\prime}},u(t_{0})-x\Big)\leq\frac{1}{h^{\prime}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+h^{\prime}}(f(s)-y,u(s)-x)\,ds\qquad\forall[x,y]\in A.

Taking the limit
h^{\prime}\to 0
and using the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, we obtain

(\xi,u(t_{0})-x)\leq(f(t_{0})-y,u(t_{0})-x)\Longleftrightarrow(f(t_{0})-\xi-y,u(t_{0})-x)\geq 0\qquad\forall[x,y]\in A,

which combined with the maximal monotonicity of
A
implies that
u(t_{0})\in D(A)
and
f(t_{0})-\xi\in Au(t_{0})
, i.e.,
\xi\in f(t_{0})-Au(t_{0})
.

Now it follows from lower-semicontinuity of a norm with respect to weak convergence, (
A.3
) divided by
h
, and another use of the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, that

\|\xi\|_{H}\leq\liminf_{h^{\prime}\downarrow 0}\Big\|\frac{u(t_{0}+h^{\prime})-u(t_{0})}{h^{\prime}}\Big\|_{H}\leq\limsup_{h\downarrow 0}\Big\|\frac{u(t_{0}+h)-u(t_{0})}{h}\Big\|_{H}\leq\|(f(t_{0})-Au(t_{0}))^{0}\|_{H}.

By the property of the minimal section we must have
\xi=(f(t_{0})-Au(t_{0}))^{0}
, which results in
\Big\|\frac{u(t_{0}+h)-u(t_{0})}{h}\Big\|_{H}\to\|\xi\|_{H}
and in
\frac{u(t_{0}+h)-u(t_{0})}{h}\rightharpoonup\xi
with respect to the whole sequence
h\to 0
.
The proposition is concluded by the well-known fact that a weakly converging sequence in a Hilbert space is strongly convergent if its norm converges.
∎

Finally let us prove the right-continuity of of the right derivative.

Proposition A.2
.

In addition to the hypotheses of Proposition
A.1
, assume that
f\in W^{1,1}(0,T;H)
.
Then
\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u:[0,T)\to H
is right-continuous, that is,
\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t)\to\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t_{0})
as
t\downarrow t_{0}
for all
0\leq t_{0}<T
.

Proof.

We multiply
\partial_{t}u(t+h)+Au(t+h)\ni f(t+h)
and
\partial_{t}u(t)+Au(t)\ni f(t)
by
u(t+h)-u(t)
and
u(t)-u(t+h)
, respectively, and add the resulting two inclusions.
It follows that, for some
y\in Au(t)
and
z\in Au(t_{0})
,

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_{H}^{2}+\underbrace{(y-z,u(t)-u(t_{0}))}_{\geq 0}=(f(t)-f(t_{0}),u(t)-u(t_{0}))\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in(0,T),\;h\in(0,T-t).

Dividing the both sides by
\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_{H}
and integrating for
t_{0}\leq s\leq t
, we have

\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_{H}\leq\|u(t_{0}+h)-u(t_{0})\|_{H}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|f(s+h)-f(s)\|_{H}\,ds\qquad\text{for all}\;t\geq t_{0}\geq 0,\;h\in[0,T-t].

Dividing by
h
, taking the limit
h\to 0
, and using the dominated convergence theorem, we deduce that

\Big\|\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t)\Big\|_{H}\leq\Big\|\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t_{0})\Big\|_{H}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\partial_{s}f\|_{H}\,ds\qquad\text{for all}\;t\geq t_{0}\geq 0.

We now let
t\downarrow t_{0}
.
Since the right-hand side of the above estimate can be bounded uniformly in
t
, there exist a subsequence
t^{\prime}\downarrow t_{0}
and some
\xi\in H
such that
\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t^{\prime})\rightharpoonup\xi
weakly in
H
as
t^{\prime}\downarrow t_{0}
.
It then follows that

\|\xi\|_{H}\leq\liminf_{t^{\prime}\downarrow t_{0}}\Big\|\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t^{\prime})\Big\|_{H}\leq\limsup_{t\downarrow t_{0}}\Big\|\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t)\Big\|_{H}\leq\Big\|\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t_{0})\Big\|_{H}=\|(f(t_{0})-Au(t_{0}))^{0}\|_{H}.

Observing that
u(t^{\prime})\to u(t_{0})
strongly in
H
and that
Au(t^{\prime})\ni f(t^{\prime})-\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t^{\prime})\rightharpoonup f(t_{0})-\xi
weakly in
H
, we find from the demi-closedness of
A
(see
[
2
, Proposition 3.4]
) that
f(t_{0})-\xi\in Au(t_{0})
, i.e.,
\xi\in f(t_{0})-Au(t_{0})
.
By the property of the minimal section we have
\xi=(f(t_{0})-Au(t_{0}))^{0}=\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u(t_{0})
.
The proof can be completed similarly to the last argument in the proof of Proposition
A.1
.
∎

Individual and joint association of Life’s Essential 8 metrics with pre-sarcopenia among U.S. adults
Authors: Mao-Sen Lin, Zhao-Rong Lin, Xiao-Qi Guo, Hui-Zhong Lin, Ming-Fang Ye
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn recent times, the American Heart Association has updated its approach to evaluating cardiovascular health (CVH) by replacing the previous “Life’s Simple 7” with the more demanding “Life’s Essential 8” (LE8). However, the impact of enhancing CVH on reducing the risk of pre-sarcopenia and the association of LE8 metrics with pre-sarcopenia remain unexplored.MethodsLE8 score was calculated among 9857 participants. Multivariable logistic regression was utilized to investigate the associations between LE8 and pre-sarcopenia. Additionally, the weighted quantile sum (WQS) model was employed to determine the combined and individual impact of LE8 metrics on pre-sarcopenia. To assess the indirect effects of peripheral immune responses on the relationships between cardiovascular health and pre-sarcopenia, mediation analyses were performed.ResultsIn this study, 827 participants had pre-sarcopenia. After accounting for potential confounding factors, the group with excellent cardiovascular health demonstrated an 83% lower risk of pre-sarcopenia compared to the poor cardiovascular health group (OR: 0.17, 95% CI: 0.11–0.27) and a 33% decreased risk of pre-sarcopenia for each 10-point increase in LE8 score (OR: 0.67, 95% CI: 0.62–0.73). Body mass index (BMI) and physical activity (PA) were the critical contributors that decreased the prevalence of pre-sarcopenia in the obese and non-obese populations, respectively.ConclusionsA negative association was found between LE8 score and pre-sarcopenia prevalence. Body mass index and physical activity are the primary contributors to the obese and non-obese populations, respectively.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-024-07911-2.Keywords: Life’s essential 8 metrics, Cardiovascular health, Pre-sarcopenia, Weighted quantile sum model, NHANES

Introduction

Despite the growing focus on cardiovascular health, cardiovascular diseases remain the leading factor behind global deaths. The high and sustained mortality and morbidity rates of cardiovascular diseases represent an urgent and significant global public health issue that requires resolution [1]. Currently, there is an immediate need for the implementation of an effective intervention strategy to control the progression of cardiovascular diseases. This strategy should be readily understandable and easily adaptable, and it should consider cost-effectiveness.

The American Heart Association (AHA) implemented Life’s Simple 7 (LS7) in 2010 as a strategy to improve cardiovascular health (CVH) and decrease disease burden [2]. LS7 is composed of four ideal health behaviours and three ideal health factors. Subsequent studies found that Life’s Simple 7 could predict adverse cardiovascular events and assist clinicians in assessing individual disease risk [3, 4]. Nevertheless, when compared to the recently established Life’s Essential 8 (LE8) in 2022, Life’s Simple 7 still exhibits limitations, including the absence of emphasis on sleep health and solely relies on the simple summation of scores for the seven indicators, lacking a more rigorous and individual-specific assessment method that takes into account individual differences.

The issue of population ageing is becoming increasingly prevalent in a global society. Against the backdrop of a growing elderly population, health problems arising from natural ageing are gradually receiving more attention. Sarcopenia is identified by a decrease in muscle mass, weakened muscle strength or physical function that happens during the ageing process, and pre-sarcopenia is determined by a lack of muscle mass [5]. The etiological factors and mechanisms of sarcopenia are multifaceted and intricate. Age-related muscle loss may be attributed to metabolic disruptions, chronic inflammation status, malnutrition, and insufficient physical activity [6]. Currently, there are no specific drugs available for the delay or treatment of sarcopenia. Therefore, the emphasis is placed on recommending therapeutic strategies that focus on improving health behaviours and factors through nutrition, exercise and other means to treat sarcopenia. For the prevention and management of sarcopenia or pre-sarcopenia, the LE8 score may be an effective strategy, considering the inclusion of four healthy behaviours and factors in sarcopenia therapeutic approaches. Several studies over the past decades have underscored the significance of inflammation in the onset of cardiovascular diseases [7–10]. Moreover, previous studies have found a positive correlation between inflammation and the heightened prevalence of sarcopenia or pre-sarcopenia among adults [11, 12].

Nevertheless, no prior research has examined the correlation between LE8 and pre-sarcopenia. Hence, the aim of this research is to explore the possible individual and joint correlation between cardiovascular health estimated by LE8 and pre-sarcopenia among U.S. adults. Additionally, we hope to determine if the connection between LE8 score and pre-sarcopenia is mediated by inflammatory markers.

Method

Study population

The NHANES is a continuous, sizeable cross-sectional survey, and subjects were selected by a complex, multistage probability sampling method to examine the health and nutritional status of the U.S. population. Since sleep health data was initially gathered during the 2005–2006 NHANES cycle and appendicular skeletal muscle mass was not assessed in the 2007–2010 NHANES cycles, this cross-sectional study utilized a decade’s worth of data from the 2005 to 2006 NHANES cycle and from 2011 to 2018 NHANES cycles as baseline data. The analysis included individuals aged 20–59 years who were without any missed dates to calculate the LE8 score and had reliable dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) (n = 9857; Additional file: Figure S1). Figure S1 showed the detailed flow chart.

Measurement of life’s essential 8 score

LE8 score (range 0-100) was the average of the scores for the 2 domains of health behaviours (healthy dietary, physical activity, smoking, sleep health) and health factors (BMI, blood lipids, blood glucose, blood pressure). LE8 scores were computed based on the mean value of each of the 8 components, whose scoring scheme was explained in accordance with the recommendations of the AHA. Detailed methods were provided in the Table S1 (Additional file: Table S1) [13]. High CVH was considered 80–100 points; moderate CVH was 50–79 points; and low CVH was 0–49 points [14].

Diet metric was evaluated by the Healthy Eating Index 2015 (HEI-2015) [15] based on 24 h-dietary recall. Data analysis included participants who provided dietary information for 2 days, while those with data for only 1 day were not included. An assessment of physical activity was based on self-reported minutes of moderate or vigorous physical activity per week. Sleep duration, nicotine exposure, drug usage, and diabetes history were also collected by self-reported questionnaires. BMI was calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. Non-HDL cholesterol was calculated as enzymatically measured total cholesterol minus HDL-C. Blood samples were collected to determine plasma glucose levels and HbA1c levels, in order to assess blood glucose control. Blood pressure was determined as the average reading over 3 consecutive measurements.

Evaluations of pre-sarcopenia

European working group on sarcopenia in older people (EWGSOP) defined the different stages of sarcopenia using low muscle mass and low muscle function. The pre-sarcopenia stage is characterized by low muscle mass without affecting muscle strength or physical performance [16]. Following the recommendations of the Foundation for the National Institutes of Health (FNIH) and previous research [5, 17], we assessed muscle mass reduction using appendicular lean mass adjusted for body mass index (ALMBMI). A lower index indicates a greater loss of muscle mass. The dual-energy x‐ray absorptiometry scans of both arms and legs were used to assess the appendicular lean mass, and BMI was measured as described above. Pre-sarcopenia was considered present when ALMBMI < 0.512 in females or < 0.789 in males pursuant.

Other variable definitions

We abstracted the baseline survey data from NHANES, including sociodemographic characteristics, alcohol consumption, medical conditions, and laboratory tests. Sociodemographic characteristics comprise age (in years continuous and categorized as < 40 or ≥ 40 years), gender (male and female), race (non-Hispanic white, non-Hispanic black, Mexican-Americans, and other races), educational attainment (college or above, high school, and less than high school) and poverty income ratio (0-1.3, 1.3-3.0, or ≥ 3.0) [13]. Alcohol use status was categorized as non-drinkers (defined as < 12 drinks in lifetime or ≥ 12 drinks in lifetime but no alcohol use in the past 12 months) or heavy drinkers (≥ 3 drinks/day in women, ≥ 4 drinks/day in men, or binge ≥ 5 day/month in the past 12 months), and mild drinkers (who did not meet the above criteria) [18]. The history of cardiovascular disease (CVD) and malignancy were collected by self-reported questionnaires. Participants were classified as having CKD if the estimate of glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was < 60 mL/min/1.73m2 or if albuminuria was present. Albuminuria was defined as a urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio of 30 mg/g or greater [19].

Imputing missing data

The missing data for drinking status and poverty income ratio are the top two, with 6.46% and 5.79%, respectively. Meanwhile, the remaining variables have an absence rate of less than 1%. To reduce the bias caused by missing values, the missing covariates were interpolated using the “missRanger” package, and the interpolated data were used for subsequent data analysis.

Statistical analysis

In order to acquire precise approximations that are indicative of the U.S. civilian population, the appropriate weights were considered in the analysis due to the NHANES review sampling design. Continuous variables were presented as means (standard error), while categorical variables were expressed as counts (weighted proportions). The study analyzed differences among different CVH groups using the design-adjusted one-way ANOVA test and the design-adjusted Rao-Scott chi-square test. The population projections from the 2000 Census were utilized to compute age-standardized rates for pre-sarcopenia.

This study employed the Restricted Cubic Spline (RCS) method with three nodes to visualize potential nonlinear associations between LE8 and pre-sarcopenia, and the anova function of the R “rms” package was used to estimate the P value for nonlinearity.

We used a survey-weighted logistics regression analysis to assess the relationship between LE8 and pre-sarcopenia. Three models were constructed to adjust for confounders. Model 1 was accounted for age, gender, and race. Model 2 was further controlled for poverty income ratio, education, and alcohol consumption. Model 3 was further adjusted for the history of CVD, CKD, and malignancy. When assessing the association between CVH (as a categorical variable) and pre-sarcopenia, the statistical significance of trends was ascertained by tests for trends which modelled the ordered CVH categories as a one-degree-of-freedom linear term.

We utilized the weighted quantile sum (WQS) regression model [20] to estimate the combined and individual impacts of Life’s Essential 8 metrics on the risk of pre-sarcopenia. We divided randomly the data into the training set (60%) and the validation set (40%) and bootstrapped the data with 1000 simulations to acquire WQS indexes. Additionally, to more carefully account for the possible confounding or modifying effects of BMI and explore which metrics were the primary factors linked to the reduced risks of pre-sarcopenia in the obese and non-obese populations, we also stratified the analysis by baseline BMI levels (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2 or < 30 kg/m2).

The potential mediating effects of circulating immune cells on the connections between CVH and the risk of pre-sarcopenia were estimated by mediation regression analysis (R package “bruceR”). The moderate and high CVH categories were combined, and the counts of circulating immune cells (leukocytes, neutrophils, and lymphocytes) were natural log-transformed before analysis. The data was bootstrapped with 5000 iterations to acquire the confidence interval of mediating effects.

We used age (< 40 or ≥ 40 years), sex, race, CVD, and CKD as subgroups to analyze the relationship between LE8 and pre-sarcopenia and to explore the interactions. Finally, we performed multiple sensitivity analyses to determine the stability of the results. First, we further adjusted for survey cycles (categorical variable), statin drugs (categorical variable), alanine transaminase, aspartate transaminase, total protein, albumin, and uric acid (all in continuous variables) to minimize the potential confounding effects of these variables. Second, although the random forest has been widely used and shown to produce low compensation errors and exemplary performance in predictive models, we excluded participants with missing covariates in order to evaluate the influence of the interpolation method on the results. Third, we used a two-piecewise logistics regression model to obtain the optimal thresholds of the LE8 score and dichotomized the thresholds (LE8 score = 82.5) to investigate the relationship between LE8 and pre-sarcopenia further. Fourth, we reanalyzed the main analyses without considering complex sampling designs.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software, version 4.2.2 (R Core Team, Vienna, Austria). P < 0.05 was statistical significance.

Associations between LE8 and its components with pre-sarcopenia

After controlling for covariates in model 3, the RCS model based on logistics regression analysis revealed a nonlinear correlation between LE8 score and pre-sarcopenia (Additional file: Figure S2). To further investigate the correlation between LE8 score and pre-sarcopenia, we performed multivariable logistic regression models (Table 2). The findings revealed that, after adjusting for potential confounding factors, a rise of 10 points in the LE8 score corresponded to a reduction in the risk of pre-sarcopenia (OR: 0.67; 95% CI: 0.62–0.73). Furthermore, multivariable logistics regression analysis demonstrated that individuals with moderate and high CVH exhibited 47% (OR: 0.53; 95% CI: 0.41–0.68) and 83% (OR: 0.17; 95% CI: 0.11–0.27) reduced risk of pre-sarcopenia, respectively, as compared to those individuals with a low CVH.

Subgroup analyses

Subgroup analysis further confirmed the strong association between LE8 and pre-sarcopenia across various subgroups (Additional file: Figure S3). Notably, this association was particularly pronounced in non-CVD individuals, as evidenced by lower hazard ratios for pre-sarcopenia (non-CVD vs. CVD, 0.65 vs. 0.93) compared to CVD participants. The interaction between LE8 and CVD status was statistically significant. Additionally, similar findings were observed in males (male vs. female, 0.61 vs. 0.71, Pinteraction < 0.05).

Sensitivity analysis

Table 3 presented the results of multiple sensitivity analyses, which examine the impact of various factors on the estimated effect of LE8. Overall, the effect estimates of LE8 remained relatively stable across the different analyses. After further adjusting for NHANES survey cycles, alanine transaminase, aspartate transaminase, total protein, albumin, uric acid, and statin, the effect estimates for pre-sarcopenia were 0.74 (95% CI: 0.68–0.80). Exclusion of the participants with missing covariates did not alter risk estimates. Compared to the lower LE8 score group (LE8 score < 82.5), the higher LE8 score group had decreased risks of pre-sarcopenia (OR: 0.26; 95% CI: 0.17–0.37). Discussion

Based on our current understanding, this study is the first to provide a fresh perspective on the connection between Life’s Essential 8 and pre-sarcopenia occurrence. The findings of this research emphasized that LE8 components serve as significant predictors of pre-sarcopenia in participants. In this research, we observed the inverse dose-response correlation between the occurrence of pre-sarcopenia and higher CVH scores evaluated by LE8, especially among males and participants without CVD. In order to prevent pre-sarcopenia in different populations with varying levels of CVH, stratified management strategies may be necessary due to the potential benefits of LE8. Clinicians can prioritize certain groups by popularizing the use of LE8, which may help reduce the incidence of pre-sarcopenia, lower the financial burden for patients with chronic diseases, and improve their condition. Life’s Essential 8 is an efficient and convenient evaluation index for the benefits of musculoskeletal health.

Sarcopenia is a condition in which muscle mass, strength, and function decline as a result of the ageing process. This disease is characterized by muscle atrophy, leading to muscle weakness, that affects the elderly’s daily activities and life quality. The lack of routine physical activity and an unhealthy diet may be the contributing factors to this condition, according to previous studies.

The prognosis of cardiovascular disease is commonly linked to sarcopenia. According to a study conducted across multiple centres, sarcopenia may increase the all-cause mortality of elderly individuals suffering in Japan [21]. Randomization research found that a reduction in muscle mass and strength may elevate the risk of CVD based on the genetic causal aspect and exacerbate its conditions [22]. A meta-analysis study demonstrated the importance of early diagnosis and intervention for individuals with sarcopenia or sarcopenia obesity, which may impact the incidence of cardio-cerebrovascular disease [23]. The findings from previous studies consistently supported the present study and were crucial in motivating physicians to play a more proactive role in preventing pre-sarcopenia or sarcopenia. LE8 score, as a novel indicator of cardiovascular health proposed by the AHA, may more conveniently and effectively help physicians evaluate the risk of sarcopenia or pre-sarcopenia, thereby reducing cardiovascular incidence and mortality and improving individual health in the future.

Previous studies revealed the link between cardiovascular health and sarcopenia. A longitudinal prospective observational study in Sub-Saharan Africa found a relationship between worsening cardiovascular health in men and poor physical function [24]. A meta-analysis found that CKD patients with sarcopenia may increase the risk of higher prevalence of cardiovascular events and mortality [25]. However, fewer studies explored whether there was a difference in the risk of sarcopenia among the three levels of CVH based on LE8 score. Our study found that individuals with higher CVH may decrease the risk of pre-sarcopenia. Therefore, improving the score from eight metrics to improve overall cardiovascular health will contribute to healthy ageing and prevent sarcopenia and its associated comorbidities.

The benefits of physical activity are highly influenced by exercise volume and intensity. The results of a prospective cohort research based on a UK biobank revealed that there are various viable strategies and approaches for promoting physical activity participation, and increasing exercise volume and intensity may potentially decline the risk of CVD [26]. The health benefits of PA with different levels of exercise volume and intensity showed a dose-dependent relationship with cardiovascular health. The underlying mechanisms may be closely associated with various factors, such as enhanced metabolic levels, myocardial regeneration, anti-inflammatory effects, and the discharge of exerkines [27]. In our study, people of normal weight spent more effort on physical activity, which may improve cardiovascular health and prevent pre-sarcopenia more efficiently. Furthermore, our findings indicated that inadequate sleep was the third significant factor contributing to pre-sarcopenia among normal-weight participants. Compared to LS7’s confined scope of health behaviours, LE8 took sleep health into account to update our cognition and recommend the optimized CVH evaluation tool. A meta-analysis indicated that elderly individuals who sleep for extended periods of time exhibit a comparatively elevated occurrence of sarcopenia [28]. A Chinese prospective study revealed that unhealthy sleep duration in older individuals might be harmful to their musculoskeletal health and develop sarcopenia with the ageing process, which was in line with our findings [29]. The underlying mechanisms of the relationship are still unclear. Previous studies found that inadequate sleep could affect the secretion of hormones in the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis and physical movement among elderly individuals [30, 31]. Overall, these results emphasize the importance of improving sleep health to prevent sarcopenia, which is a potential basis for future research.

According to our research, it is advisable for individuals who are overweight to prioritize lowering their BMI in order to reduce the chances of developing pre-sarcopenia, consistent with a multicentric study in Chile that determined the link between obesity, as defined by BMI, and the risk of sarcopenia among older people [32]. The relationship between obesity and sarcopenia may be attributed to metabolic disorders of obese populations. Obese patients often accumulate fat in subcutaneous, visceral, perivascular and other parts of the body. However, adipose tissue can secrete adipokine to induce systemic inflammation, insulin resistance and other metabolic disorders, thereby affecting musculoskeletal health or cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, resulting in adverse clinical outcomes [33]. Inflammation plays a crucial role in sarcopenia and pre-sarcopenia. Our study found that increased circulating inflammation indicators play a mediated role between LE8 score and pre-sarcopenia. A meta-analysis of 17 studies, including 11,249 participants, identified the association between inflammatory parameters and sarcopenia [34]. In 2016, an investigation conducted in Korea discovered that the occurrence rates of sarcopenia were correlated with the quartiles of leukocytes [35]. In order to attain a satisfactory overall score of LE8, it is indispensable to adhere to an anti-inflammatory lifestyle, which holds particular significance for preventing sarcopenia in obese individuals. Cultivating such a lifestyle entails various factors, such as controlling proper BMI, adhering to a nutritious eating plan, participating in consistent exercise, and ensuring adequate sleep.

Based on a large, complex, multistage probability sample of U.S. adults, this present study has yielded a crucial insight into the relationship between cardiovascular health and pre-sarcopenia. We found an inverse dose relationship that individuals with high scores on the Life’s Essential 8 CVH metrics were significantly less likely to develop pre-sarcopenia. The findings of the present study indicated that body mass index and physical activity are the primary contributors to the obese and non-obese populations, respectively. The findings indicated that initiatives targeting the reduction of pre-sarcopenia in non-obesity populations should prioritize physical activity promotion, while those targeting improving the CVH in the obesity populations should prioritize maintaining optimal BMI levels.

A notable aspect of this cohort study was its utilization of a large, complex, multistage probability sample of American individuals to explore the relationship between LE8 score and the occurrence of pre-sarcopenia. Furthermore, we explored the reverse correlation between CVH and pre-sarcopenia and identified different metrics of LE8 that contribute the most to the development of pre-sarcopenia.

The present study still has several potential limitations that should be taken into account. First, the healthy factors of LE8 collected from self-report may be inevitable to the recall bias. Second, although various confounders were considered, there were other factors that may affect the outcome that were not considered. Third, cross-sectional studies could not infer a causal relationship, and the results are based on a study of the U.S. population and may not be applicable to populations in other countries. Fourth, LE8 measures contain only baseline data and lack follow-up data to obtain changes in each metric. Fifth, because the NHANES database assessed grip strength only in certain cycles and lacked data related to gait speed, we only evaluated the relationship between LE8 and muscle mass, and could not further evaluate its relationship with muscle strength.

Conclusion

Life’s Essential 8 score is inversely associated with pre-sarcopenia. Peripheral immunological responses could partially mediate the association. Moreover, body mass index and physical activity are the primary contributors to a decreased risk of pre-sarcopenia in the obese and non-obese populations, respectively. By establishing connections between LE8 metrics and pre-sarcopenia, we aim to emphasize the potential importance of improving overall cardiovascular health in order to decrease the risk of pre-sarcopenia.

Nonlinear forward-backward-half forward splitting with momentum for monotone inclusions
Liqian Qin, Yuchao Tang, Jigen Peng

Abstract
In this work, we propose a new splitting algorithm for solving structured monotone inclusion problems composed of a maximally monotone operator, a maximally monotone and Lipschitz continuous operator and a cocoercive operator. Our method augments the forward–backward-half forward splitting algorithm with a nonlinear momentum term. Under appropriate conditions on the step-size, we prove the weak convergence of the proposed algorithm. A linear convergence rate is also obtained under the strong monotonicity assumption. Furthermore, we investigate a stochastic variance-reduced forward-backward-half forward splitting algorithm with momentum for solving finite-sum monotone inclusion problems. Weak almost sure convergence and linear convergence are also established under standard condition. Preliminary numerical experiments on synthetic datasets and real-world quadratic programming problems in portfolio optimization demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed algorithm.



Keywords:
Monotone inclusions; Forward-backward-half forward splitting algorithm; Momentum; Variance reduction.

Forward–backward-half forward splitting algorithm.

For monotone inclusion problems involving two operators, many classical algorithms have been proposed and extensively studied

In particular, for the monotone inclusion problem considered in this paper,

In their approach, while the continuous monotone operator still requires two evaluations per iteration, but reduce the cost for the cocoercive operator to a single evaluation by leveraging its cocoercivity. The FBHF splitting algorithm takes the advantage of the inherent structural properties of individual operators, providing a unified generalization of both Forward-Backward splitting algorithm and Tseng’s method. The detailed FBHF splitting algorithm is defined as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}y_{k}=J_{\gamma_{k}A}(x_{k}-\gamma_{k}(B+C)x_{k}),&\\

Warped resolvent.

Variance reduction stochastic method.

Within optimization frameworks, monotone inclusion problems are often formulated as finite-sum problems. For example, finite-sum minimization is ubiquitous in machine learning, where one typically minimizes the empirical risk
[
28
]
. Stochastic optimization plays a central role in this context. In recent years, a major advancement in this field has been the development of variance-reduction (VR) techniques for stochastic algorithms.
It is important to note that the existing results for solving problem (
1

\sum_{i=1}^{N}B_{i}x
can become computationally prohibitive in high-dimensional settings. To alleviate this significant computational burden, we consider the problem (
1

B
has a finite-sum structure. VR techniques are particularly well-suited to exploit this structure and improve algorithmic efficiency. As a result, numerous stochastic algorithms incorporating variance reduction have been developed for solving finite-sum monotone inclusion problems
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
.
To effectively address the computational challenges posed by high-dimensional data in monotone inclusion problems with a finite-sum structure, variance-reduction (VR) techniques have emerged as a powerful tool. Stochastic optimization serves as the backbone of modern machine learning, with stochastic gradient descent (SGD) being one of the pioneering algorithms in this domain
[
35
]
. Building on this foundation, VR methods such as SAGA
[
36
]
, the stochastic average gradient method (SAG)
[
37
,
38

]
have fundamentally reshaped the landscape of stochastic optimization by achieving provably faster convergence rate than SGD
[
40
]
. Among these, the stochastic variance-reduced gradient method (SVRG)

]
stands out as a representative approach that leverages control variates to reduce gradient variance. More recently, Kovalev et al.
[
42

introduced a loopless variant of SVRG, which eliminates the outer loop in the original algorithm and instead applies a probabilistic mechanism to update the full gradient, further improving practicality and efficiency.

Building upon these developments, Alacaoglu et al.
[

recently proposed a loopless variant of the extragradient method that incorporates variance-reduction techniques for solving variational inequalities in a finite dimensional Euclidean space. Specifically, they consider the following problem:

Contributions

The FBHF splitting algorithm is the first splitting algorithm proposed for solving problem (
1
). It integrates the classical forward-backward splitting algorithm with Tseng’s forward-backward-forward splitting scheme. Due to its flexibility and efficiency, it has been extended and generalized to various problem settings.
In this work, we propose a nonlinear forward-backward-half forward splitting with momentum (Algorithm
3.1
) to solve the monotone inclusion problem (
1
). Unlike existing approach
[
6
]
that require operators
M_{k}
to be the bounded linear operators,
M_{k}
in this paper are nonlinear operators. The convergence analysis in this paper imposes distinct assumptions on
M_{k}
compared to
[
6
]
, we emphasize that these assumptions are neither stronger nor weaker but provide an alternative theoretical framework with a tailored proof procedure. Furthermore, we develop a stochastic variance-reduced forward-backward-half forward splitting algorithm with momentum (Algorithm
4.1
) for the finite-sum structured problem (
9
) (a special case of (
1
)). Under appropriate parameter settings, Algorithm
4.1
reduces to the deterministic Algorithm
3.1
.
The key contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:

(i)

We propose a nonlinear forward-backward-half forward splitting with momentum for solving problem (
1
). Furthermore, weak convergence is established under the well-defined parameter condition. Under strong monotonicity conditions, we establish that the Algorithm
3.1
achieves linear convergence rate.

(ii)

Based on the characteristics of the proposed momentum-based algorithm, we not only recover several existing algorithms but also develop a new class of operator splitting algorithms for solving monotone inclusions involving the sum of four operators. This approach effectively leverages the structural properties of each operator, ensuring good convergence behavior and computational efficiency.

(iii)

We propose a stochastic variance-reduced forward-backward-half forward splitting algorithm with momentum to solve problem (
9
), where the problem possesses a finite-sum structure. In a separable real Hilbert space, the weak almost sure convergence of Algorithm
4.1
is obtained. And the linear convergence rate of Algorithm
4.1
is also given under the stronger assumption, i.e. strong monotonicity.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls some fundamental definitions and lemmas essential for subsequent analysis. In Section 3, we introduce a nonlinear forward-backward-half forward splitting algorithm with momentum for solving problem (
1
), establishing its weak convergence under standard assumptions and proving linear convergence under additional conditions of strong monotonicity. Section 4 further develops a stochastic variance-reduced variant with momentum to address problem (
9
), where we demonstrate the weak almost sure convergence and establish the linear convergence rates. Section 5 presents a series of numerical experiments conducted on both synthetic datasets and real-world quadratic programming problems arising in portfolio optimization. Finally, we summarize the main conclusions.

.

(

]

Algorithm 3.1
.

Nonlinear forward-backward-half forward with momentum

Let
S\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H}),

M_{k}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H},

\gamma_{k}>0\ (\forall k\in\mathbb{N})
and initial point
x_{0},\ u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
. For
k=0,1,2,\cdots
, do

\displaystyle y_{k}=(M_{k}+A)^{-1}(M_{k}x_{k}-(B+C)x_{k}+\gamma_{k}^{-1}u_{k}),

(15)

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=y_{k}-\gamma_{k}S^{-1}By_{k}+\gamma_{k}S^{-1}Bx_{k},

\displaystyle u_{k+1}=(\gamma_{k}M_{k}-S)y_{k}-(\gamma_{k}M_{k}-S)x_{k}.

3.1
Weak convergence

In this subsection, we present the convergence analysis for Algorithm
3.1
. Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
3.1
. For any

, we define the function

\Phi_{k}(x):=\|x_{k}-x\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2},

(16)

which helps to establish the weak convergence of the Algorithm
3.1

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that Assumption
3.1
holds. Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, and

\Phi_{k+1}(x^{*})\leq\Phi_{k}(x^{*})-(1-L_{k-1}-L_{k}-2\gamma_{k}L_{k}\mu-\gamma_{k}^{2}\mu^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2})\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

(17)

Proof.

From Algorithm
3.1
, we have

y_{k}=(M_{k}+A)^{-1}(M_{k}x_{k}-(B+C)x_{k}+\gamma_{k}^{-1}u_{k}),

which is equivalent to the inclusion

M_{k}x_{k}-M_{k}y_{k}-(B+C)x_{k}+\gamma_{k}^{-1}u_{k}\in Ay_{k}.

(18)

Since
x^{*}\in\zer(A+B+C)
, we have

-(B+C)x^{*}\in Ax^{*}.

(19)

By combining (
18
) and (
19
), and using the monotonicity of

, we obtain

0\leq\langle M_{k}x_{k}-M_{k}y_{k}-(B+C)x_{k}+\gamma_{k}^{-1}u_{k}+(B+C)x^{*},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle.

(20)

Multiplying both sides of (

2\gamma_{k}
and using the definition of
u_{k+1}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq 2\langle Sx_{k}-Sy_{k}+u_{k}-u_{k+1}-\gamma_{k}(B+C)x_{k}+\gamma_{k}(B+C)x^{*},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle

(21)

\displaystyle=2\langle Sx_{k}-Sy_{k},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+2\langle u_{k}-u_{k+1},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-2\gamma_{k}\langle Bx_{k}-Bx^{*},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle-2\gamma_{k}\langle Cx_{k}-Cx^{*},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle.

We now analyze the four inner products appearing on the right-hand side of inequality (
21
). Recalling the definition of
x_{k+1}

\displaystyle 2\langle Sx_{k}-Sy_{k},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}-\|y_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}-\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}-\|x_{k+1}+\gamma_{k}S^{-1}By_{k}-\gamma_{k}S^{-1}Bx_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}-\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}-\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}-2\gamma_{k}\langle x_{k+1}-x^{*},By_{k}-Bx_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle-\gamma_{k}^{2}\|By_{k}-Bx_{k}\|_{S^{-1}}^{2}-\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2},

and

\displaystyle 2\langle u_{k}-u_{k+1},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+2\langle u_{k},y_{k}-x_{k}\rangle-2\langle u_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle-2\gamma_{k}\langle u_{k+1},S^{-1}By_{k}-S^{-1}Bx_{k}\rangle.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+2\|u_{k}\|_{S^{-1}}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}-2\langle u_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle+2\gamma_{k}\|u_{k+1}\|_{S^{-1}}\|By_{k}-Bx_{k}\|_{S^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-2\langle u_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+2\gamma_{k}L_{k}\mu\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we get

-2\gamma_{k}\langle Cx_{k}-Cx^{*},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle\leq\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

(24)

Combining (
22
), (
23
), and (
24
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle

(25)

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\gamma_{k}\langle Bx_{k}-Bx^{*},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-2\gamma_{k}\langle x_{k+1}-x^{*},By_{k}-Bx_{k}\rangle-\gamma_{k}^{2}\|By_{k}-Bx_{k}\|_{S^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+L_{k-1}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+2\gamma_{k}L_{k}\mu\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\gamma_{k}\langle Bx_{k}-Bx^{*},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-2\gamma_{k}\langle y_{k}-\gamma_{k}S^{-1}By_{k}+\gamma_{k}S^{-1}Bx_{k}-x^{*},By_{k}-Bx_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle-\gamma_{k}^{2}\|By_{k}-Bx_{k}\|_{S^{-1}}^{2}-(1-L_{k-1}-2\gamma_{k}L_{k}\mu-\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2})\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\gamma_{k}\langle By_{k}-Bx^{*},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+\gamma_{k}^{2}\|By_{k}-Bx_{k}\|_{S^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle-(1-L_{k-1}-2\gamma_{k}L_{k}\mu-\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2})\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

Applying the monotonicity of
B
and rearranging the terms yields

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

(26)

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle-(1-L_{k-1}-L_{k}-2\gamma_{k}L_{k}\mu-\gamma_{k}^{2}\mu^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2})\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

Then (

\Phi_{k}(x)
given in (

∎

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that Assumption
3.1
holds, and there exists a constant
\epsilon>0
such that

1-L_{k-1}-L_{k}-2\gamma_{k}L_{k}\mu-\gamma_{k}^{2}\mu^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2}\geq\epsilon,

(27)

holds for all

. Then the sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by Algorithm

converges weakly to a point in
\zer(A+B+C)
.

Proof.

Using the definition of
\Phi_{k}(x)
in (
16
), the Lipschitz continuity of
\gamma_{k}M_{k}-S
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\|x_{k}-x\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}

(28)

\displaystyle\geq\|x_{k}-x\|_{S}^{2}-L_{k-1}(\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}+\|x_{k}-x\|_{S}^{2})+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-L_{k-1})\|x_{k}-x\|_{S}^{2}\geq 0,

which shows that the sequence
\{\Phi_{k}(x)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is nonnegative. Since it is also nonincreasing, it is convergent. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Summing inequality (
17
) for
k=1
to
N
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1-L_{k-1}-L_{k}-2\gamma_{k}L_{k}\mu-\gamma_{k}^{2}\mu^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2})\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}(x)-\Phi_{N+1}(x)

(29)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}(x)<+\infty.

Thanks to condition (
27
), we obtain that
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}=0
. Therefore,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Phi_{k}(x)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{k}-x\|_{S}^{2},

(30)

which implies that the sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. The sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
admits at least one weakly convergent subsequence
\{x_{k_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
x_{k_{n}}\rightharpoonup\bar{x}\in\mathcal{H}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Define

\Delta_{k}:=M_{k}x_{k}-M_{k}y_{k}-(B+C)(x_{k}-y_{k})+\gamma_{k}^{-1}u_{k},

(31)

Then, by (
18
), we have
(y_{k},\Delta_{k})\in\gra(A+B+C)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
M_{k}
is
2\gamma^{-1}
-Lipschitz continuous w.r.t.
S
,
B
is
\mu
-Lipschitz continuous w.r.t.
S
, and
C
is
\beta^{-1}
-cocoercive w.r.t.
S
, if follows that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{k}\|_{S^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|M_{k}x_{k}-M_{k}y_{k}\|_{S^{-1}}+\|(B+C)(x_{k}-y_{k})\|_{S^{-1}}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\|u_{k}\|_{S^{-1}}

(32)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\gamma}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|_{S}+(\mu+\beta)\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|_{S}+\frac{L_{k-1}}{\gamma_{k}}\|x_{k-1}-y_{k-1}\|_{S}.

From the estimates above, we concludate that
\Delta_{k_{n}}\rightarrow 0
. Furthermore, since

has full domain, it follows from Corollary 25.5 (i) in
[
44
]
that the sum

is maximally monotone. Moeover, by Lemma 2.1 in
[
46
]
and the assumption that

is maximally monotone. Then, by Lemma
2.2
, we have
(\bar{x},0)\in\gra(A+B+C)
, which implies that
\bar{x}\in\zer(A+B+C)
. Finally, applying Lemma
2.3

\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

\zer(A+B+C)
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

We present four special cases of Algorithm
3.1
.

(i)

When
B=0
, we have
\mu=0
. In this case, Algorithm
3.1
reduces to the nonlinear forward-backward splitting with momentum studied in
[
26
]
, and the condition on
\gamma_{k}
simplifies to
1-L_{k-1}-L_{k}-\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2}\geq\epsilon
, which coincides with the step-size condition proposed therein.

(ii)

When
S=\Id
and
M_{k}=\frac{\Id}{\gamma_{k}}
, we have
L_{k}=0
and
u_{k}=0
. In this case, Algorithm
3.1
simplifies to the FBHF splitting algorithm (
4
) with
X=\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, the constraint in (
27
) reduces to
2\gamma_{k}^{2}\mu^{2}+\gamma_{k}\beta\leq 2-\epsilon
, which is consistent with the upper bound of
\gamma_{k}
given in
[
6
]
.

(iii)

When
C=0
,
S=\Id
, and
M_{k}=\frac{\Id}{\gamma_{k}}
, we again have
L_{k}=0
,
u_{k}=0
, and
\beta=0
. Then Algorithm
3.1
reduces to the forward-backward-forward splitting algorithm introduced by Tseng
[
20
]
, and the step-size condition on
\gamma_{k}
is recovered accordingly.

(iv)

Suppose
A=A_{1}+A_{2}
, where
A_{1}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is maximally monotone,
A_{2}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, and
A_{1}+A_{2}
is maximally monotone. Further, assume that
S=\Id
,
\gamma_{k}\equiv\gamma
, and
M_{k}=\frac{\Id}{\gamma}-A_{2}
. Then Algorithm
3.1
becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}y_{k}=J_{\gamma A_{1}}(x_{k}-\gamma A_{2}x_{k}-\gamma Bx_{k}-\gamma Cx_{k}-\gamma(A_{2}y_{k-1}-A_{2}x_{k-1})),&\\
x_{k+1}=y_{k}-\gamma By_{k}+\gamma Bx_{k}.&\\
\end{array}\right.

(33)

In this case,
\gamma M_{k}-\Id
is
\gamma L
-Lipschitz continuous. As a result, the condition in (
27
) becomes

1-2\gamma L-2\gamma^{2}L\mu-\gamma^{2}\mu^{2}-\frac{\gamma\beta}{2}\geq\epsilon.

Algorithm (
33
) can thus be interpreted as a four-operator splitting scheme for finding a zero of the sum of a maximally monotone operator, two Lipschitz continuous operators, and a cocoercive operator.

3.2
Linear convergence

In this subsection, we establish the
R
-linear convergence of the sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by Algorithm
3.1
, under the assumption that the operator
A
is strongly monotone. An operator
A
is said to be
\rho
-strongly monotone w.r.t.
S
, for some
\rho>0
, if

\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq\rho\|x-y\|_{S}^{2},\quad\forall(x,u)\in\gra(A),\forall(y,v)\in\gra(A).

(34)

If, instead,
B
is
\rho
-strongly monotone, then one may equivalently shift the operator sum by a multiple of the identity operator, as follows

A+B=(A+\rho\Id)+(B-\rho\Id)
, without affecting maximal monotonicity. Therefore, strong monotonicity
B
also implies
R
-linear convergence of the sequence, and the convergence proof proceeds analogously.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let Assumption
3.1
hold, and suppose that the operator
A
is
\rho
-strongly monotone. Assume that there exist constants
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
such that
\gamma_{k}<\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}}
, and define

t=\min\left\{\frac{2\gamma_{k}m(1-\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{1})}{1+\frac{L_{k}}{\varepsilon_{2}}},\frac{\kappa+\nu}{L_{k}(\varepsilon_{2}+1)}\right\},

where
\kappa=1-L_{k-1}-L_{k}-2\gamma_{k}L_{k}\mu-\gamma_{k}^{2}\mu^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2},\ \nu=2\gamma_{k}^{2}\rho\mu^{2}(\gamma_{k}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})
.
Then the sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by Algorithm
3.1
converges
R
-linearly to a point
x^{*}
in
\zer(A+B+C)
.

Proof.

By applying the strong monotonicity of
A
in Lemma
3.1
, (
20
) yields the following inequality

\rho\|y_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}\leq\langle M_{k}x_{k}-M_{k}y_{k}-(B+C)x_{k}+\gamma_{k}^{-1}u_{k}+(B+C)x^{*},y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle.

(35)

And the following inequality is propagated through the proof of Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+2\gamma_{k}\rho\|y_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}

(36)

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle-(1-L_{k-1}-L_{k}-2\gamma_{k}L_{k}\mu-\gamma_{k}^{2}\mu^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{k}\beta}{2})\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

With the help of the Young’s inequality (
2\langle a,b\rangle\leq\varepsilon\|a\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|b\|^{2}
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{H},
and
\varepsilon>0
), we get

\displaystyle 2\gamma_{k}\rho\|y_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\gamma_{k}\rho\|x_{k+1}+\gamma_{k}S^{-1}By_{k}-\gamma_{k}S^{-1}Bx_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\gamma_{k}\rho(\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\gamma_{k}\langle x_{k+1}-x^{*},By_{k}-Bx_{k}\rangle+\gamma_{k}^{2}\|By_{k}-Bx_{k}\|_{S^{-1}}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\gamma_{k}\rho(1-\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{1})\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\gamma_{k}^{2}\rho\mu^{2}(\gamma_{k}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

Set

a_{k}=2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-x_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2},

b_{k}=(\kappa+\nu)\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

Combining (
36
) and (
37
) yields the following inequality

(1+2\gamma_{k}\rho(1-\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{1}))\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+a_{k+1}+b_{k}\leq\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+a_{k}.

(38)

Using Lipschitzness of
u_{k+1}
, we have

\displaystyle a_{k+1}

\displaystyle=2\langle u_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

(39)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{2}L_{k}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+\frac{L_{k}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+L_{k}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=L_{k}(\varepsilon_{2}+1)\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+\frac{L_{k}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}_{S}.

Then by the assumption that

t=\min\left\{\frac{2\gamma_{k}\rho(1-\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{1})}{1+\frac{L_{k}}{\varepsilon_{2}}},\frac{\kappa+\nu}{L_{k}(\varepsilon_{2}+1)}\right\},

we get

ta_{k+1}\leq(2\gamma_{k}\rho(1-\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{1})-t)\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}_{S}+b_{k}.

(40)

Combining (
38
) and (
40
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1+t)(\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}_{S}+a_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\gamma_{k}\rho(1-\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{1}))\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+a_{k+1}+b_{k}

(41)

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+a_{k}.

On the other hand, by (
28
), we know that there exists
k_{1}>0
such that

\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}_{S}+a_{k+1}\geq(1-L_{k})\|x_{k+1}-x\|_{S}^{2}\geq k_{1}\|x_{k+1}-x\|_{S}^{2}.

(42)

Therefore,

\displaystyle k_{1}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}_{S}

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{k+1}-x\|_{S}^{2}+a_{k+1}

(43)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+a_{k}}{1+t}\leq\ldots\leq\frac{\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+a_{1}}{(1+t)^{k}},

which shows that the sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by Algorithm
3.1
converges
R
-linearly to a point
x^{*}
in
\zer(A+B+C)
.
∎

4
Stochastic variance-reduced nonlinear forward-backward-half forward with momentum

In this section, we mainly consider the structured monotone inclusion problem (
9
) in a separable real Hilbert space, which is recalled below

\mbox{find}\ x\in\mathcal{X}\ \ \mbox{such that}\ 0\in Ax+\sum_{i=1}^{N}B_{i}x+Cx,

where
A:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{X}}
is a maximally monotone operator,
C:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is a
\frac{1}{\beta}
-cocoercive operator, and each
B_{i}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is a maximally monotone and
L_{i}
-Lipschitz operator.

Throughout this section, we assume access to an unbiased stochastic oracle
B_{\xi}
satisfying
B(x)=\mathbb{E}[B_{\xi}(x)]
. We consider using the stochastic oracle
B_{\xi}
in place of
B
to perform the half forward step in Algorithm
3.1
. This substitution leverages the computational efficiency of stochastic approximations, thereby reducing the per-iteration cost while preserving the algorithm’s convergence properties.

Algorithm 4.1
.

Stochastic variance-reduced nonlinear FBHF with momentum

Let
S\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}),

M_{k}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\ (\forall k\in\mathbb{N}),

\gamma>0
and initial point
x_{0},\ u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}.
Probability
p\in(0,1]
, probability distribution
Q
,
\lambda\geq 0
. For
k=0,1,2,\cdots
, do

\displaystyle(1-p)\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-\bar{x}_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle-(1-p-L_{k-1}-\gamma L_{k}\theta-(1-p)(\gamma L_{k}\theta+L_{k}))\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle-(p-\gamma^{2}\theta^{2}-\frac{\gamma\beta}{2}-p(\gamma L_{k}\theta+L_{k})-2\gamma^{3}\theta^{2}\mu)\|y_{k}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

On the one hand, using the assumptions
\gamma\leq\frac{\alpha}{L_{k}\theta}
,
0<p\leq 1
, and
L_{k}+\alpha\leq\frac{1-\sqrt{p}}{4}
for
\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain the following inequality:

\displaystyle 1-p-L_{k-1}-\gamma L_{k}\theta-(1-p)(\gamma L_{k}\theta+L_{k})

(72)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-p-L_{k-1}-\alpha-(1-p)(\alpha+L_{k})\geq 0.

Besides, by
\gamma=\min\{\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2\theta},\frac{p}{\beta},\frac{\alpha}{L_{k}\theta}\}
,
\mu\leq\theta
, and
L_{k}+\alpha\leq\frac{1-\sqrt{p}}{4}
for
\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle p-\gamma^{2}\theta^{2}-\frac{\gamma\beta}{2}-p(\gamma L_{k}\theta+L_{k})-2\gamma^{3}\theta^{2}\mu

(73)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle p-\frac{p}{4}-\frac{p}{2}-\alpha p-L_{k}p-\frac{p^{\frac{3}{2}}}{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p(1-\sqrt{p})}{4}-(L_{k}+\alpha)p\geq 0.

Thus,

\displaystyle(1-p+\gamma\mu)\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]+2\mathbb{E}_{k}\langle u_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle

(74)

\displaystyle+L_{k}\|y_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-p)\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-\bar{x}_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle-(1-p-L_{k-1}-\alpha-(1-p)(\alpha+L_{k}))\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle-(\frac{p(1-\sqrt{p})}{4}-(L_{k}+\alpha)p)\|y_{k}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

Set

\displaystyle d_{k}=

\displaystyle 2\langle u_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k-1}\|y_{k-1}-\bar{x}_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2},

\displaystyle e_{k}=

\displaystyle(1-p-L_{k-1}-\alpha-(1-p)(\alpha+L_{k}))\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle+(\frac{p(1-\sqrt{p})}{4}-(L_{k}+\alpha)p-\frac{c(1-p)(4+p)}{2L})\|y_{k}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2},

where
L\geq\frac{3-p}{2}
.
Then (
74
) is equivalent to the following inequality

\displaystyle(1-p+\gamma\mu)\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{k}[d_{k+1}]

(75)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-p)\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+d_{k}-e_{k}-\frac{c(1-p)(4+p)}{2L}\|y_{k}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

Using Lipschitzness of
u_{k+1}
and Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle d_{k+1}=

\displaystyle 2\langle u_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle+L_{k}\|y_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

(76)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{3}L_{k}\|y_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+\frac{L_{k}}{\varepsilon_{3}}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+L_{k}\|y_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{k}(\varepsilon_{3}+1)\|y_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+\frac{L_{k}}{\varepsilon_{3}}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}_{S}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda L_{k}(\varepsilon_{3}+1)\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+(1-\lambda)L_{k}(\varepsilon_{3}+1)\|y_{k}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{k}}{\varepsilon_{3}}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-p)L_{k}(\varepsilon_{3}+1)\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}+pL_{k}(\varepsilon_{3}+1)\|y_{k}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{k}}{\varepsilon_{3}}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}_{S}.

By the assumption that

\displaystyle c=\min\big\{\frac{\gamma\mu}{1+\frac{L_{k}}{2L\varepsilon_{3}}},

\displaystyle\frac{2L(1-p-L_{k-1}-\alpha-(1-p)(\alpha+L_{k}))}{(1-p)L_{k}(\varepsilon_{3}+1)},

(77)

\displaystyle\frac{Lp(1-\sqrt{p}-4(L_{k}+\alpha))}{2(1-p)(4+p)+2pL_{k}(\varepsilon_{3}+1)}\big\},

and
L_{k}+\alpha\leq\frac{1-\sqrt{p}}{4}
with
L_{k}\geq 0,\ \alpha\geq 0,
we have that
c\geq 0.
Then we get

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2L}d_{k+1}\leq

\displaystyle(\gamma\mu-c)\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+(1-p-L_{k-1}-\alpha-(1-p)(\alpha+L_{k}))\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

(78)

\displaystyle+(\frac{p(1-\sqrt{p})}{4}-(L_{k}+\alpha)p-\frac{c(1-p)(4+p)}{2L})\|y_{k}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2},

Taking expectation
\mathbb{E}_{k}
on (
78
) and combining (
75
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-p+c)\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]+(1+\frac{c}{2L})\mathbb{E}_{k}[d_{k+1}]

(79)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-p)\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+d_{k}-\frac{c(1-p)(4+p)}{2L}\|y_{k}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

Similar to (
70
),

\displaystyle\frac{c}{L}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{2L}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]-\frac{c}{L}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\mathbb{E}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|x_{k+1}-\omega_{k+1}\|_{S}^{2}]

(80)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2L}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]-\frac{c(1-p)}{L}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|x_{k+1}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{2L}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]-\frac{2c(1-p)(1+\gamma^{2}\theta^{2})}{L}\|y_{k}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{2L}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]-\frac{c(1-p)(4+p)}{2L}\|y_{k}-\omega_{k}\|_{S}^{2}.

Putting (
80
) into (
79
) gives us

\displaystyle(1-p+\frac{(L-1)c}{L})\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]+(1+\frac{c}{2L})\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}]

(81)

\displaystyle+(1+\frac{c}{2L})\mathbb{E}_{k}[d_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-p)\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+d_{k}.

Then using
1-p+\frac{(L-1)c}{L}\geq(1-p)(1+\frac{c}{2L})
and taking the full expectation on (
81
), yields

\displaystyle(1+\frac{c}{2L})\mathbb{E}[(1-p)\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+d_{k+1}]

(82)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(1-p)\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+d_{k}].

In addition, by (
65
), we know that

\displaystyle(1-p)\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+d_{k+1}

(83)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-p-L_{k})\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-p-L_{k})\|x_{k+1}-x\|_{S}^{2}.

Taking the full expectation
\mathbb{E}
on (
83
) and combining (
82
), we obtain

\displaystyle

\displaystyle(1-p-L_{k})\mathbb{E}\|x_{k+1}-x\|_{S}^{2}

(84)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(1-p)\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k+1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+d_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}[(1-p)\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{k}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+d_{k}]}{1+c/(2L)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ldots\leq\frac{(1-p)\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\omega_{1}-x^{*}\|_{S}^{2}+d_{1}}{(1+c/(2L))^{k}},

which implies that (
66
). This completes the proof.

5.1
Nonlinear constrained optimization problems

In this subsection, we consider the following nonlinear constrained optimization problem, which constitutes an important application of the monotone inclusion problem (
1
):

\min_{x\in C}f(x)+h(x),

(85)

where
C=\{x\in\mathcal{H}|(\forall i\in\{1,...,q\})\ g_{i}(x)\leq 0\}
,
f:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
is a proper, convex and lower semi-continuous function, for every
i\in\{1,...,q\}
,
g_{i}:\dom(g_{i})\subset\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and
h:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are
C^{1}
convex functions in
\nt\dom g_{i}
and
\mathcal{H}
, respectively, and
\nabla h
is
\beta
-Lipschitz. The solution to problem (
85
) corresponds to the saddle points of the Lagrangian

L(x,u)=f(x)+h(x)+u^{\top}g(x)-\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q}}(u),

where
\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q}}
denotes the indicator function of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q}
. Under standard qualification conditions, the saddle points can be characterized as solutions to the following monotone inclusion problem
[
47
,
6
]
: find
x\in Y
such that
\exists u\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q}
,

(0,0)\in A(x,u)+B(x,u)+C(x,u),

(86)

where
Y\subset\mathcal{H}
is a nonempty closed convex set modeling the prior information of the solution. The operators are defined as follows:
A:(x,u)\mapsto\partial f(x)\times{N_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q}}u}
, which is maximally monotone;
B:(x,u)\mapsto(\sum_{i=1}^{q}u_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x),-g_{1}(x),...,-g_{q}(x))
, which is nonlinear, monotone and continuous, and
C:(x,u)\mapsto(\nabla h(x),0)
is
\frac{1}{\beta}
-cocoercive.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
f=\iota_{[0,1]^{N}}
,
g_{i}(x)=d_{i}^{\top}x
(
\forall i\in\{1,...,q\}
) with
d_{1},\ldots,d_{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
h(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|Gx-b\|^{2}
with
G
being an
m\times N
real matrix,
N=2m
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then the operators in (
86
) become

\displaystyle A:(x,u)\mapsto\partial{\iota_{[0,1]^{N}}(x)}\times{N_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q}}u},

(87)

\displaystyle B:(x,u)\mapsto(D^{\top}u,-Dx),

\displaystyle C:(x,u)\mapsto(G^{\top}(Gx-b),0),

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
u\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q}
,
D=[d_{1},\ldots,d_{q}]^{\top}
. It is easy to see that the operator
A
is a maximally monotone operator,
B
is a
L
-Lipschitz operator with
L=\|D\|
, and
C
is a
\frac{1}{\beta}
-cocoercive operator with
\beta=\|G\|^{2}
. Therefore, problem (
86
) can be solved by FBHF splitting algorithm (
4
).

On the other hands, we decompose
B
into
B_{1}+B_{2}
, where

\displaystyle B_{1}:(x,u)\mapsto(\frac{1}{2}D^{\top}u,-\frac{1}{2}Dx),

(88)

\displaystyle B_{2}:(x,u)\mapsto(\frac{1}{2}D^{\top}u,-\frac{1}{2}Dx).

We know that
A+B_{1}
is maximally monotone,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are both
\frac{L}{2}
-Lipschitz operator with
L=\|D\|
. Therefore, problem (
86
) can also be solved by Algorithm (
33
).

In the numerical experiments, the matrices
G,D
, and the vector
b
, as well as initial values
(x_{0},u_{0})
, are all randomly generated. In the FBHF, we set
\gamma_{k}=0.9\chi
where
\chi=\frac{4}{\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+16L^{2}}}
. For Algorithm (
33
), the step-size is chosen as
\gamma_{k}=0.9\bar{\gamma}
, with
\bar{\gamma}=\frac{(-\beta/2+2L)+\sqrt{(\beta/2+2L)^{2}+12L^{2}}}{6L^{2}}
. We use

E_{k}=\frac{\|(x_{k+1}-x_{k},u_{k+1}-u_{k})\|}{\|(x_{k},u_{k})\|}<10^{-6},

as the stopping criterion. We test eight problem sizes, each with ten randomly generated instances. The average number of iterations (denoted as “av.iter”) and the average CPU time in seconds (denoted as “av.time”) over the ten instances are reported in Table
1
. The results demonstrate that Algorithm (
33
) outperforms FBHF in both iteration count and computational time.

Portfolio optimization problem

This subsection focuses on the classical mean-variance model for minimizing portfolio risk. Given a portfolio consists of
n
different assets, and the rate of return of asset
i
is a random variable with an expected value
m_{i}
. The objective is to determine the investment weights
x_{i}
that minimize risk, subject to achieving a specified minimum expected portfolio return. Let
H
represent the covariance matrix of the asset returns. The size of
H
is
225\times 225
, and
\|H\|=0.2263
. Specifically, the portfolio optimization problem is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in R^{225}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Hx,

(89)

\displaystyle s.t.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{75}x_{i}\geq 0.3,\ \sum_{i=76}^{150}x_{i}\geq 0.3,\ \sum_{i=151}^{225}x_{i}\geq 0.3,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{225}m_{i}x_{i}\geq r,\ \sum_{i=1}^{225}x_{i}=1,0\leq x_{i}\leq 1,i=1,\cdots,225.

Define
g(x)=Dx+b=

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}-m_{1}&\cdots&-m_{75}&-m_{76}&\cdots&-m_{150}&-m_{151}&\cdots&-m_{225}\\
-1&\cdots&-1&0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&0\\

x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{225}\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}r\\
0.3\\
0.3\\
0.3\end{array}\right]

(90)

and

89
) can be found via the saddle points of the Lagrangian function

L(x,u)=\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Hx+\iota_{C_{2}}(x)+u^{\top}g(x)-\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}(u).

Under some standard qualifications, it can be obtained by solving a monotone inclusion problem: find
x\in\mathbb{R}^{225}
such that
\exists u\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}
,

(0,0)\in A(x,u)+B(x,u)+C(x,u),

(91)

where

\displaystyle A:(x,u)\mapsto\partial{\iota_{C_{2}}(x)}\times{N_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}u},

(92)

\displaystyle B:(x,u)\mapsto(D^{\top}u,-Dx-b),

\displaystyle C:(x,u)\mapsto(Hx,0).

It is easy to see that the operator
A
is a maximally monotone operator,
B
is a
L
-Lipschitz operator with
L=\|D\|
, and
C
is a
\frac{1}{\beta}
-cocoercive operator with
\beta=\|H\|
. Therefore, problem (
89
) can be solved by FBHF splitting algorithm (
4
). Similar to (
88
), we can decompose
B
into
B_{1}+B_{2}

86

33
).

6
Conclusions

Monotone inclusion problems have emerged as a pivotal framework in applied mathematics, with significant applications across computational domains including signal processing, computer vision, and machine learning. In this paper, we introduced a novel splitting algorithm for solving (
1
), which extended the FBHF splitting algorithm by incorporating a nonlinear momentum term. We established the weak convergence of the algorithm under appropriate step-size conditions and proved a linear convergence rate under the strong monotonicity assumption. We further developed a stochastic variance-reduced version of the algorithm with momentum, tailored for solving finite-sum monotone inclusion problems. Under standard conditions, we proved both weak almost sure convergence and linear convergence of the stochastic algorithm. Preliminary numerical experiments on nonlinear constrained optimization problems and portfolio optimization problems are presented to demonstrate the computational efficacy of the proposed algorithms.

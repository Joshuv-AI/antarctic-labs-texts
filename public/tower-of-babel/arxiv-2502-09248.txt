Sequential Covariance Fitting for InSAR Phase Linking
Dana EL HAJJAR, Guillaume GINOLHAC, Yajing YAN, and Mohammed Nabil EL KORSO

Abstract
Traditional Phase-Linking algorithms are known for their high cost, especially with the huge volume of Synthetic Aperture Radar images generated by Sentinel- 1 1 Synthetic Aperture Radar missions. Recently, a COvariance Fitting Interferometric Phase Linking approach has been proposed, which can be seen as a generic framework for existing Phase-Linking methods. Although this method is less computationally expensive than traditional PL approaches, COvariance Fitting Interferometric Phase Linking exploits the entire covariance matrix, which poses a challenge with the increasing time series of Synthetic Aperture Radar images. However, COvariance Fitting Interferometric Phase Linking, like traditional Phase-Linking approaches, cannot accommodate the efficient inclusion of newly acquired Synthetic Aperture Radar images. This paper overcomes this drawback by introducing a sequential integration of a block of newly acquired Synthetic Aperture Radar images. Specifically, we propose a method for effectively addressing optimization problems associated with phase-only complex vectors on the torus based on the Majorization-Minimization framework.

From above, it is clear that the estimation of the covariance matrix plays an important role.
An alternative of the Maximum Likelihood Estimator approach was proposed in
[
11
]
, called COvariance Matching Estimation Technique (COvariance Matching Estimation Technique), often requiring less computational effort while maintaining similar asymptotic properties. They consist of a set of statistical methods used for parameter estimation by matching the empirical and theoretical covariance matrices. They gained attention in the signal processing field due to their efficiency in solving various estimation problems. Covariance fitting approach was used in InSAR in
[
12
]
, namely COvariance Fitting Interferometric Phase Linking (

The main idea is to refine the structure of a covariance matrix estimator by minimizing a projection criterion. COvariance Fitting Interferometric Phase Linking can be seen as a generalization of most of the existing Phase-Linking problems
[
12
]
while maintaining a lower cost than traditional Phase-Linking methods. The problem centers on two key components: the chosen method for covariance matrix estimation and the selected matrix distance. These factors can significantly influence the overall performance. Various covariance matrix plug-in can be used such as the Sample Covariance Matrix, the Phase-Only Sample Covariance Matrix (Phase-Only Sample Covariance Matrix), Tyler estimator, etc.   Additionally, regularization techniques can also be adopted. Although theoretically less effective,
COFI-PL
has demonstrated strong and promising results in practice with real-world data
[
12
]
. A key advantage of this approach is its robustness, which stems from to the choice of the covariance matrix plug-in.

However, when a new image or several new images are added, traditional methods become computationally intensive because they depend on the estimation of the full covariance matrix. This matrix increases with each new Synthetic Aperture Radar image, necessitating a complete re-estimation every time a new image is introduced. As a result, traditional methods struggle to manage this growth, imposing significant constraints on real-time processing of
SAR
image time series. This often leads to inefficiencies and may fall short of operational requirements.

To the best of our knowledge, only few studies addressed the aforementioned issue. Specifically, a sequential Phase-Linking approach was proposed in
[
13
]
, namely, Sequential Phase Linking based on Maximum Likelihood Estimation (
S-MLE-PL
), which incorporates each newly acquired Synthetic Aperture Radar image with a much lower computational cost while keeping the same performances as Phase Linking based on Maximum Likelihood Estimation. Despite the good performance of this approach and the reduction in computation time, it incorporates new Synthetic Aperture Radar images one by one within a Gaussian model framework.
A sequential approach, proposed in
[
14
]
, is able to incorporate a bloc of new images. It is based on treating each mini-stack by applying the classic Phase-Linking and then compressing it into a single virtual image through Principal Component Analysis (Principal Component Analysis). Each obtained virtual image is then connected to the next mini-stack. This method uses a partial coherence matrix, which can limit the amount of information available for analysis and reduce the accuracy of the estimates.

In this paper, we aim to develop a sequential approach that incorporates a stack of new Synthetic Aperture Radar images, based on COvariance Fitting Interferometric Phase Linking framework. We employ two matrix distances : Kullback-Leibler (
KL
) divergence and the Frobenius Norm, along with various covariance matrix estimators and potential regularization techniques. For each problem, we propose a Majorization-Minimization algorithm to solve it. Results from simulations and real data demonstrate comparable outcomes to the benchmark, namely the offline approach COvariance Fitting Interferometric Phase Linking.

Between two centered Gaussian distributions, the Kullback-Leibler divergence has the following form

\displaystyle KL(\mathcal{CN}(0,\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1})

\displaystyle\parallel\mathcal{CN}(0,\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}))

\displaystyle=\text{tr}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}^{-1}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1})+\log(|\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}^{-1}|)-l

II-B
2
Frobenius norm

The Frobenius norm (also called Euclidean distance) of a matrix is defined as the sum of the absolute squares of its elements. The Frobenius norm between two symmetric matrices is defined as

d^{2}_{F}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2})=||\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}-\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}||^{2}_{F}

(5)

By setting
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}=\mathbf{\widetilde{\Sigma}}

f^{\text{FN}}_{\mathbf{\widetilde{\Sigma}}}(\mathbf{\widetilde{w}}_{\theta})=-2\mathbf{\widetilde{w}}_{\theta}^{H}(\mathbf{\widetilde{\Psi}}\circ\mathbf{\widetilde{\Sigma}})\mathbf{\widetilde{w}}_{\theta}\\

(6)

As it appears, the main interest between the two objective functions is the absence of the covariance matrix inversion in equation (

Shrinkage to identity

As known, most covariance matrix estimators are unsatisfactory for large covariance estimation problems since they require huge sample support. In standard Phase-Linking approaches, there is a significant chance of needing to invert a matrix such as Sample Covariance Matrix. Each of these two methods has its advantages, but importantly, both ultimately lead to similar results

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a novel sequential approach based on covariance fitting interferometric Phase-Linking. The proposed framework accommodates the Kullback-Leibler divergence and the Frobenius norm as well as various plug-in for the covariance matrix estimation, which, through different regularization methods, ensure the robustness of the approach. We provide a Majorization-Minimization algorithm to solve the different optimization problems. Numerical experiments and real-world study shows the efficiency of the proposed approach in terms of performances and computation time.

\mathbf{\bar{w}}_{\theta}\end{pmatrix}

\mathbf{\bar{w}}_{\theta}\end{pmatrix}

Ground state solutions of a class of ( 2 , q ) (2,q) -Laplacian Schrödinger equations with inhomogeneous nonlinearity
Ying Huang, Tingjian Luo, Youde Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we systematically investigate the ground state solutions of a class of ( 2 , q ) (2,q) -Laplacian Schrödinger equations with inhomogeneous nonlinearity. By analyzing global and local constrained variational problems, we establish the existence, non-existence, and asymptotic behavior of ground states, addressing the mass-subcritical, mass-critical, and mass-supercritical regimes. As a byproduct, we prove a multiplicity of bound states with prescribed mass. Some of our existence results are sharp. The proofs are based primarily on constrained variational techniques.

1.
Introduction

In the paper,we are concerned with the following
(2,q)
-Laplacian Schrödinger equations with inhomogeneous nonlinearity:

(1.1)

-\triangle u-\triangle_{q}u=\lambda u+\frac{|u|^{p-2}u}{|x|^{b}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\triangle_{q}u=div(|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)
, with
q>2
,
N\geq 1,0<b<\min\{2,N\}
and
2<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
. Here,
\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}:=\frac{q(N-b)}{N-q}
if
q<N
, and
\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}:=+\infty
if
q\geq N
. Such stationary equations stems from the study of the general reaction-diffusion system:

(1.2)

\partial_{t}\phi(t,x)-\Delta_{r}\phi(t,x)-\Delta_{q}\phi(t,x)=f(x,\phi(t,x)),\quad(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

which has been widely applied on the chemical reaction, plasma physics and biophysics. In those models, the wave function
\phi(t,x)
describes a concentration; the
(r,q)
-Laplacian operator, defined as
-\Delta_{r}\phi-\Delta_{q}\phi
, arises in the physical systems where nonstandard diffusion processes occur. Such equations model phenomena with double-phase heterogeneity, where two distinct diffusion mechanisms (with exponents
r
and
q
) compete or coexist
[
2
,
18
]
. For instance, in quantum physics, they describe Bose-Einstein condensates with spatially dependent interactions, where
r
and
q
reflect different regimes of particle dispersion
[
20
]
. In nonlinear optics,
(r,q)
-Laplacian equations govern electromagnetic wave propagation in composite materials with anisotropic refractive indices. The inhomogeneous nonlinearity
f(x,\phi)
further accounts for spatially localized potentials or nonuniform media
[
1
,
22
]
. From a variational viewpoint, the interplay between
r
- and
q
-growth conditions leads to rich mathematical structures, including bifurcations of ground states and multiplicity of solutions—key for understanding phase transitions in such systems. The
(r,q)
-framework thus bridges mathematical analysis with multiscale physical modeling, where nonhomogeneous diffusion is essential.

Considering the stationary solutions of Eq. (
1.2
), leads to the following time-independent equation:

(1.3)

-\Delta_{r}u(x)-\Delta_{q}u(x)=f(x,u),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

In
[
17
]
, Cherfils and Il’yasov considered the existence and non-existence of solutions of Eq. (
1.3
) on bounded domain, which exposit some essential differences between the
(r,q)
-laplacians equation and single Laplacian equation. Since then, using mainly the variational method, Eq. (
1.3
) has been intensively studied. See for example,
[
4
]
for the existence of infinitely many weak solutions with negative energy, and
[
5
]
for infinitely many solutions with positive energy. See also the early work on the existence and multiplicity of week solutions under general assumption on the nonlinearity in
[
26
,
27
]
, and also
[
3
,
25
]
. The reference concerned with the study of
(p,q)
-Laplacian equations is huge and rich, here we refer the readers to the recent work
[
4
,
37
,
41
]
and the reference therein.

Let
r=2
, then Eq. (
1.3
) is reduced to a
(2,q)
-Laplacian equation. In bounded domain, with a Dirichlet boundary condition, Benci et. al.
[
13
]
initially considered its eigenvalue problem. Later, Papageorgiou et. al. established a serious of work on the existence and multiplicity of solutions with precise sign information, see
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
.

In this paper, we aim to investigate the solutions of Eq. (
1.1
) with a prescribed
L^{2}
-norm. Specifically, for a given mass
c>0
, we find a pair
(\lambda_{c},u_{c})
that serves as a solution to Eq. (
1.1
). Such solutions are of particular significance in certain physical applications. This is because the quantity represents the mass, which remains conserved throughout the time evolution described by Eq. (
1.2
). In the existing literature, solutions with a prescribed
L^{2}
-norm are often referred as “
normalized solutions
”. Over the past four decades, normalized solutions have garnered an increasingly growing amount of attention. By the variational method, when aiming to find a normalized solution of Eq. (
1.1
) for a given
c>0
, we consider the constrained critical points of the following functional:

(1.4)

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx,

on the
L^{2}
-constraint:

S(c):=\{u\in X:\int|u|^{2}dx=c\},

where
X:=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap D^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In this case, the parameter
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
appears as the corresponding Lagrange Multiplier, which is a part of unknown. Here we fucus on the ground state solutions with prescribed
L^{2}
-norm, which is in the following sense:

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
u_{c}\in S(c)
is
a ground state solution
to Eq. (
1.1
), if it is a solution having minimal energy
among all the solutions which belong to
S(c)
. Namely,

I(u_{c})=\inf\{I(u)\ |\ u\in S(c),(I|_{S(c)})^{\prime}(u)=0\}.

When
b=0
, the nonlinearity is homogeneous. The reference
[
15
]
seems to be the first trying on the study of
L^{2}
-normalized solutions for the
(2,q)
-Laplacian equation with homogeneous nonlinearity. Precisely, the authors use the concentration-compactness principle and the constrained variational method to prove the existence, non-existence, and multiplicity of normalized solutions of Eq. (
1.1
) with
b=0
. We also note a recent work
[
21
]
for the study of
L^{2}
-normalized solutions for the
(2,q)
-Laplacian equation with logarithmic nonlinearity
f(x,u)=u\log|u|^{2}
. In
[
21
]
, the authors established the existence and multiplicity of
L^{2}
-normalized solutions.

When
b\neq 0
, the corresponding nonlinearity is referred as inhomogeneous one, which having more meanings in application, for example, in nonlinear optics, the inhomogeneous nonlinearity appears in some models as the corrections to the nonlinear power-law response
[
1
]
. Also, in some case, the inhomogeneous nonlinearity causes new phenomena compared with the homogeneous case, see
[
38
]
, the inhomogeneous nonlinearity may induce to the spontaneous symmetry breaking of solutions. In view of the importance of the inhomogeneous case, our main motivation of this paper is to establish systematically the existence and multiplicity of the
L^{2}
-normalized solutions of Eq. (
1.1
), under different assumptions with respect to the parameters in the equations. In analysis, as we shall see, it is not a analogous with the case
b=0
. In fact, we develop some new elements which may be used to improve some results in
[
15
]
. Also some of our existence results are described sharply.

Before giving our main results, let us recall the study of the following
L^{q}
-normalized problem (where
q>2
):

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\triangle u-\triangle_{q}u=\lambda|u|^{q-2}u+\frac{|u|^{p-2}u}{|x|^{b}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\int|u|^{q}dx=c,\ c>0.\\
\end{array}\right.

The first study of such type of problems should refer to
[
24
]
, where the authors proved the existence and asymptotic behavior of
L^{q}
-normalized solutions for a class of purely
p
-Laplacian equations with the homogeneous nonlinearity of mass-critical growth. Also recently, Cai and Rădulescu proved in
[
18
]
the existence of
L^{q}
-normalized solutions under more general assumption on the homogeneous nonlinearity. More studies on the
L^{q}
-normalized solutions for the equations involving the
p
-Laplcian can be referred to the reference given in
[
18
]
, see also a very recent work
[
40
]
for the case with power nonlinearity. We remark that,
\lambda|u|^{q-2}u
as
q=2
in Eq. (
1.5
) (namely the case in Eq. (
1.1
)) is called in the reference the chemical potential, which has more important applications, see e.g.
[
16
]
. Also, the analysis on the compactness of the
L^{2}
-normalized problem is more complicated than the
L^{q}
-normalized one. In other words, using the same ideas of the proofs, one could extend the results in this paper without further essential difficulties for Eq. (
1.5
).

When it comes to apply the variational method, as an initial and fundamental step, we are required to establish the inhomogeneous
L^{q}
-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality with sharp constant, which is crucial to establish some sharp existence results in this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Sharp inhomogeneous
L^{q}
-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality) Assume that
2<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
, with
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
,
N\geq 1
,
q\geq 2
, then there exists a sharp constant
\mathcal{K}_{N,p,q}:=\frac{p}{\|Q_{p,q}\|_{2}^{p-2}}>0
, such that

(1.6)

\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\leq\mathcal{K}_{N,p,q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}}\|u\|_{2}^{p-\sigma_{p,q}},\quad\forall u\in D^{1,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\sigma_{p,q}:=\frac{q[N(p-2)+2b]}{N(q-2)+2q}
, and
Q_{p,q}
is a fixed ground state solution of

(1.7)

\sigma_{p,q}\triangle_{q}Q+(p-\sigma_{p,q})Q-|x|^{-b}|Q|^{p-2}Q=0.

Moreover, the equality in (
1.6
) holds if
u=Q_{p,q}
. In particular, let
q=2
, then (
1.6
) is reduced to

(1.8)

\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\leq\mathcal{K}_{N,p,2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2N+2b-(N-2)p}{2}},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

Due to (
1.6
), one could verify easily that the functional
I(u)
is of class
C^{1}
on
S(c)
. Thus, for given
c>0
, to find a ground state type of critical point of the functional
I(u)
on
S(c)
, when the functional is bounded from below, it is natural to consider the following global minimization problem:

(1.9)

\displaystyle m(c):=\inf_{u\in S(c)}I(u),\ c>0.

It is standard to verify that a minimizer of
m(c)
is indeed a ground state solution of Eq. (
1.1
), see for example
[
16
]
. As we shall see, the existence of minimizers of
m(c)
strongly relies on the parameters
p,N,c
.

Denote by
p_{q}^{*}:=\frac{2(q-b)}{N}+q
the mass critical exponent which is in the sense that the functional is bounded from below as
p<p_{q}^{*}
and unbounded from below as
p>p_{q}^{*}
, see Lemma
3.1
and Remark
3.1
, and let

(1.10)

c_{1}^{*}:=\inf\{c>0\mid m(c)<0\},

then our first result is concerned with the sharp existence of minimizers of
m(c)
.

Theorem 1.2

(Mass subcritical case)
.

Assume that
2<p<p_{q}^{*}
, with
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
,
N\geq 1
, and
q>2
. Then,

(1)

If
2<p<\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2
, then for all
c>0
,
m(c)
admits a minimizer;

(2)

If
p=\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2
, then
0<c_{1}^{*}<+\infty
, and
m(c)
admits a minimizer
if and only if

c>c_{1}^{*}
;

(3)

If
\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2<p<p_{q}^{*}
, then
0<c_{1}^{*}<+\infty
, and
m(c)
admits a minimizer
if and only if

c\geq c_{1}^{*}
.

In addition, any minimizer of
m(c)
can be assumed to be non-negative, radially symmetric and radially decreasing with respect to some point.

Remark 1.1
.

To prove the existence of minimizers of
m(c)
, it is essential to prove that
m(c)<0
, which helps to rule out the vanishing and dichotomy of a minimizing sequence of
m(c)
. Then the concentration-compactness principle derives the compactness of the minimizing sequence. Note particularly that in the critical case
c=c_{1}^{*}
, Lemma
3.1
shows that
m(c_{1}^{*})=0
, the former strategy does not work, instead, we use some approximation arguments, saying
c_{k}\searrow c_{1}^{*}
, with
m(c_{k})<0
having a minimizer
u_{k}
, then by showing the convergence of
u_{k}
, we have the limit function as a minimizer of
m(c_{1}^{*})
. However, it should be distinguished the difference that, except for the critical case
c=c_{1}^{*}
, we have proved that any minimizing sequence of
m(c)
is pre-compact, which then implies the orbital stability of solutions in the sense of Cazenave and Lions
[
16
]
, for the corresponding Cauchy problem.

Concerned with the mass critical case, we prove the non-attain of
m(c)
.

Theorem 1.3

(Mass critical case)
.

Assume that
p=p_{q}^{*}
, with
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
,
N\geq 1
, and
q>2
. Then, there exists
c_{2}^{*}>0
which is given precisely by (
4.3
), such that the functional
I(u)
has no any critical points for all
c\leq c_{2}^{*}
. In particular, for any
c>0
,
m(c)
has no minimizers.

Remark 1.2
.

In Lemma
4.1
, we show that

(1.11)

m(c)=\begin{cases}0,&0<c\leq c_{2}^{*};\\
-\infty,&c>c_{2}^{*}.\end{cases}

Though in this case for any
c>0
,
m(c)
has no minimizers, we can observe from (
4.6
) that when
c>c_{2}^{*}
, there is a mountain pass geometry of
I(u)
on
S(c)
, then one could expect in this case the existence of a mountain-pass type of solution.

In what follows, we consider the mass-supercritical case,
p>p_{q}^{*}
, where as mentioned above the functional is unbounded from below, then we had to turn to consider other type of critical points of
I(u)
on
S(c)
. Inspired by the work of
[
8
,
29
,
33
]
, we consider the following local type minimization problem:

(1.12)

\gamma(c):=\inf_{u\in V(c)}I(u),\ c>0,

where
V(c):=\{u\in S(c)\ :\ P(u)=0\}
, with

(1.13)

P(u):=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

In Lemma
2.2
, we show that if
u
is a weak solution of Eq. (
1.1
), then necessarily
P(u)=0
. Therefore, “
P(u)=0
” is referred as a variant of Pohozaev identity. In addition, from Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
, we see that for all
c>0
,
V(c)\neq\emptyset
, hence
\gamma(c)
is well-defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
.

Theorem 1.4

(Mass supercritical case)
.

Assume that
p_{q}^{*}<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
. Then
\gamma(c)
admits a minimizer, provided that
p,q
and
c
satisfy one of the following conditions:

(1)

If
N=1,2
,
p_{q}^{*}<p<+\infty
, and
c>0
;

(2)

If
N\geq 3
with
q<\frac{2(N^{2}-2b)}{N^{2}-4}
,
p_{q}^{*}<p<2_{b}^{*}:=\frac{2(N-b)}{(N-2)}
, and
c>0
.

In addition, any minimizer of
\gamma(c)
can be assumed to be non-negative, radially symmetric and radially decreasing with respect to some point. Moreover, for a minimizer
u_{c}
of
\gamma(c)
, there exists
\lambda_{c}<0
, such that
(\lambda_{c},u_{c})
solves Eq. (
1.1
), and

(1.14)

\displaystyle I(u_{c})\to+\infty,\ \mbox{and }\ \lambda_{c}\to-\infty,\ \mbox{as }\ c\to 0^{+}.

(1.15)

\displaystyle I(u_{c})\to 0,\ \mbox{and }\ \lambda_{c}\to 0^{-},\ \mbox{as }\ c\to\infty.

Remark 1.3
.

The main idea of the proof is to show the compactness of a minimizing sequence of
\gamma(c)
. However, compared with the case of
m(c)
in Theorem
1.2
, here it is more delicate since there additional information
P(u)=0
is involved. To show that the limit function of a minimizing sequence (up to a subsequence if necessary), locates on

, one needs to prove the strictly decreasing of the mapping:

. To this aim, we prove in Lemma
5.5
that if its corresponding Lagrange multiplier is negative, then the strictly decreasing holds. It is where the conditions
(1)(2)
of Theorem
1.4
are proposed. See Lemma
5.4
for the details.

Remark 1.4
.

Let us note that, when
N\geq 3
,
p_{q}^{*}<2_{b}^{*}
if and only if
2<q<\frac{2(N^{2}-2b)}{N^{2}-4}
. When
2_{q}^{*}\leq p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}

admits a minimizer as

small. However, a rigours proof is still open for us. In Remark

To improve further the condition of
(2)
, one may need to know whether the uniqueness or regularity of positive solutions to the zero-mass problem of Eq. (
1.1
) (i.e.

) holds or not. Once we know that the positive solution of the zero-mass problem of Eq. (

) is unique, or solutions do not belong to

, then the conditions on

can be given sharply for the existence of minimizers of
\gamma(c)
. In this direction, there have already been some trying. We refer the readers to the recent work
[

]
and
[

]
for other models. However, since here mixing a Laplacian operator and

-Laplaican one with different rates, the situation is more complicated than the one in

As a byproduct of some elements developed in the mass-supercritical case, inspired mainly by the work of Bartsch et.al

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that

, and

(1)

If

(2)

Similar results have been established for the Schrödinger-Poisson-Slater equations in

2.
Some Preliminaries

In this section, we give some preliminaries. Inspired by the work of Genoud
[
23
]
, we first prove the following sharp inhomogeneous type of
L^{q}
- Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, which is interesting by itself but also essential for various estimates in our proofs.

Lemma 2.1

(Sharp inhomogeneous
L^{q}
-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality)
.

Assume that
2<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
, with
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
,
N\geq 1
,
q\geq 2
, then there exists a sharp constant
\mathcal{K}_{N,p,q}:=\frac{p}{\|Q_{p,q}\|_{2}^{p-2}}>0
, such that

(2.1)

\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\leq\mathcal{K}_{N,p,q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}}\|u\|_{2}^{p-\sigma_{p,q}},\quad\forall u\in D^{1,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\sigma_{p,q}:=\frac{q[N(p-2)+2b]}{N(q-2)+2q}
, and
Q_{p,q}
is a fixed ground state solution of

(2.2)

\sigma_{p,q}\triangle_{q}Q+(p-\sigma_{p,q})Q-|x|^{-b}|Q|^{p-2}Q=0.

Moreover, the equality in (
2.1
) holds if
u=Q_{p,q}
. In particular, let
q=2
, then (
2.1
) is reduced to

(2.3)

\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\leq\mathcal{K}_{N,p,2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2N+2b-(N-2)p}{2}},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

Proof.

To prove (
2.1
), we define the following Weinstein-type functional:

J(u):=\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}}\|u\|_{2}^{p-\sigma_{p,q}}}{\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}dx},\quad\forall u\in D^{1,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}.

Note that
p-\sigma_{p,q}=\frac{2[(N-b)q-(N-q)p]}{(N+2)q-2N}>0
if
p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
. Hence, it is easy to verify that
J(u)
is of class
C^{1}
, and
J(u)\geq 0
in
D^{1,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}
.

Define

(2.4)

m:=\inf_{u\in D^{1,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}}J(u),

then clearly
m\geq 0
. We shall show that
m>0
is attained by some non-trivial function. As a first step, let
\{u_{n}\}
be a minimizing sequence of
m
. Since
J(|u|)\leq J(u)
, hence
u_{n}
can be assumed to be non-negative. Thus we can denote by
\{u_{n}^{*}\}
the Schwartz-symmetrization of
\{u_{n}\}
, and by the Polya-Szegö inequality and Lemma
7.2
, we have
J(u_{n}^{*})\leq J(u_{n})
. This means that
\{u_{n}^{*}\}
is also a minimizing sequence of
m
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\{u_{n}\}
is non-negative, radial and radially decreasing.

Now for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we set

v_{n}:=\lambda_{n}u_{n}(\eta_{n}x),

with

\lambda_{n}:=\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{\frac{2(N-q)}{(N+2)q-2N}}}{\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{q}^{\frac{Nq}{(N+2)q-2N}}},\quad\eta_{n}:=(\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{2}}{\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{q}})^{\frac{2q}{(N+2)q-2N}}.

Then
\|v_{n}\|_{2}=\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{q}=1
and
J(v_{n})=J(u_{n})
. Clearly,
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
D^{1,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then there exists
v^{*}\in D^{1,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
v^{*}\neq 0
, such that
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v^{*}
in
D^{1,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and by Lemma
7.1
,
\int|x|^{-b}|v_{n}|^{p}dx\rightarrow\int|x|^{-b}|v^{*}|^{p}dx
. Thus,

m\leq J(v^{*})\leq\frac{1}{I(v^{*})}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}J(v_{n})=m,

where
I(\cdot):=\int|x|^{-b}|\cdot|^{p}dx
. From this, we deduce that

(2.5)

m=J(v^{*})=\frac{1}{I(v^{*})}=\Big(\int|x|^{-b}|v^{*}|^{p}dx\Big)^{-1},\ \|v^{*}\|_{2}=\|\nabla v^{*}\|_{q}=1.

Hence,
v_{n}\rightarrow v^{*}
in
D^{1,q}\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In particular,
v^{*}
is a minimizer of
m
. Furthermore,
v^{*}
is a critical point of
J(u)
, then we have

\frac{d}{dt}J(v^{*}+t\varphi)\mid_{t=0}=0,\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Due to (
2.5
), this implies that

\sigma_{p,q}\int|x|^{-b}|v^{*}|^{p}dx\langle\triangle_{q}v^{*},\varphi\rangle+(p-\sigma_{p,q})\int|x|^{-b}|v^{*}|^{p}dx\langle v^{*},\varphi\rangle-p\langle|x|^{-b}|v^{*}|^{p-2}v^{*},\varphi\rangle=0,

or equivalently

(2.6)

\sigma_{p,q}\triangle_{q}v^{*}+(p-\sigma_{p,q})v^{*}-mp|x|^{-b}|v^{*}|^{p-2}v^{*}=0.

Taking
\omega:=(mp)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}v^{*}
, then
\omega
satisfies

(2.7)

\sigma_{p,q}\triangle_{q}\omega+(p-\sigma_{p,q})\omega-|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p-2}\omega=0.

Note from (
2.5
) that

m=\frac{1}{\int|x|^{-b}|v^{*}|^{p}dx}=\frac{1}{(mp)^{\frac{-p}{p-2}}\int|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p}dx},

which implies that

(2.8)

m=p^{-\frac{p}{2}}\Big(\int|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p}dx\Big)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}.

Next, we shall prove

(2.9)

\int|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p}dx=p\|w\|_{2}^{2}.

Due to (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), then
m=p^{-1}\|\omega\|_{2}^{p-2}
, thus letting
\mathcal{K}_{N,p,q}:=m^{-1}=\frac{p}{\|\omega\|_{2}^{p-2}}
, we then obtain (
2.1
).

To end the proof, we only need to verify (
2.9
). Multiplying the equation (
2.7
) by
\omega
and integrating by parts, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\sigma_{p,q}\|\nabla\omega\|_{q}^{q}+(p-\sigma_{p,q})\|\omega\|_{2}^{2}=\int|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p}dx.

Let

\displaystyle E(\omega):=\frac{\sigma_{p,q}}{q}\|\nabla\omega\|_{q}^{q}+\frac{(p-\sigma_{p,q})}{2}\|\omega\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\int|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p}dx,

and set
\omega_{t}:=t^{\frac{N}{2}}\omega(tx)
, then

E(\omega_{t})=\frac{\sigma_{p,q}}{q}t^{\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2}}\|\nabla\omega\|_{q}^{q}+\frac{(p-\sigma_{p,q})}{2}\|\omega\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{t^{\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2}}}{p}\int|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p}dx.

Since
\omega
is a solution of (
2.7
), then
\frac{d}{dt}E(\omega_{t})|_{t=1}=0
. By direct calculation, we have

\frac{\sigma_{p,q}}{q}\|\nabla\omega\|_{q}^{q}=\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{p[(N+2)q-2N]}\int|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p}dx=\frac{\sigma_{p,q}}{pq}\int|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p}dx.

Therefore,
\|\nabla\omega\|_{q}^{q}=\frac{1}{p}\int|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p}
. Taking it into (
2.10
), we have

\frac{p-\sigma_{p,q}}{p}\int|x|^{-b}|\omega|^{p}dx=(p-\sigma_{p,q})\|\omega\|_{2}^{2}.

Since
p-\sigma_{p,q}>0
if
p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
, then (
2.9
) follows. The proof is completed.
∎

Next, we prove the following Pohozaev identity.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
2<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
. If
u_{0}\in X
is a weak solution of the following equation:

(2.11)

-\triangle u-\triangle_{q}u=\lambda u+\frac{|u|^{p-2}u}{|x|^{b}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then
P(u_{0})=0
, where
P(u)
is given in (
1.13
).

Proof.

If
u_{0}\in X
is a weak solution of Eq. (
2.11
), then multiplying (
2.11
) by
u_{0}
and
x\cdot\nabla u_{0}
respectively, and integrating by part, we have

(2.12)

\begin{split}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{q}^{q}-\int\frac{|u_{0}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx-\lambda\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}=0,\end{split}

(2.13)

\begin{split}\frac{-(N-2)}{2}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{N-q}{q}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{b-N}{p}\int\frac{|u_{0}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx+\frac{N\lambda}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}=0.\end{split}

From (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), we have

\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2q}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2p}\int\frac{|u_{0}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=0,

which is exactly
P(u_{0})=0
.

∎

3.
THE MASS SUBCRITICAL CASE

In this section, we deal with the existence of minimizers to
m(c)
in the mass subcritical. As a first step, we prove the properties of
m(c)
, which is essential to establish a sharp existence of minimizers of
m(c)
, which depends on different assumptions on the parameter
p
and the mass
c>0
. Then, we use the concentration compactness principle to show the existence of minimizers of
m(c)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
2<p<p_{q}^{*}:=\frac{2(q-b)}{N}+q
, with
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
,
N\geq 1
, and
q>2
. Then, for any
c>0
, there holds that

-\infty<m(c)\leq 0.

Moreover, define

(3.1)

c_{1}^{*}:=\inf\{c>0\mid m(c)<0\},

then

(1)

If
2<p<\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2
, then
-\infty<m(c)<0
, for all
c>0
;

(2)

If
\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2\leq p<p_{q}^{*}
, then
0<c_{1}^{*}<+\infty
, and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}m(c)=0,\qquad\qquad 0<c\leq c_{1}^{*};\\
-\infty<m(c)<0,\quad c>c_{1}^{*}.\end{array}\right.

(3)

The mapping
c\mapsto m(c)
is continuous and non-increasing on
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
;

(4)

If
m(c)
is attained by some
u_{c}\in S(c)
, then for all
\bar{c}>c
,
m(\bar{c})<m(c)
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Given
c>0
, for any
u\in S(c)
, using the
L^{q}
-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.1
), we have

(3.3)

\begin{split}I(u)&=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{K}_{N,p,q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}}\|u\|_{2}^{p-\sigma_{p,q}}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{K}_{N,p,q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}}c^{\frac{p-\sigma_{p,q}}{2}}.\end{split}

Since
q>\sigma_{p,q}
if
p<\frac{2(q-b)}{N}+q
, then from (
3.3
) we deduce that
m(c)>-\infty
.

In addition, the proof of the continuity and non-increasing of
m(c)
is somehow standard, here we only refer the readers to the reference such as
[
7
, Theorem 1.2 (4)]
.

Let
u\in S(c)
be fixed, and denote
u_{t}(x):=t^{\frac{N}{2}}u(tx)\in S(c)
, then
u_{t}\in S(c)
for all
t>0
, and

(3.4)

I(u_{t})=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{t^{\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2}}}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{t^{\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2}}}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

This shows that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}I(u_{t})=0
and then
m(c)\leq 0
. Then we conclude that if
p<\frac{2(q-b)}{N}+q
, then
-\infty<m(c)\leq 0
, for all
c>0
.

When
2<p<\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2
, then
\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2}<\min\{2,\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2}\}
, thus we observe from (
3.4
) that
I(u_{t})<0
as
t>0
small enough. This implies that
m(c)<0
for all
c>0
, and then the point
(1)
is verified.

When
\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2\leq p<p_{q}^{*}
, by the continuity and non-increasing of
m(c)
, and also the definition of
c_{1}^{*}
, to verify the point
(2)
it suffices to prove that
0<c_{1}^{*}<+\infty
.

We first show that
c_{1}^{*}\neq 0
. Indeed, let
p_{2}^{*}=\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2
and
p_{q}^{*}=\frac{2(q-b)}{N}+q
, since
p_{2}^{*}\leq p<p_{q}^{*}
, then there exist constants
c_{1}>0,c_{2}>0
, such that

(3.5)

\frac{1}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\leq c_{1}\int\frac{|u|^{p_{2}^{*}}}{|x|^{b}}dx+c_{2}\int\frac{|u|^{p_{q}^{*}}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

Then the
L^{q}
-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.1
) yields to

(3.6)

c_{1}\int\frac{|u|^{p_{2}^{*}}}{|x|^{b}}dx\leq c_{1}(p_{2}^{*})\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\|u\|_{2}^{p_{2}^{*}-2},

(3.7)

c_{2}\int\frac{|u|^{p_{q}^{*}}}{|x|^{b}}dx\leq c_{2}(p_{q}^{*})\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}\|u\|_{2}^{p_{q}^{*}-q}.

From (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we have for all
u\in S(c)
that

(3.8)

\begin{split}I(u)&=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}\\
&\geq\Big[\frac{1}{2}-c_{1}(p_{2}^{*})c^{\frac{p_{q}^{*}-2}{2}}\Big]\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\Big[\frac{1}{q}-c_{2}(p_{2}^{*})c^{\frac{p_{q}^{*}-q}{2}}\Big]\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}.\end{split}

Note that
p_{2}^{*}-2>0,p_{q}^{*}-q>0
, then (
3.8
) shows that
m(c)\geq 0
if
c>0
is sufficiently small. Since we have proved that
m(c)\leq 0
for all
c>0
. Then we have
m(c)=0
as
c>0
small, which verifies that
c_{1}^{*}\neq 0
.

Next we show that
c_{1}^{*}\neq+\infty
. Note that choosing
u_{0}\in X
such that
\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}=1
, and let
u_{0}^{c}:=c^{\frac{1}{N+2}}u_{0}(c^{-\frac{1}{N+2}}x)
, then
u_{0}^{c}\in S(c)
, and

(3.9)

I(u_{0}^{c})=\frac{c^{\frac{N}{N+2}}}{2}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{c^{\frac{N}{N+2}}}{q}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{c^{\frac{p-b}{N+2}+\frac{N}{N+2}}}{p}\int\frac{|u_{0}|^{p}}{{|x|^{b}}}dx,

from which, we see that
I(u_{0}^{c})<0
, as
c>0
is sufficiently large, so when
c>0
is large,
m(c)<0
. Thus by the definition of
c_{1}^{*}
, we have
c_{1}^{*}\neq+\infty
.

Finally, to show the point
(4)
, by assumption,
m(c)=I(u_{c})\leq 0
, then we observe that for
t:=\sqrt{\frac{\bar{c}}{c}}>1
, since
p>q>2
, then

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I(tu_{c})&=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{t^{q}}{q}\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{t^{p}}{p}\int\frac{|u_{c}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\\
&<t^{q}I(u_{c})=t^{q}m(c)\leq m(c),\end{split}

which implies that
m(\bar{c})<m(c)
. Then the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

From (
3.4
), we can observe that if
p>p_{q}^{*}
, then for all
c>0
, given
u\in S(c)
, then
I(u_{t})\to-\infty
as
t\to\infty
, which implies that

m(c)=-\infty
, or equivalently, when
p>p_{q}^{*}
, then for all
c>0
, the functional
I(u)
is unbounded from below on
S(c)
.

Using the points
(2)
and
(4)
of Lemma
3.1
, we obtain immediately that following non-existence.

Corollary 3.1
.

When
\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2\leq p<p_{q}^{*}
, then for all
0<c<c_{1}^{*}
,
m(c)
has no minimizers.

Proof.

Indeed, if we assume that there exists
c^{\prime}<c_{1}^{*}
, such that
m(c^{\prime})
admits a minimizer
u_{c^{\prime}}\in S(c^{\prime})
, then using
(4)
of Lemma
3.1
, we have for any
c
with
c^{\prime}<c<c_{1}^{*}
,
m(c)<m(c^{\prime})=0
, this contradicts with the point
(2)
. Then the non-existence has been proved.
∎

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
2<p<p_{q}^{*}
, with
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
,
N\geq 1
, and
q>2
. Suppose that for some
c>0
,
m(c)
satisfies that

(3.11)

\displaystyle-\infty<m(c)<0.

Then any minimizing sequence of
m(c)
is pre-compact in
X
. In particular,
m(c)
admits a minimizer
u_{c}\in S(c)
. Moreover,
u_{c}\in S(c)
can be assumed to be non-negative, radially symmetric and radially decreasing with respect to some point.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a minimizing sequence of
m(c)
, namely

\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=c\ \mbox{ and }\ I(u_{n})\to m(c).

First, we show that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
X
. Indeed, as (
3.3
), using (
2.1
) we have

(3.12)

m(c)+o_{n}(1)=I(u_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{K}_{N,p,q}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}}c^{\frac{p-\sigma_{p,q}}{2}}.

This proves that
\{\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}\}
is bounded, since
q>\sigma_{p,q}
. Using (
2.3
) (
q=2
) and the same arguments, we obtain that
\{\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\}
is bounded. Hence,
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
X
.

Thus, up to a subsequence, there exists
u\in X
, such that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\mbox{ in }X,\ \mbox{ and }\ u_{n}\to u\ \mbox{in}\ L^{r}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\forall r\in[2,\frac{2N}{(N-2)^{+}}).

In particular, by Lemma
7.1
,

(3.13)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\frac{|u_{n}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

Then clearly
u\neq 0
, since if
u=0
, then as
n
large enough,
m(c)=I(u_{n})+o_{n}(1)\geq 0
, which contradicts with the assumption
m(c)<0
.

Now, we prove that
u
is a minimizer of
m(c)
. Indeed, since
u\neq 0
, by the Brezis-Lieb Lemma and Lemma
7.1
, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle c=\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u_{n}-u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}+o_{n}(1).

(3.15)

\displaystyle I(u_{n})=I(u_{n}-u)+I(u)+o_{n}(1).

If
\|u\|_{2}^{2}<c
, then we denote
u^{t_{0}}:=t_{0}u(t_{0}^{-1}x)
with
t_{0}=\Big(\frac{c}{\|u\|_{2}^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{N+2}}
, thus
t_{0}>1,u^{t_{0}}\in S(c)
and

(3.16)

\displaystyle I(u^{t_{0}})=\frac{t_{0}^{N}}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{t_{0}^{N}}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{t_{0}^{p-b+N}}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx,

which implies that

(3.17)

\displaystyle I(u)=\frac{1}{t_{0}^{N}}I(u^{t_{0}})+\frac{(t_{0}^{p-b}-1)}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

Similarly, let
t_{n}=\Big(\frac{c}{\|u_{n}-u\|_{2}^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{N+2}}
, thus
t_{n}>1,(u_{n}-u)^{t_{n}}\in S(c)
and

(3.18)

\displaystyle I(u_{n}-u)=\frac{1}{t_{n}^{N}}I((u_{n}-u)^{t_{n}})+\frac{(t_{n}^{p-b}-1)}{p}\int\frac{|u_{n}-u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

Therefore, by (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) we have

\displaystyle m(c)=

\displaystyle I(u_{n})+o_{n}(1)=I(u_{n}-u)+I(u)+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m(c)\left(\frac{1}{t_{0}^{N}}+\frac{1}{t_{n}^{N}}\right)+\frac{(t_{0}^{p-b}-1)}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx+\frac{(t_{n}^{p-b}-1)}{p}\int\frac{|u_{n}-u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx

(3.19)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m(c)+\frac{(t_{0}^{p-b}-1)}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx+o_{n}(1).

Since
p>b
and
t_{0}>1
, then (
3.19
) is impossible. Hence,
\|u\|_{2}^{2}=c
, namely
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. Then using (
3.13
) and the lower semi-continuity of norm, we have

(3.20)

\displaystyle m(c)\leq I(u)\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}I(u_{n})=m(c).

This implies that
I(u)=m(c)
and
\|u_{n}\|_{X}\rightarrow\|u\|_{X}
, then
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
X
, and
u
is a minimizer of
m(c)
. Furthermore, we observe that
|u_{c}|
is also a minimizer, hence without loss of generality, we can assume that
u_{c}\geq 0
.

Let
u_{c}^{*}
be the Schwartz symmetrization of
u_{c}
, then by the Polya-Szegö inequality (see e.g.
[
34
]
) and Lemma
7.2
, we have that

\displaystyle\|u_{c}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}=c,\ \|\nabla u_{c}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}^{2},\ \|\nabla u_{c}^{*}\|_{q}^{q}\leq\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{q},\ \int\frac{|u_{c}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\leq\int\frac{|u_{c}^{*}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

This implies that

\displaystyle m(c)\leq I(u_{c}^{*})\leq I(u_{c})=m(c).

Hence,
u_{c}^{*}
is also a minimizer of
m(c)
. Moreover,

(3.21)

\displaystyle\int\frac{|u_{c}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=\int\frac{|u_{c}^{*}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

Thus, using Lemma
7.2
, we deduce from (
3.21
) that
u_{c}=u_{c}^{*}
. Hence
u_{c}
is radially symmetric and decreasing with respect to some point. At this point, the proposition is proved.
∎

We deal with the critical case
c=c_{1}^{*}
in a single lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2\leq p<p_{q}^{*}
, then

(1)

When
\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2<p<p_{q}^{*}
, then
m(c_{1}^{*})
admits a minimizer;

(2)

When
p=\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2
, then
m(c_{1}^{*})
has no minimizers.

Proof.

We use an approximation to derive the existence. Let
c_{k}:=c_{1}^{*}+\frac{1}{k}
, then for all
k
,
c_{k}>c_{1}^{*}
and
m(c_{k})
admits a minimizer
u_{k}\in S(c)
, and
m(c_{k})<0
. Now we want to show that when
\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2<p<p_{q}^{*}
, then

(3.22)

\displaystyle u_{k}\to u_{c}\ \mbox{ in }X.

Indeed, if (
3.22
) holds, then by the continuity of
m(c)
in Lemma
3.1
,
u_{c}\in S(c)
is a minimizer of
m(c_{1}^{*})=0
. Now we verify (
3.22
). Note that

I(u_{k})=m(c_{1}^{*})+o_{n}(1)=o_{n}(1),

then as in Proposition
3.1
, by (
3.12
),
\{u_{k}\}
is bounded in
X
.

Thus, up to a subsequence, there exists
u_{c}\in X
, such that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{c}\mbox{ in }X,\ \mbox{ and }\ u_{n}\to u_{c}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{r}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\forall r\in[2,\frac{2N}{(N-2)^{+}}).

By Lemma
7.1
,

(3.23)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\frac{|u_{n}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=\int\frac{|u_{c}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

Then we claim that
u_{c}\neq 0
. Indeed, if
u_{c}=0
, then as
n
large enough,

o_{n}(1)=I(u_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{q}^{q}+o_{n}(1),

which implies that
\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}=o_{n}(1)
. However, using the
L^{q}
-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.3
), we have

o_{n}(1)=I(u_{k})\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{K}_{N,p,2}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{2}^{\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2}}(c_{1}^{*})^{\frac{2N+2b-(N-2)p}{4}}.

This is impossible, since
\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2}>2
as
p>\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2
and
\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}=o_{n}(1)
. Then the claim follows.

Having proved the boundedness and non-vanishing of
\{u_{k}\}
with
I(u_{k})=m(c_{k})
, we then using the same compactness arguments as in Proposition
3.1
, get (
3.22
).

Now, when
p=\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2
, if we assume by contradiction that
m(c_{1}^{*})
admits a minimizer
u_{1}\in S(c)
, then clearly
u_{1}
is a weak solution of Eq. (
1.1
), and by Lemma
2.2
,
P(u_{1})=0
. Thus we have

\displaystyle 0=m(c_{1}^{*})=I(u_{1})=I(u_{1})-\frac{2}{N(p-2)+2b}P(u_{1})=\dfrac{(N+2)(2-q)}{4q}\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{q}^{q}.

This is a contradiction, since
u_{1}\in S(c)
. Then we have proved that when
p=\frac{2(2-b)}{N}+2
,
m(c_{1}^{*})
has no minimizers.

∎

4.
THE MASS CRITICAL CASE

In this section, we consider the mass critical case
p_{q}^{*}:=\frac{2(q-b)}{N}+q
. Recall that when
p=p_{q}^{*}
, the
L^{q}
-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.1
) is reduced to

(4.1)

\int\frac{|u|^{p_{q}^{*}}}{|x|^{b}}dx\leq\mathcal{K}_{N,q}^{*}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}\|u\|_{2}^{p_{q}^{*}-q},\quad\forall u\in D^{1,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\mathcal{K}_{N,q}^{*}:=\frac{p_{q}^{*}}{\|Q_{q}^{*}\|_{2}^{p_{q}^{*}-2}}
, and
Q_{q}^{*}
is a fixed ground state solution of

(4.2)

q\triangle_{q}Q+(p_{q}^{*}-q)Q-|x|^{-b}|Q|^{p_{q}^{*}-2}Q=0.

Moreover, the equality in (
4.1
) holds if
u=Q_{q}^{*}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
,
N\geq 1
, and
q>2
,
p=p_{q}^{*}
, and denote

(4.3)

c_{2}^{*}:=\Big(\frac{p_{q}^{*}}{q\mathcal{K}_{N,q}^{*}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p_{q}^{*}-q}}=q^{\frac{2}{q-p_{q}^{*}}}\|Q_{q}^{*}\|_{2}^{\frac{2(p_{q}^{*}-2)}{p_{q}^{*}-q}}.

Then,

(4.4)

m(c)=\begin{cases}0,&0<c\leq c_{2}^{*};\\
-\infty,&c>c_{2}^{*}.\end{cases}

Moreover, the functional
I(u)
has no any critical points for all
c\leq c_{2}^{*}
. In particular,
m(c)
has no minimizers for all
c>0
.

Proof of Lemma 4.1.

For any
u\in S(c)
, using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
4.1
), we have

(4.5)

\begin{split}I(u)&\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p_{q}^{*}}\mathcal{K}_{N,q}^{*}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}\|u\|_{2}^{p_{q}^{*}-q}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{q}^{*}}\mathcal{K}_{N,q}^{*}c^{\frac{p_{q}^{*}-q}{2}})\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{q}\Big[1-\Big(\frac{c}{c_{2}^{*}}\Big)^{\frac{p_{q}^{*}-q}{2}}\Big]\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}.\end{split}

This yields that when
0<c\leq c_{2}^{*}
,
I(u)\geq 0
for any
u\in S(c)
. Then as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we conclude that
m(c)=0
as
0<c\leq c_{2}^{*}
.

On the other hand, using the scaling in (
3.16
) with
u=Q_{q}^{*},t_{0}=\Big(\frac{c}{\|Q_{q}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{N+2}}
and
p=p_{q}^{*}
, and also the scaling in (
3.4
), then
(u^{t_{0}})_{t}\in S(c)
for all
t>0
, and

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I((u^{t_{0}})_{t})&=t_{0}^{N}\Big[\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\nabla Q_{q}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{t^{\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2}}}{q}\|\nabla Q_{q}^{*}\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{t^{\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2}}\cdot t_{0}^{p_{q}^{*}-b}}{p_{q}^{*}}\mathcal{K}_{N,q}^{*}\|\nabla Q_{q}^{*}\|_{q}^{q}\|Q_{q}^{*}\|_{2}^{p_{q}^{*}-q}\Big]\\
&=t_{0}^{N}\Big\{\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\nabla Q_{q}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{t^{\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2}}}{q}\Big[1-\Big(\frac{c}{c_{2}^{*}}\Big)^{\frac{q-b}{N}}\Big]\|\nabla Q_{q}^{*}\|_{q}^{q}\Big\}.\end{split}

This shows that when
c>c_{2}^{*}
,
\lim_{t\to+\infty}I((u^{t_{0}})_{t})\to-\infty
, then we have
m(c)=-\infty
for
c>c_{2}^{*}
. Thus (
4.4
) is verified.

Now we show that for
c\leq c_{2}^{*}
,
I(u)
has no critical points on
S(c)
. Indeed, if we assume by contradiction that there exist
\hat{c}\leq c_{2}^{*}
and a function
\hat{u}\in S(\hat{c})
being a critical point of
I(u)
on
S(\hat{c})
. Then by Lemma
2.2
,
P(\hat{u})
=0. On the other hand, as (
4.5
), using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
4.1
), we have

(4.7)

\begin{split}0=P(\hat{u})&=\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2q}\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{N(p_{q}^{*}-2)+2b}{2p_{q}^{*}}\int\frac{|\hat{u}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\\
&\geq\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2q}\Big[1-\Big(\frac{\hat{c}}{c_{2}^{*}}\Big)^{\frac{p_{q}^{*}-q}{2}}\Big]\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{q}^{q}\geq\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

This implies that
\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{2}=0
, which clearly contradicts with the fact that
\hat{u}\in S(\hat{c})
. Then
I(u)
has no critical points on
S(c)
for all
c\leq c_{2}^{*}
. The remaining is trivial, then the proof is completed.

∎

5.
THE MASS SUPERCRITICAL CASE

In this section, we consider the mass supercritical case
p_{q}^{*}<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
, where
p_{q}^{*}=\frac{2(q-b)}{N}+q
. In this case, the functional
I(u)
is unbounded from below on
S(c)
, see Remark
3.1
. Hence one needs to find solutions as local type of minimizers of
I(u)
on
S(c)
. We shall consider both the existence of ground state solutions and also infinitely many bound state solutions on
S(c)
.

5.1.
Ground state solutions on
S(c)

To find a critical point of
I(u)
on
S(c)
which has the least energy among all solutions on
S(c)
, inspired by the work of
[
8
,
29
,
33
]
, we consider the following local type minimization problem:

(5.1)

\gamma(c):=\inf_{u\in V(c)}I(u),\ c>0,

where
V(c):=\{u\in S(c)\ :\ P(u)=0\}
, with

(5.2)

P(u):=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=0.

Note that

(5.3)

P(u)=\frac{d}{dt}I(u_{t})\mid_{t=1}=0,\quad u_{t}=t^{\frac{N}{2}}u(tx)\in S(c).

In Lemma
2.2
, we show that if
u
is a weak solution of Eq. (
1.1
), then necessarily
P(u)=0
. Therefore, “
P(u)=0
” is referred as a variant of Pohozaev identity. In addition, from Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
, we see that for all
c>0
,
V(c)\neq\emptyset
, hence
\gamma(c)
is well-defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
.

In what follows, we aim to show that for some
c>0
,
\gamma(c)
is attained by some
u_{c}\in V(c)
, and
u_{c}
is a critical point of
I(u)
on
S(c)
. Firstly, we prove that

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
p_{q}^{*}<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
, then

\gamma(c)>0,\ \forall\ c>0.

Proof.

For any
c>0
, let
u\in V(c)
, then from
P(u)=0
and the
L^{p}
-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.1
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2p}\mathcal{K}_{N,p,q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}}c^{\frac{p-\sigma_{p,q}}{2}}.

Since
\sigma_{p,q}>q
as
p>p_{q}^{*}
, then this implies that

(5.4)

\|\nabla u\|_{q}\geq C(N,p,q,\mathcal{K}_{N,p,q})>0.

On the other hand, for any
u\in V(c)
, we have

(5.5)

\begin{split}I(u)&=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\\
&=\dfrac{Np-2(N+2)+2b}{2[N(p-2)+2b]}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\dfrac{Np-q(N+2)+2b}{q[N(p-2)+2b]}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}.\end{split}

Since
Np-2(N+2)+2b>Np-q(N+2)+2b>0
as
p>p_{q}^{*}
, then we obtain from (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) that
\gamma(c)>0
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
p_{q}^{*}<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
. Then for any
u\in S(c)
, there exists a unique
t_{u}>0
, such that
P(u_{t_{u}})=0
and
I(u_{t_{u}})=\max_{t>0}I(u_{t})
. Moreover, if
P(u)\leq 0
, then
t_{u}\in(0,1]
.

As a consequence, for all
c>0
, we have

(5.6)

\gamma(c)=\inf_{u\in S(c)}\max_{t>0}I(u_{t}).

Proof.

Fixed
u\in S(c)
, we observe that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}P(u_{t})&=t^{2}\Big[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2q}\cdot t^{\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2}-2}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}\\
&-\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2p}\cdot t^{\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2}-2}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\Big]\triangleq t^{2}\cdot g(t).\end{split}

Since
2<\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2q}<\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2p}
as
p>p_{q}^{*}
, then it is easy to verify that there exists a unique
t_{u}>0
such that
g(t_{u})=0
. Equivalently, there exists a unique
t_{u}>0
, such that
P(u_{t_{u}})=0
.

On the other, from (
3.4
), we have

(5.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}I(u_{t})&=t\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2q}\cdot\frac{t^{\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2}-1}}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}\\
&-\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2p}\cdot\frac{t^{\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2}-1}}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=\frac{P(u_{t})}{t}.\end{split}

Therefore, we have

\frac{d}{dt}I(u_{t})>0,\mbox{ as }0<t<t_{u},\quad\mbox{ and }\frac{d}{dt}I(u_{t})<0,\mbox{ as }t>t_{u}.

Hence,
I(u_{t_{u}})=\max_{t>0}I(u_{t})
. Moreover, if
P(u)<0
, since
P(u_{t})>0
as
t>0
small enough, then by continuity we know that
0<t_{u}<1
.

To end the proof, we verify (
5.6
). Denote

d(c):=\inf_{u\in S(c)}\max_{t>0}I(u_{t}),

then by the definition of
\gamma(c)
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is
u_{\varepsilon}\in V(c)
, such that
I(u_{\varepsilon})<\gamma(c)+\varepsilon
. By the above proof,
\max_{t>0}I((u_{\varepsilon})_{t})=I(u_{\varepsilon})
, this shows that

d(c)\leq\max_{t>0}I((u_{\varepsilon})_{t})<\gamma(c)+\varepsilon.

Then
d(c)\leq\gamma(c)
. On the other hand, for any
u\in S(c)
, there is
t_{u}>0
such that
u_{t_{u}}\in V(c)
, and

\max_{t>0}I((u_{t})=I(u_{t_{u}})\geq\gamma(c),

which implies that
d(c)\geq\gamma(c)
. Thus (
5.6
) follows.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
p_{q}^{*}<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
. Then the mapping:
c\mapsto\gamma(c)
is non-increasing on
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. Namely, for any
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
,
\gamma(c_{2})\leq\gamma(c_{1})
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\gamma(c_{2})<\gamma(c_{1})+\varepsilon.

Indeed, for
\gamma(c_{1})
, there exists
u_{1}\in V(c_{1})
, such that

\gamma(c_{1})\leq I(u_{1})<\gamma(c_{1})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

and by Lemma
5.2
,
I(u_{1})=\max_{t>0}I((u_{1})_{t})
. Since
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is dense in
X
, then
u_{1}
can be assumed to have a bounded compact support. Without loss of generality, we assume that
supp\ u_{1}\subset B_{R}
for some
R>0
. Let
v_{0}\in X\setminus\{0\}
, such that
supp\ v_{0}\subset B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}
, and

v:=\sqrt{\frac{c_{2}-c_{1}}{\|v_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}}\cdot v_{0}.

Then
v\in S(c_{2}-c_{1})
. Now for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we denote
u_{2}:=u_{1}+v_{\lambda}
, with
v_{\lambda}:=\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}v(\lambda x)
. Then

dist\{supp\ u_{1},supp\ v_{\lambda}\}>\frac{R}{\lambda}-R>0.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|u_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{2}=c_{2},

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{q}^{q}+\lambda^{\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{q}^{q},

\displaystyle\int\frac{|u_{2}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx

\displaystyle=\int\frac{|u_{1}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx+\lambda^{\frac{N(p-2)+2b}{2}}\int\frac{|v|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

Therefore, as
\lambda>0
small enough, we have

\max_{t>0}I((u_{2})_{t})\leq\max_{t>0}I((u_{1})_{t})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

This implies from (
5.6
) that

\gamma(c_{2})\leq\max_{t>0}I((u_{2})_{t})<\gamma(c_{1})+\varepsilon.

Then (
5.9
) is verified.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
p_{q}^{*}<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
. If the couple
(u_{c},\lambda_{c})\in S(c)\times{\mathbb{R}}
solves weakly the equation:

(5.10)

-\triangle u-\triangle_{q}u=\lambda u+\frac{|u|^{p-2}u}{|x|^{b}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then

(1)

If
N=1,2
,
p_{q}^{*}<p<+\infty
, then
\lambda_{c}<0
, for all
c>0
;

(2)

If
N\geq 3
with
q<\frac{2(N^{2}-2b)}{N^{2}-4}
,
p_{q}^{*}<p\leq 2_{b}^{*}:=\frac{2(N-b)}{(N-2)}
, then
\lambda_{c}<0
, for all
c>0
;

(3)

As
c\to 0^{+}
, we have

(5.11)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}\to+\infty,\ \|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}\to+\infty,\ I(u_{c})\to+\infty,\ \lambda_{c}\to-\infty.

Proof.

For any given
c>0
, by Lemma
2.2
,
P(u_{c})=0
. In addition, multiplying (
5.10
) by
u_{c}
, and integrating by parts, we have

(5.12)

\lambda_{c}\|u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}+\int\frac{|u_{c}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{q}.

Thus, using
P(u_{c})=0
, we have

(5.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{c}\|u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}&=\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{q}-\int\frac{|u_{c}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\\
&=\frac{(N-2)p-2(N-b)}{N(p-2)+2b}\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2[(N-q)p-q(N-b)]}{q[N(p-2)+2b]}\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{q}\\
&=\frac{(N-2)(p-2_{b}^{*})}{N(p-2)+2b}\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2(N-q)(p-q_{b}^{*})}{q[N(p-2)+2b]}\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{q}.\end{split}

Since
2_{b}^{*}<q_{b}^{*}:=\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
as
q>2
and
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
, then if
p\leq 2_{b}^{*}
, then clearly
\lambda_{c}<0
.

Again using
P(u_{c})=0
and the
L^{q}
-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.1
), we have

\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{q}\leq\frac{q[N(p-2)+2b]}{p[N(q-2)+2q]}\int\frac{|u_{c}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\\
\leq C_{0}(N,p,q,b)\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}}\|u_{c}\|_{2}^{p-\sigma_{p,q}},

this leads to

C_{0}(N,p,q,b)^{-1}\leq\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{\frac{qN(p-p_{q}^{*})}{N(q-2)+2q}}\|u_{c}\|_{2}^{\frac{2(N-q)(q_{b}^{*}-p)}{N(q-2)+2q}}.

which then shows that

\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}\rightarrow+\infty,\ \text{ as }\ c\to 0.

Further, from
P(u_{c})=0
and (
2.3
), we have

\lim_{c\to 0}\int\frac{|u_{c}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=+\infty,\quad\lim_{c\to 0}\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}\rightarrow+\infty.

Hence, by (
5.5
) and (
5.13
),
I(u_{c})\to+\infty,\ \lambda_{c}\to-\infty
as
c\to 0
. Then the proof is completed.

∎

Remark 5.1
.

When
2_{b}^{*}<p<q_{b}^{*}
, we conjecture that
\lambda_{c}<0
, for all
0<c<c_{0}
as some
c_{0}:=c(N,p,q,b)>0
. In fact, we observe from (

) that

\lambda_{c}\|u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}=C_{1}(N,p,q,b)\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}-C_{2}(N,p,q,b)\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{q},

where

. Then the sign of
\lambda_{c}
depends on the comparison between
\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}
and
\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{q}
. Using
P(u_{c})=0
and the
L^{q}
-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}(N,p,q,b)\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}}\|u_{c}\|_{2}^{p-\sigma_{p,q}}

\displaystyle=C_{3}(N,p,q,b)\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{\eta}\cdot\Big(\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}-\eta}\|u_{c}\|_{2}^{p-\sigma_{p,q}}\Big),

where
\eta\in(0,q)
to be determined. If one could prove that there exist
\eta>0
and
C_{1}>0
independent of
c>0
, such that

(5.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{0<c\leq 1}\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{\sigma_{p,q}-\eta}\|u_{c}\|_{2}^{p-\sigma_{p,q}}\leq C_{1},

then

\lambda_{c}\|u_{c}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\widetilde{C_{1}}(N,p,q,b)\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{\eta}-C_{2}(N,p,q,b)\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}^{q},

from which we conclude that
\lambda_{c}<0
, for all
c>0
small enough, since
\|\nabla u_{c}\|_{q}\rightarrow+\infty
as
c\to 0
. However, a convinced proof of (
5.14
) is still open for us. In the appendix, we provide an exponential decay of

but not uniform, which may be helpful to verify (
5.14
).

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
p_{q}^{*}<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
, and
(u_{c},\lambda_{c})\in X\times\mathbb{R}
solves

-\triangle u-\triangle_{q}u=\lambda u+\frac{|u|^{p-2}u}{|x|^{b}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

with
I(u_{c})=\inf_{u\in V(c)}I(u)=\gamma(c)
. If
\lambda_{c}<0
, then the mapping:
c\mapsto\gamma(c)
is strictly decreasing in a right neighborhood of
c
.

Proof.

The idea of the proof stems from
[
8
, Lemma 5.5]
, which is based on the Implicit Function Theorem. We denote

u_{t,\theta}(x):=\theta^{\frac{N}{2}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{c}(\theta x)\in S(tc),

with
\theta>0
and
t>0
. Define

(5.15)

\alpha(t,\theta):=I(u_{t,\theta}),

(5.16)

\beta(t,\theta):=P(u_{t,\theta}).

By direct calculations, we have

\frac{\partial\alpha(t,\theta)}{\partial t}_{|_{(1,1)}}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{c}c,\quad\frac{\partial\alpha(t,\theta)}{\partial\theta}_{|_{(1,1)}}=0,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\alpha(t,\theta)}{\partial^{2}\theta}_{|_{(1,1)}}<0.

Then, for any
\delta_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\delta_{\theta}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

(5.17)

\alpha(1+\delta_{t},1+\delta_{\theta})=\alpha(1,1)+\delta_{t}\frac{\partial\alpha(t,\theta)}{\partial t}_{|_{(\bar{t},\bar{\theta})}}+\delta_{\theta}\frac{\partial\alpha(t,\theta)}{\partial\theta}_{|_{(\bar{t},\bar{\theta})}},

where
|1-\bar{t}|\leq|\delta_{t}|
and
|1-\bar{\theta}|\leq|\delta_{\theta}|
. If
\lambda_{c}<0
, then by continuity, for sufficiently small
\delta_{t}>0
and sufficiently small
|\delta_{\theta}|,
we have

(5.18)

\alpha(1+\delta_{t},1+\delta_{\theta})<\alpha(1,1).

Note that
\beta(1,1)=0
, if we have

(5.19)

\frac{\partial\beta(t,\theta)}{\partial\theta}_{|_{(1,1)}}\neq 0.

then applying the Implicit Function Theorem, for any
\varepsilon>0
, and
t\in[1,1+\varepsilon]
, there is a continuous function
\theta(t)
, such that
\beta(t,\theta(t))=0
for all
t\in[1,1+\varepsilon]
. Thus by (
5.18
), we have

\gamma((1+\varepsilon)c)=\inf_{u\in V((1+\varepsilon)c)}I(u)\leq I(u_{1+\varepsilon,\theta(1+\varepsilon)})<I(u_{c})=\gamma(c).

Hence, to end the proof, we only need to prove (
5.19

Lemma 5.7

\begin{split}\mu\Big[4\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}&+\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{2}\cdot\frac{N(q-2)+2q}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}\\

Since

5.2.
Infinitely many bound state solutions on
S(c)

As a byproduct of the above lemmas in the mass-supercritical case, inspired by the work of Bartsch et.al
[
14
]
, we shall use the fountain theorem to prove the existence of infinitely many bound state normalized solutions of (

). Similar results also have been established for the Schrödinger-Poisson-Slater equations in
[

]
, for a class of nonlinear Choquard equations in
[

]
, and recently for a type of

-Laplacian Equation with homogenous nonlinearity in

Let
\{V_{n}\}
be a strictly increasing sequence of finite-dimensional linear subspaces in

, such that

is dense in

. We denote by

in

Lemma 5.8
.

Assume that

. Then there holds that

\mu_{n}:=\inf_{u\in V_{n-1}^{\bot}}\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}}{(\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx)^{\frac{2}{p}}}=\inf_{u\in V_{n-1}^{\bot}}\frac{\|u\|_{X}^{2}}{(\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\rightarrow+\infty,\ \mbox{as}\ n\rightarrow+\infty.

Proof.

We apply the same idea from
[
14
, Lemma 2.1]
to prove this lemma. Indeed, if we argue by contradiction to suppose that there exists
\{u_{n}\}\subset X
, such that
u_{n}\in V_{n-1}^{\perp}
,
\int\frac{|u_{n}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=1
,
\|u_{n}\|_{X}=l<+\infty
. Then, up to a subsequence, there exists

, such that

in

. By Lemma

,
\int\frac{|u_{n}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\to\int\frac{|u_{0}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=1
. Since

Hence,

Then, we have

.

Assume that
p_{q}^{*}<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
. Then

. Without loss of generality, we can assume that

For any

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p\mu_{n}^{\frac{p}{2}}}\Big(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}+c\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\{u_{k}\}\subset S(c)
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}I(u_{k})\to\gamma_{n}(c),\\
I^{\prime}|_{S(c)}(u_{k})\to 0,\quad\mbox{ as }k\to\infty,\\
P(u_{k})\to 0,\end{array}\right.

where
P(u)
is given in (
5.2
). In particular,
\{u_{k}\}
is bounded in
X
.

Proof.

Indeed, due to Lemma
5.9
and Lemma
5.10
, by letting
E=S(c)\cap V_{n}
, and

\gamma(0)=\max_{u\in S(c)\cap V_{n}}I(g(0,u)),\ \gamma(1)=\max_{u\in S(c)\cap V_{n}}I(g(1,u)),

then this lemma is a consequence of
[
19
, Theorem 4.1]
. In addition, since for all
k>0
,
P(u_{k})=0
, then from (
5.5
), we see that
\{u_{k}\}
is bounded in
X
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
p_{q}^{*}<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
. Let
\{u_{k}\}\subset S(c)
be the Palais-Smale sequence obtained in Lemma
5.11
. Then there exist
\lambda_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
u_{n}\in X
, such that, up to a subsequence, as
k\to\infty
,

(1)

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{n}\neq 0
, in
X
;

(2)

-\triangle u_{k}-\triangle_{q}u_{k}-\lambda_{n}u_{k}-\frac{|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}}{|x|^{b}}\to 0
, in
X^{*}
;

(3)

-\triangle u_{n}-\triangle_{q}u_{n}-\lambda_{n}u_{n}-\frac{|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}}{|x|^{b}}=0
, in
X^{*}
.

Moreover, if
\lambda_{n}<0
, then we have

u_{k}\to u_{n},\ \mbox{ in }X.

In particular,
||u_{n}||_{2}^{2}=c
,
I(u_{n})=\gamma_{n}(c)
and
I^{\prime}(u_{n})-\lambda_{n}u_{n}=0
in
X^{*}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.11
,
\{u_{k}\}\subset S(c)
is bounded in
X
, then up to a subsequence, there exists
u_{n}\in X
, such that
u_{k}\underset{k}{\rightharpoonup}u_{n},\ \mbox{ in }X
, and by Lemma
7.1
,

(5.43)

\displaystyle\int\frac{|u_{k}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\to\int\frac{|u_{n}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

We claim that
u_{n}\neq 0
. Indeed if
u_{n}=0
, then
\int\frac{|u_{k}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\to 0
. Using
P(u_{k})\to 0
, we have
\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\to 0
and
\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{q}^{q}\to 0
. Thus
I(u_{k})\to 0
and this contradicts with the fact that
\lim_{k\to\infty}I(u_{k})=\gamma_{n}(c)\geq b_{n}\geq 1
. Thus the claim is verified.

Following
[
10
, Lemma 3]
, (see also
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]
), we have

\displaystyle I^{\prime}|_{S(c)}(u_{k})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle 0\ \mbox{ in }X

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(u_{k})-\langle I^{\prime}(u_{k}),u_{k}\rangle u_{k}\to 0\mbox{ in }X.

Thus, for any
w\in X
,

(5.44)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle I^{\prime}(u_{k})-\langle I^{\prime}(u_{k}),u_{k}\rangle u_{k},w\rangle&=\int\nabla u_{k}\nabla wdx+\int|\nabla u_{k}|^{q-2}\nabla u_{k}\nabla wdx\\
&-\int\frac{|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}w}{|x|^{b}}dx-\lambda_{k}\int u_{k}(x)w(x)dx\to 0,\end{split}

with

(5.45)

\lambda_{k}=\frac{1}{\|u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}}\Big\{\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{q}^{q}-\int\frac{|u_{k}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx\Big\}.

Since
\{u_{k}\}
is bounded in
X
, then clearly,
\{\lambda_{k}\}
is bounded in
{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus up to a subsequence,
\lambda_{k}\to\lambda_{n}
for some
\lambda_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Therefore, the point
(2)
follows from (
5.44
). Moreover, since
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{n}
in
X
, then the point
(2)
implies immediately the point
(3)
.

Since (
5.43
), then from
(2)
and
(3)
, we have

\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{q}^{q}-\lambda_{n}\|u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\underset{k}{\to}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}-\lambda_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

If
\lambda_{n}<0
, then from
u_{k}\underset{k}{\rightharpoonup}u_{n}
in
X
, we have

\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\to\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2},\ \|\nabla u_{k}\|_{q}^{q}\to\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{q}^{q},\ \|u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\to\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=c.

Thus
u_{k}\underset{k}{\to}u_{n}
in
X
, and in particular,
||u_{n}||_{2}^{2}=c
,
I(u_{n})=\gamma_{n}(c)
and
I^{\prime}(u_{n})-\lambda_{n}u_{n}=0
in
X^{*}
.

∎

7.
appendix

In this section, we give the proof of some technical lemmas, which are used in our main proofs.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume that
2<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
, with
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
,
N\geq 1
,
q\geq 2
, and that
\{u_{n}\}\subset X
and
u\in X
, being such that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u,\ \mbox{ in }X^{*},

then

(7.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\frac{|u_{n}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

Moreover, we have the following Brezis-Lieb decomposition:

(7.2)

\displaystyle\int\frac{|u_{n}|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx-\int\frac{|u_{n}-u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx-\int\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx=o_{n}(1),\quad\mbox{ as }n\to\infty,

(7.3)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}-\|\nabla(u_{n}-u)\|_{q}^{q}-\|\nabla u\|_{q}^{q}=o_{n}(1),\quad\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

First, we claim that

(7.4)

\displaystyle\int|x|^{-b}|u_{n}-u|^{p}dx=o_{n}(1).

Indeed, since
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\ \mbox{ in }X
, then
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
X
, and
u_{n}-u\to 0
in
L^{r}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, for all
r\in[2,\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}})
, thus for given
R>0
to be determined, we have

\int|x|^{-b}|u_{n}-u|^{p}dx=\int_{|x|\leq R}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}-u|^{p}dx+\int_{|x|\geq R}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}-u|^{p}dx.

Using the Hölder inequality, we have for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, that

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}-u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{|x|\leq R}|x|^{-\frac{bN}{b+\varepsilon}}dx\Big)^{\frac{b+\varepsilon}{N}}\Big(\int_{|x|\leq R}|u_{n}-u|^{\frac{Np}{N-b-\varepsilon}}dx\Big)^{\frac{N-b-\varepsilon}{N}},

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq R}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}-u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{R^{b}}\int|u_{n}-u|^{p}dx.

For given
p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
, taking
\varepsilon>0
small enough, such that
\frac{bN}{b+\varepsilon}<N
and
\frac{Np}{N-b-\varepsilon}<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
, then we get (
7.4
) from the above estimates.

Now we prove (
7.2
). Note that when
p>0
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
, such that for all
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

(7.5)

\displaystyle\Big||a+b|^{p}-|b|^{p}\Big|\leq\varepsilon|b|^{p}+C_{\varepsilon}|a|^{p}.

(See also
[
34
, Theorem 1.9]
). Taking
a=u_{n}-u,b=u
in (
7.5
), then

\Big||u_{n}|^{p}-|u|^{p}\Big|\leq\varepsilon|u|^{p}+C_{\varepsilon}|u_{n}-u|^{p},

thus, using (
7.3
), we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int|x|^{-b}|u_{n}|^{p}dx-\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int|x|^{-b}\Big||u_{n}|^{p}-|u|^{p}\Big|dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}dx+C_{\varepsilon}\int|x|^{-b}|u_{n}-u|^{p}dx

(7.6)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}dx+o_{n}(1).

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, then from (
7.6
), we obtain (
7.2
). Finally, combing (
7.2
) and (
7.4
), then (
7.1
) follows.

(
7.3
) has already been proved in the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
, here we will not repeat the same argument.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

(
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
)
Let
u(x)
be a nonnegative function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, vanishing at infinity, and let
u^{*}
be the Schwartz symmetrization of
u
. Then

(7.7)

\displaystyle\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}dx\leq\int|x|^{-b}|u^{*}|^{p}dx,\ b>0,

with the understanding that when the left side is infinite so is the right side. Moreover, the equality in (
7.7
) holds if and only if
u=u^{*}
.

In what follows, inspired by
[
16
, Theorem 8.1.1]
, we prove the exponential decay of the solutions of (
1.1
), which is interesting by itself, but also may be helpful to verify (
5.14
).

Lemma 7.3
.

Assume that
2<p<\frac{q(N-b)}{(N-q)^{+}}
, with
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
,
N\geq 1
,
q\geq 2
, and
u
satisfies Eq. (
1.1
) with
\lambda<-2
, then the following properties hold:

(1)

u\in W^{2,r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, for every
r\geq 2
. In particular,
u\in C^{2,\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, for all
\delta\in(0,1)
and as
|x|\rightarrow+\infty
,

|D^{\beta}u(x)|\rightarrow 0,\quad\forall\ |\beta|\leq 2.

(2)

There holds that

e^{\frac{1}{2}|x|}(|u(x)|+|\nabla u(x)|)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Proof of Lemma
7.3
.

We mainly use the idea from
[
16
]
to prove the theorem. The point
(1)
follows from the standard elliptic regularity theory, here we omit the details. Now we prove the point
(2)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\theta_{\varepsilon}(x)=e^{\frac{|x|}{1+\varepsilon|x|}}
, then clearly
\theta_{\varepsilon}
is bounded, Lipschitz Continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
|\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}|\leq\theta_{\varepsilon}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Multiplying Eq. (
1.1
) with
\theta_{\varepsilon}u
, we have

(7.8)

\int-\triangle u\cdot(\theta_{\varepsilon}u)dx-\int\triangle_{q}u\cdot(\theta_{\varepsilon}u)dx=\int\lambda\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx+\int\theta_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

Note that, by the integrating by parts,

\begin{split}\int-\triangle u\cdot(\theta_{\varepsilon}u)dx&=\int\nabla u\cdot\nabla(\theta_{\varepsilon}u)dx\\
&=\int\nabla u\cdot\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}\cdot udx+\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\\
&\geq-\int|\nabla u||u|\theta_{\varepsilon}dx+\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}dx,\end{split}

\begin{split}\int-\triangle_{q}u\cdot(\theta_{\varepsilon}u)dx&=\int-div(|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)\cdot(\theta_{\varepsilon}u)dx\\
&=\int|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(\theta_{\varepsilon}u)dx\\
&=\int|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}\cdot udx+\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{q}dx\\
&\geq-\int|\nabla u|^{q-1}|u|\theta_{\varepsilon}dx+\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{q}dx.\end{split}

By the Young inequality, we have

\int|\nabla u||u|\theta_{\varepsilon}dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx,

\int|\nabla u|^{q-1}|u|\theta_{\varepsilon}dx\leq\frac{q-1}{q}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{q}dx+\frac{1}{q}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx.

Then,

\int-\triangle u\cdot(\theta_{\varepsilon}u)dx\geq\frac{1}{2}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx,

\int-\triangle_{q}u\cdot(\theta_{\varepsilon}u)dx\geq\frac{1}{q}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{q}dx-\frac{1}{q}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx.

Now, from (
7.8
), we have

\int\theta_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx+\lambda\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx\geq\frac{1}{2}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{q}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{q}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{q}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx,

which leads to

(7.9)

\int\theta_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx+\frac{1}{q}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx\geq\frac{1}{2}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{q}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{q}dx-(\lambda+\frac{1}{2})\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx.

By the point
(1)
, we know that
u(x)\rightarrow 0
, as
|x|\rightarrow+\infty
. Thus, there exists
R>1
, such that

|u(x)|\leq 1,\ \mbox{as}\ |x|\geq R.

Therefore,

(7.10)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx&=\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx+\int_{|x|\geq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx\\
&\leq\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx+\int_{|x|\geq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx\\
&\leq\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx.\end{split}

Similarly,

(7.11)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\theta_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx&\leq\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx+\frac{1}{R^{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx\\
&\leq\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx.\end{split}

Thus taking (
7.10
) and (
7.11
) into (
7.9
),

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{q}\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{q}dx-(\lambda+\frac{1}{2})\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx\\
\leq&\frac{1}{q}\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx+\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx+(\frac{1}{q}+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx\\
\leq&\frac{1}{q}\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx+\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx+\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx.\end{split}

Then

(7.12)

\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{q}dx-2(\lambda+2)\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx\leq\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx+2\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx.

Since
\theta_{\varepsilon}=e^{\frac{|x|}{1+\varepsilon|x|}}\leq e^{|x|}
,
u\in C^{2,\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\lambda<-2
and
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
, then

\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{q}dx+2\int_{|x|\leq R}\theta_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{b}}dx<+\infty.

Thus, (
7.12
) implies that

\max\{\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}dx,\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{q}dx,\int\theta_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}dx\}\leq\widetilde{C}(u).

where
\widetilde{C}(u)>0
.
Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, then we have

(7.13)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{|x|}|u(x)|^{2}dx\leq\widetilde{C}(u),\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{|x|}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx\leq\widetilde{C}(u).

and by the point
(1)
,
u
and
|\nabla u|
are all globally Lipschitz continuous in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, hence we conclude from (
7.13
) that

e^{\frac{1}{2}|x|}\Big(|u(x)|+|\nabla u(x)|\Big)\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

which is the point
(2)
.
∎

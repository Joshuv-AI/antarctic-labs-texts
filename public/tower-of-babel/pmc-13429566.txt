Antimicrobial activity of echinacea-loaded metal-organic framework against Pseudomonas aeruginosa
Authors: Fatemeh Ashrafi, Pooria Moulavi, Zahra Jamalipanah, Maryam Jafari, Abolfazl Rafati Zomorodi, Sepideh Asadi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study evaluated the antibacterial and antibiofilm activity of Echinacea-loaded amino-functionalized UIO-66 MOF nanoparticles against clinical Pseudomonas aeruginosa isolates. Echinacea-loaded amino-functionalized UIO-66 metal–organic framework nanoparticles (UIO-66-NH₂-Ea) were synthesized and characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), entrapment efficiency (EE%), in vitro release studies of Echinacea angustifolia, and physical stability assessments. Antibacterial assays, including minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC), minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC), time-kill assays, and biofilm inhibition assays, were performed. Gene expression of arr-2 and arr-4 was analyzed using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) following treatment with UIO-66-NH₂-Ea. Echinacea-loaded UIO-66-NH₂ nanoparticles showed a size of 235.0 ± 7.4 nm, PDI of 0.140 ± 0.013, and entrapment efficiency of 72.53 ± 1.83%. A sustained release (~ 60% at 72 h) was observed compared to nearly complete release of free extract within 24 h. Stability showed significant changes in particle size, PDI, and entrapment efficiency over 60 days (p < 0.001). MIC (15.62–125 µg/mL) and MBC (31.25–250 µg/mL) values were up to four-fold lower than free Echinacea angustifolia (250–500 and 250–1000 µg/mL). Time-kill assays confirmed superior bactericidal activity. Biofilm formation was significantly inhibited (p < 0.001), and arr-2 and arr-4 expression was markedly downregulated compared to free extract (p < 0.001). These findings highlight the in vitro potential of Echinacea-loaded UIO-66-NH2 nanoparticles as an effective antibacterial and antibiofilm platform against P. aeruginosa isolates. However, further in vivo studies are required to evaluate their safety and efficacy, and to better assess their translational potential.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13568-026-02073-x.Keywords: Antibiofilm, Antimicrobial resistance, Echinacea angustifolia, Metal-organic framework, Pseudomonas aeruginosa

Introduction

The emergence of antibiotic-resistant bacteria has become a major global health issue and requires the discovery of new antimicrobial agents. Among these pathogens, Pseudomonas aeruginosa (P. aeruginosa) stands out due to its innate resistance mechanisms and ability to acquire resistance to multiple antibiotics (Mancuso et al. 2021). This opportunistic pathogen is associated with a variety of infections, especially in immunocompromised individuals, leading to increased mortality rates (Ahmed et al. 2025). P. aeruginosa is a gram-negative bacterium known for its adaptability and resilience in various environments, including soil, water, and hospital settings (Zomorodi et al. 2022). It is notorious for causing nosocomial infections, especially in patients with cystic fibrosis, burns, and those with indwelling medical devices (Verdial et al. 2023). The pathogenicity of P. aeruginosa is exacerbated by its ability to form biofilms, which contribute to its resistance to host immune responses and antibiotic treatments (Lashaki et al. 2025). The need for alternative therapeutic strategies to combat such infections is urgent, especially given the growing prevalence of antibiotic resistance and great ability to form biofilms (Qin et al. 2022).

Many novel advancements have recently been reported in the application of nanomaterials and nanotechnology in treating infections owing to their additional physical and chemical features (Emad et al. 2024). Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) have attracted attention due to their tunable structures and potential applications in drug delivery and antimicrobial therapy (Li et al. 2024). UIO-66, a zirconium-based MOF, exhibits remarkable stability and high surface area, making it an attractive candidate for loading therapeutic agents (Hu et al. 2024). Functionalization of UIO-66 with amine groups (UIO-66-NH₂) can enhance interactions with biological molecules and may improve antimicrobial performance. Such modifications aim to create a substrate that not only provides antimicrobial agents but also exhibits intrinsic bioactivity against pathogenic strains (Dastneshan et al. 2023).

Plants represent a promising source of alternative antibacterial agents to address the shortage of novel antibiotics and the growing problem of antimicrobial resistance (Abdallah et al. 2023). Indeed, plants have a variety of powerful defensive mechanisms, such as the synthesis of secondary metabolites, to resist pests and diseases before they can cause major harm (Chassagne et al. 2021). Echinacea angustifolia, a member of the Asteraceae family, has been traditionally used for its medicinal properties, particularly in enhancing immune function and fighting infections (Ahmadi 2024). The extract of this plant is rich in bioactive compounds such as phenolic acids and alkylamides, which have antimicrobial properties (Ahmadi 2024). Recent studies have indicated the potential of Echinacea extracts in inhibiting the growth of various pathogens and suggest that they may serve as an effective adjunct to conventional antimicrobial therapies (Zygmunt et al. 2024). However, plant extracts can improve health, but they may also have minor to severe side effects, such as allergic responses, digestive problems, and drug interactions (Ekor 2014).

Encapsulation of Echinacea angustifolia extract into UIO-66-NH₂ may represent a promising strategy to enhance antimicrobial efficacy against P. aeruginosa. The combination of MOFs with natural extracts may lead to improved inhibition of bacterial growth and biofilm formation through synergistic effects (Sainz-Urruela et al. 2021). Such nanoformulation may also contribute to reducing the emergence of resistance by improving local delivery and sustained release of antimicrobial compounds (Bazzazan et al. 2023). Previous studies have investigated antimicrobial agents and various strategies to combat P. aeruginosa, including the use of plant extracts, nanoparticles, and novel synthetic compounds. However, there is a significant lack of research focused on combining MOFs with natural extracts to enhance antimicrobial activity against this pathogen (Singh et al. 2020).

While significant progress has been made in understanding the antimicrobial properties of individual agents, there is a gap in knowledge about the synergistic effects of combining amine-containing MOFs with plant extracts. Few studies have investigated the potential of such composites to reduce bacterial viability and biofilm formation, especially against resistant strains of P. aeruginosa. Therefore, the development of an UIO-66-NH2 containing Echinacea angustifolia extract could provide a promising strategy to enhance antimicrobial activity and overcome the challenges posed by resistant pathogens. Although UIO-66-NH₂ has previously been investigated as a nanocarrier for antimicrobial agents, the conceptual novelty of the present work lies in the incorporation of a complex natural antimicrobial payload (Echinacea angustifolia extract) into an amine-functionalized UIO-66 platform, followed by a comprehensive evaluation of its antibiofilm efficacy and molecular effects on rifampicin resistance-related genes (arr-2 and arr-4) in clinical P. aeruginosa isolates.

he aim of this study was to evaluate the antimicrobial and antibiofilm activity of Echinacea angustifolia extract-loaded amino-functionalized UIO-66 nanoparticles (UIO-66-NH₂-Ea) against pathogenic strains of P. aeruginosa. In addition, this study investigated the expression of arr-2 and arr-4 genes in response to treatment.

Material and methods

UIO-66-NH2-Ea preparation

A solution was prepared by dissolving 0.41 g of 2-amino terephthalic acid (purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, Germany) and 0.54 g of ZrCl₄ in 31 mL of dimethylformamide (DMF) at 25 °C to synthesize UIO-66-NH₂. The mixture was continuously stirred until a clear solution was obtained. The solution was then transferred to a Teflon-lined hydrothermal autoclave and heated at 120 °C for 24 h. Following this, it was allowed to cool to room temperature. The resulting particles underwent three centrifugation cycles and were subjected to ultrasonic treatment in DMF and chloroform for 15 min to replace DMF and remove any residual unreacted materials. Five days after its initial synthesis, the UIO-66 sample underwent a solvent exchange process, which involved daily sonication for 15 min while being fully immersed in 15 mL of fresh chloroform. Subsequently, the nanoparticles were pressure-dried at 120 °C to eliminate any remaining solvents (Rahmanian et al. 2024).

Echinacea angustifolia was sourced from the plant bank of the Center for Biological Resources of Iran and was officially approved for research by the botanical department under the reference number 1659. To incorporate Echinacea angustifolia into UIO-66-NH₂, the plant extract was first dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and then mixed with Phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) (Gibco, USA) at a concentration of 0.25 mg/mL. This solution was subsequently combined with 10 mg of the synthesized MOFs. The resulting slurry was stirred for 24 h and subjected to three washing cycles using ethanol and distilled water. Finally, the mixture was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 20 min (Bazzazan et al. 2023).

Characterization tests

The morphology of UIO-66-NH₂-Ea was analyzed using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with a TESCAN VEGA 3SB instrument and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) with a Philips CM30 instrument (Netherlands). The hydrodynamic diameter and size distribution of the UIO-66-NH₂-Ea compound were determined using dynamic light scattering (DLS) with a Malvern Zetasizer Nano instrument (Malvern Instruments, Worcestershire, UK). The molecular interactions between the Echinacea angustifolia extract and UIO-66-NH₂-Ea were investigated using Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy (Spectrum Two, PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA) in the spectral range of 4000–400 cm⁻¹ with a resolution of 4 cm⁻¹ (Rahmanian et al. 2024).

Entrapment efficiency (EE%)

EE% was evaluated by determining the concentration of unencapsulated Echinacea angustifolia extract. The untrapped plant extract was separated from UIO-66-NH₂ using Ultracel-30 K Millipore filters with a molecular weight cut-off (MWCO) of 12,000 Da. The formulation was placed in the inner compartment of the filtration unit with a total volume of 500 µL. The system was then centrifuged at 4000×g for 20 min at 4 °C using a refrigerated centrifuge (Eppendorf®ฏ 580R, Germany). The concentration of the free extract in the outer compartment was quantified using UV-visible spectrophotometry at a wavelength of 365 nm (JASCO, V-530, Japan). The EE% was subsequently calculated using Eq. 1 (Rahmanian et al. 2024).

In vitro release study

The release of Echinacea angustifolia from UIO-66-NH₂-Ea was examined using the dialysis diffusion bag method with a molecular weight cut-off of 12 kDa (Merck, Germany). For this purpose, 2 mL of the MOF suspension was placed into a dialysis bag, which was then securely sealed. The sealed bag was immersed in 50 mL of Phosphate-buffered saline-Sodium dodecyl sulfate (PBS-SDS) (pH 5.4 and 7.4) at 37 °C, serving as the release medium. To ensure continuous mixing, the setup was placed on a magnetic stirrer set at 300 RPM. At predetermined intervals, samples were withdrawn from the buffer solution and replaced with fresh PBS-SDS (Merck, Germany) to maintain stable release conditions. The amount of released Echinacea angustifolia was quantified using an equation derived from a standard calibration curve, and a cumulative release profile was plotted over time. To characterize the release kinetics, different mathematical models—including zero-order, first-order, Higuchi, and Korsmeyer–Peppas models—were applied. A similar analysis was also performed for free drug solutions with equivalent initial concentrations using the same dialysis setup (Shiri and Ashrafi 2023).

Physical stability study

The stability of UIO-66-NH₂-Ea was monitored over a 60-day period at two different temperatures (25 ± 2 °C and 4 ± 2 °C). At predefined time intervals (14, 30, and 60 days), the dynamic particle size, polydispersity index (PDI), and EE% were analyzed and compared with freshly prepared formulations to assess any potential changes in structural integrity and stability over time (Rahmanian et al. 2024).

Bacterial isolation

In the current investigation, 15 archived P. aeruginosa isolates were analyzed to demonstrate the antimicrobial and anti-biofilm activity of Echinacea angustifolia and UIO-66-NH₂-Ea. These isolates were obtained from various clinical specimens, including blood, wounds, pus, sputum, and urine, collected from different hospitals in Iran between September 2021 and March 2022. The P. aeruginosa isolates were stored in tryptic soy broth (TSB) (Merck, Germany) supplemented with 30% glycerol at -70 °C in the Microbiology Laboratory of the North Tehran Branch, Islamic Azad University, Tehran, Iran. To ensure their purity, all isolates were recultured and reidentified using biochemical standard tests, as described previously (Zomorodi et al. 2022).

Antibacterial assay

The minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of Echinacea angustifolia and UIO-66-NH₂-Ea was determined using the standard microbroth dilution method recommended by Clinical and Laboratory of Standards Institute (CLSI) guidelines (M100-Ed31) (CLSI 2021). The concentration range tested was from 2 to 512 µg/mL, utilizing serial dilution in a 96-well microplate containing Mueller-Hinton broth (MHB) (Merck, Germany). The final inoculum size of the P. aeruginosa isolates was adjusted to 5 × 105 CFU/mL in TSB medium. To achieve this, an initial inoculum of 1 × 106 CFU/mL was prepared in TSB and then diluted 1:2 in MHB. Two wells in each row were designated as positive and negative controls. The positive control contained only the medium and the P. aeruginosa strain ATCC 27,853, while the negative control contained only the medium and either Echinacea angustifolia or UIO-66-NH₂-Ea suspensions. Plates were incubated overnight at 37 °C. Following incubation, the optical density (OD) of each well was measured using a microplate reader (EPOCH, Japan) at 600 nm. The MIC was defined as the lowest concentration at which no visible bacterial growth was observed. The minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC) was defined as the lowest concentration required to completely eradicate the bacteria following subculturing on an agar plate (CLSI 2021).

Time-kill assay

The time-kill assay was performed using the 96-well plate method to evaluate the bactericidal activity of Echinacea angustifolia and UIO-66-NH₂-Ea. The compounds were diluted to concentrations corresponding to their respective MIC values. Each well of the microtiter plate was filled with 100 µL of each sample, followed by the addition of 100 µL of the bacterial solution (equal to 1 × 106 CFU/mL) to achieve a final concentration of 105 CFU/mL in each well. The plates were then incubated at 37 °C. At various time points (8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 56, 64, and 72 h), the OD at 600 nm was measured using a microplate reader to monitor bacterial growth. The curve of bacterial proliferation served as the positive control in this assay (Sadeghi et al. 2019).

Biofilm formation assay

The ability of the P. aeruginosa isolate, treated with Echinacea angustifolia and UIO-66-NH₂-Ea, to form biofilms was evaluated using a 96-well microtiter plate technique, as previously described. In brief, the isolates capable of biofilm formation were cultured in the 96-well plates for 24 h at 37 °C. The strains were then treated with the MIC values of each sample for an additional 24 h at 37 °C. After incubation, the plates were washed with PBS to remove any unattached bacteria. The biofilms were then stained with a 1% (w/v) crystal-violet (CV) solution, followed by dissolution in 95% ethanol. The OD of each well was measured at 570 nm using an ELISA reader (Stat Fax 2100, USA). The average absorbance values of each sample were calculated and compared to the control (untreated isolates) to assess the biofilm inhibition effect (Emad et al. 2024).

Real-Time Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assay

The mRNA expression levels of arr-2 and arr-4 genes in P. aeruginosa isolates were evaluated after exposure to sub-MIC concentrations of Echinacea angustifolia and UIO-66-NH₂-Ea formulations using quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR). Initially, total bacterial RNA was extracted following the protocol provided by the RNX-Plus extraction kit (CinnaGen, Iran). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using the RevertAidTM First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Fermentas, USA). The concentration of the synthesized cDNA was measured using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer.

Gene expression was quantified by qRT-PCR using a SYBR Green-based Master Mix (Amplicon, Denmark). The qRT-PCR reaction mixture consisted of 25 µL total volume, comprising 1 µL of diluted cDNA, 1 µL of each forward and reverse primer, and 12.5 µL of SYBR Green Master Mix. The PCR protocol included an initial denaturation step at 95 °C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of: denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, annealing at 60 °C for 1 min, and extension at 72 °C for 45 s, with a final extension at 72 °C for 10 min. The 16 S rRNA gene was used as a reference control, and the relative gene expression was calculated using the 2 − ΔΔCt method, where gene expression in treated samples was normalized to the reference gene and compared to untreated controls. The sequences of the oligonucleotide primers used in this study are presented in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA) and SPSS version 20. All experiments were conducted in triplicate, with results expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Intergroup differences were evaluated using analysis of variance (ANOVA). A 95% confidence level was applied, and statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Discussion

Overuse and misuse of antibiotics have led to the development of resistance to most commonly used antibiotics (Zomorodi et al. 2022). This growing problem necessitates the development of new drug candidates, which is a time-consuming and expensive process. The advancement of nanotechnology has brought attention to the potential of enhancing the activity of existing antibiotics. MOFs have emerged as promising materials in this regard, either as antibacterial agents or as vehicles to control the release of drugs, addressing this challenge (Noorian et al. 2020).

The objective of this study was to investigate the anti-Pseudomonas activity of UIO-66-NH2-Ea. Additionally, the antibacterial activity of Echinacea angustifolia was also evaluated. The aim was extended to explore the antibacterial and antibiofilm activities of UIO-66-NH2-Ea. The UIO-66-NH2-Ea formulations were prepared, and their physicochemical properties were examined using techniques previously described in other studies (Parsaei and Akhbari 2023; Pan et al. 2022).

Our results indicated that the designed UIO-66-NH2-Ea demonstrated typical physicochemical properties, including particle dimensions, shape, and EE, which were consistent with the data reported in earlier publications (Farahbakhsh et al. 2025; Lashaki et al. 2025; Rahmanian et al. 2024). The improved antibacterial performance of the nanoformulation can be primarily attributed to its sustained release behavior, increased stability of the loaded phytochemicals, and enhanced interaction with bacterial cell surfaces compared to the free extract. These combined effects likely result in prolonged exposure of bacterial cells to active compounds and improved cellular uptake compared to the non-encapsulated extract. UIO-66-NH2-Ea exhibited a bi-phasic release pattern. The initial rapid release phase was attributed to the drug being adsorbed onto the surface of the MOF, while the sustained release could be ascribed to the high loading capacity of the drug within the nanocarrier cavities. The release kinetics were found to follow the Kremser-Peppas model, suggesting that the drug release is controlled by a combination of diffusion and erosion mechanisms (Ahmadi et al. 2022). Furthermore, the stability of UIO-66-NH2-Ea stored at 4 ± 2 °C appeared to be enhanced, possibly due to the reduced influence of temperature on the bonding between zirconium and oxygen. This sustained release behavior and improved stability may contribute to prolonged exposure of P. aeruginosa to the active phytochemicals, which could partly explain the enhanced antibacterial and antibiofilm activity of UIO-66-NH₂-Ea compared to the free extract.

The MIC and MBC values of Echinacea angustifolia and UIO-66-NH2-Ea formulations were determined in 15 bacterial isolates, with results showing that the MIC and MBC values of UIO-66-NH2-Ea were at least four times lower than those of Echinacea angustifolia. This difference could be attributed to the genetic diversity of the bacterial isolates from clinical samples and the various resistance mechanisms employed by the bacteria, which may cause variability in the MIC values (Milani et al. 2023).

The Time-kill assay further confirmed that UIO-66-NH2-Ea exhibited a stronger growth-inhibitory effect (OD-based assessment) against both standard and clinical P. aeruginosa strains. This effect reflects enhanced suppression of bacterial proliferation rather than direct CFU-based bactericidal confirmation, consistent with the OD-based methodology used in this study. This enhanced effect is likely due to synergistic interactions between the MOF carrier and the plant extract, where controlled release maintains a sustained antimicrobial concentration, while the nanoscale structure facilitates closer contact with bacterial membranes and increases permeability. Additionally, UIO-66-NH2 may stabilize bioactive compounds, prolonging their activity (Huang et al. 2011). These findings are consistent with previous studies reporting improved antimicrobial performance of encapsulated phytochemicals in nanocarriers (Moghtaderi et al. 2021; Nasrabadi et al. 2019; Lashaki et al. 2025; Rahmanian et al. 2024).

Biofilm formation is a critical factor in persistent bacterial infections (Verdial et al. 2023). The enhanced antibiofilm activity observed in this study may be attributed to improved penetration of nanoformulated compounds into the biofilm matrix, disruption of extracellular polymeric substances, and sustained release within the biofilm environment. These effects collectively prevent bacterial aggregation and reduce biofilm maturation. Similar findings have been reported for other UIO-66-based antimicrobial systems (Al-Wrafy et al. 2022); Lashaki et al. (2025).

Rifampin resistance remains a major global health concern (Sudzinová et al. 2023). Resistance is often associated with rpoB mutations or acquisition of arr genes encoding ADP-ribosyltransferases (Morgado et al. 2021). Notably, this study provides the first evidence (to our knowledge) that UIO-66-NH2-Ea can downregulate arr-2 and arr-4 gene expression in clinical isolates of P. aeruginosa, suggesting a potential interference with rifampicin resistance mechanisms at the molecular level. However, this effect should be interpreted with caution, as it may be partially related to reduced bacterial growth under treatment conditions, and therefore a direct gene-specific regulatory effect cannot be conclusively confirmed in the present study. This finding is consistent with previous reports showing gene-silencing effects of UIO-66-based nanocomposites on virulence and resistance genes (Lashaki et al. 2025).

Conclusion

In this study, UIO-66-NH2-Ea was successfully synthesized, demonstrating significant physicochemical properties, including optimal dimensions, shape, entrapped efficiency, controlled drug release, and physical stability. These properties contributed to enhanced stability and antimicrobial efficacy compared to free extract. Furthermore, the resulting MOF exhibited notable activity against biofilm formation by P. aeruginosa isolates and effectively reduced the expression of rifampicin-resistant genes compared to the free extract.

This research provides valuable insights into potential in vitro strategies for addressing infections caused by P. aeruginosa by utilizing plant-derived compounds in combination with MOFs. Future studies are essential to identify the active compounds in plant extracts and to explore the underlying mechanisms involved.

The findings presented here open the door for further research on the potential of UIO-66-NH2 and plant extracts to improve stability and antibacterial activity. Although the clinical therapeutic efficacy has not yet been established, the proposed UIO-66-NH2-based drug delivery system holds promises for reducing reliance on conventional antibiotics through controlled release of plant-derived bioactive compounds in vitro.

Solution to the uncertainty problem of nuclear matrix element for neutrinoless double- 𝜷 \bm{\beta} decay.
J. Terasaki, O. Civitarese

Abstract
The neutrinoless double- β \beta decay ( 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta ) of nuclei is one of the major research subjects of neutrino physics nowadays because of its influence on particle physics and astrophysics. The predicted nuclear matrix elements (NMEs) of the 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta decay have a large uncertainty depending on the models used to calculate them. This problem has affected the development of neutrino physics for many years. We have performed, recently, the calculation of the NMEs for the 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta and two-neutrino double- β \beta decay ( 2 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 2\nu\beta\beta ) modes with a perturbed transition operator and found that the effective axial-vector current coupling ( g A eff g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}} ) is similar for these two decay modes. Based on this finding, we calculate the 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta NMEs using the phenomenological g A eff g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}} that reproduces the measured half-life of the 2 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 2\nu\beta\beta decay. We apply this method to the NMEs for 136 Xe obtained by several groups and show that the uncertainty of the 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta NME is dramatically reduced. Owing to this finding, we calculate the effective neutrino mass, consistent with the current experimental lower limit of the half-life for the 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta decay, and the results indicate that this effective neutrino mass value does not yet reach the inverted mass hierarchy region allowed by the neutrino oscillation data and the lightest neutrino mass assumed to be smaller than 10 meV.



The
0\nu\beta\beta
decay is a door to new physics because its detection would imply the need to extend the standard model of the electroweak interactions and will establish the Majorana nature of the neutrino.
This decay is expected to occur from one nucleus to another with a suitable Q value. Thus, the study of the
0\nu\beta\beta
decay is an interdisciplinary field involving particle physics and nuclear physics.
The nuclei expected to have the
0\nu\beta\beta
decay are relatively heavy ones, so that approximations are necessary to calculate the nuclear part of the transition matrix element (NMEs). Nuclear theorists have paid intensive efforts to obtain the accurate NMEs for more than 40 years, but the calculated NMEs are distributed in a broad range
Agostini et al. 2023
, with which the researchers are not satisfied. The calculations cannot be confirmed directly by experiments, and the uncertainty in the values of the NMEs has not been reduced since this problem was recognized. The purpose of this article is to show a solution to this uncertainty problem affecting the
0\nu\beta\beta
NMEs.

Recently, we extended the equation of the NME for the
0\nu\beta\beta
decay
Terasaki and Civitarese 2025a
in terms of the perturbed transition operator. The usual equation, which we call the first order, is derived using the first-order Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbation. We derived the NME including the transition operator perturbed by the nucleon-nucleon interaction, using the second-order Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbation and the diagrams expressing the decay process. Our higher-order terms consist of the vertex correction and the two-body current terms. The corresponding equations for the two-neutrino double-
\beta
(
2\nu\beta\beta
) NME were also derived.

The framework of our calculation can be summarized as follows.
The perturbed NMEs for the
0\nu\beta\beta
and the
2\nu\beta\beta
decays of
136

The strengths of the like-particle pairing interactions are determined to reproduce the empirical pairing gaps obtained from the odd-even mass differences through the three-point formula
Bohr and Mottelson 1969
by the Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov (HFB) calculations
Terán et al. 2003
;
Blazkiewicz et al. 2005
;
Oberacker et al. 2007

The strength of the isovector proton-neutron pairing interaction is fixed at the value for which the Fermi component of the leading-order
2\nu\beta\beta

Terasaki 2016
;
Terasaki 2018

0\nu\beta\beta
NME to that obtained in the process of the two-neutron annihilation followed by the two-proton addition under the closure approximation. The convergence of the results upon changes in the dimensions of both the single-particle basis and the excited states of the participant double-odd mass nucleus was checked.

We discussed the theoretical reason for this similarity in Ref.

g_{A,0\nu}^{\mathrm{eff}}
and
g_{A,2\nu}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is not a coincidence.

This similarity enables us to approximate

The QRPA approach uses the overlap of the two intermediate states obtained by the QRPA from the initial and the final ground states. Fang et al. used an approximate equation of the QRPA intermediate-state overlap proportional to the BCS ground state overlap of the initial and the final nuclei. This property is exact, as seen from the exact equation of the QRPA intermediate-state overlap
Terasaki 2013

If the overlap of two normalized wave functions is equal to 1, the two wave functions are identical. If the particle number is a good quantum number, the overlap of the wave functions of different nuclei vanishes. The BCS overlap much smaller than 1 is more reliable than that close to 1.
Fang et al. emphasize that the NME is reduced due to the small BCS ground-state overlap.
We speculate that the previous calculations of their group did not include this BCS ground-state overlap for spherical nuclei. This is indicated in Ref.

In summary, we have proposed a new method to calculate the
0\nu\beta\beta
NMEs and demonstrated its effect using a set of different calculations for the case of the decay of
136
Xe. The principle is to apply
g_{A,2\nu}^{\mathrm{eff}}
for the
0\nu\beta\beta
calculation. This approximation is justified by the extended NMEs calculation with the perturbed transition operator and the analytical discussion paying attention to the role of the neutrino potential in
g_{A,0\nu}^{\mathrm{eff}}
.
The important support for our method is that the ratio of
g_{A,0\nu}^{\mathrm{eff}}/g_{A,2\nu}^{\mathrm{eff}}
nearly converges at the first-order perturbation. The range of the distribution of
g_{A}^{2}M_{0\nu}
is dramatically reduced by our method compared to the previous results.
We have discussed the implication of the modified NMEs for
\langle m_{\nu}\rangle
and
T_{1/2}^{0\nu}
. The neutrino oscillation experiments give a constraint on
\langle m_{\nu}\rangle
. By combining this constraint and our modified NMEs, the prospects were obtained for the possibility to find the
0\nu\beta\beta
decay. It is stressed that many results after the modification indicate these prospects unifiedly.

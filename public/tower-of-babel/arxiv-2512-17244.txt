The investigation of finite highly-arc-transitive digraphs traces back at least to 1989, when the second author
[

In this paper, a digraph

-arcs. We sometimes refer to the

such that

-arc-transitive, for some

The directed cycle

1.1.
Commentary and open problems

We finish this section with some remarks and commentary, including some open problems that arise naturally from considering Theorem
1.2
.

1.1.1.
The simple groups arising in Theorem
1.2
.

The nonabelian simple group arising as the socle of the final group
G^{(n)}
in Theorem
1.2
is a composition factor of the initial group
G^{(1)}
, and all the groups

are sections of the initial group

. To see this, note that the group arising from a

-normal quotient reduction, or a

-bipartite-half reduction, is a quotient of

or a normal subgroup of

, respectively (Proposition

(a) and Proposition

(a)). For a
G
-subnormal quotient reduction, the group arising is

with a nontrivial normal subgroup

(Proposition

(b)). Such a reduction is only used in the proof of Theorem

at the last step: the group

is quasiprimitive of type PA with socle
M
, and

is the nonabelian simple socle of the final almost simple group.

Thus, for a finite connected
(G,s)
-arc-transitive digraph with large

, it follows from Theorem
1.2
that some nonabelian composition factor of

is the socle of an almost simple group

for which there exists a finite connected
(G^{*},s^{*})
-arc-transitive digraph with

.

-arc-transitive digraphs

. The first two involve forming quotient graphs where we amalgamate vertices but essentially keep the same adjacency relation, while the third kind changes the nature of adjacency. These are discussed in Subsections
2.1
and
2.2
, respectively.

We often use the following fact:
if a connected vertex-transitive digraph is
(G,s)
-arc transitive, then it is also
(G,s^{\prime})
-arc transitive for all
s^{\prime}\leqslant s
(since each
s^{\prime}
-arc can be extended to an
s
-arc).

2.1.
Normal and subnormal quotients

Throughout this subsection let
\Gamma=(V,A)
be a finite connected digraph with vertex set
V
and arc set
A\subseteq V\times V
, and let
N
be a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
that is intransitive on
V
.
We introduce our most basic notion of a quotient
\Gamma_{N}
, and specialise to normal and subnormal quotients.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
V_{N}
denote that set of
N
-orbits in
V
and define the
quotient

\Gamma_{N}
to have vertex set
V_{N}
and arc set
A_{N}\subseteq V_{N}\times V_{N}
consisting of all pairs
(U,U^{\prime})\in V_{N}\times V_{N}
such that, for some
u\in U,u^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
, the pair
(u,u^{\prime})\in A
.

In particular suppose that there exists a vertex-transitive subgroup
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
containing
N
. If
N\unlhd G
, then
\Gamma_{N}
is called the
G
-normal quotient of
\Gamma
relative to
N
. Alternatively, if
N\unlhd M\unlhd G
,
N
is not normal in
G
, and
M
is transitive on
V
, then
\Gamma_{N}
is called a
G
-subnormal quotient of
\Gamma
relative to
N
.

In general a quotient
\Gamma_{N}
need not be a digraph in our sense, since there may be an arc
(U,U^{\prime})\in A_{N}
, such that
(U^{\prime},U)
is also an arc, for example,
\Gamma_{N}
may have loops. However we are concerned with
(G,s)
-are-transitive digraphs
\Gamma
, for some
s\geq 3
, and in this case all
G
-normal and
G
-subnormal quotients with at least three vertices are digraphs.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,A)
be a finite connected
G
-vertex-transitive
(G,s)
-arc-transitive digraph with
s\geqslant 1
and
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
. Let
N\leq G
with
|V_{N}|\geq 3
and
\Gamma_{N}
as in Definition
2.1
. Then
\Gamma_{N}
is connected, and moreover the following hold.

(a)

If
N\unlhd G
and
s\geqslant 2
, then
\Gamma_{N}
is a digraph and is
G/N
-vertex-transitive and
(G/N,s)
-arc-transitive.

(b)

If
N\unlhd M\unlhd G
and
s\geqslant 3
, with
N
not normal in
G
and
M
vertex-transitive on
\Gamma
, then the
G
-subnormal quotient
\Gamma_{N}
is a digraph and is
N_{G}(N)/N
-vertex-transitive and
(N_{G}(N)/N,s-1)
-arc-transitive.

Proof.

The fact that
\Gamma_{N}
is connected follows immediately from the connectedness of
\Gamma
. If
N\unlhd G
and
s\geqslant 2
as in part (a), then by
[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
,
\Gamma_{N}
is a digraph and is
(G/N,s)
-arc-transitive. On the other hand if
N\unlhd M\unlhd G
with
M
vertex-transitive and
s\geqslant 3
, as in part (b), then by
[
9
, Corollary 2.11]
,
\Gamma
is
(M,s-1)
-arc-transitive; and hence (since
s-1\geq 2
) by
[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
,
\Gamma_{N}
is a digraph and is
(M/N,s-1)
-arc-transitive, and hence also

(N_{G}(N)/N,s-1)
-arc-transitive
∎

Next we examine normal quotients which are in a sense minimal. Recall the definitions of quasiprimitive and bi-quasiprimitive for permutation groups, and the directed cycle graph
{\mathrm{C}}^{\to}_{r}
, from Section
1
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,A)
be a finite connected
G
-vertex-transitive
(G,s)
-arc-transitive digraph with
s\geqslant 2
and
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
. Let
N\unlhd G
with
|V_{N}|\geq 3
. Then the following hold.

(a)

If
N\leqslant M\unlhd G
with
|V_{M}|\geqslant 3
, then
M/N\unlhd G/N
and
\Gamma_{M}\cong(\Gamma_{N})_{M/N}
.

(b)

If
r\geqslant 3
and no
G
-normal quotient of
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
{\mathrm{C}}^{\to}_{r}
, then no
G/N
-normal quotient of
\Gamma_{N}
is isomorphic to
{\mathrm{C}}^{\to}_{r}
.

(c)

There exists
M\unlhd G
such that
N\leqslant M
and
|V_{M}|\geqslant 3
, and the
G
-normal quotient
\Gamma_{M}
is a
(G/M,s)
-arc-transitive digraph with
G/M
quasiprimitive or bi-quasiprimitive on
V_{M}
. Moreover, if
(\Gamma,G,s)\in{\mathscr{D}}
then also
(\Gamma_{M},G/M,s)\in{\mathscr{D}}
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose that
N\leqslant M\unlhd G
with
|V_{M}|\geqslant 3
. Then
M/N\unlhd G/N
and each
M
-orbit
u^{M}\in V_{M}
is a union of all the
N
-orbits of the form
(u^{N})^{x}=u^{Nx}
for
x\in M
, and it follows that the map
\phi:V_{M}\to(V_{N})_{M/N}
sending
u^{M}
to the
M/N
-orbit in
V_{N}
containing
u^{N}
is a bijection. Also by Definition
2.1
,
\phi
induces a bijection from the arcs of
\Gamma_{M}
to the arcs of
(\Gamma_{N})_{M/N}
, giving a graph isomorphism
\Gamma_{M}\to(\Gamma_{N})_{M/N}
.

(b) If
(\Gamma_{N})_{\overline{M}}\cong{\mathrm{C}}^{\to}_{r}
for some normal subgroup
\overline{M}=M/N
of
G/N
, then
N\leqslant M\unlhd G
and by part (a), the
G
-normal quotient
\Gamma_{M}
is isomorphic to
{\mathrm{C}}^{\to}_{r}
. Thus part (b) is proved.

(c) Choose a subgroup
M
that is maximal by inclusion such that
N\leqslant M
and
|V_{M}|\geqslant 3
. Such a subgroup exists because
N
has these two properties and
G
is finite. By Proposition
2.2
(a),
\Gamma_{M}
is a
(G/M,s)
-arc-transitive digraph. Further, each nontrivial normal subgroup of
G/M
is of the form
L/M
for some
L\unlhd G
properly containing
M
. It follows from the maximality of
M
that
|V_{L}|\leq 2
. Thus
L/M
has at most two orbits in
V_{M}
and so
G/M
is either quasiprimitive or bi-quasiprimitive on
V_{M}
. Finallly, if
(\Gamma,G,s)\in{\mathscr{D}}
then no
G
-normal quotient of
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
{\mathrm{C}}^{\to}_{r}
for any
r\geq 3
, and by part (b), no
G/M
-normal quotient of
\Gamma_{M}
is isomorphic to
{\mathrm{C}}^{\to}_{r}
for any
r\geq 3
. Hence
(\Gamma_{M},G/M,s)\in{\mathscr{D}}
.
∎

2.2.
Bipartite halves

Recall that a digraph is said to be bipartite if its underlying undirected graph is bipartite. For a bipartite digraph we now define its bipartite halves.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,A)
be a finite bipartite digraph with vertex set
V
and arc set
A\subseteq V\times V
, and let
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
be the parts of the bipartition, so that each arc
(u,v)\in A
consists of one vertex from each of
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
. The
bipartite halves
of
\Gamma
are the pairs
(\Delta,A_{2})
and
(\Delta^{\prime},A_{2}^{\prime})
, where

A_{2}=\{(u,w)\mid u,w\in\Delta,\exists\ v\in\Delta^{\prime}\ \mbox{such that}\ (u,v),(v,w)\in A\}
and

A_{2}^{\prime}=\{(u,w)\mid u,w\in\Delta^{\prime},\exists\ v\in\Delta\ \mbox{such that}\ (u,v),(v,w)\in A\}
.

If the bipartite digraph
\Gamma
is vertex-transitive, then each automorphism interchanging the parts
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
of the bipartition induces an isomorphism between the two bipartite halves in Definition
2.4
. Thus without loss of generality we work with
(\Delta,A_{2})
. With our applications in mind we present the following result for the case where
\Gamma
is
4
-arc-transitive. We show that
(\Delta,A_{2})
is a digraph with a single exception. It would be interesting to know what other exceptions may arise in the case, say, of bipartite
2
-arc-transitive digraphs.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,A)
be a finite connected
G
-vertex-transitive
(G,s)
-arc-transitive digraph with
s\geqslant 4
and
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
. Suppose that
\Gamma
is bipartite and that
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}=(\Delta,A_{2})
are as in Definition
2.4
. Let
G^{+}
be the (index
2
) subgroup of
G
fixing
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
setwise. Then the following hold.

(a)

\Gamma^{\prime}
is connected,
G^{+}
-vertex-transitive and
(G^{+},\lfloor s/2\rfloor)
-arc-transitive, and either
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a digraph or
\Gamma={\mathrm{C}}_{4}^{\to},G=Z_{4}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}=\mathrm{K}_{2}
;

(b)

provided
\Gamma\neq{\mathrm{C}}_{4}^{\to}
, there exists
N\unlhd G^{+}
such that the set
V_{N}
of
N
-orbits in
\Delta
satisfies
|V_{N}|\geqslant 3
, and the
G^{+}
-normal quotient
\Gamma^{\prime}_{N}
is a
(G^{+}/N,\lfloor s/2\rfloor)
-arc-transitive digraph with
G^{+}/N
quasiprimitive or bi-quasiprimitive on
V_{N}
;

(c)

if
\Gamma
has no
G
-normal quotient
{\mathrm{C}}_{r}^{\to}
for any
r\geq 3
, then
\Gamma^{\prime}
has no
G^{+}
-normal quotient
{\mathrm{C}}_{r}^{\to}
for any
r\geq 3
. Further, if
(\Gamma,G,s)\in{\mathscr{D}}
then also
(\Gamma^{\prime},G^{+},\lfloor s/2\rfloor)\in{\mathscr{D}}
.

Proof.

(a) The fact that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is connected follows from its definition and the fact that
\Gamma
is connected.
Let
s_{0}:=\lfloor s/2\rfloor
and note that
s_{0}\geqslant 2
and
2s_{0}\leqslant s
. Let
(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{s_{0}})
be an
s_{0}
-arc of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. Then, by the definition of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, there are vertices
v_{0},v_{1},\dots v_{s_{0}-1}\in\Delta^{\prime}
such that
(u_{0},v_{0},u_{1},v_{1},\dots,v_{s_{0}-1},u_{s_{0}})
is a
2s_{0}
-arc of
\Gamma
. Then since
\Gamma
is
(G,s)
-arc-transitive and
2s_{0}\leqslant s
, it follows that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is
(G^{+},s_{0})
-arc-transitive.

Suppose that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is not a digraph. Then there exist vertices
u,w\in\Delta
such that
(u,w),(w,u)\in A_{2}
. Thus there are vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}
such that
(u,v,w)
and
(w,v^{\prime},u)
are
2
-arcs of
\Gamma
, and since
\Gamma
is a digraph, the pair
(v,u)\not\in A
, so
v^{\prime}\neq v
. Therefore
(u,v,w,v^{\prime},u)
is a
4
-arc of
\Gamma
. Since
\Gamma
is
(G,4)
-arc-transitive, for each
4
-arc
(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{4})
of
\Gamma
we must have
u_{0}=u_{4}
. Since
\Gamma^{\prime}
is connected and since
G_{u_{0}}=G^{+}_{u_{0}}
is transitive on the
4
-arcs of
\Gamma
with first entry
u_{0}
, this implies that each vertex
w\in\Delta\setminus\{u_{0}\}
occurs as
w=u_{2}
in some
4
-arc
(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{4})
of
\Gamma
. Hence
\Gamma^{\prime}
is the complete directed graph
\mathrm{K}_{n}^{\to}
, where
n=|\Delta|
. However as each
4
-arc
(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{4})
of
\Gamma
has
u_{0}=u_{4}
, it follows that each
2
-arc
(u_{0},u_{2},u_{4})
of
\Gamma^{\prime}
has
u_{0}=u_{4}
, and this implies that
n=2
. Hence
\Gamma^{\prime}
is the complete undirected graph
\mathrm{K}_{2}
, and since
\Gamma
is a digraph we have
\Gamma={\mathrm{C}}_{4}^{\to}
and
G=Z_{4}
.

(b) By part (a), if
\Gamma\neq{\mathrm{C}}_{4}^{\to}
, then by part (a),
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a digraph and is
(G^{+},s_{0})
-arc-transitive with
s_{0}=\lfloor s/2\rfloor\geqslant 2
and
|\Delta|\geqslant 3
. Now the assertions of part (b) follow by applying Proposition
2.3
(c) with
(\Gamma^{\prime},G^{+},1,N,s_{0})
in place of
(\Gamma,G,N,M,s)
.

(c) Suppose now that
\Gamma
has no
G
-normal quotient
{\mathrm{C}}_{r}^{\to}
for any
r\geq 3
, and suppose to the contrary that
N\unlhd G^{+}
is such that the
G^{+}
-normal quotient
\Gamma^{\prime}_{N}={\mathrm{C}}_{r}^{\to}
, for some
r\geq 3
, with
G^{+}/N=Z_{r}
. If
r_{0}\geq 3
is any divisor of
r
we may replace
N
by a possibly larger normal subgroup
N_{0}
so that
\Gamma^{\prime}_{N_{0}}={\mathrm{C}}_{r_{0}}^{\to}
. Hence we may without loss of generality assume that either
r=4
or
r
is an odd prime. If
N\unlhd G
, then
\Gamma_{N}
is a
G
-normal quotient with
2r
vertices. However, in this case if
(U,U^{\prime},W)
is a
2
-arc of
\Gamma_{N}
with
U,W
being
N
-orbits in
\Delta
and
U^{\prime}\subset\Delta^{\prime}
, then
(U,W)
is an arc of
\Gamma^{\prime}_{N}={\mathrm{C}}_{r}^{\to}
; it follows that every 2-arc
(U,U^{\prime},W^{\prime})
of
\Gamma_{N}
must have
W^{\prime}=W
. Hence
\Gamma_{N}
has out-valency
1
, and so
\Gamma_{N}
is the directed cycle
{\mathrm{C}}_{2r}^{\to}
, which is a contradiction.

Hence
N
is not normal in
G
. Since
G
is vertex-transitive on
\Gamma
, we may choose an arc
(u,v)\in A
with
u\in\Delta,v\in\Delta^{\prime}
, and an element
g\in G
such that
u^{g}=v
. Then
g
must interchange
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
and hence
G=\langle G^{+},g\rangle
and
g^{2}\in G^{+}
. Now
M:=N^{g}\unlhd G^{+}
, and
M\neq N
as
N
is not normal in
G
, and we note that
M^{g}=N^{g^{2}}=N
since
g^{2}\in G^{+}
. Let
X:=N\cap M
, so
X\unlhd G
and
G^{+}/X
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
G^{+}/N\times G^{+}/M\cong Z_{r}\times Z_{r}
of order strictly greater than
r
(as
M\neq N
). We conclude that either (i)
G^{+}/X\cong Z_{r}^{2}
, or (ii)
r=4
and
G^{+}/X\cong Z_{4}\times Z_{2}
. Consider the
G
-normal quotient
\Gamma_{X}
, which by Proposition
2.2
is a
(G/X,4)
-arc-transitive digraph, and by assumption is not a directed cycle. Thus
|V\Gamma_{X}|\geq 2r
and
\Gamma_{X}
has valency
d\geqslant 2
, so the number of
4
-arcs is
|V\Gamma_{X}|\cdot d^{3}\geq 16r
. As
\Gamma_{X}
is
(G/X,4)
-arc-transitive,
|G/X|
is divisible by
|V\Gamma_{X}|\cdot d^{3}
, so in particular
|G/X|\geq 16r
. However in case (ii)
|G/X|=16
, so we are in case (i) with
2r^{2}=|G/X|\geq|V\Gamma_{X}|\cdot d^{3}.\geqslant 16r
. Thus
r\geqslant 8
; hence
r
is an odd prime,
G^{+}/X=Z_{r}\times Z_{r}
. This implies that the stabiliser
G_{U}/X=G^{+}_{U}/X
of an
X
-orbit
U
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
Z_{r}
and so cannot be transitive on the
d^{3}

4
-arcs of
\Gamma_{X}
with first entry
U
. This proves the first assertion of part (c), and the final assertion follows from the definition of
{\mathscr{D}}
in (
1
).
∎

3.
Quasiprimitive digraphs

In this section, we examine the automorphism groups of finite vertex-quasiprimitive
3
-arc-transitive digraphs. We use the subdivision of finite quasiprimitive permutation groups introduced in
[
13
, Section 5]
and conveniently described in
[
15
, Table 7.1]
. Each finite quasiprimitive group has one of the following types:
HA
,
HS
,
HC
,
TW
,
SD
,
CD
,
PA
or
AS
. The first result shows that the only possible types for a vertex-quasiprimitive
3
-arc-transitive group
G
acting on a digraph (apart from a directed cycle) are the types
PA
and
AS
(sometimes called product action and almost simple).

For type
PA
, the group
G
embeds in a wreath product
H\wr\mathrm{Sym}(m)
for some
m\geq 2
. Further,
G
induces a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Sym}(m)
and a stabiliser
G_{1}
in this action on
\{1,\dots,m\}
projects onto the group
H
, which is vertex-quasiprimitive of type
AS
on a set
\Delta
. The permutation group
H
on
\Delta
is called the
component
of the PA-type group
G
. More details of this
G
-action are given in
[
13
, Section 5]
and will be used in the proof.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
\Gamma
is a finite connected
G
-vertex-quasiprimitive
(G,s)
-arc-transitive digraph with
s\geqslant 2
, and that
\Gamma
is not a directed cycle.

(a)

Then
G
is of type
AS
,
SD
,
CD
or
PA
, and if
s\geqslant 3
then
G
is of type
PA
or
AS
.

(b)

Moreover, if
s\geqslant 3
and
G
is of type
PA
, such that
G\leqslant H\wr\mathrm{Sym}(m)
with
H
the almost simple component of
G
and
m\geqslant 2
, then for

M=G\cap H^{m}
,
N=G\cap(1\times H^{m-1})
, and
K=G\cap(H\times(H\wr\mathrm{Sym}(m-1))
, we have

(i)

N<M\unlhd G
,
N
is not normal in
G
,
K=N_{G}(N)
, and
K/N\cong H
; and

(ii)

the
G
-subnormal-quotient
\Gamma_{N}
is a connected,
H
-vertex-quasiprimitive,
(H,s-1)
-arc-transitive digraph, and
H
has type
AS
with socle a composition factor of
G
.

Remark 3.2
.

In Proposition
3.1
the assumption
s\geqslant 3
is necessary since, as discussed in Subsection
1.1.3
, there exist finite connected
(G,2)
-arc-transitive digraphs with
G
vertex-quasiprimitive of SD-type, see
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Proof.

(a) If
G
is of type
HA
,
HS
,
HC
or
TW
, then
G
contains a normal subgroup acting regularly on the vertex set of
\Gamma
, see
[
13
, Section 5]
. Since
\Gamma
is
(G,s)
-arc-transitive and
s\geqslant 2
, it follows from
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
that
\Gamma
is a directed cycle, which is a contradiction.
Thus
G
is of type
AS
,
SD
,
CD
or
PA
.
Further, if
G
were of type
SD
or
CD
, then by
[
9
, Theorem 1.2, Corollary 1.4]
the integer
s
must be
2
. This proves part (a).

(b) Now suppose that
G
is of type
PA
and
s\geqslant 3
. Then (see
[
13
, End of Section 5]
),

\mathrm{Soc}(G)=T^{m}\leqslant G\leqslant W=H\wr\mathrm{Sym}(m)\leqslant\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta)\wr\mathrm{Sym}(m),

where
m\geqslant 2
and
T
is a nonabelian simple group,
H
is a quasiprimitive permutation group on
\Delta
of type
AS
with non-regular socle
T
, and
G
acts transitively by conjugation on the
m
simple direct factors of
N
. Choose
\delta\in\Delta
and set
R:=T_{\delta}
, so
1<R<T
(as
T
is not regular) and
|\Delta|=|T:R|>2
(since
T
is transitive on
\Delta
and
T
is a nonabelian simple group). There is a
G
-invariant partition
\Sigma
of
V\Gamma
(where the parts of
\Sigma
may, or may not, have size 1), such that, for some
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
N_{\sigma}=R^{m}
, and for a vertex
\alpha\in\sigma
, the stabiliser
N_{\alpha}
is a subdirect subgroup of
R^{m}
.
In particular we identify
\Sigma
with the cartesian product
\Delta^{m}
with the natural product action of
W
.

To explain what we mean by
H
being the ‘almost simple component of
G
’, note that
G
projects to a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Sym}(m)
in the natural action on
\{1,\ldots,m\}
, and we let
W_{1}=H\times(H\wr\mathrm{Sym}(m-1))
be the stabiliser in
W
of
1\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Then
K=G\cap W_{1}
has index
m
in
G
, and
H
is chosen such that
H=\varphi(G_{1})
where
\varphi:W_{1}\to H
is the natural projection to the first direct factor. Note that
M=G\cap H^{m}
is normal in
G
and so is transitive on
V\Gamma
; also
N=G\cap(1\times H^{m-1})\lhd M
. On the other hand
N_{G}(N)=G\cap W_{1}=K
so
N
is not normal in
G
; in fact
N
is the kernel of the restriction
\varphi|_{G_{1}}
and
H=\varphi(G_{1})\cong K/N
. This proves part (b)(i).

Note that for each
N
-orbit in
\Sigma=\Delta^{m}
the
m
-tuples
(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{m})
in the orbit have a fixed first entry. Thus
N
has at least
|\Delta|
orbits in
\Sigma
, and hence
N
has at least
|\Delta|

is a connected digraph and is

-vertex-transitive and

with socle

∎

4.
Bi-quasiprimitive digraphs

Now we analyse the bi-quasiprimitive possibilities from Proposition
2.2
. Here we have a
G
-vertex-biquasiprimitive,
(G,s)
-arc-transitive, subgroup of automorphisms of a finite connected digraph
\Gamma
which is not a directed cycle. Thus there exists an index
2
(normal) subgroup
G^{+}
of
G
with two orbits
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
in
V\Gamma
, noting that such a subgroup
G^{+}
is not necessarily unique. The group
G
preserves the partition
\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\}
, and we write the second
G^{+}
-orbit
\Delta^{\prime}
and a ‘standard interchanging permutation’
(1,2)
as

\Delta^{\prime}=\{\delta^{\prime}\mid\delta\in\Delta\}\quad\mbox{and \quad$(1,2):\delta\to\delta^{\prime},\ \delta^{\prime}\to\delta$\quad for $\delta\in\Delta$.}

With this labelling of
V\Gamma
, and replacing
G
if necessary by a conjugate in
\mathrm{Sym}(V\Gamma)
, we therefore have
G\leqslant B\rtimes\mathrm{Sym}(2)=H\wr\mathrm{Sym}(2)
with base group
B=H^{2}
and ‘top group’
\mathrm{Sym}(2)=\langle(1,2)\rangle
, and we write

(2)

B=H^{2}=X\times Y
with
X=B\cap(H\times 1)
and
Y=B\cap(1\times H)
.

As
G
is vertex-transitive,
G
contains an element
g
interchanging
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
, and since
G^{+}
projects onto
Y
, we may choose such an element of the form
g=(x,1)(1,2)
for some
(x,1)\in X
. Thus
G\leqslant H\wr\mathrm{Sym}(2)=(X\times Y)\langle g\rangle
. Our first result restricts the subgroup
G^{+}
. Here, for
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, we write
\mathrm{Diag}_{\varphi}(X\times Y)=\{(h,h^{\varphi})\mid h\in H\}
for the corresponding diagonal subgroup of
B
. Also for
h\in H
we write
\iota_{h}
for the inner automorphism
\iota_{h}:y\to h^{-1}yh
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a finite connected
G
-vertex-bi-quasiprimitive,
(G,s)
-arc-transitive digraph, which is not a directed cycle, where
s\geqslant 2
. Let
G^{+}
be a subgroup of
G
of index
2
with two orbits
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
in
V\Gamma
, let
H
be the permutation group induced by
G^{+}
on
\Delta
, and let
B,X,Y
and
g=(x,1)(1,2)
be as above. Then
G^{+}=\mathrm{Diag}_{\varphi}(H\times H)
for some
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(H)
such that
\varphi^{2}=\iota_{x}\in\mathrm{Inn}(H)
. Moreover part (a)(i) or (c) of
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
holds.

The conclusion of Proposition
4.1
is rather technical and the two cases will be explored further in this section. We note that in
[
14
, Theorem 1.1(a)(i)]
, the group
H
is quasiprimitive on
\Delta
, while in
[
14
, Theorem 1.1(c)]
,
H
has an intransitive minimal normal subgroup
R
such that
R^{\varphi}\neq R
,
M=R\times R^{\varphi}
is transitive on
\Delta
, and
N=\mathrm{Diag}_{\varphi}(M\times M)
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is connected, there must be an arc
(\delta,\tau)
with
\delta\in\Delta
and
\tau\in\Delta^{\prime}
. (In fact each arc is either of this form or the reverse
(\tau,\delta)
, since
G
is arc-transitive.) The image
\delta^{g}=\delta^{(x,1)(1,2)}=(\delta^{x})^{(1,2)}\in\Delta^{\prime}
, and since
G^{+}
is transitive on
\Delta^{\prime}
, there is some
(z,y)\in G^{+}
such that
\tau=((\delta^{x})^{(1,2)})^{(z,y)}
. Note that

\tau=\delta^{g(z,y)}=((\delta^{x})^{(1,2)})^{(z,y)}=((\delta^{x})^{(y,z)})^{(1,2)}=(\delta^{xy})^{(1,2)}.

Since
g(z,y)\in G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
, the image
(\delta,\tau)^{g(z,y)}=(\tau,\tau^{g(z,y)})
is also an arc of
\Gamma
and
\mu:=\tau^{g(z,y)}\in(\Delta^{\prime})^{g(z,y)}=\Delta
. Thus
(\delta,\tau,\mu)
is a
2
-arc of
\Gamma
, and since
\Gamma
is a digraph, we conclude in particular that
\delta\neq\mu
. Note that

(3)

\mu=\tau^{g(z,y)}=((\delta^{xy})^{(1,2)})^{g(z,y)}=(\delta^{xy})^{(1,2)(x,1)(1,2)(z,y)}=(\delta^{xy})^{(1,x)(z,y)}=\delta^{xyz}\in\Delta\setminus\{\delta\}.

Since
\Gamma
is
(G,2)
-arc-transitive, it follows from
[
9
, Lemma 2.3]
that
G_{\tau}=G_{\delta\tau}G_{\tau\mu}
.

Suppose for a contradiction that
G^{+}
is not a diagonal subgroup of
X\times Y
. Then it follows from
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
that the subgroups
G^{+}\cap X
and
G^{+}\cap Y
are non-trivial, and one of the parts (a)(ii), (a)(iii) or (b) of
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
holds. In all these cases,
G^{+}\cap X
and
G^{+}\cap Y
act transitively on
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
, respectively, and these actions are faithful.

Claim.
G^{+}\cap X
and
G^{+}\cap Y
act regularly on
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
, respectively.

Note that
G_{\tau}=G^{+}_{\tau}
. Let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be the natural projections on
X
and
Y
, respectively. Then
\pi_{1}(G_{\tau})\cong G_{\tau}/(G_{\tau}\cap Y)
. Since

is transitive on
\Delta^{\prime}
the index
|G^{+}\cap Y|/|G_{\tau}\cap Y|=|\Delta^{\prime}|
, and since
\tau^{G^{+}}=\Delta^{\prime}
and
\pi_{1}(G^{+})=X
,

|\Delta^{\prime}|=\frac{|G^{+}|}{|G_{\tau}|}=\frac{|G^{+}\cap Y|\cdot|\pi_{1}(G^{+})|}{|G_{\tau}\cap Y|\cdot|{\pi_{1}}(G_{\tau})|}=|\Delta^{\prime}|\cdot\frac{|\pi_{1}(G^{+})|}{|{\pi_{1}}(G_{\tau})|}=|\Delta^{\prime}|\cdot\frac{|X|}{|\pi_{1}(G_{\tau})|}.

It follows that
\pi_{1}(G_{\tau})=X
, and so we have the factorisation
X=\pi_{1}(G_{\tau})=\pi_{1}(G_{\delta\tau})\pi_{1}(G_{\tau\mu})
.

Since
G_{\delta}\cap X\unlhd\,G_{\delta}
, it follows that
\pi_{1}(G_{\delta}\cap X)\unlhd\,\pi_{1}(G_{\delta})
, and since by definition
\pi_{1}(G_{\delta}\cap X)=G_{\delta}\cap X
, we have
G_{\delta}\cap X\unlhd\,\pi_{1}(G_{\delta})
. Thus
\pi_{1}(G_{\delta\tau})\leqslant\pi_{1}(G_{\delta})\leqslant N_{X}(G_{\delta}\cap X)
. The same argument with
\mu
in place of

gives
\pi_{1}(G_{\tau\mu})\leqslant\pi_{1}(G_{\mu})\leqslant N_{X}(G_{\mu}\cap X)
. Now by (

), the subgroups

and

are conjugate in

, and both are proper subgroups of

. Further, if

is not regular on
\Delta
, then these subgroups are nontrivial and are not normal in

. In this case the factorisation

contradicts
[

, Lemma 3.1]
. Hence

is regular on
\Delta
, and therefore also

is regular on

Now

fixes

and is transitive on

it follows that equality holds, that is,
\Gamma(\tau)=\Delta
. A similar argument shows that
\Gamma(\delta)=\Delta^{\prime}
, and hence
\Gamma
is the complete bipartite digraph

with bipartition

and with edges directed from

to
\Delta^{\prime}
, where

. This is a contradiction since this digraph is not vertex-transitive, whereas we are assuming that

is transitive on

This contradiction shows that

is a diagonal subgroup of

for some

, and hence part (a)(i) or (c) of

holds. Since

normalises

.

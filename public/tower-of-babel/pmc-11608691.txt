Establishment and validation of diagnostic model in immunoglobulin A nephropathy based on weighted gene co-expression network analysis
Authors: Haibo Liu, Lingling Dai, Jie Liu, Kai Duan, Feng Yi, Zhuo Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Bioinformatics analysis helps to understand the underlying mechanisms and adjust diagnostic and treatment strategies for immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) by screening gene expression datasets. We explored the biological function of IgAN, and established and validated a diagnostic model for IgAN using weighted gene co-expression network analysis. Using the GSE93798 and GSE37460 datasets, we performed differential expression analysis, Gene Oncology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis, constructed a protein-protein network, and identified hub genes. A diagnostic model was built using a receiver operating characteristic curve, calibration plot, and decision curve analysis. Two Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets were integrated to screen 38 differentially expressed genes between patients with IgAN and normal kidney donors in glomerular samples. KEGG enrichment analysis showed that the differentially expressed genes were mainly enriched in the IL-17 and relaxin signaling pathways. We constructed a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network of differentially expressed genes using the STRING database and cross-compared it with the results of weighted gene correlation network analysis to screen out the top 10 key genes: FOS, EGR2, FOSB, NR4A1, BR4A3, FOSL1, NR4A2, ALB, CD53, C3AR1.We also found that the immune infiltration level was remarkably increased in IgAN tissues. We established a 5-gene panel diagnostic model (ACTA2, ALB, AFM, ALDH1L1, and ALDH6A1). The combined diagnostic ability was high, with the area under the curve (AUC) was 0.964. Based on these 5 genes, we also developed a risk-scoring evaluation system for individuals. The calibration plot indicated that the nomogram-predicted probability of nonadherence was highly correlated with actual diagnosed nonadherence, and the decision curve analysis indicated that patients had a relatively good net benefit. The model and gene expression were also validated using an external dataset. Our study provides directions for exploring the potential molecular mechanisms of IgAN as well as diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

1. Introduction

Epidemiological investigations show that more than 50% of the pathological manifestations of primary glomerulonephritis are IgA nephropathy (IgAN), and approximately 15% to 40% of IgAN eventually progresses to end-stage renal disease, which is characterized by IgA1 deposition in the mesangium of the glomerulus. Abnormal glycosylation in the hinge region of IgA1 plays a central role in the development of the disease.[1] The exact cause of primary IgAN is unknown, but its onset is usually associated with upper respiratory tract infections and gross hematuria.[2] Common genetic variants affect the risk of IgAN across different ethnic groups. In a recent genome-wide association study of IgAN, new associations with IgAN were identified, with most sites either directly associated with the risk of inflammatory bowel disease or with maintenance of the intestinal epithelial barrier and response to mucosal pathogens.[3] Baseline renal function and proteinuria levels are currently considered predictive of IgAN progression; however, they do not accurately estimate prognosis due to individual variations in the disease.[4] In addition, some patients with mild clinical manifestations of IgAN may experience severe renal tissue damage.[5] The severity of glomerular and tubulointerstitial lesions can effectively predict the prognosis of IgAN.[6] Pathological analysis of renal biopsies is an essential diagnostic method for IgAN. Early diagnosis is crucial to prevent IgAN progression to end-stage renal disease. However, repeated renal biopsies are associated with a high risk, complicated operation, and technical difficulty. Therefore, there is an urgent need for reliable biological biomarkers that reflect the diagnosis and prognosis of IgAN.

Current research data indicate that the incidence of IgAN varies by race, there is a tendency to cluster in families, and the occurrence and development of IgAN is related to genetic factors.[7] Several studies have shown abnormal expression or mutation of genes in IgAN patients, such as mucosal infection (DEAF, ITGAM, ITGAX), mucosal IgA production (LIF, OSM)10, abnormal glycosylation of IgA1 (C1GALT1, C1G, C1G, ALT1C1), and complement activation (CFHR1 and CFHR3).[8–13] However, these abnormally expressed or mutated genes appear only in a subset of IgAN patients, suggesting that there may be many disease-related genes or loci that have not yet been identified. Therefore, the discovery of new key genes is needed to further understand the pathogenesis of IgAN and refine existing hypotheses as well as diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. The development of gene omics and bioinformatic technology has made this possible.

Bioinformatics analysis helps to understand the underlying mechanisms of IgAN by screening gene expression datasets. In the present study, we first explored differentially expressed genes between patients with IgAN and healthy controls. Second, we analyzed the functions and signaling pathways involved in these differentially expressed genes. Next, we built the protein–protein interactions of these genes. We then performed weighted gene co-expression network analysis to identify the key genes in IgAN. Finally, we established and assessed a diagnostic model of IgAN. Our comprehensive analyses provide a direction for exploring the potential molecular mechanisms, diagnosis, and treatment strategies of IgAN.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data source

Gene expression profiling data was from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Two datasets (GSE37460 and GSE93798) were used for analyses. The 2 datasets were obtained from human subjects. Ribose nucleic acids from the glomeruli and tubulointerstitial compartments were extracted and processed for hybridization on Affymetrix microarrays. GSE37460 included 27 patients with IgAN and 27 healthy living donors, and GSE93798 included 20 patients with IgAN and 22 healthy living donors. First, read in the gene expression data to be merged (GSE37460 and GSE93798) and convert the data frames into matrices. Then, we use the “normalizeBetweenArrays” function from the “limma” package to standardize the GEO datasets. This step eliminates the batch effects between samples within each dataset, making the samples comparable. Next, we obtain the common genes between the 2 datasets and merge them. Finally, we extract the group information for each dataset and use the “combat” algorithm to perform batch correction on the combined dataset. This study included 47 patients with IgAN and 49 healthy controls. Other tissue samples from 27 IgAN patients and 6 healthy living donors were also obtained from Nephroseq (https://www.nephroseq.org/resource/main.html), which was used for further validation.

2.2. Differentially expressed gene analysis

We used the R software “limma” package to identify differentially expressed genes (DGEs) between patients with IgAN and healthy controls.[14] The criteria were |log (fold change) |>1 and adjusted P < .05. Volcano plots and heat maps were used to obtain the results.

2.3. Protein–protein interaction network and hub genes

The protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using the STRING database (STRING, https://string-db.org/), and a protein interaction score > 0.4 was used as the criterion for screening.[15] We exported the protein-protein interaction (PPI) data to Cytoscape version 3.9.1 (http://cytoscape.org) for network analysis. Cytoscape’s plugin, CytoHubba, offers a user-friendly interface for exploring complex networks. CytoHubba provides a variety of topological algorithms (11 in total) to identify hub genes within the network. Since the Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm demonstrates superior performance in identifying PPI network hub genes,[16] we employed MCC in this study to pinpoint the key hub genes associated with IgA nephropathy.

2.4. GO and KEGG analysis

Genetic Ontological (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses of DGEs were performed through the “clusterProfiler” package of R language software.[17] The GO enrichment analysis included biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components. In the enrichment analysis, statistical significance was set at P < .05.

2.5. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis

To search for synergistically expressed gene modules (modules) and explore the association between gene networks and phenotypes of interest, as well as the core genes in the networks. We performed a weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) between patients with IgAN and healthy controls. This process was performed using the R software “WGCNA” package.[18]

2.6. Immune infiltration analysis

IgAN is closely associated with immune status. Therefore, we also compared the immune infiltration levels between patients with IgAN and healthy controls. To accurately assess the composition of immune cells in the tumor microenvironment, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis was used to quantify tumor-infiltrated immune cells from RNA sequencing data.[19] This process was achieved using TIMER 2.0 (http://timer.cistrome.org/). Twenty-eight immune-related cells and their functions were compared between the IgAN and control groups.

2.7. Establishment of diagnostic model in IgAN

To establish a diagnostic model for IgAN, We first made overlap between differentially expressed genes (P < .05) and feature genes identified by WGCNA (correlation coefficient of module-trait > 0.5).Next, we performed least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis and identified the final genes that could be included in the diagnostic model. LASSO regression is efficient variable screening method, which also can exclude the multilinearity and is very suitable for our study with relatively small sample size. The cross-validation was not used for the present study due to the limitation of sample size. Logistic regression including model genes was then performed, and the diagnostic score was calculated using the formula: score = gene1 expression*regression coefficient+…+ geneN expression*regression coefficient. The gene(N) expression is the normalized gene expression level, and the regression coefficient is the coefficient from the logistic regression, which included the dependent variable (disease status: IgAN and healthy) and independent variable (gene expression level).

2.8. Assessment of diagnostic model in IgAN

To assess the diagnostic ability of the model, we plotted the receiver operating characteristics (ROC) and calculated the area under the curve (AUC). AUC = 0.5 was cutoff value. Generally speaking: low for 0.5 to 0.7, general for 0.7 to 0.85, good for 0.85 to 0.95, Excellent for 0.95 to 1. We also obtained cutoff values for each model gene using ROC analysis (ACTA2:10.026, AFM: 6.473, ALB: 6.252, ALDH1L1: 8.163, ALDH6A1: 9.291). Using the cutoff value, the continuous data of gene expression were categorized into high- and low- expression groups. A nomogram plot based on the logistic regression was constructed to assess the individual risk of IgAN. A calibration plot was used to assess the correlation between actual diagnosed nonadherence and the nomogram-predicted probability of nonadherence. A high degree of correlation indicates a stable diagnostic model. We also performed decision curve analysis to evaluate the predictive models and diagnostic tests. Finally, we validated the expression of model genes using an external dataset.

2.9. Ethical compliance

All procedures performed in studies involving human participants were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and/or national research committee and with the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its later amendments or comparable ethical standards. For the present study, the ethical approval is not necessary because all data were from public platform. We have carefully reviewed and adhered to the database usage guidelines to ensure that our research complies with all relevant regulations. The data we use does not contain any personally identifiable information such as IDs or names. The data is solely used for data analysis and will not cause any direct or indirect harm to the participants.

3. Results

3.1. Identification of differentially expressed genes

We first performed a principal component analysis and found that there were obviously different distributions between the IgAN and control groups (Fig. 1A). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened using the limma package. Finally, we identified 38 DGEs (Supplementary Material 1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/O71), including 11 upregulated genes and 27 downregulated genes (Fig. 1B). Among these genes, HBBP1, HBB, HLA-DQA1, C1QB, LY1, CD53, HCLS1, ACTA2, COL21A1, COL1A2, and C3AR1 were significantly highly expressed. Some genes, such as FOSB, ALB, FOSL1, CSCL2, and GSTA3, were remarkably low in expression (Fig. 1C). Using these genes, we built a protein-protein interaction network and identified the top 10 hub genes using the MCC algorithm: FOS, EGR2, FOSB, NR4A1, BR4A3, FOSL1, NR4A2, ALB, CD53, and C3AR1 (Fig. 1D).

DGEs = differentially expressed genes, IgAN = immunoglobulin A nephropathy.

3.2. GO and KEGG enrichment

To explore the functions and signaling pathways involved, we performed GO enrichment and KEGG analyses. We found that these DGEs were mainly enriched in response to corticosterone, cAMP, steroid hormones, mineralocorticoids, progesterone, organophosphorus, toxic substances, and peptide hormones. Collagen trimer, endoplasmic reticulum lumen, brush border membrane, DNA-binding transcription activator activity, nuclear receptor activity, ligand-activated transcription factor activity, and peptide antigen binding were also enriched (Fig. 2A). KEGG analysis indicated that the IL-17 signaling pathway, protein digestion and absorption, relaxin signaling pathway, and Th1-Th2 cell differentiation were significantly enriched (Fig. 2B).

Function and enrichment analysis of DGEs. (A) GO enrichment analysis. (B) KEGG pathways analysis. DGEs = differentially expressed genes, GO = gene oncology, KEGG = Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes.

3.3. Weighted gene correlation network analysis identified hub genes

First, sample clustering was performed to detect outliers (Fig. 3A). In this study, a power of β = 9 (scale-free R2 = 0.95) was selected as the soft-thresholding parameter to ensure a scale-free network (Fig. 3B). Seven modules were identified using hierarchical clustering (Fig. 3C). The turquoise (eigengene value = −0.78), brown (eigengene value = −0.56), green (eigengene value = 0.54), and yellow (eigengene value = 0.50) modules appeared to be associated with IgAN (Fig. 3D). There were no module relationships for the blue and gray modules (Fig. 3E). In total, 610 genes were identified in these modules. We overlapped the DGEs and phenotypic genes, and identified 38 genes that were used for further analysis (Fig. 3F).

(C) Gene dendrogram and module colors. (D) Module-trait relationships. (E) Clustering of module eigengenes. (F) Venn plot showed the key genes. IgAN = immunoglobulin A nephropathy.

3.4. Immune infiltration levels of IgAN

IgAN is closely associated with immune status. We further evaluated immune infiltration levels between patients with IgAN and healthy controls. There were obvious differences in the immune infiltration levels between patients with IgAN and healthy controls. APC co-inhibition, APC co-stimulation, CCR, CD8+ T cells, checkpoint, HLA, inflammation-promoting, macrophages, MHC class I, NK cells, inflammation, pDCs, T-cell co-inhibition, T cell COstimulation, T helper cells, Th1 cells, Th2 cells, TIL, and type I IFN responses were significantly elevated (Fig. 4). These results indicate high levels of immune infiltration in patients with IgAN.

3.5. Establishment and assessment of diagnostic model

Using the 38 identified genes, we established a diagnostic model for IgAN. LASSO regression was performed to identify the model genes, and the optimal number of genes was 5. ACTA2, ALB, AFM, ALDH1L1, and ALDH6A1 were included in the diagnostic model (Table 1, Fig. 5A, B). The AUCs of the 5 genes were 0.922, 0.878, 0.861, 0.837, and 0.858 (Fig. 5C–G). The combined diagnostic ability was high with an AUC of 0.964 (Fig. 5H).

We divided the expression of the 5 genes into category variables using the cutoff values, and we built a nomogram to predict the individual risk of being diagnosed with IgAN (Fig. 6A). If a patient was positive for AFM, ALB, ALDH1L1, and ALDH6A1, he had a 75% chance of being diagnosed with IgAN. We further evaluated the model fitting ability and found that the nomogram-predicted probability of nonadherence was highly correlated with actual diagnosed nonadherence (Fig. 6B). 3.6. Validation of diagnostic model

We first detected the expression levels of model genes in patients with IgAN and healthy living donors. We found that ACTA2 was significantly elevated in IgAN patients, whereas ALB, AFM, ALDH1L1, and ALDH6A1 were significantly decreased in IgAN patients (Fig. 7A–E). Furthermore, we validated the diagnostic model using this external dataset and found that the AUC was 0.999 in the external dataset (Fig. 7F). The diagnostic model exhibits good extrapolation properties.

4. Discussion

IgAN is a common chronic glomerular disease worldwide, and its early diagnosis is the most important step in preventing and controlling its progression to renal failure. It is well known that the occurrence of IgAN is related to genetic factors and epigenetic factors.[20] Although multiple attack mechanisms are the mainstream hypothesis of IgAN pathogenesis, the underlying molecular mechanisms remain to be explored. With the development of high-throughput sequencing and microarray technologies, there may be opportunities to gain a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying IgAN. In this study, key genes of IgAN were screened using bioinformatics methods to explore their biological functions, and a diagnostic model was constructed using gene expression profiles, which provided directions for exploring the potential molecular mechanism of IgAN as well as diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

In this study, 2 GEO datasets were integrated to screen 38 differentially expressed genes between patients with IgAN and normal kidney donors in glomerular samples. KEGG enrichment analysis showed that the differentially expressed genes were mainly enriched in the IL-17 and relaxin signaling pathways. IL-17 is an important cytokine with multiple roles in the host’s defense response to mucosal infections, in addition, it is a major pathological cytokine and therapeutic target for many autoimmune diseases, inflammatory diseases, and cancers.[21–23] Studies have shown that IL-17 can play a role by activating the nuclear factor (NF)-κB and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathways and promoting the expression of neutrophils and T lymphocyte chemokines in mesangial cells and renal tubular epithelial cells, especially in the inflammatory response environment.[24,25] Th17 cells in the peripheral blood of patients with IgA nephropathy were positively correlated with IL-17A secretion levels, and IL-17A expression was positively correlated with mesangial cell proliferation.[26,27] Relaxin is an endogenous antifibrotic substance and a member of the insulin-like growth factor superfamily.[28] Kidney tissue is one of the target organs for relaxin, which mediates a range of biological effects by binding to its specific receptors.[29] In addition, we constructed a PPI network of differentially expressed genes using the STRING database and cross-compared it with the results of WGCNA analysis to screen out the top 10 key genes: FOS, EGR2, FOSB, NR4A1, EGR3, BR4A3, FOSL1, NR4A2, ALB, CD53, and C3AR1.

We also found that CD8+ T cells, macrophages, T helper cells, Th1, and Th2 cells were significantly elevated in patients with IgAN. Previous studies have shown that IgAN is mainly infiltrated by γδT cells. The proportion of Th2, Tfh, Th17, Th22, and γδT cells in the blood circulation increased, while the proportion of Th1 and Treg cells decreased. Changes in T lymphocyte subsets may be related to the different gene sequences and epigenetic compositions of IgAN patients.[30] It has been reported that the tonsil lymphocyte Th1/Th2 ratio of IgAN tonsillitis patients is lower than that of chronic tonsillitis patients without kidney disease. The imbalance of the Th1/Th2 cell subpopulation can secrete a large amount of IL-4, resulting in the failure of timely clearance of antigen and induction of class conversion of B lymphocytes, mediating the increased secretion of hypoglycation IgA1.[31] Th17 and Th22 lymphocytes are different from Th1 and Th2 cell subgroups, which participate in the pathological process of IgAN by promoting the expression of pro-inflammatory factors, chemokines, and metalloproteinases and are related to the increase in urinary protein.[32] Therefore, drugs targeting the imbalance in T lymphocyte subsets may have a better therapeutic effect on IgAN.

We identified 5 genes in the diagnostic model using LASSO regression: ACTA2, ALB, AFM, ALDH1L1, and ALDH6A1. The diagnostic ability of a single gene is > 80%. The combined diagnostic ability was 96.4% (sensitivity, 91.5%; specificity, 93.9), which was very high. Previous studies have built diagnostic models based on clinical parameters. However, the AUC was only 0.830, with a sensitivity of 80.8% and a specificity of 83.3%.[33] The serum biomarker Gd-IgA1 was used as the diagnostic test. Gd-IgA1 provided 0.77 sensitivity and 0.90% specificity, distinguishing patients with IgAN from healthy controls. Gd-IgA1 and its related immune complexes are also considered as diagnostic biomarkers.[34] They found that Gd-IgA1-specific IgG showed the best performance in the diagnosis of IgAN, with a sensitivity of 89% and specificity of 92%.[35] However, this diagnostic ability is still lower than that of our model. The primary reason is that renal biopsy is the gold standard for diagnosis and assessment of disease activity. Gene expression in renal tissues can more accurately reflect the state of the disease. Some miRNA diagnostic biomarkers have also been reported, but their diagnostic value is limited.[36] Our combined diagnostic model has overwhelming benefits. This study also has certain limitations. First, the sample size of this study is not large, so it is difficult to screen out key genes that are more closely related to IgAN. Second, the results of this study require additional molecular experiments to explore the relationship between the key genes and IgAN.

5. Conclusions

In conclusion, we identified differentially expressed genes between IgAN patients and healthy controls. These genes are involved in multiple biological functions and signaling pathways. Patients with IgAN had obviously different immune infiltration levels, especially T cells. A 5-gene panel can be used to distinguish IgAN patients from healthy controls, which could be helpful in adjusting the diagnosis and treatment strategy.

Unbounded sets of solutions of non-cooperative elliptic systems on symmetric spaces
Piotr Stefaniak

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to show that, for a class of non-cooperative elliptic systems on compact symmetric spaces, any continuum of nontrivial solutions bifurcating from the set of trivial solutions is unbounded. The main tool is the degree for invariant strongly indefinite functionals. The analysis relies on the torus-equivariant structure of the Laplace–Beltrami eigenspaces. The result is obtained by ruling out return to the trivial branch in an equivariant version of the Rabinowitz alternative.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35B32, 35J20

1.
Introduction

In this article we study connected sets of weak solutions of nonlinear elliptic systems of the form

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lclcl}\alpha_{1}\Delta_{M}u_{1}(x)&=&\nabla_{u_{1}}F(u(x),\lambda)\\
\alpha_{2}\Delta_{M}u_{2}(x)&=&\nabla_{u_{2}}F(u(x),\lambda)\\
&\vdots&&\ \ \ \text{on}&M,\\
\alpha_{p}\Delta_{M}u_{p}(x)&=&\nabla_{u_{p}}F(u(x),\lambda)\end{array}\right.

where
M=G/H
is a compact symmetric space, with
G
a compact connected semisimple Lie group and
H\subset G
the fixed-point set of an involutive automorphism of
G
.
The operator
\Delta_{M}
denotes the Laplace–Beltrami operator associated with the standard
G
-invariant metric on
M
. The constants
\alpha_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
allow for indefinite sign structure in the system, and
F\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
is a nonlinear potential depending on a real parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. The system is assumed to satisfy certain structural and growth conditions on the nonlinearity; see Subsection
3.1
, assumptions (A1)–(A4). In particular,
0\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is a critical point of
F(\cdot,\lambda)
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, so that
u\equiv 0
defines a stationary solution of the system.

To study such systems, we adopt a variational approach. Associating to (
1.1
) a functional
\Phi\colon\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
whose critical points correspond to weak solutions, we obtain a gradient map
\nabla_{u}\Phi
that is a completely continuous perturbation of a Fredholm operator on a Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
, which is an orthogonal
G
-representation. Fixing a maximal torus
\mathbb{T}\subset G
, we consider the induced
\mathbb{T}
-action on
\mathbb{H}
. The functional
\Phi
is then
\mathbb{T}
-invariant, and its gradient
\nabla_{u}\Phi
is
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant.

In the absence of symmetry, continua of critical points of variational problems are governed by the classical Rabinowitz alternative, which—under suitable parity assumptions—ensures that each bifurcating continuum is either unbounded or meets another characteristic value; see
[
21
,
24
,
25
]
. In the presence of group symmetries, however, the topological methods underlying this alternative may no longer apply. A fundamental result of Dancer shows that the Leray–Schauder degree of a
G
-equivariant completely continuous perturbation of the identity depends only on its restriction to the fixed-point subspace; see
[
6
]
. This illustrates the general failure of the classical degree to detect bifurcation phenomena in equivariant problems. Broader formulations of this principle have been developed both for the Brouwer and Leray–Schauder degrees; see
[
20
]
, and for the abelian case in particular, see
[
18
]
.

To address this limitation, equivariant extensions of degree theory were developed—first in finite dimensions, and later for infinite-dimensional gradient maps. A result of Parusiński (see
[
22
]
) shows that every admissible map is homotopic to a gradient one, and that two gradient maps are homotopic if and only if they are gradient-homotopic. This implies that, without additional structure, any topological invariant defined on gradient maps alone cannot capture more information than the classical Brouwer or Leray–Schauder degree. To go beyond this, one can incorporate group symmetries into the framework. In the equivariant setting, the distinction between general and gradient homotopies becomes meaningful, and the resulting theory can detect features invisible to the classical framework.

Building on this idea, further developments focused on constructing an equivariant degree that incorporates compact Lie group actions. In the finite-dimensional setting, such a degree was introduced by Gęba
[
9
]
, and later extended to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces by Rybicki
[
27
]
for equivariant gradient maps that are completely continuous perturbations of the identity. A degree for
G
-invariant strongly indefinite functionals was developed in
[
11
]
, and a method for computing it in the presence of a critical orbit of possibly non-zero dimension was given in
[
12
]
. For general treatments of equivariant degree theory, we refer the reader to
[
2
]
.

An alternative invariant for detecting bifurcation is the (equivariant) Conley index. An equivariant version suitable for strongly indefinite functionals was developed by Izydorek in
[
17
]
, and applies in particular to the class of systems considered here. While the Conley index is highly effective at confirming the
existence
of bifurcation points, it remains essentially local and does not provide information about the
global
geometry of bifurcating sets—for instance, whether continua are unbounded or how distinct branches may be connected. These global properties, which are central to our analysis, require stronger topological invariants such as the equivariant degree.

Motivated by this, we study global bifurcation phenomena for nonlinear elliptic systems on compact symmetric spaces, using the equivariant degree as the primary tool. While bifurcation theory in the Euclidean setting is relatively well developed, corresponding results on Riemannian manifolds—particularly for systems—are less common. In particular, the unboundedness of continua of solutions has been established only in a limited number of cases. As examples, we mention several results obtained on the sphere and related domains.
In
[
26
]
, global bifurcation from the trivial branch
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
and unbounded continua were established for a single equation on the sphere, and this analysis was extended to non-cooperative systems in
[
28
]
. A related result appears in
[
29
]
, where elliptic systems were studied on geodesic balls in the sphere, and unboundedness was proved in particular for hemispheres. The work
[
13
]
considered elliptic systems on the sphere with an invariant potential, allowing for bifurcation from an orbit of stationary solutions, and also established unboundedness of the resulting continua.

The present article generalizes the results from
[
26
]
and
[
28
]
by replacing the standard sphere, a symmetric space of rank one, with a compact symmetric space of arbitrary rank. In the rank-one case, unboundedness was shown with the help of an
S^{1}
-symmetry and the associated degree. For a space of rank
r\geq 1
we instead exploit the action of the
r
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}=(S^{1})^{\,r}
that arises naturally from the geometry of
M=G/H
. Although an
S^{1}
-action would still reveal the bifurcation points, the higher-rank torus provides the additional algebraic information needed to prove that the resulting continua are unbounded. Under assumptions (A1)–(A4), Theorem
3.5
shows that, for each non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{\alpha}
of
-\Delta_{M}
(and in certain sign configurations at
0
), there exists a connected set of weak solutions to (
1.1
) bifurcating from
(0,\lambda_{\alpha})
or
(0,-\lambda_{\alpha})
, and that this set is unbounded in
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
.

The proof combines an equivariant Rabinowitz alternative with an explicit computation of the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant bifurcation index. Describing each Laplace–Beltrami eigenspace by its unique highest weight as a
\mathbb{T}
-representation (Proposition
2.3
) makes the index computable and guarantees that it is non-zero along the trivial branch.

The article is organized as follows. Section
2
gathers the algebraic, analytic, and topological preliminaries. Subsection
2.1
reviews weights and highest weights for compact–Lie-group representations, focusing on their decomposition under a maximal torus. Subsection
2.2
recalls the spectrum of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on compact symmetric spaces and its torus–weight decomposition of eigenspaces. Subsection
2.3
sketches the
\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}
-degree for strongly indefinite functionals, defined via the Euler ring of the torus and used to define the bifurcation index.

Section
3
presents the main results. Subsection
3.1
introduces the elliptic system (
1.1
) and its variational setting. Subsection
3.2
defines the torus-equivariant bifurcation index and states an equivariant Rabinowitz alternative (Theorem
3.7
). Subsection
3.3
proves Theorem
3.5
by explicit index computations relying on Proposition
2.3
and Lemma
3.9
. Remark
3.10
discusses symmetry breaking, and an example with products of spheres illustrates the theory.

2.
Preliminaries

This section gathers the algebraic, analytic, and topological tools needed for system (
1.1
).
We first review maximal-torus decompositions of real representations of compact Lie groups, then recall the Laplace–Beltrami spectrum on compact symmetric spaces and the associated torus representation structure of its eigenspaces.
Finally, we outline the
\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}
-degree for invariant strongly indefinite functionals, which provides the bifurcation index used later.

2.1.
Representations and Torus Decomposition

This subsection recalls the basic facts about finite-dimensional representations of compact Lie groups and their decomposition under the action of a maximal torus. For background and further details, we refer the reader to the standard texts
[
3
,
4
,
19
]
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group and let
\mathbb{T}\subset G
be a maximal torus. By a
representation
of
G
we understand a real or complex vector space
\mathbb{V}
equipped with a smooth linear action of
G
. We are primarily concerned with real orthogonal representations, in which
\mathbb{V}
is a real inner product space and the action of
G
preserves the inner product. A representation is called
irreducible
if it has no proper, nontrivial
G
-invariant subspaces.

Let
r=\dim\mathbb{T}
. Identifying
\mathbb{T}
with the standard torus
\mathbb{T}^{r}=(S^{1})^{r}
, each
t\in\mathbb{T}
can be written as
t=(e^{i\phi_{1}},\dots,e^{i\phi_{r}})
. The action of
\mathbb{T}
on representations is then determined by homomorphisms
t\mapsto e^{i\langle\mu,\phi\rangle}
, where
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
and
\langle\mu,\phi\rangle:=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}\phi_{j}
.
Any finite-dimensional complex representation of
G
restricts to a representation of
\mathbb{T}
and therefore decomposes into weight spaces indexed by
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, the character group of
\mathbb{T}
. For a complex representation of
G
and each
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, we define the
weight space

\mathbb{V}_{\mu}:=\{\,v\in\mathbb{V}\colon t\cdot v=\chi_{\mu}(t)\,v\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{T}\,\}.

If
\mathbb{V}_{\mu}\neq 0
, we say that
\mu
is a
weight
of the representation. The representation
\mathbb{V}
then decomposes as

(2.1)

\mathbb{V}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}\mathbb{V}_{\mu},

see
[
3
, Proposition II.8.7]
.

To describe the structure of irreducible representations, one begins by fixing a system of positive roots in the root system determined by the maximal torus
\mathbb{T}\subset G
. This choice determines a set of simple roots, which form a basis of the root lattice, as well as a dual basis of fundamental weights that generate the full weight lattice. Every weight can be uniquely expressed as an integer linear combination of fundamental weights, which provides coordinates in
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
; when we write a weight as
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})
, we mean its expansion in the fundamental weight basis.

The partial order on weights is defined by declaring
\lambda\succeq\mu
if and only if the difference
\lambda-\mu
is a nonnegative integer linear combination of simple roots. Thus, while coordinates are taken with respect to the fundamental weights, the partial order is naturally expressed using the simple root basis. This order plays a central role in the classification of irreducible representations via highest weights. We now recall the classical highest weight theorem; see, for instance,
[
19
, Ch. V]
for a detailed proof.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group. Then:

(1)

Every irreducible complex representation of
G
has a unique highest weight
\mu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}
;

(2)

The corresponding highest weight space is one-dimensional;

(3)

Every
\mu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}
occurs as the highest weight of some irreducible representation;

(4)

The isomorphism class of an irreducible representation is uniquely determined by its highest weight.

Moreover, if
\mu
and
\nu
are highest weights of two irreducible representations, then
\mu
appears as a weight in the representation with highest weight
\nu
only if
\mu\preceq\nu
. In particular, if
\mu
and
\nu
are incomparable (i.e., neither
\mu\preceq\nu
nor
\nu\preceq\mu
), then neither appears as a weight in the representation of the other.

We now turn to the structure of real representations under the action of the maximal torus
\mathbb{T}
.

Definition 2.2
.

For an integer vector
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, define
\mathbb{R}[1,\mu]
to be the real orthogonal two-dimensional representation in which
t=(e^{i\phi_{1}},\dots,e^{i\phi_{r}})\in\mathbb{T}
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by rotation through angle
\langle\mu,\phi\rangle
, where
\phi=(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
.

Explicitly,

\mathbb{R}[1,\mu]:=\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{2},\,\phi\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}\cos\langle\mu,\phi\rangle&-\sin\langle\mu,\phi\rangle\\
\sin\langle\mu,\phi\rangle&\cos\langle\mu,\phi\rangle\end{pmatrix}\bigr).

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\mathbb{R}[k,\mu]:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{R}[1,\mu]
, and in the trivial case
\mu=0
we set
\mathbb{R}[k,0]:=\mathbb{R}^{k}
with the standard diagonal action.

The irreducible real orthogonal representations of
\mathbb{T}
are precisely the representations
\mathbb{R}[1,\mu]
with
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, including the trivial case
\mu=0
; see
[
3
, Proposition II.8.5]
.

Let now
\mathbb{V}
be a finite-dimensional irreducible real representation of a compact Lie group
G
, and let
\mathbb{V}^{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathbb{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
denote its complexification. Then
\mathbb{V}^{\mathbb{C}}
is easily seen to be an irreducible complex representation of
G
.

Just as complex representations decompose into weight spaces under the action of a maximal torus, the structure of real representations can be understood via complexification. In particular, if a real representation
\mathbb{V}
is irreducible, then its complexification
\mathbb{V}^{\mathbb{C}}
admits a weight space decomposition as in (
2.1
), and this structure determines the decomposition of
\mathbb{V}
itself under the action of
\mathbb{T}
, as described in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a finite-dimensional irreducible real representation of a compact Lie group
G
, and let
\mu_{\max}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}
denote the highest weight of the complexified representation
\mathbb{V}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Then the
\mathbb{T}
-representation
\mathbb{V}
decomposes as

\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{R}[k_{0},0]\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}_{\ast}}\mathbb{R}[k_{\mu},\mu],

where
\mathbb{Z}^{r}_{\ast}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{r}\setminus\{0\}
contains exactly one representative from each pair
\{\pm\mu\}
, and
k_{\mu}:=\dim\mathbb{V}_{\mu}=\dim\mathbb{V}_{-\mu}
.

Moreover:

(a)

k_{\mu_{\max}}=1
;

(b)

\mathbb{R}[1,\mu_{\max}]
does not appear in any irreducible real representation whose highest weight is smaller than or incomparable with
\mu_{\max}
.

Proof.

By (
2.1
), the complexified representation
\mathbb{V}^{\mathbb{C}}
admits the weight space decomposition

\mathbb{V}^{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}\mathbb{V}_{\mu}.

Since
\mathbb{V}^{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexification of a real representation, complex conjugation satisfies
\overline{\mathbb{V}_{\mu}}=\mathbb{V}_{-\mu}
. For
\mu\neq 0
, the sum
\mathbb{V}_{\mu}\oplus\mathbb{V}_{-\mu}
is preserved by conjugation and hence is the complexification of a real
\mathbb{T}
-invariant subspace of
\mathbb{V}
, isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}[k_{\mu},\mu]
. The zero weight space
\mathbb{V}_{0}
is already real, giving rise to the trivial block
\mathbb{R}[k_{0},0]
. Combining these components yields the stated decomposition of
\mathbb{V}
.

To prove (a), observe that by Theorem
2.1
, the highest weight
\mu_{\max}
appears in
\mathbb{V}^{\mathbb{C}}
with multiplicity one. Hence
k_{\mu_{\max}}=1
.

For (b), let
\mathbb{W}
be an irreducible real representation whose highest weight is either strictly smaller than or incomparable with
\mu_{\max}
. Then by Theorem
2.1
, the weight
\mu_{\max}
does not occur in
\mathbb{W}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and thus the component
\mathbb{R}[1,\mu_{\max}]
does not appear in the
\mathbb{T}
-decomposition of
\mathbb{W}
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

For each weight
\mu
, the multiplicity
k_{\mu}
is given by Kostant’s formula:

k_{\mu}=\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{l(w)}\,P\bigl(w(\mu_{\max}+\rho)-(\mu+\rho)\bigr),

where
W=N_{G}(\mathbb{T})/\mathbb{T}
is the Weyl group,
l(w)
is the length of
w
, and
P
counts decompositions into sums of positive roots. The vector
\rho
is the half-sum of the chosen positive roots. See
[
3
, Chap. VI, Thm. 3.2]
for details.

2.2.
Laplace–Beltrami Spectrum

Let
G
be a compact connected semisimple Lie group and let
H\subset G
be the fixed-point set of an involutive automorphism. The quotient
M=G/H
is a compact Riemannian symmetric space, equipped with the
G
-invariant metric induced by the negative Killing form on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
. For background on compact symmetric spaces, see
[
16
]
; for the spectral theory of the Laplace–Beltrami operator, see
[
14
]
.

Write the Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is the Lie algebra of
H
and
\mathfrak{m}
its orthogonal complement with respect to the Killing form. Choose a maximal abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{m}
and set
A=\exp(\mathfrak{a})
. The weights arising from the
A
–action on finite‐dimensional
G
-representations form a discrete additive subgroup
\Lambda\subset\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, called the
restricted weight lattice
.
Fixing a system of positive restricted roots selects a closed positive Weyl chamber and induces a basis of
restricted fundamental weights
. In these coordinates the lattice
\Lambda
is identified with
\mathbb{Z}^{l}
, where
l=\dim\mathfrak{a}
is the rank of
M
; every restricted weight is written uniquely as an integer vector
(m_{1},\dots,m_{l})
.

Denote by

\Lambda^{+}\;=\;\bigl\{(m_{1},\dots,m_{l})\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}\;:\;m_{j}\geq 0\text{ for all }j\bigr\}

the intersection of
\Lambda
with the closed positive chamber. Its elements are precisely the restricted highest weights of the
G
-representations that appear in the
L^{2}
-spectrum of
\Delta_{M}
. A partial order on
\Lambda
is defined by declaring
\alpha\succeq\beta
whenever
\alpha-\beta
is a non-negative integer linear combination of restricted simple roots; thus the coordinates
(m_{1},\dots,m_{l})
are taken in the fundamental-weight basis, while the order itself is naturally expressed in terms of simple roots. This ordering will be used below to analyse the torus-equivariant structure of Laplace–Beltrami eigenspaces.

Let
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
denote half the sum of the positive restricted roots, expressed in the chosen basis. For each
\alpha
, define

\lambda_{\alpha}:=(\alpha+\rho,\alpha+\rho)-(\rho,\rho),

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the inner product on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}\cong\mathbb{R}^{l}
induced by the Killing form. In these coordinates, the inner product can be computed componentwise and coincides with the standard Euclidean scalar product up to normalization. The map
\alpha\mapsto\lambda_{\alpha}
is strictly increasing with respect to the partial order: if
\beta\prec\alpha
, then
\lambda_{\beta}<\lambda_{\alpha}
.

The following result gathers the basic spectral properties we will use; a detailed proof may be found in
[
14
, Thm. 5.7.2]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{l}
, the number
\lambda_{\alpha}
is an eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{M}
, and every eigenvalue arises in this way.
The corresponding eigenspace, denoted
\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
, is an irreducible
G
-representation with restricted highest weight
\alpha
.

We now describe how the basic torus representations appear inside the eigenspaces of
-\Delta_{M}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

For every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{l}
, the real torus representation

\mathbb{R}[1,\alpha]
occurs with multiplicity
1
in the eigenspace

\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
, and is absent from

\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta})
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{l}
such that
\beta\prec\alpha
or
\beta
is incomparable with
\alpha
.

In particular, whenever
\lambda_{\beta}<\lambda_{\alpha}
, the representation
\mathbb{R}[1,\alpha]
appears with multiplicity
1
in
\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
but is absent from
\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.5
, the space

\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
is an irreducible
G
‑representation with
restricted highest weight
\alpha
. Proposition
2.3
then implies that
\mathbb{R}[1,\alpha]
appears exactly once in the torus decomposition of

\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
and is absent from the decomposition of
any irreducible representation whose highest weight is strictly smaller than or incomparable with
\alpha
.
∎

2.3.
The
\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}
-degree for strongly indefinite functionals

We now recall basic properties of the Euler ring of a torus, which will be used in the construction of an equivariant bifurcation index. For the general theory of the Euler ring
U(G)
of a compact Lie group
G
, we refer to
[
30
,
31
]
. In this article, we restrict attention to the case where
G=\mathbb{T}
, a torus, and follow the notation from
[
8
]
.

Let
\overline{\mathrm{sub}}(\mathbb{T})
denote the set of closed subgroups of
\mathbb{T}
. For
H\in\overline{\mathrm{sub}}(\mathbb{T})
, the orbit space
\mathbb{T}/H
is defined via the right
H
-action on
\mathbb{T}
, given by
(h,g)\mapsto gh^{-1}
. We denote by
\mathbb{T}/H^{+}
the pointed space obtained by adjoining a disjoint base point
\{\star\}
to
\mathbb{T}/H
. It is known that
\mathbb{T}/H^{+}
is a pointed
\mathbb{T}
-CW-complex with exactly one
0
-cell. The
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Euler characteristic of
\mathbb{T}/H^{+}
, denoted
\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H^{+})
, is defined as in
[
8
, Definition 2.3]
.
The Euler ring
U(\mathbb{T})
is the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module generated by the elements
\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H^{+})
as
H
ranges over
\overline{\mathrm{sub}}(\mathbb{T})
, equipped with a multiplication operation defined below. This identifies
U(\mathbb{T})
with the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module on the set of conjugacy classes of closed subgroups of
\mathbb{T}
.

The ring structure on
U(\mathbb{T})
is induced by the smash product of pointed
\mathbb{T}
-CW-complexes. For tori, an explicit multiplication formula has been given in
[
8
]
; we recall it here for reference. If
H,H^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathrm{sub}}(\mathbb{T})
and
H^{\prime\prime}:=H\cap H^{\prime}
, then

(2.2)

\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H^{+})\star\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H^{\prime+})=\begin{cases}\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H^{\prime\prime+}),&\dim H+\dim H^{\prime}=\dim\mathbb{T}+\dim H^{\prime\prime},\\
\Theta,&\dim H+\dim H^{\prime}<\dim\mathbb{T}+\dim H^{\prime\prime},\end{cases}

where
\Theta
is the zero element, and the unit is
\mathbb{I}:=\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/\mathbb{T}^{+})
.

Corollary 2.7
.

From equation (
2.2
), it follows that when multiplying any element of
U(\mathbb{T})
by linear combinations of terms
\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H^{+})
with
\dim H\leq\dim\mathbb{T}-2
, the result is again a linear combination of terms
\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H^{+})
with
\dim H\leq\dim\mathbb{T}-2
.

The Euler ring
U(\mathbb{T})
is used to define the topological degree for invariant strongly indefinite functionals introduced in
[
11
]
, which we now briefly recall.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be a (real) Hilbert space being an orthogonal
\mathbb{T}
-representation. Assume that
\mathbb{H}
admits an approximation scheme
\{\pi_{n}\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
, in the sense of
[
11
]
, and write
\mathbb{H}^{n}:=\pi_{n}(\mathbb{H})
for each
n
. Consider a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant functional
\Phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{H},\mathbb{R})
of the form

\Phi(u)=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle Lu,u\rangle-\eta(u),

where:

•

L\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
is a bounded, self-adjoint,
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Fredholm operator of index
0
, with
\ker L=\mathbb{H}^{0}
and
\pi_{n}\circ L=L\circ\pi_{n}
for all
n
;

•

\nabla\eta\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
is a completely continuous,
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant operator.

Here,
\mathbb{T}
-invariance of
\Phi
means that
\Phi(t\cdot u)=\Phi(u)
for all
t\in\mathbb{T}
and
u\in\mathbb{H}
, so that
\nabla\Phi
is
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant in the sense that
\nabla\Phi(t\cdot u)=t\cdot\nabla\Phi(u)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}
be an open, bounded subset, invariant under the action of
\mathbb{T}
(i.e.,
t\cdot x\in\Omega
for all
x\in\Omega
and
t\in\mathbb{T}
), such that

\partial\Omega\cap(\nabla\Phi)^{-1}(0)=\emptyset
.
Then the degree for
\mathbb{T}
-invariant strongly indefinite functionals,

\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}\text{-}\deg(\nabla\Phi,\Omega)\in U(\mathbb{T}),

is well-defined. It generalizes the degree from the finite-dimensional setting and satisfies the expected formal properties: excision, additivity, homotopy invariance, multiplicativity and linearization at isolated nondegenerate critical points.

The degree of the negative identity map is given by

(2.3)

\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}\text{-}\deg(-\mathrm{Id},B(\mathbb{V}))=\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(S^{\mathbb{V}}),

where
B(\mathbb{V})
denotes the unit ball in a finite-dimensional orthogonal
\mathbb{T}
-representation
\mathbb{V}
, and
S^{\mathbb{V}}=B(\mathbb{V})/\partial B(\mathbb{V})
is its one-point compactification; see
[
9
]
.

Fix a finite-dimensional orthogonal
\mathbb{T}
-representation

\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{R}[k_{0},0]\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{R}[k_{i},m_{i}],\qquad k_{0},k_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\;m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}.

For each
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
set

H_{m}:=\bigl\{(e^{i\phi_{1}},\dots,e^{i\phi_{r}})\in\mathbb{T}\colon e^{i\langle m,\phi\rangle}=1\bigr\},\quad\phi=(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}.

The group
H_{m}
is the isotropy group of the
\mathbb{T}
-action on
\mathbb{R}[1,m]
at any nonzero point. By Corollary 4.6.7 of
[
7
]
and the classification of real
\mathbb{T}
-representations, the subgroups
H_{m}
are precisely the closed subgroups of codimension one in
\mathbb{T}
, and every proper closed subgroup of
\mathbb{T}
is an intersection of finitely many such subgroups.

This allows for an explicit formula for the equivariant Euler characteristic of representation spheres. The result below, taken from
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
, expresses
\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(S^{\mathbb{V}})
in terms of the isotropy subgroups
H_{m}
introduced above.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{R}[k_{0},0]\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{R}[k_{i},m_{i}]
be a
\mathbb{T}
-representation. Then

\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(S^{\mathbb{V}})=(-1)^{k_{0}}\left(\mathbb{I}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}\cdot\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H_{m_{i}}^{+})\right)+\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{V}),

where
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{V})
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of terms
\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H^{+})
with
\dim H\leq\dim\mathbb{T}-2
.

3.
Main Results

In this section we formulate and prove the main result on the unboundedness of global bifurcation continua for the system (
3.1
). We develop the variational framework, identify the possible bifurcation levels, introduce an equivariant bifurcation index, and apply it to show that all expected bifurcations from the trivial branch do occur and give rise to unbounded continua.

3.1.
System and Variational Structure

Let
G
be a compact semisimple Lie group, and let
H\subset G
be the fixed point set of an involutive automorphism, so that
M=G/H
is a compact Riemannian symmetric space. We endow
M
with the
G
-invariant Riemannian metric induced by the negative of the Killing form on the Lie algebra of
G
.

Consider the non-cooperative elliptic system on
M
given by

(3.1)

a_{i}\,\Delta_{M}u_{i}(x)=\nabla_{u_{i}}F\bigl(u(x),\lambda\bigr),\quad i=1,\dots,p,\;x\in M,

where:

(A1)

F\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
with gradient
\nabla_{u}F=(\nabla_{u_{1}}F,\dots,\nabla_{u_{p}}F)
.

(A2)

\nabla_{u}F(u,\lambda)=\lambda\,u+\nabla_{u}h(u,\lambda)
, and
h(u,\lambda)=o(|u|^{2})
as
u\to 0
.

(A3)

a_{i}\in\{+1,-1\}
; denote by
n_{+}
(resp.
n_{-}
) the number of
i
with
a_{i}=+1
(resp.
-1
).

(A4)

There exists
q<2n/(n-2)
(or arbitrary
q<\infty
if
n\leq 2
) such that for all
(u,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}
one has
|\nabla_{u}h(u,\lambda)|\leq C(1+|u|^{q-1}).

Obviously
p=n_{-}+n_{+}
.

Consider the Sobolev space
H^{1}(M)
, defined as the completion of
C^{\infty}(M)
under the norm

\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and set
\mathbb{H}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}H^{1}(M),

equipped with the orthogonal
\mathbb{T}
-action
(t\cdot u)(x):=u(t^{-1}x)
. With this action,
\mathbb{H}
becomes a real orthogonal
\mathbb{T}
-representation.
Define the
\mathbb{T}
-invariant functional

\Phi\colon\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\Phi(u,\lambda):=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}\int_{M}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}-\int_{M}F(u,\lambda).

Weak solutions of (
3.1
) correspond precisely to the critical points of
\Phi
, that is, the zeros of
\nabla_{u}\Phi(\cdot,\lambda)
.
This places the system within the standard variational framework for strongly indefinite problems, with a Fredholm linear part and a completely continuous nonlinearity. We record this in the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under assumptions
(A1)
–
(A4)
, the gradient mapping

\nabla_{u}\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{H}

is a completely continuous perturbation of the bounded self-adjoint Fredholm operator

L=(-a_{1}\Delta_{M})\oplus\cdots\oplus(-a_{p}\Delta_{M}).

Proof.

This follows from the subcritical growth condition (A4) and standard Sobolev embedding theory. Writing the gradient as
\nabla_{u}\Phi(u,\lambda)=Lu+\eta(u,\lambda)
, the nonlinear term
\eta(u,\lambda)
is completely continuous due to the compactness of the embedding
H^{1}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(M)
for
q<2n/(n-2)
(or all
q<\infty
if
n\leq 2
), see
[
1
,
15
]
. The result then follows as in
[
25
]
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The structural result above ensures that for any bounded
\mathbb{T}
-invariant open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}
such that
\nabla_{u}\Phi(\cdot,\lambda)^{-1}(0)\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
, the degree

\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}\text{-}\deg(\nabla_{u}\Phi(\cdot,\lambda),\Omega)

is well defined.

3.2.
Global bifurcations

We now turn to the bifurcation structure of the variational equation
\nabla_{u}\Phi(u,\lambda)=0
. In this context, we define a global continuum of nontrivial solutions bifurcating from the trivial branch.

We call a solution
(u,\lambda)\in\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
of the equation
\nabla_{u}\Phi(u,\lambda)=0

trivial
if
u=0
, and
nontrivial
otherwise. The set of trivial solutions is thus given by
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 3.3
.

A set
\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{0})\subset\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
is called a
global bifurcation continuum from
(0,\lambda_{0})
if:

(1)

(0,\lambda_{0})\in\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{0})
,

(2)

\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{0})
contains nontrivial critical points of
\Phi
,

(3)

\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{0})
is closed and connected in
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
,

(4)

either
\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{0})
is unbounded in
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
, or it intersects the trivial branch at another point
(0,\lambda_{1})
with
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{0}
.

In this case, the point
(0,\lambda_{0})
is called a
global bifurcation point
for nontrivial solutions of the equation
\nabla_{u}\Phi(u,\lambda)=0
.

We denote by

\mathcal{P}(\Phi)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\colon\ker\nabla^{2}_{u}\Phi(0,\lambda)\neq\{0\}\}

the set of parameter values at which the linearized equation fails to be locally solvable via the Implicit Function Theorem. These are precisely the values where bifurcation from the trivial branch may occur.
The next lemma describes the structure of
\mathcal{P}(\Phi)
in terms of the spectrum of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
M
.

Lemma 3.4
.

With notation as above:

(i)

The set
\mathcal{P}(\Phi)
is discrete.

(ii)

\mathcal{P}(\Phi)=\begin{cases}\sigma(-\Delta_{M}),&n_{-}>0,\;n_{+}=0,\\
-\sigma(-\Delta_{M}),&n_{+}>0,\;n_{-}=0,\\
\sigma(-\Delta_{M})\cup-\sigma(-\Delta_{M}),&n_{+}n_{-}>0.\end{cases}

(iii)

If
(0,\lambda_{\alpha_{0}})
is a bifurcation point, then
\lambda_{\alpha_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Phi)
. Moreover:

(a)

If
\lambda_{\alpha_{0}}>0
, then
n_{-}>0
and

\ker\nabla^{2}_{u}\Phi(0,\lambda_{\alpha_{0}})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n_{-}}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha_{0}}).

(b)

If
\lambda_{\alpha_{0}}<0
, then
n_{+}>0
and

\ker\nabla^{2}_{u}\Phi(0,\lambda_{\alpha_{0}})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n_{+}}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(-\lambda_{\alpha_{0}}).

(c)

If
\lambda_{\alpha_{0}}=0
, then

\ker\nabla^{2}_{u}\Phi(0,0)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(0).

This characterization follows from the structure of the Hessian at the origin:

\nabla^{2}_{u}\Phi(0,\lambda)=(-a_{1}\Delta_{M}-\lambda)\oplus\cdots\oplus(-a_{p}\Delta_{M}-\lambda),

and the fact that the nonlinear terms vanish quadratically near
u=0
by assumption (A2). Since the spectrum of
-\Delta_{M}
is discrete, so is
\mathcal{P}(\Phi)
.

The next theorem is the main result of this paper. It shows that, with the possible exception of
\lambda=0
, every parameter value at which bifurcation is expected actually gives rise to bifurcation from the trivial branch. Moreover, it establishes that the corresponding continua of nontrivial solutions are unbounded in
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
, thus providing not only existence but also qualitative information about the global structure of the solution set.

Theorem 3.5
.

Consider the system (
3.1
) with potential
F
satisfying assumptions
(A1)
–
(A4)
, and let
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\sigma(-\Delta_{M})\setminus\{0\}
.

(1)

If
n_{-}>0
, then the continuum
\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{\alpha})\subset\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
of weak solutions is unbounded.

(2)

If
n_{+}>0
, then the continuum
\mathcal{C}(-\lambda_{\alpha})\subset\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
of weak solutions is unbounded.

(3)

If
p
is odd, then the continuum
\mathcal{C}(0)\subset\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
of weak solutions is unbounded.

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
3.5
. The argument relies on the presence of torus symmetry. The key tool is an equivariant bifurcation index adapted to
\mathbb{T}
-invariant strongly indefinite functionals.

Definition 3.6
.

Fix
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}(\Phi)
. The
bifurcation index
at
\lambda_{\alpha}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{\alpha})=\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}\text{-}\deg(\nabla\Phi(\cdot,\lambda_{\alpha}+\epsilon),B_{\delta}(\mathbb{H}))-\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}\text{-}\deg(\nabla\Phi(\cdot,\lambda_{\alpha}-\epsilon),B_{\delta}(\mathbb{H}))\in U(\mathbb{T}),

where
\epsilon,\delta>0
are sufficiently small, and
B_{\delta}(\mathbb{H})
denotes the open ball of radius
\delta
around the origin in
\mathbb{H}
.

The index
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
measures the jump in the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant degree across the critical parameter
\lambda_{\alpha}
and generalizes the classical Rabinowitz bifurcation index
[
24
]
to the equivariant, strongly indefinite setting. As in the classical case, if
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{\alpha})\neq\Theta
(with
\Theta\in U(\mathbb{T})
the zero element), then a global bifurcation from the trivial branch occurs at
(0,\lambda_{\alpha})
.

Theorem 3.7

(
\mathbb{T}
-Symmetric Rabinowitz Alternative)
.

Suppose that either
\lambda_{\alpha}\neq 0
, or
\lambda_{\alpha}=0
and
p
is odd. Then
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{\alpha})\neq\Theta
and consequently exactly one of the following alternatives holds:

(1)

The continuum
\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{\alpha})\subset\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
of weak solutions is unbounded.

(2)

The continuum
\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{\alpha})
is bounded, and

\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{\alpha})\cap\bigl(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}\bigr)=\{0\}\times\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}\}\subset\{0\}\times\mathcal{P}(\Phi),

with

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{1})+\dots+\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{s})=\Theta\in U(\mathbb{T}),

where
\Theta
is the zero element of the Euler ring
U(\mathbb{T})
.

The proof follows the strategy of
[
24
]
, adapted to the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant, strongly indefinite setting by replacing the Leray–Schauder degree with the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant degree. Non-vanishing of the bifurcation index is ensured by Theorem 4.5 in
[
8
]
: it holds whenever the Laplace–Beltrami eigenspace
\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
is a non-trivial
\mathbb{T}
-representation, which is the case for
\lambda_{\alpha}\neq 0
by Proposition
2.6
; for
\lambda_{\alpha}=0
, it holds because the kernel of the Hessian at the origin has odd dimension, which follows from Lemma
3.4
.

We now compute the bifurcation index explicitly in the three relevant cases. The expressions below follow from the definition of
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}
and properties of the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant degree.

Lemma 3.8
.

Fix
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\sigma(-\Delta_{M})\setminus\{0\}
.

(1)

If
n_{-}>0
, then

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{\alpha})=\left(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}\text{-}\deg(-\mathrm{Id},B(\bigoplus_{\lambda_{\beta}<\lambda_{\alpha}}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta})))\right)^{n_{-}}\star\left(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}\text{-}\deg(-\mathrm{Id},B(\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})))^{n_{-}}-\mathbb{I}\right).

(2)

If
n_{+}>0
, then

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(-\lambda_{\alpha})=\left(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}\text{-}\deg(-\mathrm{Id},B(\bigoplus_{\lambda_{\beta}\leq\lambda_{\alpha}}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta})))\right)^{n_{+}}\star\left(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}}\text{-}\deg(-\mathrm{Id},B(\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})))^{n_{+}}-\mathbb{I}\right).

(3)

If
\lambda_{\alpha}=0
, then

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(0)=\left((-1)^{n_{-}}-(-1)^{n_{+}}\right)\cdot\mathbb{I}.

Proof.

The proof is identical in structure to that of
[
13
, Lemma 3.4]
, and follows from the properties of the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant degree.
∎

To apply Lemma
3.8
, we now focus on the coordinate
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\pm\lambda_{\alpha})_{H_{\alpha}}
, defined as the coefficient of
\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H_{\alpha}^{+})
in the element
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\pm\lambda_{\alpha})\in U(\mathbb{T})
. The following lemma provides the explicit values of these coordinates.

Lemma 3.9
.

Fix
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\sigma(-\Delta_{M})\setminus\{0\}
. Let

k_{0}=\dim\!\Bigl(\bigoplus_{\lambda_{\beta}<\lambda_{\alpha}}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta})^{\mathbb{T}}\Bigr),\qquad l_{0}=\dim\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})^{\mathbb{T}}.

Then

k_{0}+l_{0}=\dim\!\Bigl(\bigoplus_{\lambda_{\beta}\leq\lambda_{\alpha}}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta})^{\mathbb{T}}\Bigr)

and for every
\lambda_{\beta}<\lambda_{\alpha}
we have

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\pm\lambda_{\beta})_{H_{\alpha}}=0.

Moreover,

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{\alpha})_{H_{\alpha}}=(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{-}+1}n_{-},\qquad\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(-\lambda_{\alpha})_{H_{\alpha}}=(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{+}+1}n_{+}.

Proof.

To compute the coordinate of
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\pm\lambda_{\alpha})
at
H_{\alpha}
, we analyze the corresponding expressions involving Euler characteristics of representation spheres. By construction, the summands corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda_{\beta}<\lambda_{\alpha}
do not contribute to the
H_{\alpha}
-component since the representation
\mathbb{R}[1,\alpha]
first appears in
\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
and is absent from all
\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta})
with
\lambda_{\beta}<\lambda_{\alpha}
. Hence,

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\pm\lambda_{\beta})_{H_{\alpha}}=0\quad\text{for all }\lambda_{\beta}<\lambda_{\alpha}.

By (
2.3
) and Lemma
3.8
, the bifurcation index
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\pm\lambda_{\alpha})
takes the form

\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(S^{\mathbb{V}})^{\pm N}\star\left(\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(S^{\mathbb{W}})^{N}-\mathbb{I}\right),

for suitable finite-dimensional orthogonal
\mathbb{T}
-representations
\mathbb{V}
,
\mathbb{W}
, and an integer
N>0
.

We first carry out the coefficient computation abstractly for general representations. Write

\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{R}[a_{0},0]\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{R}[a_{i},m_{i}],\qquad\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{R}[b_{0},0]\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{p}\mathbb{R}[b_{j},n_{j}],

and apply Theorem
2.8
to obtain the expansions

\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(S^{\mathbb{V}})^{N}=(-1)^{a_{0}N}\Bigl(\mathbb{I}-N\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\,\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H_{m_{i}}^{+})\Bigr)+\text{(codim $\geq 2$)},

\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(S^{\mathbb{W}})^{N}-\mathbb{I}=\bigl((-1)^{b_{0}(N-1)}-1\bigr)\mathbb{I}-(-1)^{b_{0}N}N\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_{j}\,\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H_{n_{j}}^{+})+\text{(codim $\geq 2$)}.

Multiplying the expansions, the coefficient of
\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H_{\alpha}^{+})
in the product is

(-1)^{a_{0}N}\Bigl(-(-1)^{b_{0}N}Nb_{\alpha}-\bigl((-1)^{b_{0}(N-1)}-1\bigr)Na_{\alpha}\Bigr),

where
a_{\alpha}
(resp.
b_{\alpha}
) denotes the multiplicity of
\mathbb{R}[1,\alpha]
in
\mathbb{V}
(resp.
\mathbb{W}
).

For
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
take

\mathbb{V}=\bigoplus_{\lambda_{\beta}<\lambda_{\alpha}}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta}),\qquad\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha}),\qquad N=n_{-}.

Then, by Proposition
2.6
,
a_{\alpha}=0
,
b_{\alpha}=1
, and we set
a_{0}=k_{0}
,
b_{0}=l_{0}
. Substituting into the formula gives

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{\alpha})_{H_{\alpha}}=(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{-}+1}\,n_{-}.

For
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(-\lambda_{\alpha})
take

\mathbb{V}=\bigoplus_{\lambda_{\beta}\leq\lambda_{\alpha}}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta}),\qquad\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha}),\qquad N=n_{+},

so that, again by Proposition
2.6
,
a_{\alpha}=b_{\alpha}=1
, and the dimension of the trivial summand in
\mathbb{V}
is
a_{0}=k_{0}+l_{0}
. Noting that the inverse is given by

\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(S^{\mathbb{V}})^{-N}=(-1)^{a_{0}N}\Bigl(\mathbb{I}+N\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\,\chi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}/H_{m_{i}}^{+})\Bigr)+\text{(codim $\geq 2$)},

we compute the coordinate at
H_{\alpha}
as

(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})N}\Bigl(-(-1)^{l_{0}N}N-\bigl((-1)^{l_{0}(N-1)}-1\bigr)N\Bigr),

which simplifies to
(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})N+1}N
, and thus

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(-\lambda_{\alpha})_{H_{\alpha}}=(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{+}+1}\,n_{+}.

∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.5

Fix a non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\sigma(-\Delta_{M})
and assume
n_{-}>0
; the case
n_{+}>0
is treated analogously.
By the
\mathbb{T}
-symmetric Rabinowitz alternative (Theorem
3.7
), there exists a continuum

\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{\alpha})\subset\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
issuing from
(0,\lambda_{\alpha})
.
Suppose, towards a contradiction, that
\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{\alpha})
is bounded.
Then Theorem
3.7
yields parameters

\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}\in\mathcal{P}(\Phi)
such that

\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{\alpha})\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R})=\{0\}\times\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}\},\quad\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{j})=\Theta\in U(\mathbb{T}).

Without loss of generality, assume that
\lambda_{\alpha}=\max\{|\lambda_{j}|\colon 1\leq j\leq s\}
.
Proposition
2.6
implies that the torus representation
\mathbb{R}[1,\alpha]
first appears at
\lambda_{\alpha}
and is absent from all earlier eigenspaces.
As in Lemma
3.9
, denote

k_{0}=\dim\!\Bigl(\bigoplus_{\lambda_{\beta}<\lambda_{\alpha}}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta})^{\mathbb{T}}\Bigr),\qquad l_{0}=\dim\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})^{\mathbb{T}},

so that
k_{0}+l_{0}=\dim\!\Bigl(\bigoplus_{\lambda_{\beta}\leq\lambda_{\alpha}}\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\beta})^{\mathbb{T}}\Bigr).

For every
j
with
|\lambda_{j}|<\lambda_{\alpha}
, Lemma
3.9
implies that
\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{j})_{H_{\alpha}}=0
. Hence,

\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{j})_{H_{\alpha}}=\theta_{+}\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{\alpha})_{H_{\alpha}}+\theta_{-}\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(-\lambda_{\alpha})_{H_{\alpha}},\qquad\theta_{\pm}\in\{0,1\}.

Using Lemma
3.9
we obtain

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{\alpha})_{H_{\alpha}}=(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{-}+1}n_{-},\qquad\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(-\lambda_{\alpha})_{H_{\alpha}}=(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{+}+1}n_{+}.

Taking the
H_{\alpha}
-component of
\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(\lambda_{j})=\Theta
gives

\theta_{+}(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{-}+1}n_{-}+\theta_{-}(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{+}+1}n_{+}=0.

If
\theta_{-}=0
then
(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{-}+1}n_{-}=0
, contradicting
n_{-}>0
; hence
\theta_{+}=\theta_{-}=1
. The resulting equality

(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{-}+1}n_{-}+(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})n_{+}+1}n_{+}=0

rewrites as

(-1)^{(k_{0}+l_{0})(n_{-}-n_{+})}n_{-}=-n_{+},

which is impossible because
n_{-}
and
n_{+}
are nonnegative integers and
n_{-}+n_{+}=p>0
.
Thus
\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{\alpha})
cannot be bounded, so it is unbounded.
The same argument with
n_{+}
in place of
n_{-}
proves the case
n_{+}>0
.

It remains to consider
\lambda_{\alpha}=0
when
p
is odd.
Lemma
3.8
(3) gives

\mathcal{BIF}_{\mathbb{T}}(0)=\bigl((-1)^{n_{-}}-(-1)^{n_{+}}\bigr)\mathbb{I}\neq 0,

so by Theorem
3.7
there is a continuum
\mathcal{C}(0)\subset\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
bifurcating from
(0,0)
.
If
\mathcal{C}(0)
was bounded, its intersection with the axis
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
would contain finitely many points
(0,\lambda_{j})
with
\lambda_{j}\neq 0
, but each corresponding continuum
\mathcal{C}(\lambda_{j})
is already unbounded by the first part of the proof. All these continua are contained in the same connected set
\mathcal{C}(0)
, so
\mathcal{C}(0)
must also be unbounded, a contradiction.
Therefore every continuum obtained from Theorem
3.7
is unbounded, completing the proof of Theorem
3.5
.
∎

3.4.
Further remarks and examples

We conclude this section with two complementary additions. First, we discuss a general symmetry-breaking phenomenon that occurs at bifurcation points associated with nonzero eigenvalues. Then, we illustrate the applicability of our framework in the concrete setting of products of spheres, which allow for explicit spectral computations and recover earlier results as special cases.

Remark 3.10
.

Let
\lambda_{\alpha}\neq 0
. If
\ker\nabla_{u}^{2}\Phi(0,\lambda_{\alpha})^{G}=\{0\}
, then all nontrivial solutions on the bifurcating branch have strictly smaller isotropy group than the trivial solution. This criterion goes back to Dancer
[
5
]
, and its generalisation to bifurcations from orbits is given in
[
10
, Lemma 3.11]
. In our setting, Lemma
3.4
(iii)
together with the irreducibility of the eigenspaces (Theorem
2.5
) implies that for each
\lambda_{\alpha}\neq 0
, the kernel
\ker\nabla_{u}^{2}\Phi(0,\lambda_{\alpha})
is a direct sum of irreducible, nontrivial
G
-representations and contains no
G
-invariant elements. The conclusion follows.

Example: products of spheres

Let
M=\prod_{i=1}^{d}S^{n_{i}}
be a product of standard spheres. Then
M
is a compact symmetric space of rank
d
, and can be realized as a homogeneous space
G/H
, where
G=\prod_{i}\operatorname{SO}(n_{i}+1)
and
H=\prod_{i}\operatorname{SO}(n_{i})
. The restricted root system consists of
d
mutually orthogonal simple roots
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}
with multiplicities
m_{\alpha_{i}}=n_{i}-1
and normalized inner products
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})=1
.

In this setting, the restricted weight lattice identifies with
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and the set
\Lambda^{+}
corresponds to
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
. The vector
\rho
is given by

\rho=\left(\tfrac{n_{1}-1}{2},\dots,\tfrac{n_{d}-1}{2}\right),

and the eigenvalues of the Laplace–Beltrami operator are indexed by
\alpha=(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})\in\Lambda^{+}
via the formula

\lambda_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}(k_{i}+n_{i}-1).

Each eigenspace is an irreducible
G
-representation with highest restricted weight
\alpha
, and has the form

\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal{H}^{n_{i}}_{k_{i}},

where
\mathcal{H}^{n_{i}}_{k_{i}}
denotes the space of spherical harmonics of degree
k_{i}
on
S^{n_{i}}
. These spaces are irreducible under the canonical action of
\operatorname{SO}(n_{i}+1)
, and the tensor product
\mathbb{V}_{-\Delta_{M}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
is irreducible under the natural action of
G=\prod_{i}\operatorname{SO}(n_{i}+1)
.

The case
d=1
recovers the standard sphere
S^{n}
, whose Laplace spectrum is given by
\lambda_{k}=k(k+n-1)
, with eigenspaces
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{k}
forming irreducible representations of
\operatorname{SO}(n+1)
with highest restricted weight
k
. In this setting, the results of
[
26
]
correspond to the case of a single equation, while
[
28
]
treats the case of a system. Both are recovered as special cases of the present framework, which generalizes these bifurcation results to arbitrary compact symmetric spaces.

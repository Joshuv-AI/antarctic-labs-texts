A Nehari manifold method for nonvariational problems
Radu Precup, Andrei Stan

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to extend the Nehari manifold method from the variational setting to the nonvariational framework of fixed point equations. This is achieved by constructing a radial energy functional that generalizes the standard one from the variational case. Furthermore, the solutions obtained through our method are localized in conical annular sets, which leads to the existence of multiple solutions. The abstract results are illustrated by two representative applications.

Introduction and preliminaries

The Nehari manifold method is a well-known technique for finding nonzero
critical points of functionals. Given a
C^{1}
functional
E
defined on a
Banach space
X,
the Nehari manifold is defined as:

\mathcal{N}=\left\{u\in X\setminus\left\{0\right\}:\ \left\langle E^{\prime}\left(u\right),u\right\rangle_{X}=0\right\},

where
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{X}
denotes the duality
between
X
and its dual
X^{\ast}.
It is clear that any nonzero critical
point of
E
lies in
\mathcal{N}.
Therefore, one may expect that it is
easier to find nonzero critical points by restricting the search to this
smaller set, as fewer or weaker conditions may be required compared to
working in the entire space
X.
This is the case, for example, with the
Palais-Smale compactness condition. Once the Nehari manifold is defined, the
method proceeds by minimizing
E
on
\mathcal{N}.
This approach is
effective when
E
has a specific geometric structure, namely, for each
direction
v\in X,

\left|v\right|=1,
the function

0<t\ \mapsto\ E\left(tv\right)

has a unique critical point at some
t_{v}>0.
In this case, the derivative
satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\left.E\left(tv\right)\right|_{t=t_{v}}=\left\langle E^{\prime}\left(t_{v}v\right),v\right\rangle_{X}=0,

so
u=t_{v}v\in

\mathcal{N}.
Conversely, if
u\in\mathcal{N},
then

0=\left\langle E^{\prime}\left(u\right),u\right\rangle_{X}=\left\langle E^{\prime}\left(\left|u\right|\frac{u}{\left|u\right|}\right),\frac{u}{\left|u\right|}\right\rangle_{X},

showing that
t=\left|u\right|
is a critical point of the
function
E\left(t\frac{u}{\left|u\right|}\right),
i.e.,
t_{\frac{u}{\left|u\right|}}=\left|u\right|.
Thus, under
this geometric condition, the Nehari manifold can be characterized as

\mathcal{N}=\left\{t_{v}v:\ v\in X,\ \left|v\right|=1\right\}.

This method has received increasing attention in recent years and has been successfully applied to various types of problems
nehari_opt
;
nehari_parabolic1
;
faraci-silva
;
nehari_parabolic2
;
n1
.
In the recent papers
a
;
pa
, this technique has been refined to locate critical points
within cones, which is useful when searching for solutions with additional
properties such as positivity. Also, in
pa2
, the method has been combined with Schauder’s fixed point theorem to obtain a localized solution for a system.

Compared to other techniques in critical point theory—such as general
linking methods, and particularly the mountain pass theorem—the Nehari
manifold method essentially requires only a radial behavior of the energy
functional. Motivated by this observation, in this paper we propose a
similar approach within the framework of fixed point theory, specifically
for equations of non-variational type. To extend the technique to a fixed
point equation of the form
u=T\left(u\right),
we begin by introducing a
three-variable functional
\mathcal{F}\left(u,v,w\right)
which is used to
define a radial energy type function
\mathcal{E}
in any direction
v.

That is, for any unit vector
v
and
t>0,
we define:

\mathcal{E}\left(v\right)\left(t\right)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}\left(T\left(sv\right),sv,v\right)ds.

For each direction
v,
the derivative of
\mathcal{E}\left(v\right)
with
respect to
t
is given by

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}\left(v\right)\left(t\right)=\mathcal{F}\left(T\left(tv\right),tv,v\right).

This expression suggests that,
for some direction
v
, the quantity

t\mapsto\mathcal{F}\left(T\left(tv\right),tv,v\right)

can be interpreted
as the (radial) potential of the energy
functional
\mathcal{E}(v)
at the moment
t
. This interpretation is natural since, as shown in the paper,
in the particular case of equations
possessing a variational structure, i.e., the operator
T
is of potential type in the sense that
T=I-E^{\prime}
, for some functional
E
, then the value of
\mathcal{E}(v)(t)
corresponds to
E(tv)
. In this situation, the classical Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}
is recovered, and existing methods apply.
We
emphasize that even in this case, our results significantly refine the
classical ones by localizing the critical point and by imposing a weaker
condition on the radial geometry of the energy function, which, in our case,
requires only a unique maximum point in each direction rather than a unique
critical point which is a maximum.

Under suitable conditions on the operator
T
and the functional
\mathcal{F}
, and through a surprising combination with the Birkhoff–Kellogg invariant-direction theorem, we
develop a Nehari-type method for establishing the existence of fixed points
of
T
within a conical annular region of a Banach space.

To summarize, the novelties of this paper are the following:

(a):

First and most importantly, we extend the Nehari manifold method from the variational setting to a nonvariational framework. This is achieved by constructing a radial energy that corresponds to the usual energy in the variational case.

(b):

The solutions obtained by this method are localized in conical annular sets. By applying the method repeatedly on different annular sets, we obtain the existence of multiple solutions.

(c):

For a given operator, we may construct multiple radial energies by choosing different radial potentials
\mathcal{F}
. Thus, even in the variational case, the energy corresponds to a particular choice of
\mathcal{F}
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we present the main abstract result, introducing the functionals
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{E}
, together with the associated Nehari manifold. Section 3 is devoted to applications: first, we discuss a nonvariational
p
-Laplace problem illustrating our theoretical findings; second, we construct an example of
\mathcal{F}
and
T
such that, for each direction
v
, the associated radial energy functional
\mathcal{E}(v)
has a unique maximum but two critical points, contrary to the common setting in the literature, to which, however, our result still applies.

Main results

2.1
Existence, localization and multiplicity in conical annular sets

Let
\left(X,\lvert\cdot\rvert\right)
be a Banach space,
K\subset X

be a nondegenerate cone (also called a wedge), i.e., a closed, convex set such that
\lambda K\subset K
for all

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
K\setminus\{0\}\neq\emptyset
. Note that, in particular, one may take
K=X
. For any
\rho>0,
denote

K_{\rho}:=\left\{v\in K:~\left|v\right|=\rho\right\}.

Let

T\colon K\rightarrow K\ \ \ \text{and\ \ \ }\mathcal{F}:K\times K\times K_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

be a completely continuous operator and a continuous functional,
respectively.

For each
v\in K_{1},
we associate to
\mathcal{F}
and
T
the
”radial energy functional”
in direction
v,

\mathcal{E}(v):(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\ \ \ \mathcal{E}(v)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}(T(sv),sv,v)ds.

It is straightforward to verify that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(v)(t)=\mathcal{F}(T(tv),tv,v).

Let
r,R
be two real numbers such that
0\leq r<R\leq\infty
, and define

K_{rR}:=\left\{u\in K\,:\,r\leq|u|\leq R\right\}.

Clearly, if
r=0
, then

K_{0R}=\left\{u\in K\,:\,|u|\leq R\right\};

if
R=\infty
, then

K_{r\infty}=\left\{u\in K\,:\,|u|\geq r\right\};

and if
0=r,\,R=\infty
, then

K_{0\infty}=K.

Our main assumption on the functional
\mathcal{E}
is the following:

(h1)

There exist
r_{0}>0
and
R_{0}<\infty
with
r\leq r_{0}<R_{0}\leq R
such that, for every
v\in K_{1}
, the mapping
\mathcal{E}(v)
attains a unique maximum
t_{v}
on the interval
[r,R]
, and this maximum satisfies
r_{0}<t_{v}<R_{0}
.

In particular, one may set

r_{0}=r\ \ \text{if }r>0\ \ \ \text{and\ \ \ }R_{0}=R\ \ \text{if }R<\infty.

Lemma 1
.

Under the assumption
(h1)
, the mapping

v\in K_{1}\mapsto t_{v}

is continuous.

Proof.

Let
v_{k}\in K_{1}
with
v_{k}\to v^{0}
as
k\to\infty
.
Clearly,
v^{0}\in K_{1}
, and by assumption (h1), the sequence
\left(t_{v_{k}}\right)
is bounded.
In order to prove that
t_{v_{k}}\to t_{v^{0}}
, it suffices to show that any convergent subsequence of
\left(t_{v_{k}}\right)
has limit
t_{v^{0}}
(see, e.g.,
(
herve
, Lemma 1.1)
).
Thus, let
\left(t_{v_{k}}\right)
be a subsequence converging to some
t^{0}\in[r_{0},R_{0}]
.
Using (h1) we have that

\mathcal{E}\left(v_{k}\right)\left(t_{v_{k}}\right)\geq\mathcal{E}\left(v_{k}\right)\left(t\right)\ \ \ \text{for all\ }t\in\left[r,R\right],

that is

\int_{0}^{t_{v_{k}}}\mathcal{F}(T(sv_{k}),sv_{k},v_{k})ds\geq\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}(T(sv_{k}),sv_{k},v_{k})ds\ \ \left(t\in\left[r,R\right]\right).

Passing to the limit we obtain

\int_{0}^{t^{0}}\mathcal{F}\left(T(sv^{0}),sv^{0},v^{0}\right)ds\geq\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}(T(sv^{0}),sv^{0},v^{0})ds\ \ \left(t\in\left[r,R\right]\right),

so
t^{0}
is a maximum on
\left[r,R\right]
of
\mathcal{E}(v^{0}).

Again from (h1), we have
t^{0}=t_{v^{0}}.
Since the convergent subsequence
\left(t_{v_{k}}\right)
was arbitrarily chosen, we infer that the entire sequence
\left(t_{v_{k}}\right)
converges to
t_{v^{0}},
which proves our statement.

In what follows we denote

\displaystyle U:=\left\{tv:\ v\in K_{1},\ 0\leq t<t_{v}\right\}

and

U_{b}:=\left\{t_{v}v:\ v\in K_{1}\right\}.

We call
U_{b}
the
Nehari-type manifold
associated with the functional
\mathcal{F}
, operator
T
and the conical annular region

.

Remark 1
.

Under assumption (h1), any point
u\in U_{b}
satisfies

r\leq r_{0}<\left|u\right|<R_{0}\leq R.

Thus, any fixed point of
T
that belongs to
U_{b}
lies in the conical annular set
K_{rR}
. Moreover, assuming that
0<r<R<\infty
, if
u\in U_{b}
is a fixed point of
T
, then the associated energy in the direction

, that is
\mathcal{E}\left(\frac{u}{|u|}\right)(t)

at the unique point

.
Therefore, looking for a fixed point of the operator
T
within the Nehari-type manifold

, we not only obtain a fixed point lying in a conical annular region, but also one that maximizes the energy functional along its own direction.

Remark 2
.

Under assumption (h1), one has

\mathcal{F}\left(T\left(u\right),u,\frac{u}{|u|}\right)=0\ \ \ \text{for all }u\in U_{b}.

Indeed, if
u\in U_{b},
then
u=t_{v}v
for

Also,

being the maximum of

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}\left(v\right)\left(t_{v}\right)=\mathcal{F}(T(t_{v}v),t_{v}v,v)=0.

.

Under the assumption
(h1)
, the set

Proof.

Clearly, by definition, we have

(ii) Openness.

Since

Next, observe that

Passing to the limit in (

(iii)
\partial U=U_{b}
.

In order to prove that

we need to show that

(2)

Let
u\in\overline{U}
be any point. Then

is the limit of a sequence
u_{k}\in U
, where

u_{k}=t_{k}v_{k}
with

Taking to a subsequence we may assume that

If
t^{0}=0,
then

Otherwise, if
t^{0}>0,
we
deduce that
v_{k}\rightarrow\frac{1}{t^{0}}u=:v^{0}
and

Also, based on Lemma

, one has
t_{v_{k}}\rightarrow t_{v^{0}},
and

we deduce that

Hence

where

and

that
is

Thus
\overline{U}\subset D.

For the converse inclusion, take any
u\in D.
Then

u=tv\ \ \ \text{ for some\ \ }v\in K_{1}\ \ \text{and\ }t\ \text{with\ \ }0\leq t\leq t_{v}.

If

then
u=0\in\overline{U}.
For

we may choose an increasing

\left(t_{k}\right)
with

Clearly

and

Hence

Therefore

finishing the proof of (

Finally, from the representations of

and

(h2)

u_{i}\in K
with

r_{i}<\left|u_{i}\right|<R_{i},\ \ \ i=1,2,...,n.

\left(r_{i},R_{i}\right),\ i=1,2,...\

r_{i}<R_{i}\leq r_{i+1}\ \ {\ \text{f}or\ }i=1,2,...

and Theorem
4

2.2
Recover the classical variational framework

In Section
2.1
, a Nehari-type method has been introduced in a nonvariational framework, starting from a fixed point problem

T\left(u\right)=u

associated with an operator
T.
In this respect, an energy-type function and a Nehari-type manifold have been defined. The natural question is whether we can recover the classical Nehari method within the variational framework.

To give an answer, let
X^{\ast}
be the dual of
X
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the duality between
X^{\ast}
and
X,
and let the norms
on
X
and
X^{\ast}
be denoted by the same symbol
\left|\cdot\right|.
We shall denote by
J
the
duality mapping
corresponding to a normalization function
\theta,
i.e. the set-valued
operator
J:X\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}(X^{\ast})
defined by

Jx=\big\{x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast}:\ \langle x^{\ast},x\rangle=\theta\left(|x|\right)\left|x\right|,\ |x^{\ast}|=\theta\left(|x|\right)\big\},\quad x\in X.

Recall that by a normalization function we mean a continuous strictly
increasing function
\theta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\theta\left(0\right)=0
and lim
{}_{t\rightarrow\infty}\theta\left(t\right)=\infty.
We assume that
\theta\left(1\right)=1.
Obviously, one has

J\left(\lambda x\right)=\theta\left(\lambda\right)J(x)

for every
x\in X
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.
We assume that
J
is single-valued, bijective, and that both
J
and its
inverse
J^{-1}
are continuous.

Furthermore, assume that
E\in C^{1}(X)
is a functional satisfying, without loss of generality,
E(0)=0
. We can easily see that for any
t>0
and
u\in X
, one has

E(tu)=E(tu)-E(0)=\int_{0}^{1}\langle E^{\prime}(\theta tu),\,tu\rangle d\theta=\int_{0}^{t}\langle E^{\prime}(su),\,u\rangle ds.

Our goal is to obtain critical point of
E,
that is to solve the equation

E^{\prime}\left(u\right)=0.

Take
K=X
. We consider the operator
T:K\rightarrow K
defined by

T\left(u\right):=J^{-1}\left(Ju-E^{\prime}\left(u\right)\right).

Obviously, the critical points of the functional
E
are the fixed points of
the operator
T.

Let us consider the functional
\mathcal{F}:K\times K\times K_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(recall that
K_{1}=\{u\in K\,:\,|x|=1\}
),

\mathcal{F}\left(u,v,w\right):=\left\langle Jv-Ju,w\right\rangle.

Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\left(v\right)\left(t\right)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}\left(T\left(sv\right),sv,v\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle J\left(sv\right)-JT\left(sv\right),v\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle E^{\prime}\left(sv\right),v\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=E(tv).

From this, we observe that the classical energy functional
E
is recovered through
\mathcal{E}
. Consequently, the classical Nehari manifold method (see
nehari
) is also retrieved; both in terms of the maximized mapping

t\mapsto E(tu),

and in the classical Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}
given by

\mathcal{N}=\left\{u\in X:\langle E^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\right\}.

Corollary 1
.

Under the previous conditions, if in addition

J^{-1}\left(Ju-E^{\prime}\left(u\right)\right)(K)\subset K,

then
E
has a critical point
u
in the Nehari manifold
U_{b},
with

u\in K,\ \ r<\left|u\right|<R.

In addition,
u
maximizes the radial energy functional

t\mapsto\mathcal{E}(v)\left(t\right)=E(tv),

on the interval
\left(r,R\right),
along its own direction
v=\frac{1}{\left|u\right|}u.

Proof.

We only need to check conditions (h2) and (h3).

Check of (h2)
: Let
u\in U_{b}
and denote

w:=T\left(u\right)=J^{-1}\left(Ju-E^{\prime}\left(u\right)\right).

Then
\left|u\right|\geq r_{0}
and

Jw=Ju-E^{\prime}\left(u\right),

whence, since
\left\langle E^{\prime}\left(u\right),u\right\rangle=0,

one has

\left\langle Jw,u\right\rangle=\left\langle Ju-E^{\prime}(u),u\right\rangle=\left\langle Ju,u\right\rangle=\theta\left(\left|u\right|\right)\left|u\right|\leq\left|Jw\right|\left|u\right|=\theta\left(\left|w\right|\right)\left|u\right|.

From this, we have
\theta(|w|)\geq\theta(|u|)
. Thus, by the monotonicity of the function
\theta
, it follows that

|w|\geq|u|\geq r_{0}.

Therefore,

\inf_{u\in U_{b}}\left|T\left(u\right)\right|\geq r_{0}>0.

Check of (h3)
: Assume that

\mathcal{F}\left(tu,u,\frac{u}{|u|}\right)=0,

for some
t>0
and
u\in K\setminus\{0\}.
Then

0=\left\langle Ju-J\left(tu\right),\frac{u}{|u|}\right\rangle,

which is equivalent to

0=\left\langle Ju-\varphi\left(t\right)Ju,u\right\rangle=\left(1-\varphi\left(t\right)\right)\varphi\left(\left|u\right|\right)\left|u\right|.

Hence,
\varphi(t)=1
, and therefore
t=1
, according to our assumption that
\varphi(1)=1
.

Thus Theorem
4
applies and gives the result.
∎

3
Applications

3.1
A nonvariational Dirichlet problem with
p
-Laplacian

To illustrate the theoretical results, we first consider the following nonvariational Dirichlet problem for
a
p
-Laplace equation

\begin{cases}-\left(\left|u^{\prime}\right|^{p-2}u^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}(t)=f(u(t),u^{\prime}(t)),\quad t\in\left(0,1\right),\\
u(t)\geq 0\\
u(0)=u(1)=0,\end{cases}

(4)

where
1<p<\infty
, and
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a
continuous function.

Consider the Banach space
X:=W_{0}^{1,p}\left(0,1\right)
endowed with
the usual norm
|u|_{1,p}:=|\nabla u|_{p}
, where

|w|_{p}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}|w|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad(w\in L^{p}(0,1)).

It is well known (see, e.g.,

(
djm
, Chapter 1.2)
) that
W_{0}^{1,p}(0,1)
is a uniformly convex and
reflexive Banach space with
W_{0}^{-1,q}(0,1)
its dual, where
q
is the conjugate of
p
, i.e.,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the duality pairing between

W_{0}^{-1,q}(0,1)
and
W_{0}^{1,p}(0,1)
. Then, for any
v\in L^{q}(0,1)\subset W_{0}^{-1,q}(0,1)
, we have

\langle v,u\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}v(t)u(t)dt,

for all
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(0,1)
(see, e.g.,
(
brezis
, Proposition 8.14)
).

In what follows,
J\colon W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)\to W^{-1,q}_{0}(0,1)
denotes the duality mapping of
W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)
corresponding to the normalization function
\theta(t)=t^{p-1}
(
t\geq 0
).
It is known that

Ju=-\left(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime}\right)^{\prime},\quad u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1),

and that
J
is bijective and continuous, while its inverse
J^{-1}
is strongly monotone, bounded (maps bounded sets into bounded sets), and continuous (see, e.g.,
(
djm
, Theorem 8)
).

Let
\lambda_{p}
denote the first eigenvalue of the Euler-Lagrange
equation

Ju=\lambda|u|_{1,p}^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\,(0,1),\quad u(0)=u(1)=0.

Then (see, e.g.,
(
djm
, Remark 6)
),

\lambda_{p}=\min_{u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(0,1)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|u|_{1,p}^{p}}{|u|_{p}^{p}},

that is,
c_{p}:=\lambda_{p}^{-1/p}
is the smallest constant such that, for all
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(0,1)
, we have

|u|_{p}\leq c_{p}\,|u|_{1,p}.

(5)

Given the continuous
embedding of
W_{0}^{1,p}(0,1)
in
C[0,1]
(see e.g.,
brezis
), one has

\left|u(t)\right|\leq|u|_{1,p}\ \ \ \left(t\in\left[0,1\right]\right),

(6)

for every
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(0,1)
.

In
W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)
, we consider the cone

\displaystyle K=\Big\{u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)\,:

\displaystyle u\geq 0,\,u\text{
is concave},\,u(t)=u(1-t)

\displaystyle\text{ and }u(t)\geq\phi(t)\,|u|_{1,p}\text{ for all }t\in[0,1/2]\Big\},

where
\phi
is given by

\phi:[0,1/2]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+},\text{ \quad$\phi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(1-2s)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}ds$.}

(7)

For
0<r<R<\infty
, we assume that the following conditions hold:

(H1)

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the function
f(x,\cdot)
is decreasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and for each
y\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
f(\cdot,y)
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Moreover,
f
is even in its second variable, that is,

f(x,y)=f(x,-y),

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
.

(H2)

Let

f_{0}(x)=f(x,0)\quad\text{ and }\quad f_{\infty}(x)=\lim_{y\to\infty}f(x,y).

The functions
f_{0}
and
f_{\infty}
satisfy

f_{0}(r)<\frac{r^{p-1}}{c_{p}}\quad\text{ and }\quad f_{\infty}(R\phi(\beta))>\frac{R^{p-1}}{2\Phi},

(8)

where
\beta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
is a fixed value, and

\Phi:=\int_{\beta}^{1/2}\phi(s)ds.

(H3)

For each
x\in(0,\infty)
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, the mapping

t\mapsto\frac{f(tx,ty)}{t^{p-1}}\,\text{ }

is strictly increasing on
(0,R].

In order to apply Theorem
4
, we choose the functional
\mathcal{F}\colon K\times K\times K_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
to be

\mathcal{F}(u,v,w)=\langle Jv-Ju,w\rangle.

Clearly
\mathcal{F}
is continuous as
J
is continuous.

We observe that the problem (
4
) allows for a fixed point formulation

u=T(u),

where
T\colon K\to W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)
is given by

T(u)=J^{-1}N_{f}(u,u^{\prime}),

(9)

and

N_{f}\colon K\times L^{p}(0,1)\to L^{q}(0,1),\quad N_{f}(u,\tilde{u})(t)=f(u(t),\tilde{u}(t)),

(10)

is the Nemytskii operator associated with the function
f
.

In the subsequent, we will present a series of auxiliary results that will be used to prove the invariance of the operator
T
over
K
. When we say that a function
u
is symmetric, we understand symmetric with respect to
\frac{1}{2}
.

The first result concerns the symmetry and nonnegativity of the solution to a
p
-Laplace equation.

Lemma 3
.

Let
h\in L^{q}(0,1)
be nonnegative a.e. on
(0,1)
and symmetric. Then so is
J^{-1}h
.

Proof.

Clearly
J^{-1}h
is well defined (recall that
L^{q}(0,1)\subset W^{-1,q}_{0}
(0,1)). Nonnegativity follows directly from the comparison principle for the
p
-Laplace operator (see
(
ac
, Lemma 1.3)
). Let
u_{h}=J^{-1}h
and define
w(t)=u_{h}(1-t)
.
A simple computation shows that
Jw=h
.
Thus, by the uniqueness of the solution to the
p
-Laplace equation, we deduce that
u_{h}=w
, which proves that
J^{-1}h
is symmetric.
∎

Our second result concerns the preservation of concavity for a
p
-Laplace equation.
Before stating it, let us define

\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\varphi(x)=|x|^{p-2}x=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\,|x|^{p-1}.

Clearly,
\varphi
is a homeomorphism from
\mathbb{R}
onto
\mathbb{R}
, and its inverse
\varphi^{-1}
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, the problem (
4
) can be written in terms of
\varphi
as

-\left(\varphi(u^{\prime})\right)^{\prime}=f(u,u^{\prime}),\quad u(0)=u(1)=0.

Lemma 4
.

For any
h\in L^{q}(0,1)
that is symmetric with respect to
\frac{1}{2}
, we have the representation

\left(J^{-1}h\right)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(\varphi^{-1}\circ\mu)(s)ds,

(11)

where

\mu(t)=\int_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}h(s)ds.

If in addition
h
is
nonnegative a.e. on
(0,1)
and nondecreasing a.e. on
\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, then
J^{-1}h
is concave on
(0,1)
.

Proof.

Denote

w(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(\varphi^{-1}\circ\mu)(s)\,ds,

Since
\mu
is continuous (see, e.g.,
(
brezis
, Theorem 8.2)
), it follows that
w\in C^{1}[0,1]
. Thus,

\varphi(w^{\prime})=\mu,

and by
(
brezis
, Lemma 8.2)
, we have that

-\varphi(w^{\prime})^{\prime}=h.

By the uniqueness of the solution to the
p
-Laplace equation (see, e.g.,
(
djm
, Remark 3)
), we obtain (
11
) as the unique representation of
J^{-1}h
.

Assume now that
h
is nonnegative a.e. on
(0,1)
and nondecreasing a.e. on
\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
.
We observe that
\mu^{\prime}=-h\leq 0
a.e. on
(0,1)
; hence
\mu
is nonincreasing on
(0,1)
(see, e.g.,
(
folland
, Chapter 3)
).
Since

(J^{-1}h)^{\prime}=\varphi^{-1}\circ\mu,

and the right-hand side is nonincreasing on
(0,1)
(as the composition of an increasing and a nonincreasing function), it follows that
J^{-1}h
is concave on
(0,1)
.
∎

In the following, we establish a Harnack type inequality.

Lemma 5
.

Let
h\in L^{q}(0,1)
be nonnegative a.e. on
(0,1)
, symmetric with respect to
\frac{1}{2}
, and nondecreasing a.e. on
\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Then, for all
t\in

\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, one has

\left(J^{-1}h\right)(t)\geq\phi(t)\left|J^{-1}h\right|_{1,p},

(12)

where
\phi
is given in (
7
).

Proof.

Let
u_{h}=J^{-1}h
.
Since
u_{h}
is symmetric with respect to
\frac{1}{2}
(see Lemma
3
), one has (recall that
u_{h}\in C^{1}[0,1]
),

u_{h}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=0.

It is not difficult to prove that

u_{h}^{\prime}(t)\geq(1-2t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u_{h}^{\prime}(0),

(13)

for all
t\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right).
To see this, let

\sigma\colon\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\to\mathbb{R},\quad\sigma(t)=u_{h}^{\prime}(t)^{p-1}-(1-2t)u_{h}^{\prime}(0)^{p-1}.

Since
u_{h}
is concave and
u_{h}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=0
, we deduce that
u_{h}^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
.
Thus,

u_{h}^{\prime}(t)^{p-1}=-Ju_{h},

which implies

\left(u_{h}^{\prime}(t)^{p-1}\right)^{\prime}=-h.

Therefore,

\sigma^{\prime}(t)=-h(t)+2u_{h}^{\prime}(0)^{p-1}.

which is nonincreasing a.e. on
\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Following
(
jp
, Remark 2.5)
, we conclude that
\sigma
is concave. As
\sigma(0)=\sigma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=0
, we have that
\sigma(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, i.e., (
13
) holds.

Since
u_{h}(0)=0
, integrating (
13
) from
0
to
t
(
t\leq\frac{1}{2}
), we obtain

u_{h}(t)\geq u^{\prime}_{h}(0)\int_{0}^{t}(1-2s)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}ds=\phi(t)u^{\prime}_{h}(0).

Finally, the conclusion follows from the above inequality and

|u_{h}|_{1,p}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(s)|^{p}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(2\int_{0}^{1/2}u^{\prime}(s)^{p}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(u^{\prime}(0)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=u_{h}^{\prime}(0).

∎

Now we prove that the Nemytskii’s operator
N_{f}
is well-defined, bounded (maps bounded sets into bounded sets) and continuous.

Lemma 6
.

Assume condition
(H1)
holds true. Then the Nemytskii operator
N_{f}
is well-defined, bounded (maps bounded sets into bounded sets) and continuous from
K\times L^{p}(0,1)
to
L^{q}(0,1)
.

Proof.

From (H1), we have

0\leq f(x,y)\leq f(x,0),

(14)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Indeed, for
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, the evenness of
f
in its second variable gives
f(x,y)=f(x,|y|)
.
Since
f(x,\cdot)
is decreasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we obtain
f(x,|y|)\leq f(x,0)
.
The left-hand side of (
14
) follows from the definition of
f
.

Well-definedness.
Let
u\in K
and
\tilde{u}\in L^{p}(0,1).

Since
f
is continuous, it follows that
N_{f}(u,\tilde{u})
is measurable (see, e.g.,
(
p semilinear
, Proposition 9.1)
).
Moreover, as
f(u,0)\in C[0,1]
, using (
14
) we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}f(u,\tilde{u})^{q}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{1}f(u,0)^{q}\,dx=|f(u,0)|_{q}^{q},

which shows that
N_{f}(u,\tilde{u})\in L^{q}(0,1)
.

Boundedness.
Let
u\in K
and
\tilde{u}\in L^{p}(0,1).

Using (
14
), to prove the boundedness of
N_{f}
, it suffices to show that the operator
u\mapsto f(u,0)
maps bounded sets into bounded sets from
W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)
to
L^{q}(0,1)
. This however follows immediately from the continuous embedding of
W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)
into
C[0,1]
and the continuity of
f
.

Continuity.
Let
(u_{k},\tilde{u}_{k})\in K\times L^{p}(0,1)
be such that
(u_{k},\tilde{u}_{k})\to(u,\tilde{u})
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)\times L^{p}(0,1)
as
k\to\infty
. Then, there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
(u_{k},\tilde{u}_{k})
) with the property that (see, e.g.,
(
brezis
, Theorem 4.9)
)

(u_{k}(x),\tilde{u}_{k}(x))\to(u(x),\tilde{u}(x))\,\,\text{ a.e. on }(0,1).

Passing again to a subsequence if necessarily, we may assume that

N_{f}(u_{k},\tilde{u}_{k})(x)\to N_{f}(u,\tilde{u})(x)\,\,\text{ a.e. on }(0,1).

Since
u_{k}\to u
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)
, using (
6
), there exists
M>0
such that
|u_{k}(t)|\leq M
, for all
t\in[0,1]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Denote
M_{1}:=\sup_{x\in[0,M]}|f(x,0)|
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and set
\delta=\frac{\varepsilon^{q}}{M_{1}}
.
Then, for any
D\subset[0,1]
with
meas(D)<\delta
, using (
14
), we estimate

|N_{f}(u_{k},\tilde{u}_{k})|_{L^{q}}\leq\left(\int_{D}|f(u_{k}(x),0)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq meas(D)^{\frac{1}{q}}M_{1}\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{q}}M_{1}=\varepsilon.

By Vitali’s theorem,
N_{f}(u_{k},\tilde{u}_{k})
converges to
N_{f}(u,\tilde{u})
in
L^{q}(0,1)
.
Since the limit is the same regardless of the chosen subsequence, we conclude that the entire sequence
N_{f}(u_{k},\tilde{u}_{k})
converges to
N_{f}(u,\tilde{u})
in
L^{q}(0,1)
(see, e.g.,
(
herve
, Lemma 1.1)
).
∎

Now we are ready to show that
T(K)\subset K
.

Lemma 7
.

Assume condition
(H1)
holds true. Then,

T(K)\subset K.

Proof.

Let
u\in K
. Then, by Lemma
6
, we have

N_{f}(u,u^{\prime})\in L^{q}(0,1)
. Since
u\geq 0
, it follows that

f(u,u^{\prime})\geq 0
. The symmetry of
u
implies that
u^{\prime}
is
antisymmetric, i.e.,

u^{\prime}(t)=-u^{\prime}(1-t)\quad\text{a.e.\ on $(0,1)$}.

Thus, condition (H1) ensures that
N_{f}(u,u^{\prime})
is symmetric. Moreover, since
u

is nondecreasing on
\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
and
u^{\prime}
is
nonincreasing a.e. on
\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, condition (H1) also
ensures that
N_{f}(u,u^{\prime})
is nondecreasing a.e. on

\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Now applying Lemmas
3
,
4
and
5
, we find that
J^{-1}f(u,u^{\prime})=T(u)
is nonegative, symmetric, concave and satisfies the Harnack inequality (
12
). Therefore,
T(u)\in K
.
∎

Next, we prove the complete continuity of the operator
T
.

Lemma 8
.

Under assumption
(H1)
, the operator
T
is completely continuous from
K
to
K
.

Proof.

We observe that

T=J^{-1}\circ P\circ N_{f}\circ I,

where

P\colon K\to K\times L^{p}(0,1),\quad P(u)=(u,u^{\prime}),

and
I
is the embedding operator

I\colon L^{q}(0,1)\to W^{-1,q}_{0}(0,1),\quad I(u)=\langle u,\cdot\rangle.

Since, by the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem (see, e.g.,
brezis
),

W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)
embeds compactly into
L^{p}(0,1)
, it follows that

L^{q}(0,1)
embeds compactly into
W^{-1,q}_{0}(0,1)
, as
the dual spaces of
L^{p}(0,1)
and
W^{1,p}_{0}(0,1)
, respectively. This follows from the Schauders’s theorem on compact operators (see,
(
brezis
, Theorem 6.4)
,
(
rudinFA
, Theorem 4.19)
or
(
ka
, Chapter 9)
). Additionally, it is clear that
I
is continuous and bounded.
Since
J^{-1}
,
P
,
N_{f}
, and
I
are continuous and bounded, and one of these operators is compact, it follows that
T
is completely continuous, as the composition of all these four operators.

∎

Theorem 7
.

Under conditions
(H1)-(H3)
, the problem (
4
) admits a
solution
u\in K
such that

r<|u|_{1,p}<R.

Moreover,
u
maximizes the energy function
\mathcal{E}(v)
on the interval
\left(r,R\right),
along its own direction
v=\frac{1}{\left|u\right|}u.

Proof.

Check of (h1).
We will show that for any
v\in K_{1}
, we have that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(v)(t)|_{t=r}>0,\quad\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(v)(t)|_{t=R}<0,

(15)

and the mapping

t\mapsto\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(v)(t),

(16)

has a unique zero. It is easily to see that whenever (
15
) and (
16
) hold, then the function
t\mapsto\mathcal{E}(v)(t)
has a unique maximum, as required by the abstract result.

For any
v\in K_{1}
and
t>0
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(v)(t)

\displaystyle=t^{p-1}|v|_{1,p}^{p}-\int_{0}^{1}f\left(tv(s),tv^{\prime}(s)\right)v(s)ds

\displaystyle=t^{p-1}\left(|v|_{1,p}^{p}-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f\left(tv(s),tv^{\prime}(s)\right)}{t^{p-1}}v(s)ds\right).

Using (
14
) and the monotonicity of
f
in the first variable, we obtain

f(rv(s),rv^{\prime}(s))\leq f(rv(s),0)\leq f(r,0)=f_{0}(r),\quad s\in(0,1),

(17)

where the latter inequality follows from (
6
), which ensures that
v(s)\leq 1
for all
s\in[0,1]
.

Thus, (
17
) yields

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f\left(rv(s),rv^{\prime}(s)\right)}{r^{p-1}}v(t)ds\leq\frac{f_{0}\left(r\right)}{r^{p-1}}\int_{0}^{1}v(s)ds<\frac{1}{c_{p}}|v|_{p}\leq 1.

Consequently,

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(v)(t)|_{t=r}=r^{p-1}\left(1-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f\left(rv(s),rv^{\prime}(s)\right)}{r^{p-1}}v(s)ds\right)>0,

for all
v\in K_{1}
.

To obtain the second inequality in (
15
),
observe that for any
v\in K_{1}
, the Harnack inequality, together with the monotonicity of
v
on
\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, gives

v(t)\geq\phi(t)\,\,\,\text{for all }t\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad v(t)\geq\phi(\beta)\,\,\,\text{for all }t\in\left(\beta,\frac{1}{2}\right).

(18)

From (
18
) we obtain

\int_{\beta}^{1}v(s)\,ds\geq\Phi.

(19)

Next, by (H1) we have

f(x,y)\geq f_{\infty}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ y\in\mathbb{R}.

(20)

Therefore, the monotonicity of
f
in its first variable, together with (
18
) and (
20
), ensures that

f(Rv(s),Rv^{\prime}(s))\geq f_{\infty}(Rv(s))\geq f_{\infty}(Rv(\beta))\geq f_{\infty}(R\phi(\beta)),

(21)

for all
s\in\left(\beta,\frac{1}{2}\right)
.
Combining the second inequality from (H2), (
19
) and (
21
), we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(Rv(s),Rv^{\prime}(s))}{R^{p-1}}v(s)ds

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{f(Rv(s),Rv^{\prime}(s))}{R^{p-1}}v(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq 2\frac{f(R\phi(\beta))}{R^{p-1}}\int_{\beta}^{\frac{1}{2}}v(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq 2\frac{f(R\phi(\beta))}{R^{p-1}}\Phi.

\displaystyle>1.

Thus,

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(v)(t)|_{t=R}=R^{p-1}\left(1-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f\left(Rv(s),Rv^{\prime}(s)\right)}{R^{p-1}}v(s)ds\right)<0,

for all
v\in K_{1}
, so
both inequalities in (
15
) are valid.

To verify (
16
), we note that by (H3), for each
v\in K_{1}
, the mapping

t\mapsto\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f\left(tv(s),tv^{\prime}(s)\right)}{t^{p-1}}v(s)ds

is strictly increasing. Therefore, by (
15
), we have that

1-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f\left(rv(s),rv^{\prime}(s)\right)}{r^{p-1}}v(s)ds>0\quad\text{ and }\quad 1-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f\left(Rv(s),Rv^{\prime}(s)\right)}{R^{p-1}}v(s)ds<0,

so the equation

1-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f\left(tv(s),tv^{\prime}(s)\right)}{t^{p-1}}v(s)ds=0,

has a unique solution. This however implies that, for all
v\in K_{1},
the mapping

t\mapsto\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(v)(t)=t^{p-1}\left(1-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f\left(tv(s),tv^{\prime}(s)\right)}{t^{p-1}}v(s)ds\right),

has a unique zero, as desired.

Check of (h2).
Let
u\in U_{b}
. Then, by definition, we have

0=\mathcal{F}\left(T(u),u,\frac{u}{|u|_{1,p}}\right)=\left\langle Ju-J(T(u)),\frac{u}{|u|_{1,p}}\right\rangle,

which gives,

\langle J(T(u)),u\rangle=\langle Ju,u\rangle.

One has,

\theta(|u|_{1,p})|u|_{1,p}=\langle Ju,u\rangle=\langle J(T(u)),u\rangle\leq|J(T(u))|_{-1,q}\,|u|_{1,p}=\theta(|T(u)|_{1,p})|u|_{1,p},

whence

\theta(|u|_{1,p})\leq\theta(|T(u)|_{1,p}).

By the monotonicity of
\theta
we obtain

|T(u)|_{1,p}\geq|u|_{1,p}\geq r,

which shows that (h2) is satisfied.

Check of (h3).
Since
\theta(t)=t^{p-1}
, as shown in Corollary

, the functional
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (h3).

The conclusion follows from Theorem
4
, as

is completely continuous and invariant over

(see Lemmas
7
and
8
), and conditions (h1)–(h3) are satisfied.

\frac{f_{\infty}(r\phi(\beta))}{(r\phi(\beta))^{p-1}}>\frac{1}{2\Phi\,\phi(\beta)^{p-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{f_{0}(R)}{R^{p-1}}<\frac{1}{c_{p}},

Example 1
.

A typical example of a function
f
that satisfies conditions (H1)–(H3) is

f(x,y)=x^{p}\left(1+\frac{1}{1+|y|}\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Clearly,
f
satisfies (H1). Moreover,
f_{0}(x)=|x|^{p}
and
f_{\infty}(x)=2|x|^{p}
, and they satisfy

\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{f_{0}(x)}{x^{p-1}}=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{f_{\infty}(x)}{x^{p-1}}=0,

and

Therefore, (H2)–(H3) hold for sufficiently small

, respectively.

3.2
Radial functional energy with two critical points on each direction

In this subsection, we construct an explicit example of an operator
T
and a functional
\mathcal{F}
such that, for each direction
v
, the associated radial energy functional
\mathcal{E}(v)

has two critical points: one corresponding to a global maximum point and the other to a local minimum. We remark that, for such a problem, a an approach similar to Theorem
7
is not applicable, since there we require that require the mapping
t\mapsto\mathcal{E}(v)(t)
has a global maximizer, which is the unique critical point (relation (
16
)). However, the abstract result, Theorem
4
, can still be applied, as it only requires
\mathcal{E}(v)
to have a unique maximum point.

This example is illustrative since many results based on the Nehari manifold method typically require that, for each direction
v
, the mapping
t\mapsto E(tv)
has a unique critical point, which is a global
maximum point (see, e.g.,
(
nehari
, Chapter 3)
,
(
figueredo
, Theorem 2.1)
). Therefore, our results can indeed be applied to a larger class of problems.

We consider the fixed point problem

\displaystyle u(t)=\int_{0}^{1}k(t,s)\,f(u(s))\,ds=:T(u)(t),\quad t\in(0,1),

(24)

where the kernel
k\colon[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is given by

k(t,s)=\begin{cases}t(1-s),&t\leq s,\\
s(1-t),&s\leq t,\end{cases}

and
f(x)=a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}
with coefficients

a_{2}=10^{-2},\quad a_{1}=2.5\times 10^{-3},\quad a_{0}=1.

In order to apply the abstract result, let
X=C[0,1]
, endowed with the supremum norm

|u|_{\infty}=\max_{t\in[0,1]}\,|u(t)|,

and let the
cone
K\subset X
be given by

K=\left\{u\in C[0,1]:u\geq 0\ \text{and}\ \min_{t\in\left[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\right]}u(t)\geq\frac{1}{4}|u|_{\infty}\right\}.

The functional
\mathcal{F}:K\times K\times K_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
is chosen to be

\mathcal{F}(u,v,w)=\int_{0}^{1}v(t)w(t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{1}u(t)w(t)\,dt.

Standard arguments (see, e.g.,
infante
) show that
T
is completely continuous from
X
to
X
. It follows immediately that the cone
K
is invariant under the operator
T
, i.e.,
T(K)\subset K
. Indeed, first note that since
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}>0
then for any
u\geq 0
we have
T(u)\geq 0
. Next, let
u\in K
and
\sigma\in\left[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\right].
Since (see, e.g.,
infante
),

k(t,s)\geq\frac{1}{4}\Phi(s)\,\,\,\text{ for all }t\in\left[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\right]\,\,\text{and}\,\,s\in[0,1],

and

k(t,s)\leq\Phi(s)\,\,\,\text{ for all }\,t,s\in[0,1],

where
\Phi(s)=s(1-s)
, we have

\displaystyle T(u)(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}k(\sigma,s)f(u(s))ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(s)f(u(s))ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}k(t,s)f(u(s))ds

\displaystyle=T(u)(t),

for all
t\in[0,1]
. Thus,
T(u)\in K
.

Next, in order to provide an explicit expression for the radial energy functional
\mathcal{E}
, we introduce the following notation

\alpha_{u}(k)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}k(t,s)\,u(s)^{k}\,u(t)\,ds\,dt,

for all
u\in K
and
k\geq 0
.
Let
v\in K_{1}
and
\sigma>0
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(T(\sigma v),\sigma v,v)

\displaystyle=\sigma|v|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{0}^{1}T(\sigma v)(t)\,v(t)\,dt

(25)

\displaystyle=\sigma|v|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}k(t,s)f(\sigma v(s))\,ds\right)v(t)\,dt.

\displaystyle=-a_{2}\sigma^{2}\alpha_{v}(2)+\sigma\left(|v|_{L^{2}}^{2}-a_{1}\alpha_{v}(1)\right)-a_{0}\alpha_{v}(0).

Therefore, from (
25
), one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(v)(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}(T(\sigma v),\sigma v,v)d\sigma

(26)

\displaystyle=-a_{2}\frac{t^{3}}{3}\alpha_{v}(2)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\left(|v|_{L^{2}}^{2}-a_{1}\alpha_{v}(1)\right)-a_{0}\alpha_{v}(0)t.

(27)

In what follows, we show that conditions (h1)-(h3) of Theorem
4
are satisfied in the entire cone
K
, that is,

r=0\quad\text{ and }\quad R=+\infty.

Check of (h1).
Let
v\in K_{1}.

Then, one has

\displaystyle|v|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}v(s)^{2}ds\geq\int_{1/4}^{3/4}v(s)^{2}ds\geq\int_{1/4}^{3/4}\left(\frac{1}{4}|v|_{\infty}\right)^{2}ds=\frac{1}{32},

\displaystyle|v|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}v(s)^{2}ds\leq|v|_{\infty}=1,

\displaystyle\alpha_{v}(k)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}k(t,s)\,v(s)^{k}\,v(t)\,ds\,dt\leq|v|_{\infty}^{k+1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}k(t,s)ds\,dt=\frac{1}{12},

\displaystyle\alpha_{v}(k)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}k(t,s)\,v(s)^{k}\,v(t)\,ds\,dt\geq\frac{1}{4^{k+1}}|u|_{\infty}^{k+1}\int_{1/4}^{3/4}\int_{1/4}^{3/4}k(t,s)ds\,dt=\frac{1}{32}\frac{1}{4^{k+1}}.

From these, we can deduce the subsequent robust estimates

\displaystyle a_{2}\alpha_{v}(2)\in\left[4.88\times 10^{-6},\,8.34\times 10^{-4}\right],

(28)

\displaystyle|v|_{L^{2}}^{2}-a_{1}\alpha_{v}(1)\in\left[3\times 10^{-2},\,1\right],

\displaystyle a_{0}\alpha_{v}(0)\in\left[7.81\times 10^{-3},\,8.34\times 10^{-2}\right].

Our next goal is to show that, for any choice of the parameters

\displaystyle b_{2}\in\left[4.88\times 10^{-6},\,8.34\times 10^{-4}\right],\,\,b_{1}\in\left[3\times 10^{-2},\,1\right],

(29)

\displaystyle b_{0}\in\left[7.81\times 10^{-3},\,8.34\times 10^{-2}\right],

the cubic function

g(t):=-\,b_{2}\frac{t^{3}}{3}+b_{1}\frac{t^{2}}{2}-b_{0}t

admits exactly one maximum point on the positive semi-axis (
t>0
).
To prove this, we look for the critical points of
g
. Differentiating gives

g^{\prime}(t)=-b_{2}t^{2}+b_{1}t-b_{0},

so critical points are solutions of the quadratic equation

0=-b_{2}t^{2}+b_{1}t-b_{0}.

The discriminant satisfies

b_{1}^{2}-4b_{2}b_{0}\geq 9\times 10^{-4}-2.79\times 10^{-4}>4\cdot 10^{-4}>0,

(30)

hence there are two distinct positive roots,

t_{\pm}=\frac{b_{1}\pm\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}-4b_{2}b_{0}}}{2b_{2}}>0.

Notice that by (
29
) and (
30
), we have

t_{+}=\frac{b_{1}+\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}-4b_{2}b_{0}}}{2b_{2}}>\frac{3\times 10^{-2}}{1.7\times 10^{-3}}>10,

(31)

and

t_{+}=\frac{b_{1}+\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}-4b_{2}b_{0}}}{2b_{2}}<\frac{1}{b_{2}}<10^{7}.

(32)

One clearly has,

g(0)=0,\quad g(t)<0\ \text{for sufficiently small }t>0,\quad\text{and}\qquad g(t)\to-\infty\ \text{as }t\to\infty.

Hence, if

g(t_{+})>0\quad\text{and}\quad g(t_{-})<0,

(33)

then
t_{+}
is the unique global maximizer, while
t_{-}
corresponds to a local minimizer.

To verify (
33
), observe that since

-b_{2}t_{\pm}^{2}+b_{1}t_{\pm}-b_{0}=0,

we have

\displaystyle g(t_{\pm})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}t_{\pm}\left(-b_{2}t_{\pm}^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}b_{1}t_{\pm}-3b_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}t_{\pm}\left(-b_{2}t_{\pm}^{2}+b_{1}t_{\pm}-b_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2}b_{1}t_{\pm}-2b_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}t_{\pm}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}b_{1}t_{\pm}-2b_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{6}t_{\pm}\left(b_{1}t_{\pm}-4b_{0}\right).

Therefore, establishing
g(t_{+})>0
and
g(t_{-})<0
reduces to proving

b_{1}t_{+}-4b_{0}>0\quad\text{and}\quad b_{1}t_{-}-4b_{0}<0,

respectively. The first inequality is immediate, since by
(
30
), we have

\displaystyle b_{1}t_{+}-4b_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2b_{2}}\Big(b_{1}^{2}+b_{1}\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}-4b_{2}b_{0}}-8b_{0}b_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2b_{2}}\Big(9\times 10^{-4}+610^{-4}-8\times 8.34^{2}\times 10^{-6}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2b_{2}}\Big(15\times 10^{-4}-5.57\times 10^{-4}\Big)>0.

For the second one,
denote
\gamma=4b_{0}b_{2}.

Then,

\displaystyle b_{1}t_{-}-4b_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2b_{2}}\left(b_{1}^{2}-b_{1}\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}-\gamma}-2\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2b_{2}}\left(b_{1}^{2}-\gamma-b_{1}\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}-\gamma}-\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2b_{2}}\left(\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}-\gamma}\left(\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}-\gamma}-b_{1}\right)-\gamma\right)

\displaystyle<0,

where the latter inequality follows since
\gamma>0
and
\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}-\gamma}-b_{1}<0
. Hence,
g(t_{-})<0,
as desired.

From the above results and given the uniform estimates in (
28
) (which are independent of
v\in K_{1}
), we deduce that for each
v\in K_{1}
, the mapping

t\mapsto\mathcal{E}(v)(t),

has exactly one maximum point on the positive semi-axis, that is
t_{v}=t_{+}
, which by (
31
) and (
32
) is bounded away from both zero and infinity, more exactly,

r_{0}=10
and
R_{0}=10^{7}.

Hence, condition (h1) is verified.

Check of (h2)
. Note that for any nonnegative function
u\in C[0,1]
, we have

|T(u)|_{\infty}\geq\max_{t\in[0,1]}a_{0}\int_{0}^{1}k(t,s)\,ds=a_{0}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\frac{t(1-t)}{2}=\frac{a_{0}}{4}.

Since every
u\in U_{b}
is nonnegative, it follows that

\inf_{u\in U_{b}}|T(u)|_{\infty}\geq\frac{a_{0}}{4}>0,

and therefore condition (h2) is satisfied.

Check of (h3)
. Assume that

\mathcal{F}\left(tu,u,\frac{u}{|u|_{\infty}}\right)=0,

for some
t>0
and
u\in K\setminus\{0\}.
Then,

\frac{1}{|u|_{\infty}}\left(|u|^{2}_{L^{2}}-t|u|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right)=0,

which yields
t=1
, so (h3) holds.

Consequently, applying Theorem
4
, we deduce that
T
has a fixed point in
U_{b}
.

Bounds on Deep Neural Network Partial Derivatives with Respect to Parameters
Omkar Sudhir Patil, Brandon C. Fallin, Cristian F. Nino, Rebecca G. Hart, Warren E. Dixon

Abstract
Deep neural networks (DNNs) have emerged as a powerful tool with a growing body of literature exploring Lyapunov-based approaches for real-time system identification and control. These methods depend on establishing bounds for the second partial derivatives of DNNs with respect to their parameters, a requirement often assumed but rarely addressed explicitly. This paper provides rigorous mathematical formulations of polynomial bounds on both the first and second partial derivatives of DNNs with respect to their parameters. We present lemmas that characterize these bounds for fully-connected DNNs, while accommodating various classes of activation function including sigmoidal and ReLU-like functions. Our analysis yields closed-form expressions that enable precise stability guarantees for Lyapunov-based deep neural networks (Lb-DNNs). Furthermore, we extend our results to bound the higher-order terms in first-order Taylor approximations of DNNs, providing important tools for convergence analysis in gradient-based learning algorithms. The resulting framework replaces previously assumed bounds with explicit, computable expressions, thereby strengthening the mathematical foundation of neural network applications in safety-critical control systems.

An important component of these approaches involves Lyapunov-based

for the second partial derivatives of deep neural networks (DNNs)
with respect to their parameters. Despite the theoretical significance
of these bounds, their explicit mathematical formulation has remained
largely unaddressed in the literature.

partial derivatives of fully-connected DNNs with respect to their
parameters. These results provide the theoretical foundation necessary
for guaranteeing stability in Lyapunov-based deep learning control

expressions.

deep learning results. While our analysis focuses on this standard
fully-connected feedforward DNN architecture, we note that the methodologies
and results developed herein can be readily extended to accommodate

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{j}\left(y\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{1}\left\|y\right\|+\mathfrak{a}_{0},

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{j}^{\prime}\left(y\right)\right\|

\mathcal{Q}_{j}
,

\mathcal{T}_{w,j}\triangleq\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-j+1}\left(\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right),

and

\mathcal{R}_{w,q,j}\triangleq\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\bigg(\sum_{h=1}^{w-q}\mathfrak{c}_{0}\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{2w-q-j-h-1}\bigg(\prod_{l=q+1}^{w-h}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\bigg)\bigg)\\
&\quad\bigg(\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\bigg),\end{aligned}&j\leq q\leq w,\\
\mathcal{R}_{w,j,q},&q\leq j\leq w,\end{cases}

for all
j,q\leq w\leq k
, implying that
\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}

is bounded by a quadratic polynomial in terms of
\left\|\sigma_{a}\right\|
.
Furthermore, consider the bound
\left\|V_{j}\right\|\leq\bar{\theta}

for all
j\in\left\{0,\ldots,k\right\}
. Then, the terms
\mathcal{Q}_{j}
,

\mathcal{T}_{w,j}
, and
\mathcal{R}_{w,j,q}
reduce to

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{j}\bar{\theta}^{j}\left\|\sigma_{a}\right\|+\mathfrak{a}_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\left(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{j-i}\bar{\theta}^{j-i}\right)+\mathfrak{a}_{0},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{w,j}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-j+1}\bar{\theta}^{w-j},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{w,q,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{w-q}\mathfrak{c}_{0}\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{2w-q-j-h-1}\bar{\theta}^{2w-h-q-j}.

Furthermore, if the activation functions are uniformly bounded by
constants, i.e.,
\mathfrak{a}_{1}=0
in Assumption
1
,
then
\mathcal{Q}_{j}
reduces to
\mathcal{Q}_{0}=\left\|\sigma_{a}\right\|

and
\mathcal{Q}_{j}=\mathfrak{a}_{0}
for all
j\geq 1
.

Proof.

For the ease of subsequent exposition, we consider scalar outputs
for
\Phi_{w}
. This reduction does not compromise generality because
the bounds apply element-wise for the vector case. The second partial
derivative term
\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}

is expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}V_{w}^{\top}\varphi_{w}\right)

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}\left(\overset{\curvearrowleft}{\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}}V_{l}^{\top}\phi_{l}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{l-1}\right)\right)\\
&\quad\left(I_{L_{j+1}}\otimes\varphi_{j}^{\top}\right),\end{aligned}&w\geq j,\\
0,&j>w.\end{cases}

Due to the symmetry of Hessian matrices, it follows that
\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}=\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}
.
Without loss of generality, consider the case
w\geq q\geq j
. Such
consideration does not affect generality because, if
q<j
, then
the analysis can be performed by exchanging
q
and
j
. Differentiating
the product chain
\overset{\curvearrowleft}{\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}}V_{l}^{\top}\phi_{l}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{l-1}\right)

with respect to
\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)
produces
w-q+1

nonzero terms by the product rule. For each
l\in\left\{q+1,\ldots,w\right\}
,
differentiating
\phi_{l}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{l-1}\right)
via the
chain rule contributes one term containing
\phi_{l}^{\prime\prime}\left(\Phi_{l-1}\right)\frac{\partial\Phi_{l-1}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}
,
and one additional term arises from differentiating the factor
V_{q}^{\top}

directly. The factors
\phi_{l}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{l-1}\right)

for
l\in\left\{j+1,\ldots,q\right\}
contribute zero because
\Phi_{l-1}

does not depend on
V_{q}
when
l-1<q
. Applying
the product rule and chain rule to (
16
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{w}^{\top}\left(\phi_{w}^{\prime\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-1}\right)\frac{\partial\Phi_{w-1}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle V_{w-1}^{\top}\phi_{w-1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-2}\right)\ldots

\displaystyle V_{j+1}^{\top}\phi_{j+1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\left(I_{L_{j+1}}\otimes\varphi_{j}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle+V_{w}^{\top}\phi_{w}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-1}\right)V_{w-1}^{\top}\left(\phi_{w-1}^{\prime\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-2}\right)\frac{\partial\Phi_{w-2}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle V_{w-2}^{\top}\phi_{w-2}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-3}\right)\ldots

\displaystyle V_{j+1}^{\top}\phi_{j+1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\left(I_{L_{j+1}}\otimes\varphi_{j}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle+V_{w}^{\top}\phi_{w}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-1}\right)\ldots V_{q+1}^{\top}\left(\phi_{q+1}^{\prime\prime}\left(\Phi_{q}\right)\frac{\partial\Phi_{q}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle V_{q}^{\top}\phi_{q}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{q-1}\right)\ldots

\displaystyle V_{j+1}^{\top}\phi_{j+1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\left(I_{L_{j+1}}\otimes\varphi_{j}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle+V_{w}^{\top}\phi_{w}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-1}\right)\ldots\left(I_{L_{q+1}}\otimes\phi_{q}^{\prime\top}\left(\Phi_{q-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\ldots V_{j+1}^{\top}\phi_{j+1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\left(I_{L_{j+1}}\otimes\varphi_{j}^{\top}\right).

Taking norms and applying the triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|V_{w}\right\|\left\|\phi_{w}^{\prime\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-1}\right)\right\|\left\|\frac{\partial\Phi_{w-1}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}\right\|

(17)

\displaystyle\left\|V_{w-1}\right\|\left\|\phi_{w-1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-2}\right)\right\|\ldots

\displaystyle\left\|V_{j+1}\right\|\left\|\phi_{j+1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\right\|\left\|\varphi_{j}\right\|

\displaystyle+\left\|V_{w}\right\|\left\|\phi_{w}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-1}\right)\right\|\left\|V_{w-1}\right\|

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{w-1}^{\prime\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-2}\right)\right\|\left\|\frac{\partial\Phi_{w-2}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}\right\|

\displaystyle\left\|V_{w-2}\right\|\left\|\phi_{w-2}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-3}\right)\right\|\ldots

\displaystyle\left\|V_{j+1}\right\|\left\|\phi_{j+1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\right\|\left\|\varphi_{j}\right\|

\displaystyle+\left\|V_{w}\right\|\left\|\phi_{w}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-1}\right)\right\|\ldots\left\|V_{q+1}\right\|

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{q+1}^{\prime\prime}\left(\Phi_{q}\right)\right\|\left\|\frac{\partial\Phi_{q}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}\right\|

\displaystyle\left\|V_{q}\right\|\left\|\phi_{q}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{q-1}\right)\right\|\ldots

\displaystyle\left\|V_{j+1}\right\|\left\|\phi_{j+1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\right\|\left\|\varphi_{j}\right\|

\displaystyle+\left\|V_{w}\right\|\left\|\phi_{w}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{w-1}\right)\right\|\ldots\left\|\phi_{q}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{q-1}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\ldots\left\|V_{j+1}\right\|\left\|\phi_{j+1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\right\|\left\|\varphi_{j}\right\|.

We express the bounds from Lemma
2
using
the definition of
\mathcal{Q}_{j}
as

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-j}\left(\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{j},

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-q}\left(\prod_{l=q+1}^{w}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{q}.

Applying these bounds to (
17
) yields

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{0}\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-j-1}\left(\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-q-1}\left(\prod_{l=q+1}^{w-1}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{q}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{c}_{0}\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-j-1}\left(\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-q-2}\left(\prod_{l=q+1}^{w-2}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{q}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{c}_{0}\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-j-1}\left(\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{0}\left(\prod_{l=q+1}^{q}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{q}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-j+1}\left(\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{j}.

Factoring the common term
\left(\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{j}

out of every line and combining the
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
exponents

\left(w-j-1\right)+\left(w-q-h\right)=2w-q-j-h-1
in the
h
-th line of the sum yields

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{l=j+1}^{w}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\right)\mathcal{Q}_{j}\bigg[\sum_{h=1}^{w-q}\mathfrak{c}_{0}\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{2w-q-j-h-1}

\displaystyle\quad\bigg(\prod_{l=q+1}^{w-h}\left\|V_{l}\right\|\bigg)\mathcal{Q}_{q}+\mathfrak{b}_{0}^{w-j+1}\bigg].

Identifying the bracketed expression with the definitions of
\mathcal{R}_{w,q,j}

and
\mathcal{T}_{w,j}
gives

\text{$\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}\right\|$}\leq\mathcal{R}_{w,q,j}\mathcal{Q}_{j}\mathcal{Q}_{q}+\mathcal{T}_{w,j}\mathcal{Q}_{j}.

Because the terms
\mathcal{Q}_{j}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{q}
are linear
in
\left\|\sigma_{a}\right\|
, it follows that the bound
on
\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{w}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}\right\|

is quadratic in
\left\|\sigma_{a}\right\|
.
∎

4
Bounds on Higher-Order Terms in First-Order Taylor Series Approximation

The bounds obtained in the previous section can be used to compute
analytical bounds on the first-order Taylor series approximation of

\text{$\theta$}\mapsto\Phi\left(\sigma,\theta\right)
.
To this end, consider a compact convex set of admissible DNN parameters
given by
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Additionally, consider any
arbitrary elements
\theta^{*},\hat{\theta}\in\Theta
, and let
\tilde{\theta}\triangleq\theta^{*}-\hat{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

denote their difference. Furthermore, let
\Phi^{(i)}
denote the

i^{th}
element of
\Phi
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, where

n=L_{\textrm{out}}
. Then performing
a first-order application of Taylor’s theorem
[
11
, Theorem 4.7]

element-wise to
\Phi
yields

\Phi(\sigma,\theta^{*})-\Phi\left(\sigma,\hat{\theta}\right)=\frac{\partial\Phi\left(\sigma,\hat{\theta}\right)}{\partial\hat{\theta}}\tilde{\theta}+R\left(\sigma,\tilde{\theta}\right),

(18)

where
R:\mathbb{R}^{L_{\textrm{in}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes
the Lagrange remainder term given by

\displaystyle R\left(\sigma,\tilde{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg[\tilde{\theta}^{\top}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{(1)}}{\partial\hat{\theta}^{2}}\left(\sigma,\theta^{*}+\varpi_{1}\left(\hat{\theta}\right)\tilde{\theta}\right)\tilde{\theta},

\displaystyle\ldots,\tilde{\theta}^{\top}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{(n)}}{\partial\hat{\theta}^{2}}\left(\sigma,\theta^{*}+\varpi_{n}\left(\hat{\theta}\right)\tilde{\theta}\right)\tilde{\theta}\bigg]^{\top},

where
\varpi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\left[0,1\right]
denote unknown
functions parameterizing a convex combination of
\theta^{*}
and

\hat{\theta}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The following theorem
provides a polynomial bound on the Lagrange remainder term.

Theorem 1
.

There exists a polynomial function

\rho_{0}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
of the form
\rho_{0}\left(\left\|\sigma\right\|\right)=a_{2}\left\|\sigma\right\|^{2}+a_{1}\left\|\sigma\right\|^{1}+a_{0}

with constants
a_{2},a_{1},a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that the
Lagrange remainder term is bounded as
\left\|R\left(\sigma,\tilde{\theta}\right)\right\|\leq\rho_{0}\left(\left\|\sigma\right\|\right)\left\|\tilde{\theta}\right\|^{2}
.

Proof.

Using the triangle inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|R\left(\sigma,\tilde{\theta}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|\tilde{\theta}\right\|^{2}}{2}\bigg(\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{(1)}}{\partial\hat{\theta}^{2}}\left(\sigma,\theta^{*}+\varpi_{1}\left(\hat{\theta}\right)\tilde{\theta}\right)\right\|

(19)

\displaystyle+\ldots+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{(n)}}{\partial\hat{\theta}^{2}}\left(\sigma,\theta^{*}+\varpi_{n}\left(\hat{\theta}\right)\tilde{\theta}\right)\right\|\bigg).

Due to the facts that
\Theta
is a convex set,
\varpi_{i}\left(\hat{\theta}\right)\in\left[0,1\right]
,
and
\theta^{*},\hat{\theta}\in\Theta
, it follows that
\theta^{*}+\varpi_{i}\left(\hat{\theta}\right)\tilde{\theta}\in\Theta

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\hat{\theta}\in\Theta
. Because

\varpi_{i}
is unknown, the term
\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{(i)}}{\partial\hat{\theta}^{2}}\left(\sigma,\theta^{*}+\varpi_{i}\left(\hat{\theta}\right)\tilde{\theta}\right)\right\|

is bounded in the following analysis considering the worst case bound
in which
\left\|\theta^{*}+\varpi_{i}\left(\hat{\theta}\right)\tilde{\theta}\right\|\leq\bar{\theta}

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. To this end, a bound is developed on

\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{(i)}}{\partial\hat{\theta}^{2}}\left(\sigma,\theta\right)

considering an arbitrary
\left\|\theta\right\|\leq\bar{\theta}
.
The Hessian matrix
\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{(i)}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\left(\sigma,\theta\right)

is comprised of blocks of the form
\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{(i)}\left(\sigma,\theta\right)}{\partial\mathrm{vec}(V_{q})\partial\mathrm{vec}(V_{j})}

for all
q,j\in\left\{0,\ldots,k\right\}
and
i\in\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
.
Writing the Hessian as the sum of its
\left(q,j\right)
-blocks (each
embedded at the appropriate position with zeros elsewhere) and applying
the triangle inequality bounds the spectral norm by the sum of block
norms. Each block satisfies
\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{(i)}}{\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{q}\right)\partial\mathrm{vec}\left(V_{j}\right)}\right\|\leq\mathcal{R}_{k,q,j}\mathcal{Q}_{j}\mathcal{Q}_{q}+\mathcal{T}_{k,j}\mathcal{Q}_{j}

by Lemma
3
with
w=k
(since
\Phi=\Phi_{k}
).
Therefore, using (
19
), the Lagrange
remainder can be bounded as

\left\|R\left(\sigma,\tilde{\theta}\right)\right\|\leq\frac{\left\|\tilde{\theta}\right\|^{2}}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{q=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\mathcal{R}_{k,q,j}\mathcal{Q}_{j}\mathcal{Q}_{q}+\mathcal{T}_{k,j}\mathcal{Q}_{j}\right)\right),

(20)

where the terms
\mathcal{Q}_{j},\mathcal{Q}_{q},\mathcal{T}_{k,j},

and
\mathcal{R}_{k,q,j}
are defined in Lemma
3
.
As a result, defining the polynomial function
\rho_{0}
as

\rho_{0}\left(\left\|\sigma\right\|\right)\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{q=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\mathcal{R}_{k,q,j}\mathcal{Q}_{j}\mathcal{Q}_{q}+\mathcal{T}_{k,j}\mathcal{Q}_{j}\right)

(21)

yields
\left\|R\left(\sigma,\tilde{\theta}\right)\right\|\leq\rho_{0}\left(\left\|\sigma\right\|\right)\left\|\tilde{\theta}\right\|^{2}
.
Because Lemma
3
provides quadratic polynomial
bounds on such terms with respect to the input, it follows that
\rho_{0}

is a quadratic function of the form
\rho_{0}\left(\left\|\sigma\right\|\right)=a_{2}\left\|\sigma\right\|^{2}+a_{1}\left\|\sigma\right\|^{1}+a_{0}

with constants
a_{2},a_{1},a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. We note that
the explicit values of
a_{2},a_{1},a_{0}
can be computed by expanding
the polynomial formulation in (
21
).
∎

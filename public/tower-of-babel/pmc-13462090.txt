m6A modification-mediated lncRNA UNC5B-AS1 enhances HCC progression through miR-760/IL6 axis
Authors: Li-ping Yu, Sheng-yu Huang, Kun-peng Li, Jun-sheng Ni, Zhi-ping Huang, Zhou-feng Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWT1-associated protein (WTAP)-catalyzed m6A methylation drives oncogenic processes in HCC. Long noncoding RNA UNC5B-AS1 are associated with various types of cancer including hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Previous study indicated that UNC5B-AS1 promotes HCC progression. However, the precise roles of WTAP/UNC5B-AS1 axis in HCC progression remain unclear. In this study, we found that the m6A modification sites are highly abundant on UNC5B-AS1, and methylase WTAP enhances UNC5B-AS1 stability through m6A reader-YTHDF1 and up-regulates its expression in HCC. Functional tests showed that WTAP/UNC5B-AS1 axis drives HCC progression. Mechanically, UNC5B-AS1 acts as a molecular sponge to hsa-miR-760, to up-regulate the expression of Interleukin-6 (IL6), and plays a role in enhancing HCC progression. Our study reveals a mechanism by which m6A-RNA regulates oncogene activity, which might result in the creation of a therapeutic strategy for the treatment of HCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54523-y.Keywords: WTAP, UNC5B-AS1, N6-methyladenosine, IL6, Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)Subject terms: Cancer, Molecular biology, Oncology

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the sixth most common human malignancy worldwide1,2. Despite substantial progress in diagnostic and treatment methodologies over the past decades, the long-term survival rate of HCC patients continues to be low, primarily due to its rapid metastasis and high recurrence rates3. Oncogene signaling pathway plays a crucial role in liver inflammatory responses by controlling the expression of an array of growth factors and cytokines4. One of these cytokines is IL-6, which is best known for its role in the liver acute phase response5. IL-6 exerts many of its functions via activation of STAT3, a transcription factor found to be important for HCC development6. Therefore, identifying alternative therapies and elucidating the underlying mechanisms of the regulatory mechanism of IL6 abnormal expression are crucial in this context.

N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification is the most abundant post-transcriptional modification across multiple RNA species, including non-coding RNA (ncRNA)7–9. In malignant tumors, m6A methyltransferases mediate the methylation of oncogene or tumor suppressor RNA transcripts, which are subsequently recognized by specific m6A reader proteins, resulting in the modulation—either upregulation or downregulation—of oncogene and tumor suppressor gene expression10–12. m6A modification is widely observed in GC, and its dynamically regulated processes have been found to be strongly associated with the expression levels of genes13. Recent studies have shown that several m6A writers and erasers are involved in HCC progression10–14. Accumulating evidence identifies Wilms tumor 1-associated protein (WTAP) as a significant modulator of diverse pathological conditions, particularly in cancer12–14. However, the precise role of WTAP in regulating long ncRNAs (lncRNAs) to promote HCC progression remains largely unclear.

LncRNAs constitute a diverse group of RNA molecules (> 200 nt) that are transcribed but are not translated into proteins15. LncRNAs appear to be extensively involved in cellular mechanisms governing carcinogenesis and metastatic progression16,17. The association between lncRNAs and HCC development has been established based on multiple studies. UNC5B-AS1 is a novel oncogenic lncRNA that has been implicated in multiple malignancies, including in HCC18–23. Nevertheless, the precise biological function and specific molecular targets of this lncRNA in HCC progression have not yet been fully elucidated. This study was designed to investigate the role of UNC5B-AS1 in HCC elucidate the underlying mechanisms through which it may contribute to tumor development. We expect that our findings may offer a potential biomarker and could serve as a therapeutic target for hepatocellular carcinoma.

Materials and methods

Patients and HCC samples

A cohort of 31 patients diagnosed with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who underwent curative resection at the Third Affiliated Hospital, Naval Medical University, was enrolled. Matched tumor and adjacent non-tumor specimens were collected and processed for RT-PCR and meRIP-PCR analysis. The study received approval from the Institutional Review Board of the Third Affiliated Hospital, Naval Medical University (Shanghai, China) and was conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in 2013). All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment.

Cell culture

Human HCC cell lines HCCLM3 and Huh7 were obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (China) and cultured in an incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ in DMEM (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Invitrogen, USA).

RNA half-life assay

To assess RNA stability, cells were treated with actinomycin D (5 µg/mL; Merck-Millipore, Germany), a transcriptional inhibitor that blocks de novo RNA synthesis. Cells were collected at the designated time points (0, 12, 24, 36, and 48 h) post-treatment, followed by total RNA isolation and RT-qPCR analysis.

Xiantao academic tools

Xiantao Academic (https://www.xiantao.love/) was applied for computational analyses and visualization. Modules used included Clinical Significance, Pan-Cancer Analysis, and Functional Clustering. The dataset comprised TCGA HCC RNA-seq data (STAR alignment, TPM format). Samples lacking clinical data and normal controls were excluded. A Log₂ transformation (value + 1) was applied before evaluation of cancer characteristics, molecular correlation, immune infiltration, prognosis, and diagnostic potential.

Subcellular fractionation

The Minute™ Cytoplasmic and Nuclear Extraction Kit (SC-003, Invent, USA) was employed to purify cytoplasmic and nuclear RNA fractions.cDNA was synthesized from RNA, followed by qRT-PCR to determine the expression of UNC5B-AS1, ACTIN, and U6. ACTIN for cytoplasmic and U6 for nuclear fractions served as internal controls.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 6.0 (USA) and SPSS 20.0 (SPSS Inc., USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages). Differences between two groups were analyzed by using the student’s t-test. Comparison between multiple groups was done using one-way ANOVA test followed by post hoc test (least significant difference). Survival was assessed by the Kaplan–Meier method and log-rank test. P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Diagnostic potentials were assessed by depicting receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves.

Additional experimental details are provided in the Supplementary Information.

Results

UNC5B-AS1 is up-regulated in HCC

To identify UNC5B-AS1 expression level in HCC, we analyzed the expression of UNC5B-AS1 in TCGA sequencing data (https://www.xiantaozi.com), the result showed that UNC5B-AS1 was significantly overexpressed in multiple malignant tumors, including HCC (Fig. 1A-B). Moreover, UNC5B-AS1 expression progressively increased with advancing tumor grade, as shown by comparisons across pathologic T stages (T1-2 vs. T3–T4) (Fig. 1C). ROC curve analysis demonstrated a diagnostic value for UNC5B-AS1 in HCC, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.700 (Fig. 1D). Notably, UNC5B-AS1 high expression patients exhibit a poorer prognosis compared to low expression levels (Fig. 1E). Meanwhile, UNC5B-AS1 high expression also exhibited poor outcome in HCC patients with pathologic N0 stage (Fig. 1F). Collectively, these findings indicate that UNC5B-AS1 is overexpressed in HCC and could function as a novel diagnostic biomarker.

WTAP upregulates UNC5B-AS1 expression in HCC in an m6A-dependent manner

Given the reported importance of m6A modification in HCC progression10, the relationship between m6A regulators and UNC5B-AS1 expression was investigated. TCGA data revealed positive correlations between UNC5B-AS1 and the m6A “writers” (METTL3 and WTAP) as well as “erasers” (ALKBH5) (Fig. 2A–C). Among these, knockdown of WTAP significantly reduced UNC5B-AS1 expression in HCC cell lines (Fig.S1A-B). Following, MeRIP-qPCR confirmed that WTAP knockdown markedly decreased UNC5B-AS1 m6A enrichment in both HCC cells (Fig.S1C). Using the SRAMP algorithm, 2 putative m6A sites were identified within the UNC5B-AS1transcript (Fig. 2D). MeRIP-qPCR analysis demonstrated that m6A enrichment of above m6A sites markedly decreased in both HCC cells under WTAP knockdown (Fig. 2E). RIP-qPCR analysis further demonstrated that WTAP exhibited the strongest binding affinity for UNC5B-AS1 in HCC cells (Fig. 2F). To further elucidate the effect of m6A modification on UNC5B-AS1transcript, we performed luciferase reporter and mutagenesis assays. As expected, WTAP knockdown decreased the luciferase activity in WT group but not in MUT group (Fig. 2G). Next, we analyzed the expression of WTAP in TCGA sequencing data (https://www.xiantaozi.com), the result showed that WTAP was significantly overexpressed in multiple malignant tumors, including HCC (Fig. 2H, I). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis demonstrated a diagnostic value for WTAP in HCC, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.749 (Fig. 2J). Importantly, WTAP high expression patients exhibit worse clinical outcome compared to the patients with lower WTAP expression (Fig. 2K). Together, these findings indicate that WTAP increases UNC5B-AS1expression via m6A modification.

YTHDF1 stabilizes UNC5B-AS1 RNA in HCC

Emerging evidence indicates that m6A readers are indispensable for orchestrating the functional consequences of m6A methylation24. Analysis of TCGA sequencing data revealed that multiple m6A readers were positively correlated with expression of UNC5B-AS1in HCC tissues (Fig. 3A–C). Functionally, knockdown of YTHDF1 significantly decreased UNC5B-AS1 expression in HCC cell lines (Fig. 3D, E). RIP-qPCR analysis further demonstrated that YTHDF1 exhibited the strongest binding affinity for UNC5B-AS1 in HCC cells (Fig. 3F). Moreover, treatment with actinomycin D (10 µg/mL) to inhibit RNA polymerase elongation revealed that the decay rate of UNC5B-AS1 was increased upon YTHDF1 silencing (Fig. 3G).

Clinical analysis of YTHDF1 expression using TCGA sequencing data (https://www.xiantaozi.com) demonstrated its significant overexpression in HCC and other malignant tumors (Fig. 3H, I). ROC curve analysis demonstrated a diagnostic value for YTHDF1 in HCC, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.749 (Fig. 3J). Importantly, patients with elevated YTHDF1 levels exhibited worse prognosis compared to those with reduced expression (Fig. 3K). Collectively, these findings indicate that WTAP promotes UNC5B-AS1 expression via m6A-dependent recruitment of YTHDF1 to stabilize UNC5B-AS1 transcripts in HCC cells.

WTAP/UNC5B-AS1 axis enhanced HCC progression

Subsequently, we investigated whether WTAP promotes HCC progression by elevating UNC5B-AS1 expression. Knockdown of WTAP significantly suppressed proliferation, migration, and invasion in HCC cells (Fig. 4A-C). Simultaneously, elevated WTAP expression was found to markedly promote proliferative, migratory, and invasive capacities in HCC cells (Fig. 4D-F & Fig.S2A). Furthermore, elevating UNC5B-AS1 expression reversed the inhibited proliferative, migratory, and invasive phenotypes caused by WTAP knockdown in HCC cells (Fig. 4A-C & Fig.S2B). The knockdown of UNC5B-AS1 impaired the effect of enhanced HCC migration and invasion ability caused by WTAP overexpression (Fig. 4D-F&Fig.S2C). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the WTAP/UNC5B-AS1 axis is critical for promoting HCC progression.

UNC5B-AS1 promotes HCC progression via IL6

We next explored the downstream mechanisms by which UNC5B-AS1 promotes proliferation and metastasis in HCC. IL-6 directly targets tumour cells to upregulate STAT3 target genes encoding proteins that drive tumour progression25. Correlation analysis of TCGA data showed IL6 positively associating with UNC5B-AS1 in HCC(Fig. 5A). Meanwhile, UNC5B-AS1 knockdown reduced IL6 mRNA levels as well as IL6 levels in the supernatant (Fig. 5B, C). Finally, we evaluated whether UNC5B-AS1 promotes HCC progression via the IL6. Functional rescue assays, including Transwell and CCK-8 assays, showed that UNC5B-AS1 overexpression significantly promoted proliferation, migration, and invasion in HCC cells. Importantly, these inhibitory effects were reversed by co-transfection with IL6 knocked down (Fig. 5D–G). These data demonstrate that UNC5B-AS1 drives HCC oncogenesis through IL6 pathway activation.

UNC5B-AS1 acts as the molecular sponge of miR-760 to regulate IL6 in HCC cells

To elucidate how UNC5B-AS1 up-regulates IL6, its subcellular localization was first examined. Nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation revealed that UNC5B-AS1 was predominantly localized in the cytoplasm of HCC cells, suggesting a posttranscriptional regulatory mechanism (Fig. 6A). Bioinformatic prediction using the miRanda database (https://mirdb.org/) identified 11 candidate miRNAs potentially interacting with both UNC5B-AS1and IL6 (Fig. 6B). RNA pull-down assays demonstrated that miR-760 was most strongly enriched by biotin-labeled UNC5B-AS1(Fig. 6C). Sequence analysis revealed shared binding sites for miR-760 within UNC5B-AS1(Fig. 6D) and the IL6 3′UTR (Fig. 6G). Before performing dual-luciferase reporter (DLR) assays, we overexpressed miR-760 using miR-760 mimics (Fig. 6E). DLR assays confirmed that miR-760 mimics significantly reduced luciferase activity of UNC5B-AS1-WT constructs containing the wild-type binding sequences for miR-760 but not mutant constructs (Fig. 6F) and further proved the binding between IL6 and miR-760 (Fig. 6H). Functionally, miR-760 mimics suppressed IL6 mRNA expression in HCC cells (Fig. 6I).

Discussion

HCC has a complex pathogenesis and often lacks obvious early symptoms. Consequently, many patients present with advanced disease, at which point prognosis is poor and treatment options are limited20,21. Therefore, identifying diagnostic biomarkers for early-stage HCC and developing effective therapeutic targets remains an urgent clinical priority26. The development of high-throughput sequencing technologies has enabled the identification of a wide range of lncRNAs, and accumulating evidence suggests their essential roles in tumorigenesis and progression26. Although they lack protein-coding capacity, lncRNAs regulate gene expression through diverse mechanisms, including interactions with DNA, RNA, and proteins26–28. Cytoplasmic lncRNAs often act as ceRNAs, sequestering miRNAs and thereby modulating the downstream target gene expression28.

This study elucidates the m6A-mediated mechanisms and oncogenic role of UNC5B-AS1 in HCC progression, regulated by WTAP/YTHDF1.Our findings suggest that m6A-modified UNC5B-AS1 as a potential HCC therapeutic target.

m6A is a critical epigenetic regulator of lncRNA expression and stability29. In line with this, we found that UNC5B-AS1 harbors abundant predicted m6A binding sites. Using public databases, candidate m6A-associated proteins likely to interact with UNC5B-AS1 were identified, and results suggest that YTHDF1 and WTAP regulate its m6A methylation. WTAP functions as a canonical m6A “writer,” catalyzing RNA methylation by recognizing consensus motifs30. WT1-associated protein (WTAP), a methyltransferase regulatory subunit, has attracted growing interest due to its aberrant expression across numerous cancers, where it impacts tumor progression and patient outcomes31. Growing attention on WTAP is supported by studies that span multiple cancer types, including hepatocellular carcinoma, esophageal cancer, breast cancer, bladder cancer, lung cancer, and lymphoma32–34. YTHDF1, an m6A “reader,” stabilizes RNAs by binding to m6A motifs on target transcripts and contributes to the development of HCC35. In the present study, we propose that UNC5B-AS1 expression is regulated by RNA methylation, based on the observation that inhibition of the m6A methylase resulted in a decrease in UNC5B-AS1 levels, while knockdown of the m6A demethylase led to an increase in UNC5B-AS1 levels. Furthermore, we found that WTAP, but not other m6A methylases or demethylases, mediates the regulation of UNC5B-AS1 in HCC. Additionally, we identified YTHDF1 as a key reader responsible for stabilizing m6A-modified UNC5B-AS1. Consistent with their established oncogenic roles, our results demonstrate that WTAP and YTHDF1 upregulate UNC5B-AS1 in an m6A-dependent manner. These findings confirm that UNC5B-AS1 is an m6A-modified lncRNA with oncogenic potential. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate the involvement of m6A modification in UNC5B-AS1 dysregulation in HCC.

Mechanistically, the ceRNA is a regulatory process involving non-coding RNAs. Moreover, since UNC5B-AS1 is predominantly localized withinthe cytoplasmic compartment, our findings demonstrated that it acts as a ceRNA, sponging miR-760 to relieve the suppression of IL6 and thereby promoting HCC progression. The IL-6/JAK/STAT3 axis critically mediates oncogenesis in diverse human cancers36. Elevated IL-6 is commonly detected in patients with chronic inflammatory disorders (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease) and in cancer patients, including those with hematopoietic malignancies or solid tumors. In cancer progression, increased IL-6 levels hyperactivate the JAK/STAT3 signaling pathway, frequently correlating with unfavorable patient outcomes36. In this study, we demonstrated that UNC5B-AS1 indirectly upregulates IL6 through miR-760 sequestration, and promoting HCC progression.

There are still shortcomings in this study. In vivo validation of current study is absent. Further study needs to supplement xenograft models to confirm the WTAP/UNC5B-AS1 axis promotes tumor growth and metastasis in physiological contexts. Our study focuses primarily on IL-6 expression but does not adequately demonstrate activation of downstream signaling pathways. Assessment of downstream signaling pathways inhibition experiments would help establish IL-6 as a functional mediator. Supplement experiments to show STAT3 inhibition abrogates UNC5B-AS1-mediated HCC progression should be performed in further. Most functional assays related to the study appear to be conducted in HCC cell monocultures, which do not recapitulate the inflammatory and stromal components critical to IL-6 biology in vivo while complementary co-culture or in vivo validation should be provided in further study. Considering the tumor heterogeneity that causes HCC cells, single-cell analyses might strengthen the biological interpretation of the proposed mechanism in further. Finally, our study noted that alongside the writer (METTL3 and WTAP), the eraser (ALKBH5) is also upregulated and demonstrates a positive correlation with UNC5B-AS1. Further study needs to demonstrate that the eraser does not interfere with the writer in this context.

Conclusion

In summary, this study establishes the role of the m6A/WTAP/UNC5B-AS1 promoting HCC progression and furnishes robust evidence supporting the oncogenic WTAP/UNC5B-AS1 axis. Mechanistically, WTAP-driven m6A modification stabilized UNC5B-AS1 expression in a YTHDF1-dependent manner, while UNC5B-AS1 promoted IL6 upregulation by competitively binding miR-760. Collectively, these results validate the tumor-promoting function of UNC5B-AS1 and its interplay with miR-760 in hepatocellular carcinoma, elucidate a novel mechanism integrating m6A methylation with ceRNA activity, and shed light on the regulatory mechanisms and m6A transcriptome interactions driving malignancy, and identify promising therapeutic targets for HCC.

Long-Term Trends and Regional Heterogeneity in Overweight and Obesity Among Adults — 11 Intermittent Surveys in China, 1991–2024
Authors: Jiale Li, Xiaofan Zhang, Ziyu Zhang, Li Li, Chengdong Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Introduction: Overweight and obesity have increased substantially in China. Limited studies integrate long-term trends, regional heterogeneity, and multilevel factors.

Methods: Eleven China Health and Nutrition Survey waves (1991 to 2024) provided 115,541 person–wave observations (ages 18–79). Prevalence was age-standardized to the 2020 Chinese census population. Inverse-variance weighted regression assessed trends; Cochran's Q test assessed regional heterogeneity. Three-level logistic regression examined individual-, community-, and provincial-level factors.

Results: From 1991 to 2024, age-standardized prevalence increased from 20.20% (1,567/8,445) to 53.10% (7,286/13,112) for combined overweight and obesity, from 16.51% (1,308/8,445) to 35.00% (4,983/13,112) for overweight, and from 3.70% (259/8,445) to 18.10% (2,303/13,112) for obesity (all P<0.001). The prevalence was the highest for combined overweight and obesity and for overweight in North China and for obesity in Northeast China. Men and adults age 40–79 years had higher odds of combined overweight and obesity and of overweight; high education was inversely associated with all outcomes. Drinking and meat intake were positively associated with combined overweight and obesity and with obesity. Community Western fast-food restaurant availability was associated with combined overweight and obesity, and provincial PM2.5 with all outcomes.

Conclusion: Overweight and obesity increased over three decades but varied regionally. Population-wide prevention should be complemented by targeted measures for higher-burden groups and for North and Northeast China.

Overweight and obesity pose challenges to noncommunicable disease prevention and control. Over recent decades in China, their prevalence has risen substantially (1), with national surveillance efforts having identified considerable provincial variation in adult obesity (2). Temporal and geographic patterns have been examined separately; separating overweight from obesity can show whether regional differences reflect either/both outcomes. Because excess weight is associated with multilevel factors (3), whether provincial heterogeneity persists after considering individual and community characteristics and how it relates to provincial socioeconomic and environmental indicators remain unclear. This repeated cross-sectional study used 11 China Health and Nutrition Survey (CHNS) waves to estimate age-standardized trends among adults and compare seven regions.

The 11 CHNS waves used in this study were 1991, 1993, 1997, 2000, 2004, 2006, 2009, 2011, 2015, 2018, and the combined 2022–2024 follow-up round (4). Participating provincial-level administrative divisions (PLADs) increased from nine in 1991–2009 to 12 in 2011, 15 in 2015, and 16 in 2018. In 2022–2024, Hainan was added and Beijing was not included, leaving 16 PLADs; all others were unchanged. Analyses included adults aged 18–79 years with non-missing body mass index (BMI) and excluded identified pregnant women. Standing height and weight were measured with participants in light clothing and no shoes. BMI was calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. Overweight, obesity, and combined overweight and obesity were defined as 24.0≤BMI<28.0 kg/m2, BMI≥28.0 kg/m2, and BMI≥24.0 kg/m2, respectively (5). Prevalence and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were directly age-standardized to the 2020 Chinese population using 12 groups: 18–24 and five-year groups from 25–29 through 75–79 years (6). Standard errors were derived from age-specific binomial variances and CIs used the normal approximation.

Temporal trends were assessed by the inverse-variance weighted least-squares regression of age-standardized prevalence on survey year. Coefficients were average annual percentage-point changes with 95% CIs. The 16 participating PLADs were grouped into North, Northeast, East, Central, South, Southwest, and Northwest China. For each outcome, regional observations were pooled before direct age standardization. Cochran's Q tests with inverse-variance weighting assessed regional heterogeneity.

Three-level random-intercept logistic models were fitted using only 2022–2024 data to reassess provincial heterogeneity, with individuals (level 1) nested within communities (level 2) and communities within PLADs (level 3). Each binary outcome was modeled separately; participants not meeting its definition formed the comparison group. Covariates were sex, age (18–39, 40–59, or 60–79 years), education, household income, smoking, alcohol consumption, total energy intake, percentage energy from fat, and daily staple food, vegetable, fruit, and meat intakes. Community variables were residence, agricultural labor force proportion, and availability of Western fast-food restaurants and a park or stadium. PLAD variables were mean GDP per capita from the 2023–2025 editions of the China Statistical Yearbook (7) and mean PM2.5 concentration from the 1-km ChinaHighPM2.5 V4 product (8). Household income and continuous dietary variables were winsorized at the 0.5th and 99.5th percentiles; income was log2-transformed. Continuous covariates were scaled and grand-mean-centered at their respective analytical levels. Multilevel analyses used complete cases. Model 0 (M0) included community- and PLAD-level random intercepts; Models 1–3 (M1–M3) sequentially added individual-, community-, and PLAD-level covariates. PLAD intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) and community variance partition coefficients used the latent logistic scale with π2/3 individual-level variance. Parametric-bootstrap likelihood-ratio tests evaluated the M0 PLAD variance component. The proportional change in PLAD variance from M2 to M3 quantified reduction after adding GDP per capita and PM2.5. Fixed effects were reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% CIs. PLAD random effects were assumed independent and did not incorporate geographic adjacency; models quantified between-PLAD heterogeneity and associated factors, not spatial clustering.

Analyses used R (version 4.4.3, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and two-sided tests (significance: P<0.05). All participants provided written informed consent.

After exclusions (adults aged ≥80 years, 2,967 observations; pregnant women, 577; missing BMI, 5,562), 115,541 adult person–wave observations remained across 11 waves. The 2022–2024 round comprised 13,112 observations from 16 PLADs; multilevel analyses included 12,748 participants with complete covariates from 380 communities.

The age-standardized combined overweight and obesity prevalence increased from 20.20% (1,567/8,445) (95% CI: 19.27%–21.13%) in 1991 to 53.10% (7,286/13,112) (52.02%–54.18%) in 2024; overweight from 16.51% (1,308/8,445) (15.65%–17.36%) to 35.00% (4,983/13,112) (34.01%–36.00%); obesity from 3.70% (259/8,445) (3.24%–4.15%) to 18.10% (2,303/13,112) (17.25%–18.94%) (Table 1). Average annual percentage-point increases were 0.991 (0.911–1.071), 0.552 (0.464–0.640), and 0.422 (0.362–0.482), respectively (all P<0.001; Figure 1).

In 2022–2024, the age-standardized prevalence differed across regions for all outcomes (all Cochran's Q P<0.001; Table 2). Combined prevalence was the highest in North China at 67.56% (456/668) (95% CI: 62.82%–72.29%) and the lowest in South China at 42.74% (763/1,710) (40.12%–45.36%); results for overweight were similar (North China: highest, 42.34%, 274/668, 95% CI: 37.37%–47.31%; South China: lowest, 30.86%, 566/1,710, 95% CI: 28.51%–33.21%). Obesity was the highest in Northeast China at 27.12% (346/1,575) (22.65%–31.60%) and the lowest in South China at 11.88% (197/1,710) (10.10%–13.66%).

In intercept-only models, PLAD-level ICCs were 2.77%, 0.56%, and 3.18% for combined overweight and obesity, overweight, and obesity, respectively. Bootstrap tests supported a PLAD variance component for each outcome (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/). In M3 (adding GDP per capita and PM2.5), the estimated PLAD-level variance was 46.02% lower for combined overweight and obesity and 25.75% lower for obesity (vs. M2; Supplementary Table S2, available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/).

Fully adjusted models showed higher odds of combined overweight and obesity and of overweight among men (ORs=1.349 and 1.299), adults aged 40–59 years (1.378 and 1.400), and those aged 60–79 years (1.179 and 1.425). High education was inversely associated with all outcomes (ORs=0.727, 0.853, and 0.780). Each income doubling was positively associated with combined overweight and obesity and with overweight (1.026 and 1.016). Current smoking was inversely associated with all outcomes (ORs=0.799, 0.874, and 0.862; all P<0.05). Current drinking (ORs=1.158 and 1.215) and meat intake per 100 g/day (1.058 and 1.071) were positively associated with combined overweight and obesity and with obesity. Vegetable intake per 100 g/day was inversely associated with obesity (OR=0.941). At the community–level, Western fast-food restaurant availability was associated with combined overweight and obesity (OR=1.167, 95% CI: 1.024–1.331). At the PLAD–level, per 10 μg/m3 PM2.5 increase, the ORs were 1.366 (95% CI: 1.136–1.642) for combined overweight and obesity, 1.156 (1.082–1.234) for overweight, and 1.304 (1.029–1.653) for obesity (Table 3).

DISCUSSION

From 1991 to 2024, combined overweight and obesity, overweight, and obesity prevalence increased substantially among adult CHNS participants. Combined prevalence more than doubled, while obesity increased nearly fivefold, indicating a broad expansion of excess weight and increase in its more severe form. This sustained age-standardized rise is consistent with previous evidence from China and occurred alongside major changes in food consumption, transportation, work patterns, and living environments during the country's rapid socioeconomic development (1,3).

Increases were widespread but geographically unequal. North China had the highest combined and overweight prevalence, whereas obesity was the highest in Northeast China, consistent with reported disparities among Chinese adults (2). This regional distribution suggests that conditions associated with moderate and more severe excess weight may differ. The substantial burden across regions, nonetheless, shows that excess weight is not confined to a few high-prevalence areas. Regional differences should guide intervention delivery within population-wide prevention.

Individual-level findings situated this transition within demographic, socioeconomic, and behavioral changes. Men and adults aged 40–79 years had higher odds of combined overweight and obesity and of overweight, making them potential targets for prevention and weight-management services. Higher education was consistently associated with lower odds of all outcomes, whereas income was positively associated with combined overweight and obesity and with overweight. Accordingly, increased economic resources do not necessarily translate into healthier weight. This is despite education being associated with healthier choices during socioeconomic transitions (3). Positive associations of current drinking and meat intake with combined overweight and obesity and with obesity, and the inverse vegetable–obesity association connect excess weight with changing consumption patterns. Inverse associations with current smoking may reflect post-cessation weight gain, illness-related weight loss, or residual confounding by other health behaviors — not smoking's protective effect. These findings support health education and practical support for healthier diets.

These individual characteristics operated within community and provincial contexts. Community fast-food availability was positively associated with combined overweight and obesity, while higher PLAD-level PM2.5 concentrations were associated with all outcomes, consistent with evidence connecting food environments and air pollution to excess-weight risk (9–10). The lower estimated between-PLAD variance in M3 (vs. M2) suggests that GDP per capita and PM2.5 accounted (without establishing causality) for part of the observed between-PLAD variation. Most of the variation occurred among individuals and communities within PLADs; provincial conditions remain relevant, but prevention should also address local conditions.

The findings in this report are subject to at least five limitations. First, CHNS participants were recruited from selected communities, reducing generalizability to included PLADs' complete populations (4). Second, PLAD participation varied across waves, so part of the temporal and regional variation may reflect coverage changes. Third, only 16 PLADs were included, limiting macro-level characteristics assessments. Fourth, the latest-round multilevel analysis was cross-sectional; ORs indicate associations, and the available community and PLAD indicators could not capture all relevant contextual conditions. Finally, complete-case analysis, measurement error, residual confounding, and BMI's inability to characterize body-fat distribution may also have affected the findings.

The continuing shift toward obesity calls for coordinated actions toward the general adult population that are responsive to regional- and community-level circumstances. Prevention should combine health education, weight-management services, and healthy food environment promotion. The role of broader environmental conditions warrants evaluation. Population groups and areas experiencing a greater burden require attention without limiting prevention to selected regions or PLADs.

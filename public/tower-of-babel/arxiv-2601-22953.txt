A novel Hamiltonian formulation of 1 + 1 1+1 dimensional ϕ 4 \phi^{4} theory in Daubechies wavelet basis: momentum space analysis
*

Abstract
We employ the wavelet formalism of quantum field theory to study field theories in the nonperturbative Hamiltonian framework. Specifically, we make use of Daubechies wavelets in momentum space. These basis elements are characterised by a resolution and a translation index that provides for a natural nonperturbative infrared and ultraviolet truncation of the quantum field theory. As an application, we consider the ϕ 4 \phi^{4} theory and demonstrate the emergence of the well-known nonperturbative strong-coupling phase transition in the m 2 > 0 m^{2}>0 sector.

Daubechies wavelet basis

In this section, we outline the Daubechies wavelet basis formalism used in this work. For a comprehensive review, we refer the reader to

The Daubechies basis constitutes an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
composed of scaling functions and wavelet functions. The mother scaling function,
s(x)
, is defined by the renormalization group equation, also known as the scaling equation:

s(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{2K-1}h_{l}\sqrt{2}s(2x-l).

(1)

Here,
K
is the order of the wavelet (determining the support
[0,2K-1]
) and
h_{l}
are the filter coefficients. We utilize the translation operator
\hat{T}f(x)=f(x-1)
and the dilation operator
\hat{D}f(x)=\sqrt{2}f(2x)
. A basis function at resolution
k
and position
m
is defined as
s^{k}_{m}(x):=\hat{D}^{k}\hat{T}^{m}s(x)=2^{k/2}s(2^{k}x-m)
.

The set of scaling functions at resolution
k
spans the subspace
\mathcal{H}^{k}
. The exact relation between resolutions allows
\mathcal{H}^{k}
to be embedded in the finer resolution space,
\mathcal{H}^{k}\subset\mathcal{H}^{k+1}
, creating a nested structure where
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{k}
. The relation between scales is governed by the filter coefficients:

s^{k}_{n}(x)=\sum_{m}h_{m-2n}s^{k+1}_{m}(x).

(2)

The orthogonal complement to
\mathcal{H}^{k}
in
\mathcal{H}^{k+1}
is the wavelet subspace
\mathcal{W}^{k}
, such that
\mathcal{H}^{k+1}=\mathcal{H}^{k}\oplus\mathcal{W}^{k}
. The mother wavelet
w(x)
is constructed via the quadrature mirror filter relation:

w(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{2K-1}g_{l}\sqrt{2}s(2x-l),\quad\text{where }g_{l}=(-1)^{l}h_{2K-1-l}.

(3)

Any function
f(x)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
can be decomposed into a coarse scaling approximation and a sum of wavelet details:

f(x)=\sum_{n}f^{s,k}_{n}s^{k}_{n}(x)+\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}\sum_{n}f^{w,l}_{n}w^{l}_{n}(x),

(4)

or, it can also be expanded using only the scaling functions:

\displaystyle f(x)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{n}f^{s,k}_{n}s^{k}_{n}(x)

(5)

3
Wavelet-based Fock Space

In this section, we construct a wavelet-based Fock space for the
1+1
dimensional free scalar field theory. We derive the Hamiltonian matrix elements in this basis and demonstrate that the wavelet formulation preserves locality through the structure of the hopping amplitudes.

3.1
Scalar Field Hamiltonian

The normal-ordered Hamiltonian for a
1+1
dimensional scalar field
\phi
with unit bare mass is

\mathrm{H}_{0}=\int dp\,E_{p}\,a^{\dagger}(p)a(p),

(6)

where
E_{p}=\sqrt{p^{2}+1}
and the operators satisfy standard commutation relations. We expand the momentum-space creation and annihilation operators in the scaling basis defined in Eq.(
5
).

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a^{s,k\dagger}_{n}s^{k}_{n}(p),

(7)

\displaystyle a(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a^{s,k}_{n}s^{k}_{n}(p).

(8)

Using the orthogonality of the basis, we invert the expansion to define scaling-mode operators:

a^{s,k\dagger}_{n}=\int dp\,a^{\dagger}(p)s^{k}_{n}(p),\quad a^{s,k}_{n}=\int dp\,a(p)s^{k}_{n}(p).

(9)

Physically,
a^{s,k\dagger}_{n}
creates a state smeared over a momentum range of width
\approx(2K-1)/2^{k}
centered at the translation index
n
. Substituting these into Eq. (
6
) yields the following discretized form of the Hamiltonian:

\mathrm{H}_{0}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{m,n}E^{k}_{ss,mn}\,a^{s,k\dagger}_{m}a^{s,k}_{n},

(10)

where the hopping matrix elements are given by the overlap integral

E^{k}_{ss,mn}=\int dp\sqrt{p^{2}+1}\,s^{k}_{m}(p)s^{k}_{n}(p).

(11)

The matrix
E^{k}_{ss,mn}
represents the amplitude for a mode to hop between momentum indices
n
and
m
. Due to the compact support of the Daubechies scaling functions, this matrix is band diagonal;
E^{k}_{ss,mn}
vanishes rapidly as
|m-n|
increases, ensuring the theory remains local in the wavelet representation.

3.2
Fock Space Construction

We define a generic multi-particle Fock state
|\Psi\rangle
by the occupation numbers
\{N_{p}\}
of scaling modes at resolution
k
and position
p
:

|\Psi\rangle=|\dots,N_{p},\dots\rangle,

(12)

where
N_{p}
denotes the number of quanta in mode
p
. The operators act typically as
a^{s,k\dagger}_{n}|\dots,N_{n},\dots\rangle=\sqrt{N_{n}+1}|\dots,N_{n}+1,\dots\rangle
.
The Hamiltonian matrix elements between two Fock states
|\Psi_{1}\rangle=\{N^{(1)}_{p}\}
and
|\Psi_{2}\rangle=\{N^{(2)}_{p}\}
are non-zero only if particle number is conserved. The matrix element separates into diagonal (number operator) and off-diagonal (hopping) terms:

\langle\Psi_{1}|\mathrm{H}_{0}|\Psi_{2}\rangle=\sum_{p,q}E^{k}_{ss,pq}\bra{\Psi_1}a^{s,k\dagger}_{p}a^{s,k}_{q}\ket{\Psi_2}.

(13)

Explicitly, this yields:

\mathcal{H}_{1,2}=\begin{cases}\sum_{p}E^{k}_{ss,pp}N^{(2)}_{p}&\text{if }|\Psi_{1}\rangle=|\Psi_{2}\rangle\\
E^{k}_{ss,pq}\sqrt{(N^{(2)}_{p}+1)N^{(2)}_{q}}&\text{if }|\Psi_{1}\rangle=a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{q}|\Psi_{2}\rangle\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(14)

This discrete Hamiltonian matrix can be truncated and diagonalized to obtain the energy spectrum at finite resolution
k
.

3.3
Hamiltonian Eigenvalues

We diagonalize the Hamiltonian by imposing physical truncations to obtain a finite-dimensional matrix. The truncation scheme involves three constraints:

1.

Momentum Cutoff:
The momentum space volume is restricted by limiting the translation indices of the scaling modes.

2.

Energy Cutoff:
We restrict the many-body basis states to those where the total average energy does not exceed a cutoff value
\Lambda
(set to
10
in dimensionless units for this simulation).

3.

Volume Cutoff:
The resolution
k
effectively acts as a real-space volume cutoff determined by the support of the basis functions.

4.1
Interaction Hamiltonian Construction

The normal-ordered interaction Hamiltonian is given by:

\mathrm{H_{I}}=\frac{\lambda}{4!}\int dx:\!\phi^{4}(x)\!:\quad(\lambda>0).

(15)

By expanding the field operators in the scaling basis, the interaction term takes the discretized form:

\mathrm{H_{I}}=\frac{\lambda}{4!(4\pi)^{2}}\sum_{\{q_{i}\}}\Gamma^{k}_{q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}q_{4}}\,:\prod_{i=1}^{4}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{q_{i}}:,

(16)

where
\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{q_{i}}
represents either a creation (
a^{s,k\dagger}_{q_{i}}
) or annihilation (
a^{s,k}_{q_{i}}
) operator. The vertex factor
\Gamma^{k}
is the momentum-space overlap integral of four scaling functions:

\Gamma^{k}_{\{q_{i}\}}=\int\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{4}dp_{j}\,s^{k}_{q_{j}}(p_{j})}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{4}E_{j}}}\,\delta(\sum p_{j}),

(17)

where
E_{j}=\sqrt{p_{j}^{2}+m^{2}}
. Efficient evaluation of these integrals is detailed in
[
9
]
.

The matrix elements of
\mathrm{H_{I}}
between two Fock states
|\Psi_{1}\rangle
and
|\Psi_{2}\rangle
are calculated similarly to the free case. The non-vanishing terms correspond to the standard normal-ordered operator combinations (
a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}
,
a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}a
, etc.):

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{1}|\mathrm{H_{I}}|\Psi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{4!(4\pi)^{2}}\sum_{\{q_{i}\}}\Gamma^{k}_{\{q_{i}\}}\Big[\mathcal{A}^{k}-4\mathcal{B}^{k}+6\mathcal{C}^{k}-4\mathcal{D}^{k}+\mathcal{E}^{k}\Big].

(18)

Here, the terms
\mathcal{A}^{k}
through
\mathcal{E}^{k}
represent the matrix elements of the operator sequences
aaaa
,
a^{\dagger}aaa
,
a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}aa
,
a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}a
, and
a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}
, respectively. For details calculation see
[
9
]

5
Conclusion and Outlook

In this work, we developed a nonperturbative Hamiltonian formulation of the
1+1
dimensional
\phi^{4}
theory using a momentum-space Daubechies wavelet basis, realizing Wilson’s original vision of a "wave-packet" expansion. By decomposing field operators into wavelet modes characterized by resolution and translation indices, we constructed a systematic truncation scheme that serves as a robust alternative to standard Fourier discretization.

Our results highlight two distinct advantages of this framework. First, the compact support of the Daubechies wavelets results in a free Hamiltonian with finite-range hopping (locality preservation). Second, in the interacting
\phi^{4}
case, the matrix representation exhibits high compressibility, as the dominant contributions arise from a limited number of degrees of freedom.

Crucially, we demonstrated that the quantum critical point can be reliably extracted even at coarse resolutions. Our estimated critical couplings converge toward established literature values as the resolution increases, validating the consistency of the method.

Looking ahead, we identify several promising directions for this formalism:

1.

Optimization:
Future work will implement projection techniques to the zero-momentum sector to reduce computational costs, enabling access to higher resolutions and more precise spectra.

2.

Higher Dimensions & Gauge Theories:
The multiresolution structure and inherent locality of the basis make it well-suited for extension to higher-dimensional systems and potentially gauge theories or QCD-like models.

3.

Position-Space Formulations:
We are currently developing a complementary position-space wavelet Hamiltonian approach, which will be reported in subsequent studies.

In summary, this work establishes the momentum-space wavelet basis as a scalable and effective tool for Hamiltonian truncation in quantum field theory.

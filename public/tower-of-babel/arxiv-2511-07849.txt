Lectures on Local Theta Correspondence Thanks: Author’s note: The choices of contents of these notes reflect interests and biases of the author. A good part of the notes are based on the work of the author and his collaborators.
Chen-Bo Zhu

Abstract
. This set of lecture notes on local theta correspondence is the written version of a mini-course the author gave in March of 2025 for the program “Representation Theory and Noncommutative Geometry” at the Institut Henri Poincaré, Paris. The emphasis is on the Archimedean theory, which concerns representations of classical Lie groups. Section 1 is about the basic theory, including the Howe Duality Theorem, and the conservation relations. Second 2 highlights the invariant and distributional nature of local theta correspondence via the proof of the conservation relations. Sections 3 and 4 explain how two fundamental invariants of representations behave under local theta correspondence. The final section discusses applications to unitary representation theory.

In what follows,
{\mathrm{F}}
will always denote a local field of characteristic
0
. In addition we will fix a nontrivial unitary character
\psi

{\mathrm{F}}

1.1.
Reductive dual pairs and classification

Let
W
be a finite-dimensional symplectic vector space over
{\mathrm{F}}
with symplectic form
{\langle},{\rangle}_{W}:W\times W\rightarrow{\mathrm{F}}
. Let
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
be the isometry group of
{\langle},{\rangle}_{W}
.

Definition 1.1.1

(Howe)
.

A reductive dual pair in
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
is a pair of closed reductive subgroups
G,G^{\prime}
of
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
such that
G
and
G^{\prime}
are mutual centralizers.

If
(G,G^{\prime})
is a reductive dual pair in
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
, and if
W=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}
is an orthogonal direct sum decomposition where
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are invariant by
G\cdot G^{\prime}
, then we say
(G,G^{\prime})
is reducible. The restrictions
(G_{i},G_{i}^{\prime})
of
(G,G^{\prime})
to the
W_{i}
define reductive dual pairs in the
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W_{i})
. We say that
(G,G^{\prime})
is the direct product of the
(G_{i},G^{\prime}_{i})
. If
(G,G^{\prime})
is not reducible, it is then called irreducible. Any reductive dual pair is a direct product in an essentially unique way of irreducible reductive dual pairs.

Here are the alternative definitions. Denote by
\sigma
the anti-involution of
\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{F}}(W)
specified by

{\langle}x\cdot u,v{\rangle}_{W}={\langle}u,x^{\sigma}\cdot v{\rangle}_{W},\qquad u,v\in W,\,x\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{F}}(W).

Then
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)=\{x\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{F}}(W)\mid x^{\sigma}x=1\}
.
Let
(A,A^{\prime})
be a pair of
\sigma
-stable semisimple
{\mathrm{F}}
-subalgebras of
\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{F}}(W)
that are mutual centralizers. Set

G:=A\cap{\mathrm{Sp}}(W),\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\,G^{\prime}:=A^{\prime}\cap{\mathrm{Sp}}(W),

which are closed subgroups of
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
. The pair of groups
(G,G^{\prime})
is then a reductive dual pair in
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
. The dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
is irreducible if the algebra
A
(or equivalently,
A^{\prime}
) is either simple or the product of two simple algebras that are exchanged by
\sigma
.

Complete classification of irreducible reductive dual pairs has been given by Howe (
[
22
,
23
]
), as described in what follows. See
[
46
, Lecture 5]
for an exposition.

For simplicity, we shall describe this classification for the case
{\mathrm{F}}={\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
({\mathrm{D}},\natural)
be one of the following seven pairs so that
{\mathrm{D}}
is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra and
\natural
is an anti-involution of
{\mathrm{D}}
:

(I)

({\mathbb{R}},\textrm{id}),\quad({\mathbb{C}},\textrm{id}),\quad({\mathbb{C}},\overline{\phantom{a}}),\quad({\mathbb{H}},\overline{\phantom{a}}),

(II)

({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}},((x,y)\mapsto(y,x)),\quad({\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}},((x,y)\mapsto(y,x)),({\mathbb{H}}\times{\mathbb{H}},((x,y)\mapsto(\bar{y},\bar{x})),

where
{\mathbb{H}}
denotes the algebra of Hamiltonian quaternions,
id
denotes the identity map, and
\overline{\phantom{a}}\,
indicates the complex conjugation or the quaternionic conjugation.

Let
\epsilon=\pm 1
. Let
V
be an
\epsilon
-Hermitian
{\mathrm{D}}
-space, namely a free right
{\mathrm{D}}
-module of finite rank, equipped with a non-degenerate
{\mathbb{R}}
-bilinear map

{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}_{V}:V\times V\rightarrow{\mathrm{D}}

such that

{\langle}ua,v{\rangle}_{V}={\langle}u,v{\rangle}_{V}a,\quad{\langle}u,v{\rangle}_{V}=\epsilon{\langle}v,u{\rangle}_{V}^{\natural},\quad\textrm{for all }u,v\in V,\,a\in{\mathrm{D}}.

This
{\mathbb{R}}
-bilinear map is called the
\epsilon
-Hermitian form on
V
, whose isometry group is denoted by
{\mathrm{G}}(V)
.

Let
V^{\prime}
be an
\epsilon^{\prime}
-Hermitian
{\mathrm{D}}
-space, equipped with an
\epsilon^{\prime}
-Hermitian form

{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}_{V^{\prime}}
, where
\epsilon\epsilon^{\prime}=-1
. Let
W:=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathrm{D}}}(V,V^{\prime})
, equipped with the symplectic form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{W}
given by

{\langle}T,S{\rangle}_{W}:=\operatorname{Tr}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(T^{\ast}S),\qquad\mbox{$T$, $S\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathrm{D}}}(V,V^{\prime})$,}

where
\operatorname{Tr}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(T^{\ast}S)
is the trace of
T^{\ast}S
as a
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear transformation, and
T^{\ast}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathrm{D}}}(V^{\prime},V)
is the adjoint of
T
defined by

(1.1.2)

\langle Tv,v^{\prime}\rangle_{V^{\prime}}=\langle v,T^{\ast}v^{\prime}\rangle_{V},\qquad\mbox{for all $\,v\in V$, $v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}$.}

There is a natural homomorphism:
{\mathrm{G}}(V)\times{\mathrm{G}}(V^{\prime})\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
given by

(g,g^{\prime})\cdot T=g^{\prime}Tg^{-1}\qquad\mbox{for $T\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathrm{D}}}(V,{V}^{\prime})$, $g\in G$, $g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}$}.

If both
V
and
V^{\prime}
are nonzero, then
{\mathrm{G}}(V)
and
{\mathrm{G}}(V^{\prime})
are both identified with subgroups of
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
, and
({\mathrm{G}}(V),{\mathrm{G}}(V^{\prime}))
is an irreducible reductive dual pair in
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
. Moreover, all irreducible reductive dual pairs arise in this way.

When
({\mathrm{D}},\natural)
consists of a division algebra with an anti-involution, i.e. from (I), the irreducible dual pair
({\mathrm{G}}(V),{\mathrm{G}}(V^{\prime}))
is called type I. When
({\mathrm{D}},\natural)
is from (II), the irreducible dual pair
({\mathrm{G}}(V),{\mathrm{G}}(V^{\prime}))
is called type II. A list of irreducible dual pairs of type I and type II is as follows.

Type I:

({\mathrm{O}}_{p,q},{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}({\mathbb{R}}))\subseteq{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2(p+q)n}({\mathbb{R}})

({\mathrm{O}}_{p}({\mathbb{C}}),{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}}))\subseteq{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4pn}({\mathbb{R}})

({\mathrm{U}}_{p,q},{\mathrm{U}}_{r,s})\subseteq{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2(p+q)(r+s)}({\mathbb{R}})

({\mathrm{Sp}}_{p,q},{\mathrm{O}}^{*}_{2n})\subseteq{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4(p+q)n}({\mathbb{R}})

Type II:

(\mathrm{GL}_{m}({\mathbb{R}}),\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}))\subseteq{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2mn}({\mathbb{R}})

(\mathrm{GL}_{m}({\mathbb{C}}),\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\subseteq{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4mn}({\mathbb{R}})

(\mathrm{GL}_{m}({\mathbb{H}}),\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}}))\subseteq{\mathrm{Sp}}_{8mn}({\mathbb{R}})

1.2.
Oscillator representations and Howe Duality Theorem

Write
{\mathrm{H}}(W):=W\times{\mathrm{F}}
for the Heisenberg group with group multiplication

(u,t)\cdot(u^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(u+u^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}{\langle}u,u^{\prime}{\rangle}_{W}),\qquad u,u^{\prime}\in W,\ t,t^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{F}}.

Its center is obviously identified with
{\mathrm{F}}
.
Recall the Stone-von
Neumann Theorem which asserts that up to isomorphism, there exists a unique irreducible
unitary representation of

{\mathrm{H}}(W)
with central character
\psi
. See
[
6
,
24
]
, for example.

Let
\widetilde{{\mathrm{Sp}}}(W)
be the metaplectic group: it is the unique topological central extension of the symplectic group
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
by
\{\pm 1\}
which does not split unless
W=0
or
{\mathrm{F}}\cong{\mathbb{C}}
.
According to Shale
[
65
]
and Weil
[
75
]
, there is a unitary representation
\widehat{\omega}
of
\widetilde{{\mathrm{Sp}}}(W)\ltimes{\mathrm{H}}(W)
whose restriction
\widehat{\omega}|_{{\mathrm{H}}(W)}
to
{\mathrm{H}}(W)
is irreducible with central character
\psi
. We call this representation the oscillator representation (attached to the central character
\psi
).

We are given
(G,G^{\prime})
, an arbitrary reductive dual pair in
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
. We consider the following general setting. Let
\widetilde{G}
and
\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
be a pair of reductive groups together with continuous surjective group homomorphisms
\widetilde{G}\rightarrow G
and
\widetilde{G^{\prime}}\rightarrow G^{\prime}
. The
group
\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
acts on the Heisenberg group
{\mathrm{H}}(W)
as group automorphisms through its obvious action on
W
.
Using this action, we define the Jacobi group

J:=(\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}})\ltimes{\mathrm{H}}(W).

Suppose that
J
has a unitary representation whose restriction to
{\mathrm{H}}(W)
is irreducible with central character
\psi
. (For example, this is the case if we take
(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{G^{\prime}})
to be the inverse image of
(G,G^{\prime})
in
\widetilde{{\mathrm{Sp}}}(W)
.)
By the Stone-von Neumann Theorem, all such representations, if they exist, are isomorphic to each other up to twisting by unitary characters (which are trivial on

{\mathrm{H}}(W)
). We fix one such
\widehat{\omega}
and write
\omega
for the space of smooth vectors of
\widehat{\omega}|_{{\mathrm{H}}(W)}
, which is
J
-stable and is a smooth representation of
J
. We will refer to
\omega
as a smooth oscillator representation.

The basic problem is to understand
\omega|_{\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}}}
, and this is what the theory of local theta correspondence addresses. The main assertion of the theory is the Howe duality conjecture
[
22
, Conjecture (local duality)]
. We shall give the precise statement for
{\mathrm{F}}={\mathbb{R}}
, established by Howe in
[
28
]
.

The results of
[
28
]
are stated in terms of infinitesimal equivalent classes of irreducible (quasisimple) admissible representations. With the benefit of the Casselman–Wallach theorem, it is quite natural to formulate the results in the category of Casselman–Wallach representations. We refer the reader to
[
73
, Chapter 11]
for generalities on Casselman-Wallach representations.

For a reductive Lie group
E
, denote by
\operatorname{Irr}(E)
the set of equivalent classes of irreducible Casselman-Wallach representations of
E
. Note that
\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}})\simeq\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G})\times\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G^{\prime}})
. The identification associates to
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G})
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G^{\prime}})
the completed projective tensor product
\pi\widehat{\otimes}\pi^{\prime}
.

We fix a realization of the smooth oscillator representation
\omega
, on a space
\mathscr{Y}
. Denote by
\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G},\omega)
the set of elements of

\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G})
which are realized as quotients by
\omega(\widetilde{G})
-invariant closed subspaces of
{\mathcal{Y}}
. Namely,

\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G},\omega):=\{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G})\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{\widetilde{G}}(\omega,\pi)\neq 0\}.

Define
\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G^{\prime}},\omega)
and
\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}},\omega)
, likewise.

Theorem 1.2.1

(Howe,
[
28
]
)
.

The set
\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}},\omega)
is the graph of a bijection between (all of)
\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G},\omega)
and (all of)
\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G^{\prime}},\omega)
. Moreover, an element
\pi\widehat{\otimes}\pi^{\prime}
of
\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}},\omega)
occurs as a quotient of
\omega
in a unique way.

Remark 1.2.2
.

For a nonarchimedean local field, the Howe duality conjecture is established by Waldspurger (
[
71
]
, residue characteristic not
2
), and completed by Minguez
[
49
]
, Gan-Takeda
[
15
]
and Gan-Sun
[
14
]
, without any restriction on the residue characteristics. Thus the Howe duality conjecture is now a theorem in its full generality.

It is convenient to introduce the following version of the Howe Duality Theorem. Given
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G})
, consider the maximal
\pi
-isotypic quotient of
\mathscr{Y}
, called the Howe’s (maximal) quotient of
\pi
. It is of the form

\pi\widehat{\otimes}\Theta(\pi)
, where
\Theta(\pi)
is a representation of
\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
. It is clear that
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G},\omega)
if and only if
\Theta(\pi)\neq 0
.
The representation
\Theta(\pi)
is often referred to as the
full theta lift (or the big theta lift) of
\pi
.

Recall that for a reductive Lie group
E
, a representation of
E
is called quasisimple if
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{E}})^{E}
act on the representation space as scalar operators. Here
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{E}})
denotes the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{E}}
of
E
, and the superscript
E
indicates the invariant space under the adjoint action
\text{Ad}E
.

Theorem 1.2.3

(Howe,
[
28
]
)
.

For any
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G},\omega)
, the representation
\Theta(\pi)
is a quasisimple Casselman-Wallach representation of
\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
. Furthermore,
\Theta(\pi)
has a unique irreducible
\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
quotient
\pi^{\prime}
so that
\pi\widehat{\otimes}\pi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}},\omega)
.

We refer to the correspondence of representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
in Theorems
1.2.1
and
1.2.3
as local theta correspondence or Howe correspondence. In
[
28
]
, Howe proves an algebraic version of Theorems
1.2.1
and
1.2.3
and shows that Theorems
1.2.1
and
1.2.3
follow from this algebraic version. It remains an interesting problem to give a suitably distribution theoretic proof of the Howe Duality Theorem, as envisaged in
[
22
, Section 11]
.

To get a sense of the algebraic formulation of the Howe Duality Theorem and what’s involved in its proof, we shall consider the Harish-Chandra module of
\omega
. Write
{\mathrm{Sp}}={\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
, and
{\mathfrak{sp}}
its Lie algebra. Fix a maximal compact subgroup
U\simeq U_{N}
(the unitary group in
N
variables), where
N=\frac{\dim W}{2}
. We then have the Cartan decomposition:

(1.2.4)

{\mathfrak{sp}}={\mathfrak{u}}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}.

The
({\mathfrak{sp}},\tilde{U})
module associated to
\omega
is then realized in the space
\mathscr{P}=\mathscr{P}_{N}
of polynomials on
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
, known as the Fock model
[
5
,
23
]
. In this model, we have a decomposition of the complex Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{sp}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
(the complexfication of
{\mathfrak{sp}}
):

(1.2.5)

\omega({\mathfrak{sp}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})={\mathfrak{sp}}^{(1,1)}\oplus{\mathfrak{sp}}^{(2,0)}\oplus{\mathfrak{sp}}^{(0,2)},\quad\text{where}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\mathfrak{sp}}^{(1,1)}&=&\text{span}\{z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N},\\
{\mathfrak{sp}}^{(2,0)}&=&\text{span}\{z_{i}z_{j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N},\\
{\mathfrak{sp}}^{(0,2)}&=&\text{span}\{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{i}\partial z_{j}}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}.\end{array}

The relation between decompositions (
1.2.4
) and (
1.2.5
) is

\omega({\mathfrak{u}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})={\mathfrak{sp}}^{(1,1)},\qquad\omega({\mathfrak{q}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})={\mathfrak{sp}}^{(2,0)}\oplus{\mathfrak{sp}}^{(0,2)}.

We may assume that
G
and
G^{\prime}
are embedded in
{\mathrm{Sp}}
in such a way that the Cartan decomposition of
{\mathfrak{sp}}
also induces Cartan decompositions of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
. Naturally
\mathscr{P}
is a
({\mathfrak{g}},\widetilde{K})
module and a
({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\widetilde{K^{\prime}})
module. Note that the action of
\widetilde{U}
, and therefore
\widetilde{K}
and
\widetilde{K^{\prime}}
, preserves the degree of a polynomial in
\mathscr{P}
. In this algebraic context, we will adopt analogous notations such as
\operatorname{Irr}({\mathfrak{g}},\widetilde{K},\omega)
and
\operatorname{Irr}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\widetilde{K^{\prime}},\omega)
without further explanation.

When
G
or
G^{\prime}
is compact, in which case we say that the dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
is compact, the duality theorem is known earlier (see
[
32
,
27
]
) and amounts essentially to a version of Classical Invariant Theory
[
76
,
22
,
27
]
. We will state the duality result for a compact dual pair, which has additional structure and which also serves as a bridge to the general case. Let us write a compact dual pair as
(K,G^{\prime})
(to signal that
G=K
is compact.) We may assume that
K
is contained in
U
, which we have fixed. The decomposition (
1.2.5
) induces a decomposition for
\omega({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
:

\omega({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}})={\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(1,1)}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(2,0)}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(0,2)},\quad\text{ where}\quad{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(i,j)}=\omega({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}})\cap{\mathfrak{sp}}^{(i,j)}.

Define the space of
K
-harmonics in
\mathscr{P}
:

\mathscr{H}(K)=\{P\in\mathscr{P}:\Delta(P)=0\text{ for all }\Delta\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(0,2)}\},

which is a
\widetilde{K}\times{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(1,1)}
-module. Consider the isotypic decomposition of
\mathscr{P}
as an
\widetilde{K}
-module:

\mathscr{P}=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{K},\omega)}\mathscr{P}_{\sigma}.

Generalizing theory of spherical harmonics, the following result may be considered as the main result of Classical Invariant Theory. This is explained in
[
27
]
and appears in
[
28
]
. See also
[
32
]
.

Theorem 1.2.6

(
[
28
,
32
]
)
.

(a).

The joint action of
\widetilde{K}\times{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
on
\mathscr{P}_{\sigma}
is irreducible for each
\sigma\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{K},\omega)
.

(b).

The space
\mathscr{H}(K)_{\sigma}:=\mathscr{H}(K)\cap\mathscr{P}_{\sigma}
consists precisely of the space of polynomials of lowest degree in
\mathscr{P}_{\sigma}
.

(c).

One has
\mathscr{P}_{\sigma}={\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(2,0)})\cdot\mathscr{H}(K)_{\sigma}
, where
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(2,0)})
is the universal enveloping algebra of

{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(2,0)}
.

(d).

The group
\widetilde{K}
and the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(1,1)}
generate mutual commutants on
\mathscr{H}(K)
. Equivalently, each
\mathscr{H}(K)_{\sigma}
is irreducible under the joint action of
\widetilde{K}\times{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(1,1)}
, and if we write
\mathscr{H}(K)_{\sigma}\simeq\sigma\otimes\tau
for
\tau\in\operatorname{Irr}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(1,1)},\omega)
, then
\sigma
determines
\tau
and vice versa, so that
\sigma\rightarrow\tau
is an injection from
\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{K},\omega)
into
\operatorname{Irr}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime(1,1)},\omega)
.

The above result for a compact dual pair is algebraic in nature and serves as a stepping stone to the duality theorem in the general setting. For the general setting, the key lies in some remarkable relationship between
(G,G^{\prime})
and three other dual pairs in
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
. The three pairs are
(K,M^{\prime})
,
(K^{\prime},M)
, and
(M^{(1,1)},M^{\prime(1,1)})
, where
M^{\prime}
(resp.
M
) is the centralizer of
K
(resp.
K^{\prime}
), and
M^{(1,1)}
(resp.
M^{\prime(1,1)}
) is a maximal compact subgroup of
M
(resp.
M^{\prime}
). Note that all three are compact dual pairs. For
(G,G^{\prime})=({\mathrm{O}}_{p,q},{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}({\mathbb{R}}))
, the relevant dual pairs are depicted in Figure
1
in two diamonds: the pairs of Lie groups similarly placed in the two diamonds are dual pairs.

We have the see-saw dual pairs
(G,G^{\prime})
and
(K,M^{\prime})
, and the containment of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
in
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
:

{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}={\mathfrak{k}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\subset{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}={\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime(1,1)}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime(2,0)}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime(0,2)}.

The non-compact part
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
is transversal in the non-compact part
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime(2,0)}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime(0,2)}
of
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
in the following sense:

(1.2.7)

{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime(2,0)}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime(0,2)}={\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime(0,2)}={\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime(2,0)}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{C}}}.

Likewise for the see-saw dual pairs
(G,G^{\prime})
and
(M,K^{\prime})
, we have the transversality of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
in
{\mathfrak{m}}^{(2,0)}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}^{(0,2)}
.

Applying Theorem
1.2.6
for the compact dual pair
(K,M^{\prime})
and using (
1.2.7
), one immediately obtains the following

Proposition 1.2.8

(
[
28
]
)
.

The space
\mathscr{H}(K)
generates
\mathscr{P}
as a
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
-module, and similarly for
K^{\prime}
. That is,

\mathscr{P}={\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime})\cdot\mathscr{H}(K)={\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\cdot\mathscr{H}(K^{\prime}),

where
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime})
is the universal enveloping algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
. Thus, for any
\sigma\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{K},\omega)
or

\sigma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{K^{\prime}},\omega)
, one has

(1.2.9)

\mathscr{P}_{\sigma}={\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime})\cdot\mathscr{H}(K)_{\sigma},\qquad\mathscr{P}_{\sigma^{\prime}}={\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\cdot\mathscr{H}(K^{\prime})_{\sigma}.

Howe then proves a few facts about the structure of
\mathscr{P}
as a
({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\widetilde{K}\cdot\widetilde{K^{\prime}})
module, and the main character in these structure results is the so-called space of joint harmonics.

Definition 1.2.10
.

The space of joint harmonics
\mathscr{H}_{K,K^{\prime}}
is the space of polynomials which are both
K
-harmonics and
K^{\prime}
-harmonics, namely

\mathscr{H}_{K,K^{\prime}}=\mathscr{H}(K)\cap\mathscr{H}(K^{\prime}).

For the proof of the next proposition, Howe uses the dual pair
(M^{(1,1)},M^{\prime(1,1)})
and the fact that both members are compact, in addition to the dual pairs
(K,M^{\prime})
and
(K^{\prime},M)
.
Define
\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{K},\mathscr{H}_{K,K^{\prime}})
and

Proposition 1.2.11

)

(a).

There is a bijective correspondence

\sigma\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{K},\mathscr{H}_{K,K^{\prime}})\longleftrightarrow\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{K^{\prime}},\mathscr{H}_{K,K^{\prime}})\ni\sigma^{\prime},

(b).

\mathscr{H}_{K,K^{\prime}}

{\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime})\cdot\mathscr{H}_{K,K^{\prime}}=\mathscr{P}

Write

2.
The invariant and distribution theoretic nature of local theta correspondence

The invariant and distributional nature of the theory of local theta correspondence is emphasized by Howe in the beginning
[
22
]
as well as in his fundamental paper
[
28
]
establishing the duality theorem (for the archimedean case).

In this section, we will illustrate these viewpoints via the proof of conservation relations
[
68
]
. We first discuss
the so-called doubling method, in which invariant theoretical considerations play a significant role. Then we discuss the two complementary aspects of the conservation relations, non-occurrence (or vanishing) and occurrence (or non-vanishing), providing the necessary inequalities leading to an equality.

2.1.
The doubling method and the invariant distribution theorem

A principal idea in the theory of local theta correspondence, which appears in
[
22
]
and
[
62
]
, is the so-called doubling method. Suppose that we are given a reductive dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})\subset{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
, with a smooth oscillator representation
\omega
. We may consider
2W:=W\oplus W^{-}
, referred to as the double of
W
. There is a group
2G^{\prime}
(which contains
G^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}
) such that
(G_{\Delta},2G^{\prime})
forms a reductive dual pair in
{\mathrm{Sp}}(2W)
, with a suitably compatible smooth oscillator representation
\Omega
. Here
G_{\Delta}
is an isomorphic copy of
G
. A key role in the doubling method is played by
\Omega^{2G^{\prime}}_{G_{\Delta}}({1\!\!1})
(or
\Omega({1\!\!1})
in short), the maximal quotient of
\Omega
on which
G_{\Delta}
acts trivially. (We shall refer to
\Omega({1\!\!1})
as the Rallis quotient of
G_{\Delta}
.) This is a module for
\widetilde{2G^{\prime}}
, and the relevant problem is to analyze
\Omega({1\!\!1})
as a
\widetilde{G^{\prime}}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
-module, and in particular to understand its irreducible quotients. We refer the reader to
[
22
, Section 11]
for a more detailed discussion.

We will consider the orthogonal-symplectic dual pair:

(G,G^{\prime})=({\mathrm{O}}(V),{\mathrm{Sp}}(V^{\prime}))\subset{\mathrm{Sp}}(W),\quad W=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathrm{F}}}(V,V^{\prime}).

Set
{\mathbb{V}}^{\prime}:=V^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime-}
, the double of

is at least

\textrm{the Witt index of $(V_{1}\oplus V_{2}^{-})$}=\frac{\dim V_{1}+\dim V_{2}-4}{2}\qquad(=2n).

Therefore
{\mathbf{t}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{t}}_{2}
are adjacent.
∎

3.
Correspondence of invariants (I): generalized Whittaker models

In the representation theory of (real or
p
-adic) reductive groups, an important invariant of smooth representations is the generalized Whittaker models (attached to nilpotent orbits in the Lie algebra). This is the local version of the Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms. (See
[
30
]
for discussions on their role in a general theory of periods of automorphic forms.)

In this section we will discuss the main result of
[
80
]
on the transition of generalized Whittaker models, more precisely how generalized Whittaker models of the full theta lift of a representation
\pi
relate to those of
\pi
. (A global analogue was recently established by B. Wang
[
74
]
.). This is a refinement of the previous result of Gomez-Zhu in
[
18
]
. We include comprehensive details in these notes, partly because the key technical proposition of (
[
80
, Section 3.3]
), which computes the twisted Jaquet modules of the oscillator representation, should be made more precise. For instance, the isomorphism
\Psi_{T}
there was not made explicit, and there were some unexplained notations and actions. Historically there were many earlier results on transition of models, including those of Furusawa
[
11
]
, Mœglin
[
51
]
and Ginzburg-Jiang-Soudry
[
17
]
. See also Gan’s article
[
12
]
, which offers another perspective on relating certain models of two groups in the dual pair setting.

3.1.
Generalized Whittaker models associated to an
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-triple

We recall some basics of generalized Whittaker models
[
78
,
52

. (See also the earlier work of Kawanaka
[
31

in the finite field case.) Recall that
{\mathrm{F}}
is a local field of characteristic
0
, on which we fix a nontrivial unitary character
\psi

Let

be a reductive group
over
{\mathrm{F}}
, and

be its Lie algebra. We also fix a
\operatorname{Ad}\,G

.
Let

. We complete

{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-triple

Set

, for
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}

Define the Lie subalgebras
{\mathfrak{u}}=\oplus_{j\leq-2}{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}

{\mathfrak{n}}=\oplus_{j\leq-1}{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}

{\mathfrak{p}}=\oplus_{j\leq 0}{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}

Let

, the stabilizer group of

In particular

is reductive.

For the moment assume that

(3.1.2)

\kappa_{-1}(S,T)=\kappa(\operatorname{ad}(X)S,T)=\kappa(X,[S,T]),\qquad\mbox{for $S$, $T\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$}.

Denote by
{\mathrm{H}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1})

to
{\mathrm{H}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1})
which maps

to

\exp R\cdot\exp Z\mapsto(R,\kappa(X,Z)),\ \ \mbox{for $R\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$, $Z\in{\mathfrak{u}}$.}

Then, according to the Stone-von
Neumann theorem, there exists a unique, up to equivalence, smooth

of

One may thus extend the representation

of

to a representation of the semi-direct product

of

Here

denotes the inverse image of

in

. Similar notation applies without further explanation.

If

to denote the

-dimensional

given by the character

, and

acts on it trivially.

Definition 3.1.3

Let

be a smooth representation of

(a)

We now assume that

3.5.

We are given a type I reductive dual pair

Theorem 3.5.1

(Gomez-Zhu
[
80
]
)
.

Let

, and
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
be a nilpotent orbit.

(a).

If
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}
is not in the image of the moment map
\varphi^{\prime}
, then

\operatorname{Wh}_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(\Theta(\pi))=0.

(b).

If
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}
is in the image of the moment map
\varphi^{\prime}
, let
{\mathcal{O}}=\nabla^{V^{\prime}}_{V}({\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})
be its descent. We have (as in (
3.2.11
) and (
3.2.12
))

\displaystyle M_{X}\supset M_{{X},{X}^{\prime}}\times L,\qquad(\text{as a symmetric subgroup})

\displaystyle M^{\prime}_{X^{\prime}}=M_{{X},{X}^{\prime}}\times L^{\prime},

and

forms a dual pair of the same type as

. Then for any
\tau^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{L^{\prime}})

\operatorname{Wh}_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}(\Theta(\pi))\simeq\operatorname{Wh}_{{\mathcal{O}},\Theta(\tau^{\prime})^{\vee}}(\pi^{\vee})

-modules. Here

is the full theta lift of
\tau^{\prime}
with respect to the dual pair

, and the symbol

indicates the dual in the category of Casselman-Wallach representations.

Proof.

Part (a) follows from Lemma
3.4.14
, and so we just prove Part (b).

By Proposition
3.4.1
and Lemma
3.3.7
, we have

\mathscr{Y}_{{U^{\prime}},\chi_{{\gamma}^{\prime}}}\simeq\mathscr{S}(N\backslash\widetilde{G};\mathscr{S}_{\check{\gamma}}\otimes\mathscr{S}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\otimes\omega_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}})\simeq\mathscr{S}(N\backslash\widetilde{G};\mathscr{S}_{\check{\gamma}})\otimes\mathscr{S}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\otimes\omega_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}},

as
\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{M_{X,X^{\prime}}}N^{\prime}\times\widetilde{L^{\prime}}
-modules. Here the action of
\widetilde{M_{X,X^{\prime}}}N^{\prime}\times\widetilde{L^{\prime}}
is induced by the representation of
(\widetilde{\Delta M_{X,X^{\prime}}}\ltimes(N\times{N^{\prime}}))\times(\widetilde{L}\times\widetilde{L^{\prime}})

\mathscr{S}_{\check{\gamma}}\otimes\mathscr{S}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\otimes\omega_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}

We take the
(L^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
-isotypic quotient on both sides to get

(3.5.2)

\mathscr{Y}_{({L^{\prime}U^{\prime}},\tau^{\prime}\otimes\chi_{\gamma^{\prime}})}\simeq\mathscr{Y}_{({U^{\prime}},\chi_{\gamma^{\prime}}),(L^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})}\simeq\mathscr{S}(N\backslash\widetilde{G};\Theta(\tau^{\prime})\otimes\mathscr{S}_{\check{\gamma}})\otimes(\tau^{\prime}\otimes\mathscr{S}_{\gamma^{\prime}}),

(G,\pi)
-isotypic quotient on both sides of (

On the left hand, we get

\mathscr{Y}_{(G,\pi),({L^{\prime}U^{\prime}},\tau^{\prime}\otimes\chi_{\gamma^{\prime}})}\simeq\pi\otimes\Theta(\pi)_{(L^{\prime}U^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\otimes\chi_{\gamma^{\prime}})}.

Put

70

Write

Now assume that
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Nil}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime})

\check{\vartheta}_{{\mathcal{O}}}^{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})

(4.4.1)

\check{\vartheta}_{{\mathcal{O}}}^{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}({\mathcal{E}})=\sum_{\mathscr{O}^{\prime}\textrm{ is a ${\mathbf{K}}^{\prime}$-orbit in ${\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}$, $\,\nabla(\mathscr{O}^{\prime})=\mathscr{O}$}}\check{\vartheta}_{\mathscr{O}}^{\mathscr{O}^{\prime}}({\mathcal{E}}),

{\mathbf{K}}
-orbit
\mathscr{O}
in
{\mathcal{O}}\cap{\mathfrak{p}}

-equivariant algebraic vector bundle

over

Recall that

Given

\text{either ${\mathcal{O}}$ is the pure descent of ${\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}$, or ${\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}$ is a pure nilpotent}.

The relevance of the notion just defined is clear by the following lemma, which follows easily from

Suppose that

\check{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{{\mathbf{V}}}^{{\mathbf{V}}^{\prime}}({\mathcal{O}})={\mathcal{O}}^{\prime},\text{ i.e., }\mathscr{M}^{\prime}(\mathscr{M}^{-1}(\overline{{\mathcal{O}}}))=\overline{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}.

In the regular descent setting, we have the following upper bound of associated cycles.

Theorem 4.4.4

(
[
3
, Theorem 8.17]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Nil}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime})
and

.
Suppose that

-bounded

.

When

Proof (outline).

The starting assertion is the following: there exists a good filtration
{\mathcal{F}}
on
\Pi

{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
on
\check{\Theta}(\Pi)
, and a surjective

-module homomorphism

(4.4.6)

\check{\Theta}(\mathrm{Gr}(\Pi,{\mathcal{F}}))\rightarrow\mathrm{Gr}(\check{\Theta}(\Pi),{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}).

Here
\check{\Theta}(\sigma)
, for a
({\mathbb{C}}[{\mathfrak{p}}],{\mathbf{K}})
-module
\sigma

\sigma

which is naturally a

-module. This is shown by the technique of
[

Put

, which is an
{\mathcal{O}}
-bounded finitely generated

-module. By the regularity assumption of the orbit descent and Lemma

This implies that

Here and as before,

denotes the radical ideal of

corresponding to the closed subvariety

. Since

is
{\mathcal{O}}
-bounded, we conclude that

\check{\Theta}(\sigma)

{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}
-bounded. (See the definition in (

).) By (

(4.4.7)

\mathrm{AC}_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(\check{\Theta}(\sigma))\preceq\check{\vartheta}_{{\mathcal{O}}}^{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{AC}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\sigma)),\qquad\text{in ${\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})$}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\mathrm{AC}_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(\check{\Theta}(\Pi))

\displaystyle\check{\vartheta}_{{\mathcal{O}}}^{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{AC}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\sigma))\quad\qquad(\textrm{by \eqref{surAC}})

5.
Unitarity preservation and applications

In this section, the local field
{\mathrm{F}}
will again be
{\mathbb{R}}
. We will discuss a general approach on unitarity preservation (due to Barbasch, Ma, Sun and the author
[
3
]
), which refines the earlier approach by Li
[
44
]
via the
L^{p}
estimate. Other previous work related to the theme of this section include
[
45
]
and
[
21
]
. We remark that the important role of growth of matrix coefficients in unitary representation theory of classical groups was first elucidated by Howe in the context of his theory of rank in
[
26
]
.

We shall shift the notation, to facilitate uniform treatment. After complexfication, the standard module
V
and
V^{\prime}
will become classical spaces with signature
\mathsf{s}=(\star,p,q)
and
\mathsf{s}^{\prime}=(\star^{\prime},p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
, where
\star
and
\star^{\prime}
are Howe dual of each other. Recall that a classical space with signature
\mathsf{s}
comes with the structure data
({\mathbf{V}}_{\mathsf{s}},{\langle}\,,\,{\rangle}_{\mathsf{s}},J_{\mathsf{s}},L_{\mathsf{s}})
. Also the Howe dual

\star^{\prime}
of the label
\star
is defined as follows:

\star^{\prime}:=\widetilde{C},\ D,\ C,\ B,\ D^{*},\ \textrm{ or }\ C^{*}

respectively if

\star=B,\ C,\ D,\ \widetilde{C},\ C^{*},\ \textrm{ or }\ D^{*}.

We will use the subscript
\mathsf{s}
to reflect its dependence on the classical signature
\mathsf{s}
, likewise for
\mathsf{s}^{\prime}
.

For a Casselman-Wallach representation
\pi
of
G_{\mathsf{s}}
, put

\check{\Theta}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}(\pi):=(\omega_{\mathsf{s},\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}\widehat{\otimes}\pi)_{G_{\mathsf{s}}}\qquad(\textrm{the Hausdorff coinvariant space}).

This is a Casselman-Wallach representation of
G_{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}
.

5.1.
Growth of Casselman-Wallach representations

We have the complex Cartan decompsition:

{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathsf{s}}}=\mathfrak{k}_{{\mathsf{s}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathsf{s}}}.

Write
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathsf{s}}^{J_{\mathsf{s}}}
for the centralizer of
J_{\mathsf{s}}
in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathsf{s}}
, which is a real form of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathsf{s}}
. For the real group
G_{\mathsf{s}}
, we also have the Cartan decomposition:

G_{\mathsf{s}}=K_{\mathsf{s}}\cdot\exp({\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathsf{s}}^{J_{\mathsf{s}}}).

Definition 5.1.1
.

Denote by
\Psi_{\mathsf{s}}
the function on
G_{\mathsf{s}}
satisfying the following conditions:

•

it is both left and right
K_{\mathsf{s}}
-invariant;

•

for all
g\in\exp({\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathsf{s}}^{J_{\mathsf{s}}})
,

\Psi_{\mathsf{s}}(g)=\prod_{a}\left(\frac{1+a}{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where
a
runs over all eigenvalues of
g:{\mathbf{V}}_{\mathsf{s}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{V}}_{\mathsf{s}}
, counted with multiplicities.

Note that all the eigenvalues of
g\in\exp({\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathsf{s}}^{J_{\mathsf{s}}})
are positive real numbers, and come in pairs of the form
\{a,a^{-1}\}
. Thus
0<\Psi_{\mathsf{s}}(g)\leq 1
for all
g\in G_{\mathsf{s}}
.

Denote by
\Xi_{\mathsf{s}}
the bi-
K_{\mathsf{s}}
-invariant Harish-Chandra’s
\Xi
function on
G_{\mathsf{s}}
(
[
72
, Section 4.5]
).

Set

(5.1.2)

\nu_{\mathsf{s}}:=\begin{cases}\left|{\mathsf{s}}\right|,\quad&\textrm{if }\star\in\{C,\widetilde{C}\};\\
\left|{\mathsf{s}}\right|-1,\quad&\textrm{if }\star=C^{*};\\
\left|{\mathsf{s}}\right|-2,\quad&\textrm{if }\star\in\{B,D\};\\
\left|{\mathsf{s}}\right|-3,\quad&\textrm{if }\star=D^{*},\end{cases}

where

\left|{\mathsf{s}}\right|:=p+q,\qquad\qquad\text{for }\mathsf{s}=(\star,p,q).

By the well-known estimate of Harish-Chandra’s
\Xi
function (
[
72
, Theorem 4.5.3]
), we have

Lemma 5.1.3
.

There exists a real number
C_{\mathsf{s}}>0
such that

\Psi_{\mathsf{s}}^{\nu_{\mathsf{s}}}(g)\leq C_{\mathsf{s}}\cdot\Xi_{\mathsf{s}}(g)\quad\textrm{ for all $g\in G_{\mathsf{s}}$}.

Remark 5.1.4
.

For the classical group
G_{\mathsf{s}}
, the bi-
K_{\mathsf{s}}
-invariant function
\Psi_{\mathsf{s}}
may be considered as more basic than
\Xi_{\mathsf{s}}
.

For any Casselman-Wallach representation
\pi
of
G_{\mathsf{s}}
, write
\pi^{\vee}
for its contragredient representation, which is a Casselman-Wallach representation of
G_{\mathsf{s}}
equipped with a
G_{\mathsf{s}}
-invariant, non-degenerate, continuous bilinear map

{\langle}\,,\,{\rangle}:\pi\times\pi^{\vee}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}.

Recall that a bi-
K_{\mathsf{s}}
-invariant positive function
f
on
G_{\mathsf{s}}
is said to be of logarithmic growth if the function
x\mapsto f(\exp(x))
on
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathsf{s}}^{J_{\mathsf{s}}}
is of polynomial growth.

Definition 5.1.5
.

Let
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(1).

A positive function
\Psi
on
G_{\mathsf{s}}
is said to be
\nu
-bounded if there is
a bi-
K_{\mathsf{s}}
-invariant positive function
f
of logarithmic growth such that

\Psi(g)\leq f(g)\cdot\Psi_{{\mathbf{s}}}^{\nu}(g)\cdot\Xi_{\mathsf{s}}(g)\qquad\textrm{for all }g\in G_{\mathsf{s}}.

(2).

A Casselman-Wallach representation
\pi
of
G_{\mathsf{s}}
is said to be
\nu
-bounded if there
exist a
\nu
-bounded positive function
\Psi
on
G_{\mathsf{s}}
, and continuous seminorms
\left|{\,\cdot\,}\right|_{\pi}
and
\left|{\,\cdot\,}\right|_{\pi^{\vee}}
on
\pi
and
\pi^{\vee}
respectively such that

\left|{{\langle}g\cdot u,v{\rangle}}\right|\leq\Psi(g)\cdot\left|{u}\right|_{\pi}\cdot\left|{v}\right|_{\pi^{\vee}}

for all
u\in\pi
,
v\in\pi^{\vee}
, and
g\in G_{\mathsf{s}}
.

Remark 5.1.6
.

Since
0<\Psi_{\mathsf{s}}(g)\leq 1
, it is evident that a positive function
\Psi
on
G_{\mathsf{s}}
which is
\nu
-bounded is automatically
\mu
-bounded for every
\mu\leq\nu
. A similar statement holds for a Casselman-Wallach representation
\pi
of
G_{\mathsf{s}}
.

Lemma 5.1.7
.

For every real number
\nu>0
, and every bi-
K_{\mathsf{s}}
-invariant positive function
f
of logarithmic growth, the function
f\cdot\Psi_{\mathsf{s}}^{\nu}\cdot\Xi_{\mathsf{s}}^{2}
is integrable with respect to a Haar measure in
G_{\mathsf{s}}
. Consequently, if
a Casselman-Wallach representation
\pi
of
G_{\mathsf{s}}
is
\nu
-bounded for some
\nu>0
, then the integral

\int_{G_{\mathsf{s}}}{\langle}g\cdot u,v{\rangle}\cdot\Xi_{{\mathsf{s}}}(g){\mathrm{d}}\!g

is absolutely convergent for all
u,v\in\pi
.

Proof.

This follows from the integral formula for
G_{\mathsf{s}}
under the Cartan decomposition (see

[
72
, Lemma 2.4.2]
), as well as the estimate of Harish-Chandra’s
\Xi
function mentioned earlier.
∎

This follows from the proof of

Definition 5.2.2
.

5.3.
Temperedness as source of unitarity

For the notion of weakly containment of unitary representations, see
[
9
]
for example.

Lemma 5.3.1
.

Suppose that
\left|{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}\right|\geq\nu_{\mathsf{s}}
. Then as a unitary representation of
G_{\mathsf{s}}
,
\hat{\omega}_{\mathsf{s},\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}
is weakly contained
in the regular representation.

Proof.

This has been known to experts (see
[
44
, Theorem 3.2]
). Lemmas
5.1.3
and
5.2.1
imply that for a dense subspace of
\hat{\omega}_{\mathsf{s},\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}|_{G_{\mathsf{s}}}
, the diagonal matrix coefficients belong to
L^{2+\epsilon}(G_{{\mathsf{s}}})
, for every positive real number
\epsilon
.
Thus the lemma follows from
[
9
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Note that if
\star\in\{B,C^{*},D^{*}\}
,
\nu_{\mathsf{s}}
is odd, and
\left|{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}\right|
is even. Thus the smallest
\left|{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}\right|
such that
\hat{\omega}_{\mathsf{s},\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}
is weakly contained
in the regular representation is actually
\nu_{\mathsf{s}}+1
. Note that if
\star=\widetilde{C}
, a similar mismatch of parity occurs:
\nu_{\mathsf{s}}
is even and
\left|{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}\right|
is odd. Nevertheless, when
\left|{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}\right|\geq\nu_{\mathsf{s}}
, we may decompose
\mathsf{s}^{\prime}
into a direct sum
{\mathsf{s}}^{\prime}_{1}\oplus{\mathsf{s}}^{\prime}_{2}
as classical spaces, where
{\mathsf{s}}^{\prime}_{1}
is of type
B
, and
{\mathsf{s}}^{\prime}_{2}
is of type
D
such that
\left|{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}_{2}}\right|=\nu_{\mathsf{s}}
, and thus
\hat{\omega}_{\mathsf{s},\mathsf{s}^{\prime}_{2}}
is weakly contained
in the regular representation of
G_{\mathsf{s}}
. This turns out to be sufficient for our unitarity preservation argument (to follow shortly). If
\star\in\{C,D\}
, both
\nu_{\mathsf{s}}
and
\left|{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}\right|
are even. For these reasons, we introduce

(5.3.2)

\nu_{\mathsf{s}}^{+}:=\begin{cases}\nu_{\mathsf{s}},\quad&\textrm{if }\star\in\{C,D,\widetilde{C}\};\\
\nu_{\mathsf{s}}+1,\quad&\textrm{if }\star\in\{B,C^{*},D^{*}\}.\\
\end{cases}

Note that
\nu_{\mathsf{s}}^{+}
is the smallest even integer bigger than or equal to
\nu_{\mathsf{s}}
, in all cases.

The following definition is a slight variation of
5.2.2
.

Definition 5.3.3
.

A Casselman-Wallach representation of

is overconvergent for
\check{\Theta}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}
if
it is
\nu
-bounded for some
\nu>\nu_{\mathsf{s}}^{+}-\left|{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}}\right|
.

We will neeed the following positivity result for matrix coefficient integrals, due to Harris-Li-Sun.

(
[

)
.

Let

be a real reductive group with a maximal compact subgroup

and
\pi_{2}
be two unitary representations of

is weakly

(

) be vectors in

such that for all

the integral

\int_{G}{\langle}g\cdot u_{i},u_{j}{\rangle}\,\Xi_{G}(g){\mathrm{d}}\!g

is the bi-

5.4.

The goal of this subsection is to show that the upper bound of associate cycles in Theorem

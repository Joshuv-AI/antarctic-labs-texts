REPRESENTATION RINGS OF FUSION SYSTEMS
AND BRAUER CHARACTERS

Abstract




Thomas Lawrence

A
bstract
. Let
\sF
be a fusion system over a
p
-group
S
. We study the complex character ring
R_{\C}(\sF)
of
\sF
by applying techniques from modular character theory to
\sF
-stable characters. We use these techniques to investigate a conjecture posed by Jason Semeraro concerning the volume of
R_{\C}(\sF)
as a
\Z
-lattice. Proving it holds for all saturated fusion systems would allow for easy verification that a given set of linearly independent
\sF
-stable characters forms a
\Z
-basis of
R_{\C}(\sF)
. We prove that this conjecture holds for all non-exotic fusion systems and a weakened conjecture holds for all fusion systems. We also show that any minimal counter example must be indecomposable by describing the characters of a product of two fusion systems. As a byproduct of our proof method, we describe the modular character rings of
\sF
, provide analogues of the decomposition and Cartan matrices for
\sF
-stable characters, and give a method for decomposing the regular character of
S
into
\sF
-stable constituents.

A fusion system is a category designed to capture the local structure of a finite group at a prime. In more detail, a fusion system
\sF
over a
p
-group
S
is a category with objects
\text{Ob}(\sF)=\{P\colon P\leq S\}
and morphism sets
\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF}(P,Q)
consisting of injective group homomorphisms designed to mimic conjugation. For example, given a finite group
G
and
S\leq G
a
p
-subgroup, then the fusion system
realised by
G
, written
\sF_{S}(G)
, is the category with
\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF}(P,Q)=\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(P,Q):=\{c_{g}|_{P}\colon P^{g}\leq Q\}
, where
c_{g}
is the conjugation map induced by
g
. The loosest definition of a fusion system is too general for most purposes and it is standard to instead work with
saturated
fusion systems, which are fusion systems satisfying conditions ensuring that they behave like the fusion system
\sF_{S}(G)
with
S\in\text{Syl}_{p}(G)
. When
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
with
S\in\text{Syl}_{p}(G)
for some finite group
G
we say that
\sF
is
non-exotic
. This terminology correctly indicates that there exist
exotic
fusion systems, which are saturated fusion systems which are not of the form
\sF_{S}(G)
for any finite group
G
and
S\in\text{Syl}_{p}(G)
.

There has been recent interest (
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
, among others) in exploring the ring
R_{\C}(\sF)
of virtual complex
\sF
-stable characters of
S
, which by Proposition 3.11 of
[
14
]
is the Grothendieck completion of the semiring
R_{\C}^{+}(\sF)
of complex
\sF
-stable characters.

An element
\chi\in R_{\C}^{+}(\sF)
is
\sF
-indecomposable
if it is an atomic element of the additive monoid of
R_{\C}^{+}(\sF)
. The set
\text{Ind}(\sF)
of
\sF
-indecomposable characters, unlike the character theory of finite groups, need not be linearly independent;
\sF
-stable characters need not admit a
unique
decomposition into
\sF
-indecomposable constituents (see Example A.2 in
[
5
]
). Because of this fact, it is difficult to determine if a given set of
\sF
-indecomposable characters (or any set of virtual
\sF
-stable characters, for that matter) forms a
\Z
-basis of
R_{\C}(\sF)
. It is with this problem in mind that we formulate the main conjecture of this paper: given a
\Z
-basis
B
of
R_{\C}(\sF)
we may define the
\sF
-character table with respect to
B
:
X_{B}(\sF):=(\chi(x))_{\chi\in B,x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}
. Based on unpublished calculations, Jason Semeraro has conjectured the following:

Conjecture A
(Semeraro)
.

Let
\sF
be a fusion system on a
p
-group
S
and
B
be any
\Z
-basis of
R_{\C}(\sF)
. Writing
\text{fccl}(\sF)
for a set of fully
\sF
-centralised conjugacy class representatives we have

|\mathrm{det}(X_{B}(\sF))|^{2}=\displaystyle\prod_{x\in\text{fccl}(\sF)}|C_{S}(x)|.

This invariant is independent of our choice of basis (c.f. Lemma
3.5
) and in fact characterises
\Z
-bases of
R_{\C}(\sF)
via elementary lattice theory, where this determinant corresponds to the square of the volume of
R_{\C}(\sF)
(viewed as a
\Z
-lattice embedded in
\C^{|\text{cl}(\sF)|}
). Hence, if this conjecture holds, we can verify if some
B\subseteq R_{\C}(\sF)
is a basis by computing the determinant of
X_{B}(\sF)
.

In this paper we show that this conjecture holds in a large variety of examples:

Theorem A
(Proposition
1.12
, Theorem
4.13
, Corollary
4.16
, Theorem
2.5
)
.

Conjecture A holds when
\sF
is transitive or
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
with
S\in\text{Syl}_{p}(G)
for some finite group
G
. Additionally, if Conjecture A holds for
\sF_{1},\sF_{2}
then it holds for
\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}
.

We also prove the following weaker result that holds for all fusion systems, regardless of saturation:

Theorem B
(Corollary
4.16
)
.

Given any fusion system
\sF
on a
p
-group
S
,
|\mathrm{det}(X_{B}(\sF))|^{2}
is a power of
p
.

To prove these theorems we exploit a symmetry between
\sF
-stable characters for
\sF
realised by a group
G
and Brauer characters (characters of modular representations) of
G
.
For some prime
p
we will write the
p
-Brauer characters
of
G
to refer to the characters of
p
-modular representations of
G
. We omit
p
if the specific prime is unimportant. We refer the reader to Chapter 2 of
[
7
]
for the necessary background on these objects in the context of modular representation theory.

In some heuristic sense the
\sF
-stable characters behave as if they were the
p^{\prime}
-Brauer characters of some group
G
. This connection is made clear when
|G|=p^{a}q^{b}
and
\sF
is the fusion system realised by
G
over a Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
: the
\sF
-indecomposable characters are exactly the restrictions to
S
of irreducible
q
-Brauer characters of
G
(see Proposition
3.2
).

We may frame these results as a generalisation of the theory of
\pi
-partial characters (see Chapter 3 in
[
11
]
) in the case that
\pi
is every prime dividing
|G|
except
p
. The theory in
[
11
]
only holds for
\pi
-separable groups, but our methods will work for any group. After the release of the original version of this preprint the author was informed by Benjamin Sambale that these constructions are very reminiscent of unpublished work by Olsson in
[
12
]
.

As part of our proof of Theorem A we describe the ring of
\sF
-stable Brauer characters:

Theorem C
(Proposition
4.7
, Theorem
4.11
, Corollary.
4.12
)
.

Let
\sF
be any fusion system on a
p
-group
S
,
\ell
a prime. Let
R_{\overline{\F}_{\ell}}(\sF)
be the ring of
\sF
-stable
\ell
-Brauer characters and
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
the maximal ideal of the algebraic integers
\mathcal{R}
containing
\ell
. If
B
is a
\Z
-basis for
R_{\C}(\sF)
, then
\pi_{\ell}(B)
is a
\F_{p}
-basis for
R_{\ell}(\sF)

We use this theorem to prove Theorem A by utilising the isomorphism between representation rings to show that the rows of
X_{B}(\sF)
are linearly independent mod
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
for all primes
\ell\neq p
.
Combined with the fact that
|\mathrm{det}(X_{B}(\sF))|^{2}\in\Z
(see Corollary
4.2
), we have that
|\mathrm{det}(X_{B}(\sF))|^{2}
is a power of
p
.
The refinement of this result to the full statement of Conjecture A for non-exotic fusion systems is where the parallels with modular character theory are fully utilised.

As a byproduct of our proof method, we are able to elaborate on a recent conjecture (Conjecture 2.18 in
[
6
]
), which asks if all
\sF
-indecomposable characters appear as subcharacters of the regular character
\rho_{S}
of
S
.
This was shown to be false in
[
8
]
, but we prove that the decomposition of
\rho_{S}
with respect to a
\Z
-basis
B
of
R_{\C}(\sF)
is still well behaved:

Theorem D
(Proposition
3.14
)
.

Let
\rho_{S}
denote the regular character of
S
. Let
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
for some
G
and
B
a basis for
R_{\C}(\sF)
. For
\chi\in\text{Irr}(G)
we write the decomposition of
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(\chi)
over
B
as
\sum_{\psi\in B}d^{B,G}_{\chi\psi}\psi
for some
d^{B,G}_{\chi\psi}\in\Z
.
Let
\Phi^{B,G}_{\psi}:=\sum_{\chi\in\text{Irr}(G)}d^{B,G}_{\chi\psi}\chi\in R_{\C}(G)
, then we have

\rho_{S}=\sum_{\psi\in B}\frac{\Phi^{B,G}_{\psi}(1)}{[G:S]}\psi

and these coefficients are integers.

I - A brief overview of
\sF
-stable characters

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
H,K\leq G
. For
g\in G
we write
c_{g}
for the automorphism of
G
induced by conjugation by
g
. If we let

\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(H,K):=\{\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(H,K)\colon\phi=c_{g}|_{H}\text{ for some }g\in G\},

then we may define a
fusion system
\sF
over a
p
-group
S
to be a category with
\text{Ob}(\sF)=\{P\colon P\leq S\}
and morphism sets
\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF}(P,Q)
consisting of injective group homomorphisms such that

\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(P,Q)\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF}(P,Q)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall P,Q\leq S.

Furthermore, each
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF}(P,Q)
decomposes as an isomorphism followed by an inclusion.

If
\text{Iso}_{\sF}(P,Q)\neq\varnothing
we say that
P
and
Q
are
\sF
-conjugate
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\sF
be a fusion system on
S
. We say
\sF
is realised by
G
, written as
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
, if
S\leq G
and for all
P,Q\leq S

\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF}(P,Q)=\{\phi\colon P\rightarrow Q\colon\exists x\in G\text{ such that }\phi=c_{x}|_{P}\}.

If there exists a
G
with
S\in\text{Syl}_{p}(G)
such that
\sF
is realised by
G
, then
\sF
is
non-exotic
, and exotic if such a
G
does not exist and
\sF
is saturated.

Lemma 1.3

(Thm. 3 in
[
9
]
)
.

For any fusion system
\sF
on
S
, there exists some finite group
G
such that
\sF
is realised by
G
.

For most purposes the above definition of a fusion system is too vague and it is common to impose the
saturation conditions
and say that a fusion system satisfying them is
saturated
. These conditions are designed so that saturated fusion systems are virtually indistinguishable from the following family of examples:

Example 1.4

(Thm. I.2.3 in
[
1
]
)
.

Given a finite group
G
and
S\in\text{Syl}_{p}(G)
, the fusion system
\sF_{S}(G)
is saturated.

The precise details of these conditions are not important to us and we refer the curious reader to Chapter I.2 of
[
1
]
for more information. Alperin’s fusion theorem tells us that all morphisms in a saturated fusion system arise as compositions of restrictions of
\sF
-automorphisms of
essential subgroups
of
S
, together with
S
itself. (see Chapter I.3 of
[
1
]
). If
\sF
is saturated and has no essential subgroups, we say that
\sF
is essential rank
0
.

Definition 1.5
.

Given an
s\in S
, the
\sF
-conjugacy class of
s
is the set

s^{\sF}:=\{s^{\prime}\in S\colon\exists P,Q\text{ s.t. }\exists\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF}(P,Q)\text{ with }\phi(s^{\prime})=s\}.

We will write
\text{cl}(\sF)
for the set of
\sF
-conjugacy classes. If
x
satisfies
|C_{S}(x)|\geq|C_{S}(y)|
for all
y\in x^{\sF}
we say that
x
is
fully
\sF
-centralised
and write
\text{fccl}(\sF)
for an arbitrary set of fully
\sF
-centralised
\sF
-conjugacy class representatives.

Lemma 1.6

(Lem. I.1.2a in
[
1
]
)
.

If
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
with
S\in\text{Syl}_{p}(G)
then
x\in\text{fccl}(\sF)\iff C_{S}(x)\in\text{Syl}_{p}(C_{G}(x))
.

Proof.

We note that
C_{X}(x)=C_{X}(\langle x\rangle)
for
X\in\{S,G\}
and apply Lemma I.1.2a of
[
1
]
.
∎

We will now begin expositing the required background on
\sF
-stable characters and the ring
R_{\C}(\sF)
.

Definition 1.7
.

For a finite group
G
and a field
k
, we define
R_{k}^{+}(G)
to be the
semiring of characters (of
k
-representations) of
G
, and
R_{k}(G)
to be the
ring of (virtual) characters (of
k
-representations) of
G
, which is the Grothendieck completion of
R_{k}^{+}(G)
. We will also write
\text{cf}(G)
for the set of complex valued class functions on
G
.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
f\in\text{cf}(S)
. Then
f
is
\sF
-stable
if for each
P\leq S
and
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF}(P,S)
,
f|_{P}=f|_{Q}\circ\phi
. We write
\text{cf}(\sF)
for the set of
\sF
-stable class functions on
S
.

By Lemma 1.3 in
[
5
]
, these
\sF
-stable class functions are precisely the class functions
f
such that
f(x)=f(y)
for all
x\in y^{\sF}
.

Since characters of
S
are class functions, this definition of
\sF
-stability may be applied. It is clear that
\sF
-stability is preserved under product and addition, so the non-virtual
\sF
-stable characters from a subsemiring of non-virtual characters of
S
.
Given a field
k
, we will denote the semiring of
\sF
-stable characters (of
k
-representations) as
R_{k}^{+}(\sF)
.
We call the Grothendieck completion of
R_{k}^{+}(\sF)
the ring of (virtual)
\sF
-stable characters (of
k
-representations) and denote it as
R_{k}(\sF)
. By Proposition 3.11 of
[
14
]

R_{k}(\sF)
is also the subring of
\sF
-stable virtual characters of
S
.

The following elementary lemma will be useful in the subsequent sections and provides a good family of examples of
\sF
-stable characters:

Lemma 1.9
.

If
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
for some
G
, then for any
\chi\in R_{k}(G)
,
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(\chi)\in R_{k}(\sF)
.

Proof.

Clearly
\text{Res}^{G}_{S}(\chi)\in R_{k}(S)
, all that remains is to show that
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(\chi)
is
\sF
-stable. Because
\sF
is realised by
G
,
\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF}(P,Q)=\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(P,Q)
so
s^{\prime}\in s^{\sF}\iff s^{\prime}\in s^{G}
for all
s,s^{\prime}\in S
. Since
\chi\in\text{cl}(G)
, we have that for any two
s,s^{\prime}\in S
with
s^{\prime}\in s^{G}
then
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(\chi)(s)=\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(\chi)(s^{\prime})
.
∎

We end by discussing the
\sF
-indecomposables, which act as the irreducible characters for fusion systems.

Definition 1.10
.

If
\chi\in R_{\C}^{+}(\sF)
cannot be written as a sum of two other elements in
R_{\C}^{+}(\sF)
we say that
\chi
is
\sF
-indecomposable
. We write the set of
\sF
-indecomposable characters as
\text{Ind}(\sF)
.

Much like characters of groups, any
\sF
-stable character can be written as a
\Z
-linear combination of indecomposables, and any
\sF
-stable class function may be written as a
\C
-linear combination.

Proposition 1.11

(Lem. 2.1, Cor 2.2 of
[
3
]
)
.

We have
\langle\text{Ind}(\sF)\rangle_{\Z}=R_{\C}(\sF)
and
\langle\text{Ind}(\sF)\rangle_{\C}=\text{cf}(\sF)
. Viewed as free abelian group, the rank of
R_{\C}(\sF)
is
|\text{cl}(\sF)|
.

Unfortunately, in contrast to ordinary character theory of groups, each
\sF
-stable character need not factor
uniquely
into a sum of
\sF
-indecomposables, requiring us to instead fix a
\Z
-basis
B
of
R_{\C}(\sF)
as opposed to assuming that
\text{Ind}(\sF)
is linearly independent. We define the
\sF
-character table with respect to
B
as the matrix
(X_{B}(\sF))_{\psi\in B,x\in\text{fccl}(\sF)}=\psi(x)
.

Proposition 1.12
.

If
\sF
is transitive (
|\text{cl}(\sF)|=2
), then Conjecture A holds.

Proof.

By Lemma 2.18 of
[
5
]
the
\sF
-indecomposables are the trivial character
1_{S}
of
S
and
\rho_{S}-1_{S}
, hence
X_{\text{Ind}(\sF)}(\sF)
is

\begin{array}[]{|c||c|c|}\hline\cr\text{Ind}(\sF)&1&z\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr 1_{S}&1&1\\
\rho_{S}-1_{S}&|S|-1&-1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

As
S
is a
p
-group we have
|\mathcal{Z}(S)|>1
hence a fully
\sF
-centralised representative of the non-trivial
\sF
-conjugacy classes is central in
S
, yielding

\prod_{x\in\text{fccl}(\sF)}|C_{S}(x)|=|C_{S}(1)||C_{S}(z)|=S^{2}=|\mathrm{det}(X_{B}(\sF))|^{2}.

∎

II - The representation ring of a product of fusion systems.

In this section we will show that if
\sF
is a minimal counterexample to Conjecture A,
\sF
cannot be a product
\sF=\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}
of two strictly smaller fusion systems.

Definition 2.1

(Thm. I.6.6 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\sF_{i}
be fusion systems over
p
-groups
S_{i}
for
i=1,2
, then
the product fusion system

\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}
is a fusion system over
S_{1}\times S_{2}
with

\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}}(P,Q)=\{(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})|_{P}\colon\phi_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\sF_{i}}(P_{i},Q_{i}),(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})(P)\leq Q\}

Such that
P,Q\leq S_{1}\times S_{2}
and
P_{i},Q_{i}
denoting the projections of
P,Q
to
S_{i}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
(p_{1},p_{2})\in(q_{1},q_{2})^{\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}}\iff p_{1}\in{q_{1}}^{\sF_{1}}
and
p_{2}\in{q_{2}}^{\sF_{2}}
.

Proof.

By definition, if there is an
\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}
-isomorphism sending
(p_{1},p_{2})\mapsto(q_{1},q_{2})
we may assume it is of the form
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\text{Iso}_{\sF_{1}}(P_{1},Q_{1})\times\text{Iso}_{\sF_{2}}(P_{2},Q_{2})

where
p_{i}\in P_{i}\leq S_{i},q_{i}\in Q_{i}\leq S_{i}
. So
p_{i}\in{q_{i}}^{\sF_{1}}
with
\phi_{i}
being a map in
\sF_{i}
such that
\phi_{i}(p_{i})=q_{i}
.

If we now assume
p_{i}\in{q_{i}}^{\sF_{i}}
with
\phi_{i}
being the isomorphism mapping
p_{i}
to
q_{i}
. Define
P_{i},Q_{i}
as before,
then
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\text{Iso}_{\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}}((P_{1},P_{2}),(Q_{1},Q_{2}))
, so
(p_{1},p_{2})\in(q_{1},q_{2})^{\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\sF_{i}
be a fusion system on
S_{i}
for
i=1,2
, then
(x_{1},x_{2})
is fully
\sF=\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}
-centralised if and only if
x_{i}
is fully
\sF_{i}
-centralised.

Proof.

It is clear that
|C_{S_{1}\times S_{2}}((x_{1},x_{2}))|=|C_{S_{1}}(x_{1})\times C_{S_{2}}(x_{2})|
. So if
x_{i}
is fully
\sF_{i}
-centralised, then

|C_{S_{1}\times S_{2}}((x_{1},x_{2}))|=|C_{S_{1}}(x_{1})||C_{S_{2}}(x_{2})|\geq|C_{S_{1}}(x_{1}^{\prime})||C_{S_{2}}(x_{2}^{\prime})|=|C_{S_{1}\times S_{2}}((x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime}))|

for all
x_{i}^{\prime}\in x_{i}^{\sF}
. Because
(x_{1},x_{2})^{\sF}=x_{1}^{\sF_{1}}\times x_{2}^{\sF_{2}}
by Lemma
2.2
, we have our result.
∎

We now describe
R_{\C}(\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2})
:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}
be a product fusion system on
S_{1}\times S_{2}
and
k
a field, then
\chi_{i}\in R_{k}(\sF_{i})
, then
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\in R_{k}(\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2})
.

Proof.

We first assume
\chi_{i}
are
\sF_{i}
-stable. If
(p_{1},p_{2})\in(q_{1},q_{2})^{\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}}
then
p_{1}\in q_{1}^{\sF_{1}}
and
p_{2}\in q_{2}^{\sF_{2}}
.
Because
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
are
\sF_{1}
-stable and
\sF_{2}
-stable respectively, we have
\chi(p_{1},p_{2})=\chi_{1}(p_{1})\chi_{2}(p_{2})=\chi_{1}(q_{1})\chi_{2}(q_{2})=\chi(q_{1},q_{2})
. Thus
\chi
is
\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}
-stable.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

For any fusion systems
\sF_{1},\sF_{2}
over
p
-groups
S_{1},S_{2}
then, as commutative monoids and abelian groups respectively

\displaystyle R_{\C}^{+}(\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2})

\displaystyle\cong R_{\C}^{+}(\sF_{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{N}}R_{\C}^{+}(\sF_{2})

\displaystyle R_{\C}(\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2})

\displaystyle\cong R_{\C}(\sF_{1})\otimes_{\Z}R_{\C}(\sF_{2}).

Furthermore, if Conjecture A holds for
\sF_{1}
and
\sF_{2}
, it holds for
\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2

|\text{cl}(\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2})|=|\text{cl}(\sF_{1})|\times|\text{cl}(\sF_{2})|
and so the claimed isomorphisms exist by Theorem
1.11
and comparing ranks. As all representation rings involved are free, it follows from Proposition 1.10.1 of
[
15
]
that the volume of

as a
\Z
-lattice is
\text{Vol}(R_{\C}(\sF_{1}))^{|\text{cl}(\sF_{2})|}\text{Vol}(R_{\C}(\sF_{2}))^{|\text{cl}(\sF_{1})|}
. If
B
is a basis for
\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}
then by our assumption that
\sF_{1}
and
\sF_{2}
satisfy Conjecture A we have

\displaystyle|\mathrm{det}(X_{B}(\sF))|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{x\in\text{fccl}(\sF_{1})}|C_{S_{1}}(x)|\right)^{|\text{cl}(\sF_{1})|}\left(\prod_{y\in\text{fccl}(\sF_{2})}(|C_{S_{2}}(y)|\right)^{|\text{cl}(\sF_{2})|}

\displaystyle=\prod_{x\in\text{cl}(\sF_{1})}\prod_{y\in\text{cl}(\sF_{2})}|C_{S_{1}}(x)||C_{S_{2}}(y)|

\displaystyle=\prod_{x\in\text{fccl}(\sF_{1})}\prod_{y\in\text{fccl}(\sF_{2})}|C_{S_{1}\times S_{2}}(x,y))|.

Applying Lemma
2.3
yields

\prod_{x\in\text{fccl}(\sF_{1})}\prod_{y\in\text{fccl}(\sF_{2})}|C_{S_{1}\times S_{2}}((x,y))|=\prod_{z\in\text{fccl}(\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2})}|C_{S_{1}\times S_{2}}(z)|

and the result is shown.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

If
\sF:=\sF_{1}\times\sF_{2}
and

is a basis for
R_{\C}(\sF_{i})
, then

is a basis for

and

Proof.

Writing

for the set of eigenvalues of

with multiplicity, then

, hence

It then follows that

III - An Inverted Brauer Character Theory

In unpublished notes
[
12
]
Olsson generalised
p
-Brauer characters to
\pi
-Brauer characters for a
set
of primes
\pi
which are class functions on the conjugacy classes of
\pi^{\prime}
-elements of
G
. We will investigate the connection between fusion systems over a
p
-group and
p^{\prime}
-Brauer characters (the case where
\pi=\{q\text{ prime }\colon q\neq p\})
, providing a number of proofs for this specific case not given in
[
12
]
. We refer the reader to Chapter 2 of
[
7
]
for all of the required background on Brauer character theory.

Lemma 3.1

(Thm. 2.12 in
[
7
]
, Thm. 43.ii in
[
10
]
)
.

Let
q
be a prime and
G
be a finite group. Then there is a ring homomorphism
L\colon R_{\overline{\F}_{q}}(G)\rightarrow R_{\C}(G)
sending
\psi\in R_{\overline{\F}_{q}}(G)
to the class function
L(\psi)\colon\text{cl}(G)\rightarrow\C
such that
L(\psi)(g)=\psi(g_{q^{\prime}})
, where
g_{q^{\prime}}
is the
q^{\prime}
-part of
g\in G
. Moreover, if
(|G|,q)=1
then this map is an isomorphism sending
\text{IBr}_{q}(G)\rightarrow\text{Irr}(G)
.

We refer
L
as the
Brauer lifting
map and
L(\psi)
as the
Brauer lift
of
\psi
. The theory described in this section was originally inspired by the following observation:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
|G|=p^{a}q^{b}
,
S\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(G)
and
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
. Write
\text{IBr}_{q}(G)
for the set of irreducible
q
-Brauer characters of
G
. Then writing
\text{IBr}_{q}(G)|_{S}:=\{\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{G}(\psi)\colon\psi\in\text{IBr}_{q}(G)\}
,
\text{Ind}(\sF)=L(\text{IBr}_{q}(G)|_{S})
.

Proof.

Note that
\text{cl}_{q^{\prime}}(G)=\text{cl}_{p}(G)
and
\text{cl}(\sF)=\{K\cap S\colon K\in\text{cl}_{p}(G)\}
. Because
S
is a
q^{\prime}
-group, by Lemma
3.1
the Brauer lift
L\colon R_{\overline{\F}_{q}}(S)\rightarrow R_{\C}(S)
is an isomorphism, thus
L(\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{G}(\psi))=\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{G}(L(\psi))\in R_{\C}(S)
for
\psi\in\text{IBr}_{q}(G)
. Furthermore
\psi
is invariant on
\text{cl}_{q^{\prime}}(G)=\text{cl}_{p}(G)
so we have
L(\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{G}(\psi))\in R_{\C}(\sF)
.

Let
\chi\in\text{Ind}(\sF)
. We note that
\chi=\text{Res}^{G}_{S}(\psi)
for some non-virtual
q
-Brauer character
\psi
of
G
, and by Lemma
3.1

L
restricts to a bijection
\text{IBr}_{q}(S)\rightarrow\mathrm{Irr}(S)
and that lifts of non-virtual
q
-Brauer characters of
S
are non-virtual characters.

Suppose we can write
\chi=L(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}))=L(\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{G}(\psi_{1}))+L(\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{G}(\psi_{2}))
for two
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\text{IBr}_{q}(G)
. This immediately contradicts the
\sF
-indecomposability of
\chi
as
L(\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{G}(\psi_{i}))\in R^{+}_{\C}(\sF)
for
i=1,2
by the previous paragraph. Thus
\chi=L(\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{G}(\psi))
for some
\psi\in\text{IBr}_{q}(G)
and by injectivity of
L
we are done.
∎

This result motivates the following definitions:

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\sF
be a fusion system over
S
,
B
a basis of
R_{\C}(\sF)
and
G
a finite group realising
\sF
. For each
\chi\in\text{Irr}(G)
we have a decomposition

\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(\chi)=\sum_{\psi\in B}d_{\chi\psi}^{B,G}\psi

and we refer to the coefficients
d_{\chi\psi}^{B,G}
as the
decomposition numbers
. We collect these coefficients in the
decomposition matrix of
\sF
(with respect to
B
and
G
)
:

(D_{B,G}(\sF))_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G),\psi\in B}:=d_{\chi\psi}.

We also define
Cartan matrix of
\sF
(with respect to
B
and
G
)
to be

C_{B,G}(\sF):=D_{B,G}(\sF)^{T}D_{B,G}(\sF).

The Cartan matrix is indexed by
\psi,\mu\in B
.
We define the
Cartan numbers

c^{B,G}_{\psi\mu}:=C_{B,G}(\sF)_{\psi\mu}
.

The following definition mirrors the definition of the projective indecomposable characters from Brauer character theory, which are the characters of the projective covers of the simple representation corresponding to an irreducible Brauer character (c.f. Section 18.3 of
[
10
]
).

Definition 3.4
.

For
\psi\in B
, we define the associated

\displaystyle\Phi^{B,G}_{\psi}:=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}d^{B,G}_{\chi\psi}\chi.

We set
(P_{B,G})_{\psi\in B,x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}:=\Phi^{B,G}_{\psi}(x)
and write
\mathrm{Orth}_{G,B}(\sF):=\{\Phi^{B,G}_{\psi}\colon\psi\in B\}
.

For the purpose of investigating Conjecture A we show that we may fix an arbitrary choice for B:

Lemma 3.5

(Page 10 of
[
13
]
)
.

For any two
\Z
-bases

of a free
\Z
-module

with associated matrices

, then
|\mathrm{det}(X_{B})|=|\mathrm{det}(X_{B}^{\prime})|

Proof.

Given two bases

of

such that

, hence
\mathrm{det}(M)\mathrm{det}(X_{B})=\pm\mathrm{det}(X_{B})=\mathrm{det}(X_{B^{\prime}})

.
∎

We will now fix both

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)

\text{Orth}(\sF)\subset R_{\C}(G)

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{G}(\chi)\in R_{\C}^{+}(\sF)
for all

\Phi_{\psi}\in R_{\C}(G)

Lemma 3.7

(Lem. 6.2 in
[
12
]
)

Let

be the character table of
\sF
(with respect to
B

\overline{P^{T}}X(\sF)=\Delta

are both of full rank.

Proof.

Let

,

, then:

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in B}\overline{\Phi_{\psi}(g)}\psi(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\psi\in B}\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}d_{\chi\psi}\overline{\chi(g)}\psi(s)=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\overline{\chi(g)}\sum_{\psi\in B}d_{\chi\psi}\overline{\psi(s)}=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\overline{\chi(g)}\chi(s)

\displaystyle(\overline{P^{T}}X(\sF))_{x^{\sF},y^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}

We know that

|\text{cl}(\sF)|=\text{rk}(\Delta)\leq\text{min}(\text{rk}(P),\text{rk}(X(\sF)))\leq|\text{cl}(\sF)|\Rightarrow\text{rk}(P)=\text{rk}(X(\sF))=|\text{cl}(\sF)|.

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

\Phi_{\psi}(g)=0

Let

.

Proposition 3.11
.

We have

\text{Irr}(G)

.
Therefore, the decomposition matrix has exactly one non-zero entry in each row. Therefore the Cartan matrix is diagonal with

c_{\psi\psi}=\displaystyle\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{Irr}(S\rtimes W)}d_{\nu\psi}^{2}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{Irr}(S\rtimes W)}\langle\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{S\rtimes W}(\nu),\chi_{\psi}\rangle^{2}.

We will now determine
\mathrm{Orth}(\sF)
. By Proposition
3.14
we have

\sum_{\chi_{\psi}\in\text{Irr}(S)/W}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\psi}(1)}{|W|}\sum_{w\in\tau}\chi_{\phi}^{w}\right)=\sum_{\chi_{\psi}\in\text{Irr}(S)/W}\left(\chi(1)\sum_{w\in\tau}\chi_{\phi}^{w}\right).

Together with linear independence of irreducible characters we have
\Phi_{\psi}(1)=|W|\chi_{\psi}(1)=\text{Ind}_{S}^{S\rtimes W}(\chi_{\psi})(1)
. Next, for
\nu\in\text{Irr}(S\rtimes W)
and
\mu\in\text{Irr}(S)
such that
\langle\mathrm{Res}^{S\rtimes W}_{S}(\nu),\mu\rangle>0
we note that by Corollary
3.12
and Frobenius reciprocity respectively, we have

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{\psi},\nu\rangle\neq 0\iff\chi_{\psi}\in\mu^{\mathrm{Out}_{\sF}(S)}

\displaystyle\langle\text{Ind}_{S}^{S\rtimes W}(\chi_{\psi}),\nu\rangle\neq 0\iff\chi_{\psi}\in\mu^{\mathrm{Out}_{\sF}(S)}.

Finally, the Cartan matrix being diagonal together with Proposition
3.11
implies that
\mathrm{Orth}(\sF)
is orthogonal and no two associated orthogonal characters have any
S\rtimes W
-indecomposable constituents in common. Hence by Corollary
3.9
,
\text{Ind}_{S}^{S\rtimes W}(\chi_{\psi})
lies in
\langle\Phi_{\psi}\rangle_{\Q}
, but because they have equal degrees we have
\Phi_{\psi}=\text{Ind}_{S}^{S\rtimes W}(\chi_{\psi})
and we are done.
∎

We conclude this subsection with two remarks.

Remark 3.16
.

It is very common that each
S\rtimes W
-irreducible

or

. When this occurs the Cartan numbers are

by the method of little groups (c.f. Section 8.2 of
[

) and the fact that

is a

-group.

Remark 3.17
.

Secondly, in many observed cases when
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
and

we have
\Phi_{\psi}=\mathrm{Ind}_{S}^{G}(\psi)
, but this is not always so: Let

\begin{array}[]{|c||c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr\sF_{C_{3}\wr C_{3}}(A_{9})&1&3&3^{2}&3^{3}&9_{1}&9_{2}\\

IV - The proof of Theorems A, B, C

We will now begin applying our theory to first relate the determinant of the character table
X(\sF)
to the determinant of the Cartan matrix
C(\sF)
and then prove that the latter is coprime to
p
, allowing us to reduce the full fusion volume conjecture down to verifying
|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))^{2}|
is a power of
p
.

Lemma 4.1

(Thm. 6.9 in
[
12
]
)
.

For any fusion system
\sF
over
S
with
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
we have that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}=\frac{\prod_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}|C_{G}(x)|}{\mathrm{det}(C(\sF))}.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
and
K\in\text{cl}(\sF)
, then:

(D(\sF)X(\sF))_{\chi,x^{\sF}}=\sum_{\mu\in B}D(\sF)_{\chi\mu}X(\sF)_{\mu,x^{\sF}}=\sum_{\mu\in B}d_{\chi\mu}\mu(x)=\text{Res}_{S}^{G}(\chi)(x).

So writing
x_{1},...,x_{|\text{cl}(\sF)|}
for a set of
\sF
-conjugacy class representatives, by Lemma
3.7
, we have that
(\overline{(D(\sF)X(\sF))^{T}}D(\sF)X(\sF))_{ij}=\delta_{ij}|C_{G}(x_{j})|
. Therefore

\mathrm{det}(\overline{(D(\sF)X(\sF))^{T}}D(\sF)X(\sF))=\prod_{i=1}^{|\text{cl}(\sF)|}|C_{G}(x_{i})|.

Finally

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(\overline{(D(\sF)X(\sF))^{T}}D(\sF)X(\sF))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{det}(\overline{D(\sF)^{T}}D(\sF))\mathrm{det}(\overline{X(\sF)^{T}}X(\sF))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{det}(D(\sF)^{T}D(\sF))|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{det}(C(\sF))|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{|\text{cl}(\sF)|}|C_{G}(x_{i})|}{\mathrm{det}(C(\sF))}.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}
is an integer.

Proof.

Since
X(\sF)
is a matrix of complex character values,
X(\sF)\in M_{|\text{cl}(\sF)|}(\mathcal{R})
where
\mathcal{R}\subset\C
is the ring of algebraic integers.
So by Lemma
4.1
and the fact that the Cartan numbers are integers we have
|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}\in\Q\cap\mathcal{R}=\Z
.
∎

We will now prove that
(\mathrm{det}(C(\sF)),p)=1
. We note that by Corollary 2.18 in
[
7
]
, the Cartan matrix of
p
-Brauer characters has determinant equal to a power of
p
.

Lemma 4.3
.

C(\sF)^{-1}
is the matrix defined by
\displaystyle(C(\sF)^{-1})_{\psi,\mu\in B}:=\sum_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}\frac{\psi(x)\overline{\mu(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}
.

Proof.

Write
C^{\prime}
for the matrix
\displaystyle(C^{\prime})_{\psi,\mu\in B}:=\sum_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}\frac{\psi(x)\overline{\mu(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}
. Then

\displaystyle(C^{\prime}C(\sF))_{\mu\psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta\in B}C^{\prime}_{\mu\theta}C(\sF)_{\theta\psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta\in B}\sum_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}\frac{\mu(x)\overline{\theta(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}c_{\theta\psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}\frac{\mu(x)\sum_{\theta\in B}c_{\theta\psi}\overline{\theta(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}\frac{\mu(x)\overline{\mathrm{Res}_{S}^{G}(\Phi_{\psi})(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ (applying Corollary \ref{proj restriction}).}

Writing
\Delta:=\text{Diag}_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}(|C_{G}(x)|)
, then we know from Lemma
3.7
that

X(\sF)\overline{P^{T}}=\Delta\Rightarrow(X(\sF)\Delta^{-1}\overline{P^{T}})_{\mu,\psi}=\sum_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}\frac{\mu(x)\overline{\Phi_{\psi}(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}=\delta_{\mu,\psi}.

Since
x\in S
we may replace
\Phi_{\psi}
in the above expression with
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(\Phi_{\psi})
, hence
C^{\prime}C=I_{|\text{cl}(\sF)|}
and the result is proven.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
and
S\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(G)
, then
|G|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}C(\sF)^{-1}
is an integer-valued matrix.

Proof.

For
\chi\in R_{\C}(S)
we define
\tilde{\chi}
to be the class function

\tilde{\chi}(g):=\begin{cases}|G|_{p^{\prime}}\chi(g)&g\text{ is a $p$-element}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

then
\tilde{\chi}\in R_{\C}(G)
by Brauer’s characterisation of characters (c.f. Thm. 2.1 of
[
10
]
). Hence for any
\mu,\psi\in B
, we have
\langle\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\psi}\rangle_{G}\in\Z
.
Now since
\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\psi}
are
0
on anything that isn’t a
p
-element:

\langle\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\psi}\rangle_{G}=\sum_{x^{G}\in\text{cl}(G)}\frac{\tilde{\mu}(x)\overline{\tilde{\psi}(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{G}\in\text{cl}(G)\\
x^{G}\cap S\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\frac{\tilde{\mu}(x)\overline{\tilde{\psi}(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}.

All Sylow
p
-subgroups are conjugate in
G
, so we are free to choose our
G
-conjugacy class representatives
x
to lie in
S
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{G}\in\text{cl}(G)\\
x^{G}\cap S\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\frac{\tilde{\mu}(x)\overline{\tilde{\psi}(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}\frac{\tilde{\mu}(x)\overline{\tilde{\psi}(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}

\displaystyle=|G|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\sum_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}\frac{\mu(x)\overline{\psi(x)}}{|C_{G}(x)|}

\displaystyle=|G|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}(C(\sF)^{-1})_{\mu\psi}\in\Z

concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(Thm 6.7 in
[
12
]
)
.

If
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
with
S\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(G)
, then
\mathrm{det}(C)
is coprime to
p
.

Proof.

Using the notation from the proof of Lemma
4.4
, define
M
with
(M)_{\mu,\psi\in B}=\langle\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\psi}\rangle_{G}
,
which is
|G|^{2}_{p^{\prime}}C(\sF)^{-1}
by Lemma
4.4
.
So
C(\sF)M=|G|^{2}_{p^{\prime}}I_{|\text{cl}(\sF)|}
. Since
M
and
C(\sF)
are integral matrices,
\mathrm{det}(M),\mathrm{det}(C(\sF))\in\Z
.
Therefore

\mathrm{det}(C(\sF))=\frac{|G|_{p^{\prime}}^{2|\text{cl}(\sF)|}}{\mathrm{det}(M)}\in\Z\Longrightarrow\mathrm{det}(C(\sF))\text{ is coprime to }p.

∎

Following this result, Lemma
1.6
, and Corollary
4.2
, we have that

|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}_{p}=\frac{\prod_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}|C_{G}(x)|_{p}}{\mathrm{det}(C(\sF))_{p}}=\prod_{x\in\text{fccl}(\sF)}|C_{S}(x)|.

The final step is to show
|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}_{p}=|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}\iff|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}
is a power of
p
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\rho\colon G\rightarrow GL_{n}(\overline{\F_{p}})
be a
p
-modular representation of
G
. Let
G_{p^{\prime}}
be the set of
p^{\prime}
-elements of
G
. Then the
p
-Brauer character

\chi
associated to
\rho
is the map
G_{p^{\prime}}\rightarrow\overline{\F_{p}}^{\times}
given by

.

For a given prime
\ell
, we write
R^{+}_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF)
to denote the subsemiring of
R^{+}_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(S)
consisting of
\sF
-stable
\ell
-Brauer characters. As before, we write
R_{\overline{\F}_{\ell}}(\sF)
for the Grothendieck completion of
R^{+}_{\overline{\F}_{\ell}}(\sF)
. We will write cf
{}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
for the set of functions
\text{cl}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\rightarrow\overline{\F}_{p}
.

We will first take care of the easy case
\ell=p
.

Proposition 4.7
.

If
\sF
is a fusion system over a
p
-group
S
, then
R_{\overline{\F_{p}}}(\sF)=\langle 1_{S}\rangle
where
1_{S}
is the trivial
p
-Brauer character.

Proof.

By Corollary 2.10 in
[
7
]
, we have that
|\text{IBr}_{p}(G)|=|\text{cl}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|
. Because

is a

|\text{IBr}_{p}(S)|=|\text{cl}_{p^{\prime}}(S)|=1
, and so we have
\text{IBr}_{q}(S)=\{1_{S}\}
.
1_{S}
is constant thus
\sF
-stable and we are done.
∎

Our strategy is to show that
X(\sF)
is still full rank, thus invertible, “mod
\ell
” for all
\ell\neq p
. We begin by making this reduction mod
\ell

Definition 4.8
.

For a given prime
\ell
, let
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
be a maximal ideal of the algebraic integers

with
\ell\Z\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
.
Denote the canonical surjection
\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\cong\overline{\F_{\ell}}
by

.
Given a function

that maps into

, we abuse notation slightly and write

for the composition

.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
G
be a finite group. For
K\in\text{cl}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
, let
i_{K}\in cf(G)
be the indicator function for
K
.
Then
\pi_{\ell}(i_{K})|_{S}\in\langle\text{IBr}_{\ell}(G)|_{S}\rangle_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma 2.4 and Theorem 1.19 in
[
7
]
we have that
\text{IBr}_{q}(G)
is linearly independent over
\overline{\F_{\ell}}
. Thus
\text{rk}_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\langle\text{IBr}_{\ell}(G)\rangle_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}})=|\text{cl}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)|=\text{rk}_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\text{cf}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G))\Rightarrow\text{rk}_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\langle\text{IBr}_{\ell}(G)\rangle_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}})=\text{cl}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
, the result then follows upon restricting to

.
∎

Lemma 4.10

(Lem. 5.2.2 in
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\{f_{i}\colon R_{1}\rightarrow R_{2}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a set of ring homomorphisms with
R_{1}
a commutative ring and
R_{2}
an integral domain. If the
f_{i}
are distinct, they are linearly independent over
R_{2}
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\sF
be any fusion system over a
p
-group
S
. For
\ell\neq p
,
\text{rk}_{\F_{\ell}}(R_{\overline{\F}_{\ell}}(\sF))=|\text{cl}(\sF)|
.

Proof.

For any
s\in S
we define the evaluation map
e_{s}\colon R_{\overline{\F}_{\ell}}(\sF)\otimes\overline{\F_{\ell}}\rightarrow\overline{\F_{\ell}}
with
e_{s}(\psi)=\psi(s)
.
It is clear that these maps are ring homomorphisms.

Take a set of
\sF
-conjugacy class representatives
x_{K}
, with
x_{K}\in K\in\text{cl}(\sF)
, and consider the set
\mathscr{E}:=\{e_{x_{K}}\colon K\in\text{cl}(\sF)\}
.
We aim to show that each
e_{x_{K}}
is distinct, so
\mathscr{E}
is linearly independent over
\overline{\F_{\ell}}
by Lemma
4.10
.

By Corollary 2.10 in
[
7
]
, the
\ell
-Brauer characters of
S
form an
\overline{\F_{\ell}}
-basis for the class functions
S\rightarrow\overline{\F_{\ell}}
. Hence the indicator functions
\pi_{\ell}(i_{K})
are in
\overline{\F_{\ell}}\otimes_{\F_{\ell}}R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(S)
, hence
\pi_{\ell}(i_{K})\in\overline{\F_{\ell}}\otimes_{\F_{\ell}}R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF)
. We abuse notation and will also write
e_{x_{K}}
for the extension of
e_{x_{K}}
to
\overline{\F_{\ell}}\otimes_{\F_{\ell}}R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF)
.

Because
\pi_{\ell}(i_{K^{\prime}})
are indicator functions, we have
e_{x_{K}}(\pi_{\ell}(i_{K^{\prime}}))=\delta_{K,K^{\prime}}
for any

hence each
e_{x_{K}}
is distinct and
\mathscr{E}
is linearly independent over
\overline{\F_{\ell}}
by Lemma
4.10
.
Since
R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(S)
is a finite dimensional
\F_{\ell}
-algebra,
R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF)\otimes\overline{\F_{\ell}}
is a finite dimensional
\overline{\F_{\ell}}
-algebra, yielding

\text{rk}_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\langle\mathscr{E}\rangle)=|\text{cl}(\sF)|\leq\text{rk}_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\mathrm{Hom}(R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF)\otimes\overline{\F_{\ell}},\overline{\F_{\ell}}))=\text{rk}_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF)\otimes\overline{\F_{\ell}})=\text{rk}_{\F_{\ell}}(R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF)).

So
\text{rk}_{\F_{\ell}}(R_{\overline{\F}_{\ell}}(\sF))\geq|\text{cl}(\sF)|
.
We also have
R_{\overline{\F}_{\ell}}(\sF)\leq\langle\pi_{\ell}(i_{k})\colon K\in\text{cl}(\sF)\rangle
, which is rank

.
Therefore,
\text{rk}_{\F_{\ell}}(R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF))=|\text{cl}(\sF)|
as desired.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

\pi_{\ell}
induces a bijection
B\mapsto\pi_{\ell}(B)
and
\pi_{\ell}(B)
is linearly independent over
\overline{\F_{\ell}}
.

Proof.

Take
\chi\in\text{IBr}_{\ell}(G)
, we may Brauer lift
\chi
to a
L(\chi)\in R_{\C}(G)
.
Then because
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(L(\chi))
is
\sF
-stable we may write
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(L(\chi))=\sum_{\psi\in B}c_{\psi}\psi
for some
c_{\psi}\in\Z
.
Because each
c_{\psi}\in\Z\subset\mathcal{R}
,
\pi_{\ell}(c_{\psi})
is well-defined.
By Theorem 43 in
[
10
]
we have
\pi_{\ell}(\chi^{\prime})=\chi
. So
\pi_{\ell}(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(L(\chi)))=\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S}(\chi)=\sum_{\psi\in B}\pi_{\ell}(c_{\psi})\pi_{\ell}(\psi)
.

By Lemma
4.9
we have that
\langle\text{IBr}_{\ell}(G)|_{S}\rangle_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}=R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF)\otimes\overline{\F_{\ell}}
, in particular:

R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF)\otimes\overline{\F_{\ell}}=\langle\text{IBr}_{\ell}(G)|_{S}\rangle_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}=\langle\pi_{\ell}(B)\rangle_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}.

Thus
\pi_{\ell}(B)
spans
R_{\overline{\F_{\ell}}}(\sF)\otimes\overline{\F}_{\ell}
as a
\overline{\F}_{\ell}
-vector space,
which has rank
|\text{cl}(\sF)|=|B|
, so
|B|=|\pi_{\ell}(B)|
and
\pi_{\ell}(B)
is linearly independent.
∎

Theorem 4.13
.

Conjecture A holds for non-exotic saturated fusion systems.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.2
we know that
|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}
is an integer.
Viewing an element of
B
as a tuple in
\C^{|\text{cl}(\sF)|}
, then the rows of
X(\sF)
are elements of
B
,
so Corollary
4.12
implies that
\pi_{\ell}(\mathrm{det}(X(\sF)))\neq 0
for all
\ell\neq p
.
Thus
|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}\in\bigcap_{\ell\neq p}\Z-\ell\Z\Rightarrow|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}
is a power of
p
.
So by Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.5
we have that
|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}=\prod_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}|C_{G}(x)|_{p}
and
\mathrm{det}(C)=\prod_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}|C_{G}(x)|_{p^{\prime}}=\prod_{x\in\text{fccl}(\sF)}|C_{S}(x)|
.
∎

The obstructions to using Lemma
1.3
to extend this proof method to all fusion systems are

Obstruction 4.14
.

If

is not Sylow in

then we do not have

for

fully
\sF

.

Obstruction 4.15

Lemma
4.5
requires

to be Sylow in
G
due to a dependence on Lemma
4.4
, where we utilise the fact that all Sylow subgroups are conjugate in order to choose
G
-conjugacy class representatives that lie in

.

Despite these issues, we are still able to prove Theorem B:

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
\sF
be any fusion system over
S
, then
|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}
is a power of
p
.

Proof.

|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}\in\Z
and
\pi_{\ell}(\mathrm{det}(X(\sF)))\neq 0
for all primes

by Corollary

|\mathrm{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}=p^{\alpha}
for some

as before.
∎

We conclude by observing that the Obstruction
4.14
increases the

-part of
|C_{G}(x)|
whilst Obstruction
4.15
increases the
p
-part of the determinant of the Cartan matrix. These two factors may cancel with one another. This idea leads to the following proposition:

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
\sF
be an exotic fusion system on
S
and
G
be some finite group with
\sF=\sF_{S}(G)
as in Lemma
1.3
. Then the fusion volume conjecture holds for
\sF
if

|\mathrm{det}(C(\sF))|_{p}=\frac{\prod_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}|C_{G}(x)|_{p}}{\prod_{x\in\text{fccl}(\sF)}|C_{S}(x)|}.

Proof.

We have that
|\text{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}_{p}=|\text{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}
by Theorem
4.16
and thus under our assumption we have

|\text{det}(X(\sF))|^{2}=\frac{\prod_{x^{\sF}\in\text{cl}(\sF)}|C_{G}(x)|_{p}}{\text{det}(C(\sF))_{p}}=\prod_{x\in\text{fccl}(\sF)}|C_{S}(x)|.

∎

Epigenome-wide Mendelian randomization with multi-omics validation identifies epigenetic drivers of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis
Authors: Zhaoqi Li, Luna Hong, Xingyu Lv, Shuangyu Yang, Fengzhan Ye, Linjie Huang, Shanping Jiang, Huiying Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIdiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a complex disease without clear etiology or effective therapy. While DNA methylation has been implicated in IPF pathogenesis, the tissue-specific causal effects of the epigenetic factors on IPF remain undetermined. Here, we perform epigenome-wide Mendelian randomization using blood-based methylation quantitative trait loci of 420,509 CpG sites and genome-wide association study for IPF to elucidate the causal effects of the CpG sites on IPF. Totally, 452 CpG sites has shown putative causal effects on IPF risk after Bonferroni correction. Among them, 13 CpG sites have shown strong colocalization evidence with genetic factors associated with IPF. Specifically, DNA methylation at CpG sites within MAN2A2 and TRIM27 shows significant differences between IPF lungs and controls, correlating with altered mRNA expressions of these genes in lung tissues. The CpG site in MAN2A2 is a binding site of ZNF384 according to transcription factor databases. RNA sequencing in the TGFβ1-induced alveolar epithelia confirms significantly reduced expression of MAN2A2 and ZNF384 comparing to the controls. Collectively, our study suggests a putative causal link between DNA methylation within MAN2A2 and IPF risk, wherein lung-specific DNA methylation in MAN2A2 may perturb the interaction between ZNF384 and MAN2A2, revealing novel roles for these genes in IPF pathogenesis.Subject terms: Genetics, Respiratory tract diseases

Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, progressive, and lethal lung disease, characterized by inflammation and fibrosis within the alveolar interstitium of lungs1. As a disease shaped by both genetic susceptibility and environmental triggers, IPF exhibits maladaptive wound healing process in response to recurrent alveolar epithelial injury1,2. Despite substantial advances in research of IPF over the past decades, precise mechanisms driving disease development remain elusive. Moreover, the current first-line therapies, including pirfenidone and nintedanib, have proven effective in slowing lung function decline, yet they provide limited improvements for clinical symptoms and health-related quality of life of IPF patients3–6. This therapeutic gap emphasizes the critical need for more insights into the disease mechanisms to develop more effective therapeutic interventions.

IPF is impacted by many factors such as age, cigarette smoking, and dusts1. These environmental factors may contribute to the mechanisms of IPF through altering DNA methylation. DNA methylation is an epigenetic process regulating chromosomal and extrachromosomal DNA functions in response to environmental exposures7. DNA methylation patterns capture long-term lifestyle and cumulative environmental exposures, and could offer more dynamic information on diseases8 and aging9, thereby attracting increasing research attention. Many studies have implicated the important role of DNA methylation in IPF pathogenesis10–14. However, these studies are unable to determine whether the observed methylation alterations represent primary drivers of IPF progression or secondary modifications induced by fibrogenesis, which are important for therapeutic design and our understanding of IPF.

For inferring genetic causality between two traits, Mendelian randomization (MR) was presented, which has leveraged naturally allocated genetic variants as instrumental variables (IVs), and provided a robust and cost-effective alternative to randomized controlled trials9. With the development of epigenome-wide association studies (EWAS) and genome-wide association studies (GWAS), MR has been applied to molecular traits using methylation quantitative trait loci (mQTLs) to identify causal cytosine-phosphate-guanine (CpG) sites for disease risks7,15. However, most of the mQTL studies are performed using blood samples. Functions of the DNA methylation at the CpG sites in blood may not regulate the genes in tissues connecting to the disease because of tissue-specificity of DNA methylation16,17. To identify the tissue-specific mechanisms of the genes regulated by these CpG sites, it is necessary to analyse the multi-omics data, especially the expression quantitative trait methylation (eQTM) data and ChIP-seq data to link the DNA methylation to gene expressions in tissues connecting to the diseases.

Here, we leveraged large sample size-based GWAS summary data of IPF and conducted epigenome-wide MR (EWMR) on 420,509 CpG sites with accessible mQTLs to estimate the causal relationships between DNA methylation in blood and risk of IPF. We identified 13 CpG sites (mapped to 12 genes) that were causal for IPF (Bonferroni adjusted p < 0.05), and were colocalized with the single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) associated with IPF. Among them, a CpG site at MAN2A2 has shown significantly different methylation levels in lung tissues of IPF patients and controls. Moreover, this CpG site has shown significant association with the expression of MAN2A2 by analysing lung-tissue specific eQTM data. Further transcription factor (TF) prediction for MAN2A2 suggested that the CpG site is a binding site of TF ZNF384, which potentially disturbs the interaction between MAN2A2 and ZNF384, and leads to dysregulation of MAN2A2. The expression patterns of MAN2A2 and ZNF384 were validated in a transforming growth factor β1-induced alveolar epithelial cell model through RNA sequencing, suggesting the new functions of these two genes in inducing IPF. Thus, our findings provide novel insights into the importance of DNA methylation in IPF pathogenesis, and reveal potential epigenetic factor-based mechanisms of MAN2A2 and ZNF384 in IPF.

Results

Evaluating causal effects of DNA methylation on IPF risk

In order to identify causal effects of DNA methylation on IPF risk, epigenome-wide Mendelian Randomization (EWMR)9 was performed on the 420,509 CpG sites by leveraging mQTL data and the GWAS summary data for IPF (Fig. 1 and Supplementary Fig. 1). After Bonferroni correction, 766 CpG sites have shown significant causal effects on IPF risk, with no evidence of reverse causality. Among them, 765 were identified using the generalized inverse-variance weighted (gIVW) method, and one was detected via the Wald ratio method18 (Fig. 2a). Out of the 765 gIVW-significant CpG-IPF pairs, 451 were considered as robust because they exhibited consistent effect directions in generalized MR-Egger (gEgger) model18, with neither horizontal pleiotropy nor heterogeneity detected (Supplementary Data 1 and Supplementary Data 2). Annotation of these CpG sites revealed that 304 of them could be mapped to genes, with 49.67% located in the promoter region (related to gene silencing), 45.07% in gene body (related to enhanced gene expression), and 5.26% in 3’-untranslated region (UTR) (Supplementary Data 1). Out of these CpG sites, 208 have shown decreasing methylation associated with increased IPF risk, while 244 have shown increasing methylation associated with a higher risk of IPF in EWMR analysis (Supplementary Data 1).

Overview of the study.

We established a CpG-centered triangulation framework to identify epigenetic drivers of IPF. First, we obtained mQTLs of 420,509 CpG sites from GoDMC and GWAS summary statistics of IPF from GBMI. EWMR analysis was performed using gIVW, gEgger, and Wald ratio models to evaluate the causal relationships between DNA methylation and IPF, with sensitivity analyses for pleiotropy, heterogeneity, and reverse causality. Colocalisation analysis was then conducted to assess shared genetic architecture across the entire locus influencing both methylation and IPF risk, where a PP.H4 > 0.80 was considered strong colocalization evidence. For functional validation in lung tissue, we integrated epigenomic and transcriptomic data to perform eQTM analyses identifying CpG sites associated with gene expression, and further assessed relationships between these eQTM-linked genes and lung function using transcriptomic data. For mechanistic exploration, we investigated regulatory mechanisms of IPF-associated methylated CpG sites by integrating transcription factor binding information and ChIP-seq data, followed by in vitro experimental validation of IPF-related genes in a TGFβ1-induced AEC model using RNA sequencing. Identifying shared genetic factors between the CpG sites and IPF

For the CpG sites showing causal effects on IPF, colocalization analysis19 was performed to evaluate their shared genetic factors with IPF. In total, strong colocalization evidence (PP.H4 > 0.80) for 17 CpG sites and SNPs associated with IPF were identified (Fig. 2a and Supplementary Data 3), indicating a shared genetic basis between these epigenetic signals and IPF. Among them, 13 CpG sites were annotated to 12 genes that included 7 genes reported for the first time as associated with IPF risk, and 5 genes known as associated with IPF20–24 (Table 1, Fig. 2b, and Supplementary Figs. 2–14). Three of these 13 CpG sites are located within BRSK2, C6orf106, and RFC1 genes, which have the potential to promote gene expression. There were nine CpG sites located within the promoters and linked to inhibited gene expression of C10orf32, C6orf142, CLPS, MAN2A2, PON2, PON3, SLC2A5, and TRIM27. Altogether, 13 CpG sites identified as causal effects on IPF by EWMR were colocalized with SNPs associated with IPF, suggesting the shared genetic basis may contribute to the causal effects of these CpG sites on IPF.

Identifying tissue-specific functions of the causal CpG sites in IPF

To explore the functions of these CpG sites in lung tissues, differentially methylation analysis (DMA) was performed using lung tissue DNA methylation profiles of 345 IPF patients and 202 controls from GEO database (GSE175458)14. The DMA identified a total of 333,531 differentially methylated positions (DMPs) (FDR corrected p < 0.05) within 23,652 genes. We applied a gene-centric validation strategy, whereby a gene was considered as associated with IPF through DNA methylation-related mechanisms if any of its associated CpGs showed significant differential methylation in a direction and intragenic location consistent with the causal model from EWMR analysis. This approach successfully validated eight genes harboring 54 CpG sites in total (Fig. 3a and Supplementary Table 1), demonstrating a robust concordance between genetic causality and tissue-level epigenetic alteration at the gene level.

Associations between these 54 CpG sites and expressions of these eight genes were analysed using expression quantitative trait methylation (eQTM)25 in lung tissues. The transcriptomic and methylation profiles of 226 IPF patients and 156 controls were obtained from GSE175457 and GSE17545814. The eQTM analysis indicated significant associations of 24 CpG sites and the expressions of 4 genes (C10orf32, PON3, MAN2A2, and TRIM27; p-value < 0.05), and the directions of these CpG sites influencing the gene expression levels were consistent with the findings in EWMR analysis (Fig. 3b–e and Supplementary Table 2).

Furthermore, the expression levels of these 4 genes (C10orf32, PON3, MAN2A2, and TRIM27) in lung tissues of IPF patients and controls were examined by an independent GEO dataset (GSE47460)26, indicating significantly decreased MAN2A2 and increased TRIM27 expressions in IPF patients comparing to the controls (Fig. 3f). The correlations between these two genes and the lung functions were further evaluated. The result indicated that expressions of MAN2A2 and TRIM27 are significantly correlated with lung functions, showing absolute values of Pearson correlation coefficients ranging from 0.43 to 0.57 (Fig. 3g) and supporting their potential roles in the pathogenesis of IPF.

To investigate whether genetic factors regulating expressions of MAN2A2 and TRIM27 having causal effects on IPF, we performed Mendelian randomization analysis using cis-expression quantitative trait loci (cis-eQTLs) of these genes as exposure and the GWAS for IPF as the outcome. Interestingly, no significant causal associations were observed between the genetic factors regulating expression of these two genes and IPF (Supplementary Table 3), suggesting that the roles of these two genes in IPF are more likely driven by DNA methylation alterations rather than genetically regulated expression changes.

The transcriptional regulatory mechanisms of the CpG sites on MAN2A2 and TRIM27

The expressions of genes, MAN2A2 and TRIM27 were indicated significantly correlated with the CpG sites showing causal effects on IPF. The underlying mechanisms of the CpG sites regulating these two genes were investigated by searching the transcription factors (TFs) binding to the CpG sites from AnimalTFDB4.027. AnimalTFDB4.0 revealed the top 30 TFs potentially binding to cg01482407 mapped to MAN2A2 and cg00175066 mapped to TRIM27 (Supplementary Table 4). These TFs were input into hTFtarget database28 to search for the ChIP-seq experimental evidences supporting their functions in regulating genes. As a result, the TFs, PGR and ZNF384 were found likely to bind to cg01482407 and regulate MAN2A2. The mRNA expression levels of ZNF384 and MAN2A2 were positively correlated (r = 0.54, p-value < 0.001) according to the RNA-seq data from GSE175457 (Fig. 3h), whereas no significant correlation was observed between expression of PGR and MAN2A2 (r = 0.07, p-value = 0.151; Supplementary Fig. 15a). No ChIP-seq experimental evidences supporting any TF regulating TRIM27.

The roles of MAN2A2 and ZNF384 in IPF were further validated by constructing a transforming growth factor β1 (TGFβ1)-induced alveolar epithelial cell (AEC) model, a well-established model in pulmonary fibrosis research29. Comparing to controls, the TGFβ1-induced AECs exhibited significantly decreased expression of MAN2A2 (p = 0.01) and ZNF384 (p = 0.09) (Fig. 3i, j). The expression of MAN2A2 and ZNF384 were found positively correlated (Pearson correlation coefficient = 0.57; Supplementary Fig. 15b), suggesting the potential regulatory role of ZNF384 in MAN2A2 through binding to the CpG sites showing causal effect on IPF.

The gene expression analysis also indicated that significantly elevated expression of TRIM27 in TGFβ1-induced AECs comparing to the controls (Fig. 3k). The mechanism of the causal CpG site regulating TRIM27 requires further investigation.

Replication analysis in clinician-diagnosed GWAS for IPF

To assess the robustness of our findings against potential heterogeneity in IPF case definition, we performed a replication EWMR analysis for the 765 significant CpG sites using summary statistics from an independent GWAS of strictly clinician-diagnosed IPF30. Despite the smaller sample size of the validation cohort, 24.6% of CpG sites showed consistent and nominally significant effects. We observed a consistent direction of causal effect (beta sign) for 52.4% (401/765) of the CpG sites identified using the GWAS of Partanen 202231. Importantly, 24.6% (188/765) of the CpG sites were not only directionally consistent but also achieved nominal significance (p < 0.05) in the independent validation cohort. We specifically examined the 13 CpG sites showing strong colocalization evidence in our previous colocalization analysis. An overwhelming majority, 12 out of 13 (92.3%) CpG sites, showed consistent effect directions in both the discovery and validation analysis. And 6 of these 12 directionally-consistent CpG sites were nominally significant (p < 0.05) in the validation cohort. Crucially, the CpG site for our top candidate TRIM27 (cg27120125) was among the nominally significant replications (Supplementary Fig. 16). The CpG for MAN2A2 (cg05131526) showed a consistent effect direction, though it was not statistically significant (p = 0.15), likely due to the reduced power of the smaller validation dataset (Supplementary Fig. 16).

In summary, the replication in a rigorously defined, clinician-diagnosed IPF cohort provides substantive support for the generalizability of our findings. The high concordance for our key colocalized signals, including the successful replication of TRIM27 and MAN2A2, mitigates concerns about bias from the original case definition and underscores the robustness of our prioritized genes (Supplementary Data 4).

Discussion

Environmental exposures are major contributors to chronic lung conditions and are known to induce epigenetic remodeling. Dysregulated DNA methylation patterns have been implicated in the pathogenesis of various chronic respiratory diseases including IPF10,32–36. These studies have limited ability to establish causal relationships between dysregulated DNA methylation and IPF risk due to unresolved confounding factors and potential reverse causation. The mQTL study and GWAS for IPF have provided us the opportunity to discover the causal relationships between DNA methylation and IPF risk. However, most of the large-sample size based and high quality mQTL studies are conducted using blood samples, which have led to the difficulty in causal effect analysis exploring the relationships between DNA methylation and IPF in lung tissues.

In this study, we utilized mQTLs of 420,509 CpG sites and large-scale GWAS summary data of IPF from GBMI to perform EWMR analysis. This initial screen identified putative causal associations, which were then subjected to genetic colocalization analysis as an exploratory step for triangulation of evidence. Through this two-step approach, we identified 13 CpG sites for which both the MR inference and the supportive evidence of shared genetic signal at the locus (PP.H4 > 0.80) converged, suggesting a high-confidence causal relationship with IPF risk. To evaluate the tissue-specific functions of these CpG sites, DMA was performed in lung tissues, and demonstrated that the methylation levels of the CpG sites at MAN2A2 and TRIM27 loci have shown significant difference between patients and controls. Analysing the eQTM data of lung tissues indicated that DNA methylation at the CpG sites within MAN2A2 and TRIM27 loci is significantly correlated with expressions of these two genes. The underlying mechanisms of DNA methylation regulating MAN2A2 and TRIM27 were investigated, indicating that DNA methylation at cg01482407 may disrupt the binding affinity of the TF ZNF384 to the promoter region of MAN2A2, and further regulate MAN2A2 expression. The roles of ZNF384 and MAN2A2 in IPF were validated through a TGFβ1-induced alveolar epithelial cell model. Collectively, this study identified the causal effects of the CpG sites on IPF. Further analyses and experiments indicated that the CpG site disturbed the interaction of ZNF384 with promoter region of MAN2A2, and subsequently downregulating MAN2A2 and resulting in IPF. These findings enhance our understanding of the associations between DNA methylation and IPF risk, and suggest novel targets for preventive and therapeutic interventions.

Previous research has revealed altered DNA methylation and associated changes in gene expression at several fibrosis-related genes (i.e., α-SMA37, CAV138, and CDKN2B39, etc.) in IPF. Subsequent studies have further demonstrated the alterations in DNA methylation landscapes in IPF lung tissues40, as well as purified IPF fibroblasts41 and alveolar macrophages10. In line with these findings, our MR analysis replicates the previously reported associations between five of these key loci (BRSK2 [cg19494188], C6orf106 [cg06810293], PON2 [cg04270402], RAD51 [cg08032884], and SLC2A5 [cg00310940]) and IPF20–24. However, the gene regulatory functions of these five CpG sites are not confirmed by subsequent eQTM analysis and DMA in lung tissues. This pattern underscores the capacity of our multi-stage analytical design to address tissue specificity and effectively mitigates concerns about false positives driven by tissue-irrelevant mechanisms. Furthermore, the fact that established IPF-related genes such as MUC5B and DSP14 did not yield strong signals in our blood-based screen (Supplementary Table 5) is not a failure of replication but an anticipated validation of tissue-specificity. It emphasizes the necessity of our follow-up lung-specific analyses (DMA, eQTM) to identify, from the set of blood-based causal signals, those which are also functionally active in the disease-relevant organ. Our pipeline is designed precisely to overcome the limitation that blood-based EWMR alone would miss such tissue-restricted signals.

Of note, our work has revealed that DNA methylation at MAN2A2 locus has putative causal effect on IPF pathogenesis. Further investigation indicated that DNA methylation potentially influences the expression of MAN2A2 by interfering with the accessibility of TF to its binding site in the promoter region of MAN2A2. Indeed, many SNPs have the potential roles in regulating the expression of MAN2A2. Their causal effects on IPF were not significant according to MR integrating eQTL data of MAN2A2. These findings suggest that the dysregulation of MAN2A2 through DNA methylation plays an important role in IPF.

MAN2A2 (mannosidase alpha class 2a member 2) encodes a crucial enzyme in the biosynthesis of N-glycan42. The process attaching N-glycans to protein is one of the forms of protein glycosylation, a post-translational modification involved in diverse pathophysiological conditions including inflammation43. The role of MAN2A2 in IPF has not been investigated although its involvement in lung functions is observed. Previous research has observed alterations in N-glycan profiles anatomically associated with mucus hypersecretion, alveolar-bronchiolar hyperplasia, type II alveolar epithelial proliferation, macrophage aggregation, interstitial edema, and fibrosis in radiation-induced lung injury, a series of histopathological changes that closely resemble those of IPF44. A recent multiancestry epigenome-wide association meta-analysis has identified significantly differential methylation at the CpG site annotated to MAN2A2 in relation to lung function (FVC, forced vital capacity)45.

Current mechanistic evidence indicates that DNA methylation might modulate TF binding affinity and subsequently alter gene expression patterns7. Here, our integrative analysis proposes a hypothesis that altered methylation at cg01482407 influences the binding affinity of ZNF384, which in turn regulates MAN2A2 expression, thereby contributing to IPF development. The validity of this hypothesis and the precise effect of methylation on ZNF384 binding await direct experimental confirmation. Currently, there is no study indicating the role of ZNF384 in IPF. ZNF384 (zinc finger protein 384) is a TF involved in the DNA double-strand break (DSB) repair and genomic stability maintenance46. This biological function is particularly consistent with the mechanism of IPF development, as compelling evidence demonstrated that impaired DSB repair contributes to fibrogenesis47, and increased reactive oxygen species in IPF lungs induce persistent DNA damage48. These observations implicate ZNF384 as a plausible mechanistic link between DNA methylation-dependent regulation of MAN2A2 expression and maladaptive DNA damage responses in IPF pathogenesis, though the underlying mechanisms warrant systematic investigation.

In addition, our study also identified the putative causal effect of DNA methylation at the CpG site in TRIM27 on IPF. TRIM27 (tripartite motif-containing 27) encodes a member of the tripartite motif family, a diverse group of proteins that function as E3 ubiquitin ligases49. Accumulating evidence has established that TRIM27 mediates ubiquitination of multiple substrates including PTEN, JAK1, SIX3, and IκBα50–53. This post-translational modification critically modulates downstream signaling pathways such as PTEN/PI3K/AKT, JAK1/STAT3, Wnt/β-catenin, and NFκB, all of which have been mechanistically linked to the pathogenesis of IPF11. Moreover, TRIM27 is indicated as oncogenic properties across multiple cancers, with its overexpression consistently correlated with poor histopathological and clinical outcomes52,54,55. In non-small cell lung cancer, elevated TRIM27 expression significantly accelerates cell migration and invasion56. Another study reveals that TRIM27 acts as an oncogene driving epithelial-mesenchymal transition in colorectal cancer57. Given the shared pathogenic mechanisms between cancer and IPF, particularly the overlapping signaling pathways (e.g., PI3K/AKT, JAK/STAT, Wnt/β-catenin, and NFκB) and critical processes like EMT, these findings indicate that altered DNA methylation at the TRIM27 locus may contribute to IPF pathogenesis through sustained disruption of above signaling pathways and consequent promotion of fibrotic remodeling.

Our work has some limitations. First, the current study utilized whole blood-derived-mQTLs and bulk lung tissue methylation profiles, which may obscure cell-type-specific signals. Robust single-cell mQTLs or DNA methylation data are required to incorporate with GWAS for IPF to resolve cell-type-specific epigenetic regulation. Second, because of the lacks of multi-ancestry GWAS for IPF and mQTLs, our focus is on European ancestry populations, which limits the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic groups, highlighting the need for future multi-ancestry studies. Third, the primary GWAS for IPF used in our discovery analysis relied on ICD-10 codes for case definition. While this is a known limitation of large-scale biobank data, our subsequent validation in an independent, clinician-diagnosed IPF cohort has strengthened the confidence in the key findings. Fourth, the coloc.abf method assumes a single causal variant per trait and may therefore not fully capture loci with multiple causal variants. Future studies employing more flexible approaches that explicitly model allelic heterogeneity, such as SuSiE-based colocalization58, could further refine these findings. Finally, while we identified putative regulatory roles for causal CpG sites and annotated genes in IPF, the detailed molecular mechanisms require experimental validation.

In summary, the EWMR analysis has identified DNA methylation at 13 CpG sites showing robust causal effects on IPF risk. Further eQTM analysis and DMA on lung tissues highlight the methylated CpG sites within MAN2A2 and TRIM27 loci as likely contributors to IPF pathogenesis. Among these CpG sites, the site within MAN2A2 is predicted as TF binding site of ZNF384, which is supported by further ChIP-seq data analysis. This finding suggests that the DNA methylation at the CpG site disturbs the interaction between ZNF384 and the promoter of MAN2A2 and subsequently downregulating MAN2A2 and promoting fibrosis. These novel signatures could serve as promising prevention and treatment targets for IPF.

Methods

Data sources

We downloaded 420,509 CpG sites with available DNA methylation quantitative trait loci (mQTLs) from Genetics of DNA Methylation Consortium (GoDMC; http://www.godmc.org.uk). These data are from whole-blood samples of 32,851 European individuals in 36 cohorts15. The DNA methylation profiles of lung tissues from 345 IPF patients and 202 controls were sourced from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database (accession: GSE17545814). Normalized IPF-related transcriptomic profiles were also downloaded from GEO database. One was GSE17545714, which is the data from 234 IPF patients and 188 controls. The other dataset was GSE4746026, which contained a mix of different interstitial lung diseases, and we only extracted data of the 157 patients who have a histological diagnosis of usual interstitial pneumonia, a pathological hallmark of IPF, and 108 controls. Additionally, we obtained whole-blood expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs) data from the eQTLGen consortium (https://eqtlgen.org/), which included 31,684 European individuals59.

The GWAS summary statistics for IPF in the primary analysis were retrieved from Global Biobank Meta-analysis Initiative (GBMI; https://www.globalbiobankmeta.org), encompassing 6743 IPF cases and 1,056,693 controls of European ancestry across 9 biobanks31. IPF cases were defined following the unified Phecode 502, corresponding to the Internataion Classification of Disease (ICD) codes including ICD-9: 515, 515.0, and ICD-10: J84.1, J84.10, J84.17, J84.8, J84.89. To independently replicate our main findings, we leveraged another GWAS summary statistics for IPF (2668 IPF cases, 8591 controls), requested from https://github.com/genomicsITER/PFgenetics30, in which all cases were strictly diagnosed by clinicians.

Epigenome-wide Mendelian randomization (EWMR)

Mendelian Randomization (MR) is an analytical method that uses genetic variants as instrumental variables (IVs) to infer a causal relationship between an exposure (e.g., DNA methylation) and an outcome (e.g., IPF risk). This approach relies on three core assumptions: the variants must (1) be robustly associated with the exposure; (2) be independent of confounders; and (3) influence the outcome solely through the exposure. In this study, we conducted EWMR analysis, a cis-MR design, using local genetic variants (cis-mQTLs) near a CpG site as instruments for its DNA methylation level. This approach is particularly powerful because these local variants are more likely to regulate methylation directly, thereby strengthening causal inference for the role of specific epigenetic marks in IPF60.

To minimize pleiotropic effects, only cis-mQTLs (SNP-CpG distance < 1MB) were retained for further analysis. For CpG sites, we first selected cis-mQTLs significantly associated with DNA methylation (p < 1 × 10-8). We then performed linkage disequilibrium (LD) clumping (r2 < 0.3) within a 10,000 kb window based on the hg19-based 1000 Genomes European reference to obtain a set of independent IVs. This specific r2 threshold was chosen in line with established practices for EWMR analysis9,18, and it effectively balances the need to mitigate strong linkage disequilibrium while retaining a sufficient number of correlated SNPs for robust causal inference. Subsequently, cis-mQTLs with F-value ≤ 10 were removed to avoid weak IV bias. As for the GWAS summary data for IPF, SNPs with minor allele frequency < 0.01 or within the major histocompatibility complex region (chromosome 6: 28477797-33448354) were excluded. After data pre-pocessing, 145,781 CpG sites with available cis-mQTLs remained for EWMR analysis, including 34,361 (23.57%) instrumented by a single cis-mQTL and 111,420 (76.43%) by multiple cis-mQTLs. After harmonizing the genetic associations of these mQTLs with the IPF outcome data, 134,316 CpG–IPF pairs were retained for the final EWMR analysis.

To comprehensively identify the causal relationships between CpG sites and IPF, an EWMR analytical framework was employed allowing methods to complement and validate each other. Briefly, the generalized inverse-variance weighted (gIVW) method was used as the primary analysis for all CpG sites with ≥ 2 IVs, while the generalized MR-Egger (gEgger) method was additionally applied to CpG sites with ≥ 3 IVs as a sensitivity analysis to assess the potential directional pleiotropy18. The gEgger method requires a minimum of three IVs to reliably estimate its intercept18,61,62. For CpG sites with only a single IV, the Wald ratio method was applied. CpG sites showing Bonferroni corrected p < 0.05/134,316 in EWMR were considered as causal effects on IPF.

Pleiotropy of the EWMR analysis was tested according to deviation of intercept from zero by gEgger, and heterogeneity of the EWMR analysis was evaluated by the Cochran’s Q-statistics. The Steiger directionality test was conducted to filter out CpG-IPF pairs with potential reverse causality63. CpG sites that passed the Bonferroni correction threshold and showed no detectable pleiotropy, heterogeneity or reverse causality were retained as robust causal CpG sites for further analysis. The genes that are close to the CpG sites were considered as regulated by these CpG sites.

Specifically, for exposures with more than three IVs, the gIVW method was implemented using the default multiplicative random-effects model as provided in the R package ‘MendelianRandomization’ (version 0.9.0).

The EWMR analyses and the MR analysis on eQTLs were performed by the R packages ‘TwoSampleMR’ (version 0.5.10) and ‘MendelianRandomization’ (version 0.9.0).

Colocalization analysis

To investigate whether the putative causal relationships identified by EWMR were driven by shared genetic variants, we performed a colocalization analysis19. Notably, while EWMR uses independent instrumental variables (derived after LD clumping) for causal inference, colocalization analysis aims to assess shared genetic signals across the locus. Therefore, for each CpG site, we used all available cis-mQTLs (without LD clumping) within the genomic region, a 1 MB window centered on the CpG site, to accommodate scenarios with multiple causal variants. For these regions, we extracted all corresponding SNPs from the full IPF GWAS summary statistics. Colocalization analysis estimates the posterior probabilities (PP) of SNPs based on their associations with traits under five hypotheses: PP.H0 (no causal variant for either trait), PP.H1 (causal variant for trait1 only), PP.H2 (causal variant for trait2 only), PP.H3 (distinct causal variants for both traits), and PP.H4 (shared causal variants for both traits). The colocalization analysis was performed using the coloc.abf() function from the R package ‘coloc’ (version 5.2.3) with default prior probabilities (p1 = 1e-4, p2 = 1e-4, p12 = 1e-5). Importantly, no additional p-value threshold was applied to the IPF GWAS data to select SNPs in this step, as the analysis aimed to comprehensively assess genetic overlap within each predefined locus. A CpG site was considered to have strong colocalization evidence if PP.H4 exceeded 0.80. This provides supportive, exploratory genetic evidence for triangulation with the primary EWMR findings.

Differential methylation analysis (DMA)

To identify differential methylation at causal CpG sites in IPF patients and controls, we analysed the DNA methylation levels of 743,236 CpG sites downloaded from the GEO dataset, GSE17545814. The DNA methylation level is measured by β value representing the ratio of the intensity of the methylated bead type to the combined locus intensity. After data filtering and quality control, the β values of 725,078 CpG sites for 547 samples were retained for DMA using the R package ‘ChAMP’ (version 2.28.0). In DMA, the β values of each probe were compared between IPF patients and controls by fitting a general linear model. The significance of the difference was evaluated by moderated t-statistics. The CpG sites were defined as differential methylated positions (DMPs) if DMA estimated significance of the difference for mean methylation level between groups was p-valueFDR < 0.0564.

Expression quantitative trait methylation (eQTM) analysis

The correlations between DNA methylation at causal CpG sites and mRNA expression of target genes were examined via the lung-tissue specific eQTM25 analysis. We utilized DNA methylation data from GSE17545814 and matched mRNA expression data from GSE17545714, which comprised lung tissues from 226 IPF cases and 156 controls. The eQTM analysis was restricted to CpG sites that were significantly differentially methylated in our prior DMA, supported by EWMR and colocalization evidence, and had available matched DNA methylation and gene expression data in the aforementioned datasets. For each of these CpG sites, the correlations between the DNA methylation and the mRNA expression of the target genes were estimated by the Pearson correlation coefficients. Statistical significance of the correlation was defined as a two-sided p < 0.05.

Transcriptomic profiles analyses

We defined a set of validated CpG sites as those that passed our multi-stage analytical pipeline, showing consistent significance in EWMR, colocalization, DMA, and eQTM analyses. For genes annotated to these validated CpG sites, we extracted their expression data from the IPF-related GEO datasets (GSE17545714 and GSE4746026) and tested for differential expression in lung tissues of IPF patients and controls. The GSE47460 dataset also provided lung function measurements, including diffusing capacity of the lungs for carbon monoxide (DLCO) and forced vital capacity (FVC), which are key clinical indicators of disease severity and progression in IPF3,4. The correlations between gene expression and lung function of individuals were assessed by Pearson correlation coefficients to explore the potential relationship between our identified genes and the clinical manifestation of the disease. A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant.

Exploring of the transcriptional regulatory role of DNA methylation

To investigate the impact of transcription factors (TFs) in the associations between DNA methylation at causal CpG sites and expression of annotated genes, we screened for potential TF binding sites within 50 bp around the CpG sites. For each CpG site, we extracted a 100 bp genomic sequence from the human reference genome (GRCh37/hg19) centered on the site. These human genomic sequences were submitted to the “Predict TFBS” tool in the Animal Transcription Factor DataBase 4.0 (AnimalTFDB4.0; https://guolab.wchscu.cn/AnimalTFDB4/)27. The resulting predictions for human TFs were ranked by default significance score, and the top 30 candidates for each CpG site were retained for further analysis. Then we searched the hTFtarget database (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/hTFtarget)28 to determine whether these predicted TFs have shown regulatory effects on the annotated genes with ChIP-seq experimental evidence. Finally, we estimated the Pearson correlation coefficients between the mRNA expression levels of the candidate TFs and their potential target genes, using the normalized RNA-seq data from GSE175457.

Cell culture and treatment

The human alveolar type II cell line (A549) was obtained from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC; USA). The cells were cultured in DMEM/F-12 media (C11330500BT; Gibco) mixed with 10% FBS (1027016; Gibco) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (15140-122; Gibco) and maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. Cells were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 2 × 105 cells per well. After 24 hours of incubation, cell confluence reached 70% and cells were treated with TGFβ1 (10804-HNAC; Sino Biological) at 10 ng/mL for 48 h. TGFβ1 is a master profibrotic cytokine that is consistently upregulated in the lungs of IPF patients and is a well-established driver of disease pathogenesis11,65. A central mechanism by which TGFβ1 promotes fibrosis is through the induction of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in alveolar epithelial cells29. During EMT, epithelial cells lose their cell-cell adhesion and acquire a mesenchymal phenotype, which is characterized by increased migratory capacity and elevated production of extracellular matrix components. This process contributes directly to the expansion of the fibroblast population in fibrotic lungs. The experiment was performed with three independent biological replicates for both the control and TGFβ1-treated groups. Each biological replicate represents an independently cultured and treated batch of A549 cells. Cells were subsequently collected for RNA-sequencing and the gene expression changes in the target genes were assessed to evaluate their potential role in this fibrotic pathway.

RNA sequencing and data analysis

RNA samples derived from TGFβ1-induced A549 cells and control cells were sequenced on the NovaSeq6000 platform (Illumina; USA). Details of RNA sequencing are described in the Supplementary Methods. RNA expression levels were quantified by fragments per kilobase million (FPKM). We compared the mRNA expression levels of target genes and TFs using unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-tests. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Statistics and reproducibility

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.2.2). The following R packages were used: ‘TwoSampleMR’ (version 0.5.10) and ‘MendelianRandomization’ (version 0.9.0) for EWMR analysis and MR analysis on cis-eQTLs; ‘coloc’ (version 5.2.3) for colocalization analysis; and ‘ChAMP’ (version 2.28.0) for DMA.

For the primary EWMR analysis, a Bonferroni corrected p < 0.05/134,316 was applied to identify CpG sites with putative causal effects on IPF. In the colocalization analysis, strong colocalizaiton evidence was defined by a PP.H4 exceeding 0.80. For the DMA, DMPs were identified using a FDR corrected p < 0.05, based moderated t-statistics. For the eQTM and transcriptomic profiles analyses, statistical significance was set at a two-sided p < 0.05. For the MR analysis between cis-eQTLs and IPF GWAS, a threshold of p < 0.05 was used to indicate statistical significance. For the RNA sequencing data, each group contained three biologically independent samples, and comparisons of mRNA expression levels of target genes and TFs between groups were performed using unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-tests, with statistical significance defined as p < 0.05. The replication EWMR analysis also employed a significance threshold of p < 0.05.

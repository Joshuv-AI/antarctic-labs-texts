Abelian versus triangulated quotients
Henning Krause

Abstract
. It is shown that any localisation of triangulated categories induces (up to an equivalence) a localisation of abelian categories when one passes to their abelianisations. From this one obtains for any enlargement of Grothendieck universes an example of an abelian category and a Serre subcategory within the smaller universe such that the corresponding quotient does only exist within the bigger universe. The second part of this note provides an analogue for the abelian hull of an arbitrary category.

18E10 (primary); 18E35, 18G80 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

Localisations of categories naturally arise in the study of abelian
and triangulated categories
[
4
,
8
]
, but the construction
of a localisation raises some set-theoretic issues. More specifically,
given a category
\mathcal{C}
and a class of morphisms
\Sigma
in
\mathcal{C}
, there is
a universal functor
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}]
that inverts all morphisms in

\Sigma
; see
[
5
]
. For the localised category
\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}]
it
is a priori not clear that for any given pair of objects the morphisms
between them form a set (and not a proper class). Fortunately there
are explicit criteria in the literature which ensure that there is no
reason to worry, but often this issue is ignored.

For an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
and a Serre subcategory
\mathcal{C}
the
quotient
category

\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}
is by definition the localisation
\mathcal{A}[\Sigma^{-1}]
with
respect to the class
\Sigma
of morphisms with kernel and cokernel both
in
\mathcal{C}
; see
[
4
, III.1]
. In this note we present examples
of such a quotient such that for some pair of objects the morphisms
between them do not form a set. For a concise exposition it is
convenient to use the setting of Grothendieck universes.

2.
Abelian quotients

We fix a Grothendieck universe
\mathfrak{U}
which is sufficiently big to
contain the set of integers; see
[
4
, I.1]
. If not mentioned
otherwise, we assume throughout that all categories are

\mathfrak{U}
-categories
, which means that for any pair of objects

X,Y
the set
\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)
belongs to
\mathfrak{U}
. Given a pair of
categories
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
we write
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
for the category of functors

\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
, with
\operatorname{Hom}(F,G)
given by the set of natural
transformations
F\to G
for any pair of functors
F,G
. This need not
be a
\mathfrak{U}
-category.

*  *  *

For a pair
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
of abelian categories let
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
denote
the full subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
consisting of the exact functors

\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
. Given a Serre subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
, the quotient
functor
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}
can be characterised as follows.

Lemma 1
.

[
4
, III.1]

For an exact functor
F\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
between abelian categories
and a Serre subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
, the following are
equivalent.

(1)

The functor
F
induces for any abelian category
\mathcal{B}
a fully faithful functor

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{B})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})

with essential image consisting of all functors
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
that
annihilate
\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

The functor
F
induces an equivalence
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.∎

3.
The abelianisation of a triangulated category

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category. Given any abelian category
\mathcal{A}

we write
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A})
for the full subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A})

consisting of the cohomological functors
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{A}
. Recall from

[
8
, II.3]
that the
abelianisation

\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T})
is a fully
faithful cohomological functor that induces for any abelian category

\mathcal{A}
an equivalence

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T}),\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}).

For a triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
we write
\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}

for the corresponding
quotient category

[
8
, II.2]
. The quotient functor has the following property.

Lemma 2
.

[
8
, II, Théorème 2.2.6]

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
a
triangulated subcategory. The quotient functor
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
induces for any abelian
category
\mathcal{A}
a fully faithful functor

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A})

with image consisting of all functors
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{A}
that annihilate
\mathcal{S}
.∎

We obtain a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S})}

and claim that the bottom horizontal functor is equivalent to a quotient
functor.

Let
\operatorname{Ab}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{T})
denote the Serre subcategory of

\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T})
that is generated by the image of the embedding

\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T})
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
a
triangulated subcategory. The canonical functor
\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T})\to\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S})
induces an
equivalence

\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T})/\operatorname{Ab}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{T})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}).

Proof.

For any abelian category
\mathcal{A}
we obtain the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}),\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{T}),\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}

The bottom horizontal functor is fully faithful by
Lemma
2
. Thus the top horizontal functor is fully
faithful, and its essential image is given by the functors that
annihilate
\operatorname{Ab}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{T})
. Then the assertion follows from
Lemma
1
.
∎

*  *  *

Now assume that there is a universe
\mathfrak{V}
strictly containing
\mathfrak{U}

and fix a set
I
in
\mathfrak{V}\smallsetminus\mathfrak{U}
. Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field
in
\mathfrak{U}
and consider the polynomial algebra

\Lambda=\mathbb{F}\langle I\rangle
. Modules over
\Lambda
are nothing but

\mathbb{F}
-vector spaces with a distinguished set of endomorphisms indexed
by the elements of
I
. Let
\mathbb{F}
denote the trivial
\Lambda
-module (so
the endomorphisms given by the elements of
I
are zero) and let
\mathcal{A}

denote the category of
\Lambda
-modules
M
that admit a submodule

M^{\prime}\subseteq M
such that
M^{\prime}
and
M/M^{\prime}
are finite direct sums of
copies of
\mathbb{F}
. The category
\mathcal{A}
is abelian and we write

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})/{\mathbf{Ac}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})

for its
bounded derived
category
, which is the triangulated quotient of the homotopy category
with respect to the subcategory of acyclic complexes. Then

\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})
is a
\mathfrak{U}
-category, whereas

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})
is not. For instance, there is an isomorphism

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{F}[I],\mathbb{F})\cong\mathbb{F}^{I}

where
\mathbb{F}[I]
denotes the free
\mathbb{F}
-module with basis
I
. This
example is a variation of
[
2
, Example 6.A]
due to
Freyd. The calculation of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})
shows that the set
of objects of
\mathcal{A}
belongs to
\mathfrak{V}
, and therefore

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})
is a
\mathfrak{V}
-category.

Proposition 4
.

The abelian category
\operatorname{Ab}(\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A}))
is a
\mathfrak{U}
-category, and
there exists a Serre subcategory such that the corresponding
quotient is not a
\mathfrak{U}
-category.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{Ab}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A}))
denote the Serre subcategory
of
\operatorname{Ab}(\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A}))
that is generated by all acyclic
complexes in
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})
. Then Proposition
3

yields an equivalence

\operatorname{Ab}(\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A}))/\operatorname{Ab}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A}))\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Ab}(\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A}))

of
\mathfrak{V}
-categories. We have seen that
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})
is not a

\mathfrak{U}
-category, and therefore
\operatorname{Ab}(\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A}))
is not a

\mathfrak{U}
-category.
∎

4.
The abelian hull of an arbitrary category

For a category
\mathcal{C}
let
\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C})
denote its
additive hull
,
which is obtained by taking formal finite direct sums of the objects of
the
\mathbb{Z}
-linearisation
\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{C}
. The canonical functor

\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C})
induces for any additive category
\mathcal{A}
an equivalence

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\operatorname{add}}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}),

where
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\operatorname{add}}(-,-)
denotes the full subcategory of additive
functors.

For an additive category
\mathcal{C}
let
\operatorname{mod}(\mathcal{C})
denote its completion
under finite colimits, which is obtained by adding formal cokernels of
the morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
, for instance by using the category of coherent
functors
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}
into the category of abelian groups

[
1
]
. The canonical fully faithful functor
\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{mod}(\mathcal{C})

induces for any additive category
\mathcal{A}
with cokernels an equivalence

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\operatorname{mod}}(\operatorname{mod}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\operatorname{add}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}),

where
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\operatorname{mod}}(-,-)
denotes the full subcategory of additive
functors that are right exact.

The
abelian hull
of an additive category
\mathcal{C}
is by
definition

\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{C}):=\operatorname{mod}((\operatorname{mod}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{op}}))^{\mathrm{op}}.

This
construction goes back to Freyd and Gruson
[
3
,
6
]
. The
canonical inclusion
\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{C})
induces for any abelian
category
\mathcal{A}
an equivalence

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\operatorname{Ab}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\operatorname{add}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A});

see
[
7
, Proposition 12.4.1]
. We summarise these
constructions and obtain the following.

Lemma 5
.

For a category
\mathcal{C}
and any abelian category
\mathcal{A}
the canonical functor

\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}))

induces an equivalence

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C})),\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}).\qed

Now consider a localisation
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}]
.
This yields a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}\left[\Sigma^{-1}\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}\left[\Sigma^{-1}\right]))}

and we claim that the bottom horizontal functor is equivalent to a
quotient functor.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category and
\Sigma
a class of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
. Then
the localisation
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}]
induces an
equivalence

\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}))/\mathcal{S}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}])),

where
\mathcal{S}
denotes the Serre subcategory generated by the kernels and
cokernels of the morphisms in
\Sigma
, viewed as morphisms in
\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}))
.

Proof.

For any abelian category
\mathcal{A}
we obtain the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}])),\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C})),\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}],\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}

The vertical functors are equivalences by Lemma
5
.
The bottom horizontal functor is fully faithful by the universal
property of the localisation
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}]
, and therefore the top
horizontal functor is fully faithful. Thus the assertion follows from
Lemma
1
.
∎

Fix again a universe
\mathfrak{V}
strictly containing
\mathfrak{U}
and a set
I

in
\mathfrak{V}\smallsetminus\mathfrak{U}
. We consider the following quiver

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma\colon}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z\;\;\;(i\in I)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{i}}

and let
\mathcal{C}
denote its
path category
. Thus the objects of

\mathcal{C}
are the vertices of
\Gamma
and the morphisms are given by the
paths in
\Gamma
, including the trivial path at each vertex. Let

\Sigma=\{\sigma_{i}\mid i\in I\}
. Then we have in

\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}]

\operatorname{Hom}(x,z)=\{\tau_{i}\sigma_{i}^{-1}\mid i\in I\}.

Proposition 7
.

The abelian category
\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}))
is a
\mathfrak{U}
-category, and
there exists a Serre subcategory such that the corresponding
quotient is not a
\mathfrak{U}
-category.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the Serre subcategory
of
\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}))
that is generated by the kernels and
cokernels of the morphisms in
\Sigma
, viewed as morphisms in
\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}))
.
Then Proposition
6

yields an equivalence

\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}))/\mathcal{S}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}]))

of
\mathfrak{V}
-categories. We have seen that
\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}]
is not a

\mathfrak{U}
-category, and therefore
\operatorname{Ab}(\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{C}[\Sigma^{-1}]))
is not a

\mathfrak{U}
-category.
∎

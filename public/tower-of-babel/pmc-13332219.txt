IL-11 receptor is a novel target for drug development with pharmacological activity in fibro-inflammatory disease
Authors: David J King, James S Swaney, Cadmus Rich, H Toni Jun, Yasmin Vasquez, Shira Geller, Christine Chidester, Merrick Chai, Eric Elliott, Deborah Witherden, Hannah Hershoff, Aarya Kafi, Amy Strauwald, Andrea L Kossler, Rona Z Silkiss, Alan Oester, Antonio M Garrido-Hermosilla, Mohsan Malik, Don O Kikkawa, Maria Fardis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIL-11, a novel target for drug development, has been associated with several fibroinflammatory diseases including thyroid eye disease (TED), where it plays an important role in signaling to stromal cells activating multiple intracellular pathways. In TED patient tissue, IL-11 is elevated and stimulates multiple effects important in disease progression, including the production of proinflammatory cytokines, hyaluronic acid (HA) and fibrotic markers. LASN01, a potent antibody to IL-11 receptor, inhibits these effects and is a potential therapeutic agent for TED. Teprotumumab, an antibody to IGF-1 receptor, inhibits HA production and adipogenesis and is effective in reduction of proptosis. Activation of the IGF-1 and IL-11 pathways in TED tissue induces the expression of fibroinflammatory genes regulated by LASN01 and lipid biosynthetic genes regulated by Teprotumumab. Clinical studies show that LASN01 is well tolerated and in a placebo-controlled phase II trial in TED, LASN01 resulted in a statistically significant resolution of clinical activity score (CAS) in 88% of treated patients (p = 0.028), but had lesser effects on proptosis. The data supports the importance of IL-11 biology in fibroinflammatory disease and that IL-11 receptor is a pharmacologically active target for drug development.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-51600-0.Subject terms: Diseases, Drug discovery, Immunology, Medical research

Introduction

Interleukin 11 (IL-11) is a pleiotropic cytokine that activates intracellular signaling through binding to IL-11 receptor α (IL-11Rα or IL-11R) which then associates with gp130, to form a ternary signaling complex. On formation of this complex, downstream phosphorylation of multiple intracellular pathway components is induced depending on the cellular context, resulting in activation of the canonical STAT3 and/or non-canonical ERK and AKT pathways with subsequent effects on cellular proliferation, migration/invasion, differentiation and fibrosis1,2. IL-11Rα is expressed mostly on stromal cells, including fibroblasts, smooth muscle cells, endothelial and epithelial cells, as well as certain myeloid cell subsets3. Under normal physiological conditions, IL-11 and IL-11R are expressed at exceptionally low levels in tissue and in circulation4. However, in several fibroinflammatory diseases, IL-11 and IL-11R are upregulated and such increases have been correlated with disease severity3,5–8. IL-11 signaling has been implicated as an important regulator of stromal inflammation, fibroblast activation and tissue remodeling and as such has been implicated in the pathobiology of several diseases characterized by fibroinflammatory processes including idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), metabolic dysfunction associated steatohepatitis (MASH), kidney disease, ulcerative colitis, and thyroid eye disease (TED)8–13.

Thyroid eye disease (TED), also known as Graves’ orbitopathy (GO) or Graves’ eye disease, is a progressive fibroinflammatory disorder of orbital tissue with approximately, 13 in 100,000 new cases each year13. Patients commonly present with redness and swelling of eyelids and conjunctiva associated with ocular discomfort, photophobia, and/or orbital pain. In severe disease, patients may develop eyelid retraction, proptosis and diplopia, leading to disfigurement and if untreated irreversible sight loss affecting quality of life17. TED is characterized by infiltration of lymphocytic cells, orbital fat expansion, extraocular muscle enlargement, and tissue remodeling14,15.

Management of TED has focused on generalized reduction in inflammation using corticosteroids, orbital radiation and other immunosuppressive agents. Since 2020 in the USA, the anti-IGF-1R monoclonal antibody, teprotumumab (Tepezza) has demonstrated more target specific response and is now currently standard of care. However, the adverse event profile for offered therapeutic options and high recurrence rate suggests a continuing unmet medical need for improved safe and effective therapies for this disease.

Recently, IL-11 expression was found to be increased in TED patients compared to healthy individuals, with higher IL-11 levels reported both in orbital tissue and in patient serum, the latter correlating with clinical activity score8. The observed upregulation of IL-11 in TED may be functionally relevant, since exposure of TED-derived orbital fibroblasts to recombinant IL-11 promoted a fibrotic phenotype. Furthermore, IL-11 expression and release increased following stimulation of orbital fibroblasts with IL-1β or TGFβ, factors that are reported to be pathogenic mediators in TED18,19. In addition it has recently been suggested that the IL-11 pathway is important to fibrosis in TED driven by the PDK2 enhancement of glycolysis20. These observations suggest that IL-11 may play a pathological role in TED and suggest that blockade of the IL-11 pathway could serve as a potential therapeutic intervention point in TED progression, that is distinct from other pathways that are currently under clinical investigation.

In the current study to investigate the role of IL-11 and its therapeutic blockade, we have evaluated LASN01, a potent fully human monoclonal antibody to IL-11R which blocks IL-11 signaling, both preclinically and clinically. Initially, TED patient derived orbital fibroblasts were freshly isolated from surgical orbital decompression tissue and used in translational, in vitro studies. Consistent with literature reports, IL-11 and IL-11R expression were increased in TED orbital fibroblasts relative to healthy controls. Using disease-relevant stimuli including IL-11, IGF-1 and TGFβ, the effects of IL-11 and LASN01 on multiple disease parameters were measured. IL-11 was found to stimulate inflammatory cytokine production as well as cell proliferation and the production of hyaluronan (HA), which were efficiently blocked by LASN01. As the IGF-1 pathway is an established disease driver in TED, we also compared the ability of LASN01 and Teprotumumab to block disease relevant readouts. Bulk RNAseq analysis revealed that combined activation of the IGF-1 and IL-11 pathways induces the expression of an array of fibroinflammatory and lipid biosynthetic genes, and that LASN01 can modulate multiple fibroinflammatory genes, whereas Teprotumumab inhibited more lipid biosynthetic pathways.

Clinical studies show that LASN01 is well tolerated, and in a placebo-controlled phase II trial in TED, LASN01 resulted in a statistically significant clinical activity score (CAS) response (achieving a CAS of 0 or 1) in 88% of treated patients but had lesser effects on reducing proptosis. Adipogenesis in TED is an important contributor to disease16, and it is therefore hypothesized here that response to Teprotumumab therapy, may be at least in part be dependent on blockade of adipogenesis and the expansion of fatty tissue, whereas other disease signs and symptoms, measured by the composite endpoint of CAS, can be driven by IL-11 dependent fibroinflammatory processes. Based on the preclinical and clinical results in this study, IL-11 signaling is clearly relevant to fibroinflammatory biology in TED which may have broad implications in multiple diseases.

Results

IL-11 activates multiple signaling pathways in TED patient derived orbital fibroblasts (OF)

Assembly of the IL-11 signaling complex requires IL-11 binding to IL-11 receptor α (IL-11Rα) followed by recruitment of gp130 to form a hexameric complex on the cell surface containing two molecules each of IL-11, IL-11R and gp13021,22. Formation of this signaling complex can result in activation of multiple intracellular pathways dependent on the cellular context, including JAK/STAT3, ERK/MAPK and PI3K/AKT1. To assess which pathways might play a role in the action of IL-11 in TED, orbital tissue samples, consisting of adipose and connective tissues, were obtained from TED patients undergoing surgical decompression. Orbital fibroblasts were isolated from this tissue from 22 individual donors. Initially we sought to replicate published results that IL-11 and IL-11Rα were upregulated in TED decompression tissue8,23 and demonstrated that this was also true in OF from the tissue we obtained (Fig. 1a, b). The standard of care therapeutic agent for TED in the US is Teprotumumab, an antibody to IGF-1 receptor (IGF-1R) which blocks signaling through IGF-1, and so we compared the action of IL-11 and IGF-1 directly on these cells. Because IGF-1 and IL-11 signaling have been reported to activate similar downstream signaling pathways, especially ERK/MAPK and PI3K/AKT24, we directly measured phosphorylation of STAT3, ERK and AKT in TED patient derived cells and found that all three pathways were stimulated by IL-11, whereas IGF-1 appeared to result in weaker activation of ERK and stronger activation of AKT (Fig. 1c, Supplemental Figure S1). The combination of IL-11 and IGF-1 gave a particularly strong activation of the AKT pathway. It was also demonstrated that LASN01, a human monoclonal antibody to IL-11Rα, was able to block the induction of signaling in all three pathways.

LASN01 was originally isolated from a human phage display library and matured to high affinity to IL-11Rα25. The binding affinity was evaluated by biolayer interferometry using an Octet instrument and resulted in a KD measurement of 37pM. Functional potency in the inhibition of IL-11 induced signaling was also evaluated and resulted in determination of sub-nM potency with IC50 values of 0.4 nM and 0.1 nM for pSTAT3 and pERK, respectively (Fig. 1d, e).

Blocking IL-11 signaling inhibits biological processes relevant to TED

There are a number of biological processes which contribute to the pathophysiology of TED including the recruitment and activation of immune cells, overproduction of hyaluronan (HA) contributing to tissue edema, adipogenesis and the expansion of fatty tissue, and production of extracellular matrix molecules such as collagen leading to tissue stiffening and fibrosis16. IL-11 has been associated with the induction of collagen and hyaluronan in orbital fibroblasts obtained from TED patients8,23, and so we sought to extend the investigation of LASN01 activity across these different biological readouts and compare that activity with Teprotumumab. HA production has been used as a translational readout for TED therapeutics including during the development of Teprotumumab26,27. IL-11 induced the production of HA in OF and HA was potently inhibited by LASN01 with an IC50 of 50 ng/mL (Fig. 2a). In order to compare anti-IL-11Rα with anti-IGF-1R antibodies, we developed conditions in which both the IGF-1 and IL-11 pathways were fully activated as determined experimentally from IL-11 and IGF-1 dose responses in TED patient OF (supplemental Fig. S2). This was achieved with 10 ng/mL IL-11 plus 100 ng/mL of IGF-1. Activation of both pathways led to HA production which exceeded that from stimulating either pathway alone (Supplemental Fig. S3), and was blocked to a similar extent by either LASN01 or Teprotumumab at their maximally effective doses (Fig. 2b).

Orbital fibroblasts can secrete multiple pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines when stimulated with IL-11 and IGF-1, which likely contributes to the extensive local inflammation seen in active TED. In a proteomic experiment we compared LASN01 and Teprotumumab under the same stimulatory conditions and measured an array of proinflammatory factors (Fig. 2c). LASN01 demonstrated broad inhibition of multiple proinflammatory mediators and follow up experiments verified this data with several cytokines and chemokines by ELISA measurement. IL-6 and CCL2 two factors previously associated with the recruitment and activation of immune cells in TED28–30, were effectively inhibited by LASN01 treatment (Fig. 2d, e).

TGFβ is widely recognized as a potent pro-fibrotic factor, which can also induce the secretion of IL-11 across cell types3,31. LASN01 was able to inhibit the production of TGFβ-induced collagen from TED OF as shown by the decreased release of procollagen I from these cells (Fig. 2f). In addition, the amount of procollagen released significantly correlated with the secretion of IL-11 from the cells (Fig. 2g).

Regulation of gene expression in TED induced by IL-11 and IGF-1

Further understanding of the effects of IL-11 on TED OF was gained by a comparison of differentially expressed genes (DEG) induced by IL-11 or the combination of IL-11 and IGF-1 and their regulation by LASN01 or teprotumumab. OF from 8 TED donors were stimulated for 6–48 h with IL-11 (10 ng/mL) or IL-11 plus IGF-1 (10 and 100 ng/mL respectively) and the full transcriptome profiled by bulk RNAseq analysis. IL-11 resulted in significant upregulation of 568 genes and downregulation of 387 genes at 6 h, which increased to 960 upregulated and 764 downregulated at 48 h (Fig. 3a, supplemental table ST1). As expected, co-stimulation with IL-11 and IGF-1 resulted in regulation of a larger number of genes, with 1798 up,1597 down at 6 h, and 1679 up and 1679 down at 48 h (Fig. 3b, Supplemental table ST2).

Conversely, 522 of the IL-11 stimulated genes at 6 h were decreased by LASN01 treatment and 300 genes were increased. VENN analysis was then used to identify 39 common genes that were inversely regulated by both IL-11 and LASN01 with a high statistical probability (Log2 fold change ≥ 1; padj ≤ 0.05), indicating that these are true IL-11-dependent DEG. Those DEG with the highest degree of regulation at this early timepoint included GBP1P, STEAP4, IDO1, SERPINB4 and MYCL (Supplemental table ST3). Similar analysis at the 48 h timepoint resulted in identification of 95 IL-11 pathway-specific genes including some of those seen at 6 h as well as other TED-related (HAS2), complement factor (C3 and C4B), fibrotic (APLNR, CHI3L1) and lipofibroblast-associated (RASD1) genes (supplemental table ST4).

Under joint stimulation with IL-11 and IGF-1 for 6 h 539 DEG were blocked by LASN01 treatment. 27 of these genes were commonly regulated by IL-11 + IGF-1 and blocked by LASN01 (Supplemental table ST5). At 48 h a greater number of genes were regulated by IL-11 + IGF-1 and a larger subset of these genes were downregulated by LASN01 (845 DEG). Analysis of the commonly regulated genes within this group revealed 84 DEG that were seen in response to IL-11 stimulation alone (Supplemental table ST6). To identify specific genes that are commonly regulated by both IL-11 and IGF-1, VENN analysis was performed on data from TED OF that were stimulated with IL-11, IL-11 + LASN01, IL-11 + IGF-1 and IL-11 + IGF-1 + LASN01 treatment groups. (Supplemental Fig S4). This analysis revealed 63 genes that were impacted by all 4 treatments. Heat map analysis revealed a number of genes that were increased by IL-11 + IGF-1 stimulation, compared to IL-11 alone, but that were also inhibited by LASN01 under both stimulation conditions (Supplementary Fig. S2b). These genes included STEAP4, HTRA2, DSG2, MIAT SLC2A5, DDIT4L, CROCC2, CLCA2, JUNB, S1PR1, ABLIM2 and HAS2.

Gene ontology (GO) analysis was performed to better understand which biological pathways were impacted by LASN01 compared to Teprotumumab. GO analysis was performed on LASN01 and teprotumumab-treated OF following stimulation with IL-11 + IGF-1. 1950 total genes were impacted with 1361 being regulated by LASN01, 509 genes regulated by teprotumumab and 80 genes being commonly impacted by both (Fig. 3c) GO analysis indicated that IL-11 + IGF-1 stimulation significantly (padj < 0.05) affected a diverse array of genes including those associated with lipid metabolism, ER stress, oxygen response, cell: cell and cell: substrate interactions,, focal adhesions and glycosaminoglycan binding (Fig. 3c). Among these, LASN01 primarily inhibited pathways associated with inflammation, cytokine signaling, glycosaminoglycan production and fibrosis but did not appear to directly impact genes related to lipid biosynthesis or adipogenesis (Fig. 3d). In contrast, teprotumumab, downregulated GO pathways related to lipid, cholesterol and steroid biosynthesis and metabolism (Fig. 3e). These data suggest potentially diverse roles of IL-11 and IGF-1 in regulating key drivers of TED pathogenesis.

Signaling pathways responsible for IL-11 effects

Since the RNAseq studies indicated substantial gene regulation by LASN01 we verified that biological effects were driven at the transcriptional level by qRT-PCR. Key effector molecules regulated at the gene level included HAS2, the major HA synthetase identified in OF32, and representatives of the induced proinflammatory factors IL-6 and CCL2 (Fig. 4a-c). Teprotumumab appeared to have lesser effects on HAS2 gene regulation and may inhibit HA production through an alternate mechanism. Since the two pathways appear distinct, we evaluated the combination of LASN01 and Teprotumumab on the production of HA in OF and found that combination treatment was able to further reduce HA to near baseline levels (Fig. 4d). Contrasting results were obtained when assessing the effect on IL-6 where LASN01 inhibited secretion but addition of Teprotumumab had no further impact (Fig. 2). To determine which of the IL-11 intracellular signaling pathways were involved in HA production we evaluated inhibitors of STAT3, ERK and AKT on the production of HA in OF (Fig. 4e). Inhibiting the PI3K/AKT pathway appeared to abrogate IL-11 stimulation of HA production, whereas no inhibition was observed with inhibitors of the other pathways, suggesting that PI3K/AKT signaling is responsible at least in part for the downregulation of HAS2 by LASN01. Other biological effects of IL-11 may be driven through the STAT3 and/or ERK pathways, and further studies are required to fully elucidate the pathways involved.

Safety and PK of LASN01 in human healthy volunteers, TED and IPF patients

A phase 1 clinical trial was initiated with a single ascending dose study of LASN01 in healthy volunteers, starting at a fixed dose of 25 mg and rising to100, 300, 600 and 1200 mg per subject (see study schema in Supplemental Figure S5). Each dose group consisted of 8 subjects, randomized in a 3:1 ratio, active to placebo, with the aim to assess safety, pharmacokinetics and anti-drug antibody responses (Part A). Subject demographics and baseline characteristics are shown in supplementary table ST7. The behavior of LASN01 was typical for a human monoclonal antibody with a long half-life allowing extended exposure (Fig. 5a; Table 1). Clearance appeared faster at the lowest dose (25 mg), suggesting some target mediated disposition, but above 100 mg dose-linear PK was observed. LASN01 was well tolerated, and no treatment related serious or severe adverse events were observed. The only drug-related event observed in more than 5% of patients was mild to moderate headache readily treated with standard of care therapy acetaminophen or ibuprofen. Overall, a similar incidence of adverse events was observed in the placebo group and the LASN01 treated participants, and such events were not dose related (Table 2). As the single dose of LASN01 was well tolerated a multi-dose study was conducted with subjects receiving two doses each at the top two doses (600 and 1200 mg) two weeks apart (Part B). The results were consistent with the single dose study with the expected PK and no new safety observations (Fig. 5c; Table 2).

IL-11 signaling has been reported to take place through both classic (cis) and trans mechanisms33,34. Classic signaling occurs when both IL-11R and gp130 are present on the same cell, whereas trans signaling occurs through shedding of soluble IL-11R, its association with IL-11 and subsequent binding to a different cell. As we were unable to detect IL-11R on circulating blood cells, or significant levels of IL-11 in the circulation of healthy individuals, we devised an assay of trans-signaling to assess whether the antibody in circulation remained active for a prolonged period.

This used whole blood samples from treated individuals stimulated ex vivo with hyper IL-11, a reagent consisting of IL-11R fused to IL-11 to form a stable complex35. This assay revealed complete suppression of pSTAT3 with the duration of suppression being dose dependent, demonstrating that the circulating antibody remained active for up to two months after the last dose at the 600 and 1200 mg doses administered (Fig. 5b, d).

In order to assess safety and PK in patients we expanded the phase 1 study to include two small patient cohorts, one with pulmonary fibrosis (Part C), including IPF, and one with TED (Part D). Ten patients were enrolled in the pulmonary disease group and seven in the TED group, randomized to receive either 1200 mg LASN01 or placebo at the 3:1 randomization ratio of active vs. placebo. Patients with pulmonary disease received a second dose after two weeks, and then Q4W dosing for up to 22 weeks and patients with TED received Q4W dosing up to 24 weeks. LASN01 was similarly well tolerated in these patient groups with no serious or severe drug-related adverse events (Table 2). No new safety events were observed during the patient cohorts. PK was also similar to the healthy volunteers (Fig. 5e, g), and the antibody again remained available in circulation (Fig. 5f, h). No significant anti-drug antibodies were observed in any of the phase 1 subjects, even after prolonged dosing in the patient cohorts.

Targeting IL-11R is pharmacologically active in human subjects

While the phase I study was not designed to assess efficacy, we did observe that all patients with TED treated with LASN01 had an improvement in their clinical activity score (CAS) (Suppl. Table ST8), a composite endpoint which measures disease activity and includes clinical signs of pain and inflammation36. To assess this potential activity in a larger study we initiated a placebo-controlled double-blinded phase 2 clinical study in patients with active moderate to severe TED (see study schema in Supplemental Figure S6). Patients were randomized to receive monthly treatment with one of 300 mg LASN01, 600 mg LASN01 or placebo every 4 weeks with 8 subjects per group for up to 48 weeks. Patient demographics are shown in supplemental table ST9. LASN01 was again well tolerated with no serious or severe drug-related adverse events observed (Table 3). Again, the only TEAE observed at over 5% was mild or moderate headache mainly observed on the first dose of LASN01 that was similar in incidence to placebo. Efficacy was assessed using proptosis response and CAS response with the primary endpoint assessments at 37 weeks. A pooled analysis of patients on LASN01 300 mg and 600 mg vs. placebo was conducted. Patient characteristics are shown in table ST9. We note that the patients enrolled in the Ph 2 study have milder disease than other reported TED studies possibly contributing to a smaller proptosis change observed over the course of the study (mean proptosis score 21.5 mm/CAS 4.5 in 600 mg treatment arm, 22.2 mm/CAS 5.1 in the 300 mg arm compared to mean proptosis 22.6 mm/CAS 5.1 in the Teprotumumab OPTIC study37. Results demonstrated a non-significant trend toward improvement in proptosis, with 35% of patients responding to therapy compared to 33.5% of placebo treated patients (Table 4). Proptosis response is defined as 2 mm or greater reduction in proptosis from baseline measurement. Response rates were similar between the 300 mg and 600 mg dose groups at 3/9 (33.3%) and 3/8 (37.5%). The placebo response rate was higher than seen in many TED studies, although some recent studies demonstrate a similar high placebo response in TED36. In a post-hoc analysis excluding patients with baseline proptosis < 21 mm (who were all of Hispanic or Arabian descent) the response rate was 50% (6/12) in the pooled treatment groups and 37.5% (3/8) in the placebo group. In the 600 mg treatment group 75% (3/4) of these patients responded, and excluding one of these patients who only received one dose 100% (3/3) responded, suggesting that low baseline proptosis may limit the ability to accurately assess response rates. The small sample sizes limits the statistical interpretation of these findings.

LASN01 therapy did result in a clinically and statistically significant CAS response in 88% of the pooled LASN01 group compared to 44% in the placebo group (p = 0.028) (Fig. 6; Table 4). The CAS response rate observed was larger than observed in any other trial to date. Patients were required to have a CAS score of 4 or more to enter into the study (see methods section) and resolved to a CAS score of zero or one. 59% of patients responded with a CAS score of zero, which represents a complete resolution of all CAS parameters. As the CAS parameters include measures of redness, inflammation and pain, this suggests that LASN01 demonstrates clinically significant anti-inflammatory activity in this patient population.

Discussion

The role of the IL-11 pathway in fibroinflammatory diseases has been controversial. Initial reports suggested that IL-11 had anti-inflammatory activity whereas later work reported more proinflammatory activity2,39–41. Recent studies have suggested that a misconception of anti-inflammatory activity arose due to the use of human IL-11 in mouse models rather than the native mouse IL-11, since human IL-11 may function as an antagonist rather than agonist of the mouse pathway2. Since then, more extensive studies in knockout animals and therapeutic models suggest that IL-11 is a potent proinflammatory factor acting on stromal cells to promote fibroinflammatory disease in a number of tissues6–8,10,12,13,42,43. In this study we sought to determine the therapeutic potential of blocking IL-11 signaling with an anti-IL-11 receptor monoclonal antibody, LASN01, in an exemplary disease characterized by inflammation transitioning to fibrosis, thyroid eye disease.

To avoid species specific issues, we chose to understand the preclinical biology in patient derived tissue, a system as close as possible to clinical application, and then demonstrated that LASN01 was also pharmacologically active in human subjects. We confirmed that the IL-11 pathway is likely activated in human subjects by demonstrating that IL-11 and its sole receptor are upregulated in TED orbital fibroblasts which are strongly associated as disease driving cells in TED16. In the context of TED fibroblasts three intracellular pathways are activated by IL-11 (STAT3, ERK and AKT) emphasizing the potential for pleiotropic effects on the biology. IL-11 signaling was involved across multiple pathobiological effects in TED patient derived orbital fibroblasts, including the stromal secretion of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines, and the production of extracellular matrix molecules such as HA and collagen. We then compared the activity of blocking IL-11 signaling with the anti-IL-11 receptor antibody LASN01 with blocking the IGF-1 pathway using the standard of care therapeutic agent Teprotumumab.

Tissue fibroblasts play an important role in local inflammation44, and the increased production of proinflammatory factors under the influence of IL-11 suggests an important role in potentiation of chronic inflammation. Notably this was also suggested by our gene regulation studies and importantly by the clinical activity observed by blocking IL-11 signaling, resulting in significant resolution of inflammatory disease signs and symptoms. IL-11 has also been associated with the differentiation of fibroblasts and deposition of extracellular matrix molecules across different tissues2, but this finding has also been controversial with some reports suggesting a lesser role for IL-11 in fibroblast differentiation45,46. In TED orbital fibroblasts we demonstrate that inhibition of the IL-11 pathway can lead to reduction of the AKT dependent generation of the glycosaminoglycan HA, as well as collagen, and multiple proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines, suggesting the potential for the reduction of fibroinflammatory signs and symptoms in this disease.

Further support for the proinflammatory effect of IL-11 and anti-inflammatory effect of blockade is provided by analysis of differential gene expression with LASN01 regulating pathways linked to inflammation, cytokine signaling and extracellular matrix production. This is in contrast to the IGF-1R mAb Teprotumumab which appeared to preferentially regulate pathways related to adipogenesis and lipid production.

Clinically, we demonstrate that blocking IL-11 signaling in adults for a prolonged period up to 12 months is safe and well tolerated, with no serious or severe drug related events observed. LASN01 demonstrated pharmacokinetics typical of a monoclonal antibody and remained active in circulation as demonstrated by its continued ability to inhibit the phosphorylation of STAT3 under ex vivo stimulation in the whole blood assay. In a phase 2 clinical trial LASN01 resulted in a statistically significant resolution of CAS, a composite disease score made of several inflammatory disease assessments. This suggests both that the IL-11 axis is activated in TED and that the anti-inflammatory effect of blocking IL-11 signaling seen in in vitro experiments, both by inhibition of cytokine production and by gene expression studies, is also clinically relevant in human patients with TED.

Proptosis is an important clinical sign in TED, and an improvement in proptosis requires reduction in the volume of orbital tissue. The production of HA has been used as a translational readout in a number of studies looking at novel drug development for TED26,27, and as a hydrophilic polymer it is thought to contribute to the tissue edema, and hence orbital tissue volume, seen in the disease. Blocking both IL-11R and IGF-1R can inhibit HA production under dual pathway activation, albeit through potentially different mechanisms. Tissue volume in the orbit is also influenced by the expansion of fatty tissue though differentiation of cells to adipocytes and the accumulation of fat. Gene expression studies with Teprotumumab revealed the regulation of a number of lipid biosynthetic and adipogenic pathways, consistent with reported anti-adipogenic activity of IGF-1R inhibitors47. Given that Teprotumumab has significant activity in reducing proptosis in TED patients with response rates reported of 70–80% (50–70% placebo adjusted)39 it is possible that the regulation of adipogenesis may play a role in tissue expansion and the development of proptosis. Further studies would be required to determine whether inhibition of adipogenesis may play a more important role in reduction of proptosis than previously considered. In contrast Teprotumumab and other anti-IGF1R mAbs37,48 have a lower reported percent of patients that achieve a CAS response (score of 0 or 1), consistent with lesser effects on fibroinflammatory mediators seen in our gene expression studies. Given the different pathways affected by IGF-1R inhibitors and LASN01 and their different clinical profiles, a combination of these agents targeting both pathways may bring additional benefits to TED patients offering a novel, safe and well-tolerated combination regimen.

IL-11 is a member of the IL-6 family of cytokines, and IL-6 is also upregulated in TED and has been implicated in the disease biology, particularly in inflammatory aspects49–51. Therapy with anti-IL6 agents such as the anti-IL6 receptor antibody Tocilizumab has also demonstrated reductions in CAS and inflammation in disease52. The receptors for IL-6 and IL-11 are generally restricted to different cell types with IL-6R on cells of the immune system such as B and T cells, and IL-11R on stromal cells such as fibroblasts3, and it is likely that there is interaction between these pathways and cell types. As we have demonstrated IL-11 is an inducer of IL-6 production, as well as other pro-inflammatory mediators, from OF and therefore inhibition of IL-6 production may contribute to the mechanism of action of IL-11R blockade on inhibition of inflammatory symptoms of TED.

This study demonstrates that the IL-11 pathway is important in fibroinflammation in TED and suggests that inhibition of this pathway has therapeutic activity. IL-11 upregulation is common in several other fibroinflammatory conditions, such as IPF, systemic sclerosis, IBD and others3,5–7. In addition, IL-11 signaling has recently been reported to play an important role in aging. IL-11 is a major component of the senescence associated secretory phenotype53, and IL-11 genetic knockout or pharmacological blockade in mice led to extended lifespan and healthspan54. Aged individuals are reported to have higher levels of inflammatory markers and systemic chronic low-grade inflammation, often referred to as inflammaging55. This is consistent with the active proinflammatory role for IL-11 demonstrated here and suggests that investigation of IL-11R blockade in diseases associated with aging may be worthwhile. Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is one such disease that is associated with aging and characterized by fibroinflammation. IL-11 is upregulated in IPF and its expression is associated with both disease severity and prognosis56. In addition, blocking IL-11 signaling has been shown to be beneficial in models of IPF9,57 an attractive disease for further evaluation of IL-11 biology and the therapeutic potential of LASN01.

Materials and methods

Antibodies

Antibodies and reagents: LASN01 IgG4 and non-specific isotype control IgG4 were generated at Lassen Therapeutics (San Diego, CA.). Teprotumumab biosimilar antibody was purchased from Ichorbio (Oxfordshire, UK). Recombinant human Interleukin-11 and IGF-1 were purchased from R&D systems (Minneapolis, MN.)

Analysis of binding affinity by Biolayer Interferometry (Octet) analysis

Binding kinetic measurements were taken on a Fortébio Octet RED96e instrument. LASN01 was captured on the biosensor surface using anti-human antibody (Fc specific) and the binding of soluble IL-11Rα was determined by using a concentration series of IL-11Rα up to 10 nM.

Orbital fibroblast isolation

Orbital adipose/connective tissue was isolated from thyroid eye disease patients during orbital decompression surgery. All surgeries were performed at the Byers Eye Institute, Stanford University School of Medicine, Palo Alto, CA. or Silkiss Eye Surgery, Oakland, CA. The research was approved following independent IRB review by Advarra (Columbia, MD) or by Stanford University IRB and all tissue were collected under patient informed consent. Tissue from 22 individuals was collected in total. Following collection, tissues were minced into 1–2 mm pieces, placed in T75 culture flasks containing 15 mL of growth media (high glucose Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/ streptomycin; all from Gibco Laboratories, New York, USA), and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Culture medium was renewed every 3–4 days and tissue was maintained in culture for 2–3 weeks to allow the OF to migrate out of the tissue and adhere. Floating debris was cleared, and attached cells were expanded and used up to 6 passages.

pSTAT3 and pERK assays

DLD-1 cells were purchased from ATCC (cat. no. CCL-221) and then grown and passaged as recommended by the vendor. For the assay, cells were plated in full growth media (RPMI Thermo Fisher cat. no. 61870036 + 10% FBS + 1x pen/strep) at 5 × 105 cells per well in a 6-well plate and allowed to attach overnight. Media was then removed, cells were washed once with PBS, and switched into 1.35 mL of starvation media (RPMI + 0.1% BSA) for 18–24 h. On the day of the assay, 150 µL of 10x test article was added in a serial dilution series to each well of the 6-well plate. Assays were run using 6–8 different concentrations to generate an inhibition curve. After 30 min of pre-incubation with test article, cells were stimulated with either 50 ng/mL of IL-11 or 100 ng/mL of hyper IL-11 depending upon the assay and incubated for 30 min prior to lysis. For lysis, cells were washed once with 2 mL of ice-cold PBS before addition of 400 µL of lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher cat. no. FNN0071) containing 1 x HALT protease and phosphatase inhibitor (LifeTechnologies cat. no. 78441) was added to the cell monolayer. Cells were incubated with lysis buffer for 5 min on ice before lysates were transferred into Eppendorf tubes and spun at maximum speed (> 16k rpm) at 4 °C for 5 min. Lysate samples were then transferred to a new Eppendorf tube leaving the pellet behind to be discarded. The phosphoSTAT3 ELISA was performed using the human/mouse phospho-STAT3 (Y705) DuoSet from R&D systems (cat. no. DYC4607B-2) according to manufacturer specifications. In brief, plates were coated with 6 µg/mL Human/Mouse Phospho-STAT3 (Y705) Capture Antibody (Part 842631) at 4 °C overnight. After 3 washes using wash buffer (PBS + 0.05% Tween 20), plates were then blocked by addition of PBS containing 1% BSA + 0.05% sodium azide for 1–2 h at room temperature. Plates were then washed 3 times in wash buffer prior to addition of lysate samples or standards. Cell lysate was added neat to the coated plate, and diluent #3 alone was also added to the plate as the zero standard. Plates were sealed and incubated with samples or standards for 2 h at room temperature on a shaking incubator at 600RPM followed by washing 3 times with wash buffer. Detection antibody (Part #842632) was diluted to 100 ng/mL in the manufacturer provided Diluent 1 before 100 µL per well was added to the plate. Plates were incubated with detection antibody for 2 h at room temperature. Plates were then washed 3 times in wash buffer before 100 µL of streptavidin A diluted 1:200 in Diluent 1 was added to each well. The plate was then incubated for 20 min at room temperature in the dark. Plates were subsequently washed 3 times in wash buffer before adding 100 µL per well of Ultra TMB (Thermo Fisher cat. no. 34028) to visualize. Reaction was stopped after sufficient development by adding 50 µL per well of 2 M sulfuric acid stop solution. Plates were read at 450 nm with wavelength correction at 540 nm on a SpectraMax iD5 plate reader (Molecular Devices). The phospho-ERK1 (T202/Y204) was similarly performed using the Phospho-ERK1 (T202/Y204)/ERK2 (T185/Y187) DuoSet IC ELISA kit (R&D Systems cat. no. DYC101B-2). For this assay plates were coated with 4 µg/mL Human/Mouse Phospho-ERK1 (T202/Y204) Capture Antibody (Part 841452), and 500 ng/mL Human/Mouse Phospho-ERK1 Detection Antibody (Part 841453) was used for detection. All other steps in the assay protocol were identical to human phospho-STAT3 detection described above.

Capillary blot

Orbital fibroblasts were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells/well (2 mL volume) in growth media and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2. After 24 h media was replaced with DMEM supplemented with penicillin, streptomycin and 1% FBS for an additional 24 h. For assaying phospho-proteins, media was replaced with Opti-MEM I (Cat#31985070, Gibco) for 1 h for complete serum starvation. Serum-starved cells were pretreated with LASN01 antibody at 1 µg/mL or 10 µg/mL in Opti-MEM I for 1 h. Cells were subsequently treated with agonists IL-11 (10ng/mL), IGF-1 (100ng/mL) or a combination of IL-11 and IGF-1. Media was aspirated and cells were briefly washed with chilled PBS before collection for lysis and protein isolation. Whole cell lysates prepared by briefly sonicating cells in RIPA lysis and extraction buffer (Cat#89901, Thermo Scientific) supplemented with Halt Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail, EDTA-free (Cat#78445, ThermoScientific). Capillary blot was performed on a Protein Simple Jess capillary blot machine (Bio-techne, Minneapolis, MN.) using a 12–230 kDA Separation Module (Bio-techne Protein Simple Cat# SM-W004) in Replex Mode with default detection (High Dynamic Range) for chemiluminescence according to manufacturer’s instructions. STAT3 (79D7) Rabbit antibody (Cat# 4904; 1:50 dilution), Phospho-STAT3 (Tyr705) (D3A7) XP® Rabbit antibody (Cat# 9145; 1:50 dilution) were purchased from Cell Signaling Technologies (Danvers, MA.). Signal intensities for individual capillaries and predicted molecular weights for each antibody target were quantified using Dropped Lines (manual) mode in the Peak Fit analysis function of the Protein Simple Compass software.

Inhibitor assays

Cells were seeded as described above and subsequently serum-reduced for 24 h, Serum-reduced cells were pre-treated for 1 h with an AKT Inhibitor (MK2206 2HCl; Cat# S1078, Selleckchem), and then cells were treated with 10 ng/mL of IL-11 (Cat#218-IL/CF-MTO, R&D Systems) for 96 h. Conditioned media was analyzed by Hyaluronan DuoSet ELISA (Cat# DY3614, R&D Systems) according to manufacturer’s instructions.

Cytokine, HA and procollagen release

For cytokine, HA and procollagen release, OF (passage < 5) were plated on clear 6-well flat bottom plates (Gen Clone, cat #25–105) at 1 × 105 cells/ well (2 mL volume) in growth media consisting of Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) with high glucose GlutaMAX Supplement, pyruvate (Cat#10569010, Invitrogen, Life Technologies, Paisley, UK), supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum (Cat# S11150, Lot B23041), R&D Systems), 100 IU/mL penicillin, and 100 g/mL streptomycin (Cat#15140122, Invitrogen), and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2. The following day, media was changed to 100 µL (96 well plate) or 2 mL (6 well plate) of starvation media (growth media with 1% FBS) for 24 h to synchronize cells. The following day, the media was replaced with fresh serum-starvation media and cells were preincubated with the conditions listed for an additional 96 h. Following the 96 h stimulation period, an aliquot of media was removed and frozen at − 80 °C for analysis of HA, IL-6 and CCL2 release using the human HA (R&D Systems, catalog# DY3614), IL-6 (Cat#: DY206, R&D Systems) CCL2 (Cat#DY279, R&D Systems) DuoSet ELISA’s according to manufacturer’s recommendations. The cytokine panel was performed by Luminex multiplex assay (Eve Technologies, Calgary, CAN.). Procollagen I was analyzed using the Human Pro-Collagen I alpha 1 ELISA Kit (Abcam; ab210966) per manufacturer’s recommendations.

RNA Isolation and qRT-PCR

RNA was isolated from fibroblast cells using the RNeasy Plus Mini kit (Cat#74134, Qiagen) according to manufacturer instructions. Briefly, media was aspirated from wells and replaced with RLT plus buffer supplemented with betamercaptoethanol. Lysates were homogenized through a QIAshredder (Cat# 79656, Qiagen) column by centrifugation according to manufacturer’s instructions. The flow-through was combined at a ratio of 1:1 v/v with 70% ethanol and passed through the RNA binding columns via centrifugation from the RNeasy Plus Mini kit. The columns were sequentially washed once with RW1 and twice with RPE buffers. RNA was eluted with nuclease-free water. RNA concentration and quality (average 260/280 ratio of 2) was determined by spectrophotometry. Reverse transcription of 0.6 µg of RNA was performed by PCR using High-Capacity cDNA Reverse transcription kit (Cat# 4368814, Life Technologies). After completion of cDNA synthesis, samples were diluted 1:5 with nuclease-free water and assay by qPCR using TaqMan primers for HAS2 (Hs00193435_m1), HPRT1 (Hs99999909_m1), IL6 (Hs00985639_m1), and IL33 (Hs04931857_m1) and TaqMan™ Fast Advanced Master Mix (Cat# 4444963, ThermoFisher Scientific). The qPCR data were analyzed using the ΔΔCt method. Data was analyzed with an ordinary one-way ANOVA with Fisher’s LSD test (no correction for multiple comparisons, each comparison stands alone) on Prism GraphPad version 10.0.2. Data are plotted as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM).

RNAseq analyses

Bulk RNA sequencing and Bioinformatics analyses were performed by Novogene (Sacramento, CA.). Treatment comparisons are listed in Table 1. After the gene expression was quantified, statistical analysis of the expression data was performed to identify the genes whose expression levels were significantly different based on the treatment conditions. Volcano plots were used to infer the overall distribution of differentially expressed genes whereby the x-axis shows the fold change in gene expression (Log2 fold change) between different samples, and the y-axis shows the statistical significance of those differences (– log10 p value). The dashed line indicates the threshold line of Log10 p-value ≥ 1.3 (p ≤ 0.05) for differential gene expression. Red dots represent up-regulated genes and blue dots represent down-regulated genes. For the volcano plot analyses, all DEG with a Log2 Fold Change ≥ 0 and p ≤ 0.05 are shown to provide a DEG summary across the entire transcriptome for each treatment. For all subsequent VENN and heat map analyses, the statistical cut-off was set at Log 2 fold change ≥ 1 and padj ≤ 0.05 to identify more robust DEGs .

Statistical analyses

All values are expressed as the means ± S.E.M. Statistical analysis was performed by use of Student’s t test for two-group analysis or one-way ANOVA for multiple group comparisons followed by Dunnett’s post hoc comparisons (Prism; GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Phase 1 clinical study (LASN01-CL-1101)

This multicenter study was conducted in Australia, New Zealand and Hong Kong in compliance with ICH E6(R2) Good Clinical Practice (GCP) standards and all local regulatory requirements. The study was conducted at two Phase 1 Units and six outpatient clinics. The trial was approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the IRB or independent ethics committee at each site, and all participants provided informed consent. The study was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov on 16/03/2022 under the identifier: NCT05331300 (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05331300). This was a randomized (3:1), placebo controlled study conducted in 4 parts (Parts A-D) : Part A (40 healthy volunteers in 6 dose escalating cohorts of 25 mg to 1200 mg LASN01 or placebo) and Part B (18 healthy volunteers in 3 multiple dose cohorts of 600 mg LASN01, 1200 mg LASN01or placebo) evaluated the safety, PK, immunogenicity, target engagement of LASN01. Part C (10 participants with IPF/PF-ILD) and D (7 participants with TED) evaluated these parameters in patients with LASN01 1200 mg or placebo randomized 3:1 and given IV. Part C was dosed on day one, 15 and then Q4W, and Part D at Q4W throughout for 7 doses. The main endpoint visit was at Week 27 in Part C and Week 29 in Part D. The design of the study is illustrated in supplementary fig. S5.

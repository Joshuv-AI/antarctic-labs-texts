Abstract
. We prove that the jump quasi-seminorm of order ϱ = 2 \varrho=2 for a general Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup ( ℋ t ) t > 0 \left(\mathcal{H}_{t}\right)_{t>0} in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} defines an operator of weak type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) with respect to the invariant measure. This provides an example of a weak-type jump inequality for a nonsymmetric semigroup in a nondoubling measure space. Our result may be seen as an endpoint refinement of the weak type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) inequality for the ϱ \varrho -th order variation seminorm of ( ℋ t ) t > 0 \left(\mathcal{H}_{t}\right)_{t>0} , recently proved by the authors when ϱ > 2 \varrho>2 , and disproved for ϱ = 2 \varrho=2 .

Introduction

In this paper we study jump and variational
bounds of weak type
(1,1)
for a nonsymmetric Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup,
that is, for the semigroup
(\mathcal{H}_{t})_{t\geq 0}

generated by the operator

\mathcal{L}f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\,\tr\big(Q\nabla^{2}f\big)(x)+\langle Bx,\nabla f(x)\rangle\,,\qquad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Here
\nabla
is the gradient and
\nabla^{2}
the Hessian,
and
n\geq 1
.
The definition of
\mathcal{L}
depends on two real
n\times n
matrices,
Q
and
B
;
we assume that
Q
is symmetric and positive definite, and that all the eigenvalues of

B
have negative real parts.
In the following,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is equipped
with a Gaussian measure denoted by
\gamma_{\infty}
, which
is invariant under the action of
\mathcal{H}_{t}
; see (
3.7
)
for a precise definition of
\gamma_{\infty}
in terms of
Q
and
B
. This measure is locally but not globally doubling.
Under our assumptions on
Q
and
B
, neither
\mathcal{L}
nor
\mathcal{H}_{t}
is symmetric in
L^{2}(\gamma_{\infty})
in general.

If
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
is an interval and
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
, the
\varrho
-th order variational seminorm of
\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x)
on
\mathcal{I}
is defined by

(1.1)

\|\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f(x)\|_{v(\varrho),\mathcal{I}}=\sup\,\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\mathcal{H}_{t_{i}}f(x)-\mathcal{H}_{t_{i-1}}f(x)|^{\varrho}\right)^{1/\varrho},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad 1\leq\varrho<\infty,

the supremum being over all finite, increasing
sequences
\left(t_{i}\right)_{0}^{N}
of points in
\mathcal{I}
.
We emphasize that in this paper the
v(\varrho)
seminorm will be always taken in the variable
t
.

In a recent paper
the authors proved that for
\varrho>2
the variation operator mapping
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
to the function

x\mapsto\|\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\varrho),\mathbb{R}_{+}}

is bounded
from
L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
to
L^{1,\infty}(\gamma_{\infty})
(see
[
15
]
, and
[
14
]
for a different proof in the one-dimensional case).
The boundedness of this variation operator on
L^{p}(\gamma_{\infty}),\;1<p<\infty,

was already known
(see
[
26
, Corollary 4.5]
, and also
[
2
]
).

These results break down for
1\leq\varrho\leq 2
, as proved in
[
15
, Section 8]
.

Our main result in this paper is a jump inequality of weak type
(1,1)

for
\mathcal{H}_{t}
at
\varrho=2
.
We therefore need to introduce jump quasi-seminorms,
which we do only in the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck framework.

If
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{R}_{+}
is an interval,
\lambda>0
and
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
, the
\lambda
-jump counting function for

\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f
in
\mathcal{I}
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

is defined as

(1.2)

N_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x):\>t\in\mathcal{I})=\sup\big\{J\in\mathbb{N}:\,\text{ there exist points }\,t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{J}\text{ in $\mathcal{I}$ }\\
\text{ such that }\,|\mathcal{H}_{t_{i}}f(x)-\mathcal{H}_{t_{i-1}}f(x)|>\lambda\,\text{ for }\,i=2,\dots,J\big\}.

Then the jump quasi-seminorm of order
\varrho
of

\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f
in
\mathcal{I}
is

(1.3)

J_{\varrho}^{p}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f:\,t\in\mathcal{I})=\sup_{\lambda>0}\|\lambda\,\big(N_{\lambda}\big(\mathcal{H}_{t}f(\cdot):t\in\mathcal{I}\big)\big)^{1/\varrho}\|_{L^{p}(\gamma_{\infty})},\quad 1\leq p,\varrho<\infty.

Notice that the quasi-seminorms
J_{\varrho}^{p}(\cdot)
are decreasing in
\varrho
.

One can define
weak jump quasi-seminorms in a similar way,
by replacing the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\gamma_{\infty})
in (
1.3
) by a Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(\gamma_{\infty})

(see
[
31
, formula (1.2)]
);
we are mainly interested in the case
(p,q)=(1,\infty)
, where

(1.4)

J_{\varrho}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f:\,t\in\mathcal{I})=\sup_{\lambda>0}\|\lambda\,\big(N_{\lambda}\big(\mathcal{H}_{t}f(\cdot):t\in\mathcal{I}\big)\big)^{1/\varrho}\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\gamma_{\infty})}.

Since

\displaystyle\lambda\big(N_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x):\,\,t\in\mathcal{I})\big)^{1/\varrho}\leq\|\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f(x)\|_{v(\varrho),\mathcal{I}}\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for all
\varrho\geq 1
and
\lambda>0
(see
[
31
, formula (2.3)]
),
the jump quasi-seminorms

J_{\varrho}^{p}
are dominated by
the
L^{p}
norms of
variational seminorms of order
\varrho
, that is,

(1.5)

J_{\varrho}^{p}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f:\,t\in\mathcal{I})\leq\|\,\|\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f(\cdot)\|_{v(\varrho),\mathcal{I}}\|_{L^{p}(\gamma_{\infty})},\qquad\varrho\geq 1,\quad p\geq 1.

It also follows that

\displaystyle\big|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle:\lambda\left(N_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x):\,t\in\mathcal{I})\right)^{1/\varrho}>\alpha\}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\varrho),\mathcal{I}}>\alpha\}\right|,\qquad\alpha>0,

and thus

(1.6)

J_{\varrho}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f:\,t\in\mathcal{I})\leq\|\,\|\mathcal{H}_{t}f(\cdot)\|_{v(\varrho),\mathcal{I}}\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\gamma_{\infty})},\qquad\varrho\geq 1.

Notice that combining (
1.5
) with the results in
[
26
, Corollary 4.5]
and
[
2
, p. 31]

on the strong boundedness
L^{p}-L^{p}
of the operator

f\mapsto\|\mathcal{H}_{t}f\|_{v(\varrho),\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, for
1<p<\infty
and
\varrho>2

one immediately arrives at the following result.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\varrho>2
.
Then the jump quasi-seminorms of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup

\left(\mathcal{H}_{t}\right)_{t>0}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\gamma_{\infty})
, that is,

J_{\varrho}^{p}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f:\,t>0)\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\gamma_{\infty})}.

On the other hand, for
\varrho>2
one has

(1.7)

\|\,\|\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f\|_{v(\varrho),\mathcal{I}}\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\gamma_{\infty})}\lesssim_{\varrho}J_{2}^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f:\,t\in\mathcal{I})\leq\|\,\|\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f\|_{v(2),\mathcal{I}}\|_{L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})},

with the first inequality failing for
\varrho=2
.
See
[
32
, formula (1.4)]
or
[
31
, Lemma 2.3]
for the first inequality; the second is (
1.5
) with
p=1
.
Thus a jump inequality of weak type
(1,1)
for
\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f
at
\varrho=2

can be considered an endpoint refinement of the variational estimate.

We recall that jump and variational
inequalities
say that the jump quasi-seminorms or
the
L^{p}
norms of the variation seminorms of

(\mathcal{H}_{t}f)_{t\in\mathcal{I}}

are
bounded by the
L^{p}
norm of
f
.
Since the pioneering works of Lépingle, Gaposhkin and Bourgain
[
27
,
17
,
18
,
7
,
8
]
,
this kind of bounds has proved to be very useful
to measure and control the fluctuations of families of linear operators, and it often provides an answer to the problem of pointwise convergence.
The range of operators for which
variational bounds have been proved in the last thirty years is vaste
(see
e.g.

[
20
,
9
,
21
,
22
,
24
,
19
,
23
,
16
,
4
,
29
,
30
,
28
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
3
,
5
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
6
]
).
But only a few results
seem to concern the case of an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup,
despite the prototypical nature of
(\mathcal{H}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
as a possibly nonsymmetric semigroup in a nondoubling measure space.

The standard case of a symmetric Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup
is given by
Q=-B=I_{n}
, where
I_{n}
is the
n
-dimensional identity matrix.
In that particular case, a variational approach was successfully addressed in

[
19
,
16
]
, for
\varrho>2
.
Recent contributions in the nonsymmetric Ornstein–Uhlenbeck context are due to Almeida, Betancor, Farina, Quijano and Rodriguez-Mesa

[
1
,
2
]
; they prove some interesting variational inequalities, under the condition
\varrho>2
.

The counterexample exhibited in
[
15
, Section 8]
, which is based on earlier work due to Qian
[
39
]
, says that
for
\varrho=2

the variation seminorm of
\left(\mathcal{H}_{t}\right)_{t>0}

is neither of strong nor of weak type
(p,p)
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
, with respect to
\gamma_{\infty}
.
In the light of this counterexample and of (
1.5
) and (
1.6
),
it is reasonable to look for a weak type
(1,1)
jump inequality at
\varrho=2
.
Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

For all
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
one has

(1.8)

J_{2}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f:\,t>0)\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})}.

For
\varrho<2
an analogous estimate is not possible. Indeed, it has been proved in
[
31
, Lemma 2.3]
and in
[
34
, formula (2.20)]
, both applied with
r=2
and
p=1
, that
for all
1\leq\varrho<2
one has

(1.9)

\|\,\|\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f(x)\|_{v(2),\mathcal{I}}\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\gamma_{\infty})}\lesssim_{\varrho}J_{\varrho}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f:\,t\in\mathcal{I}).

As a consequence, the weak jump quasi-seminorms
J_{\varrho}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{t}\,f:\,t\in\mathcal{I})
are unbounded for all

\varrho\in[1,2)
.
We remark that in (
1.9
) one cannot replace
L^{1,\infty}(\gamma_{\infty})
by
L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
, as proved in

[
35
, Lemma 2.5, p. 3395]
;
this lemma is stated with
\varrho=2
, but the proof holds for any
1\leq\varrho\leq 2
.

In order better to understand the scheme of the proof of Theorem
1.2
in the following sections, we need to go a little deeper into the proof
of
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
, concerning the weak type
(1,1)
of the
\varrho
-th order variation seminorm of
\left(\mathcal{H}_{t}\right)_{t>0}
.
A basic ingredient of our analysis was
the Mehler kernel
K_{t}=K_{t}(x,u)
, that is, the integral kernel of the semigroup operator
\mathcal{H}_{t}
with respect to the invariant measure; see (
4.2
) for an
explicit expression of
K_{t}
.
It is well-known that
K_{t}
behaves very differently
in the global and in the local region,
that is, roughly speaking, away from or close to the diagonal
x=u

(more precise definitions will be given in Section

5
).
It also matters whether the time parameter
t
is large or small.
For this reason,
the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
requires several distinctions both in space and in time.

Some parts of the operator

f\mapsto\|\mathcal{H}_{t}f\|_{v(\varrho),\mathbb{R}_{+}}

turn out to be
of weak type
(1,1)
not only for
\varrho>2
, but even for
\varrho\geq 1
. Because of (
1.6
), the corresponding parts of

J_{\varrho}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f:\,t>0)

will then be controlled by the
L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
norm of
f
.
In this way, we shall see in Section
5

how the proof of Theorem
1.2
can be reduced to the case of the local region for
t\in(0,1]
.
This case will be treated in the last four sections, where we compare our semigroup with a convolution semigroup (essentially the heat semigroup), for which jump bounds are known
[
28
]
.
It is worth mentioning that, differently from
[
15
]
, we prove Theorem
1.2
without appealing to vector-valued Calderón–Zygmund theory.

Unexpectedly, the question whether
the jump quasi-seminorm
of a general Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

defines an operator of strong type
(p,p),\;1<p<\infty,
with respect to the invariant measure,
is still open.
Notice however that in the symmetric case (that is, when each operator
\mathcal{H}_{t}
is a self-adjoint operator)
the boundedness of
J^{p}_{2}(\mathcal{H}_{t}f:t>0)
follows
from

[
31
, Theorem 1.2]
.

The structure of the article is as follows. Section
2
gives a subadditivity property for the counting function. Then in Sections
3
and
4

we state basic facts concerning
the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup and the Mehler kernel, respectively.
The distinction between the local and the global part of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup operator
is presented in Section
5
, and some cases of Theorem
1.2
are verified (see above).
The remaining case of Theorem
1.2
is proved in
Sections 6–9. This is the local part for
t\in(0,1]
, and
the localization allows us to deal with one patch of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
at a time.
More precisely, in Section
6

we treat the case when
t\leq 1
is not too small (essentially
|x|^{-2}\leq t\leq 1
), proving the strong type
(p,p)
for
p>1
and the weak type
(1,1)

for the variation of the local part of
\mathcal{H}_{t}f
, and here
\varrho\geq 1
.
Dealing with the local part of
\mathcal{H}_{t}f
for small
t
(essentially
0<t\leq\min(1,{|x|^{-2}})
) is more involved.
In Section
7
we decompose
the semigroup operator into a sum of three difference operators
and a main operator. By means of some technical estimates
one proves in Section
8

strong and weak type estimates for the variation of
the difference operators, with
\varrho\geq 1
. In Section
9
we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
by showing that the main operator
satisfies both strong and weak jump inequalities for
\varrho=2
.

2.
Subadditivity

In this section, we describe a subadditivity property of the counting function, in a generic setting.
For a more comprehensive treatment of the variational and jump inequalities, we refer the reader to
[
34
,
35
]
or the recent monograph
[
25
]
.

Let
\varphi=\varphi(t)
be a continuous
real- or complex-valued function defined in an interval
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
The
\lambda
-jump counting function of
\varphi
in
\mathcal{I}
may be defined as in (
1.2
), with
\varphi(t)
replacing
\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x)
.
Then

N_{\lambda}\big(\varphi(t):\,t\in\mathcal{I}\big)
is subadditive in
\mathcal{I}
.
Moreover,
it satisfies a kind of quasi-subadditivity property with respect to
\varphi
;
more specifically, one has

(2.1)

N_{\lambda}\big(\varphi(t)+\psi(t):\,t\in\mathcal{I}\big)\leq 2\Big(N_{\lambda/4}\big(\varphi(t):\,t\in\mathcal{I}\big)+N_{\lambda/4}\big(\psi(t):\,t\in\mathcal{I}\big)\Big)

for all
\varphi,\psi
defined on
\mathcal{I}
and all
\lambda>0
.
For a proof we refer to
[
23
, formulas (2) and (3), p. 6712]
, where a slightly different
\lambda
-jump counting function is also involved.
This justifies calling
J_{\varrho}^{p}(\cdot)
and
J_{\varrho}^{1,\infty}(\cdot)
quasi-seminorms.

3.
A general Gaussian setting

We introduce some basic notions in the setting of Ornstein–Uhlenbeck analysis and recall some facts from
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

(1)

As stated in the introduction,

Q
is a real, symmetric and positive definite
n\times n
matrix, and

B
a real
n\times n
matrix whose eigenvalues have negative real parts.

(2)

The covariance matrices are defined by

(3.1)

Q_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{sB}Qe^{sB^{*}}ds,\qquad\text{ $t\in(0,+\infty]$}.

Both
Q_{t}
and
Q_{\infty}
are well defined, symmetric and positive definite.
Since
Q_{t}=t\,Q(1+\mathcal{O}(t))
as
t\to 0
, one also has

(3.2)

Q_{t}^{-1}=t^{-1}\,Q^{-1}(1+\mathcal{O}(t))

and

(3.3)

\det\,Q_{t}=t^{n}\,\det Q\,(1+\mathcal{O}(t))

as
t\to 0
. Further,

(3.4)

|Q_{t}^{-1}|\lesssim t^{-1}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad 0<t<1.

(3)

R(x)
denotes
the quadratic form

R(x)={\frac{1}{2}\left\langle Q_{\infty}^{-1}x,x\right\rangle},\qquad\text{$x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$.}

(4)

D_{t}
is a one-parameter semigroup of matrices, defined by

(3.5)

D_{t}=Q_{\infty}e^{-tB^{*}}Q_{\infty}^{-1},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

For
|t|\leq 1
and
0\neq x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
these matrices satisfy

(3.6)

|x-D_{t}x|\simeq|t|\,|x|,

(5)

\gamma_{t}
denotes the
normalized Gaussian measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, given by

d\gamma_{t}(x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}(\text{det}\,Q_{t})^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle Q_{t}^{-1}x,x\rangle}dx\,,\qquad\text{ $t\in(0,+\infty]$. }

In particular,

(3.7)

d\gamma_{\infty}(x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}(\text{det}\,Q_{\infty})^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-R(x)}dx,

and this is the unique invariant measure for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup.

4.
The Mehler kernel and its derivative

The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup operator
\mathcal{H}_{t}
is given, for all
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
and all
t>0
,
by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int K_{t}(x,u)\,f(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The integral kernel
K_{t}
is called the Mehler kernel and is given, for all

x,u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
, by

(4.2)

\displaystyle K_{t}(x,u)\!=\!\Big(\frac{\det\,Q_{\infty}}{\det\,Q_{t}}\Big)^{{1}/{2}}e^{R(x)}\exp\Big[{-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left(Q_{t}^{-1}-Q_{\infty}^{-1}\right)(u-D_{t}\,x),u-D_{t}\,x\right\rangle}\Big],\qquad

see
[
10
, formula (2.6)]
.

The following
estimate, holding for
0<t\leq 1
and all
x,u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is proved in
[
10
, (3.4)]
:

(4.3)

\frac{e^{R(x)}}{t^{n/2}}\exp\left[-C\,\frac{|u-D_{t}\,x|^{2}}{t}\right]\,\lesssim\,K_{t}(x,u)\,\lesssim\,\frac{e^{R(x)}}{t^{n/2}}\exp\left[-c\,\frac{|u-D_{t}\,x|^{2}}{t}\right].

We shall also need the derivative of
K_{t}(x,u)

with respect to
t
, which we denote by
\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)
.
For all
(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

t>0
, we proved in
[
13
, Lemma 4.2]
that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)=K_{t}(x,u)\,N_{t}^{(0)}(x,u),

where the function
N_{t}^{(0)}
is given by

\displaystyle N_{t}^{(0)}(x,u)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\,{\tr\big(Q_{t}^{-1}\,e^{tB}\,Q\,e^{tB^{*}}\big)}+\frac{1}{2}\,\left|Q^{1/2}\,e^{tB^{*}}\,Q_{t}^{-1}\,(u-D_{t}\,x)\right|^{2}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\left\langle Q_{\infty}\,B^{*}\,Q_{\infty}^{-1}\,D_{t}\,x\,,\,(Q_{t}^{-1}-Q_{\infty}^{-1})\,(u-D_{t}\,x)\right\rangle.

For future convenience, we describe in the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck framework
a simple way of estimating the variation seminorm, which is sometimes applicable. In the case when
\varrho=1
, the variation of
\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x)
is a supremum of sums
\sum|\mathcal{H}_{t_{i}}f(x)-\mathcal{H}_{t_{i-1}}f(x)|
, which can be estimated by the sum

\sum\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}|\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x)|\,dt
. Since the variation seminorm is decreasing in
\varrho
, we get for each
\varrho\in[1,\infty)
and any interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, at least formally,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\varrho),I}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{H}_{t}f(x)\|_{v(1),I}\leq\int_{I}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int K_{t}(x,u)f(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)\right|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int\int_{I}\big|\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)\big|\,dt\,|f(u)|\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u),\qquad f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty}).

This argument was justified and used in
[
13
, Subsection 5.1]
.
Below we will apply it to some other operators and their kernels.

5.
Localization

The distinction between a local and a global part of the operator is a standard tool in the study of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup; we refer to
[
37
,
40
,
38
]

for more details on this idea. Here we just describe in a concise way the localization procedure introduced in

[
13
, Section 7]
.

We choose a sequence
B_{j}=B(x_{j},1/(1+|x_{j}|)),\;j=0,1,\dots,
of closed balls whose interiors are pairwise disjoint, and they form a maximal family with this property. As shown in
[
13
, Section 7]
, the scaled balls

Some parts of our jump operator are easy to treat, by means of the following proposition.

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(1)}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(2)}(x,u)\right)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{e^{R(x)}}{t^{n/2}}\,e^{R(x)}\exp\left(-c\,\frac{|u-x|^{2}}{t}\right)\,\left(\frac{|u-x|^{2}}{t}+\frac{|u-x|^{4}}{t^{2}}\right)\,

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{e^{R(x)}}{t^{n/2}}\,\exp\left(-c\,\frac{|u-x|^{2}}{t}\right),

with a modified value of
c
.

The estimate of the lemma for
\kappa=2
follows from this and (
8.16
).

Moving to
\kappa=3
, we observe by (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\left|N_{t}^{(2)}(x,u)-N_{t}^{(3)}(x,u)\right|

\displaystyle=\Big|-\frac{1}{2}\,{\tr\big(Q_{t}^{-1}\,e^{tB}\,Q\,e^{tB^{*}}\big)}+\frac{n}{2t}\Big|

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{1}{2t}\,{\tr\big(\,I_{n}+\mathcal{O}(t)\big)}+\frac{n}{2t}\right|=\mathcal{O}(1),

and (
7.5
) implies

(8.18)

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(2)}(x,u)\left(N_{t}^{(2)}(x,u)-N_{t}^{(3)}(x,u)\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{e^{R(x)}}{t^{n/2}}\exp\left(-c\,\frac{|u-x|^{2}}{t}\right).

Then we consider the term

N_{t}^{(3)}(x,u)\left(\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(2)}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(3)}(x,u)\right)

and observe that

(8.19)

\displaystyle\left|N_{t}^{(3)}(x,u)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}+\frac{|x-u|^{2}}{t^{2}}.

From (
3.3
) we see that

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(2)}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(3)}(x,u)\right|

\displaystyle=\big({\det Q_{\infty}}\big)^{1/2}e^{R(x)}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t}\,\left|Q^{-1/2}(u-x)\right|^{2}\right)\left|\frac{1}{{(\det Q_{t})}^{1/2}}-\frac{1}{(\det Q)^{1/2}\,t^{n/2}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{e^{R(x)}}{t^{n/2}}\,\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2t}\,|u-x|^{2}\Big)\,t.

With
(
8.19
) we then have

\displaystyle\Big|N_{t}^{(3)}(x,u)\,

\displaystyle\Big(\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(2)}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(3)}(x,u)\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{e^{R(x)}}{\,t^{n/2}}\,\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t}\,|u-x|^{2}\right)\,\left(1+\frac{|x-u|^{2}}{t}\right)\,

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{e^{R(x)}}{t^{n/2}}\,\exp\left(-c\,\frac{|u-x|^{2}}{t}\right),

Together with (
8.18
), this proves the lemma for
\kappa=3
.
∎

8.2.
Boundedness of the variation operator for the differences

By means of the estimates proved in the previous subsection,
we can finally estimate the variation of
\Delta_{t}^{(\kappa)}\,f
, for
\kappa=1,2,3
.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
1\leq\varrho<\infty
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
. The operator that maps
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
to the function

x\mapsto\|\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\Delta_{t}^{(\kappa)}\,(f\,r_{j})(x)\|_{v(\varrho),(0,\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})]},\qquad\;\kappa=1,2,3,

is of strong type
(p,p)
for all
p\geq 1
with respect to the invariant (and Lebesgue) measure.
This is uniform in
j
.

Because of (
1.6
) and (
1.5
), this proposition implies

(8.20)

J_{2}^{1,\infty}\left(\widetilde{r}_{j}\,\Delta_{t}^{(\kappa)}\,(f\,r_{j}):\,0<t\leq\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})\right)\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(4B_{j},\gamma_{\infty})},\qquad\;\kappa=1,2,3,

and for
1<p<\infty

(8.21)

J_{2}^{p}\left(\widetilde{r}_{j}\,\Delta_{t}^{(\kappa)}\,(f\,r_{j}):\,0<t\leq\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})\right)\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(4B_{j},\gamma_{\infty})},\qquad\;\kappa=1,2,3.

Proof of Proposition
8.4
.

We start with the case of
\Delta_{t}^{(1)}
.
Let
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
.
We want to apply an argument like (
4.6
) to the operator

f\mapsto\widetilde{r}_{j}\,\Delta_{t}^{(1)}\,(f\,r_{j})
and its kernel

\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\left(K_{t}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(1)}(x,u)\right)\,r_{j}(u).

What must then be verified is only the swap of differentiation and integration in the last step of (
4.6
), that is,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\int\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\left(K_{t}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(1)}(x,u)\right)\,r_{j}(u)\,f(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)

(8.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>\int\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\left(K_{t}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(1)}(x,u)\right)\,r_{j}(u)\,f(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)

for
0<t<\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2}).

For this, we choose two points
t_{0}<t
in the interval
\left(0,\,\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})\right)
and replace
t
by
\tau
in the right-hand side of (
8.2
), which we then integrate
d\tau
over
t_{0}<\tau<t.
We get

(8.23)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\,\int\,\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\,\left(K_{\tau}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{\tau}^{(1)}(x,u)\right)\,r_{j}(u)\,f(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)\,d\tau

(8.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>\int\,\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\,\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\,\left(K_{\tau}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{\tau}^{(1)}(x,u)\right)\,r_{j}(u)\,f(u)\,d\tau\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u),

the equality by means of Fubini’s theorem. To see that Fubini can be applied here, we observe that Lemma
8.2
implies

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\,\int\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\,\left(K_{\tau}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{\tau}^{(1)}(x,u)\right)\right|\,r_{j}(u)\,|f(u)|\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\>(1+|x|)\,e^{R(x)}\,\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{(n+1)/2}}\,\int|f(u)|\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)<\infty.

Having thus verified (
8.23
), we can rewrite its right-hand side by a trivial evaluation of the inner integral, and obtain

(8.25)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\,\int\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\,\left(K_{\tau}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{\tau}^{(1)}(x,u)\right)\,r_{j}(u)\,f(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)\,d\tau

(8.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>\int\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\big(K_{t}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(1)}(x,u)\big)\,r_{j}(u)\,f(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)

(8.27)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\>\int\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\big(K_{t_{0}}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{t_{0}}^{(1)}(x,u)\big)\,r_{j}(u)\,f(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u).

Differentiating this equation with respect to
t
, we obtain (
8.2
) as desired.

We can thus apply (
4.6
) with the indicated kernel. Together with Lemma
8.2
, this yields

(8.28)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\Delta_{t}^{(1)}(f\,r_{j})(x)\|_{v(\varrho),(0,\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})]}

(8.29)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int\,\int_{0}^{\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})}\,\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\left(K_{\tau}(x,u)-\widetilde{K}_{\tau}^{(1)}(x,u)\right)\right|\,r_{j}(u)\,dt\,|f(u)|\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)

(8.30)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,(1+|x|)\,\,e^{R(x)}\int\int_{0}^{\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})}t^{-(n+1)/2}\,e^{-c\,\frac{|u-x|^{2}}{t}}\,dt\,\,\,|f(u)|\,r_{j}(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)\,\mathbbm{1}_{6B_{j}}(x).

In the inner integral here, we make the transformation
s=|u-x|^{2}/t
and find

(8.31)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})}t^{-(n+1)/2}\,\exp\big(-c\,\frac{|u-x|^{2}}{t}\big)\,dt

(8.32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|u-x|^{2}/\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})}^{\infty}|u-x|^{1-n}\,s^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\,e^{-cs}\,ds.

If
n>1
, the last integral is at most
C\,|u-x|^{1-n}
.
But if
n=1
, it is bounded by

C+\log_{+}\frac{\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})}{|u-x|^{2}}.

We insert these estimates in the last double integral in (
8.28
), after observing that there

u-x\in{4B_{j}}-{6B_{j}}\subset B(0,10/(1+|x_{j}|))
and also
e^{R(u)}\simeq e^{R(x)}\simeq e^{R(x_{j})}
. Thus we can replace

d\gamma_{\infty}(u)
by
du
and delete the factor
e^{R(x)}
, in this integral.
Further,
1+|x|\lesssim 1+|x_{j}|
there. For
n>1
we get

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,\Delta_{t}^{(1)}(f\,r_{j})(x)\|_{v(\varrho),(0,\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})]}\lesssim(1+|x_{j}|)\,\int\,|u-x|^{1-n}\,|f(u)|\,r_{j}(u)\,du\,\mathbbm{1}_{6B_{j}}(x).

The right-hand side here is essentially a convolution, and we can estimate it by

\displaystyle|f\,r_{j}|*g(x),

where

g(y)=C\,(1+|x_{j}|)\,|y|^{1-n}\,\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,10/(1+|x_{j}|))}(y)
, when
n>1.

For
n=1
we arrive at a similar convolution, but now

g(y)=(1+|x_{j}|)\,\left(C+2\log_{+}\frac{\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-1})}{|y|}\right)\,\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,10/(1+|x_{j}|))}(y).

In both cases, we find that

\|g\|_{L^{1}(dy)}\lesssim 1
, and so convolution by
g
defines a bounded operator on all
L^{p}(dx)
spaces.

This proves Proposition
8.4
for
\kappa=1
.

The cases
\kappa=2,\;3
can be done in exactly the same way, since, as already observed, Lemma
8.3
gives a stronger estimate than Lemma
8.2
.

The proposition is proved.
∎

9.
The local case for small
t
: the main operator
M_{t}

We are left with the estimate of the main part
M_{t}f
in (
7.1
), that is, the integral operator with the kernel

\widetilde{K}_{t}^{(3)}
defined in (
7.6
).

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
. For all
p>1
one has

(9.1)

J_{2}^{p}\left(\widetilde{r}_{j}\,M_{t}(f\,r_{j}):0<t\leq\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})\right)\lesssim\|f\,r_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\gamma_{\infty})}

and, moreover,

(9.2)

J_{2}^{1,\infty}\left(\widetilde{r}_{j}\,M_{t}(f\,r_{j}):0<t\leq\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})\right)\lesssim\|f\,r_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})}.

The implicit constants in these inequalities are uniform in
j
.

Proof.

Let for
t>0

\psi_{t}(y)=\Big(\frac{\det Q_{\infty}}{\det Q}\Big)^{1/2}\,{t^{-n/2}}\,\exp\big(-\frac{1}{2t}\,|Q^{-1/2}\,y|^{2}\big),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then

\displaystyle M_{t}(f\,r_{j})(x)

\displaystyle=e^{R(x)}\Big(\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)\Big)(x).

for
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
.
We recall that
e^{R(x)}\simeq e^{R(x_{j})}
for
x\in 6B_{j}\supset\mathrm{supp}\,\widetilde{r}_{j}
.

If
\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,M_{t}(f\,r_{j})(x)=\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,e^{R(x)}\left(\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)\right)(x)
has a jump of amplitude larger than
\lambda
for some
x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\widetilde{r}_{j}
,
then

\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)(x)
has a jump of amplitude larger than
c\,\lambda\,e^{-R(x_{j})}
for some
c>0
.
As before, the jumps are in the variable
t
.
Thus

(9.3)

\displaystyle{N_{\lambda}\big(\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,M_{t}(f\,r_{j})(x)}\big)\leq N_{c\,\lambda e^{-R(x_{j})}}\left(\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)\right)(x)\,\mathbbm{1}_{6B_{j}}(x),

and for all
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\infty}\left\{x\in 6B_{j}:\,\lambda\,\sqrt{N_{\lambda}\left(\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,M_{t}(f\,r_{j})\right)(x)}>\alpha\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\gamma_{\infty}\left\{x\in 6B_{j}:\,\lambda\,\sqrt{N_{c\lambda e^{-R(x_{j})}}\,\left(\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)\right)(x)}>\alpha\right\}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\infty}\left\{x\in 6B_{j}:\,c\,e^{-R(x_{j})}\,\lambda\,\sqrt{N_{c\lambda e^{-R(x_{j})}}\,\left(\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)\right)(x)}>c\,e^{-R(x_{j})}\,\alpha\,\right\}

\displaystyle\simeq e^{-R(x_{j})}\left|\left\{x\in 6B_{j}:\,ce^{-R(x_{j})}\,\lambda\,\sqrt{N_{c\lambda e^{-R(x_{j})}}\,\left(\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)\right)(x)}>\,c\,e^{-R(x_{j})}\,\alpha\right\}\right|.

Now we apply Theorem 2.4 in
[
28
]
to the last expression here, getting

\displaystyle\gamma_{\infty}

\displaystyle\Big\{x\in 6B_{j}\,:\,\lambda\,\sqrt{N_{\lambda}\left(\widetilde{r}_{j}(x)\,M_{t}(f\,r_{j})\right)(x)}>\alpha\Big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\frac{e^{-R(x_{j})}}{e^{-R(x_{j})}\,\alpha}\,\left\|\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)\right\|_{L^{1}(du)}\lesssim\,\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\|f\,r_{j}\,e^{-R(\cdot)}\|_{L^{1}(du)}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\|f\,r_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})},

where we also used the fact that convolution with
\psi_{t}
defines a bounded operator on
L^{1}(du)
.
This is uniform in
\lambda>0
and in
j
, and proves (
9.2
).

We now consider the strong type
(p,p)
for
1<p<\infty
. Starting from (
9.3
),
moving to Lebesgue measure and then applying
[
28
, Theorem 2.4]
, one finds

\displaystyle\left\lVert\,\lambda\sqrt{N_{\lambda}\big(\widetilde{r}_{j}(\cdot)\,M_{t}(f\,r_{j})\big)(\cdot)}\,\right\rVert_{L^{p}(6B_{j},\gamma_{\infty})}\leq\left\lVert\,\lambda\sqrt{N_{c\,\lambda e^{-R(x_{j})}}\left(\big(\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)\right)}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(6B_{j},\gamma_{\infty})}

\displaystyle=c^{-1}e^{R(x_{j})}\left\lVert\,ce^{-R(x_{j})}\lambda\sqrt{N_{c\,\lambda e^{-R(x_{j})}}\left(\big(\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)\right)}\,\right\rVert_{L^{p}(6B_{j},\gamma_{\infty})}

\displaystyle\simeq e^{R(x_{j})}\,e^{-R(x_{j})/p}\left\lVert\,ce^{-R(x_{j})}\lambda\sqrt{N_{c\,\lambda e^{-R(x_{j})}}\left(\big(\psi_{t}*\big(fr_{j}e^{-R(\cdot)}\big)\right)}\,\right\rVert_{L^{p}(6B_{j},dx)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}e^{R(x_{j})}\,e^{-R(x_{j})/p}\left(\int\big|f(u)\,r_{j}(u)\,e^{-R(u)}\big|^{p}du\right)^{1/p}\simeq_{p}\|f\,r_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\gamma_{\infty})},

uniformly in
\lambda>0
and
j
. This is (
9.1
), and Proposition
9.1
is proved.
∎

Now the remaining part of Theorem 5.2 with
t\in(0,\min(1,|x_{j}|^{-2})]
follows as a combination of
(
8.20
) and (
9.2
). Theorem
1.2
is completely proved.

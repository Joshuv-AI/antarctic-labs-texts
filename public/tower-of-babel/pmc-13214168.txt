Molecular and antigenic characteristics of the influenza B virus in Guangzhou, 2022–2023
Authors: Yang Liu, Shengzhen Wu, Yong Liu, Jingbin Chen, Juncong Xiao, Jiawei Zhang, Qingsheng Huang, Weiqi Pan, Dongni Lin, Yang Wang, Hon Chitin, Zifeng Yang, Xiaoyan Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe Influenza B virus (IBV), which accounts for nearly a quarter of annual global influenza-associated morbidity, represents a major human respiratory pathogen. Since late 2019, IBV transmission patterns have shifted markedly following the emergence of COVID-19, yet systematic studies on IBV prevalence before and after the pandemic remain limited.MethodsWe conducted systematic surveillance of 66 IBV isolates collected from Guangzhou during 2022–2023. Phylogenetic analysis of HA/NA genes was complemented with evolutionary rate estimation, hemagglutination inhibition assays, antigenic cartography, and structural modeling of hemagglutinin variants.ResultsWe revealed the distinct epidemic trends of IBVs in Southern China: clade V1A.3a.1/V1A.3a.2 co-circulated in 2022–2023 influenza season, while complete replacement of V1A.3a.1 by V1A.3a.2 in 2023–2024 season, which was driven by a 1.36-fold increase in the evolutionary rate of the HA gene (1.632 × 10–3, P < 0.05) post-NPIs. Spatially, discrete Bayesian phylogeographic analysis confirmed that Jiangxi Province acted as the core upstream hub of the nationwide transmission of V1A.3a.1, which persisted endemically from Jiangxi in 2020 to six adjacent provinces in 2021 before its final extinction in China. Notably, cross-reactive HI and quantitative antigenic cartography elucidated antigenic divergence between V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2 subclades, and revealed that the complete displacement of V1A.3a.1 by V1A.3a.2 was mediated by the broadened antigenic recognition of 2023 V1A.3a.2 isolates. Structural modeling revealed L144P in the 150-loop may be the primary driver of antigenic divergence, inducing conformational rearrangement in residues 146–148.ConclusionThis study elucidates the unique evolutionary and epidemic patterns of IBV in the subtropical region of southern China after the relaxation of COVID-19 NPIs, deciphers the molecular mechanism of clade replacement and antigenic diversification of the Victoria lineage V1A.3a subclade, and validates the sustained effectiveness of the WHO 2022 influenza B vaccine strain update in southern China. Our findings fill the gap in systematic surveillance of IBV in subtropical sentinel regions, identify Guangzhou as a key monitoring site for IBV evolutionary dynamics, and provide critical molecular and antigenic evidence for the optimization of regional influenza surveillance systems and vaccine strain selection strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12985-026-03141-1.Keywords: Influenza B virus, antigenic characterization, Molecular epidemiology

Introduction

Seasonal influenza causes 290,000–650,000 annual respiratory deaths globally [1–3]. The Influenza B virus (IBV) drives approximately 25% of these cases and exhibits striking severity in children, accounting for 22–44% of pediatric fatalities [4–6]. Since it was identified in 1940, IBV has diverged into Yamagata/Victoria lineages, which exhibit distinct spatiotemporal patterns [4, 7–9]. Notably, Victoria lineage dominates in tropical regions and younger populations [4, 10–12], while Yamagata circulated more widely in temperate zones until its recent decline [13].

Antigenic evolution of IBV is primarily driven by hemagglutinin (HA) gene indels and epistatic mutations[14, 15]. The Victoria lineage demonstrates significantly higher evolutionary rates (mean turnover interval: 3.9–5.1 years) compared to the Yamagata lineage (6.3–7.2 years) [16]. Critical deletions in the HA1 160-loop correlate with antigenic drift of Victoria IBVs [8, 17]. Recent clade diversification (e.g., V1A.3 subclades) underscores the need for real-time surveillance to identify emerging variants [15, 16].

The unique subtropical monsoon climate in Southern China exerts a profound impact on transmission dynamics, seasonal patterns, and evolutionary trajectories of the IBV. Characterized by year-round warm and humid conditions with no extreme low-temperature restriction in winter, this environment eliminates the strict seasonal transmission bottleneck observed in temperate regions, allowing IBV to maintain low-level circulation throughout the year without interruption of transmission chains, presents a typical dual-peak epidemic pattern (winter-spring and summer) in this region [18–20]. High ambient humidity prolongs the in vitro survival of influenza viruses and enhances transmission efficiency, which is particularly prominent for IBV [21]. Meanwhile, the year-round frequent population mobility in the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area provides sustained opportunities for viral transmission and genetic reassortment, further driving the local adaptation and evolution of IBV [11]. In addition, compared with temperate regions, the continuous transmission pressure in subtropical zones significantly accelerates the antigenic drift rate of IBV, especially for the Victoria lineage with an inherently shorter lineage turnover interval (3.9–5.1 years), creating a hotbed for the emergence and persistence of region-specific variants [22].

The COVID-19 pandemic significantly disrupted IBV transmission, leading to Yamagata lineage extinction and Victoria lineage dominance [13, 23]. In China, four IBV epidemics occurred during 2019–2024, with the 2022 WHO vaccine update (B/Austria/1359417/2021 replacing B/Washington/02/2019) coinciding with the third and fourth waves [24]. Notably, a major influenza outbreak occurred in China in 2025, driven almost exclusively by influenza A viruses (over 99% H3N2), while IBV did not cause a large-scale epidemic during this wave. However, genetic and antigenic dynamics of IBV during this critical transition remain uncharacterized, particularly in subtropical sentinel regions like Guangzhou-a hub for influenza surveillance in Southern China [25]. Recent domestic genetic evolutionary analysis on influenza B virus (IBV) have confirmed that the V1A.3a subclade is the dominant circulating lineage in China after the relaxtion of COVID-19 NPIs, with key mutations in HA antigenic epitopes and potential antigenic drift detected [26–28]. Other researcher and our previous studies have demonstrated that the influenza B virus (IBV) V1A.3a.1 clade has only been detected with limited circulation in Guangdong and Shandong provinces to date [29, 30]. However, its overall epidemic trend and underlying causes of disappearance across China remain unclear. Additionally, our prior work revealed that the evolutionary rate of the IBV hemagglutinin (HA) gene in Guangdong was 1.7-fold faster than that before the COVID-19 pandemic, accompanied by a significant reduction in genetic diversity—with strains predominantly clustering with domestic isolates—and the emergence of key mutations in antigenic epitopes [29]. Nevertheless, existing studies lack a detailed comparative analysis of the evolutionary rate of the IBV HA gene following the implementation of COVID-19 non-pharmaceutical interventions (NPIs), as well as the differences in evolutionary rates among distinct subclades.

This study systematically analyzed the molecular epidemiology and antigenic evolution of IBVs in Guangzhou during 2022–2023. We aimed to elucidate the strain replacement dynamics and antigenic variation of IBV Victoria lineage V1A.3a subclade in Guangzhou (2022–2023) following the relaxation of COVID-19 NPIs, assess the potential functional significance of key mutations, explore the overall epidemic trend and causes of disappearance of the IBV V1A.3a.1 clade across China, and provide a basis for optimizing regional influenza surveillance and vaccine strategies.

Materials and methods

Ethic statement

All sample collection from human subjects and viral isolation procedures in this study were approved by the Ethic Committee of the KingMed Diagnostics (approval number: 2023254). All live viruses related experiments were performed in Biosafety level 2 (BSL2) facility at the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University.

Cells and viruses

Influenza B virus strains and Madin-Darby canine kidney cells (MDCK) were kindly provided by the State Key Laboratory of Respiratory Diseases. Cells were passaged up to 20 times and cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (Gibco) containing 10% foetal bovine serum (Excellbio, FSP500), 2 mM l-glutamine and the antibiotics penicillin and streptomycin (Gibco). All Influenza B Virus stock solution were stored at -80 °C until use.

Case definition and sources

The respiratory sample from 9412 patients with influenza-like illness (ILI) cases were recruited from 10 Sentinel Hospitals (Table S1) in Guangzhou (including Yuexiu, Liwan, Haizhu, Tianhe, Baiyun, Panyu, Nansha, Zengcheng, Huangpu and Huadu district) during January 2022 to December 2023, stored in the Guangzhou Institute of Respiratory Health (GIRH). Samples were transported at 2–8 ℃ and either tested immediately or stored at -80 ℃ until testing.

IBV identification and HA/NA gene sequencing

All ILI samples were identified using CLEARVIEW EXACT INFLUENZA A & B KIT (Colloidal Gold Immunochromatographic Assay, GICA) following manufacturer’s instrument (Abbott, Cat.20163400418). Samples were eluted in the provided lysis buffer by rotating the swab vigorously for 10 s, after which 3 drops (~ 100 μL) of the eluate were applied to the sample well of the test cassette. The assay was incubated at room temperature (18- 25 ℃) for 15 min, and results were interpreted within 15–20 min: positive (one band in control zone (C) plus 1–2 bands in test zones (A/B)), negative (only one band in C zone), or invalid (no band in C zone, requiring retesting).The IBV positive samples were propagated in Madin-Darby canine kidney (MDCK) cells for 48-72 h, followed by hemagglutination assay (HA) using 1% chicken red blood cells (CRBC).(Merye, Cat. M52780) which stored at -20 ℃ for no more than 2 weeks. HA-positive samples were stored at -80 ℃. The HA and NA genes of IBVs were amplified with PCR according to previous study [31] and perform Sange sequencing analysis at Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

Phylogenetic analysis of the IBV HA and NA gene

Full-length nucleotide sequences of hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA) genes from global influenza B Victoria lineage viruses (2004–2025) were retrieved from the GISAID database. Time-stratified sampling was implemented to reduce sequence redundancy through temporal distribution balancing. The dataset was augmented with 66 IBV strains from this study and relevant vaccine strains, yielding 432 high-quality NA gene sequences and 609 HA gene sequences. Both gene datasets underwent multiple sequence alignment using MAFFT v7.505 with default parameters. The Nextclade analytical workflow in the Nextstrain platform was employed to identify appropriate reference sequences (Influenza B Victoria NA and HA) for phylogenetic reconstruction [32]. Time-scaled phylogenetic trees were generated through Bayesian inference utilizing TreeTime’s strict molecular clock model (substitution rate: 0.8 subs/site/year). Phylogenetic topologies and temporal patterns were visualized using the ggtree v3.10 package in R, accompanied by parallel generation of evolutionary heatmaps documenting clade-specific amino acid substitutions.

Bayesian phylogeographic analysis

A total of 229 V1A.3a.1 IBV HA gene sequences in China (2019–2023) were aligned with MAFFT v7.505. Phylogeographic inference was performed in BEAST2 (3 M framework, discrete geographic model, BSSVS) with an uncorrelated relaxed clock and coalescent constant polulation prior following previous studies [33, 34]. 100-million-step MCMC chains (ESS > 200) were run for 3,000,000 steps (sampled every 10,000 steps), and MCC trees were generated using TreeAnnotator. High-confidence migration events (PP > 0.8) were visualized in ArcGIS REST API with 64 domestic inter-provincial and year-color-coded arcs.

Evolutionary rate and selection pressure assay

The root-to-tip distances for strains within different years and lineages were assessed using the Treetime package in Python under a strict molecular clock model. A strict molecular clock model implemented in TreeTime was selected for phylodynamic analysis, drawing upon the analytical framework established in our prior research concerning NPIs before and during the spread of the influenza B/Victoria lineage in southern China [29]. The 95% confidence interval for each dataset were calculated (Table S2). To verify the applicability of the model, root-to-tip regression analysis within different years and lineages were performed to assess the temporal signal of the dataset. The ratio between the number of non-synonymous and synonymous mutations (dN/dS) was calculated employing the Single-Likelihood Ancestor Counting (SLAC) method via DataMonkey (http://www.datamonkey.org) [35]. Likelihood ratio test was performed to verify the significance of codon sites under episodic diversifying positive selection, with P < 0.1 set as the significance threshold for the MEME analysis, and P < 0.05 considered statistically significant for the likelihood ratio test.

Hemagglutinin inhibition assay

Ferrets were immunized intranasally in an ABSL-2 laboratory with 500 μL of virus strains B/Brisbane/60/2008 (Bris, Clade V1A), B/Washington/02/2019 (WA/02, Clade V1A.3), B/Austria/1359417/2021 (AU21, Clade V1A.3a.2) and B/Guangzhou/50/2022 (GZ/50, Clade V1A.3a.1) (≥ 106TCID50/mL) combined with 500 μL of PBS (pH 7.4), administered at two-week intervals, with booster immunizations performed identically. Cardiac blood was collected 14 days post-immunization from animals exhibiting an antibody titer of ≥ 160. serum was aliquoted and stored at –20 ℃. Ferret antisera raised against the strains Bris, WA/02, AU21, and GZ/50 were pretreated with receptor-destroying enzyme II (RDE II; Denka, Cat. 340,122). The HI titers of the representative virus were determined according to established WHO protocols (WHO 2011). Briefly, to ascertain HI titers, a sequence of 25 μL post-infection double dilutions of the ferret antiserum with 4 haemagglutination units (HAU) per 25 μL virus were incubated for 1 h at ambient temperature, followed by an additional 1 h at 4 °C in a 1:1 ratio with a PBS solution containing 1% CRBC. The HI titer was documented as the reciprocal of the maximum dilution of murine antiserum that entirely obstructed erythrocyte hemagglutination.

Antigenic cartography

Antigenic map construction was conducted using established protocols [23]. This approach quantifies and visualizes serological data by transforming neutralizing titers into spatial relationships. Within the map configuration, the separation between antiserum coordinate S and antigen coordinate A reflects the differential between the maximum log2 titer recorded for antiserum S across all antigens and its specific log2 titer against antigen A. Cross-titration measurements collectively define optimal spatial constraints for antigen-antiserm alignment. Through multidimensional scaling adaptations, antigens and antisera are positioned to optimally match these empirical distance parameters, creating a visualization where spatial proximity inversely correlates with log2 titer values in neutralization assays. The integration of multiple antisera-antigen interactions enhances positional accuracy within the coordinate system. Computational implementation utilized the Racmacs package (https://acorg.github.io/Racmacs/, version 1.1.18) within the R environment, with complementary web-based tools accessible at www.antigenic-cartography.org. Map generation involved 1000 iterative optimizations, employing the "none" setting for the minimum column basis parameter.

Three-dimensional structural modeling of influenza B virus hemagglutinin protein.

The 3D structural modeling for wild-type B/Austria/1359417/2021 and antigenic variants corresponding to clades V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2 variants were generated via AlphaFold 3 (https://deepmind.google/science/alphafold/alphafold-server/), using the top-scoring template among 5 candidates and default parameters. Structural differences among proteins were analyzed, aligned, and visualized using PyMOL (version 3.01) and globally aligned via the “align-to molecule (*/ca)” algorithm, with a focus on the globular head region. Key substitutions were displayed in stick format with distinct color differentiation.

Results

Influenza virus activity in China between Clade 2021-December 2023

During October 2020-September 2021, Influenza A virus activity in the Chinese mainland was below pre-pandemic levels of SARS-CoV-2. In contrast, B/Victoria lineage detection rates exhibited regional elevation, particularly in Southern China from March 2021 to April 2022, surpassing both baseline levels and comparable pandemic-period data from preceding years [1]. In this study, we summarized the IBV epidemiology in Southern China and China Mainland (October 2021-December 2023) from weekly epidemiological surveillance data of Chinese National Influenza Center (https://ivdc.chinacdc.cn/cnic/zyzx/lgzb/, surveillance Week 39 in 2021 to Week 52 in 2023) analysis during epidemic phases. Four distinct epidemic waves were delineated: Wave 1 (from October 2021 to March 2022) was predominantly driven by influenza B (Victoria lineage), accounting for 98.14% of positive cases; Wave 2 (from April 2022 to December 2022) was primarily characterized by influenza A (H3N2), representing 96.7% of infections; Wave 3 (from February 2023 to May 2023) was distinguished by the co-circulation of influenza A (H3N2) and A (H1N1)pdm09, with respective proportions of 26.83% and 73.03%; Wave 4 (from September 2023 to December 2023) was marked by the simultaneous prevalence of influenza A (H3N2) and B (Victoria), comprising 83.12% and 16.24% of positive cases, respectively. Notably, The B (Victoria) virus contributed to two of these epidemics—namely, the Wave 1 and Wave 4 outbreaks. This result indicated that the epidemic trend of IBVs in Southern China (Fig. 1B) revealed transmission synchronization with the surveillance data for mainland China (Fig. 1A).

The phylogenetic analysis of the HA and NA gene of IBVs in Southern China (2022–2023)

A total of 66 Influenza B virus (IBV) positive samples were identified using GICA (Table S3). Subsequently, the hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA) genes were sequenced using the Sanger method (Accession ID: EPI_ISL_20227036, EPI_ISL_20228771-20228833). The maximum-likelihood phylogenetic reconstruction of the HA and NA genes classified all strains within the B/Victoria lineage. Phylogenetic analysis revealed distinct stratification patterns in the HA genes of winter 2022–2023 isolates, which diverged into two subclades: V1A.3a.1 A3.1.1 (n = 3, including representative strains B/Guangzhou/16/2022, B/Guangzhou/47/2022, and B/Guangzhou/50/2022) and V1A.3a.2 C.1 (n = 29). Subsequent surveillance in winter 2023–2024 demonstrated complete clade replacement, with all isolates (n = 34) exclusively clustering within Clade V1A.3a.2. These strains further stratified into four subclades: C.1 (n = 18), C.5 (represented by B/Guangzhou/101/2023), C5.6 (represented by B/Guangzhou/312/2023), and C5.7 (n = 14) (Fig. 2A). Neuraminidase gene evolution paralleled this diversification pattern. The 2022–2023 isolates bifurcated into Clade A1.1 (B/Guangzhou/16/2022, B/Guangzhou/47/2022, B/Guangzhou/50/2022) and Clade A1.2 (n = 29). Subsequent phylogenetic analysis of 2023–2024 specimens revealed novel clade emergence, with 94.1% (31/34) segregating into Clade B7.3 and three isolates (B/Guangzhou/312/2023, B/Guangzhou/92/2023, B/Guangzhou/580/2023) forming Clade B8 (Fig. 2B). Longitudinal surveillance data from Southern China (2022–2024) document two principal epidemiological transitions: the complete displacement of V1A.3a.1/V1A.3a.2 co-circulation by V1A.3a.2 clade dominance; progressive genetic diversification within Clade V1A.3a.2 influenza B viruses. The subclade manifested only one genotype in 2022–2023 but evolved into four distinct genotypes by 2023–2024, demonstrating accelerated genetic diversification over successive influenza seasons.

Spatiotemporal epidemiology characteristics of Clade V1A.3a.1 in China during 2018–2023

To elucidate the circulation dynamics of Clade V1A.3a.1, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of 789 GISAID-documented cases spanning 2018–2024. Global surveillance data demonstrate that the V1A.3a.1 clade first emerged on a global scale in 2019, but subsequently experienced a rapid global extinction event by 2020, with the exception of persistent circulation within China (Fig. 3A). Notably, epidemiological surveillance of China identified Shanghai and Hong Kong as primary entry points during the clade’s initial emergence in 2019 (Fig. 3B), with subsequent establishment of transient endemicity persisting through 2023 (Fig. 3A). Spatial–temporal epidemiological analysis revealed distinctive transmission patterns: in 2020, focal outbreaks concentrated in Jiangxi and Guizhou provinces (Fig. 3C), followed by 2021 expansion into six adjacent provinces (Zhejiang, Fujian, Guangdong, Guangxi, Henan, and Hunan) with epidemic peaks in Jiangxi, Guizhou, and Hubei (Fig. 3D). Following limited sporadic occurrences during 2022–2023, this viral clade became undetectable, coinciding with complete replacement by Clade V1A.3a.2 (Fig. 3E and F).

To further decipher the transmission routes, migration dynamics, and key hub regions underlying the unique endemic circulation of V1A.3a.1 in China, we performed a discrete Bayesian phylogeographic analysis using BEAST2 software with 3 million Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) steps. Only migration events with a posterior probability > 0.8 were considered as high-confidence transmission events for subsequent interpretation, including 64 domestic inter-provincial migration arcs (Table S4, Fig. 3G). The domestic phylogeographic reconstruction fully delineated the stepwise expansion process of the V1A.3a.1 clade across mainland China, which was completely consistent with the spatiotemporal epidemic patterns observed in epidemiological surveillance. The earliest domestic inter-provincial transmission event was a high-confidence migration from Jiangxi to Guizhou in mid-2020 (posterior probability = 1.0), which coincided with the focal outbreaks in Jiangxi and Guizhou in 2020, and marked the formal establishment of sustained domestic transmission chains of the V1A.3a.1 clade in China. Jiangxi province acted as the primary initiator and upstream core hub of domestic transmission. It contributed 6 independent high-confidence output transmission events (posterior probability = 1.0) from 2020 to 2021, covering 5 geographic regions including Southwest China (Guizhou), South China (Hainan), Central China (Hubei), East China (Fujian), and North China (Henan). As the origin of the earliest domestic transmission chain, Jiangxi initiated the core transmission backbone of "Jiangxi → Guizhou → Hunan → nationwide spread", and was the key driver of the nationwide epidemic peak in 2021. Guangdong province served as the core convergence hub in South China and the key bridge linking domestic and global transmission. It exhibited a typical "convergence first, diffusion later" transmission pattern: in the second half of 2021, it received high-confidence viral inputs from 4 geographic regions including Central China (Hunan, Hubei), East China (Zhejiang), and North China (Shandong), acting as the only core convergence node in South China. From late 2021 to 2022, Guangdong transformed into a key output hub, driving long-distance cross-region transmission to North China (Hebei), Southwest China (Yunnan), and Northeast China (Liaoning), which completed the final coverage of the nationwide transmission network. The number of high-confidence inter-provincial transmission events decreased significantly in 2022, and no high-confidence migration events were detected after 2023, which corresponded to the gradual extinction of the clade and its full replacement by Clade V1A.3a.2. These findings collectively demonstrate that Clade V1A.3a.1 suffered global extinction by 2020 but maintained sustained endemic circulation in China via a hub-mediated spatiotemporal transmission network centered on Jiangxi and Guangdong, resulting in a unique co-circulation pattern with Clade V1A.3a.2 that implies potential antigenic divergence between the two clades, a hypothesis that merits further experimental validation.

Evolutionary rates and selection pressure of HA and NA genes from B/Victoria in Guangdong, China

To assess the impact of NPIs release on HA and NA gene evolution in B/Victoria viruses, we performed a comprehensive analysis of evolutionary rates and selection pressure using B/Victoria strains from Guangdong, China. The results demonstrated that the estimated evolutionary rate for B/Victoria HA genes rose from 1.196 × 10–3 (P < 0.05) substitutions per site per year (subs/site/year) during COVID-19 to 1.632 × 10–3 (P < 0.05) subs/site/year post-COVID-19 (Fig. 4 A and B). Conversely, the rate for NA genes slightly decreased, moving from 1.366 × 10–3 (P < 0.05) subs/site/year during COVID-19 to 1.313 × 10–3 (P < 0.05) subs/site/year post-COVID-19 (Fig. 4 C and D). Root-to-tip regression distances (RTT) analysis revealed a 36.4% increase in the HA evolutionary rate post-NPIs. Furthermore, selection pressure analysis indicated both HA and NA genes from B/Victoria viruses underwent strong purifying selection (dN/dS < 1). Episodic diversifying positive selection sites identified at positions 169 and 214 of the HA gene and position 86 of the NA gene following NPI relaxation are located near the antigenic epitopes/receptor-binding regions of HA and the catalytic functional domain of NA, respectively, suggesting that viral surface proteins may underwent adaptive mutations driven by host immune pressure and transmission demands during this period (Table S5).

To further access the differences in HA and NA gene evolution between clades, we analyzed evolutionary rates and selection pressure for Clade V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2 in China. Results revealed HA gene evolutionary rates of 1.065 × 10–3 (P < 0.05) and 1.068 × 10–3 (P < 0.05) subs/site/year (Fig. 4 E and F) for V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2, respectively. The NA genes evolved at rates of 1.132 × 10–3 (P < 0.05) and 1.067 × 10–3 (P < 0.05) subs/site/year (Fig. 4 G and H), respectively. Both HA and NA genes from these clades underwent purifying selection (dN/dS < 1), with similar levels of selection pressure on both clades (Table S6). No mutation loci for zanamivir and oseltamivir resistance were identified in the NA protein.

Antigenic divergence analysis among different clades of the HA genes and pandemic seasons

Following WHO’s February 2022 vaccine strain update for B/Victoria lineage viruses: B/Austria/1359417/2021 (AU21, Clade V1A.3a.2) replacing B/Washington/02/2019 (WA/02, Clade V1A.3). To investigate the antigenic differences among different clades and pandemic seasons, we conducted systematic cross-reactive antigenic profiling of 24 representative isolates through hemagglutination inhibition (HI) assays using ferret antiserum of B/Brisbane/60/2008 (Bris, Clade V1A), WA/02, AU21, and B/Guangzhou/50/2022 (GZ/50, Clade V1A.3a.1). Serological HI analysis delineated the antigenic evolution of contemporary IBV Victoria lineage (Fig. 5A). The uniformly low reactivity of all prevalent strains (21/21, 100%) against the Bris antiserum indicated substantial antigenic drift from the early Victoria lineage. Notably, the majority of isolates from the 2022 season, spanning both V1A.3a.1 (2/3, 66.7%) and V1A.3a.2 (8/9, 88.9%) viruses, also exhibited significant antigenic divergence from the then-employed vaccine strain WA/02, suggesting a suboptimal vaccine match. Further analysis using AU21 and GZ/50 antisera elucidated the replacement dynamics of the V1A.3a.1 by V1A.3a.2 viruses. Their initial co-circulation was characterized by asymmetric cross-reactivity: GZ/50 antiserum demonstrated weak neutralization against V1A.3a.2 viruses (9/9, 100%, HI titer < 40), whereas AU21 antiserum was largely ineffective against V1A.3a.1 viruses (3/3, 100%), confirming their relative antigenic independence. Notably, the V1A.3A.2 2023 isolates exhibited a significantly enhanced cross-reactivity to the WHO-recommended vaccine strain AU21 compared to the V1A.3A.2 2022 isolates, with 100% of strains showing HI titers ≥ 160 (Fig. 5A), indicating a markedly antigenic match with the current vaccine strain. We also find a pivotal transition occurred upon the dominance of the V1A.3a.2 2023 isolates, which exhibited significantly enhanced reactivity to the GZ/50 antiserum (7/12, 58.3%, HI titer ≥ 80), a phenomenon indicative of "broadened antigenic recognition".

To further validate these serological findings, we transformed the full HI matrix into Euclidean antigenic distances via Racmacs-based antigenic cartography. The overall average antigenic distances across four reference antisera for each clade indicated that V1A.3a.2 2022 isolates showed a markedly elevated (4.17 Antigen Unit (AU)) compared to the V1A.3a.1 clade (2.75 AU, P < 0.001), consistent with the antigenic independence observed in HI assays. Strikingly, the V1A.3a.2 2023 isolates exhibited a substantial reduction relative to the V1A.3a.2 2022 isolates (2.73 AU, P < 0.0001), while remaining statistically indistinguishable from the clade V1A.3a.1 (Fig. 5B). This quantitative spatial analysis directly validates the broadened antigenic recognition of V1A.3a.2 2023 strains at the antigenic cartography level. This convergent antigenic evolution indicates that the dominant clade evolved to exploit pre-existing immunity against V1A.3a.1 more effectively, outcompeting this ancestral clade for its ecological niche and ultimately driving the extinction of the V1A.3a.1 transmission chain.

Molecular characteristics in the epitope and antigenic cartography of B/Victoria in Guangzhou, China

To further investigate the antigenic relationships and molecular characteristics among clades, substitions at epitope were statisticed and antigenic map from the HI data were constructed using antigenic cartography. Comparative analysis with the AU21 strain reveals that the clade V1A.3a.1 strains share consistant molecular characteristics in epitope region: T127A in the 120-loop, L144P in the 150-loop, and R200K within the 190-helix (H3 numbering). The GZ/50 strain demonstrates additional mutations, featuring H122N in the 120-loop and A199T in the 190-helix. The clade V1A.3a.2 2022 isolates showed substitution H122Q (9/9, 100%) and D129N (GZ/7) in the 120-loop. We constructed antigenic cartography using a multidimensional scaling (MDS) algorithm was employed, and pairwise inter-clade antigenic distances were quantified following described previously[23]. Initially, pairwise inter-clade antigenic distances were measured to characterize the antigenic shift between co-circulating clades ((Fig. 5C). The result confirmed the significant antigenic separation between V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2 2022 clades, with a mean inter-clade distance of 4.08 AU. In stark contrast, the mean inter-clade distance between V1A.3a.1 and 2023 V1A.3a.2 demonstrating a reduction (1.51 AU) in antigenic separation, indicating marked antigenic convergence between the two clades. Meanwhile, the mean inter-clade distance betwee V1A.3a.2 2022 and 2023 isolates reached 2.59 AU, confirming a significant antigenic shift within the V1A.3a.2 clade between the 2022 and 2023 epidemic seasons.

Next, we further validated the antigenic differences between the V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2 clades and revealed the fine-scale antigenic evolutionary dynamics and molecular basis of the clade V1A.3a.2 (Fig. 5D). We confirmed a significant spatial separation between the antigenic clusters formed by the isolates and the Bris and WA/02 antisera, indicating substantial antigenic divergence from prevalent strains. Most V1A.3a.2 viruses (except for GZ/70) were located within a fourfold dilution from the AU21 antiserum between maps, forming a distinct cluster characterized by H137Q mutation (14/21, 66.7%). In contrast, V1A.3a.1 viruses exhibited a greater than eightfold HI titer difference from both the AU21 antiserum and the 2022 V1A.3a.2 viruses, forming a separate cluster defined by mutations T127A, L144P, and R200K. Notably, the 2023 V1A.3a.2 viruses demonstrated a less than fourfold HI titer difference from both the V1A.3a.1 and the 2022 V1A.3a.2 viruses. Within the V1A.3a.2 cluster, formed new subclusters characterized by mutations P169S + L170S or D194E. These results confirm the broadened antigenic recognition of the V1A.3a.2 2023 isolates, which effectively increased their competitive advantage over the V1A.3a.1 strain, ultimately leading to the gradual disappearance of the clade V1A.3a.1 after 2023.

Three-dimensional structural homology modeling of distinct epitope variants in the B/Victoria hemagglutinin protein

To further elucidate the structural mechanisms underlying the antigenic divergence between clades V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2, we employed AlphaFold3 for structural homology modeling of hemagglutinin (HA) variants encompassing distinct epitope regions. Structural analysis revealed that mutations in the clade V1A.3a.1 (H122N, T127A, A199T, and R200K) did not induce significant structural alterations in the HA1 protein. Notably, the substitution L144P precipitated pronounced conformational changes (Fig. 6A), particularly in the 150-loop domain spanning residues 146–148, suggesting this modification may act as a potential candidate determinant for the antigenic divergence between clade V1A.3a.1 and the AU21 strain. For the 2022 V1A.3a.2 variants carrying H122Q, D129N, S142A, N145S, D194G, and A199V substitutions, structural modeling showed that the overall structural integrity of the HA1 subunit was maintained (Fig. 6B), which provides a structural basis for the observed antigenic conservation relative to the vaccine strain. In the clade V1A.3a.2 of 2023, additional substitutions P169S + L170S or D194E also did not lead to significant structural alterations (Fig. 6C). This finding indicates that the L144P substitution may may contribute to the immune evasion of the clade V1A.3a.1, via inducing conformational rearrangement of the 150-loop domain. Of note, its specific functional role and underlying molecular mechanism need to be further validated.

Discussion

Global transmission of IBV underwent profound restructuring during the COVID-19 pandemic, marked by the extinction of the Yamagata lineage and the complete dominance of the Victoria lineage[10, 21]. However, the antigenic and genetic evolutionary trajectories of IBV in subtropical sentinel regions remain largely uncharacterized. Our study bridges this critical knowledge gap through systematic surveillance conducted in Guangzhou (2022–2023), revealing two pivotal phenomena: (i) the striking co-circulation of clades V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2 prior to V1A.3a.2’s complete dominance in Southern China during 2022–2023, contrasting with global V1A.3a.1 extinction post-2020 (Fig. 2A), and (ii) an accelerated diversification within the clade V1A.3a.2 following the relaxation of NPIs (Fig. 4A-D). These distinct dynamics contrast sharply with global patterns and provide vital insights for shaping regional vaccine strategies.

Phylogenetic analysis of the HA gene demonstrated that V1A.3a.1/V1A.3a.2 were co-circulating during the winter 2022–2023 epidemics, with complete replacement by V1A.3a.2 observed in the 2023–2024 seasonal outbreaks. This clade succession pattern was found to parallel global trends toward V1A.3a.2 dominance post-pandemic[36, 37]. The mutation H122Q is a distinct molecular characteristic of viruses from clade V1A.3a.2 in China [38]. In this study, we found it showed distinct epidemiological characteristics in different influenza seasons. In the representative strain of clade V1A.3a.2 in 2022, the mutation proportion was 100%, while in 2023 it was only 41.7%. The increased diversity of this strain may be a factor for the accelerated evolutionary rate of the HA gene after NPIs relaxation. It also sped up the local replacement of clade V1A.3a.1, leading to the disappearance of this subclade. Notably, Clade V1A.3a.1 eradicated globally by 2020 and persisted endemically in China until 2023. Spatial–temporal analysis designated Jiangxi Province as a potential epicenter during the 2020–2021 outbreaks, suggesting disparities in localized transmission networks or heterogeneity in pre-existing population immunity. This conclusion was further robustly validated by our discrete Bayesian phylogeographic analysis based on full-length HA gene sequences: we identified the earliest high-confidence (posterior probability = 1.0) domestic inter-provincial transmission event originated from Jiangxi to Guizhou in mid-2020, and confirmed Jiangxi as the primary upstream core hub of nationwide transmission, which contributed 6 independent high-confidence (posterior probability = 1.0) output events covering 5 geographic regions of China and initiated the core transmission backbone of the clade’s national spread (Fig. 3G). Phylogenetic evidence supported Jiangxi Province as the putative origin of domestic V1A.3a.1/V1A.3a.2 outbreaks during 2019–2021 [39]. Subtropical region has been epidemiologically characterized as an influenza outbreak source [40]. Analysis of influenza epidemic dynamics in southern China (1995–2009) established that the region cannot be classified as a global influenza epicenter [25]. Our study suggesting that Jiangxi’s potential undiscovered significance in global or China seasonal influenza circulation given its subtropical location. However, alternative hypotheses also propose that viral biological properties, rather than geographical determinants, govern outbreak origins and temporal transmission patterns[41]. Further studies are needed to elucidate the complex factors driving the endemic transmission and evolutionary dynamics of seasonal influenza viruses.

Vaccination is established as a primary strategy for preventing and managing seasonal influenza. In February 2022, the World Health Organization revised the vaccine strains for the B/Victoria lineage, replacing B/Washington/02/2019 (WA02, clade V1A.3) with AU21. Our monitoring timeframe covered the vaccine replacement cycle. We found that most isolates from the 2022—2023 season showed notable antigenic divergence from the WA/02, suggesting a sub-optimal vaccine match. This offers data-based support for the antigenicity of V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2 during their co-circulation in the 2022—2023 season. For the current WHO-recommended AU21 vaccine strain, our results further confirmed a high degree of antigenic consistency between the clade V1A.3a.2 2023 and the vaccine strain: 100% of 2023 isolates exhibited HI titers ≥ 160 against AU21 ferret antiserum (Fig. 5A), far exceeding the WHO-defined 50% protective threshold (HI titer ≥ 40) [42]. During the COVID-19 pandemic, clade V1A.3a.2 spread globally, causing substantial importation into Chinese provinces such as Anhui, Beijing, Fujian, and Guangdong [29]. We considered that this extensive global transmission significantly contributed to V1A.3a.2 becoming the predominant strain in China. Cross—reactive HI assays with AU21 and GZ/50 antisera clarified antigenic differences between V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2, and replacement dynamics of V1A.3a.1 by V1A.3a.2 viruses, induced by antigenic recognition expansion of the clade V1A.3a.2 2023 viruses. Notably, the convergent antigenic evolution of the 2023 V1A.3a.2 clade further expanded the protective coverage of the AU21 vaccine: vaccine-induced antibodies not only effectively neutralized the currently dominant strains, but also provided cross-protection against the previously co-circulating V1A.3a.1 clade, validating the forward-looking and sustained effectiveness of WHO’s 2022 vaccine strain update decision. The dominant clade evolved to exploit pre-existing immunity against V1A.3a.1 more effectively, outcompeting it for the ecological niche and causing the collapse of its transmission chain.

The global head of the IBV HA protein encompasses four major antigenic recognition domains: 120-loop (75,77,116–137), 150-loop (141–150), 160-loop (162–167), and 190-helix (194–202). At least four amino acid substitutions in these domains, particularly in the 120-loop and 150-loop areas, can drive substantial shifts in viral antigenicity [43]. Hemagglutination inhibition assays from ferret serum indicate that V1A.3a.1 demonstrates significant antigenic escape, containing consistant mutations T127A, L144P, and R200K. The substitution L144P has been identified as a critical antigenic determinant for the H5N1 avian influenza virus HA protein [44]. Three-dimensional homology modeling of the HA variant shows that L144P induces a conformational shift in the 150-loop region (146–148) of IBV HA, potentially serving as the structural basis for antigenic divergence of V1A.3a.1. Given that the 150-loop of IBV HA is structurally adjacent to the conserved receptor-binding site (RBS), this L144P-induced conformational shift not only directly disrupts the spatial structure of the core antigenic epitope in this region to evade neutralizing antibody recognition (providing the direct structural basis for the observed antigenic divergence of the V1A.3a.1 clade), but also may fine-tune the local conformation of the RBS to modulate viral binding affinity to human-type α-2,6 sialic acid receptors [45]. Moreover, the mutation R200K is recognized as an antigenic determinant in multiple influenza A virus subtypes, including H1N1, H2N2, and H3N2 [46]. This mutation in swine H9N2 has also been confirmed to function as an antigenic determinant and boost pathogenicity in mice [47]. However, our modeling results imply that this specific R200K change may not produce significant conformational alterations in the HA’s 190-helix; its influence on IBV pathogenicity and antigenicity necessitates further investigation. During 2022–2023 and 2023–2024 winter, genetic analysis identified mutations in V1A.3A.2/V1A.3a.2 epitope: H122Q, E128G, D129N, P169S, L170S, S142A, N145S, D194G/E, A199V. H122Q is a unique marker for clade V1A.3a.2 in China [38]. HA169 mutation associates with virulence in mice and antigenic drift; HA194 mutation increases α-2,6 sialoside binding and antigenic drift [44]. Homology modeling suggests these mutations collectively may not cause major conformational changes in the HA protein. Furthermore, the current low prevalence of coexisting mutations indicates they have not profoundly modified the antigenicity of the dominant V1A.3a.2 strain. Further investigation is needed to clarify molecular mechanisms governing enhanced antigenic recognition of V1A.3a.2 variants after NPI relaxation.

This study also has several limitations, limited surveillance cohort and sample size may introduce potential bias into the analysis of evolutionary rate, selection pressure, and phylogenetic reconstruction. Due to ethical constraints and data privacy policies, detailed demographic information (e.g., age and sex) of participants was unavailable, preventing a comprehensive analysis of associations between host demographics and viral evolutionary dynamics, including antigenic variation. Restricted genomic scope to the HA and NA genes, with no whole-genome sequencing performed to evaluate the potential impacts of variations in other internal segments (including NS, M, NP, PA, PB1, and PB2) on the virulence, replication capacity, and reassortment trajectory of IBV. Functional inferences of antigenic site variations (including the HA L144P substitution) were only based on homology modeling and sequence analysis, lacking in vitro microneutralization assays, receptor binding assays, and in vivo animal model validation, which limits the translational value of relevant conclusions. Furthermore, our conclusion that Jiangxi is the endemic epidemic center of the V1A.3a.1 clade, although supported by spatial distribution characteristics and Bayesian phylogeographic analysis, lacks in-depth interpretation and cross-validation with local prevention and control policies as well as population mobility data, leading to limitations in the supporting dimensions of this conclusion. Future studies incorporating travel data and more granular sequencing could more definitively identify transmission routes.

In summary, our study elucidated the epidemic pattern, genetic characteristics, and antigenic evolution of IBVs circulating in Guangzhou, China, during 2022–2023, with supplementary surveillance data up to 2024. We decoded a China-specific IBV epidemic pattern: co-circulation then replacement of antigenically divergent clades (V1A.3a.1 to V1A.3a.2), driven by HA evolution post-NPIs (Fig. 4) and epitope destabilization (Fig. 6). Combined spatial–temporal analysis and discrete Bayesian phylogeography designated Jiangxi Province as a potential epicenter during the 2020–2021 outbreaks, establishing a distinct IBV epidemic profile in China. We also revealed striking antigenic disparities between V1A.3a.1 and V1A.3a.2; the L144P mutation within V1A.3a.1 HA prompted a structural alteration in the HA 150-loop region (146–148), which may serve as the key structural basis for their antigenic divergence. Broadened antigenic recognition of the clade V1A.3a.2 2023 viruses led to the replacement dynamics of V1A.3a.1 by V1A.3a.2 viruses, with these isolates showing high antigenic matching with the WHO-recommended vaccine strain. This study identifies Guangzhou as a key sentinel site for monitoring IBV evolution in subtropical regions, and provides critical molecular and antigenic evidence for the optimization of regional influenza vaccination strategies, especially for vaccine strain selection and regional immunization program adjustment.

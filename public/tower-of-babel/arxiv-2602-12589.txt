Berry–Esseen Bounds and Moderate Deviations for Catoni-Type Robust Estimation Equal Contributions
ZhiJun Cai, Xiang Li, Lihu Xu

Abstract
A powerful robust mean estimator introduced by Catoni (2012) allows for mean estimation of heavy-tailed data while achieving the performance characteristics of classical mean estimator for sub-Gaussian data. While Catoni’s framework has been widely extended across statistics, stochastic algorithms, and machine learning, fundamental asymptotic questions regarding the Central Limit Theorem and rare event deviations remain largely unaddressed. In this paper, we investigate Catoni-type robust estimators in two contexts: (i) mean estimation for heavy-tailed data, and (ii) linear regression with heavy-tailed innovations. For the first model, we establish the Berry–Esseen bound and moderate deviation principles, addressing both known and unknown variance settings. For the second model, we demonstrate that the associated estimator is consistent and satisfies a multi-dimensional Berry-Esseen bound.



Keywords:
Catoni estimato; heavy-tailed distributions; Berry–Esseen bound;
moderate deviations; robust regression.

Introduction

Parameter estimation is a fundamental task in statistics. Among such tasks, estimating the mean of a distribution is one of the most basic and central problems. Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables with expectation
\mu
, the parameter of interest. A standard estimator of
\mu
is the sample mean, defined by

\bar{X}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}.

However, the sample mean is highly sensitive to extreme values and may be suboptimal for heavy-tailed distributions. To mitigate the variance inflation and instability caused by outliers under heavy tails,
17
introduced M-estimators based on the Huber loss, striking a tunable balance between efficiency and robustness. Building on this line,

4
proposed a new robust estimator of the mean, defined as the solution
\hat{\theta}
to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi\big[\alpha_{n}(X_{i}-\theta)\big]=0,

(1.1)

where
\alpha_{n}
is a positive tuning parameter, and
\varphi(x)
is a non-decreasing and continuous function satisfying that

\displaystyle-\log(1-x+x^{2}/2)\leq\varphi(x)\leq\log(1+x+x^{2}/2),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.2)

Importantly,
4
developed a finite-sample analytical framework for robust mean estimation, establishing nonasymptotic guarantees that highlight the advantages of robust estimators and the limitations of the sample mean under heavy tails.

Since Catoni’s seminal work, many related studies have appeared, including applications to empirical risk minimization under unbounded losses
(
3
)
, investigations of cases with finite
1+\delta
(
0<\delta<1
) moments
(
8
)
, extensions to confidence sequences
(
27
;
28
)
, as well as other related works, see
16
for stochastic algorithm,
26
;
11
for high dimensional statistics, and
29
for robust estimation in the deep neural network.

However, despite the many subsequent studies, the asymptotic properties of Catoni-type estimators have rarely been investigated.
30
analyzed the asymptotic behavior of
\hat{\theta}
in a particular setting. Specifically, they considered the special case
\varphi(x)=\varphi_{1}(x):=\text{sign}(x)\log(1+|x|+x^{2}/2)
under the assumptions
X_{1}-u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}u-X_{1}
and
\alpha_{n}=O(n^{-1/2})
, and proved that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{1}^{2}(\alpha_{n}(X_{1}-u))]/\alpha_{n}^{2}}}\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}N(0,1).

(1.3)

However, the symmetry assumption is overly restrictive, and qualitative results such as the central limit theorem offer limited guidance for quantitative calibration or tuning. In fact, in the absence of symmetry, the bias induced by Catoni-type estimators becomes apparent (see the main theorems in Section
2
). Consequently, once the symmetry assumption is removed, further quantitative analyses such as Berry–Esseen bounds and moderate deviation results become considerably more valuable.

Berry–Esseen bounds and moderate deviation theory offer practical, finite-sample guidance for Gaussian-style inference. Berry–Esseen bounds tell us how accurate normal approximations are for standardized estimators and common test statistics, so we can judge confidence-interval coverage and test size in real samples. Moderate deviation results describe mid-tail probabilities beyond the core CLT range, improving p-values near typical cutoffs and signaling when normal critical values remain trustworthy or need slight adjustment. Recent studies across diverse settings attest to their sustained centrality in statistical research—for example, non-normal approximation
(
23
;
22
)
; high-dimensional normal approximation
(
9
;
10
;
12
)
; self-normalized processes

(
18
;
25
;
15
)
; exponential random graph models
(
5
;
13
)
.

In this paper, we establish the asymptotic properties of
\hat{\theta}
for arbitrary functions satisfying (
1.2
),
without
the condition
X_{1}-u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}u-X_{1}
. Our results include Berry–Esseen bounds as well as Cramér-type moderate deviation results.
At the same time, recognizing that the robustness tuning parameter in Catoni’s estimator can be inconvenient to select when the variance is unknown, we also study Cramér-type moderate deviations for a self-normalized formulation of Catoni’s estimator. Specifically, the estimator we analyze is
\hat{\theta}_{s}
defined as the solution to the following equation

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\sigma}}{na_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi\Big[\frac{X_{i}-\theta}{\hat{\sigma}}a_{n}\Big]=0,

(1.4)

with
\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\bar{X})^{2}
and tuning parameter
a_{n}
.

As a generalization of mean estimation, we also consider the following regression model.
Let
(y_{1},\boldsymbol{x}_{1}),\dots,(y_{n},\boldsymbol{x}_{n})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
be independent data samples such that

\displaystyle y_{i}=\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}+\varepsilon_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

(1.5)

where
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}
are independent random variables with
\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{i}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{i}^{2}]=\sigma_{i}^{2}<\infty
, and are independent of
\{\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{x}_{n}\}
.
Here, the parameter of interest is the regression coefficient vector
\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}
.
We estimate
\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}
by solving the estimating equation

\displaystyle h(\boldsymbol{\beta}):=\frac{1}{n\alpha_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\,\varphi\!\left[\alpha_{n}\bigl(y_{i}-\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{\beta}\bigr)\right]=0,

(1.6)

where
\alpha_{n}
is a sequence of constants depending on
n
and
\varphi
is a function satisfying (
1.2
).
We denote the resulting estimator by
\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}
. In this paper, we establish both non-asymptotic and asymptotic results for
\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}
.

Our main contributions can be summarized as follows: (i) For mean estimation, we derive general Berry–Esseen bounds and Cramér-type moderate deviation results. Notably, unlike classical studies that require certain restrictions on the derivative of
\mathbb{E}\{\varphi[\alpha_{n}(X_{1}-\cdot\,)]\}
(e.g.,
1
;
19
), we do not assume any such conditions. When the variance is unknown, we also establish Berry–Esseen bounds and moderate deviation results for the corresponding self-normalized statistics, under the assumption of a finite
2+\delta
moment.
(ii) We extend the Catoni-type mean estimation to regression models and study the properties of this estimator. The results show that the deviations of such estimator have the same order as in the Gaussian sample case. Moreover, under some additional mild conditions, we also develop the asymptotic theory of this estimator.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
presents our main results in two parts.
The first part focuses on asymptotic results for mean estimation, including Berry–Esseen bounds and Cramér-type moderate deviations for both known and unknown variance.
The second part covers results for Catoni-type regression, including non-asymptotic results and Berry–Esseen bounds.
Section
3
contains the proofs of main results for mean estimation, while the proofs of main results for Catoni-type robust regression are postponed to Section
4
.

BEDs and MDs for mean estimation with known variance

Assumption 2.1

We assume that

1.

\displaystyle-\log\big(1-x+x^{2}/2\big)\leq\varphi(x)\leq\log\big(1+x+x^{2}/2\big)\quad\text{for any }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.1)

2.

Let

The following theorem is one of our main results.

Theorem 2.1

Under Assumption
2.1

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{P}\big(\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-u_{n})/\sigma\leq z\big)-\Phi(z)\big|\leq C\beta_{2}+C\beta_{3},

(2.2)

Remark 2.1
.

We first make some remarks on
u_{n}.
Here, we subtract
u_{n}
rather than the expectation of
X_{1}
when performing the standardization. This is because

\hat{\theta}
is a biased estimator of
u
(at least in most cases). More precisely, similar to the case of

should be regarded as an estimate of

In some special cases, such as when condition
X_{1}-u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}u-X_{1}
holds and
\varphi(x)=-\varphi(-x)
, we have
u_{n}=u
. In the general case, however,
u_{n}\neq u
, and Lemma
3.2
implies that

\displaystyle|u_{n}-u|\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}\sigma^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha_{n}^{2}\sigma^{2}}}.

(2.3)

Observe that for any
0<\varepsilon<1

\displaystyle\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big\{|X_{1}-u|^{2}\mathbf{1}(|X_{1}-u|\geq\sqrt{n}\sigma)\big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma^{3}}\mathbb{E}\big\{|X_{1}-u|^{3}\mathbf{1}(|X_{1}-u|\leq\varepsilon\sqrt{n}\sigma)\big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma^{3}}\mathbb{E}\big\{|X_{1}-u|^{3}\mathbf{1}(\varepsilon\sqrt{n}\sigma<|X_{1}-u|\leq\sqrt{n}\sigma)\big\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big\{|X_{1}-u|^{2}\mathbf{1}(|X_{1}-u|>\varepsilon\sqrt{n}\sigma)\big\}+\varepsilon.

(2.4)

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, then by Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem,

as

So we immediately have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

Under Assumption
2.1

\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-u_{n})/\sigma\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1)\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

Moreover, if
\sqrt{n}a_{n}\to 0
as

then

\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-u)/\sigma\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1)\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

Remark 2.2
.

Using the fact that
|\varphi(x)|\leq x
and
|\varphi(x)-x|\leq x^{2}
yields that
|\varphi^{2}(x)-x^{2}|\leq 4x^{2}\mathbf{1}(|x|\geq 1)+2x^{3}\mathbf{1}(|x|\leq 1)
, which further implies that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}[\varphi^{2}(\alpha_{n}(X_{1}-u))]/\alpha_{n}^{2}-\sigma^{2}\big|

\displaystyle\quad\leq 4\mathbb{E}\big\{|X_{1}-u|^{2}\mathbf{1}(\alpha_{n}|X_{1}-u|\geq 1)\big\}+2\alpha_{n}\mathbb{E}\big\{|X_{1}-u|^{3}\mathbf{1}(\alpha_{n}|X_{1}-u|\leq 1)\big\}

\displaystyle\quad\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

(2.5)

From this perspective, our result covers the result of
30
.

Combine Theorem
2.1
and (
2.3
), we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

Under Assumption
2.1

\mathbb{E}|X_{1}-u|^{2+\delta}<\infty

\delta\in(0,1]
, then

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{P}\big(\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-u_{n})/\sigma\leq z\big)-\Phi(z)\big|\leq Cn^{-\delta/2}\sigma^{-2-\delta}\mathbb{E}|X_{1}-u|^{2+\delta},

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{P}\big(\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-u)/\sigma\leq z\big)-\Phi(z)\big|\leq Cn^{-\delta/2}\sigma^{-2-\delta}\mathbb{E}|X_{1}-u|^{2+\delta}+C\sqrt{n}a_{n},

where
C
is a positive constant depending on the distribution of
X_{1}
.

Theorem
2.1
establishes a Berry–Esseen bound for
\hat{\theta}
,
theoretically demonstrating that its distribution can be approximated by a normal distribution.
However, the accuracy of this result for the tail probability

To address this, we further present a Cramér-type moderate deviation result,

Theorem 2.4
.

Under Assumption

3.1
Some preliminary lemmas

In this subsection, we present several preliminary lemmas. We begin by introducing some properties of

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi(\cdot)
be a function that satisfies condition
1
, then
for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle|\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi(x_{2})-(x_{1}-x_{2})|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\log\big(1+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2\big)+|x_{1}-x_{2}|\Big(\frac{x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2}{1+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2}+|x_{1}-x_{2}|\Big).

(3.1)

Proof.

By (
2.1
) and the basic inequality
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi(x_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\log(1+x_{1}+x_{1}^{2}/2)+\log(1-x_{2}+x_{2}^{2}/2)

\displaystyle=\log\big(1+x_{1}-x_{2}+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}/2+x_{1}x_{2}(x_{2}-x_{1})/2+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/4\big)

\displaystyle\leq\log\big(1+x_{1}-x_{2}+3(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}/4+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2\big).

(3.2)

Noting that for any
x>-1

1+x+y=(1+x)\cdot[1+y/(1+x)]\leq(1+x)\cdot\exp[y/(1+x)]

\displaystyle\log(1+x+y)\leq\log(1+x)+\frac{y}{1+x}.

(3.3)

Combining (
3.1

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi(x_{2})-(x_{1}-x_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\log(1+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2)-(x_{1}-x_{2})\frac{x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2}{1+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2}+\frac{3(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}{4(1+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2)}

\displaystyle\leq\log(1+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2)+|x_{1}-x_{2}|\frac{x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2}{1+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}/2}+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}.

(3.4)

By (
3.1
) and the symmetry, we obtain (
3.1
).
∎

The following Lemma provides an upper bound for
|u_{n}-u|.

Lemma 3.2
.

If the condition
1
is satisfied, then

\displaystyle|u_{n}-u|\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}\sigma^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha_{n}^{2}\sigma^{2}}}.

Proof.

Note that
f(x)=\mathbb{E}\{\varphi[\alpha_{n}(X_{1}-x)]\}
is non-increasing for
x
, to bound
u_{n}
, we only need to find
u_{+}
,
u_{-}
such that

\displaystyle u_{-}<u_{+},\quad f(u_{+})<0\quad\text{and}\quad f(u_{-})>0.

By Jensen’s inequality and (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\log\Big(1+\alpha_{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{1}-x)+\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}(X_{1}-x)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\log\Big(1+\alpha_{n}(u-x)+\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2}}{2}(\sigma^{2}+(u-x)^{2})\Big):=f_{+}(x).

(3.5)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle\geq-\log\Big(1-\alpha_{n}(u-x)+\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2}}{2}(\sigma^{2}+(u-x)^{2})\Big):=f_{-}(x).

(3.6)

By (
3.1
) and (
3.6
), if we can find
u_{+}
,
u_{-}
such that

u_{-}<u_{+},\quad f_{+}(u_{+})<0\quad\text{and}\quad f_{-}(u_{-})>0

then
u_{-}-u\leq u_{n}-u\leq u_{+}-u.
By the definition of
f_{+}(x)
, if
x\leq u
, then
f_{+}(x)>0
. Now, set

u_{+}=u+\eta_{+}
, then

\displaystyle f_{+}(u_{+})=\log\Big(1-\alpha_{n}\eta_{+}+\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2}}{2}(\sigma^{2}+\eta_{+}^{2})\Big).

In order to ensure
f_{+}(u_{+})<0
, we only need to find
\eta_{+}

Consequently, there must exists a

Meanwhile, the other half of the inequality follows by performing the same analysis for
f_{-}

∎

Lemma 3.3

(

For a sequence of i.i.d. random variables

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}^{c}\mid\boldsymbol{x})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(L_{n}\alpha_{n}|\varepsilon_{i}|\geq C^{\prime}\mid\boldsymbol{x})\leq CL_{n}^{3}n^{-1/2}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}/\sigma^{3}.

(4.28)

To choose a suitable
\Delta
, let us first give an upper bound for
\|D\|_{2}\mathbf{1}(\mathcal{O})
.
For any
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{1}(L_{n}\alpha_{n}|\varepsilon_{i}|\leq C^{\prime})
and
\boldsymbol{\bar{\beta}}
be the solution of equation (
4.4
). Under the event
\mathcal{O}
, it follows from (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle\|D\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\frac{\boldsymbol{S}_{n}^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{n}\alpha_{n}\tilde{\sigma}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\big(\varphi\big[\alpha_{n}(\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}+\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}-\boldsymbol{\bar{\beta}}))\big]-\varphi[\alpha_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}]-\alpha_{n}[\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}+\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}-\boldsymbol{\bar{\beta}})]\big)\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{l}^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{n}\alpha_{n}\tilde{\sigma}}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\big(\varphi\big[\alpha_{n}(\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}+\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}-\boldsymbol{\bar{\beta}}))\big]-\varphi[\alpha_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}]-\alpha_{n}[\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}+\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}-\boldsymbol{\bar{\beta}})]\big)\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{l}^{-1/2}\sqrt{n}}{\tilde{\sigma}}\|R\|_{2},

(4.29)

with
R
defined in (
4.7
). Combining this with (
4.2
) and Lemma
4.1
gives us, under the event
\mathcal{O}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|D\|_{2}&\leq\frac{CL_{n}}{\sqrt{n}\tilde{\sigma}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{n}|\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}-\boldsymbol{\bar{\beta}})|\\
&\leq\frac{CL_{n}\alpha_{n}}{\sqrt{n}\tilde{\sigma}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\varepsilon_{i}|\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}-\boldsymbol{\bar{\beta}}\|_{2}\\
&\leq\frac{CL_{n}\alpha_{n}}{\sqrt{n}\tilde{\sigma}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\varepsilon_{i}|\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}\bigg\{\Big\|\frac{1}{n\alpha_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\big\{\varphi[\alpha_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{j}]-\mathbb{E}\varphi[\alpha_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{j}]\big\}\Big\|_{2}+\sqrt{p}L_{n}a_{n}\sigma\bigg\}.\end{split}

(4.30)

What’s more, for
\tilde{\sigma}
, note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big|\tilde{\sigma}^{2}/\sigma^{2}-1\big|&=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\left|\operatorname{Var}(\varphi[\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1}])/\alpha_{n}^{2}-\sigma^{2}\right|\\
&=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\Big|\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[\varphi^{2}(\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1})]-\sigma^{2}\Big)+\Big[\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\mathbb{E}\varphi(\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1})\Big]^{2}\Big|.\end{split}

(4.31)

By the same argument as in (
2.2
), inequalities
|\varphi(x)|\leq|x|
,
|\varphi(x)-x|\leq|x|^{2}
and noting that Assumption
2.1
ensures
\alpha_{n}=a_{n}\sigma^{-1}\leq Cn^{-1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[\varphi^{2}(\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1})]-\sigma^{2}\Big|\leq 4\alpha_{n}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}\leq Cn^{-1/2}\sigma^{-1}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3},

and

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\mathbb{E}\varphi(\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1})\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{2}}\big[\mathbb{E}\{\varphi(\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1})-\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1}\}\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left|\varphi(\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1})-\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1}\right|\cdot\mathbb{E}\{|\varphi(\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1})|+\alpha_{n}|\varepsilon_{1}|\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha_{n}\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{1}^{2}]\cdot\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|\leq 2\alpha_{n}\mathbb{E}\left|\varepsilon_{1}\right|^{3}\leq Cn^{-1/2}\sigma^{-1}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}.

These together with (
4.31
) gives us

\displaystyle\big|\tilde{\sigma}^{2}/\sigma^{2}-1\big|\leq c_{0}n^{-1/2}\frac{\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}}{\sigma^{3}}

(4.32)

for some positive constant
c_{0}
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that

\displaystyle pL_{n}^{3}n^{-1/2}\frac{\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}}{\sigma^{3}}\leq 0.5\min\big\{c_{l}^{3/2}c_{0}^{-1},1\big\}.

(4.33)

Otherwise, (
2.11
) holds with
C=2\max\big\{c_{0}c_{l}^{-3/2},1\big\}.
By (
4.32
), (
4.33
) and the fact that
L_{n}\geq\sqrt{c_{l}p}
, we have
\tilde{\sigma}^{2}\geq 0.5\sigma^{2}.
Combining this with (
4.30
) and the fact that
\alpha_{n}\leq Cn^{-1/2}
, we obtain, under the event
\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|D\|_{2}&\leq\frac{CL_{n}\alpha_{n}}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\varepsilon_{i}|\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}\bigg\{\Big\|\frac{1}{n\alpha_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\big\{\varphi[\alpha_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{j}]-\mathbb{E}\varphi[\alpha_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{j}]\big\}\Big\|_{2}+\sqrt{p}L_{n}a_{n}\sigma\bigg\}\\
&\leq c_{1}L_{n}T_{n}\big\{\|M_{n}\|_{2}+\sqrt{p}L_{n}n^{-1/2}\big\},\end{split}

(4.34)

for some constant
c_{1}
, where

Y_{i}=\frac{\varphi[\alpha_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}]-\mathbb{E}\varphi[\alpha_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}]}{\alpha_{n}},\quad M_{n}=\frac{1}{n\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}Y_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{n}=\frac{1}{n\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}|\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}|.

Accordingly, we take

\Delta:=c_{1}L_{n}T_{n}\big\{\|M_{n}\|_{2}+\sqrt{p}L_{n}n^{-1/2}\big\},

and
\Delta_{(i)}
as the random variable obtained by substituting
\varepsilon_{i}
in
\Delta
with an independent copy
\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}
.

Up to this point,

\Delta
and
\Delta_{(i)}
have been well-defined. In what follows, we sequentially provide upper bounds for the first three terms of (
4.2
). For convenience, in the following, we use
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}[\,\cdot\,]
to denote
\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\mid\boldsymbol{x}]
.

For the first term of (
4.2
), by the definition of
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}
and the inequality
|\varphi(x)|\leq|x|
, we have

\displaystyle p^{1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\|_{2}^{3}\leq Cp^{1/2}n^{-3/2}\frac{\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}}{\sigma^{3}}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{3}\leq Cp^{3/2}L_{n}n^{-1/2}\frac{\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}}{\sigma^{3}},

(4.35)

where the last inequality follows from

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=\text{trace}(\boldsymbol{S}_{n})\leq c_{u}p.

(4.36)

For the second term of (
4.2
), by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\{\|W\|_{2}\Delta\}

\displaystyle\quad\leq CL_{n}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\{\|W\|_{2}^{2}\}\big)^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\{|T_{n}|^{4}\}\big)^{1/4}\big[\big(\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\{\|M_{n}\|_{2}^{4}\}\big)^{1/4}+\sqrt{p}L_{n}n^{-1/2}\big].

(4.37)

It follows from the definition of
W
and
M_{n}
, the inequality
|\varphi(x)|\leq x
,
\tilde{\sigma}^{2}\geq 0.5\sigma^{2}
and the independence that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\{\|W\|_{2}^{2}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\alpha_{n}^{2}\tilde{\sigma}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\boldsymbol{S}_{n}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\operatorname{Var}\left(\varphi[\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{i}]\right)=p,

(4.38)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}[M_{n}^{4}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{4}\sigma^{4}}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|^{2}Y_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}Y_{i}Y_{j}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\|^{2}Y_{k}^{2}+\sum_{k\neq l=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{k}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{x}_{l}Y_{k}Y_{l}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{4}\sigma^{4}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|^{2}Y_{i}^{2}\Big)^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}|^{2}Y_{i}^{2}Y_{j}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n^{4}\sigma^{4}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|^{4}\mathbb{E}|\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}|^{4}+\sum_{i\neq j=1}^{n}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|^{2}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\|^{2}\sigma^{4}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n^{4}}\Big(n\alpha_{n}^{-1}pL_{n}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}/\sigma^{2}+n^{2}p^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-2}p^{2}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}/\sigma^{3}.

(4.39)

For
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\{|T_{n}|^{4}\}
, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
4.36
), we have

\displaystyle T_{n}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{c_{u}p}}{\sigma}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Hence, it then follows from (
4.33
),
|\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\alpha_{n}L_{n}}
and
\alpha_{n}=a_{n}\sigma^{-1}\leq Cn^{-1/2}
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\{|T_{n}|^{4}\}&\leq\frac{c_{u}^{2}p^{2}}{n^{2}\sigma^{4}}\mathbb{E}\Big\{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}\Big)^{2}\Big\}\leq\frac{c_{u}^{2}p^{2}}{n^{2}\sigma^{4}}\left(n(n-1)\sigma^{4}+n\mathbb{E}|\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}|^{4}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{Cp^{2}}{\sigma^{4}}\Big(\sigma^{4}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}L_{n}n}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}\Big)\leq Cp^{2}.\end{split}

(4.40)

Now, combining (
4.2
), (
4.38
), (
4.2
) and (
4.40
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\{\|W\|_{2}\Delta\}\leq\frac{CL_{n}^{2}p^{3/2}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}}{\sqrt{n}\sigma^{3}}.

(4.41)

As for the third term of (
4.2
), let
Y_{i}^{\prime}

M_{n}^{(i)}
and
T_{n}^{(i)}
be the versions of
Y_{i}
,
M_{n}
and
T_{n}
in which
\varepsilon_{i}
is replaced by an independent copy
\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}
. Then, it follows from the definitions of
\Delta
and
\Delta_{i}
that, for
1\leq i\leq n
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\Delta-\Delta_{i}|&=c_{1}L_{n}\left|\sqrt{p}L_{n}n^{-1/2}(T_{n}-T_{n}^{(i)})+T_{n}\|M_{n}\|_{2}-T_{n}^{(i)}\|M_{n}^{(i)}\|_{2}\right|\\
&\leq c_{1}L_{n}\Big\{\sqrt{p}L_{n}n^{-1/2}|T_{n}-T_{n}^{(i)}|+\big|T_{n}-T_{n}^{(i)}\big|\|M_{n}\|_{2}\\
&\hskip 156.49014pt+|T_{n}^{(i)}|\Big|\|M_{n}\|_{2}-\|M_{n}^{(i)}\|_{2}\Big|\Big\}.\end{split}

Applying this with the Hölder inequality, we obtain, for
1\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\|_{2}|\Delta-\Delta_{i}|\}\\
&\leq CL_{n}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\|_{2}^{3}\right)^{1/3}\bigg\{\sqrt{p}L_{n}n^{-1/2}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}|T_{n}-T_{n}^{(i)}|^{3}\right)^{1/3}\\
&\hskip 99.58464pt+\left(\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}|T_{n}-T_{n}^{(i)}|^{3}\right)^{1/3}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\|M_{n}\|_{2}^{3}\right)^{1/3}\\
&\hskip 99.58464pt+\left(\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}|T_{n}^{(i)}|^{3}\right)^{1/3}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\Big|\|M_{n}\|_{2}-\|M_{n}^{(i)}\|_{2}\Big|^{3}\Big)^{1/3}\bigg\}.\end{split}

(4.42)

Since
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
are identically distributed, by the definition of
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}
, the property
|\varphi(x)|\leq|x|
and
\tilde{\sigma}^{2}\geq 0.5\sigma^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\|_{2}^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{3/2}\alpha_{n}^{3}\tilde{\sigma}^{3}}\|\boldsymbol{S}_{n}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{3}\mathbb{E}\left|\varphi[\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{1}]\right|^{3}\leq\frac{C\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{3}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}}{n^{3/2}\sigma^{3}}.

(4.43)

At the same time, by definitions and
|\varphi(x)|\leq|x|
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\big|T_{n}^{(i)}-T_{n}\big|^{3}=\frac{1}{n^{3}\sigma^{3}}\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{3}\mathbb{E}\left||\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}^{\prime}|-|\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}|\right|^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{3}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}}{n^{3}\sigma^{3}},

(4.44)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\big|\|M_{n}\|_{2}-\|M_{n}^{(i)}\|_{2}\big|^{3}&\leq\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\|M_{n}-M_{n}^{(i)}\|_{2}^{3}\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\Big\|\frac{1}{n\alpha_{n}\sigma}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\left\{\varphi[\alpha_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}]-\varphi[\alpha_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}^{\prime}]\right\}\Big\|_{2}^{3}\\
&\leq\frac{C\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{3}}{n^{3}}\mathbb{E}|\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}^{3}|\leq\frac{C\|\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{3}\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}}{n^{3}\sigma^{3}}.\end{split}

(4.45)

Substituting (
4.2
)–(
4.40
) and (
4.43
)–(
4.45
) into (
4.42
), and using (
4.36
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\!\left[\|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\|_{2}\,|\Delta-\Delta_{i}|\right]\leq Cp^{3/2}L_{n}^{2}n^{-1/2}\frac{\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|^{3}}{\sigma^{3}}.

(4.46)

Finally, noting that
L_{n}\geq\sqrt{c_{l}p}
, the proof is completed by combining the estimates for the four terms in the upper bound of (
4.2
) obtained in (
4.28
), (
4.35
), (
4.41
) and (
4.46
).

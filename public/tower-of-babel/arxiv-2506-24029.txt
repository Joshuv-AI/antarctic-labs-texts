The regular representation of Neretin groups is factorial
Basile Morando

Abstract
We show that the left regular representation of Neretin groups is factorial, providing the first example of a non-discrete simple group with this property. This is based on a new criterion of factoriality for totally disconnected groups. For groups G G satisfying the criterion, we determine the type of the factor ℒ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{L}(G) and derive factoriality results for crossed products associated to G G -actions on von Neumann algebras.



Introduction

Let
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
be a unitary representation of a locally compact group
G
. It is
irreducible
if it cannot be decomposed as a direct sum of two subrepresentations, and
factorial
if it cannot be decomposed as a direct sum of two
disjoint
subrepresentations. These properties are encoded in the commutant of the representation
\pi(G)^{\prime}=\{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid\forall g\in G,T\pi(g)=\pi(g)T\}
. The representation
\pi
is irreducible if and only if
\pi(G)^{\prime}
is trivial, and
\pi
is factorial if and only if
\pi(G)^{\prime}
has a trivial center.

Any multiple of an irreducible representation is factorial, but the converse does not hold in general. A second-countable locally compact group
G
is said to be of
type
\I
if every factorial unitary representation of
G
is a multiple of an irreducible representation. The class
of type
\I
groups includes, among others, compact groups, abelian locally compact groups, connected semisimple Lie groups, connected nilpotent Lie groups and the group of automorphisms of a regular tree (see
[
BH20
, Theorem 6.E.19]
).

The terms
factorial representation
and
type
\I
group
are adopted from the theory of von Neumann algebras introduced by Murray and von Neumann in their seminal papers
[
MV36
]
. Von Neumann algebras can be defined as algebras of bounded operators on a Hilbert space that are equal to their double commutant. Consequently,
\pi(G)^{\prime}
and
\pi(G)^{\prime\prime}
are natural examples of von Neumann algebras. A von Neumann algebra is a
factor
if its center is trivial, and such factors have been classified by Murray and von Neumann into types
\I
,
\II
and
\III
based on the geometry of their lattices of projections. Type
\I
factors are exactly the factors of the form
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1})\otimes\1_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
. A representation
\pi
is factorial if and only if the von Neumann algebra
\pi(G)^{\prime}
(equivalently
\pi(G)^{\prime\prime}
) is a factor, and a second-countable group is of type
\I
if and only if
\pi(G)^{\prime\prime}
is of type
\I
for every factorial representation
\pi
of
G
.

In the same series of papers, Murray and von Neumann highlight that not every group is of type
\I
by examining left and right regular representations
\lambda_{G}
and
\rho_{G}
of discrete groups. They investigate the associated
group von Neumann algebra

\lambda_{G}(G)^{\prime}=\rho_{G}(G)^{\prime\prime}\simeq\lambda_{G}(G)^{\prime\prime}\doteq\mathcal{L}(G).

They show that when
G
is discrete,
\lambda_{G}
is factorial if and only if the nontrivial conjugacy classes of
G
are infinite: the resulting von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{L}(G)
is then a type
\II
factor, as it admits a faithful normal tracial state.

While this result completely settles the question of the factoriality of the regular representation of discrete groups, obtaining a similar result for non-discrete locally compact groups is a notoriously tricky problem (see, for instance,
[
Vae25
]
which highlights fundamentally new phenomena in the non-discrete case). Over the years, several examples of non-discrete groups whose group von Neumann algebra is a factor appeared in the literature.

Proposition 1

(
[

Let
\alpha:G\to\Aut(N)
be a continuous action by automorphisms of a locally compact group
G
on an abelian locally compact group

\hat{\alpha}:G\curvearrowright\hat{N}
the associated dual action on the Pontryagin dual of

is a factor.

For example, given a local field
\mathbb{K}
,
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{K}\rtimes\mathbb{K}^{*})
is a factor of type
\I
. Semi-restricted direct products of such groups are used in
[

(and more recently in

is a factor of type

Any Neretin group

Theorem A

(

The regular representation

As a consequence,

\II_{\infty}

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{N}_{d,k})

\II_{\infty}

To our knowledge, this provides the first example of a non-discrete simple group whose von Neumann algebra is a factor. The type of

]
for

1
Factoriality Criterion for Group von Neumann Algebras

1.1
Preliminaries

Operator topologies.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space with scalar product denoted by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and norm denoted by
\left\|\cdot\right\|
. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the algebra of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
be its subgroup of unitary operators. For any
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, let
\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}
denote its operator norm. Given vectors
\xi,\eta
in
\mathcal{H}
, consider the following seminorms on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\xi}\;:\;

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{C}\colon\quad x\mapsto\left\|x\xi\right\|

\displaystyle\omega_{\xi,\eta}\;:\;

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{C}\colon\quad x\mapsto\left\lvert\left\langle x\xi,\eta\right\rangle\right\rvert.

Then the
strong operator topology
(abbreviated as
\operatorname{sot}
) on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the topology induced by the seminorms
\{\sigma_{\xi}\mid\xi\in\mathcal{H}\}
, and the
weak operator topology
(abbreviated as
\operatorname{wot}
) on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the topology induced by the seminorms
\{\omega_{\xi,\eta}\mid\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}\}
.

Von Neumann algebras.

Let
A\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a non-empty set. Its
commutant
is the
*
-subalgebra

A^{\prime}\doteq\{x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid\forall a\in A,xa=ax\}
of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
and
A^{\prime\prime}\doteq(A^{\prime})^{\prime}
is the
bicommutant
of
A
. A
von Neumann algebra

M
is a unital
*
-subalgebra of some
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that
M^{\prime\prime}=M
. By the von Neumann bicommutant theorem, a unital
*
-subalgebra
M
of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a von Neumann algebra if and only if it is closed in strong operator topology (equivalently, in weak operator topology). Two von Neumann algebras
M\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
N\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
are isomorphic if there exists an algebraic
*
-isomorphism between the
*
-algebras
M
and
N
.

A von Neumann algebra
M
is a
factor
if its center
M^{\prime}\cap M
is trivial, i. e. reduced to the scalar multiple of the identity. Factors are classified into types
\I_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
\I_{\infty}
,
\II_{1}
,
\II_{\infty}
and
\III_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
. A factor is of type
\I_{n}
if it is finite dimensional, of type
\I_{\infty}
if it is infinite dimensional and admits a minimal projection. Any type
\I_{n}
factor, with
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, is of the form
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\otimes 1_{\mathcal{K}}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{K})
, where
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is a Hilbert space of dimension
n
. It is therefore isomorphic to the factor
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
. A factor is of type
\II_{1}
if it is not of type
\I
and admits a
trace
(that is a faithful normal tracial
state
), and of type
\II_{\infty}
if it is not of the previous types, but admits a faithful normal semifinite tracial
weight
. Other factors are of type
\III
, and their further classification into subtypes
\III_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
, was obtained in
[
Con73
]
via spectral properties of weights on the factor. For more precise and detailed definitions of these objects, see
[
GLN25
]
.

Let
M\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra, and
p\in M
be a projection. Then
pMp\subset\mathcal{B}(p\mathcal{H})
is again a von Neumann algebra, called
corner
of the von Neumann algebra
M
.

Let
N\subset M
be an inclusion of von Neumann algebras. A
conditional expectation

E:M\to N
is a projection of norm one between the Banach space
M
and
N
. A von Neumann algebra
M\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is
amenable
if there exists a conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to M
. Amenable von Neumann algebras are very well understood compared to the non-amenable ones since Connes and Haagerup have completely classified the amenable factors. In particular, there is only one isomorphism class of amenable factors of types
\II_{1}
,
\II_{\infty}
, and
\III_{\lambda}
,
\lambda>0
, such factors being called
hyperfinite
. If
G
is amenable as a group, then
\mathcal{L}(G)
is amenable. The converse holds for discrete groups, but not in general.

Locally compact groups.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. We denote by
\mu_{G}
a left Haar measure on
G
, and by
\Delta_{G}
its modular function defined by the relation
\mu_{G}(Ag)=\Delta_{G}(g)\mu_{G}(A)
for every Borel subset
A\subset G
. We denote by
L^{2}(G,\mu_{G})
the Hilbert space of square-integrable functions on
G
with respect to
\mu_{G}
. Subgroups
H\leq G
will always be assumed to be closed. We write
K<_{co}G
to indicate that
K
is a
compact open
subgroup of
G
.

If
G
is
totally disconnected
, we denote by
\operatorname{Sub_{co}}(G)
the directed set of compact open subgroups of
G
, and by
\operatorname{Cosets_{co}}(G)
the directed set of cosets of compact open subgroups:
\operatorname{Cosets_{co}}(G)=\{gK\mid K<_{co}G,g\in G\}=\{Kg\mid K<_{co}G,g\in G\}
. By van Dantzig theorem,
\operatorname{Sub_{co}}(G)
is a basis of neighborhood at the identity of

.

Unitary representations.

A unitary representation is a pair
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
consisting of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and a continuous homomorphism
\pi:G\to(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}),\operatorname{sot})
. We will mostly consider the left and right regular representations defined for
\xi\in L^{2}(G,\mu_{G})
,
g,x\in G
by

\displaystyle\lambda_{G}:G\to\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(G,\mu_{G}))\colon\quad\lambda_{G}(g)\xi(x)=\xi(g^{-1}x)

\displaystyle\rho_{G}:G\to\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(G,\mu_{G}))\colon\quad\rho_{G}(g)\xi(x)=\Delta_{G}(g)^{1/2}\xi(xg).

When the group is clear from the context we write
\lambda_{g}
and

for
\lambda_{G}(g)
and
\rho_{G}(g)
respectively.

Group von Neumann algebras.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. The
group von Neumann algebra
of
G
, denoted by
\mathcal{L}(G)
, is the von Neumann algebra
\lambda_{G}(G)^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G,\mu_{G}))
. By the von Neumann bicommutant theorem,

\mathcal{L}(G)=\overline{\vect\{\lambda_{g}\mid g\in G\}}^{\operatorname{sot}}=\overline{\vect\{\lambda_{g}\mid g\in G\}}^{\operatorname{wot}}.

Since
\lambda_{G}
and
\rho_{G}
are unitarily equivalent (
[
BHV08
, Proposition A.4.1]
),
\mathcal{L}(G)\simeq\rho_{G}(G)^{\prime\prime}
. We even have
\mathcal{L}(G)=\rho_{G}(G)^{\prime}
(
[
Tak03
, Proposition VII.3.1]
): while the inclusion
\mathcal{L}(G)\subset\rho_{G}(G)^{\prime}
is obvious, the reverse inclusion is a non-trivial result of modular theory.

Every group von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{L}(G)
is endowed with endowed its
Plancherel weight

\varphi_{G}
(
[
Tak03
, VII.3]
). This is a normal semifinite faithful weight, characterized by
\varphi_{G}(\lambda_{G}(f))=f(e)
for every continuous function
f
with compact support on
G
. When
G
is unimodular, this weight is moreover tracial, and it is finite if and only if
G
is discrete.

Given a closed subgroup
H\leq G
, the group von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{L}(H)
is isomorphic to the subalgebra
\lambda_{G}(H)^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathcal{L}(G)
(see
[

), and therefore we identify them. For example, the expression
\mathcal{L}(H)^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{L}(G)
denotes the algebra of elements of
\mathcal{L}(G)
that commutes with
\{\lambda_{G}(h)\mid h\in H\}
.

Given a compact open subgroup
K
of
G
, we identify
\ell^{2}(G/K)
with the Hilbert subspace

of

consisting of right
K

\ell^{2}(K\backslash G)
. We denote by

the
left averaging projection
associated to

, and it belongs to
\mathcal{L}(G)
. Given a pair
(G,K)
where
K
is a compact open subgroup of a locally compact group

, the
Hecke von Neumann algebra
of the pair is the von Neumann algebra
p_{K}\mathcal{L}(G)p_{K}
. By
[
Tza03
, Theorem 4.2]
, this definition coincides with the usual one, obtained as the appropriate closure of the algebra of compactly supported continuous

-biinvariant functions on

. By
[
LL09
, Theorem 4.5]
, the Hecke von Neumann algebra

is the von Neumann algebra associated to the

\lambda_{G/K}
, that is the von Neumann algebra
\lambda_{G/K}(G)^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(G/K))
. Factoriality result for Hecke von Neumann algebras can therefore be interpreted as factoriality results for quasi-regular representations.

Finally, we recall the following classical fact about averaging projections in totally disconnected group von Neumann algebra. For any
A\subset\operatorname{Cosets_{co}}(G)
,
\xi_{A}\doteq\1_{A}/{\mu_{G}(A)}^{1/2}
will denote the normalized indicator function of

in

.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a totally disconnected locally compact group. Then
(p_{K})_{K\in\operatorname{Sub_{co}}(G)}

\operatorname{sot}
-converges to the identity.

Proof.

Consider

\mathcal{E}\doteq\vect\{\xi_{gK}\mid gK\in\operatorname{Cosets_{co}}(G)\}\subset L^{2}(G,\mu_{G}).

Note that
\mathcal{E}
is dense in

because

is totally disconnected.
Then, given any

such that for every

strongly converges to the identity as the group

shrinks to

∎

1.2
Fourier Coefficients on Group von Neumann Algebras

In this section, we introduce an adapted notion of
Fourier coefficients
for locally compact groups von Neumann algebras. Let
G
be a locally compact group.

Definition 1.2
.

Given
x\in\mathcal{L}(G)
,
g\in G
and
K
a compact open subgroup of
G
, the
Fourier coefficient of
x
at the coset
gK
is the complex number

\widehat{x}(gK)\doteq\left\langle x\xi_{K},\xi_{gK}\right\rangle.

We therefore consider
\widehat{x}
as a map from
\operatorname{Cosets_{co}}(G)
to
\mathbb{C}
.

Let us establish some basic properties of these Fourier coefficients.
1.3
states their basic continuity properties,
1.4
describes their behavior under the replacement of
K
by a compact open subgroup
K^{\prime}<_{co}K
, while
1.5
and
1.6
establish their definiteness properties when

is totally disconnected.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{L}(G)
,
g\in G
and
K
be a compact open subgroup of
G
.

1.

\widehat{x}:\operatorname{Cosets_{co}}(G)\ni gK\mapsto\widehat{x}(gK)
is bounded by
\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}
.

2.

\mathcal{L}(G)\ni x\mapsto\widehat{x}(gK)\in\mathbb{C}
is linear and weakly continuous.

Proof.

The first item is a direct consequence of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. The second one is a consequence of the fact that the absolute value of the map
x\mapsto\widehat{x}(gK)
coincides with
\omega_{\xi_{K},\xi_{gK}}
which is one of the seminorms defining the weak operator topology.
∎

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{L}(G)
,
g\in G
and
K,K^{\prime}
be compact open subgroups of
G
such that
K^{\prime}\leq K
. Pick
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
in
gK
such that
gK=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}K^{\prime}
. Then

\widehat{x}(gK)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{x}(g_{i}K^{\prime}).

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in G
: we first prove the result for

.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{\lambda_{\gamma}}(g_{i}K^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{G}\xi_{\gamma K^{\prime}}(h)\xi_{g_{i}K^{\prime}}(h)d\mu_{G}(h)=\frac{1}{\mu_{G}(K^{\prime})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{G}(\gamma K^{\prime}\cap g_{i}K^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu_{G}(K^{\prime})}\mu_{G}(\gamma K^{\prime}\cap gK)=\frac{1}{\mu_{G}(K)}\mu_{G}(\gamma K\cap gK)=\widehat{\lambda_{\gamma}}(gK).

By linearity, the equality holds for any

. This space is
\operatorname{wot}
-dense in
\mathcal{L}(G)
; therefore, the
\operatorname{wot}
-continuity of Fourier coefficients conclude.
∎

Proposition 1.5
.

Let

and

\widehat{x}|_{G/K}=0\text{ if and only if }xp_{K}=0.

\widehat{x}(gK)=0

Let

such that

Conversely, assume that

is such that for every

. By sesquilinearity of the scalar product and density of

.

The second part of the proposition follows: it is clear that the Fourier coefficients of scalar multiples of

are zero except maybe in cosets containing the identity. Conversely, if for every

∎

Proposition 1.6
.

Assume that
G
is a totally disconnected locally compact group. Then, for any

\widehat{x}=0\text{ if and only if }x=0.

As a consequence,

is scalar if and only if for every compact open subgroup

of

and for every

,
\widehat{x}(gK)=0

Proof.

Assume that every Fourier coefficient of

is zero. It follows from

that for any

,

is zero.
On the other hand, by

,

\operatorname{sot}

is zero. The other direction is trivial.

For the second part, assume that

is such that for all

,
\widehat{x}(gK)=0
when

. The previous lemma shows that for all

\operatorname{sot}

∎

1.3
Factoriality Criterion

We can now establish a locally compact adapted version of the inequality used in the discrete case to show that infinite conjugacy class groups have factorial group von Neumann algebras. Given
G
a locally compact group,
K
a compact open subgroup of
G
and
H
a closed subgroup of
G
,
\mathcal{N}_{G}(K)\doteq\{g\in G\mid gKg^{-1}=K\}
is the normalizer of
K
in
G
and we set
\mathcal{N}_{H}(K)\doteq H\cap\mathcal{N}_{G}(K)
. The group
\mathcal{N}_{H}(K)
acts by conjugation on

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of a locally compact group
G
, and
z\in\mathcal{L}(H)^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{L}(G)
. If
H\leq\ker\Delta_{G}
, then for every
g\in G
and for every compact open subgroup
K
of
G
,

\left\|z\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\geq c_{H}(gK)\left\lvert\widehat{z}(gK)\right\rvert^{2},

where
c_{H}(gK)\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\cup\{\infty\}
is the cardinal of the orbit of
gK
for the conjugation action of
\mathcal{N}_{H}(K)
on
G/K
.

Proof.

Given
gK\in G/K
, denote by
\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)
the orbit of
gK
under the conjugation action of
\mathcal{N}_{H}(K)
on
G/K
.

The family
(\xi_{B})_{B\in\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)}\in L^{2}(G,\mu_{G})
is orthonormal. Given any
B\in\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)
, let
h_{B}\in\mathcal{N}_{H}(K)
such that
B=h_{B}(gK)h_{B}^{-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|z\right\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|z\xi_{K}\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq\sum_{B\in\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)}\left\lvert\left\langle z\xi_{K},\xi_{B}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)}\left\lvert\left\langle z\xi_{K},\xi_{h_{B}(gK)h_{B}^{-1}}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)}\left\lvert\left\langle z\xi_{K},\lambda_{h_{B}}\rho_{h_{B}}\xi_{gK}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)}\left\lvert\left\langle z\rho_{h_{B}^{-1}}\lambda_{h_{B}^{-1}}\xi_{K},\xi_{gK}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}

(because

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)}\left\lvert\left\langle z\xi_{h_{B}^{-1}Kh_{B}},\xi_{gK}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)}\left\lvert\left\langle z\xi_{K},\xi_{gK}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{H}(gK)\left\lvert\widehat{z}(gK)\right\rvert^{2}.

∎

1.7
implies the following factoriality criterion for Hecke von Neumann algebras.

Proof.

We first explain how to drop the unimodularity assumption that appears in

and

\Delta_{G}
give their modular functions. The same holds for

\mathcal{N}_{A}(K)^{0}=\mathcal{N}_{A\cap\ker\Delta_{G}}(K)

\left\lvert\mathcal{N}_{B}(K)^{0}/K\right\rvert\geq 2

Let

We claim that
c_{H}(gK)=\infty
. This follows from the following case analysis. If

,

,

then
\{(a_{1}b)^{n}(gK)(a_{1}b)^{-n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

under the conjugation action.

g_{1}\in B

g_{n}\in B
,

then

under the conjugation action.

g_{1}\in A

,

We now apply this criterion to groups acting on trees. Given any graph

, denote by

By Bass-Serre theory, every closed subgroup of

[

[

]
as an application.

Burger-Mozes groups.

Fix

and let
\mathcal{T}_{d}
be the

is a map

such that for every

is a bijection. Given

at the vertex

is the permutation

Given a subgroup

of

, the

is defined as

These groups do not depend on the choice of the coloring. They always act transitively on

denote the index

is an index two open subgroups of

. The groups

and

are discrete if and only if

for every

.

In the next corollary, we obtain factoriality for some Hecke von Neumann algebras of Burger-Mozes groups. In this situation, we are able to describe this von Neumann algebra quite explicitly, thanks to Dykema’s general results on amalgamated free products of multimatrix von Neumann algebra. We therefore introduce the so called
interpolated free group factors
.

Interpolated free group factors

Given
n\geq 2
, let
\mathbb{F}_{n}
denote the free group with
n
generators. As
\mathbb{F}_{n}
has infinite conjugacy classes,
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
is a factor called a
free group factor
. A continuous interpolation
(\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{t}))_{t>1}
of this sequence of factors has been independently constructed in
[
Răd94
]
and
[
Dyk94
]
under the name of
interpolated free group factors
:
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{t})
is isomorphic to any corner
pM_{k}(\mathbb{C})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{n})p
where
p
is a projection in
M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
with
\operatorname{Tr}(p)^{2}=\frac{n-1}{t-1}
,
\operatorname{Tr}
being the tensor product of the normalized traces on
M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
. These factors are non-amenable.

Corollary 1.14
.

Let
F\leq\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, and
K=\Fix_{\widetilde{U}(F)}(\{e\})\leq\widetilde{U}(F)
for a given
e\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{T}_{d})
. If
F\curvearrowright\{1,\dots,d\}
is transitive and
\left\lvert\{1,\dots,d\}^{\Fix_{F}(\{1\})}\right\rvert\geq 3
, then

p_{K}\mathcal{L}(\widetilde{U}(F))p_{K}
is a factor.
More precisely,
p_{K}\mathcal{L}(\widetilde{U}(F))p_{K}\simeq\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{t})
for some
t>0
.

Proof.

Let
v_{1},v_{2}
be the two endpoints of
e
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\operatorname{col}(e)=1
. Set
G=\widetilde{U}(F)
,
H=\Fix_{G}(v_{1})\simeq\Fix_{G}(v_{2})
,
K=\Fix_{G}(e)
and
F_{k}=\Fix_{F}(\{k\})
for every
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. By Bass-Serre theory,
G
is isomorphic to
H\ast_{K}H
.
Moreover,

\mathcal{N}_{H}(K)=\Stab_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{v_{0}}(\mathcal{T}_{d})^{K})),

so that

\mathcal{N}_{H}(K)/K\simeq\Stab_{F}(\{1,\dots,d\}^{F_{1}})/F_{1}.

By transitivity of
F
,
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{F_{1}}
if and only if
F_{k}=F_{1}
. Let
k,l\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{F_{1}}
, and
\sigma\in F
such that
\sigma(k)\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{F_{1}}
. Then
F_{\sigma(l)}=\sigma F_{l}\sigma^{-1}=\sigma F_{k}\sigma^{-1}=F_{\sigma(k)}=F_{1}
and thus
\sigma(l)\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{F_{1}}
. Therefore,
\sigma\in\Stab_{H}(\{1,\dots,d\}^{F_{1}})
if and only if
\sigma(1)\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{F_{1}}
. By transitivity of
F
,
\left\lvert\mathcal{N}_{H}(K)/K\right\rvert=\left\lvert\Stab_{H}(\{1,\dots,d\}^{F_{1}})/F_{1}\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert\{1,\dots,d\}^{F_{1}}\right\rvert\geq 3
, and therefore
p_{K}\mathcal{L}(\widetilde{U}(F))p_{K}
is a factor by
1.13
.

This factor is an interpolated free group factor due to Dykema’s abstract description of the amalgamated free product of multimatrix von Neumann algebras
[
Dyk95
, Theorem 5.1]
. As
H
and
K
are compact, their von Neumann algebras are precisely multimatrix algebras, by the Peter-Weyl theorem, and therefore

\mathcal{L}(\widetilde{U}(F))=\mathcal{L}(H\ast_{K}H)\simeq\mathcal{L}(H)\ast_{\mathcal{L}(K)}\mathcal{L}(H)=M_{0}\oplus M_{1}

where
M_{0}
is a finite amenable von Neumann algebra and
M_{1}
is a direct sum of interpolated free group factors. The factor
p_{K}\mathcal{L}(\widetilde{U}(F))p_{K}
being non-amenable, it must be a corner of an interpolated free group factor, i. e. an interpolated group factor itself.
∎

Application 2: HNN Extensions of Locally Compact Groups

In this section, we reformulate the ideas of

in the von Neumann algebraic setting rather than the

-algebraic one, using

[

Let
H
be a locally compact group, and let
K_{1}
and

be two open subgroups of
H

\theta:K_{1}\to K_{-1}
be a continuous and open isomorphism of locally compact groups. Then the group

\HNN(H,K_{1},K_{-1},\theta)\doteq\left\langle H,t\mid tkt^{-1}=\theta(k)\quad\forall k\in K_{1}\right\rangle

Let

be such an

if, whenever

admits such reduced forms, and the quantities

and

are well-defined, that is they do not depend on the choice of a reduced form
[
LS77
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Proposition 1.16
.

Let

be a locally compact
\HNN

.

Proof.

Let

Then

is clearly a subgroup of

centralizes

(therefore

, which implies the strong form of
(⋆

L

)
used in

. Let

. Consider a reduced form of

, and distinguish the following cases:

If

.

Let

such that

. If

. Set

. Then for any

,

and

. Moreover,

x^{N}gx^{-N}=x^{N-1}x_{-1}t^{-s}x_{1}t(t^{s-1}gt^{-(s-1)})t^{-1}x_{1}^{-1}t^{s}x_{-1}^{-1}x^{N-1}

If

.

With

are mutually disjoint and therefore

.

If

1.6
Application 3: Neretin Groups

Let
d,k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and denote by
\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
the rooted tree such that the root
v_{0}
has degree
k
, and the other vertices have degree
d
. For the rest of this section, we fix an embedding of this tree in the plane that induces an order on the descendants of any vertex of
\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
. Denote by
h:\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{T}_{d,k})\to\mathbb{N}
the
height
function that assigns its distance from the root to each vertex. Denote by
\mathcal{V}^{(n)}(\mathcal{T}_{d,k})
the set of vertices of height
n
. Given
v\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{T}_{d,k})
, we denote by
\mathcal{T}_{d,k}^{v}
the subtree of
\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
consisting of descendants of
v
, and by
\mathcal{T}_{d,k}^{\leq n}
the finite subtree of
\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
obtained by keeping only vertices (and edges between them) at distance at most
n
from the root. If
v\neq v_{0}
, then
\mathcal{T}_{d,k}^{v}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{T}_{d,d}
, the isomorphism being canonical once an embedding of each tree is fixed.

The boundary
\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
of the tree is the set of infinite geodesics (viewed as non-backtracking sequences of adjacent vertices) rooted in
v_{0}
. Given
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
, let
h(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\doteq\sup\{h(v)\mid v\in\xi\cap\xi^{\prime}\}
. We define the visual distance on
\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
by setting
\delta(\xi,\xi^{\prime})=d^{-h(\xi,\xi^{\prime})}
. With this distance,
\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
is a Cantor set of unit diameter. Given
v\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{T}_{d,k})
, let
B^{v}\subset\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
be the set of
\xi\in\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
such that
v\in\xi
. Note that
B^{v}
is the clopen ball of
\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
consisting of elements at distance at most
d^{-h(v)}
of any of its elements. Denote by
P^{(n)}=\{B^{v}\mid v\in\mathcal{V}^{(n)}(\mathcal{T}_{d,k})\}
the recovering of
\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k}
in such balls of radius
d^{-n}
.

We say that an element
\varphi\in\Homeo(\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k})
is a
local similarity
if there exists two partitions
\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}B^{v_{i}}
,
\partial\mathcal{T}_{d,k}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}B^{w_{i}}
, such that for every

,
\varphi|_{B^{v_{i}}}
is a similarity onto
B^{w_{i}}
(in symbols, for every
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in B^{v_{i}}
,
\varphi(\xi)
and
\varphi(\xi^{\prime})
both belong to
B^{w_{i}}
and
\delta(\varphi(\xi),\varphi(\xi^{\prime}))=d^{h(v_{i})-h(w_{i})}\delta(\xi,\xi^{\prime})
). A local similarity is a
local isometry
if
h(v_{i})=h(w_{i})
for every

.

Definition 1.18

(
[

]

The

\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in B^{w}
such that the distance between
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
is not preserved by
h^{-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\delta(hgh^{-1}\xi_{1},hgh^{-1}\xi_{2})=\delta(gh^{-1}\xi_{1},gh^{-1}\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha\delta(h^{-1}\xi_{1},h^{-1}\xi_{2})

\displaystyle\neq\alpha\delta(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}).

Therefore,
hgh^{-1}K_{d,k}^{(n)}\cap gK_{d,k}^{(n)}=\emptyset
so that
c_{\mathcal{O}_{d,k}}(gK_{d,k}^{(n)})\geq\left[H^{(w,n)}:L^{w}\right]
.

Note that
\mu(K_{d,k}^{(n)})=\left[K_{d,k}:K_{d,k}^{(n)}\right]^{-1}
: we therefore establish the estimates necessary to ensure that
\left(\left[H^{(w,n)}:L^{w}\right]\left[K_{d,k}:K_{d,k}^{(n)}\right]^{-2}\right)_{n\geq n_{0}}
tends to infinity.

•

Recall that
H^{(w,n)}/L^{w}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{d,d}^{(n-n_{0})}/K_{d,d}
. Therefore,

\left[H^{(w,n)}:L^{w}\right]=\left[\mathcal{O}_{d,d}^{(n-n_{0})}:K_{d,d}\right]=\frac{\left[\mathcal{O}_{d,d}^{(n-n_{0})}:K_{d,d}^{(n-n_{0})}\right]}{\left[K_{d,d}:K_{d,d}^{(n-n_{0})}\right]}.

•

\mathcal{O}_{d,k}^{(n)}/K_{d,k}^{(n)}
is the group of permutations of
\mathcal{V}^{(n)}(\mathcal{T}_{d,k})
. Therefore,
\left[\mathcal{O}_{d,k}^{(n)}:K_{d,k}^{(n)}\right]=\left\lvert V^{(n)}(\mathcal{T}_{d,k})\right\rvert!
, whence

\left[\mathcal{O}_{d,d}^{(n-n_{0})}:K_{d,d}^{(n-n_{0})}\right]=\left\lvert\mathcal{V}^{(n-n_{0})}(\mathcal{T}_{d,d})\right\rvert!=(d^{n-n_{0}})!\geq\left(\frac{d^{n-n_{0}}}{e}\right)^{d^{n-n_{0}}}.

•

K_{d,k}/K_{d,k}^{(n)}
acts faithfully on the finite tree
\mathcal{T}_{d,k}^{\leq n}
. Then

\left[K_{d,k}:K_{d,k}^{(n)}\right]\leq\left\lvert\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d,k}^{\leq n})\right\rvert=k!\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(d!)^{\left\lvert\mathcal{V}^{(i)}(\mathcal{T}_{d,k})\right\rvert}=k!(d!)^{k\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}d^{i}}\leq k!(d!)^{kd^{n}}.

Finally, for

,

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{O}_{d,k}}(gK_{d,k}^{(n)})\mu(K_{d,k}^{(n)})^{2}\geq\frac{\left[\mathcal{O}_{d,d}^{(n-n_{0})}:K_{d,d}^{(n-n_{0})}\right]}{\left[K_{d,d}:K_{d,d}^{(n-n_{0})}\right]}\left\lvert K_{d,k}/K_{d,k}^{(n)}\right\rvert^{-2}\geq\frac{\left(\frac{d^{{n-n_{0}}}}{e}\right)^{d^{n-n_{0}}}}{(d!)^{d^{n-n_{0}}}}\frac{1}{k!(d!)^{2kd^{n}}}\displaystyle\mathop{\overset{}{\longrightarrow}}_{n\rightarrow\infty}\infty.

Therefore, given any nontrivial group element
g

c_{\mathcal{O}_{d,k}}(gK_{d,k}^{(n)})\mu(K_{d,k}^{(n)})^{2}\to\infty
, and by
1.9

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O}_{d,k})^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{N}_{d,k})=\mathbb{C}
. By
1.11

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O}_{d,k})
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{N}_{d,k})

factors. As
\mathcal{O}_{d,k}
is amenable,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O}_{d,k})
is the hyperfinite
\II_{\infty}
factor.
∎

Preliminaries

Von Neumann algebras.

In this section, we will consider von Neumann algebras as abstract operator algebras. Consequently, given
M
a von Neumann algebra, we denote by
(M_{*},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{*})
its predual, by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{M}
the dual norm on
M
, and the

topology on

is the weak-

M=(M_{*})^{*}

\psi\in M^{+}_{*}
and

\left\|x\right\|_{\psi}\doteq\psi(x^{*}x)^{\frac{1}{2}}

is the topology generated by the seminorms
\{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\psi}\mid\psi\in M_{*}^{+}\}

Standard form.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra, and denote by
M^{\operatorname{op}}
its opposite von Neumann algebra, that is the von Neumann algebra with the same elements and adjoint operation as in
M
, but with the reversed product
x\cdot_{\operatorname{op}}y\doteq yx

[
Haa75
]

(M,L^{2}M,J,P)
the

of

, and by

the associated left regular representation. We define its right regular representation
\rho_{M}:M^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathcal{B}(L^{2}M)
by
\rho_{M}(x)=J\lambda_{M}(x^{*})J
, so that
J\lambda_{M}(M)J=\rho_{M}(M^{\operatorname{op}})=\lambda_{M}(M)^{\prime}
. For example, for
G
a locally compact group, the standard form of

is given by
(\mathcal{L}(G),L^{2}(G,\mu_{G}),J,P)
, where

J:L^{2}G\ni\xi\mapsto\left(h\mapsto\Delta_{G}(h^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{\xi(h^{-1})}\right)\in L^{2}G,

\lambda_{\mathcal{L}(G)}(u_{g})=\lambda_{g}

Automorphisms groups, actions, and unitary implementations.

The

M_{*}
by
\theta(\omega)=\omega\circ\theta

and

. It is endowed with the

M_{*}
: a net
(\theta_{i})_{i\in I}
converges to
\theta
if and only if for all
\omega\in M_{*}
,
\left\|\theta_{i}(\omega)-\theta(\omega)\right\|\to 0
.
Given

there exists a unique unitary

such that for all

\theta(x)=v_{\theta}xv_{\theta}^{*}

of

. Given

for the
u
-topology on
\Aut(M)
. Once the action is fixed, we denote by

Smoothing map.

Let
\alpha:G\to M
be a continuous action of a locally compact group on a von Neumann algebra and
K
be a compact open subgroup of

. By continuity of the action and given

, the application

\widetilde{\alpha_{K}}:M_{*}\ni\omega\mapsto\frac{1}{\mu_{G}(K)}\int_{K}\alpha_{k}(\omega)d\mu_{G}(k)\in(M_{*},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{*})

\alpha_{K}:M\ni x\mapsto(\omega\mapsto\widetilde{\alpha_{K}}(\omega)(x))\in(M_{*})^{*}=M.

By construction,
\alpha_{K}
is weakly continuous.

Fourier Coefficients on Crossed Products

In this section, we adapt the notion of Fourier coefficient on group von Neumann algebras to crossed products by locally compact groups. For any Borel subset
A\subset G
with non-zero finite Haar measure,
\xi_{A}\doteq\1_{A}/{\mu_{G}(A)}^{1/2}
still denote the normalized indicator function of
A
in
L^{2}(G,\mu_{G})
. Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
be a continuous action of a locally compact group on a von Neumann algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
x\in M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
,
K
be a compact open subgroup of
G
and
g\in G
. We define the
Fourier coefficient of
x
at the coset
gK
as the unique element
\widehat{x}(gK)
of
M
such that given
\xi,\eta\in L^{2}(M)
,

\left\langle\lambda_{M}(\widehat{x}(gK))\xi,\eta\right\rangle=\left\langle\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(x)\xi\otimes\xi_{K},(\1\otimes\lambda_{G})(g)\eta\otimes\xi_{K}\right\rangle.

We therefore consider
\widehat{x}
as a function from
\operatorname{Cosets_{co}}(G)
to
M
.

Proof that
\widehat{x}(gK)
is well defined.

It is clear that
\widehat{x}(gK)
can be defined as an operator in
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}M)
. However, we have to check that
\widehat{x}(gK)
indeed belongs to
\lambda_{M}(M)
and can therefore be defined as an element of
M
itself. Given
b\in M
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\widehat{x}(gK)\rho_{M}(b)\xi,\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(x)(\rho_{M}\otimes 1)(b)\xi\otimes\xi_{K},(\1\otimes\lambda_{G})(g)\eta\otimes\xi_{K}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle(\rho_{M}\otimes 1)(b)\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(x)\xi\otimes\xi_{K},(\1\otimes\lambda_{G})(g)\eta\otimes\xi_{K}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(x)\xi\otimes\xi_{K},(\rho_{M}\otimes 1)(b^{*})(\1\otimes\lambda_{G})(g)\eta\otimes\xi_{K}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\widehat{x}(gK)\xi,\rho_{M}(b^{*})\eta\right\rangle=\left\langle\rho_{M}(b)\widehat{x}(gK)\xi,\eta\right\rangle.

Therefore,
\widehat{x}(gK)
commutes with
\rho_{M}(M)
, and can be identified with the element of
M
characterized by

\left\langle\lambda_{M}(\widehat{x}(gK))\xi,\eta\right\rangle=\left\langle\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(x)\xi\otimes\xi_{K},(\1\otimes\lambda_{G})(g)\eta\otimes\xi_{K}\right\rangle.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

,
K
be a compact open subgroup of
G
and

. Then

1.

\widehat{x}(gK)
is
K
-invariant.

2.

Let

. Then
\widehat{a}(K)=\alpha_{K}(a)
, and if

, then
\widehat{a}(gK)=0
.

3.

\widehat{x}:\operatorname{Cosets_{co}}(G)\ni gK\mapsto\widehat{x}(gK)\in(M,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{M})

.

4.

M\rtimes_{\alpha}G\ni x\mapsto\widehat{x}(gK)\in M

Proof.

1.

The Fourier coefficient
\widehat{x}(gK)
is
K
-invariant as given

,

\displaystyle\left\langle\lambda_{M}(\alpha_{k^{-1}}(\widehat{x}(gK)))\xi,\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle v_{k}^{*}\lambda_{M}(\widehat{x}(gK)),v\right\rangle_{k}\xi{\eta}=\left\langle\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(x)v_{k}\xi\otimes\xi_{K},(\1\otimes\lambda_{G})(g)v_{k}\eta\otimes\xi_{K}\right\rangle

If
a\in M

\displaystyle=\left\langle\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(a)(\xi\otimes\xi_{K}),(\1\otimes\lambda_{G})(g)\eta\otimes\xi_{K}\right\rangle=\left\langle\lambda_{M}^{\alpha}(a)(\xi\otimes\xi_{K}),\eta\otimes\xi_{gK}\right\rangle

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
x\in M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
,
g\in G

be compact open subgroups of

such that

. Pick

in

Given

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that
G
is totally disconnected. Then, for any

\widehat{x}=0\text{ if and only if }x=0.

As a consequence,

of

Let
x\in M\rtimes_{\alpha}G

,

\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(x)(L^{2}M\otimes\xi_{hKh^{-1}})\subset(L^{2}M\otimes\xi_{g{hKh^{-1}}})^{\perp}

\rho^{\alpha}_{G}(h)
commutes with
\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(x)
and with
(\1\otimes\lambda_{G})(g)
. Then
\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(x)(L^{2}M\otimes\xi_{hK})\subset(L^{2}M\otimes\xi_{g{h^{-1}}K})^{\perp}

Let

. By density of

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright(M,\psi)
be a continuous action of a totally disconnected locally compact group on a von Neumann algebra
M
endowed with a faithful normal state
\psi
.
Let
H_{\psi}=\{h\in G\mid\psi\circ\alpha_{h}=\psi\}
be the subgroup of
\psi
-preserving elements. Then for any
H\leq H_{\psi}
and
z\in\mathcal{L}(H)^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
,

\displaystyle\left\|z\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\geq c_{H}(gK)\left\|\widehat{z}(gK)\right\|_{\psi}^{2}

for every
K<_{co}G
,
g\in G
.

Proof.

Let
A=gK
, and denote by
\xi_{\psi}\in L^{2}M
the unit vector such that for all
x\in M
,
\psi(x)=\left\langle x\xi_{\psi},\xi_{\psi}\right\rangle
.
Let
\eta_{\psi}\doteq\lambda_{M}(\widehat{z}(gK))\xi_{\psi}\in L^{2}M
. Then
\left\|\widehat{z}(gK)\right\|_{\psi}^{2}=\left\langle\lambda_{M\rtimes G}(x)\xi_{\psi}\otimes\xi_{K},\eta_{\psi}\otimes\xi_{A}\right\rangle
.
Given any
B\in\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)
, let
h_{B}\in\mathcal{N}_{H}(K)
such that
B=h_{B}Ah_{B}^{-1}
. Then
((\1\otimes\lambda_{G})(h_{B})\rho_{G}^{\alpha}(h_{B})\eta_{\psi}\otimes\xi_{A})_{B\in\mathcal{C}_{H}(gK)}
is a family of orthogonal vectors of norm at most one. Let
z
be as in the lemma:

\displaystyle\left\|z\right\|_{\infty}^{2}

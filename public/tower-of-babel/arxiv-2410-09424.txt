Abstract




Abstract.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which may be non-doubling and only satisfies
\mu(Q(x,l))\leq C_{0}l^{n}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
l(Q)>0
, with some fixed constants
C_{0}>0
and
n\in(0,d]
. We introduce a new type of
bmo(\mu)
space which looks bigger than the
rbmo(\mu)
space of Dachun Yang (JAMS, 2005). And its four equivalent norms are established by constructing some special types of auxiliary doubling cubes. Then we further obtain that this new
rbmo(\mu)
space actually coincides with the
rbmo(\mu)
space of Dachun Yang.

1
Introduction

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<n\leq d
. We assume that
\mu
is a positive Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying the
growth\ condition

\mu(Q(x,l))\leq C_{0}l^{n}\quad\mathrm{for\ all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ \mathrm{and}\ l>0\ \mathrm{with\ some\ fixed\ constant}\ C_{0}>0.

(1.1)

We don’t assume that
\mu
is doubling and the cube in the measure can also be replaced by a ball.

It is well known that the doubling condition on Radon measure plays an important role in most results of classical function spaces, Harmonic Analysis and so on. However, in 2002, it has been shown that many results in the classical Calderón-Zygmund theory and the classical

Hardy
and
BMO
spaces also hold without the doubling assumption and only need the measure to satisfy the growth condition in
[
7
,
9
]
.

In 1961, John and Nirenberg
[
4
]
introduced the
BMO
space. The space
BMO
shares similar properties with the space

L^{\infty}
, and it often serves as a substitute for it. For instance, classical singular integrals do not map
L^{\infty}
to
L^{\infty}
but
L^{\infty}
to
BMO
. And in many instances interpolation
between
L^{p}
and
BMO
works just as well between
L^{p}
and
L^{\infty}
. In 1979, Goldberg
[
1
]
introduced a local
BMO
-type space
bmo
which is the dual of the local
Hardy
space
h^{1}
.

Definition 1.1
.

(i)

[
1
, p.15]

A function
f\in L_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to be in the space
bmo(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if there exists a nonnegative constant
C_{a}
such that, for any cube
Q
with
l(Q)<1
,

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\bigg|f(y)-\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f(x)dx\bigg|dy\leq C_{a}

(1.2)

and that, for any cube
Q
with
l(Q)\in[1,\infty)
,

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|dy\leq C_{a}.

(1.3)

Then, we write
\|f\|_{bmo(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\inf C_{a}
, where the infimum is taken over all the constants
C_{a}
.

(ii)

[
9
, p.2]
If there exists some constant
C_{b}>0
such that (
1.2
) holds for
C_{b}
instead of
C_{a}
, (
1.3
) holds for any cube
Q
with
l(Q)=1
and
C_{b}
instead of
C_{a}
. Then, the best constants
C_{b}
is comparable to the
bmo(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm of
f
.

(iii)

If there exists some constant
C_{c}>0
and a fixed constant
k>1
such that (
1.2
) holds for
C_{c}
instead of
C_{a}
, (
1.3
) holds for any cube
Q
with
l(Q)\in[1,k]
and
C_{c}
instead of
C_{a}
. Then, by (i) and (ii), it is easy to check that the best constants
C_{c}
is comparable to the
bmo(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm of
f
.

From Definition
1.1
, it implies that the set of functions in
bmo(\mathbb{R}^{d})
doesn’t depend on the choice of the value range of
l(Q)
in (
1.3
).

In 2000, Mateu, Mattila, Nicolau and Orobitg
[
5
]
wanted to find a good substitute for the space
BMO
when
the underlying measure is non-doubling and hoped to prove some results via
BMO
-
H^{1}
interpolation, but in this respect they were unsuccessful. Then, in 2001, Tolsa
[
7
]
found a suitable substitute for the classical
BMO
space in this setting, which is denoted by
RBMO(\mu)
. This space is small enough to posses the properties
such as the John-Nirenberg inequality and big enough for Calderón-Zygmund operators which are bounded on
L^{2}(\mu)
to be also bounded from
L^{\infty}(\mu)
into
RBMO(\mu)
(see
[
3
, p.2]
). The
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes and the coefficients
K_{Q,R}
introduced by Tolsa play important roles for their
RBMO
theory. Precisely,

Definition 1.2
.

[
7
, p.6]

Let
0<n\leq d
,
\alpha>1
and
\beta>\alpha^{n}
. We say that some cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
(\alpha,\beta)
-
doubling
if
\mu(\alpha Q)\leq\beta\mu(Q)
.

In
[
7
, p.8]
, for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Tolsa introduced the smallest expansive concentric
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube of
Q
, denoted as
\widetilde{Q}:=\alpha^{m}Q
with some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and the existence of such cube is guaranteed by Proposition
2.2
(i) below.

Definition 1.3
.

[
7
, p.6]

Give two cubes
Q,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
Q\subset R
. We denote

K_{Q,R}:=1+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{Q,R}}\frac{\mu(2^{k}Q)}{l(2^{k}Q)^{n}},

where
N_{Q,R}
is the first integer
k
such that
l(2^{k}Q)\geq l(R)
(in case
R=\mathbb{R}^{d}\not=Q
, we set
K_{Q,R}=\infty
).

Definition 1.4
.

[
7
, p.8]

Let
0<n\leq d
,
1<\eta,\,\alpha<\infty
and
\beta>\alpha^{n}
. We say that
f\in L_{loc}(\mu)
is in
RBMO(\mu)
if there exists some constant
C_{1}\geq 0
such that, for any cube
Q
,

\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}\left|f(x)-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f\right|d\mu(x)\leq C_{1},

(1.4)

and for any two
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes
Q\subset R
,

|m_{Q}f-m_{R}f|\leq C_{1}K_{Q,R},

(1.5)

where
m_{Q}f
stands for the mean of
f
over
Q
with respect to
\mu
, i.e.,

m_{Q}f:=\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}f(x)d\mu(x).

The infimum constant
C_{1}
is the
RBMO(\mu)
norm of
f
, and denoted by
||f||_{RBMO(\mu)}
.

In 2005, Dachun Yang
[
8
]
considered the local versions of the space
RBMO(\mu)
, i.e.,
rbmo(\mu)
in the sense of Goldberg
[
1
]
by using ideas coming from
[
7
]
.

Definition 1.5
.

[
8
, Definition 2.1(ii)]

Let
1<\eta\leq\alpha<\infty
,
0<n\leq d
and
\beta\geq\alpha^{n}
. We say that
f\in L_{loc}(\mu)
is in
rbmo(\mu)
if there exists some constant
C_{2}\geq 0
such that, for any cube
Q
with
l(Q)\leq 1
,

\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}\left|f(x)-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f\right|d\mu(x)\leq C_{2},

(1.6)

and that, for any two
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes
Q\subset R
with
l(Q)\leq 1
,

|m_{Q}f-m_{R}f|\leq C_{2}K_{Q,R},

(1.7)

and that, for any cube
Q
with
l(Q)>1
,

\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}\left|f(x)\right|d\mu(x)\leq C_{2}.

(1.8)

We define the
rbmo(\mu)
norm of
f
by the infimum constant
C_{2}
and we denote this by
\|f\|_{rbmo(\mu)}
.

Furthermore, Yang
[
8
]
gave some basic properties including several equivalent definitions of this local space. By using these properties, he established the John-Nirenberg’s inequality for the functions in the
rbmo(\mu)
space and a
rbmo(\mu)
-
h^{1}(\mu)
interpolation of linear operators.

In
[
8
]
, the
rbmo(\mu)
space introduced by Yang is the non-doubling version of Definition
1.1
(i).
So, naturally, two questions come out that whether the non-doubling versions of (ii) and (iii) of Definition
1.1
are equivalent with
rbmo(\mu)
of Yang. In this article, we have made some progress for Definition
1.1
(iii) but still open for Definition
1.1
(ii). Precisely, for the definition of the
rbmo(\mu)
space, if we only replace the condition
l(Q)>1
of (
1.8
) to
1<l(Q)\leq k
, we still don’t know how to prove the equivalent of them. Alternatively, if the condition
l(Q)>1
of (
1.8
) is replaced by
\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}
, where

\mathscr{Q}:=\left\{Q:l(Q)\leq 1\right\},\ \mathscr{Q}_{ex}:=\left\{Q:l(Q^{\prime})\leq 1\right\},

Q^{\prime}
denotes the biggest contractive concentric
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube of a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,

with some

, and the existence of such

-doubling cube is guaranteed by Proposition

(ii).
Then the equivalence of this new
rbmo(\mu)
space and

space of Yang can be verified in this article. The new space is denoted as
rbmo^{1}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)
, which is a non-doubling version of Definition
1.1
(iii). Here we remark that
\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}=\left\{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}:1<l(Q)\leq\alpha\right\}
when
\mu
is doubling.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\eta\in(1,\infty)
and
\alpha\in[\eta,\infty)
,
\mathscr{Q}:=\left\{Q:l(Q)\leq 1\right\}
and
\mathscr{Q}_{ex}:=\left\{Q:l(Q^{\prime})\leq 1\right\}
. We say that

is in the

C_{3}\geq 0
such that, for any cube

\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}\left|f(x)-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f\right|d\mu(x)\leq C_{3},

(1.9)

and for any two
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes
Q\subset R
with
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
and

|m_{Q}f-m_{R}f|\leq C_{3}K_{Q,R},

(1.10)

and for any cube

\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}\left|f(x)\right|d\mu(x)\leq C_{3}.

(1.11)

We write
\|f\|_{\ast 1}=\inf C_{3}
, where the infimum is taken over all the constants
C_{3}
satisfying (
1.9
), (
1.10
) and (
1.11

From the definitions of

and

of Yang such as John-Nirenberg’s inequality (see

space and that the dual space of local Hardy space

Some results for
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes

In this section, we will prove the existence of the two special
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes
\widetilde{Q}
and
Q^{\prime}
for a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then some basic properties of
\mathscr{Q}_{ex}
and
\mathscr{Q}
will be given.
Let us begin with the Besicovich covering lemma, which is very useful and important in our context.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.1.1]
)
Let
E
be a bouned set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If, for every
x\in E
, there exists a closed cube
Q_{x}
centered at
x
, then it is possible to choose, from among the given cubes
\left\{Q_{x}\right\}_{x\in E}
, a subsequence
\left\{Q_{k}\right\}_{k}
(possibly finite) such that

(i)

E\subset\bigcup\limits_{k}Q_{k};

(ii)

no point of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is in more than
C_{d}
(a number that only depends on d) cubes of the sequences
\left\{Q_{k}\right\}_{k}
, namely, for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\sum_{k}\chi_{Q_{k}}(z)\leq C_{d}.

In Proposition
2.2
, we obtain that there exist a lot of big
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes by (i) and many small
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes by (ii). The proof of Proposition
2.2
is inspired by
[
9
, Proposition 1.2.2]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
0<n\leq d
,
\alpha>1
and
\beta>\alpha^{n}
.

(i)

For
\mu
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
c\in(0,\infty)
, there exists some
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube
Q
centered at
x
with
l(Q)=\alpha^{N}c
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

(ii)

If
\beta>\alpha^{d}
, for
\mu
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
c\in(0,\infty)
, there exists some
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube
Q
centered at
x
with
l(Q)=\alpha^{-N}c
and
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

To prove (i), we assume that (i) doesn’t hold true, i.e., there exist some
x_{0}\in{\rm{\,supp\,}}(\mu)
and positive constant
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all cubes
Q
centered at
x_{0}
with
l(Q)=\alpha^{N}c
and the integer
N\geq M
, we have
\mu(\alpha Q)>\beta\mu(Q)
.

Now let us take
Q_{0}
be such a cube with
\mu(Q_{0})>0
. By our assumption and the growth condition, we see that, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\beta^{k}\mu(Q_{0})<\beta^{k-1}\mu(\alpha Q_{0})<\cdots<\beta\mu(\alpha^{k-1}Q_{0})<\mu(\alpha^{k}Q_{0})\leq C_{0}\alpha^{kn}[l(Q_{0})]^{n},

which in turn implies that

\mu(Q_{0})<C_{0}\left(\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\beta}\right)^{k}[l(Q_{0})]^{n}.

Letting
k\to\infty
, we have
\mu(Q_{0})=0
, which contracts to
\mu(Q_{0})>0
. This implies that, for any
x_{0}\in{\rm{\,supp\,}}(\mu)
, there exists some
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube
Q
centered at
x_{0}
with
l(Q)=\alpha^{N}c
, i.e., (i) holds true.

Then we prove (ii). For any fixed
c>0
,
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
and
\beta\in(\alpha^{d},\infty)
, let

\displaystyle\Omega:=\left\{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\mathrm{there\ doesn^{\prime}t\ exist\ any\ (\alpha,\beta)-doubling\ cubes}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.{\mathrm{\ centered\ at}\ x\ \mathrm{whose\ side\ lengths\ are\ }\alpha^{-N}c\mathrm{\ for\ all}\ N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\right\}.

We show that
\mu(\Omega)=0
. For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let

\displaystyle\Omega_{m}:=\left\{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\mathrm{all\ cubes\ centered\ at}\ x\mathrm{\ whose\ side\ lengths\ are\ }\ \alpha^{-N}c}\right.

\displaystyle\left.{\mathrm{\ are\ not\ (\alpha,\beta)-doubing\ cubes}\mathrm{\ for\ all}\ N\geq m\mathrm{\ with}\ N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\right\}.

Observe that
\Omega=\cup^{\infty}_{m=1}\Omega_{m}
. It suffices to prove that
\mu(\Omega_{m})=0
for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Let us fix a cube
Q
with
l(Q)=\alpha^{-(m+1)}c
and denote by
Q^{n}_{x}
the cube centered at
x
whose side length is
\alpha^{-n}l(Q)
for any
x\in\Omega_{m}\cap Q
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. By the Besicovitch covering lemma (Lemma
2.1
), there exists a sequence of cubes,
\left\{Q^{n}_{k}\right\}_{k\in I_{n}}
, such that

\Omega_{m}\cap Q\subset\bigcup_{k\in I_{n}}Q^{n}_{k}.

Since the center of
Q^{n}_{k}
is in
\Omega
and
l(Q^{n}_{k})=\alpha^{-(m+n+1)}c
,
Q^{n}_{k}
is not a
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube for each
k
. Therefore, from this and the fact that
\alpha^{n}Q^{n}_{k}\subset 3Q
, it follows that

\mu(Q^{k}_{n})<\beta^{-1}\mu(\alpha Q^{k}_{n})<\dots<\beta^{-n}\mu(\alpha^{n}Q^{k}_{n})\leq\beta^{-n}\mu(3Q)

(2.1)

Then by
[
9
, (1.2.3)]
, we have
\#(I_{n})\lesssim\alpha^{nd}
. By this and (
2.1
), we obtain

\mu(\Omega_{m}\cap Q)\leq\sum_{k\in I_{n}}\mu(Q^{n}_{k})\lesssim\alpha^{nd}\beta^{-n}\mu(3Q).

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, we see that
\mu(\Omega_{m}\cap Q)=0
by
\beta>\alpha^{d}
.

Notice that, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{R}^{d}=\cup_{i}Q_{m,i}
, where
\left\{Q_{m,i}\right\}_{i}
are cubes with

l(Q_{m,i})=\alpha^{-(m+1)}c\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\ i.

Then we have

\mu(\Omega_{m})\leq\sum_{i}\mu(\Omega_{m}\cap Q_{m,i})=0.

This further implies that
\mu(\Omega)=0
.

Remark 2.3
.

Given a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can pick a cube-chain
\left\{Q_{N}:=\alpha^{N}Q\right\}_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}
. By Proposition

-doubling cubes in the cube-chain

(i)

For any

are two

-doubling cubes beside

\widetilde{Q}_{n}
is the smallest

-doubling cube with side length greater than

in the cube-chain,

is the biggest
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube with side length less than

in the cube-chain. So there are no doubling cubes between

and
\widetilde{Q}_{n}
in the cube-chain. And for any cube

and

including

.

(ii)

For any

, if

or
l(\widetilde{\alpha^{N}Q})>l(\widetilde{Q})
, then by (i), we have

.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
C>0

(i)

If

(ii)

.

(iii)

If

-doubling cube with

, then

\beta
,
\rho
and

.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\alpha>1

The definition of

(iii), and in this section, we will further introduce the other three non-doubling versions and prove the equivalence of them with
rbmo^{1}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)
, which are given by Theorems

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\eta\in(1,\infty)
,
\mathscr{Q}:=\left\{Q:l(Q)\leq 1\right\}
and
\mathscr{Q}_{ex}:=\left\{Q:l(Q^{\prime})\leq 1\right\}
. Suppose that for a given function
f\in L_{loc}(\mu)
, we say that
f
is in
rbmo^{2}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)
if there exist some constant
C_{4}\geq 0
and a collection of numbers
\left\{f_{Q}\right\}_{Q}
(that is, for each cube
Q
there exists
f_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}
) such that

\sup_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}\left|f(x)-f_{Q}\right|d\mu(x)\leq C_{4},

(3.1)

and that, for any two cubes
Q\subset R
with
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
and
R\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}
,

|f_{Q}-f_{R}|\leq C_{4}K_{Q,R},

(3.2)

and that, for any cube
R\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}
,

|f_{R}|\leq C_{4}.

(3.3)

Then, we write
\left\|f\right\|_{\ast 2}=\inf\ {C_{4}}
, where the infimum is taken over all the constants
C_{4}
and all the numbers
\left\{f_{Q}\right\}
satisfying (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
).

It is easily
checked that
\|f\|_{\ast 1}
and
\left\|f\right\|_{\ast 2}
are the norms in the space of functions modulo constants.

The
RBMO(\mu)
space was introduced by Tolsa and it was proved that the definition of
RBMO(\mu)
is independent of the choice of
\eta
(see
[

introduced by Dachun Yang is also independent of the choice of

(see

). Next, we will also verify that the definition of

is independent of the choice of
\eta
. Let us begin with two lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\rho>0
and
Q
be a fixed cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For any cube
Q_{x}
centered at
x\in Q\cap{\rm{\,supp\,}}(\mu)
, if
l(Q_{x})\leq\rho l(Q)
, then
Q_{x}\subset(\rho+1)Q
.

Proof.

Let
d=2
, the proof for other values of
d
is similar. Let
l_{x}(ab)
and
l_{y}(ab)
be the projection lengths of segment
ab
on the
x
-axis and
y
-axis,
x_{Q}
be the center of
Q
and
x
be the center of
Q_{x}
. Then we obtain

l_{x}(xx_{Q})\leq\frac{1}{2}l(Q)
,
l_{y}(xx_{Q})\leq\frac{1}{2}l(Q)
.

For any
x^{\prime}\in Q_{x}
, it is easy to find that

l_{x}(x^{\prime}x)\leq\frac{1}{2}l(Q_{x})\leq\frac{\rho}{2}l(Q),l_{y}(x^{\prime}x)\leq\frac{1}{2}l(Q_{x})\leq\frac{\rho}{2}l(Q).

So we have

l_{x}(x^{\prime}x_{Q})\leq l_{x}(x^{\prime}x)+l_{x}(xx_{Q})\leq\frac{1}{2}l(Q)+\frac{\rho}{2}l(Q)\leq\frac{1}{2}l((\rho+1)Q),

l_{y}(x^{\prime}x_{Q})\leq l_{y}(x^{\prime}x)+l_{x}(xx_{Q})\leq\frac{1}{2}l(Q)+\frac{\rho}{2}l(Q)\leq\frac{1}{2}l((\rho+1)Q),

which implies
x^{\prime}\in(\rho+1)Q
, i.e.,
Q_{x}\subset(\rho+1)Q
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f\in rbmo^{2}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)
and
\left\{f_{Q}\right\}_{Q}
be a collection of numbers satisfying (
3.1
),(
3.2
) and (
3.3
). If
Q
and
R
are cubes in
\mathscr{Q}_{ex}
such that
l(Q)\approx l(R)
,
\min(l(Q),l(R))\leq 1
and
x_{Q}\in R

(
x_{Q}
is the center of the cube Q
)
, then

|f_{Q}-f_{R}|\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

Proof.

Let us prove Lemma
3.3
by two cases.

Case 1. If
l(Q)\leq l(R)
, then we have
l(Q)\leq 1
and hence
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
. Because the center of
Q
is in
R
, then there exists a cube
S
such that
l(S)=\frac{1}{2}l(Q)
and
S\subset Q\cap R
. From this we can conclude that
l(S)\approx l(Q)\approx l(R)
,
S\subset Q
,
S\subset R
and
S\in\mathscr{Q}
. By (
3.2
) and Proposition
2.4
(ii), we have

\displaystyle|f_{Q}-f_{R}|\leq|f_{Q}-f_{S}|+|f_{S}-f_{R}|\leq(K_{S,Q}+K_{S,R})\|f\|_{\ast 2}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

Case 2. If
l(Q)>l(R)
, by
x_{Q}\in R
, we can find that
x_{R}\in Q
,
l(R)\leq 1
and
R\in\mathscr{Q}
. By this and using a similar estimate of (i), we further obtain
|f_{R}-f_{Q}|\leq\|f\|_{\ast 2}
.

∎

The following proposition indicates that
\|f\|_{\ast 2(\eta)}
is independent of

, the proof is a slight variation of

, Lemma 2.6]
with

replaced by Lemma

. The details are omitted.

Proposition 3.4
.

The norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\ast 2,(\eta)}
are independent of
\eta\in(1,\infty)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
\eta\in(1,\alpha]
. Then
rbmo^{1}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)=rbmo^{2}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)
with equivalent norms.

Proof.

To prove
\|f\|_{\ast 2}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 1}
for
f\in rbmo^{1}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)
, we set

f_{Q}:=\begin{cases}m_{\widetilde{Q}}f&\widetilde{Q}\in\mathscr{Q};\\
0&\widetilde{Q}\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}.\end{cases}

Let us prove this by three steps.

Step 1. Prove

\underset{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}{\sup}\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|d\mu(x)\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 1}.

(3.4)

If
\widetilde{Q}\in\mathscr{Q}
, obviously, (
3.4
) holds with
C=1
.

If
\widetilde{Q}\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}
, then by
\eta\leq\alpha
and that the cube
\widetilde{Q}
is
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|d\mu(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|d\mu(x)+\frac{\mu(Q)}{\mu(\eta Q)}|m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\ast 1}+\frac{\mu(\eta\widetilde{Q})}{\mu(\widetilde{Q})}\|f\|_{\ast 1}\leq\|f\|_{\ast 1}+\frac{\mu(\alpha\widetilde{Q})}{\mu(\widetilde{Q})}\|f\|_{\ast 1}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 1}.

which implies that (
3.4
) holds.

Step 2. Prove

|f_{Q}-f_{R}|\leq CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{\ast 1}

(3.5)

for any two cubes
Q\subset R
with
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
and
R\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}
. We prove this by six cases.

Case 1.
l(\widetilde{R})\geq l(\widetilde{Q})>1
. (
3.5
) is obviously true.

Case 2.
l(\widetilde{R})>1\geq l(\widetilde{Q})
. By (
1.10
), (
1.11
),
\eta\leq\alpha
, Remark
2.3
(i), (i) and (iii) of Proposition
2.4
and that cube
\widetilde{R}
is
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling, we obtain

\displaystyle|f_{Q}-f_{R}|=

\displaystyle|f_{Q}|=|m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|m_{\widetilde{Q}}f-m_{Q^{\prime}}f|+|m_{Q^{\prime}}f-m_{\widetilde{R}}f|+|m_{\widetilde{R}}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{Q^{\prime},\widetilde{Q}}\|f\|_{\ast 1}+K_{Q^{\prime},\widetilde{R}}\|f\|_{\ast 1}+\frac{\mu(\eta\widetilde{R})}{\mu(\widetilde{R})}\|f\|_{\ast 1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{\ast 1}+C(K_{Q^{\prime},Q}+K_{Q,R}+K_{R,\widetilde{R}})\|f\|_{\ast 1}+C\|f\|_{\ast 1}\leq CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{\ast 1},

which implies that (
3.5
) holds.

Case 3.
l(\widetilde{Q})\leq l(\widetilde{R})\leq 1
. By (
1.10
), Remark
2.3
(i), (i) and (iii) of Proposition
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle|f_{Q}-f_{R}|=

\displaystyle|m_{\widetilde{Q}}f-m_{\widetilde{R}}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|m_{\widetilde{Q}}f-m_{Q^{\prime}}f|+|m_{Q^{\prime}}f-m_{\widetilde{R}}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{Q^{\prime},\widetilde{Q}}\|f\|_{\ast 1}+K_{Q^{\prime},\widetilde{R}}\|f\|_{\ast 1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{\ast 1}+C(K_{Q^{\prime},Q}+K_{Q,R}+K_{R,\widetilde{R}})\|f\|_{\ast 1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{\ast 1}+CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{\ast 1}\leq CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{\ast 1},

which implies that (
3.5
) holds.

Case 4.
1\leq l(\widetilde{R})\leq l(\widetilde{Q})
. (
3.5
) is obviously true.

Case 5.
l(\widetilde{R})\leq 1<l(\widetilde{Q})
. This case is similar to Case 2, and its proof is omitted.

Case 6.
l(\widetilde{R})\leq l(\widetilde{Q})\leq 1
. This case is similar to Case 3, and its proof is omitted.

Thus (
3.5
) holds in all cases.

Step 3. Prove
|f_{R}|\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 1}
for any cube
R\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}
.

It is obviously true since
|f_{R}|=0\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 1}
with
R\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}
.

Combining Steps 1-3, we finally obtain that
\|f\|_{\ast 2}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 1}
.

Let us now prove
\|f\|_{\ast 1}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}
by three steps.

Step I. Prove

\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)|d\mu(x)\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}

(3.6)

for any cube
Q\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}
by two cases.

Case 1.
1<\eta<2
. For
\mu
-almost any
x\in Q
, by Proposition
2.2
(ii), there exists some
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube
Q_{x}
centered at
x
with side length
\alpha^{-k}(\eta-1)
, where
k
is chosen to be the smallest non-negative integer, i.e.,
Q_{x}
is the biggest
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube with
l(Q_{x})\leq\eta-1
. From the choice of
Q_{x}
, it’s not hard to see that
l(Q_{x})\leq\eta-1<l(\widetilde{\alpha Q_{x}})
, then
l(\alpha^{k}Q_{x})=\eta-1<l(\alpha^{k+1}Q_{x})
.

Let
n:=\log_{\alpha}{\frac{1}{\eta-1}}
. By
1<\eta<2
, we have
n>0
. We find that

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}l(\alpha^{k}Q_{x})\leq\alpha^{n}(\eta-1)<\alpha^{n}l(\alpha^{k+1}Q_{x})\Longleftrightarrow l(\alpha^{n+k}Q_{x})\leq 1<l(\alpha^{n+k+1}Q_{x}).

Then the length of the cube
\alpha^{\left\lceil n\right\rceil+k}Q_{x}
or
\alpha^{\left\lceil n\right\rceil+k+1}Q_{x}
is in the range of
(1,\alpha]
and we denote it by
Q_{vx}
. And by the definition of
\mathscr{Q}
and
\mathscr{Q}_{ex}
, we have

Q_{x}\in\mathscr{Q},Q_{vx}\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q},Q_{x}\subset\alpha^{k}Q_{x}\subset Q_{vx}.

(3.7)

Notice that
l(Q_{x})\leq\eta-1\leq(\eta-1)l(Q)
, then by Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

Q_{x}\subset\eta Q.

(3.8)

By the Besicovich covering lemma (Lemma
2.1
), we can find a family of points
\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i}\subset Q\cap{\rm{\,supp\,}}(\mu)
such that
Q\cap{\rm{\,supp\,}}(\mu)
is covered by a family of cubes
\left\{Q_{x_{i}}\right\}_{i}
with bounded overlap. From this, (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.7
) and Proposition
2.4
(i), it follows that

\displaystyle|f_{Q_{x_{i}}}|\leq

\displaystyle|f_{Q_{x_{i}}}-f_{Q_{vx_{i}}}|+|f_{Q_{vx_{i}}}|\leq K_{Q_{x_{i}},Q_{vx_{i}}}\|f\|_{\ast 2}+C\|f\|_{\ast 2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{Q_{x_{i}},\alpha^{k}Q_{x_{i}}}\|f\|_{\ast 2}+K_{\alpha^{k}Q_{x_{i}},Q_{vx_{i}}}\|f\|_{\ast 2}+C\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

The first item on the right hand side is bounded by
C\|f\|_{\ast 2}
due to the fact that there are no doubling cubes of the form
\alpha^{m}Q_{x_{i}}
between
Q_{x_{i}}
and
\alpha^{k}Q_{x_{i}}
, and hence
K_{Q_{x_{i}},\alpha^{k}Q_{x_{i}}}\leq C
. The second item is also bounded by
C\|f\|_{\ast 2}
, because
\alpha^{k}Q_{x_{i}}
and
Q_{vx_{i}}
have comparable sizes. So,

\displaystyle|f_{Q_{x_{i}}}|\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

(3.9)

Since
Q_{x_{i}}
is
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling,
\eta\leq\alpha
and (
3.8
), we also have

\sum_{i}\mu(\eta Q_{x_{i}})\leq\sum_{i}\mu(\alpha Q_{x_{i}})\leq\beta\sum_{i}\mu(Q_{x_{i}})\leq C\mu(\eta Q).

(3.10)

From (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)|d\mu(x)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\sum_{i}\int_{Q_{x_{i}}}|f(x)|d\mu(x)

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\sum_{i}\left[\int_{Q_{x_{i}}}|f(x)-f_{Q_{x_{i}}}|d\mu(x)+\mu(Q_{x_{i}})|f_{Q_{x_{i}}}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\left[\sum_{i}\mu(\eta Q_{x_{i}})\|f\|_{\ast 2}+\sum_{i}\mu(Q_{x_{i}})\|f\|_{\ast 2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\left[C\mu(\eta Q)\|f\|_{\ast 2}+C\mu(\eta Q)\|f\|_{\ast 2}\right]\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2},

which implies (
3.6
) holds.

Case 2.
\eta\geq 2
. For
\mu
-almost any
x\in Q\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}
, by Proposition
2.2
(ii), there exists some
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube
Q_{x}
centered at
x
with side length
\alpha^{-k}
, where
k
is chosen to be the smallest non-negative integer. From the choice of
Q_{x}
, it’s not hard to see that
l(Q_{x})\leq 1<l(\widetilde{\alpha Q_{x}})
, and
(\widetilde{\alpha Q_{x}})^{\prime}=Q_{x}
. Then,

Q_{x}\in\mathscr{Q},\ \widetilde{\alpha Q_{x}}\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q},\ Q_{x}\subset\widetilde{\alpha Q_{x}}\ \mathrm{and}\ Q_{x}\subset 2Q\subset\eta Q.

(3.12)

By the Besicovich covering lemma (Lemma
2.1
), we can find a family of points
\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i}\subset Q\cap{\rm{\,supp\,}}(\mu)
such that
Q\cap{\rm{\,supp\,}}(\mu)
is covered by a family of cubes
\left\{Q_{x_{i}}\right\}_{i}
with bounded overlap. From this, (
3.12
) and Proposition
2.4
, it follows that

\displaystyle|f_{Q_{x_{i}}}|\leq

\displaystyle|f_{Q_{x_{i}}}-f_{\widetilde{\alpha Q_{x_{i}}}}|+|f_{\widetilde{\alpha Q_{x_{i}}}}|\leq K_{Q_{x_{i}},\widetilde{\alpha Q_{x_{i}}}}\|f\|_{\ast 2}+C\|f\|_{\ast 2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(K_{Q_{x_{i}},\alpha Q_{x_{i}}}+K_{\alpha Q_{x_{i}},\widetilde{\alpha Q_{x_{i}}}})\|f\|_{\ast 2}+C\|f\|_{\ast 2}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

By this and repeating the estimate of (
3.11
), we obtain

\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)|d\mu(x)\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

Step II. Prove

\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|d\mu(x)\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}\qquad\mathrm{for\ any\ cube\ }Q\in\mathscr{Q}.

If
Q
is a
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube with
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
, by (
3.1
) and
\eta\leq\alpha
, we have

|f_{Q}-m_{Q}f|\leq\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|d\mu(x)\leq\|f\|_{\ast 2}\frac{\mu(\eta Q)}{\mu(Q)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

(3.13)

Thus for any cube
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
, if
\widetilde{Q}\in\mathscr{Q}
, by Proposition
2.4
(iii), (
3.2
) and (
3.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle|f_{Q}-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|\leq

\displaystyle|f_{Q}-f_{\widetilde{Q}}|+|f_{\widetilde{Q}}-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{Q,\widetilde{Q}}\|f\|_{\ast 2}+C\|f\|_{\ast 2}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2},

or if
\widetilde{Q}\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}
, by (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.6
) and
\eta\leq\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle|f_{Q}-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|\leq

\displaystyle|f_{Q}-f_{\widetilde{Q}}|+|f_{\widetilde{Q}}-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{Q,\widetilde{Q}}\|f\|_{\ast 2}+|f_{\widetilde{Q}}|+|m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{\ast 2}+C\|f\|_{\ast 2}+\frac{\mu(\eta\widetilde{Q})}{\mu(\widetilde{Q})}\|f\|_{\ast 2}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

Therefore, if
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|d\mu(x)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}(|f(x)-f_{Q}|+|f_{Q}-m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{\ast 2}+C\frac{\mu(Q)}{\mu(\eta Q)}\|f\|_{\ast 2}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

Step III. Prove

|m_{Q}f-m_{R}f|\leq CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{\ast 2}

for any two
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes
Q\subset R
with
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
and
R\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}
.

If
R\in\mathscr{Q}
, by (
3.13
) and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle|m_{Q}f-m_{R}f|\leq

\displaystyle|m_{Q}f-f_{Q}|+|f_{Q}-f_{R}|+|f_{R}-m_{R}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{\ast 2}+CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{\ast 2}+C\|f\|_{\ast 2}\leq CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

If
R\in\mathscr{Q}_{ex}\setminus\mathscr{Q}
, by (
3.13
), (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.6
) and
\eta\leq\alpha
, we obtain

\displaystyle|m_{Q}f-m_{R}f|\leq

\displaystyle|m_{Q}f-f_{Q}|+|f_{Q}-f_{R}|+|f_{R}-m_{R}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{\ast 2}+CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{\ast 2}+|f_{R}|+|m_{R}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{\ast 2}+CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{\ast 2}+\|f\|_{\ast 2}+C\frac{\mu(\eta R)}{\mu(R)}\|f\|_{\ast 2}\leq CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{\ast 2}.

Combining Steps I
-
III, we finally obtain that
\|f\|_{\ast 1}\leq C\|f\|_{\ast 2}
.

Therefore we finish the proof of Theorem
3.5
.
∎

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)|d\mu(x)+\frac{\mu(Q)}{\mu(\eta Q)}|m_{\widetilde{Q}}f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{rbmo(\mu)}.

Then let us prove that, for any two
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes
Q\subset R
,

|m_{Q}f-m_{R}f|\leq CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{rbmo(\mu)}.

If
l(Q)\leq 1
, it is obviously true.

If
l(Q)>1
, by (
1.8
),
K_{Q,R}\geq 1
and
\eta\leq\alpha
, we obtain

\displaystyle|m_{Q}f-m_{R}f|\leq

\displaystyle|m_{Q}f|+|m_{R}f|\leq m_{Q}|f|+m_{R}|f|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(\eta Q)}{\mu(Q)}\frac{1}{\mu(\eta Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)|d\mu(x)+\frac{\mu(\eta R)}{\mu(R)}\frac{1}{\mu(\eta R)}\int_{R}|f(x)|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{rbmo(\mu)}\leq CK_{Q,R}\|f\|_{rbmo(\mu)}.

From this and (
4.7
), it follows that
\|f\|_{RBMO(\mu)}\leq C\|f\|_{rbmo(\mu)}
for any
f\in rbmo(\mu)
and hence
rbmo(\mu)\subset RBMO(\mu)
.
∎

By Theorems
3.5
,
3.7
and
4.1
, we know that the four bmo-type spaces,
rbmo^{i}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)

(i=\mathrm{1},\mathrm{2},\mathrm{3},\mathrm{4})
, and
rbmo(\mu)
of Yang are equivalent with each other. Then the spaces
rbmo^{1}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)\textendash rbmo^{4}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)
inherit all the properties of
rbmo(\mu)
. Therefore, by
[
8
, Proposition 2.2]
and Theorem
4.3
, we have the following Proposition
4.4
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
i=\mathrm{1},\mathrm{2},\mathrm{3},\mathrm{4}
.

(i)

rbmo^{i}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\mu)
is a Banach space of functions (modulo

(ii)

Integrated inference of cellular compositions and gene expression programs by deconvolution
Authors: Ze Zhang, Xu Wang, Fan Hong, Pei Yu, Shengbao Suo, Ye-Guang Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWhile computational deconvolution is routinely used to estimate cell-type proportions from tissue mixtures, reconstructing cell-type-specific transcriptomes at single-sample resolution remains a fundamentally underdetermined algorithmic challenge. Consequently, accurate single-sample, gene-level inference is rarely achieved by existing tools. Here, we systematically benchmarked multiple deconvolution approaches across diverse biological contexts using both pseudo-bulk mixtures and real bulk RNA-seq datasets derived from multiple tissues. Evaluating the critical computational limitations in these models, we developed BayesPrism-DWLS, a framework that enables integrated inference of cell-type proportions and cell-type-specific expression at single-sample resolution. Applied to mouse colon bulk RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics, BayesPrism-DWLS revealed cell-type-specific genes and pathways that were undetectable at the bulk or spot level. Therefore, this framework provides a robust, high-resolution tool for dissecting cell heterogeneity and supports mechanistic studies informed by cell-type-specific transcriptional programs using cost-effective sequencing data.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13619-026-00299-5.Keywords: Bulk RNA-seq, Spatial transcriptomics, Cell-type deconvolution, Gene expression deconvolution, BayesPrism-DWLS

Background

Tissues comprise diverse cell types that maintain normal physiological function, with individual cells exhibiting distinct states and gene expression profiles. Consequently, the transcriptomic signal measured in a bulk tissue sample represents a composite of cell-type-specific expression weighted by their relative abundances (Avila Cobos et al. 2018; Mohammadi et al. 2016). These cellular compositions are highly dynamic, modulated by developmental stage, local microenvironment, and external experimental conditions (Galluzzi et al. 2018; Glastonbury et al. 2019; Pelvig et al. 2008). Alteration of cellular composition contributes to numerous pathological contexts, including cancer (Roerink et al. 2018), inflammation (Muema et al. 2020), and neurodegeneration (Malm et al. 2015). Therefore, accurate characterization of tissue cell composition is essential for understanding disease mechanisms and guiding precision therapies.

Current experimental approaches face limitations in resolving cell heterogeneity. While conventional bulk RNA sequencing (bulk RNA-seq) effectively captures expressed genes, it only reports average expression levels (Wilks et al. 2021), fundamentally lacking cell-type resolution. Emerging spatial transcriptomics technologies, such as 10 × Visium (Ståhl et al. 2016) and Slide-seq (Rodriques et al. 2019), retain spatial information but generally fail to achieve single-cell resolution. Conversely, techniques such as flow cytometry (De Matos et al. 2010) and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) (Denisenko et al. 2020) provide high-resolution cellular profiling, but they are hindered by high cost, technical variability, high dropout rates (limited transcript capture efficiency), and scalability challenges across large sample cohorts.

To address these limitations, computational cell-type deconvolution methods have emerged as a powerful alternative (Avila Cobos et al. 2018). These approaches leverage multi-omics data—including transcriptomics, DNA methylation (Salas et al. 2018), and chromatin accessibility (Li et al. 2020)—to infer both the proportions of constituent cell types and their specific expression patterns from heterogeneous tissue mixtures. Among these modalities, transcriptomic data, which directly reflect cellular functional states, are highly accessible and the most widely used, benefiting from standardized processing pipelines. Deconvolution algorithms generally fall into two categories: reference-based methods, which estimate cell-type proportions using purified or single-cell expression profiles as priors via linear regression, support vector regression (Newman et al. 2015), or Bayesian modeling (Chu et al. 2022); and reference-free methods, which typically rely on unsupervised matrix factorization (Tang et al. 2020) to decompose bulk expression signal.

Beyond estimating cell-type fractions, advanced deconvolution methods can be extended to infer cell-type-specific expression profiles at single-sample resolution. This gene-level deconvolution provides a more refined view of localized transcriptional activity, overcoming the inherent averaging effect of conventional bulk assays and enabling the dissection of cell-type-specific functional contributions (Jaakkola and Elo 2021). Consequently, it facilitates sophisticated downstream analyses, including cell-type-specific differential expression analysis, functional annotation, and mechanistic investigation.

While multiple benchmarking studies have recently benchmarked cell-type deconvolution methods, significant gaps remain. Previous evaluations have primarily focused on deconvolution accuracy within specific cancer types or tumor immune microenvironments (Tran et al. 2023), or assessed the impact of technical and biological variables—such as data transformation, normalization, and compositional variance—on algorithmic performance (Avila Cobos et al. 2020). However, most existing methods were originally optimized for blood-derived or immune-enriched tissues (Aran et al. 2017; Sturm et al. 2019), and their generalizability to other solid tissues remains insufficiently validated. Furthermore, while the estimation of cell-type proportion has reached relative maturity, methods capable of accurately recovering cell-type-specific gene expression profiles at the individual-sample level remain limited. These limitations restrict their utility in highly heterogeneous tissues, particularly in complex tumors and spatially resolved transcriptomics data.

To bridge these gaps, we conducted a comprehensive benchmarking and experimental evaluation of 25 cell-type proportion deconvolution methods and seven gene expression deconvolution algorithms across multiple tissue types, spanning a diverse spectrum of modeling strategies. Leveraging insights from this systematic assessment, we formulated BayesPrism-DWLS, an integrated framework capable of high-fidelity inference of both cell-type proportions and cell-type-specific expression profiles at single-sample resolution. Application of this framework to bulk RNA-seq datasets from multiple tissues revealed cell-type-specific transcriptional programs that are not detectable from bulk measurements alone. Furthermore, applying BayesPrism-DWLS to dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis 10 × Visium spatial transcriptomics data enabled the identification of cell-type-specific differentially expressed genes and pathways that were obscured at the spot level. This approach provides deeper, biologically interpretable insights into the cellular mechanisms underlying tissue heterogeneity and disease progression.

Results

Multi-tissue deconvolution benchmarking framework

To systematically evaluate cell-type proportion and gene expression deconvolution methods, we established a comprehensive and multi-tissue benchmarking framework spanning multiple tissues and diverse cellular contexts (Fig. 1). This framework integrates high-quality, publicly available scRNA-seq datasets with matched or independent bulk RNA-seq datasets from gastric, colon, small intestine, lung, and liver tissues. Within each single cell dataset, cells were annotated into 10–17 major cell types using canonical marker genes (Fig. S1), ensuring that the evaluation captured a representative biological diversity.

Performance was evaluated using Pearson correlation, RMSE, cosine similarity, and Bray–Curtis dissimilarity, together with additional experimental validation. Based on these results, the BayesPrism-DWLS framework was developed to infer cell-type proportions and cell-type-specific gene expression from bulk RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics data. To enable a comprehensive comparison of deconvolution methods, we evaluated method performance not only on simulated mixtures with known ground truth but also on independent bulk RNA-seq datasets. For each single cell dataset, we simulated 1,000 pseudo-bulk mixtures by randomly sampling and aggregating single cells, producing highly heterogeneous compositions with cell-type proportions ranging from 1% to 95%. Among these, 100 randomly selected mixtures exhibited distinct cellular compositions, closely approximating the complexity of real tissues (Fig. S2A). To construct the reference matrices required for deconvolution, cell-type signature reference expression profiles were constructed by averaging gene expression across cells of the same type and selecting cell-type-specific marker genes. These profiles were subsequently used as reference matrices for cell-type deconvolution analyses (Fig. S2B). Using this framework, we benchmarked 25 cell-type and seven gene expression deconvolution methods on both pseudo-bulk and real bulk RNA-seq datasets. Cell-type proportion deconvolution methods were broadly categorized into two groups. The first group relies on predefined cell-type signature references and includes classical approaches such as ordinary least squares (OLS) and non-negative least squares (NNLS) and related regression-based methods. The second group leverages single cell references, including methods such as the semi-supervised approach BisqueRNA (Jew et al. 2020) and Bayesian generative models such as Bayesian cell proportion reconstruction inferred using statistical marginalization (BayesPrism) (Chu et al. 2022).

Deconvolution performance was evaluated separately for cell-type proportion estimation and gene expression inference. For pseudo-bulk datasets with known ground truth, predicted cell-type proportions were compared with true proportions using the Pearson correlation coefficient (R), root mean square error (RMSE), and Bray–Curtis dissimilarity. For gene expression deconvolution, predicted cell-type-specific expression profiles were evaluated using the Pearson correlation coefficient and cosine similarity. RMSE and Bray–Curtis quantify absolute compositional deviations, whereas cosine similarity evaluates high-dimensional directional concordance between predicted and true expression vectors. For real bulk RNA-seq datasets, where ground-truth cellular compositions are not directly available, inferred changes in cell-type proportions were experimentally validated using immunofluorescence staining.

While existing deconvolution methods have achieved high accuracy in specialized tasks, they are typically limited to single-task optimization, failing to resolve cell-type proportions and specific gene expression profiles concurrently. This computational decoupling precludes a holistic understanding of tissue heterogeneity. To bridge these complementary limitations, we developed BayesPrism-DWLS as an integrated framework in which DWLS-derived cell-type proportions guide BayesPrism-based inference of cell-type-specific gene expression, thereby enabling the recovery of cell-type-specific transcriptional programs at single-sample resolution. Importantly, this integration extends applicability to both bulk RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics data, providing a unified and high-resolution framework for dissecting complex tissue composition and transcriptional heterogeneity.

Benchmarking of cell-type deconvolution methods on simulated data

Using gastric tissue as a representative example, scRNA-seq data were annotated into 14 major cell types, and deconvolution performance was assessed across all mixtures and cell types (Fig. 2A). DWLS (dampened weighted least squares) (Tsoucas et al. 2019) achieved the highest fidelity with the ground truth, with near-perfect Pearson correlation coefficient (0.996) and minimal RMSE (0.01). Other methods, including FARDEEP (fast and robust deconvolution of expression profiles) (Hao et al. 2019), RLR (robust linear regression), EpiDISH (epigenetic dissection of intra-sample heterogeneity) (Teschendorff et al. 2017), SCDC (Dong et al. 2021), and MuSiC (multi-subject single cell deconvolution) (Wang et al. 2019), performed slightly worse, with Pearson correlations coefficients around 0.99 and RMSE values close to 0.02.

To further quantify absolute compositional deviations between predicted and true cell-type proportions, we applied Bray–Curtis dissimilarity, which captures abundance-weighted divergence rather than merely linear concordance. This metric is particularly informative for evaluating performance in heterogeneous tissues containing rare populations. Across all pseudo-bulk mixtures, DWLS, FARDEEP, RLR, EpiDISH, and MuSiC consistently exhibited lower Bray–Curtis distances compared with other methods (Fig. 2B). Notably, DWLS achieved the smallest overall deviations across mixtures, indicating superior compositional accuracy. At the cell-type level, T cells, B cells, and Tuft cells were generally more difficult to resolve, likely due to limited transcriptomic distinctiveness or lower capture efficiency in the reference data. Nevertheless, DWLS maintained comparatively smaller deviations (< 0.25) for these challenging populations, further demonstrating its robustness across diverse cellular contexts.

Accurate detection of cell types is critical for reliable interpretation of tissue composition. We therefore evaluated false positive and false negative predictions using a 1% proportion threshold. False positives were defined as predicting a cell type when the true proportion was below 1% and were further stratified by predicted proportion (1% − 10%, 10% − 50%, and > 50%) to assess the robustness of deconvolution methods across different proportion ranges. In contrast, false negative was defined as failing to detect a cell type present above this threshold (Fig. 2E). With 1,000 simulated mixtures and 14 cell types, we evaluated 14,000 mixture–cell type prediction instances, treating each instance as an independent unit for calculating false positive and false negative rates.

To assess false positives, we analyzed 9,497 instances in which the true proportion was < 1% (Fig. 2C). DWLS produced the lowest false positive rate (1.3%), outperforming FARDEEP (7.3%), RLR (12%), EpiDISH (12%), SCDC (6%), and MuSiC (6%). CDSeq (complete deconvolution for sequencing data) (Kang et al. 2021) exhibited a high false positive rate (97.5%), with most predictions falling between 1%–10%.

False negatives were evaluated using the remaining 4,503 instances with ≥ 1% true proportion (Fig. 2D). DebCAM (deconvolution by convex analysis of mixtures) (Chen et al. 2020) had the lowest false negative rate (0.05%) but a high false positive rate (72.5%). DWLS had the second-lowest false negative rate (1.7%), followed by FARDEEP (2.7%), RLR (3.6%), EpiDISH (3.6%), MuSiC (7.6%), and SCDC (8.8%). BayesPrism had relatively balanced false positive (3.5%) and false negative (8.0%) rates but a low overall accuracy (Pearson correlation = 0.84). DeconvSeq (deconvolution of RNA-seq data) (Du et al. 2019) maintained a low false positive rate (5.2%) but exhibited a marked lack of sensitivity, failing to detect most present cell types (false negative rate 75.8%).

Overall, using a 1% detection threshold, prediction accuracy was defined as the proportion of correctly classified cases, including both true positives and true negatives. DWLS achieved the highest prediction accuracy (98.9%), outperforming all other evaluated methods (Fig. S2C). To assess robustness across a broader range of cell-type abundances, we repeated this analysis across detection thresholds spanning 0.1% to 100%. DWLS consistently displayed superior accuracy, particularly at low thresholds corresponding to rare cell types, highlighting its capacity to reliably detect low-abundance populations while effectively avoiding spurious identification of absent cell types (Fig. 2F).

Benchmarking of cell-type deconvolution methods on real bulk data

As DWLS consistently demonstrated the strongest quantitative performance, we therefore applied DWLS to real bulk RNA-seq datasets to evaluate its performance under biologically realistic conditions. Specifically, we analyzed bulk RNA-seq data (Hong et al. 2025) from gastric epithelium–specific ALK3 mutant mice and matched controls. DWLS-based deconvolution revealed biologically consistent compositional patterns in different gastric regions. Compared with the antrum, the corpus exhibited a markedly higher proportion of HCl-secreting parietal cells and a lower proportion of mucus-secreting pit cells (Fig. 3A), reflecting the distinct functional roles of these regions: the corpus is primarily responsible for gastric acid secretion, whereas the antrum contributes to hormone release and mechanical processing of food (Hong et al. 2025).

In the corpus of ALK3 mutant mice, basal/neck, chief, and proliferating cells were increased, while parietal and pit cells were reduced (Fig. 3B). Loss of ALK3, a BMP receptor, disrupted BMP-mediated maintenance of parietal and pit cell differentiation, altering the composition of epithelial cells. These results demonstrate that DWLS predictions quantitatively capture the biologically relevant changes induced by ALK3 deficiency in the gastric corpus.

To validate these predictions, we performed immunofluorescence staining using markers and detected five gastric epithelial cell types with well-identified markers (Fig. 3C). Manual cell counting showed strong concordance between experimentally measured trends and DWLS-based estimates (Fig. 3D). DWLS accurately quantified abundant cell populations, while discrepancies were observed for rare cell types; for example, proliferating cells increased from 10 to 20% based on DWLS estimates, compared with an increase from 7.5% to 17.6% measured by immunofluorescence. Notably, among all evaluated methods, only DWLS consistently recovered the direction of proportional changes across all five epithelial cell types (Fig. S3).

A pronounced discrepancy was observed in gastric corpus tissue when comparing scRNA-seq–derived cell compositions with experimentally measured cell proportions from immunofluorescence staining. In scRNA-seq data, parietal cells accounted for only ~4.5% of epithelial cells (Fig. 3E), whereas histological studies indicate that this population can constitute up to ~60% of the gastric corpus epithelium. In contrast, DWLS-based deconvolution of bulk RNA-seq data yielded substantially higher and more biologically plausible estimates of parietal cell abundance. This observation suggests that bulk transcriptomic deconvolution can partially compensate for intrinsic technical artifacts of scRNA-seq, such as dissociation inefficiency and selective cell loss (Bakken et al. 2018; Wen et al. 2022), and better reflect tissue-level cellular composition. We further evaluated DWLS performance in SMAD4 knockout mice, where it consistently identified increased proliferating cells and reduced parietal and pit cells in both the antrum and corpus (Fig. 3F). These trends were also supported by immunofluorescence staining (Fig. 3G-H).

To further evaluate robustness across diverse biological contexts, we applied DWLS to multiple independent bulk RNA-seq datasets encompassing gastric, intestinal, and liver tissues, including inflammatory, tumor, and organoid models. In a dataset generated from wild type mice and Tristetraprolin overexpression (TTPΔARE) mice following adrenalectomy (ADX) (Busada et al. 2021), a gastritis mouse model, DWLS predicted a decrease in the parietal cell proportion and increased macrophage infiltration and pit cell expansion in WT mice after ADX, compared with sham-operated WT controls. In TTPΔARE mice under the ADX condition, DWLS indicated restoration of parietal cells along with reduced macrophage infiltration and pit cell expansion (Fig. 3I), consistent with the known anti-inflammatory and tissue-restorative functions of TTP (Busada et al. 2021). By contrast, other deconvolution methods failed to reliably capture these directional compositional changes (Fig. S4). In a gastric tumor cohort (Leibold et al. 2024), tumor samples exhibited elevated proportions of fibroblasts, proliferating epithelial cells, and immune populations, accompanied by a marked reduction in differentiated epithelial cells (Fig. 3J), reflecting stromal expansion and immune infiltration characteristic of tumor progression (Leibold et al. 2024). In an organoid culture model (Kim et al. 2022b), DWLS revealed highly similar cellular compositions between organoids grown in hydrogel and those cultured in Matrigel (Fig. 3K), indicating stable maintenance of epithelial lineage structure across culture conditions. In the small intestinal epithelium (Cosovanu et al. 2022), DWLS predicted a substantial reduction in enterocytes and Paneth cells, coupled with the expansion of stem cells, transit-amplifying cells, and goblet cells in c-Maf–deficient mice (MafΔIEC) compared to controls (Fig. S5A). These shifts are consistent with the critical role of c-Maf in enterocyte differentiation and maturation (Cosovanu et al. 2022), highlighting impaired epithelial homeostasis following its deletion. In a hepatocyte-to-biliary reprogramming model (Katsuda et al. 2023), DWLS captured a progressive decrease in hepatocyte proportions alongside a marked increase in cholangiocyte-like cells as reprogramming advanced (Fig. S5B), reflecting hepatocyte plasticity and biliary lineage conversion (Katsuda et al. 2023).

Collectively, these results demonstrate that DWLS robustly and accurately captures cellular compositional changes in genetically perturbed tissues, diverse disease states, and in vitro organoid systems, indicating its broad applicability for dissecting tissue heterogeneity.

DWLS-enhanced deconvolution improves cell-type-specific gene expression inference

Inferring gene expression at the cell-type level enables dissection of lineage-specific transcriptional activity beyond averaged bulk signals to support more refined functional and mechanistic analyses. Having established that DWLS is able to reliably assess cell-type proportions, we next sought to determine whether the DWLS deconvolution framework could more precisely resolve cell-type-specific gene expression programs from bulk transcriptomic data.

To quantify concordance between predicted and ground-truth cell-type-specific expression, we employed cosine similarity, which measures agreement in high-dimensional expression patterns while remaining insensitive to differences in absolute magnitude. Among 14 gastric epithelial and stromal cell types, BayesPrism, which incorporates scRNA-seq data as prior information within a Bayesian generative framework to jointly infer cell-type proportions and cell-type-specific gene expression profiles (Chu et al. 2022), achieved the highest overall cosine similarity under its default joint inference setting (Fig. 4A), with a mean value of approximately 0.5. However, performance declined markedly for rare populations, including B cells, T cells, and tuft cells. CellR (Doostparast Torshizi et al. 2021) ranked second in overall prediction accuracy. Because proportion estimation and gene expression inference are tightly coupled, errors in proportion estimates–especially for low-abundance cell types–can propagate to gene-level predictions, underscoring the need for further methodological refinement.

To improve gene expression prediction accuracy, we incorporated the highly accurate cell-type proportion estimates derived from DWLS into BayesPrism (BayesPrism-DWLS). In this implementation, DWLS-derived proportions were supplied as fixed inputs, decoupling proportion estimation from gene expression inference. For systematic comparison, the same DWLS-derived proportions were also provided to other gene expression deconvolution methods, generating CellR-DWLS, bMIND-DWLS, and swCAM-DWLS variants. In contrast, TAPE (tissue-adaptive autoencoder) (Chen et al. 2022b), ENIGMA (deconvolution based on regularized matrix completion) (Wang et al. 2023), and CDSeq do not support externally supplied proportion constraints, as their model architectures intrinsically couple proportion estimation with gene expression inference, and therefore could not be modified in this manner. Among all cell types, BayesPrism-DWLS consistently outperformed all other methods, demonstrating a substantial improvement over the original BayesPrism model (Fig. 4A). The mean cosine similarity increased from approximately 0.5 to ~0.75. BayesPrism-DWLS also generated predicted expression profiles that closely matched the ground truth, with Pearson correlation coefficients exceeding 0.95 for most cell types (Fig. 4B). Notably, low-abundance populations were accurately recovered, including B cells, T cells, and tuft cells, highlighting the capacity of BayesPrism-DWLS to robustly resolve cell-type-specific gene expression at single-sample resolution. The CellR-DWLS variant also exhibited marked improvement, with an approximately twofold increase in mean cosine similarity, reaching ~0.5. These findings indicate that more accurate cell-type proportion estimates significantly enhance the performance of gene expression deconvolution. In contrast, integration of Bayesian method (bMIND) (Wang et al. 2021a) and swCAM (sample-wise convex analysis of mixtures) (Chen et al. 2022a) with DWLS showed minimal improvements over their original implementations. This could be due to low sensitivity of these methods to externally supplied proportion constraints: bMIND employs a multi-sample Bayesian hierarchical framework that primarily leverages cross-sample covariance structure for gene-level inference, whereas swCAM is based on matrix factorization–driven decomposition.

We then validated BayesPrism-DWLS using bulk RNA-seq data (Zheng et al. 2024) from a DSS-induced mouse colitis model, with matched scRNA-seq data (Zheng et al. 2024) serving as an independent reference. DSS treatment was associated with reduced proportions of enterocytes, goblet cells, and stem cells, together with increased fibroblasts and macrophages (Fig. S6A-D), consistent with epithelial injury and inflammatory cell infiltration characteristic of DSS-induced intestinal inflammation (Zheng et al. 2024). By analyzing the bulk RNA-seq data across multiple time points, we identified the top 50 cell-type-specific differentially expressed genes (Fig. 4C), many of which showed similar enrichment patterns in the corresponding cell types in the scRNA-seq data (Fig. 4D). Notably, Ffar4 was upregulated in goblet cells after two days of DSS treatment (Fig. 4E), consistent with its known role in promoting goblet cell differentiation and mucus secretion (Anbazhagan et al. 2016). In contrast, Gpx2 was downregulated in stem cells, TA cells, and goblet cells, reflecting impaired epithelial barrier function and heightened inflammatory responses (Esworthy et al. 2005). The inferred expression patterns of these genes closely matched those observed in the scRNA-seq data (Fig. 4F). Other gene expression deconvolution methods failed to capture these cell-type-specific changes (Fig. S6E-F).

To further evaluate the robustness of BayesPrism-DWLS in predicting cell-type-specific gene expression across diverse biological contexts, we applied the framework to mouse lung bulk RNA-seq data (Zhu et al. 2023) from an influenza infection model treated with the insulin sensitizer MSDC-0602 K (MSDC). BayesPrism-DWLS inferred an increased proportion of epithelial cells along with a reduction in immune and stromal cells, including neutrophils, macrophages, monocytes, and fibroblasts following MSDC treatment (Fig. S7A). These compositional changes are consistent with attenuation of pulmonary inflammation, as influenza infection typically triggers strong recruitment of innate immune cells that contributes to inflammatory tissue damage (Zhu et al. 2023). BayesPrism-DWLS also revealed a downregulation of key pro-inflammatory mediators, including Tnf, Il1b, and Ccl2, primarily within macrophage and neutrophil populations (Fig. S7B), consistent with the anti-inflammatory effects of MSDC observed in treated lungs (Zhu et al. 2023).

Together, these results indicate that BayesPrism-DWLS substantially improves the recovery of cell-type-specific transcriptional profiles from bulk RNA-seq data, particularly for low-abundance cell populations. It consistently recaptures both cellular compositional shifts and cell-type-resolved transcriptional changes that were concordant with independent single-cell references and established biological mechanisms.

Recovery of cell-type-specific gene expression in spatial transcriptomics

Spatial sequencing is widely used to understand cell organization in tissues, but its resolution is often constrained by cell-type-specific signals due to the mixing of multiple cell types within a single spot. To assess whether BayesPrism-DWLS framework is able to resolve spatial heterogeneity, we applied this framework to matched spatial transcriptomic datasets from a DSS-induced colitis model covering three stages: naive, inflammation, and regeneration (Fig. 5A). Spot-level 10 × Visium data were collected at Day 0 (D0), 12, and 30 (Mayassi et al. 2025), whereas imaging-based single-molecule MERFISH data were obtained at Day 0, 3, 9, and 21 (Cadinu et al. 2024). Day 12 in Visium and Day 9 in MERFISH corresponded to the peak inflammatory phase, while Day 30 in Visium and Day 21 in MERFISH represented the regeneration stage. Cell-type deconvolution using BayesPrism-DWLS revealed cell spatial distributions that are consistent with known colonic architecture: for example, enterocytes were predominantly localized to the upper regions of colonic crypts, whereas stem cells and TA cells were localized to the crypt base (Fig. S8). We next used BayesPrism-DWLS to generate cell-type-specific gene expression profiles across all time points. Comparing D12 enterocytes with D0, we identified sets of upregulated (list A) and downregulated (list B) genes (Fig. 5B). In contrast, spot-level differential expression analysis (upregulated list C and downregulated list D) captured only a subset of these changes, such as Nos2, while missing others, such as Areg, an EGF family member that activates MAPK/ERK and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways to regulate proliferation, differentiation, and tissue repair (Li et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2025). Spatially, Nos2 and Areg were primarily expressed in the upper crypt regions, peaking at D12 (Fig. 5C), which was confirmed by MERFISH validation (Fig. 5D).

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of differentially expressed genes in enterocytes identified by BayesPrism-DWLS revealed functional remodeling under DSS treatment (Fig. 5E-F). Upregulated genes were enriched for inflammatory and stress-related processes (Hausmann et al. 2024; Yao et al. 2024), including response to lipopolysaccharide, antimicrobial humoral response, I-κB kinase/NF-κB signaling, wound healing, and oxidative stress, as well as TNF, IL-17, ECM–receptor, PI3K-Akt, and HIF-1 signaling, reflecting activation of inflammation, tissue repair, and stress responses. Downregulated genes were associated with epithelial structural integrity and transport/metabolic functions (Li et al. 2025; Yang et al. 2025; Zhou et al. 2025), including apical plasma membrane, brush border, transaminase activity, active transmembrane transport, and metabolic pathways such as mineral absorption, selenocompound metabolism, short-chain fatty acid metabolism, and tryptophan metabolism, indicating impaired nutrient absorption and metabolic activity. Overall, enterocytes under DSS treatment exhibited enhanced inflammatory and stress responses while showing reduced transport and metabolic capacity.

Collectively, these results highlight the accuracy and robustness of BayesPrism-DWLS in dissection of spatial cellular heterogeneity by disentangling mixed signals within Visium spots and recovering cell-type-specific gene expression from spatial transcriptomic data.

Computational efficiency of deconvolution methods

To complement the accuracy evaluation, we further assessed the computational efficiency of the benchmarked methods under identical hardware configurations by comparing runtime and peak memory usage across increasing numbers of bulk RNA-seq samples. For the 25 cell-type deconvolution methods, substantial differences in computational cost were observed (Fig. S9A-B, Table S1). Methods requiring the construction of cell-type-specific reference expression signatures from single-cell data generally exhibited longer runtimes, whereas methods based on precomputed reference matrices showed better computational efficiency.

We further evaluated the computational performance of the eight gene expression deconvolution methods. BayesPrism-DWLS consistently required less runtime and memory than the other benchmarked gene expression deconvolution methods while achieving superior deconvolution accuracy (Fig. S9C-D, Table S1). Moreover, its computational cost increased linearly with the number of bulk RNA-seq samples, indicating good scalability for large-scale transcriptomic analyses.

Discussion

In this study, we systematically benchmarked a broad range of cell-type deconvolution methods across diverse tissues and cellular contexts. While several methods achieve reasonable accuracy in estimating bulk cell-type proportions, their performance varied substantially in tissue origin, cell type, and cell abundance; DWLS consistently exhibits robust performance in all evaluation settings, with a particular advantage in recovering low-abundance populations.

DWLS improves upon standard ordinary least squares deconvolution by adaptively weighting genes, ensuring that rare cell types and highly informative marker genes are appropriately represented (Tsoucas et al. 2019). The method iteratively updates gene weights, constrains cell-type proportions to be non-negative, and applies a dampening constant to prevent unstable solutions driven by low-abundance genes. These features mitigate biases inherent to traditional OLS approaches and underlie the consistent performance of DWLS across diverse tissues. Leveraging this robustness, we developed BayesPrism-DWLS in which DWLS-derived cell-type proportions guide BayesPrism-based inference of cell-type-specific gene expression profiles at single-sample resolution.

Application of BayesPrism-DWLS to DSS-induced mouse colon bulk RNA-seq and 10 × Visium spatial transcriptomics data revealed cell-type-specific differentially expressed genes and pathways that were not detectable at either the bulk level or individual spatial spots. These findings emphasize that accurate cell-type proportion estimation is a prerequisite for high-quality gene expression deconvolution and that proportion-guided expression recovery enhances the interpretability of complex tissue heterogeneity.

To facilitate its application to independent bulk RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics datasets, we provide an open-source R implementation of BayesPrism-DWLS. The framework estimates cell-type proportions from mixed transcriptomic profiles and further infers cell-type-specific gene expression profiles for each bulk sample or spatial spot. Only three input files are required to conduct the analysis: a single-cell count matrix, a bulk RNA-seq or spatial transcriptomics count matrix, and cell-type annotations for the single-cell reference. The single-cell reference and target transcriptomic data do not need to be paired from the same specimen, but they should represent comparable biological contexts and use consistent gene identifiers. The outputs include the cell-type signature matrix, estimated cell-type proportions, and inferred cell-type-specific expression matrices.

A persistent challenge in benchmarking deconvolution methods is the lack of reliable gold-standard datasets for multiple tissues and diverse cell types. Most existing validation strategies rely on flow cytometry-based measurements from blood or immunofluorescence-based cell counting, which offer limited coverage of solid tissues and depend heavily on predefined marker genes, thereby constraining their generalizability (Nguyen et al. 2024). Even when paired scRNA-seq data are available, systematic biases introduced by tissue dissociation, cell-type-specific capture efficiency, and library preparation can lead to substantial deviations from true tissue architecture (Denisenko et al. 2020). In this context, our observations in gastric corpus tissue illustrate how bulk transcriptomic deconvolution, when coupled with robust proportion estimation, can partially compensate for such biases and recover more biologically plausible cellular compositions, underscoring the complementary value of bulk-based deconvolution in settings where scRNA-seq may underrepresent specific cell populations.

Several limitations should be noted. First, BayesPrism-DWLS depends on external single-cell reference expression matrices, and performance may be affected when key cell types are absent or misannotated. Second, in spatial transcriptomics analyses, selecting spots based on cell-type proportion thresholds may influence downstream differential expression results, highlighting the need for systematic sensitivity evaluation. Finally, most existing deconvolution methods, including ours, do not explicitly incorporate spatial neighborhood information (Saqib and Kim 2025). The continued accumulation of high-quality scRNA-seq reference datasets of more tissue types and physiological states is expected to further improve supervised deconvolution. Future extensions of BayesPrism-DWLS could integrate graph-based neural networks (Shao et al. 2021) or Transformer architectures (Vaswani et al. 2017) to better incorporate biological priors and spatial context. Recent advances in transcriptomic foundation models, such as scGPT (Cui et al. 2024), scFoundation (Hao et al. 2024), and BulkRNABERT (Gélard et al. 2024), provide valuable conceptual and technical guidance for the next generation of deconvolution frameworks.

Methods

Collection of scRNA-seq benchmark datasets

We collected and integrated multiple publicly available scRNA-seq datasets from five tissues of wild-type mice: gastric (Hong et al. 2025), colon (Ishikawa et al. 2022; Xiao et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2023b; Zheng et al. 2024), small intestine (Ansari et al. 2023; Huang et al. 2024; Kim et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2025; Trsan et al. 2024; Xiao et al. 2023), lung (Fei et al. 2022; Gonzalez-Ferrer et al. 2024; Kim et al. 2024; Kim et al. 2022a), and liver (Fei et al. 2022; Han et al. 2018; Kalucka et al. 2020; Liu et al. 2024; Ramirez et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2021b; Xu et al. 2023a). These datasets were used to construct large-scale pseudo-bulk benchmarks for the systematic evaluation of deconvolution methods. The selected datasets encompass diverse experimental protocols, including fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) and droplet-based microfluidics, as well as full-length transcriptome sequencing (Smart-seq2) and 3′-end tag-based sequencing (10 × Genomics). Detailed dataset information, including sample number, cell number, gene coverage, annotated cell types, experimental conditions, and GEO accession numbers, is provided in Table S2.

ScRNA-seq data preprocessing and cell-type annotation

Most scRNA-seq datasets were obtained as raw count matrices processed by CellRanger (Zheng et al. 2017), while others were provided as HDF5 files or cell-by-gene expression matrices. For tissues represented by multiple datasets, all datasets from the same tissue were first merged into a single Seurat object. Cross-dataset integration was performed using Harmony (version 1.2.3) (Korsunsky et al. 2019), with dataset identity specified as the batch variable, to correct dataset-specific technical variability. No integration was performed across different tissue types, and each tissue was analyzed independently.

All scRNA-seq data were processed using Seurat (version 5.3.0) (Hao et al. 2021) in R (version 4.5.0). Cells expressing fewer than 100 genes or with mitochondrial gene content exceeding 80% were removed as an initial quality-control step. Subsequently, cells with abnormally high mitochondrial transcript fractions were identified using a data-driven approach. Specifically, mitochondrial fractions were modeled using the median and median absolute deviation (MAD), and one-sided P-values were calculated to identify cells with significantly elevated mitochondrial content. P-values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure, and cells with FDR < 0.005 were removed. To further exclude low-quality or apoptotic cells, which often form uninformative clusters, we applied an additional filtering strategy based on gene and UMI count distributions, combined with false discovery rate (FDR) correction. Gene expression values were normalized using Seurat's NormalizeData function with the LogNormalize method (scale factor = 100,000). The top 2,000 highly variable genes were identified using FindVariableFeatures and used for principal component analysis (PCA). Scaled expression data were projected onto principal components, and the top 30 Harmony-corrected PCs were used for nonlinear dimensionality reduction with UMAP.

Cell clustering was performed using Seurat's FindClusters function with resolutions ranging from 0.5 to 2.0. The final resolution was selected to ensure clear separation of major cell types without over-partitioning. Differential expression between clusters was assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test implemented in FindMarkers. Cell-type identities were assigned based on canonical marker gene expression.

Bulk RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics data collection and processing

Bulk RNA-seq datasets (Busada et al. 2021; Cosovanu et al. 2022; Hong et al. 2025; Katsuda et al. 2023; Kim et al. 2022b; Leibold et al. 2024; Zheng et al. 2024; Zhu et al. 2023) were obtained as raw FASTQ files and processed using a unified pipeline. Reads were aligned to the mouse reference genome (mm10) using STAR (version 2.2.1) (Dobin et al. 2013). Gene-level read counts were converted to transcripts per million (TPM) to account for differences in sequencing depth and gene length. Gene lengths were derived from reference GTF annotations, and for genes with multiple transcripts, weighted average transcript lengths were used. The resulting TPM matrices served as input for cell-type and gene expression deconvolution analyses. For DSS-induced mouse colon 10 × Visium spatial transcriptomics data (Mayassi et al. 2025), raw gene expression matrices were imported into Seurat. Normalization and variance stabilization were performed using the SCTransform method to correct for sequencing depth and technical noise, providing standardized inputs for spatial deconvolution and downstream analyses. MERFISH spatial transcriptomics data (Cadinu et al. 2024) from the DSS-induced colitis model were analyzed using the cell-type annotations provided in the original study. Gene expression matrices were extracted directly from raw data files and organized into gene-by-cell matrices, which were used for validation and comparison with model-predicted cell-type-specific expression.

Construction of reference expression profiles for deconvolution

For each tissue-specific scRNA-seq dataset, cells were randomly divided into independent training and testing sets using a 60:40 split, while preserving comparable cell-type proportions in both subsets. Using the training data, we calculated the mean raw expression count of each gene within each cell type to construct a cell-type-specific reference expression matrix. This reference matrix was used as input for both cell-type proportion deconvolution and gene expression deconvolution analyses.

Selection of cell-type-specific marker genes

Cell-type-specific marker genes were identified using the training datasets. Raw scRNA-seq count matrices were first normalized using the trimmed mean of M-values (TMM) method implemented in the edgeR package (version 4.6.2) (Robinson et al. 2010). Differential expression analysis was then performed using the limma-voom framework (Law et al. 2014) to identify candidate markers. Genes were retained for further analysis if they showed nonzero expression (counts > 0) in at least 30% of cells in at least one cell type. Among these, marker genes were defined as those exhibiting an absolute fold change of at least 1.4 in the target cell type relative to the cell type with the second-highest expression, together with a Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P value < 0.05. This strategy yields robust and highly specific marker sets, even in the presence of closely related cell types across datasets.

Generation of pseudo-bulk benchmark datasets

Pseudo-bulk benchmark datasets were generated from the testing datasets. For each dataset, 1,000 pseudo-bulk mixtures were simulated with a total pool size of 2,000 cells. For each mixture, a random subset of cell types was selected and cell-type proportions were randomly generated from a uniform distribution bounded by 0.1 and 95 and subsequently normalized to sum to one. Cells were then randomly sampled without replacement according to the assigned proportions and aggregated by summing their raw expression counts. The resulting cell-type proportions were retained as ground-truth values for benchmarking deconvolution performance.

In this study, we evaluated 25 widely used cell-type deconvolution methods spanning diverse algorithmic classes. These include traditional regression approaches, such as OLS (ordinary least squares) and NNLS (non-negative least squares); weighted least squares methods (EPIC, estimating the proportion of immune and cancer cells) (Racle et al. 2017) and EpiDISH (Teschendorff et al. 2017); robust regression methods (FARDEEP (Hao et al. 2019) and RLR); support vector regression (CIBERSORT) (Newman et al. 2015); penalized regression methods (Ridge, Lasso, Elastic Net) (Friedman et al. 2010) and the digital cell quantifier (DCQ) (Altboum et al. 2014); a log-scale modeling method (Dtangle) (Hunt et al. 2019); and strategies based on convex geometry or matrix decomposition, including SECRET (semi-reference generated from single-cell RNA-seq data to estimate cell type proportions) (Lu et al. 2023), debCAM (Chen et al. 2020), ENIGMA (Wang et al. 2023), and Bayesian generative models bMIND (Wang et al. 2021a) and CDSeq (Kang et al. 2021). Additionally, eight scRNA-seq reference-based methods were included: BisqueRNA (Jew et al. 2020), SCDC (Dong et al. 2021), DWLS (Tsoucas et al. 2019), DeconvSeq (Du et al. 2019), MuSiC (Wang et al. 2019), BayesPrism (Chu et al. 2022), CellR (Doostparast Torshizi et al. 2021), and TAPE (Chen et al. 2022b). Collectively, these methods encompass a broad spectrum of supervised learning strategies, providing a comprehensive framework for systematically assessing the accuracy of cell-type proportion predictions across diverse tissues and experimental conditions. Detailed information on the software packages, versions, key parameters, and references for the 25 benchmarked cell-type proportion deconvolution methods is provided in Table S3. Evaluation metrics for cell-type proportion deconvolution

We evaluated the performance of various cell-type deconvolution methods using thousands of pseudo-bulk mixtures with known cellular compositions. For each mixture, we calculated the Pearson correlation coefficient and root mean square error (RMSE) between predicted and true cell-type proportions, and plotted cell-type fit curves across all mixtures for each method. Higher Pearson correlations and lower RMSE values indicate better deconvolution performance. Overall similarity between mixtures was further assessed using Bray–Curtis distances, with smaller distances reflecting more accurate predictions.

False positive rates (FPR) were calculated by considering cell types absent from a mixture (true proportion < 1%) as true negatives (TN). Predicted proportions were binned into four intervals (< 1%, 1% − 10%, 10% − 50%, > 50%), with predictions ≥ 1% counted as false positives (FP). FPR was defined as FP/(FP + TN). False negative rates (FNR) were calculated for cell types truly present in a mixture (true proportion ≥ 1%), considered true positives (TP). True proportions were binned into three intervals (1% − 10%, 10% − 50%, > 50%), and predicted proportions < 1% were counted as false negatives (FN). FNR was defined as FN/(FN + TP). Lower FPR and FNR values indicate higher prediction accuracy. Finally, overall prediction accuracy was evaluated across a range of known cell proportion thresholds (0.1% − 100%) and defined as (TP + TN)/(TP + TN + FP + FN), with higher values indicating superior model performance.

Evaluation of gene expression deconvolution performance

To quantify the similarity between the predicted cell-type-specific expression profiles and the corresponding ground-truth profiles, we focused on the concordance of expression patterns rather than absolute expression magnitudes. Accordingly, cosine similarity was adopted as the primary evaluation metric. For each sample and each cell type, cosine similarity was computed between the predicted and original expression profiles across genes, thereby measuring the consistency of gene expression trends. In addition, to provide an intuitive comparison of different methods in recovering cell-type-specific gene expression, we plotted gene-wise fitting curves between predicted and original expression values across all pseudo-bulk mixtures. Pearson correlation coefficients were further calculated to summarize the overall agreement between predicted and true expression profiles.

Differential expression and pathway enrichment analyses

Cell-type-specific gene expression profiles were derived for each sample using the gene expression deconvolution methods. For spot-level spatial transcriptomics differential expression analysis, spots with a high proportion of the target cell type (e.g., goblet cells > 0.4) were selected to ensure reliable detection of cell-type-specific transcriptional changes. Differential expression between DSS-treated and control groups was performed using DESeq2 (version 1.48.1) (Love et al. 2014), with genes considered differentially expressed if |log2 fold change|> 1 and P < 0.05. Functional enrichment analyses of the differentially expressed genes were subsequently conducted, including Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses. Enrichment testing was performed using the ClusterProfiler package (version 4.16.0) (Yu et al. 2012), and results were visualized with ggplot2 (version 3.5.2).

Immunofluorescence staining and cell quantification

Mouse gastric epithelial tissue samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA), dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, and embedded in paraffin (Histore, Leica). Sections were prepared and subjected to antigen retrieval, blocking, and permeabilization, followed by incubation with primary and secondary antibodies targeting specific cell types. Nuclei were counterstained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI), and fluorescent images were acquired using a confocal microscope (Olympus FV3000). For each sample, two independent tissue sections were analyzed. From each section, three randomly selected regions were imaged and manually quantified, resulting in a total of six regions per group. Cell counts were used to compare wild-type and mutant groups and to assess concordance between experimentally measured cell-type proportions and those predicted by the deconvolution methods.

Statistics

Data shown in column graphs represent means ± SD. Statistical analyses were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for multiple-group comparisons or an unpaired two-tailed Student's t-test for two-group comparisons, with Welch's correction applied when variances were unequal. For data that did not meet the assumption of normality, the Mann–Whitney U test was used for two-group comparisons. All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. P-values are indicated as *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001, n.s., no significance.

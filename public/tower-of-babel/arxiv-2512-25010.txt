Bounding regularity of VI m {\mathrm{VI}}^{m} -modules
Wee Liang Gan, Khoa Ta

Abstract
. Fix a finite field 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} . Let VI {\mathrm{VI}} be a skeleton of the category of finite dimensional 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} -vector spaces and injective 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} -linear maps. We study VI m {\mathrm{VI}}^{m} -modules over a noetherian commutative ring in the nondescribing characteristic case. We prove that if a finitely generated VI m {\mathrm{VI}}^{m} -module is generated in degree ⩽ d \leqslant d and related in degree ⩽ r \leqslant r , then its regularity is bounded above by a function of m m , d d , and r r . A key ingredient of the proof is a shift theorem for finitely generated VI m {\mathrm{VI}}^{m} -modules.



Introduction

Fix a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}
of order
q
. Fix also a noetherian commutative ring
\Bbbk
such that
q
is invertible in
\Bbbk
.

Let
{\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}
-vector space of column vectors with
n
entries, all entries lying in
{\mathbb{F}}
. In particular,
{\mathbb{F}}^{0}
is the zero vector space.

Let
{\mathrm{VI}}
be the category defined as follows:

•

the set of objects of
{\mathrm{VI}}
is
{\mathbb{N}}
;

•

for all
a,b\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the morphisms in
{\mathrm{VI}}
from
a
to
b
are the injective
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear maps from
{\mathbb{F}}^{a}
to
{\mathbb{F}}^{b}
;

•

composition of morphisms is composition of the maps.

Let
m
be an integer
\geqslant 1
and
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
the product of
m
copies of
{\mathrm{VI}}
. A
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module
is a functor from
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
to the category of
\Bbbk
-modules. A homomorphism of
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules is a natural transformation of functors. Sam and Snowden have proven that every finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module is noetherian (see Theorem 1.1.3, Proposition 4.3.5, and Theorem 8.3.1 in
[
9
]
). Thus the category of finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules is an abelian category.

The main result of our present article says that if a finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
, then its (Castelnuovo-Mumford) regularity is bounded above by a function
\rho_{m}(d,r)
. The category
{\mathrm{VI}}
is an analog of a skeleton of the category
{\mathrm{FI}}
of finite sets and injective maps. In our previous article
[
5
]
, we proved that the same function
\rho_{m}(d,r)
gives an upper bound on the regularity of any
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module over an arbitrary commutative ring if it is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. While we will use some arguments from
[
5
]
in this article, the method of proof in
[
5
]
for
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-modules does not work for
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules; we discuss where it fails for
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules in Remark
5.4
.

Recall that in the
m=1
case, Nagpal
[
8
]
*Theorem 5.11 proved that finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}
-modules have finite regularity. Subsequently, Gan and Li
[
3
]
*Theorem 3.2 proved that if a finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}
-module is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
, then its regularity is bounded above by
\max\{d,d+r-1\}
, which is the same bound for
{\mathrm{FI}}
-modules proved by Church and Ellenberg
[
1
]
*Theorem A. The proof of the result of Gan and Li
[
3
]
*Theorem 3.2 used the shift theorem for finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}
-modules due to Nagpal
[
8
]
*Theorem 4.38. To prove our main result, we shall use a shift theorem for finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules.

This article is organized as follows. In §
1
, we recall basic definitions and two spectral sequences constructed in
[
5
]
; we also prove some preliminary results.
In §
2
, we study the natural shift functors
\Sigma_{i}
and the modified shift functors

on the category of
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules.
In §
3
, we study the functors
K_{i}
and
D_{i}
; the functor

is the kernel of a natural morphism
\mathrm{id}\to\Sigma_{i}
, while the functor

is the cokernel of a natural morphism
\mathrm{id}\to{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}
.
In §
4
, we prove a shift theorem (Theorem
4.3
) for finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules.
In §
5
, we prove our main result (Theorem
5.3
) giving an upper bound on the regularity of a finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules.

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Principal projective modules

The set of objects of
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
is
{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
. For any
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
, we write
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
for the set of morphisms in
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
from
\mathbf{a}
to
\mathbf{b}
.

Let
V
be any
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module. For each
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
, we write
V_{\mathbf{a}}
for
V(\mathbf{a})
. For each
f\in{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
, we write
f_{*}
for the map
V(f):V_{\mathbf{a}}\to V_{\mathbf{b}}
.

For each
\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
, let
M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n})
be the
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module defined as follows:

•

for each
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
, let
M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n})_{\mathbf{a}}
be the free
\Bbbk
-module with basis
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{a})
;

•

for each
g\in{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
, let
g_{*}:M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n})_{\mathbf{a}}\to M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n})_{\mathbf{b}}
be the
\Bbbk
-linear map sending each
f\in{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{a})
to the composition
gf\in{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{b})
.

Then
M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n})
is a projective
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module called a
principal projective
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module
. We say that a
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module is
free
if it is isomorphic to a direct sum of principal projective
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules. A
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module is finitely generated if and only if it is a homomorphic image of a finite direct sum of principal projective
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules.

Let
V^{(1)},V^{(2)},\ldots,V^{(m)}
be any
{\mathrm{VI}}
-modules. The external tensor product

V^{(1)}\boxtimes V^{(2)}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes V^{(m)}

is a
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module with

\left(V^{(1)}\boxtimes V^{(2)}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes V^{(m)}\right)_{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})}=V^{(1)}_{a_{1}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}V^{(2)}_{a_{2}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\cdots\otimes_{\Bbbk}V^{(m)}_{a_{m}}

for all
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
. For any morphism
f=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m})
in
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
,

f_{*}:\left(V^{(1)}\boxtimes V^{(2)}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes V^{(m)}\right)_{\mathbf{a}}\to\left(V^{(1)}\boxtimes V^{(2)}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes V^{(m)}\right)_{\mathbf{b}}

is the map
(f_{1})_{*}\otimes(f_{2})_{*}\otimes\cdots\otimes(f_{m})_{*}
.

For each
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
, we have a canonical isomorphism:

(1.1)

M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n})\cong M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n_{1})\boxtimes M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n_{2})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n_{m}).

1.2.
Homology

We write
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}\Mod
for the category of
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules.

Let
V
be any
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module. For each
\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
, let
\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}
be the
\Bbbk
-submodule of
V_{\mathbf{n}}
generated by

\left\{f_{*}(v)\ \Big|\ \mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m},\ \mathbf{a}\neq\mathbf{n},\ f\in{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n}),\ v\in V_{\mathbf{a}}\right\}.

The assignment
\mathbf{n}\mapsto\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}
defines a
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-submodule
\widetilde{V}
of
V
. Now define a functor

\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}_{0}:{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}\Mod\to{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}\Mod

by
\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}_{0}(V)=V/\widetilde{V}
. Then
\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}_{0}
is a right exact functor. For each integer
i\geqslant 1
, let

\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}_{i}:{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}\Mod\to{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}\Mod

be the
i
-th left derived functor of
\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}_{0}
.

We say that a
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module
V
is
homology acyclic
if
\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}_{i}(V)=0
for all
i\geqslant 1
. For example, free
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules are homology acyclic.

1.3.
Regularity

For each
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
, set

|\mathbf{n}|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Let
V
be any
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module. The
degree

\deg V
of
V
is defined by

\deg V=\begin{cases}\sup\{|\mathbf{n}|\mid V_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0\}&\mbox{ if }V\neq 0,\\
-1&\mbox{ if }V=0.\end{cases}

We have
\deg V\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}\cup\{\infty\}
. We say that
V
has
finite degree
if
\deg V<\infty
.

For each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let

t_{i}(V)=\deg\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{i}(V).

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}
. We say that
V
is
generated in degree
\leqslant d
if
t_{0}(V)\leqslant d
. Equivalently,
V
is generated in degree
\leqslant d
if and only if there exists an epimorphism
P\to V
where
P
is a free
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module of the form

P=\bigoplus_{j\in J}M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n}_{j}),

where
J
is some indexing set and
|\mathbf{n}_{j}|\leqslant d
for all
j\in J
.

Let
d,r\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}
. We say that
V
is
generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
if there exists a short exact sequence

0\to U\to P\to V\to 0

where
P
is a free
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module generated in degree
\leqslant d
, and
U
is a
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module generated in degree
\leqslant r
. We can choose
P
with
t_{0}(P)=t_{0}(V)
and in this case, from the long exact sequence induced by the above short exact sequence, we have

t_{1}(V)\leqslant t_{0}(U)\leqslant\max\{t_{0}(V),t_{1}(V)\}.

In particular,
t_{1}(V)\leqslant r
.

The
regularity

\reg(V)
of
V
is defined by

\reg(V)=\sup\{t_{i}(V)-i\mid i\geqslant 0\}.

1.4.
Spectral sequences

Assume that
m\geqslant 2
and consider
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}

Let

.

Assume

as the product category

Write

∎

By

\displaystyle(\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{i}(V))_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}

\displaystyle\cong\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}}_{i}(V_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}),

\displaystyle(\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{i}(V))_{(n_{1},-)}

\displaystyle\cong\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m-1}}_{i}(V_{(n_{1},-)}).

Moreover, by

2.
Shift functors

2.1.
Natural shift functors

We shall write linear maps from
{\mathbb{F}}^{a}
to
{\mathbb{F}}^{b}
(
a,b\in{\mathbb{N}}
) as
b\times a
matrices; in particular,
I_{a}
is the

Let
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. Define
\mathbf{e}_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
by

\mathbf{e}_{i}=(0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0),

where
1
is in the
i
-th coordinate.

There is a functor
\iota_{i}:{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}\to{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
defined on objects by

\displaystyle\iota_{i}:\Ob({\mathrm{VI}}^{m})

\displaystyle\to\Ob({\mathrm{VI}}^{m}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}_{i},

and on morphisms by

\displaystyle\iota_{i}:{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\to{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{e}_{i}),

\displaystyle(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})

\displaystyle\mapsto(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}),

where
g_{j}=f_{j}
for all

and

g_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}1&0&\cdots&0\\
\hline\cr 0&&&\\
\vdots&&f_{i}&\\
0&&&\end{array}\right).

The
i
-th

\Sigma_{i}:{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}\Mod\to{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}\Mod

is defined to be the pullback via
\iota_{i}
. Thus, for any
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module
V
, we have

(\Sigma_{i}V)_{\mathbf{a}}=V_{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}\quad\mbox{ for all }\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}.

Notation 2.1
.

When

, we write
\iota
and
\Sigma
for
\iota_{1}
and
\Sigma_{1}
, respectively.

For any
{\mathrm{VI}}
-modules

\Sigma_{i}\left(V^{(1)}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes V^{(m)}\right)=V^{(1)}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\left(\Sigma V^{(i)}\right)\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes V^{(m)}.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
.

If
n_{i}=0
, then

\Sigma_{i}M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n})\cong M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n}).

If
n_{i}\geqslant 1

\Sigma_{i}M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n})\cong\left(M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n})^{\oplus k}\right)\oplus\left(M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e_{i}})^{\oplus\ell}\right)

Proof.

By (
1.1
) and (
2.2
), it suffices to prove the lemma when

It is plain that

\Sigma M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(0)\cong M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(0).

Let

\Sigma M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)\cong\left(M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)^{\oplus q^{n}}\right)\oplus\left(M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n-1)^{\oplus(q^{n}-1)q^{n-1}}\right).

This is sketched in
[
2
]

Let

.
If the rank of
\gamma_{f}
is

and the restriction of the row vector

For each row vector

-submodule of

,

•

.

This defines a

-submodule

of

. It is easy to see that

. There are

For each one-dimensional subspace

\Bbbk
-submodule of

•

,

•

.

This defines a
{\mathrm{VI}}
-submodule

of
\Sigma M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)
. Observe that when
\ker\gamma_{f}=B
, the map
\gamma_{f}:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{a}
factors uniquely through the quotient
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/B
(which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
).
From this, it is easy to see that
W(B,\beta)
is isomorphic to
M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n-1)
. There are
(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
choices of one-dimensional subspaces
B
of
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. For each such choice of
B
, there are
q^{n}-q^{n-1}
choices of row vectors
\beta:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}
whose restriction to
B
is nonzero. Therefore there are
(q^{n}-1)q^{n-1}
choices of such pairs
(B,\beta)
.

We conclude from the above that
\Sigma M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)
is the direct sum of
q^{n}
copies of
M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)
and
(q^{n}-1)q^{n-1}
copies of
M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n-1)
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Then
\Sigma_{i}\Sigma_{j}=\Sigma_{j}\Sigma_{i}
.

Proof.

This follows from
\iota_{i}\iota_{j}=\iota_{j}\iota_{i}
.
∎

2.2.
Modified shift functor on
{\mathrm{VI}}\Mod

Following Nagpal
[
8
]
, we shall define a functor

\overline{\Sigma}:{\mathrm{VI}}\Mod\to{\mathrm{VI}}\Mod

which turns out to be more useful than the natural shift functor. We call
\overline{\Sigma}
the
modified shift functor
on
{\mathrm{VI}}\Mod
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For each
c\in{\mathbb{F}}^{a}
, define
\sigma(c)\in{\mathrm{VI}}(a+1,a+1)
by

\sigma(c)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}1&0&\cdots&0\\
\hline\cr&&&\\
c&&I_{a}&\\
&&&\end{array}\right).

Let

U(a)=\{\sigma(c)\mid c\in{\mathbb{F}}^{a}\}.

Then
U(a)
is a subgroup of
{\mathrm{VI}}(a+1,a+1)
. We have
\sigma(c)\sigma(c^{\prime})=\sigma(c+c^{\prime})
for all
c,c^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{a}
.

Let
V
be any
{\mathrm{VI}}
-module. The
{\mathrm{VI}}
-module
{\overline{\Sigma}}V
is defined as follows:

•

For each
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let

({\overline{\Sigma}}V)_{a}=(V_{a+1})_{U(a)}.

•

For each
f\in{\mathrm{VI}}(a,b)
, let

f_{*}:({\overline{\Sigma}}V)_{a}\to({\overline{\Sigma}}V)_{b}

be the map sending the class represented by
v\in V_{a+1}
to the class represented by
\iota(f)_{*}(v)
(see Notation
2.1
). The map
f_{*}
is well-defined because for all
c\in{\mathbb{F}}^{a}
:

\iota(f)\sigma(c)=\sigma(fc)\iota(f);

hence for all
v\in V_{a+1}
:

\iota(f)_{*}(\sigma(c)_{*}(v)-v)=\sigma(fc)_{*}(w)-w\quad\mbox{ where }w=\iota(f)_{*}(v).

For any homomorphism
\eta:V\to W
of
{\mathrm{VI}}
-modules, it is plain that
\Sigma\eta:\Sigma V\to\Sigma W
descends to a homomorphism
{\overline{\Sigma}}\eta:{\overline{\Sigma}}V\to{\overline{\Sigma}}W
.

Now let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geqslant 1
. Let
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
a\geqslant n-1
. For each
f\in{\mathrm{VI}}(n,a+1)
, let
\beta_{f}
be the first row of
f
and let
\gamma_{f}
be the
a\times n
matrix formed by the last
a
rows of
f
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f\in{\mathrm{VI}}(n,a+1)
. Suppose that
\beta_{f}=(\beta_{1}\ \beta_{2}\ \ldots\ \beta_{n})
.

(i)

If
\beta_{f}=0
, then for all
c\in{\mathbb{F}}^{a}
, one has
\sigma(c)f=f
.

(ii)

If
\beta_{f}\neq 0
, then for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
\beta_{j}\neq 0
, there exists a unique
c\in{\mathbb{F}}^{a}
such that the
j
-th column of
\gamma_{\sigma(c)f}
is
0
.

Proof.

Let
c\in{\mathbb{F}}^{a}
and
g=\sigma(c)f
. Then

\beta_{g}=\beta_{f},\quad\gamma_{g}=c\beta_{f}+\gamma_{f}.

Thus if
\beta_{f}=0
, then
g=f
. This proves (i).

Suppose that
\beta_{j}\neq 0
. Let
u
be the
j
-th column of
\gamma_{f}
and
v
be the
j
-th column of
\gamma_{g}
. Then

v=c\beta_{j}+u.

Thus
v=0
if and only if
c=-\beta_{j}^{-1}u
. This proves (ii).
∎

The preceding lemma allows us to make the following definition.

Definition 2.6
.

For any morphism
f\in{\mathrm{VI}}(n,a+1)
, define the morphism
\overline{f}\in{\mathrm{VI}}(n,a+1)
as follows:

•

if
\beta_{f}=0
, then
\overline{f}=f
;

•

if
\beta_{f}=(\beta_{1}\ \beta_{2}\ \ldots\ \beta_{n})
with
\beta_{1}=\cdots=\beta_{j-1}=0
and
\beta_{j}\neq 0
, then
\overline{f}=\sigma(c)f
where
c
is the unique vector in
{\mathbb{F}}^{a}
such that the
j
-th column of
\gamma_{\sigma(c)f}
is
0
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

If
n=0
, then

{\overline{\Sigma}}M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)\cong M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n).

If
n\geqslant 1
, then

{\overline{\Sigma}}M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)\cong M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)\oplus\left(M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n-1)^{\oplus(q^{n}-1)}\right).

Proof.

The
n=0
case is trivial so assume that
n\geqslant 1
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
a\geqslant n-1
. For each row vector
\beta:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}
, let
P(\beta)_{a}
be the
\Bbbk
-submodule of
(\Sigma M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n))_{a}
generated by all
f\in{\mathrm{VI}}(n,a+1)
such that
\beta_{f}=\beta
and
\overline{f}=f
(see Definition
2.6
). In other words:

•

if
\beta=0
, then
P(\beta)_{a}
is generated by all
f\in{\mathrm{VI}}(n,a+1)
such that
\beta_{f}=0
;

•

if
\beta=(\beta_{1}\ \beta_{2}\ \ldots\ \beta_{n})
with
\beta_{1}=\cdots=\beta_{j-1}=0
and
\beta_{j}\neq 0
, then
P(\beta)_{a}
is generated by all
f\in{\mathrm{VI}}(n,a+1)
such that
\beta_{f}=\beta
and the
j
-th column of
\gamma_{f}
is
0
.

This defines a
{\mathrm{VI}}
-submodule
P(\beta)
of
\Sigma M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)
. It is easy to see that:

•

if
\beta=0
, then
P(\beta)
is isomorphic to
M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)
;

•

if
\beta\neq 0
, then
P(\beta)
is isomorphic to
M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n-1)
.

Let
P
be the sum of
P(\beta)
over all row vectors
\beta:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}
. Then

P\cong M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n)\oplus\left(M^{{\mathrm{VI}}}(n-1)^{\oplus(q^{n}-1)}\right).

Let
\eta
be the composition of the inclusion map
P\to\Sigma M(n)
and the quotient map
\Sigma M(n)\to{\overline{\Sigma}}M(n)
. It suffices to show that
\eta
is an isomorphism. Let
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
a\geqslant n-1
. We need to show that
\eta_{a}:P_{a}\to({\overline{\Sigma}}M(n))_{a}
is bijective.

Let
\zeta_{a}:(\Sigma M(n))_{a}\to P_{a}
be the
\Bbbk
-linear map sending each morphism
f\in{\mathrm{VI}}(n,a+1)
to
\overline{f}
. Then
\zeta_{a}
factors through
({\overline{\Sigma}}M(n))_{a}
to give a
\Bbbk
-linear map
\overline{\zeta}_{a}:({\overline{\Sigma}}M(n))_{a}\to P_{a}
. It is plain that
\eta_{a}
and
\overline{\zeta}_{a}
are inverses of each other, hence
\eta_{a}
is bijective.
∎

is essentially due to Nagpal; see

]

3.
The functors
K_{i}
and
D_{i}

3.1.
The functor
K_{i}

Let
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
.

For each
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
, define
\varpi_{i}\in{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}_{i})
as follows:

•

the
j
-th component of
\varpi_{i}
is
I_{a_{j}}
for all
j\neq i
,

•

the
i
-th component of
\varpi_{i}
is the
(a_{i}+1)\times a_{i}
-matrix whose first row is
0
, and whose last
a_{i}
rows form the matrix
I_{a_{i}}
.

Let
V
be a
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module. Then there is a natural homomorphism

\varepsilon_{i}:V\to\Sigma_{i}V

defined at each
\mathbf{a}\in\Ob({\mathrm{VI}}^{m})
to be the map
(\varpi_{i})_{*}:V_{\mathbf{a}}\to V_{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}
. Let

\xi_{i}:\Sigma_{i}V\to{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V

be the quotient map and let

\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}:V\to{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V

be the composition of
\varepsilon_{i}
and
\xi_{i}
.

Let
K_{i}V
be the kernel of
\varepsilon_{i}:V\to\Sigma_{i}V
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
V
be any
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module. Then
K_{i}V
is the kernel of
\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}:V\to{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
and
v\in V_{\mathbf{a}}
. We need to show that if
\xi_{i}((\varpi_{i})_{*}(v))=0
, then
(\varpi_{i})_{*}(v)=0
.

Since
|U_{i}(\mathbf{a})|
is invertible in
\Bbbk
, we have a well-defined map

\psi:(V_{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}_{i}})_{U_{i}(\mathbf{a})}\to V_{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}

which sends the class represented by

to the element

|U_{i}(\mathbf{a})|^{-1}\sum_{g\in U_{i}(\mathbf{a})}g_{*}(w).

For every
g\in U_{i}(\mathbf{a})
, we have
g\varpi_{i}=\varpi_{i}
. Thus

\displaystyle\psi(\xi_{i}((\varpi_{i})_{*}(v)))

\displaystyle=|U_{i}(\mathbf{a})|^{-1}\sum_{g\in U_{i}(\mathbf{a})}g_{*}((\varpi_{i})_{*}(v))

\displaystyle=|U_{i}(\mathbf{a})|^{-1}\sum_{g\in U_{i}(\mathbf{a})}(\varpi_{i})_{*}(v)

\displaystyle=(\varpi_{i})_{*}(v).

In particular, if
\xi_{i}((\varpi_{i})_{*}(v))=0
, then
(\varpi_{i})_{*}(v)=0
.
∎

-modules of the form
M^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}(\mathbf{n})
with
|\mathbf{n}|\leqslant r
.

Therefore
D_{i}V
is generated in degree
\leqslant d-1
and related in degree
\leqslant r
.
∎

4.
Shift theorem

4.1.
The
m=1
case

Nagpal proved that if
V
is a finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}
-module, then
\Sigma^{s}V
and
{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}V
are homology acyclic for all
s
sufficiently large; see
[
8
]
*Proposition 3.10 and Theorem 4.38. Our results in this section and the next depend on Nagpal’s result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
d,r\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}
. Let
V
be a finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}
-module which is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. Then for any non-negative integer
s\geqslant d+r
, the
{\mathrm{VI}}
-modules
\Sigma^{s}V
and
{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}V
are homology acyclic.

Proof.

For the natural shift functor, the lemma follows from
[
3
]
*Corollary 4.4 and
[
8
]
*Proposition 3.10.

To prove the lemma for the modified shift functor, we use induction on
d
. The base case
d=-1
is trivial. Now let
d\geqslant 0
and assume the following induction hypothesis:

For all finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}
-module
W
which is generated in degree
\leqslant d-1
and related in degree
\leqslant r
, if
s\geqslant d+r-1
, then
{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}W
is homology acyclic.

Let
s\geqslant d+r
. Recall that we have the exact sequence

0\to KV\to V\to{\overline{\Sigma}}V\to DV\to 0.

Applying the functor
{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}
to the above exact sequence, we obtain an exact sequence

0\to{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}KV\to{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}V\to{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s+1}V\to{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}DV\to 0.

By
[
3
]
*Lemma 2.4 and Theorem 3.2, we have
\deg KV\leqslant\max\{-1,d+r-1\}
so
{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}KV=0
. Therefore we have a short exact sequence

0\to{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}V\to{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s+1}V\to{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}DV\to 0.

By Lemma
3.10
and our induction hypothesis, we have
\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}}_{i}({\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}DV)=0
for all
i\geqslant 1
. Therefore

\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}}_{i}({\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}V)\cong\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}}_{i}({\overline{\Sigma}}^{s+1}V)\qquad\mbox{ for all }i\geqslant 1.

Since
s
is an arbitrary non-negative integer
\geqslant d+r
, it follows that

\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}}_{i}({\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}V)\cong\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}}_{i}({\overline{\Sigma}}^{n}V)\qquad\mbox{ for all }n\geqslant s\mbox{ and }i\geqslant 1.

By
[
8
]
*Proposition 3.10 and Theorem 4.38, there exists an integer
n\geqslant s
such that
\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}}_{i}({\overline{\Sigma}}^{n}V)=0
for all
i\geqslant 1
. We conclude that
\HH^{{\mathrm{VI}}}_{i}({\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}V)=0
for all
i\geqslant 1
.
∎

4.2.
The
m\geqslant 2
case

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
m\geqslant 2
and consider
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
as the product category
{\mathrm{VI}}\times{\mathrm{VI}}^{m-1}
. Let
i\in\{2,\ldots,m\}
. Then
\HH_{0}^{{\mathrm{hor}}}\Sigma_{i}\cong\Sigma_{i}\HH_{0}^{{\mathrm{hor}}}
and
\HH_{0}^{{\mathrm{hor}}}{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}\cong{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}\HH_{0}^{{\mathrm{hor}}}
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma for the modified shift functors. The proof for the natural shift functors is similar.

Let
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
. Let
V
be any
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module. Then by definition,

(\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V))_{\mathbf{n}}=({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V)_{\mathbf{n}}/({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V)^{\mathrm{hor}}_{\mathbf{n}}.

It follows that

(\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V))_{\mathbf{n}}\cong V_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}/(V^{\mathrm{hor}}_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}+C)

where
C
is the
\Bbbk
-submodule of
V_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}
generated by all elements of the form
g_{*}(v)-v
for some
g\in U_{i}(\mathbf{n})
and
v\in V_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}
.

On the other hand, we also have

\displaystyle({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}(V))_{\mathbf{n}}

\displaystyle=((\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}(V))_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}})_{U_{i}(\mathbf{n})}

\displaystyle=(V_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}/V^{\mathrm{hor}}_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}})_{U_{i}(\mathbf{n})}

\displaystyle\cong V_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}/(V^{\mathrm{hor}}_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}+C).

∎

Theorem 4.3

(Shift theorem)
.

Let
m,d,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geqslant 1
and
d,r\geqslant-1
. Let
V
be a finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module which is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. Then for any non-negative integer
s\geqslant d+r
, the
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules
\Sigma_{1}^{s}\Sigma_{2}^{s}\cdots\Sigma_{m}^{s}V
and
{\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}{\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}V
are homology acyclic.

Proof.

We prove the theorem for the modified shift functors. The proof for the natural shift functors is similar.

We use induction on
m
, the base case
m=1
being Lemma
4.1
. Now let
m\geqslant 2
and assume that the theorem holds for finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m-1}
-modules. We shall consider
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
as the product category
{\mathrm{VI}}\times{\mathrm{VI}}^{m-1}
.

Let
s\geqslant d+r
. For all
(n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m-1}
, we have

(4.4)

({\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}{\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}V)_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}={\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}(({\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}V)_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}).

By Proposition
2.9
, the
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module
{\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}V
is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
.
Hence by
[
5
]
*Lemma 2.3, the
{\mathrm{VI}}
-module

({\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}V)_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}

is also generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. It follows by Lemma
4.1
and (
4.4
) that
({\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}{\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}V)_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}
is a
{\mathrm{VI}}
-module which is homology acyclic.

Let
p\geqslant 1
. Then

\displaystyle\HH_{p}^{{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}{\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}V)\cong

\displaystyle\HH_{p}^{\mathrm{ver}}\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}{\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}V)

by Lemma
1.6

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\HH_{p}^{\mathrm{ver}}\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}{\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}V)

by Lemma
2.10

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\HH_{p}^{\mathrm{ver}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}V))

\displaystyle\mbox{by Lemma \ref{lem: hor and shift commute}}.

Hence by (
1.4
), it suffices to show that for each
n_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m-1}
-module

({\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}V))_{(n_{1},-)}

is homology acyclic. We have

({\overline{\Sigma}}_{2}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}^{s}\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}V))_{(n_{1},-)}={\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}_{m-1}^{s}((\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}V))_{(n_{1},-)}).

Therefore by inductive hypothesis, it suffices to prove that the
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m-1}
-module
(\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}V))_{(n_{1},-)}
is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
.

By Proposition
2.9
, we know that
{\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}V
is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. Hence by Lemma
1.3
, the
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m-1}
-module
(\HH_{0}^{\mathrm{hor}}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{1}^{s}V))_{(n_{1},-)}
is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
.
∎

5.
Upper bound on regularity

5.1.
The function
\rho_{m}

We recall from
[
5
]
*Definition 1.5 the function

\rho_{m}:{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}\to{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
m,d,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geqslant 1
and
d,r\geqslant-1
.

If
m=1
, then let

\rho_{m}(d,r)=\max\{d,d+r-1\}.

If
m\geqslant 2
and
d=-1
, then let

\rho_{m}(d,r)=-1.

If
m\geqslant 2
and
d\geqslant 0
, then let

\rho_{m}(d,r)=\max\{\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)),1+\rho_{m}(d-1,r)\}

where

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle=\max\{2+\rho_{m}(d-1,r),r\},

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle=\max\{3+\rho_{m}(d-1,r),4+\rho_{1}(d,r)+\rho_{m-1}(d,r)\}.

Later, we shall make use of the following properties of the functions
\rho^{\prime}_{m}
and
\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
m,d,r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
m\geqslant 2
,
d\geqslant 0
,
r\geqslant-1
. Then we have:

(a)

\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)>d
.

(b)

\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)>r
.

Proof.

(a) We have:

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle\geqslant 2+\rho_{m}(d-1,r)

\displaystyle\geqslant 2+(d-1)\quad\quad\quad\mbox{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{gta}{}{}]}*{Corollary 4.4}}

\displaystyle>d.

(b) We have:

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle\geqslant 4+\rho_{1}(d,r)+\rho_{m-1}(d,r)

\displaystyle\geqslant 4+(d+r-1)+(-1)

\displaystyle>r.

∎

5.2.
Main result

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
m,d,r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
m\geqslant 1
and
d,r\geqslant-1
. Let
V
be a finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module which is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. Then

\reg(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r).

The
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module analog of Theorem
5.3
was proved in
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.6 without assuming that
V
is finitely generated or
\Bbbk
is noetherian. In the following remark, we discuss where the method of proof of
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.6 fails for
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules.

Remark 5.4
.

(a)

We write
\Sigma_{1},\ldots,\Sigma_{m}
for the shift functors on
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}\Mod
. For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module
V
, there is a natural homomorphism
V\to\Sigma_{i}V
whose cokernel
D_{i}V
satisfies

t_{0}(D_{i}V)\leqslant\max\{-1,t_{0}(V)-1\}.

Similarly for
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules with
{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}
in place of
\Sigma_{i}
. This gives a way to prove results on (certain classes of)
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-modules or
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules
V
by induction on
t_{0}(V)
.

(b)

We recall from
[
5
]
*Lemma 3.4 that for any
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module
V
, we have

\deg(V)\leqslant 1+\max\left\{\deg(\Sigma_{i}V):i=1,\ldots,m\right\}.

This may be
false
for a
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module
V
with
{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}
in place of
\Sigma_{i}
(even when
m=1
). This lemma is used in Step 4 in the proof of
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.6 in an essential way. It is also used in the proof of
[
5
]
*Corollary 3.11 which says that for any
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module
V
with
\deg(V)<\infty
, we have

\reg(V)\leqslant\deg(V).

However, only the
m=1
case of
[
5
]
*Corollary 3.11 is actually used later (in the proof of
[
5
]
*Proposition 4.2), and this
m=1
case for finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}
-modules has been proved by Nagpal
[
8
]
*Lemma 5.10.

(c)

The method of Gan and Li in
[
4
]
for proving finiteness of regularity is not applicable to
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules.

We now prove our main result. We shall refer the reader to
[
5
]
when the arguments for
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules are essentially the same as the arguments for
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-modules.

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

For any integers
m\geqslant 1
and
d\geqslant-1
, denote by
\mathfrak{T}(m,d)
the following statement:

For any integer
r\geqslant-1
, if
V
is a finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module which is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
, then
\reg(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)
.

We shall prove that
\mathfrak{T}(m,d)
is true by nested induction. The outer induction is over
m
and the inner induction is over
d
.

By
[
3
]
*Theorem 3.2, we know that
\mathfrak{T}(1,d)
is true for all
d\geqslant-1
. It is easy to see that
\mathfrak{T}(m,-1)
is true for all
m\geqslant 1
.

Now fix
m\geqslant 2
and
d\geqslant 0
. Assume that:

•

\mathfrak{T}(m-1,c)
is true for all
c\geqslant-1
;

•

\mathfrak{T}(m,d-1)
is true.

To prove that
\mathfrak{T}(m,d)
is true, fix an integer
r\geqslant-1
and let
V
be any finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module which is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. We want to show that

\reg(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r).

We do this in several steps. In the following, let
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

Step 1. Bound
t_{2}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V)
.

By Lemma
2.12
, we know that
{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V
is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
.

Let
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
. We deduce by
[
5
]
*Lemma 2.3 that
{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}
and
{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V_{(n_{1},-)}
are generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. Hence by
\mathfrak{T}(1,d)
and
\mathfrak{T}(m-1,d)
, we have:

\displaystyle\reg\left({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho_{1}(d,r),

\displaystyle\reg\left({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V_{(n_{1},-)}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho_{m-1}(d,r).

Applying Proposition
1.7
to
{\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V
gives:

(5.5)

t_{2}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V)\leqslant 4+\rho_{1}(d,r)+\rho_{m-1}(d,r).

Step 2. Bound
t_{0}(K_{i}V)
and
t_{1}(K_{i}V)
.

Let
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then we have:

\displaystyle t_{q}(K_{i}V)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{t_{q}(V),t_{q+1}(V/K_{i}V)\}

by (
3.2
)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{t_{q}(V),t_{q+1}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V),t_{q+2}(D_{i}V)\}

\displaystyle\mbox{by \eqref{eq: les 2}}.

When
q=0
, we have:

t_{0}(K_{i}V)\leqslant\max\{t_{0}(V),t_{1}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V),t_{2}(D_{i}V)\}.

By Lemma
2.12
, Lemma
3.10
, and
\mathfrak{T}(m,d-1)
, we get:

t_{0}(K_{i}V)\leqslant\max\{d,r,2+\rho_{m}(d-1,r)\}.

Hence by Definition
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
:

(5.6)

t_{0}(K_{i}V)\leqslant\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r).

When
q=1
, we have:

t_{1}(K_{i}V)\leqslant\max\{t_{1}(V),t_{2}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}V),t_{3}(D_{i}V)\}.

By (
5.5
) from Step 1, Lemma
3.10
, and
\mathfrak{T}(m,d-1)
, we get:

t_{1}(K_{i}V)\leqslant\max\{r,4+\rho_{1}(d,r)+\rho_{m-1}(d,r),3+\rho_{m}(d-1,r)\}.

Hence by Definition
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
:

(5.7)

t_{1}(K_{i}V)\leqslant\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r).

Step 3. Bound
\reg(K_{i}V)
.

Similarly to Step 3 in the proof of
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.6, we have:

(5.8)

\reg(K_{i}V)\leqslant\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)).

The proof for finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-modules uses:

•

(
5.6
) and (
5.7
) from Step 2;

•

the
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-analog of
[
5
]
*Lemma 3.1, which is proved in the same way with
{\mathrm{VI}}
in place of
{\mathrm{FI}}
;

•

the
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-analog of
[
5
]
*Proposition 4.2, which is proved in the same way with
{\mathrm{VI}}
in place of
{\mathrm{FI}}
, and with
[
8
]
*Lemma 5.10 in place of
[
5
]
*Corollary 3.11.

Step 4. Bound
\reg(V)
.

We remind the reader that (
5.8
) in Step 3 is applicable to any
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module
V
which is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
.

We claim the following:

Let
W
be a finitely generated
{\mathrm{VI}}^{m}
-module which is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
.
Assume that
\reg({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}W)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)
. Then
\reg(W)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)
.

To prove the claim, let
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then:

\displaystyle t_{q}(W)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{t_{q}(K_{i}W),t_{q}(W/K_{i}W)\}

by (
3.4
)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{t_{q}(K_{i}W),t_{q}({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}W),t_{q+1}(D_{i}W)\}

by (
3.5
)

\displaystyle\leqslant q+\max\{\reg(K_{i}W),\reg({\overline{\Sigma}}_{i}W),1+\reg(D_{i}W)\}.

By (
5.8
) from Step 3, Lemma
3.10
, and
\mathfrak{T}(m,d-1)
, it follows that:

t_{q}(W)\leqslant q+\max\{\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)),\rho_{m}(d,r),1+\rho_{m}(d-1,r)\}.

Hence by Definition
5.1
:

t_{q}(W)\leqslant q+\rho_{m}(d,r).

This proves the claim.

Now let
s=\max\{0,d+r\}
. Then

\displaystyle\reg({\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{1}{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{2}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{m}V)

\displaystyle=t_{0}({\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{1}{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{2}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{m}V)

by Theorem
4.3

\displaystyle\leqslant d

by Lemma
2.12

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle\mbox{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{gta}{}{}]}*{Corollary 4.4}}.

Keeping Lemma
2.12
in mind, we can therefore apply the above claim successively to see that:

\displaystyle\quad\reg({\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{1}{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{2}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{m}V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\reg({\overline{\Sigma}}^{s-1}_{1}{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{2}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{m}V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\reg({\overline{\Sigma}}^{s-2}_{1}{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{2}\cdots{\overline{\Sigma}}^{s}_{m}V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\reg({\overline{\Sigma}}^{2}_{m}V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\reg({\overline{\Sigma}}_{m}V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\reg(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r).

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

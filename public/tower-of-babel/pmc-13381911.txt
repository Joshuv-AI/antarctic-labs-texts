Comparison of immediate therapeutic effects of 3% diquafosol and 2% rebamipide in dry eye disease: a prospective, randomized, paired-eye trial
Authors: Myung-Sun Song, Eunyoung Kim, Nayoon Park, Youngsub Eom, Jong Suk Song, Dong Hyun Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Both diquafosol and rebamipide improve signs and symptoms of dry eye disease (DED), but no clinical study has directly compared their immediate effects. This study compared the 30-min therapeutic and mucin-secreting effects of 3% diquafosol (DQS) and 2% rebamipide (REB). In this prospective, randomized, double-blinded study, 30 DED patients received DQS in one eye and REB in the fellow eye. Visual analog scale (VAS) score, tear break-up time (TBUT), ocular staining score (OSS), lipid layer thickness, corneal sensitivity, tear meniscus height, and tear levels of MUC5AC and MUC1 were measured before and 30 min after instillation. The mean age was 34.8 ± 13.8 years, and 23 patients were female. Both treatments significantly improved VAS score and TBUT at 30 min (all P < 0.05). VAS score improvement was greater with DQS than REB (P = 0.03), whereas TBUT changes were comparable (P = 0.141). Tear MUC5AC levels significantly increased after both treatments (both P ≤ 0.001), while MUC1 levels remained unchanged (P = 0.377/0.262). Changes in MUC5AC were similar between groups (P = 0.254). Overall, DQS and REB showed comparable early clinical benefits 30 min after instillation in DED, including symptom relief, improved tear film stability, and increased tear MUC5AC.

Introduction

Dry eye disease (DED) is a multifactorial entity characterized by tear film instability, ocular surface inflammation, and tear hyperosmolarity1. Given its various etiologies, multiple therapeutic options are available, including artificial tears, anti-inflammatory agents, mucin secretagogues, and tear retention strategies. Among mucin secretagogues, topical diquafosol (DQS) and rebamipide (REB) have gained clinical attention due to increased tear film stability2–5. This is particularly relevant as recent guidelines from the Korean Dry Eye Society and the Asia Dry Eye Society emphasize tear film stability as the most important clinical sign of DED6,7.

DQS is a purinergic P2Y2 receptor agonist that stimulates mucin secretion from conjunctival goblet cells, fluid from conjunctival epithelial cells, and increases tear film lipid layer thickness2–4,8. Numerous clinical studies have shown improvements in DED symptoms, tear break-up time (TBUT), and ocular surface staining score with DQS instillation9–14. REB is a quinolinone derivative that enhances corneal and conjunctival mucin secretion, increases goblet cell density, and exerts anti-inflammatory effects15–19. Similar to DQS, multiple randomized clinical trials have demonstrated improvements in clinical parameters of DED with REB treatment20–23.

Several clinical studies have directly compared the therapeutic effects of DQS and REB in DED. Comparative studies in occupational and post-surgical dry eye populations have demonstrated comparable therapeutic effects between the two secretagogues24,25. A recent crossover trial also revealed that both DQS and REB significantly improved DED parameters with similar efficacy26. Despite these findings, there is limited research investigating the immediate effects following instillation. An experimental study in rabbits reported a significant increase in tear MUC5AC levels after 15 min only in the DQS group27. A previous Japanese study also showed that topical DQS significantly increased the tear meniscus radius 15 min after instillation compared with the baseline28. While long-term cumulative therapeutic effects are crucial in the management of DED, immediate improvement in symptoms and signs after topical eye drop instillation is also clinically important. Rapid relief not only enhances patient satisfaction and adherence but also contributes to early restoration of visual function29,32. Therefore, prompt clinical improvement with eye drops should also be considered an essential goal in the management of DED. This study aimed to compare the short-term therapeutic effects and tolerability of 3% DQS and 2% REB in patients with DED. Short-term changes in symptoms and signs of dry eye disease, as well as tear concentrations of MUC5AC and MUC1, were evaluated before and 30 min after instillation of DQS or REB.

Methods

This prospective, randomized, double-masked, paired-eye interventional trial was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Korea University Anam Hospital (IRB No. 2022AN0535) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice. All patients provided informed consent at the time of enrollment. Patients were recruited at a single center from October 2022 to August 2024. This study was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT07297316; first posted date 22/12/2025).

Patients older than 19 who were diagnosed with DED according to the Korean Dry Eye Society (KDES) were included6. The inclusion criteria required the presence of at least one dry eye symptom and a mean TBUT < 7 s in both eyes. Exclusion criteria included a history of Sjogren’s syndrome, prescription history of systemic steroids or immunosuppressants, prior ocular surgery within six months of enrollment, treatment with DQS or REB eye drops within 2 weeks of enrollment, treatment with punctal plug within 1 month of enrollment, intraocular pressure exceeding 25 mmHg, usage of contact lens during the study, active ocular infection, and pregnancy or breast-feeding. The target sample size was set at 30 participants based on feasibility considerations without formal a priori sample size calculation. The paired-eye design increases statistical efficiency by reducing inter-subject variability, and the short-duration protocol (30 min) minimizes dropout risk.

Patients were randomly assigned to receive 3% DQS (Diquas, Santen Pharm, Osaka, Japan) in one eye and 2% REB (Rebaeye, Kukje Pharm, Seongnam, Korea) in the contralateral eye. A clinical research nurse performed randomization using a computer-generated random sequence, without blocking or stratification, and administered the assigned eye drops. The allocation sequence was concealed from the examiner, and both the examiner and patients were masked to the treatment assignment. We measured and compared the visual analog scale (VAS) score, TBUT, ocular staining score (OSS, NEI scale, 0–15), tear meniscus height (TMH), lipid layer thickness (LLT), corneal sensitivity (esthesiometer), and tear mucin levels before and 30 min after the instillation of eye drops. The assessments were performed in the following order, from least to most invasive: VAS symptom assessment, LLT and TMH, TBUT and OSS, corneal sensitivity, and tear collection. The VAS assessed overall ocular dryness symptom as a single composite score on a 0–10 cm scale (0 = no discomfort, 10 = maximum discomfort). TMH and LLT were measured using the IDRA® Ocular surface analyzer (SBM Sistemi, Orbassano, Italy). TBUT was measured using a fluorescein strip (Haag-Streit, Koeniz, Switzerland) wetted with one drop of normal saline and applied to the inferior conjunctival fornix. Under cobalt blue slit lamp illumination, the mean of three consecutive measurements was recorded. Corneal sensitivity was assessed using a Cochet-Bonnet esthesiometer (Luneau Ophtalmologie, Chartres, France). The detection threshold was defined as the longest nylon monofilament length (starting from 6.0 cm) perceived upon perpendicular application to the central cornea. Additionally, the concentrations of MUC1 and MUC5AC in tears were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) before and 30 min after instillation [#EH0406, FineTest (MUC1), and #EEL062, Invitrogen (MUC5AC)]. Tear samples were collected by irrigating and aspirating the conjunctival sac with a 200 μL micropipette filled with saline, taking care not to touch the ocular surface or induce reflex tearing. All ELISA quantifications were performed in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. The reaction was stopped with the stop solution, and the absorbances were read at 450 nm using a microplate reader. The concentration of proteins was calculated by the standard curve [MUC1; Log(y) = − 1.612 + 0.651 * Log (x) (R2 = 0.994), MUC5ac; Y = 4.232 + (0.067–4.232)/1 + (X/5.896)1.087 (R2 = 1.000)].

Patients also completed a tolerability questionnaire, and drug tolerability was compared between the two treatments. Eight symptoms (stinging/burning, sticky sensation, itching, blurred vision, foreign body sensation, dryness, light sensitivity, pain or soreness) were graded from score 0 to 3 (0, no symptom; 1, mild; 2, moderate; 3, severe) after instillation of eye drops.

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism version 10.3.0 (GraphPad Software, Boston, MA, USA). All randomized patients (n = 30) were included in the analysis. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). All data were first assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For comparisons of DED parameters and tear molecules before and after instillation within each group, a paired t-test was employed. Given the paired-eye design, comparisons between the DQS and REB groups with regard to changes in DED parameters and tear molecules were also conducted using the paired t-test33. The comparison of tolerability symptoms scores between the treatment groups was analyzed with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance for all analyses.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that DQS and REB elicited comparable immediate therapeutic effects 30 min after instillation in patients with DED, particularly in symptom relief and tear film stability. Both DQS and REB treatments led to a significant increase in tear MUC5AC levels at 30 min, but tear MUC1 levels were not notably affected. There were no significant differences in tolerability between the two groups, with similar proportions (about 15–20%) of patients in the DQS and REB groups reporting foreign body sensation, burning, and dryness upon instillation.

Both DQS and REB improved symptoms and TBUT 30 min after instillation, indicating an immediate clinical benefit. DQS, a P2Y2 purinergic receptor agonist, stimulates fluid secretion from conjunctival epithelium via calcium-dependent activation of chloride ion channels, as well as enhance mucin secretion from conjunctival goblet cells and membrane-associated mucins from the corneal epithelium2,4,34. P2Y2 receptors are also known to be expressed in the meibomian gland ductal cells, and experimental and clinical studies have demonstrated that DQS enhances meibomian gland lipid production and increases tear film lipid layer thickness8,35,36. Numerous clinical studies have shown mid- to long-term effects of DQS in improving symptoms and signs of DED37,38. Consistent with our findings on immediate efficacy, a prior study showed that DQS reduced symptoms of dryness, itching, and ocular fatigue within 1 h of administration8. Furthermore, compared to artificial tears, DQS significantly increased tear volume from 5 to 30 min after instillation in a clinical study39. Moreover, DQS significantly increased TBUT as early as 5 min after instillation, and another study showed an increase in non-invasive TBUT that lasted up to 90 minutes8,40. These findings support that not only does DQS improve tear film stability over time, but it also immediately shows clinical efficacy that rapidly alleviates symptoms.

REB, a quinolinone derivative, was originally developed as a mucoprotective agent for gastric ulcers, which was later reformulated as a topical ophthalmic preparation due to its mucin-secreting properties41. In addition to promoting mucin secretion via activation of EGFR and downstream MAPK/ERK and Akt signaling pathways, REB also exerts anti-inflammatory effects by attenuating inflammatory cytokine levels and reducing inflammatory cell infiltration on the ocular surface42–45. Clinical studies have reported amelioration of dry eye symptoms, reduced ocular staining, and increased TBUT at 2 weeks of starting REB treatment46,47. However, to the best of our knowledge, no studies have evaluated the immediate clinical effects of REB. Our results demonstrated that REB also produced rapid clinical effects, with improvements in TBUT and symptoms comparable to those observed with DQS. Notably, the change in VAS score was more prominent in the DQS group compared to the REB group, which may be due to the mucin, water, and lipid-secreting properties of DQS contributing to more immediate symptom relief. Nevertheless, both mucin secretagogues showed comparable immediate efficacy, which may enhance patient adherence. We speculate that these immediate effects are likely mediated by mucin-secreting activity.

Both DQS and REB instillation induced an increase in tear levels of MUC5AC after 30 min. MUC5AC is a major gel-forming mucin secreted by goblet cells, providing lubrication to the ocular surface and maintaining tear film stability48. Regarding DQS, previous animal studies have reported increased tear MUC5AC concentrations as early as 15 min post-instillation2,27. Also, an in vitro study using human conjunctival epithelial cells demonstrated increased expression of MUC5AC following DQS treatment throughout a 1- to 24-h period49. Moreover, a clinical study on healthy participants showed increased mucin-like substances in the tear film 5 min after DQS treatment50. In line with these past reports, this study demonstrated that tear levels of MUC5AC increased 30 min after DQS instillation, indicating an immediate mucin-secreting effect. As for REB, an in vivo study in rabbits failed to show changes in tear MUC5AC levels 15 min post-instillation27. On the other hand, an in vitro study using conjunctival epithelial cells from SOD1 knockout mice demonstrated that REB treatment increased MUC5AC mRNA levels after 24 hours51. Mouse experimental studies also demonstrated increased MUC5AC mRNA levels 1–2 weeks after REB treatment5,34. A previous human study showed that for Sjogren’s disease-related dry eye patients, REB treatment showed increased tear MUC5AC at 8 weeks52. While previous studies did not directly show immediate mucin-releasing effects of REB, this study showed that 30 min after REB treatment, tear MUC5AC levels were significantly increased. This may be due to the differences in measurement timing of MUC5AC or may be attributed to species-specific differences in drug response.

While tear MUC5AC was increased following both treatments, MUC1 levels remained unchanged. MUC1 is a type of transmembrane mucin that forms the glycocalyx in the corneal and conjunctival epithelium, making a protective barrier and preventing tear break-up53,54. Experimental data have shown increased MUC1 expression for both DQS and REB. Human conjunctival epithelial cells showed increased mRNA levels of MUC1 24 h after DQS treatment49. REB treatment also increased mRNA levels of MUC1 in human corneal epithelial cells after 2–3 hours55. In vivo mouse studies showed that 1-week DQS treatment increased MUC1 mRNA, but not REB34. In this study, neither DQS nor REB treatment induced increased tear MUC1 levels at 30 min, likely because MUC1, unlike MUC5AC, is expressed on the epithelial cells rather than secreted directly into the tear film. Since MUC1 is a transmembrane mucin, the time needed for the newly synthesized glycoprotein to manifest an observable increase in the tear film may exceed 30 min.

The rapid improvement in symptoms and signs following the instillation of DQS and REB is clinically significant for both patient satisfaction and therapeutic outcomes in DED. DED patients frequently discontinue treatment due to delayed symptom relief. For instance, topical CsA has a delayed onset of action of several months, and patient non-adherence rate is reported to reach 20–30%56–58. In contrast, the symptomatic relief provided by DQS and REB would enhance patient adherence and therapeutic persistence. Additionally, early symptom improvement correlates with faster recovery of visual function in DED32. While patients experienced instillation-related discomfort, such as burning and foreign body sensation, the incidence (around 15–20%) is comparable to that reported with topical cyclosporine A56. Notably, the tolerability symptom scores were not significantly different between DQS and REB groups. We believe their immediate benefits regarding symptom relief and tear film stability are highly relevant to real-world clinical practice.

This study has several limitations. The sample size was relatively small, and the subjects were limited to non-Sjogren dry eye patients. A larger sample size would benefit in terms of generalizability, and an inflammatory type of DED may show different results. Regarding study design, though a paired-eye design was employed to minimize inter-subject variability, patients’ reporting of subjective symptoms may have been affected by bilateral comparison, potentially compromising the independence of assessments between eyes. Also, tear mucin levels were assessed only at the protein level in the tear fluid, and impression cytology with mRNA expression analysis may have provided more insights. Moreover, comparison with an artificial tear control group could have provided a distinction between specific pharmacological actions of the study drugs and general effects of ocular surface hydration. Despite these limitations, this study has its strengths in that it directly compared the immediate clinical effects of two mucin secretagogues while also investigating mucin production.

In conclusion, this study demonstrated that both DQS and REB provide similar rapid therapeutic effects, such as immediate symptom relief, tear film stabilization, and enhanced mucin secretion. These rapid effects may contribute to improved treatment adherence and enhanced patient quality of life in clinical practice.

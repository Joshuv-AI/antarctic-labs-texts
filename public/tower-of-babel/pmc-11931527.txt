Two-Photon and Three-Photon Circular Dichroism of Au38 Gold Nanoclusters Enantiomers
Authors: Patryk Obstarczyk, Rania Kazan, Thomas Bürgi, Marek Samoć, Joanna Olesiak-Bańska
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
While circular dichroism (CD) and optical activity (OA) are well established as optical effects used in characterization of chiral media, harmonic generation and multiphoton circular dichroism are increasingly seen as new, convenient ways of exploring chirality thanks to their operation in the near-infrared range of wavelengths. However, quantitative data about two-photon circular dichroism (2PCD) of organic and inorganic materials are scarce, and even less can be found about three-photon circular dichroism (3PCD). Here, we show that both 2PCD and 3PCD can be readily detected in chiral atomically precise gold nanoclusters via polarimetric Z-scan technique. We provide quantitative data on 2PCD and 3PCD of both enantiomers of Au38(PET)24 nanoclusters, which exhibit extraordinary chiroptical properties arising from the interplay of several levels of molecular organization. Interestingly, the corresponding two- and three-photon dissymmetry factors of Au38(PET)24 enantiomers are significantly enhanced in comparison to the one-photon CD by factors of 178 and 217, respectively.

The presence of chiral structures results in the emergence of chiro-optical properties of materials defined as optical rotatory dispersion (ORD) and circular dichroism (CD). The latter property can be quantified as the difference between the absorbance for left-handed and right-handed circularly polarized light (CD = AL – AR), and a dimensionless anisotropy (dissymmetry) factor can be defined as . Usually the values of the g factor of chiral organic molecules, including those of biological significance (e.g., proteins, sugars, nucleic acids), are relatively small, on the order of 10–4 and the effect is located in the UV range of wavelengths, which poses significant limitations to its practical usefulness. A paradigm change would be shifting the measurement range for CD to NIR, by employing, similar to the case of luminescence-based techniques used in spectroscopy and microscopy, multiphoton absorption instead of the usual one-photon absorption. The theoretical descriptions of two-photon circular dichroism (2PCD) were introduced almost 50 years ago, independently by Tinoco1 and Power;2 however, the extant information on such effect is quite scarce. To evaluate the difference in the two-photon absorption coefficient for left- and right-handed circularly polarized light, apart from transition electric dipole moments, also transition magnetic dipole moments and transition electric quadrupole moments of a studied object are of importance, which makes the theoretical computations complex. The experimental studies of 2PCD have involved rare earth containing crystals,3 organics,4 among them helicenes,5 and chiral nanoparticles.6,7 An interesting issue is whether the 2PCD effects are strong enough to provide a real advantage over traditional CD measurements. Markowicz et al.4 reported the anisotropy factor of 2PCD as high as 3 × 10–3 in an organic dye, and two-photon anisotropy factors on the order of 10–2 have been detected in other systems, e.g., polyfuorene8 and nanocomposite composed of quantum dots and chiral polymer.9 Recently, we reported a ∼300-fold enhancement of the 2PCD dissymmetry with respect to the one-photon g-factor for gold nanoclusters.6,7 It is of interest to extend the studies to the case of three-photon circular dichroism (3PCD) which was already theoretically described for helicenes,10 but no experiments on 3PCD have been presented yet, even though three-photon absorption itself has been applied, e.g., as a mechanism leading to up-conversion lasing11 and in three-photon deep tissue imaging.12

Recently, there has been much interest in the chirality of nanomaterials, which is a rapidly developing area in the modern materials science13 and may lead to the development of novel nanophotonic devices.14 Among various enantioselective platforms, atomically precise gold nanoclusters (APNCs) are particularly beneficial. They are composed of a metal core surrounded by staple-like motifs with a general formula of Aux(SR)y, where SR stands for a thiolate ligand.15 The architecture of such structures may induce chirality at distinct levels:16 chiral arrangement of staples, chiral arrangement of kernel, chiral arrangement of protecting ligands or just the presence of intrinsically chiral ligands.17 Therefore, the chiro-optical effects of APNCs are hierarchical in nature, as usually more than one component of a cluster architecture contributes to the phenomenon.18 APNCs have already been shown to present strong nonlinear optical properties.19−21 To quantify two-photon absorption (2PA) of a particular material, the 2PA cross-section, σ2 (expressed in GM: 10–50 cm4 s photon–1 molecule–1) is used.22

Here, we probe the nonlinear chiro-optical properties of two enantiomers of Au38(PET)24 (PET: 2-phenylethanethiol) nanoclusters.23,24 Two-photon absorption of this nanocluster was reported at 1028 nm from femtosecond pump/probe transient absorption studies.25 Moreover, studies of Au38(DDT)24 (DDT = 1-dodecanethiol) performed with nanosecond laser pulses revealed the presence of both two-photon absorption and saturable absorption for 532–690 nm wavelength range.26

Herein, the racemic mixture of Au38(PET)24 nanoclusters was synthesized and two enantiomers (E1, E2) were separated via HPLC (Figure S1), as described in the Supporting Information. Au38(PET)24 is composed of 23 gold atoms constituting the achiral core and stabilized by staple-like motifs. Six long Au2(SR)3 and 3 short Au(SR)2 motifs are arranged in a chiral manner: either clock-wise or anticlockwise (Figure 1a).27,28 The corresponding enantiomers were dissolved in toluene, and their UV–vis–NIR as well as CD spectra were recorded (Figure 1b). Au38(PET)24 enantiomers are characterized by the same and distinctive molecular-like transitions at 490, 520, 560, 620, 750, and 1050 nm.23

The CD spectra of Au38(PET)24 enantiomers are characterized by the mirroring bands (Figure 1b), namely, for E1, at 308 (+), 345 (−), 393 (−), 440 (+), 479 (−) 564 (+), 629 (−), and 747 (+) nm. The CD band at 450 nm (negative for E2 and positive for E1) was found to possess anisotropy factors equal to −1.6 × 10–3 and +1.5 × 10–3, respectively, which is consistent with the literature.24,29 The intramolecular ligand to metal charge transfer mechanism20 was reported for numerous cluster systems. We hypothesize that chiral arrangement of staples in Au38, with achiral ligands, might change the electric and magnetic dipole transitions in the entire structure; thus, the CD effect, overall, arises from intermixed transitions involving the Au 6s electrons as well as those from the sulfur that connects the ligand body to the cluster.

The nonlinear optical properties of Au38(PET)24 enantiomers were determined with the Z-scan technique employing an amplified tunable fs laser source.31 The measurements performed in a wide range of wavelengths led to the determination of two- (σ2) and three-photon (σ3) absorption cross-sections (Figure S2). By analyzing the shapes of Z-scan traces, two wavelength regions were identified: (1) the region of 2PA (900–1300 nm) and (2) the region of 3PA (1300–1400 nm). The highest measured σ2 value was equal to 630 ± 90 GM at 1275 nm. Even higher σ2 values may be expected <900 nm; however, in that wavelength range, a strong one-photon absorption (1PA) is still present, thus the double resonance effect and excited state absorption may contribute to observed nonlinearities.25,31,32 The maximum σ3 was equal to 1750 × 10–80 cm6 s2 at 1350 nm.

In order to probe the nonlinear chiro-optical properties of Au38(PET)24, the circularly polarized fs laser beam was employed for the Z-scan experiments (Figure S3). Basing on the circular dichroism spectra and the solvent absorption characteristics (Figure S4), we selected to investigate 2PCD at 900 nm and 3PCD at 1350 nm, which both correspond to the transition at 450 nm in the 1PA spectrum. θ2PCD and θ3PCD of Au38(PET)24 enantiomers are 178 and 217 times higher than the corresponding one-photon g-factors, respectively. Previously6,7 we indicated similarly enhanced values of θ2PCD (in comparison to 1PCD g-factor) observed for other gold nanoclusters: Au25[(Capt)18]− and AuATT with l- or d-Arg ligands. 2PCD was also reported for different groups of materials where such enhancement was not observed, e.g., for rare earth ions containing crystals (i.e., Na3[Gd(C4H4O5)3]·2NaClO4·6H2O),3 and helicenes.5 The θ2PCD and θ3PCD appear to be coherent, in terms of signs (negative for E2 and positive for E1), which also corresponds with the sign of 1P CD at the selected wavelength and suggests that the transitions take place between energy levels of similar origin. Based on DFT computations 1P CD for low excitation energies (below 2.2 eV) of Au38(SR)24 (R = C6H13 and C12H25) is based on chiral arrangement of the gold–thiolate ligand shell around the bi-icosahedral Au23 core.28 Recent theoretical studies on Au38PET24 circular dichroism stress that, in order to reproduce experimental data in the range <500 nm, incorporation of ligands, solvent and conformational dynamics of ligands is essential.34 Thus significant contribution of the ligand–gold interface to the chiro-optic response of such nanostructures is confirmed in one-photon transitions regime,18 but it is still challenging to extend it to the 2P and 3P cases.

Substantial differences between σ2 and σ3 values of the Au38(PET)24 enantiomer under linear and circular excitation were observed. The presence of two-photon linear-circular dichroism is not unexpected,35 as multiphoton absorption in an isotropic system in which model chromophores are randomly oriented was described by performing averaging of the appropriate products of orientation cosines. For E1 the corresponding values of θ2PLCD and θ3PLCD are equal to 0.457 and −1.556, respectively. These values are in good agreement with our previously studied Au25 cluster system with chiral ligand and achiral core.6 The ratio of the cross-sections for circularly and linearly polarized light (Ω) is equal to 0.69 and 9.01, for 2PA and 3PA, respectively. Ω < 1, which we registered for 2PA is expected for a transition to the state of the same symmetry as the ground state.36 However, for the 3-photon absorption the corresponding Ω value is equal to 9.01, which may suggest change of symmetry upon excitation. Since there is no comprehensive description of higher-order nonlinear optical properties of gold nanoclusters yet, any further discussion of such an experimental result must be limited.20,21

Our investigations show that the chiral Au38 nanoclusters with achiral ligands already present strong one-photon CD and even stronger two- and three-photon CD. Strong multiphoton circular dichroism of gold nanoclusters offers prospective applications in chirality detection and light modulation in a wide range of NIR wavelengths. Moreover, as the origin of Au38 chirality lies in chiral pattering of staple-like motifs, a broad variety of achiral ligands can also be used to stabilize the surface, and yet, this particular system will remain optically active. Our results are an excellent starting point to systematical studies on multiphoton chiro-optical properties by tuning the properties of nanoparticles with a range of (a)chiral thiol ligands.

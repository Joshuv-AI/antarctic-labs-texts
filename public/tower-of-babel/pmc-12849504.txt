Incident hypertension among adults on ART in Malawi: a nested cohort study
Authors: Osward M Nyirenda, Brewster Moyo, Titus Divala, Andrea Buchwald, Nginache Nampota, Randy Mungwira, Wongani Nyangulu, Felix Mkandawire, Edson Mwinjiwa, Jane Mallewa, Matthew B Laurens, Miriam K Laufer, Joep J van Oosterhout
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAs populations on antiretroviral therapy (ART) in sub-Saharan Africa are aging, hypertension has become an increasingly important comorbidity among people living with HIV (PLHIV). To address a paucity of data from sub-Saharan Africa on the incidence of hypertension among PLHIV, we conducted a cohort study in Malawi to assess the frequency and correlates of incident hypertension among PLHIV established on ART.MethodsIn this prospective cohort study nested in a clinical trial, we followed participants without prevalent hypertension at enrolment and routinely screened their blood pressure (BP). In the parent study, clinically stable adult (≥ 18 years) PLHIV on ART with HIV-RNA ≤ 400 copies/mL and CD4 count ≥ 250/mm3 were randomized to daily trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TS), weekly chloroquine (CQ), or no prophylaxis. We defined hypertension as systolic BP of ≥ 140 mmHg and diastolic BP ≥ 90 mmHg obtained on two consecutive clinic visits. Poisson regression analysis was used to determine the incidence of hypertension, using univariate and multivariate analyses designed based on a Directed Acyclic Graph to identify key predictors of hypertension.Results1,240 PLHIV (76.4% female, median age 37 years, median duration on ART 3 years, 92.2% on efavirenz-based regimens) were followed from December 2012 to July 2018, accumulating 3,676 person-years of observation (PYO). The incidence of hypertension was 36.9 per 1,000 PYO (95% confidence interval [CI]: 31.0, 43.8), with 40% of diagnoses made before the age of 40 years and the highest incidence observed in the first calendar year of the study. Higher age (incidence rate ratio [IRR] = 1.05, 95%-CI 1.03, 1.07), male sex (IRR = 1,49, 95%-CI 1, 02–2,15; p = 0.029), being overweight (aIRR = 2.55, 95%-CI: 1.27, 5.47) and obese (aIRR = 5.08, 95%-CI; 2.12, 12.18) and being on a non-efavirenz-based ART regimen (aIRR = 2.25, 95%-CI: 1.37, 3.53) were predictors of incident hypertension. Receiving TS (aIRR = 0.42, 95%-CI 0.26, 0.65) compared to receiving no prophylaxis reduced risk of incident hypertension.ConclusionsThe incidence of hypertension among PLHIV who were stable on longer-term ART was high, justifying routine BP screening at ART clinics. Identified predictors may help targeting screening. A newly observed protective effect of TS prophylaxis on incident hypertension needs confirmation.Keywords: Hypertension, Incidence, People living with HIV, ART, Malawi, Africa, Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole prophylaxis

Background

While the global health burden of HIV remains high [1], antiretroviral therapy (ART) has strongly reduced HIV-related mortality and improved the quality of life for people living with HIV (PLHIV) [2]. The prevalence of cardiovascular disease (CVD) and hypertension is increasing among PLHIV, primarily due to aging of the population on ART [3]. The association between HIV and hypertension is complex and is influenced by a range of factors. The success of ART has led to longer exposures to HIV-related inflammation, traditional CVD risk factors and ART medications, contributing to a rise in hypertension cases [4–6]. Some studies have shown that the risk of hypertension in PLHIV is independently associated with the duration of HIV infection [7], some indicated that the risk increases with duration on ART [8], yet others found no association between hypertension and ART exposure [9]. Among PLHIV from the sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) region, studies identified several factors associated with hypertension, including age, sex, BMI and renal insufficiency, as well as a low CD4 nadir and the type of ART regimen [10–14].

United Nations Sustainable Development Goal number 3, target 3.4, aims to reduce premature mortality from non-communicable diseases (NCDs) by one-third [15]. To achieve this goal, a better understanding of the determinants and risk factors of hypertension in PLHIV, the most common NCD co-morbidity, is needed so that healthcare resources can be channeled and strategies developed to maximize prevention and treatment of hypertension among individuals at high risk.

Malawi has an estimated HIV prevalence of 8.9% among adults [16]. By 2023, 991,600 people were estimated to live with HIV, of whom 927,310 (93.5%) were on ART [17]. Existing literature from cross-sectional studies has demonstrated a high prevalence of hypertension in PLHIV in Malawi [18, 19]. However, few prospective studies from Malawi and the sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) region have investigated hypertension incidence, which provides insights into new cases over time on ART and into the association between variables that are associated with hypertension. We therefore studied hypertension incidence and explored risk factors for incident hypertension in a well-characterized cohort of adults participating in a trial of antimicrobial prophylaxis, while stable on ART.

Methods and materials

Parent study

We conducted a cohort study nested within a randomized clinical trial (Clinical Trials Registration NCT01650558, registration date 6 July 2012) that took place from December 2012 to July 2018 for enrolment and follow up [20]. In the trial, PLHIV aged 18 years or older, on ART for ≥ 6 months, with CD4 count of ≥ 250 cells/µL, viral load < 400 copies/mL, and without evidence of active WHO clinical stage 3 or 4 conditions were recruited and randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to continue standard of care of daily trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TS) prophylaxis, discontinue TS prophylaxis and begin weekly chloroquine (CQ) prophylaxis, or no prophylaxis. Additional inclusion/exclusion criteria were published previously [20]. Most relevantly for this analysis, pregnant women and individuals with creatinine concentration > 3.3 mg/dL (291.7µmol/L) for men and > 2.7 mg/dL (238.7µmol/L) for women were excluded from the study [20, 21].

The study took place at Ndirande Health Center, Blantyre district, and Zomba Central Hospital, Zomba district. During the study period, the standardized first-line ART for this population was a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI), mainly efavirenz with nevirapine as an alternative, and two nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors, i.e., lamivudine plus either stavudine, tenofovir, or zidovudine. Second-line treatment was protease inhibitor-based. Dolutegravir was not yet available at the time of the study. Data was collected on paper forms and then transcribed into an electronic data system.

Hypertension diagnosis and definition of incident hypertension

We used high blood pressure (BP) thresholds per World Health Organization and International Society of Hypertension guidelines [22] as systolic BP of ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic BP ≥ 90 mmHg. Standardized BP measurements using electronic monitors were performed during screening and enrollment visits, 2 to 3 weeks after screening. Once enrolled, BP was obtained every month during routine visits until six months, then every three months, and on any additional unscheduled visit. Participants were instructed to rest for at least 5 min after clinic arrival before BP measurement. If a first reading was high, a second reading was done and if still high, the second reading was transcribed on paper case report forms, and the participant was counseled on lifestyle changes. We considered an event prevalent hypertension if a hypertension diagnosis was confirmed at the screening and enrollment visits or if participants had a previous history of hypertension and were on antihypertensive medications at enrollment, regardless of BP level. We excluded participants with prevalent hypertension from this longitudinal analysis. Incident hypertension was defined as two consecutive visits with high blood pressure among participants without prevalent hypertension at enrolment. Participants who reported having been diagnosed with new-onset hypertension at another health facility after enrolment in the study were considered to have incident hypertension.

Assessment of covariates

We collected data on HIV treatment and other covariates from study records and government ART clinic records (from the latter source: duration on ART, ART regimen and WHO clinical stage). Information from the two sources was linked through the unique individual ART number. We categorized body mass index (BMI) as underweight (< 18.5 kg/m²), normal (18.5 to 24.9 kg/m²), overweight (25·0 to 29·9 kg/m²), and obesity (30·0 kg/m² or more) [23]. We assessed renal function by estimating the glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) based on the serum creatinine concentration, using the Modification of Diet in Renal Disease equation [24]. We then categorized renal function into 2 groups according to the U.S. National Kidney Foundation’s Kidney Disease Outcome Quality Initiative (K/DOQI): normal (eGFR ≥ 90mL/min) and mild, moderate, or severe insufficiency (eGFR < 90mL/min) [25]. We measured CD4 count using flow cytometry (Becton Dickenson FACS count) and categorized CD4 count results into 2 groups (< 350 or ≥ 350 cells/µL) [26]. We defined anemia as a hemoglobin (Hb) concentration of < 10 mg/dL, irrespective of sex. We determined annual weight gain (in kg/year), defined as weight gain divided by follow up time, with numerator and denominator truncated at the point of hypertension diagnosis among incident hypertension cases. Apart from weight gain, all other covariates were assessed at enrolment.

Statistical analysis

We used descriptive statistics to outline baseline characteristics by study site and sex. We summarized continuous variables as medians with their interquartile range (IQR) and categorical variables as numbers and percentages. We used Poisson regression to determine the incidence of hypertension per person-years of study follow-up with 95% confidence intervals (95%-CI). We also determined incidence rate ratios to assess associations between baseline characteristics and incident hypertension. We used univariate and multivariate analyses to determine the factors associated with incident hypertension. We developed adjusted multivariate Poisson regression models based on causal assumptions about the relationship between potential predictor variables and incident hypertension using a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) (Fig. 1). Adjusted models were designed separately for each potential predictor variable, adjusting for minimally sufficient confounders based on the DAG. Study arm was adjusted for age, CD4 count, eGFR, BMI, sex and ART regimen; eGFR, BMI and annual weight gain were adjusted for age and sex and ART regimen was adjusted for age. We performed two-sided statistical tests, and the significance level was set at 0.05. We used R software version 4.4.2 in all statistical analyses.

Results

Of 1,499 participants who were enrolled from December 2012 to August 2016, 259 were hypertensive at baseline (prevalence 17.3%, 95% CI: 15.4–19.3%) and were excluded from further analysis. The remaining 1,240 (82.8%) participants accumulated 3,676.5 person-years of observation (PYO) between December 2012 and July 2018. Among 136 (11%) individuals with newly developed hypertension during follow-up, 115 (84.6%) were alive and contributed 140.9 PYO, 5 (3.7%) died and contributed 4.2 PYO, 15 (11%) transferred to other clinics and contributed 13.3 PYO, while 1 (0.7%) participant was lost to follow-up and contributed 0.1 PYO. Among 1,104 participants without incident hypertension, 911 (82.6%) participants were alive and contributed 3,130.1 PYO, 11 (1%) died and contributed 17.9 PYO, 147 (13.2%) transferred to other clinics and contributed 289.0 PYO, and 25 (2.2%) were lost to follow-up and contributed 64.7 PYO.

Baseline characteristics

Table 1 shows the baseline characteristics of participants followed in the longitudinal analysis. Missing covariate data were extremely rare: one missing result for CD4, Hb, ART regimen and WHO stage; four missing results for eGFR and five for BMI. The median age was 37 years (IQR 32, 44); 76.4% were female, and all had African ancestry. Most participants (71%) had a normal BMI, while 14.4% were overweight and 3.3% obese. The median CD4 count was 525 (IQR 392, 707) cells/µL, and 38.9% were in WHO clinical stages 3 and 4. In 95.1% of participants, the basal eGFR was normal, and 3.3% were anemic, all of whom were female. The median duration on ART was 3 years (IQR 1, 6). Most participants (1,143; 92.2%) were on two nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTIs) plus efavirenz, 85 (6.9%) were on two NRTIs plus nevirapine and 12 (1.0%) were on two NRTIs plus a protease inhibitor. Males had a higher mean age (42 years vs. 36 years), higher percentage with a CD4 count < 350 cells/µL (36.5% vs. 11.3%), were more often in WHO clinical stage 3 (38.0% vs. 29.9%) and stage 4 (10.6% vs. 6.0%) and were less commonly overweight (3.8% vs. 17.7%) or obese (0% vs. 4.3%) compared to females. Compared to participants from the Ndirande study site, Zomba site participants had a higher median age (41 vs. 35 years), a longer median duration on ART (6 years vs. 2 years), a lower percentage with a CD4 count < 350 cells/µL (10.5% vs. 20.1%) and included more participants on non-efavirenz-based ART regimens (17.4% vs. 4.0%).

Age at hypertension diagnosis among participants with incident hypertension

The incidence rate was highest in 2013, the first year of the study: 140.7 per 1000 PYO (95%-CI: 69.9, 197.7), and reduced in subsequent years: 39.2 per 1000 PYO (95%-CI: 24.6, 59.4) in 2014; 14.5 per 1000 PYO (95%-CI: 6.9, 26.6) in 2015; 34.5 per 1000 PYO (95%-CI: 24.0, 48.0) in 2016; 24.8 per 1000 PYO (95%-CI: 16.3, 36.0) in 2017 and 21.7 per 1000 PYO (95%-CI: 9.9, 41.2) in 2018.

In univariable analyses, the following factors increased the risk of incident hypertension: longer duration on ART (IRR 1.06, 95%-CI 1.00, 1.12); being on a non-efavirenz-based regimen (IRR 2.88, 95%-CI 1.81, 4.59); higher age (IRR 1.05 95%-CI 1.03, 1.07), male sex (IRR 1.49 95%-CI 1.04, 2.15); presence of mild, moderate or severe renal insufficiency (IRR 1.82, 95%-CI 1.01, 3.30); being in WHO clinical stage 2 (IRR 1.82, 95%-CI 1.08, 3.07) compared to stage 1; and being overweight (IRR 1.83, 95%-CI 0.91, 3.70) or obese (IRR 3.84 95%-CI 1.66, 8.87) compared to being underweight. Being on the TS arm reduced the risk of incident hypertension (IRR 0.41, 95% CI 0.26, 0.64) compared to being on the no prophylaxis arm. In the adjusted analysis, being on a non-efavirenz-based ART regimen (aIRR 2.25, 95%-CI 1.37, 3.53); being overweight (aIRR 2.55, 95%-CI 1.27, 5.47) or obese (aIRR 5.08, 95%-CI 2.12, 12.18) compared to being underweight, remained significant risk factors for incident hypertension, while being on TS (aIRR 0.42 95%-CI 0.26, 0.65) significantly reduced the risk of incident hypertension (Table 2).

Discussion

In the context of a randomized controlled trial, we prospectively followed a well-characterized cohort of Malawian adults on ART who were clinically stable, virally controlled and immune reconstituted at enrolment, to determine the incidence of hypertension over a 5-year period. We found that 11% of 1,240 participants newly developed hypertension, with an incidence rate of 36.9 per 1000 PYO. Older age, male sex, being overweight or obese, renal insufficiency, and being on a non-efavirenz-based ART regimen were risk factors, while being on TS prophylaxis protected against hypertension.

The increasing impact of hypertension on morbidity and mortality among PLHIV in SSA is well recognized. A large amount of data from cross sectional studies among adult PLHIV from SSA is available, with a pooled hypertension prevalence of 21.9% in a recent, large systematic review [10]. In studies from Malawi, prevalence rates varied between 19.3% and 23.7% [18, 19]. Few studies have determined hypertension incidence among PLHIV on ART and its risk factors and these studies have shown highly variable results. A single, earlier, prospective study is available from Malawi, which included adults initiating tenofovir-lamivudine-efavirenz and TS prophylaxis at an urban clinic in the capital Lilongwe. Over 3 years of follow up, 4.5% had newly developed hypertension. However, losses to follow up were high (35.4%), making results difficult to compare with our finding (11% in 5 years) [27]. In a cohort of 733 adults on long-term ART in Kampala, Uganda, the hypertension incidence rate was 24.8 per 1000 PYO, somewhat lower than in our study [11]. However, in an earlier study from rural Uganda of 3,389 adults starting ART, incidence was much higher (111.5 per 1000 PYO) [12] and among 834 ART-naïve rural Tanzanians, of whom 75% started ART during follow up, hypertension incidence was 120.0 per 1000 PYO [13]. In a study from South Africa, hypertension incidence among 60,570 ART initiators was 54 per 1000 PYO [14]. Several factors may influence the variations in hypertension incidence between studies from the region. First, there are differences in the exclusion of individuals with prevalent hypertension at enrolment. Some studies excluded only those on antihypertensive medication, while retaining individuals with an initial high BP, who would then count as new hypertension if BP was elevated on a second visit. In our cohort, BP was measured on at least one occasion before the enrolment visit, scrutinizing the exclusion of prevalent hypertension cases. Second, the study populations differed considerably in terms of prevalence of traditional risk factors for hypertension (age, BMI and sex). Further, compared to studies that only enrolled participants on longer-term ART, those that enrolled ART starters observed higher hypertension incidence, probably due to a high prevalence of advanced HIV disease in the study population, among whom renal failure is common [28] and because ART initiators often have substantial weight gain, especially in the first year [29]. One study underlined this impact of ART status at enrolment by comparing the incidence of hypertension between adults (aged 30 + years) initiating ART and those on longer-term ART, at a tertiary hospital in Ghana. The overall incidence rate was 320.1 per 1000 PYO, and incidence was significantly higher among new ART initiators: 476.6 per 1000 PYO, compared to 222.8 per 1000 PYO among those on longer-term ART [30].

In our study, as in the studies from Malawi, Tanzania, South Africa, Ghana and Uganda [23, 24, 26, 27, 30], the impact of traditional risk factors on incident hypertension was confirmed, including higher age, male sex, higher BMI and renal insufficiency. Low nadir CD4 count was only associated with incident hypertension in studies enrolling ART starters, possibly reflecting greater weight gains upon return to health in individuals with more advanced HIV disease [12, 14]. Participants on non-efavirenz-based ART regimens in our study were at higher risk of incident hypertension. These other regimens were either nevirapine- or protease inhibitor-based, as our study took place before the transition to dolutegravir regimens. Recent studies that focused on excessive weight gain in individuals on integrase inhibitor-based ART, found that weight gain is independently associated with incident hypertension, while efavirenz seemed to reduce weight gain [31–33], supporting our findings. The high efavirenz regimen coverage in our cohort, its effect on weight and the fact that participants were stable on longer-term ART may have led to limited variation in weight over time, explaining the lack of association of weight gain during follow up with incident hypertension.

We observed that being in the TS arm strongly protected against incident hypertension. Given that TS prevented bacterial infections and malaria in the clinical trial [21, 34], we hypothesize that TS prophylaxis reduces long-term inflammation among PLHIV. Recent studies have demonstrated that chronic inflammation increases blood pressure and elevates the risk of hypertension through several mechanisms, including endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress and complement and immune cell activation [35–37]. Our study did not measure inflammatory markers and therefore could not determine if these were lower in the TS arm than in the CQ arm, which only provided protection against malaria, and the NP arm (neither protection against malaria, nor bacterial infections). Another possible explanation for the effect of TS on incident hypertension is an intrinsic action of TS on BP. It is long known that trimethoprim acts as an amiloride-like potassium sparing diuretic by inhibiting sodium channels in the renal collecting tubule [38] and it can therefore cause hyponatremia and hyperkalemia [39]. Despite potential BP reducing mechanisms, a hypertension preventing effect of TS prophylaxis has not been previously described in clinical studies as far as we know. A retrospective study of Nigerian ART starters without prevalent hypertension found that TS prophylaxis was independently associated with a shorter time to incident hypertension. However, this study suffered from major limitations, including irregular and infrequent blood pressure measurements that were incompletely documented, missing important hypertension predictors, including BMI and renal function and an unexpectedly very low coverage (39%) of TS prophylaxis among ART starters, suggesting that the risks of selection bias and confounding in this study were high [40]. More high-quality studies are required to confirm a benefit of TS prophylaxis on incident hypertension in our and other settings.

Our study has a number of strengths. As part of a randomized controlled clinical trial, it had high-quality data capture, minimal incompleteness of data over a long follow-up period and very low losses to follow-up, due to active tracing in the community. Blood pressure was always measured according to WHO guidelines and routine clinical care was of consistently high level. There are also some limitations. The parent trial was not designed to investigate hypertension and therefore some relevant variables, such as smoking, physical activity and alcohol consumption, were not collected. We also did not have an HIV-negative control group. We may have underestimated hypertension incidence as individuals with moderate and severe renal insufficiency at baseline were excluded. We did not consider specific hypertension phenotypes in the analysis, such as isolated systolic hypertension. Although the Zomba site included rural participants, the majority of participants were urban residents, restricting extrapolation of the results to other settings. We could not distinguish the effects of nevirapine- and protease inhibitor-based ART on incident hypertension due to small numbers on these regimens. Finally, results may not have been the same in a population on dolutegravir-based ART regimens, which predominate currently.

Conclusions

Incident hypertension was common in adult Malawians who were well-established on ART. Higher age, male sex, and high BMI predicted hypertension. Being on an efavirenz-based regimen protected against incident hypertension. Unexpectedly, TS prophylaxis reduced the risk of incident hypertension. While some biological mechanisms support this finding, further study needs to confirm it, including when TS prophylaxis is given with dolutegravir-based ART.

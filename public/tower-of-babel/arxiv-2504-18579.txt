ZipR1: Reinforcing Token Sparsity in MLLMs
Feng Chen, Yefei He, Lequan Lin, Jing Liu, Bohan Zhuang, [0.2cm] AIML, University of Adelaide, Australia

Abstract
Sparse attention mechanisms aim to reduce computational overhead by selectively processing a subset of salient tokens while preserving model performance. Despite the effectiveness of such designs, how to actively encourage token sparsity of well-posed MLLMs remains under-explored, which fundamentally limits the achievable acceleration effect during inference. In this paper, we propose a simple RL-based post-training method named ZipR1 that treats the token reduction ratio as the efficiency reward and answer accuracy as the performance reward. In this way, our method can jointly alleviate the computation and memory bottlenecks via directly optimizing the inference-consistent efficiency-performance tradeoff. Experimental results demonstrate that ZipR1 can reduce the token ratio of Qwen2/2.5-VL from 80% to 25% with a minimal accuracy reduction on 13 image and video benchmarks 1 1 1 Work in process. .

Introduction

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)
[
3
,
13
,
24
]
have achieved impressive results in tasks such as image captioning and visual question answering
[
5
,
12
]
. However, when processing high-resolution images or videos, the visual encoder often produces an excessive number of visual tokens, severely constraining the generative efficiency of MLLMs
[
2
]
. Existing sparse attention methods
[
2
,
9
,
11
]
exploit the inherent sparsity of attention maps to select a subset of important tokens, largely alleviating the computation bottleneck of prefilling and the memory bottleneck of decoding. However, actively encouraging token sparsity of well-trained models remains under-explored, which fundamentally limits the achievable acceleration effect during inference.

Early attempts at enhancing token sparsity either design new attention architectures or introduce sharpness-inducing losses on attention maps. For example, MOBA and NSA impose a semi-structured sparsity pattern in the attention map via trainable sparse attention. Moreover,
[
7
]
introduces a structured regularization loss that encourages attention to focus on semantically relevant regions, thereby reducing redundancy among tokens. However, these methods typically require training from scratch for MLLMs and often lack inference-time consistency, limiting their practicality in post-training deployment scenarios.

In this paper, we propose a novel post-training method named
ZipR1
that directly optimizes the performance-efficiency tradeoff of MLLMs with the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm
[
21
]
. The optimization pipeline aligns with the inference process, treating final answer accuracy and token reduction ratio as rewards.
Specifically, as shown in Figure
1
, we treat an MLLM with sparse attention as the policy model and the original MLLM as the reference model. The token reduction ratio from sparse attention serves as the efficiency reward, while the corresponding answer accuracy is used as the performance reward. By sampling multiple answers with varying token budgets, we can explore the efficiency-performance tradeoff of different responses and update the policy accordingly.

Our contributions are as follows:

(1) We propose a novel post-training
framework
that explicitly promotes token sparsity in MLLMs, aiming to enhance their achievable efficiency during inference.

(2) We introduce ZipR1, a novel RL-baseed method that directly optimize the efficiency-performance tradeoff of MLLMs.

(3) Experimental results demonstrate that ZipR1 can reduce the token ratio of Qwen2/2.5-VL from 80% to 25% with a minimal performance decline on 13 benchmarks.

2.1
Preliminaries

Token-level sparse attention.

Let
{\mathbf{Q}},{\mathbf{K}},{\mathbf{V}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\ell\times d}
be query, key, and value matrices in the attention mechanism, respectively, where
\ell
is the sequence length and
d
denotes the embedding dimension. In token-level sparse attention, the attention computation is restricted exclusively to a subset of selected tokens in the query and key matrices. Formally, two binary indicator vectors
{\mathbf{m}}_{Q},{\mathbf{m}}_{K}\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}
are introduced to specify the selection, where the value of
1
indicates a selected token, and
0
otherwise. For the query and key matrices, the corresponding masks
\mathbf{M}_{Q},\mathbf{M}_{K}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\ell\times d}
are constructed as follows:

\mathbf{M}_{Q}={\mathbf{m}}_{Q}\cdot{\mathbf{1}}_{d}^{\top},\quad\mathbf{M}_{K}={\mathbf{m}}_{K}\cdot{\mathbf{1}}_{d}^{\top},

(1)

where
{\mathbf{1}}_{d}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a vector of ones. The masks then have rows of ones for selected tokens, and zeros elsewhere. Finally, the output of the sparse attention mechanism is computed by:

\mathbf{\hat{O}}_{\text{sparse}}=\sigma\left(\frac{({\mathbf{Q}}\odot\mathbf{M}_{Q})({\mathbf{K}}\odot\mathbf{M}_{K})^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}\right){\mathbf{V}},

(2)

where
\sigma
is the row-wise
Softmax
activation function, and
\odot
denotes the element-wise matrix multiplication.

The token budget of sparse attention is given by
b=(\|\mathbf{M}_{Q}\|_{0}+\|\mathbf{M}_{K}\|_{0})/d
, where
\|\cdot\|_{0}
is the zero-norm function that counts the number of nonzero elements. In this paper, we use Top-
p
sparse attention to support dynamic adjustment of sparsity concerning distinct layers or tasks and control a theoretical upperbound of error by
(1-p)\times\|{\mathbf{V}}\|

[
14
]
. Mathematically, the query and key masks are tailored to solve the following constrained optimization problem:

\mathbf{M}_{Q}^{*},\mathbf{M}_{K}^{*}=\argmin_{\mathbf{M}_{Q},\mathbf{M}_{K}}\ b,\quad\text{s.t.}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left[\sigma\left(\frac{{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{K}}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\right]_{ij}({\mathbf{m}}_{Q})_{i}({\mathbf{m}}_{K})_{j}\geq\ell\times p,

(3)

where
p\in[0,1]
is the threshold of the minimum expected retention of attention scores, and the sum of attention scores equals to
\ell
due to the row-wise
Softmax
activation function. In this paper, our proposed method is developed on top of ZipVL
[
9
]
, an efficient Top-
p
sparse attention designed for MLLMs through a dynamic ratio allocation strategy of important tokens. ZipVL selects important tokens based on their normalized attention scores and performs sparse attention mechanism solely on those important tokens, reducing the latency in the prefilling phase. During decoding, the less important tokens are discarded to reduce KV cache size.

Inference pipeline with sparse attention.
Given a vision-language question
{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathbf{{\mathcal{X}}}
, where
\mathbf{{\mathcal{X}}}
is the joint input space of multimodal data, MLLMs first prefill the input with sparse attention and save the pruned KV cache
\{{\mathbf{K}}\odot\mathbf{M}_{K},{\mathbf{V}}\odot\mathbf{M}_{K}\}
into memory for decoding. Ideally, the final answer
{\mathbf{o}}
is expected to be the same as that without using sparse attention. Since the answer is usually short, that is,
|{\mathbf{o}}|\ll|{\mathbf{x}}|
, the final KV cache usage is approximately
2||\mathbf{M}_{K}||_{0}/d
. The token ratio is defined as the average proportion of selected tokens per sample, computed as
\tau=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{b_{j}}{\ell}
for a sample with token length
\ell
over all transformer layers
j={1,...,J}
.

2.2
ZipR1

Overview.
As shown in Figure
1
, we use an MLLM (
e.g.
, Qwen2-VL) with ZipVL as our policy model
\pi_{{\mathbf{\theta}}}
, and the same model with frozen parameters and full attention matrix as the reference model
\pi_{\text{ref}}
. For each question
{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathbf{{\mathcal{X}}}
, we perform
N
independent forward passes through the policy model to generate a set of distinct responses
\{{\mathbf{o}}_{1},...,{\mathbf{o}}_{N}\}
, where each response
{\mathbf{o}}_{n}
is generated with a random threshold
p_{n}\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1)
, for
n=1,...,N
. In addition, we record the token ratios
\{\tau_{1},...,\tau_{N}\}
associated with these responses. Then we compute the reward
r_{n}
of each answer with a specially designed reward function that simultaneously considers the performance-efficiency trade-off. Finally, we use the corresponding advantages to update the policy model. Here are two key benefits of ZipR1: (1) the reward function is designed to balance the model performance and efficiency via sparse attention; (2) the responses generated with various thresholds
p\sim{\mathcal{U}}(0,1)
enable the exploration of potential minimum tokens required for correct answers while offering flexibility with the token reduction strength at inference time.

Reward function.
To balance the performance-efficiency trade-off, we equally combine the performance reward function
r_{\text{per}}
and the efficiency reward function
r_{\text{eff}}
, which means the total reward is computed as
r=r_{\text{per}}+r_{\text{eff}}
. Specifically, the performance reward function assigns binary reward according to whether the response is consistent with the ground truth answer
[
8
]
. The efficiency reward is given by the token reduction ratio
1-\tau_{i}
, where a higher value indicates more extensive pruning of redundant tokens, thus leading to better efficiency. Let
\{r_{1},...,r_{N}\}
be the reward of the
N
generated answers, then the advantage of each answer is obtained by normalizing its reward as:

A_{i}=\frac{r_{i}-\operatorname{mean}\left(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{N}\right)}{\operatorname{std}\left(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{N}\right)}.

(4)

Eventually, the policy update follows the clipped surrogate objective of GRPO
[
21
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}({\mathbf{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathbf{{\mathcal{X}}}\\
n\in{\mathcal{U}}([N])\end{subarray}}\left[\left(\min\left(\frac{\pi_{\mathbf{\theta}}\left({\mathbf{o}}_{n}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\right)}{\pi_{{\mathbf{\theta}}_{\text{old}}}\left({\mathbf{o}}_{n}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\right)}A_{i},\kappa\left(\frac{\pi_{\mathbf{\theta}}\left({\mathbf{o}}_{n}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\right)}{\pi_{{\mathbf{\theta}}_{\text{old}}}\left({\mathbf{o}}_{n}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\right)}\right)A_{i}\right)-\beta\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\pi_{\mathbf{\theta}}\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\right)\right)\right],

(5)

where
{\mathbf{\theta}}
denotes all learnable parameters to be updated;
{\mathcal{U}}([N])
is the discrete uniform distribution from
1
to
N
;
\pi_{\mathbf{\theta}}(\cdot)
and
\pi_{{\mathbf{\theta}}_{\text{old}}}(\cdot)
are the probabilities under the updated and previous policies, respectively;
\kappa(\cdot)
is the
clip
operator that constrains the input within a narrow range
(1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)
with a small-valued parameter
\epsilon
to prevent large policy updates;
\mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\mathbf{\theta}}\|\pi_{\text{ref}})
measures the KL divergence between the updated and reference policies; and
\beta
is the weight coefficient of the KL divergence term. The first term facilitates better responses while ensuring stable updates through clipping. In addition, the second term penalizes deviation from a reference model, hence minimizing the transfer entropy caused by the sparse attention.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we have proposed a RL-based post-training method, ZipR1, designed to enhance token sparsity in well-posed MLLMs, thereby significantly improving the achievable inference-time acceleration. We employ the token reduction ratio as the efficiency reward and answer accuracy as the performance reward. Experimental results have demonstrated that ZipR1 reduces the token ratio of Qwen2/2.5-VL from 80% to 25%, with minimal accuracy degradation across 13 image and video benchmarks.

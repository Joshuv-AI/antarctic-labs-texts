On the extended Catalan arrangement of type B ℓ B_{\ell}
Hiraku Kawanoue

Abstract
. We give an explicit formula for the generators of the logarithmic vector field of the coning of the extended Catalan arrangement of type B ℓ B_{\ell} .



1.
Introduction

The extended Catalan arrangement is a class of finite truncation of
an affine Weyl arrangement for a root system.
In 2004, Yoshinaga
[
4
]
proved that the conings of the
extended Catalan arrangements are free
for all root systems.
However, the explicit description of
their logarithmic vector field are not well known
for a long time.

In 2021, Suyama and Yoshinaga
[
3
]
gave
an explicit basis of the extended Catalan arrangement of type
A_{\ell}
.
In 2023, Feigin, Wang and Yoshinaga constructed
a set of derivations in view of their integral expression
of the basis of Coxeter arrangement of type
B_{2}
, and
asked if it really gives
a basis of the extended Catalan arrangement of type
B_{2}

or not.

In this article, we generalize their construction to
the extended Catalan arrangement of type
B_{\ell}

and confirm that they give a basis.
In particular, it settles the conjecture of
Feigin, Wang and Yoshinaga in
[
1
]
affirmatively .

We work over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
.
We denote

(a)^{\downarrow}_{c}=\begin{cases}\prod_{i=a-c+1}^{a}i&(c\geq 0)\\
\left\{(a-c)^{\downarrow}_{-c}\right\}^{-1}&(c\leq 0)\end{cases},\quad(b)^{\uparrow}_{c}=(b+c-1)^{\downarrow}_{c},\quad P(a,b)=(a+b)^{\downarrow}_{2b+1}.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
The extended Catalan arrangement

\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m)
of type
B_{\ell}
is
a hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}

defined by the following equation

\Psi_{\ell,m}=\prod_{1\leq a\leq\ell}P(x_{a},m)\prod_{1\leq b<c\leq\ell}P(x_{b}+x_{c},m)P(x_{b}-x_{c},m)=0.

We also consider
a central arrangement
c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m)
in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell+1}

defined by
z\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell,m}=0
, where

\widetilde{\ }
means homogenization of a polynomial by
supplying a new variable
z
.
It is called the coning of
\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m)
.
As stated above, it is known that

c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m)
is free.

For

\ell,m,u\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we define

\displaystyle h_{\ell,m,u}(x;\bm{y})=h_{\ell,m,u}(x;y_{1},y_{2},\dotsc,y_{\ell})=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq s_{1},s_{2},\dotsc,s_{\ell}\leq m}\dfrac{(-1)^{S_{\ell}}P(x,m+u+S_{\ell})}{\binom{m+u+S_{\ell}+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{m}{s_{i}}\dfrac{P(y_{i},u+m+S_{i-1})}{P(y_{i},u+S_{i})},

where
S_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}
.
For
\ell=0
, we interpret
h_{0,m,u}(x)
as

h_{0,m,u}(x)=\dfrac{P(x,m+u)}{\binom{m+u+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}.

We define

F_{\ell,m,u,i}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{\ell})=h_{\ell-1,m,u}(x_{i};x_{1},\dotsc,\hat{x_{i}},\dotsc,x_{\ell})\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell),

where
\hat{x_{i}}
means the omission of
x_{i}
, and

\delta_{\ell,m,u}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\widetilde{F}_{\ell,m,u,i}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{\ell})\partial_{x_{i}}.

We denote the Euler derivation as

\delta_{E}=z\partial_{z}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
.
The following assertion is the main theorem of this article.

Theorem 1
.

The set

\{\delta_{E}\}\cup\{\delta_{\ell,m,u}\mid u=0,1,\dotsc,\ell-1\}

forms a basis of

D(c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m))
.

The conjectural basis of
D(c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{2},m))

constructed by Feigin, Wang and Yoshinaga
[
1
]

is based on considering
the discretization
\widetilde{f_{u}^{m}}(x,y)
of the integral

\int_{0}^{x}t^{2u}(t^{2}-x^{2})^{m}(t^{2}-y^{2})^{m}\,dt
.
Our construction is generalization of theirs.
In particular,
our
h_{1,m,u}
corresponds to
their
\widetilde{f_{u}^{m}}
. Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}t^{2u}(t^{2}-x^{2})^{m}(t^{2}-y^{2})^{m}\,dt=x^{4m+2u+1}\int_{0}^{1}r^{u}(r-1)^{m}(r-x^{-2}y^{2})^{m}\,\dfrac{dr}{2\sqrt{r}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}x^{4m+2u+1}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\binom{m}{s}(-1)^{s}(x^{-1}y)^{2(m-s)}\int_{0}^{1}r^{s+u-\frac{1}{2}}(1-r)^{m}\,dr

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\binom{m}{s}(-1)^{s}x^{2m+2u+2s+1}y^{2m-2s}B\Bigl(s+u+\dfrac{1}{2},m+1\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2(m+1)}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}x^{2m+2u+2s+1}y^{2m-2s}.\hfill

(1)

This equation implies
h_{1,m,u}=2(m+1)\widetilde{f_{u}^{m}}

by comparing the construction in
[
1
]
to ours. Thus
Theorem
1

settles the conjecture in §5 of
[
1
]

affirmatively.

Corollary 2
.

The conjecture of Feigin, Wang and Yoshinaga in
[
1
]
is true.
Namely, their candidate actually gives a basis
of
D(c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{2},m))
.

2.
Preliminaries

We investigate the properties of

h_{\ell,m,u}(x;\bm{y})
which will be used in the
proof of the main theorem.

2.1.
The case
\ell=1

We give two formulae for
h_{1,m,u}
. Recall that

h_{1,m,u}(x;y)=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}P(x,m+u+s)\dfrac{P(y,u+m)}{P(y,u+s)}.

Proposition 3
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, the following
recurrence formula for
m
holds.

\dfrac{2u-1}{2m+4}h_{1,m+1,u-1}(x;y)=\left\{x^{2}+y^{2}-(m+u+1)^{2}-u^{2}\right\}h_{1,m,u}(x;y)-2h_{1,m,u+1}(x;y).

Proof.

We denote that

M_{r}=A_{1}^{x-1}A_{2}^{-x-1}B_{1}^{y+r}B_{2}^{-y+r}

with formal variables
A_{\ast}
,
B_{\ast}
, and that

G_{m,u}(X,Y)=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}X^{m+u+s}Y^{m-s},\quad L_{m,u}=(-1)^{u}G_{m,u}(\partial_{A_{1}}\partial_{A_{2}},\partial_{B_{1}}\partial_{B_{2}}).

We denote by
\overline{\phantom{A}}

the evaluation at
A_{\ast}=B_{\ast}=1
.
Then it is easy to see that

h_{1,m,u}=x\,\overline{L_{m,u}M_{m+u}}.

On the other hand, by
(1)
, we have

\int_{0}^{x}t^{2u}(t^{2}-x^{2})^{m}(t^{2}-y^{2})^{m}\,dt=\dfrac{x}{2(m+1)}G_{m,u}(x^{2},y^{2}).

Using integration by parts, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\int_{0}^{x}t^{2u-2}(t^{2}-x^{2})^{m+1}(t^{2}-y^{2})^{m+1}\,dt=\dfrac{2(m+1)}{2u-1}\int_{0}^{x}t^{2u}(t^{2}-x^{2})^{m}(t^{2}-y^{2})^{m}(-2t^{2}+x^{2}+y^{2})\,dt.

Thus we obtain

\dfrac{2u-1}{2m+4}G_{m+1,u-1}(X,Y)=-2G_{m,u+1}(X,Y)+(X+Y)G_{m,u}

and hence

\dfrac{2u-1}{2m+4}L_{m+1,u-1}=-2L_{m,u+1}-(\partial_{A_{1}}\partial_{A_{2}}+\partial_{B_{1}}\partial_{B_{2}})L_{m,u}.

[formula]

We calculate that

\displaystyle\overline{L_{m,u+1}(M_{m+u+1}-M_{m+u})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{3}{2}}{m+1}}P(x,m+u+s+1)\left\{\dfrac{P(y,u+m+1)}{P(y,u+s+1)}-\dfrac{P(y,u+m)}{P(y,u+s)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{m+1}\dfrac{(-1)^{s-1}\binom{m}{s-1}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}P(x,m+u+s)\left\{\dfrac{P(y,u+m+1)}{P(y,u+s)}-\dfrac{P(y,u+m)}{P(y,u+s-1)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}P(x,m+u+s)\dfrac{P(y,u+m)}{P(y,u+s)}(2u+s+m+1)s,

(3)

and that

\displaystyle-\overline{(\partial_{A_{1}}\partial_{A_{2}}+\partial_{B_{1}}\partial_{B_{2}})L_{m,u}M_{m+u}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}P(y,u+m)\left\{\dfrac{P(x,m+u+s+1)}{P(y,u+s)}+\dfrac{P(x,m+u+s)}{P(y,u+s-1)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}P(x,m+u+s)\dfrac{P(y,u+m)}{P(y,u+s)}\left\{x^{2}+y^{2}-(m+u+s+1)^{2}-(u+s)^{2}\right\}.

(4)

Thus we conclude that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{x}\left(\dfrac{2u-1}{2m+4}h_{1,m+1,u-1}+2h_{1,m,u+1}\right)=\dfrac{2u-1}{2m+4}\overline{L_{m+1,u-1}M_{m+u}}+2\overline{L_{m,u+1}M_{m+u+1}}

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{=}\overline{\{-2L_{m,u+1}-(\partial_{A_{1}}\partial_{A_{2}}+\partial_{B_{1}}\partial_{B_{2}})L_{m,u}\}M_{m+u}}+2\overline{L_{m,u+1}M_{m+u+1}}

\displaystyle\overset{(3)(4)}{=}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}P(x,m+u+s)\dfrac{P(y,u+m)}{P(y,u+s)}\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\left\{2(2u+s+m+1)s+x^{2}+y^{2}-(m+u+s+1)^{2}-(u+s)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}P(x,m+u+s)\dfrac{P(y,u+m)}{P(y,u+s)}\left\{x^{2}+y^{2}-(m+u+1)^{2}-u^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{x}\left\{x^{2}+y^{2}-(m+u+1)^{2}-u^{2}\right\}h_{1,m,u}.\qed

Lemma 4
.

The following equation holds.

\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+s+u+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}\binom{k+m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{2m}=\dfrac{(m+1)(k)_{m}^{\downarrow}(u+k+\frac{3}{2})_{m}^{\uparrow}}{(u+\frac{1}{2})_{2m+1}^{\uparrow}}.

Proof.

We denote the left hand side of the equation in the assertion as
L
.
Note that

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+s+u+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}\binom{k+m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{2m}=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}(m)_{s}^{\downarrow}(m+1)!(k+m+u+s+\frac{1}{2})_{2m}^{\downarrow}}{s!(m+s+u+\frac{1}{2})_{m+1}^{\downarrow}(2m)!}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(k+m+u+\frac{1}{2})_{2m}^{\downarrow}(m+1)!}{(u+\frac{1}{2})_{m+1}^{\uparrow}(2m)!}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-m)_{s}^{\uparrow}(k+m+u+\frac{3}{2})_{s}^{\uparrow}(u+\frac{1}{2})_{s}^{\uparrow}}{s!(k-m+u+\frac{3}{2})_{s}^{\uparrow}(m+u+\frac{3}{2})_{s}^{\uparrow}}.

Now, by Pfaff-Saalschütz theorem, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-m)_{s}^{\uparrow}(k+m+u+\frac{3}{2})_{s}^{\uparrow}(u+\frac{1}{2})_{s}^{\uparrow}}{s!(k-m+u+\frac{3}{2})_{s}^{\uparrow}(m+u+\frac{3}{2})_{s}^{\uparrow}}=\left.{}_{3}F_{2}\genfrac{(}{.}{0.0pt}{}{\,{-m}\,,\,{u+\frac 12}\,,\,{k+m+u+\frac 32}\,}{\,{m+u+\frac 32}\,,\,{k-m+u+\frac 32}\,};1\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\left((m+u+\frac{3}{2})-(u+\frac{1}{2})\right)_{m}\left((m+u+\frac{3}{2})-(k+m+u+\frac{3}{2})\right)_{m}}{(m+u+\frac{3}{2})_{m}\left((m+u+\frac{3}{2})-(u+\frac{1}{2})-(k+m+u+\frac{3}{2})\right)_{m}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(m+1)_{m}^{\uparrow}(-k)_{m}^{\uparrow}}{(m+u+\frac{3}{2})_{m}^{\uparrow}(-k-u-\frac{1}{2})_{m}^{\uparrow}}=\dfrac{(m+1)_{m}^{\uparrow}(k)_{m}^{\downarrow}}{(m+u+\frac{3}{2})_{m}^{\uparrow}(k+u+\frac{1}{2})_{m}^{\downarrow}}.

Therefore we conclude that

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(k+m+u+\frac{1}{2})_{2m}^{\downarrow}(m+1)!\cdot(m+1)_{m}^{\uparrow}(k)_{m}^{\downarrow}}{(u+\frac{1}{2})_{m+1}^{\uparrow}(2m)!\cdot(m+u+\frac{3}{2})_{m}^{\uparrow}(k+u+\frac{1}{2})_{m}^{\downarrow}}=\dfrac{(m+1)(k)_{m}^{\downarrow}(u+k+\frac{3}{2})_{m}^{\uparrow}}{(u+\frac{1}{2})_{2m+1}^{\uparrow}}.\qed

Proposition 5
.

There exists a constant
A_{m,u}
satisfying the following relations.

\displaystyle h_{1,m,u}(x;y)

\displaystyle\equiv\phantom{-}A_{m,u}(x-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow}(x+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow}

\displaystyle\equiv-A_{m,u}(y-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow}(y+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow}\mod{(x+y+m)}.

Proof.

For
0\leq s\leq m
, we see that

P(x,m+u+s)\dfrac{P(y,u+m)}{P(y,u+s)}
is divisible by

\displaystyle P(x,m+u+s)(y-u-m)_{m-s}^{\uparrow}\equiv P(x,m+u+s)(-x-u-2m)_{m-s}^{\uparrow}\mod{(x+y+m)}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{m-s}P(x,m+u+s)(x+2m+u)_{m-s}^{\downarrow}=(-1)^{m-s}(x+2m+u)_{3m+2u+s+1}^{\downarrow}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{m-s}(x-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow}(x-m-u-1)_{s}^{\downarrow}.

It follows that

h_{1,m,u}(x;y)\in((x-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow})+(x+y+m).

[formula]

By applying Lemma
4
, we obtain

\displaystyle P\Bigl(-m+k+\frac{1}{2},u+m\Bigr)^{-1}h_{1,m,u}\Bigl(-k-\frac{1}{2};-m+k+\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}\dfrac{P(-k-\frac{1}{2},m+u+s)}{P(-m+k+\frac{1}{2},u+s)}=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s+1}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}\dfrac{P(k+\frac{1}{2},m+u+s)}{P(-m+k+\frac{1}{2},u+s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s+1}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}\dfrac{(k+m+u+s+\frac{1}{2})_{2m+2u+2s+1}^{\downarrow}}{(k-m+u+s+\frac{1}{2})_{2u+2s+1}^{\downarrow}}=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\dfrac{(-1)^{s+1}\binom{m}{s}(k+m+u+s+\frac{1}{2})_{2m}^{\downarrow}}{\binom{m+u+s+\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{(m+1)(2m)!(k)_{m}^{\downarrow}(u+k+\frac{3}{2})_{m}^{\uparrow}}{(u+\frac{1}{2})_{2m+1}^{\uparrow}}.

In particular, for integers
0\leq k<m
, we have

h_{1,m,u}\Bigl(-k-\frac{1}{2};-m+k+\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)=0.

It implies that

h_{1,m,u}(x;y)\in((x+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow})+(x+y+m).

[formula]

By (5) and (6), we conclude that

h_{1,m,u}(x;y)\in((x+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow}(x-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow})+(x+y+m).

Since

\deg h_{1,m,u}(x;y)\leq 4m+2u+1
, there exists a
constant
A_{m,u}
such that

h_{1,m,u}(x;y)\equiv A_{m,u}(x-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow}(x+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow}\mod{(x+y+m)}.

[formula]

Recall that we have

\displaystyle(x-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow}(x+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow}\equiv(-y-2m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow}(-y-m+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow}\mod{(x+y+m)}

\displaystyle\quad=(-y+m+u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\downarrow}(-y-1/2)_{m}^{\downarrow}=-(y-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow}(y+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow}.

Thus (7) implies

h_{1,m,u}(x;y)\equiv-A_{m,u}(y-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow}(y+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow}\mod{(x+y+m)}.

∎

(8)

by descending induction on

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\binom{m}{r}\binom{m}{k-r}\dfrac{P(y,v+m+1)P(z,v+m+r+1)}{P(y,v+r+1)P(z,v+k+1)}-\binom{m}{r}\binom{m}{k-r}\dfrac{P(y,v+m)P(z,v+m+r)}{P(y,v+r)P(z,v+k)}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+(k+1)(2v+k+m+2)\binom{m}{r}\binom{m}{k-r+1}\dfrac{P(y,v+m)P(z,v+m+r)}{P(y,v+r)P(z,v+k+1)}

\displaystyle=\binom{m}{r}\binom{m}{k-r}\dfrac{P(y,v+m)P(z,v+m+r)}{P(y,v+r+1)P(z,v+k+1)}\left\{(m-r)(2v+r+m+2)(z^{2}-(v+m+r+1)^{2})\right.

\displaystyle=\binom{m}{r}\binom{m}{k-r}\dfrac{P(y,v+m)P(z,v+m+r)}{P(y,v+r+1)P(z,v+k+1)}\dfrac{r(2v+r+m+1)(m-k+r)}{k-r+1}(y^{2}-(v+r+1)^{2})

Proposition 7
.

h_{\ell,m,u}(x;y_{1},\dotsc,y_{\ell})
is symmetric in

, and satisfies

h_{\ell,m,u}(x;y_{1},\dotsc,y_{\ell})=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}\dfrac{P(y_{i},u+m)}{P(y_{i},u+k)}h_{\ell-1,m,u+k}(x;y_{1},\dotsc,\hat{y_{i}},\dotsc,y_{\ell}).

, we have

h_{\ell,m,u}(x;y_{1},\dotsc,y_{\ell})=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}\dfrac{P(y_{1},u+m)}{P(y_{1},u+k)}h_{\ell-1,m,u+k}(x;y_{2},\dotsc,y_{\ell}),

[formula]

i.e., the recurrence formula for

For
\ell\geq 2

(9)

\displaystyle h_{\ell,m,u}(x;y_{1},\dotsc,y_{\ell})

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{a=0}^{m}\sum_{b=0}^{m}(-1)^{a+b}\binom{m}{a}\binom{m}{b}\dfrac{P(y_{1},u+m)P(y_{2},u+m+a)}{P(y_{1},u+a)P(y_{2},u+a+b)}h_{\ell-2,m,u+a+b}(x;y_{3},\dotsc,y_{\ell})

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\sum_{a=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{a}\binom{m}{k-a}\dfrac{P(y_{1},u+m)P(y_{2},u+m+a)}{P(y_{1},u+a)P(y_{2},u+k)}h_{\ell-2,m,u+k}(x;y_{3},\dotsc,y_{\ell})

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\Phi_{m,k,u}(y_{1},y_{2})h_{\ell-2,m,u+k}(x;y_{3},\dotsc,y_{\ell}).

Thus Lemma
6
implies that

h_{\ell,m,u}
is symmetric in
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
.
Now we prove
the symmetricity of
y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell}

in
h_{\ell,m,u}
by induction on
\ell
.
For
\ell=1
, we have nothing to prove.
For
\ell\geq 2
, the formula
(9)
and
induction hypothesis imply that

h_{\ell,m,u}
is symmetric in
y_{2},\dots,y_{\ell}
.
Since it is also symmetric in
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
by the above observation,
we conclude that

h_{\ell,m,u}
is symmetric in all
y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell}
.
In particular, the recurrence formula holds for all
i
.
∎

3.
Proof of the main theorem

We prove Theorem
1
.
The main tool is Saito’s criterion.

Theorem 8

(Saito’s criterion,
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
be a central arrangement defined by

F:=\prod_{H\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha_{H}
, where

\alpha_{H}
is a defining linear form for a hyperplane
H
.
The homogeneous derivations
\theta_{1},\dotsc,\theta_{\ell}\in D(\mathcal{A})

form a basis for
D(\mathcal{A})
if and only if

\det\left[\theta_{j}(x_{i})\right]=cF
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. In particular,

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\deg\theta_{i}=|\mathcal{A}|=\deg F
.

I) We show that
\delta_{\ell,m,u}\in D(c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m))

for
u\geq 0
.
Since

c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m)
is defined by

z\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell,m}=0
,
the conditions for

\delta_{\ell,m,u}\in D(c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m))

are

\delta_{\ell,m,u}(x_{a})\in(\widetilde{P}(x_{a},m)),\quad\delta_{\ell,m,u}(x_{b}\pm x_{c})\in(\widetilde{P}(x_{b}\pm x_{c},m))\quad(1\leq a\leq\ell,1\leq b<c\leq\ell),

Equivalently, we should prove

\begin{array}[]{lll}(1)&F_{\ell,m,u,a}\in({P}(x_{a},m))&(1\leq a\leq\ell),\\
(2)&F_{\ell,m,u,b}\pm F_{\ell,m,u,c}\in({P}(x_{b}\pm x_{c},m))&(1\leq b<c\leq\ell).\end{array}

The proof of this part is separated into three steps.

Step 1
. The condition (1) is clear from the definition of
F_{\ell,m,u,a}
since

F_{\ell,m,u,a}=h_{\ell-1,m,u}(y_{a};y_{1},\dotsc,\hat{y_{a}},\dotsc,y_{\ell})\in\sum_{\ell}(P(x_{a},m+u+S_{\ell-1}))\subset(P(x_{a},m)).

Step 2
. We investigate the condition (2) for

\ell=2
. We observe by Proposition
5
that

\displaystyle F_{2,m,u,1}+F_{2,m,u,2}=h_{1,m,u}(x_{1};x_{2})+h_{1,m,u}(x_{2};x_{1})

\displaystyle\equiv\left\{A_{m,u}(x_{1}-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow}(x_{1}+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow}\right\}+\left\{-A_{m,u}(x_{1}-m-u)_{3m+2u+1}^{\uparrow}(x_{1}+1/2)_{m}^{\uparrow}\right\}

\displaystyle=0\mod{(x_{1}+x_{2}+m)}.

We also observe by Proposition
3
that

\displaystyle\dfrac{2u-1}{2m+4}(F_{2,m+1,u-1,1}+F_{2,m+1,u-1,2})

\displaystyle\quad=\left\{x^{2}+y^{2}-(m+u+1)^{2}-u^{2}\right\}(F_{2,m,u,1}+F_{2,m,u,2})-2(F_{2,m,u+1,1}+F_{2,m,u+1,2}).

Combining these observations, it follows by induction on
m
that

F_{2,m,u,1}+F_{2,m,u,2}\in((x_{1}+x_{2})_{m+1}^{\uparrow}).

Remember that
h_{1,m,u}(x;y)
is odd function in
x
and even
function in
y
.
As
h_{1,m,u}(-x;-y)=-h_{1,m,u}(x;y)
, above formula implies

-(F_{2,m,u,1}+F_{2,m,u,2})\in((x_{1}+x_{2})_{m+1}^{\downarrow}),

and these two formulae verify

F_{2,m,u,1}+F_{2,m,u,2}\in(P(x_{1}+x_{2},m)).

We also observe that

\displaystyle F_{2,m,u,1}-F_{2,m,u,2}

\displaystyle=h_{1,m,u}(x_{1};x_{2})-h_{1,m,u}(x_{2};x_{1})

\displaystyle=h_{1,m,u}(x_{1};-x_{2})+h_{1,m,u}(-x_{2};x_{1})\in(P(x_{1}-x_{2},m)).

Therefore the condition (2) for
\ell=2
is proven.

Step 3
. We prove the condition (2)
by induction on
\ell
.
Take
d\in\{1,\dotsc,\ell\}\setminus\{b,c\}
and
set
\bm{x}^{\prime}=(x_{i}\mid i\neq b,c,d)
.
Then, by Proposition
7
and
induction hypothesis, we see that

\displaystyle F_{\ell,m,u,b}\pm F_{\ell,m,u,c}=h_{\ell-1,m,u}(x_{b};x_{d},x_{c},\bm{x}^{\prime})\pm h_{\ell-1,m,u}(x_{c};x_{d},x_{c},\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}\dfrac{P(x_{d},u+m)}{P(x_{d},u+k)}\Bigl\{h_{\ell-2,m,u+k}(x_{b};x_{c},\bm{x}^{\prime})\pm h_{\ell-2,m,u+k}(x_{c};x_{b},\bm{x}^{\prime})\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}\dfrac{P(x_{d},u+m)}{P(x_{d},u+k)}\Bigl\{(F_{\ell-1,m,u+k,b}\pm F_{\ell-1,m,u+k,c})\Bigr|_{x_{d}=x_{\ell}}\Bigr\}\in(P(x_{b}\pm x_{c},m))

Therefore, (2) holds for all
\ell
.
It completes the proof for

\delta_{\ell,m,u}\in D(c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m))
for
u\geq 0
.

II) We apply Saito’s criterion to the derivations

\left\{\delta_{E}\right\}\cup\left\{\delta_{\ell,m,u}\mid 0\leq u<\ell\right\}
.
We set

Q_{m,\ell}=\det\left[\delta_{E},\delta_{\ell,m,0},\delta_{\ell,m,1},\dotsc,\delta_{\ell,m,\ell-1}\right].

Since all entries above belong to

D(c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m))
, we have
Q_{m,\ell}\in(z\widetilde{\Psi}_{m,\ell})
.
Note that

\deg\delta_{E}+\sum_{u=0}^{\ell-1}\deg\delta_{\ell,m,u}\leq 1+\sum_{u=0}^{\ell-1}(2m\ell+2u+1)=1+(2m+1)\ell^{2}.

On the other hand, we have

\deg z\widetilde{\Psi}_{l,m}=1+(2m+1)\ell+2\cdot(2m+1)\cdot\binom{\ell}{2}=1+(2m+1)\ell^{2}.

It follows that there exists a constant
C\in\mathbb{C}
such that

Q_{m,\ell}=Cz\widetilde{\Psi}_{m,\ell}
.
Comparing the coefficients of

z\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}x_{i}^{(2\ell-2i+1)(2m+1)}

in both sides, we see that

\prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\dfrac{(-1)^{im}}{\binom{(i+1)(m+1)-\frac{1}{2}}{m+1}}=C\cdot 1.

It follows that
C\neq 0
.
Therefore

\left\{\delta_{E}\right\}\cup\left\{\delta_{\ell,m,u}\mid 0\leq u<\ell\right\}

gives a basis of
D(c\!\,\operatorname{Cat}(B_{\ell},m))
.

Bicriteria approximation for k -edge-connectivity
Zeev Nutov, Reut Cohen

Abstract
In the k k -Edge Connected Spanning Subgraph ( k k -ECSS ) problem we are given a (multi-)graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) with edge costs and an integer k k , and seek a min-cost k k -edge-connected spanning subgraph of G G . The problem admits a 2 2 -approximation algorithm and no better approximation ratio is known. Recently, Hershkowitz, Klein, and Zenklusen [STOC 24] gave a bicriteria ( 1 , k − 10 ) (1,k-10) -approximation algorithm that computes a ( k − 10 ) (k-10) -edge-connected spanning subgraph of cost at most the optimal value of a standard Cut-LP for k k -ECSS . We improve the bicriteria approximation to ( 1 , k − 4 ) (1,k-4) and also give another non-trivial bicriteria approximation ( 3 / 2 , k − 2 ) (3/2,k-2) . The k k -Edge-Connected Spanning Multi-subgraph ( k k -ECSM ) problem is almost the same as k k -ECSS , except that any edge can be selected multiple times at the same cost. A ( 1 , k − p ) (1,k-p) bicriteria approximation for k k -ECSS w.r.t. Cut-LP implies approximation ratio 1 + p / k 1+p/k for k k -ECSM , hence our result also improves the approximation ratio for k k -ECSM .

For an edge set
F

\delta_{F}(S,T)

one end in

Let

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{E}
let
x(F)=\sum_{e\in F}x(e)
. We will often refer to a proper subset

A standard Cut-LP for

-ECSS

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllllll}&\min&\displaystyle c^{T}\cdot x&\\
&\ \mbox{s.t.}&x(\delta_{E}(S))\geq k&\forall\emptyset\neq S\subset V\\
&&0\leq x_{e}\leq 1&\forall e\in E\end{array}

As in HKZ
[
14
]

(that previously was used for the

problem
[
31
]

This means that while

we repeatedly compute an extreme point optimal solution

and do at least one of the following steps:

1.

Relax some cut constraints

The relaxation of the cut constraints will be implemented in two different ways.

[(i)]

1.

Relaxing the cut constraint of a single cut

into a single node

We will store the nodes that correspond to such relaxed contracted cuts

Relaxing the constraints of all cuts

(that correspond to contracted sets

) to

This is achieved by adding to the partial solution a “ghost edge”

We will store the added ghost edges in a set

Instead of working with the cut constraints

To define this

•

A partial solution

•

A set

•

A set

Let

f(S)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k-d_{I}(S)-2d_{H}(S)-2&\mbox{if }S=\{u\}\mbox{ or }\bar{S}=\{u\}\mbox{ for some }u\in U\\

(1)

This function

Consider the following LP-relaxation with the function

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllllll}&\min&c^{T}\cdot x&\\
&\ \mbox{s.t.}&x(\delta_{E}(S))\geq f(S)&\forall\emptyset\neq S\subset V\\
&&0\leq x_{e}\leq 1&\forall e\in E\end{array}

(2)

A set

Given an LP solution

An

To

Lemma 8

When a core

[(i)]

Now we show that the algorithm terminates after a polynomial number of iterations.

Lemma 14
.

The algorithm terminates after
O(n)
iterations, where
n=|V|
.

Proof.

Let
x
be an extreme point computed at the first iteration of the algorithm.
It is known (c.f.
[
13
]
) that
|\{0<x_{e}<1:e\in E\}|\leq 2n-1
, namely, at most
2n-1
variables
x_{e}
are fractional.
This follows from two facts.
The first is that after the integral entries are substituted, the fractional entries are determined
by a full rank system of constraints
\{x(\delta_{E}(S))=k:S\in{\cal L}\}
where
{\cal L}
is laminar, c.f.
[
15
,
13
]
.
The second is that a laminar family on a set of
n

2n-1
.
The latter fact also implies that
|{\cal R}|\leq 2n-1
.
By Lemma
7
, the ghost edges form a
2
-matching on
{\cal R}
, thus
|H|\leq|{\cal R}|\leq 2n-1
.
At each iteration we remove an edge from
E
, or add a set to
{\cal R}
, or add a ghost edge.
Hence the number of iterations is bounded by

|{\cal L}|+|{\cal R}|+|H|\leq(2n-1)+(2n-1)+(2n-1)=3(2n-1)=O(n)
.
∎

Lemma 15

The paper of Jain

[(i)]

be the family of

-positive

[(a)]

{claim*}

There are

Proof.

Let

be a maximal laminar subfamily of

Let

Since no laminar

there is

Choose such

that overlaps the minimal number of sets in
{\cal L}
,
and let

be a set that
A
overlaps.
We show that (a) cannot hold; the proof that (b) cannot hold is similar.
Suppose to the contrary that (a) holds, namely that

\chi_{A}+\chi_{B}=\chi_{A\cap B}+\chi_{A\cup B}
and

A\cap B,A\cup B
are both

But

\Box

{claim*}

If
A,B\in{\cal T}
are not
x
-uncrossable then
A,B
cross and both (i) and (ii) hold.

Proof.

We show that
A,B
cross. If
A,B
do not overlap then

\{A,B\}=\{A\cap B,A\cup B\}
or
\{A,B\}=\{A\setminus B,B\setminus A\}

We claim that also
\bar{A}\cap\bar{B}\neq\emptyset
.
Otherwise,
A\setminus B=\bar{B}
,
B\setminus A=\bar{A}
,
and thus
\chi_{A\setminus B}=\chi_{\bar{B}}=\chi_{B}

\chi_{B\setminus A}=\chi_{\bar{A}}=\chi_{A}
,

\displaystyle f(A)+f(B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(\delta_{E}(A))+x(\delta_{E}(B))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(\delta_{E}(A\cap B))+x(\delta_{E}(A\cup B))+2\Delta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle f(A\cap B)+f(A\cup B)+2\Delta

\displaystyle f(A)+f(B)+2\Delta\ .

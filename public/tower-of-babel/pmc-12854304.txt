Parents’ knowledge about immunization with pentavalent vaccine who attended primary health care centers in Al-Diwaniyah City, Iraq
Authors: Zainalabideen Y. Jumaa, Nasir M. Younis
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The pentavalent vaccine is considered one of the most important vaccines in the routine immunization schedule because of its great importance in preventing five deadly and infectious diseases in children’s lives.MATERIALS AND METHODS:A descriptive cross-sectional study was conducted to find out the level of parents’ knowledge regarding the pentavalent vaccine. The nonprobability (convenience) was used with selected 140 parents visiting primary health care centers in Diwaniyah City. The study extended period was from November 2023 to September 2024. The study instrument consists of two part: The first part asked seven demographic questions, and the second part asked 15 questions that assessed parents’ knowledge about pentavalent vaccine. The study’s data were analyzed using SPSS “Statistical Package of Social Sciences” version 25.RESULTS:The results of this study showed that 59% of parents reported a poor knowledge level about the pentavalent vaccine, followed by those who reported fair levels (34%) and good levels (7%), respectively, with an mean of score (1.23). According to the study, there is a significant correlation (P < 0.05) between the knowledge levels of parents and certain demographic information (educational level, child’s caregiver). Therefore, we discovered that most people in society have false beliefs, whether they are social, cultural, or religious.CONCLUSION:The study concluded that level of knowledge of parents about pentavalent vaccine is unacceptable and below the required level.

Introduction

The cornerstone of adult health and wellbeing is Early Childhood Development (ECD), which is a crucial component of the Sustainable Development Goals. Although under-5 mortality has significantly decreased, from 5.9 million in 2015 to 5 million in 2021, many surviving children are unable to thrive due to the risk of infectious diseases. Childhood immunization is emphasized in the health area, one of the five main ECD domains. Every year, vaccines protect millions more from illness and disability and avert 2 to 3 million deaths. Immunization is one of the most comprehensive health interventions and a very economical way to save lives and promote good health.[1] The broader social benefits of longer and healthier lives yield a return of 51.8 USD/USD invested in vaccination, per a study done in 94 low-and middle-income countries.[2] Immunization Agenda 2030 was created by the “World Health Organization” with the goal of lowering vaccine-preventable disease (VPD) mortality and morbidity between 2021 and 2030.[3] The current National Immunization Program (NIP) in Iraq aims to prevent 12 VPDs and reduce the incidence of these illnesses by 99% by providing free vaccinations to eligible children. Additionally, there are a lot more immunizations that are advised for children now. By the time they are 2 years old, children can currently complete their immunization schedule with roughly 20 injections. However, the number of injections may increase in the years to come if more diseases and vaccines become available. So, research indicates that a complicated vaccination schedule may cause stress for parents and medical professionals, and it may even result in lower vaccination completion and compliance rates.[4] By stopping the spread of infectious diseases and lowering rates of morbidity and mortality for all societal segments, particularly children, vaccinations are regarded as one of the most significant interventions that have improved the public health status of societies. The benefits of immunizations and the outstanding outcomes that follow are comparable to two crucial interventions: the use of antibiotics to fight bacteria and the provision of safe and drinkable water.[5] Vaccines (diphtheria, whooping cough, tetanus, viral hepatitis, and hemophiles) are all included in the pentavalent vaccine, which is a compound vaccine that was created by mixing several different vaccines and then combining some of them into a triple vaccine, quadrivalent vaccine, and finally pentavalent vaccine. As of 2012, Iraq’s national immunization program included the pentavalent vaccine.[6] The government’s choice to implement the pentavalent vaccine will shield thousands of babies from some of the most harmful childhood illnesses, such as the main causes of diarrhea, pneumonia, and meningitis, which can cause irreversible brain damage. This decision follows over 3 years of day and night planning to begin a new chapter in the EPI program’s history in Iraq.[7] Because parents are responsible for following up on their child’s vaccinations and because of the lack of awareness among parents regarding the pentavalent vaccine, it was decided to conduct a study related to evaluating the knowledge of parents who attended primary health care centers regarding the pentavalent vaccine. This vaccine is considered of great importance in preventing these five deadly diseases as many people ignore this problem, which is extremely important due to the risks it poses to children’s lives, because many people do not immunize their children against these diseases. Understanding the ways in which the pentavalent vaccine prevents infectious diseases and fosters population immunity is the main goal of a theoretical framework for vaccination. Combining vaccines for five diseases, diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, hepatitis B, and Haemophilus influenzae type B, the pentavalent vaccine works by inducing the production of antibodies by the immune system against these pathogens without actually causing the disease. The framework integrates ideas from public health, immunology, and epidemiology, with a focus on how herd immunity can lower disease burden, especially in susceptible groups like newborns. The novelty of this study is that no similar studies have been conducted in Iraq and neighboring countries regarding fathers’ knowledge as most studies were limited to mothers’ knowledge. Also, most studies were limited to vaccines in general, while this study was on the pentavalent vaccine because of its importance in preserving the lives of children in particular and the family in general in order to protect them from most communicable diseases that may cause future deaths. The study’s goal was to determine the parents’ knowledge of pentavalent vaccination among those who visited primary health care facilities.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

A descriptive cross-sectional study was conducted to find out the level of parents’ knowledge regarding the pentavalent vaccine. The sampling method used was nonprobability (convenience) to selected 140 parents visiting primary health care centers in Diwaniyah city for the period from November 2023 to September 2024. Parents who refused to participate in the research and participants in the pilot study who did not complete the questions were excluded.

Study participants and sampling

Ten percent of the total sample size that visits PHCs each month was drawn from the study’s purposive sample, which was gathered from Primary Health Care Centers in Al-Diwaniyah City, Iraq. There were 140 parents at PHCs in Al-Diwaniyah City that made up the research sample. The questionnaire was presented to nine experts to evaluate the questionnaire and clarify the clarity of the paragraphs for the sample, and all modifications were taken into account by the experts. In order to ascertain the reliability of the questionnaire, a ministudy was carried out on 15 parents. The findings indicated that the Cronbach Alpha reliability of the questionnaire for parents concerning parents’ awareness of the pentavalent vaccine is 0.79, an acceptable level. Data were collected for the current study through the interview method for the period from January 2, 2024 to January 30, 2024. Personal information was obtained through a personal interview with the reviewers, and the approved and developed Arabic language questionnaire format was used, and approximately 15 minutes were spent by the reviewers to complete the interview and fill out the questionnaire form.

Data collection tools and technique

After a comprehensive review of the relevant literature and previous studies, the researcher constructed the tool to make it suitable for achieving the objectives of the study. There are two sections to the questionnaire: Section 1: Demographic Information Form for Parents: Age, gender, residence, caregiver, educational attainment, occupation, and monthly income are the seven items that make up this section. Section 2: Determination of the level of Parents’ Knowledge about Pentavalent Vaccine Immunization: This part was constructed to determine level of knowledge of parents about pentavalent vaccine who attended primary health care centers in Al-Diwaniyah city, consisting of 15 items. This scale consisted of 15 items that measure the level of knowledge of parents about pentavalent vaccine. The scale items were formulated in a gradual (I know, Not sure, I don’t know) so that the scores are as follows: (0,1,2).

Sampling of the study

Accidental sampling method or convenience was used in collection of the study sample.

Ethics consideration

The Ethics Committee at the University of Al_Qadisiyah, Community Health Nursing Department, and the Al_Qadisiyah sector of Health, Then, the discussion was held with specialized committees for the purpose of granting the necessary approvals under No. (3342) in (3,09, 2023).

Statistical analysis

The study’s data were analyzed using SPSS “Statistical Package of Social Sciences” version 25.

Discussion

Table 1 shows that participant in the 20–30 age range were the most likely to fill out the questionnaire. Approximately 57.85% more women than men participated in this group, with a 46.42% participation rate. 29.28% of the participants in the sample had finished intermediate school, according to the educational attainment survey. As indicated in Table 2, the study’s findings revealed that over 50% of the sample knew very little about the pentavalent vaccine. The results of this study showed that 59% of parents reported a poor level of knowledge about the pentavalent vaccine, followed by those who reported fair levels (34%) and good levels (7%), respectively, with a mean of score (1.23). Through the results, it is clear that parents do not have any knowledge about the pentavalent vaccine, its components, contraindications, or risks of not taking the vaccine. This is due to the lack of awareness among the general public and the lack of health education about the vaccine and immunization from it by the staff responsible for giving the vaccine or the Public Health Department and the Media Department, whether in the Al-Diwaniyah Health Department or the Ministry. A recent study found that parents’ knowledge about immunization with the pentavalent vaccine was average to good regarding immunization, so parents are less informed than mothers regarding the pentavalent vaccine because they are in contact with the team working within the primary health care institutions.[8] Regarding Table 3, which deals with the relationship between parents’ knowledge and demographic data, the results indicated that there is a relationship between who provides care for the child and the educational level, as well as the level of knowledge related to the pentavalent vaccine, with a P value of (0.000, 0.012), respectively. These results can be interpreted as mothers being more concerned about the child’s vaccinations and methods of administration, as well as the risks of not vaccinating children and following up on the child’s vaccination schedule from the rest of the parents. The results show that the higher the educational level of the parents, the more knowledgeable and informed the parents are about the pentavalent vaccine and its methods of administration, risks, and disease prevention. This result is consistent with the results of a study conducted by Al-tekreaty in Iraq in 2012,[9] where the results showed the mothers’ lack of information about immunization and methods of giving it, as well as the risks of not immunizing children. This result is not consistent with a study conducted by the World Health Organization in New Zealand in the National Immunization Program in 2015,[81011121314] where the majority of the study’s sample had good knowledge of the pentavalent vaccine and average knowledge of vaccines in general, according to the survey. 59% of the community does not know the pentavalent vaccine, and this is due to the lack of health education reaching them, which represents a problem in the success of the immunization process and achieving the desired goals, as well as the negligence of parents by not adhering to the routine immunization schedule, and the presence of religious and cultural concepts among a percentage of parents that prevent them from vaccinating their children.[151617181920] To enhance this type of studies, it is possible and better to apply them on a national and international scale, especially for national vaccination programs that are issued periodically by government organizations as well as international health organizations that are concerned with immunization and prevention of the spread of communicable diseases among children, since this group of society is the highest in comparison to other groups, and it must be focused on in future studies to avoid catastrophic problems.

Limitations and recommendations

The study recommended parents should commit to vaccinating their children at the times specified in the routine immunization schedule, clarifying the cultural concepts of society about vaccines by influential people in society and health awareness and education for society by the relevant departments. It is possible as a suggestion to conduct studies on a national and international scale related to pentavalent immunization as well as to know the knowledge of parents regarding all types of vaccines for children. One of the biggest obstacles that researchers faced was the short questionnaire period due to parents’ commitment to other appointments.

Conclusions

According to the findings of the study, the level of knowledge of parents about pentavalent vaccine is unacceptable and below the required level. This is a result of the parents’ ignorance and the staff’s failure to provide health education regarding the vaccine and its immunizations. This study demonstrated a substantial correlation between parents’ general knowledge levels and (child’s caregiver, educational level). The main reason is that the higher the educational level of parents, the better the knowledge as well as that of the child’s caregivers.

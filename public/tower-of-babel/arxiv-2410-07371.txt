Abstract
. For an odd prime p p , we determine the lower central series of a large family of non-periodic GGS-groups, which has a density of roughly ( p − 1 p ) 2 (\frac{p-1}{p})^{2} within all GGS-groups. This means a significant extension of the knowledge regarding the lower central series of distinguished classes of branch groups, which to date was basically restricted to the Grigorchuk group. As part of our results, we obtain the indices between consecutive terms of the lower central series, and we show that these groups, as well as their profinite completions, have lower central width equal to 2 2 . In particular, this confirms a conjecture of Bartholdi, Eick, and Hartung about the generalised Fabrykowski-Gupta groups.

1.
Introduction

For a given integer
d\geq 2
, the
d
-regular rooted tree
or simply the
d
-adic tree
is a tree
with a distinguished vertex (the root) of degree
d
and with every other vertex of degree
d+1
.
Groups of automorphisms of regular rooted trees are an important source of examples of groups with
very interesting properties.
Prominent among these are the (first)
Grigorchuk group
and the
Grigorchuk-Gupta-Sidki groups

(GGS-groups, for short).
The Grigorchuk group was constructed by R.I. Grigorchuk
[
12
]
in 1980 and has been extensively studied.
It was the first example of a group of intermediate word growth, thus providing an answer to Milnor’s Problem,
as well as the first example of a group that is amenable but not elementary amenable, answering Day’s Problem; cf.
[
13
]
for both these properties.
Also it is a
3
-generator infinite
2
-group, and so it gives a negative answer to the
General Burnside Problem.
For an arbitrary prime
p
, examples of
2
-generator infinite
p
-groups can be found within the class of GGS-groups,
such as the second Grigorchuk group
[
12
]
and the Gupta-Sidki groups
[
16
]
.
The GGS-groups can be defined by specifying a non-zero
defining vector

\mathbf{e}\in(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{d-1}
;
see
Section

2
for details.
They have received much attention in the past few decades, particularly in the case of the
p
-adic tree
for a prime
p
: see
[
9
]
for their basic theory and Hausdorff dimension,
[
8
]

for the position of their normal subgroups,
[
10
,
22
]
for their maximal subgroups,
or
[
23
]
for the isomorphism problem.
It is worth mentioning that GGS-groups have also found applications outside the realm of group theory; as shown in

[
15
]
, they can be used for the determination of Beauville surfaces.

The full group of automorphisms
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
of a regular rooted tree
\mathcal{T}
is a profinite group.
From now onwards we will always assume that
\mathcal{T}
is
p
-adic for a prime
p
.
In particular, when we refer to GGS-groups, we do it in this setting.
Then the set of all automorphisms whose local action at every vertex of
\mathcal{T}
is a power of the cycle
(1\ 2\ \ldots\ p)

is a Sylow pro-
p
subgroup of
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
, which we denote by
\Gamma(p)
.
Note that both the Grigorchuk group (for
p=2
) and the GGS-groups are subgroups of
\Gamma(p)
.
A subgroup
G
of
\Gamma(p)
is residually a finite
p
-group, and its topological closure in
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
is
a pro-
p
group, which is isomorphic to the profinite completion of
G
provided that
G
satisfies the

congruence subgroup property
(see
Section

2
for the definition).
It has been proved that the Grigorchuk group and all GGS-groups satisfy the congruence subgroup property, with the
only exception of the GGS-group with constant defining vector; also, these groups are just infinite i.e. every
non-trivial normal subgroup is of finite index (see
[
14
, Section 12]
and

[
8
, Theorem 2.7]
for all these properties).

Recall that a pro-
p
group
G
is said to have
finite coclass
if for some positive integer
i_{0}
we have

|\gamma_{i}(G):\gamma_{i+1}(G)|=p
for all
i\geq i_{0}
.
Then

\log_{p}|G:\gamma_{i}(G)|-i

takes a constant value for
i\geq i_{0}
, which is called the
coclass
of
G
.
Leedham-Green
[
19
]
and Shalev
[
28
]
independently proved that, for every prime
p
, there are
only finitely many infinite pro-
p
groups of each fixed coclass, and that these groups are soluble.
After that, attention was turned towards pro-
p
groups of finite lower central width, a natural generalisation of
finite coclass.
A residually nilpotent group
G
is said to have
finite lower central width
if there exists a constant
C
such
that
|\gamma_{i}(G):\gamma_{i+1}(G)|\leq C
for all
i\geq 1
.
If
G
has finite lower central width and all indices
|\gamma_{i}(G):\gamma_{i+1}(G)|
are powers of a prime
p

(in particular, if
G
is a pro-
p
group), then one usually defines the
lower central width
of
G
as

\max_{i\geq 1}\,\log_{p}|\gamma_{i}(G):\gamma_{i+1}(G)|.

A wealth of information about linear pro-
p
groups of finite width can be found in the book
[
17
]

by Klaas, Leedham-Green and Plesken, including insoluble examples.
In 1996, Zelmanov
[
31
]
conjectured that a just infinite pro-
p
group of lower central width is
either soluble,
p
-adic analytic (so linear over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
), or commensurable to a positive part of a loop group or to the Nottingham group.
This conjecture was settled in the negative by Rozhkov
[
26
]
, by proving that the Grigorchuk group (and
as a consequence also its profinite completion) has width
3
.
Indeed, the Grigorchuk group is a branch group, and so is its profinite completion.
Now as shown by Ábert, weakly branch groups do not satisfy any laws and are not linear over any field
(see in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
, respectively).
Rozhkov’s proof contained some gaps that were partially fixed in
[
27
]
, and eventually the result was fully
confirmed by Bartholdi and Grigorchuk
[
5
]
in 2000.

The results by Rozhkov, Bartholdi, and Grigorchuk do not only determine the lower central width of the Grigorchuk group
G
,
but also provide a detailed description of the terms of its lower central series.
In particular, they show that
|G:G^{\prime}|=2^{3}
, that
|G^{\prime}:\gamma_{3}(G)|=2^{2}
, and that

|\gamma_{i}(G):\gamma_{i+1}(G)|=\begin{cases}2^{2},&\text{if $2^{m}+1\leq n\leq 3\cdot 2^{m-1}$,}\\
2,&\text{if $3\cdot 2^{m-1}+1\leq n\leq 2^{m+1}$,}\end{cases}

where
m
runs over the positive integers.

Information about the lower central series of other distinguished (weakly) branch subgroups of
\Aut(\mathcal{T})

is very scarce.
Regarding GGS-groups, all results are limited to the original Gupta-Sidki examples and to the so-called

generalised Fabrykowski-Gupta groups
.
As already mentioned, the Gupta-Sidki groups are examples of
2
-generator infinite periodic groups
(actually,
p
-groups); they correspond to the defining vector
(1,-1,0,\overset{p-3}{\ldots},0)
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{p-1}
.
In turn, the Fabrykowski-Gupta group was originally defined only for
p=3
and then generalised to an arbitrary
odd prime, by considering the defining vector
(1,0,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},0)
.
Contrary to the Gupta-Sidki examples, the generalised Fabrykowski-Gupta groups are not periodic; on the other hand,
they are groups of intermediate growth, see
[
6
,
7
]
.
In
[
3
, Corollary 3.9]
, Bartholdi showed that the Gupta-Sidki
3
-group has infinite lower central width,
completing work of Vieira
[
29
]
, who had previously determined the indices between the first few terms
of the lower central series.
In the same paper (Corollary 3.14), Bartholdi also showed that the Fabrykowski-Gupta group (over the
3
-adic tree)
has lower central width
2
.
In
[
4
]
, Bartholdi, Eick, and Hartung developed a nilpotent quotient algorithm that they applied
to obtain by computer the indices between the first terms of the lower central series of both the Gupta-Sidki groups and
the generalised Fabrykowski-Gupta groups for small primes.
They performed similar calculations for other weakly branch groups not in the class of GGS-groups, namely for the
Brunner-Sidki-Vieira group, the Grigorchuk supergroup, and the Basilica group.
Based on this information, they made several conjectures about the behaviour of the lower central series of these groups.
In particular, they conjectured (Conjecture 17) that the lower central width of the generalised Fabrykowski-Gupta group
over the
p
-adic tree is
2
for every prime
p
.

The goal of this paper is to describe the lower central series of a wide class of non-periodic GGS-groups, containing
the generalised Fabrykowski-Gupta groups.
In particular, we determine the values of all indices
|\gamma_{i}(G):\gamma_{i+1}(G)|
for these groups, and as a
consequence we show that they all have lower central width
2
.
Thus we confirm Conjecture 17 from
[
4
]
and extend its validity to many other GGS-groups.
The class of GGS-groups to which our result applies is the following.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a GGS-group with defining vector
\mathbf{e}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{p-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{p-1}
, where
p
is an odd prime.
Then we define the following two elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
:

\varepsilon(\mathbf{e})=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\,e_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta(\mathbf{e})=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\,ie_{i},

and we say that
G
is of
FG-type
if
\varepsilon(\mathbf{e})\neq 0
and
\delta(\mathbf{e})\neq 0
.

Since we will always be working with a fixed GGS-group given by a fixed defining vector, in the remainder we
will simply write
\varepsilon
and
\delta
instead of
\varepsilon(\mathbf{e})
and
\delta(\mathbf{e})
.
Obviously, the generalised Fabrykowski-Gupta groups are of FG-type, having
\mathbf{e}=(1,0,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},0)
, while
the GGS-group with constant defining vector is not of FG-type, since
\delta=0
in this case.
It is well-known
[
30
, Theorem 1]
that a GGS-group is periodic if and only if
\varepsilon=0
;
thus all groups of FG-type are non-periodic.
One can easily see that, out of the possible
p^{\,p-1}-1
possible defining vectors of GGS-groups, the number of
vectors for which
\varepsilon\neq 0
and
\delta\neq 0
is
p^{\,p-1}-2p^{\,p-2}+p^{\,p-3}
.
As a consequence, the proportion of groups of FG-type among all GGS-groups is roughly
(\frac{p-1}{p})^{2}
, and a majority
of GGS-groups are of FG-type.
Similarly, the proportion of groups of FG-type among non-periodic GGS-groups is
\frac{p-1}{p}
.

Before stating the main theorem of the paper, which describes the lower central series of GGS-groups of FG-type,
we need to introduce the following notation.
We define the sequence of integers
\{\ell(m)\}_{m\geq 0}
and
\{r(m)\}_{m\geq 0}
recursively by means of

\displaystyle\ell(m)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $m=0$,}\\
p,&\text{if $m=1$,}\\
p(\ell(m-1)-1)-1,&\text{if $m\geq 2$,}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle r(m)=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if $m=0$,}\\
p+1,&\text{if $m=1$,}\\
p(r(m-1)-1)-1,&\text{if $m\geq 2$.}\end{cases}

Thus for every
m\geq 2
we have

\ell(m)=p^{m}-p^{m-1}-2\sum_{i=1}^{m-2}p^{i}-1\vskip-8.0pt

and

r(m)=p^{m}-2\sum_{i=1}^{m-2}p^{i}-1=\ell(m)+p^{m-1}.

On the other hand, we define the following elements of a GGS-group
G
of FG-type.
If
a
and
b
are the canonical generators of
G
(see
Section

2
), we set
x(1)=b

and

x(i)=[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{i-1}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b],

for every
i\geq 2
, where
\varepsilon
is as above.
We also define
y_{1}(1)=a
and

y_{j}(i)=[x(j-1),b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{i-j}{\dots},a^{\varepsilon}b],

for
2\leq j\leq i
.
Observe that
x(i),y_{j}(i)\in\gamma_{i}(G)
in every case.

Theorem A
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
G
be a GGS-group of FG-type defined over the
p
-adic tree.
Then the following hold:

(i)

If
\ell(m)\leq i<r(m)
for some
m\geq 0
, then

|\gamma_{i}(G):\gamma_{i+1}(G)|=p^{2}.

Also
\gamma_{i}(G)=\langle x(i),y_{\ell(m)}(i)\rangle\,\gamma_{i+1}(G)=\langle x(i),y_{\ell(m)}(i)\rangle^{G}
.

(ii)

If
r(m)\leq i<\ell(m+1)
for some
m\geq 0
then

|\gamma_{i}(G):\gamma_{i+1}(G)|=p.

Also
\gamma_{i}(G)=\langle x(i)\rangle\,\gamma_{i+1}(G)=\langle x(i)\rangle^{G}
.

In particular,
G
is a group of lower central width
2
.

We remark that Theorem A does not hold in this form if the GGS-group is not of FG-type
(see
Proposition

4.8
).
On the other hand, a standard argument in profinite group theory (see
Proposition

5.9
)
yields the following corollary.

Corollary B
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
G
be a GGS-group of FG-type defined over the
p
-adic tree.
Then the profinite completion of
G
is a pro-
p
group of lower central width
2
.

It has been brought to our attention by B. Klopsch and A. Thillaisundaram
[
18
]

that they have more generally proved that non-periodic multi-EGS groups with the congruence subgroup property
have finite lower central width.
However, their methods do not provide the description of the lower central series nor the exact
values of the indices
|\gamma_{i}(G):\gamma_{i+1}(G)|
for every
i
.

We conclude the introduction by sketching the strategy that we follow for the proof of Theorem A, which is
reflected in the structure of the paper:

(i)

If
G
is a GGS-group then
|G:G^{\prime}|=p^{2}
and so
|G:\gamma_{i}(G)|
is finite for every
i\geq 1
.
Now if the defining vector of
G
is not constant then
G
satisfies the congruence subgroup property
and the
n
th level stabiliser
\St_{G}(n)
is contained in
\gamma_{i+1}(G)
for some
n
.
Hence the quotient
\gamma_{i}(G)/\gamma_{i+1}(G)
is isomorphic to

\gamma_{i}(G_{n})/\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})
, where
G_{n}=G/\St_{n}(G)
is a finite
p
-group
(the
n
th congruence quotient of
G
).

(ii)

Thus our goal is to determine the lower central series of
G_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We accomplish this by induction on
n
.
The cases
n=1
and
n=2
are trivial, and we devote
Section

4
to the study of the case
n=3
,
which is critical for the induction.
This requires a very careful analysis of the position of some commutators in the generators of
G_{3}
, as well as
the fact that
G_{3}
acts uniserially on the level stabiliser
\St_{G_{3}}(2)
.
This last property is proved more generally in
Section

2
for
G_{n}
acting on
\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)
,
and for an arbitrary non-periodic GGS-group, i.e. provided that
\varepsilon\neq 0
.
The condition
\delta\neq 0
of the definition of groups of FG-type is needed precisely to settle the case

n=3
: if
\delta=0
then we get into further technical difficulties and the structure of the lower central
series of
G_{3}
is not clear, and actually differs from that in Theorem A (see
Proposition

4.8
).

(iii)

The induction step to pass from
G_{n-1}
to
G_{n}
is completed in
Section

5
.
The approach here is to compare the lower central series of
G_{n}
with that of the wreath product

W(G_{n-1})=G_{n-1}\wr C_{p}
, by using the map
\psi
that embeds the group
G
into
W(G)=G\wr C_{p}

(see
Section

2
).
As we will see, some terms of the lower central series of
\psi(G_{n})
coincide with terms of the lower central
series of
W(G_{n-1})
, while some other terms appear as “sandwiches” of the series of
W(G_{n-1})
.
At this point, it is essential to have a very detailed knowledge of the lower central series of groups of the
form
W(P)
, where
P
is a finite
p
-group with lower central factors of exponent
p
.
This problem is addressed in
Section

3
.

Notation.

In the remainder,
p
stands for an odd prime number, and
\sigma
denotes the
p
-cycle

(1\ 2\ \ldots\ p)
.
We let
\Sym(X)
be the symmetric group on a set
X
.
If
G
is a group then we write
B(G)=G\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times G
and

W(G)=G\wr\langle\sigma\rangle=B(G)\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle
.
As usual,
H^{G}
stands for the normal closure of
H
in
G
.
Also if
H
and
J
are subgroups of
G
and
N\trianglelefteq G
, we write
H\equiv J\mod N
to mean that

H
and
J
have the same image in the quotient
G/N
, in other words, that
HN=JN
.
In order to avoid cumbersome notation, we will frequently write the image of an element
g\in G
in a quotient

G/N
still as
g
rather than
gN
or
\overline{g}
: it will be clear from the context whether we are considering

g
in
G
or in
G/N
.
This will apply basically to the image of an element
g
of a GGS-group
G
in a congruence quotient
G_{n}
.
On the other hand, if a map
\varphi:K\rightarrow L
between two groups induces naturally a map between two
quotients of
K
and
L
, we usually still denote the induced map by the same letter
\varphi
.

2.
Preliminaries and uniseriality in non-periodic GGS-groups

In this section we first introduce all preliminaries about GGS-groups, and more specifically about groups
of FG-type, that we will need throughout the paper.
Our main reference for the basic theory of GGS-groups is the article
[
9
]
.
Then we move on to study the action of the congruence quotient
G_{n}
of a GGS-group
G
on the level
stabiliser
\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)
, and we prove that this action is uniserial if
G
is not periodic.

Let
X=\{1,\ldots,p\}
.
Our model of the
p
-adic tree
\mathcal{T}
consists in taking as vertices the elements of the free monoid

X^{*}
, and connecting
v
to
vx
with an edge for every
v\in X^{*}
and every
x\in X
.
The root of
\mathcal{T}
is then the empty word
\varnothing
, and the words of length
n
constitute the
n
th
level of the tree.
Then
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
, the set of automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}
as a graph, is a group with respect to the operation of
composition, which we write by juxtaposition: if
f,g\in\Aut(\mathcal{T})
then
fg
is the automorphism that results
from applying first
f
and then
g
.
This is consistent with considering the action of
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
on
\mathcal{T}
as a right action: we denote the image of

f\in\Aut(\mathcal{T})
on
v\in X^{*}
as
vf
or
(v)f
.
Also we write
\St(n)
for the normal subgroup of
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
consisting of all automorphisms acting trivially on
the
n
th level.

Every automorphism
f
of
\mathcal{T}
can be described by giving the permutation
\sigma(f)\in\Sym(p)
that it induces
on the vertices of the first level, together with the so-called sections
f|_{x}
at every vertex
x
of the first
level.
These sections are defined via the formula

(xv)f=xf\cdot vf|_{x},

for every
v\in X^{*}
and
x\in X
.
Then we have a group isomorphism

\begin{matrix}\psi&\colon&\Aut(\mathcal{T})&\longrightarrow&\Aut(\mathcal{T})\wr\Sym(p)\\[5.0pt]
&&f&\longmapsto&(f|_{1},\ldots,f|_{p})\,\sigma(f).\end{matrix}

If
f\in\St(1)
then we simply write
\psi(f)=(f|_{1},\ldots,f|_{p})
.

The map
\psi
can be used to define automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}
.
Let
a
be given by

\psi(a)=(1,\overset{p}{\ldots},1)\,\sigma,

where we recall that
\sigma=(1\ 2\ \ldots\ p)
.
Thus
a
permutes cyclically the
p
subtrees hanging from the root, and it is of order
p
.
As a consequence, it makes sense to write
a^{e}
for an element
e\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
On the other hand, for a fixed tuple
\mathbf{e}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{p-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{p-1}
,
we define the automorphism
b\in\St(1)
recursively via

\psi(b)=(a^{e_{1}},\ldots,a^{e_{p-1}},b).

Note that
b
is also of order
p
.
Then the group
G=\langle a,b\rangle
is called the
GGS-group with defining vector
\mathbf{e}
.

For a given
f\in\St(1)
, we have

(2.1)

\psi(f^{a})=(f|_{p},f|_{1},\ldots,f|_{p-1}).

As a consequence,

(2.2)

\psi([f,a])=(f|_{1}^{-1}f|_{p},f|_{2}^{-1}f|_{1},\ldots,f|_{p}^{-1}f|_{p-1}),

a fact that we will freely use multiple times throughout the paper.
From (
2.1
) and the definition of
a
and
b
, it readily follows that, for a GGS-group
G
, we have

(2.3)

G\cong\psi(G)\subseteq(G\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times G)\langle\sigma\rangle=W(G).

Note that
\psi(G)
is not equal to
W(G)
in this case.
By
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
, if the defining vector
\mathbf{e}
is not constant then
G
is

regular branch
over
\gamma_{3}(G)
.
More precisely, we have

(2.4)

\gamma_{3}(G)\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times\gamma_{3}(G)=\psi(\gamma_{3}(\St_{G}(1)))\subseteq\psi(\gamma_{3}(G)).

If furthermore the vector
\mathbf{e}
is not symmetric then
G
is regular branch over
G^{\prime}
,
and we actually have
[
9
, Lemma 3.4]

(2.5)

G^{\prime}\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times G^{\prime}=\psi(\St_{G}(1)^{\prime})\subseteq\psi(G^{\prime}).

Be also aware that
|G:\St_{G}(1)^{\prime}|=p^{\,p+1}
by Theorem 2.14 of
[
9
]
.

For a GGS-group
G
, we define
\St_{G}(n)=G\cap\St(n)
, the
n
th level stabiliser of
G
.
As mentioned in the introduction, if
\mathbf{e}
is not constant then
G
satisfies the congruence subgroup property,
i.e. every (normal) subgroup of
G
of finite index contains
\St_{G}(n)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Also
G
is just infinite: every non-trivial normal subgroup of
G
is of finite index.
It follows from (
2.4
) that

(2.6)

\psi(\St_{G}(n))=\St_{G}(n-1)\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times\St_{G}(n-1)

for every
n\geq 3
; see
[
9
, Lemma 3.3]
.
Now let
G_{n}=G/\St_{G}(n)
be the
n
th congruence quotient of
G
.
Then
G_{n}
acts faithfully on the tree
\mathcal{T}
truncated at level
n
, and consequently
G_{n}
embeds in
\Sym(p^{n})
.
Also
\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)=\St_{G}(n-1)/\St_{G}(n)
is an elementary abelian
p
-group, and in particular
\St_{G}(n-1)^{\prime}\leq\St_{G}(n)
.
From (
2.3
) and (
2.6
),
\psi
induces an injective map (that we denote by the same letter)
from
G_{n}
to
W(G_{n-1})
.
In
Section

5
we will obtain the lower central series of
G_{n}
by comparing that of
\psi(G_{n})
with
that of
W(G_{n-1})
.

Now assume that
G
is a GGS-group of FG-type, as in
Definition

1.1
.
If its defining vector
\mathbf{e}
is symmetric then
e_{i}=e_{p-i}
for
i=1,\ldots,(p-1)/2
, and so

\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\,ie_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{(p-1)/2}\,ie_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{(p-1)/2}\,(p-i)e_{p-i}=0

in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, which is a contradiction.
Consequently
\mathbf{e}
is not symmetric and
G
satisfies (
2.5
).
On the other hand, since
G
is not periodic, it follows from Theorem 2.4 and Lemma 2.7 of
[
9
]
that

|G:\St_{G}(2)|=p^{\,p+1}
.
Since
\St_{G}(1)^{\prime}\leq\St_{G}(2)
and
|G:\St_{G}(1)^{\prime}|=p^{\,p+1}
, we conclude that
\St_{G}(2)=\St_{G}(1)^{\prime}
.
Thus (
2.5
) implies that

(2.7)

\psi(\St_{G}(2))=G^{\prime}\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times G^{\prime}.

Another consequence of
G
not being periodic and
\mathbf{e}
not being symmetric is that the order of the
congruence quotient
G_{n}
is given by

(2.8)

|G_{n}|=p^{\,p^{n-1}+1}

for every
n\geq 2
; see
[
9
, Theorem A and Lemma 2.7]
.

After this quick introduction to the basic properties of GGS-groups and groups of FG-type that we are going
to need, our next purpose is to show that, for a non-periodic GGS-group
G
, the
n
th congruence quotient
G_{n}

acts uniserially on
\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)
.
We start by recalling the concept of uniserial action.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group acting on another finite
p
-group
K
.
We say that
G

acts uniserially
on
K
, or that the action of
G
on
K
is
uniserial
if
for every non-trivial
G
-invariant subgroup
L
of
K
we have
|L:[L,G]|=p
.

If
G
acts uniserially on
K
, it is well-known that the only
G
-invariant subgroups of
K
are precisely those of the
form
[K,G,\overset{i}{\ldots},G]
with
i\geq 0
; see
[
20
, Lemma 4.1.3]
.
A typical example of a uniserial action is given by a finite
p
-group of maximal class acting on one of its maximal subgroups
by conjugation.
For an arbitrary GGS-group
G
, we know that
G_{2}=\langle a\rangle\ltimes\St_{G_{2}}(1)
is a
p
-group of maximal class

[
9
, Theorem 2.4]
, and so
G_{2}
acts uniserially on
\St_{G_{2}}(1)
.
Actually, since
\St_{G_{2}}(1)
is abelian, we can further say that
\langle g\rangle
acts uniserially on
\St_{G_{2}}(1)

for every
g\in G\smallsetminus\St_{G_{2}}(1)
.
In the next theorem we generalise this property to other congruence quotients
G_{n}
, provided that
G
is non-periodic.
We are going to rely on the following fact for its proof: for a subgroup
H
of a group
G
and an element
g\in G
,
if
H
is
g
-invariant then

(2.9)

[H,\langle g\rangle]=[H,g].

This is an immediate consequence of the identities

[h,g^{i}]=[h,g][h,g]^{g}\cdots[h,g]^{g^{i-1}}=[h,g][h^{g},g]\cdots[h^{g^{i-1}},g]

for
i\geq 1
, and

[h,g^{-1}]=([h,g]^{g^{-1}})^{-1}=[h^{g^{-1}},g]^{-1},

which hold for every
h\in H
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
G
be a non-periodic GGS-group.
Then the following hold for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(i)

The element
a^{\varepsilon}b
has order
p^{n}
in
G_{n}
.

(ii)

If
g\in G_{n}
is an element of order
p^{n-1}
in
G_{n-1}
then the cyclic subgroup
\langle g\rangle
acts
uniserially on
\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)
.

In particular,
G_{n}
acts uniserially on
\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)
, and the only subgroups of
\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)
that are
normal in
G_{n}
are those of the form

[\St_{G_{n}}(n-1),G_{n},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{n}].

Also if
h
is a generator of
\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)
modulo
[\St_{G_{n}}(n-1),G_{n}]
then, for every
i\geq 1
,

[h,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{i}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b]
is a generator of

[\St_{G_{n}}(n-1),G_{n},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{n}]
modulo
[\St_{G_{n}}(n-1),G_{n},\overset{i+1}{\ldots},G_{n}]
.

Proof.

(i)
We use induction on
n
, the case
n=1
being obvious, since
\varepsilon\neq 0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Now assume that
n>1
and set
t=a^{\varepsilon}
.
Then

(tb)^{p}=b^{t^{p-1}}\cdots b^{t}b.

We are going to obtain the order of
tb
in
G_{n}
by applying
\psi
to the equality above and by looking at
the orders of the corresponding components in
G_{n-1}
.

Since
\varepsilon\neq 0
, we have
\langle t\rangle=\langle a\rangle
and consequently
\psi((tb)^{p})
is
the product of the tuples
\psi(b),\psi(b^{a}),\ldots,\psi(b^{a^{p-1}})
in some unspecified order.
Taking into account (
2.1
), it follows that every entry of
\psi((tb)^{p})
is of the form

(2.10)

a^{f_{1}}\ldots a^{f_{k}}ba^{f_{k+1}}\ldots a^{f_{p-1}}=a^{f_{1}+\cdots+f_{p-1}}b^{a^{f_{k+1}+\cdots+f_{p-1}}}=\big(a^{f_{1}+\cdots+f_{p-1}}b\big)^{a^{f_{k+1}+\cdots+f_{p-1}}},

where
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{p-1}\}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{p-1}\}
.
Thus (
2.10
) is equal to

(tb)^{a^{f_{k+1}+\cdots+f_{p-1}}}.

Thus all components of
\psi((tb)^{p})
have the same order in
G_{n-1}
, namely the same as the order of
tb
in
G_{n-1}
.
By induction, this implies that the order of
(tb)^{p}
in
G_{n}
is
p^{n-1}
, and the result follows.

(ii)
Let
S=\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)
and let
L\neq 1
be a
\langle g\rangle
-invariant subgroup of
S
.
The action of
g
on the
p^{n-1}
vertices of the
(n-1)
st level induces a permutation
\tau\in\Sym(p^{n-1})
,
and the order of
g
in
G_{n-1}
coincides with the order of
\tau
.
Since, by hypothesis,
g
has order
p^{n-1}
in
G_{n-1}
, it follows that
\tau
is a cycle of length
p^{n-1}
.
In particular,
\tau
acts transitively on the vertices of the
(n-1)
st level.

Now consider the map

\begin{matrix}\kappa&\colon&L&\longrightarrow&L\\
&&\ell&\longmapsto&[\ell,g],\end{matrix}

which is a group homomorphism, since
L
is abelian (recall that
S
is elementary abelian).
By (
2.9
), we have
[L,\langle g\rangle]=\{[\ell,g]\mid\ell\in L\}=\kappa(L)
.
Hence the first isomorphism theorem yields

|L:[L,\langle g\rangle]|=|\ker\kappa|=|C_{L}(g)|.

Now since every element
\ell\in L
fixes the vertices of the
(n-1)
st level, it permutes the
p
descendants
of each of these vertices.
Thus
\ell
defines a tuple of
p^{n-1}
permutations in
\Sym(p)
, which we denote by
\psi_{n-1}(\ell)
.
Actually, since
G
is a subgroup of
\Gamma(p)
, each of these permutations lies in the subgroup

\langle\sigma\rangle
of order
p
.
On the other hand, one can easily see that
\psi_{n-1}(\ell^{g})
is obtained from
\psi_{n-1}(\ell)
by permuting
its components as indicated by
\tau
.
Since
\tau
acts transitively on the vertices of the
(n-1)
st level, it follows that
\ell\in C_{L}(g)

if and only if the tuple
\psi_{n-1}(\ell)
is constant (with values in
\langle\sigma\rangle
).
Thus
|L:[L,\langle g\rangle]|=|C_{L}(g)|\leq p
.
Finally, since
G_{n}
is a finite
p
-group and
L\neq 1
, the commutator
[L,\langle g\rangle]
is properly
contained in
L
, and we conclude that
|L:[L,\langle g\rangle]|=p
.

The rest of the assertions follow immediately from (i) and (ii).
∎

3.
The lower central series of some wreath products

Let
G
be a group.
Recall that we denote by
W(G)
the wreath product
G\wr\langle\sigma\rangle
, where

\sigma=(1\ 2\ \ldots\ p)
.
Then
W(G)=B(G)\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle
, where
B(G)=G\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times G

is the base group of the wreath product.
Let
\mathbf{g}
be an arbitrary element of
B(G)
.
For the purpose of this section, it is more convenient to write
\mathbf{g}=(g_{0},\ldots,g_{p-1})
, with
g_{i}\in G
for all

i=0,\ldots,p-1
, rather than in the more natural form
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p})
.
In any case, we have

\mathbf{g}^{\sigma}=(g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{p-1})^{\sigma}=(g_{p-1},g_{0},\ldots,g_{p-2}).

In this setting, we define the map

\begin{matrix}\Delta&\colon&B(G)&\longrightarrow&B(G)\\
&&\mathbf{g}&\longmapsto&[\mathbf{g},\sigma].\end{matrix}

Note that
\Delta
is a group homomorphism if
G
is abelian.
Clearly, if
N\trianglelefteq G
and
N\leq H\leq G
then we have

\Delta(B(H/N))=\Delta(B(H))B(N)/B(N).

The goal of this section is to describe the lower central series of
W(G)
under the assumption that
G
is a
finite
p
-group and that the factors of the lower central series of
G
are elementary abelian
(equivalently, that the abelianisation
G/G^{\prime}
is elementary abelian; see
[
25
, Theorem 2.26]
).
We start by studying the case of
W(A)
, where
A
is an elementary abelian finite
p
-group.
Liebeck
[
21
, Theorem 5.1]
showed that the nilpotency class of
W(A)
is
p
, and our goal now is
to give a detailed description of the lower central series of
W(A)
.
We first assume that
A
is a finite field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p
, with the group structure
given by addition.
This has the advantage that the base group
B(\mathbb{F})
is also an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space.
We will see that the terms
\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))
of the lower central series of
W(\mathbb{F})
are vector subspaces of
B(\mathbb{F})
.
As usual in linear algebra, we will describe these subspaces by giving both a basis and
a set of defining linear equations.
Note that
\Delta:B(\mathbb{F})\rightarrow B(\mathbb{F})
is an
\mathbb{F}
-linear map in this case, and that for an arbitrary subgroup

H
of
B(\mathbb{F})
, we have

(3.1)

[H,W(\mathbb{F})]=[H,\sigma]=\{[\boldsymbol{\lambda},\sigma]\mid\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in H\}=\Delta(H).

Now we consider the factor algebra
\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{p}-1)
.
Since
\mathbb{F}
is of characteristic
p
, this is a local
\mathbb{F}
-algebra whose only maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}

is generated by the element
\overline{X-1}
.
All the ideals of
\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{p}-1)
are then the powers
\mathfrak{m}^{i}
for
0\leq i\leq p
.
The quotient
\mathfrak{m}^{i}/\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}
has
\mathbb{F}
-dimension
1
for
0\leq i\leq p-1
, and is generated
by the image of
(\overline{X-1})^{i}
.
We have the following isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}
-vector spaces:

\begin{matrix}\Theta&\colon&B(\mathbb{F})&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{p}-1)\\[5.0pt]
&&\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p-1})&\longmapsto&\overline{\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}X+\cdots+\lambda_{p-1}X^{p-1}}.\end{matrix}

Under this isomorphism, the action of
\sigma
on
B(\mathbb{F})
corresponds to multiplication by
\overline{X}
in

\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{p}-1)
, and taking commutators with
\sigma
corresponds to multiplication by
\overline{X-1}
.
As a consequence,
\Theta
extends to an isomorphism of
W(\mathbb{F})
with the semidirect product

\langle\overline{X}\rangle\ltimes\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{p}-1)
, where the corresponding action is given by multiplication
in the algebra
(note that
\overline{X}
is a unit of multiplicative order
p
in
\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{p}-1)
).

By (
3.1
), we have

\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))=[B(\mathbb{F}),\sigma,\overset{i-1}{\ldots},\sigma]=\Delta^{i-1}(B(\mathbb{F}))

for every
i\geq 2
.
Consequently
\Theta(\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F})))=\mathfrak{m}^{i-1}
.
This proves, in particular, Liebeck’s result that
W(\mathbb{F})
has nilpotency class
p
.
Also the subgroups
\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))
are
\mathbb{F}
-subspaces of
B(\mathbb{F})
.
Note that

|\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F})):\gamma_{i+1}(W(\mathbb{F}))|=|\mathfrak{m}^{i-1}:\mathfrak{m}^{i}|=|\mathbb{F}|

for
2\leq i\leq p
.

On the other hand, an
\mathbb{F}
-subspace
H
of
B(\mathbb{F})
is normal in
W(\mathbb{F})
, i.e. invariant under
\sigma
, if and only
if
\Theta(H)
is an
\mathbb{F}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{p}-1)
invariant under multiplication by
\overline{X}
or, equivalently,
an ideal of
\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{p}-1)
.
Hence
\Theta(H)
coincides with one of the powers
\mathfrak{m}^{i}
, and then
either
H=B(\mathbb{F})
or
H=\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))
with
2\leq i\leq p+1
.

We collect all the information obtained so far in the following theorem.
In order to make the statement more uniform, it is convenient to introduce a slight modification of notation,
replacing
W(\mathbb{F})
with
B(\mathbb{F})
in the lower central series.
In general, for any group of the form
W(G)
, we set

\gamma_{1}^{*}(W(G))=B(G)

and

\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(G))=[\gamma_{i-1}^{*}(W(G)),W(G)],\quad\text{for every $i\geq 2$.}

Since
W(G)/B(G)
is cyclic, we have
W(G)^{\prime}=[B(G),W(G)]
, and consequently

\gamma_{i}(W(G))=\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(G))
for
i\geq 2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
.
Then the following hold:

(i)

For every
i\geq 1
we have
\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))=\Delta^{i-1}(B(\mathbb{F}))
, and
\Theta(\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F})))=\mathfrak{m}^{i-1}
, where

\mathfrak{m}
is the ideal of
\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{p}-1)
generated by
\overline{X-1}
.

(ii)

The only
\sigma
-invariant
\mathbb{F}
-subspaces of
B(\mathbb{F})
are those in the chain

1=\gamma_{p+1}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))\leq\cdots\leq\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))\leq\cdots\leq\gamma_{1}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))=B(\mathbb{F}).

(iii)

We have

|\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F})):\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))|=|\mathbb{F}|

and

|\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))|=|\mathbb{F}|^{p-i+1}

for
1\leq i\leq p+1
.

In particular, the nilpotency class of
W(\mathbb{F})
is
p
.

Now it is easy to give bases for the subspaces
\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
.
For
1\leq i\leq p
, set

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}=\Delta^{i-1}(1,0,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},0)\in B(\mathbb{F}).

Then the following hold:

(i)

We have

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}=(\lambda_{i,0},\ldots,\lambda_{i,i-1},0,\overset{p-i}{\ldots},0),

in
B(\mathbb{F})
, where

\lambda_{i,r}=(-1)^{i-r-1}\dbinom{i-1}{r}

for
0\leq r\leq i-1
.

(ii)

The image of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}
generates the quotient
\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))/\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))

as an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space.

(iii)

The set
\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{p}\}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-basis of
\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))
.

Proof.

By using
Theorem

3.1
, the
\mathbb{F}
-isomorphism
\Theta
induces an
\mathbb{F}
-isomorphism between

\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))/\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(\mathbb{F}))
and
\mathfrak{m}^{i-1}/\mathfrak{m}^{i}
.
The latter has
\mathbb{F}
-dimension
1
and is generated by the image of

(\overline{X-1})^{i-1}=\sum_{r=0}^{i-1}\,(-1)^{i-r-1}\dbinom{i-1}{r}\overline{X}^{r}.

Since
\Theta
sends
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}
to
(\overline{X-1})^{i-1}
, the result follows.
∎

Since
\mathbb{F}
is of characteristic
p
and

(3.2)

\lambda_{p,r}=(-1)^{p-r-1}\frac{(p-1)(p-2)\ldots(p-r)}{r!}\equiv(-1)^{p-r-1}(-1)^{r}=1\mod p

for
0\leq r\leq p-1
, we immediately obtain the following description of the last non-trivial term
of the lower central series of
W(\mathbb{F})
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
.
Then

\gamma_{p}(W(\mathbb{F}))=\{(\lambda,\overset{p}{\ldots},\lambda)\mid\lambda\in\mathbb{F}\}

is the set of constant tuples in
B(\mathbb{F})
.

Thus
\gamma_{p}(W(\mathbb{F}))
can be described as the subspace of
B(\mathbb{F})
given by the equations
X_{0}=X_{1}=\cdots=X_{p-1}
.
Our goal now is to describe all terms of the lower central series of
W(\mathbb{F})
via linear homogeneous equations.
As it turns out, these equations will be induced by certain polynomials in
\mathbb{F}[X]
.
To this purpose, we introduce the following notation: to every polynomial
f(X)=f_{0}+f_{1}X+\cdots+f_{p-1}X^{p-1}\in\mathbb{F}[X]
of degree
less than
p
, we associate the homogeneous linear polynomial
f_{0}X_{0}+f_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+f_{p-1}X_{p-1}
, which we denote by

\mathcal{L}(f(X))
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
.
Then for every
2\leq i\leq p
, the subgroup
\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))
consists of all solutions
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in B(\mathbb{F})

of either of the following systems of homogeneous linear equations:

(i)

\mathcal{L}((X-1)^{\ell})=0
, for
p-i+1\leq\ell\leq p-1
.

(ii)

\mathcal{L}(f^{(\ell)}(X))=0
, for
0\leq\ell\leq i-2
, where
f(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}
.

Proof.

The standard bilinear product

(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{p-1})\cdot(\mu_{0},\ldots,\mu_{p-1})=\lambda_{0}\mu_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{p-1}\mu_{p-1}

on
B(\mathbb{F})
is non-degenerate.
Consequently, for every
\mathbb{F}
-subspace
U
of
B(\mathbb{F})
we have
(U^{\perp})^{\perp}=U
, and so a basis of

U^{\perp}
provides a system of homogeneous linear equations defining
U
.
We will apply this to
U=\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))
.

By non-degeneracy and (iii) of
Theorem

3.1
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))^{\perp}=p-\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))=i-1.

Also
\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))^{\perp}
is
\sigma
-invariant, since
\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))
is.
By (ii) and (iii) of
Theorem

3.1
, it follows that
\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))^{\perp}=\gamma_{p-i+2}(W(\mathbb{F}))
.
Now (i) of the same theorem yields
\Theta(\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))^{\perp})=\mathfrak{m}^{p-i+1}
.
Since

\{(\overline{X-1})^{\ell}\mid p-i+1\leq\ell\leq p-1\}

is an
\mathbb{F}
-basis of
\mathfrak{m}^{p-i+1}
,
it follows that
\gamma_{i}(W(\mathbb{F}))
can be defined by the equations in (i).

As for the equations in (ii), observe that

(3.3)

f(X)=\frac{X^{p}-1}{X-1}=\frac{(X-1)^{p}}{X-1}=(X-1)^{p-1}

in
\mathbb{F}[X]
.
Consequently the derivatives
f^{(\ell)}(X)=(p-1)\ldots(p-\ell)(X-1)^{p-\ell-1}
for
0\leq\ell\leq i-2

also provide an
\mathbb{F}
-basis of
\mathfrak{m}^{p-i+1}
.
∎

As a consequence, we can easily determine whether a tuple
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
belongs to
\gamma_{2}(W(\mathbb{F}))

or
\gamma_{3}(W(\mathbb{F}))
.
These cases will be particularly useful in the following sections.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
.
Then, for a given tuple
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{p-1})\in B(\mathbb{F})
, we have

(3.4)

\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\gamma_{2}(W(\mathbb{F}))\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{p-1}=0,

and

(3.5)

\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\gamma_{3}(W(\mathbb{F}))\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{p-1}=0,\ \ \lambda_{0}+2\lambda_{1}+\cdots+(p-1)\lambda_{p-2}=0.

For later use, it is convenient to translate the information obtained in the previous theorems from the
special case of a finite field to the general case of an arbitrary elementary abelian
finite
p
-group
A
, written multiplicatively.
Assume
A
is of order
p^{r}
and let
\rho:(A,\cdot)\rightarrow(\mathbb{F},+)
be a group isomorphism, where
\mathbb{F}

is a field with
p^{r}
elements.
Then
\rho
obviously induces an isomorphism
\tilde{\rho}
between
W(A)
and
W(\mathbb{F})
, which is defined
componentwise on
B(A)
by using
\rho
and is the identity on
\langle\sigma\rangle
.
As a consequence,

(3.6)

\tilde{\rho}(\Delta(a_{0},\ldots,a_{p-1}))=\Delta(\tilde{\rho}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{p-1}))

for all
a_{0},\ldots,a_{p-1}\in A
.
Since
\tilde{\rho}
is an isomorphism, the results about the nilpotency class, and the orders and indices of
terms of the lower central series in
Theorem

3.1
remain exactly the same, replacing
\mathbb{F}
with
A
.
Also

(3.7)

\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(A))=\Delta^{i-1}(B(A)),\quad\text{for every $i\geq 1$,}

and
Corollary

3.3
translates into

(3.8)

\gamma_{p}(W(A))=\{(a,\overset{p}{\ldots},a)\mid a\in A\}.

Let us see how we can rewrite the result regarding generators of terms of the lower central series.
Consider a vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{p-1})\in B(\mathbb{F}_{p})
(note that

\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the prime subfield of
\mathbb{F}
).
Now since
\rho
is bijective, an arbitrary element
\mu\in\mathbb{F}
can be written in the form
\rho(a)
for a unique

a\in A
, and then the scalar product
\mu\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is equal to

(3.9)

\begin{split}\rho(a)\boldsymbol{\lambda}&=(\lambda_{0}\rho(a),\ldots,\lambda_{p-1}\rho(a))\\
&=(\rho(a^{\lambda_{0}}),\ldots,\rho(a^{\lambda_{p-1}}))\\
&=\tilde{\rho}\big(a^{\lambda_{0}},\ldots,a^{\lambda_{p-1}}\big),\end{split}

and as a consequence

(3.10)

\tilde{\rho}^{-1}(\mathbb{F}\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\{(a^{\lambda_{0}},\ldots,a^{\lambda_{p-1}})\mid a\in A\}.

From this remark and
Theorem

3.2
we can easily obtain the following theorem about the lower
central series of
W(A)
.
Before proceeding, we need to introduce some general notation: if
G
is a group and
g\in G
, we set

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}(g)=\Delta^{i-1}(g,1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)\in B(G).

Then one can easily adapt the proof of
Theorem

3.2
to show the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

For every group
G
and every
g\in G
, we have

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}(g)=(g^{\lambda_{i,0}},\ldots,g^{\lambda_{i,i-1}},1,\overset{p-i}{\ldots},1),

where the elements
\lambda_{i,r}
are as in
Theorem

3.2
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
A
be an elementary abelian finite
p
-group, and let
1\leq i\leq p
.
Then the following hold:

(i)

The images of the tuples
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}(a)
, with
a\in A
, yield all
elements of the quotient
\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(A))/\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(A))
, without repetitions.
Furthermore, the map

\begin{matrix}A&\longrightarrow&\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(A))/\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(A))\\[5.0pt]
a&\longmapsto&\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}(a)\end{matrix}

is a group isomorphism.

(ii)

The elements of
\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(A))
can be written in the form

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}(a_{i})\cdots\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{p}(a_{p}),

with
a_{i},\ldots,a_{p}\in A
, without repetitions.

(iii)

For
1\leq i\leq p-1
, the map

\begin{matrix}\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(A))/\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(A))&\longrightarrow&\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(A))/\gamma_{i+2}^{*}(W(A))\\[5.0pt]
\mathbf{a}\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(A))&\longmapsto&\Delta(\mathbf{a})\gamma_{i+2}^{*}(W(A))\end{matrix}

is a group isomorphism.

Proof.

Items (i) and (ii) are an immediate consequence of (
3.10
) and the
corresponding results in
Theorem

3.2
.
Finally, (iii) follows from the isomorphism in (i), applied to both
i
and
i+1
.
∎

Now we rewrite
Theorem

3.4
for an arbitrary multiplicative elementary abelian
finite
p
-group
A
.
To start with, for a given polynomial

f(X)=f_{0}+f_{1}X+\cdots+f_{p-1}X^{p-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X],

we associate the word
X_{0}^{f_{0}}X_{1}^{f_{1}}\cdots X_{p-1}^{f_{p-1}}
, which we denote by
\mathcal{M}(f(X))
.
Since
A
is elementary abelian, this word induces a well-defined homomorphism from
A
to
A
by substitution.
Also we can speak of the word equation
\mathcal{M}(f(X))=1
in
A
and of its set of solutions in
B(A)
.
Now for a tuple
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{p-1})\in B(A)
, we have the following equivalent conditions:

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(\mathbf{a})\text{ is a solution of $\mathcal{L}(f(X))=0$}\

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ \rho(a_{0})f_{0}+\rho(a_{1})f_{1}+\cdots+\rho(a_{p-1})f_{p-1}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ \rho(a_{0}^{f_{0}}a_{1}^{f_{1}}\cdots a_{p-1}^{f_{p-1}})=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ \mathbf{a}\text{ is a solution of $\mathcal{M}(f(X))=1$.}

Since all the polynomials appearing in
Theorem

3.4
have coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
we immediately get the following result.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
A
be an elementary abelian finite
p
-group.
Then for every
2\leq i\leq p
, the subgroup
\gamma_{i}(W(A))
consists of all solutions in
B(A)
of either of the following systems of word equations:

(i)

\mathcal{M}((X-1)^{\ell})=1
, for
p-i+1\leq\ell\leq p-1
.

(ii)

\mathcal{M}(f^{(\ell)}(X))=1
, for
0\leq\ell\leq i-2
, where
f(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}
.

Finally, we record the result corresponding to
Corollary

3.5
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
A
be an elementary abelian finite
p
-group.
Then, for a given
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{p-1})\in B(A)
, we have

(3.11)

\mathbf{a}\in\gamma_{2}(W(A))\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ a_{0}\cdots a_{p-1}=1,

and

(3.12)

\mathbf{a}\in\gamma_{3}(W(A))\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ a_{0}\cdots a_{p-1}=1,\ \ a_{0}a_{1}^{2}\cdots a_{p-2}^{p-1}=1.

After having completed a thorough analysis of the lower central series of
W(A)
, where
A
is an elementary
abelian finite
p
-group, now we proceed to the general case of
W(G)
, where
G
is a finite
p
-group all of
whose lower central factors are elementary abelian.
Before describing the lower central series of
W(G)
, we need a lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group and let
T
be a
\sigma
-invariant subgroup of
B(\gamma_{j}(G))
for some
j\geq 1
.
Then

(3.13)

[T,W(G)]\equiv[T,\langle\sigma\rangle]\equiv\Delta(T)\mod B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)).

In particular, if
J\leq\gamma_{j}(G)
then

(3.14)

[\Delta^{k-1}(B(J)),W(G)]\equiv[\Delta^{k-1}(B(J)),\langle\sigma\rangle]\equiv\Delta^{k}(B(J))\mod B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))

for every
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

We may assume that
B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))=1
.
Since
T\leq B(\gamma_{j}(G))
, it follows that
B(G)
centralizes
T
and so

[T,W(G)]=[T,\langle\sigma\rangle]
.
On the other hand, since
T
is
\sigma
-invariant, we have
[T,\langle\sigma\rangle]=[T,\sigma]

by (
2.9
).
Also, since
T
is abelian, the restriction of
\Delta
to
T
is a group homomorphism and
consequently
\Delta(T)=\{[\mathbf{g},\sigma]\mid\mathbf{g}\in T\}
is a subgroup of
W(G)
.
Thus
[T,\sigma]=\Delta(T)
, which proves (
3.13
).

Now (
3.14
) readily follows by induction on
k
, by observing that
the subgroup
B(J)
is
\sigma
-invariant.
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group of nilpotency class
c
whose abelianisation is elementary abelian, and let
i\geq 1
.
If we write
i=(j-1)p+k
with
j\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq p
, then we have

(3.15)

\gamma_{i}^{*}\big(W(G)\big)=\Delta^{k-1}\big(B(\gamma_{j}(G))\big)\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)).

In particular,

(3.16)

\gamma_{jp+1}^{*}\big(W(G)\big)=B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))

for every
j\geq 0
, and the nilpotency class of
W(G)
is
cp
.

Proof.

We use induction on
i
.
The basis of the induction is obvious, so let
i\geq 2
.
We first assume that
2\leq k\leq p
.
(Note that the basis of the induction corresponds to
k=1
, so in the induction step we can deal
with
k=1
after having proved the result for
2\leq k\leq p
.)
By the induction hypothesis and (
3.14
), we have

(3.17)

\begin{split}\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(G))\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))&=[\gamma_{i-1}^{*}(W(G)),W(G)]\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))\\
&=[\Delta^{k-2}\big(B(\gamma_{j}(G))\big)\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)),W(G)]\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))\\
&=\Delta^{k-1}(B(\gamma_{j}(G)))\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)).\end{split}

This proves in particular, for
k=p
, that

(3.18)

\gamma_{jp}^{*}(W(G))\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))=\Delta^{p-1}(B(\gamma_{j}(G)))\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)).

Now the factor group
\gamma_{j}(G)/\gamma_{j+1}(G)
is elementary abelian, and then

\Delta^{p-1}(B(\gamma_{j}(G)/\gamma_{j+1}(G)))=\gamma_{p}(W(\gamma_{j}(G)/\gamma_{j+1}(G)))

coincides with the set of constant tuples with entries in
\gamma_{j}(G)/\gamma_{j+1}(G)
, by (
3.8
).
Thus we get

(3.19)

\Delta^{p-1}(B(\gamma_{j}(G)))\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))=C_{j}\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)),

where

C_{j}=\{(g,\overset{p}{\ldots},g)\mid g\in\gamma_{j}(G)\}.

Observe that

(3.20)

[C_{j},W(G)]=[C_{j},B(G)]=B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)),

where the first equality holds because constant tuples commute with
\sigma
, and the second
because

[(g,\overset{p}{\ldots},g),(h,1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)]=([g,h],1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)

for every
g\in\gamma_{j}(G)
and every
h\in G
.
Hence taking the commutator with
W(G)
in (
3.18
) and using (
3.19
) and (
3.20
),
we get

(3.21)

\gamma_{jp+1}^{*}(W(G))\cdot[B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)),W(G)]=B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)).

In other words, the normal subgroup
B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))
of
W(G)
coincides with the commutator

[B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)),W(G)]
modulo
\gamma_{jp+1}^{*}(W(G))
.
Since
W(G)/\gamma_{jp+1}^{*}(W(G))
is a finite
p
-group, this can only happen if
B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))

is trivial in this quotient group.
Hence (
3.21
) implies that

(3.22)

\gamma_{jp+1}^{*}(W(G))=B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)).

By taking this value to (
3.17
), we obtain (
3.15
) for
k=2,\ldots,p
.

Finally, assume that
k=1
.
Then since
i\geq 2
, we have
j\geq 2
, and the result follows from (
3.22
), applied with
j-1

in place of
j
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

Note that (
3.22
) can also be written as

\gamma_{jp+1}^{*}(W(G))=\Delta^{p}(B(\gamma_{j}(G)))\cdot B(\gamma_{j+1}(G)).

In other words, (
3.15
) is also valid if
i=(j-1)p+k
and
k=p+1
.

Equality (
3.15
) above says that for
i=(j-1)p+k
with
1\leq k\leq p
, the subgroup
\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(G))

coincides with the lift to
W(G)
of the subgroup
\gamma_{k}^{*}(W(\gamma_{j}(G)/\gamma_{j+1}(G)))
, taking into
account that
W(\gamma_{j}(G)/\gamma_{j+1}(G))
is naturally isomorphic to the quotient

W(\gamma_{j}(G))/B(\gamma_{j+1}(G))
.
By
Remark

3.12
, this also holds for
k=p+1
.
Then the following corollary is an immediate consequence of the results that we have proved for
W(A)
, where

A
is elementary abelian.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group of nilpotency class
c
whose abelianisation is elementary abelian, and let

1\leq i\leq cp
.
If we write
i=(j-1)p+k
with
1\leq j\leq c
and
1\leq k\leq p
, then the following hold:

(i)

The subgroup
\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(G))
consists of all tuples in
B(\gamma_{j}(G))
that are solutions of either of the
following sets of equations:

(a)

\mathcal{M}((X-1)^{\ell})\equiv 1\mod\gamma_{j+1}(G)
, for
p-k+1\leq\ell\leq p-1
.

(b)

\mathcal{M}(f^{(\ell)}(X))\equiv 1\mod\gamma_{j+1}(G)
, for
0\leq\ell\leq k-2
, where
f(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}
.

In particular, if
\mathbf{g}=(g_{0},\ldots,g_{p-1})\in B(\gamma_{j}(G))
then

\mathbf{g}\in\gamma_{(j-1)p+2}(W(G))\ \Longleftrightarrow\ g_{0}\cdots g_{p-1}\in\gamma_{j+1}(G),

and

\mathbf{g}\in\gamma_{(j-1)p+3}(W(G))\ \Longleftrightarrow\ g_{0}\cdots g_{p-1}\in\gamma_{j+1}(G),\ g_{0}g_{1}^{2}\cdots g_{p-2}^{p-1}\in\gamma_{j+1}(G).

(ii)

|\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(G)):\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(G))|=|\gamma_{j}(G):\gamma_{j+1}(G)|
.

(iii)

The map

\begin{matrix}\gamma_{j}(G)/\gamma_{j+1}(G)&\longrightarrow&\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(G))/\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(G))\\[5.0pt]
g\gamma_{j+1}(G)&\longmapsto&\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}(g)\,\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(G))\end{matrix}

is a group isomorphism.

(iv)

For
1\leq k\leq p-1
, the map

\begin{matrix}\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(G))/\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(G))&\longrightarrow&\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(G))/\gamma_{i+2}^{*}(W(G))\\[5.0pt]
\mathbf{g}\gamma_{i+1}^{*}(W(G))&\longmapsto&\Delta(\mathbf{g})\gamma_{i+2}^{*}(W(G))\end{matrix}

is a group isomorphism.

As it turns out, in order to determine the lower central series of GGS-groups of FG-type, we are not going
to need the characterisation of the tuples lying in
\gamma_{i}^{*}(W(G))
given in (i) above
for all
i=(j-1)p+k
with
1\leq k\leq p
, but rather the two special cases
k=2
and
k=3
that appear
explicitly developed therein.
However, our experience with computations carried out with GAP hints that this result will be needed for higher
values of
k
if one wants to study the lower central series of arbitrary GGS-groups.
For this reason, and for completeness, we have decided to state
Corollary

3.13
in all generality.

By (iii) of the previous corollary, every element of
B(\gamma_{j}(G))=\gamma_{(j-1)p+1}^{*}(W(G))
is congruent
modulo
\gamma_{(j-1)p+2}^{*}(W(G))
to a tuple of the form
(g,1,\ldots,1)
with
g\in\gamma_{j}(G)
.
We make this explicit in the following result.

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group of nilpotency class
c
whose abelianisation is elementary abelian, and let

1\leq j\leq c
.
Then for every
\mathbf{g}=(g_{0},\ldots,g_{p-1})\in B(\gamma_{j}(G))
, we have

\mathbf{g}\equiv(g_{0}\cdots g_{p-1},1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)\mod\gamma_{(j-1)p+2}(W(G)).

Proof.

We have

\mathbf{g}^{-1}\cdot(g_{0}\cdots g_{p-1},1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)=(g_{1}\cdots g_{p-1},g_{1}^{-1},\ldots,g_{p-1}^{-1}),

and by (i) of
Corollary

3.13
, this last tuple belongs to
\gamma_{(j-1)p+2}(W(G))
.
∎

4.
Lower central series of
G_{n}
for
n\leq 3

In this section, we will determine the lower central series of the congruence quotient
G_{n}=G/\text{Stab}_{G}(n)

for
n\leq 3
.
The result is trivial for
G_{1}
, which is cyclic of order
p
.
In the case of
G_{2}
, by Theorem 2.4 of
[
9
]
we know that, for an arbitrary GGS-group
G
,
the quotient
G_{2}
is a
p
-group of maximal class of order
p^{t+1}
, where
t
is the rank of the
circulant matrix whose first row is
(e_{1},\dots,e_{p-1},0)
.
By Lemma 2.7 of
[
9
]
, if
G
is non-periodic then
t=p
and
G_{2}
has nilpotency class
p

(actually
G_{2}
is isomorphic to
C_{p}\wr C_{p}
).
As already mentioned at the beginning of
Section

2
, if
g\in G_{2}\smallsetminus\St_{G_{2}}(1)

then
\langle g\rangle
acts uniserially on
\St_{G_{2}}(1)
, and consequently the quotient

\gamma_{i}(G_{2})/\gamma_{i+1}(G_{2})
can be generated by the image of
[b,g,\overset{i-1}{\ldots},g]

for every
i=2,\ldots,p
.
We summarise all this information in the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a non-periodic GGS-group.
Then the following hold:

(i)

We have
|G_{2}:G_{2}^{\prime}|=p^{2}
and
|\gamma_{i}(G_{2}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{2})|=p
for every
i=2,\ldots,p
.
Also
\gamma_{p+1}(G_{2})=1
and
G_{2}
has nilpotency class
p
.

(ii)

For every
i\geq 2
and every
g\in G\smallsetminus\St_{G}(1)
, we have

\gamma_{i}(G_{2})=\langle[b,g,\overset{i-1}{\ldots},g]\rangle\gamma_{i+1}(G_{2}).

In particular,

\gamma_{i}(G_{2})=\langle[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{i-1}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b]\rangle\gamma_{i+1}(G_{2})
.

In the remainder of this section we deal with the case of
G_{3}
.
The idea is to exploit the uniseriality of the action of
G_{3}
on
\St_{G_{3}}(2)
, which we proved in

Theorem

2.2
.
Since

|\St_{G_{3}}(2)|=\frac{|G_{3}|}{|G_{2}|}=\frac{p^{\,p^{2}+1}}{p^{\,p+1}}=p^{\,p^{2}-p}

by Theorem A of
[
9
]
, it follows that the subgroups
[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{3}]

for
0\leq i\leq p^{2}-p
are the only subgroups of
\St_{G_{3}}(2)
that are normal in
G_{3}
, with consecutive
indices equal to
p
.
As a consequence, if a subgroup of
\St_{G_{3}}(2)
of index
p^{i}
is normal in
G_{3}
then it coincides with

[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{3}]
.
On several occasions, we will need to identify in which difference

[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{3}]\smallsetminus[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{i+1}{\ldots},G_{3}]
an element

g\in\St_{G_{3}}(2)
lies, and to this purpose we will use the map
\psi
as follows.

Observe that, by (
2.7
) and (
2.4
), we obtain that

(4.1)

\psi\left(\frac{\St_{G_{3}}(2)}{\gamma_{3}(\St_{G_{3}}(1))}\right)=\frac{G_{2}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{3}(G_{2})}\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times\frac{G_{2}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{3}(G_{2})}

is equal to the base group
B(G_{2}^{\prime}/\gamma_{3}(G_{2}))
of
W(G_{2}^{\prime}/\gamma_{3}(G_{2}))
.
(Recall that we still denote by
\psi
the maps induced by
\psi
in quotients.)
In particular,

|\St_{G_{3}}(2):\gamma_{3}(\St_{G_{3}}(1))|=|G_{2}^{\prime}:\gamma_{3}(G_{2})|^{p}=p^{\,p}

and

\gamma_{3}(\St_{G_{3}}(1))=[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{p}{\ldots},G_{3}].

Let us see how we can use (
4.1
) in order to identify the elements lying
in the subgroups
[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{3}]
for
0\leq i\leq p
.
Since
\St_{G_{3}}(2)=\St_{G_{3}}(1)^{\prime}
is central in
\St_{G_{3}}(1)
modulo
\gamma_{3}(\St_{G_{3}}(1))
, we have

[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{3}]=[\St_{G_{3}}(2),a,\overset{i}{\ldots},a]\gamma_{3}(\St_{G_{3}}(1))

for
0\leq i\leq p
.
Then

\displaystyle\psi\left(\frac{[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{3}]}{\gamma_{3}(\St_{G_{3}}(1))}\right)

\displaystyle=[B(G_{2}^{\prime}/\gamma_{3}(G_{2})),\sigma,\overset{i}{\ldots},\sigma]

\displaystyle=\Delta^{i}(B(G_{2}^{\prime}/\gamma_{3}(G_{2})))

\displaystyle=\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{2}^{\prime}/\gamma_{3}(G_{2}))),

by (
3.7
).
Since
G_{2}^{\prime}/\gamma_{3}(G_{2})
is cyclic of order
p
generated by the image of
[b,a]
, we can detect whether an element

g\in\St_{G_{3}}(2)
lies in
[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{3}]
simply by applying
\psi
to
g
, writing the
components of
\psi(g)
as powers of
[b,a]
modulo
\gamma_{3}(G_{2})
, and checking whether the corresponding exponents
(considered as elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
) satisfy the equations that describe the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
\gamma_{i+1}(W(\mathbb{F}_{p}))
,
as given in
Theorem

3.4
.

We will use this approach in the proof of
Lemma

4.4
, where we determine the position of
some iterated commutators in
a
and
b
of lengths
p
and
p+1
, which will be key to the determination of
the lower central series of
G_{3}
.
We need the following two preliminary results.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{p-1})\in B(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
Then in
W(\mathbb{F}_{p})
we have

[\boldsymbol{\lambda},\sigma,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},\sigma]=(-\alpha+\beta,-2\alpha+\beta,\ldots,-(p-1)\alpha+\beta,\beta),

where
\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\,\lambda_{i}
and
\beta=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\,i\lambda_{i}
.

Proof.

We use the map
\Theta:B(\mathbb{F}_{p})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]/(X^{p}-1)
introduced in
Section

3
.
Let
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\,\lambda_{i}X^{i}
.
Then taking the commutator of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
with
\sigma
,
p-2
times, corresponds under
\Theta
to multiplying

\overline{f(X)}
with
\overline{X-1}^{\,p-2}
.
Since
f(1)=\alpha
and
f^{\prime}(1)=\beta
, we can write
f(X)=\alpha+\beta(X-1)+g(X)(X-1)^{2}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]

and then

\overline{f(X)}\cdot\overline{X-1}^{\,p-2}=\alpha\cdot\overline{X-1}^{\,p-2}+\beta\cdot\overline{X-1}^{\,p-1}

in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]/(X^{p}-1)
.
Now we have

(X-1)^{p-2}=-\sum_{i=0}^{p-2}\,(i+1)X^{i}

in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
, since

(4.2)

\binom{p-2}{i}=\frac{(p-2)(p-3)\cdots(p-i-1)}{i!}\equiv(-1)^{i}(i+1)\mod p,

and we already know from (
3.3
) that

(X-1)^{p-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\,X^{i}.

Hence

\overline{f(X)}\cdot\overline{X-1}^{\,p-2}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-2}\,\left(-(i+1)\alpha+\beta\right)\cdot\overline{X}^{i}+\beta\cdot\overline{X}^{p-1},

and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a GGS-group with defining vector
\mathbf{e}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{p-1})
.
Then

\psi([b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a])\equiv(a^{\delta-2\varepsilon}b^{-2},a^{\delta-3\varepsilon}b^{-3},\ldots,a^{\delta-(p-1)\varepsilon}b^{-(p-1)},a^{\delta},a^{\delta-\varepsilon}b^{-1})\\
\mod(G^{\prime}\times\cdots\times G^{\prime}).

Proof.

Since
G/G^{\prime}=\langle aG^{\prime}\rangle\times\langle bG^{\prime}\rangle
, we can determine separately
the components of the tuple
\psi([b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a])
modulo
\langle a\rangle G^{\prime}
as powers
of
b
, and its components modulo
\langle b\rangle G^{\prime}
as powers of
a
, and then bring these two
results together.

We first determine the components modulo
\langle a\rangle G^{\prime}
.
We have

\psi(b)\equiv(1,\ldots,1,b)\mod(\langle a\rangle G^{\prime}\times\cdots\langle a\rangle G^{\prime}).

Now taking the commutator with
a
in
G
corresponds to taking the commutator with
\sigma
after applying
\psi
.
Since we are interested in the components modulo
\langle a\rangle G^{\prime}
, we want the value of the commutator
of
(1,\ldots,1,b)
with
\sigma
,
p-2
times, in the group
W(G/\langle a\rangle G^{\prime})
.
Since
G/\langle a\rangle G^{\prime}
is cyclic of order
p
, generated by the image of
b
, we can use

Lemma

4.2
, applied to the vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(0,\ldots,0,1)
, and get

\psi([b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a])\equiv(b^{-2},b^{-3},\ldots,b^{-(p-1)},1,b^{-1})\mod(\langle a\rangle G^{\prime}\times\cdots\times\langle a\rangle G^{\prime}).

We can similarly obtain the components modulo
\langle b\rangle G^{\prime}
, again by using
Lemma

4.2
,
this time with
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{p-1},0)
.
Note that we have
\alpha=\varepsilon
and

\beta=e_{2}+2e_{3}+\cdots+(p-2)e_{p-1}=\delta-\varepsilon.

Hence

\psi([b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a])\equiv(a^{\delta-2\varepsilon},a^{\delta-3\varepsilon},\ldots,a^{\delta-(p-1)\varepsilon},a^{\delta},a^{\delta-\varepsilon})\mod(\langle b\rangle G^{\prime}\times\cdots\times\langle b\rangle G^{\prime}).

From the calculations above, it follows that

\psi([b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a])\equiv(a^{\delta-2\varepsilon}b^{-2},a^{\delta-3\varepsilon}b^{-3},\ldots,a^{\delta-(p-1)\varepsilon}b^{-(p-1)},a^{\delta},a^{\delta-\varepsilon}b^{-1})\\
\mod(G^{\prime}\times\cdots\times G^{\prime}),

as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a GGS-group of FG-type.
Then the following hold:

(i)

[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a]\in\gamma_{p}(G_{3})\smallsetminus\St_{G_{3}}(2)
.

(ii)

[b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a,b]\in\St_{G_{3}}(2)\smallsetminus[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]
.

(iii)

[b,a,\overset{p}{\ldots},a]\in[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}]
.

(iv)

[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a,b]\in[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]\smallsetminus[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}]
.

(v)

[b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a,b,a][b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a,b]\in[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]\smallsetminus[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}]
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, let
g=[b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a]
.
Recall from
Lemma

4.3
that

(4.3)

\begin{split}\psi(g)\equiv(a^{\delta-2\varepsilon}b^{-2},a^{\delta-3\varepsilon}b^{-3},\ldots,a^{\delta-(p-1)\varepsilon}b^{-(p-1)},a^{\delta},a^{\delta-\varepsilon}b^{-1})\\
\mod(G^{\prime}\times\cdots\times G^{\prime}),\end{split}

and take into account that

(4.4)

G^{\prime}\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times G^{\prime}=\psi(\St_{G}(1)^{\prime})=\psi(\St_{G}(2)).

(i)
This follows directly from
Theorem

4.1
.

(ii)
Obviously
[g,b]\in\St_{G}(1)^{\prime}=\St_{G}(2)
.
Let us now see that this commutator does not belong to
[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]
.
To this purpose, we take the commutator of (
4.3
) with
\psi(b)
, getting

(4.5)

\begin{split}\psi([g,b])\equiv([b,a]^{-2e_{1}},[b,a]^{-3e_{2}},\ldots,[b,a]^{-(p-1)e_{p-2}},1,[b,a]^{\varepsilon-\delta})\\
\mod(\gamma_{3}(G)\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times\gamma_{3}(G)).\end{split}

By working in
W(G_{2}^{\prime}/\gamma_{3}(G_{2}))
as explained above, the condition

[g,b]\not\in[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]
is equivalent to

(-2e_{1},-3e_{2},\ldots,-(p-1)e_{p-2},0,\varepsilon-\delta)\not\in\gamma_{2}(W(\mathbb{F}_{p})).

By
Corollary

3.5
, we need to check that the sum of the components
of this tuple is not
0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Since

-\sum_{i=1}^{p-2}\,(i+1)e_{i}=-\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\,(i+1)e_{i}=-\varepsilon-\delta,

the sum that we want is
-2\delta
.
This value is not
0
, since
p
is odd and
\delta\neq 0
, because
G
is of FG-type.

(iii)
This time we take the commutator of (
4.3
) with
\psi(a)
.
Taking (
4.4
) into account, together with the fact that

\gamma_{3}(G)\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times\gamma_{3}(G)=\psi(\gamma_{3}(\text{Stab}_{G}(1)))\leq\psi([\St_{G}(1)^{\prime},G])=\psi([\St_{G}(2),G]),

we get

(4.6)

\begin{split}\psi([g,a])&\equiv(ba^{\varepsilon}[a^{\varepsilon},b^{-1}],ba^{\varepsilon}[a^{\varepsilon},b^{-2}],\ldots,ba^{\varepsilon}[a^{\varepsilon},b^{-(p-1)}],ba^{\varepsilon})\\
&\equiv(ba^{\varepsilon}[b,a]^{\varepsilon},ba^{\varepsilon}[b,a]^{2\varepsilon},\ldots,ba^{\varepsilon}[b,a]^{(p-1)\varepsilon},ba^{\varepsilon})\mod{\psi([\St_{G}(2),G])}.\end{split}

Taking the commutator with
a
again, we have

(4.7)

\psi([g,a,a])\equiv([b,a]^{-\varepsilon},\overset{p}{\ldots},[b,a]^{-\varepsilon})\mod{\psi([\St_{G}(2),G,G])}.

By working in
W(G_{2}^{\prime}/\gamma_{3}(G_{2}))
and observing that

(-\varepsilon,\overset{p}{\ldots},-\varepsilon)\in\gamma_{p}(W(\mathbb{F}_{p}))

by
Corollary

3.3
, it follows that

([b,a]^{-\varepsilon},\overset{p}{\ldots},[b,a]^{-\varepsilon})\in\psi([\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{p-1}{\cdots},G_{3}]).

Since
p
is odd, from this and (
4.7
) we conclude that
[g,a,a]\in[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}]
.

(iv)
By taking the commutator of (
4.6
) with
\psi(b)
, we get

(4.8)

\psi([g,a,b])\equiv([b,a]^{e_{1}},[b,a]^{e_{2}},\ldots,[b,a]^{e_{p-1}},[b,a]^{-\varepsilon})\mod(\gamma_{3}(G)\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times\gamma_{3}(G)).

Working again in
W(G_{2}^{\prime}/\gamma_{3}(G_{2}))
, the condition

[g,a,b]\in[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]\smallsetminus[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}]

is equivalent to

(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{p-1},-\varepsilon)\in\gamma_{2}(W(\mathbb{F}_{p}))\smallsetminus\gamma_{3}(W(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

This follows immediately from
Corollary

3.5
, since

e_{1}+e_{2}+\cdots+e_{p-1}-\varepsilon=0

by the definition of
\varepsilon
, and

e_{1}+2e_{2}+\cdots+(p-1)e_{p-1}\neq 0,

since
G
is of FG-type.

(v)
By (ii) and (iv) it is clear that
[g,b,a][g,a,b]\in[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]
.
Let us see that this commutator is not in
[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}]
.
To this end, we take the commutator of (
4.5
) with
\psi(a)
, obtaining

\psi([g,b,a])\equiv([b,a]^{2e_{1}+\varepsilon-\delta},[b,a]^{3e_{2}-2e_{1}},\ldots,[b,a]^{(p-1)e_{p-2}-(p-2)e_{p-3}},\\
[b,a]^{-(p-1)e_{p-2}},[b,a]^{\delta-\varepsilon})\mod(\gamma_{3}(G)\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times\gamma_{3}(G)).

By combining this congruence with (
4.8
), we get that the exponents of
[b,a]

in the tuple
\psi([g,b,a][g,a,b])
are given by the vector

(4.9)

\ \ \ \ (3e_{1}+\varepsilon-\delta,4e_{2}-2e_{1},5e_{3}-3e_{2},\ldots,-(p-2)e_{p-3},e_{p-1}-(p-1)e_{p-2},\delta-2\varepsilon).

Working in
W(G_{2}^{\prime}/\gamma_{3}(G_{2}))
, it follows that
[g,b,a][g,a,b]\not\in[\text{Stab}_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}]

if and only if the vector in (
4.9
) does not lie in
\gamma_{3}(W(\mathbb{F}_{p}))
.
By
Corollary

3.5
, this is equivalent to

3e_{1}+\varepsilon-\delta+\sum_{i=2}^{p-1}\,i\big((i+2)e_{i}-ie_{i-1}\big)=\varepsilon-\delta-\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\,e_{i}=-\delta

being non-zero in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Now this is true, since
G
is of FG-type.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Note that (i) and (iii) hold for an arbitrary non-periodic GGS-group, not only for groups of FG-type.
However, if
G
is not of FG-type then the same proof above shows that

[b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a,b]\in[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]

and

[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a,b]\in[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}].

Now we can start describing the lower central series of
G_{3}
.
We start with the terms
\gamma_{i}(G_{3})
with
i\geq p+1
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
G
be a GGS-group of FG-type.
Then for every
i\geq 1
, we have

(4.10)

\gamma_{p+i}(G_{3})=[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{3}]=\langle[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{p+i-1}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b]\rangle\gamma_{p+i+1}(G_{3}).

Furthermore,

(4.11)

\St_{G_{3}}(2)=\langle[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b,b]\rangle\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3}).

Proof.

By
Theorem

4.1
we have

\gamma_{p}(G_{3})\equiv\langle[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a]\rangle\mod\St_{G_{3}}(2).

It readily follows that

\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})\equiv\langle[b,a,\overset{p}{\ldots},a],[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a,b]\rangle^{G_{3}}\mod[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}].

From (iii) and (iv) of
Lemma

4.4
,
\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})
is contained in

[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]
and contains the element

[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a,b]\in[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]\smallsetminus[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}].

The uniseriality of the action of
G_{3}
on
\St_{G_{3}}(2)
then implies that

(4.12)

\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})=[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3}]=\langle[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a,b]\rangle[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}],

and consequently also that

\gamma_{p+i}(G_{3})=[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},\overset{i}{\ldots},G_{3}]

for every
i\geq 1
.
Since
|\St_{G_{3}}(2):\gamma_{3}(\St_{G}(1))|=p^{\,p}
, it follows in particular that
\gamma_{3}(\St_{G}(1))=\gamma_{2p}(G_{3})
.

On the other hand,

(4.13)

[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{p}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b]\equiv[b,a,\overset{p}{\ldots},a]^{\varepsilon^{p}}\,\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}\,[b,a,\overset{i}{\ldots},a,b,a,\overset{p-i-1}{\ldots},a]^{\varepsilon^{p-1}}\mod\gamma_{p+2}(G_{3}),

since commutators of length
p+1
are multilinear modulo
\gamma_{p+2}(G_{3})
and any commutator with at least three
occurrences of
b
lies in
\gamma_{3}(\St_{G}(1))
.
Also all elements
[b,a,\overset{p}{\ldots},a]
and

[b,a,\overset{i}{\ldots},a,b,a,\overset{p-i-1}{\ldots},a]
for
0\leq i\leq p-3

belong to
[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}]=\gamma_{p+2}(G_{3})
.
Thus (
4.13
) reduces to

[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{p}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b]\equiv\big([b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a,b,a]\,[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a,b]\big)^{\varepsilon^{p-1}}\mod\gamma_{p+2}(G_{3}).

Since
\varepsilon\neq 0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we conclude from (v) in
Lemma

4.4
that

\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})=\langle[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{p}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b]\rangle\gamma_{p+2}(G_{3}).

Now (
4.10
) follows from uniseriality and from
Theorem

2.2
.

Finally, since
|\St_{G_{3}}(2):\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})|=p
, we obtain (
4.11
) from (ii) of

Lemma

4.4
arguing similarly to the last paragraph.
∎

In the previous lemma, we could have given more easily a generator of each of the sections

\gamma_{p+i}(G_{3})/\gamma_{p+i+1}(G_{3})
by using commutators of the form

[b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a,b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{i}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b].

However, we have taken the extra effort of replacing this
(p+i)
-fold commutator with

[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{p+i-1}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b]

for the sake of a more uniform writing of these generators, not only for
G_{3}
but also for an
arbitrary congruence quotient
G_{n}
, as we will prove in
Section

5
.

Now it is easy to give the final result about the lower central series of
G_{3}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
G
be GGS-group of FG-type.
Then the following hold:

(i)

The indices between consecutive terms of the lower central series of
G_{3}
are given as follows:

\displaystyle|\gamma_{i}(G_{3}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{3})|=\begin{cases}p^{2}\quad&\text{if $i\in\{1,p\}$},\\
p\quad&\text{if $i\in\{2,\dots,p-1,p+1\dots,p^{2}-1\}$},\\
1\quad&\text{if $i\geq p^{2}$}.\end{cases}

In particular,
G_{3}
has nilpotency class
p^{2}-1
.

(ii)

Furthermore, for
i\in\{2,\dots,p-1,p+1\dots,p^{2}-1\}
, we have

\gamma_{i}(G_{3})=\langle[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{i-1}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b]\rangle\gamma_{i+1}(G_{3}),

and, on the other hand,

\gamma_{p}(G_{3})=\langle[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b],[b,a^{\varepsilon}b,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a^{\varepsilon}b,b]\rangle\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3}).

(iii)

For all
i\geq p+1
we have
\psi(\gamma_{i}(G_{3}))=\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{2}))
.

Proof.

We start by proving (i) and (ii).
These immediately follow from
Eq.

4.10
for
i\geq p+1
, using the uniseriality of the action of

G_{3}
on
\St_{G_{3}}(2)
to ensure that the indices between consecutive terms are equal to
p
.
Also, since
|\St_{G_{3}}(2)|=p^{\,p^{2}-p}
, it follows that the first trivial term of the lower central series
of
G_{3}
is
\gamma_{p^{2}}(G_{3})
, and
G_{3}
is nilpotent of class
p^{2}-1
.

Regarding the sections
\gamma_{i}(G_{3})/\gamma_{i+1}(G_{3})
with
i\leq p-1
, observe that (
4.11
)
implies that
\St_{G_{3}}(2)\leq\gamma_{p}(G_{3})
.
Hence (i) and (ii) follow immediately from
Theorem

4.1
in this case.
Now the result for
\gamma_{p}(G_{3})/\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})
is a direct consequence of
Theorem

4.1
and
(
4.11
).

Let us finally prove (iii).
We have
\gamma_{p+1}(W(G_{2}))=B(G_{2}^{\prime})=\psi(\St_{G_{3}}(2))
, where the first equality follows from

Theorem

3.11
and the second from (
4.4
).
On the other hand, since
|\gamma_{j}(G_{2}):\gamma_{j+1}(G_{2})|=p
for
j=2,\ldots,p
, it follows from (ii)
of
Corollary

3.13
that
|\gamma_{i}(W(G_{2})):\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{2}))|=p
for
i=p+1,\ldots,p^{2}
.
Also
\psi^{-1}(\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{2})))
is normal in
G_{3}
and properly contained in
\St_{G_{3}}(2)

for
i\geq p+1
.
Now (
4.10
) and the uniseriality of the action of
G_{3}
on
\St_{G_{3}}(2)
yield that

\psi^{-1}(\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{2})))=\gamma_{i}(G_{3})
for
i\geq p+1
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We end this section by showing that
Theorem

4.7
is not valid for a GGS-group that is not of FG-type.
As a consequence, also Theorem A fails in this case.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
G
be a non-periodic GGS-group that is not of FG-type.
Then
|\gamma_{i}(G_{3}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{3})|\geq p^{2}
for some
i\in\{2,\ldots,p-1\}
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that

(4.14)

|\gamma_{i}(G_{3}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{3})|=p\qquad\text{for all $i=2,\ldots,p-1$.}

In particular, by (ii) of
Theorem

4.1
, we have

\gamma_{p-1}(G_{3})=\langle[b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a]\rangle\gamma_{p}(G_{3})
,
and consequently

\gamma_{p}(G_{3})=\langle[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a],[b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a,b]\rangle\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3}).

In particular, we have

(4.15)

|\gamma_{p}(G_{3}):\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})|\leq p^{2},

since the exponent of
\gamma_{p}(G_{3})/\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})
divides
p
.

Now set
N=[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}]
.
By
Remark

4.5
,
[b,a,\overset{p-2}{\ldots},a,b]
is central in
G_{3}
modulo
N
.
Hence

\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})\equiv\langle[b,a,\overset{p}{\ldots},a],[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a,b]\rangle\gamma_{p+2}(G_{3})\mod N.

Now, by (iii) of
Lemma

4.4
and
Remark

4.5
, both

[b,a,\overset{p}{\ldots},a]
and
[b,a,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},a,b]
belong to
N
.
Thus
\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})\equiv\gamma_{p+2}(G_{3})\mod N
and, since
G_{3}
is a finite
p
-group, this implies that

\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})\leq N
.
Now, on the one hand,

|G_{3}:\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})|\geq|G_{3}:N|=|G_{3}:\St_{G_{3}}(2)|\cdot|\St_{G_{3}}(2):[\St_{G_{3}}(2),G_{3},G_{3}]|=p^{\,p+3}

while, on the other hand, by the standing assumption (
4.14
),

|G_{3}:\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})|=\prod_{i=1}^{p}\,|\gamma_{i}(G_{3}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{3})|=p^{\,p}\,|\gamma_{p}(G_{3}):\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})|.

It follows that
|\gamma_{p}(G_{3}):\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})|\geq p^{3}
, which is a contradiction by
(
4.15
).
This proves the result.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem A

In this section we prove the main theorem of the paper, describing the lower central series of
a GGS-group
G
of FG-type.
As already explained in the introduction, it relies on the solution of the same problem for the
congruence quotients
G_{n}
.

In the remainder, we denote by
c(n)
the nilpotency class of
G_{n}
.
From the previous section, we have
c(1)=1
,
c(2)=p
and
c(3)=p^{2}-1
.
We also let
\ell(m)
,
r(m)
,
x(i)
, and
y_{j}(i)
be as defined in the introduction.
Note that, by
Theorem

4.7
, we have

\gamma_{i}(G_{3})=\langle x(i)\rangle\,\gamma_{i+1}(G_{3})\quad\text{for $2\leq i\leq c(3)$, $i\neq p$,}

in which case
|\gamma_{i}(G_{3}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{3})|=p
, and

\gamma_{p}(G_{3})=\langle x(p),y_{p}(p)\rangle\,\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3}),

with
|\gamma_{p}(G_{3}):\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3})|=p^{2}
.

The proof of Theorem A will easily follow from our next theorem, which generalises the
results about
G_{3}
in the last paragraph to an arbitrary congruence quotient
G_{n}
.
More precisely, we determine the nilpotency class of
G_{n}
, and we show that the index

|\gamma_{i}(G_{n}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})|
is always
p
or
p^{2}
and that the
quotient
\gamma_{i}(G_{n})/\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})
can always be generated either by
x(i)
alone or by

x(i)
together with an element of the form
y_{j}(i)
.
In both of the last results, which of the two possibilities happens will be determined by the position of
i

with respect to the sequences
\{\ell(m)\}_{m\geq 0}
and
\{r(m)\}_{m\geq 0}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G
be a GGS-group of FG-type, and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
n\geq 3
.
Then

c(n)=p^{n-1}-p^{n-3}-\cdots-p-1

and the following hold:

(i)

If
\ell(m)\leq i<r(m)
for some
m\in\{0,\ldots,n-2\}
, then

|\gamma_{i}(G_{n}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})|=p^{2},

and
\gamma_{i}(G_{n})=\langle x(i),y_{\ell(m)}(i)\rangle\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})
.

(ii)

If
r(m)\leq i<\ell(m+1)
for some
m\in\{0,\ldots,n-3\}
, or if
r(n-2)\leq i\leq c(n)
then

|\gamma_{i}(G_{n}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})|=p,

and
\gamma_{i}(G_{n})=\langle x(i)\rangle\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})
.

Remark 5.2
.

According to
Theorem

5.1
, for a given a positive integer
i
, if
i
lies in one of the
intervals
[\ell(m),r(m)-1]
with
0\leq m\leq n-2
, then we have
|\gamma_{i}(G_{n}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})|=p^{2}
,
while for
i
in the intervals
[r(m),\ell(m+1)-1]
with
0\leq m\leq n-3
or in the final interval
[r(n-2),c(n)]
,
we have
|\gamma_{i}(G_{n}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})|=p
.
We refer to the former intervals as
p^{2}
-intervals
of
G_{n}
, and to the latter as

p
-intervals
of
G_{n}
.
Note that the number of consecutive indices
i
for which we have
|\gamma_{i}(G_{n}):\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})|=p^{2}
in the

p^{2}
-interval
[\ell(m),r(m)-1]
is

r(m)-\ell(m)=p^{m-1}.

On the other hand, if we compare the intervals of
G_{n}
with those of
G_{n-1}
, we can see that
G_{n}
has one
more interval of each type.
The extra
p^{2}
-interval for
G_{n}
is
[\ell(n-2),r(n-2)-1]
, providing
p^{n-3}
new sections with index
p^{2}

with respect to
G_{n-1}
.
Since
r(n-2)\leq c(n-1)
, these sections of index
p^{2}
come all from the final
p
-interval of
G_{n-1}
:
passing from
G_{n-1}
to
G_{n}
has the effect of increasing the index of these sections from
p
to
p^{2}
.
At the same time, all the quotients of the lower central series that were trivial in
G_{n-1}
, i.e. corresponding
to indices
i\geq c(n-1)+1
, integrate into the final
p
-interval of
G_{n}
.
This does not come as a surprise; we have
\gamma_{i}(G_{n})\leq\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)
for those indices, and as we
know from
Theorem

2.2
, the action of
G_{n}
on
\St_{G_{n}}(n-1)
is uniserial, since
G
is non-periodic.

Remark 5.3
.

Since every element of the form
x(i)
with
i>1
or of the form
y_{j}(i)
with
i>j
is an
i
-fold commutator
whose last entry is
a^{\varepsilon}b
, it follows from
Theorem

5.1
that

(5.1)

\gamma_{i}(G_{n})\equiv[\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n}),a^{\varepsilon}b]\mod\gamma_{i+1}(G_{n})

whenever
i
is not of the form
\ell(m)
for
0\leq m\leq n-2
or, using the terminology of
Remark

5.2
,
whenever
i
is not the starting index of a
p^{2}
-interval for
G_{n}
.

The proof of
Theorem

5.1
proceeds by induction on
n
, the base case
n=3
being provided by

Theorem

4.7
.
For this reason, most of the results in this section are stated assuming that

Theorem

5.1
holds true for
G_{n-1}
.
Under this assumption, and as a consequence of
Corollary

3.13
, if
i=(j-1)p+k
with

1\leq j\leq c(n-1)
and
1\leq k\leq p
, we obtain that

(5.2)

|\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})):\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))|=|\gamma_{j}(G_{n-1}):\gamma_{j+1}(G_{n-1})|

is equal to
p^{2}
if
\ell(m)\leq j<r(m)
for some
0\leq m\leq n-3
, and equal to
p

otherwise.

We start with a couple of results regarding the effect of taking commutators with
\psi(G_{n})
in some
special cases.
In the following proposition, we consider the commutator
[\mathbf{g},\psi(G_{n})]
with a single tuple

\mathbf{g}\in\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))
, and then we apply this to determine commutators of the form

[\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),\psi(G_{n})]
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G
be a non-periodic GGS-group and let
n\geq 3
.
Then for a given tuple
\mathbf{g}\in\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))
we have

(5.3)

[\mathbf{g},\psi(G_{n})]\equiv\langle[\mathbf{g},\psi(a^{\varepsilon}b)]\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

Furthermore, if we write
i=(j-1)p+k
with
j\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq p
,
then the following hold:

(i)

If
1\leq k\leq p-1
then

[\mathbf{g},\psi(G_{n})]\equiv\langle\Delta(\mathbf{g})\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

(ii)

If
k=p
and we write
\mathbf{g}\equiv(g,\overset{p}{\ldots},g)\mod\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))
with

g\in\gamma_{j}(G_{n-1})
,
then

[\mathbf{g},\psi(G_{n})]\equiv\langle([g,a^{\varepsilon}b],1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

Proof.

To begin with, note that

\psi(G_{n})=\psi(G_{n}^{\prime}\langle b\rangle\langle a\rangle)=\psi(G_{n})^{\prime}\langle\psi(b)\rangle\langle\sigma\rangle,

and that

[\mathbf{g},\psi(G_{n})^{\prime}]\leq[\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),\psi(G_{n})^{\prime}]\leq[\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),\psi(G_{n}),\psi(G_{n})]\leq\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})),

where the second inclusion follows from Hall’s Three Subgroup Lemma.
Hence

(5.4)

[\mathbf{g},\psi(G_{n})]\equiv\langle[\mathbf{g},\psi(b)],[\mathbf{g},\sigma]\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})),

taking into account that commutators of elements of
\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))
and elements of

W(G_{n-1})
behave bilinearly modulo
\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1}))
.

Suppose first that
1\leq k\leq p-1
.
Then

\displaystyle[\mathbf{g},\psi(b)]\in[B(\gamma_{j}(G_{n-1})),B(G_{n-1})]

\displaystyle=B(\gamma_{j+1}(G_{n-1}))

\displaystyle=\gamma_{jp+1}(W(G_{n-1}))\leq\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

Thus from (
5.4
) we have

[\mathbf{g},\psi(G_{n})]\equiv\langle[\mathbf{g},\sigma]\rangle=\langle\Delta(\mathbf{g})\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

On the other hand,

[\mathbf{g},\psi(a^{\varepsilon}b)]=[\mathbf{g},\sigma^{\varepsilon}\psi(b)]\equiv[\mathbf{g},\sigma]^{\varepsilon}\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

This completes the proof of the lemma in the case
1\leq k\leq p-1
, since
\varepsilon\neq 0
under the
standing assumption that
G
is non-periodic.

Assume now that
k=p
.
Since
\sigma
commutes with constant tuples, we have
[\mathbf{g},\sigma]\in\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1}))
.
Thus, by (
5.4
) we have

[\mathbf{g},\psi(G_{n})]\equiv\langle[\mathbf{g},\psi(b)]\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

Now

\displaystyle[\mathbf{g},\psi(b)]

\displaystyle\equiv[(g,\overset{p}{\ldots},g),(a^{e_{1}},\ldots,a^{e_{p-1}},b)]

\displaystyle=([g,a^{e_{1}}],\ldots,[g,a^{e_{p-1}}],[g,b])

\displaystyle\equiv([g,a^{\varepsilon}b],1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})),

where the last congruence follows from
Corollary

3.14
.
Since

[\mathbf{g},\psi(a^{\epsilon}b)]=[\mathbf{g},\sigma^{\epsilon}\psi(b)]\equiv[\mathbf{g},\psi(b)]\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})),

this concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
G
be a non-periodic GGS-group and let
n\geq 3
.
Then for a given
i\geq 1
, we have

(5.5)

[\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),\psi(G_{n})]\equiv[\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),\psi(a^{\varepsilon}b)]\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

Furthermore, if we write
i=(j-1)p+k
with
j\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq p
, then the following hold:

(i)

If
1\leq k\leq p-1
then

(5.6)

[\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),\psi(G_{n})]\equiv\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

(ii)

If
k=p
then

(5.7)

[\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),\psi(G_{n})]\equiv B([\gamma_{j}(G_{n-1}),a^{\varepsilon}b])\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

Proof.

First of all, note that (
5.5
) follows immediately from (
5.3
).

(i)
If
1\leq k\leq p-1
then

\displaystyle[\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),\psi(G_{n})]

\displaystyle\equiv\langle\Delta(\mathbf{g})\mid\mathbf{g}\in\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})),

where the first congruence follows from (i) of
Proposition

5.4
, and the second one from (iv)
of
Corollary

3.13
.
This proves the result.

(ii)
If
k=p
then by (iii) of
Corollary

3.13
and (ii) of
Proposition

5.4
, we have

[\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),\psi(G_{n})]\equiv\langle([g,a^{\varepsilon}b],1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)\mid g\in\gamma_{j}(G_{n-1})\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

By
Corollary

3.14
, this means that

[\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),\psi(G_{n})]\equiv B([\gamma_{j}(G),a^{\varepsilon}b])\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})),

as desired.
∎

Next we study the relation between the image of the lower central series of
G_{n}
under
\psi

and the lower central series of
W(G_{n-1})
.
We will need the following straightforward lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
G
be a group and let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
.
If the subgroup
\gamma_{i}(G)
has finite index in
G
for some
i\geq 1
then
we have
|G:\gamma_{i}(G)|=|G/N:\gamma_{i}(G/N)|
if and only if
N\leq\gamma_{i}(G)
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
G
be a GGS-group of FG-type, and let
n\geq 4
.
If
Theorem

5.1
is true for
G_{n-1}
then the following hold for
p+2\leq i\leq p\cdot c(n-1)
:

(i)

If we write
i=(j-1)p+k
with
1\leq k\leq p
then

(5.8)

\psi(\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n}))\equiv\langle\psi(x(i-1))\rangle\equiv\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}(x(j))\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1})).

In particular,

(5.9)

\psi(\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n}))\leq\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})).

(ii)

We have

(5.10)

|\psi(\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n}))\,\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1})):\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))|=p.

Thus if
|\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})):\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))|=p
then

(5.11)

\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))=\psi(\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n}))\,\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1})).

Proof.

(i)
We use induction on
i
.
Note that it suffices to prove (
5.8
).
Indeed, since
x(j)\in\gamma_{j}(G_{n-1})
, we have

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}(x(j))\in\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))
by (iii) of
Corollary

3.13
.
Thus (
5.8
) implies that
\psi(\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n}))\leq\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))
.

We first deal with the case
i=p+2
, relying on the information about
G_{3}
gathered in

Theorem

4.7
.
We know that

\displaystyle\gamma_{p+2}(W(G_{2}))

\displaystyle=\psi(\gamma_{p+1}(G_{3}))=\psi(\langle x(p+1)\rangle\,\gamma_{p+2}(G_{3}))

\displaystyle=\langle\,\psi(x(p+1))\,\rangle\,\gamma_{p+3}(W(G_{2})).

Since
\psi(\St_{G_{n}}(3))=B(\St_{G_{n-1}}(2))
by (
2.6
), it follows that

(5.12)

\begin{split}\gamma_{p+2}(W(G_{n-1}))\,B(\St_{G_{n-1}}(2))&=\psi(\gamma_{p+1}(G_{n}))\,B(\St_{G_{n-1}}(2))\\
&=\langle\,\psi(x(p+1))\,\rangle\,\gamma_{p+3}(W(G_{n-1}))\,B(\St_{G_{n-1}}(2)).\end{split}

Now since
Theorem

5.1
holds for
G_{n-1}
, we have

|G_{n-1}:\gamma_{p}(G_{n-1})|=p^{\,p}=|G_{2}:\gamma_{p}(G_{2})|,

and then
Lemma

5.6
implies that
\St_{G_{n-1}}(2)\leq\gamma_{p}(G_{n-1})
.
Consequently

B(\St_{G_{n-1}}(2))\leq B(\gamma_{p}(G_{n-1}))=\gamma_{p^{2}-p+1}(W(G_{n-1}))\leq\gamma_{p+3}(W(G_{n-1})),

since
p
is odd.
Hence we deduce from (
5.12
) that

\gamma_{p+2}(W(G_{n-1}))=\psi(\gamma_{p+1}(G_{n}))\,\gamma_{p+3}(W(G_{n-1}))=\langle\,\psi(x(p+1))\,\rangle\,\gamma_{p+3}(W(G_{n-1})).

On the other hand, since the image of
x(2)
generates the quotient
\gamma_{2}(G)/\gamma_{3}(G)
,
we have

\gamma_{p+2}(W(G_{n-1}))=\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{2}(x(2))\rangle\gamma_{p+3}(W(G_{n-1}))

by (iii) of
Corollary

3.13
.
This completes the proof of (
5.8
) in the case
i=p+2
.

Assume now that the result is true for
i
and let us prove it for
i+1
.
By the induction hypothesis, (
5.8
) holds true.
As a consequence,

(5.13)

\begin{split}\psi(\gamma_{i}(G_{n}))=[\psi(\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n})),\psi(G_{n})]&\equiv[\psi(x(i-1)),\psi(G_{n})]\\
&\equiv[\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}(x(j)),\psi(G_{n})]\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).\end{split}

Now, since
\psi(\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n}))\leq\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))
by induction, we get in particular

\psi(x(i-1))\in\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))
.
Then, by (
5.3
), we obtain

\psi(\gamma_{i}(G_{n}))\equiv\langle\psi(x(i))\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})),

since
x(i)=[x(i-1),a^{\varepsilon}b]
.
This is one of the congruences in (
5.8
), written for
i+1
.
Let us prove the other congruence.
On the one hand, if
1\leq k\leq p-1
then by (
5.13
) and (i) of
Proposition

5.4
,

\psi(\gamma_{i}(G_{n}))\equiv\langle\Delta(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}(x(j)))\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k+1}(x(j))\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})).

On the other hand, if
k=p
then note that, by
Lemma

3.6
and (
3.2
),
we have

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{p}(x(j))\equiv(x(j),\overset{p}{\ldots},x(j))\mod\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1})),

since
\gamma_{j}(G_{n-1})/\gamma_{j+1}(G_{n-1})
is of exponent
p
.
Then, by (ii) of
Proposition

5.4
,

\psi(\gamma_{i}(G_{n}))\equiv\langle([x(j),a^{\varepsilon}b],1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}(x(j+1))\rangle\mod\gamma_{i+2}(W(G_{n-1})),

as desired.

(ii)
By (i), we only need to determine the order of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}(x(j))
modulo

\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))
.
According to (iii) of
Corollary

3.13
, this order coincides with the order of
x(j)
modulo

\gamma_{j+1}(G_{n-1})
.
Now the latter is equal to
p
, since
Theorem

5.1
holds for
G_{n-1}
.
∎

Thus the image of
\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n})
under
\psi
covers
\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))
modulo
\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))

provided that
|\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})):\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))|=p
, but falls short if this index is
p^{2}
.
As we next see, in this latter case
\gamma_{i-2}(G_{n})
will do.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
G
be a GGS-group of FG-type, and assume that
Theorem

5.1
holds for
G_{n-1}
,
where
n\geq 4
.
If
i\geq p+1
is such that
|\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})):\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))|=p^{2}
then the following hold:

(i)

If we write
i=(j-1)p+k
with
i\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq p
, then
j
belongs to a
p^{2}
-interval

[\ell,r-1]
of
G_{n-1}
.

(ii)

We have

(5.14)

\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))\equiv\langle\psi(y_{(\ell-1)p-1}(i-2))\rangle\mod\psi(\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n}))\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1})).

In particular,

(5.15)

\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))\leq\psi(\gamma_{i-2}(G_{n}))\,\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1})).

Proof.

(i)
This follows immediately from (
5.2
).

(ii)
Clearly, it suffices to prove the first assertion.
By (i), we have
i\in[(\ell-1)p+1,rp]
.
We use induction on
i
in this range of values.
Let us first prove the result for
i=(\ell-1)p+1
.
Note that the condition
i\geq p+1
implies that
\ell>1
.
Since
[\ell,r-1]
is a
p^{2}
-interval for
G_{n-1}
, we have
|\gamma_{\ell-1}(G_{n-1}):\gamma_{\ell}(G_{n-1})|=p
.
Also
\gamma_{\ell-1}(G_{n-1})=\langle x(\ell-1)\rangle\gamma_{\ell}(G_{n-1})
, since
G_{n-1}

satisfies
Theorem

5.1
.
From (iii) of
Corollary

3.13
, the quotient
\gamma_{i-2}(W(G_{n-1}))/\gamma_{i-1}(W(G_{n-1}))

can be generated by the image of the element
\mathbf{g}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{p-1}(x(\ell-1))
.
Note that, by
Lemma

3.6
and (
4.2
),

\mathbf{g}\equiv(x(\ell-1)^{-1},x(\ell-1)^{-2},\ldots,x(\ell-1)^{-(p-1)},1)\mod\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1})),

since
\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))=B(\gamma_{\ell}(G_{n-1}))
and
\gamma_{\ell-1}(G_{n-1})/\gamma_{\ell}(G_{n-1})
is of
exponent
p
.
Then we have

(5.16)

\begin{split}[\mathbf{g},\psi(b)]&\equiv([x(\ell-1)^{-1},a^{e_{1}}],[x(\ell-1)^{-2},a^{e_{2}}],\ldots,[x(\ell-1)^{-(p-1)},a^{e_{p-1}}],1)\\
&\equiv([x(\ell-1),a]^{-e_{1}},[x(\ell-1),a]^{-2e_{2}},\ldots,[x(\ell-1),a]^{-(p-1)e_{p-1}},1)\\
&\equiv([x(\ell-1),a^{-\delta}],1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)\mod\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1})),\end{split}

where the last congruence follows from
Corollary

3.14
.
Note that
[\mathbf{g},\psi(b)]\in\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))
.

On the other hand, since
G_{n-1}
satisfies
Theorem

5.1
, we have

\gamma_{\ell}(G_{n-1})=\langle x(\ell),y_{\ell}(\ell)\rangle\,\gamma_{\ell+1}(G_{n-1}).

As a consequence,

(5.17)

\begin{split}\gamma_{i}(W(G_{n-1}))&=\langle(x(\ell),1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1),(y_{\ell}(\ell),1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)\rangle\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1}))\\
&=\langle(y_{\ell}(\ell),1,\overset{p-1}{\ldots},1)\rangle\psi(\gamma_{i-1}(G_{n}))\gamma_{i+1}(W(G_{n-1})),\end{split}

where the first equality follows from (iii) of
Corollary

3.13
, and the second, from
(
5.8
).
Now, since
x(\ell)=[x(\ell-1),a^{\varepsilon}b]
and
y_{\ell}(\ell)=[x(\ell-1),b]
, we have

[x(\ell-1),a]\equiv y_{\ell}(\ell)^{-\varepsilon^{-1}}\mod\langle x(\ell)\rangle\gamma_{\ell+1}(G_{n-1}),

Finally, the proof of Corollary B is an immediate consequence of the following standard result in
profinite group theory.
We include its proof for the convenience of the reader.
Take into account that, by definition, we consider
\gamma_{i}(\hat{G})
to be the
closed

generated by all
i

Proposition 5.9
.

Let

be a residually finite group and let

be its profinite completion.
If
|G:\gamma_{i}(G)|
is finite for some

|\hat{G}:\gamma_{i}(\hat{G})|=|G:\gamma_{i}(G)|
.

Proof.

The group
\hat{G}
is the inverse limit of the finite quotients of
G
.
Since
|G:\gamma_{i}(G)|
is finite, it follows that

is isomorphic to a quotient

, and consequently
|\hat{G}:\gamma_{i}(\hat{G})|\geq|G:\gamma_{i}(G)|

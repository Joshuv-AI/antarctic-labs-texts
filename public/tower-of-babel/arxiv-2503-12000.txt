Types of elements in non-commutative Poisson algebras and Dixmier Conjecture
Zhennan Pan, Gang Han School of Mathematics, Zhejiang University pzn1025@163.com, mathhgg@zju.edu.cn

Abstract
Non-commutative Poisson algebras ar e the algebras having an associative algebra structure and a Lie algebra structure together with the Leibniz law. Let P P be a non-commutative Poisson algebra over some algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. For any z ∈ P z\in P , there exist four subalgebras of P P associated with the inner derivation a ​ d z ad_{z} on P P . Based on the relationships between these four subalgebras, elements of P P can be divided into eight types. We will mainly focus on two types of non-commutative Poisson algebras: the usual Poisson algebras and the associative algebras with the commutator as the Poisson bracket. The following problems are studied for such non-commutative Poisson algebras: how the type of an element changes under homomorphisms between non-commutative Poisson algebras, how the type of an element changes after localization, and what the type of the elements of the form z 1 ⊗ z 2 z_{1}\otimes z_{2} and z 1 ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ z 2 z_{1}\otimes 1+1\otimes z_{2} is in the tensor product of non-commutative Poisson algebras P 1 ⊗ P 2 P_{1}\otimes P_{2} . As an application of above results, one knows that Dixmier Conjecture for A 1 A_{1} holds under certain conditions. Some properties of the Weyl algebras are also obtained, such as the commutativity of certain subalgebras.

2
Types of elements in non-commutative Poisson algebras

Let us recall the definition of non-commutative Poisson algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
P
be a vector space over some field
F
endowed with two bilinear operations: a product ”
\cdot
” and a bracket

\{\cdot,\cdot\}
, such that

(1)
(P,\ \cdot)
is
an associative unital algebra over
F
, which is not necessarily commutative,

(2)
(P,\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a Lie algebra over
F
,

(3) (Leibniz rule)
For any
x,y,z\in P
,

\{x,y\cdot z\}=\{x,y\}\cdot z+y\cdot\{x,z\}
.

Then we will call
P
a non-commutative Poisson algebra.

By (3) in the Definition
2.1
and the skew-symmetry of
\{,\}
, one has

\{y\cdot z,x\}=y\cdot\{z,x\}+\{y,x\}\cdot z,\quad for\ any\ x,y,z\in P.

We will mainly deal with two (large) classes of non-commutative Poisson algebras in this paper: A non-commutative Poisson algebra
P
such that
(P,\ \cdot)
is commutative will be referred to as the non-commutative Poisson algebras of Class 1; a non-commutative associative algebra
A
such that
\{a,b\}=[a,b]=ab-ba
for any
a,b\in A
will be referred to as a non-commutative Poisson algebra of Class 2. Note that there are also non-commutative Poisson algebras such that
\{,\}
is not the commutator.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be non-commutative Poisson algebras over
K
. A
K
-linear map
f:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
is called a Poisson homomorphism if
f
is a homomorphism of associative algebras and is also a homomorphism of Lie algebras. Then the objects of non-commutative Poisson algebras with morphisms being the Poisson homomorphisms form a categoty. Note that the category of Poisson algebras and the category of unital associative algebras are both subcategories of the category of non-commutative Poisson algebras.

Let
P
be a non-commutative Poisson algebra. The center of

(P,\ \cdot)
and
(P,\{,\})
will be denoted by
Z(P,\ \cdot)
and
Z(P,\{,\})
respectively. Let
Z(P)=Z(P,\ \cdot)\cap Z(P,\{,\})
be the center of
P
. It is directly verified that
Z(P,\{,\})
is a Poisson subalgebra of
P
, while
Z(P)
is an associative subalgebra of

(P,\ \cdot)
. It is easy to see that for non-commutative Poisson algebras of Class 1 or 2,

Z(P)=Z(P,\{,\}).

In the rest of this section,
P
will always denote a non-commutative Poisson algebra over
K
of Class 1 or 2.

Let

Der_{K}(P)=\{f\in End_{K}(P)\mid\forall z,w\in P,f(zw)=f(z)w+zf(w),f(\{z,w\})=\{f(z),w\}+\{z,f(w)\}.\}

It is clear that
Der_{K}(P)
is a Lie algebra over
K
. For
z\in A
, define

ad_{z}:P\rightarrow P,\quad w\mapsto\{z,w\}.

Then
ad_{z}\in Der_{K}(P)
. One has

ad:P\rightarrow Der_{K}(P),\quad z\mapsto ad_{z},

which is a Lie algebra homomorphism with
\ker ad=Z(P,\{,\})
. We define

\displaystyle Ev(z)=\big\{\lambda\in K\mid\exists x\in P^{*}\text{ s.t. }ad_{z}(x)=\lambda x\big\},

\displaystyle F(z)=\big\{x\in P\mid\dim_{K}K[ad_{z}](x)<\infty\big\},

\displaystyle N(z)=\big\{x\in P\mid\exists m\text{ s.t. }ad_{z}^{m}(x)=0\big\},

\displaystyle C(z)=\big\{x\in P\mid ad_{z}(x)=0\big\},

\displaystyle D(z)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in Ev(z)}D(z,\lambda),\quad D(z,\lambda):=\big\{x\in P\mid ad_{z}(x)=\lambda x\big\}.

The set
Ev(z)
is an additive sub-monoid of
K
. In particular,
0\in Ev(z)
.

F(z)
also has a similar expression:

F(z)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in Ev(z)}F(z,\lambda),\quad F(z,\lambda)=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\ker(ad_{z}-\lambda)^{n}.

Thus
N(z)=F(z,0)
. One has
F(z)=N(z)
and
D(z)=C(z)
iff
Ev(z)=\{0\}
, and
D(z)\supsetneq C(z)
iff
Ev(z)\supsetneq\{0\}
.

We call
F(z)
the torsion algebra of
z
,
N(z)
the nil-algebra of
z
,
D(z)
the eigenvalue algebra of
z
,
C(z)
the centralizer of
z
. We will refer to
F(z),N(z),D(z),C(z)
as the algebras
associated with

z
. They satisfy the following relations:

N(z)\subseteq F(z),\quad D(z)\subseteq F(z),\quad C(z)=N(z)\cap D(z).

The following lemma obviously holds.

Lemma 2.2
.

Take
z\in P
. If
F(z)=N(z)
, then
D(z)=C(z)
and
Ev(z)=\{0\}
. If
F(z)=D(z)
, then
N(z)=C(z)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
P
be a non-commutative Poisson algebra and
z\in P
.

1. If
Ev(z)=\{0\}
, then write

z\in\Omega_{0}(P)
, if
C(z)=P
; Clearly,
\Omega_{0}(P)=Z(P)
;

z\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime}(P)
, if
C(z)=F(z)\subsetneq P
;

z\in\Omega_{1}(P)
, if
C(z)\subsetneq N(z)=P
;

z\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}(P)
, if
C(z)\subsetneq N(z)\subsetneq P
;

2. If
Ev(z)\supsetneq\{0\}
, then write

z\in\Omega_{2}(P)
, if
D(z)=F(z)=P
;

z\in\Omega_{2}^{\prime}(P)
, if
D(z)=F(z)\subsetneq P
;

z\in\Omega_{3}(P)
, if
D(z)\subsetneq F(z)=P
;

z\in\Omega_{3}^{\prime}(P)
, if
D(z)\subsetneq F(z)\subsetneq P
.

When there is no ambiguity, we abbreviate
\Omega_{i}(P)
and
\Omega^{\prime}_{i}(P)
as
\Omega_{i},\Omega_{i}^{\prime},i=0,1,2,3
.

Elements in
\Omega_{1}\ (\text{resp. }\Omega_{1}^{\prime},\ \Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{1}^{\prime})
are called strictly nilpotent (resp. weakly nilpotent, nilpotent), elements in
\Omega_{2}\ (\text{resp. }\Omega_{2}^{\prime},\ \Omega_{2}\cup\Omega_{2}^{\prime})
are called strictly semisimple (resp. weakly semisimple, semisimple), and elements in
\Omega_{3}\ (\text{resp. }\Omega_{3}^{\prime},\ \Omega_{3}\cup\Omega_{3}^{\prime})
are called strictly Jordan (resp. weakly Jordan, Jordan).

So the elements in
P
are divided into the eight disjoint sets above and each element of
P
is of one of the eight types above. It is clear that the type of an element does not change after applying an automorphism of
P
.

Let

\Omega(P)=\Omega_{0}(P)\cup\Omega_{1}(P)\cup\Omega_{2}(P)\cup\Omega_{3}(P),\quad\Omega^{\prime}(P)=\Omega^{\prime}_{0}(P)\cup\Omega^{\prime}_{1}(P)\cup\Omega^{\prime}_{2}(P)\cup\Omega^{\prime}_{3}(P).

It is clear that
\Omega^{\prime}(P)=P\setminus\Omega(P)
. If
u\in\Omega(P)
then
F(u)=P
, and if
w\in\Omega^{\prime}(P)
, then
F(w)\subsetneq P
. Elements in
\Omega(P)
will be called strict, and elements in
\Omega^{\prime}(P)
will be called weak.

For the Weyl algebra
A_{1}
, the sets
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{1}^{\prime},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{2}^{\prime},\Omega_{0}^{\prime}
are respectively
\Delta_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,5
in
[
D1968
]
.

If
z
is nilpotent, then
Ev(z)=\{0\}
and
D(z)=C(z)
; if
z
is semisimple, then
N(z)=C(z)
. The following conclusion holds:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
z\in P,Ev(z)\supsetneq\{0\}
. If
C(z)\subsetneq N(z)
and
F(z)=P
, then
z\in\Omega_{3}
.
If
C(z)\subsetneq N(z)
and
F(z)\subsetneq P
, then
z\in\Omega_{3}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

When
C(z)\subsetneq N(z)
,
D(z)\subsetneq N(z)D(z)\subseteq F(z)
, so the above conclusion holds.
∎

We can consider two elements commuting under the bracket in the non-commutative Poisson algebra
P
. Let
z,w\in P,\{z,w\}=0
, we have
ad_{z}ad_{w}=ad_{w}ad_{z}
, and it is direct to prove that

F(z)\cap F(w)\subseteq F(z+w).

One has the following property.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
z,w\in P.
Assume
\{z,w\}=0
. If
z\in\Omega_{1},w\in\Omega_{2}
, then
z+w\in\Omega_{3}
.

Proof.

We have
P=F(z)\cap F(w)\subseteq F(z+w)\subseteq P
, so equality holds. It is known that
\{z+w,w\}=0
. For any
\lambda\in Ev(w),v\in D(w,\lambda)
, there exists a sufficiently large integer
n
such that

(ad_{z+w}-\lambda)^{n}(v)=(ad_{z+w}-ad_{w})^{n}(v)=ad_{z}^{n}(v)=0.

Thus
Ev(w)\subseteq Ev(z+w),D(w,\lambda)\subseteq F(z+w,\lambda)
. Since

P=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in Ev(w)}D(w,\lambda)=\bigoplus_{\mu\in Ev(z+w)}F(z+w,\mu),

we conclude that
Ev(w)=Ev(z+w),D(w,\lambda)=F(z+w,\lambda)
.

If for any
\lambda\in Ev(w),D(w,\lambda)=D(z+w,\lambda)
, then
z=(z+w)-w\in Z(P)
, which is a contradiction. Therefore, there exists
\lambda_{0}\in Ev(z+w)
such that
D(z+w,\lambda_{0})\subsetneq F(z+w,\lambda_{0})
. Hence
D(z+w)\subsetneq F(z+w)=P,z+w\in\Omega_{3}
.
∎

On the contrary, we can consider how to decompose the elements in
\Omega_{3}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
P
be a non-commutative Poisson algebra of Class 1 or 2. Assume

and
z\in\Omega_{3}
. Then there exist
z_{1}\in\Omega_{1},z_{2}\in\Omega_{2},
such that
\{z_{1},z_{2}\}=0
and

. If there also exist

such that
\{z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime}\}=0
and

, then
z_{1}-z_{1}^{\prime}=z_{2}-z_{2}^{\prime}\in Z(P)

Proof.

It is known that

. We can define an inner derivation

on

as following:

d_{2}(v)=\lambda v,\quad v\in F(z,\lambda),\lambda\in Ev(z).

Clearly,

\{d_{1},d_{2}\}=0
, and

is strictly nilpotent,

The proof of the last statement is similar to the proof of Proposition

∎

How the type of an element changes under a Poisson homomorphism

Let
P
be a non-commutative Poisson algebra over
K
.
For
z\in P
,
\lambda\in Ev(z)
and
k\geq 0
, let
F^{k}(z,\lambda)=\ker(ad_{z}-\lambda)^{k+1}.
One has

F(z,\lambda)=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}F^{k}(z,\lambda),\quad F(z)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in Ev(z)}F(z,\lambda).

Then another three sets can be presented as following,

C(z)=F^{0}(z,0),\quad N(z)=F(z,0),\quad D(z)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in Ev(z)}D(z,\lambda),

where
D(z,\lambda)=F^{0}(z,\lambda)
.
By the Jacobi identity,
ad_{z}\{u,w\}=\{u,ad_{z}w\}+\{ad_{z}u,w\},u,w\in P

ad_{z}^{n}\{u,w\}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\{ad_{z}^{i}u,ad_{z}^{n-i}w\}.

Thus if
u,w\in F(z)
, then
\{u,w\}\in F(z)
. Similarly,
uw\in F(z)
, so
F(z)
is a non-commutative Poisson subalgebra of
P
.
N(z),D(z),C(z)
are also non-commutative Poisson subalgebras of
P

Let
m:P\otimes P\rightarrow P,a\otimes b\mapsto ab
be the multiplication. For

one has

(\phi-\lambda-\mu)\cdot m=m\cdot[(\phi-\lambda)\otimes 1+1\otimes(\phi-\mu)],

it follows that for

\left(\phi-\lambda-\mu\right)^{n}\left(ab\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\left(\phi-\lambda\right)^{i}\left(a\right)\cdot\left(\phi-\mu\right)^{n-i}\left(b\right).

(3.1)

Let
F^{-1}(z,\lambda)=\{0\}
. By the abvoe, one has

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
z\in P,\lambda,\mu\in Ev(z,P),k,l\geq 0
, then
m
induces

F^{k}(z,\lambda)\times F^{l}(z,\mu)\rightarrow F^{k+l}(z,\lambda+\mu),

(3.2)

and

From the above, one has

\displaystyle[F^{k}(z,\lambda)/F^{k-1}(z,\lambda)]

\displaystyle\times[F^{l}(z,\mu)/F^{l-1}(z,\mu)]\rightarrow F^{k+l}(z,\lambda+\mu)/F^{k+l-1}(z,\lambda+\mu),

\displaystyle(a+F^{k-1}(z,\lambda)

\displaystyle,b+F^{l-1}(z,\mu))\mapsto ab+F^{k+l-1}(z,\lambda+\mu).

Let

For

Definition 3.2

Let

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

GK(A)<\infty
. Let

be a subalgebra of

\exists z\in A
, such that

Proof.

First, notice that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B\cdot z^{k}

For

0=\{s,tt^{-1}\}=t\{s,t^{-1}\}+\{s,t\}t^{-1},

\{s,t^{-1}\}=-t^{-1}\{s,t\}t^{-1}.

Similarly,
\{s^{-1},t\}=-s^{-1}\{s,t\}s^{-1}.
Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle 0=\{s^{-1},tt^{-1}\}

\displaystyle=t\{s^{-1},t^{-1}\}+\{s^{-1},t\}t^{-1}

\displaystyle=t\{s^{-1},t^{-1}\}-s^{-1}\{s,t\}s^{-1}t^{-1}.

Thus

\{s^{-1},t^{-1}\}=t^{-1}s^{-1}\{s,t\}s^{-1}t^{-1}.

Similarly, by
\{ss^{-1},t^{-1}\}=0

\{s^{-1},t^{-1}\}=s^{-1}t^{-1}\{s,t\}t^{-1}s^{-1}.

So

(4.1)

I=\bigoplus_{\mu\in Ev(x,P)}F(x,I,\mu).

So there exists
\mu\in Ev(x,P)
and
0\neq w\in D(x,I,\mu)
satisfying
zw=u\in P,
thus
z=uw^{-1}\in PC^{-1}
. So one also has
F(x,\widetilde{P})\subseteq PC^{-1}
, and the equality holds.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume
x\in P
is strictly semisimple, then
F(x,\widetilde{P})=D(x,\widetilde{P})=PC^{-1}
, where
C=\left(\bigcup_{\lambda\in Ev(x,P)}D(x,P,\lambda)\right)\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof.

Assume
u\in P,w\in C.
As
x
is strictly semisimple,
u=\sum_{i}u_{i},u_{i}\in D(x,P,\lambda_{i})
. One has
u_{i}w^{-1}\in D(x,\widetilde{P}),
thus
uw^{-1}\in D(x,\widetilde{P})
,
PC^{-1}\subseteq D(x,\widetilde{P}).

On the other hand, as
F(x,P)=P
, by Theorem
4.3

F(x,\widetilde{P})=PC^{-1}\subseteq D(x,\widetilde{P})\subseteq F(x,\widetilde{P}),

thus the equalities hold.
∎

For example, the strictly semisimple element
pq\in A_{1}
is weakly semisimple in
\widetilde{A_{1}}
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Assume
x\in P
is strictly nilpotent, then
F(x,\widetilde{P})=N(x,\widetilde{P})=PC^{-1},
where
C=C(x,P)\backslash\{0\}.

Proof.

Assume
u\in P,w\in C,
then
ad_{x}(uw^{-1})=ad_{x}(u)w^{-1}.
As
x
is strictly nilpltent,
\exists m>0
such that
ad_{x}^{m}(u)=0
. Thus
ad_{x}^{m}(uw^{-1})=0.
So
PC^{-1}\subseteq N(x,\widetilde{P}).

On the other hand, as
F(x,P)=P
and
Ev(P)=\{0\}
, by Theorem
4.3

F(x,\widetilde{P})=PC^{-1}\subseteq N(x,\widetilde{P})\subseteq F(x,\widetilde{P}),

thus the equalities hold.
∎

For example, the strictly nilpotent element
p\in A_{1}
is weakly nilpotent in
\widetilde{A_{1}}
. The following result can be proved analogously.

Corollary 4.6
.

Assume
x\in P
is of strictly Jordan type
(x\in\Omega_{3}(P)),
then
F(x,\widetilde{P})=PC^{-1}
with
C=\left(\bigcup_{\lambda\in Ev(x,P)}D(x,P,\lambda)\right)\backslash\{0\}
.

5
Tensor Product of non-commutative Poisson algebras and classification of elements of the form
z_{1}\otimes z_{2}

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be two non-commutative Poisson algebras over
K
. Let
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
be their tensor product, which is an associative algebra. Let
\phi_{i}:P_{i}\rightarrow P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
be the natural inclusion for
i=1,2
. Assume that there exist a bracket
\{,\}
on
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
such that
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
is a non-commutative Poisson algebra, and the natural inclusions
\phi_{i}
are Poisson homomorphisms for
i=1,2
with
\{\phi_{1}(P_{1}),\phi_{2}(P_{2})\}=0
. It is clear that if the bracket exists then it must be unique.

For
a_{1}\otimes a_{2},b_{1}\otimes b_{2}\in P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\{a_{1}\otimes a_{2},b_{1}\otimes b_{2}\}

\displaystyle=\{a_{1}\otimes a_{2},b_{1}\otimes 1\cdot 1\otimes b_{2}\}

\displaystyle=b_{1}\otimes 1\cdot\{a_{1}\otimes a_{2},1\otimes b_{2}\}+\{a_{1}\otimes a_{2},b_{1}\otimes 1\}\cdot 1\otimes b_{2}

\displaystyle=b_{1}a_{1}\otimes\{a_{2},b_{2}\}+\{a_{1},b_{1}\}\otimes a_{2}b_{2}.

(5.1)

Similarly one has

\displaystyle\{a_{1}\otimes a_{2},b_{1}\otimes b_{2}\}

\displaystyle=\{a_{1}\otimes a_{2},1\otimes b_{2}\cdot b_{1}\otimes 1\}

\displaystyle=\{a_{1},b_{1}\}\otimes b_{2}a_{2}+a_{1}b_{1}\otimes\{a_{2},b_{2}\}.

(5.2)

By the equality of (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) one has

\{a_{1},b_{1}\}\otimes[a_{2},b_{2}]=[a_{1},b_{1}]\otimes\{a_{2},b_{2}\},

(5.3)

which does not hold for arbitrary non-commutative Poisson algebras
P_{1},P_{2}
.

If
P_{1},P_{2}
are both non-commutative Poisson algebras of Class 1, then (
5.3
) holds. It is easy to verify that the bracket on
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
defined by

\{a_{1}\otimes a_{2},b_{1}\otimes b_{2}\}=\{a_{1},b_{1}\}\otimes a_{2}b_{2}+b_{1}a_{1}\otimes\{a_{2},b_{2}\}

makes
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
into a non-commutative Poisson algebra of Class 1 satisfying the desired properties.

If
P_{1},P_{2}
are both non-commutative Poisson algebras of Class 2, then (
5.3
) holds. It is easy to verify that the bracket on
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
defined by

\{a_{1}\otimes a_{2},b_{1}\otimes b_{2}\}=[a_{1}\otimes a_{2},b_{1}\otimes b_{2}]

makes
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
into a non-commutative Poisson algebra of Class 2 satisfying the desired properties.

Now we provide the definition of degree maps and filtrations on non-commutative Poisson algebras which are suitable for our purpose.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(P,\cdot,\{,\})
be a non-commutative Poisson algebra. A map
u:P\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\}
is called a degree map if it satisfies that for any
z,w\in P
,

1.
u(\{z,w\})\leq u(zw)=u(z)+u(w).

2.
u(z+w)\leq\max\{u(z),u(w)\}.

3.
u(z)=-\infty
iff
z=0.

Definition 5.2
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-filtration or simply a filtration of the non-commutative Poisson algebra
P
is a sequence of
K
-subspaces

\cdots\subseteq P_{i-1}\subseteq P_{i}\subseteq P_{i+1}\subseteq\cdots,\quad i\in\mathbb{Z},

such that

1\in P_{0},\quad P_{i}\cdot P_{j}\subseteq P_{i+j}\ \text{and}\ \{P_{i},P_{j}\}\subseteq P_{i+j}\text{ for all }i,j\in\mathbb{Z},\quad P=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{i}.

The filtration
\{P_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
(or simply
\{P_{t}\}
) will be called finite if each
P_{t}
is finite dimensional, and will be called discrete if
P_{t}=\{0\}
for all
t<0
.

With the filtration, we can obtain the associated graded non-commutative Poisson algebra.

Proposition and Definition 5.3
.

Let
\{P_{t}\}
be a filtration on the non-commutative Poisson algebra
P
. Let

gr\ P=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{i}/P_{i-1},

The product and bracket on
gr\ P
are defined as follows. For
x+P_{i-1}\in P_{i}/P_{i-1},y+P_{j-1}\in P_{j}/P_{j-1}
,
let

(x+P_{i-1})\cdot(y+P_{j-1})=xy+P_{i+j-1},\quad\{x+P_{i-1},y+P_{j-1}\}=\{x,y\}+P_{i+j-1},

which are well-defined and can be extended to
gr\ P
bilinearly.
Then
gr\ P
is a graded non-commutative Poisson algebra, which will be called the associated (graded) non-commutative Poisson algebra of
P
.

The proof is straightforward and is omitted.

If
P
is of Class 1 (resp. Class 2), then the associated algebra
gr\ P
is of Class 1 (resp. Class 2).

Given a degree map
u:P\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\}
over the non-commutative Poisson algebra
P
, there exists a filtration
\{P_{t}\}
on
P
as follows:

P_{t}=\{a\in P\mid u(a)\leq t\},\quad t\in\mathbb{Z}.

It is clear that
P_{i}\cdot P_{j}\subseteq P_{i+j}\ \text{and}\ \{P_{i},P_{j}\}\subseteq P_{i+j}\text{ for all }i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
which will be referred as the filtration induced by
u
.

Using the definitions above, it can be concluded that
(P,\ \cdot)
is a domain at this point. Furthermore, if
(gr\ P,\{,\})
is commutative, then
\{P_{i},P_{j}\}\subseteq P_{i+j-1}
, so
u(\{z,w\})\leq u(z)+u(w)-1
for all
z,w\in P.

When the induced filtration
\{P_{t}\}
is finite, the non-commutative Poisson algebra
P
has another property.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
u:P\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\}
is a degree map on the non-commutative Poisson algebra
P
,
\{P_{t}\}
is the induced filtration which is finite. Then for
z\in P
,
u(z)=0
iff
z\in K^{\times}
.

Proof.

The set
B=u^{-1}(0)\cup\{0\}
is a finite-dimensional domain over
K
, which must be a division algebra. As
K
is algebraically closed, one has
B=K
.
∎

Now, we proceed to discuss the classification of the element
z_{1}\otimes z_{2}
in
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
(with
z_{i}\in P_{i}
for
i=1,2
) in the following setup:

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be non-commutative Poisson algebras of the same class (Class 1 or Class 2)
, and let
u_{i}:P_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{-\infty\}
for
i=1,2
be degree maps on
P_{1},P_{2}
respectively. Assume that the filtrations
\{P_{1,t}\}
and
\{P_{2,t}\}
induced by
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
respectively are both finite and discrete. The associated non-commutative Poisson algebras are
gr\ P_{1}
and
gr\ P_{2}
.

The sequences
\{P_{1,t}\otimes P_{2}\}
and
\{P_{1}\otimes P_{2,t}\}
are filtrations on
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
, and we can define maps
u_{1}^{*},u_{2}^{*}
on
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
as follows:

For
0\neq T\in P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
, let
u_{1}^{*}(T)
denote the smallest integer
n
such that
T\in P_{1,n}\otimes P_{2}
, and let
u_{2}^{*}(T)
denote the smallest integer
m
such that
T\in P_{1}\otimes P_{2,m}
. Let
u_{1}^{*}(0)=u_{2}^{*}(0)=-\infty
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Take
z_{i}\in P_{i}
for
i=1,2
and let
\Theta=z_{1}\otimes z_{2}
. Then

1. Assume
z_{i}\in Z(P_{i})
for
i=1,2
, then
\Theta\in Z(P_{1}\otimes P_{2})
.

2. Assume exactly one of
z_{i}
belongs to
Z(P_{i})
,
i=1,2
. Without loss of generality, we let
z_{1}\in Z(P_{1}),z_{2}\notin Z(P_{2})
. In this case, the possible forms of the four subalgebras related to
\Theta
are as follows.

F(\Theta)
: If
z_{1}\notin K
, then
F(\Theta)=P_{1}\otimes N(z_{2})
; if
z_{1}\in K^{\times}
, then
F(\Theta)=P_{1}\otimes F(z_{2})
.

D(\Theta)
: If
z_{1}\notin K
, then
D(\Theta)=P_{1}\otimes C(z_{2})
; if
z_{1}\in K^{\times}
, then
D(\Theta)=P_{1}\otimes D(z_{2})
.

N(\Theta)
:
N(\Theta)=P_{1}\otimes N(z_{2})
.

C(\Theta)
:
C(\Theta)=P_{1}\otimes C(z_{2})
.

Proof.

1. When
z_{1}\in Z(P_{1}),z_{2}\in Z(P_{2})
, it is clear that
z_{1}\otimes z_{2}\in Z(P_{1}\otimes P_{2})=\Omega_{0}(P_{1}\otimes P_{2})
.

2. When
z_{1}\in Z(P_{1}),z_{2}\notin Z(P_{2})
, let
T=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes w_{i}\in P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
.
I
is a finite set,
\{v_{i}\}
are linearly independent over
K
,
\{w_{i}\}
are also linearly independent over
K
. Let
\varphi_{2}=ad_{z_{2}}
. It is easy to obtain by induction that

ad_{\Theta}^{n}(T)=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}z_{1}^{n}\otimes\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{i}).

(5.4)

There is a degree map
u_{2}
on
P_{2}
, so
(P_{2},\ \cdot)
is a domain. Therefore for any
n\geq 1
,
\{v_{i}z_{1}^{n}\}_{i}
are also linearly independent over
K
. Clearly,
P_{1}\otimes N(z_{2})\subseteq N(\Theta)
. If
T\in N(\Theta)
and
ad_{\Theta}^{n}(T)=0
, then
\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{i})=0
for all
i\in I
, so
N(\Theta)\subseteq P_{1}\otimes N(z_{2})
, and equality holds. Similarly,
C(\Theta)=P_{1}\otimes C(z_{2})
. The remaining cases to consider are
F(\Theta)
and
D(\Theta)
.

For
F(\Theta):

If
z_{1}\notin K,

u_{1}(z_{1})>0,
and there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that
w_{i_{0}}\notin N(P_{2})
, then

u_{1}^{*}(ad_{\Theta}^{n}(T))\geq u_{1}(v_{i_{0}}z_{1}^{n})=u_{1}(v_{i_{0}})+nu_{1}(z_{1}),\quad n\geq 0.

Given
u_{1}(z_{1})>0
, it follows that
\sup_{n\geq 0}u_{1}^{*}(ad_{\Theta}^{n}(T))=+\infty,T\notin F(\Theta)
. Conversely, if
T\in F(\Theta)
, then for all
i\in I,w_{i}\in N(z_{2}),T\in P_{1}\otimes N(z_{2})\subseteq N(\Theta)\subseteq F(\Theta)
, Thus the equalitis hold, and
Ev(\Theta)=\{0\}
.

If
z_{1}\in K^{\times}
,
u_{1}(z_{1})=0
and there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that
w_{i_{0}}\notin F(z_{1})
, then
\sup_{n\geq 0}\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{i_{0}})=+\infty
. Since
\{v_{i}z_{1}^{n}\}_{i}
are linearly independent over
K
, we have
u_{2}^{*}(ad^{n}_{\Theta}(T))\geq u_{2}(\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{i_{0}}))
, so

\sup_{n\geq 0}u_{2}^{*}(ad_{\Theta}^{n}(T))=+\infty,

which implies
T\notin F(\Theta)
. Conversely, if for all
i\in I,w_{i}\in F(z_{2})
, we can let
W_{i}=K[\varphi_{2}](w_{i})
,
M=\max_{i\in I}\{u_{1}(v_{i})\}
, then for all
n\geq 0,u_{1}(v_{i}z_{1}^{n})=u_{1}(v_{i})\leq M
, so
v_{i}z_{1}^{n}\in P_{1,M}
, and

ad^{n}_{\Theta}(T)\in\sum_{i\in I}P_{1,M}\otimes W_{i},

where
P_{1,M},W_{i}
are finite-dimensional over
K
. Thus
T\in F(\Theta)
.

For
D(\Theta)
: As we have proved, when
z_{1}\notin K
we have
Ev(\Theta)=\{0\},D(\Theta)=C(\Theta)=P_{1}\otimes C(\Theta)
. When
z_{1}\in K^{\times}
, we have

\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes(z_{1}\varphi_{2}(w_{i})-\lambda w_{i})=0.

Since
\{v_{i}\}
are linearly independent over
K
,
w_{i}\in D(z_{2}),T\in P_{1}\otimes D(z_{2})\subseteq D(\Theta)
, and the equality holds.
∎

The remaining case to consider is when both
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are non-central elements. We have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume
z_{1}\notin Z(P_{1}),z_{2}\notin Z(P_{2})
and the associated non-commutative Poisson algebras
gr\ P_{1},gr\ P_{2}
are both commutative under Poisson brackets. Let
\Theta=z_{1}\otimes z_{2},
then

F(\Theta)=N(\Theta)=N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2}),\quad D(\Theta)=C(\Theta),\quad Ev(\Theta)=\{0\}.

Proof.

Take
v\in P_{1},w\in P_{2}
, let

\varphi_{1}=ad_{z_{1}},\quad\varphi_{2}=ad_{z_{2}},\quad R_{1}(v)=vz_{1},\quad L_{2}(w)=z_{2}w.

The equations
\varphi_{1}R_{1}=R_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}L_{2}=L_{2}\varphi_{2}
hold, and

(\varphi_{1}\otimes L_{2})\cdot(R_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2})=(R_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2})\cdot(\varphi_{1}\otimes L_{2}).

Regardless of whether
P
is of Class 1 or Class 2, one has

\displaystyle ad_{\Theta}(v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=\{z_{1},v\}\otimes z_{2}w+vz_{1}\otimes\{z_{2},w\}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{1}(v)\otimes L_{2}(w)+R_{1}(v)\otimes\varphi_{2}(w)

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{1}\otimes L_{2}+R_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2})(v\otimes w).

Thus

\displaystyle ad_{\Theta}^{n}(v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{1}\otimes L_{2}+R_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2})^{n}(v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\varphi_{1}^{k}(v)z_{1}^{n-k}\otimes z_{2}^{k}\varphi_{2}^{n-k}(w)

\displaystyle=vz_{1}^{n}\otimes\varphi_{2}^{n}(w)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\varphi_{1}^{k}(v)z_{1}^{n-k}\otimes z_{2}^{k}\varphi_{2}^{n-k}(w).

(5.5)

If
\varphi_{1}^{m}(v)=0,\varphi_{2}^{l}(w)=0,
where
m,l>0
, then
ad_{\Theta}^{m+l}(v\otimes w)=0
. Therefore

\displaystyle N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2})\subseteq N(\Theta)\subseteq F(\Theta).

(5.6)

Define the natural map
\pi:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{2}/N(z_{2}),w\mapsto\bar{w}
. If
T\notin P_{1}\otimes N(z_{2})
, then let

T=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes w_{i}+\sum_{j\in J}\widetilde{v}_{j}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{j},

where
I
and
J
are finite sets,
I\neq\emptyset
, and
\{\bar{w_{i}}\}
are linearly independent over
K
. If
J\neq\emptyset
, then
\{\widetilde{w}_{j}\}\subseteq N(z_{2})
are also linearly independent over
K
. When the coefficients
c_{i}\in K
are not all zero,
\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}w_{i}\notin N(z_{2})
. Furthermore, for
n\geq 1
,

\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{i})=\varphi_{2}^{n}(\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}w_{i})\neq 0.

Hence,
\{\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{i})\}_{i\in I}
are linearly independent over
K
.

It is known that
(gr\ P_{1},\{,\})
is commutative, thus

u_{1}(\varphi_{1}(v))\leq u_{1}(v)+u_{1}(z_{1})-1,\quad v\in P_{1}.

Define
M_{n}=\max_{i\in I}\{u_{1}(v_{i})\}+nu_{1}(z_{1})
. Using the above inequality, for
1\leq k\leq n
,

\displaystyle u_{1}(\varphi_{1}^{k}(v_{i})z_{1}^{n-k})

\displaystyle\leq u_{1}(v_{i})+k(u_{1}(z_{1})-1)+(n-k)u_{1}(z_{1})

\displaystyle=u_{1}(v_{i})+nu_{1}(z_{1})-k

\displaystyle\leq M_{n}-k.

For any
j\in J
, there exists
k_{j}\geq 0
such that
\varphi_{2}^{k_{j}}(\widetilde{w}_{j})\neq 0,\varphi_{2}^{k_{j}+1}(\widetilde{w}_{j})=0
. When
n,k
satisfy
0\leq n-k\leq k_{j}
, the following holds:

\displaystyle u_{1}(\varphi_{1}^{k}(\widetilde{v}_{j})z_{1}^{n-k})

\displaystyle\leq u_{1}(\widetilde{v_{j}})+nu_{1}(z_{1})-k

\displaystyle=u_{1}(\widetilde{v}_{j})-\max_{i\in I}\{u_{1}(v_{i})\}+\max_{i\in I}\{u_{1}(v_{i})\}+nu_{1}(z_{1})-n+n-k

\displaystyle\leq u_{1}(\widetilde{v}_{j})-\max_{i\in I}\{u_{1}(v_{i})\}+M_{n}-n+k_{j}

\displaystyle=M_{n}-n+l_{j},\quad l_{j}=u_{1}(\widetilde{v}_{j})-\max_{i\in I}\{u_{1}(v_{i})\}+k_{j}.

For any
j\in J,

l_{j}
is a constant. Therefore when
n>\max_{j\in J}\{k_{j},l_{j}\}
, the following two inequalities hold:

u_{1}(\varphi_{1}^{k}(v_{i})z_{1}^{n-k})<M_{n},\quad 1\leq k\leq n,i\in I,

u_{1}(\varphi_{1}^{k}(\widetilde{v}_{j})z_{1}^{n-k})<M_{n},\quad n-k_{j}\leq k\leq n,j\in J.

From equation (
5.5
), it can be concluded that:

\displaystyle ad_{\Theta}^{n}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}(v_{i}z_{1}^{n}\otimes\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{i})+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\varphi_{1}^{k}(v_{i})z_{1}^{n-k}\otimes z_{2}^{k}\varphi_{2}^{n-k}(w_{i}))+

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k:0\leq n-k\leq k_{j}}\binom{n}{k}\varphi_{1}^{k}(\tilde{v}_{j})z_{1}^{n-k}\otimes z_{2}^{k}\varphi_{2}^{n-k}(\tilde{w}_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i:u_{1}(v_{i})=M_{0}}v_{i}z_{1}^{n}\otimes\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{i})+\sum_{h}\widehat{v}_{h}\otimes\widehat{w}_{h},\quad u_{1}(\widehat{v}_{h})<M_{n}.

\{\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{i})\}_{i\in I}
are linearly independent over
K
, so

u_{1}^{*}(ad_{\Theta}^{n}(T))=M_{n},\quad n>\max_{j\in J}\{k_{j},l_{j}\}.

Also, because
z_{1}\notin Z(P_{1}),u_{1}(z_{1})>0
, we have
M_{n}<M_{n+1}
, so
T\notin F(\Theta)
. Conversely
T\in F(\Theta)
implies
T\in P_{1}\otimes N(z_{2}),F(\Theta)\subseteq P_{1}\otimes N(z_{2})
.
By symmetry, we have
T\in N(z_{1})\otimes P_{2}
, so
F(\Theta)\subseteq N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2})
.

From equation (
5.6
) and Lemma
2.2
, we can conclude that
F(\Theta)=N(\Theta)=N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2}),

D(\Theta)=C(\Theta)
, and
Ev(\Theta)=\{0\}
.
∎

Based on the above theorem, we can further discuss the possible types of
\Theta
when both
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are non-central elements.

Corollary 5.7
.

With the same conditions as in Theorem
5.6
.

If
C(z_{1})\subsetneq N(z_{1})
or
C(z_{2})\subsetneq N(z_{2})
, then
\Theta
is nilpotent, and
\Theta
is strictly nipotent iff
z_{1},z_{2}
are strictly nilpotent.

If
N(z_{1})=C(z_{1})
and
N(z_{2})=C(z_{2})
, then
\Theta\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime}(P_{1}\otimes P_{2})
.

If
N(z_{1})=C(z_{1})
or
N(z_{2})=C(z_{2})
, then
C(\Theta)=C(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2})
.

Proof.

If
C(z_{1})\subsetneq N(z_{1})
, take
v\in N(z_{1})\setminus C(z_{1})
. Then
v\otimes 1\in N(\Theta)
while

ad_{\Theta}(v\otimes 1)=\{z_{1},v\}\otimes z_{2}\neq 0.

Thus
C(\Theta)\subsetneq N(\Theta)
, and
\Theta
is nilpotent by Theorem
5.6
. Similarly, when
C(z_{2})\subsetneq N(z_{2})
, we can deduce that
\Theta
is nilpotent. Theorem
5.6
also tells us that
F(\Theta)=P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
iff
N(z_{1})=P_{1},N(z_{2})=P_{2}
, which means that
z_{1},z_{2}
are strictly nilpotent.

If
N(z_{1})=C(z_{1})
and
N(z_{2})=C(z_{2})
, then

F(\Theta)=C(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2})\subseteq C(\Theta)\subseteq F(\Theta),

with equalities holding. Additionally, since
z_{1}\notin Z(P_{1}),C(z_{1})\subsetneq P_{1}
, it follows that
\Theta\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime}(P_{1}\otimes P_{2})
.

If
N(z_{1})=C(z_{1})
, once again, let
T=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes w_{i}\in C(\Theta),
where
I
is a finite set and
\{v_{i}\}\subseteq C(z_{1})
are linearly independent over
K
. Since there is a degree map
u_{1}
on
P_{1}
,
(P_{1},\ \cdot)
is a domain, and
\{v_{i}z_{1}\}
are linearly independent over
K
. It’s known that

ad_{\Theta}(T)=\sum_{i\in I}(\varphi_{1}(v_{i})\otimes L_{2}(w_{i})+R_{1}(v_{i})\otimes\varphi_{2}(w_{i}))=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}z_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2}(w_{i})=0.

Therefore
\varphi_{2}(w_{i})=0,w_{i}\in C(z_{2}),\forall i\in I
. Thus
T\in C(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2})\subseteq C(\Theta)
, and the equality holds. The same reasoning applies when
N(z_{2})=C(z_{2})
, leading to
C(\Theta)=C(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2})
.
∎

Next we apply the above results to the Weyl algebra.

For a general non-commutative Poisson algebra

and

could be a proper inclusion rather than equality. However, the situation is relatively simple in
A_{1}
, because for any

at least one of the equalities

or

holds
[

]
.

Let
p,q
be the generators of
A_{1}
, and
[q,p]=1
. Define a degree map on
A_{1}
:

\beta_{1}:A_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{-\infty\},\quad 0\mapsto-\infty,\quad 0\neq\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}a_{ij}p^{i}q^{j}\mapsto\max\{i+j\mid a_{ij}\neq 0\}.

Let
P_{1}=A_{1}
,

. In this case, the induced filtration

is finite and discrete, and
gr\ P_{1}=K[X,Y]
, so

satisfies the above theorem and corollary. For example, taking

, by Theorem
5.5
, the type of

in

z
in
A_{1}
.

For the
n
-th Weyl algebra

with

, the Berstein filtration on it is finite and discrete with

a commutative domain.
Therefore, it also satisfies the conditions of the above theorem.

We have the following corollary:

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in A_{1}\setminus K
. If
z_{1}
or
z_{2}
is nilpotent, then
z_{1}\otimes z_{2}
is nilpotent in
A_{2}
, and

and
z_{2}
are strictly nilpotent.

If neither
z_{1}
nor
z_{2}
is nilpotent, then

.

If
z_{1}
or
z_{2}
is not nilpotent, then

, which is commutative. If both

and

are semisimple, then

C(z_{1}\otimes z_{2})=K[z_{1}]\otimes K[z_{2}].

Proof.

It is known that the centralizer of non-constant elements in

commutes
[
A1958
]
. Furthermore, Joseph proved that for
x\in\Omega_{2}(A_{1})\cup\Omega_{2}^{\prime}(A_{1})
,
C(x)=K[x]
[
J1975
]
, and by applying Theorem
5.6
and Corollary
5.7
, the result follows.
∎

Bavula pointed out in
[
B2005
]
that if the type of

is known, and
f(X)\in K[X]
with

, then the possible types of

are as follows:

1. If
z
is not semisimple, then the type of

is the same as that of
z
.

2. If

is semisimple, then

.

By Corollary
5.8
, we can draw the following conclusion:

Corollary 5.9
.

Given
f,g\in K[X]
,
\deg f,\deg g>1
, and

, then
f(z_{1})\otimes g(z_{2})
and
z_{1}\otimes z_{2}
have the same type in

.

Proof.

If
z_{1}
or
z_{2}
is nilpotent, then
f(z_{1})
or
g(z_{2})
is nilpotent. By Corollary
5.8
, it follows that
z_{1}\otimes z_{2}
and

are both nilpotent.

is strictly nilpotent iff
f(z_{1}),g(z_{2})\in\Omega_{1}(A_{1})
, iff

, iff

is strictly nilpotent. Therefore, in this case, the type of

.

If neither

nor

is nilpotent, then

. Hence, by Corollary

,

, and they have the same type.
∎

By analogy with Proposition
3.9
, we have:

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
\varphi:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{1}
and
\psi:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{1}
be endomorphisms such that Im
\,\varphi\subsetneq A_{1}
,Im
\,\psi\subsetneq A_{1}
. Let
\xi=\varphi\otimes\psi:A_{2}\rightarrow A_{2},v\otimes w\mapsto\varphi(v)\otimes\psi(w)
. If
z_{1},z_{2}\in A_{1}\setminus K
and
\Theta=z_{1}\otimes z_{2}
, then

F(\xi(\Theta))=N(\varphi(z_{1}))\otimes N(\psi(z_{2})).

If there is a nilpotent element among
z_{1}
or
z_{2}
, then
\xi(\Theta)\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}(A_{2})
;
If
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are both semisimple elements, then
\xi(\Theta)\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime}(A_{2})
.

Proof.

If there is a nilpotent element among
z_{1}
or
z_{2}
, let’s assume it is
z_{1}
. By Proposition
3.9
, we know that
\varphi(z_{1})\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}(A_{1}),C(\varphi(z_{1}))\subsetneq N(\varphi(z_{1}))\subsetneq A_{1}
. Therefore, we have:

C(\xi(\Theta))\subsetneq N(\varphi(z_{1}))\otimes N(\psi(z_{2}))=F(\xi(\Theta))\subsetneq A_{2},

and thus
\xi(\Theta)\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}(A_{2})
.

If
z_{1}
and

are both semisimple elements, by Proposition
3.9
, we have
\varphi(z_{1}),\psi(z_{2})\in\Omega_{2}^{\prime}(A_{1})
, and

. Therefore,

the equalities hold, and

.
∎

6
The classification of elements of the form
z_{1}\otimes 1+1\otimes z_{2}

Now we will discuss the classification of the element
z_{1}\otimes 1+1\otimes z_{2}
in
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
(with
z_{i}\in P_{i}
for
i=1,2
) in the same setup as in last section:

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be non-commutative Poisson algebras of the same class (Class 1 or Class 2), and let
u_{i}:P_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{-\infty\}
for
i=1,2
be degree maps on
P_{1},P_{2}
respectively. Assume that the filtrations
\{P_{1,t}\}
and
\{P_{2,t}\}
induced by
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
respectively are both finite and discrete.

Theorem 6.1
.

Take
z_{1}\in P_{1},z_{2}\in P_{2},\Gamma=z_{1}\otimes 1+1\otimes z_{2}\in P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
, then

F(\Gamma)=F(z_{1})\otimes F(z_{2}).

We make a remark before the proof. Unlike Theorem
5.6
, Theorem
6.1
does not require
gr\ P_{1}
and
gr\ P_{2}
to be commutative under brackets. All other results in this section are related to Theorem
6.1
, and similarly do not require
gr\ P_{1}
and
gr\ P_{2}
to be commutative under brackets.

Proof.

If
z_{1}\in Z(P_{1}),z_{2}\in Z(P_{2})
, it is evident that
\Gamma\in Z(P_{1}\otimes P_{2}),F(\Gamma)=P_{1}\otimes P_{2}=F(z_{1})\otimes F(z_{2})
.

If
z_{1}\in Z(P_{1}),z_{2}\in P_{2}\setminus Z(P_{2})
, then by Theorem
5.5
,
F(\Gamma)=F(1\otimes z_{2})=P_{1}\otimes F(z_{2})=F(z_{1})\otimes F(z_{2})
.

If
z_{1}\in P_{1}\setminus Z(P_{1}),z_{2}\in Z(P_{2})
, by symmetry, it follows that
F(\Gamma)=F(z_{1})\otimes F(z_{2})
.

If
z_{1}\in P_{1}\setminus Z(P_{1}),z_{2}\in P_{2}\setminus Z(P_{2})
, suppose
0\neq T=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes w_{i}\in P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
, where
I
is a finite set,
\{v_{i}\}
are linearly independent over
K
, and
\{w_{i}\}
are also linearly independent over
K
.

Let
\varphi_{1}=ad_{z_{1}},\varphi_{2}=ad_{z_{2}},V_{i}=K[\varphi_{1}](v_{i}),W_{i}=K[\varphi_{2}](w_{i})
, then we have

\displaystyle ad_{\Gamma}^{n}(T)

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{1}\otimes 1+1\otimes\varphi_{2})^{n}(\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes w_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{t=0}^{n}\binom{n}{t}\varphi_{1}^{t}(v_{i})\otimes\varphi_{2}^{n-t}(w_{i})\in\sum_{i\in I}V_{i}\otimes W_{i}.

(6.1)

If for all
i\in I,

v_{i}\in F(z_{1}),w_{i}\in F(z_{2})
, then
\dim_{K}(V_{i})<+\infty,\dim_{K}(W_{i})<+\infty
, and

\dim_{K}(\sum_{i\in I}V_{i}\otimes W_{i})<+\infty.

Thus
T\in F(\Gamma)
, and
F(z_{1})\otimes F(z_{2})\subseteq F(\Gamma)
.

If
T\notin F(z_{1})\otimes F(z_{2})
, then there exists
i\in I
such that

v_{i}\notin F(z_{1})
or
w_{i}\notin F(z_{2})
.

Assume
w_{j}\notin F(z_{2}),j\in I
. Since
\{P_{2,t}\}
is finite, we have
\sup_{n\geq 0}u_{2}(\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{j}))=+\infty
. Let

M_{k}=\max_{0\leq n\leq k,i\in I}\left\{u_{2}(\varphi^{n}_{2}(w_{i}))\right\}.

In particular
M_{0}=\max_{i\in I}\{u_{2}(w_{i})\}
, and

\sup_{k\geq 0}{M_{k}}=+\infty.

Let
k_{1}
be the smallest positive integer such that
M_{k}>M_{0}
holds, then

u_{2}(\varphi_{2}^{n}(w_{i}))<M_{k_{1}},\quad 0\leq n<k_{1},i\in I.

Thus

ad_{\Gamma}^{k_{1}}(T)=\sum_{i:u_{2}(\varphi_{2}^{k_{1}}(w_{i}))=M_{k_{1}}}v_{i}\otimes\varphi_{2}^{k_{1}}(w_{i})+\sum_{h}\widehat{v}_{h}\otimes\widehat{w}_{h},\quad u_{2}(\widehat{w}_{h})<M_{k_{1}}.

From the linear independence of
\{v_{i}\}
over
K
, it follows that
u_{2}^{*}(ad_{\Gamma}^{k_{1}}(T))=M_{k_{1}}
.

By repeating the above steps, we obtain a sequence of positive integers:
0<k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots<k_{m}<\cdots
, such that the following holds:

u_{2}^{*}(ad_{\Gamma}^{k_{m}}(T))=M_{k_{m}},\quad M_{k_{m}}<M_{k_{m+1}},m\geq 1.

Thus
\dim_{K}\ K[ad_{\Gamma}](T)=+\infty,T\notin F(\Gamma)
. Conversely, if
T\in F(\Gamma)
, then
T\in P_{1}\otimes F(z_{2})
.

By symmetry, we can conclude from
T\in F(\Gamma)
that
T\in F(z_{1})\otimes P_{2}
. Therefore
F(\Gamma)\subseteq F(z_{1})\otimes F(z_{2})
, and equality holds.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Using equation (
6.1
), we have the following obvious inclusions:

D(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2})\subseteq D(\Gamma),

N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2})\subseteq N(\Gamma),

C(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2})\subseteq\bigoplus_{\lambda\in K}D(z_{1},\lambda)\otimes D(z_{2},-\lambda)\subseteq C(\Gamma).

Given that
F(\Gamma)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in K}F(\Gamma,\lambda),F(\Gamma,\lambda)=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\ker(ad_{\Gamma}-\lambda)^{n}
, using equation (
6.1
), we can obtain the following characterization of
F(\Gamma)
and
N(\Gamma)
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Take
\lambda\in K
. We have

F(\Gamma,\lambda)=\bigoplus_{\mu\in K}F(z_{1},\mu)\otimes F(z_{2},\lambda-\mu).

In particular, when
\lambda=0
we have

N(\Gamma)=F(\Gamma,0)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in K}F(z_{1},\lambda)\otimes F(z_{2},-\lambda).

Let
-Ev(z_{2})=\{\lambda\mid-\lambda\in Ev(z_{2})\}
, then
N(\Gamma)=N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2})
iff

Ev(z_{1})\cap(-Ev(z_{2}))=\{0\}.

(6.2)

Proof.

Let
v\in F(z_{1},a),w\in F(z_{2},b)
, where
a,b\in K
. Then there exist positive integers
n_{1},n_{2}
such that

(\varphi_{1}-a)^{n_{1}}(v)=0,\quad(\varphi_{2}-b)^{n_{2}}(w)=0,

where
\varphi_{1}=ad_{z_{1}}
and
\varphi_{2}=ad_{z_{2}}
. It is known that

ad_{\Gamma}-(a+b)=(\varphi_{1}-a)\otimes 1+1\otimes(\varphi_{2}-b).

Hence

\displaystyle[ad_{\Gamma}-(a+b)]^{n_{1}+n_{2}}(v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\binom{n_{1}+n_{2}}{t}(\varphi_{1}-a)^{t}(v)\otimes(\varphi_{2}-b)^{n_{1}+n_{2}-t}(w)

\displaystyle=0.

Thus
v\otimes w\in F(\Gamma,a+b),
and we have

\bigoplus_{\mu\in K}F(z_{1},\mu)\otimes F(z_{2},\lambda-\mu)\subseteq F(\lambda,\Gamma).

Let
T=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes w_{i}\in F(\Gamma,\lambda)
, where
I
is a finite set. For any
i\in I
, by Theorem
6.1
, we can assume that
v_{i}\in F(z_{1},a_{i}),w_{i}\in F(z_{2},b_{i}),a_{i},b_{i}\in K
, and that
\{v_{i}\otimes w_{i}\}
is linearly independent over
K
. Since
v_{i}\otimes w_{i}\in F(\Gamma,a_{i}+b_{i})
, we have
a_{i}+b_{i}=\lambda
, and the equation holds.

Finally,
N(\Gamma)=N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2})
if and only if

\bigoplus_{\lambda\in K^{\times}}F(z_{1},\lambda)\otimes F(z_{2},-\lambda)=0,

if and only if

Ev(z_{1})\cap(-Ev(z_{2}))=\{0\}.

∎

In special cases, the equation (
6.2
) can hold, such as when
Ev(z_{1})=\{0\}
. So we have the following corollary.

Corollary 6.4
.

When
F(z_{1})=N(z_{1})
or
F(z_{2})=N(z_{2})
, one has

N(\Gamma)=N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2}).

Proof.

We may assume
F(z_{1})=N(z_{1})
. We have
F(z_{1})=N(z_{1})=F(z_{1},0)
iff
Ev(z_{1})=\{0\}
, and hence by Lemma
6.3
, we conclude that
N(\Gamma)=N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2}).

∎

We have characterized
N(\Gamma)
in
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
, along with a condition for equality in Remark
6.2
. The subalgebra
D(\Gamma)
is not easy to characterize. We present the following conclusion, which is similar to Corollary
6.4
:

Corollary 6.5
.

When
F(z_{1})=D(z_{1})
or
F(z_{2})=D(z_{2})
, for any
\lambda\in K
one has

D(\Gamma,\lambda)=\bigoplus_{\mu\in K}D(z_{1},\mu)\otimes D(z_{2},\lambda-\mu),

and in this case
D(\Gamma)=D(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
T=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes w_{i}\in D(\Gamma,\lambda)\subseteq F(\Gamma,\lambda),\lambda\in K,I
is a finite set. From Lemma
6.3
, we know that for all
i\in I
, we can assume
v_{i}\in F(z_{1},a_{i}),w_{i}\in F(z_{2},b_{i})
, with
\{v_{i}\}
being linearly independent over
K
, and
a_{i}+b_{i}=\lambda
. We assume
F(z_{1})=D(z_{1})
, and let
\varphi_{1}=ad_{z_{1}}
,
\varphi_{2}=ad_{z_{2}}
, then

\displaystyle(ad_{\Gamma}-\lambda)(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}(\varphi_{1}(v_{i})\otimes w_{i}+v_{i}\otimes\varphi_{2}(w_{i})-\lambda v_{i}\otimes w_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}(a_{i}v_{i}\otimes w_{i}+v_{i}\otimes\varphi_{2}(w_{i})-v_{i}\otimes\lambda w_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes(\varphi_{2}(w_{i})-(\lambda-a_{i})w_{i})

\displaystyle=0.

Since
\{v_{i}\}
are linearly independent over
K
, for all
i\in I,w_{i}\in D(z_{2},\lambda-a_{i})
. Therefore

T\in\bigoplus_{\mu\in K}D(z_{1},\mu)\otimes D(z_{2},\lambda-\mu)\subseteq D(\Gamma,\lambda),

with equality holding.
D(\Gamma)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in K}D(\Gamma,\lambda)=D(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2})
. The same conclusion holds when
F(z_{2})=D(z_{2})
.
∎

Now we can attempt to determine the type of
\Gamma
in
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
. If
z_{1}\in Z(P_{1})
or
z_{2}\in Z(P_{2})
, the type of
\Gamma
is the same as that of
1\otimes z_{2}
, and the type of
1\otimes z_{2}
can be determined using Theorem
5.5
. In the case where neither
z_{1}
nor
z_{2}
is central, we will determine the type of
\Gamma
under various conditions.

Corollary 6.6
.

If
F(z_{1})=N(z_{1})
and
F(z_{2})=N(z_{2})
, then

F(\Gamma)=N(\Gamma)=N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2}),\quad D(\Gamma)=C(\Gamma),\quad Ev(\Gamma)=\{0\}.

Moreover, if
C(z_{1})\subsetneq N(z_{1})
or
C(z_{2})\subsetneq N(z_{2})
, then

D(\Gamma)=C(\Gamma)\subsetneq N(\Gamma)=F(\Gamma).

In this case,
\Gamma\in\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
is a nilpotent element.

Proof.

From Theorem
6.1
, it follows that
F(\Gamma)=N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2})\subseteq N(\Gamma)\subseteq F(\Gamma)
, with equalities holding. From Lemma
2.2
, we know that
D(\Gamma)=C(\Gamma),Ev(\Gamma)=\{0\}
.

If
C(z_{1})\subsetneq N(z_{1})
, we choose
v\in N(z_{1})\setminus C(z_{1})
. Then
v\otimes 1\in N(\Gamma)\setminus C(\Gamma)
, so
C(\Gamma)\subsetneq N(\Gamma)
. Similarly, if
C(z_{2})\subsetneq N(z_{2})
, we can prove that
C(\Gamma)\subsetneq N(\Gamma)
.
∎

Similarly, it can be proved that:

Corollary 6.7
.

If
F(z_{1})=D(z_{1}),F(z_{2})=D(z_{2})
, then

F(\Gamma)=D(\Gamma)=D(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2}),\quad N(\Gamma)=C(\Gamma).

Furthermore, if
C(z_{1})\subsetneq D(z_{1})
or
C(z_{2})\subsetneq D(z_{2})
, then

N(\Gamma)=C(\Gamma)\subsetneq D(\Gamma)=F(\Gamma),

and in this case
\Gamma\in\Omega_{2}\cup\Omega_{2}^{\prime}
is semisimple.

Corollary 6.8
.

If
F(z_{1})=N(z_{1}),F(z_{2})=D(z_{2})
, then

F(\Gamma)=N(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2}),

N(\Gamma)=N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2})=N(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2}),

D(\Gamma)=D(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2})=C(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2}),

C(\Gamma)=C(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2}).

If
C(z_{1})\subsetneq N(z_{1})
, then
C(\Gamma)\subsetneq N(\Gamma)
; if
C(z_{2})\subsetneq D(z_{2})
, then
C(\Gamma)\subsetneq D(\Gamma)
; if
C(z_{1})\subsetneq N(z_{1})
and
C(z_{2})\subsetneq D(z_{2})
, then

N(\Gamma)\supsetneq C(\Gamma)\subsetneq D(\Gamma)\subsetneq F(\Gamma),

and in this case,
\Gamma\in\Omega_{3}\cup\Omega_{3}^{\prime}
is of Jordan type.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.2
, it’s known that,
D(z_{1})=C(z_{1}),N(z_{2})=C(z_{2})
. From Theorem
6.1
, it’s known that

F(\Gamma)=F(z_{1})\otimes F(z_{2})=N(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2}).

From Corollary
6.4
, it’s known that

N(\Gamma)=N(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2})=N(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2})
.
From Corollary
6.5
, it’s known that

D(\Gamma)=D(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2})=C(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2})
.
Therefore,

C(\Gamma)=(N(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2}))\cap(C(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2}))=C(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2}).

If
C(z_{1})\subsetneq N(z_{1})
, then

C(\Gamma)=C(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2})\subsetneq N(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2})=N(\Gamma).

If
C(z_{2})\subsetneq D(z_{2})
, then

C(\Gamma)=C(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2})\subsetneq C(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2})=D(\Gamma).

If
C(z_{1})\subsetneq N(z_{1})
and
C(z_{2})\subsetneq D(z_{2})
, from Lemma
2.4
, it’s known that
\Gamma\in\Omega_{3}\cup\Omega_{3}^{\prime}
.
∎

The following conclusion can be proved in a similar way.

Corollary 6.9
.

If
F(z_{1})=C(z_{1})\subsetneq P_{1}
i.e.
z_{1}\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime}(P_{1})
, then

F(\Gamma)=C(z_{1})\otimes F(z_{2}),\quad N(\Gamma)=C(z_{1})\otimes N(z_{2}),

D(\Gamma)=C(z_{1})\otimes D(z_{2}),\quad C(\Gamma)=C(z_{1})\otimes C(z_{2}).

In this case, the possible types of
\Gamma
are as follows:

If
z_{2}\in\Omega_{0}(P_{1})\cup\Omega_{0}^{\prime}(P_{1})
, then

Corollary 6.10
.

(ad_{\Gamma}-\lambda)^{n+1}(w\otimes 1)=0

D(\Gamma)\subsetneq F(\Gamma)

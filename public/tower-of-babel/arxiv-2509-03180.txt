Deformations of twisted sheaves and formality results
Francesco Meazzini, Claudio Onorati

Abstract
. We show that infinitesimal deformations of twisted sheaves are controlled by the DG Lie algebra of their derived automorphisms. We prove that such DG Lie algebra is formal for polystable twisted sheaves on minimal surfaces of Kodaira dimension 0 0 and for projectively hyper-holomorphic locally free twisted sheaves on hyper-Kähler manifolds.



Introduction

The first purpose of this paper is to give a rigorous proof of the following folklore result.

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.1
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over a field
\mdmathbb{k}
of characteristic
0
, or a projective complex manifold, and let
\alpha\in\Br(X)
a Brauer class. The infinitesimal deformations of a coherent
\alpha
-twisted sheaf
F
on
X
are controlled by the quasi-isomorphism class of DG Lie algebras
\RHom_{(X,\alpha)}(F,F)
.

When the
\alpha
-twisted sheaf
F
is locally free, we can drop the projectivity assumptions (see Theorem
3.6
).

We refer to
[
8
,
13
,
14
]
for a general treatment of twisted sheaves and to
[
16
,
26
]
for a description of first order deformations and moduli spaces of twisted sheaves.

Let us briefly recall what the statement above means. A differential graded (DG) Lie algebra is a DG vector space
(L=\bigoplus_{i\in\mdmathbb{Z}}L^{i},d)
endowed with a graded bracket
[-,-]\colon L\times L\to L
satisfying certain properties.
To a DG Lie algebra
L
one can associate a deformation functor
\Def_{L}\colon\mathsf{Art}{k}\to\mathsf{Set}
. Then one says that a given deformation problem
\mathcal{D}\colon\mathsf{Art}{k}\to\mathsf{Set}
is
controlled
by the DG Lie algebra
L
if there exists a natural isomorphism
\mathcal{D}\cong\Def_{L}
of functors. One advantage of this approach is that the DG Lie algebra
L
provides both the tangent space
\oH^{1}(L)
and a complete obstruction theory with values in
\oH^{2}(L)
for the controlled deformation problem. We refer to the book
[
18
]
for details concerning the approach to deformation theory via DG Lie algebras.

When the Brauer class is trivial, the statement is proved in
[
10
]
and our arguments closely follow theirs.

The idea of the proof of Theorem A is classical; for simplicity, let us suppose that
F
is locally free. Then up to take a sufficiently refined open cover of
X
, the deformation data are reduced to gluing data, which are then encoded in the claimed DG Lie algebra using the formalism of semicosimplicial DG Lie algebras (
[
11
]
).

As a corollary, we extend to twisted sheaves formality results of
[
2
,
6
,
22
]
.

Recall that a DG Lie algebra is
formal
if it is quasi-isomorphic to its cohomology. Geometrically, this implies the quadraticity of the corresponding Kuranishi map. More precisely, in this case we have that the versal deformation can be described via quadratic equations in
\oH^{1}(L)
as the preimage of the obstruction map. We also wish to point out that, for a coherent sheaf on a smooth projective surface, formality and quadraticity are indeed equivalent
[
3
]
.

Theorem B

(Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
5.5
)
.

(1)

Let
S
be a smooth and projective minimal surface of Kodaira dimension
0
and
H
an ample class. If
F
is a
H
-polystable
\alpha
-twisted sheaf, then the DG Lie algebra
\RHom_{(S,\alpha)}(F,F)
is formal.

(2)

Let
X
be a compact hyper-Kähler manifold and
\omega
a Kähler class. If
E
is a
\omega
-projectively hyper-holomorphic locally free
\alpha
-twisted sheaf, then the DG Lie algebra
\RHom_{(X,\alpha)}(E,E)
is formal.

Here a locally free
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on a compact hyper-Kähler manifold is called
\omega
-
projectively hyper-holomorphic
if the untwisted endomorphism sheaf
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
is
\omega
-hyper-holomorphic, i.e. it deforms along the twistor line associated to
\omega
(
[
25
]
). Projectively hyper-holomorphic twisted sheaves are already present in literature (e.g.
[
19
]
) and they have good modular properties. For example, they have been used to prove the D-equivalence conjecture (
[
20
]
) and to construct new projective models of hyper-Kähler manifolds (
[
5
]
).
We point out though that it is still unclear what the right notion of a twisted hyper-holomorphic locally free sheaf is. We speculate on it in Remark
5.2
.

Plan of the paper

We collect in Section
1
the basic properties of twisted sheaves. In particular, we define the deformation functor of a twisted sheaf and prove that it does not depend on the choice of the representative of the Brauer class and the open cover. In Section
2
we collect the algebraic background needed for the proof of Theorem A, which is then proved in Section
3
. We divide the proof in two cases, the locally free case and the general case, each proved in a different section. Beside making the arguments more clear, this has the advantage of make evident where the hypothesis are used. Finally, we prove Theorem B in Section
4
and Section
5
, respectively addressing the case of minimal surfaces of Kodaira dimension
0
and the case of hyper-Kähler manifolds.

1.
Twisted sheaves

1.1.
Basics on twisted sheaves

We consider a field
\mdmathbb{k}
of characteristic
0
, and we work on a finite-dimensional, separated, quasi-compact, quasi-projective, Noetherian
\mdmathbb{k}
-scheme
X
. We will also consider the analytic case, in which case
X
will be a complex manifold. In this section we mostly focus on the algebraic setting, the analytic one being similar.

Recall that the
cohomological Brauer group

\Br^{\prime}(X)
is by definition the torsion part of the cohomology group
\oH^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})
. On the other hand, the
geometric Brauer group

\Br(X)
is the group of equivalence classes of Azumaya
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras. We will always work with the geometric Brauer group
\Br(X)
; recall though that
\Br^{\prime}(X)=\Br(X)
for quasi-projective varieties, see
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Let then
\alpha\in\Br(X)
be a Brauer class, and let us choose a Čech representative
\tilde{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{ijk}\}
for it with respect to an open covering
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.

Definition 1.1
.

An
(\tilde{\alpha},\mathcal{U})
-
twisted sheaf

F
on
X
is the data of a collection of sheaves
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}
-modules and a collection of isomorphisms
\{g_{ij}\colon F_{i}|_{U_{ij}}\to F_{j}|_{U_{ij}}\}_{i,j\in I}
such that:

(1)

g_{ii}=\id
;

(2)

g_{ij}^{-1}=g_{ji}
;

(3)

g_{ij}\circ g_{jk}\circ g_{ki}=\alpha_{ijk}\id
.

A
morphism

f\colon F\to G
of
(\tilde{\alpha},\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaves is given by a collection
\{f_{i}\colon F_{i}\to G_{i}\}
of morphisms that commute with the local isomorphisms.

An
(\tilde{\alpha},\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaf
F
is
coherent
if
F_{i}
is a coherent sheaf for every
i\in I
.

In this way one can define an abelian category
\operatorname{Coh}(X,\tilde{\alpha},\mathcal{U})
of coherent
(\tilde{\alpha},\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaves.

Lemma 1.2

(
[
8
, Lemma 1.2.3, Lemma 1.2.8]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\Br(X)
be a cohomological Brauer class and let
(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\mathcal{U})
and
(\tilde{\alpha}_{2},\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
be two representatives. Then there is an equivalence of categories

\operatorname{Coh}(X,\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\mathcal{U})\cong\operatorname{Coh}(X,\tilde{\alpha}_{2},\mathcal{U}^{\prime}).

In view of Lemma
1.2
, it is customary to simply talk about
\alpha
-twisted sheaves. Nevertheless, we shall keep track of the chosen cover in our notation, whenever such choice is important for some practical reasons. Notice that if
\alpha
is trivial, then a
\alpha
-twisted sheaf is a classical sheaf and
\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)\cong\operatorname{Coh}(X)
.

In a similar way, we will consider the derived category

\operatorname{D}^{b}(X,\alpha):=\operatorname{D}^{b}(\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha))

of
\alpha
-twisted coherent sheaves on
X
.

The following properties can be found at the end of
[
8
, Section 1.2]
. Let
F
be an
\alpha
-twisted sheaf and
G
a
\beta
-twisted sheaf, where
\alpha,\beta\in\Br(X)
are two cohomological Brauer classes. Then:

•

F\otimes G
is a
\alpha\beta
-twisted sheaf;

•

\mathcal{H}om(F,G)
is a
\alpha^{-1}\beta
-twisted sheaf

Also, if
f\colon Y\to X
is a morphism, then:

•

if
F
is a
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
X
, then
f^{*}F
is an
f^{*}\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
Y
;

•

if
G
is a
f^{*}\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
Y
, then
f_{*}G
is a
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
X
;

In particular, it is important to point out that for any
\alpha
-twisted sheaf
F
on
X
, the endomorphism sheaf
\mathcal{E}nd(F)
is always a regular sheaf.

1.2.
Infinitesimal deformations of twisted sheaves

The aim of this section is to introduce the deformation functor associated to a twisted sheaf.

Notation.
We shall adopt the following conventions.

•

For any
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}
, we shall talk about
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
-modules, instead of
\mathcal{O}_{X}\times_{\Spec(\mdmathbb{k})}\Spec(A)
-modules.

•

For any
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}
we consider the morphism
\iota_{A}\colon\Spec(\mdmathbb{k})\to\Spec(A)
corresponding to the projection
A\to\nicefrac{{A}}{{\mathfrak{m}_{A}}}=\mdmathbb{k}
. Given a
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
-module
F
we shall write
F\otimes_{A}\mdmathbb{k}
to denote the pullback of
F
under
\iota_{A}
. Similarly, given a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
E
, with an abuse of notation we shall keep writing
E
to denote its pushforward under
\iota_{A}
.

•

For any
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}

\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
-modules

, we denote either by
\bar{f}\colon E\otimes_{A}\mdmathbb{k}\to G\otimes_{A}\mdmathbb{k}
or by

its reduction modulo the maximal ideal of
A
.

•

For any
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}
, given an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
E
we shall denote by
\pi_{A}\colon E\otimes A\to E
the morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
-modules given by the natural projection. Notice that the reduction
\bar{\pi}_{A}
is the identity on
E
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an open cover of
X
and let

be a
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaf. A
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-
deformation
of

over
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}
is a triple
\left(\{F_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\}\right)
such that:

(1)

F_{i,A}
is a coherent sheaf on
U_{i}\times\Spec(A)
, flat over
\Spec(A)
, for every
i\in I
;

(2)

h_{i,A}\colon F_{i,A}\to F_{i}
is a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}\otimes A
-modules such that the reduction
\bar{h}_{i}\colon F_{i,A}\otimes_{A}\mdmathbb{k}\to F_{i}
is an isomorphism, for every
i\in I
;

(3)

g_{ij,A}\colon F_{i,A}|_{U_{ij}}\to F_{j,A}|_{U_{ij}}
is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{U_{ij}}\otimes A
-modules such that

\textstyle{F_{i,A}|_{U_{ij}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{ij,A}}
\scriptstyle{h_{i,A}}
\textstyle{F_{j,A}|_{U_{ij}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h_{j,A}}
\textstyle{F_{i}|_{U_{ij}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{ij}}
\textstyle{F_{j}|_{U_{ij}}}

is a commutative diagram of
\mathcal{O}_{U_{ij}}\otimes A
-modules, for every
i,j\in I
;

(4)

for every

, the twisted cocycle condition is satisfied:

g_{ij,A}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}\circ g_{jk,A}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}\circ g_{ki,A}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}=(\alpha_{ijk}\otimes 1_{A})\id\;.

Remark 1.4
.

In the setting of Definition

, choosing
\{\alpha_{ijk}=1\}
we recover the usual definition of deformation of the coherent sheaf
F
over

.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an open cover of
X
and let
F=\left(\{F_{i}\},\{g_{ij}\},\{\alpha_{ijk}\}\right)
be an
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaf. Let

; an

between two

-deformations
\left(\{F_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\}\right)

\left(\{F_{i,A}^{\prime}\},\{h_{i,A}^{\prime}\},\{g_{ij,A}^{\prime}\}\right)
of

such that

\textstyle{F_{i,A}|_{U_{ij}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{ij,A}}

\textstyle{F_{j,A}|_{U_{ij}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{j,A}|_{U_{ij}}}
\textstyle{F_{i,A}^{\prime}|_{U_{ij}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{ij,A}^{\prime}}
\textstyle{F_{j,A}^{\prime}|_{U_{ij}}}

\textstyle{F_{i,A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
F
be an
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaf on
X
. Consider the functor

\Def^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}_{F}\colon\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}

defined on
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}
as the set of isomorphism classes of
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-deformations of
F
over
A
.

Remark 1.7
.

The functor
\Def^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}_{F}
is easily described on morphisms. If
f\colon A\to B
is a morphism in
\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}
and
F_{A}=\left(\{F_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\}\right)
is a
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-deformation of
F
over
A
, then

\Def^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}_{F}(f)(F_{A})=\left(\left(\id_{U_{i}}\times f^{\sharp}\right)^{*}F_{i,A},\left(\id_{U_{i}}\times f^{\sharp}\right)^{*}h_{i,A},\left(\id_{U_{ij}}\times f^{\sharp}\right)^{*}g_{ij,A}\right),

where
f^{\sharp}\colon\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)
.

It is now important to point out that this definition does not depend neither on the choice of the representative of the class
\alpha\in\Br(X)
nor on the choice of the open cover
\mathcal{U}
.

Lemma 1.8
.

The definition of
\,\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}
does not depend on the choice of the representative
\tilde{\alpha}
of the class
\alpha
.

Proof.

This follows at once from the fact that the definition of twisted sheaf does not depend on the choice of the representative of the Brauer class
\alpha
, see
[
8
, Lemma 1.2.8]
.
∎

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
\alpha\in\Br(X)
and
F
a coherent
\alpha
-twisted sheaf. If
\,\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
are two open covers of
X
, then

\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}\cong\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U}^{\prime})}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}=\{U_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j\in J}
; define
\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}=\{U_{(i,j)}^{\prime\prime}\}_{(i,j)\in I\times J}
where
U_{(i,j)}=U_{i}\cap U_{j}^{\prime}
. For the choice of a representative
\tilde{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{ijk}\}\in\check{C}^{2}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})
of the class
\alpha\in\Br(X)
, we have a natural transformations

\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}\to\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime})}

provided by restriction.

Notice that for any
i\in I
the open subset
U_{i}
is covered by the
U_{(i,j)}
’s, with
j
varying in
J
. Therefore the map is an isomorphism, being
F
untwisted on each
U_{i}
.
The statement follows from Lemma
1.8
.

Putting all together we get
\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}\cong\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime})}\cong\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U}^{\prime})}
.
∎

1.3.
A global approach to deformations of twisted sheaves

In the previous section we chose to define deformation functors with respect to an open cover. We can refer to this as a
local definition
. On the other hand there is another natural definition that is independent of the choice of an open cover, so that being a
global definition
. Of course the two notions are isomorphic, and the purpose of this section is to state rigorously this identification.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
X
and
B
be two schemes over a field
\mdmathbb{k}
; we denote by
p_{X}\colon X\times B\to X
and
p_{B}\colon X\times B\to B
the two projections.
Let
\alpha\in\Br(X)
be a Brauer class.

(1)

Let
B
be a scheme over
\mdmathbb{k}
.
A family of
\alpha
-twisted sheaves on
X
parametrised by
B
is a
p_{X}^{\ast}\alpha
-twisted sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
X\times B
that is flat over
B
. We denote by
(\mathcal{F},B)
a family of
\alpha
-twisted sheaves.

(2)

Let
F
be an
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
X
and
(B,0)
a pointed space. A
family of deformations
of
F
over
(B,0)
is a pair
(\mathcal{F}_{B},\phi_{B})
, where
(\mathcal{F}_{B},B)
is a family of
\alpha
-twisted sheaves and
\phi_{B}\colon\mathcal{F}_{B}\otimes\mdmathbb{k}(0)\to F
is an isomorphism.

(3)

Two families of deformations
(\mathcal{F}_{1},\phi_{1})
and
(\mathcal{F}_{2},\phi_{2})
of
F
over
(B,0)
are isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism
\Phi\colon\mathcal{F}_{1}\to\mathcal{F}_{2}
such that
\phi_{2}\circ(\Phi\times\mdmathbb{k}(0))=\phi_{1}
.

Definition 1.11
.

For
i=1,2
, let
(B_{i},0_{i})
be a pointed space and
(\mathcal{F}_{i},\phi_{i})
a family of deformations of
F
over
(B_{i},0_{i})
. A morphism
(f,\tilde{f})\colon(B_{1},\mathcal{F}_{1},\phi_{1})\to(B_{2},\mathcal{F}_{2},\phi_{2})
is the datum of:

•

a morphism
f\colon B_{1}\to B_{2}
such that
f(0_{1})=0_{2}
;

•

a morphism
\tilde{f}\colon(\id\times f)^{*}\mathcal{F}_{2}\to\mathcal{F}_{1}
of
p_{X}^{*}\alpha
-twisted sheaves commuting with
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
.

Remark 1.12
.

If
(B_{1},0_{1})
and
(B_{2},0_{2})
are two pointed spaces, we denote by
p_{i}\colon X\times B_{i}\to X
the projection on the first factor, and by
p_{B_{i}}\colon X\times B_{i}\to B_{i}
the projection on the second factor. Then, in the definition above,
(\id\times f)^{*}\mathcal{F}_{2}
is a
(\id\times f)^{*}p_{2}^{*}\alpha=p_{1}^{*}\alpha
-twisted sheaf. This justifies the last requirement that
\tilde{f}
is an isomorphism of
p_{X}^{*}\alpha
-twisted sheaves.

Definition 1.13
.

Let
F
be an
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
X
. Consider the functor

(1.1)

\Def^{\alpha}_{F}\colon\mathsf{Art}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}

defined on any
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{k}
as the set of isomorphism classes of families of
\alpha
-deformations
(\mathcal{F}_{A},\phi_{A})
of
F
over
(\Spec(A),0)
.

Remark 1.14
.

As before, the functor
\Def^{\alpha}_{F}
is easily described on morphisms. If
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}}(A,B)
, then

\Def_{F}^{\alpha}(f)(\mathcal{F}_{A},\phi_{A})=\left(\left(\id_{X}\times f^{\sharp}\right)^{*}\mathcal{F}_{A},\left(\id_{X}\times f^{\sharp}\right)^{*}\phi_{A}\right).

Our aim is now to prove that the functor
\Def^{\alpha}_{F}
is isomophic to the functor
\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}
introduced in Definition
1.6
.

Proposition 1.15
.

Let
F
be an
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
X
. For any open cover
\mathcal{U}
representing
\alpha
, there exists a natural isomorphism

(1.2)

\Def^{\alpha}_{F}\longrightarrow\,\Def^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}_{F}.

Proof.

Let us write
F=\{F_{i},\phi_{i,j}\}
relative to an open cover
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}
where the twist
\alpha
is represented. Let
(\mathcal{F}_{B},\phi_{B})
be a family of deformations of
F
parametrised by
(B,0)
. Recall that
\mathcal{F}_{B}
is a sheaf on the product space
X\times B
flat over
B
. Then the restrictions
\mathcal{F}_{B,i}=\mathcal{F}_{B}|_{U_{i}\times B}
are families of deformations of the sheaves
F_{i}
(in the classical sense). Moreover, the twist
p_{X}^{*}\alpha
can be represented on the open cover
\mathcal{U}_{B}=\{U_{i}\times B\}
and the
p_{X}^{*}\alpha
-twisted sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{B}
is obtained by patching together the sheaves
\mathcal{F}_{B,i}
via the cocycle
\{\alpha_{ijk}\otimes 1_{B}\}
. This defines the natural transformation (
1.2
), which is easily seen to be an isomorphism.
∎

1.4.
Twisted complex vector bundles

For later use, we include here a quick digression on complex manifolds and complex twisted vector bundles. We refer to

]

Let
X
be a complex manifold and
\alpha\in\oH^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})
a twist. If

;

A

\alpha
-twisted complex vector bundle

, and a
\alpha
-twisted holomorphic vector bundle
is a
\alpha
-twisted vector bundle
E
admitting a holomorphic structure such that the transinction morphisms
g_{ij}
are holomorphic.

In particular there is an equivalence of categories between the category of
\alpha
-twisted locally free sheaves on
X
and the category of
\alpha
-twisted holomorphic complex vector bundles on
X
.

The definitions of deformation functors above can be carried out in this setting with minimum changes.

2.
Algebraic background

2.1.
DG Lie algebras and their deformation functors

Recall that a DG Lie algebra is a triple
(L,d,[-,-])
such that
L=\oplus_{n\in\mdmathbb{Z}}L^{n}
is a graded
\mdmathbb{k}
-vector space,
d
is the differential and
[-,-]
is a graded bracket satisfying some compatibility conditions (see
[
18
]
).
For short, we denote by
\operatorname{DGLA}
the category of DG Lie algebras, where morphisms are maps of DG vector spaces commuting with brackets.

We say that two DG Lie algebras
L
and
L^{\prime}
are quasi-isomorphic if there exists a roof
L\leftarrow M\to L^{\prime}
, where both maps are quasi-isomorphisms (i.e. the induced map on cohomology is a degreewise isomorphism).

If
L\in\operatorname{DGLA}
is a DG Lie algebra, there is a deformation functor associated to it defined as

\Def_{L}\colon\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}\to\mathsf{Set},\qquad A\mapsto\frac{\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)}{\sim},

where
\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)=\{\ell\in L^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}\mid d\ell+\frac{1}{2}[\ell,\ell]=0\}
is the set of Maurer–Cartan solutions, and
\sim
is the equivalence relation induced by the gauge action of
L^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
on
\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)
.

A given deformation functor
\mathcal{D}\colon\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}\to\mathsf{Set}
is said to be controlled by a DG Lie algebra
L
if there is an isomorphism of deformation functors
\mathcal{D}\cong\Def_{L}
.

If
L
and
L^{\prime}
are quasi-isomorphic DG Lie algebras, then
\Def_{L}\cong\Def_{L^{\prime}}
(e.g.
[
18
, Corollary 6.6.3]
), so that we can say that a quasi-isomorphism class of DG Lie algebras controls a given deformation problem.

Finally, let us recall two properties of DG Lie algebras that we need later. If
L\in\operatorname{DGLA}
, we say that

(1)

L
is
formal
, if it is quasi-isomorphic to its cohomology;

(2)

L
is
homotopy abelian
, if it is quasi-isomorphic to an abelian DG Lie algebra (i.e. a DG Lie algebra with trivial bracket).

Homotopy abelian DG Lie algebras are formal (but the vice versa is not true in general).

Geometrically, we have the following interpretations. If
L
is homotopy abelian, then the associated deformation functor
\Def_{L}
is unobstructed. If
L
is formal, then the associated deformation functor
\Def_{L}
has only quadratic obstructions, meaning that the natural morphisms

\Def_{L}\left(\nicefrac{{\mdmathbb{k}[t]}}{{(t^{k})}}\right)\to\Def_{L}\left(\nicefrac{{\mdmathbb{k}[t]}}{{(t^{2})}}\right)\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\Def_{L}\left(\nicefrac{{\mdmathbb{k}[t]}}{{(t^{3})}}\right)\to\Def_{L}\left(\nicefrac{{\mdmathbb{k}[t]}}{{(t^{2})}}\right)

have the same image for every integer
k\geq 3
.

2.2.
Semicosimplicial objects

We begin by introducing semicosimplicial objects in a very general setting.

Definition 2.1
.

For every non negative integer we denote by
[n]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
the set of non negative integers less than or equal to
n
, endowed with the usual order. The category
\overrightarrow{\Delta}
is defined as follows:

•

the objects are
\text{Ob}\left(\overrightarrow{\Delta}\right)=\left\{[n]\,\Bigl|\,n\geq 0\right\}
,

•

the morphisms
\Hom_{\,\overrightarrow{\Delta}\,}([n],[m])
are strictly monotone maps.

For future purposes we now investigate concrete examples.

Example 2.2

(Face maps)
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. Then the set of morphisms
\Hom_{\,\overrightarrow{\Delta}\,}([n-1],[n])
consists of
n+1
strictly monotone maps, called
face maps
:

\delta_{k}\colon[n-1]\to[n]\qquad\delta_{k}(a)=\begin{cases}a&\text{ if }a<k\\
a+1&\text{ if }a\geq k\end{cases}\qquad 0\leq k\leq n\;.

With a little abuse of notation we do not emphasize the dependence on
n
of the face map
\delta_{k}
.
For future reference we collect here some useful properties of face maps.

Remark 2.3

(Properties of face maps)
.

It is immediate to verify the following statements.

•

Let
m,n\geq 0
be non negative integers. Every morphism
[n]\to[m]
is the composition of a finite number of face maps, except for the identity.

•

For every
0\leq h\leq k
the
semicosimplicial identities
hold:

(2.1)

\delta_{h}\delta_{k}=\delta_{k+1}\delta_{h}\;.

•

Let
n\geq 0
and
k>0
be two given integers. Every morphism
f\colon[n]\to[n+k]
admits a unique factorisation as composition of
k
face maps

f=\delta_{i_{1}}\circ\ldots\delta_{i_{k}}\qquad\text{ such that }\qquad n+k\geq i_{1}>i_{2}>\ldots>i_{k}\geq 0\;.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category. A
semicosimplicial object in

\mathcal{C}
is a covariant functor
\alpha\colon\overrightarrow{\Delta}\to\mathcal{C}
. A morphism between semicosimplicial objects in
\mathcal{C}
is a natural transformation of functors.

It is often convenient to visualise a semicosimplicial object
\alpha\colon\overrightarrow{\Delta}\to\mathcal{C}
as a diagram

\textstyle{\alpha_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\alpha_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\alpha_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ldots}

where each
\alpha_{n}
is an object in
\mathcal{C}
(namely, the image of
[n]
via the functor
\alpha
), while the morphisms between them are the images of the face maps via the functor
\alpha
.

In the same spirit, one can think of a morphism
f\colon\alpha\to\beta
between semicosimplicial objects in
\mathcal{C}
as a collection of maps

f_{n}\colon\alpha_{n}\to\beta_{n}\qquad\text{ such that }\qquad\delta_{k}f_{n-1}=f_{n}\delta_{k}\text{ for every }n\geq 1\text{ and every }k\leq n\;.

We point out that some additional useful construction can be performed when the category
\mathcal{C}
is the category of abelian groups.

Definition 2.5

(Cochain complex of a semicosimplicial abelian group)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the category of abelian groups and consider a semicosimplicial object
\alpha\colon\overrightarrow{\Delta}\to\mathcal{C}
. The
associated cochain complex

\left(C(\alpha),\delta\right)
is defined as follows:

C(\alpha)^{m}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }m<0\\
\alpha_{k}&\text{ if }m\geq 0\end{cases}\qquad\delta^{n-1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\delta_{k}\colon C(\alpha)^{n-1}=\alpha_{n-1}\to C(\alpha)^{n}=\alpha_{n}\text{ for every }n\geq 1\;.

The cochain complex introduced in Definition
2.5
is indeed a cochain complex, the condition
\delta^{2}=0
being satisfied thanks to the semicosimplicial identities (
2.1
).

The following example will be crucial for us. Essentially the Čech cochain complex of a sheaf of abelian groups with respect to a given open covering can be tought as the cochain complex associated to a semicosimplicial object.

\operatorname{Tot}_{TW}(V^{\Delta})=\left\{(x_{n})\in\prod_{n\geq 0}{}_{n}\otimes V_{n}\,\Bigl|\,(\delta_{k}^{\ast}\otimes\id)x_{n}=(\id\otimes\delta_{k})x_{n-1}\text{ for every }0\leq k\leq n\right\}\;,

where we denoted by

the DG algebra of polynomial differential forms on the affine standard
n
-symplex. Of course, the differential on
\operatorname{Tot}_{TW}(V^{\Delta})
is induced by the one of
\prod_{n\geq 0}{}_{n}\otimes V_{n}
. Moreover, if we endow
V^{\Delta}
with a Lie bracket, then
\operatorname{Tot}_{TW}(V^{\Delta})
inherits such additional structure. In fact, this will be the case in our situation.

The importance of the Thom-Whitney totalization of a semicosimplicial DG Lie algebra relies on the following result.

Theorem 2.13

(
[
10
, Theorem 7.6]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}
be a semicosimplicial DG Lie algebra. Assume that
\oH^{j}(\mathfrak{g}_{i})=0
for every
i\geq 0
and
j<0
. Then there is a natural isomorphism of functors of Artin rings

\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})\cong\Def_{\operatorname{Tot}_{TW}(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})}\;.

Theorem
2.13
is telling us that the functor of Artin rings
\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})
naturally arises as the deformation functor associated to a DG Lie algebra. Notice that it is very deep since it provides a concrete representative of the homotopy class controlling the deformation problem
\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})
, and this is in general a very difficult task.

Once we have the DG Lie algebra controlling a deformation problem, the first goal is to compute its cohomology in order to obtain useful information concerning first order deformations and obstructions. The following result is due to Whitney and it provides a simpler way to compute the cohomology of
\operatorname{Tot}_{TW}(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})
.

Lemma 2.14

(
[
18
, Theorem 7.4.5]
)
.

Let
V^{\Delta}
be a semicosimplicial DG vector space. Then there exists a quasi-isomorphism of DG vector spaces

\operatorname{Tot}_{TW}(V^{\Delta})\longrightarrow C(V^{\Delta})

where
C(V^{\Delta})
is the associated cochain complex as in Definition
2.5
.

Remark 2.15
.

When we deal with a semicosimplicial DG Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}
, its cochain complex
C(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})
is not endowed with a natural DG Lie algebra structure. This is the reason why Lemma
2.14
is only stated for DG vector spaces, and the main motivation to introduce the Thom–Whitney totalization, which is instead a DG Lie algebra. As outlined before, by a deformation theoretic point of view, one is often mainly interested in the cohomology of the DG Lie algebra; therefore Lemma
2.14
is particularly useful since to deal with the complex
C(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})
is easy with respect to the Thom–Whitney totalization.

Corollary 2.16
.

\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})
is a deformation functor with tangent space

T^{1}_{\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})}\cong\oH^{1}(C(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}))

and a complete obstruction theory with values in

T^{2}_{\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})}\cong\oH^{2}(C(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})).

Proof.

Immediate from Lemma
2.14
and Theorem
2.13
.
∎

Example 2.17
.

If
\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}
is the semicosimplicial DG Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}^{\Delta}(\mathcal{U})
associated to a sheaf
\mathcal{L}
of DG Lie algebras on
X
, and to an open cover
\mathcal{U}
(see (

)), then

T^{1}_{\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})}\cong\oH^{1}(X,\mathcal{L})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad T^{2}_{\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})}\cong\oH^{2}(X,\mathcal{L}).

Example 2.18

(
[
21
, Theorem 6.13]
)
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian and separated scheme of finite type, and let
F
be a quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
. Assume that there exists a locally free resolution
E^{\bullet}
of
F
. If
\mathcal{L}^{\Delta}(\mathcal{U})
is the semicosimplicial DG Lie algebra of
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{E}nd^{\ast}(E^{\bullet})
, then
\operatorname{Tot}_{TW}(\mathcal{L}^{\Delta}(\mathcal{U}))
is a representative of the homotopy class
\RHom_{\operatorname{D}^{b}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X))}(F,F)
. Notice that if we deal with a
\alpha
-twisted sheaf

together with a locally free
\alpha
-twisted resolution

is a sheaf of DG Lie algebras and the construction of

3.
DG Lie algebra controlling deformations of twisted sheaves

The goal of this section is to determine the DG Lie algebra controlling the deformations of a twisted sheaf. The main result is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth and projective variety over a field
\mdmathbb{k}
of characteristic
0
, or a projective complex manifold, and let
\alpha\in\Br(X)
a Brauer class. The infinitesimal deformations of a coherent
\alpha
-twisted sheaf
F
on
X
are controlled by the quasi-isomorphism class of DG Lie algebras
\RHom_{(X,\alpha)}(F,F)
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\alpha=1
is trivial, then the result is proved in
[
10
]
.

The differences in treating the algebraic and analytic situation are minimal. We will give full proofs in the algebraic setting and relegate to remarks the due changes in the analytic setting.

We divide the proof in two cases: the locally free case and the general case. The idea behind the two cases is the same, and in fact the general case is reduced to the locally free one by passing through a locally free resolution. We decided to keep the two cases separate both to improve the exposition and to make clear where the hypotheses are needed.

The following corollary can also be found in
[
16
]
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth and projective variety over a field
\mdmathbb{k}
of characteristic
0
, or a projective complex manifold, and let
\alpha\in\Br(X)
a Brauer class. If
F
is a coherent
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
X
then:

(1)

first order deformations of
F
are paramentrised by the space
\Ext^{1}_{(X,\alpha)}(F,F)
;

(2)

obstructions of
F
are contained in the space
\Ext^{2}_{(X,\alpha)}(F,F)
.

3.1.
The locally free case

In this case we can drop the projectivity assumptions. In the complex analytic setting, we will simply work with complex manifolds.

First of all, let us observe that any infinitesimal deformation of a locally free (
\alpha
-twisted) sheaf is locally trivial, so that the deformation data is completely determined by the gluing data.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\,\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an open affine cover of
X
and let
E=\left(\{E_{i}\},\{g_{ij}\}\right)
be a locally free
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaf.

Then every deformation
\left(\{E_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\}\right)
of
E
over
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}
is isomorphic to a deformation of the form
\left(\{E_{i}\otimes A\},\{\pi_{i,A}\},\{\tilde{g}_{ij,A}\}\right)
.

Proof.

Recall that every deformation of the locally free sheaf
E_{i}
on the affine
U_{i}
is trivial (see e.g.
[
18
, add precise reference]
). Now, since
h_{i,A}\colon E_{i,A}\to E_{i}
is such a deformation, for every
i\in I
there exists an isomorphism
\varphi_{i,A}\colon E_{i}\otimes A\to E_{i,A}
such that

\textstyle{E_{i}\otimes A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{i,A}}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{i,A}}
\textstyle{E_{i,A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h_{i}}
\textstyle{E_{i}}

is a commutative diagram of
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
-modules. Now define
\tilde{g}_{ij,A}
as the composition

\tilde{g}_{ij,A}\colon E_{i}|_{U_{ij}}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\varphi_{i,A}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}}E_{i,A}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}\xrightarrow{g_{ij,A}}E_{j,A}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{j,A}^{-1}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}}E_{j}|_{U_{ij}}\otimes A\;.

Notice that

•

\tilde{g}_{ij,A}\circ\tilde{g}_{kj,A}^{-1}\circ\tilde{g}_{kj,A}=\varphi_{i}\circ\left(g_{ij,A}\circ g_{kj,A}^{-1}\circ g_{kj,A}\right)\circ\varphi_{i}^{-1}=(\alpha_{ijk}\otimes 1_{A})\id
for every
i,j,k\in I
,

•

the reduction
\tilde{g}_{ij,A}\otimes_{A}\mdmathbb{k}=g_{ij}
for every
i,j\in I
.

Hence
\left(\{E_{i}\otimes A\},\{\pi_{i,A}\},\{\tilde{g}_{ij,A}\}\right)
is a deformation of
E
over
A
isomorphic to
\left(\{E_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\}\right)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

If
X
is a complex manifold, then the same holds up to take a Stein open cover of
X
instead. In fact, every deformation of a locally free sheaf on a Stein manifold is trivial (e.g.
[
18
, Lemma 4.2.4]
).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\,\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an open affine cover of
X
and let
E=\left(\{E_{i}\},\{g_{ij}\}\right)
be an
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaf. Assume that
E_{i}
is locally free for every
i\in I
. There is an isomorphism of deformation functors

\Def_{E}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})),

where
\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}
is the Čech semicosimplicial Lie algebra associated to the sheaf of DG Lie algebras
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
(see Definition
2.10
).

Proof.

Fix
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}
and consider a deformation
\left(\{E_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\}\right)
of
E
over
A
. By Proposition
3.4
, there exists an isomorphism of deformations

\varphi\colon\left(\{E_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\}\right)\to\left(\{E_{i}\otimes A\},\{\pi_{i,A}\},\{\tilde{g}_{ij,A}\}\right)\;.

where
\pi_{i}\colon E_{i}\otimes A\to E_{i}
is the natural projection and

\tilde{g}_{ij,A}=\varphi_{j,A}^{-1}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}\circ g_{ij,A}\circ\varphi_{i,A}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}\in\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{U_{ij}}}\left(E_{i}|_{U_{ij}},E_{j}|_{U_{ij}}\right)(U_{ij})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}

for some isomorphisms
\varphi_{i}\colon E_{i}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\cong}E_{i,A}
satisfying
\pi_{i}=h_{i,A}\circ\varphi_{i}
for every
i\in I
.

It is straightforward to check that the
\tilde{g}_{ij,A}
’s satisfy the cocycle identity

\tilde{g}_{ij,A}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}\circ\tilde{g}_{jk,A}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}\circ\tilde{g}_{ki,A}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}=\alpha_{ijk}\id_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}\;.

It follows by Example
2.11
that
\{\tilde{g}_{ij,A}\}\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}))(A)
.

Let us now take another choice of isomorphisms

\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\colon E_{i}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\cong}E_{i,A}\qquad\text{ satisfying }\pi_{i}=h_{i,A}\circ\varphi^{\prime}_{i}\qquad\text{ for every }i\in I\;.

We can then define

\sigma_{i}=\varphi_{i}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\colon E_{i}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\cong}E_{i}\otimes A\;,

so that

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{ij,A}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle(\varphi_{j,A}^{\prime})^{-1}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}\circ g_{ij,A}\circ\varphi_{i,A}^{\prime}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}=(\varphi_{j,A}\circ\sigma_{j})^{-1}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}\circ g_{ij,A}\circ(\varphi_{i,A}\circ\sigma_{i})|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma_{j}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{j,A}^{-1})|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}\circ g_{ij,A}\circ(\varphi_{i,A}\circ\sigma_{i})|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}=\sigma_{j}^{-1}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}\circ\tilde{g}_{ij,A}\circ\sigma_{i}|_{U_{ij}\otimes A}\,.

Again by Example
2.11
, the above relation proves that the map

\xi_{A}\colon\Def_{E}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}(A)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}))(A),\qquad\left(\{E_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\}\right)\mapsto\prod_{ij}\tilde{g}_{ij,A}.

is well defined.
Moreover, by
[
18
, Lemma 4.2.2]
, the equivalence relation defined on
\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}))(A)
is the same as the equivalence relation of isomorphisms of the form
\sigma_{i}\in\End_{\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}}(E_{i})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, thus concluding that
\xi_{A}
is injective. On the other hand
\xi_{A}
is clearly surjective, so that it is a bijection.

Finally, since the construction above is functorial in
A
, it defines the required isomorphism of functors

\xi\colon\Def_{E}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}\longrightarrow\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}))\;.

∎

Remark 3.7
.

The argument goes through unchanged for complex manifolds, up to choose a Stein open cover.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
E
be a
\alpha
-twisted locally free sheaf on a smooth variety
X
. Then the infinitesimal deformations of
E
are controlled by the (homotopy class of the) DG Lie algebra
\RHom_{(X,\alpha)}(E,E)
.

Proof.

First of all, by Proposition
1.15
it follows that for any choice of open cover
\mathcal{U}
there is an isomorphism
\Def_{E}^{\alpha}\cong\Def_{E}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}
. We can then choose an affine cover
\mathcal{U}
, so that by Theorem
3.6
we have an isomorphism
\Def_{E}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}\cong\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}))
. Finally, by Theorem
2.13
and Example
2.18
we get that
\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}))
is controlled by the (homotopy class of the) DG Lie algebra
\RHom_{(X,\alpha)}(E,E)
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold and
E
a
\alpha
-twisted locally free sheaf. With an abuse of notation we keep denoting by
E
the associated complex
\alpha
-twisted vector bundle, and we denote by
\bar{\partial}_{E}
the holomorphic structure. This simply means that there exists an open cover
\{U_{i}\}
of
X
and
\bar{\partial}_{E}
is a collection of holomorphic structures
\bar{\partial}_{E|_{U_{i}}}
(see Section

).
There is then a well-defined DG Lie algebra

L:=\left(A^{0,\ast}_{X}(\mathcal{E}nd(E)),[\bar{\partial}_{E},-],[-,-]\right)

where

•

A^{0,\ast}_{X}(\mathcal{E}nd(E))
is the space of
(0,\ast)
-forms with coefficients in the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
;

•

[\bar{\partial}_{E},-]
is the induced holomorphic structure on
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
, thus being a differential;

•

[-,-]
is the Lie bracket induced by composition of sections of
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
.

Then

is a representative of
\RHom_{(X,\alpha)}(E,E)
.

3.2.
The general case

As already remarked, the general case passes through the existence of a finite locally free resolution. Let us then start by recalling the following remark due to Căldăraru.

Lemma 3.10

(
[
8
, Lemma 2.1.4]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth and projective variety,
\,\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
an open cover of
X
and
F=\left(\{F_{i}\},\{g_{ij}\}\right)
an
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaf. Then there exists a finite
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted locally free resolution of
F
, i.e.

\cdots\to E^{-1}\to E^{0}\to F\to 0.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Lemma 2.1.4]
every coherent
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaf is a quotient of a locally free
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted sheaf, from which the claim follows.
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
X
be a smooth and projective variety, let
\,\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an open affine cover of
X
and let
F=\left(\{F_{i}\},\{g_{ij}\}\right)
be an
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted coherent sheaf. Choose a
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted locally free resolution
E^{\bullet}\to F
. Then there is an isomorphism of deformation functors

\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta})),

where
\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}
is the Čech semicosimplicial Lie algebra associated to the sheaf of DG Lie algebras
\mathcal{E}nd^{\ast}(E^{\bullet})
(see Definition
2.10
).

Proof.

Let
E^{\bullet}\to F
be an
(\alpha,\mathcal{U})
-twisted locally free resolution. In particular,
(E_{i}^{\bullet},d_{i})
is a locally free resolution of the sheaf
F_{i}
on each
U_{i}
, for every
i\in I
. Now consider any deformation
F_{A}=(\{F_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\})
of
F
over
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{K}}
. Then
h_{i,A}\colon F_{i,A}\to F_{i}
is a classical infinitesimal deformation over
\Spec(A)
, so that it lifts to a locally free resolution of sheaves
\left(E_{i}^{\bullet}\otimes A,d_{i}+\ell_{i}\right)
over
U_{i}\times\Spec(A)
, for some choice of
\,\ell_{i}\in End^{1}(E_{i}^{\bullet})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, in such a way that the diagram of
\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}\otimes A
-modules

\textstyle{\left(E_{i}^{\bullet}\otimes A,d_{i}+\ell_{i}\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\epsilon_{i,A}}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{i,A}}
\textstyle{F_{i,A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h_{i,A}}
\textstyle{\left(E_{i}^{\bullet},d_{i}\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\epsilon_{i}}
\textstyle{F_{i}}

is commutative, for every
i\in I
. Notice that we applied Proposition
3.4
to state that, for every
k\in\mdmathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
and every
i\in I
, the deformation of
E_{i}^{k}
can be assumed of the form
E_{i}^{k}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\pi_{i,A}}E_{i}^{k}
.

We claim that such local resolutions are glued together on double intersections by isomorphisms of complexes
g_{ij,A}^{E^{\bullet}}
lifting
g_{ij,A}^{F}
and
g_{ij}^{E^{\bullet}}
; this means that we have commutative diagrams (of complexes) of
\mathcal{O}_{U_{ij}}\otimes A
-modules:

(3.1)

passing to cohomology we have isomorphisms (see e.g.

applied to the affine case

). In particular, this proves that the lifting

exists and it is uniquely defined up to cochain homotopy.
More precisely, we actually proved that the left square in (

) is commutative, but we still need to check the other one. Nevertheless, taking the reduction modulo the maximal ideal

, we see that

. Since also

is a lifting of

(and, as we saw above, liftings are unique up to homotopy), we conclude that there exists

such that

Hence, if necessary, we can replace

in order to get the required lifting.

Let us now look at the cocycle condition, that reads as

g_{ij,A}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}\circ g_{jk,A}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}\circ g_{ki,A}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}=\alpha_{ijk}\id_{E_{i}^{\bullet}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}}+\text{homotopy}\;,

since the left hand side lifts
\alpha_{ijk}\id_{F_{i,A}|_{U_{ijk}\otimes A}}
, and liftings are unique up to homotopy.

Let us now recall the definition of the functor
\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathcal{L}^{\Delta}(\mathcal{U}))(A)
in the case
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{E}nd^{\ast}(E^{\bullet})
:
an element

(\{l_{i}\},\{m_{ij}\})\in\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{E}nd^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}}(E_{i}^{\bullet})(U_{i})\oplus\prod_{i,j\in I}\mathcal{H}om^{0}_{\mathcal{O}_{U_{ij}}}\left(E_{i}^{\bullet}|_{U_{ij}},E_{j}^{\bullet}|_{U_{ij}}\right)(U_{ij})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}

lies in

if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

d_{i}l_{i}+\frac{1}{2}[l_{i},l_{i}]=0
, that is equivalent to
(d_{i}+l_{i})^{2}=0
for every

,

(2)

\partial_{1,1}l=e^{m}\ast\partial_{0,1}l
, that is equivalent to

for every

,

(3)

\exists n\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
such that
\partial_{0,2}m\bullet-\partial_{1,2}m\bullet\partial_{2,2}m=dn+[\partial_{2,2}\partial_{0,1}l,n]
, that is equivalent to

e^{m_{bc}|_{U_{abc}}}e^{-m_{ac}|_{U_{abc}}}e^{m_{ab}|_{U_{abc}}}=\alpha_{abc}\id+\text{homotopy}\;.

Now, for every
A\in\mathsf{Art}_{\mdmathbb{k}}
, we can explicitly describe the required morphism

\displaystyle\xi\colon\Def_{F}^{(\alpha,\mathcal{U})}(A)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}))(A)

\displaystyle\left(\{F_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\left\{g^{F}_{ij,A}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(\{\ell_{i}\},\left\{\log\left(g^{E^{\bullet}}_{ij,A}-\id\right)\right\}\right)\;,

and it remains to be shown that

is well defined and bijective.

By construction, the pair
\left(\{\ell_{i}\},\left\{\log\left(g^{E^{\bullet}}_{ij,A}-\id\right)\right\}\right)
satisfies the conditions (1), (2), (3). Moreover, given another deformation
\left(\{F_{i,A}^{\prime}\},\{h_{i,A}^{\prime}\},\{g_{ij,A}^{\prime}\}\right)
of
F
over
A
, isomorphic to
\left(\{F_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\}\right)
via the collection of isomorphisms
\{\varphi_{i,A}\colon F_{i,A}\to F_{i,A}^{\prime}\}
, it is sufficient to consider the local resolutions
\epsilon_{i,A}^{\prime}=\varphi_{i,A}\circ\epsilon_{i,A}
of

to conclude that
\chi\left(\{F_{i,A}\},\{h_{i,A}\},\{g_{ij,A}\}\right)=\chi\left(\{F_{i,A}^{\prime}\},\{h_{i,A}^{\prime}\},\{g_{ij,A}^{\prime}\}\right)
.

We need to show that different choices of
\{\ell_{i}\}
and
\{g^{E^{\bullet}}_{ij,A}\}
give the same element in
\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}))(A)
. To this aim, we begin by recalling the definition of the equivalence relation on
\oH^{1}_{\operatorname{sc}}(\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\Delta}))(A)
in our case:

(l,m)\sim(l^{\prime},m^{\prime})
if and only if there exist elements

a\in\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathcal{E}nd^{0}_{\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}}(E_{i}^{\bullet})(U_{i})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}\quad\text{ and }\quad b\in\prod\limits_{i,j\in I}\mathcal{H}om^{-1}_{\mathcal{O}_{U_{ij}}}\left(E_{i}^{\bullet}|_{U_{ij}},E_{j}^{\bullet}|_{U_{ij}}\right)(U_{ij})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}

such that:

(1)

e^{a}\ast l=l^{\prime}
, that is equivalent to
(d_{i}+l^{\prime}_{i})\circ e^{a_{i}}=e^{a_{i}}\circ(d_{i}+l_{i})
;

(2)

-m\bullet-\partial_{1,1}a\bullet m^{\prime}\bullet\partial_{0,1}a=db+[\partial_{0,1}l,b]
, that is equivalent to require that the isomorphisms of complexes

e^{m_{ij}^{\prime}}\circ e^{a_{i}}\colon E_{i}^{\bullet}|_{U_{ij}}\otimes A\to E_{j}^{\bullet}|_{U_{ij}}\otimes A\quad\text{ and }\quad e^{a_{j}}\circ e^{m_{ij}}\colon E_{i}^{\bullet}|_{U_{ij}}\otimes A\to E_{j}^{\bullet}|_{U_{ij}}\otimes A

are homotopic to each other for every

.

Now notice that in our notation we have
e^{m_{ij}}=g_{ij,A}^{E^{\bullet}}
, so that the morphism
\xi
is well defined and injective. Finally, for the surjectivity of

, it is sufficient to take the

\left(E_{i}^{\bullet}|_{U_{ij}}\otimes A,d_{i}+\ell_{i}\right)
and the corresponding gluing data

.
∎

4.

Let

Theorem 4.1

Let

\alpha

The notion of (semi)stability for twisted sheaves is the analogous for non-twisted ones. We refer to

Remark 4.2

If

Theorem
4.1

Corollary 4.3

Let
S
be either a projective K3 surface or an abelian surface,
\sigma\in\operatorname{Stab}^{\dagger}(S)
a full numerical geometric stability condition and

In the proofs of

and a derived equivalence

such that

To conclude the proof is then enough to apply

holds as soon as
\operatorname{D}^{b}(X,\alpha)
and

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
4.1

We closely follow the proof of

Since
S
is a minimal surface of Kodaira dimension

If

\mathcal{E}nd^{\ast}(E^{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{S}),

and define the DG Lie algebra

\widetilde{L}:=A^{0,\ast}_{S}\left(\mathcal{E}nd^{\ast}(E^{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{S})\right).

Claim

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
4.1
, the DG Lie algebra

is formal.

The claim allows us to conclude the proof of Theorem
4.1
as in the last part of the proof of

and the formality of

is a representative of

, thus concluding the proof.

Proof of the Claim.

Let us collect some facts.
The first one is well-known.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth and projective variety and
H
an ample line bundle. If
F
is a
H
-polystable
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on

, then the group
\Aut_{X}(F)
is linearly reductive. ∎

The second one asserts the existence of special
\alpha
-twisted locally free resolutions of a coherent
\alpha
-twisted sheaf.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth and projective variety and
H
an ample line bundle. If
F
is a
H
-polystable
\alpha
-twisted sheaf, then there exists a
\alpha
-twisted locally free resolution

such that

(1)

E^{\bullet}\to F
is
\Aut_{X}(F)
-equivariant;

(2)

the action of
\Aut_{X}(F)
on

is rational and finitely supported, i.e. for every open subset

the space of sections
\Gamma(U,E^{\bullet})
is degree-wise a rational representation of every subgroup
G\leq\Aut_{X}(F)
that is supported on finitely many irreducible representations.

(3)

If
X
is a minimal surface of Kodaira dimension
0
and
\mathcal{C}_{X}
is the locally free sheaf of commutative

-algebras defined above, then every endomorphism of

Proof.

Let us write
F=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}F_{k}\otimes V_{k}
, where

is stable and

is a vector space. Let us also consider the equivalence relation

F_{i}\sim F_{j}\;\Leftrightarrow F_{i}\cong F_{j}\otimes K_{X}^{m}\;\;\mbox{for some }m.

Then, if

is a set of representatives we can write

where

is a direct sum of line bundles of the form

(for appropriate exponents).

Now, for every

-twisted locally free resolution

and define

Clearly

is a

-twisted locally free resolution of

, and it is easy to see that it satisfies all the sought conditions.
∎

The last fact is about the properties of the DG Lie algebra

Lemma 4.7
.

The DG Lie algebra
\widetilde{L}
above is quasi-cyclic of degree

, i.e. there exists a symmetric bilinear form

(-,-)\colon\widetilde{L}\times\widetilde{L}\longrightarrow\mdmathbb{C}[-2]

that is non-degenerate in cohomology and such that

(4.1)

(dx,y)=(-1)^{\bar{x}+1}\,(x,dy)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad([x,y],z)=(x,[y,z])

for every homogeneous elements

Proof.

First of all, let us consider the trace map

This map is the extension of the more natural trace map

(-,-)\colon\widetilde{L}\times\widetilde{L}\to\mdmathbb{C}[-2],\qquad(x,y)\mapsto\int_{S}\pi\left(\widetilde{\tr}(xy)\right),

5.
Formality for twisted sheaves on hyperkähler manifolds

In
[
22
]
we proved that the DG Lie algebra controlling the deformations of a hyper-holomorphic vector bundle on a hyper-Kähler manifold is formal. In this section we extend that result to twisted vector bundles.

5.1.
A quick guide to hyper-Kähler manifolds and hyper-holomorphic bundles

Let us start by recalling the main definitions and notions. If
X
is a complex manifold, we also write
X=(M,I)
when we want to specify the underlying differential manifold
M
and the complex structure
I
. If
g
is a riemannian metric on
M
, then
\omega_{I}=g(I(-),-)
is the associated
2
-form of type
(1,1)
; recall that
(M,g,I)
is Kähler if
\omega_{I}
is closed. An
hyper-Kähler
manifold is then a
5
-uple
(M,g,I,J,K)
where
I
,
J
and
K
are complex structures satisfying the quaternionic relations and such that the induced
2
-forms
\omega_{I}
,
\omega_{J}
and
\omega_{K}
are Kähler. If we fix the complex structure
I
, then the
2
-form
\sigma_{I}=\omega_{J}+i\omega_{K}
is of type
(2,0)
and non-degenerate, i.e. it is an holomorphic symplectic form on
X=(M,I)
. Vice versa, Yau’s solution to Calabi’s conjecture says that if
X=(M,I)
is a compact Kähler manifold with an holomorphic symplectic form, then for any Kähler class
\omega
there exists a riemannian metric
g
and a hyper-Kähler structure
(M,g,I,J,K)
such that
\omega=\omega_{I}
.

Once a hyper-Kähler structure is fixed (for example, by fixing a Kähler class), there is a natural action of the group
\SU(2)
on the complex tangent bundle
T_{M}
. This action is the one generated by the actions of
I
,
J
and
K
. Extending multiplicatively, we can further define an action of
\SU(2)
on the de Rham algebra
A^{*}_{X}
.

If
E
is a complex vector bundle on a hyper-Kähler manifold
X
, then we can define an action of
\SU(2)
on the algebra
A^{*}_{X}(E)
by letting
\SU(2)
act trivially on the coefficients, i.e. on the sections of
E
.
Suppose that
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle. We say that
E
is
\omega
-
hyper-holomorphic
if there exists an hermitian metric and a Chern connection
\nabla
on
E
such that the curvature
\nabla^{2}\in A^{1,1}(End(E))
is
\SU(2)
-invariant, where the action of
\SU(2)
is associated to the Kähler class
\omega
. If this is the case, the connection
\nabla
is Yang–Mills.

Among the main properties of being hyper-holomorphic, we want to recall here that any such vector bundle deforms holomorphically along the twistor family. More precisely, we can endow the product
M\times S^{2}
with a complex structure
\mathcal{X}=(M\times S^{2},\tilde{I})
such that the projection
p\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
is holomorphic and
p^{-1}(a,b,c)=(M,aI+bJ+cK)
. The other projection
q\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
is never holomorphic and hyper-holomorphic vector bundles
E
on
X
are characterised by the property that
q^{*}E
is an holomorphic vector bundle on
\mathcal{X}
(
[
15
, Lemma 5.1]
).

Finally, let us recall that a holomorphic vector bundle
E
is
\omega
-
projectively
hyper-holomorphic
if there exists a metric connection on
E
such that the induced connection on
End(E)
is
\omega
-hyper-holomorphic.

5.2.
Connections on twisted vector bundles

We refer to Section
1.4
for the definition of twisted complex vector bundle, and to
[
24
]
for a general account on connections on them.

Let
X=(M,I)
be a complex manifold and
\alpha\in\Br^{\prime}(X)
a twist. We further fix here a
B
-field associated to the twist. In particular, if
\mathfrak{U}=\{U_{i}\}
is an open cover where
\alpha
is represented, this means that we fix a collection
\{B_{i}\}
of
2
-forms on each
U_{i}
and a collection
\{\eta_{ij}\}
of
1
-forms on each
U_{ij}
such that
B_{i}-B_{j}=d\eta_{ij}
.
Consider now the morphism

\gamma\colon\oH^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})\longrightarrow\oH^{3}(X,\mdmathbb{Z})

induced by the exponential sequence.
By definition the
3
-forms
\{dB_{i}\}
glue to form a global and closed
3
-form
dB
, and the twist and the
B
-field are associated if
\gamma(\alpha)=[dB]
.
Following
[
24
]
, we further assume that the
B_{i}
are purely imaginary forms of type
(1,1)
and the
\eta_{ij}
are forms of type
(1,0)
.

Let
E
be an
\alpha
-twisted complex vector bundle on
X
. A
connection
on
E
is a collection of connections
\{\nabla_{i}\}
on each
E_{i}
satisfying a twisted compatibility condition. More precisely, if we represent each
\nabla_{i}
with a matrix
\Gamma_{i}
of
1
-forms relative to a local frame, then

\Gamma_{i}=\phi_{ij}^{-1}\Gamma_{j}\phi_{ij}+\phi_{ij}^{-1}d\phi_{ij}+\eta_{ij}\id.

As usual we can decompose
\nabla
in its
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
parts. The assumption that
\omega_{ij}
are of type
(1,0)
then implies that
\nabla^{0,1}
is a semi-connection in the classical sense. In particular, if the twisted vector bundle is holomorphic, i.e. there is a holomorphic structure
\bar{\partial}_{E}=\{\bar{\partial}_{E_{i}}\}
, then
\nabla
is compatible with the holomorphic structure if
\nabla^{0,1}=\bar{\partial}_{E}
.

An hermitian metric on
E
is a collection of hermitian metrics on each
E_{i}
. A connection is then
metric
if it is compatible with the metric (see
[
24
, Section 2.5]
). If the
\alpha
-twisted vector bundle is holomorphic and we fix a hermitian metric on
E
, then there is a unique connection, called
twisted Chern connection
, compatible with both the holomorphic structure and the metric (
[
24
, Lemma 2.25]
).

Finally, if
\nabla
is a connection on a
\alpha
-twisted vector bundle
E
, then we can induce a connection
\tilde{\nabla}
on the (untwisted) vector bundle
End(E)
as follows. Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}
be an open cover representing
\alpha
,
E
and
\nabla
. Then
End(E)
is obtained by gluing together the vector bundles
End(E_{i})
in the usual way. The connections
\nabla_{i}
induce now connections
\tilde{\nabla}_{i}
on each
End(E_{i})
, which can again be glued together to form a connection
\tilde{\nabla}
on
End(E)
(see also
[
24
, Section 2.6]
).

5.3.
Projectively hyper-holomorphic twisted vector bundles

The following is a very natural definition, see for example
[
19
,
20
,
5
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler manifold,
\omega
a Kähler class and
E
a
\alpha
-twisted holomorphic vector bundle. Then
E
is called
\omega
-
projectively hyper-holomorphic
if there exists a hermitian metric, inducing a twisted Chern connection
\nabla
on
E
, such that the induced connection
\tilde{\nabla}
on
End(E)
is
\omega
-hyper-holomorphic.

Remark 5.2

(Speculation on the notion of twisted hyper-holomorphic connections)
.

If
\nabla
is a connection on an
\alpha
-twisted vector bundle and
\{B_{i},\eta_{ij}\}
is a
B
-field associated to
\alpha
, then the curvature
R_{\nabla}
of
\nabla
is obtained (cf.
[
24
, Definition 2.16]
) by gluing together the local forms

R_{i}=\nabla_{i}^{2}-B_{i}\otimes\id\in A^{2}_{X}(End(E_{i})).

If we assume that the
2
-forms
B_{i}
are
\SU(2)
-invariant, then it would be natural to define an
\omega
-
hyper-holomorphic
connection as a connection
\nabla
such that each
\nabla_{i}
is
\omega
-hyper-holomorphic. This definition is then equivalent to ask that the curvature
R_{\nabla}
is
\SU(2)
-invariant. Notice that the B-field being
\SU(2)
-invariant implies that
B_{i}\in A^{1,1}_{U_{i}}
so that
R_{\nabla}\in A^{1,1}_{X}(End(E))
in this case.

It would be very interesting to have a good theory of twisted hyper-holomorphic connections as the one developed in
[
25
]
. For example, using Perego’s theory of twisted Yang–Mills connections (
[
24
]
), it is not difficult to see that such a connection is (twisted) Yang–Mills and, if metric, its
(0,1)
part induces a holomorphic structure on
E
. Moreover, we expect that the following statement holds:

if
E
is a slope stable holomorphic vector bundle with twisted first and second Chern classes that are
\SU(2)
-invariants, then the unique Yang–Mills connection is hyper-holomorphic.

This statement passes through the twisted Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence, which is now proved in
[
24
, Section 5]
. The twisted Chern classes are defined by twisting by the classical Chern classes by the B-field (see
[
24
, Section 3.1]
and
[
13
, Section 1]
).

Our definition of twisted projectively hyper-holomorphic connections by-passes these questions inasmuch as it is a condition on the induced connection on the endomorphism bundle, which is untwisted. We do not need a complete theory of twisted hyper-holomorphic connections, which will be studied elsewhere.

Remark 5.3
.

As in the untwisted case, in Definition
5.1
we assume that the connection is metric (for example, if
End(E)
is slope polystable, we can take the unique Yang–Mills metric). This assumption will be essential in our proof of Theorem
5.5
below, as it is essential in
[
22
, Theorem 4.8]
.

Remark 5.4
.

As we have already remarked, by
[
15
, Lemma 5.1]
the holomorphic vector bundle
End(E)
being hyper-holomorphic means that it deforms along the twistor family. In particular this means that the projectified bundle
\mdmathbb{P}(E)
deforms (as a projective bundle) along the twistor family. Twisted projectively hyper-holomorphic vector bundles are then the natural analog of (untwisted) projectively hyper-holomorphic vector bundles.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
E
be a
\alpha
-twisted projectively hyper-holomorphic vector bundle on a compact hyper-Kähler manifold
X
. Then the DG Lie algebra
A^{0,*}_{X}(End(E))
is formal.

Proof.

Let
\nabla
be a connection on
E
and
\tilde{\nabla}
the induced connection on
End(E)
. If
(M,g,I,J,K)
is the hyper-Kähler structure on
X
, we define the operator

\bar{\partial}_{J}:=J^{-1}\circ\tilde{\nabla}^{1,0}\circ J\colon A^{0,*}_{X}(End(E))\longrightarrow A^{0,*}_{X}(End(E)).

We claim that the triple

(A^{0,*}_{X}(End(E)),\bar{\partial}=\tilde{\nabla}^{0,1},\bar{\partial}_{J})

is a DGMS algebra as defined in
[
22
, Definition 1.5]
. Then the theorem follows at once from
[
22
, Theorem 1.8]
.
We need to prove the following statements:

(1)

the pair
(A^{0,*}_{X}(End(E)),\bar{\partial}_{J})
is an associative DG algebra;

(2)

\bar{\partial}_{J}^{2}=0
and
[\bar{\partial},\bar{\partial}_{J}]=0
;

(3)

the strong
\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}_{J}
-lemma holds.

Let us show item (1). First of all, since
End(E)
(resp.
\tilde{\nabla}
) is obtained by gluing together
End(E_{i})
(resp.
\tilde{\nabla}_{i}
), it follows that
\tilde{\nabla}
acts as a derivation on
A^{0,*}_{X}(End(E))
. In fact, each
\tilde{\nabla}_{i}
acts as a derivation on
A^{0,*}_{X}(End(E_{i}))
by a direct computation (cf.
[
22
, Remark 3.12]
). Therefore the difference
\tilde{\nabla}^{1,0}=\tilde{\nabla}-\tilde{\nabla}^{0,1}
acts as a derivation. Now, by definition
J
acts multiplicatively on
A^{0,*}_{X}(End(E))
so that
\bar{\partial}_{J}
must act as a derivation on
A^{0,*}_{X}(End(E))
, turning it into an associative DG algebra as claimed.

Finally, items (2) and (3) follow from
[
22
, Proposition 3.7, Lemma 3.9]
since
\tilde{\nabla}
is hyper-holomorphic by definition (and it is metric by
[
24
, Lemma 2.33]
).
∎

Monitoring glioblastoma dynamics during chemoradiation on the 1.5 T magnetic resonance imaging-linear accelerator
Authors: Kurl Jamora, Dominique Mathieu, Su Chen Fong, Morikatsu Wada, Richard Khor, Mark Tacey, Benjamin Harris, Sandra Fisher, Brayden Geary, Hui Gan, Lawrence Cher, Eddie Lau, Augusto Gonzalvo, Ricky O’Brien, Daryl Wilding-McBride, Andrew M Scott, Sweet Ping Ng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Serial 1.5 T MR-Linac imaging captured GBM volumetric and spatial changes.•T2/FLAIR volume change (Vrel) diverged by early progression during treatment.•Migration distance (dmigrate) increased during treatment and in early progressors.•Volume change and spatial migration represented distinct tumor dynamics.•On-treatment MRI may support risk-adaptive MR-guided radiotherapy.

Introduction

Glioblastoma (GBM) is the most aggressive malignant primary brain tumor, with an incidence rate of 3.19 per 100,000 persons [1]. The current standard of care consists of maximal safe surgical resection followed by concurrent radiotherapy and temozolomide, and then adjuvant temozolomide. Despite this multimodal approach, first-line treatment has remained largely unchanged, and prognosis remains poor, with a median overall survival of approximately 15 months, [2] underscoring the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies. Outcomes have remained unchanged since the introduction of concurrent chemoradiation strategies two decades ago.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is integral to treatment planning and response assessment due to superior visualisation of the tumor compared to computerized tomography. Although MRI is routinely performed at key time points during treatment (postoperatively during treatment planning, or after chemoradiation completion), few studies have investigated the role of mid-treatment imaging to assess dynamic tumor changes. Mid-treatment imaging has demonstrated significant variations in gross tumor volume and resection cavity size during radiotherapy, which may lead to geographic miss and have been correlated with clinical outcomes [3], [4], [5], [6], [7], [8], [9]. These findings support the potential value of adaptive radiotherapy.

The advent of MR-guided linear accelerators (MR-Linac) has enabled real-time visualization of the treatment region during each fraction of radiotherapy. This has the potential to study MRI volumetry during a course of radiotherapy. Studies utilizing the 0.35 T MR-Linac have demonstrated dynamic volumetric changes during treatment, including progressive lesion growth and reduction in resection cavity size [10], [11], [12]. Moreover, target volumes delineated on MR-Linac images have shown good concordance with those obtained from standalone contrast-enhanced MRIs [12]. In parallel, emerging work has focused on the potential of functional imaging during MR-guided radiotherapy, with longitudinal diffusion imaging on a 1.5 T MR-Linac linking treatment-course changes in apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) to clinical outcomes [13].

While earlier studies have highlighted the potential of on-treatment MRI, most were limited by the use of lower field strength 0.35 T MR-Linac systems or stand-alone MRI scans acquired only at select fractions. These typically correlated volumetric changes with immediate post-treatment imaging which may be confounded by radiation effects or pseudoprogression. In contrast, data integrating on-treatment tumor dynamics with longer‑term clinical outcomes remain scarce. This study addresses that gap by utilizing a higher‑field 1.5 T MR‑Linac, offering superior spatial and contrast resolution, to characterize serial volumetric and spatial changes throughout chemoradiotherapy and assess their association with early disease progression over a minimum follow‑up of six months. By linking daily adaptive imaging with clinical progression outcomes, this work provides new insights that may guide adaptive replanning and early identification of patients at risk for poor outcomes.

Methods

Patients with histologically confirmed GBM suitable for treatment on a 1.5 T MR-Linac were enrolled in a prospective study (HREC/74716/XXX-2021). All patients had undergone either maximal safe resection or diagnostic biopsy and were planned for concurrent chemoradiotherapy using hypofractionation (40.05 Gy in 15 fractions) or conventional fractionation (60 Gy in 30 fractions).

For radiotherapy planning, all patients underwent MR simulation on a 1.5 T MRI scanner (Philips Ingenia) with gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted, T2-weighted, diffusion-weight imaging (DWI), and fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) volumetric sequences. Typical acquisition parameters included voxel sizes of approximately 1.2–1.3 × 1.2–1.3 × 2.4–2.6 mm for T2/FLAIR sequences (reconstructed to ∼ 0.8 mm in-plane) and 2.2 × 2.4 × 4.8 mm for DWI (b-values 0 and 1000 s/mm2). The gross tumor volume (GTV) was defined as the postoperative resection cavity and any residual contrast-enhancing tumor. The clinical target volume (CTV) was generated by applying a 1.5 cm isotropic expansion around the GTV and included areas of T2/FLAIR hyperintensity considered suspicious for infiltrative tumor, while respecting anatomical boundaries. A planning target volume (PTV) margin of 3 mm was added to the CTV. All patients were treated using a single-phase plan to the prescribed dose.

All patients received radiotherapy on a 1.5 T MR-Linac with daily MR imaging. A pre-treatment mock session (F0) was performed for setup verification and patient acclimatization. The MR-Linac imaging protocol included T2-weighted, FLAIR, and DWI acquired using echo planar imaging (EPI) at each treatment fraction. A formal diagnostic gadolinium-enhanced MRI was performed approximately one month after completing radiotherapy (PM1) where available. While the PM1 time point does not capture interfraction tumor dynamics, it serves as an early post-treatment reference scan for response assessment and as a baseline for subsequent surveillance imaging.

Patients were categorized as either early progressors or non-early progressors. Early progression was defined as radiographic or clinical progression occurring within six months of completing chemoradiation, consistent with prior pragmatic definitions (e.g., Perry et al.) [14]. Because this interval overlaps with the period in which pseudoprogression may occur, progression was not defined based on imaging alone, but required assessment according to the Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology (RANO) criteria [15] or a treatment change (e.g., shift in systemic therapy or transition to palliative care) attributable to disease progression or clinical deterioration.

Target definitions and tumor dynamic assessment

For the purpose of this study, only T2-weighted and FLAIR sequences were used to assess interfraction tumor dynamics. Daily images were rigidly registered to the planning scan before contouring and volumetric analysis. A T2/FLAIR region of interest (ROI) was delineated at predefined timepoints (F0, F1, F3, F5, then every two fractions thereafter, as well as F10, F20, and F30), and PM1 when available. The ROI encompassed regions of abnormal signal on T2-weighted and FLAIR imaging, using both sequences reviewed together on co-registered images. The postoperative resection cavity was included in the ROI and contoured consistently across timepoints. No contrast was administered during daily MR-Linac imaging. All contours were independently reviewed and verified by a senior radiation oncologist to ensure consistency and accuracy. Quantitative assessments were conducted using methods adapted from Stewart et al., including (1) absolute volume and percentage change in volume relative to F0 (Vrel), and (2) migration distance (dmigrate), defined as the maximum linear displacement in any direction that the ROI departs from its position at F0.9 Dmigrate was categorized using thresholds of > 5 mm, >10 mm, and > 15 mm. To explore the relationship between dmigrate and Vrel, Vrel was dichotomized as positive (>0%) or negative (≤0%) at each fraction (F5-F30), and joint distributions of Vrel (positive vs negative) and dmigrate (>5 mm vs ≤ 5 mm) were summarized descriptively. In patients with ≥ 6 months follow-up, the frequency of Vrel ≥ 10% and dmigrate ≥ 10 mm (relative to F0) at prespecified timepoints during treatment and at PM1 was summarized, overall and by methylation and early progression status.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were reported as either counts and frequencies for categorical variables. Following inspection using the Shapiro-Wilk test, the two primary outcome variables of percentage change in volume (Vrel) and migration distance (dmigrate) were approximately normally distributed, with evidence of a slight skew at some fractions. Thus, results were reported as mean (standard deviation [SD]) and median (interquartile ranges). Median values were used for descriptive summaries and figures, whereas linear mixed-effects models estimate mean changes and between-group differences; therefore, model results are reported as adjusted means. Linear mixed-effects models were used to assess the interaction between early progression and methylation with fractions, with adjustments for baseline volume and extent of resection. Data for the 15-fraction and 30-fraction group were included in the same model. Data was collected in Microsoft Excel with Stata v18 (StataCorp, College Station, Texas, USA) used to conduct the statistical analysis. P-values were reported for all statistical tests, with a Bonferroni adjusted p-value of less than 0.025 considered to indicate statistical significance for the primary outcome variables of Vrel and dmigrate from the results of the linear mixed effects models.

Results

Thirty-three patients were included (Table 1). Median age was 64 years (70 in the 15-fraction group; 59 in the 30-fraction group), and 19 patients were male. Fifteen patients had O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) promoter methylated tumors; all were IDH-wild type. Extent of resection was gross total resection (GTR) in 9 patients, subtotal resection (STR) in 16, and had biopsy only in 8. Eleven patients received 40.05 Gy in 15 fractions, while 22 received 60 Gy in 30 fractions. Thirty-two patients completed their full course of treatment on the MR-Linac; one was transferred to a conventional linac at fraction 5 due to machine-related issues. Thirty-one underwent a post-treatment diagnostic gadolinium-enhanced MRI approximately at PM1. The median interval from surgery to planning MRI was 18.5 days, and from planning MRI to start of radiotherapy was 13 days. The median time from completion of chemoradiation to radiographic or clinical disease progression was 6.5 months (8 months for the 15-fraction group and 3 months for the 30-fraction group). Among 33 patients, 28 had a minimum follow-up period of 6 months following completion of radiotherapy. The predominant migration range shifted during treatment, from dmigrate 5–10 mm at earlier fractions to dmigrate > 10 mm from F20 onward; rising from 25% at F5 to 47% at F30 and 63% at PM1 (Supplementary Table S13). Across fractions, the most common joint pattern was positive Vrel with dmigrate > 5 mm (52–64% from F10-F30) and this remained predominant at PM1 (65%) (Supplementary Table S14). Negative Vrel with dmigrate > 5 mm occurred in 13–27% during treatment and in 29% at PM1 (Supplementary Table S14). Among patients with ≥ 6 months follow-up, Vrel ≥ 10% at end of treatment occurred in 12/28 (43%) (Supplementary Table S15); 10/12 (83%) were early progressors, including all unmethylated tumors (7/7) and 3/5 (60%) methylated tumors. Dmigrate ≥ 10 mm at end of treatment occurred in 13/28 (46%) (Supplementary Table S16); 9/13 (69%) were early progressors, including all unmethylated tumors (6/6) and 3/7 (43%) methylated tumors. At PM1, Vrel ≥ 10% occurred in 11/27 (41%) (15 fractions: 3/10 [30%]; 30 fractions: 8/17 [47%]), and dmigrate ≥ 10 mm occurred in 16/27 (59%) (15 fractions: 6/10 [60%]; 30 fractions: 10/17 [59%]).

Discussion

This study characterizes longitudinal volumetric change (Vrel) and migration (dmigrate) of MR-Linac-visible T2/FLAIR signal abnormalities during chemoradiotherapy on a 1.5 T MR-Linac, and evaluates their association with early progression. Serial on-treatment MRI demonstrated substantial inter-fraction variability in both Vrel and dmigrate, with divergence by progression status emerging during treatment.

MR-guided radiotherapy enables repeated imaging and has been used to study GBM dynamics. Prior work has described measurable anatomic and spatial changes on treatment imaging, [9], [12] and functional MRI studies (notably DWI/ADC) suggest that persistently adverse diffusion signatures during treatment may be associated with poorer outcomes [13]. Most on-treatment studies have relied on gadolinium-enhanced MRI and focused on enhancing GTV, often reporting stability or reduction of enhancing tumor during treatment [3], [4], [5], [9]. By contrast, T2/FLAIR studies have largely been post-treatment, where FLAIR volume and interval change have been associated with survival outcomes [16], [17], [18], [19] Our findings extend this literature by showing that T2/FLAIR abnormalities can diverge during treatment according to clinical outcome, suggesting that on-treatment imaging may provide earlier signals than routine post-treatment surveillance.

Across the cohort, descriptive summaries showed that most patients exhibited positive Vrel during treatment and at PM1, consistent with overall expansion of T2/FLAIR abnormalities (Supplementary Tables S3 and S4, Fig. 2). In contrast, non-early progressors demonstrated declining Vrel during treatment that persisted to PM1, whereas early progression was associated with higher Vrel, with statistically significant between-group differences emerging from F7 onward and widening later in the course. In adjusted linear mixed-effects models, baseline volume, MGMT promoter methylation status, and extent of resection were not independently associated with Vrel, indicating that early progression status best explained the divergent Vrel trajectories. Early progression classification incorporated radiographic and clinical criteria, and the small number of patients without a documented 6-month assessment largely reflected limited follow-up at the time of analysis.

By extent of resection, descriptive summaries suggested lower (often negative) Vrel after GTR compared with STR/biopsy, particularly at later fractions (Supplementary Tables S3 and S4). However, Vrel did not differ significantly by resection status after adjustment. This contrasts with prior MR-Linac work using 0.35 T imaging and T2-weighted bSSFP sequences, where volume increases were more prominent in intact lesions and decreases were seen in cavity-only targets [12]. While postoperative cavity contraction can continue for weeks to months after surgery, [4], [20] T2/FLAIR-defined abnormalities may also reflect treatment-related edema and evolving non-enhancing disease, which could attenuate or obscure resection-driven cavity effects [21]. A similar pattern was observed by methylation status. Descriptive summaries suggested higher Vrel in unmethylated tumors compared with methylated tumors (Supplementary Tables S3 and S4, Fig. 1), but methylation status was not independently associated with Vrel in adjusted models. This is notable given that pseudoprogression is more commonly reported in methylated tumors early after chemoradiotherapy [22]. These early post-treatment changes are often attributed to transient blood–brain barrier disruption and can be radiologically difficult to distinguish from progression [23]. Our findings suggest that on-treatment T2/FLAIR evolution may not mirror post-treatment pseudoprogression patterns, potentially reflecting different processes during radiotherapy compared with those seen on early post-treatment contrast-enhanced imaging.

Exploratory descriptive analyses using a threshold definition (Vrel ≥ 10%) further contextualize the association between on-treatment T2/FLAIR expansion and early progression (Supplementary Table S15). Across time points, most patients meeting this threshold were early progressors, including 83% at end of treatment. This pattern was particularly pronounced among unmethylated tumors, where all cases meeting the threshold from F20 onward were early progressors, including 7/7 at end of treatment. Among methylated tumors meeting the same threshold, 3/5 (60%) were early progressors at end of treatment. These findings are exploratory and based on small numbers; however, they suggest that a simple on-treatment growth threshold may be linked to early progression in unmethylated tumors, while similar changes in methylated tumors may reflect more heterogeneous processes.

For migration, dmigrate increased progressively during treatment. Although migration also increased among non-early progressors, larger displacements later in treatment were observed in early progressors (from F19 onward). Similar to the Vrel findings, patients with dmigrate ≥ 10 mm were more frequently early progressors, particularly among unmethylated tumors (Supplementary Table S16). Migration was observed even in patients without early progression and, in some cases, despite stable or decreasing T2/FLAIR ROI volume, reinforcing that volume change and migration capture related but distinct aspects of evolution [9] Clinically, migration toward the CTV boundary may affect coverage, especially where margins are tight. However, acting on T2/FLAIR migration is challenging because this can reflect treatment-related effects (e.g., surgery/radiation-related change, edema, and corticosteroid effects) as well as non-enhancing tumor [24] This uncertainty is relevant where frequent contrast-enhanced imaging is not feasible. The UNITED trial provides a useful reference framework: patients were treated on a 1.5 T MR-Linac with relatively small margins, and contrast-enhanced MRI was performed at the first fraction and then every fifth fraction to reassess targets and adapt treatment. Initial findings reported a low marginal failure rate of 4% [25]. Prospective work is needed to define when on-treatment T2/FLAIR migration should trigger replanning and what imaging and clinical criteria best distinguish biologically meaningful extension from transient treatment effect.

Future studies should evaluate whether longitudinal Vrel and dmigrate are associated with survival and whether incorporating these metrics into multivariable prognostic models improves prediction beyond established clinical and molecular factors. Identifying treatment timepoints at which these changes acquire prognostic value may also be of value. Strengths of this study include its prospective design and serial 1.5 T MR-Linac imaging. Limitations include the small, single-institution cohort, which limits generalizability and precluded formal prognostic modeling. Contrast-enhanced imaging was not performed during treatment, limiting assessment of enhancing tumor dynamics. Corticosteroid use was not systematically captured; steroids can alter MRI appearance by reducing edema and modifying T2/FLAIR signal changes that may not reflect true tumor response [26], [27]. Larger prospective trials will be needed to validate these findings and clarify their role in MR-guided adaptive radiotherapy for GBM.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that longitudinal changes in T2/FLAIR ROI volume (Vrel) and migration distance (dmigrate) can be characterized using serial on-treatment imaging on a 1.5 T MR-Linac. Vrel trajectories diverged by early progression status, with early progressors showing greater volumetric expansion during treatment, whereas non-early progressors generally exhibited decline. Migration distance increased over time and was greater in early progressors later in treatment, although migration was also observed despite decreasing volumes. These findings suggest that on-treatment MRI may provide early insight into tumor dynamics during treatment and support risk-adaptive MR-guided radiotherapy strategies for glioblastoma.

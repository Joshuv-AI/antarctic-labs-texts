Preliminaries

In this section, we give some preliminary results regarding packings, including bounds on the number of times elements may occur within a (directed) packing design.

Definition 2.1

Let

In the case that every

Straightforward counting gives the following well-known bound on

Lemma 2.2

If

\mathrm{PD}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)

{r(x)}\leq\left\lfloor\lambda\binom{v-1}{t-1}/\binom{k-1}{t-1}\right\rfloor

\mathrm{PD}_{\lambda}(n;v,k,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}iN_{i}

In a

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{i}{\lambda+1}N_{i}\leq(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}.

If

We note, in addition, that there cannot be too many points of high frequency.

Lemma 2.4

In any

Suppose

3

The main result in this section is Theorem

\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
when the parameters satisfy
kn-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}\leq\lambda v<k(n+1)-(t-1)\binom{n+1}{\lambda+1}

Note that in expressions of the form

We begin by proving a result which generalises the second Johnson bound to packing designs with arbitrary values of

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
v\geq k\geq t
and
\lambda
be positive integers. If there exists a

(t-1)\binom{d}{\lambda+1}\geq v\binom{q}{\lambda+1}+r\binom{q}{\lambda}

where

are the integers such that
dk=qv+r
and
0\leq r<v
.

Proof.

Consider a
\mathrm{PD}_{\lambda}(d;v,k,t)

2.3
we have

(t-1)\binom{d}{\lambda+1}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}N_{i}\binom{i}{\lambda+1}.

(6)

Since
\sum_{i=1}^{d}N_{i}=v
,
\sum_{i=1}^{d}iN_{i}=dk
and the function
\binom{i}{\lambda+1}
is convex in

N_{q+1}=r
,
N_{q}=v-r
, and

for all

. Thus

(t-1)\binom{d}{\lambda+1}\geq r\binom{q+1}{\lambda+1}+(v-r)\binom{q}{\lambda+1}=v\binom{q}{\lambda+1}+r\binom{q}{\lambda}

where the equality follows by substituting
\binom{q+1}{\lambda+1}=\binom{q}{\lambda+1}+\binom{q}{\lambda}
and simplifying.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
v\geq k\geq t\geq 1
and
n\geq 0
, there is a
\mathrm{PD}_{\left\lceil nk/v\right\rceil}(n;v,k,t)
,
(V,\mathcal{B})
, such that
r(x)\in\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{nk}{v}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil\frac{nk}{v}\right\rceil\right\}
for all
x\in V
.

Proof.

If
v=k
, then the multiset consisting of
n
copies of
V
forms the blocks of the required packing design, so we may assume
v>k
. Fix
v
and
k
, and proceed by induction on
n
. The result is trivial for
n=0
and
n=1
. Otherwise, take a
\mathrm{PD}_{\left\lceil nk/v\right\rceil}(n;v,k,t)
,
(V,\mathcal{B})
, such that
r_{{\cal B}}(x)\in\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{nk}{v}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil\frac{nk}{v}\right\rceil\right\}
for all
x\in V
. Let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}\cup\{B\}
, where
B
is a
k
-subset of

such that

if

, and

if

.

Then we have that
|r_{{\cal B}^{\prime}}(x)-r_{{\cal B}^{\prime}}(y)|\leq 1
for all

. Thus, since

, we have
r_{{\cal B}^{\prime}}(x)\in\{\lfloor\frac{(n+1)k}{v}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{(n+1)k}{v}\rceil\}
. Since any point is in at most
\lceil\frac{(n+1)k}{v}\rceil
blocks, the same is certainly true of any set of

is a
\mathrm{PD}_{\left\lceil(n+1)k/v\right\rceil}(n+1;v,k,t)

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
v\geq k\geq t\geq 2
,
\lambda\geq 1
and
n\geq 1
be integers. If
k\geq(t-1)\binom{n-1}{\lambda}

and
\lambda v\geq nk-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}
,
then there is a
\mathrm{PD}_{\lambda}(n;v,k,t)
,
(V,\mathcal{B})
, such that
r(x)\leq\lambda+1
for all
x\in V
.

Proof.

If
n\leq\lambda
, then taking
\mathcal{B}
to be any collection of
n

k
-subsets of
V
gives a packing satisfying the required conditions. So we may assume that
n\geq\lambda+1
. Note that by combining our two hypotheses and simplifying we have
v\geq(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}
with equality only if both hypotheses hold with equality.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all
(\lambda+1)
-subsets of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
U=\{u_{S,j}\mid S\in\mathcal{S},j\in\{1,\ldots,t-1\}\}
be a set of
(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}
elements indexed by
\mathcal{S}\times\{1,\ldots,t-1\}
, and let
W
be a set of
v-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}
elements that is disjoint from
U
. If
k=(t-1)\binom{n-1}{\lambda}
, let
W_{1}=\cdots=W_{n}=\emptyset
. Otherwise, we claim that there is a
\mathrm{PD}_{\lambda}\left(n;v-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1},k-(t-1)\binom{n-1}{\lambda},t\right)
on point set
W
with block set
\mathcal{W}=\{W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{n}\}
such that
|\mathcal{W}_{x}|\leq\lambda
for each
x\in W
. Such a packing design exists by Lemma
3.2
because, using our hypothesis that
\lambda v\geq nk-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}
,

\displaystyle\frac{n\left(k-(t-1)\binom{n-1}{\lambda}\right)}{|W|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{nk-(\lambda+1)(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}}{v-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(nk-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}\right)-\lambda(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}}{v-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda v-\lambda(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}}{v-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda.

So the packing design
(W,\mathcal{W})
does indeed exist.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}
, where, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
B_{i}
is the
k
-set

W_{i}\cup\{u_{S,j}\mid S\in\mathcal{S},i\in S,j\in\{1,\ldots,t-1\}\}.

Note that
|\mathcal{B}_{x}|=\lambda+1
for all
x\in U
and
|\mathcal{B}_{x}|\leq\lambda
for all
x\in W
. We claim
(U\cup W,\mathcal{B})
is the required packing design. To see this, let
T
be a
t
-subset of
U\cup W
. If
T\not\subseteq U
, then there is some
y\in T\cap W
and we have
|\bigcap_{x\in T}\mathcal{B}_{x}|\leq|\mathcal{B}_{y}|\leq\lambda
. Otherwise,
T\subseteq U
, and we must have
\{u_{S,j},u_{S^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\}\subseteq T
for some

and

where

. So,

\left|\bigcap_{x\in T}\mathcal{B}_{x}\right|\leq|\{B_{i}\mid i\in S\cap S^{\prime}\}|=|S\cap S^{\prime}|\leq\lambda.\qed

Example 3.4
.

Let
W_{1},\ldots,W_{4}
, where

, be the blocks of a

to this packing design, we obtain a

whose blocks are

\begin{array}[]{l}\{w_{11},w_{12},u_{12},u_{13},u_{14}\}\\

.

By combining Lemmas
3.1
and

we can prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
v\geq k\geq t\geq 2
,
\lambda\geq 1
and
n\geq 1
be integers. We have that
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)=n
if

nk-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}\leq\lambda v<(n+1)k-(t-1)\binom{n+1}{\lambda+1}.

Proof.

First note that
nk-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}<(n+1)k-(t-1)\binom{n+1}{\lambda+1}
implies

k>(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda}>(t-1)\binom{n-1}{\lambda}.

(7)

Since (
7
) and
nk-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}\leq\lambda v
hold, there exists a
\mathrm{PD}_{\lambda}(n;v,k,t)
by Lemma
3.3
.

We will complete the proof by showing that no
\mathrm{PD}_{\lambda}(n+1;v,k,t)
exists. By Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to show that

(t-1)\binom{n+1}{\lambda+1}<v\binom{q}{\lambda+1}+r\binom{q}{\lambda}

(8)

where
q
and
r
are the integers such that
(n+1)k=qv+r
and
0\leq r<v
. To determine
q
and
r
, note that
\lambda v<(n+1)k-\binom{n+1}{2}
implies
\lambda v<(n+1)k
and hence
q\geq\lambda
. On the other hand, (
7
) yields
(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}<\frac{n-\lambda}{\lambda+1}k
. Substituting this into
nk-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}\leq\lambda v
and simplifying produces
(n+1)k<(\lambda+1)v
. Hence
q<\lambda+1
. Thus
q=\lambda
and
r=k(n+1)-\lambda v
, so (
8
) becomes

(t-1)\binom{n+1}{\lambda+1}<k(n+1)-\lambda v,

which is equivalent to our hypothesis that
\lambda v<k(n+1)-(t-1)\binom{n+1}{\lambda+1}
.
∎

We now determine the values of
k
for which Theorem
3.5
applies and the results it gives for these values. For given values of
v
,
t
and
\lambda
, Theorem
3.5
shows that
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)=n
where
n
is the smallest nonnegative integer such that

\lambda v<(n+1)k-(t-1)\binom{n+1}{\lambda+1}

(9)

if such an integer exists.
Rearranging (
9
) to solve for
k
, Theorem
3.5
gives the value of
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
when

k>\frac{\lambda v}{n+1}+\frac{t-1}{\lambda+1}\binom{n}{\lambda}

(10)

for some positive integer
n
. As
v
becomes large, the value of
n
that minimizes the right hand side of (
10
) approaches
((\lambda+1)!\frac{v}{t-1})^{1/(\lambda+1)}
and the minimum value approaches
c_{t,\lambda}v^{\lambda/(\lambda+1)}
where
c_{t,\lambda}=(\lambda+1)(\frac{t-1}{(\lambda+1)!})^{1/(\lambda+1)}
. So, as
v
becomes large for fixed
t
and
\lambda
, Theorem
3.5
determines the value of
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
if and only if
k\geq f_{t,\lambda}(v)
for some function
f_{t,\lambda}
asymptotic to
c_{t,\lambda}v^{\lambda/(\lambda+1)}
.
Using a similar analysis, it can be shown that Theorem 3.1 can be applied to produce a bound only when
k\geq g_{t,\lambda}
for some function
g_{t,\lambda}
asymptotic to
(t-1)^{1/(\lambda+1)}v^{\lambda/(\lambda+1)},
which differs from the asymptotic value of
f_{t,\lambda}
only by a constant factor. Note that
f_{2,1}=\sqrt{2v}
and
g_{2,1}=\sqrt{v}
.

Let
c>0
and
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
be constants, and consider a regime in which
t
and
\lambda
are fixed and
k\sim cv^{\alpha}
as
v
becomes large. Assume
k\geq f_{t,\lambda}(v)
so that Theorem
3.5
applies. By considering the dominant terms of (
9
) in this regime we can deduce the following.

•

If
\alpha=1
, then
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)\sim\lfloor\frac{\lambda}{c}\rfloor
.

•

If
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}<\alpha<1
, then
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)\sim\frac{\lambda}{c}v^{1-\alpha}
.

•

If
\alpha=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}
, then
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)\sim dv^{1-\alpha}
where
x=d
is the least positive real solution to the equation
\lambda=cx-\frac{t-1}{(\lambda+1)!}x^{\lambda+1}
. (That such a solution exists is guaranteed by our assumption that
k\geq f_{t,\lambda}(v)
.)

Theorem
3.5
captures most, but not all, of the situations in which Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.3
combine to determine a packing number. For fixed positive integers
k
,
t
and
\lambda
with
k\geq t
, and nonnegative integers
n
, Theorem
3.5
requires that

kn-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}<k(n+1)-(t-1)\binom{n+1}{\lambda+1},

which holds if and only if
(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda}<k
.
We define
\ell
to be the least integer such that
(t-1)\binom{\ell}{\lambda}>k
, and note that
Theorem
3.5
will determine values of
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
when
\lambda v<k\ell-(t-1)\binom{\ell}{\lambda+1}
and these values will be less than
\ell
.
Since
k<(t-1)\binom{\ell}{\lambda}
, Lemma
3.3
cannot produce packing designs with more than
\ell
blocks. However, it can produce packing designs with exactly
\ell
blocks when
\lambda v\geq\ell k-(t-1)\binom{\ell}{\lambda+1}
, and in some cases these will achieve the bound of Theorem
3.1
. We encapsulate this in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
k\geq t\geq 2
and
\lambda\geq 1
be integers and let
\ell
be the least integer such that
(t-1)\binom{\ell}{\lambda}>k
. Then
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)=\ell
if

\ell k-(t-1)\binom{\ell}{\lambda+1}\leq\lambda v<\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda+2}(\ell+1)k-\frac{t-1}{\lambda+2}\binom{\ell+1}{\lambda+1}.

(11)

Proof.

Note that

(t-1)\binom{\ell-1}{\lambda}\leq k

(12)

by the definition of
\ell
. In view of (
12
) and the first inequality of (
11
), Lemma
3.3
guarantees the existence of a
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(\ell;v,k,t)
. We will complete the proof by showing that Theorem
3.1
can be applied with
q=\lambda+1
to show that no
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(\ell+1;v,k,t)
exists.

From (
12
) we have
(t-1)\binom{\ell}{\lambda+1}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda+1}\ell k
and substituting this into the first inequality of (
11
) and simplifying yields
\ell k\leq(\lambda+1)v
. Thus
(\ell+1)k<(\lambda+2)v
, since
v>k
follows from (
12
) and the first inequality of (
11
). Now
(t-1)\binom{\ell}{\lambda}>k
implies
(t-1)\binom{\ell+1}{\lambda+1}>\frac{1}{\lambda+1}(\ell+1)k
and substituting this into the second inequality of (
11
) and simplifying yields
(\ell+1)k>(\lambda+1)v
. So, applying Theorem
3.1
with
q=\lambda+1
and
r=(\ell+1)k-(\lambda+1)v
, shows that no
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(\ell+1;v,k,t)
exists provided

(t-1)\binom{\ell+1}{\lambda+1}<v+(\lambda+1)\bigl((\ell+1)k-(\lambda+1)v\bigr).

Routine manipulation shows this is equivalent to the second inequality of (
11
).
∎

A tedious simplification, using first
\binom{\ell+1}{\lambda+1}=\binom{\ell}{\lambda+1}+\binom{\ell}{\lambda}
and then
(\lambda+1)\binom{\ell}{\lambda+1}=(\ell-\lambda)\binom{\ell}{\lambda}
, shows that the leftmost expression in (
11
) is less than the rightmost precisely because
(t-1)\binom{\ell}{\lambda}>k
. So, in particular, we may apply Theorem
3.6
when we have equality in the first inequality in (
11
). Using this, we immediately obtain a generalization of a result in
[
15
]
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
t\geq 2
,
\lambda\geq 1
and
n\geq\lambda+1
be integers such that
(t-1)\tbinom{n}{\lambda+1}\equiv 0\pmod{\lambda}
. We have

\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}\left(\tfrac{t-1}{\lambda}\tbinom{n}{\lambda+1},(t-1)\tbinom{n-1}{\lambda},t\right)=n.

Specializing Corollary
3.7
to the case
(t,\lambda)=(2,1)
yields
[
15
, Theorem 2]
.

We note that Lemma

Similarly, we define a

5
Summary and Discussion

For given
t
and
\lambda
, Theorem
3.5
completely characterizes the packing number
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
when the block size,
k
, is large with respect to
v
. Similarly, for given
\lambda
, Theorem
4.3
characterizes the directed packing number
\mathrm{DPDN}(v,k)
when
k
is large with respect to
v
.
Specifically, Theorem
3.5
shows that
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)=n
when
n
is an integer satisfying

nk-(t-1)\binom{n}{\lambda+1}\leq\lambda v<(n+1)k-(t-1)\binom{n+1}{\lambda+1}.

(13)

This theorem applies when, for given integers
v
,
k
and
\lambda
,

k>\frac{\lambda v}{n+1}+\frac{t-1}{\lambda+1}\binom{n}{\lambda}

for some integer
n
. Theorem
4.3
shows that
\mathrm{DPDN}(v,k)=\mathrm{PDN}_{2}(v,k)
when
v
and
k
satisfy the same constraints for
(t,\lambda)=(2,2)
.

The upper bound for Theorem
3.5
is obtained from Theorem
3.1
, so for any parameter sets to which it applies, it establishes the bound of Theorem
3.1
is tight. In particular, when
\lambda=1
, Theorem
3.1
specializes to the stronger form of the second Johnson bound and hence shows that this bound is tight. Of course, unlike the second Johnson bound, the bound in Theorem
3.1
applies for arbitrary
\lambda
.

For arbitrary
\lambda
, it is natural to ask how close the previously known general upper bounds on
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
are to the actual value of the packing number. We can now answer this question with respect to the range of interest. The Johnson-Schönheim bound
U_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
is known to perform relatively poorly when
k
is large with respect to
v
. For instance, when
(t,\lambda)=(2,1)
, it is noted in
[
34
]
that
U_{1}(v,k,2)
is a good bound when
v\geq k^{2}-k+1
but the second Johnson bound is better otherwise.

Our results bear out this observation. As in our discussion after Theorem
3.1
, we let
c>0
and
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
be constants, and consider a regime in which
\lambda
is fixed and
k\sim cv^{\alpha}
as
v
becomes large. Again, we assume
k\geq f_{t,\lambda}(v)
so that Theorem
3.5
applies.

•

If
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}\leq\alpha<1
, then we have

U_{\lambda}(v,k,t)\sim\frac{\lambda v^{t}}{k^{t}}\sim\frac{\lambda v^{t(1-\alpha)}}{c^{t}}.

However, in these cases we have seen that
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)\sim dv^{1-\alpha}
for a constant
d
depending on
\lambda
and
c
(in fact
d=\frac{\lambda}{c}
if
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}<\alpha<1
). So the order of
U_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
is the
t
th power of the order of the true packing number.

•

If
\alpha=1
, then the asymptotic behaviour of
U_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
is complicated by the floors. However, we certainly have that

U_{\lambda}(v,k,t)\geq\frac{v}{k}\left(\frac{v}{k}\left(\cdots\left(\frac{v}{k}\left(\frac{\lambda v}{k}-1\right)-1\right)\cdots\right)-1\right)-1\\
\sim\frac{\lambda}{c^{t}}-\frac{1}{c^{t-1}}-\frac{1}{c^{t-2}}-\cdots-\frac{1}{c}-1.

(14)

In this case we have seen
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)\sim\lfloor\frac{\lambda}{c}\rfloor
. So
U_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
will be far from a tight bound when
c
is small.

We note that the behaviour above contrasts sharply with the case where
k
is fixed as
v
grows. For instance, it is known that if
k\in\{3,4\}
and
v\geq 20
,
U_{1}(v,k,t)-\mathrm{PDN}(v,k)\leq 1
, see
[
26
]
. In fact, Theorem
3.5
explains two of the small cases in which
\mathrm{PDN}(v,4)<U_{1}(v,4,2)=B(v,4)
,
namely that
\mathrm{PDN}(8,4)=2
and
\mathrm{PDN}(9,4)=3
.

Recall that a
\mathrm{PD}(v,k,t)
is equivalent to a binary code with length
v
, minimum distance
2(k-t+1)
and constant weight
k
. The maximum size of a binary code of length
n
, minimum distance
d
and constant weight
w
has been well studied in the coding theory literature and is often denoted
A(n,d,w)
(see, for example,
[
3
]
). Thus, our results determining
\mathrm{PDN}_{\lambda}(v,k,t)
, when specialized to the case
\lambda=1
, equivalently determine
A(v,2(k-t+1),k)
for large
k
.

As we discussed, a
\mathrm{DPD}(v,k,t)
, yields a
(k-t)
-insertion/deletion code of length
k
over an alphabet of size
v
. If the definition of such a code forbids codewords containing symbols more than once, then the two objects are equivalent. So, in particular, the values of
\mathrm{DPDN}(v,k)
given by Theorem
4.3
are also the maximum sizes of
(k-2)
-insertion/deletion codes of length
k
over an alphabet of size
v
that forbid repeated symbols. In fact, this also determines the maximum sizes of the corresponding codes allowing repeated symbols, because it is not too difficult to see that a maximum-sized code allowing repeats can be obtained by adding the
v
‘constant’ codewords to a maximum-sized code forbidding repeats. Note that
d=k-2
is the largest choice of
d
for which
d
-insertion/deletion codes of length
k
are interesting objects.

Our results show that when
k
is large with respect to
v
,
\mathrm{DPDN}(v,k)=\mathrm{PDN}_{2}(v,k)
. This assertion stems from Lemma
4.1
, which proves that if no element has frequency greater than three, then the blocks of a
\mathrm{PD}_{2}(v,k)
can be directed to produce a
\mathrm{DPD}(v,k)
. Directing algorithms have previously been studied for balanced incomplete block designs. Although the blocks of every BIBD
(v,3,2\lambda)
can be ordered to form a DBIBD
(v,3,\lambda)

[
13
,
17
,
18
]
,
it is known that not every
2
-fold design can be directed to form a directed design. The smallest known example of a BIBD
(v,k,2\lambda)
whose blocks cannot be ordered to form a DBIBD
(v,k,\lambda)
is a BIBD
(28,7,2)
, see
[
9
]
. Additionally, in
[
25
]
, it was shown that none of the (finitely many) symmetric BIBD
(v,k,2)
with
v\geq 37
known at that time are directable.
In particular, there are exactly four non-isomorphic symmetric BIBD
(37,9,2)

[
7
]
, none of which are directable
[
25
]
. Hence
\mathrm{DPDN}(37,9)\neq\mathrm{PDN}_{2}(37,9)
.
Nevertheless, the equality
\mathrm{DPDN}(v,k)=\mathrm{PDN}_{2}(v,k)
holds for the small values of
k
which have been investigated in the literature, namely all cases with
k\in\{3,4,5\}
for which both
\mathrm{DPDN}(v,k)
and
\mathrm{PD}_{2}(v,k)
are known; see
[
9
]
.
This observation leads to the following question:

Question 5.1
.

For which integers
v\geq k\geq 3
is it true that
\mathrm{DPDN}(v,k)=\mathrm{PDN}_{2}(v,k)
?

By the above discussion, the answer is negative for
(v,k)=(37,9)
, but this seems to be a rare occurrence. We thus conjecture that there is only a small number of pairs
(v,k)
which do not satisfy the equation in Question
5.1
.

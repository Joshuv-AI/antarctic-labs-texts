The Bohr’s Phenomenon involving multiple Schwarz functions
Vasudevarao Allu, Raju Biswas and Rajib Mandal

Abstract
. The primary objective of this paper is to establish several sharp versions of Bohr inequalities for bounded analytic functions in the unit disk 𝔻 := { z ∈ ℂ : | z | < 1 } \mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\} involving multiple Schwarz functions. Moreover, we obtain an improved version of the classical Rogosinski inequality for analytic functions in 𝔻 \mathbb{D} .

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
H_{\infty}
denote the class of all bounded analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
with the supremum norm
\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|
.
If
f\in H_{\infty}
has the Taylor series expansion
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

(1.1)

Here, the quantity
1/3
is known as Bohr radius and it cannot be improved. The inequality (
1.1
) is known as the classical Bohr inequality.
In fact, H. Bohr
[
10
]
showed the inequality (
1.1
) only for
r\leq 1/6
. However, subsequently, Weiner, Riesz, and Schur
[
13
]
independently proved that (
1.1
) holds for
r\leq 1/3
.
Note that, if
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|f(z_{0})|=1
for some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
, then
f(z)
reduces to a unimodular constant function (see
[
17
, Strict Maximum
Principle (Complex Version), P. 88]
).

In addition to the notion of the Bohr radius, there is another concept known as the Rogosinski radius
[
30
]
, which is defined as follows:
Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|f(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then,
\left|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_{n}z^{n}\right|<1
for every
N\geq 1
in the disk
|z|<1/2
. The number
1/2
is sharp. This inequality is called the classical Rogosinski inequality for bounded analytic
functions. In 2017, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
21
]
considered the Bohr-Rogosinski sum
R_{N}^{f}(z)
which is defined as follows:

\displaystyle R_{N}^{f}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n},\quad N\in\mathbb{N}.

It is evident that
|S_{N}(z)|=\left|f(z)-\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right|\leq R_{N}^{f}(z)
. Moreover, the Bohr-Rogosinski sum

R_{N}^{f}(z)
is related to the classical Bohr sum in which
N=1
and
f(z)
is replaced by
f(0)
. Let
f
be an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|<1

in
\mathbb{D}
. Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
21
]
have defined the Bohr-Rogosinski radius as the largest number
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that the inequality
R_{N}^{f}(z)\leq 1
holds for
|z|<r_{0}
. In
[
21
]
, Kayumov and Ponnusamy have obtained several results regarding to the Bohr-Rogosinski radius.

Prior to proceeding with the discussion, it is necessary to establish certain notations.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\{f\in H_{\infty}:\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m}=\left\{\omega\in\mathcal{B}:\omega(0)=\omega^{\prime}(0)=\cdots=\omega^{(m-1)}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\omega^{(m)}(0)\not=0\right\}.

Let
f
be an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}_{r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}
for
0<r<1
. Let
S_{r}
denote the planar integral

\displaystyle S_{r}=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z).

Furthermore, if
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then it is well-known that
S_{r}/\pi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}
and if
f
is univalent, then
S_{r}
is the area of the image
f(\mathbb{D}_{r})
.

In 2018, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
22
]
obtained several improved versions of Bohr’s inequality for the bounded analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.
Based on the initiation of Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
21
,
22
]
, a number of authors have studied several versions of Bohr-type inequalities (see
[
2
,
27
,
20
]
).

Recently, Liu
et al.

[
25
]
have obtained the following refined version of Bohr inequalities.

Theorem A
.

[
25
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then,

\displaystyle|f(z)|+|f^{\prime}(z)|r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1

for
|z|=r\leq(\sqrt{17}-3)/4
. The number
(\sqrt{17}-3)/4
is best possible.

Theorem B
.

[
25
]
Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
. Then,

\displaystyle|f(z)|^{2}+|f^{\prime}(z)|r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1

for
|z|=r\leq r_{0}
, where
r_{0}\approx 0.385795
is the unique positive root of the equation
1-2r-r^{2}-r^{3}-r^{4}=0
. The number
r_{0}
is best possible.

Theorem C
.

[
25
]
Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}+\frac{8}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1

for
|z|=r\leq 1/3
. The numbers
1/3
and
8/9
cannot be improved.

In addition to these, a number of authors have studied several other aspects of Bohr’s inequality.

In light of the aforementioned findings, several questions naturally arise with regard to this study.

Question 1.1
.

Is it possible to establish several improved and refined versions of the Bohr inequality for bounded analytic functions that involve more than one Schwarz function?

Question 1.2
.

Can an improved version of the classical Rogosinski inequality be formulated that includes multiple Schwarz functions and substitutes the Taylor series coefficients with the derivatives of the analytic function?

The purpose of this paper is primarily to provide the affirmative answers to Questions
1.1
and
1.2
.

2.
Some lemmas

The following lemmas are essential to prove the main results of this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
24
, Pick’s invariant form of Schwarz’s lemma]
Suppose
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leq\frac{|f(0)|+|z|}{1+|f(0)||z|}\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
12
]
Suppose
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left|f^{(n)}(z)\right|}{n!}\leq\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{(1-|z|)^{n-1}(1-|z|^{2})}\quad{and}\quad|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}\;\;\text{for}\quad n\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad|z|<1.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
25
]
Suppose
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
, then for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\text{sgn}(t)\sum_{n=1}^{t}|a_{n}|^{2}\frac{r^{N}}{1-r}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=t+1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})r^{N}}{1-r}

for
r\in[0,1)
, where
t=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor
and
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the largest integer not exceeding the real number
x
.

3.
Main Results

In the following result, we establish the sharp refined version of the Bohr inequality involving several Schwarz functions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
\omega_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
for
k\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle|f(\omega_{m}(z))|+|\omega_{p}(z)||f^{\prime}(\omega_{m}(z))|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{k}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{|\omega_{k}(z)|}{1-|\omega_{k}(z)|}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}|\omega_{k}(z)|^{2n}\leq 1

for
r\leq R_{m,p,k}\leq r_{m,p}
, where
r_{m,p}\in(0,1)
is the unique root of the equation
r^{2m}+2r^{p}-1=0
and
R_{m,p,k}\in(0,1)
is the smallest positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{2r^{p}}{1+r^{m}}+\frac{2r^{2k}(1+r^{m})}{1-r^{k}}-(1-r^{m})=0.

The number
R_{m,p,k}
cannot be improved.

Proof.

Since
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, in view of Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
, we have
|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}
for
n\geq 1
and

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leq\frac{|a_{0}|+|z|}{1+|a_{0}||z|}\quad\text{and}\quad|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

Let
|a_{0}|=a\in[0,1]
. Since
\omega_{m}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
, in view of the classical Schwarz Lemma, we have
|\omega_{m}(z)|\leq|z|^{m}
.
Let
F_{1}(x)=(a+x)/(1+ax)
and
F_{2}(x)=x/(1-x)
, where
0\leq x\leq x_{0}(\leq 1)
and
a\geq 0
. It is evident that both
F_{1}(x)
and
F_{2}(x)
are monotonically increasing functions of
x
, and it follows that
F_{i}(x)\leq F_{i}(x_{0})

(i=1,2)
.
Thus

\displaystyle|f(\omega_{m}(z))|\leq\frac{a+|\omega_{m}(z)|}{1+a|\omega_{m}(z)|}\leq\frac{a+r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}},\quad|z|=r<1.

(3.1)

In view of Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+a}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{2}}{1-r}.

(3.2)

Using (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{k}(z)|^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{|\omega_{k}(z)|}{1-|\omega_{k}(z)|}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}|\omega_{k}(z)|^{2n}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{nk}+\left(\frac{1}{1+a}+\frac{r^{k}}{1-r^{k}}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2kn}\leq\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}.

Let
G(t)=t+\alpha(1-t^{2})
, where
0\leq t\leq t_{0}(\leq 1)
and
\alpha\geq 0
. Then,
G(t)\leq G(t_{0})
for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1/2
(see
[
8
]
). Therefore

\displaystyle|f(\omega_{m}(z))|+|\omega_{p}(z)||f^{\prime}(\omega_{m}(z))|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{k}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{|\omega_{k}(z)|}{1-|\omega_{k}(z)|}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}|\omega_{k}(z)|^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f(\omega_{m}(z))|+\frac{r^{p}}{1-r^{2m}}\left(1-|f(\omega_{m}(z))|^{2}\right)+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{a+r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}}+\frac{r^{p}}{1-r^{2m}}\left(1-\left(\frac{a+r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}}\right)^{2}\right)+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{(1-a)G_{1}(a,r)}{1+ar^{m}}

for
r\in[0,r_{m,p}]
, where
r_{m,p}\in(0,1)
is the unique root of the equation
r^{2m}+2r^{p}-1=0
and

\displaystyle G_{1}(a,r)=\frac{(1+a)r^{p}}{(1+ar^{m})}+\frac{(1+a)(1+ar^{m})r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}-(1-r^{m}).

Differentiating
G_{1}(a,r)
partially with respect to
a
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}G_{1}(a,r)=\frac{r^{p}(1-r^{m})}{(1+ar^{m})^{2}}+\frac{r^{2k}(1+r^{m}+2ar^{m})}{1-r^{k}}\geq 0.

Therefore,
G_{1}(a,r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
a\in[0,1]
and hence, we have

\displaystyle G_{1}(a,r)\leq G_{1}(1,r)=\frac{2r^{p}}{1+r^{m}}+\frac{2r^{2k}(1+r^{m})}{1-r^{k}}-(1-r^{m})\leq 0,

for
r\leq R_{m,p,k}
, where
R_{m,p,k}\in(0,1)
is the smallest positive root of the equation

\displaystyle G_{2}(r):=\frac{2r^{p}}{1+r^{m}}+\frac{2r^{2k}(1+r^{m})}{1-r^{k}}-(1-r^{m})=0.

(3.4)

We claim that
R_{m,p,k}\leq r_{m,p}
. For
r>r_{m,p}
, we have
r^{2m}+2r^{p}-1>0
and

\displaystyle\frac{2r^{p}}{1+r^{m}}+\frac{2r^{2k}(1+r^{m})}{1-r^{k}}-(1-r^{m})=\frac{r^{2m}+2r^{p}-1}{1+r^{m}}+\frac{2r^{2k}(1+r^{m})}{1-r^{k}}>0.

This shows that
R_{m,p,k}\leq r_{m,p}
.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the following functions

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=\frac{a+z}{1+az}=A_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\omega_{m}(z)=z^{m}\quad\text{for}\quad m\geq 1,

where
A_{0}=a\in[0,1)
,
A_{n}=(1-a^{2})(-a)^{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
.
Thus, for
z=r
, we have

\displaystyle|f_{1}(r^{m})|+|r^{p}||f_{1}^{\prime}(r^{m})|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|A_{n}||r|^{nk}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|A_{0}|}+\frac{|r^{k}|}{1-|r^{k}|}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|A_{n}|^{2}|r|^{2nk}

\displaystyle=\frac{a+r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{p}}{(1+ar^{m})^{2}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})ar^{2k}}{1-ar^{k}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})^{2}r^{2k}(1+ar^{k})}{(1+a)(1-r^{k})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(ar^{k})^{2(n-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{a+r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{p}}{(1+ar^{m})^{2}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})ar^{2k}}{1-ar^{k}}+\frac{1+ar^{k}}{(1+a)(1-r^{k})}\frac{(1-a^{2})^{2}r^{2k}}{1-a^{2}r^{2k}}

\displaystyle=1+(1-a)G_{3}(a,r),

where

\displaystyle G_{3}(a,r)=\frac{(1+a)r^{p}}{(1+ar^{m})^{2}}+\frac{(1+a)ar^{2k}}{1-ar^{k}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{2k}}{(1-ar^{k})(1-r^{k})}-\frac{1-r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}}.

It is evident that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 1^{-}}G_{3}(a,r)=\frac{2r^{p}}{(1+r^{m})^{2}}+\frac{2r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}-\frac{1-r^{m}}{1+r^{m}}>0

for
r>R_{m,p,k}
, where
R_{m,p,k}
is the smallest positive root of the equation (
3.4
) in
[0,r_{m,p}]
. This shows that the radius
R_{m,p,k}
is the best possible. This completes the proof.
∎

In the following result, we establish the sharp refined version of the Bohr inequality in the context of several Schwarz functions.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
\omega_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
for
k\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle|f(\omega_{m}(z))|^{2}+|\omega_{p}(z)||f^{\prime}(\omega_{m}(z))|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{k}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{|\omega_{k}(z)|}{1-|\omega_{k}(z)|}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}|\omega_{k}(z)|^{2n}\leq 1

for
r\leq R_{2,m,p,k}
, where
R_{2,m,p,k}\in(0,r_{2,m,p})
is the smallest positive root of
the equation
r^{2k}/(1-r^{k})-(1-r^{2m}-r^{p})/(1+r^{m})^{2}=0
and
r_{2,m,p}\in(0,1)
is the unique root of the equation
1-r^{2m}-r^{p}=0
. The number
R_{2,m,p,k}
cannot be improved.

Proof.

Using similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain (
3.1
) and (
3.3
). Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|f(\omega_{m}(z))|^{2}+|\omega_{p}(z)||f^{\prime}(\omega_{m}(z))|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{k}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{|\omega_{k}(z)|}{1-|\omega_{k}(z)|}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}|\omega_{k}(z)|^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f(\omega_{m}(z))|^{2}+\frac{r^{p}}{1-r^{2m}}\left(1-|f(\omega_{m}(z))|^{2}\right)+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{r^{p}}{1-r^{2m}}\right)\left(\frac{a+r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}}\right)^{2}+\frac{r^{p}}{1-r^{2m}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1-r^{2m}-r^{p}}{1-r^{2m}}\left(\left(\frac{a+r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}}\right)^{2}-1\right)+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+(1-a^{2})G_{4}(a,r),

where the second inequality holds for such
r\in[0,1]
satisfying
1-r^{p}/(1-r^{2m})\geq 0
,
i.e.,
for
r\in[0,r_{2,m,p}]
, where
r_{2,m,p}\in(0,1)
is the unique root of the equation
1-r^{2m}-r^{p}=0
and

\displaystyle G_{4}(a,r)=-\frac{(1-r^{2m}-r^{p})}{(1+ar^{m})^{2}}+\frac{r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}.

It is evident that the function
G_{4}(a,r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
a
and it follows that

\displaystyle G_{4}(a,r)\leq G_{4}(1,r)=-\frac{(1-r^{2m}-r^{p})}{(1+r^{m})^{2}}+\frac{r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}\leq 0

for
r\leq R_{2,m,p,k}
, where
R_{2,m,p,k}
is the smallest positive root of the equation

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-r^{2m}-r^{p})}{(1+r^{m})^{2}}+\frac{r^{2k}}{1-r^{k}}=0.

(3.5)

It is clear that
R_{2,m,p,k}\leq r_{2,m,p}
.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the following functions

\displaystyle f_{2}(z)=\frac{a+z}{1+az}=A_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\omega_{m}(z)=z^{m}\quad\text{for}\quad m\geq 1,

where
A_{0}=a\in[0,1)
,
A_{n}=(1-a^{2})(-a)^{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
.
Thus, for
z=r
, we have

\displaystyle|f_{2}(r^{m})|^{2}+|r^{p}||f_{2}^{\prime}(r^{m})|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|A_{n}||r|^{nk}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|A_{0}|}+\frac{|r^{k}|}{1-|r^{k}|}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|A_{n}|^{2}|r|^{2nk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a+r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}}\right)^{2}+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{p}}{(1+ar^{m})^{2}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})ar^{2k}}{1-ar^{k}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})^{2}r^{2k}(1+ar^{k})}{(1+a)(1-r^{k})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(ar^{k})^{2(n-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{2}(a,r)=-(1+r^{m})+(1+a)\frac{r^{k}(1-ar^{m})^{N}-a^{N}r^{k(N+1)}}{(1-ar^{m})^{N}(1-ar^{k}-ar^{m})}.

It is evident that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 1^{-}}H_{2}(a,r)=\frac{(1+r^{m})}{(1-r^{m})}\left(\frac{2r^{k}(1-r^{m})^{N}-2r^{k(N+1)}}{(1-r^{m})^{N-1}(1-r^{k}-r^{m})(1+r^{m})}-(1-r^{m})\right)>0

for
r>R_{4,m,k}
, where
R_{4,m,k}
is the smallest positive root of the equation (
3.8
). Thus the radius
R_{4,m,k}
is the best possible. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We discuss some special cases of Theorem
3.5
.

(i)

Setting
k=1
,
m\to\infty
with
\omega_{m}(z)=z^{m}
in Theorem
3.5
, we see that
R_{4,m,k}\to 1/2
. Hence, we have
\sum_{n=0}^{N}|a_{n}|z^{n}\leq 1
for
r\leq 1/2
and
N\geq 1
, which is the classical Rogosinski inequality.

(ii)

Setting
k=1
,
m\to\infty
and
N\to\infty
with
\omega_{m}(z)=z^{m}
in Theorem
3.5
, we see that
R_{4,m,k}\to 1/3
. Hence, we have
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|z^{n}\leq 1
for
r\leq 1/3
, which is the classical Bohr inequality.

In the following result, we establish the sharp improved version of the Bohr inequality involving two Schwarz functions.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
\omega_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
for
k\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle|f(\omega_{m}(z))|^{2}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|\frac{f^{(n)}(\omega_{m}(z))}{n!}\right||\omega_{k}(z)|^{n}\leq 1

for
r\leq R_{5,m,k}
, where
R_{5,m,k}\in(0,1)
is the smallest positive root of the equation
r^{2k}-(1-r^{2m})(1-r^{k}-r^{m})=0
.
The number
R_{5,m,k}
cannot be improved.

Proof.

Using similar argument as in the proof of Theorems
3.1
and
3.4
, and in view of Lemma
2.2
, we obtain (
3.1
) and (
3.6
).
Therefore

\displaystyle|f(\omega_{m}(z))|^{2}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|\frac{f^{(n)}(\omega_{m}(z))}{n!}\right||\omega_{k}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f(\omega_{m}(z))|^{2}+\frac{r^{2k}}{(1-r^{2m})(1-r^{k}-r^{m})}\left(1-|f(\omega_{m}(z))|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\left(1-\frac{r^{2k}}{(1-r^{2m})(1-r^{k}-r^{m})}\right)\left(\left(\frac{a+r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}}\right)^{2}-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{(1-a^{2})\left((1-r^{2m})(1-r^{k}-r^{m})-r^{2k}\right)}{(1-r^{k}-r^{m})(1+ar^{m})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{(1-a^{2})H_{3}(a,r)}{(1+ar^{m})^{2}},

where the second inequality holds for such
r\in[0,1]
satisfying
r^{2k}/((1-r^{2m})(1-r^{k}-r^{m}))\leq 1
,
i.e.,
for
r\in[0,R_{5,m,k}]
, where
R_{5,m,k}\in(0,1)
is the smallest positive root of the equation
r^{2k}-(1-r^{2m})(1-r^{k}-r^{m})=0
and

\displaystyle H_{3}(a,r)=\frac{(1-r^{2m})(1-r^{k}-r^{m})-r^{2k}}{(1-r^{k}-r^{m})}\geq 0\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq R_{5,m,k}.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the following functions

\displaystyle f_{6}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-az}=A_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\omega_{m}(z)=z^{m}\quad\text{for}\quad m\geq 1,

where
A_{0}=a\in[0,1)
,
A_{n}=-(1-a^{2})a^{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
.
Thus, for
z=r<\sqrt[m]{a}
, we have

\displaystyle|f_{6}(\omega_{m}(z))|^{2}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|\frac{f_{6}^{(n)}(\omega_{m}(z))}{n!}\right||\omega_{k}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a-r^{m}}{1-ar^{m}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{a^{n-1}(1-a^{2})r^{kn}}{(1-ar^{m})^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{(1-a^{2})H_{4}(a,r)}{(1-ar^{m})^{2}},

where

\displaystyle H_{4}(a,r)=-(1+r^{2m})+\frac{ar^{2k}}{(1-ar^{k}-ar^{m})}.

It is evident that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 1^{-}}H_{4}(a,r)=-(1+r^{2m})+\frac{r^{2k}}{(1-r^{k}-r^{m})}>0

for
r>R_{5,m,k}
, where
R_{5,m,k}
is the smallest positive root of the equation
r^{2k}-(1-r^{2m})(1-r^{k}-r^{m})=0
. Thus the radius
R_{5,m,k}
is the best possible. This completes the proof.
∎

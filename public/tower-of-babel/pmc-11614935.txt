Underwater endoscopic mucosal resection is superior to conventional endoscopic mucosal resection for medium-sized colorectal sessile polyps: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Qifeng Deng, Zhenhua Wu, Jingsen Li, Guixia Liang, Chenghai Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The comparison of underwater endoscopic mucosal resection (UEMR) and conventional endoscopic mucosal resection (CEMR) in the treatment of medium-sized (10 mm ≤ diameter ≤ 20 mm) colorectal sessile polyps is unknown. This randomized controlled trial (RCT) was designed to compare the efficacy and safety of UEMR and CEMR in the treatment of medium-sized colorectal sessile polyps. 200 patients with medium-sized colorectal sessile polyps were randomly divided into UEMR group and CEMR group equally. The resection rates, patient tolerance and complications of the two groups were evaluated. The R0 resection rate (73.3% vs. 56.3%, P = 0.011) and the En bloc resection rate (91.1% vs. 80.6%, P = 0.032) of the UEMR group were significantly higher than those of the CEMR group; The mean abdominal pain score of the UEMR group was significantly lower than that of the CEMR group [(3.2 ± 1.9) vs. (4.1 ± 2.1), P = 0.006]; There was no significant difference in the intraoperative bleeding rate between the two groups (4.0% vs. 6.1%, P = 0.516). There was no delayed bleeding and perforation in both groups. UEMR was effective in the treatment of medium-sized sessile colorectal polyps with few complications, and patient tolerance was good, which is worthy of clinical promotion.The study was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400082051) on 19/03/2024 and can be accessed at www.chictr.org.cn.

Introduction

Colorectal polyps are a common digestive tract disease, which can be classified into neoplastic polyps and non-neoplastic polyps based on the pathology. Among them, neoplastic polyps are the main cause of colorectal cancer1.

At present, the treatment methods of colorectal polyps mainly include high-frequency electroresection, endoscopic mucosal resection(EMR), and argon plasma coagulation(APC)2. EMR is an effective treatment for sessile polyps of the large intestine. Conventional EMR(CEMR) requires injection of saline or epinephrine saline(1:10000) into the submucosal of the base margin of the polyps before resection. Underwater EMR(UEMR) is a method to resect sessile polyps of the large intestine without submucosal injection3. Several studies have confirmed its effectiveness4–10. Among these studies, there were only two focusing on the comparison of UEMR and CEMR in treating medium-sized sessile polyps in the large intestine9,10, and the results were not consistent. In addition, none of them paid attention to the patient’s comfort. Although anesthesia endoscopy is becoming increasingly popular, in some places anesthesia is not yet widespread because of anesthesia risks and costs. So the comfort of non anesthetic endoscopy is a clinical issue that needs to be considered. We conducted a study to compare the clinical efficacy and safety of the two above-mentioned methods through non anesthetic endoscopy for treating medium-size colorectal sessile polyps.

Materials and methods

Participants

Two hundred consecutive inpatients with medium-sized sessile polyps of the large intestine were recruited at Maoming People’s Hospital in Guangdong province between December 2022 and February 2024 under the following inclusion criteria: aged 18–70 years; Diagnostic colonoscopy, pathological biopsy, and digital chromoendoscopy were performed before inclusion; Benign polyps showed by pathological biopsy; Lesions were sessile, with a diameter between 10 mm and 20 mm. Exclusion criteria included: Patients who were difficult to cooperate with; Malignant polyps; Patients with severe heart and lung disease; Patients who had a hemorrhagic tendency; Long-term use of aspirin and other antiplatelet drugs.

Study design

This study was a prospective, open-label, randomized, controlled trial. Eligible patients were randomly allocated into the UEMR group or the CEMR group in 1:1 according to a computer-generated randomization sequence by using randomly permuted blocks (4 patients per block).

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Maoming People’s Hospital (No.PJ2022MI-K077-01) and adhered to the principles of Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki. All the patients were fully informed and voluntarily signed the written informed consent. The study was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry as ChiCTR2400082051.

Endoscopic procedure

All procedures were performed by 3 experienced endoscopists (Qifeng Deng, Zhenhua Wu and Jingsen Li), with at least five years of CEMRs experience and more than 20 UEMRs experience. Each patient was well prepared before treatment and received endoscopic resection without sedation and anesthesia. The endoscopists were required to attempt en bloc resection at the initial resection. For cases where en bloc resection could not be performed, endoscopic piecemeal mucosal resection was used. For small residual lesions, APC cauterization was used. For bleeding lesions, electrocoagulation or titanium clips were used. For deep wounds, titanium clips were used to prevent perforation. All specimens were retrieved for histopathological examination. The UEMR procedure was as followed: Operated the endoscope (CF-H260AI, Olympus, Japan) to the lesion site with normal saline insufflation by using a mechanical water pump (JSFP-E1, Jinshan, China), then continued to inject normal saline until the polyp was fully immersed. Snared the lesion and surrounding mucosa with a disposal snare (20 mm Rounded-Stiff, Boston Scientific, America) and then resected using electrocautery (Endo-cut)(ERBE VIO200D + APC2, Erbe, Germany) (Fig. 1). Approximately 100-500mL normal saline was injected throughout the entire process and then drained after resection. The CEMR procedure was as followed: Operated the endoscope to the lesion site, injected normal saline with a disposable injection needle (Interject™ Injection Therapy Needle Catheter, Boston Scientific, America) in the submucosa around the lesion site, entrapped the mucosal protrusion with a snare device, and then resected applying the same electrocautery setting as was used for UEMR. Clips were used to close the resected wounds if needed according to the operator’s assessment.

Post-operative management

After operation, patients were instructed to fast for 12 h, slag-free diet for 2 days, semi-liquid diet for 1 week, avoid strenuous activity for 2 week, during which the abdominal and stool conditions were closely observed to assess the presence of adverse events such as perforation and bleeding. If the pathological results indicated infiltration of cancer into the deep submucosal layer or muscularis propria, additional surgical intervention would be performed. Patients were followed up for 1 month after discharge.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome was the difference in R0 resection rates between the two groups. The secondary outcomes comprised the en bloc resection rate, R1 and Rx resection rates, visual analogue scale (VAS) of abdominal pain, and adverse events. En bloc resection was defined as a complete resection of a lesion and acquisition of a single specimen. R0 resection was defined as a complete resection of a lesion with laterally and basally negative margins. R1 resection was defined as residual tumor under the microscope but no macroscopic residual. Rx resection was defined as an indeterminate margin. VAS of abdominal pain was used to assess patient tolerance11 and was divided into five levels: 0 point meant painless, 1–3 points meant mild pain, 4–6 points meant moderate pain, 7–9 meant points severe pain, and 10 points meant unbearable pain. Adverse events were comprised of perforation and bleeding during or after operation within two weeks. Both perforation and bleeding were divided into intraoperative and delayed types. Intraoperative perforation was defined as the visible muscle layer rupture on the wound base after polyp resection. Intraoperative bleeding was defined as continuous hemorrhage needed endoscopic treatment during procedure. Delayed perforation and bleeding were defined as events within 14 days after resection.

Sample size

According to the previous study10, the R0 resection rate of CEMR for intermediate-size (10–20 mm) colorectal polyps was about 50%, and UEMR might increase it by 20–70%. The sample size of 91 for each group was required to achieve 80% power to detect a difference between the group proportions of 20%. The two-sided Z-Test was used with a significance level of 0.05. So we planned to recruit 200 patients, taking into consideration a dropout rate of approximately 10%.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative data, such as age and score of VAS, were presented as mean ± standard deviation (M ± SD) and were compared using the independent sample t test or nonparametric Mann-Whitney U test as appropriate. Qualitative data, such as resection rates and incidence rates of adverse events, were presented as n(%) and were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. The data were analyzed using the software of IBM SPSS Statistics 23.0. Statistical significance was considered if p-values were less than 0.05.

Results

Trial profile and characteristics of patients

Two hundred and sixty one consecutive patients were assessed for eligibility. Two hundred patients were eligible and assigned to the UEMR group (n = 100) or the CEMR group (n = 100) randomly. One patient in the UEMR group underwent protocol deviation. Another patient in the CEMR group withdrew informed consent without receiving operation. Therefore, 99 patients in each group completed the trial and were analyzed both for intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis and per-protocol analysis (Fig. 2). The number of medium-sized sessile polyps per patient ranged from 1 to 2. A total of 204 polyps were found in the 198 patients, with 123 polyps lying in the left half colon, 50 in the transverse colon and 31 in the right half colon. Resection rates

The R0 resection rate was significantly higher in the UEMR group than that in the CEMR group (73.3% vs. 56.3%, p = 0.011). The en bloc resection rate of UEMR group was 91.1%, which was significantly higher than 80.6% in the CEMR group (p = 0.032). There was no significant difference in the R1 resection rates or Rx resection rates between the two groups (Table 2). In subgroup analysis, there was significant difference in the R0 resection rates between the two groups in the 10–15 mm subgroup (UEMR 77.9% vs. CEMR 58.3%, p = 0.013), but not in the 16–20 mm subgroup (63.6% vs. 51.6%, p = 0.330) according to the lesion size stratification (Table 3). According to the lesion location stratification, the R0 resection rate of the UEMR group was significantly higher than that in the CEMR group in the left half colon subgroup (76.6% vs. 55.9%, p = 0.015), but not in the transverse colon subgroup (p = 0.488) and the right half colon subgroup (p = 0.524). Regardless of the lesion size stratification or the lesion location stratification, there was no significant difference in the R0 resection rates among the subgroups both within the UEMR group or the CEMR group.

Histopathology

According to the pathological results, the most majority of polyps were adenoma in both groups (Table 2). In the UEMR group, one polyp was diagnosed as intramucosal adenocarcinoma and one as submucosal adenocarcinoma. In the CEMR group, three polyps were diagnosed as intramucosal adenocarcinoma and two as submucosal adenocarcinoma, and one polyp diagnosed as submucosal adenocarcinoma underwent surgery because of positive lateral and basal margins (less than 500 μm from the resection margin).

VAS of abdominal pain

The mean VAS of abdominal pain in the UEMR group was (3.2 ± 1.9), and (4.1 ± 2.1) in the CEMR group, indicating a statistically significant difference (p = 0.006) (Fig. 3).

Adverse events

Four patients in the UEMR group and six patients in the CEMR group experienced intraoperative bleeding, which stopped after electro- coagulation or metal clip closure. There was no statistically significant difference in the bleeding rates between the two groups (4.0% vs. 6.1%, p = 0.516). Both groups had no perforation or delayed bleeding (Table 4).

Discussion

The morbidity and mortality of colorectal cancer (CRC) is increasing year by year, and the age of onset is getting younger and younger, which seriously endangers the health and life of people. Deep learning algorithms based on artificial intelligence have shown great potential in the classification and diagnosis of CRC histopathology images. The advancement made possible by deep learning algorithms has the potential to improve CRC detection’s accuracy and efficacy12,13. The occurrence of CRC mostly goes through the stage of adenomatous polyps1. Early diagnosis and treatment of colorectal polyps are crucial for the prevention of colorectal cancer14. There are many methods for treating colorectal polyps. For sessile polyps with a diameter of less than 10 mm, APC, hot biopsy forceps, or snare removal can be used, especially cold snare polypectomy, which can reduce the incidence of delayed bleeding15,16. For polyps with a diameter more than 10 mm, it is recommended to undergo a complete mucosal resection surgery at once, but the maximum diameter should not exceed 20 mm in principle. For those exceeding 20 mm, endoscopic submucosal dissection(ESD) is recommended for treatment17. In this prospective, open-label, randomized, controlled trial, we aimed to treat sessile polyps with a diameter between 10 mm and 20 mm using UEMR and CEMR, and found that the resection rates and patient tolerance of UEMR were significantly better than that of CEMR, while the complication rates were comparable in the two groups.

The use of water injection instead of air injection for colonoscopy or resection of colorectal polyps is a recently developed technique gradually receiving attention18. Replacing air insufsessileion with water injection for colonoscopy can avoid irritation to the intestines, alleviate discomfort such as abdominal pain and bloating, and is not easy to form angles or loops, greatly improving patient tolerance and disease detection rate19,20. In a single-center, randomized trial, Falt et al. conducted colonoscopy using water injection or air insufsessileion methods on 92 patients with inflammatory bowel disease under non anesthesia, and found that the success rate of cecal intubation using water injection was significantly higher than that using air injection (73.9 vs. 45.7%, p = 0.01), while the discomfort score during insertion was signiffcantly lower in the water than in the air arm21. In a two-center randomized controlled trial, Cadoni et al. found that water-aided colonoscopy not only required less on-demand sedation and the mean pain score was lower, but also had a significantly higher overall adenoma detection rate than air insufsessileion colonoscopy22. In addition, during polypectomy, after soaking the polyps with water in the intestinal cavity, the buoyancy of water can lift the polyps and submucosal tissues upwards, making it easier to expose the root of the polyp, facilitating a comprehensive evaluation of polyp size, range, morphology, and observing the surrounding conditions of the lesion, thereby improving the resection rate and reducing complications23. For small size non-pedunculated colorectal polyps (4–9 mm in size), Zhang et al. found that UEMR is safer and just as effective as CEMR in en-bloc resection rate24. A multicenter randomized controlled trial conducted in Japan showed that the en bloc resection rate and R0 resection rate of UEMR for the treatment of intermediate-size colorectal polyps were 89% and 69%, respectively, which were significantly higher than the 75% and 50% of CEMR, while the median operation time and complication rates were similar10. In another study9, results showed that the incomplete resection rates of large (≥ 10 mm) nonpedunculated lesions for UEMR and CEMR did not differ (2% vs. 1.9%, p = 0.91), which meaning that there was no significant difference in resection rates between UEMR and CEMR. In addition, UEMR was performed significantly faster for large lesions. These results were not consistent with the above-mentioned multicenter trial10. To further compare the efficacy and safety of UEMR and CEMR for treating medium-size sessile polyps of the large intestine, a total of 204 polyps were included in 200 patients in this study, of which 101 were removed by UEMR and 103 by CEMR. The en bloc resection rate and R0 resection rate of UEMR were significantly higher than those of CEMR (91.1% vs. 80.6%, P = 0.032; 73.3% vs. 56.3%, P = 0.011), while there was no significant difference in the incidence of complications between the two methods, which were similar to the results of Yamashina et al.10. These results further confirmed the clinical efficacy and safety of UEMR.

In subgroup analysis, the R0 resection rates of the UEMR group were significantly higher than those in the CEMR group in the 10–15 mm subgroup and in the left half colon subgroup, but not in the 16–20 mm subgroup and the transverse colon and right half colon subgroups. This may be related to the factors such as difficulty in fully lifting the root of the larger polyps, difficulty in fully filling the transverse colon and right half colon with water, and high requirements for operation far from the anus. This may also be related to the small samples of these subgroups, which should be further confirmed by more studies.

Because of anesthesia risks and costs, anesthetic endoscopy is not very popular in our city. So we conducted this study recruting patients who wanted to received non anesthetic endoscopy. In terms of the patient tolerance, the mean VAS of abdominal pain of UEMR was significantly lower than that of CEMR [(3.2 ± 1.9) vs. (4.1 ± 2.1), P = 0.006], indicating that replacing submucosal injection with water immersion improved patient comfort. However, it should be pointed out that insufficient coordinate determination ability or understanding ability, as well as inadequate explanation by medical staff, may affect the VAS score. Further studies are warranted.

There was no statistically significant difference in the adverse events between the two groups in this study. No surgical site infection (SSI) happened in both groups. The SSI is the most common postoperative complication after colorectal surgery, causing pain and suffering to patients. In addition, this complication has been associated with negative economic impact, increased morbidity, extended postoperative hospital stay, readmission, sepsis, and death25,26. According to the literature, Butyrylcholinesterase (BChE) has been proved a marker for the risk of developing SSI and septic complications in patients undergoing colorectal surgery27.

There were some limitations of this study: firstly, this study was single-center and the sample was small; Secondly, there was no comparison between the operation time of the two groups; Thirdly, without long-term follow-up of patients, it was impossible to evaluate their long-term effects and polyp recurrence; Finally, and most importantly, there was no blindness to the endoscopist during randomization, which may cause some bias to the final results.

In conclusion, the therapeutic effect of UEMR for the treatment of medium-sized sessile colorectal polyps was superior to CEMR, and the patient tolerance was good with few complications, which is worthy of clinical promotion.

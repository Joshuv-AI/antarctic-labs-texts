Abstract
. It is known that there is no a Type I singularity for the Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class. In this paper, we study translating solitons which are important models of Type II singularities. A necessary condition for a blow-up limit arising at a Type II singularity of a Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class is provided. As an application, we try to understand the important open question proposed by Joyce-Lee-Tsui [ 10 ] and Neves-Tian [ 14 ] , whether the Lagrangian translating solitons constructed by Joyce-Lee-Tsui can be a blow-up limit for a Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M,\overline{\omega},J,\overline{g},\Omega)
be a Calabi-Yau manifold of complex dimension
n
with Kähler form
\overline{\omega}
, compatible complex structure
J
, associated Riemannian metric
\overline{g}
and parallel holomorphic
n
-form
\Omega
. An
n
-dimensional oriented submanifold
\Sigma
immersed into
M
is called
Lagrangian submanifold
if
\overline{\omega}|_{\Sigma}=0
. For a Lagrangian submanifold, the induced area form
d\mu_{\Sigma}
on
\Sigma
and the holomorphic
n
-form
\Omega
are related by (
[
5
]
):

(1.1)

\Omega|_{\Sigma}=e^{i\theta}d\mu_{\Sigma}=\cos\theta d\mu_{\Sigma}+i\sin\theta d\mu_{\Sigma},

where
\theta
is multi-valued and is well-defined up to an additive constant
2k\pi
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Nevertheless,
\cos\theta
and
\sin\theta
are single-valued functions on
\Sigma
. The function
\theta
is called
Lagrangian angle
of
\Sigma
.

A Lagrangian submanifold
\Sigma
is called
Special Lagrangian
if
\theta
is a constant;
almost calibrated
if
\cos\theta>0
. We say that
\Sigma
is
Lagrangian with zero Maslov class
if
\theta
is a single-valued function on
\Sigma
. It is known that the Lagrangian angle and the mean curvature vector of
\Sigma
are related by (
[
18
]
)

(1.2)

{\bf H}=J\nabla\theta.

In particular, a connected Lagrangian submanifold is special if and only if it is minimal.

The existence of special Lagrangian submanifolds is an important problem in differential geometry and mathematical physics. In a compact Kähler-Einstein surface, Schoen and Wolfson have shown the existence of a branched surface which minimizes areas among Lagrangian competitors in each Lagrangian homology class by variational methods (
[
15
]
).
Since a special Lagrangian submanifold is minimal, it makes it possible to approach the existence via the mean curvature flow.

Let
(M^{n+k},\overline{g})
be a Riemannian manifold and
\Sigma^{n}
be a manifold. Given an initial immersion

F_{0}:\Sigma\to M,~~~~\Sigma_{0}=F_{0}(\Sigma),

we consider a one-parameter family of smooth maps

F_{t}=F(\cdot,t):\Sigma\rightarrow M

with corresponding images

\Sigma_{t}=F_{t}(\Sigma)

satisfying the mean curvature flow equation (MCF):

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{d}{dt}F(x,t)={\bf H}(x,t),\\
\,\,\,\,\,F(x,0)=F_{0}(x).\end{array}\right.

Here
{\bf H}(x,t)
is the mean curvature vector of
\Sigma_{t}
at
F(x,t)
in
M
.

When the ambient manifold
M
is a Kähler-Einstein manifold of complex dimension
n
and the initial submanifold
\Sigma
is Lagrangian, Smoczyk (
[
16
]
) proved that along the mean curvature flow, the Lagrangian property is preserved and the Lagrangian angle
\theta
satisfies the evolution equation

(1.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\theta=\Delta\theta.

In this case, the flow is called
Lagrangian mean curvature flow
. From (
1.4
), it is easy to see that “almost calibrated Lagrangian” and “zero Maslov class” properties are preserved along the mean curvature flow. These flows are called
almost calibrated Lagrangian mean curvature flow
and
Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class
, respectively.

In general, the mean curvature flow may develop a singularity if the maximum of the norm of the second fundamental form blows up (
[
6
]
). In
[
12
]
, Neves constructed Lagrangians with arbitrarily small Lagrangian angles Hamiltonian isotopic to a plane in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, which
develop finite time singularities along the mean curvature flow. Several years later, Neves constructed a Lagrangian mean curvature flow in a compact Calabi-Yau surface which develops a finite time singularity (
[
13
]
). Recently, Su-Tsai-Wood constructed infinite time singularities of the Lagrangian mean curvature flow (
[
17
]
). These examples show that singularities of the Lagrangian mean curvature flow are generally unavoidable.

The counterexamples constructed by Neves are Lagrangians with zero Maslov classes, but not almost calibrated. Actually, it was conjectured by Thomas and Yau that

Conjecture (
[
18
]
):

Let L be a flow-stable Lagrangian in a Calabi-Yau manifold. Then the Lagrangian mean curvature flow will exist for all time and converge to the unique special Lagrangian in its Hamiltonian isotopy class.

According to the blow-up rate of the second fundamental form, Huisken classified the finite time singularities of the mean curvature flow into two types (
[
7
]
). With the help of the monotonicity formula (
[
7
]
), Huisken proved that the smooth blow-up limits of a Type I singularity consist of self-shrinkers. By establishing a new monotonicity formula, Chen-Li (
[
1
]
) proved that there is no Type I singularity for an almost calibrated Langrangian mean curvature flow (Wang (
[
19
]
) independently proved it). In
[
1
]
, Chen-Li showed further that the tangent flow of an almost calibrated Lagrangian mean curvature flow is a stationary tangent cone. Neves proved that these results also hold for zero-Maslov class case (
[
12
]
).

Since there are no Type I singularities, it is crucial to study finite time Type II singularities. By the standard blow-up analysis (
[
8
]
), it is known that the blow-up limit of a Type II singularity of the mean curvature flow consists of an eternal solution to the mean curvature flow in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with uniformly bounded second fundamental form.

As a typical Type II singularity, translating soliton plays an important role in the study of the mean curvature flow.

Definition 1.1
.

A submanifold
\Sigma^{n}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+k}
is a
translating soliton
to the mean curvature flow if there is a constant vector
{\bf T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+k}
, such that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t}=\Sigma+t{\bf T}

is a solution to the mean curvature flow. Equivalently, it holds

(1.5)

{\bf T}^{\perp}={\bf H}

on
\Sigma
everywhere, where
{\bf H}
is the mean curvature vector of
\Sigma
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+k}
.

There are a few results on the Type II blow-up limits for the Lagrangian mean curvature flow (
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
11
]
, etc.). For instance, we proved that any eternal solution to the mean curvature flow with nonnegative Gauss curvature cannot be a blow-up limit of an almost calibrated Lagrangian mean curvature flow. Neves-Tian proved a rigidity result for Lagrangian translating solitons (
[
14
]
). They showed that under some natural assumptions, if the first Betti number of the Lagrangian translating soliton is finite, and either
\int_{\Sigma}|{\bf H}|^{2}d\mu
is finite or
\Sigma
is static and almost calibrated, then the translating soliton is a plane.

In (
[
10
]
), Joyce-Lee-Tsui constructed interesting examples for Lagrangian translating solitons with oscillation of the Lagrangian angles arbitrarily small (see Proposition
2.1
in Section 2). These examples are Lagrangian with zero Maslov class but not almost calibrated. It is an interesting question that whether the example can arise as a blow-up limit of the Lagrangian mean curvature flow. This question was proposed by Joyce-Lee-Tsui (
[
10
]
, Question 3.4) and Neves-Tian (
[
14
]
, 1.1 Open questions).

Open Question:
(
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
) Can the translating solitons with small Lagrangian angle oscillation constructed by Joyce-Lee-Tsui in Proposition
2.1
arise as blow-ups of finite time singularities for the Lagrangian mean curvature flow, particularly when
n=2
?

Let
X_{0}\in M
be the blow-up point of the Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class at the first singular time
T
and
\Sigma
be a translating soliton arising as blow-up limit of the flow at
(X_{0},T)
(we refere to Section 3 and Section 4 for the details of the blow-up procedure). Denote
\underline{\theta}
and
\overline{\theta}
the infimum and supremum of the Lagrangian angle
\theta
on
\Sigma
, respectively. Then we have

(1.6)

\underline{\theta}=\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{t\to T}\inf_{s\in[t,T)}\inf_{B_{r}(X_{0})\cap\Sigma_{s}}\theta.

Our main purpose in this paper is to understand the above important open question, and prove the following result, which may be seen as a partial answer to the above open question:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T)}
be a smooth solution to the Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class which develops a singularity at a finite time
T
. Assume that there exist positive constants
r_{0}
and
t_{1}<T
, such that

(1.7)

\underline{\theta}=\inf_{s\in[t_{1},T)}\inf_{B_{r_{0}}(X_{0})\cap\Sigma_{s}}\theta,

and

(1.8)

\int_{t_{1}}^{T}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\log(A-\log|\theta-\underline{\theta}|)d\mu_{t}dt<\infty,

where
A
is a positive constant such that
A-\log|\theta-\underline{\theta}|>0
, then the translating soliton
\Sigma
constructed by Joyce-Lee-Tsui in Proposition
2.1
cannot be a blow-up limit at
(X_{0},T)
of the flow for
n\geq 2
.

Remark 1.2
.

Denote

\underline{\theta}(t):=\inf_{\Sigma_{t}}\theta.

Then (
1.4
) implies that
\underline{\theta}(t)
is monotonically increasing. Therefore, the main assumption (
1.7
) roughly says that the singularity of the flow occurs away from the minimal points of the Lagrangian angle.

The idea in the proof of the above theorem can be described as follows: we establish a new monotonicity formula for the Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class. Applying it we obtain a necessary condition for a blow-up limit of the flow (see Theorem
4.1
and Corollary
4.2
). Then we prove the main theorem by a contradiction, which follows by constructing suitable test functions in the necessary condition.

The subsequent sections are organized as follows: in Section 2, we recall Joyce-Lee-Tsui’s example of Lagrangian translating solitons with zero Maslov classes; in Section 3, we establish a new monotonicity formula for the Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class; in Section 4, we derive the necessary condition for a blow-up limit of the flow; in Section 5, we prove the main result in this paper.

2.
Joyce-Lee-Tsui’s Example of Lagrangian Translating Solitons

In this section, we will recall Joyce-Lee-Tsui’s example of Lagrangian translating solitons with zero Maslov classes.

In
[
10
]
, Joyce-Lee-Tsui constructed very interesting examples for Lagrangian translating solitons with oscillations of the Lagrangian angles arbitrarily small. Their construction is as follows (Corollary I of
[
10
]
):

Proposition 2.1
.

For given constants
\alpha\geq 0
and
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1}>0
, define

(2.1)

\phi_{j}(y)=\int_{0}^{y}\frac{dt}{\left(\frac{1}{a_{j}}+t^{2}\right)\sqrt{P(t)}}~,

where

P(t)=\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}t^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha t^{2}}-1\right],

for
j=1,2,\cdots,n-1
and
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then when
\alpha\neq 0
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\left(x_{1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+y^{2}}e^{i\phi_{1}(y)},\cdots,x_{n-1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{n-1}}+y^{2}}e^{i\phi_{n-1}(y)},\frac{y^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}x_{j}^{2}}{2}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\frac{i}{\alpha}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\phi_{j}(y)+\arg\left(y+iP(y)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right]\right):x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y\in{\mathbb{R}}\right\}

is a closed, embedded Lagrangian in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
diffeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which is a Lagrangian translating soliton with translating vector
{\bf T}=(0,0,\cdots,0,0,\alpha,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
.

There exist
\overline{\phi}_{1},\cdots,\overline{\phi}_{n-1}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
such that
\phi_{j}(y)\to\overline{\phi}_{j}
as
y\to\infty
and
\phi_{j}(y)\to-\overline{\phi}_{j}
as
y\to-\infty
for
j=1,\cdots,n-1
, and
\overline{\phi}_{1}+\cdots+\overline{\phi}_{n-1}<\frac{\pi}{2}
. For fixed
\alpha>0
, the map
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1})\mapsto(\overline{\phi}_{1},\cdots,\overline{\phi}_{n-1})
is a 1-1 correspondence from
(0,\infty)^{n-1}
to
\{(\overline{\phi}_{1},\cdots,\overline{\phi}_{n-1})\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})^{n-1}:\overline{\phi}_{1}+\cdots+\overline{\phi}_{n-1}<\frac{\pi}{2}\}
.

The Lagrangian angle of
L
in (
2.2
) varies between
\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\overline{\phi}_{j}
and
\pi-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\overline{\phi}_{j}
. In particular, by choosing
\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\overline{\phi}_{j}
close to
\frac{\pi}{2}
, the oscillation of the Lagrangian angle can be made arbitrarily small.

For our later use, we will compute all the geometric quantities for the translating soliton
L
in the above proposition.

The submanifold is parametrized by

(2.3)

\displaystyle F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(x_{1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+y^{2}}\cos\phi_{1}(y),x_{1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+y^{2}}\sin\phi_{1}(y),\cdots,\right.

\displaystyle\left.x_{n-1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{n-1}}+y^{2}}\cos\phi_{n-1}(y),x_{n-1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{n-1}}+y^{2}}\sin\phi_{n-1}(y),\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{y^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}x_{j}^{2}}{2},-\frac{1}{\alpha}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\phi_{j}(y)+\gamma(y)\right]\right),

where

(2.4)

\gamma(y)=\arg\left(y+iP(y)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\begin{cases}\arctan\frac{1}{\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}},&{\rm if}\ y>0;\\
\frac{\pi}{2},&{\rm if}\ y=0;\\
-\arctan\frac{1}{\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}}+\pi,&{\rm if}\ y<0.\\
\end{cases}

By direct computations, we have

(2.5)

\frac{d\phi_{j}}{dy}=\begin{cases}\frac{y}{(\frac{1}{a_{j}}+y^{2})\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}},&{\rm if}\ y>0;\\
-\frac{y}{(\frac{1}{a_{j}}+y^{2})\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}},&{\rm if}\ y<0,\end{cases}

and

(2.6)

\frac{d\gamma}{dy}=\begin{cases}-\frac{\alpha y}{\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}}-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{y}{\frac{1}{a_{j}}+y^{2}}}{\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}},&{\rm if}\ y>0;\\
\frac{\alpha y}{\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}}+\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{y}{\frac{1}{a_{j}}+y^{2}}}{\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}},&{\rm if}\ y<0.\end{cases}

Hence we have

(2.7)

\frac{d}{dy}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\phi_{j}(y)+\gamma(y)\right)=\begin{cases}-\frac{\alpha y}{\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}},&{\rm if}\ y>0;\\
\frac{\alpha y}{\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}},&{\rm if}\ y<0.\end{cases}

For any
y
, we have

(2.8)

\frac{d\phi_{j}}{dy}>0,\ \ \frac{d\gamma}{dy}<0,\ \ \frac{d}{dy}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\phi_{j}(y)+\gamma(y)\right)<0.

We also have by definition that

\lim_{y\to\infty}\phi_{j}(y)=\overline{\phi}_{j},\ \lim_{y\to-\infty}\phi_{j}(y)=-\overline{\phi}_{j},\ \lim_{y\to\infty}\gamma(y)=0,\ \lim_{y\to-\infty}\gamma(y)=\pi.

Therefore, we obtain

(2.9)

\lim_{y\to\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\phi_{j}(y)+\gamma(y)\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\overline{\phi}_{j},\ \lim_{y\to-\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\phi_{j}(y)+\gamma(y)\right)=\pi-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\overline{\phi}_{j}.

To proceed further, we first consider the case that
y>0
. In this case, it is clear that the tangent space of
L
is spanned by

F_{x_{1}}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+y^{2}}\cos\phi_{1}(y),\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+y^{2}}\sin\phi_{1}(y),0,0,\cdots,0,0,-x_{1},0\right)^{T},

\cdots

F_{x_{n-1}}=\left(0,0,0,0,\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{n-1}}+y^{2}}\cos\phi_{n-1}(y),\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{n-1}}+y^{2}}\sin\phi_{n-1}(y),\cdots,-x_{n-1},0\right)^{T}

and

F_{y}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{x_{1}y}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+y^{2}}}\left(\cos\phi_{1}(y)-\frac{\sin\phi_{1}(y)}{\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}}\right)\\

\end{array}\right).

The induced metric on
L
is given by

g_{x_{j}x_{j}}=\langle F_{x_{j}},F_{x_{j}}\rangle=\frac{1}{a_{j}}+x_{j}^{2}+y^{2},

g_{x_{i},x_{j}}=\langle F_{x_{i}},F_{x_{j}}\rangle=x_{i}x_{j},\ \ g_{x_{j},y}=\langle F_{x_{j}},F_{y}\rangle=0,

g_{yy}=\langle F_{y},F_{y}\rangle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{\frac{1}{a_{j}}+y^{2}}+1\right)\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}y^{2}}{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{k}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}.

In other words,

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}g_{x_{1}x_{1}}&\cdots&g_{x_{1}x_{n-1}}&g_{x_{1}y}\\

By direct computations, we see that
\nu_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle\int_{-R_{2}}^{-R_{1}}\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}^{\infty}\cap B_{R}(0)}(f^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon^{2}f)|{\bf H}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}^{\infty}}|^{2}\frac{1}{(-\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(\frac{|\widetilde{X}_{\infty}-{\bf a}|^{2}}{4\tau}\right)d\widetilde{\mu}^{\infty}_{\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{T}\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap{\rm supp}\phi}fd\mu_{t}dt+\frac{1}{(T-t_{1})^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\phi fd\mu_{t_{1}}\right).

Letting
R_{1}\to 0
,
R_{2}\to\infty
first and
R\to\infty
next, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}^{\infty}}(f^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon^{2}f)|{\bf H}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}^{\infty}}|^{2}\frac{1}{(-\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(\frac{|\widetilde{X}_{\infty}-{\bf a}|^{2}}{4\tau}\right)d\widetilde{\mu}^{\infty}_{\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{T}\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap{\rm supp}\phi}fd\mu_{t}dt+\frac{1}{(T-t_{1})^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\phi fd\mu_{t_{1}}\right)

holds for any fixed
{\bf a}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
.
∎

As a consequence, we can obtain the necessary condition for a translating soliton to be a blow-up limit at a finite time singularity of the Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class:

Corollary 4.2
.

If
\Sigma
is a Lagrangian translating soliton arising as a blow-up limit of a finite time singularity of the Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class, then for any nonnegative function
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
with the second derivative piecewise continuous satisfying

(4.8)

f^{\prime\prime}\geq\varepsilon^{2}f

for some
\varepsilon>0
and any fixed
t_{1}<T
, there is a constant
c_{4}
depending only on the initial surface
\Sigma_{0}
,
M
,
\varepsilon
,
T
and
{\bf T}
, such that for any
{\bf a}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(f^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon^{2}f)\frac{|{\bf H}_{\Sigma}|^{2}}{|X-{\bf a}|^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}e^{\frac{\langle X-{\bf a},{\bf T}\rangle}{2}}K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(\frac{|{\bf T}||X-{\bf a}|}{2}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{4}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{T}\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap{\rm supp}\phi}fd\mu_{t}dt+\frac{1}{(T-t_{1})^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\phi fd\mu_{t_{1}}\right),

where

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(z):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{\nu}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{1}{t^{\nu+1}}\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}-t\right)dt

is the modified Bessel function of the second kind.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma
is a translating soliton, we have
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\infty}_{\tau}=\Sigma+\tau{\bf T}
. Using the fact that
|{\bf H}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}^{\infty}}|^{2}(X+\tau{\bf T})=|{\bf H}_{\Sigma}|^{2}(X)
, we have by Theorem
4.1
that

\displaystyle c_{3}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{T}\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap{\rm supp}\phi}fd\mu_{t}dt+\frac{1}{(T-t_{1})^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\phi fd\mu_{t_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}^{\infty}}(f^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon^{2}f)|{\bf H}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}^{\infty}}|^{2}\frac{1}{(-\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(\frac{|\widetilde{X}-{\bf a}|^{2}}{4\tau}\right)d\widetilde{\mu}^{\infty}_{\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{\Sigma}(f^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon^{2}f)|{\bf H}_{\Sigma}|^{2}\frac{1}{(-\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(\frac{|X+\tau{\bf T}-{\bf a}|^{2}}{4\tau}\right)d\mu d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\int_{\Sigma}(f^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon^{2}f)|{\bf H}_{\Sigma}|^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{|X-\tau{\bf T}-{\bf a}|^{2}}{4\tau}\right)d\mu d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\int_{\Sigma}(f^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon^{2}f)|{\bf H}_{\Sigma}|^{2}\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{|X-{\bf a}|^{2}}{4\tau}-\frac{|{\bf T}|^{2}\tau}{4}+\frac{\langle X-{\bf a},{\bf T}\rangle}{2}\right)d\mu d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(f^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon^{2}f)|{\bf H}_{\Sigma}|^{2}e^{\frac{\langle X-{\bf a},{\bf T}\rangle}{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{|X-{\bf a}|^{2}}{4\tau}-\frac{|{\bf T}|^{2}\tau}{4}\right)d\tau d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|{\bf T}|^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\int_{\Sigma}(f^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon^{2}f)\frac{|{\bf H}_{\Sigma}|^{2}}{|X-{\bf a}|^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}e^{\frac{\langle X-{\bf a},{\bf T}\rangle}{2}}K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(\frac{|{\bf T}||X-{\bf a}|}{2}\right)d\mu,

where

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(z):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{\nu}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{1}{t^{\nu+1}}\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}-t\right)dt

is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.
Proof of the Main Theorems

In this section, we will first prove that a special function is integrable along the mean curvature flow by using the volume estimate by Jiang, Li and Wang (
[
9
]
). We then show that the example constructed by Joyce, Lee and Tsui (
[
10
]
) cannot arise as blow up limit under some natural assumptions. This partially answers the open question proposed by Joyce-Lee-Tsui and Neves-Tian (see Scetion 1). The following theorem is a consequence of Theorem 1.8 of
[
9
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{0\leq t<T}
be the Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class. Denote by
\underline{\theta}
a fixed constant. Then for any
t\in[0,T)
, the following inequality holds

(5.1)

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\log(A-\log|\theta-\underline{\theta}|)d\mu_{t}\leq\Lambda(t)

where
A
is a positive constant such that
A-\log|\theta-\underline{\theta}|>0
. Here
\Lambda
is a constant depending on
A
,
\Sigma
and
t
.

Proof.

Denote by
\theta_{max}:=\sup_{\Sigma_{t}}\theta
and
\theta_{min}:=\inf_{\Sigma_{t}}\theta
.
Then we have using the co-area formula and Theorem 1.8 of
[
9
]
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\log(A-\log|\theta-\underline{\theta}|)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\log(A-\log((\theta-\underline{\theta})_{+}+(\underline{\theta}-\theta)_{+})d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\{\theta-\underline{\theta}\geq 0\}}\log(A-\log(\theta-\underline{\theta}))d\mu_{t}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\{\theta-\underline{\theta}\leq 0\}}\log(A-\log(\underline{\theta}-\theta))d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta_{max}-\underline{\theta}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\{\theta-\underline{\theta}=\alpha\}}\frac{\log(A-\log(\theta-\underline{\theta}))}{|\nabla\theta|}d\sigma_{t}d\alpha

\displaystyle+\int^{0}_{\theta_{min}-\underline{\theta}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\{\theta-\underline{\theta}=\alpha\}}\frac{\log(A-\log(\underline{\theta}-\theta))}{|\nabla\theta|}d\sigma_{t}d\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta_{max}-\underline{\theta}}\log(A-\log\alpha)d\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\{0<\theta-\underline{\theta}<\alpha\}}d\mu_{t}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\underline{\theta}-\theta_{min}}\log(A-\log\alpha)d\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\{0<\underline{\theta}-\theta<\alpha\}}d\mu_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log(A-\log(\theta_{max}-\underline{\theta}))\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\{0<\theta-\underline{\theta}<\theta_{max}-\underline{\theta}\}}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\theta_{max}-\underline{\theta}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\{0<\theta-\underline{\theta}<\alpha\}}d\mu_{t}\right)\frac{1}{\alpha(A-\log\alpha)}d\alpha

\displaystyle+\log(A-\log(\underline{\theta}-\theta_{min}))\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\{0<\underline{\theta}-\theta<\underline{\theta}-\theta_{min}\}}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\underline{\theta}-\theta_{min}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\{0<\underline{\theta}-\theta<\alpha\}}d\mu_{t}\right)\frac{1}{\alpha(A-\log\alpha)}d\alpha

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[\log(A-\log(\theta_{max}-\underline{\theta}))+\log(A-\log(\underline{\theta}-\theta_{min}))]{\rm Area}(\Sigma_{t})

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\theta_{max}-\underline{\theta}}\frac{C\alpha^{\varepsilon}}{\alpha(A-\log\alpha)}d\alpha+\int_{0}^{\underline{\theta}-\theta_{min}}\frac{C\alpha^{\varepsilon}}{\alpha(A-\log\alpha)}d\alpha

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(A,\theta_{max}-\underline{\theta},\underline{\theta}-\theta_{min},n,\Sigma_{t},\theta)<\infty

where
C
and
\varepsilon
are positive constants depending on
\Sigma_{t}
,
n
and
\theta
. The last inequality follows from Theorem 1.8 in
[
9
]
.
∎

For a solution
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T)}
to the Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class in a compact Calabi-Yau manifold, we denote
\theta
as the Lagrangian angle of
\Sigma_{t}
. We also denote
\underline{\theta}(t)
and
\overline{\theta}(t)
as the minimum and the maximum of
\theta
on
\Sigma_{t}
respectively. Then
\underline{\theta}(t)
and

\frac{d}{dt}\underline{\theta}(t)\geq 0,\ \ \frac{d}{dt}\overline{\theta}(t)\leq 0.

In particular, we have

\overline{\theta}(t)-\underline{\theta}(t)\leq\overline{\theta}(0)-\underline{\theta}(0):=D.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

. On the Lagrangian translating soliton
\Sigma
, we will also denote
\theta
as the Lagrangian angle of the translating soliton
\Sigma
,
\underline{\theta}
as its minimum and
\overline{\theta}
as its maximum. Since
\theta
is uniformly bounded along the flow, we may assume that along the flow and consequently on the translating soliton we have

Let

\Sigma
be a translating soliton arising as blow-up limit of the flow at
(X_{0},T)

\underline{\theta}
and

the infimum and supremum of the Lagrangian angle

on
\Sigma

\underline{\theta}=\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{t\to T}\inf_{s\in[t,T)}\inf_{B_{r}(X_{0})\cap\Sigma_{s}}\theta.

Now we will prove the main theorem in this paper.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T)}
be a smooth solution to the Lagrangian mean curvature flow with zero Maslov class and develop singularity at finite time
T
. Assume that there exist positive constants

and

, such that

(5.3)

\underline{\theta}=\inf_{s\in[t_{1},T)}\inf_{B_{r_{0}}(X_{0})\cap\Sigma_{s}}\theta,

and

(5.4)

\int_{t_{1}}^{T}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\log(A-\log|\theta-\underline{\theta}|)d\mu_{t}dt:=c_{5}<\infty,

where

is a positive constant such that

, then the translating soliton

constructed by Joyce-Lee-Tsui in Proposition
2.1
cannot be a blow-up limit at
(X_{0},T)
of the flow.

Proof.

We prove the theorem by contradiction. Assume that the translating soliton
\Sigma
constructed by Joyce-Lee-Tsui in Proposition
2.1
is a blow-up limit at
(X_{0},T)
of the flow.
Without loss of generality, we may choose
{\bf T}=(0,0,\cdots,0,0,1,0)

\alpha=1
. We will choose the cutoff function
\phi
in Section 3 such that
{\rm supp}\phi\subset B_{r_{0}}(X_{0})

By the assumption
5.3
, we see that for any
\delta\in(0,1)
independent of
r_{0}
and
t_{1}

Set

and we define the function

f_{\delta}(v)=\log(A-\log(v+\delta)),

\Sigma

for

. We compute

\displaystyle f_{\delta}^{\prime}(v)=\frac{-1}{[A-\log(v+\delta)](v+\delta)},

\displaystyle f_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(v)=\frac{1}{[A-\log(v+\delta)](v+\delta)^{2}}-\frac{1}{[A-\log(v+\delta)]^{2}(v+\delta)^{2}}.

By the choice of

are satisfied for some
\varepsilon

(5.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}f_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}\frac{|{\bf H}_{\Sigma}|^{2}}{|X|^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}e^{\frac{\langle X,{\bf T}\rangle}{2}}K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(\frac{|{\bf T}||X-{\bf a}|}{2}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{6}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{T}\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap{\rm supp}\phi}f_{\delta}d\mu_{t}dt+\frac{1}{(T-t_{1})^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\phi f_{\delta}d\mu_{t_{1}}\right).

For the right hand side of (

5.1
and the assumption (
5.4

(5.8)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{T}\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap{\rm supp}\phi}f_{\delta}d\mu_{t}dt+\frac{1}{(T-t_{1})^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\phi f_{\delta}d\mu_{t_{1}}\leq c_{7}

for some constant
c_{7}

c_{5}
and
t_{1}
, but independent of
\delta
.

In fact, without using Theorem 5.1, applying the assumption (
5.4
), we can see that (
5.8
) holds for some
t_{1}
in
(0,T)
.

We next estimate the left hand side of (
5.7

A

(5.9)

\displaystyle f_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(v)\geq\frac{1}{2[A-\log(v+\delta)](v+\delta)^{2}}.

It remains to compute the quantity

\displaystyle\frac{|{\bf H}_{\Sigma}|^{2}}{|X|^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}e^{\frac{\langle X,{\bf T}\rangle}{2}}K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(\frac{|{\bf T}||X-{\bf a}|}{2}\right)d\mu.

From (
2.3
), it follows that

(5.10)

\displaystyle|X|^{2}=\left(\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}x_{j}^{2}+y^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{a_{j}}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\phi_{j}(y)+\gamma(y)\right)^{2}.

Let
\theta
be the Lagrangian angle of the translating soliton
\Sigma
and
\underline{\theta}
its infimum. Then it is known from Section 2 that for
y>0

\displaystyle\theta(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\phi_{j}(y)+\gamma(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{tdt}{\left(\frac{1}{a_{j}}+t^{2}\right)\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{i}t^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha t^{2}}-1}}+\arctan\frac{1}{\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{i}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}},

and

\underline{\theta}=\lim_{y\to\infty}\theta(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\overline{\phi}_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{tdt}{\left(\frac{1}{a_{j}}+t^{2}\right)\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{i}t^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha t^{2}}-1}}>0.

So,

\displaystyle v(y)=\theta(y)-\underline{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arctan\frac{1}{\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{i}y^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha y^{2}}-1}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{tdt}{\left(\frac{1}{a_{j}}+t^{2}\right)\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{i}t^{2})\cdot e^{\alpha t^{2}}-1}}.

We therefore have
\lim\limits_{y\to\infty}v(y)=0
,

\frac{dv}{dy}=-\frac{y}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}y^{2})\cdot e^{y^{2}}-1}},

and for

(5.11)

v(y)=\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{t}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}t^{2})\cdot e^{t^{2}}-1}}dt.

Lemma 5.3
.

We have for
y\geq 1

(5.12)

\frac{1}{n\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j})}}y^{1-n}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}\leq v(y)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j}}}y^{1-n}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}.

Proof.

By (
5.11
), we compute by integration by parts that

\displaystyle v(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{t}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}t^{2})\cdot e^{t^{2}}-1}}dt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{y^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}t)\cdot e^{t}-1}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{y^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{t}{2}}}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}t)-e^{-t}}}dt=-\int_{y^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}t)-e^{-t}}}de^{-\frac{t}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}y^{2})-e^{-y^{2}}}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{y^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}t)+e^{-t}\right)}{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}t)-e^{-t}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}y^{2})-e^{-y^{2}}}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j})}}y^{1-n}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}.

To prove the lower bound, we compute for
y\geq 1

\displaystyle v(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{t}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}t^{2})\cdot e^{t^{2}}-1}}dt\geq\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{t}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j})\cdot t^{2n-2}\cdot e^{t^{2}}}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j})\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{y^{2}}^{\infty}t^{\frac{1-n}{2}}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}dt=2^{\frac{1-n}{2}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j})\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\frac{y^{2}}{2}}^{\infty}s^{\frac{1-n}{2}}e^{-s}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{1-n}{2}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j}\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{3-n}{2},\frac{y^{2}}{2}\right),

where

is the incomplete Gamma function defined by

\Gamma(a,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{a-1}e^{-t}dt.

Notice that we have the estimate for

(5.13)

\frac{x}{x+1-a}<x^{1-a}e^{x}\Gamma(a,x)<\frac{x+1}{x+2-a}.

Therefore, we have for

v(y)\geq\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j})\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{y^{3-n}}{y^{2}+n-1}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}\geq\frac{1}{n\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j})}}y^{1-n}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

We next estimate
K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(\frac{|X|}{2}\right)
. Since

\displaystyle|X|^{2}\geq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\phi_{j}(y)+\gamma(y)\right)^{2}\geq\underline{\theta}^{2}>0.

In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{|X|}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\underline{\theta}>0.

Lemma 5.4
.

For each
\nu
, there is a positive constant
c_{8}
depending on
\underline{\theta}
,

and
\nu
such that

(5.15)

\sqrt{z}e^{z}K_{\nu}(z)\geq c_{8}>0,\ \ \forall z\geq\frac{1}{2}\underline{\theta}.

Proof.

It is known that the asymptotic behaviour of
K_{\nu}(z)
as

is given by

K_{\nu}(z)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}e^{-z}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(\nu)}{z^{k}}.

Therefore, there is a positive constant
c_{9}>0
and
B_{1}
sufficiently large, such that

\sqrt{z}e^{z}K_{\nu}(z)\geq c_{9},\ \ \forall z\geq B_{1}.

Since the function
\sqrt{z}e^{z}K_{\nu}(z)
is positive and continuous on
\left[\frac{1}{2}\underline{\theta},B_{1}\right]
, there is
c_{10}>0
such that

\sqrt{z}e^{z}K_{\nu}(z)\geq c_{10},\ \ \forall z\in\left[\frac{1}{2}\underline{\theta},B_{1}\right].

Choose
c_{8}=\min\{c_{9},c_{10}\}
, and then we have

\sqrt{z}e^{z}K_{\nu}(z)\geq c_{8}>0,\ \ \forall z\in\left[\frac{1}{2}\underline{\theta},\infty\right).

This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma
5.4
tells us that for
y\geq 1
,

K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(\frac{|X|}{2}\right)\geq c_{8}\left(\frac{|X|}{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{|X|}{2}}.

We also have

e^{\frac{\langle X,{\bf T}\rangle}{2}}=e^{\frac{y^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}x_{j}^{2}}{4}}.

Therefore, we have from (

\displaystyle\frac{|{\bf H}_{\Sigma}|^{2}}{|X|^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}e^{\frac{\langle X,{\bf T}\rangle}{2}}K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(\frac{|X|}{2}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{y^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}x_{j}^{2}}{4}}|X|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(\frac{|X|}{2}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{\frac{1}{a_{j}}+y^{2}}+1\right)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}y^{2})\cdot e^{y^{2}}}

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{\frac{1}{a_{j}}+y^{2}}+1\right)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{a_{j}}+y^{2}\right)\cdot\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j}\cdot e^{y^{2}}y^{2}}}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_{j}y^{2})\cdot e^{y^{2}}-1}}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n-1}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{15}\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\frac{v}{2[A-\log(v+\delta)](v+\delta)^{2}}dv
